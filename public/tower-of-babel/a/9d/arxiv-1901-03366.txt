Continuous Regular Functions
Alexi Block Gorman, Philipp Hieronymi, Elliot Kaplan, Ruoyu Meng, Erik Walsberg, Zihe Wang, Ziqin Xiong, Hongru Yang

Abstract
. Following Chaudhuri, Sankaranarayanan, and Vardi, we say that a function f : [ 0 , 1 ] → [ 0 , 1 ] f:[0,1]\to[0,1] is r r -regular if there is a Büchi automaton that accepts precisely the set of base r ∈ ℕ r\in\mathbb{N} representations of elements of the graph of f f . We show that a continuous r r -regular function f f is locally affine away from a nowhere dense, Lebesgue null, subset of [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] . As a corollary we establish that every differentiable r r -regular function is affine. It follows that checking whether an r r -regular function is differentiable is in PSPACE \operatorname{PSPACE} . Our proofs rely crucially on connections between automata theory and metric geometry developed by Charlier, Leroy, and Rigo.

Introduction

The study of regular real analysis was proposed by Chaudhuri, Sankaranarayanan, and Vardi in
[
CSV13
]
as the analysis of real functions whose graphs are encoded by automata on infinite words. The motivation for such an investigation is to produce automata-theoretic decision procedures deciding continuity and other properties of regular functions. As part of that study in
[
CSV13
]
, the authors establish novel topological-geometric results about regular functions, in particular Lipschitzness and Hölder-continuity. This investigation is part of a larger enterprise of studying subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
described in terms of such automata, and has seen various applications in the verification of systems with unbounded mixed variables taking integer or real values (see Boigelot et al.
[
BBR97
,
BJW05
,
BRW98
]
). Recently, there has been a new focus on the study of topological and metric-geometrical properties of such sets (see Adamczewski and Bell
[
AB11
]
and Charlier, Leroy, and Rigo
[
CLR15
]
). We continue the investigation by giving answers to two questions raised in
[
CSV13
]
in the case of regular functions with equal input and output base:

(1)

Is there a simple characterization of continuous or differentiable regular functions?

(2)

Is it decidable to check whether a regular function is differentiable?

Let
r,s\geq 1
be positive integers, set
\Sigma_{r}=\{0,\dots,r-1\}
and
\Sigma_{s}=\{0,\dots,s-1\}
. A function
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is
(r,s)
-regular
if there is a non-deterministic Büchi automaton in the language
\Sigma_{r}\times\Sigma_{s}
accepting precisely all words
(w_{1,1},w_{2,1})(w_{1,2},w_{2,2})\dots\in{(\Sigma_{r}\times\Sigma_{s})}^{\omega}
for which there are
a,b\in[0,1]
such that
0.w_{1,1}w_{1,2}\dots
is a base
r
expansion of
a
,
0.w_{2,1}w_{2,2}\dots
is a base
s
expansion of
b
, and
f(a)=b
. If
r=s
, we simply say
f
is
r
-regular.

An instructional example of a continuous
3
-regular function is the function that maps
x\in[0,1]
to the distance between
x
and the classical middle-thirds Cantor set; see Figure
1
. Observe that this function is locally affine away from a nowhere dense set. The main technical result of this paper shows that this holds for all continuous
r
-regular functions. We say that a function
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
on an interval
I
is
\mathbb{Q}
-
affine
if it is of the form
g(t)=\alpha t+\beta
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be a continuous,
r
-regular function. Then there is a nowhere dense Lebesgue null set
C\subseteq[0,1]
such that
f
is locally
\mathbb{Q}
-affine away from
C
.

We know that Theorem A fails for
(r,s)
-regular functions when
r>s
.

Theorem B
.

A differentiable
r
-regular function is
\mathbb{Q}
-affine. Given a Büchi automaton that recognizes an
r
-regular function
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
, the problem of checking whether
f
is differentiable, is in
\operatorname{PSPACE}
.

We do not know whether Theorem B also fails in general, nor whether Question (2) has a positive answer in general. Furthermore, Theorem A gives an affirmative answer to a special case of an important question in mathematical logic about continuous definable functions in first-order expansions of the ordered additive group of real numbers. We discuss this question in Section
7
.

Theorem B improves several results in the literature. Let
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be
r
-recognizable. In
[
HW17
]
a model-theoretic argument was used to show that
f
is affine whenever
f
is continuously differentiable. Similar results had been proven before for
C^{2}
functions, or for more restricted classes of automata, by Anashin
[
Ana15
]
, Konečný
[
Kon02
]
, and Muller
[
Mul94
]
. In unpublished work, Bhaskar, Moss, and Sprunger
[
Bha
]
also showed that any differentiable function computable by a letter-to-letter transducer is affine.

Our proof of Theorem A depends on three ingredients. The first is the theorem, proven in
[
CSV13
]
, that every continuous
r
-regular function
[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz.
The second ingredient is the main result from
[
CLR15
]
which implies that the graph of an
r
-regular function is the attractor of a graph-directed iterative function system. This observation allows us to use the full power of metric geometry to study regular functions. In particular, we adjust to our setting the proof of the famous result of Hutchinson
[
Hut81
, Remark 3.4]
that a Lipschitz curve in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which is the attractor of a linear iterated function system is a line segment.

2.1.
Büchi Automata

A
Büchi automaton (over an alphabet
\Sigma
)
is a quintuple

\Sigma
is a finite alphabet,

We fix a Büchi automaton

for the remainder of this section.
We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
deterministic
if

is a singleton and for all

(q,\sigma,p)\in\Delta
.

Throughout a

and a set of edges

The graph underlying

is
(Q,\Delta,s,t)
where
s(p,\sigma,q)=p

.

Let
p
and

be states in
Q
. A

of length

to

\left((p,\sigma_{1},s_{1}),(s_{1},\sigma_{2},s_{2}),\ldots,(s_{n-1},\sigma_{n},q)\right)\in\Delta^{<\omega}.

We call the word
\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n}
the label of the path. We denote by
P_{p,q}(n)\subseteq\Sigma^{n}
the set of all labels of paths of length
n
from
p
to
q
. Note that
P_{p,q}(0)
is the set containing just the empty string if

is the empty set otherwise.

Let
\sigma
be a finite or infinte word over alphabet
\Sigma
. A

\sigma
from
p
is a finite or infinite sequence
s

Q
such that
s_{0}=p
,
(s_{n},\sigma_{n},s_{n+1})\in\Delta
for all
n<|\sigma|

and,
|s|=|\sigma|+1

is finite, and infinite otherwise. If
p\in I
, we say

is a
run of
\sigma

\sigma
is
accepted by
\mathcal{A}
if there is a run
s_{0}s_{1}\ldots
of
\sigma
such that
\{n:s_{n}\in F\}
is infinite.
We let
L(\mathcal{A})
be the set of words accepted by
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
q\in Q
be a state of
\mathcal{A}
. We say that
q
is
accessible
if there is a path from
I
to
q
, and that
q
is

if there is a path from

We say that
\mathcal{A}
is

if all states are accessible and co-accessible. By removing all states that are not both accessible and co-accessible from

, we obtain a trim Büchi Automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
that accepts exactly the same words as
\mathcal{A}
.

We say that
\mathcal{A}
is

\{q\in Q:\text{there is a nonempty path from }q\text{ to }q\}\subseteq F.

The

of
\mathcal{A}
is the Büchi automaton
\bar{\mathcal{A}}=(Q,\Sigma,\Delta,I,Q)
; that is the automaton in which all states are changed to accept states. Note that the closure of
\mathcal{A}
is closed.

Two elements of
p,q
of
Q

lie in the same strongly connected component
if there is a path from
p
to
q
and vice versa. Being in the same strongly connected component is an equivalence relation on
Q
. We call each equivalence class a
strongly connected component
of
\mathcal{A}
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
strongly connected
if it only has one strongly connected component.
The
condensation
of
\mathcal{A}
is the graph

whose vertices are the strongly connected components of the underlying graph of
\mathcal{A}
such that two strongly connected components
C_{1},C_{2}
are connected by an edge if

•

C_{1}\neq C_{2}
,

•

there are vertices
q_{1}\in C_{1},q_{2}\in C_{2}
and a path from
q_{1}
to
q_{2}
.

A
sink
of
\mathcal{A}
is a strongly connected compontent of
\mathcal{A}
that has no outgoing edges in the condensation of
\mathcal{A}
.

We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
weak
if
whenever
p,q\in Q
belong to the same strongly connected component,

p\in F
if and only if
q\in F
. Observe that every closed automaton is weak. We will use the fact that whenever
\mathcal{A}
is weak, there is a weak deterministic automata
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
such that

(see Boigelot, Jodogne, and Wolper
[
BJW05

2.4.
Affine regular functions

In this subsection we show that every affine
r
-regular function
J\to[0,1]
defined on an interval
J\subseteq[0,1]
is
\mathbb{Q}
-affine.
We require the following well-known fact.

Fact 1
.

Every Büchi automaton that accepts an infinite word, accepts an infinite periodic word.

Lemma
2
below is a corollary to Fact
1
.
One can use Lemma
2
to show that many familiar non-affine functions
[0,1]\to[0,1]
are not
r
-regular.
For example, it is relatively easy to show that a polynomial of degree
\geq 2
cannot be
r
-regular using Lemma
2

Lemma 2
.

Let
J
be a nonempty subinterval of
[0,1]
and let
f:J\to[0,1]
be
r
-regular. Then
f(q)\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
q\in\mathbb{Q}\cap J
.

The proof below shows that Lemma
2
holds more generally for
(r,s)
-regular functions.

Proof 2.1
.

By Fact
1
every
r
-recognizable subset of
{[0,1]}^{n}
contains a point with rational coordinates.
In particular, every
r
-recognizable singleton has rational coordinates.
Fix a rational
0\leq q\leq 1
.
It is easy to see that
\{(q,y):0\leq y\leq 1\}
is
r
-recognizable.
As
\Gamma(f)
is
r
-recognizable, and the intersection of two
r
-recognizable sets is
r
-recognizable, it follows that

\{(q,f(q))\}=\{(q,y):0\leq y\leq 1\}\cap\Gamma(f)

is
r
-recognizable. Thus
f(q)
is rational.

Lemma
2
and the fact that an affine function that takes rational values on rational points is necessarily
\mathbb{Q}
-affine, together imply the following:

Proposition 3
.

Let
J\subseteq[0,1]
be a nonempty open interval and let
f:J\to[0,1]
be
r
-regular and affine. Then
f
is
\mathbb{Q}
-affine.

Proposition
3
is a special case of a result on first-order expansions
(\mathbb{R},<,+,0)
, see
[
FHW17
, Theorem 3.9]
.
This more general result shows that Proposition
3
still holds when the graph of
f
is
r
-recognized by an Büchi automaton with advice, where the automaton that recognizes the graph can access a fixed infinite advice string.

2.5.
Graph Directed Iterated Function Systems and Self-similar sets

Let
(X,d)
and
(X^{\prime},d^{\prime})
be metric spaces.
A
similarity of ratio
r
between
(X,d)
and
(X^{\prime},d^{\prime})
is a function
f:X\to X^{\prime}
such that

d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))=\frac{1}{r}d(x,y)\quad\text{for all}\quad x,y\in X.

A
graph-directed iterated function system (of ratio
r
)
, or a GDIFS for short, is a quadruple
\mathcal{G}=(V,E,s,t,X,S)
such that

(1)

(V,E,s,t)
is a graph,

(2)

X={(X_{v},d_{v})}_{v\in V}
is a family of metric spaces, and

(3)

S={(S_{e}:X_{t(e)}\to X_{s(e)})}_{e\in E}
is a family of functions such that
S_{e}
is a similarity of ratio
r
for all
e\in E
.

We typically drop the source and range maps.
We let
E_{u,v}
denote the set of edges
e\in E
from
u
to
v
.
An
attractor
for
\mathcal{G}
is a collection
K={(K_{u})}_{u\in V}
such that each
K_{u}
is a non-empty compact subset of
X_{u}
and

K_{u}=\bigcup_{v\in V}\bigcup_{e\in E_{u,v}}S_{e}(K_{v})\quad\text{for all }u\in V.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma_{r}^{n},\Delta,I,F)
be a Büchi automaton. We associate a GDIFS
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
of ratio
r
to
\mathcal{A}
as follows.
Let
V:=Q
.
Let
(X_{v},d_{v})
be
{[0,1]}^{n}
with the usual euclidean metric for each
v\in V
.
Set
E:=\Delta
, and for each
\delta=(u,\sigma,v)\in\Delta
let

S_{\delta}(x)=\frac{x+\sigma}{r}\quad\text{for all }x\in{[0,1]}^{n}.

All GDFIS considered will be of the form
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
for some Büchi automaton
\mathcal{A}
over
\Sigma_{r}^{n}
. While this is just a small subset of all GDFIS, we need the generality of GDFIS in contrast to just iterative function systems to account for the case when
\mathcal{A}
has more than a single state.

For
p,q\in Q
, let
W_{p,q}(n)
be the set of paths of length
n
from
p
to
q
. Since
{(K_{q})}_{q\in Q}
is the attractor of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
, we obtain the following fact by an iterative application of the attractor property.

Fact 4
.

Let
n\in N
and
p\in Q
. Then

K_{p}=\bigcup_{q\in Q}\bigcup_{(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})\in W_{p,q}(n)}(S_{\delta_{1}}\circ\ldots\circ S_{\delta_{n}})(K_{q}).

Throughout this paper will use the following result from
[
CLR15
]
connecting attractors of GDFIS and
r
-recognizable sets.

{factC}

[
[
CLR15
, Theorem 57]
]
Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is trim and closed. Then

V_{r}(\mathcal{A})=\bigcup_{q\in I}K_{q},

where
{(K_{q})}_{q\in Q}
is the unique attractor of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
.

We observe that the GDFIS
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
only depends on
Q,n,r
and
\Delta
, but not on
I
or
F
. Therefore the unique attractor of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
also only depends on
Q,n,r
and
\Delta
, but not on
I
or
F
.

2.6.
Logic

As shown in Boigelot, Rassart, and Wolper
[
BRW98
]
, there is also a connection between
r
-recognizable sets and sets definable in a particular first-order expansion of the ordered real additive group
(\mathbb{R},<,+,0)
.
Let
V_{r}(x,u,k)
be the ternary predicate on
\mathbb{R}
that holds whenever
u=r^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 1
and there is a base-
r
expansion of
x
with
n
-th digit
k
.
Let
\mathscr{T}_{r}
be the first-order structure
(\mathbb{R},<,+,0,V_{r})
.

{factC}

[
[
BRW98
, Theorem 5]
]
Let
X\subseteq{[0,1]}^{n}
. Then
X
is
r
-recognizable if and only if
X
is definable in
\mathscr{T}_{r}
(without parameters).

Fact
2.6
is a powerful tool for establishing that the collection of
r
-recognizable sets is closed under various operations.
For example it may be used to easily show that the interior and closure of an
r
-recognizable set are also
r
-recognizable.

3.
Tools from metric geometry

3.1.
Hausdorff Measure

We recall the definition of the one-dimensional Hausdorff measure (generalized arc length) of a subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We let
\operatorname{Diam}(B)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be the diameter of a subset
B
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Given
\delta>0

we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}(X)
the collection of all countable collections
\mathcal{B}
of closed balls of diameter
\leq\delta
covering
X
.
Given
\delta>0
we declare

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\delta}(X)=\inf_{\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta}}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname{Diam}(B)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}.

Note that
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\delta}(X)
decreases with
\delta
. Thus either
\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\delta}(X)
or is infinite. The one-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
X
is

\mathcal{H}^{1}(X):=\sup_{\delta>0}H^{1}_{\delta}(X)=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\delta}(X).

Recall that
\mathcal{H}^{1}
agrees with the usual Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
.
We now prove a general lemma about graphs of Lipschitz functions.

Lemma 5
.

Let
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be a Lipschitz function and let
X
be a connected subset of
\Gamma(f)
.
Then

\mathcal{H}^{1}(X)\geq\operatorname{Diam}(X).

Proof 3.1
.

Set
Y:=\pi(X)
. Since
X
is connected, so is
Y
.
Since
f|_{Y}
is a Lipschitz function and
Y
is connected,
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Gamma(f|_{Y}))
is equal to the arc length of
\Gamma(f|_{Y})
.
Thus
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Gamma(f|_{Y}))\geq\operatorname{Diam}(\Gamma(f|_{Y}))
.
Now observe that
X=\Gamma(f|_{Y})
.

3.2.
Nowhere dense recognizable sets are Lebesgue null

We first recall an important definition from metric geometry.
A subset
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\varepsilon
-porous
if every open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with radius
t
contains an open ball of radius
\varepsilon t
that is disjoint from
X
.
A subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
porous
if it is
\varepsilon
-porous for some
\varepsilon>0
.
Lemma
6
holds for recognizable subsets of
{[0,1]}^{n}
.
We only require the one-dimensional case, so to avoid very mild technicalities we only treat that case.

Lemma 6
.

Let
X\subseteq[0,1]
be
r
-recognizable and nowhere dense.
Then
X
is porous.

In the following proof an
r^{-n}
-
interval
is an interval of the form
\left[\frac{m}{r^{n}},\frac{m+1}{r^{n}}\right]
.

Proof 3.2
.

Recall that the
r
-kernel of
X
is the collection of sets of the form

r^{n}\left(X\cap\left[\frac{m}{r^{n}},\frac{m+1}{r^{n}}\right]-m\right),

where
n\geq 1
and
0\leq m\leq r^{n}-1
.
By Fact
2.2
, the
r
-kernel of
X
consists of only finitely many distinct subsets of
[0,1]
, each of which has empty interior.
Thus, for some
k\geq 1
, there is an
r^{-k}
-interval that is disjoint from every element of the
r
-kernel of
X
.
It follows that any
r^{-n}
-interval contains an
r^{-(n+k)}
-interval which is disjoint from
X
.
Note that any interval
(x-t,x+t)\subseteq[0,1]
contains an
r^{-n}
-interval of length
\geq r^{-1}2t
.
Thus any interval
(x-t,x+t)\subseteq[0,1]
contains an
r^{-n}
-interval with length
\geq r^{-(k+1)}2t
that is disjoint from
X
.
Thus
X
is
2r^{-(k+1)}
-porous.

It is shown in Luukkainen
[
Luu98
, Theorem 5.2]
that a subset
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is porous if and only if the Assouad dimension of
X
is strictly less than
n
.
The Hausdorff dimension of a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is bounded above by its Assouad dimension.
It follows that a nowhere dense
r
-recognizable subset of
{[0,1]}^{n}
has Hausdorff dimension
<n
.
In particular a nowhere dense
r
-recognizable subset of
[0,1]
is Lebesgue null.
For the sake of completeness we provide the following more elementary proof.

Proposition 7
.

Every porous subset of
[0,1]
is Lebesgue null. In particular, every nowhere dense
r
-recognizable subset of
[0,1]
is Lebesgue null.

Proof 3.3
.

Let
X\subseteq[0,1]
be
\varepsilon
-porous.
Note that the closure of
X
is also
\varepsilon
-porous.
After replacing
X
by its closure if necessary we suppose
X
is closed, and so in particular, Lebesgue measurable.
Every interval
(x-t,x+t)\subseteq[0,1]
contains an interval with length at least
2\varepsilon t
that is disjoint from
X
.
Hence

\mathcal{H}^{1}((x-t,x+t)\cap X)\leq(1-\varepsilon)2t.

Thus

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}((x-t,x+t)\cap X)}{2t}\leq 1-\varepsilon

for all
x,t>0
such that
(x-t,x+t)\subseteq[0,1]
.
Suppose
X
has positive Lebesgue measure.
By the Lebesgue density theorem we have

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}((x-t,x+t)\cap X)}{2t}=1

for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-almost all
x\in X
.
This is a contradiction.

4.
The strongly connected case

In this section we prove Theorem A in the special case when the
r
-regular function under consideration is accepted by a strongly connected determinstic Büchi automaton. Indeed, we establish the following strengthening of Theorem A in this case.

Theorem 8
.

Let
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be an
r
-regular continuous function whose graph is
r
-accepted by a strongly connected deterministic Büchi automaton
\mathcal{A}
. Then
f
is
\mathbb{Q}
-affine.

This extends a classical result of Hutchinson
[
Hut81
, Remark 3.4]
for functions whose graph is the attractor of an iterative function system. The Hutchinson’s result almost directly implies Theorem
8
when the automaton
\mathcal{A}
only has one state.

4.1.
Two lemmas

We collect two lemmas about strongly connected Büchi automata before proving Theorem
8
.
We let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,\Delta,I,F)
be a Büchi automaton.
Recall that for
p,q\in Q
, the set
P_{p,q}(n)\subseteq\Sigma^{n}
is the set of all labels of paths of length
n
from
p
to
q
.

Lemma 9
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is strongly connected. Let
p\in Q
be such that

\left|\bigcup_{q\in Q}P_{p,q}(n)\right|\geq r^{n}\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then

\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{|P_{p,q}(n)|}{r^{n}}>0\quad\text{for all }q\in Q.

Proof 4.1
.

Since
Q
is finite, the pigeonhole principle yields a state
p_{0}\in Q
such that

\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{|P_{p,p_{0}}(n)|}{r^{n}}>0.

Let
q\in Q
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is strongly connected, there is a path of length
k
from
p_{0}
to
q
. Thus for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

|P_{p,q}(n+k)|\geq|P_{p,p_{0}}(n)|.

Therefore

\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{|P_{p,q}(n)|}{r^{n}}=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{|P_{p,q}(n+k)|}{r^{n+k}}\geq\frac{1}{r^{k}}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{|P_{p,p_{0}}(n)|}{r^{n}}>0.

Lemma 10
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is strongly connected, deterministic and
\Sigma=\Sigma_{r}
. If the interior of
V_{r}(\mathcal{A})
is non-empty, then
V_{r}(\mathcal{A})=[0,1]
.

Proof 4.2
.

Set
X:=V_{r}(\mathcal{A})
.
Let
J\subseteq[0,1]
be an open interval contained in
X
.
Towards a contradiction, we suppose that
[0,1]\setminus X
is non-empty. Fix
z\in[0,1]\setminus X
.
Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in\Sigma_{r}^{\omega}
be such that

\{w\in\Sigma_{r}^{\omega}\ :\ v_{r}(w)=z\}=\{w_{1},w_{2}\}.

The main idea is as follows: By strong connectedness of
\mathcal{A}
we can preceed either
w_{1}
or
w_{2}
with any prefix that is consistent with being in
X
, and still land outside of
X
. This will contradict
J\subseteq X
. We now make this argument precise.

Since
z\notin X
, there is a state
p_{0}\in Q
such that there is no run of either
w_{1}
or
w_{2}
from
p_{0}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is strongly connected, there are
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
u\in\Sigma_{r}^{t}
and a run
s_{0}s_{1}\dots s_{t}
of
u
such that
s_{t}=p_{0}
and

v_{r}(\{uw\ :\ w\in\Sigma_{r}^{\omega}\})\subseteq J.

(1)

Since
\mathcal{A}
is deterministic, our assumption on
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
implies that the set
\{w\in\Sigma_{r}\ :\ uw\in L(\mathcal{A})\}
contains neither
w_{1}
nor
w_{2}
. Set
y:=v_{r}(uw_{1})
. Since
v_{r}(uw_{1})=v_{r}(uw_{2})
, we have that

\{w\in\Sigma_{r}^{\omega}\ :\ v_{r}(w)=y\}=\{uw_{1},uw_{2}\}.

Thus
y\notin X
. Together with (
1
), this contradicts
J\subseteq X
.

Recall that
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is the coordinate project onto the first coordinate.

Corollary 11
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is strongly connected, weak, deterministic and
\Sigma=\Sigma_{r}^{2}
. If the interior of
\pi(V_{r}(\mathcal{A}))
is non-empty, then
\pi(V_{r}(\mathcal{A}))=[0,1]
.

Proof 4.3
.

Assume that the interior of
\pi(V_{r}(\mathcal{A}))
is non-empty.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is weak and strongly connected and
L(\mathcal{A})
is non-empty, all states in
\mathcal{A}
are final.
Let

U:=\{u_{1}\in\Sigma_{r}^{\omega}\ :\ \exists u_{2}\in\Sigma_{r}^{\omega}\ u_{1}\times u_{2}\in L(\mathcal{A})\}.

Note that
v_{r}(U)=\pi(V_{r}(\mathcal{A}))
. It is easy to see that there is a strongly connected Büchi automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
over
\Sigma_{r}
such that
L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=U
. Since all states of
\mathcal{A}
are final, we can choose
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
such that all states of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are final as well. Because of this, we can use the usual powerset construction to obtain a strongly connected
deterministic
Büchi automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
over
\Sigma_{r}
such that
L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
. The corollary now follows from Lemma
10
.

Corollary 12
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is strongly connected, weak, deterministic and
\Sigma=\Sigma_{r}^{2}
. Let
p,p^{\prime}\in Q
and let
{(K_{q})}_{q\in Q}
be the attractor of the GDFIS
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
\pi(K_{p})=[0,1]
if and only if
\pi(K_{p^{\prime}})=[0,1]
.

Proof 4.4
.

Set
\mathcal{A}_{p}:=(Q,\Sigma_{r}^{2},\Delta,\{p\},F)
and set
\mathcal{A}_{p^{\prime}}:=(Q,\Sigma_{r}^{2},\Delta,\{p^{\prime}\},F)
. Since the GDFIS
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
does not dependent on the initial states of
\mathcal{A}
, we obtain from Fact
2.5
that

V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{p})=K_{p}\hbox{ and }V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{p^{\prime}})=K_{p^{\prime}}.

By Corollary
11
it is left to show that the interior of
\pi(K_{p})
is non-empty if and only if the interior of
\pi(K_{p^{\prime}})
is non-empty.
Suppose that
\pi(K_{p^{\prime}})
has non-empty interior. Since
\mathcal{A}
is strongly connected, there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that there is a path
(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{n})
of length
n
from
p
to
p^{\prime}
. Thus by Fact
4

(S_{\delta_{1}}\circ\dots\circ S_{\delta_{n}})(K_{p^{\prime}})\subseteq K_{p}.

(2)

It is easy to see that since the interior
\pi(K_{p^{\prime}})
is non-empty, so is the interior of
\pi\big((S_{\delta_{1}}\circ\dots\circ S_{\delta_{n}})(K_{p^{\prime}})\big)
. By (
2
) the interior of
\pi(K_{p})
is non-empty.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
8

Throughout this subsection, fix an
r
-regular continuous

-accepted by a strongly connected deterministic Büchi automaton
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma_{r},\Delta,\{p\},F)
. As
\mathcal{A}

\Gamma(f)

\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}=(V,E,X,S)

\mathcal{A}
. Let
{(K_{q})}_{q\in Q}

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}

.

Recall that for

, we denote by

n
from
q
to

. Since
{(K_{q})}_{q\in Q}

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}

\Gamma(f)=K_{p}=\bigcup_{q\in Q}\bigcup_{(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})\in W_{p,q}(n)}(S_{\delta_{1}}\circ\ldots\circ S_{\delta_{n}})(K_{q}).

(3)

For
q,q^{\prime}\in Q
,
each path
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})
of length

from

\xi_{\sigma}:X_{q^{\prime}}\to X_{q}

Then

\xi_{\sigma}(x)=\frac{x+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}r^{n-i}}{r^{n}}

With this new notation, equation (

\Gamma(f)=K_{p}=\bigcup_{q\in Q}\bigcup_{\sigma\in P_{{p},q}(n)}\xi_{\sigma}(K_{q}).

(4)

Lemma 13
.

Let

, and let

\mathcal{H}^{1}\big(\xi_{\sigma}(K_{q})\cap\xi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(K_{q^{\prime}})\big)=0.

.

The main difficulty is to show that the overlap of the boundaries of the two sets has zero measure. Set

\xi_{\sigma}(x)=\frac{x+a}{r^{n}}\ \hbox{ and }\ \xi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(x)=\frac{x+b}{r^{n}}.

Observe that
\max\{a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\}<r^{n}

\xi_{\sigma}(K_{q})\subseteq\xi_{\sigma}\left({[0,1]}^{2}\right)=\left[\frac{a_{1}}{r^{n}},\frac{a_{1}+1}{r^{n}}\right]\times\left[\frac{a_{2}}{r^{n}},\frac{a_{2}+1}{r^{n}}\right]

\xi_{\sigma}\neq\xi_{\sigma^{\prime}}

a_{i}\neq b_{i}

Since
\xi_{\sigma}(K_{q})\cap\xi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(K_{q^{\prime}})\subseteq\Gamma(f)
by (

f
is constant and equal to
\frac{a_{2}+1}{r^{n}}
on the interval
J
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is strongly connected, there is
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and

such that

\xi_{\sigma\nu}({[0,1]}^{2})\subseteq J\times\left[\frac{a_{2}}{r^{n}},\frac{a_{2}+1}{r^{n}}\right].

(5)

Set
c:=(c_{1},c_{2}):=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\nu_{i}r^{\ell-i}
. Note that

. Let

be the index of the element in the acceptance run of
(x,f(x))
at which
q
is visited for the
i^{th}
time; i.e.,
\beta_{i}
is the
i
-th element of the set

\{k\ :\ s_{k}=q\}.

Since
q
is visited infinitely many times in the acceptance run of
(x,f(x))
,
{\{\beta_{i}\}}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is cofinal in
\mathbb{N}
.
We write

x|_{\beta_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\beta_{i}}w_{1,j}r^{-j}\quad\text{and}\quad f(x)|_{\beta_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\beta_{i}}w_{2,j}r^{-j}.

Now let
Q^{\prime}
be a sink of
\mathcal{A}
that can be reached from
q
and let
q^{\prime}
be a state in
Q^{\prime}
such that
\pi(V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{Q^{\prime},\{q^{\prime}\}}))=[0,1]
. Let
g:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be the affine function associated with
Q^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
, and let
\alpha\in S
be such that
g(y)=g(0)+\alpha y
for all
y\in[0,1]
. Take
u,v\in{(\Sigma_{r}^{2})}^{k}
with
u\times v\in P_{q,q^{\prime}}(k)
. Set

c:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{i}r^{-i}\quad\text{and}\quad d:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}r^{-i}.

To compute
f^{\prime}(x)
we declare

H(\beta_{i},y):=\frac{f(x)-\big(f(x)|_{\beta_{i}}+r^{-\beta_{i}}d+r^{-\beta_{i}-k}(g(0)+\alpha y)\big)}{x-(x|_{\beta_{i}}+r^{-\beta_{i}}c+r^{-\beta_{i}-k}y)},

where
y\in\mathbb{R}
. By our choice of
u,v
and
\alpha
, we have that for every
y\in[0,1]

f(x|_{\beta_{i}}+r^{-\beta_{i}}c+r^{-\beta_{i}-k}y)=f(x)|_{\beta_{i}}+r^{-\beta_{i}}d+r^{-\beta_{i}-k}(g(0)+\alpha y).

To further simplify notation we define

\displaystyle h_{1}(\beta_{i}):

\displaystyle=f(x)-\big(f(x)|_{\beta_{i}}+r^{-\beta_{i}}d+r^{-\beta_{i}-k}g(0)\big),

\displaystyle h_{2}(\beta_{i}):

\displaystyle=x-(x|_{\beta_{i}}+r^{-\beta_{i}}c).

Thus

H(\beta_{i},y)=\frac{h_{1}(\beta_{i})-r^{-\beta_{i}-k}\alpha y}{h_{2}(\beta_{i})-r^{-\beta_{i}-k}y}=\frac{r^{\beta_{i}}h_{1}(\beta_{i})-r^{-k}\alpha y}{r^{\beta_{i}}h_{2}(\beta_{i})-r^{-k}y}.

Note here we have

-r^{-\beta_{i}+1}\leq h_{1}(\beta_{i})\leq r^{-\beta_{i}+1}\quad\text{and}\quad-r^{-\beta_{i}+1}\leq h_{2}(\beta_{i})\leq r^{-\beta_{i}+1}.

Hence
|r^{\beta_{i}}h_{1}(\beta_{i})|
and
|r^{\beta_{i}}h_{2}(\beta_{i})|
are both bounded above by
r
.
Applying compactness we pick a subsequence
{\{\beta_{i_{\ell}}\}}_{\ell\geq 0}
of
{\{\beta_{i}\}}_{i\geq 0}
such that the sequences
\big(r^{\beta_{i_{\ell}}}h_{1}(\beta_{i_{\ell}})\big)
and
\big(r^{\beta_{i_{\ell}}}h_{2}(\beta_{i_{\ell}})\big)
have limits in
\mathbb{R}
, which we denote by
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
, respectively.
As
f
is differentiable, we have

f^{\prime}(x)=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}H(\beta_{i_{\ell}},y)=\frac{h_{1}-r^{-k}\alpha y}{h_{2}-r^{-k}y}.

Thus the equation

f^{\prime}(x)h_{2}-h_{1}=r^{-k}y(f^{\prime}(x)-\alpha)

holds for infinitely many
y\in\mathbb{R}
. This can only happen if
\alpha=f^{\prime}(x)
, which contradicts our choice of
x
.

In order to prove that checking the differentiability of regular functions is in
\operatorname{PSPACE}
, we now recall an elementary fact from real analysis.

Lemma 21
.

Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous.
Then
f
is affine if and only if

f\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)=\frac{f(x)+f(y)}{2}\quad\text{for all }0\leq x,y\leq 1.

Corollary 22
.

Given an Büchi automaton
\mathcal{A}
that recognizes an
r
-regular function
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
, the problem of checking whether
f
is differentiable, is in
\operatorname{PSPACE}
.

Proof 6.2
.

Let
f_{1}:{[0,1]}^{2}\to[0,1]
be the function that maps
(x,y)
to
f\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)
, and let
f_{2}:{[0,1]}^{2}\to[0,1]
be the function that maps
(x,y)
to
\frac{f(x)+f(y)}{2}
. It is easy to see that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are both
r
-regular. By applying
[
CSV13
, Theorem 4 & 5]
we can construct in polynomial time automata
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
that
r
-recognize
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
. Now let
\mathcal{A}_{0}
be a Büchi automaton that recognizes

\{w_{1}\times w_{2}\in{(\Sigma_{r}^{2})}^{\omega}\ :\ \exists w_{3}\in\Sigma_{r}^{\omega}\ w_{1}\times w_{2}\times w_{3}\in L(\mathcal{A}_{1})\cap L(\mathcal{A}_{2})\}.

Again, it is not hard to see that such an automaton can be constructed in polynomial time from
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
.
Recall that the universality problem for Büchi automata is
\operatorname{PSPACE}
-complete by Sistla, Vardi and Wolper
[
SVW87
]
. Checking differentiability of
f
is equivalent to checking whether
\mathcal{A}_{0}
accepts
{(\Sigma_{r}^{2})}^{\omega}
, and hence in
\operatorname{PSPACE}
.

7.
An application to model theory

A study of continuous functions definable in expansions of the ordered additive group
(\mathbb{R},<,+,0)
of real numbers was initiated in
[
HW17
]
. By Fact
2.6
a continuous
r
-regular function is just a function definable (without parameters) in the expansion
\mathscr{T}_{r}
. Thus this enterprise is a vast generalization of the study of continuous regular functions. In this section, we will briefly recall earlier results from this inquiry and explain how the results in this paper give partial answers to some wide-open questions in this area.

We first fix some notation. Throughout,
\mathscr{R}
is an expansion of
(\mathbb{R},<,+,0)
, and “definable” without modification means “
\mathscr{R}
-definable, possibly with parameters from
\mathbb{R}
”. A
dense
\omega
-order
is a definable subset of
\mathbb{R}
that is dense in some nonempty open interval and admits a definable ordering with order type
\omega
.
We say that
\mathscr{R}
is
type A
if it does not admit a dense
\omega
-order,
type C
if it defines all bounded Borel subsets of all
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and
type B
if it is neither type A nor type C. It is easy to see that an expansion cannot be both type A and type C, and thus each expansion
\mathscr{R}
of
(\mathbb{R},<,+,0)
has to be either type A, type B or type C.

Type A is a generalization of o-minimality and singles out structures whose definable sets are geometric-topologically tame (see
[
vdD98
]
for an account of o-minimal expansions). Expansions of type C have undecidable theories and satisfy no logical tameness whatsoever. Type B expansions live in between these two extremes. Indeed, it is an easy exercise to check that
\mathscr{T}_{r}
is type B. The set of all
x
in
[0,1]
with a finite base-
r
expansion is definable in
\mathscr{T}_{r}
and admits a definable order with order type
\omega
(see
[
BH17
, Section 3]
).
We regard
\mathscr{T}_{r}
as the prototype of a type B expansion. As seen in
[
HW17
]
many interesting properties of
\mathscr{T}_{r}
generalize to arbitrary type B expansions. Therefore the study of definable sets and definable function in type B expansions is a suitable generalization of the study of
r
-recognizable sets and
r
-
regular functions.

Question
7
is one of the main motivating questions in this enterprise. We say
\mathscr{R}
is of
field-type
if there is an interval
I
, definable functions
\oplus,\otimes:I^{2}\to I
, and
0_{I},1_{I}\in I
such that
(I,<,\oplus,\otimes,0_{I},1_{I})
is isomorphic to the ordered field
(\mathbb{R},<,+,\cdot,0,1)
of real numbers.

{qu}

Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and definable. If
f
is nowhere locally affine, must
\mathscr{R}
be of field-type?

This question essentially asks whether one can recover an ordered field from a nowhere locally affine continuous function over the real additive group.
Observe that any type C expansion is trivially of field-type. Furthermore, by
[
HW17
, Theorem B]
a type A expansions that defines a nowhere locally affine function is also of field-type. Since type B expansions are not of field-type by
[
HW17
, Fact 1.2]
, Question
7
is equivalent to the following question about type B structures.

{qu}

Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and definable. If
\mathscr{R}
is type B, must
f
be locally affine on a dense open subset of
[0,1]
?

Question
7
(and hence Question
7
) is one of the most important questions on definability in first-order expansions of
(\mathbb{R},<,+,0)
. We give a positive answer to an interesting special case of Question
7
.

Theorem 23
.

Every
\mathscr{T}_{r}
-definable continuous function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is locally affine on a dense open subset of
[0,1]
.

Observe that Theorem
23
follows easily from Theorem A and Fact
2.6
when
f
is definable without parameters. We just need to explain how to handle the case when
f
is defined using additional parameters. Conversely, by Fact
2.6
we can deduce from Theorem
23
that any continuous function
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
whose graph is
r
-recognized by an Büchi automaton with advice (i.e., by an automaton that can access a fixed infinite advice string) is locally affine away from a nowhere dense subset of
[0,1]
.

Theorem
23
requires the following lemma. Model-theorists might recognize it as a weak form of definable choice.

Lemma 24
.

Every nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
that is
\mathscr{T}_{r}
-definable without parameters contains an element that is definable without parameters.

Proof 7.1
.

Recall Fact
1
: a Büchi automaton that accepts an infinite word accepts an infinite periodic word.
It follows from Fact
2.6
that any subset of
{[0,1]}^{n}
that is definable without parameters in
\mathscr{T}_{r}
has an element with rational coordinates.
Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be definable without parameters in
\mathscr{T}_{r}
.
Fix
q\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
such that
X+q
intersects
{[0,1]}^{n}
.
Then
(X+q)\cap{[0,1]}^{n}
is
\mathscr{T}_{r}
-definable without parameters and thus contains a point
p
with rational coordinates.
So
p-q\in X
has rational coordinates and is thus definable without parameters.

Proof 7.2

(Proof of Theorem
23
)
.

Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and
\mathscr{T}_{r}
-definable. After rescaling by a rational factor if necessary, we may assume that
f
takes values in
[0,1]
.
If
f
is definable without parameters, then
f
is somewhere locally affine. Towards a contradiction, we now suppose
f
is nowhere locally affine. To reach a contradiction, we will construct a parameter-free
\mathscr{T}_{r}
-definable function
[0,1]\to[0,1]
that is continuous and nowhere locally affine.

Suppose
\varphi(x,y;z)
is a formula and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that
\mathscr{T}_{r}\models\varphi(a,b;c)
if and only if
0\leq a,b\leq 1
and
f(a)=b
.
Let
X
be the set of
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

•

\{(a,b)\in{[0,1]}^{2}:\mathscr{T}_{r}\models\varphi(a,b;d)\}
is the graph of a continuous function
[0,1]\to[0,1]
, and

•

for all non-empty open subintervals
J
of
[0,1]
there are
x,y\in J
and
0\leq x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\leq 1
such that

\mathscr{T}_{r}\models\varphi(x,y;d),\quad\mathscr{T}_{r}\models\varphi(x^{\prime},y^{\prime};d),\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{T}_{r}\nvDash\varphi\left(\frac{x+y}{2},\frac{x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}}{2};d\right).

Lemma
21
implies
X
is the set of
d
such that
\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\mathscr{T}_{r}\models\varphi(a,b;d)\}
is the graph of a continuous nowhere locally affine function
[0,1]\to[0,1]
.
Note that
X
is
\mathscr{T}_{r}
-definable without parameters.
By Lemma
24
there is a parameter-free definable
c^{\prime}\in X
.
Then
\{(a,b)\in{[0,1]}^{2}:\mathscr{T}_{r}\models\varphi(a,b;c^{\prime})\}
is the graph of a continuous nowhere locally affine function
[0,1]\to[0,1]
that is definable in
\mathscr{T}_{r}
without parameters.
Contradiction.

Of course, there are more questions that can be asked about the definable continuous function in type B expansions.
For our purposes a
space filling curve
is a continuous surjection
[0,1]\to{[0,1]}^{2}
.

{qu}

Is there a type B expansion that defines a space filling curve?

If
\mathscr{R}
is type A, then there is no continuous definable surjection
{[0,1]}^{n}\to{[0,1]}^{m}
when
m>n
(see
[
FHW17
, Theorem E]
). Thus Question
7
is equivalent to the following: must
(\mathbb{R},<,+,0,f)
be type C when
f
is a space filling curve? We give a negative answer to Question
7
in a special case.

Proposition 25
.

The structure
\mathscr{T}_{r}
does not define a space filling curve.

Proof 7.3

(Sketch of proof)
.

An argument similar to that given in the proof above shows that if
\mathscr{T}_{r}
defines a space filling curve, then there is a space filling curve that is
\mathscr{T}_{r}
definable without parameters. It follows from Fact
2.3
that every function
f:[0,1]\to{[0,1]}^{2}
that is
\mathscr{T}_{r}
definable without parameters is Lipschitz. Such a function cannot be surjective, as Lipschitz maps cannot raise Hausdorff dimension.

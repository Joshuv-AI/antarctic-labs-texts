SMOOTHING AND GLOBAL ATTRACTORS FOR THE HIROTA-SATSUMA SYSTEM ON THE TORUS Thanks: The authors were partially supported by the TÜBİTAK grant 118F152 and the Boğaziçi University Research Fund grant BAP-14081.
E. Başakoğlu, T. B. Gürel

Abstract
. We consider the Hirota-Satsuma system, a coupled KdV-type system, with periodic boundary conditions. The first part of the paper concerns with the smoothing estimates for the system. More precisely, it is shown that, for initial data in a Sobolev space, the difference of the nonlinear and linear evolutions lies in a smoother space. The smoothing gain we obtain depends very much on the arithmetic nature of the coupling parameter a a which determines the structure of the resonant sets in the estimates. In the second part, we address the forced and damped Hirota-Satsuma system and obtain counterpart smoothing estimates. As a consequence of these estimates, we prove the existence and smoothness of a global attractor in the energy space.

1.
Introduction

The Hirota-Satsuma system is a system of coupled KdV equations, introduced by Hirota and Satsuma in
1981
,
[
16
]
. In this paper, we consider the Hirota-Satsuma system with periodic boundary conditions

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+au_{xxx}+3a(u^{2})_{x}+\beta(v^{2})_{x}=0,\hskip 14.22636ptx\in\mathbb{T}\\
v_{t}+v_{xxx}+3uv_{x}=0,\\
(u,v)\rvert_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0})\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\end{cases}

where
a\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})=\{f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}):\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(x)\,\text{d}x=0\}
. Here the choice of the parameter
a
is related to the resonance equations coming from after applying the normal form transformation to the system (
1
). The system (
1
) is a generalization of the KdV equation (when
v=0
) and describes the interplay of two long waves evolving with different dispersion relations. Note that the mean zero condition on
v
cannot be applicable since the system (
1
) does not preserve the mean value of
v
, and that the momentum conservation holds for
u
only:

\displaystyle\int u(x,t)\,\text{d}x=\int u_{0}(x)\,\text{d}x.

The system (
1
) also satisfies the following conservation laws
[
16
]
:

(2)

\begin{split}&E_{1}(u,v)=\int u^{2}-\frac{2\beta}{3}v^{2}\,\text{d}x,\hskip 14.22636pt\\
&E_{2}(u,v)=\int(1-a)u_{x}^{2}-2\beta v_{x}^{2}-2(1-a)u^{3}+2\beta uv^{2}\,\text{d}x.\end{split}

As a consequence of these conserved energies, it turns out that the energy space for the system is
H^{1}\times H^{1}
. No other conserved quantities seem to exist for (
1
) that holds for any
a
and
\beta
; nevertheless for
a=-\frac{1}{2}
, the system is known to be completely integrable,
[
16
,
26
]
. Before discussing the literature of the coupled KdV type systems, it makes sense to review more recent well-posedness results of the KdV equation

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{xxx}+uu_{x}=0\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{s}(K)\,\,\text{for}\,\,K=\mathbb{R}\,\,\text{or}\,\,\mathbb{T}.\end{cases}

Introducing Fourier restriction spaces Bourgain extended the previous local well-posedness results of the KdV equation to the
L^{2}
level on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{T}
,
[
5
]
. Later in
[
20
]
, Kenig, Ponce and Vega proved the local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>-\frac{3}{4}
and in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s>-\frac{1}{2}
. The local well-posedness in
H^{-\frac{3}{4}}(\mathbb{R})
was established by Christ-Colliander-Tao in
[
6
]
. In
[
7
]
, Colliander-Keel-Staffilani-Takaoka-Tao obtained the local and global well-posedness in
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})
. The global well-posedness for
\mathbb{R}
at the endpoint
s=-\frac{3}{4}
was proved by Guo
[
14
]
. By using the integrability properties of the KdV equation, Kappeler and Topalov established the local and global well-posedness in
H^{-1}(\mathbb{T})
,
[
19
]
. Later, in
[
23
,
24
]
, Molinet showed that the KdV equation is ill-posed in
H^{s}(K)
for
K=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{T}
,
s<-1
. Finally, the global well-posedness in
H^{-1}(\mathbb{R})
has recently been obtained by Killip and Visan
[
22
]
, and the study of the well-posedness of (
3
) has been brought to a satisfactory conclusion.

The well-posedness theory of the Hirota-Satsuma system began with the work of He
[
15
]
, with the assumptions
0<a<1
and
\beta<0
on the coefficients, He obtained the existence and uniqueness of the global solutions in
L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{3}(K)\times H^{3}(K))
for
K=\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R}
. In the real case, Feng
[
12
]
improved this result to the range
s\geq 1
by considering slightly general coupled KdV-KdV system. In particular, it was shown that for
a\neq 1
and
\beta<0
, the system is locally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s\geq 1
. Also with the additional assumption
0<a<1
, the system was shown to be globally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s\geq 1
. Later, Alvarez and Carvajal pushed the local result down to
s>\frac{3}{4}
for the real case. They also showed that the system with
a\neq 0
is ill-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
for
s\in[-1,-3/4)
and
s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
. Recently, Yang and Zhang
[
31
]
have studied the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for a class of coupled KdV-KdV (cKdV) systems in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, those including Gear-Grimshaw system, Hirota-Satsuma system, the Majdo-Biello system etc. In particular, regarding the Hirota-Satsuma system, they have given critical index
s^{*}\in\{-\frac{3}{4},0,\frac{3}{4}\}
depending on the numeric value of the coefficient
a
for which the Hirota-Satsuma Cauchy problem is locally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
when
s>s^{*}
. As regards to the periodic case, Angulo
[
2
]
showed that for
a\neq 0,\,1
the Hirota-Satsuma system is locally well-posed in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})\times\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s\geq 1
. With the additional assumptions
\beta<0
and
a<1
, Angulo further obtained the global well-posedness in the same space within the same Sobolev index range that follows from the conservation of the energy. Finally, in
[
32
]
, Yang and Zhang have recently obtained the well-posedness results of the cKdV systems on the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}
as a follow up of their corresponding work
[
31
]
. Here we shall merely summarize the well-posedness results of
[
32
]
concerning the Cauchy problem (
1
). The results depend on the arithmetic properties of the coefficients
a
and
\beta
. When
a=1
and
\beta=0
, the system (
1
) is locally well-posed in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
. In the case
a\in(-\infty,\frac{1}{4})\setminus\{0\}
, as the resonace interactions are relatively easier to control, the local well-posedness is established in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s\geq-\frac{1}{4}
; whereas in the remaining regime,
a\in[\frac{1}{4},\infty)\setminus\{1\}
, the resonances raise special difficulties in which case one needs to know how well a given number can be approximated by rational numbers (Diophantine approximation). The idea of controlling resonances via the Diophantine approximation was initially implemented by Oh
[
25
]
to the Majdo-Biello system on the torus to establish the well-posedness. Using this approach, Yang and Zhang proved the local well-posedness in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s\geq\min\{1,s_{a}+\}
with the mean zero assumption on the initial data
u_{0}
. Here
s_{a}
is defined by means of a number theoretic parameter based on the arithmetic properties of
a
. On account of the conserved energies (
2
) for the system (
1
), when
\frac{1}{4}\leq a<1
and
\beta<0
, the local well-posedness can be upgraded to global well-posedness for
s\geq 1
. Also when
a\in(-\infty,\frac{1}{4})\setminus\{0\}
and
\beta<0
, the direct application of the conservation of
E_{1}(u,v)
and the corresponding local result yield the global well-posedness for
s\geq 0
.

In the first part of the paper, we study the smoothing property of the Hirota-Satsuma system, in other words, we prove that the difference between the nonlinear evolution and the linear evolution lies in a more regular space than the inital data under consideration. The proof is based on the method of normal forms through differentiation by parts introduced by Babin-Ilyin-Titi
[
3
]
and the Bourgain’s Fourier restricted norm method,
[
5
]
. The idea of using combination of these methods in proving nonlinear smoothing effect on a bounded domain was first used by Erdoğan and Tzirakis for the KdV equation
[
9
]
and the Zakharov system
[
11
]
. The result for the KdV equation is somewhat surprising since the KdV equation is known to have no smoothing estimate on the real line. Recently, Compaan
[
8
]
studied the smoothing properties of the Majdo-Biello system on the torus and proved the existence of global attractors in the sense of arguments in
[
9
,
11
]
. Here we continue the program initiated by these papers. In our proof, via the normal form reduction, the derivatives in the nonlinearities can be eliminated, and in return for this, the orders of the nonlinearities increase (from quadratic to cubic) and many resonant terms come into play based on the arithmetic properties of the coupling parameter
a
. In order to control the new trilinear nonlinearities we rely on the
X^{s,b}
estimates.

In the second part, we concentrate on the description of long time dynamics of the forced and weakly damped system:

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+au_{xxx}+\gamma_{1}u+3a(u^{2})_{x}+\beta(v^{2})_{x}=f\\
v_{t}+v_{xxx}+\gamma_{2}v+3uv_{x}=g\\
(u,v)\rvert_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0})\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{T})\end{cases}

where the damping coefficients
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
are positive,
\beta<0
,
f,\,g
are time independent, and
f\in H^{1}
is mean zero,
g\in H^{1}
. To simplify the calculations, we will take
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
; the general case follows from minor modifications in the computations. The smoothing estimates obtained in the first part will play an essential role in demonstrating that the system (
4
) possesses a global attractor, also the result here answers the regularity of the attractor above the energy level. Using the method of
[
8
,
10
,
11
]
, roughly, the idea is to write the solution in terms of linear and nonlinear parts and then to implement the smoothing estimates to the nonlinear part.

Notation

We write

to denote that there is some constant

A\leq CB
, and

is defined accordingly. Moreover,

means that

The Fourier sequence of a

is defined as

\displaystyle u_{k}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(x)e^{-ikx}\,\text{d}x,\hskip 14.22636ptk\in\mathbb{Z}.

By introducing the notation

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}=\left\lVert\langle k\rangle^{s}\widehat{u}(k)\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}_{k}}.

Note that for a mean zero function

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}\approx\left\lVert|k|^{s}\widehat{u}(k)\right\rVert_{\ell_{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}_{a}}=\left\lVert\langle k\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-ak^{3}\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}(k,\tau)\right\rVert_{\ell_{k}^{2}L^{2}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}_{1}}=\left\lVert\langle k\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-k^{3}\rangle^{b}\widehat{v}(k,\tau)\right\rVert_{\ell_{k}^{2}L^{2}_{\tau}}.

The restricted norms are also defined by

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}_{a,\delta}}=\inf_{\tilde{u}=u\,\text{on}\,[-\delta,\delta]}\left\lVert\tilde{u}\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}_{a}}

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}_{1,\delta}}=\inf_{\tilde{v}=v\,\text{on}\,[-\delta,\delta]}\left\lVert\tilde{v}\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}}.

2.
Statement of Results

2.1.
Preliminaries

In order to state well-posedness results and hence to study the smoothing properties of the system (
1
) on the torus, we require special parameters related to given numbers, called irrationality exponent, which might be used to learn how close given numbers can be approximated by rational numbers. Irrationality exponent of a real number arises in the study of Diophantine approximation theory. In our discussion, we utilize these quantities in controlling resonances.

Definition 2.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

A number
r\in\mathbb{R}
is said to be approximable with power
\mu
, if the inequality

\displaystyle 0<\Big|r-\frac{n}{k}\Big|<\frac{1}{|k|^{\mu}}

holds for infinitely many
(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{*}
, and

(5)

\displaystyle\mu(r)=\sup\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}:r\,\text{is approximable with power}\,\mu\}

is called the irrationality exponent of
r
.

We now review some properties of the irrationality exponent. Irrationality exponent maps the set of real numbers onto the set
\{1\}\cup[2,\infty]
, see
[
17
,
18
]
. In particular, for
r\in\mathbb{Q}
,
\mu(r)=1
whereas for irrational number
r
we have
\mu(r)\geq 2
. By the Thue-Siegel-Roth theorem
[
27
,
28
,
30
]
, for an irrational algebraic number
r
,
\mu(r)=2
, also by the Khintchine theorem
[
21
]
, for almost every
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mu(r)=2
. The local theory for the Cauchy problem (
1
) is based on the critical index
s_{r}
for
r\geq\frac{1}{4}
defined by

\displaystyle s_{r}=\begin{cases}1\hskip 42.67912pt\text{if}\,\,\mu(\rho_{r})=1\,\,\text{or}\,\,\mu(\rho_{r})\geq 3\\
\frac{\mu(\rho_{r})-1}{2}\hskip 15.6491pt\text{if}\,\,2\leq\mu(\rho_{r})<3\end{cases}

where
\rho_{r}=\sqrt{12r-3}
. In connection with the well-posedness of the system (
1
), we state some of the results of
[
32
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
32
]
)
.

For
a\in[\frac{1}{4},\infty)\setminus\{1\}
and
s\geq\min\{1,s_{a}+\}
, the Hirota-Satsuma system (
1
) is locally well-posed in
\dot{H}^{s}\times H^{s}
. In particular, given any initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\dot{H}^{s}\times H^{s}
there exists
\delta\approx(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}+\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}
and a unique solution

\displaystyle(u,v)\in C([-\delta,\delta];H^{s}\times H^{s})

satisfying
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{s,1/2}_{a,\delta}}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X^{s,1/2}_{1,\delta}}\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}+\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
32
]
)
.

Let
\beta<0
and
a\in[\frac{1}{4},1)
. Then for
s\geq 1
, the Hirota-Satsuma initial value problem is globally well-posed in
\dot{H}^{s}\times H^{s}
.

2.2.
Smoothing Estimates for the Hirota-Satsuma System

Applying normal form transformation to the system (
1
) leads to the resonance equations (for
k_{1}+k_{2}=k
)

\displaystyle ak^{3}-k_{1}^{3}-k_{2}^{3}=-3k(k_{1}-r_{1}k)(k_{1}-r_{2}k)\,\,\text{and}\,\,k^{3}-ak_{1}^{3}-k_{2}^{3}=(1-a)k_{1}(k_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}k)(k_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{2}k)

where

(6)

\displaystyle r_{1}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{12a-3}}{6},\,\,\,r_{2}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{12a-3}}{6}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\widetilde{r}_{1}=1/r_{1},\,\,\,\widetilde{r}_{2}=1/r_{2}.

Note that
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
are the roots of the equation
3x^{2}-3x+(1-a)
, which immediately implies that
\widetilde{r}_{1}
,
\widetilde{r}_{2}
are the roots of the equation
(1-a)x^{2}-3x+3
. Therefore,
r_{j}
and
\widetilde{r}_{j}
are algebraic only when
a\in\mathbb{Q}
. By (
6
), we notice that
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if
a\geq\frac{1}{4}
, and that
\widetilde{r}_{1},\widetilde{r}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if
a\in[\frac{1}{4},1)\cup(1,\infty)
. In this paper, we consider the problem (
1
) for
a\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
, also the problem for interval
(1,\infty)
can be handled in a similar vein. As performing smoothing estimates, we will be dealing with many expressions such as
ak^{3}-k_{1}^{3}-k_{2}^{3}
and
k^{3}-ak_{1}^{3}-k_{2}^{3}
that appear in the denominators. Controlling such expressions in the case they get close to zero relies on the following lemma:

Lemma 2.4

(
[
32
]
)
.

If
r\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
with
\mu(r)<\infty
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
K=K(r,\epsilon)>0
such that the inequality

(7)

\displaystyle\Big|r-\frac{n}{k}\Big|\geq\frac{K}{|k|^{\mu(r)+\epsilon}}

holds for any
(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{*}
.

Note that when
r_{j}
,
\widetilde{r}_{j}
, as introduced in (
6
), are rational numbers, by the Proposition
3.1
below, we will still be able to use the inequality (
7
) for these numbers with
\mu(r_{j})=\mu(\widetilde{r}_{j})=1
. Next lemma collects some invariance properties of the irrationality exponent, which is proved in
[
32
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

The irrationality exponent defined by (
5
) satisfies

(1)

For any
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\mathbb{Q}
,
\mu(q+r)=\mu(r)
,

(2)

For any
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}
,
\mu(qr)=\mu(r)
,

(3)

For any
r\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,
\mu(\frac{1}{r})=\mu(r)
.

Using the lemma above we may write

(8)

\displaystyle\mu(\widetilde{r}_{2})=\mu(\widetilde{r}_{1})=\mu(r_{1})=\mu(r_{2})=\mu(\sqrt{12a-3})=:\mu(\rho_{a}).

With the notations introduced above we state our smoothing result as follows:

Theorem 2.6
.

Fix
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
a\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
. Consider the solution of (
1
) with initial data
(u(0,x),v(0,x))=(u_{0}(x),v_{0}(x))\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
. Then for any
s_{1}-s<\min\{1,s-\frac{1}{2},s+2-\mu(\rho_{a}),2s+1-\mu(\rho_{a})\}
,
we have

(9)

\displaystyle u(t,x)-e^{-ta\partial_{x}^{3}}u_{0}\in C_{t}^{0}H^{s_{1}}_{x},

(10)

\displaystyle v(t,x)-e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}v_{0}\in C_{t}^{0}H^{s_{1}}_{x}.

For almost every
a
, (
9
) and (
10
) hold with
s_{1}-s<\min\{1,s-\frac{1}{2}\}
. When
a=\frac{3p(p-q)+q^{2}}{q^{2}}
,
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
with
\frac{q}{2}<p<q
, fix
s>1
, then the smoothing statements (
9
) and (
10
) are valid for
s_{1}-s\leq\min\{1-,s-1\}
instead.
Assume that we have a growth bound
\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{H^{s}}+\left\lVert v(t)\right\rVert_{H^{s}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{\beta(s)}
,
for some
\beta(s)>0
. Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert u(t)-e^{-ta\partial_{x}^{3}}u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s_{1}}}+\left\lVert v(t)-e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}v_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s_{1}}}\leq C(a,s,s_{1},\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}},\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}})\langle t\rangle^{1+\frac{9}{2}\beta(s)}.

Remark 2.7
.

In the case the coefficients
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
in (
6
) are rational numbers, which is the case when
a
is a rational of the form
\frac{3p(p-q)+q^{2}}{q^{2}}
as stated in the above theorem, we have to control some additional terms due to the resonances, in which case smoothing is attained solely for
s>1
. On the other side, if the coefficients
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
are irrational algebraic numbers, which is the case when

is a rational number such that

with
\frac{q}{2}<p<q
, then by (
8
),
\mu(\rho_{a})=2
in Theorem

yields the best possible smoothing. Indeed, the best regularity gain given by the Theorem

is reached for almost every
a\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
. As a consequence, with regards to smoothing, the above discussion shows how the regularity level is unstable under a slight perturbation of

(\frac{1}{4},1)
.

Using smoothing estimates one can obtain growth bounds for higher order Sobolev norms in the lack of complete integrability. As a corollory of the smoothing theorem above, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 2.8
.

For any
s\geq 1
and almost every
a\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
for which
\mu(\rho_{a})=2
, the global solution of (
1
) with
\dot{H}^{s}\times H^{s}
data satisfies the growth bound

\displaystyle\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{H^{s}}+\left\lVert v(t)\right\rVert_{H^{s}}\leq C_{0}(1+|t|)^{C_{1}}

where
C_{0}
depends on
a,s,\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}},\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}
and
C_{1}
depends on
s
.

Proof.

Due to the conserved energies,
H^{1}
norms of
u
and
v
are bounded for all times. The idea is to use the result of Theorem
2.6
repeatedly to obtain the growth bound for the Sobolev norms above the energy level. To use this, suppose that the claim of the corollory holds for
s\geq 1
. Hence for
s_{1}\in(s,s+\min\{1,s-\frac{1}{2}\})
, Theorem
2.6
leads to

\displaystyle\left\lVert u(t)-e^{-at\partial_{x}^{3}}u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s_{1}}}+\left\lVert v(t)-e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}v_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s_{1}}}\leq C(1+|t|)^{1+\frac{9}{2}\beta(s)},

2.3.
Existence of a Global Attractor for the Hirota-Satsuma System

The essential tool in establishing the existence of a global atractor for the dissipative Hirota-Satsuma system (
4
) in this context will be the smoothing estimates which are to be discussed later. The existence of global attractors makes sense only for the globally well-posed problems, in this regard we note that the initial value problem (
4
) is locally and globally well-posed in

. The local well-posedness follows by using the estimates of
[
32
]
and the global well-posedness follows from the energy estimate of Lemma
5.1
which also implies the existence of an absorbing ball. To set the stage for the description of the problem, let
U(t)
be the semigroup operator mapping data to solution in the phase space.

Definition 2.9

(
[
29
]
)
.

We say that a compact subset
\mathcal{A}
of the phase space

is a global attractor for the semigroup

U(t)\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}
for all

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}d(U(t)g,\mathcal{A})=0,\,\,\forall g\in H.

Next we define an absorbing set into which all solutions enter eventually.

Definition 2.10

(
[
29
]
)
.

A bounded subset
\mathcal{B}
of a phase space

is called an absorbing set if for any bounded
\mathcal{S}\subset H
, there exists

such that

for all

.

Note that the existence of a global attractor for a semigroup implies the existence of an absorbing set whereas the converse holds only when a semigroup is asymptotically compact:

Theorem 2.11

(
[
29
]
)
.

Suppose that

is a metric space and

is a continuous semigroup defined for all

. Furthermore suppose that there is an absorbing set

. If the semigroup

is asymptotically compact, that is, for every bounded sequence
\{x_{k}\}
in

and every sequence of times

is relatively compact in

Therefore, in the presence of an absorbing set the proof of existence of a global attractor reduces to proving asymptotic compactness of the evolution operator. In our discussion, the proof of the asymptotic compactness of the flow depends very much on the smoothing estimate for the forced and weakly damped system that we establish later. Thus we have the following result:

n\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\sum}\frac{\langle k_{1}\rangle^{-2s-2}\langle k_{1}-k\rangle^{-2s+1}}{\langle k^{3}-ak_{1}^{3}-a(n-k_{1})^{3}-(k-n)^{3}\rangle^{2-2b}}\\
\lesssim\sup_{k}\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}-4}\sum\limits_{k_{1}\neq 0}\langle k_{1}\rangle^{-2s}\langle k_{1}-k\rangle^{-2s+1}\lesssim\sup_{k}\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}-2s-3}\lesssim 1

whenever
s_{1}-s\leq\frac{3}{2}
. In the same way, the boundedness of
\text{I}_{2}
can be shown provided that
s_{1}-s\leq 1
.

Case C.2.

|k_{1}|<\delta|k|

In this case,
|k-k_{1}|\lesssim|k|
implies the bound

\text{I}_{1}+\text{I}_{2}\lesssim\sup_{k}\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}-2}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\neq 0\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\sum}\frac{\langle k_{1}\rangle^{-2s-2}\langle n-k_{1}\rangle^{-2s+1}\langle n-k\rangle^{-2s+1}}{\langle k^{3}-ak_{1}^{3}-a(n-k_{1})^{3}-(k-n)^{3}\rangle^{2-2b}}\\
+\sup_{k}\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}-1}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\neq 0\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\sum}\frac{\langle k_{1}\rangle^{-2s-2}\langle n-k_{1}\rangle^{-2s}\langle n-k\rangle^{-2s+1}}{\langle k^{3}-ak_{1}^{3}-a(n-k_{1})^{3}-(k-n)^{3}\rangle^{2-2b}}=:\text{J}_{1}+\text{J}_{2}.

Case C.2.1.

|n-k_{1}|\leq\delta|k|

Note here that
|n-k|\geq|k|-|k_{1}|-|n-k_{1}|\geq(1-2\delta)|k|
. Moreover, since
u
is mean zero, we have, for some
\eta_{j}\neq 0
satisfying
|k|^{-1}\leq|\eta_{j}|<\delta
,

, that
k_{1}=\eta_{1}k
and
n-k_{1}=\eta_{2}k
. Hence the restriction
(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})k=n\neq 0
provides us with a parameter
\eta:=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}
satisfying
|k|^{-1}\leq|\eta|<2\delta
, and yielding the following bound

|k^{3}-ak_{1}^{3}-a(n-k_{1})^{3}-(k-n)^{3}|=|\big(1-a(\eta_{1}^{3}+\eta_{2}^{3})-(1-\eta)^{3}\big)k^{3}|\\
=|k|^{3}|\eta||3(1-\eta)+\eta^{2}(1-a)+3a\eta_{1}\eta_{2}|\gtrsim|k|^{2}.

Exploiting the above estimates we arrive at

\displaystyle\text{J}_{1}\lesssim\sup_{k}\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}-2s-5+4b}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\neq 0\\
|n|\lesssim|k|\end{subarray}}{\sum}\langle k_{1}\rangle^{-2s-2}\lesssim\sup_{k}\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}-2s-4+4b}\lesssim 1

and

\displaystyle\text{J}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{k}\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}-5+4b}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\neq 0\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\sum}\langle k_{1}\rangle^{-2s-2}\langle n-k_{1}\rangle^{-2s}\langle n-k\rangle^{-2s+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{k}\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}-5+4b}\sum\limits_{k_{1}\neq 0}\langle k_{1}\rangle^{-2s-2}\langle k_{1}-k\rangle^{-2s+1}\lesssim\sup_{k}\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}-2s-4+4b}\lesssim 1

provided that
s_{1}-s\leq 2-2b
.

Case C.2.2.

|n-k|\leq\delta|k|

In this region,
|n-k_{1}|\geq|k|-|k_{1}|-|n-k|>(1-2\delta)|k|
. Then, we write
k_{1}=\eta_{1}k
for some
\eta_{1}
with
|k|^{-1}\leq|\eta_{1}|<\delta
, and

for some
\eta_{2}

. Via these

|k^{3}-ak_{1}^{3}-a(n-k_{1})^{3}-(k-n)^{3}|=|\big(1+\eta_{2}^{3}-a(\eta_{1}^{3}+(1-\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})^{3})\big)k^{3}|=|k|^{3}|1-a+\mathcal{O}(\delta)|\\
\gtrsim|k|^{3}.

As above we get

\displaystyle\text{J}_{1}+\text{J}_{2}\lesssim\sup_{k}\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}-2s-6+6b}\lesssim 1

whenever

.

5.
Existence of Global Attractor

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.12
. We consider the system

(32)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+au_{xxx}+\gamma u+3a(u^{2})_{x}+\beta(v^{2})_{x}=f\\
v_{t}+v_{xxx}+\gamma v+3uv_{x}=g\\
(u,v)\rvert_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0})\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{T}).\end{cases}

Recall that
\beta<0
. Firstly we show the existence of an absorbing set corresponding to the system (
32
). To achieve this we use conserved energies (
2
) to obtain:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(u,v)
be a solution of the system (
32
) with data
(u_{0},v_{0})
, we have the a priori estimate:

\displaystyle\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert v(t)\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq C=C(a,\beta,\gamma,\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{1}}),

for
t>0
.

Proof.

We start by noting that the constants in the following calculations are denoted by
C
,
C_{0}
, and
C_{1}
whose value may change, their dependence are to be highlighted though. To obtain the
L^{2}
bounds for
u
and
v
, we use
E_{1}(t):=E_{1}(u,v)(t)=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{2\beta}{3}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Thus

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E_{1}(t)+2\gamma E_{1}(t)=2\int uf-\frac{2\beta}{3}vg\,\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}-\frac{4\beta}{3}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2(\sqrt{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\sqrt{-\beta}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}})\sqrt{E_{1}(t)}.

Setting
E_{1}(t)=e^{-2\gamma t}F_{1}(t)
and using the above inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sqrt{F_{1}(t)}\leq e^{\gamma t}(\sqrt{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\sqrt{-\beta}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}).

Integrating this inequality from
0

to
t
and then utilizing the resulting inequality in the norms of
u
and
v
, we arrive at

\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{-2\beta}{3}}\left\lVert v(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
\leq\sqrt{2}e^{-\gamma t}\sqrt{\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{2\beta}{3}\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}+\frac{1-e^{-\gamma t}}{\gamma}(2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\sqrt{-2\beta}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}).

Regarding the bounds for the spatial derivatives of
u
and
v
, we consider
E_{2}(t):=E_{2}(u,v)(t)=(1-a)\big(\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\int u^{3}\text{d}x\big)-2\beta\big(\left\lVert v_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int uv^{2}\text{d}x\big)
. Note that

(1-a)\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\beta\left\lVert v_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=E_{2}(t)+2(1-a)\int u^{3}\,\text{d}x-2\beta\int uv^{2}\,\text{d}x\\
\leq E_{2}(t)+C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\big(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\leq E_{2}(t)+C+C\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

the constants depend on the bounds on
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
,
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
in the final inequality. By this inequality, we have

\sqrt{1-a}\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}-\frac{C}{2\sqrt{1-a}}\leq\sqrt{\Big(\sqrt{1-a}\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}-\frac{C}{2\sqrt{1-a}}\Big)^{2}-2\beta\left\lVert v_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}\\
\leq\sqrt{E_{2}(t)+C+C^{2}/4(1-a)}\lesssim\sqrt{|E_{2}(t)|}+C

and

\displaystyle\sqrt{-2b}\left\lVert v_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\Big(\sqrt{1-a}\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}-\frac{C}{2\sqrt{1-a}}\Big)^{2}-2\beta\left\lVert v_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{|E_{2}(t)|}+C.

Thus
\left\lVert u_{x}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert v_{x}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{|E_{2}(t)|}+C
. To end up the argument it suffices to show that
E_{2}
is bounded.
Using this bound and the embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
we obtain

\partial_{t}E_{2}(t)+2\gamma E_{2}(t)=2(1-a)\int f_{x}u_{x}-3fu^{2}+\gamma u^{3}\,\text{d}x-2\beta\int 2g_{x}v_{x}-fv^{2}-2guv+\gamma uv^{2}\,\text{d}x\\
\leq C_{0}(\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert v_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})+C_{1}\leq C_{0}\sqrt{|E_{2}(t)|}+C_{1}

where the constants
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
depend on the norms
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{1}}
,
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{1}}
, the constants
a
,
\beta
,
\gamma
and the bounds on
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
,
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
. Setting
E_{2}(t)=e^{-2\gamma t}F_{2}(t)
, we get that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F_{2}(t)\leq e^{\gamma t}\big(C_{0}\sqrt{|F_{2}(t)|}+C_{1}e^{\gamma t}\big),

from which we have that

\displaystyle E_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\delta t}E_{2}(0)+C_{1}\frac{1-e^{-2\gamma t}}{2\gamma}+C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\gamma(t-t^{\prime})}\sqrt{|E_{2}(t^{\prime})|}\,\text{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq|E_{2}(0)|+C_{1}+C_{0}\left\lVert\sqrt{|E_{2}|}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,t])}

for
t>0
. This shows that
E_{2}
is bounded from above because if it were the case that
t
might be the first time at which
E_{2}
assumes its largest value, say
C
, over
[0,t]
with
E_{2}(t)=C\gg|E_{2}(0)|+C_{1}+C_{0}=:\widetilde{C}
, then by the above inequality we would have
C\leq\widetilde{C}(1+\sqrt{C})
, but this is impossible for sufficiently large
C\gg 1
. Also the Sobolev embedding and the bounds on
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
,
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
suggest that
E_{2}
is bounded below.
∎

As a consequence of the Lemma
5.1
, the existence of an absorbing ball
\mathcal{B}_{0}\subset H^{1}\times H^{1}
follows. As for the verification of the asymptotic compactness of the flow, the second task is to obtain smoothing estimate as done in the non-dissipative case.

Theorem 5.2
.

Consider the solution of (
32
) with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\dot{H}^{1}\times H^{1}
. Then for any
\alpha<\min\{\frac{1}{2},3-\mu(\rho_{a})\}
, we have

\left\lVert u(t)-e^{-(a\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)t}u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(a\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)(t-r)}\rho_{2}(v,v)(r)\,\text{d}r\right\rVert_{H^{1+\alpha}}\\
+\left\lVert v(t)-e^{-(\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)t}v_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)(t-r)}\rho_{3}(u,v)(r)\,\text{d}r\right\rVert_{H^{1+\alpha}}\\
\leq C(\alpha,\gamma,\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{1}})

where
\rho_{2}
and
\rho_{3}
are as in Proposition
3.1
.

Proof.

We write the system (
32
) by the Fourier transform as follows

(33)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{k}-(iak^{3}-\gamma)u_{k}+3iak\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}u_{k_{1}}u_{k_{2}}+i\beta k\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}v_{k_{1}}v_{k_{2}}=f_{k}\\
\partial_{t}v_{k}-(ik^{3}-\gamma)v_{k}+3i\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}k_{2}u_{k_{1}}v_{k_{2}}=g_{k}.\end{cases}

Using the change of variables
y_{k}=e^{-iak^{3}t+\gamma t}u_{k}
,
z_{k}=e^{-ik^{3}t+\gamma t}v_{k}
, and
d_{k}=e^{-iak^{3}t+\gamma t}f_{k}
,
h_{k}=e^{-ik^{3}t+\gamma t}g_{k}
, the above system transforms to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y_{k}=-3iak\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}e^{-iat(k^{3}-k_{1}^{3}-k_{2}^{3})}y_{k_{1}}y_{k_{2}}-i\beta k\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}e^{-it(ak^{3}-k_{1}^{3}-k_{2}^{3})}z_{k_{1}}z_{k_{2}}+d_{k}\\
\partial_{t}z_{k}=-3i\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}k_{2}e^{-it(k^{3}-ak_{1}^{3}-k_{2}^{3})}y_{k_{1}}z_{k_{2}}+h_{k}.\end{cases}

After differentiation by parts as in Proposition
3.1
, the system (
33
) can be written in the form

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\Big[e^{-iak^{3}t+\gamma t}u_{k}\Big]+e^{-\gamma t}\partial_{t}\Big[e^{-iak^{3}t+2\gamma t}(B_{1}(u,u)_{k}+B_{2}(v,v)_{k})\Big]=\\
\hskip 85.35826pte^{-iak^{3}t+\gamma t}\big[R_{1}(u,v,v)_{k}+R_{2}(u,u,u)_{k}+R_{3}(u,v,v)_{k}+2B_{1}(u,f)_{k}\\
\hskip 227.62204pt+2B_{2}(g,v)_{k}+\rho_{1}(u,u)_{k}+\rho_{2}(v,v)_{k}+f_{k}\big]\\
\partial_{t}\Big[e^{-ik^{3}t+\gamma t}v_{k}\Big]+e^{-\gamma t}\partial_{t}\Big[e^{-ik^{3}t+2\gamma t}B_{3}(u,v)_{k}\Big]=\\
\hskip 142.26378pte^{-ik^{3}t+\gamma t}\big[R_{4}(u,u,v)_{k}+\frac{\beta}{3a}R_{4}(v,v,v)_{k}+R_{5}(u,u,v)_{k}\\
\hskip 227.62204pt+B_{3}(f,v)_{k}+B_{3}(u,g)_{k}+\rho_{3}(u,v)_{k}+g_{k}\big],\end{cases}

where
B_{j}
,
R_{j}
, and
\rho_{j}
are as in Proposition
3.1
. Integrating these equations from
0
to
t
leads to the equations

u_{k}(t)-e^{iak^{3}t-\gamma t}u_{k}(0)=-B_{1}(u,u)_{k}-B_{2}(v,v)_{k}+e^{iak^{3}t-\gamma t}\big[B_{1}(u_{0},u_{0})_{k}+B_{2}(v_{0},v_{0})_{k}\big]\\
+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(iak^{3}-\gamma)(t-s)}\big[-\gamma B_{1}(u,u)_{k}-\gamma B_{2}(v,v)_{k}+\rho_{1}(u,u)_{k}+\rho_{2}(v,v)_{k}+f_{k}+2B_{1}(u,f)_{k}\\
\hskip 142.26378pt+2B_{2}(g,v)_{k}+R_{1}(u,v,v)_{k}+R_{2}(u,u,u)_{k}+R_{3}(u,v,v)_{k}\big]\text{d}s\\
v_{k}(t)-e^{ik^{3}t-\gamma t}v_{k}(0)=-B_{3}(u,v)_{k}+e^{ik^{3}t-\gamma t}B_{3}(u_{0},v_{0})_{k}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(ik^{3}-\gamma)(t-s)}\big[-\gamma B_{3}(u,v)_{k}\\
+\rho_{3}(u,v)_{k}+g_{k}+R_{4}(u,u,v)_{k}+\frac{\beta}{3a}R_{4}(v,v,v)_{k}+R_{5}(u,u,v)_{k}+B_{3}(f,v)_{k}+B_{3}(u,g)\big]ds.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{(-a\partial_{x}^{3}-\gamma)(t-s)}f(x)\text{d}s\right\rVert_{H^{1+\alpha}}=\left\lVert\frac{\langle k\rangle^{1+\alpha}f_{k}}{iak^{3}-\gamma}(1-e^{(iak^{3}-\gamma)t})\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{\alpha-2}},

analogous estimate holds for
e^{(-\partial_{x}^{3}-\gamma)(t-s)}g
as well. These bounds, the estimates utilized in obtaining main smoothing result, and the growth bound of Lemma
5.1
yield, for
t<\delta
, that

\left\lVert u(t)-e^{-(a\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)t}u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(a\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)(t-r)}\rho_{2}(v,v)(r)\,\text{d}r\right\rVert_{H^{1+\alpha}}\\
+\left\lVert v(t)-e^{-(\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)t}v_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)(t-r)}\rho_{3}(u,v)(r)\,\text{d}r\right\rVert_{H^{1+\alpha}}\\
\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{\alpha-2}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{\alpha-2}}+\big(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\big)^{2}+\big(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X_{a,\delta}^{1,1/2}}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X_{1,\delta}^{1,1/2}}\big)^{3}\\
\leq C\big(\alpha,\gamma,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\big)

where we use the local theory bounds for
X_{a,\delta}^{1,1/2}
,
X_{1,\delta}^{1,1/2}
norms for the local existence time
\delta
in the final inequality. By virtue of dissipation, this bound also holds for arbitrarily large times making use of the local bound above, for the full discussion, see Section
6
in
[
11
]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.12
.

For the existence of a global attractor, we check the asymptotic compactness of the flow. It suffices to show that for any sequence
(u_{0,r},v_{0,r})
in an absorbing set
\mathcal{B}_{0}
and for any sequence of times
t_{r}\rightarrow\infty
, the sequence
U_{t_{r}}(u_{0,r},v_{0,r})
possesses a convergent subsequence in
\dot{H}^{1}\times H^{1}
. Next we use Theorem
5.2
, for almost every
a\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
such that
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
and
\rho_{2}=\rho_{3}=0
, to write

\displaystyle U_{t_{r}}(u_{0,r},v_{0,r})=(e^{-(a\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)t_{r}}u_{0,r},e^{-(\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)t_{r}}v_{0,r})+N_{t_{r}}(u_{0,r},v_{0,r})

where the nonlinear part
N_{t_{r}}(u_{0,r},v_{0,r})
is contained within a ball in
H^{1+\alpha}\times H^{1+\alpha}
. Therefore by Rellich’s theorem the sequence
\{N_{t_{r}}(u_{0,r},v_{0,r}):r\in\mathbb{N}\}
has a convergent subsequence in
H^{1}\times H^{1}
. This implies the existence of a convergent subsequence of the sequence
\{U_{t_{r}}(u_{0,r},v_{0,r}):r\in\mathbb{N}\}
, since

\displaystyle\left\lVert(e^{-(a\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)t_{r}}u_{0,r},e^{-(\partial_{x}^{3}+\gamma)t_{r}}v_{0,r})\right\rVert_{H^{1}\times H^{1}}\lesssim e^{-\gamma t_{r}}(\left\lVert u_{0,r}\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\left\lVert v_{0,r}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})\lesssim e^{-\gamma t_{r}}\rightarrow 0

as
t_{r}\rightarrow\infty
uniformly. Therefore
U_{t}
is asymptotically compact. To prove the compactness of the attractor
\mathcal{A}
in the space
H^{1+\alpha}\times H^{1+\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we need to show, by using Rellich’s theorem, that the attractor is bounded in
H^{1+\alpha+\epsilon}\times H^{1+\alpha+\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon>0
satisfying
\alpha+\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
. In this regard, it suffices to find some closed ball
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha+\epsilon}\subset H^{1+\alpha+\epsilon}\times H^{1+\alpha+\epsilon}
such that
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\alpha+\epsilon}
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\bigcap_{\tau\geq 0}\overline{\bigcup_{t\geq\tau}U_{t}\mathcal{B}_{0}}=:\bigcap_{\tau\geq 0}V_{\tau}.

As above, using Theorem
5.2
, we can express each element of
V_{\tau}
as a sum of linear evolution which decays to zero exponentially and the nonlinear evolution contained by some ball
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha+\epsilon}
in
H^{1+\alpha+\epsilon}\times H^{1+\alpha+\epsilon}
. This implies that the set
V_{\tau}
is contained in a
\delta_{\tau}
neighbourhood
N_{\tau}
of
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha+\epsilon}
in
H^{1+\alpha+\epsilon}\times H^{1+\alpha+\epsilon}
. Here
\delta_{\tau}\rightarrow 0
as
\tau\rightarrow\infty
due to the exponential decay of linear evolutions. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\bigcap_{\tau\geq 0}V_{\tau}\subset\bigcap_{\tau\geq 0}N_{\tau}=\mathcal{B}_{\alpha+\epsilon}.

∎

Lemma 6.1

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\frac{1}{\langle n^{2}+an+b\rangle^{\beta}}\lesssim 1,\,\,\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\sum_{n}\frac{1}{\langle n^{3}+an^{2}+bn+c\rangle^{\gamma}}\lesssim 1

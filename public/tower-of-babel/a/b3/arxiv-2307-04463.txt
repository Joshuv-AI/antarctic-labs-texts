Abstract
. We prove that the distance from an n × n n\times n complex matrix M M to the set of nilpotents is at least 1 2 ​ sec ⁡ π n + 2 \frac{1}{2}\sec\frac{\pi}{n+2} if there is a nonzero projection P P such that P ​ M ​ P = M PMP=M and M ∗ ​ M ≥ P M^{*}M\geq P . In the particular case where M M equals P P , this verifies a conjecture by G.W. MacDonald in 1995. We also confirm a related conjecture in D.A. Herrero’s book.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this note,
n
is a positive integer.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
denote the set of
n\times n
complex matrices, and let
{\mathcal{N}}_{n}
denote the subset of
n\times n
nilpotent matrices.
In the current paragraph, we assume that
A,B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
.
We endow
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
with the usual distance
d
that comes from the matrix (operator) norm, that is,
d(A,B)=\lVert A-B\rVert
.
Let
\nu(A)
stand for the distance
\inf_{N\in{\mathcal{N}}_{n}}d(A,N)
between
A
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{n}
.
Some properties of
\nu
can be derived easily.
For example, the inequality
\lvert\nu(A)-\nu(B)\rvert\leq\lVert A-B\rVert
holds.
We have
\nu(\lambda A)=\lvert\lambda\rvert\nu(A)
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.
If
U
is a unitary in
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
, then
\nu(A)=\nu(UAU^{*})
.
Let the symbol
\rho(A)
denote the spectral radius of
A
.
Since every matrix is unitarily equivalent to an upper triangular matrix (Schur triangulation), we obtain
\nu(A)\leq\rho(A)
.

However, in general, it is apparently very hard to determine the precise value of
\nu(A)
for a matrix
A
.
The following question concerning
\nu
has long been studied in the literature:
What is the infimum of
\nu(P)
over all nonzero projections (hermitian idempotents)
P
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
?
For
0\leq m\leq n
, let
{\mathcal{P}}_{n,m}
denote the set of projections in
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
of rank
m
.
To the best of our knowledge, the first research on the above question was given in
[
3
, Section 1]
a half century ago, in which Hedlund writes that the determination of the precise value seems difficult.
More than 20 years later, MacDonald proved that
\nu(P)=\frac{1}{2}\sec\frac{\pi}{n+2}
for
P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n,1}
, and conjectured that
\nu(P)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sec\frac{\pi}{n+2}
holds for every nonzero projection
P
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}

[
5
]
.
He verified it for
n\leq 4

[
6
, Lemma 3.3]
.
In the recent paper
[
2
]
, Cramer conjectured that
\nu(P)=\frac{1}{2}\sec\frac{\pi}{\frac{n}{m}+2}
for every
1\leq m\leq n
and
P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n,m}
, and proved that this is the case when
m=n-1
.
For more information around this topic, see
[
4
, Chapter 2]
,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
.
The main purpose of this note is to verify MacDonald’s conjecture.

We also consider a similar problem concerning operators on a (possibly infinite-dimensional) complex Hilbert space
H
.
Let
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
denote the set of bounded linear operators on
H
.
For
A\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
, let
\nu_{n}(A)
stand for the distance between
A
and the set
\{N\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)\mid N^{n}=0\}
.
We show that
\nu_{n}(P)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sec\frac{\pi}{n+2}
for every nonzero projection
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
This gives a proof to a conjecture posed by Herrero in the first part of
[
4
, Conjecture 2.16]
. Note that the latter part of
[
4
, Conjecture 2.16]
is not correct, see
[
5
, page 849]
.

In fact, below we prove Theorems
4
and
6
, which are much more general than MacDonald’s conjecture and Herrero’s respectively.

2.
Proofs

When
A
and
B
are hermitian matrices (operators), the symbol
A\leq B
means that
B-A
is positive semidefinite.
If
0\leq A
, then
A^{1/2}
stands for the positive square root of
A
.
For a projection
P
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
, we define
P^{\perp}:=I_{n}-P
.
Let
\Pi_{n}
denote the set of
(n+1)
-tuples of projections
(P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
satisfying
P_{0}\leq P_{1}\leq\cdots\leq P_{n}
and
P_{k}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n,k}
for every
0\leq k\leq n
.
Recall that a matrix
N\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is nilpotent if and only if
N^{n}=0
, and this is equivalent to the existence of
(P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\in\Pi_{n}
such that
P_{k-1}^{\perp}NP_{k}=0
for every
k
.
Thus, Power’s version
[
7
, Lemma]
of Arveson’s distance formula
[
1
]
gives the following equation (see also
[
5
, Theorem 2]
): For
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
,

\nu(A)=\inf\{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert P_{k-1}^{\perp}AP_{k}\rVert\mid(P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\in\Pi_{n}\}.

Lemma 1
.

Let
X,Y\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
. Then
\lVert XY\rVert=\lVert(X^{*}X)^{1/2}(YY^{*})^{1/2}\rVert
.

Proof.

By considering polar decompositions of
X
and
Y
, we get partial isometries
V,W
such that

XY=V(X^{*}X)^{1/2}(YY^{*})^{1/2}W
and
(X^{*}X)^{1/2}(YY^{*})^{1/2}=V^{*}XYW^{*}
.
This leads to the desired equation.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Assume that
0\leq A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
. Let
\Pi(A)
denote the set of
(n+1)
-tuples of matrices
(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
satisfying
0=A_{0}\leq A_{1}\leq\cdots\leq A_{n}=A
and
{\operatorname{rank}\,}(A_{k}-A_{k-1})\leq 1
for every
1\leq k\leq n
.
Then

\nu(A)=\alpha:=\inf\{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(A-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert\mid(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\Pi(A)\}.

Proof.

Let
(P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\in\Pi_{n}
.
For each
k
, Lemma
1
with
X=P_{k-1}^{\perp}A^{1/2}
and
Y=A^{1/2}P_{k}
shows

\lVert P_{k-1}^{\perp}AP_{k}\rVert=\lVert(A-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert
,
where
A_{k}=A^{1/2}P_{k}A^{1/2}
.
It is easy to see
(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\Pi(A)
, thus we obtain
\alpha\leq\nu(A)
.

To get the other inequality, assume that
(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\Pi(A)
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Take a matrix
C_{k}\geq 0
of rank one that is close to
A_{k}-A_{k-1}
for each
k
such that
B=C_{1}+C_{2}+\cdots+C_{n}
has rank
n
.
We may assume
\lVert B-A\rVert<\varepsilon
and
\lVert(B-B_{k-1})^{1/2}B_{k}^{1/2}-(A-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert<\varepsilon
, where
B_{0}=0
and
B_{k}=C_{1}+C_{2}+\cdots+C_{k}
, for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Thus
\lVert(A-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert>\lVert(B-B_{k-1})^{1/2}B_{k}^{1/2}\rVert-\varepsilon
.
Since
B
has rank
n
,
B^{1/2}
is invertible.
Set
P_{k}:=B^{-1/2}B_{k}B^{-1/2}
for each
k
.
Then we have
0=P_{0}\leq P_{1}\leq\cdots\leq P_{n}=I_{n}
and
{\operatorname{rank}\,}(P_{k}-P_{k-1})\leq 1
for every
1\leq k\leq n
.
It follows that
{\operatorname{rank}\,}P_{k}\leq k
and
{\operatorname{rank}\,}(I_{n}-P_{k})\leq n-k
, which imply
(P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\in\Pi_{n}
.
Lemma
1
implies
\lVert(B-B_{k-1})^{1/2}B_{k}^{1/2}\rVert=\lVert P_{k-1}^{\perp}BP_{k}\rVert
for each
k
.
By

\lvert\nu(B)-\nu(A)\lvert\leq\lVert B-A\lVert<\varepsilon
, we obtain

\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(A-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert>\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert P_{k-1}^{\perp}BP_{k}\rVert-\varepsilon\geq\nu(B)-\varepsilon>\nu(A)-2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we get
\alpha\geq\nu(A)
.
∎

Essentially the same proof gives

Lemma 3
.

Let
0\leq A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
and
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
.
Then

\nu(A^{1/2}XA^{1/2})=\inf\{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(A-A_{k-1})^{1/2}(XA_{k}X^{*})^{1/2}\rVert\mid(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\Pi(A)\}.

We fix
Q\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n,1}
. Recall that MacDonald gave
\nu(Q)=\frac{1}{2}\sec\frac{\pi}{n+2}

[
5
]
.

Theorem 4
.

If
M\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
and there is a nonzero projection
P\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
such that
PMP=M
and
M^{*}M\geq P
, then
\nu(M)\geq\nu(Q)
.

Proof.

Since
Q
is of rank
1
, we obtain

\Pi(Q)=\{(c_{0}Q,c_{1}Q,\ldots,c_{n}Q)\mid 0=c_{0}\leq c_{1}\leq\cdots\leq c_{n}=1\}.

Hence Lemma
2
implies

\nu(Q)=\inf\{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sqrt{c_{k}(1-c_{k-1})}\mid 0=c_{0}\leq c_{1}\leq\cdots\leq c_{n}=1\}.

Let
(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\Pi(P)
and set
a_{k}=\lVert A_{k}\rVert
for each
k
.
Since
0=A_{0}\leq A_{1}\leq\cdots\leq A_{n}=P
, the inequality
0=a_{0}\leq a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}=1
holds.
For each
k
, we have

\begin{split}\lVert(P-A_{k-1})^{1/2}(MA_{k}M^{*})^{1/2}\rVert^{2}&=\lVert(MA_{k}M^{*})^{1/2}(P-A_{k-1})(MA_{k}M^{*})^{1/2}\rVert\\
&\geq\lVert(MA_{k}M^{*})^{1/2}(1-a_{k-1})P(MA_{k}M^{*})^{1/2}\rVert\\
&=(1-a_{k-1})\lVert MA_{k}M^{*}\rVert.\end{split}

Moreover, the assumption
M^{*}M\geq P
implies

\begin{split}\lVert MA_{k}M^{*}\rVert&=\lVert(MA_{k}^{1/2})(MA_{k}^{1/2})^{*}\rVert=\lVert(MA_{k}^{1/2})^{*}(MA_{k}^{1/2})\rVert=\lVert A_{k}^{1/2}M^{*}MA_{k}^{1/2}\rVert\\
&\geq\lVert A_{k}^{1/2}PA_{k}^{1/2}\rVert=\lVert A_{k}\rVert=a_{k}.\end{split}

It follows that

\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(P-A_{k-1})^{1/2}(MA_{k}M^{*})^{1/2}\rVert\geq\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sqrt{a_{k}(1-a_{k-1})}
.
Lemma
3
with
A=P
and
X=M
together with the preceding paragraph leads us to
\nu(M)=\nu(P^{1/2}MP^{1/2})\geq\nu(Q)
.
∎

If
{\operatorname{rank}\,}P\,(={\operatorname{rank}\,}M)=m
in the above proof, then
a_{n-m+1}=1
since
{\operatorname{rank}\,}(A_{k}-A_{k-1})\leq 1
for each
k
.
Thus we may actually get
\nu(M)\geq\nu(R)
with
R\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n-m+1,1}
, or equivalently,
\nu(M)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sec\frac{\pi}{n-m+3}
.

Note that the assumption on
M
in Theorem
4
is satisfied if and only if
M
is unitarily equivalent to a block matrix of the form
\begin{pmatrix}M_{0}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
for some
M_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m}
,
1\leq m\leq n
, with
M_{0}^{*}M_{0}\geq I_{m}
.
In particular, every nonzero normal matrix whose spectrum is contained in
\{0\}\cup\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\lvert z\rvert\geq 1\}
satisfies the assumption of Theorem
4
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space.
Let
\Phi_{n}(H)
denote the set of
(n+1)
-tuples of projections
(P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
satisfying
0=P_{0}\leq P_{1}\leq\cdots\leq P_{n}=I\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
As in the case of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
, for
A\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
, the Arveson-type distance formula
[
7
, Lemma]
shows

\nu_{n}(A)=\inf\{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert P_{k-1}^{\perp}AP_{k}\rVert\mid(P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\in\Phi_{n}(H)\}.

Using this and imitating the first half of the proof of Lemma
2
, we obtain

Lemma 5
.

Let
0\leq A\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and
X\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
. Let
\Phi_{n}(A)
denote the set of
(n+1)
-tuples of operators
(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
with
0=A_{0}\leq A_{1}\leq\cdots\leq A_{n}=A
.
Then

\nu_{n}(A^{1/2}XA^{1/2})\geq\inf\{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(A-A_{k-1})^{1/2}(XA_{k}X^{*})^{1/2}\rVert\mid(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\Phi_{n}(A)\}.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}(H)
denote the set of operators
M\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
with the property that there is a nonzero projection
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
satisfying
PMP=M
and
M^{*}M\geq P
.
Imitating the proof of Theorem
4
, we obtain

Theorem 6
.

If
M\in{\mathcal{X}}(H)
, then
\nu_{n}(M)\geq\nu(Q)
.

Corollary 7
.

If
\dim H\geq n
, then the distance
\delta
from
{\mathcal{X}}(H)
to
\{N\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)\mid N^{n}=0\}
is
\nu(Q)
.

Proof.

The preceding theorem implies
\delta\geq\nu(Q)
.
Note that
\nu(Q)=\nu_{n}(Q)
because
N^{n}=0
for every nilpotent
N\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
.
(Here,
\nu_{n}(Q)
is the distance from
Q
to
\{N\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\mid N^{n}=0\}
.)
Take a linear isometry
V\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to H
.
Then the map
X\mapsto VXV^{*}
is a linear isometry from
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
into
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
that preserves the
∗
-structure and product.
Thus
\nu(Q)=\nu_{n}(Q)\geq\nu_{n}(VQV^{*})
.
Since
VQV^{*}
is a nonzero projection, it clearly belongs to
{\mathcal{X}}(H)
.
Therefore,
\nu_{n}(VQV^{*})\geq\delta
.
∎

Low energy phonons in single crystal ZrW 2 O 8
R. A. Ewings, K. Refson, T. G. Perring, J. Ollivier

Abstract
ZrW 2 O 8 is the prototypical example of a material exhibiting negative thermal expansion (NTE). As well as being among the first NTE materials to be studied in detail, it has served as a test bed for ideas for the physical mechanisms that lead to this unusual behavior. It is now widely accepted that here, and in many other framework materials exhibiting NTE, a collection of low energy phonon modes, as opposed to just one or two, are responsible for the anomalous thermal properties. However, quantitative verification and analysis of the density functional theory (DFT) calculations which underpin this proposal are still lacking. In particular, probing the low energy phonons directly throughout reciprocal space using inelastic neutron scattering, as opposed to other techniques which only probe the Brillouin zone center, is technically challenging and hence rarely done. Here we report inelastic neutron scattering measurements in a large number of Brillouin zones, achieved via the time of flight technique, over a 400 K temperature range. We find excellent agreement between DFT calculations and experimental data at low temperature, albeit with a rescaling of the calculated phonon energies, and of the lattice parameter used in the calculations compared to the measured value. However, the shifts in phonon modes due to the reduction in lattice parameter on warming, that can be predicted using DFT, are not observed. This is most likely due to counteractive anharmonic effects arising from the increased amplitude of lattice vibrations at elevated temperatures. Notwithstanding, the good agreement between experiment and DFT gives high confidence that the theory’s predictions of atomic motions for different modes is correct, and hence can be used as a reliable basis for quantitative analysis of NTE effects.



I
Introduction

Negative thermal expansion (NTE) is when a material contracts rather than expands on warming. This effect can be uniform, i.e. along all crystallographic axes
Dapiaggi et al. 2003
;
Greve et al. 2010
;
Lock et al. 2010
, or non-uniform
Goodwin et al. 2008
;
Sartbaeva et al. 2004
;
Grobler et al. 2013
. It may also exist over a wide or a more limited range of temperatures
Kim et al. 2018
. ZrW
2
O
8
is in many respects the canonical example, with its NTE having been re-discovered in the 1990s
Martinek and Hummel 1968
;
Mary et al. 1996
;
Evans et al. 1997
. It is particularly notable that its NTE behavior is uniform, extant over a wide temperature range, from
\sim 10
K to
\sim 1000
K, and its coefficient of thermal expansion is large and negative (
\sim 10^{-5}\rm{K}^{-1}
) (see review articles ref.
Dove and Fang 2016
and ref.
Mittal et al. 2018
and references therein). In common with many other materials known to exhibit NTE, ZrW
2
O
8
has a framework structure, i.e. it contains many ions that form coordinated polyhedra, most of which share corners, with large pores or spaces between the polyhedra. Indeed, it has more recently been found that intercalation of water into these pores can also result in significant anomalous lattice expansion or contraction
Baise et al. 2018
. The low temperature crystal structure of ZrW
2

is shown in figure

As a material is warmed phonon modes with gradually increasing energy are populated. If the potentials for these modes are asymmetric as a function of displacement, then thermal expansion will arise
Dove 1993
;
Dove 2003
. Studies of the phonons in NTE materials often involve measurement of the phonon density of states (PDOS) using either Raman spectroscopy, inelastic x-ray scattering, or inelastic neutron scattering measurements of powder samples
Ravindran et al. 2003
;
Oishi et al. 2017
;
Mittal et al. 2009
;
Chapman et al. 2006
;
Gupta et al. 2012
;
Mittal et al. 2007
;
Li et al. 2011
, as this is relatively straightforward to do and can be compared directly to harmonic lattice dynamics calculations such as those from density functional theory (DFT). ZrW
2
O
8
is no exception, with the phonon density of states having been measured as a function of temperature soon after the seminal works on the NTE behavior
Ernst et al. 1998
. It was found that the low energy peaks in the PDOS shift to higher energy on warming. Qualitatively, this is the opposite behavior to normal (positive thermal expansion) in which increased bond lengths on warming result in weaker interatomic potentials and hence a reduction in the energy of phonon modes.

In terms of interpretation of the PDOS measurements, and NTE more generally, early theories concerned the role of rigid unit modes (RUMs), in which Zr–O and W–O polyhedra remain undistorted but the whole structure flexes considerably along their shared vertices. As the amplitude of such modes increases, the root-mean-square distance between metal centers decreases, even though individual metal–oxygen bonds do not change in length
Dove and Fang 2016
. Such modes would typically be found at the lower energy end of the phonon spectrum. However, various measurements have indicated that the picture is somewhat more complex than this. In particular, it has been found from EXAFS
Bridges et al. 2014
;
Vila et al. 2018a
;
Vila et al. 2018b
that all of the polyhedra in the structure distort somewhat on warming, although the WO
4
tetrahedra are noticeably more rigid than the ZrO
6
octahedra. Notwithstanding, significant flex occurs along some of the Zr–O–W bonds that comprise the corner-sharing polyhedra. In this context, it would be appealing to search for a particular phonon mode that facilitates this distortion. However, numerous computational studies of the lattice dynamics in ZrW
2
O
8

Rimmer et al. 2023
;
Sanson 2014
;
Gupta et al. 2013
;
Figueirêdo and Perottoni 2007
have demonstrated that there is no such single mode responsible for NTE. Rather, a large number of low energy phonon modes distributed throughout the Brillouin zone have a character that leads to NTE.

The direct measurements of phonons in ZrW
2
O
8
(and many other NTE materials) to date have had a number of limitations. Raman spectroscopy can only measure the
\mathbf{q}=0
Raman-active modes and thus does not provide a comprehensive picture, although the energy resolution available with this technique is usually superior to that available with neutron or x-ray spectroscopy. Neutron (or x-ray) scattering PDOS measurements are by their very nature a spherical average over reciprocal space of all possible phonon modes at a particular energy, so there is no direct sensitivity to the Q-space origin of shifts in energy with temperature. This can be ameliorated somewhat by combining and interpreting these measurements with DFT calculations. However, this approach is challenging in systems containing many atoms, and hence many phonon modes which may be more closely spaced than the energy resolution of the neutron spectrometer used for the measurements. In contrast, a measurement of a single crystal sample would allow individual modes to be isolated via measurements performed in many different Brillouin zones
Sup
. This would then provide a more stringent test of the DFT.

The motivation of the present study is therefore to develop a more comprehensive understanding of the low energy phonons in ZrW
2
O
8
, by measuring in more Brillouin zones and with good energy resolution, to identify which particular individual or groups of phonon modes that are especially sensitive to temperature. Such measurements are sensitive to both the eigenvalues and eigenvectors extracted from DFT calculations, and hence pose a stringent test of the range of their validity, most notably for the low energy phonons thought to be important for NTE effects.

II.1
Density functional theory

DFT calculations were performed using the plane-wave pseudopotential method as implemented in the CASTEP package
Clark et al. 2005
. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzehof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional
Perdew et al. 1996
was used with norm-conserving pseudopotentials of the RRKJ type

from the Rappe and Bennett library

et al

Rimmer et al. 2023

\sim 9.18

The phonon dispersion was calculated using Density Functional Perturbation Theory (DFPT)
Refson et al. 2006
, with a
3\times 3\times 3
Monkhorst-Pack grid of phonon wavevectors and Fourier interpolation of the resulting dynamical matrices, in order to sample the entire Brillouin zone. Additional calculations were performed for fixed unit cells of smaller dimension (9.23, 9.22, 9.215, 9.21 and 9.16 Å), at each of which the structure was relaxed and phonons calculated in the same way as for the fully relaxed structure. The calculated stress for each of these cell dimensions was 0.81, 1.13, 1.29, 1.45 and 3.20  GPa respectively. In the first four of these cases no soft phonon modes were observed, indicating that the computed structure remained dynamically stable. For the calculation with a lattice parameter of 9.16 Å a large number of negative energy modes were observed, indicating that for such a compressed lattice the structure becomes unstable. From this point onwards when we refer to these DFT calculations we mean those with
9.21\leq a\leq 9.23
Å.

When comparing the computed dispersion with that measured experimentally, an empirical scale factor is applied to the eigenvalues (energies) but not the eigenvectors (structure factors). This is determined by finding the simulation that agrees most closely with the data after such a uniform re-scale: the structure factors from the simulations with different lattice parameters are fitted to the neutron data with a single adjustable parameter (the energy scale). For ZrW
2
O
8
the best overall agreement was found for the case of
a=9.215
Åand a scale factor
\sim 0.95

Additional calculations were performed with the same DFT parameters and a lattice parameter of 9.215 Å, but using the PBESol functional
Perdew et al. 2008
, which has been reported as yielding values for the bulk properties (elastic constants) of ZrW

O

II.2
Inelastic neutron scattering

The time of flight (ToF) neutron scattering experiments were performed on the IN5 spectrometer at the Institut Laue-Langevin
Ollivier and Mutka 2011
. Data were collected with incident neutron energy
E_{i}=8.24
meV, with the final chopper spun at 16,000 r.p.m. to give a resolution at the elastic line of 0.27 meV (full-width half-maximum, FWHM). The sample was aligned so that the
(H,H,0)
and
(0,0,L)
reciprocal space directions were in the horizontal plane of the instrument. The data were collected by scanning the sample orientation about the vertical axis in 0.5 degree steps over a range of 161 degrees, starting from the
(1,1,0)
-axis perpendicular to the incident neutron beam. Data were collected at temperatures of 100 K and 400 K, with these temperatures chosen in order to increase the signal via the Bose-Einstein population factor while maintaining a large temperature difference between measurements, but avoiding the structural phase transition at
\sim 425
K
Mary et al. 1996
. Additional data were collected with the sample aligned with the
(H,0,0)
and
(0,K,0)
directions in the horizontal plane of the spectrometer, with the sample orientation scanned over a range of 88 degrees in 1 degree steps. These data were collected with
E_{i}=10.43
meV and a final chopper speed of 16,000 r.p.m. giving a FWHM energy resolution at the elastic line of 0.35 meV. Data with this configuration were collected at temperatures of 2 K and 295 K.

The raw data were processed (units conversion, normalisation of intensities, etc.) using the Mantid software package
Arnold et al. 2014
. Further processing of the data, and all subsequent visualisation and analysis, including convolution of calculated spectra with the instrumental resolution, were performed using the
Horace

However, the way mode Grüneisen parameters are determined from DFT is rather different to what actually happens when warming and cooling a sample. In the DFT, the lattice dynamics are calculated at zero temperature in the quasi-harmonic approximation for a series of different fixed lattice parameters close to the ground state parameters determined from a minimization of the overall forces and energies. This therefore corresponds to the lattice contracting or expanding under pressure / stress, rather than warming / cooling. In reality, the warming will result in an increased amplitude of lattice vibrations, which is not accounted for in the quasi-harmonic approximation, with the consequence that anharmonic effects may become more significant at higher temperatures. This point has been discussed extensively by Mittal
et al

Gupta et al. 2013
;
Mittal et al. 2018
, who have calculated this effect for a few individual modes at particular wavevectors in ZrW
2
O
8
. Some attempt has been made to quantify the effect more broadly using Raman spectroscopy data

;
Oishi et al. 2017

There are two effects which govern the behavior of a phonon mode energy with temperature. On the one hand, in a conventional material, thermal expansion (on increasing temperature) gives rise to longer bonds between atoms which are weaker and hence give rise to lower energy phonon modes. On the other hand, as temperature increases the amplitudes of normal modes increase and intrinsic anharmonicity comes into play. This would then yield phonon modes that harden slightly with increasing temperature
Dove 2003
. However, in practice in conventional materials this effect is often much smaller than the softening due to thermal expansion and so is masked. In an NTE material one might imagine that if the lattice expansion effect is opposite to normal, then both of the effects described above would add together and result in phonon mode hardening substantially with increasing temperature. Or even in a RUM scenario, in which the bonds between atoms to not change length and hence strength, the intrinsic anharmonicity would be left over to give mode hardening, albeit perhaps smaller in magnitude. Neutron measurements of the phonon density of states in ZrW

IV
Discussion

From the results and analysis presented so far, we can see that there is generally very good agreement between the phonon dispersion relations and structure factors computed from DFT, and our inelastic neutron scattering measurements. Of course, anharmonicity is essential to describe any kind of thermal expansion, negative or positive. We have already cited extensively the predictions made by Gupta
et al

Gupta et al. 2013
, which were based on a phenomenological theory developed for understanding librational modes at
q=0
in the
\alpha
phase of solid nitrogen
Kuchta and Luty 1982
. In this approach the phonon modes are decomposed into the displacement and rotation of molecular units (analogous to the RUM approach) and the computed potential rewritten as a polynomial to
4^{\rm{th}}
order in terms of these coordinates. This is clearly an approximation, though it should be acknowledged that it does a reasonable job, estimating correctly that in ZrW
2
O
8
at some wavevectors the effects of implicit and explicit anharmonicity seem to approximately cancel each other.

It would seem, then, that for the low energy excitations studied here there is a cancellation of two large effects on the energy of the modes. Both arise due to anharmonicity, and given the unconventional behavior of ZrW
2
O
8
it is clear that anharmonicity has an important role in governing the material’s thermal properties. In order to cancel the mode softening expected from the mode Grüneisen parameters, large amplitude thermal motions are required. This would naturally arise in a structure that is especially flexible, with individual atoms subject either only to weak constraints or strongly constrained only in one or two directions.

Alternative methods exist in addition to DFT of computing the phonon spectra, that include anharmonicity. These include the temperature-dependent effective potential (TDEP) method
Hellman et al. 2011
;
Hellman et al. 2013
;
Hellman and Abrikosov 2013
and the self-consistent phonon (SCPH) method
Tadano et al. 2014
;
Tadano and Tsuneyuki 2015
. These would in principle allow us to investigate in detail the effect of temperature and anharmonicity on the phonon modes, and hence NTE. However, the calculations to do this for a system like ZrW
2
O
8
would be prohibitively computationally expensive, and are thus outside the scope of the present work.

Notwithstanding the aforementioned shortcomings of the DFT computations, it should be acknowledged that they perform an admirable job of reproducing many of the details of the INS measurements, both in terms of the eigenvalues (mode energies) and eigenvectors (measured neutron scattering intensities). This then gives high confidence that they can be used to give both a qualitative and quantitative commentary on the nature of the atomic displacements involved in the low energy phonon modes measured here. This was discussed extensively already by Gupta
et al

Gupta et al. 2013
, who correlated the translational and rotational motions of quasi-rigid ZrO
6
octahedra and WO
4
tetrahedra, together with distortions of those polyhedra, with reported distortions found from local structure probes
Hancock et al. 2004
. We can also have good confidence that the DFT results can be used as a general interpretative tool, e.g. in the flexibility analysis of NTE undertaken by Rimmer
et al

Rimmer et al. 2023
.

Molecular dynamics simulations
Sanson 2014
suggest that the oxygens that bridge Zr–O–W bonds are subject to large transverse vibrations. The distortions we observe from our DFT computations indicate that some modes do indeed include such motions. Because of the relative rigidity of the WO
4
tetrahedra (it is well-known that the W–O bonds are shorter than the Zr–O bonds, so the WO
4
tetrahedra are more tightly bonded and stiffer than the ZrO
6
octahedra
Tucker et al. 2005
) a consequence of such vibrations is that the lone unbonded oxygen on each tetrahedron is also subject to large vibrations
Bridges et al. 2014
. Indeed, displacements of the lone oxygen would have a lower energy cost due to weaker bonding. The flexibility analysis performed by Rimmer
et al

Rimmer et al. 2023
, using DFT results similar to those reported here, showed that the low energy phonon modes responsible for most of the NTE (those with negative Grüneisen parameters) had largely fixed Zr–O and W–O bond lengths. These results together suggest that the tension effect description of NTE offers an appropriate description of the behavior of ZrW
2
O
8
: rigid Zr–O and W–O bonds together with a large transverse vibration of oxygens that bridge Zr and W lead naturally to a reduced distance between Zr and W and hence NTE. By validating the DFT that underpins this interpretation against INS data, we are able to give greater credence to it. We also note that in the high pressure orthorhombic phase of
\gamma
-ZrW
2
O
8

Jorgensen et al. 1999
the lone oxygen becomes bonded to a tungsten ion, making its motion more constrained than in the ambient pressure cubic
\alpha
-ZrW
2
O
8
phase, which is then manifest in a significantly smaller (negative) coefficient of thermal expansion in the high pressure polymorph.

V
Conclusion

In conclusion, we present a comprehensive set of INS measurements of the phonons in ZrW
2
O
8
, and compare them to predictions from DFT. We find that we have to compress the lattice compared to the equilibrium in the DFT in order to get agreement with the neutron data, but that after doing so the agreement is excellent. This gives confidence that the overall picture of atomic motions predicted by DFT closely matches the experimental situation, so that DFT can be confidently used as a basis for additional analysis of NTE. The calculated mode Grüneisen parameters suggest that most low energy phonon modes should soften on warming as the lattice shrinks, but instead most modes either remain unchanged or harden slightly, as well as broadening somewhat. This can be understood in terms of anharmonicity arising from an increased amplitude of atomic motions associated with the phonon modes.

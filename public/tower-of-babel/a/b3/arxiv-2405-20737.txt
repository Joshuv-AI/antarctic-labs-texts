Regular Subgradients of Marginal Functions with Applications to Calculus and Bilevel Programming
Le Phuoc Hai, Felipe Lara, Boris S. Mordukhovich

Abstract
\vskip-34.14322pt Abstract . The paper addresses the study and applications of a broad class of extended-real-valued functions, known as optimal value or marginal functions, which frequently appear in variational analysis, parametric optimization, and a variety of applications. Functions of this type are intrinsically nonsmooth and require the usage of tools of generalized differentiation. The main results of this paper provide novel evaluations and exact calculations of regular/Fréchet subgradients and their singular counterparts for general classes of marginal functions via their given data. The obtained results are applied to establishing new calculus rules for such subgradients and necessary optimality conditions in bilevel programming. Keywords : Variational analysis, generalized differentiation, marginal functions, calmness, variational calculus, bilevel programming Mathematics Subject Classification (2020) 49J52, 49J53, 90C31

1
Introduction

Our main interest in this paper is attracted to the class of
optimal
value/marginal functions
given in the form

\mu(x):=\inf\big\{\varphi(x,y)\;\big|\;y\in G(x)\big\},

(1.1)

where
\varphi:X\times Y\rightarrow{\mathbb{\bar{R}}}
is a cost/objective
function defined on the product of normed spaces
X
and
Y
and taking values
in the extended real line
\bar{\mathbb{R}}:=(-\infty,\infty]
, and where

G:X\rightrightarrows Y
is a constraint set-valued mapping. The
solution/argminimum mapping

M:X\rightrightarrows Y
associated with
(
1.1
) is defined by

M(x):=\big\{y\in G(x)\;\big|\;\;\varphi(x,y)=\mu(x)\big\}.

(1.2)

Over the years, it has been well recognized in variational analysis and optimization
that the class of marginal functions (
1.1
) plays a highly important role
in many aspects of variational theory and applications; see, e.g., the books

[
7
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
20
,
25
,
27
,
28
]

with the discussions and references therein. It follows from the definition that
(
1.1
) can be viewed as the optimal value in the
parameterized
problem
of minimizing the cost function
\varphi(x,y)
with respect to the decision
variable
y
subject to the parameter-dependent constraint set
G(x)
. Besides
this obvious interpretation, marginal functions arise in various settings that may
not be related to optimization; see the books mentioned above.

A characteristic feature of (
1.1
) is the
intrinsic nondifferentiability
of this function even in the case of smooth objectives and simple convex constraint sets. Therefore, sensitivity analysis of marginal functions requires the usage of suitable machinery of
generalized differentiation
, which has been done at some levels for various generalized derivatives under diverse assumptions; see, e.g.,
[
8
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
23
,
25
,
28
]
and the bibliographies therein for more details.

In this paper, we focus on the study and applications of
regular/Fréchet subgradients
and
singular regular subgradients
of marginal functions given in the general form (
1.1
). These subdifferential constructions for marginal functions seem to be much less investigated in comparison with the classical subdifferential of convex analysis, the generalized gradient of Clarke, and the limiting and singular subdifferentials of Mordukhovich. Some results for regular subgradients of marginal functions can be found in
[
10
,
22
]
and are discussed in what follows. The results of our paper significantly improve the known ones while providing also completely new conditions for lower and upper estimates and exact calculation formulas for both regular and singular regular subdifferentials of marginal functions in general settings of normed spaces. Moreover, we present applications of the results obtained for marginal functions to deriving new
calculus rules
for regular subgradients and their singular counterparts with further developing the
marginal function approach
to optimistic
bilevel programming
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains some preliminaries from variational analysis and generalized differentiation together with definitions of the major constructions and notions that are largely used to establish our main results in the subsequent sections.

In Section
3
, we derive new results on
lower and upper estimates
for both regular and singular regular subdifferentials of the general class of marginal functions (
1.1
) on normed spaces that are expressed via the given data of (
1.1
). To the best of our knowledge, the lower subdifferential estimates for marginal functions have never been obtained in the literature outside of equalities. Our results in this direction are established under the alternative assumptions of either the newly defined notion of
nearly-isolated calmness
for constraint mappings, or
upper Lipschitzian selections
of the solution mapping (
1.2
).

Section
4
establishes
exact calculation formulas
for the regular and singular regular subdifferentials of marginal functions on arbitrary normed spaces. Besides the aforementioned assumptions of Section
3
, the obtained exact subdifferential calculations require either the Fréchet differentiability of the cost function, or certain
metric qualification conditions
imposed on the initial data of (
1.1
). All these assumptions are illustrated by examples. It has been well realized in variational analysis that developing
variational calculus rules
is crucial for applications of any subdifferential construction. Not much has been done in this direction for regular subgradients and their singular counterparts.

Section
5
is devoted to applications of the subdifferential formulas for marginal functions obtained in Section
4
to deriving
exact/equality type
chain, sum, product, and quotient rules for regular and singular regular subgradients of functions on normed spaces. Some of the calculus results of this type for regular subgradients were obtained in
[
21
]
under the local Lipschitz continuity of the functions involved. Besides establishing novel calculus rules, we systematically replace the Lipschitz continuity assumptions in the results of
[
21
]
by the corresponding
calmness
properties, which lead us to refined calculus.

Section
6
develops the
marginal function approach
to deriving
optimality conditions
in problems of
optimistic bilevel programming
in
Asplund spaces
, which constitutes a broad class of Banach spaces that includes, in particular, all reflexive ones. Involving subdifferentiation of marginal functions and calculus rules for the regular and limiting subdifferentials together with the aforementioned metric qualification conditions, we establish new necessary optimality conditions for local optimal solutions to optimistic bilevel programs in generally non-Lipschitzian settings, which significantly improve those obtained in
[
18
]
for problems with fully Lipschitzian data in finite-dimensional spaces under additional conditions and more restrictive constraint qualifications.

In Section
7
, we present concluding remarks and discuss some directions of our future research and applications.

2
Preliminaries and Basic Definitions

Throughout the paper, we use standard notation of variational analysis and generalized differentiation; see, e.g.,
[
16
]
. Given a
normed space

X
endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|
, the topologically dual space of
X
is denoted by
X^{*}
, where
w^{*}
stands for the weak
∗
topology of
X^{*}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
indicates the canonical pairing between
X
and
X^{*}
. The symbols
\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},r)
and

{\mathbb{\overline{B}}}_{X^{*}}
signify the open ball centered at

\bar{x}
with radius
r>0
and the closed unit ball of
X^{*}
, respectively.
We also use the notations
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{R}_{-}:=(-\infty,0]
and
x\xrightarrow{S}\bar{x}
when

x\to\bar{x}
with
x\in S
.

For a set-valued mapping
F:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, the symbol

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Limsup}\nolimits\limits_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}\,F(x):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;\exists\,x_{k}\to\bar{x},\,\,\,x^{*}_{k}\xrightarrow{w^{*}}x^{*}\,\,\,\text{with}\,\,\,x^{*}_{k}\in F(x_{k}),\;k\in\mathbb{N}\big\}

stands for the (Painlevé-Kuratowski)
sequential outer limit
of
F
as

x\to\bar{x}
with respect to the norm topology of
X
and the weak
∗
topology of the dual space
X^{*}
, where
\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
signifies the collection of natural numbers.

Given a set-valued mapping/multifunction
F:X\rightrightarrows Y
between normed spaces, the
domain
and
graph
of
F
are defined by

\dom F:=\big\{x\in X\;\big|\;F(x)\neq\emptyset\big\},\;\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits F:=\big\{(x,y)\in X\times Y\;\big|\;\;y\in F(x)\big\},

respectively. Unless otherwise stated, the
norm
on the product space
X\times Y
is defined by
\|(x,y)\|:=\|x\|+\|y\|
for any
(x,y)\in X\times Y
.

Given an extended-real-valued function
f:X\to\mathbb{\bar{R}}
on a normed space
X
, its
domain
and
epigraph
are defined, respectively, by

\dom f:=\big\{x\in X\;\big|\;f(x)\neq\emptyset\}\;\mbox{ and }\;\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f:=\big\{(x,\alpha)\in X\times\mathbb{R}\;\big|\;\alpha\geq f(x)\big\}.

Following the terminology of
[
27
]
, the (Fréchet)
regular subdifferential

\hat{\partial}f(\bar{x})
of
f
at
\bar{x}\in\dom f
is the collection of subgradients
x^{*}\in X^{*}
such that for every

\varepsilon>0
there exists
\gamma>0
satisfying

\displaystyle f(x)-f(\bar{x})-\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle\geq-\varepsilon\|x-\bar{x}\|\;\mbox{ whenever }\;x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\gamma).

(2.1)

Along with
\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})
, we consider also the
upper regular subdifferential
of
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
at
\bar{x}
with
|f(\bar{x})|<\infty
defined symmetrically as

\widehat{\partial}^{+}f(\bar{x}):=-\widehat{\partial}(-f)(\bar{x}).

(2.2)

As well known
[
16
, Proposition 1.87]
, the sets
\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})
and
\widehat{\partial}^{+}f(\bar{x})
are nonempty simultaneously if and only if
f
is Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
.

The corresponding
regular normal cone
to a set
S\subset X
at
\bar{x}\in S
is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(\bar{x};S):=\Big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\Big|\;\limsup\limits_{x\xrightarrow{S}\bar{x}}\Big\langle x^{*},\dfrac{x-\bar{x}}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\Big\rangle\leq 0\Big\}

(2.3)

with
\widehat{N}(\bar{x};S):=\emptyset
if
\bar{x}\notin S
. Note that the regular subdifferential (
2.1
) of
f
at
\bar{x}\in S
can be geometrically expressed via the regular normal cone (
2.3
) by

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;(x^{*},-1)\in\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f\big)\big\}.

Along with the regular subdifferential (
2.1
), we consider the
singular regular subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}\in\dom f
defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}f(\bar{x}):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;(x^{*},0)\in\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f\big)\big\},

(2.4)

which can be equivalently described as follows:
x^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}f(\bar{x})
if and only if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varepsilon\big(\|x-\bar{x}\|+\lvert\beta-f(x)\rvert\big)\;\mbox{ whenever }\;\big(x,\beta)\in\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f\cap\mathbb{B}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})),\gamma\big).

For a set
S\subset X
with its
indicator function

\delta_{S}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\delta_{S}(x):=0
if
x\in S
and
\delta_{S}(x):=\infty
if
x\notin S
, we have the relationships

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(\bar{x};S)=\widehat{\partial}\delta_{S}(\bar{x})=\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\delta_{S}(\bar{x})\;\mbox{ for all }\;\bar{x}\in S.

Now we define, following the scheme from
[
15
]
, a counterpart of the regular constructions (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) for set-valued mappings between normed spaces. The
regular coderivative
of
F:X\rightrightarrows Y
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in X\times Y
is the multifunction
\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})\colon Y^{*}\rightrightarrows X^{*}
with the values

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*}):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits F\big)\}

(2.5)

if
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits F
, and
\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*}):=\emptyset
if

(\bar{x},\bar{y})\notin\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits F
. We omit
\bar{y}
in the coderivative notion if
F(\bar{x})
is a singleton. Observe that

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x})(y^{*})=\big\{\nabla F(\bar{x})^{*}y^{*}\big\}\;\mbox{ for all }\;y^{*}\in Y^{*}

if
F
is Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
via the adjoint derivative operator
\nabla F(\bar{x})^{*}
.

Recall that a mapping
h:D\subset X\to Y
is
upper Lipschitzian

[
26
]
at
\bar{x}\in D
or
calm

[
27
]
at this point if there exist numbers
r>0
and
\ell\geq 0

such that

\displaystyle\|h(x)-h(\bar{x})\|\leq\ell\|x-\bar{x}\|\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},r)\cap D.

(2.6)

This notion is definitely weaker than the the Lipschitz continuity of
h
around
\bar{x}
as is illustrated by the simple example of
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
h(x):=x^{3/2}\sin(1/x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
h(0):=0
. This function is calm at
\bar{x}=0
while not Lipschitz continuous around this point.

It is not difficult to deduce from the definitions (see, e.g., the proof of
[
21
, Proposition 3.5]
) that the calmness of
h\colon X\to Y
at
\bar{x}
yields the
regular coderivative scalarization formula

\widehat{D}^{*}h(\bar{x})(y^{*})=\widehat{\partial}\langle y^{*},h\rangle(\bar{x})\;\mbox{ for all }\;y^{*}\in Y^{*}

(2.7)

in any normed spaces
X
and
Y
.

Given further a set-valued mapping
F:X\rightrightarrows Y
between normed spaces, we say as in
[
7
]
that
F
enjoys the
isolated calmness
property at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits F
with modulus
L>0
if there exists
\gamma>0
such that

F(u)\cap\mathbb{B}_{Y}(\bar{y},\gamma)\subset\{\bar{y}\}+L\|u-\bar{x}\|{\mathbb{\overline{B}}}_{Y}\;\mbox{ for all }\;u\in\mathbb{B}_{X}(\bar{x},\gamma).

(2.8)

Next we formulate and discuss certain
metric qualification conditions
the importance of which has been well realized in different aspects of variational analysis, generalized differential calculus, and optimization theory; see below.

Definition 2.1

(
metric qualification conditions).

Let
C,D
be subsets of a normed space
X
, and let
\bar{x}\in C\cap D
.
It is said that these sets satisfy:

(i)

The
metric qualification condition (

Q
1

)
at
\bar{x}
if there exist
a,r>0
such that

\displaystyle d({x},C\cap D)\leq ad({x},C)+ad({x},D)\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},r),

(
Q_{1}
)

where
d(x,S)
stands for the distance from
x
to the set
S
.

(ii)

The
metric qualification condition (

Q
2

)
at
\bar{x}
with respect to some subdifferential
\partial^{\textasteriskcentered}
if there exists
a>0
such that

\displaystyle\partial^{\textasteriskcentered}d(\bar{x},C\cap D)\subset a\partial^{\textasteriskcentered}d(\bar{x},C)+a\partial^{\textasteriskcentered}d(\bar{x},D).

(
Q_{2}
)

The terminology of Definition
2.1
is taken from
[
10
]
while
such conditions are traced back to
[
11
]
in the case of (

Q
1

) and to

[
12
]
in the case of (

Q
2

). Observe that condition
(

Q
2

), in contrast to (

Q
1

), depends on the choice of the
subdifferential
\partial^{\textasteriskcentered}
. In
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
24
,
25
,
28
]
among others, the reader
can find sufficient conditions for the fulfillment of (

Q
2

) with respect to
different subdifferentials and their applications. On the other hand, the
subgradient-free condition (

Q
1

), which is easily to deal with, yields the
fulfillment of (

Q
2

) in some important subgradient settings; see, e.g.,

[
10
]
and the references therein. Furthermore, note that the proper
(

Q
1

) is also called subtransversality, linear regularity, or linear coherence
in the literature.

We conclude this section with three lemmas broadly used in what follows. The
first one, taken from
[
16
, Corollary 1.96]
, provides relationships
between regular normals to sets and regular subgradients of the associated
distance functions.

Lemma 2.2

(
normals to sets and subgradients of distance functions).

Let
S\subset X
be a nonempty subset
of a normed space. Then for every
\bar{x}\in S
we have the regular
normal-subgradient relationships

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}d(\cdot,S)(\bar{x})=\mathbb{B}_{X^{*}}\cap{\widehat{N}}(\bar{x};S)\;\mbox{ and }\;{\widehat{N}}(\bar{x},S)=\bigcup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\widehat{\partial}d(\cdot,S)(\bar{x}).

The next result is a consequence of Lemma
2.2
for the epigraphical set
S:=\epi f
with the replacement of
\bar{x}
by
(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))
.

Lemma 2.3

(
normals and subgradients for epigraphs).

Let
f\colon X\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be defined on a normed space, and let
\bar{x}\in S
. Then we have

\displaystyle\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},f(\bar{x});\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f)\big)=\bigcup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\widehat{\partial}d(\cdot,\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f)\big(\bar{x},f(\bar{x})\big).

The last lemma, taken from
[
16
, Proposition 1.88]
, provides a smooth variational description of regular subgradients for general functions on normed spaces.

Lemma 2.4

(
smooth variational description of regular subgradients).

Let
f\colon X\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be defined on a normed space
X
, and let
\bar{x}\in\dom f
. Then the inclusion
x^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})
for regular subgradients is equivalent to the existence of a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
and a function
s\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
such that
s(\cdot)
is Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
with
\nabla s(\bar{x})=x^{*}
, that
s(\bar{x})=f(\bar{x})
, and that
s(x)\leq f(x)
for all
x\in U
.

3
Estimates for Regular and Singular Regular Subgradients of Marginal Functions

In this section, we establish lower and upper estimates for regular subgradients and their singular counterparts of general marginal functions (
1.1
). Without further mentioning, all the spaces under consideration are
arbitrarily normed
.

The lower estimates for both regular and singular regular subgradients of marginal functions, new in the literature, are obtained below under the alternative conditions formulated in the following two definitions.

The first notion, used in
[
22
]
to derive equalities for regular (while not singular regular) subgradients of marginal functions, employs the upper Lipschitzian/calmness property of single-valued mappings defined in (
2.6
).

Definition 3.1

(
upper Lipschitzian selections).

A set-valued mapping
F:D\rightrightarrows Y
defined on some set

D\subset X
, admits an
upper Lipschitzian selection
at

(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits F
if there exists a single-valued mapping
h:D\to Y
,
which is calm at
\bar{x}
satisfying
h(\bar{x})=\bar{y}
and

h(x)\in F(x)
for all
x\in D
in a neighborhood of
\bar{x}
.

The next definition introduces two new well-posedness properties of set-valued mappings that are related to isolated calmness in (
2.8
).

Definition 3.2

(
nearly-isolated calmness).

Given a set-valued mapping
F:X\rightrightarrows Y
with
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits F
, consider the following properties:

(i)

F
is
locally nearly-isolated calm
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with
modulus
L>0
if there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle F(u)\cap\mathbb{B}_{Y}(\bar{y},\gamma)\subset\{\bar{y}\}+L\|u-\bar{x}\|{\mathbb{\overline{B}}}_{Y}\;\mbox{ for all }\;u\in\mathbb{B}_{X}(\bar{x},\gamma)\setminus\{\bar{x}\}.

(3.1)

(ii)

F
is
semilocally nearly-isolated calm
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with
modulus
L>0
if there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle F(u)\subset\{\bar{y}\}+L\|u-\bar{x}\|{\mathbb{\overline{B}}}_{Y}\;\mbox{
for all }\;u\in\mathbb{B}_{X}(\bar{x},\gamma)\setminus\{\bar{x}\}.

(3.2)

Clearly, the introduced properties are weaker than the isolated calmness (
2.8
) even in simple examples as for
F\colon\mathbb{R}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}

defined by
F(x):=\mathbb{R}
if
x=0
and
F(x):=\emptyset
. This mapping satisfies (
3.1
) but not (
2.8
) at
(0,0)
. It is important to note that
property (
3.2
) is a stronger condition than property (
3.1
).
For instance, consider
F\colon\mathbb{R}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}

defined by
F(x)=\{0\}
if
x=0
and
F(x)=\{1\}
. This mapping
satisfies condition (
3.1
), but it does not satisfy condition
(
3.2
) at the point
(0,0)
.

Besides, the emergence of the notion of semilocal near-isolated calmness is quite
natural because this notion is indeed connected to the notion of upper Lipschitz multifunctions, as mentioned in
[
31
]
. Semilocal near-isolated calmness represents a variant of upper Lipschitz continuity for a multifunction. Furthermore, the authors
[
31
]
have already provided criteria, similar to the Levy-Rockafellar criterion for isolated calmness (see
[
7
]
), to verify this property using the so-called outer derivative of a multifunction (see
[
31
, Theorem 3.2]
) and the contingent derivative of the multifunction (see
[
31
, Propositions 3.4, Corollaries 3.6 and 3.7]
).

Our first theorem presents both lower and upper estimates of
regular subgradients
(
2.1
) of marginal functions in the general normed space setting.

Theorem 3.3

(
estimates for regular subgradients).

Let the marginal function
\mu(\cdot)
in (
1.1
) be finite
at some
\bar{x}\in\dom M
from (
1.2
), and let
\bar{y}\in M(\bar{x})
. The
following assertions are satisfied:

(i)

If
\widehat{\partial}^{+}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\neq\emptyset
for the upper regular subdifferential (
2.2
), then

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})\subset\bigcap_{(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in\widehat{\partial}^{+}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\big[x^{\ast}+\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{\ast})\big].

(3.3)

(ii)

We have the lower subgradient estimate

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})\supset\bigcup_{(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\big[x^{\ast}+\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{\ast})\big]

(3.4)

provided that at least one of the conditions below holds:

(a)

G
is semilocally nearly-isolated calm at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

(b)

G
is locally nearly-isolated calm at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, and for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle\mu(x)=\inf_{y\in G(x)}\varphi(x,y)=\inf_{y\in G(x)\cap\mathbb{B}(\bar{y},\varepsilon)}\varphi(x,y)\;\mbox{ for }\;x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\gamma).

(3.5)

(c)

The solution mapping
M:\dom G\rightrightarrows Y
admits an upper Lipschitzian selection at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

Proof.

The upper estimate in (i) is taken
[
22
, Theorem 1]
formulated there in the Banach space setting, while the given proof does not require the completeness of the normed spaces in question. Let us now verify the lower estimate of regular subgradients in (ii) in all the three cases therein.

\bullet

Case
(a)
, where
G
is semilocally nearly-isolated calm at

(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with modulus
L
.
Fix an arbitrary number
\varepsilon>0
and pick

u^{\ast}
in the right-hand side of (
3.4
). Then there
exists
{(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})}
such that
u^{\ast}-{x}^{\ast}\in\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{\ast})
, and hence
(u^{\ast}-{x}^{\ast},-y^{\ast})\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G)
. The inclusion

Exact Calculations of Regular and Singular Regular Subdifferentials of Marginal Functions

The results of this section provide sufficient conditions ensuring

(i.e.,

for calculating both regular and singular regular subdifferentials of marginal functions. We present two theorems of this type: one for marginal functions with Fréchet differentiable costs, and the other one for fully nonsmooth data under additional qualification conditions. As in the previous section, all the spaces in question are
arbitrarily normed

The first theorem concerns the case where the cost function in (
1.1

4.1

under condition (a) formulated in Theorem

Theorem 4.1

(

\mu(\cdot)

from (

some

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})=x^{\ast}+\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{\ast})\;\mbox{ with }\;(x^{\ast},y^{\ast}):=\nabla\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}),

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\mu(\bar{x})=\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(0)

(4.2)

of Theorem

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is Fréchet differentiable at

Therefore, equality (

telling us that
[

cannot be applied.
Nevertheless, since

is (semilocally) nearly-isolated calm at

, we deduce the claimed equalities (

) and (

) from

under the nearly-isolated calmness.

The next example illustrates that neither of the assumptions of Theorem

Example 4.3

(

Let

and
(\bar{x},\bar{y})=(0,0)

This example is taken from

) holds. In this case, it is easy to see that the constraint mapping

Now we establish new calculation formulas for both regular and singular subdifferentials of marginal functions with
nondifferentiable

on the data of (

Theorem 4.4

(
marginal functions with nondifferentiable costs).

Let
\mu(\cdot)
the marginal function from (
1.1
) be finite at
some
\bar{x}\in\dom M
, and let
\bar{y}\in M(\bar{x})
.
Suppose that the following conditions are fulfilled:

(
\bf\mathcal{A}_{1}
)

The sets
{\rm epi}\,\varphi
and
\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\times\mathbb{R}
satisfy the metric qualification condition (

Q
2

) for
\partial^{\textasteriskcentered}=\widehat{\partial}

at
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
.

(
\bf\mathcal{A}_{2}
)

At least one of the conditions
(a)–(c)
of Theorem
3.4
holds.

Then we have the subdifferential calculation formulas

{\widehat{\partial}}\mu(\bar{x})=\bigcup_{(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in{\widehat{\partial}}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\big[x^{\ast}+\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{\ast})\big],

(4.3)

{\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}}\mu(\bar{x})=\bigcup_{(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in{\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\big[x^{\ast}+\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{\ast})\big].

(4.4)

Proof.

Denoting
C:=\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\varphi
and
D:=\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\times\mathbb{R}
, we intend to show that

\displaystyle(x^{*},\beta)\in\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x});\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\mu\big)\Longrightarrow(x^{*},0,\beta)\in\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}));C\cap D\big).

(4.5)

Indeed, it follows from Lemma
2.4
that there exists a
Fréchet differentiable function
s:X\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\nabla s(\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x}))=(x^{\ast},\beta)
and

s(x,r)\leq s(\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x}))=0\;\mbox{ whenever }\;(x,r)\in\text{epi}\,\mu.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and use the Fréchet differentiability of
s(\cdot)
to find

\delta_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle|s(x,r)-\langle(x^{\ast},\beta),(x-\bar{x},r-\mu(\bar{x}))\rangle\lvert\leq\varepsilon(\|x-\bar{x}\|+|r-\mu(\bar{x})|)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\,\langle(x^{\ast},\beta),(x-\bar{x},r-\mu(\bar{x}))\rangle\leq\varepsilon(\|x-\bar{x}\|+|r-\mu(\bar{x})|)

for every
(x,r)\in\mathbb{B}((\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x})),\delta_{1})
. This yields the estimate

\displaystyle\langle x^{\ast},x-\bar{x}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle-\beta,r-\mu(\bar{x})\rangle+\varepsilon(\|x-\bar{x}\|+|r-\mu(\bar{x})|)

whenever
(x,r)\in\mathbb{B}((\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x})),\delta_{1})\cap\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\mu
. Since
\mu(\bar{x})=\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
\mu(x)\leq\varphi(x,y)\leq r
for every
(x,y,r)\in\mathbb{B}((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})),\delta_{1})\cap C\cap D
, we have

\displaystyle\langle x^{\ast},x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\,\langle-\beta,r-\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\rangle+\varepsilon(\|x-\bar{x}\|+\|y-\bar{y}\|+|r-\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})|)

\displaystyle\mbox{for all }\;(x,y,r)\in\mathbb{B}((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})),\delta_{1})\cap C\cap D,

which therefore justifies the implication in (
4.5
).

To verify (
4.3
), pick
p^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})
and get
(p^{*},-1)\in\widehat{N}(\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x});\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\mu)
. By Lemma
2.3
, there exist
(x^{*},\beta)\in\widehat{\partial}d(\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x}),\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\mu)
and
\lambda>0

such that
p^{*}=\lambda x^{*}
and
-1=\lambda\beta
, where

\lambda\geq 1
by Lemma
2.2
. Since
\bar{y}\in M(\bar{x})
, we claim that

\displaystyle(x^{*},\beta)\in\widehat{\partial}d(\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x}),\text{epi}\,\mu)\Longrightarrow(x^{*},0,\beta)\in\widehat{\partial}d((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})),C\cap D).

Indeed, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle(x^{*},\beta)\in\widehat{\partial}d(\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x}),\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\mu)=\mathbb{B}_{X^{*}\times\mathbb{R}}\cap\widehat{N}((\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x}));\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\mu),

which implies that
\|(x^{*},\beta)\|\leq 1
and
(x^{*},\beta)\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},\mu(\bar{x}));\text{epi}\,\mu)
. By using (
4.5
),
we get
(x^{*},0,\beta)\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}));C\cap D)
and
\|(x^{*},0,\beta)\|\leq 1
. Employing again
Lemma
2.2
tells us that

\displaystyle(x^{*},0,\beta)\in\widehat{\partial}d((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})),C\cap D).

(4.6)

On the other hand, assumption (
\mathcal{A}_{1}
) ensures the subdifferential estimate

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}d((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})),C\cap D)

\displaystyle\subset a\big[\widehat{\partial}d((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})),C)+\widehat{\partial}d((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})),D)\big],

which yields in turn together with (
4.6
) the existence of the triples
(z^{*},y^{*},\gamma)\in\widehat{\partial}d((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})),\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\varphi)
and
(u^{*},v^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}d((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G)
satisfying

\displaystyle\hskip 59.75095pt\big[x^{*}=a(z^{*}+u^{*}),~~0=a(y^{*}+v^{*}),~~\beta=a\gamma\big]

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\;\big[p^{*}=\lambda x^{*}=\lambda a(z^{*}+u^{*}),~~\lambda ay^{*}=-\lambda av^{*},~~-1=\lambda\beta=a\lambda\gamma\big].

Therefore,
(\lambda az^{*},\lambda ay^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
,
p^{*}\in\lambda az^{*}+\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(\lambda ay^{*})
, and so

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})\subset\bigcup_{(x^{*},y^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\big[x^{*}+\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*})\big].

Unifying the latter with the opposite inclusion (
3.4
) in
Theorem
3.3
justifies the claimed equality (
4.3
) for regular subgradients of marginal functions.

It remains to verify equality (
4.4
) for singular regular subgradients. In fact, the upper singular subdifferential estimate

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\mu(\bar{x})\subset\bigcup_{(x^{*},y^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})}x^{*}+\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*})

can be justified by using the same device as above for
\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})
, while the lower estimate for
\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\mu(\bar{x})
is (
3.15
) obtained in
Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Finally in this section, we construct an example of a

Although regular subgradients and normals for convex functions and sets reduce to the classical ones of convex analysis, we will keep the hat-notation for them for notation consistency.

Example 4.5

(

Let

and
(\bar{x},\bar{y})=(0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}})

\infty&{\rm otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and consider the marginal function
\mu(\cdot)
in
(
1.1
) with the data

\emptyset&{\rm otherwise}\end{array}\right.~~~{\rm and}~~~\varphi(x,y):=f(y),\quad(x,y)\in X\times Y.

Since

\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G=\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\{0\}

[

cannot be
applied. We also are not able to use
[
1

due to

Moreover, observe that
[
20
, Theorem 4.56]
is not applicable because
\varphi
is discontinuous, the set

\mathop{\rm ri}\nolimits(\dom\varphi)\cap\mathop{\rm ri}\nolimits(\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G)=\emptyset

\displaystyle M(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\}&{\rm if}~x=0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\\

On the other hand, the direct calculations show that

\displaystyle\text{
$\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\varphi=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}$},

Observe furthermore the representation

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\widehat{N}((0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},0);\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\varphi\cap(\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\times\mathbb{R}))

\displaystyle=\widehat{N}((0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},0);\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\varphi)+\widehat{N}((0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},0);\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\times\mathbb{R}),

which ensures by
[

that assumption
({\cal A}_{1})
is satisfied. Since

clearly enjoys the semilocal nearly-isolated calmness property at

)
and (

. We can also confirm this by the direct calculations, which show that

\displaystyle\mu(x)=\inf\big\{f(y)\;\big|\;y\in G(x)\big\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&{\rm if}~x=0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\\
\infty&{\rm otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and
\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\mu(0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}})=\widehat{\partial}\mu(0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}})=\mathbb{R}^{2}
due to

\widehat{N}(0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}};\epi\mu)=\widehat{N}(0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}};\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{-},

\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\varphi(0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}})=\widehat{\partial}\varphi(0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}})=\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\;\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{*}G(0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}})(y^{*}_{1},y^{*}_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{R}^{2}&{\rm if}~y^{*}_{1}\geq 0,\\
\emptyset&{\rm otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

5
Applications to Variational Calculus

This section develops new
calculus rules as equalities
for regular subgradients and their singular counterparts in general
normed spaces
. Needless to saying that such a
variational subdifferential calculus
plays a crucial role in applications of variational analysis to structured problems of optimization and related areas.

The obtained calculus rules are mainly derived from the calculation results for marginal functions established in Section
4
with the systematical usage of the
calmness
and
metric qualification
conditions. The calculus rules for both regular and singular regular subgradients obtained below under the metric qualification conditions seem to be completely new, while some of the results for regular subgradients involving calmness (
2.6
) can be found in
[
21
]
under the more restrictive local Lipschitz continuity. Note that the chain rules of the inclusion type derived in
[
10
]
under metric qualification conditions in Asplund spaces are fully different from ours while providing upper estimates of regular and singular regular subdifferentials in terms of the limiting ones by Mordukhovich.

When the constraint mapping
G:=g:X\to Y
in (
1.1
) is
single-valued
, the marginal function
\mu(\cdot)
reduces to the (generalized) composition

\displaystyle\mu(x)=\left(\varphi\circ g\right)(x):=\varphi\big(x,g(x)\big),\quad x\in X,

(5.1)

which is the standard one
\varphi(g(x))
when
\varphi
does not depend
on
x
.

First we present chain rules for regular and singular regular subdifferentials of generalized compositions (
5.1
).

Theorem 5.1

(
subdifferentiation of generalized compositions).

Let
\varphi:X\times Y\to\mathbb{\bar{R}}
be finite at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})

with
\bar{y}:=g(\bar{x})
, and let
g:X\to Y
be calm at
\bar{x}
.
Assume that
\varphi
is Fréchet differentiable at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with

\nabla\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}):=(\nabla_{x}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}),\nabla_{y}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
. Then the following chain rules hold for generalized compositions (
5.1
):

\begin{array}[]{ll}\widehat{\partial}\left(\varphi\circ g\right)(\bar{x})&=\nabla_{x}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\widehat{D}^{*}g(\bar{x})\left(\nabla_{y}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\right)\\
&=\nabla_{x}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\widehat{\partial}\langle\nabla_{y}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}),g\rangle(\bar{x}),\end{array}

\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\left(\varphi\circ g\right)(\bar{x})=\{0\}.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
4.1
specified for (
5.1
) with observing that the calmness condition (
2.6
) imposed on
G=g
yields the semilocal near-isolated calmness in (a) of Theorem
3.4
, while ensuring the regular coderivative scalarization (
2.7
) and thus the fulfillment of both claimed formulas by (
4.2
).
∎

As consequences of Theorem
5.1
, we get the following
product and quotient rules
under the calmness assumptions.

Corollary 5.2

(
products and quotient rules).

Let the functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}:X\to\mathbb{\bar{R}}
be calm at
\bar{x}
. The following assertions hold:

(i)

\widehat{\partial}\left(\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2}\right)(\bar{x})=\widehat{\partial}(\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})
and
\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\left(\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2}\right)(\bar{x})=\{0\}
.

If in addition
\varphi_{1}
is Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
, then

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\left(\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2}\right)(\bar{x})=\nabla\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})+\widehat{\partial}\left(\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})\varphi_{2}\right)(\bar{x}).

(ii)

Assume that
\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\left(\varphi_{1}/\varphi_{2}\right)(\bar{x})=\dfrac{\widehat{\partial}\left(\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})\varphi_{2}\right)(\bar{x})}{[\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})]^{2}},\quad\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\left(\varphi_{1}/\varphi_{2}\right)(\bar{x})=\{0\}.

If in addition
\varphi_{1}
is Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
, then

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\left(\varphi_{1}/\varphi_{2}\right)(\bar{x})=\dfrac{\nabla\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})+\widehat{\partial}\left(-\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})\varphi_{2}\right)(\bar{x})}{[\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})]^{2}}.

(iii)

Let
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{\bar{R}}
be calm at
\bar{x}

with
\varphi(\bar{x})\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}(1/\varphi)(\bar{x})=-\dfrac{\widehat{\partial}^{+}\varphi(\bar{x})}{[\varphi(\bar{x})]^{2}},\quad\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\left(1/\varphi_{2}\right)(\bar{x})=\{0\}.

Proof.

To verify (i), observe that
\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2}
is represented as composition
(
5.1
) with
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
and
g:X\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by
\varphi(y_{1},y_{2}):=y_{1}\cdot y_{2}
and
g(x):=(\varphi_{1}(x),\varphi_{2}(x))
. Thus
Theorem
5.1
yields the first equalities in (i), which implies
the second one due to the elementary sum rule from
[
16
, Proposition 1.107(i)]
.

The proof of (ii) is similar with
\varphi(y_{1},y_{2}):=y_{1}/y_{2}
and the same mapping
g
in (
5.1
), while (iii) is a special
case of (ii) with
\varphi_{1}:=1
and
\varphi_{2}:=\varphi
.
∎

The next results provide new
sum rules
for both regular and singular regular subdifferentials of nonsmooth functions under the simultaneous fulfillment of the calmness and metric qualification conditions.

Theorem 5.3

(

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\colon X\to\mathbb{\bar{R}}
be
extended-real-valued functions such that
\varphi_{2}
is calm at
\bar{x}
and that
\varphi(x,y):=\varphi_{1}(x)+y
for all
(x,y)\in X\times\mathbb{R}

(\bar{x},\varphi_{2}(\bar{x}))
. Suppose in addition that the sets

and
\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits\varphi_{2}\times\mathbb{R}

Q
2

) for
\partial^{\textasteriskcentered}=\widehat{\partial}
at
(\bar{x},\varphi_{2}{(\bar{x})},\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})+\varphi_{2}(\bar{x}))
. Then we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})=\widehat{\partial}\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})+\widehat{\partial}\varphi_{2}(\bar{x}),

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})=\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}\varphi_{1}(\bar{x}).

Proof.

It follows from (
5.1
) that

\mu(x)=\left(\varphi\circ\varphi_{2}\right)(x)=\varphi(x,\varphi_{2}(x))=\varphi_{1}(x)+\varphi_{2}(x),

and thus the result follows by applying Theorem

with

∎

Theorem 5.4

(
chain rules for subdifferentials).

Let
h:Y\to\mathbb{\bar{R}}
be finite at
\bar{y}
with
\bar{y}:=g(\bar{x})
and

be calm at
\bar{x}
. Suppose that the
sets
X\times\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits h
and
\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits g\times\mathbb{R}
satisfies
condition (

Q
2

) for
\widehat{\partial}
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y},h(\bar{y}))
. Then

\displaystyle{\widehat{\partial}}\left(h\circ g\right)(\bar{x})=\bigcup_{y^{\ast}\in{\widehat{\partial}}h(\bar{y})}\widehat{\partial}\langle y^{\ast},g\rangle(\bar{x}),

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}}\left(h\circ g\right)(\bar{x})=\bigcup_{y^{\ast}\in{\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}}h(\bar{y})}\widehat{\partial}\langle y^{\ast},g\rangle(\bar{x}).

(5.3)

Proof.

For the marginal function
\mu(\cdot)
in (
1.1
), we consider

\varphi:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
defined by

\varphi(x,y):=h(y)
for every

, and

∎

To get an effective consequence of Theorem

in the case of real-valued functions

, the next lemma of its own interest is useful. The first equivalence below can be deduced from
[

while we give another,

. The second equivalence seems never been mentioned in the literature.

Lemma 5.5

(
regular normals to epigraphs and graphs).

Let
f:X\to\mathbb{\bar{R}}
be finite at
\bar{x}
, let
(\bar{x},\alpha)\in\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f
, and let
0\neq\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the following hold:

(x^{*},-\lambda)\in\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},\alpha);\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f\big)\Longleftrightarrow\lambda>0,\,\alpha=f(\bar{x}),\,x^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}(\lambda f)(\bar{x}),

(5.4)

(x^{*},-\lambda)\in\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits f)\Longleftrightarrow x^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\left(\lambda f\right)(\bar{x}\big).

(5.5)

Proof.

Observe that implication “
\Longleftarrow
” is straightforward in both cases of (
5.4
) and (
5.5
). To verify “
\Longrightarrow
” in (
5.4
), take
(x^{*},-\lambda)\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},\alpha);\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f)
and find by Lemma
2.4
a Fréchet differentiable function
s:X\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\nabla s(\bar{x},\alpha)=(x^{\ast},-\lambda)
and
s(x,r)\leq s(\bar{x},\alpha)=0
for all
(x,r)\in\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f
. It follows from the Fréchet differentiability of
s(\cdot)
that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
0<\delta_{1}
such that

|s(x,r)-\langle(x^{\ast},-\lambda),(x-\bar{x},r-\alpha)\rangle\lvert\leq\varepsilon(\|x-\bar{x}\|+|r-\alpha|)

for every
(x,r)\in\mathbb{B}((\bar{x},\alpha),\delta_{1})
. This yields

\langle(x^{\ast},-\lambda),(x-\bar{x},r-\alpha)\rangle\leq\varepsilon(\|x-\bar{x}\|+|r-\alpha|)

(5.6)

whenever
(x,r)\in\mathbb{B}((\bar{x},\alpha),\delta_{1})\cap\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f
. Note that
(\bar{x},\beta+\alpha)\in\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f
for any
\beta\geq 0
, and so for all small

\beta
we have
(\bar{x},\beta+\alpha)\in\mathbb{B}((\bar{x},\alpha),\delta_{1})\cap\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f
and
\langle\beta,-\lambda\rangle\leq\varepsilon\beta
. Thus
-\lambda\leq\varepsilon
, and hence
\lambda>0
since
\varepsilon>0
was chosen arbitrarily.

Next we show that
\alpha=f(\bar{x})
. Indeed, suppose on the contrary
that
\alpha>f(\bar{x})
. Then for any
\beta>0
satisfying
\beta<\delta_{1}
and
\alpha-\beta>f(\bar{x})
, we have
(\bar{x},\alpha-\beta)\in\mathbb{B}((\bar{x},\alpha),\delta_{1})\cap\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f
, and thus it
follows from (
5.6
) that
\lambda\beta\leq\varepsilon\beta
.
This brings us to the contradiction
\lambda\leq 0
by the arbitrary choice of
\varepsilon>0
.

To verify now implication “
\Longrightarrow
” in (
5.5
), pick
(x^{*},-\lambda)\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits f)
with
\lambda\neq 0
. It follows from the definition that for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that, whenever
x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle+\langle-\lambda,f(x)-f(\bar{x})\rangle\leq\varepsilon\left(\|x-\bar{x}\|+|f(x)-f(\bar{x})|\right).

(5.7)

To proceed further, consider the following two cases.

Case
1
, where
\lambda>0
. Suppose without loss of generality that
\varepsilon<\lambda
. For any
x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
with
(x,\alpha)\in\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f
, we
deduce from (
5.7
) that

\displaystyle\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle\lambda,\alpha-f(\bar{x})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\langle\lambda,\alpha-f(x)\rangle+\varepsilon\left(\|x-\bar{x}\|+|f(x)-f(\bar{x})|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left(\|x-\bar{x}\|+|\alpha-f(\bar{x})|\right).

This yields
(x^{*},-\lambda)\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f)
, and thus it follows from (
5.4
) that

x^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\left(\lambda f\right)(\bar{x})
, which justifies (
5.5
) in this case.

Case
2
, where
\lambda<0
.
We may assume that
\varepsilon<-\lambda
. For any
x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
with
(x,\alpha)\in\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits(-f)
, we derive from (
5.7
) that

\displaystyle\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle-\lambda,\alpha-(-f)(\bar{x})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle\lambda,\alpha-(-f)(x)\rangle+\varepsilon\left(\|x-\bar{x}\|+|f(x)-f(\bar{x})|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left(\|x-\bar{x}\|+|\alpha-(-f)(\bar{x})|\right).

This yields
(x^{*},\lambda)\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},(-f)(\bar{x}));\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits(-f))
, which implies in turn that

x^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\left(\lambda f\right)(\bar{x})
due to (
5.4
) and thus completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Finally, we are ready to derive, as a consequence of Theorem
5.4
, the desired chain rules for both regular and singular regular subdifferentials of compositions with real-valued inner functions defined on normed spaces.

Corollary 5.6

(
chain rules with real-valued inner functions).

Let
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{\bar{R}}
be finite at
\bar{y}
with

\bar{y}:=g(\bar{x})
, and let
g:X\to\mathbb{R}
be calm at

\bar{x}
. Suppose that the sets
X\times\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits h
and
\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits g\times\mathbb{R}
satisfy condition (

Q
2

) for
\widehat{\partial}

at
(\bar{x},\bar{y},h(\bar{y}))
. Then

\displaystyle~~{\widehat{\partial}}\left(h\circ g\right)(\bar{x})=\left(\bigcup_{\lambda\in{\widehat{\partial}}h(\bar{y}),\,\lambda\geq 0}\lambda\widehat{\partial}g(\bar{x})\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{\lambda\in{\widehat{\partial}}h(\bar{y}),\,\lambda<0}\lambda\widehat{\partial}^{+}g(\bar{x})\right),

(5.8)

\displaystyle{\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}}\left(h\circ g\right)(\bar{x})=\left(\bigcup_{\lambda\in{\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}}h(\bar{y}),\,\lambda\geq 0}\lambda\widehat{\partial}g(\bar{x})\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{\lambda\in{\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}}h(\bar{y}),\,\lambda<0}\lambda\widehat{\partial}^{+}g(\bar{x})\right).

(5.9)

Proof.

Observe that, by the scalarization formula (
2.7
) under the imposed calmness assumption, the subgradient set standing on the right-hand sides of (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) reduces to
D^{*}g(\bar{x})(y^{*})
with

due to

in the setting of the corollary. The coderivative definition (

) tells us that
x^{*}\in\widehat{D}^{*}g(\bar{x})(\lambda)
if and only if
(x^{*},-\lambda)\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits g)
. Consider the following two cases.

Case
1
, where
\lambda=0
. Since
g
is calm at

\bar{x}
, we have
x^{*}=0
by scalarization (

). Then the sets on the right-hand sides of (

) and (

) are obviously zeros, while the left-hand sides reduce to zeros by (

) and (

) in Theorem

.

Case

. In this case, it follows from (

that

. Using the latter inclusion and applying Theorem

with taking into account definition (

6
Bilevel Programming in Asplund Spaces

This section is mainly devoted to deriving
necessary optimality conditions
in nonsmooth models of
optimistic bilevel programming
. Developing the
marginal functions approach
to bilevel programs and having in hands the results obtained above, we invoke here the following
two major ingredients
that are new in comparison with the previous sections. The
first
one is dealing with not general normed spaces while with the class of
Asplund spaces
, which is fairly broad (containing, in particular, every reflexive Banach spaces) and has been well recognized and applied in variational analysis. The
second
major ingredient of our developments is involving, along with regular normal/subdifferential constructions, the
limiting normals and subgradients
by Mordukhovich.

Recall that a Banach space
X
is
Asplund
if any separable subspace of
X
has a separable dual. This class of spaces have been comprehensively investigated in geometric theory of Banach spaces admitting various characterizations and a great many applications to different aspects of variational analysis, optimization, and optimal control; see both volumes of
[
16
]
and the references therein. Unless otherwise stated, all the spaces considered below are assumed to be Asplund.

Next we recall the limiting generalized differential constructions for general sets and functions introduced by the third author and largely used below; see, e.g.,
[
8
,
16
,
25
,
27
,
28
]
and their bibliographies.

Definition 6.1

(
limiting normals and subgradients).

Consider a nonempty set
S\subset X
and a function
f:X\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
with
{\rm dom}\,f<\infty
. Then:

(i)

The
limiting normal cone
to
S
at
\bar{x}\in S
is defined by

\displaystyle N(\bar{x};S):=\mathop{\rm Limsup}\nolimits\limits_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}\widehat{N}({x};S).

(6.1)

(ii)

The
limiting subdifferential
of
f
at

\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}\,f
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial f(\bar{x}):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;(x^{*},-1)\in N\big((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})\big);\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f)\big\}.

(6.2)

(iii)

The
singular limiting subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}\in\dom f
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x}):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;(x^{*},0)\in N\big((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits f\big)\big\}.

(6.3)

Definition
6.1
presents geometric constructions of limiting and singular limiting subgradients. There are several equivalent representations of the subdifferentials (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) that can be founded in the aforementioned books but are not employed in what follows. It is important to emphasize that the sets in (
6.1
)–(
6.3
) are often
nonconvex
while enjoying comprehensive
calculus rules
based on
variational/extremal principles
of variational analysis.

Now we proceed with formulating the bilevel programming model of our study. The
lower-level problem
of parametric optimization is the following:

\displaystyle\min\varphi(x,y)\quad\text{subject to $y\in G(x)$},

(6.4)

where the lower-level cost function
\varphi:X\times Y\to\mathbb{\bar{R}}
depend on the decision variable
y\in Y
and parameter
x\in X
, and where
G:X\rightrightarrows Y
is the parameter-dependent constraint mapping. The
lower-level optimal value function
for (
6.4
) is the marginal function
\mu(\cdot)
defined in (
1.1
), and the corresponding
lower-level solution mapping

M(\cdot)
is taken from (
1.2
).

The
upper-level problem
is formulated as follows:

\displaystyle\min\psi(x,y)\quad\text{subject to $y\in M(x)$ for each
$x\in\Omega\subset X$},

where
\psi:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
is the upper-level cost function, and where the underlying, most involved constraint
y\in M(x)
is formed by the solution mapping
M(\cdot)
associated with the lower-level problem (
6.4
).

Using the marginal function approach, we finally define the hierarchical
optimization problem known as the
optimistic bilevel model
(see, e.g.,

[
5
,
6
,
18
,
29
,
30
]
with the discussions and references therein):

\displaystyle\min\vartheta(x)\quad\text{subject to $x\in\Omega$, where
$\vartheta(x):=\inf\left\{\psi(x,y)\,\,|\,\,y\in M(x)\right\}$}.

(6.5)

For simplicity, we consider below the case of (
6.5
) with
\Omega=X
and

\displaystyle G(x):=\big\{y\in Y\;\big|\;g_{i}(x,y)\leq 0\;\mbox{ for all }\;i=1,\ldots,p\big\}.

(6.6)

Having this in mind, we can rewrite (
6.5
) in the following form:

\displaystyle\min\psi(x,y)\quad\text{subject to $g_{i}(x,y)\leq 0$, $i=1,\ldots,p$, and $\varphi(x,y)\leq\mu(x)$,}

(6.7)

where the marginal function
\mu(\cdot)
is intrinsically nonsmooth and generally nonconvex even for nice initial data
\varphi
,
\psi
, and
g_{i}
. It has been realized that standard constraint qualifications fail to fulfill in optimal solutions of (
6.7
). In this setting, a new constraint qualification was introduced in
[
30
]
under the name of “partial calmness”. To define it, consider the
perturbed
version of (
6.7
) given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\psi(x,y)\quad\text{subject to $g_{i}(x,y)\leq 0$, $i=1,\ldots,p$,}\\
\text{and $\varphi(x,y)-\mu(x)+\nu=0$ as $\nu\in\mathbb{R}$.}\end{array}

(6.8)

Definition 6.2

(
partial calmness).

Problem (
6.7
) is
partially calm
at its local optimal solution

(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with modulus
\kappa>0
if there exists a neighborhood

U
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y},0)\in X\times Y\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)-\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\kappa|\nu|\geq 0

for all the triples
(x,y,\nu)\in U
feasible to (
6.8
).

We refer the reader to
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
30
]
for various explicit conditions ensuring partial calmness. The essence of partial calmness is the possibility of
exact penalization
of the troublesome constraint
\varphi(x,y)\leq\mu(x)
in (
6.7
), which was observed in
[
30
]
in finite-dimensional spaces. The following statement is taken from
[
18
, Proposition 7.8.12]
, where the proof holds in general normed spaces.

Lemma 6.3

(
exact penalization).

Let (
6.7
) be partially calm at its local optimal
solution
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with modulus
\kappa>0
, and let
\psi
be
continuous at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. Then
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a local optimal
solution to the penalized problem

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\min\psi(x,y)+\kappa\big(\varphi(x,y)-\mu(x)\big)\\
&{\rm subject\;to}\;g_{i}(x,y)\leq 0,\;i=1,\ldots,p.\end{array}

The next lemma, valid in normed spaces, is taken from
[
21
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Lemma 6.4

(
optimality conditions for difference functions).

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}:X\to\mathbb{\bar{R}}
be functions
finite at
\bar{x}
, and let
\widehat{\partial}\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
. Then any local minimizer
\bar{x}
of the difference function

\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
satisfies the regular subdifferential inclusion

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})\subset\widehat{\partial}\varphi_{2}(\bar{x}).

(6.9)

The last lemma here, taken from
[
16
, Theorems 3.8 and
3.86]
, gives us a useful representation of limiting normals to sets defined by inequality constraints.

Lemma 6.5

(
limiting normals to inequality constraints).

Let
g_{i}\colon X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
be locally Lipschitzian around
({\bar{x}},\bar{y})
, and let
G_{i}:=\{(x,y)\in X\times Y\,\,|\,\,g_{i}(x,y)\leq 0\}
for all
i=1,\ldots,p
. Denote
I(\bar{x},\bar{y}):=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}\,\,|\,\,g_{i}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0\}
and impose the constraint
qualification condition

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\lambda_{i}(x^{*}_{i},y^{*}_{i})=0,\,(x^{*}_{i},y^{*}_{i})\in\partial g_{i}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),\,\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\,i\in I(\bar{x},\bar{y})\Big]\\
\Longrightarrow\big[\lambda_{i}=0\;\mbox{ for all }\;i\in I(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big].\end{array}

(6.10)

Then for any
(x^{*},y^{*})\in N\left((\bar{x},\bar{y});\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}G_{i}\right)

we have the inclusion

\displaystyle(x^{*},y^{*})\in\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\lambda_{i}\partial g_{i}(\bar{x},\bar{y})

with
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
whenever
i\in I(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

Now we are ready to derive necessary optimality conditions for optimistic bilevel programs in arbitrary Asplund spaces.

Theorem 6.6

(
necessary optimality conditions for bilevel programs).

Let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
be a local optimal solution to the bilevel program (
6.7
), where
\varphi
is lower semicontinuous while
g_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,p
are locally Lipschitzian around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, where
\psi
is Fréchet differentiable at this point, and where (
6.7
) is partially calm at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with modulus
\kappa>0
. Suppose in addition that the constraint qualification (
6.10
) holds, that

\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
, and that

(\cal{A}
)

the sets
{\rm epi}\,\varphi
and

\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\times\mathbb{R}
for
G
from (
6.6
) satisfy the metric qualification condition (

Q
2

) for
\partial^{\textasteriskcentered}=\partial
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
.

Then for each
u^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})
, there exist
multipliers
\lambda_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
with
i=1,\ldots,p
such that we have the following necessary optimality conditions:

\displaystyle(u^{*},0)\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\beta_{i}\partial g_{i}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),

(6.11)

\displaystyle(u^{*},0)\in\kappa^{-1}\nabla\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\partial\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}\partial g_{i}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),

(6.12)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i},\beta_{i}\geq 0,\quad\lambda_{i}g_{i}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\beta_{i}g_{i}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0\;\mbox{ as }\;i=1,\ldots,p.

(6.13)

Proof.

From Lemma
6.3
and the infinite constraint penalization via the
indicator function
\delta(\cdot;\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G)
, we get that the pair
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
be a local optimal solution to the single-objective unconstrained problem

\displaystyle\min\big[\psi(x,y)+\kappa\big(\varphi(x,y)-\mu(x)\big)+\delta(\cdot,\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G)\big].

(6.14)

Using the necessary optimality condition (
6.9
) from
Lemma
6.4
for the difference function in (
6.14
) under
\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
together with the elementary sum rule for regular subgradients in

[
16
, Proposition 1.107(i)]
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\big(\kappa\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x}),0\big)&\subset\nabla\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\widehat{\partial}\big(\kappa\varphi(\cdot)+\delta(\cdot,\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G)\big)(\bar{x},\bar{y})\\
&\subset\nabla\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\kappa{\partial}\big(\varphi(\cdot)+\delta(\cdot,\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G)\big)(\bar{x},\bar{y}).\end{array}

(6.15)

Moreover, recalling that
\mu(x)=\inf_{y\in Y}(\varphi(\cdot)+\delta(\cdot,\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G))(x,y)
and then applying
[
25
, Theorem 4.47]
tell us that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\big(\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x}),0\big)&\subset\widehat{\partial}\big(\varphi(\cdot)+\delta(\cdot,\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G)\big)(\bar{x},\bar{y})\\
&\subset{\partial}\big(\varphi(\cdot)+\delta(\cdot,\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G)\big)(\bar{x},\bar{y}).\end{array}

(6.16)

Observing that
\partial\delta(s;S)=N(s;S)
for any set
S
with
s\in S
and using

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits(\varphi+\delta_{\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G})=\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\varphi\cap[\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\times\mathbb{R}]\quad\text{and}\quad(\varphi+\delta_{\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G})(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}),

we therefore arrive at the equality

\displaystyle N\big((\bar{x},\bar{y},(\varphi+\delta_{\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G})(\bar{x},\bar{y}));\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\varphi\cap[\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\times\mathbb{R}]\big)

\displaystyle=N\big((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}));\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\varphi\cap[\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\times\mathbb{R}]\big).

Since assumption
(\cal A)
is satisfied, the result of
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
ensures that

\displaystyle N\big((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}));\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\varphi\cap[\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\times\mathbb{R}]\big)

\displaystyle=N\big((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big);\mathop{\rm epi}\nolimits\varphi)+N\big((\bar{x},\bar{y},\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}));\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\times\mathbb{R}\big).

Combining the latter with (
6.15
) and (
6.16
) brings us to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\big(\kappa\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x}),0\big)&\subset\nabla\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\kappa{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+N\big((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\big),\\
\big(\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x}),0\big)&\subset{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})+N\big((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mathop{\rm gph}\nolimits G\big).\end{cases}

Employing finally Lemma
6.5
under the assumed constraint qualification (
6.10
), we readily get the existence of
u^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})
together with multipliers
\lambda_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
satisfying all the conditions in (
6.11
)–(
6.13
) and thus complete the proof.
∎

Now we present two remarks comparing Theorem
6.6
with previous results in this direction and discussing the major assumptions imposed.

Remark 6.7

(
discussions on optimality conditions).

Several types of necessary optimality conditions for optimistic bilevel programs have been obtained in the literature; see, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
,
17
,
18
,
30
]
with further references and discussions. The optimality conditions derived therein are all in finite-dimensional spaces, while Theorem
6.6
is established in general Asplund space setting. The closest statement to Theorem
6.6
is given in
[
18
, Theorem 7.9.3]
, where the same necessary optimality conditions (
6.11
)–(
6.13
) are also obtained while under essentially weaker assumptions even in finite dimensions. Indeed, we do not require here, in contrast to
[
18
]
, that the lower-level cost
\varphi
is locally Lipschitzian and that the solution mapping
M
is inner semicontinuous. Moreover, our assumption (
{\cal A}
) significantly improves the qualification conditions in
[
18
, Theorem 7.9.3]
. Observe finally that Theorem
6.6
is free of SNC/PSNC assumptions (see
[
16
]
), which are usually imposed in deriving necessary optimality conditions for non-Lipschitzian infinite-dimensional problems.

The next remark discusses the two major assumptions of Theorem
6.6
.

Remark 6.8

(
discussing major assumptions).

(i)

The metric qualification condition (

Q
2

) for limiting subgradients has been well recognized and largely employed in variational analysis and optimization; in particular, it significantly improves other qualification conditions used in subdifferential calculus and optimization theory; see,
e.g.,
[
10
,
11
,
24
,
25
,
28
]
and compare with
[
16
]
.

(ii)

Regarding the condition
\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
, observe first that this assumption holds when
\mu
is convex, but it is way too much to assume. In particular, it follows from
[
25
, Propositions 4.4 and 4.6]
that if
X
is finite-dimensional and
\mu(\cdot)
is
tangentially convex
as in
[
25
]
, then we have
\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset

if and only if
the function
\mu(\cdot)
is
calm
at
\bar{x}
in the sense of (
2.6
). Furthermore, the
lower regularity
of
\mu(\cdot)
at
\bar{x}
, in the sense that
\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})=\partial\mu(\bar{x})
, ensures the nonemptiness of
\widehat{\partial}\mu(\bar{x})
in Asplund spaces under a certain mild SNC property; see
[
16
, Corollar 2.24]
. The latter class of functions is sufficiently broad including, e.g., maximum, semiconvex, lower-
{\cal C}^{1}
functions, etc.; see the book
[
28
]
with many references therein.

We conclude this section with the following simplification of Theorem
6.6
in the case of bilevel programs with differentiable data. Recall that a function
f\colon Z\to\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}
defined on a normed space
Z
is
strictly differentiable
at
\bar{z}
with the strict derivative
\nabla f(\bar{z})
if

\lim_{z,u\to\bar{z}}\frac{f(z)-f(u)-\langle\nabla f(\bar{z}),z-u\rangle}{\|z-u\|}=0.

It is well known that this notion lies between Fréchet differentiable of
f
at
\bar{z}
and continuous differentiability of

7
Conclusions

The paper provides a systematic study of regular subgradients and their singular counterparts for a general class of marginal functions defined on normed spaces with applications to variational calculus rules for such constructions. Further applications are given, together with using the corresponding limiting normals and subgradients, to deriving necessary optimality conditions for the optimistic model of bilevel programming in Asplund spaces via the marginal function approach. Although our developments are given in infinite-dimensional spaces, the obtained results are new in finite dimensions. In the future research, we will continue the lines of this study with applying the newly introduced nearly-isolated calmness properties to other aspects of variational analysis and optimization and aiming, in particular, at the pessimistic model of bilevel programming.

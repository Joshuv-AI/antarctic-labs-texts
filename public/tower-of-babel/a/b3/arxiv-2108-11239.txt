Three-body model of the d + A d+A system in an antisymmetrised, translationally invariant many nucleon theory
R.C. Johnson

Abstract
This paper concerns the theory of direct A ⁡ ( d , p ) ​ B A(d,p)B reactions and how practical 3-body models of them are related to the optical model potentials describing the interaction of the deuterons constituents and the target A A . A new definition of the nucleon optical potential is introduced which necessitates a careful examination of the boundary conditions involved in the associated coupled channel formalism. The new definition turns out to be very convenient for making a connection with the theory of the A ⁡ ( d , p ) ​ B A(d,p)B reaction. The analysis is carried out within an antisymmetrised, translationally invariant formalism and provides a firm basis from a many-body point of view for the three-body force effects calculated in [Physical Review C 99 064612(2019)].

Introduction

Recent work
Johnson17

II
Definition of the nucleon optical potential in terms of overlap functions.

In this section it is shown how a nucleon optical potential can be defined within an antisymmetrised, translationally invariant many-body theory using the concept of overlap functions. In order to keep the formalism within reasonable bounds of complexity it will be assumed that all Coulomb forces are screened at large distances, that the
n-p
mass difference can be neglected and that only two-nucleon interactions are present in the many-nucleon Hamiltonian. None of these assumptions introduce unsurmountable difficulties. The modifications needed when 3-nucleon forces are included are indicated where relevant. In general, the notations of
Johnson17
and
Johnson19
, and more recently
Birse2020
, will be used.

A set of one-body overlap function associated with
\mid\Psi\rangle\rangle
, an N-nucleon state in Fock space, is defined by the expression

\displaystyle\langle\langle\psi_{n}\mid a_{i}\mid\Psi\rangle\rangle,

(1)

where the
\mid\psi_{n}\rangle\rangle
are a complete set of
(N-1)
-body states and
a_{i}
destroys a nucleon in a one-body state
\mid i\rangle
with quantum numbers
i
.
Knowledge of a set of these overlap functions is sufficient to determine many properties of the state
\mid\Psi\rangle\rangle
. Note that the states
a^{\dagger}_{i}\mid\psi_{n}\rangle\rangle
are not orthonormal so that the determination of
\mid\Psi\rangle\rangle
itself in terms of them requires the inversion of a matrix of their overlaps. Ways of dealing with this step in the context of nucleon optical model theory are discussed in detail in
Birse2020
. However, as emphasised in
Johnson19
, for example, the optical potential is not unique. The definition used here does not require the inversion of overlap matrix. It will be shown that this step is not necessary for a complete discussion of an optical model operator and the on-shell elastic transition operator, although the price to be paid is that the complete
N+A
scattering wavefunction is not available directly. The new definition, which differs from the definitions used in
Johnson19
, turns out to be very convenient for the discussion of the
n+p+A
system.

A Fock-space scattering state
\mid\Psi_{E,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}^{\epsilon}\rangle\rangle
relevant to the scattering of a nucleon by an
A
-nucleon target nucleus in its ground state is defined by

\displaystyle(E-H+\imath\epsilon)\mid\Psi_{E,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}^{\epsilon}\rangle\rangle=\imath\epsilon(2\pi)^{3/2}a^{\dagger}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}\mid-\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle\rangle.

(2)

where
H
is the many-nucleon Hamiltonian operator in Fock-space
Johnson17
.

In the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
the ket
\mid\Psi_{E,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}^{\epsilon}\rangle\rangle
describes an antisymmetric
(A+1)
-nucleon scattering state of total momentum zero in the overall c.m. system. The incident channel has an incident nucleon with momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}
. In this channel the
A
-nucleon target has a total momentum
-\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}
and is in its intrinsic ground state
\psi_{0}
. All other channel components of
\mid\Psi_{E,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}^{\epsilon}\rangle\rangle

(2\pi)^{3/2}
arises because

creates a normalised plane wave state, whereas scattering states are conventionally normalised to an incoming plane wave of unit amplitude. Explicit references to spin and isospin degrees of freedom are omitted.

By taking the inner product of both sides of eq.(
2

a^{\dagger}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}\mid-\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\psi_{n}\rangle\rangle
, with
n,\mbox{\boldmath$k$},
arbitrary, a set of inhomogeneous coupled equations can be derived for the overlap functions

\langle\langle-\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\psi_{n}\mid a_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}\mid\Psi_{E,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}^{\epsilon}\rangle\rangle

H

\mid\Psi(n,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0\rangle\rangle

n

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0
. These basis states are defined in eqs.(5)-(8) of
Johnson19

Johnson17

Johnson19

\displaystyle\int d\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1}\sum_{n^{\prime}}((E+\imath\epsilon-E_{n}-\frac{(A+1)}{A}\epsilon_{k})\delta(\mbox{\boldmath$k$}-\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1})\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}-\langle\mbox{\boldmath$k$},n\mid\hat{\mathcal{U}}\mid\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1},n^{\prime}\rangle)\langle\langle\Psi(n^{\prime},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0)\mid a_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1}}\mid\Psi_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\imath\epsilon(2\pi)^{3/2}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{n,\mbox{\boldmath$k$};0,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}.

(3)

where

A

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{n,\mbox{\boldmath$k$};0,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}=\langle\langle\Psi(n,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0)\mid a_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}a^{\dagger}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}\mid-\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle\rangle.

(4)

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i,j}\langle i\mid T\mid j\rangle a_{i}^{\dagger}a_{j}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j.k.l}\langle i,j\mid V_{\mathcal{A}}\mid k,l\rangle a_{i}^{\dagger}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{l}a_{k},

\displaystyle\mid i,j\rangle_{(a,b)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mid i\rangle_{(a)}\mid j\rangle_{(b)}-\mid j\rangle_{(a)}\mid i\rangle_{(b)}).

(6)

The coupling potential
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$k$},n\mid\hat{\mathcal{U}}\mid\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1},n^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\mbox{\boldmath$k$},n\mid\hat{\mathcal{U}}\mid\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1},n^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int d\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{2}\int d\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{4}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{2}\mid V_{\mathcal{A}}\mid\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{4}\rangle\langle\langle\Psi(n,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0)\mid a^{\dagger}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{2}}a_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{4}}\mid-\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1},\psi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle

(7)

where, in the notation of
Johnson19

\mathcal{V}(\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1})

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1})=\int d\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{2}\int d\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{4}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{2}\mid V_{\mathcal{A}}\mid\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{4}\rangle a^{\dagger}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{2}}a_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{4}}.

(8)

If a three-body force of the general form given in eq.(12) of

It can be seen from eqs.(

II.1
Introduction of
B
-space.

It is convenient here to recall the concept of
B
-space introduced following eq.(58) of
Johnson19
.
B
-space consists of
A
-nucleons in states
\psi_{n}
that interact with a fictitious particle of reduced mass
\frac{Am}{(A+1)}
but are not identical to it. A basis in this space is, by definition, an orthonormal set of states
\mid\mbox{\boldmath$k$},n\rangle,n=0,\dots,\infty,\,\,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}
arbitrary, where the fictitious particle has momentum
k
and the
A
target nucleons are in an intrinsic state
n
and have a total momentum
-\mbox{\boldmath$k$}
. The interaction between the fictitious particle and the target is described by an operator
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
in this extended space with matrix elements defined by eq.(
7
). The ”hat” notation over a quantity will be use to denote operators in
B
-space, as opposed to the bare-headed operators of Fock-space.

The Hamiltonian of the system ”fictitious particle+
A
target nucleons” is

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}=\hat{T}+\hat{h}_{A}+\hat{\mathcal{U}},

(9)

where
\hat{T}
is the kinetic energy operator associated with a particle of mass
\frac{A}{(A+1)}m
and
\hat{h}_{A}
is diagonal in the
\mid\mbox{\boldmath$k$},n\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{\prime},n^{\prime}\mid\hat{h}_{A}\mid\mbox{\boldmath$k$},n\rangle=\delta(\mbox{\boldmath$k$}-\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{\prime})\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}E_{n}.

(10)

Using these concepts the coupled equations (

\displaystyle(E+\imath\epsilon-\hat{\mathcal{H}})\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle=\imath\epsilon\hat{\mathcal{K}}(2\pi)^{3/2}\mid 0,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}\rangle,

(11)

where
\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle
is a ket in
B
-space with components

\displaystyle\langle\mbox{\boldmath$k$},n\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle=\langle\langle\Psi(n,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0)\mid a_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}\mid\Psi_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle\rangle.

II.2
Interpretation of the coupled equations. The optical model wave function.

In
Johnson17
, the optical model wave function,
\xi_{E,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}^{\epsilon}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})
, corresponding to the elastic scattering of a nucleon of momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}
in the overall c.m. system by an
A

\displaystyle\xi_{E,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}^{\epsilon}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})=\langle\langle\Psi(0,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0)\mid\psi(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})\mid\Psi_{E,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}^{\epsilon}\rangle\rangle.

(13)

The operator
\psi(\mbox{\boldmath$r$})
destroys a nucleon at a point labelled

\displaystyle\xi_{E,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}^{\epsilon}(\mbox{\boldmath$k$})=\langle\langle\Psi(0,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0)\mid a_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}\mid\Psi_{E,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}}^{\epsilon}\rangle\rangle.

(14)

Comparing with the definition (

11

(1) The full effects of antisymmetrisation and recoil are included in the coupling potential
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
defined in eq.(
7
) through direct and exchange matrix elements of the nucleon-nucleon interaction
V
and the the occurrence in eq.(
7

\langle\langle\Psi(n,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0)\mid a^{\dagger}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{2}}a_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{4}}\mid-\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1},\psi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle
in a mixed basis with a localised target centre-of-mass in the bra and a definite target centre-of-mass momentum in the ket.

(2) The non-standard form of the source term
\imath\epsilon\hat{\mathcal{K}}(2\pi)^{3/2}\mid 0,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}\rangle
that appears on the right in eq.(
11
). Standard scattering theory would have a source term

\hat{\mathcal{K}}=1

\displaystyle(E+\imath\epsilon-\hat{\mathcal{H}})\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle=\imath\epsilon(2\pi)^{3/2}\mid 0,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}\rangle.

(15)

In the 3-body case, i.e.,

Feshbach’s theory of the optical potential applied to

In this Section the Feshbach

In the notation of eq.(
9
)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}=\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}+\hat{\mathcal{U}},

(16)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=\hat{T}+\hat{h}_{A}.

(17)

The objective of the following algebra is to derive uncoupled equations for the components
P_{0}\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle
and

Q_{0}\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle

\mid\mbox{\boldmath$k$},0\rangle

B
-space with the target is in its ground state,
n=0
, and
Q_{0}
projects onto the orthogonal sub-space of states with

Using these projection operators, eq.(

\displaystyle(E+\imath\epsilon-\hat{\mathcal{H}_{0}}-P_{0}\hat{\mathcal{U}}P_{0})P_{0}\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle=

\displaystyle\imath\epsilon(2\pi)^{3/2}\mid 0,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0}\rangle+P_{0}\hat{\mathcal{U}}Q_{0}\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle,

\displaystyle(E+\imath\epsilon-\hat{\mathcal{H}_{0}})Q_{0}\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle=

\displaystyle Q_{0}\hat{\mathcal{U}}Q_{0}\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle+Q_{0}\hat{\mathcal{U}}P_{0}\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle.

\displaystyle(E+\imath\epsilon-\hat{\mathcal{H}_{0}}-P_{0}\hat{\mathcal{U}}P_{0}-P_{0}\hat{\mathcal{U}}Q_{0}\frac{1}{(E+\imath\epsilon-\hat{\mathcal{H}_{0}}-Q_{0}\hat{\mathcal{U}}Q_{0})})Q_{0}\hat{\mathcal{U}}P_{0}\mid\hat{\Psi}_{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{0},0}^{(\epsilon)}\rangle=

(19)

III.1
Deuteron creation and destruction operators.

Following Villars
Villars1967
, the operator that creates a deuteron with total momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}}
and intrinsic angular momentum projection
M_{d_{0}}=\pm 1,0
is

\displaystyle A^{\dagger}_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}},M_{d_{0}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int d\,1d\,2\langle 1,2\mid d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}},M_{d_{0}}\rangle a^{\dagger}_{1}a^{\dagger}_{2},

(23)

where

denote possible values of a set of space, intrinsic spin (
\sigma
) and isospin(
\tau
) coordinates and the
a_{i},a^{\dagger}_{j},
are nucleon operators.. The function
\langle 1,2\mid d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}},M_{d_{0}}\rangle
describes the deuteron ground state with total momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}}
. In a basis in which the space degree of freedom is chosen to be nucleon position, it has the form

\displaystyle\langle 1,2\mid d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}},M_{d_{0}}\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\exp(\imath\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}}.((\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{1}+\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{2})/2)\phi_{d_{0},M_{d_{0}}}((\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{1}-\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{2}),\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\chi_{0,0}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}).

(24)

A destruction operator corresponding to eq.(
23
) is given by

\displaystyle A_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}^{\prime}_{d_{0}},M^{\prime}_{d_{0}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int d\,3d\,4(\langle 3,4\mid d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}^{\prime}_{d_{0}},M^{\prime}_{d_{0}}\rangle)^{*}a_{4}a_{3},

(25)

where the ordering of the labels
3,\,4
should be noted.

The states
A^{\dagger}_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}},M_{d_{0}}}\mid 0\rangle\rangle
are normalised so that

\displaystyle\langle\langle 0\mid A_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K^{\prime}_{d_{0}}$},M^{\prime}_{d_{0}}}A^{\dagger}_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}},M_{d_{0}}}\mid 0\rangle\rangle=\delta(\mbox{\boldmath$K$}^{\prime}_{d_{0}}-\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}})\delta_{M_{d_{0}},M^{\prime}_{d_{0}}},

(26)

where
\mid 0\rangle\rangle
is the nucleon vacuum.

Note that

\displaystyle[A_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K^{\prime}_{d_{0}}$},M^{\prime}_{d_{0}}},A_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}},M_{d_{0}}}]_{-}=[A^{\dagger}_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K^{\prime}_{d_{0}}$},M^{\prime}_{d_{0}}},A^{\dagger}_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}},M_{d_{0}}}]_{-}=0,

(27)

as expected if boson creation and destruction operators were involved. These follow trivially from
[a_{4}a_{3},a_{1}a_{2}]_{=}0
, etc. However the other boson commutation relation is not generally valid, i.e.,

\displaystyle[A_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K^{\prime}_{d_{0}}$},M^{\prime}_{d_{0}}},A^{\dagger}_{d_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}},M_{d_{0}}}]_{-}\neq\delta(\mbox{\boldmath$K$}^{\prime}_{d_{0}}-\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d_{0}})\delta_{M^{\prime}_{d_{0}},M_{d_{0}}.}

(28)

The equality sign is only valid when the the pair of two-nucleon systems are in a state where they are spatially separated by distances greater than the range of nucleon-nucleon forces or when acting on the vacuum. The correct value of the anticommutator is given in the Appendix C.

III.2
Generalisation to include
n-p
break-up states.

The above analysis is easily generalised to include continuum states of the two-nucleon system. A complete set of antisymmetrised states
\mid d(a,b)\rangle
of two nucleons labelled
a
and
b
and definite total momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d}
is introduced. The label
d
ranges over all possible values, continuum and discrete, of a complete set of eigenvalues needed to specify eigenstates of the two-nucleon Hamiltonian. Among these states will be the deuteron ground state and a set of nucleon-nucleon scattering states. The labels
d,d^{\prime},\dots,
will be used to designate any of these state, with
d_{0}
used if the deuteron ground state is meant. The states
\mid d(a,b)\rangle
will be assumed to be orthonormalised and complete, i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle d(a,b)\mid d^{\prime}(a,b)\rangle=\delta_{d,d^{\prime}},\,\,\,\,\sum_{d}\mid d(a,b)\rangle\langle d(a,b)\mid=\mathcal{I},

(29)

where
\mathcal{I}
is the unit operator in the space of antisymmetrised two-nucleon states.

The generalisation of eq.(

\bar{B}

It is convenient to introduce the concept of

A basis in this space is, by definition, an orthonormal set of states

\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d}

The constituents of the fictitious particle in the

\bar{B}

\mathcal{V}

includes knock-on exchange effects arising from the identity of the nucleons in the target and nucleons in the fictitious particle. These relations are reproduced here for convenience.

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{U}}_{d,n;d^{\prime},n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\int\,d1\,d2\,d3\,\delta(\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1}+\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{3}-\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d})\delta(\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{1}+\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{2}-\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d^{\prime}})

(56)

\displaystyle=\bar{T}+\bar{h}_{A}+\bar{h}_{2}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{0}+\bar{\mathcal{U}},

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=\bar{T}+\bar{h}_{A}+\bar{h}_{2},

(58)

\bar{h}_{2}

\mid d,n\rangle

and
\epsilon_{d}

\displaystyle\langle d^{\prime},n^{\prime}\mid(\bar{h}_{A}+\bar{h}_{2})\mid d,n\rangle=\delta_{d^{\prime},d}\delta_{n^{\prime},n}(E_{n}+\epsilon_{d}).

(59)

The coupled equations (

\bar{B}

in the model Hamiltonian

is given by (eq.(

It is clear from this expression that

(62)

The kinematics involved in the
(A+2)
-nucleon and the

-nucleon problems differ in detail and make the relation between the interactions that appear in the three-body model and nucleon optical potentials more complicated (see Appendix 1 of

\displaystyle\sum_{d^{\prime},d}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\mid\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d^{\prime}},d^{\prime}\rangle\bar{\mathcal{U}}_{d^{\prime},n^{\prime}:d,n}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$K$}_{d},d\mid

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{U}}_{n^{\prime},n}

\displaystyle=\int\,d1\,d2\,d3\,d\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\mid 1(a),3(b)\rangle\langle 1(a),2(b)\mid\int d\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\exp(-\imath\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$P$}}.\mbox{\boldmath$x$})\langle\langle\Psi(n^{\prime},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0)\mid\mathcal{V}(3,2)\mid\Psi(n,\mbox{\boldmath$x$})\rangle\rangle

In a momentum basis for the one-nucleon states the operator

IV.4
Deuteron elastic and inelastic scattering, elastic and inelastic deuteron break-up.

Using the results of eqs.(

The solution to this equation is

\displaystyle\langle K_{d_{0}}\mid V_{d_{0}}\mid K^{\prime}_{d_{0}}\rangle=\langle K_{d_{0}},d_{0},0\mid\bar{U}^{(d_{0},0)}_{\mathrm{eff}}\mid K^{\prime}_{d_{0}}d_{0},0\rangle

\displaystyle\bar{U}^{(d_{0},0)}_{\mathrm{eff}}=(\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA})+(\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA})\frac{1}{(E+\imath\epsilon-\bar{T}-\bar{h}_{A}-\bar{h}_{2})}\bar{Q}^{d_{0}}_{0}\bar{U}^{(d_{0},0)}_{\mathrm{eff}},

V
Application to 3-body models of
A(d,p)B
reactions.

Three-body models of the
n+p+A
system are based on an effective interaction that differs from
\bar{U}^{(d_{0},0)}_{\mathrm{eff}}
of eq.(
78
),
TJ21
. The
\bar{Q}^{d_{0}}_{0}
space includes elastic deuteron break-up, whereas 3-body deuteron stripping models treat these and their coupling to the elastic deuteron channel explicitly. Accordingly, the correct effective interaction that describes the couplings between these states that begin and end with the target in its ground state is
\langle d,0\mid\bar{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}\mid d^{\prime}0\rangle
where
\bar{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\bar{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}=(\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA})+(\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA})\frac{1}{(E+\imath\epsilon-\bar{T}-\bar{h}_{A}-\bar{h}_{2})}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{U}_{\mathrm{eff}},

(79)

and where
\bar{Q}_{0}
projects onto states
\mid d,n\rangle
which include all
d
states and target states with
n\neq 0
. By construction,
\langle d,0\mid\bar{U}^{(d,0)}_{\mathrm{eff}}\mid d^{\prime},0\rangle
is an operator in the space of the two nucleons in
d
space and in the space of degrees of freedom associated with the target intrinsic ground state. This description is readily generalised to treat a finite number of excited target states on the same footing as the ground state and exclude them from
\bar{Q}^{d}_{0}
.

The operators
\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}
and
\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}
each involve the interaction of one of the nucleons in the
d
pair with the target nucleons. As such they describe the same physical processes as the interaction
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
defined in eq.(
7
) and appears in the expression (
22
) for the nucleon+
A
optical potential, but here written in a form appropriate for a non-local, translationally and rotationally invariant interaction in the 3-body context.

V.1
Relation of the 3-body effective interaction
\bar{U}^{(d,0)}_{\mathrm{eff}}
to nucleon optical potentials.

It is straight forward to separate out the
PA
and NA components together with their the target excitation contributions to
\bar{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}
, eq.(
79
) using standard multiple scattering manipulations.

Following
Tim14
, Appendix 2, the operators
\bar{V}^{PA}
and
\bar{V}^{NA}
are defined by

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{PA}=

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}\bar{V}^{PA},

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{NA}=

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}\bar{V}^{NA}.

(80)

where

\displaystyle\bar{G}_{0}=\frac{1}{(E+\imath\epsilon-\bar{T}-\bar{h}_{A}-\bar{h}_{2})}.

(81)

The contributions of
\bar{V}^{PA}
and
\bar{V}^{NA}
to
\bar{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}
are most simply obtained by first defining the subsidiary operators
\bar{W}^{NA}
and
\bar{W}^{PA}
through the coupled equations

\displaystyle\bar{W}^{PA}=

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{PA}+\bar{V}^{PA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}\bar{W}^{NA},

\displaystyle\bar{W}^{NA}=

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{NA}+\bar{V}^{NA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}\bar{W}^{PA}.

(82)

The definitions (
80
) give

\displaystyle(1-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0})\bar{V}^{PA}=

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA},

\displaystyle(1-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0})\bar{V}^{NA}=

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}.

(83)

Multiplying the first and second equations in eq.(
82
) by, respectively,
1-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}
and
1-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}
, adding the results and using (
83
) gives

\displaystyle(1-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0})\bar{W}^{PA}+(1-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0})\bar{W}^{NA}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=(\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA})+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}\bar{W}^{NA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}\bar{W}^{PA}

(84)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=(\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA})+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}(\bar{W}^{NA}+\bar{W}^{PA})+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}(\bar{W}^{PA}+\bar{W}^{NA})

\displaystyle-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}\bar{W}^{PA}-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}\bar{Q}_{0}\bar{G}_{0}\bar{W}^{NA}.

Hence

\displaystyle(\bar{W}^{NA}+\bar{W}^{PA})=(\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA})+(\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}+\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA})\bar{G}_{0}\bar{Q}_{0}(\bar{W}^{NA}+\bar{W}^{PA}),

(85)

and a comparison with eq.(
79
) shows that

\displaystyle\bar{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}=(\bar{W}^{PA}+\bar{W}^{NA}).

(86)

Eqs.(
86
), (
82
) and (
80
) provide a method for expressing
\bar{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}
in terms of the separate contributions from
\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{PA}
and
\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{NA}
.

The operators
\bar{V}^{PA}
and
\bar{V}^{NA}
show a strong resemblance to the operator
\hat{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}
defined in eq.(
21
) whose target ground state expectation value gives the nucleon optical potential. However, the Green’s function,
\bar{G}_{0}
, that appears in eqs.(

and
P
and their interaction

rather than just the kinetic energy operator associated with the single incident nucleon that appears in the Green’s function in eq.(

A(d,p)B
reactions ignored these considerations and simply replaced
\bar{V}^{PA}
and
\bar{V}^{NA}
by phenomenological optical potentials corresponding to nucleons with half the incident deuteron c.m. energy. Approximate ways of handling this situation and their quantitative effects of corrections to standard procedures in the context of the ADWA method for
(d,p)
reactions are reviewed in detail in
TJ21

arising from the second terms on the right in eqs.(

Din19
.

VI
Conclusions.

It has been shown that the set of overlap functions,
\langle\langle\Psi(n,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=0\mid A_{d}\mid\Psi^{\epsilon}_{d_{0},0}\rangle\rangle
, associated with the antisymmetrised, translationally invariant centre-of-mass wave function,
\mid\Psi^{\epsilon}_{d_{0},0}\rangle\rangle
, corresponding to a scattering state in which a plane wave deuteron is incident on an
A
-nucleon target in its ground state, satisfy coupled equations, eq. (
), in a 3-body model in which a fictitious particle with the reduced mass of the deuteron that can exist in any one of a set of states corresponding to the spectrum of the real 2-nucleon system and interacts with an A-nucleon system with the excitation spectrum of the real target.. States of the fictitious particle and the target are coupled by an interaction generated by the nucleon-nucleon interaction including exchange terms, eq.(
52
). The physical meaning of this interaction is discussed and its relation to to the interaction that generates the nucleon optical potential is elucidated. The structure of the theory suggests that it might be possible to usefully generalise the treatment here to other systems for which a few-body model is appropriate.

In the application of 3-body models to
A(d,pB)
reactions it is frequently the case that only the component of the many body scattering wave function with the target in its ground state is explicitly taken in to account in the calculation of the reaction matrix element, but deuteron elastic and break-up components are explicitly taken into account using approximate or exact treatments of the 3-body dynamics
TJ21
. It has been shown here how to construct the effective 3-body interaction, including exchange effects, that should be used to calculate the overlap functions associated with this component, including the effects of target excitation. Note however that because of the non-orthogonality of antisymmetrised channel states, to reproduce the fully antisymmetrised many-nucleon scattering wave function requires a further step using a matrix of target transition densities. However, for the purpose of formulating a theory of the nucleon optical potential and relating it to the effective interaction used in standard 3-body models of the
A(d,p)B
reaction, this step is not necessary. The analysis in this provides a justification for the models used in
Tim14
and
Din19
which were based on a theory that was not fully antisymmetrised.

Jiagang Yang

Abstract
. In this article we consider Cherry flows on torus which have two singularities: a source and a saddle, and no periodic orbits. We show that every Cherry flow admits a unique physical measure, whose basin has full volume. This proves a conjecture given by R. Saghin and E. Vargas in [ 21 ] . We also show that the perturbation of Cherry flow depends on the divergence at the saddle: when the divergence is negative, this flow admits a neighborhood, such that any flow in this neighborhood belongs to the following three cases: (a) has a saddle connection; (b) a Cherry flow; (c) a Morse-Smale flow whose non-wandering set consists two singularities and one periodic sink. In contrary, when the divergence is non-negative, this flow can be approximated by non-hyperbolic flow with arbitrarily larger number of periodic sinks.



1.
Introduction

In this article we consider Cherry flows on torus which have two singularities: a source and a saddle,
and no periodic orbits and saddle connections. Such an example was given first by Cherry in
[
4
]
(see also
[
18
]
), and the classification
of Cherry flows was treated in
[

]
. In this article we are interested in the physical measures and
perturbation theory for Cherry flows.

An invariant probability
\mu

\phi

if its

B(\mu)=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}:\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f(\phi_{s}(x))ds=\int fd\mu\\
\;\;\text{for every continuous function}\;\;f\}

has positive volume.

We prove the following conjecture given by Saghin and Vargas (
[
21
]
):

Theorem A
.

Every
\operatorname{C}^{\infty}
Cherry flow admits a unique physical measure, whose basin has full volume.

Throughout the paper we consider a Cherry flow
\phi^{X}
on torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
generated by a vector field

a source
\tilde{\sigma}^{X}
and a quasi-minimal attractor
\Lambda^{X}
, where

is called

if it is transitive, contains a saddle
\sigma^{X}
, and has neither periodic orbits nor saddle connections.
We denote by

the divergence at
\sigma^{X}
. It was observed by
[
16
,
17
,
21
]

that the behavior of physical measures for Cherry flows depends on the divergence at the saddle. And the flows
in general were assumed to be
\operatorname{C}^{\infty}
, this is because the
\operatorname{C}^{\infty}

\operatorname{C}^{1+\text{bounded variation}}

The existence of physical measures for

[

]
[Theorem 1.1]. Theorem

is a corollary of the following

Theorem B
.

Suppose
\phi^{X}
is
\operatorname{C}^{1}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(\sigma^{X})>0
. Then
\phi^{X}
admits a non-trivial physical

\Lambda^{X}

An ergodic measure is

singularities or on periodic orbits. Theorem

generalizes the results of
[
21
,
20
]
.
Different to most of the dynamical systems with physical measures, where the physical measures in general are

[

]

In

16

17
]
, a different kind of Cherry flows was considered, where

divergence at the singularity. For a flow

Suppose
\phi^{X}
is
\operatorname{C}^{\infty}

\phi^{X}
is a star flow. Moreover, there is a

neighborhood

of

, such that every flow

belongs to one of the following three cases:

•

has a saddle connection;

•

or is a Morse-Smale flow, whose

•

or is still a Cherry flow, which admits

Theorem D
.

Suppose
\phi^{X}
is
\operatorname{C}^{1}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(\sigma^{X})\geq 0

\phi^{X}
is
\operatorname{C}^{1}
approximated by non-hyperbolic
flows with arbitrarily large number of periodic sinks.

The techniques of Liao in

is important for us to remove the regularity assumption on

2.1.
Ergodic measures for Cherry flow

One can always take a circle
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
which does not bound a disk and is everywhere transverse to
X

[

[Proposition 7.1]. The inverse of the Cherry flow
\phi^{X}
is a suspension of a continuous circle map
g^{X}:{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
,

8

has no periodic point, which implies that
g^{X}

\rho(g^{X})

R_{\rho(g^{X})}:x\to x+\rho(g^{X})\mod\;1

\pi^{X}
is continuous, monotone, has degree one and maps orbits of

to orbits of
R_{\rho(g^{X})}
. Because
the
\pi_{X}
pre-image of every point in the circle is either a singular point or a non-trivial connected interval,
and by the fact that a circle contains at most countable many disjoint open intervals, there are at most countable
many points have non-trivial
\pi^{X}
pre-images.
In particular, this set has vanishing Lebesgue measure. Then
\nu^{X}=(\pi^{X})^{*}\operatorname{vol}|_{S^{1}}

the first return time of

\int\tau_{X}(\theta)d\nu^{X}(\theta)<\infty
if and only if

\mu^{X}=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{1}}\int_{t\in[0,\tau_{X}(\theta))}\delta_{\phi^{X}_{t}(\theta)}d\nu^{X}(\theta)

is an ergodic measure of
\phi^{X}
. As a summary, we have that:

Proposition 2.1
.

\phi^{X}
has three ergodic measures

\delta_{\sigma^{X}}
,
\delta_{\tilde{\sigma}^{X}}
and
\mu^{X}
if and only if
\int\tau_{X}(\theta)d\nu^{X}(\theta)<\infty
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
[
21
]
[Theorems 1.1, 1.2]]

(a)

Suppose
\phi^{X}
is
\operatorname{C}^{\infty}

{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(\sigma^{X})\leq 0

\delta_{\tilde{\sigma}^{X}}
and
\delta_{\sigma^{X}}

are the only two ergodic measures for
\phi^{X}

\delta_{\sigma^{X}}

\mathbb{T}^{2}\setminus\tilde{\sigma}^{X}
.

(b)

Suppose
\phi^{X}

\operatorname{C}^{1}

2.2.
Ergodic theory for flows

Throughout this subsection, we consider
\phi^{Y}
a flow over surface
M
which is generated by vector field
Y
,
and it admits a non-trivial ergodic measure
\mu
. Denote by
{\rm Sing}(Y)
the set of singularities.

The
linear Poincaré flow
\psi_{t}
is defined as following. Denote the normal bundle of
\phi^{Y}
over
M
by

{\mathcal{N}}_{M}=\cup_{x\in M\setminus{\rm Sing}(Y)}{\mathcal{N}}_{x},

where
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
is the orthogonal complement of the flow direction
Y(x)
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{N}}_{x}=\{v\in T_{x}M:v\bot Y(x)\}.

Denote the orthogonal projection of
T_{x}M
to
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
by
\pi_{x}
. Write the tangent flow by
\Phi_{t}=d\phi^{Y}_{t}:TM\to TM
.
Given
v\in{\mathcal{N}}_{x},x\in\Lambda\setminus\sigma
,
\psi_{t}(v)
is the orthogonal projection of
\Phi_{t}(v)
on
{\mathcal{N}}_{\phi^{Y}_{t}(x)}

along the flow direction, i.e.,

\psi_{t}(v)=\pi_{\phi^{Y}_{t}(x)}(\Phi_{t}(v))=\Phi_{t}(v)-\frac{<\Phi_{t}(v),Y(\phi^{Y}_{t}(x))>}{\|Y(\phi^{Y}_{t}(x))\|^{2}}Y(\phi^{Y}_{t}(x)),

where
<.,.>
is the inner product on
T_{x}M
given by the Riemannian metric.

2.2.1.
Oseledets Theorem for flows

The following is the flow version of Oseledets theorem.

Proposition 2.3
.

There exists a real number
\lambda
such that for
\mu
almost every
x
:

\underset{t\to\pm\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{t}\log\|\psi_{t}(v)\|=\lambda\;\;\text{for every non-zero}\;\;v\in{\mathcal{N}}_{x}\setminus\{0\}.

When the time
t_{0}>0
is fixed,
\phi^{Y}_{t_{0}}
is a diffeomorphism and
\mu
is still an invariant measure of this
diffeomorphism. Suppose
\hat{\mu}
is an ergodic decomposition of
\mu
for
\phi^{Y}_{t_{0}}
, then

\mu=\int_{t\in[0,t_{0})}d(\phi^{Y}_{t})_{*}\hat{\mu}.

We may always choose
t_{0}
such that
\mu
is ergodic for
\phi^{Y}_{t_{0}}
. Taking such a time
t_{0}
and
denote
f=\phi^{Y}_{t_{0}}
. Now let us state the following relation between Lyapunov exponent of
\mu
for
\phi^{Y}_{t}
and for
f
, whose
proof is simple and we will not provide.

Theorem 2.4
.

The Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
for
f
coincide to
\{t_{0}\lambda\}\cup\{0\}
, and for
\mu
almost every
x\in M\setminus{\rm Sing}(Y)
, the flow direction
<Y(x)>
is contained in the subbundle of the Oseledets splitting of
f
corresponding
to zero exponent. Moreover, we have
\lim\frac{1}{n}\log\det(Df^{n}(x))=t_{0}\lambda
.

Remark 2.5
.

The above theorem implies that, for
\mu
almost every
x
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\det\Phi_{t}|_{T_{x}M}=\lambda.

Definition 2.6
.

A non-trivial ergodic measure
\mu
for flow
\phi^{Y}
is a
hyperbolic measure
if its exponent is non-vanishing.

2.2.2.
Divergence

We need the following version of Liouville Theorem.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
x\in M
and
t>0
,

\operatorname{ln}\det\Phi_{t}|_{T_{x}M}=\int_{0}^{t}div_{Y}(\phi^{Y}_{s}(x))ds.

The definition of divergence depends on the Riemmanian metric. Because in this paper we only consider
two dimensional torus, for simplicity, in Appendix we provide a short proof of the above lemma with
the assumption that
M=\mathbb{T}^{2}
and the divergence is defined by the flat metric on torus. More precisely,
every small open set of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
can be looked as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, choosing such a local coordinate

(x,y)
, in this coordinate we may write
Y=(Y_{1},Y_{2})
and
\phi^{Y}_{t}=(f_{t}(x,y),g_{t}(x,y))
.
Then for any
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, the
divergence of
Y
at
x
is defined by
div_{Y}(x)=\frac{\partial Y_{1}}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial Y_{2}}{\partial y}
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
{\rm Orb}(p)
be a periodic orbit of
\phi^{Y}
with period
\tau_{Y}(p)
:

•

the
divergence of
{\rm Orb}(p)
is defined by

{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}({\rm Orb}(p))=\frac{1}{\tau_{Y}(p)}\int_{0}^{\tau_{Y}(p)}div_{Y}(\phi^{Y}_{s}(p))ds;

•

the
divergence of
\mu
is defined by
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(\mu)=\int div_{Y}(x)d\mu(x)
.

Remark 2.9
.

For an atomic measure
\delta_{\sigma}
supported on a singularity
\sigma\in{\rm Sing}(Y)
,

{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(\delta_{\sigma})
coincides to the classical definition of divergence at
\sigma
,
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(\sigma)
. And when

\mu
is supported on a periodic orbit
{\rm Orb}(p)
, the above two definitions coincide, and by Lemma
2.7
,
which equal to
\operatorname{ln}\det\Phi_{\tau_{Y}(p)}|_{T_{p}M}=\operatorname{ln}\|\psi_{\tau_{Y}(p)}|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{p}}\|
.

As a corollary of Remark
2.5
and Lemma
2.7
, by Birkhoff Ergodic Theorem, we obtain that:

Corollary 2.10
.

{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(\mu)
coincides to the Lyapunov exponent of
\mu
for the flow
\phi^{Y}
.

The following lemma implies
[
21
]
[Theorem 1.2]:

Lemma 2.11
.

There exists an ergodic measure
\mu
of
Y
such that
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(\mu)\leq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that the divergence of every ergodic measure of
\phi^{Y}
is positive. Denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{Y}
the space of
invariant measures of
\phi^{Y}
. Then by Ergodic Decomposition Theorem, there is
a>0
such that
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{Y}}>2a>0
.
Take
{\mathcal{V}}
a small neighborhood of
{\mathcal{M}}_{Y}
in the probability space over
M
, such that for any
\tilde{\mu}\in{\mathcal{V}}
,

\int div_{Y}(x)d\tilde{\mu}(x)>a.

Note that the measures contained in
{\mathcal{V}}
are not necessary to be invariant. It is well
known that for any
x\in M
, there is
t_{x}>0
such that
\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{\phi^{Y}_{s}(x)}ds\in{\mathcal{V}}

for any
t\geq t_{x}
. A short proof is following:
Suppose this is false, then there are
t_{n}\to\infty
such that

\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\delta_{\phi^{Y}_{s}(x)}ds\notin{\mathcal{V}}
. Because the probability space
over any compact manifold is compact, we can take a converging point
\mu_{0}
of
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Then
\mu_{0}
is an invariant measure but not contained in
{\mathcal{V}}
, a contradiction.

Let us continue the proof. For
n>0
, denote by
M_{n}=\{x;t_{x}\leq n\}
. Because
\bigcup_{n}M_{n}=M
, we can
choose
N
sufficiently large, such that
\operatorname{vol}(M_{N})>0
. Observe that for any

x\in M_{N}
and
n>N
,

\int div_{Y}(y)d\big(\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}\delta_{\phi^{Y}_{s}(x)}ds\big)(y)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}div_{Y}(\delta_{\phi^{Y}_{s}(x)})ds>a.

Then by Lemma
2.7
,

\det\Phi_{n}|_{T_{x}M}=\exp^{\int_{0}^{n}div_{Y}(\delta_{\phi^{Y}_{s}(x)})ds}>\exp^{na},

which implies that
\lim_{n}\operatorname{vol}(\phi_{n}^{Y}(M_{N}))=\infty
, a contradiction to the fact that the volume of
M
is bounded.

∎

2.2.3.
Star flow

Now let us give a general criterion for a two-dimensional flow
Y
to be a star flow.

Theorem 2.12
.

Suppose all the singularities of
Y
are hyperbolic,
and all the ergodic measures of
\phi^{Y}
have negative divergence, then
Y
is a star flow.

Example 2.13
.

Consider a 2-dimensional flow
Y_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a saddle
\sigma
of negative divergence,
and each branch of the unstable manifold of
\sigma
is connected to a branch of its stable manifold–
locally this set looks like a figure ‘8’. Denote by

\Gamma
the set consisting of
\sigma
and its two saddle connections. It is easy to show that
\Gamma

is an attractor: for each branch of unstable manifold, we take a transverse section near to
\sigma
and analyze
the Poincaré return maps, which are uniformly contracting. Taking a small contracting neighborhood
U
,

\delta_{\sigma}
is the unique invariant measure supported in
U
. Applying Theorem
2.12
,

Y_{0}
is a star flow in
U
.

Proof of Theorem
2.12
:

Because all the singularities of
Y
are hyperbolic,
\phi^{Y}
has only finitely many singularities.
By the continuation of hyperbolic singularities, the singularities for flows in a small neighborhood are all hyperbolic.
To prove this theorem, it suffices to show that the periodic orbits of nearby flows are all periodic sinks.

Write
{\mathcal{M}}_{Y}
the space of invariant measures of
\phi^{Y}
. By the assumption, for any ergodic measure

\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}_{Y}
,
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(\mu)<0
. Then by Ergodic Decomposition Theorem,
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(.)|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{Y}}<0
. Because
{\mathcal{M}}_{Y}
is
a compact space and
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(.)
is a continuous function, this implies that
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(.)|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{Y}}<a<0
for some
a<0
.

For any vector field
Z\in{\mathcal{U}}
, suppose
{\rm Orb}_{Z}(p)
is a periodic orbit of
Z
. Denote
\mu_{{\rm Orb}_{Z}(p)}

the
\phi^{Z}
ergodic measure supported on
{\rm Orb}_{Z}(p)
. Then by the upper semi-continuation of the invariant
measures space, for
{\mathcal{U}}
sufficiently small,
\mu_{{\rm Orb}_{Z}(p)}
is close to
{\mathcal{M}}_{Z}
in the weak star topology.
From the definition of divergence for measures,

{\mathcal{D}}_{Z}({\rm Orb}_{Z}(p))={\mathcal{D}}_{Z}(\mu_{{\rm Orb}_{Z}(p)})<\frac{a}{2}<0.

By Remark
2.9
,
{\rm Orb}_{Z}(p)
is a periodic sink. The proof is complete.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
2.7

Proof of Lemma
2.7
:

For
s\in[0,t]
, denote by
x_{s}=\phi^{Y}_{s}(x)
. Because

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\big|_{s_{0}}(\operatorname{ln}\det\Phi_{s}|_{T_{x}M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta s\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta s}(\operatorname{ln}\det\Phi_{s_{0}+\Delta s}|_{T_{x}M}-\operatorname{ln}\det\Phi_{s_{0}}|_{T_{x}M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta s\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta s}(\operatorname{ln}\det\Phi_{\Delta s}|_{T_{x_{s_{0}}}M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta s\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta s}\operatorname{ln}\det\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\partial f_{\Delta s}}{\partial x}|_{x_{s_{0}}}&\frac{\partial g_{\Delta s}}{\partial x}|_{x_{s_{0}}}\\
\frac{\partial f_{\Delta s}}{\partial y}|_{x_{s_{0}}}&\frac{\partial g_{\Delta s}}{\partial y}|_{x_{s_{0}}}\\
\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\Delta s}|_{\Delta s=0}\operatorname{ln}\big(\frac{\partial f_{\Delta s}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial g_{\Delta s}}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial g_{\Delta s}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial f_{\Delta s}}{\partial y}\big)\big|_{x_{s_{0}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{d}{d\Delta s}|_{\Delta s=0}\big(\frac{\partial f_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial x}\frac{\partial g_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial g_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial x}\frac{\partial f_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial y}\big)}{\det\Phi_{0}|_{T_{x_{s_{0}}}M}}

Note that

\frac{d}{d\Delta s}|_{\Delta s=0}(\frac{\partial f_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial x})=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(\frac{d}{d\Delta x}|_{\Delta s=0}(f_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})))=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(Y_{1}(x_{s_{0}})).

Similarity, we have that

\frac{d}{d\Delta s}|_{\Delta s=0}(\frac{\partial g_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial y})=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(Y_{2}(x_{s_{0}})),

\frac{d}{d\Delta s}|_{\Delta s=0}(\frac{\partial g_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial x})=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(Y_{1}(x_{s_{0}})),

\frac{d}{d\Delta s}|_{\Delta s=0}(\frac{\partial f_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial y})=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(Y_{2}(x_{s_{0}})).

Because
\Phi_{0}|_{T_{x_{s_{0}}}M}=\operatorname{ID}
, we have

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\partial f_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial x}&\frac{\partial g_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial x}\\
\frac{\partial f_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial y}&\frac{\partial g_{\Delta s}(x_{s_{0}})}{\partial y}\\
\end{array}\right)_{\Delta s=0}=\operatorname{ID}.

Combining the above argument, we show that

\frac{d}{ds}\big|_{s_{0}}(\operatorname{ln}\det\Phi_{s}|_{T_{x}M})=\frac{\partial{Y_{1}}}{\partial{x}}+\frac{\partial{Y_{2}}}{\partial{y}}=div_{Y}(x_{s_{0}})

Hence,

\operatorname{ln}\det\Phi_{t}|_{T_{x}M}=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{ds}(\operatorname{ln}\det\Phi_{s}|_{T_{x}M})ds=\int_{0}^{t}div_{Y}(x_{s})ds.

The proof is complete.

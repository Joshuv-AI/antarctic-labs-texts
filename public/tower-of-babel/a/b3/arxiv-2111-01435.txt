Global well-posedness to the two-dimensional incompressible vorticity equation in the half plane
Quansen Jiu 1 , You Li 2 , Wanwan Zhang 3

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the global well-posedness of the two-dimensional incompressible vorticity equation in the half plane. Under the assumption that the initial vorticity ω 0 ∈ W k , p ​ ( ℝ + 2 ) \omega_{0}\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}) with k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 and 1 < p < 2 1<p<2 , it is shown that the two-dimensional incompressible vorticity equation admits a unique solution ω ∈ C ⁡ ( [ 0 , T ] , W k , p ​ ( ℝ + 2 ) ) \omega\in C([0,T];W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})) for any T > 0 T>0 . An elementary and self-contained proof is presented and delicate estimates of the velocity and its derivatives are obtained in this paper. It should be emphasized that the uniform estimate on ∫ 0 t ‖ u ⁡ ( τ ) ‖ W 1 , ∞ ​ ( ℝ + 2 ) ​ 𝑑 τ \int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}d\tau is required to complete the global regularity of the solution. This is different from the proof of global well-posedness of the Euler velocity equations in the Sobolev spaces, in which a Kato-type or logarithmic-type estimate of the gradient of the velocity is enough to close the energy estimates.

1.
Introduction and Main Results

The two-dimensional incompressible Euler equations read as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0,~(x,t)\in D\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&{\rm div}~u=0,\end{aligned}\ \right.

(1.1)

where
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a domain and the unknown functions are the velocity field
u(x,t)=(u_{1}(x,t),u_{2}(x,t))
and the pressure function
p
. The second equation means that the flow is incompressible, which enables us to determine the pressure
p
from
u
through a singular integral operator for a specific domain
D
. The initial condition to (
1.1
) is imposed as

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~x\in D.

(1.2)

Furthermore, when
D
is a domain with a boundary, the natural boundary condition is the no penetration one:

\displaystyle u(x,t)\cdot\nu(x)=0,~(x,t)\in\partial D\times\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

(1.3)

Here
\nu(x)
denotes the outward unit normal vector of domain
D
at
x\in\partial D
.

In two-dimensional case, the vorticity
\omega=\partial_{x_{2}}u_{1}-\partial_{x_{1}}u_{2}
is a scalar function satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=0,~(x,t)\in D\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
&u=\nabla^{\bot}\Delta^{-1}_{D}\omega,\\
&\omega(x,0)=\omega_{0}(x).\end{aligned}\ \right.

(1.4)

The second equation of (
1.4
) is the so-called Biot-Savart law with
\nabla^{\bot}=(\partial_{2},-\partial_{1}).

For smooth domains
D
with boundaries,
\Delta^{-1}_{D}
denotes the inverse of the Laplacian operator with the homogeneous boundary condition.
It is deduced from (
1.4
) that the quantity
\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{s}(D)}(1\leq s\leq\infty)
is conserved for all times, which plays a vital role in the proof of global regularity of solutions to the two-dimensional incompressible Euler equations.

The global well-posedness theory to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) or initial-boundary problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) has been established in various settings.
Hölder
[
27
]
and Wolibner
[
55
]
independently obtained the global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) in Hölder spaces for a smooth bounded domain
D
.
The global well-posedness result in
[
55
]
was later improved by Kato in
[
30
]
without the zero-circulation assumption for each inner component of the boundary of a bounded (not necessarily simply connected) domain, where the author used the Schauder fixed-point theorem and constructed the solutions through the vorticity equation.
In
[
48
]
, McGrath utilized the Schauder fixed-point theorem as well, but considered the vorticity equation in the stream function form to prove the global well-posedness of classical solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with
D=\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Later, Bourguignon and Brezis
[
7
]
proved the global existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) with
D
a smooth bounded domain in Sobolev spaces with a suitable external force for the initial velocity in
W^{m,s}(D)
with
1<s<\infty
and
m>1+\frac{s}{2}
.
For a recent proof of the global regularity, we also refer the reader to
[
41
]
, where Koch proved the global existence and uniqueness of
C^{1}
classical solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) with a smooth bounded domain

for

initial velocity via the method of Schauder fixed-point theorem.
Existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions to (

)-(

) on exterior domain

were obtained by Kikuchi in
[

]
.
Concerning the half plane

, Secchi
[

showed the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to (

)-(
1.3
) via the method of vanishing viscosity limit under specific Navier-boundary conditions.
In addition, it is well-known that Yudovich
[

]
obtained the existence and uniqueness of global weak solutions if the initial vorticity
\omega_{0}
lies in
L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
for the domain

(see

Our goal in this paper is to consider the global well-posedness of strong solutions to the two-dimensional Euler in the half plane via the vorticity equation (
1.4
). As we know, in the half plane, it has been widely accepted and used in the boundary layer theory that there exists a unique global strong (classical) solution to the two-dimensional incompressible Euler equations (see e.g.
[
45
]
and
[
46
]
). And as mentioned above, it can be proved by using the viscous approximation (the Navier-Stokes equations) with Navier boundary condition (see
[

]
and
[
53
]
) to the Euler equations. However, we will utilize the vorticity equation instead of the velocity equation to present an elementary and self-contained proof the global well-posedness in this paper. Note that the vorticity equation is one of a class of transport equations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=0,~(x,t)\in D\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&u=\nabla^{\bot}\Delta_{D}^{-1+\alpha}\omega,\\
&\omega(x,0)=\omega_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\ \right.

(1.5)

where
0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
and the domain
D
is the whole plane, the torus, the upper half plane or the bounded domain. In fact, when
\alpha=0
, (
1.5
) is the two-dimensional incompressible vorticity equation. When
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, (
1.5
) reduces to the celebrated two-dimensional inviscid
SQG

When
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, (
1.5
) is the so-called inviscid modified or generalized
SQG
in the literature (see
[

]

Our main results are stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
\omega_{0}\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
k\geq 3
and
1<p<2
, there exists a time
T_{0}=T_{0}(\|\omega_{0}\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})})
such that (
1.4
) admits a unique solution
\omega\in C([0,T_{0}];W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\omega_{0}\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
k\geq 3
and
1<p<2
. For any
T>0
, (
1.4
) admits a unique solution
\omega\in C([0,T];W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
.

Remark 1.1
.

The restriction of
p\in(1,2)
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
is required when estimating
\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
in terms of
\|\omega\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
in Proposition
3.1
with help of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality and when establishing the maximum estimate of the velocity and its gradient in Lemma
5.2
.
It would be interesting to obtain the local (global) well-posedness to (
1.4
) in the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
for more general values of
k
and
p
, especially for
k\geq 3
and
2\leq p<\infty
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
can be also established for (
1.4
) with
D=\mathbb{R}^{2}
by the same but simpler arguments .

Now we explain our approaches in a more detail. To prove Theorem
1.1
, starting from the linearized equation (
4.2
), we construct a suitable approximate system (see (
A.3
)) and obtain the uniform estimate of the approximate solutions. In particular, we will establish a delicate Sobolev-type estimate on the singular integral involving the expression
u=\nabla^{\bot}\Delta^{-1}_{D}\omega
in (
1.4
) with
D=\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, that is,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{m,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{m,p}\|\omega\|_{W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

for any integers
m\geq 0
and
1<p<2
(see Proposition
3.1
).
With the global well-posedness result of the linearized equation in hand (see Lemma
4.1
), we will utilize the contraction mapping principle to prove the local well-posedness Theorem
1.1
.
To prove Theorem
1.2
, we will show a Kato-type estimate on the velocity and its gradient, which is

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{\gamma}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big(1+\log\Big(1+\frac{[\omega(t)]_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)\Big)+C_{p}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

for any
0<\gamma<1
and
1<p<2
(see Lemma
5.2
), and hence, for any
1<p<2
, by the Sobolev embedding
W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\hookrightarrow C^{0,2-\frac{2}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{p}(\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})})\Big(1+\log\Big(e+\|\omega(t)\|_{W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big)\Big),

which is not sufficient to obtain the uniform estimate on
\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t<T}\|\omega(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
with
k\geq 2
and
1<p<2
for any
T>0
(see (
5.14
) and (
5.16
)).
This phenomenon is very different from the well-known global well-posedness to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) or initial-boundary problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) in the Sobolev spaces, where the logarithmic-type estimate of the gradient of the velocity is enough to prove the global regularity of the solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) or (
1.1
)-(
1.3
).
It is easily seen that this difference between the velocity equations and the vorticity equation is caused by their different non-linear structures.
In particular, there is a new term
\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
appearing in the energy estimate of
\|\omega(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
when utilizing the calculus inequalities to the vorticity equation.
To complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
, the uniform estimate on
\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}d\tau
is then required.
To this end, we will derive the double exponential growth in time of the gradient of the vorticity in the half plane (see Proposition
5.3
), which in turn implies that, for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{p}ABe^{C_{p}At},

where
A=\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
and
B=1+\log\Big(3+\frac{\|\nabla\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)
.
Moreover, to establish the Kato-type estimate, we will also show a Schauder-type estimate as

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{\gamma}[\omega]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})},

for any
0<\gamma<1
(see Proposition
3.2
).
The similar estimate in the whole plane can be directly derived by making use of the expression of the gradient of the velocity and the cancellation of the singular kernel (see for instance Lemma 4.6 in
[
49
]
). However, in the half-plane case, the cancellation property of the singular kernel does not hold due to the presence of the boundary and the proof of in the whole plane can not be applied. We will overcome this difficulty with help of the stream function. In particular, the local maximum estimate of the stream function will be derived (see (
3.17
)).
Finally, it should be remarked that the optimal growth of the vorticity in the half plane remains open (see
[
39
]
for the unit disc and
[
56
]
for symmetric smooth bounded domains).

Section 3 is devoted to some delicate estimates between the velocity and the vorticity.
The proof of Theorems

2.1.
Notations

Some notations are introduced as follows. Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For
s\geq 1
, we denote
L^{s}(\Omega)
the usual function space consisting of measurable functions on
\Omega
which are s-integrable, of which norm is defined by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{s}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}.

When
f
is a vector or matrix function the same notation will be used and the notation
|f|
denotes the usual Euclidean norm. For
s=\infty
,
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
denotes the Banach space of essentially bounded functions on
\Omega
, of which norm is

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\inf\{c:|f(x)|\leq c{\rm~a.e.~on}~\Omega\}.

An useful characterization of the
L^{s}
norm of a function is by its distribution function. More precisely, for any
s>0
and
|f|^{s}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{s}dx=s\int^{\infty}_{0}\tau^{s-1}\mu_{f}(\tau)d\tau,

(2.1)

where
\mu_{f}
is the distribution function of
f
defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{f}(\tau)=|\{x\in\Omega:|f(x)|>\tau\}|

(2.2)

for any
\tau>0
(see, e.g.,
[
24
]
).

For
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
a positive integer and
1\leq s\leq\infty
, we define the usual Sobolev spaces
W^{m,s}(\Omega)=\{f\in L^{s}(\Omega):\partial^{\alpha}f\in L^{s}(\Omega),|\alpha|\leq m\}
, where
\partial^{\alpha}f
is the weak (or distributional) derivative. These spaces are equipped with the following norms:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{m,s}(\Omega)}=\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}u\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}},1\leq s<\infty,

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega)}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

There will be no notational distinction between Sobolev spaces of scalar-valued and vector-valued functions.
One can consult
[
1
,
8
,
21
,
24
,
54
]
and the references therein for more materials of Sobolev spaces. Here we focus on the half-space case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.
Some Sobolev embeddings are listed here for convenient applications. More precisely,

W^{m,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{Ns}{N-ms}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
if
ms<N
and
W^{m,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\hookrightarrow C^{k}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
if
ms>N
(see, e.g.,
[
8
]
).

For
0<\gamma<1
, a function
f
is uniformly Hölder continuous with exponent
\gamma
in
\Omega
if the quantity

\displaystyle[f]_{C^{\gamma}(\Omega)}=\sup_{x\neq y\in\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}

is finite, and locally Hölder continuous with exponent
\gamma
in
\Omega
if
f
is uniformly Hölder continuous with exponent
\gamma
on compact subset of
\Omega
. For
k
a non-negative integer and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, the Hölder spaces
C^{k,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})

(C^{k,\gamma}(\Omega))
are defined as the subspaces of
C^{k}(\bar{\Omega})~(C^{k}(\Omega))
consisting of functions whose
k-{\rm th}
order partial derivatives are uniformly Hölder continuous (locally Hölder continuous) with exponent
\gamma
in
\Omega
. Set

\displaystyle[f]_{C^{k}(\Omega)}=\|D^{k}f\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}=\sup_{|\beta|=k}\sup_{\Omega}|D^{\beta}f|,~k=0,1,2...,

\displaystyle[f]_{C^{k,\gamma}(\Omega)}=[D^{k}f]_{C^{\gamma}(\Omega)}=\sup_{|\beta|=k}[D^{\beta}f]_{C^{\gamma}(\Omega)},

which are semi-norms in
C^{k}(\Omega)
and
C^{k,\gamma}(\Omega)
respectively. With these semi-norms, we can define the related norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{k}(\bar{\Omega})}=\sum^{k}_{j=0}[f]_{C^{j}(\Omega)}=\sum^{k}_{j=0}\|D^{j}f\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{k,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{k}(\bar{\Omega})}+[f]_{C^{k,\gamma}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{k}(\bar{\Omega})}+[D^{k}f]_{C^{\gamma}(\Omega)},

on the spaces
C^{k}(\bar{\Omega})
,
C^{k,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})
, respectively.

We also introduce the non-dimensional norms on
C^{k}(\bar{\Omega})
,
C^{k,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})
. If
\Omega
is bounded, with
d=\rm diam~\Omega
(the diameter of
\Omega
), we set

\displaystyle\|f\|^{\prime}_{C^{k}(\bar{\Omega})}=\sum^{k}_{j=0}d^{j}[f]_{C^{j}(\Omega)}=\sum^{k}_{j=0}d^{j}\|D^{j}f\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\|f\|^{\prime}_{C^{k,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|^{\prime}_{C^{k}(\bar{\Omega})}+d^{k+\gamma}[f]_{C^{k,\gamma}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|^{\prime}_{C^{k}(\bar{\Omega})}+d^{k+\gamma}[D^{k}f]_{C^{\gamma}(\Omega)}.

The spaces
C^{k}(\bar{\Omega})
,
C^{k,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})
equipped with the respective norms are Banach spaces. We refer readers to
[
24
]
for more details.

Throughout this paper, we will use
C
to denote a generic positive constant, whose value may
change from line to line, and write
C({\alpha})
or
C_{\alpha}
to emphasize the dependence of a
constant on
\alpha
.

.

Next we recall the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality

]

.

Let

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}+\frac{\beta}{N}=1+\frac{1}{r}.

A constant

\displaystyle\||\cdot|^{-\beta}\ast f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{r,s}\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

\displaystyle f(t)\leq f(0)+\int^{t}_{0}\varphi(f(\tau))d\tau,~t\leq T.

and of (
2.4
) with
s\in(1,\infty),s_{1}=s_{3}=s,s_{2}=s_{4}=\infty
are proved in
[
49
]
and
[
43
]
, respectively.

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

When
|\alpha|=1
, (
2.3
) is a direct consequence of the Leibniz differential formula and the Hölder inequality.
For
|\alpha|\geq 2
, the Leibniz differential formula gives

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}(fg)=(\partial^{\alpha}f)g+\sum^{|\alpha|-1}_{|\beta|=1}C_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}f\partial^{\alpha-\beta}g+f\partial^{\alpha}g.

(2.5)

For
1\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|-1
, by using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see
[
51
]
), we get

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{N,\alpha,\beta,s_{1},s_{4}}\|D^{|\alpha|}f\|^{\frac{|\beta|}{|\alpha|}}_{L^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|f\|^{1-\frac{|\beta|}{|\alpha|}}_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

(2.6)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha-\beta}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{N,\alpha,\beta,s_{2},s_{3}}\|D^{|\alpha|}g\|^{1-\frac{|\beta|}{|\alpha|}}_{L^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|^{\frac{|\beta|}{|\alpha|}}_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

(2.7)

where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are determined by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{|\beta|}{|\alpha|}\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\Big(1-\frac{|\beta|}{|\alpha|}\Big)\frac{1}{s_{4}},

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\Big(1-\frac{|\beta|}{|\alpha|}\Big)\frac{1}{s_{3}}+\frac{|\beta|}{|\alpha|}\frac{1}{s_{2}}.

Then it is easily seen that
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
. Therefore, utilizing the Hölder inequality and the Young inequality together with (
2.5
)-(
2.7
) yields that, for any
|\alpha|\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\partial^{\alpha}(fg)\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
\leq&\|D^{|\alpha|}f\|_{L^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\sum^{|\alpha|-1}_{|\beta|=1}C_{\alpha,\beta}\|\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|\partial^{\alpha-\beta}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\ +\|f\|_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|D^{|\alpha|}g\|_{L^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
\leq&\|D^{|\alpha|}f\|_{L^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|f\|_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|D^{|\alpha|}g\|_{L^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\ +\sum^{|\alpha|-1}_{|\beta|=1}C_{N,\alpha,\beta,s_{i}}\Big(\|D^{|\alpha|}f\|_{L^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big)^{\frac{|\beta|}{|\alpha|}}\Big(\|f\|_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|D^{|\alpha|}g\|_{L^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big)^{1-\frac{|\beta|}{|\alpha|}}\\
\leq&C_{N,\alpha,\beta,s_{i}}(\|D^{|\alpha|}f\|_{L^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|f\|_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|D^{|\alpha|}g\|_{L^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}),\end{split}

which is exactly (
2.3
).

Next we turn to the proof of (
2.4
). When
|\alpha|=1~{\rm or}~2
, (
2.4
)
is straightforward by using the Leibniz differential formula and the Hölder inequality. Thus we proceed to assume
|\alpha|\geq 3
.
Similar to (
2.5
), one writes

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}(fg)-f\partial^{\alpha}g=(\partial^{\alpha}f)g+\sum_{|\beta|=1}C_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}f\partial^{\alpha-\beta}g+\sum^{|\alpha|-1}_{|\beta|=2}C_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}f\partial^{\alpha-\beta}g.

(2.8)

Furthermore, similar to (
2.6
)-(
2.7
), we deduce that, for any
2\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|-1
,

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{N,\alpha,\beta,s_{1},s_{4}}\|D^{|\alpha|}f\|^{\frac{|\beta|-1}{|\alpha|-1}}_{L^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|\nabla f\|^{1-\frac{|\beta|-1}{|\alpha|-1}}_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

(2.9)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha-\beta}g\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{N,\alpha,\beta,s_{2},s_{3}}\|D^{|\alpha|-1}g\|^{\frac{|\alpha|-|\beta|}{|\alpha|-1}}_{L^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|^{1-\frac{|\alpha|-|\beta|}{|\alpha|-1}}_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

(2.10)

where
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{|\beta|-1}{|\alpha|-1}\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\Big(1-\frac{|\beta|-1}{|\alpha|-1}\Big)\frac{1}{s_{4}},

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{|\alpha|-|\beta|}{|\alpha|-1}\frac{1}{s_{3}}+\Big(1-\frac{|\alpha|-|\beta|}{|\alpha|-1}\Big)\frac{1}{s_{2}}.

Then it is clear that
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
. Therefore, utilizing the Hölder inequality and Young inequality together with (
2.8
)-(
2.10
) deduces that, for any
|\alpha|\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\partial^{\alpha}(fg)-f\partial^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
\leq&\|D^{|\alpha|}f\|_{L^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|D^{|\alpha|-1}g\|_{L^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\ +\sum_{2\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|-1}C_{\alpha,\beta}\|\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|\partial^{\alpha-\beta}g\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
\leq&\|D^{|\alpha|}f\|_{L^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|D^{|\alpha|-1}g\|_{L^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\ +\sum^{|\alpha|-1}_{|\beta|=2}C_{N,\alpha,\beta,s_{i}}(\|D^{|\alpha|}f\|_{L^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})^{\frac{|\beta|-1}{|\alpha|-1}}(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|D^{|\alpha|-1}g\|_{L^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})^{\frac{|\alpha|-|\beta|}{|\alpha|-1}}\\
\leq&C_{N,\alpha,\beta,s_{i}}(\|D^{|\alpha|}f\|_{L^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|D^{|\alpha|-1}g\|_{L^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}),\end{split}

which concludes the proof of (
2.4
).

The corresponding calculus inequalities in the half-space are then easily derived from Stein’s linear Sobolev extension operators (
[
54
]
) and Proposition
2.5
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Suppose that
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
with
|\alpha|=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}>0
and
s\in(1,\infty)
with
s_{1},s_{3}\in(1,\infty)
such that
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{1}{s_{2}}=\frac{1}{s_{3}}+\frac{1}{s_{4}}
.

(1)
If
f\in W^{|\alpha|,s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
and
g\in W^{|\alpha|,s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
, then there exists a constant
C=C(N,\alpha,s,s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})
such that

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{W^{|\alpha|,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}\leq C(\|f\|_{W^{|\alpha|,s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}\|g\|_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}\|g\|_{W^{|\alpha|,s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}).

(2)
If
f\in W^{|\alpha|,s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap W^{1,s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
and
g\in W^{|\alpha|-1,s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
, then there exists a constant
C=C(N,\alpha,s,s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})
such that

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}(fg)-f\partial^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}\leq C(\|f\|_{W^{|\alpha|,s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}\|g\|_{L^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}+\|f\|_{W^{1,s_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}\|g\|_{W^{|\alpha|-1,s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}).

3.
Estimates on the velocity fields

Let us start with some estimates on the velocity field
u
in the half plane through the vorticity
\omega
. These estimates are of interest in itself. For convenience, we will make a notation suppression for the time variable in this section. All functions could be understood at a fixed time
t>0
.

As usual, the stream function
\Psi(x)
, which vanishes at infinity, is introduced and satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\Psi(x)=\omega(x),~x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\\
\ &\Psi(x_{1},0)=0.\end{aligned}\ \right.

(3.1)

Clearly, the stream function
\Psi
can be represented as

\displaystyle\Psi(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\log|x-y|-\log|x-\bar{y}|\Big)\omega(y)dy,~x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

(3.2)

And the velocity field
u=\nabla^{\bot}\Psi
can be explicitly formulated as

\displaystyle u(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\frac{(x-y)^{\bot}}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{(x-\bar{y})^{\bot}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}\Big)\omega(y)dy,~x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},

(3.3)

which is called the Biot-Savart law. We use here the notations
z^{\perp}:=(z_{2},-z_{1})
and
\bar{z}:=(z_{1},-z_{2})
for
z=(z_{1},z_{2}).

Then the velocity field
u
satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm div}~u=0,~x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}~{\rm and}~u_{2}(x_{1},0)=0,~x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.4)

Indeed, the slip boundary condition and the divergence-free condition in (
3.4
) can be derived from (
3.3
) and the expression of the gradient of the velocity, respectively. In particular, the expression of the gradient of the velocity is calculated in Lemma
A.2
.

The following estimate is a Sobolev-type estimate of the velocity field
u
in terms of the vorticity
\omega
by the Biot-Savart law.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\omega\in W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
m\geq 0
an integer and
1<p<2
. Suppose that
u
is defined by (
3.3
). Then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{m,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{m,p}\|\omega\|_{W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

Proof.

When
m=0
, from (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\frac{(x-y)^{\bot}}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{(x-\bar{y})^{\bot}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}\Big)\omega(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(x-y)^{\bot}}{|x-y|^{2}}\bar{\omega}(y)dy,

where
\bar{\omega}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the odd extension of
\omega
to the whole plane, which is

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}(y_{1},y_{2})=\begin{cases}\omega(y_{1},y_{2}),&y_{2}>0,\\
-\omega(y_{1},-y_{2}),&y_{2}<0.\\
\end{cases}

Hence, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,

\displaystyle|u(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\bar{\omega}(y)|dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}(|\cdot|^{-1}\ast|\bar{\omega}|)(x).

It follows from Lemma
2.2
that, for
p\in(1,2)
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\frac{1}{2\pi}|\cdot|^{-1}\ast|\bar{\omega}|\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\frac{1}{2\pi}|\cdot|^{-1}\ast|\bar{\omega}|\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\|\bar{\omega}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

We proceed to show the case
m=1
.
From Lemma
A.2
, we see that

\displaystyle\nabla u(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}M(x,y)\omega(y)dy+\frac{\omega(x)}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

where the kernel matrix
M(x,y)
is the sum of singular part
M_{s}(x,y)
and regular part
M_{r}(x,y)
:

\displaystyle M_{s}(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{-2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}-y_{2})}{|x-y|^{4}}&\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}\\
\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}&\frac{2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}-y_{2})}{|x-y|^{4}}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle M_{r}(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}+y_{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}&-\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}\\
-\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}&-\frac{2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}+y_{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}\end{pmatrix}.

Utilizing the same odd extension technique, we can obtain

\displaystyle\nabla u(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}M_{s}(x,y)\bar{\omega}(y)dy+\frac{\omega(x)}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, by the classical Calderón-Zygmund singular integral theory (see, e.g.,
[
54
]
), we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\|\bar{\omega}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+C\|\omega\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\|\omega\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\|\omega\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

where we have used the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
for any
p\in(1,2)
.
Combining (
3.5
) with (
3.6
) leads to

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{p}\|\omega\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

(3.7)

We now turn to the case
m=2
. In view of (
3.7
), it suffices to bound
\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
. To this end, we first estimate the tangential derivative
\partial_{x_{1}}\nabla u
of the gradient velocity
\nabla u
, that is,
\nabla_{x}\nabla^{\bot}_{x}\partial_{x_{1}}\Psi
.
After differentiating (
3.2
) directly and integrating by parts, together with the homogeneous boundary condition of the Green’s function, we can obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{1}}\Psi(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{x_{1}}\Big(\log|x-y|-\log|x-\bar{y}|\Big)\omega(y)dy

(3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{y_{1}}\Big(\log|x-y|-\log|x-\bar{y}|\Big)\omega(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\log|x-y|-\log|x-\bar{y}|\Big)\partial_{y_{1}}\omega(y)dy.

By differentiating (
3.8
), we can arrive at

\displaystyle\nabla^{\bot}_{x}\partial_{x_{1}}\Psi(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\frac{(x-y)^{\bot}}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{(x-\bar{y})^{\bot}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}\Big)\partial_{y_{1}}\omega(y)dy.

Computations similar to those in the proof of Lemma
A.2
give

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\nabla^{\bot}_{x}\partial_{x_{1}}\Psi(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}M(x,y)\partial_{y_{1}}\omega(y)dy+\frac{\partial_{x_{1}}\omega(x)}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Similar to (
3.6
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{1}}\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}\nabla^{\bot}_{x}\partial_{x_{1}}\Psi\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\|\partial_{x_{1}}\omega\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\|\omega\|_{W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

Next we consider the normal derivative
\partial_{x_{2}}\nabla u
, that is,
\partial_{x_{2}}\nabla_{x}\nabla_{x}^{\bot}\Psi
. In view of (
3.9
), it remains to bound
\|\partial^{3}_{x_{2}}\Psi\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
.
By (
3.1
), we proceed to represent
\partial^{3}_{x_{2}}\Psi
as

\displaystyle\partial^{3}_{x_{2}}\Psi=\partial_{x_{2}}(\omega-\partial^{2}_{x_{1}}\Psi).

Using (
3.9
) again, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\partial^{3}_{x_{2}}\Psi\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{2}}\omega\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|\partial^{2}_{x_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}\Psi\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\|\omega\|_{W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

Finally, (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) yield

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{p}\|\omega\|_{W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

which is the desired conclusion.
The case
m\geq 3
can be proved by the induction argument. We omit the details for simplicity and the proof of Proposition
3.1
is complete.

The following estimate is a Schauder-type one on the gradient of the velocity, which will be used to derive its maximum bound in Lemma
5.2
. The estimate in the whole plane can be found in
[
49
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\omega\in C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with some
0<\gamma<1
and
1\leq q<2
. Suppose that
\Psi
is defined by (
3.2
) and
u=\nabla^{\bot}\Psi
. Then the Hessian
\nabla^{2}\Psi
satisfies

\displaystyle[\nabla^{2}\Psi]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{\gamma}[\omega]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{\gamma}[\omega]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}.

Remark 3.1
.

The condition
\omega\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
1\leq q<2
in Proposition
3.2
is needed to obtain the local maximum estimate of the stream function
\Psi

(
see (
3.17
)
)
. But the Hölder semi-norm estimates of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}\Psi
and the gradient velocity
\nabla u
do not depend on the quantity
\|\omega\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
. Therefore the integrability condition
\omega\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
1\leq q<2
may be removed by a suitable density argument.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.
Since

u=\nabla^{\bot}\Psi
, we have
[\nabla u]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}\leq[\nabla^{2}\Psi]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}
.
Thus it suffices to estimate
[\nabla^{2}\Psi]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}
.
For any
R>0
,
it follows from (
3.1
) that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\Psi(x)=\omega(x),~x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in B^{+}_{2R}(0),\\
\ &\Psi(x_{1},0)=0.\end{aligned}\ \right.

By the definition of
\|\Psi\|^{\prime}_{C^{2,\gamma}(\bar{B}^{+}_{R}(0))}
and Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle(2R)^{2+\gamma}[\nabla^{2}\Psi]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{B}^{+}_{R}(0))}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\Psi\|^{\prime}_{C^{2,\gamma}(\bar{B}^{+}_{R}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}\Big(\|\Psi\|_{C^{0}(\bar{B}^{+}_{2R}(0))}+R^{2}\|\omega\|^{\prime}_{C^{0,\gamma}(\bar{B}^{+}_{2R}(0))}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}\Big(\|\Psi\|_{C^{0}(\bar{B}^{+}_{2R}(0))}+R^{2}\|\omega\|_{C^{0}(\bar{B}^{+}_{2R}(0))}+R^{2+\gamma}[\omega]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{B}^{+}_{2R}(0))}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}\Big(\|\Psi\|_{C^{0}(\bar{B}^{+}_{2R}(0))}+R^{2}\|\omega\|_{C^{0}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}+R^{2+\gamma}[\omega]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}\Big).

Dividing the factor
(2R)^{2+\gamma}
on both sides of (
3.11
) implies

\displaystyle[\nabla^{2}\Psi]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{B}^{+}_{R}(0))}\leq C_{\gamma}\Big(\frac{1}{R^{2+\gamma}}\|\Psi\|_{C^{0}(\bar{B}^{+}_{2R}(0))}+\frac{1}{R^{\gamma}}\|\omega\|_{C^{0}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}+[\omega]_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}\Big).

(3.12)

We proceed to estimate the term
\|\Psi\|_{C^{0}(\bar{B}^{+}_{2R}(0))}
by utilizing the explicit expression (
3.2
).
For any
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\bar{B}^{+}_{2R}(0)
, one easily has that
x_{2}\leq 2R
. We rewrite the expression (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle\Psi(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\log|x-y|-\log|x-\bar{y}|\Big)\omega(y)dy

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B_{1}(x)}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{1}(x))^{c}}\Big)\Big(\log|x-y|-\log|x-\bar{y}|\Big)\omega(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B_{1}(x)}\log|x-y|\omega(y)dy-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B_{1}(x)}\log|x-\bar{y}|\omega(y)dy

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{1}(x))^{c}}\Big(\log|x-y|-\log|x-\bar{y}|\Big)\omega(y)dy

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle J_{11}+J_{12}+J_{2}.

J_{11}
is estimated as

\displaystyle|J_{11}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B_{1}(x)}|\log|x-y|||\omega(y)|dy

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\int_{B_{1}(x)}\log|x-y|dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\int^{1}_{0}r\log rdr

\displaystyle=

Lemma 4.2
.

The mapping

.

, there exists exactly one

such that

which implies that

belongs to

The uniqueness is sketched as follows, which is similar to
Step 3
in the proof of Lemma

.
Suppose that
\omega_{i}\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))\cap{\rm Lip}([0,T_{0}];W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
with

and
1<p<2
,

are two solutions to (

) with the same initial data

. Then

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\omega_{1}+u_{1}\cdot\nabla\omega_{1}=0,\\

(4.9)

Subtracting the equations in (

) yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})+(u_{1}-u_{2})\cdot\nabla\omega_{1}+u_{2}\cdot\nabla(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})=0.

Similar to (

), we can obtain

]
). In fact, it is direct to deduce that

.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
which is on global well-posedness to (
1.4
). Before that, we prove some useful facts for preparations. The first lemma is the maximum principle of the solutions to (
1.4
), which states that the
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
norm of the vorticity is conserved for all times.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
1\leq s\leq\infty
. If
\omega
is a smooth solution to (
1.4
), then
\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}=\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
for any
t>0
.

Proof
.
Denote by
\Phi_{t}(x)
the flow map corresponding to the two-dimentional Euler evolution in the half plane:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{t}(x)=u(\Phi_{t}(x),x),\\
\ &\Phi_{0}(x)=x.\end{aligned}\ \right.

Then it follows from (
3.4
) and sufficient regularity of
u
that (see, e.g.,
[
49
]
)

\displaystyle\omega(x,t)=\omega_{0}(\Phi_{-t}(x))~{\rm and}~{\rm det}\Big(\nabla_{x}\Phi_{t}(x)\Big)=1,

(5.1)

where
\Phi_{-t}
is the inverse of
\Phi_{t}
. For
s\in[1,\infty)
, in view of (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|\omega(t)\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\int^{\infty}_{0}\tau^{s-1}\mu_{\omega_{t}}(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\int^{\infty}_{0}\tau^{s-1}\mu_{\omega_{0}}(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\omega_{0}\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

where we have used the fact that the distribution function of the vorticity
\omega(\cdot,t):=\omega_{t}

keeps same for all times:

\displaystyle\mu_{\omega_{t}}(\tau)=\mu_{\omega_{0}}(\tau)~for~all~\tau>0.

Indeed, by virtue of (
2.2
), (
5.1
) and a change of variables, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{\omega_{t}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:|\omega(x,t)|>\tau\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:|\omega_{0}(\Phi_{-t}(x))|>\tau\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:|\omega_{0}(\Phi_{-t}(x))|>\tau\}}1~dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:|\omega_{0}(y)|>\tau\}}{\rm det}\Big(\nabla_{y}\Phi_{t}(y)\Big)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:|\omega_{0}(y)|>\tau\}}1~dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:|\omega_{0}(y)|>\tau\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\omega_{0}}(\tau).

For
s=\infty
,
\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}=\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
is a consequence of (
5.1
). The proof of the lemma is finished.

The second lemma is concerned with a Kato-type estimate of the velocity in the half plane case. The similar bound was first obtained by Kato
[
31
]
in the whole plane case. We sketch its proof for completeness in the spirit of
[
39
]
, where the bounded domain case was treated. The similar estimates in three-dimensional whole space and smooth bounded domains of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
can be found in
[
5
]
and
[
22
]
, respectively.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
0<\gamma<1
and
1\leq q<2
, it holds

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{\gamma}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big(1+\log\Big(1+\frac{[\omega(t)]_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)\Big)+C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{q}(\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})})\Big(1+\log\Big(3+\frac{\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)\Big),

for
1\leq q<2
.

Proof
. We first estimate
\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
.
From (
3.3
), the velociry is expressed as

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\frac{(x-y)^{\bot}}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{(x-\bar{y})^{\bot}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}\Big)\omega(y,t)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(x-y)^{\bot}}{|x-y|^{2}}\bar{\omega}(y,t)dy,

where
\bar{\omega}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the odd extension of
\omega
to the whole plane.
Therefore, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
and
1\leq q<2
, by virtue of Hölder inequality and Lemma
5.1
, it yields

\displaystyle|u(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\bar{\omega}(y,t)|dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\bar{\omega}(y,t)|dy+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|x-y|>1}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\bar{\omega}(y,t)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\|\bar{\omega}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}\frac{dy}{|x-y|}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\|\bar{\omega}(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\||z|^{-1}\|_{L^{q\prime}((B_{1}(x))^{c},dz)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+C_{q}\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

where
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
for
1<q<2
and we define
q^{\prime}=\infty
when
q=1
.
It shows that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

Hence it remains to estimate
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
.
By Lemma
A.2
, the gradient of the velocity is expressed as

\displaystyle\nabla u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}M(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy+\frac{\omega(x,t)}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

(5.2)

where the kernel matrix
M(x,y)
is the sum of singular part
M_{s}(x,y)
and regular part
M_{r}(x,y)
:

\displaystyle M_{s}(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{-2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}-y_{2})}{|x-y|^{4}}&\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}\\
\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}&\frac{2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}-y_{2})}{|x-y|^{4}}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle M_{r}(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}+y_{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}&-\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}\\
-\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}&-\frac{2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}+y_{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}\end{pmatrix}.

The second term of (
5.2
) is controlled by
C\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
by Lemma
5.1
.
Therefore it suffices to deal with the principle integral in (
5.2
). We proceed to set
\delta(t):=\Big(\frac{\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega(t)\|_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}
. Then it is clear that
0<\delta(t)\leq 1
.

For an interior point
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
with
x_{2}={\rm dist}(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})>2\delta(t)
, we have
B_{2\delta(t)}(x)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. Then it follows that

\displaystyle|P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}M(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy|\leq I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

(5.3)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B_{\delta(t)}(x)}M(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy|

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B_{\delta(t)}(x)}M_{s}(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy|+|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B_{\delta(t)}(x)}M_{r}(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy|

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{11}+I_{12},

\displaystyle I_{2}=|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{1}(x)\setminus B_{\delta(t)}(x))}M(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy|,

and

\displaystyle I_{3}=|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{1}(x))^{c}}M(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy|.

For
I_{11}
, utilizing
P.V.\int_{|x-y|<\delta(t)}M_{s}(x,y)dy=0
,
|M_{s}(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{-2}
, Lemma
5.1
and the definition of
\delta(t)
, we have

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|P.V.\int_{|x-y|<\delta(t)}M_{s}(x,y)\Big(\omega(y,t)-\omega(x,t)\Big)dy|

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P.V.\int_{|x-y|<\delta(t)}|M_{s}(x,y)||\omega(y,t)-\omega(x,t)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C[\omega(t)]_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\int_{|x-y|<\delta(t)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}|x-y|^{\gamma}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\omega(t)\|_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\int_{|z|<\delta(t)}\frac{dz}{|z|^{2-\gamma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\gamma}(\delta(t))^{\gamma}\|\omega(t)\|_{C^{\gamma}\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\gamma}\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\gamma}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

For
I_{12}
, using
|M_{r}(x,y)|\leq C|x-\bar{y}|^{-2}
, Lemma
5.1
and
x_{2}>2\delta(t)
, we can estimate
I_{12}
as follows.

\displaystyle I_{12}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B_{\delta(t)}(x)}|M_{r}(x,y)||\omega(y,t)|dy

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B_{\delta(t)}(x)}|x-\bar{y}|^{-2}|\omega(y,t)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big(\frac{\delta(t)}{x_{2}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

where we have used
|x-\bar{y}|>x_{2}
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
.

For
I_{2}
, utilizing
|M(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{-2}
, Hölder inequality, the definition of
\delta(t)
and Lemma
5.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{1}(x)\setminus B_{\delta(t)}(x))}|M(x,y)||\omega(y,t)|dy

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\int_{\delta(t)\leq|x-y|<1}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\int_{\delta(t)\leq|z|<1}\frac{dz}{|z|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\log\frac{1}{\delta(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\gamma}\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\log\Big(1+\frac{[\omega(t)]_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\gamma}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\log\Big(1+\frac{[\omega(t)]_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big),

where in the last inequality we have used the fact that the function
x\mapsto x{\rm log}(1+\frac{\beta}{x})
is non-decreasing on
(0,\infty)
for all
\beta\geq 0
.

For
I_{3}
, using
|M(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{-2}
, Hölder inequality and Lemma
5.1
, we obtain, for
1\leq q<2
,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{1}(x))^{c}}|M(x,y)||\omega(y,t)|dy

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{1}(x))^{c}}\frac{|\omega(y,t)|}{|x-y|^{2}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\||z|^{-2}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}((B_{1}(x))^{c},dz)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{q}\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

Combining (
5.3
)-(
5.8
) yields the desired estimate of
\nabla u
at interior points.

For a point nearby the boundary,
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
with
x_{2}={\rm dist}(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\leq 2\delta(t)
, we define
z=(x_{1},x_{2}+2\delta(t))
. Then it is clear that
{\rm dist}(z,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})=x_{2}+2\delta(t)>2\delta(t)
and
|x-z|=2\delta(t)
.
It follows that

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x,t)-\nabla u(z,t)|+|\nabla u(z,t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[\nabla u(t)]_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}|x-z|^{\gamma}+|\nabla u(z,t)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{\gamma}(\delta(t))^{\gamma}[\nabla u(t)]_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+|\nabla u(z,t)|.

Utilizing Proposition
3.2
, the definition of
\delta(t)
, Lemma
5.1
and the estimate of
\nabla u
at interior points
z
above, we obtain, for
0<\gamma<1
and
1\leq q<2
,

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}(\delta(t))^{\gamma}[\omega(t)]_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+|\nabla u(z,t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big(1+\log\Big(1+\frac{[\omega(t)]_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)\Big)+C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

which is the desired estimate of
\nabla u
at points near the boundary. Furthermore, applying the fact
[\omega(t)]_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq 2\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
finally implies that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{q}(\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})})\Big(1+\log\Big(3+\frac{\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)\Big),

for
1\leq q<2
.

The proof of the lemma is complete.

We are now in a position to derive the double exponential growth in time of the gradient vorticity of the two-dimensional incompressible Euler equations in the half plane, which is a key ingredient in the proof of global regularity of the solutions to (
1.4
).

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\omega_{0}
be smooth initial data for the 2-D Euler equation (
1.4
) in the half plane. Then the solution
\omega
to (
1.4
) satisfies

\displaystyle 1+\log\Big(3+\frac{\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)\leq\Big(1+\log\Big(3+\frac{\|\nabla\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)\Big)e^{C(\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})})t},

where the constant
C
depends on
q\in[1,2)
.

Proof
.
Applying the gradient operator
\nabla
on the first equation in (
1.4
), and taking its dot product by
\nabla\omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\omega|^{2}\Big)+u\cdot\nabla\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\omega|^{2}\Big)+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\partial_{i}u^{j}\partial_{i}\omega\partial_{j}\omega=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|\nabla\omega|+u\cdot\nabla|\nabla\omega|=-\beta|\nabla\omega|,

(5.9)

where

\displaystyle\beta(x,t):=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\partial_{i}u^{j}\frac{\partial_{i}\omega}{|\nabla\omega|}\frac{\partial_{j}\omega}{|\nabla\omega|}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\nabla\omega|(\Phi_{t}(x),t)=-\beta(\Phi_{t}(x),t)|\nabla\omega|(\Phi_{t}(x),t),

where
\Phi
is the flow map generated by the velocity field
u
. Hence,

\displaystyle|\nabla\omega|(\Phi_{t}(x),t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla\omega_{0}(x)|e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(\Phi_{\tau}(x),\tau)d\tau}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\nabla\omega_{0}(x)|e^{\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla u|(\Phi_{\tau}(x),\tau)d\tau},

where we have used
|\beta(x,t)|\leq|\nabla u(x,t)|
in the last inequality.
It deduces that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}e^{\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}d\tau}.

It follows from Lemma
5.2
that

\displaystyle\log\Big(\frac{\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)\leq\log\Big(\frac{\|\nabla\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)+CA\int_{0}^{t}\Big(1+\log\Big(3+\frac{\|\nabla\omega(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big)\Big)d\tau,

with
A:=\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
.
Consider

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt}\log z(t)=CA(1+\log(3+z(t))),\\
&z(0)=\frac{\|\nabla\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}.\end{aligned}\ \right.

(5.10)

By Lemma
2.3
, we have
\frac{\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\leq z(t)
. To bound
z(t)
, we solve (
5.10
) directly to obtain

\displaystyle\int^{z(t)}_{z(0)}\frac{dy}{y(1+\log(3+y))}=CAt.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int^{z(t)}_{z(0)}\frac{dy}{(3+y)(1+\log(3+y))}\leq CAt,

and

\displaystyle 1+\log\Big(3+z(t)\Big)\leq\Big(1+\log\Big(3+z(0)\Big)\Big)\exp(CAt),

which concludes the proof of Lemma
5.3
.

Combining Lemma
5.2
and Proposition
5.3
immediately yields the estimate on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))}
for any
T>0
.

Corollary 5.4
.

For any
1\leq q<2
, it holds that, for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{q}ABe^{C_{q}At},

with

\displaystyle A=\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

and

\displaystyle B=1+\log\Big(3+\frac{\|\nabla\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}}\Big).

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For any fixed
T>0
. By Theorem
1.1
, (
1.4
) has a local unique solution
\omega\in C([0,T^{\ast});W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
for the initial data
\omega_{0}\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
k\geq 3
and
1<p<2
, where
T^{\ast}
is the first blow-up time.
To prove the global regularity of
\omega
, it suffices to show that
T^{\ast}>T
. By the classical bootstrap and continuity argument, we only need to derive the uniform estimate of
\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t<T^{\ast}}\|\omega(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
.
For this purpose, by a standard process of approximating the initial data and local well-posedness Theorem
1.1
, we can assume
\omega\in C([0,T^{\ast});W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
. Then,
for arbitrary multi-indice
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
0<|\alpha|\leq k
, applying
\partial^{\alpha}
on the first equation of (
1.4
) gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\partial^{\alpha}\omega+\partial^{\alpha}(u\cdot\nabla\omega)-u\cdot\nabla\partial^{\alpha}\omega=0.

(5.11)

Multiplying (
5.11
) by
|\partial^{\alpha}\omega|^{p-2}\partial^{\alpha}\omega
, integrating the resulting equation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, making
integration by parts, and utilizing (
3.4
), Hölder inequality, Corollary
2.6
and Proposition
3.1
, we can finally arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}\omega(t)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}(\partial^{\alpha}(u\cdot\nabla\omega)-u\cdot\nabla\partial^{\alpha}\omega)|\partial^{\alpha}\omega|^{p-2}(\partial^{\alpha}\omega)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}(u\cdot\nabla\omega)-u\cdot\nabla\partial^{\alpha}\omega\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\partial^{\alpha}\omega\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\|u\|_{W^{|\alpha|,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\nabla\omega\|_{W^{|\alpha|-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big)\|\partial^{\alpha}\omega\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big)\|\omega(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\partial^{\alpha}\omega\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

It follows that, for any
0<|\alpha|\leq k
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}\omega(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C\Big(\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big)\|\omega(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

(5.12)

Moreover, it is straightforward to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}=0.

(5.13)

Combining (
5.12
) with (
5.13
) implies that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\omega(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C\Big(\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big)\|\omega(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

(5.14)

Hence, applying the Gronwall’s inequality leads to

\displaystyle\|\omega(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq\|\omega_{0}\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}e^{C{\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\omega(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|u(\tau)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}d\tau}},

(5.15)

for all
t\in[0,T^{\ast})
.

We proceed to give the estimate of
\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
.
Taking the inner product of (
5.9
) with
|\nabla\omega|
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, making integration by parts, utilizing (
3.4
) and Hölder inequality, we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\omega(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\beta|\nabla\omega|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\beta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\nabla\omega(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\nabla\omega(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

which deduces that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

Therefore, by the Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain, for all
t\in[0,T^{\ast})
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq\|\nabla\omega_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}e^{\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}d\tau}.

(5.16)

By Corollary
5.4
, we have, for any
t\in[0,T^{\ast})
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq Ce^{Ct},

(5.17)

where the constants
C=C\Big(p,\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},\|\nabla\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big)
.
Combining (
5.16
) and (
5.17
) yields that, for all
t\in[0,T^{\ast})
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq\|\nabla\omega_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}e^{Ce^{Ct}}.

(5.18)

Substituting (
5.17
)-(
5.18
) into (
5.15
) implies

\displaystyle\|\omega(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq\|\omega_{0}\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}e^{Ce^{Ct}}e^{C\|\nabla\omega_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\int_{0}^{t}e^{Ce^{C\tau}}d\tau},

for all
t\in[0,T^{\ast})
.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
is complete.

Appendix A
EXISTENCE AND UNIQUENESS OF SOLUTIONS TO A LINEAR TRANSPORT EQUATION

In this appendix, we give the proof of Lemma
4.1
, which asserts the existence and uniqueness of solutions to a linear transport equation (
4.2
). For convenience, we first state a global approximation by functions smooth up to boundary (see, e.g.,
[
24
]
).

Lemma A.1
.

C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
is dense in
W^{m,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
for
m\geq 1
an integer and
1\leq s<\infty
. That is, for
g\in W^{m,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
, there exists a sequence
\{g^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
such that
g^{n}
converges to
g
in
W^{m,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
.

Proof
. For
g\in W^{m,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
, we define the approximate functions as the translated mollifications of
g
, given by

\displaystyle g^{n}(x)=n^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho\Big(n(x-y)+2e_{N}\Big)g(y)dy,~x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},

where
e_{N}
denotes the unit coordinate vector in the
x_{N}
direction and
\rho
is a non-negative function in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
vanishing outside the unit ball
B_{1}(0)
and satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho(x)dx=1
.
Then it is readily verified that
g^{n}
is in
C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
and
g^{n}
converges to
g
in
W^{m,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
. The proof of the lemma is finished.

We are now in a position to prove Lemma
4.1
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.
We will focus on the existence part. The uniqueness part is a direct result of the standard
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
energy estimate.
For any fixed time
t\in[0,T]
,
\omega(t)\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
,
by Lemma
A.1
, there exist functions
\omega^{n}(t)\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})
such that

\displaystyle\|\omega^{n}-\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))}\rightarrow 0,~{\rm as}~n\rightarrow\infty,

and

\displaystyle\|\omega^{n}-\omega\|_{C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))}\rightarrow 0,~{\rm as}~n\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore
\{\omega^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in

L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
.
Define the stream function sequence
\{\Psi^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\Psi^{n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\log|x-y|-\log|x-\bar{y}|\Big)\omega^{n}(y,t)dy.

Clearly,
\Psi^{n}
satisfies the following Poisson equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition£º

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\Psi^{n}(\cdot,t)=\omega^{n}(\cdot,t),~x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\\
&\Psi^{n}(x,t)=0,~x\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.\end{aligned}\ \right.

The velocity field sequence
u^{n}(\cdot,t):=\nabla^{\bot}\Psi^{n}(\cdot,t)
can be written as

\displaystyle u^{n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\frac{(x-y)^{\bot}}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{(x-\bar{y})^{\bot}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}\Big)\omega^{n}(y,t)dy.

(A.1)

Clearly,
u^{n}(x,t)
is divergence free and tangential to the boundary
\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,
that is,

\displaystyle{\rm div}~u^{n}(x,t)=0,~x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}~{\rm and}~u^{n}_{2}(x_{1},0,t)=0,~x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}.

(A.2)

By the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
for
1<p<2

and Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla u^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\omega^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))}.

Then the standard Cauchy-Lipchitz theorem ensures that we can define a smooth flow map
\Phi^{n}_{t}(\cdot)
on
[0,T]\times\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
through
u^{n}
.
From the characteristic method, the function
\theta^{n}:[0,T]\times\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle\theta^{n}(x,t)=\omega^{n}(\Phi^{n}_{-t}(x),0),

is a unique smooth solution to the following linear transport equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\theta^{n}+u^{n}\cdot\nabla\theta^{n}=0,~(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&\theta^{n}(\cdot,0)=\omega^{n}(\cdot,0).\end{aligned}\ \right.

(A.3)

Here
\Phi^{n}_{-t}
is the inverse of
\Phi^{n}_{t}
.

Next, we derive some uniform estimates for the approximate sequence
\{\theta^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
It is direct to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta^{n}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}=0.

(A.4)

For arbitrary multi-indice
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
0<|\alpha|\leq k
, operating
\partial^{\alpha}
on the first eqaution of (
A.3
) gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}+u^{n}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}+\partial^{\alpha}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla\theta^{n})-u^{n}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}=0.

(A.5)

Multiplying (
A.5
) by
|\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}|^{p-2}\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}
, integrating the resulting equation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, making integration by parts and utilizing (
A.2
), Hölder inequality, Corollary
2.6
, (
4.8
), Sobolev embedding
W^{1,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
for
1<p<2
and Proposition
3.1
, we can finally obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}(t)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\partial^{\alpha}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla\theta^{n})-u^{n}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}\Big)|\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}|^{p-2}(\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla\theta^{n})-u^{n}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\|u^{n}\|_{W^{|\alpha|,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\nabla\theta^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\ +\|u^{n}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\nabla\theta^{n}\|_{W^{|\alpha|-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big)\|\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u^{n}\|_{W^{k,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\theta^{n}\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\omega^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\theta^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

where the constant
C
is independent of
n
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}\theta^{n}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C\|\omega^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\theta^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

(A.6)

for any
0<|\alpha|\leq k
.
Combining (
A.4
) with (
A.6
) leads to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C\|\omega^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\theta^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

Using the Gronwall’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|\theta^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\theta^{n}(0)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}e^{C{\int_{0}^{t}\|\omega^{n}(\tau)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}d\tau}}

(A.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\omega^{n}(0)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}e^{CT\|\omega^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))}}.

Since
\{\omega^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in

L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
, it follows that
\{\theta^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
.
Furthermore, utilizing (
A.3
), Corollary
2.6
, Proposition
3.1
, (
4.8
) and the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
for
1<p<2
, we can arrive at

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\theta^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|u^{n}\cdot\nabla\theta^{n}\|_{W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\|u^{n}\|_{W^{k-1,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\nabla\theta^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}+\|u^{n}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\nabla\theta^{n}\|_{W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\omega^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\theta^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

which shows that
\{\partial_{t}\theta^{n}(t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
.
Then
\{\theta^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is also uniformly bounded in
{\rm Lip}([0,T];W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
.
Next we show that
\{\theta^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
.
It follows from (
A.3
) that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\theta^{m}+u^{m}\cdot\nabla\theta^{m}=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\theta^{n}+u^{n}\cdot\nabla\theta^{n}=0.\end{aligned}\ \right.

(A.8)

Subtracting the equations in (
A.8
) gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\theta^{m}-\theta^{n})+u^{m}\cdot\nabla(\theta^{m}-\theta^{n})+(u^{m}-u^{n})\cdot\nabla\theta^{n}=0.

Then, similar to (
4.7
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta^{m}(t)-\theta^{n}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C\|\theta^{n}(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|\omega^{m}(t)-\omega^{n}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

which shows that
\{\theta^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
.
By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|f\|_{W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C\|f\|^{\frac{1}{k}}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\|f\|^{1-\frac{1}{k}}_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}

and the uniform boundness of
\{\theta^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
, we can find that the sequence

\{\theta^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is also Cauchy in
C([0,T];W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
.
Thus, there exists a
\theta
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))\cap{\rm Lip}([0,T\rfloor;W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
such that

\displaystyle\theta_{n}\rightarrow\theta~{\rm in}~C([0,T];W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})),~{\rm as}~n\rightarrow\infty,

(A.9)

\displaystyle\theta_{n}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\theta~{\rm in}~L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})),~{\rm as}~n\rightarrow\infty,

(A.10)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta_{n}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\partial_{t}\theta~{\rm in}~L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})),~{\rm as}~n\rightarrow\infty.

(A.11)

Moreover, by Proposition
3.1
, we have
\|u^{n}-u\|_{W^{k,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{k,p}\|\omega^{n}-\omega\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
. Thus
u^{n}
converges to
u
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{k,\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}))
. Then it follows from (
A.9
)-(
A.11
) that
\theta
is a solution to (
4.2
).
Also, from (
A.7
), we can obtain, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|\theta(t)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq\|\omega_{0}\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}e^{C{\int_{0}^{t}\|\omega(\tau)\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}d\tau}}.

The proof of the lemma is complete.

Lemma A.2
.

Let the velocity field
u(x,t)
be defined by

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}K(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy,

where
K(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big(\frac{(x-y)^{\bot}}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{(x-\bar{y})^{\bot}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}\Big)
, and
\omega
is the vorticity of
u
.
Then

\displaystyle\nabla u(x,t)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\nabla_{x}K(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy+\frac{\omega(x,t)}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\nabla u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}M(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy+\frac{\omega(x,t)}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

where the kernel matrix

\displaystyle M(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{-2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}-y_{2})}{|x-y|^{4}}+\frac{2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}+y_{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}&\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}-\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}\\
\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}-\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}-(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}&\frac{2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}-y_{2})}{|x-y|^{4}}-\frac{2(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}+y_{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{4}}\end{pmatrix}.

Consequently, the velocity field
u
satisfies the divergence-free condition

\displaystyle{\rm div}~u(x,t)=0,~x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

Proof
.
For any
1\leq i,j\leq 2
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, by the definition of the distributional derivative and Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{j}u^{i},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle u^{i},\partial_{j}\varphi\rangle

(A.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}u^{i}(x)\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}K^{i}(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy\Big)\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega(y,t)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}K^{i}(x,y)\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(x)dx\Big)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega(y,t)I_{ij}(y)dy,

where the integrals
I_{ij}(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}K^{i}(x,y)\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(x)dx
.

By the dominated convergence theorem and integration by parts,
I_{ij}(y)
is expressed as

\displaystyle I_{ij}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}K^{i}(x,y)\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(x)dx

(A.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{\varepsilon}(y))^{c}}K^{i}(x,y)\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\Big(\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}K^{i}(x,y)\varphi(x)n_{j}(x)dS_{x}-\int_{|x-y|=\varepsilon}K^{i}(x,y)\varphi(x)\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{|x-y|}dS_{x}\Big)

\displaystyle\ -\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{\varepsilon}(y))^{c}}\partial_{x_{j}}K^{i}(x,y)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{\varepsilon}(y))^{c}}\partial_{x_{j}}K^{i}(x,y)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle\ -\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon\int_{|z|=1}K^{i}(y+\varepsilon z,y)\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}.

Therefore, using the Fubini’s theorem again and (
A.12
)-(
A.13
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{j}u^{i},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega(y,t)\Big(\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{\varepsilon}(y))^{c}}\partial_{x_{j}}K^{i}(x,y)\varphi(x)dx\Big)dy

(A.14)

\displaystyle\ +\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega(y,t)\Big(\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon\int_{|z|=1}K^{i}(y+\varepsilon z,y)\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}\Big)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap(B_{\varepsilon}(x))^{c}}\partial_{x_{j}}K^{i}(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy\Big)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle\ +\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega(y,t)\Big(\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon\int_{|z|=1}K^{i}(y+\varepsilon z,y)\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}\Big)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{x_{j}}K^{i}(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy\Big)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle\ +\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega(y,t)\Big(\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon\int_{|z|=1}K^{i}(y+\varepsilon z,y)\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}\Big)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{x_{j}}K^{i}(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega(y,t)\Big(\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon\int_{|z|=1}K^{i}(y+\varepsilon z,y)\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}\Big)dy.

\int_{|z|=1}z_{j}z_{2}dS_{z}=\delta_{2j}\pi
deduce that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon\int_{|z|=1}K^{1}(y+\varepsilon z,y)\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}

(A.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon\int_{|z|=1}\Big(\frac{y_{2}+\varepsilon z_{2}-y_{2}}{|y+\varepsilon z-y|^{2}}-\frac{y_{2}+\varepsilon z_{2}+y_{2}}{|y+\varepsilon z-\bar{y}|^{2}}\Big)\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\int_{|z|=1}\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}z_{2}dS_{z}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\int_{|z|=1}\frac{\varepsilon(2y_{2}+\varepsilon z_{2})}{|2y_{2}e_{2}+\varepsilon z|^{2}}\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\varphi(y)\int_{|z|=1}z_{j}z_{2}dS_{z}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\varphi(y)\delta_{2j},

where

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if $i=j,$ }\\
0,&\mbox{if $i\neq j.$ }\\
\end{cases}

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon\int_{|z|=1}K^{2}(y+\varepsilon z,y)\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}

(A.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon\int_{|z|=1}\Big(\frac{y_{1}-(y_{1}+\varepsilon z_{1})}{|y+\varepsilon z-y|^{2}}-\frac{y_{1}-(y_{1}+\varepsilon z_{1})}{|y+\varepsilon z-\bar{y}|^{2}}\Big)\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\int_{|z|=1}\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{1}z_{j}dS_{z}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0^{+}}\int_{|z|=1}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}z_{1}}{|2y_{2}e_{2}+\varepsilon z|^{2}}\varphi(y+\varepsilon z)z_{j}dS_{z}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\varphi(y)\int_{|z|=1}z_{1}z_{j}dS_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\varphi(y)\delta_{1j},

where we have used
\int_{|z|=1}z_{1}z_{j}dS_{z}=\delta_{1j}\pi
.

Combining (

)-(

), it then holds that, for every

,

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{j}u^{1},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{x_{j}}K^{1}(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy,\varphi\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega(y,t)\frac{1}{2}\varphi(y)\delta_{2j}dy

(A.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{x_{j}}K^{i}(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2j}\omega(\cdot,t),\varphi\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{j}u^{2},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{x_{j}}K^{2}(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy,\varphi\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega(y,t)\frac{1}{2}\varphi(y)\delta_{1j}dy

(A.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{x_{j}}K^{i}(x,y)\omega(y,t)dy-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1j}\omega(\cdot,t),\varphi\rangle.

By the definition of the distribution derivatives and (
A.17
)-(
A.18
), we conclude the proof of the first part of Lemma
A.2
.
The rest of the proof can be deduced from some standard computations.

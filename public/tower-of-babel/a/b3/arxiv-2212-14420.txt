Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to identify the pong algebra defined in our earlier work with a certain endomorphism algebra in the wrapped Fukaya category of the symmetric product of a disk.



1.
Introduction

In
[
17
]
, we introduced a differential graded algebra, the
pong algebra
, which is an enrichment of the strands algebra
from
[
12
]
. The aim of this note is to identify this
algebra with the endomorphism algebra in a wrapped, relative Fukaya
category, of a distinguished set of objects.

Consider
\mathbb{C}
, equipped with punctures at the points
{\mathbf{P}}=\{j+\frac{1}{2}\}_{j=0}^{m-1}
, and let
M=\mathbb{C}\setminus{\mathbf{P}}
.
Consider
m-1
disjoint vertical lines
e_{j}=j\times\mathbb{R}
for

j=1,\dots,m-1
.

Given any
k
-element subsequence
1\leq x_{1}<\dots<x_{k}\leq m-1
, which
we call a
k
-idempotent state
, there is a corresponding
Lagrangian
{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{x}}=e_{x_{1}}\times\dots\times e_{x_{k}}\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(M\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})

The manifold
\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(M)
is a Liouville manifold, and the Lagrangians

\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}
are exact and conical at infinity. One can then consider
the
wrapped Fukaya category
of introduced by Abouzaid and
Seidel
[
2
]
relative to the divisor
{\mathbf{P}}\times\mathrm{Sym}^{k-1}(\mathbb{C})
; see also
[
1
,
4
]
. We
will consider the endomorphism algebra of the set of objects

\{\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}\}_{\mathbf{x}}
indexed by
k
-idempotent states.

This is an A-infinity algebra
A
over
\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]
,
equipped with idempotents
{\mathbf{I}}_{\mathbf{x}}
correspondingto the idempotent states
\mathbf{x}
,
and
{\mathbf{I}}_{\mathbf{x}}\cdot A\cdot{\mathbf{I}}_{\mathbf{y}}
is given by the chain complex

\mathit{CF}(\phi_{H}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}),\Lambda_{\mathbf{y}})
(again, relative to the divisor

{\mathbf{P}}
). The composition is given by the chan map

\circ\colon\mathrm{Mor}(\Lambda_{x_{2}},\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{3}})\otimes\mathrm{Mor}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{1}},\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{2}})\to\mathrm{Mor}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{1}},\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{3}}),

is specified by the following diagram

(1.1)

\begin{CD}\mathit{CF}(\phi_{H}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{1}}),\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{2}})\otimes\mathit{CF}(\phi_{H}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{2}}),\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{3}})@>{\circ}>{}>\mathit{CF}(\phi_{H}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{1}}),\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{3}})\\
@V{(\phi_{H})_{*}\otimes\Id}V{}V@A{\sigma}A{}A\\
\mathit{CF}(\phi^{2}_{H}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{1}}),\phi_{H}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{2}})\otimes\mathit{CF}(\phi_{H}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{2}}),\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{3}})@>{}>{}>\mathit{CF}(\phi^{2}_{H}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{1}}),\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{3}}),\\
\end{CD}

where
\sigma
is a map induced from the Liouville flow, and the
bottom arrow is induced by counting pseudo-holomorphic triangles. The
base ring is
\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]
; and for the map in the bottom arrow,
each holomorphic triangle

u
is counted as a monomial
v_{1}^{n_{p_{1}}(u)}\dots v_{m}^{n_{p_{m}}(u)}
.

We can take the homology to obtain an ordinary category,
H({\mathcal{C}})
; or alternatively, we can consider the
A_{\infty}
category,
where the higher compositions are defined by counting
pseudo-holomorphic polygons.

Our aim is to prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

The pong algebra is isomorphic to the endomorphism algebra, in the
wrapped relative Fukaya category, of the objects

\{\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}\}_{\mathbf{x}}
.

This proof is in the spirit of Auroux
[
3
]
; see
also
[
13
]
. Indeed, the proof is a fairly straightforward
application of a suitable Heegaard diagram, which can be thought of as
the analogue of the “Auroux-Zarev piece” for the pong algebra;
see
[
3
,
26
,
11
]
.

It is interesting to compare the results herein with the results
of
[
8
,
14
]
, and the constructions
of
[
21
,
6
,
27
,
7
]
.

Our interest in the pong algebra stems from our
goal of understanding knot Floer homology, a topic which we do not
discuss in the present paper, but hope to return to in future
work
[
16
]
.

2.
The wrapped Fukaya category

2.1.
The symmetric product

We start with some of the geometric setup, as explained
in
[
2
,
5
,
4
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A Liouville domain is a
2n
-dimensional manifold with
boundary, equipped with a one-form
\lambda
such that

\omega=d\lambda
is symplectic, and the dual vector field, called
the Liouville vector field,
Z
characterized by

i_{Z}\omega=\lambda
points strictly outwards along
\partial M
.

A special case of a Liouville domain is a Stein manifold, which is a
complex manifold
(V,J)
, equipped with a proper, smooth function

\phi\colon V\to\mathbb{R}
, which is
strictly psudo-plurisubharmonic
;
i.e. for which the two-form
\omega=-dd^{\mathbb{C}}\phi
is symplectic and

J
-compatible. (Here,
d^{\mathbb{C}}\phi=d\phi\circ J
.) In this case,

\lambda=-d^{\mathbb{C}}\phi
; i.e.
d\phi=\lambda\circ J
.

Our basic example is the following. Let
A
denote the infinite
cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}\cong\mathbb{C}\setminus 0
. Let
(t,\theta)
denote
the coordinates with respect to the parameterization
\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}
;
so that the isomorphism
\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}\cong\mathbb{C}\setminus 0
is given by

(t,\theta)\mapsto e^{t+i\theta}
; sometimes we write
r=e^{t}
. The
function
\log(r)^{2}
is strictly pluri-subharmonic, with
\omega=2dt\wedge\theta
. Let

H=r^{2}
. The Hamiltonian flow for
H
, written
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}\times A\to A
, is
given by

(2.1)

\Phi(s,t,\theta)=(t,2st\theta).

The Liouville flow, written
\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}\times A\to A
, is given by

(2.2)

\Psi(s,t,\theta)=(te^{s},\theta).

To a Liouville domain Abouzaid and Seidel associate an
A_{\infty}

category, the
wrapped Fukaya category
. Objects are Lagrangian
submanifolds
L\subset M
that intersect
\partial M
transversely,
with the property that
\theta|L\in\Omega^{1}(L)
is exact, and

\theta
vanishes to infinite order along the boundary
\partial L=L\cap\partial M
.

We will be considering Lagrangians in the symmetric product of
\mathbb{C}
,

\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})
. There is a quotient map
\pi\colon\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})
,
and also there is a diffeomorphism
\mathbb{C}^{k}\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})
.
The
relationship between Lagrangians in a symmetric product of a curve
with the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}
-invariant Lagrangians in the
k
-fold
Cartesian product is unclear; but there is a nice bridge offered by
work of Perutz
[
19
]
, building on work of
Varouchas
[
25
]
, who constructs a new symplectic form on
the symmetric product that agrees with group-invariant the symplectic
structure on the Cartesian product on an open set.
The case at hand is a
particularly simple, local version. (See Proposition
2.2
below.)

In the interest of concreteness, we find it convenient to have some explicit
parameterizations. Consider the
map from the infinite cylinder

A=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\cong\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}=\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})
to
\mathbb{C}
,
specified by

p(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(z+\frac{1}{z}\right),

This map has the following properties:

•

p
is is a branched double-cover, with two branched points
at
1
and
-1
.

•

p
is proper

•

The image under
p
of the circle
\{0\}\times S^{1}
is the interval

[-1,1]\subset\mathbb{C}
.

Given a
k
-element subset
\mathbf{x}\subset S^{1}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
, we can view
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbf{x}

as a subset of
\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(A)
. This image is a smooth submanifold,
whose image under
\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(p)
is a smooth submanifold
of
\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})
. We denote this subspace
\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}
.

More generally, fix
{\mathbf{x}}
as above and an element
\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
.
There is a submanifold of
A\cong\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}
of elements of
the form
\{(t,e^{i\phi\cdot t/2}\mathbf{x})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, which induces
a submanifold
\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}^{\phi}\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})
.
Clearly,
\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}^{0}=\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}
.

Let
t\colon A\to\mathbb{R}
be projection to the first coordinate or, equivalently,

z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\mapsto\log|z|
.
Consider the function
\delta\colon C^{k}\to\mathbb{R}\geq 0
defined by

\delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})=\min(\min_{i}t(z_{i}),\min_{i\neq j}|z_{i}-z_{j}|),

which descends to a continuous function
\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{R}
,
so that

\delta^{-1}(0)=\Delta\cup\left(S^{1}\times\mathrm{Sym}^{k-1}(A)\right).

The following is an adaptation of a theorem of Perutz
[
19
]
;
see also Varouchas
[
25
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Given any bounded open set
W\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})
containing

\mathrm{Sym}^{k}[-1,1]
and any
\eta>0
,
there is a smooth plurisubharmonic
function
\psi\colon W\to\mathbb{R}
with the following properties:

•

Given any
k
-elements subset
\mathbf{x}\subset(S^{1}\setminus\{\pm 1\})

with
\delta(\mathbf{x})\geq\eta
,
the intersection of
W
with the submanifold

\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})

is Lagrangian
with respect to the symplectic structure
dd^{\mathbb{C}}\psi
.

•

Given
s\geq 0
, there is an exact Hamiltonian diffeomorphism

\Phi^{s}\colon\mathbb{R}\times W\to W

with the property that

\Phi^{s}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}\cap W)=\Lambda^{s}_{\mathbf{x}}\cap W
.

Proof.

This follows easily from Varouchas’s “Lemme
Principal”
[
25
]
, which which we state in a slightly
simplified form. Given
the data:

•

Open subsets
U
,
V
,
W
, and
X
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

so that
U
,
V
, and
W
are bounded,
with
{\overline{U}}\subset V
,
{\overline{V}}\subset W
,
{\overline{W}}\subset X

•

a continuous, strictly pluri-subharmonic function
\phi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}

so that
\phi|_{X\setminus U}
is smooth.

there is a smooth, strictly pluri-subharmonic function
\chi\colon W\to\mathbb{R}

so that

\psi|_{W\setminus(V\cap W)}=\chi|_{W\setminus(V\cap W)}.

The function
t^{2}\colon\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
t^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|t_{i}|^{2}
is a smooth. Let
X=(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})^{\times k}
,
and
\Pi\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times k}\to\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})
be the quotient map.
As
in
[
25
]
, since
t^{2}\colon A\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth,
strictly pluri-subharmonic function and
\Pi\circ p^{\times k}\colon A^{k}\to\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})
is a branched cover, the push-forward
\psi=(\Pi\circ p^{\times k})_{*}(t^{2})
is a
continuous, strictly pluri-subharmonic function on
X
.
Given
W
as in the statement of the proposition, apply Varouchas’ lemma to
\psi
,

U=\delta^{-1}(0,\frac{\eta}{3})
, and
V=\delta^{-1}(0,\frac{\eta}{2})
. The exact
Hamiltonian is associated to the Hamiltonian function
\chi
coming from the lemma. Since

\chi
agrees with
t^{2}
over the complement of
V
, it is easy to
see that the integral of
\chi
preserves
\delta
over that
set. The second point now follows readily; see Equation (
2.1
).

We fix the following data:

•

integers
m
and
k
with
0<k<m

•

m
basepoints

-1=O_{1},O_{2},\dots,O_{m-1},1=O_{m}

so that there is a positively oriented arc in
S^{1}

from
O_{i+1}
ot
O_{i}
containing no other
O_{j}
.

•

m-1
additional points
p_{1},\dots,p_{m-1}
,
so that
z_{i}
is on the arc from
O_{i+1}
to
O_{i}
.

•

\delta(z_{i},z_{i+1})\geq 2/m-1
.

Choose
W\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{C})
as in
Proposition
2.2
, and let
\psi
be the
function supplied by that proposition. There are

\binom{m-1}{k}
Lagrangians
\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}\cap W
, associated to the
k
-element subsets of

\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m-1}\}
. We will be considering these as our basic
objects in the wrapped Fukaya category of
W
.

Let
\Psi^{c}\colon W\to W
be the time
\log(c)
flow of the Liouville vector field
induced from
\psi
.
It is an easy consequence of Equation (
2.2
)
that

(2.3)

\Psi^{c}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}^{\phi})=\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}^{\phi/c}.

2.2.
The relative Fukaya category

In our case, the symplectic manifold
W
is equipped also with
m

divisors, of the form
\{O_{i}\}\times\mathrm{Sym}^{k-1}(\mathcal{H})
. Correspondingly,
as in
[
15
]
; see also, cite
[
20
]
for a
general construction, the Floer complexes are to be thought of as
modules over a polynomial algebra
\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,vm]
.
Specifically, for
L_{1}=\Phi_{H}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{1}})
,
L_{2}=\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}_{2}}
,
the complex
\mathit{CF}(L_{1},L_{2})
is a module over
\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]

freely generated by
L_{1}\cap L_{2}
, with differential determined by

\partial\mathbf{x}=\sum_{\mathbf{y}}\sum_{\{\phi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mid\mu(\phi)=1\}}\#\mathcal{M}(\phi)\cdot\mathbf{y}\cdot v_{1}^{n_{O_{1}}(\phi)}\cdots v_{m}^{n_{O_{m}}(\phi)}.

Here,
n_{O_{i}}(\phi)
denotes the
(non-negative) algebraic intersection number of
\phi
with the
divisor
\{O_{i}\}\times\mathrm{Sym}^{k-1}(\mathcal{H})
. The moduli space

\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is to be taken with respect to a suitable
perturbation of the Floer equation.

3.
Lifted partial permutations and the pong algebra

We recall the construction of the pong algebra from
[
17
, Section 4]
;
we refer the reader to that reference for a more leisurely account.

Let
r_{t}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the reflection
r_{t}(x)=2t-x
; and consider
the subgroup
G_{m}
of the reflection group of the real line generated
by
r_{\frac{1}{2}}
and
r_{m-\frac{1}{2}}
. The quotient of the integral lattice by
this group of rigid motions is naturally an
m-1
point set; generated
by
\{1,\dots,m-1\}
. Let

(3.1)

Q_{1}\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\{1,\dots,m-1\}

denote this quotient map.

Note that
G_{m}
also acts on the set
\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
. The quotient of
\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
by
G_{m}
is naturally the
m
-point set,
\{\frac{1}{2},\dots,m-\frac{1}{2}\}
. We think of these points as being in one-to-one
correspondence with the underlying variables in the pong algebra,
where the point
j\in\{\frac{1}{2},\dots,m-\frac{1}{2}\}
corresponds to the
variable
v_{\frac{1}{2}+j}
.
Explicitly, we have the map

(3.2)

Q_{2}\colon\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}\to\{1,\dots,m\},

defined so that
Q_{2}(j-\frac{1}{2})
is the element
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}

with

i\equiv j\pmod{2m-2}
or
i\equiv 2-j\pmod{2m-2}
.

A
G_{m}
invariant subset
\widetilde{S}
of
\mathbb{Z}
has a natural quotient
\widetilde{S}/G_{m}
,
which is a subset of
\{1,\dots,m-1\}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A lifted partial permutation on
k
letters
is a pair
({\widetilde{S}},{\widetilde{f}})
where:

•

{\widetilde{S}}\subset\mathbb{Z}

is a
G_{m}
-invariant subset

•

{\widetilde{f}}\colon{\widetilde{S}}\to\mathbb{Z}

is a
G_{m}
-equivariant map;

subject to the following two conditions:

•

{\widetilde{S}}/G_{m}
consists of
k
elements

•

the induced map
{\widetilde{f}}\colon{\widetilde{S}}/G_{m}\to\mathbb{Z}/G_{m}

is injective.

Definition 3.2
.

A lifted partial permutation
({\widetilde{S}},{\widetilde{f}})
has a
weight vector
{\vec{\mathfrak{w}}}=(\mathfrak{w}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{w}_{m})\in(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z})^{m}
, specified
by

\mathfrak{w}_{j}({\widetilde{f}})=\frac{1}{2}\#\{i\in{\widetilde{S}}\big|i<j-\frac{1}{2}<{\widetilde{f}}(i)~\text{or}~i>j-\frac{1}{2}>{\widetilde{f}}(i)\}.

We extend the weight vector to
\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]
so that
\mathfrak{w}(v_{i})

is the
i^{th}
basis vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.

Definition 3.3
.

A crossing in a lifted partial permutation
{\widetilde{f}}
is
an equivalence class of pairs of integers
(i,j)
with the property
that
i<j
and
{\widetilde{f}}(i)>{\widetilde{f}}(j)
. We say that

(i,j)
and
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
determine the same crossing if there is some

g\in G_{m}
so that
\{g\cdot i,g\cdot j\}=\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}
. We write

\langle i,j\rangle
for the equivalence class of the pair of
integers
(i,j)
.
Let
\mathrm{Cross}({\widetilde{f}})
denote the set of crossings in
{\widetilde{f}}
.

Note that
\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}({\widetilde{f}})
does not exclude
cases where
[i]=[j]
.
Let
\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{f}})
denote the number of
crossings in
{\widetilde{f}}
.

Let

({\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}})
and

({\widetilde{g}},{\widetilde{T}})
be two partial permutations with

{\widetilde{T}}={\widetilde{f}}({\widetilde{S}})
.
Then, the composite
({\widetilde{g}}\circ{\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}})

is a lifted partial permutation.

It is elementary to verify that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}({\widetilde{g}}\circ{\widetilde{f}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{w}({\widetilde{g}})+\mathfrak{w}({\widetilde{f}})

\displaystyle\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{g}}\circ{\widetilde{f}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{g}})+\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{f}})

The pong algebra
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
is the algebra over

\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]
freely generated by lifted partial
permutations, with a multiplication map

\mu_{2}\colon{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]}{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)\to{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)

characterized by

\mu_{2}([{\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}}],[{\widetilde{g}},{\widetilde{T}}])]=\begin{cases}0&\text{if ${\widetilde{T}}\neq{\widetilde{f}}({\widetilde{S})}$}\\
0&\text{if $\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{g}}\circ{\widetilde{f}})<\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{g}})+\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{f}})$}\\
v\cdot[{\widetilde{g}}\circ{\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}}]&{\text{otherwise,}}\end{cases}

where
v
is the monomial in
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
chosen so that

\mathfrak{w}(\mu_{2}([{\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}}],[{\widetilde{g}},{\widetilde{T}}]))=\mathfrak{w}[{\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}}]+\mathfrak{w}[{\widetilde{g}},{\widetilde{T}}].

Given
a,b\in{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
, we abbreviate
\mu_{2}(a,b)
by
a\cdot b
.

For each
\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}({\widetilde{f}})
, there is a new lifted partial permutation
{\widetilde{f}}_{\langle i,j\rangle}
characterized as follows:

{\widetilde{f}}_{\langle i,j\rangle}(k)=\begin{cases}{\widetilde{f}}(k)&{\text{if~$[k]\not\in\{[i],[j]\}$}}\\
g\cdot{\widetilde{f}}(j)&{\text{if $k=g\cdot i$}}\\
g\cdot{\widetilde{f}}(i)&{\text{if $k=g\cdot j$}}\end{cases}.

It is elementary to verify that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}({\widetilde{f}}_{\langle i,j\rangle})

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{w}({\widetilde{f}})

\displaystyle\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{f}}_{\langle i,j\rangle})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{f}})-1

Given
\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}({\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}})
,
let
\partial_{\langle i,j\rangle}{\widetilde{f}}\in{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
be the element defined by

\partial_{\langle i,j\rangle}{\widetilde{f}}=\begin{cases}0&{\text{if $\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{f}}_{\langle i,j\rangle})<\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{f}})-1$}}\\
v\cdot{\widetilde{f}}_{\langle i,j\rangle}&{\text{otherwise,}}\end{cases}

where now
v
is the monomial in
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
characterized by the property that

\mathfrak{w}[{\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}}]=\mathfrak{w}[{\widetilde{f}}_{\langle i,j\rangle},{\widetilde{S}}].

Define a map

\partial\colon{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)\to{\mathcal{P}}(m,k),

characterized by

\partial({\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}})=\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle\in\mathrm{Cross}({\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}})}\partial_{\langle i,j\rangle}[{\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{S}}].

With the above definitions,
{\mathcal{P}}(m,k)
is a differential graded
algebra over
\mathbb{F}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]
;
see
[
17
, Proposition
]
for details.

It will be convenient to have the following:

Lemma 3.4
.

If

\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{f}}\circ{\widetilde{g}})=\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{f}})+\mathrm{cross}({\widetilde{g}})

then

\mathfrak{w}_{1}({\widetilde{f}}\circ{\widetilde{g}})=\mathfrak{w}_{1}({\widetilde{f}})+\mathfrak{w}_{1}({\widetilde{g}})\qquad{\text{and}}\qquad\mathfrak{w}_{m}({\widetilde{f}}\circ{\widetilde{g}})=\mathfrak{w}_{m}({\widetilde{f}})+\mathfrak{w}_{m}({\widetilde{g}}).

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{w}_{1}({\widetilde{f}}\circ{\widetilde{g}})\neq\mathfrak{w}_{1}({\widetilde{f}}))+\mathfrak{w}_{1}({\widetilde{g}})
, then there exists some
1<i
with

(3.3)

1<i\text{~such that~}{\widetilde{g}}(i)<1;

and

(3.4)

{\widetilde{f}}\circ{\widetilde{g}}(i)>1.

On the other hand, Equation (
3.3
) implies that

{\widetilde{g}}(1-i)=1-{\widetilde{g}}(i)>1-i;

while Equation (
3.4
) implies that

{\widetilde{f}}\circ{\widetilde{g}}(1-i)>{\widetilde{g}}(1-i);

i.e.
i
and
1-i
have a crossing in
{\widetilde{g}}
, while

{\widetilde{g}}(i)
and
{\widetilde{g}}(1-i)
have a crossing in

{\widetilde{f}}
.

An analogous argument works for

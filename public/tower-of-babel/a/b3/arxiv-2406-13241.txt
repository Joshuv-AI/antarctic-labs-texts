Achirality of Sol 3-Manifolds, Stevenhagen Conjecture and Shimizu’s L-series
Ye Tian, Shicheng Wang, Zhongzi Wang

Abstract
. A closed orientable manifold is achiral if it admits an orientation reversing homeomorphism. A commensurable class of closed manifolds is achiral if it contains an achiral element, or equivalently, each manifold in ℳ {\mathcal{M}} has an achiral finite cover. Each commensurable class containing non-orientable elements must be achiral. It is natural to wonder how many commensurable classes are achiral and how many achiral classes have non-orientable elements. We study this problem for Sol 3-manifolds. Each commensurable class ℳ {\mathcal{M}} of Sol 3-manifold has a complete topological invariant D ℳ D_{{\mathcal{M}}} , the discriminant of ℳ {\mathcal{M}} . Our main result is: (1) Among all commensurable classes of Sol 3-manifolds, there are infinitely many achiral classes; however ordered by discriminants, the density of achiral commensurable classes is 0. (2) Among all achiral commensurable classes of Sol 3-manifolds, ordered by discriminants, the density of classes containing non-orientable elements is 1 − ρ 1-\rho , where ρ := ∏ j = 1 ∞ ( 1 + 2 − j ) − 1 = 0.41942 ⋯ . \rho:=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(1+2^{-j}\right)^{-1}=0.41942\cdots.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we assume that all manifolds
M
are closed. A closed orientable manifold is called
achiral
, if it admits an orientation reversing homeomorphism, and is called virtually achiral if it has an achiral finite cover.
We call two manifolds
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
commensurable, if they have a common finite cover. A commensurable class of closed manifolds is called achiral if it contains an achiral element. A commensurable class
{\mathcal{M}}
is achiral is equivalent to that each manifold in
{\mathcal{M}}
is virtually achiral (Lemma
2.9
). Since each non-orientable manifold has an orientable double cover which is achiral, each commensurable class containing non-orientable elements must be achiral.

The achirality is fundamental in topology for the classification of oriented manifolds, specially in dimension 3, where the concept achirality is originated. The study of various virtual properties, say virtually Haken, virtually positive volume, virtual dominations, are important and active topics in 3-manifolds, see a survey
[
LS
]
.

It will be natural to wonder the following questions: In cartain range,

1. What is the density of achiral manifolds among all?

2. What is the density of manifolds have an achiral finite cover among all? or what is the density of achiral classes among all commensurable classes?

3. What is the density of classes containing non-orientable element among all achiral classes?

Question 1 is for manifolds and Question 2 and Question 3 are for commensurable classes. Note to study those questions, one need first to order the manifolds or classes in the range considered, which is subtle, and often
difficult.

In dimension 1 and 2, the answers to those questions are direct: There is only one closed 1-manifold, the circle, which is achiral.
All orientable 2-manifolds are achiral.
Denote
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
,
{\mathbb{E}}^{n}
,
S^{n}
the
n
-dimensional hyperbolic, Euclidean, and spherical geometries, respectively. In dimension two case, every closed surface supports one of the three geometries:

S^{2},{\mathbb{E}}^{2},{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

Each geometry has only one commensurable class which also contains non-orientable elements.

Once we come to dimension 3, those questions become highly non-trivial.
Recall that Thurston’s eight geometries among 3-manifolds are
[
Th
]

[
Sc
]
,

S^{3},\quad{\mathbb{E}}^{3},\quad S^{2}\times{\mathbb{E}}^{1},\quad{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{E}}^{1},\quad\mathrm{Nil},\quad\widetilde{{\mathrm{PSL}}(2,\mathbb{R})},\quad{\mathbb{H}}^{3},\quad\mathrm{Sol}.

It is neither meaningful nor possible for us to order all those manifolds from those eight geometries, and to get answers to those questions.
Let’s see what happen when we restrict to each individual geometry.

We first have a look of Question 1. Four cases are essentially known: Each manifolds supports either
\mathrm{Nil}-
or

\widetilde{{\mathrm{PSL}}(2,\mathbb{R})}-
geometry is non-achiral, so the density is 0.
There are only two orientable manifolds supporting
S^{2}\times E^{1}
-geometry, both are achiral, so the density is 1.
There are only seven orientable manifolds supporting the
E^{3}
-geometry, four of them are achiral and three are not (
[
SWW
, Theorem 1.7]
and Lemma
2.7
), so the density is
4/7
.
It is not clear for us how to order the manifolds for the remaining geometries to get the density.

Now we discuss Questions 2 and 3, which is the issue of the paper.
The situation is known for the first six classes: 3-manifolds supporting one of the first six geometries form only one commensurable class (
[
Sc
]
), and
commensurable classes are achiral for the manifolds supporting first four geometries, and non-achiral for manifolds supporting
next two geometries.
Moreover there is no non-orientable 3-manifolds supporting
S^{3}
-geometric, and there are non-orientable 3-manifolds supporting the next three geometries. On the other hand 3-manifolds supporting one of the last two geometries, Sol or
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, form infinitely many commensurable classes, and
the achirality of those commensurable classes are not addressed.

We study the achirality of commensurable classes of Sol 3-manifolds in this paper.

Each commensurable class
{\mathcal{M}}
of Sol 3-manifold contains an orientable torus bundle
M=M_{\phi}
, eigenvalues of whose monodromy map
\phi
generates over
{\mathbb{Q}}
a real quadratic field. The discriminant of the real quadratic field is the complete topological invariant of
{\mathcal{M}}
, denoted by
D_{{\mathcal{M}}}
and called the discriminant of
{\mathcal{M}}

Our main result is:

Theorem 1.1
.

(1) Among all commensurable classes of Sol 3-manifolds,
there are infinitely many achiral classes; however ordered by discriminants, the density of achiral commensurable classes:

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{\text{achiral}\ {\mathcal{M}}\ \ |D_{{\mathcal{M}}}<X\}}{\#\{{\mathcal{M}}\ |\ D_{{\mathcal{M}}}<X\ {}\}}=0,

(2) Among all achiral commensurable classes of Sol 3-manifolds ordered by discriminants, the density of classes containing non-orientable elements:

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{{\mathcal{M}}\ {}\text{contains an non-orientable element \,}|\,D_{\mathcal{M}}<X\ \}}{\#\{\text{achiral}\ {\mathcal{M}}\ \ |D_{{\mathcal{M}}}<X\}}=1-\rho,

where

\rho:=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(1+2^{-j}\right)^{-1}=0.41942\cdots.

The project is started by wondering if each Sol 3-manifold has an achiral finite cover.
Theorem
1.1
(1) is unexpected to us which claims that to have achiral finite covers are rare among Sol 3-manifolds.
Theorem
1.1
(2) is a big surprise that the Stevenhagen’s conjecture, which was solved by P. Koymans and C. Pagano
[
KP
]
in 2022, has its avatar in topology.

For each orientable Sol torus bundle
M=M_{\phi}
, we introduce the discriminant

of

\phi

Another result, Theorem
3.7
, in this paper is that for any given
D\equiv 0,1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
non-square positive integer, the following conditions are equivalent

•

there exists an achiral Sol torus bundle

with discriminant

•

no prime factor of

is

and

Consequences of Theorem

is achiral if and only if

contains no prime

(Theorem

); each achiral commensurable class contains non-achiral manifolds (Corollary

);
and

contains an non-orientable element if and only if it contains an achiral torus semi-bundle (Corollary

).

In his study on cusp cross-sections of Hilbert modular varieties, Hirzebruch made a conjecture
relates the signature defects of an orientable Sol torus bundle to the special value of its corresponding Shimizu’s L-function.
In this paper, we also show that, Theorem

(1), for an oriented Sol torus bundle

,

If monodormies of two oriented Sol torus bundles are negative to each other, they are not homeomorphic but their Shimuz L-series are the same. We have that, Theorem
5.1
(2), non-achiral oriented Sol tours bundles are determined by their Shimizu L-functions up to such a relation.

Theorem

follows from the positive answer of Stevenhagen Conjecture, Theorem

, and Corollary

which claims that a commensurable class contains a non-orientable element if and only if the corresponding negative Pell equation

in class group

Any Sol 3-manifold is homeomorphic to one of the following

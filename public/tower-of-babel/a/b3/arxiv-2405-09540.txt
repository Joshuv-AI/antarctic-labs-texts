Definition 2.4

4.2.1

In particular, when

Definition 4.7
.

To shorten some statements we also write

\displaystyle W^{2,p}_{v}(\alpha,\alpha,m)=W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,\alpha,m)\qquad\text{when\hskip 10.22217pt$c=0$\hskip 10.22217ptor\hskip 10.22217pt$d=0$}.

Under this identification, we also define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{v}

\displaystyle:=\left\{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\infty)\right),\ \left(v\cdot\nabla u\right)(x,y)=0\ {\rm for}\ y\leq\delta\ {\rm and\ some\ }\delta>0\right\}

(25)

with the convention that
\mathcal{C}_{v}:=\mathcal{C}
when

and
\mathcal{C}

In what follows we clarify the relation between the spaces
W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,\alpha,m)
and
W^{2,p}_{v}(\alpha,\alpha,m)
. The next Proposition shows that

is related to

by means of the isometry

of

defined in (

and

, namely

\displaystyle:=u\left(x-\frac{d}{c}y,y\right),\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}.

Proposition 4.8
.

One has

\displaystyle S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}\,\left(W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,\alpha,m)\right)=W^{2,p}_{v}(\alpha,\alpha,m)

(26)

In particular the set
\mathcal{C}_{v}
defined in (
25
) is dense in
W^{2,p}_{v}(\alpha,\alpha,m)
.

Proof.
Let
u\in W^{2,1}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)
and let us set
\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}u
. Then by Proposition
3.4
one has

1.

y^{\alpha}D_{x_{i}x_{j}}\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}\left(y^{\alpha}D_{x_{i}x_{j}}u\right)
,
y^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}D_{x_{i}}\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}\left(y^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}D_{x_{i}}u\right)
,

y^{\alpha-1}D_{x_{i}}\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}\left(y^{\alpha-1}D_{x_{i}}u\right)
;

2.

y^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}D_{y}\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}\Big(-\frac{1}{c}y^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\nabla_{x}u,d)+y^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}D_{y}u\Big)
,

y^{\alpha-1}D_{y}\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}\Big(-\frac{1}{c}y^{\alpha-1}(\nabla_{x}u,d)+y^{\alpha-1}D_{y}u\Big)
;

3.

y^{\alpha_{2}}D_{yy}\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}\Big(\frac{1}{c^{2}}y^{\alpha}(D^{2}_{x}u\cdot d,d)-\frac{1}{c}2y^{\alpha}(\nabla_{x}D_{y}u,d)+y^{\alpha}D_{yy}u\Big)

4.

.

In particular

y^{\alpha-1}d\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{u}+cy^{\alpha-1}D_{y}\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}\Big(cy^{\alpha-1}D_{y}u\Big).

The above relations shows that

if and only if

. This proves the required claim. The last claim follows by the density of
\mathcal{C}
in
W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,\alpha,m)
since
\mathcal{C}_{v}=S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
.

As in Propositions
4.5
and
4.6
we can provide an equivalent description of

, adapted to the degenerate operator

. In the first formulation we shows that the oblique boundary condition in the integral form

is actually equivalent to the trace condition
\displaystyle\lim_{y\to 0}y^{c}v\cdot\nabla u=0
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\frac{m+1}{p}<c+1-\alpha
. Then one has

\displaystyle W^{2,p}_{v}(\alpha,\alpha,m)=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):u,\ y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}u\in L^{p}_{m}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 43.05542pty^{\alpha}D_{yy}u+y^{\alpha-1}v\cdot\nabla u\in L^{p}_{m}\text{\;\;and\;\;}\lim_{y\to 0}y^{c}v\cdot\nabla u=0\right\}

and the norms
\|u\|_{W^{2,p}_{v}(\alpha,\alpha,m)}
and

.
Finally, when
0<\frac{m+1}{p}\leq c-1
then

\displaystyle W^{2,p}_{v}(\alpha,\alpha,m)=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):u,\;\;y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}u,\;\;y^{\alpha}D_{yy}u+y^{\alpha-1}v\cdot\nabla u\in L^{p}_{m}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{u}\in W^{2,p}_{v}(\alpha,\alpha,m)
. By Proposition
4.8
,
\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}u
where

. We observe that by Proposition
4.5
,
u
is characterized by

\displaystyle u,\;\;y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}u,\quad y^{\alpha}D_{yy}u+cy^{\alpha-1}D_{y}u\in L^{p}_{m}\quad\text{and\quad}\lim_{y\to 0}y^{c}D_{y}u=0.

(27)

We remark preliminarily that the Calderon-Zygmund inequality (see e.g.
[
17
, Theorem 2, Chapter 4, Section 1]
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D_{x_{i}x_{j}}u(x,y)|^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta_{x}u(x,y)|^{p}\,dx.

Multiplying by

and integrating over

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|y^{\alpha}D_{x_{i}x_{j}}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\leq C\|y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}

and the same relation obviously holds for

. Moreover by Proposition

again we also obtain

\displaystyle\|y^{\alpha}\nabla_{x}D_{y}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\leq C\Big[\|u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+\|y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+\|y^{\alpha}D_{yy}u+cy^{\alpha-1}D_{y}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\Big].

(29)

By Proposition
3.4
and Corollary
3.6
with
\beta=0
and
\omega=-\frac{d}{c}
we have

1.

y^{\alpha}D_{x_{i}x_{j}}\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}\left(y^{\alpha}D_{x_{i}x_{j}}u\right)
;

2.

y^{\alpha-1}v\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}\Big(cy^{\alpha-1}D_{y}u\Big)
;

3.

y^{\alpha}D_{yy}\tilde{u}+y^{\alpha-1}v\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}={S_{0,-\frac{d}{c}}}\Big(\frac{1}{c^{2}}y^{\alpha}\left(D^{2}_{x}u\cdot d,d\right)-\frac{2}{c}y^{\alpha}\left(d,\nabla_{x}D_{y}u\right)+y^{\alpha}D_{yy}u+cy^{\alpha-1}D_{y}u\Big)
.

The above relations and (
28
), (
29
), then shows that the requirements in (

\displaystyle\tilde{u},\quad y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}\tilde{u},\;\;y^{\alpha}D_{yy}\tilde{u}+y^{\alpha-1}v\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}\in L^{p}_{m}\text{\quad and\quad}\lim_{y\to 0}y^{c}v\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}=0

which proves the first required claim. The claim for

4.2.2
The general case: the space

We now extend the previous definition also to the case
\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha_{2}
. In contrast to the case
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle\beta_{\alpha}\overset{def}{=}\frac{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}{2}

y^{\alpha_{1}}
,
y^{\alpha_{2}}
which appear in the
x
and
y

\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}
, where
\beta_{\alpha}=0
, but also when

(see Remark
4.11
). This correction is essential for the validity of equalities (
33
) and (
37
) and for characterizing, in Section
7
, the domain of the degenerate operator
\mathcal{L}
defined in (

We start by defining

\displaystyle F^{2,p}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},m)=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):\ u,\ y^{\alpha_{1}}D_{x_{i}x_{j}}u,\ y^{\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}}D_{x_{i}}u,\,y^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}D_{y}\nabla_{x}u,\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 103.33301pty^{\alpha_{2}}\left(D_{yy}u+\beta_{\alpha}\frac{D_{y}u}{y}\right),\ y^{\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}}D_{y}u\in L^{p}_{m}\right\}

with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{F^{2,p}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},m)}{=}\|u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}

4.15
) in the integral form

\displaystyle y^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}-1}d\cdot\nabla_{x}u+cy^{\alpha_{2}-1}D_{y}u=y^{\alpha_{2}-1}\Big(y^{\beta_{\alpha}}\,d\cdot\nabla_{x}u+cy^{\alpha_{2}-1}D_{y}u\Big)\in L^{p}_{m}.

\displaystyle W^{2,p}_{v}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}=\{u\in F^{2,p}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},m):\ y^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}-1}d\cdot\nabla_{x}u+cy^{\alpha_{2}-1}D_{y}u\in L^{p}_{m}\}

Remark 4.11
.

(i)

The difference between the spaces
W^{2,p}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},m)
and

(ii)

Corollary 6.4

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

\alpha<2

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:

\displaystyle=\gamma y^{\alpha}D_{yy}+y^{\alpha-1}v_{q}\cdot\nabla-by^{\alpha-2},\qquad v_{q}:=\left(d-2s_{1}q,c\right).

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{L})=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):u,\ y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}u,\ \mathcal{A}u\in L^{p}_{m}\text{\;\;and\;\;}\lim_{y\to 0}y^{s_{2}}u=0\right\}

(ii)

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{L})=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):u,\ y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}u,\ \mathcal{A}u\in L^{p}_{m}\text{\;\;and\;\;}\lim_{y\to 0}y^{s_{2}}u\in\mathbb{C}\right\}

Proof.
Let us prove claim (i). By Theorem

where

We apply Proposition
4.10

\displaystyle W^{2,p}_{w}(\alpha,\alpha,m-s_{1}p)=

\displaystyle\left\{v\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):v,\ y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}v,\ \tilde{\mathcal{A}}v\in L^{p}_{m-s_{1}p}\text{\;\;and\;\;}\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\frac{\tilde{c}}{\gamma}-1}v=0\right\}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}:

\displaystyle=y^{\alpha}D_{yy}+y^{\alpha-1}\frac{\omega}{\gamma}\cdot\nabla.

\displaystyle T_{-s_{1},0}^{-1}\left(y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}\right)T_{-s_{1},0}=y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x},\qquad T_{-s_{1},0}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)T_{-s_{1},0}=\gamma\tilde{\mathcal{A}}.

\displaystyle y^{\frac{\tilde{c}}{\gamma}-1}v=y^{\frac{\tilde{c}}{\gamma}-1+s_{1}}u=y^{2\sqrt{D}+s_{1}}u=y^{s_{2}}u.

In the following Corollary, in continuity with Remark
6.3

Corollary 6.5

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=y^{\alpha}\mbox{Tr }\left(AD^{2}\right)+y^{\alpha-1}\left(v,\nabla\right).

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
\alpha_{2}<2
,
\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}<2
such that

s_{1}+\alpha_{1}^{-}<\frac{m+1}{p}<s_{2}+2-\alpha_{2}.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:

\displaystyle=\gamma y^{\alpha_{2}}D_{yy}+y^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}-1}(d-2s_{1}q)\cdot\nabla_{x}+cy^{\alpha_{2}-1}D_{y}-by^{\alpha_{2}-2}.

(i)

If

then

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{L})=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):u,\ y^{\alpha_{1}}\Delta_{x}u,\ \mathcal{A}u\in L^{p}_{m}\text{\;\;and\;\;}\lim_{y\to 0}y^{s_{2}}u=0\right\}

(ii)

If
D=0
then
s_{1}=s_{2}
and

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{L})=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):u,\ y^{\alpha_{1}}\Delta_{x}u,\ \mathcal{A}u\in L^{p}_{m}\text{\;\;and\;\;}\lim_{y\to 0}y^{s_{2}}u\in\mathbb{C}\right\}

\|u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+\|y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}

Proof.
Let us prove claim (i). By Theorem
7.3
we have

D(\mathcal{L})=T_{-s_{1},0}\left(W^{2,p}_{w}\left(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},m-s_{1}p\right)\right)

where

w=(d,c+\beta_{\alpha}\gamma)-2s_{1}\left(q,\gamma\right):=\left(w_{x},w_{N+1}\right).

Then we proceed as in the proof of Corollary
6.4
: we apply Proposition Proposition
4.16
with
\mu=\frac{c}{\gamma}-2s_{1}=1+2\sqrt{D}>1
obtaining

\displaystyle W^{2,p}_{w}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},m-s_{1}p)=

\displaystyle\left\{v\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):v,\ y^{\alpha_{1}}\Delta_{x}v,\ \tilde{\mathcal{A}}v\in L^{p}_{m-s_{1}p}\text{\;\;and\;\;}\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\mu-1}v=0\right\}

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}:

\displaystyle=y^{\alpha_{2}}D_{yy}+y^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}-1}\tilde{d}\cdot\nabla_{x}+\mu y^{\alpha_{2}-1}D_{y},\qquad\tilde{d}=\frac{\mu+\beta_{\alpha}}{w_{N+1}}w_{x}=\frac{d-2s_{1}q}{\gamma}.

The required claim then follows from the previous equalities after noticing that, by Proposition
3.2
and Corollary
3.3

\displaystyle T_{-s_{1},0}^{-1}\left(y^{\alpha_{1}}\Delta_{x}\right)T_{-s_{1},0}=y^{\alpha_{1}}\Delta_{x},\qquad T_{-s_{1},0}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)T_{-s_{1},0}=\gamma\tilde{\mathcal{A}}.

and that for any

\displaystyle y^{\mu-1}v=y^{\mu-1+s_{1}}u=y^{s_{2}}u.

Claim (ii) follows similarly.

Corollary 7.5

Let

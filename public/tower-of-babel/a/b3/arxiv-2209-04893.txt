Universal potential estimates for 1 < p ≤ 2 − 1 n 1<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n}
Quoc-Hung Nguyen and Nguyen Cong Phuc

Abstract
We extend the so-called universal potential estimates of Kuusi-Mingione type (J. Funct. Anal. 262: 4205–4269, 2012) to the singular case 1 < p ≤ 2 − 1 / n 1<p\leq 2-1/n for the quasilinear equation with measure data − div ⁡ ( A ⁡ ( x , ∇ u ) ) = μ -\operatorname{div}(A(x,\nabla u))=\mu in a bounded open subset Ω \Omega of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , with a finite signed measure μ \mu in Ω \Omega . The operator div ⁡ ( A ⁡ ( x , ∇ u ) ) \operatorname{div}(A(x,\nabla u)) is modeled after the p p -Laplacian Δ p u := div ( | ∇ u | p − 2 ∇ u ) \Delta_{p}u:={\rm div}\,(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u) , where the nonlinearity A ⁡ ( x , ξ ) A(x,\xi) ( x , ξ ∈ ℝ n x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n} ) is assumed to satisfy natural growth and monotonicity conditions of order p p , as well as certain additional regularity conditions in the x x -variable. MSC2020: primary: 35J92; secondary: 35J75, 31B10. Keywords: pointwise estimate, potential estimate, Wolff’s potential, Riesz’s potential, fractional maximal function, Calderón space, p p -Laplacian, quasilinear equation, measure data.



1
Introduction and main results

We are concerned here with the quasilinear elliptic equation with measure data

-\operatorname{div}(A(x,\nabla u))=\mu,

(1.1)

in a bounded open subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Here
\mu
is a finite signed measure in
\Omega
and
the nonlinearity
A=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is vector valued function.
Throughout the paper, we assume that there exist
\Lambda\geq 1
and
p>1
such that

|A(x,\xi)|\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{p-1},\quad|D_{\xi}A(x,\xi)|\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{p-2},

(1.2)

\langle D_{\xi}A(x,\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle\geq\Lambda^{-1}|\xi|^{p-2}|\eta|^{2}

(1.3)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
. More regularity assumptions on function
x\mapsto A(x,\xi)

will be needed later.

A typical example of (
1.1
) is the
p
-Laplace equation with measure data

-\Delta_{p}\,u:=-{\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=\mu\quad\text{in~}\Omega.

(1.4)

Since the seminal work of Kilpeläinen and Malý
[
7
]
(see also
[
16
]
for a different approach), the study of pointwise behaviors of solutions to quasilinear equations with measure data (
1.1
) has undergone substantial progress. In particular, the series of works
[
4
,
5
,
9
]
(see also
[
12
]
) provide interesting pointwise bounds for gradients of solutions to the seemingly unwieldy equation (
1.1
), at least for
p>2-\frac{1}{n}
. These pointwise gradient bounds have been extended recently in
[
14
,
3
,
15
]
for the more singular case
1<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n}
.

On the other hand, a more unified approach to pointwise bounds for solutions and their gradients was presented in
[
8
]
. The results of
[
8
]
give pointwise bounds
not only for the size but also for the oscillation of solutions and their derivatives expressed in terms of bounds by linear or nonlinear potentials in certain Calderón spaces. These cover different kinds of pointwise fractional derivative estimates as well as estimates for (sharp) fractional maximal functions of the solutions and their gradients.

However, the treatment of
[
8
]
is still confined to the range
p>2-\frac{1}{n}
, and the purpose of this note is to extend it to the singular case
1<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n}
. Note that, for
1<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n}
, by looking at the fundamental solution we see that in general distributional solutions of (
1.4
) may not even belong to
W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

Thus in this paper we shall restrict ourselves only to the case

1<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n},

and note that the main results obtained here also hold in the case
2-\frac{1}{n}<p<2
thanks to
[
8
]
. Moreover, except for the comparison estimates obtained earlier in
[
13
,
15
]
, the methods used in this paper are very much guided by those of
[
8
]
. We would also like to point out that there are analogous results in the case
p\geq 2
that we refer to
[
8
]
for the precise statements.

In some sense our pointwise regularity for the non-homogeneous equation (
1.1
) is obtained from perturbation/interpolation arguments involving the associated homogeneous equations. Thus information on the regularity of associated homogeneous equations will play an important role. In this direction, we first recall a quantitative version
of the well-known De Giorgi’s result that established
C^{\alpha_{0}}
,
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
, regularity for solutions of
{\rm div}\left({A(x,\nabla w)}\right)=0
.
Henceforth, by
Q_{r}(x_{0})
we mean the open cube
Q_{r}(x_{0}):=x_{0}+(-r,r)^{n}
with center
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and side-length
2r
. In other words,

Q_{r}(x_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-x_{0}|_{\infty}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|x_{i}-x_{0i}|<r\}.

Lemma 1.1

Under (
1.2
)–(
1.3
), let
w\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
p>1
, be a solution of the equation
{\rm div}\left({A(x,\nabla w)}\right)=0
in
\Omega
. Then there exists

\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending only on

and
\Lambda
, such that for any cubes
Q_{\rho}(x_{0})\subset Q_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, and

\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}|w-(w)_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}|^{p}dx\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha_{0}p}\fint_{Q_{R}(x_{0})}|w-(w)_{Q_{R}(x_{0})}|^{p}dx,

(1.5)

and

\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}|w-q|^{\epsilon}dx\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha_{0}\epsilon}\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\fint_{Q_{R}(x_{0})}|w-q|^{\epsilon}dx.

(1.6)

We point out that the proof of (
1.5
) follows from
[
6

1.6

1.5

In the case the nonlinearity
A(x,\xi)
is independent of

C^{1,\beta_{0}}
,
\beta_{0}\in(0,1)
, regularity the the homogeneous equation (see, e.g.,
[

,

Lemma 1.2

Let

,

, be a solution of
\operatorname{div}\left({A_{0}(\nabla v)}\right)=0
in
\Omega
, where

satisfies (

\beta_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending only on

and
\Lambda
, such that for any cubes
Q_{\rho}(x_{0})\subset Q_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and
\epsilon\in(0,1]

\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}|\nabla w-(\nabla w)_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}|dx\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\beta_{0}}\fint_{Q_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla w-(\nabla w)_{Q_{R}(x_{0})}|dx,

\inf_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}|\nabla v-\mathbf{q}|^{\epsilon}dx\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\beta_{0}\epsilon}\inf_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\fint_{Q_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla v-\mathbf{q}|^{\epsilon}dx.

(1.7)

In what follows, we shall use the (maximal) constants
\alpha_{0}
in Lemma
1.1
and
\beta_{0}
in Lemma

Also, henceforth, we reserve the letter
\kappa
for the following constant

(1.8)

Our first result provides a De Giorgi’s theory for non-homogeneous equations with measure data, which also includes
[
15

as an end-point case.
For the case
p>2-1/n
, see
[

Theorem 1.3

Under (
1.2
)–(
1.3
), with
1<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n}
, let
\kappa
be as in (
1.8
), and suppose that
u\in C^{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is a solution of (
1.1
). Let
Q_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and
\bar{\alpha}\in(0,\alpha_{0})
, where
\alpha_{0}
is as in Lemma
1.1
. Then for any
x,y\in Q_{R/8}(x_{0})
we have

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[{\bf W}_{1-\alpha(p-1)/p,p}^{R}(|\mu|)(x)+{\bf W}_{1-\alpha(p-1)/p,p}^{R}(|\mu|)(y)\right]|x-y|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\fint_{Q_{R}(x_{0})}|u|^{\kappa}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{R}\right)^{\alpha}

(1.9)

uniformly in
\alpha\in[0,\bar{\alpha}]
. Here the implicit constant depends only on
n,p,\Lambda
, and
\bar{\alpha}
.

In (
1.3
), the function
{\bf W}_{1-\alpha(p-1)/p,p}^{R}(|\mu|)(\cdot)
is a truncated Wolff’s potential of
|\mu|
. In general, given a nonnegative measure
\nu
and

\rho>0
, the Wolff’s potential
{\bf W}^{\rho}_{\alpha,s}\nu
,
\alpha>0,s>1
, is defined by

{\bf W}^{\rho}_{\alpha,s}(\nu)(x):=\int_{0}^{\rho}\left[\frac{\nu(Q_{t}(x))}{t^{n-\alpha s}}\right]^{\frac{1}{s-1}}\frac{dt}{t},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Note that
{\bf W}^{\rho}_{\alpha,2}(\nu)={\bf I}^{\rho}_{2\alpha}(\nu)
, where
{\bf I}^{\rho}_{\gamma}(\nu)
,
\gamma>0
, is a truncated Riesz’s potential defined by

{\bf I}^{\rho}_{\gamma}(\nu)(x):=\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\nu(Q_{t}(x))}{t^{n-\gamma}}\frac{dt}{t},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We remark that, except for (
1.2
)–(
1.3
), no further regularity assumption is needed in Theorem
1.3
. However, this will force the constant
\bar{\alpha}
to be small in general.

On the other hand, it is possible to allow
\bar{\alpha}
to be arbitrarily close to
1
as long as we further impose a ‘small BMO’ condition on the map

x\mapsto A(x,\xi)
. This condition entails the smallness of the limit
\limsup_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\omega(\rho)
, where

\omega(\rho):=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\fint_{Q_{r}(y)}\Upsilon(A,Q_{r}(y))(x)^{2}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\rho>0,

(1.10)

and for each cube
Q_{r}(y)
we set

\Upsilon(A,Q_{r}(y))(x):=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|A({x,\xi})-\overline{A}_{Q_{r}(y)}({\xi})|}{|\xi|^{p-1}},

with
\overline{A}_{Q_{r}(y)}(\xi)=\fint_{Q_{r}(y)}A(x,\xi)dx
.
The precise statement is as follows.

2
Comparison and Poincaré type inequalities

The study of regularity problems for equation (
1.1
) is based on the following comparison
estimate that connects the solution
of measure datum problem to a solution of a homogeneous problem.

To describe it, we let
u\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be a solution of (
1.1
).
Then for a cube
Q_{2R}=Q_{2R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
, we consider the unique solution
w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(Q_{2R}(x_{0}))+u
to the local interior problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\operatorname{div}\left({A(x,\nabla w)}\right)&=&0\quad\text{in}\quad Q_{2R}(x_{0}),\\
w&=&u\quad\text{on}~~\partial Q_{2R}(x_{0}).\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

Lemma 2.1

Suppose that
Q_{3R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
for some
R>0
. Let
u
and
w
be as in (
2.1
) and let
\kappa
be as in (
1.8
), where
1<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n}
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{2R}(x_{0})}|\nabla(u-w)|^{\kappa}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3R}(x_{0}))}{R^{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3R}(x_{0}))}{R^{n-1}}\left(\fint_{Q_{3R}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dx\right)^{\frac{2-p}{\kappa}}.

(2.2)

Proof.
For
1<p\leq\frac{3n-2}{2n-1}
, inequality (
2.1
) was obtained in
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
. For
\frac{3n-2}{2n-1}<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n}
,
by
[
13
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{2R}(x_{0})}|\nabla(u-w)|^{\gamma_{0}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{2R}(x_{0}))}{R^{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{|\mu|(Q_{2R}(x_{0}))}{R^{n-1}}\left(\fint_{Q_{2R}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{\gamma_{0}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-p}{\gamma_{0}}}

for some
\gamma_{0}\in[\frac{2-p}{2},\frac{n(p-1)}{n-1})
. In fact, an inspection of the proof of
[
13
, Lemma 2.2]
reveals that we can take
any

\gamma_{0}\in(\frac{n}{2n-1},\frac{n(p-1)}{n-1})
. Thus we may assume that

\kappa=(p-1)^{2}/2<\gamma_{0}
. To conclude the proof, it is therefore enough to show that

\left(\fint_{Q_{2R}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{\gamma_{0}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}\lesssim\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3R}(x_{0}))}{R^{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\left(\fint_{Q_{3R}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}.

(2.3)

To this end, let
\gamma_{1}\in(\gamma_{0},\frac{n(p-1)}{n-1})
. By
[
15
, Corollay 2.4]
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\gamma_{1}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{9\rho/8}(x))}{\rho^{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\fint_{Q_{9\rho/8}(x)}|u-\lambda|^{\gamma_{0}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}

(2.4)

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and any cube
Q_{\rho}(x)
such that
Q_{9\rho/8}(x)\subset\Omega
.

Now, with
Q_{8\rho/7}(x)\subset\Omega
, let
w_{1}
be the unique solution
w_{1}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(Q_{8\rho/7}(x))+u
to the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\operatorname{div}\left({A(x,\nabla w_{1})}\right)&=&0\quad\text{in}\quad Q_{8\rho/7}(x),\\
w_{1}&=&u\quad\text{on}\quad\partial Q_{8\rho/7}(x).\end{array}\right.

Then from the proof of
[
13
, Lemma 2.2]
(using (2.8) and (2.18) in
[

]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\fint_{Q_{8\rho/7}(x)}|u-w_{1}|^{\gamma_{0}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{8\rho/7}(x))}{\rho^{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{|\mu|(Q_{8\rho/7}(x))}{\rho^{n-1}}\left(\fint_{Q_{8\rho/7}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\gamma_{0}}dy\right)^{\frac{2-p}{\gamma_{0}}}.

(2.5)

By Young’s inequality, this yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\fint_{Q_{8\rho/7}(x)}|u-w_{1}|^{\gamma_{0}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{8\rho/7}(x))}{\rho^{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\left(\fint_{Q_{8\rho/7}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\gamma_{0}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\fint_{Q_{9\rho/8}(x)}|u-\lambda|^{\gamma_{0}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{8\rho/7}(x))}{\rho^{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\left(\fint_{Q_{8\rho/7}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\gamma_{0}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}

such that

\gamma_{1}>\gamma_{0}

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\gamma_{1}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}}\quad\lesssim\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{8\rho/7}(x))}{\rho^{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\left(\fint_{Q_{8\rho/7}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\epsilon}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}

(2.8)

3
Pointwise fractional maximal function bounds

As in
[
8
]
, our proofs of Theorems
1.3
–
1.6
are based on the corresponding pointwise estimates for the associate fractional and sharp fractional maximal functions, which are interesting in their own right. This section is devoted to such pointwise fractional maximal function bounds.

Given
R>0
and
q>0
, following
[
1
]
, we define the following truncated sharp fractional maximal function of a function
f\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

{\bf M}^{\#,R}_{\alpha,q}(f)(x):=\sup_{0<\rho\leq R}\inf_{m\in\mathbb{R}}\rho^{-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|f-m|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\qquad\alpha\geq 0.

Also, we define a truncated fractional maximal function by

{\bf M}^{R}_{\beta,q}(f)(x):=\sup_{0<\rho\leq R}\rho^{\beta}\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|f|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\qquad\beta\in[0,n/q].

In the case
q=1
, we usually drop the index
q
in the above notation, i.e., we set
{\bf M}^{\#,R}_{\alpha,1}(f)={\bf M}^{\#,R}_{\alpha}(f)

and
{\bf M}^{R}_{\beta,1}(f)={\bf M}^{R}_{\beta}(f)
. Moreover, the definition of
{\bf M}^{R}_{\beta}(f)
can also be naturally extended to the case where
f=\mu
is a locally finite signed measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

{\bf M}^{R}_{\beta}(\mu)(x):=\sup_{0<\rho\leq R}\rho^{\beta}\frac{|\mu|(Q_{\rho}(x))}{|Q_{\rho}(x)|},\qquad\beta\in[0,n/q].

Note that by Poincaré inequality we have

{\bf M}^{\#,R}_{\beta}(f)(x)\lesssim{\bf M}^{R}_{1-\beta}(\nabla f)(x),\qquad\beta\in[0,1],

for any
f\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

On the other hand, if
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
,
1<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n}
, then it follows from Corollary
2.3
that

{\bf M}^{\#,R}_{\beta,\epsilon}(u)(x)\lesssim\left[{\bf M}^{3R/2}_{p-\beta(p-1)}(\mu)(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+{\bf M}^{3R/2}_{1-\beta,\epsilon}(\nabla u)(x),\beta\in[0,1],

(3.1)

for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and any cube
Q_{3R/2}(x)\subset\Omega
.

The following fractional maximal function bound will be needed in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 3.1

Under (
1.2
)–(
1.3
), let
1<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n}
, and suppose that
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is a solution of (
1.1
). Let
Q_{3R}(x)\subset\Omega
and
\bar{\alpha}\in(0,\alpha_{0})
, where
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
is as in Lemma
1.1
. Then we have

\displaystyle{\bf M}^{\#,2R}_{\alpha,\kappa}(u)(x)+{\bf M}^{3R}_{1-\alpha,\kappa}(\nabla u)(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left[{\bf M}^{3R}_{p-\alpha(p-1)}(\mu)(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+R^{1-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{3R}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}

(3.2)

uniformly in
\alpha\in[0,\bar{\alpha}]
. Here the implicit constant depends on
n,p,\Lambda
, and
\bar{\alpha}
.

Proof.
The main idea of the proof of (
3.1
) lies the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
that treated the case
p>2-\frac{1}{n}
. Note that by (
3.1
)
it is enough to show

\displaystyle{\bf M}^{\epsilon R}_{1-\alpha,\kappa}(\nabla u)(x)\lesssim\left[{\bf M}^{3R}_{p-\alpha(p-1)}(\mu)(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+R^{1-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{3R}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}},

(3.3)

for some
\epsilon=\epsilon_{1}(n,p,\Lambda,\bar{\alpha})\in(0,1)
.

Let
0<\rho\leq r\leq R
, and choose
w
as in (
2.1
) with
Q_{2r}(x)
in place of
Q_{2R}(x_{0})
.
We have

\displaystyle\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\lesssim\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{(\alpha_{0}-1)\kappa}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{(\alpha_{0}-1)\kappa}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left\{\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{(\alpha_{0}-1)\kappa}+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n}\right\}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{(\alpha_{0}-1)\kappa}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy,

where we used the inequality

\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{(\alpha_{0}-1)\kappa}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy,

which is a modified version of
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
, in the second inequality.

Thus by Lemma
2.1
we get

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\alpha_{0}-1}\left(\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3r}(x))}{r^{n-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3r}(x))}{r^{n-1}}\right)\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{(2-p)/\kappa}.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, and choose
\rho=\epsilon r
. Then by Young’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{\epsilon r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}\leq C(\epsilon)\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3r}(x))}{r^{n-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+[C\epsilon^{\alpha_{0}-1}+1]\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}.

Multiplying both sides by
(\epsilon r)^{1-\alpha}
,
0<\alpha\leq\bar{\alpha}<\alpha_{0}
, and taking the supremum with respect to
r\in(0,R]
, we find

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r\leq\epsilon R}r^{1-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}\leq C(\epsilon)\sup_{0<r\leq R}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3r}(x))}{r^{n-p+\alpha(p-1)}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+[C\epsilon^{\alpha_{0}-1}+1](\epsilon/3)^{1-\alpha}\sup_{0<r\leq R}(3r)^{1-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}.

We now choose
\epsilon\in(0,1)
such that

[C\epsilon^{\alpha_{0}-1}+1](\epsilon/3)^{1-\bar{\alpha}}\leq 1/2,

to deduce that

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r\leq\epsilon R}r^{1-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}\leq C(\epsilon)\sup_{0<r\leq R}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3r}(x))}{r^{n-p+\alpha(p-1)}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sup_{\epsilon R<r\leq 3R}r^{1-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left[{\bf M}^{3R}_{p-\alpha(p-1)}(\mu)(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+R^{1-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{3R}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}.

This is (
3.3
) and the proof is complete.

The following result will be needed for the proof of Theorem

.

Theorem 3.2

Let
1<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n}
and
u\in C^{0}(\Omega)
be a solution to (
1.1
). Suppose that
Q_{3R}(x)\subset\Omega
. Then for any positive
\bar{\alpha}<1

there exists a small
\delta=\delta(n,p,\Lambda,\bar{\alpha})>0

1.11
) holds,

\displaystyle{\bf M}^{\#,2R}_{\alpha,\kappa}(u)(x)+{\bf M}^{3R}_{1-\alpha,\kappa}(\nabla u)(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left[{\bf M}^{3R}_{p-\alpha(p-1)}(\mu)(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+R^{1-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{3R}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}

holds uniformly in
\alpha\in[0,\bar{\alpha}]
. Here the implicit constant depends on
n,p,\Lambda,\bar{\alpha},\omega(\cdot)
, and
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
.

Proof.
The proof is similar to that of Theorem
3.1
, but this time we need to use Lemma
2.4
.
As above, by (
3.1
)
it is enough to show (
3.3
) for some
\epsilon=\epsilon_{1}(n,p,\Lambda,\bar{\alpha})\in(0,1)
.
Let
0<\rho\leq r\leq R
, and choose
w
as in (
2.1
) with
Q_{2r}(x)
in place of
Q_{2R}(x_{0})
. Then choose
v
as in (
2.9
) with
Q_{r}(x)
in place of
Q_{R}(x_{0})
.
This time we have

\displaystyle\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla v|^{\kappa}dy+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n}\fint_{Q_{r}(x)}|\nabla v-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\fint_{Q_{r}(x)}|\nabla v|^{\kappa}dy+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n}\fint_{Q_{r}(x)}|\nabla v-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\fint_{Q_{r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n}\fint_{Q_{r}(x)}|\nabla v-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n}\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy.

Here we used

\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla v|^{\kappa}dy\lesssim\fint_{Q_{r}(x)}|\nabla v|^{\kappa}dy,

(3.4)

which is a a modified version of (2.6) in
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
in the second inequality.

Then by Lemma
2.4
we get

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{Q_{r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\omega(r)^{\sigma_{0}}\left(\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\left(\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{1+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\omega(r)^{\sigma_{0}}\right\}\left(\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\{\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\omega(r)^{\sigma_{0}}+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\right\}\left(\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u-\nabla w|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa},

for a small constant
\sigma_{0}>0
.
Thus using Lemma
2.1
and the fact that
\omega(r)\leq 2\Lambda
, we find

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{1+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\omega(r)^{\sigma_{0}}\right\}\left(\fint_{Q_{2r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3r}(x))}{r^{n-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3r}(x))}{r^{n-1}}\right)\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{(2-p)/\kappa}.

(3.5)

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, and choose
\rho=\epsilon r
. Then by Young’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{\epsilon r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}\leq C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3r}(x))}{r^{n-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left[c_{1}\epsilon^{-n/\kappa}\omega(r)^{\sigma_{0}}+c_{2}\right]\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}.

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r\leq\epsilon R}r^{1-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}\leq C_{\epsilon}\sup_{0<r\leq R}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3r}(x))}{r^{n-p+\alpha(p-1)}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left[c_{1}\epsilon^{-n/\kappa}\sup_{0<r\leq R}\omega(r)+c_{2}\right](\epsilon/3)^{1-\alpha}\sup_{0<r\leq R}(3r)^{1-\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}.

Arguing as in the proof of (

) (in Lemma

2.5
instead of Lemma
2.4
,
we have for

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u-{\bf q}_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\beta_{0}}\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}(x)}|\nabla u-{\bf q}_{Q_{3r}(x)}|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\omega(r)^{\sigma_{1}}\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}+\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{3r}(x))}{r^{n-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

(3.6)

Here

{\bf q}_{Q_{\rho}(x)}:=\argmin_{{\bf q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u-{\bf q}|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa},\quad Q_{\rho}(x)\Subset\Omega.

Note that for
Q_{\rho}(x)\subset Q_{s}(x)\Subset\Omega
, one has

\displaystyle|{\bf q}_{Q_{s}(x)}|

and also

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u-{\bf q}_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}+\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x)}|\nabla u-{\bf q}_{Q_{s}(x)}|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left(\frac{s}{\rho}\right)^{n/\kappa}\left(\fint_{Q_{s}(x)}|\nabla u-{\bf q}_{Q_{s}(x)}|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}.

(3.8)

and

, for all
x,y\in Q_{R/8}(x_{0})
.

On the other hand, for a.e.
x,y\in Q_{R/8}(x_{0})
and
f\in L^{\kappa}(Q_{R}(x_{0}))
, we have
the inequality

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\left(\frac{c}{\alpha}\right)|x-y|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\left[{\bf M}^{\#,R/2}_{\alpha,\kappa}(f)(x)+{\bf M}^{\#,R/2}_{\alpha,\kappa}(f)(y)\right],

provided
\alpha\in(0,1]
. See inequalities (4.9) and (4.10) in

[

]
.
Applying this with
f=u
and
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0}/2,\bar{\alpha}]
, and using Theorem
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\left(\frac{c}{\alpha_{0}}\right)|x-y|^{\alpha}\left[{\bf M}^{3R/4}_{p-\alpha(p-1)}(\mu)(x)+{\bf M}^{3R/4}_{p-\alpha(p-1)}(\mu)(y)\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{c}{\alpha_{0}}\right)|x-y|^{\alpha}R^{1-\alpha}\left\{\left(\fint_{Q_{3R/4}(x)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}+\left(\fint_{Q_{3R/4}(y)}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\right\}.

Then invoking the Caccioppoli type inequality of

we obtain (
1.4
) uniformly in
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0}/2,\bar{\alpha}]
as desired.

Let
Q_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
be as given in the theorem. For any

for all integers

.

Then by choosing

) such that

\displaystyle A_{j+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}A_{j}+C_{\epsilon}\omega(\delta_{j}/3)^{\sigma_{1}}\left(\fint_{Q_{j}}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}+C_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{j})}{\delta_{j}^{n-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{j})}{\delta_{j}^{n-1}}\right)\left(\fint_{Q_{j}}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{(2-p)/\kappa}.

Summing this up over
j\in\{0,1,,\dots,m-1\}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and then simplifying, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}

\displaystyle\leq A_{0}+C\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\omega(\delta_{j}/3)^{\sigma_{1}}\left(\fint_{Q_{j}}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}+C\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{j})}{\delta_{j}^{n-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{j})}{\delta_{j}^{n-1}}\right)\left(\fint_{Q_{j}}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{(2-p)/\kappa}.

On the other hand, by (
3
),

\displaystyle|{\bf q}_{m+1}-{\bf m}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(|{\bf q}_{j+1}-{\bf m}|-|{\bf q}_{j}-{\bf m}|)+|{\bf q}_{0}-{\bf m}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}(|{\bf q}_{j+1}-{\bf q}_{j}|+|{\bf q}_{0}-{\bf m}|\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{m}A_{j}+|{\bf q}_{0}-{\bf m}|,

which holds for any
{\bf m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and integer

.

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle|{\bf q}_{m+1}-{\bf m}|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\omega(\delta_{j}/3)^{\sigma_{1}}\left(\fint_{Q_{j}}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}+C\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{j})}{\delta_{j}^{n-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

which can be proved as in (

), we get

\displaystyle|{\bf q}_{m+1}-{\bf m}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\omega(\delta_{j}/3)^{\sigma_{1}}\left(\fint_{Q_{j}}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{j})}{\delta_{j}^{n-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{j})}{\delta_{j}^{n-1}}\right)\left(\fint_{Q_{j}}|\nabla u|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{(2-p)/\kappa}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\fint_{Q_{\delta}(x)}|\nabla u-{\bf q}_{Q_{\delta}(x)}|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}+\left(\fint_{Q_{\delta}(x)}|\nabla u-{\bf m}|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}.

We next set

3.3
with
\alpha=1
and

Plugging this into the last bound for

we deduce that

\displaystyle|{\bf q}_{m+1}-{\bf m}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\delta_{j}^{-\alpha}\omega(\delta_{j}/3)^{\sigma_{1}}M(x,\delta)+\delta^{\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left[\frac{|\mu|(Q_{j})}{\delta_{j}^{n-1+\alpha}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\delta^{\frac{\alpha(2-p)}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta^{\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac{|\mu|(Q_{j})}{\delta_{j}^{n-1+\alpha}}\right)M(x,\delta)^{2-p}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\fint_{Q_{\delta}(x)}|\nabla u-{\bf q}_{Q_{\delta}(x)}|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}+\left(\fint_{Q_{\delta}(x)}|\nabla u-{\bf m}|^{\kappa}dy\right)^{1/\kappa}.

Also, note that as in (

), we obtain, after some simple manipulations,

\displaystyle|{\bf q}_{m+1}-{\bf m}|

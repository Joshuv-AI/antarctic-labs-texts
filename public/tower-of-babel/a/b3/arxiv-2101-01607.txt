Abstract
A minimal model for gene expression, consisting of a switchable promoter together with the resulting messenger RNA, is equivalent to a Poisson channel with a binary Markovian input process. Determining its capacity is an optimization problem with respect to two parameters: the average sojourn times of the promoter’s active (ON) and inactive (OFF) state. An expression for the mutual information is found by solving the associated filtering problem analytically on the level of distributions. For fixed peak power, three bandwidth-like constraints are imposed by lower-bounding (i) the average sojourn times (ii) the autocorrelation time and (iii) the average time until a transition. OFF-favoring optima are found for all three constraints, as commonly encountered for the Poisson channel. In addition, constraint (i) exhibits a region that favors the ON state, and (iii) shows ON-favoring local optima.

I-A

Problem statement and outline

We consider the Poisson channel with BMPs as input class and investigate the following bandwidth-like constraints:

•

(C1)
0<c_{1}\leq r_{1},0<c_{2}\leq r_{2}
, i.e., lower-bounding the average sojourn times by
\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{1}]\geq r_{1}^{-1},\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{2}]\geq r_{2}^{-1}
. A special case is the homogeneous constraint
\max\{c_{1},c_{2}\}\leq r_{0}
, in analogy to
[
21
]
.

•

(C2)
0<c_{1}+c_{2}\leq r_{0}
. Since
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{ov}[X(t),X(t+s)]\propto\exp(-(c_{1}+c_{2})s)
, this bounds the autocorrelation time of
X(t)
from below.

•

(C3)
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{X(t)}|X(t)]]=c_{1}^{-1}+c_{2}^{-1}-2/(c_{1}+c_{2})\geq r_{0}^{-1}
, which lower-bounds the average sojourn time, similarly to (C1), but regardless of the transition type.

As the biophysical interpretation and value of the rates
c_{1},c_{2}
strongly depends on the context of the promoter model
[
15
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
we list some generic motivations. The timescales of the rates may be determined by binding affinity to the promoter, temperature, diffusion
[
25
,
26
]
, and availability of activating and deactivating constituents, such as transcription factors, polymerases, other enzymes, or ATP. Highly autocorrelated dynamics may be caused by upstream regulation or feedback of downstream elements in a reaction network
[
8
,
27
]
.

We consider the path-wise MI
\mathbb{I}(X_{[0,T]},Y_{[0,T]})

[
28
]
and the information rate, defined as

\bar{\mathbb{I}}(X,Y):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{I}(X_{[0,T]},Y_{[0,T]}).

Optimizing (i) the MI and (ii) the information rate with respect to all admissible input path distributions
\mu_{X}
yields (i) the capacity
C_{T}
and (ii) the information rate capacity
C
. For fixed OFF and ON states
x_{1},x_{2}
, the distribution
\mu_{X}
is parametrized solely by the system parameters
c_{1},c_{2}\geq 0
. Fixing
c_{3}
, the respective capacity-achieving path distributions are characterized by the following constrained optimization problems

\mathrm{(i)}\;\max_{c_{1},c_{2}}\,\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{I}(X_{[0,T]},Y_{[0,T]}),\quad\mathrm{(ii)}\;\max_{c_{1},c_{2}}\,\bar{\mathbb{I}}(X,Y)

subject to one of the constraints (C1) to (C3).

The remainder of the work is organized as follows. Exploiting the link between MI and filtering,
section II addresses the associated filtering problem
[
29
]
analytically on the level of distributions. We recognize the conditional mean as a piece-wise deterministic Markov process
[
30
]
, and solve the partial differential equation (PDE) given by the hybrid generator
[
31
]
. In section III we express the MI and information rate as nonelementary Riemann integrals related to hypergeometric series. This permits further analysis of the constraints (C1) to (C3).
Capacity-achieving input was reported so far as asymmetric, favoring the OFF state
[
17
,
18
]
. In contrast, we find that constraint (C1) either enforces a symmetric allocation of ON and OFF state at optimality for certain
r_{1},r_{2}
or favors the ON state. ON-favoring local optima are implied by (C3).

I-B

Previous Work

The following expression due to
[

]
links the path-wise MI with the filtering problem of observing the input

\mathbb{I}(X_{[0,T]},Y_{[0,T]})=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\phi(c_{3}X(t))]-\mathbb{E}[\phi(c_{3}Z(t))]\ \mathrm{d}t,

Applying Jensen’s inequality to the second integrand term of (

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{I}(X_{[0,T]},Y_{[0,T]})}{c_{3}T}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{2}\phi(x_{1})+c_{1}\phi(x_{2})}{c_{1}+c_{2}}-\phi\left(x_{1}+\frac{c_{1}\Delta x}{c_{1}+c_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=:J(x_{1},x_{2},c_{1}/(c_{1}+c_{2})),

where
\Delta x:=x_{2}-x_{1}
is the dynamic range. Optimizing over the average power constraint
0<c_{1}/(c_{1}+c_{2})\leq p_{0}
implies

C\leq c_{3}J(x_{1},x_{2},\min\{p_{0},\bar{p}\}),

(2)

where
C
can be replaced by

and

\bar{p}=\left\{\exp\left(\frac{\phi(x_{2})-\phi(x_{1})}{\Delta x}-1\right)-x_{1}\right\}/{\Delta x}.

Kabanov and Davis showed that for

the bound (
2

[
17
,
18
]
.
The case

(no leakage) with

with

We contribute by analyzing how the capacity-achieving BMP input behaves for finite

For the process class of BMPs,

where

\ \mathrm{d}Z(t_{i})=Z(t_{i}+)-Z(t_{i}-)

II-A

The probability evolution equation

In absence of leakage the stochastic reset condition in (
3
) simplifies, because
Y(t)
increases solely if
X(t)
is in the ON state. Hence,
Z(t)
is reset to
x_{2}=1
upon jumps of
Y
.
The joint process
\{Z(t),Y(t)\}_{t}
is then a piece-wise deterministic Markov process
[
30
]
that jumps stochastically from state
\{Z(t_{i}-),Y(t_{i}-)\}
to state
\{Z(t_{i}+),Y(t_{i}+)\}=(1,Y(t_{i}-)+1)
. Hence, jump times
t_{i}
of
Y
and of
Z
are identical, and jumps occur with propensity
c_{3}Z(t)

[
29
]
. Since the propensity depends only on the first component, the projection onto
Z(t)
is a piece-wise deterministic Markov process itself. Its probability evolution equation is given by a hybrid generator, composed of the drift (Liouville) and the jump (Poisson) part
[
31
]
:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(t,z)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\{A(z)p(t,z)\}-c_{3}zp(t,z),\;\omega<z<1,

(4)

where

A(z):=c_{3}(c_{1}\Delta x/c_{3}-\gamma z+z^{2})
with
\gamma:=(c_{1}+c_{2}+\Delta xc_{3})/c_{3}
is the drift dynamics, i.e., the ODE part of (
3
) with stable equilibrium
\omega:=\frac{\gamma-\rho}{2}
, where
\rho:=\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-4c_{1}\Delta x/c_{3}}
. The reason why (
4
) is lacking an inflow of probability due to jumps is because all inflow enters at
z=1
. This is reflected in the proof of the subsequent theorem
II.1
. Although expression (
1
) has been used and extended
[
32
]
in multiple ways to address the capacity problem, to the best of our knowledge, the method introduced here is new to the field.

II-B

Transient and asymptotic distribution

Aiming for the distribution of
Z(t)
, we envision the stochastic ensemble of trajectories. All trajectories are initiated in the stationary mean
m:=c_{1}/(c_{1}+c_{2})
, because
Z(0)=\mathbb{E}[X(0)]
. They slide down according to the function

f_{\iota}(t)=\omega+\frac{\rho(\iota-\omega)}{\iota-\omega+e^{\rho c_{3}t}(\rho+\omega-\iota)}

with
\iota=m
(see Fig.
1
b), which solves the Riccati equation
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}Z(t)=A(Z(t))
.
The solution curve
f_{1}(t)
with initial value

at
t=0
separates the
(t,z)
-plane into two regions summarized in the equivalence that involves the first jump time
t_{1}
of

:

t<t_{1}\Leftrightarrow Z(t)\leq f_{1}(t)\Leftrightarrow Z(t)=f_{m}(t).

(5)

Fig.
1

, while Fig.
1

at any time point

Theorem II.1
.

The probability measure
\mu_{t}\colon\mathcal{B}(\omega,1]\to[0,1],\mu_{t}(B)=\mathbb{P}[Z(t)\in B]
, defined for Borel sets
B\subseteq(\omega,1]
, is a hybrid measure

\mu_{t}(B)=\kappa(t)\delta_{f_{m}(t)}(B)+\nu_{t}(B),

(6)

composed of a Dirac measure
\delta_{a}(B)=\mathbbm{1}_{B}(a),a\in\mathbb{R}

at
f_{m}(t)
with weight

\kappa(t)=e^{-\omega c_{3}t}\cdot\left(1-\rho^{-1}(m-\omega)(1-e^{-\rho c_{3}t})\right)

and an absolutely continuous measure
\nu_{t}(\mathrm{d}z)=\pi(z)\mathrm{d}z
supported on
(f_{1}(t),1]
with time-independent density

\pi(z)=\alpha(z-\omega)^{\beta-\frac{3}{2}}(\omega+\rho-z)^{-(\beta+\frac{3}{2})},

(7)

where

\alpha:=(1-\omega)^{\frac{3}{2}-\beta}(\omega+\rho-1)^{\beta+\frac{3}{2}}\cdot\frac{c_{3}m}{c_{2}},\quad\beta:=\frac{\gamma}{2\rho}.

Proof.

We use (
5
) and compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[Z(t)\in B]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[Z(t)\in B,t_{1}>t]+\mathbb{P}[Z(t)\in B,t_{1}\leq t]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[t_{1}>t]\delta_{f_{m}(t)}(B)+\mathbb{P}[Z(t)\in B\cap(f_{1}(t),1]]

having the form (
6
). First,

\mathbb{P}[t_{1}>t]=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}f_{m}(s)\ \mathrm{d}s\right)=\kappa(t).

Second, by the equivalence

Z(t)\in B\cap(f_{1}(t),1]\Leftrightarrow t-\sup\{t_{i}\mid t_{i}\leq t\}\in f_{1}^{-1}(B),t_{1}\leq t

the absolutely continuity of the
t_{i}
implies that
\nu_{t}(B)=\mathbb{P}[Z(t)\in B\cap(f_{1}(t),1]]
is an absolute continuous measure, i.e.,
\nu_{t}(\mathrm{d}z)=p(t,z)\mathrm{d}z
with some density
p(t,z)
supported on
(f_{1}(t),1]
. Its solution is obtained by the method of characteristics
[
33
]
, initiated at the boundary condition
p_{1}(t):=p(t,1)
and propagated through the rewritten linear PDE (
4
)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(t,z)+A(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial z}p(t,z)=p(t,z)\left\{-c_{3}z-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}A(z)\right\}.

It remains to evaluate
p_{1}(t)
.
We compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[c_{3}X(t)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}[Y(t+h)-Y(t)=1\mid X_{[0,t]}]}{h}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}[Y(t+h)-Y(t)=1]}{h}

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}[Z(t+h)\in(f_{1}(h),1]]}{h}

\displaystyle=-f_{1}^{\prime}(t)p(t,1)=-A(1)p_{1}(t)=c_{2}p_{1}(t).

The absolute continuity of
\nu_{t}
was used in the fourth equality. Stationarity of
X
implies that
p_{1}(t)\equiv c_{3}m/c_{2}
independent of
t
. Plugging this in,
p(t,z)=\pi(z)\mathbbm{1}_{(f_{1}(t),1]}(z)
is obtained.
∎

Theorem II.2
.

The asymptotic distribution is absolutely continuous, supported on
(\omega,1]
with density
\pi(z)
as in (
7
).

Proof.

Since
Z(t)
is an ergodic Markov process, the asymptotic equals the stationary distribution. The distribution

satisfies the stationarity condition obtained from (

III
The information surface

We consider the Poisson channel without leakage, unless mentioned otherwise.

III-A

Analytic expression

The results of the previous section allow us to state the main result of this paper.

Theorem III.1
.

Let
x_{1}=0,x_{2}=1
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{I}(X_{[0,T]},Y_{[0,T]})=

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{3}}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\phi(f_{m}(t))\kappa(t)\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-c_{3}\int_{\omega}^{1}\phi(z)\pi(z)(1-f_{1}^{-1}(z)/T)\ \mathrm{d}z,

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{I}}(X,Y)=

\displaystyle-c_{3}\int_{\omega}^{1}\phi(z)\pi(z)\ \mathrm{d}z.

Proof.

Using theorems
II.1
,
II.2
, the identity
\phi(cz)=c\phi(z)+z\phi(c)
, and Fubini, evaluate (
1
).
∎

We use a linear transformation of the integration variable
z\to(1-z)(1-\omega)^{-1}
and the series expansion of the logarithm as well as
z\mapsto(\rho+\omega-z)^{-\beta-3/2}
. Then by uniform convergence of the integrand, we may express

\bar{\mathbb{I}}(X,Y)=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}A_{ij}\left(\frac{1-\omega}{\rho}\right)^{j}(1-\omega)^{i}\,,

(8)

as an absolutely convergent series with coefficients

\displaystyle A_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{3}\alpha\rho^{-\beta-\frac{3}{2}}(1-\omega)^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\beta+\frac{3}{2})}\times

\displaystyle\frac{i!\Gamma(\beta+\frac{3}{2}+j)\Gamma(\beta-\frac{1}{2}+j)(\beta-\frac{1}{2}+j+\omega(i+2))}{j!\Gamma(\beta+\frac{5}{2}+i+j)},

establishing a link with Appell’s hypergeometric
F_{3}
series
[
34
]
.
Using (
8
), it can be verified that for
c_{1},c_{2}\to\infty
with asymptotic ratio
c_{1}/(c_{1}+c_{2})\to p_{0}
, it holds that
\beta\to 1/2,\omega\to p_{0}
and
(1-\omega)/\rho\to 0
. Hence
\bar{\mathbb{I}}(X,Y)\to c_{3}J(0,1,p_{0})
, in accordance with paragraph
I-B
. Expanding

, the MI
\mathbb{I}(X_{[0,T]},Y_{[0,T]})
is given in terms of Srivastava’s triple hypergeometric series
[
35
]
by analogous proceeding, implying

\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{I}(X_{[0,T]},Y_{[0,T]})\to c_{3}J(0,1,p_{0})

for

with asymptotic ratio
c_{1}/(c_{1}+c_{2})\to p_{0}
.

The above linear transformation and subsequent differentiation under the integral sign or, alternatively, summand-wise differentiation of (
8
) make (higher-order) partial derivatives with respect to

accessible for the phase plane analysis.

We introduce the relative rates
\tilde{c}_{1}=c_{1}/c_{3}
and
\tilde{c}_{2}=c_{2}/c_{3}
. The parameters

and

are input and output of the correspondent channel with relative rates

and normalized

III-E

Constraint (C3) allows for local optima

The quantity

The constraint (C3) allows one rate to be infinite given the other is less than

The constraint defines a family of level sets

IV
Conclusion

We considered the Poisson channel with BMPs as input class. The method of using the hybrid generator for the evolution of the causal estimator allowed to express its distribution in closed form and represent the MI and information rate by Riemann integrals. The information surface was then analyzed for different constraints on sojourn times. The mathematical derivation heavily relied on the Markov assumption and on neglecting leakage. Advancing mathematical results on leaking, Semi-Markov and multi-state inputs remain open problems. Among general binary inputs, our result yields a lower bound on the capacity.

Optimizing the allocation of the ON and the OFF state under constraint (C1) can be intuitively explained as an interplay between different forces that maximize the efficiency and precision of signal transmission. On the one hand, a force, reducing average sojourn times is predominant in region B of Fig.
2
. This force aims at increasing the amount of signals transmitted. On the other hand, forces that increase the sojourn time in the ON and OFF states are predominant in regions A and C, respectively. A larger sojourn time in the ON state increases the likelihood of observing the ON state at the channel output. A larger sojourn time in the OFF state decreases the likelihood of misinterpreting the period between consecutive channel output pulses as an input OFF phase. The phase diagram in Fig.
2
explicitly quantifies how the ensemble of forces is balanced. Constraint (C3) admits an analogous driving force towards its local maximum.

Given the analyzed model’s rather minimal nature it might not account for biophysical reality. Yet assuming that evolutionary strategies aim at achieving capacity
[
25
]
the model supplies the hypothesis that system parameters
\tilde{c}_{1},\tilde{c}_{2}
are located in or near the optimal region B
[
37
]
. The prediction made by this interpretation is yet to be verified by experimentalists.

1. Introduction

Bessel functions of purely imaginary order play an important role in quantum mechanics in the solution of the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation with exponential potentials. The Bessel function
K_{i\nu}(x)
stands alone among the standard Bessel functions
since it is real when the argument
x
is positive and decays as
x\to+\infty
; the Bessel functions
J_{i\nu}(x)
,

Y_{i\nu}(x)
and the modified Bessel function
I_{i\nu}(x)
are all complex when
\nu
and
x
are real and nonzero.

A comprehensive discussion of the properties of the Bessel functions of imaginary order has been given by Dunster in
[
3
]
. In particular, he introduced the real function
[
3
, (2.2)]

L_{i\nu}(x)=\frac{\pi}{2\sinh\pi\nu}\{I_{i\nu}(x)+I_{-i\nu}(x)\}\qquad(\nu\neq 0),

(1.1)

which is an appropriate numerically satisfactory companion to
K_{i\nu}(x)
when
\nu
is real and nonzero and
x>0
.
This may be contrasted with the standard definition of
K_{i\nu}(x)
given by

K_{i\nu}(x)=\frac{\pi i}{2\sinh\pi\nu}\{I_{i\nu}(x)-I_{-i\nu}(x)\}.

(1.2)

For the
J
-Bessel function of imaginary order, Dunster
[
3
, (3.4)]
introduced the real functions (when
x>0
)

F_{i\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{2\cosh\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi\nu}\{J_{i\nu}(x)+J_{-i\nu}(x)\},

(1.3)

G_{i\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{2\sinh\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi\nu}\{J_{i\nu}(x)-J_{-i\nu}(x),\}

(1.4)

which clearly satisfy
F_{-i\nu}(x)=F_{i\nu}(x)
and
G_{-i\nu}(x)=G_{i\nu}(x)
.
He obtained several properties satisfied by these functions, such as connection formulas, Wronskians, integral representations and the behaviour at singularities. In addition, he considered the zeros of
L_{i\nu}(x)
,
F_{i\nu}(x)
and
G_{i\nu}(x)
as a function of the real argument
x
.

In a recent paper, Bagirova and Khanmamedov
[
1
]
considered the
\nu
-zeros of
K_{i\nu}(x)
when
x>0
is fixed (that is, the zeros considered as a function of
\nu
rather than the argument
x
). They have shown that
K_{i\nu}(x)
has a countably infinite number of (simple) real zeros in
\nu
when
x>0
is fixed. We label the zeros
\nu_{n}
(
n=1,2\ldots
) and observe that it is sufficient to consider only the case
\nu>0
since
K_{-i\nu}(x)=K_{i\nu}(x)
. By transforming the differential equation satisfied by
K_{i\nu}(x)
into a one-dimensional Schrödinger equation with an exponential potential, these authors employed the well-known quantisation rule to deduce the leading asymptotic behaviour of the
n
th zero given by

\nu_{n}\sim\frac{\pi n}{\log\,n}\qquad(n\to+\infty).

(1.5)

This approximation was further discussed and refined in
[
5
]
. The
\nu
-zeros of the Hankel functions have been considered in
[
2
,
4
]
.
The numerical computation of the Bessel functions of purely imaginary order has been discussed in
[
6
]
.

A more detailed investigation of the asymptotic behaviour of the
n
th zero of
K_{i\nu}(x)
for fixed
x>0
has been given by the author in
[
8
]
.
This relied on use of the well-known asymptotic expansion of
K_{i\nu}(x)
for
\nu\to+\infty
and yielded very accurate results from a three-term expansion for
\nu_{n}
. Here we consider the
\nu
-zeros of the functions
L_{i\nu}(x)
,
K_{i\nu}(x)
,
F_{i\nu}(x)
and
G_{i\nu}(x)
employing a simpler, unified approach. Although this approach does not produce results as accurate as those obtained in
[
8
]
, it nevertheless yields very satisfactory agreement (from a three-term expansion) with the numerically computed
\nu
-zeros of these functions.

2. The modified Bessel functions

The modified Bessel function
I_{i\nu}(x)
is defined by

I_{i\nu}(x)=\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{i\nu}}{\Gamma(1+i\nu)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{2k}}{k!(1+i\nu)_{k}},

(2.1)

where
(a)_{\nu}=\Gamma(a+\nu)/\Gamma(a)
is the Pochhammer symbol.
We can obtain the expansion of
I_{i\nu}(x)
for
\nu\to+\infty
by making use of the well-known expansion

\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+i\nu)}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\nu}}\,e^{i\nu}(i\nu)^{-i\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{k}}{(i\nu)^{k}}\qquad(\nu\to+\infty),

where the first few Stirling coefficients
\gamma_{k}
are

\gamma_{0}=1,\quad\gamma_{1}=-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{12}}$},\quad\gamma_{2}=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{288}}$},\quad\gamma_{3}=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{139}{51840}}$},\quad\gamma_{4}=-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{571}{2488320}}$}\quad\gamma_{5}=-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{163879}{209018880}}$}.

We observe that the sum appearing in (
2.1
) for fixed
x
can be written in the form

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{2k}}{k!(1+i\nu)_{k}}\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{C_{k}(\chi)}{(i\nu)^{k}},\qquad\chi:=\frac{x^{2}}{4}\qquad(\nu\to+\infty),

where the coefficients
C_{k}(\chi)
for
0\leq k\leq 5
are

C_{0}(\chi)=1,\quad C_{1}(\chi)=\chi,\quad C_{2}(\chi)=\frac{\chi}{2}(-2+\chi),

C_{3}(\chi)=\frac{\chi}{6}(6-9\chi+\chi^{2}),\quad C_{4}(\chi)=\frac{\chi}{24}(-24+84\chi-24\chi^{2}+\chi^{3}),

(2.2)

C_{5}(\chi)=\frac{\chi}{120}(120-900\chi+500\chi^{2}-50\chi^{3}+\chi^{4}).

Then we obtain the large-
\nu
expansion of
I_{i\nu}(x)
in the form

I_{i\nu}(x)\sim\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi\nu}}{\sqrt{2\pi\nu}}\,e^{-i\Phi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{k}}{(i\nu)^{k}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{C_{k}(\chi)}{(i\nu)^{k}}=\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi\nu}}{\sqrt{2\pi\nu}}\,e^{-i\Phi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{(i\nu)^{k}},

(2.3)

where

\Phi:=\nu\log\,\nu-\nu+\nu\log\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x+\frac{\pi}{4}=\nu\log\,\lambda\nu+\frac{\pi}{4},\qquad\lambda:=\frac{2}{ex}.

(2.4)

The coefficients
a_{k}
are

a_{0}=1,\quad a_{1}=C_{1}(\chi)+\gamma_{1}C_{0}(\chi),\quad a_{2}=C_{2}(\chi)+\gamma_{1}C_{1}(\chi)+\gamma_{2}C_{0}(\chi),

and in general,

a_{k}=C_{k}(\chi)+\sum_{r=1}^{k}\gamma_{r}C_{k-r}(\chi)\qquad(k\geq 1).

The same procedure applied to
I_{-i\nu}(x)
leads to the expansion

I_{-i\nu}(x)\sim\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi\nu}}{\sqrt{2\pi\nu}}\,e^{i\Phi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{(-i\nu)^{k}}\qquad(\nu\to+\infty).

(2.5)

The expansions (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) can now be employed in the definitions of the modified Bessel functions
L_{i\nu}(x)
and
K_{i\nu}(x)
defined in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). We then obtain

L_{i\nu}(x)\sim\frac{\pi}{\sinh\pi\nu}\,\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi\nu}}{\sqrt{2\pi\nu}}\biggl\{\cos\Phi\biggl(1-\frac{a_{2}}{\nu^{2}}+\frac{a_{4}}{\nu^{4}}-\cdots\biggr)-\sin\Phi\biggl(\frac{a_{1}}{\nu}-\frac{a_{3}}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{a_{5}}{\nu^{5}}-\cdots\biggr)\biggr\}

(2.6)

and

K_{i\nu}(x)\sim\frac{\pi}{\sinh\pi\nu}\,\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi\nu}}{\sqrt{2\pi\nu}}\biggl\{\sin\Phi\biggl(1-\frac{a_{2}}{\nu^{2}}+\frac{a_{4}}{\nu^{4}}-\cdots\biggr)+\cos\Phi\biggl(\frac{a_{1}}{\nu}-\frac{a_{3}}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{a_{5}}{\nu^{5}}-\cdots\biggr)\biggr\}

(2.7)

for
\nu\to+\infty
.

2.1 Determination of the zeros

We first consider the
\nu
-zeros of
L_{i\nu}(x)
and, in (
2.6
), accordingly set

\Phi=(n+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\pi-\epsilon,

(2.8)

where
n
is a large positive integer and
\epsilon
is a small quantity. From (
2.6
), the
\nu
-zeros are determined by

\sin\epsilon\biggl(1-\frac{a_{2}}{\nu^{2}}+\frac{a_{4}}{\nu^{4}}-\cdots\biggl)-\cos\epsilon\biggl(\frac{a_{1}}{\nu}-\frac{a_{3}}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{a_{5}}{\nu^{5}}-\cdots\biggr)=0,

(2.9)

whence

\tan\epsilon=\frac{\frac{a_{1}}{\nu}-\frac{a_{3}}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{a_{5}}{\nu^{5}}-\cdots}{1-\frac{a_{2}}{\nu^{2}}+\frac{a_{4}}{\nu^{4}}-\cdots}=\frac{a_{1}}{\nu}+\frac{a_{1}a_{2}-a_{3}}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{a_{1}a_{2}^{2}-a_{2}a_{3}-a_{1}a_{4}+a_{5}}{\nu^{5}}+\cdots\ .

Inversion of the tangent then yields

\epsilon=\frac{A_{0}}{\nu}+\frac{A_{1}}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{A_{2}}{\nu^{5}}+\cdots\

(2.10)

for
\nu\to+\infty
, where

A_{0}=a_{1},\quad A_{1}=a_{1}a_{2}-a_{3}-\frac{1}{3}a_{1}^{3},

A_{2}=a_{1}a_{2}^{2}+a_{1}^{2}a_{3}-a_{1}^{3}a_{2}-a_{2}a_{3}-a_{1}a_{4}+a_{5}+\frac{1}{5}a_{1}^{5}.

(2.11)

From (
2.4
) and (
2.8
), we finally obtain the equation describing the large-
n
zeros of
L_{i\nu}(x)
given by

\nu\log\,\lambda\nu=(n+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$})\pi-\epsilon=m_{+}-\frac{A_{0}}{\nu}-\frac{A_{1}}{\nu^{3}}-\frac{A_{2}}{\nu^{5}}-\cdots,

(2.12)

where for convenience we have put
m_{+}=(n+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$})\pi
.

To solve this equation we now expand
\nu\equiv\nu_{n}
as

\nu_{n}=\xi+\frac{b_{0}}{\xi}+\frac{b_{1}}{\xi^{3}}+\frac{b_{2}}{\xi^{5}}+\cdots,

where the
b_{k}
are constants to be determined and we suppose that
\xi
is large as
n\to\infty
. Substitution in (
2.12
) then produces

\xi\log\lambda\xi+\frac{b_{0}(1+\log\lambda\xi)}{\xi}+\frac{b_{1}(1+\log\lambda\xi)+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}b_{0}^{2}}{\xi^{3}}+\frac{b_{2}(1+\log\lambda\xi)+b_{0}b_{1}-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{6}}$}b_{0}^{3}}{\xi^{5}}+\cdots

=m_{+}-\biggl(\frac{A_{0}}{\xi}+\frac{A_{1}-A_{0}b_{0}}{\xi^{3}}+\frac{A_{2}-3A_{1}b_{0}+A_{0}(b_{0}^{2}-b_{1})}{\xi^{5}}+\cdots\biggr)\,.

Equating coefficients of like powers of
\xi
, we obtain

\xi\log\lambda\xi=m_{+},

(2.13)

and

b_{0}=\frac{-A_{0}}{1+\log\lambda\xi},\quad b_{1}=\frac{A_{0}-A_{1}b_{0}-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}b_{0}^{2}}{1+\log\lambda\xi},

b_{2}=\frac{3A_{1}b_{0}-A_{0}(b_{0}^{2}-b_{1})-A_{2}-b_{0}b_{1}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{6}}$}b_{0}^{3}}{1+\log\lambda\xi}~.

The solution of (
2.13
) for the lowest-order term
\xi
can be expressed in terms of the Lambert
W
function, which is the (positive) solution
1
1

1

In
[
7
, p. 111]
this is denoted by Wp
(z)
.
of
W(z)e^{W(z)}=z
for
z>0
.
Rearrangement of (
2.13
) shows that

\frac{m_{+}}{\xi}\,e^{m_{+}/\xi}=\lambda m_{+},

whence

\xi=\frac{m_{+}}{W(\lambda m_{+})}.

(2.14)

If we define
\chi:=\xi/m
, so that by (
3.1
)
1+\log\lambda\xi=(1+\chi)/\chi
and introduce the coefficients

B_{0}=\frac{-A_{0}}{1+\chi},\quad B_{1}=\frac{A_{0}b_{0}-A_{1}-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}b_{0}^{2}}{\chi^{2}(1+\chi)},\quad B_{2}=\frac{3A_{1}b_{0}-A_{0}(b_{0}^{2}-b_{1})-A_{2}-b_{0}b_{1}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{6}}$}b_{0}^{3}}{\chi^{4}(1+\chi)},

(2.15)

then we finally have the result:

Theorem 1

\!\!\!.
The expansion for the
n
th
\nu
-zero of
L_{i\nu}(x)
for fixed
x>0
is

\nu_{n}\sim\frac{m_{+}}{W(\lambda m_{+})}+\frac{B_{0}}{m_{+}}+\frac{B_{1}}{m_{+}^{3}}+\frac{B_{2}}{m_{+}^{5}}+\cdots\qquad(n\to\infty),

(2.16)

where
m_{+}=(n+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$})\pi
,
\lambda=2/(ex)
and the coefficients
B_{k}
are given in (
2.15
).

The same procedure can be applied to determine the
\nu
-zeros of
K_{i\nu}(x)
. In (
2.7
), we now set

\Phi=n\pi-\epsilon,

where again
n
is a large positive integer and
\epsilon
is a small quantity, to produce the same equation given in (
2.9
). Consequently, the value of
\epsilon
is given by (
2.10
) with the same coefficients
A_{k}
. Then

\nu\log\,\lambda\nu=(n-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$})\pi-\epsilon=m_{-}-\frac{A_{0}}{\nu}-\frac{A_{1}}{\nu^{3}}-\frac{A_{2}}{\nu^{5}}-\cdots,

where
m_{-}=(n-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$})\pi
and so we obtain the final result:

Theorem 2

\!\!\!.
The expansion for the
n
th
\nu
-zero of
K_{i\nu}(x)
for fixed
x>0
is

\nu_{n}\sim\frac{m_{-}}{W(\lambda m_{-})}+\frac{B_{0}}{m_{-}}+\frac{B_{1}}{m_{-}^{3}}+\frac{B_{2}}{m_{-}^{5}}+\cdots\qquad(n\to\infty),

(2.17)

where
m=(n-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$})\pi
,
\lambda=2/(ex)
and the coefficients
B_{k}
are given in (
2.15
).

The leading behaviour
\nu_{n}\sim\xi
can be seen from the asymptotic expansion of
W(z)
for
z\to+\infty

[
7
, (4.13.10)]

W(z)\sim\log\,z-\log\log\,z+\frac{\log\log\,z}{\log\,z}+\cdots,

from which it follows that

\frac{1}{W(z)}\sim\frac{1}{\log\,z}\biggl\{1+\frac{\log\log\,z}{\log\,z}+\cdots\biggr\}.

Then, for the Bessel functions
L_{i\nu}(x)
and
K_{i\nu}(x)
, we have the leading behaviour of the
\nu
-zeros given by

\nu_{n}\sim\frac{m_{\pm}}{\log\lambda m_{\pm}}\qquad(n\to\infty),

(2.18)

respectively.

3. The Bessel functions
F_{i\nu}(x)
and
G_{i\nu}(x)

The
J
-Bessel function of purely imaginary order is defined by

J_{i\nu}(x)=\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{i\nu}}{\Gamma(1+i\nu)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{2k}}{k!(1+i\nu)_{k}},

from which we obtain in an analogous manner to that described in Section 2 the large-
\nu
expansion

J_{\pm i\nu}(x)\sim\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi\nu}}{\sqrt{2\pi\nu}}\,e^{\mp i\Phi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\hat{a}}_{k}}{(\pm i\nu)^{k}}\qquad(\nu\to+\infty),

(3.1)

where
\Phi
is defined in (
2.4
).
The coefficients
{\hat{a}}_{k}
are given by

{\hat{a}}_{0}=1,\quad{\hat{a}}_{k}=C_{k}(-\chi)+\sum_{r=1}^{k}\gamma_{k}C_{r-k}(-\chi)\qquad(k\geq 1),

with the first few
C_{k}(\chi)
defined in (
2.2
).

If the expansion (
3.1
) is substituted into the definitions of
F_{i\nu}(x)
and
G_{i\nu}(x)
in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), we obtain

\cosh\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi\nu\,F_{i\nu}(x)\sim\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi\nu}}{\sqrt{2\pi\nu}}\biggl\{\cos\Phi\biggl(1-\frac{{\hat{a}}_{2}}{\nu^{2}}+\frac{{\hat{a}}_{4}}{\nu^{4}}+\cdots\biggr)-\sin\Phi\biggl(\frac{{\hat{a}}_{1}}{\nu}-\frac{{\hat{a}}_{3}}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{{\hat{a}}_{5}}{\nu^{5}}-\cdots\biggr)\biggr\}

and

\sinh\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi\nu\,G_{i\nu}(x)\sim-\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi\nu}}{\sqrt{2\pi\nu}}\biggl\{\sin\Phi\biggl(1-\frac{{\hat{a}}_{2}}{\nu^{2}}+\frac{{\hat{a}}_{4}}{\nu^{4}}+\cdots\biggr)+\cos\Phi\biggl(\frac{{\hat{a}}_{1}}{\nu}-\frac{{\hat{a}}_{3}}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{{\hat{a}}_{5}}{\nu^{5}}-\cdots\biggr)\biggr\}

as
\nu\to+\infty
. Proceeding as in Section 2, we set
\Phi=(n+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\pi-\epsilon
for
F_{i\nu}(x)
and
\Phi=n\pi-\epsilon
for
G_{i\nu}(x)
. This produces

\epsilon=\frac{A_{0}}{\nu}+\frac{A_{1}}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{A_{2}}{\nu^{5}}+\cdots\ ,

where the coefficients
A_{k}
are specified in (
2.11
) with the
a_{k}
replaced by
{\hat{a}}_{k}
.

Omitting the details, we finally obtain the result:

Theorem 3

\!\!\!.
The expansion for the
n
th
\nu
-zero of
F_{i\nu}(x)
for fixed
x>0
is

\nu_{n}\sim\frac{m_{+}}{W(\lambda m_{+})}+\frac{B_{0}}{m_{+}}+\frac{B_{1}}{m_{+}^{3}}+\frac{B_{2}}{m_{+}^{5}}+\cdots\ \qquad(n\to\infty),

(3.2)

and that for
G_{i\nu}(x)
is

\nu_{n}\sim\frac{m_{-}}{W(\lambda m_{-})}+\frac{B_{0}}{m_{-}}+\frac{B_{1}}{m_{-}^{3}}+\frac{B_{2}}{m_{-}^{5}}+\cdots\ \qquad(n\to\infty),

(3.3)

where
m_{\pm}=(n\pm\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$})\pi
and
\lambda=2/(ex)
. The coefficients
B_{k}
are given in (
2.15
) and the
A_{k}
in
(
2.11
) with the
a_{k}
replaced by
{\hat{a}}_{k}
.

n

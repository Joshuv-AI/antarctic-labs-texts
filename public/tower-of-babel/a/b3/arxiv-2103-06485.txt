Monte Carlo Scattering–by–Scattering Simulation of
3-Dimensional Elastic WIMP–Nucleus Scattering Events

Abstract
In this paper, as the first part of the third step of our study on developing data analysis procedures for using 3-dimensional information offered by directional direct Dark Matter detection experiments in the future, we present our double–Monte Carlo “scattering–by–scattering” simulation of the 3-dimensional elastic WIMP–nucleus scattering process, which can provide 3-D velocity information (the magnitude, the direction, and the incoming/scattering time) of each incident halo WIMP as well as the recoil direction and the recoil energy of the scattered target nucleus in different celestial coordinate systems. For readers’ reference, (animated) simulation plots with different WIMP masses and several frequently used target nuclei for all functionable underground laboratories can be found and downloaded on our online (interactive) demonstration webpage ( http://www.tir.tw/phys/hep/dm/amidas-2d/ ).

4.2.1
Validation of 3-D elastic WIMP–nucleus scattering events

For one WIMP event
generated in the Galactic coordinate system
and transformed step by step
into the laboratory coordinate system
with the velocity of

{\bf v}_{\chi}(v_{\chi,{\rm Lab}},\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab},\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})
,
the kinetic energy
can be given by

E_{\chi}=\frac{1}{2}m_{\chi}|{\bf v}_{\chi}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}m_{\chi}v_{\chi,{\rm Lab}}^{2}~\!.

(18)

Then
the recoil energy of the scattered target nucleus
in the incoming–WIMP coordinate system
can be estimated by
the recoil angle
\eta

or the equivalent recoil angle

\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}}
as

Q=\left[\frac{4m_{\chi}m_{\rm N}}{(m_{\chi}+m_{\rm N})^{2}}~\!\cos^{2}(\eta)\right]E_{\chi}=\left[\left(\frac{2m_{\rm r,N}^{2}}{m_{\rm N}}\right)v_{\chi,{\rm Lab}}^{2}\right]\sin^{2}(\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}})~\!,

(19)

where

m_{\rm r,N}\equiv\frac{m_{\chi}m_{\rm N}}{m_{\chi}+m_{\rm N}}

(20)

is the reduced mass of
the WIMP mass
m_{\chi}
and
that of the target nucleus
m_{\rm N}
.
From Eq. (
19
),
one can get that

\frac{dQ}{d\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}}}=\left[\left(\frac{2m_{\rm r,N}^{2}}{m_{\rm N}}\right)v_{\chi,{\rm Lab}}^{2}\right]\sin(2\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}})~\!.

(21)

Hence,
the differential cross section
d\sigma

given by
the absolute value of the momentum transfer
from the incident WIMP to the recoiling target nucleus,

q=|{\bf q}|=\sqrt{2m_{\rm N}Q}~\!,

(22)

can be obtained as

[
2
]

d\sigma=\frac{1}{v_{\chi,{\rm Lab}}^{2}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{0}}{4m_{\rm r,N}^{2}}\right)F^{2}(q)~\!dq^{2}=\sigma_{0}F^{2}(Q)\sin(2\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}})~\!d\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}}~\!.

(23)

Then
the differential WIMP–nucleus scattering cross section
with respect to
the recoil angle

\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}}

can generally be given by

\frac{d\sigma}{d\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}}}=\bigg[\sigma_{0}^{\rm SI}F_{\rm SI}^{2}(Q)+\sigma_{0}^{\rm SD}F_{\rm SD}^{2}(Q)\bigg]\sin(2\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}})~\!.

(24)

Here

\sigma_{0}^{\rm(SI,SD)}
are
the spin–independent (SI)/spin–dependent (SD) total cross sections
ignoring the form factor suppression
and

F_{\rm(SI,SD)}(Q)
indicate the elastic nuclear form factors
corresponding to the SI/SD WIMP interactions,
respectively.
Remind that
the recoil energy
Q

is the function of the recoil angle
\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}}

given by Eq. (
19
).

Finally,
taking into account
the proportionality of the WIMP flux
to the incident velocity,
the generating probability distribution of
the recoil angle

\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}}
,
which is proportional to
the scattering event rate of
incident halo WIMPs
with an incoming velocity
v_{\chi,{\rm Lab}}

off target nuclei
going into recoil angles of

\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}}\pm d\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}}/2

with recoil energies of
Q\pm dQ/2
,
can generally be given by

\displaystyle f_{{\rm N_{R}},\chi_{\rm in},\theta}(\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}})

\displaystyle\hskip-5.59721pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.59721pt\left(\frac{v_{\chi,{\rm Lab}}}{v_{\chi,{\rm cutoff}}}\right)\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}}}\right)

(25)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.59721pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.59721pt\left(\frac{v_{\chi,{\rm Lab}}}{v_{\chi,{\rm cutoff}}}\right)\bigg[\sigma_{0}^{\rm SI}F_{\rm SI}^{2}(Q)+\sigma_{0}^{\rm SD}F_{\rm SD}^{2}(Q)\bigg]\sin(2\theta_{\rm N_{R},\chi_{in}})~\!,

where

v_{\chi,{\rm cutoff}}\simeq 800
km/s is
a cut–off velocity of incident halo WIMPs
in the laboratory coordinate system.

for using and/or combining 3-dimensional information
offered by directional direct Dark Matter detection experiments
in the future,
we finally achieved
our double–Monte Carlo scattering–by–scattering simulation of
3-dimensional elastic WIMP–nucleus scattering process,
which can provide
3-D information
(the magnitude,
the direction,
and the incoming/scattering time) of
each incident halo WIMP
as well as
the experimentally measurable
recoil direction
and recoil energy of
the WIMP–scattered target nucleus
event by event
in different celestial coordinate systems.

In this paper,
we described at first
the overall workflow of our simulation procedure.
After the summary of
the MC generation process of
the 3-D velocity information of
Galactic WIMPs
and
the transformations of
the 3-D (velocity) information
between different celestial coordinate systems,
we introduced the incoming–WIMP coordinate system
for describing the 3-D WIMP–nucleus scattering process
and derived the validation criterion
in our MC generation of
the 3-D recoil information of
scattered target nuclei,
which is basically according to
the cross section (nuclear form factor) suppression
on the
recoil–angle–dependent
recoil energy.

Currently,
several approximations
about the Earth’s orbital motion
in the Solar system
and the observation periods/daily shifts
have been used in our simulation package.
First,
the Earth’s orbit around the Sun is perfectly circular
on the Ecliptic plane
and the orbital speed is thus a constant.
Second,
the date of the vernal equinox is exactly fixed
at the end of the May 20th (the 79th day) of
a 365-day year
and the few extra hours
in an actual Solar year
have been neglected.
Nevertheless,
considering the very low WIMP scattering event rate
and thus maximal a few (tens) of total WIMP events
observed in at least a few tens (or even hundreds) of days
(an optimistic overall event rate of
\>\raisebox{-3.01385pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}$}\>~\!{\cal O}(1)
event/day)
for the first–phase analyses,
these approximations should be acceptable.

Hopefully,
this (and more works fulfilled in the future)
could help our colleagues
to develop analysis methods
for understanding
the astrophysical and particle properties of
Galactic WIMPs
as well as
the structure of Dark Matter halo
by using directional direct detection data.

A.4
Incoming–WIMP coordinate system

For our Monte Carlo simulation of
3-D elastic WIMP–nucleus scattering events,
we have introduced the “incoming–WIMP” coordinate system
defined in Sec.
4.1
.

A.4.1
Transformation matrices

Similar to the transformations
between the horizontal
and the Earth coordinate systems,
the transformation
from the incoming–WIMP coordinate system
to the laboratory coordinate system
can be done by rotating
at first
\pi/2-\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab}
around the
{\bf Y}_{\chi_{\rm in}}
–axis
and then
\pi-\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab}
around the
{\bf Z}_{\chi_{\rm in}}
–axis
(see Fig.
6
)
and
can thus be given by

\displaystyle\hskip-5.59721pt{\bf M}_{\rm\chi_{in}\to Lab}(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab},\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})

\displaystyle\hskip-5.59721pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.59721pt\left[\begin{array}[]{c c c}-\cos(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})\sin(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~\sin(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~\cos(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})\cos(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})\\
-\sin(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})\sin(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~-\cos(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~\sin(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})\cos(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})\\
\cos(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~0&~\sin(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})\\
\end{array}\right]~\!.

Conversely,
we also have

\displaystyle\hskip-5.59721pt{\bf M}_{\rm Lab\to\chi_{in}}(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab},\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})

\displaystyle\hskip-5.59721pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.59721pt\left[\begin{array}[]{c c c}-\cos(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})\sin(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~-\sin(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})\sin(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~\cos(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})\\
\sin(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~-\cos(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~0\\
\cos(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})\cos(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~\sin(\phi_{\rm\chi,Lab})\cos(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})&~\sin(\theta_{\rm\chi,Lab})\\
\end{array}\right]~\!.

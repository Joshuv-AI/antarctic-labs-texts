Abstract
. We study the S ​ U SU -linear operations in complex cobordism and prove that they are generated by the well-known geometric operations ∂ i \partial_{i} . For the theory W W of c 1 c_{1} -spherical bordism, we describe all S ​ U SU -linear multiplications on W W and projections 𝑀𝑈 → W \mathit{MU}\to W . We also analyse complex orientations on W W and the corresponding formal group laws F W F_{W} . The relationship between the formal group laws F W F_{W} and the coefficient ring W ∗ W_{*} of the W W -theory was studied by Buchstaber in 1972. We extend his results by showing that for any S ​ U SU -linear multiplication and orientation on W W , the coefficients of the corresponding formal group law F W F_{W} do not generate the ring W ∗ W_{*} , unlike the situation with complex bordism.

Introduction

Complex bordism
, or
U
-bordism
, is the bordism theory of stably complex manifolds. Geometrically, a stably complex structure (
U
-structure) on a manifold
M
is a choice of a complex structure on its stable tangent bundle, or a reduction of the structure group of the stable tangent bundle to the unitary group
U=U(\infty)
. Homotopically, a stably complex structure is the homotopy class of a lift of the map
M\to BO
classifying the stable tangent bundle to a map
M\to BU
. The bordism classes of stably complex manifolds form a graded ring with respect to the operations of disjoint union and cartesian product, called the
complex bordism ring
and denoted by
MU_{*}
. It is the coefficient ring of the
complex bordism theory
, the generalised (co)homology theory defined by the
Thom spectrum

\mathit{MU}=\{\mathit{MU}(n)\}
, where
MU(n)
is the Thom space of the universal
U(n)
-bundle
EU(n)\to BU(n)
. Given a CW-pair
(X,A)
, its bordism and cobordims groups are defined by

\displaystyle MU_{n}(X,A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi_{2k+n}\bigl((X/A)\wedge\mathit{MU}(k)\bigr),

\displaystyle MU^{n}(X,A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\bigl[\varSigma^{2k-n}(X/A),\mathit{MU}(k)\bigr]\quad\text{for a finite CW-pair $(X,A)$}.

In particular,
MU_{*}=\pi_{*}(\mathit{MU})=MU_{*}(\mathit{pt})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi_{2k+*}\bigl(\mathit{MU}(k)\bigr)
. We also denote
MU^{*}=MU^{*}(\mathit{pt})
, the
complex cobordism ring
, graded nonpositively.

SU
-bordism
is the bordism theory of smooth manifolds with a special unitary structure in the stable tangent bundle. Geometrically, an
SU
-structure on a manifold
M
is defined by a reduction of the structure group of the stable tangent bundle of
M
to the group
SU(N)
. Homotopically, an
SU
-structure is the homotopy class of a lift of the classifying map

M\to BO(2N)
to a map
M\to BSU(N)
. A manifold
M
admits an
SU
-structure whenever it admits a stably complex structure with
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}M)=0
. The
SU
-bordims ring

\mathit{MSU}_{*}=\pi_{*}(\mathit{MSU})
is the coefficient ring of the
\mathit{SU}
-bordism theory
, defined by the Thom spectrum

\mathit{MSU}=\{\mathit{MSU}(n)\}
.

The details of the construction of the
\mathit{SU}
-bordism and the description of the coefficient ring
\mathit{MSU}_{*}
can be found in
[
14
,
17
,
11
]
.

A (stable)
operation

f
of degree
n
in complex cobordism is a family of additive maps

f\colon\mathit{MU}^{k}(X,A)\to\mathit{MU}^{k+n}(X,A)

which are functorial with respect to
(X,A)
and commute with the suspension isomorphisms. The set of all operations is an
\mathit{MU}^{*}
-algebra, denoted by
A^{U}
. It can be identified with the self-maps of the
\mathit{MU}
spectrum:

A^{U}\cong[\mathit{MU},\mathit{MU}]_{*}=MU^{*}(\mathit{MU})=\lim\limits_{\longleftarrow}\mathit{MU}^{*+2N}(\mathit{MU}(N)).

There is an isomorphism of left
\mathit{MU}^{*}
-modules

A^{U}\cong\mathit{MU}^{*}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}S,

where
S
is the
Landweber–Novikov algebra
, generated by the operations
S_{\omega}=\varphi^{*}(s^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{\omega})
corresponding via the Thom isomorphism
\varphi^{*}
to the universal characteristic classes
s^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{\omega}\in\mathit{MU}^{*}(\mathit{BU})
defined by symmetrising the monomials
t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{k}^{i_{k}}
indexed by partitions
\omega=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
. Therefore, any element
a\in A^{U}
can be written uniquely as an infinite series
a=\sum_{\omega}\lambda_{\omega}S_{\omega}
where
\lambda_{\omega}\in\mathit{MU}^{*}
. The Hopf algebra structure of
S
is described in
[
10
]
and
[
14
, §5]
.

The spectrum
\mathit{MU}
is an
\mathit{MSU}
-module via the forgetful morphism
\mathit{MSU}\to\mathit{MU}
, and an operation
f\colon\mathit{MU}\to\mathit{MU}
is
\mathit{MSU}
-linear
if it is an
\mathit{MSU}
-module map. By the standard property of spectra with torsion-free homotopy and homology groups, the
\mathit{MSU}
-linearity of an operation
f\colon\mathit{MU}\to\mathit{MU}
can be detected by its action on the coefficients
MU_{*}=\pi_{*}(\mathit{MU})
. Namely, an operation
f
is
\mathit{SU}
-linear if and only if it satisfies
f(ab)=af(b)
for any
a\in\mathit{MSU}_{*}
,
b\in\mathit{MU}_{*}
(see Theorem
1.3
).

A family of geometric operations
\partial_{i}\in[\mathit{MU},\mathit{MU}]_{-2i}=[\mathit{MU},\Sigma^{2i}\mathit{MU}]
was defined by Conner and Floyd
[
8
]
and studied further by Novikov
[
14
]
. The operation
\partial_{i}
assigns to a complex bordism class
[M]\in\mathit{MU}_{2n}
the bordism class of the submanifold
M_{i}\subset M
dual to
(\det\mathcal{T}M)^{\oplus i}
(the
i
-fold direct sum of the determinant of the tangent bundle). In particular,
\partial_{1}=\partial\colon\mathit{MU}_{2n}\to\mathit{MU}_{2n-2}
is the “boundary operation” that takes
[M]
to the bordism class of the submanifold dual to
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}M)
. Clearly,
\partial[M]
belongs to the image of the forgetful map
\mathit{MSU}_{*}\to\mathit{MU}_{*}
. Furthermore, the operations
\partial_{i}
are
SU
-linear by inspection.

In Section
1
we describe the algebra of
\mathit{SU}
-linear operations in complex cobordism. We show that the operations
\partial_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots
, form a topological basis of the left
MU^{*}
-module of
\mathit{SU}
-linear operations. That is, any
SU
-linear operation
f\in[\mathit{MU},\mathit{MU}]_{\mathit{MSU},*}
can be written uniquely as a series
f=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\mu_{i}\partial_{i}
with
\mu_{i}\in\mathit{MU}^{-2i-*}
, see Theorem
1.12
. In Theorem
1.14
we describe the product structure of
\mathit{SU}
-linear operations under composition via the coefficients of the formal group law in complex cobordism.

Conner and Floyd
[
8
]
and Stong
[
17
]
defined
c_{1}
-spherical bordism

W
, an intermediate theory between the
\mathit{SU}
- and
U
-bordism, following a similar construction of Wall in oriented bordism. The theory
W
was a key technical tool in Conner and Floyd’s calculation of torsion in
SU
-bordism. Both
[
8
]
and
[
17
]
defined a multiplicative structure on
W
using an
\mathit{SU}
-linear projection
\pi\colon\mathit{MU}\to W
. Stong
[
17
]
showed that the coefficient ring of the theory
W
is polynomial with respect to the multiplication defined by his projection. Although Conner–Floyd and Stong defined their projections differently, in the subsequent literature on
\mathit{SU}
- and
c_{1}
-spherical bordism the two projections were used interchangeably, suggesting that the two may coincide. As shown in
[
11
, §6]
, the Conner–Floyd and Stong projections are different, despite defining the same multiplication on
W
(see Example
2.20
).

In Section
2
we give several descriptions of projections
\pi\colon\mathit{MU}\to W
and give the conditions specifying
SU
-linear projections. We express the
SU
-linear Stong projection
\pi_{0}\colon\mathit{MU}\to W
as a series in operations
\partial_{i}
via the coefficients of the formal group law in complex cobordism (Proposition
2.9
), and show that any other projection
\pi\colon\mathit{MU}\to W
has the form
\pi_{0}(1+f\varDelta)
for some operation
f\in[\mathit{MU},\Sigma^{-4}\mathit{MU}]
(Theorem
2.14
), where
\varDelta\in[\mathit{MU},\Sigma^{4}\mathit{MU}]
is the Conner–Floyd operation satisfying
W=\mathop{\rm Ker}\varDelta
. In this description,
\mathit{SU}
-linear projections correspond to
\mathit{SU}
-linear operations
f
. In Theorem
2.21
we describe all
\mathit{SU}
-linear multiplications on
c_{1}
-spherical bordism
W
, and specify a condition for the multiplication to be defined by an
\mathit{SU}
-linear projection.

In Section
3
we study complex orientations of the theory
W
and the corresponding formal group laws. Any complex orientation
w\in\widetilde{W}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
is obtained by applying an
SU
-linear projection to an orientation

\widetilde{u}\in\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
of complex cobordism (Proposition
3.1
). A multiplication on
W
together with a complex orientation

define a formal group law
F_{W}\in W_{*}[[u,v]]
. This formal group law was studied by Buchstaber in
[
6
]
, where it was stated that the coefficients of
F_{W}
do not generate the ring
W_{*}
, unlike the situation with complex cobordism. We give a complete proof of this claim in Theorem
3.12
. It is shown that the coefficients of
F_{W}
do not generate

W_{*}
for any multiplication and complex orientation of
W
, not just for the standard ones defined by the Stong projection. We also prove another statement from
[
6
]
: after inverting

F_{W}
for some orientation

coincides with
W_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
(Theorem
3.13
).

It would be interesting to give a geometric construction for the multiplicative transformation (genus)
\mathit{MU}\to W
classifying the formal group law
F_{W}
, for example, in terms of the geometric generators of
W_{*}
and
\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
described in
[

. A related construction of polynomial generators of
\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
associated with the classifying maps for the Abel, Buchstaber and Krichever formal group laws was recently given by Bakuradze
[
3

(the Krichever formal group law gives rise to the Krichever–Hoehn complex elliptic genus).

The proof consists of three lemmata. First, there is the following useful result relating a morphism of spectra to its action on the homotopy groups.

Lemma 1.4

(

Let

Lemma

Given three spectra

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
E
,
F
and
G
be connective spectra of finite type such that
H_{*}(E)
,
H_{*}(F)
and

\pi_{*}(G)

Then the natural map

q\colon[E\wedge F,G]\to\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\pi_{*}(E)\otimes\pi_{*}(F),\pi_{*}(G))

Applying Lemma

to the spectra

\textstyle{\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\pi_{*}(E\wedge F),\pi_{*}(G))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

1.2.

\mathit{SU}

The following proposition is contained implicitly in Conner and Floyd

Proposition 1.7
.

There is an equivalence of
\mathit{MSU}
-modules

\mathit{MU}\simeq\mathit{MSU}\wedge\Sigma^{-2}\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}=\mathit{MU}(1)
, we have an
\mathit{MSU}
-module map

\mathit{MSU}\wedge\Sigma^{-2}\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}=\mathit{MSU}\wedge\Sigma^{-2}MU(1)\xrightarrow{1\wedge i}\mathit{MSU}\wedge\mathit{MU}\to\mathit{MU}.

It is a map of Thom spectra induced by the map of spaces

\mathit{BSU}\times\mathit{BU}(1)\to\mathit{BU}\times\mathit{BU}\xrightarrow{\oplus}\mathit{BU},

This composite map is a homotopy equivalence, with the homotopy inverse given by

\mathit{BU}\to\mathit{BSU}\times\mathit{BU}(1),\quad\eta\mapsto(\eta-\det\eta)\times\det\eta.

Hence, it induces an equivalence of the corresponding Thom spectra.
∎

Given
R
-module spectra
E,F
, denote by
[E,F]_{R}
the abelian group of
R
-linear morphisms. For a free
R
-module

, there is a natural isomorphism between
[R\wedge X,F]_{R}
and

X\xrightarrow{f}F
corresponds to an
R

R\wedge X\xrightarrow{1\wedge f}R\wedge F\to F
. Conversely, an
R
-linear morphism

corresponds to a morphism
X\simeq S\wedge X\xrightarrow{e\wedge 1}R\wedge X\xrightarrow{g}F
, where

is the unit of

.

We have
\mathit{MU}^{*}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})=\mathit{MU}^{*}[[u]]
, where
\mathit{MU}^{*}=\mathit{MU}^{*}(\mathit{pt})
is the cobordism ring of point,

Proposition 1.8
.

The abelian group

is the canonical orientation in complex cobordism, then the map

[\mathit{MU},\mathit{MU}]_{*}\to\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2-*}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}),\quad f\mapsto f(u),

becomes an isomorphism when restricted to the subgroup
[\mathit{MU},\mathit{MU}]_{\mathit{MSU},*}
of

Proof.

\Sigma^{-2}\mathit{MU}(1)\simeq S\wedge\Sigma^{-2}\mathit{MU}(1)\xrightarrow{e\wedge 1}\mathit{MSU}\wedge\Sigma^{-2}\mathit{MU}(1)\xrightarrow{1\wedge i}\mathit{MSU}\wedge\mathit{MU}\to\mathit{MU}\xrightarrow{f}\Sigma^{-k}MU

\mathit{MSU}
-module self-maps of

By Proposition

-linear operation

-linear operations acting on

is an arbitrary series with

The required operations were introduced by Novikov in
[

]
.

The stable Thom isomorphism

\varphi^{*}\colon\mathit{MU}^{n}(\mathit{BU})\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathit{MU}^{n}(\mathit{MU})=[\mathit{MU},\mathit{MU}]_{-n}

identifies universal characteristic classes with cohomological operations in complex cobordism.

Given two complex line bundles
\xi
,
\eta
with

,

, the first Conner–Floyd class of their tensor product is expressed as a power series in

with coefficients in
\mathit{MU}^{*}
, known as the
formal group law of geometric cobordisms

]
,
[

, or simply the

(1.1)

F_{U}(u,v)=c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\xi\otimes\eta)=u+v+\sum_{i\geqslant 1,\,j\geqslant 1}\alpha_{ij}u^{i}v^{j},\quad\alpha_{ij}\in\mathit{MU}^{-2(i+j-1)}.

We denote by
\overline{u}
the inverse series of

with respect to
F_{U}
; it satisfies
F_{U}(u,\overline{u})=0
.

Construction 1.9

(operations
\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}
)
.

Consider the universal characteristic class
d^{\scriptscriptstyle U}\in\mathit{MU}^{2}(BU)
given by
d^{\scriptscriptstyle U}(\xi)=c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\xi)
. We also set
\overline{d}\phantom{d}\!\!\!^{\scriptscriptstyle U}=c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\overline{\det\xi})
, which can be identified with the inverse series of
d^{\scriptscriptstyle U}
and satisfies
F_{U}(d^{\scriptscriptstyle U},\overline{d}\phantom{d}\!\!\!^{\scriptscriptstyle U})=0
.

Given non-negative integers
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
, define the operation

\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}=\varphi^{*}\bigl((\overline{d}\phantom{d}\!\!\!^{\scriptscriptstyle U})^{k_{1}}(d^{\scriptscriptstyle U})^{k_{2}}\bigr)\in[\mathit{MU},\mathit{MU}]_{-2(k_{1}+k_{2})}

Geometrically, the operation

\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}
takes a bordism class
[M]\in\mathit{MU}_{*}
to the class
[M_{k_{1},k_{2}}]
of a submanifold dual to
(\det\mathcal{T}M)^{\oplus k_{1}}\oplus(\overline{\det\mathcal{T}M})^{\oplus k_{2}}
.

The action of
\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}
on the canonical orientation

u\in\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
is given by

(1.2)

\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}u=u\,\overline{u}^{k_{1}}u^{k_{2}}

Proposition 1.10
.

The operation
\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}
is
\mathit{SU}
-linear.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.3
it is enough to check that
\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}
is
SU
-linear on the homotopy groups
\pi_{*}(\mathit{MU})=\mathit{MU}_{*}
. Take bordism classes
[M]\in\mathit{MU}_{m}
,
[N]\in\mathit{MSU}_{n}
. We need to prove that
\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}([N\times M])=[N]\cdot\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}([M])
.

By definition,

\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}([N\times M])=\varepsilon D_{U}\bigl(\bigl(c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\mathcal{T}(N\times M))\bigr)^{k_{1}}\bigl(c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\overline{\det\mathcal{T}(N\times M)})\bigr)^{k_{2}}\bigr),

where

is the Poincaré–Atiyah duality isomorphism and
\varepsilon\colon\mathit{MU}_{*}(N\times M)\to\mathit{MU}_{*}(\mathit{pt})
is the augmentation.
We have
\det\mathcal{T}(N\times M)=\det\mathcal{T}N\otimes\det\mathcal{T}M=\det\mathcal{T}M
, as

is an
\mathit{SU}
-manifold. Hence,

\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}([N\times M])=\varepsilon D_{U}\bigl(\bigl(c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\mathcal{T}(M))\bigr)^{k_{1}}\bigl(c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\overline{\det\mathcal{T}(M)})\bigr)^{k_{2}}\bigr).

Clearly, the submanifold dual to
\bigl(c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\mathcal{T}(M))\bigr)^{k_{1}}\bigl(c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\overline{\det\mathcal{T}(M)})\bigr)^{k_{2}}
in the product

is

, which implies the statement.
∎

We denote

\varDelta=\varDelta_{(1,1)},\quad\partial_{k}=\varDelta_{(k,0)},\quad\partial=\partial_{1},\quad\overline{\partial}_{k}=\varDelta_{(0,k)}.

Note that
\partial_{0}
is the identity operation. It follows from definition that each operation

\varDelta_{(k_{1},k_{2})}
can be expressed as a series in
\partial_{k}
(as well as in
\overline{\partial}_{k}

Lemma 1.11
.

Under the isomorphism of Proposition
1.8
, a power series
\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\lambda_{i}u^{i+1}\in\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2-2k}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
corresponds to the operation
\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\lambda_{i}\overline{\partial}_{i}
,
\lambda_{i}\in\mathit{MU}^{-2i-2k}
.

Proof.

Indeed, we have
\sum\lambda_{i}\overline{\partial}_{i}(u)=\sum\lambda_{i}u^{i+1}
by formula (

).
∎

Now we can formulate the main result of the section.

Theorem 1.12
.

Any
\mathit{SU}
-linear operation
f\in[\mathit{MU},\mathit{MU}]_{\mathit{MSU},*}
can be written uniquely as a series
f=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\mu_{i}\partial_{i}
, where
\mu_{i}\in\mathit{MU}^{-2i-*}
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
1.8
and Lemma

that any
\mathit{SU}
-linear operation

can be represented as a series

\sum\lambda_{i}\overline{\partial}_{i}
. Since
\overline{\partial}_{i}
is a series in

\partial_{i}
, we can also write

as a series
\sum\mu_{i}\partial_{i}
. The coefficients
\lambda_{i}
and
\mu_{i}
are determined uniquely by the action of

on the canonical orientation

. Namely,

∎

The product structure (with respect to the composition) of
\mathit{SU}
-linear operations
f=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\beta_{i}\partial_{i}
can be described via the coefficients of the formal group law in complex cobordism, as described next.

For an integer

, the
k

[k](u)
in the formal group law
F_{U}
is defined inductively:

[0](u)=0,\quad[k](u)=F\bigl([k-1](u),u\bigr)\text{ for }k>0,\quad[k](u)=F\bigl([k+1](u),\overline{u}\bigr)\text{ for }k<0.

For a complex line bundle
\xi
, we have
[k](c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\xi))=c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\xi^{\otimes k})
and

[-k](c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\xi))=c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\overline{\xi}^{\otimes k})
,
k\geqslant 0
.

We can now describe the product structure of
\mathit{SU}
-linear operations.

Theorem 1.14
.

Given non-negative integers
k,m
, consider the two power series

(F(u,v))^{k}=\sum_{i,j\geqslant 0}\alpha_{ij}^{(k)}u^{i}v^{j},\quad\bigl([1-m](u)\bigr)^{k}u^{m}=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\beta_{i}^{(k,m)}u^{i}.

Then

(1.3)

\partial_{k}(a\cdot b)=\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}^{(k)}\,\partial_{i}a\cdot\partial_{j}b,\quad\partial_{k}\partial_{m}=\sum_{i}\beta_{i}^{(k,m)}\partial_{i},

where
a,b\in[\mathit{MU},\mathit{MU}]_{*}
are arbitrary operations, and
a\cdot b\in[\mathit{MU}\wedge\mathit{MU},\mathit{MU}]_{*}
denotes the exterior product.

Proof.

By Lemmata
1.4
and
1.5
it is enough to verify the identities on the homotopy groups of the
\mathit{MU}
-spectrum, that is, on elements of

\mathit{MU}_{*}=\pi_{*}(\mathit{MU})
.

Let
a=[M],\,b=[N]\in\mathit{MU}_{*}
. We denote
u=c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\mathcal{T}M)
,
v=c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\mathcal{T}N)
. The first identity follows by calculation

\partial_{k}([M\times N])=\varepsilon D_{U}\bigl(\bigl(c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\mathcal{T}(M\times N))\bigr)^{k}\bigr)=\varepsilon D_{U}\bigl(\bigl(c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\mathcal{T}M\otimes\det\mathcal{T}N)\bigr)^{k}\bigr)\\
=\varepsilon D_{U}\bigl((F(u,v))^{k}\bigr)=\varepsilon D_{U}\Bigl(\sum_{i,j}\alpha^{(k)}_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}\Bigr)=\sum_{i,j}\alpha^{(k)}_{ij}\partial_{i}([M])\partial_{j}([N]),

where the last equality follows from the fact that the submanifold
\partial_{i}(M)\times\partial_{j}(N)
is dual to
u^{i}v^{j}
in the product
M\times N
.

For the second identity, let
\partial_{m}([M])=[N]
, where
i\colon N\hookrightarrow M
is an embedding. The normal bundle of
N
is isomorphic to
i^{*}(\det\mathcal{T}M)^{\oplus m}
, therefore,

\det\mathcal{T}N\otimes i^{*}(\det\mathcal{T}M)^{\otimes m}=\det\bigl(\mathcal{T}N\oplus i^{*}(\det\mathcal{T}M)^{\oplus m}\bigr)\cong i^{*}\det\mathcal{T}M.

Hence,
\det\mathcal{T}N=i^{*}(\overline{\det\mathcal{T}M})^{\otimes(m-1)}
. Denoting

u=c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\mathcal{T}M)
, we obtain
c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\mathcal{T}N)=i^{*}[1-m](u)
. On the other hand, the submanifold
N
is dual to
u^{m}
, that is,
i_{*}[N]=D_{U}(u^{m})=u^{m}\frown[M]
. Then the following calculation proves the second identity:

\partial_{k}[N]=\bigl\langle\bigl(c^{\scriptscriptstyle U}_{1}(\det\mathcal{T}N)\bigr)^{k},[N]\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle i^{*}\bigl([1-m](u)\bigr)^{k},[N]\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle\bigl([1-m](u)\bigr)^{k},i_{*}[N]\bigr\rangle\\
=\bigl\langle\bigl([1-m](u)\bigr)^{k},u^{m}\frown[M]\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle\bigl([1-m](u)\bigr)^{k}u^{m},[M]\bigr\rangle=\sum_{i}\beta_{i}^{(k,m)}\partial_{i}([M]).\qed

The second relation in (
1.3
) implies
\partial_{k}\partial=0
, as
\beta_{i}^{(k,1)}=0
. The identity

\partial_{k}\partial=0
follows also from the geometric definition of
\partial_{k}
.

Relations (
1.3
) can be used to express an arbitrary composition of operations
f=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\partial_{i}
as an operation of the same form.

Proposition 2.3

Complex orientations of
W
and formal group laws

This last section is motivated by the work
[
6
]
of Buchstaber. We start by an observation that the
c_{1}
-spherical bordism theory
W
is complex oriented for any multiplication (
2.2
). Furthermore, any complex orientation of
W
is obtained from a complex orientation of
\mathit{MU}
by applying an
\mathit{SU}
-linear projection
\pi
. A complex orientation
w
of
W
defines a formal group law

F_{W}(u,v)
in the theory
W
. Unlike the situation with complex bordism, the coefficients of the formal group law
F_{W}

do not
generate the coefficient ring
W_{*}
of the theory
W
, for any choice of
w
and
\pi
. This result is stated in
[
6
]
with a short sketch of proof (more details are provided in the case of the Stong projection
\pi_{0}
). We give a complete proof using the technique developed in the previous sections.

By Theorem
2.21
, an
\mathit{SU}
-bilinear multiplication on
W
is given by the formula

(3.1)

a*b=ab+\delta\partial a\partial b,

where
\delta=2[V]-\omega
,
\omega\in W_{4}
. As
\partial\delta=0
, we also have

(3.2)

\partial(a*b)=\partial(ab)=a\,\partial b+b\,\partial a-[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]\partial a\,\partial b.

In what follows we fix an
\mathit{SU}
-bilinear multiplication on
W
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The theory
W
is complex orientable. For any
\mathit{SU}
-linear projection
\pi\colon\mathit{MU}\to W
and a complex orientation
\widetilde{u}\in\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
, the element
\pi(\widetilde{u})\in\widetilde{W}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
is a complex orientation for
W
. Furthermore, for any complex orientation
w
of
W
and any
\mathit{SU}
-linear projection
\pi\colon\mathit{MU}\to W
, there exists a complex orientation
\widetilde{u}\in\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
such that
w=\pi(\widetilde{u})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
be the canonical orientation of complex cobordism. Then
\widetilde{u}|_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}=u|_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}
. Hence,
\pi(\widetilde{u})|_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}=\pi(u)|_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}=u|_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}
by Proposition
2.13
, as
\partial_{i}u|_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}=0
for
i\geqslant 1
. This implies that

\pi(\widetilde{u})
is a complex orientation of
W
.

Conversely, given an orientation
w\in\widetilde{W}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
, its image under the forgetful map
\widetilde{W}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})\to\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
is a complex orientation

\widetilde{u}
of
\mathit{MU}
. Therefore,
w=\pi(\widetilde{u})
for any
\mathit{SU}
-linear projection
\pi\colon\mathit{MU}\to W
.
∎

Remark
.

As one can see from the proof, the statement holds because a complex orientation of a cohomology theory is defined via the unit of the theory. The forgetful map
W\to\mathit{MU}
and projections
\mathit{MU}\to W
preserve the standard unit (from
\mathit{MSU}
) and therefore send orientations to orientations.

A complex orientation
w\in\widetilde{W}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
defines a formal group law with coefficients in
W^{*}
, which we denote by
F_{W}(u,v)
(it depends on both the multiplication and
w
, but we do not reflect this in the notation). For example, we may take
w=\pi_{0}(u)
, where
u\in\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
is the canonical orientation and
\pi_{0}
is the Stong projection.
The formal group law
F_{W}
is classified by a multiplicative transformation

\psi\colon\mathit{MU}\to W
sending
u
to
w
. However, even in the case

w=\pi_{0}(u)
, the transformation
\psi
does not coincide with the projection

\pi_{0}\colon\mathit{MU}\to W
, as the latter is
not
multiplicative.
To study the formal group law
F_{W}
we map
W
further to a one-parameter extension of the
U
-theory, as described next.

Construction 3.2
.

Following
[
6
]
, consider the multiplicative cohomology theory
\varGamma
defined by

\varGamma^{*}(X)=\mathit{MU}^{*}(X)[t]/(t^{2}=-[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]t+\delta)

for any CW-complex
X
. Additively,
\varGamma^{*}(X)
is a free
\mathit{MU}^{*}(X)
-module with basis
\{1,t\}
, and the multiplication is defined by the relation
t^{2}=-[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]t+\delta
.

There is a natural transformation
\varphi\colon W\to\varGamma
, given by
\varphi(x)=x+t\partial x
.

Proposition 3.3

(
[
6
, Lemma 2]
)
.

The transformation
\varphi\colon W\to\varGamma
is multiplicative.

Proof.

Given
a,b\in W_{*}
, we use (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) to calculate

(a+t\partial a)(b+t\partial b)=ab+t(a\partial b+b\partial a)+t^{2}\partial a\partial b=\\
=a*b+t\partial(a*b)+(t^{2}+t[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]-\delta)\partial a\partial b=a*b+t\partial(a*b).\qed

There is a canonical orientation of the theory
\varGamma
given by the image of the canonical orientation
u\in\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
under the natural inclusion
\mathit{MU}\hookrightarrow\varGamma
. This orientation of
\varGamma
will be also denoted by
u
.

The map
\varphi
sends the orientation
w
to an orientation
\varphi(w)
of the theory
\varGamma
. Hence,
\varphi(w)
can be written as a power series
\gamma(u)
in
u
with coefficients in
\varGamma^{*}=\varGamma^{*}(pt)
.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
6
, Lemma 3]
)
.

We have

\varphi_{*}F_{W}(u,v)=\gamma F_{U}(\gamma^{-1}(u),\gamma^{-1}(v)),

where
F_{U}(u,v)
is the formal group law in complex cobordism, considered as a formal group law over

\varGamma^{*}
via the natural inclusion
\mathit{MU}\hookrightarrow\varGamma
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is multiplicative, the formal group law corresponding to the orientation
\varphi(w)
is

\varphi_{*}F_{W}
. Similarly, since the inclusion
\mathit{MU}\hookrightarrow\varGamma
is also multiplicative, the formal group law corresponding to the orientation
u
is
F_{U}
, regarded as a formal group law
over
\varGamma^{*}
. Now
\varphi(w)=\gamma(u)
implies the required identity.
∎

Construction 3.5
.

Denote by
J=\mathit{MU}^{<0}\subset\mathit{MU}^{*}
the ideal of elements of non-zero degree. Then
J^{2}
is the ideal of decomposables in
\mathit{MU}^{*}
. Clearly,
J^{2}+tJ
is an ideal in
\varGamma^{*}
.

Consider the quotient ring
R=\varGamma^{*}/(J^{2}+tJ)
. As a graded abelian group,

R=(\mathit{MU}^{*}\!/J^{2})\oplus\mathbb{Z}\langle t\rangle
,
\deg t=-2
. The multiplication in
R
is defined by
ab=0
,
at=0
for
a,b\in J/J^{2}
and
t^{2}=\delta
, so that
t^{3}=0
. This implies
R^{<-2}R^{<0}=0
.

We write

F_{W}(u,v)=u+v+\sum_{i\geqslant 1,\,j\geqslant 1}\omega_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}.

In order to compare the subring generated by the coefficients
\omega_{ij}
with the whole ring

W^{*}
we need to identify the characteristic
s_{k}
-numbers of
\omega_{ij}
. (Recall that
s_{k}
is the Chern characteristic number corresponding to the symmetric polynomial
t_{1}^{k}+\cdots+t_{n}^{k}
in Chern roots; it vanishes on decomposables
J^{2}\subset\mathit{MU}^{*}
.)

We shall calculate the formal group law
\varphi_{*}F_{W}(u,v)=u+v+\sum(\omega_{ij}+t\partial\omega_{ij})u^{i}v^{j}
over the ring
R
(that is, reducing the coefficents mod
J^{2}+tJ
), using the formula from Proposition
3.4
. As the
s_{k}
-numbers vanish on
J^{2}
, in this way we obtain information about the
s_{k}
-numbers of the coefficients of the formal group law
F_{W}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

The following identity holds in
\varGamma^{*}
:

\gamma(u)=u-(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)u^{2}+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\gamma_{i+1}u^{i+1}\mod J^{2}+tJ,

where
\lambda\in\mathit{MU}^{-2}=W^{-2}
,
2\ell=\partial\lambda
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

\gamma_{i+1}=(-1)^{i}\alpha_{1i}+\omega_{i}
,
\omega_{i}\in W^{-2i}
.
Furthermore, any
\lambda
and
\omega_{i}
can be obtained from some orientation

w\in\widetilde{W}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
, every complex orientation
w
has the form
\pi_{0}(\widetilde{u})
for some orientation
\widetilde{u}
in
\mathit{MU}
. Writing
\widetilde{u}\in\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
as a power series
f(u)
in the standard orientation
u
and observing that
u^{i+1}=\overline{\partial}_{i}u
, we obtain

(3.3)

\widetilde{u}=f(u)=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 1}\lambda_{i}u^{i+1}=\bigl(1+\sum_{i\geqslant 1}\lambda_{i}\overline{\partial}_{i}\bigr)u=(1+\lambda\partial+g\varDelta)u.

In the last identity we used Theorem
1.12
and Lemma
2.15
to write the
\mathit{SU}
-linear operation
f=1+\sum_{i\geqslant 1}\lambda_{i}\overline{\partial}_{i}
as
1+\lambda\partial+g\varDelta
, for a certain
\mathit{SU}
-linear operation
g
. Note that any

\lambda
and
g
can be obtained from an orientation
\widetilde{u}
.

Now we calculate:

(3.4)

\gamma(u)=\varphi(w)=w+t\partial w=\pi_{0}(\widetilde{u})+t\partial\pi_{0}(\widetilde{u})=\pi_{0}f(u)+t\partial f(u)\\
=(\pi_{0}+t\partial)(1+\lambda\partial+g\varDelta)u=\pi_{0}(u)+\lambda\partial u+\pi_{0}g\varDelta u+(2\ell+1)t\partial u+t\partial g\varDelta u\\
=\pi_{0}(u)+(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)\partial u+\pi_{0}g\varDelta u+t\partial g\varDelta u,

where we have used the identity
\partial\pi_{0}=\partial
from Proposition
2.9
, and the identities

\pi_{0}(\lambda\partial)=\pi_{0}(\lambda)\partial=\lambda\partial
,
t\partial(\lambda\partial)=t(\partial\lambda)\partial=2\ell t\partial
, which are valid because both
\pi_{0}
and
\partial
are
\mathit{SU}
-linear.

Next we consider each of the four summands on the right hand side of (
3.4
) separately.

Note that
\partial_{i}u=u\overline{u}^{i}=(-1)^{i}u^{i+1}\mod J
. Using the formula from Proposition
2.9
we obtain

(3.5)

\pi_{0}(u)=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\alpha_{1i}\partial_{i}u=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}(-1)^{i}\alpha_{1i}u^{i+1}\mod J^{2}.

Similarly,

(3.6)

(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)\partial u=-(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)u^{2}\mod J^{2}+tJ.

By Lemma
2.15
, we have
g\varDelta(u)=\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\mu_{i}u^{i+1}
.
It follows that
g\varDelta(u)
is an element of

\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
with the property
g\varDelta(u)|_{\mathbb{C}P^{2}}=0
. Furthermore, by Lemma
2.15
any

v\in\widetilde{\mathit{MU}}\vphantom{MU}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
with

v|_{\mathbb{C}P^{2}}=0
has the form
g\varDelta(u)
for some
\mathit{SU}
-linear operation
g
. Applying the projection
\pi_{0}
we obtain the element

\pi_{0}g\varDelta(u)\in\widetilde{W}^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})
with the same property. Therefore,

\pi_{0}g\varDelta(u)
can be written as a power series in
w
without quadratic part with respect to the product
*
:

\pi_{0}g\varDelta(u)=\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\omega_{i}*w^{*(i+1)},

where
\omega_{i}\in W^{-2i}
can be arbitrary.
By (
3.1
),
\omega_{i}*w=\omega_{i}w+\delta\partial\omega_{i}\partial w=\omega_{i}w\mod J^{2}
, where the last identity holds because
\delta
and
\partial\omega_{i}
are in
J
for
i\geqslant 2
. Furthermore,

w=\pi_{0}(\widetilde{u})=\pi_{0}(u)+\lambda\partial(u)+\pi_{0}g\varDelta(u)=u\mod J.

It follows that

(3.7)

\pi_{0}g\varDelta(u)=\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\omega_{i}u^{i+1}\mod J^{2}.

Finally, by Lemma
2.15
,
\partial g\varDelta=\sum\limits_{i\geqslant 2}\lambda_{i}\partial_{i}
, where
\lambda_{i}\in\mathit{MU}^{2-2i}\subset J
. It follows that
\partial g\varDelta(u)=0\mod J
and
t\partial g\varDelta(u)=0\mod tJ
. Substituting this together with (
3.5
), (
3.6
), (
3.7
) in (
3.4
) we obtain the required identity
\mod J^{2}+tJ
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
3.6
implies the following condition on the coefficients of the series expansion of
\mathit{SU}
-linear projections
\pi
in Proposition
2.13
:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\pi=1+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\lambda_{i}\partial_{i}
be an
\mathit{SU}
-linear projection
\mathit{MU}\to W
. Then
\lambda_{i}=\alpha_{1i}+\omega_{i}
modulo decomposables in
\mathit{MU}^{*}
, where
\omega_{i}
can be an arbitrary element of
W^{-2i}
.

Proof.

We have

\pi(u)=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\lambda_{i}\partial_{i}u=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\lambda_{i}u\overline{u}^{i}=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}(-1)^{i}\lambda_{i}u^{i+1}\mod J^{2}.

On the other hand, using Theorem
2.14
, (
2.1
) and (
3.7
) we obtain

\pi(u)=\pi_{0}(u)+\pi_{0}f\varDelta(u)=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\alpha_{1i}\partial_{i}u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\omega_{i}u^{i+1}=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}((-1)^{i}\alpha_{1i}+\omega_{i})u^{i+1}\mod J^{2}.

Comparing the coefficients in the above two expressions for
\pi(u)
we obtain the result.
∎

Now we return to the formal group law
\varphi_{*}F_{W}(u,v)=u+v+\sum(\omega_{ij}+t\partial\omega_{ij})u^{i}v^{j}
of the theory
\varGamma
.

Lemma 3.8
.

In the notation of Lemma
3.6
,

\varphi_{*}F_{W}(u,v)=u+v-2\bigl(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t\bigr)uv-2\delta(2\ell+1)^{2}(uv^{2}+vu^{2})+\\
+\sum_{i\geqslant 1,\,j\geqslant 1}\alpha_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}+\sum_{i\geqslant 3}\gamma_{i}\bigl((u+v)^{i}-u^{i}-v^{i}\bigr)\mod J^{2}+tJ.

Proof.

We have
\varphi_{*}F_{W}(u,v)=\gamma F_{U}(\gamma^{-1}(u),\gamma^{-1}(v))
by Proposition
3.4
. Furthermore,

\gamma(u)=u-(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)u^{2}+\sum_{i\geqslant 3}\gamma_{i}u^{i}\mod J^{2}+tJ

by Lemma
3.6
. The identity

(F_{U}(x,y))^{i}=(x+y)^{i}\mod J
implies

(3.8)

\gamma(F_{U}(x,y))=F_{U}(x,y)-\bigl(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t\bigr)(x+y)^{2}+\sum\limits_{i\geqslant 3}\gamma_{i}(x+y)^{i}\mod J^{2}+tJ.

Next we need to calculate
x=\gamma^{-1}(u)
. We denote

\gamma^{-1}(u)=u+\sum_{j\geqslant 2}\varepsilon_{j}u^{j},\quad\gamma(u)=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\gamma_{i}u^{i},\text{ where }\gamma_{2}=-\lambda-(2\ell+1)t.

All subsequent calculations will be carried out in the ring
R=\varGamma^{*}/(J^{2}+tJ)
, that is,

\mod J^{2}+tJ
, see Construction
3.2
. We have
\varepsilon_{j}\in R^{2-2j}
and
R^{<0}R^{<-2}=0
, which implies

\varepsilon_{2}(u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\gamma_{i}u^{i})=\varepsilon_{2}(u-(2\ell+1)tu^{2})
and

\varepsilon_{j}(u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\gamma_{i}u^{i})=\varepsilon_{j}u
for
j\geqslant 3
. Hence,

u=\gamma^{-1}(\gamma(u))=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\gamma_{i}u^{i}+\sum_{j\geqslant 2}\varepsilon_{j}(u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\gamma_{i}u^{i})^{j}\\
=u+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\gamma_{i}u^{i}+\varepsilon_{2}(u-(2\ell+1)tu^{2})^{2}+\sum_{j\geqslant 3}\varepsilon_{j}u^{j}.

Comparing the coefficients of
u^{j}
we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{2}=\lambda+(2\ell+1)t,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{3}

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon_{2}(2\ell+1)t-\gamma_{3}=2(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)(2\ell+1)t-\gamma_{3}=2(2\ell+1)^{2}\delta-\gamma_{3},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{j}\quad\text{for }j\geqslant 4.

Therefore,

\gamma^{-1}(u)=u+\bigl(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t\bigr)u^{2}+\bigl(2(2\ell+1)^{2}\delta-\gamma_{3}\bigr)u^{3}-\sum_{j\geqslant 4}\gamma_{j}u^{j}.

It remains to substitute
x=\gamma^{-1}(u)
and
y=\gamma^{-1}(v)
in (
3.8
).
We have
F_{U}(x,y)=x+y+\sum\alpha_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}=x+y+\sum\alpha_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}
in
R
, and similarly

\sum_{i\geqslant 3}\gamma_{i}(x+y)^{i}=\sum_{i\geqslant 3}\gamma_{i}(u+v)^{i}
. For the remaining summand of (

), we calculate

\bigl(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t\bigr)(x+y)^{2}=\bigl(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t\bigr)\bigl(u+v+(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)(u^{2}+v^{2})\bigr)^{2}\\
=\bigl(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t\bigr)\bigr((u+v)^{2}+2(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)(u+v)(u^{2}+v^{2})\bigr)\\
=(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)(u+v)^{2}+2(2\ell+1)^{2}\delta(u+v)(u^{2}+v^{2}).

Substituting these expressions in (
3.8
) we obtain

\gamma(F_{U}(\gamma^{-1}(u),\gamma^{-1}(v)))=\gamma^{-1}(u)+\gamma^{-1}(v)+\sum\alpha_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}+\sum\limits_{i\geqslant 3}\gamma_{i}(u+v)^{i}\\
-(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)(u+v)^{2}-2(2\ell+1)^{2}\delta(u+v)(u^{2}+v^{2})=u+(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)u^{2}\\
+(2(2\ell+1)^{2}\delta-\gamma_{3})u^{3}-\sum\limits_{i\geqslant 4}\gamma_{i}u^{i}+v+(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)v^{2}+(2(2\ell+1)^{2}\delta-\gamma_{3})v^{3}-\sum\limits_{i\geqslant 4}\gamma_{i}v^{i}\\
+\sum\alpha_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}+\sum\limits_{i\geqslant 3}\gamma_{i}(u+v)^{i}-(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)(u+v)^{2}-2(2\ell+1)^{2}\delta(u^{3}+uv^{2}+vu^{2}+v^{3})\\
=u+v-2(\lambda+(2\ell+1)t)uv-2\delta(2\ell+1)^{2}(uv^{2}+vu^{2})+\sum\alpha_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}+\sum\limits_{i\geqslant 3}\gamma_{i}((u+v)^{i}-u^{i}-v^{i}),

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

The coefficients of the formal group law
F_{W}(u,v)=u+v+\sum\omega_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}

satisfy

(3.9)

\sum\limits_{i+j=k+1}\omega_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}=\sum\limits_{i+j=k+1}\alpha_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}+\gamma_{k+1}\bigl((u+v)^{k+1}-u^{k+1}-v^{k+1}\bigr)\mod J^{2}

for
k\geqslant 3
.

Proof.

We have
t\partial\omega_{ij}=0\mod tJ
for
i+j>2
, which implies

\varphi_{*}F_{W}(u,v)=u+v+(\omega_{11}+t\partial\omega_{11})uv+\sum_{i+j>2}\omega_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}\mod J^{2}+tJ
. Now the required identity follows from the identity of Lemma
3.8
.
∎

For an integer
k\geqslant 1
let

m_{k}=\gcd\biggl\{{k+1\choose i},\,1\leqslant i\leqslant k\biggr\}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $k+1\neq p^{\ell}$ for any prime $p$,}\\
p&\text{if $k+1=p^{\ell}$ for some prime $p$ and integer $\ell>0$.}\end{cases}

Theorem 3.10

(see
[
17
, Chapter X]
or
[
11
, Theorem 6.10]
)
.

With respect to the multiplication defined by the Stong projection
\pi_{0}
, the ring
W_{*}
is polynomial on generators in every positive even degree
except
4
:

W_{*}\cong\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{k}\colon k\geqslant 3],\quad x_{1}=[\mathbb{C}P^{1}],\quad x_{k}\in W_{2k}.

The polynomial generators
x_{k}
are specified by the condition
s_{k}(x_{k})=\pm m_{k}m_{k-1}
for
k\geqslant 3
.

Lemma 3.11

(
[
6
]
)
.

For the coefficients of the formal group law
F_{W}(u,v)
, we have

\gcd\bigl\{s_{i+j-1}(\omega_{ij})\colon i+j=k+1\bigr\}=m_{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)+c_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1}\bigr)

for
k\geqslant 3
, where
c_{k}
can be an arbitrary integer depending on the orientation
w
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathit{MU}_{*}\cong\mathbb{Z}[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
,
a_{k}\in\mathit{MU}_{2k}
and
s_{k}(a_{k})=m_{k}
.

There is the following formula for the coefficients of the formal group law
F_{U}
modulo decomposables:

\sum\limits_{i+j=k+1}\alpha_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}=-a_{k}\frac{(u+v)^{k+1}-u^{k+1}-v^{k+1}}{m_{k}}\mod J^{2},

see, e. g.,
[
1
]
. Hence,
\alpha_{ij}=-\frac{{i+j\choose i}}{m_{i+j-1}}a_{i+j-1}\mod J^{2}
and, in particular,
\alpha_{1j}=-\frac{j+1}{m_{j}}a_{j}\mod J^{2}
.
This implies that
\gamma_{k+1}=(-1)^{k}\alpha_{1k}+\omega_{k}=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{k+1}{m_{k}}a_{k}+\omega_{k}\mod J^{2}
. Substituting this in (
3.9
) we obtain

\sum\limits_{i+j=k+1}\omega_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}=-\bigl(a_{k}+(-1)^{k}(k+1)a_{k}-m_{k}\omega_{k}\bigr)\frac{(u+v)^{k+1}-u^{k+1}-v^{k+1}}{m_{k}}\mod J^{2}.

It follows that

\gcd\bigl\{s_{i+j-1}(\omega_{ij})\colon i+j=k+1\bigr\}\\
=s_{k}\bigl(a_{k}+(-1)^{k}(k+1)a_{k}-m_{k}\omega_{k}\bigr)=m_{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)-s_{k}(\omega_{k})\bigr).

Formula (
3.1
) implies that if an element
x\in W_{2i}
,
i\geqslant 3
, is decomposable in
W_{*}
(with respect to an arbitrary multiplication), then its forgetful image in
\mathit{MU}_{*}
is also decomposable. Hence,
\omega_{k}=c_{k}x_{k}\mod J^{2}
for an integer
c_{k}
. Therefore,
s_{k}(\omega_{k})=c_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1}
, and the result follows.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

For any complex orientation of
W
, the coefficients of the corresponding formal group law
F_{W}
do not generate the ring
W_{*}
.

Proof.

Consider a polynomial generator
x_{k}
from Theorem
3.10
for
k\geqslant 3
. Suppose
x_{k}
lies in the ring generated by the coefficients of
F_{W}
. Since an element decomposable in
W_{*}
is also decomposable in
\mathit{MU}_{*}
(in dimensions
\geqslant 6
), we get

s_{k}(x_{k})=\pm\gcd\bigl\{s_{i+j-1}(\omega_{ij})\colon i+j=k+1\bigr\}=\pm m_{k}(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)+c_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1}).

On the other hand, by Theorem
3.10
we have
s_{k}(x_{k})=\pm m_{k}m_{k-1}
. We show that there is
k\geqslant 3
such that the two numbers do not agree even up to a sign.

Indeed, recall that
m_{k}=p
if
k+1=p^{s}
for some prime
p
, and
m_{k}=1
otherwise. Therefore, if
k=2^{\ell}
and, in addition,
k+1=p^{s}
for an odd prime
p
, then
m_{k}m_{k-1}=2p
, whereas
m_{k}(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)+c_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1})=2p+p(2^{\ell}+2c_{k}p)=2p(1+2^{\ell-1}+c_{k}p)
. Suppose
\pm 2p=2p(1+2^{\ell-1}+c_{k}p)
or, equivalently,
1+2^{\ell-1}+c_{k}p=\pm 1
. Since
p
is odd,
2^{\ell-1}+c_{k}p\neq 0
for any
c_{k}
. So,
1+2^{\ell-1}+c_{k}p\neq 1
. If
1+2^{\ell-1}+c_{k}p=-1
, then
-2c_{k}p=4+2^{\ell}=3+p^{s}
. This is impossible for
p>3
. As a result, we obtain a contradiction in dimensions of the form
k=2^{\ell}=p^{s}-1
for
\ell>1
.
∎

We can also prove the following result stated in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
A
be the subring of
W_{*}
generated by the coefficients of the formal group law
F_{W}
. Then there is an orientation of
W
such that
A[\frac{1}{2}]=W_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
.

Remark
.

The proof given below would be simpler if we knew that

W_{*}
is a polynomial ring
for arbitrary
\mathit{SU}
-linear multiplication
on
W
. However, the description of Theorem
3.10
is valid only for the multiplication defined by the Stong projection.

The proof is based on three lemmata. The first lemma says that
the case
k=2^{\ell}=p^{s}-1
considered in the proof of Theorem
3.12
is the only case when the gcd of the
s
-numbers of the coefficients of the formal group law
F_{W}
does not agree
with
m_{k}m_{k-1}
:

Lemma 3.14
.

If
k
is not of the form
k=2^{\ell}=p^{s}-1
for some odd prime
p
, then
\gcd\bigl\{s_{i+j-1}(\omega_{ij})\colon i+j=k+1\bigr\}=m_{k}m_{k-1}
for some value of
c_{k}
(see Lemma
3.11
).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.11
, we need to find
c_{k}
such that
1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)+c_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1}=m_{k-1}
.

If
m_{k-1}=1
, then we set
c_{k}=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{k+1}{m_{k}}
, which is an integer as
m_{k}
always divides
k+1
.

If
m_{k-1}=2
, then
k=2^{\ell}
. By assumption,
k\neq p^{s}-1
, so
m_{k}=1
. The required identity becomes
1+(2^{\ell}+1)+2c_{k}=2
, which is satisfied for
c_{k}=-2^{\ell-1}
.

If
m_{k-1}=p
is an odd prime, then
k=p^{s}
. Hence,
m_{k}=1
or
2
. The required identity becomes
1-(p^{s}+1)+pc_{k}m_{k}=p
, which is satisfied for

c_{k}=\frac{p^{s-1}+1}{m_{k}}
. The latter is an integer as
p^{s-1}+1
is even.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

If
p^{s}=2^{\ell}+1
for odd prime
p
and positive integers
\ell,s
, then either
s=1
and
\ell=2^{n}
(so
p
is a Fermat prime), or
p=3
,
s=2
and
\ell=3
.

Proof.

Case 1:

p=3
.

There are obvious solutions
s=1,\ell=1
and
s=2,\ell=3
.

Now suppose
s>2
, so that
\ell>3
. Then
3^{s}=2^{\ell}+1\equiv 0\pmod{9}
.
It is easy to check that
2^{\ell}\equiv-1\pmod{9}
if and only if
\ell=6m+3
.
Then
3^{s}=2^{\ell}+1=(2^{2m+1})^{3}+1=(2^{2m+1}+1)(2^{4m+2}-2^{2m+1}+1)
. Hence,
2^{4m+2}-2^{2m+1}+1=3^{s^{\prime}}
and
2^{2m+1}+1=3^{s^{\prime\prime}}
. Now
\ell>3
implies
m>0
and therefore
s^{\prime}>1
. Hence,
2^{4m+2}-2^{2m+1}+1\equiv 0\pmod{9}
. Similarly,
s^{\prime\prime}>1
, hence,
2^{2m+1}+1=3^{s^{\prime\prime}}\equiv 0\pmod{9}
. The latter implies
2^{4m+2}-2^{2m+1}+1\equiv 1+1+1=3\not\equiv 0\pmod{9}
. A contradiction.

Case 2:

p>3
.

Reducing the identity
p^{s}=2^{\ell}+1
modulo 3 we obtain
(\pm 1)^{s}\equiv(-1)^{\ell}+1\pmod{3}
. This implies that
s
is odd and
\ell
is even.

Write
p-1=a2^{q}
with odd
a
. Then

2^{\ell}+1=(a2^{q}+1)^{s}=a^{s}2^{qs}+\cdots+sa2^{q}+1
. Suppose
s>1
. Then

\ell>q
and
as+2^{q}(a^{s}2^{q(s-2)}+\cdots)=2^{\ell-q}
is even. This is a contradiction because
as
is odd. Hence,
s=1
and
p=2^{\ell}+1
.

Write
\ell=r2^{n}
with odd
r
. Then
p=2^{\ell}+1=(2^{2^{n}}+1)(2^{(r-1)2^{n}}-\cdots+1)
. Since
p
is prime, we obtain
p=2^{2^{n}}+1
and
\ell=2^{n}
.
∎

Lemma 3.16
.

If
[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]\in A
and there are elements
x_{k}\in A_{2k}
,
k\geqslant 2
, such that
s_{k}(x_{k})=m_{k}m_{k-1}
up to a power of
2
, then
A[\frac{1}{2}]=W_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
.

Proof.

There is a polynomial subring
\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]\subset W_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
. This follows from the fact that any
\mathit{SU}
-linear multiplication (
3.1
) induces the standard multiplication on
\mathit{MSU}_{*}
, because
\mathit{MSU}_{*}\subset\mathop{\rm Ker}\partial
. Furthermore,
\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
coincides with
\mathop{\rm Ker}\partial=\mathop{\rm Im}\partial
on
W_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
(see
[
17
, Chapter X]
or
[
11
, Theorem 5.11]
). From (
3.2
) we obtain the identity
x=\frac{1}{2}(\partial([\mathbb{C}P^{1}]x)+[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]\partial x)
for any
x\in W_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
, which implies that
W_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
is a free
\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
-module with basis
\{1,[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]\}
. Since
[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]\in A
by assumption, we need to show that
\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]\subset A[\frac{1}{2}]
.

By the theorem of Novikov
[
13
]
,
\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]\cong\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2}][y_{2},y_{3},\ldots]
,
\dim y_{k}=2k
. The polynomial generators
y_{k}
are specified by the condition
s_{k}(y_{k})=\pm m_{k}m_{k-1}
up to a power of
2
(see
[
17
, Chapter X]
).

Let
x_{1}=[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]\in A
. We can assume by induction that
\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]\subset A[\frac{1}{2}]
in dimensions less than
2k
. For
x_{k}\in A
, let
\widetilde{y}_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\partial(x_{1}x_{k})=x_{k}-\frac{1}{2}x_{1}\partial x_{k}
. We have
\widetilde{y}_{k}\in\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
. By the induction hypothesis,
\partial x_{k}\in A[\frac{1}{2}]
, so
\widetilde{y}_{k}\in A[\frac{1}{2}]
. Since
x_{1}\partial x_{k}
is decomposable,
s_{k}(\widetilde{y}_{k})=s_{k}(x_{k})=m_{k}m_{k-1}
up to a power of 2. It follows that
\widetilde{y}_{k}
is a polynomial generator of
\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]
. Therefore, we obtain
\mathit{MSU}_{*}[\frac{1}{2}]\subset A[\frac{1}{2}]
by induction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.13
.

By Lemma
3.16
, we need to specify an orientation of
W
and elements
x_{k}\in A_{2k}
,
k\geqslant 2
, such that
s_{k}(x_{k})=m_{k}m_{k-1}
modulo a power of
2
.

The formula from Lemma
3.8
implies
\omega_{11}=\alpha_{11}-2\lambda=-[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]-2\lambda
. Choosing an orientation of
W
with
\lambda=0
we get

x_{1}=[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]\in A
.

Next we need to find
x_{2}\in A
with
s_{2}(x_{2})=m_{2}m_{1}=3
up to a power of
2
.

The multiplication on
W_{*}
is given by
a*b=ab+\delta\partial a\partial b
,
\delta=2[V]+\omega
,
\omega\in W_{4}
,
[V]=[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]^{2}-[\mathbb{C}P^{2}]
. We have
W_{4}=\mathbb{Z}\langle 9[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]^{2}-8[\mathbb{C}P^{2}]\rangle
, so
s_{2}(\omega)=24\alpha
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence,
s_{2}(\delta)=-6+24\alpha
.

The formula from Lemma
3.8
implies

\omega_{12}=-2\delta(2\ell+1)^{2}+\alpha_{12}+3\gamma_{3}\mod J^{2}.

Substituting here
2\ell=\partial\lambda=0
and
\gamma_{3}=\omega_{2}+\alpha_{12}
(see Lemma
3.6
), we obtain

\omega_{12}=4\alpha_{12}+3\omega_{2}-2\delta\mod J^{2},

where
\omega_{2}\in W_{4}
can be chosen arbitrarily depending on the orientation of
W
. Since
s_{2}(\alpha_{12})=3
and
s_{2}(\omega_{2})=24\beta
with
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain
s_{2}(\omega_{12})=24-48\alpha+72\beta
.

Case 1:

\alpha=3n
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\beta=2n
. Take
x_{2}=\omega_{12}\in A
. Then
s_{2}(x_{2})=24=3\cdot 2^{3}
.

Case 2:

\alpha=3n+\varepsilon
,

,
\varepsilon=1
or
2
. Let
\beta=-2n

x_{2}=\omega_{12}+x_{1}*x_{1}\in A

x_{1}*x_{1}=(x_{1})^{2}+4\delta

s_{2}(x_{2})=s_{2}(\omega_{12})+4s_{2}(\delta)=24(3\beta+2\alpha)=3\cdot\varepsilon 2^{4}
, which is equal to
3
up to a power of
2
.

It remains to choose
x_{k}
for

. By Lemma
3.14
, there is an integral linear combination
x_{k}
of the coefficients
\omega_{ij}
,

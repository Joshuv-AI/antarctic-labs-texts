Stability of the cone-volume measure with near constant density
Yingxiang Hu, Mohammad N. Ivaki

Abstract
. We prove that if the density of the cone-volume measure of a smooth, strictly convex body with respect to the spherical Lebesgue measure is nearly constant, then a homothetic copy of the body is close to the unit ball in the L 2 L^{2} -distance.



1.
Introduction

The following theorem in the origin-symmetric case was proved by Firey
[
Fir74
]
, and without the origin-symmetry assumption is due to Gage for
n=1

[
Gag84
]
, Andrews for
n=2

[
And99
]
, and Choi-Daskalopoulos for
n\geq 3

[
CD16
,
BCD17
]
.

Theorem
.

Let
K
be a smooth, strictly convex body with the support function
h
and Gauss curvature
\mathcal{K}
.
If
\mathcal{K}=h
, then
K
is the unit ball.

We prove a stability version of this theorem here. Write
B
for the unit ball of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Throughout this paper,
\int
refers to integration on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a smooth, strictly convex body with the support function
h>0
.
Then

(1.1)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}\left(\bar{K},B\right)\leq\gamma\left(\frac{\max\frac{h}{\mathcal{K}}}{\min\frac{h}{\mathcal{K}}}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\gamma
depends only on
n
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\bar{K}=\frac{K-c(K)}{\int hd\theta/\int d\theta},\quad c(K)=\frac{\int DhdV}{\int dV},

and
\frac{1}{n+1}V
is the cone-volume measure of
K
.

Note that
1.1
does not require any assumption of smallness for the quantity

\frac{\max\frac{h}{\mathcal{K}}}{\min\frac{h}{\mathcal{K}}}-1.

When
K
is assumed to be origin-symmetric, a similar stability result was proved in
[
Iva22
]
. See also
[
BD2
]
for a related stability result.

To prove
1.1
, we employ an inequality from
[
IM23a
]
, where a short proof of uniqueness for
\mathcal{K}=h
was given. However, the final steps of that proof, relying on the Poincaré inequality on the sphere, are not suitable for our purpose. The refined approach presented here has the added advantage of also allowing us to establish the uniqueness of solutions to
\mathcal{K}=h^{1-p}
for the whole range
-n-1<p\leq 0
. The interval
p\in(-1,0)
was previously absent in the argument of
[
IM23a
]
; see
section 4
.

A quick corollary of
1.1
is the uniqueness of solutions to the regular logarithmic Minkowski problem without a symmetry condition, provided the prescribed data is sufficiently close to
1
in the
C^{\alpha}
-norm.

Corollary 1.2
.

[
CFL22
,
BS23
]
Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
n\geq 2
and
f\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
depending only on
n,\,\alpha
, such that if
\|f-1\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, then the log-Minkowski problem
h/\mathcal{K}=f
has a unique, positive, strictly convex solution.

This corollary was recently proved by Chen, Feng, and Liu
[
CFL22
]
for
n=2
, and by Böröczky and Saroglou for the case
n\geq 2
and
h^{1-p}/\mathcal{K}=f
for
0\leq p<1
in
[
BS23
]
. We refer the reader to
[
Bor24
,
IM23b
,
Mil23
,
KM22
]
and the references therein for the importance of uniqueness results on the log-Minkowski problem.

2.
Background

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\delta:=\langle\,,\rangle,D)
denote the Euclidean space with its standard inner product and flat connection, and let
(\mathbb{S}^{n},\bar{g},\bar{\nabla})
denote the unit sphere equipped with its standard round metric and Levi-Civita connection. Moreover, for
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
, we define
\bar{\Delta}f=\operatorname{div}_{\bar{g}}(\bar{\nabla}f)
.

A compact, convex set with non-empty interior is called a convex body. Let
L
be a convex body. The support function of
L
is defined as

\displaystyle h_{L}(x):=\max\{\langle x,y\rangle:\penalty\ y\in L\},\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

The
L^{2}
-distance of two convex bodies
L_{1},L_{2}
is defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}(L_{1},L_{2}):=\left(\frac{1}{\int d\theta}\int|h_{L_{1}}-h_{L_{2}}|^{2}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and their Hausdorff distance is defined as

(2.2)

\displaystyle\delta_{H}(L_{1},L_{2}):=\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|h_{L_{1}}-h_{L_{2}}|.

Let
K
be a smooth, strictly convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with the origin in its interior. Write
\mathcal{M}=\partial K
for the boundary of
K
. The Gauss map of
\mathcal{M}
, denoted by
\nu_{K}
, takes the point
p\in\mathcal{M}
to its unique unit outward normal
x=\nu_{K}(p)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
The inverse Gauss map
X=\nu_{K}^{-1}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is given by

\displaystyle X(x)=Dh_{K}(x)=\bar{\nabla}h_{K}(x)+h_{K}(x)x,\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

The support function of
K
can also be expressed as

\displaystyle h_{K}(x)=\langle X(x),x\rangle=\langle\nu_{K}^{-1}(x),x\rangle,\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

The Gauss curvature of
K
(or
\mathcal{M}
) viewed as a function on the unit sphere is defined by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}_{K}(x)}:=\left.\frac{\det(\bar{\nabla}^{2}h_{K}+\bar{g}h_{K})}{\det(\bar{g})}\right|_{x},\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Moreover, define the measure
dV_{K}=(h_{K}/\mathcal{K}_{K})d\theta
, where
\theta
is the spherical Lebesgue measure of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The measure
\frac{1}{n+1}V_{K}
is called the cone-volume measure of
K
. From now on, when we work with the convex body
K
, it is convenient to drop the index
K
: for example,
h=h_{K}
,
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{K}
,
V=V_{K}
.

3.
Stability

We recall the following inequality in
[
IM23a
, Lem. 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X=Dh:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\partial K
. Then we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle n\int|X|^{2}dV\leq\int h(\bar{\Delta}h+nh)dV+n\frac{|\int XdV|^{2}}{\int dV}.

Recall that
c(K)=\frac{\int XdV}{\int dV}
. Let us put
\tilde{K}:=K-c(K)
and
\tilde{h}=h_{\tilde{K}}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
m\leq\frac{h}{\mathcal{K}}\leq M
. Then we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle n\int|D\tilde{h}|^{2}d\theta\leq\frac{M}{m}\int\tilde{h}(\bar{\Delta}\tilde{h}+n\tilde{h})d\theta.

Proof.

Inequality (
3.1
) can be rewritten as

(3.3)

\displaystyle n\int|Dh-c(K)|^{2}dV\leq\int h(\bar{\Delta}h+nh)dV.

Using
\bar{\Delta}x+nx=0
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}\tilde{h}(x)+n\tilde{h}(x)=\bar{\Delta}h(x)+nh(x).

Therefore,

(3.5)

\displaystyle n\int|D\tilde{h}|^{2}dV\leq\int h(\bar{\Delta}\tilde{h}+n\tilde{h})dV

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle n\int|D\tilde{h}|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{m}\int(\bar{\Delta}\tilde{h}+n\tilde{h})hd\theta=\frac{M}{m}\int(\bar{\Delta}\tilde{h}+n\tilde{h})\tilde{h}d\theta.

∎

Proof of
1.1
.

Let

(3.7)

\displaystyle M=\max\frac{h}{\mathcal{K}},\quad m=\min\frac{h}{\mathcal{K}},\quad\varepsilon:=\frac{M}{m}-1.

In view of
3.2
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left(n+1+\varepsilon\right)\int|\bar{\nabla}\tilde{h}|^{2}d\theta\leq n\varepsilon\int\tilde{h}^{2}d\theta.

Applying the Poincaré inequality to
\tilde{h}
, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle n\int\left(\tilde{h}-\fint\tilde{h}d\theta\right)^{2}d\theta\leq\int|\bar{\nabla}\tilde{h}|^{2}d\theta,

where

(3.10)

\displaystyle\fint\varphi d\theta:=\frac{1}{\int d\theta}\int\varphi d\theta,\quad\forall\varphi\in C(\mathbb{S}^{n}).

Combining (
3.8
) with (
3.9
) yields

(3.11)

\displaystyle\fint\left(\tilde{h}-\fint\tilde{h}d\theta\right)^{2}d\theta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{n+1}\fint\tilde{h}^{2}d\theta.

It follows that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\fint\left(\frac{\tilde{h}}{\fint\tilde{h}d\theta}-1\right)^{2}d\theta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{n+1}\frac{\fint\tilde{h}^{2}d\theta}{\left(\fint\tilde{h}d\theta\right)^{2}}.

Next, by a standard argument, we show that the right-hand side is bounded. Note that
for
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\langle c(K),v\rangle\leq\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}\langle Dh(x),v\rangle=\max_{y\in K}\langle y,v\rangle=h(v).

Hence,
\tilde{h}\geq 0
.
Let
M_{\tilde{h}}=\max\tilde{h}
. There exists a unit vector
w\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

M_{\tilde{h}}=\tilde{h}(w)
. Due to convexity, for any
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}(x)=\max_{y\in\tilde{K}}\langle x,y\rangle\geq\langle x,w\rangle M_{\tilde{h}}.

Therefore, for some
c_{1}
, depending on
n
, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\fint\tilde{h}d\theta\geq\frac{1}{\int d\theta}\int_{\langle x,w\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{h}d\theta\geq\frac{M_{\tilde{h}}}{2\int d\theta}\int_{\langle x,w\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}}d\theta\geq c_{1}M_{\tilde{h}}

and

(3.16)

\displaystyle\left(\fint\tilde{h}^{2}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq M_{\tilde{h}}\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}\fint\tilde{h}d\theta.

Hence,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\fint\left(\frac{\tilde{h}}{\fint\tilde{h}d\theta}-1\right)^{2}d\theta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{(n+1)c_{1}^{2}}.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

A simple example of a non-origin centred ball shows that the exponent
1/2
in
1.1
cannot be replaced by an exponent smaller than one. Let
K=B+w
be the unit ball centred at the point
w
with
|w|<1
. Then
h(x)=1+\langle w,x\rangle
and
Dh(x)=x+w
for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Moreover, we have

(3.18)

\displaystyle c(K)=\frac{\int DhdV}{\int dV}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int(x+w)(1+\langle w,x\rangle)d\theta(x)}{\int 1+\langle w,x\rangle d\theta(x)}

\displaystyle=w+\frac{\int x\langle w,x\rangle d\theta(x)}{\int d\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{n+1}w,

where we used the identity

(3.19)

\displaystyle\int x\otimes xd\theta(x)=\frac{1}{n+1}\int d\theta\operatorname{Id}.

Therefore, we have

(3.20)

\displaystyle K-c(K)

\displaystyle=B+w-\frac{n+2}{n+1}w=B-\frac{1}{n+1}w,

\displaystyle\delta_{2}\left(\bar{K},B\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\int d\theta}\int\frac{\langle w,x\rangle^{2}}{(n+1)^{2}}d\theta(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{|w|}{(n+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}},

\displaystyle\frac{\max\frac{h}{\mathcal{K}}}{\min\frac{h}{\mathcal{K}}}-1

\displaystyle=\frac{1+|w|}{1-|w|}-1=\frac{2|w|}{1-|w|}.

As an application of
1.1
, we obtain a uniform diameter bound when the density
f
of the cone-volume measure with respect to the spherical Lebesgue measure is close to
1
. This diameter bound is the main ingredient in the proof of
1.2
.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
Sch14
, Lem. 7.6.4]

Let
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
be two convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then there holds

(3.21)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}(K_{1},K_{2})^{2}\geq\alpha_{n}\operatorname{diam}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})^{-n}\delta_{H}(K_{1},K_{2})^{n+2},

where
\alpha_{n}
is a dimensional constant and
\operatorname{diam}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})
is the diameter of the set
K_{1}\cup K_{2}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

There exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
C=C(\varepsilon_{0},n)
with following property. If
K
is a smooth, strictly convex body containing the origin in its interior, such that

(3.22)

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon\leq\frac{h}{\mathcal{K}}\leq 1+\varepsilon

for some
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, then
h\leq C
.

Proof.

In view of (
3.15
), the support function of
\bar{K}
satisfies

(3.23)

\displaystyle h_{\bar{K}}(x)=\frac{\tilde{h}(x)}{\fint\tilde{h}d\theta}\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Then we have

(3.24)

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}(\bar{K}\cup B_{1})\leq 2\left(1+\frac{1}{c_{1}}\right).

On the other hand, by
1.1
we have

(3.25)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}(\bar{K},B_{1})\leq\gamma\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence, from (
3.21
) it follows for some constant
c_{2}
, depending only on
n
,

(3.26)

\displaystyle\delta_{H}(\bar{K},B_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n+2}}\operatorname{diam}(\bar{K}\cup B_{1})^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\delta_{2}(\bar{K},B_{1})^{\frac{2}{n+2}}\leq c_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+2}}.

Thus, we have

(3.27)

\displaystyle 1-c_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+2}}\leq\frac{\tilde{h}}{\fint\tilde{h}d\theta}\leq 1+c_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+2}},

and for
\varepsilon_{0}
with
c_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+2}}<1
,

(3.28)

\displaystyle\frac{\max\tilde{h}}{\min\tilde{h}}\leq\frac{1+c_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+2}}}{1-c_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+2}}}.

Using (
3.22
), we have

(3.29)

\displaystyle(\max\tilde{h})^{n+1}\geq\frac{V(\tilde{K})}{V(B)}=\frac{V(K)}{V(B)}\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Hence, there exists a constant
c_{3}>0
depending on
n
such that

(3.30)

\displaystyle\max\tilde{h}\geq c_{3}.

Substituting this into (
3.28
) and assuming
c_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{n+2}}<\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

(3.31)

\displaystyle\min\tilde{h}\geq\frac{c_{3}}{3}.

This means that an origin-centred ball of radius
c_{3}/3
is contained in
K-c(K)
, and hence the inradius of
K
is at least
c_{3}/3
. Due to (
3.22
),
V(K)\leq(1+\varepsilon)V(B)
. Therefore, there exists
C
, depending only on
\varepsilon_{0},\,n
, such that
h<C
.
∎

Proof of
1.2
.

Follows from
3.5
; see, for example,
[
CFL22
]
for details.

4.
Uniqueness

Theorem 4.1
.

[
Gag84
,
And03
,
And99
,
AC12
,
BCD17
]

Let
K
be a smooth, strictly convex body. If
\mathcal{K}=h^{1-p}
with
p\in(-n-1,1)
, then
K
is the unit ball.

In
[
IM23a
]
, using the local Brunn-Minkowski inequality, a new proof of this theorem was given for the cases
-n-1<p\leq-1
and
p=0
. Here, we present an argument that can also handle the case
p\in(-1,0)
. The new ingredient is the following integral identity.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p\neq-(n+1)
. If
dV=h^{p}d\theta
, then

\displaystyle\int x\otimes xdV=\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\int dV\right)\mathrm{Id}.

Proof.

For
p=0
the identity is trivial. We may assume
p\neq 0
. Let us take
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and define
\ell_{i}(x):=\langle x,w_{i}\rangle:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. By the divergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int\langle w_{1},x\rangle\langle X(x),w_{2}\rangle\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}}d\theta(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{u\in\partial K}\langle w_{1},\nu(u)\rangle\langle u,w_{2}\rangle\mathcal{H}^{n}(du)

\displaystyle=\int_{u\in K}\operatorname{div}_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\langle u,w_{2}\rangle w_{1})du

\displaystyle=\frac{\int dV}{n+1}\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle.

Since
h^{p-1}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}}
, we obtain

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int\langle w_{1},x\rangle\langle X(x),w_{2}\rangle h^{p-1}d\theta(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int dV}{n+1}\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle.

Moreover, by integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\bar{\Delta}h^{p}d\theta=-p\int\ell_{1}\langle\bar{\nabla}\ell_{2},\bar{\nabla}h\rangle h^{p-1}d\theta-p\int\ell_{2}\langle\bar{\nabla}\ell_{1},\bar{\nabla}h\rangle h^{p-1}d\theta.

The left-hand side of this identity can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\bar{\Delta}h^{p}d\theta

\displaystyle=\int\bar{\Delta}(\ell_{1}\ell_{2})dV

\displaystyle=-2(n+1)\int\ell_{1}\ell_{2}dV+2\int dV\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle.

On the other hand, using (
4.1
), the first term on the right-hand side (similarly, the second term) simplifies to

\displaystyle-p\int\ell_{1}\langle\bar{\nabla}\ell_{2},\bar{\nabla}h\rangle h^{p-1}d\theta

\displaystyle=-p\int\ell_{1}\langle w_{2},X-hx\rangle h^{p-1}d\theta

\displaystyle=p\int\ell_{1}\ell_{2}dV-\frac{p}{n+1}\int dV\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle.

Putting everything together, we find

\displaystyle(n+1+p)\left(\int\ell_{1}\ell_{2}dV-\frac{\int dV}{n+1}\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle\right)=0.

Hence, for
p\neq-(n+1)
there holds

\int\ell_{1}\ell_{2}dV=\frac{\int dV}{n+1}\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle\quad\forall w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
dV=h^{p}d\theta
and
p\neq-n-1
, then

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2}dV=\int|\bar{\nabla}\tilde{h}|^{2}dV+\frac{n(n+1-p)|c|^{2}}{(n+1)(n+1+p)}\int dV.

Proof.

Let
c=c(K)
. Recall that
\tilde{h}(x)=h(x)-\langle x,c\rangle
. We have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2}-|\bar{\nabla}\tilde{h}|^{2}dV

\displaystyle=\int\langle\bar{\nabla}(h-\tilde{h}),\bar{\nabla}(h+\tilde{h})\rangle dV

\displaystyle=\int\langle\bar{\nabla}\langle c,x\rangle,2\bar{\nabla}h-\bar{\nabla}\langle c,x\rangle\rangle dV

\displaystyle=\int\langle\bar{\nabla}\langle c,x\rangle,2X-\bar{\nabla}\langle c,x\rangle\rangle dV

\displaystyle=\int\langle c-\langle c,x\rangle x,2X-c+\langle c,x\rangle x\rangle dV

\displaystyle=\int\langle c-\langle c,x\rangle x,2X-c\rangle dV

\displaystyle=\int|c|^{2}-2\langle c,x\rangle h+\langle c,x\rangle^{2}dV.

Here, we used
c-\langle c,x\rangle x=\bar{\nabla}\langle c,x\rangle\in T\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\int XdV=(\int dV)c
.
Using
\bar{\Delta}x+nx=0
and by integrating by parts, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int hxdV

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n}\int h^{p+1}\bar{\Delta}xd\theta=\frac{p+1}{n}\int h^{p}\bar{\nabla}hd\theta,

and

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int XdV

\displaystyle=\int(\bar{\nabla}h+hx)h^{p}d\theta=\frac{n+p+1}{n}\int h^{p}\bar{\nabla}hd\theta.

Therefore,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int hxdV

\displaystyle=\frac{p+1}{n+p+1}\int XdV=\frac{p+1}{n+p+1}\left(\int dV\right)c.

Moreover, by
4.2
, for any
p\neq-(n+1)
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\int\langle c,x\rangle^{2}dV=\int x\otimes x\Big|_{(c,c)}dV=\frac{\int dV}{n+1}|c|^{2}.

Now, substituting (
4.6
) and
(
4.7
)
into (
4.3
), we finally obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2}-|\bar{\nabla}\tilde{h}|^{2}dV

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{2(p+1)}{n+p+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}\right)|c|^{2}\int dV

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n+1-p)|c|^{2}}{(n+1)(n+1+p)}\int dV.

∎

Proof of
4.1

when
p\in(-n-1,0]
.

By
[
IM23a
, (4.4)]
, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2}dV\leq\frac{n|c|^{2}}{n+1+p}\int dV.

Due to
4.3
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\int|\bar{\nabla}\tilde{h}|^{2}dV

\displaystyle=\int|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2}dV-\frac{n(n+1-p)|c|^{2}}{(n+1)(n+1+p)}\int dV

\displaystyle\leq\frac{np|c|^{2}}{(n+1)(n+1+p)}\int dV.

Therefore, for
-n-1<p\leq 0
,
\tilde{h}
is constant. Now, the equation
\mathcal{K}=h^{1-p}
implies that
h
is also constant.
∎

It might be of independent interest that
4.2
is, in fact, a simple consequence of the following two general identities.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
K
be a smooth, strictly convex body. Then

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int\bar{\nabla}\log\frac{h^{n+2}}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes xdV

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\int X\otimes\frac{x}{h}dV

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\int dV\right)\operatorname{Id}.

Proof.

For the background in centro-affine geometry, see
[
Mil23
]
. By
[
HI24
, Thm 1.3]
, we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle\Delta X+nX=h\bar{\nabla}\log\frac{h^{n+2}}{\mathcal{K}},

where
\Delta
is defined in
[
HI24
, p. 3]
.
Moreover, by the centro-affine Gauss equation for
\xi^{\ast}(x):=x/h(x)
, we have

\Delta\xi^{\ast}+n\xi^{\ast}=0
. Hence, the first identity follows by integrating by parts (cf.
[
Mil23
, (4.9)]
). The second identity follows from the divergence theorem; see the proof of (
4.1
).
∎

Now
4.2
follows from

(4.13)

\displaystyle\int x\otimes xdV-\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\int dV\right)\operatorname{Id}

\displaystyle=-\int\bar{\nabla}\log h\otimes xdV

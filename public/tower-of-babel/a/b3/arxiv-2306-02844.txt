Strong Persistence of a Class of Strongly Coupled Parabolic Systems of m m Equations.


Abstract
We establish one of the most important assumptions of the strong persistence theory for dynamical systems associated to cross diffusion systems of m m equations ( m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 ): the stable sets of semi-trivial steady cannot intersect the interior of the positive cone of C ⁡ ( Ω , I ​ R m ) C(\Omega,{\rm I\kern-1.6pt{\rm R}}^{m}) . Many examples will be provide to show the effects of the cross diffusion.

2
Persistence

To begin, we need the concept of attractors for dissipative dynamical systems. We will discuss it briefly below and refer the readers to another comprehensive literature (e.g.
[
4
,
18
,
17
]
and the reference therein)

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a semiflow on
X
. A
subset
A\subset X
is said to be an attractor for
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
if
A
is nonempty, compact, invariant, and
there exists some open neighborhood
U
of
A
in
X
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}d(\Phi(t)(u),A)=0
for
all
\in U
. Here,
d(x,A)
is the usual Hausdorff distance from
x
to the set
A
. If
A
is an
attractor which attracts every point in
X
,
A
is called global attractor.

For a nonempty
invariant set
M
, the set

W^{s}(M):=\{x\in X:\lim_{t\to\infty}d(\Phi(t)(x),M)=0\}

is called the
stable set of
M
. A nonempty invariant subset
M
of
X
is said to be isolated if it is the
maximal invariant set in some neighborhood of itself.

Let
A
and
B
be two isolated invariant sets.
A
is said to be chained to
B
, denoted by

A\to B
, if there exists a globally defined trajectory
\phi(t)(x)
,
t\in(-\infty,\infty)
, through some

x\not\in A\cup B
whose range has compact closure such that the omega limit set
\omega(x)\subset B
and
the alpha limit set
\alpha(x)\subset A
. A finite sequence
\{M_{1},M_{2},...,M_{k}\}
of isolated invariant sets
is called a chain if
M_{1}\to M2\to\ldots\to M_{k}
. The chain is called a cycle if
M_{k}=M_{1}
. These concepts were introduced in
[
3
]
.

Let
X_{0}\subset X
be an open set and
\partial X_{0}=X\setminus X_{0}
. Assume that
X_{0}
is positively
invariant. Let
d(x,\partial X_{0})
be the distance from
x
to
\partial X_{0}
.
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is said to be uniformly
persistent with respect to
(X_{0},\partial X_{0})
if there exists
\eta>0
such that

\liminf_{t\to\infty}d(\Phi(t)(x),\partial X_{0})\geq\eta\quad\mbox{for all }x\in X_{0}.

As in
[
14
]
, in order to discuss the uniform persistence property of our parabolic systems we rely on the following result

Theorem 2.1

(
[
15
, Theorem 4.3]
) Assume that

C.1)

\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
has a global attractor

;

C.2)

There exists a finite sequence

of pairwise disjoint, compact
and isolated invariant sets in

with the following properties:

m.1)

\cup_{x\in\partial X_{0}}\omega(x)\subset\cup_{i=1}^{k}M_{i}
,

m.2)

no set of M forms a cycle in
\partial X_{0}
,

m.3)

M_{i}
is isolated in
X
,

m.4)

W^{s}(M_{i})\cap X_{0}=\emptyset
for each
i=1,\ldots,k
.

Then
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly persistent with respect to
(X_{0},\partial X_{0})
.

\mathbb{M}=(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{pos}\mathbb{T}+(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}(\kappa Id-\mathbf{P_{coop}})\mathbb{T}-kId=(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{pos}\mathbb{T}+\nu_{*}\mathbb{M}_{*},

where
\mathbb{M}_{*}=\nu_{*}[(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}(\kappa Id-\mathbf{P_{coop}})\mathbb{T}-kId]
for some
k,\kappa>0
and matrices
\mathbf{P}_{pos}>0
, cooperative
\mathbf{P_{coop}}
, and
\mathbb{T}
such that
\nu_{*}\mathbb{T}>0
and
D\mathbb{T}\mathbb{T}^{-1}\in{\cal M}_{lt}(\Omega,n)
.

If
\mathbb{M}_{*}>0
then there is
\hat{{\cal L}}
as in Theorem
6.2
such that
\hat{{\cal L}}^{-1}\mathbb{M}
is strongly positive. In addition, we have that
(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{pos}\mathbb{T}

is positive if
\nu_{*}=1
and negative if
\nu_{*}=-1
.

Because
D\mathbb{T}\mathbb{T}^{-1}
is diagonal,
\mathbb{T}=\mbox{diag}[\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n}]C
, where
\delta_{i}
’s are functions and
C
is a constant matrix (see Remark
6.4
). Consider the simple case
\delta_{i}=\delta
and the diagonal entries
\mu_{i}
’s of
\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B}
are positive (the case
C
is diagonal is trivial as
\hat{{\cal L}}
will be diagonal too and we do not have cross diffusion). Then, in order
\mathbb{M}=(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{pos}\mathbb{T}+(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}(\kappa Id-\mathbf{P_{coop}})\mathbb{T}-kId
we must have some
\mathbf{P}_{pos},\mathbf{P_{coop}}
such that

\mathbb{M}-kId=\mbox{diag}[\alpha_{i}](\mathbf{P}_{pos}+\kappa Id-\mathbf{P_{coop}})C
where
\alpha_{i}=\delta\mu_{i}^{-1}
which have the same sign.

In the simplest case, if we choose
\alpha_{i}\sim 1
and
C\sim Id
then
\mathbb{M}\sim(\kappa-k)Id+\mathbf{P}_{pos}-\mathbf{P_{coop}}
and
\mathbb{M}_{*}\sim\nu_{*}[(\kappa-k)Id-\mathbf{P_{coop}}]
. We then see that if
\mathbb{M}\sim\kappa_{*}Id+M
for some
\kappa_{*}>0
(we can choose
\kappa>k>0
) and
\mathbf{P}_{pos},\mathbf{P_{coop}}
are the positive and negative parts of
M
with
\kappa_{*}Id-\mathbf{P_{coop}}
is negative then we choose
\nu_{*}=-1
and
\hat{{\cal L}}
exists. In this case,
\mathbb{M}
can be completely competitive and a maximum principles is available if we have certain appropriate cross diffusion. Meanwhile, it is easy to construct an example where this is not the case when the system is diagonal.

6.2
\tau>1
and the effects of cross diffusion

We can use Theorem
6.2
, and various choices of
M,M_{1}
to show that
\tau>0
.
Let us recall the settings we assumed in order to apply Theorem
6.2
to (
3.4
). We assume first that

\hat{\mathbf{C}}\mbox{ is {cooperative} and }\kappa\mathbb{T}^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{pos}^{-1}\mathbb{T}>\mathbb{T}^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{pos}^{-1}(\mathbf{P_{coop}}\mathbb{T}+k(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B}))

(6.7)

and then set
\mathbb{M}=\mathbf{m_{bg}}+kId
with
\mathbf{m_{bg}}:=\mathbf{b}^{21}_{v}(W^{*})Du^{*}+\mathbf{g}^{22}(W^{*})+K
to obtain
\hat{{\cal L}}^{-1}\mathbb{M}
. We need
\hat{{\cal L}}^{-1}\mathbb{M}
, the operator
in Theorem
6.2
, is strongly positive to show first that
\tau>0
. Thus,

\mathbf{m_{bg}}=(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}[\mathbf{P}_{pos}+\kappa Id-\mathbf{P_{coop}}]\mathbb{T}-2kId.

(6.8)

We then apply the argument in Section
4.2
to check the condition E) for
L=\hat{{\cal L}}
to show that
\tau>1
and (
3.4
) holds. These examples show that the introduction of cross diffusion does induce persistence which may not occur in diagonal cases. One should also note that the argument in Section
3
do not require that
K
is a constant matrix.

When
{\cal L}
is given:
It is easy to construct the reactions such that
\tau>1
. In this case
\mathbb{L},\mathbb{B},\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbf{A}_{d}
are given. Let
\mathbf{P_{coop}}
(resp.
\mathbf{P}_{pos}
) be a cooperative (resp. positive) matrix satisfying (
6.7
) (see also Lemma
6.7
).

Let
\mathbb{M}_{1}=(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}[\mathbf{P}_{pos}+\kappa Id-\mathbf{P_{coop}}]\mathbb{T}-kId
. By Theorem
6.2

\hat{{\cal L}}^{-1}\mathbb{M}_{1}
is strongly positive so that there is
\lambda_{*},\phi_{*}>0
such that

\hat{{\cal L}}^{-1}\mathbb{M}_{1}\phi_{*}=\lambda_{*}^{-1}\phi_{*}\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{M}_{1}\phi_{*}=\lambda_{*}^{-1}\hat{{\cal L}}\phi_{*}.

Take
\mathbb{M},\mathbf{b}^{21}_{v}(W^{*})Du^{*}
and
\mathbf{g}^{22}
be such that
\mathbb{M}\phi_{*}=\lambda^{-1}\lambda_{*}\mathbb{M}_{1}\phi_{*}
. There are many such
\mathbf{b}^{21}_{v},\mathbf{g}^{22}
. That is,

(\mathbf{b}^{21}_{v}(W^{*})Du^{*}+\mathbf{g}^{22}+K+kId)\phi_{*}=\lambda^{-1}\lambda_{*}(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}[\mathbf{P}_{pos}+\kappa Id-\mathbf{P_{coop}}]\mathbb{T}\phi_{*}-k\phi_{*}.

Then
\mathbb{M}_{1}\phi_{*}=\lambda_{*}^{-1}{\cal L}\phi_{*}\Rightarrow\mathbb{M}\phi_{*}=\lambda^{-1}{\cal L}\phi_{*}
. If
\lambda<1
then (
3.4
) have a solution
\psi=\phi_{*}>0
and
\tau=\lambda^{-1}>1
.

The converse problem is harder. That is, if the reaction is given then we can design the diffusion in a way that
\tau>1
. If the reaction is of certain form then we can use Lemma
6.9
and usual scalings to get the desired. On the other hand, we can scale the domain too. We have the following
partial
answer.

When
\mathbf{b}^{22}=0
, and
\mathbf{a}^{21},\mathbf{b}^{21},\mathbf{g}^{22}
are given on
\Omega
:
We need to find
\mathbb{L},\mathbb{B},\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbf{A}_{d}
to determine
\mathbf{A}
(then
\mathbf{B}=-\mathbf{a}^{21}_{v}(W^{*})Du^{*}
has the structure determined by
\mathbf{a}^{22}
as in the theorem to give
\hat{{\cal L}}
) such that (
6.7
) holds first. Of course, Lemma
6.7
can be used here to obtain
\hat{{\cal L}},\mathbb{M}
.

Note that for any matrix
\mathbf{m}
if we choose
K=(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{B})^{-1}[\mathbf{P}_{pos}+\kappa Id-\mathbf{P_{coop}}]\mathbb{T}-2kId-\mathbf{m}
then there are
\phi_{*}>0,\tau>0
independent of
\mathbf{m}
such that
\hat{{\cal L}}^{-1}\mathbb{M}\phi_{*}=\tau\phi_{*}
. That is

\tau[-\mbox{div}(\mathbf{a}^{22}(x)D\phi_{*}(x))-\mathbf{a}^{21}_{v}(x)Du^{*}(x)D\phi_{*}(x)]=[\mathbf{m}+K+kId]\phi_{*}(x).

(6.9)

For such fixed
\phi_{*},\tau
above we can choose
R>0
such that
R^{-2}\tau>1
. Define
\hat{x}=Rx
and write
\hat{\alpha}(x)=\alpha(Rx)
for any function matrix
\alpha
. Let
\Omega^{R}=\{x\,:\,Rx\in\Omega\}
.

By elementary calculus we have

\tau[-\mbox{div}(\hat{\mathbf{a}}^{22}(x)D\hat{\phi}_{*}(x))-\hat{\mathbf{a}}^{21}_{v}(x)D\hat{u}^{*}(x)D\hat{\phi}_{*}(x)]
is

\tau R^{2}[-\mbox{div}_{\hat{x}}(\mathbf{a}^{22}(\hat{x})D_{\hat{x}}\phi_{*}(\hat{x}))-\mathbf{a}^{21}_{v}(\hat{x})D_{\hat{x}}u^{*}(\hat{x})D_{\hat{x}}\phi_{*}(\hat{x})]

So that from (
6.9
) with
x
being
\hat{x}
and
\mathbf{m}(\hat{x})=\mbox{div}_{\hat{x}}(R^{2}\mathbf{a}^{21}_{v}(\hat{x})D_{\hat{x}}u^{*}(\hat{x}))+R\mathbf{b}^{21}_{v}(\hat{x})D_{\hat{x}}u^{*}(\hat{x})+\mathbf{g}^{22}(\hat{x})
, we have by chain rules and combining the above calculations that

\tau[-\mbox{div}(\hat{\mathbf{a}}^{22}(x)D\hat{\phi}_{*}(x))-\hat{\mathbf{a}}^{21}_{v}(x)D\hat{u}^{*}(x)D\hat{\phi}_{*}(x)]=R^{2}[\mathbf{m}(\hat{x})+K+kId]\phi_{*}(\hat{x}).

Thus,

R^{-2}\tau[-\mbox{div}(\hat{\mathbf{a}}^{22}(x)D\hat{\phi}_{*}(x))-\hat{\mathbf{a}}^{21}_{v}(x)D\hat{u}^{*}(x)D\hat{\phi}_{*}(x)]=[\mathbf{m}(x)+K+kId]\hat{\phi}_{*}(x).

where
\mathbf{m}(x)=\mbox{div}(\mathbf{a}^{21}_{v}(Rx)Du^{*}(Rx))+\mathbf{b}^{21}_{v}(Rx)Du^{*}(Rx)+\mathbf{g}^{22}(Rx)
. Therefore, (
3.4
) holds with
\tau
being
R^{-2}\tau>1
and
\psi(x):=\hat{\phi}_{*}(x)=\phi_{*}(Rx)>0
for
x\in\Omega^{R}
.

Thus, for any given
\mathbf{a}^{21}_{v},\mathbf{b}^{21}_{v},\mathbf{g}^{22}
one has to combine an appropriate cross diffusion and scaling to have persistence on a suitably domain scaled from the original one.

A simple example when
\mathbf{A}
is a constant matrix:
We recall the counter example in Section
5.2
where we consider
\mathbf{A}=\mbox{diag}[a_{i}]
,
M=[c_{ij}]
and the functions
w_{i}=e^{k_{i}t}\phi
and
W=\left[w_{i}\right]_{i}^{m_{2}}
on
\Omega\times(0,\infty)
. Of course,
W
is a solution of
W_{t}=\mbox{div}(\mathbf{A}DW)+MW
if

k_{i}=-\lambda_{*}a_{i}+\sum_{j}c_{ij}

for all
i
. We see that there are choices of
a_{i}>0
and
c_{ij}
such that
k_{i}<0
so that
w_{i}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. The matrix
g
can be cooperative or competitive. Thus, we cannot have
\tau>1
in this case.

For some numbers
k_{i}
’s we consider the functions
w_{i}=e^{k_{i}t}\phi
and
W=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}w_{i}\end{array}\right]_{i}^{m_{2}}
on
\Omega\times(0,\infty)
. It is clear that
W_{t}=\mbox{div}(ADW)+GW
is equivalent to

k_{i}e^{k_{i}t}\phi=-\lambda_{*}\sum_{j}a_{ij}e^{k_{j}t}\phi+\sum_{j}c_{ij}e^{k_{j}t}\phi

for all
i
. This is true if

k_{i}=-\lambda_{*}a_{ii}+c_{ii},\;-\lambda_{*}a_{ij}+c_{ij}=0\quad\forall i\neq j.

We now choose positive
a_{ij},c_{ij}
such that
0<c_{ii}<\lambda_{*}a_{ii}
and
c_{ij}=\lambda_{*}a_{ij}
if
j\neq i
. Then
k_{i}<0
so that
w_{i}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. This has been discussed in our previous counterexamples when the condition (
5.5
) of the Corollary
5.7
is violated (also the diagonality). Note that this situation happen even when
\lambda_{*}
is large by scaling the domain
\Omega
(we can choose
a_{ij}=c_{ij}/\lambda_{*}
very small if
i\neq j
and
a_{ii}
large for given
c_{ij}
’s).

Thus, we can have persistence if
\mathbf{A}
is full but appropriately designed but no persistence when
\mathbf{A}
is replaced by full/diagonal matrices
A
while using the same reaction. Therefore, the structures of
{\cal L}
in Theorem

seems to be necessary for persistence.

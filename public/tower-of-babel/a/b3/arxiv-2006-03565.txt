Multiple solutions to cylindrically symmetric curl-curl problems and related Schrödinger equations with singular potentials
Michał Gaczkowski, Jarosław Mederski, Jacopo Schino

Abstract
. We look for multiple solutions 𝐔 : ℝ 3 → ℝ 3 \mathbf{U}\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3} to the curl-curl problem ∇ × ∇ × 𝐔 = h ( x , 𝐔 ) , x ∈ ℝ 3 , \nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{U}=h(x,\mathbf{U}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}, with a nonlinear function h : ℝ 3 × ℝ 3 → ℝ 3 h\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3} which is critical in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , i.e., h ⁡ ( x , 𝐔 ) = | 𝐔 | 4 ​ 𝐔 h(x,\mathbf{U})=|\mathbf{U}|^{4}\mathbf{U} , or has subcritical growth at infinity. If h h is radial in 𝐔 \mathbf{U} and a = 1 a=1 below, then we show that the solutions to the problem above are in one to one correspondence with the solutions to the following Schrödinger equation − Δ ​ u + a r 2 ​ u = f ⁡ ( x , u ) , u : ℝ 3 → ℝ , -\Delta u+\frac{a}{r^{2}}u=f(x,u),\qquad u\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}, where x = ( y , z ) ∈ ℝ 2 × ℝ x=(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R} , r = | y | r=|y| , and a ≥ 0 a\geq 0 . In the critical case, the multiplicity problem for the latter equation has been studied only in the autonomous case a = 0 a=0 and the available methods seem to be insufficient for the problem involving the singular potential, i.e., a ≠ 0 a\neq 0 , due to the lack of conformal invariance. Therefore we develop methods for the critical curl-curl problem and show the multiplicity of bound states for both equations. In the subcritical case, instead, studying the Schrödinger equation in higher dimensions, we find infinitely many bound states for both problems.

Introduction

We look for solutions
\mathbf{U}\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
to the following
curl-curl problem

(1.1)

\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{U}=h(x,\mathbf{U})\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}

with a nonlinear function
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
which arises in the study of the propagation of time-harmonic electromagnetic fields in a nonlinear medium by means of Maxwell’s equations and the constitutive laws, see
[
24
,
25
,
1
,
14
,
16
]
and the references therein.
The first existence results concerning (
1.1
) have been obtained for the cylindrically symmetric media in
[
3
]
and for nonsymmetric ones in
[
16
]
by means of variational methods for subcritical
h
at infinity.

Most of known results (see the survey
[
19
]
and the references therein) concern the subcritical regime, and the problem in the purely critical case
h(x,\mathbf{U})=|\mathbf{U}|^{4}\mathbf{U}
was an open question for some time. Recently, the second author and Szulkin
[
21
]
obtained finally the existence of a least energy solution to (
1.1
) in the critical case by developing new concentration compactness argument and variational approach for the curl-curl operator. However, the problem of the existence of multiple solutions to the critical curl-curl problem remained open and will be investigated below as one of the main aims of the work.

On the other hand, we are also interested in finding solutions
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
to the problem

(1.2)

-\Delta u+\frac{a}{r^{2}}u=f(x,u)\qquad\textnormal{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
x=(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{K}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-K}
,
N>K\geq 2
,
r=|y|
is the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{K}
,
a>a_{0}\in(-\infty,0]
.
Here
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonlinear function with critical growth, i.e.,
f(x,u)=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u
, or subcritical growth at infinity, see assumptions (F1)–(F5) below. The problem appears in the study of stationary solutions to nonlinear Schrödinger or Klein–Gordon equations
[
4
]
.

We also study the relation between (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) as follows. Suppose that
N=3
,
K=2
,
a=1
, and
h(x,\alpha w)=f(x,\alpha)w
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
|w|=1
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Then one can easily calculate that if
u(x)=u(r,x_{3})
with
r=|(x_{1},x_{2})|
is a classical solution to (
1.2
), then

(1.3)

\mathbf{U}(x)=\frac{u(x)}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\Bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-x_{2}\\
x_{1}\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr),\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus(\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}),

satisfies
\mathop{\mathrm{div}\,}(\mathbf{U})=0
and
\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{U})=-\Delta\mathbf{U}=h(x,\mathbf{U})
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus(\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{R})
, cf.
[
17
,
26
]
. Our first aim is to show that (
1.3
) is the key to build solutions to (
1.2
) from solutions to (
1.1
) and vice versa also in the context of nonclassical solutions, which is not immediate and will be demonstrated in Theorem
2.1
below. In particular, it will be crucial in the Sobolev-critical regime to study (
1.2
) by means of (
1.1
). As an additional application of Theorem
2.1
, we will prove existence results for (
1.2
) in the Sobolev-noncritical regime, thus providing new results also for (
1.1
).

By a
solution
to (
1.1
) we mean a critical point
\mathbf{U}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
of
{\mathcal{E}}
, where the functional
{\mathcal{E}}\colon{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

(1.4)

{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbf{U})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\times\mathbf{U}|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}H(x,\mathbf{U})\,dx,

H(x,\mathbf{U}):=\int_{0}^{1}\langle h(x,t\mathbf{U}),\mathbf{U}\rangle\,dt
, and
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
is the completion of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
with respect to the norm
|\nabla\mathbf{U}|_{2}
(observe that
{\mathcal{E}}
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
under reasonable assumptions about
h
). Here and in the sequel,
|\cdot|_{q}
denotes the
L^{q}
-norm for
q\in[1,\infty]
. Clearly every solution to (
1.1
) is a
weak solution
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{U})(\phi)=0
for every
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Inspired by
[
24
,
3
]
, we define
{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}
as the subspace of
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
consisting of vector fields of the form (
1.3
), which will be defined rigorously in Section
2
.

Observe that the kernel of
\nabla\times(\cdot)
is of infinite dimension since
\nabla\times(\nabla\phi)=0
for all
\phi
of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
and
{\mathcal{E}}
is strongly indefinite, i.e., it is unbounded from above and from below, even on subspaces of finite codimension. In order to avoid the indefiniteness we use Theorem
2.1
; in particular, the property that
\nabla\times\nabla\times(\cdot)=-\Delta
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}
(Sobolev-critical regime) or the one that
\mathbf{U}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a solution to (
1.1
) if and only if
u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
is a solution to (
1.2
) with
a=1
,
N=3
, and
K=2
(Sobolev-noncritical regime), where

is defined below.

Regarding (
1.2
), we introduce the energy functional

{\mathcal{J}}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{a}{r^{2}}|u|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,u)\,dx,

which, under usual assumptions about

, is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
in

, where

,
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the completion of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the usual norm
|\nabla u|_{2}
, and

X:=\Big\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dx<\infty\Big\},

endowed with the norm
\|u\|=(|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}+|u/r|_{2}^{2})^{1/2}
. Due to the singular term, we consider the group
{\mathcal{O}}:={\mathcal{O}}(K)\times\{I_{N-K}\}\subset{\mathcal{O}}(N)
, which acts isometrically on

, and let

denote the subspace of

consisting of invariant functions with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}
. Here and in the sequel,
{\mathcal{O}}(d)
is the orthogonal group in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Note that this is equivalent to requiring that the functions be invariant with respect to
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(K)\times\{I_{N-K}\}
, since for every
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
d\geq 2
, there exists
g\in{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(d)
such that
gy=x
, where
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(K)\subset{\mathcal{O}}(K)
stands for the special orthogonal group in
\mathbb{R}^{K}
.

If
K>2
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dx\leq\biggl(\frac{2}{K-2}\biggr)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

for every

(see
[
6
]
) and

and
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
coincide. Note also that

{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi\rangle+\frac{a}{r^{2}}u\phi\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u)\phi\,dx

need not be finite for
K=2
,
u\in X
,
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Therefore, by
a
solution
to (
1.2
) we mean a critical point

of
{\mathcal{J}}
.
By

to (
1.2
) in

we mean a nontrivial solution to (
1.2
) that minimizes
\{{\mathcal{J}}(u):u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}\setminus\{0\}
,

is a solution to (
1.2
)
\}
. If
K>2
, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is dense in
X
and, by the Palais principle of symmetric criticality
[

]
, critical points of
{\mathcal{J}}|_{X_{{\mathcal{O}}}}
correspond to critical points of

and are
weak solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)(\phi)=0
for every

. If

, then in view of

any nonnegative solution to (

\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

We begin by investigating the problems with the critical nonlinearities when
N=3
:

h(x,\mathbf{U})=|\mathbf{U}|^{4}\mathbf{U}\hbox{ and }f(x,u)=|u|^{4}u\quad\hbox{ for }u\in\mathbb{R},\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

The existence of a solution to (
1.1
) that minimizes
{\mathcal{E}}
among

the nontrivial solutions (not only those of the form (

)) has been recently obtained in
[

Definition 1.1

For

g=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}g_{1}&0\\
0&g_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\equiv(g_{1},g_{2})\in{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(2)\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(2)

\frac{\mathbf{U}\Bigl(\pi\bigl(g\pi^{-1}(x)\bigr)\Bigr)}{\varphi\Bigl(\pi\bigl(g\pi^{-1}(x)\bigr)\Bigr)}=\frac{\widetilde{g_{1}}\mathbf{U}(x)}{\varphi(x)}

The subspace of
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(2)\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(2)

the main result concerning (

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a sequence
(\mathbf{U}_{n})\subset{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(2)\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(2)}

(1.5)

\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{U}=|\mathbf{U}|^{4}\mathbf{U}\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}

is of the form (

Since each energy level

has a minimax characterization,

can be considered as a bound state solution. In order to overcome the difficulties owing to the indefiniteness mentioned above, we consider vector fields

Lemma 4.2

because

, in particular Theorems 3.3 and 3.5(b) therein. We need to prove that assumptions (I1)–(I8), (G), and
(M)_{0}^{\beta}
for every
\beta>0
, which in our setting read as follows, are satisfied. For simplicity, we set

{\mathcal{I}}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,u)\,dx\quad\hbox{for }u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}

and

{\mathcal{N}}:=\bigl\{u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}\setminus\{0\}:{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)u=0\bigr\}

stands for the
Nehari constraint
, which need not be a manifold of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
; see
[
20
]
. We list the required conditions:

(I1)

{\mathcal{I}}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(X_{{\mathcal{O}}},\mathbb{R})
and
{\mathcal{I}}(u)\geq{\mathcal{I}}(0)=0
for every
u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
.

(I2)

{\mathcal{I}}
is sequentially lower semicontinuous.

(I3)

If
u_{n}\to u
and
{\mathcal{I}}(u_{n})\to{\mathcal{I}}(u)
, then
u_{n}\to u
.

(I4)

\|u\|+{\mathcal{I}}(u)\to\infty
as
\|u\|\to\infty
.

(I6)

There exists
r>0
such that
\displaystyle\inf_{u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}},\|u\|=r}{\mathcal{J}}(u)>0
.

(I7)

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathcal{I}}(u_{n})}{t_{n}^{2}}\to\infty
if
t_{n}\to\infty
and
u_{n}\to u_{0}\neq 0
as
n\to\infty
.

(I8)

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}-1}{2}{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}(u)u+{\mathcal{I}}(u)-{\mathcal{I}}(tu)\leq 0
for every
u\in{\mathcal{N}}
and every
t\geq 0
.

(G)

\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}
is a group that acts on
X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
by isometries and such that for every
u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
,
(\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}*u)\setminus\{u\}
is bounded away from
u
.

The
\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}
-action is given as follows:
z\ast u(x):=u(x+(0,z))
for
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}
and
u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
.
\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}\ast u
is called the
orbit
of
u
and if, in addition,
u
is a critical point of
\mathcal{J}
, then
\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}\ast u
is a
critical orbit
.

Note that (I1)–(I4) and (G) are obviously satisfied when (F4) holds and (I6) follows easily from (F2) and the embedding of
X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
into
L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We have skipped (I5) from
[
20
, Section 3]
, since it is an empty condition. (I7), (I8), and the following variant of Cerami condition will be verified in the next lemmas.

(M)_{0}^{\beta}

(a)

There exists
M_{\beta}>0
such that
\limsup_{n}\|u_{n}\|\leq M_{\beta}
for every
(u_{n})\subset X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
such that
0\leq\liminf_{n}{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})\leq\limsup_{n}{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})\leq\beta
and
\lim_{n}(1+\|u_{n}\|){\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})=0
.

(b)

If
{\mathcal{J}}
has finitely many critical orbits, then there exists
m_{\beta}>0
such that, if
(u_{n}),(v_{n})\subset X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
are as above and
\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|<m_{\beta}
for
n
large, then
\liminf_{n}\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|=0
.

Lemma 4.4
.

(a) Suppose
f
satisfies (F3). If
t_{n}\to\infty
and
u_{n}\to u_{0}\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}\setminus\{0\}
as
n\to\infty
, then

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{t_{n}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,t_{n}u_{n})\,dx=\infty.

(b) Suppose
f
satisfies (F4). For every
u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
and every
t\geq 0

\frac{t^{2}-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u)u\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,u)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,tu)\,dx\leq 0.

Proof.

(a)
Since
X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
is locally compactly embedded into
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, up to a subsequence
u_{n}\to u_{0}\neq 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\limsup_{n}|\Omega_{n}|>0
, where
\Omega_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|u_{n}(x)|\geq\varepsilon\}
, for otherwise
u_{n}\to 0
in measure and consequently, up to a subsequence, a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
It follows from (F3) that

\frac{1}{t_{n}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,t_{n}u_{n})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(x,t_{n}u_{n})}{t_{n}^{2}|u_{n}|^{2}}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx\geq\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega_{n}}\frac{F(x,t_{n}u_{n})}{t_{n}^{2}|u_{n}|^{2}}\,dx\to\infty

as
n\to\infty
.

(b)
For fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
we prove that
\phi(t)\leq 0
for every
t\geq 0
, where

\phi(t):=\frac{t^{2}-1}{2}f(x,u)u+F(x,u)-F(x,tu).

This is trivial for
u=0
, so suppose
u\neq 0
.
Note that
\phi(1)=0
, so it is enough to prove that
\phi
is nondecreasing on
[0,1]
and nonincreasing on
[1,\infty)
. This is the case in view of (F4) and because

\phi^{\prime}(t)=tf(x,u)u-f(x,tu)u=t|u|u\biggl(\frac{f(x,u)}{|u|}-\frac{f(x,tu)}{|tu|}\biggr)

for
t>0
, therefore
\phi(t)\leq 0
for every
t\geq 0
as
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}([0,\infty))
.
∎

The following lemma shows that
(M)_{0}^{\beta}
holds for every
\beta>0
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
f
satisfies (F3) and (F4).

(a) For every
\beta>0
there exists
M_{\beta}>0
such that
\limsup_{n}\|u_{n}\|\leq M_{\beta}
for every
(u_{n})\subset X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
such that
{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})\leq\beta
for
n
large and
\lim_{n}(1+\|u_{n}\|){\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})=0
.

(b) If the number of critical orbits of
{\mathcal{J}}
is finite, then there exists
\kappa>0
such that, if
(u_{n}),(v_{n})\subset X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
are as above for some
\beta>0
and
\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|<\kappa
for
n
large, then
\lim_{n}\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|=0
.

Proof.

(a)
Let
(u_{n})\subset X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
as in the assumptions, suppose that
(u_{n})
is unbounded, and define
\bar{u}_{n}:=u_{n}/\|u_{n}\|
. Passing to a subsequence we may assume that
\|u_{n}\|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
4.3
, for any
\varepsilon>0
we find
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,\bar{u}_{n})\,dx\leq\varepsilon|\bar{u}_{n}|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+C_{\varepsilon}\Phi(\bar{u}_{n})

for every
n
, where
\Phi
is defined therein. If
(\bar{u}_{n})
satisfies (
4.1
) for every
R>0
, hence the same holds for
(s\bar{u}_{n})
with
s\geq 0
, then in view of Lemma
4.1

\limsup_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,s\bar{u}_{n})\,dx\leq\varepsilon s^{2}\limsup_{n}|\bar{u}_{n}|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}

for every
\varepsilon>0
, hence
\lim_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,s\bar{u}_{n})\,dx=0
.
Then applying Lemma
4.4
(b) with
u=u_{n}
and
t=t_{n}:=s/\|u_{n}\|
we obtain, up to a subsequence, that for every
s\geq 0

\begin{split}\beta&\geq\limsup_{n}{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})\geq\limsup_{n}{\mathcal{J}}(s\bar{u}_{n})-\lim_{n}\frac{t_{n}^{2}-1}{2}{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})u_{n}\\
&=\limsup_{n}{\mathcal{J}}(s\bar{u}_{n})\geq Cs^{2}-\lim_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,s\bar{u}_{n})\,dx=Cs^{2}\end{split}

for some
C>0
, a contradiction. Hence, up to a subsequence,
\lim_{n}\int_{B((0,z_{n}),R)}|\bar{u}_{n}|^{2}\,dx>0
for some
R>\sqrt{N-K}
and
(z_{n})\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}
, where
z_{n}
maximizes
z\mapsto\int_{B((0,z),R)}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx
. Exploiting the
\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}
-invariance, we can assume that

\int_{B(0,R)}|\bar{u}_{n}|^{2}\,dx\geq c

for
n
large and some
c>0
.

It follows that there exists
\bar{u}\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}\setminus\{0\}
such that, up to a subsequence,
\bar{u}_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}
in
X
and
\bar{u}_{n}\to\bar{u}
in
L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

From (F4),
2{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})-{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})u_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})u_{n}-2F(x,u_{n})\,dx\geq 0
, thus
\bigl({\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})\bigr)
is bounded and due to (F3) we obtain

o(1)=\frac{{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})}{\|u_{n}\|^{2}}\leq C-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(x,u_{n})}{|u_{n}|^{2}}|\bar{u}_{n}|^{2}\,dx\to-\infty

for some
C>0
, which is a contradiction. This shows that
(u_{n})
is indeed bounded. If by contradiction there exists no upper bound
M_{\beta}
, then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
(u_{n}^{k})\subset X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
as in the statement such that
\limsup_{n}\|u_{n}^{k}\|>k
and it is easy to build a subsequence
(u_{n_{k}}^{k})
that is unbounded, again a contradiction.

(b)
Assume that there are finitely many critical orbits of
{\mathcal{J}}
. From (G) we easily see that

\kappa:=\inf\big\{\|u-v\|:u\neq v\text{ and }{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)={\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(v)=0\big\}>0.

Let
(u_{n}),(v_{n})
be as in the assumptions of
(b)
. In view of
(a)
, they are bounded.

If
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})(u_{n}-v_{n})\,dx
or
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,v_{n})(u_{n}-v_{n})\,dx
do not converge to
0
, then in view of Lemma
4.3
and the
\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}
-invariance there exist
R>\sqrt{N-K}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\int_{B(0,R)}|u_{n}-v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\geq\varepsilon.

We can assume that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
,
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v
in
X
and
u\neq v
. Hence
{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)={\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(v)=0
and consequently

\liminf_{n}\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|\geq\|u-v\|\geq\kappa,

in contrast with the assumptions.

Therefore it follows that
\lim_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})(u_{n}-v_{n})\,dx=\lim_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,v_{n})(u_{n}-v_{n})\,dx=0
and, finally,

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})(u_{n}-v_{n})-{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(v_{n})(u_{n}-v_{n})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigl(f(x,u_{n})-f(x,v_{n})\bigr)(u_{n}-v_{n})\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(1)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})(u_{n}-v_{n})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,v_{n})(u_{n}-v_{n})\,dx\to 0.\qed

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Note that
{\mathcal{N}}
contains all the nontrivial critical points of
{\mathcal{J}}
. Applying
[
20
, Theorem 3.3]
we obtain a Cerami sequence
(u_{n})\subset X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
at level
c:=\inf_{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{J}}>0
. Lemma
4.5
(a) implies that there exists
u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
up to a subsequence, thus
{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)=0
.

If by contradiction
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})u_{n}\,dx\to 0
, then similarly to the proof of Lemma
4.5
(b) we infer that
u_{n}\to 0
, in contrast with
{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})\to c
. Hence, again similarly to the proof of Lemma
4.5
(b),
u\neq 0
.

Fatou’s Lemma and (F4) imply

\begin{split}c&=\lim_{n}{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})=\lim_{n}{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})u_{n}=\lim_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{2}f(x,u_{n})u_{n}-F(x,u_{n})\,dx\\
&\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{2}f(x,u)u-F(x,u)\,dx={\mathcal{J}}(u)-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)u={\mathcal{J}}(u)\geq c\end{split}

and we conclude
{\mathcal{J}}(u)=c
.

Now assume
f
is odd in
u
, which implies that
{\mathcal{J}}
is even. The existence of infinitely many
\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}
-distinct critical points of
{\mathcal{J}}
follows directly from
[
20
, Theorem 3.5(b)]
. As for the fact that the ground state solution is nonnegative, since
{\mathcal{J}}(u)={\mathcal{J}}(|u|)
and
{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)u={\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(|u|)|u|
for
u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
, we can replace
(u_{n})
with
(|u_{n}|)
and still we obtain a weak limit point, which is a nonnegative ground state solution.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose that
f
does not depend on
y
and satisfies (F5). Then there exists
w\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(z,w)\,dx>\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{z}w|^{2}\,dx
.

Proof.

Similarly as in
[
10
, page 325]
,
for any
R\geq 3
we define an even and continuous function
\phi_{R}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi_{R}(t)=0
for
|t|<1
and for
|t|>R+1
,
\phi_{R}(t)=1
for
2\leq|t|\leq R
and
\phi_{R}
is affine for
1\leq|t|\leq 2
and for
R\leq|t|\leq R+1
. Then let
w_{R}(x):=u_{0}\phi_{R}(|y|)\phi_{R}(|z|)
. Observe that
w_{R}\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
and there are constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(z,w_{R})\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{1}R^{N}\mathrm{essinf}_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N-K}}F(z,u_{0})-C_{2}R^{N-1}\sup_{R\leq|u|\leq R+1}\mathrm{esssup}_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N-K}}|F(z,u)|

\displaystyle-C_{3}\sup_{1\leq|u|\leq 2}\mathrm{esssup}_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N-K}}|F(z,u)|.

Moreover,

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{z}w_{R}|^{2}\leq C_{4}R^{N-1}

for some constant
C_{4}>0
.
Then for sufficiently large
R>0
we conclude.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

First we prove that
{\mathcal{J}}
has the mountain pass geometry
[
2
,
23
]
. Let
w\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
as in Lemma
4.6
. Due to
(F2) and the embedding of
X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
into
L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
there exists
0<\rho<\|w\|
such that
\inf\{{\mathcal{J}}(u):u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
and
\|u\|=\rho\}>0
.
Moreover, for every
\lambda>0
we have

{\mathcal{J}}\bigl(w(\lambda\cdot,\cdot)\bigr)=\frac{1}{2\lambda^{K-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{y}w|^{2}+\frac{a}{r^{2}}|w|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{\lambda^{K}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{z}w|^{2}-F(z,w)\,dx\to-\infty

as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
. The existence of a Palais-Smale sequence
(u_{n})\subset X
for
{\mathcal{J}}|_{X_{{\mathcal{O}}}}
at the mountain pass level
c>0
follows. Such a sequence is bounded because (F5) holds.

Now, suppose by contradiction that (
4.1
) holds for every
R>0
. Fix
R>\sqrt{N-K}
such that (
4.1
) holds with the supremum being taken over
\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}
. Since
F
and
(z,u)\mapsto f(z,u)u
satisfy (
4.2
) uniformly with respect to
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N-K}
, arguing as in Lemmas
4.3
and
4.1
we obtain

c=\lim_{n}{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})u_{n}=\lim_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{2}f(z,u_{n})u_{n}-F(z,u_{n})\,dx=0,

which is a contradiction. It follows that there exist
R>\sqrt{N-K}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

(4.3)

\int_{B((0,z_{n}),R)}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx\geq\varepsilon

up to a subsequence, where
z_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}
maximizes
z\mapsto\int_{B((0,z),R)}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx
.
Since
{\mathcal{J}}
is invariant with respect to
\mathbb{Z}^{N-K}
translations, up to replacing
u_{n}
with
u_{n}(\cdot-z_{n})
we can suppose that
z_{n}=0
.
Since
(u_{n})
is bounded, there exists
u\in X_{{\mathcal{O}}}
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
X
, which in turn implies that
{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)=0
and that
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{2}\bigl(B(0,R)\bigr)
and a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
; in particular,
u\neq 0
because (
4.3
) holds.
∎

1.6

The proof follows from Theorems
1.4
,
1.5
, and
2.1
.

Notes on Fock space
Peter Tingley

Abstract
. These notes are intended as a fairly self-contained explanation of Fock space and various algebras that act on it, including a Clifford algebra, a Weyl algebra, an infinite rank matrix algebra, and an affine Kac-Moody algebra. We also discuss how the various algebras are related, and in particular describe the celebrated boson-fermion correspondence. We finish by briefly discussing a deformation of Fock space, which is a representation for the quantized universal enveloping algebra U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ^ ℓ ) U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{\ell}) .



1.
Introduction

The term Fock space comes from particle physics, where it is the state space for a system of a variable number of elementary particles. There are two distinct types of elementary particles, bosons and fermions, and their Fock spaces look quite different. Fermionic Fock spaces are naturally representations of a Clifford algebra, where the generators correspond to adding/removing a particle in a given pure energy state. Bosonic Fock space is naturally a representation of a Weyl algebra.

Here we focus on one example of this construction each for Bosons and Fermions. Our fermionic Fock space
{\bf F}
corresponds to a system of fermionic particles with pure energy states indexed by
\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
. Our space
{\bf B}^{(0)}
corresponds to a system of bosons with pure energy states indexed by
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and our full bosonic Fock space
{\bf B}
is a superposition of
\mathbb{Z}
shifted copies of
{\bf B}^{(0)}
. There is a natural embedding of the Weyl algebra for
{\bf B}^{(0)}
into a completion of the Clifford algebra for
{\bf F}
, which leads to the celebrated boson-fermion correspondence.

We begin by discussing
{\bf F}
and
{\bf B}
as vector spaces. We present various ways of indexing the standard basis of
{\bf F}
, and describe the boson-fermion correspondence as a vector space isomorphism between
{\bf F}
and
{\bf B}
. Since
{\bf F}
and
{\bf B}
are both vector spaces with countable bases it is immediate that they are isomorphic, the isomorphism we present is only interesting in that it has nice properties with respect to the actions of the Clifford and Weyl algebras. A more intuitive development of the theory (and also how it is presented in
[
Kac
, Chapter 14]
) is probably to first notice the relationship between the Clifford and Weyl algebras, then derive the corresponding relationship between the vector spaces. However, we find it useful to first have the map explicitly described in an elementary way.

We next discuss the various algebras that naturally act on Fock space, and how these actions are related. These include an infinite rank matrix algebra and various affine Kac-Moody algebras, as well as the Weyl and Clifford algebras. We finish by presenting the Misra-Miwa action of the quantized universal enveloping algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{\ell})
on
{\bf F}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[q,q^{-1}].
Understanding the relationship between the actions of these various algebras on
{\bf F}
has proven very useful, see e.g.
[
Kac
, Chapter 14]
. It is really this representation theory that we are interested in, not the physics motivations. There will be very little physics beyond this introduction.

These notes are intended to be quite self-contained in the sense that they should be comprehensible independent of other references on Fock space. We do however refer to other sources for some important proofs. Much of our presentation loosely follows
[
Kac
, Chapter 14]
. Alternate references include
[
DJM
,
KRR
]
.

2.
Fock space as a vector space

Fermionic Fock space
{\bf F}
is an infinite dimensional vector space. It has a standard basis, which can be indexed by a variety of objects. In this section we discuss indexings by Maya diagrams, charged partitions, and normally ordered wedge products.
Bosonic Fock space
{\bf B}
is essentially a space of polynomials in infinitely many variables. This has a standard basis consisting of character polynomials, which are related to Schur symmetric functions.
Schur symmetric functions are indexed by partitions, which leads to a natural bijection between the standard bases of
{\bf F}
and
{\bf B}
, and hence a vector space isomorphism.

2A.
Maya diagrams

Definition 2.1
.

A
Maya diagram
is a placement of a white or black bead at each position in
\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
, subject to the condition that all but finitely many positions
m<0
have a black bead and all but finitely many positions
m>0
have a white bead. For instance,

It is sometimes convenient to think of the black beads as “filled positions” and the white beads as “empty positions” in a “Dirac sea.”
Note that we label positions from right to left.
This is to match the conventions for normally-ordered semi-infinite wedges, where the indices decrease to the right (see §
2C
and
[
Kac
, §14.9]
).

2B.
Charged partitions

Definition 2.2
.

A
charged partition
is a pair
(\lambda,h)
consisting of a partition
\lambda
and an integer
h
(the charge).

There is a natural bijection between Maya diagrams and charged partitions: draw a line above the Maya diagram by placing a segment sloping down and to the right over every white bead, and a segment sloping up and to the right over every black bead. See Figure
1
. The result is the outer boundary of a partition, where by convention the parts of the partition are the lengths of the “rows” of boxes going up and to the left. The charge is determined by superimposing axes with the origin above position
0
in the Maya diagram, and so that far to the right the axis follows the diagram. The charge is the signed distance between the diagram and the axis far to the left. Equivalently, it is the number under the vertex of the partition.

Figure 1.
The bijection between Maya diagrams and charged partitions from §
2B
. The black beads in the Maya diagram correspond to the positions where the rim of the partition slopes up and to the right. The parts of
\lambda
are the lengths of all the finite “rows” of boxes sloping up at to the left, so here
\lambda=(4,3,3,1,1)
. The charge is the horizontal position of the vertex of the partition, so here the charge is
h=-1
. Later on in §
3E
we will choose a “level”
\ell
and color the squares of
\lambda
with
c_{\bar{i}}
for residues
\bar{i}
mod
\ell
, where the color of a box
b
is the position of that box in the horizontal direction mod
\ell
.

2C.
Semi-infinite wedges

Definition 2.3
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
be the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space with basis
\{e_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A semi-infinite wedge is a (formal) infinite wedge
e_{m_{1}}\wedge e_{m_{2}}\wedge e_{m_{3}}\wedge\cdots
. This can be thought of as living inside a formal semi-infinite wedge space
V_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\wedge V_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\wedge V_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\cdots
.

As usual, “wedge” is anti-commutative. So, for example,

e_{2.5}\wedge e_{0.5}\wedge e_{-1.5}\wedge e_{-2.5}\cdots=-e_{0.5}\wedge e_{2.5}\wedge e_{-1.5}\wedge e_{-2.5}\cdots

Definition 2.5
.

A semi-infinite wedge
e_{m_{1}}\wedge e_{m_{2}}\wedge e_{m_{3}}\wedge\cdots
is called
normally ordered
if
m_{1}>m_{2}>m_{3}>\cdots
.

Definition 2.6
.

A semi-infinite wedge
e_{m_{1}}\wedge e_{m_{2}}\wedge e_{m_{3}}\wedge\cdots
is called
regular
if
m_{k+1}=m_{k}-1
for all sufficiently large
k
.

There is a bijection between regular normally ordered semi-infinite wedges and Maya diagrams which takes
e_{m_{1}}\wedge e_{m_{2}}\wedge\cdots
to the Maya diagram with black beads exactly in positions
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots
. For instance,

(1)

e_{2.5}\wedge e_{0.5}\wedge e_{-0.5}\wedge e_{-3.5}\wedge e_{-4.5}\wedge e_{-6.5}\wedge e_{-7.5}\wedge e_{-8.5}\cdots

corresponds to the Maya diagram shown in Definition
2.1
and Figure
1
.

2D.
Fermionic Fock space
{\bf F}

The fermionic Fock space
{\bf F}
is the
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space with basis indexed by any one of the following:

(i)

Maya Diagrams.

(ii)

Charged partitions.

(iii)

Regular normally-ordered semi-infinite wedges.

As in §
2B
and §
2C
these three sets are naturally in bijection, and we take the point of view that they all index the same basis of
{\bf F}
. We most often refer to a basis element using the corresponding charged partition, using the notation
|\lambda,h\rangle
to denote the standard basis element of
F
corresponding to the charged partition
(\lambda,h)
.

The
charge

h
part of
{\bf F}
is

{\bf F}^{(h)}:=span\{\text{charged partitions with charge }h\}.

In particular, ordinary partitions index a basis for
{\bf F}^{(0)}
.
As a vector space,
{\bf F}=\oplus_{h\in\mathbb{Z}}{\bf F}^{(h)}
.

2E.
Bosonic Fock space
{\bf B}

The
bosonic Fock space

{\bf B}
is

{\bf B}:=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots;q,q^{-1}].

The
charge

h
part of
{\bf B}
is

{\bf B}^{(h)}:=q^{h}\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots].

As vector spaces,
{\bf B}=\oplus_{h\in\mathbb{Z}}{\bf B}^{(h)}
.

2F.
The boson-fermion correspondence as a map of vector spaces

Definition 2.7
.

Fix a partition
\lambda
. A
column-strict filling
of
\lambda
is a function
t
from the set of boxes in the diagram of
\lambda
to
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
which is weakly increasing along rows and strictly increasing along columns. See Figure
2
.

Figure 2.
A column-strict filling of
\lambda=(4,2,2,1)
. The function
t
takes
b
to the integer in box
b
. Recall that “rows” slope up and to the left, and “columns” slope up and to the right.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition. The
Schur symmetric function

s_{\lambda}
in infinitely many variables
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots
corresponding to
\lambda
is

(2)

s_{\lambda}({\bf y}):=\sum_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}t\text{ a column strict}\\
\text{filling of }\lambda\end{array}}\prod_{\text{boxes }b\text{ of }\lambda}y_{t(b)}.

Comment 2.9
.

The Schur symmetric functions are symmetric in the sense that they are invariant under permutations of the variables
y_{i}
(this can be shown using combinatorics
[
Sta
, Theorem 7.10.2]
, or by appealing to representation theory
[
Mac
, §I3)]
). They are polynomials in the sense that, if all but finitely many of the variables are set to 0, the result is a polynomial. They have many important properties, most of which arise because (once all but the first
n
variables are set to 0) they are the characters of irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

Definition 2.10
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, let
p_{k}=y_{1}^{k}+y_{2}^{k}+\cdots
be the
k^{th}
power symmetric function.

Definition 2.11
.

The character polynomial
\chi_{\lambda}
is the unique polynomial such that

\chi_{\lambda}(p_{1},\frac{1}{2}p_{2},\frac{1}{3}p_{3},\frac{1}{4}p_{4}\ldots)=s_{\lambda}.

Comment 2.12
.

To find
\chi_{\lambda}
, one may set
y_{j}=0
for all
j
larger than the longest column of
\lambda
. As well,
\chi_{\lambda}
cannot depend on
p_{k}
for any
k
bigger than the number of boxes in
\lambda
(since
s_{\lambda}
is homogeneous of degree
<k
), so finding any given
\chi_{\lambda}
is a finite problem.

Proposition 2.13
.

There is an isomorphism of vector spaces
\sigma:{\bf F}\rightarrow{\bf B}
given by

\sigma(|\lambda,h\rangle)=q^{h}\chi_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)

for any charged partition
(\lambda,h)
.
This restricts to an isomorphism
{\bf F}^{(h)}\rightarrow B^{(h)}
for all
h\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

As in
[
Mac
, §I3]
, Schur polynomials are a basis for the space of all symmetric functions in infinitely many variables, from which it follows that
\{q^{h}\chi_{\lambda}\}
is a basis for
{\bf B}
. By definition, the set of all charged partitions is a basis for
{\bf F}
. Thus each restriction
\sigma:{\bf F}^{(h)}\rightarrow{\bf B}^{(h)}
is a vector space isomorphism, and so is the full map
\sigma:{\bf F}^{(h)}\rightarrow{\bf B}^{(h)}
.
∎

2G.
The inner product

It is often convenient to put an inner product on
{\bf F}
, where we declare
\{|\lambda,h\rangle\}
to be an orthonormal basis.

3.
Fock space as a representation

3A.
{\bf F}
as a representation of a Clifford algebra

The Clifford algebra is the associative algebra
{\mathbb{C}l}
generated by
\psi_{m},\psi^{*}_{m}
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
subject to the relations

(3)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}\psi_{m}+\psi_{m}\psi_{n}=0,

(4)

\displaystyle\psi^{*}_{n}\psi^{*}_{m}+\psi^{*}_{m}\psi_{n}^{*}=0,

(5)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}\psi^{*}_{m}+\psi^{*}_{m}\psi_{n}=\delta_{m,n}.

One can check that
{\mathbb{C}l}
acts on Fock space as follows: Use the description of
{\bf F}
in terms of semi-infinite wedges from §
2C
. For
m\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
and
v
a regular semi-infinite wedge, define

(6)

\displaystyle\psi_{m}\cdot v=e_{m}\wedge v.

The generator
\psi_{n}^{*}
acts on
{\bf F}
as the adjoint of
\psi_{n}^{*}
with respect to the inner product from §
2G
. Explicitly, for
v
a regular semi-infinite wedge,

(7)

\psi_{m}^{*}\cdot v=\begin{cases}0\quad\text{ if }e_{m}\text{ does not appear as a factor in }v,\\
v^{\prime}\quad\text{if }v\text{ can be expressed as }e_{m}\wedge v^{\prime}\text{ for a wedge }v^{\prime}.\end{cases}

One can obtain any regular semi-infinite wedge
v
from any other by applying a finite number of operators
\psi_{m}
and
\psi_{m}^{*}
. Also, given any linear combination of regular semi-infinite wedges, one can project onto a single term by applying enough
\psi_{n}\psi_{n}^{*}
and
\psi^{*}_{n}\psi_{n}
. Together this implies that
{\bf F}
is irreducible as a representation of
{\mathbb{C}l}
.

Definition 3.1
.

\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}l}}
is the completion of
{\mathbb{C}l}
in the topology generated by the open sets
X+{\mathbb{C}l}\psi_{m}^{*}
and
X+{\mathbb{C}l}\psi_{-m}
for all
X\in{\mathbb{C}l}
,
m>0
. Explicitly, an element of
\widetilde{\mathbb{C}l}
is an infinite sum

(8)

z+\sum_{m<N}X_{m}\psi_{m}+\sum_{m>M}Y_{m}\psi^{*}_{m},

where
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
N,M\in\mathbb{Z}
, and all
X_{m}
,
Y_{m}
are elements of
{\mathbb{C}l}
.

The completion
\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}l}}
acts in a well-defined way on
{\bf F}
, since, for each regular semi-infinite wedge
v
, all but finitely many of
\psi^{*}_{m},\psi_{-m}
for
m>0
act by 0 on
v
.

Comment 3.2
.

When it seems prudent to distinguish
X\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}l}}
from its action of
{\bf F}
, we use the notation
\pi_{\bf F}(X)
to denote the action.

3B.
{\bf B}
as a representation of a Weyl algebra

Definition 3.3
.

The infinite dimensional Heisenberg algebra
{\mathbb{H}}
is generated by
\alpha_{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}
and a central element
c
subject to the relations:

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k}\alpha_{j}=j\delta_{j,-k}c.

Definition 3.4
.

The Weyl algebra
\mathcal{W}
is

\mathcal{W}:={\mathbb{H}}/(c-1).

Proposition 3.5
.

The Weyl algebra
\mathcal{W}
acts on
{\bf B}
by the follows formulas. Each
B^{(h)}
is preserved under this action and forms an irreducible representation for
\mathcal{W}
.

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\rightarrow\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}

\displaystyle\text{ if }k>0,

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\rightarrow\text{ multiplication by }-kx_{-k}

\displaystyle\text{ if }k<0.

Proof.

A direct calculation shows that, defining the actions of
\alpha_{k}
and
\alpha_{-k}
as above and allowing
c
to act as multiplication by
1
, the defining relations in Definition
3.3
hold. So this gives an action of
\mathcal{W}
on
{\bf B}
. Since none of these operators affect the power of
q
in any monomial each
B^{(h)}
is preserved.
To see that each
B^{(h)}
is irreducible as a
\mathcal{W}
module, notice that

•

Any non-zero
p\in B^{(h)}
can be brought to a non-zero multiple of
q^{h}
by applying a sequence of partial derivatives, which are the actions of
\alpha_{k}
for
k>0
.

•

Any monomial in
B^{(h)}
can be constructed from
q^{h}
by applying a sequence off multiplications by the generators which, up to scalar, are the actions of
\alpha_{k}
for
k<0
.

∎

Comment 3.6
.

When it seems prudent to distinguish
Y\in\mathcal{W}
from its action of
{\bf B}
we use the notation
\pi_{\bf B}(Y)
to denote the action.

3C.
The boson-fermion correspondence

Proposition
2.13
gives an isomorphism of vector spaces
\sigma:{\bf F}\rightarrow{\bf B}
. This isomorphism was chosen because it reveals an important relationship between the algebras
{\mathbb{C}l}
and
\mathcal{W}
, which is known as the boson-fermion correspondence. We now explain that relationship, referring to
[
Kac
, Chapter 14]
for rigorous proofs.

Definition 3.7
.

For
Y\in\mathcal{W}
, let
\pi_{\bf F}(Y)=\sigma^{-1}\circ\pi_{\bf B}(Y)\circ\sigma
. That is,
\pi_{\bf F}(Y)
is the operator on
{\bf F}
induced from
\pi_{\bf B}(Y)
by the vector space isomorphism
\sigma:{\bf F}\rightarrow{\bf B}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

(see
[
Kac
, §14.10]
)
For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}

\displaystyle\pi_{\bf F}(\alpha_{k})=\pi_{\bf F}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{m}\psi^{*}_{m+k}.

In

\displaystyle\sum_{m>0}\psi_{m}\psi^{*}_{m}-\sum_{m<0}\psi^{*}_{m}\psi_{m}

Comment 3.15

It may seem unfortunate that we need to introduce the power series (

)-(

), but these are in fact very useful. Each acts on any

to give a Laurent power series in
z
with coefficients in
{\bf F}
, so each of the products below is well-defined as a map from
{\bf F}
to Laurent power series in two variables with coefficients in
{\bf F}
. The following commutation relations follow from results in
[
Kac
, Chapter 14]
(see also
[
OR2
, Appendix B2]
).

(16)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(x)\Gamma_{-}(y)=(1-xy)\Gamma_{-}(y)\Gamma_{+}(x)

(17)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(x)\psi(z)=(1-z^{-1}x)^{-1}\psi(z)\Gamma_{+}(x)

(18)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(x)\psi(z)=(1-xz)^{-1}\psi(z)\Gamma_{-}(x)

(19)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(x)\psi^{*}(z)=(1-z^{-1}x)\psi^{*}(z)\Gamma_{+}(x)

(20)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(x)\psi^{*}(z)=(1-xz)\psi^{*}(z)\Gamma_{-}(x).

Here
(1-a)^{-1}
is always expanded as
1+a+a^{2}+\cdots
. Equality is interpreted as saying that, once each side is applied to any fixed
v\in{\bf F}
, all coefficients of the resulting power series agree. These facts have been put to great use. For example, in
[
OR1
]
Okounkov and Reshetikhin interpret the
\Gamma_{\pm}(z)
as transition functions, and use the above commutation relations to find limit shapes and correlation functions for systems of random plane partitions.

Definition 3.16
.

A Lie-associative map from a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
to an associative algebra
A
is a map
\sigma
such that, for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\sigma([X,Y])=\sigma(X)\sigma(Y)-\sigma(Y)\sigma(X)
.

An action of a Lie algebra
L
on a space
V
is a map
L\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
which preserves the Lie bracket, which is the same thing as a Lie-Associative map from
L
to
\mathrm{End}(V)
. More generally, if an associative algebra
A
acts on
V
, then any Lie-Associative map
L\rightarrow A
induces an acton of
L
on
V
. In the following we use this framework to construct actions of various Lie algebras on

by finding Lie-Associative maps from these algebras to

Definition 3.17
.

M_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
is the algebra of matrices with rows and columns indexed by
\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
, in which all but finitely many entries are 0. Let
E_{m,n}
denote the matrix with a single 1 in position
(m,n)
and zeros everywhere else.

Definition 3.18
.

\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
is the Lie algebra corresponding to
M_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
. That is,
\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
is equal to
M_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
as a vector space, and the Lie bracket is defined by

[X,Y]=XY-YX.

Proposition 3.19
.

(see
[
Kac
, §14.9]
)
There is a Lie-associative embedding of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
into
{\mathbb{C}l}
given by

E_{m,n}\rightarrow\psi_{m}\psi^{*}_{n}
, and hence an action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
on
{\bf F}
.

Example 3.20
.

Both
\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
and
{\mathbb{C}l}
are associative algebras, so one might hope that the map in Proposition
3.19
was a map of associative algebra. However,

E_{12}E_{31}=0\quad\text{but}\quad\psi_{1}\psi_{2}^{*}\psi_{3}\psi_{1}^{*}=\psi_{1}\psi_{3}\psi_{1}^{*}\psi_{2}^{*}\neq 0.

Working with these as Lie algebras though,

E_{12}E_{31}-E_{31}E_{12}=-E_{32}

and

\psi_{1}\psi_{2}^{*}\psi_{3}\psi_{1}^{*}-\psi_{3}\psi_{1}^{*}\psi_{1}\psi_{2}^{*}=\psi_{2}^{*}\psi_{3}\psi_{1}\psi_{1}^{*}+\psi_{2}^{*}\psi_{3}\psi_{1}^{*}\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}^{*}\psi_{3}(\psi_{1}\psi_{1}^{*}+\psi_{1}^{*}\psi_{1})=\psi_{2}^{*}\psi_{3}=-\psi_{3}\psi_{2}^{*},

and the map respects the bracket. One can prove Proposition
3.19
by checking relations like this in more generality.

We would like to extend the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
on
{\bf F}
from Proposition
3.19
to a larger algebra that contains some matrices with infinitely many non-zero entries. To do this, we will need to first take a central extension. The reason for this extension is explained in Comment

once we’ve defined a few things.

Definition 3.21
.

(see
[
Kac
, §7.12]
)
Let
\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}^{c}
be the central extension of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
by a central element
c
with
Lie bracket defined as follows. We use the notation
\overline{X}
to mean the matrix
X
thought of as an element of
\mathfrak{gl}^{c}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle[\overline{E}_{m,n},\overline{E}_{p,q}]=\begin{cases}\overline{E_{m,n}E_{p,q}-E_{p,q}E_{m,n}}+\delta_{m,q}\delta_{n,p}c\quad\text{ if }m<0\text{ and }n>0,\\

\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}

Comment 3.22
.

This central extension is trivial since there is an isomorphism of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{gl}^{c}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\oplus\mathbb{C}c
given by

\overline{E}_{m,n}\mapsto\begin{cases}E_{m,n}\qquad\quad\text{ if }m\neq n\text{ or }m>0\\
E_{m,n}-c\quad\ \text{ if }m=n\text{ and }m<0.\end{cases}

Proposition 3.23
.

There is a Lie-associative imbedding of
\mathfrak{gl}^{c}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
into
{\mathbb{C}l}
defined by

(21)

\displaystyle\overline{E}_{m,n}

\displaystyle\mapsto\psi_{m}\psi^{*}_{n}\quad\text{ if }m\neq n\text{ or }m>0,

(22)

\displaystyle\overline{E}_{n,n}

\displaystyle\mapsto-\psi^{*}_{n}\psi_{n}\quad\text{ if }n<0,

(23)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle\rightarrow 1.

In particular, the action of

on

\mathfrak{gl}^{c}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}

{\bf F}

Consider the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\oplus\mathbb{C}c
on
{\bf F}
by allowing
\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
to act via the embedding from Proposition

or

is immediate. For

is sent to

, which acts as

∎

Definition 3.24
.

The Lie algebra

\widetilde{\mathfrak{gl}}_{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}
consists of all infinite matrices

Definition 3.25

Proposition 3.26

The imbedding of

3E.
{\bf F}

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{\ell}
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{\ell}

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{\ell}^{\prime}

Definition 3.29
.

Let
\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell}
be the Lie algebra of
\ell\times\ell

the Lie algebra of all
\ell\times\ell

and zeros everywhere else (this is to distinguish

Definition 3.30
.

The affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{\ell}
is, as a vector space
(\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}])\oplus\mathbb{C}c\oplus\mathbb{C}d

(25)

\displaystyle[X\otimes t^{n},Y\otimes t^{m}]

\displaystyle=(XY-YX)\otimes t^{m+n}+n\delta_{n,-m}\mathrm{tr}(XY)c,

(26)

\displaystyle[d,X\otimes t^{n}]

\displaystyle=nX\otimes t^{n},

(27)

\displaystyle c

is central.

(which is also a renormalization of the Killing form).

Definition 3.31

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{\ell}^{\prime}
is the derived algebra of
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{\ell}

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}^{\prime}_{\ell}=(\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}])\oplus\mathbb{C}c

Proposition 3.32
.

(see
[
Kac
, Chapter 7.4 and Exercise 7.11]

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{\ell}
is isomorphic to the affine Kac-Moody algebra with the
\ell

is generated by Chevalley generators
\tilde{E}_{\bar{i}},\tilde{F}_{\bar{i}}
for

(28)

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\bar{i}}\rightarrow X_{i,i+1}\otimes t^{0}\quad i=1,\ldots\ell-1

(29)

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\bar{i}}\rightarrow X_{i+1,i}\otimes t^{0}\quad i=1,\ldots\ell-1

(30)

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\bar{0}}\rightarrow X_{\ell,1}\otimes t^{1}

(31)

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\bar{0}}\rightarrow X_{1,\ell}\otimes t^{-1}.

Proposition 3.33
.

(see
[
Kac
, Chapter 14]

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

A Nečas-Lions inequality with symmetric gradients on star-shaped domains based on a first order Babuška-Aziz inequality
Michele Botti, Lorenzo Mascotto

Abstract
We prove a Nečas-Lions inequality with symmetric gradients on two and three dimensional domains of diameter R R that are star-shaped with respect to a ball of radius ρ \rho ; the constants in the inequality are explicit with respect to R R and ρ \rho . Crucial tools in deriving this inequality are a first order Babuška-Aziz inequality based on Bogovskiĭ’s construction of a right-inverse of the divergence and Fourier transform techniques proposed by Durán. As a byproduct, we derive arbitrary order estimates in arbitrary dimension for that operator. AMS subject classification : 35C05; 35C15; 35S05; 42B20; 42B37; 53A45 Keywords : Babuška-Aziz inequality; Nečas-Lions inequality; inf-sup condition; Bogovskiĭ operator; symmetric tensor.

Introduction

We derive a Nečas-Lions inequality with symmetric gradients
on star-shaped domains in two and three dimensions;
first order Babuška-Aziz and (vector) Nečas-Lions inequalities are crucial tools
to show estimates that are explicit
with respect to certain geometric quantities of the domain.
As a byproduct, we also derive explicit arbitrary order
Babuška-Aziz inequalities in arbitrary dimension.

1.1
The lowest order Babuška-Aziz inequality

The standard, lowest order version of the Babuška-Aziz inequality
was proven as early as 1961 by Cattabriga
[
10
]
.
However, the name is associated to the authors
of the later work
[
5
]

and was assigned by Horgan and Payne
[
20
]
;
see also
[
12
]
.

The inequality reads as follows:
there exists a positive constant
C_{\mathcal{BA},0}
such that
for any
f
in
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

one can construct
\mathbf{u}
in
[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n}
satisfying

\div\mathbf{u}=f,\qquad\qquad\qquad{\left|{\mathbf{u}}\right|}_{1,\Omega}\leq C_{\mathcal{BA},0}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}.

(4)

The subscript in
C_{\mathcal{BA},0}
relates to Babuška-Aziz.
Explicit constructions of a vector field
\mathbf{u}

as in (
4
)
may be performed in different ways;
here, we shall follow the approach by
Bogovskiĭ in
[
6
,
7
]
,
where he showed a particular construction of the right inverse
of the divergence based on integral kernels.
Alternative avenues, which give less information on the constant
C_{\mathcal{BA},0}

and construct less smooth right-inverses of the divergence,
are based on solving curl-div, diffusion, or Stokes problems;
see, e.g.,
[
4
]
and
[
9
, Lemma 11.2.3]
;
as such, higher order estimates based on this approach
require extra regularity assumptions on the boundary of the domain,
which are instead not needed following Bogovskiĭ’s approach.

Minimal literature on the lowest order Babuška-Aziz inequality.

The literature associated with inequality (
4
) is widespread.
We refer to
[
16
, pp. 227-228]
,
[
19
]
,
and
[
12
]
for a thorough historical review.

Here, we only mention that the divergence problem in (
4
)
was raised as early as in 1961 by Cattabriga
[
10
]
;
see also the later works
[
21
,
24
]
.
Bogovskiĭ introduced an explicit representation for
\mathbf{v}
solving (
4
)
in
[
6
,
7
]
.
Several references discuss the validity of similar estimates;
for instance, implicit constants for general Lipschitz domains are available
in
[
8
, Theorems 2.4 and 2.9]
.
The case of negative Sobolev norms is described in
[
17
]
.

The explicit dependence of the constant
C_{\mathcal{BA},0}
on geometric parameters of
\Omega

is studied in fewer references;
see
[
1
]
for a list.
For
R
and
\rho
as in (
3
),
Galdi
[
16
]
gives estimates of the form

C_{\mathcal{BA},0}\lesssim_{n}\left(\frac{R}{\rho}\right)^{n+1}.

(5)

The main tool in the analysis is the one
discussed originally by Bogovskiĭ
[
6
,
7
]
,
i.e., the Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator theory.

Improved estimates of the form

C_{\mathcal{BA},0}\lesssim_{n}\frac{R}{\rho}\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{|B_{\rho}|}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}\left(\log\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{|B_{\rho}|}\right)^{\frac{n}{2(n-1)}}

(6)

were proven by Durán
[
14
]
based on the properties
of the Fourier transform.
In the same reference, it is shown that the estimates
are optimal up to the logarithmic factor for
n=2
.
More precisely, a 2D counterexample is exhibited showing that
the following holds true:

C_{\mathcal{BA},0}\gtrsim\frac{R}{\rho}.

For the two dimensional case,
Costabel and Dauge
[
12
, Theorem 2.3]
proved
that the logarithmic factor in Durán’s estimates can be removed
again for
n=2
.

Minimal literature on higher order Babuška-Aziz inequalities.

Higher order Babuška-Aziz inequalities are far less investigated.
They are stated in the original paper by Bogovskiĭ
[
6
]

without mention on the behaviour of the constants.
Galdi
[
16
, Remark III.3.2]

claims that similar bounds to the lowest order case can be derived;
however, no explicit constants are given in that case sa well;
the analysis hinges upon the Calderón-Zygmund theory.
Costabel and McIntosh prove arbitrary order estimates
[
13
]

without explicit dependence on the geometry on the domain.
Guzmán and Salgado
[
19
]

prove an explicit first order generalised Poincaré inequality,
which is related to Bogovskiĭ’s operator
without imposition of boundary conditions,
and give a road map on how to prove higher order explicit estimates;
tools as those in
[
14
]
are employed;
no estimates are given for Bogovskiĭ’s operator.

1.2
The lowest order Nečas-Lions inequality

The standard, lowest order Nečas-Lions inequality
is a very well known result in the theory of Sobolev spaces.
It is proven in the book by Nečas
[
24
, Lemma 3.7.1]
;
the connection to the name of Lions is less clear,
and is probably due to
[
22
, Note 27, page 320]
,
where the result is mentioned as a private communication
by Lions himself to Magenes and Stampacchia, yet without an explicit proof.

Given
\Pi^{0}:L^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
the average operator over
\Omega
,
the inequality reads as follows:
there exists a positive constant
C_{\mathcal{NL},0}
such that

{\left\|{f-\Pi^{0}f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\leq C_{\mathcal{NL},0}{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}\qquad\qquad\forall f\in L^{2}(\Omega).

(7)

The subscript in
C_{\mathcal{NL},0}
relates to Nečas-Lions.
An equivalent statement for (
7
)
is that the following constants are bounded from above
and below, respectively:

C_{\mathcal{NL},0}:=\sup_{f\in L^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{{\left\|{f-\Pi^{0}f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}{{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}},\qquad\qquad C_{\mathcal{NL},0}^{-1}:=\inf_{f\in L^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}}{{\left\|{f-\Pi^{0}f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}.

(8)

The constants
C_{\mathcal{BA},0}
and
C_{\mathcal{NL},0}

in (
4
) and (
7
) are related
to constants in other relevant inequalities in Sobolev spaces
as well, including the standard inf-sup constant
\beta_{0}

defined as

\inf_{f\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n}}\frac{(\div\mathbf{u},f)_{0,\Omega}}{{\left\|{\mathbf{u}}\right\|}_{1,\Omega}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}=:\beta_{0}.

(9)

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
C_{\mathcal{BA},0}
,
C_{\mathcal{NL},0}
, and
\beta_{0}
be given
in (
4
), (
7
),
and (
9
).
Then, the following holds true:

C_{\mathcal{BA},0}\geq C_{\mathcal{NL},0}=\beta_{0}^{-1}.

Proof.

For all
f
in
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, the definition
of negative norms in (
2
) implies

\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n}}\frac{(\div\mathbf{u},f)_{0,\Omega}}{{\left\|{\mathbf{u}}\right\|}_{1,\Omega}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}=\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n}}\frac{{}_{-1}\langle\nabla f,\mathbf{u}\rangle_{1}}{{\left\|{\mathbf{u}}\right\|}_{1,\Omega}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}=\frac{{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}}{{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}.

We take the
\inf
over all possible
f
in
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
,
use (
8
),
and deduce that
\beta_{0}=C_{\mathcal{NL},0}^{-1}
.

On the other hand, for all
f
in
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
,
we can consider a specific
\mathbf{u}
satisfying (
4
),
which gives

\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n}}\frac{(\div\mathbf{u},f)_{0,\Omega}}{{\left\|{\mathbf{u}}\right\|}_{1,\Omega}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}\geq\frac{{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}^{2}}{{\left\|{\mathbf{u}}\right\|}_{1,\Omega}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}\geq C_{\mathcal{BA},0}^{-1}.

Taking the inf over all possible
f
in
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

and recalling the standard inf-sup condition (
9
)
give
\beta_{0}\geq C_{\mathcal{BA},0}^{-1}
.
The assertion follows.
∎

Since an upper bound on
C_{\mathcal{BA},0}
is available from Lemma
4
,
which is explicit in terms of
n
,
R
, and
\rho
as in (
3
),
then Proposition
1.1

implies an upper bound for
C_{\mathcal{NL},0}

and a lower bound for
\beta_{0}

with the same explicit dependence.
The relation with constants appearing in other inequalities
is discussed, amongst others,
in
[
2
,
11
,
12
,
20
]
.

1.5
Main result
3

The spaces

in two and three dimensions have

3

\mathbb{RM}(\Omega):=\begin{cases}\left\{\mathbf{r}(x)=\boldsymbol{\alpha}+b\mathbf{(}x_{2},-x_{1})^{\mathrm{T}}\text{ for any }\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;b\in\mathbb{R}\right\}&\text{ in $2$D}\\
\left\{\mathbf{r}(x)=\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})^{\mathrm{T}}\text{ for any }\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\right\}&\text{ in $3$D}.\end{cases}

(17)

Introduce the spaces

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}:=\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mid\langle\boldsymbol{\tau}\ \mathbf{n},\boldsymbol{w}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=0\quad\forall\boldsymbol{w}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{n}\};\qquad\qquad\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}:=\{\mathbf{v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{n}\mid\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{\mathbb{RM}}\mathbf{v}=0\}.

(20)

Proposition 1.5
.

Let

Then, the inf-sup condition (

Proof.

For all tensors
\boldsymbol{\tau}
, let
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{S}

denote its symmetric part.
We have

\begin{split}\beta^{*}_{0}&=\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}}\ \sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}}\frac{(\divbf\boldsymbol{\tau},\mathbf{v})_{0,\Omega}}{{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\right\|}_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}{\left\|{\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}\geq\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}}\ \sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\cap[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n\times n}}\frac{(\divbf\boldsymbol{\tau},\mathbf{v})_{0,\Omega}}{{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\right\|}_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}{\left\|{\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}\\
&\overset{\text{IBP}}{=}\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}}\ \sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\cap[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n\times n}}\frac{{}_{-1}\langle\nabla\mathbf{v},\boldsymbol{\tau}\rangle_{1}}{{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\right\|}_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}{\left\|{\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}\overset{\eqref{symmetric-tensors-property}}{=}\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}}\ \sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\cap[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n\times n}}\frac{{}_{-1}\langle\nabla_{S}\mathbf{v},\boldsymbol{\tau}\rangle_{1}}{{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\right\|}_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}{\left\|{\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}\\

&\overset{\eqref{Poincare}}{\geq}\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}}\ \sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\cap[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n\times n}}\frac{{}_{-1}\langle\nabla_{S}\mathbf{v},\boldsymbol{\tau}\rangle_{1}}{(1+C_{P}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}R{\left|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\right|}_{1,\Omega}{\left\|{\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}\\
&=\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}}\ \sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n\times n}}\frac{{}_{-1}\langle\nabla_{S}\mathbf{v},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{S}\rangle_{1}}{(1+C_{P}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}R{\left|{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{S}}\right|}_{1,\Omega}{\left\|{\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}.\end{split}

Since
{\left|{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{S}}\right|}_{1,\Omega}\leq{\left|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\right|}_{1,\Omega}

for all tensors
\boldsymbol{\tau}

and the numerator involves
\nabla_{S}\mathbf{v}
,
we deduce the assertion:

\begin{split}\beta^{*}_{0}&\geq\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}}\ \sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n\times n}}\frac{{}_{-1}\langle\nabla_{S}\mathbf{v},\boldsymbol{\tau}\rangle_{1}}{(1+C_{P}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}R{\left|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\right|}_{1,\Omega}{\left\|{\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}\\
&\overset{\eqref{negative-norms}}{=}(1+C_{P}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}}\frac{{\left\|{\nabla_{S}\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}}{{\left\|{\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}}\overset{\eqref{Necas-Lions-symmetric-tensors}}{\geq}(1+C_{P}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}(C_{\mathcal{NL},0}^{*})^{-1}.\end{split}

∎

\displaystyle\partial^{3}_{x_{j}^{2}x_{\ell}}\varphi(x)=\rho^{-n-3}\partial^{3}_{x_{j}^{2}x_{\ell}}\varphi(\rho^{-1}x),\quad

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left\|{\partial^{3}_{x_{j}^{2}x_{\ell}}\varphi}\right\|}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\rho^{-3},\quad

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left\|{\partial^{3}_{x_{j}^{2}x_{\ell}}\varphi}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\rho^{-n-3}.

The definition of
\widetilde{T}_{k,j\ell,2}(y_{k}f)
,
the chain rule,
the inequality
|y_{k}|\leq R
,
and the fact that
\rho^{-1}\leq R\rho^{-2}
imply

\begin{split}{\left|{\mathbf{u}}\right|}_{2,\Omega}&\lesssim_{n}R\left[\rho^{-1}+\rho\ \rho^{-2}\right]{\left|{f}\right|}_{1,\Omega}+\left[\rho^{-1}+\rho\rho^{-2}\right]{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt+R[\rho^{-1}\rho^{-1}+\rho\rho^{-3}]{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}+R\frac{2^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\left(1-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}{\left|{\Omega}\right|}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}(\rho^{-2})^{\frac{p}{2}}(\rho^{-n-2})^{1-\frac{p}{2}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\\
&\lesssim R\left[\rho^{-1}{\left|{f}\right|}_{1,\Omega}+\rho^{-2}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}+\frac{2^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\left(1-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}{\left|{\Omega}\right|}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\rho^{-2-n\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\right]\\
&\lesssim_{n}\frac{R}{\rho}{\left|{f}\right|}_{1,\Omega}+\frac{R}{\rho^{2}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\left[1+\left(1-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{-\frac{p}{2}}{\left|{\Omega}\right|}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\rho^{-n\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}\right].\end{split}

(35)

We cope with part of the last coefficient on the right-hand side separately.
Using that

{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\rho^{-n\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}\approx 1,

we can write

\begin{split}&\left(1-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{-\frac{p}{2}}{\left|{\Omega}\right|}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\rho^{-n\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}=\left(1-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\rho^{-n\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}\\
&\approx\left(1-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{n-1}-p\right)}.\end{split}

(36)

For
{\left|{\Omega}\right|}/{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}
sufficiently large
(the ball
B_{\rho}
is anyhow fixed once and for all in the reference framework),
we choose
p
such that

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{n-1}-p\right)=\frac{1}{\log\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)}.

Equivalently, we pick
p
such that

\log\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{n-1}-p\right)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}=e^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{n-1}-p\right)}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{n-1}-p\right)}=e^{1}.

We plug the last identity in (
36
) and deduce

\left(1-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{-\frac{p}{2}}{\left|{\Omega}\right|}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\rho^{-n\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}\approx\left(1-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}e^{1}.

Going back to (
35
), we write

{\left|{\mathbf{u}}\right|}_{2,\Omega}\lesssim_{n}\frac{R}{\rho}{\left|{f}\right|}_{1,\Omega}+\frac{R}{\rho^{2}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\left[1+\left(1-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}\right].

We further note that

1-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}=\left(\frac{n-1}{p}\right)\left(\frac{n}{n-1}-p\right)=\frac{2(n-1)}{p\log\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)}.

We combine the two above displays:

{\left|{\mathbf{u}}\right|}_{2,\Omega}\lesssim_{n}\frac{R}{\rho}{\left|{f}\right|}_{1,\Omega}+\frac{R}{\rho^{2}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\left[1+\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}\frac{p^{\frac{p}{2}}}{2(n-1)^{\frac{p}{2}}}\log\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right].

Using
p<n/(n-1)
, the following quantity is uniformly bounded in
n
and thus in
p
:

\frac{p^{\frac{p}{2}}}{2(n-1)^{\frac{p}{2}}}.

Moreover, we know that
p/2<n/(2(n-1))
.
We deduce that

{\left|{\mathbf{u}}\right|}_{2,\Omega}\lesssim_{n}\frac{R}{\rho}{\left|{f}\right|}_{1,\Omega}+\frac{R}{\rho^{2}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\left[1+\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}\log\left(\frac{{\left|{\Omega}\right|}}{{\left|{B_{\rho}}\right|}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2(n-1)}}\right],

which is the assertion.

∎

Compared to the lowest order estimate (
6
),
we have an extra term involving the gradient of
f

and an extra
\rho^{-1}
scaling factor for the term involving
f
.

]
.

2.5
On the optimality of the estimates in Theorem
1.2

in 2D

We comment on the optimality of the estimates
in Theorem
1.2

for planar domains
based on a counterexample in
[
14
, Section 3]
.
Introduce the domain

\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}:=(-a,a)\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)

and the function

f(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be the solution to the divergence problem (
10
). We have

{\left\|{x_{1}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}x_{1}\div\mathbf{u}=-\int_{\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}\mathbf{u}_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}x_{2}^{2}\partial_{x_{2}^{2}}^{2}\mathbf{u}_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}{\left\|{x_{2}^{2}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}{\left\|{\partial_{x_{2}^{2}}^{2}\mathbf{u}_{1}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}.

We use estimates as in (
10
) for the last term on the right-hand side:
there exists positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
depending
on
R=2a
and
\rho=\varepsilon
such that

{\left\|{x_{1}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}{\left\|{x_{2}^{2}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}\left[C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{A}{\left\|{x_{1}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}+C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}{\left\|{1}\right\|}_{0,\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}\right].

We have

{\left\|{x_{1}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}^{2}=\frac{4}{3}a^{3}\varepsilon,\qquad\qquad{\left\|{x_{2}^{2}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}^{2}=\frac{4}{5}a\varepsilon^{5},\qquad\qquad{\left\|{1}\right\|}_{0,\Omega_{a,\varepsilon}}^{2}=4a\varepsilon.

Combining the two displays, we get

a^{3}\varepsilon\lesssim C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{A}a^{2}\varepsilon^{3}+C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}a\varepsilon^{3},

whence

1\lesssim C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{A}a^{-1}\varepsilon^{2}+C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}a^{-2}\varepsilon^{2}\approx C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{A}\frac{\rho^{2}}{R}+C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}\frac{\rho^{2}}{R^{2}}.

This inequality implies that
at least one of the following must hold true:

C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{A}\gtrsim\frac{R}{\rho^{2}},\qquad\qquad\qquad C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}\gtrsim\frac{R^{2}}{\rho^{2}}.

Using (
11
),
we deduce

\frac{R^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\lesssim C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}\lesssim\frac{R}{\rho},

which cannot be valid in general with hidden constants
independent of
R
and
\rho
as in (
3
).
This entails that

C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{A}\gtrsim\frac{R}{\rho^{2}},

i.e., the first bound in (
11
)
is optimal up to a logarithmic factor.

We are not able to infer a clear statement on the optimality of
C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}
from the bound above.
A heuristic argument based on scaling techniques, suggests
however that bound (
11
) on
C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}
should be also optimal.

3
Proof of a Nečas-Lions inequality with symmetric gradients

We prove Theorem
1.4
.
To this aim, we first show an auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be a sufficiently smooth vector field.
Then, the following identities hold true:

[\nabla(\nabla\times\mathbf{u})]^{T}=\nabla\times(\nabla\mathbf{u}+(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{T})=2\nabla\times(\nabla_{S}\mathbf{u}),

(37)

where, for a given tensor
\mathbf{A}
,

\nabla\times\mathbf{A}

denotes the matrix obtained by
applying the curl operator row-wise to
\mathbf{A}
.

Proof.

Direct computations give

[\nabla(\nabla\times\mathbf{u})]^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{x_{1}}(\partial_{x_{2}}\mathbf{u}_{3}-\partial_{x_{3}}\mathbf{u}_{2})&\partial_{x_{1}}(\partial_{x_{3}}\mathbf{u}_{1}-\partial_{x_{1}}\mathbf{u}_{3})&\partial_{x_{1}}(\partial_{x_{1}}\mathbf{u}_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}\mathbf{u}_{1})\\
\partial_{x_{2}}(\partial_{x_{2}}\mathbf{u}_{3}-\partial_{x_{3}}\mathbf{u}_{2})&\partial_{x_{2}}(\partial_{x_{3}}\mathbf{u}_{1}-\partial_{x_{1}}\mathbf{u}_{3})&\partial_{x_{2}}(\partial_{x_{1}}\mathbf{u}_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}\mathbf{u}_{1})\\
\partial_{x_{3}}(\partial_{x_{2}}\mathbf{u}_{3}-\partial_{x_{3}}\mathbf{u}_{2})&\partial_{x_{3}}(\partial_{x_{3}}\mathbf{u}_{1}-\partial_{x_{1}}\mathbf{u}_{3})&\partial_{x_{3}}(\partial_{x_{1}}\mathbf{u}_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}\mathbf{u}_{1})\end{bmatrix}.

Similarly, we may show that the above tensor coincides with

\nabla\times(\nabla\mathbf{u}+(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{T})
,
which gives the first identity in (
37
);
the second identity in (
37
)
is a consequence of the definition of the symmetric gradient.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.4
.
For any generic
\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}}
in the space
of rigid body motions
\mathbb{RM}(\Omega)\cap[L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n}

as discussed in Section
1.5
,
we have

{\left\|{\mathbf{v}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{\mathbb{RM}}\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\leq{\left\|{\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}})}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\qquad\qquad\forall\mathbf{v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{n}.

An immediate consequence of the standard, vector version,
lowest order Nečas-Lions
inequality (
7
) is that

{\left\|{\mathbf{v}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{\mathbb{RM}}\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\leq C_{\mathcal{NL},0}{\left\|{\nabla(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}})}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}\qquad\qquad\forall\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}}\in\mathbb{RM}(\Omega)\cap[L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n}.

(38)

We are left to prove the existence of a positive constant
C

with explicit dependence on
R
and
\rho
as in (
3
),
such that,
for a specific choice of
\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}}
,
the following inequality is valid:

{\left\|{\nabla(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}})}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}\leq C{\left\|{\nabla_{S}\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{-1}.

Using splitting (
1
)
of the gradient into symmetric and skew-symmetric parts,
and the triangle inequality entails

{\left\|{\nabla(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}})}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}\leq{\left\|{\nabla_{S}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}})}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}+{\left\|{\nabla_{SS}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}})}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}.

(39)

Since
\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}}
is a rigid body motion,

\nabla_{S}\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}}
is the zero tensor in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
:
the first term on the right-hand side is equal to
{\left\|{\nabla_{S}\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}
.
As for the second term on the right-hand side,
we define
A^{n\times n}
as the space of
(n\times n)
skew-symmetric matrices,

n=2,3
.
We take
\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}}
such that

{\left\|{\nabla_{SS}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}})}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}:=\inf_{\widetilde{\mathbf{q}}^{\text{RM}}\in\mathbb{RM}(\Omega)\cap[L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{n\times n}}{\left\|{\nabla_{SS}(\mathbf{v}-\widetilde{\mathbf{q}}^{\text{RM}})}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}=\inf_{\mathbf{c}\in A^{n\times n}}{\left\|{\nabla_{SS}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{c}}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}.

With this at hand, elementary computations give

{\left\|{\nabla_{SS}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{q}^{\text{RM}})}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}=\inf_{\mathbf{c}\in A^{n\times n}}{\left\|{\nabla_{SS}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{c}}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\inf_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n-3}}{\left\|{\nabla\times\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{c}}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}.

(40)

Note that the last two norms involve tensors and vectors, respectively.

An immediate consequence of
the scalar and vector versions of (
12
),
(
15
),
and (
16
) is that

\inf_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n-3}}{\left\|{\nabla\times\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{c}}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}\leq C_{\mathcal{NL},1}{\left\|{\nabla(\nabla\times\mathbf{v})}\right\|}_{-2,\Omega}\leq(C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{A}C_{P}R+C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}){\left\|{\nabla(\nabla\times\mathbf{v})}\right\|}_{-2,\Omega}.

Lemma
3.1
reveals that

\begin{split}\inf_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n-3}}{\left\|{\nabla\times\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{c}}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}&\leq 2(C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{A}C_{P}R+C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}){\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\times}\nabla_{S}\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{-2,\Omega}\\
&\leq 2(C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{A}C_{P}R+C_{\mathcal{BA},1}^{B}){\left\|{\nabla_{S}\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{-1,\Omega}.\end{split}

(41)

Combining (
38
), (
39
),
(
40
), and (
41
)
yields the assertion.

to
R
and
\rho
in (
3
),
based on a generalisation of the analysis in Section
2
.
In particular, we prove the following generalisation of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
d
in
\mathbb{N}
be larger than or equal to
1
,

and
\rho
be as in (

).

is in

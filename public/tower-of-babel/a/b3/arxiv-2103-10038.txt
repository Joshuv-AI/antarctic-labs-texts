Introduction

Modules and twisted modules have been extensively studied in vertex algebra theory. We usually associate Lie algebras with vertex algebras through modules or twisted modules (see
[
FLM
,
FZ
,
Li1
,
Li6
]
, etc). But these theories are not valid for some algebras which generating functions are not “local”, we need generalize the notion of modules. A notion of quasi module of vertex algebra was introduced in
[
Li5
]
to associate more infinite dimensional Lie algebras like quantum torus Lie algebras with vertex algebras.
In order to associate quantum affine algebras with quantum vertex algebras, a theory of
\phi
-coordinated module for the quantum vertex algebra was developed in
[
Li2
]
. The parameter
\phi
is an associate of the one-dimensional additive formal group
F(x,y)=x+y
.
When
\phi=x+z
, a
\phi
-coordinated module of vertex algebra is just a module.
To establish the explicit connections of quantum vertex algebras to various algebraic structures like quantum affine algebras, a notion of
(G,\chi_{\phi})
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi module for nonlocal vertex algebras was introduced in
[
JKLT
]
, where
G
is a group and

\chi_{\phi}
is a linear character of
G
.
In
[
Li2
]
, the author proved that for every
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}((x))
,
\phi=e^{zp(x)\frac{d}{dx}}(x)
is an associate
of
F(x,y)
and every associate is of this form.
Moreover, an explicit association between quantum vertex algebra and certain quantum
\beta\gamma
-system was established in
[
Li2
]
through
\phi
-coordinated modules, where
\phi=xe^{z}
, i.e.,
p(x)=x
.
Recently, the
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated modules of vertex algebras have been studied precisely in
[
BLP
]
for
p(x)=x^{\epsilon}
with
\epsilon
an integer, and they use the theory of
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated modules to associate vertex algebras with Novikov algebras. Specifically, for
\phi=xe^{z}
, a notion of
G
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi module for quantum vertex algebras was introduced in
[
Li4
]
. A commutator formula for
G
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi module was obtained and a natural connection between the
deformed Virasoro algebra

of a certain Clifford vertex superalgebra through
G
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi module was established therein. Later in
[
JKLT
]
, a generalized commutator formula and a general construction of
(G,\chi_{\phi})
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi modules for nonlocal vertex algebras were established, and by using these results, they give a construction of (equivariant)
\phi
-coordinated quasi modules for lattice vertex algebras. We note that if
\phi=xe^{z}
, the notion of
(G,\chi_{\phi})
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi module coincides with the notion of
G
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi module. Motivated by these works, in this paper, we focus on the frame of vertex algebra. We expect to unify the association of Lie algebras and vertex algebras though
(G,\chi_{\phi})
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi modules.

\displaystyle(z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}Y_{W}(u,z_{1})Y_{W}(v,z_{2})\in\mathrm{Hom}(W,W((z_{1},z_{2}))),

\displaystyle(\phi(z_{2},z_{0})-z_{2})^{k}Y_{W}(Y(u,z_{0})v,z_{2})=((z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}Y_{W}(u,z_{1})Y_{W}(v,z_{2}))\mid_{z_{1}=\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}.

can generate a vertex algebra with

for some
a_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Then from (
2.9
) we obtain (cf.
[
Li4
, Lemma 2.3]
)

(2.11)

\begin{split}[a(z_{1}),b(z_{2})]=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{i}-1}a_{i,j+1}\lambda_{i}^{-j}z_{2}^{j+1}A(z_{1},z_{2})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(j)}z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right),\end{split}

where
A(z_{1},z_{2})=q(z_{1}/z_{2})a(z_{1})b(z_{2})\in\mathrm{Hom}(W,W((z_{1},z_{2})))
.

Note that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
f_{0}(z_{2}),\cdots,f_{n}(z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}((z_{2}))
such that

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(n)}=f_{n}(z_{2})\left(p(z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(n)}+f_{n-1}(z_{2})\left(p(z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(n-1)}+\cdots+f_{0}(z_{2}).

An induction argument shows that for any
B(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathrm{Hom}(W,W((z_{1},z_{2})))
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exist
B_{0}(z),\cdots,B_{n}(z)\in{\mathcal{E}}(W)
such that

\begin{split}&B(z_{1},z_{2})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(n)}z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}(z_{2})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(i)}\bar{p}(\lambda_{i}^{-1}z_{1})\delta\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right).\end{split}

Thus (
2.10
) follows.
∎

We fix a
\hat{p}(x)\in\mathbb{C}((x))
such that

\frac{d}{dx}\hat{p}(x)=p^{-1}(x)
, and set

f_{p(x)}(x,z):=\hat{p}(x(1+z))-\hat{p}(x).

Then we can deduce that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\phi(x,f_{p(x)}(x,z))=x(1+z).

We have (cf.
[
BLP
,
Li2
]
):

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

\displaystyle A(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathrm{Hom}\big(W,W((z_{1}))((z_{2}))\big),\quad B(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathrm{Hom}\big(W,W((z_{2}))((z_{1}))\big),

\displaystyle C(z_{0},z_{2})\in\big(\mathrm{Hom}\big(W,W((z_{2}))\big)\big)((z_{0})).

If there exists a nonnegative integer
k
such that

\displaystyle(z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}A(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}B(z_{1},z_{2}),

\displaystyle\left((z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}A(z_{1},z_{2})\right)|_{z_{1}=\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}=(\phi(z_{2},z_{0})-z_{2})^{k}C(z_{0},z_{2}),

then

\begin{split}&(z_{2}z)^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{2}z}\right)A(z_{1},z_{2})-(z_{2}z)^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{2}z}\right)B(z_{1},z_{2})\\
&\quad\quad=z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}(1+z)}{z_{1}}\right)C\left(f_{p(z_{2})}(z_{2},z),z_{2}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

When
p(x)=x^{\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Z}
, the assertion
is proved in
[
BLP
, Lemma 3.16]
, where
f_{p(x)}(x,z)
is denoted as
f_{\epsilon}(x,z)
.
In view of (
2.12
), the proof therein also valid for a general

.
∎

Now we are in a position to state the main result of this section, which says that those
A_{ij}(z)
in (
2.10
) are nothing but
the
\phi
-products
a(\lambda_{i}z)_{j}^{\phi}b(z)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(a(z),b(z))
be a pair in
{\mathcal{E}}(W)
satisfying

\displaystyle q(z_{1}/z_{2})[a(z_{1}),b(z_{2})]=0

for some
q(z)\in\mathbb{C}[z]
.
Then

\displaystyle[a(z_{1}),b(z_{2})]=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}}\sum_{j\geq 0}a(\lambda z_{2})_{j}^{\phi}b(z_{2})\left(p(z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(j)}\bar{p}(\lambda^{-1}z_{1})\delta\left(\frac{\lambda z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right),

which is a finite sum. Furthermore, the coefficients
a(\lambda z_{2})_{j}^{\phi}b(z_{2})\neq 0
only if
\lambda
is a root
of
q(z)
and
j
is less than the multiplicity of
\lambda
.

Proof.

We assume that
q(z)
has the form as in (
2.8
) and fix an
s
with
1\leq s\leq r
.
From (
2.10
), we only need to prove that

(2.13)

\displaystyle A_{sj}(z_{2})=a(\lambda_{s}z_{2})_{j}^{\phi}b(z_{2}),\quad j=0,\cdots,k_{s}-1.

Let us introduce the polynomials

\displaystyle q_{s}(z)=\prod_{1\leq i\neq s\leq r}((z-\lambda_{s})^{k_{s}}-(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{s})^{k_{s}})^{k_{i}}.

Note that
(z-\lambda_{i})^{k_{i}}
divides
q_{s}(z)
and so there is a
g_{s}(z)\in\mathbb{C}[z]
such that

(2.14)

\displaystyle q_{s}(z)(z-\lambda_{s})^{k_{s}}=g_{s}(z)q(z).

Then from (
2.14
) and (
2.3
), we have

(2.15)

\begin{split}&q_{s}(z_{1}/z_{2})[a(z_{1}),b(z_{2})]\\
=\,&\sum_{j=0}^{k_{s}-1}q_{s}(z_{1}/z_{2})A_{sj}(z_{2})\left(p(z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(j)}\bar{p}(\lambda_{s}^{-1}z_{1})\delta\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right).\end{split}

By replacing
z_{1}
with
\lambda_{s}z_{1}
in (
2.15
) and multiplying
(z_{1}/z_{2}-1)^{k_{s}}
on both sides of (
2.15
) we have

\displaystyle(z_{1}/z_{2}-1)^{k_{s}}q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z_{2})[a(\lambda_{s}z_{1}),b(z_{2})]=0.

Thus we have (see Definition
2.1
)

\displaystyle\quad(\phi(z,z_{0})/z-1)^{k_{s}}q_{s}(\lambda_{s}\phi(z,z_{0})/z)Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(a(\lambda_{s}z),z_{0})b(z)

\displaystyle=(z_{1}/z-1)^{k_{s}}q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z)a(\lambda_{s}z_{1})b(z)\mid_{z_{1}=\phi(z,z_{0})}.

In view of Lemma
2.5
, this implies that

\begin{split}&(z_{2}z)^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{2}z}\right)q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z_{2})a(\lambda_{s}z_{1})b(z_{2})\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-(z_{2}z)^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{2}z}\right)q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z_{2})b(z_{2})a(\lambda_{s}z_{1})\\
&=z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}(1+z)}{z_{1}}\right)q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z_{2})Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(a(\lambda_{s}z_{2}),f_{p(z_{2})}(z_{2},z))b(z_{2}).\end{split}

By taking
\mathrm{Res}_{z}z_{2}
in the above identity we find

\displaystyle\quad q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z_{2})[a(\lambda_{s}z_{1}),b(z_{2})]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z}z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}(1+z)}{z_{1}}\right)z_{2}q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z_{2})Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(a(\lambda_{s}z_{2}),f_{p(z_{2})}(z_{2},z))b(z_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\left(\frac{\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{2}}-1\right)z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{1}}\right)z_{2}q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z_{2})Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(a(\lambda_{s}z_{2}),z_{0})b(z_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z_{0}}p(\phi(z_{2},z_{0}))z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{1}}\right)q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z_{2})Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(a(\lambda_{s}z_{2}),z_{0})b(z_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z_{0}}p(z_{1})z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{1}}\right)q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z_{2})Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(a(\lambda_{s}z_{2}),z_{0})b(z_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(p(z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(j)}\left(\bar{p}(z_{1})\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right)\right)q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z_{1}/z_{2})a(\lambda_{s}z_{2})_{j}^{\phi}b(z_{2}),

where in the second equality we have used the fact (
2.12
).
Combining this with (
2.15
) (replacing
z_{1}
with
\lambda_{s}z_{1}
therein) we obtain

(2.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k_{s}-1}q_{s}\left(\lambda_{s}\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\right)(A_{sj}(z_{2})-a(\lambda_{s}z_{2})_{j}^{\phi}b(z_{2}))\left(p(z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(j)}\bar{p}(z_{1})\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right)=0.

Set

\alpha=\prod_{1\leq i\neq s\leq r}(-1)^{k_{i}}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{s})^{k_{s}k_{i}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

Note that there is an
h(z)\in\mathbb{C}[z]
such that

q_{s}(\lambda_{s}z)=\prod_{1\leq i\neq s\leq r}((\lambda_{s}z-\lambda_{s})^{k_{s}}-(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{s})^{k_{s}})^{k_{i}}=(z-1)^{k_{s}}h(z)+\alpha.

In view of this and (
2.3
), (
2.16
) can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{s}-1}\alpha(A_{sj}(z_{2})-a(\lambda_{s}z_{2})_{j}^{\phi}b(z_{2}))\left(p(z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(j)}\bar{p}(z_{1})\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right)=0.\end{split}

Note that
\alpha\neq 0
, this together with Lemma
2.3
proves (
2.13
).
∎

3.
\phi
-coordinated modules of vertex algebras

In this section we establish some conceptual results on
\phi
-coordinated (quasi) modules
for vertex algebras.
Let
(V,Y,\mathbf{1})
be a vertex algebra,
the following notion was introduced in
[
Li2
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
\phi
-coordinated quasi
V
-module is a vector space
W
equipped with a linear map

\displaystyle Y_{W}(\cdot,z):V

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(W,W((z)))\subset(\mathrm{End}W)[[z,z^{-1}]],

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle Y_{W}(v,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}v_{n}z^{-n-1},

satisfying the conditions that
Y_{W}(\mathbf{1},z)=1_{W}
and that for
u,v\in V
, there exists
a polynomial
f(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2}]
such that

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})Y_{W}(u,z_{1})Y_{W}(v,z_{2})\in\mathrm{Hom}(W,W((z_{1},z_{2}))),

\displaystyle f(\phi(z_{2},z_{0}),z_{2})Y_{W}(Y(u,z_{0})v,z_{2})=(f(z_{1},z_{2})Y_{W}(u,z_{1})Y_{W}(v,z_{2}))\mid_{z_{1}=\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}.

A
\phi
-coordinated
V
-module is defined as above except that
f(z_{1},z_{2})
is assumed to be a polynomial
of the form
(z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 3.2
.

When
p(x)=1
, a
\phi
-coordinated
V
-module is just a
V
-module.

In what follows we collect some basic properties of
\phi
-coordinated quasi
V
-modules for later use.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
Li2
]
Let
(W,Y_{W})
be a
\phi
-coordinated quasi
V
-module and let
u,v\in V
.
Assume that there exists a nonzero polynomial
q(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2}]
such that

\displaystyle q(z_{1},z_{2})Y_{W}(u,z_{1})Y_{W}(v,z_{2})\in\mathrm{Hom}(W,W((z_{1},z_{2}))).

Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle q(z_{1},z_{2})[Y_{W}(u,z_{1}),Y_{W}(v,z_{2})]

\displaystyle=0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle q(\phi(z_{2},z_{0}),z_{2})Y_{W}(Y(u,z_{0})v,z_{2})

\displaystyle=(q(z_{1},z_{2})Y_{W}(u,z_{1})Y_{W}(v,z_{2}))\mid_{z_{1}=\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}.

We define the linear operator
\mathcal{D}
on
V
by
\mathcal{D}v=v_{-2}\mathbf{1}
for
v\in V
.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
Li2
]
Let
(W,Y_{W})
be a
\phi
-coordinated quasi
V
-module.
Then

(3.3)

\displaystyle Y_{W}(\mathcal{D}v,z)

\displaystyle=\left(p(z)\frac{d}{dz}\right)Y_{W}(v,z),\quad v\in V,

(3.4)

\displaystyle Y_{W}(Y(u,z_{0})v,z)

\displaystyle=Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(Y_{W}(u,z),z_{0})Y_{W}(v,z),\quad u,v\in V.

The following is an analogue of Borcherd’s commutator formula (cf.
[
BLP
]
).

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(W,Y_{W})
be a
\phi
-coordinated
V
-module. Then for
u,v\in V
,

\begin{split}[Y_{W}(u,z_{1}),Y_{W}(v,z_{2})]=\sum_{j\geq 0}Y_{W}(u_{j}v,z_{2})\left(p(z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(j)}\bar{p}(z_{1})\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right).\end{split}

Conversely, if
W
is faithful and

\begin{split}[Y_{W}(u,z_{1}),Y_{W}(v,z_{2})]=\sum_{j\geq 0}Y_{W}(c_{j},z_{2})\left(p(z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(j)}\bar{p}(z_{1})\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right),\end{split}

for some
c_{j}\in V
, then
c_{j}=u_{j}v
for all
j\geq 0
.

Proof.

The second assertion follows from Lemma
2.3
.
For the first one, let
u,v\in V
. In view of (
3.1
), there is a nonnegative integer
k
such that

(z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}\,[Y_{W}(u,z_{1}),Y_{W}(v,z_{2})]=0.

It then follows from Proposition
2.6
that

\begin{split}[Y_{W}(u,z_{1}),Y_{W}(v,z_{2})]&=\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}Y_{W}(u,z_{2})^{\phi}_{j}Y_{W}(v,z_{2})\left(p(z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{(j)}\bar{p}(z_{1})\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right).\end{split}

This together with (
3.4
) proves the first assertion.
∎

Next we are going to give a conceptual construction of
\phi
-coordinated quasi modules for vertex algebras.
The following is a
\phi
-generalization of
[
Li5
, Proposition 4.3]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
a(z),b(z),c(z)\in{\mathcal{E}}(W)
. Assume that

\begin{split}f(z_{1},z_{2})[a(z_{1}),b(z_{2})]=g(z_{1},z_{2})[a(z_{1}),c(z_{2})]=h(z_{1},z_{2})[b(z_{1}),c(z_{2})]=0\end{split}

for some nonzero polynomials
f(z_{1},z_{2}),g(z_{1},z_{2}),h(z_{1},z_{2})
. Then for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a nonnegative integer
k
such that

g(z_{1},z_{2})^{k}h(z_{1},z_{2})[a(z_{1})_{n}^{\phi}b(z_{1}),c(z_{2})]=0.

Proof.

Fix an
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and take a nonnegative integer
k
such that

z_{0}^{k+n}f(\phi(z,z_{0}),z)^{-1}\in\mathbb{C}((z))[[z_{0}]].

Using this and the fact

\phi(\phi(z,z_{0}),-z_{0})=\phi(z,0)=z=\phi(\phi(z,-z_{0}),z_{0}),

we obtain

\displaystyle\quad g(z_{2},z_{3})^{k}h(z_{2},z_{3})(a(z_{2})_{n}^{\phi}b(z_{2}))c(z_{3})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z_{0}}z_{0}^{n}g(z_{2},z_{3})^{k}h(z_{2},z_{3})\left(Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(a(z_{2}),z_{0})b(z_{2})\right)c(z_{3})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z_{1}}\mathrm{Res}_{z_{0}}z_{0}^{n}g(z_{2},z_{3})^{k}h(z_{2},z_{3})f(\phi(z_{2},z_{0}),z_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{1}}\right)(f(z_{1},z_{2})a(z_{1})b(z_{2}))c(z_{3})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z_{1}}\mathrm{Res}_{z_{0}}z_{0}^{n}g(\phi(z_{1},-z_{0}),z_{3})^{k}h(z_{2},z_{3})f(\phi(z_{2},z_{0}),z_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{1}}\right)(f(z_{1},z_{2})a(z_{1})b(z_{2}))c(z_{3})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z_{1}}\mathrm{Res}_{z_{0}}z_{0}^{n}\left(e^{-z_{0}p(z_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}}g(z_{1},z_{3})^{k}\right)h(z_{2},z_{3})f(\phi(z_{2},z_{0}),z_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{1}}\right)(f(z_{1},z_{2})a(z_{1})b(z_{2}))c(z_{3})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z_{1}}\mathrm{Res}_{z_{0}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}z_{0}^{n+i}\left(\left(-p(z_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{(i)}g(z_{1},z_{3})^{k}\right)h(z_{2},z_{3})f(\phi(z_{2},z_{0}),z_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{1}}\right)(f(z_{1},z_{2})a(z_{1})b(z_{2}))c(z_{3})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z_{1}}\mathrm{Res}_{z_{0}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}z_{0}^{n+i}\left(\left(-p(z_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{(i)}g(z_{1},z_{3})^{k}\right)h(z_{2},z_{3})c(z_{3})

\displaystyle\quad f(\phi(z_{2},z_{0}),z_{2})^{-1}z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{1}}\right)(f(z_{1},z_{2})a(z_{1})b(z_{2}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{z_{1}}\mathrm{Res}_{z_{0}}z_{0}^{n}\left(e^{-z_{0}p(z_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}}g(z_{1},z_{3})^{k}\right)h(z_{2},z_{3})c(z_{3})f(\phi(z_{2},z_{0}),z_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{\phi(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{1}}\right)(f(z_{1},z_{2})a(z_{1})b(z_{2}))

\displaystyle=g(z_{2},z_{3})^{k}h(z_{2},z_{3})c(z_{3})(a(z_{2})_{n}^{\phi}b(z_{2})).

∎

A subset
S
of
{\mathcal{E}}(W)
is said to be
(resp. quasi) local
if any pair in
S
is (resp. quasi) local, and
a (resp. quasi) local subspace
U
of
{\mathcal{E}}(W)
is said to be
Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}
-closed
if

a(z)^{\phi}_{n}b(z)\in U\quad\textnormal{{for all }}a(z),b(z)\in U,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

In view of Proposition
3.6
, we can formulate the following notion.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
S
be a quasi local subset of
{\mathcal{E}}(W)
.
We define
{\langle}S{\rangle}_{\phi}
to be the smallest
Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}
-closed quasi local subspace of
{\mathcal{E}}(W)
which contains
S
and
1_{W}
.

We need the following
\phi
-generalization of
[
Li5
, Proposition 4.8]
for later use.

Proposition 3.8
.

For
a(z),b(z)\in{\mathcal{E}}(W)
, if

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})\,[a(z_{1}),b(z_{2})]=0

for some
f(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2}]
,
then

(z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}\,[Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(a(z),z_{1}),Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(b(z),z_{2})]=0,

where
k
is the order of the zero of
f(z_{1},z_{2})
at
z_{1}=z_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
a(z),b(z)
,
f(z_{1},z_{2})
and
k
be as in the proposition.
Then it follows from
[
JKLT
, Proposition 2.18]
that

(3.5)

\displaystyle f(\phi(z,z_{1}),\phi(z,z_{2}))[Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(a(z),z_{1}),Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{\phi}(b(z),z_{2})]=0.

Write

f(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}f_{0}(z_{1},z_{2})

for some polynomial
f_{0}(z_{1},z_{2})
with
f_{0}(z_{1},z_{1})\neq 0
.
Note that
\phi(z,0)=z
.
This implies that
f_{0}(\phi(z,0),\phi(z,0))
is nonzero and hence
f_{0}(\phi(z,z_{1}),\phi(z,z_{2}))
is
invertible in
\mathbb{C}((z))[[z_{1},z_{2}]]
.
On the other hand, from the definition of
\phi(z,z_{1})=e^{z_{1}p(z)\frac{d}{dz}}(z)
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(z,z_{1})-\phi(z,z_{2})=(z_{1}-z_{2})g(z,z_{1},z_{2})

for some
g(z,z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}((z))[[z_{1},z_{2}]]
.
Furthermore, we have
g(z,0,0)=p(z)\neq 0
and hence

g(z,z_{1},z_{2})
is also invertible in
\mathbb{C}((z))[[z_{1},z_{2}]]
.
Thus, we have shown that

f(\phi(z,z_{1}),\phi(z,z_{2}))
is a product of
(z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}
and an invertible element in

\mathbb{C}((z))[[z_{1},z_{2}]]
.
This together with (
3.5
) proves the proposition.

4.
\phi
-coordinated
V_{\mathcal{C}}
-modules

In this section, we first recall the notion of a conformal algebra
\mathcal{C}

[
K
]
(also known as
a vertex Lie algebra in
[
DLM
,
P
]
) and then determine the
\phi
-coordinated modules for the universal enveloping
vertex algebra of
\mathcal{C}
in terms of restricted modules of a Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}
arising from
\mathcal{C}
and
\phi
.

Definition 4.1
.

A conformal algebra
\mathcal{C}
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module with a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear product
a_{n}b
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following axioms hold
(a,b,c\in\mathcal{C},\ m,n\in\mathbb{N})
:

(C0)

\displaystyle a_{n}b=0\quad\textnormal{{for }}n\gg 0,

(C1)

\displaystyle(\partial a)_{n}b=-na_{n-1}b,

(C2)

\displaystyle a_{n}b=-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j+n}\partial^{(j)}(b_{n+j}a),

(C3)

\displaystyle a_{m}(b_{n}c)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(a_{j}b)_{m+n-j}c+b_{n}(a_{m}c).

A linear map
\varphi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of conformal algebras is called
a
homomorphism
if

\varphi\partial=\partial\varphi\quad\textnormal{{and}}\quad\varphi(a_{{n}}b)=\varphi(a)_{n}\varphi(b)\quad\textnormal{{ for }}a,b\in\mathcal{C},\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a conformal algebra. Recall that
\phi
is an associate of
F(x,y)
, which is determined by a nonzero
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}((x))
.
We define a multiplication on the formal loop space
\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}((x))
by

(4.1)

\displaystyle[a\otimes f(x),b\otimes g(x)]=\sum_{i\geq 0}(a_{i}b)\otimes\left(\left(p(x)\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{(i)}f(x)\right)g(x)

for
a,b\in\mathcal{C}
and
f(x),g(x)\in\mathbb{C}((x))
.
Form the quotient space

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}:=\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}((x))/\mathrm{Im}\,(\partial\otimes 1+1\otimes p(x)\frac{d}{dx}).

It was known from
[
K
]
that the multiplication (
4.1
) defines a Lie algebra structure on the quotient space
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}
.
When
\phi=x+z
, i.e.,
p(x)=1
, we also write

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}=\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}.

For
a\in\mathcal{C}
and
f(x)\in\mathbb{C}((x))

\overline{a\otimes f(x)}=\rho(a\otimes f(x)),

where
\rho:\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}((x))\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}
is the quotient map.

For
a\in\mathcal{C}
, we set

a_{\phi}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{a\otimes x^{n}p(x)}z^{-n-1}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}[[z,z^{-1}]].

Definition 4.2
.

A
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}
-module

is called restricted if
a_{\phi}(z)\in{\mathcal{E}}(W)
for any
a\in\mathcal{C}
.

One notices from definition that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left(p(z)\frac{d}{dz}\right)a_{\phi}(z)=(\partial a)_{\phi}(z),\quad a\in\mathcal{C}.

Furthermore, in view of (
4.1
), we have for
a,b\in\mathcal{C}
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle[a_{\phi}(z),b_{\phi}(w)]=\sum_{i\geq 0}(a_{i}b)_{\phi}(w)\left(p(w)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\right)^{(i)}\bar{p}(z)\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right).

We have the following useful lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
W
be a vector space and
\mathcal{C}
be a conformal algebra. Assume that there is a linear map

\pi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(W),\quad a\mapsto a(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{[n]}z^{-n-1},

such that for
a\in\mathcal{C}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left(p(z)\frac{d}{dz}\right)a(z)=(\partial a)(z),

and for
a,b\in\mathcal{C}

(4.5)

\displaystyle[a(z),b(w)]=\sum_{i\geq 0}(a_{i}b)(w)\left(p(w)\frac{\partial}{\partial_{w}}\right)^{(i)}\bar{p}(z)\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right).

Then there is a restricted
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}
-module structure on
W
with
a_{\phi}(z)=a(z)
for
a\in\mathcal{C}

Proof.

Let
a\in\mathcal{C}
and

.
Set

\displaystyle f_{0}(x)=p(x)^{-1}f(x)=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}b_{n}x^{n},

where
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We define an action of
\overline{a\otimes f(x)}
on
W
by

(4.6)

\displaystyle\overline{a\otimes f(x)}w=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{a,w}}b_{n}a_{[n]}w,\quad w\in W,

where

is a sufficiently large integer such that

for all

It follows from (
4.4
) that this action is well-defined.
Furthermore, by comparing (
4.3
) with (
4.5
), one can check that (

) defines a restricted
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}
-module structure on
W

with
a_{\phi}(z)=a(z)
for
a\in\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Now we recall the notion of universal enveloping vertex algebra of a conformal algebra
\mathcal{C}
.
Set

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{+}=\rho(\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}[[x]]),

which is a Lie subalgebra of

.
Let

be the one-dimensional trivial

-module

Set

can be identified as a subspace of

through the linear map

Note that each vertex algebra is naturally a conformal algebra by setting

and forgetting
the

The following result is well-known (cf.
[

,

,

,

]

Proposition 4.4

There is a vertex algebra structure on

.
Moreover, for any vertex algebra

to

Theorem 4.5

-coordinated

\displaystyle[Y_{W}(a_{\phi}(z_{0}),z),Y_{W}(b_{\phi}(z_{0}),w)]=[a_{\phi}(z),b_{\phi}(w)]

Remark 4.6

For a subgroup
\Gamma

[

]

(V,R)

\displaystyle f(x)(\frac{d}{dx}g(x))\frac{d}{dx}-g(x)(\frac{d}{dx}f(x))\frac{d}{dx}

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathbf{c}}{2}\mathrm{Res}_{x}((\frac{d}{dx})^{(3)}f(x))g(x).

Lemma 6.6
.

The assignment

\displaystyle\overline{L\otimes f(x)}\mapsto p(x)f(x)\frac{d}{dx}+\alpha_{\phi}(f(x))\mathbf{c},\quad\overline{\mathrm{c}\otimes x^{-1}p(x)}\mapsto\mathbf{c}

determines a

to
\mathcal{V}
, where

is a linear map defined by

f(x)\mapsto\frac{1}{24}\mathrm{Res}_{x}f(x)\big(2(\frac{d}{dx})^{2}p(x)-p(x)^{-1}(\frac{d}{dx}p(x))^{2}\big).

Proof.

Obviously, this assignment gives rise to a linear isomorphism
\varphi:\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}}}_{\phi}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}
. Now we prove that
\varphi
is a Lie algebra homomorphism. For any
f(x),g(x)\in\mathbb{C}((x))
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\varphi\Big([\overline{L\otimes f(x)},\overline{L\otimes g(x)}]\Big)

\displaystyle=\varphi\Big(-\overline{L\otimes p(x)\frac{d}{dx}(f(x)g(x))}+2\overline{L\otimes p(x)(\frac{d}{dx}f(x))g(x)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\overline{\mathrm{c}\otimes((p(x)\frac{d}{dx})^{(3)}f(x))g(x)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\varphi\Big(\overline{L\otimes p(x)g(x)\frac{d}{dx}f(x)}-\overline{L\otimes p(x)f(x)\frac{d}{dx}g(x)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{12}\overline{\mathrm{c}\otimes x^{-1}p(x)}\mathrm{Res}_{x}\Big((\frac{d}{dx}p(x))^{2}\frac{d}{dx}f(x)+3p(x)(\frac{d}{dx}p(x))(\frac{d}{dx})^{2}f(x)

\displaystyle\quad+p(x)((\frac{d}{dx})^{2}p(x))\frac{d}{dx}f(x)+p(x)^{2}(\frac{d}{dx})^{3}f(x)\Big)g(x)\Big)

\displaystyle=p(x)^{2}f(x)(\frac{d}{dx}g(x))\frac{d}{dx}-p(x)^{2}g(x)(\frac{d}{dx}f(x))\frac{d}{dx}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{12}\mathbf{c}\mathrm{Res}_{x}\Big(f(x)(\frac{d}{dx})^{3}p(x)+3(\frac{d}{dx}p(x))(\frac{d}{dx})^{2}f(x)

\displaystyle\quad+3((\frac{d}{dx})^{2}p(x))\frac{d}{dx}f(x)+p(x)(\frac{d}{dx})^{3}f(x)\Big)p(x)g(x)

\displaystyle=p(x)^{2}f(x)(\frac{d}{dx}g(x))\frac{d}{dx}-p(x)^{2}g(x)(\frac{d}{dx}f(x))\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{\mathbf{c}}{2}\mathrm{Res}_{x}(\frac{d}{dx})^{(3)}(p(x)f(x))p(x)g(x)

\displaystyle=[p(x)f(x)\frac{d}{dx},p(x)g(x)\frac{d}{dx}]=[\varphi(\overline{L\otimes f(x)}),\varphi(\overline{L\otimes g(x)})],

noticing that

\overline{\mathrm{c}\otimes p(x)h(x)}=\overline{\mathrm{c}\otimes x^{-1}p(x)}\mathrm{Res}_{x}h(x)\quad\textnormal{{for any }}h(x)\in\mathbb{C}((x)).

Thus
\varphi
is a Lie algebra isomorphism.

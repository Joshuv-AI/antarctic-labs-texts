Conformally covariant differential symmetry breaking operators for a vector bundle of rank 3 on S 3 S^{3}
Víctor PÉREZ-VALDÉS

Abstract
We construct and give a complete classification of all the differential symmetry breaking operators 𝔻 λ , ν m : C ∞ ​ ( S 3 , 𝒱 λ 3 ) → C ∞ ​ ( S 2 , ℒ m , ν ) \mathbb{D}_{\lambda,\nu}^{m}:C^{\infty}(S^{3},\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{3})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(S^{2},\mathcal{L}_{m,\nu}) , between the spaces of smooth sections of a vector bundle of rank 3 3 over the 3 3 -sphere 𝒱 λ 3 → S 3 \mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{3}\rightarrow S^{3} , and a line bundle over the 2 2 -sphere ℒ m , ν → S 2 \mathcal{L}_{m,\nu}\rightarrow S^{2} . In particular, we give necessary and sufficient conditions on the tuple of parameters ( λ , ν , m ) \left(\lambda,\nu,m\right) for which these operators exist.

1
Introduction

Given a representation
\Pi
of a Lie group
G
, and Lie subgroup
G^{\prime}\subset G
, we can consider the restriction
\Pi\rvert_{G^{\prime}}
of
\Pi
to
G^{\prime}
and think about its nature: how does it behave? Or, more concretely, how is
\Pi\rvert_{G^{\prime}}
decomposed (in a broad sense) into irreducible representations of
G^{\prime}
? The problem of understanding the behaviour of the restriction, in particular, its irreducible decomposition (supposed that
\Pi
is irreducible), is what in representation theory is called a
branching problem
.

T. Kobayashi proposed a program (
the ABC Program

[
11
]
), divided in three stages, to address these branching problems for real reductive Lie groups from different points of view:

\bullet

Stage A
: Abstract features of the restriction.

\bullet

Stage B
: Branching laws.

\bullet

Stage C
: Construction of symmetry breaking operators.

The goal of Stage A is to analyze abstract aspects of the restriction
\Pi\rvert_{G^{\prime}}
, such as estimates on the multiplicities of irreducible representations of
G^{\prime}
occurring in
\Pi\rvert_{G^{\prime}}
, or the study of the spectrum of
\Pi|_{G^{\prime}}
.
Note that while the multiplicities may be infinite even when
G^{\prime}
is a maximal subgroup of G, there are some cases when they are finite or even uniformly bounded (
[
13
]
). There are also cases when the multiplicities are at most one, as is the case of visible actions (
[
9
]
), or the Gross-Prasad conjecture (
[
4
]
,
[
23
]
), among others.

Stage B addresses the irreducible decomposition of the restriction. If
\Pi
is a finite-dimensional representation such that the restriction
\Pi\rvert_{G^{\prime}}
is completely reducible, it is clear what its irreducible decomposition means (as is the case of the Clebsch-Gordan formula for the tensor product of finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})
). When
\Pi
is a unitary representation (not necessarily finite-dimensional), we can think about its irreducible decomposition by using the direct integral of Hilbert spaces (Mautner–Teleman theorem
[
19
]
,
[
24
]
). In a more general situation when
\Pi
is not necessarily unitary, Stage B may be thought of as the study of the space
\text{{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}}(\Pi|_{G^{\prime}},\pi)
(or
\text{{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}}(\pi,\Pi|_{G^{\prime}})
), where
\pi
is a fixed representation of
G^{\prime}
.

The
G^{\prime}
-intertwining operators in
\text{{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}}(\Pi|_{G^{\prime}},\pi)
are called
symmetry breaking operators
. Stage C asks for concrete symmetry breaking operators in order to understand better the nature of the space
\text{{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}}(\Pi|_{G^{\prime}},\pi)
.

In contrast with the abstract flavour of Stages A and B, Stage C depends on concrete realizations of the representations, so it often interacts with geometric or analytic problems.

In this paper, we focus on Stage C. Concretely, we focus on the construction of symmetry breaking operators that can be written as
differential
operators for a concrete pair of reductive Lie groups
(G,G^{\prime})
. One classical example following this line is given by the Rankin–Cohen bidifferential operators (
[
2
]
,
[
22
]
), which are a special case of symmetry breaking operators for the tensor product of two holomorphic discrete series representations of
SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.

In the following, we give a specific geometric setting that we consider throughout the paper. This setting allows us to consider Stage C in a significantly wide class of cases.

Given two smooth manifolds
X
and
Y
, two vector bundles over them
\mathcal{V}\rightarrow X
and
\mathcal{W}\rightarrow Y
and a smooth map
p:Y\rightarrow X
, we can talk about the notion of a
differential operator
between the spaces of smooth sections
T:C^{\infty}(X,\mathcal{V})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(Y,\mathcal{W})
, following the line of the famous result of J. Peetre (
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
) about the characterization of differential operators in terms of their support (see Definition
2.2
). In addition, suppose that we have a pair of Lie groups
G^{\prime}\subset G
acting equivariantly on
\mathcal{W}\rightarrow Y
and
\mathcal{V}\rightarrow X
respectively, and that
p
is a
G^{\prime}
-equivariant map between
Y
and
X
. In this setting, we can consider the following problem:

Problem 1.1
.

Give a description of the space of
G^{\prime}
-intertwining differential operators (
differential symmetry breaking operators
)

D:C^{\infty}(X,\mathcal{V})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(Y,\mathcal{W}).

Even if this is a geometric setting for Problem
1.1
, it is very general and differential symmetry breaking operators may or may not exist. For instance, if
\mathcal{W}
is isomorphic to the pullback
p^{*}\mathcal{V}
, the restriction map
f\mapsto f\lvert_{Y}
is clearly a
G^{\prime}
-intertwining differential operator from
C^{\infty}(X,\mathcal{V})
to
C^{\infty}(Y,\mathcal{W})
.

In the general setting where there is no morphism between
p^{*}\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{W}
, there are cases where non-zero differential symmetry breaking operators exist, but determining when this occurs is a considerably hard task, and there are many unsolved problems. As for the ones that have been solved during the past years, for example, T. Kobayashi and B. Speh constructed and classified not only the ones that can be written as differential operators, but all the symmetry breaking operators for the tuple
(X,Y,G,G^{\prime})=(S^{n},S^{n-1},O(n+1,1),O(n,1))
and for a concrete pair of vector bundles
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
(
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
).

In the case where
X=G/P\supset Y=G^{\prime}/P^{\prime}
are flag varieties and the vector bundles
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}
are those associated to representations of the parabolic subgroups
P
and
P^{\prime}
respectively, T. Kobayashi proposed a method (
the F-method

[
8
]
) to construct and classify all differential symmetry breaking operators

D:C^{\infty}(G/P,\mathcal{V})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(G^{\prime}/P^{\prime},\mathcal{W}).

This F-method allows us to reduce the problem of constructing differential symmetry breaking operators to the problem of finding polynomial solutions of a system of PDE’s by applying the “algebraic Fourier transform” to certain generalized Verma modules, and
to use invariant theory to solve the latter. It is known (
[
15
, Prop. 3.10]
) that if the nilradical of the parabolic subgroup
P
is abelian, then the principal term of the equations of the system of PDE’s is of order two.

In this setting, T. Kobayashi and M. Pevzner used the F-method to construct differential symmetry breaking operators for some concrete pairs of hermitian symmetric spaces
X\supset Y
(
[
16
]
). In a slightly different context, T. Kobayashi together with B. Ørsted, V. Souček and P. Somberg used the F-method to solve a similar problem in the setting of conformal geometry. In addition, in
[
12
]
all conformal symmetry breaking operators for differential forms on spheres were constructed and classified by T. Kobayashi, T. Kubo and M. Pevzner.

In the present paper, we consider the case
(X,Y,G,G^{\prime})=(S^{3},S^{2},SO_{0}(4,1),SO_{0}(3,1))
and solve Problem
1.1
for a concrete pair of vector bundles
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})=(\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{3},\mathcal{L}_{m,\nu})
(see (
3.4
) and (
3.6
) for the definition). In particular, we provide complete solutions of the following two problems (see Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
respectively):

Problem A
.

Give necessary and sufficient conditions on the parameters
\lambda,\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}

such that the space

\text{{Diff}}_{SO_{0}(3,1)}\left(C^{\infty}(S^{3},\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{3}),C^{\infty}(S^{2},\mathcal{L}_{m,\nu})\right)

of differential symmetry breaking operators

,
[

]

]
,

First, we start recalling the definition of the algebraic Fourier transform. Let
E
be a finite-dimensional complex vector space and denote by
\mathcal{D}(E)
the Weyl algebra over

Definition 2.1

(
[

, Def. 3.1]
)
.

The

is defined as an isomorphism between the Weyl algebras on

E^{\vee}

\mathcal{D}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(E^{\vee}),\kern 5.0ptT\mapsto\widehat{T},

induced by the following relations

\widehat{\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}}:=-\zeta_{j},\kern 5.0pt\widehat{z_{j}}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{j}},\quad\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq n:=\dim E,

where
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
are coordinates on
E
and
(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})
are the dual coordinates on
E^{\vee}
.

Now, let
X,Y
be two smooth manifolds and suppose that a smooth map between them
p:Y\rightarrow X
is given. Let
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}
be two vector bundles over
X
and
Y
respectively. We write
C^{\infty}(X,\mathcal{V})
(resp.

4.1

Here we recall a well-known result about the classification of the finite-dimensional irreducible representations of

(see, for example

]
,

It is known that any finite-dimensional irreducible representation of

is parametrized by a non-negative integer

and can be realized in the vector space of homogeneous polynomials of degree

in two variables as follows:

\begin{gathered}\theta^{k}:SU(2)\longrightarrow GL_{\mathbb{C}}\left(V^{k+1}\right),\quad g\longmapsto\left(p(\cdot)\mapsto p\left(g^{-1}\cdot\right)\right),\end{gathered}

where

.

Let
\varpi
be the
2:1
covering map of
SO(3)
:

\varpi:SU(2)\twoheadrightarrow SO(3),

which has kernel
\ker\varpi=\{\pm I_{2}\}
and can be written explicitly as follows (cf.
[

):

\begin{gathered}\varpi(U(x,y))=\begin{pmatrix}\text{{Re}}(x^{2}-y^{2})&\text{{Im}}(x^{2}+y^{2})&-2\text{{Re}}(xy)\\[3.0pt]
-\text{{Im}}(x^{2}-y^{2})&\text{{Re}}(x^{2}+y^{2})&2\text{{Im}}(xy)\\[3.0pt]
2\text{{Re}}(x\overline{y})&2\text{{Im}}(x\overline{y})&|x|^{2}-|y|^{2}\end{pmatrix},\end{gathered}

(4.3)

for
U(x,y):=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
-\overline{y}&\overline{x}\end{pmatrix}\in SU(2)
.

We denote by
\widetilde{\varpi}
the associated isomorphism
\widetilde{\varpi}:SU(2)/\{\pm I_{2}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\thicksim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}SO(3)
.

factors through

as a representation of

if and only if

Thus, any finite-dimensional representation of

is of the form

\begin{gathered}\sigma^{2N+1}:SO(3)\longrightarrow GL_{\mathbb{C}}\left(V^{2N+1}\right),\quad B\longmapsto\left(p(\cdot)\mapsto p\left(\left(\widetilde{\varpi}^{-1}(B)\right)^{-1}\cdot\right)\right).\end{gathered}

Lemma 6.4
.

Suppose that

(i)

Proof.

The direction (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) is trivial. We prove (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Suppose (i), then

\zeta_{1}^{2}p_{1}+\zeta_{2}^{2}p_{2}+\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}p_{3}=-p_{4}\in\text{{Pol}}({\mathbb{C}}^{3})^{O(2,{\mathbb{C}})}.

(6.12)

We denote by
\cdot
the natural action of

on
\text{{Pol}}({\mathbb{C}}^{3})

Take

, and take invariants with respect to
B_{1}
in both sides of (

). We obtain:

\begin{gathered}B_{1}\cdot(\zeta_{1}^{2}p_{1}+\zeta_{2}^{2}p_{2}+\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}p_{3})=B_{1}\cdot(-p_{4})=-p_{4}\\
\Longrightarrow\zeta_{2}^{2}p_{1}+\zeta_{1}^{2}p_{2}+\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}p_{3}=\zeta_{1}^{2}p_{1}+\zeta_{2}^{2}p_{2}+\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}p_{3}\\
\Longrightarrow(\zeta_{1}^{2}-\zeta_{2}^{2})(p_{2}-p_{1})=0.\end{gathered}

Thus

.

On the other hand, if we take invariants with respect to

in both sides of (

\begin{gathered}B_{2}\cdot((\zeta_{1}^{2}+\zeta_{2}^{2})p_{1}+\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}p_{3})=B_{2}\cdot(-p_{4})=-p_{4}\\

\lambda\in\Lambda^{m}_{a+m-2(k+1)}
.

(II)

2i\gamma(\lambda-1,a+m)p+(a+m-2(k+1))q=0
.

By Remark

(1),
(\star_{1})
together with (I) amounts to

\lambda\in\Lambda^{m}_{a+m-2(k+1)}.

Similarly, by Remark

(2),
(\star_{1})
together with (II) amounts to

\lambda+a+m-2-k=2i\gamma(\lambda-1,a+m)p+(a+m-2(k+1))q=0.

Since
-a-m+k+1\in\Lambda^{m}_{a+m-2(k-1)}\setminus\Lambda^{m}_{a+m-2k}
, and
\Lambda^{m}_{a+m-2k}\supset\Lambda^{m}_{a+m-2(k+1)}
,
(\star_{2})
and (I) cannot be simultaneously satisfied. The same happens with
(\star_{2})
and (II) since if they were both satisfied, that would imply

which contradicts the hypothesis

. If
a+m\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
,
\frac{-a-m+2}{2}\notin\Lambda^{m}_{a+m-2(k+1)}
, so
(\star_{3})
and (I) cannot be satisfied at the same time neither. On the other hand,
(\star_{3})
together with (II) amounts to

a+m\in 2{\mathbb{N}}\text{ and }q=\lambda+\frac{a+m-2}{2}=0.

The last two pairs of conditions are
(\star_{4})
with (I) or (II), but in either case we obtain
(p,q)=(0,0)
. In conclusion, the only possibilities we obtained in order to have

are the following four:

•

\lambda\in\Lambda^{m}_{a+m-2(k+1)}
.

•

\lambda+a+m-2-k=2i\gamma(\lambda-1,a+m)p+(a+m-2(k+1))q=0
, with
(p,q)\neq(0,0)
.

•

a+m\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
and
q=\lambda+\frac{a+m-2}{2}=0
, with
p\neq 0
.

•

(p,q)=(0,0)
.

These are precisely the conditions
(\star_{1})
–
(\star_{4})
for
k+1
. The second statement is now proved by induction on

10
Appendix: Gegenbauer polynomials

In this section we define and give some properties of the renormalized Gegenbauer polynomials we use throughout the paper.

10.1
Renormalized Gegenbauer polynomials

For any
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
and any
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the Gegenbauer polynomial (or ultraspherical polynomial), is defined as follows (
[
1
, 6.4]
,
[
3
, 3.15(2)]
):

\displaystyle C_{\ell}^{\mu}(z)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{\ell}{2}]}(-1)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(\ell-k+\mu)}{\Gamma(\mu)k!(\ell-2k)!}(2z)^{\ell-2k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\ell+2\mu)}{\Gamma(2\mu)\Gamma(\ell+1)}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-\ell,\ell+2\mu;\mu+\frac{1}{2};\frac{1-z}{2}\right).

This polynomial satisfies the Gegenbauer differential equation

G_{\ell}^{\mu}f(z)=0,

where
G_{\ell}^{\mu}
is the following differential operator known as the Gegenbauer differential operator

G_{\ell}^{\mu}:=(1-z^{2})\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}-(2\mu+1)z\frac{d}{dz}+\ell(\ell+2\mu).

Observe that
C_{\ell}^{\mu}\equiv 0
if
\mu=0,-1,-2,\cdots,-[\frac{\ell-1}{2}]
. As in
[
12
, (14.23)]
, we renormalize the Gegenbauer operator as follows:

\widetilde{C}^{\mu}_{\ell}(z):=\frac{\Gamma(\mu)}{\Gamma\left(\mu+[\frac{\ell+1}{2}]\right)}C^{\mu}_{\ell}(z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\mu+[\frac{\ell+1}{2}]\right)}\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{\ell}{2}]}(-1)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(\mu+\ell-k)}{k!(\ell-2k)!}(2z)^{\ell-2k}.

(10.1)

By doing this, we obtain a non-zero polynomial for any value of the parameters. That is, for any
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
and any
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the renormalized Gegenbauer polynomial
\widetilde{C}^{\mu}_{\ell}(z)
is a non-zero polynomial with degree at most
\ell
. We write below the first seven Gegenbauer polynomials.

•

\widetilde{C}^{\mu}_{0}(z)=1
.

•

\widetilde{C}^{\mu}_{1}(z)=2z
.

•

\widetilde{C}^{\mu}_{2}(z)=2(\mu+1)z^{2}-1
.

•

\widetilde{C}^{\mu}_{3}(z)=\frac{4}{3}(\mu+2)z^{3}-2z
.

•

\widetilde{C}^{\mu}_{4}(z)=\frac{2}{3}(\mu+2)(\mu+3)z^{4}-2(\mu+2)z^{2}+\frac{1}{2}
.

•

\widetilde{C}^{\mu}_{5}(z)=\frac{4}{15}(\mu+3)(\mu+4)z^{5}-\frac{4}{3}(\mu+3)z^{3}+z
.

•

\widetilde{C}^{\mu}_{6}(z)=\frac{4}{45}(\mu+3)(\mu+4)(\mu+5)z^{6}-\frac{2}{3}(\mu+3)(\mu+4)z^{4}+(\mu+3)z^{2}-\frac{1}{6}
.

Observe that from the definition, the degree of each term of
\widetilde{C}_{\ell}^{\mu}(z)
has the same parity as
\ell
, and the number of terms is at most
\left[\frac{\ell}{2}\right]+1
. Depending on the value of
\mu
, some terms may vanish, but the constant term is always non-zero.

The next result shows that the solutions of the Gegenbauer differential equation on
\text{{Pol}}_{\ell}[z]_{\text{{{even}}}}
are precisely the renormalized Gegenbauer polynomials.

Theorem 10.1

(
[
16

\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
and any

For any
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
and any
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the

S_{\ell}^{\mu}=-\left((1+t^{2})\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}+(1+2\mu)t\frac{d}{dt}-\ell(\ell+2\mu)\right).

This operator and

Abstract
. It is known that any m m -gonal form of rank ⁡ n ≥ 5 \operatorname{rank}n\geq 5 is almost regular. On the other words, any m m -gonal form of rank ⁡ n ≥ 5 \operatorname{rank}n\geq 5 represents every sufficiently large integer which is locally represented by the form. In this article, we treat the sufficiently large integers which are indeed represented by m m -gonal forms of rank ⁡ n ≥ 5 \operatorname{rank}n\geq 5 (those should be almost regular) among the locally represented integers.

3.
Local representation

The following proposition makes determine the local representability of an integer by an

-gonal form.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})=a_{1}P_{m}(x_{1})+\cdots+a_{n}P_{m}(x_{n})
be a primitive
m
-gonal form.

(1)

When
p

p|m-2
,
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is universal over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

(2)

When
m\not\equiv 0\pmod{4}
,
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is universal over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

(3)

When
p
is an odd prime with
(p,m-2)=1
, an integer
N
is represented by
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
if and only if the integer
8(m-2)N+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})(m-4)^{2}
is represented by the diagonal quadratic form
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

(4)

When
m\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, an integer
N
is represented by
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
if and only if the integer
\frac{m-2}{2}N+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})\left(\frac{m-4}{4}\right)^{2}
is represented by the diagonal quadratic form
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

Proof.

(1) One may easily show that for any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
,
P_{m}(x)=N
is solovable over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
by using Hensel’s Lemma (13:9 in
[
7
]
).
Which yields the claim from the assumption that
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is primitive.

(2) For any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, the equation

has a
2
-adic integer solution
\frac{m-4-\sqrt{(m-4)^{2}-8N(m-2)}}{2(m-2)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.
Which yields the claim from the assumption that
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is primitive.

(3) Note that

N=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}P_{m}(x_{i})

if and only if

8(m-2)N+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})(m-4)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(2(m-2)x_{i}-(m-4))^{2}

where
2(m-2)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
.
It completes the proof.

(4) Note that

N=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}P_{m}(x_{i})

if and only if

\frac{m-2}{2}N+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})\left(\frac{m-4}{4}\right)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\left(\frac{m-2}{2}x_{i}-\frac{m-4}{4}\right)^{2}

where
\frac{m-2}{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{\times}
and
\frac{m-4}{4}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.
It completes the proof.
∎

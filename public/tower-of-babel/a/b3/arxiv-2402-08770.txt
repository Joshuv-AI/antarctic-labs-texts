Unitarily equivalent bilateral weighted shifts with operator weights
Michał Buchała

Abstract
. We study unitarily equivalent bilateral weighted shifts with operator weights. We establish a general characterization of unitary equivalence of such shifts under the assumption that the weights are quasi-invertible. We prove that under certain assumptions unitary equivalence of bilateral weighted shifts with operator weights defined on ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} can always be given by a unitary operator with at most two non-zero diagonals. We provide examples of unitarily equivalent shifts with weights defined on ℂ k \mathbb{C}^{k} such that every unitary operator, which intertwines them has at least k k non-zero diagonals.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
47B37, 47B02.

1.
Introduction

Classical weighted shifts (both unilateral and bilateral) have been studied extensively for many years (see
[
14
]
for comprehensive work on weighted shifts). One possible generalization of classical weighted shifts is to replace scalar weights with operator weights. We consider unilateral weighted shifts:

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N},H)\ni(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mapsto(0,S_{1}x_{0},S_{2}x_{1},\ldots)\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N},H)

and bilateral weighted shifts:

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)\ni(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mapsto(S_{i}x_{i-1})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H),

where all
S_{i}
’s are bounded operators on a Hilbert space
H
. The matrix representation of bilateral weighted shift takes the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
\ddots&0&0&0&\ddots\\
\ddots&S_{0}&\boxed{0}&0&\ddots\\
\ddots&0&S_{1}&0&\ddots\\
\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix},

where
\boxed{\,\cdot\,}
denotes the entry in zeroth column and zeroth row of this matrix.
In
[
10
]
Lambert studied unitary equivalence of unilateral weighted shifts with invertible weights. In
[
11
]
Orovčanec characterized unitarily equivalent unilateral weighted shifts with quasi-invertible weights (see also
[
1
]
for the similar result for shifts with weights having dense ranges). In
[
8
]
the authors studied similar and quasi-similar unilateral weighted shifts and in
[
7
]
the similarity and quasi-similarity relation for bilateral weighted shifts was investigated. In
[
4
]
there was shown that if the weights can be divided into two sequences of normal and commuting operators, then the bilateral shift with such weights is unitarily equivalent to the shift with non-negative weights. Recently, in
[
9
]
Kośmider characterized bilateral shifts with quasi-invertible weights, which are unitarily equivalent by a unitary operator of diagonal form. The aim of this paper is to generalize the results of
[
9
]
. In Section
2
we introduce the notation and recall several basic facts about bilateral weighted shifts, which are crucial in the subsequent part of this paper. The main results are presented in Section
3
. In Theorem
3.1
we present the counterpart of
[
10
, Corollary 3.3]
for bilateral weighted shifts. In Corollary
3.5
we show how to deduce the condition given in
[
9
, Corollary 2.4]
from our result. We also give some more convenient characterizations of unitary equivalence under further assumptions on weights. In Theorem
3.8
we characterize unitary equivalence in terms of factors in polar decomposition of the bilateral weighted shift. The remaining part of Section
3
is devoted to study certain shifts with positive weights. In Theorem
3.10
we prove that when
H
is two dimensional, then under certain assumptions on weights the unitary equivalence is always given by a unitary operator with at most two non-zero diagonals. At the end of this section we provide a class of examples of two unitarily equivalent shifts with weights on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
such that every unitary operator intertwining these two shifts has at least
k
non-zero diagonals.

2.
Preliminaries

Denote by
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{Z}
the set of non-negative integers and integers, respectively and by
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
the field of real and complex numbers, respectively. For
p\in\mathbb{R}
and
A\subset\mathbb{R}
we set

A_{p}=\{x\in A\!:x\geqslant p\}.

If
H,K
are complex Hilbert spaces, then
\mathbf{B}(H,K)
stands for the space of all linear and bounded operators from
H
to
K
; if
H=K
, then we simply write
\mathbf{B}(H)
. For
T\in\mathbf{B}(H)
we denote by
\mathcal{R}(T)
and
\mathcal{N}(T)
the range of
T
and the kernel of
T
, respectively. An operator
T\in\mathbf{B}(H)
is called
normal
if
T
commutes with its adjoint;
T
is
positive
if
\langle Th,h\rangle>0
for every
h\in H
,
h\not=0
. We say that an operator
T\in\mathbf{B}(H)
is
quasi-invertible
if
\mathcal{N}(T)=\{0\}
and
\mathcal{N}(T^{\ast})=\{0\}
. It can be easily seen that

(2.1)

T,S\text{ -- quasi-invertible}\implies TS\text{ -- quasi-invertible},\qquad T,S\in\mathbf{B}(H),

and that

T\text{ -- positive}\implies T\text{ -- quasi-invertible},\qquad T\in\mathbf{B}(H).

An operator
U\in\mathbf{B}(H)
is called a
partial isometry
if
U|_{\mathcal{N}(U)^{\perp}}
is isometric. If
T\in\mathbf{B}(H)
, then there exists the unique partial isometry
U\in\mathbf{B}(H)
such that
\mathcal{N}(T)=\mathcal{N}(U)
and
T=U\lvert T\rvert
, where
\lvert T\rvert=(T^{\ast}T)^{1/2}
(see
[
15
, Theorem 7.20]
).

(2.2)

The polar decomposition of
T
is the decomposition
T=U\lvert T\rvert
, where
U
is the unique partial isometry satisfying
\mathcal{N}(U)=\mathcal{N}(T)
.

We say that the operators
T,S\in\mathbf{B}(H)
are
unitarily equivalent
if there exists a unitary operator
U\in\mathbf{B}(H)
such that
US=TU
. We can easily show that if
T,S\in\mathbf{B}(H)
are unitarily equivalent, then so are
S^{\ast}
and
T^{\ast}
(by the same unitary operator). A family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathbf{B}(H)
of operators is
doubly commuting
if for every
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
,
AB=BA
and
A^{\ast}B=BA^{\ast}
. If
(M_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the sequence of subspaces of
H
, then we denote by
\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
the closed linear span of
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
. If
(H_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the sequence of Hilbert spaces, then we define its orthogonal sum as follows:

\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{i}=\left\{h=(h_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{i}\!:\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\lVert h_{n}\rVert^{2}<\infty\right\};

this is a Hilbert space with the inner product given by the formula:

\langle(h_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},(h_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\rangle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle h_{i},h_{i}^{\prime}\rangle,\qquad(h_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},(h_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{i}.

If
H_{i}=H
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, then the above orthogonal sum will be denoted by
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)
.
For
x\in H
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
denote by
x^{(k)}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)
the vector given as follows:
x^{(k)}_{k}=x
and
x^{(k)}_{i}=0
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}
.
Note that every operator
T\in\mathbf{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))
has a matrix representation
[T_{i,j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}
, where
T_{i,j}\in\mathbf{B}(H)
, satisfying the following formula (see
[
5
, Chapter 8]
):

T(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}T_{i,j}x_{j}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!,\qquad(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H).

Observe that

(2.3)

T=[T_{i,j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbf{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))\implies T^{\ast}=[T^{\ast}_{j,i}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}},

and

(2.4)

T=[T_{i,j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}},S=[S_{i,j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbf{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))\implies TS=\left[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}T_{i,k}S_{k,j}\right]_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}.

For the convenience we introduce the following notation: if
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbf{B}(H)
is a uniformly bounded sequence of operators, then by
D[(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]\in\mathbf{B}\left(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)\right)
we denote the diagonal operator with operators
A_{i}
(
i\in\mathbb{Z}
) on the diagonal, that is,
D[(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]\!:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)
is defined as

(2.5)

\displaystyle D[(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}](x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}=(A_{i}x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},\qquad(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H).

If
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbf{B}(H)
is a uniformly bounded sequence of operators, then we define the bilateral weighted shift
S\in\mathbf{B}\left(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)\right)
with weights
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
by the formula:

S(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}=(S_{i}x_{i-1})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},\qquad(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H).

By
F
we denote the bilateral weighted shift with all weights equal to the identity operator on
H
; it is easy to see that
F
is a unitary operator. We say that an operator
T\in\mathbf{B}\left(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)\right)
is of diagonal form if there exists a uniformly bounded sequence
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbf{B}(H)
such that
T=F^{k}D[(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In the following lemma we gather basic properties of bilateral weighted shifts with operator weights; the proof (which is a straightforward application of (
1.1
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
)) is left to the reader.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
be a complex Hilbert space. Let
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbf{B}(H)
be a uniformly bounded sequence of operators and let
S
be the bilateral weighted shift
S\in\mathbf{B}\left(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)\right)
with weights
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Then:

(i)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,

(S^{n})_{i,j}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{for }i\not=j+n\\
S_{j+n}\cdots S_{j+1},&\text{for }i=j+n\end{cases},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

are the entries of the matrix representation of
S^{n}
,

(ii)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,

(S^{\ast n})_{i,j}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{for }j\not=i+n\\
S_{i+1}^{\ast}\cdots S_{i+n}^{\ast},&\text{for }j=i+n\end{cases},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

are the entries of the matrix representation of
S^{\ast n}
,

(iii)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,

(S^{\ast n}S^{n})_{i,j}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{for }i\not=j\\
\lvert S_{j+n}\cdots S_{j+1}\rvert^{2},&\text{for }i=j\end{cases},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

are the entries of the matrix representation of
S^{\ast n}S^{n}
,

(iv)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,

(S^{n}S^{\ast n})_{i,j}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{for }j\not=i\\
\lvert S_{i-n+1}^{\ast}\cdots S_{i}^{\ast}\rvert^{2},&\text{for }j=i\end{cases},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

are the entries of the matrix representation of
S^{n}S^{\ast n}
.

The next lemma characterizes the operators, which intertwines two bilateral weighted shifts (see also
[
12
, Lemma 4]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
H
be a complex Hilbert space. Let
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},(T_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbf{B}(H)
be two uniformly bounded sequences of operators. Denote by
S,T
the bilateral weighted shifts with weights
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
(T_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, respectively. Suppose
A=[A_{i,j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbf{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
. Then:

(i)

AS^{n}=T^{n}A
if and only if

A_{i+n,j+n}(S_{j+n}\cdots S_{j+1})=T_{i+n}\cdots T_{i+1}A_{i,j},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z};

(ii)

AS^{\ast n}=T^{\ast n}A
if and only if

A_{i,j}(S_{j+1}^{\ast}\cdots S_{j+n}^{\ast})=T_{i+1}^{\ast}\cdots T_{i+n}^{\ast}A_{i+n,j+n},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z}

Proof.

Observe that, by Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle(AS^{n})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i,k}(S^{n})_{k,j}=A_{i,j+n}S_{j+n}\cdots S_{j+1},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle(T^{n}A)_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(T^{n})_{i,k}A_{k,j}=T_{i}\cdots T_{i-n+1}A_{i-n,j},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

are the entires of matrix representations of
AS^{n}
and
T^{n}A
, respectively. Replacing
i
with
i+n
in the above equalities, we obtain (i). In turn, again by Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle(AS^{\ast n})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i,k}(S^{\ast n})_{k,j}=A_{i,j-n}S_{j-n+1}^{\ast}\cdots S_{j}^{\ast},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle(T^{\ast n}A)_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(T^{\ast n})_{i,k}A_{k,j}=T_{i+1}^{\ast}\cdots T_{i+n}^{\ast}A_{i+n,j},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

are the entries of matrix representations of
AS^{\ast n}
and
T^{\ast n}A
, respectively. Replacing
j
with
j+n
in the above equalities, we obtain (ii).
∎

In the following lemma we describe the polar decomposition of a bilateral weighted shift.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
H
be a complex Hilbert space. Let
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbf{B}(H)
be a uniformly bounded sequence of operators and let
S
be the bilateral weighted shift with weights
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
. For
i\in\mathbb{Z}
let
S_{i}=V_{i}\lvert S_{i}\rvert
be the polar decomposition of
S_{i}
. Denote by
V
the bilateral weighted shift with weights
(V_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
S=V\lvert S\rvert
is the polar decomposition of
S
.

Proof.

For every
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)
we have

\displaystyle S(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle=(S_{i}x_{i-1})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}=(V_{i}\lvert S_{i}\rvert x_{i-1})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle=FD[(V_{i+1})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]D[(\lvert S_{i+1}\rvert)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}](x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}.

By Lemma
2.1
,
D[(\lvert S_{i+1}\rvert)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]=\lvert S\rvert
. It can be easily verified that
V=FD[(V_{i+1})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]
. Next,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(S)

\displaystyle=\left\{(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)\!:S_{i}x_{i-1}=0\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{FormPolarDecompositionGeneral}}}{{=}}\left\{(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)\!:V_{i}x_{i-1}=0\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}=\mathcal{N}(V).

Thus,
V
is a partial isometry satisfying
\mathcal{N}(S)=\mathcal{N}(V)
, so
S=V\lvert S\rvert
is the polar decomposition of
S
as in (
2.2
).
∎

3.
Unitary equivalence of weighted shifts

In this section we provide a general characterization of unitarily equivalent weighted shifts with operator weights. The result presented below is the counterpart of the theorem of Lambert (cf.
[
10
, Corollary 3.3]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space. Let
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},(T_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbf{B}(H)
be two uniformly bounded sequences of quasi-invertible operators and let
S,T
be the bilateral weighted shifts with weights
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
(T_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, respectively.

(i)

If
US=TU
for a unitary operator
U\in\mathbf{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))
, then for the isometry
U_{0}\in\mathbf{B}(H,\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))
defined by
U_{0}x=Ux^{(0)}
,
x\in H
, the following conditions hold:

(3.1)

\displaystyle U_{0}\lvert S_{n}\cdots S_{1}\rvert=D[(\lvert T_{i+n}\cdots T_{i+1}\rvert)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]U_{0},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{1},

(3.2)

\displaystyle U_{0}\lvert S_{-n+1}^{\ast}\cdots S_{0}^{\ast}\rvert=D[(\lvert T_{i-n+1}^{\ast}\cdots T_{i}^{\ast}\rvert)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]U_{0},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{1},

(3.3)

\displaystyle T^{[k]}(\mathcal{R}(U_{0}))\perp T^{[m]}(\mathcal{R}(U_{0})),\qquad k,m\in\mathbb{Z},\ k\not=m,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\bigvee_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}T^{[i]}(\mathcal{R}(U_{0}))=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H),

where

T^{[k]}=\begin{cases}T^{k},&\text{if }k\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\\
I,&\text{if }k=0\\
T^{\ast-k},&\text{if }-k\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\end{cases}.

(ii)

If there exists an isometry
U_{0}\in\mathbf{B}(H,\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))
satisfying (
3.1
)-(
3.4
), then
S
and
T
are unitarily equivalent.

Proof.

(i). Let
U=[U_{i,j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbf{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))
be a unitary operator satisfying
US=TU
. Then, we also have
US^{\ast}=T^{\ast}U
. This implies that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,
U\lvert S^{n}\rvert^{2}=\lvert T^{n}\rvert^{2}U
and
U\lvert S^{\ast n}\rvert^{2}=\lvert T^{\ast n}\rvert^{2}U
. Using the square root theorem (see
[
13
, p.265]
) and Berberian’s trick (see
[
2
]
) we obtain that
U\lvert S^{n}\rvert=\lvert T^{n}\rvert U
and
U\lvert S^{\ast n}\rvert=\lvert T^{\ast n}\rvert U
. By Lemma
2.1
, the above equalities take the form

(3.5)

\displaystyle U_{i,j}\lvert S_{j+n}\cdots S_{j+1}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert T_{i+n}\cdots T_{i+1}\rvert U_{i,j},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z},\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{1},

(3.6)

\displaystyle U_{i,j}\lvert S_{j-n+1}^{\ast}\cdots S_{j}^{\ast}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert T_{i-n+1}^{\ast}\cdots T_{i}^{\ast}\rvert U_{i,j},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z},\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}.

We will prove that the isometry
U_{0}
satisfies (
3.1
)-(
3.4
). By (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
define
U_{n}\!:H\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)
by the formula:

U_{n}x=Ux^{(n)},\qquad x\in H.

Since
U
is unitary,
U_{n}
is isometric for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Next, by Lemma
2.2
,

(3.7)

U_{i+n,n}S_{n}\cdots S_{1}=T_{i+n}\cdots T_{i+1}U_{i,0},\qquad i\in\mathbb{Z},\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
let

(3.8)

\displaystyle S_{n}\cdots S_{1}

\displaystyle=V_{n}\lvert S_{n}\cdots S_{1}\rvert

(3.9)

\displaystyle T_{i+n}\cdots T_{i+1}

\displaystyle=W_{n,i}\lvert T_{i+n}\cdots T_{i+1}\rvert

be the polar decompositions. Since, by (
2.1
),
S_{n}\cdots S_{1}
and
T_{i+n}\cdots T_{i+1}
are quasi-invertible, it follows from (
2.2
) that partial isometries
V_{n}
and
W_{n,i}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
) are unitary operators. We have

\displaystyle W_{n,i}\lvert T_{i+n}\cdots T_{i+1}\rvert U_{i,0}=T_{i+n}\cdots T_{i+1}U_{i,0}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ProofFormShiftInterweavingPositive}}}{{=}}U_{i+n,n}S_{n}\cdots S_{1}=U_{i+n,n}V_{n}\lvert S_{n}\cdots S_{1}\rvert,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{1},\ i\in\mathbb{Z}.

This implies that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1},\ i\in\mathbb{Z}
,

W_{n,i}^{\ast}U_{i+n,n}V_{n}\lvert S_{n}\cdots S_{1}\rvert=\lvert T_{i+n}\cdots T_{i+1}\rvert U_{i,0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ProofFormModulusOfShift}}}{{=}}U_{i,0}\lvert S_{n}\cdots S_{1}\rvert.

Since
S_{n}\cdots S_{1}
is quasi-invertible,
\mathcal{N}(\lvert S_{n}\cdots S_{1}\rvert)=\{0\}
, which implies that
\overline{\mathcal{R}(\lvert S_{n}\cdots S_{1}\rvert)}=H
. It follows from (
3.5
) that
W_{n,i}^{\ast}U_{i+n,n}V_{n}=U_{i,0}
; using the fact that
W_{n,i}
is unitary, it can be written equivalently as

(3.10)

U_{i+n,n}=W_{n,i}U_{i,0}V_{n}^{\ast},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{1},\ i\in\mathbb{Z}.

From (
3.10
) and (
2.5
) we can derive that

(3.11)

U_{n}=F^{n}D[(W_{n,i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]U_{0}V_{n}^{\ast},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}.

Now we prove similar formula for
U_{-n}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
); we provide the whole reasoning, because it differs from the above in some details. Again, by Lemma
2.2
we have

(3.12)

U_{i-n,-n}(S_{-n+1}^{\ast}\cdots S_{0}^{\ast})=T_{i-n+1}^{\ast}\cdots T_{i}^{\ast}U_{i,0},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{1},\ i\in\mathbb{Z}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
let

(3.13)

\displaystyle S_{0}\cdots S_{-n+1}

\displaystyle=V_{-n}\lvert S_{0}\cdots S_{-n+1}\rvert

(3.14)

\displaystyle T_{i}\cdots T_{i-n+1}

\displaystyle=W_{-n,i}\lvert T_{i}\cdots T_{i-n+1}\rvert

be the polar decompositions. As before, since
S_{0}\cdots S_{-n+1}
and
T_{i}\cdots T_{i-n+1}
are quasi-invertible, partial isometries
V_{-n}
and
W_{-n,i}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
) are unitary operators. We infer from
[
15
, Exercise 7.26(c)]
that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(3.15)

\displaystyle(S_{0}\cdots S_{-n+1})^{\ast}

\displaystyle=S_{-n+1}^{\ast}\cdots S_{0}^{\ast}=V_{-n}^{\ast}\lvert S_{-n+1}^{\ast}\cdots S_{0}^{\ast}\rvert

U_{-n}=F^{-n}D[(W_{-n,i}^{\ast})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]U_{0}V_{-n},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}.

Now we will prove
(
3.3
)
. First, observe that if

\displaystyle=F^{k}D[(W_{k,i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]U_{0}\lvert S_{k}\cdots S_{1}\rvert

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ProofFormModulusOfShift}}}{{=}}F^{k}D[(W_{k,i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]D[(\lvert T_{i+k}\cdots T_{i+1}\rvert)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]U_{0}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lemma }\ref{LemBasicPropertiesOfOperatorShifts}}}{{=}}T^{k}U_{0}.

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ProofFormModulusOfAdjoint}}}{{=}}F^{k}D[(W_{k,i}^{\ast})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]D[(\lvert T_{i+k+1}^{\ast}\cdots T_{i}^{\ast}\rvert)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]U_{0}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lemma }\ref{LemBasicPropertiesOfOperatorShifts}}}{{=}}T^{\ast-k}U_{0}.

Since

Remark 3.2
.

The careful look on the proof of Theorem

Next, we show a convenient necessary condition for bilateral weighted shifts to be unitarily equivalent by a unitary operator of diagonal form.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that
S
and
T
are as in Theorem
3.1
. Assume that there exists a unitary operator
U=[U_{i,j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbf{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))
of diagonal form such that
US=TU
. Then there exists
p\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\lvert S_{i}\rvert
and
\lvert T_{i+p}\rvert
are unitarily equivalent for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
; in particular,
\sigma(\lvert S_{i}\rvert)=\sigma(\lvert T_{i+p}\rvert)
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Since
U
is of diagonal form, there exists
p\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
U_{i+p,i}
(
i\in\mathbb{Z}
) are the only non-zero entries of the matrix representation of
U
. This implies that
U_{i+p,i}
has to be unitary for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. The equalities
US=TU
and
US^{\ast}=T^{\ast}U
implies that
U\lvert S\rvert=\lvert T\rvert U
. Now, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

U_{i+p,i}\lvert S_{i+1}\rvert=\lvert T_{i+p+1}\rvert U_{i+p,i},\qquad i\in\mathbb{Z}.

Hence,
\lvert S_{i}\rvert
and
\lvert T_{i+p}\rvert
are unitarily equivalent for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

It can be easily deduced from the Schur decomposition (see
[
6
, Theorem 2.31]
) that if
A\in\mathbf{B}(\mathbb{C}^{m})
, then

. Hence, in case

(

) the necessary condition

in Lemma

actually says that if the two bilateral weighted shifts

and

are unitarily equivalent by a unitary operator of diagonal form, then the moduli of eigenvalues of

and

are equal for some

(see
[
9
, Proposition 2.7]
for the proof in the case

). However, in
[
9
, Example 2.8]
the author showed that this condition does not guarantee that the shifts are unitarily equivalent by an operator of diagonal form, even if the weights are normal and commuting. The next result shed a new light on the aforementioned example.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
H=\mathbb{C}^{m}
,

. Let

be the bilateral weighted shifts with weights
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
(T_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, respectively. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

S
and
T
are unitarily equivalent by a unitary operator
U\in\mathbf{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))
of diagonal form,

(ii)

there exist
p\in\mathbb{Z}
and two orthonormal basis

of

(a)

\lvert S_{i}\rvert

\lvert T_{i}\rvert

and

(c)

and every

Proof.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). Since
S_{i}

) are normal and commuting, we infer from Fuglede-Putnam theorem (see
[

T_{i}
’s (
i\in\mathbb{Z}
). It follows from Corollary
3.4
.(i) that the isometry

satisfies (

), (
3.25
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). By (i),
U_{0}
is such that

for all

and

is unitary with some

. Since
\lvert S_{i}\rvert

) are normal and commuting, we deduce from
[

, Theorem 2.5.5]
that there exists an orthonormal basis

such that

\lvert S_{i}\rvert v_{n}=\lambda_{i,n}v_{n},\qquad i\in\mathbb{Z},\ n=1,\ldots,m;

in particular, (a) holds. Using (
3.24
) and (

\lambda_{i,n}U_{p,0}v_{n}=U_{p,0}\lvert S_{i}\rvert v_{n}=\lvert T_{p+i}\rvert U_{p,0}v_{n},\qquad i\in\mathbb{Z},\ n=1,\ldots,m.

By the above,
(U_{p,0}v_{n})_{n=1}^{m}
is the orthonormal basis of

satisfying (b) and (c).

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i). Define

as follows:

Clearly,

is unitary. For every

, if

, then

\displaystyle U_{p,0}\lvert S_{i}\rvert v_{n}=\lambda_{i,n}U_{p,0}v_{n}=\lambda_{i,n}w_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{=}}\lvert T_{i+p}\rvert w_{n}=\lvert T_{i+p}\rvert U_{p,0}v_{n}.

Then

is an isometry satisfying (

∎

Now let us present another characterization of unitary equivalence of bilateral shifts with operator weights in terms of factors in their polar decompositions.

Theorem 3.8
.

Suppose that
S
and
T
are as in Theorem
3.1
. Let
S=V_{S}\lvert S\rvert
and
T=V_{T}\lvert T\rvert
be the polar decompositions of
S
and
T
, respectively. For a unitary operator

the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

US=TU
,

(ii)

U\lvert S\rvert=\lvert T\rvert U
and
UV_{S}=V_{T}U
.

Proof.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). Repeating an argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have
U\lvert S\rvert=\lvert T\rvert U
. Moreover,

\displaystyle UV_{S}\lvert S\rvert=US=TU=V_{T}\lvert T\rvert U=V_{T}U\lvert S\rvert.

Since
\overline{\mathcal{R}(\lvert S\rvert)}=\overline{\mathcal{R}(D[(\lvert S_{i+1}\rvert)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}])}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H)
, it follows that
UV_{S}=V_{T}U
.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i). We have
US=UV_{S}\lvert S\rvert=V_{T}U\lvert S\rvert=V_{T}\lvert T\rvert U=TU
.
∎

By Lemma
2.3
, the operators
V_{S}
and
V_{T}
in the above theorem are weighted shifts with unitary weights. By Corollary
3.3
, the equality
UV_{S}=V_{T}U
always holds with some unitary operator

; however, this operator

does not have to satisfy
U\lvert S\rvert=\lvert T\rvert U
. Since the unitary operator

making
S
and
T
unitarily equivalent can be found among all unitary operators intertwining
V_{s}
and
V_{T}
, the possible further research is to describe all unitary operators, which intertwine two shifts with unitary weights.

The rest of this section is devoted to study unitary equivalence of shifts with positive commuting weights. First, we prove that unitary operator intertwining bilateral shifts with positive weights has to be constant on diagonals (see
[
12
, Lemma 5]
for similar result for self-adjoint operator intertwining bilateral shifts).

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space. Let
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},(T_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbf{B}(H)
be two uniformly bounded sequences of positive operators on
H
and denote by
S,T
the bilateral weighted shifts with weights
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
(T_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, respectively. Suppose
U=[U_{i,j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbf{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},H))
is a unitary operator such that
US=TU
. Then
U
is constant on diagonals, that is,
U_{i+1,j+1}=U_{i,j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.8
we obtain that
U\lvert S\rvert=\lvert T\rvert U
. Since the weights of
S
and
T
are positive, by Lemma
2.1
.(iii) we have
U_{i,j}S_{j+1}=T_{i+1}U_{i,j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. On the other hand, from Lemma
2.2
it follows that
U_{i+1,j+1}S_{j+1}=T_{i+1}U_{i,j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\overline{\mathcal{R}(S_{j})}=H
for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain that
U_{i+1,j+1}=U_{i,j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

The next result states that if

Theorem 3.10
.

Suppose
H=\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Let
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},(T_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbf{B}(H)

and

(i)

and

(ii)

Before we state the proof we need two lemmata.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let

\langle U_{i_{k},0}v_{j},w_{k}\rangle\not=0.

(i). For

This implies that there exists

\displaystyle 1

U_{0}S_{n}=D[(T_{i+n})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]U_{0},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
j=1,\ldots,k
we have

U_{0}S_{n}e_{j}=U_{0}x_{n,j}e_{j}=(x_{n,j}e_{j})^{(j)}

and

D[(T_{i+n})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]U_{0}e_{j}=D[(T_{i+n})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}]e_{j}^{(j)}=(T_{j+n}e_{j})^{(j)}=(x_{n,j}e_{j})^{(j)}.

Hence, (
3.36
) holds. Next, we will check (
3.3
). Suppose
n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and
n\not=m
. We will show that

(3.37)

\langle T^{[n]}U_{0}x,T^{[m]}U_{0}y\rangle=0,\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{k}.

It is enough to verify (
3.37
) for
x=e_{j_{n}}
,
y=e_{j_{m}}
, where
j_{n},j_{m}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. By Lemma
2.1
, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and
j=1,\ldots,k
,

T^{\ell}U_{0}e_{j}=T^{\ell}e_{j}^{(j)}=(T_{j+n}\cdots T_{j+1}e_{j})^{j+\ell}=(x_{\ell,j}\cdots x_{1,j}e_{j})^{j+\ell}.

Hence,
\langle T^{n}U_{0}e_{j_{n}},T^{m}U_{0}e_{j_{m}}\rangle=0
if
j_{n}+n\not=j_{m}+m
. If
j_{n}+n=j_{m}+m
, then
j_{n}\not=j_{m}
, because
n\not=m
. Thus,
\langle T^{n}U_{0}e_{j_{n}},T^{m}U_{0}e_{j_{m}}\rangle=0
also when
j_{n}+n=j_{m}+m
. Similarly, we can show that (
3.37
) holds for
m,n
being both non-positive or of different sign. It remains to verify (
3.4
). It is enough to check that

x^{(n)}\in\bigvee_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}T^{[\ell]}\mathcal{R}(U_{0}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{C}^{k},n\in\mathbb{Z}.

It is a matter of routine to verify that for
x\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

x^{(n)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle x,e_{j}\rangle e_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle x,e_{j}\rangle\frac{1}{\lVert T^{[n-j]}U_{0}e_{j}\rVert^{2}}T^{[n-j]}U_{0}e_{j}\in\bigvee_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}T^{[\ell]}\mathcal{R}(U_{0})

(see the proof of Theorem
3.10
).
By Corollary
3.4
and Remark
3.2
,
S
and
T
are unitarily equivalent by a unitary operator with
k
non-zero diagonals. Now we will show that there is no unitary operator with at most
k-1
non-zero diagonals making
S
and
T
unitarily equivalent. Suppose to the contrary
U
is such an operator. By Corollary
3.4
.(i),
U_{0}\in\mathbf{B}(\mathbb{C}^{k},\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k}))
has
k-1
non-zero entries and satisfies (
3.36
); suppose
U_{n_{1},0},\ldots,U_{n_{\ell},0}\in\mathbf{B}(\mathbb{C}^{k})
are the only non-zero entries of
U_{0}
, where
\ell\leqslant k-1
,
n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
j=1,\ldots,\ell
and
n_{1}<\ldots<n_{\ell}
. By (
3.36
),

x_{n,j}U_{n_{i},0}e_{j}=U_{n_{i},0}S_{n}e_{j}=T_{n_{i}+n}U_{n_{i},0}e_{j},\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell,\ j=1,\ldots,k.

Since,
x_{n,j}
is the eigenvalue only of the weight
T_{n+j}
, it follows that

U_{n_{i},0}e_{j}\not=0\implies n_{i}=j,\qquad j=1,\ldots,k,\ i=1,\ldots,\ell.

From the fact that
U_{0}
is isometric, we deduce that for every
j=1,\ldots,k
there exists
n_{i}
such that
U_{n_{i},0}e_{j}\not=0
. Hence,
\{1,\ldots,k\}=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{\ell}\}
, which is impossible (we asssumed
\ell\leqslant k-1
).

Monotonicity of inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials
J. Baine

Abstract
For arbitrary Coxeter systems, we prove that inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials satisfy a monotonicity property. This follows from the validity of Soergel’s conjecture and the existence of injective morphisms between Rouquier complexes in the mixed perverse Hecke category. The monotonicity property is generalised to parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials.



1
Introduction

Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials
h_{y,x}
arise as the change of basis coefficients between the standard and Kazhdan-Lusztig bases of a Hecke algebra.
Since their introduction it was immediately apparent these polynomials exhibit many remarkable properties.
Two of the most famous properties they satisfy are:

\begin{array}[]{lC}\text{non-negativity:}&h_{y,x}\in\Z_{\geq 0}[v],\\
\text{monotonicity:}&h_{y,x}\preceq v^{\ell(z)-\ell(y)}~h_{z,x}\end{array}

where
z\leq y\leq z
in the Bruhat order and
\preceq
denotes coefficient-wise comparison.
Similarly, inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials
h^{y,x}
are known to satisfy:

\begin{array}[]{lC}\text{non-negativity:}&h^{y,x}\in\Z_{\geq 0}[v]\end{array}

and, in the finite case, an analogous monotonicity property.
Despite their combinatorial origins, no elementary proofs of these properties are known.
Instead a fecund approach has been to study categories that categorify Hecke algebras, and deduce these properties from the structure of objects therein.

The main result of this note is the following monotonicity property of inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials, which holds for arbitrary Coxeter systems.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(W,S)
be an arbitrary Coxeter system.
For any
x,y,z\in W
satisfying
z\leq y\leq x
in the Bruhat order, the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials satisfy

\displaystyle h^{z,y}\preceq v^{\ell(x)-\ell(y)}~h^{z,x}.

More generally, for any subset
I\subseteq S
and minimal length coset representatives
x,y,z\in W^{I}
satisfying
z\leq y\leq x
in the induced Bruhat order, the antispherical inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials satisfy

\displaystyle n^{z,y}\preceq v^{\ell(x)-\ell(y)}~n^{z,x}.

This result can be seen as a combinatorial shadow of the existence of injective morphisms between certain Rouquier complexes in the heart of the perverse
t
-structure on the bounded homotopy category of diagrammatic Soergel bimodules associated to a realisation satisfying Soergel’s conjecture.
This approach is natural and, in a sense we will soon make clear, brings this chapter of the story of monotonicity in Kazhdan-Lusztig theory full circle.

In
[
Irv
]
Irving first showed that Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials for finite Weyl groups exhibit a monotonicity property.
That proof is remarkable as Irving actually proved the corresponding statement for inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials by studying the socle filtrations of Verma modules in the principal block of category
\cO_{0}
.
He then deduced the monotonicity of classical Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials using the Kazhdan-Lusztig inversion formula.

Since the work of Beilinson, Ginzburg and Soergel in
[
BGS
]
, the paradigm has emerged that Kazhdan-Lusztig theory is most evident when studying
\Z
-graded Hecke categories.
A
\Z
-graded analogue of
\cO_{0}
was constructed in
[
BGS
]
and it was shown that Verma modules admit lifts to this category.
Thus, monotonicity follows from the existence of injective morphisms between graded Verma modules.

In
[
Soe1
,
Soe3
]
Soergel constructed various categories of (bi)modules associated to Coxeter systems.
Soergel conjectured that, under various assumptions, certain filtrations of these bimodules are encoded by Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials.
Elias and Williamson proved Soergel’s conjecture in
[
EW1
]
.
When the Coxeter group is a Weyl group, the graded category
\cO
considered in
[
BGS
]
is equivalent to the heart of the perverse
t
-structure on the bounded homotopy category of Soergel modules (satisfying Soergel’s conjecture).
Under this equivalence, graded Verma modules correspond to the Rouquier complexes we consider.

Thus, Rouquier complexes for arbitrary Coxeter systems are the natural objects to consider if one is interested in inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials of arbitrary Coxeter systems.
Given that the story of monotonicity in Kazhdan-Lusztig theory commenced with Irving’s study of Verma modules in
[
Irv
]
, it seems apt that the final families of (inverse) Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials shown to exhibit monotonicity properties are those arising from natural generalisations of Verma modules.

The structure of this note is as follows:
In section 1 we fix notation relating to Hecke algebras.
In section 2 we introduce various Hecke categories.
In section 3 we show that injective morphisms between objects in mixed perverse Hecke categories give rise to families of polynomials satisfying a monotonicity property.
In section 4 we introduce Rouquier complexes and prove the existence of injective morphisms between them, and deduce the main theorem.
In section 5 we recall parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig theory and deduce the antispherical result from the non-parabolic setting.

2
Hecke algebras

We begin by fixing notation related to Hecke algebras, their Kazhdan-Lusztig bases and the associated (inverse) Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials.

Let
(W,S)
be an arbitrary Coxeter system.
Denote by
\ell
the length function on
W
, and
\leq
the Bruhat order.

Associated to the Coxeter system
(W,S)
is the
Hecke algebra

H
.
It is the associative
\Z[v,v^{-1}]
-algebra on the symbols
\{\delta_{x}~|~x\in W\}
subject to the relations:

\displaystyle(\delta_{s}+v)(\delta_{s}-v^{-1})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{for each }s\in S,\text{ and,}

\displaystyle\delta_{x}\delta_{y}

\displaystyle=\delta_{xy}

\displaystyle\text{whenever }\ell(x)+\ell(y)=\ell(xy).

It follows from
[
Tits
]
that the symbols
\{\delta_{x}~|~x\in W\}
are a basis of
H
, which we call the standard basis.
Observe that
\delta_{s}^{-1}=\delta_{s}+v-v^{-1}
, so each
\delta_{x}
is invertible.

The Hecke algebra
H
has a unique
\Z
-linear involution satisfying
\overline{v}=v^{-1}
and
\overline{\delta_{x}}=\delta_{x^{-1}}^{-1}
for each
x\in W
.
It is a classical result of Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
KL
]
that
H
has a unique basis
\{b_{x}~|~x\in W\}
, called the
Kazhdan-Lusztig basis
, satisfying
\overline{b_{x}}=b_{x}
and
b_{x}\in\delta_{x}+\sum_{y<x}v\Z[z]\delta_{y}
for each
x\in W
.
We adopt the normalisation of
[
Soe2
]
, where
b_{s}=\delta_{s}+v
for each
s\in S
.

For each
x,y\in W
, the
Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

h_{y,x}
and
inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

h^{y,x}
are defined so that the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle b_{x}=\sum_{y}h_{y,x}\delta_{y},

\displaystyle\delta_{x}=\sum_{y}(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(y)}h^{y,x}b_{y}.

That is, they arise as the change of basis coefficients between the standard and Kazhdan-Lusztig bases of
H
.
Define
\mu(y,x)\in\Z
to be the coefficient of
v
in
h_{y,x}
.
It is a celebrated result of Elias and Williamson
[
EW1
]
that
h_{y,x},h^{y,x}\in\Z_{\geq 0}[v]
and consequently
\mu(y,x)\in\Z_{\geq 0}
.

We conclude with the following well-known, combinatorial lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Fix
x\in W
and
s\in S
.
If
x<xs
, then
b_{x}b_{s}=b_{xs}+\sum_{ys<y}\mu(y,x)b_{y}
.
Alternatively, if
xs<x
, then
b_{x}b_{s}=(v+v^{-1})b_{x}
.

Proof.

The first claim is immediate from the fact that

\displaystyle\delta_{x}b_{s}=\begin{cases}\delta_{xs}+v\delta_{x}&\text{if }xs>x,\\
\delta_{xs}+v^{-1}\delta_{x}&\text{if }xs<x,\end{cases}

and the requirement that
h_{y,x}\in v\Z[v]
if
y<x
.
The second follows from a simple induction on
\ell(x)
and the fact that
b_{s}^{2}=(v+v^{-1})b_{s}
.
∎

3
Hecke categories

Any category whose (split, triangulated, etc.) Grothendieck group is isomorphic to the Hecke algebra is called a Hecke category.
In this section we briefly recall various Hecke categories that will be the stage upon which our are arguments are set.

3.1
Preliminaries

Recall the notion of a realisation, as introduced in
[
EW2
, §3.1]
.
This is the data from which we will construct the diagrammatic categories in section
3.2
.

Fix a Coxeter system
(W,S)
and, for any simple reflections
s,t\in S
, let
m_{st}
denote the order of
st
in
W
.
Define the
\R
-vector space
\h:=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\R\alphac_{s}
and let
\h^{*}:=\Hom_{\R}(\h,\R)
.
Denote by
\left\langle-,-\right\rangle:\h\times\h^{*}\rightarrow\R
, the natural pairing.
For each
s\in S
fix an element
\alpha_{s}\in\h^{*}
so that

\displaystyle\left\langle\alphac_{s},\alpha_{t}\right\rangle=-2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{m_{st}}\right)

where
\left\langle\alphac_{s},\alpha_{t}\right\rangle=-2
when
m_{st}=\infty
.
Finally, we endow
\h
with the structure of a
W
-module by requiring
s(\lambda)=\lambda-\left\langle\lambda,\alpha_{s}\right\rangle\alphac_{s}
for each
s\in S
and
\lambda\in\h
.
The data
(\h,\{\alphac_{s}\},\{\alpha_{s}\})
is called the
geometric realisation
of
(W,S)
.
It is a realisation in the sense of
[
EW2
, §3.1]
.

Set
R:=\Sym(\h)
, the symmetric algebra on the
\R
-vector space
\h
, which we consider as a graded
\R
-vector space where
\h
is in degree
2
.
Extend the
W
-module structure on
\h
to a
W
-module structure on
R
in the obvious way.

Remark 3
.

We consider the geometric realisation (over
\R
) because it is known to satisfy Soergel’s conjecture, see
[
EW1
]
.
The arguments in this note can easily be adapted to any balanced realisation satisfying Soergel’s conjecture.

If each homogeneous polynomial appearing in a diagram is a monomial, then the degree of the morphism is the sum of the degrees of the vertices (as given above) and degrees of the monomials.
We write

Categories enriched over
\Z

[

We denote the corresponding category with shift by

.
The objects in
\sD^{(1)}_{\BS}(\h)
are

, where
\underline{x}

.
Morphisms in this category are then defined by

The monoidal structure is again given by concatenation of expressions in a manner that is compatible with the shift functor, namely

.
For want of a better name, we will say that the object

n
.

The
Elias-Williamson diagrammatic category

is the Karoubi envelope of the additive hull of

.
It is a Krull-Schmidt,
\R
-linear, additive, monoidal category with shift-functor
(1)
.
Every indecomposable object in

is isomorphic to an object of the form

and

[

, §6.6]

Moreover, if

occurs as a direct summand of

with multiplicity 1, and
B_{x}
does not occur as a direct summand of any

\underline{y}
is an expression of length less than

.

Let
[\sD(\h)]
denote the split Grothendieck group of
\sD(\h)
.
The monoidal structure on
\sD(\h)
endows
[\sD(\h)]
with a ring structure, where

.
Further, we endow

with the structure of a

We conclude by recalling

Namely, there is a unique isomorphism of

-algebras

[

[

In particular, if we identify

with

(1)

In particular, if

3.3
Mixed categories

Finally, we introduce various mixed Hecke categories.
The identity
\delta_{s}=b_{s}-v
implies there are no objects in
\sD(\h)
which categorify the standard basis of

.
Thus, this extra level of complexity is necessary if one is interested in inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials.

Let
\Ch(\h)
denote the category of bounded cochain complexes in
\sD(\h)
.
It is an additive, monoidal category.
Moreover, it has autoequivalence shift-functors
(1)

\sD(\h)
, and
[1]

Given a complex

for the object in cohomological degree

The monoidal structure on
\sD(\h)
induces a monoidal structure on

.
In particular given complexes
C
and

, one defines the complex

by

\displaystyle(C\otimes D)^{i}:=\bigoplus_{i=j+k}C^{j}\star D^{k},

and the differential on

as
d_{C}\otimes 1+(-1)^{j}\otimes d_{D}
.
We will abuse notation by identifying
\sD(\h)
with the full subcategory of complexes concentrated in cohomological degree 0.

It will often be convenient to choose a splitting of each

.
Since
\sD(\h)
is Krull-Schmidt, the multiplicity of each indecomposable summand in

is independent of the choice of splitting, but the summands themselves are not.
No argument we use relies on a particular choice of splitting.
Once a splitting has been chosen, the differential

is represented by a matrix of morphisms in
\sD(\h)
.
Following the notation of
[
Eli
, §4.1]
, an entry of this matrix is called a
summand of the differential
.
In other words, a summand of the differential is the restriction of the differential on
C^{i}
so that its domain is a single indecomposable summand of
C^{i}
and its codomain is a single indecomposable summand of
C^{i+1}
.

A complex
C
in
\Ch(\h)
is said to be

if
C
is has no contractible summands.
Equivalently, no summand of the differential is an isomorphism, see
[
Eli
, §4.1]
.
Given a complex
D
, a complex
C
is said to be a
minimal subcomplex
of

if:
C
is a subcomplex of
D
;
C
is minimal; and, the inclusion
C\hookrightarrow D
is a homotopy equivalence.
Every complex
D
has a minimal complex

, and this complex is unique up to isomorphism in
\Ch(\h)
, see
[
EW1
, §6.1]
.

We define the
mixed Hecke category

.

Consider the full subcategories

4
Minimal complexes and injective morphisms

Having introduced the mixed perverse Hecke category
\Perv(\h)
, we now formulate a numerical condition on the number of indecomposable summands appearing in the minimal complexes of objects that is necessarily satisfied if there is an injective morphism between these objects.

Fix a minimal complex
C^{\min}
for each
C
in
\Perv(\h)
.
As previously noted,
C^{\min}
is unique up to isomorphism
[
EW1
, §6.1]
.
Typically, working with representatives of homotopy classes will not be functorial as composition will only be defined up to chain homotopy.
Fortunately, the validity of Soergel’s conjecture ensures there are no non-trivial chain homotopies between minimal complexes.
Indeed, we have:

Lemma 4
.

For any objects
C
and
D
in
\Perv(\h)
we have

\displaystyle\Hom_{\Perv(\h)}(C,D)\cong\Hom_{\Ch(\h)}(C^{\min},D^{\min}).

Proof.

Since the geometric realisation satisfies Soergel’s conjecture, Soergel’s Hom formula (
2
) implies that for any two objects
B,B^{\prime}
in Soergel degree 0, we have
\Hom_{\sD(\h)}(B(i),B^{\prime}(i-1))=0
.
It follows from the characterisation of
\Perv(\h)
in (
3
) that any chain homotopy is of the form:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phantom{C^{\min}}\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\min,i-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\min,i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\min,i+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phantom{C^{\min}}\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\min,i-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\min,i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\min,i+1}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{i-1}\phantom{x}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phantom{x}f_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{i}\phantom{x}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phantom{x}f_{i+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{i+1}\phantom{x}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

so

.
Hence the claim.
∎

Thus, taking minimal complexes defines a functor
(-)^{\mix}:\Perv(\h)\rightarrow\Ch(\h)
.
Moreover, the following is immediate from Lemma
4
.

Corollary 5
.

The functor
(-)^{\mix}:\Perv(\h)\rightarrow\Ch(\h)
is fully faithful.

Let us now fix some notation regarding the multiplicities of indecomposable summands in minimal complexes.
For any object
C
in
\Perv(\h)
recall that
C^{\min,i}
denotes the object in
\sD(\h)
in cohomological degree
i
in the minimal complex
C^{\min}
.
By the Krull-Schmidt property of
\sD(\h)
and the characterisation of the perverse
t
-structure in (
3
) there is a (non-canonical) splitting

\displaystyle C^{\min,i}\cong\bigoplus_{y\in W}B_{y}(i)^{\oplus m_{y,C}^{i}}

where
m_{y,C}^{i}\in\Z_{\geq 0}
.
However, the Krull-Schmidt property implies the multiplicities
m_{y,C}^{i}
are independent of the choice of splitting and the choice of
C^{\min}
.
Thus, we define
m_{y,C}\in\Z_{\geq 0}[v,v^{-1}]
as

\displaystyle m_{y,C}:=\sum_{i\in\Z}m^{i}_{y,C}~v^{i}.

Finally, we define a partial order
\preceq
on
\Z_{\geq 0}[v,v^{-1}]
where
p\preceq q
if
q-p\in\Z_{\geq 0}[v,v^{-1}]
.
That is,
\preceq
denotes
coefficient-wise inequality
on Laurent polynomials.

We now show that injective morphisms between perverse objects give rise to polynomials satisfying a monotonicity property.

Proposition 6
.

Suppose there is an injective morphism
C\hookrightarrow D
in
\Perv(\h)
, then
m_{y,C}\preceq m_{y,D}
for each
y\in W
.

Proof.

We begin by noting that for any objects
C
and
D
in
\Perv(\h)
, Soergel’s Hom formula implies

\displaystyle\Hom_{\sD(\h)}(C^{\min,i},D^{\min,i})

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\sD(\h)}\left(\bigoplus_{y}B_{y}(i)^{\oplus m^{i}_{y,C}},\bigoplus_{z}B_{z}(i)^{\oplus m^{i}_{z,D}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{y\in W}\Hom_{\sD(\h)}\left(B_{y}^{\oplus m^{i}_{y,C}},B_{y}^{\oplus m^{i}_{z,D}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{y\in W}\text{Mat}_{m^{i}_{y,C}\times m^{i}_{y,D}}(\R)

where Mat

denotes the space of

matrices over
\R
.
In particular, the full, additive, non-monoidal subcategory

W

\R
-vector-spaces, where
W
is only considered as a set.
Note that
\sD(\h,i)
can also be considered as the non-full subcategory of Soergel bimodules in Soergel degree
i

Let

and assume for a contradiction there exists some

.
By Corollary

\sD(\h,i)

By assumption, there is some

Now consider the cochain complex

and whose differential is induced by the differential on

Since

.
Consequently,

is a minimal complex which is non-zero, so it is not homotopic to the zero complex.
By construction

is in Soergel degree

of

is in
\Perv(\h)
.
As

By construction the composition

is zero.
Hence Corollary

implies

is a subobject of the kernel of

, which contradicts the fact that

is injective.
∎

for all
i\in\Z
.
If both
\Delta_{s}\otimes\sigma_{\geq i+1}C
and
\Delta_{s}\otimes C^{\min,i}[-i]
are objects in
\cT^{\geq 0}
, then so is
\Delta_{s}\otimes\sigma_{\geq i}C
, by
[
EW1
, §6.3]
.
Hence left
t
-exactness of
\Delta_{s}\otimes(-)
follows from showing
\Delta_{s}\otimes C^{\min,i}[-i]
is in
\cT^{\geq 0}
, and induction on
i
.

Suppose
B_{y}(j)
is any indecomposable summand of
C^{\min,i}[-i]
.
If
sy>y
then Soergel’s conjecture (
1
) and Lemma
2
imply

\displaystyle(\Delta_{s}\otimes B_{y}(j))^{\min}\cong~~\dots\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow\left(B_{sy}\oplus\bigoplus_{sz<z}B_{z}^{\oplus\mu(z,y)}\right)(j)\longrightarrow B_{y}(j+1)\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow\dots

Hence
\Delta_{s}\otimes B_{y}(j)
is in
\cT^{\geq 0}
.
Alternatively, if
sy<y
then Soergel’s conjecture (
1
) and Lemma
2
imply

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}^{\min}\otimes B_{y}(j)\cong~~\dots\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow B_{y}(j-1)\oplus B_{y}(j+1)\longrightarrow B_{y}(j+1)\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow\dots

Since
\Delta_{s}\otimes(-)
is an autoequivalence and
B_{y}(j)
is indecomposable, it follows
\Delta_{s}\otimes B_{y}(j)
is indecomposable.
Indecomposability and Soergel’s Hom formula (
2
), imply that the subcomplex
B_{y}(j+1)\rightarrow B_{y}(j+1)
has a differential which is an isomorphism, so is contractible.
Hence

\displaystyle(\Delta_{s}\otimes B_{y}(j))^{\min}\cong~~\dots\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow B_{y}(j-1)\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow\dots

and
\Delta_{s}\otimes B_{y}(j)
is in
\cT^{\geq 0}
.
Hence the claim.
∎

We can now show the existence of injective morphisms between Rouquier complexes.

Proposition 8
.

Suppose
y\leq x
, then there exists an injective morphism
\Delta_{y}\langle\ell(y)\rangle\hookrightarrow\Delta_{x}\langle\ell(x)\rangle
.

Proof.

Recall that the Bruhat order is generated by relations of the form
x^{\prime}<x
where:
x=usw
;
x^{\prime}=uw
;
s\in S
;
\ell(x)=\ell(u)+\ell(s)+\ell(w)
; and
\ell(x^{\prime})=\ell(u)+\ell(w)
.
Hence it suffices to show that there is an injective morphism
\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\langle-1\rangle\hookrightarrow\Delta_{x}
, as the desired morphism can then be obtained by composition.

It is easy to show that, for each
s\in S
, there is an exact sequence

\displaystyle\Delta_{\id}\langle-1\rangle\hookrightarrow\Delta_{s}\twoheadrightarrow\Delta_{\id}\otimes B_{s}

in
\Perv(\h)
, which gives rise to a distinguished triangle

\displaystyle\Delta_{\id}\langle-1\rangle\longrightarrow\Delta_{s}\longrightarrow\Delta_{\id}\otimes B_{s}\longrightarrow

in
D^{\mix}(\h)
.
Since we must necessarily have
u<us
, applying the functor
\Delta_{u}\otimes(-)
to the preceding distinguished triangle gives the distinguished triangle

\displaystyle\Delta_{u}\langle-1\rangle\longrightarrow\Delta_{us}\longrightarrow\Delta_{u}\otimes B_{s}\longrightarrow.

By
[
EW1
, §6.5]
both
\Delta_{u}
and
\Delta_{us}
are perverse, so
\Delta_{u}\otimes B_{s}
is an object in
\cT^{\leq 0}
.
By Lemma
7
,
\Delta_{u}\otimes(-)
is left
t
-exact, so
\Delta_{u}\otimes B_{s}
is an object in
\cT^{\geq 0}
.
Hence
\Delta_{u}\otimes B_{s}
is perverse.

Again, applying the functor
(-)\otimes\Delta_{w}
, gives rise to a distinguished triangle

\displaystyle\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\langle-1\rangle\longrightarrow\Delta_{x}\longrightarrow\Delta_{u}\otimes B_{s}\otimes\Delta_{w}\longrightarrow,

where we have used the length conditions on
x
and
x^{\prime}
.
By an argument identical to above, we see
\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\langle-1\rangle
,
\Delta_{x}
and
\Delta_{u}\otimes B_{s}\otimes\Delta_{w}
are all perverse.
Hence we obtain an exact sequence

\displaystyle\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\langle-1\rangle\hookrightarrow\Delta_{x}\twoheadrightarrow\Delta_{u}\otimes B_{s}\otimes\Delta_{w}

from which we obtain the desired injection.
∎

Remark 9

In

the authors construct an analogue of
\Perv(\h)

In this setting they show the existence of injective morphisms

[

From this, one can deduce that for each simple object

In particular, inverse
p

5.2
The first half of the first theorem.

If we wish to prove the statement in Theorem
1
pertaining to inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials, it suffices, by Propositions
6
and
8
, to show

\displaystyle m_{y,\Delta_{x}}=h^{y,x}

for each
x,y\in W
.
This statement is asserted without proof in
[
EW1
, §6.5]
.
For the sake of completeness, we provide a proof.

We first require the following parity property of inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials.

Lemma 10
.

For any
x,y\in W
we have
h^{y,x}\in v^{\ell(xy)}\Z[v^{-2},v^{2}]
.

Proof.

We prove the claim by induction on
\ell(x)
.
Note that it is trivially true for
x=\id
, as
h^{\id,\id}=1
.
Now suppose the claim holds for
x
, and
s\in S
is chosen so that
xs>x
.
Then, by assumption, one has

\displaystyle\delta_{xs}=\delta_{x}(b_{s}-v)

\displaystyle\in\sum_{y}v^{\ell(xy)}\Z[v^{-2},v^{2}]b_{y}(b_{s}-v)

Now note that if
ys<y
then Lemma
2
implies

\displaystyle v^{\ell(xy)}\Z[v^{-2},v^{2}]b_{y}(b_{s}-v)=v^{\ell(xy)-1}\Z[v^{-2},v^{2}]b_{y}=v^{\ell(xsy)}\Z[v^{-2},v^{2}]b_{y}.

Alternatively, if
ys>y
then Lemma
2
implies

\displaystyle v^{\ell(xy)}\Z[v^{-2},v^{2}]b_{y}(b_{s}-v)=v^{\ell(xy)}\Z[v^{-2},v^{2}]\left(b_{ys}-vb_{y}+\sum_{zs<z}\mu(z,y)b_{z}\right).

The claim then follows from the fact that:
\ell(xy)\equiv\ell(xsys)\mod 2
; and, if
zs<z
then
\ell(xy)\equiv\ell(xsz)\mod 2
.
∎

We now show that inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials encode the Jordan-Hölder multiplicities of Rouquier complexes.

Proposition 11
.

For any
x,y\in W
we have
m_{y,\Delta_{x}}=h^{y,x}
.

Proof.

We first show that
[\Delta_{x}]=\delta_{x}
for each
x\in W
.
We proceed by induction on
\ell(x)
.
By definition we have
\Delta_{\id}\cong B_{\id}
, so
[\Delta_{\id}]=b_{\id}=1
.
Now observe that since
\Delta_{s}=F_{s}
, the description of the complex in Equation (
5.1
) implies
[\Delta_{s}]=[B_{s}]-v[B_{\id}]=b_{s}-v
.
As
b_{s}=\delta_{s}+v
, it follows
[\Delta_{s}]=\delta_{s}
.
Now assume that
[\Delta_{x}]=\delta_{x}
and
s\in S
is chosen such that
xs>x
then

\displaystyle[\Delta_{xs}]=[F_{s_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes F_{s_{k}}\otimes F_{s}]=[\Delta_{x}\otimes\Delta_{s}]=[\Delta_{x}][\Delta_{s}]=\delta_{x}\delta_{s}=\delta_{xs}.

Hence the first claim.

Now observe that by the validity of Soergel’s conjecture (
1
), we have

\displaystyle[\Delta_{x}]=\sum_{i}\sum_{y}(-1)^{i}~m_{y,\Delta_{x}}^{i}v^{i}[B_{y}]=\sum_{y}\left(\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}~m_{y,\Delta_{x}}^{i}v^{i}\right)b_{y},

where we may exchange the order of summation as objects in
D^{\mix}(\h)
are bounded.
Then the parity property of Lemma
10
implies

\displaystyle(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(y)}h^{y,x}=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}~m_{y,\Delta_{x}}^{i}v^{i}=(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(y)}m_{y,\Delta_{x}}

which proves the claim.
∎

6
Parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

We conclude by showing certain parabolic inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials, introduced by Deodhar in
[
Deo
]
, satisfy an analogous monotonicity property.

Fix a Coxeter system
(W,S)
and a subset
I\subseteq S
.
Let
H_{I}
denote the Hecke algebra associated to the Coxeter system
(W_{I},I)
, which we consider as a subalgebra of
H
.
We can endow
\Z[v,v^{-1}]
with the structure of a right
H_{I}
-module in two distinct ways:
let
\triv^{I}
denote the rank
1

H_{I}
-module where
\delta_{s}
acts by
v^{-1}
for each
s\in I
; and,
\sign^{I}
denote the rank
1

H_{I}
-module where
\delta_{s}
acts by
-v
for each
s\in I
.
The
spherical module

{}^{I}M
and
antispherical module

{}^{I}N
are the right
H
-modules

\displaystyle{}^{I}M:=\Ind_{H^{I}}^{H}\triv^{I},

\displaystyle{}^{I}N:=\Ind_{H^{I}}^{H}\sign^{I}.

Denote by
{}^{I}W
the set of minimal length coset representatives for
W_{I}\backslash W
and
\leq
the induced Bruhat order.
For either module we (abusively) denote by
\delta_{x}^{I}
the element
1\otimes_{H_{I}}\delta_{x}
.
It is a well-known fact that
\{\delta_{x}^{I}~|~x\in{}^{I}W\}
is a basis of each module, which we call the
standard basis
.

The Kazhdan-Lusztig involution restricts to a
\Z
-linear involution of
\Z[v,v^{-1}]
.
Moreover, it is easy to check that this induces involutions on
{}^{I}M
and
{}^{I}N
.
In
[
Deo
]
Deodhar showed
{}^{I}M
and
{}^{I}N
admit bases which are analogous to the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis of
H
.
Namely, the
spherical Kazhdan-Lusztig basis

\{c_{x}~|~x\in{}^{I}W\}
of
{}^{I}M
and the
antispherical Kazhdan-Lusztig basis

\{d_{x}~|~x\in{}^{I}W\}
of
{}^{I}N
are the bases which are uniquely characterised by the properties:

\displaystyle\overline{c_{x}}=c_{x}~~~\text{ and }~~~

\displaystyle c_{x}\in\delta_{x}^{I}+\sum_{y<x}v\Z[v]\delta_{y}^{I}

\displaystyle\overline{d_{x}}=d_{x}~~~\text{ and }~~~

\displaystyle d_{x}\in\delta_{x}^{I}+\sum_{y<x}v\Z[v]\delta_{y}^{I}

for each
x\in{}^{I}W
.

As in the classical case, for each
x,y\in{}^{I}W
the
spherical Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

m_{y,x}
and
spherical inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

m^{y,x}
are defined so that the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle c_{x}=\sum_{y}m_{y,x}\delta_{y}^{I},

\displaystyle\delta_{x}^{I}=\sum_{y}(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(y)}m^{y,x}c_{y}.

Similarly, the
antispherical Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

n_{y,x}
and
antispherical inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

n^{y,x}
are defined so that:

\displaystyle d_{x}=\sum_{y}n_{y,x}\delta_{y}^{I},

\displaystyle\delta_{x}^{I}=\sum_{y}(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(y)}n^{y,x}d_{y}.

Having established this notation, we can now finish the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

The claim for antispherical inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials follows immediately from the classical case and
[
Soe3
, Proposition 3.7]
.
In particular, the latter asserts that for an arbitrary Coxeter system
(W,S)
and subset
I\subseteq S
, for any
x,z\in{}^{I}W
we have an equality
n^{z,x}=h^{z,x}
.
∎

Remark 12
.

We conclude by noting that the spherical inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

This is evident even when

\tA_{n}

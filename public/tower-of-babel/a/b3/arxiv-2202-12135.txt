McKay correspondence and orbifold equivalence
Andrei Ionov

Abstract
. We prove that a pair of singularities related by a transformation arising from the McKay correspondence are orbifold equivalent. From this we deduce a new proof of a McKay type equivalence for the matrix factorization categories.



1.
Introduction

For an algebraic variety
V
with an action of a finite group
G
and a crepant resolution
Y
of the quotient
V/G
the
McKay correspondence
refers to various statements relating the
G
-equivariant geometry of
V
and the geometry of
Y
. The categorified version provides an equivalence of categories

D^{G}({\mathsf{Coh}}(V))\cong D({\mathsf{Coh}}(Y))

under various assumptions on
V,G
and
Y
. For example, in case of
\dim V=2,3
and
Y
being a
G
-Hilbert scheme of
V
the result of this type were established in
[
5
]
and in
[
4
]
this was proved for the case of symplectic resolutions.

Motivated by conformal field theories and defined in
[
10
]
, the
orbifold equivalence
is an equivalence relation on the set of polynomial potentials
W\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. Namely,
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are equivalent if there exists a matrix factorization of
W_{2}-W_{1}
, whose
left
and
right quantum dimensions
in the sense of bicategories are invertible (see also
[
9
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
).

To unify these two concepts we associate to a
G
-invariant function
f
on
V
a function
\hat{f}
on
Y
. In the present paper we prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
3.6
)
Under the assumptions of Section
3
the singularities of
f
and
\hat{f}
are orbifold equivalent.

The observation of this sort were made in
[
1
]
, which was a motivation for the present paper. It was noticed there that several of the known examples of orbifold equivalence fit into this setting. Note that in the most of the known cases the orbifold equivalences are constructed by the computational methods (
[
8
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
14
]
). Our methods are geometric and allow to reprove some of the known results. Studying the examples makes us hope that a class of orbifold equivalences given by a matrix factorization
X
, such that the product of the quantum dimensions is in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{>0}
, is generated, in a sense, by the equivalences provided by the McKay correspondence (Conjecture
5.4
).

From the orbifold equivalence we deduce a new proof of the McKay type equivalence of matrix factorizations categories proved in the most general setting in
[
6
]
(Theorem
3.5
).

Our assumptions mainly concern the McKay correspondence itself. They are satisfied in the setting of
[
5
]
and it was communicated to the author by R. Bezrukavnikov that they are expected to hold in the setting of
[
4
]
, although they are not known to hold in general. In particular, in the essential special case of symplectic resolutions, the Hilbert scheme of points on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
resolving the singularity of
({\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{n}/S_{n}
, they are known to hold (see
[
13
]
).

It would be interesting to see what do our results mean in the language of the conformal field theories. In particular, it seems natural to ask if there is a "physical" or "categorical" version of the McKay correspondence, which could provide orbifold equivalences in a wider range of bicategories. We refer to
[
21
]
for the discussion of Landau-Ginzburg/conformal field theory correspondence, which should be a key to these questions.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we briefly recall the background on matrix factorizations and orbifold equivalence. In Section
3
we introduce our setting, including the discussion of the McKay correspondence, and state our results in details. In Section
4
we prove our results. In Section
5
we will look at the known examples of the orbifold equivalences and see how they fit into our setting and conjecture what type of orbifold equivalences are generated by McKay correspondence.

2.1.
Matrix factorizations

We refer to
[

]

Let
S
be an algebraic variety and let
W\in\Gamma({\mathcal{O}}_{S})
be a global function on
S
. Respectively, let
R
be a commutative ring and
W\in R
be its element.

Definition 2.2
.

The category of matrix factorizations
\mathrm{MF}(W)=\mathrm{MF}(S,W)
(respectively
\mathrm{MF}(R,W)
) of
W
is a differential
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded category, whose objects are pairs
(X,d_{X})
, where
X=X^{0}\oplus X^{1}
is a locally free
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded sheaf (of finite rank) on
S
(respectively projective module over
R
) and
d_{E}
is its odd endomorphism satisfying
d_{X}^{2}=W\cdot{\rm id}_{X}
.

The morphism complex between
(X,d_{X})
and
(X^{\prime},d_{X^{\prime}})
is a graded space
\mathop{\sf Hom}\nolimits(X,X^{\prime})
of all maps between
X
and
X^{\prime}
with a differential given by

\partial(\alpha)=d_{X^{\prime}}\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha d_{X}.

We put
\mathrm{HMF}(W)=\mathrm{HMF}(S,W)
(respectively
\mathrm{HMF}(R,W)
) for the idempotent completion of the corresponding homotopy category.

One easily adds additional structures, e.g. grading or equivariance, into the definition.

Let

D_{sing}(\{W=0\}):=D(\{W=0\})/\textup{Perf}(\{W=0\})

be the singularity category of
W
. The following is the key result on the matrix factorizations:

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
19
, Theorem 3.5]
) There is an equivalence of triangulated categories:

(2.1)

D_{sing}(\{W=0\})\cong\mathrm{HMF}(W).

Let
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
be three varieties together with three functions
W_{1},W_{2},W_{3}
on them. Then the tensor product over
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{2}}
induces a functor

(2.2)

-\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{2}}}-\colon\mathrm{HMF}(S_{3}\times S_{2},W_{3}-W_{2})\times\mathrm{HMF}(S_{2}\times S_{1},W_{2}-W_{1})\to

\to\mathrm{HMF}(S_{3}\times S_{1},W_{3}-W_{1}).

Note that there is a technical difficulty that could be overcome: naively, the tensor product will be a matrix factorization of infinite rank. This issue could be resolved using, for example
[
12
, Section 12]
.

Note also that we can plug
S_{i}=pt,W_{i}=0
for some
i
into (
2.2
). For
S_{1}=pt,S_{2}=S_{3}=S
and
W_{1}=0,W_{2}=W_{3}=W
put

(2.3)

\widetilde{W}:=W_{3}-W_{2}=W\otimes 1-1\otimes W\in\Gamma({\mathcal{O}}_{S})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma({\mathcal{O}}_{S})=\Gamma({\mathcal{O}}_{S\times S}).

Then there is the unique up to homotopy equivalence object
\Delta_{W}
of
\mathrm{HMF}(S\times S,\widetilde{W})
under (

) representing the identity functor on
\mathrm{HMF}(S,W)
and playing the role of the monoidal unit.

The tensor product and objects

2.4.
Orbifold equivalence

We refer to
[
9
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
and
[

]
for the detailed discussion of this topic.

Let
R_{1},R_{2}
be either two polynomial rings or two rings of formal power series, such that the number of variables has the same parity. In the latter case one should work with the completed tensor products. The localization result
[
11
, Theorems 4.11, 5.7]
allows one to switch between these settings and transfer the results.

Pick
W_{1}\in R_{1},W_{2}\in R_{2}
and let
X=(X,d_{X})
be in
\mathrm{HMF}(R_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}R_{1},W_{2}-W_{1})
. We define
X^{\vee}=(X^{\vee},d_{X^{\vee}})
to be the dual matrix factorization given by
X^{\vee}=\mathop{\sf Hom}\nolimits_{R_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}R_{1}}(X,R_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}R_{1})
and
d_{X^{\vee}}\alpha=(-1)^{1+|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{X}
. This is an object of
\mathrm{HMF}(R_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}R_{2},W_{1}-W_{2})
. Shifting it by the parity of the number of variables we obtain an object
X^{\dagger}
of
\mathrm{HMF}(R_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}R_{2},W_{1}-W_{2})
. The functors
X\otimes_{R_{1}}-
and
X^{\dagger}\otimes_{R_{2}}-
are two sided adjoint (
[
7
, Section 7]
). Note that it is here that we use the fact that the numbers of variables in the two considered rings have the same parity, as otherwise the shift by
1
will appear in the adjunction. The evaluation and coevaluation maps are induced by
4
morphisms in
\mathrm{HMF}(\widetilde{W_{1}})
and
\mathrm{HMF}(\widetilde{W_{2}})
:

\Delta_{W_{1}}\to X^{\dagger}\otimes_{R_{2}}X\to\Delta_{W_{1}},

\Delta_{W_{2}}\to X\otimes_{R_{1}}X^{\dagger}\to\Delta_{W_{2}}.

Taking the compositions one obtains elements

\dim_{l}(X)\in\mathop{\sf End}\nolimits(\Delta_{W_{1}})=\mathrm{Jac}({W_{1}})

and

\dim_{r}(X)\in\mathop{\sf End}\nolimits(\Delta_{W_{2}})=\mathrm{Jac}({W_{2}}),

where
\mathrm{Jac}({W})
is the Jacobi ring of
W
. The elements
\dim_{l}(X)
and
\dim_{r}(X)
are respectively called
left
and
right quantum dimensions
.

By
[
7
, Proposition 8.5]
the quantum dimensions enjoy the following properties:

(2.4)

\dim_{l}(\Delta_{W})=\dim_{r}(\Delta_{W})=1;

(2.5)

\dim_{l}(X)=\dim_{r}(X^{\dagger});

(2.6)

\dim_{l}(X_{1}\otimes X_{2})=\dim_{l}(X_{2})\dim_{l}(X_{1}),\enskip\dim_{r}(X_{1}\otimes X_{2})=\dim_{r}(X_{1})\dim_{r}(X_{2}).

The quantum dimensions can be computed by the direct formulas in terms of
d_{X}
given by
[
7
, Proposition 8.2]
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that
W_{1}\in R_{1}
and
W_{2}\in R_{2}
are orbifold equivalent and write
W_{1}\sim W_{2}
if there is an object
X
of
\mathrm{HMF}(R_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}R_{1},W_{2}-W_{1})
with invertible left and right quantum dimensions.

Given a grading on rings
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
, such that
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are of pure degree, in Definition
2.5
one may additionally ask for
X
to be graded, which provides the graded version of the orbifold equivalence. In the case of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\geq 0}
-grading, such that the
0
degree component of
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are equal to
{\mathbb{C}}\cdot 1
, the quantum dimension is known to be an element of
{\mathbb{C}}
(
[
8
, Lemma 2.5]
). This localization procedure can always be done.

The orbifold equivalence provides an important relation between the categories
\mathrm{HMF}(W_{1})
and
\mathrm{HMF}(W_{2})
. The endofunctor

X^{\dagger}\otimes_{R_{2}}X\otimes_{R_{1}}-\colon\mathrm{HMF}(W_{1})\to\mathrm{HMF}(W_{1})

by adjunction admits a structure of a monad, i.e. it has a multiplication structure given by the composition

X^{\dagger}\otimes_{R_{2}}X\otimes_{R_{1}}X^{\dagger}\otimes_{R_{2}}X\to X^{\dagger}\otimes_{R_{2}}\Delta_{W_{2}}\otimes_{R_{2}}X\cong X^{\dagger}\otimes_{R_{2}}X.

It makes sense to talk about objects in
\mathrm{HMF}(W_{1})
with an action of
X^{\dagger}\otimes_{R_{2}}X
. We denote by
X^{\dagger}\otimes_{R_{2}}X-\bmod_{\mathrm{HMF}(W_{1})}
the category of such module objects. We have

Theorem 2.6
.

(
[
8
, Corollary 1.6]
, see also
[
10
]
)
There is an equivalence of triangulated categories

\mathrm{HMF}(W_{2})\cong X^{\dagger}\otimes_{R_{2}}X-\bmod_{\mathrm{HMF}(W_{1})}.

2.6.1.
Orbifold equivalence for functions on varieties

We need to also make sense of the orbifold equivalence in a more general setting of a function
W
on an algebraic variety
S
. Assume that
W
has an isolated singularity at a unique smooth point
s\in S
and is smooth everywhere else. Assume that there is an affine chart isomorphic to an affine space
U\subset S
and containing
s
. We can then restrict
W
to
U
to get an element
W|_{U}
of the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}[U]
. We will discuss the existence of such affine charts in the relevant situations later in Section
5.1
. Alternatively, we can consider the completion of the local ring
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{S,s}
of
S
at
s
. It is a formal power series ring and the germ
W_{s}
of
W
at
s
provides its element. This localization procedure can always be done. The localization equivalences of the matrix factorization categories
[
18
, Proposition 1.14]
and
[
11
, Theorem 4.11, 5.7]
provide canonical equivalences

\mathrm{HMF}(S,W)\cong\mathrm{HMF}({\mathbb{C}}[U],W|_{U})\cong\mathrm{HMF}(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{S,s},W_{s}).

If
(S_{1},W_{1})
and
(S_{2},W_{2})
are two such pairs we similarly have

\mathrm{HMF}(S_{1}\times S_{2},W_{1}+W_{2})\cong

\cong\mathrm{HMF}({\mathbb{C}}[U_{1}]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{C}}[U_{2}],W_{1}|_{U_{1}}+W_{2}|_{U_{2}})\cong\mathrm{HMF}(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{S,s_{1}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{C}}\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{S,s_{2}},W_{s_{1}}+W_{s_{2}}),

where
s_{i}
are the unique singularity points and
U_{i}
, if they exist, are two suitable affine charts. The equivalences are compatible with the tensor products. This implies that we can canonically fit
\mathrm{HMF}(S,W)
for
W
with isolated singularity at the unique point into the bicategory of polynomial potentials (in case of the existence of the affine chart) or power series potentials (in all cases) and their matrix factorizations. In particular, we can talk about orbifold equivalence between such functions. Canonicity of the equivalences implies that the choices made at this point will not impact our results.

Remark 4.2

As

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

By definition, the tensor product functors
X_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}-
and
X_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{V}}-
are the Fourier-Mukai transforms with the kernels
X_{\mathcal{E}}
and
X_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}}
respectively. It follows that under the equivalence (
2.1
) these functors respectively match the functors induced by
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\Phi_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}}
.

Now Lemma
3.3
implies that under the equivalence (
2.1
) applied to
W=f
the functor
X_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{V}}X_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}-
on the right hand side matches the functor induced by
p_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}-
on the left. Since
p
is flat and finite, the sheaf
p_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
is locally free. Moreover, its fiber over a point is identified with
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
. As we work locally near

p_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
is isomorphic to the trivial sheaf with the fiber

. We see that the functor induced by
p_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}-
on the singularity category corresponds to
{\mathbb{C}}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Delta_{\hat{f}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}-
on the matrix factorization category. The statement follows.
∎

{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}

\mathrm{HMF}(f)\cong X_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{V}}X_{\mathcal{E}}-\bmod_{\mathrm{HMF}(\hat{f})}\cong{\mathbb{C}}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Delta_{\hat{f}}-\bmod_{\mathrm{HMF}(\hat{f})}.

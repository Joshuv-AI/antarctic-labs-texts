Hybrid Beamforming for Intelligent Reflecting Surface Aided Millimeter Wave MIMO Systems
Sung Hyuck Hong, Jaeyong Park, Sung-Jin Kim, and Junil Choi

Abstract
As communication systems that employ millimeter wave (mmWave) frequency bands must use large antenna arrays to overcome the severe propagation loss of mmWave signals, hybrid beamforming has been considered as an integral component of mmWave communications. Recently, intelligent reflecting surface (IRS) has been proposed as an innovative technology that can significantly improve the performance of mmWave communication systems through the use of low-cost passive reflecting elements. In this paper, we study IRS-aided mmWave multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems with hybrid beamforming architectures. We first exploit the sparse-scattering structure and large dimension of mmWave channels to develop the joint design of IRS reflection matrix and hybrid beamformer for narrowband MIMO systems. Then, we generalize the proposed joint design to broadband MIMO systems with orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) modulation by leveraging the angular sparsity of frequency-selective mmWave channels. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed joint designs can significantly enhance the spectral efficiency of the systems of interest and achieve superior performance over the existing designs.

Introduction

The explosive growth in wireless data traffic and resultant issue of bandwidth shortage have increased the necessity of resorting to higher frequency bands that are relatively uncongested

Since large bandwidth can be utilized at millimeter wave (mmWave) frequency bands to attain possibly up to gigabit-per-second data rates, mmWave communications have been viewed as one of the key technologies that can realize many new applications and services that the fifth generation (5G) wireless networks aim to support

To address such issue, hybrid beamforming architectures that use the combination of a low-dimensional digital baseband beamformer and a high-dimensional analog beamformer have been proposed to increase the energy efficiency of massive MIMO systems, significantly reducing the number of RF chains at the cost of only slight performance degradation

[

[
27
]
focused on minimizing bit error rate (BER) and thus entails an inevitable loss in spectral efficiency. Furthermore, the designs in [26] and [27] construct the hybrid beamformer without considering the effect of the IRS reflection matrix on mmWave channels, although doing so can lead to significant performance improvement and complexity reduction.

Motivated by these facts, we propose in this paper the joint designs of IRS reflection matrix and hybrid beamformer for narrowband and broadband mmWave MIMO systems. The proposed designs aim to maximize the spectral efficiency of IRS-aided mmWave MIMO systems in which the reflected channel from the transmitter (TX) to IRS to receiver (RX) and the direct channel from the TX to RX coexist. To the best of our knowledge, no prior work on IRS-aided mmWave MIMO systems with hybrid beamforming architectures has considered the presence of the direct channel. Furthermore, by exploiting how the IRS reflection matrix influences the structure of mmWave channels when constructing the hybrid beamformer, the proposed joint designs achieve significant performance gains over the existing benchmarks. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We develop an IRS reflection matrix design that successfully establishes the favorable communication environments for narrowband mmWave MIMO systems. Then, to demonstrate the effectiveness of the design, we provide an asymptotic analysis of the effect of the proposed IRS reflection matrix on the structure of mmWave channels.

•

We propose a hybrid beamformer design that takes into account the inherent structure of mmWave channels so as to attain the performance close to that of fully-digital beamforming. By carefully examining how the channels are adjusted according to the proposed IRS reflection matrix design, we construct hybrid precoders and combiners that fully reap considerable spectral efficiency gains offered by IRS.

•

We generalize the proposed joint design of IRS reflection matrix and hybrid beamformer for narrowband MIMO systems to broadband MIMO systems with orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) modulation. Smartly leveraging the sparsity of frequency-selective mmWave channels in the angular domain, the generalized design can significantly enhance the spectral and energy efficiency of IRS-aided MIMO-OFDM systems.

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N_{\text{s}}}\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{P_{l}}{\sigma^{2}_{n}}|[{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\text{eff}}]_{l,l}|^{2}\right)=R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}).

(9)

We therefore aim to maximize
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}})
in (
9
) by properly designing
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}},{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
, and
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
according to the given channels
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}},{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}},
and
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}
. That is, we focus on solving the effective channel design problem, which is formulated as

\displaystyle\text{(P3)}\hskip 57.0pt\max_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}},{\boldsymbol{\Phi}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}}\medspace R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}})

(10a)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\text{subject to }\hskip 21.0pt{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}=\text{diag}([e^{j\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{j\theta_{M}}]),

(10b)

\displaystyle\hskip 82.0pt|[{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}]_{m,n}|=1/\sqrt{N_{\text{r}}},\medspace\forall m,n,

(10c)

\displaystyle\hskip 82.0pt|[{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}]_{m,n}|=1/\sqrt{N_{\text{t}}},\medspace\forall m,n,

(10d)

\displaystyle\hskip 82.0pt{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}.

(10e)

Note that, given the transmit power constraint
P_{\text{TX}}
and noise power
\sigma^{2}_{n}
, the value of the objective function
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}})
of the problem (P3) is solely determined by
\left\{|[{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\text{eff}}]_{l,l}|^{2}\right\}_{l=1}^{N_{\text{s}}}
, which are necessarily the
N_{\text{s}}
largest eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\mathrm{H}}
.

III-B

IRS Reflection Matrix Design

Although the effective channel design problem (P3) developed in Section
III-A
involves less variables than the original problem (P1) of interest, it is still challenging to find the optimal solution of (P3) since the IRS reflection matrix
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
directly influences the total combined channel
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}
, which in turn determines the appropriate analog combiner
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}
and precoder
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
. In order to address this difficulty, we propose in this subsection the IRS reflection matrix design that smartly leverages the structure of mmWave MIMO channels to significantly increase the spectral efficiency that
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}
can support. After adjusting
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
according to the proposed IRS design, we will construct
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}
and
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
that successfully translate the spectral efficiency gain in
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}={\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}+{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}
to that in
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}={\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
, details of which are explained in Section
III-C
.

Let the SVD of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}
be expressed as
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}={\mathbf{U}}_{\text{tot}}{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{tot}}
. Then,
similar to
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}})
in (
9
), the maximum achievable spectral efficiency
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}})
under
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}
depends only on
\left\{|[{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\text{tot}}]_{l,l}|^{2}\right\}_{l=1}^{N_{\text{s}}}
, or equivalently the
N_{s}
largest eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{tot}}
, for given
P_{\text{TX}}
and
\sigma^{2}_{n}
. To mathematically characterize the eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{tot}}
, we first define the receive array response matrix
{\mathbf{A}}^{i}_{\text{r}}
, complex gain matrix
{\mathbf{G}}_{i}
, and transmit array response matrix
{\mathbf{A}}^{i}_{\text{t}}
for the mmWave channel
{\mathbf{H}}_{i}
in (
3
) as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}^{i}_{\text{r}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{i,0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{i,0})&\cdots&{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{i,N_{\text{path}}^{i}-1},\theta^{\text{r}}_{i,N_{\text{path}}^{i}-1})\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\hskip 33.0pt{\mathbf{G}}_{i}=\text{diag}\big([\alpha_{i,0},\dots,\alpha_{i,N_{\text{path}}^{i}-1}]\big),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}^{i}_{\text{t}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{i,0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{i,0})&\cdots&{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{i,N_{\text{path}}^{i}-1},\theta^{\text{t}}_{i,N_{\text{path}}^{i}-1})\end{bmatrix}.

(11)

The channel matrix
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{i}}
can then be decomposed as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{i}={\mathbf{A}}^{i}_{\text{r}}{\mathbf{G}}_{i}({\mathbf{A}}^{i}_{\text{t}})^{\mathrm{H}},

(12)

where we assume, without loss of generality, that
|[{\mathbf{G}}_{i}]_{m,m}|\geq|[{\mathbf{G}}_{i}]_{n,n}|,\forall m,n\in\{1,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{i}\}
such that
m<n
. We now pay particular attention to
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}^{\mathrm{H}}
, which can be written as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}^{\mathrm{H}}={\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}^{\text{TR}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{TR}}({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{TR}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{TI}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{TI}}^{\mathrm{H}}({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}^{\text{TI}})^{\mathrm{H}}.

(13)

Since the AoDs of different propagation paths can be considered as continuous random variables that are independent from one another, it follows that the event
E=\big\{\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{TR},s}\neq\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{TI},j},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{TR},s}\neq\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{TI},j},\forall s\in\{0,\ldots,N^{\text{TR}}_{\text{path}}-1\},\forall j\in\{0,\ldots,N^{\text{TI}}_{\text{path}}-1\}\big\}
occurs with probability one
[
33
,
26
]
. Then, by the asymptotic orthogonality of UPA array response vectors
[
34
]
, we have

\displaystyle({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{TR}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{TI}}\rightarrow{\boldsymbol{0}}^{N^{\text{TR}}_{\text{path}}\times N^{\text{TI}}_{\text{path}}},\medspace\text{as }N_{\text{t}}\rightarrow\infty,

(14)

which implies that each element of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}^{\mathrm{H}}
converges to
0
in the limit of large
N_{\text{t}}
. Using similar arguments, we can show that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{tot}}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}^{\mathrm{H}}+{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}+{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}^{\mathrm{H}}+{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\sum_{s=0}^{N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}-1}|\alpha_{\text{TR},s}|^{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s})^{\mathrm{H}}+\sum_{j=0}^{N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}-1}|\alpha_{\text{TI},j}|^{2}{\mathbf{q}}_{j}{\mathbf{q}}_{j}^{\mathrm{H}},

(15)

as
N_{\text{t}}\rightarrow\infty
, where
{\mathbf{q}}_{j}={\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j}),\medspace j\in\{0,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}-1\}
.

We now present the following lemma to establish the connection between the magnitude of
{\mathbf{q}}_{j}
and IRS reflection matrix
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
.

Lemma 1:

For any
j\in\{0,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}-1\}
,
\lVert{\mathbf{q}}_{j}\rVert^{2}_{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{q}}_{j}\rVert^{2}_{2}\leq\lambda_{0}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}),

(16)

where the equality holds if
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j})
is the eigenvector corresponding to the maximum eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}})
of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}
, i.e.,
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j})=\lambda_{0}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}){\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j}).

Proof:
See Appendix
A
.

\square

With the aid of Lemma 1, we now show in the following proposition that the IRS reflection matrix
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
that asymptotically maximizes
\lVert{\mathbf{q}}_{j}\rVert^{2}_{2}
can be obtained in closed-form.

Proposition 1:

Let
j\in\{0,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}-1\}
and define the vector
{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times 1}
that satisfies
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}=\text{diag}({\mathbf{v}})
. Then, when
{\mathbf{v}}=M\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j})^{\mathrm{H}}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{q}}_{j}\rVert^{2}_{2}\rightarrow\lambda_{0}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}),\medspace\text{as }N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty.

(17)

Proof:
Set
j\in\{0,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}-1\}
and
{\mathbf{v}}=M\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j})^{\mathrm{H}}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0})
. We can then write

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j})

\displaystyle=\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j})){\mathbf{v}}={\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0}).

(18)

By a similar argument used to derive (
14
), we have for each
m\in\{0,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{IR}}-1\}
that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m})

\displaystyle={\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{IR}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}^{\text{IR}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}^{\text{IR}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{IR}}({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{IR}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m})

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{IR}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{IR}}[{\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{path}}^{\text{IR}}}]_{:,m+1}=|\alpha_{\text{IR},m}|^{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m}),

(19)

as
N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
. Since Lemma 1 states that
\lVert{\mathbf{q}}_{j}\rVert^{2}_{2}=\lambda_{0}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}})
when
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j})
is the eigenvector of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}
associated with
\lambda_{0}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}})
, we can prove Proposition 1 by showing that
|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2}
asymptotically becomes the maximum eigenvalue of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}
in the limit of large
N_{\text{r}}
and
M
. We first note that, by the inequality
\text{rank}({\mathbf{H}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}})\leq\text{min}(M,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{IR}})
,
{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}
can have at most
N_{\text{path}}^{\text{IR}}
nonzero eigenvalues, counting multiplicities, for sufficiently large
M
such that
M\geq N_{\text{path}}^{\text{IR}}
.
In fact, (
19
) implies that the number of nonzero eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}
approaches
N_{\text{path}}^{\text{IR}}
as
N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
. Since
|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2}\geq|\alpha_{\text{IR},m}|^{2}
for each
m
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}})\rightarrow|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2},\text{ as }N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty.

(20)

This completes the proof of Proposition 1.

\square

To examine how setting
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
as described in Proposition 1 modifies the value of
\lVert{\mathbf{q}}_{p}\rVert^{2}_{2},p\in\{0,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}-1\}\setminus\{j\},
we first express
{\mathbf{q}}_{p}
as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{q}}_{p}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p})={\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}^{\text{IR}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{IR}}({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{IR}})^{\mathrm{H}}\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p})){\mathbf{v}}.

(21)

By direct computation, we can express the
(m+1)
-th element of
({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{IR}})^{\mathrm{H}}\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p})){\mathbf{v}}
as

\displaystyle[({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{IR}})^{\mathrm{H}}\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p})){\mathbf{v}}]_{m+1}={\mathbf{a}}(f_{m,p},g_{m,p})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{a}}(f_{0,j},g_{0,j}),

(22)

where
m\in\{0,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{IR}}-1\},f_{m,p}=\sin(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m})\sin(\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m})-\sin(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p})\sin(\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p})
, and
g_{m,p}=\cos(\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m})-\cos(\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p})
. The vector
{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times 1}
is expressed as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\Big[1,\dots,e^{j\frac{2\pi d}{\lambda}(m_{h}f+m_{v}g)},\dots,e^{j\frac{2\pi d}{\lambda}((M^{h}-1)f+(M^{v}-1)g)}\Big]^{\mathrm{T}}.

(23)

Here,
0\leq m_{h}<M^{h}
and
0\leq m_{v}<M^{v}
each denote the horizontal and vertical indices for the IRS elements, where
M=M^{h}M^{v}
.
Since the vector
{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)
has the same structure as an
M\times 1
UPA array response vector, it follows that each element of
({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{IR}})^{\mathrm{H}}\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},p})){\mathbf{v}}
tends to zero as
M\rightarrow~\infty
. We can thus conclude from (
21
) that

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{q}}_{p}\rVert^{2}_{2}\rightarrow 0,\text{ as }M\rightarrow\infty,

(24)

for any
p\in\{0,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}-1\}\setminus\{j\}
.

We now explain in the following proposition how the eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\mathrm{H}}
asymptotically behave when
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
is set as described in Proposition 1.

Proposition 2:

Let
j^{\star}\in\{0,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}-1\}
and
s\in\{0,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}-1\}
. When
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}=\text{diag}({\mathbf{v}})
,
{\mathbf{v}}=M\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j^{\star}},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},{j^{\star}}})^{\mathrm{H}}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0})
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0})\rightarrow|\alpha_{\text{TI},j^{\star}}|^{2}|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0}),

(25)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s})\rightarrow|\alpha_{\text{TR},s}|^{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s}),

(26)

as
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof:
Set
{\mathbf{v}}=M\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j^{\star}},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},{j^{\star}}})^{\mathrm{H}}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0})
,
j^{\star}\in\{0,\ldots,N^{\text{TI}}_{\text{path}}-1\}
. According to (
12
), (
18
), and the asymptotic orthogonality of UPA array response vectors, we can express
{\mathbf{q}}_{j^{\star}}
as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{q}}_{j^{\star}}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}^{\text{IR}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{IR}}({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{IR}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0})\rightarrow\alpha_{\text{IR},0}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0}),\text{ as }M\rightarrow\infty.

(27)

Combining (
24
) and (
27
) with (
15
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{tot}}\rightarrow\sum_{s=0}^{N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}-1}|\alpha_{\text{TR},s}|^{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s})^{\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle\hskip 120.0pt+|\alpha_{\text{TI},j^{\star}}|^{2}|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0})^{\mathrm{H}},

(28)

as
N_{\text{t}},M\rightarrow\infty
. Also, it holds for each
s\in\{0,\ldots,N^{\text{TR}}_{\text{path}}-1\}
that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0})\rightarrow 0,\text{ as }N_{\text{r}}\rightarrow\infty,

(29)

since the event

occurs with probability one. It then follows from (

) and (

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0})\rightarrow|\alpha_{\text{TI},j^{\star}}|^{2}|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0}),\text{ as }N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty,

(30)

which finishes the proof of (
25
). The proof of (

\square

According to Proposition 2,
|\alpha_{\text{TI},j^{\star}}|^{2}|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2}
and
\big\{|\alpha_{\text{TR},s}|^{2}\big\}_{s=0}^{N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}-1}

when
{\mathbf{v}}=M\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},j^{\star}},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},{j^{\star}}})^{\mathrm{H}}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0})

, we set the IRS reflection matrix as

, where

\displaystyle{\mathbf{v}}^{\star}=M\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TI},{0}})^{\mathrm{H}}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0}),

in the limit of large

and

.

To investigate how the structure of mmWave MIMO channels is adjusted by the proposed IRS reflection matrix design, we first express the reflected channel
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}
as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}^{\text{IR}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{IR}}({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{IR}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}^{\text{TI}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{TI}}({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{TI}})^{\mathrm{H}}.

(32)

Using the expression in (
23
), we can write the element in the
(m+1)
-th row and
(j+1)
-th column of
{\mathbf{C}}=({\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{\text{IR}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}^{\text{TI}}
as

\displaystyle[{\mathbf{C}}]_{m+1,j+1}=\begin{cases}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},m})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{IR},0})\hskip 8.0pt\text{ if }j=0,\\
{\mathbf{a}}(f_{m,j},g_{m,j})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{a}}(f_{0,0},g_{0,0})\hskip 31.0pt\text{if }j\neq 0,\end{cases}

(33)

for each

and

. It is evident from (

) that the asymptotic behavior of

can be characterized as

\displaystyle[{\mathbf{C}}]_{m+1,j+1}\rightarrow\delta_{m0}\delta_{j0},\text{ as }M\rightarrow\infty,

(34)

where

. As a result, the adjusted total channel
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}={\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}+{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}
satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}\rightarrow\sum_{s=0}^{N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}-1}\alpha_{\text{TR},s}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{TR},s})^{\mathrm{H}}+\alpha_{\text{TI},0}\alpha_{\text{IR},0}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{TI},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{TI},0})^{\mathrm{H}},

(35)

as
M\rightarrow\infty
. It can thus be concluded that, by asymptotically maximizing the eigenvalue associated with the dominant transmit and receive path pair at the IRS, the proposed IRS design successfully establishes a strong communication link between the TX and RX. Also, since
\alpha_{\text{TI},0}
and
\alpha_{\text{IR},0}
are independent, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\alpha_{\text{TI},0}|^{2}|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2}\right]\geq M^{2}c,

(36)

where
c=N_{\text{t}}N_{\text{r}}10^{-0.1(PL(d_{\text{TI}})+PL(d_{\text{IR}}))}/N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}N_{\text{path}}^{\text{IR}}
. The inequality in (
36
) shows that, for a fixed value of
c
, the average magnitude of the asymptotic eigenvalue
|\alpha_{\text{TI},0}|^{2}|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2}
of
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
scales at least quadratically with
M
. This suggests that the proposed design will offer significant increases in spectral efficiency for IRS-aided mmWave MIMO systems, where the IRS passive elements are expected to be deployed in large numbers thanks to their low costs and hardware simplicity
[
17
,
19
]
.

III-C

Analog Beamformer Design

In this subsection, we propose the analog beamformer design that leverages the structure of the favorably adjusted total channel
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}={\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}+{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}
. Specifically, we construct the analog precoder
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
and combiner
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}
so that the effective channel
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
can asymptotically support the maximum spectral efficiency
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})
achievable with fully-digital beamforming.

With the IRS reflection matrix designed according to Section
III-B
, i.e.,
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}={\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}
, the effective channel design problem (P3) reduces to

\displaystyle\text{(P4)}\hskip 45.0pt\max_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}}\medspace R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}})

(37a)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\text{subject to }\hskip 21.0pt|[{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}]_{m,n}|=1/\sqrt{N_{\text{r}}},\medspace\forall m,n,

(37b)

\displaystyle\hskip 82.0pt|[{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}]_{m,n}|=1/\sqrt{N_{\text{t}}},\medspace\forall m,n,

(37c)

\displaystyle\hskip 82.0pt{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}.

(37d)

Removing the constant modulus constraints (
37b
), (
37c
) of the problem (P4), we can formulate the relaxed problem
(\text{P4}^{\prime})
as

\displaystyle(\text{P4}^{\prime})\hskip 45.0pt\max_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}}\medspace R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}})

(38a)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\text{subject to }\hskip 21.0pt{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}.

(38b)

With the SVD of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}
expressed as
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}={\mathbf{U}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{V}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
, it is possible to explicitly obtain the optimal solution of
(\text{P4}^{\prime})
, as described in the following proposition.

Proposition 3:

Let
\{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}\}
be a feasible solution to the problem
(\text{P4}^{\prime})
. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}})\leq R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}),

(39)

where the equality holds if
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}=[{\mathbf{U}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}]_{:,1:N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}}
and
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}=[{\mathbf{V}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}]_{:,1:N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}
. That is,
\{\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}=[{\mathbf{U}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}]_{:,1:N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}},\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}=[{\mathbf{V}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}]_{:,1:N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}\}
is optimal for the problem
(\text{P4}^{\prime})
.

Proof:
Suppose there exists a feasible solution
\{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}\}
of
(\text{P4}^{\prime})
such that

R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}})>R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star})
. Also, let
\{\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}^{\prime}_{\text{BB}},\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}^{\prime}_{\text{BB}}\}
denote the optimal solution of
(\text{P2})
, with
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}
set as
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}={\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
. Then, the spectral efficiency equal to
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}})
can be attained under
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}
by using the hybrid combiner
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}^{\prime}_{\text{BB}}
and precoder
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}^{\prime}_{\text{BB}}
. This contradicts the definition of
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star})
and the proof of the inequality in (
39
) is complete.

We now verify the optimality of
\{\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}\}
for the problem
(\text{P4}^{\prime})
by showing that
R_{\text{max}}(\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star})=R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star})
, where
\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}=(\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}
. Direct computation reveals that
\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}(\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star})^{\mathrm{H}}
is the diagonal matrix that satisfies

\displaystyle[\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}(\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star})^{\mathrm{H}}]_{n,n}=\begin{cases}|[{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}]_{n,n}|^{2}\hskip 15.0pt&\text{if }1\leq n\leq N_{\text{min}}^{\text{RF}},\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(40)

where
N_{\text{min}}^{\text{RF}}=\text{min}(N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}},N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}})
. Since
N_{\text{s}}\leq N_{\text{min}}^{\text{RF}}
, the
N_{\text{s}}
largest eigenvalues of
\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}(\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star})^{\mathrm{H}}
coincide with those of
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
, which finishes the proof of Proposition 3.

\square

To develop the analog beamformer design that provides an asymptotically optimal solution of (P4), we will now examine the asymptotic property of
\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}
and
\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}
. Let us first focus on
\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}=[{\mathbf{U}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}]_{:,1:N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}}
, whose column vectors are necessarily the eigenvectors of
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
. The result in (
28
) indicates that the number of nonzero eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
, counting multiplicities, approaches
N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}+1
as
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
. Since Proposition 2 states that
\{{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR}})\}_{s=0}^{N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}-1}
and
{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0})
asymptotically become the
N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}+1
eigenvectors associated with the nonzero eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
, we can conclude that, as
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
, each of the eigenvectors that correspond to the nonzero eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
and are in the columns of
\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}
can be expressed as a scalar multiple of one of the column vectors of
\hat{{\mathbf{U}}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{A}}^{\text{TR}}_{\text{r}}&{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0})\\
\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{r}}\times(N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}+1)}
. By similar argument, we can also show that each eigenvector that is associated with a nonzero eigenvalue of
({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}
and is in one of the columns of
\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}=[{\mathbf{V}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}]_{:,1:N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}
approaches a scalar multiple of one of the column vectors of
\hat{{\mathbf{V}}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{A}}^{\text{TR}}_{\text{t}}&{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{\text{TI},0},\theta^{\text{t}}_{\text{TI},0})\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{t}}\times(N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}+1)}
in the limit of large
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}}
, and
M
.

Motivated by the above formulation, we now construct the analog precoder
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}
and combiner
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}
that are highly effective in IRS-aided mmWave MIMO systems with a large number of antennas and IRS elements. First, we construct the set
\{[{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}]_{:,c_{u}}\}_{u=1}^{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}
that consists of the
N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}
column vectors of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}
with the
N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}
largest
\ell_{2}
-norm, where
c_{u}\in\{1,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}+N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}\}
. We then set the analog precoder
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}
as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}=\left[[{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}]_{:,c_{1}},\dots,[{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}]_{:,c_{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}}\right].

(41)

Similarly, we construct the analog combiner
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}
as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}=\left[[{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}]_{:,p_{1}},\dots,[{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}]_{:,p_{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}}}\right],

(42)

where
\{[({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}]_{:,p_{v}}\}_{v=1}^{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}}
is the set of
N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}
column vectors of
({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}
with the
N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}
largest
\ell_{2}
-norm, and
p_{v}\in\{1,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}+N_{\text{path}}^{\text{IR}}\}
.
According to Proposition 3 and the asymptotic property of
\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}
and
\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}
discussed in the preceding paragraph, we see that the effective channel
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}=({\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}
satisfies

\displaystyle R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star})\rightarrow R_{\text{max}}(\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star})=R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}),

(43)

as
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, even with limited number of RF chains, the proposed analog beamformer design successfully constructs the effective channel
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}
whose maximum supportable spectral efficiency
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star})
is very close to
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star})
in the systems of interest, thereby harvesting the substantial spectral efficiency gain provided by the IRS reflection matrix design in Section
III-B
.

III-D

Baseband Beamformer Design

In the previous two subsections, we described how to design the IRS reflection matrix and analog beamformer for the channel matrices
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}},{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}
and
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}
. In this subsection, we propose the design of the baseband combiner and precoder that are nearly optimal for the effective channel
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}=({\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}
. As explained in Section
III-A
, when
({\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N^{\text{RF}}_{\text{r}}}
and
({\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N^{\text{RF}}_{\text{t}}}
hold, the baseband combiner
\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
and precoder
\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
that maximize the spectral efficiency in (
6
) for given
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}={\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}
are

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}=[{\mathbf{U}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}]_{:,1:N_{\text{s}}},\hskip 5.0pt\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}=[{\mathbf{V}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}]_{:,1:N_{\text{s}}}({\mathbf{P}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}})^{1/2},

(44)

where the SVD of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}
is expressed as
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}={\mathbf{U}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}({\mathbf{V}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star})^{\mathrm{H}}
.
The power allocation matrix
({\mathbf{P}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}})^{1/2}
is given by
({\mathbf{P}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}})^{1/2}=\text{diag}\left(\big[\sqrt{P^{\star}_{1}},...,\sqrt{P^{\star}_{N_{\text{s}}}}\big]\right)
, where
P^{\star}_{l}=\left(\frac{1}{\eta^{\star}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}_{n}}{|[{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}]_{l,l}|^{2}}\right)^{+},\forall l\in\{1,\dots,N_{\text{s}}\}
, and
\eta^{\star}
is chosen such that
\sum_{l=1}^{N_{\text{s}}}P^{\star}_{l}=P_{\text{TX}}
. As both
{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}
and
{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}
have UPA array response vectors in their respective columns,
({\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}
and
({\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}
each approach
{\mathbf{I}}_{N^{\text{RF}}_{\text{r}}}
and
{\mathbf{I}}_{N^{\text{RF}}_{\text{t}}}
as
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}}\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, using
\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
and
\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
as the baseband combiner and precoder is nearly optimal in mmWave systems, where large antenna arrays are employed at the TX and RX. Note that, while
{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
can be directly used as the hybrid combiner, there is no guarantee that the hybrid precoder
{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
satisfies the transmit power constraint (
5c
) of the problem (P1), i.e.,
\lVert{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}
might be greater than
P_{\text{TX}}
. With this difference in mind, we adopt
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{BB}}^{\star}=\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
and
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}^{\star}=\gamma^{\star}\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
as the baseband combiner and precoder, where

\displaystyle\gamma^{\star}=\frac{\sqrt{P_{\text{TX}}}}{\|{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}}

(45)

is the normalization factor that ensures the transmit power constraint is met with equality, i.e.,
\|{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{F}}=P_{\text{TX}}
. Since the asymptotic orthogonality of UPA array response vectors guarantees that
{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
converges to
\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
as
N_{\text{t}}\rightarrow\infty
, we can conclude from (
43
) that the proposed hybrid precoder
{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
and combiner
{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}
achieve spectral efficiency that asymptotically approaches
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star})
, or equivalently
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star})
, as
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, in typical IRS-aided mmWave MIMO systems with a large number of antennas and IRS elements, the proposed hybrid beamformer design can perform close to the optimal fully-digital beamformer design, providing the systems with considerable benefits in terms of cost and energy efficiency.

IV
Extension of Joint Design to MIMO-OFDM Systems

As one of the key features of mmWave communications is the usage of large bandwidth, it is important to investigate the design of IRS reflection matrix and hybrid beamformer for frequency-selective mmWave channels. In this section, we extend the proposed joint design of IRS reflection matrix and hybrid beamformer in Section
III
to broadband MIMO-OFDM systems.

IV-A

System Model and Problem Formulation

Consider an IRS-aided MIMO-OFDM system where the TX performs digital precoding to the symbol vector
{\mathbf{s}}[k]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{s}}\times 1}
in the frequency domain by using the baseband precoder
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}[k]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}\times N_{\text{s}}}
at each subcarrier
k\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}
. The precoded symbol vector is then transformed into the time domain through the
K
-point inverse fast Fourier transform (FFT) followed by cyclic prefix (CP) addition at each of the
N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}
RF chains. Subsequently, the TX applies the analog precoder
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
to the transformed vector to produce the final transmitted signal. We assume that
{\mathbf{s}}[k]
satisfies
\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{s}}[k]{\mathbf{s}}[k]^{\mathrm{H}}]={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{s}}}
, and that the TX is subject to the per-subcarrier power constraint, i.e.,
\lVert{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}[k]\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq P_{\text{TX}}[k]
. The received signal at the subcarrier
k
can be expressed as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{y}}[k]

\displaystyle=({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}[k]+{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}[k]{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}[k]){\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}[k]{\mathbf{s}}[k]+{\mathbf{n}}[k]={\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}[k]{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}[k]{\mathbf{s}}[k]+{\mathbf{n}}[k]

(46)

where
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}[k]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{r}}\times N_{\text{t}}},{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}[k]\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N_{\text{t}}}
, and
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}[k]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{r}}\times M}
denote the frequency-domain channel matrices at the subcarrier
k
from the TX to RX, from the TX to IRS, and from the IRS to RX, respectively. The total combined channel matrix at the subcarrier
k
from the TX to RX is denoted by
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}[k]={\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}[k]+{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}[k]{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}[k]
, and the AWGN vector
{\mathbf{n}}[k]
has entries that are i.i.d with
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2}_{n})
. The RX first applies the analog combiner
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}
to the received signal in the time domain, and then transforms it into the frequency domain by removing the CP and performing the
K
-point FFT at each of its
N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}
RF chains. Finally, the RX uses the baseband combiner
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{BB}}[k]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}\times N_{\text{s}}}
to obtain the processed received signal
\tilde{{\mathbf{y}}}[k]={\mathbf{W}}_{\text{BB}}[k]^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{y}}[k]
at each subcarrier
k
. For each
i\in\{\text{TR, TI, IR}\}
, the channel matrix
{\mathbf{H}}_{i}[k]
at the subcarrier
k
is expressed as
[
9
]

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{i}[k]=\sum_{q=0}^{N_{\text{path}}^{i}-1}\alpha_{i,q}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{i,q},\theta^{\text{r}}_{i,q}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{t}}(\phi^{\text{t}}_{i,q},\theta^{\text{t}}_{i,q})^{\mathrm{H}}e^{-j2\pi qk/K}.

(47)

The achievable spectral efficiency over the subcarrier
k
is given by

\displaystyle R[k]=\log_{2}\text{det}({\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{s}}}+{\mathbf{R}}_{\bar{{\mathbf{n}}}[k]}^{-1}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{BB}}[k]^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}[k]{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}[k]{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}[k]^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{F}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}[k]^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{BB}}[k]),

(48)

where
{\mathbf{R}}_{\bar{{\mathbf{n}}}[k]}=\sigma^{2}_{n}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{BB}}[k]^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{BB}}[k]
is the covariance matrix of
\bar{{\mathbf{n}}}[k]={\mathbf{W}}_{\text{BB}}[k]^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{n}}[k]
.

The problem of designing the IRS reflection matrix and hybrid beamformer that maximize the spectral efficiency of MIMO-OFDM systems can be formulated as

\displaystyle\text{(P5)}\hskip 13.0pt\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hskip 12.0pt\{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{BB}}[k],{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}[k]\}_{k=0}^{K-1},\\
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}},{\boldsymbol{\Phi}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}\end{subarray}}\hskip 2.0pt\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}R[k]

(49a)

\displaystyle\hskip 4.0pt\text{subject to }\hskip 7.0pt{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}=\text{diag}([e^{j\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{j\theta_{M}}]),

(49b)

\displaystyle\hskip 54.0pt\lVert{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}[k]\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq P_{\text{TX}}[k],

(49c)

\displaystyle\hskip 54.0pt|[{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}]_{m,n}|=1/\sqrt{N_{\text{r}}},\medspace\forall m,n,

(49d)

\displaystyle\hskip 54.0pt|[{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}]_{m,n}|=1/\sqrt{N_{\text{t}}},\medspace\forall m,n.

(49e)

Following the similar steps used to derive the problem (P3) in Section
III-A
, we can formulate the effective channel design problem for MIMO-OFDM systems as

\displaystyle\text{(P6)}\hskip 35.0pt\max_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}},{\boldsymbol{\Phi}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}}\medspace\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}[k])

(50a)

\displaystyle\hskip 4.0pt\text{subject to }\hskip 11.0pt{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}=\text{diag}([e^{j\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{j\theta_{M}}]),

(50b)

\displaystyle\hskip 58.0pt|[{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}]_{m,n}|=1/\sqrt{N_{\text{r}}},\medspace\forall m,n,

(50c)

\displaystyle\hskip 58.0pt|[{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}]_{m,n}|=1/\sqrt{N_{\text{t}}},\medspace\forall m,n,

(50d)

\displaystyle\hskip 58.0pt{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}},

(50e)

where
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}[k])
denotes the maximum achievable spectral efficiency under the effective channel
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}[k]={\mathbf{W}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}[k]{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
at the subcarrier
k
. Note that, due to the lack of baseband processing capabilities at the IRS
[
27
,
25
]
, the design of
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
must take the channel matrices of all the subcarriers into account. Likewise,
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
and
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}
are set to be identical for all the subcarriers since both of them are applied after the inverse FFT operation. These two characteristics of MIMO-OFDM systems make it impossible to directly apply the joint design proposed in Section
III
to frequency-selective mmWave channels.

IV-B

IRS Reflection Matrix Design for MIMO-OFDM Systems

To tackle the effective channel design problem (P6) for MIMO-OFDM systems, we adopt the strategy similar to the one described in Section
III
, where we first designed the IRS reflection matrix
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
, and then constructed the analog precoder
{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}
and combiner
{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}
accordingly. We first define the complex gain matrix
{\mathbf{G}}_{i}[k]
for the mmWave channel
{\mathbf{H}}_{i}[k]
in (
47
) as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}_{i}[k]=\text{diag}\left(\big[\alpha_{i,0},\dots,\alpha_{i,N_{\text{path}}^{i}-1}\cdot e^{-j2\pi(N_{\text{path}}^{i}-1)k/K}\big]\right),

(51)

where, without loss of generality, we assume that
|[{\mathbf{G}}_{i}[k]]_{m,m}|\geq|[{\mathbf{G}}_{i}[k]]_{n,n}|,\forall m,n\in\{1,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{i}\}
such that
m<n
. We can then express the decomposition of
{\mathbf{H}}_{i}[k]
as

{\mathbf{H}}_{i}[k]={\mathbf{A}}^{i}_{\text{r}}{\mathbf{G}}_{i}[k]({\mathbf{A}}^{i}_{\text{t}})^{\mathrm{H}}
,
which has the identical structure as that of
{\mathbf{H}}_{i}
in (
12
). Therefore, by following the similar steps used to derive (
28
), we can show that
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k]={\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TR}}[k]+{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{IR}}[k]{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{TI}}[k]
satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k]({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k])^{\mathrm{H}}\rightarrow\sum_{s=0}^{N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}-1}|\alpha_{\text{TR},s}|^{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{TR},s})^{\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle\hskip 150.0pt+|\alpha_{\text{TI},0}|^{2}|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0}){\mathbf{a}}_{\text{r}}(\phi^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0},\theta^{\text{r}}_{\text{IR},0})^{\mathrm{H}},

(52)

as
N_{\text{t}},M\rightarrow\infty
, where
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}=\text{diag}({\mathbf{v}}^{\star})
is the IRS reflection matrix proposed in Section
III-B
, and
{\mathbf{v}}^{\star}
is given in (
31
). Since (
28
) and (
52
) indicate that
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k]({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k])^{\mathrm{H}}
and
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
converge to the same matrix for each
k\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}
, the IRS reflection matrix
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}
has the same asymptotic effect on the eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}[k]({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}[k])^{\mathrm{H}}
as it does on those of
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
. As the inequality in (
36
) guarantees that the asymptotic eigenvalue
|\alpha_{\text{TI},0}|^{2}|\alpha_{\text{IR},0}|^{2}
of
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
and
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}[k]({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}[k])^{\mathrm{H}}
increases at least quadratically with the number of IRS elements
M
, it can be concluded that the proposed IRS reflection matrix design in Section
III-B
will provide both narrowband and broadband MIMO systems with considerable gains in spectral efficiency.

IV-C

Analog Beamformer Design for MIMO-OFDM Systems

In the previous subsection, we showed that every frequency-domain channel matrix
{\mathbf{H}}_{i}[k]
at the subcarrier
k
shares the same receive array response matrix
{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}^{i}
and transmit array response matrix
{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}^{i}
. In this subsection, we exploit this particular property of frequency-selective mmWave channels to extend the proposed analog beamformer design in Section
III-C
to MIMO-OFDM systems. With
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}={\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\star}
, the effective channel design problem (P6) is simplified as

\displaystyle\text{(P7)}\hskip 33.0pt\max_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}}\medspace\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k]{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}})

(53a)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\text{subject to }\hskip 10.0pt|[{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}]_{m,n}|=1/\sqrt{N_{\text{r}}},\medspace\forall m,n,

(53b)

\displaystyle\hskip 71.0pt|[{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}]_{m,n}|=1/\sqrt{N_{\text{t}}},\medspace\forall m,n,

(53c)

\displaystyle\hskip 71.0pt{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}.

(53d)

Now consider
{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},k^{*}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{t}}\times N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}
and
{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},k^{*}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{r}}\times N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}}
, which denote the analog precoder and combiner obtained by applying the proposed analog beamformer design in Section
III-C
to
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k^{*}]
for some
k^{*}\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}
. Then, it holds for each
k\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}
that
R_{\text{max}}(({\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},k^{*}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k]{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},k^{*}})
asymptotically approaches the maximum achievable spectral efficiency
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k])
under
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k]
. To see this, note that, as discussed in Section
IV-B
,

{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k]({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k])^{\mathrm{H}}
and
{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}
converge to the same matrix as
N_{\text{t}},M\rightarrow\infty
. Likewise, it can be shown that
({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k])^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k]
and
({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}
converge to the same
N_{\text{t}}\times N_{\text{t}}
matrix as
N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
. Furthermore, since Proposition 3 implies that
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}[k]{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}})\leq R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k])
for each feasible solution
\{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}\}
of the problem (P7), we have that
\{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},k^{*}},{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}={\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},k^{*}}\}
approaches the optimal solution of (P7) as
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
.

With these formulations in mind, we now explain the analog beamformer design for frequency-selective mmWave channels. First, we use the analog beamformer design proposed in Section
III-C
to create the block matrices
{\mathbf{F}}^{\text{block}}_{\text{RF}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{t}}\times KN_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}
and
{\mathbf{W}}^{\text{block}}_{\text{RF}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{r}}\times KN_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}}
, where

\displaystyle{\mathbf{F}}^{\text{block}}_{\text{RF}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},0}&{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},1}&\dots&{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},K-1}\end{bmatrix},{\mathbf{W}}^{\text{block}}_{\text{RF}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},0}&{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},1}&\dots&{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF},K-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(54)

Then, the proposed analog precoder and combiner are given by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}=\left[[{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}]_{:,e_{1}},\dots,[{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}]_{:,e_{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}}\right],

(55)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{RF}}^{\star}=\left[[{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}]_{:,f_{1}},\dots,[{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}]_{:,f_{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}}}\right],

(56)

where

\{[{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{t}}]_{:,e_{u}}\}_{u=1}^{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}}
is the set of
N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}
vectors that appear most frequently in the columns of
{\mathbf{F}}^{\text{block}}_{\text{RF}}
,
e_{u}\in\{1,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}+N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TI}}\}
,
\{[{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{r}}]_{:,f_{v}}\}_{v=1}^{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}}
is the set of
N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}
vectors that appear most frequently in the columns of
{\mathbf{W}}^{\text{block}}_{\text{RF}}
, and
f_{v}\in\{1,\dots,N_{\text{path}}^{\text{TR}}+N_{\text{path}}^{\text{IR}}\}
. Analogous to (
43
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}[k])\rightarrow\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{tot}}^{\star}[k]),

(57)

as
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
, where
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}[k]=({\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k]{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}
. The result in (
57
) demonstrates that the proposed analog precoder
{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}
and combiner
{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}
are asymptotically optimal for the problem (P7), and thus are highly effective in practical mmWave MIMO-OFDM systems.

IV-D

Baseband Beamformer Design for MIMO-OFDM Systems

In contrast to the IRS reflection matrix and analog precoder/combiner, the baseband precoder and combiner can be designed differently for each subcarrier. This flexibility greatly simplifies the generalization of the proposed baseband beamformer design from narrowband to broadband MIMO systems. That is, at each subcarrier
k\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}
, we can directly utilize the baseband beamformer design in Section
III-D
to construct the baseband combiner
{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}[k]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{r}}^{\text{RF}}\times N_{\text{s}}}
and precoder
{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}[k]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{t}}^{\text{RF}}\times N_{\text{s}}}
that asymptotically attain the maximum achievable spectral efficiency
R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}[k])
under
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}[k]=({\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k]{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}
. Denoting the SVD of
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}[k]
as
{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}[k]={\mathbf{U}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}[k]{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}[k]({\mathbf{V}}_{\text{eff}}^{\star}[k])^{\mathrm{H}}
, we can express
{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}[k]
and
{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}[k]
as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}[k]

\displaystyle=[{\mathbf{U}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}[k]]_{:,1:N_{\text{s}}},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}[k]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\star}[k][{\mathbf{V}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}[k]]_{:,1:N_{\text{s}}}({\mathbf{P}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}[k])^{1/2},

(58)

where
({\mathbf{P}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}[k])^{1/2}=\text{diag}\left(\left[\sqrt{P^{\star}_{1}[k]},...,\sqrt{P^{\star}_{N_{\text{s}}}[k]}\medspace\right]\right)
,
P^{\star}_{l}[k]=\left(1/\eta^{\star}[k]-\sigma^{2}_{n}/|[{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}[k]]_{l,l}|^{2}\right)^{+},\forall l\in\{1,\dots,N_{\text{s}}\}
, and
\eta^{\star}[k]
is chosen such that
\sum_{l=1}^{N_{\text{s}}}P^{\star}_{l}[k]=P_{\text{TX}}[k]
. Similar to
\gamma^{\star}
in (
45
), the normalization factor
\gamma^{\star}[k]
is defined as

\displaystyle\gamma^{\star}[k]=\frac{\sqrt{P_{\text{TX}}[k]}}{\|{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}[{\mathbf{V}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}[k]]_{:,1:N_{\text{s}}}({\mathbf{P}}^{\star}_{\text{eff}}[k])^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{F}}},

(59)

so that the transmit power constraint is met with equality at each subcarrier, i.e.,
\|{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}^{\star}[k]\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=P_{\text{TX}}[k],\medspace\forall k\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}
. Since
({\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{I}}_{N^{\text{RF}}_{\text{t}}}
and
({\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{I}}_{N^{\text{RF}}_{\text{r}}}
as
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}}\rightarrow\infty
, it follows directly from the discussions in Section
III-D
and (
57
) that the proposed hybrid beamformer
\{{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}_{\text{BB}}[k],{\mathbf{F}}^{\star}_{\text{RF}}{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{BB}}^{\star}[k]\}_{k=0}^{K-1}
achieves spectral efficiency that approaches arbitrarily close to
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}R_{\text{max}}({\mathbf{H}}^{\star}_{\text{tot}}[k])
as
N_{\text{t}},N_{\text{r}},M\rightarrow\infty
. Given that IRS-aided mmWave MIMO-OFDM systems typically employ a large number of antennas and IRS elements, it can be concluded that, even with a significantly reduced number of RF chains, the proposed hybrid beamformer can attain performance close to that of fully-digital beamformer, as will be demonstrated in Section
VI
.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we studied narrowband and broadband IRS-aided mmWave MIMO systems with hybrid beamforming architectures. We first formulated the problem of designing the IRS reflection matrix and analog beamformer for narrowband MIMO systems into the effective channel design problem. By leveraging the sparse-scattering structure and large dimension of mmWave channels, we developed the joint design of IRS reflection matrix and hybrid beamformer for narrowband MIMO systems. We generalized the proposed joint design for narrowband MIMO systems to broadband MIMO-OFDM systems by carefully exploiting the sparsity of frequency-selective mmWave channels in the angular domain. Simulation results demonstrated that the proposed designs can provide the systems of interest with significant spectral efficiency gains and outperform the existing state-of-the-art designs while requiring lower computational complexity. Interesting future research directions include the investigation of the IRS reflection matrix and beamformer design that minimizes the inter-user interference in IRS-aided multi-user systems with hybrid beamforming architectures.

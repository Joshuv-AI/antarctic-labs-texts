Introduction

The prime number theorem, proved independently by Hadamard
[
Had96
]
and de la Vallée Poussin
[
Val96
]
, describes the asymptotic behavior

function
f(x)
has the same asymptotic behaviour of function
g(x)
iff
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=1
of the prime counting
\pi(x)
as follows:

Theorem 1
.

\displaystyle\pi(x)\sim\frac{x}{\log(x)}
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi(x)}{\left(\frac{x}{\log(x)}\right)}=1

Dirichlet in
[
Lej38
]
conjectured that a better approximation to
\pi(x)
is the offset logarithmic integral function
\text{Li}(x)=li(x)-li(2)
which captures the notion of prime numbers density function.

Definition 2
.

\displaystyle li(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log(t)}
is the
logarithmic integral
function. Here,
\log(x)
is the natural logarithm function.

Function
li(x)
is defined for all positive real number
x\neq 1
where function
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log(t)}
has a singularity.

In
[
Val99
]
, de la Vallée Poussin proved that
\pi(x)\sim\text{Li}(x)
.

Theorem 3
.

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\text{Li}(x)+O(xe^{-a\sqrt{\log(x)}})
as
x\to\infty
for some positive constant
a
.

In fact, assuming the Riemann Hypothesis it is possible to show that the absolute difference between
\pi(x)
and
li(x)
is
\left|\pi(x)-li(x)\right|=O\left(\sqrt{x}\log(x)\right)
, see
[
Nar00
]
. Moreover, to prove the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to prove the following statement:
\left|\pi(x)-li(x)\right|=O\left(x^{1/2+\alpha}\right)
for any
\alpha>0
.

Function
li(x)
has an asymptotic expansion in the form of a divergent power series that must be truncated after some finite number of terms for obtaining an approximated value of
li(x)
.

Lemma 4
.

\displaystyle li(x)\sim\frac{x}{\log(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{\log^{k}(x)}
.

Stieltjes in
[
Sti86
]
determined the optimal truncation point of the asymptotic approximation series (series is truncated at term minimizing
\varepsilon_{n}(x)
) is
n=\lfloor\log(x)\rfloor
. Also, van Boven et al. in
[
BWW12
]
introduced a fractional term that works like a ’remainder’ of the asymptotic expansion series, see Definition
5
.

Definition 5
.

The
Stieltjes asymptotic approximation
of
li(x)
is the function
\displaystyle li_{*}(x)=\frac{\alpha xn!}{\log^{n+1}(x)}+\frac{x}{\log(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{k!}{\log^{k}(x)}
, defined for all
x\geq e
, with
\displaystyle n=\lfloor\log(x)\rfloor
and
\alpha=\log(x)-n
.

Lemma 6
.

\displaystyle\frac{x}{\log(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{k!}{\log^{k}(x)}\leq li_{*}(x)\leq\frac{x}{\log(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{k!}{\log^{k}(x)}
for all
x\geq e
, with
\displaystyle n=\lfloor\log(x)\rfloor
.

Proof.

Follows from Definition
5
and
0\leq\alpha=\log(x)-n\leq 1
.
∎

The Stieltjes asymptotic approximation error is
\varepsilon(x)=li(x)-li_{*}(x)
for all real number
x\geq 0
. In
[
Gom24
]
, we proved that
\displaystyle|\varepsilon(x)|\leq 1.265692883422\ldots
for all
x\geq e
.

In this paper, we bound the error of approximating
li(x)
when using the asymptotic series if it is truncated at term
\displaystyle m=\lfloor\kappa\log(x)\rfloor
with
0<\kappa<e
. For doing this, we use the upper bound of
n!
defined by Stirling’s approximation formula
[
Sti30
]

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n+1}}<n!<\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n}}\text{ for all }n\geq 1.

2.1.
Stirling’s Fractional Extension

The following bounds can be obtained from Stirling’s approximation formula (Equation
1.1
):

Lemma 7
.

\displaystyle\frac{m!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}<\frac{C_{\kappa}}{x^{\kappa(1-\log(\kappa))}\sqrt{\log(x)}}
for all
0<\kappa\leq e
and all
\displaystyle x\geq e^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}
real numbers, with
\displaystyle m=\lfloor\kappa\log(x)\rfloor
and
\displaystyle C_{\kappa}=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}}e^{\frac{13}{12}}
.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle x\geq e^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}
then
\displaystyle\kappa\log(x)\geq 1
and
m=\lfloor\kappa\log(x)\rfloor\geq 1
. Now,
\displaystyle\frac{m!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}<\frac{\sqrt{2\pi m}\left(\frac{m}{e}\right)^{m}e^{\frac{1}{12m}}}{\log^{m+1}(x)}
(divide Stirling’s approximation right inequality by
\log^{m+1}(x)
). Clearly,
\displaystyle\frac{m!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}<\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa\log(x)}\left(\frac{\kappa\log(x)}{e}\right)^{m}e^{\frac{1}{12m}}}{\log^{m+1}(x)}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa}\left(\frac{\kappa}{e}\right)^{m}e^{\frac{1}{12m}}}{\sqrt{\log(x)}}
(since
\displaystyle m\leq\kappa\log(x)
). So,
\displaystyle\frac{m!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}<\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa}\left(\frac{e}{\kappa}\right)\left(\frac{\kappa}{e}\right)^{\kappa\log(x)}e^{\frac{1}{12m}}}{\sqrt{\log(x)}}
(Lemma
39
), i.e.,
\displaystyle\frac{m!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}<\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa}\left(\frac{e}{\kappa}\right)e^{\frac{1}{12m}}}{x^{\kappa(1-\log(\kappa))}\sqrt{\log(x)}}=\frac{\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}}e^{1+\frac{1}{12m}}}{x^{\kappa(1-\log(\kappa))}\sqrt{\log(x)}}
(Corollary
37
). The inequality holds because
\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}}e^{1+\frac{1}{12m}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}}e^{1+\frac{1}{12}}=C_{\kappa}
for all
m\geq 1
.
∎

.

\displaystyle\frac{xm!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}<C_{\kappa}\frac{x^{1-\kappa(1-\log(\kappa))}}{\sqrt{\log(x)}}

0<\kappa\leq e

\displaystyle x\geq e^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}

\displaystyle m=\lfloor\kappa\log(x)\rfloor

\displaystyle C_{\kappa}=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}}e^{\frac{13}{12}}

Lemma 9
.

\displaystyle\frac{B_{\kappa}}{x^{\kappa(1-\log(\kappa))}\sqrt{\log(x)}}<\frac{m!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}
for all real numbers
0<\kappa\leq e
and
\displaystyle x\geq e^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}
, with
\displaystyle m=\lfloor\kappa\log(x)\rfloor
and
\displaystyle B_{\kappa}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\kappa}}
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\kappa\log(x)\geq 1
and
m=\lfloor\kappa\log(x)\rfloor\geq 1
(
\displaystyle x\geq e^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}
). Now,
\displaystyle\frac{m!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}>\frac{\sqrt{2\pi m}\left(\frac{m}{e}\right)^{m}e^{\frac{1}{12m+1}}}{\log^{m+1}(x)}
(divide Stirling’s approximation left inequality by
\log^{m+1}(x)
). Clearly,
\displaystyle\frac{m!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}>\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}m^{m+1}\kappa\left(\frac{\kappa}{e}\right)^{m}e^{\frac{1}{12m+1}}}{\sqrt{m}\left(\kappa\log(x)\right)^{m+1}}>\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}m^{m+1}\kappa\left(\frac{\kappa}{e}\right)^{m}e^{\frac{1}{12m+1}}}{\sqrt{m}(m+1)^{m+1}}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\kappa\left(\frac{\kappa}{e}\right)^{m}e^{\frac{1}{12m+1}}}{\sqrt{m}}\left(\frac{m}{m+1}\right)^{m+1}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa}\left(\frac{\kappa}{e}\right)^{m}e^{\frac{1}{12m+1}-1}}{2\sqrt{\log(x)}}

\displaystyle\frac{m}{m+1}\geq\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{m!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}>\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa}\left(\frac{e}{\kappa}\right)\left(\frac{\kappa}{e}\right)^{\kappa\log(x)}e^{\frac{1}{12m+1}-1}}{2\sqrt{\log(x)}}

\displaystyle\frac{m!}{\log^{m+1}(x)}>\frac{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\kappa}}e^{\frac{1}{12m+1}}}{x^{\kappa(1-\log(\kappa))}\sqrt{\log(x)}}
(Corollary

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\kappa}}e^{\frac{1}{12m+1}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\kappa}}=B_{\kappa}

m\geq 1

∎

Proof.

Follows from
li_{0}(x)\leq\underline{li}(x)
and
\overline{li}(x)\leq li_{1}(x)
.
∎

Lemma 34
.

If Conjecture
31
holds then
\displaystyle|li(x)-\pi(x)|=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{x}{\log(x)}}\right)
for all

\displaystyle|li(x)-\pi(x)|\leq\underline{D}\sqrt{\frac{x}{\log(x)}}+1.265692883422\ldots

∎

3.
Auxiliary Facts and Proofs

We introduce some customary statements that we use for proving our main results.

Lemma 36
.

\displaystyle\alpha^{\alpha\log(x)}=x^{\alpha\log(\alpha)}
for all
x>0
and all
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

\log(\alpha^{\alpha\log(x)})=\alpha\log(x)\log(\alpha)=\alpha\log(\alpha)\log(x)=\log\left(x^{\alpha\log(\alpha)})\right)
(logarithm properties). Therefore,
\displaystyle\alpha^{\alpha\log(x)}=x^{\alpha\log(\alpha)}
.
∎

Corollary 37
.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha}{e}\right)^{\alpha\log(x)}=\frac{1}{x^{\alpha(1-\log(\alpha))}}
for all
x>0
and all
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha}{e}\right)^{\alpha\log(x)}=\frac{\alpha^{\alpha\log(x)}}{e^{\alpha\log(x)}}=\frac{x^{\alpha\log(\alpha)}}{e^{\alpha\log(x)}}
(Lemma
36
). Therefore,
\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha}{e}\right)^{\alpha\log(x)}=\frac{x^{\alpha\log(\alpha)}}{x^{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{x^{\alpha(1-\log(\alpha))}}
.
∎

Lemma 38
.

a-1<\lfloor a\rfloor\leq\lceil a\rceil<a+1
for all real number
0<a
.

Proof.

Clearly,
a-1<\lfloor a\rfloor=a=\lceil a\rceil<a+1
if
a
is a natural number. Now,
\lceil a\rceil-\lfloor a\rfloor=1
if
a
is not a natural number. Then,
a<\lceil a\rceil
so
a-\lceil a\rceil+\lfloor a\rfloor<\lfloor a\rfloor
, i.e.,
a-1\leq\lfloor a\rfloor
. Moreover,
\lfloor a\rfloor<a
then
\lceil a\rceil\leq a+\lceil a\rceil-\lfloor a\rfloor
, i.e.,
\lceil a\rceil\leq a+1
.
∎

Lemma 39
.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\lfloor a\rfloor}\leq\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{a}
and
\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{a}\leq\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\lceil a\rceil}
for all pair of real numbers
0<r\leq s
and all real number
0<a
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\displaystyle\frac{r}{s}\leq 1
(since
0<r\leq s
). Since
a-1\leq\lfloor a\rfloor
(Lemma
38
) then
\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\lfloor a\rfloor}\leq\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{a-1}=\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{a}
. Now
a+1\geq\lceil a\rceil
(Lemma
38
) then
\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\lceil a\rceil}\geq\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{a+1}=\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{a}
.
∎

Lemma 40
.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{k!}{(n+1)^{k}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k!}{n^{k}}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

We have that
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k!}{n^{k}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{k!}{(n+1)^{k}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k!\left(\frac{1}{n^{k}}-\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k}}\right)-\frac{n!}{(n+1)^{n}}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}k!\left(\frac{1}{n^{k}}-\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k}}\right)+n!\left(\frac{1}{n^{n}}-\frac{2}{(n+1)^{n}}\right)\geq\frac{n!}{(n+1)^{n}}\left(\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^{n}-2\right)\geq 0
(since
\displaystyle\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^{n}
is an increasing function then
\displaystyle\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^{n}\geq\left(\frac{1+1}{1}\right)^{1}=2
).
∎

Corollary 41
.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k!}{n^{k}}\leq 1
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

We use induction. For
m=1
we have
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{1}\frac{k!}{1^{k}}=1
. For
m=n+1
we have
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{k!}{m^{k}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{k!}{(n+1)^{k}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k!}{n^{k}}\leq 1
(Lemma
40
and induction step).
∎

Lemma 42
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(m+i)\leq\frac{1}{y^{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}(m+i)\leq 1
for all real number
y\geq 1
and all natural numbers
m
,
k
, and
j
such that
\displaystyle m<\lfloor y\rfloor
and all natural number
1\leq j\leq k\leq\lfloor y\rfloor-m
.

Proof.

If
j=k
the statement holds trivially. If
j<k
then
\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(m+i)}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{y^{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}(m+i)}=\frac{1}{y^{k-j}}\prod_{i=1}^{k-j}(m+j+i)\leq 1
(since
m+j+i\leq m+k=\lfloor y\rfloor
). Clearly,
\displaystyle\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(m+i)\leq\frac{1}{y^{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{1}(m+i)=\frac{m+1}{y}\leq\frac{\lfloor y\rfloor}{y}\leq\frac{y}{y}=1
.
∎

Lemma 43
.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-m}\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(m+i)\leq n-m
for all real number
y\geq 1
and all natural number
m
such that
m<n
, with
n=\lfloor y\rfloor
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-m}\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(m+i)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-m}1=n-m
(Lemma
42
).
∎

Lemma 44
.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-m}\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(m+i)\leq 2\left(\frac{y+m}{y-m}\right)
for all real number
y\geq 1
and all natural number
\displaystyle m<n
, with
n=\lfloor y\rfloor
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-m}\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(m+i)=\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(m+i)+\sum_{k=m^{\prime}+1}^{n-m}\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(m+i)
for
\displaystyle m^{\prime}=\left\lfloor\frac{n-m}{2}\right\rfloor
. Since every term in the first summation of the right part is greater or equal than every term in the second summation of the right part (Lemma
42
), and the number of terms in the first summation is greater or equal than the number of terms in the second summation then
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-m}\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(m+i\right)\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(m+i\right)
. Notice that,
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-m}\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(m+i\right)\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{m+k}{y}\right)^{k}\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{m+\left\lfloor\frac{n-m}{2}\right\rfloor}{y}\right)^{k}\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{n+m}{2y}\right)^{k}\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{y+m}{2y}\right)^{k}=2\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{y+m}{2y}\right)^{k}-1\right)
. Since
m<n=\lfloor y\rfloor\leq y
then
\displaystyle y+m<2y
,
\displaystyle\frac{y+m}{2y}<1
, and
\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{y+m}{2y}\right)^{k}<\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{y+m}{2y}\right)^{k}\leq\frac{1}{1-\displaystyle\frac{y+m}{2y}}=\frac{2y}{y-m}
(geometric series).
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-m}\frac{1}{y^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(m+i\right)\leq 2\left(\frac{2y}{y-m}-1\right)=2\left(\frac{y+m}{y-m}\right)
.
∎

Lemma 45
.

\displaystyle y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}\leq y^{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{m-i}\leq\frac{y}{m}\leq 1
for all real number
y\geq 1
and all natural numbers
m
,
k
, and
j
such that
\displaystyle n<m
and
1\leq j\leq k\leq m-n
. Here,
n=\lfloor y\rfloor
.

Proof.

If
k=j
the statement holds trivially. If
j<k
then
\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}}{\displaystyle y^{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{m-i}}=y^{k-j}\prod_{i=0}^{k-j-1}\frac{1}{m-j-i}\leq y^{k-j}\prod_{i=0}^{k-j-1}\frac{1}{m-k+1}\leq\left(\frac{y}{n+1}\right)^{k-j}\leq\frac{y}{y}=1
. Clearly,
\displaystyle y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}\leq y\prod_{i=0}^{0}\frac{1}{m-i}=\frac{y}{m}\leq 1
.
∎

Lemma 46
.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}\leq m-n
for all real number
y\geq 1
and all natural number
m
such that
m-n\geq 1
, with
n=\lfloor y\rfloor
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\displaystyle k-1\leq m-n-1=m-\lfloor y\rfloor-1\leq m-y
and
m-i\geq m-k+1\geq n+1
for all
k=1,\ldots,m-n
and all
i=0,\ldots,k-1
. Then,
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}\left(\frac{y}{m-k+1}\right)^{k}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}\left(\frac{y}{n+1}\right)^{k}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}\left(\frac{y}{y}\right)^{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}1=m-n
.
∎

Lemma 47
.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}\leq 2\left(\frac{2y}{m-y+1}\right)
for all real number
y\geq 1
and all natural number
m
such that
m-n\geq 1
, with
n=\lfloor y\rfloor
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}=\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}+\sum_{k=m^{\prime}+1}^{m-n}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}
for
\displaystyle m^{\prime}=\left\lfloor\frac{m-n}{2}\right\rfloor
. Since every term in the first summation of the right part is greater or equal than every term in the second summation of the right part (Lemma
45
) and the number of terms in the first summation is greater or equal than the number of terms in the second summation then
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}
. Since
m-i<m-k+1
for all
i=0,\ldots,k-1
then,
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{y}{m-k+1}\right)^{k}\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{y}{m-m^{\prime}+1}\right)^{k}=2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{y}{m-\left\lfloor\displaystyle\frac{m-n}{2}\right\rfloor+1}\right)^{k}\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{y}{m-\displaystyle\frac{m-n}{2}+1}\right)^{k}\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{2y}{n+m+2}\right)^{k}=2\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{2y}{n+m+2}\right)^{k}-1\right)
. Since
n+m+2\geq n+n+3>2(n+1)>2\lceil y\rceil\geq 2y
then
\displaystyle\frac{2y}{n+m+2}<1
and
\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\frac{2y}{n+m+2}\right)^{k}<\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2y}{n+m+2}\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{1-\displaystyle\frac{2y}{n+m+2}}=\frac{n+m+2}{n+m+2-2y}
. So,
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}\leq 2\left(\frac{n+m+2}{n+m+2-2y}-1\right)=2\left(\frac{2y}{n+m+2-2y}\right)
(geometric series). Since
n+1\geq y
then
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}y^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m-i}\leq 2\left(\frac{2y}{m-y+1}\right)
.
∎

4.
Conclusions and Future Work

We establish bounds for logarithmic integral function
li(x)
using truncated asymptotic approximation series: (1)
\displaystyle li_{\underline{\omega},\alpha}(x)=\frac{x}{\log(x)}\left(\alpha\frac{\underline{m}!}{\log^{\underline{m}}(x)}+\sum_{k=0}^{\underline{m}-1}\frac{k!}{\log^{k}(x)}\right)
, and (2)
\displaystyle li_{\overline{\omega},\beta}=\frac{x}{\log(x)}\left(\beta\frac{\overline{m}!}{\log^{\overline{m}}(x)}+\sum_{k=0}^{\overline{m}-1}\frac{k!}{\log^{k}(x)}\right)

for all
x\geq e
with
0<\omega<1
,
\alpha\in\{0,\underline{\kappa}\log(x)\}
,
\underline{m}=\lfloor\underline{\kappa}\log(x)\rfloor
,
\beta\in\{\overline{\kappa}\log(x),1\}
,
\overline{m}=\lfloor\overline{\kappa}\log(x)\rfloor
, and
\underline{\kappa}<\overline{\kappa}
the solutions of
\kappa(1-\log(\kappa))=\omega
. Such bounds are of the order
\displaystyle|li(x)-li_{0}(x)|=|li_{1}(x)-li(x)|=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{x}{\log(x)}}\right)
for all
x\geq e
. We conjecture that
li_{0}(x)\leq\pi(x)\leq li_{1}(x)
and
\underline{li}(x)\leq\pi(x)\leq\overline{li}(x)
and show that if one of such conjectures is true, the Riemann Hypothesis is true. Our future work will concentrate on trying to demonstrate such conjectures.

Asynchronous Distributed Optimization with Delay-free Parameters
Xuyang Wu, Changxin Liu, Sindri Magnússon, Mikael Johansson

Abstract
Existing asynchronous distributed optimization algorithms often use diminishing step-sizes that cause slow practical convergence, or use fixed step-sizes that depend on and decrease with an upper bound of the delays. Not only are such delay bounds hard to obtain in advance, but they also tend to be large and rarely attained, resulting in unnecessarily slow convergence. This paper develops asynchronous versions of two distributed algorithms, Prox-DGD and DGD-ATC, for solving consensus optimization problems over undirected networks. In contrast to alternatives, our algorithms can converge to the fixed point set of their synchronous counterparts using step-sizes that are independent of the delays. We establish convergence guarantees for convex and strongly convex problems under both partial and total asynchrony. We also show that the convergence speed of the two asynchronous methods adjusts to the actual level of asynchrony rather than being constrained by the worst-case. Numerical experiments demonstrate a strong practical performance of our asynchronous algorithms.

Introduction

Distributed optimization has attracted much attention in the last decade and has found applications in diverse areas such as cooperative control, machine learning, and power systems.
The literature on distributed optimization has primarily focused on synchronous methods that iterate in a serialized manner, proceeding to the next iteration only after the current one is completed. Synchronous methods require all nodes to maintain a consistent view of the optimization variables without any information delay. This makes the algorithms easier to analyze, but more difficult to realize. Global synchronization through a network can be challenging in practice. Additionally, synchronized updates are inefficient and unreliable since the time taken per iteration is determined by the slowest node and the optimization process is vulnerable to single-node failures.

Asynchronous Prox-DGD

In this section, we introduce the asynchronous Prox-DGD and analyze its convergence. The algorithm is fully asynchronous in the sense that it requires neither global synchronization between nodes nor access to a global clock.

III-A

In the asynchronous Prox-DGD algorithm, each node is activated at discrete points in time.
When node

III-B

Convergence Analysis

In this section, we analyse the convergence of the asynchronous Prox-DGD under two typical models of asynchrony and show that the convergence can adjust to real delays rather than be determined by the worst-case delay. A key difference with the literature is that our results are established under a condition on the step-size
\alpha
that does not depend on any information about the delays. We refer to this type of condition as a
delay-free
step-size condition.
Unless stated otherwise, all convergence results in this section assume that
W
is an averaging matrix associated with
\mathcal{G}
(see Definition
1
).
Our first result allows for total asynchrony in the sense of Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis
[
38
]
.

Assumption 6

(total asynchrony)
.

The following holds:

1.

\mathcal{K}_{i}
is an infinite subset of
\mathbb{N}_{0}
for each
i\in\mathcal{V}
.

2.

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}s_{ij}^{k}=+\infty
for any
i\in\mathcal{V}
and
j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
.

Assumption
6
pose conditions on the update frequency of each node and the delays. Specifically, condition 1) requires each node to update for infinite times, and condition 2) allows the information delays
k-s_{ij}^{k}
to grow arbitrarily large but requires that old information must eventually be purged from the system.

This assumption is well-suited for scenarios where communication and computation delays are unpredictable, e.g., in massively parallel computing grids with heterogeneous computing nodes where delays can quickly add up if a node is saturated
[

. Under total asynchrony, even the asynchronous consensus method

Assumption 7

To analyse the effects of network topology, we suppose that

III-C

Convergence in terms of the Realized Level of Asynchrony

As shown in Fig.
1
, the typical delays in real systems may be (and often are) much smaller than the worst-case. In these scenarios, the convergence of asynchronous methods whose parameters depend on the worst-case delay may be slowed down significantly by the conservative parameters (See Fig. 1 in
[
18
]
). In this subsection, we will show that the convergence of the asynchronous Prox-DGD
adjusts
to the actual level of asynchrony (
the update frequency of each node and the delays
) rather than being hampered by the worst-case delay.
Our main idea is to split the set
\mathbb{N}_{0}
of all iteration indexes into the union of disjoint intervals
[k^{m},k^{m+1})
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and show that after each interval, an upper bound on the optimality error
\|\mathbf{x}^{k}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\|_{\infty}^{b}
decreases. In particular, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\|\mathbf{x}^{k}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\|_{\infty}^{b}\leq\rho\|\mathbf{x}^{k^{m}}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\|_{\infty}^{b},\quad\forall k\geq k^{m+1}.

(14)

The length of the intervals
[k^{m},k^{m+1})
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
reflects the level of asynchrony, which will be detailed later below Theorem
4
.

To introduce
\{k^{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, we define

t_{i}^{k}=\max\{\ell:\ell\leq k,\ell\in\mathcal{K}_{i}\}

(15)

as the iteration index of the most recent update by node
i
.
Because
x_{i}^{t+1}=x_{i}^{t}
for all
t\in[t_{i}^{k}+1,k]
, we have
x_{i}^{k+1}=x_{i}^{t_{i}^{k}+1}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Therefore, for all
i\in\mathcal{V}
,

x_{i}^{k+1}=\operatorname{prox}_{\alpha h_{i}}\left(\sum_{j\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{i}}w_{ij}x_{j}^{s_{ij}^{t_{i}^{k}}}-\alpha\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{t_{i}^{k}})\right).

(16)

Note that (
16
) is not an update rule, but expresses
x_{i}^{k+1}
for all
i\in\mathcal{V}
even if
i
does not update at
k
.

The maximum age of the information used to obtain each block of
\mathbf{x}^{k+1}
is then

\tau^{k}=k-\min_{i\in\mathcal{V}}\min_{j\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{i}}s_{ij}^{t_{i}^{k}}.

(17)

We define
\{k^{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
as:
k^{0}=0
and

k^{m+1}=\min\{k:t-\tau^{t}\geq k^{m}~\forall t\geq k\}+1,\forall m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(18)

which describes the following Markov property: for any
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, when
k+1\geq k^{m+1}
, the computation of
\mathbf{x}^{k+1}
does not depend on any
\mathbf{x}^{\ell}
with
\ell<k^{m}
(by (
16
) and

).

To be more clear,

VI
Conclusion

We have investigated the asynchronous version of two distributed algorithms, Prox-DGD and DGD-ATC, for solving consensus optimization problems. We analysed the optimality gap of the synchronous Prox-DGD and DGD-ATC, and proved that their asynchronous variants can converge to the fixed point set of the synchronous counterparts under
delay-free
parameter conditions. We also show that the convergence of both asynchronous algorithms adjusts to the actual level of asynchrony and is not determined by the worst-case. Finally, we demonstrated superior practical convergence of the two asynchronous algorithms in implementations on a multi-core computer.
Future works include extensions to stochastic optimization problems and directed networks

\begin{split}&\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\|^{2}=\langle\sum_{j\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{i}}\tilde{w}_{ij}(x_{j}-x_{j}^{\star}),x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\rangle\\
&-\gamma_{i}\alpha\langle\nabla f_{i}(x_{i})+g_{i}^{+}-\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{\star})-g_{i}^{\star},x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\rangle.\end{split}

(91)

Note that (
13
) implies

\begin{split}&\langle\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{+})-\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}),x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\rangle\\
\leq&\frac{1+\gamma_{i}(w_{ii}-1)}{2\gamma_{i}\alpha}\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\|^{2}\\
=&\frac{\tilde{w}_{ii}}{2\gamma_{i}\alpha}\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\|^{2}\end{split},

by which and the convexity of
f_{i}
,

\begin{split}&\langle\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}),x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\rangle\\
\geq&f_{i}(x_{i}^{+})-f_{i}(x_{i})-\frac{\tilde{w}_{ii}}{2\gamma_{i}\alpha}\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\|^{2}.\end{split}

By the above equation and the convexity of
f_{i}
,

\begin{split}&\langle\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}),x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\rangle\\
=&\langle\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}),x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\rangle+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}),x_{i}-x_{i}^{\star}\rangle\\
\geq&f_{i}(x_{i}^{+})-f_{i}(x_{i}^{\star})-\frac{\tilde{w}_{ii}}{2\gamma_{i}\alpha}\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(92)

By (
92
), the convexity of
h_{i}
, and the
\mu_{i}
-strong convexity of
f_{i}+h_{i}
, we have

\begin{split}&\langle\nabla f_{i}(x_{i})+g_{i}^{+}-\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{\star})-g_{i}^{\star},x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\rangle\\
\geq&F_{i}(x_{i}^{+})-F_{i}(x_{i}^{\star})-\langle\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{\star})+g_{i}^{\star},x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\rangle\\
&-\!\frac{\tilde{w}_{ii}}{2\gamma_{i}\alpha}\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\|^{2}\\
\geq&\frac{\mu_{i}}{2}\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\|^{2}-\!\frac{\tilde{w}_{ii}}{2\gamma_{i}\alpha}\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\|^{2}\end{split}

Moreover,

\begin{split}&\langle\sum_{j\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{i}}\tilde{w}_{ij}(x_{j}-x_{j}^{\star}),x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\rangle\\
=&\sum_{j\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{i}}\tilde{w}_{ij}\langle x_{j}-x_{j}^{\star},x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\rangle\\
\leq&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\tilde{w}_{ij}(\|x_{j}-x_{j}^{\star}\|^{2}+\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\|^{2})\\
&+\frac{\tilde{w}_{ii}}{2}(\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\|^{2}+\|x_{i}-x_{i}^{\star}\|^{2}-\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\|^{2})\\
\leq&\frac{1}{2}((\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\|_{\infty}^{b})^{2}+\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\|^{2}-\tilde{w}_{ii}\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}\|^{2}).\end{split}

Substituting the above two equations into (
91
) yields

\begin{split}&\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\|^{2}\leq(\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\|_{\infty}^{b})^{2}-\mu_{i}\gamma_{i}\alpha\|x_{i}^{+}-x_{i}^{\star}\|^{2},\end{split}

which, together with
\gamma_{i}\geq\gamma_{i}^{\min}
, leads to (
89
) with
\rho=\sqrt{\frac{1}{1+\alpha\min_{i\in\mathcal{V}}(\gamma_{i}^{\min}\mu_{i})}}
. With (
89
) in hand and following the procedure of proving Theorem
1
and (
10
), we obtain the results in Theorem
3
.

-H

Proof of Lemma
3

DGD-ATC (
21
) can be viewed as a weighted gradient method
[
49
, Section 1.2.1]
with weight matrix
\mathbf{W}
for solving

\underset{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{nd}}{\operatorname{minimize}}~f(\mathbf{x})+\frac{\mathbf{x}^{T}(\mathbf{W}^{-1}-I)\mathbf{x}}{2\alpha}.

(93)

Therefore, the existence of fixed point to DGD-ATC can be guaranteed by the non-emptiness of the optimal solution set of problem (
93
).

-H
1
Non-empty solution set of (
93
)

Let
z^{\star}
be an optimum to (
1
). Define
\mathbf{z}^{\star}=\mathbf{1}_{n}\otimes z^{\star}
,
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})=F(\mathbf{x})+\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{W}^{-1}-I}^{2}}{2\alpha}
, and

\mathcal{C}=\{\mathbf{x}:\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})\leq\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{z}^{\star})\}.

Since
\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{nd}}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})
is equivalent to
\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})
, the minimum of
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
exists if the optimum of the later problem exists which holds true if
\mathcal{C}
is non-empty and compact. Clearly,
\mathcal{C}
is non-empty since
\mathbf{z}^{\star}\in\mathcal{C}
.

Below, we prove that
\mathcal{C}
is compact. Let
\mathbf{x}
be an arbitrary vector in
\mathcal{C}
and denote
\bar{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{1}_{n}\otimes\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}}x_{i}
. By the
L
-smoothness of
f
,

\begin{split}f(\bar{\mathbf{x}})-f(\mathbf{x})&\leq\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}),\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}\|^{2}\\
&\leq\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\|\cdot\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}\|+\frac{L}{2}\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(94)

By Assumption
3
and
h_{i}\equiv 0
, each
f_{i}
is bounded from the below, and we let
\underline{f}
be a lower bound for all
f_{i}
’s. Then, by the
L_{i}
-smoothness of
f_{i}
,

\underline{f}\leq f_{i}(x_{i}-\frac{1}{L_{i}}\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}))\leq f_{i}(x_{i})-\frac{1}{2L_{i}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x_{i})\|^{2},

so that

\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}\leq 2L(f(\mathbf{x})-n\underline{f})\leq 2L(f^{\star}-n\underline{f})

(95)

where the last step is due to
f(\mathbf{x})\leq\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})\leq\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{z}^{\star})=f^{\star}
. In addition, because
f(\mathbf{x})\geq n\underline{f}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})\leq f^{\star}
, we have

\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{W}^{-1}-I}^{2}=2\alpha(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})-f(\mathbf{x}))\leq 2\alpha(f^{\star}-n\underline{f}).

(96)

Since
\mathcal{G}
is connected, we have
\operatorname{Range}(\mathbf{W}^{-1}-I)=\{\mathbf{x}:x_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}=\mathbf{0}\}
, so that
\mathbf{x}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}
is the projection of
\mathbf{x}
onto
\operatorname{Range}(\mathbf{W}^{-1}-I)
. Moreover, because
W\succ\mathbf{0}
, we have
\beta=\lambda_{2}(W)
, so that the minimum non-zero eigenvalue of
\mathbf{W}^{-1}-I
is
\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}(W)}-1=\frac{1}{\beta}-1=\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}
. Therefore,

\|\mathbf{x}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\beta\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{W}^{-1}-I}^{2}}{1-\beta},

which, together with (
96
) and
\beta\leq 1
, yields

\|\mathbf{x}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2\alpha(f^{\star}-n\underline{f})}{1-\beta}.

(97)

Substituting (
95
) and (
97
) into (
94
) and using
f(\mathbf{x})\leq f^{\star}
gives

f(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq C_{0}:=f^{\star}+\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha L}{1-\beta}}+\frac{\alpha L}{1-\beta}\right)(f^{\star}-h),

or equivalently,
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}}f_{i}(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}}x_{i})\leq C_{0}
. Moreover, by
[
49
, Proposition B.9]
and the bounded optimum set of problem (
1
), every level set of
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}}f_{i}(x)
is bounded and, therefore, the following set is compact:

\displaystyle\bigg\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}}f_{i}(y)\leq C_{0}\bigg\}.

(98)

Due to the arbitrariness of
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}
, we have that for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}
,
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
belongs to the compact set (
98
) and (
97
) holds. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}
is compact.

Concluding all the above, the optimal solution set of problem (
93
) is non-empty.

-H
2
Optimality gap

Because
\mathbf{x}^{\star}
is a fixed point of (
21
),

\mathbf{x}^{\star}=\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{x}^{\star}-\alpha\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})),

(99)

which, together with
I\succeq\mathbf{W}\succ\mathbf{0}
, yields

\begin{split}\|(I-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{x}^{\star}\|=\alpha\|\mathbf{W}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})\|\leq&\alpha\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})\|.\end{split}

(100)

Moreover, because
\mathbf{x}^{\star}\in\mathcal{C}
, (
95
) with
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}^{\star}
holds, i.e.,

\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})\|^{2}\leq 2L(f^{\star}-n\underline{f}).

(101)

By (
40
), (
100
), and (
101
), we obtain

\|\mathbf{x}^{\star}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{\star}\|\leq\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}\sqrt{2L(f^{\star}-n\underline{f})},

(102)

i.e., (
5
) holds.

Next, we prove (
6
). Since
\mathbf{z}^{\star}
is the minimum of
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
,

f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})\leq\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}^{\star})\leq\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{z}^{\star})=f^{\star}.

(103)

By letting
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}^{\star}

\begin{split}f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{\star})\leq&f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})+\left(\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}+\frac{L\alpha^{2}}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\right)\cdot 2L(f^{\star}-n\underline{f}),\end{split}

which, together with (

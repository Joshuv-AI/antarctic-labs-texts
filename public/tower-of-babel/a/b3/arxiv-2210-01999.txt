Locally finite infinity-modules and weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over differential graded Lie algebras Thanks: Research partially supported by NSFC grants 12071241 (Chen), 11971282 (Qiao), 11901221 (Xiang), and 11961049 (Zhang).
Zhuo Chen, Yu Qiao*, Maosong Xiang, Tao Zhang

Abstract
. Motivated by recent developments of ∞ \infty -categorical theories related to differential graded (dg for short) Lie algebras, we develop a general framework for locally finite ∞ \infty - 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -modules over a dg Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . We show that the category of such locally finite ∞ \infty - 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -modules is almost a model category in the sense of Vallette. As a homotopy theoretical generalization of Loday and Pirashvili’s Lie algebra objects in the tensor category of linear maps, we further study weak Loday-Pirashvili modules consisting of ∞ \infty -morphisms from locally finite ∞ \infty - 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -modules to the adjoint module 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . From the category of such weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , we find a functor that maps to the category of Leibniz ∞ algebras enriched over the Chevalley-Eilenberg dg algebra of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . This functor can be regarded as the homotopy lifting of Loday and Pirashvili’s original method to realize Leibniz algebras from Lie algebra objects in the category of linear maps.

Another important source of inspiration is the close connection between Leibniz algebras and Lie algebras revealed by the works of Loday and Pirashvili
[
30
,
31
]
. Here is a quick sketch —
The notion of Leibniz algebras (also known as Loday algebras) is a non-commutative generalization of Lie algebras (cf.
[
32
]
). In
[
30
]
, Loday and Pirashvili constructed and studied the universal enveloping algebra functor
UL
from the category of Leibniz algebras to that of associative algebras.
Unlike the universal enveloping algebra functor
U
from the category of Lie algebras to that of associative algebras, the functor
UL
does not factor through the category of cocommutative Hopf algebras.
In order to understand the factorization nature of Leibniz algebras, Loday and Pirashvili introduced in
[
31
]
the tensor category
\mathcal{LM}
of linear maps. They proved that there exists an adjunction between the category of Leibniz algebras and the category of Lie algebra objects in
\mathcal{LM}
, such that the functor
UL
factors through the category of Hopf objects in
\mathcal{LM}
.
Moreover, unravelling a Lie algebra object in
\mathcal{LM}
yields a morphism from a Lie algebra module to the Lie algebra itself viewed as the adjoint module. In this article and in our previous work
[
11
]
, we refer to such a morphism as a
Loday-Pirashvili module
.
The adjunction between the category of Leibniz algebras and that of Lie algebra objects in
\mathcal{LM}
, can be enhanced to the dg setting. That is, there exists an adjunction between the category of dg Leibniz algebras and the category of morphisms of modules of a dg Lie algebra with target being the adjoint module (see Section
3.1
).
We call the latter one the category of Loday-Pirashvili modules (over a given dg Lie algebra), and denote it by
{\mathbf{LP}}
. Objects in
{{\mathbf{LP}}}
can be viewed as adjoint module points in the category of ordinary dg Lie algebra modules in the sense of functor of points due to Grothendieck.

Note that the adjoint module
\mathfrak{g}
is a particular locally finite
\infty
-
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
Inspired by Loday and Pirashvili’s foundational minds, as reviewed earlier, we focus on “adjoint-points” in the category
\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}
. Such a “point” is just an
\infty
-morphism
f\colon M\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
from a locally finite
\infty
-
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
to the adjoint module
\mathfrak{g}
, and we call it a
weak Loday-Pirashvili module
. To expand,
f
consists of a family of maps
\{f_{k}\colon M\to C^{k}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})\}_{k\geqslant 0}
satisfying certain conditions (see Definition
3.9
).
The collection of such
\infty
-morphisms forms a category, which we call the category of
weak Loday-Pirashvili modules
and denote by
{\mathbf{wLP}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
.
Two weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
are said to be homotopic if they are homotopic as
\infty
-morphisms of locally finite
\infty
-
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.

It is worth pointing out that weak Loday-Pirashvili modules have a geometric characterization in formal dg geometry. In fact, each dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
can be viewed as a (formal pointed) dg manifold
B\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g}[1],d_{\mathrm{CE}})

[
38
]
, whose space of functions
C^{\infty}(B\mathfrak{g})
is naturally identified with the commutative dg algebra
C(\mathfrak{g})
. Moreover, we have the following facts.

(1)

The category
\mathbf{fgsMod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}
of finitely generated and semifree
C(\mathfrak{g})
-modules is equivalent to the category
\mathbf{dgVec}_{B\mathfrak{g}}
of dg vector bundles over
B\mathfrak{g}
;

(2)

The semifree
C(\mathfrak{g})
-modules
C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is identified with the space of global sections of the shifted tangent bundle
T_{[-1]}B\mathfrak{g}
of the dg manifold
B\mathfrak{g}
; and

(3)

The category
{\mathbf{wLP}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
is equivalent to the category
\left(\mathbf{dgVec}_{B\mathfrak{g}}\right)_{C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})}
of dg vector bundles of
B\mathfrak{g}
over its shifted tangent bundle
T_{[-1]}B\mathfrak{g}
(see Proposition
3.16

As the second main result of this paper, we find that Leibniz
∞
algebras show up naturally from the category
\mathbf{wLP}_{\mathfrak{g}}
of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
. Here the notion of Leibniz

The category of Leibniz
∞

algebras first introduced by Lada and Stasheff
[
28

(also known as strongly homotopy Lie algebras), e.g., the minimal model theorem proved by Ammar and Poncin

[

Our point is to show an interesting mechanism of how to obtain Leibniz

we find a canonical Leibniz

)
.

Given a dg Lie algebra

Moreover,

1.
Locally finite infinity-modules over dg Lie algebras

1.1.
Dg Lie algebras and Chevalley-Eilenberg functors

We start with some basic knowledge of dg Lie algebras.

Definition 1.1

(
[
33
]
)
.

A
dg Lie algebra
over
\mathbb{K}
is a triple
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},d,[-,-])
where
(\mathfrak{g},d)
forms a cochain complex of vector spaces, and
[-,-]\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a degree
0
bilinear map called the graded Lie bracket, satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

For all
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{p},y\in\mathfrak{g}^{q}
, we have
[x,y]=-(-1)^{pq}[y,x]
;

(2)

For all
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{p},y\in\mathfrak{g}^{q},z\in\mathfrak{g}^{r}
, we have

(-1)^{pr}[x,[y,z]]+(-1)^{qp}[y,[z,x]]+(-1)^{rq}[z,[x,y]]=0;

(3)

The differential
d
is a derivation with respect to the graded Lie bracket
[-,-]
, i.e., for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{p},y\in\mathfrak{g}^{q}
, we have

d[x,y]=[dx,y]+(-1)^{p}[x,dy].

A morphism of dg Lie algebras from
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},d,[-,-])
to
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},d^{\prime},[-,-]^{\prime})
is a map of cochain complexes
f\colon(\mathfrak{g},d)\to(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},d^{\prime})
such that
f([x,y])=[f(x),f(y)]^{\prime}
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.
The collection of all dg Lie algebras and their morphisms forms a category, denoted by
\mathbf{dgLie}
.

Given a dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the cohomology of the underlying cochain complex
(\mathfrak{g},d)
, called the
tangent cohomology
and denote by
H_{\mathrm{tan}}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
, when equipped with the induced bracket
[-,-]
, is a graded Lie algebra. Each morphism
f\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
of dg Lie algebras induces a morphism of graded Lie algebras
H_{\mathrm{tan}}(f)\colon(H_{\mathrm{tan}}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}),[-,-])\to(H_{\mathrm{tan}}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}),[-,-]^{\prime})
. Hence,
H_{\mathrm{tan}}
is indeed a functor from
\mathbf{dgLie}
to
\mathbf{gLie}
, the category of graded Lie algebras.

The
Chevalley-Eilenberg dg algebra

C(\mathfrak{g})
of a dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is defined as follows:

(1)

The underlying space is the

(2)

, is the sum

, where

is induced from the dual of the differential

of the complex

, and

is induced from the graded Lie bracket

by

Note that a morphism of dg Lie algebras

corresponds to a degree

.
Thus, we obtain a functor

called the Chevalley-Eilenberg functor, from the category
\mathbf{dgLie}
of dg Lie algebras to the category
{\mathbf{dgAlg}}
of dg algebras.

Lemma 2.11

Proof.

Since

p+q=k+1\end{subarray}}\Phi_{0}^{-1}\Phi_{q}\Psi_{p},

where

\infty

3.
Weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over dg Lie algebras

3.1.
Loday-Pirashvili modules and dg Leibniz algebras

In
[
31
]
, Loday and Pirashvili showed that there exists a pair of adjoint functors between the category of Leibniz algebras and that of Lie algebra objects in the category
\mathcal{LM}
of linear maps.
It is also proved
loc.cit.
that each Lie algebra object in the category
\mathcal{LM}
is equivalent to a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules to the adjoint module for some ordinary Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we call such a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules a
Loday-Pirashvili module
. We first generalize this notion in the dg Lie algebra setting as follows:

Definition 3.1
.

A
Loday-Pirashvili module
consists of a dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
, and a morphism
f\colon M\to\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.
A morphism
\Phi=(\underline{\phi},\phi)
of Loday-Pirashvili modules from
f\colon M\to\mathfrak{g}
to
f^{\prime}\colon{M^{\prime}}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is specified by the following commutative diagram in
{\mathbf{Ch}}(\mathbb{K})
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\phi}}

such that

•

\underline{\phi}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is a morphism of dg Lie algebras;

•

\phi\colon M\to{M^{\prime}}
is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules covering
\underline{\phi}
, that is,

\phi(\rho_{\mathfrak{g},M}(x;m))=\rho_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},{M^{\prime}}}(\underline{\phi}(x);\phi(m)),

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},m\in M
.

We denote by
{\mathbf{LP}}
the category of Loday-Pirashvili modules.

Given a dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the category
{{\mathbf{LP}}}
admits a subcategory
{\mathbf{LP}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
consisting of Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
, together with morphisms specified by commutative triangles in the category

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}

In the language of functor of points, the category

(“adjoint-points” for short) in the category

-modules.

Definition 3.2
.

A
dg Leibniz algebra

L
over a dg algebra

is a Leibniz algebra in the category of
\mathscr{A}
-modules, i.e., an
\mathscr{A}
-module
(L,d)
together with a morphism of
\mathscr{A}

, called the Leibniz bracket, which is subject to the (left) Leibniz rule:

x\diamond(y\diamond z)=(x\diamond y)\diamond z+(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}y\diamond(x\diamond z),\quad\forall~x,y,z\in L.

\mathscr{A}
from

to

is a morphism of dg
\mathscr{A}
-modules

such that

for all

.

We denote by
\mathbf{dgLeib}_{\mathscr{A}}
the category of dg Leibniz algebras over
\mathscr{A}
.
In particular, when the dg algebra
\mathscr{A}
is the base field
\mathbb{K}
with zero differential, we obtain the category
\mathbf{dgLeib}_{\mathbb{K}}
of dg Leibniz algebras over
\mathbb{K}
. For simplicity,
\mathbf{dgLeib}_{\mathbb{K}}
is written as
\mathbf{dgLeib}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Given a Loday-Pirashvili module

, there exists a dg Leibniz algebra structure (over
\mathbb{K}
) on the cochain complex

defined by

.

This proposition is clearly a generalization of the original construction of Leibniz algebras in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\operatorname{Lie}}}

When we concern with the restriction of

in the category

of

-modules.
Hence, the Chevalley-Eilenberg functor

induces a functor which we write again as

Combining with the restricted functor
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{g}}
in (

), we obtain the following functor

\displaystyle(f\colon M\to\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle\mapsto(C(\mathfrak{g},M),d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{M},\diamond),

where the Leibniz bracket

) by

-bilinear extensions. The definition of
\mathcal{G}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}
on morphisms in
{\mathbf{LP}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
is apparent.

It is natural to ask if all dg Leibniz algebras

over

arise from those

), the morphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbf{Mod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})})_{C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathfrak{g},-)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{inclusion}}

In fact, objects in the category

corresponds to “adjoint-points” in the larger category

of locally finite
\infty
-
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. Such “adjoint-points” are called weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over

, which is explained in the subsequent section.

3.2.
Weak Loday-Pirashvili modules

Definition 3.9
.

A
weak Loday-Pirashvili module
over a dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a locally finite
\infty
-
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M=(M,d^{M}_{\mathrm{tot}})
together with an
\infty
-morphism
f\colon M\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
from
M
to the adjoint module of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e., a collection of linear maps

f_{k}\colon M\to C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\quad k\geqslant 0,

subject to the following constraints:

(1)

(Local finiteness) For all
m\in M
, there exists a sufficiently large integer
N_{m}
such that
f_{k}(m)=0
for all
k\geqslant N_{m}
;

(2)

The map
F=\operatorname{id}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}\otimes\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}

\colon C(\mathfrak{g},M)\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
intertwines the relevant differentials, i.e.,

F\circ d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{M}=d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\circ F.

Here
d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{M}=d_{\mathrm{CE}}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_{k}^{M}
is the total differential on
M
as in (
1.5
), and
d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathfrak{g}}
is the one in Equation (
1.3
).

We denote such a weak Loday-Pirashvili module over
\mathfrak{g}
by
(f\colon M\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g})
.
Passing to the tangent cohomology, it is easy to see that
H_{\mathrm{tan}}(f_{0})\colon H_{\mathrm{tan}}^{\bullet}(M)\to H_{\mathrm{tan}}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
is a Loday-Pirashvili module, i.e., a morphism of
H_{\mathrm{tan}}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules.

The notion of a dg Loday-Pirashvili module introduced in
[
11
]
, is a special kind of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules.

Example 3.10
.

Let
\alpha\in Z^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
be a Chevalley-Eilenberg
2
-cocycle of total degree
k
, in other words,
\alpha\in(S^{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[-1]))^{k}
is
d_{\mathrm{CE}}
-closed.
Consider the tensor product
M:=\mathfrak{g}[1-k]\otimes\mathfrak{g}
of the adjoint module
\mathfrak{g}
with a shifted copy
\mathfrak{g}[1-k]
. Define a linear map

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle\colon\mathfrak{g}[1-k]\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to C^{1}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle f_{1}(y[1-k]\otimes z)

\displaystyle:=\iota_{y[1]}\alpha\otimes z

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. It follows from direct verification that
f_{1}
defines a weak Loday-Pirashvili module
\mathfrak{g}[1-k]\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 3.11
.

By a dg Lie algebra pair
(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{g})
, we mean that both
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},d^{\mathfrak{g}},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
\mathfrak{L}=(\mathfrak{L},d^{\mathfrak{L}},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{L}})
are finite dimensional dg Lie algebras and
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{L}
is a dg Lie subalgebra.
Let us denote the quotient complex by
({\mathfrak{B}},d^{\mathfrak{B}})
. Both cochain complexes
(\mathfrak{L},d^{\mathfrak{L}})
and
({\mathfrak{B}},d^{\mathfrak{B}})
admit
\mathfrak{g}
-module structures, and they are defined by

\displaystyle x\triangleright z

\displaystyle:=[x,z]_{\mathfrak{L}},

and

\displaystyle x\triangleright\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{B}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{B}}([x,z]_{\mathfrak{L}}),

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},z\in\mathfrak{L}
, respectively,.
We also have a short exact sequence of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

(3.12)

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{i}\mathfrak{L}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\mathfrak{B}}\rightarrow 0.

Choose a splitting of Sequence (
3.12
) as graded vector spaces, i.e., a pair of linear maps
j\colon{\mathfrak{B}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}
and
\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathfrak{L}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

\displaystyle\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ i

\displaystyle=\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}},

\displaystyle\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{B}}\circ j

\displaystyle=\operatorname{id}_{{\mathfrak{B}}},

\displaystyle\mbox{ and }~i\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}+j\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{B}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{L}}.

Via the splitting
(j,\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}})
, we have a decomposition
\mathfrak{L}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{B}
of graded vector spaces and a decomposition
\mathfrak{L}^{\vee}=\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\oplus\mathfrak{B}^{\vee}
of dual spaces as well.

The failure of
j
being a cochain map determines a linear map

f_{0}\colon\mathfrak{B}[-1]\to\mathfrak{g},

which is defined for all homogeneous element
b[-1]\in\mathfrak{B}[-1]
by

f_{0}(b[-1])=(-1)^{\lvert b\rvert}d(j)(b):=(-1)^{\lvert b\rvert}\left(d^{\mathfrak{L}}(j(b))-j(d^{\mathfrak{B}}(b))\right).

It is clear that
f_{0}
satisfies

d^{\mathfrak{g}}\circ f_{0}-f_{0}\circ d^{\mathfrak{B}}=0\colon\mathfrak{B}[-1]\to\mathfrak{g}[1].

The
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
\mathfrak{L}
can be described by

d^{\mathfrak{L}}_{1}l=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[-1]\otimes\mathfrak{L}}(d^{\mathrm{ad}}l)=(i^{\ast}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{L}})(d^{\mathrm{ad}}l),\quad\forall~l\in\mathfrak{L},

where
d^{\mathrm{ad}}\colon\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}^{\vee}[-1]\otimes\mathfrak{L}
stands for the adjoint
\mathfrak{L}
-module structure of
\mathfrak{L}
.
Similarly, the
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
\mathfrak{B}
can be described by

d^{\mathfrak{B}}_{1}b=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[-1]\otimes\mathfrak{B}}(d^{\mathrm{ad}}b)=(i^{\ast}\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{B}})(d^{\mathrm{ad}}b),\quad\forall~b\in\mathfrak{B}.

Consider the linear map

f_{1}\colon\mathfrak{B}[-1]\to C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[-1]\otimes\mathfrak{g},

which is defined for all
b[-1]\in{\mathfrak{B}[-1]}
by

f_{1}(b[-1])=(-1)^{\lvert b\rvert}\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[-1]\otimes\mathfrak{g}}(d^{\mathrm{ad}}b)=(-1)^{\lvert b\rvert}(i^{\ast}\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}})(d^{\mathrm{ad}}b).

It follows from a direct verification that
f=f_{0}+f_{1}
yields a weak Loday-Pirashvili module
{\mathfrak{B}}[-1]\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 3.13
.

It is natural to ask when we choose two cohomologous cocycles
\alpha
and

in Example

, what is the relationship between the resulting weak Loday-Pirashvili modules

and

;
We also wish to see how the weak Loday-Pirashvili module in Example

depends on the choice of splitting

of Sequence (

). These questions are dealt with in Examples

and

of the subsequent section, where we construct a homotopy theory for weak Loday-Pirashvili modules.

Definition 3.14
.

A
morphism
of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
from
(f\colon M\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g})
to

is an

-morphism of locally finite
\infty
-

-modules
\phi\colon M\rightsquigarrow N
such that the following triangle is commutative in the category

:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

Compositions of morphisms of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
are defined in an obvious manner.
With the descriptions provided in Definitions
3.9
and
3.14
, it follows that weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
are naturally gathered in a category which we denote by
{\mathbf{wLP}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
. In other words, it is the category consisting of “adjoint-points” in the category
\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}
of locally finite
\infty
-
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

\mathbf{wLP}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\{\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}(M,\mathfrak{g})\mid M\in\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}\}.

One can also consider the category
\mathbf{wLP}
of
weak Loday-Pirashvili modules without specifying the dg Lie algebra.
We defer the discussion of related content to Section
4.2
.

Applying Proposition
1.9
, we immediately obtain the following fact.

Proposition 3.15
.

The Chevalley-Eilenberg functor
C(\mathfrak{g},-)\colon\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}
induces an equivalence of categories

C(\mathfrak{g},-)\colon{\mathbf{wLP}_{\mathfrak{g}}}\xrightarrow{~\simeq~}\left(\mathbf{fgsMod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}\right)_{C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})}.

Here
\left(\mathbf{fgsMod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}\right)_{C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})}
stands for the category of finitely generated and semifree
C(\mathfrak{g})
-modules over
C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
.

3.3.
Weak Loday-Pirashvili modules in terms of dg geometry

From the dg geometry point of view, a dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a (formal) pointed dg manifold
B\mathfrak{g}:=(\mathfrak{g}[1],d_{\mathrm{CE}})
, whose space of functions
C^{\infty}(B\mathfrak{g})
is naturally identified with the Chevalley-Eilenberg dg algebra
C(\mathfrak{g})
.
Now we give an equivalent characterization of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
(via formal geometry) in terms of the dg manifold
B\mathfrak{g}
.

Denote the category of dg vector bundles over the dg manifold
B\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathbf{dgVec}_{B\mathfrak{g}}
. According to Mehta, Stiénon, and Xu
[
38
]
*Lemma 1.5, this category is equivalent to the category
\mathbf{fgsMod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}
of finitely generated semifree
C(\mathfrak{g})
-modules.
Consider the essentially surjective and fully faithful functor obtained by taking global sections

\Gamma\colon\mathbf{dgVec}_{B\mathfrak{g}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbf{fgsMod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})},

i.e., given a dg vector bundle
\mathcal{E}\to B\mathfrak{g}
, its space
\Gamma(\mathcal{E})
of global sections is a semifree
C(\mathfrak{g})
-module.
Meanwhile, by Proposition
1.9
,
C(\mathfrak{g},-)\colon\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbf{fgsMod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}
is an equivalence of categories. Therefore, we have an equivalence of categories

\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}\simeq\mathbf{dgVec}_{B\mathfrak{g}}.

Thus, given a locally finite
\infty
-
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
, there exists a dg vector bundle
\mathcal{E}\to B\mathfrak{g}
, whose space of global sections is naturally identified with the semifree
C(\mathfrak{g})
-module
C(\mathfrak{g},M)
.
Moreover, since the global section space
\Gamma(T_{[-1]}B\mathfrak{g})
of the shifted tangent bundle
T_{[-1]}B\mathfrak{g}

\to

B\mathfrak{g}
can be naturally identified with the
C(\mathfrak{g})
-module
C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, combining with Proposition
3.15
, we immediately obtain the following fact.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a dg Lie algebra. There is a one-to-one correspondence between weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
and morphisms in the category
\mathbf{dgVec}_{B\mathfrak{g}}
targeting at the shifted tangent bundle
T_{[-1]}B\mathfrak{g}
.

Dually, we have a characterization of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
by the cotangent complex of
B\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 3.17
.

Let
\mathcal{U}\in\mathbf{Mod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}
be a
C(\mathfrak{g})
-module. A
\mathcal{U}
-valued dg derivation of
C(\mathfrak{g})
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear cochain map
\delta:C(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
satisfying the Leibniz rule

\delta(\xi\odot\eta)=(-1)^{\lvert\xi\rvert\lvert\eta\rvert}\eta\odot\delta(\xi)+\xi\odot\delta(\eta),\;\;\forall~\xi,\eta\in C(\mathfrak{g}).

Remark 3.18
.

The trivial square zero extension of
C(\mathfrak{g})
by
\mathcal{U}
is an augmented
C(\mathfrak{g})
-algebra fitting into the following natural morphisms:

\displaystyle C(\mathfrak{g})\xrightarrow{i}

\displaystyle C(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathcal{U}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{pr}_{1}}C(\mathfrak{g}).

Each section of the projection
\operatorname{pr}_{1}:C(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathcal{U}\to C(\mathfrak{g})
gives rise to a
\mathcal{U}
-valued dg derivation of
C(\mathfrak{g})
, and vice versa.

Let
\operatorname{Der}(C(\mathfrak{g}),\mathcal{U})
be the set of
\mathcal{U}
-valued dg derivations of
C(\mathfrak{g})
, and it admits an obvious
C(\mathfrak{g})
-module structure. Meanwhile, each morphism
\Phi\colon\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{M}
of
C(\mathfrak{g})
-modules induces a morphism
\Phi\circ-\colon\operatorname{Der}(C(\mathfrak{g}),\mathcal{U})\to\operatorname{Der}(C(\mathfrak{g}),\mathcal{M})
of dg derivations of
C(\mathfrak{g})
defined by compositions. Hence, we obtain a functor
\operatorname{Der}(C(\mathfrak{g}),-)
from the category
\mathbf{Mod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}
of
C(\mathfrak{g})
-modules to itself.
It is well-known that this functor is corepresented by the
C(\mathfrak{g})
-module
\Omega_{C(\mathfrak{g})\mid\mathbb{K}}
of Kähler differentials in the following sense:

\operatorname{Der}(C(\mathfrak{g}),-)\cong\mathbf{Mod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}(\Omega_{C(\mathfrak{g})\mid\mathbb{K}},-)\colon~\mathbf{Mod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}.

For this reason,
\Omega_{C(\mathfrak{g})\mid\mathbb{K}}
is also called the cotangent complex of the dg manifold
B\mathfrak{g}
.

Given a locally finite
\infty
-
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
, let
M^{\vee}[-1]\in\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}
be the dual object with shifted degrees. There exists a one-to-one correspondence between weak Loday-Pirashvili modules
f\colon M\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
and
C(\mathfrak{g},M^{\vee}[-1])
-valued dg derivations
\delta
of
C(\mathfrak{g})
.
In fact, if
f=\sum_{k}f_{k}
, where
f_{k}\colon M\to C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, is a weak Loday-Pirashvili module over
\mathfrak{g}
, then its linear dual map
f^{\vee}=\sum_{k}(f_{k}^{\vee}\colon\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[-1]\to C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},M^{\vee}[-1]))
determines a morphism
F^{\vee}\colon\Omega_{C(\mathfrak{g})\mid\mathbb{K}}\to C(\mathfrak{g},M^{\vee}[-1])
of
C(\mathfrak{g})
-modules, and gives rise to a
C(\mathfrak{g},M^{\vee}[-1])
-valued dg derivation
\delta
of
C(\mathfrak{g})
.
As a consequence, we obtain the following characterization of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 3.19
.

The category
{\mathbf{wLP}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
is equivalent to the category
\operatorname{Der}(C(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbf{fgsMod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})})
of dg derivations of
C(\mathfrak{g})
valued in finitely generated and semifree dg
C(\mathfrak{g})
-modules.

3.4.
A homotopy theory for weak Loday-Pirashvili modules

Since weak Loday-Pirashvili modules are defined to be “adjoint-points” in the category
\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}
,
they inherit a homotopy equivalence relation as well.

Definition 3.20
.

Two weak Loday-Pirashvili modules
f,f^{\prime}\colon M\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
over a dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
are said to be
homotopic
if they are homotopic as
\infty
-morphisms in the category
\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}
, i.e., there exists an
\infty
-morphism
H\colon M\rightsquigarrow J(\mathfrak{g})
which we call a homotopy from
f
to
f^{\prime}
, such that the following diagram in
\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}
commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J(\mathfrak{g})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overset{{f}}{\overset{~\overset{~}{~}}{~\overset{~}{~}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{1}}

For each weak Loday-Pirashvili module
(f\colon M\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g})

\in{\mathbf{wLP}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
, we will denote its homotopy class by
[f\colon M\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}]
, or simply
[f]
.
Morphisms between homotopy classes of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules are defined in a natural fashion:
Given two weak Loday-Pirashvili module classes
[f]=[f\colon M\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}]
and
[g]=[g\colon N\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}]
, a morphism
\phi
from
[f]
to
[g]
is an
\infty
-morphism
\phi\colon M\rightsquigarrow N
such that the triangle in Definition
3.14
commutes in the homotopy category
\operatorname{Ho}(\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty})(\simeq~\mathbf{lfMod}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\infty}{/_{\sim}})
, i.e.,
[f]=[g\circ\phi]
.
So we can form the category
\operatorname{Ho}(\mathbf{wLP}_{\mathfrak{g}})
of
homotopy classes
of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
.

Similarly, we can define the homotopy category

\operatorname{Ho}(\operatorname{Der}(C(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbf{fgsMod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})}))
whose objects are
homotopy classes
of dg derivations of
C(\mathfrak{g})
valued in finitely generated and semifree
C(\mathfrak{g})
-modules. From Propositions
3.19
and
2.10
, we get an important fact.

Proposition 3.21
.

There is an equivalence of categories

\operatorname{Ho}(\mathbf{wLP}_{\mathfrak{g}})\simeq\operatorname{Ho}(\operatorname{Der}(C(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbf{fgsMod}_{C(\mathfrak{g})})).

At the end of this section, we unfold Definition
3.20
to describe explicitly homotopy equivalence of weak Loday-Pirashvili modules.

Proposition 3.22
.

Two weak Loday-Pirashvili modules
f,f^{\prime}\colon M\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
are homotopic if and only if there exists a sequence of linear maps

h_{n}\colon M\to C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})[-1],\quad n=0,1,2,\cdots,

which is locally finite (similar to (1) of Definition
3.9
) and
such that the associated
C(\mathfrak{g})
-linear extensions
F
of
f
and
F^{\prime}
of
f^{\prime}
satisfy

F-F^{\prime}=d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\circ h+h\circ d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{M}\colon~~~C(\mathfrak{g},M)\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),

where
h=\sum_{n}h_{n}\colon C(\mathfrak{g},M)\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})[-1]
is
C(\mathfrak{g})
-linear.
We call the sequence of maps
\{h_{n}\}
an
\infty
-homotopy between maps
f
and
f^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Note that the path object for the adjoint module of
\mathfrak{g}
, namely

J(\mathfrak{g})=(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}t\oplus\mathfrak{g}[-1]dt,d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{J(\mathfrak{g})}=d_{0}^{J(\mathfrak{g})}+d_{1}^{J(\mathfrak{g})}),

is defined by

\displaystyle d_{0}^{J(\mathfrak{g})}(x+ty+(dt)z)

\displaystyle=d_{0}^{\mathfrak{g}}(x)+td_{0}^{\mathfrak{g}}(y)+dt(y-d_{0}^{\mathfrak{g}}(z)),

\displaystyle d_{1}^{J(\mathfrak{g})}(x+ty+(dt)z)

\displaystyle=d_{1}^{\mathfrak{g}}(x)+td_{1}^{\mathfrak{g}}(y)+dt(d_{1}^{\mathfrak{g}}(z)),

for all
x+ty+(dt)z\in\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}t\oplus\mathfrak{g}[-1]dt
.

Assume that
f
and
f^{\prime}
are homotopic via the right homotopy
H\colon M\rightsquigarrow J(\mathfrak{g})
. We show how to find the maps
h_{n}
.

By definition,
H
is a
C(\mathfrak{g})
-linear map

H=\sum_{n}H_{n}\colon M\to\oplus_{n}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},J(\mathfrak{g})),

satisfying

H\circ d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{M}=d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{J(\mathfrak{g})}\circ H\colon C(\mathfrak{g},M)\to C(\mathfrak{g},J(\mathfrak{g})).

Since
J(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}t+\mathfrak{g}[-1]dt
, we may write

, where

\displaystyle H_{n}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\colon M\to C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),

\displaystyle H_{n}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\colon M\to C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})t,

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\quad H_{n}^{(3)}

\displaystyle\colon M\to C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})[-1]dt.

Since
\epsilon_{0}\circ H=f
and
\epsilon_{1}\circ H=f^{\prime}
, it follows that

\displaystyle H_{n}^{(1)}=f,

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{and}\quad H_{n}^{(2)}=f^{\prime}-f.

Accordingly, we can define a sequence of multilinear maps

(for all

) by

H_{n}^{(3)}(m)=h_{n}(m)dt.

The resulting

is subject to the constraint stated by the proposition. The converse argument is achieved similarly, and thus omitted.
∎

Indeed, this theorem is a direct application of the Kapranov functor that we introduced in

*Theorem 3.8.
The proof is a straightforward adaptation of the argument

Firstly, we show that for each weak Loday-Pirashvili module

5.
Leibniz infinity algebra structures via rooted trees

This section is an addition to the previous one, so we inherit all the earlier symbols and conventions. Before introducing the main result, we need to recall some basic terminology about trees.

5.1.
Rooted trees with monotonic labelings

A directed graph
\Gamma
is a pair
({\mathrm{V}(\Gamma)},{\mathrm{E}(\Gamma)})
, where
{\mathrm{V}(\Gamma)}
, called the set of vertices, is a non-empty finite set, and
{\mathrm{E}(\Gamma)}
, called the set of edges, is a set of ordered pairs of elements of
{\mathrm{V}(\Gamma)}
.
A directed tree is a connected directed graph without cycles. The valency of a vertex
v
in a directed tree, denoted by
\lVert v\rVert
, is the number of edges
pointing to

v
. Note that our definition of valency is slightly different from that in
[
16
]
.

In what follows, we assume that
T
is a rooted tree (also known as a planted tree), i.e., a directed tree whose set
{\mathrm{V}(T)}
of vertices admits a distinguished element
v_{R}\in{\mathrm{V}(T)}
of valency
1
, called the
root vertex
, such that
T
is oriented toward
v_{R}
.
The edge adjacent to
v_{R}
is called the
root edge
. Vertices of valency
0
are called
leaves
(or tails). A vertex is called
internal
if it is neither a root nor a leaf. We use the symbols
{\mathrm{Int}(T)}
and
{\mathrm{Leaf}(T)}
to denote the sets of internal vertices and leaves, respectively.

Each non-root vertex
v
of
T
admits a unique edge initiating from
v
. Denote by
\mathrm{Next}(v)
the end vertex of this edge. There is a unique path
p_{v}
connecting
v
to the root vertex
v_{R}
.
Call the number of edges in this path the height of
v
, and denote it by
h_{v}
.
For the root vertex
v_{R}
, set
\mathrm{Next}(v_{R})=v_{R}
and
h_{v_{R}}=0
. Meanwhile, we can define a partial order
\prec
on the set
{\mathrm{V}(T)}
:
v\prec v^{\prime}
for any vertex
v^{\prime}
in
p_{v}\setminus\{v\}
.

We define the height
h(T)
of
T
to be the maximum of heights of all vertices of
T
, i.e.,
h(T)=\max\{h_{v}\mid v\in{\mathrm{V}(T)}\}
.
Assume that
h(T)
is
k+1
. We can decompose the set of vertices

\displaystyle{\mathrm{V}(T)}

\displaystyle=\sqcup_{j=0}^{k+1}{\mathrm{V}^{j}(T)},

where
{\mathrm{V}^{j}(T)}
consists of vertices of height
j
. Both
{\mathrm{V}^{0}(T)}=\{v_{R}\}
and
{\mathrm{V}^{1}(T)}=\{\mathrm{Next}^{-1}(v_{R})\}
are single-element sets, and
{\mathrm{V}^{k+1}(T)}
is a subset of
{\mathrm{Leaf}(T)}
.
We also have a decomposition of
{\mathrm{Int}(T)}
of internal vertices

{\mathrm{Int}(T)}=\sqcup_{j=1}^{k}{\mathrm{Int}^{j}(T)}:=\sqcup_{j=1}^{k}{\mathrm{V}^{j}(T)}\cap{\mathrm{Int}(T)}.

Let
\mathbf{RT}(n)
be the set of rooted trees with
n
non-root vertices. Those rooted trees with a particular type of labelling on vertices are what we concern.

Definition 5.1
.

A
monotonic labeling
on a rooted tree

is a bijection

l\colon{\mathrm{V}(T)}\setminus\{v_{R}\}\to\{1,2,\cdots,n\}

which preserves the partial order
\prec
, i.e.,
v_{1}\prec v_{2}
implies
l(v_{1})<l(v_{2})
.
The collection of monotonic labelings of a rooted tree

is denoted by
\mathbf{ML}(T)
.

Two monotonic labelings
l
and
l^{\prime}
on

are said to be equivalent, if there exists an automorphism
\sigma:{\mathrm{V}(T)}\to{\mathrm{V}(T)}
of the set
{\mathrm{V}(T)}
of vertices satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

(
\mathrm{Next}
-compatibility)
\sigma(\mathrm{Next}(v))=\mathrm{Next}(\sigma(v))
for all
v\in{\mathrm{V}(T)}
;

(2)

(Labelling transfer)
l^{\prime}(v)=l(\sigma(v))
for all
v\in{\mathrm{V}(T)}\setminus\{v_{R}\}
.

Denote by
[\mathbf{ML}(T)]
the equivalence classes of monotonic labelings on
T
.

C(\mathfrak{g})
-linear extension
F_{0}
of
f_{0}
is weight-preserving, while the
C(\mathfrak{g})
-linear extension
F_{1}
of
f_{1}
raises weight by
1
.
Thus, for each
n\geqslant 2
, there exists only one monotonically labelled planar tree
T\in\mathbf{RT}(n)
satisfying
\lVert v\rVert=1
for all internal vertices
v\in{\mathrm{Int}(T)}
.
More precisely, this map
\Theta_{n}
is the
C(\mathfrak{g})
-linear extension of the following two linear maps

\Theta^{(0)}_{n}\colon\mathfrak{B}[-1]^{\otimes n}\to\mathfrak{g}[1-n],\qquad\Theta^{(1)}_{n}\colon\mathfrak{B}[-1]^{\otimes n}\to C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})[1-n],

defined respectively by

\Theta^{(0)}_{n}(\bar{z_{1}}[-1],\cdots,\bar{z_{n}}[-1])=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}[\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([\cdots\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([f_{0}(z_{1}),z_{2}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),\cdots]_{\mathfrak{L}}),z_{n-1}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),z_{n}]_{\mathfrak{L}},

and

\Theta^{(1)}_{n}(\bar{z_{1}}[-1],\cdots,\bar{z_{n}}[-1])(x[1])=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}[\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([\cdots\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([x,z_{1}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),z_{2}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),\cdots]_{\mathfrak{L}}),z_{n-1}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),z_{n}]_{\mathfrak{L}},

for all homogeneous
\bar{z_{i}}[-1]\in\mathfrak{B}[-1]
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Hence, the higher structure maps
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 2}
are the
C(\mathfrak{g})
-linear extension of the linear maps

\lambda_{n}^{(0)}\colon\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}\to\mathfrak{B}[1],\qquad\lambda_{n}^{(1)}\colon\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}\to C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{B})[1],

defined respectively by

\lambda^{(0)}_{n}(\bar{z_{1}},\cdots,\bar{z_{n}})=\operatorname{pr}_{{\mathfrak{L}}/\mathfrak{g}}([\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([\cdots\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([f_{0}(\bar{z_{1}}),z_{2}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),\cdots]_{\mathfrak{L}}),z_{n-1}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),z_{n}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),

and

\lambda^{(1)}_{n}(\bar{z_{1}},\cdots,\bar{z_{n}})(x)=\operatorname{pr}_{{\mathfrak{L}}/\mathfrak{g}}([\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([\cdots\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([x,z_{1}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),z_{2}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),\cdots]_{\mathfrak{L}}),z_{n-1}]_{\mathfrak{L}}),z_{n}]_{\mathfrak{L}}).

In particular, when
(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{g})
is a pair of Lie algebras, the component
\lambda^{(0)}_{n}
vanishes and the Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
algebra structure on
C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{B})
coincides with the Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
algebra obtained from Chen, Stiénon and Xu’s construction
[
12
]
for the Lie algebroid pair
({\mathfrak{L}},\mathfrak{g})
over the one-point space.

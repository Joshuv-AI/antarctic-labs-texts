Efficient simulation of Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill states with Gaussian circuits
Cameron Calcluth, Alessandro Ferraro, Giulia Ferrini

Abstract
We study the classical simulatability of Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) states in combination with arbitrary displacements, a large set of symplectic operations and homodyne measurements. For these types of circuits, neither continuous-variable theorems based on the non-negativity of quasi-probability distributions nor discrete-variable theorems such as the Gottesman-Knill theorem can be employed to assess the simulatability. We first develop a method to evaluate the probability density function corresponding to measuring a single GKP state in the position basis following arbitrary squeezing and a large set of rotations. This method involves evaluating a transformed Jacobi theta function using techniques from analytic number theory. We then use this result to identify two large classes of multimode circuits which are classically efficiently simulatable and are not contained by the GKP encoded Clifford group. Our results extend the set of circuits previously known to be classically efficiently simulatable.

In this work, we tackle the unexplored question: are GKP states in combination with arbitrary displacements, Gaussian gates and homodyne measurement universal, or classically efficiently simulatable?
For these types of circuits, neither continuous-variable theorems based on the non-negativity of quasi-probability distributions nor discrete-variable theorems such as the Gottesman-Knill theorem can be employed to assess the simulatability.

Here, we prove that a large class of such Gaussian circuits is classically efficiently simulatable. Furthermore, for single-mode measurements, simulation can be achieved in time which increases linearly with respect to the number of modes. Meanwhile, we extend the class of simulatable displacements from rational to all possible continuous displacements.

3
PDF of a Gaussian-transformed GKP state

The PDF with respect to position of a
0
-logical GKP state following a general single-mode Gaussian operation consists of a Gaussian operator applied to an infinite sum of Dirac delta peaks. The Gaussian operator will introduce non-trivial coefficients at each of these peaks. It is known that for a restricted set of Gaussian operations — e.g., those which can be described in terms of GKP-encoded single-qubit Clifford operations — it is possible to analytically evaluate the wave function, and therefore also the PDF of a transformed GKP state
[
2
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, no general result is known for arbitrary Gaussian operations.

In this section, we provide the evaluation of the PDF of a
0
-logical GKP state that has undergone a generic Gaussian transformation — which generally cannot be described in terms of GKP-encoded qubit Clifford operations — in the position basis. We note that we can decompose any single-mode symplectic operation using the Iwasawa decomposition
[
38
]
in terms of a shear operation followed by a squeezing operation and then a rotation. Furthermore, when measuring in the position basis, we can discard the initial shear operation, as it has no effect on the position quadrature. Therefore, the effect of all single-mode Gaussian operations acting on the position quadrature can be expressed in terms of a displacement, squeezing and a rotation. Since the displacement has the effect of a displacement of the position variable in the PDF, we can, without loss of generality, ignore the effect of displacement in the following calculation.

The wave function of the rotated and squeezed
0
-logical GKP state can be expressed in terms of these single-mode operations as

\displaystyle\psi_{\theta,s}(x)

\displaystyle=\bra{q=x}\hat{S}(s)\hat{R}(\theta)\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}.

(22)

Furthermore, we can isolate the squeezing parameter

, which has the effect of rescaling the wave function of a rotated 0-logical GKP state,

\displaystyle\psi_{\theta,s}(x)=\psi_{\theta}(x/s).

(23)

The full calculations detailed in Appendix
A.1
show that the PDF of a rotated 0-logical GKP state measured in the position basis can be written compactly as being proportional to

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\psi_{\theta}(x)}^{2}\propto

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\absolutevalue{\vartheta(\zeta=-x \csc\theta/\sqrt\pi;\tau=2\cot\theta)}^{2},

(24)

where the Jacobi theta function
\vartheta(\zeta;\tau)
is defined as

\displaystyle\vartheta(\zeta;\tau)\equiv\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\pi im^{2}\tau}e^{2\pi im\zeta}.

(25)

We consider two cases for the rotation angle

for which the PDF can be evaluated analytically.
For case 1 we consider rotation angles

These cases correspond to selecting

\displaystyle\Theta=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}:\cot\theta=u/v\in\mathbb{Q}_{(2)}\}\cup\{0,\pi\}

First, we summarize the calculations of case 1, where the rotation angles can be expressed as

\displaystyle G(\zeta,\tau)\sum_{n^{\prime}}\delta(\zeta-n^{\prime}/v).

The delta function evaluates to

In the second case, we identify that a rotation of

7
Realistic GKP states

Here we briefly sketch how to extend the analysis that we have performed to the case of realistic GKP states. In principle, our simulation method could be extended to such case.
We show that an analytical expression for the PDF of a rotated and squeezed
single-mode GKP state, analogous to Eq. (
34
), can also be derived for the case of realistic GKP states. However, due to the more complex form of this PDF, which displays Gaussian peaks rather than a Dirac comb, we cannot directly apply the same derivation as before to evaluate the PDF after multiple modes have undergone symplectic operations.

The rotated and squeezed wave function of a realistic GKP state is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{\theta,s}(x)

\displaystyle=\bra{q=x}\hat{S}(s)\hat{R}(\theta)\ket{ 0_{\text{GKP}}^\Delta}

(66)

which can — as in the case of infinitely squeezed GKP states, see Eq. (
23
) — be expressed in terms of the wave function
\psi_{\theta}^{\Delta}(x)
of the rotated GKP state, i.e.

\displaystyle\psi_{\theta,s}^{\Delta}(x)=\psi_{\theta}^{\Delta}(x/s).

(67)

In Appendix
F
we demonstrate that it is possible to write this expression analytically in the form

\displaystyle\psi^{\Delta}_{\theta}(x)=e^{x^{2}\gamma}\vartheta(\zeta=x\eta,\tau)

(68)

which is defined in terms of the constants

\displaystyle\eta=

\displaystyle-\csc\theta/\left(\sqrt{\pi}(s+\Delta^{4}s-i\Delta^{2}s\cot\theta)\right),

\displaystyle\tau=

\displaystyle 2i(\Delta^{2}-i\cot\theta)/(1+\Delta^{4}-i\Delta^{2}\cot\theta),

\displaystyle\gamma=

\displaystyle\frac{i(i\Delta^{2}+(1+\Delta^{4})\cot\theta)}{2s^{2}(1+\Delta^{4}-i\Delta^{2}\cot\theta)}

(69)

and results in a single-mode rotated realistic GKP state having a PDF of the form

\displaystyle|\psi^{\Delta}_{\theta}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\propto e^{x^{2}(\gamma+\gamma^{*})}|\vartheta(\zeta=x\eta,\tau)|^{2}.

(70)

The PDF of a circuit with input realistic GKP states followed by Gaussian operations and homodyne measurements is given by

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(\mathbf{x})=\bra{\mathbf 0_{\text{GKP}}^\Delta}\prod_{j}\delta(\hat{Q}_{j}-x_{j})\ket{\mathbf 0_{\text{GKP}}^\Delta}.

(71)

Solving this expression analytically would require solving integrals over a product of Jacobi theta functions, which hinders us from using the same techniques used for the ideal case.
We are unaware of a method to evaluate this expression. We provide a more detailed overview of the challenges of evaluating the PDF for realistic GKP states, for both single-mode and multimode measurement, in Appendix
F
.

8
Conclusion

In this work, we have shown that large classes of Gaussian operations, in combination with input
0
-logical GKP states and homodyne measurements, are classically efficiently simulatable. The proof structure used to prove our result is based on decompositions of the symplectic matrix and directly calculating the corresponding PDF, hence introducing very different methods compared to the ones used to prove the simulatability of a restricted class of Gaussian circuits in Ref.
[
22
]
. As a matter of fact, compared to
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
, for single-mode measurements we define a larger class
\mathcal{A}
of simulatable operations which includes symplectic operations with parameters which are dense in the reals and all continuous displacements. Meanwhile, for multimode measurements, we define a new set
\mathcal{B}
, not contained by
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
which is also simulatable.

Identifying circuits which are simulatable is, beyond fundamentally relevant, also useful for the development of quantum computers, providing a possibility for benchmarking and verification. Indeed a quantum computer can be initially programmed to solve problems which are simulatable with classical computers, as a test to identify whether the computation result is accurate
[
48
]
.

Our proof of efficient simulatability only holds for the case of infinitely squeezed
0
-logical GKP states, and we have sketched the difficulties encountered when trying to extend this result to the case of realistic, i.e. finitely squeezed, GKP states
[
2
]
. Establishing conclusively whether realistic GKP states are also simulatable following Gaussian operations is hence left for future work.

state can be tracked by considering the evolution of the position quadrature in the Heisenberg representation.

We will begin by first deriving an appropriate decomposition, inspired by Ref.
[
38
]
(but ignoring the shear gate which acts solely on the
\hat{p}
quadrature), which can be applied to the symplectic operation for all values of
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

Within the symplectic formalism, the rotation operator
\hat{R}(\theta)=e^{i\theta(\hat{q}^{2}+\hat{p}^{2})/2}
has the effect of transforming the mode quadratures as

\displaystyle\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\hat q'\\\hat p'\endlx@physics@matrix)=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\cos\theta& -\sin\theta\\\sin\theta& \cos\theta\endlx@physics@matrix)\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\hat q \\ \hat p\endlx@physics@matrix),

(73)

while the squeezing operator
\hat{S}(s)=e^{-i\frac{\ln s}{2}(\hat{q}\hat{p}+\hat{p}\hat{q})}
has the effect of

\displaystyle\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\hat q'\\\hat p'\endlx@physics@matrix)=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix s & 0 \\ 0 & 1/s\endlx@physics@matrix)\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\hat q \\ \hat p\endlx@physics@matrix),

(74)

meaning that the operation
\hat{S}(s)\hat{R}(\theta)
has the effect

\displaystyle\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\hat q'\\\hat p'\endlx@physics@matrix)=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix s & 0 \\ 0 & 1/s\endlx@physics@matrix)\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\cos\theta& -\sin\theta\\\sin\theta& \cos\theta\endlx@physics@matrix)\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\hat q \\ \hat p\endlx@physics@matrix)=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix s & 0 \\ 0 & 1/s\endlx@physics@matrix)\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\hat q \cos\theta-\hat p \sin\theta\\ \hat q \sin\theta+\hat p \cos\theta\endlx@physics@matrix)=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\hat q s\cos\theta-\hat p s\sin\theta\\ \frac 1 s\left(\hat q \sin\theta+\hat p \cos\theta\right)\endlx@physics@matrix).

(75)

This means that we can obtain the evolved quadrature
\hat{Q}
by acting on
\hat{q}
with a rotation and squeezing operation

\displaystyle\hat{Q}=\hat{R}^{\dagger}(\theta)\hat{S}^{\dagger}(s)\hat{q}\hat{S}(s)\hat{R}(\theta)=\hat{q}s\cos\theta-\hat{p}s\sin\theta.

(76)

This allows us to parameterize the operator
\hat{U}=\hat{S}(s)\hat{R}(\theta)
where

\displaystyle a=s\cos\theta;

\displaystyle b=-s\sin\theta.

(77)

Then, we can calculate the wave function of the transformed mode as

\displaystyle\psi_{\theta,s}(x)

\displaystyle=\bra{q=x}\hat{S}(s)\hat{R}(\theta)\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=\bra{q=x/s}\hat{R}(\theta)\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=\bra{q=x/s}\hat{R}(\theta)\sum_{m}\ket{q=2m\sqrt\pi}.

(78)

We can interpret calculating each summand as a wave mechanics problem with Hamiltonian
{\hat{H}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{q}^{2}+\hat{p}^{2})}
parameterized with
\theta
. This becomes

\displaystyle\bra{q=x/s}\hat{R}(\theta)\ket{q=x'}=\bra{q=x/s}e^{-i\hat{H}\theta}\ket{q=x'}=K(x/s,x^{\prime};\theta)

(79)

which is the propagator for the simple harmonic oscillator
[
49
]
and can be evaluated to

\displaystyle K(x,x^{\prime};\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}\exp(\frac{i}{2\sin\theta}\left((x^2+x'^2)\cos\theta-2xx'\right)).

(80)

By completing the square, we can rewrite the propagator in the form

\displaystyle K(x,x^{\prime};\theta)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}\exp(\frac{i\cot\theta}{2}\left(x^2+x'^2-2xx'\sec\theta\right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}\exp(\frac{i\cot\theta}{2}\left((x-x'\sec\theta)^2+(1-\sec^2\theta)x'^2\right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}\exp(\frac{i\cot\theta}{2}\left((x-x'\sec\theta)^2-\tan^2\theta x'^2\right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}\exp(\frac{i}{2}\left((x-x'\sec\theta)^2\cot\theta-\tan\theta x'^2\right))

(81)

which gives the expression for the wave function

\displaystyle\psi_{\theta,s}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}\sum_{m}\exp(\frac{i}{2}\left((x/s-2m\sqrt\pi\sec\theta)^2\cot\theta-4\pi m^2\tan\theta\right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}\sum_{m}\exp(\frac{i}{2}\left(x^2\cot\theta/s^2-4x m\sqrt\pi\csc\theta/s+4m^2\pi(\csc\theta\sec\theta-\tan\theta) \right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}\sum_{m}\exp(\frac{i}{2}\left(x^2\cot\theta/s^2-4x m\sqrt\pi\csc\theta/s+4m^2\pi\cot\theta\right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}e^{ix^{2}\cot\theta/(2s^{2})}\sum_{m}e^{\pi im^{2}2\cot\theta}e^{-2\pi imx\csc\theta/(s\sqrt{\pi})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}e^{ix^{2}\cot\theta/(2s^{2})}\vartheta(\zeta=-x\csc\theta/(s\sqrt{\pi});\tau=2\cot\theta)

(82)

where we have used the definition of the theta function,i.e.

\displaystyle\vartheta(\zeta;\tau)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\pi im^{2}\tau}e^{2\pi im\zeta}.

(83)

This gives a succinct analytic expression for the PDF of measuring the
\hat{q}
quadrature of a GKP state under any arbitrary single-mode symplectic operation,

\displaystyle|\psi(x)|^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi\sin\theta}\absolutevalue{\vartheta(\zeta=-x \csc\theta/(s\sqrt\pi);\tau=2\cot\theta)}^{2}.

(84)

In order to incorporate phase space displacements we note that any Gaussian operation involving a symplectic operation

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{M}\hat{D}_{\mathbf{r}}

(85)

can be reordered while preserving the structure of the symplectic operation
[
37
]
, i.e.

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{M}\hat{D}_{{\mathbf{r}}}=\hat{D}_{M^{-1}{\mathbf{r}}}\hat{U}_{M}.

(86)

Therefore, any Gaussian operation acting on a single mode, for which we will measure the
\hat{q}
quadrature, can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\hat{U}=\hat{D}_{\bar{\mathbf{r}}}\hat{S}(s)\hat{R}(\theta).

(87)

The PDF of measuring the
\hat{q}
quadrature of a single-mode GKP state under any Gaussian operation can therefore be written as

\displaystyle|\psi(x)|^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi\sin\theta}\absolutevalue{\vartheta(\zeta=- (x-c) \csc\theta/(s\sqrt\pi);\tau=2\cot\theta)}^{2},

(88)

where
\bar{r}_{1}=c
is the magnitude of displacement in position.

The PDF involving the theta function in Eq. (
88
) can be evaluated for certain values of the angle
\theta
, or equivalently certain values of
\tau
. The angle

\theta
cannot take values of the form
k\pi
for integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, but as we shall see later, these rotations are trivial to evaluate using a different method.

We now consider different cases where we can evaluate the PDF.

(Case 1:

\cot\theta=u/v
s.t.
u\in\mathbb{Z},v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\text{odd}}

)

The theta function can be reduced to a sum over Gauss sums whenever
\cot\theta=u/v
is a fraction, i.e. a rational number.
We also assume that the fraction
u/v
is written in its simplest form, i.e.
\gcd(u,v)=1
. If it is not then it should be reduced as such by first calculating
\gcd(u,v)
, which can be calculated in polynomial time with respect to the size of
u,v

[
50
]
. Then,
\exp(\pi i \tau)=\exp(2\pi i u/v )
becomes a root of unity and so the summation repeats in cycles
[
51
]
. This can be utilized by expanding the summation over
n
into an infinite summation over
n^{\prime}
and a summation over
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}=\{0,1,\dots,v-1\}

\displaystyle\vartheta(\zeta;\tau=2u/v)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\pi in^{2}\tau}e^{2\pi in\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi in^{2}u/v}e^{2\pi in\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n^{\prime}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}}e^{2\pi i(n^{\prime}v+m)^{2}u/v}e^{2\pi i(vn^{\prime}+m)\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n^{\prime}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}}e^{2\pi i(v^{2}n^{\prime 2}+2mvn^{\prime}+m^{2})u/v}e^{2\pi i(vn^{\prime}+m)\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n^{\prime}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}}e^{2\pi i(vn^{\prime 2}u+2mn^{\prime}u+m^{2}u/v)}e^{2\pi i(vn^{\prime}+m)\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n^{\prime}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}}e^{2\pi im^{2}u/v}e^{2\pi ivn^{\prime}\zeta}e^{2\pi im\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}}e^{2\pi im^{2}u/v}e^{2\pi im\zeta}\sum_{n^{\prime}}e^{2\pi ivn^{\prime}\zeta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}}e^{2\pi im^{2}u/v}e^{2\pi im\zeta}\sum_{n^{\prime}}\delta(\zeta-n^{\prime}/v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\zeta,\tau)\sum_{n^{\prime}}\delta(\zeta-n^{\prime}/v),

(89)

where we have identified the Gauss sum
[
52
,
53
,
54
]

\displaystyle G(\zeta=x\sec\theta/(2s\sqrt{\pi}),\tau=2u/v)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}}\exp(2\pi i m^2 u/v)\exp(2\pi i m \zeta).

(90)

By using the fact that the delta function evaluates to
0
for all values of
\zeta
not equal to
n^{\prime}/v
for some
n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we can rewrite the theta function as

\displaystyle\vartheta(\zeta;\tau=2u/v)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n^{\prime}}G(\zeta=n^{\prime}/v,\tau)\delta(\zeta-n^{\prime}/v),

(91)

where the Gauss sum needs only to be identified for certain values of
\zeta
, rather than the entire real axis. For these values of
\zeta
, the Gauss sum is given by

\displaystyle G(\zeta=n^{\prime}/v,\tau=2u/v)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}}\exp(2\pi i m^2 u/v)\exp(2\pi i m n'/v).

(92)

Since we have confined to the case that
\gcd(u,v)=1
and
v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\text{odd}}
then we immediately have the result that

\displaystyle|G(\zeta=n^{\prime}/v,\tau=2u/v)|=\sqrt{v}\absolutevalue{\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)}>0\quad\text{ for }\gcd(u,v)=1,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\text{odd}}\text{ and }n^{\prime},u\in\mathbb{Z}

(93)

is a fixed constant dependent only on
u,v

[
53
]
, where
\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)
denotes the Jacobi symbol, a generalization of the Legendre symbol.

Then, we can substitute this into Eq. (
91
)

\displaystyle|\vartheta(\zeta;\tau=2u/v)|=

\displaystyle\sum_{n^{\prime}}\delta(\zeta-n^{\prime}/v)|G(\zeta=n^{\prime}/v,\tau)|

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\sum_{n^{\prime}}\delta(\zeta-n^{\prime}/v).

(94)

We can reinsert the theta function into the probability in Eq. (
88
) to get

\displaystyle|\psi_{\theta,s}(x)|^{2}\propto

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\vartheta(\zeta;\tau=2\cot\theta)}^{2}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\delta(-x\csc\theta/(s\sqrt{\pi})-m/v)

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\delta(x-sm\sqrt{\pi}\sin\theta/v).

(95)

(Case 2:
\theta=0,\pi

When
\theta=0,\pi
the probability function appears to break down because

is undefined. However, we can clearly identify that
\theta=0
is simply the identity. For
\theta=\pi
, this is equivalent to applying the Fourier transform twice, which on the
\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}
state has the effect of the identity. In both cases we obtain

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\psi_{\theta,s}(x)}^{2}=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\bra{q=x}S(s)R(\theta=\pi)\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\bra{q=x}S(s)\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\bra{q=x/s}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\delta(x-2m\sqrt{\pi}s).

(96)

A.2
Evaluation of the separation between the peaks

We can now encompass both cases above, and write a general PDF for all rotation angles
\theta\in\Theta
that we define in Eq. (
26
), in terms of the separation between peaks. Note that the squeezing parameter corresponds to a rescaling of the wave function in position space, and we can trivially write the squeezed and rotated GKP PDF in terms of the rotated PDF, i.e.

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\psi_{\theta,s}(x)}^{2}\propto

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\psi_{\theta}(x/s)}^{2}.

(97)

Ignoring the normalization constant, as in

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\psi_{\theta}(x)}^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\delta(x-m\sqrt{\pi}\Delta)

\displaystyle\Delta=\begin{cases}\sin(\theta)/v\quad&\text{ (case 1) if }\cot\theta=u/v:u\in\mathbb{Z},v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\text{odd}}\text{ and }\gcd(u,v)=1,\\
2\quad&\text{ (case 2) if }\theta=k\pi\text{ for }k\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{cases}

(99)

Note that in the special case when
\theta=\pi/2
we have
\cot(\pi/2)=0
, so we identify
u=0
and

, as the simplest form of the fraction
u/v

. This coincides with what we would expect when performing a Fourier transform (equivalent to a
\pi/2
rotation) on the input state in the logical basis
F\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}=\ket{+_{\text{GKP}}}=\sum_{m}\ket{q=m\sqrt\pi}
followed by a measurement.

B.1
Calculating the PDF

For the class of circuits that we consider in Section
4
, namely those belonging to the class
\mathcal{A}
defined in the main text, the PDF is given by

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(x_{1})=

\displaystyle\Tr(\hat\rho\ket{\hat q_1=x_1}\bra{\hat q_1=x_1}),

(100)

where
\hat{\rho}
is the state of all of the modes after the multimode operation
\hat{U}
(i.e.
{\hat{\rho}=\hat{U}\hat{\rho}_{0}\hat{U}^{\dagger}}
, where
\hat{\rho}_{0}
is the density matrix of
n
modes prepared in the
0
-logical GKP state
\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}
), and
\ket{\hat q_1=x_1}
is the position eigenket for the first mode. Using the cyclic property of the trace, and then evaluating it as a trace over all the other modes in the position basis, we find that the PDF can be written as

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(x_{1})=

\displaystyle\Tr(\hat U\hat\rho_0\hat U^\dagger\ket{\hat q_1=x_1}\bra{\hat q_1=x_1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr(\bra{\hat q_1=x_1}\hat U\hat\rho_0\hat U^\dagger\ket{\hat q_1=x_1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential x_{2}\dots\int\differential x_{n}\bra{\hat q_1=x_1}\dots\bra{\hat q_n=x_n}\hat{U}\hat{\rho}_{0}\hat{U}^{\dagger}\ket{\hat q_1=x_1}\dots\ket{\hat q_n=x_n}.

(101)

The PDF can then be evaluated by inserting the identity over all rotated quadratures
\hat{z}^{\theta_{i}}_{i}
which is given by

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\int\differential z_{j}\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}=\int\differential\mathbf{z}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j},

(102)

and by writing the projection operators as a Dirac delta function
[
36
]
,

\displaystyle\ket{\hat Q_j=x_j}\bra{\hat Q_j=x_j}=\delta\left(\hat{Q}_{j}-x_{j}\right).

(103)

Using the expression for the Heisenberg evolved operator Eq. (
40
) allows us to evaluate the PDF as

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(\hat{Q}_{1}=x_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\ket{\hat Q_1=x_1}\bra{\hat Q_1=x_1}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\delta(\hat{Q}_{1}-x_{1})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\hat{z}^{\theta_{i}}_{i}+c-x_{1}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}z_{i}+c-x_{1}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}z_{i}+c-x_{1}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\absolutevalue{\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}} \ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}}^{2}.

(104)

Then inserting the PDF of the single-mode rotated 0-logical GKP state given in Eq. (
98
), we can express the PDF of the single-mode measurement on a multimode circuit as

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(\hat{Q}_{1}=x_{1})=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}z_{i}+c-x_{1}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(z_{j}-m_{j}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}z_{i}+c-x_{1}\right)\sum_{m_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(z_{1}-m_{1}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{1})\dots\sum_{m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(z_{n}-m_{n}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}m_{i}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{i}+c-x_{1}\right)

(105)

where we have the
\Delta_{j}
specified by Eq. (
99
) for each mode, i.e.

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}=\begin{cases}\sin(\theta_j)/v_{j}\quad&\text{ (case 1) if }\cot\theta_{j}=u_{j}/v_{j}:u\in\mathbb{Z},v_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\text{odd}}\text{ and }\gcd(u_{j},v_{j})=1,\\
2\quad&\text{ (case 2) if }\theta_{j}=k\pi\text{ for }k\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{cases}

B.2
Simple Example

In this section, we provide a simple example to illustrate how our decomposition methods works.
We would like to simulate the following operations

\displaystyle\hat{U}=e^{-i\hat{q}_{2}\hat{p}_{1}}\hat{F}_{2}

(106)

acting on GKP input states, i.e.

\displaystyle\hat{U}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}.

(107)

For simplicity in this example case, these operations are chosen to be Clifford operations in terms of the encoded qubit. By considering the logical operations only, we know that the result of this circuit should be

\displaystyle e^{-i\hat{q}_{2}\hat{p}_{1}}\hat{F}_{2}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}=e^{-i\hat{q}_{2}\hat{p}_{1}}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\ket{+_{\text{GKP}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}+\ket{1_{\text{GKP}}}\ket{1_{\text{GKP}}}\right),

(108)

so that measurement of the quadrature
\hat{q}
on a single mode (e.g. the first mode) will give a PDF

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(x_{1})=\sum_{m}\delta(x_{1}-m\sqrt{\pi}).

(109)

Using our technique we can track the operations on the quadrature

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{1}\to\hat{Q}_{1}

\displaystyle=\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{q}_{1}\hat{U}=\hat{q}_{1}-\hat{p}_{2}.

(110)

Next, analyzing each quadrature we see that

\displaystyle s_{1}\cos\theta_{1}\hat{q}_{1}-s_{1}\sin\theta_{1}\hat{p}_{1}=\hat{q}_{1}

\displaystyle s_{2}\cos\theta_{2}\hat{q}_{2}-s_{2}\sin\theta_{2}\hat{p}_{2}=-\hat{p}_{2}

(111)

for which we can identify

\displaystyle\theta_{1}=0

\displaystyle\quad s_{1}=1,

\displaystyle\theta_{2}=\pi/2

\displaystyle\quad s_{2}=1.

(112)

From these values, we can calculate the
\Delta_{j}
from Eq. (
B.1
) as

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}=2\quad\Delta_{2}=1.

(113)

This results in the following PDF:

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(\hat{Q}_{1}=x_{1})=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{m_{j}}\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{i}+c-x_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}}\delta\left(m_{1}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{1}+m_{2}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{2}-x_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}}\delta\left(2m_{1}\sqrt{\pi}+m_{2}\sqrt{\pi}-x_{1}\right).

(114)

In this example, a further simplification can be made by reparameterizing the variables
{m_{2}\to m_{2}-2m_{1}}
such that

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(\hat{Q}_{1}=x_{1})=

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}}\delta\left(m_{1}\sqrt{\pi}-x_{1}\right),

(115)

which informs us that the variable
x_{1}
will be measured as some randomly selected integer multiple of
\sqrt{\pi}
. This agrees with the qubit-based simulation at the beginning of this subsection since we measure, with equal probability, an outcome which would correspond to a
\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}
or
\ket{1_{\text{GKP}}}
state.

C.1
Calculating the PDF

To calculate the PDF of measuring quadratures
\hat{q}_{1},\dots,\hat{q}_{n}
for the class of operations
\mathcal{B}
considered in Section
4
, we will again utilize the Heisenberg picture formalism to track the measurement operators and then find an expression for the PDF in terms of rotated single-mode GKP states.

The PDF of the measurement in the multimode case can be evaluated analogously to in the single-mode case by inserting the identity over all rotated quadratures
\hat{z}^{\theta_{i}}_{i}
,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\int\differential z_{j}\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}=\int\differential\mathbf{z}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}.

(119)

The PDF can then be evaluated in terms of the Heisenberg evolved measurement operators Eq. (
57
) as

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\prod_{j=1}\ket{\hat Q_j=x_j}\bra{\hat Q_j=x_j}\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\prod_{j=1}\delta(\hat{Q}_{j}-x_{j})\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\prod_{j=1}\delta(\hat{Q}_{j}-x_{j})\prod_{i}\left(\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\right)\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\prod_{j=1}\delta\left(\sum_{i}a^{(j)}_{i}\hat{r}^{\theta_{i}}_{i}+c_{j}-x_{j}\right)\prod_{i}\left(\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\right)\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\prod_{j=1}\delta\left(\sum_{i}a^{(j)}_{i}z_{i}+c_{j}-x_{j}\right)\prod_{i}\left(\ket{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\bra{\hat z_j^{\theta_j}=z_j}\right)\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}\dots\ket{0_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\prod_{j=1}\left(\delta\left(\sum_{i}a^{(j)}_{i}z_{i}+c_{j}-x_{j}\right)\right)\prod_{i}\absolutevalue{\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\ket{\hat z_i^{\theta_i}=z_i}}^{2}.

(120)

We can then use

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}}\ket{\hat z_i^{\theta_i}=z_i}}^{2}=\absolutevalue{\psi_{\theta}(z_i)}^{2}\propto

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(z_{i}-m\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{i})

(121)

to get

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(\mathbf{x})=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a^{(j)}_{i}z_{i}+c_{j}-x_{j}\right)\right)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(z_{k}-m_{k}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential\mathbf{z}\left(\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a^{(1)}_{i}z_{i}+c_{1}-x_{1}\right)\dots\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a^{(n)}_{i}z_{i}+c_{n}-x_{n}\right)\right)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(z_{k}-m_{k}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\delta(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{(1)}m_{i}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{i}+c_{1}-x_{1})\right)\dots\left(\delta(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{(n)}m_{i}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{i}+c_{n}-x_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\delta(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{(j)}m_{i}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{i}+c_{j}-x_{j}).

(122)

We again have the
\Delta_{j}
specified by Eq. (
99
) for each mode, i.e.

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}=\begin{cases}\sin(\theta_j)/v_{j}\quad&\text{ (case 1) if }\cot\theta_{j}=u_{j}/v_{j}:u\in\mathbb{Z},v_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\text{odd}}\text{ and }\gcd(u_{j},v_{j})=1,\\
2\quad&\text{ (case 2) if }\theta_{j}=k\pi\text{ for }k\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{cases}

(123)

\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{1}\to

\displaystyle A=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 1&0\\1&1\endlx@physics@matrix).

(129)

\displaystyle\sin(\pi/2)/1=1

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\delta(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{(j)}m_{i}\sqrt{\pi}\Delta_{i}+c_{j}-x_{j})

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(m_{1}\sqrt{\pi}-x_{1})\delta(m_{1}\sqrt{\pi}+2m_{2}\sqrt{\pi}-x_{2}).

Appendix D
Density of the set of simulatable operations in class
\mathcal{A}

In this Appendix, we will demonstrate that the circuit class
\mathcal{A}
contains symplectic matrices which have parameters which are dense in the reals.
Using the decomposition in Section
2
we have shown that we can construct a symplectic matrix
M
with a free choice parameters
{A_{1,i}=a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}}
and
{B_{1,i}=b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}}
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. However, in order to simulate the measurement of the output mode we must make a further restriction on these variables. We know that for a symplectic matrix to exist in
\text{RSp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
it must have either one of
A_{1,i}=0
or
B_{1,i}=0
or
A_{1,i}/B_{1,i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{(2)}
.

By introducing a new set

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{\text{odd}}=\{u/v:u\in\mathbb{Z}_{\text{odd}},v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\text{odd}}\}

(143)

which is contained by
\mathbb{Q}_{(2)}
but also contains fewer elements that
\mathbb{Q}_{(2)}
, we can create arbitrary symplectic matrices which will always be contained within
\text{RSp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
.
If we select the matrix elements
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\text{odd}}
we can guarantee that the matrix will be contained in
\text{RSp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
, since the division of one element in
\mathbb{Q}_{\text{odd}}
by another element in
\mathbb{Q}_{\text{odd}}
, will always give an element in
\mathbb{Q}_{(2)}
. Next, we can show that the set from which we choose the matrix elements
\mathbb{Q}_{\text{odd}}
is dense on the reals.

Formally we can prove this following the technique of Ref.
[
55
]
to prove the density of the rational numbers on the reals. The density of a set
\mathcal{\chi}
on the reals formally means that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
where
x<y
then there exists
\alpha\in\mathcal{\chi}
such that
x<\alpha<y
.

First we recall the demonstration that the rationals are dense on the reals, following the proof given by Ref.
[
55
]
. This means that for any
x<y
with
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
we must have some
q\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
x<q<y
.

By the Archimedian property
[
55
]
, for any
x,y
with
x<y
there exists some integer
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0<1/\beta<(y-x)
. There must also exist some integer
\alpha
such that
\alpha>\beta x
. Let
\alpha
be the smallest integer such that
\alpha>\beta x
, which implies
\alpha-1\leq\beta x
. This leads us to the relation

\displaystyle\beta x<\alpha<\beta+1.

(144)

We know that
1<\beta(y-x)
and therefore

\displaystyle\beta x<\alpha<\beta x+\beta(y-x)=\beta y\implies

\displaystyle x<\alpha/\beta<y.

(145)

This means that for any
x<y
such that
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
we can define
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\dots\}
are the natural numbers, not including
0
.

Now we will follow the same proof technique to show that the set
\mathbb{Q}_{\text{odd}}
is dense on the reals. Again, by the Archimedian property, there exists some
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
s.t.
0<3/(2\beta+1)<(y-x)
. There must also exist some integer
\alpha
such that
{(2\alpha+1)>(2\beta+1)x}
. Let
\alpha
be the smallest integer such that
{(2\alpha+1)>(2\beta+1)x}
, which implies
{2\alpha-1\leq(2\beta+1)x\implies 2\alpha\leq(2\beta+1)x+1\implies 2\alpha<(2\beta+1)x+2}
. This leads us to the relation

\displaystyle(2\beta+1)x<2\alpha+1<(2\beta+1)x+3.

(146)

We know that
3<(2\beta+1)(y-x)
and therefore

\displaystyle(2\beta+1)x<2\alpha+1<(2\beta+1)x+(2\beta+1)(y-x)=(2\beta+1)y.

(147)

This means that for any
x<y
such that
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
we can define
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle x<\frac{2\alpha+1}{2\beta+1}<y

(148)

i.e. there exists a number
r\in\mathbb{Q}_{\text{odd}}
such that
x<r<y
.

Therefore, for any two symplectic matrices which have the effect on the output mode as

\displaystyle M:\hat{q}_{1}\to a_{1}\hat{q}_{1}+b_{1}\hat{p}_{1}+\dots+a_{n}\hat{q}_{n}+b_{n}\hat{p}_{n}

(149)

and

\displaystyle M^{\prime}:\hat{q}_{1}\to a_{1}^{\prime}\hat{q}_{1}+b_{1}^{\prime}\hat{p}_{1}+\dots+a_{n}^{\prime}\hat{q}_{n}+b_{n}^{\prime}\hat{p}_{n}

(150)

where
a_{i},b_{i},a^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, it is always possible to select parameters
\bar{a}_{i},\bar{b}_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\text{odd}}
such that

\displaystyle a_{i}<\bar{a}_{i}<a_{i}^{\prime}\quad\text{ or }\quad a_{i}^{\prime}<\bar{a}_{i}<a_{i},\quad\text{and}\quad b_{i}<\bar{b}_{i}<b_{i}^{\prime}\quad\text{ or }\quad b_{i}^{\prime}<\bar{b}_{i}<b_{i}\quad\forall i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n},

(151)

whereby

\displaystyle\bar{M}:\hat{q}_{1}\to\bar{a}_{1}\hat{q}_{1}+\bar{b}_{1}\hat{p}_{1}+\dots+\bar{a}_{n}\hat{q}_{n}+\bar{b}_{n}\hat{p}_{n}

(152)

is simulatable by our technique.

Appendix E
The Clifford group is not contained in the set of simulatable operations

We show using a simple example that the Clifford group is not contained within
\text{RSp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
. We will use the Fourier transform
\hat{F}
which transforms the quadratures according to Eq. (
6
). We will also use the phase gate
\hat{P}_{1}=e^{i\hat{q}_{1}^{2}/2}

[
2
]
which transforms the quadratures as

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{1}

\displaystyle\to\hat{q}_{1}

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{1}

\displaystyle\to\hat{q}_{1}+\hat{p}_{1}.

(153)

We can define a single qubit Clifford operation in the GKP encoding as
\hat{U}=\hat{F}_{1}\hat{P}_{1}^{2}\hat{F}_{1}
and consider its effect on the Heisenberg measurement operators as

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{1}=\hat{U}\hat{q}_{1}\hat{U}^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{1}\hat{P}_{1}\hat{P}_{1}\hat{F}_{1}\hat{q}_{1}\hat{F}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{P}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{P}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{F}_{1}^{\dagger}

(154)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{1}\hat{P}_{1}\hat{P}_{1}(-\hat{p}_{1})\hat{P}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{P}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{F}_{1}^{\dagger}

(155)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{1}(-(2\hat{q}_{1}+\hat{p}_{1}))\hat{F}_{1}^{\dagger}

(156)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-2\hat{p}_{1}+\hat{q}_{1})

(157)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\hat{p}_{1}-\hat{q}_{1}.

(158)

The top blocks of the
2\times 2
symplectic matrix
M
corresponding to the operation
\hat{U}
can be written as

\displaystyle\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix-1&2\endlx@physics@matrix).

(159)

We know that a symplectic matrix
M
exists in
\text{RSp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
if and only if

\displaystyle M_{1,i}=0\text{ or }M_{1,n+i}=0\text{ or }M_{1,i}/M_{1,n+i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{(2)}\forall i\in\{1,\dots,n\}

(160)

which we can check for the symplectic matrix
M
. For a
2\times 2
symplectic matrix, this amounts to a single condition, i.e. that

\displaystyle M_{1,1}=0\text{ or }M_{1,2}=0\text{ or }M_{1,1}/M_{1,2}\in\mathbb{Q}_{(2)}.

(161)

However, we know that
\mathbb{Q}_{(2)}
is defined as the rationals such that the denominator is odd in its simplest form. The fraction
-1/2
is already in its simplest form and has even denominator. Therefore
-1/2\notin\mathbb{Q}_{(2)}
, which means it is not possible to simulate the circuit using our method. We have provided an example of a Clifford circuit which does not exist within the set
\text{RSp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
and hence it also cannot be contained within
\text{DSp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
.

Note, however, that the simulatability of Clifford operations was proven in Ref.

]
.

Appendix F
Route to extending simulatability to realistic GKP states

The circuit classes we have shown to be simulatable use ideal GKP states. Formally, these are not normalizable and therefore are also not physical states. Tackling the extension of our results on the simulatability of Gaussian circuits to the case of realistic GKP states is challenging. In this Appendix, we will sketch the calculations required to extend our result to include realistic GKP states. We first demonstrate that it is possible to express a rotated and squeezed GKP state in an analytic form using the Jacobi theta function, given in Eq. (
25
). We then point to the difficulty of extending our results to the case of realistic GKP states, by providing the unsolved equation of the PDF of a circuit involving multiple realistic GKP states, and showing that it cannot be easily solved in a closed form.

F.1
PDF of a rotated and squeezed realistic GKP state

We can evaluate the PDF of a rotated and squeezed realistic GKP state using similar techniques to the case of the ideal GKP states. We know that it is possible to write the wave function of the realistic GKP states in the form
[
56
,
57
]

\displaystyle\psi_{\text{GKP}}^{\Delta}(x)=e^{-x^{2}\Delta^{2}/2}\vartheta(\zeta=x/2\sqrt{\pi},\tau=i\Delta^{2}/2)

(162)

where
\vartheta
is the Jacobi theta function given in Eq. (
25
) and
\Delta
is the squeezing parameter of the peaks and envelope of the GKP state.

Applying rotation and squeezing to this wave function gives

\displaystyle\psi^{\Delta}_{\theta,s}(x)

\displaystyle=\bra{q=x}\hat{S}(s)\hat{R}(\theta)\ket{0^\Delta_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=\int\differential x^{\prime}\bra{q=x}\hat{S}(s)\hat{R}(\theta)\ket{ q=x'}\bra{ q=x'}\ket{0^\Delta_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=\int\differential x^{\prime}\bra{q=x/s}\hat{R}(\theta)\ket{ q=x'}\bra{ q=x'}\ket{0^\Delta_{\text{GKP}}}.

(163)

We can use the propagator, which is also used in the case of infinite squeezing, Eq. (
80
),

\displaystyle K(x,x^{\prime};\theta)=\bra{q=x}\hat{R}(\theta)\ket{q=x'}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}\exp(\frac{i}{2}\left((x-x'\sec\theta)^2\cot\theta-\tan\theta x'^2\right))

(164)

to write

\displaystyle\psi^{\Delta}_{\theta,s}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\differential x^{\prime}\bra{q=x}\hat{S}(s)\hat{R}(\theta)\ket{ q=x'}\bra{ q=x'}\ket{0^\Delta_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=\int\differential x^{\prime}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}e^{\frac{i}{2}\left((x/s-x^{\prime}\sec\theta)^{2}\cot\theta-\tan\theta x^{\prime 2}\right)}\bra{ q=x'}\ket{0^\Delta_{\text{GKP}}}

\displaystyle=\int\differential x^{\prime}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}e^{\frac{i}{2}\left((x/s-x^{\prime}\sec\theta)^{2}\cot\theta-\tan\theta x^{\prime 2}\right)}e^{-x^{\prime 2}\Delta^{2}/2}\vartheta(\zeta=\frac{x^{\prime}}{2\sqrt{\pi}},\tau=\frac{i\Delta^{2}}{2})

\displaystyle=\int\differential x^{\prime}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi i\sin\theta}}e^{\frac{i}{2}\left((x/s-x^{\prime}\sec\theta)^{2}\cot\theta-\tan\theta x^{\prime 2}\right)}e^{-x^{\prime 2}\Delta^{2}/2}\sum_{n}e^{-\pi n^{2}\Delta^{2}/2}e^{2\pi inx^{\prime}/2\sqrt{\pi}}

\displaystyle\propto e^{\frac{ix^{2}(i\Delta^{2}+(1+\Delta^{4})\cot\theta)}{2s^{2}(1+\Delta^{4}-i\Delta^{2}\cot\theta)}}\vartheta(\zeta=-\frac{x\csc\theta}{\sqrt{\pi}s(1+\Delta^{4}-i\Delta^{2}\cot\theta)},\tau=\frac{2i(\Delta^{2}-i\cot\theta)}{1+\Delta^{4}-i\Delta^{2}\cot\theta})

\displaystyle=e^{x^{2}\gamma_{\Delta,s,\theta}}\vartheta(\zeta=x\eta_{\Delta,s,\theta},\tau=\tau_{\Delta,\theta})

(165)

which is defined in terms of the constants

\displaystyle\eta_{\Delta,s,\theta}=

\displaystyle-\csc\theta/\left(\sqrt{\pi}(s+\Delta^{4}s-i\Delta^{2}s\cot\theta)\right),

(166)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta,\theta}=

\displaystyle 2i(\Delta^{2}-i\cot\theta)/(1+\Delta^{4}-i\Delta^{2}\cot\theta),

(167)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Delta,s,\theta}=

\displaystyle\frac{i(i\Delta^{2}+(1+\Delta^{4})\cot\theta)}{2s^{2}(1+\Delta^{4}-i\Delta^{2}\cot\theta)}.

(168)

For convenience, we drop the indices in the notation of these constants. From the wave function, we can determine that the PDF is

\displaystyle|\psi^{\Delta}_{\theta,s}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\propto e^{x^{2}(\gamma+\gamma^{*})}|\vartheta(\zeta=x\eta,\tau)|^{2},

(169)

where we have left out the normalization constant for brevity.
Expanding these variables in series and assuming
\Delta^{4}\to 0
, we have

\displaystyle\eta^{(3)}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\csc\theta}{\sqrt{\pi}s}(1+i\cot\theta\Delta^{2}),

(170)

\displaystyle\tau^{(3)}=

\displaystyle 2\cot\theta+2i\csc^{2}\theta\Delta^{2},

(171)

\displaystyle\gamma^{(3)}=

\displaystyle\frac{i\cot\theta}{2s^{2}}-\frac{\csc^{2}\theta\Delta^{2}}{2s^{2}},

(172)

which somewhat simplifies the PDF to

\displaystyle|\psi^{\Delta}_{\theta,s}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\approx e^{-x^{2}\csc^{2}\theta\Delta^{2}/s^{2}}|\vartheta(\zeta=-x\frac{\csc\theta}{\sqrt{\pi}s}(1+i\cot\theta\Delta^{2}),\tau=2\cot\theta+2i\csc^{2}\theta\Delta^{2})|^{2}.

(173)

However, even with this approximation, it remains challenging to evaluate the PDF of the circuit even in the case of single-mode measurement.

Note that in the limit
\Delta=0
we can identify that these parameters will reach

\displaystyle\eta^{(3)}_{\Delta\to 0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\csc\theta}{\sqrt{\pi}s},

(174)

\displaystyle\tau^{(3)}_{\Delta\to 0}=

\displaystyle 2\cot\theta,

(175)

\displaystyle\gamma^{(3)}_{\Delta\to 0}=

\displaystyle\frac{i\cot\theta}{2s^{2}},

(176)

which returns the infinite-squeezing GKP state PDF given in Eq. (
84

\displaystyle|\psi^{\Delta\to 0}_{\theta,s}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\propto|\vartheta(\zeta=-x\frac{\csc\theta}{\sqrt{\pi}s},\tau=2\cot\theta)|^{2}.

(177)

F.2
Single-mode measurement

We showcase the complexity of evaluating the PDF of a single-mode measurement result by providing an example of a calculation involving two modes. The PDF is given by

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(\hat{Q}_{1}=x_{1})=

\displaystyle\int\differential z_{1}\differential z_{2}\delta\left(s_{1}z_{1}+s_{2}z_{2}+c-x_{1}\right)\absolutevalue{\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}} \ket{\hat z_1^{\theta_1}=z_1}}^{2}\absolutevalue{\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}} \ket{\hat z_2^{\theta_2}=z_2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential z_{1}\differential z_{2}\delta\left((s_{1}z_{1}+c-x_{1})/s_{2}+z_{2}\right)\absolutevalue{\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}} \ket{\hat z_1^{\theta_1}=z_1}}^{2}\absolutevalue{\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}} \ket{\hat z_2^{\theta_2}=z_2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\differential z_{1}\absolutevalue{\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}} \ket{\hat z_1^{\theta_1}=z_1}}^{2}\absolutevalue{\bra{0_{\text{GKP}}} \ket{\hat z_2^{\theta_2}=(x_1-s_1 z_1-c)/s_2}}^{2}.

(178)

Note that in the case of infinite squeezing, the PDF of the rotated and squeezed GKP state is a Dirac comb, i.e. Eq. (
34
), which is a summation over delta functions. When inserted into the multimode PDF, the latter yields a product of summations of delta functions in terms of
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
, which results in a function that can be integrated over these variables, leading to Eq. (
4
). However, in the case of finite-squeezing, the rotated and squeezed single-mode PDF cannot be expressed in terms of delta functions, which limits the possibility of analytically solving this expression.
Using the fact that the PDF of the rotated and squeezed GKP state is given by Eq. (
169
), we have

\displaystyle\text{PDF}(\hat{Q}_{1}=x_{1})=

\displaystyle\int\differential z_{1}e^{z_{1}^{2}(\gamma+\gamma^{*})}|\vartheta(\zeta=z_{1}\eta,\tau)|^{2}e^{((x_{1}-s_{1}z_{1}-c)/s_{2})^{2}(\gamma+\gamma^{*})}|\vartheta(\zeta=(x_{1}-s_{1}z_{1}-c)/s_{2}\eta,\tau)|^{2}.

(179)

We are not aware of any theorem which rules out the possibility to evaluate such a function in general. However, we are also unable to provide a general analytic solution of this integral.

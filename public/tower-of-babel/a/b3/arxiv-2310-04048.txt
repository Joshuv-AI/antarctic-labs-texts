Simple Modules and Azumaya Loci over the PI quantized Weyl Algebras
Sanu Bera 1 and Snehashis Mukherjee 2

Abstract
. In this article, both versions of multiparameter quantum Weyl algebras have been studied at the roots of unity. The center, PI degree, maximal-dimensional simple modules, and Azumaya locus have been explicitly computed for such algebras.

Introduction

Let

\displaystyle=q_{i}^{-1}\lambda_{ij}^{-1}x_{j}y_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ \ \ 1\leq i<j\leq n,

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we begin by revisiting essential results related to the quantum Weyl algebra. Subsequently, we recall relevant information about the Polynomial Identity algebra and the process of deleting derivation, enabling us to comment on the

2.1.
Torsion and Torsionfree modules

Let
A
be an algebra and
M
be a right
A
-module and
S\subset A
be a right Ore set. The submodule

\tors_{S}(M):=\{m\in M:ms=0\ \text{for some}\ s\in S\}

is called the
S
-torsion submodule of
M
. The module
M
is said to be
S
-torsion if
\tors_{S}(M)=M
and
S
-torsionfree if
\tors_{S}(M)=0
. If Ore set
S
is generated by
x\in A
, we simply say that the
S
-torsion/torsionfree module
M
is
x
-torsion/torsionfree.

A non zero element
x
of an algebra
{A}
is called a normal element if
x{A}={A}x
. Clearly, if
x
is a normal element of
A
, then the set
\{x^{i}:~i\geq 0\}
is an Ore set generated by
x
. The next lemma is obvious.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
A
is an algebra,
x
is a normal element of
A
and
M
is a simple
A
-module. Then either
Mx=0
(if
M
is
x
-torsion) or the map

x_{M}:M\rightarrow M\ \text{given by}\ m\mapsto mx

is an isomorphism (if
M
is
x
-torsionfree).

The above lemma says that the action of a normal element on a simple module is either trivial or invertible.

2.2.
Quantum Weyl Algebras

The quantum Weyl algebra
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
has an iterated skew polynomial presentation with respect to the ordering of variables
y_{1},x_{1},y_{2},x_{2},\cdots,y_{n},x_{n}
of the form:

\mathbb{K}[y_{1}][x_{1},\tau_{1},\delta_{1}][y_{2},\sigma_{2}][x_{2},\tau_{2},\delta_{2}]\cdots[y_{n},\sigma_{n}][x_{n},\tau_{n}.\delta_{n}],

where the
\tau_{j}
and
\sigma_{j}
are
\mathbb{K}
-linear automorphisms and the
\delta_{j}
are
\mathbb{K}
-linear
\tau_{j}
-derivations such that

\displaystyle\tau_{j}(y_{i})

\displaystyle=q_{i}\lambda_{ij}y_{i},\ i<j

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}(y_{i})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{ij}^{-1}y_{i},\ i<j

\displaystyle\tau_{j}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=q_{i}^{-1}\lambda_{ij}^{-1}x_{i},\ i<j

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{ij}x_{i},\ i<j

\displaystyle\tau_{j}(y_{j})

\displaystyle=q_{j}y_{j},\ \forall~j

\displaystyle\delta_{j}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\delta_{j}(y_{i})=0,\ i<j

\displaystyle\delta_{j}(y_{j})

\displaystyle=1+\sum\limits_{i<j}(q_{i}-1)y_{i}x_{i},\ \forall~j

This observation along with the skew Hilbert Basis Theorem (cf.
[
20
, Theorem 2.9]
) yields that the algebra
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is an affine noetherian domain. Moreover, the family of ordered monomials

(2.1)

\{y_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{a_{n}}x_{n}^{b_{n}}~:~a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}

is a
\mathbb{K}
-basis of
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
.
Let us define
z_{0}:=1
and

z_{i}:=x_{i}y_{i}-y_{i}x_{i}\ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

These elements are going to play a crucial role throughout this chapter. It is easy to verify that for all
1\leq i\leq n
,

z_{i}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{i}(q_{j}-1)y_{j}x_{j}=q_{i}^{-1}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{i}(q_{j}-1)x_{j}y_{j}\right)=z_{i-1}+(q_{i}-1)y_{i}x_{i}.

Therefore the last of the listed defining relations for
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
can be rewritten as

x_{i}y_{i}-q_{i}y_{i}x_{i}=z_{i-1},\ \ \forall\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

Proposition 2.2
.

(
[
3
]
)

Direct computations yield the following results for
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
.

(1)

For all
1\leq i\leq n
,
z_{i}
is a normal element of
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
. More precisely, we have:

(a)

For all
i,j
with
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
z_{i}x_{j}=x_{j}z_{i}
and
z_{i}y_{j}=y_{j}z_{i}

(b)

For all
i,j
with
1\leq j\leq i\leq n
,
z_{i}x_{j}=q_{j}^{-1}x_{j}z_{i}
and
z_{i}y_{j}=q_{j}y_{j}z_{i}

(c)

For all
i,j
with
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
z_{i}z_{j}=z_{j}z_{i}
.

(2)

The following identities hold in the algebra
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
, for
k\geq 1
:

(a)

x_{i}^{k}y_{i}=q_{i}^{k}y_{i}x_{i}^{k}+\left(1+q_{i}+\cdots+q_{i}^{k-1}\right)z_{i-1}x_{i}^{k-1}
,

(b)

x_{i}y_{i}^{k}=q_{i}^{k}y_{i}^{k}x_{i}+\left(1+q_{i}+\cdots+q_{i}^{k-1}\right)z_{i-1}y_{i}^{k-1}
.

In Section 7, we have presented all the necessary information and essential properties regarding the alternative quantum Weyl algebra
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
. It is important to note that
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
and
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
share a common localization, which plays a crucial role in studying
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
.

2.3.
Polynomial Identity Algebras

In this subsection, we recall some known facts concerning Polynomial Identity algebra that we shall be applying on (both) quantized Weyl algebras at roots of unity for further development.

A ring
R
is said to be a polynomial identity (PI) ring if
R
satisfies some monic polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\rangle
i.e.,
f(r_{1},\cdots,r_{k})=0
for all
r_{i}\in R
. The minimal degree of a PI ring
R
is the least degree of all monic polynomial identities for
R
. PI rings cover a large class of rings including commutative rings. Commutative rings satisfy the polynomial identity
x_{1}x_{2}-x_{2}x_{1}
and therefore have minimal degree
2
. Let us recall a result which provides a sufficient condition for a ring to be PI.

Proposition 2.3
.

(
[
20
, Corollary 13.1.13]
)

If
R
is a ring that is a finitely generated module over a commutative subring, then
R
is a PI ring.

Now in the root of unity setting, the above proposition yields the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

The quantum Weyl algebra
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is a PI algebra if and only if
q_{i}
’s and
\lambda_{ij}
’s are roots of unity.

Proof.

Suppose
q_{i}
and
\lambda_{ij}
are roots of unity such that
l=\lcmu(\ord(q_{i}),\ord(\lambda_{ij}):1\leq i,j\leq n)
. Let
Z
be the subalgebra of
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
generated by
x_{i}^{l},y_{i}^{l},\ \ \forall\ \ 1\leq i\leq n
. Then from the defining relations of
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
along with Proposition
2.2
, the subalgebra
Z
is central. Now from the
\mathbb{K}
-basis of
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
, one can easily verify that
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is a finitely generated module over a central subalgebra
Z
. Hence it follows from Proposition
2.3
that
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is a PI algebra.

For the converse, just note that the
\mathbb{K}
-subalgebra of
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
generated by
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
with relation
x_{i}x_{j}=q_{i}\lambda_{ij}x_{j}x_{i}
is not PI if
q_{i}
or
\lambda_{ij}
is not a root of unity (cf.
[
4
, Proposition I.14.2.]
).
∎

We now define the PI degree of prime PI-algebras. This definition will suffice because the algebras covered in this article are all prime. As a consequence of the Artin-Wedderburn Theorem, any central simple algebra
A
is isomorphic to a matrix ring over a central simple division ring. Hence
\dime_{Z(A)}(A)=n^{2}
for some natural number
n
. From this, we define the PI degree of
A
to be
n
. We now recall one of the fundamental results from the Polynomial Identity theory.

Theorem 2.5

(Posner’s Theorem
[
20
, Theorem 13.6.5]
)
.

Let
A
be a prime PI ring with centre
Z(A)
and minimal
degree
d
. Let
S=Z(A)\setminus\{0\}
,
Q=AS^{-1}
and
F=Z(A)S^{-1}
. Then
Q
is a central simple algebra with centre
F
and
\dime_{F}(Q)=(\frac{d}{2})^{2}
.

Note that the
Q
in Posner’s theorem is PI and, since
Q
is a central simple algebra, we can state its PI degree to be
\frac{d}{2}
by the discussion above. Furthermore, as a result,
[
4
, I.13.2(6)]
,
Q
has the same minimal degree as
A
, namely
d
. Recognizing that the PI degree can be interpreted as some measure of how close to being commutative a PI-algebra is and that this, in turn, is related to its minimal degree, the definition of PI degree given above can be extended to all prime PI rings in the following way.

Definition 2.6
.

The PI degree of a prime PI ring
A
with minimal degree
d
is
\pideg(A)=\frac{d}{2}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Based on this definition, for a prime PI ring
R
:

(1)

\pideg(S)\leq\pideg(R)
for all subalgebra
S
of
R
.

(2)

\pideg(R/P)\leq\pideg(R)
for all prime ideals
P\in\spect(R)
.

As a consequence of Posner’s Theorem, every prime PI ring
R
has a total ring of fractions
\cf(R)
obtained by inverting all nonzero central elements of
R
. Now we obtain the following result
[
4
, Corollary I.13.3]
:

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
R
be a prime PI ring. If
S
is a subring of
\cf(R)
with
R\subseteq S
, then
S
is also a prime PI ring and
\pideg(S)=\pideg(R)
.

Primitive PI ring exhibits a particularly nice structure, established in Kaplansky’s Theorem (cf.
[
20
, Theorem 13.3.8]
). Now Kaplansky’s Theorem has a striking consequence in the case of a prime affine PI algebra over an algebraically closed field. The following result provides an important link between the PI degree of a prime affine PI algebra over an algebraically closed field and the
\mathbb{K}
-dimension of its irreducible representations (cf.
[
4
, Theorem I.13.5, Lemma III.1.2]
):

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
A
be a prime affine PI algebra over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
, with PI-deg(
A
) =
n
and
V
be a simple
A
-module. Then
V
is a vector space over
\mathbb{K}
of dimension
t
, where
t\leq n
, and
A/ann_{A}(V)\cong M_{t}(\mathbb{K})
. Moreover, the upper bound PI-deg(
A
) is attained by some simple
A
-modules.

Remark 2.10
.

In the roots of unity context, the quantum Weyl algebra is classified as a prime affine PI algebra. Consequently, according to Proposition
2.9
, it is quite clear that each simple
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module is finite dimensional and can have dimension at most
\pideg({A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
). In Section
3
, our focus will be on computing the PI degree of
{A_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
.

In the following two subsections, we will focus on deleting derivation process and PI degree of quantum affine spaces to compute the PI degree of the concerned algebras.

2.4.
Derivation Erasing Process

In this discussion we shall recall some results of PI degree parity between iterated Ore extension and associated quantum polynomial algebra depending on various assumptions on base ring, existing derivations, and base field.

The main result of
[
16
]
states that if
R
is a noetherian domain which is also an algebra over a field
\mathbb{K}
then, under some quantum-like hypothesis, the iterated Ore extension
R_{n}=R[x_{1},\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}]\cdots[x_{n},\sigma_{n},\delta_{n}]
and
T_{n}=R[x_{1},\sigma_{1}]\cdots[x_{n},\sigma_{n}]
have the same PI degree. This result was known earlier only in the case where
R
is a prime algebra over a field of
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
(cf.
[
18
]
). In
[
19
]
, A. Leroy and J. Matczuk presented a short and elementary proof of the derivation erasing process under the hypothesis that the iterated Ore extension is a prime PI ring. In particular, the following result was shown:

Proposition 2.11
.

(
[
19
, Theorem 7]
)

Suppose that
R=R_{0}
is a prime PI algebra over a field
\mathbb{K}
and
n\geq 1
. Let
R_{i}:=R_{i-1}[x_{i},\sigma_{i},\delta_{i}],~1\leq i\leq n
, be a sequence of Ore extensions such that each
\sigma_{i}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear automorphism of
R_{i-1}
and each
\delta_{i}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear
\sigma_{i}
-derivation of
R_{i-1}
such that:

(i)

\sigma_{i}|_{R_{0}}
is an automorphism of
R_{0}
of finite order, for any
1\leq i\leq n
.

(ii)

\sigma_{i}(x_{j})=q_{ij}x_{j}
, where
q_{ij}\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
, for any
1\leq j<i\leq n
.

(iii)

\delta_{i}
is a
q_{i}
-skew
\sigma_{i}
-derivation of
R_{i-1}
, where
1\neq q_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
, for any
1\leq i\leq n
.

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R_{n}
is a PI algebra.

(2)

T_{n}=R_{0}[y_{1},\sigma^{\prime}_{1}]\cdots[y_{n},\sigma^{\prime}_{n}]
is a PI algebra, where
\sigma^{\prime}_{i}|_{R_{0}}=\sigma_{i}|_{R_{0}}
and
\sigma^{\prime}_{i}(y_{j})=q_{ij}y_{j}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j<i\leq n
;

(3)

q_{ij}
is a root of unity, for any
1\leq j<i\leq n
;

(4)

\sigma_{i}
is an automorphism of finite order of
R_{i-1}
, for any
1\leq i\leq n
.

Moreover, if one of the above equivalent conditions holds, then the algebras
R_{n}
and
T_{n}
have isomorphic classical rings of quotients and equal PI degree.

This erasing derivation process does not assume that the derivations are locally nilpotent or that the prime base ring is of zero characteristic. In this sense, it gives a generalization of the well-known Cauchon process of erasing derivations (cf.
[
11
]
). Both Theorem
1.2(1)
and Corollary
(4.7)
of
[
16
]
are direct consequences of the above proposition.

Most of the quantum algebras can be presented as iterated Ore extension of the form
\mathbb{K}[x_{1}][x_{2},\sigma_{2},\delta_{2}]\cdots[x_{n},\sigma_{n},\delta_{n}]
, where the appearing automorphisms and skew derivations are as in the proposition (
2.11
) by taking
R_{0}=\mathbb{K},\sigma_{1}=id_{\mathbb{K}},\delta_{1}=0
. Thus such algebras have the same PI degree with quantum affine space
\mathbb{K}[x_{1}][x_{2},\sigma_{2}]\cdots[x_{n},\sigma_{n}]
.

2.5.
PI Degree of Quantum Affine Spaces

Let
\mathbf{q}=(q_{ij})
be a multiplicatively antisymmetric
(n\times n)
-matrix over
\mathbb{K}
, that is,
q_{ii}=1
for all
i
and
q_{ji}={q_{ij}}^{-1}
for all
i\neq j
. Given such a matrix, the multiparameter quantum affine space of degree
n
is the
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
generated by the variables
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
subject only to the relations

(2.2)

x_{i}x_{j}=q_{ij}x_{j}x_{i},\ \ \ \forall\ \ \ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

It is of interest to know when a quantum affine space
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
is a PI ring and what its PI degree is. Using Kaplansky’s Theorem and Proposition
2.3
, one can prove that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
is a PI ring if and only if all the
q_{ij}
are roots of unity. The following result of De Concini and Procesi provides one of the key techniques for calculating the PI degree of a quantum affine space independent of characteristic.

Proposition 2.12
.

(
[
7
, Proposition 7.1]
)

Let
\mathbf{q}=\left(q_{ij}\right)
be an
n\times n
multiplicatively antisymmetric matrix over
\mathbb{K}
.
Suppose that
q_{ij}=q^{h_{ij}}
for all
i,j
, where
q\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
is a primitive
m
-th root of unity and the
h_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}

It is well known that a skew-symmetric matrix over

WHW^{t}=\diagonal\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&h_{1}\\
-h_{1}&0\end{pmatrix},\cdots,\begin{pmatrix}0&h_{s}\\
-h_{s}&0\end{pmatrix},\bf{0}_{n-2s}\right),

where

is the square matrix of zeros of dimension equals
\dime(\kere H)
, so that

and
h_{i}\mid h_{i+1}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},~~\forall\ \ 1\leq i\leq s-1
. This nonzero

(each occurs twice) are called the invariant factors of

Lemma 2.13
.

(
[
22

)

Take

, a primitive
m

\pideg(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbb{K}^{n}))=\pideg(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\mathbb{(K^{*})}^{n}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{n-\dime(\kere H)}{2}}\frac{m}{\gcdi(h_{i},m)}.

Let us mention that Haynel applied Corollary
(4.7)

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

be a maximal

-dimensional (i.e., equal to the

-torsionfree for all

4.1.
Construction of Simple Modules:

For any two subsets

and
J

[1,n]

\underline{\mu}(I,J):=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n})\in\mathbb{K}^{n}

\mu_{i}=0
iff

\underline{\gamma}(I):=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n})\in\mathbb{({K}^{*})}^{n}
such that
q_{i}\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{i-1}
if
i\in I
. Given such
\underline{\mu}(I,J)
and
\underline{\gamma}(I)
, let
M\left(\underline{\mu}(I,J),\underline{\gamma}(I)\right)
be the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space with basis consisting of

\{e(\underline{a})~|~\underline{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}),~0\leq a_{i}\leq l_{i}-1,~1\leq i\leq n\}.

Let
\epsilon_{i}(\pm 1)
denote the vector
(0,\cdots,{\pm 1},\cdots,0)
whose
i
-th coordinate is
\pm 1
and the remaining are zero. Define the
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module structure on the
\mathbb{K}
-space
M\left(\underline{\mu}(I,J),\underline{\gamma}(I)\right)

1\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})x_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mu_{i}\mathbb{A}(\underline{a};i)e(\underline{a}\oplus\epsilon_{i}(+1)),&\ i\in[1,n]\setminus I\\
\mu_{i}^{-1}\mathbb{A}(\underline{a};i)\displaystyle\frac{(q_{i}^{-a_{i}}-1)\gamma_{i-1}}{q_{i}-1}~e(\underline{a}\oplus\epsilon_{i}(-1)),&\ i\in I\setminus(I\cap J)\\
\mathbb{A}(\underline{a};i)\displaystyle\frac{(q_{i}^{-a_{i}}-1)\gamma_{i-1}}{q_{i}-1}~e(\underline{a}\oplus\epsilon_{i}(-1)),&\ a_{i}\neq 0,\ i\in I\cap J\\
0,&a_{i}=0,~i\in I\cap J\end{cases}

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})y_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mu_{i}^{-1}\mathbb{B}(\underline{a};i)\displaystyle\frac{q^{a_{i}}_{i}\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{i-1}}{q_{i}-1}~e(\underline{a}\oplus\epsilon_{i}(-1)),&\ i\in[1,n]\setminus I\\

\mathbb{B}(\underline{a};i)~e(\underline{a}\oplus\epsilon_{i}(+1)),&a_{i}\neq l_{i}-1,\ i\in I\cap J\\
0,&a_{i}=l_{i}-1,~i\in I\cap J\end{cases}

where
\gamma_{0}=1
and
\oplus

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}/l_{i}\mathbb{Z}
and the scalars
\mathbb{A}(\underline{a};i)
and
\mathbb{B}(\underline{a};i)
are given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(\underline{a};i)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&i=1\\
\displaystyle\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<i\\
s\notin I\end{subarray}}(q_{s}\lambda_{si})^{a_{s}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<i\\
s\in I\end{subarray}}(q_{s}\lambda_{si})^{-a_{s}}\right),&2\leq i\leq n\end{cases}

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(\underline{a};i)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&i=1\\
\displaystyle\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<i\\
s\notin I\end{subarray}}\lambda_{si}^{-a_{s}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<i\\
s\in I\end{subarray}}\lambda_{si}^{a_{s}}\right),&2\leq i\leq n\end{cases}

\mathbb{K}
-endomorphisms of

{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
. Indeed we can compute

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})x_{i}y_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathbb{A}(\underline{a};i)\mathbb{B}(\underline{a}\oplus\epsilon_{i}(+1);i)\displaystyle\frac{q_{i}^{a_{i}+1}\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{i-1}}{q_{i}-1}e(\underline{a}),&i\in[1,n]\setminus I\\
\mathbb{A}(\underline{a};i)\mathbb{B}(\underline{a}\oplus\epsilon_{i}(-1);i)\displaystyle\frac{(q_{i}^{-a_{i}}-1)\gamma_{i-1}}{q_{i}-1}e(\underline{a}),&a_{i}\neq 0,i\in I\\
0,&a_{i}=0,i\in I\end{cases}

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})y_{i}x_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathbb{A}(\underline{a}\oplus\epsilon_{i}(-1);i)\mathbb{B}(\underline{a};i)\displaystyle\frac{q_{i}^{a_{i}}\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{i-1}}{q_{i}-1}e(\underline{a}),&i\in[1,n]\setminus I\\
\mathbb{A}(\underline{a}\oplus\epsilon_{i}(+1);i)\mathbb{B}(\underline{a};i)\displaystyle\frac{(q_{i}^{-(a_{i}+1)}-1)\gamma_{i-1}}{q_{i}-1}e(\underline{a}),&a_{i}\neq l_{i}-1,i\in I\\
0,&a_{i}=l_{i}-1,i\in I\\
\end{cases}

so that for each
i\in[1,n]
:

e(\underline{a})(x_{i}y_{i}-q_{i}y_{i}x_{i})=\displaystyle\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<i\\
s\notin I\end{subarray}}q_{s}^{a_{s}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<i\\
s\in I\end{subarray}}q_{s}^{-a_{s}}\right)\gamma_{i-1}e(\underline{a})

e(\underline{a})\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}(q_{k}-1)y_{k}x_{k}\right)=\displaystyle\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<i\\
s\notin I\end{subarray}}q_{s}^{a_{s}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<i\\
s\in I\end{subarray}}q_{s}^{-a_{s}}\right)\gamma_{i-1}e(\underline{a}).

The remaining relations can be easily verified. Therefore the above action on the
\mathbb{K}
-space
M\left(\underline{\mu}(I,J),\underline{\gamma}(I)\right)
is well defined. Now we have the following result:

Theorem 4.2
.

The module
M\left(\underline{\mu}(I,J),\underline{\gamma}(I)\right)
is a simple
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module of dimension
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
P
be a nonzero submodule of
M\left(\underline{\mu}(I,J),\underline{\gamma}(I)\right)
. We claim that
P
contains a basis vector of the form
e(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
. Indeed, any member
p\in P
is a finite
\mathbb{K}
-linear combination of such vectors. i.e.,

p:=\sum_{\text{finite}}\lambda_{k}~e\left(a_{1}^{(k)},\cdots,a_{n}^{(k)}\right)

for some
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{K}
. Suppose there exist two non-zero coefficients, say,
\lambda_{u},\lambda_{v}
.
We can choose the smallest index
1\leq r\leq n
such that
a_{r}^{(u)}\neq a_{r}^{(v)}
in
{\mathbb{Z}}/{l_{r}\mathbb{Z}}

e\left(a_{1}^{(u)},\cdots,a_{n}^{(u)}\right)
and
e\left(a_{1}^{(v)},\cdots,a_{n}^{(v)}\right)
are eigenvectors of

associated with the eigenvalues

\Lambda_{u}=\displaystyle\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<r\\
s\notin I\end{subarray}}q_{s}^{a_{s}^{(u)}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<r\\
s\in I\end{subarray}}q_{s}^{-a_{s}^{(u)}}\right)\gamma_{r}q_{r}^{a^{(u)}_{r}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \Lambda_{v}=\displaystyle\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<r\\
s\notin I\end{subarray}}q_{s}^{a_{s}^{(v)}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<r\\
s\in I\end{subarray}}q_{s}^{-a_{s}^{(v)}}\right)\gamma_{r}q_{r}^{a^{(v)}_{r}}

respectively. We claim that

\Lambda_{u}=\Lambda_{v}\implies\gamma_{r}q_{r}^{a^{(u)}_{r}}=\gamma_{r}q_{r}^{a^{(v)}_{r}}\implies q_{r}^{\left(a_{r}^{(u)}-a_{r}^{(v)}\right)}=1\implies l_{r}\mid\left(a_{r}^{(u)}-a_{r}^{(v)}\right),

which is a contradiction.

Now
pz_{r}-\Lambda_{u}p
is a non zero element in

of smaller length than

contains a basis vector of the form
e(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
and hence

by the actions of
x_{i}
and

for all

Proposition 5.2

(1)

The element

(2)

For any

6.
The Azumaya Locus for Quantum Weyl Algebras

Assume the assumption (

⋆

‣
1
) on the defining multiparameters of
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
. Then the algebra
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is prime affine PI algebra satisfying the hypothesis (H) with
\pideg{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}
. This allows us to define the Azumaya locus of
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
, which parameterizes the simple
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-modules of maximal dimension. As
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed, it is clear from Proposition
5.1
, the maximal ideals of
Z({{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})
are of the form

\mathfrak{m}:=\langle x_{i}^{l_{i}}-\alpha_{i},y_{i}^{l_{i}}-\beta_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\rangle

for some scalars
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
. Suppose

\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}:Z({{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})\rightarrow\mathbb{K}

is the central character corresponding to
\mathfrak{m}\in Z({{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})
. Then
\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}(x_{i}^{l_{i}})=\alpha_{i}
and
\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}(y_{i}^{l_{i}})=\beta_{i}
. As each
z_{i}^{l_{i}}
is central, therefore using Proposition
5.2
, we can write

\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}(z_{i}^{l_{i}})=\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}(z_{i-1}^{l_{i}})+q_{i}^{l_{i}(l_{i}-1)/2}(q_{i}-1)^{l_{i}}\beta_{i}\alpha_{i}\ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

By Theorem
4.5
, a simple
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module
N
is maximal dimensional if and only if the action of each
z_{i}
on
N
is invertible. Therefore a simple
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module is maximal dimensional if and only if it is a simple module over
{{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
. Consequently, the Azumaya locus of
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{AL}\left({{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{m}\in\mspect Z({{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}):\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}(z_{i}^{l_{i}})\neq 0,~\forall~1\leq i\leq n\}

\displaystyle=\mspect Z({{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}).

Thus we have established the following

Theorem 6.1
.

The Azumaya locus of
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is
\mspect Z({{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})
. Moreover,
{{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is Azumaya algebra over
Z({{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})
.

7.
Alternative Quantum Weyl Algebras

Another family of multiparameter quantized Weyl algebras has been studied in the literature, including
[
3
]
. This family exhibits more symmetric defining relations compared to those of
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
. Given such
\Lambda
and
\underline{q}
, the multiparameter quantized Weyl algebra
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
of symmetric type due to M. Akhavizadegan and D. Jordan, called as
alternative quantum Weyl algebra
, is the algebra generated over the field
\mathbb{K}
by the variables
x_{1},y_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{n}
subject to the following relations:

\displaystyle y_{i}y_{j}=\lambda_{ij}y_{j}y_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ \ \ 1\leq i<j\leq n,

\displaystyle x_{i}x_{j}=\lambda_{ij}x_{j}x_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ \ \ 1\leq i<j\leq n,

\displaystyle x_{i}y_{j}=\lambda_{ij}^{-1}y_{j}x_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ \ \ 1\leq i<j\leq n,

\displaystyle y_{i}x_{j}=\lambda_{ij}^{-1}x_{j}y_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ \ \ 1\leq i<j\leq n,

\displaystyle x_{i}y_{i}-q_{i}y_{i}x_{i}=1\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ \ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

The algebra
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
has an iterated skew polynomial presentation with respect to the ordering of indices
y_{1},x_{1},\cdots,y_{n},x_{n}
of the form:

\mathbb{K}[y_{1}][x_{1},\tau_{1},\delta_{1}][y_{2},\sigma_{2}][x_{2},\tau_{2},\delta_{2}]\cdots[y_{n},\sigma_{n}][x_{n},\tau_{n},\delta_{n}]

where the
\tau_{j}
and
\sigma_{j}
are
\mathbb{K}
-linear automorphisms and the
\delta_{j}
are
\mathbb{K}
-linear
\tau_{j}
-derivations such that

\displaystyle\tau_{j}(y_{i})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{ij}y_{i},\ i<j

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}(y_{i})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{ij}^{-1}y_{i},\ i<j

\displaystyle\tau_{j}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{ij}^{-1}x_{i},\ i<j

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{ij}x_{i},\ i<j

\displaystyle\tau_{j}(y_{j})

\displaystyle=q_{j}y_{j},\ \forall~j

\displaystyle\delta_{j}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\delta_{j}(y_{i})=0,\ i<j

\displaystyle\delta_{j}(y_{j})

\displaystyle=1,\ \forall~j

Using the skew Hilbert basis theorem, we can conclude that the algebra
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is an affine noetherian domain. Moreover, the family

\{y_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{a_{n}}x_{n}^{b_{n}}~:~a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}

is a
\mathbb{K}
-basis of
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
. Similar to the quantum Weyl algebra, in the context of the alternative quantum Weyl algebra, we define
z_{0}=1
and

z_{i}=x_{i}y_{i}-y_{i}x_{i}\ \ \forall\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

These elements will also play a crucial role in this section. It can be easily verified that for all
1\leq i\leq n
,

{z}_{i}=1+(q_{i}-1)y_{i}x_{i}=q_{i}^{-1}\left(1+(q_{i}-1)x_{i}y_{i}\right).

The following proposition is well known (cf.
[
3
]
) for the algebra
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
and can be proved using the defining relations.

Proposition 7.1
.

Direct computations yield the following results for
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
.

(1)

For all
1\leq i\leq n
,
z_{i}
is a normal element of
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
. More precisely, we have:

(a)

For all

(b)

(7.5)

\pideg({\mathcal{A}_{n-1}^{\underline{q}(r),\Lambda(r)}})\leq\pideg({\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}/\langle z_{r}\rangle)

As a consequence of (
7.1
), we can establish that

(7.6)

\pideg({\mathcal{A}_{n-1}^{\underline{q}(r),\Lambda(r)}})=\displaystyle\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq r\end{subarray}}^{n}l_{i}.

Consequently, we have established the following

Theorem 7.7
.

Assuming the root of unity assumption (

⋆

1
) on the defining multiparameters, the PI degree of a prime factor of
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}
by the normal element
z_{r}
is given by
\displaystyle\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq r\end{subarray}}^{n}l_{i}.

Question:
In view of Proposition
2.9
, there exists a
z_{r}

\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\

for each

. It would be interesting to explore the construction of such simple modules and subsequently classify them. So let us give it a try!

7.3.
Classification of Maximal Dimensional Simple Modules

Here we classify all maximal dimensional simple
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-modules. As a direct consequence of Theorem
7.3
, we can derive the following result, establishing a significant connection between the simple modules of both versions of quantum Weyl algebras.

Proposition 7.8
.

There is a a one-to-one correspondence between the simple
z_{i}
-torsionfree
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-modules and the simple
z_{i}
-torsionfree
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-modules.

In light of this proposition along with Theorem
4.4
, we can classify simple
z_{i}
-torsionfree
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}
-modules. In particular, each
z_{i}
-torsionfree for all
1\leq i\leq n
simple
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module is isomorphic to a simple
{{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module
M\left(\underline{\mu}(I,J),\underline{\gamma}(I)\right)
. Moreover, the
\mathbb{K}
-dimension of each simple
z_{i}
-torsionfree
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module is equal to
\pideg{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
which is maximal.

Next, we aim to prove that each maximal dimensional (i.e., equal to the
\pideg{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
) simple
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module is
z_{i}
-torsionfree for all
1\leq i\leq n
. If possible let
M
be a maximal dimensional simple
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module which is
z_{r}
-torsion for some
1\leq r\leq n
. As
z_{r}
is a normal element then
M
becomes a simple module over the factor algebra
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}/\langle z_{r}\rangle
. Hence by Proposition
2.9
along with Theorem

, we obtain

\dime_{\mathbb{K}}(M)\leq\pideg({\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}/\langle z_{r}\rangle)<\pideg({\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}).

This is a contradiction.

Consequently, we have achieved a comprehensive classification of maximal dimensional simple
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-modules. In particular, we have established the following.

Theorem 7.9
.

A simple
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module

is
z_{i}
-torsionfree for all
1\leq i\leq n
if and only if it is maximal dimensional, i.e.,
\dime_{\mathbb{K}}(M)=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}
.

7.4.
The Center and Azumaya Locus for
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}

In this subsection, we describe
Z({\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})
and determine the Azumaya locus. By the isomorphism
\theta
in Theorem
7.3
, we have

(7.7)

Z({\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})=\theta^{-1}(Z({{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}))=\theta^{-1}(\mathbb{K}[x_{1}^{l_{1}},y_{1}^{l_{1}},\cdots,x_{n}^{l_{n}},y_{n}^{l_{n}}][S_{c}^{-1}]).

From the definition of
\theta
, it follows that

\theta^{-1}(x_{i}^{l_{i}})=(z_{1}\cdots z_{i-1}x_{i})^{l_{i}},\ \theta^{-1}(y_{i}^{l_{i}})=y_{i}^{l_{i}},\ \theta^{-1}(z_{i}^{l_{i}})=(z_{1}\cdots z_{i-1}z_{i})^{l_{i}}

and
\mathcal{S}_{c}:=\theta^{-1}(S_{c})
is also a multiplicative set in
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
contained in the multiplicative set
T_{c}
generated by the
z_{i}^{l_{i}},\ 1\leq i\leq n
. Now applying
\theta^{-1}
in the identity of Proposition
5.2
, we obtain the following identity for the central element
z_{i}^{l_{i}}
in
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}

(7.8)

z_{i}^{l_{i}}=1+q_{i}^{l_{i}(l_{i}-1)/2}(q_{i}-1)^{l_{i}}y_{i}^{l_{i}}x_{i}^{l_{i}},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n

Therefore from (
7.7
),

\displaystyle Z({\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{K}[\theta^{-1}(x_{i}^{l_{i}}),\theta^{-1}(y_{i}^{l_{i}}):1\leq i\leq n][\mathcal{S}_{c}^{-1}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{K}[x_{i}^{l_{i}},y_{i}^{l_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n][T_{c}^{-1}].

Thus the center of
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}
is

Z(\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda})=Z({\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})\cap\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}=\mathbb{K}[x_{i}^{l_{i}},y_{i}^{l_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n].

As
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed, the maximal ideals of
Z({\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})
are of the form

\mathfrak{m}:=\langle x_{i}^{l_{i}}-\alpha_{i},y_{i}^{l_{i}}-\beta_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\rangle

for some scalars
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
. Suppose

\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}:Z({\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})\rightarrow\mathbb{K}

is the central character corresponding to
\mathfrak{m}\in Z({\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})
. Then
\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}(x_{i}^{l_{i}})=\alpha_{i}
and
\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}(y_{i}^{l_{i}})=\beta_{i}
. Hence from (
7.8
), we can write

\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}(z_{i}^{l_{i}})=1+q_{i}^{l_{i}(l_{i}-1)/2}(q_{i}-1)^{l_{i}}\beta_{i}\alpha_{i}\ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

By Theorem
7.9
, a simple
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
-module
N
is maximal dimensional if and only if the action of each
z_{i}
on
N
is invertible. Consequently, the Azumaya locus of
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{AL}\left({\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{m}\in\mspect Z({\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}):\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}(z_{i}^{l_{i}})\neq 0,~\forall~1\leq i\leq n\}

\displaystyle=\mspect Z({\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}).

Thus we have established the following

Theorem 7.10
.

The center of
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}
is

\mathbb{K}[x_{i}^{l_{i}},y_{i}^{l_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n]
and the Azumaya locus of
{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is
\mspect Z({\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
is Azumaya algebra over
Z({\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}})
.

Finally, it is worth mentioning that the Azumaya property of the algebra
{\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\underline{q},\Lambda}}
can also be established by utilizing the isomorphism presented in Theorem
7.3
along with Theorem
6.1
.

8.
Concluding Remarks

In conclusion, this chapter provides significant contributions to the study of multiparameter quantized algebras at roots of unity. We have achieved an explicit expression for the PI degree and the center, as well as a comprehensive classification of the maximal dimensional simple modules. Consequently, we have obtained the Azumaya locus of the multiparameter quantized algebras at roots of unity.

It is worth noting that recent studies by Brown and Yakimov
[
5
]
have shed light on the connection between the Azumaya locus
\mathcal{AL}(R)
and the discriminant ideal
D_{l}
of a prime PI algebra
R
. Specifically, in their main theorem
[
5
, Main Theorem]
, they established that the zero set of
D_{l}
is the complement of the Azumaya locus of
R
, in the case when
l
is the square of the PI degree of
R
. As an application, the classification of the Azumaya locus of the multiparameter quantized algebras at roots of unity was obtained in
[
5
, Theorem 6.2]
by determining its discriminant ideal.

Thus, in particular, this chapter provides an alternative approach to determine the Azumaya locus of the multiparameter quantized Weyl algebras without explicitly finding its discriminant ideal.

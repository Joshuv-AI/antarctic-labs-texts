Euler Characteristics and their Congruences in the Positive Rank Setting
Anwesh Ray, R. Sujatha

Abstract
. The notion of the truncated Euler characteristic for Iwasawa modules is an extension of the notion of the usual Euler characteristic to the case when the homology groups are not finite. This article explores congruence relations between the truncated Euler characteristics for dual Selmer groups of elliptic curves with isomorphic residual representations, over admissible p p -adic Lie extensions. Our results extend earlier congruence results from the case of elliptic curves with rank zero to the case of higher rank elliptic curves. The results provide evidence for the p p -adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer formula without assuming the main conjecture.



1.
Introduction

Iwasawa theoretic invariants for modules arising from the Iwasawa theory of ordinary Galois representations
provide key insights into the arithmetic of such objects. Of particular interest is the behaviour of these invariants when one considers two ordinary Galois representations whose associated residual representations are isomorphic. Greenberg and Vatsal
[
8
]
initiated such a study for elliptic curves, and this was developed further in the works of Emerton-Pollack-Weston
[
7
]
. Similar investigations were carried out in the context of non-commutative Iwasawa theory in, for instance,
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
.

In all the works referenced above, congruences between the corresponding
L
-values and Euler characteristics of dual Selmer groups of elliptic curves were established over special
p
-adic Lie extensions. A fundamental hypothesis when considering Euler characteristics was that the Euler characteristic was defined, which often entailed the assumption of finiteness of the dual Selmer group over the ground field. This article sets out to explore possible thematic generalizations of such congruence results when this finiteness hypothesis is removed. The natural substitute for the Euler characteristic is the truncated Euler characteristic as considered in
[
19
]
for the cyclotomic extensions. This definition was generalized to a broader class of admissible
p
-adic Lie extensions in
[
4
]
and
[
12
]
. It is striking that the congruence results extend to the rank one case, and to more general
p
-adic Lie-extensions. This leads us to believe that the truncated Euler characteristic is also an intrinsic arithmetic invariant in non-commutative Iwasawa theory.

This paper consists of five sections including this introduction. In section 2, we set up notation and requisite preliminaries. Section 3 proves the congruence results in the case of the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension and section 4 establishes similar congruence results in the setting of admissible, non-commutative
p
-adic Lie extensions. More specifically, we prove our results for false Tate curve extensions and
\text{GL}_{2}
extensions. In section 5, we discuss explicit numerical examples which demonstrate that our results are optimal.

Throughout, let
p\geq 5
be a prime. Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be two elliptic curves over the field
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers. Let
\rho_{i}:\text{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be the Galois representation on the
p
-adic Tate module of
E_{i}
and
E_{i}[p]
denote the Galois module of the
p
-torsion points of the elliptic
E_{i}
. Denote by
\bar{\rho}_{i}:\text{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
the residual Galois representation. Let
N_{i}
be the level of
E_{i}
and set
N=N_{1}N_{2}
. Assume that

(1)

E_{1}
and
E_{2}
both have the same algebraic rank
g
,

(2)

the
p
-adic Galois representations

\rho_{1}\equiv\rho_{2}\mod{p}
,

(3)

the residual Galois representations
\bar{\rho}_{i}
are irreducible,

(4)

E_{1}
and
E_{2}
both have good ordinary reduction at
p
,

(5)

\Sh(E_{i}/\mathbb{Q})[p]
is finite for
i=1,2
,

(6)

the
p
-adic height pairings on
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are non-degenerate.

Conditions
(\ref{five})
and
(\ref{six})
are always expected to be true. Let
\mathbb{Q}^{cyc}
denote the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and put
\Gamma:=\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{cyc}/\mathbb{Q})
. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}
be an admissible
p
-adic Lie-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
G:=\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
. Let
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E_{i})
(resp.
\chi_{t}(G,E_{i})
) denote the truncated Euler characteristic of
E_{i}
with respect to
\Gamma
(resp.
G
), see section 2 for the definition.

The main conjecture of Iwasawa theory relates the algebraic invariants attached to the dual Selmer group of an elliptic curve with the
p
-adic L-function, which interpolates values of the complex L-functions. Vatsal
[
18
]
and Greenberg-Vatsal
[
8
]
study congruence properties for complex
L
-values as well as for the
p
-adic L-functions attached to
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. On the algebraic side, the main conjecture in conjunction with work of Schneider
[
15
]
and Perrin-Riou would then predict congruences for the Euler characteristic of the Selmer group.

For an elliptic curve
E
with algebraic rank zero, denote the Euler characteristic of the Selmer group of
E
over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension by
\chi(\Gamma,E)
(cf.
[
5
, chapter 3]
). When
E
has algebraic rank zero, the Euler-characteristic
\chi(\Gamma,E)
coincides with the truncated Euler-characteristic
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E)
. Shekhar and the second author
[
16
]
deduced congruence results for the Euler characteristic of pairs of congruent elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
of rank zero. In particular, they deduce that if
\chi(\Gamma,E_{1})=1
, then
\chi(\Gamma,E_{2})=1
. Let
m
be a
p
-power free integer coprime to
p
as well as to the conductors of both elliptic curves. For the false Tate curve extension defined by
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},m^{\frac{1}{p^{\infty}}})
it is shown in
[
16
, Theorem 3.4]
if
\chi(G,E_{1})=1
then so is
\chi(G,E_{2})=1
. In this article, analogous results for truncated Euler characteristics in the positive rank setting are proved. We stress that our methods are different from those in
[
16
]
, and hence yield another proof of the results in the rank zero case as well. Generalizations of the results in this paper to modular forms and abelian varieties over arbitrary number fields are currently being investigated.

The
p
-adic version of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture has been studied, for instance in
[
1
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
15
]
. The results in this paper for the truncated Euler characteristic yield interesting consequences for the
p
-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
for which
E[p]
is irreducible as a Galois module. The
p
-adic
L
-function, denoted by
\mathcal{L}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)
, is a power series in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]
. The characteristic series of the dual Selmer group of
E
is denoted by
f_{E}^{alg}(T)
. The main conjecture predicts that
\mathcal{L}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)
coincides with
f_{E}^{alg}(T)
up to a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]
. The
p
-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture predicts that the order of vanishing of
\mathcal{L}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)
at
T=0
is equal to the rank of
E(\mathbb{Q})
and that there is an exact formula for the leading coefficient of
\mathcal{L}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)
. The leading coefficient is predicted to be equal to
p^{-g}\rho_{p}(E)
, where

\rho_{p}(E):=\frac{R_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})\times\#(\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[p])}{\#(E(\mathbb{Q})[p])^{2}}\times\tau(E)\times\#\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{p})^{2}.

(see Proposition
2.3
). In the above formula,
R_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})
is the
p
-adic regulator of
E
, defined as the determinant of the
p
-adic height pairing studied in
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
. When the rank of
E(\mathbb{Q})
is equal to zero, the
p
-adic regulator is set to be equal to
1
. When the rank of
E(\mathbb{Q})
is positive, the
p
-adic regulator is conjectured to be nonzero. The term
\mathcal{\tau}(E):=\prod_{l}c_{l}
where
c_{l}
is the index of
E_{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{l})
in
E(\mathbb{Q}_{l})
, with
E_{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{l})
being the subgroup of
l
-adic points with non-singular reduction modulo
l
. The term
\tilde{E}
is the reduced curve at
p
.

It is shown by Perrin-Riou and Schneider that if the
p
-adic regulator of
E
is not zero, then the order of vanishing of
f_{E}^{alg}(T)
is equal to the rank of
E(\mathbb{Q})
and the leading term of
f_{E}^{alg}(T)
is equal to
p^{-g}\rho_{p}(E)
, up to a
p
-adic unit. As a consequence, the
p
-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for
E
follows from the main conjecture for
E
. This is where the truncated Euler characteristic intervenes. The truncated Euler-characteristic
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E)
is equal to the leading coefficient of
f_{E}^{alg}(T)
up to a unit (see
[
19
, Lemma 2.11]
). As a consequence of the results of Schneider and Perrin-Riou mentioned above,
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E)=p^{-g}\rho_{p}(E)
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves satisfying conditions
(\ref{one})
to
(\ref{six})
. In particular,
E_{1}[p]
and
E_{2}[p]
are isomorphic as Galois representations. We show that there is an explicit relationship between
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E_{1})
and
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E_{2})
(see Theorem
3.3
) which further relates
p^{-g}\rho_{p}(E_{1})
and
p^{-g}\rho_{p}(E_{2})
(see Corollory
3.4
). Next we consider the coefficient of
T^{g}
of the
p
-adic L-function
\mathcal{L}(E_{i}/\mathbb{Q},T)
which is given by

\mathcal{L}^{(g)}(E_{i}/\mathbb{Q},0):=\frac{1}{g!}\left(\frac{d^{g}}{dT^{g}}\mathcal{L}(E_{i}/\mathbb{Q},T)\right)_{\restriction T=0}.

The
p
-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture predicts that
\mathcal{L}^{(g)}(E_{i}/\mathbb{Q},0)=p^{-g}\rho_{p}(E_{i})
. Using the results of Perrin-Riou and Schneider, which related the latter term
p^{-g}\rho_{p}(E_{i})
to the truncated Euler-characteristic, our results would translate under the
p
-adic Birch Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, to a congruence relation between
\mathcal{L}^{(g)}(E_{1}/\mathbb{Q},0)
and
\mathcal{L}^{(g)}(E_{2}/\mathbb{Q},0)
. However, we show that this relationship is indeed satisfied independent of the
p
-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture (see Theorem
3.7
), thereby providing evidence for the conjecture.

We provide a sketch of the methods used in this manuscript. The set of primes
\Sigma_{0}
is chosen to consist of exactly those primes at which
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
have bad reduction. One may choose a larger set, however, results are optimal for this set. Greenberg and Vatsal in
[
8
]
consider the
\Sigma_{0}
-imprimitive Selmer group over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, which we denote by
\operatorname{Sel}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
(see section
2
for the definition). Set
f_{E_{i},\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}(T)
for the characteristic series of
\operatorname{Sel}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E_{i}/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
and denote by
\mu_{E_{i},\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}
and
\lambda_{E_{i},\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}
the
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants of
f_{E_{i},\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}(T)
respectively. Greenberg and Vatsal show that

\mu_{E_{1},\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}=0\Leftrightarrow\mu_{E_{2},\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}=0\text{, and if }\mu_{E_{1},\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}=\mu_{E_{2},\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}=0\text{, then }\lambda_{E_{1},\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}=\lambda_{E_{2},\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}

(see the discussion on
[
8
, pp. 43]
preceding Remark (2.10)). Using these relations, it becomes possible to deduce that the coefficient of
T^{g}
in
f_{E_{1},\Sigma_{0}}(T)
is
p
-adic unit if and only if that of
T^{g}
in
f_{E_{2},\Sigma_{0}}(T)
is a
p
-adic unit (see the proofs of Corollory
2.7
and Theorem
3.3
). By the results of Schneider and Perrin-Riou discussed earlier, the order of vanishing of
f_{E_{i}}^{alg}(T)
at
T=0
is
g
, and its leading coefficient is equal to
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E_{i})
, up to a
p
-adic unit. Setting
\Phi_{E_{i}}:=\prod_{l\in\Sigma_{0}}|L_{l}(E_{i},1)|_{p}
, we then compare
\Phi_{E_{i}}\times\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E_{i})
for the two elliptic curves.

Our results are proved independent of the main conjecture. Note that the main conjecture is known in this setting for
E_{i}
if there is a prime
q\neq p
such that
q||N_{i}
and the residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{E_{i},p}
on
E_{i}[p]
is ramified at
q
. This is the celebrated theorem of Skinner and Urban
[
17
]
. We stress that this additional condition is not imposed in proving our results. Leveraging results proved for the cyclotomic extension allows us to prove analogous results for other
p
-adic Lie-extensions (Theorem
4.3
for the false-Tate extension and Theorem
4.5
for certain
\text{GL}_{2}
-extensions).

2.
Preliminaries

Let
p
be a prime number and assume that
p\geq 5
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at the prime
p
and
E_{p^{\infty}}
denote the Galois-module of
p
-power division points on
E
. Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of primes containing
p
and the primes at which
E
has bad reduction.

2.1.
Selmer groups and their Characteristic Polynomials

Denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}
the maximal extension of
\mathbb{Q}
in which all primes
l\notin\Sigma
are unramified, and denote by
\text{G}_{\mathbb{Q},\Sigma}
the Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
L\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}
be a subfield, we shall be concerned with the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension or an admissible
p
-adic Lie extension. Set
\text{G}_{\Sigma}(L):=\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/L)
, the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}(E/L):=\text{ker}\lambda_{\Sigma}(L)
. Here
\lambda_{\Sigma}(L)
is the localization map

\lambda_{\Sigma}(L):H^{1}\left(\text{G}_{\Sigma}(L),E_{p^{\infty}}\right)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{l\in\Sigma}\mathcal{H}_{l}(L),

where
\mathcal{H}_{l}(L)
is taken as in
[
8
, pp. 23]
.
Let
\Sigma_{0}\subseteq\Sigma
be a subset of primes which contains the primes at which
E
has bad reduction and does not contain
p
. In section
3
, we shall specialize
\Sigma_{0}
further. The
\Sigma_{0}
-imprimitive (or non-primitive) Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/L)
is the kernel of the localization map

\lambda_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma_{0}}(L):H^{1}\left(G_{\Sigma}(L),E_{p^{\infty}}\right)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{l\in\Sigma\backslash\Sigma_{0}}\mathcal{H}_{l}(L).

Let
G
be a profinite group, the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda(G)
is defined as

\Lambda(G):=\varprojlim_{U}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]

where the inverse limit is taken with respect to open subgroups of
G
. Put
\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{cyc}/\mathbb{Q})
and set
\Lambda
to be equal to the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda(\Gamma)
. Note that for any extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
, finite or infinite, the Selmer groups
\operatorname{Sel}(E/L)
and
\operatorname{Sel}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/L)
are discrete modules over the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}))
. Denote by

X(E/L):=\text{Hom}\left(\operatorname{Sel}(E/L),\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)

X^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/L):=\text{Hom}\left(\operatorname{Sel}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/L),\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)

the compact Pontrjagin duals of the Selmer and
\Sigma_{0}
-imprimitive Selmer groups respectively. It follows from a deep theorem of Kato
[
10
]
that the dual Selmer group
X(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
is a torsion
\Lambda
-module. This is equivalent to the localization map
\lambda_{\Sigma}(\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
being surjective (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
). It is an easy result that
X(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
is a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module. By the classification theorem of finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-modules, there is a pseudo-isomorphism

X(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})\sim\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda/(f_{i}(T)^{a_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\Lambda/p^{\mu_{j}}\right).

Here, one identifies
\Lambda
with the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]
after making a choice of a topological generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
and letting
T:=\gamma-1
. The
f_{i}(T)
are irreducible monic polynomials all of whose non-leading coefficients are divisible by
p
. Such polynomials are called distinguished polynomials. The algebraic Iwasawa invariants are defined by

\lambda_{E}^{alg}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\operatorname{deg}f_{i}\text{, and }\mu_{E}^{alg}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu_{j}.

The characteristic polynomial of
X(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
is defined by

f_{E}^{alg}(T)=p^{\mu_{E}^{alg}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(T)^{a_{i}}.

It is the unique element generating the characteristic ideal of
X(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
which is a power of
p
times a distinguished polynomial. It is expected that when
E
is an elliptic curve for which
E[p]
is irreducible, then
\mu_{E}^{alg}=0
. It is well known that the condition
\mu_{E}^{alg}=0
depends only on the residual representation
E[p]
(see
[
8
, pp. 19]
). That is, if two elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
have isomorphic residual representation at
p
, then
\mu_{E_{1}}^{alg}=0
if and only if
\mu_{E_{2}}^{alg}=0
. The
\lambda
-invariant
\lambda_{E}^{alg}
need not be zero and moreover, need not depend on the residual representation (see
[
8
, pp. 22]
). We define Iwasawa invariants
\mu_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}
and
\lambda_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}
associated to the
\Sigma_{0}
-imprimitive dual Selmer group
X^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
. For
l\in\Sigma_{0}
, let
h_{E}^{(l)}(T)
be the characteristic polynomial of the Pontryajin dual of the
\mathcal{H}_{l}(\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
. Let
\sigma_{E}^{(l)}
be such that
\mathcal{H}_{l}(\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})\simeq(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\sigma_{E}^{(l)}}
as in
[
8
, pp. 23]
. The characteristic polynomial of the
\Lambda
-module
\operatorname{Sel}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
is denoted by
f_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}(T)
. There is a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})\rightarrow\prod_{l\in\Sigma_{0}}\mathcal{H}_{l}(\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})\rightarrow 0.

As a result,

f_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}(T)=f_{E}^{alg}(T)\prod_{l\in\Sigma_{0}}h_{E}^{(l)}(T).

Letting
\lambda_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}
denote the
\lambda
-invariant of
X^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
. It is related to
\lambda_{E}^{alg}
according to the formula
[
8
, equation (7)]

\lambda_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}=\lambda_{E}^{alg}+\sum_{l\in\Sigma_{0}}\sigma_{E}^{(l)}.

2.2.
p
-adic L-functions

There is a similar story for
p
-adic L-functions. Let
g
denote the algebraic rank of
E
and
\Omega_{E}
denote the the real Néron period of
E
. For any even Dirichlet character
\rho
, it is well known that
L(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,1)/\Omega_{E}\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Recall that in the previous subsection, a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
has been fixed. Let
\mu_{p^{\infty}}
denote the Galois module of
p
-torsion roots of unity. Given
\xi\in\mu_{p^{\infty}}
, let
m
be the integer such that the order of
\xi
is
p^{m-1}
. Associate to
\xi
a character
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\mu_{p^{\infty}}
of finite order defined by
\rho(\gamma)=\xi
. Let
\alpha_{p}\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
denote the unit root of the characteristic polynomial
1-a_{p}(E)X+pX^{2}
. Mazur and Swinnerton-Dyer associate to
E
an element
\mathcal{L}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)\in\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
satisfying the property

\mathcal{L}(E/\mathbb{Q},\xi-1)=\tau(\rho^{-1})\cdot\alpha_{p}^{-m}\frac{L(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,1)}{\Omega_{E}}.

Here,
\tau(\rho^{-1})
denotes the Gauss-sum of the character
\rho^{-1}
. Let
\chi
be the
p
-adic cyclotomic character and
let
\kappa=\chi_{\restriction\Gamma}:\Gamma\xrightarrow{\sim}(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. The
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}(E,s)
is defined by

L_{p}(E,s)=\mathcal{L}(E/\mathbb{Q},\kappa(\gamma)^{1-s}-1).

Set

L_{p}^{(g)}(E,s):=\frac{1}{g!}\left(\frac{d}{ds}\right)^{g}L_{p}(E,s),

likewise, set

\mathcal{L}^{(g)}(E/\mathbb{Q},T):=\frac{1}{g!}\left(\frac{d}{dT}\right)^{g}\mathcal{L}^{(g)}(E/\mathbb{Q},T).

Let
P_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,T)
be the local-factor defined as follows

P_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,T)=\begin{cases}1-a_{l}\rho(l)T+l\rho(l)^{2}T^{2}\text{ if }l\text{ is a prime of good reduction,}\\
1-a_{l}\rho(l)T\text{ if }E\text{ has bad reduction at }l.\end{cases}

If
l
is a prime of bad reduction then

a_{l}=\begin{cases}1\text{ if }E\text{ has split multiplicative reduction},\\
-1\text{ if }E\text{ has non-split multiplicative reduction},\\
0\text{ if }E\text{ has additive reduction.}\end{cases}

When
\rho
is the trivial character it is dropped from the notation. The local Euler factor
L_{l}(E,s)
at
l
is equal to
L_{l}(E,s):=P_{l}(E,l^{-s})^{-1}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)\in\Lambda
be defined by the relation

\mathcal{P}_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},\xi-1)=P_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,l^{-1})
. The non-primitive
L
-function is

L^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,s)=L(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,s)\prod_{l\in\Sigma_{0}}P_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,l^{-s}).

The function
\mathcal{L}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)
is modified to a non-primitive version
\mathcal{L}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)
for which

\mathcal{L}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/\mathbb{Q},\xi-1)=\tau(\rho^{-1})\alpha_{p}^{-m}\frac{L^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho,1)}{\Omega_{E}}

(cf.
[
8
]
and
[
11
]
).
For
l\in\Sigma_{0}
, let
\gamma_{l}
denote the Frobenius automorphism of
l
in
\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
. Factor
P_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)=(1-\alpha_{l}T)(1-\beta_{l}T)
. Consider the power series
\mathcal{P}_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]
corresponding to

\mathcal{P}_{l}:=\left(1-\alpha_{l}l^{-1}\gamma_{l}\right)\left(1-\beta_{l}l^{-1}\gamma_{l}\right)\in\Lambda.

Observe that
\mathcal{P}_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},0)=P_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},l^{-1})=L_{l}(E,1)^{-1}
. The following relationship is satisfied

(2.1)

\mathcal{L}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)=\mathcal{L}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)\prod_{l\in\Sigma_{0}}\mathcal{P}_{l}(E/\mathbb{Q},T).

It is well known that if
E[p]
is irreducible as a Galois module, then
\mathcal{L}(E,T)\in\Lambda
(see
[
8
, Proposition 3.7]
). By the Weierstrass Preparation theorem,

\mathcal{L}(E/\mathbb{Q},T)=p^{\mu}\cdot u(T)g(T)

where
u(T)
is a unit and
g(T)
a distinguished polynomial. Let
\mu_{E}^{anal}:=\mu
as above and
f_{E}^{anal}(T):=p^{\mu_{E}^{anal}}g(T)
. Let
\lambda_{E}^{anal}
denote the degree of
f_{E}^{anal}(T)
. The main conjecture states that
f_{E}^{alg}(T)=f_{E}^{anal}(T)
. Under additional hypotheses, this has been proved by Skinner and Urban
[
17
]
. In this manuscript we do not assume these additional hypotheses and the results are independent of the celebrated theorem of Skinner-Urban.

One has analogous definitions for analytic invariants associated to the
\Sigma_{0}
-imprimitive
p
-adic L-function
\mathcal{L}^{\Sigma_{0}}(E,T)
. For
l\in\Sigma_{0}
, it is the case that
\mathcal{P}_{l}
generates the characteristic ideal of the Pontryajin dual of
\mathcal{H}_{l}(\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})

[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
. It follows from the relation
2.1
that

\lambda_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{anal}=\lambda_{E}^{anal}+\sum_{l\in\Sigma_{0}}\sigma_{E}^{(l)}.

Greenberg and Vatsal
[
8
, Theorem 1.4]
show that if
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are two elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
which are congruent modulo
p
, then if the equalities

\mu_{E_{1}}^{alg}=\mu_{E_{1}}^{anal}=0\text{, and }\lambda_{E_{1}}^{alg}=\lambda_{E_{1}}^{anal}

hold, then so do the equalities

\mu_{E_{2}}^{alg}=\mu_{E_{2}}^{anal}=0

and

\lambda_{E_{2}}^{alg}=\lambda_{E_{2}}^{anal}.

Kato showed that the polynomial
f_{E}^{alg}(T)
always divides
f_{E}^{anal}(T)
. As a result, if the main conjecture is true for

and the
\mu
-invariant for

is zero, then the same is true for

.

2.3.
Generalized Euler Characteristics

Recall that an admissible
p
-adic Lie-extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension for which

•

G=\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to a compact
p
-adic Lie group,

•

all but finitely many primes are unramified in
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
,

•

\mathbb{Q}^{\text{cyc}}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
,

•

G
does not contain an element of order
p
.

Set
H=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}^{\text{cyc}})
and note that
G/H\simeq\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\text{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q})
. The Euler characteristic
\chi(\Gamma,E):=\chi(\Gamma,\operatorname{Sel}(E/\mathbb{Q}^{\text{cyc}}))
. When
E
has positive rank, the Euler characteristic is not defined. The natural analog is the truncated Euler characteristic which we define below. For any homomorphism of abelian groups
f:M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
, denote by
\operatorname{ker}f
and
\operatorname{cok}f
the kernel of
f
and the cokernel of
f
respectively.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
D
be a discrete
p
-primary
\Gamma
-module let
\phi_{D}
be the natural map

\phi_{D}:H^{0}\left(\Gamma,D\right)=D^{\Gamma}\rightarrow D_{\Gamma}\simeq H^{1}\left(\Gamma,D\right)

for which
\phi_{D}(x)
is the residue class of
x
in
D_{\Gamma}
. If both
\operatorname{ker}(\phi_{D})
and
\operatorname{cok}(\phi_{D})
are finite
D
is said to have finite truncated Euler-characteristic. In this case, the truncated Euler characteristic is defined by

\chi_{t}\left(\Gamma,D\right):=\frac{\#\text{ker}(\phi_{D})}{\#\text{cok}(\phi_{D})}.

The truncated
G
-Euler characteristic is a variation of the above definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
D
be a discrete
p
-primary
G
-module, define

\psi_{D}:H^{0}(\Gamma,D^{H})\rightarrow H^{1}(G,D)

as the composite of the natural map

\phi_{D^{H}}:H^{0}(\Gamma,D^{H})\rightarrow H^{1}(\Gamma,D^{H})

with the inflation map
H^{1}(\Gamma,D^{H})\rightarrow H^{1}(G,D)
. The module
D
has
finite truncated

-Euler characteristic
if both
\operatorname{ker}(\psi_{D})
and
\operatorname{cok}(\psi_{D})
are finite and the truncated
G
-Euler characteristic is then defined by

\chi_{t}(G,D):=\frac{\#\operatorname{ker}(\psi_{D})}{\#\operatorname{cok}(\psi_{D})}.

Denote by

\begin{split}&\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E)=\chi_{t}\left(\Gamma,\operatorname{Sel}(E,\mathbb{Q}^{\text{cyc}})\right)\\
&\chi_{t}(G,E)=\chi_{t}\left(G,\operatorname{Sel}(E,\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\right).\end{split}

The truncated Euler characteristic
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E)
is, up to a
p
-adic unit, the leading term of the
p
-adic L-function

(cf. Theorem
3.5
) and is therefore a
p
-adic integer. Let
\tilde{E}
denote the reduced curve at
p
. For a prime
l
, we let

and set

. Two
p
-adic integers
a\sim b
if

and

are not zero and
a/b
is a
p
-adic unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. In the rank zero case, if
\operatorname{Sel}(E/\mathbb{Q})
is finite, the Euler characteristic

is related to the Birch Swinnerton-Dyer exact formula as follows

(2.2)

\chi(\Gamma,E)\sim\frac{\#\left(\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[p]\right)}{\#\left(E(\mathbb{Q})[p]\right)^{2}}\times\tau(E)\times\#\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{p})^{2}.

In the positive rank case, the
p
-adic height pairing (cf.
[
14
]
and
[

]
) plays a role. For an elliptic curve

over
\mathbb{Q}
with positive rank, the

-adic height pairing is conjectured to be non-degenerate (cf.
[
15
]
). The

-adic regulator

is the determinant of the

-adic height pairing.

.

(cf.

]
). These conditions are imposed on the elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. The truncated Euler characteristic is related to the characteristic series

. The following is a direct consequence of
[

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that

(1)

E

(2)

(3)

(4)

.

In particular if

Definition 2.5
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\Sigma_{0}
a set of primes containing the primes at which

has bad reduction and not containing
p
. Set
\Phi_{E}:=\prod_{l\in\Sigma_{0}}|L_{l}(E,1)|_{p}
. Here,
|L_{l}(E,1)|_{p}
is set to be equal to

if

.

Writing

, the following is a direct consequence of Lemma

.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve satisfying the conditions of Lemma
2.4
. Then

\Phi_{E}\times\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E)=|g_{E,\Sigma_{0}}(0)|_{p}^{-1}.

In the above

is taken to be

if

.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve satisfying the conditions of Lemma
2.4
. Then
\Phi_{E}\times\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E)=1
if and only if
\mu_{E}^{alg}=0
and
\lambda_{E,\Sigma_{0}}=g
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Phi_{E}\times\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E)=1
, then
g_{E,\Sigma_{0}}(T)
is a unit. As a result,
f_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}(T)
is
T^{g}
times a unit. It follows that
\mu_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}=0
and that
f_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}(T)
is a distinguished polynomial. As a result,
f_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}(T)=T^{g}
and in particular,
\lambda_{E,\Sigma_{0}}=g
. Finally, we point out that since each factor
\mathcal{P}_{l}
is not divisible by

and consequently,
\mu_{E}^{alg}=\mu_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}=0
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mu_{E}^{alg}=0
and that
\lambda_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}=g
. Since

is divisible by

it follows that
f_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}(T)=p^{m}T^{g}
, where

. However,
\mu_{E,\Sigma_{0}}^{alg}=\mu_{E}^{alg}
and the result follows.
∎

This section proves congruences for truncated

and

.

and

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
E

The inflation map fits the inflation-restriction sequence

E_{1}
and
E_{2}
satisfying conditions
(\ref{one})
to
(\ref{six})
, it is the case that
\Phi_{E_{1}}\times\chi_{t}(\Gamma^{\prime},E_{1})=1
if and only if
\Phi_{E_{2}}\times\chi_{t}(\Gamma^{\prime},E_{2})=1
.
If in addition condition
(\star)
is satisfied for
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, then
\chi_{t}(\Gamma^{\prime},E_{1})=1
if and only if
\chi_{t}(\Gamma^{\prime},E_{2})=1
.

4.
Congruences of
G
-Euler Characteristics

In this section, we prove congruences over
G
where
G=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
is the Galois group of an admissible extension. This is achieved by relating the truncated Euler characteristic over
G
to that over the cyclotomic extension. The extensions considered will be the false Tate curve extension and the admissible
p
-adic Lie-extension arising from the
p^{\infty}
-torsion points of the elliptic curves
E_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. In the false Tate curve case, the corresponding Galois group is a semidirect product
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and in the other case it is a finite index subgroup of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

4.1.
Congruences for the False Tate Curve Extension

Let
m
be a positive integer coprime to
Np
, where we recall that
N
is the product of the conductors of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
be the false Tate curve extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}:=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},m^{\frac{1}{p^{\infty}}})
. Our assumptions imply that the reduction type of
E
does not change in any number field extension of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
. In particular, if
w
is a prime above
l
in
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})
then
E
has the same reduction type at
l
as well as
w
. Recall that
G:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
and
H:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}^{\text{cyc}})
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q})
and identify
G/H\simeq\Gamma
. Let
\Sigma
be a set finite set of primes containing all primes
l|mp
and all primes
l
at which either
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
has bad reduction.

Definition 4.1
.

[
16
, Definition 2.4]

Let
\mathfrak{M}(E)
denote the subset of primes
l\in\Sigma
consisting of the primes
l
dividing
m
such that
E
has good reduction at
l
and the corresponding reduced curve has a
point of order
p
.

Let
P_{0}
be the set of primes
l\neq p
which are ramified in
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
, this is the set of primes
l|m
. Recall that it is stipulated that
E
has good reduction at each prime
l|m
and as a consequence the set of primes
\mathfrak{M}(E)\subset P_{0}
coincides with the set of primes
\Sigma_{3}(E)
defined in
[
16
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
9
, Theorem 4.10]

\chi_{t}(G,E)\sim\chi_{t}(\Gamma^{\prime},E)\times\prod_{l\in\mathfrak{M}(E)}L_{l}(E,1)^{-1}.

Theorem 4.3
.

For elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
satisfying conditions
(\ref{one})
to
(\ref{six})
as in the introduction. It is the case that
\Phi_{E_{1}}\times\chi_{t}(G,E_{1})=1
if and only if
\Phi_{E_{2}}\times\chi_{t}(G,E_{2})=1
.
If in addition condition
(\star)
is satisfied for
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, then
\chi_{t}(G,E_{1})=1
if and only if
\chi_{t}(G,E_{2})=1
.

Proof.

Let
E
denote any one of the elliptic curves
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
. Recall that
m
is by assumption, coprime to
pN_{E}
and hence
E
has good reduction at each prime
l|m
. By
[
16
, Lemma 2.5]
, the set of primes
\mathfrak{M}(E)
is the set of primes
l|m
at which
p|L_{l}(E,1)^{-1}
. At each prime
l|m
,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
both have good reduction, therefore,
a_{l}(f_{1})\equiv a_{l}(f_{2})\mod{p}
and therefore,

L_{l}(E_{1},1)^{-1}\equiv L_{l}(E_{2},1)^{-1}\mod{p}.

As a consequence,
\mathfrak{M}(E_{1})=\mathfrak{M}(E_{2})
. Let
l\in\mathfrak{M}(E_{1})=\mathfrak{M}(E_{2})
.
By Theorem
4.2
,

\chi_{t}(G,E_{i})\sim\chi_{t}(\Gamma^{\prime},E_{i})\times\prod_{l\in\mathfrak{M}(E_{i})}L_{l}(E_{i},1)^{-1}.

On the other hand, by Corollary
3.10
,

\Phi_{E_{1}}\times\chi_{t}(\Gamma^{\prime},E_{1})=1\Leftrightarrow\Phi_{E_{2}}\times\chi_{t}(\Gamma^{\prime},E_{2})=1.

Putting it all together, one obtains the congruence

\Phi_{E_{1}}\times\chi_{t}(G,E_{1})=1\Leftrightarrow\Phi_{E_{2}}\times\chi_{t}(G,E_{2})=1.

By Lemma
3.1
, if condition
(\star)
is satisfied then
\Phi_{E_{1}}=\Phi_{E_{2}}=1
. The assertion of the Theorem follows.
∎

4.2.
Congruences for
\text{GL}_{2}
-extensions

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
for which the Galois module
E[p]
is irreducible. Denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}(E):=\mathbb{Q}(E_{p^{\infty}})
be the field obtained by adjoining the coordinates of the group of
p
-power division points of
E
. Let
G_{E}:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}(E)/\mathbb{Q})
and identify
G_{E}
with its image in
\operatorname{Aut}(T_{p}(E))\simeq\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, where as usual
T_{p}(E):=\varprojlim_{n}E_{p^{n}}
is the
p
-adic Tate-module of
E
. By a well-known theorem of Serre,
G_{E}
is an open subgroup of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Let
H_{E}:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}(E)/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
and as usual,
\Gamma=G_{E}/H_{E}
. It was shown by Kato
[
10
]
that
\operatorname{Sel}(E/\mathbb{Q}^{cyc})
is a cotorsion
\Lambda(\Gamma)
-module. It follows from this that the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}(E))
is a cotorsion
\Lambda(G_{E})
-module (cf.
[
3
]
). Let
\mathcal{M}_{E}
be the set of primes at which the
j
-invariant of
E
is non-integral.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
4
, Theorem 3.1]

Suppose that
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E)
is finite then so is
\chi_{t}(G_{E},E)
and we have

\chi_{t}(G_{E},E)\sim\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E)\times\prod_{l\in\mathcal{M}_{E}}L_{l}(E,1)^{-1}.

We let
G_{i}:=G_{E_{i}}
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves satisfying conditions
(\ref{one})
to
(\ref{six})
as in the introduction. And further assume that at each prime
l
where
E_{i}
does not have potentially good reduction,

p\nmid l-a_{l}(E_{i}).

Then, it is the case that
\Phi_{E_{1}}\times\chi_{t}(G_{1},E_{1})=1
if and only if
\Phi_{E_{2}}\times\chi_{t}(G_{2},E_{2})=1
.
If in addition condition
(\star)
is satisfied for
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, then
\chi_{t}(G_{1},E_{1})=1
if and only if
\chi_{t}(G_{2},E_{2})=1
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.3
,

\Phi_{E_{1}}\cdot\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E_{1})=1\Leftrightarrow\Phi_{E_{2}}\cdot\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E_{2})=1.

By Theorem
4.4
,

\chi_{t}(G_{i},E_{i})\sim\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E_{i})\times\prod_{l\in\mathcal{M}_{E_{i}}}L_{l}(E_{i},1)^{-1}.

By
[
13
, Proposition 5.5]
, a prime
l\in\mathcal{M}_{E_{i}}
if and only if
E_{i}
does not have potentially good reduction at
l
. By our assumption, at every prime
l\in\mathcal{M}_{E_{i}}
, we have that
L_{l}(E_{i},1)
is a
p
-adic unit. The assertion follows from this.
∎

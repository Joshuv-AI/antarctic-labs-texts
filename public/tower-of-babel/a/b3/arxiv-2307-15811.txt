Introduction

Given

Problem (

equations defined in two dimensional spaces with an exponential nonlinear term are very

mean field equation, Liouville type equations from the Chern-Simons self-dual theory,
and systems of equations of the Toda system are a few examples of this family. The
analysis of these equations is usually challenging as the interesting exponential nonlinear term is always related to the lack of compactness in the variational approach. One
important feature of these equations is the blow-up phenomenon, the understanding of
which is closely related to results on existence, compactness, a-priori estimates, etc.

The case

So, from analytical viewpoints the study of non-simple blow-up solutions is far more challenging than simple blow-up solutions, but the impact of this study may be even more significant because they represent certain situations in the blow-up analysis of systems of Liouville equations. Indeed, if local maxima of blow-up solutions in a system tend to one point, the profile of solutions can be described by a Liouville equation with quantized singular source. For all this reasons, it is desirable to know exactly when non-simple blow-up phenomenon happens.

The phenomena of non-simple bubbling solutions not only occur in single equations, but also in systems. In a recent work of the second author and Gu (

The proofs use singular perturbation methods which combine the variational approach with a Lyapunov-Schmidt type procedure. Roughly speaking, the first step consists in the construction of an approximate solution, which should turn out to be precise enough. In view of the expected asymptotic behaviour, the shape of such approximate solution will resemble, after the change of variables
x\mapsto x^{1/2}
, a
bubble
of the form (

) with a suitable choice of the parameter
\delta=\delta(\lambda,b)
. Then we look for a solution to (
1.2
) in a small neighborhood of the first approximation. As quite standard in singular perturbation theory, a crucial ingredient is nondegeneracy of
the explicit family of
solutions of the limiting Liouville problem (
2.5
),
as established in
[
2
]
.
This allows us to study the invertibility of the linearized operator associated to the problem (
1.2
) under suitable orthogonality conditions. Next we introduce an intermediate problem and a fixed point argument will provide a solution for an auxiliary equation, which turns out to be solvable for any choice of

. Finally we test the auxiliary equation on the elements of the kernel of the linearized operator and we find out that,
in order to find an

1.2
), the location of the maximum points, which is detected by the parameter

, should be a zero for a reduced finite dimensional map. The main technical difficulty in the proof is that we need to expand the reduced map up to higher orders to catch a nontrivial zero

, which will give rise to a non-simple blow-up solution. Moreover the method fails for

Let us reformulate the main theorem for problem (

We end this section by setting notation and basic well-known

3.

.

There exist
\lambda_{0}>0

Lemma 4.1

Assume that hypotheses

In order to solve (

For any

\displaystyle=8\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{1}{(1+|z-\delta^{-1}b|^{2})^{3}}(z_{i}-\delta^{-1}b_{i})dz

\displaystyle=8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+|z-\delta^{-1}b|^{2})^{3}}(z_{i}-\delta^{-1}b_{i})dz+O(\delta^{3})

\displaystyle=8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{z_{i}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz+O(\delta^{3})=O(\delta^{3}),

since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{z_{i}}{(1+|z||^{2})^{3}}dz=0
by oddness. Next

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}Z^{i}_{\lambda}(x_{i}-b_{i})dx

\displaystyle=8\delta\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{1}{(1+|z-\delta^{-1}b|^{2})^{3}}(z_{i}-\delta^{-1}b_{i})^{2}dz

\displaystyle=8\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+|z-\delta^{-1}b|^{2})^{3}}(z_{i}-\delta^{-1}b_{i})^{2}dz+O(\delta^{3})

\displaystyle=8\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{z_{i}^{2}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz+O(\delta^{3})

\displaystyle=2\pi\delta+O(\delta^{3})

where we have used the identity
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(z_{i})^{2}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}=\frac{\pi}{4}
. Similarly for
i\neq j

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}Z^{i}_{\lambda}(x_{j}-b_{j})dx

\displaystyle=8\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz+O(\delta^{3})=O(\delta^{3})

since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz=0.
Next,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}|Z^{i}_{\lambda}||x-b|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq 8\delta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|x-b|^{3}}{(\delta^{2}+|x-b|^{2})^{3}}dx=8\delta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|z|^{3}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz\leq C\delta^{2}.

Using that
B(b,1-|b|)\subset B(0,1)\subset B(b,1+|b|)
, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}Z^{i}_{\lambda}(x_{i}-b_{i})^{3}dx

\displaystyle=8\delta^{3}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{(x_{i}-b_{i})^{4}}{(\delta^{2}+|x-b|^{2})^{3}}dx

\displaystyle=8\delta^{3}\int_{B(b,1-|b|)}\frac{(x_{i}-b_{i})^{4}}{(\delta^{2}+|x-b|^{2})^{3}}dx+O\bigg(\delta^{3}\int_{B(b,1+|b|)\setminus B(b,1-|b|)}\frac{|x-b|^{4}}{(\delta^{2}+|x-b|^{2})^{3}}dx\bigg)

\displaystyle=8\delta^{3}\int_{B(0,1-|b|)}\frac{(x_{i})^{4}}{(\delta^{2}+|x|^{2})^{3}}dx+O\bigg(\delta^{3}\int_{B(0,1+|b|)\setminus B(0,1-|b|)}\frac{|x|^{4}}{(\delta^{2}+|x|^{2})^{3}}dx\bigg)

\displaystyle=8\delta^{3}\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1-|b|}{\delta}}\frac{(z_{i})^{4}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz+O(\delta^{3})

\displaystyle=6\pi\delta^{3}|\log\delta|+O(\delta^{3})

where we have used the identity
\int_{|z|\leq r}\frac{(z_{i})^{4}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz=\frac{3}{8}\pi\log(1+r^{2})+\frac{3}{4}\frac{\pi}{1+r^{2}}-\frac{3}{16}\frac{\pi}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}-\frac{9}{16}\pi
.

Similarly, for
i\neq j

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}Z^{i}_{\lambda}(x_{j}-b_{j})^{3}dx

\displaystyle=8\delta^{3}\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1-|b|}{\delta}}\frac{z_{i}(z_{j})^{3}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz+O(\delta^{3})=O(\delta^{3})

since
\int_{|z|\leq r}\frac{z_{i}(z_{j})^{3}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz=0
.

Next, for
i\neq j

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}Z^{i}_{\lambda}(x_{j}-b_{j})^{2}(x_{i}-b_{i})dx

\displaystyle=8\delta^{3}\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1-|b|}{\delta}}\frac{(z_{i})^{2}(z_{j})^{2}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz+O(\delta^{3})

\displaystyle=2\pi\delta^{3}|\log\delta|+O(\delta^{3})

where the last equality follows by
\int_{|z|\leq r}\frac{(z_{i})^{2}(z_{j})^{2}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz=\frac{\pi}{8}\log(1+r^{2})+\frac{1}{4}\frac{\pi}{1+r^{2}}-\frac{1}{16}\frac{\pi}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}-\frac{3}{16}\pi
.
Similarly for
i\neq j

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}Z^{i}_{\lambda}(x_{i}-b_{i})^{2}(x_{j}-b_{j})dx

\displaystyle=8\delta^{3}\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1-b}{\delta}}\frac{(z_{i})^{3}z_{j}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz+O(\delta^{3})=O(\delta^{3})

by
\int_{|z|\leq r}\frac{(z_{i})^{3}z_{j}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz=0
.
Finally

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}|Z^{i}_{\lambda}||x-b|^{\frac{7}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq 8\delta^{2}\int_{B(b,1+|b|)}\frac{\delta|x-b|^{\frac{9}{2}}}{(\delta^{2}+|x-b|^{2})^{3}}dx=8\delta^{\frac{7}{2}}\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1+|b|}{\delta}}\frac{|z|^{\frac{9}{2}}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}dz\leq C\delta^{3}.

Taking into account that
PZ^{i}_{\lambda}=Z^{i}_{\lambda}+O(\delta)
by (
3.2
), and recalling Lemma
A.1
, the above integral estimates give the thesis.
∎

In order to derive next integral estimate we need to expand the projections
PZ_{\lambda}^{i}
to a higher order with respect to (
3.2
).

Lemma A.3
.

For
i=1,2
the following holds:

PZ_{\lambda}^{i}=Z_{\lambda}^{i}(x)-\delta(x_{i}-b_{i})+O(\delta^{3})+O(\delta|b|)\hbox{ in }B_{1}

uniformly for
b
in a small neighborhood of
0
.

Proof.

Let us consider
i=1
. Observe that

\displaystyle\hbox{if }|x|=1:\;\;Z_{\lambda}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta(x_{1}-b_{1})}{\delta^{2}+|x-b|^{2}}=\frac{\delta(x_{1}-b_{1})}{1+\delta^{2}+O(|b|)}

\displaystyle=\delta(x_{1}-b_{1})\Big(1+O(|b|)+O(\delta^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=\delta(x_{1}-b_{1})+O(|b|\delta)+O(\delta^{3}).

Therefore, if we set

\hat{Z}_{\lambda}^{1}:=Z_{\lambda}^{1}(x)-\delta(x_{1}-b_{1}),

we get

\hat{Z}_{\lambda}^{1}(x)=O(\delta^{3})+O(\delta|b|)\hbox{ if }|x|=1

and

-\Delta\hat{Z}_{\lambda}^{1}(x)=-\Delta Z_{\lambda}^{1}(x)=\Delta PZ_{\lambda}^{1}(x)\hbox{ in }B_{1}.

Hence, since by construction
PZ_{\lambda}^{1}=0
for
|x|=1
, the maximum principle applies and gives

PZ_{\lambda}^{1}=\hat{Z}_{\lambda}^{1}+O(\delta^{3})+O(\delta|b|)=Z_{\lambda}^{1}(x)-\delta(x_{1}-b_{1})+O(\delta^{3})+O(\delta|b|)\hbox{ in }B_{1}.

∎

Lemma A.4
.

Let
P
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree 2. Then the following holds:

\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}PZ^{i}_{\lambda}P(x-b)dx=O(\delta^{3})+O(\delta^{2}|b|)\quad i=1,2

uniformly for
b
in a small neighborhood of
0
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}Z^{i}_{\lambda}P(x-b)dx

\displaystyle=8\delta^{2}\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{P(z-\delta^{-1}b)}{(1+|z-\delta^{-1}b|^{2})^{3}}(z_{i}-\delta^{-1}b_{i})dz

(A.1)

\displaystyle=8\delta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{P(z)}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}z_{i}dz+O(\delta^{3})=O(\delta^{3})

where we have used that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{P(z)}{(1+|z|^{2})^{3}}z_{i}dz=0
by oddness.
Taking into account of Lemma
A.3

we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}PZ^{i}_{\lambda}P(x-b)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}Z^{i}_{\lambda}P(x-b)dx-\delta\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}(x_{i}-b_{i})P(x-b)dx

\displaystyle+\Big(O(\delta^{3})+O(\delta|b|)\Big)\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}|x-b|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}Z^{i}_{\lambda}P(x-b)dx+O(\delta)\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}|x-b|^{3}dx

\displaystyle+\Big(O(\delta^{3})+O(\delta|b|)\Big)\int_{B_{1}}e^{W_{\lambda}}|x-b|^{2}dx

and the thesis follows by (
A.1
), and recalling Lemma
A.1
.
∎

Finally we deduce some integral identities associated to the change of variable
x\mapsto x^{\alpha}
which appears frequently when dealing with
\alpha
-symmetric functions.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\geq 2
, and let
f\in L^{1}(B_{1})
.
Then we have that
|x|^{2(\alpha-1)}f(x^{\alpha})\in L^{1}(B_{1})
and

\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2(\alpha-1)}f(x^{\alpha})dx=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{B_{1}}f(y)dy.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove the thesis for a smooth function
f
. Using the polar coordinates
(\rho,\theta)
and then applying the change of variables
(\rho^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=(\rho^{\alpha},\alpha\theta)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2(\alpha-1)}f(x^{\alpha})dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\rho\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rho^{2\alpha-1}f(\rho^{\alpha}e^{{\rm i}\alpha\theta})d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\rho^{\prime}\int_{0}^{2\alpha\pi}\rho^{\prime}f(\rho^{\prime}e^{{\rm i}\theta^{\prime}})d\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\rho^{\prime}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rho^{\prime}|f(\rho^{\prime}e^{{\rm i}\theta^{\prime}})|^{2}d\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{B_{1}}f(y)dy.

∎

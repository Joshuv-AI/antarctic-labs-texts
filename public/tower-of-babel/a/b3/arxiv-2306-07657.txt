Best constants in subelliptic fractional Sobolev and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities and ground states on stratified Lie groups Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35R03, 35H20, 35P30, 22E30, 35R11
Sekhar Ghosh, Vishvesh Kumar and Michael Ruzhansky

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the sharp fractional subelliptic Sobolev inequalities and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities on stratified Lie groups. The best constants are given in terms of a ground state solution of a fractional subelliptic equation involving the fractional p p -sublaplacian ( 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty ) on stratified Lie groups. We also prove the existence of ground state (least energy) solutions to nonlinear subelliptic fractional Schrödinger equation on stratified Lie groups. Different from the proofs of analogous results in the setting of classical Sobolev spaces on Euclidean spaces given by Weinstein (Comm. Math. Phys. 87(4):576-676 (1982/1983)) using the rearrangement inequality which is not available in stratified Lie groups, we apply a subelliptic version of vanishing lemma due to Lions extended in the setting of stratified Lie groups combining it with the compact embedding theorem for subelliptic fractional Sobolev spaces obtained in our previous paper (Math. Ann. (2023)). We also present subelliptic fractional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities with explicit constants on stratified Lie groups. The main results are new for p = 2 p=2 even in the context of the Heisenberg group.



1.
Introduction and main results

The Sobolev and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities play an important role in the study of partial differential equations (PDEs) (see
[
12
]
).
The classical Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
was established by Gagliardo
[
37
]
and Nirenberg
[
59
]
in their celebrated papers independently. They proved that for every
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that the following inequality holds.

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{2q-N(q-2)}{4}},

where
2<q<2^{*}.
Here,
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
for
N\geq 3
and
2^{*}=+\infty
for
N=2
. The smallest
C
such that the inequality (
1.1
) holds is called the best constant and we denote it by
C_{GN,\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. Finding the best constants in the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities has an analytical or geometrical significance and proved to be vital in studying the well-posedness of Cauchy problems and stability theorems for nonlinear parabolic PDEs. The pioneering work establishing a relationship between the best constant and least energy solutions is due to Weinstein
[
72
]
. For
N=1,
the best constant in the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality was calculated by Nagy
[
60
]
. Weinstein
[
72
]
provided a form of
C_{GN,\mathbb{R}^{N}}
given by the least energy solution of the following stationary Schrödinger equation.

(1.2)

-\Delta u+u=|u|^{p-2}u,\quad u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

It is noteworthy to mention that the expression for the best constant does not depend on the least energy solution. Following these studies, there have been many contributions to the literature on the Euclidean case which either extended and improved these inequalities or deal with the best constants and extremisers for such inequalities. For instance, we mention
[

and the references therein without making an attempt to present an exhaustive list.

In this paper, we are interested in the analysis of the best constants appearing in the fractional subelliptic Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities on stratified Lie groups, a subclass of nilpotent Lie groups. These groups naturally appear in analysis, representation theory, and geometry. One prominent example of such a group is the Heisenberg group. It was noted in the celebrated paper
[
63
]
by Rothschild and Stein that nilpotent Lie groups play an important role in deriving sharp subelliptic estimates for differential operators on manifolds. In 1970, Stein
[
67
]
delivered a visionary program at the ICM in Nice for studying analysis and PDEs on stratified Lie groups (see also
[
16
]
). He demonstrated it in his seminal joint paper with Rothschild on the Rothschild-Stein lifting theorem that says that a general Hörmander’s sums of squares of vector fields on manifolds can be approximated by the sublaplacian on some stratified Lie groups (see also,
[

In this paper, we are concerned with the fractional subelliptic Sobolev and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities on stratified Lie groups and their best constants. In fact, we will show, as in the seminal paper of Weinstein
[
72
]
, that the best constant in the subelliptic fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality can be explicitly expressed as the least energy solution of the nonlocal subelliptic stationary Schrödinger equation on the stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}
:

(1.3)

\left(-\Delta_{p,\mathbb{G}}\right)^{s}u+|u|^{p-2}u=|u|^{q-2}u,\quad u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G}),

where
s\in(0,1),

p\in(1,\infty)
and
p<q<p_{s}^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}
with
Q
being the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}
associated with the group dilations
(D_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
. Here the operator
\left(-\Delta_{p,\mathbb{G}}\right)^{s}
on
\mathbb{G}
is the nonlinear nonlocal counterpart of the sublaplacian on a stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}.
For
p=2,
the operator turns out to be the fractional sublaplacian
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}})^{s},
which was an object of deep investigation and its connection with different areas of mathematics in many intriguing papers
[
10
,
68
,
35
,
39
,
46
,
69
]
and references cited therein. This is our guiding operator defined, initially for
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),
by

(1.4)

(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}})^{s}(u)(x):=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash B(x,\epsilon)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+2s}}dy=C_{Q,s}\,\,P.V.\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+2s}}dy.

It is known that for a
H
-type group the operator
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}})^{s}
is a multiple of the pseudo-differential operator defined as

(1.5)

\mathcal{L}_{s}:=2^{s}(-\Delta_{z})^{\frac{s}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}(-\Delta_{z})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1+s}{2})}{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}(-\Delta_{z})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1-s}{2})},

where
-\Delta_{z}
is the positive Laplacian in the center of the
H
-type group
\mathbb{G}
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
is the sublaplacian on
\mathbb{G}.
For more details, we refer to
[
10
,
68
,
69
]
. It is worth noting that
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is a “conformal invariant” operator and is very important in CR geometry (see
[
35
]
). Finally, we would like to point out that the operator
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}})^{s}
does not coincide with the standard fractional power
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{s}
of the sublaplacian
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
in the Heisenberg group or in general,
H
-type groups for any value of
s\in(0,1),
which is defined as

(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{s}u)(x):=-\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{1+s}}(H_{t}u(x)-u(x))\,dt,

where
H_{t}:=e^{-t\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}
is the heat semigroup constructed by Folland
[
32
]
.

p

p\in[1,\infty)

(1.6)

\left(-\Delta_{p,{\mathbb{G}}}\right)^{s}u(x):=C_{Q,s,p}\,\,P.V.\int_{{\mathbb{G}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dy,\quad x\in{\mathbb{G}},

for
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).

To state our first result regarding the existence of the least energy solution of (
1.3
) involving the fractional
p
-sublaplacian on
\mathbb{G}
, we first recall some basic definitions.
Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{G}}
be an open subset. Then for
0<s<1\leq p<\infty
, the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
on stratified groups is defined as

(1.7)

W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):[u]_{s,p,\Omega}<\infty\},

endowed with the norm

(1.8)

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{p}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[u]_{s,p,\Omega}^{p},

where
[u]_{s,p,\Omega}
denotes the Gagliardo semi-norm defined by

(1.9)

[u]_{s,p,\Omega}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Observe that for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have
[u]_{s,p,\Omega}<\infty
. We define the space
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
as the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
. We would like to point out that
W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})=W^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
(see
[
42
]
).

With the definitions of
I
and
\mathcal{N}
as in Section
4
, the following theorem is our first main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified Lie group with homogeneous dimension
Q.
Let
0<s<1<p<\infty
and
p<q<p_{s}^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}
. Then the problem (
1.3
) has a least energy solution
\phi\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
. Moreover, we have the least energy
d=I(\phi):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}I(u)
.

This result in the classical case, that is, for (
1.2
) was proved by Weinstein
[
72
]
and using this he obtained the sharp estimates of the best constant
C_{GN,\mathbb{R}^{N}}
in the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.1
) by solving a minimisation problem. The key ingredients of his proof were symmetric rearrangement and the compact embedding of the radial Sobolev space
W_{r}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
N\geq 2
and
2<q<2^{*}.
It is now well-known that symmetric rearrangement techniques are not available for the stratified Lie groups and therefore one needs to seek a different proof. Chen and Rocha
[
19
]
extended the results of Weinstein
[
72
]
in the Heisenberg group by using the classical compact embedding of the Folland-Stein-Sobolev space (
[
38
]
) and Lions type vanishing lemma (
[
50
,
51
]
) for respective Folland-Stein-Sobolev space on the Heisenberg group. Based on this existence result, they (
[
19
]
) obtained the expression for the best constant of the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on the Heisenberg group:

(1.10)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{H}u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{Q(q-2)}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{2q-Q(q-2)}{4}}.

Here,
Q=2N+2
refers to the homogeneous dimension of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{N}
,
\nabla_{H}
is horizontal gradient,
q\in(2,2^{*})
, where
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
.
The best constant in (
1.10
), denoted by
C_{GN,\mathbb{H}^{N}},
is expressed in terms of the least energy (or ground state) solution of the subelliptic partial differential equation.

(1.11)

-\triangle_{H}u+u=|u|^{q-2}u,\;\;u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{N}),

where
\triangle_{H}
is the sublaplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
, and
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
is the Sobolev space on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}(|\nabla_{H}u|^{2}+|u|^{2})dx\right)^{1/2}.

Recently, the third author and his collaborators
[
66
]
characterised the best constant of the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality over a graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}
:

(1.12)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\frac{a_{1}}{\nu_{1}}}u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)-a_{2}pq}{(a_{1}-a_{2})p^{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\frac{a_{2}}{\nu_{2}}}u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{a_{1}pq-Q(q-p)}{(a_{1}-a_{2})p^{2}}}

for all
u\in{W}^{a_{1},p}(\mathbb{G})\cap{W}^{a_{2},p}(\mathbb{G})
. Here
a_{1}>a_{2}\geq 0
,
1<p<\frac{Q}{a_{1}},

\frac{pQ}{Q-a_{2}p}\leq q\leq\frac{pQ}{Q-a_{1}p}
and,
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
are positive Rockland operators of homogeneous degrees
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
, respectively. They proved that the best constant in (
1.12
) can be identified by the least energy solutions of the following higher order nonlinear hypoelliptic Schrödinger equation with the power nonlinearities:

(1.13)

\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\frac{a_{1}}{\nu_{1}}}(|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\frac{a_{1}}{\nu_{1}}}u|^{p-2}\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\frac{a_{1}}{\nu_{1}}}u)+\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\frac{a_{2}}{\nu_{2}}}(|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\frac{a_{2}}{\nu_{2}}}u|^{p-2}\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\frac{a_{2}}{\nu_{2}}}u)=|u|^{q-2}u.

In this paper, we continue the aforementioned studies
[
19
,
66
]
for a more general nonlocal operator, namely, the fractional
p
-sublaplacian on the stratified Lie groups. In
[
42
]
, we have proved a compact embedding result for the fractional Sobolev spaces on the stratified Lie groups (see Theorem
2.4
in the next section), which is a nonlocal version of the embedding result for the Folland-Stein-Sobolev space obtained in
[
38
]
. This is one of the main tools in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The second main tool as in the aforementioned works
[
19
,
66
]
is a subelliptic version of the Lions vanishing Lemma (
[
50
,
51
]
) for
W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G}),
which is stated below and will be proved in Section
4
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1),p\in(1,\infty),
and
q
be such that
p\leq q<p_{s}^{*}=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}.
Let
(u_{k})
be a bounded sequence in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
with the property

(1.14)

\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{q}dy=0.

Then there exists a subsequence, also denoted by
(u_{k}),
such that
u_{k}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{t}(\mathbb{G})
for
t\in(q,p_{s}^{*}).

We now turn our attention to deriving the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. We mentioned that the proof is contained in
[
48
]
but the proof is quite elementary using Hölder’s inequality so we include it here. Recall that the following fractional subelliptic Sobolev inequality

(1.15)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p_{s}^{*}}dx\leq C\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right]^{\frac{p_{s}^{*}}{p}}

for
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
was proved in
[
49
, Theorem 2]
.
Now, we derive, from the Hölder inequality, that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{ql}|u|^{q(1-l)}dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{p_{s}^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{ql}{p_{s}^{*}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q(1-l)}{p}},

where
\frac{ql}{p_{s}^{*}}+\frac{q(1-l)}{p}=1
. Thus,
l=\frac{(q-p)Q}{spq}
. Therefore, eliminating
l
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{p_{s}^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{(Q-ps)(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{spq-Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}.

Using the inequality (
1.15
), we get the following subelliptic fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality:

(1.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{spq-Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}.

The best constant for the inequality (
1.16
) is the smallest positive constant
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}>0
such that the inequality (
1.16
) is true. Thus, we can define
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}
as

(1.17)

C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}:=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{spq-Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}}{\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx}.

Now, we state the following theorem which says that the best constants of the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality can be characterised by the least energy solutions of the nonlinear subelliptic fractional Schrödinger equation (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<s<1<p<\infty
and
Q>ps
and
p<q<p_{s}^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}
, where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of a stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}
. Let
\phi
be a least energy solution of (
1.3
). Then the smallest positive constant
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}
of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.17
) can be characterized by

\displaystyle C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{pqs}\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{q-p}

(1.18)

\displaystyle=\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{pqs}\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{(q-p)s}d\right)^{\frac{q-p}{p}}

where
d=I(\phi):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}I(u)
,
\phi
being a least energy solution of (
1.3
).

We also obtain a similar result for the fractional subelliptic Sobolev inequality on stratified Lie groups. Let us now recall the following Sobolev inequality from
[
42
, Theorem 1.1]

(1.19)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq C\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right],

for
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G}).

The following result expresses the best constant in terms of the least energy solution of (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0<s<1<p<\infty
and
Q>ps
and
p<q<p_{s}^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}
, where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of a stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}
. Let
C_{S,\mathbb{G}}>0
be the least positive constant such that the inequality (
1.19
) is true. Then we have

(1.20)

\displaystyle C_{S,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{spq}{spq-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}

(1.21)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{pq}{q-p}d\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}},

where
d=I(\phi):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}I(u)
,
\phi
being a least energy solution of (
1.3
) and

(1.22)

C_{S,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}:=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p}dx}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}.

Remark 1.5
.

We do not know if
\phi
is a unique least energy solution of (
1.3
). But equality in (
1.21
) and (
1.3
) implies that
C_{S,\mathbb{G}}
and
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}
are independent of the choice of
\phi
. Further, we want to mention here that if we replace
Q
with the Euclidean dimension
N
, and
\mathbb{G}
by
\mathbb{R}^{N}
then both of these constants reduce to the classical results. For instance, for
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
s=1
,
p=2,
it is the same as the result due to Weinstein
[
72
]
whereas for
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{H}^{N}
,
s=1
,
p=2,
it coincides with the results due to Chen and Rocha
[
19
]

Remark 1.6
.

We can easily establish a relation between the sharp constant
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}
in the fractional subelliptic Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.17
) and the sharp constant
C_{S,\mathbb{G}}
in the fractional subelliptic Sobolev inequality (
1.19
) on the stratified Lie groups. Indeed, from Theorem
1.3
we have

\displaystyle C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{pqs}\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{(q-p)s}d\right)^{\frac{q-p}{p}},

which implies that

C^{-\frac{p}{q}}_{GN,\mathbb{G}}=\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{pqs}\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs}}C^{-1}_{S,\mathbb{G}}.

Now, we derive the logarithmic fractional Sobolev inequalities with an explicit constant with the help of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
. In this regard, our result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
s\in(0,1),

p\in(1,\infty),Q>ps
and
p<q<p^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps},
where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of a stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}.

Then for any
u\neq 0,
we have

(1.23)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}\log\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}}\right)dx\leq\frac{Q}{s}\log\left(C_{S,\mathbb{G},p}\frac{\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p})dx\right]}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(G)}^{p}}\right),

where
C_{S,\mathbb{G},p}
is given by

C_{S,\mathbb{G},p}:=\left(\frac{s}{Qd}\right)^{\frac{sp}{Q}}.

Here
d=I(\phi):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}I(u)
,
\phi
being a least energy solution of (
1.3
).

Before stating our next result, let us discuss the idea of the proof of Theorem
1.7
and the next theorem. The method is quite simple and uses the logarithmic Hölder inequalities (see Theorem
7.2
) combined with the subelliptic fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg and Sobolev inequalities. For the Euclidean case, this method was possibly first used by Merker
[
56
]
and later in many papers in several different scenarios, for example,
[
44
]
for the logarithmic Sobolev and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities under the Lorentz norms and
[
47
,
14
]
for logarithmic Sobolev and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities on Lie groups. We now state our last result concerning a different version of the logarithmic fractional Sobolev inequality with a homogeneous norm. This result was first proved in
[
48
]
without an explicit constant.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
s\in(0,1),

p\in(1,\infty),Q>ps
and
p<q<p^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps},
where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of a stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}.

Then for any
u\neq 0,
we have

(1.24)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}\log\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}}\right)dx\leq\frac{Q}{s}\log\left(C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{ps}{Q(q-p)}}\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(G)}}\right),

where
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}
is the best constant in the fractional subelliptic Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality given by

C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}:=\frac{pqs}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{Q(p-q)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{(q-p)s}d\right)^{\frac{p-q}{p}}.

Here
d=I(\phi):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}I(u)
,
\phi
being a least energy solution of (
1.3
).

The rest of the paper is organised as follows: in Section
2
we present some basics of analysis on stratified Lie groups and fractional subelliptic Sobolev spaces. Section
3
will be devoted to the proof of Lions vanishing lemma for stratified Lie groups. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is contained in Section
4
. The proof of Theorem
1.3
regarding the best constant of the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on stratified Lie groups is given in Section
5
. The proof of Theorem
1.4
is presented in Section
6
. We end this paper in Section
7
by discussing the proof of Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.8
talking about the logarithmic version of fractional subelliptic Sobolev inequalities with explicit constants.

2.
Preliminaries: Stratified Lie groups and fractional Sobolev spaces

Prior to proceeding with the main results, we recall some preliminary tools of the stratified Lie groups and the fractional Sobolev spaces defined on them. There are many ways to introduce the notion of stratified Lie groups, for instance, one may refer to books and monographs
[
34
,
7
,
31
,
65
]
. In his seminal paper
[
32
]
, Folland extensively investigated the properties of function spaces on these groups. For precise studies and properties on stratified Lie group, we refer
[
38
,
32
,
34
,
7
,
31
,
42
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A Lie group
\mathbb{G}
(on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
) is said to be homogeneous if, for each
\lambda>0
, there exists an automorphism
D_{\lambda}:\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}
defined by
D_{\lambda}(x)=(\lambda^{r_{1}}x_{1},\lambda^{r_{2}}x_{2},...,\lambda^{r_{N}}x_{N})
for
r_{i}>0,\,\forall\,i=1,2,...,N
. The map
D_{\lambda}
is called a
dilation
on
\mathbb{G}
.

For simplicity, we sometimes prefer to use the notation
\lambda x
to denote the dilation
D_{\lambda}x
. Note that, if
\lambda x
is a dilation then
\lambda^{r}x
is also a dilation. The number
Q=r_{1}+r_{2}+...+r_{N}
is called the homogeneous dimension of the homogeneous Lie group
\mathbb{G}
and the natural number
N
represents the topological dimension of
\mathbb{G}.
The Haar measure on
\mathbb{G}
is denoted by
dx
and it is nothing but the usual Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Definition 2.2
.

A homogeneous Lie group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ)
is called a stratified Lie group (or a homogeneous Carnot group) if the following two conditions are fulfilled:

(i)

For some natural numbers
N_{1}+N_{2}+...+N_{k}=N
the decomposition
\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}\times...\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{k}}
holds, and for each
\lambda>0
there exists a dilation of the form
D_{\lambda}(x)=(\lambda x^{(1)},\lambda^{2}x^{(2)},...,\lambda^{k}x^{(k)})
which is an automorphism of the group
\mathbb{G}
. Here
x^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{i}}
for
i=1,2,...,k
.

(ii)

With
N_{1}
as in the above decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, let
X_{1},...,X_{N_{1}}
be the left invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{G}
such that
X_{k}(0)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}|_{0}
for
k=1,...,N_{1}
. Then the Hörmander condition
rank(Lie\{X_{1},...,X_{N_{1}}\})=N

holds for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In other words, the Lie algebra corresponding to the Lie group
\mathbb{G}
is spanned by the iterated commutators of
X_{1},...,X_{N_{1}}
.

Here
k
is called the step of the stratified Lie group. Note that, in this case, the homogeneous dimension becomes
Q=\sum_{i=1}^{i=k}iN_{i}
. Furthermore, the left-invariant vector fields
X_{j}
satisfy the divergence theorem and they can be written explicitly as

(2.1)

X_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}^{(1)}}+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{1}}a^{(j)}_{i,l}(x^{1},x^{2},...,x^{j-1})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}^{(j)}}.

For simplicity, we set
n=N_{1}
in the above Definition
2.2
.

An absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be admissible, if there exist functions
c_{i}:[0,1]:\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, for
i=1,2,...,n,
such that

{\dot{\gamma}(t)}=\sum_{i=1}^{i=n}c_{i}(t)X_{i}(\gamma(t))~\text{and}~\sum_{i=1}^{i=n}c_{i}(t)^{2}\leq 1.

Observe that the functions
c_{i}
may not be unique as the vector fields
X_{i}
may not be linearly independent. For any
x,y\in\mathbb{G}
the Carnot-Carathéodory distance is defined as

\rho_{cc}(x,y)=\inf\{l>0:~\text{there exists an admissible}~\gamma:[0,l]\rightarrow\mathbb{G}~\text{with}~\gamma(0)=x~\text{\&}~\gamma(l)=y\}.

The Hörmander condition for the vector fields
X_{1},X_{2},...X_{N_{1}}
ensures that
\rho_{cc}
is a metric. The space
(\mathbb{G},\rho_{cc})
is is known as a Carnot-Carathéodory space.

Let us now define the quasi-norm on the stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}
.

Definition 2.3
.

A continuous function
|\cdot|:\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is said to be a homogeneous quasi-norm on a stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

(definiteness):
|x|=0
if and only if
x=0
.

(ii)

(symmetric):
|x^{-1}|=|x|
for all
x\in\mathbb{G}
, and

(iii)

(
1
-homogeneous):
|\lambda x|=\lambda|x|
for all
x\in\mathbb{G}
and
\lambda>0
.

An example of a quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
is the norm defined as
d(x):=\rho_{cc}(x,0),\,\,x\in\mathbb{G}
, where
\rho
is the Carnot-Carathéodory distance related to Hörmander vector fields on
\mathbb{G}.
It is known that all homogeneous quasi-norms are equivalent on
\mathbb{G}.
In this paper we will work with a left-invariant homogeneous distance
d(x,y):=|y^{-1}\circ x|
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{G}
induced by the homogeneous quasi-norm of
\mathbb{G}.

The sublaplacian (or Horizontal Laplacian) on
\mathbb{G}
is defined as

(2.2)

\mathcal{L}:=X_{1}^{2}+\cdots+X_{N_{1}}^{2}.

The horizontal gradient on
\mathbb{G}
is defined as

(2.3)

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}:=\left(X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{N_{1}}\right).

The horizontal divergence on
\mathbb{G}
is defined by

(2.4)

\operatorname{div}_{\mathbb{G}}v:=\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\cdot v.

For
p\in(1,+\infty)
, we define the
p
-sublaplacian on the stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}
as

(2.5)

\Delta_{\mathbb{G},p}u:=\operatorname{div}_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\left|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right|^{p-2}\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right).

Let
\Omega
be a Haar measurable subset of
\mathbb{G}
. Then
\mu(D_{\lambda}(\Omega))=\lambda^{Q}\mu(\Omega)
where
\mu(\Omega)
is the Haar measure of
\Omega
. The quasi-ball of radius
r
centered at
x\in\mathbb{G}
with respect to the quasi-norm
|\cdot|
is defined as

(2.6)

B(x,r)=\left\{y\in\mathbb{G}:\left|y^{-1}\circ x\right|<r\right\}.

Observe that
B(x,r)
can be obtained by the left translation by
x
of the ball
B(0,r)
. Furthermore,
B(0,r)
is the image under the dilation
D_{r}
of
B(0,1)
. Thus, we have
\mu(B(x,r))=r^{Q}\mu(B(0,1))
for all
x\in\mathbb{G}
.

We are now in a position to define the notion of fractional Sobolev-Folland-Stein type spaces related to our study.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{G}}
be an open subset. Then for
0<s<1<p<\infty
, the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
on stratified groups is defined as

(2.7)

W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):[u]_{s,p,\Omega}<\infty\},

endowed with the norm

(2.8)

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{p}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[u]_{s,p,\Omega}^{p},

where
[u]_{s,p,\Omega}
denotes the Gagliardo semi-norm defined by

(2.9)

[u]_{s,p,\Omega}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Observe that for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have
[u]_{s,p,\Omega}<\infty
. We define the space
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
as the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
. We would like to point out that
W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})=W^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
(see
[
42
]
).

Now for an open bounded subset
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{G}}
, define the space
X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
as the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]_{s,p,\mathbb{G}}
. Note that the spaces
X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
and
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
are different even in the Euclidean case unless
\Omega
is an extension domain (see
[
58
]
). Note that when
\Omega=\mathbb{G}
, then we have
X_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})=W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})=W^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
. The space
X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
is a reflexive Banach space for
1<p<\infty
.

The space
X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
can be defined as a closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the homogeneous norm
[u]_{s,p,\mathbb{G}}
. That is

\|u\|_{X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\cong[u]_{s,p,\mathbb{G}}~\text{for all}~u\in X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega).

Moreover, the construction of the space
X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
suggests that if
\Omega
is bounded then we can represent
X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
as follows:

X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)=\{u\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{G}}):u=0~\text{in}~{\mathbb{G}}\setminus\Omega\}.

For
u\in X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle[u]_{s,p,\mathbb{G}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy=\iint_{\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}\setminus(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c})}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy.

We conclude this section with the following two definitions. For
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, we define the fractional
p
-sublaplacian as

(2.10)

\left(-\Delta_{p,{\mathbb{G}}}\right)^{s}u(x):=C_{Q,s,p}\,\,P.V.\int_{{\mathbb{G}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dy,\quad x\in{\mathbb{G}}.

For any
\varphi\in X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, we have

(2.11)

\langle\left(-\Delta_{p,{\mathbb{G}}}\right)^{s}u,\varphi\rangle=\iint_{\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy.

Let us recall the following theorem on the subelliptic fractional embedding results proved in
[
42
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
42
, Theorem 1.1]
Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
be an open set.
Let
0<s<1\leq p<\infty
and
Q>sp.
Then the fractional Sobolev space
X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for
p\leq r\leq p_{s}^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-sp}
, that is, there exists a positive constant
C=C(Q,s,p,\Omega)
such that for all
u\in X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, we have

(2.12)

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}

In particular, we have

(2.13)

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{G})}\leq C_{Q,s,p}\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})}.

Moreover, if
\Omega
is bounded, then the following embedding

(2.14)

\displaystyle X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega)

is continuous for all
r\in[1,p_{s}^{*}]
and is compact for all
r\in[1,p_{s}^{*})
.

3.
A subelliptic version of Lions vanishing lemma for
W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})

In this section, we prove the subelliptic vanishing lemma established for classical Sobolev space by Lions
[
50
,
51
]
in his seminal papers.
Recently, this lemma was obtained by Wang
[
71
]
in the setting of the Heisenberg group. We follow the proof presented in a recent paper
[
73
]
dealing with the Euclidean space scenario.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified Lie group with homogeneous dimension
Q.

Let
s\in(0,1),p\in(1,\infty),
and let
q
be such that
p\leq q<p_{s}^{*}=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}.
Let
(u_{k})
be a bounded sequence in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
with the property

(3.1)

\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{q}dy=0.

Then there exists a subsequence, again denoted by
(u_{k}),
such that
u_{k}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{r}(\mathbb{G})
for
r\in(q,p_{s}^{*}).

Proof.

Let
(u_{k})
be a sequence in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
and
q\in[p,p_{s}^{*}).
We will first prove that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u_{k}|^{r}dy\leq C\Big(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{q}dy\Big)^{\frac{p_{s}^{*}-r}{p_{s}^{*}-q}}\|u_{k}\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}

for any
r\in(q,p_{s}^{*})
and for all
u_{k}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G}).

For any
r\in(q,p_{s}^{*})
we write
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\beta}{q}+\frac{1-\beta}{p_{s}^{*}}
for some
\beta\in(0,1).
Then, using the Hölder inequality we have

\|u_{k}\|_{L^{r}(B(x,1))}^{r}\leq\|u_{k}\|_{L^{q}(B(x,1))}^{q\beta}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p_{s}^{*}}(B(x,1))}^{(1-\beta)p_{s}^{*}}.

Now, by choosing
\beta=\frac{p_{s}^{*}-r}{p_{s}^{*}-q}\in(0,1)
and using the local Sobolev embedding (
2.12
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{r}dy

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{q}dy\Bigg)^{\frac{p_{s}^{*}-r}{p_{s}^{*}-q}}\Bigg(\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{p_{s}^{*}}dy\Bigg)^{\frac{r-q}{p_{s}^{*}-q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{q}dy\Bigg)^{\frac{p_{s}^{*}-r}{p_{s}^{*}-q}}\|u_{k}\|_{X^{s,p}_{0}(B(x,1))}^{p_{s}^{*}\frac{r-q}{p_{s}^{*}-q}}.

Next, for this choice of
\beta
we have
r=\frac{p(p_{s}^{*}-q)+p_{s}^{*}q}{p_{s}^{*}}
so that
\frac{p_{s}^{*}}{p}\frac{r-q}{p_{s}^{*}-q}=p.
Thus we get

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{r}dy\leq C\Bigg(\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{q}dy\Bigg)^{\frac{p_{s}^{*}-r}{p_{s}^{*}-q}}\|u_{k}\|_{X^{s,p}_{0}(B(x,1))}^{p}.

Using the covering lemma
[
31
, Lemma 5.7.5]
, we can choose a covering
\{B(x_{i},1)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
\mathbb{G}
such that no point of
\mathbb{G}
belongs to more than
(4C_{0}^{2})^{Q}
such balls, where
C_{0}
is the constant appearing in the triangle inequality of the quasi-norm
|\cdot|
on
\mathbb{G}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u_{k}|^{r}dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\cup_{i}B(x_{i},1)}|u_{k}|^{r}dy\leq\sum_{i}\int_{B(x_{i},1)}|u_{k}|^{r}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i}\Bigg(\int_{B(x_{i},1)}|u_{k}|^{q}dy\Bigg)^{\frac{p_{s}^{*}-r}{p_{s}^{*}-q}}\|u_{k}\|_{X^{s,p}_{0}(B(x_{i},1))}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C(4C_{0})^{Q}\Bigg(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{q}dy\Bigg)^{\frac{p_{s}^{*}-r}{p_{s}^{*}-q}}\sum_{i}\|u_{k}\|_{X^{s,p}_{0}(B(x_{i},1))}^{p}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq C(4C_{0})^{Q}\Big(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}\int_{B(x,1)}|u_{k}|^{q}dy\Big)^{\frac{p_{s}^{*}-r}{p_{s}^{*}-q}}\|u_{k}\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})},

proving (
3.2
).
Thus, applying the hypothesis (
3.1
) in (
3.2
) we conclude that

\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u_{k}|^{r}dy=0,

for
r\in(q,p_{s}^{*}).
This implies that
u_{k}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{r}(\mathbb{G})
for
r\in(q,p_{s}^{*}).
∎

4.
Existence of least energy solutions for (
1.3
)

This section is devoted to proving Theorem
1.1
. Throughout this section, we always assume that
p<q<p_{s}^{*}
. For the convenience of readers, let us recall the subelliptic fractional stationary Schrödinger equation (
1.3
) which is the main object of study in Theorem
1.1
:

(4.1)

\left(-\Delta_{p,\mathbb{G}}\right)^{s}u+|u|^{p-2}u=|u|^{q-2}u,\quad u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G}),

where
p<q<p_{s}^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}
.
Next, we will discuss several definitions which would be crucial for the proof of Theorem
1.1
below. We begin by defining the notion of a weak solution for the nonlocal equation (
4.1
).

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
is a weak solution of (
4.1
) if and only if for all
\phi\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
, we have

(4.2)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(|u|^{p-2}u\phi-|u|^{q-2}u\phi\right)dx=0.

The energy functional
I:W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
associated to the problem (
4.1
) is defined by

I(u):=\frac{1}{p}\iint_{\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p}dx-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx

such that

\displaystyle\langle I^{\prime}(u),\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(|u|^{p-2}u\phi-|u|^{q-2}u\phi\right)dx,

for every
\phi\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G}).
In particular, by setting
\mathcal{L}(u):=\langle I^{\prime}(u),u\rangle,
we get

\mathcal{L}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(|u|^{p}-|u|^{q}\right)dx.

Denote the Nehari manifold by

(4.3)

\mathcal{N}:=\left\{u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})\setminus\{0\}:\mathcal{L}(u)=\langle I^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\right\}

and set

(4.4)

d:=\inf\{I(u):u\in\mathcal{N}\}.

Since it is clear that the weak solutions of (
4.1
) are the critical points of the energy functional
I
, we define the following set:

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be the set of weak solutions of (
4.1
), namely, we define

\Gamma:=\left\{\phi\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G}):I^{\prime}(\phi)=0\text{ and }\phi\neq 0\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the set of least energy solutions of (
4.1
), that is,

\mathcal{G}:=\{u\in\Gamma:I(u)\leq I(v)\text{ for any }v\in\Gamma\}.

The following supporting lemmas will be useful in what follows.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})\setminus\{0\}
, there is a unique
\theta_{u}>0
such that
\theta_{u}u\in

\mathcal{N}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{L}(u)<0
, then
0<\theta_{u}<1
.

Proof.

For any arbitrary
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})\setminus\{0\}
and

\theta_{u}=\|u\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})}^{\frac{p}{q-p}}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}^{-\frac{q}{q-p}},

a simple calculation yields that

\theta_{u}u\in\mathcal{N}
. It is easy to see that this
\theta_{u}
is unique. From above and the expression of
\mathcal{L}(u)=\|u\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}-\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}^{q}
, we know that if
\mathcal{L}(u)<0
, that is,
\|u\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}<\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}^{q}
, then
0<\theta_{u}<1
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

There is
\rho>0
such that for all
u\in\mathcal{N}
we have
\|u\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})}\geq\rho
.

Proof.

Since
u\in\mathcal{N},
we have
\|u\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}^{q}.
Therefore, from the subelliptic fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.16
) and subelliptic fractional Sobolev inequality (
2.13
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}^{q}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{spq-Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{\frac{spq-Q(q-p)}{sp}}\leq C_{Q,s,p}\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp}}\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{\frac{spq-Q(q-p)}{sp}}=C_{Q,s,p}\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{q},

which implies that
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{p-q}\geq C_{Q,s,p}^{-1}
. Setting
\rho=C_{Q,s,p}^{-\frac{1}{p-q}}>0
, we get that
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}\geq\rho
for all
u\in\mathcal{N}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
v\in\mathcal{N}
and
I(v)=d:=\inf\{I(u):u\in\mathcal{N}\}.
Then
v
is a least energy solution of (
4.1
).

Proof.

Let
I(v)=d
for
v\in\mathcal{N}.
This implies that

v
is a minimiser for
d
. Therefore, from the Lagrange multiplier rule, we infer that there is
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
such that for any
\psi\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
,

(4.5)

\Big\langle I^{\prime}(v),\psi\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{G}}=\theta\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\prime}}(v),\psi\right\rangle_{\mathbb{G}}.

Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{G}}
is a dual product between
W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
and its dual space.
Note that as
q>p
we conclude, noting that
v\in\mathcal{N}
and so
\mathcal{L}(v)=\|u\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}-\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}^{q}=0,
that

\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\prime}}(v),v\right\rangle_{\mathbb{G}}=p\|v\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}-q\int_{\mathbb{G}}|v|^{q}dx=(p-q)\int_{\mathbb{G}}|v|^{q}dx<0.

In addition,
\left\langle I^{\prime}(v),v\right\rangle_{\mathbb{G}}=\mathcal{L}(v)=0
by the choice of
v
. Consequently, (
4.5
) forces that
\theta=0
. Hence
I^{\prime}(v)=0
. From Definition
4.2
, one sees easily that
v
is a least energy solution of (
4.1
).
∎

Having these supporting lemmas in hand, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us choose
\left(v_{n}\right)\subset\mathcal{N}
as a minimising sequence. Then, from the Ekeland variational principle, we deduce that there is a sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)\subset\mathcal{N}
such that

I(u_{n})\rightarrow d\quad\text{ and }\mathcal{L}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0.

The subellitic fractional Sobolev inequality (
2.13
) and Lemma
4.4
imply that there exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

C_{1}\leq\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}\leq C_{2}.

Combining this with the fact that
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|u_{n}\right|^{q}dx
as
(u_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{N}
, we have that there exists a positive constant
C_{3}
such that

(4.6)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|u_{n}(x)\right|^{q}dx\geq C_{3}>0.

Using the subelliptic Lions vanishing lemma (see Lemma
3.1
), we have that
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{q}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)
for any
p<q<p_{s}^{*}
provided that

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{G}}\int_{B(y,1)}\left|u_{n}(x)\right|^{q}dx=0.

Therefore, (
4.6
) implies that there exists
C_{4}>0
such that

(4.7)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{G}}\int_{B(y,1)}\left|u_{n}(x)\right|^{q}dx\geq C_{4}>0.

Therefore, we may assume that there is a
\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{G}
such that

(4.8)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B\left(\tilde{y},1\right)}\left|u_{n}(x)\right|^{p}dx\geq\frac{C_{4}}{2}>0.

Using the bi-invariance property of the Haar measure
dx
on
\mathbb{G},
a direct computation yields that

I(u_{n}(y\circ\tilde{y}))=I(u_{n}(y))

and

\mathcal{L}(u_{n}(y\circ\tilde{y}))=\mathcal{L}(u_{n}(y))

for all
\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{G}.

Let us define a new sequence
w_{n}(y):=u_{n}(y\circ\tilde{y})
. It is clear that
I(w_{n})=I(u_{n})
and
\mathcal{L}(w_{n})=\mathcal{L}(u_{n})
. Moreover, it provides that
(w_{n})
is bounded in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
satisfying

(4.9)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B(0,1)}\left|w_{n}(x)\right|^{q}dx\geq\frac{C_{4}}{2}>0.

Passing to a subsequence we may assume
w_{n}\rightarrow\phi
weakly in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
. Theorem
2.4
implies that
w_{n}\rightarrow\phi
strongly in
L_{\text{loc}}^{q}(\mathbb{G})
. Combining this with (
4.9
), we know that
\phi\neq 0
.

Next we will show that
(w_{n})
converges to
\phi
strongly in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
. For this purpose, we first prove that
\mathcal{L}(\phi)=0.
Contrary to this, let us consider the first case, if possible,
\mathcal{L}(\phi)<0
. Then, Lemma
4.3
immediately implies that there exits a
0<\theta_{\phi}<1
such that
\theta_{\phi}\phi\in\mathcal{N}
. Therefore using the Fatou’s lemma and the fact that
\mathcal{L}(w_{n})=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle d+o(1)

\displaystyle=I(w_{n})=\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|w_{n}\right|^{q}dx-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|w_{n}\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|w_{n}\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|\phi\right|^{q}dx+o(1)=\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\theta_{\phi}^{-q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|\theta_{\phi}\phi\right|^{q}dx+o(1)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\theta_{\phi}^{-q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|\theta_{\phi}\phi\right|^{q}dx-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\theta_{\phi}^{-q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|\theta_{\phi}\phi\right|^{p}dx+o(1)

(4.10)

\displaystyle=\theta_{\phi}^{-q}I(\theta_{\phi}\phi)+o(1).

We now deduce from
0<\theta_{\phi}<1
that
I(\theta_{\phi}\phi)<d
, which is a contradiction as
\theta_{\phi}\phi\in\mathcal{N}
.

Now, we consider the remaining case,
\mathcal{L}(\phi)>0
. Then, from Brezis-Lieb lemma
[
13
, Theorem 1.3.5]
with
\psi_{n}=w_{n}-\phi
we have that

0=\mathcal{L}(w_{n})=\mathcal{L}(\phi)+\mathcal{L}(\psi_{n})+o(1).

As a consequence, along with the fact that
\mathcal{L}(\phi)>0
, one deduces that

(4.11)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{L}\left(\psi_{n}\right)<0.

By Lemma
4.3
, there is positive constant
\theta_{n}:=\theta_{\psi_{n}}
such that
\theta_{n}\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{N}
. Moreover, we claim that
\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\theta_{n}\in(0,1)
. Indeed, suppose that
\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\theta_{n}=1
. Then, we can extract a subsequence
\left(\theta_{n_{j}}\right)
such that
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\theta_{n_{j}}=1
. Thus from the property
\theta_{n_{j}}\psi_{n_{j}}\in\mathcal{N}
, we obtain that
\mathcal{L}\left(\psi_{n_{j}}\right)=\mathcal{L}\left(\theta_{n_{j}}\psi_{n_{j}}\right)+o(1)=o(1)
. This is not possible in view of (
4.11
). Therefore, we obtain the required conclusion that
\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\limsup}\,\theta_{n}\in(0,1)
. Since
\mathcal{L}(w_{n})=0,
similar to (
4
), using the Fatou’s lemma we get

\displaystyle d+o(1)

\displaystyle=I\left(w_{n}\right)=\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|w_{n}\right|^{q}dx-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|w_{n}\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|w_{n}\right|^{q}dx\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|\psi_{n}\right|^{q}dx+o(1)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\theta_{n}^{-q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|\theta_{n}\psi_{n}\right|^{q}dx-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\theta_{n}^{-q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|\theta_{n}\psi_{n}\right|^{p}dx+o(1)

\displaystyle=\theta_{n}^{-q}I\left(\theta_{n}\psi_{n}\right)+o(1).

This along with the fact that
\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim\sup}\,\theta_{n}\in(0,1)
implies that
d>I\left(\theta_{n}\psi_{n}\right)
, which is a contradiction as
\theta_{n}\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{N}
.

By joining both cases together, we conclude that

\mathcal{L}(\phi)=0
.

We are now in a position to prove our claim that
\psi_{n}=w_{n}-\phi\rightarrow 0
in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
. Indeed, if possible, suppose that it is not true, that means,
\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})}\nrightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Then as an application of the continuous fractional Sobolev embedding (
2.13
), the following two situations are possible:

(i) When
\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|\psi_{n}\right|^{q}dx\nrightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
we have from

0=\mathcal{L}\left(w_{n}\right)=\mathcal{L}(\phi)+\mathcal{L}\left(\psi_{n}\right)+o(1)=\mathcal{L}\left(\psi_{n}\right)+o(1)

and therefore,
\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{N}.

The Brezis-Lieb lemma
[
13
, Theorem 1.3.5]
implies that

d+o(1)=I\left(w_{n}\right)=I(\phi)+I\left(\psi_{n}\right)+o(1)\geq d+d+o(1),

which is not true as
d>0
.

(ii) When
\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|\psi_{n}\right|^{q}dx\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
we have that

d+o(1)=I\left(w_{n}\right)=I(\phi)+\frac{1}{p}\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}+o(1)\geq d+\frac{1}{p}\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}+o(1)>d

which is impossible.
Thus, our assumption that
\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})}\nrightarrow 0
is not correct. Therefore, we deduce that
(w_{n})
converges strongly to
\phi
in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{G})
and
\phi
is a minimiser for
d
. Finally, Lemma
4.5
implies that
\phi
is a least energy solution of (
4.1
). This completes the proof. ∎

5.
Best constants in the subelliptic fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities

Throughout this section, we continue to assume the same range of
q,
that is,
p<q<p_{s}^{*}
as in the previous section. The aim of this section is to give a sharp estimate of
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}
in (
1.16
). Firstly, we give some properties of the least energy solutions of (
4.1
) which will be used in what follows.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\phi
be a least energy solution of (
4.1
). Then

(5.1)

\iint_{\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy=\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx

and

(5.2)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{q}dx=\frac{pqs}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx.

Proof.

Since
\phi
is a least energy solution of (
4.1
), we obtain from (
4.2
) that

(5.3)

\iint_{\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{q}dx.

Define a function
\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda}
for
\lambda>0
by
\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda}(x):=\lambda^{\frac{Q}{p}}\phi\left(D_{\lambda}(x)\right)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle I(\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{Q}}{p}\iint_{\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda}(x)-\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda}(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\frac{\lambda^{Q}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda}(x)|^{p}dx-\frac{\lambda^{\frac{Qq}{p}}}{q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda}(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{sp}}{p}\iint_{\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx-\frac{\lambda^{\frac{Qq}{p}-Q}}{q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{q}dx.

Then, it follows that

(5.4)

0=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}I\left(\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\right)\right|_{\lambda=1}=s\iint_{\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy-\frac{Q(q-p)}{pq}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{q}dx.

Using (
5.4
) in (
5.3
) we get that

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx=\Bigg(1-\frac{Q(q-p)}{spq}\Bigg)\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{q}dx,

which implies that

(5.5)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{q}dx=\frac{pqs}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx

establishing (
5.2
). Using this in (
5.3
), we get

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy=\Bigg(\frac{pqs}{pqs-Q(q-p)}-1\Bigg)\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx=\frac{Q(p-q)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx,

proving (
5.1
).
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

T_{\rho,p,q,s}:=\inf\left\{\|u\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}:u\in W_{0}^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)~\text{ and }~\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx=\rho\right\}.

If
\phi
is a minimiser obtained in Theorem
1.1
, then
\phi
is a minimiser of
T_{\rho_{0},p,q,s}
with
\rho_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{q}dx
.

Proof.

It is clear from the definition of
T_{\rho,p,q,s}
that
\|\phi\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}\geq T_{\rho_{0},p,q,s}
. Now, for any
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx=

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{q}dx
, by Lemma
4.3
there is a unique

\lambda_{0}=\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{\frac{p}{q-p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx\right)^{-\frac{1}{q-p}}

such that
\lambda_{0}u\in\mathcal{N},
which implies that
\mathcal{L}\left(\lambda_{0}u\right)=0
. Since
\lambda_{0}u\neq 0
and
\phi
achieves the minimum
d
, a simple calculation asserts that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\|\phi\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}

\displaystyle=I(\phi)\leq I\left(\lambda_{0}u\right)=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\lambda_{0}^{p}\|u\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{\frac{p^{2}}{q-p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{\frac{pq}{q-p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{q}dx
and
\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{q}dx=\|\phi\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})},
we get that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\|\phi\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{\frac{pq}{q-p}}\|\phi\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}^{-\frac{p^{2}}{q-p}}

implying that

\|\phi\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}\leq\|u\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}.

Since
u
is chosen arbitrarily, we get that
T_{\rho_{0},p,q,s}\geq\|\phi\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}
. Therefore, we have
T_{\rho_{0},p,q,s}=\|\phi\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}
and this show that
\phi
is a minimiser of
T_{\rho_{0},p,q,s}
. ∎

Now we are ready to obtain an expression for
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}
. In other words, we will now present a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We define the functional, for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})\backslash\{0\},

J(u):=\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{spq-Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}}{\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx}.

Then the sharp estimate of
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}
can be estimated by studying the following minimisation problem

C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}=\inf\left\{J(u):u\in W_{0}^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)\backslash\{0\}\right\}.

Let
\phi\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
be a least energy solution of (
1.3
). Then, by definition of
J
we obtain

C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}\leq J(\phi).

From Lemma
5.1
we obtain that

\displaystyle C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}\leq J(\phi)=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{spq-Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}}{\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{q}dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{spq-Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}}{\frac{pqs}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{pqs}\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{p}}.

Now, we will estimate
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}
from below. For any
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)\backslash\{0\}
, we define

w(x):=\lambda u\left(D_{\mu}(x)\right),

where
\lambda
and
\mu
are two positive parameters, which will be determined later. It is easy to see, by direct calculations, that

(5.8)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy=\lambda^{p}\mu^{ps-Q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy,

(5.9)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|w(x)|^{p}dx=\lambda^{p}\mu^{-Q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p}dx

and

(5.10)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|w(x)|^{q}dx=\lambda^{q}\mu^{-Q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx.

We choose
\lambda
and
\mu
such that

\lambda^{q}\mu^{-Q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx:=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{q}dx=\frac{pqs}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx

and

\lambda^{p}\mu^{-Q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p}dx:=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx.

From the above two equalities, it is straightforward to see that

\lambda^{p}=\left(\frac{pqs}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{p}{q-p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q-p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}

and

\displaystyle\mu^{ps-Q}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{pqs}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{p}{q-p}\cdot\frac{ps-Q}{Q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p}\cdot\frac{ps-Q}{Q}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}\cdot\frac{ps-Q}{Q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx\right)^{-\frac{ps-Q}{Q}}.

From the choice of the parameters
\lambda
and
\mu
as above and from (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), we have that

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|w|^{p}dx=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx

and

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|w|^{q}dx=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{q}dx.

Since
\phi
is a minimiser of
T_{\rho_{0},p,q,s}
with
\rho_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{q}dx
, by Lemma
5.2
we get that

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\geq\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy.

Therefore, using (
5.8
) and by Lemma
5.1
we get

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}\mu^{ps-Q}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy=\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx.

Therefore, by substituting the value of
\lambda^{p}
and
\mu^{ps-Q}
we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{pqs}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{p}{q-p}\left(1+\frac{ps-Q}{Q}\right)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q-p}+\frac{q}{q-p}\cdot\frac{ps-Q}{Q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{q}dx\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}\left(1+\frac{ps-Q}{Q}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx\right)^{-\frac{ps-Q}{Q}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\geq\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx.

It is now deduced by direct calculations that

J(u)\geq\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{pqs}\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{p^{2}s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{p}}.

Since
u
is chosen arbitrarily, we get that

C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}\geq\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{pqs}\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{p^{2}s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{p}}.

Therefore, combining the lower and upper estimate of
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}
we get the first equality of (
1.3
).

Now, using the property
d=I(\phi)
and Lemma
5.1
we have

\displaystyle d=I(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\iint_{\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|y^{-1}x|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{Q(q-p)}{p^{2}qs-pQ(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx-\frac{ps}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{(q-p)s}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx.

This implies that

(5.11)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx=\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{(q-p)s}d,

which combined with the first equality of (
1.3
) gives

C^{-1}_{GN,\mathbb{G}}=\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{pqs}\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{(q-p)s}d\right)^{\frac{q-p}{p}},

proving the second equality of (
1.3
). Hence, the proof of Theorem
1.3
is completed.
∎

6.
Best contants on the subelliptic fractional Sobolev inequalities

The purpose of this section is to discuss the sharp constant
C_{S,\mathbb{G}}
in the subelliptic fractional Sobolev inequalities on the stratified Lie groups.
Here
C_{S,\mathbb{G}}
is the smallest constant among all positive constants
C
such that

(6.1)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq C\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p})dx\right]

holds for any
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)
. Then
C_{S,\mathbb{G}}
can be given in the following form:

(6.2)

C_{S,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}:=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p})dx\right]}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}.

In the following result, we present an expression for
C_{S,\mathbb{G}}
in terms of the least energy solution of (
1.3
).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
p<q<p_{s}^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}
and
s\in(0,1)
. Let
\phi
be a least energy solution of (
1.3
) and let
C_{S,\mathbb{G}}>0
be the least positive constant such that the inequality (
6.1
) is true. Then we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle C_{S,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{spq}{spq-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}=\left(\frac{pq}{q-p}d\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}},

where
d=I(\phi):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}I(u)
.

Proof.

Since
\phi
is a least energy solution of (
4.1
),
\mathcal{L}(\phi)=0
and Lemma
5.1
imply that

\displaystyle\frac{\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p})dx\right]}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{spq}{spq-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}},

which further shows that

(6.4)

C_{S,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}\leq\left(\frac{spq}{spq-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}.

We define
\tilde{u}(x):=\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}^{-1}u(x)
for any
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)\backslash\{0\}
so that

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\tilde{u}(x)|^{q}dx=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{q}dx.

Therefore we have from Lemma
5.2
that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\tilde{u}(x)|^{p})dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p})dx

(6.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{spq}{spq-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx,

where we have used (
5.1
) in the last equality. Now a simple calculation gives that

\displaystyle\frac{\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p})dx\right]}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\tilde{u}(x)|^{p})dx\Bigg)\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}^{-p},

and therefore, using (
5.2
) and (
6
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p})dx\right]}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{spq}{spq-Q(q-p)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi|^{p}dx\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{-\frac{p}{q}}

(6.6)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{spq}{spq-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}.

By the arbitrariness of
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
in (
6
) we deduce that

(6.7)

C_{S,\mathbb{G}}^{-1}\geq\left(\frac{spq}{spq-Q(q-p)}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}.

Finally, the estimates (
6.4
) and (
6.7
) imply the first equality in (
6.3
). The last equality is an immediate consequence of a substitution (
5.11
) in the first equality of (
6.3
). This completes the proof. ∎

7.
Subelliptic fractional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities with explicit constants

The aim of this section is to obtain the subelliptic fractional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities with explicit constants on the stratified Lie groups. We adopt the method developed by Merker
[
56
]
(see also
[
14
]
). In this regard, we first prove the following result extending a result of Merker from the Euclidean space to general measure spaces.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a measure space.
The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The Hölder type inequality

(7.1)

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{X})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{a}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}^{1-a}

is valid for all
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{X})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{X})
for the parameters
p,q
and
r
ranging over
0<p\leq r\leq q\leq\infty
and satisfying
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{a}{p}+\frac{1-a}{q}
with
a\in[0,1].

(ii)

The logarithmic Hölder type inequality

(7.2)

\int_{\mathbb{X}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{p}}\log\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{p}}\right)dx\leq\frac{q}{q-p}\log\left(\frac{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{p}}\right),

is valid for all
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{X})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{X})
for the parameters
p
and
q
satisfying
0<p<q\leq\infty.

Proof.

We first observe that the validity of (
7.1
) for the parameters
p,q
and
r
ranging over
0<p\leq r\leq q\leq\infty
and satisfying
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{a}{p}+\frac{1-a}{q}
with
a\in[0,1]
is equivalent to the convexity of the function
\phi:\frac{1}{r}\mapsto\log(\|u\|_{r}).
Indeed, this follows by taking logarithms on both sides of (
7.1
) to have

\phi\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\leq a\phi\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)+(1-a)\phi\left(\frac{1}{q}\right),

with
a\in[0,1]
satisfying
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{a}{p}+\frac{1-a}{q}.

Now, the derivative of the function

\phi(h):=\log(\|u\|_{\frac{1}{h}})=h\log\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}|u(x)|^{\frac{1}{h}}dx\Big)

is given by

\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(h)=\log\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}|u(x)|^{\frac{1}{h}}dx\Big)-\frac{1}{h}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{X}}|u(x)|^{\frac{1}{h}}\log(|u(x)|)dx}{\int_{\mathbb{X}}|u(x)|^{\frac{1}{h}}dx}.

Since the convexity of
\phi
on
[0,\infty)
is equivalent to
\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(h)\geq\frac{\phi(h_{1})-\phi(h)}{h_{1}-h}
for
h>h_{1}\geq 0,
by taking
h=\frac{1}{p}
and
h_{1}=\frac{1}{q}
the convexity of
\phi
is equivalent to

(7.3)

\log\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\Big)-p\frac{\int_{\mathbb{X}}|u(x)|^{p}\log(|u(x)|)dx}{\int_{\mathbb{X}}|u(x)|^{p}dx}\geq\frac{qp}{p-q}\log\Big(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}}\Big)

and therefore, equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{p}}\log\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{p}}\right)dx

(7.4)

\displaystyle=p\frac{\int_{\mathbb{X}}|u(x)|^{p}\log(|u(x)|)dx}{\int_{\mathbb{X}}|u(x)|^{p}dx}-\log\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\Big)\leq\frac{q}{q-p}\log\left(\frac{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{p}}\right),

establishing the validity of (
7.2
) for the range
0<p<q\leq\infty
and its equivalence with (
7.1
) as this process is reversible at every step.
∎

The following result follows from Theorem
7.1
by noting that the inequality (
7.1
) holds for all
1\leq p\leq r\leq q\leq\infty
satisfying
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{a}{q}+\frac{1-a}{q}
with
a\in[0,1]
as a result of the usual Hölder inequality in a measure space. We note here that this result is an improvement of a result established by the third author and collaborators
[
14
]
in view of the inclusion of endpoints.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a measure space. Let
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{X})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{X})\backslash\{0\}
with
1\leq p<q\leq\infty.
Then we have

(7.5)

\int_{\mathbb{X}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{p}}\log\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}}\right)dx\leq\frac{q}{q-p}\log\left(\frac{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{X})}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{p}}\right).

Next result is the subelliptic fractional logarithmic Sobolev inequality for inhomogeneous fractional Sobolev spaces on stratified Lie groups.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1),

p\in(1,\infty),Q>ps,
and
p<q<p^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps},
where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of a stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}.

Then for any
u\neq 0
we have

(7.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}\log\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}}\right)dx\leq\frac{Q}{s}\log\left(C_{S,\mathbb{G},p}\frac{\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p})dx\right]}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(G)}^{p}}\right),

where
C_{S,\mathbb{G},p}
is given by

C_{S,\mathbb{G},p}:=\left(\frac{s}{Qd}\right)^{\frac{sp}{Q}}.

Here
d=I(\phi):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}I(u)
,
\phi
being a least energy solution of (
1.3
).

Proof.

The logarithmic Hölder inequality (
7.2
) in the case when
\mathbb{X}=(\mathbb{G},dx)
and the fractional subelliptic Sobolev inequality (
1.19
) imply that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}}\right)dx\leq\frac{q}{q-p}\log\left(\frac{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}\right)

(7.7)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q}{q-p}\log\left(C_{S,\mathbb{G}}\frac{\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p})dx\right]}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}\right).

Observe that the only place where the parameter
q
appears in (
7
) are constants
\frac{q}{q-p}
and
C_{S,\mathbb{G}}.
Therefore, by minimising the constant
\frac{q}{q-p}
over the range of
q\in(p,p_{s}^{*})
we get that

\underset{{q\in(p,p_{s}^{*})}}{\min}\frac{q}{q-p}=\frac{\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}}{\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}-p}=\frac{Q}{sp}.
Therefore, from (
7
) we deduce that

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}\leq\frac{Q}{sp}\log\left(C_{S,\mathbb{G},p}\frac{\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(x)|^{p})dx\right]}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}\right),

where the constant
C_{S,\mathbb{G},p}
is given by

C_{S,\mathbb{G},p}:=\limsup_{q\rightarrow\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}}C_{S,\mathbb{G}}=\limsup_{q\rightarrow\frac{Qp}{Q-ps}}\left(\frac{pq}{q-p}d\right)^{\frac{p-q}{q}}=\left(\frac{s}{Qd}\right)^{\frac{sp}{Q}},

completing the proof.
∎

The following theorem gives the subelliptic fractional logarithmic Sobolev inequality for homogeneous fractional Sobolev spaces on stratified Lie groups.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
s\in(0,1),

p\in(1,\infty),Q>ps,
and
p<q<p^{*}:=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps},
where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of a stratified Lie group
\mathbb{G}.

Then for any
u\neq 0
, we have

(7.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}\log\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}}\right)dx\leq\frac{Q}{s}\log\left(C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{ps}{Q(q-p)}}\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(G)}}\right),

where
C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}
is the best constant in the fractional subelliptic Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality given by

C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}:=\frac{pqs}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\left(\frac{Q(q-p)}{pqs-Q(q-p)}\right)^{\frac{Q(p-q)}{sp^{2}}}\left(\frac{pqs-Q(q-p)}{(q-p)s}d\right)^{\frac{p-q}{p}}.

Here
d=I(\phi):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}I(u)
,
\phi
being a least energy solution of (
1.3
).

Proof.

Using the fractional subelliptic Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.16
) and the logarithmic Hölder inequality (
7.5
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}}\right)dx\leq\frac{q}{q-p}\log\left(\frac{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q}{q-p}\log\left(C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{p}{q}}\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{spq}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{spq-Q(q-p)}{spq}}}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(G)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{q-p}\log\left(C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{p}{q}}\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{spq}}}{\|u\|^{\frac{Q(q-p)}{sq}}_{L^{p}(G)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{Q}{s}\log\left(C_{GN,\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{ps}{Q(q-p)}}\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{\left|y^{-1}x\right|^{Q+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(G)}}\right),

completing the proof of (
7.8
).
∎

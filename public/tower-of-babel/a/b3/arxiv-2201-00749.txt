Spectral cocycle for substitution tilings Thanks: The research of B. S. was supported by the Israel Science Foundation grant 911/19.
Boris Solomyak, Rodrigo Treviño

Abstract
. The construction of spectral cocycle from the case of 1-dimensional substitution flows [ 10 ] is extended to the setting of pseudo-self-similar tilings in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} , allowing expanding similarities with rotations. The pointwise upper Lyapunov exponent of this cocycle is used to bound the local dimension of spectral measures of deformed tilings. The deformations are considered, following [ 39 ] , in the simpler, non-random setting. We review some of the results on quantitative weak mixing from [ 39 ] in this special case and illustrate them on concrete examples.

1.
Introduction

We extend the construction of spectral cocycle and partially extend the results, from the case of 1-dimensional substitution flows treated in
[
10
]
, to higher dimensions.
Another motivation for us is to make the results of
[
39
]
more accessible by presenting them in the simplest possible nontrivial case of a single self-similar tiling (corresponding to a stationary Bratteli diagram in
[
39
]
). We also indicate how the results of
[
39
]
, where it is assumed that the expansion map is a pure dilation, may be extended
to the case of general expanding similarities with rotations, which necessitates dealing with fractal boundaries and passing from self-similar to pseudo-self-similar tiling spaces.
We do not repeat the rather technical proofs of
[
39
]
, but illustrate the results on concrete examples (Kenyon’s tilings defined via free group endomorphisms
[
24
]
and a “square” tiling).

The
spectral cocycle
was introduced in
[
10
]
for translation flows and
S
-adic systems; here we are concerned with the case of a single substitution. Briefly, given a
primitive aperiodic substitution
\zeta
on
m
symbols with a non-singular substitution matrix
{\sf S}_{\zeta}
, the spectral cocycle from
[
10
]
is a complex matrix
m\times m
cocycle over the endomorphism of the
m
-torus induced by the transpose
{\sf S}_{\zeta}^{\sf T}
.

(In fact, in the case when
{\sf S}_{\zeta}
is singular, one can restrict the underlying system to a lower-dimensional sub-torus on which non-singularity holds, see
[
41
]
.)
Implicitly, the spectral cocycle appeared already in
[
8
]
as a generalized matrix Riesz product.
In
[
10
]
it was shown that the (top, pointwise upper) Lyapunov exponents of this cocycle in some sense control the local spectral behavior of substitution
{\mathbb{R}}
-actions – suspension flows over the usual substitution
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action, with a piecewise-constant roof function. By “control” we mean lower and upper bounds for the local dimension of spectral measures of cylindrical functions, see
[
8
]
for details. Independently, Baake et al
[
4
,
5
]
introduced a
Fourier matrix cocycle
,
which coincides with the spectral cocycle on the one-dimensional
{\sf S}_{\zeta}^{\sf T}
-invariant manifold on the torus corresponding to the Perron-Frobenius eigenvector; it is tailored for the spectral analysis of (geometrically) self-similar tiling flows and was used to prove singularity of the diffraction spectrum for a class of non-Pisot substitution systems. In
[
39
]
, it appeared as the application of traces applied to elements of certain AF algebras.

The tools of spectral cocycle (without calling it such) provided a framework for the proof of almost sure Hölder regularity of translation flows on higher genus flat surfaces, first in
[
9
]
for genus 2 and then in
[
11
]
for an arbitrary genus greater than 1, including many surfaces of infinite genus and finite area. (The last paper appeared after the preprint of Forni
[
15
]
who used a different technique.)

The work
[
39
]
extended almost sure Hölder regularity results of
[
11
]
to the case of (globally) random substitution tilings in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. In a sense, this is a higher-dimensional
version of an
S
-adic system, in which self-similar tile substitutions are applied randomly, according to an underlying ergodic dynamical system, resulting in a tiling
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-action. A crucial distinction with the one-dimensional case is that one needs to define what is meant by a “deformation” of the tiling dynamical system. Whereas for
d=1
it was rather natural to vary the tile lengths, or equivalently, view the system as a suspension with a piecewise-constant roof function and vary the “heights”, this issue becomes more complicated for
d\geq 2
. In this setting
admissible deformations
were studied,
first by Clark and Sadun
[
13
]
and then by Kellendonk
[
22
]
and Julien and Sadun
[
21
]
; this required dealing with the (e.g., Čech) cohomology group
\check{H}^{1}(X,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, where
X
is the tiling space. The spectral cocycle for
d\geq 2

is still a complex matrix
m\times m
cocycle, where
m
is the number of prototiles, but the cocycle is over the endomorphism induced by the inflation map on the first cohomology group. Incidentally, this point of view offers an advantage even for
d=1
.

The Fourier matrix cocycle has been introduced for self-similar tilings in
[
5
]
, see also
[
6
]
; it is a cocycle over the inflation map of the entire
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and it coincides with the spectral cocycle on a measure zero subset. In this case it was also used to prove singularity for some systems, such as the Godrèche-Lançon-Billard tiling.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the background on tilings and tiling dynamical systems, which is mostly standard and appeared in many articles and books.
We also discuss pseudo-self-similar tilings, which are needed in order to define deformations when the prototiles of the original self-similar tiling have fractal boundaries.
Section
3
deals with cohomology and deformations. It also includes a treatment of geometric properties of deformed tiling spaces and their consequences, in particular, unique ergodicity and a
formula for measures of cylinder sets. In Section
4
we define the spectral cocycle and state the main result on local dimension estimates. In Section
5
we first state the result saying that for deformed tiling spaces weak mixing is equivalent to topological weak mixing, which was characterized in
[
13
]
.
The proof is a minor variation of the argument from
[
34
]
; we sketch it in Section
7
. Next we explain how a natural quantitative strengthening of the condition, which we call the
“quantitative Host-Veech criterion”, yields Hölder regularity of spectral measures and quantitative weak mixing.
Further, we state a theorem on quantitative weak mixing, which is essentially a special case of
[
39
, Theorem 1]
,
but we believe it is more accessible and easier to apply. A brief proof outline is included. Finally, we revisit the case of one-dimensional substitution tilings and show how to extend

[
8
, Th. 4.1]
to the case of reducible substitution matrix.
Section
6
is devoted to examples. There is a detailed discussion of a family of pseudo self-similar planar tilings due to Kenyon
[
24
]
, to which our results apply. An example with
of “square tilings” is included as well.
Section
7
contains the remaining proofs.

2.
Background

2.1.
Tilings and tiling spaces

A
tiling

{\mathcal{T}}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a covering by compact sets, called
tiles
, such that their interiors don’t intersect. The tiles need not be homeomorphic to balls or even connected.
We assume that every tile is a closure of its interior.
We will consider the
translation
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-action
on tiling spaces (defined below in
2.1
): the translation of
{\mathcal{T}}
by
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is
\{T-t:\ T\in{\mathcal{T}}\}
.
Strictly speaking, a tile is a pair
T=(A,j)
, where
A
is the
support
of
T
and
j
is its
type
,
color
, or
label
. This is needed, since frequently we need to distinguish geometrically equivalent
tiles. However, to avoid cluttered notation, we will often think of tiles as sets with colors, or labels. The colors are assumed to be preserved under translations, linear maps, etc.

A
patch
of the tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
is a finite subset of
{\mathcal{T}}
. For two patches
{\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{T}}
we write
{\mathcal{P}}\sim{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}
if there exists
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{P}}-t
; such patches are called

translation-equivalent
.
For a bounded
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
define the patches:

{\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{\mathcal{T}}(A)\ =\ \mbox{the largest patch of ${\mathcal{T}}$ completely contained in $A$};

{\mathcal{O}}^{+}_{\mathcal{T}}(A)\ =\ \mbox{the smallest patch of ${\mathcal{T}}$ containing $A$}.

We will always assume that all our tilings have (translational)
finite local complexity
, or FLC: for any
R>0
there are finitely many patches of the form
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{T}}^{-}(B_{R}(x))
up to translation equivalence. In particular, there are finitely many tiles up to translation. We fix a collection of representatives
{\mathcal{A}}=\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}\}
and call them
prototiles
. Another standing assumption will be that
{\mathcal{T}}
is
repetitive
, i.e., for any patch
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}
there exists
R_{\mathcal{P}}>0
such that any ball
B_{R_{\mathcal{P}}}(x)
, with
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, contains a
{\mathcal{T}}
-patch
{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}\sim{\mathcal{P}}
.

The
tiling space
(or “hull”) determined by
{\mathcal{T}}
is the closure of the translation orbit:

(2.1)

X_{\mathcal{T}}:={\rm clos}\{{\mathcal{T}}-x:\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}

in the standard “local” metric, in which
{\mathcal{T}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
are
{\varepsilon}
-close for
{\varepsilon}>0
sufficiently small if and only if
there exists
t\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)
such that
{\mathcal{T}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}-t
coincide in
B_{1/{\varepsilon}}(0)
.
The
tiling dynamical system

(X_{\mathcal{T}},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is the
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-action by translations.
If
{\mathcal{T}}
is repetitive, then
X_{\mathcal{T}}=X_{{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}}
for any
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}\in X_{{\mathcal{T}}}
, so the tiling dynamical system is minimal. We will often omit the subscript
{\mathcal{T}}
and simply write
X
for a tiling space.
An alternative way to define the tiling space is via the
atlas
of admissible patches (analogous to a language in symbolic dynamics).

2.1.1.
Functions

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a tiling of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. A function
h:X_{\mathcal{T}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is
transversally locally constant
(TLC) if there exists
R>0
such that

{\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}}(B_{R}(0))={\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime\prime}}(B_{R}(0))\implies h({\mathcal{T}}^{\prime})=h({\mathcal{T}}^{\prime\prime}).

A function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is
{\mathcal{T}}
-equivariant if there exists
R>0
such that

(2.2)

{\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(B_{R}(x))={\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(B_{R}(y))+(x-y)\implies f(x)=f(y).

Given a TLC-function
h:X_{\mathcal{T}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we get a
{\mathcal{T}}
-equivariant function via
f(x):=h({\mathcal{T}}-x)
.

2.1.2.
Mutual local derivability

For two tilings
{\mathcal{T}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{2}
, we say that
{\mathcal{T}}_{2}
is
locally derivable (LD)
from
{\mathcal{T}}_{1}
with radius
R>0
if for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

{\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{{\mathcal{T}}_{1}}(B_{R}(x))={\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{{\mathcal{T}}_{1}}(B_{R}(y))+(x-y)\Rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}^{+}_{{\mathcal{T}}_{2}}(\{x\})={\mathcal{O}}^{+}_{{\mathcal{T}}_{2}}(\{y\})+(x-y).

If
{\mathcal{T}}
is LD from
{\mathcal{S}}
and vice-versa, then

we say that tilings
{\mathcal{T}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}
are
mutually locally derivable
(MLD). Note that this is similar in spirit to
{\mathcal{T}}
-equivariance. MLD implies conjugacy of tiling dynamical systems, but not the other way round, see
[
31
,
32
]
.
We will say that two minimal tiling spaces
X
and
X^{\prime}
are MLD if there exist
{\mathcal{T}}\in X
and
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
that are MLD.

2.1.3.
Frequency of patches and unique ergodicity

Denote
Q_{R}=[-R,R]^{d}
. The following is well-known and may be considered folklore. We refer the reader to
[
27
]
, which is written in the language of Delone multisets; passing to the tiling setting is routine.

Given an FLC tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
, we say that
{\mathcal{T}}
has
uniform patch frequencies
(UPF) if for any patch
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}
, the limit

{\rm freq}({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{T}})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\ t+{\mathcal{P}}\subset Q_{R}+x\}}{(2R)^{d}}\geq 0

exists uniformly in
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. For a repetitive tiling space, the UPF property and the uniform frequencies do not depend on the choice of the tiling.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
27
, Theorem 2.7]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be an FLC tiling in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The dynamical system
(X_{\mathcal{T}},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is uniquely ergodic if and only if
{\mathcal{T}}
has the UPF property.

Under the UPF assumption, we also have an explicit formula for the measure of “cylinder sets”. For a tiling space
X
we define the
transversal
of a patch
{\mathcal{P}}
by

\Upsilon({\mathcal{P}})=\{{\mathcal{T}}\in X:\ {\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}\}.

For a patch
{\mathcal{P}}
and a Borel set
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the corresponding cylinder set is

\Upsilon({\mathcal{P}})+U:=\{{\mathcal{T}}+x:\ {\mathcal{T}}\in\Upsilon({\mathcal{P}}),\ x\in U\}.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
27
, Corollary 2.8]
)
.

Let
X
be a uniquely ergodic FLC tiling space, with the unique invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
.
Let
\eta=\eta(X)
be the diameter of the largest ball contained in every prototile. Then for any patch
{\mathcal{P}}
and a Borel set
U
with
{\rm diam}(U)<\eta(X)
,

(2.3)

\mu(\Upsilon({\mathcal{P}})+U)={\mathcal{L}}^{d}(U)\cdot{\rm freq}({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{T}}).

2.2.
Self-similar tilings

Now we define a self-similar tiling space, in the sense of
[
38
,
24
,
34
]
.
Here we start with a substitution rule.
As before, suppose that we have a finite prototile set
\{{\mathfrak{T}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{T}}_{m}\}
, where each
{\mathfrak{T}}_{j}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is the closure of its interior, possibly with a fractal boundary.
Let
\varphi
be an expanding similarity on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, with expansion constant
\theta>1
(we do not assume that
\varphi
is a pure dilation; in general,
\varphi=\theta{\mathcal{O}}
, where
{\mathcal{O}}
is an orthogonal transformation). Assume that there is a
tile substitution

(2.4)

\omega({\mathfrak{T}}_{j})=\bigcup_{k\leq m}({\mathfrak{T}}_{k}+{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}),\ \ j\leq m,

where
{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}
is a finite set of translations and the right-hand side represents a patch such that

(2.5)

\varphi A_{j}=\bigcup_{k}(A_{k}+{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}),\ \ j\leq m,\ \ A_{j}={\rm supp}({\mathfrak{T}}_{j}).

For a translated prototile the substitution acts by

(2.6)

\omega({\mathfrak{T}}_{j}+x)=\omega({\mathfrak{T}}_{j})+\varphi(x),\ \ j\leq m,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

This and the property (
2.5
) imply that the substitution map can be iterated, resulting in larger and larger patches. In particular,

(2.7)

\omega^{2}({\mathfrak{T}}_{j})=\{{\mathfrak{T}}_{s}+\varphi{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}+{\mathcal{D}}_{ks}\}_{s\leq m,k\leq m},\ \ 1\leq j\leq m,

etc.
The
substitution tiling space

X_{\omega}
corresponding to
\omega
is, by definition, the collection of all tilings
{\mathscr{T}}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that every
{\mathscr{T}}
-patch is
a sub-patch of
\omega^{n}({\mathfrak{T}}_{j})
for some
j\leq m
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We will sometimes omit the subscript
\omega
and simply write
X
for the tiling space.
By definition, one can pass from
\omega
to
\omega^{k}
for
k\geq 2
, without affecting the substitution tiling space.
The substitution naturally extends to a continuous self-map of the tiling space
\omega:\,X_{\omega}\to X_{\omega}
. This map is surjective, but not necessarily injective. Note that

(2.8)

\omega({\mathscr{T}}-t)=\omega({\mathscr{T}})-\varphi(t),\ \ {\mathscr{T}}\in X_{\omega},\ t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

The
substitution matrix
is defined by

(2.9)

{\sf S}_{\omega}(j,k)=\#{\mathcal{D}}_{kj}.

We will assume that the substitution is
primitive
, i.e., some power of
{\sf S}_{\omega}
has all entries strictly positive.
Repetitivity of
{\mathscr{T}}\in X_{\omega}
implies primitivity. A tiling is called
self-similar
if it is a fixed point of
the substitution:
\omega({\mathscr{T}})={\mathscr{T}}
. Such a tiling always exists, after passing to a higher power of
\omega
, if necessary.
FLC repetitive self-similar tiling spaces are known to be uniquely ergodic, see
[
34
,
27
]
.

2.2.1.
Aperiodicity and recognizability; hierarchical structure

We will assume that our tiling spaces are
aperiodic
, i.e.,
{\mathscr{T}}={\mathscr{T}}-t
implies
t=0
.
It follows from (
2.8
) that aperiodicity of
X_{\omega}
is necessary for
\omega
to be injective. It turns out that it is also sufficient
[
35
]
. Then
\omega
is a homeomorphism, and we can “desubstitute” any
{\mathscr{T}}\in X_{\omega}
in a unique way; this property is usually called “recognizability”. Equivalently, there is a unique way to compose the tiles of
{\mathscr{T}}
into a collection of patches
of the form
\omega({\mathfrak{T}}_{j}),\ j\leq m
, in such a way that we get a tiling from the space
\varphi(X_{\omega})
. These patches are called
supertiles
of order 1. This procedure can be iterated; thus any
{\mathfrak{T}}\in{\mathscr{T}}
is contained in a unique increasing sequence of supertiles of order
n
.

2.2.2.
Substitution Delone set associated with a self-similar tiling

Let
{\mathscr{T}}
be a self-similar tiling,
\omega({\mathscr{T}})={\mathscr{T}}
, which we assume exists, without loss of generality. Specify the location of each prototile
{\mathfrak{T}}_{j}
in
{\mathscr{T}}
. Then

\omega({\mathfrak{T}}_{j})
is a
{\mathscr{T}}
-patch, with the support
\varphi({\rm supp}({\mathfrak{T}}_{j}))
. The definition (
2.4
) implies that each element of
{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}
is translation vector between two
occurrences of equivalent tiles in
{\mathscr{T}}
, namely,
{\mathfrak{T}}_{k}
and its translate. This means that
{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}
is a set of
return vectors
, which will be important in what follows.
We can write

(2.10)

{\mathscr{T}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{m}\bigl({\mathfrak{T}}_{k}+{\mathscr{L}}_{k}({\mathscr{T}})\bigr),

where
{\mathscr{L}}_{k}({\mathscr{T}})
represents the set of locations of tiles of type
k
in
{\mathscr{T}}
(relative to the prototiles). Each
{\mathscr{L}}_{k}({\mathscr{T}})
is a
Delone set
,
that is, a uniformly discrete relatively dense set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. By convention,
0\in{\mathscr{L}}_{k}({\mathscr{T}})
for each
k
.
Note that (
2.10
) and (
2.4
) yield a “dual system of equations” for the Delone sets:

(2.11)

{\mathscr{L}}_{k}({\mathscr{T}})=\coprod_{j\leq m}\bigl(\varphi{\mathscr{L}}_{j}({\mathscr{T}})+{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}\bigr),\ \ k\leq m.

This means that
\bigl({\mathscr{L}}_{k}({\mathscr{T}})\bigr)_{k\leq m}
is a
substitution Delone multiset
in the sense of
[
26
,
27
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
27
]
.
A family of Delone sets
\bigl({\mathscr{L}}_{k}\bigr)_{k\leq m}
is called a substitution Delone multiset with expansion
\varphi
if
there exist finite sets
{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}
such that

(2.12)

{\mathscr{L}}_{k}=\coprod_{j\leq m}\bigl(\varphi{\mathscr{L}}_{j}+{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}\bigr),\ \ k\leq m.

This notion was introduced by Lagarias and Wang
[
26
]
, except that they allowed each
\Lambda_{k}
to be a set “with multiplicities”.

We will also need a set of
control points
for the tiling
{\mathscr{T}}
and other tilings in
X_{\omega}
. The sets
{\mathscr{L}}_{k}
are not convenient, since they represent locations of tiles of type
k

in
{\mathscr{T}}

relative
to the prototiles
{\mathfrak{T}}_{k}\in{\mathscr{T}}
. To this end, pick a point
c({\mathfrak{T}}_{k})\in{\rm int}({\mathfrak{T}}_{k})
for each prototile,
and let
c({\mathfrak{T}}_{k}+x)=c({\mathfrak{T}}_{k})+x
for translated tiles. Then

\Lambda_{k}({\mathscr{T}}):={\mathscr{L}}_{k}({\mathscr{T}})+c({\mathfrak{T}}_{k})

represents the set of control points in all the tiles of type
k
, and

(2.13)

\Lambda({\mathscr{T}}):=\coprod_{k\leq m}\Lambda_{k}({\mathscr{T}})

is a Delone set of all the control points for the tiling. Note that

(\Lambda_{k}({\mathscr{T}}))_{k\leq m}
is a substitution Delone multiset as well, satisfying (
2.12
), with
{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}
replaced by
{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}+c({\mathfrak{T}}_{k})-\varphi c({\mathfrak{T}}_{j})
.

2.3.
Pseudo self-similar (PSS) tilings

Our main object in this paper is
tiling deformations
, which are more conveniently defined for tilings whose tiles are convex polytopes meeting face-to-face.
When the tile boundaries are fractal, which is necessarily the case if the expansion map
\varphi
involves an irrational rotation, an extra step is needed.
We are going to use (a variant of) the construction of
Derived Voronoi (DV) tilings
introduced by N. Priebe Frank
[
16
]
, which turns a self-similar tiling into a pseudo-self-similar one, but with “nice” convex polytope tiles.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an expanding similarity map. A repetitive tiling
\mathcal{T}
of finite local complexity is called a
pseudo-self-similar tiling
, or PSS, with expansion
\varphi
if
\mathcal{T}
is locally derivable from
\varphi\mathcal{T}
.

In fact, in
[
16
]
the map
\varphi
is allowed to be any expanding linear map, but here we restrict ourselves to similitudes.

Let
\Lambda
be a Delone set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The
Voronoi cell
of a point
x\in\Lambda
is, by definition,

V(x)=\bigl\{{\bf t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\ \|{\bf t}-x\|\leq\|{\bf t}-y\|,\ \forall\,y\in\Lambda\setminus\{x\}\bigr\},

and the corresponding
Voronoi tiling (tesselation)
is

{\mathcal{T}}_{\Lambda}:=\{V(x):\ x\in\Lambda\}.

The tiles of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\Lambda}
are convex polytopes meeting face-to-face. Applying this procedure to
\Lambda({\mathscr{T}})
, we want to make sure that the cells corresponding to equivalent tiles are also
translationally equivalent. This will usually require increasing the set of labels, by “decorating” each point with the translation equivalence class of a sufficiently large neighborhood.
It is always possible to achieve this, see
[
16
, Section 4]
. Below we assume that this has already been done and the equations (
2.4
),(
2.5
) still apply, possibly with a larger
m
.
Now let
\Lambda=\Lambda({\mathscr{T}})
be from (
2.13
) and consider the tiling
{\mathcal{T}}_{\Lambda}
, where the tiles “inherit” the labels from the corresponding control points. It can be shown, as in
[
16
]
, that, assuming that the labels carry the information about a sufficiently large neighborhood, the tiling
{\mathcal{T}}_{\Lambda}
is MLD with the original
{\mathscr{T}}
and hence the translation dynamics are conjugate.
If
{\mathscr{T}}
is self-similar, then
{\mathcal{T}}_{\Lambda}
, with
\Lambda=\Lambda({\mathscr{T}})
, is pseudo-self-similar with the same expansion map. We have

{\mathcal{T}}_{\Lambda}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{m}\bigl(T_{k}+{\mathscr{L}}_{k}({\mathscr{T}})\bigr),

where

{\rm supp}(T_{k})={\rm supp}\bigl(V_{\Lambda-x}(c({\mathfrak{T}}_{k})\bigr)\ \ \mbox{for}\ x\in{\mathscr{L}}_{k}({\mathscr{T}}),

and consistency (independence of
x
) is guaranteed by construction. For the pseudo-self-similar tiling
{\mathcal{T}}_{\Lambda}
we have a substitution, “inherited” from
\omega
, which we denote by
the same letter by abuse of notation. In fact, similarly to (
2.4
) and (
2.6
),

(2.14)

\omega(T_{j})=\bigcup_{k\leq m}\bigl(T_{k}+{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}\bigr),\ \ \ \ \omega(T_{j}+x)=\omega(T_{j})+\varphi(x),\ \ \ j\leq m,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

but the analog of (
2.5
) does not hold exactly – only approximately. Then the pseudo-self-similar (PSS) tiling satisfies
\omega({\mathcal{T}}_{\Lambda})={\mathcal{T}}_{\Lambda}
.
The atlas of patches of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\Lambda}
, or equivalently, its orbit closure in the natural topology, defines the PSS tiling space.
By construction, the PSS tiling space is MLD with the initial self-similar tiling space.

We summarize this discussion in the following proposition, but first we need some terminology.

Definition 2.5
.

A tiling of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
will be called polytopal if its tiles are convex polytopes meeting face-to-face, and this induces a structure of a CW-complex.

Definition 2.6
.

A repetitive FLC PSS tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
, with the prototile set
\{T_{j}\}_{j\leq m}
,
an expansion map
\varphi
, and a (combinatorial-geometric) substitution
\omega
, will be called an L-PSS tiling (L is for “lucky”) if it is polytopal and
there exists a substitution Delone multi-set
\bigl({\mathscr{L}}_{k}\bigr)_{k\leq m}
with expansion
\varphi
, satisfying (
2.12
), such that

{\mathcal{T}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{m}\bigl(T_{k}+{\mathscr{L}}_{k}\bigr)=\omega({\mathcal{T}})
and
\omega
acts by (
2.14
).

We have proved the following

Proposition 2.7
.

If
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
is a self-similar tiling with an expansion map
\varphi
, then there exists an L-PSS tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
with the expansion map
\varphi
, such that

{\mathcal{T}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
are MLD.

Remark 2.8
.

(a) Although the procedure described above is general, for specific examples there is frequently a direct and relatively simple way to obtain a PSS tiling from a self-similar one without increasing the number of prototiles. In fact, often a planar PSS tiling is given directly, equipped with a “substitution-with-amalgamation”, see
[
13
]
, and a procedure of “redrawing the boundary” is
used to show that it is MLD to a genuine self-similar one
[
18
]
. This is the case for the class of Kenyon’s tilings
[
24
]
which we analyze in Section
6
.

(b) The step of passing from a self-similar tiling to a PSS tiling is not needed if the self-similar tiling is polytopal to begin with.

(c) It is proved in
[
18
]
for tilings in the plane and in
[
36
]
in the general case, that any PSS tiling with an expansion map
\varphi
is MLD with a genuine
self-similar tiling, with the expansion map
\varphi^{n}
, for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Thus in the last proposition we could start with an arbitrary PSS tiling, at the cost of raising the associated
expansion map to a power.

3.
Cohomology and deformations

3.1.
Pattern equivariant cohomology

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an aperiodic repetitive tiling of finite local complexity and recall the definition of a
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant function (
2.2
). A
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant
k
-form is a
k
-form
\alpha
such that its coefficients are
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant functions. We denote the set of
C^{\infty}
,
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant
k
-forms by
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{k}
, which is a subspace of the set of smooth
k
-forms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. As such the restriction of the usual de Rham differential operator gives a differential operator on the complex
\{\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{k}\}_{k}
of
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant forms.

Definition 3.1
.

The cohomology of the complex of smooth
\mathcal{T}
equivariant forms

H^{k}(X;\mathbb{R}):=\frac{\ker d:\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{k}\rightarrow\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{k+1}}{\mathrm{im}\,d:\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{k-1}\rightarrow\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{k}}

is called the
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant cohomology
.

We denoted the cohomology as
H^{k}(X;\mathbb{R})
since it is independent of which tiling
\mathcal{T}\in X
we used to define it
[
23
]
.

3.2.
Čech cohomology

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an aperiodic, repetitive tiling of finite local complexity, which we now assume has a CW structure. More specifically, we assume that all the tiles of
\mathcal{T}
are
d
-cells of the CW complex
\mathcal{T}
where all tiles meet face-to-face. (In practice, we will work with polytopal tilings, see Definition
2.5
.)
For any tile
t\in\mathcal{T}
we define
\mathcal{T}(t)={\mathcal{T}}^{(1)}
, called the
1-corona of
t
, to be the set of all tiles in
\mathcal{T}
which intersect
t
. Continue recursively as follows:
Given a
(k-1)
-corona of a tile
\mathcal{T}^{(k-1)}(t)
, the
k
-corona
of the tile
t
is the patch

\mathcal{T}^{(k)}(t)=\{t^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}:t^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{T}^{(k-1)}(t)\neq\varnothing\}.

A
k
-collaring
of a tile
t
is the tile with the same support as
t
, but the label being the translation-equivalence class of
{\mathcal{T}}^{(k)}(t)
.
This is a useful tool to keep track of bigger neighborhoods of tiles by increasing the set of labels.

3.2.1.
The Anderson-Putnam complex

Following Anderson-Putnam
[
2
]
, we define a cell complex
{AP}_{0}(X)
by gluing together the prototiles along their faces in all ways in which they can be adjacent in the tiling space.
This can be done equally well for
k
-collared tiles (this just increases the set of labels). The resulting space is denoted
AP_{k}(X)
. Here is a formal definition:

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a tiling space of repetitive, aperiodic tilings of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of finite local complexity. We define on
X\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
an equivalence relation
\sim_{1}
, where
X
carries the discrete topology and
\mathbb{R}^{d}
the usual topology, as
(\mathcal{T}_{1},v_{1})\sim_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{2},v_{2})
if and only if
\mathcal{T}_{1}(t_{1})-v_{1}=\mathcal{T}_{2}(t_{2})-v_{2}
for some tiles
t_{1},t_{2}
with
v_{1}\in t_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
v_{2}\in t_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
. The space
X\times\mathbb{R}^{d}/\sim_{1}
is the
Anderson-Putnam complex
of
X
, denoted by
AP(X)
.
For
k\geq 0
the
k^{th}
-collared Anderson-Putnam complex
AP_{k}(X)
is similarly obtained by using
k
-collared tiles instead of
1
-collared tiles, where 0-collared tiles are just tiles.
Note that
AP(X)=AP_{1}(X)
by definition.

Assuming a tile substitution rule as in (
2.14
) is a cellular map, it defines a map
\gamma:AP(X)\rightarrow AP(X)
. The important observation of Anderson and Putnam is that, as defined,

X\cong\lim_{\longleftarrow}(AP(X),\gamma),

that is, the tiling space
X
is homeomorphic to the set of all infinite sequences
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in AP(X)^{\infty}
with the property that
\gamma(x_{i})=x_{i-1}
. What this result gives is the easy calculation of the Čech cohomology of
X
using the induced maps
\gamma^{*}
on the cohomology of
AP(X)
:

(3.1)

\check{H}^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong\lim_{\longrightarrow}\left(\check{H}^{*}(AP(X);\mathbb{Z}\right),\gamma^{*}).

3.2.2.
Cohomology for PSS tilings

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a polytopal
pseudo-self-similar tiling. The latter
means that there exists an
R>0
and expansive map
\varphi
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathcal{O}^{-}_{\mathcal{\varphi T}}(B_{R}(x))=\mathcal{O}^{-}_{\mathcal{\varphi T}}(B_{R}(x))+(x-y)\Longrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{-}_{\mathcal{T}}(B_{1}(x))=\mathcal{O}^{-}_{\mathcal{T}}(B_{1}(x))+(x-y).

Let
X
be the tiling space of
\mathcal{T}
and
X_{\varphi}
the tiling space of
\varphi\mathcal{T}
. If
\varphi
is pure dilation, then
AP(X_{\varphi})
is a rescaled copy of
AP(X)
by
\theta:=|\det\varphi|^{1/d}
, since linear expansive maps do not affect the process of collaring. If
\varphi
is not pure dilation, then
AP(X_{\varphi})
is a rescaled copy of
AP(X)
by
\theta
, but where all the cells have also been rotated.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be the tiling space of a polytopal pseudo-self-similar tiling
\mathcal{T}
.
Then there exists a
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
and a map
\gamma:AP_{\kappa}(X)\rightarrow AP_{\kappa}(X)
such that

(3.2)

X\cong\lim_{\longleftarrow}\left(AP_{\kappa}(X),\gamma\right).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{T}
is PSS with expanding map
\varphi
, there exists an
R>0
such that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the
R
-neighborhood of
x
in
\varphi\mathcal{T}
determines the tile(s) to which
x
belongs in
\mathcal{T}
. Let
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest integer
k
such that the
R
-neighborhood of any tile
t\in\varphi\mathcal{T}
is contained in
(\varphi\mathcal{T})^{(k)}(t)
. We will first show that any
\ell\geq k^{\prime}
allows us to define a map
\gamma_{\ell}:AP_{\ell}(X)\rightarrow AP_{\ell}(X)
.

Let
X_{\varphi}
be the tiling space of
\varphi\mathcal{T}
. Since the pseudo self-similarity
\varphi
maps
n
-cells of
\mathcal{T}
to
n
-cells of
\varphi\mathcal{T}
and respects collaring, it determines a bijection
r_{\ell}:AP_{\ell}(X)\rightarrow AP_{\ell}(X_{\varphi})
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. We now define a map
s_{\ell}:AP_{\ell}(X_{\varphi})\rightarrow AP_{\ell}(X)
for any
\ell\geq k^{\prime}
as follows. Let
[x]_{\varphi}\in AP_{\ell}(X_{\varphi})
and let
x\in t\in\varphi\mathcal{T}\in X_{\varphi}
be a representative on the tiling. Then the
R
neighborhood of
x
in
\varphi\mathcal{T}
determines a point in
\mathcal{T}
whereon
x
lies, and thus a point
[x]\in AP_{\ell}(X)
. Since
[x]
was completely determined by the
R
-neighborhood of
x
and this neighborhood is completely contained in
(\varphi\mathcal{T})^{(\ell)}
, then the map
s_{\ell}([x]_{\varphi})=[x]\in AP_{\ell}(X)
is well-defined (that is, independent of representatives of classes). Let
\gamma_{\ell}:=s_{\ell}\circ r_{\ell}:AP_{\ell}(X)\rightarrow AP_{\ell}(X)
.

Now the inverse limit of
(AP_{k^{\prime}}(X),\gamma_{k^{\prime}})
is well defined, but it may or may not be homeomorphic to
X
. By the argument of Anderson and Putnam, collaring once more guarantees that the map forces the border. Therefore, picking
\kappa=k^{\prime}+1
ensures that and we obtain (
3.2
).
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X
be the tiling space of a polytopal pseudo-self-similar tiling
\mathcal{T}
and
\gamma:AP_{\kappa}(X)\rightarrow AP_{\kappa}(X)
the map from Proposition
3.3
. Then

(3.3)

\check{H}^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong\lim_{\longrightarrow}\left(\check{H}^{*}(AP_{\kappa}(X);\mathbb{Z}),\gamma^{*}\right).

Finally, we note that for polytopal tilings of finite local complexity in which cells meet face to face, we have that the
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant and Čech cohomologies are isomorphic

[
23
]
, that is,

(3.4)

H^{*}(X;\mathbb{R})\cong\check{H}^{*}(X;\mathbb{R}).

3.3.
Deformations

In this section we go over deformations of tiling spaces, following
[
13
,
21
]
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a repetitive, aperiodic polytopal tiling of finite local complexity
and let
X
be the corresponding tiling space.
Observe that for L-PSS tiling spaces
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R})
is finite dimensional by (
3.3
).
Each class
[\alpha]\in H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R})
is represented by a
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant smooth 1-form
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
(up to a
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant exact form). Now consider the space
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{d})=H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Each class
[{\mathfrak{f}}]\in H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is represented by a
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant smooth 1-form
{\mathfrak{f}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}\cong M_{d\times d}
(up to a
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant exact form). That is, the representative
{\mathfrak{f}}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant choice of linear transformation of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

A representative
{\mathfrak{f}}
of a class in
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called
shape deformation
as it defines a deformation of
\mathcal{T}
as follows. (We essentially quote the beginning of
[
21
, Section 8]
here.)
Suppose that
{\mathfrak{f}}
is
{\mathcal{T}}
-equivariant with some radius
R
. Let
H_{\mathfrak{f}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be defined as
H_{\mathfrak{f}}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}{\mathfrak{f}}
. We will deform the
vertices
of tiles,
assuming that one of the vertices is at the origin
. Each of these vertices is decorated with the equivalence class of the pattern of radius
R_{0}>R

around it, where
R_{0}
is greater than
R
plus the greatest distance between the adjacent vertices (i.e., connected by an edge).
If
x
is a vertex, we let
H_{\mathfrak{f}}(x)
be the
deformed vertex (with the same label).
If
v_{1}v_{2}
was an edge, then the displacement vector
v_{2}-v_{1}
has been changed to
\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}{\mathfrak{f}}
. The label of
v_{1}
determines the pattern of
{\mathcal{T}}
out to distance
R
along the
entire edge and hence determines
\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}{\mathfrak{f}}
. Thus the local patterns of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathfrak{f}}
are determined from local patterns of
{\mathcal{T}}
, and
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathfrak{f}}
has FLC.

Since we deform the tiles by deforming their edges, for
d>2
we need to make sure that higher-dimensional faces are well-defined. For instance, for 2-dimensional faces we would need the side edges to remain co-planar. In order to avoid imposing such a condition, we can simply triangulate all the tiles to begin with. It is easy to see that there exists a triangulation which is equivariant and preserves the face-to-face property. When all the faces are simplices, they stay being simplices after perturbing the edges. The new simplex sub-tiles should carry a label which contains both the label of the tile it came from, and its location in the triangulation; then the triangulated tiling is MLD to the original one, and we can work with it from the start
without loss of generality.

What is not clear at this point is whether the deformation is in fact a homeomorphism or not. As proved by
[
21
]
, what determines the answer to this question is invertibility of the Ruelle-Sullivan cycle applied to
[{\mathfrak{f}}]
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a polytopal, repetitive, aperiodic tiling of finite local complexity, with uniform patch frequency.
The
Ruelle-Sullivan map
is the map
C:H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow M_{d\times d}
defined, for a class
[{\mathfrak{f}}]\in H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as

C([{\mathfrak{f}}])=\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(B_{R})}\int_{B_{R}}{\mathfrak{f}}(t)\,dt,

which is independent of the representative
{\mathfrak{f}}
.

We can now recall one of the main results of
[
21
]
: if
C([{\mathfrak{f}}])
is invertible, then one can choose a representative
{\mathfrak{f}}
so that
H_{\mathfrak{f}}
is a homeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then the shape deformation
{\mathfrak{f}}
induces an orbit equivalence between the tiling spaces
X
and
X^{\mathfrak{f}}
. In particular, the spaces
X
and
X^{\mathfrak{f}}
are homeomorphic. As such, the set

\mathcal{M}(X):=\left\{[{\mathfrak{f}}]\in H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{d}):\det C([{\mathfrak{f}}])\neq 0\right\}

parametrizes deformations of the tiling space
X
, up to deformations given by coboundaries. Deformations given by representatives of classes in
\mathcal{M}(X)
are all orbit equivalences.
Moreover,
{\mathfrak{f}}
can be chosen so that the homeomorphism
H_{\mathfrak{f}}
will be arbitrarily close to the identity, see the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 8.1]
.
This, however, will come at the cost of collaring to a possibly very large radius.

It is important for us that the deformation preserves the combinatorial structure of the tiling, in the sense neighbor graph and faces of all dimensions. This can be achieved either by collaring, or by
working only with super-tiles of sufficiently large size and ignoring the “small-scale” combinatorics.

Definition 3.6
.

A shape deformation of an FLC polytopal tiling space
X
will be called admissible if it preserves the local combinatorial structure of the tilings and it defines a homeomorphism via the map
H_{{\mathfrak{f}}}
introduced above. In practical terms this means that we will consider
deformations that are small compared to the size of the prototiles.

Now consider the tiling space
X_{\omega}
of an
L-PSS tiling, see Definition
2.6
. Picking
[{\mathfrak{f}}]\in\mathcal{M}(X_{\omega})
and applying an admissible
{\mathfrak{f}}
to
{\mathscr{S}}\in X_{\omega}
having a vertex at the origin,
we obtain a tiling
{\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}
and the
{\mathfrak{f}}
-deformed substitution tiling space
X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}
(the translation orbit closure), whose prototiles will be denoted by

T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}}
. To be precise, we need to specify the
location
of
T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. By construction, the L-PSS tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
has distinguished prototiles
T_{k}\in{\mathcal{T}}
and is also
a fixed point of the substitution
\omega({\mathcal{T}})={\mathcal{T}}
. Choose any vertex
v
in
{\mathcal{T}}
and consider the shifted tiling
{\mathcal{T}}-v
, with a vertex at the origin. Then the deformation

({\mathcal{T}}-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}
is well-defined.
We then let the
deformed prototiles
to be

(3.5)

T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}}:=(T_{j}-v)^{\mathfrak{f}},\ \ j\leq m.

Further,
\omega^{n}(T_{j})-v\subset{\mathcal{T}}-v
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, so we can define
deformed higher order super-prototiles
by

(3.6)

T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}=(\omega^{n}(T_{j})-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}.

(Strictly speaking, these are patches rather than individual tiles. The exact location is not that important, but we want their supports to be subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, e.g., in order to be able to perform
integration over them.) The assumption that
{\mathfrak{f}}
is admissible implies that the tilings in the deformed tiling space
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
will have a hierarchical structure combinatorially
equivalent to those of

X_{\omega}
.

3.4.
Recurrences, return vectors and recurrence vectors

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a repetitive FLC polytopal tiling and
X
the corresponding tiling space.
Following
[
13
]
, we say that a pair of points
(z_{1},z_{2})
in a tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
is a
recurrence
of size
r
, if

(3.7)

{\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{\mathcal{T}}(B_{r}(z_{2}))={\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{\mathcal{T}}(B_{r}(z_{1}))+(z_{2}-z_{1}),

and
r
is maximal possible. The vector
z_{2}-z_{1}
is called a
return vector
of size
r
.
We will always assume that
r\geq 2D_{\max}
, where
D_{\max}
is the diameter of the largest prototile, then
(
3.7
) implies that
{\mathcal{O}}^{+}_{\mathcal{T}}(\{z_{2}\})={\mathcal{O}}^{+}_{\mathcal{T}}(\{z_{1}\})+(z_{2}-z_{1})
.
We can assume, without loss of generality, that
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are vertices of the tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
. Each path along edges from
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
projects to a
closed loop in
AP(X)
, and hence to a closed chain in
C_{1}(AP(X))
. Different paths from
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
project to homologous chains. The class in
H_{1}(AP(X),{\mathbb{Z}})
of a recurrence is called
a
recurrence class
. Recurrences of size greater than
(k+1)D_{\max}
project to closed chains in
AP_{k}(X)
and define classes
in
H_{1}(AP_{k}(X),{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a polytopal tiling. A pair of points
(z_{1},z_{2})
in
{\mathcal{T}}
will be called an elementary recurrence if there exists a tile
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
such that

z_{1}\in{\rm supp}(T)
and
T+(z_{2}-z_{1})\in{\mathcal{T}}
.
Similarly to the above, we can associate to
(z_{1},z_{2})
a closed loop in
{AP}_{0}(X)
which defines a class in
H_{1}({AP}_{0}(X),{\mathbb{Z}})
, called an elementary recurrence class.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
[{\mathfrak{f}}]\in\mathcal{M}(X)\subset H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. We will call it elementary if it is a pull-back of a class in
H^{1}({AP}_{0}(X),{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
under the “forgetful” map. In particular, it is a
class of a shape deformation which acts on elementary recurrences. A representative
{\mathfrak{f}}
of an elementary class
[{\mathfrak{f}}]
will also be called elementary.

In the rest of the paper we will restrict ourselves to elementary shape deformations. This is not an essential restriction, since we can always “collar” the tiles of
{\mathcal{T}}
to the level
\kappa
,
given in Proposition
3.3
, and then an elementary deformation defines a class in
H^{1}(AP_{\kappa}(X);{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, hence in
H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, by Proposition
3.3
.

We continue with the construction, following
[
13
]
. Let
X
be an L-PSS tiling space.
The set of integer linear combinations of elementary recurrence classes is a subgroup
\Gamma<H_{1}({AP}_{0}(X),{\mathbb{Z}})
,
which is a finitely generated free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module. Let
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
be a basis (set of free generators) for
\Gamma
. For each elementary recurrence
(z_{1},z_{2})
, which defines a class

[(z_{1},z_{2})]
, the corresponding

recurrence vector
in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
is the decomposition of the class in this basis; it will be denoted
\alpha(z_{1},z_{2})
. The transformation
\alpha:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
is sometimes called the

address map
.
For a shape parameter
{\mathfrak{f}}
, define

(3.8)

{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}=({\mathfrak{f}}(a_{1}),\ldots,{\mathfrak{f}}(a_{s})),

which can be thought of as a
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued row vector that gives the displacements of the deformed vectors in the basis. Then the deformation of the displacement vector
z_{2}-z_{1}
, corresponding to the recurrence vector
{\bf v}=\alpha(z_{1},z_{2})

is equal to
{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}{\bf v}
.

Given an elementary recurrence
(z_{1},z_{2})
in
{\mathcal{T}}
, we have that
(\varphi(z_{1}),\varphi(z_{2}))
is an elementary recurrence as well, because
{\mathcal{T}}
is a PSS tiling with expansion

\varphi
, see (
2.14
). (In fact, applying the substitution increases the size of the recurrence, but is still an elementary too.)
It follows that the expansion map
\varphi
induces an endomorphism of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module generated by elementary recurrence
vectors. Thus there exists an integer
s\times s
matrix
M
satisfying

(3.9)

\alpha(\varphi(z_{1}),\varphi(z_{2}))=M\alpha(z_{1},z_{2}),\ \ z_{1},z_{2}\in\Lambda({\mathcal{T}}).

Let
{\mathfrak{f}}
be an elementary admissible deformation.
Then, combining (
2.14
) and (
3.6
) (for
n=1
) yields

(3.10)

T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},1}=\bigcup_{j\leq m}\bigl(T_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}}+{\mathfrak{f}}({\mathcal{D}}_{jk})\bigr),

where the right-hand side is a patch of a tiling in
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
.
Here we view the elements of
{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}
as elementary recurrences, since they represent the prototile
T_{k}\in{\mathcal{T}}
and its translated copy in
\omega({\mathcal{T}})\subset{\mathcal{T}}
.
Using the address map, as above, we get an associated set of recurrence vectors, denoted
\alpha({\mathcal{D}}_{jk})
. By definition,

{\mathfrak{f}}({\mathcal{D}}_{jk})={\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}\alpha({\mathcal{D}}_{jk}).

We can iterate the procedure and obtain the decomposition of higher order deformed super-tiles as well.
For instance,

(3.11)

\displaystyle T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{s\leq m,k\leq m}\bigl(T_{s}^{\mathfrak{f}}+{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi{\mathcal{D}}_{jk})+{\mathfrak{f}}({\mathcal{D}}_{ks})\bigr),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{s\leq m,k\leq m}\bigl(T_{s}^{\mathfrak{f}}+{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}M\alpha({\mathcal{D}}_{jk})+{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}\alpha({\mathcal{D}}_{ks})\bigr),\ \ 1\leq j\leq m.

3.5.
Geometric properties of deformed tilings and consequences

The following geometric lemma will be useful. We will write
\asymp
to indicate that the equality holds up to a uniformly bounded from
0
and
\infty
multiplicative constant.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\omega
be an L-PSS tile substitution with expansion
\varphi
and
X_{\omega}
the corresponding tiling space.
Fix an elementary admissible shape deformation
{\mathfrak{f}}
, and consider the corresponding deformed tiling space
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
. For the
{\mathfrak{f}}
-deformed supertiles

T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n},\ j\leq m,

let
R_{n}^{{\mathfrak{f}}}
be the radius of the smallest ball containing (a translate of) every
T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}
, and let
r_{n}^{{\mathfrak{f}}}
be the radius of largest ball contained in a (translate of) any
T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}
.
Then there exists
C_{\mathfrak{f}}>1
depending only on
{\mathfrak{f}}
such that

(3.12)

r_{n}^{\mathfrak{f}}\geq C_{\mathfrak{f}}^{-1}\theta^{n}

and

(3.13)

R^{\mathfrak{f}}_{n}\leq C_{\mathfrak{f}}\theta^{n},

where
\theta=\|\varphi\|
.

Proof.

We use the fact that, combinatorially, the hierarchical structure of the tilings in
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
is the same as in
X_{\omega}
.
The L-PSS tile substitution was obtained from a self-similar, “geometric” tile substitution, so that
{\mathcal{T}}
is MLD with a self-similar tiling
{\mathscr{T}}
having the expansion
\varphi
.
For the tiles and super-tiles of
{\mathscr{T}}
the properties (
3.12
)
and (
3.13
) are immediate by self-similarity. It follows that the same properties hold the for the PSS tiling, since it was built using a derived Voronoi tesselation construction, using

\Lambda=\Lambda({\mathscr{T}})
.

Let us call a sequence of tiles
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}
(of arbitrary type) a
chain
if
S_{j}\cap S_{j+1}\neq\emptyset
for
j=1,\ldots,k-1
(recall that our tiles are compact sets which share faces).

Claim 1.

Let
T_{j}
be a prototile of the L-PSS tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
, with
c(T_{j})
(control point) in its interior,
and consider the
{\mathcal{T}}
-patch
\omega^{n}(T_{j})
, as well as
A_{j}^{n}:={\rm supp}(\omega^{n}(T_{j}))
.
Let
S
be any
{\mathcal{T}}
-tile in the patch
\omega^{n}(T_{j})
containing the point
\varphi^{n}(c(T_{j}))
. Then any chain of
{\mathcal{T}}
-tiles in

\omega^{n}(T_{j})
connecting
S
to
\partial A_{j}^{n}
contains at least
c_{1}\theta^{n}
tiles, where
c_{1}>0
depends only on
{\mathcal{T}}
.

The claim is immediate since
A^{n}_{j}
, being a bounded displacement of
\varphi^{n}A_{j}
, with
A_{j}={\rm supp}(T_{j})
, contains a ball of radius
\asymp\theta^{n}
centered at
\varphi^{n}(c(T_{j}))
, by the (
3.12
) property for
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Now consider the
{\mathfrak{f}}
-deformed super-tile
T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}=(\omega^{n}(T_{j})-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}
, see (
3.6
), with the deformed tile
(S-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}
inside. Denote
A_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}:={\rm supp}(T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n})
.

Claim 2.

Let
d((S-v)^{\mathfrak{f}},\partial A_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n})
be the usual Euclidean distance between the compact boundary of the deformed super-tile and the compact
(S-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}
in its interior.
Then there exists a chain of
{\mathfrak{f}}
-deformed tiles in the patch
T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}
connecting
(S-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}
to
\partial A_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}
of cardinality
N_{\mathfrak{f}}(n)
, such that

(3.14)

N_{\mathfrak{f}}(n)\cdot V_{\min}^{\mathfrak{f}}\leq c_{d-1}\cdot\bigl[d((S-v)^{\mathfrak{f}},\partial A_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n})+2{\sf d}^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\max}\bigr]\cdot({\sf d}^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\max})^{d-1},

where
V^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\min}
is the minimal volume of an
{\mathfrak{f}}
-deformed tile,
{\sf d}^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\max}
is the maximal diameter of an
{\mathfrak{f}}
-deformed tile, and
c_{d-1}
is the volume of a unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
.

Combining Claim 2 with Claim 1 yields (
3.12
). Indeed, for every
{\mathfrak{f}}
-chain in
T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}
there is a combinatorially equivalent chain in the pseudo-self-similar

\omega^{n}(T_{j})
, and this yields a lower bound
\asymp\theta^{n}
for
d((S-v)^{\mathfrak{f}},\partial A_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n})
for large enough
n
. (For small
n
both (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) are trivial.)

For the proof of Claim 2, consider the shortest straight line segment
J
from
(S-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}
to
\partial A_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}
and consider the union of deformed tiles in
T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}
intersecting
J
.
One can certainly form a chain of
{\mathfrak{f}}
-deformed tiles connecting
(S-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}
to
\partial A_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}
out of them, and their total volume is at least the expression in the left-hand side of
(
3.14
). On the other hand, these tiles are all contained in the
{\sf d}^{\mathfrak{f}}
-neighborhood of
J
, whose volume is less that the right-hand side of (
3.14
).

The proof of (
3.13
) is similar. Indeed, the maximal distance from a point in
S
to a point on the boundary
\partial A^{n}_{j}
is at most
C_{2}\theta^{n}
by
self-similarity, hence for any point on
\partial A^{n}_{j}
there exists a chain of
{\mathcal{T}}
-tiles connecting that point to
S
, of cardinality at most
\widetilde{C}_{2}\theta^{n}
. For any such chain there is a corresponding deformed chain of the same cardinality, showing that the diameter of
A_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}
is bounded above by
{\rm const}\cdot\theta^{n}
, with the constant depending only on the deformation. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

For each prototile
T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}}
of the deformed tiling space
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
we also fix a control point
c(T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}})
in the interior and then in all tiles at the same relative location. For a tiling
{\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}\in X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
let

\Lambda({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}):=\{c(T^{\mathfrak{f}}):\ T^{\mathfrak{f}}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}\}.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}\in X_{\omega}
be an L-PSS tiling,
{\mathfrak{f}}
and admissible elementary deformation, and
{\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}=({\mathcal{T}}-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}\in X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
, where
v
is a vertex of

{\mathcal{T}}
.
Then
\Lambda({\mathcal{T}})
and
\Lambda({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})
are quasi-isometric; in fact, there exists
C_{\omega,{\mathfrak{f}}}
(independent of
{\mathcal{T}}
) such that for any

z_{1},z_{2}\in\Lambda({\mathcal{T}})
and the corresponding
z_{1}^{\mathfrak{f}},z_{2}^{\mathfrak{f}}\in\Lambda({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})
, holds

(3.15)

C_{\omega,{\mathfrak{f}}}^{-1}|z_{1}-z_{2}|\leq|z_{1}^{\mathfrak{f}}-z_{2}^{\mathfrak{f}}|\leq C_{\omega,{\mathfrak{f}}}|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

Proof.

Consider the graph
{\rm Graph}({\mathcal{T}})
associated with a tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
, in which the vertices are the tiles and graph edges connect neighboring tiles (tiles whose compact supports intersect). By construction, this graph is stable under the deformations that we consider. It follows from the proof of
Lemma
3.9
that for the deformed tiling
{\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}
, the set
\Lambda({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})
is quasi-isometric to the set of vertices of the graph
{\rm Graph}({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})\cong{\rm Graph}({\mathcal{T}})
, endowed with the graph metric. This implies the desired claim.
∎

Definition 3.11
.

A tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
is called linearly repetitive if there exists
C>0
such that for every patch
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}
a translationally equivalent
{\mathcal{T}}
-patch may be found in every ball of radius

C{\rm diam}({\mathcal{P}})
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
X_{\omega}
be an FLC primitive aperiodic L-PSS tiling space.
Fix an admissible elementary shape deformation
{\mathfrak{f}}
and consider the corresponding deformed tiling space

X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
.
Then

(i)
all the tilings in
X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}
are linearly repetitive;

(ii)
the deformed tiling dynamical system
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is uniquely ergodic.

Proof.

Linear repetitivity of primitive self-similar tiling spaces is well-known, see, e.g.
[
35
]
, and the last lemma implies that this is the case for
X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}
. Linear repetitivity
implies the existence of uniform patch frequencies
[
25
]
and hence unique ergodicity.
∎

The unique invariant probability measure for
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
will be denoted by
\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}
.

Remark 3.13
.

The deformed tiling space
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
falls into the very general framework of fusion, developed by Sadun and Frank
[
17
]
. In fact, our situation satisfies the conditions of
transition-regular, primitive, and recognizable fusion rules, for which unique ergodicity was proved in
[
17
, Corollary 3.10]
. Moreover, we have a constant number of prototiles (equal to
m
) at each level, and a constant transition matrix, hence the tiling dynamical system
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, with the unique invariant probability measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}
, is not strongly
mixing by
[
17
, 4.13]
.

We will need a formula for the unique invariant measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}
on
X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}
. Recall the notion of
transversal
, defined by

\Upsilon({\mathcal{P}}^{\mathfrak{f}})=\{{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathfrak{f}}\in X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}:\ {\mathcal{P}}^{\mathfrak{f}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathfrak{f}}\}

for a deformed tiling space
X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}
and a deformed patch
{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathfrak{f}}
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
X_{\omega}
be an FLC primitive aperiodic L-PSS tiling space, with expansion
\varphi=\theta{\mathcal{O}}
. Fix an elementary admissible shape deformation
{\mathfrak{f}}
and consider the corresponding deformed tiling space
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
.
Then there exists
c_{\omega,{\mathfrak{f}}}>0
, such that for any Borel set
U
and all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(3.16)

{\rm diam}(U)\leq c_{\omega,{\mathfrak{f}}}\theta^{n}\implies\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}\bigl(\Upsilon(T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n})+U\bigr)\geq{\rm const}\cdot{\mathcal{L}}^{d}(U)\cdot\theta^{-nd},

with the constants depending only on
\omega
and on
{\mathfrak{f}}
.

Proof.

It is proved in
[
35
, Lemma 2.4]
that for an aperiodic primitive self-similar tiling
{\mathscr{T}}
, there exists
0<c<1
such that if
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathscr{T}}
is a patch containing a ball of radius
R>0
in its support, then
{\mathcal{P}}+x\not\subset{\mathscr{T}}
for all
x
with
\|x\|\leq cR
. Since the Delone set of the deformed tiling
{\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}=({\mathcal{T}}-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}

is quasi-isometric to
\Lambda({\mathcal{T}})=\Lambda({\mathscr{T}})
by Lemma
3.10
, this
property persists, with an appropriate constant, for
{\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}
. By (
3.12
), the patch
T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}
contains a ball of radius
\asymp\theta^{n}
in its support.
We are going to use Proposition
2.2
. Represent the set
U
as a union of disjoint Borel sets
U_{k}
, each of diameter less than
\eta({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})=2r_{\mathfrak{f}}
, the maximal diameter of a ball contained in the support of every deformed tile.
It follows that
if
{\rm diam}(U)\leq c_{\omega,{\mathfrak{f}}}\theta^{n}
, with a sufficiently small constant, independent of
n
, then the sets
\Upsilon(T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n})+U_{k}
are mutually disjoint, and hence
by (
2.3
),

(3.17)

\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}\Bigl(\Upsilon(T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n})+U\Bigr)=\sum_{k}\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}\Bigl(\Upsilon(T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n})+U_{k}\Bigr)=\sum_{k}{\mathcal{L}}^{d}(U_{k})\cdot{\rm freq}(T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n})={\mathcal{L}}^{d}(U)\cdot{\rm freq}(T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}).

By primitivity of the substitution and the Perron-Frobenius Theorem, the frequency of
\omega^{n}(T_{j})
in the self-similar tiling space is at least
{\rm const}\cdot\theta^{-nd}
(in fact, an upper bound
holds as well, using
[
35
, Lemma 2.4]
again, but we do not need it), just by counting the number of
n
-level super-tiles inside
(n+k)
-level super-tiles. By quasi-isometry, the same
asymptotic bound holds for the deformed tiling space, and (
3.16
) follows.
∎

4.
The spectral cocycle; statement of result on local dimension

Let
X_{\omega}
be an FLC primitive aperiodic L-PSS tiling space. The
Fourier matrix
is an
m\times m
complex matrix-function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
whose
(j,k)
-entry is

(4.1)

[{\mathscr{B}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})]_{(j,k)}:=\Bigl[\sum_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}}\exp\bigl(-2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},{\mathbf{x}}\rangle\bigr)\Bigr],\ \ \boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ \ 1\leq j,k\leq m,

see
[
5
, (29)]
.
First, we need to define a “deformed” Fourier matrix. Recall that
the set of integer linear combinations of elementary recurrence classes is a subgroup
\Gamma<H_{1}(AP_{0}(X_{\omega}),{\mathbb{Z}})
, for which we fixed a set of free generators

\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
. The decomposition with respect to this basis is given by
\alpha:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
, the address map.
Recall (
3.9
) saying that the expansion induces a linear mapping on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
given by a matrix
M
, so that

\alpha\varphi=M\alpha.

For a shape parameter
{\mathfrak{f}}
(a representative of a class in
H^{1}(AP_{0}(X_{\omega});{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
), we considered the row vector

{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}=({\mathfrak{f}}(a_{1}),\ldots,{\mathfrak{f}}(a_{s})),

see (
3.8
).
We also discussed that elements of
{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}
can be identified with elementary recurrences, so
\alpha({\mathbf{x}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
is well-defined for
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}
.
Now we define the deformed Fourier matrix by

(4.2)

\Bigl[\sum_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}}\exp\bigl(-2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}\alpha({\mathbf{x}})\rangle\bigr)\Bigr]_{j,k\leq m}=\Bigl[\sum_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}}\exp\bigl(-2\pi i\langle{\bf L}^{\sf T}_{\mathfrak{f}}\boldsymbol{\lambda},\alpha({\mathbf{x}})\rangle_{{}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s}}}\bigr)\Bigr]_{j,k\leq m},\ \ \boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

The
spectral cocycle
will be over
the toral endomorphism

{\mathbf{z}}\mapsto M^{\sf T}{\mathbf{z}}\ \mbox{mod}\ {\mathbb{Z}}^{s},

where the superscript
{\sf T}
indicates the transpose.
It will be an endomorphism of the
s
-torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{s}
if
\det(M)\neq 0
; otherwise, we can restrict it to a lower-dimensional invariant sub-torus.
Let

(4.3)

{\mathscr{M}}({\mathbf{z}}):=\Bigl[\sum_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}}\exp\bigl(-2\pi i\langle{\mathbf{z}},\alpha({\mathbf{x}})\rangle_{{}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s}}}\bigr)\Bigr]_{j,k\leq m},\ \ {\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s};

this is closely related to the deformed Fourier matrix, which was defined as
{\mathscr{M}}({\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}^{\sf T}\boldsymbol{\lambda})
, for
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, in (
4.2
).
Because of periodicity,
{\mathscr{M}}
is well-defined on the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{s}={\mathbb{R}}^{s}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The matrix product

{\mathscr{M}}({\mathbf{z}},n):={\mathscr{M}}\bigl({(M^{\sf T})}^{n-1}{\mathbf{z}}\bigr)\cdot\ldots\cdot{\mathscr{M}}({\mathbf{z}}),\ \ {\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{s},\ n\in{\mathbb{N}},

will be called the spectral cocycle associated with the L-PSS tiling space
X_{\omega}
.

We will see below that for

{\mathbf{z}}={\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}^{\sf T}\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, the growth behavior of
{\mathscr{M}}({\mathbf{z}},n)
in some sense “controls” the local behavior of spectral measures at
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.

As a consistency check, consider the case of unperturbed self-similar tiling, when
{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}]
. Then
{\mathbf{z}}=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}]^{\sf T}\boldsymbol{\lambda}
and

\langle{\mathbf{z}},\alpha({\mathbf{x}})\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},[a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}]\alpha({\mathbf{x}})\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},{\mathbf{x}}\rangle,

by the definition of the address map
\alpha
. So we obtain
{\mathscr{M}}({\mathbf{z}})={\mathscr{B}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
(the Fourier matrix) from (
4.3
), (
4.1
). Further,

(4.4)

M^{\sf T}{\mathbf{z}}=M^{\sf T}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}]^{\sf T}\boldsymbol{\lambda}=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}]^{\sf T}\varphi^{\sf T}\boldsymbol{\lambda},

by the definition of
M
,
hence

{\mathscr{M}}({\mathbf{z}},n)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}{\mathscr{B}}\bigl((\varphi^{\sf T})^{j}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\bigr),

which agrees with the Fourier matrix cocycle of Baake et al., see
[
5
,
6
]
.

Define the
pointwise upper Lyapunov exponent
of the cocycle
{\mathscr{M}}({\mathbf{z}},n)
at the point
{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
by

(4.5)

\chi^{+}({\mathbf{z}}):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|{\mathscr{M}}({\mathbf{z}},n)\|;

we omit the superscript if the limit exists. In the “unperturbed,” self-similar case, it becomes

\chi^{+}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\Bigl\|\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}{\mathscr{B}}\bigl((\varphi^{\sf T})^{j}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\bigr)\Bigr\|.

We will also need a more refined version of the Lyapunov exponent: for
\vec{\zeta}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, let

(4.6)

\chi^{+}({\mathbf{z}},\vec{\zeta}):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|{\mathscr{M}}({\mathbf{z}},n)\vec{\zeta}\|;

Obviously,
\chi^{+}({\mathbf{z}},\vec{\zeta})\leq\chi^{+}({\mathbf{z}})
for all
\vec{\zeta}
.

Observe that
{\mathscr{M}}({\bf 0})={\sf S}_{\omega}^{\sf T}
, the transpose substitution matrix of
\omega
, see (
2.9
).
Its PF eigenvalue is
\vartheta_{1}=|\det\varphi|=\theta^{d}
, where
\theta
is the expansion constant, by self-similarity.
Thus
\chi({\bf 0})=d\log\theta
. Since every entry of
{\mathscr{M}}({\mathbf{z}})
in absolute value
is less than or equal to the corresponding entry of
{\mathscr{M}}({\bf 0})
, a non-negative primitive matrix, we obtain that

\chi^{+}({\mathbf{z}})\leq d\log\theta\ \ \mbox{for all}\ {\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}.

The
lower local dimension
of a finite positive Borel measure
\nu
at a point
x
is defined by

(4.7)

{d}^{-}(\nu,x)=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\nu(B_{r}(x))}{\log r}.

Equivalently,

(4.8)

{d}^{-}(\nu,x)=\sup\bigl\{\gamma\geq 0:\ \nu(B_{r}(x))\leq Cr^{\gamma},\ \mbox{for all}\ r>0,\ \mbox{for some}\ C>0\bigr\}.

Note that the definition is not changed if we only require the upper bound for the measure of balls of sufficiently small radius, since the measure is finite.
We will say that a measure
\nu
is
Hölder regular
on a set
F
if

(4.9)

\exists\,\alpha>0\ \ \mbox{such that}\ \ {d}^{-}(\nu,x)\geq\alpha\ \ \mbox{for all}\ x\in F.

Let
{\mathfrak{f}}
be an elementary admissible deformation of
X_{\omega}
. Recall that the deformation was well-defined only on a
transversal
of the tiling space, namely, on the tilings
having a vertex at the origin, whereas the deformed tiling space
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}

is simply the translation orbit closure of one representative deformed tiling. For concreteness, we fixed a vertex
v\in{\mathcal{T}}
,
an L-PSS tiling in
X_{\omega}
, and define the deformed prototiles and super-prototiles for
j\leq m
by

T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}}:=(T_{j}-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}\in({\mathcal{T}}-v)^{\mathfrak{f}};\ \ \ \ T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}=(\omega^{n}(T_{j})-v)^{\mathfrak{f}}\subset({\mathcal{T}}-v)^{\mathfrak{f}},\ \ j\leq m.

see (
3.5
), (
3.6
).

For a tiling
{\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}\in X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
and
j\leq m
, let
{\mathscr{L}}_{j}({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})
be the Delone set of translation vectors between the prototiles
T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}}
and the tiles in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}
equivalent to it,
that is,

(4.10)

{\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}=\bigcup_{j\leq m}\bigl(T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}}+{\mathscr{L}}_{j}({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})\bigr).

Now we are ready to state our main result.
Assume for simplicity that the test function
\phi
is a TLC function of level 0 on
X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}
, represented as

(4.11)

\phi({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{L}}_{j}({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})}\delta_{x}*\psi_{j}(0),

where
\psi_{j}
is an integrable function, with

{\rm supp}(\psi_{j})\subset T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}}
. General TLC functions may be represented in a similar way, using higher-level supertiles.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be an FLC primitive aperiodic L-PSS tiling and
X_{\omega}
the corresponding tiling space.
Let
\Gamma
be the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module generated by elementary recurrences for
{\mathcal{T}}

and
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
a set of free generators for
\Gamma
.
Let
{\mathfrak{f}}
be a shape
parameter defining an admissible elementary deformation,

{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}=[{\mathfrak{f}}(a_{1}),\ldots,{\mathfrak{f}}(a_{s})]
, and

X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}
the deformed substitution tiling space. Consider the measure-preserving system
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mu_{\mathfrak{f}})
.
For a TLC function
\phi
on
X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}
of the form (
4.11
), let
\sigma_{\phi}
be the corresponding spectral measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Then

(4.12)

{d}^{-}(\sigma_{\phi},\boldsymbol{\lambda})\geq 2\min\left\{d-\frac{\chi^{+}({\mathbf{z}},\vec{\zeta})}{\log\theta},1\right\},\ \ \boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\},

where
{\mathbf{z}}={\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}^{\sf T}\boldsymbol{\lambda}
and
\vec{\zeta}=[\widehat{\psi}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}),\ldots,\widehat{\psi}_{m}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})]^{\sf T}
.

For
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=0
, suppose that
\phi
is orthogonal to constants, i.e.,
\int_{X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}}\phi\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}=0
. Then

(4.13)

{d}^{-}(\sigma_{\phi},0)\geq 2\min\left\{d-\frac{\log|\vartheta_{2}|}{\log\theta},1\right\},

where
\vartheta_{2}
is the second eigenvalue of
{\sf S}_{\omega}
(the PF eigenvalue being
\vartheta_{1}=\theta^{d}
).

Remark 4.3
.

(a) This is an extension to higher dimensions of the lower bound in
[
10
, Theorem 4.6]
(in the case of a single substitution); it is essentially contained in
[
39
]
, although it is not stated there in terms of the spectral cocycle.

(b) The reason for the “switch” in the estimate at
\chi^{+}({\mathbf{z}})=(d-1)\log\theta
is due to “boundary effects,” as in
[
7
,
33
,
39
]
.

(c) For unperturbed tiling self-similar spaces, there are more precise, two-sided bounds for the local dimension of the spectral measure at zero and even asymptotic expansions; see
[
7
]
(for one-dimensional tilings) and
[
14
]
(for
d>1
).

(d) Juan Marshall Maldonaldo
[
30
, Theorem 3.10]
proved that for a self-similar tiling in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, with an expansion diagonalizable over
{\mathbb{R}}
that is strongly non-Pisot (i.e., has an eigenvalue outside the unit circle), spectral
measures are log-Hölder regular. This means that they satisfy an estimate of the form
\sigma_{f}(B_{r}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))\leq C_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\log(1/r)^{\gamma}
,
r>0
, with
\gamma>0
independent of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\neq 0
. This is an extension of
[
8
, Theorem 5.1]

which obtained the result for
d=1
.

(e) Using the Fourier matrix cocycle (
4.4
),
Baake, Grimm, and Mañibo
[
5
, Theorem 5.7]
proved that for an unperturbed self-similar tiling (even for self-affine and even without the FLC
assumption), if there exists
{\varepsilon}>0
such that
\chi^{+}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\leq\frac{d}{2}\log\theta-{\varepsilon}
for Lebesgue-a.e.
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, then the diffraction spectrum (which is essentially a “part” of the
dynamical spectrum) is purely singular. For
d=1
this follows from
[
10
, Cor. 4.7]
, but for
d\geq 2
we cannot make such a conclusion by our methods, essentially because

(d-1)\log\theta\geq\frac{d}{2}\log\theta
.

(f) It would be interesting to also obtain upper bounds for the local dimension of spectral measures, by analogy with
[
10
]
. We have some partial results in this direction and
hope to return to this question in the future.

5.
Eigenvalues, quantitative Host-Veech criterion, and Hölder regularity of spectral measures

The material in this section is not particularly new, but included for completeness, and we also emphasize the connections.

5.1.
Eigenvalues

Recall that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a
topological eigenvalue
for the tiling dynamical system
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
if there exists a continuous function
\phi:X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

(5.1)

\phi({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbf{z}})=\exp\bigl(-2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},{\mathbf{z}}\rangle\bigr)\cdot\phi({\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ {\mathscr{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}\in X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},\ {\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Theorem 5.1

(variant of
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be an FLC primitive aperiodic L-PSS tiling and
X_{\omega}
the corresponding tiling space.
Let
{\mathfrak{f}}
be a shape
parameter defining an elementary admissible deformation,

X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}
the deformed substitution tiling space, and
{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}
given by (
3.8
).
A vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is in the topological point spectrum of
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
if and only if for every elementary recurrence vector
{\bf v}
of
{\mathcal{T}}
,

(5.2)

\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}M^{n}{\bf v}\rangle\to 0\ \mbox{(mod 1)},\ \ n\to\infty,

where the convergence is exponentially fast and uniform in
{\bf v}
. Here
M
is the matrix from (
3.9
).

In fact, our setting is more general, since
[
13
]
assumed a pure dilation expansion map, whereas we allow an expansion which is a general similitude. However, the proof transfers
almost verbatim. Furthermore,
in
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
only uniform in
{\bf v}
convergence is claimed, but the exponential rate follows from the proof immediately.

Next we show that the same condition characterizes
measurable
eigenvalues for the uniquely ergodic system
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mu)
. Thus, for admissible deformations of
pseudo-self-similar
tiling spaces weak mixing is equivalent to topological weak mixing.
These results follow a long line of earlier work, starting with Host
[
20
]
, who obtained a similar criterion for the eigenvalues of (symbolic) substitution
{\mathbb{Z}}
-actions, in terms of return words, and also proved that every measure-theoretic eigenvalue is topological. For interval exchange transformations and translation flows an analogous
criterion was obtained by Veech
[
40
]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
,
a vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is in the topological point spectrum of
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
if and only if it is in the point spectrum of the uniquely ergodic measure-preserving system

(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mu_{\mathfrak{f}})
.

The proof is a minor variation of the argument in
[
34
]
; we sketch it for completeness in Section
7
.
Note that

(5.3)

\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}M^{n}{\bf v}\rangle=\langle{\bf L}^{\sf T}_{\mathfrak{f}}\boldsymbol{\lambda},M^{n}{\bf v}\rangle_{{}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s}}}=\bigl\langle{(M^{\sf T})}^{n}({\bf L}^{\sf T}_{\mathfrak{f}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}),{\bf v}\bigr\rangle_{{}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s}}},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the inner product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, as opposed to the inner product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
in the right-hand side.

Lemma 5.3
.

There exists a uniform
c_{1}>0
with the following property.
Let
{\bf v}
be a recurrence vector for
{\mathcal{T}}
, such that for all
j\leq m
there exists
k
satisfying

(5.4)

\exists\,z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}\ \ \mbox{such that}\ \ {\bf v}=\alpha(z_{1},z_{2}).

Then

(5.5)

\|{\mathscr{M}}({\bf L}^{\sf T}_{\mathfrak{f}}\boldsymbol{\lambda},n)\|\leq{\rm const}\cdot\theta^{nd}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(1-c_{1}\bigl\|\bigl\langle{(M^{\sf T})}^{i}({\bf L}^{\sf T}_{\mathfrak{f}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}),{\bf v}\bigr\rangle_{{}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s}}}\bigr\|_{{}_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}}^{2}\right),\ \ n\geq 1.

For the proof, see
[
39
]
; it is a generalization of
[
8
]
to higher rank actions. ∎

In view of primitivity and repetitivity, for any given recurrence vector
{\bf v}
, the property (
5.4
) holds if we replace
\omega
by a sufficiently hight power.
Passing from
\omega
to
\omega^{k}
for some

, we can assume without loss of generality that (

) holds for a set of recurrence vectors
{\bf v}
generating
{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
.

The following lemma is elementary, see e.g.,
[
11
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}={\mathbb{Z}}[{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{\ell}]
for some

. Then we have for
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
:

\max_{j\leq\ell}\|\langle{\mathbf{x}},{\bf v}_{j}\rangle\|_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\asymp\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}},

with implied constants depending only on the generating set
{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{\ell}
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, there exists
k=k(\omega)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(i)

\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is in the discrete spectrum of the system
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(in the topological or measurable category) if and only if

\lim_{i\to\infty}{\bigl\|(M^{\sf T})^{ki}({\bf L}^{\sf T}_{\mathfrak{f}}\boldsymbol{\lambda})\bigr\|}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s}/Z^{s}}=0,

and the convergence is exponential.

(ii)

\|{\mathscr{M}}({\bf L}^{\sf T}_{\mathfrak{f}}\boldsymbol{\lambda},n)\|\leq{\rm const}\cdot\theta^{nd}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(1-\widetilde{c}_{1}{\bigl\|(M^{\sf T})^{ki}({\bf L}^{\sf T}_{\mathfrak{f}}\boldsymbol{\lambda})\bigr\|}^{2}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}}\right),\ \ n\geq 1.

Proof.

(i) follows from Theorem
5.1
and Lemma
5.4
.

(ii) is immediate from (

) and Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
.
∎

Thus, weak mixing of the tiling dynamical system is equivalent to

\forall\ \boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\},\ \|(M^{\sf T})^{ki}({\bf L}^{\sf T}_{\mathfrak{f}}\boldsymbol{\lambda})\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s}/{{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}}}\not\to 0,\ \ i\to\infty,\ \ \mbox{for}\ k=k(\omega).

(this is analogous to the Veech criterion
[
40
]
). On the other hand, if
\|(M^{\sf T})^{ki}({\bf L}^{\sf T}_{\mathfrak{f}}\boldsymbol{\lambda})\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{s}/{{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}}}\not\to 0
in some “quantitative way” (say, with a positive frequency the distance is greater than

\delta
) for all
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, then we get quantitative weak mixing.

5.2.
Hölder regularity

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be an FLC primitive aperiodic L-PSS tiling and
X_{\omega}
the corresponding tiling space.
Let
\Gamma
be the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module generated by elementary recurrences for
{\mathcal{T}}

and
M
be the integer matrix of the induced action of the expansion map on
\Gamma
.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be an open set of elementary admissible deformations.
If the dimension of the (strictly) expanding subspace of
M
is at least
d+1
, then for Lebesgue-a.e.
[{\mathfrak{f}}]\in{\mathcal{M}}
, there exist a representative
{\mathfrak{f}}
of
[{\mathfrak{f}}]
such that the deformed
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
action on
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
has
uniformly Hölder-regular spectral measures.
More precisely, there exists
\alpha>0
, depending only on the tiling space and the substitution, such that for a.e.
[{\mathfrak{f}}]\in{\mathcal{M}}
with admissible representative
{\mathfrak{f}}

and every transversally locally constant function
\phi
on
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
, the spectral measure
\sigma_{\phi}
, associated with the uniquely ergodic dynamical system

(X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mu_{\mathfrak{f}})
, satisfies
d^{-}(\sigma_{\phi},\boldsymbol{\lambda})\geq\alpha
for all

\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
.

This is an extension of
[
39
, Cor. 1]
to the case of pseudo self-similar tilings with a general (not necessarily pure dilation) expansion map.
Analogously to
[
39
, Th. 2]
, one can also deduce
uniform
rates of weak mixing for functions with sufficient regularity in the leaf direction, as well as bounds on integrals of correlations.

In many cases Theorem
5.6

may be applied based only on the knowledge of the algebraic properties of the expansion
\varphi
. It is well-known that all the eigenvalues of
\varphi

must be (real or complex) algebraic integers, see e.g.,
[
28
, Cor. 4.2]
.
A family of algebraic integers
\Theta=\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d}\}
, all of absolute value greater than one, is called a
Pisot family
if every Galois conjugate of every
\theta_{j}\in\Theta
is either another element of
\Theta
or lies inside the unit circle. For
d=1
this is the definition of a (real) Pisot number, and for
d=2
, with
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
complex conjugates, this is the definition of a complex Pisot number. Under very general conditions, if the set of eigenvalues of
\varphi
is a Pisot family, then the tiling dynamical
system is not weakly mixing, see
[
29
]
, and sometimes even pure discrete. We will say that
\Theta
is
strongly non-Pisot
if there exists
\theta_{j}\in\Theta
such that
at least one of its Galois conjugates has absolute value strictly greater than one.

Corollary 5.7
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.6
, suppose that
the set of eigenvalues of
\varphi
is strongly non-Pisot. Then for Lebesgue-a.e. admissible
deformation
[{\mathfrak{f}}]\in{\mathcal{M}}
the deformed
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
action on
X^{\mathfrak{f}}
has uniformly Hölder-regular spectral measures.

Derivation of Corollary
5.7
assuming Theorem
5.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module generated by elementary recurrences for
{\mathcal{T}}
. The expansion
\varphi
induces an endomorphism of
\Gamma
given by the integer matrix
M
.
It follows that all the eigenvalues of
\varphi
are algebraic integers, and moreover, every eigenvalue of
\varphi
is also an eigenvalue of
M
, see e.g.,
[
37
, Lemma 1.4.5]
.
But
M
is an integer matrix, hence every
Galois conjugate of its every eigenvalue is also an eigenvalue of
M
. By the strongly non-Pisot assumption, the dimension of the expanding subpace for
M
is at least
d+1
, and the claim follows
from Theorem
5.6
.
∎

To conclude this section, we want to show that the above results can be extended to general PSS tilings, not necessarily the “special” L-PSS ones, and we summarize now how this is done.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a PSS tiling and
X
its tiling space. By Remark
2.8
(c), there is a tiling space
X^{\prime}
and a tiling
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
which is L-PSS and MLD equivalent to
\mathcal{T}
. Denote by
\Phi:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
the homeomorphism of tiling spaces defined by the MLD equivalence. Let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime})\subset H^{1}(X^{\prime};\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the set of non-singular classes of deformations, as defined in §
3.3
, and
\mathcal{M}:=\Phi^{*}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\subset H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{d})
its pullback under the MLD map.

Consider a class
[\mathfrak{f}]^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\subset H^{1}(X^{\prime};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with representative
\mathfrak{f}
, a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
-equivariant, admissible smooth 1-form. First, we claim that
\mathfrak{f}
is also
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant. Indeed, the value of
\mathfrak{f}(x)
depends on
\mathcal{O}^{+}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(B_{R^{\prime}}(x))
for some
R^{\prime}>0
. Moreover, by MLD equivalence, the patch
\mathcal{O}^{+}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(B_{R^{\prime}}(x))
is determined by the patch
\mathcal{O}^{+}_{\mathcal{T}}(B_{R}(x))
for some
R>0
. Thus whenever
\mathcal{O}^{+}_{\mathcal{T}}(B_{R}(x))=\mathcal{O}^{+}_{\mathcal{T}}(B_{R}(y))+x-y
we have that
\mathfrak{f}(x)=\mathfrak{f}(y)
, that is,
\mathfrak{f}
is
\mathcal{T}
-equivariant. Thus
\mathfrak{f}
has a class in
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which is denoted by
[\mathfrak{f}]
, and it is indeed the image of
[\mathfrak{f}]^{\prime}
under the pullback map:
[\mathfrak{f}]=\Phi^{*}[\mathfrak{f}]^{\prime}
.

Since
[\mathfrak{f}]^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, by the results of §
3.3
, the representative
\mathfrak{f}
defines a homeomorphism
H_{\mathfrak{f}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
through
H_{\mathfrak{f}}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\mathfrak{f}
, which satisfies
H_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathcal{T}-t)=\mathcal{T}-H_{\mathfrak{f}}(t)
. Let
\mathcal{T}^{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}:=H_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{T}^{\mathfrak{f}}:=H_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathcal{T})
be the deformed tilings, which are both FLC and repetitive. As such, they have well-defined tiling spaces
X^{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
and
X^{\mathfrak{f}}
, respectively. Consider the map
\Phi_{\mathfrak{f}}:=H_{\mathfrak{f}}\circ\Phi\circ H_{\mathfrak{f}}^{-1}
sending
\mathcal{T}^{\mathfrak{f}}
to
\mathcal{T}^{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
. This map extends by minimality to all of
X^{\mathfrak{f}}
, and gives a map
\Phi_{\mathfrak{f}}:X^{\mathfrak{f}}\rightarrow X^{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
. This map is an MLD equivalence: indeed, since
\mathfrak{f}
is
C^{\infty}
and bounded, the distorsion of the map
H_{\mathfrak{f}}
(and that of its inverse) is bounded over sets with diameter less than some finite fixed diameter. As such, the tile(s) covering
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
\mathcal{T}^{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
is determined by the patch
\mathcal{O}^{-}_{\mathcal{T}^{\mathfrak{f}}}(B_{R}(x))
for some
R
large enough.

Finally, note that a function
\phi:X^{\mathfrak{f}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is TLC if and only if
\phi=\Phi_{\mathfrak{f}}^{*}\phi^{\prime}
for some TLC function
\phi^{\prime}:X^{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. As such, we have that
S_{R}^{y}(\phi,\boldsymbol{\lambda})=S_{R}^{\Phi_{\mathfrak{f}}(y)}(\phi^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda})
for any
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
S_{R}^{y}(\phi,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
is the twisted ergodic integral of
\phi
, introduced in §
7.1
, and used in all proofs of bounds of lower local dimension. As such, bounds on
S_{R}^{y}(\phi,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
are equivalent to bounds on
S_{R}^{\Phi_{\mathfrak{f}}(y)}(\phi^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda})
(see Lemma
7.1
). Thus we have proved the following.

Corollary 5.8
.

Theorem
5.6
holds with the weaker assumption of
\mathcal{T}
being pseudo self-similar, not necessarily L-PSS. Moreover, if the set of eigenvalues of the inflation of a PSS tiling is strongly non-Pisot, then for Lebesgue-a.e. class
[{\mathfrak{f}}]\in{\mathcal{M}}
, there exists a representative
{\mathfrak{f}}
such that the deformed
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
action on
X^{\mathfrak{f}}
has uniformly Hölder-regular spectral measures.

Recall that when we deform tilings and their spaces we do so through admissible

Proof sketch of Theorem

.

Denote

and
P_{-}
be the projections
onto
E^{+},E^{-}
respectively, commuting with
M^{\sf T}
.
From (
5.9
) we obtain, using that
P_{+}
commutes with
M^{\sf T}
:

P_{+}{\mathbf{z}}=(M_{+}^{\sf T})^{-n}P_{+}({\mathbf{K}}_{n}({\mathbf{z}})+{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{n}({\mathbf{z}})).

Let
\kappa=\dim E^{+}
and let
\theta_{\kappa}
be the minimal eigenvalue of
M
greater than 1. Fix any
r\in(1,\theta_{\kappa})
. Then

\|(M_{+}^{\sf T})^{-n}P_{+}\|\leq Cr^{-n},\ \ n\geq 0,

and hence

\|P_{+}{\mathbf{z}}-(M_{+}^{\sf T})^{-n}P_{+}{\mathbf{K}}_{n}({\mathbf{z}})\|\leq Cr^{-n},\ \ n\geq 0,

where
C
depends only on
M
. Thus, the knowledge of
{\mathbf{K}}_{n}({\mathbf{z}})
yields an approximation of order
\sim r^{-n}
for
{\mathbb{P}}\,_{\mathbf{z}}
.
Now, using the combinatorial counting argument from
[
11
, §5]
, we can conclude that the set
\{P_{+}{\mathbf{z}}:{\mathbf{z}}\in E_{N}(\rho,\delta,B)\}
may be covered by

O_{B,M}(1)\cdot\exp[L(\frac{1}{\delta}\log\delta)N]
balls of radius
r^{-N}
, where
L
is a uniform constant. Choosing
\delta>0
sufficiently small
guarantees that this number is at most
O_{B,M}(1)\cdot r^{N{\varepsilon}_{1}}
. This yields a covering of
E_{N}(\rho,\delta,B)
by

O_{B,M}(1)\cdot r^{N(s-\kappa+{\varepsilon}_{1})}
balls of radius
Cr^{-N}
, since
s-\kappa=\dim E^{-}
and there are a priori bounds
\|z\|\leq B
.

Finally, we need to pass from the covering of
E_{N}(\rho,\delta,B)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
to a covering of
{\mathcal{E}}_{N}(\rho,\delta,B)\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{\bf a}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{ds}
.
The latter is the set of matrices
{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}
such that
{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}^{\sf T}\boldsymbol{\lambda}={\mathbf{z}}\in E_{N}(\rho,\delta,B)
. The assumptions on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bf a}

guarantees that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is bounded away from 0 and
\infty
in norm.

For
{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
the set of
{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}
such that
{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}^{\sf T}\boldsymbol{\lambda}={\mathbf{z}}
, with
{\mathbf{z}}
fixed, can be parameterized (at least, locally)
as follows. Choose
d
rows of
{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}
with a determinant bounded away from zero in modulus. Solving the
d\times d
system yields a unique solution
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
. Then
the remaining
s-d
rows in the linear system define a co-dimension 1 hyperplane each as the set of possibilities for the rows of
{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}
, resulting in a set of total dimension

d\times d+(s-d)\times(d-1)=sd-s+d
. Hence the set
{\mathcal{E}}_{N}(\rho,\delta,B)
may be covered by

O_{B,M}(1)\cdot r^{N(sd+d-\kappa+{\varepsilon}_{1})}=O_{B,M}(1)\cdot r^{\gamma}

balls of radius
Cr^{-N}
.
Now (
5.8
) follows by a standard estimate of the Hausdorff dimension of limsup sets.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

The last proof highlights the fact that the main “play” in the proof of Hölder regularity occurs in the strictly expanding subspace
E^{+}
for
M^{\sf T}
. One may ask what is the role of the
contracting subspace. The following result of Clark and Sadun
[
13
]
shows that by perturbing the deformation in the contracting direction we obtain a topologically conjugate system,
hence all the spectral properties remain unchanged.

Proposition 5.13

(corollary of
[
13
, Theorems 2.2 and 3.1]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be an FLC primitive aperiodic L-PSS tiling and
X_{\omega}
the corresponding tiling space.
For two classes
[{\mathfrak{f}}],[{\mathfrak{g}}]\in\mathcal{M}\subset H^{1}(X_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
consider the deformed tiling spaces

X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
and
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{g}}
, where
{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathfrak{g}}
are admissible representatives.
Let
E^{-}
be the contracting subspace for
M^{\sf T}
, i.e., the span of (generalized) eigenvectors corresponding to eigenvalues less than one in modulus. If there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(M^{\sf T})^{k}({\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}})-{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{g}}\in E^{-}
or
(M^{\sf T})^{k}({\bf L}_{\mathfrak{g}})-{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}\in E^{-}
, then
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
(X^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
are topologically conjugate.

Although
[
13
]
assumed a pure dilation expansion map, their proof extends verbatim.

5.4.
One-dimensional substitution tilings revisited

For one-dimensional substitution tilings, there is a space of “natural” deformations, obtained simply by changing the tile sizes.
Similarly to
[
13
, Section 5]
, we can extend
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
. First, we need to introduce some notation. Let
\zeta
be a primitive aperiodic substitution on

d
symbols,
(X_{\zeta},T,\mu)
the (2-sided) uniquely ergodic tiling
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action, and
({\mathfrak{X}}_{\zeta}^{\vec{s}},h_{t},\widetilde{\mu})
the suspension flow corresponding to a “roof vector”
\vec{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}
.
A word
v
is called a return vector for

if

occurs in

where

is the 1st letter of
v
, that is,
v
separates the two consecutive occurrences of

.
Denote by
\vec{\ell}(v)
the “population vector” of a word
v
. Let
\Gamma_{\zeta}<{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be the

-module generated by
\{\vec{\ell}(v):\ v
is a return vector for
\zeta\}
, and
the “essential subspace”
V_{\zeta}:=\Gamma_{\zeta}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
the real linear span of
\Gamma_{\zeta}
. First we recall the earlier result:

Theorem 5.14

(
[
8
, Th. 4.1]
)
.

Let
\zeta
be a primitive aperiodic substitution on

d
symbols with a substitution matrix
{\sf S}_{\zeta}
. Suppose that the characteristic polynomial of
{\sf S}_{\zeta}
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and the second eigenvalue satisfies
|\theta_{2}|>1
. Then for
Lebesgue-a.e.
\vec{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}
the spectral measures of TLC functions for the system
({\mathfrak{X}}_{\zeta}^{\vec{s}},h_{t},\widetilde{\mu})
are Hölder regular away from zero, with a uniform Hölder exponent.

For comparison, now we have the following result, which is essentially a special case of Theorem
5.6
, with

.

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
\zeta
be a primitive aperiodic substitution on

d
symbols with a substitution matrix
{\sf S}_{\zeta}
and the essential subspace
V_{\zeta}
. Suppose that the second eigenvalue of
{\sf S}_{\zeta}|_{V_{\zeta}}
satisfies

. Then for
Lebesgue-a.e.
\vec{s}\in V_{\zeta}
the spectral measures of TLC functions for the system

Remark 5.16
.

(a) It is easy to see that if
{\sf S}_{\zeta}
is irreducible, then

, however, the latter often happens even when

is reducible.

(b) Suppose that

. Then it follows from
[

]
that for

that is orthogonal to

has point spectrum
containing

are such that

(c) As it was pointed out in

.

6.
Examples

6.1.
Kenyon’s (pseudo) self-similar tilings

In
[
24
]
, given integers
p,q\geq 0
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, Kenyon introduced an algebraic construction of pseudo self-similar tilings using parallelograms, from which one can obtain a true self-similar tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The nature of the construction is such that there is an obvious subspace
\mathcal{M}_{p,q,r}
of deformation parameters which are accessible without having to compute the cohomology of the associated tiling spaces. In this section we give sufficient conditions under which a typical deformation of Kenyon’s tilings, in the natural deformation space
\mathcal{M}_{p,q,r}
, we obtain tiling spaces which are quantitatively weak mixing.

Let us review the construction: let
a,b,c
be three vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
pointing in different directions, and let
F
be the set of polygonal paths starting at the origin, each of which is a translate of
\pm a,\pm b,
or
\pm c
without backtracking (i.e.
x
is not followed by
-x
). A product can be defined on
F
by concatenating paths and errasing any backtrack. As such any element in
F
defines an element of the free group
F(a,b,c)
on three generators and a natural isomorphism
h:F(a,b,c)\rightarrow F
.

Pick
p,q
be non-negative integers,
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\phi:F(a,b,c)\rightarrow F(a,b,c)
be the endomorphism defined by

(6.1)

\begin{split}\phi(a)&=b,\\
\phi(b)&=c,\\
\phi(c)&=c^{p}a^{-r}b^{-q}.\end{split}

The three commutators
A=[a,b]
,
B=[b,c]
and
C=[a,c]
define three closed paths which enclose parallelograms which we label
A,B,C
. The action of the endomorphism on the commutators is thus

(6.2)

\begin{split}\phi(A)&=B,\\
\phi(B)&=c^{p}a^{-r}[a^{r},c](b^{-q}[b^{q},c]b^{q})a^{r}c^{-q},\\
\phi(C)&=[b,c^{p}]c^{p}a^{-r}[a^{r},b]a^{r}c^{-p}.\end{split}

Consider the polynomial

(6.3)

f(z)=z^{3}-pz^{2}+qz+r.

We have to impose the assumptions that
f
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and has a complex root
\lambda
of absolute value greater than 1: a complex Perron number, that is, a non-real
algebraic integer strictly greater in absolute value than its Galois conjugates other than
\overline{\lambda}
.
This does not always happen: for example,
f
has a root
-1
, hence reducible, if
r=p+q+1
, and
z^{3}-4z^{2}+z+1
has three real zeros. Later we will also need the condition for all three roots to be outside of the unit circle, that is, for
\lambda
not to be a complex Pisot number.

Lemma 6.1
.

(i)
The polynomial
f
has a complex root if and only if
p^{2}<3q
, or
p^{2}\geq 3q
and

(6.4)

27r>2(p^{2}-3q)(p+\sqrt{p^{2}-3q})-3pq.

(ii)
If
f
does have a complex root
\lambda
, then it is complex Perron, unless
p=q=0
.

(iii)
If the above conditions are satisfied, all the roots are outside of the unit circle if and only if
r>p+q+1
. If
r<p+q+1
, then
\lambda
is a complex Pisot number.

(iv)
The polynomial
f
is reducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
if and only if it has an integer root, which is necessarily a divisor of
r
.

Proof.

(i) Note first that
df/dz=3z^{2}-2pz+q
has zeros
(p\pm\sqrt{p^{2}-3q})/3
,
hence if
p^{2}<3q
, we know that there is only one real root of
f
, hence there is a complex root.
If
p^{2}\geq 3q
, then both (or the unique) extremal points
z_{1}\leq z_{2}
are non-negative, and the condition for having a complex root is that
f(z_{2})>0
(keeping in mind that
f(0)=r>0
).
A computation (left to the reader) shows that this is equivalent to (
6.4
).

(ii) Suppose that the conditions from part (i) hold, and let
\lambda
be the complex root of
f
with a positive imaginary part.
If
p=q=0
, then
\lambda^{3}=-r
, hence it is not a complex Perron number. Suppose that
\max\{p,q\}\geq 1
.
There is one negative zero
-\alpha
and two complex zeros
\lambda
and
\overline{\lambda}
, such that

|\lambda|^{2}\cdot\alpha=r
. Observe that

f(-r^{1/3})=-r-pr^{2/3}-qr^{1/3}+r<0,

hence
\alpha<r^{1/3}\implies|\lambda|>\alpha
,
and so
\lambda
is complex Perron.

(iii) Assuming
\lambda
is complex Perron, all zeros are greater than one in absolute value if and only if
-\alpha<-1
, and this is equivalent to
f(-1)=-1-p-q+r>0
, implying the first claim.
If
f(-1)<0
, then
-\alpha\in(-1,0)
and
\lambda
is complex Pisot.

(iv) The (ir)reducibility claim is immediate, since
f
is a monic polynomial.
∎

For the rest of the section we assume that
p,q,r
are such that
f
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\lambda
is a complex Perron number. In the above construction let

a,b,c
be
1,\lambda,\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{C}
.
We can now express the operation in terms of parallelograms obtained from (
6.2
). With slight abuse of notation,

(6.5)

\begin{split}\omega(A)&=B,\\
\omega(B)&=\left[\bigcup_{j=0}^{q-1}B-r-j\lambda+p\lambda^{2}\right]\cup\left[\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}C-j+p\lambda^{2}\right],\\
\omega(C)&=\left[\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}A-j+p\lambda^{2}\right]\cup\left[\bigcup_{j=0}^{p-1}B-j\lambda^{2}+p\lambda^{2}\right].\end{split}

This is not exactly a substitution rule, but a “substitution with amalgamation”. We will show how this gives both pseudo self-similar tilings and self-similar tilings as a limit of the pseudo self-similar ones.

Using the rules
\omega(K+x)=\omega(K)+\lambda x
and
\omega(K_{1}\cup K_{2})=\omega(K_{1})\cup\omega(K_{2})
for
K\in\{A,B,C\}
, we can iterate the rules given in (
6.5
) to obtain larger and larger patches
K_{n}=\omega^{n}(K)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The patches
K_{n}
grow in area exponentially with
n
while containing the origin, and so in the limit they define tilings
\mathcal{T}_{K}
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
belonging to the same tiling space
X_{p,q,r}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

(i)
The space
X_{p,q,r}
is the tiling space of a pseudo self-similar tiling given by the “substitution with amalgamation” rule (
6.5
). Moreover, it is an L-PSS tiling.

(ii)
This tiling space is FLC, repetitive, and aperiodic.

Proof.

(i) For
K\in\{A,B,C\}
and
\mathscr{K}\in\{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B},\mathscr{C}\}
define the rescaled tiles

\mathscr{K}_{n}=\lambda^{-n}K_{n}=\lambda^{-n}\omega^{n}(K).

These satisfy

\omega(\mathscr{K}_{n})=\omega(\lambda^{-n}\omega^{n}(K_{n}))=\lambda^{-n}\omega^{n+1}(K)=\lambda^{-n}K_{n+1}=\lambda\mathscr{K}_{k+1}.

Taking the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
, there is a convergence of tiles
\mathscr{K}_{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{K}
(see, e.g.,
[
34
, Lemma 7.7]
)
and we obtain an actual self-similar tiling
[
24
]
defined by

(6.6)

\begin{split}\lambda\mathscr{A}&=\mathscr{B},\\
\lambda\mathscr{B}&=\left[\bigcup_{j=0}^{q-1}\mathscr{B}-r-j\lambda+p\lambda^{2}\right]\cup\left[\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}\mathscr{C}-j+p\lambda^{2}\right],\\
\lambda\mathscr{C}&=\left[\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}\mathscr{A}-j+p\lambda^{2}\right]\cup\left[\bigcup_{j=0}^{p-1}\mathscr{B}-j\lambda^{2}+p\lambda^{2}\right].\end{split}

Let
\widetilde{X}_{p,q,r}
the tiling space associated to the primitive substitution (
6.6
). It follows by construction that
\mathcal{T}\in X_{p,q,r}
if and only if there exist countable sets
\Lambda_{A},\Lambda_{B},\Lambda_{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

(6.7)

\mathcal{T}=(A+\Lambda_{A})\cup(B+\Lambda_{B})\cup(C+\Lambda_{C})\hskip 21.68121pt\mbox{ and }\hskip 21.68121pt\mathscr{T}=(\mathscr{A}+\Lambda_{A})\cup(\mathscr{B}+\Lambda_{B})\cup(\mathscr{C}+\Lambda_{C}).

As such,
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathscr{T}
are MLD and therefore so are
\lambda\mathcal{T}
and
\lambda\mathscr{T}
. Since
\mathscr{T}
is locally derivable from
\lambda\mathscr{T}
,
\mathcal{T}
is locally derivable from
\lambda\mathcal{T}
, so
\mathcal{T}
is pseudo self-similar with expanding map
\lambda
.

(ii) The substitution matrix from Kenyon’s construction from the polynomial
x^{3}-px^{2}+qx+r=0
, where
p,q\geq 0
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, is obtained from (
6.6
) and is

(6.8)

{\sf S}_{p,q,r}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&r\\
1&q&p\\
0&r&0\end{array}\right),

which has characteristic polynomial
x^{3}-qx^{2}-prx-r^{2}
, with the Perron-Frobenius eigenvalue equal to
|\lambda|^{2}
. The FLC property is immediate by construction, and repetitivity follows from
the fact that the substitution matrix
{\sf S}_{p,q,r}
is primitive. To show aperiodicity, it is convenient to work with the self-similar tiling space
\widetilde{X}_{p,q,r}
. Note that if
0\neq x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a
period, then
\lambda\cdot x
is a period as well, whence there is a lattice of periods and all tile frequencies must be rational. However, the frequencies are given by the components of the Perron
eigenvector of
{\sf S}_{p,q,r}
, which are irrational, since we assumed irreducibility of
f(z)
. This is a contradiction, and the proof is complete.

Finally, observe that all the conditions of an L-PSS tiling are satisfied, see Definition
2.6
.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Suppose that
f(z)=z^{3}-pz^{2}+qz+r
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and has a complex zero
\lambda
. Then
the dynamics on the tiling spaces
X_{p,q,r}
and
\widetilde{X}_{p,q,r}
are weakly mixing if and only if
r>p+q+1
.

Proof.

By
[
34
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
35
]
, the dynamics on the tiling space
\widetilde{X}_{p,q,r}
are weakly mixing if and only if
\lambda
is not a complex Pisot number.
Lemma
6.1
says that this is equivalent (provided the other assumptions hold) to
r>p+q+1
.
The result for
X_{p,q,r}
then follows by MLD equivalence to
\widetilde{X}_{p,q,r}
.
∎

6.1.1.
Deformations

Although
all
deformations of the tiling space
X_{p,q,r}
are given by an open subset of
H^{1}(X_{p,q,r};\mathbb{R}^{2})
(see Section
3
), here we focus on
elementary deformations, corresponding to perturbations of the vectors
1,\lambda,\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{C}
which define the parallelograms. This saves us the the work of having to compute the entire space of deformations
H^{1}(X_{p,q,r};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, which may or may not have higher dimension than 6.
This means that the role of the subgroup
\Gamma<H_{1}(AP_{0}(X),{\mathbb{Z}})
in Section
3.4
will be played by the subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}}[1,\lambda,\lambda^{2}]<{\mathbb{C}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

From (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) it follows that the tiling spaces
X_{p,q,r}
and
\widetilde{X}_{p,q,r}
have the same group generated by the return vectors
\Gamma_{p,q,r}
, and this group is necessarily a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}[1,\lambda,\lambda^{2}]
. In fact, we have

Lemma 6.4
.

\Gamma_{p,q,r}=\mathbb{Z}[1,\lambda,\lambda^{2}]
.

Proof.

By construction,
\Gamma_{p,q,r}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}[1,\lambda,\lambda^{2}]
, invariant under multiplication by
\lambda
. If
r\geq 2
, we immediately obtain that
1\in\Gamma_{p,q,r}

from (
6.6
), since the substitution of
{\mathscr{B}}
contains two translates of
{\mathscr{C}}
differing by 1, and the claim follows. If
r=1
and
q\geq 2
, then
\lambda\in\Gamma_{p,q,r}
by
the formula for
\lambda{\mathscr{B}}
, and then
\{\lambda,\lambda^{2},\lambda^{3}\}\subset\Gamma_{p,q,r}
. If
r=1
, then the algebraic number
\lambda
is a unit, and we conclude that
1\in\Gamma_{p,q,r}
by
(
6.3
).
A similar argument works for
r=1
and
p\geq 2
, since then
\lambda^{2}\in\Gamma_{p,q,r}
. The remaining cases are when
(p,q,r)\in\{(1,0,1),(0,1,1),(1,1,1)\}
, which are treated
separately. For instance, if
(p,q,r)=(1,0,1)
we obtain by iterating (
6.6
) that
\lambda^{3}{\mathscr{C}}
contains
{\mathscr{C}}-1+\lambda^{3}
and
{\mathscr{C}}-1+\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{3}
, hence
\lambda^{2}\in\Gamma_{p,q,r}
and we conclude as above. The remaining two cases are similar and are left to the reader.
∎

To proceed, we need the sets
{\mathcal{D}}_{ij}
from the definition of self-similar tiling (
2.4
) to be subsets of
\Gamma_{p,q,r}
,
and this holds in our case. In fact, identifying the prototile labels by
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{B}},{\mathscr{C}})\equiv(1,2,3)
, we obtain from (
6.6
):

\begin{split}{\mathcal{D}}_{12}=\{0\},\ \ \ \ \ &{\mathcal{D}}_{22}=\{-r-j\lambda+p\lambda^{2}\}_{j=0}^{q-1},\ \ \ \ \ {\mathcal{D}}_{23}=\{-j+p\lambda^{2}\}_{j=1}^{r},\\
&{\mathcal{D}}_{31}=\{-j+p\lambda^{2}\}_{j=1}^{r},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ {\mathcal{D}}_{32}=\{-j\lambda^{2}+p\lambda^{2}\}_{j=0}^{p-1},\end{split}

with all the remaining
{\mathcal{D}}_{ij}
being empty.

Let
\alpha_{p,q,r}:{\mathbb{Z}}[1,\lambda,\lambda^{2}]=\Gamma_{p,q,r}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{3}
be the address map defined by
\alpha_{p,q,r}(n_{1}+n_{2}\lambda+n_{3}\lambda^{2})=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.
The inverse map is explicitly given by

\alpha^{-1}_{p,q,r}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\Re(\lambda)&\Re(\lambda^{2})\\
0&\Im(\lambda)&\Im(\lambda^{2})\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{1}\\
n_{2}\\
n_{3}\end{array}\right)=V_{p,q,r}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{1}\\
n_{2}\\
n_{3}\end{array}\right)=n_{1}+n_{2}\lambda+n_{3}\lambda^{2},

where
V_{p,q,r}
is the matrix with column vectors
1,\lambda,\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{C}
.

There is a neighborhood
\mathcal{M}_{p,q,r}
of
(1,\lambda,\lambda^{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\cong\mathbb{R}^{6}
which parametrizes non-degenerate deformations of parallelograms

and
\phi({\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{L}}_{k}({\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})}\delta_{x}*\psi_{k}(0)
has mean zero on
(X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}},\mu_{\mathfrak{f}})
, where

{\rm supp}(\psi_{k})\subset{\rm int}(T_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}})
.
Since
{\mathscr{M}}(0,n)=({\sf S}_{\omega}^{\sf T})^{n}
, we have

\chi^{+}(0,\vec{\zeta})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|({\sf S}_{\omega}^{\sf T})^{n}\cdot\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}(0)\|.

The claim will follow if we show that
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}(0)
is orthogonal to the PF eigenvector of
{\sf S}_{\omega}
.
By Birkhoff’s Ergodic Theorem and the definition of
\phi
, we have for a.e.
{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}\in X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
:

\displaystyle\int_{X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}}\phi\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}(2R)^{-d}\int_{Q_{R}}\phi({\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}-{\bf t})\,d{\bf t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{T_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}}}\psi_{k}\Bigr)\cdot{\rm freq}(T_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\widehat{\psi}_{k}(0)\cdot{\rm freq}(T_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}),

where we also used the existence of uniform patch frequencies.

Thus it suffices to check that

\bigl({\rm freq}(T_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})\bigr)_{k\leq m}
is a PF eigenvector for
{\sf S}_{\omega}
. But since uniform frequencies exist, for any
j\leq m
,

{\rm freq}(T_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})=\frac{\#\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\ T^{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}+t\subset T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}\}}{{\mathcal{L}}^{d}({\rm supp}(T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}))}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\sf S}_{\omega}^{n}(k,j)}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\sf S}_{\omega}^{n}(i,j)\cdot{\mathcal{L}}^{d}(T_{i}^{\mathfrak{f}})}

By the Perron-Frobenius Theorem,
{\sf S}_{\omega}^{n}(i,j)\sim\vartheta_{1}^{n}r_{i}\ell_{j}
, where
(r_{i})_{i\leq m}
and
(\ell_{j})_{j\leq m}
are the (normalized) right and left PF eigenvectors of
{\sf S}_{\omega}

respectively. It follows that
{\rm freq}(T_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})=r_{k}
, as claimed.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.2

Proof.

Every continuous eigenfunction is certainly measurable, so it suffices to show that if
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a “measurable eigenvalue,”
then (
5.2
) holds.

Let
{\bf v}
be an elementary recurrence vector in
X_{\omega}
. This implies, by definition (
3.7
),
that there exist a tile
T_{j}\in{\mathcal{T}}
and
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
T_{j}+{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{T}}
, with
{\bf v}=\alpha(z_{1},z_{2})
for
z_{1}\in T_{j}
and
{\mathbf{x}}=z_{2}-z_{1}
.

Then
T_{j}\cup(T_{j}+{\mathbf{x}})\subset{\mathcal{T}}
, and applying a
power of the tile substitution
\omega^{n}
we obtain

\omega^{n}(T_{j})\cup(\omega^{n}(T_{j})+\varphi^{n}{\mathbf{x}})\subset\omega^{n}({\mathcal{T}})\subset{\mathcal{T}}.

Now fix a vertex
v
of
{\mathcal{T}}
and apply the deformation
{\mathfrak{f}}
to the patch

\bigl(\omega^{n}(T_{j})-v\bigr)\cup\bigl((\omega^{n}(T_{j})+\varphi^{n}{\mathbf{x}})-v\bigr)\subset{\mathcal{T}}-v,

which yields

T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}\cup(T_{j}^{{\mathfrak{f}},n}+{\mathbf{x}}_{n})\subset({\mathcal{T}}-v)^{\mathfrak{f}},

for some
{\mathbf{x}}_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, see (
3.6
). In order to compute
{\mathbf{x}}_{n}
, note
that
(\varphi^{n}z_{1},\varphi^{n}z_{2})
is a recurrence in
{\mathcal{T}}
, and the corresponding recurrence vector is given by
\alpha\varphi^{n}(z_{1},z_{2})=M^{n}{\bf v}
, hence

{\mathbf{x}}_{n}={\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}M^{n}{\bf v},

in view of (
3.9
).

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be any
X_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{f}}
-patch and
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
a Borel set of diameter less than
\eta(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega})
(the diameter of the largest ball contained in every
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathfrak{f}}
prototile).
Consider the cylinder set

X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}},U}:=\Upsilon({\mathcal{P}})+U\subset X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}.

Lemma 7.4
.

There exists
\delta=\delta({\mathfrak{f}},{\mathbf{x}})>0

such that

(7.7)

\mu\bigl(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}},U}\cap(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}},U}+{\mathbf{x}}_{n})\bigr)\geq\delta\cdot\mu(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}},U})\ \ \mbox{for all}\ n\geq n({\mathcal{P}}).

Assuming (
7.7
) is verified, the proof of (
5.2
) proceeds exactly as in the proof of
[
34
, Theorem 4.3]
. In fact, in
[
34
, Lemma 1.6]
it is shown that any
finite local complexity and repetitive tiling space can be partitioned into a finite union of cylinder sets of arbitrarily small radius. Let
X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega}=\coprod_{\ell}X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell},V_{\ell}}
be such a
partition in our case. A measurable eigenfunction
f
for
(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{\omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mu_{\mathfrak{f}})
(which can be assumed equal to one in modulus a.e. by ergodicity), corresponding to an eigenvalue
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, can be approximated uniformly on a set of full measure by a linear combination
g
of characteristic functions of the cylinder sets
X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell},V_{\ell}}
. Suppose
\|f-g\|_{\infty}<{\varepsilon}
, where

{\varepsilon}
can be made arbitrarily small. Let

A_{n,{\varepsilon}}:=\coprod_{\ell}(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell},V_{\ell}}\cap(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell},V_{\ell}}+{\mathbf{x}}_{n})),

and note that
\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}(A_{n,{\varepsilon}})\geq\delta
by Lemma
7.4
.
Thus,

{\mathcal{J}}:=\int_{A_{n,{\varepsilon}}}|f({\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{n})-f({\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})|\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}=\Bigl|e^{-2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},{\mathbf{x}}_{n}\rangle}-1\Bigr|\cdot\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}(A_{n,{\varepsilon}})\geq\delta\cdot\Bigl|e^{-2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},{\mathbf{x}}_{n}\rangle}-1\Bigr|,

for
n\geq n_{0}=n_{0}({\varepsilon})
by the eigenvalue equation. (Note that
n_{0}
depends on the partition, which in turn depends on
{\varepsilon}
.) On the other hand,

{\mathcal{J}}\leq 2\|f-g\|_{\infty}+\int_{A_{n,{\varepsilon}}}|g({\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{n})-g({\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})|\,d\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}<2{\varepsilon},

since
g({\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{n})=g({\mathcal{S}}^{\mathfrak{f}})
on
A_{n,{\varepsilon}}
by construction. It follows that

\Bigl|e^{-2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},{\mathbf{x}}_{n}\rangle}-1\Bigr|\leq\frac{2{\varepsilon}}{\delta},\ \ n\geq n_{0}({\varepsilon}),\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ {\mathbf{x}}_{n}={\bf L}_{\mathfrak{f}}M^{n}{\bf v},

which is equivalent to (
5.2
). Thus it remains to prove the lemma. ∎

Proof of Lemma
7.4
.

For
n
sufficiently large, such that
{\mathcal{P}}
does not intersect
{\mathcal{P}}+{\mathbf{x}}_{n}
, we have

X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}},U}\cap(X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}},U}+{\mathbf{x}}_{n})\supset X^{\mathfrak{f}}_{{\mathcal{P}}\cup({\mathcal{P}}+{\mathbf{x}}_{n}),U}.

Hence by Proposition
2.2
, it suffices to show that

(7.8)

{\rm freq}({\mathcal{P}}\cup({\mathcal{P}}+{\mathbf{x}}_{n}),{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathfrak{f}})\geq\delta\cdot{\rm freq}({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathfrak{f}}),

for some
\delta>0
, independent of
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For the undeformed, self-similar tiling
{\mathcal{T}}
, the analogous inequality is proved in
[
34
, Lemma 4.2]
, and for the deformation
it follows by the quasi-isometry (
3.15
).
More precisely, by repetitivity, there exists
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a deformed super-tile
T_{i}^{{\mathfrak{f}},k_{0}}
such that

T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}}\cup(T_{j}^{\mathfrak{f}}+{\mathbf{x}})\subset T_{i}^{{\mathfrak{f}},k_{0}},

Delay-Doppler Alignment Modulation with Orthogonal Time Frequency Space


Abstract
The recently proposed delay-Doppler alignment modulation (DDAM) is a novel mitigation technique to mitigate time-frequency doubly-selective channels by leveraging leverages the high spatial resolution offered by large antenna arrays and multi-path sparsity of millimeter wave (mmWave) and TeraHertz. By introducing per-path based delay and Doppler compensation, followed by designing suitable beam-forming, it is possible to reshape the channel, significantly reducing the channel’s delay spread and Doppler spread. When combined with waveforms such as orthogonal time frequency space (OTFS), the reshaped channel can greatly relax the constraints on parameter selection. At the same time, it also significantly reduces the complexity of signal detection at the receiver.In this paper, we propose the novel DDAM-OTFS technique. For different acquisition situations of channel state information, two DDAM-OTFS implementation schemes are proposed. Finally, simulations illustrate the significant improvement in spectral efficiency and peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR) performance of DDAM-OTFS compared to conventional OTFS.



I
Introduction

Future wireless networks will introduce many new application scenarios, leading to increased demands on mobile communication systems. These diverse application scenarios may have very different performance requirements for communication systems. Among them, there is a class of communication scenarios, such as vehicular networks, high-speed trains, and aircraft communications, which due to their high-speed mobility characteristics, experience the Doppler effect, ultimately resulting in their corresponding wireless communication channels becoming fast time-varying channels.

In fourth and fifth-generation mobile communication systems, orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) is the mainstream waveform. OFDM converts high-speed serial data into multiple low-speed parallel data streams, resulting in OFDM symbols having a duration much longer than the channel delay spread, effectively mitigating the inter-symbol interference(ISI) issue. Additionally, the high efficiency and low complexity implementation have made OFDM widely used. However, in high-speed mobility scenarios, the Doppler frequency dispersion issues and resulting intercarrier interference (ICI) may challenge its reliability performance. To address time-varying channels, shortening the duration of OFDM symbols to make the channel variations over each symbol inconsequential is one approach
[
1
]
. However, this increases the overhead of the cyclic prefix, consequently reducing spectral efficiency.

OTFS modulation, proposed by in 2017, is a modulation scheme suitable for both frequency-selective fading and time-selective fading channels
[
2
]
. Unlike OFDM which carries signals in the time-frequency domain, OTFS multiplexes information symbols in the delay-Doppler domain. In scenarios with quasi-static and sparse time-varying channels, OTFS exhibits good communication performance in high-speed mobility scenarios. However, the quasi-static nature of the channel also imposes limitations on the frame format of OTFS
[
3
]
. When these limitations are not met, the channel may not exhibit the desired slowly varying characteristics.

Delay alignment modulation
(DAM), recently proposed in 2022, effectively addresses inter-symbol interference by leveraging the high spatial resolution of large antenna arrays and the sparsity of multi-path in millimeter-wave or higher frequency channels
[
4
]
. By performing path-based time-delay compensation and beamforming processing at the transmitter and/or receiver, DAM is able to manipulating channel delay spread without sacrificing the time-frequency resources.
[
5
]
proposes a DAM-OFDM technology that combines DAM and OFDM to achieve better performance in terms of spectral efficiency, bit error rate (BER), and PAPR.

To address time-frequency doubly selective channels, DDAM has been introduced as an extension of DAM
[
6
]
. DDAM introduces additional Doppler compensation to manipulate Doppler spread. However, DDAM requires higher channel prior information when the transmission bandwidth and duration are insufficient. System Model

In this section, we first review the main ideas of OTFS and DDAM, as well as their implementation and input-output relationships in the context of high mobility scenarios with time-frequency doubly selective channels. It is also pointed out that due to the constraints imposed by meeting the crystallization condition to ensure the non-fading property of the delay-Doppler domain, the spectral efficiency and peak-to-average ratio performance of OTFS will be limited. II-A

OTFS Concepts and Implementation

The core feature of OTFS technology is that the multiplexing of modulation symbols occurs in the delay-Doppler domain. OTFS modulation maps signals into the Doppler-delay domain
{X_{DD}}[k,l]
and then transforms the signals into the time domain
{x_{TD}}(t)
for transmission through certain transformation methods. Fig.
1
shows the OTFS implementation.

Fig. 1:
OTFS mod/demod.

Currently, there are two primary implementation approaches for OTFS. The first method involves mapping the signal to the time-frequency domain using symplectic finite Fourier transform (SFFT), followed by conversion to the time domain for transmission using the Heisenberg transform. While this method exhibits high complexity, it offers compatibility with existing communication modules, necessitating only the addition of preprocessing and postprocessing modules.

The second approach entails direct conversion of the signal to the time domain using inverse discrete Zak transform (IDZT)
[
7
]
, subsequently processing it into analog signals for transmission through digital-to-analog conversion (DAC). This implementation method is characterized by lower complexity.

At the receiver end, the time-domain signal
{x_{TD}}(t)
is transmitted through the wireless channel to obtain the signal
{y_{TD}}(t)
. Subsequently, the signal is processed through the inverse transformations discussed earlier to obtain the corresponding Doppler-delay domain signal
{Y_{DD}}[k,l]
. Further processing, such as equalization, can be applied to the received signal thereafter.

Consider the bandwidth occupied by an OTFS symbol frame in the time-frequency domain as
{B_{OTFS}}
, with a duration of
{T_{OTFS}}
for an OTFS frame. Let an OTF frame be divided into M subcarriers and N OTFS symbols, with a subcarrier spacing of
\Delta f=\frac{{{B_{OTFS}}}}{M}
and symbol duration of
T=\frac{{{T_{OTFS}}}}{N}

\{{X_{DD}}[k,l],k=0,...,N-1,l=0,...,M-1\}

\Gamma=\left\{{\left({\frac{k}{{NT}},\frac{l}{{M\Delta f}}}\right),k=0,...,N-1,l=0,...,M-1}\right\}

and

III
DDAM-OTFS System Model

The previous section discussed OTFS and DDAM under the condition of large bandwidth and duration, with sufficient delay and Doppler resolution. However, in practical communication systems, bandwidth or duration is often insufficient, leading to fractional delays and fractional Doppler shifts. At this point, DDAM cannot simplify the channel to an ideal Gaussian white noise channel, but can still achieve channel expansion and shortening effects. When introduced into the OTFS system, it can relax the frame format constraints of OTFS.

Fig.

From Fig.

{x_{TD}}[n]

M

{Y_{DD}}[k,l]

Let’s discuss the specific input-output relationship. Performing an IDZT transformation on an OTFS frame yields
{x_{TD}}[n]
.
Then, the time-domain signal undergoes DDAM processing. Considering the occurrence of fractional Doppler frequency shifts and fractional delays in practical communication scenarios, there are two implementation approaches for DDAM processing.

The first approach involves aligning physical paths. This method is suitable when channel information is known and sufficiently accurate. The drawback is the difficulty in accurately obtaining channel information. However, the advantage is that it closely matches the physical channel characteristics with minimal inter-symbol interference.The second approach involves aligning the Doppler-delay taps closest to each physical path. This approach is suitable when channel information is difficult to obtain or lacks precision. The disadvantage is increased inter-symbol interference, while the advantage is less stringent requirements on channel conditions, making implementation easier. The specific implementations and input-output relationships of these two approaches will be discussed next.

V
CONCLUSION

This paper investigates the communication system of DDAM-OTFS joint transmission. It analyzes the average spectral efficiency and PAPR performance of OTFS in relation to the constraints imposed by channel delay expansion and Doppler spread, elucidating how the introduction of DDAM relaxes these constraints by reducing channel delay expansion and Doppler spread, thereby leading to improved performance. Subsequently, the paper presents two implementation approaches for DDAM-OTFS, catering to scenarios where channel prior information is readily available or not easily accessible, along with the corresponding input-output relationships and SNR performance for both approaches. Finally, through a series of simulations, the paper validates the enhancement in average spectral efficiency and PAPR performance of DDAM-OTFS system compared to traditional OTFS.

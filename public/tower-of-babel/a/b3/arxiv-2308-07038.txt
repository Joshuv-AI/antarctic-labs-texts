Hyperelliptic genus 3 curves with involutions and a Prym map
Paweł Borówka, Anatoli Shatsila

Abstract
We characterise genus 3 complex smooth hyperelliptic curves that contain two additional involutions as curves that can be build from five points in ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} with a distinguished triple. We are able to write down explicit equations for the curves and all their quotient curves. We show that, fixing one of the elliptic quotient curve, the Prym map becomes a 2:1 map and therefore the hyperelliptic Klein Prym map, constructed recently by the first author with A. Ortega, is also 2:1 in this case. As a by-product we show an explicit family of ( 1 , d ) (1,d) polarised abelian surfaces (for d > 1 d>1 ), such that any surface in the family satisfying a certain explicit condition is abstractly non-isomorphic to its dual abelian surface.



1
Introduction

Curves with involutions are interesting objects to study. The existence of automorphisms provides a way to find models of curves (e.g. equations for hyperelliptic curves) or periods of Jacobians (e.g.
[
BL04
, Section 11.7]
). Moreover, involutions enable to consider (quotient) coverings that are central constructions in the Prym theory.

In the paper, we consider hyperelliptic curves of genus 3 that contain two additional involutions. The motivation to study them came from the Klein covering construction that has been described in
[
BO18
,
BO20
]
and
[
BO23
]
. A covering is called Klein if it is Galois 4:1 covering with the deck group being isomorphic to the Klein four-group. A covering is called hyperelliptic if both top and bottom curves are hyperelliptic. In
[
BO23
]
the authors show that the Prym map of the hyperelliptic Klein covering is injective in all non-trivial cases (i.e. when genera of curves in the construction increases).

The covering that has been left in
[
BO23
]
is the Klein covering of hyperelliptic genus 3 curve over an elliptic curve, called baby mixed case. It is because in such a case, one has a tower of curves
C\to E^{\prime}\to E
, where
E,E^{\prime}
are elliptic, so
P(C/E)=P(C/E^{\prime})
, i.e. the Klein Prym variety equals the classical Prym variety. The main result of the paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.10
)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{H}_{1,4}
be a moduli space of hyperelliptic Klein coverings of elliptic curves branched in 4 points and let
\mathcal{E}_{d}(d,d)
be the moduli space of
(1,d)
polarised abelian surfaces containing a pair of complementary elliptic curves of exponent
d
.

Then the considered Prym map
Pr:\mathcal{R}^{H}_{1,4}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)
is finite, dominant, not surjective map of generic degree two.

Such result is a little surprising because on the one hand the classical Prym maps of small genus curves are usually not finite and on the other hand all other hyperelliptic Klein Prym maps are injective.

The main idea of the proof is to show that there are precisely two smooth hyperelliptic genus 3 curves on a general abelian surface that belongs to
\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)
.
In order to obtain the above result, we describe the moduli space of genus 3 hyperelliptic curves with two additional commuting involutions in Theorem
3.3
. It turns out that they can be encoded as the 5-tuple of points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with the distinguished triple (up to projective equivalence).

As a by-product of some lemmas needed for the main result, we have been able to show that most of abelian surfaces that belong to
\mathcal{E}_{d}(d,d)
for
d>1
are not isomorphic to their duals:

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
2.5
)
.

Let
E,F
be non-isogenous elliptic curves with
P\in E[d]
and
Q\in F[d]
primitive
d
-torsion points. Assume that
E/P
is not isomorphic to
E
or
F/Q
is not isomorphic to
F
. Then
E\times F/\langle(P,Q)\rangle
(of type
(1,d)
) is not (abstractly) isomorphic to its dual.

The existence of the involutions implies that the Jacobian of the genus 3 curve is completely decomposable i.e., isogenous to the product of three quotient elliptic curves. Such curves are classically interesting examples, since the decomposition allows us to understand their periods in terms of periods of curves of smaller genera, especially in terms of elliptic curves, see
[
ES93
,
KR89
,
Pau08
,
PR17
,
LGRS23
]
. In particular, genus 3 curves with completely decomposable Jacobians have recently gained a lot of attention
[
HLP00
,
BO20
,
Kat21
,
MK22
]
.
Therefore, in Proposition
4.3
we show explicit equations of all the curves appearing in the construction. Since the Prym map is generically 2:1, we are able to write an explicit (rational) deck involution for the Prym map, see Proposition
4.6
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 is devoted to the theory of non-simple abelian surfaces and the result of dual surfaces being non-isomorphic is proved there. In Section 3 we describe the moduli space and prove the main result concerning the Prym map.
The last section of the paper is devoted to finding equations of the curves that appear in the construction and describing the deck involution. Moreover, we provide a construction of a period matrix of the Prym variety in terms of periods of elliptic curves. We conclude the paper with some problems for future work.

2
Non-simple abelian surfaces

We would like to start by recalling some results from the theory of abelian surfaces. Let
\mathcal{A}_{2}(1,d)
be the moduli of
(1,d)
polarised abelian surfaces and
\mathcal{A}_{2}=\mathcal{A}_{2}(1,1)
be the moduli of principally polarised ones. Recall from
[
BL04
]
and
[
Bor16
]
that for a principally polarised abelian surface we have the following:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
be a principally polarised abelian surface that contains an elliptic curve
E
of exponent 2. Then there exists a complementary elliptic curve
F
also of exponent 2 such that the addition map
E\times F\to A
is a polarised isogeny of degree 4. Note that
E\cap F=E[2]=F[2]\subset A
.

Moreover, the locus of principally polarised abelian surfaces containing an elliptic curve of exponent 2 is an irreducible surface in
\mathcal{A}_{2}
that is an image of a so- called singular relation of discriminant
4
.

Proof.

The first part is proved in
[
BL04
, Cor 12.1.2 and 12.1.4]
. The second part is essentially due to Humbert, see
[
Bor16
, Thm 4]
.
∎

Now, we would like to show a similar result for a
(1,2)
polarised abelian surfaces.
Denote by

\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)=\{A\in\mathcal{A}_{2}(1,2):A\text{ contains a complementary pair of elliptic curves of exponents }2,2\}.

Recall the notation from
[
BO18
]
: for an abelian variety
X
and its abelian subvarieties
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
with associated symmetric idempotents
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{k}
, we write

A=M_{1}\boxplus\ldots\boxplus M_{k}\ \text{ if }\ \epsilon_{1}+\ldots+\epsilon_{k}=1.

In particular, for a pair of complementary elliptic curves
E,F
, we often write
A=E\boxplus F
.
As usual, we also abuse notation by identifying an elliptic curve
E
with its image under an embedding
E\to A
unless we consider the product
A=E\times F
.

The following lemma is a special case of
[
AB22
, Thm 5.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The locus
\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)
is a (non-empty) irreducible surface in
\mathcal{A}_{2}(1,2)
.

Proof.

Let
X(2)
be the moduli space of triples
(E,P,P^{\prime})
, where
P,P^{\prime}\in E
is a (symplectic) basis of 2-torsion points. Consider the map

\displaystyle\Phi:X(2)\times X(2)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{A}_{2}(1,2)

\displaystyle((E,P,P^{\prime}),(F,Q,Q^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\mapsto((E\times F)/\langle(P,Q)\rangle,H),

where
\Phi^{*}H=\mathcal{O}_{E}(2)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{F}(2).
Since
(P,Q)\in(E\times F)[2]
, the quotient
X=(E\times F)/\langle(P,Q)\rangle
is a
(1,2)
-polarised abelian surface. Moreover, it follows from
[
BL04
, Cor. 6.3.5]
that the quotient map
\pi
is a polarised isogeny hence
\Phi
is well-defined. Since
X(2)
is irreducible, it is enough to show that
\im(\Phi)=\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)
. Since
\pi_{|E}
is injective,
E
has exponent 2 in
X
so the image of
\Phi
lies in
\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2).
Now, take
(A,H)\in\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)
and let
(E,F)
be a complementary pair of elliptic curves in
A
. Poincare’s Reducibility Theorem yields an addition map
\mu
that is a polarised isogeny of degree 2

\mu:(E\times F,\mathcal{O}_{E}(2)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{F}(2))\to(A,H).

By
[
BL04
, Prop. 5.3.11]
we know that
\ker\mu=\langle(P,Q)\rangle
for
P\in E[2],Q\in F[2]
hence
(A,H)\in\im(\Phi)
.
∎

Since we want to show an auxiliary result (i.e. Theorem
2.5
), from now on, let
d>1
and assume that
P\in E[d],\ Q\in F[d]
are primitive
d
-torsions (i.e. their orders are precisely
d
). In this case
A=(E\times F)/\langle(P,Q)\rangle
is
(1,d)
polarised and
A\in\mathcal{E}_{d}(d,d)
.
We have the following.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
E,F
are non-isogenous then
A
does not contain any other elliptic curve.

Proof.

Let
G
be en elliptic curve contained in
A
and consider the Norm map of
E
.
Note that either
\im(\Nm_{E}|_{G})=\{0\}
or
\im(\Nm_{E}|_{G})=E
and the latter occurs when
E
and
G
are isogenous.
Now, recall that
m_{d}\Nm_{E}+m_{d}\Nm_{F}=m_{d^{2}}
where
m_{k}
is multiplication by
k
(see
[
BL04
, p. 124]
), so
\im(\Nm_{E}|_{G}+\Nm_{F}|_{G})=G
, hence if one of the images is
0
then
G
is equal to the other curve. However, if both maps are non-zero then
G
is isogenous to both curves, hence
E,F
are isogenous.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
E,F
be complementary elliptic curves in
A=(E\times F)/\langle(P,Q)\rangle
of exponent d. Then
Q=-P\in A
and
E\cap F=\langle P\rangle\subset\ker\phi_{H}
. Moreover
E\cap\ker\phi_{H}=F\cap\ker\phi_{H}=\langle P\rangle
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mu(P,0)=P\in E,\ \mu(0,Q)=Q\in F
. Now, the first equation follows trivially from the fact that
(P,0)+(0,Q)=(P,Q)\in\ker\mu
.

To see that
E\cap F=\langle P\rangle
one can check that
\mu(x,0)=\mu(0,y)\iff(x,-y)\in\ker\mu=\langle(P,Q)\rangle
and use the fact that
P,Q
are primitive
d
-torsions.

Now, in order to finish the proof, one needs to use the fact that
\ker\phi_{H}=\mu((\ker\mu)^{\perp})
where
\perp
stands for the symplectic complement (with respect to the symplectic pairing
\omega
), see
[
BL04
, Prop 6.2.1 and 6.3.3]
.
Since
x\in E[d]
is of the form
aP+bP^{\prime}
(where
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}
) with
\omega(P,P^{\prime})=1\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}
we get that
(aP+bP^{\prime},0)\in(\ker\mu)^{\perp}\iff b=0
.
∎

Now, we are ready to show a way to find an explicit condition that proves that a surface is not abstractly isomorphic to its dual.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
E,F
be non-isogenous elliptic curves with
P\in E[d]
and
Q\in F[d]
primitive
d
-torsion points. Assume that
E/P
is not isomorphic to
E
or
F/Q
is not isomorphic to
F
. Then
E\times F/\langle(P,Q)\rangle
(of type
(1,d)
) is not (abstractly) isomorphic to its dual.

Proof.

By symmetry we can assume
E/P
is not isomorphic to
E
.
Denote by
A=E\times F/\langle(P,Q)\rangle
. Then its dual is
\hat{A}=A/\ker\phi_{H}
.
By Lemma
2.4
, we get that
E\cap\ker\phi_{H}=\langle P\rangle
, so
E^{\prime}=E/\langle P\rangle
is an elliptic curve embedded in
\hat{A}
. If
\hat{A}
was isomorphic to
A
then the image of
E^{\prime}
via the isomorphism would be an elliptic curve embedded in
A
. By assumption it cannot be
E
and it cannot be
F
since they are non-isogenous. Since this contradicts Proposition
2.3
, we get that
\hat{A}
is not isomorphic to
A
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

It is well-known that a general non-principally polarised abelian variety is not isomorphic to its dual. The advantage of Theorem
2.5
is that it provides a family of examples that enables to write down explicit period matrices of surfaces.

We would like to finish this section by describing better the connection between
A
and
\hat{A}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

If
A=E\times F/\langle(P,Q)\rangle
, then
A/P=E^{\prime}\times F^{\prime}
and

\hat{A}=E^{\prime}\times F^{\prime}/G
, where
G\simeq\ker\phi_{H}/P\simeq\langle(P^{\prime},-Q^{\prime})\rangle
.
In particular,
A\in\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)\iff\hat{A}\in\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)
.

Proof.

Note that
A/P=(E\times F)/\langle(P,0),(0,Q)\rangle
and
\langle(P,0),(0,Q)\rangle
is an isotropic subgroup of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{d}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{d}
coming from
\mathcal{O}_{E}(d)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{F}(d)
. Thus,
A/P=E^{\prime}\times F^{\prime}
(with a principal polarization) and the quotient map is a polarised isogeny by
[
BL04
, Cor. 6.3.5]
. Since
\hat{A}=A/\ker\phi_{H}
, we get the desired equality for
\hat{A}
. In the case
d=2
,
(P^{\prime},-Q^{\prime})
is a 2-torsion point of
E^{\prime}\times F^{\prime}
, hence
\hat{A}
lies in the image of
\Phi
, which finishes the proof.
∎

3
Geometry of hyperelliptic genus 3 curves with involutions

Now, we are ready to set up the construction. We start by recalling a useful result concerning involutions on hyperelliptic curves that is an easy application of Accola’s Lemma
[
Acc69
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth hyperelliptic curve of genus
g=3
with
\iota
the hyperelliptic involution. For any
\tau\in\Aut(X)
,
\tau^{2}=\id
,
\tau\neq\iota
we have

\Fix(\tau)=\varnothing,\>|\Fix(\iota\tau)|=4,\>\>\>\text{or}\>\>\>|\Fix(\tau)|=4,\Fix(\iota\tau)=\varnothing.

Now, let
\sigma\neq\tau
be commuting involutions on
X
such that
|\Fix(\sigma)|=|\Fix(\tau)|=4
. Then
|\Fix(\sigma\tau)|=4
and
g(X/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle)=0
.

Proof.

By Accola’s Lemma (
[
Acc69
, Lemma 1]
) applied to
G_{0}=\langle\tau,\iota\rangle
we get

3=g(X/\tau)+g(X/\iota\tau).

Then, Hurwitz formula yields
g(X/\tau)=2
and
g(X/\iota\tau)=1
or vice versa.

For the second part, applying Accola’s Lemma (
[
Acc69
]
) to
G_{0}=\langle\tau,\sigma\rangle
we have

3+2g(X/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle)=g(X/\sigma)+g(X/\tau)+g(X/\sigma\tau).

By Hurwitz formula,
g(X/\sigma)=g(X/\tau)=1
, hence
g(X/\sigma\tau)
is odd. The only possibility is
g(X/\sigma\tau)=1
, hence
|\Fix(\sigma\tau)|=4
. Since the right hand side of the equation equals 3, we get that
g(X/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle)=0
.
∎

From now on, we will view elliptic curves both as one-dimensional abelian varieties and genus one curves with a chosen point. We will also use the fact that any elliptic curve is canonically isomorphic to its dual (as an abelian variety).
With such convention, we state the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
g>1
and
E
an elliptic curve. Then the following are equivalent:

i)

there exists a double covering
f:C\to E
;

ii)

E
can be embedded into
JC
as an abelian subvariety of exponent
2
.

Proof.

The equivalence follows from the fact that
f
does not factorise via a cyclic étale covering (because it is branched of degree 2), hence
f^{*}\circ Nm_{f}=Nm_{E}
, see
[
BL04
, Prop 12.3.2]
.
∎

In
[
BO23
]
the authors have investigated hyperelliptic curves with additional commuting involutions and they have shown the injectivity of the hyperelliptic Klein Prym map in a general case. The only case that has been left is a genus 3 hyperelliptic curve that is a Klein covering of an elliptic curve. Now, we are ready to investigate such a case.

be a hyperelliptic genus 3 curve such that

3.1
From bottom to up and a Prym map

Now, we would like to describe the construction starting from one of elliptic curves, say

Lemma 3.5
.

The following are equivalent:

the locus of 5 points in

2.

the locus of pairs

, where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}\subset\Aut(\tilde{C})

is an involution with

the locus of pairs

4.

the locus of genus 2 curves

whose images under both covering maps are equal.

Proof.

The equivalence

with four fixed points in the case
(2)
and the points of the distinguished triple in the case

To obtain

we take

and

\sigma

one considers the covering branched at

It remains to show

be two Weierstrass points on

Remark 3.6
.

Note that changing a distinguished point within a triple means that one starts with another elliptic curve (another involution) but the genus 3 curve
\tilde{C}
is the same.

Using the fact that
\tilde{C}/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
we get that

Recall that the Prym variety of the covering

can be defined as the complementary abelian subvariety to

in

and is usually denoted by

or

In our case, by choosing

is equal to

Remark 3.7
.

Note that
P(\tilde{C}/E_{\sigma})=P(\tilde{C}/E_{\sigma,\iota\tau})
, hence we also study Pryms of hyperelliptic Klein coverings.

The main point of the Prym theory is to consider
the Prym map
that assigns to a covering its Prym variety, see for example
[

BO22

.
Let

\mathcal{R}^{H}_{1,4}=\{(\tilde{C},\sigma)\>|\>g(\tilde{C})=3,(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{3}\subset\Aut(\tilde{C}),\sigma^{2}=\id,|\Fix(\sigma)|=4\}

be the moduli space considered in Lemma

.
It follows that we have a well-defined Prym map

We want to find the generic degree of
\Pr
. We start by recalling some results.

Proposition 3.8

(
[
Bar87
]
, Duality Theorem 1.12)
.

Let
C
be a hyperelliptic genus 3 curve and let

be a
(1,2)
polarised abelian surface. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

C
can be embedded in
\hat{A}
;

2.

A
can be embedded in
JC
;

3.

C
is a double covering of an elliptic curve that is complementary to

in
JC
.

Note that by
[
Bor16
, Prop 6]
, there are precisely three smooth hyperelliptic curves on a general
(1,2)
polarised abelian surface. Now, we are ready to show a similar result in our case.

Proposition 3.9
.

On
A\in\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)
there are at most two smooth hyperelliptic genus 3 curves (up to a translation) and there is an open dense set
U\subset\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)
such that for

there are precisely two smooth curves on

.

Proof.

Firstly, by
[
Bor16
, Prop 6]
, there is a 1:1 correspondence between smooth hyperelliptic curves on
A
and polarised isogenies to Jacobians of smooth curves and there are at most three of them.

Recall that a principally polarised abelian surface is either a Jacobian of a smooth curve or a polarised product of two elliptic curves. Hence if
\pi:A\to E^{\prime}\times F^{\prime}
is a polarised isogeny of degree 2, then
\pi^{-1}(E^{\prime}\times\{0\})
and
\pi^{-1}(\{0\}\times F^{\prime})
are elliptic curves in

. By Proposition

there are precisely two elliptic curves on a general

.

Moreover, if

, then

, so indeed there is at least one product of elliptic curves and at most two Jacobians of smooth curves downstairs.

Let
U=\{A:\exists P,Q\in A[2],A/P\text{ and }A/Q\text{ are Jacobians of smooth curves}\}
.
Note that
U
is open and dense because the set of Jacobians is open and dense.
∎

For low genera, the Prym map is usually dominant and with positive dimensional fibres for dimensional reasons. Since we are interested in special coverings, both domain and codomain have the same dimension, so we can consider the (generic) degree of the Prym map.
Now we would like to compute the degree of the Prym map.

Theorem 3.10
.

The considered Prym map
Pr:\mathcal{R}^{H}_{1,4}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)
is finite, dominant, not surjective map of generic degree two.

Proof.

Let

. By Proposition
2.7
, we get that
\widehat{P}\in\mathcal{E}_{2}(2,2)
, so by Proposition
3.9
there exists at most 2 smooth genus 3 hyperelliptic curves (precisely 2 on a general

), called

(that are coverings of

) that are embedded into
\widehat{P}
.
On the other hand, Proposition
3.8
says that this is a necessary and sufficient condition for

to be the Prym of a covering

.
The fact that

is not surjective follows from Example

.
∎

Example 3.11
.

There exists

not in the image of

.

Proof.

Let

be an elliptic curve given by the equation

. Then

as follows:

\displaystyle E_{\Delta}

\displaystyle:=\{(s,s):s\in E\};

\displaystyle E_{-\Delta}

\displaystyle:=\{(s,-s):s\in E\};

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\{(s,\alpha(s)):s\in E\};

\displaystyle E_{-\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\{(s,-\alpha(s)):s\in E\}.

Let

and

be the quotient map. We abuse the notation by writing

and

Note that
\mu(E_{\Delta})
and

are isomorphic to

and are complementary in

of exponent 2, and

\mu(E_{\alpha})
and
\mu(E_{-\alpha})
are isomorphic to

and complementary in

of exponent 2, and
\mu(E_{\alpha})\cap\mu(E_{-\alpha})=\{0,\mu(e_{1}+f_{1},f_{2})\}

\displaystyle A/\langle\mu(e_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{1}/\langle e_{1}\rangle\times E_{2}/\langle e_{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle A/\langle\mu(f_{1},f_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{\Delta}/E_{\Delta}[2]\times E_{-\Delta}/E_{-\Delta}[2],

\displaystyle A/\langle\mu(e_{1}+f_{1},f_{2})\rangle

∎

Remark 3.12

By

In the following proposition we find the involution on the moduli space corresponding to the involution exchanging points in the fibres of the Prym map in Theorem
3.10
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

\varphi:\mathcal{R}^{H}_{1,4}\ni([-a:1],[-b:1];[1:0];[2:1],[-2:1])\mapstochar\dashrightarrow\\
([-b:1],[-a:1];[1:0];[-2-a-b:1],[-2:1])\in\mathcal{R}^{H}_{1,4}

(4.1)

be the rational involution on
\mathcal{R}^{H}_{1,4}
. Here we modify the notation from Proposition
4.5
to indicate, that
[1:0]
is the distinguished point in the triple.

Then
\Pr(T)=\Pr(\varphi(T))
whenever
\varphi
is defined on
T\in\mathcal{R}^{H}_{1,4}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{C}/E_{\sigma}
and
\tilde{C}^{\prime}/E_{\sigma}^{\prime}
be the coverings associated to the 5-tuple

([-a:1],[-b:1];[1:0];[2:1],[-2:1]),

and

([-b:1],[-a:1];[1:0];[-2-a-b:1],[-2:1])

respectively. Note that the latter 5-tuple is projectively equivalent to

([-a:1],[-b:1];[1:0];[2:1],[2-a-b:1]).

By looking at 2-torsion points we obtain
E_{\iota\sigma,\tau}
is isomorphic to
E_{\iota\sigma,\tau}^{\prime}
and

E_{\iota\sigma,\iota\tau}
is isomorphic to
E_{\iota\sigma,\iota\tau}^{\prime}
.

Applying the projective transformation given by
\begin{pmatrix}4&8+2a+2b\\
0&-a-b\end{pmatrix}
to the 5-tuple
([-b:1],[-a:1];[1:0];[-2-a-b:1],[-2:1])
we have another description of
\varphi

\varphi([-a:1],[-b:1];[1:0],[2:1],[-2:1])=\\
\left(\left[-\frac{2b-2a-8}{a+b}:1\right],\left[-\frac{2a-2b-8}{a+b}:1\right];[1:0];[2:1],[-2:1]\right).

(4.2)

One can check that
j
-invariants of
E_{\tau}
(resp.
E_{\sigma\tau}

It follows that the two-torsion points

Remark 4.7
.

Note that if
b=-a

and

), hence on such curves

4.1
Period matrix of
P(\tilde{C}/E_{\sigma})

Let us compute a period matrix of
P(\tilde{C}/E_{\sigma})
. We know that
P(\tilde{C}/E_{\sigma})=(E_{\tau}\times E_{\sigma\tau})/\langle e_{1}+f_{1}\rangle
. Possibly after a symplectic transformation, we can assume that small period matrices of
E_{\tau}
and
E_{\sigma\tau}

[z_{1}]
and

(2,2)
).

Proposition 4.8
.

With the assumptions above, a (big) period matrix of
P(\tilde{C}/E_{\sigma})
is

\begin{bmatrix}z_{1}&z_{1}&1&0\\
z_{1}&z_{1}+z_{2}&0&2\end{bmatrix}

The embeddings of
E_{\tau}
and
E_{\sigma\tau}
are given by
x\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}x\\
x\end{bmatrix}

A big period matrix of a
(2,2)
-polarized product

is

0&z_{2}&0&2\end{bmatrix},

We would like to conclude the paper with the following problems.

1.

Is it possible to naturally compactify the domain, codomain and the Prym map from Theorem
3.10
?

2.

The investigated moduli space (Theorem
3.3
) is of dimension 2. Therefore, one can expect that if three elliptic curves satisfy some relation there should exists a hyperelliptic genus 3 curve that is a covering of these three elliptic curves. Can one find such an explicit relation?

3.

We have been able to compute period matrices of Prym varieties. Is it possible to use them to construct explicit period matrices of completely decomposable Jacobians of hyperelliptic genus 3 curves? Note that our construction lives in the boundary of a family considered in

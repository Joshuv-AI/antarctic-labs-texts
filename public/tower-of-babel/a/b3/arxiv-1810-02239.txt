Fixed point combinators as fixed points of higher-order fixed point generators
Andrew Polonsky

Abstract
. Corrado Böhm once observed that if Y Y is any fixed point combinator (fpc), then Y ( λ y x . x ( y x ) ) Y(\lambda yx.x(yx)) is again fpc. He thus discovered the first “fpc generating scheme” – a generic way to build new fpcs from old. Continuing this idea, define an fpc generator to be any sequence of terms G 1 , … , G n G_{1},\dots,G_{n} such that Y is fpc ⟹ Y G 1 ⋯ G n is fpc . Y\text{ is fpc }\;\Longrightarrow\;YG_{1}\cdots G_{n}\text{ is fpc}. In this contribution, we take first steps in studying the structure of (weak) fpc generators. We isolate several robust classes of such generators, by examining their elementary properties like injectivity and (weak) constancy. We provide sufficient conditions for existence of fixed points of a given generator ( G 1 , ⋯ , G n ) (G_{1},\cdots,G_{n}) : an fpc Y Y such that Y = Y G 1 ⋯ G n Y=YG_{1}\cdots G_{n} . We conjecture that weak constancy is a necessary condition for existence of such (higher-order) fixed points. This statement generalizes Statman’s conjecture on non-existence of “double fpcs”: fixed points of the generator ( G ) = ( λ y x . x ( y x ) ) (G)=(\lambda yx.x(yx)) discovered by Böhm. Finally, we define and make a few observations about the monoid of (weak) fpc generators. This enables us to formulate new conjectures about their structure.

1.
Introduction

Fixed point combinators (fpcs) are a fascinating class of lambda terms. Arising in the proof of the Fixed Point Theorem, their dynamical character affects the global structure of the Lambda Calculus in a fundamental way. Being a mechanism of unrestricted recursion,
they are directly responsible for the Turing-completeness of the lambda calculus as a programming language.
1
1

1

In fact, the very notion of Turing-completeness
traces back to Church’s bold suggestion that lambda calculus can encode arbitrary computational processes. Yet the idea was only accepted after Kleene and Turing, using fixed
point constructions, showed equivalence between Church’s formalism
and their own ones.

And when lambda terms are used as the computational basis of a logical system

.

fixed point combinators appear unexpectedly as the untyped skeletons of paradoxes, heralding inconsistency of the logic lying over the computational calculus.

Böhm’s observations revealed that fpcs themselves have a compositional structure, where one constructs new fpcs from old by applying them to

In this paper, we will explore such fpc generators “in the abstract”, studying their general properties and providing a basic taxonomy. We formulate several new problems, including a significant strengthening of Statman’s conjecture.

4.
The four main classes of generators

Let us consider again Statman’s conjecture on the non-existence of fixed points of the generator
\vec{G}=(\delta)
. This conjecture is intuitively compelling, because applying any fpc
Y
to
\delta
leads to a slowdown of head reductions that seems impossible to remove:

\displaystyle Yx

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow_{w}x(Y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=_{\beta}x(Yx)

\displaystyle Y\delta x

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow_{w}\delta(Y^{\prime}[x:=\delta])x\twoheadrightarrow_{w}x(Y^{\prime}[x:=\delta]x)

\displaystyle=_{\beta}x(Y\delta x)

Upon closer inspection, the central property of the generator
(\delta)
that this reasoning depends on is that
\delta(Y\delta)x

adds to
the reduction length needed for the Böhm tree to develop, while still using the given fpc
Y
in constructing this Böhm tree infinitely often.
Since any conversion between the two will synchronize their Böhm reductions, no such conversion can be possible.

If this reasoning proves to be correct for
\delta
, it should remain valid for any generator possessing the same property. This leads us to the following definition and conjecture.

Recall, for any wfpc
(Y_{n})
,
Y_{0}=\lambda x.x^{k}(Y_{k}x)=\delta^{k}(Y_{k})=\delta^{k}(z)[z:=Y_{k}]
.

{defi}

A generator
\vec{G}
is
accretive
if, for each

z\in^{\infty}\delta^{k}(z)\vec{G}
.

That is,
\vec{G}
is accretive if it actually uses every level of the input fpc
in constructing the output fpc, so that replacing
Y
with any approximant will
also cut the Böhm tree of
Y\vec{G}
.

Conjecture 8
.

If
\vec{G}
is accretive, then there exists no
Y\in\mathsf{WFPC}
such that
Y=Y\vec{G}
.

Theorem 15
.

A fgv

Proof 5.1
.

(\Rightarrow)
:

Suppose

Then

\displaystyle Y=(Y_{0},Y_{1},\dots)\in\mathsf{WFPC}

\displaystyle\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle Y\vec{G}=Y_{0}G_{0}\cdots G_{n}=G_{0}^{k}(Y_{k}G_{0})G_{1}\cdots G_{n}=G_{0}^{k}(z)G_{1}\cdots G_{n}[z:=Y_{k}G_{0}]\in\mathsf{FPC}.

(\Leftarrow)

\vec{G}

Fix

\displaystyle W_{x,p}

\displaystyle=\lambda v.x(v(cp)v)

\displaystyle=\lambda p.W_{x,p}

\displaystyle=\lambda x.V_{x}\mathtt{I}V_{x}

6.
The monoid of wfgvs

The wfpc and fpc generators have an obvious monoid structure:

(G_{0},\dots,G_{n})\odot(G_{0}^{\prime},\dots,G_{m}^{\prime})\quad:=\quad(G_{0},\dots,G_{n},G_{0}^{\prime},\dots,G_{m}^{\prime})

The identity is the trivial generator
()
.
The concatenation operation is associative, and satisfies
the identity laws.
We thus have a monoid
(\mathcal{G},\odot,())
of wfgvs,
containing a submonoid
(\mathcal{F},\odot,())
of fgvs.

Neither of these monoids is finitely generated, as there are infinitely many constant fgvs of the form
\mathtt{K}\Theta_{M}
that cannot be obtained by composition of more elementary ones.

Extensional equality

Since the primary interest in (w)fgvs is in their ability to generate new (w)fpcs from old, it is natural to identify generators having the same functional behavior.

{defi}

We say a fgv or wfgv
\vec{G}
is
extensionally equal
to
\vec{G}^{\prime}
, written
G\simeq G^{\prime}
, if for every
fpc

Y
,
Y\vec{G}=Y\vec{G}^{\prime}
.
(Note that this is an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{G}
, preserved by
\odot
.)

{exas}

•

If
\vec{G}
is a constant generator, say,
Y\vec{G}=Z
for all
Y
, then
\vec{G}\simeq(\mathtt{K}Z)
:

Y(\mathtt{K}Z)=\mathtt{K}Z(Y^{\prime}(\mathtt{K}Z))=Z=Y\vec{G}

•

Recall the combinator
\mathtt{C}=\lambda fxy.fyx
.
Note that
\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K}=\mathtt{K}\mathtt{I}
, and
\mathtt{C}(\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K})=\mathtt{C}(\mathtt{K}\mathtt{I})=\mathtt{K}
.
Let
Gyz=z(y(\mathtt{C}z))(\delta(y(\mathtt{C}z)))
.
Then
(G,\mathtt{K})
, and
(G,\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K})
are fgvs, and
(G,\mathtt{K})\simeq(G,\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K})

\displaystyle Y\in\mathsf{FPC}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad YG\mathtt{K}

\displaystyle=G(YG)\mathtt{K}=\mathtt{K}(YG(\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K}))(\delta(YG(\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K})))=YG(\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K})

\displaystyle=G(YG)(\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K})=(\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K})(YG(\mathtt{C}(\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K})))(\delta(YG(\mathtt{C}(\mathtt{C}\mathtt{K}))))

\displaystyle\hskip 68.71321pt=(\mathtt{K}\mathtt{I})(YG\mathtt{K})(\delta(YG\mathtt{K}))=\delta(YG\mathtt{K})\in\mathsf{FPC}

The reason that in the definition of

the quantifier ranges over fpcs both in the case of fgvs as well as wfgvs is that, when the quantifier is taken over all wfpcs, it makes the resulting notion of equality much more restrictive, as we shall now demonstrate.

Because we obviously want equal fgvs to remain equal as wfgvs, the definition of extensional equality is expressed in terms of behavior on fpcs in both contexts.

The structure of many monoids can be characterized in terms of Green’s relations. Here we record several observations about these relations in

{defi}

\displaystyle\mathpzc{L}(\vec{G})

\displaystyle\!\quad=\quad\{{\vec{H}\vec{G}\mid\vec{H}\in\mathcal{G}}\}

\displaystyle\mathpzc{R}(\vec{G})

\displaystyle\!\quad=\quad\{{\vec{G}\vec{H}\mid\vec{H}\in\mathcal{G}}\}

\displaystyle\iff\,\mathpzc{L}(\vec{G})\subseteq\mathpzc{L}(\vec{G}^{\prime})\,\iff\vec{G}\in\mathpzc{L}(\vec{G}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\vec{G}\preccurlyeq_{\mathpzc{R}}\vec{G}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\iff\mathpzc{R}(\vec{G})\subseteq\mathpzc{R}(\vec{G}^{\prime})\iff\vec{G}\in\mathpzc{R}(\vec{G}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\iff\,\mathpzc{L}(\vec{G})=\mathpzc{L}(\vec{G}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\iff\mathpzc{R}(\vec{G})=\mathpzc{R}(\vec{G}^{\prime}).

(1)

was arbitrary,

\mathtt{K}Z\sim_{\mathpzc{R}}\mathtt{K}Z^{\prime}
for all
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
.
That is, constant generators are all in the same right class.
Since constant generators form an ideal,
\vec{G}{\sim{R}}(\mathtt{K}Z)
or
\vec{G}\preccurlyeq{R}(\mathtt{K}Z)
imply
\vec{G}\simeq(\mathtt{K}Z^{\prime})
for some
Z^{\prime}
.
So
\mathpzc{R}(\mathtt{K}Z)=\{{(\mathtt{K}Z^{\prime})\mid Z^{\prime}\in\mathsf{WFPC}}\}

is the ideal of all constant generators.

(3)

Similarly, if
\vec{G}
is weakly constant, then so is every element of
\mathpzc{L}(\vec{G})
and
\mathpzc{R}(\vec{G})
.
That is, the only (w)fgvs that can be congruent to
\vec{G}
modulo
\sim{L}
or
\sim{R}
are again weakly constant.

(4)

If
\vec{G}
is compact, then so is every element of
\mathpzc{L}(\vec{G})
.
When restricted to
\mathcal{F}
, both
\mathpzc{L}(\vec{G})
and
\mathpzc{R}(\vec{G})
consist of compact generators.

(5)

Suppose
\vec{G}\sim{R}\vec{G}^{\prime}
. Then we can find
\vec{F},\vec{F}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\vec{G}\simeq\vec{G}^{\prime}\vec{F}
, and
\vec{G}^{\prime}\simeq\vec{G}\vec{F}^{\prime}
.
But then
\vec{G}\simeq\vec{G}\vec{F}^{\prime}\vec{F}
,
and
\vec{G}^{\prime}\simeq\vec{G}^{\prime}\vec{F}\vec{F}^{\prime}
.
If
\vec{G}\simeq\vec{G}\vec{F}^{\prime}\vec{F}
, then for every
Y
,
Y\vec{G}=Y\vec{G}\vec{F}^{\prime}\vec{F}
is a fixed point of
\vec{F}^{\prime}\vec{F}
.
By Conjecture
8
,
\vec{F}^{\prime}\vec{F}
is weakly constant.
By Proposition
20
, so is

\vec{G}\vec{F}^{\prime}\vec{F}
.
But
\vec{G}\vec{F}^{\prime}\vec{F}\simeq\vec{G}
. So
\vec{G}
is weakly constant.
Of course, everything we just said applies to
\vec{G}^{\prime}
as well.
We conclude that, modulo Conjecture
8
, nontrivial

\sim{R}
-relations can only exist between weakly constant wfgvs.

In fact, we believe that for accretive
\vec{G},\vec{G}^{\prime}
, either
\vec{G}\sim{R}\vec{G}^{\prime}
or
\vec{G}\sim{L}\vec{G}^{\prime}
implies
\vec{G}\simeq\vec{G}^{\prime}
.
Indeed, this is a consequence of the “freeness” conjecture we formulate next.

Unique factorization of accretive generators

{defi}

An accretive generator
\vec{G}
is
prime
if

\vec{G}=\vec{G}_{1}\vec{G}_{2}
implies
\{{\vec{G}_{1},\vec{G}_{2}}\}=\{{\vec{G},()}\}
.

The following “unique factorization conjecture” states that the accretive generators are freely generated by the prime ones.

Conjecture 21
.

If
\vec{G}\in\mathcal{G}
is accretive, then
\vec{G}=\vec{G}_{1}\cdots\vec{G}_{k}
, where each
\vec{G}_{i}
is prime.

Furthermore, this decomposition is unique up to extensional equality. That is, for all prime
\vec{G}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\vec{G}^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}
,
if
\vec{G}=\vec{G}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots\vec{G}^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}
, then
k=k^{\prime}

and
\vec{G}_{i}\simeq\vec{G}^{\prime}_{i}
for all
i\in\{{1,\dots,k}\}
.

The conjecture implies that any relations in the monoid of wfpc generators under composition can only arise between non-accretive i.e., weakly compact generators. In particular, the equations

\displaystyle\vec{F}\vec{G}

\displaystyle=\vec{F}

(6)

\displaystyle\vec{F}\vec{G}

\displaystyle=\vec{G}

(7)

admit no (non-trivial) solutions among the accretive generators.
As a result, the left and right classes of accretive generators would indeed all be distinct if Conjecture
21
was to be validated.

And what about the solutions to (
6
) or (
7
) among the rest of
\mathcal{G}
? The following examples show that there indeed exist solutions to these equations under certain conditions. In all cases, (weak) compactness plays an essential role.

Proposition 22
.

For
\vec{G}
weakly constant, there exists non-constant
\vec{F}
with
\vec{F}\vec{G}\simeq\vec{F}
.
(In particular,
\vec{F}\preccurlyeq{L}\vec{G}
.)

Proof 6.3
.

The idea is to make
\vec{F}
generate the fixed points of
\vec{G}
according to the scheme in Proposition
13
.
Let
k
,
F_{0}
,
F_{k}
be chosen as in the proof of that proposition.
Put
A=\lambda yb.b(y\delta)
,
B=\lambda y.F_{0}[y(\lambda u.F_{k}[uG_{0}])G_{0}]
, and
\vec{F}=(A,B)
.
Observe that

\displaystyle Y\in\mathsf{FPC}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Y\!A\delta

\displaystyle=A(Y\!A)\delta=\delta(Y\!A\delta)

\displaystyle Y\!AB

\displaystyle=A(Y\!A)B=B(Y\!A\delta)=F_{0}[(Y\!A\delta)(\lambda u.F_{k}[uG_{0}])G_{0}].

Since
Y\!A\delta
is thereby forced to be fpc, it follows that
Y\!AB=F_{0}[UG_{0}]
, where
U=F_{k}[UG_{0}]
. This allows us to calculate as in the proof of Proposition
13
that
Y\!AB
is a fixed point of
\vec{G}
:

Y\vec{F}\vec{G}=Y\!AB\vec{G}=Y\!AB

Note however, that
\vec{F}
will not be constant in general, because it uses its fpc argument to define
U
.

Proposition 23
.

Let
\vec{F}=(F_{0},\cdots,F_{n})
be wfgv with
n\geq 1
.
There exists a compact fgv
\vec{G}
such that
\vec{F}\vec{G}\simeq\vec{G}
.
(In particular,
\vec{G}\preccurlyeq{R}\vec{F}
.)

Proof 6.4
.

First, recall that
F_{0}=\lambda v_{0}..v_{l}.v_{m}\vec{P}
is solvable. Since
Y\vec{F}=F_{0}(Y^{\prime}F_{0})F_{1}\cdots F_{n}
, we also know that the head variable
v_{m}
cannot be
v_{0}
, for otherwise the result would be unsolvable, while it must be a wfpc.

We let
\vec{G}=(F_{0},G_{1},\cdots,G_{n+1})
. We will only need to specify a couple of
G_{i}
s.
Set
G_{m}=\lambda\vec{p}.\lambda g_{l+1},...,g_{n+1}.\Theta_{g_{n+1}(F_{0}v_{0}F_{1}\cdots F_{n})}
,
G_{n+1}y=\Theta(\lambda gy.g(y\vec{F}))=G_{n+1}(y\vec{F})
.

\displaystyle Y\vec{G}

\displaystyle=G_{0}(Y^{\prime}G_{0})G_{1}\cdots G_{n+1}

\displaystyle=F_{0}(Y^{\prime}F_{0})G_{1}\cdots G_{n+1}

\displaystyle=G_{m}\vec{P}[v_{0}:=Y^{\prime}F_{0}][v_{i}:=G_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq l}G_{l+1}\cdots G_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{G_{n+1}(F_{0}(Y^{\prime}F_{0})F_{1}\cdots F_{n})}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{G_{n+1}(YF_{0}\cdots F_{n})}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{G_{n+1}(Y\vec{F})}

(
\star
)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{G_{n+1}(Y\vec{F}\vec{F})}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{G_{n+1}((Y\vec{F})\vec{F})}

\displaystyle=(Y\vec{F})\vec{G}\quad\textrm{by ($\star$), with $Y:=Y\vec{F}$}

Our final observation is a corollary to one of the first ones.

Proposition 24
.

The monoid
\mathcal{G}
is
zerosum-free
: If
\vec{F}\vec{G}\simeq()
, then
\vec{F}\simeq()\simeq\vec{G}
.

Proof 6.5
.

Suppose
\vec{F}\vec{G}\simeq()
. Then, considered as endofunctions on
\mathsf{WFPC}/{=_{\beta}}
,
\vec{G}
acts as a left inverse of
\vec{F}
, making
\vec{F}
a split mono (modulo beta).
But we have seen in Proposition
5
that no wfgv is injective, so no wfgv can be monic.
Specifically, take
Y\neq Y^{\prime}
such that
Y\vec{F}=Y^{\prime}\vec{F}
.
Since
()\simeq\vec{F}\vec{G}
, we have
Y=Y\vec{F}\vec{G}=Y^{\prime}\vec{F}\vec{G}=Y^{\prime}
, a contradiction.

7.
Concluding remarks

In this paper,we have broached the topic of abstract fpc generators. Our first investigations revealed that these operators naturally fall into a few robust classes. We established elementary relationships between these classes.

What becomes clear from our investigations is that there is yet much to be uncovered about the structure of fixed point combinators. Some of the possible future research directions include the following.

(1)

The most pressing issue is the status of Conjecture
8
. All the evidence available points to this conjecture being true, yet current techniques in untyped lambda calculus decidedly come up short in settling the question. However it will be decided, the insights to be gathered from the new approaches will greatly deepen our understanding of lambda terms.

(2)

Of course, one could take the next step and ask whether the converse to the first claim in Proposition
13
is also valid. Considering how difficult the former question is, this one will likely remain out of reach for the foreseeable future.

(3)

What else can be said about the structure of the monoid
\mathcal{G}
? Is the submonoid of accretive generators freely generated by the prime generators, as Conjecture
21
asserts? What about the (weakly) compact generators? How can their compositional structure be characterized?

(4)

Since the monoid of (w)fgvs naturally acts on the set of (w)fpcs, how much of the structure of fpcs is captured by this monoidal action? Does every fpc have a representation in terms of the prime elements of the monoid — again, modulo extensional equality, and the ideal of compact generators?

(5)

Finally, while not directly relevant to the earier discussion, an answer to the following question could also shed light on recursion-theoretic properties of FPCs:

Let
\mathtt{Y}
be Curry’s simplest fpc. Is
\{{\#M\mid M=\mathtt{Y}}\}
a decidable subset of
\mathsf{FPC}
?
Specifically, does there exist a term
\Delta_{\mathtt{Y}}
satisfying, for all
closed

Y\in\mathsf{FPC}
, the following:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathtt{Y}}\ulcorner Y\urcorner xy=\begin{cases}x&\mathtt{Y}=_{\beta}Y\\
y&\mathtt{Y}\neq_{\beta}Y\end{cases}

Notice that Scott’s theorem does not apply here because
\mathsf{FPC}
is not all of
\Lambda
, but is only a computably enumerable subset of it.
\Delta_{\mathtt{Y}}
is allowed to diverge outside of this set.

The recent paper
[
12
]
proposes another approach to Statman’s conjecture based on simple types. We note that the generalization of the conjecture stated there is consistent with ours, since every simply-typed generator is accretive thanks to strong normalization of (
\mathtt{Y}
-free) typed terms.

Convergence rates of a discrete feedback control arising in mean-field linear quadratic optimal control problems Thanks: This work is supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (11801467).
Yanqing Wang

Abstract
In this work, we propose a feedback control based temporal discretization for linear quadratic optimal control problems (LQ problems) governed by controlled mean-field stochastic differential equations. We firstly decompose the original problem into two problems: a stochastic LQ problem and a deterministic one. Secondly, we discretize both LQ problems one after another relying on Riccati equations and control’s feedback representations. Then, we prove the convergence rates for the proposed discretization and present an effective algorithm. Finally, a numerical example is provided to support the theoretical finding.



Keywords:
Convergence rate, mean-field linear quadratic optimal control problem, closed–loop optimal control strategy,
Riccati equation

Introduction

The present work is concerned with the numerical scheme for the linear quadratic optimal control problems
(LQ problems, for short) governed
by controlled mean-field stochastic differential equations (MF-SDEs, for short), which are special stochastic optimal
control problems.

In 1938, the idea of mean field interaction in mathematical physics was proposed
by Vlasov. From 1950s, a kind of MF-SDEs, named by McKean-Vlasov SDEs, was proposed and then systematically studied. Up to now, theory of MF-SDEs has significant impact in several areas such as physics, financial mathematics, stochastic optimal control, social interactions.

As three well-recognized fundamental results of stochastic optimal control theory, Pontryagin-type maximum principle
(MP, for short) (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
26
]
), Bellman dynamic programming principle (DPP, for short) (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
12
,
27
]
) and LQ theory (see, e.g.,
[
22
,
23
]
) have been established in the past
half century.
For the stochastic optimal control problems subject to MF-SDEs, there still exists some works; see, e.g.,

[
1
,
11
,
3
,
25
,
33
,
21
,
24
]
.

However, general stochastic optimal control problems, except for some special case
such as one-dimensional state equation,
can not admit explicitly closed form solutions. Therefore, effective numerical schemes for these problems are necessary,
and attract some attentions.
Up to now, there are two widely applied and popular approaches to numerically solve stochastic optimal control problems:
DPP based approach and MP based one.
The first approach relies on DPP and Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman
(HJB, for short) equation. By establishing the bridge between the original problem and an HJB equation,
then numerically solving the HJB equation, one can obtain an approximation of the optimal control (see, e.g.,

[
16
,
17
,
6
]
). However, due to the nonlinearity of the HJB equation,
this approach is computational expensive.
For the second approach,
thanks to MP, the optimal pair satisfies a coupled
forward-backward stochastic differential equation (FBSDE, for short).
The difficulty and complexity of the second approach stem from the fact that this equation is coupled and the solution to
this equation should be adapted. To decouple this FBSDE,
a (stochastic) gradient descent algorithm can be adopted (see, e.g.,
[
14
,
4
,
5
,
28
,
29
]
). Besides,
gradient projection methods for stochastic optimal control problems with deterministic control variables are also
proposed; see
[
13
,
19
]
.
To guarantee the solution’s adaptivity, conditional expectations are introduced (see, e.g.,
[
35
,
8
]
). Although there exists numerous works to simulate conditional expectations (see, e.g.,
[
18
,
8
,
20
,
30
,
10
]
), the study of computation on conditional expectations is
far from mature till now.
From this perspective, the computational cost of MP based approach is high.

To reduce the computation cost, recently based on LQ theory, Riccati based schemes for LQ problems subject to
controlled SDEs are proposed in
[
32
]
. Avoiding solving coupled equations and
simulating conditional expectations,
these schemes can greatly save computation time.

In this work, adopting LQ theory of
MF-SDEs and Riccati equations, we aim at proposing an efficient algorithm with convergence rates to
solve LQ problems governed by MF-SDEs, which will be called MF-LQ problems. To be specific,
Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
be a complete filtered probability space on which a
standard one-dimensional Brownian motion
W=\{W(t):\,t\geq 0\}
is defined, where
\mathbb{F}=\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the natural filtration of
W
augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
{\cal F}
.
Consider the following cost functional

\displaystyle{\cal J}(u(\cdot))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\big(\langle Qx(t),x(t)\rangle+\langle\bar{Q}\mathbb{E}[x(t)],\mathbb{E}[x(t)]\rangle+\langle Ru(t),u(t)\rangle+\langle\bar{R}\mathbb{E}[u(t)],\mathbb{E}[u(t)]\rangle\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\langle Gx(T),x(T)\rangle+\langle\bar{G}\mathbb{E}[x(T)],\mathbb{E}[x(T)]\rangle\Big]

subject to a controlled MF-SDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\,\mathrm{d}x(t)=\left(Ax(t)+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}[x(t)]+Bu(t)+\bar{B}\mathbb{E}[u(t)]\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left(Cx(t)+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}[x(t)]+Du(t)+\bar{D}\mathbb{E}[u(t)]\right)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\quad&\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
&x(0)=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
A,\,\bar{A},\,C,\,\bar{C},\,Q,\,\bar{Q},\,G,\,\bar{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
B,\,\bar{B},\,D,\,\bar{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
,
R,\,\bar{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
, and
u(\cdot)
is the control variable. The space of admissible controls for (
1.1
) is

\displaystyle L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})\triangleq\Big\{\varphi:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\,\big|\,\varphi(\cdot)\hbox{ is $\mathbb{F}$-adapted and }\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\|\varphi(t)\|^{2}]\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\Big\}\,.

The considered MF-LQ problem can be stated as follows.

Problem (MF-LQ)
. For given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, find an optimal control
u^{*}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that

\displaystyle{\cal J}(u^{*}(\cdot))=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}{\cal J}(u(\cdot))\,.

Our goal is to numerically approximate the optimal stochastic control

u^{*}(\cdot)
of Problem (MF-LQ).

The main strategy for discretization of Problem (MF-LQ) is as
follows: Firstly, thanks to the optimal control’s state feedback form and two corresponding differential Riccati equations,
we introduce a stochastic LQ problem, Problem (LQ)
1,t
, related to the first Riccati equation, and
a discrete stochastic LQ problem, Problem (LQ)
{}^{1,t}_{\tau}
, to approximate Problem (LQ)
1,t
;
Secondly, relying on the solution to the first aforementioned differential Riccati equation, we introduce a deterministic LQ problem,
Problem (LQ)
2,t
, and its temporal discretization, Problem (LQ)
{}_{\tau}^{2,t}
, whose related difference Riccati equation is proved
to converge to the solution of the second aforementioned differential Riccati equation;
Thirdly, by inverting the above two steps and adopting the optimal controls’ state feedback representations of the above
four auxiliary LQ problems, we can propose a discretization for Problem (MF-LQ), prove the convergence of our proposed discretization and derive the rates of convergence. This is one contribution of this work.

As an application of our scheme, we actually can propose efficient and accurate algorithms with rates to numerically solve differential Riccati equations
and a family of coupled mean-field forward-backward stochastic differential equations.
This is another contribution of this work.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce some notations, review
results on solvability of Problem (MF-LQ), present temporal discretization of stochastic LQ problems and their corresponding
Riccati equations, and state the convergence with rates for aforementioned discretization of Riccati equation which is
related to the optimal control’s state feedback representation.
In Section
3
,
by virtue of closed–loop optimal control strategy, we
prove the convergence rates of temporal discretization for Problem (MF-LQ), and propose an accurate and efficient algorithm, as well as carry out an
example to verify our theoretical results.

Temporal discretization of parametrized MF-LQ problems

The goal of this section is to present a discretization for Problem (MF-LQ), deduce the convergence rate of this

Before proving the main results, we need make some preparations.

Problem (LQ)
2,t
. For given

&x(t)=x_{t}\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\widetilde{Q}(\cdot)=\widehat{Q}+\widehat{C}^{\top}P(\cdot)\widehat{C}-\widehat{C}^{\top}P(\cdot)\widehat{D}\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(\cdot)\widehat{D}^{\top}P(\cdot)\widehat{C}\,,\\
&\widetilde{R}(\cdot)=\Sigma_{1}(\cdot)\,,\\
&\widetilde{G}=\widehat{G}\,,\\
&\widetilde{A}(\cdot)=\widehat{A}-\widehat{B}\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(\cdot)\widehat{D}^{\top}P(\cdot)\widehat{C}\,,\\

(3.2)

Lemma 3.1
.

(i)
For any

\widetilde{Q}(\cdot)

and
\widetilde{R}(\cdot)

(ii)
For any

\widetilde{Q}_{k}
is positive semi-definite and
\widetilde{R}_{k}
is uniformly positive definite, where

\widetilde{Q}_{\cdot}

Proof.

We only prove assertion (i), and (ii) can be deduced in the same vein.

By Lemma
2.3
, we know that
P(\cdot)
is positive semi-definite, and then

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}(t)=\widehat{R}+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{D}\geq\widehat{R}>0\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,

which is the second assertion.

To prove the first assertion, we need the following claim: For any
K\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m},R_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}

satisfying
R_{0}>0
, it holds that

\|K(R_{0}+K^{\top}K)^{-1}K^{\top}\|\leq 1\,.

(3.5)

Indeed, by setting
K_{0}=KR_{0}^{-1/2}
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle K(R_{0}+K^{\top}K)^{-1}K^{\top}=K_{0}(I+K_{0}^{\top}K_{0})^{-1}K_{0}^{\top}\,.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
n\leq m
. By singular value decomposition, it follows that

\displaystyle K_{0}=U\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda&0\end{pmatrix}V^{\top}\,,

where
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,

are orthogonal matrices, and

\Lambda=\mbox{diag}\{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\|K(R_{0}+K^{\top}K)^{-1}K^{\top}\|

\displaystyle=\Big\|\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda&0\end{pmatrix}\Big(I_{m}+\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda&0\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda&0\end{pmatrix}\Big)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda&0\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\Big\|

\displaystyle=\|\Lambda(I_{n}+\Lambda^{2})^{-1}\Lambda\|

\displaystyle=\max_{i=1,2,\cdots,n}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{1+\lambda_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 1\,,

that prove (
3.5

For any
t\in[0,T]
, by taking

in (

\displaystyle\left\|\big(\sqrt{P(t)}D\big)\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\big(\sqrt{P(t)}D\big)^{\top}\right\|\leq 1\,.

Subsequently, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\big[P(t)-P(t)\widehat{D}\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\big]x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\|\sqrt{P(t)}x\right\|^{2}-\left\langle\big[\big(\sqrt{P(t)}\widehat{D}\big)\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\big(\sqrt{P(t)}\widehat{D}\big)^{\top}\big]\sqrt{P(t)}x,\sqrt{P(t)}x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0\,.

Finally, by the assumption (A), we arrive at

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}(t)=\widehat{Q}+\widehat{C}^{\top}\big[P(t)-P(t)\widehat{D}\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\big]\widehat{C}\geq\widehat{Q}\geq 0\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,.

That completes the proof.

, then the following sharp estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{k=0,1,\cdots,N}\big[\|\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t_{k})]-\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\|+\|\mathbb{E}[u^{*}(t_{k})]-\mathbb{E}[u_{\tau}(t_{k})]\|\big]\leq{\cal C}\tau\|x_{0}\|\,.

(3.16)

Proof.

The proof is long, and we divide it into two steps.

(1).
By Lemma
2.3
, we take the optimal control’s state feedback form (
2.6
) for Problem (MF-LQ), and then
\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(\cdot)]

satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]=\big[\widehat{A}-\widehat{B}\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)\big]\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
&\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(0)]=x_{0}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

For system (
2.8
), we use the control’s state feedback form (
3.13
) which may be not optimal, and
derive the following difference equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k+1})]=\big[I_{n}+\widehat{A}\tau-\tau\widehat{B}(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\big]\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\qquad k=0,1,\cdots,N-1\,,\\
&\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(0)]=x_{0}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

It is easy to find that

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\max_{k=0,1,\cdots,N-1}\Big[\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\left\|\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t_{k})]\right\|+\|\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k+1})]-\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\|\Big]\leq{\cal C}\tau\,,\\
\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]\|+\max_{k=0,1,\cdots,N}\|\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\|\leq{\cal C}\,.\end{array}

(3.17)

By setting
\check{e}_{k}=\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t_{k})]-\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]
, we can arrive at

\displaystyle\check{e}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\check{e}_{k}+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\Big\{\big[\widehat{A}-\widehat{B}\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)\big]\big[\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t_{k})]\big]

(3.18)

\displaystyle+\big[\widehat{A}-\widehat{B}\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)\big]\check{e}_{k}

\displaystyle-\widehat{B}\Big[\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)-(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\Big]\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

Now, applying Lemmas
2.5
,
3.2
and the assumption (A), we can deduce that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\widehat{A}-\widehat{B}\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)\right\|\leq{\cal C}\,.

(3.19)

By (
2.15
)
2
, (
3.7
)
1
, and the fact that
P(\cdot),\,P_{\cdot},\,\Pi_{\cdot}
are positive semi-definite, we have the following estimate: for any
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,

\displaystyle\left\|\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)-(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\big[W_{k}^{2}-\Sigma_{1}(t)\big](W_{k}^{2})^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\Big[\widehat{D}^{\top}\big(P_{k+1}-P(t)\big)\widehat{D}+\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi_{k+1}\widehat{B}\tau\Big](W_{k}^{2})^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}\tau\|\widehat{R}^{-1}\|^{2}\,,

which, together with (
2.15
)
3
in Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
3.7
, leads to

\left\|\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)-(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\right\|\leq{\cal C}\sqrt{\tau}\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,.

(3.20)

Subsequently, by virtue of (
3.17
), (
3.18
), (
3.19
), (
3.20
) and discrete Gronwall’s inequality, we
can derive

\displaystyle\max_{k=0,1,\cdots,N}\|\check{e}_{k}\|\leq{\cal C}\sqrt{\tau}\,.

Next, applying optimal controls’ feedback representations (
2.6
) and (
3.13
), we can conclude that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[u^{*}(t_{k})]-\mathbb{E}[u_{\tau}(t_{k})]\|\leq{\cal C}\sqrt{\tau}\,.

Hence we get (
3.15
)
1
.

When
C=\bar{C}=0
, (
3.20
) turns to

\displaystyle\left\|\Sigma_{1}^{-1}(t)\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)-(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\right\|\leq{\cal C}\tau\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,

which can be used to derive (
3.16
).

(2).
In this step we tend to prove the estimate (
3.15
)
2
.
Based on the feedback representations (
2.6
) and (
3.13
), the state equations
(
2.7
) (with
t
suppresed) and
(
2.8
) satisfy

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\,\mathrm{d}x^{*}=\Big\{Ax^{*}-B\Big[\Sigma_{0}^{-1}\big(B^{\top}P+D^{\top}PC\big)\big(x^{*}-\mathbb{E}[x^{*}]\big)+\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi+\widehat{D}^{\top}P\widehat{C}\big)\mathbb{E}[x^{*}]\Big]\\
&\qquad\qquad+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}[x^{*}]+\bar{B}\mathbb{E}[u^{*}]\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad\qquad+\Big\{Cx^{*}-D\Big[\Sigma_{0}^{-1}\big(B^{\top}P+D^{\top}PC\big)\big(x^{*}-\mathbb{E}[x^{*}]\big)+\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi+\widehat{D}^{\top}P\widehat{C}\big)\mathbb{E}[x^{*}]\Big]\\
&\qquad\qquad+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}[x^{*}]+\bar{D}\mathbb{E}[u^{*}]\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
&x^{*}(0)=x_{0}\,,\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &x_{\tau}(t_{k+1})=\big[I_{n}+A\tau\big]x_{\tau}(t_{k})+\tau\bar{A}\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]+\tau\bar{B}\mathbb{E}[u_{\tau}(t_{k})]\\
&\qquad\qquad-\tau B\big[(W_{k}^{1})^{-1}H_{k}^{1}(x_{\tau}(t_{k})-\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})])+(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\big]\\
&\qquad\qquad+\Big[Cx_{\tau}(t_{k})+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]+\bar{D}\mathbb{E}[u_{\tau}(t_{k})]\\
&\qquad\qquad-D\big[(W_{k}^{1})^{-1}H_{k}^{1}(x_{\tau}(t_{k})-\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})])+(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\big]\Big]\Delta_{k+1}W\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall\,k=0,1,\cdots,N-1\,,\\
&x_{\tau}(0)=x_{0}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

Set
\bar{e}_{k}=x^{*}(t_{k})-x_{\tau}(t_{k})
. Then by above two equations, we can see that

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{k+1}-\bar{e}_{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\Big\{A(x^{*}(t)-x_{\tau}(t_{k}))+\bar{A}\big(\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\big)+\bar{B}\big(\mathbb{E}[u^{*}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[u_{\tau}(t_{k})]\big)

\displaystyle\quad-B\Big[\Sigma_{0}^{-1}\big(B^{\top}P(t)+D^{\top}P(t)C\big)\big(x^{*}(t)-\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]\big)-(W_{k}^{1})^{-1}H_{k}^{1}(x_{\tau}(t_{k})-\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})])\Big]

\displaystyle\quad-B\Big[\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]-(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\Big]\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\Big\{C(x^{*}(t)-x_{\tau}(t_{k}))+\bar{C}\big(\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\big)+\bar{D}\big(\mathbb{E}[u^{*}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[u_{\tau}(t_{k})]\big)

\displaystyle\quad-D\Big[\Sigma_{0}^{-1}\big(B^{\top}P(t)+D^{\top}P(t)C\big)\big(x^{*}(t)-\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]\big)-(W_{k}^{1})^{-1}H_{k}^{1}(x_{\tau}(t_{k})-\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})])\Big]

\displaystyle\quad-D\Big[\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]-(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}(t_{k})]\Big]\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}W(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}I(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}J(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\,.

(3.21)

By multiplying
\bar{e}_{k+1}
, and then taking conditional expectation on both sides of (
3.21
), we can find that

\mbox{left side}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{e}_{k+1}\|^{2}-\|\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\bar{e}_{k+1}-\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}\big]\,.

(3.22)

Now we estimate the derived terms on the right side of (
3.21
).

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\langle A(x^{*}(t)-x_{\tau}(t_{k})),\bar{e}_{k+1}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\langle A(x^{*}(t)-x^{*}(t_{k}))+A\bar{e}_{k},\bar{e}_{k+1}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|A\|}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\|x^{*}(t)-x^{*}(t_{k})\|^{2}+\|\bar{e}_{k+1}\|^{2}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\|A\|}{2}\tau\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\bar{e}_{k+1}\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}\tau^{2}+{\cal C}\tau\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\bar{e}_{k+1}\|^{2}\big]\,.

Here we apply the fact that
\mathbb{E}[\|x^{*}(t)-x^{*}(s)\|^{2}]\leq{\cal C}|t-s|
. In the same vein, we can estimate other Lebesgue integrals and deduce that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\langle I(t),\bar{e}_{k+1}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]\leq{\cal C}\tau^{2}+{\cal C}\tau\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\bar{e}_{k+1}\|^{2}\big]\,.

(3.23)

For the Itô integrals, following the procedure in Step (1), we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}D\Big[\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]-(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}^{*}(t_{k})]\Big]\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\bar{e}_{k+1}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}D\Big[\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]-(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}^{*}(t_{k})]\Big]\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\bar{e}_{k+1}-\bar{e}_{k}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{e}_{k+1}-\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\Big\|D\Big[\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\big(\widehat{B}^{\top}\Pi(t)+\widehat{D}^{\top}P(t)\widehat{C}\big)\mathbb{E}[x^{*}(t)]-(W_{k}^{2})^{-1}H_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}[x_{\tau}^{*}(t_{k})]\Big]\Big\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{e}_{k+1}-\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}\big]+{\cal C}\tau^{2}+{\cal C}\tau\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}]\,.

Also, by the same trick, we can conclude that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\langle J(t),\bar{e}_{k+1}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\Big]\leq\frac{3}{8}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{e}_{k+1}-\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}\big]+{\cal C}\tau^{2}+{\cal C}\tau\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}]\,.

(3.24)

Finally, by (
3.22
), (
3.23
) and (
3.24
), for sufficiently small
\tau
, we can arrive that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{e}_{k+1}\|^{2}]\leq(1+{\cal C}\tau)\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}]+{\cal C}\tau^{2}\,,

and then by discrete Gronwall’s inequality derive

\displaystyle\max_{k=0,1,\cdots,N}\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{e}_{k}\|^{2}]\leq{\cal C}\tau\,.

Subsequently, by controls’ feedback representations (
2.6
) and (
3.13
), we can derive

\displaystyle\max_{k=0,1,\cdots,N}\mathbb{E}[\|u^{*}(t_{k})-u_{\tau}(t_{k})\|^{2}]\leq{\cal C}\tau\,.

That completes the proof.

, we can present the following Riccati based algorithm.

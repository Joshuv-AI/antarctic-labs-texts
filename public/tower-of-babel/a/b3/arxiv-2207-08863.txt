Complete general solutions for equilibrium equations of isotropic strain gradient elasticity
Y.O. Solyaev

Abstract
In this paper, we consider isotropic Mindlin-Toupin strain gradient elasticity theory in which the equilibrium equations contain two additional length-scale parameters and have the fourth order. For this theory we developed an extended form of Boussinesq-Galerkin (BG) and Papkovich-Neuber (PN) general solutions. Obtained form of BG solution allows to define the displacement field through the single vector function that obeys the eight-order bi-harmonic/bi-Helmholtz equation. The developed PN form of the solution provides an additive decomposition of the displacement field into the classical and gradient parts that are defined through the standard Papkovich stress functions and modified Helmholtz decomposition, respectively. Relations between different stress functions and completeness theorem for the derived general solutions are established. As an example, it is shown that a previously known fundamental solution within the strain gradient elasticity can be derived by using the developed PN general solution.

In the present paper we derive the general solutions for equilibrium equations of Mindlin-Toupin SGET and prove its completeness in a deductive manner that was suggested by Papkovich

Based on Helmholtz decomposition and analysis of equilibrium equations of SGET we introduce the definition of the modified Galerkin vector that appears to obey the eight-order governing equation and that allows to define the displacement field in the presence of arbitrary bulk force, i.e. we obtaine the extended variant of Boussinesq-Galerkin (BG) solution within SGET.
Generalizing the approach of classical elasticity, we introduce then the relations between the Galerkin vector and Papkovich-Neuber stress functions.
The resulting modified form of PN solution becomes very attractive for applications because it provides a very simple form of the general solution of Mindlin-Toupin SGET with additive decomposition into the classical and gradient parts of the displacement field. The classical part is defined in a standard PN form through the harmonic vector and harmonic function, while the gradient part is simply represented through the modified Helmholtz decomposition. In such a way we prove the correctness of the previously supposed simplified form of PN solution within SGET

Combining both results for BG and PN forms of solution we prove the theorem of their completeness within SGET in a sense of similar proofs developed by Mindlin
[

]
and Gurtin
[
46
]
within classical elasticity. Considered kind of a proof can be formulated according to Truesdell as follows:
”corresponding to any stress field satisfying the given equations there exists at least one suitable choice of the stress functions”

[
47

Hence, to prove the completeness of BG solution we show an explicit representation for the modified Galerkin stress function through a given displacement field that satisfies the equilibrium equation of SGET. Similarly to classical elasticity, this is done by using the analogy between SGET equilibrium equation and relation between Galerkin stress function and the displacement field and also involving established PN representation of solution within SGET. Then, based on the relations between the stress functions of PN and BG solutions we prove completeness of the former. Thus, in the presented proof it is essential to have both representation of solutions in BG and PN forms.

The key point of the presented results is the proposed modified definition of Galerkin stress function and its specific relations to the Papkovich stress functions within SGET that have not been established previously for the best of author’s knowledge. Applications of the obtained results can be related to the wide class of boundary value problems that can be solved analytically in a simple manner by using PN representation for which we show the completeness

Completeness theorem

The main next step is to prove the completeness of the developed solutions. Reformulating the similar classical theorem given by Gurtin
[

Theorem 2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}
be a displacement field that satisfies equilibrium equation (
1

. Then there exists a field
\boldsymbol{W}
that satisfies (

Proof.

Consider the definition for the displacement field through the modified Galerkin stress function within SGET (

This relation can be rewritten in the following equivalent form:

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}(1-\hat{l}_{1}^{\,2}\nabla^{2})\nabla\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{W}-(1-\hat{l}_{2}^{\,2}\nabla^{2})\nabla\times\nabla\times\boldsymbol{W}=-\frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{b}}}{\mu}

where
\hat{\alpha}=1/\alpha

\hat{l}_{2}=l_{1}

The form of equation (

\boldsymbol{W}

\boldsymbol{u}
(

\boldsymbol{W}
that satisfies (

\hat{\boldsymbol{b}}
. This can be done by using the derived form of Papkovich-Neuber solution of SGET (

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{W}=\hat{\boldsymbol{B}}_{c}-\frac{1}{4(1-\hat{\nu})}\nabla(\boldsymbol{r}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{B}}_{c}+\hat{\varphi}_{c})+\hat{\boldsymbol{B}}_{g}+\hat{l}_{1}^{\,2}\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{g}

in which the stress functions can be defined based on (

), (

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{B}}_{c}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}\ast\boldsymbol{u},\qquad\hat{\varphi}_{c}=-\mathcal{N}\ast(\boldsymbol{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}+2\hat{l}_{1}^{\,2}(1-2\hat{\nu})\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}),

(54)

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{B}}_{g}

\displaystyle=\nabla\times(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{c}+\hat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{g}),\quad\hat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{c}=-\hat{l}^{\,2}_{2}\,\mathcal{N}\ast(\nabla\times\boldsymbol{u}),\quad\hat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{g}=-\hat{l}^{\,2}_{2}\,\mathcal{H}_{2}\ast(\nabla\times\boldsymbol{u})

\displaystyle=\hat{l}_{1}^{\,2}\,\frac{1-2\hat{\nu}}{2(1-\hat{\nu})}\mathcal{H}_{1}\ast\left(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}\right)

and

)

Thus, we prove that there exist a vector field

6
Fundamental solution

Derivation of the fundamental solution based on PN general solution within the classical elasticity can be found, e.g. in
[
9
,
46
]
. Within SGET similar result can be obtained by using developed form of PN solution (
49
), (
50
). Thus, let us consider the concentrated body force
\boldsymbol{Q}
applied at the origin of coordinate system:

\boldsymbol{b}=\boldsymbol{Q}\delta(\boldsymbol{r})

(65)

where
\delta(\boldsymbol{r})
is Dirac delta function.

Based on (
49
) the fundamental (Kelvin) solution of SGET that corresponds to the body force (
65
) can be presented as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{c}-\frac{1}{4(1-\nu)}\nabla(\boldsymbol{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{c}+\varphi^{*}_{c})+\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}+l_{1}^{2}\nabla\varphi^{*}_{g}

(66)

where the stress functions
\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{c}
,
\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}
,
\varphi^{*}_{c}
,
\varphi^{*}_{g}
are the particular solutions of the following equations

\nabla^{2}\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{c}=-\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\mu}\delta(\boldsymbol{r}),

(67)

\nabla^{2}\varphi^{*}_{c}=\frac{\boldsymbol{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{Q}}{\mu}\delta(\boldsymbol{r})+2l_{1}^{2}(1-2\nu)\frac{\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{Q}\delta(\boldsymbol{r}))}{\mu},

(68)

(1-l_{2}^{2}\nabla^{2})\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}=-l_{2}^{2}\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\mu}\delta(\boldsymbol{r}),\qquad\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}=0,

(69)

(1-l_{1}^{2}\nabla^{2})\varphi^{*}_{g}=-l_{1}^{2}\,\frac{1-2\nu}{2(1-\nu)}\,\frac{\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{Q}\delta(\boldsymbol{r}))}{\mu}

(70)

The remaining task is to found the particular solutions of equations (
67
)-(
70
). Solution for vector stress function
\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{c}
(
67
) is similar to classical elasticity and according to potential theory it is given by
[
46
]
:

\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{c}=\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{4\pi\mu r},

(71)

where
r=\lvert\boldsymbol{r}\rvert
.

Scalar stress function
\varphi^{*}_{c}
in classical elasticity vanishes since it is valid that:

\nabla^{2}\varphi^{*}_{c}=\frac{\boldsymbol{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{Q}}{\mu}\delta(\boldsymbol{r})\quad\implies\quad\varphi^{*}_{c}\equiv 0

(72)

therefore, equation (
68
) can be defined in a simpler form as

\nabla^{2}\varphi^{*}_{c}=2l_{1}^{2}(1-2\nu)\frac{\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{Q}\delta(\boldsymbol{r}))}{\mu},

(73)

Particular solution to this equation can be presented in the following form:

\varphi^{*}_{c}=-l_{1}^{2}\frac{1-2\nu}{2\pi\mu}\,\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)

(74)

which follows from formula for the particular solutions of Poisson equation (
14
) and the identity that can be proven based on divergence theorem:

\int\limits_{D}f(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{\xi})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\cdot(\boldsymbol{Q}\delta(\boldsymbol{\xi}))dv_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}=\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{r}}\,f(\boldsymbol{r})

(75)

where
f(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{\xi})
is an arbitrary function;
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is integration variable;
\boldsymbol{r}
is radial distance.

Solution for the gradient vector stress function
\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}
(
69
) can be found in several steps. At first, we neglect the requirement that
\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}
is the divergence-free field and find the particular solution
\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}^{*}_{g}
to the equation:

(1-l_{2}^{2}\nabla^{2})\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}^{*}_{g}=-l_{2}^{2}\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\mu}\delta(\boldsymbol{r})\quad\implies\quad\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}^{*}_{g}=-\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{4\pi\mu}\frac{e^{-r/l_{2}}}{r}

(76)

Then we should subtract the potential part of the obtained solution
\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}^{*}_{g}
to find the field
\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}
, which will satisfy
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}=0
. Potential part of
\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}^{*}_{g}
can be found by using Helmholtz decomposition and representation similar to (
41
). As the result, we obtain:

\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}^{*}_{g}-\nabla\Psi^{*}

(77)

in which the potential
\Psi^{*}
is defined according to relation (see (
41
))

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}^{*}_{g}=\nabla^{2}\Psi^{*}

(78)

Then, combining equation for
\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}^{*}_{g}
(
76
) and relation (
78
) and using (
14
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(l_{2}^{2}\nabla^{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}^{*}_{g}-l_{2}^{2}\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\mu}\delta(\boldsymbol{r})\right)=\nabla^{2}\Psi^{*}

(79)

\displaystyle\implies\Psi^{*}=\frac{l_{2}^{2}}{4\pi\mu}\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{1-e^{-r/l_{2}}}{r}\right)

Substituting (
76
), (
79
) into (
77
) we find the desirable solenoidal field
\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}
in the following form:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{B}^{*}_{g}=-\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{4\pi\mu}\frac{e^{-r/l_{2}}}{r}-\frac{l_{2}^{2}}{4\pi\mu}\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla\nabla\left(\frac{1-e^{-r/l_{2}}}{r}\right)

(80)

Solution for the gradient scalar stress function
\varphi^{*}_{g}
(
70
) is given by:

\displaystyle(1-l_{1}^{2}\nabla^{2})\varphi^{*}_{g}=-l_{1}^{2}\,\frac{1-2\nu}{2(1-\nu)}\,\frac{\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{Q}\delta(\boldsymbol{r}))}{\mu}

(81)

\displaystyle\implies\quad\varphi^{*}_{g}=-\frac{1-2\nu}{8\pi(1-\nu)\mu}\,\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{e^{-r/l_{1}}}{r}\right)

Substitution of relations (
71
), (
74
), (
80
), (
81
) into the representation for the displacement field (
66
) provides us

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{4\pi\mu r}-\frac{1}{16\pi\mu(1-\nu)}\nabla\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{Q}}{r}\right)

(82)

\displaystyle+l_{1}^{2}\frac{1-2\nu}{8\pi(1-\nu)\mu}\,\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla\nabla\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{4\pi\mu}\frac{e^{-r/l_{2}}}{r}-\frac{l_{2}^{2}}{4\pi\mu}\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla\nabla\left(\frac{1-e^{-r/l_{2}}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle-l_{1}^{2}\frac{1-2\nu}{8\pi(1-\nu)\mu}\,\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla\nabla\left(\frac{e^{-r/l_{1}}}{r}\right)

Finally, after some standard simplifications from (

) we obtain the form of fundamental solution of SGET that exactly coincides to those one presented previously, e.g. in
[
59
]
:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi\mu}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{r}-\frac{1}{2(1-\nu)}\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla\nabla r\right)

(83)

\displaystyle+l_{1}^{2}\frac{1-2\nu}{8\pi(1-\nu)\mu}\,\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla\nabla\left(\frac{1-e^{-r/l_{1}}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\boldsymbol{Q}}{4\pi\mu}\frac{e^{-r/l_{2}}}{r}-\frac{l_{2}^{2}}{4\pi\mu}\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\nabla\nabla\left(\frac{1-e^{-r/l_{2}}}{r}\right)

7
Conclusion

We derived and extended form of Boussinesq-Galerkin and Papkovich-Neuber general solution within Mindlin-Toupin strain gradient elasticity. We prove the theorem of completeness for these solutions, i.e. we show their generality
[
47
]
. In the presented proof it is essential to have both forms of general solution (BG and PN) and established relations between different stress functions. The further work should be related to the analysis of the nonuniqueness and the degree of nonuniqueness of the derived general solutions within SGET.

Several issues in science and technology
concern topological algebras and modules
(see e. g.
[
4

25

and the references therein). In this context
a relevant and fascinating question
with an algebraic flavor arise quite
naturally,
wether the algebra or module
under consideration possess the so-called factorization
property. That is whether they are factorization algebras
and factorization modules, or not. More specifically,
consider the pair
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M},\,\cdot\,)
of
algebras and modules, where

\mathcal{B}
is a topological algebra,

\mathcal{M}
is a topological (right) module over
\mathcal{B}

with multiplication
\cdot
between elements in
\mathcal{B}

and
\mathcal{M}
.
(See Sections
1
,
4
or

[
32
]
for notations.)
Is it then true that for arbitrary elements
\phi\in\mathcal{B}

and
f\in\mathcal{M}
, there are elements
\phi_{j}\in\mathcal{B}

and
f_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\phi=\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad f=\phi_{0}\cdot f_{0}?

Evidently, if
\mathcal{B}
is unital, i. e. it contains
the unitary element
\varrho
, then

\phi=\varrho\phi\quad\text{and}\quad f=\varrho\cdot f,

giving the factorization property. On the other hand,
if such unitary element is missing, the question on
factorization property becomes more subtle
and challenging, and less trivial.

As examples, let
p\in[1,\infty)
, and
consider the pairs of Banach algebras and modules

(L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\,\cdot\,),\quad(L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),*),\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathscr{I}_{2},\mathscr{I}_{\infty},\circ).

(0.1)

Here

and
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}
are the
sets of Hilbert-Schmidt and continuous
operators on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, respectively,
and

We also note that Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities
guarantee the Banach algebra and module
properties for the first two pairs in

Then the first pair in (

possess the factorization property because
the multiplicative identity
1
is an element
in the Banach algebra
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

For the second pair in (

),
there is no identity element in the algebra
(L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),*)
.
However, despite this fact we indeed have

(0.2)

when

. Thereby it follows that this pair
possess the factorization property.

Our investigations concerns factorization
properties for certain algebras in the
following sense.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an algebra and

\mathscr{M}
be a left (right) module over

\mathscr{A}
.

(1)

\mathscr{A}
is called a

factorization algebra
or

possess factorization property

if for every
\phi\in\mathscr{A}
,
there are
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathscr{A}
such
that

\phi=\phi_{1}\phi_{2}
.

(2)

\mathscr{M}
is called a

left (right) factorization module
or

possess factorization property

if for every
f\in\mathscr{M}
,
there are
\phi_{0}\in\mathscr{A}

and
f_{0}\in\mathscr{M}
such that

f=\phi_{0}\cdot f_{0}

(
f=f_{0}\cdot\phi_{0}
).

In some examples later on, it is convenient
to have the following abstract result in hands.

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an algebra,
\mathscr{A}_{0}

a factorization subalgebra to
\mathscr{A}
,

G
be an ordered semi-group with ordering

\prec
and
let
\{\mathscr{A}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
be a family
of subalgebras to
\mathscr{A}
such that
the following is true:

•

if
g_{1}\prec g_{2}
, then
\mathscr{A}_{g_{2}}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{g_{1}}
;

•

if
g\in G
and
m,n\in\mathbf{N}

satisfy
m<n
, then
g^{m}\prec g^{n}
;

•

if
g_{0}\in G
is fixed, then

\bigcap_{n\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}}\mathscr{A}_{g_{0}^{n}}=\bigcap_{g\in G}\mathscr{A}_{g}.

(1.12)

Then the following conditions are
equivalent:

(1)

\mathscr{A}_{0}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{g_{0}}

for some
g_{0}\in G
;

(2)

\mathscr{A}_{0}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{g}
for every

g\in G
.

Proof.

Evidently, (2) implies (1). Therefore
assume that (1) holds, and let

\phi\in\mathscr{A}
be arbitrary.
Also let
n\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
be arbitrary.
Since
\mathscr{A}
is a factorization algebra,
there are
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}\in\mathscr{A}_{0}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{g_{0}}
such that

\phi=\phi_{1}\cdots\phi_{n}\in\mathscr{A}_{g_{0}}\cdots\mathscr{A}_{g_{0}}=\mathscr{A}_{g_{0}^{n}}.

Hence
\phi\in\mathscr{A}_{g_{0}^{n}}
for every

n\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
. By
(
1.12
)
it follows that
\phi\in\mathscr{A}_{g}
for
every
g\in G
. Since
\phi\in\mathscr{A}_{0}

was arbitrarily chosen it follows that

\mathscr{A}_{0}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{g}
for every

g\in G
, and the result follows.
∎

1.2.
Weight functions

A
weight
or
weight function

\omega
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
is a
positive function such that
\omega,1/\omega\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Let
\omega
and
v
be weights on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
Then
\omega
is called
v
-moderate
or
moderate
,
if

\omega(x+y)\lesssim\omega(x)v(y),\quad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(1.13)

Here
f(\theta)\lesssim g(\theta)
means
that
f(\theta)\leq cg(\theta)
for some
constant
c>0
which is independent of
\theta

in the domain of
f
and
g
. We also write

f(\theta)\asymp g(\theta)
when
f(\theta)\lesssim g(\theta)\lesssim f(\theta)
.

The function
v
is called
submultiplicative
, if
it is even and (
1.13
) holds for
\omega=v
. We notice that
(
1.13
) implies that if
v
is submultiplicative on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, then
there is a constant
c>0
such that

v(x)\geq c
when
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

We let
\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
be the set of all moderate weights on

\mathbf{R}^{d}
. We recall that if
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, then

\omega
is moderated by
v(x)=e^{r|x|}
, provided the constant

r\geq 0
is chosen large enough. A combination of this fact
with (
1.13
) implies that

e^{-r|x|}\lesssim\omega(x)\lesssim e^{r|x|},\quad\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),

(1.14)

where
r
depends on
\omega
. (See
[
28
]
.)

1.3.
A Gelfand-Shilov space

In several parts of our investigations it is suitable to
perform the discussions in the framework of
the Gelfand-Shilov space
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

of Beurling type, which consists of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

such that

\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbf{N}}^{d}}\left(\frac{\|x^{\alpha}D^{\beta}f\|_{L^{\infty}}}{h^{|\alpha+\beta|}\alpha!\beta!}\right)

(1.15)

is finite for every
h>0
(cf.

[
35
]
).
It follows that
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

is a Fréchet space under the
semi-norms in
(
1.15
). The (strong)
dual of
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, i. e.
the (Gelfand-Shilov) distribution
space of
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
is denoted by
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
It follows that the duality between

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and

\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is given by a unique
extension
of the
L^{2}
scalar product

(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{L^{2}}
on

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\times\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. We have

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subsetneq\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subsetneq L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subsetneq\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subsetneq\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

with continuous and dense embeddings.

The spaces
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

possess several convenient properties and
characterizations. If
\mathscr{F}
denotes the
Fourier transform

(\mathscr{F}f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\equiv(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\,dx

when
f\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, then
\mathscr{F}

restricts to homeomorphisms
on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and on
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
The definition of
\mathscr{F}
on
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

extends uniquely to homeomorphisms
on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and on
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
and to an isometric bijection on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
The inverse of
\mathscr{F}
is given by

\mathscr{F}^{-1}=U\circ\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{F}\circ U,\quad\text{when}\quad(Uf)(x)=f(-x),\ f\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

In particular, the Fourier’s inversion formula is given by

f(x)=(\mathscr{F}^{-1}\widehat{f})(x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi,

when
\widehat{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

The following result shows that
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
may in convenient ways be characterized
by growth and decay conditions on their Fourier transforms
and their short-time Fourier transform. Here recall that
the short-time Fourier transform of a
distribution
f\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with respect
to the window function

\phi\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\setminus 0
is defined as

\displaystyle V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\equiv\mathscr{F}(f\cdot\overline{\phi(\,\cdot\,-x)})(\xi),

\displaystyle\quad x,\xi

\displaystyle\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

We note that for
f\in L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
for some

p\in[1,\infty]
, then

\displaystyle V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\equiv(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(y)\overline{\phi(y-x)}e^{-i\langle y,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi,

\displaystyle\quad x,\xi

\displaystyle\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

In some parts of our exposition it
is more convenient
to use the transform

\mathcal{T}_{\phi}f(x,\xi)\equiv\mathscr{F}(f(\,\cdot\,+x)\cdot\overline{\phi})(\xi)=e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}V_{\phi}f(x,\xi),\quad x,\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

instead of the short-time Fourier
transform, because of the simple
convolution relation

(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(f*g))(x,\xi)=\big((\mathcal{T}_{\phi}f)(\,\cdot\,,\xi)*g\big)(x).

(1.16)

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
\phi\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\setminus 0
be
fixed and let
f\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Then the following
is true:

(1)

it holds

|V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)|=|\mathcal{T}_{\phi}f(x,\xi)|\lesssim e^{r(|x|+|\xi|)}

for some
r>0
;

(2)

f\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, if and only if

|V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)|=|\mathcal{T}_{\phi}f(x,\xi)|\lesssim e^{-r(|x|+|\xi|)}

for every
r>0
;

(3)

f\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, if and only if

|f(x)|\lesssim e^{-r|x|}\quad\text{and}\quad|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\lesssim e^{-r|\xi|}

for every
r>0
.

We refer to
[
48
]
for the proof of (1),

[
29
]
for the proof of (2) and

[
9
]
for the proof of (3)
in Proposition
1.12
.

1.4.
Wiener amalgam and modulation spaces

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
,
q\in(0,\infty]
and

\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
Then the Wiener amalgam space

W\!\!L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

consists of all measurable functions
f
on

\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\|f\|_{W\!\!L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}
is
finite, where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W\!\!L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}

\displaystyle\equiv\|a_{f}\|_{\ell^{q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{Z}^{d})},

(1.17)

\displaystyle a_{f}(k)

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{p}(k+Q)},\quad k\in\mathbf{Z}^{d},\ Q=[0,1]^{d}.

The set
W\!\!L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

with quasi-norm (
1.17
)
is a quasi-Banach
space of order
\min(q,1)
. In particular,

W\!\!L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

is a Banach space when
q\geq 1
.
We observe that if
p,q<\infty
, then

C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

are dense in
W\!\!L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
For convenience we set

W\!\!L^{p,q}=W\!\!L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}

when
\omega(x)=1
for every
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
We also recall that

W\!\!L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is often
denoted by

W(L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),L^{q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d}))=W(L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\ell^{q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{Z}^{d})),

in the literature. (See e. g.

[
18
,
22
]
.) We observe that

W\!\!L^{p,p}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

is equal to
L^{p}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
the set of all measurable functions

f
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{(\omega)}}\equiv\|f\cdot\omega\|_{L^{p}}

is finite. Hence the
Wiener amalgam spaces extend the notion on
Lebesgue spaces.

Next we recall the definition of

classical modulation
spaces
(see e. g.
[
16
,
26
]
).
Let
p,q\in(0,\infty]

be fixed, and set

\|F\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})}\equiv\|f_{p}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbf{R}^{d})},\qquad f_{p}(\xi)\equiv\|F(\,\cdot\,,\xi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d})},

when
F
is measurable on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
. Then

L^{p,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
is the quasi-Banach space
with quasi-norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{L^{p,q}}
which
consists of all measurable functions
F

on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
such that
\|F\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})}

is finite. We observe that if
p,q\geq 1
, then

L^{p,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
is a Banach space and

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{L^{p,q}}
is a norm.

Definition 1.13
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty]
,

\phi\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\setminus 0

and let
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.

(1)

The modulation space
M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

consists of all
f\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

such that

\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}\equiv\|\mathcal{T}_{\phi}f\cdot\omega\|_{L^{p,q}}\asymp\|V_{\phi}f\cdot\omega\|_{L^{p,q}}

(1.18)

is finite.

(2)

The modulation space
W^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

consists of all
f\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

such that

\|f\|_{W^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}\equiv\|G\|_{L^{q,p}}<\infty,\quad\text{where}\quad G(\xi,x)=V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)\cdot\omega(x,\xi).

(1.19)

For convenience we set

M^{p}_{(\omega)}=M^{p,p}_{(\omega)}=W^{p,p}_{(\omega)}
.
We also set

M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}=M^{p,q},\quad W^{p,q}_{(\omega)}=W^{p,q}\quad\text{and}\quad M^{p}_{(\omega)}=M^{p},\quad\text{when}\quad\omega=1.

We observe that the modulation spaces
in Definition
1.13

(2) is certain types of Wiener amalgam
spaces (see e. g.
[
16
]
).
The
modulation spaces in Definition

1.13

[
18
]

by Feichtinger, and is often refer as

.

There are several extensions of classical

[

19
,

,

,
38
,

,

,

]
).
We shall consider a broader family of

parameterized by quasi-Banach function
spaces given in the following definition.

Definition 1.14
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a quasi-Banach
space of measurable

\mathbf{R}^{d}
of order

containing

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with continuous embedding,
and let

.

is called a

\mathbf{R}^{d}
,
or

of order

such that the following is true:

(1)

and

,

, and

(1.20)

(2)

if

and

are measurable on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
which
satisfy

and

, then

and

\mathbf{R}^{d}
which is a Banach space is called
a
translation invariant Banach function space on
\mathbf{R}^{d}

or

\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

Definition 1.15

Let
\mathscr{B}
be an invariant

.

(1)

(1.21)

is finite.

(2)

The modulation space
M_{0}(\omega,\mathscr{B})

is the completion of

The spectral resolution of elements in

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

The following proposition describes fundamental links
between Wigner distributions and pseudo-differential

as a continuous
operator from
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to

\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

The result follows by straight-forward
computations. The details are left

Proposition 4.3
.

Let

Recall that

Some properties in pseudo-differential

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Op}_{A}(\mathfrak{a}),

\displaystyle\quad s_{p,A}

\displaystyle=s_{p}^{w},\quad W_{f,g}=W_{f,g}^{A}

(4.19)

\displaystyle K_{\mathfrak{a}}^{w}

\displaystyle=K_{\mathfrak{a},A}

\displaystyle\quad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{a}{\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}\mathfrak{b}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{a}{\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}_{A}\mathfrak{b},\quad\text{when}\quad A={\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot I_{d},

)

For example, beside (

)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}{\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}\mathfrak{b}

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}(\mathfrak{a}{\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}\mathfrak{b})

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}(\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\mathfrak{a})*_{\sigma}(\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\mathfrak{b}),

\displaystyle K_{\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\mathfrak{a}}^{w}

\displaystyle=S\circ K_{\mathfrak{a}}^{w},

\displaystyle\quad(SK)(x,y)

\displaystyle=K(-x,y)

(4.21)

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}W_{f,g}

\displaystyle=W_{\check{f},g},

\displaystyle\quad\check{f}(x)

when
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

are suitable and

4.4.
Convolutions of
Schatten-von Neumann symbols

For convolutions we have the following.

Proposition 4.11
.

Suppose
p,q,r\in(0,\infty]
and

A\in\mathbf{M}(d,\mathbf{R})
satisfy

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1+\frac{1}{r},\qquad p,q,r\geq 1,

or
q\leq p\leq r\leq 1
.
Then the map
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})\mapsto\mathfrak{a}*\mathfrak{b}
from

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\times\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
to

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
is uniquely extendable to
a continuous map from

W\!\!L^{\max(q,1),q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\times s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\quad\text{or}\quad s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\times W\!\!L^{\max(q,1),q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

to
s_{r,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.

Proof.

By (
4.15
) and that

e^{i\langle AD_{\xi},D_{x}\rangle}(\mathfrak{a}*\mathfrak{b})=(e^{i\langle AD_{\xi},D_{x}\rangle}\mathfrak{a})*\mathfrak{b},

(4.25)

we reduce ourself to the Weyl case

A=\frac{1}{2}I_{d}
.
For
p,q,r\geq 1
, the result follows from

[
47
,
56
]
.

We need to consider the case when

q\leq p\leq r\leq 1
. Then the
facts that
s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
and

W\!\!L^{1,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
increase with

p
and
q
, respectively, reduce
ourselves to the case when
p=q=r\leq 1
.

First we assume that
\mathfrak{a}=W_{f,f}^{A}=W_{f,f}

with
f\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and that

0\leq\mathfrak{b}\in W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
Then
\mathfrak{a}
is continuous and real-valued.
Let
Q=[0,1]^{2d}
.
The mean-value theorem gives that
for some
Z_{j}\in Q
,
j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{a}*\mathfrak{b}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\int_{j+Q}\mathfrak{a}(\,\cdot\,-Y)\mathfrak{b}(Y)\,dy\right\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\mathfrak{a}(\,\cdot\,-j-Z_{j})\int_{j+Q}\mathfrak{b}(Y)\,dy\right\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\|\mathfrak{a}(\,\cdot\,-j-Z_{j})\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}\left(\int_{j+Q}\mathfrak{b}(Y)\,dy\right)^{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\|\mathfrak{a}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}\left(\int_{j+Q}\mathfrak{b}(Y)\,dy\right)^{r}=\|\mathfrak{a}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}\|\mathfrak{b}\|_{W\!\!L^{1,r}}^{r},

and the result follows in this case.

Next assume that
\mathfrak{a}=W_{f,f}
as before
but
\mathfrak{b}\in W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
being general.
By splitting up
\mathfrak{b}
in positive and
negative real and imaginary parts

\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{1}-\mathfrak{b}_{2}+i(\mathfrak{b}_{3}-\mathfrak{b}_{4}),

with

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{1}

\displaystyle=\max(\operatorname{Re}(\mathfrak{b}),0),

\displaystyle\quad\mathfrak{b}_{2}

\displaystyle=\max(-\operatorname{Re}(\mathfrak{b}),0),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{3}

\displaystyle=\max(\operatorname{Im}(\mathfrak{b}),0)

\displaystyle\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{b}_{4}

\displaystyle=\max(-\operatorname{Im}(\mathfrak{b}),0),

the first part of the proof gives

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{a}*\mathfrak{b}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathfrak{a}*\mathfrak{b}_{1}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}+\|\mathfrak{a}*\mathfrak{b}_{2}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}+\|\mathfrak{a}*\mathfrak{b}_{3}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}+\|\mathfrak{a}*\mathfrak{b}_{4}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathfrak{a}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}\sum_{k=1}^{4}\|\mathfrak{b}_{k}\|_{W\!\!L^{1,r}}^{r}\leq 4\|\mathfrak{a}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}\|\mathfrak{b}\|_{W\!\!L^{1,r}}^{r},

and the result follows in this case as well.

Next suppose that
\mathfrak{a}=W_{f,g}
with

\|f\|_{L^{2}}=\|g\|_{L^{2}}=1

and that
\mathfrak{b}\in W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
Then

\mathfrak{a}=\frac{1}{4}(\mathfrak{a}_{1}-\mathfrak{a}_{2}+i(\mathfrak{a}_{3}-\mathfrak{a}_{4})),

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{k}

\displaystyle=W_{f+\theta_{k}g,f+\theta_{k}g},

\displaystyle\quad\theta_{1}=1,\ \theta_{2}=-1,\ \theta_{3}=i\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{4}=-i.

We have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{a}_{k}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}

\displaystyle=\|W_{f+\theta_{k}g,f+\theta_{k}g}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}=(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\|f+\theta_{k}g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}(\|f\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{2}})^{2}=4(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}=4\|\mathfrak{a}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}.

By the previous case we get

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{a}*\mathfrak{b}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{-r}\sum_{k=1}^{4}\|\mathfrak{a}_{k}*\mathfrak{b}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{-r}\sum_{k=1}^{4}4\|\mathfrak{a}_{k}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}\|\mathfrak{b}\|_{W\!\!L^{1,r}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq 16\|\mathfrak{a}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}\|\mathfrak{b}\|_{W\!\!L^{1,r}}^{r},

and the result follows in this case as well.

Finally, for general
\mathfrak{a}
given by
(
4.12
) with
p=r
,
we get

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{a}*\mathfrak{b}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}W_{f_{j},g_{j}}*\mathfrak{b}\right\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}^{r}\|W_{f_{j},g_{j}}*\mathfrak{b}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}^{r}\right)\|\mathfrak{b}\|_{W\!\!L^{1,r}}^{r}\asymp\|\mathfrak{a}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}\|\mathfrak{b}\|_{W\!\!L^{1,r}}^{r},

and the result follows.
∎

4.5.
Convolution factorizations of
Schatten-von Neumann symbols

The following result shows that any Schatten-von Neumann
symbol can be factorized through convolutions
into elements in the same class and elements in the
Wiener amalgam space
W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,
for suitable
r\in(0,1]
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Suppose
r\in(0,1]
,
p\in[r,\sharp]

and
A\in\mathbf{M}(d,\mathbf{R})
. Then

W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})*s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})*W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}).

(4.26)

Proof.

By (
4.15
) and
(
4.25
), we reduce ourself to
the Weyl case
A=\frac{1}{2}I_{d}
. In particular
it follows that

W_{f,f}(x,\xi)=W_{f,f}^{A}(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}

when
f\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
x,\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
By the commutativity of the convolution
product it suffices to prove

s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})*W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}).

Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{2d};[0,1])

be such that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\phi(Y)\,dY=1\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{supp}(\phi)\subseteq Q_{M}\equiv[-1,1]^{2d},

and let

\phi_{\varepsilon}(X)=\varepsilon^{-2d}\phi(\varepsilon^{-1}X)

for any
\varepsilon>0
,
X=(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
.
It follows by straight-forward computations
that if
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, then

(W_{f,g}*\phi_{\varepsilon})(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}W_{f_{\varepsilon Y},g_{\varepsilon Y}}(x,\xi)\phi(Y)\,dY,

where

f_{Y}(x)=f(x-y)e^{-i\langle x,\eta\rangle},\quad g_{Y}(x)=g(x-y)e^{-i\langle x,\eta\rangle},\quad Y=(y,\eta)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}.

Hence, if
\mathfrak{a}
is expressed as in (
4.12
), then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}*\phi_{\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{a}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{b}_{\varepsilon}+\mathfrak{c}_{\varepsilon}

(4.27)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{M}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y},g_{j,\varepsilon Y}-g_{j}}\right)\phi(Y)\,dY

(4.28)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{0}},g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{0}}-g_{j}}

(4.29)

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y}-f_{j},g_{j}}\right)\phi(Y)\,dY.

(4.30)

Since
\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{W\!\!L^{1,r}}\leq C

for some constant
C>0
which is
independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
the assertion will follow if we prove

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|\mathfrak{b}_{\varepsilon}\|_{s_{p}^{w}}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|\mathfrak{c}_{\varepsilon}\|_{s_{p}^{w}}=0,

(4.31)

in view of Theorem
1.5
.

We have

\mathfrak{a}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}W_{f_{j},g_{j}}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
with convergence in
s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
. By combining
this with Proposition
4.11
,
it follows that

\left(\mathfrak{a}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}W_{f_{j},g_{j}}\right)*\phi\to 0

as
N\to\infty
, again with convergence in
s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
From these limits we reduce ourself
to the case where we may assume that

\mathfrak{a}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}W_{f_{j},g_{j}},

(4.32)

for some integer
N\geq 0
.

Our aim is to apply the mean-value theorem and Minkowski’s
inequality to reach desired estimates. We then need to
reformulate
\mathfrak{b}_{\varepsilon}
in suitable ways. We have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\mathfrak{b}_{j,\varepsilon},\qquad\mathfrak{b}_{j,\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{b}_{1,j,\varepsilon}+i\mathfrak{b}_{2,j,\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{b}_{3,j,\varepsilon}-i\mathfrak{b}_{4,j,\varepsilon},

(4.33)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{k,j,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{M}}W_{h_{k,j,\varepsilon Y},h_{k,j,\varepsilon Y}}\phi(Y)\,dY,\qquad h_{k,j,Y}=f_{j,Y}+i^{k-1}(g_{j,Y}-g),

(4.34)

k=1,2,3,4
. Since
\phi\geq 0
satisfies

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\phi(Y)\,dY=1

and
W_{h_{k,j,\varepsilon Y},h_{k,j,\varepsilon Y}}

is real-valued and smooth, the mean value theorem gives

\mathfrak{b}_{k,j,\varepsilon}=W_{h_{k,j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}},h_{k,j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}},

for some
Y_{k,j}=Y_{k,j,\varepsilon}\in Q_{M}
.

By rearranging terms we obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{j,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{1,j,\varepsilon}+i\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{2,j,\varepsilon}+\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{3,j,\varepsilon}+i\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{4,j,\varepsilon}

(4.35)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{1,j,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=4W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}},g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j}},

(4.36)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{2,j,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{2,j}},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}+ig_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}}-iW_{g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{2,j}},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}+ig_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}}

\displaystyle+iW_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{2,j}}+ig_{j,\varepsilon Y_{2,j}},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{2,j}}}+W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{2,j}}+ig_{j,\varepsilon Y_{2,j}},g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{2,j}}}

\displaystyle+2\operatorname{Im}\left(W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{2,j}},g_{j}}-iW_{g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{2,j}},g_{j}}\right)

(4.37)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{3,j,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{3,j}},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}}-W_{g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{3,j}},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}}

\displaystyle+W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{3,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{3,j}},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{3,j}}}-W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{3,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{3,j}},g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{3,j}}}

\displaystyle+2\operatorname{Re}\left(W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{3,j}},g_{j}}-W_{g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{3,j}},g_{j}}\right)

(4.38)

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{4,j,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{4,j}},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-ig_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}}-iW_{g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{4,j}},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-ig_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}}

\displaystyle+W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{4,j}}-ig_{j,\varepsilon Y_{4,j}},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{4,j}}}+iW_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{4,j}}-ig_{j,\varepsilon Y_{4,j}},g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{4,j}}}

\displaystyle+2\operatorname{Im}\left(W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{4,j}},g_{j}}-iW_{g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{1,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{4,j}},g_{j}}\right).

(4.39)

Since
\overline{W_{f,g}(x,\xi)}=W_{g,f}(x,\xi)
, giving that

2\operatorname{Re}(W_{f,g})=W_{f,g}+W_{g,f}\quad\text{and}\quad 2\operatorname{Im}(W_{f,g})=i^{-1}(W_{f,g}-W_{g,f}),

it follows that all terms in (
4.36
)–(
4.39
)
are of the forms

\displaystyle W_{h_{1,j},g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-g_{j}},

\displaystyle W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}},h_{2,j}},

\displaystyle W_{g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}},h_{3,j}},

(4.40)

\displaystyle W_{h_{4,j},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}}}

\displaystyle\text{and}

\displaystyle W_{h_{5,j},g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}}},

for some
h_{k,j}\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
k=1,\dots,5
.
Since
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
a straight-forward application of Lebesgue’s theorem
gives

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-g_{j}\|_{L^{2}}=0,

which in turn leads to

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}}\|_{L^{2}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}}\|_{L^{2}}=0.

An application of (
4.14
) now gives

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|W_{f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}},h_{2,j}}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}\\[4.30554pt]
=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\left((2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}}\|_{L^{2}}\|h_{2,j}\|_{L^{2}}\right)=0

for the second expression in (
4.40
).
In the same way we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|W_{h_{1,j},g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-g_{j}}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|W_{g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}},h_{3,j}}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|W_{h_{4,j},f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-f_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}}}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|W_{h_{5,j},g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{k,j}}-g_{j,\varepsilon Y_{m,j}}}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}=0

for the other expressions in (
4.40
).

Since
\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{k,j,\varepsilon}
are linear combinations of
Wigner distributions in (
4.40
),
it now follows from the previous estimates and
the quasi-norm properties for
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{s_{r}^{w}}
that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{k,j,\varepsilon}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}=0,\quad k=1,2,3,4,

which leads to

0\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{j,\varepsilon}\|_{s_{p}^{w}}\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|\mathfrak{b}^{0}_{j,\varepsilon}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}=0.

The first limit in (
4.31
) now follows
from the previous limit and (
4.33
).

By similar arguments, the second limit in
(
4.31
) follows. The details are
left for the reader, and the result follows.
∎

4.6.
Symbol product and twisted convolution
factorizations of Schatten-von Neumann symbols

The following result shows among others that
any Schatten-von Neumann symbol in the
Weyl calculus can be factorized through twisted convolutions
or Weyl products into elements in the same class and
elements in the Wiener amalgam space

W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,
for suitable
r\in(0,1]
.

Theorem 4.13
.

Suppose
p\in(0,\sharp]
and
r\in(0,1]
.
Then

\displaystyle s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

\displaystyle*_{\sigma}W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})*_{\sigma}L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

\displaystyle=s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})*_{\sigma}s_{\sharp}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})*_{\sigma}s_{\infty}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

(4.41)

and

\displaystyle s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

\displaystyle{\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}){\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

\displaystyle=s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}){\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}s_{\sharp}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}){\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}s_{\infty}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}).

(4.42)

Proof.

By (
4.20
) and
(
4.23
), it follows that
(
4.42
) is equivalent to
(
4.41
). Hence it suffices to
prove (
4.41
). By
Proposition
4.10

it follows that

s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})*_{\sigma}s_{\infty}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\subseteq s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}).

Hence it suffices to prove

s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})*_{\sigma}W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

(4.43)

in view of (
4.24
).

Let

\displaystyle\phi(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\psi(x)\psi(\xi),

where

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\quad\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\psi(x)\,dx=1

(4.44)

and set
\phi_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-2d}\phi(\varepsilon^{-1}\,\cdot\,)
.
Since
\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{W\!\!L^{1,r}}\leq C

for some constant
C>0
which is
independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
the result follows from Theorem

1.5
if we prove that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}*_{\sigma}\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}\|_{s_{p}^{w}}=0,

(4.45)

for every
\mathfrak{a}\in s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.

By Proposition
4.9

and (
4.24
) it follows that
the map
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})\mapsto\mathfrak{a}*_{\sigma}\mathfrak{b}

is continuous from
L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\times s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

to
s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
. Hence it suffices to prove
(
4.45
) when
\mathfrak{a}\in s_{0}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,
in view of Proposition
4.4
.
That is, we may assume that
\mathfrak{a}
is given
by (
4.32
) for some
integer
N\geq 1
,

\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\operatorname{ON}_{0}
,

\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\operatorname{ON}_{0}

and
\lambda_{j}\geq\lambda_{j+1}\geq 0
,

j=1,\dots,N-1
.

Let
r=\min(1,p)
. By triangle inequality we get

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\varepsilon}*_{\sigma}\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}\|_{s_{p}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\big(\phi_{\varepsilon}*_{\sigma}W_{f_{j},g_{j}}-W_{f_{j},g_{j}}\big)\right\|_{s_{p}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}^{r}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}*_{\sigma}W_{f_{j},g_{j}}-W_{f_{j},g_{j}}\|_{s_{p}^{w}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}^{r}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}*_{\sigma}W_{f_{j},g_{j}}-W_{f_{j},g_{j}}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}^{r}.

(4.46)

Since

\displaystyle\phi_{\varepsilon}*_{\sigma}W_{f_{j},g_{j}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\phi_{\varepsilon}{\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}(\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}W_{f_{j},g_{j}})

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\phi_{\varepsilon}{\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}W_{\check{f}_{j},g_{j}}=(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}W_{\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\check{f}_{j},g_{j}},

in view of Proposition

4.3
and
(
4.20
), we get

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\varepsilon}*_{\sigma}W_{f_{j},g_{j}}-W_{f_{j},g_{j}}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}

\displaystyle=\|W_{h_{\varepsilon,j},g_{j}}\|_{s_{r}^{w}}

(4.47)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|h_{\varepsilon,j}\|_{L^{2}}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{2}}=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|h_{\varepsilon,j}\|_{L^{2}},

where

h_{\varepsilon,j}=(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\check{f}_{j}-f_{j}.

Hence, in view of (
4.46
)
and (
4.47
), the result will
follow if we prove

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|h_{\varepsilon,j}\|_{L^{2}}=0.

(4.48)

By (
4.44
) we have

\displaystyle h_{\varepsilon,j}(x)

\displaystyle=2^{-d}\varepsilon^{-2d}\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\psi((2\varepsilon)^{-1}(x+y))\psi(\varepsilon^{-1}\xi)f_{j}(-y)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\,dyd\xi-f_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=2^{-d}\varepsilon^{-2d}\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\psi((2\varepsilon)^{-1}y)\psi(\varepsilon^{-1}\xi)f_{j}(x-y)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\,dyd\xi-f_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\psi(y)\psi(\xi)f_{j}(x-2\varepsilon y)e^{i\langle x-2\varepsilon y,\xi\rangle}\,dyd\xi-f_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\psi(y)\psi(\xi)f_{j}(x-2\varepsilon y)e^{i\langle x-2\varepsilon y,\xi\rangle}\,dyd\xi-f_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\psi(y)((2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\widehat{\psi}(\varepsilon x-2\varepsilon^{2}y)f_{j}(x-2\varepsilon y)-f_{j}(x))\,dy,

and Minkowski’s inequality gives

\|h_{\varepsilon,j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\psi(y)|\cdot\|(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\widehat{\psi}(\varepsilon\,\cdot\,-2\varepsilon^{2}y)f_{j}(\,\cdot\,-2\varepsilon y)-f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\,dy.

(4.49)

We have
(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\widehat{\psi}(0)=1

in view of (
4.44
),
which implies

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\|(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\widehat{\psi}(\varepsilon\,\cdot\,-2\varepsilon^{2}y)f_{j}(\,\cdot\,-2\varepsilon y)-f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}=0.

(4.50)

In fact, the latter limit is evident when

f_{j}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and follows
for
f_{j}\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
by density arguments,
using that
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is dense
in
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

Furthermore, since

(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\widehat{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}

we obtain

y\mapsto|\psi(y)|\cdot\|(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\widehat{\psi}(\varepsilon\,\cdot\,-2\varepsilon^{2}y)f_{j}(\,\cdot\,-2\varepsilon y)-f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\\[4.30554pt]
\leq|\psi(y)|(\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}+1)\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

(4.51)

A combination of
(
4.49
)–(
4.51
)
and Lebesgue’s theorem now gives
(
4.48
), and the
result follows.
∎

A combination of (
4.10
)
and Theorem
4.13
gives
the following. The details are left for the reader.

Theorem 4.14
.

Suppose
p\in(0,\sharp]
and
A\in\mathbf{M}(d,\mathbf{R})
.
Then

\displaystyle s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}){\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}_{A}s_{\sharp,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}){\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}_{A}s_{\infty,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{p,A}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}).

(4.52)

Example 4.15
.

Let
r\in(0,1]
. Due to
(
4.24
),
a natural question arise whether

W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
is contained
in
s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
or not,
for some choice of
p\in(0,\infty)
.
Observe that for such
p
we have

s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\subsetneq s_{\sharp}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
. Our results now
give negative answer on this. That is,
we have

W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\nsubseteq\bigcup_{p\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}s_{p}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\qquad\Big(\text{but}\quad W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\subseteq s_{\sharp}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\Big).

(4.53)

In fact, let

\mathscr{A}=s_{\sharp}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\quad\mathscr{A}_{0}=W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{A}_{t}=s_{1/t}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\ t>0,

which are algebras under the twisted
convolution. Now recall that

\mathscr{A}_{0}=W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

is a factorization algebra under
the twisted convolution
and that Hölder’s inequality
holds for the
s_{p}^{w}
classes
in view of Theorem
2.5

and Proposition

4.10
.
Consequently, the hypothesis of
Proposition
1.11
is fulfilled
for
G=(\mathbf{R}_{+},+)
with the usual
ordering. Since

W\!\!L^{1,r}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\nsubseteq L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=s_{2}^{w}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=\mathscr{A}_{1/2},

it now follows from
Proposition
1.11

that (
4.53
) holds.

Appendix A
Factorization properties for
quasi-Banach modules

In this section we extend well-known
factorization properties
for Banach algebras and modules
to quasi-Banach algebras and modules.
Especially we follow the framework in
[
30
, Section 2]

and extend (2.5) Theorem in
[
30
]
in such a way that
we assume that the involved spaces are quasi-Banach
algebras and modules, instead of the more restrictive
Banach algebras and modules.

In the first part we deduce some preparatory results which are

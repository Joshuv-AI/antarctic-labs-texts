Martin van Beek

Abstract
. We determine all reduced saturated fusion systems supported on a finite p p -group of nilpotency class two. As a consequence, we obtain a new proof of Gilman & Gorenstein’s classification of finite simple groups with class two Sylow 2 2 -subgroups.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
20D20; 20D05; 20E42

1.
Introduction

In recent years, much consideration has been given to fusion systems with restricted or small structure. This generally takes one of two paths. The structure of the fusion system itself may be restricted (e.g. by limiting its essential subgroups or the structure of its local subsystems); or the structure of the underlying
p
-group of the fusion system may be limited. In this article, we will take the latter path.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system on a finite
p
-group
S
. By a classical result of Burnside, if
S
is abelian then
S
is
normal
in
\mathcal{F}
. That is,
\mathcal{F}=N_{\mathcal{F}}(S)
and
\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to the
p
-fusion category of a finite
p
-solvable group. With regards to the nilpotency of the underlying
p
-group, one next natural step is to analyze the saturated fusion systems supported on
p
-groups of nilpotency class two, which is the main remit of this article. We wish to indicate that this is not the only next natural step. For instance, fusion systems supported on
p
-groups of maximal nilpotency class have been investigated in
[
GP22
]
.

For some motivation for the fusion systems under analysis, one may consider the non-abelian
p
-groups of order
p^{3}
. Necessarily, these groups have nilpotency class two. If
\mathcal{F}
is a saturated fusion system on a non-abelian
p
-group of order
p^{3}
then
\mathcal{F}
is known. The interesting outcomes here are in the work of Ruiz and Viruel who handled the case when
p
is odd and the underlying
p
-group has exponent
p

[
RV04
]
. That result provides a large number of examples of realizable systems associated to certain finite simple groups, and provides three examples of exotic fusion systems at the prime
7
. In fact, as our
Main Theorem
indicates, these are, in some sense, the only exotic fusion systems supported on a
p
-group of nilpotency class at most two.

Main Theorem
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a saturated fusion system on a finite
p
-group
S
, where
S
has nilpotency class two. Assume that
O_{p}(\mathcal{F})=\{1\}
. Then
O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}_{1}\times\dots\times\mathcal{F}_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
where each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is isomorphic to either:

(i)

a saturated fusion system on a
p
-group isomorphic to a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
\mathrm{PSL}_{3}(p^{a_{i}})
for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{F}_{T}(G)
where
G\cong\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(2^{a_{i}})
and
p=2
for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
; or

(iii)

\mathcal{F}_{T}(G)
where
G\cong(\mathrm{Alt}(6)\times\dots\times\mathrm{Alt}(6)):A
where
A\leq\Omega_{1}(Z(S))
and
p=2
.

The groups arising in (iii) are isomorphic to normal subgroups of
\mathrm{Sym}(6)\times\dots\times\mathrm{Sym}(6)
of index a power of
2
.

To illustrate the difference between finite groups and fusion systems, one can compare this paper with Gilman and Gorenstein’s treatment of the finite simple groups which have a Sylow
2
-subgroup of nilpotency class two
[
GG75
]
. We being by pointing out some parallels with their work. First, both works operate under a “minimal counterexample” hypothesis and so have access to detailed information about substructures in the relevant groups/fusion systems. Next, both works partition the analysis into a
component type
case and a
characteristic
p
case, albeit we use a broader definition of characteristic
p
than the direct translation of Gilman and Gorenstein’s choice for groups. In spite of this, the characteristic
p
analysis for fusion systems is similar in spirit to the finite group analysis.

In contrast to the work of Gilman and Gorenstein, we answer the question for every prime
p
, rather than solely focusing on the case for
p=2
, and consider the larger class of
reduced
fusion systems in place of simple fusion systems. Moreover, our analysis in the component type case is quite different. Gilman and Gorenstein require delicate results concerning balance and signalizer functor theory, whereas our treatment primarily makes use of Aschbacher’s balance argument for fusion systems
[
Asc11
, Theorem 7]
. Aschbacher’s result is far easier than the analogous statement for finite groups. From the perspective of analyzing finite groups locally, this is one of the key reasons for using fusion systems in the first place and is the mantra behind Aschbacher’s programme to reprove large parts of the classification of the finite simple groups. Aschbacher’s programme has made significant progress towards this goal, and while this work will not play a part in that programme (indeed, the fusion systems we considering end up being of characteristic
2
-type), we hope that the analysis within highlights the benefits of working with fusion systems to make statements about finite simple groups. See
[
Asc19
]
for an overview of Aschbacher’s programme. With this in mind, in the final section of this paper, we use our fusion systems result to reprove Gorenstein and Gilman’s result concerning finite simple groups with Sylow
2
-subgroups of nilpotency class two.

Theorem GG
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite simple group such that a Sylow
2
-subgroup
S
of
G
has nilpotency class at most two. Then one of the following holds:

(i)

S
is abelian and
G
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{J}_{1}
,
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
where
q\equiv 3,5\mod 8
,
\mathrm{Ree}(3^{2n+1})
, or
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(2^{n})
for
n>1
;

(ii)

S\cong\mathrm{Dih}(8)
and
G
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{PSL}_{3}(2)
,
\mathrm{Alt}(7)
, or
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
where
q\equiv 7,9\mod 16
; or

(iii)

G\cong\mathrm{Sz}(2^{n})
,
\mathrm{PSU}_{3}(2^{n})
,
\mathrm{PSL}_{3}(2^{n})
, or
\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(2^{n})
for
n>1
.

Throughout, a group of nilpotency class two will be a non-abelian group. Our notation and terminology for groups is reasonably standard and generally follows that used in
[
Asc00
]
and
[
Gor07
]
. For fusion systems, we follow the notation used in
[
AKO11
]
and
[
Asc11
]
.

2.
Group Theory Preliminaries

We begin with some elementary results regarding finite
p
-groups.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
S
is a finite
p
-group of nilpotency class two,
T\trianglelefteq S
and
T=T_{1}\times\dots\times T_{n}
where the
T_{i}
are pairwise isomorphic and of nilpotency class two. Assume that
s\in S\setminus T
permutes the set
\{T_{i}\}
. Then
s
normalizes each
T_{i}
.

Proof.

Assume that
S
is as described in the lemma, and choose
s\in S\setminus T
such that
s
permutes the set
\{T_{i}\}
. Aiming for a contradiction, suppose that there is
T_{k}
with
T_{k}^{s}=T_{j}
for
k\neq j
. Let
y\in T_{k}
and choose
x\in T_{k}\setminus Z(T_{k})
. Then
y^{s}\in T_{j}
and so
[x,y^{s}]=1
. On the other hand,
y^{-1}y^{s}=[y,s]\in Z(S)
since
S
has nilpotency class two. Hence,
[x,y^{-1}]=1
and since
y
was chosen arbitrarily, we deduce that
x\in Z(T_{k})
, a contradiction. Hence, no such
k
exists and
s
normalizes each
T_{i}
.
∎

In the analysis of fusion systems on
2
-groups of class two, those with components which are isomorphic to the
2
-fusion category of
\mathrm{Alt}(6)
cause considerably more difficulty than any other type of component. We provide the following lemma which helps in treating these cases.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
S
is a
2
-group of nilpotency class
2
,
P\trianglelefteq S
and
P\cong\mathrm{Dih}(8)
. Then
S=PC_{S}(P)
.

Proof.

We observe that
Z(S)\cap P=Z(P)
and so, as
S
has nilpotency class two, we deduce that
[S,P]=Z(P)
. Indeed,
[\mathrm{Aut}_{S}(P),\mathrm{Inn}(P)]=\{1\}
. Since
|\mathrm{Inn}(P)|=4
and
\mathrm{Aut}(P)
is non-abelian of order
8
, we deduce that
\mathrm{Aut}_{S}(P)=\mathrm{Inn}(P)
and
S=PC_{S}(P)
.
∎

Finally, comparing with the
Main Theorem
, we see that the simple
2
-fusion systems supported on a
2
-group of nilpotency class two are either isomorphic to the
2
-fusion category of
\mathrm{PSL}_{3}(2^{a})
, or to the
2
-fusion category of
\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(2^{a})
where
a\in\mathbb{N}
. The underlying
2
-group in each case has exactly two elementary abelian subgroups of maximal order. We present a well known lemma which identifies such structure in
2
-groups. For this, and for a
p
-group
S
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}(S)
the elementary abelian subgroups of
S
of maximal rank.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
S
be a
2
-group and suppose that there is
A,B\in\mathcal{A}(S)
with
AB=S
. If
C_{B}(a)=A\cap B
for every
a\in A\setminus(A\cap B)
, then
\mathcal{A}(S)=\{A,B\}
and every involution of
S
lies in
A\cup B
.

Proof.

Aiming for a contradiction, let
C\in\mathcal{A}(S)
with
A\neq C\neq B
and choose
c
an involution in
S
. Then
c=ab
for some
a\in A
and
b\in B
and
1=c^{2}=(ab)^{2}=[a,b]
. Hence, either
a\in A\cap B
so that
c\in B
, or
a\not\in B
and
b\in C_{B}(a)=A\cap B
so that
c=ab\in A
. Thus,
c\in A\cup B
and we now have that
C=(C\cap A)(C\cap B)
.

Let
c\in C\cap A
with
c\not\in B
. Then
c\in A\setminus(A\cap B)
so that
C_{B}(c)=A\cap B
. But
C\cap B\leq C_{B}(c)
so that
C\cap B\leq A
and
C\leq A
. Since
A,C\in\mathcal{A}(S)
, we deduce that
C=A
, a contradiction. Hence, no such
C
exists and
\mathcal{A}(S)=\{A,B\}
.
∎

3.
Fusion Systems

In this section, we collect various important concepts regarding fusion systems, all the time assuming a familiarity with fusion systems in line with the first part of
[
AKO11
]
. We recall some of the most pivotal results which shall be used throughout this work.

We first recall the notion of an
essential subgroup
of a saturated fusion system
\mathcal{F}
, and denote the set of essential subgroups of
\mathcal{F}
by
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{F})
. As with many other works, the following theorem underpins large parts of our methodology in classifying fusion systems.

Theorem 3.1

(Alperin – Goldschmidt Fusion Theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system on a
p
-group
S
. Then

\mathcal{F}=\langle\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)\mid Q\,\,\text{is essential or}\,\,Q=S\rangle.

Proof.

See
[
AKO11
, Theorem I.3.5]
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system on a
p
-group
S
and
E\in\mathcal{F}^{frc}
. Set
\{1\}=E_{0}\trianglelefteq E_{1}\trianglelefteq E_{2}\trianglelefteq\dots\trianglelefteq E_{m}=E
such that, for all
0\leq i\leq m
,
E_{i}\alpha=E_{i}
for each
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)
. If
Q\leq S
is such that
[Q,E_{i}]\leq E_{i-1}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
then
Q\leq E
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
,
S
,
E
and
Q
be as described in the lemma. Then
N_{QE}(E)
embeds in
\mathrm{Aut}(E)
as
\mathrm{Aut}_{QE}(E)
and we can arrange that
N_{QE}(E)
, and hence
\mathrm{Aut}_{QE}(E)
, centralizes (an
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)
-invariant refinement of)
E_{0}\trianglelefteq E_{1}\trianglelefteq E_{2}\trianglelefteq\dots\trianglelefteq E_{m}
. Setting
A:=\langle\mathrm{Aut}_{QE}(E)^{\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)}\rangle
, we have by
[
Gor07
, Corollary I.5.3.3]
that
A
is a
p
-group. Since
A\trianglelefteq\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)
, we see that
A\leq O_{p}(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(E))=\mathrm{Inn}(E)
, as
E
is
\mathcal{F}
-radical. Hence,
\mathrm{Aut}_{QE}(E)\leq\mathrm{Inn}(E)
and since
E
is
\mathcal{F}
-centric, we infer that
N_{QE}(E)\leq E
and
N_{QE}(E)=E
. The only possibility is that
E=QE
and
Q\leq E
, as required.
∎

Again, looking to the typical examples of fusion systems supported on
p
-groups of class two, as in the
Main Theorem
, we notice that the local subsystems often involve the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(p^{n})
acting on its
natural module
. In order to prove the
Main Theorem
, we will have to recognize this action.

Definition 3.3
.

A
natural
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(p^{n})
-module
is any irreducible
2
-dimensional
\mathrm{GF}(p^{n})\mathrm{SL}_{2}(p^{n})
-module regarded as a
2n
-dimension module for
\mathrm{GF}(p)\mathrm{SL}_{2}(p^{n})
by restriction.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that
E
is an essential subgroup of a saturated fusion system
\mathcal{F}
on a
p
-group
S
, and assume that there are
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)
-invariant subgroups
U\leq V\leq E
such that
E=C_{S}(V/U)
and
V/U
is an FF-module for
G:=\mathrm{Out}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)
. Then, writing
L:=O^{p^{\prime}}(G)
and
W:=V/U
, we have that
L/C_{L}(W)\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(p^{n})
,
C_{L}(W)
is a
p^{\prime}
-group and
W/C_{W}(O^{p}(L))
is a natural
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(p^{n})
-module.

Proof.

This follows from
[
Hen10
, Theorem 5.6]
(c.f.
[
Hen10
, Theorem 1]
).
∎

The following definitions are standard in the study of fusion systems.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system on a
p
-group
S
and
Q\leq S
.

(i)

Say that
Q
is strongly closed in
\mathcal{F}
if, for all
P\leq Q
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(P,S)
we have that
P\alpha\leq Q
.

(ii)

Say that
Q
is normal in
\mathcal{F}
, written
Q\trianglelefteq\mathcal{F}
, if for all
P,R\leq S
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(P,R)
there is
\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(PQ,RQ)
such that
\widehat{\alpha}|_{P}=\alpha
and
Q\widehat{\alpha}=Q
.

We note that if
Q
is normal in
\mathcal{F}
, then
Q
is strongly closed in
\mathcal{F}
; and if
Q
is strongly closed in
\mathcal{F}
then
Q\trianglelefteq S
.

There is a unique largest normal subgroup of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
O_{p}(\mathcal{F})
. We will sometimes refer to this group as the
p
-core
of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system over a
p
-group
S
. Then the following are equivalent for a subgroup
Q\leq S
:

(i)

Q\trianglelefteq\mathcal{F}
;

(ii)

Q
is strongly closed in
\mathcal{F}
and contained in every centric radical subgroup of
\mathcal{F}
; and

(iii)

Q
is contained in each essential subgroup,
Q
is
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)
-invariant for any essential subgroup
E
of
\mathcal{F}
and
Q
is
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)
-invariant.

Moreover, if
Q
is an abelian subgroup of
S
, then
Q\trianglelefteq\mathcal{F}
if and only if
Q
is strongly closed in
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

See
[
AKO11
, Proposition I.4.5]
and
[
AKO11
, Corollary I.4.7]
.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system on
S
. Say that
\mathcal{F}
is
constrained
if
C_{S}(O_{p}(\mathcal{F}))\leq O_{p}(\mathcal{F})
.

We will make liberal use of the following theorem, often without reference.

Theorem 3.8

(Model Theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a constrained fusion system on a
p
-group
S
and set
Q:=O_{p}(\mathcal{F})
. Then the following hold:

(i)

There is a model for
\mathcal{F}
. That is, there is a finite group
G
with
S\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(G)
,
F^{*}(G)=O_{p}(G)
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
.

(ii)

If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are two models for
\mathcal{F}
, then there is an isomorphism
\phi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
such that
\phi|_{S}=\mathrm{Id}_{S}
.

(iii)

For any finite group
G
with
S\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(G)
,
F^{*}(G)=Q
and
\mathrm{Aut}_{G}(Q)=\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)
, there is
\beta\in\mathrm{Aut}(S)
such that
\beta|_{Q}=\mathrm{Id}_{Q}
and
\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)=\mathcal{F}^{\beta}
. Thus, there is a model for
\mathcal{F}
which is isomorphic to
G
.

Proof.

See
[
AKO11
, Theorem I.4.9]
.
∎

As with the proofs of many theorems in finite group theory, the proof of the
Main Theorem
comes through analysis of a minimal counterexample. For this inductive approach, we require a reasonable handle on subsystems and quotient systems of fusion systems. In
[
AKO11
, Section II.5]
, quotients are defined with respect to strongly closed subgroups of a fusion system. We will only ever require quotients with respect to normal subgroups, and so we specialize the definition.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system on a
p
-group
S
and
Q\trianglelefteq\mathcal{F}
. For each
P,R\leq S
where
Q\leq P\cap R
, and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(P,R)
define
\alpha^{+}:P/Q\to R/Q
by
(xQ)\alpha^{+}:=(x\alpha)Q
.

Define
\mathcal{F}/Q
to be the category whose objects are subgroups of
S/Q
such that for
Q\leq P\cap R
where
P,R\leq S
, we have
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}/Q}(P/Q,R/Q):=\{\alpha^{+}\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(P,R)\}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system on a
p
-group
S
and
Q\trianglelefteq\mathcal{F}
. Then
\mathcal{F}/Q
is a saturated fusion system supported on
S/Q
.

Proof.

See
[
AKO11
, Lemma II.5.2(a), Lemma II.5.5]
.
∎

Again in parallel with finite group theory, the notion of normality and simplicity in fusion systems is of key importance. More than this, we will heavily focus on the structure of certain
subnormal
systems of fusion systems.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system on
S
and
\mathcal{E}
a fusion subsystem of
\mathcal{F}
supported on
T\leq S
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is normal in
\mathcal{F}
, written
\mathcal{E}\trianglelefteq\mathcal{F}
, if
T
is strongly closed in
\mathcal{F}
and the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\mathcal{E}
is saturated;

(ii)

\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}=\mathcal{E}
for each
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(T)
;

(iii)

for each
P\leq T
and each
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(P,T)
there are
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(T)
and
\phi_{0}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(P,T)
such that
\phi=\phi_{0}\circ\alpha
; and

(iv)

each
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{E}}(T)
extends to some
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(TC_{S}(T))
such that
[\alpha,C_{S}(T)]\leq Z(T)
.

Condition (ii) will be referred to as the invariance condition, while (iii) will be referred to as the Frattini condition. If a subsystem
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
satisfies (ii) and (iii) then it is said to be
\mathcal{F}
-invariant.

Define subnormality in fusion systems to be the transitive extension of normality in fusion systems, and write
\mathcal{E}\trianglelefteq\trianglelefteq\mathcal{F}
if
\mathcal{E}
is a subnormal subsystem of
\mathcal{F}
.

We recall that for
Q\leq S
, we have that
Q\trianglelefteq\mathcal{F}
if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{Q}(Q)\trianglelefteq\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system on a
p
-group
S
and let
\mathcal{H}
be a saturated subsystem of
\mathcal{F}
supported on
T
. Say that

(i)

\mathcal{H}
has
index prime to
p
in
\mathcal{F}
if
S=T
and
O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(P))\leq\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{H}}(P)
for all
P\leq S
; and

(ii)

\mathcal{H}
has
p
-power index
in
\mathcal{F}
if

\mathfrak{hyp}(\mathcal{F}):=\langle g^{-1}(g\alpha)\mid g\in P\leq S,\alpha\in O^{p}(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(P))\rangle\leq T

and
O^{p}(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(P))\leq\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{H}}(P)
for all
P\leq S
.

By
[
AKO11
, Theorem I.7.7]
, there is a unique smallest fusion subsystem of index prime to
p
in
\mathcal{F}
which we denote by
O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})
. This fusion system is normal in
\mathcal{F}
.

Similarly, as in
[
AKO11
, Theorem I.7.4]
, we denote the unique smallest fusion system of
p
-power index in
\mathcal{F}
by
O^{p}(\mathcal{F})
. We remark that
O^{p}(\mathcal{F})
is normal in
\mathcal{F}
and supported on
\mathfrak{hyp}(\mathcal{F})
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a saturated fusion system on a
p
-group
S
. If
\mathcal{E}\trianglelefteq\mathcal{F}
is supported on
S
then
\mathcal{E}
has index prime to
p
in
\mathcal{F}
and
O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{E})\trianglelefteq\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{E}
is supported on
S
, for all
P\leq S
we have that
\mathrm{Aut}_{S}(P)\leq\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{E}}(P)
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is normal in
\mathcal{F}
, it is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant and so by
[
AKO11
, Proposition I.6.4]
, we have that
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{E}}(P)\trianglelefteq\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(P)
. In particular,
O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{E}}(P))=O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(P))\leq\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{E}}(P)
and
\mathcal{E}
has index prime to
p
in
\mathcal{F}
.

Now,

Corollary 4.8
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a saturated fusion system on a
p
-group
S
such that

has class two. Assume that

\mathcal{X}
has no components isomorphic to the

-fusion system of
\mathrm{Alt}(6)
.

Throughout, we set

such that

is supported on
T
. By

4.6
,

normalizes each component of
\mathcal{F}
and by

4.7
we have that

is supported on

of components of
\mathcal{F}

with

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=T_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}\left(\prod_{\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}}T_{\mathcal{C}}\right)\left(\left(\bigcap_{\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}}C_{S}(\mathcal{C})\right)\cap C_{S}(\mathcal{C}_{n})\right)

Theorem 7.6
.

\mathcal{F}
is not a minimal counterexample to the

.

Proof.

By

, we may assume throughout that

to

normalizes

8.
Finite Simple Groups With Sylow
2
-subgroups of Nilpotency Class Two

In this final section, we use our fusion system result to reprove Gilman and Gorenstein’s classification of finite simple groups with Sylow
2
-subgroups of nilpotency class at most two. We recall this theorem as stated in the introduction.

Theorem GG
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite simple group such that a Sylow
2
-subgroup
S
of
G
has nilpotency class at most two. Then one of the following holds:

(i)

S
is abelian and
G
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{J}_{1}
,
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
where
q\equiv 3,5\mod 8
,
\mathrm{Ree}(3^{2n+1})
, or
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(2^{n})
for
n>1
;

(ii)

S\cong\mathrm{Dih}(8)
and
G
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{PSL}_{3}(2)
,
\mathrm{Alt}(7)
or
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
where
q\equiv 7,9\mod 16
; or

(iii)

G\cong\mathrm{Sz}(2^{n})
,
\mathrm{PSU}_{3}(2^{n})
,
\mathrm{PSL}_{3}(2^{n})
or
\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(2^{n})
for
n>1
.

For use later, we recall the following facts about the groups mentioned in

.

Lemma 8.1
.

Suppose that

is a finite simple group such that a Sylow

-subgroup

of

(i)

If

is abelian then a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Out}(G)

2

2
-subgroup of the Schur multiplier of

2
.

2
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Out}(G)

2
-subgroup of the Schur multiplier of

. Moreover, a

-central extension of

-subgroup if and only if it is a trivial extension.

(iv)

If

for

2

2
-subgroup of

-subgroup of the Schur multiplier of

-central extension of

Proof.

Most of the statements concerning Schur multipliers can be gleaned from

If

Throughout, we let

Proposition 8.2

Lemma 8.4

Lemma 8.5
.

For

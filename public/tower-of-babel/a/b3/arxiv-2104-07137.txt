The mean number of divisors for rough, dense and practical numbers
Andreas Weingartner

Abstract
. We give asymptotic estimates for the mean number of divisors of integers without small prime factors, integers with bounded ratios of consecutive divisors, and for practical numbers. In the last case, this confirms a conjecture of Margenstern.

11N25, 11N37

1.
Introduction

Let
\tau(n)
be the number of positive divisors of
n
.
We explore the average size of
\tau(n)
as
n
runs through one of several integer sequences.
First, we look at the
y
-rough numbers, i.e. integers with no prime factors
\leq y
.
Let
P^{-}(n)
denote the smallest prime factor of
n\geq 2
and put
P^{-}(1)=\infty
.
Define the function
\xi(u)
by
\xi(u)=0
for
u<1
and

u\xi(u)=2+2\int_{1}^{u-1}\xi(t)\,dt\qquad(u\geq 1).

(1)

Theorem 1
.

Uniformly for
x\geq 1
,
y\geq 2
,
u=\log x/\log y
,

S(x,y):=\sum_{n\leq x\atop P^{-}(n)>y}\tau(n)=1+x(\log x)\prod_{p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}\\
+\frac{x}{\log y}\left\{\xi(u)-ue^{-2\gamma}-\left.\frac{2y}{x}\right|_{x\geq y}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log y}\right)\right\}

and

\xi(u)=(u+2)e^{-2\gamma}+O\left(u^{-u}\right)\qquad(u\geq 1).

With its three main terms of magnitude
xu/\log y
, one of which is negative, Theorem
1
is somewhat difficult to
make sense of. However, this form is preferable when we apply it to prove Theorems
2
and
3
.
The estimate for
\xi(u)
and Mertens’ formula allow us to simplify Theorem
1
as follows.

Corollary 1
.

We have

S(x,y)=x\log(xy^{2})\prod_{p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}\left\{1+O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}+u^{-u}\right)\right\}\quad(x\geq y\geq 2),

S(x,y)=\frac{x\xi(u)-2y}{\log y}\left\{1+O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}+e^{-\sqrt{\log y}}\right)\right\}\quad(x\geq 2y\geq 4).

Dividing these estimates by that of Lemma
2
for
\Phi(x,y)
, the number of
y
-rough integers
n\leq x
,
we obtain the following results for the mean value of
\tau(n)
.
Throughout,
\omega(u)
denotes Buchstab’s function.

Corollary 2
.

We have

\frac{S(x,y)}{\Phi(x,y)}=\log(xy^{2})\prod_{p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\left\{1+O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}+u^{-u}\right)\right\}\quad(x\geq y\geq 2),

\frac{S(x,y)}{\Phi(x,y)}=\frac{\xi(u)}{\omega(u)}\left\{1+O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}+e^{-\sqrt{\log y}}\right)\right\}\quad(x\geq 2y\geq 4).

Corollary

shows that, for fixed

, the average of
\tau(n)
over

-rough integers

is asymptotic to
\xi(u)/\omega(u)
, as

.

The

-dense integers are a special case of a family of integer sequences that arise as follows.
Let
\theta
be an arithmetic function.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{\theta}
be the set of positive integers containing

with prime factorization

p_{1}<p_{2}<\ldots<p_{k}

p_{i}\leq\theta\big(p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{i-1}^{\alpha_{i-1}}\big)\qquad(1\leq i\leq k).

(4)

We write
\mathcal{B}(x)=\mathcal{B}\cap[1,x]
and
B(x)=|\mathcal{B}(x)|
.
When
\theta(n)=nt
, then
\mathcal{B}
is the set of
t
-dense integers
[
6
,
10
,
13
]
.
If
\theta(n)=\sigma(n)+1
, where
\sigma(n)
is the sum of the positive divisors of

, then

\mathcal{B}
is the set of practical numbers
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10

, i.e. integers

such that every

can be expressed as a sum of distinct positive divisors of

. By making some adjustments to the proof of Theorem
2
,
we will obtain the following result.

Theorem 3
.

Assume
\max(2,n)\leq\theta(n)\ll n\exp((\log n)^{a})
for
n\geq 1
, where
a
is any constant
with
a<(1-\delta)/(2-\delta)=0.2226...
Then

T(x):=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)}\tau(n)=\nu_{\theta}x(\log x)^{\delta}+O(x),

where
\delta=0.7136125...
is as in Lemma
6
and
\nu_{\theta}
is a positive constant.

In 1991, Margenstern
[
3
]
proposed seven conjectures about practical numbers, together with empirical evidence to support them.
Theorem

confirms Conjecture 4 of
[
3
]
, which says that
T(x)\sim\nu x(\log x)^{\delta}
for
some constants
\nu,\delta\in(1/2,1)
.
Although we have not determined the constant factor
\nu
, empirical evidence suggests that
\nu=0.54...
in the case of practical
numbers.
Thirty years after the publication of Margenstern’s paper
[
3
]
, six of the seven conjectures have now essentially been resolved
[
4
,
5
,
13
,
16
,
17
]
, while the remaining one
[
3
, Conjecture 2]
, which is about the count of twin practical numbers, has seen significant progress
[
5
,
17
]
.

We take this opportunity to give an improved version of
[
13
, Thm. 5.1]
, an asymptotic estimate for the
counting function
B(x)=|\mathcal{B}(x)|
. Theorem
4
has a sharper error term and allows for
\theta(n)

to grow faster.
Let

l(x)=\exp\left(\frac{\log x}{\log_{2}x\log_{3}^{3}x}\right).

Theorem 4
.

Assume
\max(2,n)\leq\theta(n)\leq nf(n)
, where
f(x)\ll l(x)
,
f(x)
is non-decreasing and

(\log f(x))/\log x
is eventually decreasing.
Then

B(x)=\frac{c_{\theta}x}{\log x}\bigl\{1+O\left(E(x)\right)\bigr\},

where

E(x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\log f(y)}{y\log^{2}y}dy

and the positive constant
c_{\theta}
is given by

c_{\theta}=\frac{1}{1-e^{-\gamma}}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{n}\Biggl(\sum_{p\leq\theta(n)}\frac{\log p}{p-1}-\log n\Biggr)\prod_{p\leq\theta(n)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right).

For example, if
\theta(n)\ll n(\log n)^{A}
, where
A
is an arbitrary constant, then
E(x)\ll\log\log x/\log x
,
while
[
13
, Thm. 5.1]
only allows for
A<1
and has a relative error term of
O(\log^{A}x/\log x)
in that case.
Comparing Theorem
4
with
[
13
, Thm. 5.1]
, we have essentially replaced

f(y)
with
\log f(y)
in the definition of the error term
E(x)
.
The improvement comes from a more careful treatment of the penultimate term in Lemma
2
,
when this estimate is inserted into Lemma
4
.
If
B(x)\sim c_{\theta}x/\log x
, then
\theta(n)
cannot grow much faster than is allowed by Theorem
4
,
since
\theta(n)\asymp n^{1+\varepsilon}
implies
B(x)\sim c_{\theta}x/(\log x)^{1-\eta}
for some
\eta>0
,
by
[
14
, Thm. 3]
.

2.
The function
\xi(u)

If we sort the integers
n\leq x
according to their smallest prime factor
p=P^{-}(n)
and
ignore higher powers of
p
, we expect that

S(x,y)=\sum_{n\leq x\atop P^{-}(n)>y}\tau(n)\approx\sum_{\sqrt{x}<p\leq x}2+\sum_{y<p\leq\sqrt{x}}2S(x/p,p).

This suggests the definition (
1
), provided
S(x,y)\approx x\xi(u)/\log y
.
We have

u\xi(u)=\begin{cases}0&u<1\\
2&1\leq u<2\\
4\log(u-1)+2\quad&2\leq u<3\end{cases}

and

(u\xi(u))^{\prime}=2\xi(u-1)\qquad(u\neq 1,2).

(5)

As with Buchstab’s function
\omega(u)
,
\xi(u)
is
k
times continuously differentiable for
u>k+1
.
By induction on
\lfloor u\rfloor
, (
1
) easily implies the bounds

\frac{u+2}{4}\leq\xi(u)\leq u+1\qquad(u\geq 1).

(6)

Equation (
5
) shows that
\xi(u)
belongs to a family of functions
f
that satisfy

uf^{\prime}(u)+af(u)+bf(u-1)=0
, where
a
and
b
are constants. These equations were studied in great detail
by Hildebrand and Tenenbaum
[
2
]
.

Lemma 1
.

We have

\displaystyle\xi(u)=2\omega(u)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{u}\omega(t)\omega(u-t)dt\qquad

\displaystyle(u\geq 0),

(7)

\displaystyle|\xi^{\prime\prime}(u)|

\displaystyle<\frac{2^{u}}{7\Gamma(u+1)}\qquad

\displaystyle(u\geq 3.5),

(8)

\displaystyle|\xi^{\prime}(u)-e^{-2\gamma}|

\displaystyle<\frac{2^{u}}{7\Gamma(u+1)}\qquad

\displaystyle(u\geq 2.5),

(9)

\displaystyle|\xi(u)-(u+2)e^{-2\gamma}|

\displaystyle<\frac{2^{u}}{7\Gamma(u+1)}\qquad

\displaystyle(u\geq 1.5),

(10)

\displaystyle\xi(u)-(u+2)e^{-2\gamma}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{e}{u\log 2u}\right)^{u}\qquad

\displaystyle(u\geq 1).

(11)

Proof.

With equation (
5
), we find that the Laplace transform

\hat{\xi}(s)
is given by

1+\hat{\xi}(s)=s^{-2}\exp\left(-2\gamma+2\int_{0}^{s}(1-e^{-t})t^{-1}dt\right)=(1+\hat{\omega}(s))^{2},

(12)

by
[
11
, Thm. III.6.5]
. This implies (
7
).

For the next three estimates, we find exact formulas for
\xi(u),\xi^{\prime}(u),\xi^{\prime\prime}(u)
in terms of polylogarithms, on
the interval
[0,5]
, with the help of Mathematica. This shows that the claims are valid for
u\leq 5
.
For
u>3
,
\xi(u)
is twice continuously differentiable, so that (
5
) implies

u\xi^{\prime\prime}(u)=2\xi^{\prime}(u-1)-2\xi^{\prime}(u)=-2\int_{u-1}^{u}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)dt\qquad(u>4).

Assuming that (
8
) is false, we define

M:=\inf\{u\geq 3.5:|\xi^{\prime\prime}(u)|\geq 2^{u}/(7\Gamma(u+1))\},

so that
M>5
. We have

M|\xi^{\prime\prime}(M)|\leq 2\int_{M-1}^{M}|\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)|dt<2\int_{M-1}^{M}\frac{2^{t}}{7\Gamma(t+1)}dt<2\frac{2^{M-1}}{7\Gamma(M)},

since
2^{t}/(7\Gamma(t+1))
is decreasing for
t\geq 2
. Dividing by
M
, we get
|\xi^{\prime\prime}(M)|<2^{M}/(7\Gamma(M+1)
, which
is impossible in light of the continuity of
\xi^{\prime\prime}(u)
at
u=M>5
. This establishes (
8
).

Since
\omega(u)-e^{-\gamma},\omega^{\prime}(u)\ll 1/\Gamma(u+1)
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}(\omega(u)-e^{-\gamma})du=e^{-\gamma}-1
,
(
7
) implies
\xi(u)-(u+2)e^{-2\gamma}=o(1)
and
\xi^{\prime}(u)-e^{-2\gamma}=o(1)
as
u\to\infty
.
The estimate (
8
) yields

\left|\xi^{\prime}(u)-e^{-2\gamma}\right|=\left|-\int_{u}^{\infty}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)dt\right|\leq\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{2^{t}}{7\Gamma(t+1)}dt<\frac{2^{u}}{7\Gamma(u+1)},

where the last inequality is valid for
u\geq 5
. Similarly, (
9
) yields

\left|\xi(u)-(u+2)e^{-2\gamma}\right|=\left|\int_{u}^{\infty}(\xi^{\prime}(t)-e^{-2\gamma})dt\right|\leq\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{2^{t}dt}{7\Gamma(t+1)}<\frac{2^{u}}{7\Gamma(u+1)},

for
u\geq 5
.

The estimate (
11
) follows from
[
2
, Thms. 1 and 2 ]
, which contains more
precise information about the error term.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

Define

\Phi(x,y)=|\{n\leq x:P^{-}(n)>y\}|.

In Lemma 5 of
[
17
]
, we obtained the following estimate for
\Phi(x,y)

by combining several results from Section III.6 of Tenenbaum
[
11
]
.
Having three separate terms of size
x/\log y
in our estimate of
\Phi(x,y)
,
rather than one composite term, simplifies our proof.

Lemma 2
.

Uniformly for
x\geq 1
,
y\geq 2
,
u=\log x/\log y
,

\begin{split}\Phi(x,y)&=1+x\prod_{p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+\frac{x}{\log y}\!\left\{\omega(u)-e^{-\gamma}\!-\left.\frac{y}{x}\right|_{x\geq y}+O\left(\frac{e^{-u/3}}{\log y}\right)\!\right\}.\\
\end{split}

Lemma 3
.

Uniformly for
x\geq 1
,
y\geq 2
,
u=\log x/\log y
,

\sum_{n\leq x\atop P^{-}(n)>y}\frac{1}{n}=1+(\log x)\prod_{p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+\int_{0}^{u}(\omega(v)-e^{-\gamma})dv+O\left(\frac{1}{\log y}\right).

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2
and partial summation.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We write

S(x,y):=\sum_{n\leq x\atop P^{-}(n)>y}\tau(n)=\sum_{n\leq x\atop P^{-}(n)>y}\sum_{d|n}1=\sum_{d\leq x\atop P^{-}(d)>y}\Phi(x/d,y).

With Lemma
2
, we obtain

\begin{split}S(x,y)=&O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}y}\right)+\Phi(x,y)+\Biggl(\sum_{d\leq x\atop P^{-}(d)>y}\frac{1}{d}\Biggr)x\prod_{p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\\
&+\frac{x}{\log y}\sum_{d\leq x\atop P^{-}(d)>y}\frac{1}{d}\left\{\omega\left(\frac{\log x/d}{\log y}\right)-e^{-\gamma}-\left.\frac{y}{x/d}\right|_{d\leq x/y}\right\}.\end{split}

We estimate
\Phi(x,y)
with Lemma
2
, and the first sum with Lemma
3
.
In the second sum, the contribution from
d=1
is

\frac{x(\omega(u)-e^{-\gamma})-y|_{x\geq y}}{\log y},

while the contribution from
1<d\leq x
is

\frac{x}{\log y}\int_{0}^{u}\omega(v)(\omega(u-v)-e^{-\gamma})dv+O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}y}\right),

by partial summation, Lemma
3
and integration by parts.
Combining these estimates with Mertens’ formula, we obtain Theorem
1
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
2

We assume that
\theta
satisfies

\theta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\},\quad\theta(1)\geq 2,\quad\theta(n)\geq P^{+}(n)\quad(n\geq 2).

(13)

Lemma 4
.

Assume (
13
) and let
f(n)
be multiplicative. For
x\geq 0
we have

\sum_{m\leq x}f(m)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)}f(n)\Bigl(1+\sum_{2\leq r\leq x/n\atop P^{-}(r)>\theta(n)}f(r)\Bigr).

Proof.

Each
m\geq 1
factors uniquely as
m=nr
, with
n\in\mathcal{B}
and

P^{-}(r)>\theta(n)
if
r>1
.
∎

Theorem 5
.

Assume (
13
).
We have

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)}\tau(n)=(1-L)x\log x+o(x\log x),

where
0\leq L\leq 1
and

L=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{\tau(n)}{n}\prod_{p\leq\theta(n)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}.

(14)

Thus,
L=1
if and only if
\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)}\tau(n)=o(x\log x)
.

Proof.

For every
N\leq x
, Lemma
4
yields

\sum_{m\leq x}\tau(m)\geq\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(N)}\tau(n)\Bigl(1+\sum_{2\leq r\leq x/n\atop P^{-}(r)>\theta(n)}\tau(r)\Bigr).

We divide both sides by
x\log x
, fix
N
and let
x\to\infty
. Theorem
1
implies

1\geq\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(N)}\frac{\tau(n)}{n}\prod_{p\leq\theta(n)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}.

This shows that the series in (
14
) converges to a value
L\in[0,1]
.
Inserting Theorem
1
into the last sum of Lemma
4
, the convergence of the series (
14
) implies

x\log x=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)}\tau(n)+Lx\log x+o(x\log x).

∎

Corollary 6
.

Assume (
13
). If
\max(2,n)\leq\theta(n)\ll n^{2}
for

, then

x(\log x)^{0.58}\ll\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)}\tau(n)\ll x(\log x)^{0.99998}

and

.

Proof.

Theorem 3 and Table 1 of
[

]
show that
\theta(n)\ll n^{2}
implies

We have

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)\atop\Omega(n)\leq 2.01\log_{2}x}\tau(n)\leq B(x)2^{2.01\log_{2}x}\ll\frac{x(\log x)^{(2.01)\log 2}}{(\log x)^{0.4191}}\ll x(\log x)^{0.98}

and

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)\atop\Omega(n)\geq 2.01\log_{2}x}\tau(n)\leq\sum_{n\leq x\atop\Omega(n)\geq 2.01\log_{2}x}\tau(n)\ll x(\log x)^{0.99998},

by Lemma

. Theorem

shows that

.

The lower bound follows from
[

, Cor. 3]
. The exponent

N_{k}=\sqrt{x2^{-k}}
and
\log(N_{k}/M_{k})\ll\log f(x)
.
Partial summation yields

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)\atop n\theta(n)\in I_{k}}\frac{1}{n}\leq\frac{B(N_{k})}{N_{k}}+\int_{M_{k}}^{N_{k}}\frac{B(y)}{y^{2}}dy\ll\frac{1}{\log N_{k}}+\log\log N_{k}-\log\log M_{k}.

Writing
\log M_{k}=\log N_{k}-\log N_{k}/M_{k}=\log N_{k}+O(\log f(x))
, we find that the last expression
is
O((\log f(x))/\log x)
. Inserting this into (
19
), we conclude that the
contribution from the penultimate term of Lemma
2
is
O(x(\log f(x))/\log^{2}x)
.

For the contribution from the term
x\prod_{p\leq y}(1-1/p)
in Lemma
2
,
we have

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{n}\prod_{p\leq\theta(n)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)=1,

by
[
14
, Thm. 1]
.
With Mertens’ formula and the fact that
\omega(u)=0
for
u<0
, we obtain

B(x)=x\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{n\log\theta(n)}\left\{e^{-\gamma}-\omega\left(\frac{\log x/n}{\log\theta(n)}\right)\right\}+O\left(\frac{x\log f(x)}{\log^{2}x}\right).

We now replace each instance of
\theta(n)
by
2n
, incurring an error of

\ll\frac{x\log f(x)}{\log^{2}x}+x\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\log f(y)}{y\log^{3}y}dy\ll\frac{x\log f(x)}{\log^{2}x},

since
\log f(y)/\log y
is eventually decreasing.
Next, we use partial summation on the last sum, with an error
\ll x/\log^{2}x
, to obtain

B(x)=x\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{B(y)}{y^{2}\log 2y}\left\{e^{-\gamma}-\omega\left(\frac{\log x/y}{\log 2y}\right)\right\}dy+O\left(\frac{x\log f(x)}{\log^{2}x}\right).

The rest of the proof is the same as that of
[
13
, Thm. 5.1]
.
In the process of inverting a Laplace transform, two additional error terms appear. They are

\ll\frac{x}{\log^{3}x}\int_{1}^{x}\frac{\log f(y)}{y}dy+\frac{x}{\log x}\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\log f(y)}{y\log^{2}y}dy\leq\frac{x\log f(x)}{\log^{2}x}+\frac{xE(x)}{\log x}.

Since
f(x)
is non-decreasing,
f(x)\geq f(1)\geq 2
and
E(x)\geq(\log f(x))/\log x\geq\log 2/\log x
, so that
all error terms are
O(xE(x)/\log x)
.

The proof of the formula for
c_{\theta}
is identical to that of
[
15
, Thm. 1]
, where this formula is
derived under a more restrictive upper bound condition on
\theta(n)
. This stronger condition was needed in
[
15
]
,
only because
[
13
, Thm. 5.1]
required it to ensure that
B(x)\sim c_{\theta}x/\log x
holds.

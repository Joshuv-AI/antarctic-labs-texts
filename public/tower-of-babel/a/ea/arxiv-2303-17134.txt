1.
Introduction

Metric Diophantine approximation is originated from the study of how well a real number can
be approximated by rationals in the sense of measure and dimension. Since the pioneer work of Khintchine
[
26
]
, Jarník
[
25
]
, Groshev
[
21
]
, Sprindzuk
[
35
]
and Schmidt
[
33
]
where the metric theory of concrete sets in classic Diophantine approximation are presented, a corner stone is the introduction of the terminology of
regular systems
by Baker & Schmidt
[
3
]
in 1970, which has opened up a possibility about general principles for the metric theory of limsup sets.

Definition 1.1

(Regular system)
.

Let
I
be an interval in
\mathbb{R}
and let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of real numbers in
I
, together with a positive function
\mathcal{N}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Call
(\Gamma,\mathcal{N})
a regular system, if for any subinterval
J
of length
|J|
there exists an integer
K_{J}
such that for any
K\geq K_{J}
, there exist elements
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{t}
in
\Gamma\cap J
such that

t\geq c(\Gamma)\cdot|J|\cdot K,\ \ {\text{and}}\ \ \mathcal{N}(\gamma_{i})\leq K,\ |\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{j}|\geq K^{-1}\ {\text{for all}}\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq t,

where
c(\Gamma)
is an absolute constant.

Equipped with the assumption of
(\Gamma,\mathcal{N})
being a regular system, one is able to set up a complete metric theory for sets of the form

\{x\in I:|x-\gamma|<\psi\big(\mathcal{N}(\gamma)\big),\ {\text{for infinitely many}}\ \gamma\in\Gamma\}.

See Baker & Schmidt
[
3
]
for Hausdorff theory and Beresnevich
[
4
]
for Lebesgue theory among others. As special cases, it can be applied to the approximation by rational numbers and algebraic numbers.

The notion of regular systems in
\mathbb{R}
was extended to ubiquitous systems in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by Dodson, Rynne & Vickers
[
19
]

in 1990 to establish general principle for the Hausdorff theory of limsup sets in higher dimensional spaces. This includes Diophantine approximation for systems of linear forms and beyond. In 2006 the notion of ubiquitous systems in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
was further generalized to the setting of abstract metric spaces by Beresnevich, Dickinson & Velani
[
7
]
. In this paper we call it
ubiquity for balls
.

Definition 1.2

(Ubiquity for balls)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact metric space with a finite Borel measure
\mu
. Let
\{\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in J\}
be a sequence of subsets in
X
and let
\beta:J\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a positive function attaching a weight to
\alpha\in J
. Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a function (henceforth referred to as the ubiquitous function), and let
\{\ell_{n},u_{n}:n\geq 1\}
be two sequences of positive numbers with
u_{n}\geq\ell_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Call
\big(\{\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in J},\rho\big)
a ubiquitous system if for any ball
B\subset X
there exists
n_{o}(B)
such that for all
n\geq n_{o}(B)

(1.1)

\mu\left(B\cap\bigcup_{\alpha\in J:\ell_{n}\leq\beta_{\alpha}\leq u_{n}}\Delta\big(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha},\rho(u_{n})\big)\right)\geq c(J)\cdot\mu(B),

where
\Delta(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha},\epsilon)
denotes the
\epsilon
-neighborhood of
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}
, and
c(J)
is an absolute constant.

Equipped with the assumption of ubiquity, Beresnevich, Dickinson & Velani
[
7
]
established a complete metric theory on the set of the form

\mathcal{W}(\psi):=\ \big\{x\in X:x\in\Delta\big(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha},\psi(\beta_{\alpha})\big),\ {\text{for infinitely many}}\ \alpha\in J\big\},

including Hausdorff measures as well as
\mu
-measure under
some mild and natural conditions. Instead of citing the full generality of the measure theory in
[
7
]
, we state the following special case (all the necessary notation will be explained in §
2.1
).

Theorem 1.3

(Beresnevich, Dickinson & Velani
[
7
]
)
.

Assume the
\delta
-Ahlfors regularity for a measure
\mu
on
X
, the
\kappa
-scaling property for every resonant set
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in J
, and the ubiquity for balls with respect the function
\rho
and the sequences
\{\ell_{n},u_{n}:n\geq 1\}
. Assume that
\psi
is decreasing and that either
\psi
or
\rho
is
\lambda
-regular for some
0<\lambda<1
. Then

\mu\big(\mathcal{W}(\psi)\big)=\mu(X)\ \ \ {\text{if}}\ \ \ \ \sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{\psi(u_{n})}{\rho(u_{n})}\right)^{\delta(1-\kappa)}=\infty.

Among many of applications of Theorem
1.3
in
[
7
]
, we cite one application to the classical Diophantine approximation, i.e. the Khinthchine-Groshev theorem.

Corollary 1.4
.

[
7
]

Let
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a non-increasing function. Then the set

\Big\{A\in[0,1]^{dh}:\|A_{i}\mathbb{q}\|<\psi(|\mathbb{q}|),\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq d,\ {\text{for infinitely many}}\ \mathbb{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}\Big\}

is of Lebesgue measure zero or one according to

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{h-1}\psi(q)^{d}<\infty\ {\text{or}}\ =\infty.

Here
A_{i}
are the rows of
A
,
|\mathbb{q}|=\max_{1\leq k\leq h}|q_{k}|
, and
\|x\|
stands for the distance of a real number
x
from integers.

These general principles have become fundamental in metric number theory.
Besides the wide usage in classical Diophantine approximation (for example
[
6
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
29
,
31
]
), they are also used in Diophantine approximation of
p
-adic fields and formal power fields
[
16
,
28
]
, as well as Diophantine approximation on manifolds (for example
[
2
,
5
,
8
,
13
]
).

It should be noted that all these principles are designed to attack the metric theory of limsup sets defined by balls, that is, starting from
Dirichlet’s theorem in simultaneous Diophantine approximation.
However, more generally one can take Minkowski’s theorem, see
[
32
,
34
]
, as a starting point.
Here is a special case:

For any
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, any non-negative vector
(\hat{a}_{1},\dots,\hat{a}_{d})
with
\hat{a}_{1}+\cdots+\hat{a}_{d}=1
, and any
Q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an integer
1\leq q\leq Q
such that

\|qx_{i}\|<Q^{-\hat{a}_{i}},\ 1\leq i\leq d.

Consequently, by letting
a_{i}=1+\hat{a}_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq d
, there exist infinitely many integers
p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q
such that

|x_{i}-p_{i}/q|<q^{-a_{i}},\ 1\leq i\leq d.

This means that all vectors will fall into a sequence of rectangles centered at rational vectors infinitely often.

Minkowski’s theorem provides a more profound understanding of the distribution of rational vectors,
which works sufficiently well in high dimensional Diophantine approximation compared with Dirichlet’s theorem
(for example, Minkowski’s theorem intervenes as an essential tool in Diophantine approximation on manifolds,
see e.g.
[
13
,
8
]
).
So it should be valuable to study the improvement based on Minkowski’s theorem, or more precisely, to consider the metric theory of limsup sets defined
by rectangles. However, besides some specific examples found in the work of Sprindzuk
[
35
]
, Schmidt
[
33
]
, Gallagher
[
20
]

and Hussain & Yusupova
[
23
]
on linear forms, no general theory has been put forth. So the study on limsup sets defined by rectangles lags much behind the study on limsup sets defined by balls. In this paper we hope to push this forward by presenting a general principle for the measure theory of limsup sets defined by rectangles.

2.
The Framework and Main Results

In this section, we describe our general framework and state the main result of the paper. In fact, one of the major tasks is to find suitable assumptions which could possibly catch the nature for the metric theory of limsup sets defined by rectangles.

2.1.
The Framework

Throughout, fix an integer
d\geq 1
. Let
(X_{i},{\text{dist}}_{i},\mu_{i})
be a bounded locally compact metric
space with
\mu_{i}
a Borel probability measure and
\text{dist}_{i}
a metric on
X_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq d
.
We consider the product space
(X,{\text{dist}},\mu)
, where

X=\prod_{i=1}^{d}X_{i};\ \mu=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mu_{i};\ \ {\text{dist}}(x,y)=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}{\text{dist}}_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}).

A ball
B(x,r)
in
X
is the product of balls in
\{X_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
, i.e.

B(x,r)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}B(x_{i},r),\ {\text{for}}\ x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}).

•

Let
J
be an infinite countable index set and let
\beta:J\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a positive function such that for any
M>1
,
\{\alpha\in J:|\beta(\alpha)|<M\}
is finite;

•

Let
\{\ell_{n},u_{n}:n\geq 1\}
be two sequences of integers such that
u_{n}\geq{\ell}_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, and
define

J_{n}=\{\alpha\in J:\ell_{n}\leq\beta(\alpha)\leq u_{n}\}.

•

Let
\rho=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{d})
be a
d
-tuple of functions with
\rho_{i}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\rho_{i}(u)\to 0
as
u\to\infty
for each
1\leq i\leq d
.

For each
1\leq i\leq d
, let
\{\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i}:\alpha\in J\}
be a sequence of subsets of
X_{i}
. The resonant sets in
X
we are considering are

\Big\{\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i},\ \ \alpha\in J\Big\}.

For any
\mathbb{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})
, denote a rectangle-like set as

\Delta(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha},\mathbb{r})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Delta(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i},r_{i}),

where
\Delta(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i},r_{i})
is the
r_{i}
-neighborhood of
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i}
in
X_{i}
.

Let
\Psi=(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{d})
be a
d
-tuple of positive functions defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The set we would like to describe is:

\mathcal{W}(\Psi)=\Big\{x\in X:x\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Delta\Big(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i},\psi_{i}\big(\beta(\alpha)\big)\Big),\ {\text{i.m.}}\ \alpha\in J\Big\},

i.e. the set of points which fall into the ‘
rectangle
’
\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Delta(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i},\psi_{i}\big(\beta_{\alpha})\big)
for infinitely many
\alpha\in J
.

Next we impose some regularity assumptions on the measures
\mu_{i}
and the resonant sets
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}
.

Definition 2.1

(
\delta
-Alhfors regularity)
.

Let
\Omega
be a complete metric space and
\nu
be a Borel probability measure. Call
\nu
Ahlfors regular with exponent
\delta
if there exist constants
c>0
and
r_{0}>0
such that for any
x\in\Omega
and
r<r_{0}
,

c^{-1}r^{\delta}\leq\nu\big(B(x,r)\big)\leq cr^{\delta}.

In the following, we will assume the measure
\mu_{i}
to be
\delta_{i}
-Ahlfors regular for each
1\leq i\leq d
. We will also require the resonant sets to have a special form generalizing the Euclidean space set-up where the resonant sets are points or, more generally, affine subspaces.

Definition 2.2

(
\kappa
-scaling property)
.

Let
0\leq\kappa<1
. For each
1\leq i\leq d
, say that
\{\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i}\}_{\alpha\in J}
has a

\kappa
-scaling property if for any
\alpha\in J
and any ball
B(x_{i},r)
in
X_{i}
with center
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i}
and
0<\epsilon<r
,
one has

c^{-1}\cdot r^{\delta_{i}\kappa}\cdot\epsilon^{\delta_{i}(1-\kappa)}\leq\mu_{i}\big(B(x_{i},r)\cap\Delta(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i},\epsilon)\big)\leq c\cdot r^{\delta_{i}\kappa}\cdot\epsilon^{\delta_{i}(1-\kappa)}

for some absolute constant
c>0
.

We list some examples for which the
\kappa
-scaling property holds.

(1)

For each
\alpha\in J
, the
i
th coordinate
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i}
is a point in
X_{i}
, so
\kappa=0
.

(2)

Let
X_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each
\alpha\in J
, the
i
th coordinate
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i}
is an
l
-dimensional affine subspace in
X_{i}
, so
\delta_{i}=n
and
\kappa=l/n
.

(3)

Let
X_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and for all
\alpha\in J
let
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i}
be an
l
-dimensional smooth compact manifold embedded in
X_{i}
. Then
\delta_{i}=n
and
\kappa=l/n
.

(4)

Let
X_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all
\alpha\in J
let
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i}
be a self-similar set of Hausdorff dimension
l
satisfying the open set condition. Then
\delta_{i}=n
and
\kappa=l/n
.

For a proof of the scaling property in the last two examples, one is referred to Allen & Baker
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.3

(
\lambda
-regularity)
.

Let
0<\lambda<1
. A function
\varphi
is said to be
\lambda
-regular with respect to the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
if

\varphi(u_{n+1})\leq\lambda\cdot\varphi(u_{n})\ \ {\text{for all}}\ \ n\gg 1.

2.2.
Ubiquitous systems for rectangles

The ubiquity condition for balls (
1.1
) is mainly rooted in Dirichlet’s theorem in Diophantine approximation
[
7
]
. Thus, as far as the metric theory
of limsup sets defined by rectangles is concerned, it is reasonable to expect that Minkowski’s theorem should intervene in some form.
The following notion of
“ubiquity for rectangles”
is designed to catch the nature of the rectangles inspired by Minkowski’s theorem.
It first appeared in the previous work of the second-named author with Xu and Wu
[
36
,
37
]
,
where the Hausdorff theory for limsup sets defined by rectangles was investigated.

Definition 2.4

(Ubiquity for rectangles)
.

Call
(\{\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in J},\beta)
a ubiquitous system for rectangles with respect to the function
\rho=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{d})
and the sequences
\{\ell_{n},u_{n}:n\geq 1\}
if there exist a constant
c>0
such that for any ball
B
in
X

(2.1)

\mu\left(B\cap\bigcup_{\alpha\in J_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Delta\big(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i},\rho_{i}(u_{n})\big)\right)\geq c\cdot\mu(B)\ \ {\text{for all}}\ \ n\geq n_{o}(B).

Our main result is the following general principle for the measure theory of limsup sets defined by rectangles, which together with the Hausdorff theory developed
in
[
37
]
provides a rather complete metric theory for this set-up (under the ubiquity hypothesis). For ease of notation, for two
d
-tuple
s
of functions
\rho
and
\Psi
we write

\displaystyle\rho\ {\text{is $\lambda$ regular}}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\,\rho_{i}\ {\text{is $\lambda$ regular for all}}\ 1\leq i\leq d;

\displaystyle\Psi(u)\leq\rho(u)\

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\,\psi_{i}(u)\leq\rho_{i}(u)\ {\text{for all}}\ 1\leq i\leq d.

Theorem 2.5

(Measure theory)
.

Under the setting given above, assume the
\delta_{i}
-Ahlfors regularity for
\mu_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq d
, the
\kappa_{i}
-scaling property for every
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i}
with
\alpha\in J
and
1\leq i\leq d
, and the ubiquity for rectangles. Assume that
\Psi
is decreasing, that either
\Psi
or
\rho
is
\lambda
-regular, and that
\Psi(u_{n})\leq\rho(u_{n})
for all
n\gg 1
. Then

\mu\big(\mathcal{W}(\Psi)\big)=\mu(X)\ \ \ {\text{if}}\ \ \ \ \sum_{n\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{\psi_{i}(u_{n})}{\rho_{i}(u_{n})}\right)^{\delta_{i}(1-\kappa_{i})}=\infty.

The first application of our result is a solution to a simple shrinking target problem. Though it can be proved by the exponential mixing property of
the underlying dynamics, however with the help of Theorem
2.5

one can see that the proof uses only very basic arithmetic features of the system.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
b_{1},\dots,b_{d}\geq 2
be integers, and let
T_{i}(x)=b_{i}x\ (\bmod\ 1)
. Then for any
x_{o}\in[0,1]^{d}
and a
d
-tuple
\Psi
of positive functions

\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{d}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the Lebesgue measure of the set

\mathfrak{S}(\Psi)=\Big\{x\in[0,1]^{d}:|T_{i}^{n}x_{i}-x_{o,i}|<\psi_{i}(n)\ \forall\,1\leq i\leq d,\ {\text{for infinitely many}}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}

is zero or one according to

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\psi_{i}(n)<\infty\ {\text{or}}\ =\infty.

We next apply our result to systems of linear forms mainly to illustrate the way for choosing the ubiquity function. Let
\varphi=\{\varphi_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
be a
d
-tuple of non-increasing positive functions defined on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with

\varphi_{i}(q)\to 0,\ {\text{as}}\ q\to\infty,

and let
\Phi=\{\Phi_{k}\}_{1\leq k\leq h}
be an
h
-tuple of non-decreasing positive integer-valued functions defined on
\mathbb{N}
with

\Phi_{k}(u)\to\infty\ {\text{as}}\ u\to\infty.

Consider the following set:

\displaystyle W(\varphi,\Phi):

\displaystyle=\bigg\{A\in[0,1]^{dh}:{\text{the system}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|A_{i}\mathbb{q}\|<\varphi_{i}(u),1\leq i\leq d,&\\
|q_{k}|\leq\Phi_{k}(u),\ \ \,1\leq k\leq h,&\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad{\text{has a solution in $\mathbb{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}\smallsetminus\{0\}$}}\ {\text{for infinitely many}}\ u\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\big\{A\in[0,1]^{dh}:\|A_{i}\mathbb{q}\|<\varphi_{i}\big(\max\{\Phi_{1}^{-1}(|q_{1}|^{+}),\dots,\Phi_{h}^{-1}(|q_{h}|^{+})\}\big),\ 1\leq i\leq d,\ {\text{i.m.}}\ \mathbb{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}\big\}.

Here
|q|^{+}
stands for
\max\{1,|q|\}
. Also in what follows we will denote Lebesgue measure on Euclidean spaces by
\mathcal{L}
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume that there exists
M>1
such that for all
n\gg 1
,

c_{1}\Phi_{k}(M^{n})\leq\Phi_{k}(M^{n+1})\leq c_{2}\Phi_{k}(M^{n}),\ 1\leq k\leq h

for some absolutely constants
c_{1},c_{2}>1
. Then

\mathcal{L}\big(W(\varphi,\Phi)\big)
is zero or one according to

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi_{i}(q)\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(q)<\infty\ {\text{or}}\ =\infty.

It should be mentioned that Sprindžuk
[
35
]
established a metric result for systems of linear forms which goes beyond the set-up involving rectangles. Though in Sprindžuk’s result only primitive vectors
\mathbb{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}
are involved, Theorem
2.7
can be obtained from
[
35
, Chapter 1, Theorem 13]
with the help of an elementary calculation.

It is instructive to state the special case
h=1
of the above theorem. Then one can take
\Phi_{1}(q)=q
and thus study the set

{W}(\varphi):=\big\{x\in[0,1]^{d}:\|qx_{i}\|<\varphi_{i}(q),\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq d,\ \ {\text{i.m.}}\ q\in\mathbb{N}\big\}.

Theorem
2.7
immediately implies

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\varphi=\{\varphi_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
be as above; then

\mathcal{L}\big(W(\varphi)\big)

is zero or one according to

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi_{i}(q)<\infty\ {\text{or}}\ =\infty.

Remark 2.9
.

The
necessity of the ubiquity assumption in Theorem
2.5
can be justified by alluding to a result of Boshernitzan & Chaika
[
12
]
about Borel-Cantelli sequences. According to

,
if
\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset[0,1]
is a sequence such that for some ball

and for any

such that

(2.2)

{\mathcal{L}\Big(B\cap\bigcup_{n\leq N_{\epsilon}}B(x_{n},N_{\epsilon}^{-1})\Big)\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathcal{L}(B),}

then there exists a non-increasing positive function

3.
Proof of the Main Result

To estimate the measure of a limsup set from below, the following Chung-Erdös inequality
[
14
]
is widely used.

Lemma 3.1

(Chung-Erdös inequality
[
14
]
, see also
[
27
]
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\nu)
be a a finite measure space, and let
\{E_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of measurable sets. If
\sum_{n\geq 1}\nu(E_{n})=\infty
, then

\nu\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_{n}\right)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\left(\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}\nu(E_{n})\right)^{2}}{\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\nu(E_{i}\cap E_{j})}.

The Chung-Erdös lemma enables one to conclude the positivity of the measure of a set. In applications, to arrive at a full measure result, one often uses the Chung-Erdös lemma restricted to a local set and then applies the following proposition.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a metric space, and let
\nu
be a finite doubling
1
1

1

A measure
\nu
on
\Omega
is called doubling if
\exists\,c>0
such that for any
x\in\Omega
and
r>0
,
\nu\big(B(x,2r)\big)\leq c\cdot\nu\big(B(x,r)\big)
.

Lemma 5.2

6.
Systems of Linear Forms

In this section, we prove Theorem
2.7
by applying Theorem
2.5
. The main task is to find the suitable ubiquitous function. Recall that

\{\varphi_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
are
d
non-increasing positive functions defined on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with

\varphi_{i}(u)\to 0,\ {\text{as}}\ u\to\infty,

and
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{1\leq k\leq h}
are
h
non-decreasing integer valued functions with

\Phi_{k}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N},\ \ \Phi_{k}(u)\to\infty,\ {\text{as}}\ u\to\infty.

Recall that we are considering the set

\displaystyle W(\varphi,\Phi)

\displaystyle=\bigg\{A\in[0,1]^{dh}:{\text{the system}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|A_{i}\mathbb{q}\|<\varphi_{i}(u),1\leq i\leq d,&\\
|q_{k}|\leq\Phi_{k}(u),\ \ \,1\leq k\leq h,&\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad{\text{has a solution in $\mathbb{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}\smallsetminus\{0\}$}}\ {\text{for infinitely many}}\ u\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\mathbb{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}}E_{\mathbb{q}}(\varphi,\Phi),

where

E_{\mathbb{q}}(\varphi,\Phi):=\left\{A\in[0,1]^{dh}:\|A_{i}\mathbb{q}\|<\varphi_{i}\big(\max\{\Phi_{1}^{-1}(|q_{1}|^{+}),\dots,\Phi_{h}^{-1}(|q_{h}|^{+})\}\big),\ 1\leq i\leq d,\ {\text{i.m.}}\ \mathbb{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}\right\}.

We begin with a technical lemma which enables us to choose the ubiquitous functions fulfilling the conditions that
\rho_{i}(u)\to 0
and
\psi_{i}\leq\rho_{i}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that there exists
M>1
such that for all
n\gg 1
,

c_{1}\Phi_{k}(M^{n})\leq\Phi_{k}(M^{n+1})\leq c_{2}\Phi_{k}(M^{n}),\ 1\leq k\leq h

for some absolute constants
c_{1},c_{2}>1
. Then, without changing the measure of
W(\varphi,\Phi)
,
we can assume, without loss of generality, that

(6.1)

\displaystyle\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u)\Big)^{d}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(u)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi_{i}(u)\to\infty\ {\text{when}}\ u\to\infty.

Proof.

We define a new collection of functions
\widetilde{\varphi}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d
satisfying condition (
6.1
) and

\mathcal{L}\big(W(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)\big)=\mathcal{L}\big(W({\varphi},\Phi)\big).

Fix an increasing function
f
, say
f(u)=\big(\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u)\big)^{\epsilon}
for example, which tends to infinity with a slow speed as
u\to\infty
, and check that

\frac{f(u)}{\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u)\Big)^{d}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(u)}\ \ {\text{is decreasing with respect to}}\ u.

Partition
\mathbb{N}
into two classes:

\mathcal{N}_{1}=\Big\{u\in\mathbb{N}:\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u)\Big)^{d}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(u)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi_{i}(u)\geq f(u)\Big\}

and its complement.

We can assume
\mathcal{N}_{1}
to be non-empty by redefining

\varphi_{i}(1)
and
\Phi_{i}(1)
so that they are large enough. Let
u_{0}
be the smallest element in
\mathcal{N}_{1}
. We define a new collection of functions
\widetilde{\varphi}_{i}
for
u\geq u_{0}
with
1\leq i\leq d
inductively as follows:

(1)

For
u=u_{0}
, define

\widetilde{\varphi}_{i}(u)=\varphi_{i}(u),\ \ {\text{for all}}\ 1\leq i\leq d;

(2)

Let
u=u_{0}+1
.

•

if
u\in\mathcal{N}_{1}
, define

\widetilde{\varphi}_{i}(u)=\varphi_{i}(u),\ \ {\text{for all}}\ 1\leq i\leq d;

•

if
u\not\in\mathcal{N}_{1}
, we increase the value of
\varphi_{i}(u)
as follows. Define a function

G(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Big(t\widetilde{\varphi}_{i}(u_{0})+(1-t)\varphi_{i}(u)\Big),\ \ {\text{for}}\ t\in[0,1].

Then

\displaystyle G(1)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\widetilde{\varphi_{i}}(u_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{f(u_{0})}{\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u_{0})\Big)^{d}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(u_{0})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{f(u)}{\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u)\Big)^{d}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(u)}\geq\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\varphi_{i}}(u)=G(0),

where the last inequality holds because
u\not\in\mathcal{N}_{1}
. So there exists some
t^{*}\in[0,1]
such that

G(t^{*})=\frac{f(u)}{\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u)\Big)^{d}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(u)}.

Thus define

\widetilde{\varphi_{i}}(u)=t^{*}\widetilde{\varphi}_{i}(u_{0})+(1-t^{*})\varphi_{i}(u),\ \ 1\leq i\leq d.

It is clear that

(6.2)

\widetilde{\varphi_{i}}(u_{0})\geq\widetilde{\varphi_{i}}(u)\geq\varphi_{i}(u).

(3)

Assume that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{i}(u^{\prime})
for all
1\leq i\leq d
have been defined. Then for
u^{\prime\prime}=u^{\prime}+1
the process is the same with the role of
\widetilde{\varphi_{i}}(u_{0})
and
\varphi_{i}(u)
replaced by
\widetilde{\varphi_{i}}(u^{\prime})
and
\varphi_{i}(u^{\prime\prime})
respectively.

To summarize, for the new functions
\widetilde{\varphi_{i}}(u)
for
1\leq i\leq d
one has:

•

by (
6.2
),

\widetilde{\varphi_{i}}\ \ {\text{is decreasing, and}},\ \ \widetilde{\varphi_{i}}\geq\varphi_{i};

•

for
u\in\mathcal{N}_{1}
,

(6.3)

\widetilde{\varphi_{i}}(u)=\varphi_{i}(u);

•

for
u\not\in\mathcal{N}_{1}
,

\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u)\Big)^{d}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(u)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{d}\widetilde{\varphi_{i}}(u)=f(u).

Finally, we consider the measure of the set
W(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)
. Write

\mathcal{M}_{1}=\big\{\mathbb{q}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{h})\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}:u=\max\{\Phi_{1}^{-1}(|q_{1}|^{+}),\dots,\Phi_{h}^{-1}(|q_{h}|^{+})\}\in\mathcal{N}_{1}\big\}.

By (
6.3
), one sees that

\displaystyle W(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\mathbb{q}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}E_{\mathbb{q}}(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)\cup\limsup_{\mathbb{q}\not\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}E_{\mathbb{q}}(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)

\displaystyle\subset W({\varphi},\Phi)\cup\limsup_{\mathbb{q}\not\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}E_{\mathbb{q}}(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)

We claim that the second set is of measure zero by the convergence part of the Borel-Cantelli lemma, which results in

\mathcal{L}\big(W(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)\big)=\mathcal{L}\big(W({\varphi},\Phi)\big)

as wanted. More precisely,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbb{q}\not\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}\mathcal{L}\big(E_{\mathbb{q}}(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\not\in\mathcal{N}_{1}}\sum_{\mathbb{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}:u=\max\{\Phi_{1}^{-1}(|q_{1}|^{+}),\dots,\Phi_{h}^{-1}(|q_{h}|^{+})\}}\ \mathcal{L}\big(E_{\mathbb{q}}(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\not\in\mathcal{N}_{1}}\sum_{\mathbb{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}:u=\max\{\Phi_{1}^{-1}(|q_{1}|^{+}),\dots,\Phi_{h}^{-1}(|q_{h}|^{+})\}}\ \prod_{i=1}^{d}\widetilde{\varphi}_{i}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{\mathbb{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}:u=\max\{\Phi_{1}^{-1}(|q_{1}|^{+}),\dots,\Phi_{h}^{-1}(|q_{h}|^{+})\}}\frac{f(u)}{\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq t}\Phi_{k}(u)\Big)^{d}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(u)}.

By the monotonicity of the terms in the summation and by dividing the integers
u\in\mathbb{N}
into
M
-adic blocks, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbb{q}\not\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}\mathcal{L}\big(E_{\mathbb{q}}(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{h}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(M^{t+1})\cdot\frac{1}{\big(\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(M^{t})\big)^{d-\epsilon}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(M^{t})}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{h}\cdot c_{2}^{h}\cdot\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\big(c_{1}^{-t}\big)^{d-\epsilon}}<\infty,

where the second inequality uses the assumptions posed on
\Phi
.
Thus, by the Borel-Cantelli lemma,
\limsup_{\mathbb{q}\not\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}\mathcal{L}\big(E_{\mathbb{q}}(\widetilde{\varphi},\Phi)\big)=0.

∎

2.7

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{L}\left(B\cap\bigcup_{\alpha\in{J}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Delta\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i},\frac{M\cdot\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(u_{n})}{\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u_{n})}\right)\right)\geq\mathcal{L}\left(B\cap\bigcup_{\alpha\in\widetilde{J}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Delta\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i},\frac{M\cdot\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(u_{n})}{\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u_{n})}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{L}\left(B\cap\bigcup_{\alpha\in\widetilde{J}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Delta\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha,i},\frac{\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(u_{n})}{\max_{1\leq k\leq h}|q_{k}|}\right)\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mathcal{L}(B).

This shows the ubiquity property with the ubiquitous function

\rho_{i}(u)=\frac{M\cdot\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(u)}{\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u)},\ 1\leq i\leq d.

∎

To summarize, we have

•

the ubiquitous system
(\{\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in J},\beta)
with respect to
\rho
: by Lemma
6.2
.

•

the
\lambda
-regularity property: by the monotonicity of
\varphi_{i}
and the condition assumed on
\Phi_{k}
, namely

\psi_{i}(u_{n+1})=\frac{\varphi_{i}(u_{n+1})}{\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u_{n+1})}\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}\cdot\frac{\varphi_{i}(u_{n})}{\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u_{n})}=\frac{1}{c_{1}}\cdot\psi_{i}(u_{n}).

Recall the definitions of
\rho_{i}
and
\psi_{i}
:

\rho_{i}(u)=\frac{M\cdot\varphi_{i}(u)}{\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u)}\cdot\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi_{i}(u)\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(u)\right)^{-1/d},\ \ \psi_{i}(u)=\frac{1}{h}\cdot\frac{\varphi_{i}(u)}{\max_{1\leq k\leq h}\Phi_{k}(u)}.

Then one has

•

\rho_{i}(u)\geq\psi_{i}(u)
: by (
6.4
);

•

\rho_{i}(u)\to 0
as
u\to\infty
: by Lemma
6.1
, since the denominator in
\rho_{i}(u)
tends to infinity.

Thus all the conditions in Theorem
2.5
are satisfied, and then we apply it to arrive at the desired result, i.e.

\mathcal{L}\big(W(\varphi,\Phi)\big)=1\ \Longleftarrow\ \sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi_{i}(M^{t})\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(M^{t})=\infty.

Finally, by monotonicity of
\varphi
and the assumption on
\Phi
, one has

\displaystyle\infty=\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi_{i}(q)\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum_{M^{t}\leq q<M^{t+1}}q^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi_{i}(q)\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(q)

\displaystyle\leq(M-1)c_{2}^{h}\cdot\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\varphi_{i}(M^{t})\prod_{k=1}^{h}\Phi_{k}(M^{t}).

This proves the divergence part of Theorem
2.7
, while the convergence part follows easily from the Borel-Cantelli lemma.

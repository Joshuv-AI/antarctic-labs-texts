Equidistribution of lattice orbits in the space of homothety classes of rank 2 2 sublattices in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}
Michael Bersudsky, Hao Xing

Abstract
We study the distribution of orbits of a lattice Γ ≤ SL ​ ( 3 , ℝ ) \Gamma\leq\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R}) in the moduli space X 2 , 3 X_{2,3} of covolume one rank-two discrete subgroups in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . Each orbit is dense, and our main result is the limiting distribution of these orbits with respect to norm balls, where the norm is given by the sum of squares. Specifically, we consider Γ T = { γ ∈ Γ : ‖ γ ‖ ≤ T } \Gamma_{T}=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\|\gamma\|\leq T\} and show that, for any fixed x 0 ∈ X 2 , 3 x_{0}\in X_{2,3} and φ ∈ C c ​ ( X 2 , 3 ) \varphi\in C_{c}(X_{2,3}) , lim T → ∞ 1 # ​ Γ T ​ ∑ γ ∈ Γ T φ ⁡ ( x 0 ⋅ γ ) = ∫ X 2 , 3 φ ⁡ ( x ) ​ d ​ ν ~ x 0 ​ ( x ) , \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\Gamma_{T}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}}\varphi(x_{0}\cdot\gamma)=\int_{X_{2,3}}\varphi(x)d\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}(x), where ν ~ x 0 \tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}} is an explicit probability measure on X 2 , 3 X_{2,3} depending on x 0 x_{0} . To prove our result, we use the duality principle developed by Gorodnik and Weiss which recasts the above problem into the problem of computation of certain volume estimates of growing skewed balls in H H and proving ergodic theorems of the left action of the skewed balls on SL ​ ( 3 , ℝ ) / Γ \text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma . The ergodic theorems are proven by applying theorems of Shah building on the linearisation technique. The main contribution of the paper is the application of the duality principle in the case where H H has infinitely many non-compact connected components.

Introduction

1.1
The problem set-up and statement of the main result

In this paper we study the asymptotic distributional properties of the action of lattice subgroups of the special linear group on a moduli space of discrete subgroups of the Euclidean space. Such a research direction is in particular motivated by
[
SS17
]
and the more recent work
[
GLS22
]
studying random walks on such spaces. We will study the dynamics with respect to the action of growing norm balls.

We proceed to describe our specific setting. We say that
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a 2-lattice, if
\Lambda
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-Span of a tuple of linearly independent vectors
{v_{1},v_{2}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, that is,

\Lambda:=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{v_{1},v_{2}\}.

For
\Lambda
we let

\text{Cov}(\Lambda):=\sqrt{\det(\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle)},

which is the area of a fundamental parallelogram of
\Lambda
.
The
unimodular
2-lattices
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
are those
\Lambda
with
\text{Cov}(\Lambda)=1
.
Next, we recall the definition of the shape of
\Lambda
, a notion that was extensively studied in e.g
[
EMSS16
,
AES16
]
, which refined the classical work of Schmidt
[
Sch98
]
.
We view
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as row vectors, and for a unimodular 2-lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, let
w\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
such that
w\perp\Lambda
. We choose
\rho\in\text{SO}(3,\mathbb{R})
such that
w\rho=e_{3}:=(0,0,1)
, and we define

\textbf{s}(\Lambda,w):=\Lambda\rho\begin{bmatrix}\text{SO}(2,\mathbb{R})&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},

(1.1)

which is independent of the choice of
\rho
. By identifying the unimodular 2-lattices
\Lambda\subset{e}_{3}^{\perp}
with
X_{2}
, we identify
\textbf{s}(\Lambda,w)
as a point in
X_{2}/\penalty\text{SO}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Note that in general

\textbf{s}(\Lambda,w)\neq\textbf{s}(\Lambda,-w).

Remark 1.1
.

A more intrinsic definition of a shape of a discrete subgroup, see e.g.
[
SS17
,
Sch98
]
, is defined by the equivalence class under the equivelence relation of scaling and rotations. When defining shape in this way, one gets a point in
X_{2}/\text{O}(2,\mathbb{R})
, and it captures slightly less information. Our definition (
1.1
) is mainly motivated by the definition in
[
EMSS16
,
AES16
]
, which produces a point in the more familiar space
X_{2}/\text{SO}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

We consider

X_{2,3}:=\{(\Lambda,w):\text{Cov}(\Lambda)=1,w\in\mathbb{S}^{2},w\perp\Lambda\},

and note that
s
defined in (
1.1
) yields a map

\textbf{s}:X_{2,3}\to X_{2}/\text{SO}(2,\mathbb{R}).

We define a right
\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
action on
X_{2,3}
using the usual right matrix multiplication by

(\Lambda,w).g:=\left(\frac{\Lambda g}{\sqrt{\text{Cov}(\Lambda g)}},\frac{w(^{t}g^{-1})}{\|w(^{t}g^{-1})\|}\right),~g\in\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R}),~w\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

(1.2)

where
\|\cdot\|
is the usual Euclidean norm.

It turns out that for each lattice subgroup
\Gamma\leq\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
and each
x_{0}\in X_{2,3}
, the orbit
x_{0}\Gamma
is dense in
X_{2,3}
. This follows for example by
[
SS17
]
, and a different proof of this fact is obtained by applying a duality argument. As we explain below
X_{2,3}
is a homogeneous space identified with
H\backslash G
, and by Proposition 1.5 of
[
DR80
]
, it follows that all
H
-orbits in
G/\Gamma
are dense.

Our goal in this paper will be to compute the limiting distribution of
\Gamma
orbits in
X_{2,3}
with respect to growing Hilbert-Schmidt norm balls. Namely, let
\|g\|=\sqrt{\text{Trace}(^{t}gg)}=\sqrt{\sum_{ij}(g_{ij}^{2})}
be the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
g\in\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
, let

\Gamma_{T}:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\|\gamma\|\leq T\},

and for

x_{0}:=(\Lambda_{0},w_{0})\in X_{2,3},

consider the probability measures

\mu_{T,x_{0}}:=\frac{1}{\#\Gamma_{T}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}}\delta_{x_{0}.\gamma},~T>0.

Our main result is that the probability measures
\mu_{T,x_{0}}
converge as

to a certain probability measure
\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}
depending on
{x_{0}}\in X_{2,3}
which we describe now. We observe that
X_{2,3}
has a natural projection to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
defined by

\pi_{\perp}(\Lambda,w):=w,

which endows

with a fiber-bundle structure, where the fibers are isomorphic to

X_{2}:=\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}).

To define the measure
\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}
, we define a measure
\mu_{x_{0},w}
on each fiber
\pi_{\perp}^{-1}(w)
, and we integrate those measures by the unique rotation invariant probability measure
\mu_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. We note that the measures

have a slightly surprising form; they are a combination of a
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-invariant measure with a density involving the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of operators which we define now.

For an operator
T
from a hyperplane

to another hyperplane

\|T\|^{2}_{\text{HS}}:=\|Tu_{1}\|^{2}+\|Tu_{2}\|^{2},

(1.3)

where

is an orthonormal basis of

, and where the norm on the right hand side is the usual Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We note that this norm is independent of the choice of an orthonormal basis
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
. Moreover, this norm is bi-

invariant in the following sense. If
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\text{SO}(3,\mathbb{R})

\rho_{2}\circ T\circ\rho_{1}:\rho_{1}^{-1}U\to\rho_{2}V

\|\rho_{2}\circ T\circ\rho_{1}\|_{\text{HS}}=\|T\|_{\text{HS}}.

For an ordered tuple of linearly independent vectors

and
e_{2}\mapsto u_{2}
. Now fix unimodular 2-lattice
\Lambda_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and let

be an ordered tuple of linearly independent vectors forming a

-basis for
\Lambda_{0}
. We define for an arbitrary unimodular 2-lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

\Psi_{\Lambda_{0}}(\Lambda):=\sum_{\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathscr{B}}=\Lambda}\frac{1}{\|T_{\mathscr{B}}\circ T_{\mathscr{B}_{0}}^{-1}\|_{\text{HS}}^{4}}.

(1.4)

We note that
\Psi_{\Lambda_{0}}
is independent of the choice of basis
\mathscr{B}_{0}
(see (
5.22
)), and we observe that by bi-
\text{SO}(3,\mathbb{R})
invariance of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm that the values of the function
\Psi_{\Lambda_{0}}(\Lambda)
only depends on the shapes of
\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{0}
.

By identifying
\pi_{\perp}^{-1}(e_{3})
with
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, we obtain the
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
invariant measure
\mu_{e_{3}}
supported on
\pi_{\perp}^{-1}(e_{3})
scaled such that the measure
\mu_{x_{0},e_{3}}
defined by

\mu_{x_{0},e_{3}}(f):=\int_{\pi^{-1}_{\perp}(e_{3})}f(\Lambda,e_{3})\Psi_{\Lambda_{0}}(\Lambda)d\mu_{e_{3}}(\Lambda),~f\in C_{c}(X_{2,3}),

is a probability measure. Then, we obtain the measure supported on
\pi_{\perp}^{-1}(w),\text{ for }w\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, by choosing
\rho_{w}\in\text{SO}(3,\mathbb{R})
such that
w=e_{3}\rho_{w}
, and defining,

\mu_{x_{0},w}:=(\rho_{w})_{*}\mu_{x_{0},e_{3}},

which is the push-forward of the right translation by
\rho_{w}
via the right action of
\text{SO}(3,\mathbb{R})
on
X_{2,3}
defined in (

). Note that
\mu_{x_{0},w}
is independent of the choice of

. Finally, we define
\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}
by

\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\mu_{x_{0},w}(f)d\mu_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(w).

The main goal of this paper is to prove the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma\leq\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
be a lattice and fix
{x_{0}}\in X_{2,3}
. Then,
\mu_{T,{x_{0}}}
converges in the weak-* topology to

as

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\Gamma_{T}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}}f(x_{0}\cdot\gamma)=\int_{X_{2,3}}f(x)d\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}(x).

(1.5)

Now consider the map

(\textbf{s}\times\pi_{\perp}):X_{2,3}\to X_{2}/\penalty\text{SO}(2,\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{S}^{2},

defined by

(\textbf{s}\times\pi_{\perp})(\Lambda,w):=(\textbf{s}(\Lambda,w),w).

We observe that that the push-forward of

(\textbf{s}\times\pi_{\perp})_{*}(\nu_{x_{0}})=\textbf{s}_{*}\mu_{x_{0},e_{3}}\otimes\mu_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

Let
\Gamma\leq\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
be a lattice and fix
{x_{0}}\in X_{2,3}

1.2
Connection to homogeneous dynamics - the duality principle

To simplify notation, we denote
G:=\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
. We note that the
G
-action on
X_{2,3}
is transitive, and we observe that the stabilizer subgroup of the base point
(\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{(1,0,0),(0,1,0)\},(0,0,1))
is

H=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\gamma&0\\
(x,y)&t\end{bmatrix}:t>0,\gamma\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}),(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\right\}.

(1.6)

Then we obtain the identification
H\backslash G\cong X_{2,3}
.

This connects our problem to the study of distribution of orbits of closed subgroups in homogeneous spaces. In general, let
G
be a connected Lie group,
\Gamma
be a lattice in
G
and
H
be a closed subgroup of
G
(or more generally for two closed subgroups

and
H_{2}
). The duality principle allows us to connect the equidistributional properties of the
\Gamma
-orbits on

to the equidistributional
properties of the
H
-orbits in the dual action of
H
on
G/\Gamma
, see Section 1.7 of
[
GN14
]
for an extensive exposition of the existing literature on this principle. A general recipe for applying the duality principle was developed in
[
GN14
]
and
[
GW04
]
. Our approach in this paper uses a theorem of Gorodnik and Weiss (
[
GW04
]
), since it allows to prove equidistribution for every starting point.

A novelty of our work is the application of the duality principle for a closed subgroup
H
with infinitely many non-compact connected components. We note that the case when
H
is connected algebraic was studied in great generality in
[
GN12
,
GW04
]
, and the case where
H
is discrete was studied in
[

]
and in greater generality in
[

]
.

To begin with, we shall study the invariant measure on

and introduce an equidistribution theorem on

.

1.4
Structure of the paper and further work

•

The outline of our proof of Theorem
1.5
is as follows. We will first give an estimate for the volume of

(Section 2). This estimate will help us to show the unipotent invariance of limiting measure (Section 3), which in turn opens the gate to the application of Ratner theory. In particular, a dichotomy theorem (Theorem

) due to Shah plays the central role here. To exclude the first outcome of the dichotomy theorem, we use a lemma (Lemma

) on the expansion of the norms of vectors under the action of unipotent subgroups. This allows us to prove the non-escape of mass on the space of one-point compactification of
G/\Gamma
and the
G
-invariance will follow from Ratner’s measure classification theorem (
[
Rat91
]
,
[
MS95
]
).

•

In Section 5 we prove Theorem
1.2
as a consequence of the duality principle stated in Theorem 2.2 to Corollary 2.4 in
[
GW04
]
.

We remark that despite that the theorem is stated from the case of rank-two lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Our work in progress is to generalize the results to higher ranks and dimensions. The main difficulty lies in the estimtate of the volumes of balls as well as the expansion of norms under unipotent actions.

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{\sum_{\gamma\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}\frac{1}{\|\gamma\|^{4}}}\int_{\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}\frac{1}{\|G_{1}^{-1}g\|^{4}}dg

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{\sum_{\gamma\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}\frac{1}{\|\gamma\|^{4}}}\int_{\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}\frac{1}{\|g\|^{4}}dg.

g=\begin{bmatrix}1&x\\

\displaystyle\int_{\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}\frac{1}{\|g\|^{4}}dg=

\displaystyle 2\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\left(a^{2}+\frac{1}{a^{2}}+\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)^{2}}\frac{dxda}{a^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\left(y+\frac{1}{y}+\frac{x^{2}}{y}\right)^{2}}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

(let

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\left(1+x^{2}+y^{2}\right)^{2}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}

Therefore,

\nu_{Hg_{0}}(H\backslash G)=\frac{2\pi^{3}}{m(G/\Gamma)\sum_{\gamma\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}\frac{1}{\|\gamma\|^{4}}}

(5.17)

(5.18)

Appendix A
Two copies of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
generates
\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})

Lemma A.1
.

Let

G_{1}:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0&b\\
0&1&0\\
c&0&d\end{bmatrix}:ad-bc=1\right\},

and

G_{2}:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&a&b\\
0&c&d\end{bmatrix}:ad-bc=1\right\}.

Let
G
be a closed subgroup of
\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
which includes

G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Then we claim that
G=\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let us first show that

G\supseteq G_{3}:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b&0\\
c&d&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}:ad-bc=1\right\}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&-s_{2}&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&s_{1}\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&s_{2}&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-s_{1}\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&0&s_{1}\\
0&1&0\\
0&-s_{2}&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&s_{2}&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-s_{1}\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&s_{1}s_{2}&s_{1}\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-s_{1}\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&s_{1}s_{2}&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

So for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\begin{bmatrix}1&s&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\in G
.
By applying the matrix transpose to the above, we get that for all

r\in\mathbb{R}
,
\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
r&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\in G
.
Observe that
Let
\epsilon
be non-zero close to zero, and note that for
r=\frac{e^{t}-1}{\epsilon}

and
s=\frac{-r}{1+r\epsilon}
we get

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&r\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\epsilon&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&s\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1+r\epsilon&r+s+rs\epsilon\\
\epsilon&1+s\epsilon\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}e^{t}&0\\
\epsilon&e^{-t}\end{bmatrix}.

So, for all
\epsilon>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\begin{bmatrix}e^{t}&0&0\\
\epsilon&e^{-t}&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\in G

and since
G
is closed, we get by taking
\epsilon\to 0
that

\begin{bmatrix}e^{t}&0&0\\
0&e^{-t}&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\in G.

The above show that in fact
G_{3}\leq G
. It follows that
that the full diagonal
group and the unipotent upper and lower triangular subgroups are all contained in
G
. By using the dimension matching argument on Lie algebras, we see their product form a neighborhood of identity in
\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
.

Therefore,
G=\text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
follows from the fact that a neighborhood of identity of a Lie group generates the whole group.
∎

Appendix B
Estimates on the number of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
points in skewed balls

Consider

B_{\tau}=\left\{g\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}):\left|\left|g\right|\right|\leq\tau\right\}.

From
[
GN12
]
Example 4.3, we have the following estimate

N(\tau):=\left|\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\cap B_{\tau}\right|=\frac{\text{vol}(B_{\tau})}{\text{vol}(\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}))}+O_{\eta}(\text{vol}(B_{\tau})^{5/6+\eta}).

We now compute
\text{vol}(B_{\tau})
. First, recall that
the Haar measure on
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is expressed by

\nu(f)=\int f(n_{x}a_{t}k_{\theta})\frac{1}{e^{t}}d\theta\ dt\ dx,

where
n_{x}=\begin{bmatrix}1&x\\
&1\end{bmatrix},

a_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}e^{t/2}&0\\
&e^{-t/2}\end{bmatrix},\ k_{\theta}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{bmatrix}
.

It follows from the bi-invariance of the Haar measure that

\text{vol}(B_{\tau})=\text{vol}\left\{g\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}):\left|\left|g\right|\right|\leq\tau\right\}.

and since the norm is
k_{\theta}
invariant, we get

\text{vol}\left\{g\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}):\left|\left|g\right|\right|\leq\tau\right\}=2\pi\text{vol}\left\{\begin{bmatrix}e^{t/2}&xe^{-t/2}\\
&e^{-t/2}\end{bmatrix}:\left|\left|\begin{bmatrix}e^{t/2}&xe^{-t/2}\\
&e^{-t/2}\end{bmatrix}\right|\right|\leq\tau\right\}.

We have

e^{t}+\left(x^{2}+1\right)e^{-t}\leq\tau^{2}\iff x^{2}\leq e^{t}\left(\tau^{2}-2\cosh t\right).

By Fubini’s theorem, we see that

\text{vol}\left\{\begin{bmatrix}e^{t/2}&xe^{-t/2}\\
&e^{-t/2}\end{bmatrix}:\left|\left|\begin{bmatrix}e^{t/2}&xe^{-t/2}\\
&e^{-t/2}\end{bmatrix}\right|\right|\leq\tau\right\}=\int_{\cosh t\leq\tau^{2}/2}\underbrace{\frac{2\sqrt{\tau^{2}-2\cos ht}}{e^{t/2}}}_{=f(t)}dt=I_{+}+I_{-},

where
I_{+}=\int_{t\geq 0,\ \cosh t\leq\tau^{2}/2}f(t)dt
,
I_{-}=\int_{t\leq 0,\ \cosh t\leq\tau^{2}/2}f(t)dt
.

1.

Computation of
I_{+}
: The function
\cosh t
is injective
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. and its inverse
\text{$\cosh$}^{-1}:[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

has the following expression

\text{$\cosh$}^{-1}(\alpha)=\ln(\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}).

We have the following identities,

\sinh(\text{cosh}^{-1}(\alpha))=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1},

We put
\alpha=\cosh t,
then
d\alpha=\sinh t\ dt
, which gives

I_{+}=\int_{\alpha\leq\tau^{2}/2}\frac{2\sqrt{\tau^{2}-2\alpha}}{\sqrt{\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}d\alpha.

Put
\beta=\frac{\alpha}{\tau^{2}}
, then

I_{+}=\tau^{2}\int_{\beta\leq 1/2}\frac{2\sqrt{\tau^{2}-2\tau^{2}\beta}}{\sqrt{\tau^{2}\beta+\sqrt{\tau^{4}\beta^{2}-1}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau^{4}\beta^{2}-1}}d\beta

2.

Computation of
I_{-}
: The function
\cosh t
is injective
on
\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}
and its inverse
\text{$\cosh$}^{-1}:[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}

has the following expression

\text{$\cosh$}^{-1}(\alpha)=-\ln(\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}).

We have the following identities,

\sinh(\text{cosh}^{-1}(\alpha))=-\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1},

We put
\alpha=\cosh t,
then
d\alpha=\sinh t\ dt
, which gives,

I_{+}=\int_{\alpha\leq\tau^{2}/2}2\sqrt{\tau^{2}-2\alpha}\sqrt{\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}d\alpha.

Put
\beta=\frac{\alpha}{\tau^{2}}
, then

I_{+}=\tau^{2}\int_{\beta\leq 1/2}2\sqrt{\tau^{2}-2\tau^{2}\beta}\sqrt{\tau^{2}\beta+\sqrt{\tau^{4}\beta^{2}-1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau^{4}\beta^{2}-1}}d\beta.

Define

I_{1}=\int_{\beta\leq 1/2}\frac{2\sqrt{1-2\beta}}{\sqrt{2\beta}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}-1}}d\beta

and

I_{2}=\int_{\beta\leq 1/2}2\sqrt{2\beta}\sqrt{1-2\beta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}-1}}d\beta.

Then as
\tau\to\infty
, we get that

I_{\tau}\asymp\tau^{2}I_{1}+I_{2}.

To summarize, the above shows that
\text{vol}(B_{\tau})=c^{\prime}\tau^{2}+O(\frac{1}{\tau}),

whence

N(\tau)=c\tau^{2}+O_{\eta}(\tau^{5/3+\eta}).

On the limiting amplitude principle for the wave equation with variable coefficients
Anton Arnold 1 , Sjoerd Geevers 2 , Ilaria Perugia 2 , Dmitry Ponomarev 1,3,4

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove new results on the validity of the limiting amplitude principle (LAP) for the wave equation with nonconstant coefficients, not necessarily in divergence form. Under suitable assumptions on the coefficients and on the source term, we establish the LAP for space dimensions 2 and 3. This result is extended to one space dimension with an appropriate modification. We also quantify the LAP and thus provide estimates for the convergence of the time-domain solution to the frequency-domain solution. Our proofs are based on time-decay results of solutions of some auxiliary problems.



Keywords
wave equation, variable coefficients, limiting amplitude principle, long-time asymptotics

Mathematics Subject Classification
35L05, 35L10, 35B10, 35B40

1.
Introduction

An essential ingredient in connecting time- and frequency-domain wave problems is the limiting amplitude principle (LAP). Originally proposed as one of the tools to select the unique solution of the Helmholtz equation problem in an infinite domain, it has been studied in numerous works over the last 70 years.

The LAP can be crudely stated as follows: The solution to the time-dependent wave equation with time-harmonic source term converges, for large times, to the solution of the Helmholtz equation with the spatial source term and frequency corresponding to the original time-harmonic source.
Our main motivation for revisiting the LAP comes from numerical analysis.
Helmholtz problems
can be challenging to solve in practice for large wavenumbers. Numerical methods have
been proposed to address a classical Helmholtz problem efficiently through its reformulations in the time domain. They include the controllability
method introduced in
[
7
,
17
]
, together with its spectral version
[
22
]
and
its extensions
[
21
,
20
]
, the WaveHoltz
method
[
1
]
, the time-domain preconditioner
of
[
32
]
, and the front-tracking adaptive method of
[
2
]
.
Numerical results presented in the latter paper, for instance, have shown that solving the Helmholtz equation in the time domain can be advantageous for high frequencies, when computations are essentially reduced to the neighborhood of a lower-dimensional manifold (wave-front area).

The analysis of these methods requires a quantification of the modeling error (reformulation of the frequency-domain problem into a time-domain problem), which will add to the error due to the numerical approximation of the problem in the time domain.
This motivates the study of the LAP under some new angles, with
particular focus on the quantification of large-time convergence.

As opposed to a direct study of the resolvent operator, our
analysis is based on decay estimates for the solutions of some
auxiliary PDE problems. Since decay results are still the subject of
intense investigation, an advantage of this approach is that new findings in that area directly translate into improvements in the quantification of the large-time convergence in the LAP.

Previous results on the LAP.
Let us provide a brief overview of previous works on the LAP.
The simplest version of the LAP dealing with the constant-coefficient,
three-dimensional wave equation has been known at least since 1948

[
34
,
35
]
. There, it is proven that this physical principle selects the unique solution of the stationary problem satisfying the Sommerfeld radiation condition.

Starting from the seminal work by Ladyzhenskaya
[
24
]
, variable-coefficient equations
of the form
\partial_{t}^{2}u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)-c^{2}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\Delta u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)+q\left(\mathbf{x}\right)u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)=f\left(\mathbf{x}\right)e^{-i\omega t}
have been considered. Namely, while
[
24
,
26
]
treat the case
c\equiv 0
,
paper
[
27
]
deals with the case
q\equiv 0
. When
q
,
\nabla c
and
f
are sufficiently localised, the validity of the LAP is proven in a pointwise sense but a rate of the convergence is not specified.

For the case
c\equiv 0
and dimension
d=3
, Ramm
[
29
]
establishes an algebraic pointwise convergence and shows that the convergence rate is directly related to the localisation of
q
and
f
.

Eidus’ paper
[
12
]
provides an extensive overview of the
results available at the time and treats the problem in great
generality. In particular, it deals with the wave equation arising
from a positive second-order differential operator in divergence form
-\sum_{k,j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{k}}\left(a_{kj}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\partial_{x_{j}}\right)+q\left(\mathbf{x}\right)
. It
is assumed that
q
is real-valued and locally Hölder
continuous, each
a_{kj}\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is real-valued,
a_{kj}=a_{jk}
,
1\leq k,j\leq d
, and for any vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\sum_{k,j}^{d}a_{kj}v_{k}v_{j}\geq a_{0}{\left|\mathbf{v}\right|^{2}}
with some
a_{0}>0
; moreover, it is assumed that
\left|\nabla a_{kj}\right|
and

q
decay fast enough at infinity. The
problem is posed in an unbounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a
finite boundary where the zero Dirichlet boundary condition is
imposed. However, it is mentioned in
[
12
, Ch. 2, p. 21]
that
the obtained results must also hold if this unbounded (exterior)
domain is taken to be the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The time convergence
is proven in
H^{1}
-norm of the solution and in the
L^{2}
-norm of
its time derivative, with both norms taken over bounded sets.

As a generalization, Vainberg
[
36
]
, besides geometrical features, also considers higher-order constant coefficient hypoelliptic operators in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, whereas Iwasaki
[
23
]
treats dissipative wave equations with variable dissipation and potential terms.

Ramm
[
30
]
considers a general linear operator and formulates
necessary and sufficient conditions for the validity of the LAP in
terms of certain properties of the resolvent operator.
A more general form of the LAP is formulated, involving time convergence in mean, namely, the convergence of the quantity
\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\omega\tau}u\left(\mathbf{x},\tau\right)d\tau
, for
t\rightarrow\infty
, to the stationary solution. This is shown to be equivalent to the validity of the limiting absorption principle.

More recently, the LAP was established for the wave equation of the form (
1.2
)
by Tamura
[
33
]
, but only in dimension
d=3
and without quantification of the convergence.

In this brief literature review, we have almost entirely
omitted the geometrical issues, which are
the most commonly discussed
aspects
in the literature, see the classical works of Morawetz, e.g.
[
25
]
, and her collaborators. More on that can also be
found in the introductory part of
[
12
]
. Finally, we mention some very recent works related to the validity of the LAP for wave propagation in metamaterials
[
9
,
10
,
8
]
.

In the present work, we study the LAP for a problem where
both material parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
are allowed
to be nonconstant and prove our
results in spatial dimensions
d=1,2
and
3
. The main result
given in Theorem
1.4

establishes
the validity of the LAP
and
estimates the convergence rates. Additionally,
Theorem
1.5
covers the one-dimensional case
where a classical formulation of the LAP (i.e. when
U_{\infty}=0
) is
known not to be valid
[
11
, Sect. 3, Thm. 6]
.
On a technical side, the novelty of our approach to the proof of the LAP is that it avoids the direct study of the resolvent operator and relies instead on several decay/convergence results.
The main features of the present work are:

•

The LAP is proven for the wave equation with
nonconstant coefficients, which is not necessarily in divergence form (i.e. the equation may have a nonconstant coefficient in front of the divergence operator
\nabla\cdot
). Besides the “classical” case
d=3
, we also consider
d=2
.

•

The validity of the LAP is extended to the case
d=1
with an appropriate modification.

•

The convergence in the LAP is quantified and is shown to be algebraic in time for
d=2,3
and exponential for
d=1
.

We believe that the exponential and algebraic convergence behavior
for the cases
d=1
and
d=2
are generally sharp, but that the rate
of the decay for the case
d=3
might be improved.

. We shall focus here only on

(4.1)

\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{H^{s+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{2}u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{H^{s-1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}\leq C\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}\right)

,

,
the divergence theorem gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\cdot\left(\alpha\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\nabla u_{n}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right)\overline{u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)}q_{\mu}^{-2}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)d\mathbf{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\alpha\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\nabla u_{n}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\cdot\nabla\left(\overline{u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)}q_{\mu}^{-2}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\right)d\mathbf{x}.

Therefore, adding and subtracting
u_{n}\left(\cdot,t\right)
to
u\left(\cdot,t\right)
under the divergence sign in the right-hand side of (
4.8
) gives

\displaystyle\left\langle q_{\mu}^{-1}Pu,q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\rangle_{L_{\beta}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\cdot\left(\alpha\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\nabla\left(u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)-u_{n}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right)\right)\overline{u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)}q_{\mu}^{-2}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\alpha\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\nabla\left(u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)-u_{n}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right)\cdot\nabla\left(\overline{u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)}q_{\mu}^{-2}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\right)d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\alpha\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\nabla u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\cdot\nabla\left(\overline{u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)}q_{\mu}^{-2}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\right)d\mathbf{x}.

The absolute values of the terms on the first and second lines are estimated by multiples of

\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)-u_{n}\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Therefore,
they vanish in the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle q_{\mu}^{-1}Pu,q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\rangle_{L_{\beta}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\alpha\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\left|\nabla u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|^{2}q_{\mu}^{-2}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\alpha\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\overline{u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)}\nabla u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\cdot\nabla q_{\mu}^{-2}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)d\mathbf{x}.

Rearranging the terms and employing

\left(\nabla u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\cdot\nabla q_{\mu}^{-2}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\right)=-2\mu\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}{1+\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{q_{\mu}^{-2}}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\partial_{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right),

we arrive at

(4.9)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\min}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\alpha\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\left|\nabla u\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|^{2}q_{\mu}^{-2}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\langle q_{\mu}^{-1}Pu,q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\rangle_{L_{\beta}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\right|+\mu\left\|\alpha\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left|\left\langle q_{\mu}^{-1}\partial_{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}u,q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\right|.

Furthermore, employing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we can estimate

\left|\left\langle q_{\mu}^{-1}Pu,q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\rangle_{L_{\beta}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\right|\leq\left\|\beta\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}Pu\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)},

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle q_{\mu}^{-1}\partial_{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}u,q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha_{\min}}{4\mu\left\|\alpha\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}+\frac{\mu\left\|\alpha\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}}{\alpha_{\min}}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}.

Here, on the second line, we used the elementary inequality

|a|\,|b|\leq\frac{{\delta_{0}}}{2}|a|^{2}+\frac{1}{2{\delta_{0}}}|b|^{2}
,
valid for any
{\delta_{0}}>0
.
Therefore, estimate (
4.9
) entails

\displaystyle{\frac{3}{4}\,\alpha_{\min}}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\beta\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}Pu\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}

\displaystyle+{\frac{\mu^{2}}{\alpha_{\min}}}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}.

Recalling (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), this leads to

(4.10)

\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}\leq{\frac{4}{3}}\left({\frac{C_{0}C_{2}}{\alpha_{\min}}}\left\|\beta\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}+{\frac{\mu^{2}C_{0}^{2}}{\alpha_{\min}^{2}}}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}\right)\frac{1}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{d-1}}.

Finally, denoting with
\chi_{\Omega}
the characteristic function of the bounded set
\Omega
, we have

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}=\left\|u\chi_{\Omega}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\leq C_{\Omega,\mu}\left\|q_{\mu}^{-1}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)},

and similarly for
\nabla u
and
\partial_{t}u
. Hence, the estimates
(
4.5
), (
4.6
), and
(
4.10
) furnish (
2.4
).
∎

(4.13)

\displaystyle\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\sin\left(\left(t-\tau\right)\sqrt{P}\right)}{\sqrt{P}}f\left(\cdot,\tau\right)\right\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{C_{0}}{\left(1+\left(t-\tau\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\left\|f\left(\cdot,\tau\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}d\tau,

where, in the second line, we also took into account the assumption that

\Omega

Employing the assumed estimate (

(4.14)

\displaystyle\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{C\,d\tilde{\tau}}{\left(1+\left(t-\tilde{\tau}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\left(1+\tilde{\tau}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{t^{d+p-2}}\left[\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{d\tau}{\left(1/t^{2}+\left({1}-\tau\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\left(1/t^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{d\tau}{\left(1/t^{2}+\left({1}-\tau\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\left(1/t^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}\right]

\displaystyle\frac{2^{d-1}C}{t^{p-1}}\frac{1}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{d\tau}{\left(1/t^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}

in the integrals over

and
\left[1/2,1\right]
,
respectively. In the last line of (

Using Lemma

\displaystyle\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{q}C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\begin{cases}C_{1,p}t^{1-p},&0<p<1,\\

Here and in the sequel we assume that

By rearranging the factors, we can write

Q\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{a_{1}/t}^{1}\frac{(rt)^{-3/2}}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\int_{\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1}\left(\frac{rt}{\rho}\right)^{5/2}\rho^{5/2}V_{1}\left(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right)d\sigma_{\mathbf{s}}dr.

We can assume

From the triangle inequality

(4.28)

rc_{0}t=|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t-\mathbf{x}|\leq\rho+|\mathbf{x}|\quad

and
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\Subset\mathbb{B}_{\rho_{0}}
,
\rho>\rho_{0}

Moreover, assumption (

. Using this and (

\displaystyle\left|Q\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq

Conclusion of the proof.

So far, we have proved the decay of the solution under the assumption

v_{0}\equiv 0
. We now extend the result to the general case
and show that estimates analogous to (
4.21
)
hold true for the derivatives of the solution.

To proceed, it is convenient to introduce the following notation.
Given a function
w
and constants
\omega
,
c_{0}
,
\rho_{0}>0
,
\rho_{1}>\rho_{0}
, we say that

(4.61)

w\in\mathcal{A}\equiv\mathcal{A}_{\omega,c_{0},\rho_{0},\rho_{1}}

if the following conditions are satisfied:

-

When
d=2
, we can write

w(\mathbf{x})=A_{w}(|\mathbf{x}|)Y_{w}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\right)+V_{w}(\mathbf{x})

for some functions
A_{w}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
Y_{w}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
,
V_{w}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that

A_{w}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)\equiv 0\equiv V_{w}(\mathbf{x}),\quad|\mathbf{x}|\leq\rho_{0},\qquad A_{w}(\rho)=\dfrac{e^{i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}\rho}}{\rho^{3/2}},\quad\rho>\rho_{1},

and we have

|\mathbf{x}|^{5/2}|V_{w}(\mathbf{x})|\leq C,\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

for some constant
C>0
.

-

When
d=3
, we have
w\in C(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

|\mathbf{x}|^{2}|w(\mathbf{x})|\leq C,\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

for some constant
C>0
.

Let us denote
Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]\equiv Z
the solution of the wave equation
\partial_{t}^{2}Z\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)-c_{0}^{2}\Delta Z\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)=0
for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t>0
, subject to the initial conditions
Z\left(\mathbf{x},0\right)=v_{0}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)
,
\partial_{t}Z\left(\mathbf{x},0\right)=v_{1}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)
. The assumptions
v_{0}\in C^{6}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and
v_{1}\in C^{5}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
entail

(4.62)

Z\in C^{5}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right),\quad\partial_{t}Z\in C^{4}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right);

recall Theorems 2 and 3 in
[
15
, Par. 2.4.1]
(applied to
Z
and its derivatives), see also (
4.76
) and (
4.113
).

We recall that, in proving the decay for
Z\left[0,v_{1}\right]
given by (
4.21
), we have only used that
v_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
.
But, for
d=3
, due to assumption (
2.10
), we also have

v_{0},\,\Delta v_{0},\,\Delta^{2}v_{0},\,v_{1},\,\Delta v_{1},\,\Delta^{2}v_{1}\in\mathcal{A},

which will be used to prove the decay of
Z
with such initial conditions.

For
d=2
, we observe that

v_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
entails that
\Delta v_{1}
,
\Delta^{2}v_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
under the regularity assumptions on
v_{1}
made in (
2.8
), (
2.9
).
Indeed, a short computation in polar coordinates yields that

\displaystyle\Delta v_{1}\left(x\right)=\begin{cases}0,&\left|\mathbf{x}\right|\leq\rho_{0},\\
-\left(\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}\right)^{2}\frac{e^{i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{3/2}}Y_{1}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{5/2}}\right),&\left|\mathbf{x}\right|>\rho_{1},\end{cases}

so
\Delta v_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
with
Y_{w}=-\left(\omega/c_{0}\right)^{2}Y_{1}
. Iterating, we also obtain
\Delta^{2}v_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
(with
Y_{w}=\left(\omega/c_{0}\right)^{4}Y_{1}
).
Since the assumptions on
v_{0}
made in (
2.8
), (
2.9
) provide

v_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have similarly
\Delta v_{0}
,
\Delta^{2}v_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Consequently, we deduce both for
d=2
and
d=3
that

\left|\Delta Z\left[0,v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|=\left|Z\left[0,\Delta v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,

where the first equality is implied by
\partial_{t}^{2}\Delta Z\left[0,v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)=\Delta\partial_{t}^{2}Z\left[0,v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)
which is due to
v_{1}{\in C^{5}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\subset}C^{4}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.
Therefore, in view of validity of the wave equation, we deduce

\left|\partial_{t}^{2}Z\left[0,v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0.

For fixed
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
, we interpolate between the decay estimates of
Z[0,v_{1}]
and
\partial_{t}^{2}Z[0,v_{1}]
by using Lemma
A.4
. Then, we have

\left|\partial_{t}Z\left[0,v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0.

We can write
Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]=Z\left[0,v_{1}\right]+\partial_{t}Z\left[0,v_{0}\right]
, and hence we have

(4.63)

\left|Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,

due to
v_{1}
,
v_{0}
,
\Delta v_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Also, writing
\Delta Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]=Z\left[0,\Delta v_{1}\right]+\partial_{t}Z\left[0,\Delta v_{0}\right]
, we have

(4.64)

\left|\Delta Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,

since
\Delta v_{1}
,
\Delta v_{0}
,
\Delta^{2}v_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
and
v_{0}{\in C^{6}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\subset}C^{5}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,
v_{1}{\in C^{5}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\subset}C^{4}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.
Using the wave equation, we interpolate between the decay estimates of
Z[v_{0},v_{1}]
and
\partial_{t}^{2}Z[v_{0},v_{1}]
by employing Lemma
A.4
. We thus obtain

(4.65)

\left|\partial_{t}Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0.

Moreover, since
\Delta Z\left(0,\Delta v_{0}\right)=Z\left(0,\Delta^{2}v_{0}\right)
due to
v_{0}\in C^{6}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, we can write

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Delta Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z\left[0,\Delta v_{1}\right]+\partial_{t}^{2}Z\left[0,\Delta v_{0}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z\left[0,\Delta v_{1}\right]+c_{0}^{2}Z\left[0,\Delta^{2}v_{0}\right].

Consequently,

(4.66)

\left|\partial_{t}\Delta Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,

as
v_{1}
,
\Delta v_{1}
,
\Delta^{2}v_{1}
,
\Delta^{2}v_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
and
v_{0}\in C^{6}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,
v_{1}\in C^{5}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.

To deduce the estimates analogous to (
4.64
) and (
4.66
)
but involving the gradient of
Z
instead of the Laplacian, we use an
interpolation argument.
In particular, for a function
u\in C^{2}\big(\widetilde{\Omega}\big)
, with some
\widetilde{\Omega}

such that
\Omega\Subset\widetilde{\Omega}
,
using interior elliptic regularity results, we obtain

(4.67)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{d_{x}}\right)\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left(d_{x}\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|\right)\leq C_{01}\sup_{x\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\left(d_{x}\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{01}C_{02}\left[\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\widetilde{\Omega}\right)}+\sup_{x\in\widetilde{\Omega}}d_{x}^{2}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\widetilde{\Omega}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{03}\left[\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\widetilde{\Omega}\right)}+\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\widetilde{\Omega}\right)}\right],

with some constants
C_{01}
,
C_{02}
,
C_{03}>0
. Here, we employed
the notation

d_{x}:=\textrm{dist}\big(x,\partial\widetilde{\Omega}\big)
and,
in the second line, we used the first estimate of
[
16
, Thm
3.9]
. Observe now that, since in the statement of the
present lemma, the domain
\Omega
was arbitrary, all the
previous steps of this proof remain valid (with different
constants) for the larger domain
\widetilde{\Omega}
. In particular,
estimates (
4.63
)–(
4.66
) are valid with
\Omega
replaced by
\widetilde{\Omega}
. Consequently,
according to (
4.67
) applied to
Z
and to

\partial_{t}Z
(permitted by the regularity (
4.62
)),
we deduce

\left|\nabla Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|+\left|\partial_{t}\nabla Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,

and the proof is complete.

∎

(4.77)

\displaystyle v\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{rt}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\left[\partial_{t}A\left(\rho\right)-c_{0}A^{\prime}\left(\rho\right)\right]Y\left(\phi\right)drd\sigma_{\mathbf{s}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}}A\left(\rho\right)\left[t\partial_{t}Y\left(\phi\right)+Y\left(\phi\right)\right]drd\sigma_{\mathbf{s}}.

As
A\left(\rho\right)\equiv 0
for
\rho\leq\rho_{0}
, then
\phi
is well-defined whenever it appears in the above integrands
(i.e.
\rho=\left|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right|>\rho_{0}
whenever

).

Here and in the sequel, we use the following notation to avoid having too many brackets:
\partial_{t}A(\rho):=\partial_{t}(A(\rho))
,
\partial_{t}Y(\phi):=\partial_{t}(Y(\phi))
,
\nabla Y(\phi):=(\nabla Y)(\phi)
.

Since
\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1
, we have

(4.78)

\partial_{t}A\left(\rho\right)=rc_{0}A^{\prime}\left(\rho\right)\frac{\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{s}+rc_{0}t}{\left|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right|},\hskip 20.00003pt{r\partial_{r}A\left(\rho\right)=t\partial_{t}A\left(\rho\right),}

(4.79)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}Y\left(\phi\right)=\frac{rc_{0}\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla Y\left(\phi\right)}{\left|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right|},\qquad{r\partial_{r}Y\left(\phi\right)=t\partial_{t}Y\left(\phi\right)},\end{split}

where we have used (
4.75
).
Note that, using (
4.78
), we can rewrite, for
t>0
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}A\left(\rho\right)-c_{0}A^{\prime}\left(\rho\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\left(r-1\right)\partial_{r}A\left(\rho\right)-\frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right|}{\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{s}+rc_{0}t}-1\right)\partial_{r}A\left(\rho\right)

(4.80)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\left(r-1\right)\partial_{r}A\left(\rho\right)+c_{0}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{s}+rc_{0}t}{\left|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right|}-1\right)A^{\prime}\left(\rho\right).

Note that
\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{s}+rc_{0}t
cannot be zero on
\mathrm{supp}\,A
if
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Indeed,
\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{s}+rc_{0}t{=0}
would imply

\rho^{2}=|\mathbf{x}|^{2}+2rc_{0}t\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{s}+r^{2}c_{0}^{2}t^{2}=|\mathbf{x}|^{2}-r^{2}c_{0}^{2}t^{2}\leq|\mathbf{x}|^{2}<\rho_{0}^{2},

and hence
A(\rho)=0
.

Plugging (
4.5
) into (
4.77
), we observe
that the term with
\partial_{r}A
can be integrated by parts in
the variable
r
, due to the cancellation of the singularity at
r=1
.
In doing so, both boundary terms at
r=0
and
r=1
, respectively, vanish.
With some simplifications that employ (
4.79
)
and the identity

\left(\frac{r\left(1-r\right)}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{r}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}}=\frac{1}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(1+r\right)},

we arrive at

(4.81)

\displaystyle v\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\left[\frac{1}{1+r}A\left(\rho\right)Y\left(\phi\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+rc_{0}t\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{s}+rc_{0}t}{\left|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right|}-1\right)A^{\prime}\left(\rho\right)Y\left(\phi\right)\right]drd\sigma_{\mathbf{s}}+Q\left(\mathbf{x},t\right).

Here, we have set

\displaystyle Q\left(\mathbf{x},t\right):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{rc_{0}t}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\,\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right|}A\left(\rho\right)\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla Y\left(\phi\right)drd\sigma_{\mathbf{s}}

(4.82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{A\left(\rho\right)}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\,\frac{\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla Y\left(\phi\right)}{\left|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right|}drd\sigma_{\mathbf{s}},

where we used

(4.83)

\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla Y(\phi)=-rc_{0}t\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla Y(\phi),

which follows from (
4.75
).

For later reference, we note that, for
rt\gg 1
, uniformly in
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
,

\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{S}^{{1}}
,

(4.84)

-\frac{\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla Y\left(\phi\right)}{\left|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right|}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{rt}\right),

where we used
|\mathbf{x}|\leq\rho_{0}
and

\rho\geq|\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t|-|\mathbf{x}|\geq rc_{0}t-\rho_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}rc_{0}t\qquad\mbox{ for }rt\geq\frac{2\rho_{0}}{c_{0}}.

By setting

(4.85)

P_{1}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\int_{\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1}rc_{0}t\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{s}+rc_{0}t}{\left|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}rc_{0}t\right|}-1\right)A^{\prime}\left(\rho\right)Y\left(\phi\right)d\sigma_{\mathbf{s}}dr,

(4.86)

P_{2}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(1+r\right)}\int_{\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1}A\left(\rho\right)Y\left(\phi\right)d\sigma_{\mathbf{s}}dr,

we can rewrite (
4.81
) as

(4.87)

2.3
, we shall prove that

such that the bounds

\left|P_{1}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\dfrac{\widetilde{C}_{0}}{t},\quad\left|P_{2}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\dfrac{\widetilde{C}_{1}}{t},\quad\left|Q\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\dfrac{\widetilde{C}_{2}}{t},\quad

\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}/t}^{1}\frac{1}{\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(rt\right)^{3/2}}F_{1}\left(\mathbf{x},rt\right)dr=\int_{a_{1}/t}^{1/2}\dots\phantom{.}+\int_{1/2}^{1}\dots

Conclusion of the proof.

Given a pair of functions
\left(w_{0},w_{1}\right)
and constants
\omega
,
c_{0}
,
\rho_{0}>0
,
\rho_{1}>\rho_{0}
, we say that

(4.115)

\left(w_{0},w_{1}\right)\in\mathcal{B},

if we can write

(4.116)

w_{0}(\mathbf{x})=A_{w}(|\mathbf{x}|)Y_{w}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\right)+V_{w}^{\left(1\right)}(\mathbf{x}),

(4.117)

w_{1}(\mathbf{x})=-c_{0}A_{w}^{\prime}(|\mathbf{x}|)Y_{w}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\right)+V_{w}^{\left(2\right)}(\mathbf{x})

for some functions

(4.118)

A_{w}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\quad Y_{w}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}),\quad V_{w}^{\left(1\right)}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad V_{w}^{\left(2\right)}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d})

such that

A_{w}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)=V_{w}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x})=V_{w}^{(2)}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\equiv 0,\quad|\mathbf{x}|\leq\rho_{0},\qquad A_{w}(\rho)=\dfrac{e^{i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}\rho}}{\rho^{\frac{d-1}{2}}},\quad\rho>\rho_{1},

and we have

|\mathbf{x}|^{{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\left(|V_{w}^{\left(1\right)}(\mathbf{x})|+|V_{w}^{\left(2\right)}(\mathbf{x})|+|\nabla V_{w}^{\left(1\right)}(\mathbf{x})|\right)\leq C,\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

for some constant
C>0
.

Let
Z\left[w_{0},w_{1}\right]\equiv Z_{w}
denote the solution of
the wave equation
\partial_{t}^{2}Z_{w}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)-\linebreak c_{0}^{2}\Delta Z_{w}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)=0

for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t>0
, subject to the initial conditions

Z_{w}\left(\mathbf{x},0\right)=w_{0}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)
,
\partial_{t}Z_{w}\left(\mathbf{x},0\right)=w_{1}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)
.
By linearity, we have

(4.119)

Z_{w}=Z_{AY}+Z_{V},

where the first term corresponds to the solution produced by the
A_{w}Y_{w}

terms whereas the second one is due to the
V_{w}^{\left(1\right)},

V_{w}^{\left(2\right)}
terms in (
4.116
), (
4.117
).

Note that, in the proof of the present lemma, we have already shown the decay

\left|Z_{AY}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,

since the regularity of
A
and
Y
in (
4.118
) was sufficient for this decay.
An analogous time-decay estimate holds for
the
Z_{V}
term in (
4.119
), as follows from Lemma
4.1
.
Consequently, we obtain

\left|Z_{w}\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega.

In other words, class (
4.115
) consists of the initial conditions with somewhat minimal assumptions for which we can deduce the
\mathcal{O}\left(1/t\right)
decay of the solution (but not necessarily of its derivatives).

Now we consider
Z\left[v_{0},v_{1}\right]\equiv:Z
. Clearly, we have
\left(v_{0},v_{1}\right)\in\mathcal{B}
with
V_{w}^{(1)}=V_{w}^{(2)}\equiv 0
and thus

(4.120)

\left|Z\left(\mathbf{x},t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}},\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{x}\in\Omega.

We shall deduce similar results for
\Delta Z
and
\Delta^{2}Z
.
To this effect, we first compute, for
\rho>\rho_{1}
,

A^{\prime}\left(\rho\right)=\left(\frac{i\omega}{c_{0}}-\frac{d-1}{2\rho}\right)\frac{e^{i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}\rho}}{\rho^{\frac{d-1}{2}}},

A^{\prime\prime}\left(\rho\right)=-\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}+i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}\frac{d-1}{\rho}\right)\frac{e^{i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}\rho}}{\rho^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\right),

A^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(\rho\right)=-\frac{\omega^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\left(i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}-\frac{3\left(d-1\right)}{2\rho}\right)\frac{e^{i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}\rho}}{\rho^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\right).

Therefore, we have for
\left|\mathbf{x}\right|>\rho_{1}
(with a computation in polar/spherical coordinates):

(4.121)

\displaystyle\Delta v_{0}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle=A^{\prime\prime}\left(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|\right)Y\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\right)+\frac{d-1}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}A^{\prime}\left(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|\right)Y\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\right)+\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}A\left(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|\right)\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\frac{e^{i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}Y\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\right),

(4.122)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{c_{0}}\Delta v_{1}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle=A^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|\right)Y\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\right)+\frac{d-1}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}A^{\prime\prime}\left(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|\right)Y\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\right)+\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}A^{\prime}\left(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|\right)\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\left(i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}-\frac{d-1}{2\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\right)\frac{e^{i\frac{\omega}{c_{0}}\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}Y\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\right),

where

is the Laplace-Beltrami operator
on the

Note that, due to cancellation, we do not have any

terms in (

that we can rewrite these quantities as

respectively. This, together with the regularity assumptions of this lemma, yields that

(with

Y_{w}=-\left(\omega/c_{0}\right)^{2}Y
and some

,

which are no longer identically zero). Since

(implied by

5.
Conclusions and outlook

Motivated by the development of time-domain methods for the
numerical solution of Helmholtz problems with variable
coefficients, we have established a rigorous proof of the
LAP under physically reasonable assumptions on the
coefficients of the wave equation and the source
term. Under an appropriate modification, the LAP has been extended to 1D.
Moreover, since the speed of stabilisation towards the harmonic regime
is a deciding factor for using time-domain approaches in
practice, we have also provided rigorous estimates for this
convergence in time.

Our main focus was on the 1D and 2D cases for which the LAP was
generally understudied previously. In these cases,
exponential (for 1D) and algebraic (for 2D) convergence
rates are generally sharp. In the 3D case, previous works
on wave equations of similar form and some of our numerical
experiments (for radial data, see Appendix
B
) seem to suggest that our
algebraic convergence result could be improved to the
exponential one.

An interesting extension of our results would be to remove the non-trapping assumption on the coefficients. Even though the LAP is still expected to be valid, in this case, the time convergence rate would be much slower. Namely,
[
31
, Thm. 3]
suggests that the convergence rate
1/\left(1+t\right)
would be replaced by
1/\left[\log\left(2+t\right)\right]^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma>0
.

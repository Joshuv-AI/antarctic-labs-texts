1.
Introduction

In recent years, we have witnessed that some mathematicians have explored various degenerate versions of many special polynomials and numbers by using various tools, which was initiated by Carlitz when he studied degenerate versions of some special polynomials and numbers, namely the degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials and numbers (see [2]).

The
r
-Stirling number of the second kind
{n\brace k}_{r}
counts the number of partitions of the set
[n]=\left\{1,2,\dots,n\right\}
into
k
non-empty disjoint subsets in such a way that the numbers
1,2,\dots,r
are in distinct subsets. The degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n\brace k}_{r,\lambda}
are a degenerate version of the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n\brace k}_{r}
. They can be viewed also as natural extensions of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n\brace k}_{\lambda}
, which were introduced earlier (see [11,12]).

The aim of this paper is to derive an explicit expression for the
n
-th generating function
g_{n}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}(n,m)t^{m}
, from the initial generating function
g_{0}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}(0,m)t^{m}
, and the recurrence relation given by (
12
). If we choose
a_{\lambda}(0,k)=\frac{1}{k+1}
, then
a_{\lambda}(n,0)=\beta_{n,\lambda}
. Here
\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)
are the fully degenrate Bernoulli polynomials and
\beta_{n,\lambda}=\beta_{n,\lambda}(0)
are the fully degenerate Bernoulli numbers (see (
10
)). This is generalized to slightly more general recurrence relation in (
29
) with initial generating function
g_{0}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)t^{m}
. Then we get
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\beta_{n,\lambda}(r)
, for
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)=\frac{1}{m+1}
, and
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(r)
, for
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)=\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)^{m}
. Here
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x)
are the degenerate Euler polynomials.

In more detail, we show the following. For a given initial degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}(0,m),\,\,(m=0,1,2,\dots)
, the doubly indexed degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}(n,m),\,\,(n\geq 1,\,m=0,1,2,\dots)
are defined by the recurrence relation in (
12
). For each nonnegative integer
n
, let
g_{n}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}(n,m)t^{m}
be the
n
-th generating function. Then we show that

\displaystyle g_{n}(t,\lambda)=\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}\Big)_{n,\lambda}g_{0}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(t-1)^{k}\big(\frac{d}{dt}\big)^{k}g_{0}(t,\lambda),

where
(x)_{n,\lambda}
are the degenerate falling factorials, and
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
are the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind (see (
3
)). Then, by letting
t=0
, we express
a_{\lambda}(n,0)
in terms of the initial sequence
a_{\lambda}(0,k)
, namely
a_{\lambda}(n,0)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(-1)^{k}k!a_{\lambda}(0,k)
. For
a_{\lambda}(0,k)=\frac{1}{k+1}
,
a_{\lambda}(n,0)=\beta_{n,\lambda}
. This idea is generalized to the case of slightly more general recurrence relation in (
29
), starting with the initial sequence
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)
. Indeed, by proceeding similarly to the previous case we obtain the expression

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n+r\brace m+r}_{\lambda}(-1)^{m}m!a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m),

where
r
is a nonnegative integer. For
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)=\frac{1}{m+1}
,
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\beta_{n,\lambda}(r)
. In addition, we express the sum
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
in term of the initial generating function
F(t)=g_{0}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)
. Observe here that the sum is over the first argument. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)F(1-e_{\lambda}(t))=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Here
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(r)
, for
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)=\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)^{m}
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponential functions are defined by

(1)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5,8-12]).

When
x=1
, we let
e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)
. Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}\displaystyle
.

The degenerate falling factorials are defined by

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots\big(x-(n-1)\lambda\big),\quad(n\geq 1).

In [5], the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(2)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),

where
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

As the inversion formula of (
2
), the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(3)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5]).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{1}(n,k),\ \lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{2}(n,k)\displaystyle
, where
S_{1}(n,k)
and
S_{2}(n,k)
are respectively the Stirling numbers of the first kind and the Stirling numbers of the second kind defined by

(4)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9,13-17]).

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
r\geq 0
, the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(5)

(x+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11,12]).

From (
5
), we note that

(6)

\frac{1}{k!}e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n+r\brace k+r}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11,12]).

By (
5
) and (
6
), we easily get

(7)

{n+r\brace k+r}_{\lambda}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}S_{2,\lambda}(m,k)(r)_{n-m,\lambda}.

For
r=0
, we note that
{n\brace k}_{\lambda}=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
.

In [2], Carlitz introduced the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials defined by

(8)

\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x|\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}B_{n}(x|\lambda)=B_{n}(x)\displaystyle
, where
B_{n}(x)
are the Bernoulli polynomials defined by

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,3,14]).

He also defined the degenerate Euler polynomials given by

(9)

\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2]).

When
x=0
,
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(0)
are called the degenerate Euler numbers.

The fully degenerate Bernoulli polynomials arise from a
p
-adic bosonic integral over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and are given by

(10)

\frac{\log(1+\lambda t)}{\lambda(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9]).

When
x=0
,
\beta_{n,\lambda}=\beta_{n,\lambda}(0)
are called the fully degenerate Bernoulli numbers.

For
n\geq 0
, we note that

(11)

\beta_{n,\lambda}(r)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{\lambda}(-1)^{k}\frac{k!}{k+1},\quad(r\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9]).

2.
A new approach to fully degenerate Bernoulli numbers and polynomials

In this section, we prove the main results of this paper.
Assume that
\lambda
is a fixed real number. For a given initial degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}(0,m)

(m=0,1,2,\dots)
, we define the doubly indexed degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}(n,m),\ (n\geq 1,\,\,m\geq 0)
, which are given by

(12)

a_{\lambda}(n,m)=ma_{\lambda}(n-1,m)-(m+1)a_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)-(n-1)\lambda a_{\lambda}(n-1,m).

For any nonnegative integer
n
, let
g_{n}(t,\lambda)
be the generating function of
a_{\lambda}(n,m),\,\,(m\geq 0)
, given by

(13)

g_{n}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}(n,m)t^{m}.

From (
12
) and (
13
), we note that

(14)

\displaystyle g_{n}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}(n,m)t^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big\{ma_{\lambda}(n-1,m)-(m+1)a_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)-(n-1)\lambda a_{\lambda}(n-1,m)\Big\}t^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}ma_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{m}-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)a_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)t^{m}-(n-1)\lambda\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{n}

\displaystyle=t\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)a_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)t^{m}-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)a_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)t^{m}

\displaystyle\quad-(n-1)\lambda\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{m}

\displaystyle=(t-1)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)a_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)t^{m}-(n-1)\lambda\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{m}

\displaystyle=(t-1)\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}ma_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{m-1}-(n-1)\lambda\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{m}

\displaystyle=(t-1)\frac{d}{dt}g_{n-1}(t,\lambda)-(n-1)\lambda g_{n-1}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}-(n-1)\lambda\Big)g_{n-1}(t,\lambda).

Thus, by (
14
), we get

(15)

\displaystyle g_{n}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}-(n-1)\lambda\Big)g_{n-1}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}-(n-1)\lambda\Big)\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}-(n-2)\lambda\Big)g_{n-2}(t,\lambda).

Continuing this process, we have

(16)

g_{n}(t,\lambda)=\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}\Big)_{n,\lambda}g_{0}(t,\lambda),\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Here one has to observe that

\displaystyle\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}-i\lambda\Big)\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}-j\lambda\Big)=\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}-j\lambda\Big)\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}-i\lambda\Big),

for any distinct nonnegative integers
i,j
. Similar observations to this are needed for other results in below.

Lemma 1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k\geq 0
, we have

\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}\Big)_{k,\lambda}=\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,m)(t-1)^{m}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{m}.

Proof.

Let
f(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(t-1)^{n}
.
Then we have

(17)

\displaystyle\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}\Big)_{k,\lambda}f(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(n)_{k,\lambda}(t-1)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,m)(n)_{m}\Big)(t-1)^{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,m)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(n)_{m}(t-1)^{n}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,m)(t-1)^{m}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{m}\Big)f(t).

By (
17
), we get

(18)

\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}\Big)_{k,\lambda}=\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,m)(t-1)^{m}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{m}.

∎

From (
16
) and Lemma 1. we note that

(19)

\displaystyle g_{n}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}\Big)_{n,\lambda}g_{0}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(t-1)^{k}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{k}g_{0}(t,\lambda),

where
\displaystyle g_{n}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}(n,m)t^{m}\displaystyle
,

Let
t=0
in (
19
). Then we have

(20)

a_{\lambda}(n,0)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(-1)^{k}k!a_{\lambda}(0,k).

Therefore, by (
20
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For a given initial degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}(0,m),\ (m=0,1,2,\dots)
, let the doubly indexed degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}(n,m)
be defined by the recurrence relation

a_{\lambda}(n,m)=ma_{\lambda}(n-1,m)-(m+1)a_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)-(n-1)\lambda a_{\lambda}(n-1,m),

where
n
is a positive integer. Then we have

a_{\lambda}(n,0)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(-1)^{k}k!a_{\lambda}(0,k).

Let
a_{\lambda}(0,k)=\frac{1}{k+1}
in Theorem 2. Then, from (
11
), we see that

(21)

a_{\lambda}(n,0)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(-1)^{k}\frac{k!}{k+1}=\beta_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

For a given initial degenerate sequence
b_{\lambda}(0,m),\ (m=0,1,2,\dots)
, consider the doubly indexed degenerate sequence
b_{\lambda}(n,m),\ (n\geq 1,\,\,m\geq 0)
, which are defined by

(22)

b_{\lambda}(n,m)=(m+1)b_{\lambda}(n-1,m)-(m+1)b_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)-(n-1)\lambda b_{\lambda}(n-1,m).

Let
g_{n}^{*}(t,\lambda)
be the generating function of
b_{\lambda}(n,m),\,\,(m\geq 0)
, which is given by

(23)

g_{n}^{*}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{\lambda}(n,m)t^{m}.

Then, by (
22
) and (
23
), we get

(24)

\displaystyle g_{n}^{*}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{\lambda}(n,m)t^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}mb_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{m}-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)b_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)t^{m}+\big(1-(n-1)\lambda\big)\sum_{m=0}b_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{m}

\displaystyle=(t-1)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)b_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)t^{m}+\big(1-(n-1)\lambda\big)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{m}

\displaystyle=(t-1)\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}mb_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{m-1}+\big(1-(n-1)\lambda\big)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{\lambda}(n-1,m)t^{m}

\displaystyle=(t-1)\frac{d}{dt}g_{n-1}^{*}(t,\lambda)+\big(1-(n-1)\lambda\big)g_{n-1}^{*}(t,\lambda)=\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}+1-(n-1)\lambda\Big)g_{n-1}^{*}(t,\lambda).

Thus, by (
24
), we get

\displaystyle g_{n}^{*}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}+1-(n-1)\lambda\Big)g_{n-1}^{*}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}+1-(n-1)\lambda\Big)\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}+1-(n-2)\lambda\Big)g_{n-2}^{*}(t,\lambda).

Continuing this process, we have

(25)

g_{n}^{*}(t,\lambda)=\Big(1+(t-1)\frac{d}{dt}\Big)_{n,\lambda}g_{0}^{*}(t,\lambda),

where
\displaystyle g_{n}^{*}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{\lambda}(n,m)t^{m}\displaystyle
.

From (
18
) and (
25
), we note that

(26)

\displaystyle g_{n}^{*}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}\Big)_{k,\lambda}g_{0}^{*}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,m)(t-1)^{m}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{m}g_{0}^{*}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(t-1)^{m}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{m}\sum_{k=m}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}S_{2,\lambda}(k,m)g_{0}^{*}(t,\lambda).

By (
7
) and (
26
), we get

(27)

g_{n}^{*}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(t-1)^{m}{n+1\brace m+1}_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{d}{dt}\bigg)^{m}g_{0}^{*}(t,\lambda),

where
\displaystyle g_{n}^{*}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{\lambda}(n,k)t^{k}\displaystyle
.

Let
t=0
in (
27
). Then we have

(28)

b_{\lambda}(n,0)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}{n+1\brace m+1}_{\lambda}m!b_{\lambda}(0,m).

Therefore, by (
28
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For a given initial degenerate sequence
b_{\lambda}(0,m),\ (m=0,1,2,\dots)
, let the doubly indexed degenerate sequence
b_{\lambda}(n,m),\ (n\geq 1,\,\,m\geq 0)
, be defined by the recurrence relation

b_{\lambda}(n,m)=(m+1)b_{\lambda}(n-1,m)-(m+1)b_{\lambda}(n-1,m+1)-(n-1)\lambda b_{\lambda}(n-1,m).

Then we have

b_{\lambda}(n,0)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}{n+1\brace m+1}_{\lambda}m!b_{\lambda}(0,m).

Let
b_{\lambda}(0,m)=\frac{1}{m+1}
in Theorem 3. Then, by (
11
), we have

\displaystyle b_{\lambda}(n,0)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}\frac{m!}{m+1}{n+1\brace m+1}_{\lambda}=\beta_{n,\lambda}(1),\quad(n\geq 0).

Let
r
be a nonnegative integer. For a given initial degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m),\ (m=0,1,2,\dots)
, consider the doubly indexed degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,m),\ (n\geq 1,\,\,m\geq 0)
, such that

(29)

a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,m)=(m+r)a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n-1,m)-(m+1)a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n-1,m+1)-(n-1)\lambda a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n-1,m).

Let
g_{n}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)
be the generating function of
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,m),\,\,(m\geq 0)
, which is given by

(30)

g_{n}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,m)t^{m}.

By the same method as in (
14
) and (
27
), we get

(31)

\displaystyle g_{n}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Big(r+(t-1)\frac{d}{dt}\Big)_{n,\lambda}g_{0}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(r)_{n-k,\lambda}\Big((t-1)\frac{d}{dt}\Big)_{k,\lambda}g^{(r)}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(r)_{n-k,\lambda}\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,m)(t-1)^{m}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{m}g_{0}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(t-1)^{m}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{m}\Big(\sum_{k=m}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(r)_{n-k,\lambda}S_{2,\lambda}(k,m)\Big)g_{0}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(t-1)^{m}{n+r\brace m+r}_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{m}g_{0}^{(r)}(t,\lambda),

where
\displaystyle g_{n}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)t^{k}\displaystyle
.

Let
t=0
in (
31
). Then we have

(32)

a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n+r\brace m+r}_{\lambda}(-1)^{m}m!a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m).

Therefore, by (
32
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
r
be a nonnegative integer. For a given initial degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m),\ (m=0,1,2,\dots)
, let the doubly indexed degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,m),\ (n\geq 1,\,\,m\geq 0)
, be defined by the recurrence relation

a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,m)=(m+r)a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n-1,m)-(m+1)a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n-1,m+1)-(n-1)\lambda a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n-1,m).

Then we have

a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n+r\brace m+r}_{\lambda}(-1)^{m}m!a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m).

Let
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)=\frac{1}{m+1}
. Then, by (
11
), we get

a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n+r\brace m+r}_{\lambda}(-1)^{m}\frac{m!}{m+1}=\beta_{n,\lambda}(r),\quad(n\geq 0).

For a given initial degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m),\ (m=0,1,2,\dots)
, for the ease of notation we let

(33)

F(t)=g_{0}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)t^{m}.

From (
6
), Theorem 4 and (
33
), we note that

(34)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n+r\brace m+r}_{\lambda}(-1)^{m}m!a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)\bigg)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}m!a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}{n+r\brace m+r}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}m!a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)\frac{1}{m!}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{m}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)(1-e_{\lambda}(t))^{m}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)F(1-e_{\lambda}(t)).

Therefore, by (
34
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\displaystyle F(t)=g_{0}^{(r)}(t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)t^{m}\displaystyle
. Then we have

3.
Conclusion

In recent years, explorations for degerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials regained the interests of many mathematicians which began with Carlitz’s work on the degenerate Bernoulli and Euler numbers. They have been done by using various tools like combinatorial methods, generating functions, differential equations, umbral calculus techniques,
p
-adic analysis, special functions, operator theory, probability theory, and analytic number theory.

For a given initial degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m),\ (m=0,1,2,\dots)
, and the doubly indexed degenerate sequence
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,m),\ (n\geq 1,\,\,m\geq 0)
, defined by the recurrence relation

a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,m)=(m+r)a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n-1,m)-(m+1)a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n-1,m+1)-(n-1)\lambda a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n-1,m),

it was shown by making use of a differential operator that

a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n+r\brace m+r}_{\lambda}(-1)^{m}m!a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m).

Then we noted that

a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\beta_{n,\lambda}(r)
, for
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(0,m)=\frac{1}{m+1}
, and
a_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(r)
, for

It is one of our future projects to continue to explore many degenerate special numbers and polynomials with the help of aforementioned tools.

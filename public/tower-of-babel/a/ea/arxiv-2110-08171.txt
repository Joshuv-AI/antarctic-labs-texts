Comment on: Dense entire curves … by F. Campana and J. Winkelmann
János Kollár

Abstract




We explain how modifications of the methods of
[
FW05
,
CW20
]
give the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be a smooth, proper variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
. Then there is a holomorphic map
\phi:{\mathbb{D}}\to X
such that, for every finite cover
p:Y\to X
, the map
\phi
lifts to
\phi_{Y}:{\mathbb{D}}\to Y
iff
p
is étale.

If
X
is rationally connected, then there is also such a map

\phi:{\mathbb{C}}\to X
.

By a
finite cover
we mean a dominant, finite morphism from an irreducible variety
Y
to
X
; thus ramification is allowed. If
X
is rationally connected, then it is simply connected, hence a nontrivial cover must have ramification.

The open (resp. closed) complex unit disc is denoted by
{\mathbb{D}}
(resp.
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
).
A map
g:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to X
is holomorphic if it extends holomorphically to a larger disc.

The
\phi:{\mathbb{C}}\to X
variant of Theorem
1
is called the
Nevanlinna version of the Hilbert property
in
[
CW20
]
, following
[
CZ17
, §2.4]
.
It is proved in
[
CW20
, 9.4]
for Galois covers. Also,

[
CW20
, 9.9]
shows that for every nontrivial
p:Y\to X
there is a

\phi_{Y}:{\mathbb{C}}\to X
that can not be lifted to
Y
, but the
\phi_{Y}
constructed there does depend on
Y
. See Example
9
for the difference between Galois covers and arbitrary covers in this context.

Background results

The first ingredients of the proof are
the following. They are not stated, but essentially proved in
[
FW05
]
and
[
CW20
, Sec.5]
; see especially
[
CW20
, 5.1]
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
X
be a smooth, proper variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
and fix any Riemannian metric on
X
.
Let
\psi_{i}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to X
be countably many holomorphic maps
and
\epsilon_{i}>0
. Then there is a holomorphic map

\phi:{\mathbb{D}}\to X
and embeddings
\tau_{i}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\lhook\joinrel\to{\mathbb{D}}
such that

\operatorname{dist}\bigl(\phi(\tau_{i}(z)),\psi_{i}(z)\bigr)<\epsilon_{i}

for every
z\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and every
i
. ∎

In the rationally connected case, we get a similar results for discs that come from rational curves.

Proposition 3
.

Let
X
be a smooth, proper, rationally connected variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
and fix any Riemannian metric on
X
.
Let
g_{i}:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to X
be countably many free rational curves,
\sigma_{i}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\lhook\joinrel\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
closed discs
and
\epsilon_{i}>0
. Set
\psi_{i}:=g_{i}\circ\sigma_{i}
. Then there is a holomorphic map

\phi:{\mathbb{C}}\to X
and embeddings
\tau_{i}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\lhook\joinrel\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\operatorname{dist}\bigl(\phi(\tau_{i}(z)),\psi_{i}(z)\bigr)<\epsilon_{i}

for every
z\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and every
i
. ∎

We also use the following Lefschetz-type result for surjectivity of

\pi_{1}
.

Proposition 4
.

[
HT85
,
Kol03
]

Let
X
be a smooth, proper variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Then there is a family of smooth curves

W\leftarrow C_{W}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u}}{{\to}}X

with the following property.

For every finite cover

p:Y\to X
there is a dense, open
W_{Y}\subset W

such that for every
w\in W_{Y}

(4.1)

the fiber product
C_{w}\times_{X}Y
is irreducible, smooth, and

(4.2)

C_{w}\times_{X}Y\to C_{w}
is étale iff
p
is étale.

(4.3)

Furthermore, if
X
is rationally connected, we can choose

C_{W}=W\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

Proof. If
X
is projective, then
the universal family of smooth curve sections with linear spaces works by

[
HT85
]
. In the proper case we use the images of linear space sections of a projective modification
X^{\prime}\to X
.

The rationally connected case is proved in
[
Kol03
, Cor.7]
.∎

We also need some lemmas that countably many maps suffice in Theorem
1
if
\dim X=1
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
S
be a Riemann surface with universal cover

\pi:{\mathbb{D}}\to S
. Let
p:S^{\prime}\to S
be a finite cover such that
\pi
lifts to

\pi^{\prime}:{\mathbb{D}}\to S^{\prime}
. Then
p
is unramified.

Proof. After base change to
{\mathbb{D}}
we have

\tilde{\pi}:{\mathbb{D}}\times_{S}{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}\times_{S}S^{\prime}
.
Here
{\mathbb{D}}\times_{S}{\mathbb{D}}
is a disjoint union of countably many copies of
{\mathbb{D}}
, and each one gives a section of the coordinate projection
\tilde{p}:{\mathbb{D}}\times_{S}S^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{D}}
.
Since the group of deck transformations acts transitively on the connected components of
{\mathbb{D}}\times_{S}S^{\prime}
, we see that
\tilde{p}
is unramified, and so is
p
. ∎

Corollary 6
.

Using the notation of Lemma
5
,
let
\pi_{m}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}(1-1/m)\to S
denote the restriction of

\pi
for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If infinitely many of the
\pi_{m}
lift to

\pi^{\prime}_{m}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}(1-1/m)\to S^{\prime}
, then
p
is unramified.

Proof. Fix
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{D}}
such that
p
is unramified over
\pi(z_{0})
.
Then
\pi^{\prime}_{m}(z_{0})
is the same point
z^{\prime}_{0}\in S^{\prime}
for infinitely many
m
.
These define a lifting
\pi^{\prime}:{\mathbb{D}}\to S^{\prime}
. So
p
is unramified by Lemma
5
. ∎

Lemma 7
.

There are countably many discs

\sigma_{j}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\lhook\joinrel\to{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
such that a cover
p:S^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is trivial iff every
\sigma_{j}
lifts to
\sigma^{\prime}_{j}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\lhook\joinrel\to S^{\prime}
.

Proof. Choose discs
\sigma_{j}
such that every finite subset of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is contained in the image of some
\sigma_{j}({\mathbb{D}})
. For example, we can use the interiors and exteriors of all circles
|z|=r
where either
r
or
r^{-1}
is a natural number.

For any
p:S^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
there is a
\sigma_{j}
whose image contains all branch points. Then
p^{-1}(\sigma_{j}({\mathbb{D}}))
is irreducible. So
\sigma_{j}
lifts iff

p
is an isomorphism over
\sigma_{j}({\mathbb{D}})
, hence everywhere. ∎

8
Proof of Theorem
1
.

We choose curves

C_{w_{*}}\to X
, discs
\tau_{*}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to C_{w_{*}}
and
\epsilon_{*}>0
as follows.

(8.1)

First we pick a dense set of points
w_{i}\in W
. (Zariski dense is sufficient.)

(8.2)

Next we choose discs
\tau_{ij}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to C_{w_{i}}
as in
Corollary
6
(resp. Lemma
7
).

(8.3)

Set
\epsilon_{k}=2^{-k}
.

We apply Propositions
2
–
3
to the triply infinite family of all

\tau_{ijk}:=\tau_{ij},\quad\epsilon_{ijk}:=\epsilon_{k}.

We claim that any resulting
\phi:{\mathbb{D}}\to X
(resp.
\phi:{\mathbb{C}}\to X
) works for Theorem
1
.

To see this, choose any finite, non-étale cover
p:Y\to X
.
By Proposition
4
and (
8
.1)
there is a
w_{i}
such that

C_{w_{i}}\times_{X}Y
is smooth, irreducible and

C_{w_{i}}\times_{X}Y\to C_{w_{i}}

is non-étale.

Next, by (
8
.2) there is a

\tau_{ij}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to C_{w_{i}}
that does not lift to
C_{w_{i}}\times_{X}Y
.

If
\phi
lifts to
\phi_{Y}:{\mathbb{D}}\to Y
(resp.
\phi_{Y}:{\mathbb{C}}\to Y
) then, fixing
i,j
and letting
k\to\infty
, a subsequence of the holomorphic maps

\phi_{Y}\circ\tau_{ijk}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to Y

converges to a lifting of
\tau_{ij}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to X
.
Thus we get a lifting of
\tau_{ij}
to
C_{w_{i}}\times_{X}Y
.
This is impossible by Corollary
6
(resp. Lemma
7
). ∎

The difference between lifting to Galois covers and arbitrary covers is illustrated by the following.

Example 9
.

Let
\phi:{\mathbb{D}}\to S
be a holomorphic map to a Riemann surface that is not simply connected.

(9.1)

If
\phi
is surjective and étale, then it can not be lifted to any non-étale, Galois cover
S^{\prime}\to S
.

(9.2)

If
\phi^{-1}(s)
is finite for some
s\in S
, then
\phi
can be lifted to some non-étale, non-Galois cover
S^{\prime}\to S
.

For example let
u:{\mathbb{D}}\to S
be the universal cover and consider the smaller discs

\overline{\mathbb{D}}(1-c)\subset{\mathbb{D}}
. We get

\phi_{c}:\overline{\mathbb{D}}(1-c)\to S
. Then every
\phi_{c}
can be lifted to some non-étale, non-Galois cover
S^{\prime}\to S
, but if
c\ll 1
then
\phi_{c}
can not be lifted to any non-étale, Galois cover
S^{\prime}\to S
.

Claim (2) shows that a feature of the proof of Theorem
1
—that the image of
\phi
returns to some neighborhoods infinitely many times—is necessary.

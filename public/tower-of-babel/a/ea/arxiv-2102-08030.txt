Abstract
. We use a unified method to give an isomorphism between direct sums of cyclotomic affine (and degenerate affine) Hecke algebras and cyclotomic BK-subalgebras which are some KLR-type algebras.

1.
Introduction

Brundan and Kleshchev
[
3
]
gave an explicit isomorphism between blocks of cyclotimic affine Hecke algebras of symmetric groups and cyclotomic KLR algebras of type A. But their proof is complicated since the invalidity of some rational functions.

On the other hand, Lusztig has showed that there is a natural isomorphism between affine Hecke algebras of all types and their graded versions
[
7
]
. One key point is the rationalization of affine Hecke algebras. Motivated by Lusztig’s work, we introduce the Lusztig extension of affine Hecke algebras of all types and the Brundan-Kleshchev subalgebra (or BK subalgebra for short) of the Lusztig extension. Surprisingly, we can construct the KLR-type generators of Lusztig extensions by following Brundan and Kleshchev, and then fast give an isomorphism between direct sums of blocks of cyclotomic affine (and degenerate affine) Hecke algebras and cyclotomic BK subalgebras.

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$j$}\in\mathcal{C}}\prod_{i\in I,i\neq j_{1}}(y_{1}+j_{1}-i)^{\Lambda_{i}}y_{1}^{\Lambda_{j_{1}}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})

is in
\langle y_{1}^{\Lambda_{j_{1}}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})\ |\ \text{\boldmath$j$}\in\mathcal{C}\rangle
. By Corollary (
3.48
),
\prod_{i\in I,i\neq j_{1}}[(x_{1}-i)^{\Lambda_{i}}]^{-1}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})
is in
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
, thus

\displaystyle y_{1}^{\Lambda_{j_{1}}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-j_{1})^{\Lambda_{j_{1}}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in I}(x_{1}-i)^{\Lambda_{i}}\prod_{i\in I,i\neq j_{1}}[(x_{1}-i)^{\Lambda_{i}}]^{-1}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})

is in
\langle\prod_{i\in I}(x_{1}-i)^{\Lambda_{i}}\rangle
. Therefore
\langle y_{1}^{\Lambda_{i_{1}}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\ |\ \text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}\rangle=\langle\prod_{i\in I}(x_{1}-i)^{\Lambda_{i}}\rangle
in

Thus
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}/\langle\prod_{i\in I}(x_{1}-i)^{\Lambda_{i}}\rangle.

∎

3.7.
Cyclotomic degenerate affine Hecke algebras

We define the
cyclotomic degenerate affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
as

\mathcal{H}(\Lambda):=\mathcal{H}/\langle\prod_{i\in I}(x_{1}-i)^{\Lambda_{i}}\rangle.

Similar to
[
3
, Subsection 3.1]
, there is a system
\{e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\ |\ \text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}\}
of mutually orthogonal idempotents in
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
such that
1=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{n}}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
and

\displaystyle e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\{h\in\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)\ |\ (x_{r}-i_{r})^{m}h=0\ \mbox{for all}\ r\in[n]\ \mbox{and}\ m\gg 0\}.

It is easy to check that
x_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\text{\boldmath$i$})x_{r}
for all
r\in[n]
and
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}
, and for a polynomial
f(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
,
f(x)e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
is a unit if and only if
f(\text{\boldmath$i$})\neq 0
. In particular, the element
x_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
is a unit in
e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
if and only if
i_{r}\neq 0
. In this case, we write
x_{r}^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
for the inverse.

Lemma 3.52
.

The following relations hold for all
r\in[n]
and
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{n}
.

(3.53)

t_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=\begin{cases}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})t_{r}&\text{if $i_{r}=0$},\\
e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))t_{r}+x_{r}^{-1}e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))-x_{r}^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})&\text{if $i_{r}\neq 0$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For any
s\in[n]
, the element

(\sigma_{r}(x_{s})-\sigma(\text{\boldmath$i$})_{s})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=[(x_{s}-i_{s})-a_{sr}(x_{r}-i_{r})]e(\text{\boldmath$i$})

is nilpotent by the nilpotency of
(x_{s}-i_{s})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
and
(x_{r}-i_{r})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
. Similarly, we can show that

\partial_{r}((x_{s}-i_{s})^{m})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
is nilpotent by the binomial theorem for an integer
m\gg 0
whenever
i_{r}=0
. Therefore, if
i_{r}=0
, by (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle(x_{s}-i_{s})^{m}t_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=t_{r}(\sigma_{r}(x_{s})-i_{s})^{m}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})+{\partial}_{r}((x_{s}-i_{s})^{m})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=0

when
m\gg 0
. Hence
t_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\in e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
and then
t_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\text{\boldmath$i$})t_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\text{\boldmath$i$})t_{r}.

If
i_{r}\neq 0
, by (
3.10
), we get that

\displaystyle(x_{s}-\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$})_{s})^{m}[t_{r}x_{r}+1]e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=[t_{r}x_{r}+1](\sigma_{r}(x_{s})-\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$})_{s})^{m}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=0

when
m\gg 0
. Therefore

\displaystyle t_{r}x_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})+e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))[t_{r}x_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})+e(\text{\boldmath$i$})]=e(\sigma(\text{\boldmath$i$}))t_{r}x_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$}).

Then right-multiplying by
x_{r}^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
, we obtain that

t_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))t_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})-x_{r}^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$}).

Similarly, we deduce that

e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))t_{r}=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))t_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})-x_{r}^{-1}e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$})).

Therefore
t_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))t_{r}+x_{r}^{-1}e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))-x_{r}^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$}).

∎

Let

e(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\in\mathcal{H}(\Lambda).

Then
e(\mathcal{C})
is a central idempotent in
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
by (
3.53
). Furthermore, by Lemma
3.50
, Corollary
3.48
and Lemma
3.52
, there is a homomorphism

\rho\colon\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)\to\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})

sending the generators
x_{r},t_{r}
to the same named elements, and
f(x)^{-1}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})
with
f(\text{\boldmath$i$})\neq 0
to
f^{-1}(x)e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
. Now we arrive at our first main result in this article for degenerate affine Hecke algebras.

Theorem 3.54
.

There is an algebra isomorphism
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})\cong\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Apparently,
\rho
is surjective, thus we only need to construct a left-inverse of
\rho
. By lemma
3.50
, there is a homomorphism

\tau\colon\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)\to\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)

sending the generators
x_{r},t_{r}
to the same named elements. Let
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}
and
\text{\boldmath$j$}\in I^{n}
. If
\text{\boldmath$i$}\neq\text{\boldmath$j$}
, then there is some
1\leq r\leq n
such that
j_{r}\neq i_{r}
. We claim that
\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=0
. In fact, by the construction of
e(\text{\boldmath$j$})
, there is an integer
m\gg 0
such that
(x_{r}-j_{r})^{m}e(\text{\boldmath$j$})=0
. So we have

(x_{r}-j_{r})^{m}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau((x_{r}-j_{r})^{m}e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=0.

The assumption
j_{r}\neq i_{r}
implies that the element
(x_{r}-j_{r})^{-1}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\in\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
. Thus we deduce that

\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=(x_{r}-j_{r})^{-m}(x_{r}-j_{r})^{m}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=(x_{r}-j_{r})^{-m}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})0=0.

Therefore, if
\text{\boldmath$j$}\in I^{n}\setminus\mathcal{C}
we have that
\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=0.
Moreover, if
\text{\boldmath$j$}\in\mathcal{C}
, we get that

\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{n}}\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$i$}))=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})\tau(1)=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$}).

These show that
\tau|_{\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})}\colon\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})\to\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
is an algebra homomorphism. It is easy to check that
\tau\rho
is the identity on each generator of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
. Thus
\rho
is an isomorphism.
∎

3.8.
The cyclotomic BK subalgbra revisited

Following
[
3
, Subsection 2.2]
, we introduce an algebra
\mathcal{R}
which is defined to be the
\Bbbk
-algebra
\mathcal{R}
generated by

subject to the relations (1)-(2) and (4)-(10) of Theorem

has a basis

.

We denote by
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda):=\mathcal{R}/\langle y_{1}^{\Lambda_{i_{1}}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})|\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}\rangle
. Then we have the following isomorphism of cyclotomic algebras.

Proposition 3.55
.

We have
\Bbbk
-algebra isomorphsim
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)\cong\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can also show that the elements
y_{r}
are nilpotent in
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. Thus if
f(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
with
f(0)\neq 0
, the polynomial
f(y)-f(0)
is nilpotent in
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
by the nilpotency of the elements
y_{r}
. So there exists some
g(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g(y)^{m}=0
and
f^{-1}(y)=f(0)^{-1}\sum_{l=0}^{m}g(y)^{l}
in
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. Therefore, the homomorphism
\mathcal{R}\hookrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\twoheadrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
is surjective and induces a surjective homomorphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)\to\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
. Let
\tilde{F}
be the localization of the commutative ring
\Bbbk[y]^{\mathcal{W}}
of
\mathcal{W}
-invariants in
\Bbbk[y]
with respect to
\{f\in\Bbbk[y]^{\mathcal{W}}\ |\ f(0)\neq 0\}
. Similar to the proof of (
2.2
), we can show that
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\cong\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\Bbbk[y]^{\mathcal{W}}}\tilde{F}
. Since the elements
y_{r}
are nilpotent in

\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
, similar to the proof above, we know that if
f(y)\in K[y]^{\mathcal{W}}
with
f(0)\neq 0
, then it is a unit in
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. Thus the homomorphism
\Bbbk[y]^{\mathcal{W}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{1}}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
induces a morphism
\pi_{2}\colon\tilde{F}\to\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. Therefore we have an induced algebra homomorphism
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\colon\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\to\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. The homomorphism
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}
induces an algebra homomorphism
\pi^{\prime}\colon\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)\to\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. It is easy to check that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are two-sided inverses. So
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)\cong\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
.
∎

By Theorem
3.54
and the Proposition above, we get a Brundan-Kleshchev type isomorphism for the degenerate affine Hecke algebra.

Corollary 3.56
.

We have
\Bbbk
-algebra isomorphism
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})\cong\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
.

Remark 4.48

Similar to the degenerate case, the elements

are all nilpotent in
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{q}(\Lambda)
.

4.8.
The cyclotomic non-degenerate affine Hecke algebras

We define the
non-degenerate cyclotomic affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)
as

\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda):=\mathcal{H}_{q}/\langle\prod_{i\in I}(X_{1}-q^{i})^{\Lambda_{i}}\rangle.

Similar to
[
3
, Subsection 4.1]
, there is a system
\{e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\ |\ \text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}\}
of mutually orthogonal idempotents in
\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)
such that
1=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{n}}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
and

e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)=\{h\in\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)\ |\ (X_{r}-q^{i_{r}})^{m}h=0\ \mbox{for all}\ r\in[n]\ \mbox{and}\ m\gg 0\}.

It is easy to see that
X_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\text{\boldmath$i$})X_{r}
for all
r\in[n]
and
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}
, and for a polynomial
f(X)\in\Bbbk[X]
,
f(X)e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
is a unit if and only if
f(q^{\text{\boldmath$i$}})\neq 0
. In particular, the element
(1-X_{r})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
is a unit in
e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)
if and only if
i_{r}\neq 0
. In this case, we write
(1-X_{r})^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
for the inverse.

Lemma 4.49
.

For
r\in[n]
and
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{n}
, there holds that

(4.50)

T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=\begin{cases}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})T_{r}&\text{if $i_{r}=0$},\\
e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}+\tfrac{1-q}{1-X_{r}}e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))-\tfrac{1-q}{1-X_{r}}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})&\text{if $i_{r}\neq 0$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For any
s\in[n]
, the element

(\sigma_{r}(X_{s})-q^{\sigma(\text{\boldmath$i$})_{s}})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=[X_{r}^{-a_{sr}}(X_{s}-q^{i_{s}})+(X_{r}-q^{i_{r}})f_{a_{sr}}(X_{r}^{\pm 1})]e(\text{\boldmath$i$})

for some polynomial
f_{a_{sr}}(X_{r}^{\pm 1})
. Thus it is nilpotent by the nilpotency of
(X_{s}-q^{i_{s}})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
and
(X_{r}-q^{i_{r}})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
. Similarly, we can show that

\mathrm{D}_{r}((X_{s}-q^{i_{s}})^{m})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
is nilpotent an integer
m\gg 0
whenever
i_{r}=0
. Therefore, if
i_{r}=0
, by (
4.10
), there holds that

\displaystyle(X_{s}-q^{i_{s}})^{m}T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})

\displaystyle=T_{r}(\sigma_{r}(X_{s})-q^{i_{s}})^{m}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})+(1-q)\mathrm{D}_{r}((X_{s}-q^{i_{s}})^{m})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})

\displaystyle=0

when
m\gg 0
. Therefore
T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\in e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)
and then

T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\text{\boldmath$i$})T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\text{\boldmath$i$})T_{r}.

If
i_{r}\neq 0
, by (
4.10
), we get

\displaystyle(X_{s}-q^{\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$})_{s}})^{m}[T_{r}(1-X_{r})+1-q]e(\text{\boldmath$i$})

\displaystyle=[T_{r}(1-X_{r})+1-q](\sigma_{r}(X_{s})-q^{\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$})_{s}})^{m}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})

\displaystyle=0

when
m\gg 0
. Therefore

\displaystyle T_{r}(1-X_{r})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})+(1-q)e(\text{\boldmath$i$})

\displaystyle=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))[T_{r}(1-X_{r})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})+(1-q)e(\text{\boldmath$i$})]

\displaystyle=e(\sigma(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}(1-X_{r})e(\text{\boldmath$i$}).

Then right-multiplying by
(1-X_{r})^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
, we have

T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})-(1-q)(1-X_{r})^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$}).

Similarly, we can deduce that

e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})-(1-q)(1-X_{r})^{-1}e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$})).

Therefore
T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}+\tfrac{1-q}{1-X_{r}}e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))-\tfrac{1-q}{1-X_{r}}e(\text{\boldmath$i$}).

∎

Let

e(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\in\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda).

Then
e(\mathcal{C})
is a central idempotent in
\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)
by (
4.50
). Furthermore, by Lemma
4.47
, (
4.50
) and Corollary
4.45
, there is a homomorphism

\rho_{q}\colon\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{q}(\Lambda)\to\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})

sending the generators
X_{r},T_{r}
to the same named elements, and
f(X)^{-1}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})
with
f(q^{\text{\boldmath$i$}})\neq 0
to
f(X)^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
. Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.54
, we arrive at our second main result in this paper for non-degenerate affine Hecke algebras.

Theorem 4.51
.

There is an algebra isomorphism
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{q}(\Lambda)\cong\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C}).

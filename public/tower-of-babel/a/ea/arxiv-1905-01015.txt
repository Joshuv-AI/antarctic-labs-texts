On the problem of Pillai with k k –generalized Fibonacci numbers and powers of 3 3
Mahadi Ddamulira, Florian Luca

Abstract
For an integer k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , let { F n ( k ) } n ≥ 2 − k \{F^{(k)}_{n}\}_{n\geq 2-k} be the k k –generalized Fibonacci sequence which starts with 0 , … , 0 , 1 0,\ldots,0,1 (a total of k k terms) and for which each term afterwards is the sum of the k k preceding terms. In this paper, we find all integers c c with at least two representations as a difference between a k k -generalized Fibonacci number and a power of 3 3 . This paper continues the previous work of the first author for the Fibonacci numbers, and for the Tribonacci numbers.

Introduction

The problem of Pillai states that for each fixed integer
c\geq 1
, the Diophantine equation

\displaystyle a^{x}-b^{y}=c,~~~~~\min\{x,y\}\geq 2,

(1)

has only a finite number of positive solutions
\{a,b,x,y\}
. This problem is still open; however, the case
c=1
, is the conjecture of Catalan and was proved by Mihăilescu
[
21
]
. In 1936 (see
[
22
,
23
]
), in the special case
(a,b)=(3,2)
which is a continutation of the work of Herschfeld
[
18
,
19
]
in 1935, Pillai conjectured that the only integers
c
admitting at least two representations of the form
2^{x}-3^{y}
are given by

\displaystyle 2^{3}-3^{2}=2^{1}-3^{1}=-1,~~~2^{5}-3^{3}=2^{3}-3^{1}=5,~~~2^{8}-3^{5}=2^{4}-3^{1}=13.

(2)

This was confirmed by Stroeker and Tijdeman
[
24
]
in 1982. The general problem of Pillai is difficult to solve and this has motivated the consideration of special cases of this problem. In the past years, several special cases of the problem of Pillai have been studied. See, for example,
[
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
15
,
16
]
.

Let
k\geqslant 2
be an integer. We consider a generalization of Fibonacci sequence called the
k
–generalized Fibonacci sequence
\{F_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\geqslant 2-k}
defined as

F_{n}^{(k)}=F_{n-1}^{(k)}+F_{n-2}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{n-k}^{(k)},

(3)

with the initial conditions

F_{-(k-2)}^{(k)}=F_{-(k-3)}^{(k)}=\cdots=F_{0}^{(k)}=0\quad{\text{\rm and }}\quad F_{1}^{(k)}=1.

We call
F_{n}^{(k)}
the
n
th
k
–generalized Fibonacci number. Note that when
k=2
, it coincides with the Fibonacci numbers and when
k=3
it is the Tribonacci number. The first
k+1
nonzero terms in
F_{n}^{(k)}
are powers of
2
, namely

F_{1}^{(k)}=1,\quad F_{2}^{(k)}=1,\quad F_{3}^{(k)}=2,\quad F_{4}^{(k)}=4,\ldots,F_{k+1}^{(k)}=2^{k-1}.

Furthermore, the next term is
F_{k+2}^{(k)}=2^{k}-1
.
Thus, we have that

F_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n-2}\quad{\text{\rm holds for all}}\quad 2\leq n\leq k+1.

(4)

We also observe that the recursion (
3
) implies the three–term recursion

F_{n}^{(k)}=2F_{n-1}^{(k)}-F_{n-k-1}^{(k)}\quad{\text{\rm for all}}\quad n\geq 3,

which can be used to prove by induction on
m
that
F_{n}^{(k)}<2^{n-2}
for all
n\geq k+2
(see also
[
5
]
, Lemma
2
).

The generalised Fibonacci analogue of the problem of Pillai under the same conditions as in (
1
), concerns studying for fixed
(k,\ell)
all values of the integer
c
such that the equation

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(k)}-F_{m}^{(\ell)}=c

(5)

has at least two solutions
(n,m)
. We are not aware of a general treatment of equation (
5
) (namely, considering
k
and
\ell
parameters), although the particular case when
\{k,\ell\}=\{2,3\}
was treated in
[
7
]
.

Ddamulira, Gómez and Luca
[
12
]
, studied the Diophantine equation

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(k)}-2^{m}=c,

(6)

where
k
is also a parameter, which is a variation of equation (
5
). They determined all integers
c
such that equation (
6
) has at least two solutions
(n,m)
.
These
c
together with their multiple representations as in (
6
) turned out to be grouped into four parametric families.

In this paper, we study a related problem and we find all integers
c
admitting at least two representations of the form
F_{n}^{(k)}-3^{m}
for some positive integers
k
,
n
and
m
. This can be interpreted as solving the equation

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(k)}-3^{m}=F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}-3^{m_{1}}~~~~(=c)

(7)

with
(n,m)\neq(n_{1},m_{1})
. The cases
k=2
and
k=3
have been solved completely by the first author in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
, respectively. So, we focus on the case
k\geqslant 4
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For fixed integer
k\geq 4
, the Diophantine equation (
7
) with
n>n_{1}\geq 2
and
m>m_{1}\geq 1
has:

(i)

solutions with
c\in\{-1,5,13\}
and
2\leq n\leq k+1
, which arise from the classical Pillai problem for
(a,b)=(2,3)
, namely:

\displaystyle F_{5}^{(k)}-3^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{3}^{(k)}-3^{1}~~=~-1,~~~k\geq 4,

\displaystyle F_{7}^{(k)}-3^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{5}^{(k)}-3^{1}~~=~~5,~~~k\geq 6,

\displaystyle F_{10}^{(k)}-3^{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{6}^{(k)}-3^{1}~~=~~13,~~~k\geq 9;

(ii)

solutions with
c\in\{-25,-7,5\}
and
n\geq k+2
and
k\in\{4,5,6\}
. Futhermore, all the representations of
c
in this case are given by

\displaystyle F_{8}^{(4)}-3^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{3}^{(4)}-3^{3}~~=~-25,

\displaystyle F_{10}^{(5)}-3^{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{3}^{(5)}-3^{2}~~=~-7,

\displaystyle F_{10}^{(6)}-3^{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{6}^{(6)}-3^{1}~~=~~5.

for
k=4,5
and
6
, respectively.

2
Preliminary Results

In this section, we recall some general results from algebra number theory and diophantine approximations
and properties of the
k
-generalized Fibonacci sequence.

2.1
Notations and terminology from algebraic number theory

We begin by recalling some basic notions from algebraic number theory.

Let
\eta
be an algebraic number of degree
d
with minimal primitive polynomial over the integers

a_{0}x^{d}+a_{1}x^{d-1}+\cdots+a_{d}=a_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x-\eta^{(i)}),

where the leading coefficient
a_{0}
is positive and the
\eta^{(i)}
’s are the conjugates of
\eta
. Then the
logarithmic height
of
\eta
is given by

h(\eta):=\dfrac{1}{d}\left(\log a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\log\left(\max\{|\eta^{(i)}|,1\}\right)\right).

In particular, if
\eta=p/q
is a rational number with
\gcd(p,q)=1
and
q>0
, then
h(\eta)=\log\max\{|p|,q\}
. The following are some of the properties of the logarithmic height function
h(\cdot)
, which will be used in the next sections of this paper without reference:

\displaystyle h(\eta\pm\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\eta)+h(\gamma)+\log 2,

\displaystyle h(\eta\gamma^{\pm 1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\eta)+h(\gamma),

(8)

\displaystyle h(\eta^{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|s|h(\eta)\qquad(s\in\mathbb{Z}).

2.2
k
-generalized Fibonacci numbers

It is known that the characteristic polynomial of the
k
–generalized Fibonacci numbers
F^{(k)}:=(F_{m}^{(k)})_{m\geq 2-k}
, namely

\Psi_{k}(x):=x^{k}-x^{k-1}-\cdots-x-1,

is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}[x]
and has just one root outside the unit circle. Let
\alpha:=\alpha(k)
denote that single root, which is located between
2\left(1-2^{-k}\right)
and
2
(see
[
13
]
).
This is called the dominant root of
F^{(k)}
. To simplify notation, in our application we shall omit the dependence on
k
of
\alpha
. We shall use
\alpha^{(1)},\dotso,\alpha^{(k)}
for all roots of
\Psi_{k}(x)
with the convention that
\alpha^{(1)}:=\alpha
.

We now consider for an integer
k\geq 2
, the function

\displaystyle f_{k}(z)=\dfrac{z-1}{2+(k+1)(z-2)}\qquad{\text{for}}\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

(9)

With this notation, Dresden and Du presented in
[
13
]
the following “Binet–like” formula for the terms of
F^{(k)}
:

\displaystyle F_{m}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{k}(\alpha^{(i)}){\alpha^{(i)}}^{m-1}.

(10)

It was proved in
[
13
]
that the contribution of the roots which are inside the unit circle to the formula (
10
) is very small, namely that the approximation

\left|F_{m}^{(k)}-f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{m-1}\right|<\dfrac{1}{2}\quad\mbox{holds~ for~ all~ }m\geqslant 2-k.

(11)

It was proved by Bravo and Luca in
[
5
]
that

\displaystyle\alpha^{m-2}\leq F_{m}^{(k)}\leq\alpha^{m-1}\qquad{\text{\rm holds for all}}\qquad m\geq 1\quad{\text{\rm and}}\quad k\geq 2.

(12)

Before we conclude this section, we present some useful lemma that will be used in the next sections on this paper. The following lemma was proved by Bravo and Luca in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Bravo, Luca)
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
\alpha
be the dominant root of
\{F^{(k)}_{m}\}_{m\geq 2-k}
, and consider the function
f_{k}(z)
defined in (
9
).

(i)

The inequalities

\dfrac{1}{2}<f_{k}(\alpha)<\dfrac{3}{4}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|f_{k}(\alpha^{(i)})|<1,\qquad 2\leq i\leq k

hold. In particular, the number
f_{k}(\alpha)
is not an algebraic integer.

(ii)

The logarithmic height of
f_{k}(\alpha)
satisfies
h(f_{k}(\alpha))<3\log k
.

2.3
Linear forms in logarithms and continued fractions

In order to prove our main result Theorem
1.1
, we need to use several times a Baker–type lower bound for a nonzero linear form in logarithms of algebraic numbers. There are many such in the literature like that of Baker and Wüstholz from
[
2
]
. We use the following result by Matveev
[
20
]
, which is one of our main tools in this paper.

Theorem 2.2

(Matveev)
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{t}
be positive real algebraic numbers in a real algebraic number field

\mathbb{K}
of degree
D
,
b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}
be nonzero integers, and assume that

\Lambda:=\gamma_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{t}^{b_{t}}-1

(13)

is nonzero. Then

\log|\Lambda|>-1.4\times 30^{t+3}\times t^{4.5}\times D^{2}(1+\log D)(1+\log B)A_{1}\cdots A_{t},

where

B\geq\max\{|b_{1}|,\ldots,|b_{t}|\},

and

A_{i}\geq\max\{Dh(\gamma_{i}),|\log\gamma_{i}|,0.16\},\qquad{\text{for all}}\qquad i=1,\ldots,t.

During the course of our calculations, we get some upper bounds on our variables which are too large, thus we need to reduce them. To do so, we use some results from the theory of continued fractions. Specifically, for a nonhomogeneous linear form in two integer variables, we use a slight variation of a result due to Dujella and Pethő (see
[
14
]
, Lemma 5a), which itself is a generalization of a result of Baker and Davenport
[
1
]
.

For a real number
X
, we write
||X||:=\min\{|X-n|:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
for the distance from
X
to the nearest integer.

Lemma 2.3

(Dujella, Pethő)
.

Let
M
be a positive integer,
p/q
be a convergent of the continued fraction of the irrational number
\tau
such that
q>6M
, and
A,B,\mu
be some real numbers with
A>0
and
B>1
. Let further

\varepsilon:=||\mu q||-M||\tau q||
. If
\varepsilon>0
, then there is no solution to the inequality

0<|u\tau-v+\mu|<AB^{-w},

in positive integers
u,v
and
w
with

u\leq M\quad{\text{and}}\quad w\geq\dfrac{\log(Aq/\varepsilon)}{\log B}.

The above lemma cannot be applied when
\mu=0
(since then
\varepsilon<0
). In this case, we use the following criterion of Legendre.

Lemma 2.4

(Legendre)
.

Let
\tau
be real number and
x,y
integers such that

\left|\tau-\frac{x}{y}\right|<\frac{1}{2y^{2}}.

(14)

Then
x/y=p_{k}/q_{k}
is a convergent of
\tau
. Furthermore,

\left|\tau-\frac{x}{y}\right|\geq\frac{1}{(a_{k+1}+2)y^{2}}.

(15)

Finally, the following lemma is also useful. It is Lemma 7 in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 2.5

(Gúzman, Luca)
.

If
m\geqslant 1
,
T>(4m^{2})^{m}
and
T>x/(\log x)^{m}
, then

x<2^{m}T(\log T)^{m}.

3
The connection with the classical Pillai problem

Assume that
(n,m)\neq(n_{1},m_{1})
are such that

F_{n}^{(k)}-3^{m}=F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}-3^{m_{1}}.

If
m=m_{1}
, then
F_{n}^{(k)}=F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}
and since
\min\{n,n_{1}\}\geq 2
, we get that
n=n_{1}
. Thus,
(n,m)=(n_{1},m_{1})
, contradicting our assumption. Hence,
m\neq m_{1}
, and we may assume without loss of generality that
m>m_{1}\geq 1
. Since

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(k)}-F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3^{m}-3^{m_{1}},

(16)

and the right–hand side of (
16
) is positive, we get that the left–hand side of
(
16
) is also positive and so
n>n_{1}
. Furthermore, since
F_{1}^{(k)}=F_{2}^{(k)}=1
, we may assume that
n>n_{1}\geq 2
.

We analyse the possible situations.

Case 1.

Assume that
2\leq n_{1}<n\leq k+1
. Then, by (
4
), we have

\displaystyle F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}=2^{n_{1}-2}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad F_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n-2}

so, by substituting them in (
16
), we get

\displaystyle 2^{n-2}-3^{m}=2^{n_{1}-2}-3^{m_{1}}.

By comparing with the classical solutions in (
2
), and by using the fact that
F_{n}^{(k)}
is a power of
2
if and only if
n\leq k+1
(see
[
6
]
), we get the solutions

\displaystyle F_{5}^{(k)}-3^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{3}^{(k)}-3^{1}~~=~-1,~~~k\geq 4,

\displaystyle F_{7}^{(k)}-3^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{5}^{(k)}-3^{2}~~=~~5,~~~k\geq 6,

(17)

\displaystyle F_{10}^{(k)}-3^{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{6}^{(k)}-3^{1}~~=~~13,~~~k\geq 9.

Case 2.

Assume
n\geq k+2
. The following lemma is useful.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
n\geq k+2
, the conditions

F_{n}^{(k)}-3^{m}=F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}-3^{m_{1}}\quad{\text{and}}\quad 2^{n-2}-3^{m}=2^{n_{1}-2}-3^{m_{1}}

cannot simultaneously hold.

Proof 3.2
.

If they do, then

2^{n-2}-F_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n_{1}-2}-F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}.

The sequence
\{2^{n-2}-F_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\geq 2}
is
0
at
n=2,3,\ldots,k+1
and is
1
at
n=k+2
. We show that from here on it is increasing. That is

2^{n-1}-F_{n+1}^{(k)}>2^{n-2}-F_{n}^{(k)}\quad{\text{\rm holds~for}}\quad n\geq k+2.

This is equivalent to

2^{n-2}>F_{n+1}^{(k)}-F_{n}^{(k)}=F_{n-1}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{n+1-k}^{(k)},

and this last inequality holds true because in the right–hand side we have
F_{i}\leq 2^{i-2}
for
i=n+1-k,n+2-k,\ldots,n-1
and then

\sum_{i=n-k+1}^{n-1}F_{i}\leq\sum_{i=n-k+1}^{n-1}2^{i-2}<1+2+\cdots+2^{n-3}<2^{n-2}.

4
Bounding
n
in terms of
m
and
k

By the results of the previous section, we assume that
n\geq k+2
. Thus,
2^{n-2}-3^{m}\neq 2^{n_{1}-2}-3^{m_{1}}
. Since
n>n_{1}\geq 2
, we have that
F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}\leq F_{n-1}^{(k)}
and therefore

F_{n}^{(k)}=F_{n-1}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{n-k}^{(k)}\geq F_{n-1}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{n-k-1}^{(k)}\geq F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{n-k-1}^{(k)}.

So, from the above, (
12
) and (
16
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F_{n-2}^{(k)}\leq F_{n}^{(k)}-F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}=3^{m}-3^{m_{1}}<3^{m},\text{ and }

(18)

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(k)}>F_{n}^{(k)}-F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}=3^{m}-3^{m_{1}}\geq 3^{m-1},

leading to

\displaystyle 1+\left(\dfrac{\log 3}{\log\alpha}\right)(m-1)<n<\left(\dfrac{\log 3}{\log\alpha}\right)m+4.

(19)

We note that the above inequality (
19
) in particular implies that
m<n<1.6m+4
.
We assume for technical reasons that
n>600
. By (
11
) and (
16
), we get

\displaystyle\left|f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}-3^{m}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|(f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}-F_{n}^{(k)})+(F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}-3^{m_{1}})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|(f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}-F_{n}^{(k)})+(F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}-f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n_{1}-1})+(f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n_{1}-1}-3^{m_{1}})\right|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}+\alpha^{n_{1}-1}+3^{m_{1}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\alpha^{n_{1}}+3^{m_{1}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2\max\{\alpha^{n_{1}},3^{m_{1}}\}.

In the above, we have also used the fact that
|f_{k}(\alpha)|<1
(see Lemma
2.1
). Dividing through by
3^{m}
, we get

\displaystyle\left|f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}3^{-m}-1\right|<2\max\left\{\dfrac{\alpha^{n_{1}}}{3^{m}},3^{m_{1}-m}\right\}<\max\{\alpha^{n_{1}-n+6},3^{m_{1}-m+1}\},

(20)

where for the right–most inequality in (
20
) we used (
18
) and the fact that
\alpha^{2}>2
.

For the left-hand side of (
20
) above, we apply Theorem
2.2
with the data

t:=3,\quad\gamma_{1}:=f_{k}(\alpha),\quad\gamma_{2}:=\alpha,\quad\gamma_{3}:=3,\quad b_{1}:=1,\quad b_{2}:=n-1,\quad b_{3}:=-m.

We begin by noticing that the three numbers
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
are positive real numbers and belong to the field
\mathbb{K}:=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, so we can take
D:=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]=k
. Put

\Lambda:=f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}3^{-m}-1.

To see why
\Lambda\neq 0
, note that otherwise, we would then have that
f_{k}(\alpha)=3^{m}\alpha^{-(n-1)}
and so
f_{k}(\alpha)
would be an algebraic integer, which contradicts Lemma
2.1
(i).

Since
h(\gamma_{2})=(\log\alpha)/k<(\log 2)/k
and
h(\gamma_{3})=\log 3
, it follows that we can take
A_{2}:=\log 2
and
A_{3}:=k\log 3
. Further, in view of Lemma
2.1
(ii), we have that
h(\gamma_{1})<3\log k
, so we can take
A_{1}:=3k\log k
. Finally, since
\max\{1,n-1,m\}=n-1
, we take
B:=n
.

Then, the left–hand side of (
20
) is bounded below, by Theorem
2.2
, as

\log|\Lambda|>-1.4\times 30^{6}\times 3^{4.5}\times k^{4}(1+\log k)(1+\log n)(3\log k)(\log 2)(\log 3).

Comparing with (
20
), we get

\min\{(n-n_{1}-6)\log\alpha,(m-m_{1}-1)\log 3\}<6.54\times 10^{11}k^{4}\log^{2}k(1+\log n),

which gives

\min\{(n-n_{1})\log\alpha,(m-m_{1})\log 3\}<6.60\times 10^{11}k^{4}\log^{2}k(1+\log n).

Now the argument is split into two cases.

Case 1.

\min\{(n-n_{1})\log\alpha,(m-m_{1})\log 2\}=(n-n_{1})\log\alpha
.

In this case, we rewrite (
16
) as

\displaystyle\left|f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}-f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n_{1}-1}-3^{m}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|(f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}-F_{n}^{(k)})+(F_{n_{1}}^{(k)}-f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n_{1}-1})-3^{m_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}+3^{m_{1}}\leq 3^{m_{1}+1}.

Dividing through by
3^{m}
gives

\displaystyle\left|f_{k}(\alpha)(\alpha^{n-n_{1}}-1)\alpha^{n_{1}-1}3^{-m}-1\right|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 3^{m_{1}-m+1}.

(21)

Now we put

\Lambda_{1}:=f_{k}(\alpha)(\alpha^{n-n_{1}}-1)\alpha^{n_{1}-1}3^{-m}-1.

We apply again Theorem
2.2
with the following data

t:=3,\quad\gamma_{1}:=f_{k}(\alpha)(\alpha^{n-n_{1}}-1),\quad\gamma_{2}:=\alpha,\quad\gamma_{3}:=3,\quad b_{1}:=1,\quad b_{2}:=n_{1}-1,\quad b_{3}:=-m.

As before, we begin by noticing that the three numbers
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
belong to the field
\mathbb{K}:=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, so we can take
D:=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]=k
. To see why
\Lambda_{1}\neq 0
, note that otherwise, we would get the relation
f_{k}(\alpha)(\alpha^{n-n_{1}}-1)=3^{m}\alpha^{1-n_{1}}
. Conjugating this last equation with any automorphism
\sigma
of the Galois group of
\Psi_{k}(x)
over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha^{(i)}
for some
i\geq 2
, and then taking absolute values, we arrive at the equality
|f_{k}(\alpha^{(i)})((\alpha^{(i)})^{n-n_{1}}-1)|=|3^{m}(\alpha^{(i)})^{1-n_{1}}|
. But this cannot hold because,
|f_{k}(\alpha^{(i)})||(\alpha^{(i)})^{n-n_{1}}-1|<2
since
|f_{k}(\alpha^{(i)})|<1
by Lemma
2.1
(i), and
|(\alpha^{(i)})^{n-n_{1}}|<1
, since
n>n_{1}
, while
|3^{m}(\alpha^{(i)})^{1-n_{1}}|\geq 3
.

Since

h(\gamma_{1})\leq h(f_{k}(\alpha))+h(\alpha^{n-n_{1}}-1)<3\log k+(n-n_{1})\dfrac{\log\alpha}{k}+\log 2,

it follows that

Global Well-Posedness of Full Compressible Magnetohydrodynamic System in 3D Bounded Domains with Large Oscillations and Vacuum
Yazhou C HEN, Yunkun C HEN, Xue W ANG [3mm] a. College of Mathematics and Physics, Beijing University of Chemical Technology, Beijing 100029, P. R. China;b. Anshun University, Anshun, 561000, P.R. China; c. School of Mathematical Sciences,University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049, P. R. China

Abstract
The three-dimensional (3D) full compressible magnetohydrodynamic system is studied in a general bounded domain with slip boundary condition for the velocity filed, adiabatic condition for the temperature and perfect conduction for the magnetic field. For the regular initial data with small energy but possibly large oscillations, the global existence of classical and weak solution as well as the exponential decay rate to the initial-boundary-value problem of this system is obtained. In particular, the density and temperature of such a classical solution are both allowed to vanish initially. Moreover, it is also shown that for the classical solutions, the oscillation of the density will grow unboundedly with an exponential rate when the initial vacuum appears (even at a point). Some new observations and useful estimates are developed to overcome the difficulties caused by the slip boundary conditions.

Introduction

We consider the motion of a viscous, compressible, and heat conducting magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flow in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which can be described by the full compressible MHD equations (see
[
16
]

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\rho u)=0,\\
(\rho u)_{t}+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\mathbb{S}+(\nabla\times H)\times H,\\
(\mathcal{E})_{t}+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(\rho Eu+Pu)=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\big((u\!\times H)\!\times H+H\!\times(\nu\nabla\!\times H)+u\mathbb{S}+\kappa\nabla\theta\big),\\
H_{t}-\nabla\times(u\times H)=-\nu\nabla\times(\nabla\times H),\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits H=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T]
,

is time, and

is the spatial coordinate.
\rho=\rho(x,t)
denotes the density,
u=(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})^{tr}(x,t)
the velocity,
\theta=\theta(x,t)
the temperature,
H=(H^{1},H^{2},H^{3})^{tr}(x,t)
the magnetic field;
\mathbb{S}
is the viscous stress tensor given by

\mathbb{S}=2\mu\mathbb{D}u+\lambda\mathop{\mathrm{div}}u\mathbb{I}_{3},

(1.2)

and
\lambda

\mu>0

, and
\mathbb{I}_{3}
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix;
\mathcal{E}
is the total energy given by

\mathcal{E}=\rho E+\frac{|H|^{2}}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad E=e+\frac{|u|^{2}}{2},

(1.3)

with

P=P(\rho,\theta)
is the pressure, the constant
\kappa>0

\nu>0

P=R\rho\theta,\quad e=\frac{R\theta}{\gamma-1},

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a simply connected bounded domain.
Note that for the classical solutions, the system (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\rho u)=0,\\
\rho(u_{t}+u\cdot\nabla u)+\nabla P=\mu\Delta u+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\mathop{\mathrm{div}}u+H\!\cdot\!\nabla H-\nabla\frac{|H|^{2}}{2},\\
\frac{R}{\gamma-1}\rho(\theta_{t}+u\cdot\nabla\theta)+P\mathop{\mathrm{div}}u=\kappa\Delta\theta+\lambda(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}u)^{2}+2\mu|\mathbb{D}(u)|^{2}+\nu|\mathop{\rm curl}H|^{2},\\

\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits H=0,\end{cases}

(1.5)

In addition, the system is solved subject to the given initial data

\displaystyle\big(\rho,\rho u,\rho\theta,H\big)(x,0)=\big(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}u_{0},\rho_{0}\theta_{0},H_{0}\big)(x),\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.6)

and slip boundary conditions

\displaystyle u\cdot n=0,\,\,\,{\rm curl}u\times n=0,

\displaystyle\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\nabla\theta\cdot n=0,

\displaystyle\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega,

(1.8)

\displaystyle H\cdot n=0,\,\,\,{\rm curl}H\times n=0,

\displaystyle\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle\displaystyle u\cdot n=0,\,\,(2\mathbb{D}u\ n)_{\tau}+\vartheta u_{\tau}=0\,\,\,\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle u\cdot n=0,\,\,\,{\rm curl}u\times n=-(Au)_{\tau}\,\,\,

We now sketch the main idea used in the proof of Theorem

(see (

However, the basic energy estimate can not be derived directly from the system (

)-(

) as that in

]
due to the slip boundary condition and non-conservation of temperature equation. More precisely, the basic energy equality is expressed as

\displaystyle\displaystyle E^{\prime}(t)+\int\left(\frac{\lambda({\rm div}u)^{2}+2\mu|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}+\nu|{\rm curl}H|^{2}}{\theta}+\frac{\kappa|\nabla\theta|^{2}}{\theta^{2}}\right)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu\int\left(2({\rm div}u)^{2}+|\omega|^{2}-2|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}\right)dx,

where the basic energy
E(t)
is defined by

E(t)=\int\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}+RG(\rho)+\frac{R}{\gamma-1}\rho\Phi(\theta)+\frac{1}{2}|H|^{2}\Big)dx,

with

is defined in (

).
Note that the right-hand term in (

) is sign-undetermined, thus it seems difficult to obtain directly the usual standard energy estimate

in
[

]
.
To deal with this difficulty, we first assume that

(see (

which will bring us some essential difficulties to obtain all the a priori estimates (see Proposition

). Fortunately, we get the uniformly positive lower and upper bounds on the average of the pressure

(see (

Hence we can replace

by

to overcome the difficulties caused by “weaker” basic energy estimate. For the magnetic field, with the help of the magnetic diffusivity structure, we obtain the estimates of

and

to control

and
\nabla^{2}H
, which can be used to deal with the strong coupling and interplay interaction between the fluid motion and the magnetic field, such as the magnetic force

and the convection term

.
Next, in order to estimate

, we adopt the ideas due to
[

,
3
]
to estimate the material derivatives

and

(see Lemma

and

more difficult. As in

plays an important role to estimate the integrals on the boundary

and
\dot{\theta}

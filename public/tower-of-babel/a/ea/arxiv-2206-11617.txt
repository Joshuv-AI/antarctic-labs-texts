Simple precession calculation for Mercury: a linearization approach
Michael J. W. Hall

Abstract
The additional precession of Mercury due to general relativity can be calculated by a method that is no more difficult than solving for the Newtonian orbit. The method relies on linearizing the relativistic orbit equation, is simpler than standard textbook methods, and is closely related to Newton’s theorem on revolving orbits. The main result is accurate to all orders in 1 c \tfrac{1}{c} for near-circular orbits.

II
Newtonian orbits

Newton’s orbit equation for a particle moving in a given plane about a gravitational centre of mass
M
is generally solved by writing it in the Binet form

\frac{d^{2}u}{d\phi^{2}}+u=\frac{GM}{h^{2}},

(1)

where

\phi
is the orbital angle relative to the perihelion,

u_{0}=\tfrac{GM}{h^{2}}
to any solution of the homogenous equation
\tfrac{d^{2}u}{d\phi^{2}}+u=0
, to give

\frac{1}{r}=u=u_{0}(1+e\cos\phi)

(2)

for some constant

to ensure perihelion at

The parameters

and

u_{0}=\frac{r_{\max}+r_{\min}}{2r_{\max}r_{\min}},\qquad e=\frac{r_{\max}-r_{\min}}{r_{\max}+r_{\min}}.

(3)

Thus observations of the perihelion and aphelion are sufficient to fix the orbit. One also has the useful relation

a:=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}(r_{\max}+r_{\min})=\frac{1}{2u_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{1-e}+\frac{1}{1+e}\right)=\frac{1}{u_{0}(1-e^{2})}.

(4)

In general relativity, the motion of a body orbiting a much larger mass
M

\frac{d^{2}u}{d\phi^{2}}+u=\frac{GM}{j^{2}}+\frac{3GM}{c^{2}}u^{2},

The central idea is to linearize the quadratic term in Eq. (

of

(u-\bar{u})^{2}\ll\bar{u}^{2}

\displaystyle u^{2}

\displaystyle=[\bar{u}+(u-\bar{u})]^{2}=\bar{u}^{2}+2\bar{u}(u-\bar{u})+(u-\bar{u})^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\bar{u}^{2}+2\bar{u}(u-\bar{u})

\displaystyle=2\bar{u}u-\bar{u}^{2},

\frac{d^{2}u}{d\phi^{2}}+Ku=L

(7)

(8)

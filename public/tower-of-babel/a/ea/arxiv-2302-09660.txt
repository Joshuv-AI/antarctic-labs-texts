On the Existence and Properties of Left Invariant k k -Symplectic Structures on Lie Groups with Bi-Invariant Pseudo-Riemannian Metric
Ait Brik Ilham, Mohamed Boucetta

Abstract
k k -symplectic manifolds are a convenient framework to study classical field theories and they are a generalization of polarized symplectic manifolds. This paper focus on the existence and the properties of left invariant k k -symplectic structures on Lie groups having a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric. We show that compact semi-simple Lie groups and a large class of Lie groups having a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric does not carry any left invariant k k -symplectic structure. This class contains the oscillator Lie groups which are the only solvable non abelian Lie groups having a bi-invariant Lorentzian metric. However, we built a natural left invariant n n -symplectic structure on SL ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(n,\hbox{\bb R}) . Moreover, up to dimension 6, only three connected and simply connected Lie groups have a bi-invariant indecomposable pseudo-Riemannian metric and a left invariant k-symplectic structure, namely, the universal covering of SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(2,\hbox{\bb R}) with a 2-symplectic structure, the universal covering of the Lorentz group SO ⁡ ( 3 , 1 ) \mathrm{SO}(3,1) with a 2-symplectic structure, and a 2-step nilpotent 6-dimensional connected and simply connected Lie group with both a 1-symplectic structure and a 2-symplectic structure.

Introduction

Symplectic geometry is the natural arena to develop classical mechanics.
The
k
-symplectic geometry is a generalization of the polarized symplectic geometry which was developed by A. Awane
[
3
]
, Awane and Goze
[
4
]
and C. Günther in
[
10
]
as an attempt to develop a convenient geometric framework to study classical field theories (see
[
12
]
). This geometry was also an attempt to formalize Nambu mechanic which is a generalization of Hamiltonian mechanics involving multiple Hamiltonians.

A
k
-symplectic manifold is a smooth manifold
M
of dimension
(k+1)n
endowed with an involutive vector subbundle
E\subset TM
and a family
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k})
of closed differential 2-forms such that:

\mathrm{rank}(E)=nk
, the family
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k})
is nondegenerate, i.e.,
\displaystyle\cap_{i=1}^{k}\ker\omega_{i}=\{0\}
and
E
is isotropic with respect all the
\omega_{i}
. A 1-symplectic manifold is a symplectic manifold endowed with a Lagrangian foliation. There are few known examples of
k
-symplectic manifolds and one way to build new one is to consider the class of left invariant
k
-symplectic structures on a connected Lie group
G
and their quotients by lattices. In this case, a left invariant
k
-symplectic structure on a connected Lie group
G
of dimension
(k+1)n
is equivalent to the data of a Lie subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of dimension
nk
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
and a family
\{\theta_{i}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*},i=1,\ldots,k\}
of nondegenerate 2-cocycle of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\theta_{i}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}})=0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. We call
(\mathfrak{g},{\mathfrak{h}},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k})
a
k
-symplectic Lie algebra. The determination of Lie groups having a left invariant
k
-symplectic structure is an open problem and their study is at its debut
[
2
,
1
]
.

In this paper, we study left invariant
k
-symplectic structures on Lie groups having a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric. This class of Lie group is very large and contains all semi-simple Lie groups. They became relevant some years ago when they were useful in the formulation of some physical problems, for instance in the so known Adler-Kostant-Symes scheme. More recently they appeared in conformal field theory
[
8
]
. One can see
[
13
]
, for a survey on the Lie algebras of Lie groups having a bi-invariant metric.

In Section
2
,
we show that compact semi-simple Lie groups and a large class of Lie groups having a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric does not carry any left invariant
k
-symplectic structure. This class contains the oscillator Lie groups which are the only solvable non abelian Lie groups having a bi-invariant Lorentzian metric. We end the section, by building a natural left invariant
n
-symplectic structure on
\mathrm{SL}(n,\hbox{\bb R})
. Moreover, In Section
3

\mathrm{SL}(2,\hbox{\bb R})
with a 2-symplectic structure, the universal covering of the Lorentz group
\mathrm{SO}(3,1)
with a 2-symplectic structure, and a 2-step nilpotent 6-dimensional connected and simply connected Lie group with both a 1-symplectic structure and a 2-symplectic structure.

Left invariant
k
-symplectic structures on quadratic Lie groups

A
k
-symplectic manifold is a smooth manifold
M
of dimension
(k+1)n
endowed with a vector subbundle
E\subset TM
and a family
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k})
of differential 2-forms such that:

(i)

E
is of rank
nk
and is involutive, i.e, for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(E)
,
[X,Y]\in\Gamma(E)
hence
E
defines a foliation on
M
,

(ii)

for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
d\omega_{i}=0
and the family
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k})
is nondegenerate, i.e.,
\displaystyle\cap_{i=1}^{k}\ker\omega_{i}=\{0\}
,

(iv)

E
is isotropic with respect all the
\omega_{i}
, i.e.,
\omega_{i}(E,E)=0
.

A 1-symplectic manifold is a symplectic manifold endowed with a Lagrangian foliation.

A left invariant
k
-symplectic structure on a Lie group
G
is a
k
-symplectic structure
(E,\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k})
on
G
such that
E
and the
\omega_{i}
are invariant by all the left multiplications of
G
. In particular,
{\mathfrak{h}}=E_{e}
is a Lie subalgebra of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
and
\omega_{1}(e),\ldots,\omega_{k}(e)
are 2-cocycles of
\mathfrak{g}
.

A
k
-symplectic Lie algebra is a real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of dimension
nk+n
with a subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of dimension
nk
and a family
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k})
of 2-forms satisfying:

(i)

The family
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k})
is nondegenerate, i.e.,
\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\ker\theta_{i}=\{0\}
,

(ii)

for
i=1,\ldots,k
,
\theta_{i}
is a 2-cocycle, i.e.,
\mathrm{d}\theta_{i}(u,v,w):=\theta_{i}([u,v],w)+\theta_{i}([v,w],u)+\theta_{i}([w,u],v)=0
,

(iii)

{\mathfrak{h}}
is totally isotropic with respect to
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k})
, i.e.,
\theta_{i}(u,v)=0
for any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and for any
i=1,\ldots,k
.

There is a correspondence between left invariant
k
-symplectic structures on a Lie group and
k
-symplectic structures on its Lie algebra.

Note that a symplectic Lie algebra is a Lie algebra endowed with a nondegenerate 2-cocycle and a 1-symplectic Lie algebra is a symplectic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\theta)
having a Lagrangian subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
, i.e.,
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}=\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathfrak{g}
and
\theta({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}})=0
.

Example 1
.

1.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an abelian Lie algebra of dimension
n(k+1)
,
(e_{pi},e_{i})_{1\leq p\leq k,1\leq i\leq n}
a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
and
(\omega^{pi},\omega^{i})_{1\leq p\leq k,1\leq i\leq n}
its dual basis. For any
\alpha\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, put

\theta_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega^{\alpha i}\wedge\omega^{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{h}}=\ker\omega^{1}\cap\ldots\cap\ker\omega^{n}.

Then
({\mathfrak{h}},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k})
is a
k
-symplectic structure of
\mathfrak{g}
. Thus any abelian Lie algebra of dimension
n(k+1)
has a
k
-symplectic structure.

2.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{2},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\}
be the 6-dimensional Lie algebra where the non vanishing Lie brackets are given by

\begin{cases}[f_{1},f_{2}]=-f_{1},\ [f_{1},f_{4}]=-af_{1},\ [f_{2},f_{3}]=f_{3},\ [f_{3},f_{4}]=-af_{3},\\
[f_{2},e_{1}]=-e_{1},\ [f_{2},e_{2}]=-c(af_{2}-f_{4}),\ [f_{4},e_{1}]=-ae_{1},\ [f_{4},e_{2}]=-b(af_{2}-f_{4}),\quad a,b,c\in\hbox{\bb R}\end{cases}

The triple

{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{span}\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\},\;\theta_{1}=f_{1}^{*}\wedge e_{1}^{*}+f_{2}^{*}\wedge e_{2}^{*}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\theta_{2}=f_{3}^{*}\wedge e_{1}^{*}+f_{4}^{*}\wedge e_{2}^{*}

defines a 2-symplectic structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
G
be a Lie group and
(\mathfrak{g}=T_{e}G,[\;,\;])
its Lie algebra. A pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
on
G
is called bi-invariant if its invariant by the left and the right multiplications of
G
. This is equivalent to
\langle\;,\;\rangle=h(e)
satisfying

\langle[u,v],w\rangle+\langle[u,w],v\rangle=0,

(1)

for any
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
. This means that, for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,

{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}
is skew-symmetric with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
where
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}(v)=[u,v]
.

A Lie group endowed with a bi-invariant metric will be called
quadratic
and a real Lie algebra endowed with a bilinear symmetric nondegenerate form satisfying (
1
) will be called a
quadratic Lie algebra
. A metric
\langle\;,\;\rangle
satisfying (
1
) will be called quadratic or invariant. There is a correspondence between connected quadratic Lie groups and quadratic Lie algebras so we will work at the level of Lie algebras.

There are three important classes of quadratic Lie algebra: semi-simple Lie algebras endowed with their Killing form,
T^{*}
-extensions of Lie algebras and quadratic Lie algebras obtained by the process of double extension (see
[
13
]
for more details).

(a)

Recall that a semi-simple Lie algebra is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that its Killing form given by
k(u,v)={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{v})
is nondegenerate. Since
k
is invariant,
(\mathfrak{g},k)
is a quadratic Lie algebra.

(b)

The
T^{*}
-extension of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra
T^{*}\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
where the Lie bracket is given by

[u+\alpha,v+\beta]=[u,v]+{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{*}\beta-{\mathrm{ad}}_{v}^{*}\alpha,\quad u,v\in\mathfrak{g},\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},

(2)

where
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{*}\alpha(v)=-\alpha([u,v])
.
The bilinear symmetric form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
on
T^{*}\mathfrak{g}
given by

\langle u+\alpha,v+\beta\rangle=\alpha(v)+\beta(u)

(3)

is nondegenerate and invariant and hence
(T^{*}\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic Lie algebra.

(c)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a quadratic Lie algebra. The double extension of
\mathfrak{g}
by the mean of
A\in\mathrm{so}(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
is the Lie algebra
\delta_{A}(\mathfrak{g})=\hbox{\bb R}e\oplus\mathfrak{g}\oplus\hbox{\bb R}\bar{e}
where the non vanishing Lie brackets and the metric are given, for any

, by

[\bar{e},u]=Au,\;[u,v]=\langle Au,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}e+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}},\;\langle xe+u+\bar{x}\bar{e},xe+u+\bar{x}\bar{e}\rangle=2x\bar{x}+\langle u,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}.

(\delta_{A}(\mathfrak{g}),[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic Lie algebra.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a quadratic Lie algebra. If

has a symplectic form

Proof.

It a consequence of the fact that the endomorphism

However, non nilpotent quadratic Lie algebras can carry a

Example 2

We consider
\mathrm{sl}(2,\hbox{\bb R})
with its basis

\left\{e_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\

Then

Let
(\mathfrak{g},{\mathfrak{h}},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k})
be a
k
-symplectic Lie algebra of dimension
n(k+1)

The linear map
\Theta:{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{g}/{\mathfrak{h}})^{*}\times\ldots\times(\mathfrak{g}/{\mathfrak{h}})^{*}

Suppose now that

carries an invariant metric

The fact that

is the orthogonal of

with respect to

In conclusion, a

-symplectic structure on a quadratic Lie algebra

of dimension

is given by a subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of dimension
nk
and a family of skew-symmetric derivations
(D_{1},\ldots,D_{k})
such that

\bigcap_{\alpha}\ker D_{\alpha}=\{0\}
and
D_{\alpha}({\mathfrak{h}})\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
for any
\alpha\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
.

Note that in a quadratic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle)

H^{2}(\mathfrak{g})\simeq\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathrm{so}(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle)/\{{\mathrm{ad}}_{u},u\in\mathfrak{g}\},

(4)

where
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
is the Lie algebra of derivations of

and
\mathrm{so}(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is the Lie algebra of skew-symmetric endomorphisms.

.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)

If

Put

Remark 1

Note that a quadratic Lie algebra

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a quadratic nilpotent Lie algebra of dimension

. If
\mathfrak{g}
admits a

-symplectic structure then

is abelian.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
carries a
n-1
-symplectic structure. Then there exists a
n-1
-dimensional Lie subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
, a family
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n-1}
of skew-symmetric derivations such that
D_{i}({\mathfrak{h}})\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
and
\bigcap_{i}\ker D_{i}=\{0\}
. Since
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a subalgebra and
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is quadratic then
[{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}]\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
. But
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}=1
and
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}
is nilpotent for any

and hence
[{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}]=\{0\}
. Now the facts that
D_{i}({\mathfrak{h}})\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
,
[{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}]=\{0\}
and

a derivation implies that

and for any

Let us state our first main result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a quadratic Lie algebra and
({\mathfrak{h}},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k})
a
k
-symplectic structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. If
{\mathfrak{h}}
is nondegenerate with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
then
([\theta_{1}],\ldots,[\theta_{k}])
are linearly independent in
H^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
. In particular, if
k>\dim H^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
then
{\mathfrak{h}}
is degenerate.

Proof.

For
\alpha=1,\ldots,k
,

\theta_{\alpha}(u,v)=\langle D_{\alpha}u,v\rangle

where
D_{\alpha}
is a skew-symmetric derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Put
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\alpha}=\bigcap_{\beta\not=\alpha}(\ker D_{\alpha}\cap{\mathfrak{h}})
. Then

{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{h}}^{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{k}

and
(D_{\alpha})_{|{\mathfrak{h}}^{\alpha}}:{\mathfrak{h}}^{\alpha}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
is an isomorphism. Suppose that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is nondegenerate and
([\theta_{1}],\ldots,[\theta_{k}])
are linearly dependent in
H^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then there exists
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\not=(0,\ldots,0)
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}
such that

{\mathrm{ad}}_{x}=a_{1}D_{1}+\ldots+a_{k}D_{k}.

Suppose that
a_{1}\not=0
and put
x=z+t
where
z\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and
t\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
. Since
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a subalgebra and the metric is invariant then, for any
h\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{1}
,
[h,{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}]\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
and hence

[x,h]=a_{1}D_{1}(h)=[z,h]+[t,h]=[t,h].

Then
t\not=0
and
(D_{1})_{|{\mathfrak{h}}^{1}}=\frac{1}{a_{1}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{t})_{|{\mathfrak{h}}^{1}}
. But

\langle[t,h],t\rangle=0
so
(D_{1})_{|{\mathfrak{h}}^{1}}({\mathfrak{h}}^{1})\subset t^{\perp}\cap{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
. But this is impossible since

\dim(t^{\perp}\cap{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp})=\dim\mathfrak{g}-1-\dim{\mathfrak{h}}=\dim{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}-1.

This completes the proof.
∎

a

-symplectic structure on

Theorem 2.2
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie algebra then
H^{2}(T^{*}\mathfrak{g})=0

Proof.

Recall that the quadratic structure of

is given by (
2
) and (

For
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and

and
\pi_{\#}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}

\prec\omega(x),y\succ=\omega(x,y)
and
\prec\beta,\pi_{\#}(\alpha)\succ=\pi(\alpha,\beta)
.
According to (
4
), we must show that any skew-symmetric derivation of
T^{*}\mathfrak{g}
is inner. Note first that for any

, we have

{\mathrm{ad}}_{x+\alpha}=\left(\begin{matrix}{\mathrm{ad}}_{x}&0\\
-(d\alpha)^{\flat}&{\mathrm{ad}}_{x}^{*}\end{matrix}\right),

where
d\alpha(u,v)=-\alpha([u,v])
and, for any skew-symmetric derivation
D
, there exists
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,
\pi\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and
D_{1}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that

D=\left(\begin{matrix}D_{1}&\pi_{\#}\\
\omega^{\flat}&-D_{1}^{*}\end{matrix}\right),

where
D_{1}^{*}
is the dual of
D_{1}
. One can see easily that
D
is a derivation if and only if
D_{1}
is a derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\omega
is a 2-cocycle of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\pi
is
{\mathrm{ad}}^{*}
-invariant, i.e., for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}

\pi({\mathrm{ad}}_{x}^{*}\alpha,\beta)+\pi(\alpha,{\mathrm{ad}}_{x}^{*}\beta)=0.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
is simple then
\omega=d\alpha
and
D_{1}={\mathrm{ad}}_{x}
. On the other hand, let
A=\pi_{\#}\circ\kappa^{\flat}
. Since both
\pi
and
\kappa
are
{\mathrm{ad}}
-invariant then
[A,{\mathrm{ad}}_{x}]=0
for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. The complexification
A^{\mathbb{C}}
of
A
is an endomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
which commutes with
{\mathrm{ad}}_{x}
for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
and any eigenspace of
A^{\mathbb{C}}
is an ideal which implies that
A=\lambda\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and since
A
is skew-symmetric then
\lambda=0
which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\delta_{A}(\mathfrak{g})=\hbox{\bb R}e\oplus\mathfrak{g}\oplus\bar{e}
be the double extension of an abelian quadratic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
with
A
non nilpotent and let
({\mathfrak{h}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}})
be an abelian quadratic Lie algebra. Then, for any skew-symmetric derivation
D
of
\delta_{A}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
(product of the quadratic Lie algebras
\delta_{A}(\mathfrak{g})
and
{\mathfrak{h}}
),
D(e)=0
. In particular,
\delta_{A}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
has no
k
-symplectic structure.

Proof.

Let
D
be a skew-symmetric derivation of
\delta_{A}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
. We have
Z(\delta_{A}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus{\mathfrak{h}})=\hbox{\bb R}e\oplus\ker A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
. Then
D(e)=\alpha e+z
where
z\in\ker A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
. For any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,

Du=\langle Du,\bar{e}\rangle e+D_{1}u+\langle Du,e\rangle\bar{e}+D_{2}u=\langle Du,\bar{e}\rangle e+D_{1}u+D_{2}u,

where
D_{1}u\in\mathfrak{g}
and
D_{2}u\in{\mathfrak{h}}
. Note that
D_{1}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is skew-symmetric with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
. We have

\displaystyle D[\bar{e},u]

\displaystyle=DAu=\langle DAu,\bar{e}\rangle e+D_{1}Au+D_{2}Au

\displaystyle=[D\bar{e},u]+[\bar{e},Du]

\displaystyle=\langle D\bar{e},e\rangle[\bar{e},u]+[D_{1}\bar{e},u]+AD_{1}u

\displaystyle=-\alpha Au+\langle D_{1}\bar{e},u\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}e+AD_{1}u.

So

\alpha A=[A,D_{1}].

On the other hand, for any
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle D[u,v]

\displaystyle=\langle Au,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}D(e)

\displaystyle=[Du,v]+[u,Dv]

\displaystyle=\left(\langle AD_{1}u,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}+\langle u,AD_{1}v\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}\right)e

\displaystyle=\langle[A,D_{1}]u,v\rangle e

\displaystyle=\alpha\langle Au,v\rangle e.

So
D(e)=\alpha e
. If
A
is not nilpotent, then there exists
n\geq 2
such that
{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{n})\not=0
. But from the relation
\alpha A=[A,D_{1}]
we can deduce that
\alpha{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{n})=0
and hence

D(e)=0
which completes the proof.
∎

We apply Theorem
2.3
to an important class of quadratic Lie algebras, namely, the oscillator Lie algebras.

The oscillator group named so by Streater in
[
14
]
, is a four-dimensional connected, simply connected
Lie group, whose Lie algebra (known as the oscillator algebra) coincides with the one generated
by the differential operators, acting on functions of one variable, associated to the harmonic
oscillator problem. The oscillator group has been generalized to any even dimension
2n\geq 4
, there are the only solvable Lie groups which carry an invariant Lorentzian metric.

For
n\in\hbox{\bb N}^{*}
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\hbox{\bb R}^{n}
with
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}
, the
\lambda
-oscillator group, denoted by
\mathrm{0sc}_{\lambda}
, is the Lie group which the underlying manifold
\hbox{\bb R}^{2n+2}=\hbox{\bb R}\times\hbox{\bb R}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
and product

(t,s,z).(t^{\prime},s^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\left(t+t^{\prime},s+s^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}Im\bar{z}_{j}exp(it\lambda_{j})z_{j}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{j}+exp(it\lambda_{j})z^{\prime}_{j},\ldots\right).

The Lie algebra of
\mathrm{0sc}_{\lambda}
, denoted by
\mathrm{osc}(\lambda)
, admits a basis
\hbox{\bb B}=\left\{e_{-1},e_{0},e_{i},\check{e}_{i}\right\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
where the brackets are given by

[e_{-1},e_{i}]=\lambda_{i}\check{e}_{i},\qquad[e_{-1},\check{e}_{i}]=-\lambda_{i}e_{i},\qquad[e_{i},\check{e}_{i}]=e_{0},

the unspecified brackets are either zero or given by antisymmetry.
We note that the center of
\mathrm{osc}(\lambda)
is
{Z}(\mathrm{osc}(\lambda))=\hbox{\bb R}e_{0}
and
[\mathrm{osc}(\lambda),\mathrm{osc}(\lambda)]=\mathrm{span}_{\hbox{\bb R}}\left\{e_{0},e_{i},\check{e}_{i}\right\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}.

For
x=x_{-1}e_{-1}+x_{0}e_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}e_{i}+y_{i}\check{e}_{i})
, the quadratic metric on
\mathrm{osc}(\lambda)
is given by

\langle x,x\rangle=2x_{-1}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}(x_{i}^{2}+y_{i}^{2}).

Hence
\mathrm{osc}(\lambda)
is the double extension of
\hbox{\bb R}^{2{n}}
by mean of
A=\mathrm{Diag}(A_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,A_{\alpha_{n}})
where
A_{\lambda_{i}}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&-\lambda_{i}\\
\lambda_{i}&0\end{matrix}\right).
So as a corollary of Theorem
2.3
we get the following result.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
({\mathfrak{h}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}})
be an abelian quadratic Lie algebra and
\mathrm{osc}(\lambda)\times{\mathfrak{h}}
endowed with the quadratic metric product. If
D

\mathrm{osc}(\lambda)\times{\mathfrak{h}}
then

. In particular, there is no
k
-symplectic structure on
\mathrm{osc}(\lambda)\times{\mathfrak{h}}
.

We end this section, by building a
n
-symplectic structure on

For any

.

(\mathrm{sl}(n,\hbox{\bb R}),{\mathfrak{h}},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n})

n
-symplectic Lie algebra.

Proof.

We denote by
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})

\hbox{\bb R}^{n}
and
(e_{1}^{*},\ldots,e_{n}^{*})
its dual basis. We have
\dim\mathrm{sl}(n,\hbox{\bb R})=(n-1)(n+1)

\displaystyle\prec e_{\alpha}^{*},[X,E_{i,j}](e_{n})\succ

be a degenerate 5-dimensional Lie subalgebra of
T^{*}\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
. Then
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)\geq 2
.

If
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)=3
then
\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)\subset{\mathfrak{h}}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\subset\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)^{\perp}=\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
. But
[{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}]\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
an hence
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
is an ideal in
\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
which is impossible.

If
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)=2
then
{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
is a subalgebra of
\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
. According to
[
7
, Proposition 5.2]
), up to an automorphism,
{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)=\mathrm{span}(e_{1},e_{3})
. So
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}+\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)=\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)\oplus\hbox{\bb R}e_{2}^{*}
. On the other hand,
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)=0
and hence
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}=\hbox{\bb R}(x+\alpha)
with
\alpha\not=0
. We have
\alpha(e_{1})=\alpha(e_{3})=0
so we can choose
\alpha=e_{2}^{*}
. Since
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\subset{\mathfrak{h}}
(
{\mathfrak{h}}
being degenerate), we must have also
\alpha(x)=0
. Thus
x=ae_{1}+be_{3}
. But
[{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}]\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
so

[e_{1},x]=mx\quad\mbox{and}\quad[e_{3},x]=nx
and hence
x=me_{1}
. Thus

{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{span}(e_{1},e_{3},-me_{2}+e_{1}^{*},e_{2}^{*},e_{3}^{*}).

But
[e_{3}^{*},-me_{2}+e_{1}^{*}]=me_{1}^{*}
and
me_{1}^{*}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
if and only if
m=0
. Which complete the first part of the proof.

2.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a 4-dimensional Lie degenerate subalgebra of
T^{*}\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
. We have three possibilities
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)=1,2
or 3. The same argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
shows that the case
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)=3
is impossible.

\bullet
Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}_{1}={\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
is a two dimensional subalgebra of
\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
. According to
[
7
, Proposition 5.1]
, we can suppose that
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathrm{span}(e_{1},e_{3})
. On the other hand,
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)=6-\dim({\mathfrak{h}}+\dim\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2))=1
. Denote by
\alpha
a generator of
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
. Then
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\alpha^{\perp}=\alpha^{0}\oplus\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)^{*}
where
\alpha^{0}
is the annihilator of
\alpha
. This implies that
\mathfrak{g}_{2}={\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)^{*}
is 2-dimensional. So finally,
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is invariant by
{\mathrm{ad}}_{e_{1}}^{*}
and
{\mathrm{ad}}_{e_{3}}^{*}
. In the dual basis, we have

{\mathrm{ad}}_{e_{1}}^{*}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
-2&0&0\end{array}\right]\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathrm{ad}}_{e_{3}}^{*}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}2&0&0\\
0&-2&\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right].

We have
{\mathrm{ad}}_{e_{1}}^{*}
leaves invariant
\mathrm{span}(e_{2}^{*},e_{3}^{*})
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
so it leaves invariant their intersection and since 0 is the only real eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{ad}}_{e_{1}}^{*}
, we get that
e_{2}^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. We have also that

{\mathrm{ad}}_{e_{3}}^{*}
leaves invariant
\mathrm{span}(e_{1}^{*},e_{3}^{*})
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
so it leaves invariant their intersection and hence either
e_{1}^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
or
e_{3}^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Finally,

{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{3},e_{1}^{*},e_{2}^{*}\}\quad\mbox{or}\quad{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{3},e_{2}^{*},e_{3}^{*}\}.

\bullet

\dim{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)=1
. According to Lemma
3.1
, we can suppose that a generator of
{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
is either
ae_{1}+be_{2}
or
e_{3}
with
(a,b)\not=0
.

Suppose that
a\not=0
and hence we can choose
e_{1}+be_{2}
as a generator of
{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
. We must have
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\cap\mathfrak{su}(2)=\{0\}
and hence there exists
\alpha_{1}\not=0,\alpha_{2}\not=0
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{su}(2)

such that

{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}=\mathrm{span}\left(x_{1}+\alpha_{1},x_{2}+\alpha_{2}\right).

We must have
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
linearly independent. We have
\alpha_{1}(e_{1}+be_{2})=\alpha_{2}(e_{1}+be_{2})=0
and hence we can choose
\alpha_{1}=-be_{1}^{*}+e_{2}^{*}
and
\alpha_{2}=e_{3}^{*}
. Now the fact
{\mathrm{ad}}_{e_{1}}
leaves
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
invariant implies that

\begin{cases}[e_{1},x_{1}]+[e_{1},e_{2}^{*}]-b[e_{1},e_{1}^{*}]=[e_{1},x_{1}]-2be_{3}^{*}=\mu_{1}(x_{1}+e_{2}^{*}-be_{1}^{*})+\nu_{1}(x_{2}+e_{3}^{*}),\\
[e_{1},x_{2}]+[e_{1},e_{3}^{*}]=[e_{1},x_{2}]-e_{2}^{*}=\mu_{2}(x_{1}+e_{2}^{*}-be_{1}^{*})+\nu_{2}(x_{2}+e_{3}^{*}),\\
\end{cases}

So
b=0
and

[e_{1},x_{1}]=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad[e_{1},x_{2}]=-x_{1}.

This is equivalent to
x_{1}=\alpha e_{1}
and
x_{2}=\frac{\alpha}{2}e_{3}+\beta e_{1}
and hence

{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}=\{\alpha e_{1}+e_{2}^{*},\beta e_{1}+\frac{\alpha}{2}e_{3}+e_{3}^{*}\}

Then

{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{span}\left(e_{1},e_{2}^{*},ae_{2}-e_{1}^{*},\frac{a}{2}e_{3}-e_{3}^{*}\right).

An one can see that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a subalgebra if and only if
a=0
. Hence
{\mathfrak{h}}=\hbox{\bb R}e_{1}\oplus\mathrm{sl}(2,\hbox{\bb R})^{*}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}=\mathrm{span}(e_{2}^{*},e_{3}^{*})
.

Suppose that
e_{3}
is a generator of
{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)
. Then
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\cap\mathrm{sl}(\hbox{\bb R},2)=\{0\}
and there exists
\alpha_{1}\not=0,\alpha_{2}\not=0
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathfrak{su}(2)

such that

{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}=\mathrm{span}\left(x_{1}+\alpha_{1},x_{2}+\alpha_{2}\right).

We must have
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
linearly independent. We have
\alpha_{1}(e_{3})=\alpha_{2}(e_{3})=0
and hence we can choose
\alpha_{1}=e_{1}^{*}
and
\alpha_{2}=e_{2}^{*}
. Now the fact
{\mathrm{ad}}_{e_{3}}
leaves
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
invariant implies that

\begin{cases}[e_{3},x_{1}]+[e_{3},e_{1}^{*}]=[e_{1},x_{1}]-2e_{1}^{*}=\mu_{1}(x_{1}+e_{1}^{*})+\nu_{1}(x_{2}+e_{2}^{*}),\\
[e_{3},x_{2}]+[e_{3},e_{2}^{*}]=[e_{1},x_{2}]+2e_{2}^{*}=\mu_{2}(x_{1}+e_{1}^{*})+\nu_{2}(x_{2}+e_{2}^{*}),\\
\end{cases}

So

[e_{3},x_{1}]=-2x_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad[e_{3},x_{2}]=2x_{2}.

So
x_{1}=\alpha e_{2}
and
x_{2}=\beta e_{1}
. So

{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}=\{\alpha e_{2}+e_{1}^{*},\beta e_{1}+e_{2}^{*}\}

Hence
{\mathfrak{h}}=\hbox{\bb R}e_{3}\oplus\mathrm{sl}(2,\hbox{\bb R})^{*}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}=\mathrm{span}(e_{2}^{*},e_{3}^{*})
.∎

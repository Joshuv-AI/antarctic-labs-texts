Degree conditions forcing directed cycles Thanks: This work was partially supported by the National Science Centre grant 2016/21/D/ST1/00998 and the ERC Consolidator Grant LaDIST 648509.
Andrzej Grzesik, Jan Volec

Abstract
Caccetta–Häggkvist conjecture is a longstanding open problem on degree conditions that force an oriented graph to contain a directed cycle of a bounded length. Motivated by this conjecture, Kelly, Kühn, and Osthus initiated a study of degree conditions forcing the containment of a directed cycle of a given length. In particular, they found the optimal minimum semidegree, that is, the smaller of the minimum indegree and the minimum outdegree, which forces a large oriented graph to contain a directed cycle of a given length not divisible by 3 3 , and conjectured the optimal minimum semidegree for all the other cycles except the directed triangle. In this paper, we establish the best possible minimum semidegree that forces a large oriented graph to contain a directed cycle of a given length divisible by 3 3 yet not equal to 3 3 , hence fully resolve the conjecture by Kelly, Kühn, and Osthus. We also find an asymptotically optimal semidegree threshold of any cycle with a given orientation of its edges with the sole exception of a directed triangle.

Introduction

One of the most famous open problems in graph theory is the conjecture of Caccetta and Häggkvist from 1978.

Conjecture 1.1

(Caccetta–Häggkvist
[
6
]
)
.

Every oriented graph
G
on
n
vertices with minimum outdegree
\delta^{+}(G)\geq\frac{n}{\ell}
contains a directed cycle of length at most
\ell
.

By an oriented graph, we understand a directed graph without loops and multiple edges.
Since the time the conjecture was stated, it attracted a great amount of researchers.
In particular, it was the topic of a 2006 workshop at the American Institute of Mathematics.
Partial results on
Conjecture
1.1
include its proofs for large values of
\ell

[
6
,
14
,
17
,
33
]
, results with an additive error term in the bound for the cycle length
[
7
,
28
,
34
]
and a multiplicative error term in the minimum outdegree assumption
[
5
,
6
,
12
,
16
,
25
,
32
]
, as well as solutions with an additional assumption on forbidden subgraphs
[
11
,
30
]
.
For more results and problems related to the Caccetta–Häggkvist conjecture, see a summary of Sullivan
[
35
]
.

A weaker conjecture, where
\frac{n}{\ell}
lower bounds the minimum semidegree, that is, the smaller of the minimum outdegree and the minimum indegree, is also widely open and is very closely related to a conjecture of Behzad, Chartrand, and Wall
[
4
]
from 1970 on directed cages, that is, directed graphs that are regular and have a prescribed girth.

Motivated by these open problems, Kelly, Kühn, and Osthus
[
21
]
conjectured the minimum semidegree guaranteeing existence of a directed cycle of a given length.

Conjecture 1.2

(Kelly–Kühn–Osthus
[
21
]
)
.

For every
\ell\geq 4
there exists
n_{0}:=n_{0}(\ell)
such that every oriented graph
G
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta^{\pm}(G)\geq\frac{n}{k}+\frac{1}{k}
contains a directed cycle of length exactly
\ell
,
where
k
is the smallest integer greater than
2
that does not divide
\ell
.

Observe that the conjectured semidegree threshold, if true, would be the best possible.
Indeed, consider a so-called balanced blow-up of
C_{k}
, that is, an oriented graph on
n=b\cdot k
vertices split into
k
cyclically ordered parts each of size
b
, where the edges go from all the vertices of a given part to all the vertices of the next part.
The considered graph has no
\ell
-cycles and both the indegree and the outdegree of all its vertices equals to
b=\frac{n}{k}
.

In their paper, Kelly, Kühn and Osthus proved
Conjecture
1.2
for
k=3
, that is, when the cycle length is not divisible by
3
.
Moreover, they showed that the corresponding bound
(n+1)/3
on the minimum semidegree force any cycle of a fixed length
\ell\geq 4
.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
21
]
)
.

For every
\ell\geq 4
there exists
n_{0}:=n_{0}(\ell)
such that every oriented graph
G
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta^{\pm}(G)\geq\frac{n}{3}+\frac{1}{3}
contains a directed cycle of length exactly
\ell
.

Note that if
Conjecture
1.1
is true for
\ell=3
, then the bound
\frac{n+1}{3}
on the minimum semidegree of a sufficiently large
n
-vertex oriented graph guarantees existence of any directed cycle of a fixed length.
In 1978, Thomassen
[
38
]
actually conjectured that
n/3
should also be the semidegree threshold for containing a Hamilton cycle, that is, a cycle going through all the vertices.
However, this was disproved about 15 years later by Häggkivst
[
15
]
who presented non-Hamiltonian
n
-vertex graphs with the minimum semidegree
\left\lceil\frac{3n-4}{8}\right\rceil-1
.
Building on an asymptotic solution of Kelly, Kühn, and Osthus
[
20
]
, Keevash, Kühn, and Osthus
[
19
]
proved that any sufficiently large
n
-vertex graph with the minimum semidegree
\left\lceil\frac{3n-4}{8}\right\rceil
contains a Hamilton cycle.
In fact, the results in
[
19
,
21
]
yield that
\left\lceil\frac{3n-4}{8}\right\rceil
is the semidegree threshold for an oriented graph on
n
vertices to be
pancyclic
, that is, contain directed cycles of every length between
3
and
n
.

One of the natural ways to tackle
Conjecture
1.2
is to consider its asymptotic relaxation, that is, adding an extra
o(n)
term to the minimum semidegree assumption.
In
[
21
]
, Kelly, Kühn, and Osthus proved
Conjecture
1.2
asymptotically for
k\in\{4,5\}
with an additional assumption on the cycle lengths. Specifically, they assumed
\ell\geq 42
and
\ell\geq 2556
when
k=4
and
k=5
, respectively.
This was later extended by Kühn, Osthus, and Piguet
[
24

to an asymptotic version of
Conjecture

for any
k\geq 7
and
\ell\geq 10^{7}k^{6}
.

Our results

In this paper, we determine the exact value of the semidegree threshold of every directed cycle of length

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix an integer
\ell\geq 4
and let
k
be the smallest integer greater than

that does not divide
\ell
.
There exists
n_{0}:=n_{0}(\ell)
such that the following is true for every oriented graph

•

if
\ell\not\equiv 3
mod

\delta^{\pm}(G)\geq\frac{n}{k}+\frac{k-1}{2k}
, then

contains
C_{\ell}
, and

•

if
\ell\equiv 3
mod
12

\delta^{\pm}(G)\geq\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{4}
, then
G
contains
C_{\ell}
.

Moreover, for every
\ell\geq 4
the stated bound is the best possible.

In particular, the threshold conjectured in
[

]
is correct only when either

mod

.

When

, that is, when

Theorem 1.5
.

Fix an integer
\ell
that is divisible by

that does not divide
\ell
.
There exists

.

Observe now that

cannot hold in this form when

Indeed, any balanced orientation of

yields an oriented graph

with semidegree

yet every closed walk in

has an even length.
Nevertheless, when

and

, we prove the following stability-type result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Fix an integer
\ell\geq 9
that is divisible by
3
and not divisible by
4
.
Every oriented graph
H
on
n
vertices with
\delta^{\pm}(H)\geq\frac{n}{4}-o(n)
that does not contain a closed walk of length

can be made bipartite by removing
o(n)
vertices.

The case of
C_{6}
is even more exceptional.
Consider a balanced blow-up of

and place inside one of the blobs an arbitrary

Clearly, the obtained graph is

-free.
However, if

is a one-way oriented

then one must remove

vertices to make the resulting graph bipartite.

2
Notation and Preliminaries

Unless explicitly stated, all the graphs considered in this paper are
oriented graphs
, that is, directed graphs without loops and multiple edges (regardless of the direction).
Given a graph
G
, we write
V(G)
and
E(G)
to denote its vertex-set and its edge-set, respectively.
For two vertices
u\in V(G)
and
w\in V(G)
, we write
uw
to denote the edge oriented from
u
to
w
.
Note that if
uw\in E(G)
then
wu\notin E(G)
, and vice versa.

For a graph
G
and a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we denote by
N_{G}^{+}(v)
and
N_{G}^{-}(v)
the
outneighborhood
and the
inneighborhood
of
v
, respectively.
If the graph
G
is known from the context, we omit the subscript and simply write
N^{+}(v)
and
N^{-}(v)
.
More generally, for
S\subseteq V(G)
, we define
N^{+}(S):=\bigcup_{v\in S}N^{+}(v)
and
N^{-}(S):=\bigcup_{v\in S}N^{-}(v)
.

The sizes of the outneighborhood and the inneighborhood of a vertex
v
are referred as the
outdegree
and the
indegree
of
v
,
and we write
\delta^{+}(G)
and
\delta^{-}(G)
to denote the minimum outdegree and the minimum indegree over all the vertices of
G
, respectively.
By the
minimum semidegree

\delta^{\pm}(G)
we mean the minimum of
\delta^{+}(G)
and
\delta^{-}(G)
.

Whenever we write a
path
or a
walk
of length
\ell
, we always mean a directed path or a directed walk with
\ell
edges.
For brevity, we write
C_{\ell}
or
\ell
-cycle to denote a directed cycle of length
\ell
, and
P_{\ell}
to denote a directed path of length
\ell
.
Given an integer
s\geq 2
, the
s
-shortcut
is a path of length
s
with an additional edge from the first vertex to the last one, that is, the graph obtained from
C_{s+1}
after changing the orientation of one edge, see
Figure
1
.
We call its unique vertex of outdegree
2
the
source
, and the unique vertex of indegree
2
the
sink
.

Figure 1:
The
2
-shortcut, the
3
-shortcut, the
4
-shortcut and a general
s
-shortcut.

Throughout the paper, we use the standard Bachmann–Landau notation: for two functions
f
and
g
from
\mathbb{N}
to
\mathbb{R}
, we write
f(n)=o(g(n))
if
\lim\frac{f(n)}{g(n)}=0
.
With a slight abuse of notation, we also write
f(n)\geq o(g(n))
to denote that
\liminf\frac{f(n)}{g(n)}\geq 0
.

We say that a graph
F
is
homomorphic
to a graph
H
if there exists a map
f:V(F)\to V(H)
that preserves the adjacencies, that is, if
uv\in E(F)
then
f(u)f(v)\in E(H)
.
A
blow-up
of a graph
H
is a graph obtained from
H
by placing an independent set
I_{v}
for every
v\in V(H)
, and, for every
uv\in E(H)
, connecting
x
to
y
for all
x\in I_{u}
and
y\in I_{v}
.
In case
|I_{v}|=b
for every
v\in V(G)
, we call the obtained graph a
b
-blow-up
of
G
, or a
balanced blow-up
of
G
.
Note that a graph
F
is homomorphic to
H
if and only if
F
is a subgraph of some blow-up of
H
.

For a fixed graph
F
, we say that a graph
G
is
F
-free if
G
has no (not necessarily induced) subgraph isomorphic to
F
.
More generally, if
\mathcal{F}
is a fixed family of graphs, we say that
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free if
G
is
F
-free for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

One of the most important and powerful results in graph theory is the
Regularity Lemma
introduced in 1976 by Szemerédi
[
37
]
; see also
[
23
,
31
]
for excellent surveys on the topic.
A well known application of the regularity method is the so-called

[
9
]
.
Here we state its degree-form for oriented graphs, which can be derived, for example, using the regularity lemma for directed graphs of Alon and Shapira
[

]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix an
\ell
-vertex oriented graph
F
.
If an
n
-vertex oriented graph

contains
o(n^{\ell})
copies of

, then
G
contains an
F
-free subgraph

with
|E(G)|-o(n^{2})
edges.
Moreover, one can remove

vertices from

to get an

-free

with
\delta^{\pm}(H^{\prime})=\delta^{\pm}(G)-o(n)
.

2.1
Caccetta–Häggkvist conjecture

In order to prove

As we stated in the introduction, the conjecture has been extensively studied and various relaxations has been considered.
A particular interest is given to the so-called triangle case of the conjecture, that is, when

The triangle case of the conjecture states that every oriented graph

The original paper of Caccetta–Häggkvist

.
After a series of improvements due to Bondy
[

]
, Shen
[

]
and Hamburger, Haxell and Kostochka
[

]
as well as

[

]
applied the flag algebra method of Razborov
[

]
and established the following:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
16
]
)
.

Every

-vertex oriented graph

with
\delta^{+}(G)\geq 0.3465n
contains

.

Note that De Joannis de Verclos, Sereni and the second author
[

]
established an improvement to

; however, for our purposes the bound from
[
16
]
will be sufficient.

Following an idea of Shen
[
34
]
*Theorem 1, we use

to obtain a bound for longer cycle lengths in

.
The obtained bound is rather weak, nevertheless it will be sufficient for our purposes.

Lemma 2.3
.

Every

-vertex oriented graph

\delta^{+}(G)\geq(1-0.6535^{1/m})n

.

Proof.

Let

For the sake of a contradiction, let

be a counterexample with the smallest number of vertices

.
We have that
\delta^{+}(G)\geq cn
,

contains no directed cycle of length at most
3m
, but any graph

on

Consider an arbitrary vertex

X_{1}:=N^{+}(x)
and
X_{i+1}:=X_{i}\cup N^{+}(X_{i})
.
Clearly, for any

contains no directed cycle of length at most

Since it has less vertices than graph

, there exists

such that

Thus,

|X_{i+1}|>|X_{i}|+|N^{+}(x_{i})|-c|X_{i}|\geq cn+(1-c)|X_{i}|.

Since
|X_{1}|\geq cn
, we obtain that for every
1<i\leq m
,

|X_{i}|>(1-(1-c)^{i})n.

In particular,

|X_{m}|>(1-(1-c)^{m})n=0.3465n.

(1)

Therefore, from any vertex of

there is a path of length at most
m
to at least

Consider an auxiliary directed graph

on the same set of vertices as

to vertex

contains a path from

to

.
Clearly, if

, then

since

contains no directed cycle of length at most
2m
.
Also, by (

), the graph

has the minimum outdegree

However,

yields a directed triangle in

2.2
Additive number theory

In order to concatenate different closed walks together, we use Kneser’s theorem, a generalization of the classical Cauchy–Davenport theorem.

Theorem 2.7

(Kneser’s theorem
[
22
]
, see also
[
10
]
)
.

If
A
and
B
are nonempty subsets of an additive group
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, then

|A+B|\geq|A+\Stab(A+B)|+|B+\Stab(A+B)|-|\Stab(A+B)|,

where
\Stab(A+B)=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}:x+A+B=A+B\}
.

The main usage of Kneser’s theorem is the following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\ell
be a multiple of
12
,
k\geq 5
the smallest integer that does not divide
\ell
, and
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{m})
a collection of
m
sets of non-zero remainders modulo
k
. If the sum of remainders from
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
(at most one remainder from each set)
cannot be equal to
\ell
mod
k
, then there are at least
|A_{1}|+\ldots+|A_{m}|
different non-zero values modulo
k
obtainable by such sums.

Proof.

Let
B_{i}:=A_{i}\cup\{0\}
.
Observe that the elements obtainable by summing up some of the remainders from
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the elements of the sumset
B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m}
.
Since
|X+\Stab(X+Y)|\geq|X|
and
|\Stab(X+Y)|\geq|\Stab(Y)|
for any two sets
X
and
Y
, an iterative application of
Theorem
2.7
yields that

\displaystyle|B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m}|

\displaystyle\geq|B_{1}|+|B_{2}+\ldots+B_{m}|-|\Stab(B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m})|

\displaystyle\geq|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|+|B_{3}+\ldots+B_{m}|-2|\Stab(B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m})|

\displaystyle\geq\ldots\geq|B_{1}|+\ldots+|B_{m}|-(m-1)|\Stab(B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m})|.

If
\Stab(B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m})
is non-trivial, then
k
cannot be prime and thus
k=p^{q}
for some prime number
p
and an integer
q\geq 2
. Since
\ell
is divisible by all the integers smaller than
k
(so, in particular by
p^{q-1}
), we have
\ell\equiv cp^{q-1}
mod
k
. Therefore,
\Stab(B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m})
must contain
\ell
mod
k
.
Since
0\in B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m}
, the sumset
B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m}
contains
\ell
mod
k
as well.

On the other hand, if
\Stab(B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m})
is trivial, then we have

|B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m}|\geq|B_{1}|+\ldots+|B_{m}|-(m-1)=|A_{1}|+\ldots+|A_{m}|+1,

and removing
0
from the sumset
B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m}
yields the desired bound.
∎

An easy corollary of the above lemma is the following.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\ell
be a multiple of
12
and
k\geq 5
the smallest integer that does not divide
\ell
.
If
S
is a multiset of
k-1
non-zero reminders modulo

3
Constructions of

-free graphs with the best possible semidegree

In this section, we show that the minimum semidegree threshold in

is best possible.
Given a length of the forbidden cycle

denotes the smallest integer greater than

that does not divide

We describe the desired constructions separately for odd and even values of

3.1
The value of
k
is odd

We shall construct an
n
-vertex graph

with no

and minimum semidegree equal to
\delta^{\pm}(G)=\frac{n}{k}+\frac{k-3}{2k}
.
In particular,

must be divisible by
k
.
Consider first a balanced blow-up of

on
n+(k-3)/2
vertices, which has both the correct minimum semidegree and contains no

.
Our aim is to remove
(k-3)/2
vertices from the blow-up with neither changing the minimum semidegree nor creating

.
In order to do so, we use
(k-3)/2
times one of the three

described in

Figure 2:
Maneuvers used when

is odd. In the first one, adding the middle vertex and incident edges, which creates cycles of length
(k+1)/2
and
(k-1)/2

Before applying any of the maneuvers to the blow-up, one can obtain only cycles of lengths congruent to

An application of the first maneuver allows us to change the remainder modulo

Similarly, applying the second maneuver allows us to change the remainder of an obtainable cycle length twice by
(k-1)/2

Finally, the third maneuver allows us to change the remainder twice by

Let

mod
k

•

If

•

If
\frac{k}{2}<r<\frac{3k}{4}

•

If

Clearly, each application of a maneuver reduces the number of vertices by one, thus the resulting graph has
n
vertices. We assert that it also does not contain

.
Let us discuss in detail only the case

since the other three cases are resolved analogously.
In this case, any obtainable cycle length has the remainder modulo

equal to

for some

In order to obtain the remainder

must hold. However, this is equivalent to

mod
k
, which cannot be satisfied.
Therefore, the resulting graph does not contain a cycle of length
\ell
.

In particular,

(2)

Recall that

We showed that the graph

∎

Using

Recall that

Thus, the vertices of the initial graph

Let us refer to the edges inside one blob or between two blobs that do not fulfill the cycle order as

In the case

Finally, in the remaining case

The proof of

Lemma 4.3
.

Fix an integer
\ell
that is divisible by
12
and let

be the smallest integer greater than

that does not divide

. Let

be an

-vertex graph with
\delta^{\pm}(G)\geq\frac{n}{k}+\frac{k-1}{2k}
. If

contains an induced subgraph on
n-o(n)
vertices that is homomorphic to
C_{k}
, then

contains

.

Proof.

Assume by contrary, that there exists an
n
-vertex
C_{\ell}
-free graph
G
with semidegree
\delta^{\pm}(G)\geq\frac{n}{k}+\frac{k-1}{2k}
for arbitrarily large
n
, and its vertices can be partitioned into set
T:=V(G)\setminus V(H)
and sets
X_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, where
|T|=o(n)
,
|X_{i}|=\frac{n}{k}+o(n)
, and each vertex in
X_{i}
has edges only to vertices in
X_{i+1}\cup T
and only from vertices in
X_{i-1}\cup T
.

We refer to the sets
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{k}
as

and say that a blob

is an

blob or an

blob of a vertex

if
|N^{-}(t)\cap X_{i}|\geq 2k
or
|N^{+}(t)\cap X_{i}|\geq 2k
, respectively.
Using just edges in
H
one can obtain only cycles of lengths divisible by
k
.
We will use the semidegree assumption for finding sufficiently many other edges in
G
that allows us to change the remainder modulo

and construct a cycle of length

.

A

is a path of length 2 starting and finishing in blobs that has the middle vertex in
T
.
The

of a sidewalk is the change in the reminder modulo

that is achieved by this sidewalk.
Formally, if
X_{i}
is an in-pointing blob and

is an out-pointing blob of a vertex

, then

with its corresponding neighbors create a sidewalk of value
i-j+2\mod k
.
We say that two sidewalks are

if they are either vertex disjoint or they share exactly one vertex which is then the sink of one of the sidewalks and the source of the other one.
A set of sidewalks is called compatible if and only if there exists a cycle in
G
that contains all of them. In particular, the sidewalks are pairwise compatible.

We say that a remainder
r\in[k-1]
can be

using a set of sidewalks if

can be written as a sum of the sidewalk values modulo
k
, where the sum is taken over a compatible subset of sidewalks.
Our aim is to show that the remainder
\ell
mod
k
can be combined using some set of sidewalks.
After we do so, then it only remains to connect the chosen sidewalks to make
C_{\ell}
.
The main tool we will use in combining the sidewalks is
Lemma
2.8
and its consequence
Corollary
2.9
.
In our case it means that the remainder
\ell
mod
k
can be combined using any compatible set of
k-1
sidewalks of non-zero values. Let us prove that such a set exists.

Claim 4.4
.

If
t\in T
has
x
in-pointing blobs and
y
out-pointing blobs, then we can combine the reminder
\ell
mod
k
or there are at least
x+y-2
different non-zero values of sidewalks containing
t
.

Proof.

As a set
A
take all the
x
possible values of sidewalks using one chosen out-pointing blob. And as a set
B
take the set of distances in the cyclic order from the chosen out-pointing blob to all the out-pointing blobs (including himself at distance 0). The set of possible values of the sidewalks using
t
is exactly the set
A+B
. If
\Stab(A+B)
is non-trivial,
then
k
is of the form
p^{m}
for some prime
p
and integer
m
,
and
p^{m-1}
is in
\Stab(A+B)
. Since
\ell
is divisible by

, it implies that
\ell
mod
k
is in
\Stab(A+B)
, thus one can combine the reminder
\ell
modulo
k
. Otherwise, from Kneser’s theorem (

2.7
), we get that
|A+B|\geq x+y-1
and so there are at least
x+y-2
non-zero values of sidewalks containing
t
.
∎

Claim 4.5
.

There exists a compatible set of at most

sidewalks using which one can combine the reminder
\ell
mod

.

Proof.

Each vertex in
T
has less than
4k^{2}
neighbors, which are not in out-pointing or in in-pointing blobs, so the total set

of such vertices has size at most

.
Let

be the maximal set of vertices in

creating a compatible set of sidewalks of non-zero values with vertices not from

. If
|T^{\prime}|\geq k-1
, then from

we have the wanted set of sidewalks, thus assume

.

Let

be the sum of the numbers of in-pointing and out-pointing blobs of the vertices in
T^{\prime}
.
Using

4.4
for each vertex in

and

, we get that using sidewalks containing

one can combine the remainder
\ell
mod
k
or at least

non-zero remainders. If

\ell
mod
k
, so assume that

.

Take a set of vertices that contains exactly one vertex from each blob, avoiding the set

. By summing up their indegrees and outdegrees we get at least

. The chosen vertices can have at most

, so to or from

2n+k-1-z-2(n-|T|)=k-1-z+2|T|>2(|T|-|T^{\prime}|)

edges. This means that there exists a vertex

with at least

Since each in-pointing or out-pointing blob of

.
∎

In order to finish the proof of

We can connect the sidewalks by going around the

In the case

We will reduce the set

In this section we assume that

We start with proving

Proof of

Let

[

Analogously to the previous section, we start with bounding the diameter of the graph

Claim 5.1
.

For any vertices

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of

In particular, since

As noted in
[

(hence our

Through this whole section, we write oriented cycle to denote a cycle with an arbitrary orientation of its edges, not necessarily the cyclic orientation as in the directed cycle
C_{\ell}

In order to state the main result of this section, we need to define the so-called cycle-type.
For an oriented cycle
C
, we define the
cycle-type

t(C)
to be the absolute value of the difference between the number of edges oriented forwards in

and the number of edges oriented backwards in
C
with respect to the cyclic orientation of

.
In particular, the

\ell

are precisely those orientations for which there is a homomorphism into a directed path of an appropriate length.
Observe that if

, then the cycle-type is equal to the maximum length of a directed cycle into which there is a homomorphism from

.

For an arbitrary oriented cycle

with

let

be defined as the smallest integer greater than 2 that does not divide

.
Notice that if

is an
\ell

We are now ready to state the main result of this section.

8
Concluding remarks

In this paper, for any given
\ell\geq 4
we have determined the exact value of the semidegree threshold forcing
C_{\ell}
in large enough oriented graphs.
The threshold is essentially given by the degrees in a balanced blow-up of
C_{k}
, where
k
is the smallest integer greater than
2
that does not divide
\ell
.
However, unless
k=3
or
\ell\equiv 3
mod
12
, one must add to the term
\frac{n}{k}
coming from the blow-ups of
C_{k}
an extra term
\frac{k-1}{2k}
to overcome certain number-theoretic flavored perturbations.
What remains is to determine the semidegree threshold of
C_{3}
, which is a long-standing open problem.

Conjecture 8.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Any
n
-vertex oriented graph
G
with the minimum semidegree
\delta^{\pm}(G)\geq\frac{n}{3}
contains
C_{3}
.

Since
Conjecture
8.1
has the same conjectured set of extremal constructions as the triangle case of the Caccetta–Häggkvist conjecture (see, e.g.,
[
30
]
*Section 2 for its precise description), it is commonly believed that the difficulty of the two problems is very similar.

Despite the extremal constructions are not exactly the balanced blow-ups of directed cycles, we have established in
Theorem
1.5
a stability-type result for the cycle lengths divisible by
12
.
Specifically, every extremal graph in this case is close in the so-called edit distance to a balanced blow-up of
C_{k}
.
The
edit distance
of two graphs with the same number of vertices is the number of adjacencies one must alter in one of them to obtain a graph isomorphic to the other one.
Using the terminology of graph limits theory (we refer the reader to the monograph by Lovász
[
26
]
for an excellent exposition to the topic), this translates to the fact that the unique extremal so-called oriented graphon is the one representing the limit of balanced blow-ups of
C_{k}
.

As we have mentioned in the introduction, when the length of the forbidden cycle is an odd multiple of
3
, there are infinitely many extremal limits.
Indeed, any Eulerian orientation of a balanced complete bipartite graph is asymptotically extremal in this case.
The fact that these are the only extremal limits follows from
Theorem
1.6
.
However, when forbidding
C_{\ell}
for
\ell\equiv 6
mod
12
and
\ell\neq 6
, a careful inspection of the arguments presented in
Section
5
yields the following analogue of
Theorem
1.5
for these cycle lengths:

Proposition 8.2
.

Fix an integer
\ell\geq 18
that is divisible by
6
and not divisible by
12
.
Every oriented graph
H
on
n
vertices with
\delta^{\pm}(H)\geq\frac{n}{4}-o(n)
that contains no closed walk of length
\ell
is homomorphic to
C_{4}
.

In the case when
\ell=6
, we expect that the extremal graphs are close in the edit distance to a blow-up of
C_{4}
where exactly one of the independent sets might be replaced by a one-way oriented bipartite graph.
Similar to
Proposition
8.2
, maybe even a stronger statement can be made if one is concerned with closed walks.

.

Is it true that every oriented graph
H
on
n
vertices with
\delta^{\pm}(H)\geq\frac{n}{4}-o(n)
that contains no closed walk of length

Theorem 8.4

(

)

For every

Motivated by the relations between

However, this is not true and for most cycle lengths the corresponding outdegree threshold is strictly larger than the semidegree one.

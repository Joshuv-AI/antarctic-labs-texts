Abstract
. In this paper we construct a tropical moduli space parametrizing roots of divisors on tropical curves. We study the relation between this space and the skeleton of Jarvis moduli space of nets of limit roots on stable curves. We show that the combinatorics of the moduli space of tropical roots is governed by the poset of flows, a poset parametrizing certain flows on graphs.



MSC (2020): 14T05, 14H10, 14H40.

Key words: Flow, root of divisor, net of limit roots, moduli space.

1.
Introduction

The moduli space of
r
-roots of a universal invertible sheaf on smooth
n
-pointed curves is a natural unramified covering of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
. The construction of a compactification of this space over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
has attracted a lot of interest in the last three decades and has found several applications. To our knowledge, the first such compactification was given by Cornalba in
[
Cor89
]
. He constructed the moduli space of spin curves, which takes care of the case where
r=2
and the invertible sheaf is the relative canonical sheaf. This was continued with the work of Jarvis
[
Jar98
]
and
[
Jar00
]
, containing a construction of a compactification for any
r
and any sheaf via torsion-free rank-
1
sheaves. Then Abramovich and Jarvis
[
AJ03
]
and Abramovich, Corti, Vistoli
[
ACV03
]
solved the problem for any
r
using twisted bundles, and Caporaso, Casagrande and Cornalba
[
CCC07
]
construced a compactification for any
r
via invertible sheaves on semistable curves. Finally, Chiodo
[
Chi08
]
constructed a compatification for any
r
via twisted curves with the property that the natural map to (a suitable modification of)
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is unramified. The compactifications are a fundamental tool in the computation of the double ramification cycle (see for example
[
JPPZ17
]
). For a recent generalization of the work of Chiodo and Jarvis with applications to the double ramification cycle, see
[
HO22
]
.

In this paper, we mainly consider the Jarvis compactification
[
Jar00
]
. This is the space paramatrizing nets of
r
-limit roots, and it is a normalization of the space constructed by Jarvis in
[
Jar98
]
, or by Caporaso, Casagrande and Cornalba in
[
CCC07
]
(the last two spaces are isomorphic).

Since the work of
[
ACP15
]
, it is natural to construct tropical analogues of moduli spaces arising in algebraic geometry. These tropical analogues encode the combinatorial data of the objets parametrized by the algebro-geometric spaces. In some cases they even recover topological information from the algebro-geometric space, as in
[
CGP21
]
.
The tropical spaces are often isomorphic to the skeleton (in the sense of Berkovich) of the analytification of these moduli spaces. The relation between algebraic geometry and tropical geometry is made more concrete with the work of
[
CCUW20
]
that constructs Artin stacks from tropical moduli spaces.
Several such tropical analogues have been constructed: the moduli space of tropical curves in
[
Mik07
]
and
[
BMV11
]
, the moduli space of tropical admissible covers in
[
CHMR16
]
, the universal tropical Jacobian in
[
AP20
]
and
[
MMUV21
]
, and the moduli space of tropical spin curves in
[
CMP20
]
.

In this paper we consider roots of divisors on tropical curves. The first description of theta characteristics of tropical curves, namely square roots of the canonical divisor of a tropical curve, was given in
[
MZ08
]
and
[
Zha10
]
. Our first goal is to describe all
r
-roots of any divisor on tropical curves. Our construction is compatible with specializations of graphs. This allows us to construct a tropical analogue of the space of
r
-roots which is the target of a tropicalization map from the analytification of the Jarvis moduli space.
This space is a generalized cone complex over the moduli space of tropical curves and parametrizes roots of a fixed universal divisor on tropical curves. It is not isomorphic to the skeleton of its algebro-geometric counterpart, but it is the target of a natural map from the skeleton that commutes with the tropicalization map from the analytification of Jarvis space. In fact, to fully describe the skeleton of the space of
r
-roots of an invertible sheaf, an extra-datum is necessary. This is done in
[
CMP20
]
in the case of spin curves, where the extra-datum is a sign function, encoding the parity of the space of sections of the invertible sheaves. Other special cases should be doable, as for instance the case of the moduli space of Prym varieties. The general case seems quite difficult and challenging.

1.1.
The results

The main combinatorial part of this article concerns graphs and flows on graphs. Let

(Theorems

The poset

Background material

2.1.
Graphs

Let
\Gamma=(V(\Gamma),E(\Gamma))
be a graph. We will only consider connected graphs, while we will allow possible disconnected subgraphs of a graph. Given a subset
V\subset V(\Gamma)
, we set
V^{c}:=V(\Gamma)\setminus V
. Given
v\in V(\Gamma)
we denote by
\val_{\Gamma}(v)
the valence of
v
(with loops counting twice).
The set of
oriented edges
of
\Gamma
is defined as
\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma):=E(\Gamma)\sqcup E(\Gamma)
. We have a map
\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)\rightarrow E(\Gamma)
given by
e\mapsto e
.
If
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
, we will denote by
-e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
the oriented edge, distinct from
e
, such that
e
and
-e
have the same underlying edge
(we can view
e
and
-e
as the two possible orientations on a given edge).
We let
\sigma,\tau\colon\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)\to V(\Gamma)
be the source and target map, so that
\sigma(e)
and
\tau(e)
are the vertices incident to
e
, for every
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
. An
orientation

\overrightarrow{\Gamma}
on
\Gamma
is a section of the function
\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)\to E(\Gamma)
; we denote by
E(\overrightarrow{\Gamma})
the image of this section.

We denote by
(\Gamma,w_{\Gamma},L_{\Gamma})
a genus-
g
stable graph with
n
-legs, where
w_{\Gamma}\colon V(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
is the weight function and
L_{\Gamma}\colon[n]\to V(\Gamma)
is the leg function. We will usually write
\Gamma
instead of
(\Gamma,w_{\Gamma},L_{\Gamma})
. The
genus
of
\Gamma
is
g=:g_{\Gamma}:=b_{1}(\Gamma)+\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}w(v)
, where
b_{1}(\Gamma)
is the first Betti number of
\Gamma
.
We say that
\Gamma
is
stable
if
2\val_{\Gamma}(v)-2+w_{\Gamma}(v)+L^{-1}_{\Gamma}(v)>0
, for every
v\in V(\Gamma)
. In particular, if
\Gamma
is a genus-
g
stable graph with
n
legs, then
2g-2+n>0
. For a graph
\Gamma
and a subset
\mathcal{E}\subset E(\Gamma)
we denote by
\Gamma/\mathcal{E}
the graph obtained by contracting all the edges
e\in\mathcal{E}
.
We refer to
[
AP20
]
for the notions and properties of specializations of weighted graphs with legs (that is, weighted graphs obtained by contracting edges of a graph).

We say that
\Gamma
is a
tree
if
b_{1}(\Gamma)=0
, and
tree-like
if
\Gamma
becomes a tree when contracting its loops.
A
refinement

\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
is a graph whose edges are obtained by refining the edges of
\Gamma
, i.e., inserting a certain number
n_{e}
(possibly zero) of vertices in the interior of each edge
e
of
\Gamma
. We say that an edge of
\Gamma^{\prime}
is
over
and edge
e
of
\Gamma
if it is obtained by refining
e
. Notice that any orientation of an edge
e
induces a natural orientation on every edge over
e
.

Let
A
be an Abelian group.
An
A
-divisor
on
\Gamma
is a function
D\colon V(\Gamma)\to A
. The
degree
of an
A
-divisor
D
is
\deg(D)=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}D(v)\in A
. We denote by
\Div(\Gamma,A)
be the Abelian group of
A
-divisors on
\Gamma
, and for
a\in A
, we let by
\Div^{a}(\Gamma,A)
the subset of divisors of degree
a
.

2.2.
Ranked posets

Let
S=(S,\geqslant)
be a poset. We say that
S
is
ranked
if every maximal chain in
S
has the same length. A ranked poset has a rank function
\rank\colon S\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
such that
\rank(x)=\rank(y)+1
if
x
covers
y
and
\rank(x)=0
if
x
is minimal. In particular, every maximal element has the same rank, which is the
dimension
of
S
. A map between ranked posets is an order and rank preserving function.

We let
\mathbf{G}_{g,n}
be the category whose objects are genus-
g
stable graphs with
n
-legs and whose morphisms are specializations. We let
\mathcal{G}_{g,n}
be the poset whose elements are the isomorphisms classes of elements of
\mathbf{G}_{g,n}
. The poset
\mathcal{G}_{g,n}
is ranked with rank function given by
\rank(\Gamma)=|E(\Gamma)|
.

2.3.
Flows on graphs

Let
A
be an Abelian group
A
with identity

(in this paper we usually use

or
A=\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
). For elements

,

\gcd(b,a+bA)=\max\{j\in\mathbb{Z};j\text{ divides }|b|,a+bA\subset jA\}.

Notice that, when

, we have that
\gcd(b,a+bA)
is the usual
\gcd(b,a)
. If

is such that the multiplication by

is injective in the group

\gcd(db,da+dbA)=d\gcd(b,a+bA).

We will also need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian group and
r
be an integer such that the multiplication map
A\xrightarrow{\cdot r}A
is injective. If

is a torsion element of
A
, then

.

Proof.

Let

be such that

and let

. Then we have
d\frac{n}{d}a=0
. Since multiplication by
d
is injective (because

\frac{n}{d}a=0
. Repeating the argument we can assume that

is coprime with

(here we use that

, for some

, we have that
a=(xn+ry)a=r(ya)
, which means that

.
∎

Let
\Gamma
be a graph.
An

is a function
\varphi\colon\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)\to A
such that

be the

If

.

2.4.
Tropical curves and their moduli

Let

be an

Let

\displaystyle f_{*}\colon\mathcal{F}_{g}(A,\mathcal{R})

If

6.
The tropicalization of the moduli space of roots

In this section we consider the moduli space of roots on algebraic curves constructed by Jarvis and by Caporaso, Casagrande and Cornalba. We relate its Berkovich skeleton with the moduli space of roots on tropical curves constructed in the previous section.

Let

We will always assume our curves to be nodal curves over

Let

be a curve. We denote by

the singular locus of

be the component of

corresponding to

Consider a subset

of nodes of

be the partial normalization of

at
\Delta
. A

of

at the set

is a curve

which is the union of

and smooth connected rational components

(called

), for

, such that

is equal to the set of smooth points of

w\in W
is independent of
w
. Moreover, if
W^{\prime}
is another stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{N})
such that
W
is contained in the closure of
W^{\prime}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{N})
and if we let
(\Gamma_{W},\varphi_{W})
and
(\Gamma_{W^{\prime}},\varphi_{W^{\prime}})
the combinatorial types of a net of limit
r
-root parametrized by a point of
W
and
W^{\prime}
, respectively, then
(\Gamma_{W},\varphi_{W})
specializes to
(\Gamma_{W^{\prime}},\varphi_{W^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

The stratum
W
can be written as
W=W_{\Gamma,j}
for some
j
. Hence, for every point
[C]\in W
the dual graph of
C
is isomorphic to
\Gamma
.
Since
W
is irreducible, it is sufficient to prove that the combinatorial type of every net of limit
r
-root parametrized by a point
w\in W
is isomorphic to the combinatorial type of the net of limit
r
-root parametrized by the generic point of
W
. In turn, we can argue locally étale, and hence we can conclude using Remark
6.4
, since we have a section for each node.

The second statement follows from the first and Remark
6.4
.
∎

Consider a stratum
W
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{N})
.
The combinatorial type
of
W
is the combinatorial type of a net of limit
r
-root parametrized by any point of
W
. This definition is well-defined by Proposition
6.7
.
Notice that, if
(\Gamma_{W},\varphi_{W})
is the combinatorial type of
W
,
we have that
W
lies over a stratum
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
if and only if
\Gamma_{W}=\Gamma
.

Consider a stable genus-
g
graph
\Gamma
with
n
legs. We define

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}=\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\mathcal{M}_{w(v),\deg_{\Gamma}(v)+|L^{-1}(v)|}.

Recall that we have a natural quotient map
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}/\Aut(\Gamma)
. Notice that we can view
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
as the space parametrizing pairs
(C,\tau)
where
C
is a stable nodal curve with dual graph isomorphic to
\Gamma
and
\tau\colon\Gamma_{C}\to\Gamma
is such an isomorphism.

Let
W
be a stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{N})
of combinatorial type
(\Gamma,\varphi)
. The product
W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
parametrizes triples
(C,\mathfrak{F},\tau)
where
\mathfrak{F}
is a net of limit
r
-roots of
\mathcal{N}|_{C}
and
\tau\colon\Gamma_{C}\to\Gamma
is an isomorphism. Notice that we have an action of
\Aut(\Gamma)
on
W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
.
All the connected components of
W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
have the same stabilizer, since the map
W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}\to W
is Galois.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
W
be a stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{\mathcal{N}})
of combinatorial type
(\Gamma,\varphi)
. Then the stabilizer of the connected components of
W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
is contained in
\Aut(\Gamma,\varphi)
.

Proof.

Consider two points
(C,\mathfrak{F},\tau_{1})
and
(C,\mathfrak{F},\tau_{2})
in the same connected component of
W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
, for some
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\Aut(\Gamma)
. Let
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
be the restriction of the universal family over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, and set
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{C}\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
. For every edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
we have a section
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
whose image is the locus of the node corresponding to
e
. This gives rise to sections
\sigma_{e}\colon W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}\to W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
. By Remark
6.4
, we have that the flow of the combinatorial type of
\mathfrak{F}
has the same value on the edges
\tau_{1}^{-1}(e)
and
\tau_{2}^{-1}(e)
. This means that
\tau_{1}^{-1}\circ\tau_{2}\in\Aut(\Gamma,\varphi)
, which proves that the stabilizer of the connected components of
W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
is contained in
\Aut(\Gamma,\varphi)
.
∎

6.2.
The skeleton of the moduli space of nets

Let
\mathcal{Y}
be a separated, connected, proper Deligne-Mumford stack over an algebraically closed field
k
. The
Berkovich analytification

\mathcal{Y}^{an}
of
\mathcal{Y}
is an analytic stack. We work with the topological space underlying
\mathcal{Y}^{an}
, whose points are morphisms
\Spec(K)\to\mathcal{Y}
that extends to
\Spec(R)\to\mathcal{Y}
, where
K
is a non Archimedean valued field extension of the trivially valued field
k
and
R
is the valuation ring of
K
, up to equivalence by further valued field extensions (see
[
Ber90
]
and
[
Uli17
, Section 3]
). We abuse notation and use
\mathcal{Y}^{\an}
for both the stack and the topological space underlying it. One can choose a representative
\Spec(K)\to\mathcal{Y}
with
K
complete for each point in
\mathcal{Y}^{an}
.

Let
U\subset\mathcal{Y}
be a toroidal embedding of Deligne-Mumford stacks. Define the sheaves
\mon_{\mathcal{Y}}
and
\eff_{\mathcal{Y}}
as the étale sheaves over
\mathcal{Y}
such that, for every étale morphism
V\to\mathcal{Y}
from a scheme
V
, we have that
\mon_{\mathcal{Y}}(V)
(respectively,
\eff_{\mathcal{Y}}(V)
) is the group of Cartier divisors on
V
(respectively, the submonoid of effective Cartier divisors on
V
) supported on
V\setminus U_{V}
, where
U_{V}=U\times_{\mathcal{Y}}V
.

For each stratum
W\subset\mathcal{Y}
and a point
w\in W
, we have an action of the étale fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\et}(W,w)
on the stalk
\mon_{\mathcal{Y},w}
preserving
\eff_{\mathcal{Y},w}
. The
monodromy group

H_{W}
is defined as the image of
\pi_{1}^{\et}(W,w)
in
\Aut(\mon_{\mathcal{Y},w})
.

For each stratum
W\subset\mathcal{Y}
, there is an associated extended cone

\overline{\sigma}_{W}:=\Hom_{\text{monoids}}(\eff_{\mathcal{Y},w},\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geqslant 0}),

where
\overline{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
.
The
skeleton
of
\mathcal{Y}
is the extended generalized cone complex:

\overline{\Sigma}(\mathcal{Y}):=\lim_{\longrightarrow}\overline{\sigma}_{W},

where the arrows
\overline{\sigma}_{W}\to\overline{\sigma}_{W^{\prime}}
are given by the inclusions
W^{\prime}\subset\overline{W}
, where
\overline{W}
is the closure of
W
in
\mathcal{Y}
, and by the monodromy group
H_{W}
when
W=W^{\prime}
. For more details, we refer to
[
Thu07
]
and
[
ACP15
, Section 6]
.

There is a retraction map
{\bf p}_{\mathcal{Y}}\colon\mathcal{Y}^{\an}\rightarrow\overline{\Sigma}(\mathcal{Y})
defined as follows. Let
\psi\colon\Spec(R)\to\mathcal{Y}
be a point in
\mathcal{Y}^{\an}
, with
R
complete. Let
w\in\mathcal{Y}
be the image of the closed point in
\Spec(R)
and
W
be the stratum of
\mathcal{Y}
containing
w
. We have a chain of maps

\eff_{\mathcal{Y},w}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\epsilon}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{Y},w}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi^{\#}}}{{\longrightarrow}}R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu_{R}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{>0},

(20)

where
\epsilon
is the map that takes an effective divisor to its local equation and
\nu_{R}
is the valuation of
R
. The composition is a morphism of monoids. We define
{\bf p}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\psi)\in\overline{\Sigma}(\mathcal{Y})
as the equivalence class of
\nu_{R}\circ\psi^{\#}\circ\epsilon\in\overline{\sigma}_{W}
(see
[
ACP15
, Section 6]
for details, in particular
[
ACP15
, Propositions 6.1.4. and 6.2.6]
).

The inclusions
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})
are toroidal embeddings of Deligne-Mumford stacks (see
[
ACP15
, Section 3.3]
and Proposition
[
Jar00
, Theorem 2.4.2]
). We let
\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
and
\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R}))
be the skeleta of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})
, respectively.

The forgetful map
\pi_{g,\mathcal{R}}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
induces a natural map

\pi^{\an}_{g,\mathcal{R}}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})^{\an}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\an}.

Since
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
are proper and the forgetful map is toroidal (by Equation
19
), we have, by
[
ACP15
, Proposition 6.1.8]
, that the map
\pi^{\an}_{g,\mathcal{R}}
restricts to a map of generalized extended cone complexes

\overline{\Sigma}(\pi^{\an}_{g,\mathcal{R}})\colon\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R}))\rightarrow\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}).

Moreover, we have the following functorial property with respect to the retraction map:

{\bf p}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}}\circ\pi^{\an}_{g,\mathcal{R}}=\overline{\Sigma}(\pi^{\an}_{g,\mathcal{R}})\circ{\bf p}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}.

(21)

We define the
tropicalization map

\trop_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})^{\an}\to\overline{\Roots}_{g}^{r,\trop}(\mathcal{R})

(22)

as follows.
Fix a point
\psi\colon\Spec(R)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})^{\an}
with
R
complete and let
w=[C,\mathfrak{F}]
be the image of the closed point of
\Spec(R)
via
\psi
(this is the class of a pair consisting of a curve
C
and a net
\mathfrak{F}
of limit
r
-roots on
C
). We get a ring homomorphism

\psi^{\#}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R}),w}\to R.

Let
(\Gamma,\varphi)
be the combinatorial type of
(C,\mathfrak{F})
. Recall Equation (
18
). We define a length function
\ell\colon E(\Gamma)\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
given by

\ell(e)=\begin{cases}s_{e}\nu_{R}(\psi^{\#}(\tau_{e}))&\text{ if }e\in\Delta(\mathfrak{F}),\\
\nu_{R}(\psi^{\#}(t_{e}))&\text{ if }e\notin\Delta(\mathfrak{F}).\end{cases}

(23)

We define
\trop_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}(\psi)=[X,[\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}]]
, where
X
is the (possibly extended) tropical curve with model
(\Gamma,\ell)
. Note that the point
(x_{e})\in\kappa_{(\Gamma,\varphi)}
that corresponds to
[X,[\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}]]
is given by

x_{e}=\begin{cases}\nu_{R}(\psi^{\#}(\tau_{e}))&\text{ if }e\in\Delta(\mathfrak{F}),\\
\nu_{R}(\psi^{\#}(t_{e}))&\text{ if }e\notin\Delta(\mathfrak{F}).\end{cases}

(24)

Corollary 6.9
.

Let
W
be a stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})
of combinatorial type
(\Gamma,\varphi)
.
Then the monodromy of the stratum
W
is contained in
\Aut(\Gamma,\varphi)
.

Proof.

The sheaf
\text{Eff}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}
is trivial on
W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
, because it is trivial on
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
(see
[
ACP15
, proof of Propostion 7.2.1]
). In particular, this sheaf is trivial on every connected component of
W\times_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
, so the monodromy of
W
coincides with the stabilizer of one (every) connected component. The result follows from Proposition
6.8
.
∎

The following is the main result of the section.
Recall Definition
5.1
.

Theorem 6.10
.

There is a morphism of extended generalized cone complexes

\Phi_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}\colon\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R}))\to\overline{\Roots}_{g}^{r,\trop}(\mathcal{R}),

and a commutative diagram:

\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})^{\an}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\trop_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}}
\scriptstyle{\pi^{\an}_{g,\mathcal{R}}}
\scriptstyle{{\bf p}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}\;}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R}))}
\scriptstyle{{\Phi}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\Sigma}(\pi^{\an}_{g,\mathcal{R}})}
\textstyle{\overline{\Roots}_{g}^{r,\trop}(\mathcal{R})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi^{\trop}_{g,\mathcal{R}}}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\an}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\trop_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}}
\scriptstyle{{\bf p}_{\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}}\;}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})}
\scriptstyle{{\Phi}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}}
\textstyle{\overline{M}_{g,n}^{trop}}

Proof.

Let
w=[C,\mathfrak{F}]
be a point in a stratum
W
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})
of combinatorial type
(\Gamma,\varphi)
. Then there is an isomorphism of monoids
\Eff_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R}),w}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}^{E(\Gamma)}
via Equation (
18
), hence
\overline{\sigma}_{W}
is isomorphic to
\overline{\sigma}_{(\Gamma,\varphi)}
. By
[
ACP15
, Proposition 6.2.6]
and Corollary
6.9
, we get a map

\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R}))=\coprod_{W}\overline{\sigma}^{\circ}_{W}/H_{W}\to\coprod_{(\Gamma,\varphi)}\overline{\sigma}^{\circ}_{(\Gamma,\varphi)}/\Aut(\Gamma,\varphi).

Moreover, by Proposition
6.7
, if
W
and
W^{\prime}
are strata with combinatorial types
(\Gamma,\varphi)
and
(\Gamma^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
, respectively, we have that
(\Gamma,\varphi)\geqslant(\Gamma^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
in the poset
\mathcal{F}_{g}(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z},\mathcal{R})
if, and only if,
W
is contained in the closure of
\overline{W^{\prime}}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})
. This induces a map of generalized cone complexes
\Phi_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}
from
\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R}))
to
\overline{\Roots}_{g}^{r,\trop}(\mathcal{R})
.

Let
\psi\colon\Spec(R)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})
be a point in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})^{\an}
, with
R
complete. Let
W
be the stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})
containing the image
w
via
\psi
of the closed point in
\Spec(R)
. The set
E(\Gamma)
can be seen as a monoid basis of the free monoid
\Eff_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R}),w}
. Write
\trop_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}(\psi)=[X,[\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}]]\in\overline{\Roots}_{g}^{r,\trop}(\mathcal{R})
and let

(x_{e})_{e\in E(\Gamma)}
be the corresponding point in
\overline{\sigma}_{\Gamma,\varphi}^{\circ}
.

By Equation (
20
), we have that
{\bf p}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}(\psi)
is the point of
\overline{\sigma}^{\circ}_{W}
given by the coordinates

(\nu_{R}(\psi^{\#}(\tau_{e})),\nu_{R}(\psi^{\#}(t_{e^{\prime}}))),

for
e\in\Delta(\mathfrak{F})
and
e^{\prime}\in E(\Gamma)\setminus\Delta(\mathfrak{F})
.
Clearly, this is the same point as the point
(x_{e})_{e\in e(\Gamma)}
given by Equation (
24
).
It follows that
\trop_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}=\Phi_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}\circ{\bf p}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}
.

The fact that the square in the left hand side of the diagram in the statement is commutative follows from Equation (
21
).

Finally, we have that
\trop_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\circ\pi^{\an}(\psi)
is the tropical curve
(\Gamma,\ell^{\prime})
where
\ell^{\prime}(e)=\nu_{R}(\psi^{\#}t_{e})
. On the other hand,
\pi^{\trop}\circ\trop_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}(\psi)
is the tropical curve
(\Gamma,\ell)
where
\ell
is as in Equation
23
. Since
\nu_{R}(\psi^{\#}t_{e})=s_{e}\nu_{R}(\psi^{\#}\tau_{e})
by Equation (
19
), we deduce that
\ell^{\prime}(e)=\ell(e)
if
e\in\Delta(\mathfrak{F})
. It is also clear that
\ell^{\prime}(e)=\ell(e)
if
e\notin\Delta(\mathfrak{F})
, hence the tropical curves
(\Gamma,\ell^{\prime})
and
(\Gamma,\ell)
are equivalent, thus

\trop_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\circ\pi^{\an}_{g,\mathcal{R}}=\pi^{\trop}_{g,\mathcal{R}}\circ\trop_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.11
.

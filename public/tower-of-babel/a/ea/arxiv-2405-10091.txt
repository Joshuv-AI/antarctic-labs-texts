Abstract
. We characterize the multipliers from the little BMO \mathrm{BMO} of Cotlar-Sadosky to the product BMO \mathrm{BMO} of Chang-Fefferman on the polydisk.



1.
Introduction and notation

Recall that in the one parameter setting, the space of functions of bounded mean oscillation on the torus
\mathbb{T}
,
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T})
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{T}
such that

(1)

\|f\|_{*}:=\sup_{I\subset\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|f(t)-m_{I}f|dt<\infty,

where the supremum is taking on all intervals
I\subset\mathbb{T}
, and
m_{I}f=\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}f(s)ds
. Here, for any measurable set
E
,
|E|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
E
.

The multiplier algebra on
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T})
has been characterized by D. Stegenga in
[
11
]
. He proved that a function
b
is a multiplier of
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
b
is bounded and satisfies the condition

\sup_{I\subset\mathbb{T}}\frac{\log\left(|I|\right)}{|I|}\int_{I}|f(t)-m_{I}f|dt<\infty,

When comes to the multiparameter setting, there appear three main notions of bounded mean oscillation. Our interest here is for multipliers between the generalization of the little space of functions of bounded mean oscillation introduced by M. Cotlar and C. Sadosky in
[
3
]
and the product space of functions of bounded mean oscillation on open subset of
\mathbb{T}^{N}
discovered by A. Chang and R. Fefferman
[
2
]
.

Recall that the so-called small BMO space on the bitorus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, introduced by Cotlar and Sadosky, is defined as

(2)

\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{2}):=\{b\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}):\sup_{R\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}\text{ rectangle }}\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|b(s,t)-m_{R}b|dsdt<\infty\},

m_{R}b=\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}b(s,t)dsdt.
It easily generalizes as follows,

(3)

\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})=\{b\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N}):||b||_{*,N}:=\sup_{R\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}\text{ rectangle }}\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|b(t_{1},\cdots,t_{N})-m_{R}b|dt_{1}\cdots dt_{N}<\infty\},

m_{R}b=\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}b(t_{1},\cdots,t_{N})dt_{1}\cdots dt_{N}
. Seen as a quotient space with the set of constants,
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
is a Banach space with norm

\|b\|_{\mathrm{bmo}}:=||b||_{*,N}.

We observe that a function
b\in\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
if and only if for any positive integers
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
such that
0<N_{1}+N_{2}=N
,

(4)

\max\{\sup_{s\in\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}}}\|b(\cdot,s)\|_{*,N_{1}},\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}}}\|b(t,\cdot)\|_{*,N_{1}}\}=M<\infty

and
M
is comparable to
||b||_{*,N}
(see
[
10
]
). Note also that if
b\in\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
, then for any rectangle
R\subset\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}}
,
0<N_{1}<N
,
m_{R}b\in\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N-N_{1}})
uniformly.

The multi-parameter space of functions of bounded mean oscillation on open sets, or product
\mathrm{BMO}
space on
\mathbb{T}^{N}
, was introduced by Chang and Fefferman in
[
2
]
. It is the dual of the product
Hardy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
, given by

H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}):=\{f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}):H_{j}f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}),H_{k_{1}}\cdots H_{k_{j}}f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}),\,\,\,k_{l}\in\{1,\cdots,n\},\,\,\,j,l=1,\cdots,n\},

where
H_{j}
is the Hilbert transform in the
j
-th coordinate. The space
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
is a strict subspace of
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
and they coincide only when
N=1
(
[
4
]
).

Given two Banach spaces of functions
X
and
Y
, the space of pointwise multipliers from
X
to
Y
is defined as follows

\mathcal{M}(X,Y)=\{\phi:\phi f\in Y\,\,\,\textrm{for all}\,\,\,f\in X\}.

When
X=Y
, we simply write
\mathcal{M}(X,X)=\mathcal{M}(X)
. When
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(X,Y)
, we say
\phi
is a multipler from
X
to
Y
.

The author obtained in
[
10
]
that the only multipliers of
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
are the constants. The algebra of multipliers on
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
is considered by S. Pott and the author in a forthcoming paper (see
[
9
]
for the two parameter case). In this note, we focus on the multipliers from
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
to
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. To deal with this question, we observe that it is enough to consider the same question for the dyadic versions of
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
and
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
namely
\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
and
\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. Once the multipliers from
\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
to
\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
are obtained, usual arguments (
[
5
,
12
]
) allow to conclude for the multipliers from
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
to
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.

2.
Notations, definitions and statement of the result

We identify
\mathbb{T}
with the interval
[0,1)
in the usual way and write
\mathcal{D}

for the set of all dyadic subintervals. We denote by

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{D}^{N}
the set of all dyadic rectangles
R=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}
, where
I_{j}\in\mathcal{D}
,

j=1,\cdots,N
. Let
h_{I}
denote the Haar wavelet adapted
to the dyadic interval
I
,

h_{I}=|I|^{-1/2}(\chi_{I^{+}}-\chi_{I^{-}}),

where
I^{+}
and
I^{-}

are the right and left halves of
I
, respectively.

For any rectangle
R\in\mathcal{R}
, the product Haar wavelet
adapted to
R=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}
is defined by
h_{R}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{N})=h_{I_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots h_{I_{N}}(t_{N})
.
These wavelets form an orthonormal basis of

L_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\cdots,t_{j},\cdots)dt_{j}=0,j=1,\cdots,N\text{ for a.e. }t_{1},\cdots,t_{N}\in\mathbb{T}\right\},

with

f=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\langle f,h_{R}\rangle h_{R}=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}f_{R}h_{R}\qquad(f\in L^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{T}^{N})).

The mean of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
over the dyadic rectangle
R
is denoted
m_{R}f
, and
f_{R}
stands for the Haar coefficient
\langle f,h_{R}\rangle
of
f
. If
R=S\times T
, we will also write
f_{R}=f_{S\times T}
when this is necessary.

The space of functions of dyadic bounded mean oscillations in
\mathbb{T}^{N}
,

\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
, is the space of all function
f\in L_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
such that

(5)

||f||_{\mathrm{BMO}^{d}}^{2}:=\sup_{\Omega\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}}\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{R\in\Omega}|f_{R}|^{2}=\sup_{\Omega\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}}\frac{1}{|\Omega|}||P_{\Omega}f||_{2}^{2}<\infty,

where the supremum
is taken over all open sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
and
P_{\Omega}
is the
orthogonal projection on the subspace spanned by Haar functions

h_{R}
,
R\in\mathcal{R}
and
R\subset\Omega
.

Recall that
\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
is the dual space of the dyadic product Hardy space
H^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
defined by

H^{1,d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})=\{f\in L_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}):\mathcal{S}[f]\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})\},

where
\mathcal{S}
is the dyadic square function,

(6)

\mathcal{S}[f]=\left(\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{\chi_{R}}{|R|}|f_{R}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

We note that
\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
is defined by taking supremum only on dyadic rectangles in the definition of
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.

We also introduce the mean dyadic product
\mathrm{BMO}
that we denote
\mathrm{BMO}_{m}^{d}
, as the space of those functions
f\in L_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
such that there is a constant
C>0
such that for any integer
0<N_{1}<N
, and any dyadic rectangle
R\in\mathcal{D}^{N_{1}}
,

\|m_{R}f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N-N_{1}})}\leq C.

We denote by
\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{m}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}
the infimum of the constants as above. We remark that for any
f\in\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
,

\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{m}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}.

As usual, for
\vec{j}=(j_{1},\cdots,j_{N})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
we define the
j_{1}
-th generation of dyadic intervals and the
\vec{j}
-th generation of dyadic rectangles,

\mathcal{D}_{j_{1}}=\{I\in\mathcal{D}:|I|=2^{-j_{1}}\},

\mathcal{R}_{\vec{j}}=\mathcal{D}_{j_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{D}_{j_{N}}=\{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\in\mathcal{R}:|I_{k}|=2^{-j_{k}}\}.

The product Haar martingale differences are given by

\Delta_{\vec{j}}f=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}_{\vec{j}}}\langle f,h_{R}\rangle h_{R},

and the expectations by

E_{\vec{j}}f=\sum_{\vec{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N},\vec{k}<\vec{j}}\Delta_{\vec{k}}f,

where we write
\vec{k}<\vec{j}
for
k_{l}<j_{l}
,
l=1,\cdots,N
and correspondingly
\vec{k}\leq\vec{j}
for
k_{l}\leq j_{l}
,
for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.

The following operators defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
will be also needed

(7)

Q_{\vec{j}}f=\sum_{\vec{k}\geq\vec{j}}\Delta_{\vec{k}}f.

Let us recall the definition of the space of functions of dyadic logarithmic mean oscillation on the
\mathbb{T}^{N}
,
\mathrm{LMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
as introduced in
[
8
]
.

D
EFINITION
2.1
.

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. We say that
\phi\in\mathrm{LMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
, if there exists
C>0
with

\|Q_{\vec{j}}\phi\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}\leq C\frac{1}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}j_{k}\right)+N}

for all
\vec{j}=(j_{1},\cdots,j_{N})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
. The infimum of such constants is denoted by
\|\phi\|_{\mathrm{LMO}^{d}}
.

The above space has the following equivalent definition (see
[
8
]
).

P
ROPOSITION
2.2
.

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. Then
\phi\in\mathrm{LMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
, if and only if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for each
dyadic rectangle
R=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}
and each open set
\Omega\subseteq R
,

(8)

\frac{\left(\log\frac{4}{|I_{1}|}+\cdots+\log\frac{4}{|I_{N}|}\right)^{2}}{|\Omega|}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{R},Q\subseteq\Omega}|\phi_{Q}|^{2}\leq C.

Our strong version of the above space is defined as follows.

D
EFINITION
2.3
.

If we write
\mathrm{LMO}^{d,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
for the space given in Definition
2.1
but with respect to the translated grid by
\alpha\in[0,1)^{N}
,
\mathcal{D}^{N,\alpha}
of
\mathcal{D}^{N}
, then
\mathrm{LMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
is the intersection of all
\mathrm{LMO}^{d,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.

This property is well known for the product
\mathrm{BMO}
(see
[
5
,
12
]
). We denote by
\|f\|_{\mathrm{LMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}
, the infimum of the constants
C>0
such that for any
\alpha\in[0,1)^{N}
,

\|f\|_{\mathrm{LMO}^{d.\alpha}}<C.

It is clear that
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
embeds continuously in each
\mathrm{bmo}^{d,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
,
\alpha\in[0,1)^{N}
. The converse holds in the one-parameter case (see
[
5
]
). Let us suppose that the converse holds in
(N-1)
-parameter case and prove that in this case, it also holds in
N
-parameter. If
f\in\cap_{\alpha\in[0,1)^{N}}\mathrm{bmo}^{d,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
, then from what has been said at the beginning of the previous section, we have that for any
s\in\mathbb{T}
, the function
f(s,\cdot)
is uniformly in
\mathrm{bmo}^{d,\beta}(\mathbb{T}^{N-1})
for any
\beta\in[0,1)^{N-1}
. It follows from the hypothesis that
f(s,\cdot)
is uniformly in
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N-1})
. Also for any
t\in\mathbb{T}^{N-1}
, the function
f(\cdot,t)
is uniformly in
\mathrm{BMO}^{d,\alpha_{1}}(\mathbb{T})
for any
\alpha_{1}\in[0,1)
, hence
f(\cdot,t)
is uniformly in
\cap_{\alpha_{1}\in[0,1)}\mathrm{BMO}^{d,\alpha_{1}}(\mathbb{T})=\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T})
. We conclude that that
f\in\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.

Our main result in this paper is the following.

T
HEOREM
2.4
.

\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N}),\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N})\cap\mathrm{LMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N}).

To prove the above theorem, we decompose the product of two functions in the Haar basis of
L_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
and study the boundedness of each of the operators appearing in this decomposition. The boundedness of paraproducts on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
will be a useful tool among others.

All over the text, given two positive quantities
A
and
B
, the notation
A\lesssim B
means that there exists some constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
. When
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
, we write
A\simeq B
.

3.
Few useful facts

3.1.
Little
\mathrm{BMO}
, product
\mathrm{BMO}
and a useful estimate

We start by recalling the following embedding (see
[
3
]
).

L
EMMA
3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
f\in\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
,

(9)

\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}.

Note that it was proved in
[
1
]
that
for any integers
0<N_{1},N_{2}<N
with
N_{1}+N_{2}=N
, and for any
f\in\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}})
and
g\in\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}})
, the tensor product
f\otimes g
defined by

f\otimes g(s,t)=f(s)g(t),\,\,\,s\in\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}},\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}}

is uniformly in
\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
with

\|f\otimes g\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}})}\|g\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}})}.

Hence the following result is enough to conclude that the embedding
\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{BMO}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
is strict (see also
[
3
]
).

L
EMMA
3.2
.

Let
0<N_{1},N_{2}<N
be integers with
N_{1}+N_{2}=N
. Then for any
f\in\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}})
and
g\in\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}})
, the tensor product
f\otimes g
defined by

f\otimes g(s,t)=f(s)g(t),\,\,\,s\in\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}},\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}}

is in
\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
if and only if
f
and
g
are both bounded. i.e
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}})
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}})
.

Proof.

Of course if
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}})
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}})
, then
f\otimes g\in\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
uniformly. Let us now suppose that
f\in\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}})
and
g\in\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}})
are such that
f\otimes g\in\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. We may suppose without loss of generality that
\|f\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}})}=\|g\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}})}=1
. Recalling that if
h\in\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
, then for any
R\in\mathcal{D}^{N^{\prime}}
,
0<N^{\prime}<N
,
\|m_{R}h\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N-N^{\prime}})}\leq\|h\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}
, we obtain that for any
S\in\mathcal{D}^{N_{1}}
,

|m_{S}f|\|g\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}})}=\|m_{S}\left(f\otimes g\right)\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}})}\leq\|f\otimes g\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})},

that is for any
S\in\mathcal{D}^{N_{1}}
,

|m_{S}f|\leq\|f\otimes g\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}<\infty.

It follows from the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem that
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{1}})
. The same reasoning show that for any
T\in\mathcal{D}^{N_{2}}
,

|m_{T}g|\leq\|f\otimes g\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}<\infty.

Hence that
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}})
. The proof is complete.
∎

The following will be useful in the text.

L

3.4
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any dyadic rectangle
S\in\mathcal{D}^{N_{1}}
, any open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{N_{2}}
,
N_{1}+N_{2}=N

\|\chi_{S}P_{\Omega}b\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}^{2}\leq C|S||\Omega|\|b\|_{\mathrm{bmo}^{d}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}.

3.2.
Product of two functions in the Haar basis

As previously said, we would like to start by characterizing multipliers from
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
to
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
on the dyadic side. For this, we will need to split the multiplication operator by
\phi
,
M_{\phi}
into several operators that we will then estimate. We start by few definitions and observations.

For
I
a dyadic interval and
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}
, we define
h^{\varepsilon}_{I}
by

h^{\varepsilon}_{I}=\left\{\begin{matrix}h_{I}&\text{if }&\varepsilon=0\\
|I|^{-1/2}|h_{I}|&\text{ if }&\varepsilon=1\end{matrix}\right.

For
R=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\in\mathcal{R}
and
\vec{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{N})
, with
\varepsilon_{j}\in\{0,1\}
, we write

h^{\vec{\varepsilon}}_{R}(t1,\cdots,t_{N})=h^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{I_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots h^{\varepsilon_{N}}_{I_{N}}(t_{N}).

We recall that the sequence
\{h_{R}\}_{R\in\mathcal{R}}
forms an orthonormal basis of
L_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. We observe that given
f,\phi\in L_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
with finite Haar expansions, we can write

f(s,t)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}f_{I}(s)h_{I}(t),\,\,\,s\in\mathbb{T}^{N-1},\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{T}

and

\phi(s,t)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\phi_{I}(s)h_{I}(t),\,\,\,s\in\mathbb{T}^{N-1},\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{T}.

It follows from the usual one-parameter expansion that

(10)

\left(f\phi\right)(s,t)=\Pi_{\phi}^{1}f(s,t)+\Delta_{\phi}^{1}f(s,t)+\Pi_{f}^{1}\phi(s,t)

where

\Pi_{\phi}^{1}f(s,t)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\phi_{I}(s)m_{I}f(s)h_{I}(t),

\Delta_{\phi}^{1}f(s,t)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\phi_{I}(s)f_{I}(s)\frac{\chi_{I}(t)}{|I|}

and

\Pi_{f}^{1}\phi(s,t)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}f_{I}(s)m_{I}\phi(s)h_{I}(t),\,\,\,s\in\mathbb{T}^{N-1},\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{T}.

Now observe that the coefficients in each of the above operators are product of two functions of
(N-1)
variables, we repeat exactly the previous process for each of the coefficients. For example in
\Pi_{\phi}^{1}f
, let us expand
\phi_{I}(s)m_{I}f(s)
. We obtain writing
s=(u,v),\,\,\,u\in\mathbb{T}^{N-2},\,\,\,v\in\mathbb{T}
that

\displaystyle\phi_{I}(u,v)m_{I}f(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}}\phi_{I\times J}(u)m_{I\times J}f(u)h_{J}(v)+\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\phi_{I\times J}(u)f_{I\times J}(u)\frac{\chi_{J}(v)}{|J|}

\displaystyle+

Abstract
. We show that if an exponential sum with multiplicative coefficients is large then the associated multiplicative function is “pretentious”. This leads to applications in the circle method, and a natural interpretation of the local-global principle.



1.
Introduction

Diverse investigations in analytic number theory involve sums like

R_{f}(\alpha,x):=\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n){\rm e}(n\alpha)

where
{\rm e}(t)={\rm e}^{2i\pi t}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
f
is a multiplicative function. For simplicity we will restrict our attention throughout to the class
\mathcal{M}
of completely multiplicative functions

for which

for all

One can approximate any

by a rational
\tfrac{a}{q}
with
(a,q)=1
and

(with, say,
Q=x/(\log x)^{2+\varepsilon}
), so that

\left|\alpha-\frac{a}{q}\right|<\frac{1}{q^{2}}.

If

then

is on a

and Montgomery and Vaughan
[
26
]
proved that

(1.1)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n){\rm e}(n\alpha)\ll\frac{x}{\log x}.

There are many examples of
f
that attain this bound.

For example, no matter what

equals on the primes

In general we have the folklore conjecture

\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n){\rm e}(n\alpha)\ll\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{q}}.

The

-term cannot, in general, be removed since there are many examples for which
R_{f}(\alpha,x)\gg\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{q}}
when

is small.
Our main goal in this paper is to classify these examples and to determine asymptotic formulae for
R_{f}(\alpha,x)
in such cases. We will express

in terms of other quantities that arise naturally in multiplicative number theory:

Theorem 1
.

Let

,

If

\frac{\overline{\chi}(a)f(q)q^{-it}\sqrt{q}}{\phi(q)}I(x,\beta,t)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\

A certain twisted integral

We need estimates for

I(x,0,t)=\frac{x^{it}}{1+it}\text{ and }I(x,\beta,0)=\frac{{\rm e}(\beta x)-1}{2i\pi\beta x},

5.

If
h
is a function of period
q
then for any Dirichlet character
\psi\pmod{r}

divides
q
, and any integer

We modify the argument of section
4.1
to prove the following general result.

Theorem 5
.

Let
h
be a function of period
q
with

. Let

and define the
\psi_{j}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n)h(n)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\end{subarray}}^{J-1}\bigg(\sum_{r_{j}|n|q}k_{j}(n)\kappa_{j}\big(\tfrac{q}{n}\big)G_{h}(n;\psi_{j})\bigg)I(x,0,t_{j})S_{f_{j}}(x)

r_{j}|q\end{subarray}}^{J-1}\max_{r_{j}|n|q}|G_{h}(n;\psi_{j})|\cdot\frac{x}{(\log x)^{\eta-\varepsilon}}\Bigg).

The function
h
enters into the main term in Theorem

only within the pseudo-Gauss sums
G_{\psi}
.
In usual Gauss sums one can always reduce to the case where

We cannot do that here so perhaps we should use a different definition for pseudo-Gauss sums ? For example we can rewrite the main term here:
By expanding the

\frac{1}{q}\sum_{r_{j}|m|q}\frac{m}{\phi(m)}\frac{f(q/m)}{(q/m)^{it}}\,k_{j}((m,\tfrac{q}{m}))G_{h}^{{\dagger}}(m;\psi_{j})

We again use our estimates of

taking any integer

. For each fixed

Therefore the contribution from

equals, writing

and

\sum_{m|\tfrac{q}{r_{j}}}G_{h}(mr_{j};\psi_{j})\sum_{\ell|\tfrac{q}{mr_{j}}}\frac{f(q/\ell mr_{j})}{(q/\ell mr_{j})^{1+it_{j}}}\frac{k_{j}(\ell m)}{\phi(\ell mr_{j})}\mu(\ell)\psi_{j}(\ell)=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{r_{j}|n|q}G_{h}(n;\psi_{j})k_{j}(n)\kappa_{j}\bigg(\frac{q}{n}\bigg)

Oleksiy Klurman asked about the minimum and maximum proportion of solutions to

9.
Binary additive problems

The granddaddy of all additive problems in number theory is, of course, the Goldbach problem.
Typically any problem involving two variables linked by a linear equation is considered very difficult to deal with.
However Brüdern
[
5
]
has developed a method that works in many situations provided
certain hypotheses are fulfilled (see also Theorem 1.9 in
[
21
]
which uses techniques related to those used here). We shall now investigate Brüdern’s idea in our
context. As usual we evaluate

(9.1)

\sum_{a+b=N}f(a)g(b)=\int_{0}^{1}{\rm e}(-\alpha N)R_{f}(\alpha,N)R_{g}(\alpha,N)\mathrm{d}\alpha

where
a,b
are positive integers, and
f
and
g
are multiplicative functions taking values
inside or on the unit circle, using the circle method. The hope is that the main part of
the integral on the right side lies on the major arcs, and the contribution on the minor
arcs is easily proved to be negligible (that is
o(N)
). Let
{\mathfrak{M}}\cup{\mathfrak{m}}

be the partition of
[0,1)
into major and minor arcs, respectively. Then, by the
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathfrak{m}}}{\rm e}(-\alpha N)R_{f}(\alpha,N)R_{g}(\alpha,N)\mathrm{d}\alpha\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathfrak{m}}|R_{f}(\alpha,N)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha\cdot\int_{\mathfrak{m}}|R_{g}(\alpha,N)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle\leqslant N\int_{\mathfrak{m}}|R_{f}(\alpha,N)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha

since
\int_{0}^{1}|R_{g}(\alpha,N)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=\sum_{n\leqslant N}|g(n)|^{2}
. Hence the contribution of the
minor arcs in (
9.1
) is
o(N)
if
\int_{\mathfrak{m}}|R_{f}(\alpha,N)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=o(N)
.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{M}
and
|f(n)|=1
for all integers
n\geqslant 1
. Then we have

\int_{\mathfrak{m}}|R_{f}(\alpha,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=o(x)
if and only if
\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=x+o(x)
. We always have
\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha\leqslant x+o(x)
. Moreover

\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=x+o(x)
if and only if

(9.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x/Q)^{\varepsilon}<p\leqslant x\end{subarray}}\frac{1-{\Re e}(f(p)\overline{\psi}(p)p^{-it})}{p}=o(1).

Proof.

During the proof of Lemma
2
we saw that

\int_{{\mathfrak{M}}}|E_{f}(\alpha,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=o(x)\text{ and }\int_{{\mathfrak{M}}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha\ll x,

so that

\Big|\int_{\mathfrak{M}}M_{f}(\alpha)\overline{E_{f}(\alpha,x)}\mathrm{d}\alpha\Big|\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|E_{f}(\alpha,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)^{1/2}=o(x),

and therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|R_{f}(\alpha,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left|\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)\overline{E_{f}(\alpha,x)}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right|+\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|E_{f}(\alpha,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=o(x).

By Parseval we have

\int_{0}^{1}|R_{f}(\alpha,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=\sum_{n\leqslant x}|f(n)|^{2}=x+O(1),

and so

\int_{\mathfrak{m}}|R_{f}(\alpha,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=o(x)\text{ if and only if }\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=x+o(x).

Now, by Lemma
2
, we have

\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=|\mu(f_{*},x)|^{2}x^{2}\sum_{r|q\leqslant x/Q}r\frac{|\kappa(q/r)|^{2}}{\phi(q)}\int_{\beta=-1/qQ}^{1/qQ}|I(x,\beta,t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\beta.

If
q\ll x/Q
then
x\int_{\beta=-1/qQ}^{1/qQ}|I(x,\beta,t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\beta\asymp 1

by (
3.2
); and it is
1+o(1)
if
q=o(x/Q)
. Using this and
(
6.2
) we deduce that

\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha\leqslant|\mu((f_{*})_{s},x)|^{2}|\mu((f_{*})_{\ell},x)|^{2}\,x(1+o(1))\sum_{r|q\leqslant x/Q}r\frac{|\kappa(q/r)|^{2}}{\phi(q)}

with, say,
z=x/Q
. Since
|\kappa(m))|=\prod_{p|m}|1-f_{*}(p)|
this last sum is

\leqslant r\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geqslant 1\\
p|m\implies p\leqslant x/Q\end{subarray}}\frac{|\kappa(m)|^{2}}{\phi(rm)}=\prod_{p\leqslant x/Q}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-2}\left|1-\frac{f_{*}(p)}{p}\right|^{2}\sim\frac{1}{|\mu((f_{*})_{s},x)|^{2}}.

Now since
|\mu((f_{*})_{\ell},x)|\leqslant 1
, we deduce that
\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha\leqslant(1+o(1))x
.
In order to get equality here we must have (asymptotic) equality in each of the last few steps. In particular we must have
|\mu((f_{*})_{\ell},x)|\sim 1
, and so

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x/Q<p\leqslant x\end{subarray}}\frac{1-{\Re e}(f_{*}(p))}{p}=o(1)

by Halász’s Theorem. In this case we deduce that
\int_{\mathfrak{M}}|M_{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha\sim x
if and only if

\sum_{1\leqslant m=o(x/Qr)}\frac{r}{\phi(mr)}\prod_{p|m}|1-f_{*}(p)|^{2}\sim\frac{1}{|\mu((f_{*})_{s},x)|^{2}},

since the larger
q=mr
in the sum are weighted by an
I
-integral that is
<1
. One can show that this happens if and only if the primes
>(x/Q)^{\varepsilon}
do not make a significant contribution; that is,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x/Q)^{\varepsilon}<p\leqslant x/Q\end{subarray}}\frac{1-{\Re e}(f_{*}(p))}{p}=o(1)

∎

As a corollary we can recover the hypothesis (
9.3
) used by Brüdern
[
5
]
:

Corollary 3
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{M}
and
|f(n)|=1
for all integers
n\geqslant 1
. Then we have

\int_{\mathfrak{m}}|R_{f}(\alpha,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\alpha=o(x)
for all sufficiently large
x
if and only if

(9.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\end{subarray}}\frac{1-{\Re e}(f(p)\overline{\psi}(p)p^{-it})}{p}\ll 1

If (
9.2
) holds then we can evaluate the main term in
certain binary additive problems, like (
9.1
) to obtain

(9.4)

\sum_{a+b=N}f(a)g(b)=\int_{{\mathfrak{M}}}{\rm e}(-\alpha N)M_{f}(\alpha,N)M_{g}(\alpha,N)\mathrm{d}\alpha+o(N)

or

(9.5)

\sum_{n\leqslant N}f(n)g(n+1)=\int_{{\mathfrak{M}}}{\rm e}(\alpha)M_{f}(\alpha,N)M_{g}(-\alpha,N)\mathrm{d}\alpha+o(N)

using only the major arcs.
The actual asymptotics can be obtained without using the circle method, as in
[
21
]
, Corollary 1.4, so we will not give explicit details here.

Introduction

In
[
12
]
, Filippov introduced the concept of
n
-Lie algebras, which is different from Lie algebras
[
16
]
and plays an important role in mathematical fields and string theory
[
1
,
2
,
3

n
-Lie algebra is a vector space

n

[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]]=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}],

where for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\in A

A Poisson
n
-Lie algebra
[
9
]
is a commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
equipped with an
n
-Lie bracket, satisfying the generalized Leibniz rule:

[x_{1},\cdots,x_{i}\cdot\tilde{x},\cdots,x_{n}]=x_{i}\cdot[x_{1},\cdots,\tilde{x},\cdots,x_{n}]+\tilde{x}\cdot[x_{1},\cdots,x_{i},\cdots,x_{n}],

where for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},\tilde{x}\in A
. Poisson
n
-Lie algebras (also called Nambu-Poisson algebras) appeared from the study of Nambu mechanics
[
24
]
and provided an useful method to describe algebraical and geometrical structures of Nambu-Poisson manifolds
[
25
]
. Cantarini and Kac classified all complex linearly compact Poisson
n
-Lie algebras in
[

]
.

In recent years, much work has been done in transposed Poisson algebras (

,

,

,

,

where for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},\tilde{x}\in A

-Lie algebras and Poisson

-Lie algebras lies in the different positions of their multiplications and

-Lie bracket. In the special case

-Lie algebras can be deduced not only from

-Lie algebras and Poisson algebras, but also from

-Lie algebras and transposed Poisson algebras

Preliminaries

In this section, we give some preliminaries and basic results on

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
12
]
)
A
3
-Lie algebra
is a vector space
A
with a 3-ary multi-linear map (
3
-Lie bracket)
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{3}A\rightarrow A
such that the following
Fundamental Identity
holds:

[[x,y,z],u,v]=[[x,u,v],y,z]+[[y,u,v],z,x]+[[z,u,v],x,y],\quad\forall~x,y,z,u,v\in A.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
4
]
)
Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative algebra and
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a
3
-Lie algebra.
The triple
(A,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
is called a
transposed Poisson
3
-Lie algebra
if it satisfies the following condition:

(2.1)

3u\cdot[x,y,z]=[u\cdot x,y,z]+[x,u\cdot y,z]+[x,y,u\cdot z],\quad\forall~x,y,z,u\in A.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
11
]
)
Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a ternary algebra with the multiplication
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
and
\varphi
be a linear map. Then
\varphi
is a
\frac{1}{3}
-derivation
if it satisfies

(2.2)

\varphi[x,y,z]=\frac{1}{3}([\varphi(x),y,z]+[x,\varphi(y),z]+[x,y,\varphi(z)]),\quad\forall~x,y,z\in A.

Proposition 2.4
.

(
[
11
]
)
Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative algebra,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a
3
-Lie algebra, and for all
x,y\in A
,
L_{x}:A\rightarrow A
given by
L_{x}(y)=x\cdot y
be a left multiplication of
(A,\cdot)
. Then
L_{x}
is a
\frac{1}{3}
-derivation of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
if and only if
(A,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
is a transposed Poisson
3
-Lie algebra.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(A_{1},\cdot_{1},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{1})
and
(A_{2},\cdot_{2},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{2})
be two transposed Poisson
3
-Lie algebras. A
homomorphism
of transposed Poisson
3
-Lie algebra is a linear map
\varphi:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{2}
such that

(2.3)

\varphi([x,y,z]_{1})=[\varphi(x),\varphi(y),\varphi(z)]_{2},

(2.4)

\varphi(x\cdot_{1}y)=\varphi(x)\cdot_{2}\varphi(y),\quad\forall~x,y,z\in A_{1}.

An

of transposed Poisson

-Lie algebras is an invertible homomorphism. Two transposed Poisson

-Lie algebras are said to be

if there exists an isomorphism between them. In particular, if
\varphi
is an isomorphism and
(A_{1},\cdot_{1},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{1})=(A_{2},\cdot_{2},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{2})

\varphi
is called an

of transposed Poisson

-Lie algebra. If
(A_{1},\cdot_{1},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{1})
and
(A_{2},\cdot_{2},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{2})

is an isomorphism of

-Lie algebra, in particular, if

-Lie algebra.

(
[

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be an
n
-dimensional
3
-Lie algebra with a basis
\left\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\right\}
,
\varphi:A\rightarrow A
be a linear map. Suppose

(3.6)

[e_{i},e_{j},e_{k}]=\sum_{s=1}^{n}C_{ijk}^{s}e_{s},\quad\forall~C_{ijk}^{s}\in\mathbb{C},~1\leq i,j,k\leq n,

(3.7)

\varphi(e_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{ij}e_{j},\quad\forall~\beta_{ij}\in\mathbb{C},~1\leq i\leq n.

Then
\varphi
is a
\frac{1}{3}
-derivation if and only if the structure constants
C_{ijk}^{s}
and
\beta_{ij}
satisfy

(3.8)

\sum_{s=1}^{n}(C_{sjk}^{t}\beta_{is}+C_{isk}^{t}\beta_{js}+C_{ijs}^{t}\beta_{ks}-3C_{ijk}^{s}\beta_{st})=0,\quad 1\leq t\leq n.

Proof.

By (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), for all
1\leq i,j,k\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}([\varphi(e_{i}),e_{j},e_{k}]+[e_{i},\varphi(e_{j}),e_{k}]+[e_{i},e_{j},\varphi(e_{k})])-\varphi([e_{i},e_{j},e_{k}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}([\sum_{p=1}^{n}\beta_{ip}e_{p},e_{j},e_{k}]+[e_{i},\sum_{p=1}^{n}\beta_{jp}e_{p},e_{k}]+[e_{i},e_{j},\sum_{p=1}^{n}\beta_{kp}e_{p}])-\varphi([e_{i},e_{j},e_{k}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}(\sum_{s=1}^{n}\beta_{is}\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{sjk}^{t}e_{t}+\sum_{s=1}^{n}\beta_{js}\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{isk}^{t}e_{t}+\sum_{s=1}^{n}\beta_{ks}\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{ijs}^{t}e_{t})-\varphi(\sum_{s=1}^{n}C_{ijk}^{s}e_{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{3}\sum_{s=1}^{n}(\beta_{is}C_{sjk}^{t}+\beta_{js}C_{isk}^{t}+\beta_{ks}C_{ijs}^{t})-\sum_{s=1}^{n}C_{ijk}^{s}\beta_{st})e_{t}.

Then
\varphi
is a
\frac{1}{3}
-derivation if and only if (
3.8
) holds.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be an
n
-dimensional
3
-Lie algebra with a basis
\left\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\right\}

3
-Lie bracket

(3.9)

\phi(e_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{ij}e_{j},\quad\forall~\lambda_{ij}\in\mathbb{C},~1\leq i\leq n,

then
\phi
is an automorphism of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
if and only if the structure constants
C_{ijk}^{s}
and
\lambda_{ij}

(3.10)

\sum_{u,v,w}\lambda_{iu}\lambda_{jv}\lambda_{kw}C_{uvw}^{t}-\sum_{s=1}^{n}C_{ijk}^{s}\lambda_{st}=0,\quad 1\leq t\leq n.

Proof.

By (
3.6
) and (

\displaystyle[\phi(e_{i}),\phi(e_{j}),\phi(e_{k})]-\phi([e_{i},e_{j},e_{k}])

\displaystyle[\sum_{u=1}^{n}\lambda_{iu}e_{u},\sum_{v=1}^{n}\lambda_{jv}e_{v},\sum_{w=1}^{n}\lambda_{kw}e_{w}]-\phi(\sum_{s=1}^{n}C_{ijk}^{s}e_{s})

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v,w}(\lambda_{iu}\lambda_{jv}\lambda_{kw})(\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{uvw}^{t}e_{t})-\sum_{s=1}^{n}C_{ijk}^{s}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\lambda_{st}e_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}(\sum_{u,v,w}\lambda_{iu}\lambda_{jv}\lambda_{kw}C_{uvw}^{t}-\sum_{s=1}^{n}C_{ijk}^{s}\lambda_{st})e_{t}.

Then
\phi
is an automorphism of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
if and only if (
3.10
) holds.
∎

Linear constrained Cosserat-shell models including terms up to O ⁡ ( h 5 ) {O}(h^{5}) . Conditional and unconditional existence and uniqueness
Ionel-Dumitrel Ghiba, and, Patrizio Neff

Abstract
In this paper we linearise the recently introduced geometrically nonlinear constrained Cosserat-shell model. In the framework of the linear constrained Cosserat-shell model, we provide a comparison of our linear models with the classical linear Koiter shell model and the “best” first order shell model. For all proposed linear models we show existence and uniqueness based on a Korn’s inequality for surfaces. Keywords: Cosserat shell, 6-parameter resultant shell, in-plane drill rotations, constrained Cosserat elasticity, isotropy, existence of minimisers, linear theories, deformation measures, change of metric, bending measures, change of curvature measures

\displaystyle m(x_{1},x_{2})=y_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})+v(x_{1},x_{2}),

(4.1)

with
v:\omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the infinitesimal shell-midsurface displacement. For the elastic rotation tensor
\overline{Q}_{e,s}\in\rm{SO}(3)
there is a skew-symmetric matrix

\displaystyle\overline{A}_{\vartheta}:={\rm Anti}(\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2},\vartheta_{3}):=\footnotesize\begin{pmatrix}0&-\vartheta_{3}&\vartheta_{2}\\
\vartheta_{3}&0&-\vartheta_{1}\\
-\vartheta_{2}&\vartheta_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{so}(3),\quad\qquad{\rm Anti}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathfrak{so}(3),

(4.2)

where
\vartheta={\rm axl}(\overline{A}_{\vartheta})
denotes the axial vector of
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
, such that

\overline{Q}_{e,s}:=\exp(\overline{A}_{\vartheta})\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\,\overline{A}_{\vartheta}^{k}\;=\;{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}_{3}+\overline{A}_{\vartheta}+\textrm{h.o.t.}

The tensor field
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
is the infinitesimal elastic microrotation. Here, “h.o.t” stands for terms of order higher than linear with respect to
v
and
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
.

Our aim now is to express all the deformation measures and the linearised models in terms of
v
and
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
, as well as in terms of its axial vector
\vartheta
. The following definitions are used to express these quantities in terms of
\vartheta
.
For any column vector
q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and any matrix
M=(M_{1}|M_{2}|M_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
we define the cross-product
[
13
]

\displaystyle q\times M:=

\displaystyle\,(q\times M_{1}\,|\,q\times M_{2}\,|\,q\times M_{3})\qquad\qquad(\mbox{operates on columns})\quad\textrm{and}\vskip 6.0pt

(4.3)

\displaystyle M^{T}\times q^{T}:=

\displaystyle\,-(q\times M)^{T}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(\mbox{operates on rows}).

Note that
M
can also be a
3\times 2
matrix, the definition remains the same.
We note some properties of these operations: for any column vectors
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and any matrices
M,N\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
(or
\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 2}
) we have

\displaystyle(q_{1}\times M)\,q_{2}=

\displaystyle\,\,q_{1}\times(M\,q_{2})\,,\qquad q_{1}^{T}(q_{2}\times M)=\,(q_{1}\times q_{2})^{T}\,M=(q_{1}^{T}\times q_{2}^{T})\,M=-q_{2}^{T}(q_{1}\times M)

(4.4)

and, more general

\displaystyle(q_{1}\times M)N=

\displaystyle\,q_{1}\,\times(M\,N)\,,\qquad N^{T}(q_{2}\times M)=\,-(q_{2}\times N)^{T}\,M=(N^{T}\times q_{2}^{T})\,M\,.

(4.5)

With these relations, the infinitesimal microrotation
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
can be expressed as

\overline{A}_{\vartheta}:=\vartheta\times{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}_{3}={\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}_{3}\times\vartheta^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}.

(4.6)

For
m=y_{0}+v
, starting from

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-T}\,{\rm I}_{m}^{\flat}\,[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}}-\sqrt{[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-T}\,{\rm I}_{y_{0}}^{\flat}\,[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}}=\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\rm{lin}}+\textrm{h.o.t.},

(4.7)

we find the expression of the linear approximation of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
, given by

\displaystyle\ \,[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-T}\footnotesize\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathcal{G}_{\infty}^{\rm{lin}}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\rm{lin}}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-T}\,{\rm I}_{y_{0}}^{\flat}\,[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}}^{-1}\,[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-T}\left((\nabla y_{0})^{T}\nabla v+(\nabla v)^{T}\nabla y_{0}\right)^{\flat}[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-T}\left((\nabla y_{0})^{T}\nabla v+(\nabla v)^{T}\nabla y_{0}\right)^{\flat}[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}

(4.10)

\displaystyle=[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-T}[\underbrace{{\rm{sym}}((\nabla y_{0})^{T}\nabla v)}_{:=\,\mathcal{G}_{\infty}^{\rm lin}}]^{\flat}[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1},

where
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}^{\rm{lin}}
denotes the linear approximation of
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
and we have used the expansion
\sqrt{x+\delta x}-\sqrt{\delta x}=\dfrac{x}{2\,\delta x}+O(x^{2})\quad\forall\,x\geq 0,\delta x>0.

While we have the identity

\mathcal{G}_{\infty}=\mathcal{G}_{\rm{Koiter}}\in{\rm Sym}(2)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{G}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty}=\mathcal{G}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}\in{\rm Sym}(2),

(4.11)

we are only able to find the relation

\mathcal{R}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty}\,=\,\underbrace{\mathcal{R}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}}_{\in{\rm Sym}(2)}-\,\underbrace{\mathcal{G}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}\,{\rm L}_{y_{0}}}_{\not\in{\rm Sym}(2)}\not\in{\rm Sym}(2)

(4.12)

between
\mathcal{R}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty}
and
\;\mathcal{R}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}
and not between
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\rm{Koiter}}
. This is not surprising, since only symmetric stress tensors are taken into account in the classical linear Koiter model, i.e., the internal strain energy does not depend on the skew-symmetric part of the considered strain measures (since it is work conjugate to the skew-symmetric part of the stress tensor). In addition, the linear Koiter model does not consider extra degrees of freedom.

In order to see how the rotation vector
\vartheta
depends on
v
in the linear constrained Cosserat-shell model, let us do something different, and linearise the condition
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}:=\,(\overline{Q}_{\infty}\nabla y_{0})^{T}\nabla m-{\rm I}_{y_{0}}\in{\rm Sym}(2)
to obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla y_{0})^{T}({\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}-\overline{A}_{\vartheta_{\infty}})(\nabla y_{0}+\nabla v)+\textrm{h.o.t.}=

\displaystyle\,(\nabla y_{0}+\nabla v)^{T}({\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}+\overline{A}_{\vartheta_{\infty}})\,(\nabla y_{0})+\textrm{h.o.t.}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad

(4.13)

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}+\overline{A}_{\vartheta_{\infty}})n_{0}+\textrm{h.o.t.}=

\displaystyle\,n,

i.e.

\displaystyle(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)-(\nabla y_{0})^{T}\overline{A}_{\vartheta_{\infty}}\,\nabla y_{0}+\textrm{h.o.t.}=

\displaystyle\,(\nabla v)^{T}(\nabla y_{0})+(\nabla y_{0})^{T}\overline{A}_{\vartheta_{\infty}}(\nabla y_{0})+\textrm{h.o.t.}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad

(4.14)

\displaystyle n=

\displaystyle\,n_{0}+\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0}+\textrm{h.o.t.}

or alternatively

\displaystyle{\rm{skew}}[(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)]=

\displaystyle\,(\nabla y_{0})^{T}[\vartheta_{\infty}\times(\nabla y_{0})]+\textrm{h.o.t.}\qquad\textrm{and}

(4.15)

\displaystyle n=

\displaystyle\,n_{0}+\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0}+\textrm{h.o.t.}

Using that
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{\rm lin}+{\rm h.o.t.}=\mathcal{T}_{\infty}=0
, i.e.
\,(\overline{Q}_{\infty}n_{0})^{T}(\nabla m)=0
, and that the linear approximation of the
transverse shear vector

\mathcal{T}_{\infty}=(\overline{Q}_{\infty}n_{0})^{T}(\nabla m)
is given by
[
13
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty}=n_{0}^{T}(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta_{\infty}}\nabla y_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\mathcal{T}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty}=n_{0}^{T}(\nabla v-\vartheta_{\infty}\times\nabla y_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\mathcal{T}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty}=n_{0}^{T}\nabla v+\vartheta^{T}_{\infty}(n_{0}\times\nabla y_{0})\;=\;n_{0}^{T}\nabla v+(\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0})^{T}\nabla y_{0}\;,

(4.16)

we find

\displaystyle(\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0})^{T}\nabla y_{0}=-n_{0}^{T}\nabla v\;\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \bigl\langle a_{\alpha},\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0}\bigr\rangle=-\bigl\langle n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v\bigr\rangle\;\ \Leftrightarrow\ \vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0}=-\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\bigl\langle n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v\bigr\rangle\,a^{\alpha}.

(4.17)

Hence, we obtain finally

\displaystyle n=n_{0}-\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\bigl\langle n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v\bigr\rangle\,a^{\alpha}+\textrm{h.o.t.}

(4.18)

We can also express the
2\times 2
skew-symmetric matrix
{\rm C}=\sqrt{\det{\rm I}_{y_{0}}}\footnotesize\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
using the cross product.
Similar as in
[
13
]
, we have

(a^{1}\,|\,a^{2})\;{\rm C}\;=-n_{0}\times\nabla y_{0}\,.

(4.19)

Thus, we deduce

{\rm C}\;=-(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(n_{0}\times\nabla y_{0})=(n_{0}\times\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla y_{0})\,,

(4.20)

and it holds

\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},n_{0}\bigr\rangle\,{\rm C}\;=-(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\vartheta_{\infty}\times\nabla y_{0})=(\vartheta_{\infty}\times\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla y_{0})\,.

(4.21)

Let us now obtain an alternative form of
\mathcal{G}^{\rm lin}_{\infty}
.
We have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{G}^{\rm lin}_{\infty}&=&(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta_{\infty}}\nabla y_{0})\;=\;(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\partial_{x_{1}}u+a_{1}\times\vartheta_{\infty}\;|\;\partial_{x_{2}}u+a_{2}\times\vartheta_{\infty})\\
&=&(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)+(a_{1}\,|\,a_{2})^{T}(a_{1}\times\vartheta_{\infty}\;|\;a_{2}\times\vartheta_{\infty})\vskip 2.84526pt=(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)+\footnotesize\begin{pmatrix}0&\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},a_{1}\times a_{2}\bigr\rangle\\
-\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},a_{1}\times a_{2}\bigr\rangle&0\end{pmatrix}]\\
&=&(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)+\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},n_{0}\bigr\rangle\footnotesize\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{\det{\rm I}_{y_{0}}}\\
-\sqrt{\det{\rm I}_{y_{0}}}&0\end{pmatrix}=(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)+\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},n_{0}\bigr\rangle\,{\rm C}.\end{array}

(4.22)

Remember that
{\rm C}
is
a
2\times 2
skew-symmetric matrix.
Using in the following (
4.22
) and the condition
\mathcal{G}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty}\in{\rm Sym}(2)
(i.e.
\mbox{skew}\,(\mathcal{G}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty})=0
), we have

\displaystyle\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},n_{0}\bigr\rangle\,{\rm C}=

\displaystyle\,-\mbox{skew}\,\big[(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)\big]\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},n_{0}\bigr\rangle{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}_{2}=-\mbox{skew}\,\big[(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)\big]\,{\rm C}^{-1}

(4.23)

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad 2\,\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty}

\displaystyle,n_{0}\bigr\rangle=\,-{\rm tr}(\mbox{skew}\,\big[(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)\big]\,{\rm C}^{-1})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},n_{0}\bigr\rangle=\,-\frac{1}{2}\,{\rm tr}(\mbox{skew}\,\big[(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)\big]\,{\rm C}^{-1}).

Thus, the rotation vector
\vartheta_{\infty}
is completely determined by the relations (
4.17
) and (
4.23
) as a function of the midsurface displacement
v
, by knowing its component in the direction of
n_{0}
and its components in the tangent plane.

Further, we can write

\mathcal{R}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty}=(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\vartheta_{\infty}\times\partial_{x_{1}}n_{0}\;|\;\vartheta_{\infty}\times\partial_{x_{1}}n_{0})-(\nabla y_{0})^{T}\big(\partial_{x_{1}}(\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0})\;|\;\partial_{x_{2}}(\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0})\big).

(4.24)

To compute the last term in (
4.24
) we use (
4.17
) and write

\displaystyle-\partial_{x_{\beta}}(\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0})=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{x_{\beta}}\big[\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\bigl\langle n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v\bigr\rangle a^{\alpha}\big]\;=\;\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\partial_{x_{\beta}}[\bigl\langle n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v\bigr\rangle\,a^{\alpha}]+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}[\bigl\langle n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v\bigr\rangle\partial_{x_{\beta}}a^{\alpha}]\vskip 6.0pt

(4.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}[\bigl\langle n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}v\bigr\rangle\,a^{\alpha}]+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}[\bigl\langle\partial_{x_{\beta}}n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v\bigr\rangle a^{\alpha}]+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}[\bigl\langle n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v\bigr\rangle(-\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma=1,2}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\gamma\beta}\,a^{\gamma}+b^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,n_{0})]\vskip 6.0pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}[\bigl\langle n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}v-\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma=1,2}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{x_{\gamma}}v\bigr\rangle a^{\alpha}]-\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\gamma=1,2}[\bigl\langle a_{\gamma},\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v\bigr\rangle b^{\gamma}_{\beta}\,a^{\alpha}]+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}[\bigl\langle n_{0},\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v\bigr\rangle\,b^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,n_{0}]\;,

due to Gauß and Weingarten formulas
[
20
]
. Therefore, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-(\nabla y_{0})^{T}\big(\partial_{x_{1}}(\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0})\;|\;\partial_{x_{2}}(\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0})\big)&=&\mathcal{R}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}-(\partial_{x_{1}}v\,|\,\partial_{x_{2}}v)^{T}\,(a_{1}\,|\,a_{2})\footnotesize\begin{pmatrix}b^{1}_{1}&b^{1}_{2}\vskip 4.0pt\\
b^{2}_{1}&b^{2}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=&\mathcal{R}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}-\big[(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)\big]^{T}\,{\rm L}_{y_{0}}\;.\end{array}

(4.26)

To compute the first term in (
4.24
) we decompose
\;\vartheta_{\infty}=\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},n_{0}\bigr\rangle\,n_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}[\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},a^{\alpha}\bigr\rangle\,a_{\alpha}]\;
and write

\vartheta_{\infty}\times\partial_{x_{\beta}}n_{0}=\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},n_{0}\bigr\rangle n_{0}\times\partial_{x_{\beta}}n_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}[\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},a^{\alpha}\bigr\rangle a_{\alpha}\times\partial_{x_{\beta}}n_{0}]\;.

(4.27)

Using

n_{0}\times\partial_{x_{\beta}}n_{0}=n_{0}\times(-\sum_{\alpha=1,2}b^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,a_{\alpha})=\,\sqrt{\det{\rm I}_{y_{0}}}\;(-b^{1}_{\beta}\,a^{2}+b^{2}_{\beta}\,a^{1})

(4.28)

and

a_{\alpha}\times\partial_{x_{\beta}}n_{0}=a_{\alpha}\times(-\sum_{\gamma=1,2}b^{\gamma}_{\beta}\,a_{\gamma})=\,-\sum_{\gamma=1,2}b^{\gamma}_{\beta}\,\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma}\,n_{0}\;,\,

(4.29)

with
\epsilon_{12}=-\epsilon_{21}=1
, this yields

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\vartheta_{\infty}\times\partial_{x_{1}}n_{0}\;|\;\vartheta_{\infty}\times\partial_{x_{2}}n_{0})&=&\sqrt{\det{\rm I}_{y_{0}}}\;\bigl\langle\vartheta_{\infty},n_{0}\bigr\rangle\,(\nabla y_{0})^{T}\,(-b^{1}_{1}\,a^{2}+b^{2}_{1}\,a^{1}\;|\;-b^{1}_{2}\,a^{2}+b^{2}_{2}\,a^{1})\\

b^{2}_{1}&b^{2}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\\

and inserting here (

(4.31)

Substituting (

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\,\,\mathcal{R}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}-\mbox{sym}\,\big[(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)\big]\,{\rm L}_{y_{0}},\qquad\qquad\mathcal{G}^{\rm lin}_{\infty}=\,\mbox{sym}\,\big[(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)\big]=\,\mathcal{G}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm lin},

which can be written by virtue of (

\mathcal{R}^{\rm{lin}}_{\infty}\,=\,\,\mathcal{R}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}-\,\mathcal{G}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}\,{\rm L}_{y_{0}}.

Some direct calculations are also possible, see also (

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{\infty}=

\displaystyle\,{\rm polar}((\nabla m\,|\,n)[\nabla\Theta]^{-1})={\rm polar}([(\nabla y_{0}+\nabla v\,|\,n_{0}+\vartheta_{\infty}\times n_{0})[\nabla\Theta]^{-1})

\displaystyle=

(4.35)

4.2
The constrained linear
O(h^{5})
-Cosserat shell model. Conditional existence

We can now easily obtain the linearised constrained Cosserat

-shell model by inserting the linearised deformation measures in the quadratic variational problem of the nonlinear constrained Cosserat model and the obtained minimization problem is to find the midsurface displacement vector field

minimizing on

\displaystyle I=\int_{\omega}\,\,\Big[

\displaystyle+\Big(h-{\rm K}\,\dfrac{h^{3}}{12}\Big)\,W_{\mathrm{curv}}\big((\nabla\vartheta_{\infty}\,|\,0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}\big)\vskip 7.11317pt

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{sym}\,\big[(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v)\big]

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\beta}v_{\alpha}+\partial_{\alpha}v_{\beta})-\sum_{\gamma=1,2}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}v_{\gamma}-b_{\alpha\beta}v_{3}\Big)_{\alpha\beta}

6
Conclusion

We have considered the geometrically nonlinear constrained Cosserat shell model and we deduced it’s linearization. The constrained model is interesting in it’s own right since it provides a natural bridge to more classical shell models, in which no independent triad of directors is considered. We were able to show existence and uniqueness, using precisely those tools that are usually employed: the classical Korn’s inequality on a surface. However, one important caveat remained: the results provide only a conditional existence, since the requirements on the admissible set are to strong.
Therefore, we were led to modify the variational problem. More precisely, we only consider the symmetric parts of some strain measures in the elastic energy and this allowed us to prove unconditional existence results. Sine shell theory is an approximation anyway, we believe that the modified approach is vastly superior and merits further investigation if one is interested in remaining close to more classical shell models.

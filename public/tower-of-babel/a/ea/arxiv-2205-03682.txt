Reduced superfluid density in a doped spin liquid candidate
K. Wakamatsu 1, Y. Ueno 1, K. Miyagawa 1, H. Taniguchi 2, K. Kanoda 1,∗

Abstract
A quantum spin liquid (QSL) would be an exotic stage for superconductivity. A promising candidate for a doped QSL is the organic triangular-lattice system, κ \kappa -(BEDT-TTF) 4 Hg 2.89 Br 8 , which hosts a non-Fermi liquid and magnetism of a QSL nature and shows superconductivity upon cooling. Here, we report that its superfluid density is anomalously reduced, indicating the existence of substantial incoherent spectral weight and weak superconducting phase stiffness. Moreover, the ratio of the superconducting transition temperature to the nominal Fermi temperature is as large as 0.1, orders of magnitude beyond typical BCS values. These observations in a system free from competing orders that complicate the similar issue in underdoped cuprates give a clue to the enigmatic missing superfluid density in doped Mott insulators.

determines the fundamental nature of superconductivity, such as phase stiffness

We evaluated the

Among several experimental ways to estimate the

The magnetization was measured with Quantum Design SQUID magnetometers (MPMS-XL and MPMS-5S),
where a crystal was fixed to a plastic-straw sample holder with a Kapton film.
The net sample magnetization was obtained as the difference between the magnetizations with

The sample used in the present work is a rhombic single crystal with diagonal lengths of

\sim

To date, the difficulty in obtaining crystals showing bulk superconductivity at

The present work at ambient pressure was, nevertheless, made possible by confirming
the bulk superconductivity in the used crystal in terms of the ac susceptibility

Spin susceptibility in the normal state was also confirmed to

\lambda_{\parallel}
can be deduced from the field dependence of the magnetization,

The

Clearly, the superfluid density in
\kappa

that of the half-filled systems, suggesting that only a fractional spectral weight approximately
equivalent to the doped hole density contributes to the superconducting condensate.
This behavior is called gossamer superconductivity,
which is argued to emerge in doped Mott insulators.

The superfluid density is related to the phase stiffness, which is expressed as

\hbar^{2}d
/4
\mu_{0}e^{2}\lambda_{\parallel}^{2}

(
d

\kappa

This value is considerably smaller than the values of the BCS case,
typically 10

-10

As the phase stiffness measures the macroscopic phase coherence in energy,
this small value in
\kappa

strong fluctuations in superconductivity
[

17

From the penetration depth, the nominal Fermi temperature

This is a large value beyond the BCS framework, where

is
several orders of magnitude smaller than

As seen above, the superfluid density takes a reduced value as if
only the doped carriers condense into the superconducting state.
Such behavior is reminiscent of underdoped cuprates,
in which the normal-state carrier density
[
36
]
,
the Drude weight
[
37
]
and the superfluid density
[
32
,
38
]

are discussed to be governed by the doped carriers.
Theoretically, such a significant reduction in the superfluid density is suggested to occur
when double occupancies of particles are strongly prohibited
[
6
,
39
,
40
]
.
Previous transport studies on
\kappa
-HgBr at low pressures revealed that
the mobile carriers are in a quantum critical state with a spin-liquid background

[
15
,
16
,
41
,
42
]
,
which may bring about an incoherent part, preventing the entire spectral weight from
contributing to the conduction and/or superconducting condensate.
It is argued for underdoped cuprates that some competing or hidden orders are responsible for
the reduction in the superfluid density
[
43
]
because various orders or
anomalies appear in the spin and charge degrees of freedom competing with
superconductivity
[
44
,
45
]
.
In this respect,
\kappa
-HgBr is distinct from cuprates in that
no such competing orders have ever been observed,
probably because a QSL free form order is the mother phase of the superconductivity.
Although in recent years the possibility of competing orders in doped triangular lattices
has theoretically suggested in certain parameter spaces
[
46
,
47
]
,
the observation of a considerable reduction in the superfluid density without
apparent competing orders in the present system would offer a focused target to
diverse theoretical challenges with regard to the issue of missing superfluid spectral weight.

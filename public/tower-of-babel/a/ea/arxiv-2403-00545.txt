Twisted restricted conformal blocks of vertex operator algebras II: twisted restricted conformal blocks on totally ramified orbicurves
Xu Gao, Jianqi Liu, Yiyi Zhu

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a notion of twisted restricted conformal blocks on totally ramified orbicurves and establish an isomorphism between the space of twisted restricted conformal blocks and the space of twisted conformal blocks. The relationships among twisted (restricted) conformal blocks, g g -twisted (restricted) correlation functions, and twisted intertwining operators are explored. Furthermore, by introducing a geometric generalization of Zhu’s algebra and its modules, we obtain a description of the space of coinvariants by modules over associative algebras and show it is finite-dimensional under some conditions. In particular, a more conceptual proof of the g g -twisted fusion rules theorem in vertex operator algebra theory is provided.

Introduction

This paper is the second part of a series aimed at investigating the general theory of twisted (restricted) conformal blocks of vertex operator algebras (VOAs).
Here, we focus on the twisted restricted VOA-conformal blocks on a totally ramified orbicurve and shift the viewpoint from
correlation functions
to

An overview of twisted modules of vertex operator algebras and fusion rules among twisted modules was provided in

.

1.a
History of VOA-conformal blocks

A few years after Belavin, Polyakov, and Zamolodchikov introduced the two-dimensional conformal field theory with Virasoro symmetries
Belavin et al. [1984]
, Tsuchiya, Kanie, Ueno, and Yamada formulated a mathematically rigorous construction of the holomorphic conformal field theory with gauge symmetries given by affine Kac-Moody algebras
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
in
Tsuchiya and Kanie [1987]
,
Tsuchiya et al. [1989]
, corresponding to the Wess-Zumino-Novikov-Witten model in physics.
As the mathematical counterpart of the physical conformal blocks in
Belavin et al. [1984]
,
the space of conformal blocks (vacua) and the space of coinvariants (covacua) on an
n
-pointed stable curve were introduced in
Tsuchiya et al. [1989]
and constructed explicitly using the integrable highest-weight representations in a fixed level
\ell
over
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
.
The algebro-geometric formulation in
Tsuchiya et al. [1989]
facilitated extensive mathematical studies of the WZNW conformal field theory.

From the VOA point of view, the WZNW conformal field theory can be formulated using the affine VOAs
L_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\ell,0)
constructed by Frenkel and Zhu in
Frenkel and Zhu [1992]
.
More generally, the study of conformal field theory can be understood as the study of the modular tensor category structure on the representations of a VOA (see
Huang [1997]
).
In such a study, the notion of
intertwining operators
, introduced in
Frenkel et al. [1993]
, plays a crucial role: the dimension of the space of intertwining operators is a mathematical formulation of the notion of
fusion rules
.
The finiteness of the fusion rules and a formula to compute them are thus fundamental in the theory. In
Frenkel and Zhu [1992]
, I. Frenkel and Zhu claimed such a formula using the bimodule theory over
Zhu’s algebra

A(V)
.
This formula, known as the

, was first proved by Li in
Li [1999]
,
Li [2001]
under the framework of
A(V)
-theory.

The general case, with an extended formulation on arbitrary stable curves, was established by Damiolini, Gibney, Krashen, and Tarasca in

Meanwhile, in the analytic realm, a similar long march toward the proof of the

The notion of
twisted modules
of VOAs originated in the realization of irreducible representations of twisted affine Lie algebras
Lepowsky [1985]
, the construction of the celebrated moonshine module
Frenkel et al. [1988]
, and the study of orbifold models in conformal field theory
Dixon et al. [1985]
,
Dijkgraaf et al. [1989]
.
The

,

,

,

,

However, despite this progress, many fundamental topics remain unclear, including a general version of

The difficulty of orbifold theory arises from its reliance on the intricate formal calculus of Puiseux series, making constructions and computations exceedingly challenging.
Backing to its physical origin, the mathematical formulation of

A special but important case of such a formulation has been developed in
Gao et al. [2023]
, where we applied it to produce a

-twisted version of the

following the approach in
Liu [2023]
.
However, the proof employed in
Gao et al. [2023]
still necessitates a substantial amount of computation.
Given the theoretical nature of conformal blocks, they should offer a proof requiring less computation compared to correlation functions.
The algebro-geometric formulation of
twisted conformal blocks
by Frenkel and Szczesny in
Frenkel and Ben-Zvi [2004]
,
Frenkel and Szczesny [2004]
laid a foundation for this approach.

We believe the consequences of

In a recent work

2.b
Geometry of a totally ramified orbicurve

The following is our main geometric object under consideration.

2.1 Definition
.

An
orbicurve
is a
Deligne-Mumford stack
that is obtained as the quotient of a smooth algebraic curve by a finite automorphism group
G
.
We represent this stack as a
ramified covering

\mathfrak{x}\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{X}}\to\mathfrak{X}
with the
Galois group

G
and the dense
unramified locus

{\mathfrak{X}}^{\circ}
.
Sheaves on this orbicurve can be understood as
G
-equivariant sheaves on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{X}}
.
An orbicurve
\mathfrak{X}
is called
totally ramified
if each
branch point

\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{X}
is
totally ramified
in the sense that
\mathfrak{p}
has a unique lifting in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{X}}
. It follows that the group
G
has to be cyclic. In the rest of this paper, we assume that
G
has order
T
.

2.2 Example

(
twisted projective line
)
.

In
[
Gao et al., 2023
, §2.2]
, we have introduced an
ad-hoc
construction of the
twisted projective line
as a ramified covering
\mathfrak{x}\colon\overline{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Roughly speaking, it corresponds to the inclusion
\mathbb{C}[z]\subseteq\mathbb{C}[z^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{T}}}]
, where the symbol
z^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{T}}}
satisfies
(z^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{T}}})^{T}=z
.
The branch points on this covering are
0
and
\infty
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and the unramified locus is
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,\infty\}
.

2.3 Example

(
cyclic covering of the 3-pointed projective line
)
.

Consider the equation (where
w\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
)

where
a
,

, and

are all coprime to

. Then there is a connected smooth projective curve
\overline{C}_{w}
irrational to the projective curve obtained as the compactification of the affine curve defined by the above equation.
The projection

induces a totally ramified covering
\mathfrak{x}\colon\overline{C}_{w}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
with Galois group of order
T
and branch points

.

2.4 Remark
.

The obicurves in
Examples

2.2
and
2.3
forms a family

, whose fiber at

is

while the fiber at

and
\infty
are
\overline{C}
and its reverse.

2.5 Example

(
trivially ramified orbicurve
)
.

Our definition of an orbicurve allows the Galois group to be trivial. In this case, an orbicurve is the same as a smooth curve
\mathfrak{X}
equipped with a specified dense open
{\mathfrak{X}}^{\circ}

We call points in
\mathfrak{X}\setminus{\mathfrak{X}}^{\circ}

even if they are actually unramified.
Such orbicurves are totally ramified.

2.6 Example

(
cyclic covering of prime order
)
.

When

is a prime number, the Galois group

is simple, and thus the orbicurve

is automatically totally ramified.

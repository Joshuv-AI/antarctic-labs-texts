Keywords: Choquard equation, Nonlocal operator, Ground state solution, Pohozǎev identity.

Abstract.
In this paper, we study nonlinear Choquard equations

(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{2}}u=(I_{\alpha}*{|u|^{p}})|u|^{p-2}u\ \ {\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N},\ \ \ u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1)

where
(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a nonlocal operator,
p>0
,
N\geq 2
and
I_{\alpha}
is the Riesz potential with order
\alpha\in(0,N)
.
We show that there is a ground state solution to problem (
1
) if
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}
and
no solution to problem (
1
) if
0<p\leq\frac{N+\alpha}{N+1}
or
p\geq\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}
. Furthermore, the existence of infinity many solutions to
problem (
1
) is discussed when
p
satisfies that
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}
.

1.
Introduction

Our purpose of this paper is to consider the
solutions of nonlinear Choquard equations

(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{2}}u=(I_{\alpha}*{|u|^{p}})|u|^{p-2}u\ \ {\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N},\ \ \ u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.1)

where
p>0
,
N\geq 2
,
I_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
is
the Riesz potential with order
\alpha\in(0,N)
given by

I_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\alpha}{2})}{2^{\alpha}\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})|x|^{N-\alpha}},

here
\Gamma
is gamma function, see
[
12
]
.
The nonlocal operator
(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{2}}
can be characterized as
\mathcal{F}((-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi)(\xi)=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{F}(\phi)(\xi)
,
here
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform.
The Hilbert space
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as

H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\ (-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{4}}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\}

with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{4}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

As early as in 1954, in a pioneering work of Pekar
[
11
]
where described the quantum mechanics of a polaron, the nonlinear Choquard equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u+u=(I_{2}\ast|u|^{2})u&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},\\[5.69054pt]
\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0\end{array}\right.

is appeared. For general case, Moroz-Van Schaftingen in
[
9
]
studied the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u+u=(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0,\end{array}\right.

they obtained the results of existence, qualitative properties and decay asymptotic.
In this paper, we consider the related nonlocal problem (
1.1
).
To state our results, we first introduce
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality which states that if
s\in(1,\frac{N}{\alpha})
,
then for every
v\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
I_{\alpha}*v\in L^{\frac{Ns}{N-\alpha s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|I_{\alpha}*v|^{\frac{Ns}{N-\alpha s}}dx\leq C(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{s}dx)^{\frac{N}{N-\alpha s}},

where
C>0
depends on
\alpha
,
N
and
s
. We know that the fractional Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
t\in[2,2^{\sharp}]
, where
2^{\sharp}:=\frac{2N}{N-1}
, also note that
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if and only if
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}
.
Now we state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
N\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,N)
.

(i)
If
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}
,
then there exists a
C^{2}
positive ground state solution to problem (
1.1
).

(ii)
If
0<p\leq\frac{N+\alpha}{N+1}
or
p\geq\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}
, then there is no nontrivial solution to problem (
1.1
).

To prove the existence of solutions in Theorem
1.1
when
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}
, we apply the critical points theory to the associated minimizing problem

M_{p}=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{4}}u\right|^{2}dx:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})(x)|u(x)|^{p}\,dx=1\right\}.

(1.2)

We note that the minimization of
M_{p}
is a nontrivial solution of
problem (
1.1
). Here we use the concentration compactness argument and a nonlocal version of Brezis-Lieb lemma to prove that
M_{p}
can be achieved.
Then we establish Pohozǎev identity to obtain the nonexistence results in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
and
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}
.
Then there exists infinitely many distinct solutions to problem (
1.1
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
15
]

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
s>1
and
\{w_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in
L^{s}(\Omega)
.
If
w_{m}\to w
almost everywhere on
\Omega
as
m\to\infty
, then for every
q\in[1,s]
, we have that

\lim_{m\to\infty}{\int_{\Omega}|{|w_{m}|^{q}-|w_{m}-w|^{q}-|w|^{q}}|^{\frac{s}{q}}}\,dx=0.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}
and
\{w_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in

L^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Assume that

(i)
{w_{m}}
weakly converges to
{w}
in
L^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

(ii)
{w_{m}}\to w
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Then

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}|^{p})(x)|w_{m}(x)|^{p}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p}\,dx\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{p}\,dx.

Proof.

We observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}|^{p})(x)|w_{m}(x)|^{p}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}))(x)(|w_{m}(x)|^{p}-|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p})\,dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}))(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p}\,dx.

By the Hölder inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}))(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}-|w|^{p}))(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left({\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\left(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}-|w|^{p})\right)|^{\frac{2N}{N-\alpha}}}(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-\alpha}{2N}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p})^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p}\,dx.

Using Lemma
2.1
with
q=p
and
s=\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}
, we know that
|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}\to|w|^{p}
,
strongly in
L^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
m\to\infty
. By the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, this implies that

I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p})\to I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p}
in
L^{\frac{2N}{N-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
m\to\infty
. Since
|w_{m}-w|^{p}\rightharpoonup 0

in
L^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
m\to\infty
, then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|(w_{m}-w)|^{p}))(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p}\,dx\to 0

as
m\to\infty
. This ends the proof.
\Box

3.
Ground state solution

In this section, we study the existence of ground state solutions of problem (
1.1
). To this end,
let us consider the following minimizing problem

M_{p}=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{4}}u\right|^{2}dx:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})(x)|u(x)|^{p}\,dx=1\right\}.

(3.1)

Proposition 3.1
.

The minimizing problem
M_{p}
is achieved by a function
v\in{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, which is a solution of problem (
1.1
)
up to a translation.

To prove this result, we first introduce some lemma as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
2\leq q<2^{\sharp}=\frac{2N}{N-1}
and
r>0
. Suppose that
\{v_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and

\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\int_{B_{r}(z)}|v_{m}(x)|^{q}\,dx\to 0

as
m\to\infty
.
Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|v_{m}|^{p})(x)|v_{m}(x)|^{p}\,dx\to 0

as
m\to\infty
for
\frac{(N+\alpha)q}{2N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}
.

Proof.

Let
l=t=\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}
, by Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|v_{m}|^{p})(x)|v_{m}(x)|^{p}\,dx\ =\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v_{m}(x)|^{p}|v_{m}(z)|^{p}}{|x-z|^{N-\alpha}}\,dxdz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}\|{|v_{m}|^{p}}\|_{L^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|{|v_{m}|^{p}}\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\ =c_{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{m}(x)|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}

and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{r}(z)}|v_{m}(x)|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2Np}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\left(\int_{B_{r}(z)}|v_{m}(x)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]^{1-\lambda}\left[\left(\int_{B_{r}(z)}|v_{m}(x)|^{2^{\sharp}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{\sharp}}}\right]^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}{\|v_{m}\|}^{1-\lambda}_{L^{q}(B_{r}(z))}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{4}}v_{m}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{\lambda}{2}},

where
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and

\lambda=\frac{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}-q}{\frac{2N}{N-1}-q}\cdot\frac{\frac{2N}{N-1}}{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}.

Choosing some suitable
q
and
p
such that
\lambda=\frac{2}{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(z)}|v_{m}(x)|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\leq c_{2}^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}{\|v_{m}\|}^{(1-\lambda)\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}_{L^{q}(B_{r}(z))}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{4}}v_{m}|^{2}\,dx\right).

Now, covering
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by balls of radius
r
, in such a way that each point of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is contained in at most
N+1
balls, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(z)}|v_{m}(x)|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(N+1)c_{2}^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}[\int_{B_{r}(z)}{|v_{m}(x)|}^{q}\,dx]^{\frac{(1-\lambda)\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{4}}v_{m}|^{2}\,dx\right).

Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|v_{m}|^{p}(x))|v_{m}(x)|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}\left((\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\int_{B_{r}(z)}|v_{m}(x)|^{q}\,dx)^{\frac{1}{q}(\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}-2)}(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{4}}v_{m}|^{2}\,dx)\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}.

The proof is complete.
\Box

We now prove proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1

Let
\{v_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a minimizing sequence of
M_{p}
, which satisfies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla v_{m}(x)|^{2}+|v_{m}(x)|^{2})\,dx\to M_{p}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|v_{m}|^{p})(x)|v_{m}(x)|^{p}\,dx=1.

By Lemma
3.1
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\delta=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B_{1}(\tilde{x})}|v_{m}(x)|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx>0.

So we may find
x_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

\int_{B_{1}(x_{m})}|v_{m}(x)|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx>\frac{\delta}{2}.

Let
w_{m}(x)=v_{m}(x-x_{m})
, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}|^{p})(x)|w_{m}(x)|^{p}\,dx=1,\ \ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta+id)^{\frac{1}{4}}w_{m}|^{2}\,dx\to M_{p},

which yields, up to a subsequence, that
w_{m}\rightharpoonup w
in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
w_{m}\to w
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then

\int_{B_{1}(0)}|w_{m}(x)|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx>\frac{\delta}{2}.

Since
H_{loc}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L_{loc}^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is compact, so we have that
\int_{B_{1}(0)}|w(x)|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\geq\frac{\delta}{2}>0
,we can claim that
w\neq 0
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{p}\,dx\neq 0
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}|^{p})(x)|w_{m}(x)|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{p}\,dx+\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p}\,dx

and

\displaystyle M_{p}=\lim_{m\to\infty}{\|w_{m}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}={\|w\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}+\lim_{m\to\infty}{\|w_{m}-w\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle M_{p}\left(\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}

\displaystyle\ \ +M_{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}+M_{p}\left(1-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}

Then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{p}\,dx=1
. As a consequent, we get that
M_{p}=\|w\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}
.

The proof is completed.
\Box

4.
Regularity

To consider the regularity of solutions to problem (
1.1
),
we transform (
1.1
) to the following extension problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta w(x,y)+w(x,y)=0,\quad\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1},\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}(x,0)=(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x,0)|w(x,0)|^{p-2}w(x,0)\ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

as in the work of
[
14
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1})
is a weak solution of (
4.1
). Then
w\in L^{q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1})
. Moreover,
w\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1})
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
,
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
and for small fixed
R
,

\varphi(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle 1\quad\quad(x,y)\in B_{R}(0),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle 0\quad\quad(x,y)\not\in B_{2R}(0),\end{array}\right.\qquad|\nabla\varphi|\leq\frac{C}{R}.

Multiplying (
4.1
) by
\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}}w
, where
\beta_{0}>0
,and integrating by parts, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}(\nabla w\nabla(\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}}w)+\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+2})\,dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x,0)\varphi^{2}(x,0)|w(x,0)|^{2\beta_{0}+p}\,dx.

(4.2)

Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}\nabla w\nabla(\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}}w)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}\left((2\beta_{0}+1))\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}}|\nabla w|^{2}+2\varphi|w|^{2\beta_{0}}w\nabla\varphi\nabla w\right)\,dxdy.

So we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}(2\beta_{0}+1))\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+p}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}\left(\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+2}+2\varphi|w|^{2\beta_{0}}w\nabla\varphi\nabla w\right)\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+p}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}2\varphi|w|^{2\beta_{0}+1}w\nabla\varphi\nabla w\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+p}\,dx+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}2\varepsilon\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}}w|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+2C_{\varepsilon}w^{2(\beta_{0}+1)}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\right)\,dxdy.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}|\nabla(\varphi|w|^{\beta_{0}}w)|^{2}\,dxdy

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\beta_{0}+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}\left((2\beta_{0}+1)\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}}|\nabla w|^{2}+2|w|^{2\beta_{0}+2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\right)\,dxdy,

so we deduce from (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}(|\nabla(\varphi|w|^{\beta_{0}}w)|^{2}+\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+2})\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C(\beta_{0}+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}\left((2\beta_{0}+1)\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}}|\nabla w|^{2}+2|w|^{2\beta_{0}+2|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}+\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+2}\right)\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C(\beta_{0}+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+p}\,dx+C(\beta_{0}+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}w^{2(\beta_{0}+1)}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dxdy.

By the Sobolev theorem, we have that

\displaystyle C(R)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}(|\varphi||w|^{\beta_{0}+1})^{\frac{2N}{N-1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}(|\varphi||w|^{\beta_{0}+1})^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-2}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}\left(|\nabla(\varphi|w|^{\beta_{0}}w)|^{2}+\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+2})\,dxdy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\beta_{0}+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+p}\,dx+C(\beta_{0}+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}w^{2(\beta_{0}+1)}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dxdy.

By the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})\varphi^{2}|w|^{2\beta_{0}+p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{w^{p}(z)w^{2\beta_{0}+p}(x)\varphi^{2}(x)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}\,dzdx\leq\||w|^{p}\|_{L^{t_{1}}}\||w|^{2\beta_{0}+p}\varphi^{2}\|_{L_{t_{2}}},

where
\frac{1}{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{t_{2}}+\frac{N-\alpha}{N}=2
, we let
pt_{1}=2^{\sharp}=\frac{2N}{N-1}
and
\frac{1}{t_{2}}=1+\frac{\alpha}{N}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}
.
Note that

(2\beta_{0}+p)t_{2}\leq\frac{2N}{N-1},

we have that

\displaystyle\beta_{0}\leq\frac{\frac{2N}{N-1}-pt_{2}}{2t_{2}}=\frac{N}{N-1}(1+\frac{\alpha}{N}-\frac{p(N-1)}{2N})-\frac{p}{2}=\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}-p

Let
\beta_{0}=\frac{N+\alpha}{N-1}-p>0
,
(2\beta_{1}+p)t_{2}=2^{\sharp}(\beta_{0}+1)
, we have that

6.

The aim of this section is to establish the infinitely many bounded solutions in Theorem

which is constrained in a manifold and obtain the infinitely many critical points of the functional. To this end,
let us recall the following critical point theorems in
[
1

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space whose norm and inner product will be denoted respectively by
\|\cdot\|
and
(\cdot,\cdot)

\mathcal{M}:=\{w\in H:\|w\|_{H}=1\},

the tangent space of
\mathcal{M}
at a given point

T_{w}\mathcal{M}=\{w\in H:(g,w)=0\}.

Let

\langle J|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(w),g\rangle=\langle J^{\prime}(w),g\rangle\ \ \ \ g\in T|_{w}\mathcal{M}.

Let

We say that a functional

in
E_{r}
.

Next we show that
b_{k}>0
for each
k\geq 1
. Indeed, for
k\geq 1
,
we denote

\pi_{k-1}=\{l=(l_{1},l_{2},...l_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\ \ |\sum_{i=1}^{k}|l_{i}|=1\}.

Since
\pi_{k-1}
is homeomorphic to
S^{k-1}
by an odd homeomorphism, it follows that
\gamma(\pi_{k-1})=k
. The following result is due to Berestycki and Lions.

Lemma 6.3
.

For all
k\geq 1
, there exists a constant
R=R(k)>0
and an odd continuous mapping
\tau:\pi_{k-1}\to H_{0}^{1}(B_{R}(0))

such that

(i)
\pi_{(}l)
is a radial function for all
l\in\pi_{k-1}
and
0\nsubseteq\tau(\pi_{k-1})
;

(ii) there exist
p,C>0
such that
\rho\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R(0)})}\leq C
for
u\in\tau(\pi_{k-1})
;

(iii) for
u\in\tau(\pi_{k-1})
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{q}\,dx\geq 1
.

Lemma 6.4
.

When
N\geq 3
, there holds
b_{k}>0
for each
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\pi}_{k-1}=\tau(\pi_{k-1})
and define for each
u\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{R}(0))
an extension
\tilde{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of u such that
\tilde{u}=u

on
B_{R}(0)
and
\tilde{u}=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(0)
.
Denote
\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x)=\tilde{u}(\frac{x}{\sigma})
. For
u\in\hat{\pi}_{k-1}
, we define
\chi(u)=(1-y)(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}|^{p}(x))|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x)|^{p}
and
\chi(u)=0

if
y\not\in R\backslash(0,1)
,
where
\sigma=\sigma(u)>0
is determined by requiring
\chi(u)\in M
, that is,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla\chi(u))|^{2}+|\chi|^{2})\,dx=1.

We deduce that

\displaystyle 1=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}(|\nabla\chi(u))|^{2}+|\chi|^{2})\,dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\,dy\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(\nabla_{x}[(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}|^{p})(x)|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x)|^{p}],-(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}|^{p})(x)|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x)|^{p})|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\,dy\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1-y)^{2}|(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}|^{p})(x)|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x)|^{p}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\,dy\int_{B_{\sigma R}(0)}|(\nabla_{x}[(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}|^{p})(x)|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x)|^{p}],-(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x)|^{p})|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x)|^{p})|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\,dy\int_{B_{\sigma R}(0)}(1-y)^{2}|(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}|^{p})(x)|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x)|^{p}|^{2}\,dx

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}[(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}|^{p})(x)|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x)|^{p}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(z)|^{p}}{|\frac{x}{\sigma}-z|^{N-\alpha}}\,dz|u(\frac{x}{\sigma})|^{p}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(z)|^{p}(\alpha-N)|\frac{x}{\sigma}-z|^{\alpha-N-2}(\frac{x}{\sigma}-z)\frac{1}{\sigma}\,dz|u(\frac{x}{\sigma})|^{p}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(z)|^{p}}{|\frac{x}{\sigma}-z|^{N-\alpha}}\,dzp|u(\frac{x}{\sigma})|^{p-2}u(\frac{x}{\sigma})\frac{1}{\sigma}

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\sigma R}(0)}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(z)|^{p}(\alpha-N)|\frac{x}{\sigma}-z|^{\alpha-N-2}(\frac{x}{\sigma}-z)\frac{1}{\sigma}\,dz|u(\frac{x}{\sigma})|^{p}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(z,0)|^{p}}{|\frac{x}{\sigma}-z|^{N-\alpha}}\,dzp|u(\frac{x}{\sigma})|^{p-2}u(\frac{x}{\sigma})\frac{1}{\sigma}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(z)|^{p}(\alpha-N)|y-z|^{\alpha-N-2}(y-z)\frac{1}{\sigma}\,dz|u(y)|^{p}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(z,0)|^{p}}{|y-z|^{N-\alpha}}\,dzp|u(y)|^{p-2}u(y)\frac{1}{\sigma}|^{2}\sigma^{N}\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sigma^{N}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\nabla_{x}[(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p}(x))|u(x)|^{p}]|^{2}\,dx.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}(|\nabla\chi(u))|^{2}+|\chi|^{2})\,dxdy=\sigma^{N-2}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\nabla_{x}[(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p}(x,0))|u(x,0)|^{p}]|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{3}\sigma^{N}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p}(x,0))|u(x,0)|^{p}|^{2}\,dx.

Since the right hand side is increasing in
\sigma>0
, we find a unique
\sigma>0
so that
\chi\in M
. By (ii) of lemma
6.3
, we obtain

1\geq\sigma^{N-2}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}\,dx\geq\sigma^{N-2}\rho^{2}.

Hence, there exists
\bar{\sigma}>0
independent of
u\in\hat{\pi}_{k-1}
, such that
\sigma(u)\leq\bar{\sigma}
. By Poincare’s inequality, there holds

1\leq C(\sigma^{N-2}+\sigma^{N}),

which implies that
\sigma(u)
has a lower bound
\bar{\sigma}>0
independent of u.

Now we prove
b_{k}>0
for each
k\geq 1
. In fact, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{0\}}|\chi(u)|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{0\}}|(1-y)(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}|^{p})(x,0)|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x,0)|^{p}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{0\}}|(1-y)|^{p}|(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}|^{p})(x,0)|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x,0)|^{p}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(I_{\alpha}*|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}|^{p})(x,0)|\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x,0)|^{p}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\sigma R}}|(I_{\alpha}*|u(\frac{x}{\sigma},0)|^{p})(x,0)|u(\frac{x}{\sigma},0)|^{p}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{R}}|(I_{\alpha}*|u(y,0)|^{p})(x,0)|u(y,0)|^{p}|^{p}\,dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sigma^{N}\cdot 1\geq\underline{\sigma}^{N}.

Let
A_{k}=(1-y)\chi(\hat{\pi}_{k-1})
for
0\leq y\leq 1
. Since
\chi
is an odd continuous mapping,
A_{k}\in\Sigma(M)
.
Furthermore,
(1-y)\chi\cdot\tau:\pi_{k-1}\rightarrow A_{k}

is odd and continuous, so we have that
\gamma(A_{k})\geq\gamma(\pi_{k-1})=k
. Hence,
A_{k}\in\Gamma_{k}
. Set
\varepsilon_{k}=\underline{\sigma}^{N}
, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{0\}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u\,dx\geq\varepsilon_{k},\ \ \ \ u\in A_{k}.

Therefore, for each
k\geq 1
,

b_{k}=\sup_{A\in\Gamma_{k}}\inf_{u\in A_{k}}J(u)\geq\inf_{u\in A_{k}}J(u)\geq\varepsilon_{k}>0.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By lemma
6.2
,

J|_{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies the
(PS)^{+}
condition, and Lemma
6.4
yields
b_{k}>0
for
each
k>1
. Then the conclusion follows Proposition
6.1
and Proposition
6.2
.
\Box

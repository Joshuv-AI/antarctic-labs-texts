Hypergeometric Functions at Unit Argument: Simple Derivation of Old and New Identities
Asena ÇETİNKAYA a, Dmitrii KARP bc and Elena PRILEPKINA cd

Abstract
The main goal of this paper is to derive a number of identities for the generalized hypergeometric function evaluated at unity and for certain terminating multivariate hypergeometric functions from the symmetries and other properties of Meijer’s G G function. For instance, we recover two- and three-term Thomae relations for F 2 3 {}_{3}F_{2} , give two- and three-term transformations for F 3 4 {}_{4}F_{3} with one unit shift and F 4 5 {}_{5}F_{4} with two unit shifts in the parameters, establish multi-term identities for general F p − 1 p {}_{p}F_{p-1} and several transformations for terminating Kampé de Fériet and Srivastava F ( 3 ) F^{(3)} functions. We further present a presumably new formula for analytic continuation of F p − 1 p ​ ( 1 ) {}_{p}F_{p-1}(1) in parameters and reveal somewhat unexpected connections between the generalized hypergeometric functions and the generalized and ordinary Bernoulli polynomials. Finally, we exploit some recent duality relations for the generalized hypergeometric and q q -hypergeometric functions to derive multi-term relations for terminating series.

Nørlund’s coefficients

Nørlund’s coefficients

are multivariate polynomials defined by the power series generating function (

Another way to generate them is the inverse gamma series

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(z+\mathbf{x})}{\Gamma(z+\mathbf{y})}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n}^{p}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})}{\Gamma(z+\nu(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})+n)}

\mathbf{x}

\mathbf{y}

,

\displaystyle g_{n}^{2}(x;\mathbf{y})=\frac{(y_{2}-x)_{n}(y_{1}-x)_{n}}{n!}.

(2.2)

Recurrence (

\displaystyle g_{n}^{p}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})=\sum\limits_{0\leq{j_{1}}\leq{j_{2}}\leq\cdots\leq{j_{p-2}}\leq{n}}\prod\limits_{m=1}^{p-1}\frac{(\nu_{m}+j_{m-1})_{j_{m}-j_{m-1}}}{(j_{m}-j_{m-1})!}(y_{m+1}-x_{m})_{j_{m}-j_{m-1}},

Next, recall that the Bernoulli–Nørlund (or the generalized Bernoulli) polynomial

\displaystyle\frac{t^{\sigma}{\rm e}^{zt}}{(e^{t}-1)^{\sigma}}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{(\sigma)}_{k}(z)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

(2.20)

In particular,

is the classical Bernoulli polynomial. In
[
16
, Theorem 3]
we found the following
alternative representation for Nørlund’s coefficients:

\displaystyle g_{n}^{p}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})=\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n}\frac{(1+\alpha-\nu(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})-n)_{n-r}}{(n-r)!}l_{r}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y};\alpha)\mathcal{B}^{(n+\nu(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})-\alpha)}_{n-r}(1-\alpha),

is defined in (

can be chosen arbitrarily. The coefficients

are found from the recurrence relation

\displaystyle l_{r}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y};\alpha)=\frac{1}{r}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{r}q_{m}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y};\alpha)l_{r-m}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y};\alpha)

(2.22)

with

and

\displaystyle q_{m}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y};\alpha)=\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{m+1}\Bigg(\mathcal{B}_{m+1}(\alpha)+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\mathcal{B}_{m+1}(x_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mathcal{B}_{m+1}(y_{j})\Bigg)\!.

(2.23)

The recurrence (
2.22

(we use the abbreviated notation

):

\displaystyle l_{r}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y};\alpha)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+rk_{r}=r,\\
k_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{q_{1}^{k_{1}}(q_{2}/2)^{k_{2}}\cdots(q_{r}/r)^{k_{r}}}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{r}!}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{r}\frac{1}{n!}\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=r}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{q_{k_{i}}}{k_{i}}.

Another way to write this formula is to invoke the complete exponential Bell polynomials
Y_{r}
generated by
[
8
, formula (11.9)]

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}z_{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\Bigg)=1+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}Y_{r}(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})\frac{t^{r}}{r!}

and written explicitly as

\displaystyle Y_{r}(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+rk_{r}=r,\\
k_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{r!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{r}!}(z_{1}/1!)^{k_{1}}(z_{2}/2!)^{k_{2}}\cdots(z_{r}/r!)^{k_{r}}.

These polynomials can also be found using a determinantal expression, see
[
9

. Comparing the above explicit formulas for

and

we conclude that

\displaystyle l_{r}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y};\alpha)=\frac{1}{r!}Y_{r}(\hat{q}_{1},\hat{q}_{2},\dots,\hat{q}_{r}),

where
\hat{q}_{m}=(m-1)!q_{m}
with
q_{m}
from (
2.23
). Substituting this into (
2.21
) we obtain the second main result of this section.

For arbitrary
\alpha

\displaystyle g_{n}^{p}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})=\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n}\frac{(1+\alpha-\nu(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})-n)_{n-r}}{r!(n-r)!}Y_{r}(\hat{q}_{1},\hat{q}_{2},\dots,\hat{q}_{r})\mathcal{B}^{(n+\nu(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})-\alpha)}_{n-r}(1-\alpha),

Remark 3.3
.

Note that formula (

) and

we have the triple summation on the right-hand side (viewing the hypergeometric polynomial as a single sum), while (

[

represent

If condition

[

\displaystyle{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b}\end{matrix}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\frac{(\mathbf{a})_{k}}{(\mathbf{b})_{k}k!}+\frac{(\mathbf{a})_{M}z^{M}}{(\mathbf{b})_{M}M!}\,{}_{p+2}F_{p+1}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a}+M,1\\

Choosing

sufficiently large we can always ensure the condition
\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{a}_{[1,2]}+M)>0
in the second term, while the first term is a rational function. Note, that the parametric excess in the second term on the right-hand side

\displaystyle\nu((\mathbf{a}+M,1);(\mathbf{b}+M,M+1))=\nu(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b}),

which is the parametric excess of the function on the left hand side irrespective of

. Hence, for
\operatorname{Re}(\nu(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b}))<0
the above decomposition alone does not help to construct the analytic continuation. However, when combined with Theorem

or with formula (

-\nu(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
-b_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

where the symbol

after an expression means that the preceding expression is repeated with

Before we move forward we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle{}_{p}F_{q}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-n,-m,\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b},1-\alpha-n\end{matrix}\right)=\frac{(\alpha-m)_{n}}{(\alpha)_{n}}\frac{(1-\lambda_{1})_{n}\cdots(1-\lambda_{m})_{n}}{(-\lambda_{1})_{n}\cdots(-\lambda_{m})_{n}},

(3.9)

where
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}
are the zeros of the polynomial

\displaystyle P_{m}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\frac{(-m)_{k}(\mathbf{a})_{k}(-x)_{k}(x+\alpha-m)_{m-k}}{(-1)^{k}(\mathbf{b})_{k}k!}.

(3.10)

In particular, for
m=1
,
\lambda_{1}=(1-\alpha)(\mathbf{b})_{1}/[(\mathbf{b})_{1}-(\mathbf{a})_{1}]
.

Proof.

In order to establish (
3.9
) first note that for integer
0\leq{k}\leq{m}
:

\displaystyle\frac{(\alpha)_{n}}{(1-\alpha-n)_{k}}=(-1)^{k}(\alpha)_{n-m}(\alpha+n-m)_{m-k}.

This formula is also true if
m>n
with the standard convention
(a)_{r}=(-1)^{r}/(1-a)_{-r}
for integer
r<0
. Applying this relation we get

\displaystyle(\alpha)_{n}\cdot{}_{p}F_{q}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-n,-m,\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b},1-\alpha-n\end{matrix}\right)=(\alpha)_{n-m}P_{m}(n),

where
P_{m}(x)
is defined by (
3.10
). Factoring
P_{m}(x)
we get:

\displaystyle P_{m}(n)=A(n-\lambda_{1})(n-\lambda_{2})\cdots(n-\lambda_{m})=A(-\lambda_{1})\cdots(-\lambda_{m})\frac{(1-\lambda_{1})_{n}\cdots(1-\lambda_{m})_{n}}{(-\lambda_{1})_{n}\cdots(-\lambda_{m})_{n}},

where
A
denotes the coefficient at
x^{m}
in
P_{m}(x)
. It remains to note that

\displaystyle A(-\lambda_{1})(-\lambda_{2})\cdots(-\lambda_{m})=P_{m}(0)=(\alpha-m)_{m}

and

\displaystyle(\alpha-m)_{m}(\alpha)_{n-m}=(\alpha-m)_{n}.\!\!

∎

We now take
p=3
and
a_{3}=b_{3}+m
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, in (
3.8
). The term corresponding to

vanishes and we are left with two infinite series with terms involving

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-n,1-b_{1}+\mathbf{a}_{[1,2]}\\
1-b_{1}+\mathbf{b}_{[1]}\end{matrix}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-n,1-b_{2}+\mathbf{a}_{[1,2]}\\
1-b_{2}+\mathbf{b}_{[2]}\end{matrix}\right)\!.

If we apply Sheppard’s transformation (

) to each of these two functions
we obtain (keeping in mind that

,

):

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\big(\mathbf{a}_{[1,2]}\big)\sin(\pi\nu)}{\Gamma(\mathbf{b})\pi}\,{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b}\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{\Gamma\big(b_{1}-\mathbf{b}_{[1]}\big)}{\Gamma(b_{1}-\mathbf{a})}\!\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\!\frac{(1-b_{1}+a_{1})_{n}(1-b_{1}+a_{2})_{n}(b_{2}-a_{4})_{n}}{\Gamma(1-b_{1}+a_{1}+a_{2}+n)(1-b_{1}+b_{2})_{n}n!}

\displaystyle\qquad\phantom{=}{}\times{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-n,-m,1-b_{1}+a_{4}\\
1-b_{1}+b_{3},1-b_{2}+a_{4}-n\end{matrix}\right)+\mathrm{idem}(b_{1};b_{2})=\frac{\Gamma\big(b_{1}-\mathbf{b}_{[1]}\big)}{\Gamma(b_{1}-\mathbf{a})}\!

\displaystyle\qquad\phantom{=}{}\times\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\!\frac{(1\!-b_{1}\!+a_{1})_{n}(1\!-b_{1}\!+a_{2})_{n}(b_{2}\!-a_{4}\!-m)_{n}(1\!-\lambda_{1})_{n}\cdots(1\!-\lambda_{m})_{n}}{\Gamma(1-b_{1}+a_{1}+a_{2}+n)(1-b_{1}+b_{2})_{n}(-\lambda_{1})_{n}\cdots(-\lambda_{m})_{n}n!}+\mathrm{idem}(b_{1};b_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{\Gamma\big(b_{1}-\mathbf{b}_{[1]}\big)}{\Gamma(b_{1}\!-\mathbf{a})\Gamma(1-b_{1}\!+a_{1}\!+a_{2})}{}_{m+3}F_{m+2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1\!-b_{1}\!+a_{1},1\!-b_{1}\!+a_{2},b_{2}\!-a_{4}\!-m,1\!-\boldsymbol{\lambda}\\
1-b_{1}+a_{1}+a_{2},1-b_{1}+b_{2},-\boldsymbol{\lambda}\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\phantom{=}{}+\mathrm{idem}(b_{1};b_{2}),

where the vector

\displaystyle P_{m}(a_{4};b_{1},b_{2},b_{3};x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\frac{(-m)_{k}(-x)_{k}(x+b_{2}-a_{4}-m)_{m-k}(1-b_{1}+a_{4})_{k}}{(-1)^{k}(1-b_{1}+b_{3})_{k}k!}

{}_{4}F_{3}(1)
with one unit shift in the parameters:

\displaystyle\frac{b_{3}\Gamma(a_{4})\sin(\pi\nu)}{\Gamma(b_{1})\Gamma(b_{2})\pi}\,{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b}\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{\Gamma\big(b_{1}-\mathbf{b}_{[1]}\big)}{\Gamma(b_{1}-\mathbf{a})\Gamma(1-b_{1}+a_{1}+a_{2})}\,{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1-b_{1}+a_{1},1-b_{1}+a_{2},b_{2}-a_{4}-1,1-\lambda_{1}\\
1-b_{1}+a_{1}+a_{2},1-b_{1}+b_{2},-\lambda_{1}\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\phantom{=}{}+\frac{\Gamma\big(b_{2}-\mathbf{b}_{[2]}\big)}{\Gamma(b_{2}-\mathbf{a})\Gamma(1-b_{2}+a_{1}+a_{2})}\,{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1-b_{2}+a_{1},1-b_{2}+a_{2},b_{1}-a_{4}-1,1-\lambda_{2}\\
1-b_{2}+a_{1}+a_{2},1-b_{2}+b_{1},-\lambda_{2}\end{matrix}\right)\!,

where
a_{3}=b_{3}+1
,
\nu=b_{1}+b_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}-a_{4}-1
and

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\frac{(1-b_{2}+a_{4})(1-b_{1}+b_{3})}{b_{3}-a_{4}},\qquad\lambda_{2}=\frac{(1-b_{1}+a_{4})(1-b_{2}+b_{3})}{b_{3}-a_{4}}.

We now turn our attention to the consequences of (
3.4
).
For
p=2
substitution
g_{n}(a;\mathbf{b})=(b_{1}-a)_{n}(b_{2}-a)_{n}/n!
(see (
2.2
)) gives yet another proof of the two-term Thomae relation
[
2
, Corollary 3.3.6]
.
For
p=3
substituting (
2.12
) into (
3.4
) yields:

\displaystyle{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b}\end{matrix}\right)=\frac{\Gamma(\mathbf{b})\Gamma(\nu)}{\Gamma(\mathbf{a}_{[1,2]}+\nu)\Gamma(a_{1})\Gamma(a_{2})}

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}_{4}F_3\!\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b}\end{matrix}\right)=}{}\times\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\nu)_{n}(\nu-\mathbf{b}_{[1]}+a_{3}+a_{4})_{n}}{(\mathbf{a}_{[1,2]}+\nu)_{n}n!}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-n,b_{1}-a_{1},b_{1}-a_{2}\\
\nu-\mathbf{b}_{[1]}+a_{3}+a_{4}\end{matrix}\right)\!.

(3.11)

This formula differs from
[
6
, formulas (2.14) and (2.10)]
, but can be reduced to it by an application of Whipple’s transformation for terminating
{}_{3}F_{2}(1)

[
2
, p. 142, top]
. If we put
a_{1}=b_{1}+1
in (
3.11
) we arrive at a two-term transformation for
{}_{4}F_{3}
with one unit shift studied by two of us recently in
[
21
, identity (7)]
. Further consequences of (
3.4
) are explored in the following section.

4
Transformations of
\boldsymbol{{}_{5}F_{4}}
with two unit shifts

In our recent paper
[
21
]
we have studied a group of transformations of
{}_{4}F_{3}(1)
with one unit shift in the parameters. We have shown that this group is generated by two-term Thomae transformations and contiguous relations for
{}_{3}F_{2}(1)
. In this section we will demonstrate that a similar group can be generated for
{}_{5}F_{4}
with two unit shifts. Let us note that the study of the summation and transformation formulas for the generalized hypergeometric function with integral parameter differences was initially motivated by problems from mathematical physics, see, for instance,
[
26
,
28
,
36
]
. The most general linear transformations for this class of hypergeometric series at arbitrary argument were discovered by Miller and Paris
[
27
]
(see an alternative derivation in
[
19
]
), while quadratic and cubic transformations were found recently by Maier
[
25
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The following two-term transformation holds
:

\displaystyle{}_{5}F_{4}\left.\!\left(\begin{matrix}a,b,c,f+1,h+1\\
d,e,f,h\end{matrix}\right.\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{\Gamma(d)\Gamma(e)\Gamma(s)}{fh\Gamma(c)\Gamma(s+a)\Gamma(s+b)}\,{}_{5}F_{4}\left.\!\left(\begin{matrix}s,e-c-2,d-c-2,1-\gamma_{1},1-\gamma_{2}\\
s+a,s+b,-\gamma_{1},-\gamma_{2}\end{matrix}\right.\right)\!,

(4.1)

where
s=d+e-a-b-c-2
and
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
are the roots of the second degree polynomial

\displaystyle P_{2}(x)={}_{4}F_{3}\left.\!\left(\begin{matrix}-2,-x,h-c-1,\eta+1\\
e-c-2,d-c-2,\eta\end{matrix}\right.\right)

with
\eta=-2(h-c-1)/(h-f-1)
.

Proof.

Formula (
3.4
) applied to the left hand side of (
4.1
) has the form

\displaystyle{}_{5}F_{4}\!\left(\begin{matrix}a,b,f+1,h+1,c\\
f,h,d,e\end{matrix}\right)=\frac{\Gamma(d)\Gamma(e)}{\Gamma(c)fh}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+n)g_{n}^{4}(\{f+1,h+1,c\};\{f,h,d,e\})}{\Gamma(s+a+n)\Gamma(s+b+n)},

(4.2)

where
s=d+e-a-b-c-2
. According to (
2.13
) we have

\displaystyle g_{n}^{4}(\{f+1,h+1,c\};\{f,h,d,e\})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{(e-c-2)_{n}(d-c-2)_{n}}{n!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}(-2)_{k}(h-c-1)_{k}}{k!(e-c-2)_{k}(d-c-2)_{k}}\bigg(1+\frac{k(h-f-1)}{(-2)(h-c-1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{(e-c-2)_{n}(d-c-2)_{n}}{n!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}(-2)_{k}(h-c-1)_{k}}{k!(e-c-2)_{k}(d-c-2)_{k}}\frac{(\eta+1)_{k}}{(\eta)_{k}},

where
\eta=-2(h-c-1)/(h-f-1)
. Hence,

\displaystyle g_{n}^{4}(\{f\!+1,h\!+1,c\};\{f,h,d,e\})=\frac{(e\!-c\!-2)_{n}(d\!-c\!-2)_{n}}{n!}\,{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-n,-2,h-c-1,\eta+1\\
e-c-2,d-c-2,\eta\end{matrix}\right)\!.

Noting that the quadratic polynomial

\displaystyle P_{2}(x)={}_{4}F_{3}\left.\!\left(\begin{matrix}-2,-x,h-c-1,\eta+1\\
e-c-2,d-c-2,\eta\end{matrix}\right.\right)

satisfies
P_{2}(0)=1
, we conclude that
P_{2}(x)=(x-\gamma_{1})(x-\gamma_{2})/(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})
, where
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
are the roots of
P_{2}(x)
. Hence,
P_{2}(n)=(1-\gamma_{1})_{n}(1-\gamma_{2})_{n}/((-\gamma_{1})_{n}(-\gamma_{2})_{n})
and

\displaystyle g_{n}^{4}(\{f+1,h+1,c\};\{f,h,d,e\})=\frac{(e-c-2)_{n}(d-c-2)_{n}(1-\gamma_{1})_{n}(1-\gamma_{2})_{n}}{(-\gamma_{1})_{n}(-\gamma_{2})_{n}n!}.

Substituting the above formula into (
4.2
), we arrive at (
4.1
).
∎

Another transformation of a similar flavour as (
4.1
) has been recently found by us in
[
18
, formula (59)]
, namely,

\displaystyle{}_{5}F_{4}\left.\!\left(\begin{matrix}a,b,c,f+1,h+1\\
d,e,f,h\end{matrix}\right.\right)=\frac{((e-c-1)h+(d-a-b-1)(h-c))\Gamma(e)\Gamma(s)}{h\Gamma(s+c+1)\Gamma(e-c)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}_{5}F_{4}\left.\! \left( \begin{matrix}a,b,c,f+1,h+1\\
d,e,f,h\end{matrix}\right.\right)=}{}\times{}_{5}F_{4}\left.\!\left(\begin{matrix}d-a-1,d-b-1,c,\xi+1,\zeta+1\\
d,e+d-a-b-1,\xi,\zeta\end{matrix}\right.\right)\!,

(4.3)

where
s=d+e-a-b-c-2
,
d-a-1\neq 0
,
d-b-1\neq 0
and

\displaystyle\xi=h+\frac{(d-a-b-1)(h-c)}{e-c-1},\qquad\zeta=\frac{(d-a-1)(d-b-1)f}{(d-a-b-1)f+ab}.

Transformations (
4.1
) and (
4.3
) can be iterated and composed with each other. Together with the obvious invariance with respect to permutations of the upper and the lower parameters they generate a group, which can be shown to be isomorphic to the direct product of two-term Thomae transformations for
{}_{3}F_{2}
with contiguous relations for
{}_{3}F_{2}
. This claim can be verified using the approach from
[
21
]
. We further believe that similar transformations hold for higher order
{}_{p+1}F_{p}
with appropriate number of unit shifts in parameters. We envision further investigation of this topic in a future publication.

5
Multi-term transformations of the non-terminating series

Nørlund proved the following identity
[
29
, relation (5.8)]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\big(\mathbf{a}_{[1]}\big)}{\Gamma(1-a_{1})\Gamma(\mathbf{b})}\,{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b}\end{matrix}\right)=\sum\limits_{k=2}^{p+1}\frac{\Gamma(a_{k})\Gamma\big(\mathbf{a}_{[1,k]}-a_{k}\big)}{\Gamma(1-a_{1}+a_{k})\Gamma(\mathbf{b}-a_{k})}\,{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\begin{matrix}a_{k},1-\mathbf{b}+a_{k}\\
1-\mathbf{a}_{[k]}+a_{k}\end{matrix}\right)\!,

(5.1)

where all series involved converge if
\operatorname{Re}(\nu(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b}))>0
. This identity was later rediscovered by Wimp in
[
42
, Lemma 2]
. We note that it also follows immediately on substituting (
1.3
) into (
1.5
) and integrating term-wise using the beta integral:

\displaystyle{}_{p+1}F_{p}\left.\!\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b}\end{matrix}\right.\right)=\frac{\Gamma(\mathbf{b})}{\Gamma\big(\mathbf{a}_{[1]}\big)}\sum\limits_{k=2}^{p+1}\frac{\Gamma\big(a_{[1,k]}-a_{k}\big)}{\Gamma(\mathbf{b}-a_{k})}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\mathbf{b}+a_{k})_{n}}{\big(1-\mathbf{a}_{[1,k]}+a_{k}\big)_{n}n!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{n+a_{k}-1}}{(1-t)^{a_{1}}}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}_{p+1}F_p\left. \!\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b}\end{matrix}\right.\right)}{}=\frac{\Gamma(\mathbf{b})\Gamma(1-a_{1})}{\Gamma\big(\mathbf{a}_{[1]}\big)}\sum\limits_{k=2}^{p+1}\frac{\Gamma(a_{k})\Gamma\big(\mathbf{a}_{[1,k]}-a_{k}\big)}{\Gamma(1-a_{1}+a_{k})\Gamma(\mathbf{b}-a_{k})}\,{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\begin{matrix}a_{k},1-\mathbf{b}+a_{k}\\
1-\mathbf{a}_{[k]}+a_{k}\end{matrix}\right)\!.

On the other hand, formulas (
1.3
) and (
1.6
) imply immediately that for
\sum_{j=1}^{p+1}(c_{j}-b_{j})>1
we have

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p+1}\frac{\Gamma\big(\mathbf{b}_{[k]}-b_{k}\big)}{\Gamma(\mathbf{c}-b_{k})}\,{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1-\mathbf{c}+b_{k}\\
1-\mathbf{b}_{[k]}+b_{k}\end{matrix}\right)=0.

(5.2)

We note that particular cases of identities (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) appeared many times in the literature proved by various methods. For instance, the three-term Thomae relation for
{}_{3}F_{2}

[
3
, formula 3.2(2)]
is a particular case of (
5.1
), while its variation
[
11
, formula (10)]
and
{}_{4}F_{3}
generalization
[
11
, formula (19)]
reduce to (
5.2
) after appropriate change of notation.

We note also that (
5.2
) is not a straightforward rewriting of (
5.1
). Indeed, setting
b_{1}=0
brings (
5.2
) to the form:

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\big(\mathbf{b}_{[1]}\big)}{\Gamma(\mathbf{c})}\,{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1-\mathbf{c}\\
1-\mathbf{b}_{[1]}\end{matrix}\right)=\sum\limits_{k=2}^{p+1}\frac{\Gamma(1-b_{k})\Gamma\big(\mathbf{b}_{[1,k]}-b_{k}\big)}{b_{k}\Gamma(\mathbf{c}-b_{k})}\,{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1-\mathbf{c}+b_{k}\\
1+b_{k},1-\mathbf{b}_{[1,k]}+b_{k}\end{matrix}\right)\!.

Changing notation according to
1-\mathbf{c}\to\mathbf{a},

1-\mathbf{b}_{[1]}\to\mathbf{b}
we get

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(1-\mathbf{b})}{\Gamma(1-\mathbf{a})}{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b}\end{matrix}\right)=-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p}\frac{\Gamma(b_{k}-1)\Gamma\big(b_{k}-\mathbf{b}_{[k]}\big)}{\Gamma(b_{k}-\mathbf{a})}\,{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a}+1-b_{k}\\
2-b_{k},1+\mathbf{b}_{[k]}-b_{k}\end{matrix}\right)\!,

(5.3)

where all series involved converge if
\operatorname{Re}(\nu(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b}))>0
. This is manifestly different from (
5.1
). It remains unclear for us, however, if (
5.1
) and (
5.3
) can be obtained from each other by the appropriate compositions.

Our next theorem gives another multi-term identity for
{}_{p+1}F_{p}
containing
p
or more terms and not immediately found in the literature (other than for
p=2
, see remark below).

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
0\leq{n}\leq{p}
,
0\leq{m}\leq{p}
are integers that satisfy
m+n\geq{p}
and
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
,
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{p-n}
,
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{p-m}
are complex vectors that satisfy

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{p-n}c_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{p-m}d_{j}\Biggr)>0.

Then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\frac{A_{k}}{b_{k}}\,{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\left.\begin{matrix}1-\mathbf{a}+b_{k},1-\mathbf{c}+b_{k},b_{k}\\
1-\mathbf{b}_{[k]}+b_{k},1-\mathbf{d}+b_{k},b_{k}+1\end{matrix}\right|(-1)^{p-m-n}\!\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{B_{k}}{(1-a_{k})}\,{}_{p+1}F_{p}\!\left(\left.\begin{matrix}1+\mathbf{b}-a_{k},1+\mathbf{d}-a_{k},1-a_{k}\\
1+\mathbf{a}_{[k]}-a_{k},1+\mathbf{c}-a_{k},2-a_{k}\end{matrix}\right|(-1)^{p-m-n}\!\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{\Gamma(1-\mathbf{a})\Gamma(\mathbf{b})}{\Gamma(\mathbf{c})\Gamma(1-\mathbf{d})},

(5.4a)

where

\displaystyle A_{k}=\frac{\Gamma\big(\mathbf{b}_{[k]}-b_{k}\big)\Gamma(1-\mathbf{a}+b_{k})}{\Gamma(\mathbf{c}-b_{k})\Gamma(1-\mathbf{d}+b_{k})},\qquad B_{k}=\frac{\Gamma(a_{k}-\mathbf{a}_{[k]})\Gamma(1+\mathbf{b}-a_{k})}{\Gamma(a_{k}-\mathbf{d})\Gamma(1+\mathbf{c}-a_{k})}.

(5.4b)

Proof.

Combining
[
33
, formula (8.2.2.3)]
with
[
33
, fornula (8.2.2.4)]
for
x>0
we get:

\displaystyle G^{m,n}_{p,p}\!\left(x\left|\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}\\
\mathbf{b},\mathbf{d}\end{matrix}\right.\right)=H(1-x)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}x^{b_{k}}\,{}_{p}F_{p-1}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1-\mathbf{a}+b_{k},1-\mathbf{c}+b_{k}\\
1-\mathbf{b}_{[k]}+b_{k},1-\mathbf{d}+b_{k}\end{matrix}\!\vline\,(-1)^{p-m-n}x\!\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{G^{m,n}_{p,p}\!\left( x\left|\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}\\
\mathbf{b},\mathbf{d}\end{matrix}\right. \right)
}{}+H(x\!-1)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}B_{k}x^{a_{k}-1}\,{}_{p}F_{p-1}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1+\mathbf{b}-a_{k},1+\mathbf{d}-a_{k}\\
1+\mathbf{a}_{[k]}-a_{k},1+\mathbf{c}-a_{k}\end{matrix}\vline\,\frac{(-1)^{p-m-n}}{x}\!\right)\!,\!\!\!

(5.5)

where
A_{k}
,
B_{k}
are defined in (
5.4b
) and
H(t)=\partial_{t}\max\{t,0\}
is the Heaviside function.
According to
[
22
, Theorem 2.2]
the Mellin transform

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}G^{m,n}_{p,p}\left(x\left|\begin{matrix}\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}\\
\mathbf{b},\mathbf{d}\end{matrix}\right.\right){\rm d}x=\frac{\Gamma(1-\mathbf{a}-s)\Gamma(\mathbf{b}+s)}{\Gamma(\mathbf{c}+s)\Gamma(1-\mathbf{d}-s)}

exists for
-\min(\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{b}))<\operatorname{Re}(s)<\min(1-\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{a}))
under conditions of the theorem. Assuming that
s=0
belongs to this range we can take the Mellin transform with
s=0
on both sides of (
5.5
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(1-\mathbf{a})\Gamma(\mathbf{b})}{\Gamma(\mathbf{c})\Gamma(1-\mathbf{d})}={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\mathbf{a}+b_{k})_{j}(1-\mathbf{c}+b_{k})_{j}[(-1)^{p-m-n}]^{j}}{(1-\mathbf{b}_{[k]}+b_{k})_{j}(1-\mathbf{d}+b_{k})_{j}j!}\int_{0}^{1}x^{b_{k}+j-1}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}B_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+\mathbf{b}-a_{k})_{j}(1+\mathbf{d}-a_{k})_{j}[(-1)^{p-m-n}]^{j}}{(1+\mathbf{a}_{[k]}-a_{k})_{j}(1+\mathbf{c}-a_{k})_{j}j!}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{a_{k}-j-2}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\frac{A_{k}}{b_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\mathbf{a}+b_{k})_{j}(1-\mathbf{c}+b_{k})_{j}[(-1)^{p-m-n}]^{j}(b_{k})_{j}}{(1-\mathbf{b}_{[k]}+b_{k})_{j}(1-\mathbf{d}+b_{k})_{j}j!(b_{k}+1)_{j}}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{B_{k}}{1-a_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+\mathbf{b}-a_{k})_{j}(1+\mathbf{d}-a_{k})_{j}[(-1)^{p-m-n}]^{j}(1-a_{k})_{j}}{(1+\mathbf{a}_{[k]}-a_{k})_{j}(1+\mathbf{c}-a_{k})_{j}(2-a_{k})_{j}j!},

which proves (
5.4a
) under the restriction
-\min(\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{b}))<0<\min(1-\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{a}))
. This restriction can now be removed by analytic continuation.
∎

6
Multi-term transformations of the terminating series

Introduce the following notation:
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
and
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
,

\displaystyle M=m_{1}+\cdots+{m_{r}},\qquad N=n_{1}+\cdots+{n_{r}},

\displaystyle m_{\min}=\min\limits_{1\leq{i}\leq{r}}(m_{i}),\qquad n_{\max}=\max\limits_{1\leq{i}\leq{r}}(n_{i}),\qquad p=\max\{-1,M-N-r+1\}.

In
[
14
, Theorem 1]
A. Kuznetsov and the second author established the following identity for arbitrary
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
such that the components of
\mathbf{a}
are distinct modulo integers, and
|z|<1
:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\frac{(1-\mathbf{b}+a_{i})_{\mathbf{m}-n_{i}}z^{-n_{i}}}{\big(a_{i}-\mathbf{a}_{[i]}\big)_{\mathbf{n}_{[i]}-n_{i}+1}}\,{}_{r}F_{r-1}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{b}-a_{i}\\
1+\mathbf{a}_{[i]}-a_{i}\end{matrix}\Big|z\right){}_{r}F_{r-1}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1-\mathbf{b}+a_{i}+\mathbf{m}-n_{i}\\
1-\mathbf{a}_{[i]}+a_{i}+\mathbf{n}_{[i]}-n_{i}\end{matrix}\Big|z\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(1-z)^{-p-1}\sum\limits_{j=-n_{\max}}^{p-m_{\min}}\beta_{j}z^{j}.

(6.1)

The authors did not give any explicit expression for the numbers
\beta_{j}
. Our aim here is to employ the above formula for deriving some identities for the generalized hypergeometric function evaluated at
1
. To this end we will need the explicit expressions for
\beta_{j}
, which we present in the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Formula (
6.1
) holds true for

\displaystyle\beta_{k}=\sum\limits_{j=\max(-n_{\max},k-p-1)}^{k}\binom{p+1}{k-j}(-1)^{k-j}

(6.2)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\beta_{k}=}{}\times\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\frac{(1-\mathbf{b}+a_{i})_{\mathbf{m}+j}}{\big(a_{i}-\mathbf{a}_{[i]}\big)_{\mathbf{n}_{[i]}+j+1}(j+n_{i})!}\,{}_{2r}F_{2r-1}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-j\!-n_{i},\mathbf{b}-a_{i},1\mathbf{a}_{[i]}-a_{i}-\mathbf{n}_{[i]}-j-1\\
\mathbf{b}-a_{i}-\mathbf{m}-j,1+\mathbf{a}_{[i]}-a_{i}\end{matrix}\right)\!,

where each term with
j+n_{i}<0
is assumed to equal zero.

Proof.

Writing
S(z)
for the left hand side of (
6.1
) we get by collecting terms

\displaystyle S(z)=\sum\limits_{k=-n_{\max}}^{\infty}z^{k}\underbrace{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k+n_{i}}\gamma^{k+n_{i}}_{i,j}}_{=\alpha_{k}}=\sum\limits_{k=-n_{\max}}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}z^{k},

where, according to the formula on page 4 of
[
14
]
, we have the first equality below:

\displaystyle\gamma^{k+n_{i}}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{j}(1-\mathbf{b}+a_{i}-j)_{\mathbf{m}+k}}{\big(a_{i}-\mathbf{a}_{[i]}-j\big)_{\mathbf{n}_{[i]}+k+1}j!(k+n_{i}-j)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\mathbf{b}+a_{i})_{\mathbf{m}+k}(\mathbf{b}-a_{i})_{j}\big(1+\mathbf{a}_{[i]}-a_{i}-\mathbf{n}_{[i]}-k-1\big)_{j}(-k-n_{i})_{j}}{(\mathbf{b}-a_{i}-\mathbf{m}-k)_{j}\big(a_{i}-\mathbf{a}_{[i]}\big)_{\mathbf{n}_{[i]}+k+1}\big(1+\mathbf{a}_{[i]}-a_{i}\big)_{j}(k+n_{i})!j!}.

The second equality above is obtained by an application of the easily verifiable identities

\displaystyle(z-j)_{n}=\frac{(z)_{n}(1-z)_{j}}{(1-z-n)_{j}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(m-j)!=(-1)^{j}\frac{m!}{(-m)_{j}}.

Hence,

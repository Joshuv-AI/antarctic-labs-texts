Laser-induced Demagnetization in van der Waals X ​ Y XY - and Ising-like Antiferromagnets NiPS 3 and FePS 3 Preprint: APS/123-QED
D. V. Kuntu, E. A. Arkhipova, L. A. Shelukhin, F. Mertens, M. A. Prosnikov, I. A. Eliseyev, A. N. Smirnov, V. Yu. Davydov, S. Mañas-Valero, E. Coronado, M. Cinchetti, A. M. Kalashnikova

Abstract
The critical behaviour of laser-induced changes in magnetic ordering is studied experimentally in two-dimensional zigzag antiferromagnets XY -like NiPS 3 \text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} and Ising-like FePS 3 \text{FePS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . To examine laser-induced dynamics in flakes of these compounds, we employ time-resolved exchange linear dichroism effect sensitive to zigzag magnetic ordering and independent of the orientation of the antiferromagnetic vector. In both compounds laser excitation in the vicinity of the absorption edge induces partial quenching of the antiferromagnetic ordering manifested by exchange linear dichroism reduction. The amplitude of the effect varies with temperature as the derivative of the antiferromagnetic vector and exhibits a critical behaviour with the exponents corresponding to XY - and Ising-models for NiPS 3 \text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} and FePS 3 \text{FePS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} , respectively. Critical slowing down of the demagnetization in the vicinity of Néel temperature is found, however, only in FePS 3 \text{FePS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . In contrast, the increase of the demagnetization time near the ordering temperature in NiPS 3 \text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} is minor. We show that the difference in the demagnetization times correlates well with the spin specific heat in both compounds. Beyond the range of slowing down, the demagnetization times in NiPS 3 \text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} and FePS 3 \text{FePS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} are comparable, about 5 – 10 ps, and are longer than those reported earlier for CoPS 3 \text{CoPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} and considerably shorter than for MnPS 3 \text{MnPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . This points to the importance of the unquenched angular momentum of transition-metal ions in laser-induced demagnetization process.



I
Introduction

The phenomenon of ultrafast demagnetization, namely quenching of the magnetic ordering in magnets following excitation with femtosecond laser pulses, has become a challenging topic for experimental and theoretical studies.
It yielded discoveries of single- and multi-shot all-optical magnetic reversal
[
Stanciu-PRL2007
;
Ostler2012
;
Medapalli2017
]
, generation of ultrashort spin currents
[
Huisman2016
]
and emission of broadband THz pulses
[
Seifert2016
]
.
In metals, demagnetization is governed by electron-phonon coupling
[
Koopmans2010
]
, leading to characteristic timescales of the process of less than 100 femtoseconds
[
Beaurepaire1996
;
Stamm2007
;
Carpene2008
]
.
In insulators, this timescale is defined mainly by spin-lattice coupling, and the demagnetization process develops in a subnanosecond range
[
Kimel-PRL2002
;
Maehrlein-SciAdv2018
]
.
The ultrafast demagnetization process is also intrinsically sensitive to critical phenomena occurring in the vicinity of magnetic ordering temperature
[
Lopez-PRB2013
;
Kimling-PRB2014
]
.
Therefore, the type and dimensionality of the magnetic ordering can play an important role in this process, along with the electronic band structure and coupling of spins to other energy and angular momenta reservoirs.

Layered magnetic materials, such as van der Waals materials
[
Susner2017
;
Wang2018
]
, organic-inorganic hybrids
[
Bellitto2015
]
, MAX phases
[
Ingason2016
]
, etc., offer an appealing possibility to investigate ultrafast magnetic dynamics in connection to details of magnetic ordering.
Indeed, while having similar crystallographic structure within a family, these materials demonstrate a broad variety of spin arrangements.
Another common feature of such compounds is a weak interlayer exchange coupling, making them quasi two-dimensional (2D) structures.
As a result, these materials are used for examining the Mermin-Wagner theorem and ways to overcome restrictions on magnetic ordering in the 2D limit
[
jenkins_breaking_2022
]
.
In a few- and single-layer limit readily achievable for, e.g., van der Waals materials
[
kuo2016exfoliation
;
Xu_Liang_Shi_Chen_2013
;
Mas-Balleste2011
;
Butler2013
]
, their magnetic ordering can experience drastic changes such as a transition between different spin alignments
[
Huang2017
]
or quenching of magnetic ordering
[
Kim2019
]
, although the latter remains a matter of further discussion
[
Kim_Park_2021
]
.

Laser-pulse driven spin dynamics in layered magnets has recently become a subject of active studies.
They cover revealing manifestations of a quasi-2D nature of magnetic ordering
[
Caretta-PRB2015
]
, as well as examining coupling between lattice and spins and its effect on laser-driven processes in both subsystems
[
Mertens2022
;
Afanasiev2021
;
Zhou2022
;
Khusyainov2023
;
Lichtenberg-2D2023
]
.
Examination of a critical behaviour of the laser-induced spin dynamics takes a central place in many of these studies
[
Zhang2021
;
Khusyainov2023
;
Lichtenberg-2D2023
]
, with van der Waals transition metal thiophosphates MPS
3
(M=Mn,Fe,Co,Ni) playing an essential role as the materials offering a variety of antiferromagnetic (AFM) arrangements
[
Susner2017
]
.

In this Article, we report on a comparative study of laser-induced demagnetization in the van der Waals transition metal thiophosphates
\text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{FePS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
.
Within the MPS
3
family, nickel and iron phosphorus trisulfides possess a number of similarities with each other and are very distinct from the other two compounds.
Namely,
\text{FePS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
both are zigzag antiferromagnets, and their magnetism is of predominantly spin origin.
Yet,
\text{FePS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
demonstrates the 2D Ising-like critical properties, while those of
\text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
are described by the 2D
XY
-model.
By studying the time-resolved dynamics of the exchange linear dichroism (exLD),
we reveal that the demagnetization degree in both thiophosphates increases in the vicinity of the Néel temperature and is governed by the critical behaviour of the order parameter depending on the type of 2D magnetic ordering.
However, only 2D Ising-like antiferromagnet
\text{FePS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
exhibits a pronounced slowing-down of the demagnetization rate as Néel temperature is approached, while in 2D
XY
-like
\text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, such a slowing down in the vicinity of
T_{\mathrm{N}}
is minor.
Using a two-temperature model for the lattice and the spin responses to the optical excitation, we identify that the difference in demagnetization times stems from distinct spin heat capacity behaviours.

III.2
Numerical simulations of laser-induced dynamics of order parameter

ExLD scales as a squared order parameter
\mathbf{L}
with temperature
[
Zhang2021-Giant_OLD
;
Demokritov-JETP1987
]
.
According to the Landau phase transition theory
[
LANDAU1980446
]
, the order parameter near
T_{\mathrm{N}}
varies with temperature following the power law:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{split}L\propto\left(T_{\mathrm{N}}-T\right)^{~\beta},T<T_{\mathrm{N}}\\
L=0,T\geqslant T_{\mathrm{N}}\end{split}\right.

(2)

where
\beta
is the critical exponent. It characterizes the magnetic phase transition and is specific to a model describing the system.
Consequently, an exact expression for the laser-induced change of exLD
\Delta\phi_{d}
can be written as

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta\phi_{d}(T)}

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\displaystyle=D(L^{2}-L_{0}^{2})}

(3)

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\displaystyle=D\left[\left(T_{\mathrm{N}}-(T+\Delta T_{s})\right)^{2\beta}-\left(T_{\mathrm{N}}-T\right)^{2\beta}\right],}

where
\Delta T_{s}
is the laser-induced change of the effective spin temperature and
D
is the scaling coefficient.
In order to show a relation between the dynamics of laser-induced heating of the spin subsystem and
\Delta\phi_{d}(T,\Delta t)
explicitly and to separate an effect of the critical behaviour described by
\beta
from the effect of the spin temperature increase, we introduce the linear approximation of Eq. (3).
The latter is valid under the assumption of relatively small laser-induced changes of
L
, i.e., at temperatures
T
and
T+\Delta T_{s}
being far enough from
T_{\mathrm{N,exp}}
.
Then one gets
\Delta\phi_{d}\propto L\Delta L
, and the expression Eq. (3) is reduced to a form

\displaystyle\Delta\phi_{d}(T)

\displaystyle=DL\frac{dL}{dT}\Delta T_{s}(T)

(4)

\displaystyle=-2D\beta(T_{\mathrm{N}}-T)^{2\beta-1}\Delta T_{s}(T).

As readily seen from
Eq.

4
, demagnetization time
\tau_{2}
correlates with the time required for the heating of the spin subsystem
\tau_{s}
.
The temperature evolution of
\Delta\phi_{d}
, in turn, depends on both
\beta
and the heating of the spin system.
To analyze the response of the spins to optical excitation we employ a two-temperature model
[
anisimov1974electron
]
, considering that in laser-driven dielectrics energy and angular momentum transfer to spins is realized from the phonon subsystem [for a review, see Ref.
[
Kirilyuk-RMP2010
]
] and, thus, it is sufficient to consider only these two reservoirs with effective temperatures
T_{s}
and
T_{p}
, given the electron-phonon relaxation is fast and is of order 2 ps (see App.
for details).
Here we assume that the energy delivered by the pump pulse is absorbed by electrons through
d\!-\!d
and charge-transfer transitions and rapidly deposited to the lattice.

We solve the following system of equations with the initial condition
T_{p}=T_{s}=T
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{split}\frac{\rho}{\mu}C_{p}\left(T_{p}\right)\frac{dT_{p}}{dt}&=g_{sp}\left(T_{s}-T_{p}\right)+P(t),\\
\frac{\rho}{\mu}C_{s}\left(T_{s}\right)\frac{dT_{s}}{dt}&=g_{sp}\left(T_{p}-T_{s}\right),\end{split}\right.

(5)

where
C_{p}
and
C_{s}
are lattice and magnetic molar specific heat, respectively, and
g_{sp}
is the spin-lattice coupling constant.

C_{p}(T)
was determined by an approximation of experimental data on the total specific heat
C(T)
reported in Ref.
[
Siskins2020
]
with the Debye function
[
debye_zur_1912
]
. Magnetic heat capacity was then obtained as
C_{s}(T)=C(T)-C_{p}(T)
.

\rho
and
\mu
are the density and the molar mass, respectively.

P(t)
is the absorbed laser power density found for the flake with a thickness
d\approx 200
nm as
[
walowski2012PhD
]

\displaystyle P(t)=AF\frac{\left(1-e^{-\alpha d}\right)}{d}\frac{2\sqrt{\ln 2}~e^{-4\ln 2~t^{2}/\sigma^{2}}}{\sigma\sqrt{\pi}},

(6)

where
A
and
\alpha
are absorptance and absorption coefficient, respectively.
Absorptance
A=1-R
was calculated using the Fresnel formula for reflection
R
at the air-MPS
3
interface with a complex refractive index
n
.
Contributions from the reflections at other interfaces do not exceed 10 % and are not taken into account.
Other parameters used in calculations are presented in
Section

III.2
.

Synthetic gauge potentials for the dark state polaritons in atomic media
Yu-Hung Kuan, Siang-Wei Shao, I-Kang Liu, Julius Ruseckas, Gediminas Juzeliūnas, Yu-Ju Lin, Wen-Te Liao

Abstract
The quest of utilizing neutral particles to simulate the behaviour of charged particles in a magnetic field makes the generation of artificial magnetic field of great interest. The previous and the only proposal for the production of synthetic magnetic field for the dark state polaritons in electromagnetically induced transparency invokes the mechanical rotation of a sample. Here, we put forward an optical scheme to generate effective gauge potentials for stationary-light polaritons. To demonstrate the capabilities of our approach, we present recipes for having dark state polaritons in degenerate Landau levels and in driven quantum harmonic oscillator. Our scheme paves a novel way towards the investigation of the bosonic analogue of the fractional quantum Hall effect by electromagnetically induced transparency.

II
Discussion

We have put forward a possible optical method to generate synthetic gauge fields for neutral EIT DSPs.
Our optical scheme can not only produce comparable artificial magnetic field to that by mechanical rotation
Otterbach et al. 2010
but also provides a versatile platform to simulate different Hamiltonians, e. g., the demonstrated QHO for DSPs.
Moreover, highly degenerate Landau levels are expected to be prepared by carefully adjusting the distribution of the number of interacting DSPs among LLL strips.
The above DSP’s dynamics can be observed by the direct imaging technique
Campbell et al. 2017
or by the retrieving of the probe fields at different directions like tomography. An optical depth over 1000 has been experimentally achieved
Blatt et al. 2014
;
Hsiao et al. 2018
.
Our scheme is also novel for the investigation of controllable non-Hermitian quantum systems.

III
Methods

The probe light propagating in four directions are simulated by matrix method which stems from the Crank-Nicolson method. The behavior of atoms is simulated by the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method. The size of each temporal grid is about
300ns
, and the spacial grid is about
0.1mm
.

VIII
Driving quantum harmonic oscillator

In order to simulate Rabi oscillation of a two-level quantum harmonic oscillator in a 1D EIT system, we introduce
\hat{P}\cdot\vec{A}
Hamiltonian and perturbed quadratic potential by using

\displaystyle\Omega_{c}^{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega_{c}}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1+\alpha\sin\left(\omega_{d}t\right)},

(45)

\displaystyle\Omega_{c}^{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega_{c}}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1-\alpha\sin\left(\omega_{d}t\right)},

(46)

\displaystyle\Delta_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{E}\left(\frac{x}{\mathit{l}_{E}}\right)^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\beta\omega_{E}\left(\frac{x}{\mathit{l}_{E}}\right)^{4}-\alpha^{2}\frac{m\Omega_{c}^{4}}{8\hbar\eta^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\omega_{d}t\right),

(47)

where the characteristic QHO length
\mathit{l}_{E}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega_{E}}}
.
We get

\displaystyle V_{R}+V_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega_{c}^{2}}{2\eta},

(48)

\displaystyle\vec{V}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\alpha\frac{\Omega_{c}^{2}}{2\eta}\sin\left(\omega_{d}t\right),0,0\right),

(49)

and Eq. (
) becomes

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\rho_{21}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hbar\left(\frac{\Delta_{p}\Omega_{c}^{2}}{\hbar\eta^{2}}\right)\partial_{x}^{2}\rho_{21}-i\alpha\frac{\Omega_{c}^{2}}{2\eta}\sin\left(\omega_{d}t\right)\partial_{x}\rho_{21}

(50)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2\hbar}m\omega_{E}^{2}x^{2}+\beta\frac{2m^{2}\omega_{E}^{3}}{3\hbar^{2}}x^{4}+\alpha^{2}\frac{m\Omega_{c}^{4}}{8\hbar\eta^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\omega_{d}t\right)\right]\rho_{21}+i\Gamma\left(\frac{\Omega_{c}^{2}}{2\eta^{2}}\right)\partial_{x}^{2}\rho_{21}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}\left[\hbar\left(-i\partial_{x}\right)^{2}+2m\alpha\frac{\Omega_{c}^{2}}{2\eta}\sin\left(\omega_{d}t\right)\left(-i\partial_{x}\right)+m^{2}\alpha^{2}\frac{\Omega_{c}^{4}}{4\hbar\eta^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\omega_{d}t\right)\right]\rho_{21}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2\hbar}m\omega_{E}^{2}x^{2}+\beta\frac{2m^{2}\omega_{E}^{3}}{3\hbar^{2}}x^{4}\right)\rho_{21}+i\frac{\hbar\Gamma}{2\Delta_{p}}\left(\frac{\Delta_{p}\Omega_{c}^{2}}{\hbar\eta^{2}}\right)\partial_{x}^{2}\rho_{21}.

Multiplying both side with
\hbar
, Eq. (
50
) becomes an electron-like Schrödinger equation:

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\partial\rho_{21}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}\left[\left(-i\hbar\partial_{x}\right)^{2}+2m\alpha\frac{\Omega_{c}^{2}}{2\eta}\sin\left(\omega_{d}t\right)\left(-i\hbar\partial_{x}\right)+\alpha^{2}\frac{m^{2}\Omega_{c}^{4}}{4\eta^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\omega_{d}t\right)\right]\rho_{21}

(51)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{E}^{2}x^{2}+\beta\frac{2m^{2}\omega_{E}^{3}}{3\hbar}x^{4}\right)\rho_{21}-i\frac{\Gamma}{2\Delta_{p}}\left(\frac{1}{2m}\right)\left(-i\hbar\partial_{x}\right)^{2}\rho_{21}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}\left\{\left(-i\hbar\partial_{x}\right)+e\left[\alpha\frac{m\Omega_{c}^{2}}{2e\eta}\sin\left(\omega_{d}t\right)\right]\right\}^{2}\rho_{21}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{E}^{2}x^{2}+\beta\frac{2m^{2}\omega_{E}^{3}}{3\hbar}x^{4}\right)\rho_{21}-i\frac{\Gamma}{2\Delta_{p}}\left(\frac{1}{2m}\right)\left(-i\hbar\partial_{x}\right)^{2}\rho_{21}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\widehat{P}+e\vec{A}\right)^{2}}{2m}\rho_{21}+U\rho_{21}-i\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2\Delta_{p}}\right)\frac{\widehat{P}^{2}}{2m}\rho_{21}.

Here the effective wavefunction, momentum operator, effective vector potential, and effective mass are respectively

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{21},

(52)

\displaystyle\widehat{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i}\partial_{x},

(53)

\displaystyle\vec{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar\eta^{2}}{2e\Delta_{p}\Omega_{c}^{2}}\vec{V}_{g}=\left(\alpha\frac{\hbar\eta}{4e\Delta_{p}}\sin\left(\omega_{d}t\right),0,0\right),

(54)

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{E}^{2}x^{2}+\beta\frac{2m^{2}\omega_{E}^{3}}{3\hbar}x^{4},

(55)

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar\eta^{2}}{2\Omega_{c}^{2}\Delta_{p}}.

(56)

One can then get the effective electric field and magnetic field

\displaystyle\vec{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla U-\frac{\partial\vec{A}}{\partial t}=\left(-m\omega_{E}^{2}x-\beta\frac{8m^{2}\omega_{E}^{3}}{3\hbar}x^{3}-\alpha\frac{\hbar\eta\omega_{d}}{4e\Delta_{p}}\cos\left(\omega_{d}t\right),0,0\right),

(57)

\displaystyle\vec{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\times\vec{A}=0.

(58)

VIII.1
Driving Rabi frequency

We calculate the effective Rabi frequency
\Omega_{A}=\frac{e}{m\hbar}\vec{A}\cdot\hat{P}
and neglect the small
|\vec{A}|^{2}
term as typically adopted in quantum optics

\displaystyle\Omega_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e}{m\hbar}\vec{A}\cdot\langle n+1|\hat{P}|n\rangle=\frac{\alpha\eta}{4m\Delta_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar m\omega_{E}}{2}}\langle n+1|\left(\hat{a}^{+}-\hat{a}\right)|n\rangle=\frac{\alpha\eta}{4\Delta_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{E}\left(n+1\right)}{2m}}

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\Omega_{c}}{4}\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{E}\left(n+1\right)}{\Delta_{p}}},

where
\hat{a}^{+}
and
\hat{a}
are QHO raising and lowering operators, respectively.
In the main text we use

\Delta_{c}=\Delta_{p}-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{E}\left(\frac{x}{\mathit{l}_{E}}\right)^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\beta\omega_{E}\left(\frac{x}{\mathit{l}_{E}}\right)^{4},

(60)

which also neglects the
|\vec{A}|^{2}
term and leads to the same Rabi frequency.

VIII.2
The first-order perturbed eigen-energy

We invoke raising and lowering operators to calculate the first-order perturbed energy by the quartic term.

\displaystyle\delta E_{n}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\frac{2m^{2}\omega_{E}^{3}}{3\hbar}\langle n|x^{4}|n\rangle

(61)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\frac{2m^{2}\omega_{E}^{3}}{3\hbar}\left(\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega_{E}}\right)^{2}\langle n|\left(\hat{a}^{+}+\hat{a}\right)^{4}|n\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{6}\hbar\omega_{E}\langle n|\left(\hat{a}^{+2}\hat{a}^{2}+\hat{a}^{+}\hat{a}\hat{a}^{+}\hat{a}+\hat{a}^{+}\hat{a}^{2}\hat{a}^{+}+\hat{a}\hat{a}^{+2}\hat{a}+\hat{a}\hat{a}^{+}\hat{a}\hat{a}^{+}+\hat{a}^{2}\hat{a}^{+2}\right)|n\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\left(n^{2}+n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega_{E}.

The first-order perturbed eigen energy under the quartic term reads

E_{n}=\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega_{E}+\beta\left(n^{2}+n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega_{E}.

(62)

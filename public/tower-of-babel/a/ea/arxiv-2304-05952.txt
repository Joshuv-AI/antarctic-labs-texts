On a class of Schauder frames in Banach spaces
Samir Kabbaj, Rafik Karkri and Zoubeir Hicham

Abstract
In this paper, we give a characterization and a some properties of a besselian sequences, which allows us to build some examples of a besselian Schauder frames. Also for a reflexive Banach spaces (with a besselian Schauder frames) we give some characterizations.

Main definitions, principal notations and useful remarks

Let
\left(X,\left\|.\right\|_{X}\right)
be a Banach space on
\mathbb{K}\in\left\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\right\}
and
X^{\ast},

X^{\ast\ast}
and
X^{\ast\ast\ast}
respectively its first, second and
third topological duals.

1.

We denote by
\mathbb{B}_{X}
the closed unit ball of
X:

\mathbb{B}_{X}:=\left\{x\in X:\left\|x\right\|_{X}\leq 1\right\}

2.

We denote by
l^{1}(\mathbb{K)}
the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space of
sequences
\lambda:=\left(\lambda_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
such that
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{K}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
and
\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\left|\lambda_{n}\right|<+\infty.\
It is a classical result that
l^{1}(\mathbb{K)}
is a Banach
space for the norm :

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\left\|.\right\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{K)}}:&l^{1}(\mathbb{K)}&\rightarrow&\mathbb{R}^{+}\\
&\left(\lambda_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}&\mapsto&\underset{n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|\lambda_{n}\right|\end{array}

3.

We denote, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast},
by
e_{n}
the element of
l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)
defined by the
relation
e_{n}:=(\delta_{k,n})_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
, where
\delta_{k,n}=1
if
k=n
and
\delta_{k,n}=0
if
k\neq n.

4.

We denote, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast},
by
u_{n}^{\ast}
the element of
\left(l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)\right)^{\ast}
defined, for each
\lambda:=\left(\lambda_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}\in l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)
by the relation
u_{n}^{\ast}\left(\lambda\right):=\lambda_{n}.

5.

We denote by
l^{\infty}(\mathbb{K)}
the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
of sequences
\lambda:=\left(\lambda_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
such that
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{K}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
and
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}\left(\left|\lambda_{n}\right|\right)<+\infty.\
It
is a classical result that
l^{\infty}(\mathbb{K)}
is a Banach space for
the norm :

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\left\|.\right\|_{l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)}:&l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)&\rightarrow&\mathbb{R}^{+}\\
&\left(\mu_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}&\mapsto&\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}{\sup}\left(\left|\mu_{n}\right|\right)\end{array}

6.

We denote by
\Psi
the mapping :

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Psi:&l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)&\rightarrow&\left(l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)\right)^{\ast}\\
&\mu:=\left(\mu_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}&\mapsto&\Psi\left(\mu\right)\end{array}

where :

\Psi\left(\mu\right)\left(\left(\lambda_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}\right):=\underset{n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\mu_{n}\lambda_{n}

It is well-known that
\Psi
is an isometric isomorphism from
l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)
onto
l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)

[
16
, page 85, example 1.10.3]

.

7.

We denot by
J_{X}
the canonical linear mapping :

\begin{array}[]{cccc}J_{X}:&X&\rightarrow&X^{\ast\ast}\\
&x&\mapsto&J_{X}(x)\end{array}

defined for each
x\in X
and
x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast}
by the formula
J_{X}(x)(x^{\ast})=x^{\ast}\left(x\right)
. It is well-known

[
16
, page 98, proposition 1.11.3]
that the linear mapping
J_{X}
is an
isometry from
X
into
X^{\ast\ast}.

8.

Let
\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
be a sequence of
elements of
X.
The series
\sum x_{n}
of
X
is said to be weakly
unconditionally convergent
[
22
, pages 58-59]
if the series
\sum\left|x^{\ast}\left(x_{n}\right)\right|
is convergent for
each
x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast}
.

9.

The Banach space
X
is said to be weakly sequentially complete
[
16
, page 218, definition 2.5.23]

[
9
, pages 37-38]
if for each
sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
of
X
such that
\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}x^{\ast}(x_{n})
exists for every
x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast},
there exists
x\in X
such that
\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}x^{\ast}(x_{n})=x^{\ast}(x)
for every
x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast}
.

10.

A sequence
\mathfrak{X}:=\left(\left(x_{n},y_{n}^{\ast}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}\subset X\times X^{*}
is called a paire of
X
.

11.

The sequence
\mathfrak{X}^{\ast}:=\left(\left(y_{n}^{\ast},J_{X}\left(x_{n}\right)\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}\subset X^{*}\times X^{**}
is
called the dual paire of the paire
\mathfrak{X}.

12.

The paire
\mathfrak{X}
is called a Schauder frame (resp.
unconditional Schauder frame ) of
X
if for all
x\in X
, the series
\sum y_{n}^{\ast}\left(x\right)x_{n}
is convergent (resp. unconditionally
convergent) in
X
to
x
.

13.

The paire
\mathfrak{X}
is said to be a besselian paire of
X
if
there exists a constant
A>0
such that :

\underset{n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|y_{n}^{\ast}\left(x\right)\right|\left|x^{\ast}\left(x_{n}\right)\right|\leq A\left\|x\right\|_{X}\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|_{X^{\ast}}

for each
x\in X
and
x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast}.

14.

The paire
\mathfrak{X}
is said to be a besselian Schauder frame of
X
if it is both a besselian paire and a Schauder frame of
X
.

15.

A Schauder frame
\mathfrak{X}
of
X
is said to be shrinking if the
series
\sum

x^{\ast}\left(x_{n}\right)y_{n}^{\ast}
is convergent
for every
x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast}.

16.

A Schauder frame
\mathfrak{X}
of
X
is said to be boundedly
complete if the series
\sum x^{\ast\ast}\left(y_{n}^{\ast}\right)x_{n}
is convergent in
X
for every
x^{\ast\ast}\in X^{\ast\ast}.

Remark 1
.

For each
\lambda=\left(\lambda_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}\in l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right),
it is clear that the series
\sum u_{n}^{\ast}\left(\lambda\right)e_{n}
is convergent and that we have
:

\lambda=\underset{n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}u_{n}^{\ast}\left(\lambda\right)e_{n}

On the other hand let us given
\lambda=\left(\lambda_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}\in l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)
and
\xi^{\ast}\in\left(l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)\right)^{\ast}.
We set
\mu:=\Psi^{-1}\left(\xi^{\ast}\right)\in l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)
.
Hence
\xi^{\ast}=\Psi\left(\mu\right)
and we have
:

\displaystyle\underset{n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|u_{n}^{\ast}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\left|\xi^{\ast}\left(e_{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|\lambda_{n}\right|\left|\mu_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\underset{n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|\lambda_{n}\right|\right)\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}{\sup}\left|\mu_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda\right\|_{l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)}\left\|\mu\right\|_{l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda\right\|_{l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)}\left\|\xi^{\ast}\right\|_{\left(l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)\right)^{\ast}}

It follows that
\left(\left(e_{n},u_{n}^{*}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a besselian Schauder frame of
l^{1}\left(\mathbb{K}\right)
.

Remark 2
.

convergent for each

Consequently
\mathfrak{X}
is
a shrinking Schauder frame of
X.

∎

Fundamental results

Definition 1
.

[
15
]
.
Given a Banach space
\left(Y,\left\|.\right\|_{Y}\right)

and a real number
0<\alpha<1
. A linear mapping
\varphi:X\rightarrow Y
is
said to be an
\alpha
-isometry if the following condition
holds for each
x\in X:

\left(1-\alpha\right)\left\|x\right\|_{X}\leq\left\|\varphi\left(x\right)\right\|_{Y}\leq\left(1+\alpha\right)\left\|x\right\|_{X}

Theorem 1
.

The paire
\mathcal{F}
of
E
is a besselian
paire of
E
if and only if its dual paire
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}

is a besselian paire of
E^{\ast}.
In this case we have
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ast}}.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian paire of
E
. Let
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast}
,
x^{\ast\ast}\in E^{\ast\ast}
. We set for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
:
U=span(x^{\ast\ast})
and
V_{n}=span(b_{1}^{\ast},...,b_{n}^{\ast})
. It is clear that
U
and
V_{n}
are finite
dimensional subspaces of
E^{\ast\ast}
and
E^{\ast}
respectively.

According to the principle of local reflexivity
[
15
, Theorem.2]
, we
can find for each
\alpha>0
an
\alpha
-isometry
T_{n}:U\longrightarrow E
such that :

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}b_{j}^{\ast}\left(T_{n}(x^{\ast\ast})\right)=x^{\ast\ast}(b_{j}^{\ast}),\text{ }j\in\left\{1,...n\right\}\\
\left\|T_{n}(x^{\ast\ast})\right\|_{E}\leq(1+\alpha)\left\|x^{\ast\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast\ast}}\end{array}\right.

It follows that :

\displaystyle\underset{j=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\left|x^{\ast\ast}(b_{j}^{\ast})\right|\left|J_{E}(a_{j})\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\underset{j=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\left|b_{j}^{\ast}(T_{n}(x^{\ast\ast}))\right|\left|x^{\ast}(a_{j})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left\|T_{n}(x^{\ast\ast})\right\|_{E}\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left\|x^{\ast\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast\ast}}\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast}}

Consequently, we have

\underset{j=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|x^{\ast\ast}(b_{j}^{\ast})\right|\left|J_{E}(a_{j})\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right|\leq(1+\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left\|x^{\ast\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast\ast}}\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast}}

for each
\alpha>0
,
x^{\ast\ast}\in E^{\ast\ast}
and
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast}
. Hence :

\underset{j=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|x^{\ast\ast}(b_{j}^{\ast})\right|\left|J_{E}(a_{j})\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right|\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left\|x^{\ast\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast\ast}}\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast}}

for each
x^{\ast\ast}\in E^{\ast\ast}
and
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast}
.
Consequently
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}
is a besselian paire of
E^{\ast}
and we
have
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ast}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}.

Assume now that
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}
is a besselian paire of
E^{\ast}
.
Let
(x,x^{\ast})\in E\times E^{\ast}
, then we have :

\displaystyle\underset{j=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{j}^{\ast}(x)\right|\left|x^{\ast}(a_{j})\right|

\displaystyle=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left|J_{E}(x)(b_{j}^{\ast})\right|\left|J_{E}\left(a_{j}\right)(x^{\ast})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ast}}\left\|J_{E}(x)\right\|_{E^{\ast\ast}}\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ast}}\left\|x\right\|_{E}\left\|y^{\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast}}

Hence
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian paire of
E

and we have
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ast}}.

We conclude that :

(i)
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian paire of
E
if and only
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}
is a besselian paire of
E^{\ast}
.

(ii) If
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian paire of
E
then we will have
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ast}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}\text{ }}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ast}},
hence
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ast}}.

The proof of the theorem is then complete. ∎

Proposition 1
.

1.

We assume that
E
is weakly sequentially complete and
that
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian paire of
E
. Then for each
x\in E,
the series
\sum b_{n}^{\ast}\left(x\right)a_{n}
is unconditionally convergent.

2.

We assume that
E^{\ast}
is weakly sequentially
complete and that
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian paire of
E
. Then for each
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast},
the series
\sum x^{\ast}\left(a_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\ast}
is unconditionally
convergent.

Proof.

1.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian paire of
E
, it follows that we have
for each
x\in E
and
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast}:

\displaystyle\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\left|x^{\ast}\left(b_{n}^{\ast}\left(x\right)a_{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\left|b_{n}^{\ast}\left(x\right)\right|\left|x^{\ast}\left(a_{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left\|x\right\|_{E}\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast}}

\displaystyle<+\infty

Hence the series
\sum b_{n}^{\ast}\left(x\right)a_{n}
is weakly
unconditionally convergent. Hence, since
E
is weakly sequentially
complete, it follows, thanks to Orlicz’s theorem
[
19
]
;
[
22
, theorem of page 66]
,
, that the series
\sum b_{n}^{\ast}\left(x\right)a_{n}
is unconditionally convergent.

2.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian paire of
E,
it follows, thanks to
the theorem
1
, that the paire
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}
is a besselian paire of

E^{\ast}.
Hence we have for each
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast}
and
x^{\ast\ast}\in E^{\ast\ast}
:

\displaystyle\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\left|x^{\ast\ast}\left(x^{\ast}\left(a_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\ast}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\left|x^{\ast}\left(a_{n}\right)\right|\left|x^{\ast\ast}\left(b_{n}^{\ast}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ast}}\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast}}\left\|x^{\ast\ast}\right\|_{E^{\ast\ast}}

\displaystyle<+\infty

Hence for each
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast},
the series
\sum x^{\ast}\left(a_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\ast}
is weakly unconditionally convergent. Hence
since
E^{\ast}
is weakly sequentially complete it follows thanks to
Orlicz’s theorem
[
19
]
;
[
22
, theorem of page 66]
that the
series
\sum x^{\ast}\left(a_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\ast}
is, for each
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast},
unconditionally convergent.

The proof of the proposition is then complete.
∎

Proposition 2
.

1.

We assume that
E
is weakly
sequentially complete and that the paire
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}
is a
besselian Schauder frame of
E^{\ast}
. Then the paire
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian Schauder frame of
\ E.

2.

We assume that the dual space
E^{*}
is weakly
sequentially complete and that the paire
\mathcal{F}
is a
besselian Schauder frame of
E
. Then the paire
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}
is a besselian Schauder frame of
E^{*}
.

Proof.

1.

Since
E
is weakly sequentially complete and that
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}

is a besselian Schauder frame of
E^{\ast}
it follows, from the
theorem
1
and the proposition
1
,
that
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian paire of
E

and that the series
\sum b_{n}^{\ast}(x)a_{n}
is
unconditionally convergent for each
x\in E
. Let us then
consider the mapping:

\begin{array}[]{cccc}S:&E&\rightarrow&E\\
&x&\mapsto&\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}b_{n}^{\ast}\left(x\right)a_{n}\end{array}

Then we have for each
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast}
and
x\in E
:

\displaystyle x^{\ast}\left(S(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}x^{\ast}\left(a_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\ast}(x)

\displaystyle=J_{E}\left(x\right)\left(\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}x^{\ast}\left(a_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=J_{E}\left(x\right)\left(x^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=x^{\ast}\left(x\right)

Consequently,
S(x)=x
,
x\in E
. So
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian Schauder
frame of
E
.

2.

Since
E^{\ast}
is weakly sequentially complete it follows, from the
theorem (
1
) and the proposition
1
,
that
\mathcal{F}^{*}
is a besselian paire of
E^{*}
and that
the series
\sum J_{E}(a_{n})(x^{\ast})b_{n}^{\ast}
is unconditionally
convergent for each
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast}
. Let us then
consider the mapping :

\begin{array}[]{cccc}T:&E^{\ast}&\rightarrow&E^{\ast}\\
&x^{\ast}&\mapsto&\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}J_{E}\left(a_{n}\right)\left(x^{\ast}\right)b_{n}^{\ast}\end{array}

Then we have for each
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast}
and
x\in E
:

\displaystyle T(x^{\ast})(x)

\displaystyle=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}x^{\ast}\left(a_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\ast}(x)

\displaystyle=J_{E}\left(x\right)\left(\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}J_{E}(a_{n})\left(x^{\ast}\right)b_{n}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=J_{E}\left(x\right)\left(x^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=x^{\ast}\left(x\right)

Consequently we have, for each
x^{\ast}\in E^{\ast},

T(x^{\ast})=x^{\ast}
. So
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}
is a besselian Schauder frame of
E^{\ast}
.

The proof of the proposition is then complete.
∎

.

For

. We define

Hölder inequality we obtain:

\displaystyle\overset{+\infty}{\underset{m=-\infty}{\sum}}\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\left|\Phi_{p,q}\left(T_{m}\tilde{b}_{n}\right)\left(f\right)\right|\left|\Phi_{p,q}(g)\left(T_{m}\tilde{a}_{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{m=-\infty}{\sum}}\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}T_{m}\left(\tilde{b}_{n}\right)f\right|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}T_{m}\left(\tilde{a}_{n}\right)g\right|

\displaystyle=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{m=-\infty}{\sum}}\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\left|\int_{m}^{m+1}T_{m}\left(\tilde{b}_{n}\right)f\right|\left|\int_{m}^{m+1}T_{m}\left(\tilde{a}_{n}\right)g\right|

\displaystyle=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{m=-\infty}{\sum}}\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}T_{-m}\left(\chi_{m}f\right)\right|\left|\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}T_{-m}\left(\chi_{m}g\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\overset{+\infty}{\underset{m=-\infty}{\sum}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left|T_{-m}\left(\chi_{m}f\right)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left|T_{-m}\left(\chi_{m}g\right)\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{1/p^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\overset{+\infty}{\underset{m=-\infty}{\sum}}\left(\int_{m}^{m+1}\left|f\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{m}^{m+1}\left|g\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{1/p^{*}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left\|f\right\|_{\left(L_{p},l^{q}\right)}\left\|\Phi_{p,q}(g)\right\|_{\left(L_{p},l^{q}\right)^{*}}

Finally, since
(L_{p},l^{q})
is reflexive then it is also weakly
sequentially complete. It follows, according to the proposition (
1
)
that
\left(\left(T_{m}\tilde{a}_{n},\Phi_{p,q}\left(T_{m}\tilde{b}_{n}\right)\right)\right)_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}}

is an unconditional besselian Schauder frame of
(L_{p},l^{q})
.

∎

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian Schauder frame of

E
. Then
E
is reflexive if and only if
\mathcal{F}

is shrinking and boundedly complete.

Proof.

Assume that
E
is reflexive. Then
E^{\ast}
is also reflexive
[
16
, Corollary 1.11.17 page 104]
.
Consequently,
E
and
E^{\ast}
are weakly sequentially complete Banach
spaces. Since
\mathcal{F}
is a besselian paire of
E
, it follows, thanks
to the theorem
1
, that
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}
is a besselian paire of
E^{\ast}
. It follows from proposition
3
, that
\mathcal{F}
is shrinking
and boundedly complete.

Assume now that
\mathcal{F}
is shrinking and boundedly complete. Let be
given
x^{\ast\ast}\in E^{\ast\ast}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is boundedly
complete then the series
\sum x^{\ast\ast}\left(b_{n}^{\ast}\right)a_{n}
convergent to an element
x
in
E
, that is:

x=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}x^{\ast\ast}(b_{n}^{\ast})a_{n}

But
\mathcal{F}
is shrinking. Hence, thanks to the proposition
4
,
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}
is a Schauder frame of
E^{\ast}
. It follows that :

y^{\ast}=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}y^{\ast}(a_{n})b_{n}^{\ast},\;y^{\ast}\in E^{\ast}

Consequently, we have for each

\displaystyle J_{E}(x)(x^{\ast})

\displaystyle=x^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}x^{\ast}(a_{n})x^{**}\left(b_{n}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=x^{\ast\ast}\left(\overset{+\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}x^{\ast}(a_{n})b_{n}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=x^{\ast\ast}(x^{\ast})

It follows that
x^{**}=J_{E}(x)
. Thus

is surjective.
Consequently
E

The proof of the theorem is then complete.
∎

Theorem 4

Assume that

Optimal control problems for a parabolic system modeling glioma therapy
Mauro Garavello, Elena Rossi

Abstract
In this paper we consider optimal control problems for a parabolic system modeling a therapy, based on oncolytic viruses, for the glioma brain cancer. Using several techniques typical of functional analysis, we prove the global in time well posedness of the control model, the existence of optimal controls for specific objective functionals, which are natural for cancer therapies, and we derive necessary conditions for optimality.



Key Words:
Control problems; parabolic partial differential
equations; necessary and sufficient conditions for optimality;
glioma cancer
therapy; virotherapy; existence of optimal controls.

1
Introduction

In this paper we consider optimal control problems for a
3\times 3

system of parabolic partial differential equations modeling a therapy
in the case of a brain cancer, the glioma one, based on the infusion
of oncolytic viruses. They are genetically modified viruses able to
infect cancer cells and to replicate inside them, but they are not
harmful for healthy cells. With this mechanism, they eventually kill
mainly cancer cells. Moreover, when an infected cell dies, it releases
many copies of the viruses, which then spread to infect neighboring
tumor cells. The main obstacle in the use of oncolytic viruses
consists in the fact that the innate immune system
recognizes the cells infected by the virus and destroys them before
the virus multiply. In this paper, as in
[
10
]

and
[
27
]
, we are neglecting this aspect.

There is a huge mathematical literature for cancer modeling based on
differential equations; see
[
1
,
16
,
19
,
20
]
and the reference therein for a detailed description.
This is essentially due to the large variety of diseases commonly
named under the word
cancer
. Each tumor has some specific
peculiarities and dynamics; hence it requires an ad-hoc model for a
precise mathematical description. Also therapies vary
accordingly. For example, they include chemotherapy, radiotherapy, stem
cell transplant, surgery and can be dosed also combined together. This
justifies the large number of mathematical papers dealing with
this subject.
In particular, considering Glioma type cancer,
we can distinguish the various models through different categories: based on ODEs
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
or on PDEs
[
4
,
6
,
10
,
11
,
27
]
,
focusing on controlling aspects
[
6
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
, on therapy calibration
[
2
,
10
,
11
,
27
]
or on asymptotic behaviour of
solutions
[
4
,
10
]
.

The main results of this paper are the well posedness of the parabolic
3\times 3

control system, the existence of optimal controls, and first order
necessary conditions for optimality. They are
obtained through several techniques typical of functional analysis.
In particular, different from other papers in the literature,
see for instance
[
4
,
6
]
, we use the Banach fixed point theorem to prove local
in time existence and uniqueness of solution for the parabolic system.
Moreover, a combination of a-priori estimates and maximum principles
for scalar equations permits to extend the solution to arbitrary time
intervals obtaining global in time well posedness. Gronwall
inequality is used to prove the Lipschitz continuity, in the

\mathbf{L^{2}}
topology, of the solution with respect to the controls.
Existence of optimal controls is deduced using the direct
method in the calculus of variation; see for example
[
9
]
.
Here continuous embedding theorems and Ascoli-Arzelà Theorem play an
important role in the weak and strong convergence of quasi-optimal
solutions.
Finally, necessary conditions are obtained through the
derivative of the input-output map and the adjoint
system.

The main novelty of the paper consists in the study of
a nonlinear system of parabolic partial differential equations with an
open loop control function; see
[
7
,
12
,
17
,
26
]
and the references therein for
control problems for partial differential equations. Here we derive
necessary and sufficient conditions for optimal controls.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section

2
introduces the
mathematical model and the definition of solution.
In
Section

3
we prove the existence and
uniqueness of solution both local and global in time.
In
Section

4
we study the Lipschitz continuous
dependence of the solution with respect to the control function, while
in
Section

5
we deduce the existence of optimal
controls for some objective functionals, natural for cancer therapies.

Section

6
deals with necessary conditions for
optimality. Finally
Appendix

contains classical results about

Section

3
. It is mainly intended to ease the
readability of the paper.

Basic definitions and notations

In this paper we consider control problems for the system of partial
differential equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\,\rho_{1}=\Delta\rho_{1}+\left(\alpha-\delta_{1}\right)\rho_{1}-\beta\rho_{1}v\\
\partial_{t}\,\rho_{2}=\Delta\rho_{2}+\beta\rho_{1}v-\delta_{2}\rho_{2}\\
\partial_{t}\,v=\Delta v+b\delta_{2}\rho_{2}-B\rho_{1}v-\delta_{v}v+u,\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
t\geq 0
is the time,
x\in\Omega
is the spatial variable,

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an open, bounded, and connected set with
smooth boundary denoted by
\partial\Omega
, and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

N\geq 2
(typically
N=2
or
N=3
in applications). Moreover

\rho_{1},\rho_{2}:(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
describe the density
respectively of uninfected cancer cells and of infected cancer cells,
while
v:(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
represents the density of
the injected virus. The map
u=u(t,x)
is the control
function modeling the velocity of the virus infusion. Finally

\alpha
,
\beta
,
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
,
\delta_{v}
,
b
,
B
are
fixed positive constants.
In the paper we consider controls depending also on
the spatial variable, although, as detailed in
[
8
]
,
in real situations the viral therapy is administered intravenously,
so that a control depending only on time should be more
realistic.

We augment the system (
1
) with the initial
conditions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{1}(0,x)=\rho_{1,o}(x)\\
\rho_{2}(0,x)=\rho_{2,o}(x)\\
v(0,x)=v_{o}(x),\end{array}\right.

(2)

where
\rho_{1,o},\rho_{2,o},v_{o}\in\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)
, and
with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{\nu}\rho_{1}(t,\xi)=0\\
\partial_{\nu}\rho_{2}(t,\xi)=0\\
\partial_{\nu}v(t,\xi)=0\end{array}\right.

(3)

for
\xi\in\partial\Omega
, where the symbol
\partial_{\nu}
denotes
the inner normal derivative.

Throughout the paper, we deal with the following concept of weak
solution for
system (
1
)-(
2
)-(
3
).

Definition 2.1

Given
T>0
, the triple
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v)

)-(

)

;

3.

\dot{\rho}_{1},\dot{\rho}_{2},\dot{v}\in\mathbf{L^{2}}([0,T];\mathbf{H^{1}}(\Omega)^{*})
;

4.

\rho_{1}(0,x)=\rho_{1,o}(x)
,
\rho_{2}(0,x)=\rho_{2,o}(x)
,

v(0,x)=v_{o}(x)
in
\mathbf{L^{2}}(\Omega)
;

5.

for a.e.

and for any

\displaystyle\langle\dot{\rho}_{1}(t),w_{1}\rangle=

\displaystyle-\!\int_{\Omega}\nabla\rho_{1}(t,x)\cdot\nabla w_{1}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}+(\alpha-\delta_{1})\!\int_{\Omega}\rho_{1}(t,x)\,w_{1}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}

\displaystyle-\beta\int_{\Omega}\rho_{1}(t,x)\,v(t,x)\,w_{1}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}},

\displaystyle\langle\dot{\rho}_{2}(t),w_{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\rho_{2}(t,x)\cdot\nabla w_{2}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}-\delta_{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{2}(t,x)\,w_{2}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}

\displaystyle+\beta\int_{\Omega}\rho_{1}(t,x)\,v(t,x)\,w_{2}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}},

\displaystyle\langle\dot{v}(t),w_{3}\rangle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla v(t,x)\cdot\nabla w_{3}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}+b\,\delta_{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{2}(t,x)\,w_{3}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}

\displaystyle-B\int_{\Omega}\rho_{1}(t,x)\,v(t,x)\,w_{3}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}-\delta_{v}\int_{\Omega}v(t,x)\,w_{3}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}

\displaystyle{+\int_{\Omega}u(t,x)\,w_{3}(x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}}.

2.1
Model justification

In
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
the authors proposed a
mathematical model for the therapy of glioma based on oncolytic
viruses infusion. Oncolytic viruses are genetically altered viruses
able to infect cancer cells but not normal ones. They reproduce in
cancer cells and eventually kill them, and when an infected cell dies,
many new viruses are released and spread out. The model
in
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
is given by
the following system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=\alpha x-\beta xv-\delta_{x}x\\
\dot{y}=\beta xv-\xi y\frac{T}{K+T}-\delta_{y}y\\
\dot{M}=A+syM-\delta_{M}M\\
\dot{T}=\frac{\eta}{1+u_{2}}M-\omega y\frac{T}{K+T}-\delta_{T}T\\
\dot{v}=b\delta_{y}y-\rho xv-\delta_{v}v+u_{1},\end{array}\right.

(4)

where the unknowns
x
,
y
,
M
,
T
,
v
represent respectively the
density of uninfected cancer stem cells, the density of infected
cancer cells, the density of the macrophages, the concentration of
TNF-
\alpha
inhibitors, and the density of the virus. The control
functions
u_{1}=u_{1}(t)
and
u_{2}=u_{2}(t)
denote respectively the
amount of virus and of TNF-
\alpha
inhibitor that is injected at time

t
. The descriptions and realistic values of the various parameters
appearing in system (
4
) can be found
in
[
23
, Table 2]
. One can also note in
[
23
]

that the dynamics of the unknowns
M
and
T
is almost static around
the values
M\sim 0.1\frac{g}{cm^{3}}
and

T\sim 5\times 10^{-6}\frac{g}{cm^{3}}
. Hence (
4
) can be
approximated by the
3\times 3
system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=\alpha x-\beta xv-\delta_{x}x\\
\dot{y}=\beta xv-\tilde{\xi}y-\delta_{y}y\\
\dot{v}=b\delta_{y}y-\rho xv-\delta_{v}v+u_{1}.\end{array}\right.

(5)

Model (
1
) is the natural generalization
of (
5
) once we allow the densities of cancer
cells and of the virus to depend also on the spatial coordinate.

3
Local and global existence

In this section we prove both the local and global well posedness for
system (

using a fixed point technique, while a-priori estimates permit to
extend the solution to arbitrary time intervals.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle T<

\displaystyle\min\left\{\frac{1}{\alpha+\delta_{1}+\frac{3M-1}{2}\,\beta}\ln\left(\frac{M+1}{M-1}\right),\,\frac{1}{\delta_{2}}\ln\left(\frac{M}{M-1}\right),\right.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{2}}\ln\left(1+\frac{2\,\delta_{2}}{\beta\,(3M-1)^{2}}\right),\,\frac{2}{(3M-1)B+2\,\delta_{v}}\ln\left(\frac{M}{M-1}\right),\,

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in{\mathbf{C^{0}}}\left([0,T];\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)\right):\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\zeta(t)-\rho_{1,o}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq M\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in{\mathbf{C^{0}}}\left([0,T];\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)\right):\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\zeta(t)-v_{o}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq M\right\}

(9)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{1}+\beta\left\|\bar{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{2}\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\|\bar{v}(t)-\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{1}+\alpha+\beta\left\|\bar{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}.

Finally, the same arguments can be applied to
\bar{\rho}_{2}
and

\tilde{\rho}_{2}
:

\partial_{t}\,\!\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\right)=\Delta\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\right)-\delta_{2}\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\right)+\beta\bar{\rho}_{1}\bar{v}-\beta\tilde{\rho}_{1}\tilde{v}.

For a.e.
t\in[0,T]
and for every

w\in\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)
it holds

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left(\dot{\bar{\rho}}_{2}-\dot{\tilde{\rho}}_{2}\right)w\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\right)\cdot\nabla w\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}+\delta_{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\right)w\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\\
=\ &\beta\int_{\Omega}\left(\bar{\rho}_{1}\,\bar{v}-\tilde{\rho}_{1}\,\tilde{v}\right)w\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}.\end{split}

(21)

The bilinear form appearing above is weakly coercive:

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\right)\cdot\nabla\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}+\delta_{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\right)^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}&\\
+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{2}\right)\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}&\geq\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)}.\end{split}

(22)

Taking
w(x)=\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}(t,x)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t,x)\right)

in (
21
) and exploiting (
22
), we
get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{}}}{\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}}\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{H^{1}}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\beta\int_{\Omega}\bar{\rho}_{1}(t,x)\left(\bar{v}(t,x)-\tilde{v}(t,x)\right)\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}(t,x)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t,x)\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}

\displaystyle+\beta\int_{\Omega}\tilde{v}(t,x)\left(\bar{\rho}_{1}(t,x)-\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t,x)\right)\left(\bar{\rho}_{2}(t,x)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t,x)\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{2}\right)\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{v}(t)-\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left\|\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{2}\right)\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}.

Thus, collecting together the estimates obtained above, for a.e.

t\in[0,T]
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{}}}{\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}}\left(\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{v}(t)-\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{v}(t)-\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\left(B+\beta\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}+\beta\left\|\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}+1+2\,\delta_{1}+2\,\alpha+2\,\beta\left\|\bar{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\beta+\beta\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}+b\,\delta_{2}+1+2\,\delta_{2}\right)\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(2\,\beta+b\,\delta_{2}+B\left\|\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}+{2}+2\,\delta_{v}+B\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left\|\bar{v}(t)-\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle+{\left\|\bar{u}(t)-\tilde{u}(t)\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}(\Omega)}}.

Set

\displaystyle C(t)=\ \max\Big\{

\displaystyle B+\beta\left(\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\,\left\|\bar{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega\right)}\right)

\displaystyle+1+2\,\delta_{1}+2\,

\displaystyle\alpha,\,\beta+\beta\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}+b\,\delta_{2}+1+2\,\delta_{2},\,

\displaystyle 2\,\beta+b\,\delta_{2}+B\left\|\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\Omega)}^{2}+{2}+2\,\delta_{v}+B\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega\right)}\Big\}.

An application of Gronwall’s inequality yields the following bound
for the
\mathbf{L^{2}}
-norm:

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(t)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{v}(t)-\tilde{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{\left\|\bar{u}(s)-\tilde{u}(s)\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}}\exp\left({\int_{s}^{t}C(\tau)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{\tau}}}\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}.

Moreover, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\|\bar{\rho}_{1}(\tau)-\tilde{\rho}_{1}(\tau)\right\|_{\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\rho}_{2}(\tau)-\tilde{\rho}_{2}(\tau)\right\|_{\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{v}(\tau)-\tilde{v}(\tau)\right\|_{\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}C(\tau)\exp\left({\int_{\tau}^{s}C(s)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}}\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{\tau}}\right)\int_{0}^{t}{\left\|\bar{u}(\tau)-\tilde{u}(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}C(\tau)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{\tau}}\right)\int_{0}^{t}{\left\|\bar{u}(\tau)-\tilde{u}(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{\tau}}.

Note that, using the estimates (
10
),
(
11
),
and (
12
) of
Theorem

3.2
,
the function
C(t)
can be controlled by a constant depending on

T
, on the initial conditions, on
\left|\Omega\right|
, and on the
various constant appearing on system (
1
).
Therefore the estimates (
16
)
and (
17
) hold and the proof is finished.

5
Optimal control problem

In this section we consider optimal control problems for the
system (
1
)-(
2
)-(
3
),
obtained through the minimization of a functional, which explicitly
depends on the control
u
and consequently on the solution
to (
1
). To this aim, define the (continuous)
functions

\psi_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\psi_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R},

and the functional

J:\mathbf{L^{2}}\left([0,T]\times\Omega;[0,U]\right)\to\mathbb{R}
as

\begin{split}J(u)&=\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}\left(\rho_{1}(T,x),\rho_{2}(T,x),v(T,x)\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2}\left(\rho_{1}(t,x),\rho_{2}(t,x),v(t,x),{u(t,x)}\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}},\end{split}

(23)

which we aim to minimize.

Remark 2

The general form of the functional (
23
) describes in a
unified way several possible objective functionals, which are
natural in application related to cancer therapies against glioma.

For example, if the main objective is the maximal reduction of the
volume of the cancer at a time
T
, one can consider the functional

J(u)=\gamma_{1}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{1}(T,x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}+\gamma_{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{2}(T,x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}},

for suitable
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\geq 0
. This is a special version
of (
23
), obtained with the choice

\psi_{1}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v)=\gamma_{1}\rho_{1}+\gamma_{2}\rho_{2}
and

\psi_{2}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v,u)=0
.

Another similar example is derived when the objective is the minimal
cancer size at a time
T
, obtained with the least dose of
treatment, due to its side effects. In this case a meaningful
functional is

J(u)=\gamma_{1}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{1}(T,x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}+\gamma_{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{2}(T,x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}+{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}(t,x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}},

where
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
. This is a special
version of (
23
), obtained with the choice

\psi_{1}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v)=\gamma_{1}\rho_{1}+\gamma_{2}\rho_{2}
and

\psi_{2}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v,u)=u^{p}
.

A third example is related to a possible way to make the glioma a
chronic disease. In this case, if

\bar{\rho}\in\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)
denotes the distribution of
the cancer in a chronic situation, then one aims to minimize

\begin{split}J(u)&=\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho_{1}(T,x)-\bar{\rho}(x)\right)^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho_{1}(t,x)-\bar{\rho}(x)\right)^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}\\
&\quad+{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}(t,x)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}}.\end{split}

This is (
23
) with

\psi_{1}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v)=(\rho_{1}-\bar{\rho})^{2}
and

\psi_{2}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v,u)=(\rho_{1}-\bar{\rho})^{2}+{u^{p}}
.

Existence of optimal controls is guaranteed by the next result, whose
proof is based on the direct method of the calculus of variation; see
for example
[
9
,
13
]
and the references therein.

Theorem 5.1

Let
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
,
\delta_{v}
,
b
,

B
, and
U
be fixed positive constants. Assume that

\psi_{1}
is continuous, convex and
\psi_{1}(z)\geq 0
for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Suppose moreover that

\psi_{2}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v,u)=\psi_{2,1}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v)+\psi_{2,2}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v)u^{p},

where
p\geq 1
and
\psi_{2,1},\psi_{2,2}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous and positive functions. Let
\mathcal{U}\neq\emptyset
be a closed (with respect to the strong topology) and
convex subset of
\mathbf{L^{2}}\left((0,T)\times\Omega;[0,U]\right)
.

Then there exists
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
such that

J\left(\bar{u}\right)=\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}J(u).

(24)

Proof.

Consider a minimizing sequence
u_{n}
for the functional
J
, i.e. a
sequence
u_{n}\in\mathcal{U}
such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}J\left(u_{n}\right)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}J(u).

This is possible, since
J(u)\geq 0
for every
u\in\mathcal{U}
by
assumptions on
\psi_{1}
,
\psi_{2,1}
,
\psi_{2,2}
, and since

J(0)<+\infty
, which is a consequence of the estimates
in
Theorem

3.2
. We clearly have that

\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left((0,T)\times\Omega\right)}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{n}(t,x)\right|^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}\leq U^{2}{\left|\Omega\right|}T,

where
{\left|\Omega\right|}
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
,
so that there exists
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
(here
\mathcal{U}

is also closed in the
weak topology, since it is convex) and a subsequence
u_{n_{h}}
such
that
u_{n_{h}}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}
in

\mathbf{L^{2}}\left((0,T)\times\Omega;[0,U]\right)
. Without loss of
generality, we assume that the whole sequence
u_{n}
weakly converges
in
\mathbf{L^{2}}\left((0,T)\times\Omega;[0,U]\right)
to

\bar{u}
. Note, moreover, that there is weak convergence to the
same
\bar{u}
in every

\mathbf{L^{p}}((0,T)\times\Omega;[0,U])
,
p\geq 1
.

For every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, denote with

\left(\rho_{1}^{n},\rho_{2}^{n},v^{n}\right)
the solution
to (
1
) corresponding to the control
u_{n}
, in
the sense of
Definition

2.1
, which exists by
Theorem

3.2
. Moreover define

\left(\bar{\rho}_{1},\bar{\rho}_{2},\bar{v}\right)
the solution
to (
1
) corresponding to the control
\bar{u}
.

Define, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
S_{n}=\rho_{1}^{n}v^{n}
.
By (
10
)
and (
12
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|S_{n}(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\rho_{1,o}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}}\left\|v_{o}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}}e^{\left|\alpha-\delta_{1}\right|T}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\rho_{1,o}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}}\left(b\delta_{2}\left(\left\|\rho_{1,o}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}}+\left\|\rho_{2,o}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}}\right)e^{\alpha T}+U\right)T

for a.e.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
. Therefore, without loss
of generality, there exists

\bar{S}\in\mathbf{L^{2}}\left((0,T)\times\Omega\right)
so that
S_{n}

weakly converges to
\bar{S}
in

\mathbf{L^{2}}\left((0,T)\times\Omega\right)
.

Note that, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the triple

\left(\rho_{1}^{n},\rho_{2}^{n},v^{n}\right)
is a solution to the linear
system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\,\rho_{1}=\Delta\rho_{1}+\left(\alpha-\delta_{1}\right)\rho_{1}-\beta S_{n}\\
\partial_{t}\,\rho_{2}=\Delta\rho_{2}+\beta S_{n}-\delta_{2}\rho_{2}\\
\partial_{t}\,v=\Delta v+b\delta_{2}\rho_{2}-BS_{n}-\delta_{v}v+u_{n}\end{array}\right.

(25)

in the sense of
Definition

2.1
.
We apply
Corollary

A.1
to each linear equation
of (
25
). Since the operator defined
in
Corollary

A.1
is also continuous with respect to
the weak topology of both domain and codomain,
see
[
3
, Theorem 3.10]
, then there exist

\tilde{\rho}_{1}
,
\tilde{\rho}_{2}
, and
\tilde{v}
in

\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(0,T;\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right),\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)^{*}\right)
such that

\rho_{1}^{n}\rightharpoonup\tilde{\rho}_{1},\qquad\rho_{2}^{n}\rightharpoonup\tilde{\rho}_{2},\quad\textrm{and}\quad v^{n}\rightharpoonup\tilde{v}

weakly in

\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(0,T;\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right),\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)^{*}\right)
and the triple

\left(\tilde{\rho}_{1},\tilde{\rho}_{2},\tilde{v}\right)
satisfies the
linear system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\,\tilde{\rho}_{1}=\Delta\tilde{\rho}_{1}+\left(\alpha-\delta_{1}\right)\tilde{\rho}_{1}-\beta\bar{S}\\
\partial_{t}\,\tilde{\rho}_{2}=\Delta\tilde{\rho}_{2}+\beta\bar{S}-\delta_{2}\tilde{\rho}_{2}\\
\partial_{t}\,\tilde{v}=\Delta\tilde{v}+b\delta_{2}\tilde{\rho}_{2}-B\bar{S}-\delta_{v}\tilde{v}+\bar{u}.\end{array}\right.

(26)

Moreover, since

\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(0,T;\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right),\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)^{*}\right)
is continuously embedded in the space

{\mathbf{C^{0}}}\left([0,T];\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)\right)

(see
[
25
, Theorem 7.104]
), we also deduce that

\rho_{1}^{n}(T)\rightharpoonup\tilde{\rho}_{1}(T),\qquad\rho_{2}^{n}(T)\rightharpoonup\tilde{\rho}_{2}(T),\quad\textrm{and}\quad v^{n}(T)\rightharpoonup\tilde{v}(T)

(27)

weakly in
\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)
. This is a consequence that the
dual of
{\mathbf{C^{0}}}\left([0,T];\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)\right)
can be
identified with integral operators from
{\mathbf{C^{0}}}\left([0,T];\mathbb{R}\right)

to the dual space of
\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)
(see
[
24
, sections
3.2 and 3.5]
), which can be described through vector
measures on
[0,T]
over the dual of
\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)
;
see
[
24
, Proposition 5.28]
.

By
[
15
, Theorem 10.1]
, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
solution
\left(\rho_{1}^{n},\rho_{2}^{n},v^{n}\right)
is Hölder continuous
in each subset compactly embedded in

\left(0,T\right)\times\Omega
, with exponent not depending on

n
. Therefore, by Ascoli-Arzelà Theorem, there exist continuous
functions
\hat{\rho}_{1}
,
\hat{\rho}_{2}
, and
\hat{v}
such that,
possibly extracting a subsequence,

\rho_{1}^{n}\to\hat{\rho}_{1},\qquad\rho_{2}^{n}\to\hat{\rho}_{2},\qquad v^{n}\to\hat{v},

as
n\to+\infty
, and the convergence is uniform. Hence

\tilde{\rho}_{1}=\hat{\rho}_{1}
,
\tilde{\rho}_{2}=\hat{\rho}_{2}
,

\tilde{v}=\hat{v}
and so

S_{n}=\rho_{1}^{n}v^{n}\longrightarrow\hat{\rho}_{1}\hat{v}

uniformly and in
\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\left(0,T\right)\times\Omega\right)
.

Therefore, the triple

\left(\hat{\rho}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{2},\hat{v}\right)

solves (
26
) with

\bar{S}=\hat{\rho}_{1}\hat{v}
and control
\bar{u}
. Since
by
Theorem

3.2
the solution to such problem is
unique, it holds

\left(\hat{\rho}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{2},\hat{v}\right)=\left(\bar{\rho}_{1},\bar{\rho}_{2},\bar{v}\right)
.

We now show that the control
\bar{u}
is indeed optimal. Consider
the three terms defining the functional
J
separately. The
functional

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)\times\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)\times\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
\left(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},v\right)&\longmapsto&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}\left(\rho_{1}(x),\rho_{2}(x),v(x)\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\end{array}

is sequential lower semicontinuous with respect to the strong
topology. Since
\psi_{1}
is a convex function, then it is also
sequential lower semicontinuous with respect to the weak topology.
Therefore, since (
27
), we deduce that

\begin{split}&\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}\left(\rho_{1}^{n}(T,x),\rho_{2}^{n}(T,x),v^{n}(T,x)\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\\
\geq&\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}\left(\bar{\rho}_{1}(T,x),\bar{\rho}_{2}(T,x),\bar{v}(T,x)\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}.\end{split}

Moreover, since
\left(\rho_{1}^{n},\rho_{2}^{n},v^{n}\right)
converges to

\left(\bar{\rho}_{1},\bar{\rho}_{2},\bar{v}\right)
for a.e.

(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega
, the
estimates (
10
)–(
12
)
hold, and
\psi_{2,1}
and
\psi_{2,2}
are continuous functions,
then the Dominated Convergence Theorem implies that

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2,1}\left(\rho_{1}^{n}(t,x),\rho_{2}^{n}(t,x),v^{n}(t,x)\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\,\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2,1}\left(\bar{\rho}_{1}(t,x),\bar{\rho}_{2}(t,x),\bar{v}(t,x)\right)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\,\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}\end{split}

and also that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\psi_{2,2}\left(\rho_{1}^{n},\rho_{2}^{n},v^{n}\right)=\psi_{2,2}\left(\bar{\rho}_{1},\bar{\rho}_{2},\bar{v}\right)

(28)

in
\mathbf{L^{p}}\left(\Omega\right)
for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
.

Finally, by (
28
) and the fact that

\left|u_{n}^{p}(t,x)\right|\leq U
for all

(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\quad\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2,2}\left(\rho_{1}^{n}(t,x),\rho_{2}^{n}(t,x),v^{n}(t,x)\right){u^{p}_{n}(t,x)}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\,\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2,2}\left(\bar{\rho}_{1}(t,x),\bar{\rho}_{2}(t,x),\bar{v}(t,x)\right){u^{p}_{n}(t,x)}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\,\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}

\displaystyle\quad+\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\big[\psi_{2,2}\left(\rho_{1}^{n}(t,x),\rho_{2}^{n}(t,x),v^{n}(t,x)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\psi_{2,2}\left(\bar{\rho}_{1}(t,x),\bar{\rho}_{2}(t,x),\bar{v}(t,x)\right)\big]{u^{p}_{n}(t,x)}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\,\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2,2}\left(\bar{\rho}_{1}(t,x),\bar{\rho}_{2}(t,x),\bar{v}(t,x)\right){u^{p}_{n}(t,x)}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\,\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}.

Since the functional

\begin{array}[]{rcc}{\mathbf{L^{p}}\left([0,T]\times\Omega;[0,U]\right)}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
u&\longmapsto&\displaystyle\!\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2,2}\left(\bar{\rho}_{1}(t,x),\bar{\rho}_{2}(t,x),\bar{v}(t,x)\right){u^{p}(t,x)}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\,\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}\end{array}

is convex and sequentially lower semicontinuous with respect to the
strong topology, then it is also sequentially lower semicontinuous
with respect to the weak topology and so

\displaystyle\quad\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2,2}\left(\rho_{1}^{n}(t,x),\rho_{2}^{n}(t,x),v^{n}(t,x)\right){u^{p}_{n}(t,x)}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\,\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2,2}\left(\bar{\rho}_{1}(t,x),\bar{\rho}_{2}(t,x),\bar{v}(t,x)\right){\bar{u}^{p}(t,x)}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\,\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}.

This permits to conclude that

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}J(u_{n})\geq J(\bar{u}),

proving that
\bar{u}
is an optimal control,
i.e. (
24
) holds. This concludes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Remark 3

5.1
still holds under the following more

6.2
Adjoint system and necessary conditions

Let us now consider the functional

We introduce the following assumptions on the cost functions

\langle\dot{u}_{m}(t),v\rangle_{*}+B\left(u_{m}(t),v;t\right)=\langle f(t),v\rangle_{*},

(49)

where the bilinear term
B
is defined in (
47
). Note
that for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,
u_{m}(t)
converges to
u(t)
in

\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)
as
m\to+\infty
and

\dot{u}_{m}(t)\in\mathbf{H^{1}}\left(\Omega\right)

for every
m\geq 1
. Thus, substituting
v=\dot{u}_{m}(t)

in (
49
) and using (
47
) and the
hypothesis that

f\in\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(0,T;\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)\right)
, we get that

\begin{split}&\quad\left\|\dot{u}_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{m}(t)\cdot\nabla\dot{u}_{m}(t)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}}_{I_{1}}\\
&=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}f(t)\dot{u}_{m}(t)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}}_{I_{2}}-\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}c(t,x)\,u_{m}(t)\,\dot{u}_{m}(t)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}}_{I_{3}}.\end{split}

(50)

Note that

I_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phantom{i}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{\phantom{i}}}}{\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}}\left\|\nabla u_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}.

(51)

Moreover, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq\int_{\Omega}\left|f(t)\right|\left|\dot{u}_{m}(t)\right|\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\leq\left\|f(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}\left\|\dot{u}_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}\\
&\leq 2\left\|f(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\left\|\dot{u}_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}.\end{split}

(52)

Finally, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\begin{split}\left|-I_{3}\right|&\leq\int_{\Omega}\left|c(t,x)\right|\left|u_{m}(t)\right|\left|\dot{u}_{m}(t)\right|\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{x}}\\
&\leq\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left\|u_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}\left\|\dot{u}_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}\\
&\leq 2\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}^{2}\left\|u_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\left\|\dot{u}_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2},\end{split}

(53)

provided
\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}>0
.
Inserting (
51
), (
52
),
and (
53
) into (
50
) we
deduce that, for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\left\|\dot{u}_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phantom{i}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{\phantom{i}}}}{\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{t}}}\left\|\nabla u_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\|f(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+2\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}^{2}\left\|u_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}

and so, integrating in time, since

{\left\|\nabla u_{m}(0)\right\|}^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq{\left\|g\right\|}^{2}_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}
,

\begin{split}&\quad\frac{3}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\dot{u}_{m}(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u_{m}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u_{m}(0)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\!+\!2\int_{0}^{t}\!\left\|f(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}+2\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{m}(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla g\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}+2\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{m}(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}.\end{split}

Passing to the limit as
m\to+\infty
and
using (
48
), we have

\begin{split}&\quad\frac{3}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\dot{u}(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla g\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}+2\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla g\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}\\
&\quad+2\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}^{2}e^{2\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}t}\left\|g\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}t\\
&\quad+2\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}^{2}e^{2\left\|c\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}t}t\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}{s}}.\end{split}

Since, by (
48
),

u\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(0,T;\mathbf{L^{2}}(\Omega))
, the previous inequality proves
that
u\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(0,T;\mathbf{H^{1}}(\Omega))
and

\dot{u}\in\mathbf{L^{2}}(0,T;\mathbf{L^{2}}(\Omega))
. If the boundary

\partial\Omega
of
\Omega
is of class
{\mathbf{C^{2}}}
, then

u(t)\in\mathbf{H^{2}}\left(\Omega\right)
for a.e.
t\in[0,T]

(see
[
25
, Theorem 8.28]
), proving that

u\in\mathbf{L^{2}}(0,T;\mathbf{H^{2}}(\Omega))
.

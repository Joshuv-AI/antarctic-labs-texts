A High-order Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian Virtual Element Method for Convection-Diffusion Problems
H. Wells

Abstract
A virtual element discretisation of an Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian method for two-dimensional convection-diffusion equations is proposed employing an isoparametric Virtual Element Method to achieve higher-order convergence rates on curved edged polygonal meshes. The proposed method is validated with numerical experiments in which optimal H 1 H^{1} and L 2 L^{2} convergence are observed. This method is then successfully applied to an existing moving mesh algorithm for implicit moving boundary problems in which higher-order convergence is achieved.

Introduction

In Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) schemes are pivotal for accurately simulating fluid flows, especially in scenarios involving large deformations or moving boundaries
[
38
,
30
,
31
]
. By combining aspects of both Lagrangian and Eulerian methods, ALE schemes offer improved mesh adaptability and computational efficiency. Their versatility makes them suitable for a variety of CFD applications, from aerodynamics to fluid-structure interactions
[
51
,
49
,
60
]
.

Common challenges in ALE schemes, such as mesh tangling and distortion during substantial deformations, sustaining mesh quality over time, and ensuring numerical stability and accuracy, are well-documented
[
31
]
. Effectively addressing these concerns is vital for the successful application of ALE schemes in intricate fluid simulations. The adoption of polygonal discretization techniques facilitates the use of more distorted and irregular elements while preserving numerical stability. Furthermore, polygonal discretization allows for efficient resolution of complex geometries with fewer computational elements, achieving comparable numerical precision to traditional triangular and quadrilateral meshes, for instance, through discontinuous Galerkin methods
[
21
]
.
Recent literature features several noteworthy implementations of polygonal discretizations in ALE schemes
[
41
,
42
,
45
,
36
,
35
,
59
]
. These studies highlight the feasibility of achieving numerically stable solutions in ALE scenarios using various complex polygonal and polyhedral meshes. Additionally, some of these studies indicate the robustness of polygonal discretizations against time-dependent topological changes within ALE frameworks, even when faced with degenerate element faces
[
36
,
45
,
35
,
59
]
.

The Virtual Element Method (VEM) is a relatively new approach to discretising partial differential equations (PDEs) on polygonal and polytopic meshes
[
13
,
2
,
14
]
. Extending the concepts of the Finite Element Method (FEM), the VEM expands the capabilities of the FEM by accommodating general polygonal or polyhedral elements. This adaptability makes it exceptionally suitable for scenarios involving complex geometries or non-standard mesh configurations. Initial applications of VEM have successfully addressed various types of PDEs, including elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic equations
[
15
,
22
,
54
,
53
]
.
Moreover, the VEM has seen significant advancements in CFD, with applications ranging from Darcy flow to Navier-Stokes equations
[
5
,
24
,
25
,
43
,
57
,
40
,
1
,

,
6
,

]
.

The use of the VEM for ALE schemes was first proposed in
[
41
]
in which the VEM, specifically the polynomial projection operators, were employed to derive a conservative ALE scheme for the transformation of discrete data between two meshes. A velocity-based moving mesh algorithm was recently extended to the lowest order VEM for non-linear diffusion problems, attaining the same orders of convergence as the original finite element method
[
59
]
.

Motivated by these results, this paper presents an Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian Virtual Element Method (ALE-VEM) scheme for convection-diffusion equations. To extend the accuracy of this scheme beyond second-order, the isoparametric VEM of
[
20
]
is directly applied in the formulation. The proposed method is shown numerically to achieve high orders of accuracy in the

and

norms using the DUNE-VEM module
[

which is part of the Distributed and Unified Numerics Environment
[

,

.

The formulation is restricted to problems in which the ALE mapping is prescribed

or can be solved for independently of the numerical solution. To demonstrate the extensibility of this method, this paper concludes with an extension of the velocity-based moving mesh VEM of
[

to higher-order discretisations using the ALE-VEM framework. For this problem, the moving boundary is not prescribed

and must be approximated at each discrete time level. Numerical results demonstrate that higher orders of convergence are achieved.

2
The Convection-diffusion Problem

2.1
The ALE Mapping

We consider a bounded time-dependent domain
\Omega_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
where
t\in[0,T]
with a finite final time
T>0
.
The time-dependent ALE mapping is defined by
\mathscr{A}_{t}:\hat{\Omega}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\Omega_{t}
,
and we assume for all time
t\in[0,T]
that
\mathscr{A}_{t}
is bi-Lipschitz, in line with assumptions made within ALE analysis
[
17
,
16
,
37
,
33
]
and that
\mathscr{A}_{t}\in\left[W^{k+1}_{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})\right]^{2}
where
k\in\mathbb{N}
denotes the degree of VEM being used.
The Jacobian of the ALE mapping is given by

\mathbf{J}_{t}:=\frac{\partial\mathscr{A}_{t}}{\partial\bm{\xi}}=\hat{\nabla}\mathscr{A}_{t},

The determinant of
\mathbf{J}_{t}
is uniformly bounded and strictly positive
[
17
,
16
]
; namely, there exists a
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\alpha\leq j_{t}:=\text{det}\left(\mathbf{J}_{t}\right)\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

2.2
The Convection-diffusion Equation

The linear convection-diffusion problem is defined as: for
t\in(0,T]
, find
\rho(\mathbf{x},t)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}-\mu\Delta\rho+\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{b}\rho)

\displaystyle=f\qquad

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\Omega_{t},\ t\in(0,T],

(1)

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\rho_{0}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\Omega_{0},\ t=0,

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega_{t},\ t\in[0,T],

where
\mathbf{b}
is a convective velocity field and
\mu\geq 0
is a constant diffusivity parameter.

2.3
Weak Formulation

We define the space of functions which remain constant along domain trajectories by

\mathcal{X}(\Omega_{t})=\left\{v:\ \Omega_{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\ :\ v=\hat{v}\circ\mathscr{A}^{-1}_{t},\hat{v}\in H_{0}^{1}(\hat{\Omega})\right\}.

(2)

Any function
v\in\mathcal{X}(\Omega_{t})
has a vanishing material derivative with respect to the ALE velocity field
[
31
]

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla v=0.

The Reynolds Transport Theorem
[
60
,
31
]
states for
\rho
and a given test function
v\in\mathcal{X}(\Omega_{t})
that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho v\ d\mathbf{x}=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{\partial\rho v}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho v\mathbf{w})\ d\mathbf{x}.

Application of the product rule and noting that the material derivative of
v
vanishes provides

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho v\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}}v\left\{\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{w})\right\}\ +\rho\left\{\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}\right\}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}}v\left\{\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{w})\right\}\ d\mathbf{x}.

Substitution of the PDE (
1
) and integration by parts leads to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho v\ d\mathbf{x}=\int_{\Omega_{t}}fv-\mu\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla v-(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{b})\cdot\rho\nabla v\ d\mathbf{x}.

The above equation can be written as follows

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{t}(\rho,v)+\mu A_{t}(\rho,v)+B_{t}(\rho,v;\mathbf{w})=l_{t}(v),

(3)

where,

\displaystyle M_{t}(\rho,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho v\ d\mathbf{x},

(4)

\displaystyle A_{t}(\rho,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla v\ d\mathbf{x},

(5)

\displaystyle B_{t}(\rho,v;\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{b})\cdot\rho\nabla v\ d\mathbf{x},

(6)

\displaystyle l_{t}(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}}fv\ d\mathbf{x}.

(7)

The stability of this formulation is well known under the assumption that
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})
and
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega_{t})
for all
t\in[0,T]

[
33
]
.
The stability of the continuous formulation is independent on the choice of ALE mapping. Stability estimates have been presented for a FEM discretisation of the ALE method in which the stability is dependent on the choice of ALE mapping
[
33
,
34
,
17
]
. In this paper we do not present any analysis on the ALE-VEM scheme, instead we assume that the choices of
\mathbf{b}
and
f
satisfy the aforementioned regularity conditions and that this is sufficient to propose a numerically stable method.

The continuous formulation given by Equation (
3
) is independent of the choice of a conservative or non-conservative ALE approach in the continuous framework
[
33
]
. As with FEMs, this is not the case for VEM discretisation of Equation (
3
) and the VEM formulation in Section
4
is only applicable to a conservative ALE formulation of the convection-diffusion problem.

The Virtual Element Method

In this section, we outline the fundamental components of the VEM. The computational implementation of the VEM is well-documented and we omit the details in this paper

,

3.1
A Computational Reference Mesh

We construct the VEM on a polygonal discretisation of
\hat{\Omega}
using the standard
enhanced
VEM spaces
[
2
,
14
,
22
]
. The polygonal mesh
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
of
\hat{\Omega}
is a collection of simple, non-overlapping polygonal reference elements
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.

The properties of a given
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
are defined as the diameter
h_{\hat{E}}
, barycentric coordinate
\mathbf{x}_{c}^{\hat{E}}=(x_{c}^{\hat{E}},y_{c}^{\hat{E}})
and area
\left|\hat{E}\right|
, boundary edges of
\hat{E}
are denoted by
\hat{e}\subset\partial\hat{E}
. The global mesh size is defined by

h:=\max_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{\hat{E}}.

To ensure that optimal approximation results can be obtained, the following mesh regularity assumption is required
[
13
]
.

Assumption 1

(Mesh Regularity)
.

Every
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is a star-shaped domain or a finite union of star shaped domains with respect to a ball of radius greater than
\gamma h_{\hat{E}}
for some uniform
\gamma>0
. Additionally, for all edges
\hat{e}\in\partial\hat{E}
, the length of
\hat{e}
is greater than
\delta h_{\hat{E}}
for some uniform
\delta>0
.

3.2
Polynomial Projection Operators

The accuracy of the VEM is provided by using polynomial projection operators defined below
[
13
,
2
,
15
]
.
For each of these projection operators there exists stability and accuracy results under the condition that Assumption
1
is satisfied
[
18
,
29
]
.

Definition 3.1

(The
\Pi^{\nabla}
Operator)
.

The operator
\Pi^{\nabla}_{k}:H^{1}(\omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}(\omega)
is defined for all
v\in H^{1}(\omega)
by

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\nabla\Pi^{\nabla}_{k}v\cdot\nabla p\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla p\ d\mathbf{x}\qquad\forall p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\omega)

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}v-\Pi^{\nabla}_{k}v\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=0.

Definition 3.2

(The
\Pi^{0}
Operator)
.

The operator
\Pi^{0}_{k}:L^{2}(\omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}(\omega)
is defined for all
v\in L^{2}(\omega)
by

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\Pi^{0}_{k}v\ p\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}v\ p\ d\mathbf{x}\qquad\forall p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\omega).

Definition 3.3

(The
\Pi^{1}
Operator)
.

The operator
\Pi^{1}_{k}:H^{1}(\omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}(\omega)
is defined for all
v\in H^{1}(\omega)
by
\Pi^{1}_{k}v:=\Pi^{0}_{k}\nabla v
or equivalently

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\Pi^{1}_{k}v\cdot\mathbf{p}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}\nabla v\cdot\mathbf{p}\ d\mathbf{x}\qquad\forall\mathbf{p}\in\left[\mathbb{P}_{k}(\omega)\right]^{2}.

3.3
Local & Global VEM spaces

The original VEM space is built on a given polygon
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
by considering a local boundary value problem subject to piecewise polynomial boundary conditions
[
13
]
. The boundary space is defined as

\mathbb{B}_{k}(\partial\hat{E})=\{\hat{v}_{h}\in C^{0}(\partial\hat{E})\ :\ \hat{v}_{h}|_{e}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\hat{e})\ \ \ \forall\hat{e}\subset\partial\hat{E}\},

from which the original local VEM space of degree
k
can then be defined as

W_{k}(\hat{E})=\{\hat{v}_{h}\in H^{1}(\hat{E})\ :\ \hat{v}_{h}|_{\partial\hat{E}}\in\mathbb{B}_{k}(\partial\hat{E}),\ \Delta\hat{v}_{h}|_{E}\in\mathbb{P}_{k-2}(\hat{E})\}.

In order to develop the VEM for problems beyond Poisson’s Equation, the local VEM space had to be modified such that the full
L^{2}
projection was computable. A solution was presented in
[
2
]
and is commonly used as the default VEM space in most recent discretisations.

The approach to enhance the VEM space is to use the gradient projection operator
\Pi^{\nabla}
to supplement the remaining internal moments required to compute the
L^{2}
projection. The original local VEM space is enlarged via,

\tilde{W}_{k}(\hat{E})=\{\hat{v}_{h}\in H^{1}(\hat{E})\ :\ \hat{v}_{h}|_{\partial\hat{E}}\in\mathbb{B}_{k}(\partial\hat{E}),\ \Delta\hat{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\hat{E})\},

and then restricted to define the enhanced VEM space,

V_{k}(\hat{E})=\left\{\hat{v}_{h}\in\tilde{W}_{k}(\hat{E})\ :\ \int_{\hat{E}}(\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{\nabla}_{k}\hat{v}_{h})\hat{q}\ d\mathbf{x}=0\ \ \ \forall\hat{q}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\hat{E})\backslash\mathbb{P}_{k-2}(\hat{E})\right\}.

(8)

The global VEM space of degree
k
is defined as

\hat{V}_{h}=\left\{\hat{v}_{h}\in H^{1}(\hat{\Omega})\>\ \hat{v}_{h}|_{\hat{E}}\in V_{k}(\hat{E})\ \forall\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\right\}.

(9)

Using the VEM to solve PDEs with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions requires a restricted global VEM space with zero trace. This is denoted as
\hat{V}_{h,0}=\hat{V}_{h}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\hat{\Omega})
.

3.4
Degrees of Freedom

The construction of a VEM relies on choosing a set of degrees of freedom (DoFs). These DoFs serve two purposes in the method: they provide the necessary information to construct computable polynomial approximation operators, and they uniquely identify a virtual element function on any given polygon, which would otherwise be an unknown solution to a local boundary value problem.

The space of scaled monomials of degree
k
on a given element
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{k}(\hat{E})=\left\{\left(\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{\hat{E}}}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{\hat{E}}}\right)^{\beta}\ :\ \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}{\geq 0},\ \alpha+\beta=k\right\},

from which the VEM DoFs are defined as follows.

Definition 3.4

(Degrees of Freedom for the Virtual Element Method)
.

Let
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
V_{k}(\hat{E})
be the local VEM space defined in Equation (
8
). The degrees of freedom of a given function
\hat{v}_{h}\in V_{k}(\hat{E})
are defined as follows:

1.

The point values of
\hat{v}_{h}
at each vertex of
\hat{E}
.

2.

The point values of
\hat{v}_{h}
at the Gauss-Lobatto quadrature points of order
k-1
on each edge
\hat{e}\subset\partial\hat{E}
.

3.

The internal moments of
\frac{1}{|\hat{E}|}\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{v}_{h}\hat{q}\ dx
for all
\hat{q}\in\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(\hat{E})
.

A proof that these constitute a unisolvent set of DoFs is provided in
[
13
]
. The projections
\Pi^{\nabla}_{k}
and
\Pi^{0}_{k}
are computable using only these degrees of freedom
[
2
,
14
]
.

3.5
Representation of VEM Functions

For a given VEM function
\hat{v}_{h}\in\hat{V}_{h}
, the DoFs are denoted by
\text{dof}_{i}(\hat{v}_{h})
for
i=1,\ldots,N^{\text{dofs}}
, with
N^{\text{dofs}}
being the number of DoFs on
\hat{V}_{h}
. The Lagrangian VEM basis function is introduced as

\displaystyle\left\{\hat{\varphi}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{N^{\text{dofs}}}\subset\hat{V}_{h},\qquad\text{dof}_{i}(\hat{\varphi}_{j})=\delta_{i,j}\text{ for }i,j=1,\ldots,N^{\text{dofs}}.

(10)

VEM functions and interpolants can be defined respectively using the DoFs and basis functions as

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\text{dofs}}}\text{dof}_{i}(\hat{v}_{h})\hat{\varphi}_{i},\ \ \ \hat{v}_{I}=\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\text{dofs}}}\text{dof}_{i}(\hat{v})\hat{\varphi}_{i}\qquad\forall\hat{v}_{h}\in\hat{V}_{h},~\hat{v}\in L^{1}(\hat{\Omega})\cap C^{0}(\hat{\Omega}).

(11)

3.6
Approximation of Linear and Bilinear Forms

Let
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot):H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\times H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
l(\cdot):H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a symmetric bilinear and linear form respectively. Consider the general variational problem: find
\hat{\rho}\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})
such that
\mathcal{A}(\hat{\rho},\hat{v})=l(\hat{v})
for all
\hat{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})
.
The novelty of the VEM is the introduction of discrete approximations
\mathcal{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
l_{h}(\cdot)
that use the DoFs and the projection operators to approximate the integral equations to a sufficient degree of accuracy. These approximations are constructed via local element contributions

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(\hat{\rho}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{\rho}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}),\qquad l_{h}(\hat{v}_{h})=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}l_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{v}_{h})\qquad\forall\hat{v}_{h}\in V_{h,0}.

To enforce the coercivity of the discrete bilinear form, a stabilisation term is introduced
S^{\hat{E}}(\cdot,\cdot)
to ensure that the kernel of
\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\cdot,\cdot)
scales like the kernel of
\mathcal{A}^{\hat{E}}(\cdot,\cdot)

[
13
,
22
]
. We provide further details on these discrete forms in Section
4
.

4
A Conservative ALE-VEM Formulation

4.1
Approximating the ALE Mapping

In this section, we present the conservative ALE discretisation where the ALE mapping and velocity are known
a priori
and interpolated component-wise into the tensor product VEM space
\left[\hat{V}_{h}\right]^{2}
(c.f. Equation (
11
)):

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{h,t}=\left[\mathscr{A}_{I,t}^{x},\ \mathscr{A}_{I,t}^{y}\right],\qquad\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{h,t}=\left[\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{I,t}^{x},\ \hat{\mathbf{w}}_{I,t}^{y}\right],

(12)

where the
x,y
superscripts denote the
x
and
y
components of
\mathscr{A}_{t}
and
\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{t}
respectively.
These functions, being defined on the reference domain, depend spatially on
\bm{\xi}
and have a time-dependent set of degrees of freedom. The formulation of this ALE scheme does not require that
\mathscr{A}_{h,t}
is the VEM interpolant of
\mathscr{A}_{t}
. Alternatively, the
\mathscr{A}_{h,t}
only needs to be a sufficiently accurate approximation of
\mathscr{A}_{t}
at a given time
t\in[0,T]
for the isoparametric VEM
[
20
]
. In particular, the approach of the moving mesh method of Section
6
approximates the ALE mapping at each discrete time-level.

4.2
Moving Virtual Element Spaces

The virtual domain generated by the ALE map
\mathscr{A}_{h,t}
is denoted by
\Omega_{h,t}
and the corresponding virtual mesh of transformed elements is denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{h,t}
.
The discrete counterpart to Equation (
2
) is defined using the discrete ALE mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{h}(\Omega_{h,t})=\left\{v:\Omega_{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\ :\ v=\hat{v}\circ\mathscr{A}_{h,t}^{-1},\ \hat{v}\in\hat{V}_{h}\right\}.

(13)

For the VEM we choose a basis to be a subset of
\mathcal{X}_{h}

\displaystyle\left\{\varphi_{i}(\mathbf{x},t)\ :\ \varphi_{i}(\mathbf{x},t)=\hat{\varphi}_{i}(\bm{\xi})\circ\mathscr{A}_{h,t}^{-1}\ \forall t\in[0,T]\right\}_{i=1}^{N^{\text{dofs}}}\subset\mathcal{X}_{h}(\Omega_{h,t}),

(14)

with
\hat{\varphi}_{i}
being the canonical VEM basis function of
\hat{V}_{h}
defined using Equation (
10
). Using this basis, we define a time-dependent VEM function by

v_{h,t}(\mathbf{x},t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\text{dofs}}}\text{dof}_{i}(v_{h}(\mathbf{x},t))\varphi_{i}(\mathbf{x},t),

(15)

where
\varphi_{i}
is defined by Equation (
14
). We emphasis that the DoFs of the discrete function
v_{h,t}
in Equation (
14
) are time-dependent so in general
v_{h,t}\notin\mathcal{X}_{h}(\Omega_{h,t})
, instead
v_{h,t}
is a time-dependent linear combination of elements of
\mathcal{X}_{h}(\Omega_{h,t})
. The moving VEM space can then be defined, using Equations (
14
) and (
15
), as

V_{h,t}=\left\{v_{h,t}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{h,t})\ :\ v_{h,t}=\hat{v}_{h,t}\circ\mathscr{A}_{h,t}^{-1},\ \hat{v}_{h,t}\in\hat{V}_{h}\right\}.

(16)

To impose the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, the restriction of these VEM spaces to zero boundary conditions are defined by
V_{h,t,0}:=V_{h,t}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h,t})
and
\mathcal{X}_{h,0}:=\mathcal{X}_{h}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h,t})
respectively.

4.3
The Isoparametric VEM

To achieve higher-order accuracy with the VEM solution we require a higher-order representation of the moving domain. An isoparametric VEM allows for the representation of the ALE map using the same space
\hat{V}_{h}
. In this work, we consider the first isoparametric VEM of
[
20
]
which transforms the variational problem from the discrete time-dependent domain, represented by a VEM function, onto the computational reference domain (see also
[
41
,
42
,
7
]
for similar approaches to domain transformations).

Firstly, we consider at a fixed time
t\in[0,T]
approximating the semi-discrete ALE formulation of Equation (
3
) on the VEM approximation of the moving domain
\Omega_{h,t}\approx\Omega_{t}
. An approximation of the Jacobian operator is introduced to approximate a change of variables from
\Omega_{h,t}
to
\hat{\Omega}
. For a given time
t\in[0,T]
and reference element
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we define this operator approximation and it’s corresponding determinant by

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{h,t}=\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\mathscr{A}_{h,t},\qquad j_{h,t}=\text{det}\left(\mathbf{J}_{h,t}\right).

(17)

Using these polynomial, we approximate the transformation of the gradient of a VEM function
v_{h}\in V_{h,t}
from a physical element
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h,t}
to the corresponding reference element
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and the determinant of the Jacobian via

\displaystyle\nabla v_{h}\approx\mathbf{J}_{h,t}^{-\top}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{\nabla}\hat{v}_{h},\qquad\text{det}\left(\mathbf{J}_{t}\right)\approx j_{h,t}.

(18)

Analysis on the accuracy of these approximations is provided in
[
20
]
. In particular, it is shown that for sufficiently small mesh size
h
that
j_{h,t}>0
for all
t\in[0,T]
and we use this assumption in the formulations of this method.

Important to the formulation is the assumption that the PDE data, in this case
\mu
,
\mathbf{b}
and
f
are available and can be computed to a sufficient degree of accuracy on the computational reference domain. In the numerical experiments of Sections
5
and
6
, the PDE data will either be a VEM function or explicitly known on
\hat{\Omega}
.

4.4
A Semi-discretisation

In this VEM the numerical solution of the convection-diffusion equation is given by
\rho_{h,t}\in V_{h,t,0}
. This solution is only implicitly known in the ALE coordinates as the ALE-VEM scheme is computed using only the representation of
\rho_{h,t}
and the test functions
v_{h,t}\in\mathcal{X}_{h}(\Omega_{h,t})
in the reference coordinates.

For ease of reading, we drop the temporal subscript for the solution and test functions in the following formulations, instead the time-dependency will be described by the temporal subscript in the bilinear forms of the method.

The ALE-VEM semi-discrete formulation is given as follows: for a given
t\in(0,T]
find
\rho_{h,t}\in V_{h,t,0}
such that

\frac{d}{dt}M_{h,t}(\rho_{h},v_{h})+\mu A_{h,t}(\rho_{h},v_{h})+B_{h,t}(\rho_{h},v_{h};\mathbf{w}_{h})=l_{h,t}(v_{h})\qquad\forall v_{h}\in\mathcal{X}_{h,0}(\Omega_{h,t}).

The VEM discretisations of Equations (
4
), (
5
), (
6
) and (
7
) are given by element-wise contributions on the reference mesh

\displaystyle M_{h,t}(\rho_{h},v_{h})=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}M_{h,t}^{\hat{E}}(\rho_{h},v_{h}),\qquad A_{h,t}(\rho_{h},v_{h})=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}A_{h,t}^{\hat{E}}(\rho_{h},v_{h}),

\displaystyle B_{h,t}(\rho_{h},v_{h};\mathbf{w}_{h})=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}B_{h,t}^{\hat{E}}(\rho_{h},v_{h};\mathbf{w}_{h}),\qquad l_{h,t}(v_{h})=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}l_{h,t}^{\hat{E}}(v_{h}).

The local element contributions are defined using Equations (
17
) and (
18
) by

\displaystyle M_{h,t}^{\hat{E}}(\rho_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{E}}\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\rho}_{h}\ \Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h}\ j_{h,t}\ d\bm{\xi}+h_{\hat{E}}^{2}S^{\hat{E}}(\hat{\rho}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\rho}_{k},\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{k}),

(19)

\displaystyle A_{h,t}^{\hat{E}}(\rho_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{E}}\mathbf{J}_{h,t}^{-\top}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{\rho}_{h}\ \mathbf{J}_{h,t}^{-\top}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\ j_{h,t}\ d\bm{\xi}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+S^{\hat{E}}(\hat{\rho}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\rho}_{k},\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{k}),

(20)

\displaystyle B_{h,t}^{\hat{E}}(\rho_{h},v_{h};\mathbf{w}_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{E}}(\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{h}-\hat{\mathbf{b}})\cdot\Pi^{0}_{k}\rho_{h}\mathbf{J}_{h,t}^{-\top}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\ j_{h,t}\ d\bm{\xi},

(21)

\displaystyle l_{h,t}^{\hat{E}}(v_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{f}\ \Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h}\ j_{h,t}\ d\bm{\xi},

(22)

where
S^{\hat{E}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the standard dofi-dofi stabilisation term
[
13
,
2
,
14
]

S^{\hat{E}}(\hat{z}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\text{dofs}}}\text{dof}_{i}(\hat{z}_{h})\cdot\text{dof}_{i}(\hat{v}_{h})\qquad\forall\hat{z}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}\in\hat{V}_{h,t}.

(23)

The analysis of
[
20
]
implies that each bilinear form approximation is
O(h^{k})
accurate for a sufficiently small reference mesh size
h
, for any
t\in[0,T]
, provided the reference mesh is shape-regular and the assumptions on the ALE mapping detailed in Section
2
hold.

4.5
A fully Discrete Scheme

A method of lines approach is taken to perform the integration of the weak formulation with respect to time.
We define the fully discrete ALE-VEM scheme using the
\theta
-scheme. For
\theta\in[0,1]
and
0\leq t_{n}<t_{n+1}\leq t_{N^{t}}=T
with
\Delta t_{n+1}=t_{n+1}-t_{n}
, the time derivative of
M_{h,t}(\rho_{h},v_{h})
is approximated via

\displaystyle\frac{M_{h,n+1}(\rho_{h},v_{h})-M_{h,n}(\rho_{h},v_{h})}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\theta l_{h,n+1}(\rho_{h},v_{h})+(1-\theta)l_{h,n}(\rho_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle-\mu\left(\theta A_{h,n+1}(\rho_{h},v_{h})+(1-\theta)A_{h,n}(\rho_{h},v_{h})\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\theta B_{h,n+1}(\rho_{h},v_{h};\mathbf{w}_{h})+(1-\theta)B_{h,n}(\rho_{h},v_{h};\mathbf{w}_{h})\right).

(24)

By rearranging the terms of Equation (
24
), we define the matrices
\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{n+1}
and
\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{G}_{n+1})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=M_{h,n+1}(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j})+\Delta t\theta[A_{h,n+1}(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j})+B_{h,n+1}(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j};\mathbf{w}_{h})]

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{n})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=M_{h,n}(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j})+\Delta t(\theta-1)[A_{h,n}(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j})+B_{h,n}(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j};\mathbf{w}_{h})],

and the vector
\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{i}

\displaystyle=\Delta t\theta l_{h,n+1}(\varphi_{i})+\Delta t(1-\theta)l_{h,n}(\varphi_{i}).

The fully discrete ALE-VEM scheme is the defined as follows: given the DoFs vectors of
\bm{\rho}_{n}
,
\mathbf{w}_{n+1}
and
\mathbf{w}_{n}
, find
\bm{\rho}_{n+1}
such that

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{n+1}\bm{\rho}_{n+1}=\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{n}\bm{\rho}_{n}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{n}.

(25)

5.2
Error Computation

We assess the numerical error in the solution at the final time
t_{N}=T
. The Solution
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
errors are approximated by the discrete norms of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{h,1}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{h,0}
respectively. These are defined as

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{h,N}-\rho_{N}\right\|_{h,1}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{J}_{h,N}^{-\top}\ \Pi^{1}_{k-1}(\hat{\rho}_{h,N}-\hat{\rho}_{N})\right|^{2}\ j_{h,N}\ d\bm{\xi},

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{h,N}-\rho_{N}\right\|_{h,0}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\hat{E}}\left|\ \Pi^{0}_{k}(\hat{\rho}_{h,N}-\hat{\rho}_{N})\right|^{2}\ j_{h,N}\ d\bm{\xi}.

This choice of discrete norm is an approximation of the error on the physical domain. Discrete error norms on the reference domain can also be considered, in line with the error estimate of
[
20
]
. Computing errors on the reference domain produced the same empirical orders of convergence (EOC) presented in this section and are not presented here.

[

[

The time step size

To conclude this paper, we extend the conservative ALE-VEM to a velocity-based moving mesh method
[

,

,

,

]
. The lowest-order VEM was successfully applied and tested to non-linear diffusion problems
[

6.1
The Porous Medium Equation

The PME is a second-order non-linear diffusion problem that admits a family of similarity solutions with compact support. Modelling the support of these solutions as a moving boundary problem makes the PME an ideal test case for a moving mesh method. Extensive examples and theoretical analysis of the PME is provided in
[
55
]
and numerical properties of this solution are discussed in
[
9
,
46
]
. We consider a simple quasi-linear case of the PME, which in relation to Equation (
1
), has a non-linear convection term
\mathbf{b}=-\nabla\rho
and a zero diffusion parameter and forcing term
\mu=f=0
. In addition to the homogeneous boundary condition, we require an additional zero-flux boundary condition across the moving boundary
[
8
]
. The PME is given as: for
t\in(0,T]
, find
\rho(\mathbf{x},t)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\rho)\qquad

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\Omega_{t},t\in(0,T],

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\rho_{0}(\mathbf{x})\qquad

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\Omega_{0},

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega_{t},t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle\rho\nabla\rho\cdot\mathbf{n}_{t}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega_{t},t\in[0,T],

where
\mathbf{n}_{t}
denotes the time-dependent outward unit normal vector of the moving boundary
\partial\Omega_{t}
.

6.2
Solving for the ALE Velocity Field

We derive a weak formulation of the velocity field on the following two assumptions of mass conservation and an irrotational ALE velocity field for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{t}(\rho,v)=0,\ \forall v\in\mathcal{X}(\Omega_{t}),\qquad\exists\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{t})\text{ s.t. }\mathbf{w}=\nabla\phi.

Under these assumptions we can derive a weak formulation at a fixed point in time for the velocity potential
[
9
]
: given
\rho\in H^{1}(\Omega_{t})
, find
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{t})
such that
a(\phi,v)=d(v)
for all
v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{t})
, where

\displaystyle a(\phi,v)=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla v\ d\mathbf{x},\qquad d(v)=-\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla v\ d\mathbf{x}.

The ALE velocity field is then reconstructed by an
L^{2}
projection of the gradient of
\phi
: given
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{t})
, find
\mathbf{w}\in\left[H^{1}(\Omega_{t})\right]^{2}
such that
m(\mathbf{w},v)=b(v)
for all
v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{t})
where

\displaystyle m(\mathbf{w},v)=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\mathbf{w}\ v\ d\mathbf{x},\qquad b(v)=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla\phi\ v\ d\mathbf{x}.

We discretise the potential and velocity reconstruction equations using the isoparametric VEM
[
20
]
.
Given
\rho_{h}\in V_{h,n,0}
at time
t_{n}\in[0,T]
, find
\phi_{h}\in V_{h,n}
such that
a_{h,n}(\phi_{h},v_{h})=d_{h,n}(v_{h})
for all
v_{h}\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}
where

\displaystyle a_{h,n}(\phi_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\hat{E}}\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\rho}_{h}\ \mathbf{J}^{-\top}_{h,n}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{\phi}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{-\top}_{h,n}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\ j_{h,n}\ d\bm{\xi}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\bar{\rho}_{h}S^{\hat{E}}\left(\hat{\phi}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\phi}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h}\right),

\displaystyle d_{h,n}(v_{h})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\hat{E}}\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\rho}_{h}\ \mathbf{J}_{h,n}^{-\top}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{\rho}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{h,n}^{-\top}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\ j_{h,n}\ d\bm{\xi}.

Given
\phi_{h}\in V_{h,n}
at time
t_{n}\in[0,T]
, find
\mathbf{w}_{h}\in\left[V_{h,n}\right]^{2}
such that
m_{h,n}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=b_{h,n}(\mathbf{v}_{h})
for all
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\left[\mathcal{X}_{h,n}\right]^{2}
where

\displaystyle m_{h,n}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\hat{E}}\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\ j_{h,n}\ d\bm{\xi}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+h_{\hat{E}}^{2}S^{\hat{E}}\left(\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{h},\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\right),

\displaystyle b_{h,n}(\mathbf{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\hat{E}}\mathbf{J}_{h,n}^{-\top}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{\phi}_{h}\cdot\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\ j_{h,n}\ d\bm{\xi}.

6.3
The ALE Equation

In the interest of brevity, we only consider in this paper the case where
\mathbf{w}_{h}
approximates only the Lagrangian velocity field of the domain. Examples using alternative ALE velocity fields have been studied in other works
[
44
,
9
,
59
]
. The Forward Euler time-stepping scheme (
\theta=0
) is used for both the moving mesh and the ALE update, in line with existing implementations of this moving mesh method. Consequently, the ALE Equation (
24
) simplifies for the PME to

\displaystyle\frac{M_{h,n+1}(\rho_{h},v_{h})-M_{h,n}(\rho_{h},v_{h})}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=-B_{h,n}(\rho_{h},v_{h};\mathbf{w}_{h}),

(33)

Noting that the definition of
B_{h,n}(\cdot,\cdot;\cdot)
differs slightly from Equation (
21
) due to the non-linear convection term of the PME

B_{h,n}(\rho_{h},v_{h};\mathbf{w}_{h})=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\hat{E}}\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\rho}_{h}\ \mathbf{J}_{h,n}^{-\top}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\cdot\left(\mathbf{J}_{h,n}^{-\top}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{\rho}_{h}+\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\right)\ j_{h,t}\ d\bm{\xi}.

We can couple Equation (
33
) with a Forward Euler time-stepping scheme for computing the ALE map to complete the moving mesh method

\displaystyle M_{h,n+1}(\rho_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=M_{h,n}(\rho_{h},v_{h})-\Delta tB_{h,n}(\rho_{h},v_{h};\mathbf{w}_{h})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}\in V_{h,n,0},

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{h,n+1}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{h,n}+\Delta t\mathbf{w}_{h,n}.

A more comprehensive summary of the structure of this moving mesh method is provided in
[
9
]
.

Conclusion

In this paper, a high-order virtual element discretisation of a conservative Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian scheme is presented for the convection-diffusion equation. An isoparametric Virtual Element Method was used to formulate a semi-discrete ALE scheme for the convection-diffusion equation. Numerical results presented suggest the method attains optimal high-order convergence rates of
O(h^{k})
and
O(h^{k+1})
in the
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms respectively. Extending this method to a velocity-based moving mesh algorithm for implicit moving boundary problems leads to improved orders of convergence, attaining optimal
O(h^{3})
accuracy in the solution error using the quadratic VEM.

Further investigation is required to understand the sub-optimal convergence rates for the implicit moving boundary problems for higher-order VEM discretisations. The development of a three-dimensional ALE-VEM scheme remains an open problem. Advancements in this direction would first require the study of isoparametric VEMs in three dimensions.

A priori
stability and error estimates for this ALE-VEM scheme are currently being developed.
Formulating an ALE-VEM scheme for the Incompressible Navier-Stokes equations will be the subject of future studies.

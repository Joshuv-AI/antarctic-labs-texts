Abstract

1.1.
Quantum groups

Definition 1.1
.

The quantum group
\textbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
associated with
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}

is the associative algebra generated by all
E_{i}
,
F_{i}
,
D_{k}
,
D_{k}^{-1}
with

1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
and
1\leqslant k\leqslant n

over
{\mathbb{Q}}(q)
, which satisfies the following relations

\displaystyle D_{k}D_{k}^{-1}=1=D_{k}^{-1}D_{k};\;D_{k}D_{s}=D_{s}D_{k}\;(1\leq k,s\leq n);

\displaystyle D_{i}E_{i}D_{i}^{-1}=qE_{i};\;D_{i}F_{i}D_{i}^{-1}=q^{-1}F_{i};

\displaystyle D_{i+1}E_{i}D_{i+1}^{-1}=q^{-1}E_{i};\;D_{i+1}F_{i}D_{i+1}^{-1}=qF_{i};

\displaystyle D_{k}E_{i}D_{k}^{-1}=E_{i}\;(k\neq i,i+1);\;D_{k}F_{i}D_{k}^{-1}=F_{i}\;(k\neq i,i+1);

\displaystyle E_{i}F_{j}-F_{i}E_{j}=\delta_{ij}\frac{D_{i}D_{i+1}^{-1}-D_{i}^{-1}D_{i+1}}{q-q^{-1}};

\displaystyle\begin{cases}E_{i}E_{j}=E_{j}E_{i}\cr F_{i}F_{j}=F_{j}F_{i}\end{cases}\text{ if }c_{ij}=0;

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}E_{i}^{2}E_{j}-(q+q^{-1})E_{i}E_{j}E_{i}+E_{j}E_{i}^{2}=0\cr F_{i}^{2}F_{j}-(q+q^{-1})F_{i}F_{j}F_{i}+F_{j}F_{i}^{2}=0\end{cases}\text{ if }c_{ij}=-1,

where

is the Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}{}_{n}
.

In this section, we will introduce duplex Hecke algebras (of type

in
[

Recall that the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{l}
of
l

) has a set of canonical generators
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{l-1}\}
with relations
s_{i}^{2}=\mathsf{id}
for

if

, and

for

.
Moreover, we appoint that
\mathfrak{S}_{1}=\mathfrak{S}_{0}=\{\mathsf{id}\}
, the identity group, for convenience.

2.1.
Basic Definition

Definition 2.1
.

A duplex Hecke algebra
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
is a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra generated
by all
T_{i}
, with
i=0,1,\ldots\ ,m-1
, and all

, with
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{l}
for

,
subject to the follow relations:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle(T_{i}+q)(T_{i}-q^{-1})=0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{for }

\displaystyle 0\leq i\leq m-1;

(2.1)

\displaystyle T_{i}T_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i+1}T_{i}T_{i+1},

\displaystyle\text{for }

\displaystyle 0\leq i\leq m-2;

\displaystyle T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i},

\displaystyle\text{for }

\displaystyle|i-j|>1;

\displaystyle T_{0}T_{1}T_{0}T_{1}=T_{1}T_{0}T_{1}T_{0}.

and

\displaystyle x_{\sigma}^{(l)}x_{s_{i}}^{(l)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x_{\sigma s_{i}}^{(l)},&l(\sigma s_{i})=l(\sigma)+1;\cr x_{\sigma s_{i}}^{(l)}+(q^{-1}-q)x_{\sigma}^{(l)},&l(\sigma s_{i})=l(\sigma)-1;\end{cases}

(2.2)

\displaystyle x_{s_{i}}^{(l)}x_{\sigma}^{(l)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x_{s_{i}\sigma}^{(l)},&l(s_{i}\sigma)=l(\sigma)+1;\\
x_{s_{i}\sigma}^{(l)}+(q^{-1}-q)x_{\sigma}^{(l)},&l(s_{i}\sigma)=l(\sigma)-1;\end{cases}

\displaystyle x_{\sigma}^{(l)}x_{\gamma}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=0,\quad l\neq k,

along with

\displaystyle T_{i}x_{\sigma}^{(l)}

\displaystyle=x_{s_{i}}^{(l)}x_{\sigma}^{(l)},

\displaystyle\;0<i<l;

(2.3)

\displaystyle x_{\sigma}^{(l)}T_{i}

\displaystyle=x_{\sigma}^{(l)}x_{s_{i}}^{(l)},

\displaystyle\;0<i<l;

\displaystyle T_{i}x_{\sigma}^{(l)}

\displaystyle=q^{-1}x_{\sigma}^{(l)}=x_{\sigma}^{(l)}T_{i},

\displaystyle i>l.

.

From the formula (

as an element in
\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{m})
. In particular, all
T_{i}
with
i=0,1,\ldots,m-1
are invertible.

2.2.
Canonical representation of
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
on
{\underline{V}}^{\otimes m}

Keep the notations as before. In particular, we keep the notations in §
1.2.1
. In order to endow an
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
-module structure on
{\underline{V}}^{\otimes m}
, we first introduce a decomposition of
{\underline{V}}^{\otimes m}
.
Let
n
be a nonnegative integer. Set
\underline{n}:=\{1,2,\cdots,n-1,n\}

for
n>0
and
\underline{0}=\emptyset
, where
\emptyset

denotes the empty set.

For
I\subset\underline{m}
with
\#I=l
, and
J\subset\underline{m}\backslash I
,
we set
\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}
to be the subspace of
{\underline{V}}^{\otimes m}
spanned by all
M_{f}:=\eta_{f(1)}\otimes\eta_{f(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{f(m)}
with
f(k)\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+2}
for
k\in I
,
f(k)=-r-\frac{3}{2}
for
k\in J
, and
f(k)=r+\frac{3}{2}
for
k\in\underline{m}\backslash(I\cup J)
, and let

\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}=\underset{\#I=l,J\subset\underline{m}\backslash I}{\bigoplus}\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}.

In the set-up above,
{\underline{V}}^{\otimes m}
can be decomposed into a direct sum of subspaces

{\underline{V}}^{\otimes m}=\bigoplus_{l=0}^{m}\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}=\bigoplus_{I,J\subset\underline{m},I\cap J=\emptyset}\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}.

From now on, we suppose the integer
l\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
. Notice that we have
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}=V^{\otimes l}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}
.

2.2.1.

Keep the notations from §
1.4
. For
I\subset\underline{m}

with
\#I=l,

J\subset\underline{m}\backslash I
, there exists a linear transformation
\omega_{I,J}
mapping
\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}
onto
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
.
Actually, this
\omega_{I,J}
can be expressed as a composition of
T_{i}^{\pm 1}
with
0\leq i\leq m-1
and acts on
\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}
by natural representation
\Psi
of Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{m})
.
We can regard it as an element in
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
(see Remark
2.2
). We explain this operation in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
I\subset\underline{m}

with
\#I=l,

J\subset\underline{m}\backslash I
, there exists an
element
\omega_{I,J}
in
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
generated by some
T_{i}^{\pm 1}
with
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}

such that
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}=\Psi(\omega_{I,J})(\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m})
.

Proof.

We construct the desired element by induction on
\#J
.

In the case when
\#J=0
, i.e.
J=\emptyset
, we take
r
to be the minimal element of
\underline{m}\backslash I
. If further,
I=\underline{l}
, then we have nothing to do. Now we suppose
I\neq\underline{l}
. Take the minimal element
i
in
I
with
i>r
.
For any
\eta_{f(1)}\otimes\eta_{f(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{f(m)}\in\underline{V}_{I,\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
, we have
f(k)>f(i)
for any
r\leq k<i
. Then by applying
\Psi
,
we have

\Psi(T_{i-1}^{-1}T_{i-2}^{-1}\cdots T_{r}^{-1})(\underline{V}^{\otimes m}_{I,\emptyset})=\Psi(T_{r}^{-1})\Psi(T_{r+1}^{-1})\cdots\Psi(T_{i-1}^{-1})(\underline{V}^{\otimes m}_{I,\emptyset})=\underline{V}^{\otimes m}_{I^{\prime},\emptyset},

where
I^{\prime}=(I\backslash\{i\})\cup\{r\}
. Applying operation repeatedly in the case
I^{\prime}
, we can change index
I
into
\underline{l}
, which shows
\underline{V}^{\otimes m}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}=\Psi(\omega_{I,\emptyset})(\underline{V}^{\otimes m}_{I,J})
for some
\omega_{I,\emptyset}
as desired.

Next, consider the case when
\#J>0
. Then there exists a minimal element
j\in J
, i.e, all
\eta_{f(1)}\otimes\eta_{f(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{f(m)}\in\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}
satisfy
f(i)>f(j)
for any
i<j
. Then by applying the action
\Psi
, we have

\Psi(T_{j-1}^{-1}T_{j-2}^{-1}\cdots T_{1}^{-1}T_{0})(\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m})=\Psi(T_{0})\Psi(T_{1}^{-1})\cdots\Psi(T_{j-1}^{-1})(\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m})=\underline{V}_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}^{\otimes m},

where
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
is some subset of
\underline{m}
with
\#J^{\prime}=\#J-1
. We can now apply induction to
\underline{V}_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}^{\otimes m}
such that
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}=\Psi(\omega_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}})(\underline{V}_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}^{\otimes m})
for some
\omega_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}
. Thus
\omega_{I,J}=T_{j-1}^{-1}T_{j-2}^{-1}\cdots T_{1}^{-1}T_{0}\omega_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}
as desired.
∎

2.2.2.

Note that
{\underline{V}}^{\otimes m}=\bigoplus_{l=0}^{m}\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}=\bigoplus_{I,J\subset\underline{m},I\cap J=\emptyset}\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}.
By using Lemma
2.3
, we can only consider the actions on
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}
and then extend
\Psi
. We can obtain an
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
-module structure on
\underline{V}^{\otimes m}
. Such an extension seems natural by the structure of duplex Hecke algebras. Recall that
\Psi_{l}^{V}
in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sec: 1.4}
denotes the natural representation of
\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{l})
on
V^{\otimes l}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists a unique
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra
anti-homomorphism
\Xi:\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}\rightarrow{\mathrm{End}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes m})

such that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\Xi(T_{i})=\Psi(H_{i}),

\displaystyle\text{for }i=0,1,2,\ldots\ ,m-1;

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}}=\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{\sigma})\otimes\mathsf{id}^{\otimes m-l},

\displaystyle\text{for }\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{l},0\leq l\leq m;

\displaystyle\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(M_{f})=0,

\displaystyle\text{for }M_{f}\notin\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}.

Proof.

Since the action of generators of
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
are determined by formula (
2.4
), the uniqueness is obvious if this homomorphism exists.
It is enough to show that the right action
\Xi
of
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}

on
\underline{V}^{\otimes m}

keeps relations
(
2.1
)-(
2.3
).

With the start point of extending
\Psi
to
\Xi
,
\Xi|_{\langle T_{i},i=0,1,...,m-1\rangle}
is exactly the representation
\Psi
of
\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{m})
. So
\Xi
keeps relations (
2.1
).

For
0<i<l
. Recall that in this case,
the action of
H_{i}
on

M_{f}=\eta_{f(1)}\otimes\eta_{f(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{f(m)}

only changes the first

l
factors of
M_{f}
which belongs to
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
. If
M_{f}\in\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
, we then have

\Xi(T_{i})(M_{f})=\Psi(H_{i})(M_{f})=\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{i})\otimes\mathsf{id}^{\otimes m-l}(M_{f})=\Xi(x_{s_{i}}^{(l)})(M_{f}),

and hence

\Xi(T_{i}x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(M_{f})=\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})\Xi(T_{i})(M_{f})=\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})\Xi(x_{s_{i}}^{(l)})(M_{f})=\Xi(x_{s_{i}}^{(l)}x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(M_{f}),

\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)}T_{i})(M_{f})=\Xi(T_{i})\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(M_{f})=\Xi(x_{s_{i}}^{(l)})\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(M_{f})=\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)}x_{s_{i}}^{(l)})(M_{f}).

If
M_{f}\notin\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
, the both sides of equations are zero, since
\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(M_{f})=0
and
\Xi(T_{i})(M_{f})\notin\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}
.

For
i>l
, we have

\Xi(T_{i}x_{\sigma}^{(l)})=q^{-1}\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})=\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)}T_{i}),

since
\Xi(T_{i})=\Psi(H_{i})
acts as multiplication by
q^{-1}
on vector of
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
and annihilates vectors which are outside of
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
.
Thus,
\Xi
keeps relation (
2.3
).

For
l\neq k
, it follows easily from definition that
\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)}x_{\gamma}^{(k)})=\Xi(x_{\gamma}^{(k)}x_{\sigma}^{(l)})=0
.
Regard
\mathfrak{S}_{l}
as a subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
, we get similarly for
0<i<l

\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)}x_{s_{i}}^{(l)})=\Psi({H_{\sigma}H_{i}}).

The relation (
2.2
) holds by the composition formula in Hecke algebra.

We conclude the existence of
\Xi
, which is a representation of
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
on
\underline{V}^{\otimes m}
.

∎

3.
Levi-type
q
-Schur-Weyl duality of type
\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}

In this section, we establish a duality between the Levi-type
\imath
quantum
group and the duplex Hecke algebra
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
.

3.1.
\imath
quantum group of Levi-type

Definition 3.1
.

Denote by
L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2r+4})
the subalgebra of
{\textbf{U}}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4})

generated by
B_{0}:=E_{0}+qF_{0}K_{0}^{-1}+K_{0}^{-1}
, all
B_{i}:=E_{i}+F_{-i}K_{i}^{-1}
with
0\neq i\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+1}
, and all
k_{i}:=K_{i}K_{-i}^{-1}
with
i\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+3}
. Call
L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2r+4})
an
\imath
quantum group of Levi-type.

It is easy to see
L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4})\cong\textbf{U}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+2})\oplus\langle k_{r+1},k_{r+1}^{-1}\rangle

as a space.

3.2.

The action of
{\textbf{U}}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4})
on
\underline{V}^{\otimes m}
restricts to the subalgebra
L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2r+4})
and denoted by
\Phi
again. On the other hand, we establish the action of
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
on
\underline{V}^{\otimes m}
in Proposition
2.4
. We have the following observation, which reveals the relation between the Levi-type
\imath
quantum group
L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2r+4})
and the duplex Hecke algebra
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The actions of
L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2r+4})

and
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
on
\underline{V}^{\otimes m}

commute with each other, i.e.
\Phi(L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2r+4}))\subset{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathfrak{H}\kern-4.91673pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes m})
.

Proof.

We only need to show the actions of
k_{i}
with
i\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+3}
, and

B_{i}
with
i\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+1}
on
\underline{V}^{\otimes m}
commute with the action of
T_{i}
with
i=0,1,\ldots\ ,m-1
, and

x_{\sigma}^{(l)}
with
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{l}
for
0\leq l\leq m
, respectively.

The mutual commutant property of all
\Xi(T_{i})
with
\Phi(L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2r+4}))
is ensured by
\imath
Schur duality (Theorem
1.5
) and Remark
2.2
.
We first check that all
\Psi(B_{i})
with
0\neq i\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+1}
commute with all
\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})
with
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{l}
. From (
1.3
), we have that

\displaystyle\Delta^{m}(B_{i})

\displaystyle=\Delta^{m}(E_{i}+F_{-i}K_{i}^{-1})=\Delta^{m}(E_{i})+\Delta^{m}(F_{-i}K_{i}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathsf{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes E_{i}\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes(j-m)}+(\sum_{j=1}^{m}{K_{-i}}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes F_{-i}\otimes\mathsf{id}^{\otimes(m-j)})(K_{i}^{\otimes-m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathsf{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes E_{i}\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes(j-m)}+k_{i}^{\otimes(1-j)}\otimes F_{-i}K_{i}^{-1}\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes(j-m)}.

Notice any element of
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}
can be written as
\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}
with some
\alpha\in V^{\otimes l}
. For
0\neq i\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+1}
, we can get

\displaystyle\Phi(B_{i})(\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})=\Delta^{m}(B_{i})(\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathsf{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes E_{i}\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes(j-m)}+k_{i}^{\otimes(1-j)}\otimes F_{-i}K_{i}^{-1}\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes(j-m)})(\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sum_{j=1}^{l}\mathsf{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes E_{i}\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes(j-m)}+k_{i}^{\otimes(1-j)}\otimes F_{-i}K_{i}^{-1}\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes(j-m)})(\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sum_{j=1}^{l}\mathsf{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes E_{i}+k_{i}^{\otimes(1-j)}\otimes F_{-i}K_{i}^{-1})(\alpha)\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{l}(B_{i})(\alpha)\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}.

The third and fourth equalities follow from the fact

\displaystyle E_{i}\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}=F_{-i}K_{i}^{-1}\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}=0,\text{ and }K_{i}^{-1}\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}=\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}\text{ for }i\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+1}.

(3.1)

Hence, from
\imath
Schur duality on
V^{\otimes l}
, we have

\displaystyle\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})\Phi(B_{i})(\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})=

\displaystyle\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(\Delta^{l}(B_{i})(\alpha)\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{\sigma})\Delta^{l}(B_{i})(\alpha)\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{l}(B_{i})\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{\sigma})(\alpha)\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(B_{i})\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}).

Now we consider the action on the other parts outside of
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}
. We already have
\Phi(B_{i})\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})=0
. For any

M_{f}=\eta_{f(1)}\otimes\eta_{f(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{f(m)}\notin\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
,
there are two possibilities:

(i)

There exists
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,l\}
such that
f(k)\in\{-r-\frac{3}{2},r+\frac{3}{2}\}
;

(ii)

There exists
k\in\{l+1,l+2,\ldots,m\}
with
f(k)\neq r+\frac{3}{2}
.

In the first case, following from formula (
3.1
), the
k
th factor of each term of
\Psi(B_{i})(M_{f})
does not lie in
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}
for
j\neq k
and is zero for
j=k
.
It shows
\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})\Phi(B_{i})=0
as required.

For the second case, the action of
E_{i}
,
F_{-i}K_{i}^{-1}
,
K_{i}^{-1}
, and
k_{i}^{-1}
for
i\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+1}
cannot change
\eta_{k}
to
\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}
. We still have
\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})\Phi(B_{i})=0
.

It remains to show
\Phi(B_{0})
commutes with
\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})
. Notice that

\displaystyle\Delta^{m}(B_{0})=\Delta^{m}(E_{0})+\Delta^{m}(qF_{0}K_{0}^{-1})+\Delta^{m}({K_{0}^{-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathsf{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes E_{0}\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes(j-m)}+q(\sum_{j=1}^{m}{K_{-i}}^{\otimes(j-1^{)}}\otimes F_{-i}\otimes\mathsf{id}^{\otimes(m-j)})(K_{i}^{\otimes-m})+K_{0}^{\otimes-m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathsf{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes E_{i}\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes(j-m)}+qk_{i}^{\otimes(1-j)}\otimes F_{-i}K_{i}^{-1}\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes(j-m)}+K_{0}^{\otimes-m}.

Similarly, we still have

\displaystyle\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})\Phi(B_{0})(\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})=

\displaystyle\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{\sigma})\Delta^{l}(B_{0})(\alpha)\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{l}(B_{0})\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{\sigma})(\alpha)\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(B_{0})\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}),

and

\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})\Phi(B_{0})(M_{f})=\Phi(B_{0})\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(M_{f})=0,

for any
M_{f}
outside of
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}
,
since
K_{0}\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}=\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}
. The Lemma follows.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\Phi_{l}^{V}
be the natural representation of
\textbf{U}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+2})
on
V^{\otimes l}.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we have

\Phi(B_{i})(\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})=\Delta^{m}(B_{i})(\alpha\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})=\Delta^{l}(B_{i})(\alpha)\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}=\Phi_{l}^{V}(B_{i})(\alpha)\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l},

for all

. Hence, we can regard

\Phi_{l}^{V}(\textbf{U}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+2}))
as a subspace of

\Phi(L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+2}))|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}}.

3.3.
q
-Schur algebras of type
\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}
and their gradations

We recall some general facts about
q
-Schur algebras associated with Hecke algebras of type
\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}
.
For more details, one can consult
[
6
]
or
[
9
]
.

For
M_{f}=\eta_{f(1)}\otimes\eta_{f(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{f(m)}\in V
, the weight
\lambda(M_{f})
of
M_{f}
is defined to be the
(r+1)
-tuple

(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r+1}),

where
\lambda_{i}
is the total number of occurrences of the indices
i-\frac{1}{2}
and
-(i-\frac{1}{2})
in
M_{f}
. The set of all weights will be denoted by
\Lambda_{\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}}(2r+2,m)
.

The space
V_{\lambda}
is the span of all the basis vectors
M_{f}
with weight
\lambda
, the vector
\mathfrak{M}_{\lambda}
is the unique basis vector
M_{f}\in V_{\lambda}
whose indices satisfy
f(1)\leq f(2)\leq\cdots\leq f(m)
.

The parabolic subgroup
P_{\lambda}
of
\mathscr{W}(\textsf{A}_{m-1})=\left\langle s_{1},\cdots,s_{m-1}\right\rangle
is generated by some
s_{i}\in\mathscr{W}(\textsf{A}_{m-1})
, which satisfy
s_{i}\in P_{\lambda}
if and only if the
i
th and
(i+1)
th entries of
\mathfrak{M}_{\lambda}
are equal.

The elements
x_{\lambda}
of
\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{m})
is defined by

x_{\lambda}=\sum_{w\in P_{\lambda}}H_{w}.

Following
[
6
]
, we give the following definition.

Definition 3.4
.

The
q
-Schur algebra of type
\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{m}
is given by

S_{q}^{\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}}(2r+2,m)={\mathrm{End}}_{\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{m})}(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}}(2r+2,m)}x_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{m})).

Proposition 3.5
.

Keep the notations as above. The following statements hold.

(1)

V_{\lambda}\cong x_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}({\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{m}})
;

(2)

V^{\otimes m}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}}(2r+2,m)}x_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{m})
;

(3)

S_{q}^{\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}}(2r+2,m)
is a free
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-module. Moreover, there is a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-basis in
S_{q}^{\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}}(2r+2,m)
:

\{\phi^{d}_{\lambda,\mu}\mid\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}}(2r+2,m)\}
with
d
is a distinguished double
P_{\lambda}-P_{\mu}
coset representative, such that each
\phi^{d}_{\lambda,\mu}
only has nonzero image on
V_{\mu}
;

(4)

If
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\cdots,\mu_{r+1})
, then
\phi^{d}_{\lambda,\mu}
only has nonzero image on
V^{\otimes m}_{k}
where
k=m-\mu_{r+1}
.

Proof.

Parts (1)-(3) follow from
[
6
, §4]
. As to (4), we remark that
\phi^{d}_{\lambda,\mu}
only has nonzero image on
V_{\mu}
and
V_{\mu}\subset V^{\otimes m}_{k}
, where
k=m-\mu_{r+1}
(with the notation from §
2.2
). Then (4) follows immediately.
∎

3.4.

Now we can establish one part of the property of double centralizers.

Theorem 3.6
.

If
q
is transcendental
over
\mathbb{Q}
. We have
\Phi(L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4}))={\mathrm{End}}_{\mathfrak{H}\kern-4.91673pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes m}).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
, it is enough to show
{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathfrak{H}\kern-4.91673pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes m})\subset\Phi(L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4})).

Note that
{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathfrak{H}\kern-4.91673pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes m})\subset{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{m})}(\underline{V}^{\otimes m})=S_{q}^{\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}}(2r+4,m)
.
Thanks to Proposition
3.5
, for every
\phi\in{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathfrak{H}\kern-4.91673pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes m})
,
we have
\phi=\underset{\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}}(2r+4,m)}{\sum}a_{\lambda\mu d}\phi^{d}_{\lambda,\mu}
, where
a_{\lambda\mu d}\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
. More precisely,

\phi=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\phi_{l}=\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{m}\sum_{\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}}(2r+4,m),\mu_{r+2}=m-l}a_{\lambda\mu d}\phi^{d}_{\lambda,\mu}.

We have
\phi|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}=\phi_{l}
by Proposition
3.5
(4). Now we will proceed the arguments by several steps.

(a) We show that
\phi_{l}
stabilizes
\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}
with
\#I=l
.

We consider
\phi_{l}
stabilizes
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
at first.
Suppose
\nu_{j}\in\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
, and

\phi_{l}(\nu_{j})=\nu_{l}+\nu_{k}
with
\nu_{l}\in\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
,

\nu_{k}\notin\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
.
Denote the identity element of
\mathfrak{S}_{l}
by
e
, then

\Xi(x_{e}^{(l)})(\nu_{l}+\nu_{k})=\Xi(x_{e}^{(l)})\phi_{l}(\nu_{j})=\Xi(x_{e}^{(l)})\phi(\nu_{j})=\phi\Xi(x_{e}^{(l)})(\nu_{j})=\nu_{l}+\nu_{k}.

By the definition of
\Xi
, we have
\Xi(x_{e}^{(l)})(\nu_{l})=\nu_{l}
, and

\Xi(x_{e}^{(l)})(\nu_{k})=0
.
It yields that
\nu_{k}=0
, and hence
\phi_{l}
stabilizes

\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}.

Then we consider a general summand
\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}

by using Lemma
2.3
. Note that

\phi\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})=\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})\phi
, and

\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})\phi(\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m})=\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})\phi_{l}(\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m})\subset\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}
.
It yields that

\phi_{l}(\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m})=\phi_{l}\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})(\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m})=\phi\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})(\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m})=\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})\phi(\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m})\subset\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m},

as desired.

(b) We claim that
there exists an element
g_{l}\in L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4})
satisfying

\phi_{l}|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}=\Phi(g_{l})|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}.

We already have that

\displaystyle\Xi(T_{0})|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{0})\otimes\mathsf{id}^{\otimes m-l},

\displaystyle\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{\sigma})\otimes\mathsf{id}^{\otimes m-l}\text{ for any }\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{l}.

On the other hand, consider the
\imath
Schur duality of
\Phi_{l}^{V}
and
\Psi_{l}^{V}
on
\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}
(extended from
V^{\otimes l}
).
For any
\phi\in{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathfrak{H}\kern-4.91673pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes m})
, we have

\phi_{l}|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}}\in{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{l})}(\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m})=\Phi_{l}^{V}(\textbf{U}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+2}))\subset\Phi(L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4}))|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}}.

(See Remark
3.3
.)
So there exists
g_{l}\in L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4})
such that

\phi_{l}|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}}=\Phi(g_{l})|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}}
. Consequently,

\phi_{l}(\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m})=\phi_{l}\Xi(\omega_{I,J})(\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m})=\Phi(g_{l})\Xi(\omega_{I,J})(\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}).

Note that
\Phi(L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4}))\subset{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathfrak{H}\kern-4.91673pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes m})
. Hence
\Phi(g_{l})
commutes with
\Xi(\omega_{I,J})
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{l}(\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m})

\displaystyle=\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})\phi_{l}\Xi(\omega_{I,J})(\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m})

\displaystyle=\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})\Phi(g_{l})\Xi(\omega_{I,J})(\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m})

\displaystyle=\Phi(g_{l})(\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}).

Since every direct summands of
\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}
are stabilized by
\phi_{l}
,
we have
\phi_{l}|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}=\Phi(g_{l})|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}
.

(c) We construct an element of
L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4})
such that its image under
\Phi
coincides with
\phi
, which consequently complete the proof.

For this purpose, we firstly set

X:=k_{r+1}^{r+1}k_{r}^{r}\cdots k_{2}^{2}k_{1}\in L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2r+4})
. From formula (
1.2
) we have

\displaystyle\Phi(X)(\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}})=q^{-(r+1)}\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}},

\displaystyle\Phi(X)(\eta_{-r-\frac{3}{2}})=q^{-(r+1)}\eta_{-r-\frac{3}{2}},

\displaystyle\Phi(X)(\eta_{i})=q\eta_{i},\text{ for }i\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+2}.

Hence

\Phi(X)|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}=q^{l-(m-l)(r+1)}\cdot\mathsf{id}|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}=q^{l(r+2)-m(r+1)}\cdot\mathsf{id}|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}.

We set
F(l):=q^{l(r+2)-m(r+1)}
(the integers
m
and
r
are fixed). Since
q
is transcendental
over
\mathbb{Q}
, we have
F(l)\neq F(k)
, for
l\neq k
.

With the above
X
, we secondly consider the element

G_{l}:=\frac{(X-F(1))\cdots\widehat{(X-F(l))}\cdots(X-F(m))}{(F(l)-F(1))\cdots\widehat{(F(l)-F(l))}\cdots(F(l)-F(m))},

where the hat indicates as usual that the term is dropped.
Then we have

\Phi(G_{l})|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}=(\frac{(F(l)-F(1))\cdots\widehat{(F(l)-F(l))}\cdots(F(l)-F(m))}{(F(l)-F(1))\cdots\widehat{(F(l)-F(l))}\cdots(F(l)-F(m))})\cdot\mathsf{id}|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}=\mathsf{id}|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}},

and

\Phi(G_{l})|_{\underline{V}_{k}^{\otimes m}}=(\frac{(F(k)-F(1))\cdots\widehat{(F(k)-F(l))}\cdots(F(k)-F(m))}{(F(k)-F(1))\cdots\widehat{(F(k)-F(l))}\cdots(F(k)-F(m))})\cdot\mathsf{id}|_{\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}}=0

for
k\neq l
.
Thus

\displaystyle\Phi(g_{l})\Phi(G_{l})\cdot{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}

\displaystyle=\phi_{l}\cdot\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m};

\displaystyle\Phi(g_{l})\Phi(G_{l})\cdot\underline{V}_{k}^{\otimes m}

\displaystyle=\phi_{l}\cdot\underline{V}_{k}^{\otimes m}=0\;\text{ for }l\neq k.

Hence
\phi_{l}=\Phi(g_{l}\cdot G_{l})
. So we finally succeed in finding a desired element

\sum_{l=0}^{m}g_{l}\cdot G_{l}\in L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2r+4}),

which satisfies

\phi=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\phi_{l}=\Phi(\sum_{l=0}^{m}g_{l}\cdot G_{l})\in\Phi(L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2r+4})).

The proof is completed.
∎

3.5.

Now we begin to deal with the other part of the double centraliser property. Under our assumption of
q
being transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
, we have that
V^{\otimes l}
is a completely
reducible
\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{l})
-module. Suppose
V^{\otimes l}=\underset{\pi\in I_{l}}{\bigoplus}S^{\pi}
,
where
S^{\pi}
is irreducible
\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{l})
-module and
I_{l}

is the index set. (We are not going to introduce this index set for simplicity, see details in
[
3
, §5.5]
.)
Combining with notations in §
2.2
, we have

\underline{V}_{\underline{l},\emptyset}^{\otimes m}=V^{\otimes l}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}=\underset{\pi\in I_{l}}{\bigoplus}S^{\pi}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l},

and hence

\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}=\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})(\underset{\pi\in I_{l}}{\bigoplus}S^{\pi}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}).

So we get

\displaystyle\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}

\displaystyle=\underset{\#I=l,J\subset\underline{m}\backslash I}{\bigoplus}\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}

\displaystyle=\underset{\#I=l,J\subset\underline{m}\backslash I}{\bigoplus}\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})(\underset{\pi\in I_{l}}{\bigoplus}S^{\pi}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})

\displaystyle=\underset{\pi\in I_{l}}{\bigoplus}\underset{\#I=l,J\subset\underline{m}\backslash I}{\bigoplus}\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})(S^{\pi}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}).

Under the isomorphism
\underline{V}_{I,J}^{\otimes m}\simeq V^{\otimes l}
, all
\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})(S^{\pi}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})
are isomorphic to
S^{\pi}
as an
\mathcal{H}(B_{l})
-module and we denote it as
S^{\pi}_{I,J}
.
We set
D^{\pi}_{l}:=\underset{\#I=l,J\subset\underline{m}\backslash I}{\bigoplus}S^{\pi}_{I,J}
.
The following lemma shows the property of
D^{\pi}_{l}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that q is transcendental over

\mathbb{Q}
. Then
D^{\pi}_{l}

is an irreducible
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
-module.

Proof.

We first prove
D^{\pi}_{l}
is an

\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
-module.
It is sufficient to show that
D^{\pi}_{l}
is stabilized by
T_{i}
, with
i=0,1,\cdots,m-1
, and
x_{\sigma}^{(l)}
, with
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{l}
for
0\leq l\leq m
.

From the proof of Lemma
2.3
,
\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})
only changes the positions of some tensor factors and takes some
\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}
to
\eta_{-r-\frac{3}{2}}
. The action of
T_{0}
preserves
S^{\pi}_{I,J}
if
1\in I
and takes
S^{\pi}_{I,J}
to
S^{\pi}_{I,J^{\prime}}
for some subset
J^{\prime}
if
1\notin I
. Now consider
T_{i}
with
i>0
. Note that
-r-\frac{3}{2}<k<r+\frac{3}{2}
for any
k\in\mathbb{I}_{2r+2}
. If both
i
and
i+1
belong to
I,J
, or
\underline{m}\backslash(I\cap J)
, the action of
T_{i}
preserves
S^{\pi}_{I,J}
. Otherwise, the action of
T_{i}
or
T_{i}^{-1}
on
S^{\pi}_{I,J}
takes
S^{\pi}_{I,J}
to
S^{\pi}_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}
for some subsets
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
with
\#I^{\prime}=\#I=l
. In summary,
D^{\pi}_{l}
is stable under the actions of all
T_{i}
.

By definition,
\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(k)})(D^{\pi}_{l})=0
for
k\neq l

and
\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})
only has nonzero image on
S^{\pi}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}
. As in the proof of Lemma
3.2
,

\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(S^{\pi}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})=(\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{\sigma})S^{\pi})\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}
for irreducible
\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{l})
-module
S^{\pi}
. It shows that
x_{\sigma}^{(l)}

stabilizes
D^{\pi}_{l}
.
We get therefore
D^{\pi}_{l}
is an
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
-module.

We then show that
D^{\pi}_{l}
is irreducible as an
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
-module.
It is enough to show that for any nonzero vector
\nu\in D^{\pi}_{l}
, the
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
-submodule generated by
\nu
coincides with
D^{\pi}_{l}
, the former of which is denoted by
N
in the following.

Firstly, we can write

\nu=\underset{\#I=l,J\subset\underline{m}\backslash I}{\sum}\Xi(\omega_{I,J}^{-1})(\nu_{I,J}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}),

where
\nu_{I,J}\in S^{\pi}
. By definition,
\Xi(x_{e}^{(l)})\Xi(\omega_{I,J})(\nu)=\nu_{I,J}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}
for the identity element
e\in\mathfrak{S}_{l}
. Recall that
S^{\pi}
is an irreducible

\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{\mathsf{B}}_{l})
-module, and

\displaystyle\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})(\nu_{I,J}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})

\displaystyle=(\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{\sigma})\nu_{I,J})\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}\text{ for }\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{l},

\displaystyle\Xi(T_{0})(\nu_{I,J}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l})

\displaystyle=(\Psi_{l}^{V}(H_{0})\nu_{I,J})\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}.

By the action of
\Xi(x_{\sigma}^{(l)})
with
\sigma
running over
\mathfrak{S}_{l}
, along with
\Xi(T_{0})
,
it is readily known that
N

contains the subspace
S^{\pi}\otimes\eta_{r+\frac{3}{2}}^{\otimes m-l}
, which is the generator of

D^{\pi}_{l}
. Hence
N
coincides with
D^{\pi}_{l}
. We conclude that

D^{\pi}_{l}
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
-module.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

If
q
is transcendental
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
\underline{V}^{\otimes m}

is a completely reducible
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
-module.

Proof.

Recall that

\underline{V}^{\otimes m}=\bigoplus^{m}_{l=0}\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes m}=\bigoplus_{l=0}^{m}\bigoplus_{\pi\in I_{l}}D^{\pi}_{l}.

By Lemma
3.7
,
\underline{V}^{\otimes m}

is a completely
reducible
\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m}
-module.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

If
q
is transcendental
over
\mathbb{Q}
, then
\Xi(\mathfrak{H}\kern-6.00006pt\mathfrak{H}_{m})={\mathrm{End}}_{L_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2r+4})}(\underline{V}^{\otimes m})^{\rm{op}}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.6
and Corollary
3.8
, we
can use classical double commutant theorem (see
[
5
, §4.1.13]
)
to reach this conclusion.
∎

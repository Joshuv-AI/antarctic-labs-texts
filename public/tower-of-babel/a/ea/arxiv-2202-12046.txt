In this section we introduce the notion of a

Notation 1
.

For
\mathcal{I}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
,

•

let
\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})
denote the set of finite partial functions
g
from
\mathcal{I}
to
\{0,1\}
, and

•

for
g\in\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})
write
\mathcal{I}^{g}
for

\bigcap\{A\mid A\in\mathrm{dom}(g)\textnormal{ and }g(A)=1\}\cap\bigcap\{\omega\backslash A\mid A\in\mathrm{dom}(g)\textnormal{ and }g(A)=0\}.

In this notation, a family
\mathcal{I}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
is
independent
if
\mathcal{I}^{g}
is infinite for all
g\in\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})
. An independent family
\mathcal{I}
is
maximal
if

\forall X\in[\omega]^{\omega}\;\exists g\in\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})\text{ such that }\mathcal{I}^{g}\cap X\text{ or }\mathcal{I}^{g}\backslash X\text{ is finite,}

We will need a slight strengthening of maximality.

Definition 2.1
.

An independent family
\mathcal{I}
is
densely maximal
if

\forall X\in[\omega]^{\omega}\text{ and }g^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})\;\exists g\supseteq g^{\prime}\text{ in }\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})\text{ such that }\mathcal{I}^{g}\cap X\text{ or }\mathcal{I}^{g}\backslash X\text{ is finite.}

In other words, an independent family
\mathcal{I}
is
densely maximal
if for each
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
the collection of
g
’s witnessing that
\mathcal{I}\cup\{X\}
is not a larger independent family is dense in
(\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I}),\supseteq)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an independent family. The
density ideal of
\mathcal{I}
, denoted
\mathrm{id}(\mathcal{I})
, is

\{X\subseteq\omega\mid\forall g^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})\;\exists g\supseteq g^{\prime}\text{ in }\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})\text{ such that }\mathcal{I}^{g}\cap X\text{ is finite}\}.

Dual to the density ideal of
\mathcal{I}
is the
density filter of
\mathcal{I}
, denoted
\mathrm{fil}(\mathcal{I})
and defined as

\{X\subseteq\omega\mid\forall g^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})\;\exists g\supseteq g^{\prime}\text{ in }\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})\text{ such that }\mathcal{I}^{g}\backslash X\text{ is finite}\}.

Observe also that for an infinite independent family
\mathcal{I}
, none of the above definitions’ meanings change if we replace the word “finite” with “empty”. We have as well the following from
[
9
, Lemma 5.4]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

A family
\mathcal{I}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
is densely maximal if and only if

P(\omega)=\mathrm{fil}(\mathcal{I})\cup\langle\omega\backslash\mathcal{I}^{g}\mid g\in\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{I})\rangle_{\mathrm{dn}}.

Where for a set

denotes the downward closure of
\mathcal{X}
under

The following are easily verified, see

Lemma 2.4

(1)

(3)

.

Recall that given a family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets of
\omega

(1)

\mathcal{F}

(2)

If

a

-filter, Ramsey filter).

Definition 2.5
.

An independent family

Definition 3.1

For every

4.
Applications

We now turn to applications of the results from the previous section. The most obvious of these is that there is a small independent family in any model obtained by iteratively forcing with
h
-perfect tree partial orders. In particular we get the following as a corollary to Theorem

.

Corollary 4.1
.

It is consistent that
\mathfrak{i}=\aleph_{1}<{\rm non}(\mathcal{N})=\aleph_{2}
.

As mentioned in the introduction this consistent inequality was first shown in
[
4
, Theorem 3.8]
, though by a very different construction.

In the models constructed in
[
7
]
many interesting properties hold with regards to the structure of the strong measure zero sets, for example the consistency of “the additivity of the strong measure zero ideal is
\aleph_{2}=2^{\aleph_{0}}

\mathfrak{i}=\aleph_{1}
. One thing to note as a consequence of this is the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

It is independent of
\mathsf{ZFC}
whether there is a maximal independent family of strong measure zero.

Proof.

In the Laver model,
[
1
, Model 7.6.13]
every strong measure zero set is countable so no maximal independent family has strong measure zero. By contrast if the additivity of the strong measure zero ideal is
\aleph_{2}
then in particular any set of reals of size
\aleph_{1}
will be strong measure zero, in particular any selective independent family from the ground model.

∎

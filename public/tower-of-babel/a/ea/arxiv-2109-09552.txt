Functoriality for Supercuspidal L L -packets
Adèle Bourgeois, Paul Mezo

Abstract
Kaletha constructs L L -packets for supercuspidal L L -parameters of tame p p -adic groups. These L L -packets consist entirely of supercuspidal representations, which are explicitly described. Using the explicit descriptions, we show that Kaletha’s L L -packets satisfy a fundamental functoriality property desired for the Local Langlands Correspondence.

Introduction

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group defined over a
nonarchimedean local field
F
. The Local Langlands Correspondence
(LLC) is a conjectural map

\varphi\mapsto\Pi_{\varphi}

from
L
-parameters
to
L
-packets

[
Bor79
, Chapter
III]
. The latter are finite
sets of (equivalence classes of) irreducible representations of the
group of
F
-points,
G(F)
.
The LLC is
expected to satisfy numerous additional properties which give it
content. We focus on only two properties. The first property concerns

supercuspidal
representations, for which packets were developed by Kaletha

[
Kal19
,
Kal21
]
. The second concerns a basic form of

functoriality
as listed by Borel in
[
Bor79
, Desideratum 10.3(5)]
. This form of functoriality was later refined by Solleveld in
[
Sol20
, Corollary 2]
.

To describe the expected properties for supercuspidal representations,
we recall that an
L
-parameter is an
L
-homomorphism

\varphi:W_{F}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}\rightarrow{{}^{L}}G

from the Weil-Deligne group into the
L
-group of
G

[
Bor79
, Section 8.2]
. Following
[
Kal21
, Section
4.1]
, the
L
-parameter
\varphi
is defined to
be
supercuspidal
if it is trivial on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
,

i.e.

\varphi:W_{F}\rightarrow{{}^{L}}G,

and its image is not contained in a proper parabolic subgroup of
{{}^{L}}G

[
Bor79
, Section 3.3]
. As observed in
[
Kal21
, Section
4.1]
, “compound”
L
-packets (or
L
-packets when
G
is quasisplit) consisting entirely of
supercuspidal representations are conjectured to correspond precisely
to supercuspidal
L
-parameters (
[
DR09
, Section 3.5]
,
[
AMS18
]
).
In his recent works
[
Kal19
,
Kal21
]
, Kaletha
provides an explicit construction for these conjectured

L
-packets, under the additional assumptions that
G
splits over a
tamely ramified extension, and that the residual characteristic
p
of

F
does not divide the order of the the Weyl group of
G
. He further
proves that the
L
-packets satisfy some important properties
(
e.g.
stability).

The first goal of this paper is to show that these packets satisfy
the desired functorial property
[
Bor79
, Desideratum
10.3(5)]
. For this reason, and from now on, we work
under the aforementioned additional
assumptions on
G
and the residual characteristic of
F
.
For the sake of simplicity, we also assume that
G
is
quasisplit over
F
(see the discussion surrounding (
1
)).
Let
\Phi_{\mathrm{sc}}(G)
denote the set (of conjugacy classes) of
supercuspidal
L
-parameters of
G
. Given
\varphi\in\Phi_{\mathrm{sc}}(G)
, we
let
\Pi_{\varphi}
denote the associated supercuspidal

L
-packet obtained via Kaletha’s construction.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
G
splits over a tamely ramified extension, is quasisplit and that the residual characteristic
p
of
F
does not divide the order of the Weyl group of
G
.
Let
\eta:G\rightarrow\underaccent{\bar}{G}
be an
F
-morphism of connected
reductive
F
-groups such that

i)

the kernel of
d\eta:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Lie}(\underaccent{\bar}{G})
is central,

ii)

the cokernel of
\eta
is an abelian
F
-group.

Let
\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}\in\Phi_{\mathrm{sc}}(\underaccent{\bar}{G})
and set
\varphi={{}^{L}\eta}\circ\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
. Then for all
\underaccent{\bar}{\pi}\in\Pi_{\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}}
,

\underaccent{\bar}{\pi}\circ\eta
is the direct sum of finitely many irreducible
supercuspidal representations belonging to
\Pi_{\varphi}
.

We recall that the map
{}^{L}\eta
is given as follows. We let
\widehat{\eta}:\widehat{\underaccent{\bar}{G}}\rightarrow\widehat{G}
be the induced map on the Langlands dual groups
[
Spr79
, Sections 1 and 2]
, and define
{}^{L}\eta:{{}^{L}\underaccent{\bar}{G}}\rightarrow{{}^{L}G}
by
{}^{L}\eta(g,w)=(\widehat{\eta}(g),w)
for all
g\in\widehat{\underaccent{\bar}{G}},w\in W_{F}
.

Note that the above theorem is a modified version of
[
Bor79
, Desideratum 10.3(5)]
, in which
\eta
is required to have abelian kernel and cokernel. The hypothesis on
\eta
is precisely
[
Sol20
, Condition 1]
, and is stronger
[
Sol20
, Lemma 5.1]
than that of
[
Bor79
, Desideratum 10.3(5)]
. We require this stronger hypothesis to rule out purely inseparable homomorphisms, such as
[
Bor91
, V.16.1]
, that arise in positive characteristic and to ensure that the root systems of
G
and
\underaccent{\bar}{G}
are identified through
\eta
.

In addition to proving Theorem
1.1
, we also provide a description of the components of
\underaccent{\bar}{\pi}\circ\eta
. The supercuspidal representations that make up
the
L
-packets of Theorem
1.1
are constructed from

tame
F
-non-singular elliptic pairs
, which consist of a
particular kind of torus and a character thereof
[
Kal21
, Definition
3.4.1]
. Given such a pair

(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})
of
\underaccent{\bar}{G}
, we let
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}
denote the
attached supercuspidal representation of
\underaccent{\bar}{G}(F)
, which is obtained from the
J.-K. Yu construction

[
Yu01
]
after unfolding
(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})
into an appropriate
\underaccent{\bar}{G}
-datum
. The representation

\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}
may be reducible, and its
irreducible components form part of an
L
-packet. The
first big result of this paper is writing
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}\circ\eta
as a direct sum of conjugates of
\pi_{(S,\theta)}
, where
(S,\theta)
is a tame

F
-non-singular elliptic pair of
G
that satisfies
\eta(S)\subset\underaccent{\bar}{S}
and
\theta=\underaccent{\bar}{\theta}\circ\eta
. This is Theorem
2.4
.

The composition
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}\circ\eta
can be
viewed as the restriction of
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}
to
\eta(G(F))
.
Having abelian cokernel implies that
\eta(G)
is a subgroup of

\underaccent{\bar}{G}
which contains the derived subgroup
[\underaccent{\bar}{G},\underaccent{\bar}{G}]
. The kernel of
\eta
, which we denote by
Z
, is a central subgroup by
[
Sol20
, Lemma 5.1]
. It will be convenient to write
{G}_{Z}
for
\eta(G)
. In this way,
{G}_{Z}\simeq G/Z
and
\underaccent{\bar}{G}=Z(\underaccent{\bar}{G}){G}_{Z}
.
We compute the restriction of
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}
to
\eta(G(F))\simeq G(F)/Z(F)
in
two steps. First, by restricting to
\eta(G)(F)={G}_{Z}(F)
, and second, by further restricting to
G(F)/Z(F)
. The group
\eta(G(F))
is a normal subgroup of
{G}_{Z}(F)
(Corollary
2.2
) and their quotient is parameterized by a subgroup of
H^{1}(F,Z)

[
Spr09
, Proposition 12.3.4]
, which may be nontrivial. The restriction of
supercuspidal representations to subgroups that contain the derived
subgroup was extensively studied in
[
Bou21
]
. As such, we can
apply the results therein to obtain a description for

\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}|_{{G}_{Z}(F)}
(Theorem
2.5
). The second restriction can be computed via Mackey theory, as the quotient
{G}_{Z}(F)/(G(F)/Z(F))
is compact and abelian
[
Sil79
]
. The decomposition of
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}\circ\eta
is given
in Theorem
2.7
.

In order to describe the supercuspidal representations in the

L
-packets
\Pi_{\varphi}
and

\Pi_{\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}}
, one must know which tame
F
-non-singular elliptic
pairs to use. These pairs are provided by

supercuspidal
L
-packet data

[
Kal21
, Definition
4.1.4]
. The supercuspidal
L
-packet
data for
\varphi
and
\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
consist of tuples

(S,\widehat{j},\chi_{0},\theta)
and
(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\widehat{\underaccent{\bar}{j}},\underaccent{\bar}{\chi}_{0},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})
,
respectively. Unlike the previous paragraph,
S
and
\underaccent{\bar}{S}
are not subtori of
G
and
\underaccent{\bar}{G}
. Rather, they are embedded into subtori of the respective groups. The elements
\widehat{j}
and
\widehat{\underaccent{\bar}{j}}

specify families of
admissible embeddings

S(F)\rightarrow G(F)
and
\underaccent{\bar}{S}(F)\rightarrow\underaccent{\bar}{G}(F)
, denoted
J(F)
and
\underaccent{\bar}{J}(F)
,
respectively. Each embedding
j\in J(F)

(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\in\underaccent{\bar}{J}(F))
is
used to generate a tame
F
-non-singular elliptic pair
(jS,j\theta)

(
(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})
), for which we let the components
of
\pi_{(jS,j\theta)}
(
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}
) be
elements of
\Pi_{\varphi}
(
\Pi_{\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}}
).

In order to apply our decomposition formula for

\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}\circ\eta

and relate it to representations in
\Pi_{\varphi}
, we must first
establish an appropriate relationship between the supercuspidal
L
-packet data and
the admissible embeddings. This is given to us by
Theorem
3.2
, another key result of this paper, in which we
show that for all
\underaccent{\bar}{j}\in\underaccent{\bar}{J}(F)
, there exists
j\in J(F)
such
that
\eta(jS)\subset\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S}
and
j\theta=\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta}\circ\eta
. As such, we obtain a decomposition formula
for
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}\circ\eta
in terms of
certain conjugates of
\pi_{(jS,j\theta)}
. This completes the
proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The second goal of this paper is to provide a more detailed
description of
the decomposition of
\underaccent{\bar}{\pi}\circ\eta
in Theorem

1.1
in the special case
that both
\varphi
and

\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
are
regular
supercuspidal
L
-parameters

[
Kal19
, Definition 5.2.3]
.
The regularity assumptions on the
L
-parameters have several pleasant
consequences. We list them for
\varphi
, with the understanding that
their analogues hold for
\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
. First, the representations

\pi_{(jS,j\theta)}
are irreducible for all
j\in J(F)
. From this it follows
that the set
J(F)
parameterizes the
representations in
\Pi_{\varphi}
.
Second, the set
J(F)
is in bijection
with characters of the usual component group that one is accustomed to
seeing in Langlands correspondences. More precisely, the component
group of the centralizer of the image of
\varphi
is in bijection
with the Galois-fixed subgroup of some torus
\hat{S}

[
Kal19
, Lemma
5.3.4]
, and certain characters of this torus
are in bijection with
J(F)
(see (
12
)). Since

\hat{S}
is abelian, so too is the component group.

The regularity assumptions consequently allow us to write

\underaccent{\bar}{\pi}=\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}=\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi},\underaccent{\bar}{\varrho})},

where
\underaccent{\bar}{j}\in\underaccent{\bar}{J}(F)
corresponds to a character

\underaccent{\bar}{\varrho}
of the component group for
\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
. The map
\hat{\eta}

sends the component group for
\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
to the one for

\varphi
. The precise description of
\underaccent{\bar}{\pi}\circ\eta

is given in Proposition
4.12
. We summarize it
here as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\eta:G\rightarrow\underaccent{\bar}{G}
,
\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
and
\varphi={{}^{L}\eta}\circ\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
be as in Theorem

1.1
. Assume that
\varphi
and

\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
are regular. Then

\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi},\underaccent{\bar}{\varrho})}\circ\eta\simeq\underset{\varrho}{\bigoplus}\ \mathrm{Hom}\left(\underaccent{\bar}{\varrho},(\varrho\circ\hat{\eta})\right)\otimes\pi_{(\varphi,\varrho)}

where
\underaccent{\bar}{\varrho}
and
\varrho
are characters of the Langlands
component groups.

Theorem
1.2
is the proof of a conjecture of Solleveld for
regular supercuspidal
L
-parameters
[
Sol20
, Conjecture
2]
. Solleveld has proved his conjecture in a
variety of cases
[
Sol20
, Theorem
3]
. The only overlap of Theorem
1.2
with
these cases is when
G
and
\underaccent{\bar}{G}
are inner forms of

\mathrm{GL}_{n}
,
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
.

One might hope that the regularity of
\varphi={{}^{L}\eta}\circ\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
in Theorem
1.2
would follow from
the regularity of
\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
. Although we have not found a
counterexample, we suspect that this implication is not true.
However, the converse implication is true (Corollary

4.3
). Furthermore, as explained after

[
Kal19
, Definition 3.7.3]
, regular

L
-parameters are typical among all supercuspidal
L
-parameters.

Let us discuss how one might extend Theorem
1.2
to non-regular supercuspidal
L
-parameters. In this case,
\underaccent{\bar}{J}(F)
is no longer a parameterizing set for
\Pi_{\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}}
since the representations
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\in\underaccent{\bar}{J}(F),
may be reducible. For each
\underaccent{\bar}{j}\in\underaccent{\bar}{J}(F)
, the irreducible components of
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}
are parameterized by certain representations of
N(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{G})(F)_{\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta}}
, the stabilizer of the pair
(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})
in
N(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{G})(F)

[
Kal21
, Corollary 3.4.7]
. It appears that
[
Kal21
, Proposition 4.3.2]
serves as a bridge between
\{N(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{G})(F)_{\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta}}:\underaccent{\bar}{j}\in\underaccent{\bar}{J}(F)\}
and the component group of the centralizer of the image of
\underaccent{\bar}{\varphi}
. Another key step in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is the decomposition formula for
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}\circ\eta
. Removing the regularity hypothesis means one would need to derive the decomposition formula of
\underaccent{\bar}{\pi}\circ\eta
, where
\underaccent{\bar}{\pi}
is an irreducible component of
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{j}\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}
. This would require a deeper study of the results in
[
Kal21
, Section 3]
. Once one has such a decomposition formula, we believe that similar arguments as the ones in Section
4.2
could be applied.

Let us briefly indicate what is required to extend
Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
to
non-quasisplit groups. Every connected reductive
algebraic
F
-group
G^{\prime}
is an inner form of a quasisplit form

.
When
\mathrm{char}F=0
, the group
G^{\prime}
may be assigned to a class
of

for

[

, Corollary 3.8 and Section 5.1]
. This class
is an element in a set of the form

which we shall not describe. For any

,
there is a natural surjection

H^{1}(u\rightarrow W,Z^{\prime}\rightarrow jS)\ \longrightarrow\ H^{1}(u\rightarrow W,Z^{\prime}\rightarrow G),

(1)

where

is a maximal torus of
G
.
The elements in a supercuspidal
L
-packet of

are indexed by
the fibre in

over the class
in

corresponding to

[

, Section 5.3]
.
If one is only interested in the quasisplit form

, the

2
The Decomposition of
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}\circ\eta

In
[
Kal19
,
Kal21
]
, Kaletha describes a way
to construct a supercuspidal representation
\pi_{(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})}
of

\underaccent{\bar}{G}
from a tame
F
-non-singular elliptic pair
(\underaccent{\bar}{S},\underaccent{\bar}{\theta})

[
Kal21
, Definition 3.4.1]
. Here
\underaccent{\bar}{S}
is a
maximally unramified elliptic maximal torus and
\underaccent{\bar}{\theta}
is a character of
\underaccent{\bar}{S}(F)
satisfying a certain

\underaccent{\bar}{W}=\{\underaccent{\bar}{f}:\AdT(\underaccent{\bar}{g})(G_{2})\rightarrow V\text{ locally constant }|\underaccent{\bar}{f}(gh)=(\pi\circ\AdT(\underaccent{\bar}{g}))(h)^{-1}\underaccent{\bar}{f}(g)\text{ for all }h\in\AdT(\underaccent{\bar}{g})(G_{1})\}

and

W=\{f:G_{2}\rightarrow V\text{ locally constant }|f(gh)=\pi(h)^{-1}f(g)\text{ for all }h\in G_{1}\},

respectively. Define a linear map
\mathcal{F}
as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:W

\displaystyle\rightarrow\underaccent{\bar}{W}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto f\circ\AdT(\underaccent{\bar}{g}^{-1}).

The map
\mathcal{F}
is bijective as a consequence of
\AdT(\underaccent{\bar}{g}^{-1}):\AdT(\underaccent{\bar}{g})(G_{2})\rightarrow G_{2}
being bijective. It is then an easy computation to verify that
\mathcal{F}
intertwines the representations
\Ind_{\AdT(\underaccent{\bar}{g})(G_{1})}^{\AdT(\underaccent{\bar}{g})(G_{2})}\left(\pi\circ\AdT(\underaccent{\bar}{g}^{-1})\right)
and
\Ind_{G_{1}}^{G_{2}}\circ\AdT(\underaccent{\bar}{g}^{-1})
. ∎

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\mu:H^{\prime}_{2}\rightarrow H_{2}
be a morphism of groups,
H_{1}\subset H_{2}
and
H_{1}^{\prime}\subset H_{2}^{\prime}
subgroups such that
\mu(H_{2}^{\prime})\cap H_{1}=\mu(H_{1}^{\prime})
and
\ker(\mu)\subset H_{1}^{\prime}
. Let
\pi
be a representation of
H_{1}
. Then,

\Ind_{H_{1}^{\prime}}^{H_{2}^{\prime}}(\pi\circ\mu)\simeq\left(\Ind_{\mu(H_{1}^{\prime})}^{\mu(H_{2}^{\prime})}\Res_{\mu(H_{1}^{\prime})}^{H_{1}}\pi\right)\circ\mu.

Proof.

Let
V
denote the vector space on which the representations
\pi
and
\pi\circ\mu
act. Then, the representations
\left(\Ind_{\mu(H_{1}^{\prime})}^{\mu(H_{2}^{\prime})}\Res^{H_{1}}_{\mu(H_{1}^{\prime})}\pi\right)\circ\mu
and
\Ind_{H_{1}^{\prime}}^{H_{2}^{\prime}}(\pi\circ\mu)
act on the vector spaces

W_{\mu}=\{f_{\mu}:\mu(H_{2}^{\prime})\rightarrow V\text{ locally constant }|f_{\mu}(gh)=\pi(h)^{-1}f_{\mu}(g)\text{ for all }h\in\mu(H_{1}^{\prime})\}

and

W=\{f:H_{2}^{\prime}\rightarrow V\text{ locally constant }|f(gh)=\pi\circ\mu(h)^{-1}f(g)\text{ for all }h\in H_{1}^{\prime}\},

respectively. Define a linear map
\mathcal{F}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:W_{\mu}

\displaystyle\rightarrow W

\displaystyle f_{\mu}

\displaystyle\mapsto f_{\mu}\circ\mu.

One sees that the map
\mathcal{F}
is injective, as
\mu:H_{2}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mu(H_{2}^{\prime})
is surjective. Since
\ker\mu\subset H_{1}^{\prime}
, elements of
W
are constant on the cosets of
H_{2}^{\prime}/\ker\mu
, allowing us to define an inverse map and conclude that
\mathcal{F}
is bijective. It is then an easy computation to verify that
T
intertwines the representations
\left(\Ind_{\mu(H_{1}^{\prime})}^{\mu(H_{2}^{\prime})}\Res_{\mu(H_{1}^{\prime})}^{H_{1}}\pi\right)\circ\mu
and
\Ind_{H_{1}^{\prime}}^{H_{2}^{\prime}}(\pi\circ\mu)
.

∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
A,B,C
be subgroups such that
A\subseteq B
. Then

{\raisebox{2.04439pt}{$AC/C$}\left/\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$BC/C$}\right.}\simeq AC/BC\simeq{\raisebox{2.04439pt}{$A$}\left/\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$B(A\cap C)$}\right.}.

Proof.

The first isomorphism comes from the third isomorphism theorem. For the second isomorphism, consider the map

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\rightarrow AC/BC

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto aBC.

This map is clearly surjective. Furthermore, its kernel is given by

\{a\in A:aBC=BC\}=\{a\in A:a\in BC\}=A\cap BC=B(A\cap C).

The conclusion then follows from the first isomorphism theorem.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

Let
A,B,C,\bar{A},\bar{B},\bar{C}
be groups that satisfy the following conditions.
A,C\subseteq B
,
\bar{A},\bar{C}\subseteq\bar{B}
and
\bar{C}\subseteq C
. Furthermore, assume
A
and
\bar{A}
are normal subgroups of
B
and
\bar{B}
, respectively. Let
L
be a set of coset representatives of
A\setminus B/C
and
\mathcal{L}
be a set of coset representatives of
\bar{A}\setminus\bar{B}/\bar{C}
. Write
L\mathcal{L}=\{l\ell:l\in L,\ell\in\mathcal{L}\}
.

1)

If
\bar{B}\subseteq C
,
A\cap\bar{B}\subseteq\bar{A}
and
{\raisebox{2.04439pt}{$C$}\left/\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$(A\cap C)\bar{C}$}\right.}\simeq{\raisebox{2.04439pt}{$\bar{B}$}\left/\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$\bar{A}\bar{C}$}\right.}
, then
L\mathcal{L}
is a set of coset representatives of
A\setminus B/\bar{C}
.

2)

If
\bar{A}\subseteq A
,
A\cap C=\bar{C}
,
A=\bar{B}
and
\bar{A}
is a normal subgroup of
B
, then
L\mathcal{L}
is a set of coset representatives of
\bar{A}\setminus B/C
.

Proof.

Since
A
is normal in
B
, and
\bar{A}
is normal in
\bar{C}
, we note that
A\setminus B/C=B/AC,A\setminus B/\bar{C}=B/A\bar{C}
and
\bar{A}\setminus\bar{B}/\bar{C}=\bar{B}/\bar{A}\bar{C}
.

To prove part 1), we use the normality of
A
, the inclusion
\bar{C}\subseteq C
and the isomorphism
{\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$C$}\left/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$(A\cap C)\bar{C}$}\right.}\simeq{\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$\bar{B}$}\left/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$\bar{A}\bar{C}$}\right.}
, along with the second and third isomorphism theorems to show that

B/AC\simeq{\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$B/A\bar{C}$}\left/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$\bar{B}/\bar{A}\bar{C}$}\right.},

and thus

|B/AC|\cdot|\bar{B}/\bar{A}\bar{C}|=|B/A\bar{C}|.

We then use the inclusions
\bar{B}\subseteq C
and
A\cap\bar{B}\subseteq\bar{A}
to show that the map

\displaystyle\mu:L\times\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\rightarrow B/A\bar{C}

\displaystyle(l,\ell)

\displaystyle\mapsto l\ell A\bar{C}

is injective.

The proof of part 2) follows the exact same strategy as the proof of part 1).

∎

A.2
Virtual Characters Through Quotients

Virtual characters are required in the construction of the depth-zero part of a supercuspidal representation, as recalled in Figure
1
. In this appendix, we derive a result involving the behaviour of virtual characters through central quotients.

A brief summary of virtual characters was provided in
[
Bou21
, Appendix]
. Following the notation therein, we let
G
denote a connected reductive group over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
. Let
\mathrm{Fr}:G\rightarrow G
be a Frobenius map (as per
[
Car93
, Section 1.17]
). Given a maximal torus
T
of
G
which is
\mathrm{Fr}
-stable and an irreducible complex character
\theta
of
T^{\mathrm{Fr}}
, there is a corresponding virtual character
R^{G}_{T,\theta}
which maps
G^{\mathrm{Fr}}
into
\mathbb{C}
. When the underlying group is clear, we omit the superscript
G
and write
R_{T,\theta}
.

One formula for the virtual character is provided by
[
DL76
, Theorem 4.2]
and is described as follows. Given
g\in G^{\mathrm{Fr}}
, we let
g=su=us
be its Jordan decomposition. Then

\displaystyle R_{T,\theta}(g)=\frac{1}{|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in G^{\mathrm{Fr}}\\
x^{-1}sx\in T^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta(x^{-1}sx)R_{xTx^{-1},1}^{C_{G}(s)^{\circ}}(u).

It is known that virtual characters remain constant under isomorphism, and their behaviour under particular types of restrictions is given by
[
Bou21
, Theorem A.2]
. At the time of writing, we have not found a reference that discusses how virtual characters behave via central quotients, so we describe it with the following theorem.

Theorem A.5
.

Let
Z
be a central subgroup of
G
which is
\mathrm{Fr}
-stable, and consider the quotient map
q:G\rightarrow{G}_{Z}
, where
{G}_{Z}=G/Z
. Let
T
be a maximal torus of
G
which is
\mathrm{Fr}
-stable and let
T_{Z}=q(T)
. Let
\theta
and
\theta_{Z}
be irreducible complex characters of
T^{\mathrm{Fr}}
and
T_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}
, respectively, satisfying
\theta=\theta_{Z}\circ q
. Then
R_{T,\theta}=R_{T_{Z},\theta_{Z}}\circ q
.

In order to prove this theorem, we will require the following lemma on cohomology.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
Z,{G}_{Z}
and
q
be as in Theorem
A.5
. Let
H
be a connected subgroup of
G
which contains
Z
and is
\mathrm{Fr}
-stable. Then
H^{1}(F,Z)\simeq q(H)^{\mathrm{Fr}}/\left(H^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\right)
. In particular, if
T
is a maximal torus of
G
, then there exists a set of coset representatives of
{G}_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}/(G^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}})
that is contained in
q(T)^{\mathrm{Fr}}
.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence

1\rightarrow Z\rightarrow H\rightarrow q(H)\rightarrow 1.

Given that
H
is connected, Lang’s theorem implies
H^{1}(\mathrm{Fr},H)=1
, giving us the following exact cohomology sequence
[
Spr09
, Theorem 12.3.4]
:

1\rightarrow Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\rightarrow H^{\mathrm{Fr}}\rightarrow q(H)^{\mathrm{Fr}}\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathrm{Fr},Z)\rightarrow 1.

The exactness of the sequence implies
H^{1}(\mathrm{Fr},Z)\simeq q(H)^{\mathrm{Fr}}/\left(H^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\right)
.

For the second part, let
D
be a set of coset representatives of
{G}_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}/\left(G^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\right)
. By what precedes above, one has
H^{1}(\mathrm{Fr},Z)\simeq{G}_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}/\left(G^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\right)
and
H^{1}(\mathrm{Fr},Z)\simeq q(T)^{\mathrm{Fr}}/\left(T^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\right)
. From
[
Spr09
, Section 12.3.3]
, one sees from the definitions of the maps
{G}_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathrm{Fr},Z)
and
q(T)^{\mathrm{Fr}}\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathrm{Fr},Z)
that the elements of
D
can be chosen from
q(T)^{\mathrm{Fr}}
without loss of generality.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A.5
.

Let
g\in G^{\mathrm{Fr}}
and let
g=su=us
be its Jordan decomposition. We must show that
R_{T,\theta}(g)=R_{T_{Z},\theta_{Z}}\circ q(g)
. We start by studying the expression
R_{T,\theta}(g)
.

Let
C
be a set of coset representatives of
G^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}
. Then,
G^{\mathrm{Fr}}=CZ^{\mathrm{Fr}}
. We thus obtain the following chain of equalities.

\displaystyle R_{T,\theta}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in G^{\mathrm{Fr}}\\
x^{-1}sx\in T^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta(x^{-1}sx)R_{xTx^{-1},1}^{C_{G}(s)^{\circ}}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in C\\
z\in Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\\
(cz)^{-1}s(cz)\in T^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta((cz)^{-1}s(cz))R_{(cz)T(cz)^{-1},1}^{C_{G}(s)^{\circ}}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{|Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}{|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in C\\
c^{-1}sc\in T^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta(c^{-1}sc)R_{cTc^{-1},1}^{C_{G}(s)^{\circ}}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{|Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}{|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in C\\
c^{-1}sc\in T^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta_{Z}(q(c)^{-1}q(s)q(c))R_{cTc^{-1},1}^{C_{G}(s)^{\circ}}(u).

We claim that
R_{cTc^{-1},1}^{C_{G}(s)^{\circ}}(u)=R_{q(c)T_{Z}q(c)^{-1},1}^{C_{G_{z}}(q(s))^{\circ}}(q(u))
. Indeed, one sees from
[
Car93
, Theorem 3.5.3]
that
q(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})=C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ}
. Given that
Z
is in the centre of
C_{G}(s)^{\circ}
, it follows that
C_{G}(s)^{\circ}
and
C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ}
have the same adjoint group, and the adjoint map of
C_{G}(s)^{\circ}
is the composition of
q
and the adjoint map of
C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ}
. According to
[
DL76
, Formula 4.1.1]
, the virtual character remains constant under the adjoint map. Thus, it follows that
R_{cTc^{-1},1}^{C_{G}(s)^{\circ}}(u)=R_{q(c)T_{Z}q(c)^{-1},1}^{C_{G_{z}}(q(s))^{\circ}}(q(u))
as claimed. Returning to our above computation, it then follows that

\displaystyle R_{T,\theta}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{|Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}{|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in C\\
c^{-1}sc\in T^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta_{Z}(q(c)^{-1}q(s)q(c))R_{q(c)T_{Z}q(c)^{-1},1}^{C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ}}(q(u))

\displaystyle=\frac{|Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}{|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{c}\in G^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\\
\bar{c}^{-1}q(s)\bar{c}\in T^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta_{Z}(\bar{c}^{-1}q(s)\bar{c})R_{\bar{c}T_{Z}\bar{c}^{-1},1}^{C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ}}(q(u)).

Next, we look at the expression for
R_{T_{Z},\theta_{Z}}\circ q(g)
. By
[
Hum75
, Theorem 15.4]
,
q(g)=q(s)q(u)=q(u)q(s)
is the Jordan decomposition of
q(g)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle R_{T_{Z},\theta_{Z}}\circ q(g)=\frac{1}{|(C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{x}\in{G}_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}\\
\bar{x}^{-1}q(s)\bar{x}\in T_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta_{Z}(\bar{x}^{-1}q(s)\bar{x})R_{\bar{x}T_{Z}\bar{x}^{-1},1}^{C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ}}(q(u)).

Now, let
D
be a set of coset representatives of
{G}_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}/(G^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}})
. By Lemma
A.6
, we may assume that
D\subset T_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}
. Furthermore, by setting
H=C_{G}(s)^{\circ}
in Lemma
A.6
, we have
H^{1}(\mathrm{Fr},Z)\simeq(q(C_{G}(s)^{\circ}))^{\mathrm{Fr}}/((C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}})
. This isomorphism then implies

\frac{|H^{1}(\mathrm{Fr},Z)|\cdot|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}{|Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}=|(C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|.

This allows us to rewrite the formula of
R_{T_{Z},\theta_{Z}}(q(g))
as follows.

\displaystyle R_{T_{Z},\theta_{Z}}\circ q(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{|Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}{|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\frac{1}{|H^{1}(\mathrm{Fr},Z)|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in D\\
\bar{c}\in G^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\\
(\bar{c}d)^{-1}q(s)(\bar{c}d)\in T_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta_{Z}((\bar{c}d)^{-1}q(s)(\bar{c}d))R_{(\bar{c}d)T_{Z}(\bar{c}d)^{-1},1}^{C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ}}(q(u))

\displaystyle=\frac{|Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}{|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{c}\in G^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\\
\bar{c}^{-1}q(s)\bar{c}\in T_{Z}^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta_{Z}(\bar{c}^{-1}q(s)\bar{c})R_{\bar{c}T_{Z}\bar{c}^{-1},1}^{C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ}}(q(u))

\displaystyle=\frac{|Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}{|(C_{G}(s)^{\circ})^{\mathrm{Fr}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{c}\in G^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\\
\bar{c}^{-1}q(s)\bar{c}\in T^{\mathrm{Fr}}/Z^{\mathrm{Fr}}\end{subarray}}\theta_{Z}(\bar{c}^{-1}q(s)\bar{c})R_{\bar{c}T_{Z}\bar{c}^{-1},1}^{C_{{G}_{Z}}(q(s))^{\circ}}(q(u)).

This last expression is precisely the same expression we had for
R_{T,\theta}(g)
above. Thus
R_{T,\theta}=R_{T_{Z},\theta_{Z}}\circ q
.
∎

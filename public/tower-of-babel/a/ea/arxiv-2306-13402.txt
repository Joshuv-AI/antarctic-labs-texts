Hypocoercivity for infinite-dimensional non-linear degenerate stochastic differential equations with multiplicative noise
Alexander Bertram, Benedikt Eisenhuth, Martin Grothaus

Abstract
We analyze infinite-dimensional non-linear degenerate stochastic differential equations with multiplicative noise. First, essential m-dissipativity of their associated Kolmogorov backward generators on L 2 ​ ( μ Φ ) L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi}) defined on smooth finitely based functions is established. Here Φ \Phi is an appropriate potential and μ Φ \mu^{\Phi} is the invariant measure with density e − Φ e^{-\Phi} w.r.t. an infinite-dimensional non-degenerate Gaussian measure. Second, we use resolvent methods to construct corresponding right processes with infinite lifetime, solving the martingale problem for the Kolmogorov backward generators. They provide weak solutions, with weakly continuous paths, to the non-linear degenerate stochastic partial differential equations. Moreover, we identify the transition semigroup of such a process with the strongly continuous contraction semigroup ( T t ) t ≥ 0 (T_{t})_{t\geq 0} generated by the corresponding Kolmogorov backwards generator. Afterwards, we apply a refinement of the abstract Hilbert space hypocoercivity method, developed by Dolbeaut, Mouhot and Schmeiser, to derive hypocoercivity of ( T t ) t ≥ 0 (T_{t})_{t\geq 0} . I.e. we take domain issues into account and use the formulation in the Kolmogorov backwards setting worked out by Grothaus and Stilgenbauer. The method enables us to explicitly compute the constants determining the exponential convergence rate to equilibrium of ( T t ) t ≥ 0 (T_{t})_{t\geq 0} . The identification between ( T t ) t ≥ 0 (T_{t})_{t\geq 0} and the transition semigroup of the process yields exponential ergodicity of the latter. Finally, we apply our results to second order in time stochastic reaction-diffusion and Cahn-Hilliard type equations with multiplicative noise. More generally, we analyze corresponding couplings of infinite-dimensional deterministic with stochastic differential equations.

Preliminaries

Let
H
be a real separable Hilbert space with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}
,
\mathcal{B}(H)
the corresponding Borel-
\sigma

\mu

We denote the set of all linear bounded operators on

\mathcal{L}(H)
, the subset of positive semi-definite symmetric operators by
\mathcal{L}^{+}(H)
, and the set of operators additionally being of trace class by
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{+}(H)
.
The set of Hilbert-Schmidt operators on

is denoted by
\mathcal{L}_{2}(H)
. If
\tilde{H}
is another Hilbert space we denote by
\mathcal{L}(H,\tilde{H})

the set of all linear bounded operators from

to
\tilde{H}
.

The covariance operator corresponding to
\mu

and is an element of
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{+}(H)

\mu
is non-degenerate. Since it is symmetric and of trace class, there is a complete orthonormal basis
B_{H}=(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
H
consisting of eigenvectors of
Q
with corresponding positive eigenvalues
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})

\ell^{\tau}(\mathbb{N})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}\mid\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lvert a_{n}\rvert^{\tau}<\infty\}
for

Q
, there exists an inverse operator defined on

that satisfies
Q^{-1}e_{n}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}e_{n}
, for all

. Moreover, we can define
Q^{\theta}

on

Definition 2.1
.

For each

, define
H_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{k}(B_{H},n)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{f:H\to\mathbb{R}\mid f=\varphi\circ p_{n}\text{ for some }\varphi\in C_{b}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{k}(B_{H})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{k}(B_{H},n).

g\in W^{1,q}_{Q^{\theta}}(H;\mu)
.
Then

\int_{H}\partial_{i}fg\,\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int_{H}f\partial_{i}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{H}(x,Q^{-1}e_{i})_{H}fg\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

Definition 2.8
.

Suppose
\Phi:H\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
is measurable and bounded from below, s.t.
\int_{H}e^{-\Phi}\,\mathrm{d}\mu>0
. For such
\Phi
we consider the measure
\mu^{\Phi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{\int_{H}e^{-\Phi}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}e^{-\Phi}\mu
and set
\mu^{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mu
as well as

\mu^{\Phi}(f)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{H}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}\quad\text{for}\quad f\in L^{1}(H;\mu^{\Phi}).

As in
(
Lunardi 2014
, Chapter 2.2)
we can extend the integration by parts formula for measures of type
\mu^{\Phi}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\theta\in[0,\infty)
and consider a potential
\Phi:H\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
which is bounded from below and in

. Then for
f,g\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{1}(B_{H})
and

, it holds the integration by parts formula

\int_{H}\partial_{i}fg\,\mu^{\Phi}=-\int_{H}f\partial_{i}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}+\int_{H}(x,Q^{-1}e_{i})_{U}fg\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}+\int_{H}\partial_{i}\Phi fg\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}.

Assumption

(
\Phi 0
)
.

There exist two sequences
(\Phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\Phi_{(m,n)})_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}
of convex functions from
H
to
\mathbb{R}
such that for
\mu
-almost all
x\in H
and for all
(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}

1.

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\Phi_{(m,n)}(x)=\Phi_{n}(x)
and
-\infty<\inf_{y\in H}\Phi(y)\leq\Phi_{(m,n)}(x)
as well as

\lim_{(m,n)\rightarrow\infty}\Phi_{(m,n)}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\Phi_{(m,n)}(x)=\Phi(x)
.

2.

\Phi_{(m,n)}
is Fréchet-differentiable with Lipschitz continuous gradient.

3.

If additionally
\Phi\in W^{1,2}_{Q^{\theta}}(H,\mu)
for some
\theta\in[0,\infty)
, then for all

\lim_{(m,n)\rightarrow 0}\lVert(Q^{\theta}D\Phi_{(n,m)}-Q^{\theta}D\Phi,e_{i})_{H}\rVert_{L^{2}(H;\mu)}=0.

Remark 2.10
.

Suppose Item (i) of Assumption
Assumption

(
\Phi 0
)
.
holds true. Then
e^{-\Phi_{(m,n)}}
and
e^{-\Phi_{n}}
are bounded by
e^{-\inf_{y\in H}\Phi(y)}
. Since we also have pointwise convergence we obtain by the theorem of dominated convergence

\lim_{(m,n)\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{\Phi_{(m,n)}}(f)=\mu^{\Phi}(f)\quad\text{for all}\quad f\in L^{1}(H;\mu^{\Phi}).

The next lemma provides a general Poincaré inequality for
\mu^{\Phi}
. The result is essential for our further analysis.

Lemma 2.11
.

Suppose
\Phi=\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}
, where
\Phi_{1}:H\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
fulfills Item (i) and (ii) from Assumption
Assumption

(
\Phi 0
)
.
and
\Phi_{2}:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is measurable with
\lVert\Phi_{2}\rVert_{osc}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sup_{x\in H}\Phi_{2}(x)-\inf_{x\in H}\Phi_{2}(x)<\infty
. Then

\int_{H}(QDf,Df)_{H}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{e^{\lVert\Phi_{2}\rVert_{osc}}}\int_{H}\left(f-\mu^{\Phi}(f)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi},\quad\text{for all}\quad f\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{H}).

Proof.

For
\Phi_{2}=0
we approximate
\Phi
by
(\Phi_{(m,n)})_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}
and use
(
Eisenhuth and Grothaus 2021
, Proposition 3.12)
to get the desired Poincaré inequality for
\mu^{\Phi_{(m,n)}}
with Poincaré constant
\lambda_{1}
independent of
(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
. The observation of the previous remark shows the promised Poincaré inequality for the measure
\mu^{\Phi_{1}}
. Using that
\int_{H}\left(f-\mu^{\Phi}(f)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}\leq\int_{H}\left(f-c\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}
for all

we can conclude with the Poincaré inequality for

\displaystyle\int_{H}\left(f-\mu^{\Phi}(f)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{H}\left(f-\mu^{\Phi_{1}}(f)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\inf_{x\in H}\Phi_{2}(x)}\frac{\int_{H}e^{-\Phi_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\int_{H}e^{-\Phi}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\int_{H}\left(f-\mu^{\Phi_{1}}(f)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{-\inf_{x\in H}\Phi_{2}(x)}}{\lambda_{1}}\frac{\int_{H}e^{-\Phi_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\int_{H}e^{-\Phi}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\int_{H}(QDf,Df)_{H}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{\lVert\Phi_{2}\rVert_{osc}}}{\lambda_{1}}\int_{H}(QDf,Df)_{H}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}.

∎

Let
U
and
V

(\cdot,\cdot)_{U}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}
, respectively. Denote by
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
two non-degenerate Gaussian measures
U
and
V
, respectively.
Let
Q_{i}
be the covariance operator of
\mu_{i}
,
i=1,2
with corresponding basis of eigenvectors
B_{U}=(d_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
B_{V}=(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and positive eigenvalues
(\lambda_{1,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\lambda_{2,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, respectively. W.l.o.g. we assume that
(\lambda_{1,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\lambda_{2,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are decreasing to zero. The respective orthogonal projections to the induced subspaces
U_{n}
and
V_{n}
are denoted by
P_{n}^{U}
,
p_{n}^{U}
,
P_{n}^{V}
and
p_{n}^{V}
, respectively. Furthermore we fix a potential
\Phi:U\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
and assume that for
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
the following assumption is valid.

Assumption

(
\Phi^{\alpha}
)
.

The potential
\Phi:U\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
fulfills
\int_{U}e^{-\Phi}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=1
, is bounded from below by zero, and such that
\Phi\in W_{Q_{1}^{\alpha}}^{1,2}(U;\mu_{1})
.

Note that all considerations below also work for potentials which are bounded from below and in
W_{Q_{1}^{\alpha}}^{1,2}(U;\mu_{1})
.

Definition 3.1
.

Define the real separable Hilbert space
W=U\times V
with the canonically defined inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{W}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\cdot,\cdot)_{U}+(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}
. Moreover, define the measure
\mu_{1}^{\Phi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=e^{-\Phi}\mu_{1}
on
\mathcal{B}(U)
and let
\mu^{\Phi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mu_{1}^{\Phi}\otimes\mu_{2}
be the product measure on
\mathcal{B}(W)=\mathcal{B}(U)\otimes\mathcal{B}(V)
.
We set
\mu=\mu^{0}
. Due to
(
Prato 2006
, Theorem 1.12)
,
\mu
is a centered Gaussian measure with covariance
Q
defined by
Q(u,v)=(Q_{1}u,Q_{2}v)
.
We set

B_{W}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{(d_{n},0)\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{(0,e_{n})\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq W

as well as

\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{k}(B_{W},n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{f:W\to\mathbb{R}\mid f(u,v)=\varphi(p_{n}^{U}(u),p_{n}^{V}(v))\text{ with }\varphi\in C_{b}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\}

for
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. The space
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{k}(B_{W})
is defined analogously to
2.1
. Note that
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W})
is dense in
L^{2}(W;\mu^{\Phi})
.

Furthermore we set
\mu^{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mu_{1}^{n}\otimes\mu_{2}^{n}
, with
\mu_{i}
being centered Gaussian measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with diagonal covariance matrices
Q_{i,n}
.

Definition 3.2
.

For Fréchet-differentiable
f:W\to\mathbb{R}
and all
w=(u,v)\in W
set

\displaystyle D_{1}f(w)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(Df(w),(d_{n},0))_{W}d_{n}\in U,\quad\partial_{i,1}f(w)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(D_{1}f(w),d_{i})_{U}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle D_{2}f(w)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(Df(w),(0,e_{n}))_{W}e_{n}\in V,\quad\partial_{i,2}f(w)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(D_{2}f(w),e_{i})_{V}.

The second order derivatives and partial derivatives are defined analogously.

Now we can introduce the differential operators
S
,
A^{\Phi}
and
L^{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W})
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
K_{12}\in\mathcal{L}(U;V)
and set
K_{21}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=K_{12}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(V;U)
.
Let the map
K_{22}:V\to\mathcal{L}^{+}(V)
be Fréchet-differentiable with
DK_{22}(v)\in\mathcal{L}(V;\mathcal{L}(V))
and partial derivatives
\partial_{i}K_{22}(v)=(DK_{22}(v))(e_{i})\in\mathcal{L}(V)
for each
v\in V
. Moreover, assume there is a strictly increasing sequence
(m_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that for each
n\leq m_{k}
, it holds that

\displaystyle K_{12}(U_{n})\subseteq V_{m_{k}},\quad K_{21}(V_{n})\subseteq U_{m_{k}},\quad K_{22}(v)(V_{n})\subseteq V_{m_{k}}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle K_{22}(v)|_{V_{n}}=K_{22}(P_{m_{k}}^{V}(v))|_{V_{n}}\quad\text{ for all }v\in V.

Moreover assume that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a constant
M_{k}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in V_{m_{k}}}\|K_{22}(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}(V_{m_{k}})}

\displaystyle\leq M_{k}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\|\partial_{i}K_{22}(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}(V_{m_{k}})}

\displaystyle\leq M_{k}(1+\|v\|_{V_{m_{k}}})\quad\text{ for all }v\in V_{m_{k}},1\leq i\leq m_{k}.

In the following we set
m^{K}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\min_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{m_{k}:m_{k}\geq n\}
. Then define the differential operators
(S,\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W}))
and
(A^{\Phi},\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W}))
on
L^{2}(W;\mu^{\Phi})
by

\displaystyle Sf(u,v)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=

\displaystyle\tr\left[K_{22}(v)\circ D_{2}^{2}f(u,v)\right]+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(\partial_{j}K_{22}(v)D_{2}f(u,v),e_{j})_{V}

\displaystyle-(v,Q_{2}^{-1}K_{22}(v)D_{2}f(u,v))_{V}

and

\displaystyle A^{\Phi}f(u,v)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(u,Q_{1}^{-1}K_{21}D_{2}f(u,v))_{U}+(D\Phi(u),K_{21}D_{2}f(u,v))_{U}

\displaystyle-(v,Q_{2}^{-1}K_{12}D_{1}f(u,v))_{V},

respectively.
Finally,
(L^{\Phi},\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W}))
is defined via
L^{\Phi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=S-A^{\Phi}
. We set
L\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=L^{0}
.

Remark 3.4
.

The invariance assumptions made on
K_{12}
,
K_{21}
and
K_{22}
ensure that
S
and
A^{\Phi}
are well-defined on
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W})
. Indeed, let
f\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W},n)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
with corresponding
\varphi\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By trivially extending
\varphi
if necessary, we can assume
m^{K}(n)=n
. Then
Q_{1}^{-1}K_{21}D_{2}f(u,v)\in U_{n}
,
Q_{2}^{-1}K_{12}D_{1}f(u,v)\in V_{n}
, and
Q_{2}^{-1}K_{22}(v)D_{2}f(u,v)\in V_{n}
for all
(u,v)\in W
. Therefore, these maps are uniformly bounded in
(u,v)
due to uniform boundedness of
K_{22}:V_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(V_{n})
and the fact that all derivatives of
f
are bounded. Moreover by these invariance properties we can interpret

(D\Phi(u),K_{21}D_{2}f(u,v))_{U}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\Phi(u)(d_{i},K_{21}D_{2}f(u,v))_{U}

even though we don’t know if
\Phi\in W^{1,2}(U;\mu_{1})
.
By the observation that all sums appearing in the definition of
S
are finite, the fact that
\partial_{i}\Phi\in L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi})
, as well as
Corollary

2.3
, it follows that
Sf(u,v)=Sf(P_{n}^{U}u,P_{n}^{V}v)
,
A^{\Phi}f(u,v)=A^{0}f(P_{n}^{U}u,P_{n}^{V})+(D\Phi(u),K_{21}D_{2}f(P_{n}^{U}u,P_{n}^{V}))_{U}
and
Sf,\,A^{\Phi}f\in L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi})
. Since
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{U}^{2}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{V}^{2}
are integrable with respect to
\mu
, the arguments above also show that
Lf
is finitely based and in
L^{2}(\mu)
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W})

Lemma 3.5
.

On
L^{2}(W;\mu^{\Phi})
,
(S,\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W}))
is symmetric and negative semi-definite,
(A^{\Phi},\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W}))
is antisymmetric and therefore
(L^{\Phi},\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W}))
is dissipative. For
f,g\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W})
, we have the representation

(L^{\Phi}f,g)_{L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi})}=-\int_{W}(D_{2}f,K_{22}D_{2}g)_{V}-(D_{1}f,K_{21}D_{2}g)_{U}+(D_{2}f,K_{12}D_{1}g)_{V}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W})
. As in
Remark

3.4
, we can assume
f,g\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W},n)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n=m^{K}(n)
. For any
(u,v)\in W
, it holds that

Q_{1}^{-1}K_{21}D_{2}f(u,v)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{k,2}f(u,v)Q_{1}^{-1}K_{21}e_{k}=\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}\partial_{k,2}f(u,v)(K_{21}e_{k},d_{\ell})_{U}Q_{1}^{-1}d_{\ell}.

Using
2.9
, we obtain

\int_{W}\left((u,Q_{1}^{-1}d_{\ell})_{U}+\partial_{l}\Phi\right)\partial_{k,2}fg\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}=\int_{W}(g\partial_{\ell,1}\partial_{k,2}f+\partial_{k,2}f\partial_{\ell,1}g)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi},

which shows that

\displaystyle\left((u,Q_{1}^{-1}K_{21}D_{2}f)_{U}+(D\Phi(u),K_{21}D_{2}f)_{U},g\right)_{L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi})}

\displaystyle=\int_{W}\left(K_{21}D_{2}f,D_{1}g\right)_{U}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}+\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}(K_{21}e_{k},d_{\ell})_{U}(g,\partial_{\ell,1}\partial_{k,2}f)_{L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi})}.

Similarly, it holds that

\displaystyle\quad\left((v,Q_{2}^{-1}K_{12}D_{1}f)_{V},g\right)_{L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi})}

\displaystyle=\int_{W}\left(K_{12}D_{1}f,D_{2}g\right)_{V}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}(K_{12}d_{\ell},e_{k})_{V}(g,\partial_{k,2}\partial_{\ell,1}f)_{L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi})}.

K_{12}^{*}=K_{21}
implies that

(A^{\Phi}f,g)_{L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi})}=\int_{W}(D_{2}f,K_{12}D_{1}g)_{V}-(D_{1}f,K_{21}D_{2}g)_{U}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}.

In particular
(A^{\Phi}f,f)_{L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi})}=0
. Now consider the operator
S
. As before, we have

Q_{2}^{-1}K_{22}(v)D_{2}f(u,v)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{i,2}f(u,v)(K_{22}(v)e_{i},e_{j})_{V}Q_{2}^{-1}e_{j}

for all
(u,v)\in W
. Due to the assumptions on
K_{22}
, the maps

(u,v)\mapsto\partial_{i,2}f(u,v)(K_{22}(v)e_{i},e_{j})_{V}

are elements of
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{1}(B_{W},n)
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
, see
Remark

3.4
. Therefore, the application of the integration by parts formula is possible and yields

\displaystyle\int_{W}(v,Q_{2}^{-1}e_{j})\partial_{i,2}f(K_{22}e_{i},e_{j})_{V}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\int_{W}\partial_{j,2}\partial_{i,2}f(K_{22}e_{i},e_{j})_{V}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}

\displaystyle+\int_{W}\partial_{i,2}f(\partial_{j}K_{22}e_{i},e_{j})_{V}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}

\displaystyle+\int_{W}\partial_{i,2}f(K_{22}e_{i},e_{j})_{V}\partial_{j,2}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}.

Summing up over
i
and
j
, we get

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{W}\partial_{i,2}f(K_{22}e_{i},e_{j})_{V}\partial_{j,2}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}=\int_{W}(K_{22}D_{2}f,D_{2}g)_{V}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}

and

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{W}\partial_{j,2}\partial_{i,2}f(K_{22}e_{i},e_{j})_{V}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}=\int_{W}\tr\left[K_{22}D_{2}^{2}f\right]g\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}

due to pointwise symmetry of
K_{22}
. Therefore, we indeed get

(Sf,g)_{L^{2}(\mu^{\Phi})}=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{W}\partial_{i,2}f(K_{22}e_{i},e_{j})_{V}\partial_{j,2}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}=-\int_{W}(D_{2}f,K_{22}D_{2}g)_{V}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\Phi}.

Hence,
S
is symmetric and negative semi-definite since
K_{22}
is positive semi-definite.
Hence, all three operators are dissipative on
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W})
.
∎

4
Essential m-dissipativity

In the first part of this section we assume
\Phi=0
and prove that
(L,\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W}))
is essentially m-dissipative on
L^{2}(W;\mu)
. Since dissipativity was shown in
3.5
, it remains to show that
(\operatorname{Id}-L)(\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W}))
is dense in
L^{2}(W;\mu)
.
Since
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W})
is dense in
L^{2}(W;\mu)
, it suffices to approximate all such functions. The main idea here is to interpret
L
for all
m_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, as an operator on the finite-dimensional subspace determined by
\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W},m_{k})
, which is possible due to
Remark

3.4
.
In that case, we can apply the recent finite-dimensional m-dissipativity result from
(
Bertram and Grothaus 2023
, Thm. 1.1)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n=m^{K}(n)
. Then we define

\displaystyle K_{12,n}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=((K_{12}d_{i},e_{j})_{V})_{ij},\quad K_{21,n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(K_{12,n})^{*},\quad

\displaystyle\Sigma_{n}(y)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left(\left(K_{22}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{k}e_{k}\right)e_{i},e_{j}\right)_{V}\right)_{ij}\quad\text{ for all }y=(y_{1},...,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and denote the entry of
\Sigma_{n}
at position
i,j
by
a_{ij,n}
.

Moreover, define the operators
S_{n}
,
A_{n}
and
L_{n}
on
L^{2}(\mu^{n})
with domain
C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\displaystyle S_{n}f(x,y)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\tr[\Sigma_{n}D_{2}^{2}f](x,y)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}a_{ij}(y)\partial_{i,2}f(x,y)-\langle\Sigma_{n}(y)Q_{2,n}^{-1}y,D_{2}f(x,y)\rangle,

\displaystyle A_{n}f(x,y)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\langle K_{12,n}Q_{1,n}^{-1}x,D_{2}f(x,y)\rangle-\langle K_{21,n}Q_{2,n}^{-1}y,D_{1}f(x,y)\rangle\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle L_{n}f

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(S_{n}-A_{n})f,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\lvert\cdot\rvert
denotes the Euclidean inner product and norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively.

Note that these definitions coincide with the structure of operators considered in
Bertram and Grothaus 2023
, with the choices
\Theta(x)=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,Q_{1,n}^{-1}x\rangle
and
\Psi(y)=\frac{1}{2}\langle y,Q_{2,n}^{-1}y\rangle
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}C_{b}^{\infty}(B_{W},n)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n=m^{K}(n)
, with
f(u,v)=\varphi(p_{n}^{U}u,p_{n}^{V}v)
for
\varphi\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
Sf(u,v)=S_{n}\varphi(p_{n}^{U}u,p_{n}^{V}v)
, and analogue statements hold for
A
and
L
.

Proof.

Follows directly from
Remark

3.4
together with
\nabla\Theta(x)=Q_{1,n}^{-1}x
and
\nabla\Psi(y)=Q_{2,n}^{-1}y
.

Proof.

By the prior considerations,

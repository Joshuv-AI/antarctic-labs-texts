1
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a smooth, bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N},

N\geq 2.
For

p>N
and
1\leq q<\infty
let

\lambda_{p,q}:=\inf\left\{\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}:u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\text{ \ and \ }\left\|u\right\|_{q}=1\right\}

(1.1)

be the best Sobolev constant of the embedding
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega).
Here and in what follows
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{r}
denotes the standard norm of the Lebesgue space

L^{r}(\Omega),

1\leq r\leq\infty,
and
\left\|\nabla\cdot\right\|_{p}
denotes the standard norm of the Sobolev space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

As it is well known,
\lambda_{p,q}
is in fact a minimum: there exists a
function
u_{p,q}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\left\|u_{p,q}\right\|_{q}=1\text{ \ and \ }\lambda_{p,q}=\left\|\nabla u_{p,q}\right\|_{p}^{p}.

Moreover,
u_{p,q}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and does not chance sign in

\Omega.
Throughout this paper,
u_{p,q}
will denote a positive minimizer in
(
1.1
). Such a function will be referred as a
Sobolev extremal
function corresponding to
\lambda_{p,q}
. As a consequence of its minimizing
property
u_{p,q}
is a weak solution to the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda_{p,q}\left|u\right|^{q-2}u&\text{{in}}&\Omega\\
u=0&\text{{on}}&\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
\Delta_{p}u:=\operatorname{div}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)
is the
p
-Laplacian operator.

The Sobolev extremal function
u_{p,q}
is the only positive minimizer in
(
1.1
) if
1\leq q\leq p,
but in general this uniqueness property does
not hold if
p<q<\infty.
For this matter we refer to the paper
[
4
]

by Brasco and Lindgren and references therein.

When
q\not=p
the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\Delta_{p}v=\lambda\left|v\right|^{q-2}v&\text{{in}}&\Omega\\
v=0&\text{{on}}&\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

admits at least one positive weak solution for each
\lambda>0.

If
1\leq q<p
the problem (
1.3
) has a unique positive solution

v_{p,q}
which is given by the expression

v_{p,q}=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p,q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-q}}u_{p,q}.

(1.4)

Furthermore,
v_{p,q}
minimizes globally (i.e. on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
) the

energy functional

E_{\lambda}(v):=\frac{1}{p}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{\lambda}{q}\left\|v\right\|_{q}^{q}.

If
q>p
the problem (
1.3
) might, in principle, have multiple positive
weak solutions for each
\lambda>0.
Among such solutions, those in which the
energy functional reaches the lowest value are known as
least energy
solutions.
They are characterized as the positive minimizers of the energy
functional on the Nehari manifold

N_{\lambda}:=\left\{v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}:\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{p}^{p}=\lambda\left\|v\right\|_{q}^{q}\right\}.

An interesting fact is that least energy solutions can also be written in the
form (
1.4
) for some Sobolev extremal function
u_{p,q}:

v_{p,q}=\left(\frac{\lambda_{p,q}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-p}}u_{p,q}.

(1.5)

When
q=p
(
1.3
) becomes the homogeneous Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\Delta_{p}v=\lambda\left|v\right|^{p-2}v&\text{{in}}&\Omega\\
v=0&\text{{on}}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

which is known as the eigenvalue problem of the Dirichlet
p
-Laplacian. In
this context,
\lambda_{p,p}
is known as the first eigenvalue since it is the
least value of
\lambda
for which (
1.6
) has a nontrivial weak
solution. Moreover, (
1.6
) has a positive weak solution
v_{p,p}
if and
only if
\lambda=\lambda_{p,p}
and
v_{p,p}=ku_{p,p},
for some positive
constant
k.

In
[
14
]
, Juutinen, Lindqvist and Manfredi studied the asymptotic
behavior, as
p\rightarrow\infty,
of the pair
(\lambda_{p,p},u_{p,p}).
They
first deduced that the infimum

\Lambda_{\infty}:=\inf\left\{\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}:u\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\text{ \ and \ }\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}=1\right\}

(1.7)

is achieved by
d_{\Omega}/\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty},
where

d_{\Omega}
denotes the distance function to the boundary of
\Omega
:

d_{\Omega}(x):=\inf_{y\in\partial\Omega}\left|x-y\right|,\quad x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Thus,

\Lambda_{\infty}=\left\|\nabla(d_{\Omega}/\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty})\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\nabla d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}/\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}.

(We recall that
d_{\Omega}\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
\left|\nabla d_{\Omega}\right|=1
a.e. in
\Omega.

Moreover,
\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}
is the inradius of

\Omega,
i.e. the radius of the greatest ball inscribed in
\Omega.
)

Still in
[
14
]
, Juutinen, Lindqvist and Manfredi, showed that

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}(\lambda_{p,p})^{1/p}=\Lambda_{\infty}

and proved that any sequence
u_{p_{n},p_{n}},
with
p_{n}\rightarrow\infty,

admits a subsequence converging uniformly in
\overline{\Omega}
to a function

u_{\infty}\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
which is a
positive minimizer in (
1.7
), that is,

u_{\infty}>0\text{ in }\Omega,\text{ \ }\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}=1,\text{ \ and \ }\Lambda_{\infty}=\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}.

In addition, they proved that
u_{\infty}
is a viscosity solution to the
Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}\min\left\{\left|\nabla u\right|-\Lambda_{\infty}u,-\Delta_{\infty}u\right\}=0&\mathrm{in}&\Omega\\
u=0&\mathrm{on}&\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

where

\Delta_{\infty}u:={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{N}}u_{x_{i}}u_{x_{j}}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}

denotes the infinity Laplacian.

These results were independently obtained by Fukagai, Ito and Narukawa in

[
12
]
, where the asymptotic behavior (as
p\rightarrow\infty
) of the
higher eigenvalues of the Dirichlet
p
-Laplacian was also studied.

The value
\Lambda_{\infty}=\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}

has been referred in the literature as the first eigenvalue of the infinity
Laplacian and the Dirichlet problem (taken in the viscosity sense)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}\min\left\{\left|\nabla u\right|-\Lambda u,-\Delta_{\infty}u\right\}=0&\mathrm{in}&\Omega\\
u=0&\mathrm{on}&\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

has been referred as the eigenvalue problem for the infinity Laplacian. Thus,
the limit function
u_{\infty}
obtained in
[
12
]
and
[
14
]
is a
first eigenfunction of the infinity Laplacian.

Now, let us consider the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\Delta_{p}v=\mu_{p}\left|v\right|^{q(p)-2}v&\mathrm{in}&\Omega\\
v=0&\mathrm{on}&\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.9)

where
\mu_{p}>0
is such that

\Lambda:=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}(\mu_{p})^{1/p}\in(0,\infty).

(1.10)

We will denote by
v_{p,q(p)}
either the only positive weak solution to
(
1.9
), if
1\leq q(p)<p,
or any least energy solution to the same
problem, if
q(p)>p.

Under (
1.10
) the asymptotic behavior of
v_{p,q(p)}
was studied by
Charro and Peral in
[
6
]
(
q(p)<p
) and Charro and Parini in

[
5
]
(
q(p)>p
). Each of these papers considered a different range for
the limit

Q:=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{q(p)}{p}.

(1.11)

In
[
6
]
, Charro and Peral studied the
subdiffusive
case:

Q\in(0,1)
whereas in
[
5
]
, Charro and Parini studied the

superdiffusive
case:
Q\in(1,\infty)
.

In both works it is proved that any sequence
v_{p_{n},q(p_{n})},
with

p_{n}\rightarrow\infty,
admits a subsequence converging uniformly to a
viscosity solution
v_{\infty}\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
to the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}\min\left\{\left|\nabla v\right|-\Lambda v^{Q},-\Delta_{\infty}v\right\}=0&\mathrm{in}&\Omega\\
v=0&\mathrm{on}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.12)

Taking into account that, owing to (
1.5
),

v_{p,q(p)}=\left(\frac{\lambda_{p,q(p)}}{\mu_{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q(p)-p}}u_{p,q(p)},

(1.13)

the analysis of the behavior of
v_{p,q(p)}
under (
1.10
) can be
restricted to the behavior of the pair
\left(\lambda_{p,q(p)},u_{p,q(p)}\right).

In our first result, stated in the sequence, we simply assume that
q(p)
goes
to infinity with
p.
We do not impose any conditions on the sign of the
difference
q(p)-p
or on the limit
Q
defined in (
1.11
).

Theorem 1.1

If

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}q(p)=\infty,

(1.14)

then

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}=\Lambda_{\infty},

(1.15)

and

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left\|u_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{\infty}=1.

(1.16)

Moreover, each sequence
u_{p_{n},q(p_{n})},
with
p_{n}\rightarrow\infty,

admits a subsequence that converges uniformly to a function
u_{\infty}\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
enjoying the following properties:

1.

0\leq u_{\infty}(x)\leq\Lambda_{\infty}d_{\Omega}(x)
for all
x\in

\overline{\Omega}.

2.

\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}=1
and
\Lambda_{\infty}=\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}.

3.

M:=\left\{x\in\Omega:u_{\infty}(x)=1\right\}\subseteq M_{\Omega}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:d_{\Omega}(x)=\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}\right\}.

4.

u_{\infty}
infinity superharmonic in
\Omega
and (consequently)
positive in
\Omega.

Note from item 2 above that the function
u_{\infty}
is also a solution to
the minimization problem given by (
1.7
).

As far as we know, the limit (
1.15
) has not yet been observed in the
literature (except in the case
q(p)=p
as described above). We remark from
(
1.13
) that if
Q\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty]
and
\mu_{p}
satisfies
(
1.10
), then

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}v_{p,q(p)}=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda_{p,q(p)}}{\mu_{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q(p)-p}}u_{p,q(p)}=\left(\frac{\Lambda_{\infty}}{\Lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{Q-1}}\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}u_{p,q(p)}

(1.17)

since the limit (
1.15
) yields

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda_{p,q(p)}}{\mu_{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q(p)-p}}=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda_{p,q(p)}}{\mu_{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}{}^{\frac{1}{\frac{q(p)}{p}-1}}=\left(\frac{\Lambda_{\infty}}{\Lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{Q-1}}.

Corollary
1.2
below can be proved by adapting the proof of Theorem 1.21
in
[
14
]
to the function
u_{\infty}
obtained in Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.2

If

0<Q<\infty,

then each sequence
u_{p_{n},q(p_{n})},
with
p_{n}\rightarrow\infty,
admits
a subsequence that converges uniformly to viscosity solution to the Dirichlet
problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}\min\left\{\left|\nabla v\right|-\Lambda_{\infty}v^{Q},-\Delta_{\infty}v\right\}=0&\mathrm{in}&\Omega\\
v=0&\mathrm{on}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Corollary
1.2
extends to
q(p)\not=p
the convergence result proved
for
q(p)=p
by Juutinen, Lindqvist and Manfredi in
[
14
]
. Indeed, it
shows that if
q(p)\not=p
and
Q=1,
then any limit function (as

p\rightarrow\infty
) of the family
\left(u_{p,q(p)}\right)
is a first
eigenfunction of the infinity Laplacian.

We also observe, as consequence of (
1.17
), that Corollary
1.2

recovers the convergence results obtained by Charro-Peral in
[
6
]
and
Charro-Parini in
[
5
]
.

Our main purpose in this paper is to study the limiting behavior, as

p\rightarrow\infty,
of the least energy solutions to the Dirichlet problem
(
1.9
) in the case not yet treated in the literature:
Q=\infty,
which
we call
hyperdiffusive
case. We can admit faster growth for
\mu_{p}

by assuming that

\Theta:=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}(\mu_{p})^{1/q(p)}\in(0,\infty),

(1.18)

instead of (
1.10
). Hence, (
1.13
) and (
1.15
) yield

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}v_{p,q(p)}=\Theta^{-1}\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}u_{p,q(p)}.

(1.19)

(Note that
\Theta=1
under (
1.10
).)

Therefore, our main result, stated in the sequel, handles the hyperdiffusive
case for
u_{p,q(p)}.

Theorem 1.3

If

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{q(p)}{p}=\infty,

(1.20)

the function
u_{\infty}\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)

obtained in Theorem
1.1
is a viscosity solution to the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\Delta_{\infty}u=0&\text{in}&\Omega\setminus M\\
u=0&\text{on}&\partial\Omega\\
u=1&\text{in}&M.\end{array}\right.

(1.21)

Moreover,

u_{\infty}=\frac{d_{\Omega}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}}

(1.22)

if and only if

M=M_{\Omega}=\Sigma_{\Omega},

(1.23)

where

\Sigma_{\Omega}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:d_{\Omega}\text{ is not differentiable at
}x\right\}.

We recall that
\Sigma_{\Omega},
known as the ridge set of
\Omega,
is
precisely the set of all points of
\Omega
whose distance to the boundary is
achieved at least two points in
\partial\Omega.
Hence,
\Sigma_{\Omega}

contains
M_{\Omega}.
A consequence of Theorem
1.3
is that the
equality (
1.22
) is not possible if
\Sigma_{\Omega}
is larger than

M_{\Omega},
as it is the case of a square and other polygons.

We emphasize that the closed set
M
is given abstractly as the set of maximum
points of
u_{\infty},
but it enjoys the property of being a subset of

M_{\Omega}.
We also observe that
u_{\infty}
might depend on a particular
subsequence of the family
\left(u_{p,q(p)}\right),
the same occurring
with
M.
However, the uniqueness of
u_{\infty}
is guaranteed whenever

M=M_{\Omega}
since the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\Delta_{\infty}u=0&\text{{in}}&\Omega\setminus M_{\Omega}\\
u=0&\text{{on}}&\partial\Omega\\
u=1&\text{on}&M_{\Omega}\end{array}\right.

(1.24)

has a unique viscosity solution according to the comparison principle by
Jensen in
[
13
]
(note that
\partial(\Omega\setminus M_{\Omega})=\partial\Omega\cup M_{\Omega}
). Thus, if
M=M_{\Omega}
then
u_{\infty}

must be the uniform limit of the whole family
\left(u_{p,q(p)}\right)
.
The simplest case is when
M_{\Omega}
is a singleton, and it occurs when

\Omega
is a ball or a square, for instance. Actually,
\Sigma_{B_{R}(x_{0})}=\left\{x_{0}\right\}
for a ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
, so that the equalities
in (
1.23
) hold. Thus, one has

u_{\infty}(x)=1-\frac{\left|x-x_{0}\right|}{R}\text{ \ for all }x\in B_{R}(x_{0}).

If
M_{\Omega}
is not a singleton, the determination of
M
(or even the
verification that
M
is a proper subset of
M_{\Omega}
) seems to be a
challenging task as it would require a deeper analysis of the extremal Sobolev
functions
u_{p,q(p)}
under suitable assumptions on
\Omega
(possibly
involving geometric aspects such as convexity or special symmetries).

In view of (
1.19
) the following corollary of Theorem
1.3
is
imediate and settles the hyperdiffusive case for
v_{p,q(p)}
under
(
1.18
).

Corollary 1.4

For all
p
sufficiently large, let
v_{p,q(p)}
be a positive least energy
solution to (
1.9
), with
\mu_{p}
satisfying (
1.18
) and
q(p)

satisfying (
1.20
). Each sequence
v_{p_{n},q(p_{n})},
with

p_{n}\rightarrow\infty,
admits a subsequence that converges uniformly to a
viscosity solution
v_{\infty}\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)

to the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\Delta_{\infty}v=0&\text{in}&\Omega\setminus M\\
v=0&\text{on}&\partial\Omega\\
v=\Theta^{-1}&\text{in}&M,\end{array}\right.

where
M
is a closed subset of
M_{\Omega}.
Moreover,

v_{\infty}=\frac{d_{\Omega}}{\Theta\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}}

if and only if
M=M_{\Omega}=\Sigma_{\Omega}.

Inspecting carefully
[
6
]
one can verify that, in view of the limit
(
1.15
), the results obtained by Charro and Peral described above are also
valid for
\mu_{p}=\lambda_{p,q(p)}
when
Q=0,
but under the hypothesis
(
1.14
). More precisely, such results show that the family
(u_{p,q(p)})

converges uniformly to
u_{\infty}=\Lambda_{\infty}d_{\Omega}
since

1\leq q(p)<p,\text{ \ }\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}q(p)=\infty\text{ \ and
\ }\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{q(p)}{p}=0.

The uniqueness of
u_{\infty}
is guaranteed by the fact (see
[
13
, Theorem
2.1]
) that
\Lambda_{\infty}d_{\Omega}
is the only viscosity solution to
the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}\min\left\{\left|\nabla u\right|-\Lambda_{\infty},-\Delta_{\infty}u\right\}=0&\mathrm{in}&\Omega\\
u=0&\mathrm{on}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

In order to complement our analysis on the asymptotic behavior of

\lambda_{p,q(p)}
and
u_{p,q(p)}
we prove the following result in which

Q=0
and (
1.14
) is not fulfilled.

Proposition 1.5

If

r:=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}q(p)<\infty,

(1.25)

then

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}=\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{r}^{-1},

and

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}u_{p,q(p)}=\frac{d_{\Omega}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{r}}\text{ \ uniformly in }\overline{\Omega}.

Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
1.5
are
proved in Section
3
.

2
Preliminaries

Let us recall the concept of viscosity solution to the partial differential
equation (PDE)

F(u,\nabla u,D^{2}u)=0,

(2.1)

where
F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{S}^{N}
is a continuous
function, with
\mathbb{S}^{N}
denoting the set of all real symmetric
matrices of order
N\times N.

In the sequel,
B(x_{0})
will denote a ball centered at
x_{0}.

Definitions 2.1

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain and let
u\in C(D).
We say that:

1.

u
is a viscosity supersolution to (
2.1
) in
D
if, for each

x_{0}\in D
and each ball
B(x_{0})\subset D
one has

F(\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(x_{0}))\geq 0

for every
\phi\in C^{2}(B(x_{0}))
such that

\phi(x)-u(x)<0=\phi(x_{0})-u(x_{0})\text{ \ for all }x\in B(x_{0})\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}.

2.

u
is a viscosity subsolution to (
2.1
) in
D
if, for each

x_{0}\in D
and each ball
B(x_{0})\subset D
one has

F(\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(x_{0}))\leq 0

for every
\phi\in C^{2}(B(x_{0}))
such that

\phi(x)-u(x)>0=\phi(x_{0})-u(x_{0})\text{ \ for all }x\in B(x_{0})\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}.

3.

u
is a viscosity solution to (
2.1
) in
D
if
u
is both a
viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution to
F
in
D.

We say that
u\in C(D)
is infinity harmonic in
D
if
u
is a viscosity
solution to the PDE

-\Delta_{\infty}u=0

(2.2)

in
D.
If
u
is a viscosity supersolution (subsolution) to (
2.2
) in

D
we say that
u
is infinity superharmonic (subharmonic) in
D
and use

-\Delta_{\infty}u\geq 0
(
-\Delta_{\infty}u\leq 0
) in
D
as notation.

The following result is well known (see
[
3
, Theorem 1]
and

[
16
, Corollary 4.5]
).

Lemma 2.2

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain and let
u\in C(D).

If
u
is a nonnegative and
-\Delta_{\infty}u\geq 0
in
D,
then either

u\equiv 0
in
D
or
u>0
in
D.

By a viscosity solution to the Dirichlet problem (
1.21
) we mean a
function
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
that is infinity harmonic in

\Omega\setminus M
and such that:
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
u=1
on
M.

We observe that

\Delta_{p}u=(p-2)\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-4}\left\{\frac{1}{p-2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\Delta u+\Delta_{\infty}u\right\}

whenever
u
is a function of class
C^{2}.
Thus, in the viscosity solution
approach the partial differential equation in (
1.2
) is usually written
as

-(p-2)\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-4}\left\{\frac{1}{p-2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\Delta u+\Delta_{\infty}u\right\}-\lambda_{p,q}\left|u\right|^{q-2}u=0.

(2.3)

By a viscosity solution to (
1.2
) we mean a viscosity solution
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
to (
2.3
) that vanishes on
\partial\Omega.

We will make use of the following known facts stated in form of lemmas.

Lemma 2.3

Let
p_{n}\rightarrow\infty
and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let

u_{n}\in W_{0}^{1,p_{n}}(\Omega)
be nonnegative in
\Omega.
Suppose that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C.

There exists a subsequence of
\left(u_{n}\right)
converging uniformly in

\overline{\Omega}
to a function
u_{\infty}\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
such that

0\leq u_{\infty}(x)\leq\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}d_{\Omega}(x)\leq Cd_{\Omega}(x)\text{ \ for all }x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Lemma 2.4

For each
p>N
the embedding
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})
is compact and the infimum

\Lambda_{p}:=\inf\left\{\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}:u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\text{ \ and \ }\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}=1\right\}

is reached at a function
u_{p}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap C^{0,1-\frac{N}{p}}(\overline{\Omega}).
Moreover (see
[
9
]
),

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\Lambda_{p}=\Lambda_{\infty}.

(2.4)

The proof of the next lemma follows directly from the proof of Lemma 1.8 in

[
14
]
, where the case
q(p)=p
is treated.

Lemma 2.5

The Sobolev extremal function
u_{p,q(p)}
is a viscosity solution
to

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda_{p,q(p)}\left|u\right|^{q(p)-2}u&\text{{in}}&\Omega\\
u=0&\text{{on}}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 2.6

The function
d_{\Omega}/\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}
is the only viscosity solution to (
1.24
) if and only if
\Sigma_{\Omega}=M_{\Omega}.

Proof.
It is well known (see
[
2
]
,
[
7
, Corollary 3.4]
) that

-\Delta_{\infty}d_{\Omega}=0
in
\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{\Omega}
in the
viscosity sense. Hence, if
\Sigma_{\Omega}=M_{\Omega}
then
d_{\Omega}/\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}
is the only viscosity solution
to (
1.24
).

Inversely, if
d_{\Omega}/\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}
is the
viscosity solution to (
1.24
), then it is differentiable in
\Omega\setminus M_{\Omega}
as it is infinity harmonic on this set (see

[
11
, Theorem 3.2]
). It follows that
\Sigma_{\Omega}=M_{\Omega}.

In order to simplify the notation in the proofs, in the sequel we will denote

u_{p_{n},q(p_{n})}
by
u_{n}
and
\lambda_{p_{n},q(p_{n})}
by
\lambda_{n},
whenever
p_{n}\rightarrow\infty.

3
Proofs

3.1
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof.
As

\Lambda_{p}\leq\frac{\left\|\nabla u_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{p}}{\left\|u_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{\infty}}\text{ \ and \ }1=\left\|u_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{q(p)}\leq\left\|u_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{\infty}\left|\Omega\right|^{1/q(p)}

we have that

\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}=\left\|\nabla u_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{p}\geq\Lambda_{p}\left\|u_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{\infty}\geq\Lambda_{p}\left|\Omega\right|^{-1/q(p)}.

(3.1)

Hence, combining (
2.4
) with (
3.1
) we get, on the one hand,

\liminf_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}\geq\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\Lambda_{p}\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left|\Omega\right|^{-1/q(p)}=\Lambda_{\infty}.

(3.2)

On the other hand, as

\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}\leq\frac{\left\|\nabla d_{\Omega}\right\|_{p}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{q(p)}}=\frac{\left|\Omega\right|^{1/p}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{q(p)}}

we obtain

\limsup_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}\leq\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left|\Omega\right|^{1/p}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{q(p)}}=\frac{1}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}}=\Lambda_{\infty}.

(3.3)

Gathering (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we conclude the proof of (
1.15
). Hence,
returning to (
3.1
) and taking (
2.4
) into account again, we get
(
1.16
).

Now, let
p_{n}\rightarrow\infty.
As
\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{p_{n}}=\lambda_{n}^{1/p_{n}}\rightarrow\Lambda_{\infty}
it follows from
(
3.3
) and Lemma
2.3
that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\rightarrow u_{\infty}\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega),
uniformly in

\overline{\Omega},
with

0\leq u_{\infty}(x)\leq\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}d_{\Omega}(x)\leq\Lambda_{\infty}d_{\Omega}(x)\text{ \ for all }x\in\overline{\Omega}.

The uniform convergence and the limit in (
1.16
) imply that
\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}=1.
Therefore,

\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\Lambda_{\infty}\leq\frac{\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}}{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}}=\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty},

so that
\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}=\Lambda_{\infty},

completing thus the proof of items 1 and 2 of Theorem
1.1
.

The proof of item 3 is direct: if
x_{0}\in M,
then

1=u_{\infty}(x_{0})\leq\Lambda_{\infty}d_{\Omega}(x_{0})=\frac{d_{\Omega}(x_{0})}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}}\leq 1,

so that
x_{0}\in M_{\Omega}.

In order to prove item 4, let us first verify that
-\Delta_{\infty}u_{\infty}\geq 0
in
\Omega.
Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and fix a ball
B(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and a function
\phi\in C^{2}(B(x_{0}))
such that

\phi(x)-u_{\infty}(x)<0=\phi(x_{0})-u_{\infty}(x_{0})\text{ \ for all }x\in B(x_{0})\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}.

If
\left|\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right|=0,
then we have trivially that

-\Delta_{\infty}\phi(x_{0})=0.
Thus, we can assume that
\left|\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right|>0.

As
u_{n}\rightarrow u_{\infty}
uniformly, there exists
x_{n}\in\Omega
such
that
x_{n}\rightarrow x_{0}
and

\phi(x)-u_{n}(x)<0=\phi(x_{n})-u_{n}(x_{n})\text{ \ for all }\,x\in B(x_{n})\setminus\{x_{n}\}.

(See
[
15
, Lemma 4.5]
). We can also assume that
\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|>0.

Owing to Lemma
2.5
,

-\Delta_{p_{n}}\phi(x_{n})\geq\lambda_{n}\phi(x_{n})^{q_{n}-1}=\lambda_{n}u_{n}(x_{n})^{q_{n}-1}\geq 0,

that is,

-(p_{n}-2)\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|^{p_{n}-4}\left\{\frac{1}{p_{n}-2}\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|^{2}\Delta\phi(x_{n})+\Delta_{\infty}\phi(x_{n})\right\}\geq 0.

It follows that

-\Delta_{\infty}\phi(x_{n})\geq\frac{1}{p_{n}-2}\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|^{2}\Delta\phi(x_{n}),

and hence, after letting
n\rightarrow\infty,
we obtain

-\Delta_{\infty}\phi(x_{0})\geq\left|\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right|^{2}\Delta\phi(x_{0})\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{p_{n}-2}=0.

This shows that
-\Delta_{\infty}u_{\infty}\geq 0
in
\Omega
and in addition
proves that
u_{\infty}>0
in
\Omega
according to Lemma
2.2
.

3.2
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Proof.
According to item 4 of Theorem
1.1
,
-\Delta_{\infty}u_{\infty}\geq 0

in
\Omega\setminus M.
Thus, as
u_{\infty}=0
on
\partial\Omega
and

u_{\infty}=1
on
M,
it remains to prove that
-\Delta_{\infty}u_{\infty}\leq 0
in
\Omega\setminus M.
At this point we use (
1.20
), a stronger
hypothesis than (
1.14
).

We recall that

M:=\left\{x\in\Omega:u_{\infty}(x)=1\right\}\subseteq M_{\Omega}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:d_{\Omega}(x)=\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}\right\},

so that
\Omega\setminus M
is an open set.

Let us fix
x_{0}\in\Omega\setminus M,
a ball
B(x_{0})\subset\Omega\setminus M,
and a function
\phi\in C^{2}(B(x_{0}))
such that

\phi(x)-u_{\infty}(x)>0=\phi(x_{0})-u_{\infty}(x_{0})\text{ \ for all }x\in B(x_{0})\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}.

If
\left|\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right|=0
then we obtain directly that

-\Delta_{\infty}\phi(x_{0})=0.
Thus, we assume that
\left|\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right|>0.

Let
p_{n}\rightarrow\infty
be such that
u_{n}\rightarrow u_{\infty}

uniformly. We recall from (
1.15
) and (
1.20
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{n}^{1/p_{n}}=\Lambda_{\infty}\text{ \ and
\ }\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{q_{n}}{p_{n}}=\infty.

(3.4)

The uniform convergence
u_{n}\rightarrow u_{\infty}
guarantees the existence
of
x_{n}\in\Omega\setminus M
such that
x_{n}\rightarrow x_{0}
and

\phi(x)-u_{n}(x)>0=\phi(x_{n})-u_{n}(x_{n})\text{ \ for all }\,x\in B(x_{n})\setminus\{x_{n}\}.

As
\left|\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right|>0
and
0<u_{\infty}(x_{0})<1
we
can assume that
\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|>0
and

0<a\leq u_{n}(x_{n})\leq b<1\text{ \ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

(3.5)

where the constants
a
and
b
are uniform with respect to
n.

Using again Lemma
2.5
we have

\displaystyle-(p_{n}-2)\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|^{p_{n}-4}\left\{\frac{\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|^{2}}{p_{n}-2}\Delta\phi(x_{n})+\Delta_{\infty}\phi(x_{n})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}\phi(x_{n})^{q_{n}-1}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}u_{n}(x_{n})^{q_{n}-1}.

Hence, after rearranging terms we obtain

-\frac{\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|^{2}}{p_{n}-2}\Delta\phi(x_{n})-\Delta_{\infty}\phi(x_{n})\leq\frac{1}{p_{n}-2}\left[\frac{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{p_{n}-4}}u_{n}(x_{n})^{\frac{q_{n}-1}{p_{n}-4}}}{\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|}\right]^{p_{n}-4}.

(3.6)

Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{p_{n}-4}}u_{n}(x_{n})^{\frac{q_{n}-1}{p_{n}-4}}}{\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|}=\frac{\Lambda_{\infty}}{\left|\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right|}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u_{n}(x_{n})^{\frac{q_{n}-1}{p_{n}-4}}=0

so that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{p_{n}-2}\left[\frac{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{p_{n}-4}}u_{n}(x_{n})^{\frac{q_{n}-1}{p_{n}-4}}}{\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|}\right]^{p_{n}-4}=0.

As

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\{-\frac{1}{p_{n}-2}\left|\nabla\phi(x_{n})\right|^{2}\Delta\phi(x_{n})-\Delta_{\infty}\phi(x_{n})\right\}=-\Delta_{\infty}\phi(x_{0}),

we conclude, after letting
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.6
), that

-\Delta_{\infty}\phi(x_{0})\leq 0.

This shows that
u_{\infty}
is subharmonic in
\Omega\setminus M
and
finishes the proof that
u_{\infty}
is harmonic in
\Omega\setminus M.

The first equality in (
1.23
) implies that
u_{\infty}
is a viscosity
solution to (
1.24
) and by Lemma
2.6
the second equality
in (
1.23
) implies that
d_{\Omega}/\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}
is also a viscosity solution to the same problem. Thus, by
uniqueness
u_{\infty}=d_{\Omega}/\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}.

Inversely, if
u_{\infty}=d_{\Omega}/\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}
in
\Omega\setminus M,
then
d_{\Omega}
is harmonic and,
therefore, differentiable in
\Omega\setminus M.
Consequently,
M=\Sigma_{\Omega}
and this implies that
\Sigma_{\Omega}=M_{\Omega}
(as
M\subseteq M_{\Omega}\subseteq\Sigma_{\Omega}
).

3.3
Proof of Proposition
1.5

Proof.
The simplest case in which the function
q(p)
is constant, say
q(p)\equiv s\in[1,\infty),
has already been proved in
[
10
, Theorem 4.2]
.
According to that result

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{p,s}^{1/p}=\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{s}^{-1}

(3.7)

and

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}u_{p,s}=\frac{d_{\Omega}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{s}}\text{ \ uniformly in }\overline{\Omega}.

(3.8)

In the sequel we combine (
3.7
)-(
3.8
) with the following fact: the
function
q\mapsto\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{p}{q}}\lambda_{p,q}
is
strictly decreasing, for each fixed
p\in[1,\infty).
This monotonicity
result was proved for
1<p<N
in
[
8
, Proposition 2]
, but the proof
given there also works for
p>N
(see also
[
1
]
).

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrarily fixed. Owing to (
1.25
) we have that

q(p)<r+\epsilon
for all
p
sufficiently large, say
p>p_{0}.
Hence,
according to the above mentioned monotonicity result we have

\lambda_{p,r+\epsilon}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{p}{r+\epsilon}-\frac{p}{q(p)}}\leq\lambda_{p,q(p)}\text{ \ for all }p>p_{0},

so that

\frac{\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{r+\epsilon}-\frac{1}{r}}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{r+\epsilon}}\leq\liminf_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}.

Letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0,
we obtain on the one hand

\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{r}^{-1}\leq\liminf_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}.

(3.9)

On the other hand, the inequality

\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}\leq\frac{\left\|\nabla d_{\Omega}\right\|_{p}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{q(p)}}=\frac{\left|\Omega\right|^{1/p}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{q(p)}}

lead us to the estimate

\limsup_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}\leq\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{r}^{-1}.

(3.10)

Combining (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) we conclude that

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}=\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{r}^{-1}.

Now, let
p_{n}\rightarrow\infty.
As
\lambda_{p,q(p)}^{1/p}=\left\|\nabla u_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{p},
Lemma
2.3
and (
3.10
) imply that,
up to a subsequence,
u_{p_{n},q(p_{n})}
converges uniformly in

\overline{\Omega}
to a function
u_{\infty}\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying

0\leq u_{\infty}\leq\frac{d_{\Omega}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{r}}\text{ in }\overline{\Omega}.

(3.11)

The uniform convergence and (
1.25
) yield

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|u_{p_{n},q(p_{n})}\right\|_{r}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|u_{p_{n},q(p_{n})}\right\|_{q(p_{n})}=\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|_{r}.

Hence, recalling that
\left\|u_{p_{n},q(p_{n})}\right\|_{q(p_{n})}=1,
we have that
\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|_{r}=1,
which combined
with (
3.11
) yields
u_{\infty}\equiv\frac{d_{\Omega}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{r}}.

As the limit function
u_{\infty}
is always the same, we conclude that

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}u_{p,q(p)}=\frac{d_{\Omega}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{r}}\text{ \ uniformly in }\overline{\Omega}.

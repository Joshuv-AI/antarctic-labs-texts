Introduction

The role of combinatorics is distinguished in the current trends of commutative algebra. Particularly, the combinatorics of finite graphs has created fascinating research topics in this area. The present paper continues along these research directions and investigates algebraic properties and invariant of monomial ideals associated to finite graphs.

Let
G
be a finite
simple
graph on
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, with no loops, no multiple edges and no isolated vertices, and let
E(G)
denote the set of edges of
G
. Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
. The
edge ideal
of

is the ideal
I(G)
of
S
generated by the monomials

with
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
, and the

of

is the ideal
J(G)
of
S
generated by the squarefree monomials
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{s}}
, with
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{s}\leq n
, for which
\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{s}\}
is the vertex cover of

.

Homological aspects of edge ideals and vertex cover ideals, together with their powers, have been much studied. Especially, forces are being poured into the challenging conjectures that
(I(G))^{q}
have linear resolution, for

, if
G
is gap-free (cf.
[
2
,
15
]
), and that

are componentwise linear, for

, if
G
is chordal (cf.
[
10
,
14
]
).
In this paper, we are interested in Scarf complexes/resolutions and graded Betti numbers of vertex cover ideals.

Scarf complexes have been investigated for monomial ideals in general
[
3
]
, and for edge ideals of graphs in particular
[
7
]
. On the other hand, it follows from
[
6
,
9
]
that
J(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay, i.e.,
S/J(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay, if and only if the complementary graph
G^{c}
of

is chordal. In Theorem
2.3
, we completely characterize when a Cohen-Macaulay vertex cover ideal

admits a Scarf minimal free resolution. We prove that this is the case if and only if one (or equivalently, all) leaf order of the clique complex of

is

.

Restricting to the class of paths and cycles, we exhibit an interesting phenomenon at the highest graded Betti numbers of their vertex cover ideals. Specifically, in Theorems
3.7
and

, when

2.
Cohen–Macaulay vertex cover ideals

Let
G
be a finite graph on
[n]
and
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
. Fröberg’s theorem (cf.
[
9
]
and
[
13
, Theorem 9.2.3]
) says that the edge ideal
I(G)
has linear resolution if and only if the complementary graph
G^{c}
of
G
is chordal. On the other hand, Eagon–Reiner theorem (cf.
[
6
]
and
[
13
, Theorem 8.1.9]
) guarantees that the vertex cover ideal
J(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay, i.e., the quotient ring
S/J(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay, if and only if
I(G)
has linear resolution. In other words,
J(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
G^{c}
is chordal.

Throughout this section, we will assume that
G
is a finite graph whose complementary graph
G^{c}
is chordal. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
for a quick introduction to Scarf resolutions.

Let
\Delta(G^{c})
be the clique complex of
G^{c}
. Thus
\Delta(G^{c})
is a quasi-forest. Fix a leaf order
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{q}
of the facets of
\Delta(G^{c})
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}
denote the multiset of all intersections

F_{i_{1}}\cap F_{i_{2}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{j}},\,\,\,\,\,1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq q,\,\,\,j\geq 2,

of
F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
denote the subset of
{\mathcal{A}}
consisting of those
{\mathcal{F}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
whose multiplicity is
1
. For example, if
n=6
and
F_{1}=\{1,2,3\},F_{2}=\{2,3,4\},F_{3}=\{3,5\},F_{4}=\{4,6\}
, then the multiset
{\mathcal{A}}
is
\{\{2,3\},\{4\},\{3\}^{3},\emptyset^{6}\}
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}=\{\{2,3\},\{4\}\}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G_{i}
be the branch of
F_{i}
in the subcomplex
\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{i}\rangle
of
\Delta(G^{c})
. If a subset
H\subset[n]
belongs to
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
, then there is
1<i\leq q
with
H=G_{i}\cap F_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
F_{i_{1}}\cap F_{i_{2}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{j}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq q
and
q\geq 2
. If none of
F_{i_{k}}
is a branch
G_{i_{j}}
of
F_{i_{j}}
, then
F_{i_{1}}\cap F_{i_{2}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{j}}=F_{i_{1}}\cap F_{i_{2}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{j}}\cap G_{i_{j}}
cannot belong to
{\mathcal{A}}
. If
q\geq 3
and if, say,
F_{i_{k}}=G_{i_{j}}
, then
F_{i_{1}}\cap F_{i_{2}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{j}}=F_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{k-1}}\cap F_{i_{k+1}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{j}}
cannot belong to
{\mathcal{A}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G_{i}
be the branch of
F_{i}
in the subcomplex
\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{i}\rangle
of
\Delta(G^{c})
. Then
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
coincides with
\{G_{i}\cap F_{i}:1\leq i\leq q\}
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

G_{i}
is a unique branch of
F_{i}
; and

(ii)

G_{i}\cap F_{i}\not\subset G_{j}\cap F_{j}
for
i\neq j
.

Proof.

If
G_{i}
and
G_{i}^{\prime}
are branches of
F_{i}
with
G_{i}\neq G_{i}^{\prime}
, then
G_{i}\cap F_{i}=G_{i}^{\prime}\cap F_{i}
cannot belong to
{\mathcal{A}}
. Suppose that
G_{i}\cap F_{i}\subset G_{j}\cap F_{j}
for
i\neq j
. Let
i<j
. Then
G_{i}\cap F_{i}=G_{i}\cap F_{i}\cap F_{j}
cannot belong to
{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
j<i
. Then
G_{i}\cap F_{i}=G_{i}\cap F_{i}\cap G_{j}
cannot belong to
{\mathcal{A}}
unless
G_{j}=G_{i}
. If
G_{j}=G_{i}
, then
G_{i}\cap F_{i}=G_{j}\cap F_{i}\cap F_{j}
cannot belong to
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Thus (i) and (ii) are satisfied if
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}=\{G_{i}\cap F_{i}:1\leq i\leq q\}
.

Now, suppose that (i) and (ii) are satisfied. Let
G_{i}\cap F_{i}=F_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{j}}
with
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq q
and
j\geq 2
. Since
F_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{j}}\subset G_{i_{j}}\cap F_{i_{j}}
, one has
G_{i}\cap F_{i}\subset G_{i_{j}}\cap F_{i_{j}}
. Hence
i=i_{j}
by (ii). Thus
G_{i_{j}}\cap F_{i_{j}}=F_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{j}}
. However, since a branch of
F_{i_{j}}
is unique by (i), this cannot happen unless
j=2
and
F_{i_{1}}=G_{i_{j}}
. Hence
G_{i}\cap F_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
, as desired.
∎

We are ready to state our result that characterizes when a Cohen–Macaulay vertex cover ideal has a Scarf resolution.
We say that a leaf order
F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}
of the facets of the clique complex
\Delta(G^{c})
is
sensitive
if the conditions (i) and (ii) of Lemma
2.2
are satisfied.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite graph on
[n]
whose complementary graph
G^{c}
is chordal. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

the vertex cover ideal
J(G)
has a Scarf resolution;

(ii)

a leaf order of
\Delta(G^{c})
is sensitive;

(iii)

every leaf order of
\Delta(G^{c})
is sensitive.

Proof.

Work with a fixed leaf order
F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}
of the facets of the clique complex
\Delta(G^{c})
. The vertex cover ideal
J(G)
is generated by the monomials
u_{i}=x_{[n]\setminus F_{i}}
. Furthermore, the least common multiple of
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
is
x_{[n]\setminus(F_{i}\cap F_{j})}
. It was discussed in the proof of Theorem
2.5
that
\operatorname{proj-dim}J(G)=1
and that the first Betti number of
I_{G}
is equal to
q-1
. Hence
J(G)
has a Scarf resolution if and only if the number of monmials of the form
x_{[n]\setminus(F_{i}\cap F_{j})}
with
i<j
which belong to the Scarf complex of
J(G)
is
q-1
, in other words, if and only if the number of subsets of
[n]
of the form
F_{i}\cap F_{j}
with
i<j
which belong to
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
is
q-1
, where
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
was introduced just before Lemma
2.1
. Now, Lemma
2.1
says that each element of
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
is of the form
G_{i}\cap F_{i}
, where
G_{i}
is a branch of
F_{i}
in
\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{i}\rangle
. Furthermore, Lemma
2.2
guarantees that
|{\mathcal{A}}^{*}|=q-1
if and only if the leaf order
F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}
is sensitive. This completes the proof of (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) and (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) of Theorem
2.3
. Finally (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is obvious.
∎

Example 2.4
.

(a) Let

be a finite graph for which

is chordal and has exactly three facets

. Since

(b) Let

is a forest, i.e.,

has no cycle. Then

has a Scarf resolution if and only if

is a path, i.e., the edges of

are
\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\ldots,\{n-1,n\}
by rearranging the vertices of

.

A
complete bipartite graph
of type
(a,b)
, where
a
and
b
are positive integers, is a finite graph on
[a+b]
whose edges are those
\{i,j\}
with

and
a<j\leq a+b
. The following result was already proved in
[

. We include the result to showcase yet another application of the Eagon-Reiner formula

]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

The vertex cover ideal
J(G)
is Gorenstein, i.e.,
S/J(G)
is Gorenstein, if and only if
G
is a complete bipartite graph.

Proof.

First, suppose that
G
is a complete graph of type
(a,b)
whose vertices are
x_{1},\ldots,x_{a},y_{1},\ldots,y_{b}
and whose edges are those
\{x_{i},y_{j}\}
with
1\leq i\leq a,1\leq j\leq b
. Then
J(G)=(x_{1}\cdots x_{a},y_{1}\cdots y_{b})
. Thus
S/J(G)
is a complete intersection and, in particular, is Gorenstein.

Second, suppose that
S/J(G)
is Gorenstein. Since
I(G)
has linear resolution, one has
\operatorname{reg}I(G)=2
, where
\operatorname{reg}I(G)
is the regularity of
I(G)
. Hence
\operatorname{proj-dim}J(G)=1
, where
\operatorname{proj-dim}J(G)
is the projective dimension of
J(G)
, see
[
13
, Proposition 8.1.10]
. Now, it follows from Hilbert–Burch theorem
[
13
, Lemma 9.2.4]
that the first Betti number of
J(G)
is equal to
s-1
, where
s
is the number of monomials belonging to the minimal system of monomial generators of
J(G)
. Since
J(G)
is Gorenstein, one has
s-1=1
. Thus
J(G)
is minimally generated by exactly two monomials. In other words, there are exactly two minimal vertex covers
W
and
W^{\prime}
of
G
. Since
W\cap W^{\prime}=\emptyset
, the edges of
G
are those
\{i,j\}
, where
i\in W
and
j\in W^{\prime}
. Hence
G
is a complete bipartite graph, as desired.
∎

3.
Betti numbers of vertex cover ideals of paths

Let
P_{n}
denote the path on

with

. The present section is devoted to computing the graded Betti number

of

, where

and

It is known from

\operatorname{reg}I(P_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+1&\text{if }n\equiv 0,1(\text{mod }3)\\
&\\
\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+2&\text{if }n\equiv 2(\text{mod }3),\end{array}\right.

and

\operatorname{proj-dim}I(P_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{3}\right\rfloor-1&\text{if }n\equiv 0,1(\text{mod }3)\\
&\\
2\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{3}\right\rfloor&\text{if }n\equiv 2(\text{mod }3).\end{array}\right.

Let

denote the clique complex

of the complementary graph

of

. One has
\dim\Delta_{n}=[(n-1)/2]
. In general, if

is a simplicial complex with

, then given an integer

whose facets are the faces

of

with

. Furthermore, we defines

to be the subcomplex whose facets are those faces

of
\Delta
with

is
not
a facet of
\Delta
.

Let

. Then

and

. It follows from
[

, Theorem 2.1]
that

\beta_{i,i+j}(J(P_{n}))=h_{k}(k-1),

where
h(\Delta_{n}(k-1))-h(\Delta_{n}(k-1)^{\prime})=(h_{0}(k-1),h_{1}(k-1),\ldots,h_{k}(k-1))
.

Lemma 3.1
.

A facet
F
of
\Delta_{3k}
with
|F|=k
uniquely exists. Let

be the facet of
\Delta_{3k}
with
|F_{0}|=k
. Then each proper subset

of

is a subset of a facet

of
\Delta_{3k}
with
|F^{\prime}|>k
.

Proof.

The face

is a unique facet

of
\Delta_{3k}
with
|F|=k
. If

with

, then clearly

is a subset of a facet

of
\Delta_{3k}
with

. It then follows that each proper subset

of
F_{0}
is a subset of a facet

of
\Delta_{3k}
with

, as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

\beta_{k,k+2k}(J(P_{3k}))=1
.

4.

Let
C_{n}
denote the cycle on
[n]
with
E(C_{n})=\{\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\ldots,\{n-1,n\},\{1,n\}\}
. The present section is devoted to computing the graded Betti number

of

, where
i=\operatorname{proj-dim}J(C_{n})
and

\Delta_{n}
denote the clique complex
\Delta(C_{n}^{c})
of the complementary graph
C_{n}^{c}
of
C_{n}
. It follows from
[
13
, Corollary 8.1.4]
that

(4)

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+j}(J(C_{n}))=\sum_{F\in\Delta_{n},\,|F|=n-(i+j)}\dim_{K}\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\operatorname{link}_{\Delta_{n}}F;K).

It is known from
[
1
, Corollary 5.5]
that

\operatorname{reg}I(C_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+1&\text{if }n\equiv 0,1(\text{mod }3)\\
&\\
\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+2&\text{if }n\equiv 2(\text{mod }3),\end{array}\right.

Thus by using

13

\operatorname{proj-dim}J(C_{3k})=k,\,\,\operatorname{proj-dim}J(C_{3k+1})=k,\,\,\,\operatorname{proj-dim}J(C_{3k+2})=k+1,

\operatorname{reg}J(C_{3k})=2k,\,\,\operatorname{reg}J(C_{3k+1})=2k+1,\,\,\,\operatorname{reg}J(C_{3k+2})=2k+1.

Our target is to compute the graded Betti numbers

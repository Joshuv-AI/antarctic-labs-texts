Classical and quantum symmetries of T ​ T ¯ {T\bar{T}} - deformed CFTs
Monica Guica, Ruben Monten and Ioannis Tsiares Université Paris-Saclay, CNRS, CEA, Institut de Physique Théorique, 91191 Gif-sur-Yvette, France Mani L. Bhaumik Institute for Theoretical Physics, Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of California, Los Angeles, CA 90095, USA

Abstract
It has previously been proven that T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} - deformed CFTs possess Virasoro × \times Virasoro symmetry at the full quantum level, whose generators are obtained by simply transporting the original CFT generators along the T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} flow. In this article, we explicitly solve the corresponding flow equation in the classical limit, obtaining an infinite set of conserved charges whose action on phase space is well-defined even when the theory is on a compact space. The field-dependent coordinates that are characteristic of the T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformation are shown to emerge unambiguously from the flow. Translating the symmetry transformations from the canonical to the covariant formalism, we find that they are different from those that have been previously proposed in the Lagrangian context, but that they agree precisely with those obtained from holography. We also comment on different possible bases for the symmetry generators and on how our explicit classical results could be extended perturbatively to the quantum level.

Introduction

The

[
3
]
, but which is non-local at the scale set by the irrelevant deformation parameter
[
4
]
. Second, this deformation has found applications to many different areas of theoretical physics, such as integrable QFT bootstrap
[

]
, the bosonic and the effective string in QCD
[

,

,

,

,

, phenomenology
[

]
as well as non-AdS holography, either in the standard sense of a different asymptotic background metric
[
10
]
, or as a change in the asymptotic boundary conditions for an AdS one
[
11
,
12
]
. Finally, the deformation is entirely solvable, as many observables have been computed exactly, such as the finite-size spectrum and the S-matrix
[
1
,
2
,
13
,
14
]
, as well as correlation functions
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
, to a certain extent. In all these cases, the deformation of the given observable is universally determined by the corresponding quantity in the undeformed QFT.

Of course, one of the most basic properties of a theory are its symmetries; those of
T\bar{T}
- deformed
CFTs
, i.e., when the seed theory already possesses Virasoro
\times
Virasoro symmetry, deserve particular attention. The reason is that these infinite symmetries are preserved by the deformation, despite the theory becoming explicitly non-local. This fact is interesting both from the perspective of
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs, and as a proof of concept that Virasoro symmetries need not be associated to
local
two-dimensional CFTs. Non-local UV-complete QFTs with Virasoro symmetry have been termed “non-local CFTs” in
[
20
]
, where it was also shown how their non-locality could be reconciled with the Virasoro symmetry. Such non-local CFTs are expected to be quite relevant, in the least, to non-AdS holography, for example to the Kerr/CFT correspondence
[
21
]
and possibly also to flat space holography, as suggested by the recent discovery
of an infinite set of
T\bar{T}
- like symmetries in three-dimensional asymptotically flat spacetimes with a linear dilaton
[
22
]
.

The first hint that
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs may possess a doubly-infinite set of Virasoro symmetries came from the holographic analysis of
[
11
]
, who showed that the holographic dual to
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs with a large central charge is AdS
3
gravity with mixed boundary conditions for the metric, and worked out the associated asymptotic symmetries. The asymptotic symmetry generators were found to be parametrized by two arbitrary functions of certain “field-dependent” coordinates that, as emphasized in
[
4
,
13
]
, capture much of the dynamics of
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs. It was also shown that the generators corresponding to the natural Fourier basis for these functions
rescaled
by the field-dependent radius of the coordinates satisfy a Virasoro
\times
Virasoro algebra, with the same central charge as the undeformed CFT. This analysis was further perfected in
[
22
]
, which resolved certain subtleties related to coordinate periodicities and also showed how to obtain the non-linear algebra of the “unrescaled” generators directly from the covariant phase space formalism.

The infinite set of symmetries uncovered by the holographic analysis were later confirmed to exist also from the perspective of classical field theory
[
23
]
, through both a Lagrangian and a Hamiltonian analysis. More precisely, it was shown that the classical action of a
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFT was invariant under a certain set of field-dependent coordinate transformations, whose form closely resembled, but was however not identical to, that of the asymptotic symmetry generators in the holographic analysis. The algebra of the associated conserved charges was computed using the Hamiltonian formalism; however, while one clearly found two commuting copies of the Witt algebra in infinite volume, for
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs on a compact space the symmetry algebra was not entirely fixed, due to an ambiguity in the zero mode of the field-dependent coordinate.

The full quantum proof of the existence of Virasoro symmetries in
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs was given in
[
20
]
. It simply relies on the fact that when placed on a circle,
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs should possess a well-defined quantum operator, herein denoted as
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}}
, that drives the flow of the energy eigenstates. Then, the quantum operators defined via

\partial_{\mu}\tilde{L}_{n}=[\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}},\tilde{L}_{n}]\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left.\tilde{L}_{n}\right|_{\mu=0}=L_{n}^{\mbox{\tiny{CFT}}}

(1.1)

are well-defined, satisfy by construction a Virasoro algebra with the same central extension as that of the undeformed CFT and, moreover, are conserved. The latter fact, which is the only one that does not follow trivially from our definition of the flowed generators, is a consequence of the universality of the spectrum of
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs
[
24
,
20
]
. This rather formal definition of the extended symmetries of
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs is the most robust one that has been given to date.

In this article, we revisit the symmetries of classical
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs
in compact space by taking the classical limit of the quantum generators defined above, as the latter are guaranteed to have a well-defined action on the Hilbert space. The corresponding classical Hamiltonian generators are derived by explicitly solving the flow equation (
1.1
) in the classical limit. The field-dependent coordinates that are inherent to
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs turn out to simply emerge from the flow equation, which also fixes the ambiguity in their zero mode. The algebra of the resulting flowed generators is therefore completely unambiguous, and is given as expected by two commuting copies of the Witt algebra. The flowed generators turn out to precisely correspond to rescaled versions - by the field-dependent radii - of the ones in the natural Fourier basis, in perfect agreement with the holographic results. The algebra of the unrescaled generators is accordingly given by a non-linear modification of the Witt algebra.

We then translate the symmetries of
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs generated by these charges to Lagrangian language. We find that the resulting transformations are distinct from the previously proposed symmetries
[
23
]
in the Lagrangian context,
in two qualitatively different ways: first, the field-dependent coordinate transformation is different, and as a result it now needs to be accompanied by a Weyl rescaling of the metric in order for the action to be invariant; second, a rescaling of space and time by an amount proportional to the conserved charges of the background is necessary in order for the transformation of the fields to be consistent with momentum quantization. These symmetries are identical to those described in the recent holographic analysis of
[
22
]
. We thus obtain a fully consistent classical picture for the extended
T\bar{T}
symmetries, by showing they agree across all approaches that have been attempted.

Finally, we discuss how to extend our explicit classical results to the quantum case. The main challenge is that the
T\bar{T}
operator is not explicitly known at the quantum level. We discuss a perturbative algorithm that could fix it, at least in part. We also comment on a possible quantum definition of the “unrescaled” generators that classically correspond to the natural Fourier basis of symmetry generators.

This article is organised as follows. In section
2

T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs, following
[
25

[
17

4

2
Conserved charges from the classical limit of the
{T\bar{T}}
flow

We start by reviewing the rather remarkable fact
[
26
,
25
]
that, classically, the Hamiltonian density of a
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFT is given by an extremely simple and universal formula. We will be following a slightly rewritten version of
[
25
]
.

2.1
Hamiltonian formulation of classical
{T\bar{T}}
- deformed CFTs

In the Hamiltonian formalism, the
{T\bar{T}}
deformation is defined as

\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{H}=-\mathcal{O}^{cls}_{T\bar{T}}=-(T_{tt}T_{\sigma\sigma}-T_{t\sigma}^{2})

(2.1)

where we denote the stress tensor components
T_{tt}
and
T_{t\sigma}
by the standard notation
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{P}
and, in a classical field theory, the
T_{t\sigma}
and
T_{\sigma\sigma}
component are determined by the canonical variables and derivatives of the Hamiltonian density with respect to them. This information is more elegantly captured by the equal-time commutation relations of
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{P}
which, for an unconstrained system with an action that depends only on first time derivatives, take the form

\{\mathcal{H}(\sigma),\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\sigma})\}=(\mathcal{H}(\tilde{\sigma})+T_{\sigma\sigma}(\sigma))\,\delta^{\prime}(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})

(2.2)

\{\mathcal{H}(\sigma),\mathcal{H}(\tilde{\sigma})\}=\{\mathcal{P}(\sigma),\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\sigma})\}=(\mathcal{P}(\sigma)+\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\sigma}))\,\delta^{\prime}(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})

(2.3)

Note these commutators imply the conservation equations upon integration over the spatial coordinate,
\sigma
.
In a CFT,
T_{\sigma\sigma}=\mathcal{H}
by the vanishing of the trace of the stress tensor, but in a general classical field theory this will not be the case.

The authors of
[
25
]
derived the deformed Hamiltonian density from the flow equation (
2.1
), finding the universal expression (
2.5
) that only depends on the original Hamiltonian and momentum density, while
\mathcal{P}
remains undeformed. Alternatively, given the universal structure of the
{T\bar{T}}
deformation,
one may start by assuming that the deformed Hamiltonian density should be just a function of the undeformed Hamiltonian and momentum density. By itself, this is an extremely strong assumption that, when plugged into the
\{H(\sigma),\mathcal{H}(\tilde{\sigma})\}
Poisson bracket, is sufficient
to
completely fix the deformed Hamiltonian density in terms of the undeformed one, irrespectively of the flow equation (
2.1
)

\mathcal{H}=\pm\sqrt{\mathcal{P}^{2}+4a\mathcal{H}^{(0)}+4a^{2}}-b

(2.4)

where
a,b
are integration constants. We will choose the positive branch
and let
b=2a
to ensure
\mathcal{H}
reduces to
\mathcal{H}^{(0)}
in the
a\rightarrow\infty
limit. We then use the
\{\mathcal{H}(\sigma),\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\sigma})\}
commutator to determine
T_{\sigma\sigma}
. Promoting
a,b
to functions of
\mu
, we find that the
T\bar{T}
flow equation is entirely consistent with this solution, provided
2a=b=1/2\mu
. The exact classical solution for the Hamiltonian density of a
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFT is then

\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(\sqrt{1+4\mu\mathcal{H}^{(0)}+4\mu^{2}\mathcal{P}^{2}}-1\right)

(2.5)

irrespectively of the form of the Hamiltonian of the original CFT that is being deformed.

It follows from this exact expression that the commutation relations of
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{P}
are entirely determined by those of
\mathcal{H}^{(0)},\mathcal{P}
and are universal, fact that has been exploited to a large extent in
[
23
]
. In particular, we can use the commutator (
2.3
) to show that

T_{\sigma\sigma}=\frac{\mathcal{H}+2\mu\mathcal{P}^{2}}{1+2\mu\mathcal{H}}

(2.6)

This implies that classical
T\bar{T}
-deformed CFTs satisfy the trace relation

\tr\,T=T_{\sigma\sigma}-T_{tt}=-2\mu\,\frac{\mathcal{H}^{2}-\mathcal{P}^{2}}{1+2\mu\mathcal{H}}=-2\mu\,\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}^{cls}

(2.7)

It is interesting to ask whether this relation survives quantum-mechanically. One may use the fact that
\mu
is the only scale in the deformed CFT to argue that the trace relation must still hold when integrated over space-time
[
12
,
27
]
,
i.e. the local relation above is valid up to at most total derivative terms. Our quantum analysis in section
4
shows these terms are not present at least to first perturbative order in the
T\bar{T}
parameter.

2.2
The flow operator and its classical limit

As mentioned in the introduction, the Virasoro symmetries of
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs can be most rigorously understood by studying the flow of the original CFT symmetry generators under the operator that drives the flow of the energy eigenstates of
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs on the cylinder

\partial_{\mu}|n_{\mu}\rangle=\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}}|n_{\mu}\rangle

(2.8)

As described in
[
17
]
, this operator can be obtained from standard first order quantum-mechanical perturbation theory, and can be related to the change in the
T\bar{T}
Hamiltonian as

\partial_{\mu}H=D-[H,\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}}]

(2.9)

where
D
are the diagonal elements of
\partial_{\mu}H
in the energy eigenbasis. Thus, in order to recover
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}}
, we need to understand how to decompose
\partial_{\mu}H
into a diagonal piece in the energy eigenbasis and an off-diagonal one, which can be further written as a total commutator with
H
.

For this, remember the original definition of the
T\bar{T}
operator
[
28
]
as an antisymmetric bilinear in the stress tensor components regulated via point splitting

\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}(x)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon^{\gamma\delta}T_{\alpha\gamma}(x+\epsilon)\,T_{\beta\delta}(x)+\ldots

(2.10)

where the
\ldots
stand for total derivative terms. Such terms drop out when integrating the
T\bar{T}
operator over the entire spacetime, as is the case when adding the deformation to the action; however, here we would like to add this operator to the Hamiltonian, which is only integrated over space at a fixed time. Consequently, the change in the Hamiltonian induced by the
{T\bar{T}}
operator above takes the form

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}H

\displaystyle=-\int d\sigma\,\left(\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}+\frac{d}{dt}V\right)

(2.11)

where now
\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}
denotes only the point-splitted current bilinear in (
2.10
), which is potentially divergent, and
V
(which is also potentially divergent) should be chosen such that the resulting
\partial_{\mu}H
is a well-defined operator. Writing the time derivative of
V
as a commutator with the Hamiltonian, this term can be absorbed by a shift in
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}}
, using (
2.9
).

We would now like to compute the contribution of the
T\bar{T}
operator itself to
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}}
, which involves splitting it into a “diagonal” and an “off-diagonal” part, where the latter is written as a commutator with the Hamiltonian. For this, we follow the manipulations of
[
17
]
, taking into account operator ordering to the largest extent possible

\displaystyle\int d\sigma\,\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int d\sigma\,d\tilde{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{R}+G^{\prime}(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})\right)\epsilon^{ab}\epsilon^{cd}T_{ac}(\sigma)T_{bd}(\tilde{\sigma})

\displaystyle=\frac{HK+KH-2P^{2}}{2R}+\frac{1}{2}\int d\sigma\,d\tilde{\sigma}\,G(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})\epsilon^{cd}\left(T_{tc}\,\partial_{\tilde{\sigma}}\tilde{T}_{\sigma d}+\partial_{\sigma}T_{\sigma c}\,\tilde{T}_{td}\right)\

\displaystyle=\frac{HK+KH-2P^{2}}{2R}+\left[H,\frac{i}{2}\int d\sigma\,d\tilde{\sigma}\,G(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})(\mathcal{H}\tilde{\mathcal{P}}-\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{H}})\right]

(2.12)

where
K\equiv\int d\sigma T_{\sigma\sigma}
and
G(\sigma)
is the Green’s function of the spatial derivative on the cylinder of circumference
R

\displaystyle G(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\text{sign}(\sigma)-\frac{\sigma}{R}\ ,\qquad G^{\prime}(\sigma)=\delta(\sigma)-\frac{1}{R}

(2.13)

In the last step, we used a Hamiltonian form of the conservation equation, i.e. the commutators
[H,\mathcal{H}]=-i\partial_{\sigma}\mathcal{P}
and
[H,\mathcal{P}]=-i\partial_{\sigma}T_{\sigma\sigma}
, which can be inferred from
eq.

2.3
and are expected to hold at the full quantum level. The point-splitting parameter
\epsilon
is implicitly present through the ordering of the current operators.

According to our previous discussion, the integrated form of the trace equation (
2.7
) is

K=H-2\mu\int\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}+\frac{dW}{dt}

(2.14)

where the last term is a potential quantum ambiguity that is not present classically. It is not hard to see this term can again be absorbed into
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}}
, yielding a contribution proportional to
HW+WH
. We are thus left with the following equation for
\int d\sigma\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}
or, almost equivalently,
\partial_{\mu}H

\partial_{\mu}H+\frac{\mu}{R}(H\,\partial_{\mu}H+\partial_{\mu}H\,H)=-\frac{H^{2}-P^{2}}{R}-\left[H,\frac{i}{2}\int d\sigma\,d\tilde{\sigma}\,G(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})(\mathcal{H}\tilde{\mathcal{P}}-\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{H}})+\ldots\right]

(2.15)

where the
\ldots
stand for the ambiguities that we have discussed. This is an equation of the type
X+\mu HX+\mu XH=A
, whose solution takes the form

X=\sum_{m,n}\alpha_{m,n}\,\mu^{m+n}H^{m}\!AH^{n}

(2.16)

where the coefficients
\alpha_{m,n}
satisfy the recursion relation
\alpha_{m,n}+\alpha_{m-1,n}+\alpha_{m,n-1}=0
. Taking
A
to consist of the
\int G\mathcal{H}\mathcal{P}
term and the
\ldots
, this formally yields the solution for the flow operator.

For most of this article, we will be interested in the classical limit of the flow equation. Then, it is easy to show that the associated classical flow operator defined through
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}}\rightarrow_{cls}i\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}
is

\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}=\frac{R}{R_{H}}\int d\sigma\,d\tilde{\sigma}\,G(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})\mathcal{H}(\sigma)\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\sigma})\ ,\;\;\;\;\;\;R_{H}\equiv R+2\mu H

(2.17)

up to an undetermined phase space function whose Poisson bracket with the Hamiltonian vanishes and that we can freely set to zero. The relationship between the classical
T\bar{T}
and classical flow operator is

\partial_{\mu}H=-\int d\sigma\,\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}^{cls}=-\frac{H^{2}-P^{2}}{R_{H}}+\{H,\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}\}

(2.18)

Introducing the “covariant derivative” along the flow

\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}+\{\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}},\;\cdot\;\}

(2.19)

the above can be written as a flow equation that the Hamiltonian satisfies

\mathcal{D}_{\mu}H=-\frac{H^{2}-P^{2}}{R_{H}}

(2.20)

This equation holds in fact also quantum-mechanically, by replacing
\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}
by the corresponding operator, and Poisson brackets by commutators. We will denote the quantum version of
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}
by
\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
.

One can easily check that the expression (
2.17
) for
\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}
is consistent with expectations from the flow of the energy eigenstates on the cylinder. As argued in
[
24
]
, the universality of the
T\bar{T}
- deformed spectrum implies that the zero modes of the flowed Virasoro operators (
1.1
) are related to the Hamiltonian and momentum operators in
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs as

\widetilde{L}_{0}+\widetilde{\bar{L}}_{0}=H+\frac{\mu}{R}(H^{2}-P^{2})\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\widetilde{L}_{0}-\widetilde{\bar{L}}_{0}=P

(2.21)

Since
\tilde{L}_{0},\tilde{\bar{L}}_{0}
satisfy the homogeneous flow equation (
1.1
), we find
precisely the quantum version of the flow equation (
2.20
) above, together with
\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}P=0
. In fact, we could have alternatively defined
\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}
through the flow equations for the Virasoro zero modes that, as shown above, determine
the Poisson brackets of
\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}
with
H,P
in the classical limit. The solution is precisely (
2.17
), taking into account the fact that
\partial_{\mu}H=\int d\sigma\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{H}
, with
\mathcal{H}
given classically by (
2.5
).

The analysis we presented assumed that the energy eigenstates of the seed theory were non-degenerate. However, this assumption is not true for two-dimensional CFTs, where the Virasoro symmetry leads to a high level of degeneracy. The states of the system then carry an additional label in addition to the energy and momentum eigenvalues, for example the eigenvalue under the KdV charges. Taking this into account,
the
{T\bar{T}}
deformation can be treated using quantum-mechanical degenerate perturbation theory that does not get lifted at any order
[
29
]
.

The flow of the KdV charges as the
T\bar{T}
parameter is changed can be studied in a similar fashion to the flow of the Virasoro generators. Namely, one can define a flowed KdV generator via

\partial_{\mu}\tilde{P}_{s}=[\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}},\tilde{P}_{s}]

(2.22)

subject to the initial condition that at
\mu=0
,
\tilde{P}_{s}
corresponds to the KdV generator in the undeformed CFT. Since in the original CFT, the
P_{s}
have a known expression in terms of the Virasoro generators
L_{n}
, the above flow equation implies that the flowed
\tilde{P}_{s}
will be given by the
same
expression, but now in terms of the flowed generators
\tilde{L}_{n}
. The flow equation also implies that the eigenvalue of
\tilde{P}_{s}
in a flowed energy eigenstate is the same as the corresponding eigenvalue in the undeformed CFT.

At a first glance, this statement appears to contradict
the known fact
[
30
]
that the expectation values of the KdV charges in the
n
th energy eigenstate,
\braket{P_{s}}_{n}
, do flow nontrivially with
\mu
. The puzzle is resolved by noting that even the lowest KdV charges, namely the left- and right-moving energies, suffer from this problem: indeed, while the flow equation correctly indicates that the expectation value of
\langle\tilde{L}_{0}\rangle
is unchanged under the flow, the physical energies of the system are related to this expectation value as

\braket{{\tilde{L}}_{0}}=H_{L}R_{u}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\braket{{\tilde{\bar{L}}}_{0}}=H_{R}R_{v}

(2.23)

where
H_{L,R}\equiv(H\pm P)/2
are the left/right Hamiltonians and the quantities
R_{u,v}
(which will turn out to correspond to the radii of the field-dependent coordinates, hence the notation) are for now defined as

R_{u}=R+2\mu H_{R}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;R_{v}=R+2\mu H_{L}

(2.24)

Since, using (
2.20
), the quantities
R_{u,v}
satisfy the following flow equations

{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}R_{u}=\frac{2H_{R}R_{u}}{R_{H}}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}R_{v}=\frac{2H_{L}R_{v}}{R_{H}}

(2.25)

it follows that the physical energies will also satisfy non-trivial flow equations, as we already saw. The KdV charges work in the same way, as the ones considered in
[
30
]
differ by a factor of
R_{u}^{s}
from the
\tilde{P}_{s}
defined above. Since
R_{u}
flows non-trivially, so does
\tilde{P}_{s}/R_{u}^{s}
, and it can be explicit checked that the flow of these charges precisely agrees with that given in
[
30
]
. The advantage of recasting them in terms of flowed generators is that, at least classically, we gain access to their explicit expression in terms of the flowed left/right-moving stress tensor, whose construction we explain next.

2.3
Explicit solution to the flow equation

We would now like to use the classical flow operator,
\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}
, obtained in the previous subsection to solve explicitly for the classical counterparts, denoted
\tilde{Q}_{m}
, of the quantum generators
\tilde{L}_{m}
, which by definition
satisfy the homogeneous flow equation
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\tilde{Q}_{m}=0
. Writing

\widetilde{Q}_{m}=\int d\sigma e^{im(\sigma+t)}\mathscr{H}_{L}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\widetilde{\bar{Q}}_{m}=\int d\sigma e^{-im(\sigma-t)}\mathscr{H}_{R}

(2.26)

our task is equivalent to solving the following homogeneous flow equations for the currents

\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\mathscr{H}_{L,R}=0\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left.\mathscr{H}_{L,R}\right|_{\mu=0}=\mathcal{H}^{(0)}_{L,R}

(2.27)

Note that charge conservation requires these currents to be (anti)chiral

\frac{d}{dt}\mathscr{H}_{L,R}=\pm\partial_{\sigma}\mathscr{H}_{L,R}

(2.28)

A natural starting point for constructing an explicit solution to the equations (
2.27
) is to consider the flow of the left/right Hamiltonian currents
\mathcal{H}_{L,R}=(\mathcal{H}\pm\mathcal{P})/2
, with
\mathcal{H}
given in (
2.5
). Setting
R=1
for simplicity, these read

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\mathcal{H}_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R_{H}}\partial_{\sigma}\left[2\mathcal{H}_{L}\left(\chi_{R}+\frac{\mu\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}}{R}\right)-\mu\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{H}\left(\chi_{\mathcal{P}}-\frac{2\mu\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}}{R}\right)\right]-\frac{2H_{R}}{R_{H}}\mathcal{H}_{L}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\mathcal{H}_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R_{H}}\partial_{\sigma}\left[2\mathcal{H}_{R}\left(\chi_{L}-\frac{\mu\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}}{R}\right)+\mu\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{H}\left(\chi_{\mathcal{P}}-\frac{2\mu\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}}{R}\right)\right]-\frac{2H_{L}}{R_{H}}\mathcal{H}_{R}

(2.29)

where
\chi_{\mathcal{P}}\equiv\chi_{L}-\chi_{R}
and the quantities
\chi_{L,R}
are defined as

\partial_{\sigma}\chi_{L,R}\equiv\mathcal{H}_{L,R}-H_{L,R}

(2.30)

which resembles the definitions of
[
23
]
, except that the zero mode of the function has been explicitly subtracted. The solution for them can be written compactly as

\chi_{L,R}=\int d\tilde{\sigma}\,G(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})\mathcal{H}_{L,R}(\tilde{\sigma})

(2.31)

One immediately notes, using the flow equations (
2.25
) for
R_{u,v}
, that the rescaled currents
R_{u}\mathcal{H}_{L}
and
R_{v}\mathcal{H}_{R}
satisfy flow equations whose right-hand-side is a total
\sigma
derivative

\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\left(R_{u}\mathcal{H}_{L}\right)=\partial_{\sigma}\mathcal{A}_{+}\;,~~~~~\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\left(R_{v}\mathcal{H}_{R}\right)=\partial_{\sigma}\mathcal{A}_{-}

(2.32)

where
\mathcal{A}_{\pm}
can be trivially read off from (
2.29
).
Introducing the notation

\Delta\hat{u}\equiv\frac{2\mu}{R_{u}}\left(\chi_{R}+\mu\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}\right)\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\Delta\hat{v}\equiv\frac{2\mu}{R_{v}}\left(\chi_{L}-\mu\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}\right),

(2.33)

we find that the flow equations for these quantities are given by

\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\Delta\hat{u}=\frac{2}{R_{u}R_{H}}\left(\chi_{R}+\mu\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}+\frac{\mu R_{H}}{R_{v}}\mathcal{A}_{-}\right)\;,~~~~~\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\Delta\hat{v}=\frac{2}{R_{v}R_{H}}\left(\chi_{L}-\mu\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}+\frac{\mu R_{H}}{R_{u}}\mathcal{A}_{+}\right)

(2.34)

To understand how the flow equation is solved, it is useful to first study the
\mu\rightarrow 0
limit of (
2.29
), in which only the
2\partial_{\sigma}(\mathcal{H}_{L}\chi_{R})
term survives on the right-hand-side of the first equation. Since
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\Delta\hat{u}=2\chi_{R}
in this limit, while
\Delta\hat{u}
itself is
\mathcal{O}(\mu)
, it is clear that this
\mathcal{O}(\mu^{0})
term in the flow equation can be absorbed by a term that corresponds to the
\mu\rightarrow 0
limit of
\partial_{\sigma}(\mathcal{H}_{L}\Delta\hat{u})
. Inspired by this, we evaluate, now exactly in
\mu

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\left(R_{u}\mathcal{H}_{L}(\Delta\hat{u})^{k}\right)=k(\Delta\hat{u})^{k-1}\mathcal{A}_{+}+\partial_{\sigma}\left[\mathcal{A}_{+}(\Delta\hat{u})^{k}\right]

(2.35)

An analogous equation holds for the right-movers. The solution to the flow equation (
2.27
) can now be iteratively constructed: starting from (
2.32
), one absorbs the term on the right-hand-side by subtracting
\partial_{\sigma}(R_{u}\mathcal{H}_{L}\Delta\hat{u})
from the initial trial function. Using (
2.35
) with
k=1
, we find

\mathcal{D}_{\mu}[R_{u}\mathcal{H}_{L}-\partial_{\sigma}(R_{u}\mathcal{H}_{L}\Delta\hat{u})]=-\partial_{\sigma}^{2}[\mathcal{A_{+}}(\Delta\hat{u})^{2}]

(2.36)

The term on the right-hand-side of this equation can be absorbed by adding
\partial_{\sigma}^{2}(R_{u}\mathcal{H}_{L}(\Delta\hat{u})^{2})
to the current, and so on. The full solution to the problem (
2.27
) is then, at least formally

\mathscr{H}_{L}=R_{u}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\partial_{\sigma}^{k}[\mathcal{H}_{L}(\Delta\hat{u})^{k}]\;,\;\;\;\;\;\mathscr{H}_{R}=R_{v}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\partial_{\sigma}^{k}[\mathcal{H}_{R}(\Delta\hat{v})^{k}]

(2.37)

The discussion so far holds for functions on phase space at
t=0
. One can check that these currents do not quite satisfy the chirality conditions required by charge conservation, obtained by replacing
\frac{d}{dt}
by
\partial_{t}-\{H,\;\cdot\;\}
in (

), unless they acquire an explicit time dependence. This can be easily achieved by adding to
\Delta\hat{u},\Delta\hat{v}
a piece that is linear in time, so now

\Delta\hat{u}=\frac{2\mu}{R_{u}}\left(\chi_{R}+\mu\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}\right)-\frac{4\mu H_{R}}{R_{H}}t\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\Delta\hat{v}=\frac{2\mu}{R_{v}}\left(\chi_{L}-\mu\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}\right)+\frac{4\mu H_{L}}{R_{H}}t

(2.38)

One could have alternatively obtained this time dependence by taking into account the fact that the states at
t\neq 0
differ by a factor of
e^{-iEt}
from those at
t=0
, which results in a contribution proportional to
\partial_{\mu}E\,t
to the flow operator, as also explained in
[
31
]
.

The conserved charges that satisfy the flow equation are simply given by the Fourier modes of the flowed currents, as in (
2.26
). Plugging in the explicit expressions
(
2.37
) and integrating by parts the total derivative terms, we find that the final result exponentiates into the following simple expressions

\widetilde{Q}_{n}=R_{u}\int d\sigma e^{in\left(\sigma+t+\Delta\hat{u}\right)}\mathcal{H}_{L}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\widetilde{\bar{Q}}_{n}=R_{v}\int d\sigma e^{-in\left(\sigma-t+\Delta\hat{v}\right)}\mathcal{H}_{R}

(2.39)

Thus, we note that the conserved charges can be rewritten in terms of the
physical
Hamiltonian densities
\mathcal{H}_{L,R}
, provided the functions that parametrize these charges now depend on two field-dependent coordinates given by

u\equiv R_{u}(\sigma+t+\Delta\hat{u})\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;v\equiv R_{v}(\sigma-t+\Delta\hat{v})

(2.40)

where the reason for pulling out the factors of
R_{u,v}
is that this way

\partial_{\sigma}u=1+2\mu\mathcal{H}_{R}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\partial_{\sigma}v=1+2\mu\mathcal{H}_{L}

(2.41)

exactly as in
[
23
]
, where we have used (
2.38
) and (
2.30
). From this perspective, the quantities
R_{u,v}
are the field-dependent radii of these field-dependent coordinates, thus explaining our notation.
The ratios
u/R_{u}
and
v/R_{v}
will be denoted henceforth as
\hat{u},\hat{v}
.
The time derivatives of these coordinates are given by

\partial_{t}u=\frac{R_{u}(1+2\mu P)}{R_{H}}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\frac{du}{dt}=\partial_{t}u-\{H,u\}=\frac{1+2\mu\mathcal{P}}{1+2\mu\mathcal{H}}\partial_{\sigma}u

(2.42)

and similarly for the right-movers

\partial_{t}v=-\frac{R_{v}(1-2\mu P)}{R_{H}}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\frac{dv}{dt}=\partial_{t}v-\{H,v\}=-\frac{1-2\mu\mathcal{P}}{1+2\mu\mathcal{H}}\partial_{\sigma}v

(2.43)

These equations lead to the conservation of any charge of the form (
2.45
) for arbitrary
m
.

It is interesting to note that the field-dependent coordinates (
2.40
) were not put in by hand; rather, they emerged from the flow equation for the currents. As a consequence, the expression for them is entirely unambiguous, and any Poisson bracket of these quantities can be explicitly computed using the expressions (
2.38
) and the Poisson brackets spelled out in the appendix. In particular, the Poisson brackets of
\chi_{L,R}
are entirely well-defined, as the zero mode of these quantities is zero by definition. There is therefore no obstruction to computing the charge algebra explicitly.

This is, however, an extremely tedious computation, which is superseded by the fact that the algebra of the generators
\widetilde{Q}_{m}
is Witt by construction.

i\{\widetilde{Q}_{m},\widetilde{Q}_{n}\}=(m-n)\,\widetilde{Q}_{m+n}

(2.44)

and similarly for the right-movers. Of course, the full quantum algebra is Virasoro, but our classical computation is, as usual, unable to capture the central extension. We have also computed this algebra by brute force using the explicit expressions for the charges and confirmed this result. Even though the intermediate steps of the calculation are rather involved,
the various contributions nevertheless conspire in such a way that the final result is remarkably a Witt algebra, in agreement with our expectation from the flow equation.

Note, from (
2.39
), that the flowed generators are proportional to an overall factor of
R_{u,v}
. It appears rather natural to define a set of “unrescaled” pseudoconformal symmetry generators as

Q_{m}=\int d\sigma\,e^{im\hat{u}}\,\mathcal{H}_{L}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\bar{Q}_{m}=\int d\sigma\,e^{-im\hat{v}}\,\mathcal{H}_{R}

(2.45)

which corresponds to the natural Fourier basis for the functions that label the conserved charges.
The algebra of these unrescaled generators is given by

i\{Q_{m},Q_{n}\}=\frac{1}{R_{u}}(m-n)Q_{m+n}+\frac{4\mu^{2}H_{R}}{R_{H}R_{u}}(m-n)Q_{m}Q_{n}

i\{\bar{Q}_{m},\bar{Q}_{n}\}=\frac{1}{R_{v}}(m-n)\bar{Q}_{m+n}+\frac{4\mu^{2}H_{L}}{R_{H}R_{v}}(m-n)\bar{Q}_{m}\bar{Q}_{n}

i\{Q_{m},\bar{Q}_{n}\}=-\frac{2\mu(m-n)}{R_{H}}Q_{m}\bar{Q}_{n}

(2.46)

We note that the commutators of the left/right Hamiltonian
H_{L}=Q_{0}
,
H_{R}=\bar{Q}_{0}
with the left conserved charges is given by

i\{H_{L},Q_{n}\}=-\frac{nR_{v}}{R_{H}}\,Q_{n}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;i\{H_{R},Q_{n}\}=\frac{2\mu nH_{R}}{R_{H}}\,Q_{n}

(2.47)

and similarly for the right movers. The form of these Poisson brackets is very important, as they imply that
i\{P,Q_{n}\}=-nQ_{n}
, and thus the commutation relations of the conserved charges are consistent with momentum quantization, as required if they are to have a well-defined action on the Hilbert space when the theory is in finite size. One can also check that these brackets precisely agree with the classical limit of the quantum commutators (
4.26
), first computed in
[
20
]
.

3
Lagrangian interpretation of the symmetries

In the previous section, we have discussed how the classical limit of the quantum flow equation, which is guaranteed to produce generators that have a well-defined action on phase space, yields the classical conserved charges of
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs in the Hamiltonian formalism, including an unambiguous definition of the field-dependent coordinates in this setting. In this section, we would like to understand the action of the associated symmetries in the Lagrangian formalism, as this is a more natural language for describing space-time symmetries.

This can be readily obtained by translating the action of the Hamiltonian generators on the fields to Lagrangian language. Interestingly, we find that the resulting symmetries differ from those proposed in
[
23
]
, though they exactly agree on-shell with the asymptotic symmetry group generators of the spacetime holographically dual to
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs
[
11
,
22
]
. In the following, we first review the relevant parts of these previous works, then compute the field transformations in the Hamiltonian formalism, and finally translate them to Lagrangian language, showing that the resulting transformations leave the action invariant.

3.1
Brief review of previous results

Let us start by reviewing the results of
[
23
]
. We consider a classical
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFT and work in null coordinates,
U,V=\sigma\pm t
. In these coordinates, the trace relation - which is an off-shell property - takes the form
T_{UV}=-2\mu(T_{UU}T_{VV}-T_{UV}^{2})
. Consequently, the stress tensor of this theory only has two independent components off-shell, which we parametrize by

\mu\mathcal{L}\equiv-\frac{T_{UV}}{T_{VV}}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\mu\bar{\mathcal{L}}\equiv-\frac{T_{VU}}{T_{UU}}

(3.1)

Given that under a coordinate transformation
x^{\alpha}\rightarrow x^{\alpha}+\xi^{\alpha}

\delta S=-\int d^{2}x\,T^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\,\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}

(3.2)

one can easily show that the action is invariant under
the following field-dependent coordinate transformations

U\rightarrow U+\epsilon f(u)\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;V\rightarrow V-\epsilon\bar{f}(v)

(3.3)

provided we choose the ratios of the derivatives of the field-dependent coordinates to be the same as that of the stress tensor components

\partial_{V}u=\mu\bar{\mathcal{L}}\partial_{U}u\;,\;\;\;\;\;\partial_{U}v=\mu\mathcal{L}\partial_{V}v

(3.4)

These equations were shown to always have a solution
off-shell
for
u,v
, which is important to show invariance of the action. On-shell,
\mathcal{L},\bar{\mathcal{L}}
become functions of only one of the field-dependent coordinates, namely
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(u)
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}=\bar{\mathcal{L}}(v)
, and it is moreover possible to choose the gauge
\partial_{u}U=\partial_{v}V=1
, where
\partial_{x}X
is the inverse matrix to
\partial_{X}x
. The components of this matrix satisfy

\partial_{v}U=-\mu\bar{\mathcal{L}}\partial_{u}U\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\partial_{u}V=-\mu\mathcal{L}\partial_{v}V

(3.5)

which, together with the previous gauge condition results in the following implicit on-shell solution for the field-dependent coordinates
u,v
in terms of the fixed ones and the on-shell stress tensor components

U=u-\mu\int^{v}\!\bar{\mathcal{L}}(v^{\prime})dv^{\prime}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;V=v-\mu\int^{u}\!\mathcal{L}(u^{\prime})du^{\prime}

(3.6)

The Noether charges associated with these symmetries take the form

Q_{f}=\int d\sigma f(\hat{u})\,\mathcal{H}_{L}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}}=\int d\sigma\,\bar{f}(\hat{v})\mathcal{H}_{R}

(3.7)

Due to the periodicity constraint,
f,\bar{f}
only depend on the rescaled field-dependent coordinates, which have fixed periodicity,
1
. The integrands
\mathcal{H}_{L,R}
denote the stress tensor components
T_{tU}
and, respectively,
-T_{tV}
, whose expression in terms of
\mathcal{L},\bar{\mathcal{L}}
reads

\mathcal{H}_{L}=\frac{\mathcal{L}(1+\mu\bar{\mathcal{L}})}{2(1-\mu^{2}\mathcal{L}\bar{\mathcal{L}})}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}\partial_{\sigma}u\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\mathcal{H}_{R}=\frac{\bar{\mathcal{L}}(1+\mu\mathcal{L})}{2(1-\mu^{2}\mathcal{L}\bar{\mathcal{L}})}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{\mathcal{L}}\partial_{\sigma}v

(3.8)

where the second set of equalities only holds on-shell. The left and right-moving global energies
H_{L,R}
are simply given by the choice
f=1
and, respectively,
\bar{f}=1
.

All this can be made very explicit for the case of the
T\bar{T}
- deformed free boson, whose action is

S=\frac{1}{4\mu}\int dUdV\left(1-\sqrt{1+8\mu\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}\right)

(3.9)

The stress tensor can be computed explicitly, yielding explicit formulae for
\mathcal{L},\bar{\mathcal{L}}
, and can be checked to satisfy the off-shell relation
trT=-2\mu\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}
. Under the above transformations, the variation of the basic field is

\delta_{f}\phi=-f(u)\partial_{U}\phi

(3.10)

One may use this to compute the variation of the conserved charges, for example
H_{L}
, under the diffeomorphism (
3.3
). Generally speaking, this variation is

\delta_{\xi}H_{L}=\int d\sigma\left(\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{U}T_{UU}-\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{V}T_{VU}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{L}}{\partial(\partial_{\beta}\phi)}\partial_{\beta}\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\right)

(3.11)

using the conservation of the stress tensor. For a left-moving diffeomorphism of the form (
3.3
), we find

\delta_{f}H_{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d\sigma\frac{\mathcal{L}\partial_{\sigma}f(\hat{u})}{(1+\mu\sqrt{\mathcal{L}\bar{\mathcal{L}}})^{2}}

(3.12)

which, strangely enough, does not yield a conserved charge of the form (
3.7
), as one would have expected from the assumed existence of a charge algebra.

Let us now also briefly summarize the relevant results from the holographic analyses of
[
11

and especially
[

- deformed holographic CFTs is AdS

\xi^{U}=f(u)+\mu\int^{v}\!\bar{\mathcal{L}}\bar{f}^{\prime}(v)\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\xi^{V}=\bar{f}(v)+\mu\int^{u}\!\mathcal{L}f^{\prime}(u)\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\xi^{\rho}=\rho\,(f^{\prime}(u)+\bar{f}^{\prime}(v))

(3.13)

where we have neglected the terms proportional to the holographic anomaly. These diffeomorphisms are on-shell, so
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(u)
etc. Quite importantly, the functions

must carry winding (linear in the respective coordinate) in order for these coordinate transformations to be well-defined, namely to not change the periodicity of the fixed
T\bar{T}
coordinates
U,V
. Nevertheless, the conserved charges associated to these diffeomorphisms only depend on the periodic part of the function

Note that the

w_{f}=\frac{2\mu R_{v}}{R_{H}}\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}+\frac{4\mu^{2}H_{R}}{R_{H}}Q_{f^{\prime}}\;,\;\;\;\;\;w_{\bar{f}}=-\frac{2\mu R_{u}}{R_{H}}Q_{f^{\prime}}-\frac{4\mu^{2}H_{L}}{R_{H}}\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}

The winding contribution to the asymptotic diffeomorphism has been referred to as a “compensating diffeomorphism” in

Notice that the proposed symmetry transformations in the Lagrangian and holographic settings are different. For example, for a purely left-moving transformation holography predicts

\xi^{U}=f(u)-\frac{2\mu Q_{f^{\prime}}}{R_{H}}(u-R_{u}U)\;,\;\;\;\;\xi^{V}=\mu\int^{u}\!\mathcal{L}f^{\prime}-\frac{2\mu Q_{f^{\prime}}}{R_{H}}(v+2\mu H_{R}V)\;,\;\;\;\;\xi^{\rho}=\rho\left(f^{\prime}-\frac{2\mu Q_{f^{\prime}}}{R_{H}}\right)

. In the following, we would like to understand which Lagrangian symmetries to consider, by making a comparison with the results of the Hamiltonian analysis.

3.2
Symmetry variations in the Hamiltonian formalism

The starting point of the Hamiltonian analysis are the conserved charges, which are given by

Q_{f}=\int d\sigma f(u)\mathcal{H}_{L}

(3.17)

where
\mathcal{H}_{L}
is again a function of the canonical variables, as in section

\delta_{f}\phi=\{Q_{f},\phi\}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\delta_{f}\pi=\{Q_{f},\pi\}

(3.18)

where we will be considering the action of the symmetries on a single boson; the generalisation to several fields is trivial. Translating the right-hand side to the Lagrangian formalism using
\dot{\phi}=\partial_{\pi}\mathcal{H}
points towards the transformation that we should be using. To compute the above field variations, we use the standard commutation relations

\{\mathcal{H},\tilde{\phi}\}=-\partial_{\pi}\mathcal{H}(\sigma)\,\delta^{\prime}(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\{\mathcal{P},\tilde{\phi}\}=-\phi^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\delta^{\prime}(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})

(3.19)

where the second term is a consequence of
\mathcal{P}=\pi\phi^{\prime}
. All the Poisson brackets that follow can be derived from these basic commutators. Let us first compute
\delta_{f}\phi

\{Q_{f},\phi\}=-f(\hat{u})\,\frac{\phi^{\prime}+\partial_{\pi}\mathcal{H}}{2}+\int d\tilde{\sigma}\hat{f}^{\prime}(\hat{\tilde{u}})\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{L}\left\{\frac{\tilde{u}}{R_{u}},\phi\right\}

(3.20)

denotes the fact that the derivative is taken with respect to
\hat{u}
, rather than

Using
\dot{\phi}=\partial_{\pi}\mathcal{H}

3.10

\left\{\frac{\tilde{u}}{R_{u}},\phi\right\}=\frac{2\mu\Delta\hat{u}}{R_{H}}\,\frac{\phi^{\prime}+\partial_{\pi}\mathcal{H}}{2}+\frac{2\mu}{R_{u}}\left(G(\tilde{\sigma}-\sigma)+\frac{R_{v}\Delta\hat{v}}{R_{H}}-\Delta\hat{\tilde{u}}-2t\right)\,\frac{\phi^{\prime}-\partial_{\pi}\mathcal{H}}{2}

where

\delta_{f}\phi=-\left(f(\hat{u})-\frac{2\mu\Delta\hat{u}}{R_{H}}Q_{\hat{f}^{\prime}}\right)\,\frac{\phi^{\prime}+\partial_{\pi}\mathcal{H}}{2}+\frac{2\mu}{R_{u}}\left[\mathcal{F}_{f}+Q_{\hat{f}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{R_{v}\Delta\hat{v}}{R_{H}}-2t\right)\right]\,\frac{\phi^{\prime}-\partial_{\pi}\mathcal{H}}{2}

where we have defined

\mathcal{F}_{f}(\sigma)\equiv\int d\tilde{\sigma}f^{\prime}(\hat{\tilde{u}})\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{L}[G(\tilde{\sigma}-\sigma)-\Delta\hat{\tilde{u}}]

(3.23)

\displaystyle\{Q_{f},\pi\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{\sigma}\left(f(\hat{u})\frac{\pi+\partial_{\phi^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}}{2}\right)+\int d\tilde{\sigma}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{L}\hat{f}^{\prime}(\hat{\tilde{u}})\left\{\frac{\tilde{u}}{R_{u}},\pi\right\}

(3.24)

\displaystyle-\partial_{\sigma}\left[\left(f(\hat{u})-\frac{2\mu\Delta\hat{u}}{R_{H}}Q_{\hat{f}^{\prime}}\right)\,\frac{\partial_{\phi^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}+\pi}{2}+\frac{2\mu}{R_{u}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{f}+Q_{\hat{f}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{R_{v}\Delta\hat{v}}{R_{H}}-2t\right)\right)\,\frac{\partial_{\phi^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}-\pi}{2}\right]

using the Poisson bracket of the field-dependent coordinate with the conjugate momentum

(3.25)

These expressions for the

\delta_{f}\phi=-\xi^{t}\partial_{\pi}\mathcal{H}-\xi^{\sigma}\phi^{\prime}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\delta_{f}\pi=-\partial_{\sigma}(\xi^{t}\partial_{\phi^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}+\xi^{\sigma}\pi)

where the components

\xi^{t}=\frac{\partial Q_{f}}{\partial\mathcal{H}}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\xi^{\sigma}=\frac{\partial Q_{f}}{\partial\mathcal{P}}

(3.27)

where the derivative uses the fact that the field-dependent coordinates in

- deformed CFTs are entirely constructed from the Hamiltonian and momentum densities.

3.3
From Hamiltonian to Lagrangian transformations

We would now like to translate these symmetry transformations to the Lagrangian formalism. The explicit components of the (left-moving) diffeomorphism we have found are

\xi^{U}=f(\hat{u})-\frac{2\mu Q_{\hat{f}^{\prime}}}{R_{H}}(\hat{u}-U)\;,\;\;\;\;\;\xi^{V}=-\frac{2\mu}{R_{u}}(\mathcal{F}_{f}-Q_{\hat{f}^{\prime}}U)-\frac{2\mu Q_{\hat{f}^{\prime}}}{R_{u}R_{H}}(R_{v}\hat{v}+2\mu H_{R}V)

only upon using
both
Hamiltonian equations of motion.
.
Replacing
\mathcal{H}_{L,R}
by their Lagrangian expressions (
3.8
) and using the expressions for the stress tensor components given e.g. in
[
23
]
, we deduce that in the Lagrangian formalism

\partial_{U}\mathcal{F}_{f}=-\hat{f}^{\prime}T_{UU}+Q_{\hat{f}^{\prime}}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\partial_{V}\mathcal{F}_{f}=\hat{f}^{\prime}T_{UV}

(3.30)

Note that, on-shell, the resulting diffeomorphisms precisely agree with (
3.16
), including the winding terms, upon taking into account the fact that
Q_{f^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{R_{u}}Q_{\hat{f}^{\prime}}
.

The variation of the action under this diffeomorphism is given by (
3.2
). Assuming
for simplicity that
Q_{f^{\prime}}
is constant, we compute

T_{\alpha\beta}\,\partial^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}=-2\left(f^{\prime}(u)-\frac{2\mu Q_{f^{\prime}}}{R_{H}}\right)T_{UV}

(3.31)

and thus the action is not invariant. However, since the overall non-invariance of the action is proportional to the trace of the stress tensor, one immediately notes that it can be absorbed by a Weyl transformation
g_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu}
, with

\Omega^{2}=1+\left(f^{\prime}-\frac{2\mu Q_{f^{\prime}}}{R_{H}}\right)

(3.32)

where
f
is assumed to be infinitesimal.
The last term precisely agrees with the radial component of the diffeomorphism (
3.16
) in the dual space-time, which is known to implement Weyl transformations in the boundary theory.

Thus, we find that the symmetries in the Lagrangian formalism that correspond to those generated by
Q_{f},\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}}
are given by the field-dependent coordinate transformation (
3.28
), accompanied by a Weyl rescaling (
3.32
) of the metric.
These symmetry transformations are in perfect agreement with the results of the holographic analyses. They also turn out to lead to well-defined charge variations, directly in the Lagrangian formalism. Note that from the point of view of the Lagrangian variations, the charges
Q_{f^{\prime}}
that enter the diffeomorphism much be taken to be space-time constants, e.g. by projecting onto the temporal zero mode. This is related to the fact that the Hamiltonian and Lagrangian symmetries only need to agree on-shell.

The Noether current associated with these symmetries consists of the standard
\xi^{\alpha}T_{\alpha\beta}
term, but also receives contributions from the Weyl transformation. Denoting the scale factor of the metric as
\psi
, its variation found above takes the form

\delta\psi=R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}=f^{\prime}-\frac{2\mu Q_{f^{\prime}}}{R_{H}}

(3.33)

for some tensor
R_{\alpha\beta}
, not necessarily symmetric. Taking into account the contribution of this term to the variation of the action, we find that, on-shell, we should have

\partial_{\alpha}(T^{\alpha}_{\beta}\xi^{\beta})+2T_{UV}\delta\psi=\partial_{\alpha}J^{\alpha}

(3.34)

where
J^{\alpha}
is the Noether current associated to the full symmetry. Plugging in the expression for
\delta\psi
in terms of
R_{\alpha\beta}
, this can happen if the resulting term
T_{UV}R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}
is conserved on-shell, as is the case if it is proportional to the stress tensor. The choice
R_{\alpha U}=0
and
T_{UV}R_{\alpha V}=-\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha V}
does precisely the job, where the coefficient is fixed by (
3.33
). The resulting Noether current is

J_{\alpha}=(T_{\alpha\beta}+2T_{UV}R_{\alpha\beta})\xi^{\beta}=T_{\alpha U}\xi^{U}

(3.35)

in perfect agreement with the result of the Hamiltonian formalism and our previous expressions for the charges.

4
Quantum aspects of the symmetries

What makes the analysis of
classical

T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs particularly simple is the fact that the
{T\bar{T}}
“operator” is exactly known. This is no longer the case in the quantum theory, where derivative ambiguities in the definition of the full
{T\bar{T}}
operator (which, in our notation, would correspond to the difference between the operator
\partial_{\mu}\hat{\mathcal{H}}
and the point-split expression (
2.10
)) become important. These corrections are nevertheless somewhat difficult to compute, and it is not clear whether a well-defined prescription exists for fixing all the ambiguities that arise
[
16
]
.

In this section, we study quantum
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs to leading order in the
{T\bar{T}}
coupling, and check whether the relation between
\partial_{\mu}H
and the integrated point-split operator (
2.10
), as well as the trace relation (
2.7
) receive quantum corrections to this order. We then discuss how these quantum corrections are expected to propagate to the flow operator and the conserved charges, as well as to the relation between the flowed and the “unrescaled” symmetry generators.

4.1
Perturbative corrections to the
{T\bar{T}}
operator

In section
2.1
, we have discussed how, classically, the
{T\bar{T}}
Hamiltonian density is entirely fixed by the commutation relations of the translational currents, themselves dictated by the conservation equation and the assumption that the theory only depends on first time derivatives. The
{T\bar{T}}
flow equation for the Hamiltonian density was simply consistent with this solution.

This suggests a way of proceeding in the quantum case, namely by fixing the Hamiltonian density via the current commutation relations. This is guaranteed to yield an expression for
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(\mu)
, though not necessarily an unambiguous one. The
\mu
derivative of this expression may or may not agree with the point-split
{T\bar{T}}
operator (
2.10
), and one may also explicitly check whether the local trace relation (
2.7
) is being satisfied.

We will be denoting the Fourier modes of
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{P}
and
T_{\sigma\sigma}
as
H_{m},P_{m}
and respectively
K_{m}
. The classical commutators (
2.3
) read, in this notation

[H_{m},H_{n}]=[P_{m},P_{n}]=(m-n)P_{m+n}\;,\;\;\;\;\;[H_{m},P_{n}]=mH_{m+n}-nK_{m+n}

(4.1)

In a CFT
2
one has, in terms of the more standard generators
L_{m},\bar{L}_{m}

H_{m}^{(0)}=K_{m}^{(0)}=L_{m}+\bar{L}_{-m}\;,\;\;\;\;P_{m}^{(0)}=L_{m}-\bar{L}_{-m}

(4.2)

and one can easily check the commutation relations are satisfied also quantum-mechanically if one adds
\frac{c}{6}m^{3}\delta_{m+n}
to the right-hand-side of the second equation. More generally, the first two equations in (
4.1
) must be satisfied for either
m
or
n
equal to zero, whereas the second equation must be satisfied when
m=0
, as in these cases correspond to the current conservation equations.

According to our discussion in section
2.2
, quantum-mechanically the components of the stress tensor satisfy

\partial_{\mu}H_{m}=-(\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}})_{m}+mV_{m}\;,\;\;\;\;\;K_{m}=H_{m}-2\mu(\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}})_{m}+mW_{m}

(4.3)

where
(\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}})_{m}
is the
m^{th}
Fourier mode of the point-split expression (
2.10
)

(\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}})_{m}=\sum_{k}\frac{1}{2}(H_{k}K_{m-k}+K_{k}H_{m-k})-P_{k}P_{m-k}

(4.4)

and
V_{m},W_{m}
represent total derivative ambiguities. The factor of
m
that multiplies them is due to the fact that these ambiguities are assumed to be local, case in which a time derivative (or, a commutator with the Hamiltonian) brings down a factor of
m
.

We would now like to compute these corrections to first order in perturbation theory. Classically, we have

\mathcal{H}(\sigma)=\mathcal{H}^{(0)}-4\mu\mathcal{H}_{L}^{(0)}\mathcal{H}_{R}^{(0)}+8\mu^{2}\mathcal{H}^{(0)}\mathcal{H}_{L}^{(0)}\mathcal{H}_{R}^{(0)}+\mathcal{O}(\mu^{3})\;,\;\;\;\;\;\mathcal{P}(\sigma)=\mathcal{P}^{(0)}

(4.5)

\mathcal{K}(\sigma)=\mathcal{H}^{(0)}-12\mu\mathcal{H}_{L}^{(0)}\mathcal{H}_{R}^{(0)}+10\mu^{2}\mathcal{H}^{(0)}(\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}-\mathcal{P}^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\mu^{3})

(4.6)

We see that to first order in
\mu
there are no ordering ambiguities at the quantum level, though we could have quantum corrections. We thus make the following Ansatz for the quantum generators up to
\mathcal{O}(\mu)

H_{m}=L_{m}+\bar{L}_{-m}-4\mu\sum_{k}L_{m+k}\bar{L}_{k}+\Delta H_{m}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;P_{m}=L_{m}-\bar{L}_{-m}+\Delta P_{m}

(4.7)

where
L_{m},\bar{L}_{m}
are the
undeformed
CFT generators and the corrections are assumed to take the general form

\Delta H_{m}=\alpha_{m}(L_{m}+\bar{L}_{-m})+\gamma_{m}(L_{m}-\bar{L}_{-m})\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\Delta P_{m}=\beta_{m}(L_{m}-\bar{L}_{-m})+\delta_{m}(L_{m}+\bar{L}_{-m})

(4.8)

where the coefficients are all
\mathcal{O}(\mu)
. Dimensional analysis then suggests that they should be proportional to
\mu m^{2}
, as this would correspond to e.g. a correction
of the form
\mu\,\partial^{2}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mu\,m^{2}H_{m}
. Note that the freedom to redefine the currents via an improvement term (which acts as
\partial_{\sigma}
on
\mathcal{H}
and as
[H,\,\cdot\;]
on
\mathcal{P}

\alpha_{m}
and
\beta_{m}
by the same amount, while an independent redefinition simultaneously shifts
\gamma_{m}
and
\delta_{m}
.

We will now compute the commutation relations of these currents, using the commutator of the Virasoro generators on the cylinder

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{c}{12}m^{3}\delta_{m+n}

(4.9)

where the Casimir energy has been absorbed into the definition of
L_{0}
. To
\mathcal{O}(\mu)

\displaystyle[H_{m},H_{n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m-n)P_{m+n}+(m-n)[\alpha_{m}+\alpha_{n}-\beta_{m+n}+\frac{\mu c}{3}(m^{2}+n^{2}+mn)]\,P^{(0)}_{m+n}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\;(m-n)(\gamma_{m}+\gamma_{n}-\delta_{m+n})\,H^{(0)}_{m+n}

\displaystyle\left[P_{m},P_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m-n)P_{m+n}+(m-n)(\beta_{m}+\beta_{n}-\beta_{m+n})P^{(0)}_{m+n}+(m-n)(\delta_{m}+\delta_{n}-\delta_{m+n})H^{(0)}_{m+n}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

The terms proportional to

Writing
\alpha_{m}=\alpha m^{2}

\beta_{m}=\beta m^{2}

\alpha-\beta=-\frac{\mu c}{3}

(4.11)

Thus, we find that current conservation fixes precisely the part of the correction to the current that is not affected by the improvement ambiguity.
Similarly letting
\gamma_{m}=\gamma m^{2}

\delta_{m}=\delta m^{2}

, a relation that, again, is not affected by the improvement ambiguity. The final result for the commutators to this order is thus

Let us now move on to the

\displaystyle[H_{m},P_{n}]\!

We are now finally ready to check the relations (

(\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}})_{m}=4\sum_{k}L_{m+k}\bar{L}_{k}

\partial_{\mu}H_{m}+(\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}})_{m}=\partial_{\mu}\alpha_{m}H^{(0)}_{m}+\partial_{\mu}\gamma_{m}P_{m}^{(0)}

Consequently, while the flow equation for the Hamiltonian density could acquire non-trivial corrections that depend on the choice of improvement terms, the trace relation for the stress tensor density does appear to be obeyed exactly to this order, regardless of improvement ambiguities. Note that
\partial_{\mu}H
itself is not corrected at this order, thanks to the locality assumption on the form of the corrections that we have made. However, the first-order corrections to the currents will enter the Hamiltonian at the next order, though their contribution to the
T\bar{T}
operator. The correction proportional to

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}})_{0}

\displaystyle\langle h_{\mu}|(\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}})_{0}|h_{\mu}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle h|(\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}})_{0}|h\rangle+8\mu\langle h|\sum_{m}L_{m}\bar{L}_{m}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}L_{-k}\bar{L}_{-k}|h\rangle+8\mu\langle h|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}L_{k}\bar{L}_{k}\sum_{m}L_{m}\bar{L}_{m}|h\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip-82.51282pt=\;4h\bar{h}-16\mu h\bar{h}(h+\bar{h})+2(h+\bar{h})\left(\frac{2\mu c}{3}+\alpha+\epsilon-2\beta\right)\sum_{1}^{\infty}k^{3}+2(h-\bar{h})(\gamma+\theta-\delta)\sum_{1}^{\infty}k^{3}+

where

T\bar{T}
operator to this order, and moreover we
obtain exactly the same constraint on the unambiguous coefficients as from the commutation relations. We are however left with a divergent term proportional to

(4.23)

are known

and the

4.2
Brief comment on the quantum symmetry generators

Suppose the expression for
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}}
is known at the full quantum level. Then, the full expression for the Virasoro generators (
1.1
) can be unambiguously obtained. It is an interesting question, though, to understand the fate of the “unrescaled” generators at the full quantum level.

The reason that one should be interested in this different basis of symmetry generators comes from the related study of
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs. These correspond to an irrelevant deformation of a two-dimensional CFT by an operator of dimension
(1,2)
, which results in a theory that is local and conformal on the left, and non-local only on the right
[
32
]
. There too, one may obtain two commuting sets of Virasoro-Kac-Moody generators by transporting the original CFT generators (which, in this case, also contain an affine
U(1)
current) along the
J\bar{T}
flow
[
20
]
. However, one finds that the flowed left-moving generators are explicitly
different
from the physical Virasoro generators associated to the conformal symmetry
[
31
]
. Consequently, the flowed generators are not the most natural set of generators with respect to which to define operator Ward identities, for example
[
33
]
. In the case of
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs, it appears that the closest analogues of such “physical” operators are the “unrescaled” symmetry generators, which count among them the energy and momentum operators in the theory.

At the classical level, the relation between the flowed and the unrescaled generators is given by a simple multiplicative factor of the field-dependent radius. Quantum-mechanically, the radius is an operator, and does not commute with the symmetry generator under consideration. Therefore, one needs to specify the operator ordering when writing the relation between the (completely unambiguous) Virasoro generators and the unrescaled ones. Given the symmetrized form (
2.15
) of the flow equations, a natural guess for the relation between the unrescaled (
L_{m}
) and flowed (
\widetilde{L}_{m}
) generators is

\tilde{L}_{m}=L_{m}+\mu H_{R}L_{m}+\mu L_{m}H_{R}

(4.25)

which respects hermiticity and has the correct classical limit. The non-linear algebra of the
L_{m}
generators can be straightforwardly computed using the quantum commutation relations with the Hamiltonian

[L_{m},H]=\alpha_{m}(H,P)L_{m}\;,\;\;\;\;[L_{m},P]=m\hbar L_{m}

(4.26)

where

\alpha_{m}(H,P)=\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(\sqrt{(1+2\mu H)^{2}+4\mu m\hbar(1+2\mu P)+4\mu^{2}m^{2}\hbar^{2}}-(1+2\mu H)\right)

(4.27)

that were worked out in
[
20
]
using the exact quantum relation between
\tilde{L}_{0}
and
H,P
. The result is somewhat complicated, as the algebra receives corrections to all orders in
\hbar
, but not particularly illuminating, reason for which we do not spell it out. It is worth mentioning that, unlike in the case of
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs, the algebra of
L_{0,\pm 1}
does close, thus forming a non-linear subalgebra of the infinite-dimensional one. The algebra of the right-moving generators, as well as the mixed left-right commutators can be worked out using the right-moving analogue of
\alpha_{m}
, which is simply obtained via the replacement
P\rightarrow-P
. Note also that the
\hbar\rightarrow 0
limit of the above expression for
\alpha_{m}
precisely agrees with the classical Poisson brackets (
2.47
).

The main problem with the proposal (
4.25
) is that there is nothing preventing us from adding further terms proportional to
m
and the conserved charges to it; each such modification will alter the non-linear algebra of the
L_{m}
in a complicated, but entirely predictable way. One thus needs an independent definition of the operators
L_{m}
to fix the relationship between the two.

One attempted definition would be to study the quantum version of the flow equation (
2.29
), and demand that all the terms that do not resum into the field-dependent coordinate be considered as contributors to the relationship between
\tilde{L}_{m}
and
L_{m}
. These would of course include all the terms that do not contain a
\sigma
derivative in the quantum analogue of (
2.29
), which already lead to (
4.25
); however, depending on how the formal series in the field-dependent coordinate is to be defined, there could also be derivative terms that do not fit into the definition of the “quantum” field-dependent coordinate and its various powers, which would result into corrections proportional to powers of
m
. An explicit expression for the quantum flow operator
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{T\bar{T}}
to at least a few orders in perturbation theory would be the minimum necessary to understand the required patten, if any.

Another possible way of fixing these generators would be to study their action on quantum fields in the theory. Of course, they would be implementing “operator-dependent” coordinate transformations, a concept whose meaning is not clear
a priori
; working out an explicit example, e.g. for the case of the
T\bar{T}
- deformed free boson, could shed light on this interesting question.

A final possibility is that the unrescaled generators satisfy a non-homogeneous, yet fixed flow equation with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{T\bar{T}}
, where the non-homogeneous term is known to all orders in
\hbar
, as is the case for the
T\bar{T}
Hamiltonian. If such a definition is possible, then one should of course understand the reason behind it.

5
Conclusions

In this article, we have derived the classical symmetries of
T\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs, taking the well-defined quantum generators as a starting point. The classical generators implement field-dependent coordinate transformations, where the field-dependent coordinate emerges from the classical limit of the
T\bar{T}
flow and is entirely unambiguous. We then translated our results to the Lagrangian framework, finding a different set of transformations that leave the action invariant than were previously proposed in
[
23
]
, which now consist of a field-dependent coordinate transformation accompanied by a Weyl rescaling of the flat background metric. These transformations precisely agree with those found in the holographic analyses of

A natural generalization of our work would be to understand these symmetries at the quantum level. The flow-equation-based approach presented in this article would require an explicit understanding of the flow operator at the quantum level. We have hinted at an approach based on the consistency of the current commutators that may at least partly fix the ambiguities in its construction; whether this is able to completely fix the

Another important direction is to understand the action of these symmetries on the operators in the theory, appropriately defined. An analogy with the related case of

Understanding the full physical implications of these infinitely-extended non-local symmetries may have important consequences for the various applications of the
T\bar{T}

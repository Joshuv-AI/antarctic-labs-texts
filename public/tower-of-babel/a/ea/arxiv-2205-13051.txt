Online Deep Equilibrium Learning for Regularization by Denoising
Jiaming Liu, Xiaojian Xu, Weijie Gan, Shirin Shoushtari, and Ulugbek S. Kamilov

Abstract
Plug-and-Play Priors (PnP) and Regularization by Denoising (RED) are widely-used frameworks for solving imaging inverse problems by computing fixed-points of operators combining physical measurement models and learned image priors. While traditional PnP/RED formulations have focused on priors specified using image denoisers, there is a growing interest in learning PnP/RED priors that are end-to-end optimal. The recent Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQ) framework has enabled memory-efficient end-to-end learning of PnP/RED priors by implicitly differentiating through the fixed-point equations without storing intermediate activation values. However, the dependence of the computational/memory complexity of the measurement models in PnP/RED on the total number of measurements leaves DEQ impractical for many imaging applications. We propose ODER as a new strategy for improving the efficiency of DEQ through stochastic approximations of the measurement models. We theoretically analyze ODER giving insights into its convergence and ability to approximate the traditional DEQ approach. Our numerical results suggest the potential improvements in training/testing complexity due to ODER on three distinct imaging applications.

Background

Inverse problems.
Many imaging problems—such as IDT, CT, and MRI—can be formulated as an inverse problem involving the recovery of an image
{\bm{x}^{\ast}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
from noisy measurements
{\bm{y}}={\bm{A}}{\bm{x}^{\ast}}+{\bm{e}}
,
where
{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is the measurement operator and
{\bm{e}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the noise. A common approach to estimate
{\bm{x}^{\ast}}
is to solve an optimization problem

{\widehat{\bm{x}}}=\mathop{\mathsf{arg\,min}}_{{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{g({\bm{x}})+h({\bm{x}})\right\},

(1)

where
g
is a data-fidelity term that quantifies consistency with the observed data
{\bm{y}}
and
h
is a regularizer that encodes prior knowledge on
{\bm{x}}
. A widely-used data-fidelity term and regularizer in inverse problems are
g({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{2}\|{\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}\|_{2}^{2}
and the
total variation (TV)
function
h({\bm{x}})=\tau\|{\bm{D}}{\bm{x}}\|_{1}
, where
{\bm{D}}
is the gradient operator and
\tau>0
is the regularization parameter
[
47
,
48
,
49
]
.

PnP, RED, and DU.
PnP
[
10
,
15
]
and RED
[
11
]
are two related classes of iterative algorithms that use
additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN)
denoisers, such as BM3D
[
50
]
or DnCNN
[
12
]
, as priors for inverse problems (see the recent review
[
51
]
). Since for general denoisers PnP/RED do not solve an optimization problem
[
18
]
, it is common to interpret PnP/RED as fixed-point iterations of some high-dimensional operators. For example, given a denoiser
{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
parameterized by a CNN with weights
{\bm{\theta}}
, the
steepest descent
variant of RED (SD-RED)
[
11
]
can be written as

{\bm{x}}^{k}={\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})={\bm{x}}^{k-1}-\gamma{\mathsf{G}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})\quad\text{with}\quad{\mathsf{G}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}})\,\coloneqq\,\nabla g({\bm{x}})+\tau({\bm{x}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}}))\;,

(2)

where
g
is the data-fidelity term, and
\gamma,\tau>0
are the step size and the regularization parameters, respectively. SD-RED thus seeks to compute a fixed-point
{\overline{\bm{x}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the operator
{\mathsf{T}}

{\overline{\bm{x}}}\in{\mathsf{Fix}}({\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}})\,\coloneqq\,\{{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}})={\bm{x}}\}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\mathsf{G}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})=\nabla g({\overline{\bm{x}}})+\tau({\overline{\bm{x}}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\overline{\bm{x}}}))={\bm{0}}\;,

(3)

The solutions of (
3
) balance the requirements to be both data-consistent (via
\nabla g
) and noise-free (via
({\mathsf{I}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}})
), which can be intuitively interpreted as finding an equilibrium between the physical measurement model and learned prior model. Remarkably, this heuristic of using denoisers not necessarily associated with any
h
within an iterative algorithm exhibited great empirical success
[
52
,
53
,
54
,
55
,
20
,
56
,
22
,
57
,
58
,
59
]
and spurred a great deal of theoretical work on PnP/RED
[
14
,
60
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
61
,
62
,
63
,
64
,
65
,
66
]
. It is worth mentioning that there has been considerable effort in reducing the
test-time
computational/memory complexity of PnP/RED by designing online and stochastic PnP/RED algorithms
[
61
,
21
,
67
,
65
]
.

DU (also known as
algorithm unrolling
) is a DL paradigm that has gained popularity due to its ability to systematically connect iterative algorithms and deep neural network architectures (see reviews in
[
3
,
68
]
). Many PnP/RED algorithms have been turned into DU architectures by parameterizing the operator
{\mathsf{D}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
as a CNN with weights
{\bm{\theta}}
, truncating the PnP/RED algorithm to a fixed number of iterations, and training the corresponding architecture end-to-end in a supervised fashion. Recent work has explored strategies for reducing the memory and computational complexity of training DU architectures
[
69
,
70
]
, However, a key bottleneck in DU training is the necessity to store the intermediate activation values required for computing the backpropagation updates, which fundamentally limits the number of unfolding layers one can practically use in large-scale applications.

DEQ.
DEQ
[
33
]
is a recent method for training infinite-depth, weight-tied feedforward networks by analytically backpropagating through the fixed points using implicit differentiation. The DEQ output is specified implicitly as a fixed point of an operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
parameterized by weights
{\bm{\theta}}

{\overline{\bm{x}}}={\mathsf{T}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})\;.

(4)

The DEQ forward pass estimates
{\overline{\bm{x}}}
in (
4
) by either running a fixed-point iteration or using an optimization algorithm. The DEQ backward pass produces gradients with respect to
{\bm{\theta}}
by implicitly differentiating through the fixed points without the knowledge of how they are estimated

\displaystyle\ell({\bm{\theta}})=\frac{1}{2}\|{\overline{\bm{x}}}({\bm{\theta}})-{\bm{x}^{\ast}}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}})=(\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\overline{\bm{x}}}))^{\mathsf{T}}\left({\mathsf{I}}-\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})\right)^{-{\mathsf{T}}}({\overline{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}^{\ast}}),

(5)

where
\ell
is the loss function,
{\bm{x}^{\ast}}
is the training label, and
{\mathsf{I}}
is the identity mapping. The vector product with the inverse-Jacobian in (
5
) can be approximated by solving the following fixed-point equation

\displaystyle{\overline{\bm{b}}}\,\coloneqq\,\left({\mathsf{I}}-\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})\right)^{-{\mathsf{T}}}({\overline{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}^{\ast}})\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\overline{\bm{b}}}=\left(\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})\right)^{{\mathsf{T}}}{\overline{\bm{b}}}+({\overline{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}^{\ast}})\,.

(6)

Recent work has also explored Jacobian-free DEQ by replacing the inverse-Jacobian with an identity mapping
{\mathsf{I}}
, leading to a faster training
[
37
]
.

The comparison of equations (
2
), (
3
), and (
4
) highlights an elegant connection between PnP/RED and DEQ. This connection was explored in the recent work
[
35
]
by using DEQ for learning the weights of the CNN prior
{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}
end-to-end within PnP/RED iterations. Within the framework of
[
35
]
, PnP/RED is used for the forward pass and a backward pass is obtained by using (
6
) on the PnP/RED operators. Specifically, the CNN prior in SD-RED can be trained by running the backward pass using
{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}
in (
2
)

\displaystyle{\bm{b}}^{k}={\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})=\left(\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})\right)^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{b}}^{k-1}+({\overline{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}^{\ast}}).

(7)

This work makes several new contributions to the existing literature on PnP/RED, DU, and DEQ. The focus is to explore the impact of approximating
{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}
in (
4
) with a “simpler” operator
\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}_{\bm{\theta}}
. Following
[
35
]
, we focus on inverse problems by using PnP/RED operators of form (
2
) that integrate the physical measurement models and learned CNN priors. We give algorithmic, theoretical, and numerical evidence that one can achieve significant memory/computational gains, while preserving the performance of the original DEQ approach in
[
35
]
. It is worth noting that the results here have the potential to generalize to many other implicit neural networks beyond those specified via PnP/RED.

3
Online Deep Equilibrium Method

We consider inverse problems where the data-fidelity term
g
can be expressed as

g({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{b}g_{i}({\bm{x}}),

(8)

where each
g_{i}
depends only on the subset
{\bm{y}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
of the full measurements
{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
as

\mathbb{R}^{m}=\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{b}}\quad\text{with}\quad m=m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{b}\;.

We are primarily interested in scenarios where the memory/computational complexity of the gradient
\nabla g
is proportional to
b
. Thus, when
b\rightarrow\infty
, the memory and computational complexity of traditional DEQ to train the CNN prior within the batch PnP/RED algorithms becomes impractical.

To decouple the computational/memory complexity of DEQ from
b
, we adopt
online
processing of measurements, where
g
is approximated using a minibatch of
w\ll b
measurements

{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{w}\sum_{s=1}^{w}g_{i_{s}}({\bm{x}})\quad\Rightarrow\quad\nabla{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{w}\sum_{s=1}^{w}\nabla g_{i_{s}}({\bm{x}})\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\mathsf{H}}{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{w}\sum_{s=1}^{w}{\mathsf{H}}g_{i_{s}}({\bm{x}})\;,

(9)

where
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{w}\}
are i.i.d random variables selected uniformly from the set
\{1,\dots,b\}
. Note that (
9
) directly implies the
unbiasedness
of the online gradient
\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}})\right]=\nabla g({\bm{x}})
and Hessian
\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathsf{H}}{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}})\right]={\mathsf{H}}g({\bm{x}})
with the expectations taken over the random indices
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{w}\}
.

3.1
Forward Pass

The forward-pass of ODER is performed as follows

{\bm{x}}^{k}={\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})={\bm{x}}^{k-1}-\gamma(\nabla{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})+\tau{\mathsf{R}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})),\quad k=1,2,\dots,K,

(10)

where
{\mathsf{R}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}={\mathsf{I}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
is the residual of the CNN prior
{\mathsf{D}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
. The residual
{\mathsf{R}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
takes artifact-corrupted images at the input and produces the corresponding artifacts at the output. Note how the ODER forward-pass is independent of
b
since it uses a minibatch approximation
\nabla{\widehat{{g}}}
in (
9
).

It is worth mentioning that when considered separately from DEQ, the forward pass of ODER corresponds to the existing online RED algorithm
[
71
,
21
]
. The contribution of this work is thus not the forward pass, but the integration of the online forward pass into the DEQ framework via the online backward pass, resulting in a more scalable and flexible DEQ framework for inverse problems.

3.2
Backward Pass

The backward pass of ODER for the MSE loss in (
5
) is performed as follows

\displaystyle{\bm{b}}^{k}={\widehat{\mathsf{F}}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})=\left[\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}_{\bm{\theta}}\left({\bm{x}}^{K}\right)\right]^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{b}}^{k-1}+({\bm{x}}^{K}-{\bm{x}^{\ast}}),\quad k=1,2,\dots,K,

(11)

starting from
{\bm{b}}^{0}={\bm{0}}
, where
{\bm{x}}^{K}
is the final iterate of the forward pass (
10
) at iteration
K\geq 1
. In both traditional and online backward passes, conventional auto-differentiation tools enable the computation of the Jacobian-vector products in (
7
) and (
11
). However, the key difference is that the computational complexity of ODER does not depend on the total number of measurements
b
.

3.3
ODER Learning

ODER seeks to minimize the MSE loss over
p\geq 1
training samples

\displaystyle\ell({\bm{\theta}})=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}})\quad\text{with}\quad\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}})=\frac{1}{2}\|{\overline{\bm{x}}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})-{\bm{x}}_{j}^{\ast}\|_{2}^{2}\,,

(12)

using approximate gradients
\nabla\hat{\ell}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})
computed via the online forward and backward passes that are independent of
b
. Here,
{\bm{\theta}}
denotes the weights of the CNN prior,
{\bm{x}}_{j}^{\ast}
is the
j
th training label, and
{\overline{\bm{x}}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})
is the fixed-point of the full-batch SD-RED algorithm (
3
). The backward pass of ODER can be integrated within any gradient-based optimizer, such as the
stochastic gradient descent (SGD)
. At training iteration
t\geq 1
we generate two sets of independent random variables. First, the index
j_{t}
is selected uniformly at random from
\{1,\dots,p\}
, then online forward and backward passes are computed using the measurement models in (
9
). We can thus express the SGD update rule as follows

\displaystyle{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}={\bm{\theta}}^{t}-\beta\nabla\hat{\ell}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\quad\text{with}\quad\nabla\hat{\ell}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})=\left[\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{t}}({{\bm{x}}_{j_{t}}^{K}})\right]^{{\mathsf{T}}}{\bm{b}}_{j_{t}}^{K},

(13)

where
\beta>0
is the SGD learning rate,
{\bm{x}}_{j_{t}}^{K}
and
{\bm{b}}_{j_{t}}^{K}
are the final iterates of the ODER forward and backward passes at the training index
j_{t}
after
K\geq 1
iterations.

4
Theoretical Analys

Our theoretical analysis relies on a set of explicit assumptions serving as sufficient conditions. The proofs of all the theorems will be provided in the supplement.

Assumption 1
.

Each
g_{i}
is twice continuously differentiable and convex. There exists
\lambda>0
such that each gradient
\nabla g_{i}
and Hessian
{\mathsf{H}}g_{i}
are
\lambda
-Lipschitz continuous.

The fact that
g
is twice continuously differentiable is needed for the backward pass. The assumption that all the Lipschitz constants are the same is only needed to streamline mathematical exposition.

Assumption 2
.

{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}})
is continuously differentiable with respect to
{\bm{\theta}}
and
{\bm{x}}
. There exists
\alpha>0
such that
{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}})
,
\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}})
, and
\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}})
are
\alpha
-Lipschitz continuous with respect to
{\bm{\theta}}
and
{\bm{x}}
. Finally, we also assume that
{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}
is a contraction, which means that there exists
\kappa<1
such that

\|{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{z}})-{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{y}})\|_{2}\leq\kappa\|{\bm{z}}-{\bm{y}}\|_{2},\quad\forall{\bm{z}},{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Since
{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}
is a CNN, its differentiability is a standard assumption. The contractive
{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}
and convex
g
, ensure that
{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}
is a contraction, enabling provable convergence of the forward and backward passes. The design of contractive
{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}
is a common PnP/RED strategy to ensure convergence
[
19
,
35
,
65
]
.

Assumption 3
.

There exists
R>0
such that for all
{\overline{\bm{x}}}\in{\mathsf{Fix}}({\mathsf{T}})
and
{\overline{\bm{b}}}\in{\mathsf{Fix}}({\mathsf{F}})
, we have
\|{\bm{x}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\leq R
and
\|{\bm{b}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}\leq R
for all
k\in\{1,\dots,K\}
.

The existence of the bound
R
is reasonable, as many images have bounded pixel values. Similarly, the bound on
{\bm{b}}^{k}
is also reasonable for ensuring bounded DEQ gradients.

Assumption 4
.

There exists
\nu>0
such that for all
{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla g({\bm{x}})-\nabla{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\nu^{2}}{w}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathsf{H}}g({\bm{x}})-{\mathsf{H}}{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\nu^{2}}{w},

where the expectations are taken over
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{w}\}
.

The variance bounds are standard in stochastic algorithms. The variance bounds on the gradient and Hessian approximations are thus reasonable in this context. The decrease of the bounds for higher values of
w
is natural since
{\widehat{{g}}}
is an unbiased estimator of
g
obtained averaging
w
independent terms.

Theorem 1
.

Run the forward pass of ODER for
k\geq 1
iterations under Assumptions
1
-
4
using the step size
0<\gamma<1/(\lambda+\tau)
. Then, the sequence of forward pass iterates satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{x}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\right]\leq\eta^{k}R+\frac{\gamma\nu}{(1-\eta)\sqrt{w}},

(14)

for some constant
0<\eta<1
where
{\overline{\bm{x}}}\in{\mathsf{Fix}}({\mathsf{T}})
.

Theorem
1
is a variation on the convergence results for online RED/PnP
[
61
,
21
,
67
,
65
]
, showing that the forward pass converges to
{\overline{\bm{x}}}\in{\mathsf{Fix}}({\mathsf{T}})
up to an error term that can be controlled via
\gamma
and
w
.

Theorem 2
.

Run the backward pass of ODER for
k\geq 1
iterations under Assumptions
1
-
4
from
{\bm{b}}^{0}={\bm{0}}
using the step-size
0<\gamma<1/(\lambda+\tau)
. Then, the sequence of backward pass iterates satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{b}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}\right]\leq B_{1}\eta^{k}+\frac{B_{2}}{\sqrt{w}},

(15)

where
0<\eta<1
,
B_{1}>0
and
B_{2}>0
are constants independent of
k
and
w
, and
{\overline{\bm{b}}}\in{\mathsf{Fix}}({\mathsf{F}})
.

Theorem
2
shows that the online backward pass in expectation converges to
{\overline{\bm{b}}}
up to an error term that can be controlled via
w
. The complete expressions for constants
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are in the proof.

Assumption 5
.

Function
\,\ell\,
has a global minimizer
\;{\bm{\theta}}^{\ast}
and has a
L
-Lipschitz continuous gradient, which means that for all
{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\phi}}
, we have
\|\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell({\bm{\phi}})\|_{2}\leq L\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\phi}}\|_{2}
.

Assumption 6
.

The loss function in (
12
) and indices
\{j_{t}\}
in (
13
) are such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\ell_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}})\right]\,=\,\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla\ell_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\,\leq\,\sigma^{2},

where the expectations are taken with respect to the random index uniformly as
j_{t}\in\{1,\dots,p\}
.

The existence of a minimizer and the Lipschitz continuity of the loss gradient are standard assumptions in the literature
[
72
,
73
,
42
]
. Note that we do
not
assume that the training loss
\ell
is convex.
Assumption
6
is the standard assumption used in the analysis of SGD.

Theorem 3
.

Train ODER using SGD for
T\geq 1
iterations under Assumptions 1-6 using the step-size parameters
0<\beta\leq 1/L
and the minibatch size
w\geq 1
. Select a large enough number of forward and backward pass iterations
K\geq 1
to satisfy
0<\eta^{K}\leq 1/{\sqrt{w}}
. Then, we have that

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{2(\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{0})-\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{\ast}))}{\beta T}+\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{w}}+\beta C_{2}\,.

where
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
are constants independent of
T
and
w
.

The complete expressions for constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are in the proof. Theorem
3
allows us to understand the ability of the iterates generated using (
13
) to approximate the stationary points of the desired loss (
12
). The error terms in the bound depend on the training step-size
\beta
and the minibatch size
w
, both of which can be controlled during training. In summary, we presented several theoretical results that give insights into ODER by stating explicit sufficient conditions and approximation bounds.

5
Numerical Evaluation

We numerically validate ODER in the context of three computational imaging modalities: IDT, sparse-view CT, and parallel MRI. Our goal is to both (a) empirically evaluate the performance of ODER and (b) highlight its effectiveness for processing a large number of measurements. We adopt
\ell_{2}
-norm loss
g({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{2}\|{\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}\|_{2}^{2}
as the data-fidelity term for all three imaging modalities.

ODER is compatible with any CNN architecture used to implement
{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}
. We use a
tiny
U-Net architecture
[
70
]
for ODER and the traditional RED (DEQ)
[
35
]
. We have added spectral normalization
[
74
]
to all the layers of CNN for stability (see the supplement for the numerical evaluation of the contractiveness of
{\mathsf{T}}_{\bm{\theta}}
on all three modalities). Similar to
[
35
]
, the CNN prior of ODER and RED (DEQ) are initialized using pre-trained denoisers. During the training of both ODER and RED (DEQ), we use the
Nesterov acceleration

[
72
]
for the forward pass and
Anderson acceleration

[
75
]
for the backward pass. We also adopt the stopping criterion from
[
35
,
76
]
by setting residual tolerance to
10^{-3}
for both forward and backward iterations (see supplement for additional details).

6
Conclusion and Future Work

This work proposes ODER as a new online DEQ learning method for RED, analyzes its theoretical properties in terms of convergence and accuracy, and applies it to three widely-used imaging inverse problems. ODER extends the recent DEQ approach in
[
35
]
by introducing randomized processing of measurements. Our extensive theoretical and numerical results corroborate the potential of ODER to reduce the computational/memory complexity of training and testing, while achieving similar imaging quality as RED (DEQ). The future work can explore to further improve our analysis and design distributed variants of ODER to enhance its performance on parallel computing architectures.

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem 1

The following theorem shows the convergence (in expectation) of the ODER forward pass for convex
g
and contractive
{\mathsf{D}}_{\bm{\theta}}
. Note that this proof is a variation of existing results in the literature on online PnP/RED
[
61
,
21
,
67
,
65
]
. However, this result plays an important role in the analysis of both the backward pass and the ability of ODER to approximate the traditional DEQ learning.

Theorem 1
.

Run the forward pass of ODER for
k\geq 1
iterations under Assumptions 1-4 using the step size
0<\gamma<1/(\lambda+\tau)
. Then, the sequence of forward pass iterates satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{x}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\right]\leq\eta^{k}R+\frac{\gamma\nu}{(1-\eta)\sqrt{w}},

(16)

for some constant
0<\eta<1
where
{\overline{\bm{x}}}\in{\mathsf{Fix}}({\mathsf{T}})
.

Proof.

For notation connivance, we abbreviate
{\mathsf{T}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdots)
as
{\mathsf{T}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdots)
in the following proof. From Lemma
1
,
{\mathsf{T}}
is a contraction, which means that there exists
0<\eta<1
such that

\|{\mathsf{T}}({\bm{z}})-{\mathsf{T}}({\bm{y}})\|_{2}\leq\eta\|{\bm{z}}-{\bm{y}}\|_{2}\,,

for all
{\bm{z}},{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for
{\overline{\bm{x}}}\in{\mathsf{Fix}}({\mathsf{T}})
, we have that

\displaystyle\|{\bm{x}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{T}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})\|_{2}^{2}=\|{\mathsf{T}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{T}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})+{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{T}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathsf{T}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{T}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})\|_{2}+\gamma\|{\mathsf{T}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})-{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|{\bm{x}}^{k-1}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\|\nabla g({\bm{x}}^{k-1})-\nabla{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}}^{k-1})\|_{2},

where we used the triangular inequality and that
{\mathsf{T}}
is
\eta
-Lipschitz continuous. We take the conditional expectation from both sides to obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{x}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\,|\,{\bm{x}}^{k-1}\right]\leq\eta\|{\bm{x}}^{k-1}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\frac{\gamma\nu}{\sqrt{w}},

where we applied the Jensen’s inequality to the variance bound in Assumption 4. By taking the total expectation, we thus have

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{x}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\right]\leq\eta\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{x}}^{k-1}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\right]+\frac{\gamma\nu}{\sqrt{w}}.

By iterating this inequality and using the bound in Assumption 3, we get the result

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{x}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\right]\leq\eta^{k}R+\frac{\gamma\nu}{(1-\eta)\sqrt{w}}.

∎

{\mathsf{T}}
is a contraction. The proof is a minor modification of the Theorem 1 from

, which we provide for completeness. It is worth noting that this result does not assume that the functions
\{g_{i}\}

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that Assumptions 1-2 in the main paper are true. Then, for any
0<\gamma<1/(\lambda+\tau)
, the operator
{\mathsf{T}}
in eq. (2) of the main paper is a contraction, which means that for all
{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\|\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}({\bm{x}})\|_{2}<1,

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the spectral norm.

Proof.

The Jacobian of the operator
{\mathsf{T}}
with respect to
{\bm{x}}
is given by

\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}({\bm{x}})=(1-\gamma\tau){\bm{I}}-\gamma{\mathsf{H}}g({\bm{x}})-\gamma\tau\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{D}}({\bm{x}}).

Let
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
denote sorted eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix
{\mathsf{H}}g({\bm{x}})
. Since
g
is convex, we have that
\lambda_{n}\geq 0
. Then, for any
{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}({\bm{x}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|(1-\gamma\tau){\bm{I}}-\gamma{\mathsf{H}}g({\bm{x}})-\gamma\tau\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{D}}({\bm{x}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-\gamma\tau){\bm{I}}-\gamma{\mathsf{H}}g({\bm{x}})\|_{2}+\gamma\tau\|\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{D}}({\bm{x}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left\{1-\gamma\tau-\gamma\lambda_{i}\right\}+\gamma\tau\kappa

\displaystyle\leq 1-\gamma\tau(1-\kappa)<1,

where in the first inequality we used the triangular inequality, in the second the fact that
{\mathsf{D}}
is a contraction, and in the third the convexity of
g
.
∎

Appendix B
Proof of Theorem 2

The following result is a novel analysis of the ODER backward pass. The result implies that the backward pass converges (in expectation) up to an error term that can be controlled by the minibatch parameter
w
. Our numerical results provide additional corroboration to our theory by showing that ODER nearly matches the performance of the traditional DEQ learning.

Theorem 2
.

Run the backward pass of ODER for
k\geq 1
iterations under Assumptions 1-4 from
{\bm{b}}^{0}={\bm{0}}
using the step-size
0<\gamma<1/(\lambda+\tau)
. Then, the sequence of backward pass iterates satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{b}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}\right]\leq B_{1}\eta^{k}+\frac{B_{2}}{\sqrt{w}},

(17)

where
0<\eta<1
,
B_{1}>0
and
B_{2}>0
are constants independent of
k
and
w
, and
{\overline{\bm{b}}}\in{\mathsf{Fix}}({\mathsf{F}})
.

Proof.

Let
{\bm{x}}^{K}
denote the output of the forward pass of ODER after
K\geq 1
iterations,
{\bm{x}^{\ast}}
denote the training label, and
{\overline{\bm{x}}}\in{\mathsf{Fix}}({\mathsf{T}})
. Consider the following two operators

{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}})=[\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})]^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{b}}+({\overline{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}^{\ast}})\quad\text{and}\quad{\widehat{\mathsf{F}}}({\bm{b}})=[\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}({\bm{x}}^{K})]^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{b}}+({\bm{x}}^{K}-{\bm{x}^{\ast}}),

where the first operator is used in the backward pass of RED (DEQ), while the second is its online approximation. Note also the following two Jacobians

\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})={\bm{I}}-\gamma({\mathsf{H}}g({\overline{\bm{x}}})+\tau\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{R}}({\overline{\bm{x}}}))\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}({\bm{x}}^{K})={\bm{I}}-\gamma({\mathsf{H}}{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}}^{K})+\tau\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{R}}({\bm{x}}^{K})).

Lemma
1
implies that
{\mathsf{T}}
is a contraction. Let
0<\eta<1
denote the Lipschitz constant of
{\mathsf{T}}
. Since
\nabla_{\bm{b}}{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}})=\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})
, we have
\|\nabla_{\bm{b}}{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}})\|_{2}=\|\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{T}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})\|_{2}\leq\eta
, which means that
{\mathsf{F}}
is a contraction

\|{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{z}})-{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{y}})\|_{2}\leq\eta\|{\bm{z}}-{\bm{y}}\|_{2},\quad{\bm{z}},{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We can thus show the following bound

\displaystyle\|{\bm{b}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\widehat{\mathsf{F}}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{F}}({\overline{\bm{b}}})\|_{2}=\|{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{F}}({\overline{\bm{b}}})+{\widehat{\mathsf{F}}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{F}}({\overline{\bm{b}}})\|_{2}+\|{\widehat{\mathsf{F}}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}+\|{\widehat{\mathsf{F}}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})\|_{2},

where we first used the triangular inequality and then the fact that
{\mathsf{F}}
is a contraction. By taking the conditional expectation on both sides, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{b}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}\,|\,{\bm{x}}^{K},{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\right]\leq\eta\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\widehat{\mathsf{F}}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})\|_{2}\,|\,{\bm{x}}^{K},{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\right].

(18)

We can bound the second term in (
18
) as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\widehat{\mathsf{F}}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})-{\mathsf{F}}({\bm{b}}^{k-1})\|_{2}\,|\,{\bm{x}}^{K},{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\right]\leq\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathsf{H}}g({\overline{\bm{x}}})-{\mathsf{H}}{\widehat{{g}}}({\bm{x}}^{K})\|_{2}\,|\,{\bm{x}}^{K},{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\right]\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\gamma\tau\|\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{R}}({\overline{\bm{x}}})-\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{R}}({\bm{x}}^{K})\|_{2}\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\|_{2}+\|{\bm{x}}^{K}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\lambda\|{\bm{x}}^{K}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\|_{2}+\frac{\gamma\nu}{\sqrt{w}}\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\|_{2}+\gamma\tau\alpha\|{\bm{x}}^{K}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\|_{2}+\|{\bm{x}}^{K}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq A_{1}\|{\bm{x}}^{K}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+\frac{A_{2}}{\sqrt{w}},

with
A_{1}\,\coloneqq\,(2\gamma\lambda+2\gamma\tau\alpha R+1)
and
A_{2}=2\nu R
, where in the second inequality we used Lemma
2
and the
\alpha
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla_{\bm{x}}{\mathsf{R}}
and in the third
\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\|_{2}\leq 2R
. Since
{\bm{b}}^{0}={\bm{0}}
, Assumption 3 implies that
\|{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}\leq R
, which leads to
\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\|_{2}\leq 2R
for all
k\geq 1
.

By including the last bound into (
18
), we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{b}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}\,|\,{\bm{x}}^{K},{\bm{b}}^{k-1}\right]\leq\eta\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}+A_{1}\|{\bm{x}}^{K}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}+A_{2}/\sqrt{w}.

By taking the total expectation and using Theorem
1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{b}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}\right]\leq\eta\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{b}}^{k-1}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}\right]+A_{1}R\eta^{K}+\frac{A_{1}\gamma\nu}{(1-\nu)\sqrt{w}}+\frac{A_{2}}{\sqrt{w}}.

By iterating this bound and noting that
k\leq K
, we get the final result

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{b}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}\right]\leq\eta^{k}B_{1}+\frac{B_{2}}{\sqrt{w}}\,,

where
B_{1}\,\coloneqq\,R+A_{1}R/(1-\eta)
and
B_{2}\,\coloneqq\,(((A_{1}\gamma\nu)/(1-\eta))+A_{2})/(1-\nu)
.

∎

.

Appendix C
Proof of Theorem 3

Our final theoretical result is a novel analysis on the ability of ODER to approximate the stationary points of the loss
\ell({\bm{\theta}})
. We show that ODER can approximate (in expectation) the stationary points up to an error term that can be controlled by the minibatch size
w
and the learning rate
\beta
.

Theorem 3
.

Train ODER using SGD for
T\geq 1
iterations under Assumptions 1-6 using the step-size parameters
0<\beta\leq 1/L
and the minibatch size
w\geq 1
. Select a large enough number of forward and backward pass iterations
K\geq 1
to satisfy
0<\eta^{K}\leq 1/{\sqrt{w}}
. Then, we have that

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{2(\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{0})-\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{\ast}))}{\beta T}+\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{w}}+\beta C_{2}\,.

where
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
are constants independent of
T
and
w
.

Proof.

Consider the RED (DEQ) loss
\ell
and its ODER approximation
{\hat{\ell}}

\ell({\bm{\theta}})=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}})\quad\text{and}\quad{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}})=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{p}{\hat{\ell}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})\,,

(19)

where each
\ell_{j}
and
{\hat{\ell}}_{j}
have the forms

\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}})=\frac{1}{2}\|{\overline{\bm{x}}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})-{\bm{x}}_{j}^{\ast}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad{\hat{\ell}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})=\frac{1}{2}\|{\bm{x}}^{K}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})-{\bm{x}}_{j}^{\ast}\|_{2}^{2}.

Vector
{\bm{x}}_{j}^{K}
denotes the final iterate of the online forward pass obtained after
K\geq 1
iterations for the training sample
j\in\{1,\cdots,p\}
.

From Assumption 5, we obtain the traditional Lipschitz continuity bound on the gradient

\|\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}_{1})-\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}_{2})\|_{2}\leq L\|{\bm{\theta}}_{1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{2}\|_{2},

which directly leads to traditional quadratic upper bound (see Lemma 1.2.3 in
[
72
]
).

\displaystyle\ell({\bm{\theta}}_{1})\leq\ell({\bm{\theta}}_{2})

\displaystyle+\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}_{2})^{{\mathsf{T}}}({\bm{\theta}}_{1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{2})+\frac{L}{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}_{1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

(20)

Lemma
3
in this supplement establishes the following useful bound for our analysis

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})-\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{w}},

for some constant
C>0
(see its full expression in Lemma
3
). This directly implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[-\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})^{{\mathsf{T}}}\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}]+\frac{C}{2\sqrt{w}}\,.

(21)

Consider a single iteration of SGD for optimizing ODER

{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}={\bm{\theta}}^{t}-\beta\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t}).

(22)

From the quadratic upper bound (
20
), we get

\displaystyle\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t+1})-\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})

\displaystyle\leq\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})^{{\mathsf{T}}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{t})+\frac{L}{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}^{t+1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{t}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\beta\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})^{{\mathsf{T}}}\nabla\hat{\ell}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})+\frac{\beta^{2}L}{2}\|\nabla\hat{\ell}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}\,.

For notation convenience, we use
\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\setminus j_{t}]
to denote the expectation only with respect to the training index
j_{t}\in\{1,\dots,p\}
, where we condition on
{\bm{\theta}}^{t}
and the random indices within forward and backward passes at this iteration. By taking
\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\setminus j_{t}]
on both sides of the quadratic upper bound (
20
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t+1})|\setminus j_{t}\right]-\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})

\displaystyle\leq-\beta\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})^{{\mathsf{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\hat{\ell}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})|\setminus j_{t}\right]+\frac{\beta^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}|\setminus j_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=-\beta\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})^{{\mathsf{T}}}\nabla\hat{\ell}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})+\frac{\beta^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}|\setminus j_{t}\right]\,,

(23)

where we use (
19
) and the fact that
j_{t}
is distributed uniformly at random in
\{1,\dots,p\}
.

We now estimate the last term in (
C
)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}|\setminus j_{t}\right]

(24)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})-\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})+\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}|\setminus j_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})-\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}+2(\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})-\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t}))^{{\mathsf{T}}}\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})+\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}|\setminus j_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})-\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}|\setminus j_{t}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}|\setminus j_{t}\right]\,,

where in the third equality we use the fact that
\mathbb{E}\left[(\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})-\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t}))^{{\mathsf{T}}}\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})|\setminus j_{t}\right]=0
.

By replacing the last term in (
C
) with (
24
) and taking the full expectation on both sides of (
C
) in terms of all random variables and invoking the bound (
21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t+1})]-\mathbb{E}\left[\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[-\beta\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})^{{\mathsf{T}}}\nabla\hat{\ell}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})+\frac{\beta^{2}L}{2}\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|\right]+\frac{\beta^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j_{t}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})-\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\beta}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}]+\frac{\beta C}{2\sqrt{w}}+\frac{\beta^{2}L}{2}(4\sigma^{2}+\frac{6C}{\sqrt{w}})\,,

where we used the fact that
0<\beta\leq 1/L
, and in the last inequality we used the bound from Lemma
4
in this document. By rearranging the terms, and summing this bound over
0\leq t\leq T-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{t})\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{0})-\mathbb{E}[\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{T})])}{\beta T}+\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{w}}+\beta C_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{0})-\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{\ast}))}{\beta T}+\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{w}}+\beta C_{2}\,,

where
C_{1}\,\coloneqq\,7\gamma^{2}\tau^{2}\alpha^{2}(R+R^{2})(B_{1}+B_{2}+R^{2}+\gamma\nu R/(1-\eta))
and
C_{2}\,\coloneqq\,4L\sigma^{2}
are constants independent of
t
and
w
. In the last inequality we used the fact that
\ell({\bm{\theta}}^{\ast})\leq\ell({\bm{\theta}})
for all
{\bm{\theta}}
, where
{\bm{\theta}}^{\ast}
denotes a global minimizer of
\ell
.
∎

C.1
Technical Lemmas for the Proof of Theorem 3

The following lemma are useful for relating
\nabla{\hat{\ell}}
and
\nabla\ell
in expectation up to an error term. Both are used in the proof of Theorem
3
.

Lemma 3
.

Given the loss function
\ell
of RED (DEQ) and
\hat{\ell}
of ODER, by selecting a large enough number of forward and backward iterations
K\geq 1
to satisfy
0<\eta^{K}\leq 1/{\sqrt{w}}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{w}},

where
C\,\coloneqq\,\gamma^{2}\tau^{2}\alpha^{2}(R+R^{2})(B_{1}+B_{2}+R^{2}+\gamma\nu R/(1-\eta))
is a constant.

Proof.

By using the DEQ training, we have the following bound

\displaystyle\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left[\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({{\bm{x}}_{j}^{K}})\right]^{{\mathsf{T}}}{\bm{b}}_{j}^{K}-\left[\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathsf{T}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({{\overline{\bm{x}}}_{j}})\right]^{{\mathsf{T}}}{\overline{\bm{b}}}_{j}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left[\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({{\bm{x}}_{j}^{K}})\right]^{{\mathsf{T}}}({\bm{b}}_{j}^{K}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}_{j})+\left[\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({{\bm{x}}_{j}^{K}})-\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathsf{T}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({{\overline{\bm{x}}}_{j}})\right]^{{\mathsf{T}}}{\overline{\bm{b}}}_{j}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({{\bm{x}}_{j}^{K}})\right\|_{2}\|{\bm{b}}_{j}^{K}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}_{j}\|_{2}+\left\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\widehat{{\mathsf{T}}}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({{\bm{x}}_{j}^{K}})-\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathsf{T}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({{\overline{\bm{x}}}_{j}})\right\|_{2}\|{\overline{\bm{b}}}_{j}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\tau\alpha(\|{\bm{b}}_{j}^{K}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}_{j}\|_{2}+\|{\bm{x}}_{j}^{K}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}_{j}\|_{2}\|{\overline{\bm{b}}}_{j}\|_{2}),\quad\forall j\in\{1,\cdots,p\}\,,

(25)

where in the first inequality, we used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fact that
{\mathsf{D}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
and
\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}{\mathsf{D}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{x}})
are
\alpha
-Lipschitz continuous with respect to
{\bm{x}}
and
{\bm{\theta}}
based on Assumption 2. By applying Assumption 3 which states that
\|{\bm{b}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}<R
and
\|{\bm{x}}^{k}-{\overline{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}<R
for every
{\bm{x}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}\leq\gamma\tau\alpha(R+R^{2}),\quad\forall j\in\{1,\cdots,p\}

(26)

On the other hand, by taking the expectation with respect to the stochastic approximation variables in forward and backward propagation in (
C.1
), invoking the bounds obtained from Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 and using the fact that
0<\eta^{K}\leq 1/\sqrt{w}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}\right]\leq\gamma\tau\alpha\left(\frac{B_{1}+B_{2}+R^{2}}{\sqrt{w}}+\frac{\gamma\nu R}{(1-\eta)\sqrt{w}}\right)\,

where
B_{1}>0
and
B_{2}>0
are constants obtained in Theorem
2
.
Now consider a random variable
X
which has a probability density function given by
f(x)
on the real number line such that
P(0\leq X\leq c)=1
, then we have

\mathbb{E}[X]=\int_{0}^{c}x^{2}f(x)dx\leq\int_{0}^{c}cxf(x)dx=c\mathbb{E}[X].

As a consequence, given the bound (
26
) for the random variables
\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}
and using the above fact, we have the following useful bound for our proof

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{w}}\,,

(27)

where
C\,\coloneqq\,\gamma^{2}\tau^{2}\alpha^{2}(R+R^{2})(B_{1}+B_{2}+R^{2}+\gamma\nu R/(1-\eta))
is a constant.

Note also the fact that for
{\bm{a}}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{a}}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{p}{\bm{a}}_{j}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq p\sum_{j=1}^{p}\|{\bm{a}}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}.

As a result, applying the bound (
27
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{p}(\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}}))\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla{\hat{\ell}}_{j}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla\ell_{j}({\bm{\theta}})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{w}}\,.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Given the loss function
\ell
and
\hat{\ell}
, by taking the conditional expectation with respect to the training index

(via conditioning on all other variables), we have

for ODER and RED (DEQ), while we set stopping criterion of backward passes to
\epsilon=10^{-2}

We additionally tested three network architectures including DnCNN
[
12
]
, U-Net
[
4
]
and tiny U-Net
[
70
]
. The DnCNN network has seventeen layers, including 15 hidden layers, an input layer, and an output layer. The tiny U-Net is a simplified variant of the normal U-Net with less trainable parameters. In specific, the CNN consists of four scales, each with a skip connection between downsampling and upsampling. These connections increase the effective receptive field of the CNN. The number of channels in each layer are {32, 64, 128, 256}. We make two additional modifications to the tiny U-Net. First, we drop out the second group normalization (GN)
[
90
]
at each composite convolutional layers. Second, we add spectral normalization to each layers for more stable training and better Lipschitz constrain of the neural network. It is worth to note that spectral normalization is a widely used method for Lipschitz constrained neural network, and it is
not
our aim to claim any algorithmic novelty with respect to it. In Table
4
, we present the denoising performance on AWGN removal with noise level
\sigma=5
and the run time of calculating
{\mathsf{R}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{x}})/\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}{\mathsf{R}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{x}})
with respect to a
320\times 320
image. Overall, the traditional U-Net architecture achieves the best denoising and reconstruction performance, but requires more time per iterate than tiny U-Net. As a result, we implement traditional U-Net denoiser for RED (Denoising), and we equip ODER, RED (DEQ) and RED (Unfold) for the same tiny U-Net architecture in order to decrease per-iteration computation costs during training.

\{6,8,10\}

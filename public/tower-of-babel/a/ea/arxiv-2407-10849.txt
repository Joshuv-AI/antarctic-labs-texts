Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the quantitative stability for the critical Hardy-Hénon equation

H(u):=\div(|x|^{-2a}\nabla u)+|x|^{-pb}|u|^{p-2}u=0,

(1.1)

where
u\in D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
n,a,b,p
satisfy the relations

n\geq 2,\quad a<b<a+1,\quad a<\frac{n-2}{2},\quad p=\frac{2n}{n-2+2(b-a)}.

(1.2)

The space
D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the norm

{\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-2a}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is well known that the equation (
1.1
) is the Euler-Lagrange equation of the classical Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality (see
[
6
]
)

S(a,b,n)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-bp}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-2a}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

(1.3)

Here
S(a,b,n)
denotes the sharp constant.

The classification of positive solutions to the equation (
1.1
) has been extensively studied. Here, we provide a brief summary; for further background and partial results, we refer the readers to
[
7
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
25
]
and their references. When
a\geq 0
, Chou and Chu
[
9
]
found that the set of positive solutions is given by

\mathcal{M}_{a,b,n}:=\left\{U_{\lambda}(x)\;\Big|\;U_{\lambda}(x):=\frac{\lambda^{\sqrt{\Lambda}}(2p\Lambda)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}}{(1+|\lambda x|^{\sqrt{\Lambda}(p-2)})^{\frac{2}{p-2}}},\lambda>0\right\},

(1.4)

where
\Lambda:=\left(\frac{n-2-2a}{2}\right)^{2}
. Later, Catrina and Wang
[
7
]
showed that for general
a,b,n
, the set
\mathcal{M}_{n,a,b}
in (
1.4
) gives all positive radially symmetric solutions. They also discovered that there exists a curve
b=f(a),a<0
such that if
b<f(a)
, then there exists a positive non-radially symmetric solutions to the equation (
1.1
). Making use of fine spectral analysis, Felli and Schneider
[
22
]
then proved that this curve can be taken to be

b=b_{\text{FS}}(a):=\frac{n(n-2-2a)}{2\sqrt{(n-2-2a)^{2}+4n-4}}-\frac{n-2-2a}{2},\;a<0.

(1.5)

The curve
b=b_{\text{FS}}(a)
is usually called the Felli-Schneider curve. It was conjectured for a long time that when
a<0
and
b\geq b_{\text{FS}}(a)
,
\mathcal{M}_{a,b,n}
contains all positive solutions to the equation (
1.1
). This was finally confirmed by Dolbeault, Esteban and Loss
[
15
]
using the method of nonlinear flows. In conclusion, when
a\geq 0
or
a<0,b\geq b_{\text{FS}}(a)
, the set
\mathcal{M}_{n,a,b}
in (
1.4
) gives all the positive solutions to the equation (
1.1
). In the following, we will call the functions in
\mathcal{M}_{a,b,n}

Talenti bubbles
.

The stability of the equation (
1.1
), or in other words, the stability of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality (
1.3
) in the critical point setting, can be understood as a natural extension of the classification results. Specifically, it refers to whether being almost a positive solution to the equation (
1.1
) implies being close to an appropriate linear combination of Talenti bubbles. This kind of stability traces back to the celebrated global compactness principle of Struwe
[
28
]
. In
[
28
]
, Struwe established the following qualitative stability of the classical Yamabe equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\;(n\geq 3)
: for any
\varepsilon>0
and
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, there exists
\delta=\delta(n,\nu,\varepsilon)>0
such that

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}W_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}\\
1\leq i\leq\nu\end{subarray}}{\left\lVert u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}W_{i}\right\rVert}_{D_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{if }\,{\left\lVert\Delta u+u^{2^{*}-1}\right\rVert}_{D_{0}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\delta.

Here
\mathcal{M}_{0}
denotes the set of positive solutions to the Yamabe equation and
u
is any nonnegative function satisfying

\left(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\right)S(0,0,n)^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)S(0,0,n)^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Ciraolo, Figalli and Maggi
[
10
]
were the first to quantify Struwe’s work. In the case
\nu=1
, they proved the following sharp linear estimate

\inf_{W\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}{\left\lVert u-W\right\rVert}_{D_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n){\left\lVert\Delta u+u^{2^{*}-1}\right\rVert}_{D_{0}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This estimate was later generalized by Figalli and Glaudo
[
19
]
. Assuming
\nu\geq 2
and
3\leq n\leq 5
, they proved

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}W_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}\\
1\leq i\leq\nu\end{subarray}}{\left\lVert u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}W_{i}\right\rVert}_{D_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,\nu){\left\lVert\Delta u+u^{2^{*}-1}\right\rVert}_{D_{0}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Moreover, they constructed counterexamples to show that such a linear estimate does not hold for
n\geq 6
. The remaining case
\nu\geq 2,n\geq 6
was eventually solved by Deng, Sun and Wei
[
12
]
. They obtained the following sharp estimate

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}W_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}\\
1\leq i\leq\nu\end{subarray}}{\left\lVert u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}W_{i}\right\rVert}_{D_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,\nu)F_{0}\left({\left\lVert\Delta u+u^{2^{*}-1}\right\rVert}_{D_{0}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right),

where

F_{0}(x):=\begin{cases}x|\ln{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+x,&n=6;\\
x^{\frac{n+2}{2(n-2)}},&n\geq 7.\end{cases}

Recently, Wei and Wu
[
30
]
generalized the results in
[
12
]
to the critical Hardy-Hénon equation (
1.1
). In the case
a\geq 0
or
a<0,b>b_{\text{FS}}(a)
, they derived the following sharp estimate

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
1\leq i\leq\nu\end{subarray}}{\left\lVert u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{\lambda_{i}}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,a,b,\nu)F_{1}\left({\left\lVert H(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right),

(1.6)

where

F_{1}(x):=\begin{cases}x,&p>3\;\text{or }\nu=1;\\
x|\ln{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+x,&p=3\;\text{and }\nu\geq 2;\\
x^{\frac{p-1}{2}},&2<p<3\;\text{and }\nu\geq 2\end{cases}

and
u
satisfies

\left(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\right)S(a,b,n)^{\frac{p}{p-2}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-2a}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)S(a,b,n)^{\frac{p}{p-2}}.

Here
D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the dual space of
D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. However, as was noticed in
[
13
,
24
]
, there is an inaccuracy in
[
30
]
: due to the absence of the nondegeneracy condition, the stability results of
[
30
]
do not hold when
(a,b)
falls on the Felli-Schneider curve.

We refer the readers to
[
1
,
4
,
11
,
26
,
27
,
31
,
32
]
for various quantitative stability results on other Sobolev-type inequalities.

In this paper, we aim to derive appropriate quantitative stability results of the equation (
1.1
) in the case
a<0,b=b_{\text{FS}}(a)
. There are two main motivations for our work.

The first motivation comes from the fact that Wei and Wu’s result (
1.6
) does not cover the Felli-Schneider curve and its proof can hardly be applied to this situation. One of the key tools utilized in their work is the non-degeneracy of the equation (
1.1
). Indeed, to the best of our knowledge, all such type quantitative stability results to date are dependent on the non-degeneracy. However, it is well known that (see
[
22
,
24
]
for example) the equation (
1.1
) is non-degenerate when
a\geq 0
or
a<0,b>b_{\text{FS}}(a)
, but degenerate when
a<0,b=b_{\text{FS}}(a)
. Specifically, consider the linearized equation of (
1.1
) near some Talenti bubble
U_{\lambda_{0}}
:

\displaystyle\div(|x|^{-2a}\nabla v)+(p-1)|x|^{-pb}U_{\lambda_{0}}^{p-2}v=0.

(1.7)

We use
\bar{X}_{\lambda_{0}}
to denote the set of solutions to (
1.7
). Clearly
\bar{X}_{\lambda_{0}}
contains the trivial elements
t\partial_{\lambda}U_{\lambda_{0}},t\in\mathbb{R}
. We also define
X_{\lambda_{0}}
to be the orthogonal complement of
\text{Span}\{\partial_{\lambda}U_{\lambda_{0}}\}
in
\bar{X}_{\lambda_{0}}
with respect to the
D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
scalar product. When
a\geq 0
or
a<0,b>b_{\text{FS}}(a)
,
X_{\lambda_{0}}=\emptyset
and we say (
1.1
) is non-degenerate. When
a<0
and
b=b_{\text{FS}}(a)
,
X_{\lambda_{0}}\neq\emptyset
and we say (
1.1
) is degenerate (see Lemma
2.4
for an equivalent formulation). Due to the degeneracy, it is easy to construct examples which do not satisfy (
1.6
). So a natural and challenging question is: is it possible to replace
F_{1}
by another function
F
such that the estimate (
1.6
) vaild in the case
a<0,b=b_{\text{FS}}(a)
?

The second motivation comes from the quantitative stability of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality (
1.3
) in the functional setting. The stability of functional inequalities was initially raised by Brézis and Lieb
[
5
]
. They asked whether the homogeneous Sobolev inequality can be reinforced. This question was answered affirmatively by Bianchi and Egnell
[
3
]
. After the work
[
3
]
, the stability of inequalities has drawn attention and has been addressed by lots of researchers. We do not attempt a survey of the extensive literature, but refer the readers to
[
8
,
14
,
20
,
21
,
23
,
24
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
and the references therein for more background. Similar to the stability in the critical point setting, many quantitative stability results of functional inequalities depend on the non-degeneracy condition. Recently some interesting degenerate stability results were discovered by Engelstein, Neumayer and Spolaor
[
18
]
. Their results were later improved by Frank
[
23
]
.

Let’s go back to our setting. In the case
a\geq 0
or
a<0,b>b_{\text{FS}}(a)
, Wei and Wu
[
30
]
established the following sharp quadratic estimate

\displaystyle{\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}-S(a,b,n){\left\lVert|x|^{-b}u\right\rVert}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\geq c(a,b,n)\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R},\lambda>0}{\left\lVert u-sU_{\lambda}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

In the case
a<0,b=b_{\text{FS}}(a)
, due to the degeneracy, the above estimate no longer hold. Recently, Frank and Peteranderl
[
24
]
studied this case and found the optimal exponent is
4
:

{\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{4}-S(a,b,n)^{2}{\left\lVert|x|^{-b}u\right\rVert}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{4}\geq c(a,b,n)\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R},\lambda>0}{\left\lVert u-sU_{\lambda}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{4}.

Moreover, if the infimum is attained by
s_{0}U_{\lambda_{0}}
, their proof yields

\displaystyle{\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}-S(a,b,n){\left\lVert|x|^{-b}u\right\rVert}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\geq c(a,b,n){\left\lVert\Pi_{X_{\lambda_{0}}}^{\perp}(u)-s_{0}U_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

where
\Pi_{X_{\lambda_{0}}}
is the orthogonal projection in
D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
onto
X_{\lambda_{0}}
and
\Pi_{X_{\lambda_{0}}}^{\perp}=1-\Pi_{X_{\lambda_{0}}}
. Since in some sense these two types of stability are closely related, we believe that there exist corresponding degenerate stabilities in the critical point setting.

Let us state our main results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
n\geq 2
,
a<0
,
b=b_{\mathrm{FS}}(a)
and
p=\frac{2n}{n-2+2(b-a)}
. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(a,n)
such that, for any nonnegative function
u\in D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

\frac{1}{2}S(a,b,n)^{\frac{p}{p-2}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-2a}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{3}{2}S(a,b,n)^{\frac{p}{p-2}},

(1.8)

it holds that

\inf_{\lambda>0}{\left\lVert u-U_{\lambda}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{3}\leq C{\left\lVert H(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(1.9)

The above estimate is sharp in the sense that there exists a nonnegative sequence
\{w_{k}\}_{k}\subset D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying (
1.8
) such that
w_{k}\notin\mathcal{M}_{a,b,n}
,

\inf_{\lambda>0}{\left\lVert w_{k}-U_{\lambda}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\rightarrow 0

and

\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\left\lVert H(w_{k})\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\inf\limits_{\lambda>0}{\left\lVert w_{k}-U_{\lambda}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{3}}<+\infty.

Moreover, if the infimum in (
1.9
) is attained by
U_{\lambda_{0}}
, then there exists a positive constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(a,n)
such that

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\Pi_{X_{\lambda_{0}}}^{\perp}(u)-U_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{1}{\left\lVert H(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(1.10)

provided

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\Pi_{X_{\lambda_{0}}}^{\perp}(u)-U_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq C_{1}F_{2}\left({\left\lVert\Pi_{X_{\lambda_{0}}}(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right),

(1.11)

where

\displaystyle F_{2}(x):=\begin{cases}x^{2},&p\geq 3,\\
x^{p-1},&p<3.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
n\geq 2
,
\nu\geq 2
,
a<0
,
b=b_{\mathrm{FS}}(a)
and
p=\frac{2n}{n-2+2(b-a)}
. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(a,\nu,n)
such that, for any nonnegative function
u\in D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

\left(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\right)S(a,b,n)^{\frac{p}{p-2}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-2a}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)S(a,b,n)^{\frac{p}{p-2}},

(1.12)

if the infimum

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
1\leq i\leq\nu\end{subarray}}{\left\lVert u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{s_{i}}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

is attained by
U_{\lambda_{i}},1\leq i\leq\nu
, then

{\left\lVert\Pi_{X}^{\perp}(u)-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{\lambda_{i}}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq CF_{1}\left({\left\lVert H(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)

(1.13)

provided

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\Pi_{X}^{\perp}(u)-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{\lambda_{i}}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq CF_{1}\left({\left\lVert\Pi_{X}(u)\right\rVert}^{2}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right).

(1.14)

Here
X:=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{i=1}^{\nu}X_{\lambda_{i}}
.

Remark 1.3
.

In the single bubble case (Theorem
1.1
), we in fact do not need the nonnegativity of the function
u
. Precisely, for any function
u\in D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying (
1.8
), it holds that

\displaystyle\inf_{\lambda>0,\theta\in\{-1,1\}}{\left\lVert u-\theta U_{\lambda}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{3}\leq C{\left\lVert H(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This follows from Theorem
1.7
below and the fact that for each sign-changing solution
u_{0}
to the equation (
1.1
), we have the following energy estimate

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-2a}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq 2S(a,b,n)^{\frac{p}{p-2}}.

This estimate can be proved by testing the equation (
1.1
) with
(u_{0})^{+}
and
(u_{0})^{-}
, and applying the inequality (
1.3
).

Remark 1.4
.

In the case
\nu=1
, Theorem
1.1
completes the stability analysis of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality within the critical point framework initiated by Wei and Wu
[
30
]
(see (
1.6
)). It remains unclear whether the cubic estimate (
1.9
) holds for
\nu\geq 2
.

Remark 1.5
.

Unlike quantitative stabilities in the functional setting, we do not always have (
1.10
) or (
1.13
). Indeed, in Section
3
we show that, for any
0<\delta\ll 1
, there exists a positive function
u
(see (

)) such that the infimum in (
1.9
) is attained by
U_{1}
and

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\Pi_{X_{1}}(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\delta,\quad{\left\lVert\Pi_{X_{1}}^{\perp}(u)-U_{1}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\delta^{2},\quad{\left\lVert H(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\delta^{3}.

Thus in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we pose the restrictions (
1.11
) and (

The following result is a direct consequence of the above theorems. To derive it, one just need to note that, for any
u\in D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
centrally symmetric and
\lambda>0
, we have
\Pi_{X_{\lambda}}(u)=0
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Assume
n\geq 2
,
\nu\geq 1
,
a<0
,

and

. Then there exists a positive constant

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
1\leq i\leq\nu\end{subarray}}{\left\lVert u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{s_{i}}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq CF_{1}\left({\left\lVert H(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right).

Moreover, the above estimate is sharp.

Thanks to the global compactness principle (see

Theorem 1.7

,

\displaystyle{\left\lVert u-U_{1}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\delta,

) holds and is optimal. Moreover, if the infimum is attained by

Theorem 1.8
.

Assume
n\geq 2
,
\nu\geq 2
,
a<0
,
b=b_{\mathrm{FS}}(a)

. Then there exist two positive constants

such that, for any function

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
1\leq i\leq\nu\end{subarray}}{\left\lVert u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{s_{i}}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\delta

Remark 1.9
.

Note that in these local versions, we do not need

Remark 1.10
.

By further exploiting the proof of Theorem

\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k}

and

\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\left\lVert H(\varphi_{k})\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\inf\limits_{\lambda>0}{\left\lVert\varphi_{k}-U_{\lambda}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{3}}\geq R(p,n).

(1.15)

Here

Let us briefly address our methods in deriving Theorem

and Theorem
1.8
.

To establish Theorem
1.7
, we adapt the ideas from
[
24
,
30
]
. The estimate (
1.10
) can be obtained by refining the arguments of
[
30
, Proposition 5.1]
. As for the estimate (
1.9
), we first test the equation (
1.1
) with a suitable function and then take advantage of a expansion technique presented in
[
23
,
24
]
to deal with nonlinear terms. Finally we can obtain a unexplicit lower bound of
{\left\lVert H(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, which is a quadratic form of
u
. The positivity of this lower bound has already been investigated in
[
24
]
and thus (
1.9
) follows.

It is worth mentioning the following three points. The first point is that, unlike the non-degenerate case (
[
30
, Proposition 5.1]
), the most canonical choice of the test function does not allow us to estimate the
D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-norm of the equation (
1.1
) well. The test function we choose in this paper is in some sense optimal. We explain this point further in Remark
3.1
. The second point is that we need to expand the equation (

) to the fourth order (note that in the non-degenerate case, a second-order expansion is sufficient). Since in general

, we cannot expand it directly. Here we employ a high order expansion technique from
[
23
,
24
]
to overcome this problem. The third point is that, the construction of the functions
\{w_{k}\}_{k}
in Theorem
1.1
is technical. In the non-degenerate case, the optimality can be easily proved. However, in our case, many natural choices of
\{w_{k}\}_{k}
can only yield a quadratic estimate. To achieve the desired cubic estimate, we use the solutions of some special Schrödinger equations to perturb certain natural choice of
\{w_{k}\}_{k}
. A further explanation of this point can be found in Remark
3.2
.

To establish Theorem
1.8
, we first modify the finite-dimensional reduction method employed in
[
30
]
to find an approximation function
\rho_{0}
of
\rho:=u-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{\lambda_{i}}
.
\rho_{0}
has a good pointwise estimate and its

-norm can be well controlled. Next by applying careful estimates, we manage to work out the relations between
{\left\lVert\Pi_{X}(\rho)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
,
{\left\lVert\Pi_{X}^{\perp}(\rho)-\rho_{0}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
{\left\lVert H(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. Under the restriction (
1.14
),
{\left\lVert\Pi_{X}^{\perp}(\rho)-\rho_{0}\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
can be controlled by
{\left\lVert H(u)\right\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. Finally, combining above two steps gives the estimate (
1.13
).

2
A reformulation and some technical lemmas

2.1
A reformulation

Consider the following so-called Emden-Fowler transformation

u(r,\theta)=r^{\frac{2+2a-n}{2}}v(s,\theta),

where
r=|x|
,
s=-\ln{r}
and
\theta=\frac{x}{r}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. As was observed in
[
7
]
, with this transformation, the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality (
1.3
) can be rewritten as

S(a,b,n){\left\lVert v\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{C})}\leq{\left\lVert\partial_{s}v\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\nabla_{\theta}v\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}+\Lambda{\left\lVert v\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}

(2.1)

and the critical Hardy-Hénon equation (
1.1
) is equivalent to

H_{1}(v):=\partial_{s}^{2}v+\Delta_{\theta}v-\Lambda v+|v|^{p-2}v=0.

(2.2)

Here
\Lambda:=\left(\frac{n-2-2a}{2}\right)^{2}
and
\nabla_{\theta},\Delta_{\theta}
denote the gradient and the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, respectively. This transformation provides an isometry between the spaces
D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
. Here we equip
H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
with the following norm

{\left\lVert v\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=\left({\left\lVert\partial_{s}v\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\nabla_{\theta}v\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}+\Lambda{\left\lVert v\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that in this case, the Felli-Schneider curve can be parametrized by
n\geq 2
,
2<p<2^{*}
and
\Lambda=\frac{4(n-1)}{p^{2}-4}
. Along the Felli-Schneider curve, the set of positive solutions to the equation (
2.2
) is given by

\mathcal{M}_{p,\Lambda,n}=\left\{V_{t}(s)\;\Big|\;V_{t}(s):=\beta(\cosh{(\alpha(s-t))})^{-\frac{2}{p-2}},t\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

(2.3)

where
\alpha:=\frac{p-2}{2}\sqrt{\Lambda}
and
\beta:=\left(\frac{p\Lambda}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}
. The elements in
\mathcal{M}_{p,\Lambda,n}
are also called
Talenti bubbles
. Similar to the original setting on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we define
Y_{t}=\text{Span}\{V_{t}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{i},1\leq i\leq n\}
. From Lemma
2.4
,
Y_{t}\subset H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
consists of nontrivial solutions to the linearized equation of (
2.2
) near the Talenti bubble
V_{t}
. We also define
\Pi_{Y_{t}}
and
\Pi_{Y_{t}}^{\perp}
to be the projections onto
Y_{t}
and the orthogonal complement of
Y_{t}
in
H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
, respectively.

Now we can state the following exact counterparts of Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.8
on
\mathcal{C}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
n\geq 2
,
2<p<2^{*}
and
\Lambda=\frac{4(n-1)}{p^{2}-4}
. Then there exist two positive constants
\delta=\delta(p,n)
and
C=C(p,n)
such that, for any function
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
satisfying

{\left\lVert v-V_{0}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq\delta,

it holds that

\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}{\left\lVert v-V_{t}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}^{3}\leq C{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}.

(2.4)

The above cubic estimate is sharp. Moreover, if the infimum in (
2.4
) is attained by
V_{t_{0}}
, then there exists a positive constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(p,n)
such that

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\Pi_{Y_{t_{0}}}^{\perp}(v)-V_{t_{0}}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq C{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}

(2.5)

provided

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\Pi_{Y_{t_{0}}}^{\perp}(v)-V_{t_{0}}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\geq C_{1}F_{2}\left({\left\lVert\Pi_{Y_{t_{0}}}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\right).

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume
n\geq 2
,
\nu\geq 2
,
2<p<2^{*}
and
\Lambda=\frac{4(n-1)}{p^{2}-4}
. Then there exist two positive constants
\delta=\delta(p,\nu,n)
and
C=C(p,\nu,n)
such that, for any function
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
satisfying

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\\
1\leq i\leq\nu\end{subarray}}{\left\lVert v-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{s_{i}}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq\delta

and the infimum is attained by
V_{t_{i}},1\leq i\leq\nu
with

\displaystyle|t_{i}-t_{j}|\geq\delta^{-1},\quad\forall\;i\neq j,

the following estimate holds

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\Pi_{Y}^{\perp}(v)-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{t_{i}}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq CF_{1}\left({\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}\right)

(2.6)

provided

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\Pi_{Y}^{\perp}(v)-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{t_{i}}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\geq CF_{1}\left({\left\lVert\Pi_{Y}(v)\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\right).

Here
Y:=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{i=1}^{\nu}Y_{t_{i}}
.

In the following two sections we focus on the proofs of the above two new theorems instead of the two original ones. It seems that dealing with the equation (
2.2
) on
\mathcal{C}
can simplify some expressions and computations.

2.2
Some technical lemmas

In this subsection we provide some important lemmas. The first lemma contains three elementary inequalities.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and
p>2
. Then we have

\left||x+y|^{p-2}(x+y)-|x|^{p-2}x-(p-1)|x|^{p-2}y\right|\lesssim\chi(p>3)|x|^{p-3}y^{2}+|y|^{p-1}

and

\displaystyle\left||x+y|^{p-2}-|x|^{p-2}\right|\cdot|x|\lesssim\begin{cases}|x||y|^{p-2}+|x|^{p-2}|y|,&p\geq 3,\\
|xy|^{\frac{p-1}{2}},&p<3.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if
|y|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|

\displaystyle|x+y|^{p-2}(x+y)-|x|^{p-2}x-(p-1)|x|^{p-2}y-\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{2}|x|^{p-3}y^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)}{6}|x|^{p-4}y^{3}\Big|\lesssim|x|^{p-5}y^{4}.

The first and third inequalities are standard. The second inequality can be obtained by treating the cases
2|x|\leq|y|
and
2|x|\geq|y|
separately. The second lemma concerns with the spectrum of the linearized equation of (
2.2
) near certain Talenti bubble. We refer the readers to
[
22
,
24
]
for the proof of this Lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume
n\geq 2
,
2<p<2^{*}
and
\Lambda=\frac{4(n-1)}{p^{2}-4}
. Denote by
\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i}
the eigenvalues of the equation

-\partial_{s}^{2}v-\Delta_{\theta}v+\Lambda v=\gamma V_{0}^{p-2}v,\quad v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{C}),

then one has

\gamma_{1}=1,\quad\gamma_{2}=p-1,\quad\gamma_{3}>p-1.

Moreover, the first and second eigenspaces are given by

\mathrm{span}\{V_{0}\}\;\;\mathrm{and}\;\;\mathrm{span}\left\{\partial_{s}V_{0};V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{i},1\leq i\leq n\right\},

respectively. Here
\theta_{i}\;(1\leq i\leq n)
denote the Cartesian coordinates restricted to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

The degeneracy of (
2.2
) (and thus (
1.1
)) is caused by the nontrivial elements
V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{i},1\leq i\leq n
. Note that via the Emden-Fowler transformation,
u\in D_{a}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is centrally symmetric if and only if
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
satisfies
v(s,\theta)=v(s,-\theta)
. Obviously for such
v
one has
\Pi_{Y_{t}}(v)=0,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore,
\Pi_{X_{\lambda}}(u)=0,\forall\lambda>0
.
The third lemma concerns the power series solution of a special Schrödinger equation.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume
n\geq 2
,
2<p<2^{*}
and
\Lambda=\frac{4(n-1)}{p^{2}-4}
. Then there exist
\tau,\eta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\{A_{k}\}_{k},\{B_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}
, depending only on
p
and
n
, such that the unique
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
-solution
g
of

-\partial_{s}^{2}g+(2n+\Lambda)g-(p-1)V_{0}^{p-2}g=V_{0}^{2p-3}

has the following power series representation

g(s)=\tau V_{0}^{\sqrt{1+\frac{2n}{\Lambda}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}\cosh^{-2k}(\alpha s)+\eta V_{0}^{2p-3}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k}\cosh^{-2k}(\alpha s).

Here
\alpha:=\frac{p-2}{2}\sqrt{\Lambda}
. Moreover,

A_{k}=\mathcal{O}(k^{-\frac{3}{2}}),\quad B_{k}=\mathcal{O}(k^{-\frac{3}{2}}).

The uniqueness is a direct consequence of spectral analysis, and the power series representation can be calculated concretely. We refer the readers to
[
24
, Lemma 12]
for its detailed proof. The fourth lemma comes from the discussions in
[
24
, Section 4]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume
n\geq 2
,
2<p<2^{*}
and
\Lambda=\frac{4(n-1)}{p^{2}-4}
. For any
\varepsilon
small, set

E_{\varepsilon}:=\inf_{g\in\hat{Y}}\left((1-\varepsilon){\left\lVert g\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}-(p-1)\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left(V_{0}^{p-2}g^{2}+(p-2)V_{0}^{2p-3}\theta_{n}^{2}g\right)\right)

and

F:=\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{4}\left(\frac{p-1}{{\left\lVert V_{0}\right\rVert}^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{C})}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{2p-2}\theta_{n}^{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{p-3}{3}\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{3p-4}\theta_{n}^{4}\right).

Here the space
\hat{Y}
is the orthogonal complement of
\mathrm{Span}\left\{V_{0};\partial_{s}V_{0};V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{i},1\leq i\leq n\right\}
in
H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
. Then we have

F>0,\quad E_{0}+F>0,\quad E_{\varepsilon}=E_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon).

Moreover, when
n\geq 3
,

\frac{E_{0}}{F}+1\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as }p\rightarrow 2^{*}.

Although a detailed proof of this lemma has been given in
[
24
]
, we briefly explain the main ideas here for the convenience of the readers, as this lemma is crucial in our proof of Theorem
2.1
. The value of
F
can be explicitly computed. As for the term
E_{\varepsilon}
, we expand the function
g
in spherical harmonics:
g(s,\theta):=\sum\limits_{i,j}a_{i,j}(s)Y_{i,j}(\theta)
. Here
\{Y_{i,j}\}_{j}
is an
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
-orthonormal basis of spherical harmonics of degree
i
. We take
Y_{0,0}=c_{1}
and
Y_{2,0}=c_{2}(\theta_{n}^{2}-\frac{1}{n})
, where
c_{1},c_{2}
are two normalization factors. Substituting this expansion into
E_{\varepsilon}
and employing some spectrum conditions, we can transform
E_{\varepsilon}
into a sum of two quadratic forms involving
a_{0,0}
and
a_{2,0}
. Again, using the spectrum conditions, we can show that these two forms are non-degenerate and can thus be minimized by the standard ‘completing the square’ argument. It is worth mentioning that in this procedure one needs to explicitly solve some special Schrödinger equations (see Lemma
2.5
for example). Even after obtaining a concrete expression for
E_{\varepsilon}
, additional work is required to verify that
E_{0}+F>0
.

Finally, we give some integral estimates involved Talenti bubbles and their derivatives.

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
q_{1},q_{2}\geq 0
and
q_{1}+q_{2}=p
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, if
|q_{1}-q_{2}|\geq\varepsilon
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{t_{1}}^{q_{1}}V_{t_{2}}^{q_{2}}\approx_{\varepsilon}e^{-\sqrt{\Lambda}|t_{1}-t_{2}|\min\{q_{1},q_{2}\}}.

(2.7)

When
q_{1}=q_{2}=\frac{p}{2}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{t_{1}}^{\frac{p}{2}}V_{t_{2}}^{\frac{p}{2}}\approx(|t_{1}-t_{2}|+1)e^{-\frac{p\sqrt{\Lambda}}{2}|t_{1}-t_{2}|}.

(2.8)

Lemma 2.8
.

Given
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. If
t_{1}\ll t_{2}
, it holds that

\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{t_{1}}^{p-1}\partial_{s}V_{t_{2}}\approx e^{\sqrt{\Lambda}(t_{1}-t_{2})}.

(2.9)

The proof of Lemma
2.7
is similar to
[
30
, Lemma 5.1]
. The estimate (
2.9
) can be easily computed using the expressions of Talenti bubbles
V_{t},t\in\mathbb{R}
.

3
Proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
2.1

In this section we first prove Theorem
2.1
. Theorem
1.7
then follows from the Emden-Fowler transformation. Combining Theorem
1.7
and the global compactness principle
[
30
, Proposition 3.2]
, we obtain Theorem
1.1
. We also give some remarks about our proof in the end of this section.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let us prove (
2.4
) first. If (
2.4
) does not hold for any
\delta>0
, then there exists a sequence
\{v_{k}\}_{k}
of functions in
H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
such that

v_{k}\notin\mathcal{M}_{p,\Lambda,n},\quad{\left\lVert v_{k}-V_{0}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow 0\;\;\text{as }k\rightarrow+\infty

(3.1)

and

\frac{{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}}{\inf\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}{\left\lVert v_{k}-V_{t}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}^{3}}\rightarrow 0\;\;\text{as }k\rightarrow+\infty.

(3.2)

Up to a suitable translation on
\mathbb{R}
, we can assume

\inf_{\alpha,t\in\mathbb{R}}{\left\lVert v_{k}-\alpha V_{t}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}={\left\lVert v_{k}-\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}.

(3.3)

Set
\rho_{k}:=v_{k}-\alpha_{k}V_{0}
. From (
3.1
) it is easy to see that
|\alpha_{k}-1|\rightarrow 0
and
{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow 0
. Since
\alpha_{k}V_{0}
minimizes the distance, by variation we can derive the following two orthogonality conditions:

\left\langle\rho_{k},V_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=\left\langle\rho_{k},\partial_{s}V_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=0.

(3.4)

Since
H_{1}(V_{0})=0
, we can rewrite (
3.4
) as

\left\langle\rho_{k},V^{p-1}_{0}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}=\left\langle\rho_{k},V_{0}^{p-2}\partial_{s}V_{0}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}=0.

(3.5)

Consider the following identity:

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}v_{k}+\Delta_{\theta}v_{k}-\Lambda v_{k}+|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}=

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}\rho_{k}+\Delta_{\theta}\rho_{k}-\Lambda\rho_{k}+\left(\alpha_{k}^{p-1}-\alpha_{k}\right)V_{0}^{p-1}

(3.6)

\displaystyle+\left(|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}-\alpha_{k}^{p-1}V_{0}^{p-1}\right).

Testing it with
V_{0}
, exploiting Lemma
2.3
and using the orthogonality conditions in (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we can deduce

\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{k}^{p-1}-\alpha_{k}\right|\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle\;{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}+\chi(p>3)\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p-2}|\rho_{k}|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}|\rho_{k}|^{p-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}^{\min\{2,p-1\}}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}.

Since
|\alpha_{k}-1|\rightarrow 0
, we find

|\alpha_{k}-1|\lesssim{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}^{\min\{2,p-1\}}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}.

(3.7)

The combination of (
3.1
), (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.7
) indicates

\frac{{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}}{{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}^{3}}\rightarrow 0\;\;\text{as }k\rightarrow+\infty.

(3.8)

In the following we aim to show that (
3.8
) is impossible. Up to a suitable rotation on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, we assume

\rho_{k}=\mu_{k}\left(\hat{\mu}_{k}V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right),

where
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k},\{\hat{\mu}_{k}\}_{k}
are two sequences of numbers such that

(\mu_{k},\hat{\mu}_{k})=\begin{cases}\left(\left\langle\rho_{k},V^{\frac{p}{2}}_{0}\theta_{n}\right\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}{\left\lVert V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}^{-2},1\right)&\text{if }\left\langle\rho_{k},V^{\frac{p}{2}}_{0}\theta_{n}\right\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\neq 0,\\
\left({\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})},0\right)&\text{if }\left\langle\rho_{k},V^{\frac{p}{2}}_{0}\theta_{n}\right\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=0;\end{cases}

and
\eta_{k}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
satisfies

\left\langle\eta_{k},V_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=\left\langle\eta_{k},\partial_{s}V_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=\left\langle\eta_{k},V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{i}\right\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=0\quad\text{for any }1\leq i\leq n.

(3.9)

Thanks to Lemma
2.4
, the relations in (
3.9
) can be rewritten as

\left\langle\eta_{k},V^{p-1}_{0}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}=\left\langle\eta_{k},V_{0}^{p-2}\partial_{s}V_{0}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}=\left\langle\eta_{k},V_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}p-2}\theta_{i}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}=0\quad\text{for any }1\leq i\leq n.

(3.10)

Without loss of generality, we suppose

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=L\in[0,+\infty].

(3.11)

We shall divide our analysis for (
3.8
) into two parts that are devoted to the case
L>0
and
L=0
, respectively.

Case 1:
L>0
.

Let us start by testing
H_{1}(v_{k})
against
\rho_{k}
. Using Lemma
2.3
and the orthogonality conditions in (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) yields

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=

\displaystyle-\left\langle H_{1}(v_{k}),\rho_{k}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}\rho_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+(p-1)\alpha_{k}^{p-2}\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p-2}\rho^{2}_{k}

(3.12)

\displaystyle+C\chi(p>3)\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p-3}|\rho_{k}|^{3}+C\int_{\mathcal{C}}|\rho_{k}|^{p}.

Exploiting the Hölder inequality and the estimate (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}-(p-1+o_{\delta}(1))\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p-2}\rho^{2}_{k}\lesssim{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}.

(3.13)

Thanks to the orthogonality conditions (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and Lemma
2.4
, we find

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=

\displaystyle\;\mu_{k}^{2}\hat{\mu}_{k}^{2}{\left\lVert V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+\mu_{k}^{2}{\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\;(p-1)\mu_{k}^{2}\hat{\mu}_{k}^{2}\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}+(p-1)\mu_{k}^{2}\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p-2}\eta_{k}^{2}+\mu_{k}^{2}\left(1-\frac{p-1}{\gamma_{3}}\right){\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(p-1)\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p-2}\rho^{2}_{k}+\mu_{k}^{2}\left(1-\frac{p-1}{\gamma_{3}}\right){\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}.

(3.14)

Recall that
\gamma_{3}>p-1
is the third eigenvalue defined in Lemma
2.4
. Since
L>0
, we have

{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\lesssim_{L}\mu_{k}{\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}.

(3.15)

Substituting (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) into (
3.13
) gives

{\left\lVert\rho_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\lesssim_{L}{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})},

which obviously contradicts (
3.8
).

Case 2:
L=0
.

In this case we have
\hat{\mu}_{k}\equiv 1
and
\mu_{k}\neq 0
. Since the
H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
-norm of
\rho_{k}
is comparable to
\mu_{k}
, (
3.8
) is equivalent to

|\mu_{k}|^{-3}{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow 0\;\;\text{as }k\rightarrow+\infty.

(3.16)

Let us test
H_{1}(v_{k})
against
\mu_{k}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)
. Exploiting the orthogonality conditions in (
3.9
), we deduce

\displaystyle\mu_{k}^{2}{\left\lVert V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+2\mu_{k}^{2}{\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu_{k}\left\langle H_{1}(v_{k}),V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C})}+\mu_{k}\int_{\mathcal{C}}|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;C|\mu_{k}|{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}+\mu_{k}\int_{\mathcal{C}}|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right).

(3.17)

To control the second term, we make use of a high order expansion technique presented in
[
23
,
24
]
. To be specific, we split the integration domain
\mathcal{C}
into two parts:
\left\{|\eta_{k}|<V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\right\}
and
\left\{|\eta_{k}|\geq V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\right\}
. In the first part, using the facts that
V_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
and
p>2
, we know
\left|V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right|\leq 2V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\lesssim V_{0}
. Therefore, we can apply the third estimate in Lemma
2.3
to derive

\displaystyle|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}=

\displaystyle\;\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-1}+\mu_{k}(p-1)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)

\displaystyle+\mu_{k}^{2}\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{2}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)^{2}

(3.18)

\displaystyle+\mu_{k}^{3}\frac{(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)}{6}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-4}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)^{3}+\mathcal{O}\left(\mu_{k}^{4}V_{0}^{3p-5}\right).

Note that

\displaystyle V_{0}^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)=

\displaystyle\;V_{0}^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}+2V_{0}^{p-2}\eta_{k}^{2}+3V_{0}^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)\eta_{k},

\displaystyle V_{0}^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)^{2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)=

\displaystyle\;V_{0}^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{3}+4V_{0}^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}\eta_{k}

\displaystyle+5V_{0}^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)\eta_{k}^{2}+2V_{0}^{p-3}\eta_{k}^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;V_{0}^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{3}+4V_{0}^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}\eta_{k}+\mathcal{O}\left(\eta_{k}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle V_{0}^{p-4}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)^{3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)=

\displaystyle\;V_{0}^{p-4}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{4}+5V_{0}^{p-4}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{3}\eta_{k}

\displaystyle+9V_{0}^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}+7V_{0}^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)\eta_{k}^{3}

\displaystyle+2V_{0}^{p-4}\eta_{k}^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;V_{0}^{p-4}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{4}+\mathcal{O}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{5}{2}p-4}|\eta_{k}|\right).

Substituting these three identities into (
3.18
) gives

\displaystyle\;|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-1}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)+\mu_{k}(p-1)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+2\mu_{k}(p-1)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}\eta_{k}^{2}+3\mu_{k}(p-1)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\eta_{k}

\displaystyle+\mu_{k}^{2}\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{2}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{3}+2\mu_{k}^{2}(p-1)(p-2)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}\eta_{k}

\displaystyle+\mu_{k}^{3}\frac{(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)}{6}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-4}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{4}

(3.19)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(\mu_{k}^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}+|\mu_{k}|^{3}V_{0}^{\frac{5}{2}p-4}|\eta_{k}|+\mu_{k}^{4}V_{0}^{3p-5}\right).

In the second part, since
\left|V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right|\leq 2|\eta_{k}|
and
p>2
, we can use the first estimate in Lemma
2.3
to deduce

\displaystyle|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}=

\displaystyle\;\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-1}+\mu_{k}(p-1)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(\chi(p>3)\mu_{k}^{2}V_{0}^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)^{2}+\mu_{k}^{p-1}\left|V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right|^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-1}+\mu_{k}(p-1)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(\chi(p>3)\mu_{k}^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}+|\mu_{k}|^{p-1}|\eta_{k}|^{p-1}\right)

and thus

\displaystyle\;|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-1}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)+\mu_{k}(p-1)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}

(3.20)

\displaystyle+2\mu_{k}(p-1)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}\eta_{k}^{2}+3\mu_{k}(p-1)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\eta_{k}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(\chi(p>3)\mu_{k}^{2}|\eta_{k}|^{3}+|\mu_{k}|^{p-1}|\eta_{k}|^{p}\right).

Note that in this part, we also have

\displaystyle\mu_{k}^{2}\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{2}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{3}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{O}\left(\mu_{k}^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle 2\mu_{k}^{2}(p-1)(p-2)\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}\eta_{k}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{O}\left(\mu_{k}^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\mu_{k}^{3}\frac{(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)}{6}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-4}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{4}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{O}\left(|\mu_{k}|^{3}\eta_{k}^{2}\right).

Combining these three identities with (
3.20
) shows that the pointwise estimate (
3.19
) still holds in this part, but with an additional error term

.

Exploiting our arguments above and utilizing the orthogonality conditions in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mu_{k}(p-1)\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}+2\mu_{k}(p-1)\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}\eta_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\mu_{k}^{2}(p-1)(p-2)\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}\eta_{k}

\displaystyle+\mu_{k}^{3}\frac{(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)}{6}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-4}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{4}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(|\mu_{k}|^{\min\{2,p-1\}}{\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+\mu_{k}^{4}\right).

(3.21)

In this computation we also use the Hölder inequality and the following two inequalities

\displaystyle 2|\mu_{k}|^{3}{\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq\;\mu_{k}^{2}{\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+\mu_{k}^{4},\quad|\mu_{k}|^{p-1}{\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}^{p}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq

\displaystyle|\mu_{k}|^{p-1}{\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}.

Now it follows from (
3.17
), (
3.21
) and Lemma
2.4
that the value of

\displaystyle\;\mu_{k}^{2}{\left\lVert V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+2\mu_{k}^{2}{\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}-\mu_{k}\int_{\mathcal{C}}|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+2\eta_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mu_{k}^{2}(p-1)\left(1-\alpha_{k}^{p-2}\right)\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}+2\mu_{k}^{2}\left(1-C|\mu_{k}|^{\min\{1,p-2\}}\right){\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\mu_{k}^{2}(p-1)\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-2}\eta_{k}^{2}-2\mu_{k}^{3}(p-1)(p-2)\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-3}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}\eta_{k}

(3.22)

\displaystyle-\mu_{k}^{4}\frac{(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)}{6}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\alpha_{k}V_{0}\right)^{p-4}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{4}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\mu_{k}|^{5}\right)

can be bounded above by
C|\mu_{k}|{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}
.

To simplify (
3.22
), we need to find appropriate estimates for
\left|\alpha_{k}^{p-2}-1\right|
and
\left|\alpha_{k}-1\right|
. Note that (
3.7
) does not meet our requirements. Let us test the identity in (
3.6
) with
V_{0}
again and use the orthogonality conditions in (
3.9
) to derive

\displaystyle\left|\left(\alpha_{k}^{p-1}-\alpha_{k}\right)\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left(|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}-\alpha_{k}^{p-1}V_{0}^{p-1}\right)V_{0}\right|\lesssim{\left\lVert H_{1}(v_{k})\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}

(3.23)

To control the second term, we apply the high order expansion technique and Lemma
2.3
once again. In the first region
\left\{|\eta_{k}|<V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\right\}
, we have

\displaystyle\;\left(|v_{k}|^{p-2}v_{k}-\alpha_{k}^{p-1}V_{0}^{p-1}\right)V_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mu_{k}(p-1)\alpha_{k}^{p-2}V_{0}^{p-1}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\;\mu_{k}(p-1)\alpha_{k}^{p-2}V_{0}^{p-1}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\eta_{k}\right)+\mu_{k}^{2}\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{2}\alpha_{k}^{p-3}V_{0}^{p-2}\left(V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}\right)^{2}

Combining (

\displaystyle 2\mu_{k}^{2}\left(1-C|\mu_{k}|^{\min\{1,p-2\}}\right){\left\lVert\eta_{k}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}^{2}-2\mu_{k}^{2}(p-1)\int_{\mathcal{C}}V_{0}^{p-2}\eta_{k}^{2}

Now we show that the cubic estimate (

) is optimal. Choose a sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k}
of numbers such that

and
\mu_{k}\rightarrow 0

The constant

) and (

. Thanks to Lemma

\displaystyle\;\partial_{s}^{2}w_{k}+\Delta_{\theta}w_{k}-\Lambda w_{k}+|w_{k}|^{p-2}w_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(1-C_{0}\mu_{k}^{2}\right)V_{0}^{p-1}-\mu_{k}(p-1)V_{0}^{p-2}V_{0}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{n}+\mu_{k}^{2}\left(\partial_{s}^{2}\eta+\Delta_{\theta}\eta-\Lambda\eta\right)

\displaystyle|c_{0}|\lesssim{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\rho_{1}\right\rVert}^{\min\{2,p-1\}}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\rho_{2}\right\rVert}^{\min\{2,p-1\}}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}.

Proof of Theorem

{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}^{2}+\max_{1\leq k\leq\nu}\left|\int_{\mathcal{C}}\sigma^{p-2}\rho\;\partial_{s}V_{t_{k}}\right|\gtrsim Q.

(4.2)

We adapt the ideas in
[
12

30

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}v+\Delta_{\theta}v-\Lambda v+|v|^{p-2}v=

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}\rho+\Delta_{\theta}\rho-\Lambda\rho+\left(|v|^{p-2}v-\sigma^{p-1}-(p-1)\sigma^{p-2}\rho\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\sigma^{p-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{t_{i}}^{p-1}\right)+(p-1)\sigma^{p-2}\rho.

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}=

\displaystyle{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}+\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\beta_{jk}|^{2}+F_{3}(Q)\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\beta_{jk}|^{\min\{2,p-1\}}\gtrsim Q.

(4.34)

Now we define

\displaystyle\psi_{1}:=\Pi_{Y}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{ij}V_{t_{i}}^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta_{j}

and

\displaystyle\psi_{2}:=\Pi_{Y}^{\perp}(v)-\sigma=\rho_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\beta_{i}V_{t_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\hat{\beta}_{i}\partial_{s}V_{t_{i}}+\rho_{2}.

Based on the above arguments, we have
the following two estimates:

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\psi_{2}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\lesssim F_{1}(Q)+{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}+F_{3}(Q){\left\lVert\psi_{1}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\psi_{1}\right\rVert}^{\min\{2,p-1\}}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}

(4.35)

and

\displaystyle Q\lesssim{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}+F_{3}(Q){\left\lVert\psi_{1}\right\rVert}^{\min\{2,p-1\}}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\psi_{1}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}.

(4.36)

\displaystyle F_{3}(Q){\left\lVert\psi_{1}\right\rVert}^{\min\{2,p-1\}}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\psi_{1}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})},

from (

) we get

\displaystyle Q\lesssim{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})},\quad{\left\lVert\psi_{1}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

which indicates (
2.6
). If

\displaystyle F_{3}(Q){\left\lVert\psi_{1}\right\rVert}^{\min\{2,p-1\}}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}+{\left\lVert\psi_{1}\right\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\geq{\left\lVert H_{1}(v)\right\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{C})},

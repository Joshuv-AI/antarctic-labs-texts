Compact, spatial-mode-interaction-free, ultralow-loss, nonlinear photonic integrated circuits
Xinru Ji, Junqiu Liu, Jijun He, Rui Ning Wang, Zheru Qiu, Johann Riemensberger, Tobias J. Kippenberg

Abstract




Nonlinear photonics based on integrated circuits
Moss et al. 2013
;
Eggleton et al. 2019
has enabled applications such as parametric amplifiers
Foster et al. 2006
, soliton frequency combs
Kippenberg et al. 2018
, supercontinua
Gaeta et al. 2019
, and non-reciprocal devices
Sounas and Alù 2017
.
Ultralow optical loss and the capability for dispersion engineering are essential, which necessitate the use of multi-mode waveguides.
Despite that rich interaction among different spatial waveguide eigenmodes can give rise to novel nonlinear phenomena
Yang et al. 2017
;
Guo et al. 2017
;
Cole et al. 2017
;
Karpov et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2014
;
Xue et al. 2015a
;
Matsko et al. 2016
;
Yang et al. 2016
, spatial mode interaction is typically undesired as it increases optical loss, perturbs local dispersion profile, and impedes soliton formation
Herr et al. 2014
;
Kim et al. 2021
;
Ramelow et al. 2014
;
Huang et al. 2016
.
Adiabatic bends
Chen et al. 2012
;
Cherchi et al. 2013
, such as Euler bends
Fujisawa et al. 2017
;
Vogelbacher et al. 2019
;
Jiang et al. 2018
whose curvature varies linearly with their path length, have been favoured to suppress spatial mode interaction.
Adiabatic bends can essentially connect any two waveguide segments with adiabatic mode conversion
Chen et al. 2012
, thus efficiently avoid mode mixing due to mode mismatch.
However, previous works lack quantitative measurement data and analysis to fairly evaluate the adiabaticity, and are not based on photonic integrated circuits with tight optical confinement and optical losses of few decibel per meter.
Here, we adapt
Fujisawa et al. 2017
;
Vogelbacher et al. 2019
, optimize, and implement Euler bends to build compact racetrack microresonators based on ultralow-loss, multi-mode, silicon nitride photonic integrated circuits
Liu et al. 2021
.
The racetrack microresonators feature a small footprint of only 0.21 mm
2
for 19.8 GHz free spectral range, critical for photonic integration of other functionalities, e.g. piezoelectric modulators
Tian et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2020a
whose capacitance is proportional to the device area not perimeter.
We quantitatively investigate the suppression of spatial mode interaction in the racetrack microresonators with Euler bends, in comparison with normal racetracks using circular bends.
We show that the optical loss rate (15.5 MHz) is preserved, on par with the mode interaction strength (25 MHz).
This results in an unperturbed microresonator dispersion profile.
We further demonstrate single dissipative Kerr soliton of 19.8 GHz repetition rate in the racetrack microresonator with Euler bends, without complex laser tuning schemes or auxiliary lasers.
The optimized Euler bends and racetrack microresonators can be key building blocks for integrated nonlinear photonic systems
Spencer et al. 2018
;
Newman et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2020b
;
Zhang et al. 2019
, as well as linear circuits for programmable processors
Zhuang et al. 2015
and photonic quantum computing
Wang et al. 2020
;
Arrazola et al. 2021
.

Over the past decade, integrated photonic technology has been successfully translated from laboratory research into industrial applications
Rickman 2014
, and has revolutionized today’s optical communication networks
Thomson et al. 2016
;
Agrell et al. 2016
.
Besides silicon – the backbone microelectronic material, many new material platforms have been developed, as well as heterogeneous and hybrid integration techniques
Komljenovic et al. 2016
;
Kaur et al. 2021
;
Margalit et al. 2021
to merge them with silicon.
Among these materials, amorphous silicon nitride (Si

N
4
)

;
Muñoz et al. 2019
– first proposed in 1980’s
Henry et al. 1987
for integrated photonics due to its wide transparency window from visible to mid-infrared and a wide bandgap of 5 eV – has made significant progress recently.
Stoichiometric Si
3
N
4
can be deposited via chemical vapor deposition (CVD) and is CMOS-compatible.
Recent advances in fabrication
Xuan et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2021
;
Ji et al. 2021
;

have achieved Si
3
N
4

;

and negligible two-photon absorption, Si

N

Multi-mode waveguides with tight optical confinement are widely used in integrated nonlinear photonics.
The large waveguide cross-sections reduce optical loss by reducing light interaction with waveguide surface roughness
Zhang et al. 2020
.
Indeed, state-of-the-art integrated waveguides of losses near 1 dB/m are all multi-mode
Xuan et al. 2016
;
Ji et al. 2021
;
Liu et al. 2021
;
Ye et al. 2021a
;
Zhang et al. 2017
.
In addition, stimulated Brillouin scattering
Eggleton et al. 2019
;
Poulton et al. 2012
and indirect-interband transition
Sounas and Alù 2017
;
Yu and Fan 2009
among different spatial modes are the basis of magnetic-free non-reciprocal devices
Lira et al. 2012
;
Sohn et al. 2018
;
Kittlaus et al. 2018
;
Tian et al. 2021
.
Equally important, harmonic generation is facilitated by employing phase matching among different spatial modes
Levy et al. 2011
;
Surya et al. 2018
;
Porcel et al. 2017
;
Lu et al. 2021
;
Nitiss et al. 2021
.
For Kerr nonlinear photonics, anomalous GVD is required, which is realized via geometric dispersion engineering
Okawachi et al. 2014
using multi-mode waveguides.
Novel phenomena can arise from spatial mode interaction in multi-mode microresonators, such as the generation of Stokes solitons
Yang et al. 2017
, breathing solitons
Guo et al. 2017
, soliton crystals
Cole et al. 2017
;
Karpov et al. 2019
, dark pulses
Liu et al. 2014
;
Xue et al. 2015a
, and spatial-mode-interaction-induced dispersive waves
Matsko et al. 2016
;
Yang et al. 2016
.
Single avoided-mode crossing (AMX) resulting from strong mode coupling can facilitate the stabilization of bright solitons
Bao et al. 2017
;
Yi et al. 2017
or the generation of dark pulses
Xue et al. 2015b
;
Kim et al. 2019
.
However, spatial mode coupling in highly over-moded waveguides is uncontrollable, yielding spectrally distributed AMXs with random strengths.
These coexistent AMXs can often prohibit soliton formation
Herr et al. 2014
;
Kim et al. 2021
and significantly distort soliton spectra
Ramelow et al. 2014
;
Huang et al. 2016
.
Therefore, suppression of spatial mode coupling in multi-mode waveguides is still desired for stable and reliable access to the single soliton regime
Liu et al. 2020b
.

In multi-mode waveguides, defects and surface roughness can introduce spatial mode interaction.
In addition, mode mixing occurs when waveguide curvature experiences non-adiabatic transition, e.g. from a straight section to a curved section, due to bending-induced mode mismatch.
Despite that the waveguide cross-section is identical, the waveguide bending causes changes of waveguide eigenmodes due to the modified effective indices
Subramaniam et al. 1997
, and finally yields spatial mode mixing. In ultralow-loss PICs where defects and surface roughness are carefully addressed, mode mixing due to non-adiabatic transition can be the dominant cause for spatial mode interaction.
Such mode interaction represents a major loss channel
Pfeiffer et al. 2017
, and can severely limit the performance of ultralow-loss PICs and planar microresonators.

In this work, we demonstrate a novel photonic design that suppresses spatial mode interaction and effectively operates multi-mode waveguides in the single-mode regime.
Using a racetrack microresonator that features high quality factor
Q>10^{7}
and consists of two straight waveguides and two adiabatic Euler bends, we qualitatively characterize the suppressed spatial mode interaction.

Racetrack microresonators of tens of gigahertz free spectral range (FSR) are widely used for the generation of soliton microcombs or electro-optic combs with microwave repetition rates
Liu et al. 2020b
;
Zhang et al. 2019
.
A normal racetrack microresonator consists of two straight waveguides and two circular bends.
Since the optical eigenmodes are modified due to the waveguide curvature, the eigenmode bases are different for the straight waveguide (with an infinite bending radius) and curved waveguide (with a finite bending radius).
Therefore, mode mixing is introduced due to the abrupt mode transition.
In high-
Q
racetrack microresonators where light circulates many round-trips, such mode mixing can severely impact device performance, increase losses, and shift resonance eigen-frequencies.
For example, several studies have highlighted difficulties to access soliton states of microwave repetition rates in racetrack or finger-shape microresonators
Johnson et al. 2012
;
Xuan et al. 2016
;
Kim et al. 2017
.
To avoid mode mixing, microring resonators are used and single solitons with repetition rates in the microwave X- and K-band are generated
Liu et al. 2020b
, however the devices suffer from significant footprints (larger than 4 mm
2
) and constrained device density.
Alternatively, mode filtering elements
Kordts et al. 2016
;
Kim et al. 2021
can be used to suppress mode mixing.

Here, we optimize racetrack microresonators by replacing the two circular bends with two Euler bends
Fujisawa et al. 2017
;
Vogelbacher et al. 2019
that have adiabatic radius transition from infinite (in the straight section) to finite (in the bending section).
An Euler bend has a curvature (the inverse of radius) varying linearly with its path length.
Adiabatic bends, including Euler bends, have profound applications in railroad and highway engineering, and have later been used in integrated photonics.
For example, a general design for adiabatic waveguide connection
Chen et al. 2012
has been applied to create ultralow-loss, meter-long, suspended silica photonic delay lines
Lee et al. 2012
.
Modified Euler bends
Jiang et al. 2018
have been applied on multi-mode silicon-on-insulator (SOI) racetrack microresonators
Zhang et al. 2020
to achieve high
Q
.
Nonlinear adiabatic bends
Ji et al. 2021
have been used on Si
3
N
4
microring resonators to to achieve low loss and broadband external coupling with bus waveguides.

Finally, we demonstrate single soliton generation of 19.8 GHz repetition rate in the racetrack microresonator with Euler bends (
p=1

When the continuous-wave (CW) pump laser scans across the resonance from the blue-detuned side to the red-detuned side, a step indicating soliton formation

Here we observe a soliton step length of
\sim 0.5

Due to the suppressed AMXs, the soliton step is sufficiently long, allowing for direct access to the single soliton state using simple laser piezo frequency tuning

;
Guo et al. 2016

An erbium-doped fiber amplifer (EDFA) is used to increase the on-chip pump power to approximately 55.7 mW to seed soliton formation.

The single soliton spectrum fit shows a 3-dB bandwidth of 16.3 nm, corresponding to a pulse duration of 156 fs.

We further characterize the single-sideband (SSB) phase noise of the K-band microwave carrier generated by photodetection of the soliton repetition rate, and investigate the phase noise reduction via operating the soliton at a “quiet point”

Figure

The detuning values are measured using a vector network analyser (VNA) that probes the resonance frequency relative to the laser

The “quiet point”

It is resulted from the balance of dispersive-wave-induced soliton spectral recoil and soliton-induced Raman self-frequency shift

Different from our previous studies where operation at the quiet point enables more than 20 dBc/Hz phase noise reduction, here we only observe less than 4 dBc/Hz reduction measured within 1 kHz to 100 kHz Fourier offset frequency.

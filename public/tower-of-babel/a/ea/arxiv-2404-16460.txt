A review of the tangent space in sub-Finsler geometry and applications to the failure of the 𝖢𝖣 \mathsf{CD} condition
Mattia Magnabosco and Tommaso Rossi

Abstract
We review the construction of the tangent space to a sub-Finsler manifold in the measured Gromov-Hausdorff sense. Under suitable assumptions on the measure, the metric measure tangent is described by the nilpotent approximation, equipped with a scalar multiple of the Lebesgue measure. We apply this result in the study of the Lott–Sturm–Villani curvature-dimension condition in sub-Finsler geometry. In particular, we show the failure of the 𝖢𝖣 \mathsf{CD} condition in 3D-contact sub-Finsler manifolds, equipped with a bounded measure.



1 Introduction

The celebrated curvature-dimension condition (or
\mathsf{CD}
for short) à-la Lott–Sturm–Villani
[
Stu06a
,
Stu06b
,
LV09
]
is a synthetic notion of Ricci curvature lower bounds (and dimensional upper bounds). More precisely, for a metric measure space, the
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition asks for a suitable
(K,N)
-convexity property of the Rényi entropy functionals in the Wasserstein space (see
[
Stu06b
, Def. 1.3]
for the precise definition). Here
K\in\mathbb{R}
represents a lower bound on the (Ricci) curvature while
N\in(1,\infty]
can be thought of as an upper bound on the dimension. Additionally, in a smooth Riemannian manifold, the
\mathsf{CD}
condition is a consistent curvature-dimension bound, in the sense it is equivalent to a lower bound on the Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature tensor.

A similar relation between curvature and
\mathsf{CD}
condition does not hold in the sub-Riemannian setting and sub-Finsler setting. Sub-Finsler geometry is a generalization of Finsler and sub-Riemannian geometry, where, given a smooth manifold
M
, a smoothly varying norm is defined only on a set of preferred directions, called the distribution.
Under the so-called Hörmander condition, the length-minimization procedure among admissible curves (namely those curves that are tangent to the distribution) yields a well-defined distance on
M
, called sub-Finsler distance.
In the contributions
[
Jui21
,
MR23a
,
RS23
]
, it shown that every complete sub-Riemannian manifold, equipped with a positive smooth measure, cannot satisfy the
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition for any choice of the parameters
K
and
N
. The same result has been proved for the sub-Finsler Heisenberg group
[
BT23
,
BMRT24b
,
BMRT24a
]
and for smooth sub-Finsler manifolds
[
MR23b
]
. In this paper, we generalize these results in two directions. First of all, we relax the assumptions on the measure by allowing bounded reference measures, in the sense of Definition
4.3
. Second of all, we prove the failure of the
\mathsf{CD}
condition for 3D-contact sub-Finsler manifolds. We refer to Section
5
for the precise statements of the results.

The strategy of the proofs is based on the stability of the
\mathsf{CD}
condition with respect to the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence. In particular, given a sub-Finsler manifold
M
, equipped with a bounded measure
\mathfrak{m}
, we assume by contradiction that the
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition holds. We then pass to the tangent space in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff sense. By stability of the
\mathsf{CD}
condition, the metric measure tangents of
(M,\mathsf{d}_{SF},\mathfrak{m})
satisfy the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition. Therefore, to obtain a contradiction, it is enough to show that there exists a metric measure tangent of
(M,\mathsf{d}_{SF},\mathfrak{m})
that does not satisfy the
\mathsf{CD}
condition.

The last step of the strategy is based on the characterization of the metric measure tangents to a sub-Finsler manifold.
Loosely speaking, for a general metric measure space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, a metric measure tangent at
x\in\mathsf{X}
captures its infinitesimal geometry around the base point
x
.
In a Riemannian manifold
(M,\mathrm{g})
, the metric measure tangent space at a point
x\in M
coincides with
T_{x}M
, equipped with the scalar product
\mathrm{g}_{x}
and with the Lebesgue measure.

In a sub-Finsler manifold, identifying the tangent space becomes less trivial, as the infinitesimal model is instead a quotient of a sub-Finsler Carnot group
1
1

A Carnot group is a connected and simply connected Lie group with stratified Lie algebra.
.
In
[
Mit85
,
Bel96
]
, it is shown that the metric tangent to a complete sub-Riemannian manifold
M
at a point
x\in M
is identified by its nilpotent approximation at
x
. The nilpotent approximation can be regarded as a non-holonomic first-order approximation of the sub-Riemannian structure at
x
and, in turn, is isometric to a quotient of a sub-Riemannian Carnot group (where a scalar product is defined on the first layer of its Lie algebra). In
[
ALDNG23
]
, the analogous result is proved in the greater generality of Lipschitz sub-Finsler manifolds with continuously varying norms (including also smooth sub-Finsler manifolds). For the convergence of the measure, adapting and extending the results of
[
GJ15
,
GJ16
]
, we characterize the blow-up of a general bounded measure on a sub-Finsler manifold. Here a bounded measure is a Borel measure which, in coordinates, is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, with density bounded from above and below by strictly positive constants, see Definition
4.3
. The description of the metric measure tangents of a sub-Finsler manifold is the summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a complete sub-Finsler manifold, equipped with a bounded measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Then, for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
q\in M
, there is a unique metric measure tangent space of
M
at
q
and it is its nilpotent approximation equipped with (a multiple of) the Lebesgue measure.

Structure of the paper.

In Section
3
, we describe the nilpotent approximation of a sub-Finsler manifold. In Section
4
, we identify the blow-up of the bounded measure
\mathfrak{m}
, leveraging its Lebesgue points, in the sense of Definition
4.5
. In detail, we show that
\mathfrak{m}
-almost every point is Lebesgue and, at those points, the blow-up is a scalar multiple of the Lebesgue measure of the correct dimension. Along the way, we also show that on equiregular manifolds, a measure is bounded if and only if it is locally Ahlfors regular. Finally, we prove Theorem
1.1
. We conclude the paper by detailing the aforementioned applications to the failure of the
\mathsf{CD}
condition, see Section
5
.

2 Preliminaries

Let
M
be a smooth manifold of dimension
n
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. A
sub-Finsler structure
on
M
is a couple
(\xi,{|\!\cdot\!|})
, such that:

(i)

\xi:E\rightarrow TM
is a morphism of vector bundles, where
E
is a normed vector bundle over
M
, meaning that, for every
q\in M
, the fiber
E_{q}
is a Banach space with norm
{|\!\cdot\!|}_{q}
. In addition, the dependence
q\mapsto{|\!\cdot\!|}_{q}
is smooth.

(ii)

The set of horizontal vector fields, defined as

\mathcal{D}:=\big\{\xi\circ\sigma\,:\,\sigma\in\Gamma(E)\big\}\subset\Gamma(TM),

(1)

is a
bracket-generating
family of vector fields (or it satisfies the Hörmander condtion), namely, setting

{\rm Lie}_{q}(\mathcal{D}):=\big\{X(q)\,:\,X\in\text{span}\{[X_{1},\ldots,[X_{j-1},X_{j}]]\,:\,X_{i}\in\mathcal{D},j\in\mathbb{N}\}\big\},\qquad\forall\,q\in M,

(2)

it holds that
{\rm Lie}_{q}(\mathcal{D})=T_{q}M
, for every
q\in M
.

We say that
(M,\xi,{|\!\cdot\!|})
, or simply
M
if there is no ambiguity, is a sub-Finsler manifold.

Remark 2.1
.

Every sub-Finsler structure
(\xi,{|\!\cdot\!|})
is equivalent to a free one, meaning that we can (and will) always assume that
E=M\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. See
[
ABB20
, Sec. 3.1.4]
for details. However, differently from the sub-Riemannian setting, we can not ensure that the norm
{|\!\cdot\!|}_{q}
on the fiber
E_{q}=\{q\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
is independent of
q
.

At every point
q\in M
we define the
distribution
at
q
as

\mathcal{D}_{q}:=\big\{\xi(q,w)\,:\,w\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\big\}=\big\{X(q)\,:\,X\in\mathcal{D}\big\}\subset T_{q}M.

(3)

This is a vector subspace of
T_{q}M
whose dimension is called
rank
(of the distribution) and denoted by
r(q):=\dim\mathcal{D}_{q}\leq n
. Moreover, the distribution can be described by a family of horizontal vector fields: letting
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,k}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, the
generating family
is the set
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,k}
, where

X_{i}(q):=\xi(q,e_{i})\qquad\forall\,q\in M,\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,k.

(4)

Then, according to (
3
),
\mathcal{D}_{q}=\text{span}\{X_{1}(q),\dots,X_{k}(q)\}
. On the distribution we define the
induced norm
as

\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}:=\inf\big\{\left\lvert w\right\rvert_{q}\,:\,v=\xi(q,w)\big\}\qquad\text{for every }v\in\mathcal{D}_{q}.

Since the infimum is actually a minimum, the function
{\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{q}
is a norm on
\mathcal{D}_{q}
, so that
(\mathcal{D}_{q},{\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{q})
is a normed space. Moreover, the dependence on the base point
M\ni q\mapsto{\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{q}
is smooth.

A continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
is
admissible
if its velocity
\dot{\gamma}(t)
is defined almost everywhere and there exists a function
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})\in L^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{k})
such that

\dot{\gamma}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{i}(t)X_{i}(\gamma(t)),\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,1].

(5)

The function
u
is called
control
. Furthermore, given an admissible curve
\gamma
, there exists
\bar{u}=(\bar{u}_{1},\dots,\bar{u}_{k}):[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that

\dot{\gamma}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bar{u}_{i}(t)X_{i}(\gamma(t)),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\lVert\dot{\gamma}(t)\right\rVert_{\gamma(t)}=\left\lvert\bar{u}(t)\right\rvert_{\gamma(t)},\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,1].

(6)

The function
\bar{u}
is called
minimal control
, and the map
t\mapsto\left\lvert\bar{u}(t)\right\rvert_{\gamma(t)}
belongs to
L^{\infty}([0,1])
, cf.
[
ABB20
, Lem. 3.12]
. We define the
length
of an admissible curve as

\ell(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert\dot{\gamma}(t)\right\rVert_{\gamma(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t\in[0,\infty).

(7)

Finally, for every pair of points
q_{0},q_{1}\in M
, we define the
sub-Finsler distance
between them as

\mathsf{d}(q_{0},q_{1})=\inf\left\{\ell(\gamma)\,:\,\gamma\text{ admissible, }\gamma(0)=q_{0}\text{ and }\gamma(1)=q_{1}\right\}.

(8)

Since every norm on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
is equivalent to the standard scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, it follows that the sub-Riemannian structure on
M
induces a distance which is locally equivalent to
\mathsf{d}
. Namely, denoting by
\mathsf{d}_{SR}
the induced sub-Riemannian distance, for every compact
K\subset M
there exist constants
C>c>0
(depending on
K
) such that

c\,\mathsf{d}_{SR}\leq\mathsf{d}\leq C\mathsf{d}_{SR},\qquad\text{on }K\times K.

(9)

Thus, as a consequence of the classical Filippov and Chow–Rashevskii theorems in sub-Riemannian geometry, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
M
be a sub-Finsler manifold. The sub-Finsler distance is finite, continuous on
M\times M
and the induced topology is the manifold one. In particular,
M
is locally compact.

We say that a sub-Finsler manifold
(M,\xi,{|\!\cdot\!|})
is
complete
if the metric space
(M,\mathsf{d})
is complete.

3 The nilpotent approximation of a sub-Finsler manifold

We present the notion of nilpotent approximation of a sub-Finsler manifold, see
[
Jea14
,
Bel96
]
for details. This construction is analogous to the nilpotent approximation of a sub-Riemannian manifold, taking into account the necessary adaptations to include norms on the distribution.

The flag of the distribution.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional sub-Finsler manifold with distribution
\mathcal{D}
. We define the
flag
of
\mathcal{D}
as the sequence of subsheafs
\mathcal{D}^{j}\subset TM
such that

\mathcal{D}^{1}=\mathcal{D},\qquad\mathcal{D}^{j+1}=\mathcal{D}^{j}+[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{j}],\qquad\forall\,j\geq 1,

(10)

with the convention that
\mathcal{D}^{0}=\{0\}
. Under the Hörmander condition, the flag of the distribution defines an exhaustion of
T_{q}M
, for any point
q\in M
, i.e. there exists
s(q)\in\mathbb{N}
such that:

\{0\}=\mathcal{D}^{0}_{q}\subset\mathcal{D}^{1}_{q}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{D}^{s(q)-1}_{q}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}^{s(q)}_{q}=T_{q}M.

(11)

The number
s(q)
is called
degree of nonholonomy
at
q
. We set
n_{j}(q)=\dim\mathcal{D}^{j}_{q}
, for any
j\geq 0
, then the collection of
s(q)
integers

\left(n_{1}(q),\ldots,n_{s(q)}(q)\right)

(12)

is called
growth vector
at
q
, and we have
n_{s(q)}(q)=n=\dim M
. Associated with the growth vector, we can define the
weights

w_{i}(q)
of
\mathcal{D}
at
q
: for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we set

w_{i}(q):=j,\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad n_{j-1}(q)+1\leq i\leq n_{j}(q).

(13)

A point
q\in M
is said to be
regular
if the growth vector is constant in a neighborhood of
q
, and
singular
otherwise. The sub-Finsler structure on
M
is said to be
equiregular
if all points of
M
are regular. In this case, the weights are constant

as well
on
M

as well
. Finally, given any
q\in M
, we define the
homogeneous dimension
of
M
at
q
as

\mathcal{Q}(q)=\sum_{i=1}^{s(q)}i(n_{i}(q)-n_{i-1}(q))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}(q).

(14)

We recall that, if
q
is regular, then
\mathcal{Q}(q)
coincides with the Hausdorff dimension of
(M,\mathsf{d})
at
q
, cf.
[
Mit85
]
. Moreover,
\mathcal{Q}(q)>n
, for any
q\in M
such that
\mathcal{D}_{q}\subsetneq T_{q}M
.

Privileged coordinates.

Let
M
be a sub-Finsler manifold with generating family
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,k}
and
f
be a smooth function at
q\in M
. We call
nonholonomic derivative
of order
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
of
f
at
q
the quantity

X_{j_{1}}\cdots X_{j_{\ell}}f(q),

(15)

for any family of indexes
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{\ell}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Then, the
nonholonomic order
of
f
at
q
is

\mathrm{ord}_{q}(f)=\min\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}:\exists\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{\ell}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}\text{ s.t. }X_{j_{1}}\cdots X_{j_{\ell}}f(q)\neq 0\right\}.

(16)

Definition 3.1

(Privileged coordinates)
.

Let
M
be a
n
-dimensional sub-Finsler manifold and
q\in M
. A system of local coordinates
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
centered at
q
is said to be
privileged
at
q
if

\mathrm{ord}_{q}(z_{j})=w_{j}(q),\qquad\forall\,j=1,\ldots,n.

(17)

We remark that privileged coordinates always exist, see
[
Jea14
, Sec. 2.1.2]
for the construction.

Nilpotent approximation.

Let
M
be a sub-Finsler manifold and let
q\in M
with weights as in (
13
). Consider
\widehat{\psi}^{q}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\colon U\rightarrow V
a chart of privileged coordinates centered at
q
, where
U\subset M
is a relatively compact neighborhood of
q
and
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a neighborhood of
0
. Then, for any
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, we can define the
dilation
at
q
as

\delta_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n};\qquad\delta_{\varepsilon}(z)=\left(\varepsilon^{w_{1}(q)}z_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon^{w_{n}(q)}z_{n}\right).

(18)

Using such dilations, we can obtain the nilpotent (or first-order) approximation of the generating family
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,k}
. Indeed, for any
i=1\ldots,k
we set
Y_{i}:=\widehat{\psi}^{q}_{*}X_{i}
and define the vector field

Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon\delta_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}*}(Y_{i}),\qquad\forall\,\varepsilon>0.

(19)

Observe that
Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}
is defined on the set
\delta_{1/\varepsilon}(V)
and, when
\varepsilon\to 0
, this set tends to cover the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, according to
[
ABB20
, Lem. 10.58]
, for every
i=1\ldots,k
the family
\{Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
admits a limit in the
C^{\infty}
-topology of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
In particular, for every
i=1\ldots,k
, we can define the vector field

\widehat{X}_{i}^{q}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}\in\Gamma(T\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(20)

Theorem 3.2
.

The family
\{\widehat{X}_{1}^{q},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{k}^{q}\}
of vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
generates a nilpotent Lie algebra of step
s(q)=w_{n}(q)
and satisfies the Hörmander condition.

This theorem is well-known in the sub-Riemannian setting, cf.
[
Jea14
]
. Being the result purely algebraic, it carries over to the sub-Finsler setting without modifications. Recall that a Lie algebra is said to be nilpotent of step
s
if
s
is the smallest integer such that all the brackets of length greater than
s
are zero.

According to Theorem
3.2
, we can then define the nilpotent approximation of
(M,\xi,{|\!\cdot\!|})
at
q
.

Definition 3.3

(Nilpotent approximation)
.

Let
M
be a sub-Finsler manifold and let
q\in M
. Consider the family
\{\widehat{X}_{1}^{q},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{k}^{q}\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, provided by Theorem
3.2
. The
nilpotent approximation
of
M
at the point
q
is the sub-Finsler structure
(\widehat{\xi}^{q},{|\!\cdot\!|}_{q})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where

\widehat{\xi}^{q}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n},\qquad\widehat{\xi}^{q}(z,e_{i}):=(z,\widehat{X}_{i}^{q}(z)),\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,k.

(21)

In the following, we denote by
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{q}
and by
\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q}
, the distribution and the sub-Finsler distance associated to the sub-Finsler structure
(\widehat{\xi}^{q},{|\!\cdot\!|}_{q})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively.

Remark 3.4
.

By construction, the vector fields
\widehat{X}_{i}^{q}
are homogeneous of degree
-1
with respect to
\delta_{\varepsilon}
, namely

\widehat{X}_{i}^{q}=\varepsilon(\delta_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}})_{*}\widehat{X}_{i}^{q},\qquad\forall\,\varepsilon>0,\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,k.

(22)

Moreover, by definition, the norm on
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{q}
is given by

\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{q}_{z}:=\inf\left\{|u|_{q}:\widehat{\xi}^{q}(z,u)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{i}\widehat{X}_{i}^{q}(z)=v\right\},\qquad\forall\,v\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{q}_{z},\ \forall\,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(23)

and it is
1
-homogeneous with respect to
\delta_{\varepsilon}
, meaning that
\delta_{\varepsilon}^{*}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{q}_{z}=\varepsilon\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{q}_{z}
. Thus, the distance
\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q}
of the nilpotent approximation is
1
-homogeneous with respect to
\delta_{\varepsilon}
. In addition, since
(\widehat{\xi}^{q},{|\!\cdot\!|}_{q})
induces a locally compact topology, by homogeneity,
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q})
is complete.

Theorem 3.5
.

The nilpotent approximation of
M
at a point
q
is isometric to the quotient of a sub-Finsler Carnot group
G^{q}/H^{q}
, for some normal subgroup
H^{q}\lhd G^{q}
, equipped with a left-invariant norm. If
q
is regular, then
H^{q}=\{0\}
and the nilpotent approximation is isometric to a sub-Finsler Carnot group.

The proof of the previous theorem follows the blueprint of
[
Bel96
, Thm. 5.21]
. The main difference lies of course in the norm, which is left-invariant, since the generating family is so (cf. (
23
)), and thus the metric structure of the nilpotent approximation is indeed the one of a (quotient of a) sub-Finsler Carnot group.

Ball-Box theorem.

We record here an important result that can be stated using privileged coordinates. Its proof follows the equivalence between distances (
9
) and the corresponding sub-Riemannian result, cf.
[
ABB20
, Thm. 10.67]
.

Theorem 3.6

(Ball-Box)
.

Let
M
be a complete sub-Finsler manifold, let
q\in M
and let
\widehat{\psi}^{q}:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a chart of privileged coordinates at
q
. Then, there exist constants
C_{q}\geq 1
and
r_{q}>0
such that

\text{Box}^{q}\left(\frac{1}{C_{q}}r\right)\subset\widehat{\psi}^{q}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(q,r))\subset\text{Box}^{q}(C_{q}r),\qquad\forall\,r\in(0,r_{q})

(24)

where
\text{Box}^{q}(r):=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}):|z_{i}|\leq r^{w_{i}(q)},1\leq i\leq n\}
.

Remark 3.7
.

If
M
is equiregular, the constants in the previous theorem can be chosen uniformly on compact sets. Namely, for every
K\subset M
compact, the constants
C_{q}
and
r_{q}
of Theorem
3.6
can be taken to be independent of
q\in K
, cf.
[
Jea14
, Thm. 2.3]
.

4 The metric measure tangent to a sub-Finsler manifold

In the present section, we show that the nilpotent approximation of a complete sub-Finsler manifold
M
at
q
, equipped with (a scalar multiple of) the Lebesgue measure, is its metric measure tangent. The metric side of the result is contained in
[
ALDNG23
]
. Below, we give a self-contained construction of the metric tangent.

4.1 The approximating family of sub-Finsler structures

Let
M
be a complete sub-Finsler manifold and let
q\in M
. Fix a chart of privileged coordinates centered at
q
,
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}):U\to V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. With a slight abuse of notation, we identify
M
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and work in coordinates. For every
\varepsilon>0
, we define the sub-Finsler structure
(\xi^{\varepsilon},{|\!\cdot\!|}^{\varepsilon})
on
\delta_{1/\varepsilon}(V)
as

\displaystyle\xi^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:\delta_{1/\varepsilon}(V)\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n},\qquad\xi^{\varepsilon}(z,e_{i}):=(z,Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}(z))\qquad\text{for }i=1,\dots k.

(25)

\displaystyle{|\!\cdot\!|}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:\delta_{1/\varepsilon}(V)\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\qquad\,|u|^{\varepsilon}_{z}:=|(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})|_{\delta_{\varepsilon}(z)},

(26)

where the vector fields
Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}
are defined in (
19
). For every
\varepsilon>0
, the length-minimization procedure (
8
) defines a distance on
\delta_{1/\varepsilon}(V)
, which we denote by
\mathsf{d}^{\varepsilon}
. Note that
\mathsf{d}^{1}\equiv\mathsf{d}
on
V\times V
.

A first important property of the approximating structure is a kind of homogeneity with respect to the anisotropic dilations.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
it holds that

\mathsf{d}^{\varepsilon}\big(\delta_{1/\varepsilon}(x),\delta_{1/\varepsilon}(y)\big)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathsf{d}(x,y),\qquad\text{for every }x,y\in V.

(27)

Proof.

By definition of
Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}
, cf. (
19
), we have the following:

\rho(\delta_{\frac{1}{\rho}})_{*}Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}=Y_{i}^{\rho\varepsilon},\qquad\forall\,\varepsilon,\rho>0.

(28)

In addition, by our definition of norm, for every
z\in\delta_{1/\varepsilon}(V)
and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{2k}
, we have

|u|^{\varepsilon}_{\delta_{\rho}(z)}=|(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})|_{\delta_{\rho\varepsilon}(z)}.

(29)

From (
28
) and (
29
), we immediately deduce that, for every
\varepsilon,\rho>0
:

\delta_{\rho}^{*}\mathsf{d}^{\varepsilon}=\rho\,\mathsf{d}^{\rho\varepsilon},\qquad\text{on }\delta_{\frac{1}{\rho\varepsilon}}(V)\times\delta_{\frac{1}{\rho\varepsilon}}(V).

(30)

The claim follows choosing
\rho=1/\varepsilon
.
∎

The next result is crucial in characterizing the metric tangent of a sub-Finsler manifold. We refer to
[
ALDNG23
, Thm. 1.4(iv)]
for details. (See also
[
ALDNG23
, Thm. C.2]
for a simplified proof in the case of smooth structures.)

Theorem 4.2
.

The family of distances
\{\mathsf{d}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
converges uniformly on compact sets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the distance
\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

We check the hypothesis of
[
ALDNG23
, Thm. 1.4(iv)]
. Firstly, the vector field
Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}
converges to
\widehat{X}_{i}^{q}
locally uniformly on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for every
i
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, cf. (
20
). Secondly, the norm
{|\!\cdot\!|}^{\varepsilon}
converges to
{|\!\cdot\!|}_{q}
locally uniformly as
\varepsilon\to 0
(as functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
). Thirdly, the limit sub-Finsler structure
(\widehat{\xi}^{q},{|\!\cdot\!|}_{q})
defines a complete distance on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

4.2 Bounded measures on a sub-Finsler manifold

In this section, we describe a class of measures whose blow-up is the Lebesgue measure almost-everywhere, up to a constant.

Definition 4.3

(Bounded measures)
.

Let
M
be a
n
-dimensional sub-Finsler manifold, equipped with a Borel measure
\mathfrak{m}
. We say that
\mathfrak{m}
is a
bounded measure
if, for every coordinate chart
\psi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\psi_{\#}\mathfrak{m}\ll\mathscr{L}^{n}
and

\psi_{\#}\mathfrak{m}=\rho\mathscr{L}^{n},\qquad\text{with}\qquad c\leq\rho\leq C\quad\mathscr{L}^{n}\text{-a.e.},

(31)

for some positive constants
C\geq c>0
, depending on the chart.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
M
be a complete
n
-dimensional sub-Finsler manifold, equipped with a bounded measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Then, for every
q\in M
, there exists
C_{q}\geq 1
and
r_{q}>0
such that

C_{q}^{-1}r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}\leq\mathfrak{m}(B(q,r))\leq C_{q}r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)},\qquad\forall\,q\in M,\ r\in(0,r_{q}),

(32)

where
\mathcal{Q}(q)
is the homogeneous dimension of
M
at
q
.

Proof.

Let
q\in M
and let
\widehat{\psi}^{q}:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of privileged coordinates centered at
q
and consider
\widehat{\psi}^{q}_{\#}\mathfrak{m}
. On the one hand, by assumptions, there exists a measurable function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying (
31
). Then, we easily see that, for
r>0
sufficiently small,

c\,(\widehat{\psi}^{q})_{\#}^{-1}\mathscr{L}^{n}(B(q,r))\leq\mathfrak{m}(B(q,r))\leq C\,(\widehat{\psi}^{q})_{\#}^{-1}\mathscr{L}^{n}(B(q,r)).

(33)

On the other hand, from the Ball-Box theorem, cf. Theorem
3.6
, we deduce that there exist constants
\tilde{C}_{q}
and
r_{q}\in(0,\diam U)
, such that

\tilde{C}_{q}^{-1}r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}\leq(\widehat{\psi}^{q})^{-1}_{\#}\mathscr{L}^{n}(B(q,r))\leq\tilde{C}_{q}r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)},\qquad\forall\,r\leq r_{q}.

(34)

The combination of the previous inequality and (
33
) concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.5

(Lebesgue points for
\mathfrak{m}
)
.

Let
M
be a sub-Finsler manifold, equipped with a bounded measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Then, we say that
q\in M
is a
Lebesgue point for

\mathfrak{m}
if there exists a coordinate chart
\psi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, defined on a neighborhood
U
of
q
, such that

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}}\int_{\psi(B(q,r))}|\rho-\rho(\psi(q))|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}=0,

(35)

where
\psi_{\#}\mathfrak{m}=\rho\mathscr{L}^{n}
and
\mathcal{Q}(q)
is the homogeneous dimension of
M
at
q
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
M
be a complete sub-Finsler manifold, equipped with a bounded measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Then, the definition of Lebesgue point for
\mathfrak{m}
is independent on the choice of
\psi
and
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. point of
M
is a Lebesgue point for
\mathfrak{m}
, with strictly positive density.

Proof.

Let
\psi:U\subset M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a coordinate chart, with
U
relatively compact. First of all, we show that
\mathscr{L}^{n}
satisfies the asymptotic doubling property on
(\psi(U),(\psi^{-1})^{*}\mathsf{d})
, namely

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathscr{L}^{n}(\psi(B(q,2r)))}{\mathscr{L}^{n}(\psi(B(q,r)))}<\infty,

(36)

for all
q\in U
. Indeed, by the Ball-Box theorem, cf. Theorem
3.6
, there exist constants
C_{q}\geq 1
and
r_{q}>0
such that

\frac{1}{C_{q}}r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}\leq\mathscr{L}^{n}(\widehat{\psi}^{q}(B(q,r)))\leq C_{q}r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)},\qquad\forall r<r_{q},

(37)

where
\widehat{\psi}^{q}:V_{q}\subset U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a chart of privileged coordinates at
q
. Thus, for every
r<r_{q}
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{n}(\psi(B(q,2r)))

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{\psi}^{q}(B(q,2r))}|\det d(\psi\circ(\widehat{\psi}^{q})^{-1})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}

(38)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\widehat{\psi}^{q}(V_{q})}|\det d(\psi\circ(\widehat{\psi}^{q})^{-1})|\,\mathscr{L}^{n}(\widehat{\psi}^{q}(B(q,2r)))

(39)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\widehat{\psi}^{q}(V_{q})}|\det d(\psi\circ(\widehat{\psi}^{q})^{-1})|\,C_{q}(2r)^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}

(40)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\widehat{\psi}^{q}(V_{q})}|\det d(\psi\circ(\widehat{\psi}^{q})^{-1})|\,C_{q}^{2}2^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}\mathscr{L}^{n}(\widehat{\psi}^{q}(B(q,r)))

(41)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\widehat{\psi}^{q}(V_{q})}|\det d(\psi\circ(\widehat{\psi}^{q})^{-1})|\,\sup_{\psi(U)}|\det d(\widehat{\psi}^{q}\circ\psi^{-1})|\,C_{q}^{2}2^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}\mathscr{L}^{n}(\psi(B(q,r))).

(42)

This shows that condition (
36
) is fulfilled.
Therefore, Lebesgue differentiation theorem holds, cf.
[
HKST15
, Sec. 3.4]
, and we deduce that, for
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in\psi(U)
, we have

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{\mathscr{L}^{n}(\psi(B(p,r)))}\int_{\psi(B(p,r))}|\rho-\rho(x)|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{r^{\mathcal{Q}(p)}}\int_{\psi(B(p,r))}|\rho-\rho(x)|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}=0,

(43)

having set
p:=\psi^{-1}(x)
. From this, we immediately see that, if the limit (
35
) is independent on the coordinate chart,
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. point of
M
is a Lebesgue point for
\mathfrak{m}
.

Second of all, let
\varphi:V\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be another coordinate chart containing
q
. Then,
\varphi_{\#}\mathfrak{m}=\eta\mathscr{L}^{n}
and we need to check that (
35
) implies that

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}}\int_{\varphi(B(q,r))}|\eta-\eta(\varphi(q))|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}=0.

(44)

Consider the smooth change of coordinates given by
\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and observe that, by definition, we have

(\psi\circ\varphi^{-1})_{\#}(\eta\mathscr{L}^{n})=\rho\mathscr{L}^{n},

(45)

which implies that
\eta=\rho\circ\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}
,
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e.. Hence, using the change of variables
x\mapsto\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}(x)
in (
44
), we obtain:

\frac{1}{r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}}\int_{\varphi(B(q,r))}|\eta-\rho(\psi(q))|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}=\frac{1}{r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}}\int_{\psi(B(q,r))}|\rho-\rho(\psi(q))|\cdot|\det d(\varphi\circ\psi^{-1})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}.

(46)

Since
\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}
is smooth, we easily see that the right-hand side converges to
0
, as a consequence of (
35
). Recalling that
\eta=\rho\circ\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}
,
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e., we conclude the proof of the claim, possibly choosing a different representative of
\eta
.
∎

When
M
is an equiregular sub-Finsler manifold, Lemma
4.4
can be improved to a complete characterization of sub-Finsler bounded measures, in terms of the notion of
\mathcal{Q}
-Ahlfors regularity.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and let
\mathcal{Q}>1
. We say that
\mathfrak{m}
is
locally
\mathcal{Q}
-Ahlfors regular
if, for every compact
K\subset\mathsf{X}
, there exists
C\geq 1
and
r_{0}\in(0,\diam\mathsf{X}]
, depending on
K
, such that

C^{-1}r^{\mathcal{Q}}\leq\mathfrak{m}(B(p,r))\leq Cr^{\mathcal{Q}},\qquad\forall\,p\in K,\ r\in(0,r_{0}).

(47)

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
M
be a complete equiregular sub-Finsler manifold of homogeneous dimension
\mathcal{Q}
. Then, a measure
\mathfrak{m}
on
M
is bounded if and only if it is locally
\mathcal{Q}
-Ahlfors regular.

Proof.

The only if part of the statement is the content of Lemma
4.4
, taking into account that all the constants can be chosen locally uniformly since
M
is equiregular. To prove the converse statement, let
\psi:U\subset M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a chart and define
\mu:=\psi_{\#}\mathfrak{m}
and
\nu:={\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathscr{L}^{n}\right|_{\psi(U)}}
. On the one hand, from the proof of Lemma
4.6
, we deduce that
\nu
is asymptotically doubling and thus
(\psi(U),(\psi^{-1})^{*}\mathsf{d},\nu)
satisfies Vitali’s covering lemma, cf.
[
HKST15
, Thm. 3.4.3]
. On the other hand, from the upper bound of the uniform Ball-Box theorem, cf. Remark
3.7
, we obtain the estimate:

\overline{D}_{\nu}\mu(p):=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(\psi(B(p,r)))}{\nu(\psi(B(p,r)))}\leq C,\qquad\forall\,p\in U,

(48)

for some positive constant
C
depending only on
U
. From (
48
), a classical application of Vitali covering’s theorem ensures that for every open set
A\subset\psi(U)
,
\mu(A)\leq C\nu(A)
. Finally, the monotone class theorem allows to deduce that

\mu(E)\leq C\nu(E),\qquad\forall\,E\subset\psi(U)\ \text{Borel}.

(49)

This implies that
\mu\ll\nu
and the density is bounded by
C
. To prove that
\nu\ll\mu
with a bounded density we repeat the same argument, observing that also
\mu
is asympotically doubling, thanks to the
\mathcal{Q}
-Ahlfors regularity, and using the lower bound of the uniform Ball-Box theorem.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

On an equiregular sub-Finsler manifold, the
\mathcal{Q}
-Hausdorff measure is locally
\mathcal{Q}
-Ahlfors regular, as a consequence of the (uniform) Ball-Box theorem. Therefore, it is bounded in the sense of Definition
4.3
.

4.3 Convergence to the metric measure tangent

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. The metric side is the content of
[
ALDNG23
, Thm. 1.5]
. We include a proof for completeness. The blow-up of the measure has been studied in
[
GJ15
,
GJ16
]
, only for smooth measures (in the sub-Riemannian case).

Recall that a pointed metric measure space is a metric measure space with a distinguished point. For such class of spaces, a natural notion of convergence is the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence, see
[
GMS15
]
.

Definition 4.10

(pmGH-convergence)
.

Let
(\mathsf{X}_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n},\mathfrak{m}_{n},x_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
be a collection of pointed metric measure spaces. Then, we say that
(\mathsf{X}_{n},\mathsf{d}_{n},\mathfrak{m}_{n},x_{n})
converges to
(\mathsf{X}_{\infty},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathfrak{m}_{\infty},x_{\infty})
in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff sense (pmGH for short) if for any
\varepsilon,R>0
there exists
N(\varepsilon,R)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>N(\varepsilon,R)
there exists a Borel map
f^{\varepsilon,R}_{n}:B^{\mathsf{d}_{n}}(x_{n},R)\to\mathsf{X}_{\infty}
such that

(i)

f^{\varepsilon,R}_{n}(x_{n})=x_{\infty}
,

(ii)

\sup_{x,y\in B^{\mathsf{d}_{n}}(x_{n},R)}|\mathsf{d}_{n}(x,y)-\mathsf{d}_{\infty}(f^{\varepsilon,R}_{n}(x),f^{\varepsilon,R}_{n}(y))|\leq\varepsilon
,

(iii)

the
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood of
f^{\varepsilon,R}_{n}(B^{\mathsf{d}_{n}}(x_{n},R))
contains
B^{\mathsf{d}_{\infty}}(x_{\infty},R-\varepsilon)
,

(iv)

(f^{\varepsilon,R}_{n})_{\#}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathfrak{m}_{n}\right|_{B^{\mathsf{d}_{n}}(x_{n},R)}})
weakly converges to
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathfrak{m}\right|_{B^{\mathsf{d}_{\infty}}(x_{\infty},R)}}
as
n\to\infty
, for a.e.
R>0
.

The notion of metric measured tangents for a general metric measure space can be given according to the notion of pmGH-convergence. Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space, let
x\in\supp(\mathfrak{m})
and
r\in(0,1)
. Define

\mathfrak{m}_{r}^{x}:=\left(\int_{B(x,r)}1-\frac{1}{r}\mathsf{d}(\cdot,x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\right)^{-1}\mathfrak{m}.

(50)

Definition 4.11

(The collection of tangent spaces
{\rm Tan}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},x)
)
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space, let
x\in\supp(\mathfrak{m})
and
r\in(0,1)
. and consider the rescaled and normalized pointed metric measure space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d}_{r},\mathfrak{m}^{x}_{r},x)
, where
\mathsf{d}_{r}:=r^{-1}\mathsf{d}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{r}^{x}
is defined in (
50
). A pointed metric measure space
({\sf Y},\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mathfrak{n},y)
is called a tangent to
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
at
x
if there exists a sequence of radii
r_{i}\to 0^{+}
so that

(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d}_{r_{i}},\mathfrak{m}^{x}_{r_{i}},x)\to({\sf Y},\mathsf{d}_{Y},\mathfrak{n},y)
as
i\to\infty
in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology. We denote the collection of all the tangents of
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
at
x
by
{\rm Tan}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},x)
.

We are now in position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional complete sub-Finsler manifold, equipped with a bounded measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Then, for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
q\in M
, we have

{\rm Tan}(M,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},q)=\{(\widehat{M}^{q},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q},m(q)\mathscr{L}^{n},0)\},

(51)

where
(\widehat{M}^{q},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q})
is the nilpotent approximation of
M
at
q
and is isometric to a quotient sub-Finsler Carnot group and
m(q)>0
is defined by

m(q):=\left(\int_{\hat{B}^{q}(0,1)}(1-\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q})\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}\right)^{-1}.

(52)

Proof.

Let
q\in M
be a Lebesgue point for
\mathfrak{m}
and let
\widehat{\psi}^{q}:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a chart of privileged coordinates at
q
, with
U
open and relatively compact. Fix
\varepsilon,R>0
and choose
r_{0}=r_{0}(\varepsilon,R)
sufficiently small to ensure that
B^{\mathsf{d}_{r}}(q,R)=B^{\mathsf{d}}(q,rR)\subset U
for every
r<r_{0}
. In this way, we can work in coordinates and, for ease of notation, we can identify
M
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
q
with the origin (we also drop the superscript
q
of the nilpotent approximation). Then, define

f^{\varepsilon,R}_{r}:B^{\mathsf{d}_{r}}(0,R)\to\mathbb{R}^{n};\qquad f^{\varepsilon,R}_{r}(p):=\delta_{r}^{-1}(p),

(53)

where
\delta_{r}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the dilation defined in (
18
). Note that
f^{\varepsilon,R}_{r}(0)=0
and therefore item (i) of Definition
4.10
is verified. Possibly taking a smaller
r_{0}
, we may assume that
B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,r_{0}R)\subset V
. As a consequence of Lemma
4.1
, we have

\delta_{1/r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR))=B^{\mathsf{d}^{r}}(0,R),\qquad\forall\,r\leq r_{0},

(54)

From Theorem
4.2
, we immediately deduce that
\delta_{1/r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR))\to B^{\widehat{\mathsf{d}}}(0,R)
as
r\to 0
in the Hausdorff topology, proving that also item (iii) of Definition
4.10
is verified. Combining this convergence with Lemma
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{p,p^{\prime}\in B^{\mathsf{d}_{r}}(0,R)}\Big|\mathsf{d}_{r}(p,p^{\prime})-\widehat{\mathsf{d}}(f^{\varepsilon,R}_{r}(p),f^{\varepsilon,R}_{r}(p^{\prime}))\Big|

\displaystyle=\sup_{p,p^{\prime}\in B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR)}\Big|\frac{1}{r}\mathsf{d}(p,p^{\prime})-\widehat{\mathsf{d}}\big(\delta_{\frac{1}{r}}(p),\delta_{\frac{1}{r}}(p^{\prime})\big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\sup_{p,p^{\prime}\in B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR)}\Big|\mathsf{d}^{r}\big(\delta_{\frac{1}{r}}(p),\delta_{\frac{1}{r}}(p^{\prime})\big)-\widehat{\mathsf{d}}\big(\delta_{\frac{1}{r}}(p),\delta_{\frac{1}{r}}(p^{\prime})\big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in\delta_{1/r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR))}\big|\mathsf{d}^{r}(x,x^{\prime})-\widehat{\mathsf{d}}(x,x^{\prime})\big|\xrightarrow{r\to 0}0.

Hence, we have proved that, up to possibly choosing a smaller
r_{0}
, item (ii) of Definition
4.10
is verified. Therefore we are left to check item (iv).

First of all,
\mathfrak{m}
is a bounded measure, hence
\widehat{\psi}^{q}_{\#}\mathfrak{m}=\rho\mathscr{L}^{n}
for a measurable function
\rho
with
0<c\leq\rho\leq C
. Thus, since
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Lebesgue point for
\mathfrak{m}
, after the change of variables
x\mapsto\delta^{-1}_{r}(x)
, we have

0=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}}\int_{B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,r)}|\rho-\rho(0)|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}=\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{\delta^{-1}_{r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,r))}|\rho\circ\delta_{r}-\rho(0)|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n},

(55)

where
\rho(0)
is strictly positive and finite. Second of all, for every
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we claim that

\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{\delta^{-1}_{r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR))}\varphi(\rho\circ\delta_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}=\rho(0)\int_{B^{\widehat{\mathsf{d}}}(0,R)}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}.

(56)

Indeed, recall that
\delta^{-1}_{r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR))\to B^{\widehat{\mathsf{d}}}(0,R)
as
r\to 0
, as sets and we can compute:

\left|\int_{\delta^{-1}_{r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR))}\varphi(\rho\circ\delta_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}-\rho(0)\int_{B^{\widehat{\mathsf{d}}}(0,R)}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}\right|\\
\leq\int_{\delta^{-1}_{r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR))}\left|\varphi(\rho\circ\delta_{r})-\rho(0)\varphi\right|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}+\rho(0)\left|\int_{\delta^{-1}_{r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR))}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}-\int_{B^{\widehat{\mathsf{d}}}(0,R)}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}\right|,

(57)

which converges to
0
as a consequence of (
54
) and (
55
).
We are in position to prove the weak convergence of the sequence
(f^{\varepsilon,R}_{r})_{\#}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathfrak{m}_{r}^{0}\right|_{B^{\mathsf{d}_{r}}(0,R)}}
to the tangent measure
{\left.\kern-1.2ptm(q)\mathscr{L}^{n}\right|_{B^{\widehat{\mathsf{d}}}(0,R)}}
.
Let
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then by definition of
\mathfrak{m}_{r}^{0}
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(f^{\varepsilon,R}_{r})_{\#}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathfrak{m}_{r}^{0}\right|_{B^{\mathsf{d}_{r}}(0,R)}})&=\left(\int_{B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,r)}1-\frac{1}{r}\mathsf{d}(\cdot,0)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\right)^{-1}\int_{B^{\mathsf{d}_{r}}(0,R)}(\varphi\circ\delta_{r}^{-1})\rho\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}\\
&=\left(\int_{B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,r)}1-\mathsf{d}_{r}(\cdot,0)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\right)^{-1}r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}\int_{\delta_{r}^{-1}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,rR))}\varphi(\rho\circ\delta_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n},\end{split}

(58)

having performed a change of variable
x\mapsto\delta_{r}(x)
. For the renormalization term, using a change of variable and (
56
), we have

\begin{split}\int_{B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,r)}1-\mathsf{d}_{r}(\cdot,0)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}&=r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}\int_{\delta^{-1}_{r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,r))}(1-\mathsf{d}_{r}(\delta_{r}(\cdot),0))\rho\circ\delta_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}\\
&=r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)}\int_{\delta^{-1}_{r}(B^{\mathsf{d}}(0,r))}(1-\mathsf{d}^{r}(\cdot,0))\rho\circ\delta_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r\to 0}}{{\sim}}m(q)^{-1}\,\rho(0)r^{\mathcal{Q}(q)},\end{split}

(59)

where
m(q)>0
is defined in (
52
). Hence, combining the convergence (
59
) with (
56
), we may deduce that

\begin{split}\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(f^{\varepsilon,R}_{r})_{\#}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathfrak{m}_{r}^{0}\right|_{B^{\mathsf{d}_{r}}(0,R)}})&=m(q)\int_{B^{\widehat{\mathsf{d}}}(0,R)}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}=m(q)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathscr{L}^{n}\right|_{B^{\widehat{\mathsf{d}}}(0,R)}}),\end{split}

(60)

concluding the proof of item (iv).
∎

In light of Remark
4.9
, we obtain a straightforward corollary of the previous result.

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
M
be a
n
-dimensional, complete and equiregular sub-Finsler manifold and let
\mathcal{Q}
be its homogeneous dimension. Then, for
\mathscr{H}^{\mathcal{Q}}
-a.e.
q\in M
, we have

{\rm Tan}(M,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}^{\mathcal{Q}},q)=\{(\widehat{M}^{q},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q},m(q)\mathscr{L}^{n},0)\},

(61)

where
(\widehat{M}^{q},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q})
is the nilpotent approximation of
M
at
q
and is isometric to a sub-Finsler Carnot group and
m(q)>0
is defined by (
52
).

Remark 4.14
.

For a sub-Finsler Carnot group of topological dimension
n
and homogeneous dimension
\mathcal{Q}
, the Lebesgue measure
\mathscr{L}^{n}
and the Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}^{\mathcal{Q}}
are bi-invariant Haar measures, thus they coincide up to a scalar multiple. Therefore, Corollary
4.13
says that

{\rm Tan}(M,\mathsf{d},\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{d}}^{\mathcal{Q}},q)=\{(\widehat{M}^{q},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q},c\mathscr{H}_{\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q}}^{\mathcal{Q}},0)\},\qquad\text{for }\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{d}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\text{-a.e.\ }q\in M.

(62)

Remark 4.15
.

Given a sub-Finsler manifold
M
and a regular point
p\in M
, the previous result holds in a neighborhood
U
of
p
(where
\mathcal{Q}
is replaced by the Hausdorff dimension of
U
).

5 Applications to the curvature-dimension condition

Recall that the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
is the only Carnot group of topological dimension
3
and Hausdorff dimension
4
. A sub-Finsler Heisenberg group is the Heisenberg group, equipped with a norm
{|\!\cdot\!|}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
on its first layer. We refer the reader to
[
BMRT24b
, Sec. 3]
for a precise definition. The next results deal with 3D-contact sub-Finsler manifolds, that are equiregular sub-Finsler manifolds with growth vector
(2,3)
. See
[
ABB20
]
for details. We stress that we do not make any assumptions on the norm.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(M,\xi,{|\!\cdot\!|})
be a complete 3D-contact sub-Finsler manifold, equipped with a locally
4
-Ahlfors regular measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Then, for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
q\in M
, we have

{\rm Tan}(M,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},q)=\{(\mathbb{H},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q},m(q)\mathscr{L}^{3},0)\},

(63)

where
\mathbb{H}
is the Heisenberg group,
\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q}
is the sub-Finsler distance on
\mathbb{H}
induced by
{|\!\cdot\!|}_{q}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
m(q)>0
is defined by (
52
).

Proof.

Applying Theorem
4.12
, we deduce that, at
\mathfrak{m}
-almost every point
q\in M
, the nilpotent approximation
(\widehat{M}^{q},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q})
of
M
, equipped with the measure
m(q)\mathscr{L}^{3}
and with distinguished point
0
, is the unique (pointed) metric measure tangent at
q
. Since
q
is a regular point, Theorem
3.5
implies that
(\widehat{M}^{q},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q})
is a Carnot group of (topological) dimension
3
and thus it must be the sub-Finsler Heisenberg group, where
\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q}
is defined in Definition
3.3
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(M,\xi,{|\!\cdot\!|})
be a complete 3D-contact sub-Finsler manifold, equipped with a locally
4
-Ahlfors regular measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Then,
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
does not satisfy the
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition for every
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction, we assume that
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies the
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,\infty)
.
The scaling property of the
\mathsf{CD}
condition (see
[
Stu06b
, Prop. 1.4]
) guarantees that the metric measure space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d}_{r},\mathfrak{m}^{x}_{r})
satisfies the
\mathsf{CD}(r^{2}K,N)
condition. Moreover, as the
\mathsf{CD}
condition is stable with respect to the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence (see
[
GMS15
]
), Proposition
5.1
allows us to conclude that

(\mathbb{H},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q},m(q)\mathscr{L}^{3},0)\quad\text{is a }\mathsf{CD}(0,N)\text{ space.}

(64)

Finally, Theorem 1.6 in
[
BMRT24b
]
gives the desired contradiction.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

In certain situations, the strategy used in the previous proof can be refined to show the failure of the measure contraction property
\mathsf{MCP}(K,N)
(cf.
[
Oht07
]
for a definition). Indeed, under the same assumptions of Proposition
5.2
, if there exists a positive
\mathfrak{m}
-measure set where
{|\!\cdot\!|}_{q}
is neither
C^{1}
nor strongly convex, then Theorem 1.1 of
[
BMRT24b
]
implies that
\mathsf{MCP}(K,N)
fails for every
K\in\mathbb{R}
and every
N\in(1,\infty)
.

The following corollary is a specification of Proposition
5.2
in the case of the Heisenberg group. We believe it is worth emphasize it, in light of
[
MR23b
, Conj. 1.3]
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be a sub-Finsler Heisenberg group, equipped with a locally
4
-Ahlfors regular measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Then,
(\mathbb{H},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
does not satisfy the
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition for every
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,\infty)
.

Finally, we extend the known sub-Riemannian results to bounded measures.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
M
be a complete sub-Riemannian manifold with
\dim M=n
, equipped with a bounded measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Assume there exists an
\mathfrak{m}
-positive set
\mathcal{S}\subset M
such that
r(q)<n
, for every
q\in\mathcal{S}
. Then,
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
does not satisfy the
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition for every
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
(M,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies the
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,\infty)
. Since
\mathcal{S}
has positive measure, we can find
q\in\mathcal{S}
which is a Lebesgue point for
\mathfrak{m}
, cf. Lemma
4.6
. By Theorem
3.2
, the unique (pointed) metric measure tangent of
M
at
q
is a sub-Riemannian manifold
(\widehat{M}^{q},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q})
, equipped with the measure
m(q)\mathscr{L}^{n}
and with distinguished point
0
.
Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
5.2
, we conclude that
(\widehat{M}^{q},\widehat{\mathsf{d}}^{q},m(q)\mathscr{L}^{n})
is a
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
space. This is in contradiction with
[
RS23
, Thm. 1.2]
, since
r(0)<n
.
∎

Some characterizations of multiple selfdecomposability, with extensions and an application to the Gamma function
Wissem Jedidi, Zbigniew J. Jurek, Jumanah Al Romian ⋆

Abstract
Inspirations for this paper can be traced to Urbanik urb72b ; urbanik1 where convolution semigroups of multiple decomposable distributions were introduced. In particular, the classical Gamma 𝔾 t \mathbb{G}_{t} and log ⁡ 𝔾 t \log\mathbb{G}_{t} , t > 0 t>0 , variables are selfdecomposable ( \big( i.e. have distributions in OPEN L 0 ​ ( ℝ ) ) \mathrm{L}_{0}(\mathds{R})\big) . In fact, we show that log ⁡ 𝔾 t \log\mathbb{G}_{t} is twice selfdecomposable ( \big( i.e. have distributions in OPEN L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) ) \mathrm{L}_{1}(\mathds{R})\big) if, and only if, t ≥ t 1 t\geq t_{1} where t 1 t_{1} is an explicit critical value, and this an answer to a problem raised by Akita & Maejima makoto . Moreover, we provide several new factorizations of the Gamma function and the Gamma distributions that extend many known ones in the literature. To this end, we revisit the class of multiple selfdecomposable distributions, denoted L n ​ ( ℝ ) \mathrm{L}_{n}(\mathds{R}) , and propose handy tools for its characterization, mainly based on the Mellin-Euler operator and on the Hadamard fractional integral. Finally, we give a perspective for the generalization of the class L n ​ ( ℝ ) \mathrm{L}_{n}(\mathds{R}) , based on linear operators or on stochastic integral representations.

To provide a simple characterization of multiple selfdecomposability, we give some account on Euler-Mellin differential operator

We recall that the

(or multiplicative convolution) of two measures
\mu
and
\nu

\mu\odot\nu(A)=\int_{(0,{\infty})^{2}}{\rm 1\penalty\ \hskip-6.02773ptl}_{A}(xy)\mu(dx)\nu(dy),\qquad\mbox{if}\;A\;\mbox{is a Borel set of}\;(0,{\infty})\,.

If
\mu

f

\mu\odot\nu
is the function given by

\mu\odot\nu(x)=f\odot\nu(x)=\int_{(0,{\infty})}f\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\frac{\nu(dy)}{y},\quad x>0.

Notice that the integrals above may be infinite when
\mu
(and/or
\nu
) is not a finite measure.

The
Euler-Mellin
differential
\Theta
and its discrete version
\theta_{c}

\Theta(g)(x)=xg^{\prime}(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\theta_{c}(g)(x)=g(x)-g(x/c),\qquad x,\;c>0.

(40)

will be needed in the sequel, for an account on
\Theta

Lemma 3.1

The following is true for every function
g:(0,\infty)\longrightarrow\mathds{R}
and

1)

If
g
is decreasing and
g(0+)<\infty
, then the Frullani integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{g(x)-g(x/c)}{x}\,dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta_{c}(g)(x)}{x}\,dx,

equals to
\big(g(\infty)-g(0+)\big)\log c.

Assume further that

(i)

the function

is increasing (resp. decreasing), for every fixed

\mu\;\big(\mbox{take $\mu(du)=u^{n}\;\nu(du)$ in \eqref{tmo} }\big)

We will now illustrate to which extent the class of
n
-monotone functions is intimately related to Euler-Mellin’s operator. The iterates of the usual differential operator and of

are linked by these relations: if

(43)

where

and
S(n,m)
,

denote the Stirling numbers of the first and second kind, respectively given by the positive numbers

s(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(\log(x+1))^{k}_{|x=0}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;S(n,m)=\frac{1}{m!}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(e^{x}-1)^{m}_{|x=0},

cf.
Butzer
. Notice that

is also defined as the number of partitions of the set
\{1,\cdots,n\}
into exactly
k
nonempty subsets. It is also known that

, where
[_{m}^{n}]
is the number of permutations in the symmetric group of order
n
with exactly
k
cycles. Using (
43
), write

x^{n}\,(-1)^{n}\,g^{(n)}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}[_{m}^{n}]\,(-1)^{m}\Theta^{m}(g)(x)

and it is immediate that

(-1)^{m}\Theta^{m}(g)(x)\geq 0,\quad\forall m=0,1,\cdots,n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(-1)^{n}g^{(n)}(x)\geq 0\,.

(44)

Now, assuming that
g(0)=0
and
g
is
n
times differentiable on
(0,{\infty})
, consider

. If the functions
h_{m}:=(-1)^{m-1}\Theta(h_{m-1})=(-1)^{m}\Theta^{m}(g)
are such that
h_{m}(\infty)=0
and

is integrable at
\infty

h_{n-1}(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n}(u)}{u}du=\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{n}\left(\frac{x}{u}\right){\rm 1\penalty\ \hskip-6.02773ptl}_{(0,1]}(u)\,\frac{du}{u}=({\rm 1\penalty\ \hskip-6.02773ptl}_{(0,1]}\odot h_{n})(x),\quad x>0,

then, iterating, we get following inversion formulae

g=({\rm 1\penalty\ \hskip-6.02773ptl}_{(0,1]})^{\odot{n-k}}h_{k}=({\rm 1\penalty\ \hskip-6.02773ptl}_{(0,1]})^{\odot{n}}\odot h_{n}.

Observing that

\left({\rm 1\penalty\ \hskip-6.02773ptl}_{(0,1]}\right)^{\odot n}(x)=\frac{|\log|^{n}(x)}{n!}{\rm 1\penalty\ \hskip-6.02773ptl}_{(0,1]}(x),

(45)

one retrieves that

Of course, this discussion is informal, but it illustrates the fact that the transform

Further, observing that the difference operators

Some background on infinitely divisible and selfdecomposable distributions

An equivalent definition for the infinite divisibility of a probability measure
\mu
is, that there exists a real row-wise independent random variables
\xi_{n,k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,k_{n}
,
\;n\geq 1
,
\;k_{n}\nearrow\infty
, satisfying the
infinitesimally condition

\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{1\leq k\leq k_{n}}P(|\xi_{n,k}|>\epsilon)\to 0,\quad\mbox{for each $\epsilon>0$,}

and such that we have the limit in distribution

\xi_{n,1}+\xi_{n,2}+\ldots+\xi_{n,k_{n}}\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}\mu.

(115)

Conversely, any infinitely divisible distribution can be obtained in the scheme (
115
); see Gnedenko & Kolmogorov
GK
or Loève
love
or Parthasarathy
Par1967
, for multi-dimensional spaces.
Similarly, the distribution
\mu
is selfdecomposable, if there exists two sequences
a_{n}>0,\;b_{n}\in\mathds{R}
and an infinitesimal triangular array of independent real random variables of the form
\xi_{n,k}:=a_{n}\;Z_{k}
, such that

a_{n}\;(Z_{1}+Z_{2}+\ldots+Z_{n})+b_{n}\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}\mu.

(116)

The terminology of selfdecomposability is due to the fact that the limiting distribution
\mu
corresponds to an infinitely divisible r.v.
X
that satisfies (
13
). The most general case of limits (
116
), where the
Z_{k}
’s are Banach space-valued random variables and the
a_{k}
’s are chosen from a group of bounded linear operators on a Banach space in question, was studied in Jurek

JU1
. In
(
JU1
, Theorem 3.1 and Section 4)
, taking reals as a Banach space
\mathds{B}
, the multiplication by positive scalars as linear operators and all probability measures as a set
Q
, it is shown that

\mu\in\mathrm{L}_{m}(\mathds{B}),\;\;\mbox{if, and only if, \eqref{zn} holds and}\;\;Z_{k}\sim\mathrm{L}_{m-1}(\mathds{B}),\;k\geq 1,\;m\geq 1.

Conversely, each selfdecomposable distribution can be obtained via the limiting scheme (
116
).
It might be worth to remembers that all selfdecomposable ate absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, cf.
(
JV1983
, Section 3.8, p.162)
. Additionally, if we assume that the r.v.’s

\mbox{(normal distributions)}\;\subsetneq\mathrm{S}(\mathds{R})\subsetneq\mathrm{L}_{0}(\mathds{R})\subsetneq\mathrm{I\,D}(\mathds{R}).

The class

X\sim\mathrm{L}_{m}(\mathds{R}),\;\ m\geq 0

X\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}dY_{r(t)},\quad r(t)=\frac{t^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}.

Oriented Disingquandles and Invariants of Oriented Dichromatic Singular links
Mohd Ibrahim Sheikh, Mohamed Elhamdadi, DANISH ALI

Abstract
. We introduce and investigate oriented dichromatic singular links . We also introduce oriented disingquandles and use them to define counting invariants for oriented dichromatic singular links . We provide some examples to show that these invariants distinguish some oriented dichromatic singular links.

1.
Introduction

A simple closed curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is called a mathematical knot and disjoint union of
n
knots is called an
n
-link or a link with
n
components. Knots and links are represented by knot and link diagrams in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in which we have clear information about the regions where the curve goes over or under itself. Such regions are called crossing points of the knot or link and this is referred to as classical knot theory. This theory has been generalized to some other theories such as virtual and singular knot theories. In
[
SEA
]
singular links have been generalized to dichromatic singular links in which the components of a singular link are labelled by
``1"
or
``2"
. In
[
SEA
]
a notion of unoriented dichromatic singular links was introduced and used singquandles to construct coloring invariants for such dichromatic singular links in order to classify them. In this paper we introduce an oriented version of such links and study their counting invariants. More specifically we introduce the notion of
oriented dichromatic singular links
.

Quandles, singquandles and oriented singquandles are the algebraic structures whose axioms are motivated by the classical Reidemeister moves, generalized singular Reidemeister moves and oriented generalized singular Reidemeister moves respectively
[
CCEH1
,
CCEH
,
EN
,
J
,
M
,
NOS
,
Oyamaguchi
]
. Quandles and involutive quandles have been used to distinguish oriented (respectively unoriented) knots and links.
Involutive quandles when generalized with the purpose of studying unoriented singular links, give rise to the algebraic structure called singquandles
[
CEHN
]
. Oriented singquandles were introduced in order to study and distinguish oriented singular knots and links
[
BEHY
]
.
Considering two or more quandles, one obtains a new algebraic structure, called
\mathbb{G}
-
Family of quandles
whose axioms are motivated by handlebody-knot theory
[
IMJO
]
. The case of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-
Family of quandles
was investigated by Lee and Sheikh who showed that the strucure gives an invariant of oriented dichromatic links
[
LS
]
. However considering two singquandles, a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-
Family of singquandles
(or
disingquandle
) was obtained and investigated by Sheikh
et al.
who showed that the structure gives an invariant of unoriented dichromatic singular links
[
SEA
]
.

In this paper, we introduce the notions of
oriented dichromatic singular links
and
oriented disingquandles
which are natural generalizations of the notions introduced in
[
SEA
]
. An
n
-component oriented singular link whose components are labelled either by
``1"
or
``2"
forms an
oriented dichromatic singular link
. By taking a family of oriented singquandles, we construct an algebraic structure called
oriented disingquandle
and whose axioms directly come from the generalized oriented dichromatic singular Reidemeister moves. We investigate properties of
oriented disingquandles
and give some examples. Furthermore, we define and study the counting invariant of oriented dichromatic singular links. We also distinguish some oriented dichromatic singular links by using oriented disingquandles.

This paper is organized in the following manner. In Section
2
, we review the notions of singular links, oriented singquandles and oriented dichromatic links. Section
3
deals with oriented dichromatic singular links. In Section
4
, we introduce oriented disingquandles and study some of their properties thus allowing the construction of examples. Section
5
introduces the coloring invariant of oriented dichromatic singular links and shows that the invariant distinguishes some oriented dichromatic singular links.

Definition 2.2

[

Let

\mathbf{R_{2}}(x,y)=\mathbf{R_{1}}(y,x*y),

\mathbf{R_{1}}(x,y)*\mathbf{R_{2}}(x,y)=\mathbf{R_{2}}(y,x*y).

All the axioms of the above definition

Oriented Disingquandles

In this section we introduce an analog of the notion of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-Family of Singquandles defined in section

[

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a set equipped with two binary operations
*_{1}
and
*_{2}
such that both
(X,*_{1}),(X,*_{2})
are quandles. Let
\mathbf{R_{1}},\mathbf{R_{2}}
be two maps from
\mathnormal{X}\times\mathnormal{X}
to
\mathnormal{X}
such that the quadruples
(\mathnormal{X},*_{1},\mathbf{R_{1}},\mathbf{R_{2}})
and
(\mathnormal{X},*_{2},\mathbf{R_{1}},\mathbf{R_{2}})
are oriented singquandles. Then the quintuple
(\mathnormal{X},*_{1},*_{2},\mathbf{R_{1}},\mathbf{R_{2}})
is called an
oriented disingquandle
if the following axioms are satisfied

(y\bar{*}_{1}\mathbf{R_{1}}(x,z))*_{2}x=(y*_{2}\mathbf{R_{2}}(x,z))\bar{*}_{1}z,

(y\bar{*}_{2}\mathbf{R_{1}}(x,z))*_{1}x=(y*_{1}\mathbf{R_{2}}(x,z))\bar{*}_{2}z,

\mathbf{R_{1}}(x,y)*_{1}\mathbf{R_{2}}(x,y)=\mathbf{R_{2}}(y,x*_{2}y),

\mathbf{R_{1}}(x,y)*_{2}\mathbf{R_{2}}(x,y)=\mathbf{R_{2}}(y,x*_{1}y),

In other words an oriented disingquandle is a pair of quandles with binary operations
*_{1}
,
*_{2}
and two additional maps
\mathbf{R_{1}}
and
\mathbf{R_{2}}
such that the singquandle axioms
(\ref{eq:2.2.1})-(\ref{eq:2.2.5})
as well as the additional axioms
(\ref{eq:4.1.1})-(\ref{eq:4.1.4})
are all satisfied simultaneously. Thus it follows from this definition that every oriented disingquandle is a quandle.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
(X,*_{1},*_{2},R_{1},R_{2})
and
(Y,*^{\prime}_{1},*^{\prime}_{2},R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2})
be two oriented disingquandles. A map

is called a
homomorphism
if
f(x*_{1}y)=f(x)*^{\prime}_{1}f(y)
,
f(x*_{2}y)=f(x)*^{\prime}_{2}f(y)
,
f(R_{1}(x,y))=R^{\prime}_{1}(f(x),f(y))
and
f(R_{2}(x,y))=R^{\prime}_{1}(f(x),f(y))
for all
x,y\in X.

If a homomorphism is bijective, then it is called an
isomorphism
. Two oriented disingquandles
(X,*_{1},*_{2},R_{1},R_{2})
and
(Y,*^{\prime}_{1},*^{\prime}_{2},R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2})
are said to be
isomorphic
if there exists an isomorphism between them.

Operational Markovianization in Randomized Benchmarking
P. Figueroa-Romero, M. Papič, A. Auer, M.-H. Hsieh, K. Modi, I. de Vega

Abstract
A crucial task to obtain optimal and reliable quantum devices is to quantify their overall performance. The average fidelity of quantum gates is a particular figure of merit that can be estimated efficiently by Randomized Benchmarking (RB) . However, the concept of gate-fidelity itself relies on the crucial assumption that noise behaves in a predictable, time-local, or so-called Markovian manner, whose breakdown can naturally become the leading source of errors as quantum devices scale in size and depth. We analytically show that error suppression techniques such as Dynamical Decoupling (DD) and Pauli-twirling can operationally Markovianize RB : i ) fast DD reduces non-Markovian RB to an exponential decay plus longer-time corrections, while on the other hand, ii ) Pauli-twirling generally does not affect the average, but iii ) it always suppresses the variance of such RB outputs. We demonstrate these effects numerically with a qubit noise model. Our results show that simple and efficient error suppression methods can simultaneously tame non-Markovian noise and allow for standard and reliable gate quality estimation, a fundamentally important task in the path toward fully functional quantum devices.



I
Introduction

The characterization of noise in quantum information processors will remain a necessary and unavoidable task to build fault-tolerant devices. Some of the most common sets of techniques to achieve this can be said to fall within an interval comprising tomographic techniques on one end, and
Randomized Benchmarking (RB)
techniques on the other: while the first can provide a detailed description of noise with an exponential measurement and sampling overhead in system size, the latter can estimate coarse average error rates efficiently

;

Due to the simplicity of

;
Magesan et al. 2012

;

;

Erhard et al. 2019
;

;
Flammia 2021
;
Harper and Flammia 2023

;
Helsen et al. 2022

Arguably, however, one of the most difficult simplifications to relax in any characterization technique is the one assuming that noise can be associated with each individual quantum gate separately. In reality, noise is e.g., introduced by the nature of the quantum device itself or by inherent limits in the control that the experimenter has, and errors occurring at a given time can propagate and affect future errors. These kinds of correlations and their effects are generally known as non-Markovianity

;
Breuer et al. 2016
;
de Vega and Alonso 2017

Pollock et al. 2018a
;
Pollock et al. 2018b
;
Milz and Modi 2021
. A signature aspect of non-Markovian noise in

;
Ball et al. 2016
;
Figueroa-Romero et al. 2021
;
Figueroa-Romero et al. 2022
;
Ceasura et al. 2022

Main Results (informal)
: In this manuscript, we show that for a broad class of non-Markovian noise models,

can effectively and efficiently
Markovianize

Our results show that coherent noise suppression and decoupling schemes in

III
Markovianization of RB with DD

The way
DD
works is by applying a sequence of pulses via a control Hamiltonian,
H_{\mathsf{ctrl}}
, in a way that effectively averages out undesired coupling terms in the total free-evolution Hamiltonian,
H

\mathsf{E}

Arenz et al. 2018

\sum_{v\in\mathds{V}}vXv^{\dagger}=O_{\mathsf{E}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathsf{S}}

In the setting of

The main message in Result

The continuous dynamical model for the noise as a
\mathsf{S}\mathsf{E}

In the absence of dissipation terms,
\mathcal{F}_{m}=1+\mathcal{O}(\tau_{\mathsf{dd}}^{2})

\mathsf{S}\mathsf{E}\mathsf{E}^{\prime}

The quality factor

\mathcal{D}_{\mathsf{S}}

\mathsf{S}

Higher-order,

Finally, realistic

IV
RB under Pauli-twirled noise

While the Markovianizing effect of
DD
in
RB
is somehow expected, a prominent error-suppression technique that has recently been shown to suppress non-Markovian noise in a statistically significant way

Hashim et al. 2023

Similar to

\Lambda

can be quantified to occur to a large percentage with a small number of samples

, generally scaling as an inverse fraction of the number of samples
Hashim et al. 2023
. The only caveats to keep in mind is that this sampling overhead of
RC
would compound with that of
RB

VI
Conclusions and Discussion

We have shown that noise suppression techniques, such as
DD
and Pauli-twirling, can be effective tools for dealing with non-Markovian noise sources in
RB
. In particular,
i
)
UDD
applied with fast and narrow pulses reduces a wide class of non-Markovian non-exponential
RB
decays to an exponential decay plus perturbative corrections in time,
ii
) Pauli-twirling does not Markovianize
RB
in the same sense that
DD
does, and in fact leaves the sequence fidelity decay invariant up to
SPAM
noise, however,
iii
) Pauli-twirling is ensured to decrease, or at worst leave unchanged, the variance of
RB
sequence fidelities.

Our results imply that standard noise suppression techniques can be valuable tools in taming, benchmarking, and optimizing non-Markovian noise. In particular, while we have dealt with the
standard
(or original)
RB
protocol as a benchmarking framework, this approach is amenable to be adapted to any
RB
-based technique considering e.g., scalability or specific purpose metrics
Helsen et al. 2022
.

In the case of
DD
, while Markovianization is always achieved at short times, the full effective
RB
sequence fidelities can still be dominated by non-Markovian terms, so the main limitation for
DD
is the time-scale at which pulses can be applied, which for an enhancement of fidelities would need to be shorter than those between application of the
RB
gates. Our main Result
III
regarding
DD
is stated for a broad class of continuous noise models with mild restrictions on the global Hamiltonian and dissipators. More generally, however, for any noise dynamics, the timescales at which decoupling can be efficiently achieved are connected to such dynamics
Addis et al. 2015
;
D’Arrigo et al. 2019
, but not necessarily to whether they display non-Markovianity
Burgarth et al. 2021a
. Furthermore, while they might be generally non-Markovian, some dynamics can hide non-Markovian effects
Burgarth et al. 2021b
;
Burgarth et al. 2021c
, in particular for our case in
RB
, and not display significant deviations. Finally, there is the fact that realistic
DD
pulses have a finite width and themselves can introduce errors; for our results, however, it is sufficient for them to be narrow and contain only local, time, and gate-independent small errors.

For Pauli-twirling in our main Result
IV
, it is somewhat surprising that
\mathsf{S}
-Pauli-twirling does not Markovianize
RB
data, since Pauli-twirling also effectively decouples by averaging out all non-Pauli error terms
Winick et al. 2022
. Concretely,
RB
with the Clifford group, while it is clear that in the Markovian case it leaves average gate-fidelities unchanged, as opposed to
DD
, it was expected for it to have a decoupling effect that would impact
directly
the average of the non-Markovian
RB
outputs. Nevertheless, Pauli-twirling can be extremely valuable in suppressing the uncertainty in the average sequence fidelity, which can ensure an accurate diagnosis of non-exponential deviations (as opposed to statistical fluctuations) and reliable estimation of error rates. Furthermore, as argued before, this reduction in variance due to Pauli-twirling is related to the amount of coherence of the noise, which in turn is intimately connected and relevant to average gate-fidelity and fault tolerant-relevant metrics such as diamond norm
Sanders et al. 2015
.

Noise suppression techniques, such as
DD
and Pauli-twirling, are a vital ingredient allowing for the possibility to take quantum computing beyond a noise-intermediate regime. Our results highlight the importance of incorporating such basic noise suppression techniques to deal with one of the most complicated sources of errors, namely non-Markovianity, not only for deployment but for basic error diagnostics and benchmarking. As a perspective, we expect these ideas to be useful, easily adapted, and enhanced, to other scalable and holistic benchmarking and characterization techniques, e.g.,
Proctor et al. 2022
;
Erhard et al. 2019
;
Helsen et al. 2023
;
Flammia 2021
;
Harper and Flammia 2023
, most of which are the intellectual progeny of
RB
.

A.2
Non-Markovianity

Here we briefly define formally what we mean by the term

, as summarized in
Figueroa-Romero et al. 2022

Milz and Modi 2021
.

Within classical probability theory, non-Markovianity can be described by a stochastic process,
\{X_{t}\}
, where generally probabilities are conditionally dependent on the past, i.e.

\mathds{P}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},\ldots,x_{0})=\mathds{P}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},\ldots,x_{k-\ell}),

(14)

for any integers

and sequences of event outcomes

\ell

\ell

Quantum mechanically, the process tensor (a.k.a. quantum comb
Chiribella et al. 2008
;
Chiribella et al. 2009

Portmann et al. 2017

Nurdin and Gough 2021

Costa and Shrapnel 2016

Kretschmann and Werner 2005

Gutoski and Watrous 2007

Pollock et al. 2018a
;
Pollock et al. 2018b
;
Milz et al. 2017
;
Milz et al. 2020
;
Taranto 2020
;
Milz and Modi 2021
;
Milz et al. 2019

14

\mathsf{E}

\mathsf{S}

\mathds{P}(x_{k},\ldots,x_{0}|\mathcal{J}_{k},\ldots,\mathcal{J}_{0}):=\tr\left[M_{x_{k}}\,\rho^{(k)}\right],

(15)

where

We drop the super-indices in Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathds{P}(x_{k},\ldots,x_{0}|\mathcal{J}_{k},\ldots,\mathcal{J}_{0})=\tr\left(\Upsilon_{k}\,\Theta_{k}^{\mathrm{T}}\right),

(16)

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{k}

\displaystyle:=\tr_{\mathsf{E}}\left\{\left[\Mcirc_{i=1}^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{aux}}\circ\mathscr{S}_{i})\right]\rho\otimes\psi^{\otimes{k}}\right\},

(17)

\displaystyle\Theta_{k}

\displaystyle:=M_{x_{k}}\otimes\left[\Motimes_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathsf{A}_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{x_{i}}\right)\right]\psi^{\otimes{k}},

(18)

where

\mathsf{A}_{i}

The process tensor framework thus allows to neatly separate the underlying dynamical source for any given quantum process, including all temporal correlations therein, from all experimentally controllable operations. This description is entirely general as a quantum stochastic process framework

Milz and Modi 2021

A.3
The Randomized Benchmarking protocol and the standard Markovian analysis

A standard

1.

Prepare an initial state

2.

Sample a sequence of

after

\mathcal{G}_{m+1}
as an undo-gate.

3.

Apply the composition
\mathcal{S}_{m}:=\mathcal{G}_{m+1}\circ\mathcal{G}_{m}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{G}_{1}
on
\rho
.

4.

Estimate the probability

via a
POVM
element

. Generally, a real physical implementation of these steps renders

.

5.

Repeat

m
, same initial state
\rho
, same
POVM
element
M
, and different sets of gates chosen uniformly at random,
\left\{\mathcal{G}_{i}^{(1)}\right\}_{i=1}^{m},\,\left\{\mathcal{G}_{i}^{(2)}\right\}_{i=1}^{m},\ldots,\left\{\mathcal{G}_{i}^{(n)}\right\}_{i=1}^{m}
from
\mathds{G}
, to obtain the corresponding probabilities

. We refer to
\mathcal{F}_{m}
as an

.

6.

Examine the behavior of the estimated
ASF
,
\mathcal{F}_{m}

The protocol up to this point is independent of any assumptions for the underlying sources of noise causing the outputs to deviate from the ideal expected one, as well as the choices for the gate-set (which is only restricted to be a finite group, hence contain inverses), the dimension of the system
\mathsf{S}
and the initial state and final measurement.

For several practical reasons, the system
\mathsf{S}
usually consists of one or two-qubits, i.e.,
d_{\mathsf{S}}=2^{n}
for either
n=1
or
n=2
, the initial state is taken to be the ground state in the computational basis
\rho=|0\rangle\!\langle 0|
and
M
the corresponding projector
M=|0\rangle\!\langle 0|
, so that the

is rather simply a survival probability of the ground state.

C.1
Remarks

C.1.1
Noise model

The main reason to model the noise maps
\Lambda_{k}
as a dynamical map is twofold: one is to set a time scale between the action of the control gates, parametrized by the time-intervals
\tau_{k}
, and the other is to express both the generators of non-Markovianity (the unitary part) and Markovianity (the dissipating part) within the global
\mathsf{S}\mathsf{E}
noise dynamics. This can encompass a broad class of physically motivated noise models.

The only assumption made in order to obtain Eq. (
47
) was that either there is no global
\mathsf{S}\mathsf{E}
dissipation contribution in the noise dynamics, or that it is negligible to the first order in
\tau
, as discussed below Eq. (
38
). Notice that if there is no dissipation at all, local or otherwise, to the first order in
\tau
, the
ASF
would be trivial (i.e., look noiseless) and all higher-order contributions to the decay are generally non-Markovian or from the local Hamiltonian. Numerically, in such case while it may still be seen that the
ASF
approximates a decaying exponential to a large extent –depending mainly on how fast the
DD
sequences are applied–, only higher-orders would describe this decay.

Finally, the presence or absence of dissipator terms has to be explicitly stated, as it is really the full
\Lambda^{(\tau)}
map that generates noise. For example, one could think of phrasing Result
III
in the main text for a dilated Hamiltonian
H^{\prime}
acting in a larger Hilbert space
\mathsf{S}\mathsf{E}\mathsf{E}^{\prime}
so that the Lindbladian
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(\cdot)=-i[H^{\prime},\cdot]
contains no explicit dissipators whatsoever; the point is that the actual original
\Lambda^{(\tau)}
will have to be written in terms of a partial tracing of
\mathsf{E}^{\prime}
and some fiducial initial state
\rho_{\mathsf{E}^{\prime}}
, i.e.,
\Lambda^{(\tau)}(\cdot)=\tr_{\mathsf{E}^{\prime}}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\tau\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}(\rho_{\mathsf{E}^{\prime}}\otimes\cdot)\right]
, so that the dynamics could in general still contain global
\mathsf{S}\mathsf{E}
dissipators, albeit hidden implicitly through
H^{\prime}
. In other words, the presence or absence of dissipation in the dynamics as stated for Result
III
is really a statement of non-unitarity (incoherence) or unitarity (full coherence) on
\mathsf{S}\mathsf{E}
, respectively, of the noise map
\Lambda^{(\tau)}
.

C.1.2
Non-ideal dynamical decoupling

The assumption of instantaneous, infinitely strong
DD
pulses has been studied extensively
Khodjasteh and Lidar 2007
;
Ezzell et al. 2023
, with the main consequence being that decoupling is not achieved even at the first small-time-order; however, as long as the width of the pulses is sufficiently small (relative to the inverse magnitude of the largest singular value of the evolution Hamiltonian), decoupling is approximately achieved. So in a sense, finite-width
DD
would still be effective in Markovianizing
RB
, as long as pulse widths are sufficiently small.

Similarly, one can relax the assumption of noiseless
DD
operators in a straightforward way: as long as the noise is approximately local, time-stationary and gate-independent, at each time-step of the
RB
protocol, this would amount to a relabeling of the noise maps
\Lambda_{i}\to\Lambda_{i}^{\prime}
, and Eq. (
48
) would hold at least up to the second equality with a different map
\mathcal{D}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\prime(\mathsf{dd})}
accounting for noise in the
DD
.

Appendix D
Subsystem Pauli-twirled noise

Other Markovianizing strategies different from
DD
could also be conceivable. In particular, techniques tailoring noise into a so-called
Pauli channel
have been shown to reduce the impact of non-Markovian noise
Ware et al. 2021
;
Winick et al. 2022
;
Hashim et al. 2023
in a statistically significant way.

Let us consider for now a closed
n
-qubit system. Let
\mathds{P}_{n}
be the
n
-qubit Pauli group, i.e., that generated by
n
-fold Kronecker products of single-qubit Pauli operators. The so-called twirl,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{P}_{n}}
, over
\mathds{P}_{n}
, of a
CP
map between
n
-qubits,
\Phi(\cdot):=\sum_{\mu}\phi_{\mu}(\cdot)\phi_{\mu}^{\dagger}
, can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{P}_{n}}\left(\hat{\Phi}\right)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum_{P\in\mathds{P}_{n}}{\sum_{\mu}}P^{\dagger}\phi_{\mu}{P}\otimes{P}^{\mathrm{T}}\phi_{\mu}^{*}{P}^{*},

(54)

where
\hat{\Phi}:=\sum_{\mu}\phi_{\mu}\otimes\phi_{\mu}^{*}
the vectorized form of
\Phi
, as defined in Appendix
A.1
. Hence, expanding the Kraus operators in the Pauli basis,
\phi_{\mu}=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}^{\mu}{P}_{i}
, we can rewrite the Pauli-twirled channel as

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}^{\mathsf{P}}:=\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{P}_{n}}\left(\hat{\Phi}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum\alpha_{i}^{\mu}\alpha_{j}^{*\mu}P_{k}^{\dagger}{P}_{i}{P}_{k}\otimes{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{T}}P_{j}^{*}{P}_{k}^{*}=\sum{p}_{i}P_{i}\otimes{P}_{i}^{*},

(55)

with
p_{i}:=\sum_{\mu}|\alpha_{i}^{\mu}|^{2}
. In general, any quantum channel of the form of Eq. (
55
) is called an
Pauli channel
. Such Pauli channel
\Phi^{\mathsf{P}}
has Kraus operators
\sqrt{p_{i}}P_{i}
, where
p_{i}=\sum_{\mu}|\alpha_{i}^{\mu}|^{2}
define a probability distribution satisfying
p_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}p_{i}\leq 1
(saturated if the channel
\hat{\Phi}
is also
TP
). Colloquially, then, we can say that Pauli-twirling a quantum channel, has the effect of tailoring it into a Pauli channel. In the specific case where we think of quantum channels as modeling quantum noise, we may refer to
p_{i}
as the
Pauli error-rates
, i.e., the probability distribution of any Pauli error from happening.

Throughout, we will reserve the zero
th
Pauli to denote the identity,
P_{0}:=\mathds{1}
.

A particularly useful representation when dealing with multi-qubit systems is the so-called
Pauli Transfer Matrix (PTM)
representation; in such representation, Pauli-twirled channels take a manifestly diagonal form. Explicitly, the
PTM
representation of an
n
-qubit quantum channel
\Phi
, is also a vectorized form (i.e., matrix-form) of the channel, but expanded in the Pauli basis,

R_{\Phi}:=2^{-n}\sum_{Q,P\in\mathds{P}_{n}}\llangle{Q}|\Phi(P)\rrangle\,|Q\rrangle\!\llangle{P}|,

(56)

that is, the
PTM
of
\Phi
is defined by the elements

(R_{\Phi})_{ij}=2^{-n}\llangle{P_{i}}|\Phi(P_{j})\rrangle=2^{-n}\tr\left[P_{i}\,\Phi(P_{j})\right],\qquad{P}_{i},P_{j}\in\mathds{P}_{n}.

(57)

In the case of a Pauli channel,
\Phi^{\mathsf{P}}
, the corresponding
PTM
matrix
R_{\Phi^{\mathsf{P}}}
is diagonal, with its (real) elements being related to the probabilities
p_{i}
in Eq. (
55

Flammia 2021
).

Remarkably, a Pauli-twirled noise channel has the same average gate-fidelity as the bare noise channel; this can be seen directly in Eq. (
20
), i.e.,
\mathfrak{F}_{\Phi}=\mathfrak{F}_{\Phi^{\mathsf{P}}}
; as the average gate-fidelity only takes into account the diagonal error rates. Naturally the same property holds if Pauli-twirling is done only on a subsystem. This, in turn, implies that any technique estimating average gate-fidelities will remain reliable under Pauli noise, and not greatly overestimate gate-quality by missing out on all other non-diagonal terms: indeed, the diamond norm for quantum channels with a given fidelity is minimized by Pauli channels
Sacchi 2005
;
Wallman and Flammia 2014
;
Sanders et al. 2015
and can be explicitly computed in such case through the trace-norm of the vector of Pauli error rates
Magesan et al. 2012
. Pauli-twirling can be operationally implemented through protocols such as Pauli Frame Randomization
Kern et al. 2005
;
Ware et al. 2021
or Randomized Compiling
Wallman and Emerson 2016
;
Winick et al. 2022
.

D.2
Variance Sequence Fidelity under
\mathsf{S}
-Pauli-twirled noise

While Pauli-twirling on a subspace does not necessarily Markovianize the
ASF
in the sense of removing non-exponential deviations, it must certainly reduce its statistical impact on other figures of merit sensible to all other error rates, similar to the case of full Pauli-twirling. As previously mentioned, this was pointed out in
Ware et al. 2021
;
Winick et al. 2022
. Indeed, within
RB
, it is still unclear whether Pauli-twirling generally can increase the
ASF
or in which cases it is sufficient to remove non-exponential deviations.

However, a noticeable effect of Pauli-twirled noise is to have a reduced variance sequence fidelity. This can be understood intuitively because the variance over gates of
RB
sequences,
\mathrm{f}_{m}=\llangle{M}|\hat{\tr}_{\mathsf{E}}\hat{\Lambda}_{m+1}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{m+1}\cdots\hat{\Lambda}_{1}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}|\tilde{\rho}\rrangle
is by definition
\mathbf{V}\left(\mathrm{f}_{m}\right):=\mathbf{E}\left(\mathrm{f}_{m}^{2}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{m}^{2}
, and the first term,
\mathbf{E}\left(\mathrm{f}_{m}^{2}\right)
, is precisely proportional to the so-called
unitarity
of the noise
Wallman et al. 2015
, which is generally very small for Pauli-noise (albeit not necessarily zero).

We can make this statement mathematically concrete by decomposing
\hat{\Lambda}_{i}:=\hat{\Lambda}_{i}^{\mathsf{nd}}+\hat{\Lambda}_{i}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}}
, where

\hat{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{nd}}_{i}=\sum_{j\neq{k}}\chi_{\mu_{i}\sigma_{i},j_{i}k_{i}}^{(i)}\left(P_{\mu_{i}}\otimes{P}_{\sigma_{i}}^{*}\right)\otimes\left(P_{j_{i}}\otimes{P}_{k_{i}}^{*}\right),

(68)

is the operator corresponding to the map with all the off-diagonal terms in
\mathsf{S}
. Denoting explicitly the dependence on the noise for the sequence fidelity
\mathrm{f}_{m}
, we can then write

\displaystyle\mathrm{f}_{m}(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m+1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{f}_{m}(\Lambda_{0}^{\mathsf{nd}},\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m+1})+\mathrm{f}_{m}(\Lambda_{0}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}},\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m+1}),

(69)

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}[\mathrm{f}_{m}(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m+1})]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{V}[\mathrm{f}_{m}(\Lambda_{0}^{\mathsf{nd}},\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m+1})]+\mathbf{V}[\mathrm{f}_{m}(\Lambda_{0}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}},\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m+1})],

(70)

and we can keep expanding all
\Lambda_{i}
terms to get, informally,

\mathbf{V}[\mathrm{f}_{m}(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m+1})]=\mathbf{V}[\mathrm{f}_{m}(\Lambda_{0}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}},\Lambda_{1}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}},\ldots,\Lambda_{m+1}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}})]+\sum\mathbf{V}[\mathrm{f}_{m}(\text{at least one }\Lambda_{i}^{\mathsf{nd}}\text{, rest }\Lambda_{j}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}})].

(71)

All fidelities “
\mathrm{f}_{m}(\text{at least one }\Lambda_{i}^{\mathsf{nd}}\text{, rest }\Lambda_{j}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}})
” are real numbers because
\Lambda_{i}^{\mathsf{nd}}
, the Pauli non-diagonal contribution to
\Lambda
, remains a Hermitian map. This implies that
\mathbf{V}[\mathrm{f}_{m}(\text{at least one }\Lambda_{i}^{\mathsf{nd}}\text{, rest }\Lambda_{j}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}})]\geq 0
, and so it follows that

\mathbf{V}[\mathrm{f}_{m}(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m+1})]\geq\mathbf{V}[\mathrm{f}_{m}(\Lambda_{0}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}},\Lambda_{1}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}},\ldots,\Lambda_{m+1}^{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{S}}})].

(72)

This statement remains true for more general
\mathrm{f}_{m}
, in particular for any expectation value, not necessarily a
RB
sequence fidelity. Notice the same cannot be said, however, about the
ASF
because
\Lambda_{i}^{\mathsf{nd}}
does not necessarily remain completely-positive or even simply positive
15
15

15

\Lambda

\hat{\Lambda}
; however it does correspond to positive semi-definiteness of the so-called Choi state of

, so the averages of these sequence fidelities can generally be negative too.

We exemplify the effect of Eq. (

This effect in the variance due to smaller unitarity was already pointed out in

Given the relatively low overhead of techniques enforcing Pauli-twirled noise, such as Randomized Compiling
Hashim et al. 2021

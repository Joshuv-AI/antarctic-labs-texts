1
Introduction and setting of the problem

Let
H
be a real separable Hilbert space, (norm
|\cdot|_{H}
, scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
).
We are concerned with the following stochastic differential equation on
H
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dZ_{x}(t)=(AZ_{x}(t)+b(Z_{x}(t))dt+dW(t),\quad t\geq 0,\\
\\
Z_{x}(0)=x\in H,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

under the following assumptions.

Hypothesis 1
.

(i)
A\colon D(A)\subset H\to H
is a self–adjoint operator and there is
\omega>0
such that

\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\leq e^{-\omega t},\,t\geq 0.

Moreover,
(-A)^{-1+\beta}
is of trace class for some
\beta>0
.

(ii)
b\colon H\to H
is Lipschitz continuous.

(iii)
W
is an
H
–valued cylindrical Wiener process on a filtered probability space

(\Omega,\mathscr{F},(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
.

We shall denote by
W_{A}
the
stochastic convolution

W_{A}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}\,dW(s),\quad t\geq 0.

(1.2)

Thanks to Hypothesis
1
(i),
W_{A}
is a continuous process, see, e.g.,
[
4
, Theorem 2.6]
.

We fix
T>0
and set
E_{[0,T]}=C([0,T];H)
, the space of all continuous mapping
[0,T]\to H
endowed with the sup norm; we shall denote by
\mathscr{B}(E_{[0,T]})
the
\sigma
–algebra of all Borel subsets of
E_{[0,T]}
. Moreover,
B_{b}(E_{[0,T]})
will represent the space of all mappings
E_{[0,T]}\to\mathbb{R}
which are Borel and bounded.

The law of
W_{A}
in
E_{[0,T]}
is Gaussian
\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
, that is, it has mean
0
and covariance
\mathbb{Q}_{T}
; it will be described in Section 2 below.

The goal of this paper is to determine the
law of
Z_{x}
on
E_{[0,T]}
for all
x\in H
represented as a special integral with respect to
\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
, that we shall call a
mild Girsanov
formula, and to deduce some consequences from this identity.

We shall proceed as follows. Setting
Z_{x}(t)-e^{tA}x=:K_{x}(t),\,t\in[0,T],
equation (
1.1
) becomes

K_{x}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}\,b(K_{x}(s)+e^{sA}x)ds+W_{A}(t),\quad t\in[0,T].

(1.3)

Then we associate with the stochastic equation (
1.3
) the following deterministic one,

k_{x}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}b(k_{x}(s)+e^{sA}x)ds+h(t),\quad h\in E_{[0,T]},\,t\in[0,T],

(1.4)

that, by a standard fixed point arguments, has a unique solution in
E_{[0,T]}
that we denote by
k_{x}(t),\,t\in[0,T]
.
Next, for every
x\in H
we consider the mapping

F_{x}\colon E_{[0,T]}\to E_{[0,T]},\quad h\to F_{x}(h)=k_{x}.

(1.5)

We shall denote by
G_{x}:E_{[0,T]}\to E_{[0,T]}
the inverse mapping of
F_{x}
, which reads as follows

[G_{x}(h)](t)=h(t)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}\,b(h(s)+e^{sA}x)\,ds,\quad h\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\in E_{[0,T]}.

(1.6)

Since
b
is Lipschitz continuous, both
F_{x}
and
G_{x}

are continuous; therefore they are homeomorphisms from
E_{[0,T]}
onto
E_{[0,T]}
.

A key role will be plaid in what follows by the mapping

\gamma_{x}(h)=h-G_{x}(h),\,h\in E_{[0,T]},
(see e.g. identity (
1.9
)),

[\gamma_{x}(k)](t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}b(k(s)+e^{sA}x)\,ds,\quad k\in E_{[0,T]},\,t\in[0,T].

(1.7)

Now the solution
K_{x}(\cdot)
of (
1.3
) can be written as
K_{x}(\cdot)=F_{x}(W_{A}(\cdot)).

So, the law of
Z_{x}
on
E_{[0,T]}
is given by

(\mathbb{P}\circ Z_{x}^{-1})(\Phi)=\mathbb{E}[\Phi(Z_{x}(\cdot))]=\mathbb{E}[\Phi(K_{x}(\cdot)+e^{\cdot A}x)]=\mathbb{E}[\Phi(F_{x}(W_{A}(\cdot))+e^{\cdot A}x)],\quad\forall\,\Phi\in B_{b}(\mathbb{E}_{[0,T]}).

Therefore, by a change of variables, it results

(\mathbb{P}\circ Z_{x}^{-1})(\Phi)=\int_{E_{[0,T]}}\Phi(F_{x}(h(\cdot))+e^{\cdot A}x)\,\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}(dh),\quad\forall\,\Phi\in B_{b}(E_{[0,T]}).

(1.8)

This formula is usual when dealing with stochastic equations with additive noise, see e.g.

[
9
]
.
But we look for a formula involving
G_{x}
rather than
F_{x}
because
G_{x}
is explicit while
F_{x}
is not.

So, we set

F_{x}(h)=k,

and obtain

(\mathbb{P}\circ Z_{x}^{-1})(\Phi)=\int_{E_{[0,T]}}\Phi(k+e^{\cdot A}x)\,(\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}\circ G_{x})(dk),\quad\forall\,\Phi\in B_{b}(E_{[0,T]}).

(1.9)

But now we need to show that

N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}\circ G_{x}\ll N_{\mathscr{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}}.

(1.10)

In fact, using an identity due to
R. Ramer,
[
15
]
(see Section 3.1 below) we show that

\frac{d(\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}\circ G_{x})}{d\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}}\,(h)=\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}|\gamma_{x}(h)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}}+[I(\gamma_{x})](h)\right\},\quad\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}-a.s.

(1.11)

where
\mathscr{H}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
denotes the Cameron–Martin space of
\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
and
I(\gamma_{x})
the Itô integral of
\gamma_{x}
, which results to be adapted.

So, we end up with, the identity

(\mathbb{P}\circ Z_{x}^{-1})(\Phi)=\int_{E_{[0,T]}}\Phi(h+e^{\cdot A}x)\,\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}|\gamma_{x}(h)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}}+(\gamma_{x})(h)\right\}\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}(dh),

(1.12)

which it will be proved in Theorem
3.2
below.

As a consequence of (
1.12
) we obtain an expression for the transition semigroup
P_{t},\;t\geq 0,
corresponding to the process
Z_{x}
, see Corollary
3.3
,

P_{t}\varphi(x)=\mathbb{E}[\varphi(Z_{x}(t))]=\int_{E_{[0,T]}}\varphi(k(t)+e^{tA}x)\,\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}|\gamma_{x}(k)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}}+I(\gamma_{x})(k)\right\}\,\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}(dk),\quad t\in[0,T].

(1.13)

Remark 1.1
.

When
A=0
identity (
1.12
) follows from the classical Girsanov formula, see e.g.
[
2
]
.

We end this section with some comments about identities (
1.12
) and (
1.13
).
First we note that
the classical Girsanov formula describes the law of
Z_{x}
in terms of the Wiener measure on
C([0,T];H)
, and so, does not provide any information about the asymptotic behaviour
of
Z_{x}
. Instead the mild Girsanov formula allows to guess the explicit form of the invariant measure
\nu
of
P_{t},\,t\geq 0
in case this measure exists.

To give an idea: consider the problem

Z_{x,-n}(t)=e^{(t+n)A}x+\int_{-n}^{t}e^{(t-r)A}\,b(Z_{x,-n}(r))dr+W_{A,-n}(t),\quad t\leq 0,\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\,n\in\mathbb{N},

(1.14)

where
W_{A,-n}(t)
denotes the modified
stochastic convolution

W_{A,-n}(t)=\int_{-n}^{t}e^{(t-r)A}\,dW(r),

where
W
is extended to
\mathbb{R}
in the usual way.
Letting
n\to\infty
, we are able to obtain an explicit formula for the invariant measure, see Theorem
4.3
.

In Section 4
we will examine the case when
b
is Lipschitz continuous and dissipative.
In this case it is known that there is a unique invariant mesure
\nu
, see
[
8
]
.
We shall prove explicit representation formulas both for
\nu
and for its density with respect to the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process that we obtain setting

b=0
. A final result of this theorem yields a representation formula for the law of the stationary process corresponding to (
1.1
).

Let us explain briefly the content of the paper.

The application of the Ramer formula requires some preliminaries as: the Cameron–Martin space of
\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
, determined in
Section 2 as well the Malliavin derivative and the Gaussian divergence operator. The mild Girsanov formula, Theorem
3.2
, is proven in Section 3.

Moreover, Section 4 is devoted to an explicit formula for the law of the stationary process of
P_{t}

on
(-\infty,0]
(and consequently of the invariant measure) when

is dissipative.
Here a difficulty arises because the stationary process lives
on the space of continuous functions on
(-\infty,0]
, not necessarily bounded, which is a
separable Fréchet space.
Then, as far as the Malliavin calculus is concerned on locally convex spaces, we refer to the monograph
[
2
]
.
In Section 5 we consider stochastic equations with an additive colored noise.
Finally, Appendix A is devoted to recall some maximal regularity results for abstract evolution equations,
which are needed in what follows.

2
The Cameron–Martin space

Since

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|W_{A}(t)|^{2}_{H}\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\mbox{\rm Tr}\,[(-A)^{-1}\,(1-e^{2tA})]dt<\infty,\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

we can extend the measure
\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
from
E_{[0,T]}
to
\mathbb{X}:=L^{2}(0,T;H)

which we shall denote by
\mathscr{N}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}}
.
It is well known that
\mathscr{N}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}}
is Gaussian, with mean
0
and covariance
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}
given by

(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}\,h)(t)=\int_{0}^{T}K(t,s)h(s)\,ds,\quad t\in[0,T],\;h\in\mathbb{X},

(2.1)

where

K(t,s)=\int_{0}^{\min\{t,s\}}\,e^{(t+s-2r)A}dr\quad t,s\in[0,T],

(2.2)

see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 5.4]
.
The measure
\mathscr{N}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}}

is concentrated on
E_{[0,T]}
and its Cameron–Martin space
\mathscr{H}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}^{1/2}(\mathbb{X})
coincides with that of

\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
, see e.g.
[
8
, Proposition 2.10]
.

The following lemma is well known; we give a sketch of its proof, however, for the reader convenience.

Lemma 2.1
.

Setting
\mathbb{A}_{T}=-\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}^{-1}
, it results

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbb{A}_{T}f=f^{\prime\prime}(t)-A^{2}\,f(t),\quad\forall\,f\in D(\mathbb{A}_{T})\\
\\
D(\mathbb{A}_{T})=\{f\in L^{2}(0,T;D(A^{2}))\cap W^{2,2}(0,T;H):\,\;f(0)=0,\;f^{\prime}(T)=Af(T)\}.\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

Proof.

Given
h\in\mathbb{X}
set
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}\,h=f
and write

\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)\,h(s)\,ds+\int_{t}^{T}K(t,s)\,h(s)ds=f(t),\quad t\in[0,T].

So,

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{(t+s-2r)A}\,dr\right)\,h(s)\,ds+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t+s-2r)A}\,dr\right)\,h(s)ds=f(t).

(2.4)

Note that
f(0)=0.
Introducing
Q_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{2sA}ds=(-2A)^{-1}(I-e^{2tA}),

t\geq 0,
and
differentiating (
2.4
) with respect to

t
yields

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle Q_{t}\,h(t)+A\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{(t+s-2r)A}\,dr\right)\,h(s)\,ds\\
\\
\displaystyle-Q_{t}\,h(t)+\int_{t}^{T}e^{(s-t)A}\,h(s)ds+A\int_{t}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t+s-2r)A}\,dr\right)\,h(s)\,ds=f^{\prime}(t).\end{array}

It follows that

Af(t)+\int_{t}^{T}e^{(s-t)A}\,\,h(s)ds=f^{\prime}(t),

(2.5)

which implies

f^{\prime}(T)=Af(T).

Now, differentiating (
2.5
) with respect to
t
, yields

Af^{\prime}(t)-h(t)-A\int_{t}^{T}e^{(s-t)A}\,h(s)ds=f^{\prime\prime}(t).

(2.6)

Taking into account (
2.5
), we deduce

f^{\prime\prime}(t)-A^{2}f(t)=-h(t).

(2.7)

Since
h=-\mathbb{A}_{T}f
, we have

\mathbb{A}_{T}f=f^{\prime\prime}(t)-A^{2}f(t),

as required.
∎

Now we can determine the
Cameron–Martin space
of
\mathscr{N}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}}
which, as remarked earlier, coincides with that of
\mathscr{N}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

It results, see Appendix A,

\mathscr{H}_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}}=\mathscr{H}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}}=\{u\in L^{2}(0,T;D(A))\cap W^{1,2}(0,T;H):\,u(0)=0\}:=\Gamma_{A}(H).

Moreover, if
u\in\mathscr{H}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}}
, we have

|u|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{T}}}:=|\overline{Q_{T}}^{-1/2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{X}}=|(-A)^{1/2}\,u(T)|_{H}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}(|u^{\prime}(t)|_{H}^{2}+|Au(t)|_{H}^{2})\,dt.

(2.8)

Remark 2.3
.

Note that by interpolation it results, see e.g.

12
]

\{u\in L^{2}(0,T;D(A))\cap W^{1,2}(0,T;H):\,u(0)=0\}\subset C([0,T];D(-A)^{1/2})),

Proof of Proposition

.

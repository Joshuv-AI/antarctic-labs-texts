Some Expansion Formulas for Brenke Polynomial Sets
Hamza Chaggara, Abdelhamid Gahami, Neila Ben Romdhane

Abstract
. In this paper, we derive some explicit expansion formulas associated to Brenke polynomials using operational rules based on their corresponding generating functions. The obtained coefficients are expressed either in terms of finite double sums or finite sums or sometimes in closed hypergeometric terms. The derived results are applied to Generalized Gould-Hopper polynomials and Generalized Hermite polynomials introduced by Szegö and Chihara. Some well-known duplication and convolution formulas are deduced as particular cases.

1.
Introduction

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}
be the vector space of polynomials with coefficients in
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
. A polynomial sequence in
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}
is called
polynomial set
(PS for short) if
\displaystyle\deg P_{n}=n
, for all
\displaystyle n
.

The connection and linearization problems are defined as follows.

Given two PSs
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and

\displaystyle\{Q_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
the so-called
connection problem
between them asks to find the coefficients

\displaystyle C_{m}(n)
, called connection coefficients CC, in the expression

Q_{n}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}C_{m}(n)P_{m}(x).

(1.1)

The particular cases
\displaystyle Q_{n}(x)=x^{n}
and
\displaystyle Q_{n}(x)=P_{n}(ax),\ a\neq 0
, in (
1.1
) are known, respectively, as the
inversion formula
for
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and the
duplication or multiplication formula
associated with
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
.

Given three PSs

\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
\displaystyle\{R_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

and
\displaystyle\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, then for

\displaystyle Q_{i+j}(x)=~R_{i}(x)S_{j}(x)
in (
1.1
) we are faced to the general
linearization problem

R_{i}(x)S_{j}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{i+j}L_{ij}(k)P_{k}(x).

(1.2)

The coefficients
\displaystyle L_{ij}(k)
are called
linearization coefficients LC.

The particular case of this problem,
\displaystyle P_{n}=R_{n}=S_{n}
, is known as the
standard linearization problem
or

Clebsch-Gordan-type problem
.

The computation and the positivity of the aforementioned coefficients play important roles in many situations of pure and applied mathematics ranging from combinatorics and statistical mechanics to group theory
[
4
,
21
,
23
]
.
Therefore, different methods have been developed in the literature and
several sufficient conditions for the sign properties to hold have been derived in
[
3
,
31
]
, using for this purpose specific properties of the involved polynomials such as orthogonality, generating functions, inversion formulas, hypergeometric expansion formulas, recurrence relations, algorithmic approaches, inverse relations,…(see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
13
,
24
,
32
]
). In particular, a general method, based on operational rules and generating functions, was developed for polynomial sets with equivalent lowering operators and with Boas-Buck
generating
functions
[
6
,
12
,
14
]
.

In this paper, we deeply discuss both the connection and the linearization problems when the involved polynomials are of Brenke type. These polynomials are defined by their exponential generating functions as follows
[
9
,
17
]

A(t)B(xt)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{P_{n}(x)}{n!}t^{n},

(1.3)

where
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
are two formal power series satisfying:

A(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}t^{k},\quad B(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}t^{k},\quad a_{0}b_{k}\neq 0,\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.4)

Brenke PSs are reduced to Appell ones when
\displaystyle B=\exp
and they generated many well-known polynomials in the literature, namely
monomials, Hermite, Laguerre, Gould-Hopper, Generalized Hermite,
Generalized Gould-Hopper, Appell-Dunkl,
\displaystyle d
-Hermite,
\displaystyle d
-Laguerre, Bernoulli, Euler, Al-Salam-Carlitz, Little
\displaystyle q
-Laguerre,
\displaystyle q
-Laguerre, discrete
\displaystyle q
-Hermite PSs,….

These polynomials appear in many areas of mathematics.
In particular, in the framework of the standard orthogonality of polynomials, an exhaustive classification of all Brenke orthogonal polynomials was established by Chihara in
[
16
]
.
Furthermore, Brenke polynomials play a central role in
[
25
]
,
where the authors determined all MRM-triples associated with Brenke-type generating functions.
Further, the positive approximation process discovered by Korovkin, a powerful criterion in order to decide whether a given sequence of positive linear operators on the space of continuous functions converges uniformly in this space, plays a central role and arises naturally in many problems connected with functional analysis, harmonic analysis, measure theory, partial differential equations, and probability theory.
The most useful examples of such operators are Szász operators and many authors obtained a generalization of these operators using Brenke polynomials (see
[
33
,
34
]
and the references therein).

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2,
we define the transfer linear operator between two Brenke polynomials and which is illustrated by three interesting examples in particular the hypergeometric transformation and the Dunkl operator on the real line.
Then in Section 3,
we derive expansion formulas associated to Brenke polynomials using operational rules and we give connection, linearization, inversion, duplication, and addition formulas corresponding to these polynomials.
The obtained coefficients are expressed using generating functions involving the associated transfer linear operators.
Finally, in Section 4,
we apply our obtained results to both Generalized Gould-Hopper PS (GGHPS) and Generalized Hermite PS (or Szegö-Chihara PS) and we recover many known formulas as special cases.

2.
Operators Associated to Brenke PSs

In this section, first, we introduce a transfer
operator between two Brenke families, then we state its expression as an infinite series in the derivative operator
\displaystyle D
and the multiplication operator
\displaystyle X
known as
\displaystyle XD
-expansion
[
19
]
.
Finally, we give some examples.

2.1.
Transfer Operator Associated to two Brenke Polynomials

Any Brenke PS
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
generated by (
1.3
) is
\displaystyle D_{b}
-Appell of transfer power series
\displaystyle A
, where
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle b=(b_{n})
are defined in (
1.4
). That is,

D_{b}P_{n+1}=(n+1)P_{n}\quad\textrm{and}\quad A(D_{b})(b_{n}x^{n})=\frac{P_{n}}{n!},\ n=0,1,2,\ldots,

(2.1)

where
\displaystyle D_{b}
denotes the linear operator on
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}
defined by
[
6
]
:

D_{b}(1)=0,\ D_{b}(x^{n})=\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_{n}}x^{n-1},\,n=1,2,\ldots.

(2.2)

The operator
\displaystyle D_{b}
is known as the lowering operator for the PS
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, however,
\displaystyle A
is the associated transfer series. (For more details, see
[
5
]
).

Let
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\displaystyle\{Q_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be two Brenke PSs generated respectively by:

A_{1}(t)B_{1}(xt)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{P_{n}(x)\over n!}t^{n}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad A_{2}(t)B_{2}(xt)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{Q_{n}(x)\over n!}t^{n},

(2.3)

where for
\displaystyle i=1,2
,

A_{i}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}^{(i)}t^{k},\quad B_{i}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}^{(i)}t^{k},\quad a_{0}^{(i)}b_{k}^{(i)}\neq 0,\ \forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.4)

Then, the corresponding operators
\displaystyle D_{b^{(1)}}
and
\displaystyle D_{b^{(2)}}
are related by:

D_{b^{(2)}}\theta=\theta D_{b^{(1)}},

(2.5)

where
\displaystyle\theta
is the bijective linear operator from
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}
onto
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}
(isomorphism of
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}
) acting on monomials as follows:

\theta(x^{n})=\frac{b_{n}^{(2)}}{b_{n}^{(1)}}x^{n}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\theta^{-1}(x^{n})=\frac{b_{n}^{(1)}}{b_{n}^{(2)}}x^{n}.

(2.6)

The linear operator
\displaystyle\theta
can be extended as a transfer operator taking any formal power series to another formal power series as follows

\theta(\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}x^{n})=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\theta(x^{n}),

(2.7)

and if
\displaystyle\phi(x)
denotes a formal power series then one can easily check that,

\theta\Bigl(\phi(x)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}x^{k}\Bigr)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\theta(\phi(x)x^{k}).

(2.8)

Hence, it is obvious that,

\theta(B_{1}(x))=B_{2}(x).

(2.9)

The operator
\displaystyle\theta
will be called the transfer operator from
\displaystyle B_{1}
to
\displaystyle B_{2}
or transfer operator from
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
to
\displaystyle\{Q_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
.

2.2.
\displaystyle XD
-Expansion of the Operator
\displaystyle\theta

Now, recall that any operator
\displaystyle L
acting on formal power series has the following formal expansion, known as
\displaystyle XD
-expansion (see
[
19
]
and the references therein):

L=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}(X)D^{k},

(2.10)

where
\displaystyle D
denotes the ordinary differentiation operator and

\displaystyle\{A_{k}(x)\}_{k\geq 0}
is a polynomial sequence such that:

Le^{xt}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}(x)t^{k}e^{xt}.

(2.11)

We note that the infinite sum (
2.10
) is always well defined on
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}
since when applied to any given
polynomial, only a finite number of terms makes a nonzero contribution.

The
\displaystyle XD
-expansion of the transfer operator
\displaystyle\theta
is explicitly given by

Proposition 2.1
.

The operator
\displaystyle\theta
defined by (
2.6
) has the formal expansion:

\theta=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\phi_{k}\over k!}X^{k}D^{k},

(2.12)

where

\displaystyle\quad\phi_{k}=(-1)^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{(-k)_{m}}{m!}{b_{m}^{(2)}\over b_{m}^{(1)}}.

Proof.

By using (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) and
then substituting
\displaystyle L
by
\displaystyle\theta
in
(
2.11
), we obtain

\theta(e^{xt})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{b_{k}^{(2)}\over b_{k}^{(1)}}{(xt)^{k}\over k!}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}(x)t^{k}e^{xt}.

Therefore,

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}(x)t^{k}=e^{-xt}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{b_{k}^{(2)}\over b_{k}^{(1)}}{(xt)^{k}\over k!}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{(-k)_{m}}{m!}{b_{m}^{(2)}\over b_{m}^{(1)}}\right){(xt)^{k}\over k!},

which establishes the desired result.
∎

2.3.3.
Dunkl Operator on the Real Line

The well-known Dunkl operator,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}
, associated with the parameter
\displaystyle\mu
on the real line provides a useful tool in the study of special functions with root systems associated with finite

[

[

[

\mathcal{D}_{\mu}(f)(x)=Df(x)+\frac{\mu}{x}(f(x)-f(-x)),\quad\mu\in\mathbb{C},

(2.25)

where
\displaystyle f

\displaystyle D

The Dunkl operator acts on monomials as follows:

\mathcal{D}_{\mu}(x^{n})=\frac{\gamma_{\mu}(n)}{\gamma_{\mu}(n-1)}x^{n-1},\ \mu\neq-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{3}{2},\ldots,

\gamma_{\mu}(2p+\epsilon)=2^{2p+\epsilon}p!(\mu+\frac{1}{2})_{p+\epsilon},\quad\epsilon=0,1.

(2.27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}

\displaystyle D_{b}
-operator type with
\displaystyle b_{n}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mu}(n)}

Proposition 2.4
.

\displaystyle\mu_{1}
and
\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}

\displaystyle\theta
given by

\theta(x^{n})=\frac{\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(n)}{\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(n)}x^{n}.

(2.28)

Then, for any analytic function,
\displaystyle f
on
\displaystyle]-1,1[,
the following integral representation of
\displaystyle\theta
holds true

\theta(f)(x)={1\over\beta(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2},\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})}\int_{-1}^{1}f(xt)|t|^{2\mu_{1}}(1-t)^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}-1}(1+t)^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}}\,dt.

3.
Connection and Linearization Problems

In this section, we investigate connection and linearization formulas for

3.1.
Connection Problem

Next, for two polynomial sequences of Brenke type, we state a generating function for the connection coefficients using the operator
\displaystyle\theta

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\displaystyle\{Q_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be two polynomial sequences generated by (
2.3
) and (
2.4
)
and let
\displaystyle\theta
be the corresponding transfer operator defined in (

Then the CC in (
1.1
),
\displaystyle(C_{m}(n))_{n\geq m\geq 0}
, are generated by:

A_{2}(t)\theta\left({t^{m}\over A_{1}(t)}\right)=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n}.

(3.1)

Proof.

On one hand, substituting (
1.1
) in (
2.3
) and using sum manipulations, we get:

\displaystyle\displaystyle A_{2}(t)B_{2}(xt)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Q_{n}(x){t^{n}\over n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n}C_{m}(n)P_{m}(x)\right){t^{n}\over n!}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}{m!\over n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n}\right){P_{m}(x)\over m!}.

On the other hand, from (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle A_{2}(t)B_{2}(xt)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=A_{2}(t)\theta_{t}B_{1}(xt)=A_{2}(t)\theta_{t}\left({1\over A_{1}(t)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}(x){t^{m}\over m!}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}A_{2}(t)\theta_{t}\left({t^{m}\over A_{1}(t)}\right){P_{m}(x)\over m!}.

Thus (
3.1
) follows and the proof is completed.
∎

Some known results can be deduced from Theorem
3.1
. Next, we quote the four important ones of them.

3.1.1.
Explicit Expression of the Connection Coefficients

Write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{1}(t)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{a}_{n}^{(1)}t^{n},

then

\theta_{t}\left(\frac{t^{m}}{A_{1}(t)}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{b_{n+m}^{(2)}\over b_{n+m}^{(1)}}\widehat{a}_{n}^{(1)}t^{n+m}.

By virtue of (
3.1
), we get:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}{m!\over n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}^{(2)}t^{n}\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{b_{n+m}^{(2)}\over b_{n+m}^{(1)}}\widehat{a}_{n}^{(1)}t^{n+m}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=t^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}^{(2)}{b_{n+m-k}^{(2)}\over b_{n+m-k}^{(1)}}\widehat{a}_{n-k}^{(1)}\right)t^{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-m}{b_{n-k}^{(2)}\over b_{n-k}^{(1)}}a_{k}^{(2)}\widehat{a}_{n-m-k}^{(1)}\right)t^{n}.

Thus,

C_{m}(n)={n!\over m!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-m}{b_{n-k}^{(2)}\over b_{n-k}^{(1)}}a_{k}^{(2)}\widehat{a}_{n-m-k}^{(1)},\quad m=0,\ldots,n.

(3.2)

In particular, we can deduce the explicit expansion and the inversion formula for any Brenke PS
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
generated by (
1.3
):

\frac{P_{n}(x)}{n!}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}b_{m}a_{n-m}x^{m},\quad\textrm{and}\quad b_{n}x^{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\widehat{a}_{n-m}\frac{P_{m}(x)}{m!}.

(3.3)

3.1.2.
Connection between two
\displaystyle D_{b}
-Appell PSs

If
\displaystyle B_{1}=B_{2}
,
in (
2.3
), then by using (
2.6

the expression (

) takes the following simpler form

{A_{2}(t)\over A_{1}(t)}=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n-m}.

(3.4)

3.1.3.
Addition and Convolution Type Formulas

The Brenke PS
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
generated by (
1.3
) possesses the following generalized addition formula and convolution type relation:

T_{y}^{b}P_{n}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{n!}{m!}b_{n-m}y^{n-m}P_{m}(x),

and

A(D_{b})T_{y}^{b}P_{n}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n\choose m}P_{n-m}(y)P_{m}(x),

where
\displaystyle T_{y}^{b}=B(yD_{b})
designates the generalized translation operator
satisfying
\displaystyle T_{y}^{b}(B(xt)=B(yt)B(xt)
.

In fact, for the addition formula, we remark that the PS,
\displaystyle\{T_{y}^{b}P_{n}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
, is generated by:

B(yt)A(t)B(xt)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{T_{y}^{b}P_{n}(x)\over n!}t^{n},

then we apply (
3.4
) with
\displaystyle A_{2}(t)=B(yt)A(t)
and
\displaystyle A_{1}(t)=A(t)
,
to obtain

C_{m}(n)=\frac{n!}{m!}b_{n-m}y^{n-m}.

For the convolution type relation, we apply the operator
\displaystyle A(D_{b})
to each member of the addition formula and we use (
2.1
). We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle A(D_{b})T_{y}^{b}P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n!\over m!(n-m)!}A(D_{b})((n-m)!b_{n-m}y^{n-m})P_{m}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n\choose m}P_{n-m}(y)P_{m}(x).

Brenke PS generated by (

P_{n}(ax)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n!\over m!}a^{m}\beta_{n-m}P_{m}(x),\quad a\neq 0,

(3.5)

where
\displaystyle{A(t)\over A(at)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{k}t^{k}.

In fact, the PS

Thus, by using (

Now, from (

\displaystyle A_{1}(t)=A_{2}(t)=A(t)

3.2.
Linearization Problems

In the following result, we provide a generating function for the LC involving three Brenke polynomials.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
\displaystyle\{R_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\displaystyle\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be three Brenke PS with exponential generating functions:

A_{1}(t)B_{1}(xt),\ A_{2}(t)B_{2}(xt)\ \textrm{and}\ A_{3}(t)B_{3}(xt),

(3.6)

where

\displaystyle A_{i}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}^{(i)}t^{k},\,B_{i}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}^{(i)}t^{k},\

\displaystyle a_{0}^{(i)}b_{k}^{(i)}\neq 0,\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\ i=1,2,3.

Then the LC,

\displaystyle\{L_{ij}(k)\}_{i,j\geq 0},\,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
defined in
(
1.2
) are generated by:

{A_{2}(s)A_{3}(t)\over k!}\theta_{s}^{(2)}\theta_{t}^{(3)}(\theta_{s+t}^{(1)})^{-1}\left({(s+t)^{k}\over A_{1}(s+t)}\right)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}{L_{ij}(k)\over i!j!}s^{i}t^{j}

(3.7)

where

\displaystyle\theta^{(i)}(t^{n})=n!b_{n}^{(i)}t^{n},\ \ i=1,2,3.

We note that

\displaystyle\theta^{(i)},\,i=1,2,3,

are the transfer operators from

\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
\displaystyle\{R_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\displaystyle\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
to the monomials, respectively.

Proof.

On one hand, according to (
1.2
) and with sum manipulation, we obtain:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}R_{i}(x)S_{j}(x){s^{i}\over i!}{t^{j}\over j!}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{i+j}L_{ij}(k)P_{k}(x)\right){s^{i}\over i!}{t^{j}\over j!}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(k!\sum_{i,j\geq 0}{L_{ij}(k)\over i!j!}s^{i}t^{j}\right){P_{k}(x)\over k!}.

(3.8)

On the other hand,
by using (
2.6
), we can easily verify that

\theta_{s}^{(2)}\theta_{t}^{(3)}(\theta_{s+t}^{(1)})^{-1}B_{1}((s+t)x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{k}b_{l}^{(2)}b_{k-l}^{(3)}s^{l}t^{k-l}\right)x^{k},

then

B_{2}(xs)B_{3}(xt)=\theta_{s}^{(2)}\theta_{t}^{(3)}(\theta_{s+t}^{(1)})^{-1}B_{1}((s+t)x).

Using the generating function of

\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
we obtain

B_{2}(xs)B_{3}(xt)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\theta_{s}^{(2)}\theta_{t}^{(3)}(\theta_{s+t}^{(1)})^{-1}{(s+t)^{k}\over A_{1}(s+t)}\right){P_{k}(x)\over k!}.

Thus

\sum_{i,j\geq 0}R_{i}(x)S_{j}(x){s^{i}\over i!}{t^{j}\over j!}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(A_{2}(s)A_{3}(t)\theta_{s}^{(2)}\theta_{t}^{(3)}(\theta_{s+t}^{(1)})^{-1}{(s+t)^{k}\over A_{1}(s+t)}\right){P_{k}(x)\over k!}.

Equating the coefficients of
\displaystyle P_{k}(x)
in the above equation and (
3.2
), we obtain (
3.7
) which finishes the proof.
∎

4.
Application to Generalized Gould-Hopper Polynomial Set

The
\displaystyle(d+1)
-fold symmetric generalized Gould-Hopper polynomials,

\displaystyle\{Q_{n}^{(d+1)}(\cdot,a,\mu)\}_{n\geq 0}
, are generated by
[
7
]
:

e^{at^{d+1}}\exp_{\mu}(xt)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{Q_{n}^{(d+1)}(x,a,\mu)}{n!}t^{n},\ a\in\mathbb{C},\ \mu\neq-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{3}{2},-\frac{5}{2},\ldots,

(4.1)

where a PS

\displaystyle\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

is said to be
\displaystyle(d+1)
-fold symmetric,

\displaystyle d=1,2,\ldots,

if

P_{n}\Bigl(e^{\frac{2i\pi}{d+1}}x\Bigr)=e^{\frac{2in\pi}{d+1}}P_{n}(x).

These polynomials constitute a unification of many known families such as:

•

Classical Hermite PS,
\displaystyle H_{n}(x)=Q_{n}^{(2)}(2x,-1,0)
.

•

Gould-Hopper PS,

\displaystyle g_{n}^{m}(x,h)=Q_{n}^{(m)}(x,h,0),

(same notations as in
[
22
]
).

•

Generalized Hermite polynomials
[
30
]
:

H_{n}^{\mu}(x)=Q_{n}^{(2)}(2x,-1,\mu).

(4.2)

The GGHPS
are of Brenke type with transfer power series

\displaystyle A(t)=\exp(at^{d+1})
. They
are the only
\displaystyle(d+1)
-fold symmetric Dunkl-Appell
\displaystyle d
-orthogonal PS
[
7
]
.

Next, we solve the connection and linearization problems associated to
GGHPS and we treat the particular case of generalized Hermite polynomials.

4.1.
Connection Problem

Here, we state the connection formulas for two GGHPS when one or two of the parameters are different and
we give an integral representation of these coefficients. Moreover, the inversion formula, addition and convolution relations, and duplication formula are given.

Theorem 4.1
.

The connection coefficients,

\displaystyle C_{n-i(d+1)}(n),\,0\leq i\leq[\frac{n}{d+1}]
, between
two GGHPS,

\displaystyle\{Q_{n}^{(d+1)}(\cdot,a,\mu_{1})\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\displaystyle\{Q_{n}^{(d+1)}(\cdot,b,\mu_{2})\}_{n\geq 0}
are given by

C_{n-i(d+1)}(n)=\frac{n!}{(n-i(d+1))!}\sum_{k=0}^{i}{\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(n-k(d+1))\over\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(n-k(d+1))}\frac{(-a)^{i-k}}{(i-k)!}\frac{b^{k}}{k!}.

(4.3)

Proof.

By means of (
2.6
), we have

\theta(t^{m}e^{-at^{d+1}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(-a)^{n}\over n!}{\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(n(d+1)+m)\over\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(n(d+1)+m)}t^{n(d+1)+m}.

Thus, by using (
3.1
), (
4.1
) and sum manipulation, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}{m!\over n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=e^{bt^{d+1}}\theta(t^{m}e^{-at^{d+1}})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}\sum_{k=0}^{i}{i\choose k}{\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(k(d+1)+m)\over\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(k(d+1)+m)}b^{i-k}(-a)^{k}\,t^{i(d+1)+m}.

Therefore, for

\displaystyle n=i(d+1)+m
, the desired result holds.
∎

We note that for the particular case

\displaystyle\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
,
(
4.3
)
is reduced to

C_{n-i(d+1)}(n)=\frac{n!(b-a)^{i}}{i!(n-i(d+1))!},\quad 0\leq i\leq\Bigl[\frac{n}{d+1}\Bigr].

For the connection coefficients obtained in Theorem
4.3
, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
\displaystyle\mu_{2}>\mu_{1}>-\frac{1}{2}
, the connection coefficient given by (
4.3
) has the following integral representation,

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{n-i(d+1)}(n)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{n!\beta^{-1}(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2},\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})}{i!(n-i(d+1))!}\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}t^{n-i(d+1)}|t|^{2\mu_{1}}(b-at^{d+1})^{i}\frac{(1-t^{2})^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}}}{{1-t}}\,dt.

Proof.

Using Proposition
2.4
with

\displaystyle f(x)=x^{n-k(d+1)}
and
\displaystyle x=1
, we obtain

{\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(n-k(d+1))\over\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(n-k(d+1))}=\frac{1}{\beta(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2},\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})}\int_{-1}^{1}t^{n-k(d+1)}|t|^{2\mu_{1}}\frac{(1-t^{2})^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}}}{1-t}dt.

Substituting the above equation in (
4.3
), we get:

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{n-i(d+1)}(n)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{i!(n-i(d+1))!}\frac{1}{\beta(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2},\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})}\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}t^{n}|t|^{2\mu_{1}}\frac{(1-t^{2})^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}}}{1-t}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{i}{i\choose k}(-a)^{i-k}(\frac{b}{t^{d+1}})^{k}\right)dt,

from which the desired result follows.
∎

Next, we give some specific expansion relations associated to GGHPS.

\displaystyle\bullet

Explicit and inversion formulas:

The following explicit expression and inversion formula of

\displaystyle\{Q_{n}^{(d+1)}(\cdot,a,\mu\}_{n\geq 0}
can be easily derived from (

Q_{n}^{(d+1)}(x,a,\mu)=n!\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{n}{d+1}]}{a^{k}\over k!\gamma_{\mu}(n-(d+1)k)}\,x^{n-(d+1)k},

(4.4)

and

\frac{x^{n}}{\gamma_{\mu}(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{n}{d+1}]}{(-a)^{k}\over k!(n-(d+1)k)!}Q_{n-(d+1)k}^{(d+1)}(x,a,\mu).

(4.5)

\displaystyle\bullet

Addition and convolution relations:

T_{y}^{\mu}Q_{n}^{(d+1)}(x,a,\mu)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n!y^{n-k}\over k!\gamma_{\mu}(n-k)}Q_{k}^{(d+1)}(x,a,\mu),

(4.6)

2^{\frac{n}{d+1}}T_{y}^{\mu}Q_{n}^{(d+1)}\Bigl(2^{\frac{-1}{d+1}}x,a,\mu\Bigr)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}Q_{k}^{(d+1)}(y,a,\mu)\,Q_{n-k}^{(d+1)}(x,a,\mu),

(4.7)

where
\displaystyle T_{y}^{\mu}=\exp_{\mu}(yD_{\mu}).

For
\displaystyle\mu=0
,
this equation is reduced to the well-known Gould-Hopper convolution type relation
[

and for
\displaystyle m=2,\,h=-1

[
29
]

\displaystyle\bullet

Duplication formula:

Q_{n}^{(d+1)}(\alpha x,a,\mu)=n!\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{n}{d+1}]}{\alpha^{n-k(d+1)}(1-\alpha^{d+1})^{k}a^{k}\over(n-k(d+1))!k!}Q_{n-k(d+1)}^{(d+1)}(x,a,\mu),\ \alpha\neq 0.

4.2.
Linearization Formula

Taking into account the
\displaystyle(d+1)
-fold symmetry property of the GGHPS, any LC
\displaystyle L_{ij}(k)
, in (

) vanishes when
\displaystyle k\neq i+j-r(d+1)
.
Thus, according to

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{ij}(i+j-r(d+1))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{i!j!}{(i+j-r(d+1))!}\sum_{n=0}^{[\frac{i}{d+1}]}\sum_{m=0}^{[\frac{j}{d+1}]}\frac{a_{1}^{n}a_{2}^{m}(-a_{3})^{r-m-n}}{n!m!(r-m-n)!}\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{\mu_{3}}(i+j-(m+n)(d+1))}{\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(i-n(d+1))\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(j-r(d+1))},\ 0\leq r\leq\Bigl[\frac{i+j}{d+1}\Bigr].

We remark that there is no difficulty in proving the corresponding formula for the linearization of any arbitrary number of GGHPSs. We have:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{N}Q_{i_{s}}^{(d+1)}(x,a_{s},\mu_{s})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{[\frac{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{N}}{d+1}]}{i_{1}!\cdots i_{N}!\over(i_{1}+\cdots+i_{N}-r(d+1))!}\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{[\frac{i_{1}}{d+1}]}\cdots\sum_{s_{N}=0}^{[\frac{i_{N}}{d+1}]}{a_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots a_{N}^{s_{N}}(-a_{N+1})^{r-s_{1}-\cdots-s_{N}}\over s_{1}!\cdots s_{N}!(r-s_{1}-\cdots-s_{N})!}\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\gamma_{\mu_{N+1}}(i_{1}+\cdots+i_{N}-(d+1)(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{N}))\over\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(i_{1}-(d+1)s_{1})\cdots\gamma_{\mu_{N}}(i_{N}-(d+1)s_{N})}\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle Q_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{N}-r(d+1)}^{(d+1)}(x,a_{N+1},\mu_{N+1}).

The generalized Hermite polynomials,

(4.8)

Proposition 4.3
.

The following connection relation holds:

\widehat{H}_{n}^{\mu_{2}}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{[n/2]}{(-1)^{k}\,4^{k}\over k!}(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})_{k}\widehat{H}_{n-2k}^{\mu_{1}}(x),\ \mu_{2}>\mu_{1}>-\frac{1}{2},

(4.9)

where
\displaystyle\{\widehat{H}_{n}^{\mu_{i}}\}_{n},\ i=1,2
are the normalized generalized Hermite PS given by

\widehat{H}_{n}^{\mu_{i}}(x)={\gamma_{\mu_{i}}(n)\over n![\frac{n}{2}]!}{H_{n}^{\mu_{i}}(x)}.

Proof.

From what has already been stated, the connection coefficients from
\displaystyle\{H_{n}^{\mu_{2}}\}_{n}
to
\displaystyle\{H_{n}^{\mu_{1}}\}_{n}
are generated by

e^{-t^{2}}\theta(t^{m}e^{t^{2}})=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n},

where
\displaystyle\theta
is the operator defined in (
2.28
).

Making use of the
\displaystyle\theta
-integral representation (
2.29
), intercalate 0 in the interval of integration, we get:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle={t^{m}e^{-t^{2}}\over\beta(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2},\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})}\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e^{t^{2}s^{2}}{s^{m+2\mu_{1}}\over(1-s^{2})^{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}}\left({1\over 1-s}+{(-1)^{m}\over 1+s}\right)\,ds.

It follows, for
\displaystyle m
even and after substituting
\displaystyle u=s^{2}
, that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle={t^{m}e^{-t^{2}}\over\beta(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2},\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})}\int_{0}^{1}e^{ut^{2}}u^{\frac{m-1}{2}+\mu_{1}}(1-u)^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}-1}du

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(-1)^{n}\over n!}{\beta(\mu_{1}+\frac{m+1}{2},\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}+n)\over\beta(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2},\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})}t^{m+2n},

where the term by term integration is justified by the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
2.3
.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\beta(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2}+k,\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}+n)\over\beta(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2},\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle={\Gamma(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2}+k)\Gamma(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}+n)\Gamma(\mu_{2}+\frac{1}{2})\over\Gamma(\mu_{2}+n+k+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle={\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(2k)\over 2^{2k}k!}{2^{2(k+n)}(k+n)!\over\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(2(k+n))}(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})_{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle={\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(m)\over\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(m+2n)}{4^{n}([m/2]+n)!\over[m/2]!}(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})_{n}.

Thus, by virtue of (
2.20
) and (
2.30
), we obtain

\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(-1)^{n}\over n!}{\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(m)4^{n}([\frac{m}{2}]+n)!\over\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(m+2n)[\frac{m}{2}]!}(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})_{n}t^{m+2n}.

For
\displaystyle m
odd, similar computations lead to

\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(-1)^{n}\over n!}{\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(m)\over\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(m+2n)}{4^{n}([\frac{m}{2}]+n)!\over[\frac{m}{2}]!}(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})_{n}t^{m+2n}.

Therefore, for
\displaystyle m=0,1,2,3,\ldots
, we have:

\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{n!}C_{m}(n)t^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(-1)^{n}\over n!}{\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(m)\over\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(m+2n)}{4^{n}([\frac{m}{2}]+n)!\over[\frac{m}{2}]!}(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})_{n}t^{m+2n},

Thus, for
\displaystyle\ k=0,1,2,\ldots,[\frac{n}{2}],
we get

C_{n-2k}(n)={(-1)^{k}\over k!}{n!\over(n-2k)!}{4^{k}[\frac{n}{2}]!\over[\frac{n}{2}-k]!}{\gamma_{\mu_{1}}(n-2k)\over\gamma_{\mu_{2}}(n)}(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})_{k}.

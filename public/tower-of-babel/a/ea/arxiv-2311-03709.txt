Geodesic Envelopes in the Thurston Metric on Teichmüller space
Assaf Bar-Natan

Abstract
. The Thurston metric on Teichmüller space, first introduced by W. P. Thurston is an asymmetric metric on Teichmüller space defined by d T ​ h ​ ( X , Y ) = 1 2 ​ log ​ sup α l α ​ ( Y ) l α ​ ( X ) d_{Th}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\log\sup_{\alpha}\frac{l_{\alpha}(Y)}{l_{\alpha}(X)} . This metric is geodesic, but geodesics are far from unique. In this thesis, we show that in the once-punctured torus, and in the four-times punctured sphere, geodesics stay a uniformly-bounded distance from each other. In other words, we show that the width of the geodesic envelope , E ⁡ ( X , Y ) E(X,Y) between any pair of points X , Y ∈ 𝒯 ⁡ ( S ) X,Y\in\mathcal{T}(S) (where S = S 1 , 1 S=S_{1,1} or S = S 0 , 4 S=S_{0,4} ) is bounded uniformly. To do this, we first identify extremal geodesics in E ​ n ​ v ​ ( X , Y ) Env(X,Y) , and show that these correspond to stretch vectors , proving a conjecture from [ 6 ] . We then compute Fenchel-Nielsen twisting along these paths, and use these computations, along with estimates on earthquake path lengths, to prove the main theorem.

Introduction

Let

be its Teichmüller space. In

on

, answering a question of Huang,

[
6
]
in the affirmative. We then

used to show that Thurston geodesics in these Teichmüller spaces

In Thurston’s paper on the Thurston metric
[

]

\bm{S}_{X}

In Section

2.1.
Teichmuller space & Thurston Metric

Throughout this paper, we let
S
be an orientable surface with no
boundary components, and negative Euler characteristic. In
particular, this means that
S
can be endowed with a hyperbolic
metric.

Definition 4
.

A
marking
on
S
is a homeomorphism

f:S\to X
, where
X
is a surface endowed with a hyperbolic metric.

Two markings
f:S\to X
and
g:S\to Y
are equivalent
if
fg^{-1}
is homotopic to an isometry.

Definition 5
.

We define the
Teichmüller space
of

S
, denoted by
\mathcal{T}(S)
, to be the space
of all markings on
S
up to equivalence.

We think of points in Teichmüller space
as a pair consisting of a metric space
X
, together with a marking
map
\varphi
.

If
\gamma
is a curve, arc, or arc segment on
S
, then we define

l_{\gamma}:\mathcal{T}(S)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by sending
X
to

l_{\gamma}(X)
, the
length
of the geodesic representative of

\gamma
on
X
relative to its endpoints.

Definition 6
.

We define the
Thurston metric

d_{Th}:\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(S)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

by:

\displaystyle d_{Th}(X,Y)=\sup_{\alpha}\log\left(\frac{l_{\alpha}(Y)}{l_{\alpha}(X)}\right)

where the supremum ranges over all simple closed curves
\alpha

contained in
S
.

Lemma 7
.

For any
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}(S)
,

d_{Th}(X,Y)
is equal to:

•

d_{Th}(X,Y)=\sup_{\alpha}\log\left(\frac{l_{\alpha}(Y)}{l_{\alpha}(X)}\right)
(as above)

•

L(X,Y)=\inf_{f\sim id}\log(L_{f})
, where
f:X\to Y
is
a Lipschitz map homotopic to the identity, and
L_{f}
is the
Lipschitz constant of
f
.

•

D(X,Y)=\inf_{f\sim id}\sup_{p\in X}\log(\|Df_{p}\|)
, where
f:X\to Y
is a homeomorphism that
is once differentiable almost everywhere.

Proof.

In
[
15
]
, Thurston shows that

L(X,Y)=d_{Th}(X,Y)
. It suffices to show that

d_{Th}(X,Y)\leq D(X,Y)\leq L(X,Y)
. The first
inequality follows because
\frac{l_{\alpha}(Y)}{l_{\alpha}(X)}

is always bounded above by
\sup_{p}\|Df_{p}\|
. The
latter inequality is more subtle. Trivially,
if in the definition of
L(X,Y)
,

f
is taken to be differentiable, then the inequality
follows by the fact that Lipschitz constants
give upper bounds for derivatives. In fact,
Thurston
[
15
]
explicitly
constructs a map
f
that realizes
the infimum in
L(X,Y)
, and this map is a
homeomorphism that is differentiable almost
everywhere. Thus, the inequality holds.
∎

Claim
.

For any
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{T}(S)

•

d_{Th}(X,Y)\geq 0

•

d_{Th}(X,Y)=0
if and only if

•

d_{Th}(X,Z)\leq d_{Th}(X,Y)+d_{Th}(Y,Z)

We use the third characterization of

•

.

•

If

a homeomorphism

with
\|Df_{p}\|=1
for all

and

•

This follows immediately from the chain rule.

∎

2.2.
Shearing Co-ordinates & Laminations

Throughout this section, let
(X,\varphi)

be a point in
\mathcal{T}(S)

\tilde{X}

if there exists a sequence of multicurves which
converge to
\lambda

alternate characterization of chain-recurrence is
the following:

Definition 9
.

A geodesic lamination
\lambda

if for any arc segment
I

\lambda
, and for
any

\alpha

, where

is the Hausdorff
distance.

In this sense, the space of chain-recurrent laminations
is the closure of the space of multicurves,
equipped with Hausdorff convergence
[
4
]

A
measured lamination
is a lamination

\lambda

\mu_{\lambda}

\lambda
transversely

\lambda

\lambda

As an example, consider a

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pair-of-pants decomposition
of a surface

and

be the (possibly same) pairs of pants

\tilde{c}
be a lift of

, and choose

\tilde{P}_{i}(c)
of

which are adjacent
to
\tilde{c}
on either side of
\tilde{c}
.

Let

and
c\neq c_{2}
be cuffs of
P_{1}(c)
and
P_{2}(c)

respectively, and let
\tilde{c}_{i}
be the lifts of

\tilde{P}_{i}(c)

Let
\eta_{i}
be the unique simple geodesic

\tilde{c}_{i}
with start point on
\tilde{c}
and

\tilde{c}_{i}
, which is normal
to both
\tilde{c}
and

We define the

to be the signed distance between

and

,
so that the sign is

to

. Choosing different

, and

Stretch Laminations, Chain-Recurrence, and Thurston
Geodesics

Definition 13
.

Let

A

(resp.

)

\gamma:I\to M

(resp.

If

\gamma(b)

Throughout this thesis, a “geodesic” means a

Given

, we define

\displaystyle\Outenv(X)

\displaystyle=\{Z\in\mathcal{T}(S):Z\textrm{ lies on a forward geodesic
starting at }X\}

\displaystyle\In(Y)

\displaystyle=\{Z\in\mathcal{T}(S):Z\textrm{ lies on a backwards geodesic
starting at }Y\}

For

, we define the

\Env(X,Y)=\Outenv(X)\cap\In(Y)

Given

, we can consider a sequence of multicurves

\alpha_{i}
such that

. The space of geodesic

]

converges to some

Moreover, if

is disjoint from
\lambda_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}

Thus, it makes sense to define the

Given

•

•

•

This family of metrics on

is called the

associated to

and
\lambda

\Env(X,Y)

[
4
]
.

Let

Remark 28

\alpha
for time
\mathrm{Twist}_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{L},Y_{-t}^{R})
, we
can estimate the length ratio of
\beta
by:

\displaystyle\frac{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{R})}{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{L})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{L})+l_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{L})|\mathrm{Twist}_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{R},Y_{-t}^{L})|}{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{L})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\ast}}}{{\prec}}1+\frac{l_{0}e^{-t}}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}4\left(e^{-t}\log\coth(l_{0})+\log\coth(l_{0}e^{-t})\right)

Where we used the fact that
l_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{L})\leq 4l_{0}e^{s}
.

We remark that by our choice of
\varepsilon
, we have that

\frac{1}{l_{0}e^{-t}}\geq\log\coth(l_{0}e^{-t})>0
, meaning that:

\displaystyle\frac{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{R})}{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{L})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\ast}}}{{\prec}}1+4\frac{l_{0}e^{-t}}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\left(\frac{e^{-t}}{l_{0}}+\frac{e^{t}}{l_{0}}\right)=1+\frac{1}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}(e^{-2t}+1)

Which is uniformly bounded in
l_{0}
and in
t
.
∎

Lemma 47
.

Assume that
\varepsilon\leq l_{0}\leq 2
, and that
l_{0}e^{-t}<\varepsilon
from
the previous lemma. Then
d_{Th}(Y_{-t}^{L},Y_{-t}^{R})
is bounded
uniformly.

Proof.

As in the previous lemma, we must estimate

\displaystyle\frac{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{R})}{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{L})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\ast}}}{{\prec}}1+\frac{l_{0}e^{-t}}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}4\left(e^{-t}\log\coth(l_{0})+\log\coth(l_{0}e^{-t})\right)

The
\log\coth(l_{0})
term is bounded uniformly,
so it suffices to observe that

l_{0}e^{-t}\log\coth(l_{0}e^{-t})
is
bounded uniformly.
∎

Lemma 48
.

Assume that
2\geq l_{0}
, and that
l_{0}e^{-t}<\varepsilon
from the
previous lemmas. Then
d_{Th}(Y_{-t}^{L},Y_{-t}^{R})
is bounded
uniformly.

Proof.

Let
t_{0}=\log(l_{0})
, and set
s=t-t_{0}
. Note
that
t_{0}\geq\log(2)>0
and that
s\in(-\log(\varepsilon),\infty)

is bounded from below. With this notation,
and the same argumens as before, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{R})}{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{L})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\ast}}}{{\prec}}1+4\frac{e^{-s}}{s}\|\left(e^{-s-t_{0}}\log\coth(e^{t_{0}})+\log\coth(e^{-s})\right)\|

As
t_{0}
ranges from
0
to
\infty
,
we have that
\frac{1}{e^{t_{0}}}>\log\coth(e^{t_{0}})>0
,
and similarly
\frac{1}{e^{-s}}>\log\coth(e^{-s})>0
,
giving:

\displaystyle\frac{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{R})}{l_{\beta}(Y_{-t}^{L})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\ast}}}{{\prec}}1+4\frac{e^{-s}}{s}\left(e^{-s}+e^{s})\right)

Which is bounded uniformly in
s
, since

s
is bounded from below by
-\log(\varepsilon)
.
∎

Together, the previous three lemmas tell us:

Lemma 49
.

Let
\varepsilon=\min(\varepsilon_{S_{1,1}},\varepsilon_{S_{0,4}},\log(2))
,
and assume that
l_{0}e^{-t}\leq\varepsilon
. Then

d_{Th}(Y_{-t}^{L},Y_{-t}^{R})
is bounded uniformly.

4.4.
The Thick Part of
\mathcal{T}(S)

In this subsection, we show that

d_{Th}(Y_{-t}^{L},Y_{-t}^{R})
is uniformly bounded
when
l_{0}e^{-t}
is large.

Lemma 50
.

In the setting of the above lemmas, if
1<l_{0}e^{-t}
, then

d_{Th}(Y_{-t}^{L},Y_{-t}^{R})
is uniformly bounded.

Proof.

We first treat the case when
S=S_{1,1}

is the once-punctured torus. We recall
that
l_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{L})=l_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{R})=2l_{0}
, and by Proposition
2
,
it suffices to verify that for
l_{0}e^{-t}>1
, we
have that

\displaystyle e^{l_{0}e^{-t}}\mathrm{Twist}_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{L},Y_{-t}^{R})

\displaystyle=4e^{l_{0}e^{-t}}(e^{-t}\log\coth(l_{0})-\log\coth(l_{0}e^{-t}))

\displaystyle\leq 4e^{l_{0}e^{-t}}e^{-t}\log\coth(l_{0})+4e^{l_{0}e^{-t}}\log\coth(l_{0}e^{-t})

is bounded uniformly. Note that

1<\coth(l_{0})<\coth(l_{0}e^{-t})
,
so it suffices to observe that
e^{2l_{0}e^{-t}}\log\coth(l_{0}e^{-t})

is bounded uniformly in
t
and
l_{0}
, as long
as
l_{0}e^{-t}>1
.

The case when
S=S_{0,4}
is similar; we must verify
that
e^{2l_{0}e^{-t}}\mathrm{Twist}_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{L},Y_{-t}^{R})
is uniformly bounded.
∎

Remark 51
.

It is a striking coincidence that the twisting
along a simple closed curve is decaying
at least as fast as
e^{-2l_{0}e^{-t}}
in the
case of the once-punctured torus and the four-times punctured
sphere. In higher complexity surfaces, this is not
generally the case. In fact, the factor of
\frac{1}{2}
is
crucial in Proposition
2
,
since
e^{(2+\varepsilon)l_{0}e^{-t}}\log\coth(l_{0}e^{-t})

is not uniformly bounded for any
\varepsilon>0
.

In fact, similar computations can be performed to estimate

d_{Th}(Y_{-t}^{R},Y_{-t}^{L})
, giving:

Lemma 52
.

d_{Th}(Y_{-t}^{L},Y_{-t}^{R})
and
d_{Th}(Y_{-t}^{R},Y_{-t}^{L})
are
uniformly bounded.

Proof.

We’ve seen the cases for when
l_{0}e^{-t}<\varepsilon

and when
l_{0}e^{-t}>1
.
then let
Y_{0}^{R}
(resp.
Y_{0}^{L}
) be the point on
Y_{-t}^{R}

(resp.
Y_{0}^{L}
) for which
l_{\alpha}(Y_{0}^{L})=1
.

Let
t
be such that

\varepsilon\leq l_{0}e^{-t}\leq 1
, by the triangle inequality,

d_{Th}(Y_{-t}^{R},Y_{-t}^{L})\leq 2(1-\varepsilon)+d_{Th}(Y_{0}^{R},Y_{0}^{L})

which is uniformly bounded. A similar
argument can be shown for

d_{Th}(Y_{-t}^{L},Y_{-t}^{R})

∎

Before proving Lemma

Lemma 53
.

The set

is a closed

\displaystyle In(Y,\alpha)=\{X\in\mathcal{T}(S):\tau_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{L})\leq\tau_{\alpha}(X)\leq\tau_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{R}),l_{\alpha}(X)=l_{\alpha}(Y)e^{-t}\textrm{for some $t$}\}

Where
\tau_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{R})

and

and

.

Proof.

Denote the second set by
A(Y)
. Note that
Y_{-t}^{R}
and

Y_{-t}^{L}
are two smooth paths in
\mathcal{T}(S)

. In particular, they split
\mathcal{T}(S)
into four complementary

), one of which is
A(Y)
.

If
X\in A(Y)

\mathrm{Stretch}(X,\lambda^{L},t)

and note that this path must
Y_{-t}^{R}
at

.
The contatenation of

from

Conversely, assume that
X\in In(Y,\alpha)
but not in

\tau_{\alpha}(X)>\tau_{\alpha}(Y_{-t}^{R})

Let

∎

.

Let

Serre’s theorem for coherent sheaves via Auslander’s techniques
Henning Krause

Abstract
. For an abelian category and a distinguished object with a graded endomorphism ring a necessary and sufficient criterion is given so that the category is equivalent to the abelian quotient of the category of finitely presented graded modules modulo the Serre subcategory of finite length modules. A particular example is the category of coherent sheaves on a projective variety, following a theorem of Serre from 1955. The proof uses Auslander’s theory of coherent functors, and there are no noetherianess assumptions. A theorem of Lenzing for representations of hereditary algebras is given as an application.

14A22 (primary), 16G10, 18E10 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

Serre’s theorem relating coherent sheaves on a projective variety to
graded modules over some appropriate graded ring is a cornerstone of
non-commutative geometry, for instance by the non-commutative analogue
of this theorem due to Artin and Zhang
[
1
,
21
]
. In this
note we revisit the theory, motivated by work of Lenzing which
introduces for any finite dimensional hereditary algebra of infinite
representation type a category of coherent sheaves
[
15
]
. The
corresponding graded ring is the preprojective algebra
[
7
]
,
which appeared already in work of Gel’fand and Ponomarev
[
9
]

as well as in work of Dlab and Ringel
[
8
]
. The novel aspect
of the present work is a new formulation and proof of the analogue of
Serre’s theorem which is based on Auslander’s theory of coherent
functors
[
3
]
. In particular, we do not impose any
noetherianess assumptions. This is necessary because the preprojective
algebra is noetherian only when the corresponding hereditary algebra
is of tame representation type
[
6
,
7
]
.

2.
An analogue of Serre’s theorem

We formulate the analogue of Serre’s theorem in the setting of
Krull–Schmidt categories. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an additive category that is

Krull–Schmidt
. Thus any object
X
admits a finite
decomposition
X=\bigoplus_{i}X_{i}
such that each
X_{i}
is
indecomposable with a local endomorphism ring. The
radical

\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{C}
is the ideal given by subgroups

\operatorname{Rad}(X,Y)\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)\qquad(X,Y\in\mathcal{C})

consisting of all morphisms
X\to Y
such that for any pair of
decompositions
X=\bigoplus_{i}X_{i}
and
Y=\bigoplus_{j}Y_{j}
into
indecomposable objects no component
X_{i}\to Y_{j}
is an
isomorphism. Let
\operatorname{Rad}^{0}\mathcal{C}
denote the ideal of all morphisms in
\mathcal{C}

and for
n\geq 0
we set
\operatorname{Rad}^{n+1}\mathcal{C}=(\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{C})(\operatorname{Rad}^{n}\mathcal{C})
. We say
that the
length
of a morphism
X\to Y
is bounded by
n
if all
morphisms
X^{\prime}\to Y
from
\operatorname{Rad}^{n}\mathcal{C}
factor through
X\to Y
.

For a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring
A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{n}
we consider
the category
\operatorname{GrMod}A
of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded right
A
-modules. Let
\operatorname{grmod}A
denote the
full subcategory of finitely presented modules and
\operatorname{grproj}A
denotes
the full subcategory of finitely generated projective modules. We
write
\operatorname{grmod}_{0}A
for the full subcategory of all finite length
modules and always assume
\operatorname{grmod}_{0}A\subseteq\operatorname{grmod}A
; this is
automatic if the ring is right noetherian.

We say that a graded ring
A
is
right coherent
if
\operatorname{grmod}A

is an abelian category, and
A
is
semiperfect
if

\operatorname{grproj}A
is Krull–Schmidt. These properties hold, for instance,
when
A
is a right noetherian algebra over a field such that each
homogeneous component
A_{n}
is finite dimensional; see
[
2
,
11
]

for the Krull–Schmidt property.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},C,\sigma)
be a triple consisting of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
,
a distinguished object
C
, and an equivalence

\sigma\colon\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{A}
. Suppose that
\operatorname{Hom}(C,\sigma^{n}C)=0
for all
n<0

and that the graded ring

A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{Hom}(C,\sigma^{n}C)

is right coherent
and semiperfect with
\operatorname{grmod}_{0}A\subseteq\operatorname{grmod}A
. Then the assignment

X\mapsto\Gamma_{*}(X):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}(C,\sigma^{n}X)\qquad(X\in\mathcal{A})

admits a partial left adjoint
T\colon\operatorname{grmod}A\to\mathcal{A}
,
which is right exact and determined by
T(A)=C
. The functor
T
is
exact and induces an equivalence

(\operatorname{grmod}A)/(\operatorname{grmod}_{0}A)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{A}

if
and only if the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
consisting of all
direct summands of finite direct sums of objects
\sigma^{n}C
with

n\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfies the following:

(A1)

Every object
X\in\mathcal{A}
admits an epimorphism
C\to X
with

C\in\mathcal{C}
, which can be chosen in
\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{C}
when
X\in\mathcal{C}
.

(A2)

A morphism in
\mathcal{C}
has finite length if it is an
epimorphism in
\mathcal{A}
.

Fix a triple
(\mathcal{A},C,\sigma)
as above. The algebra

A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
is the
orbit algebra
of
C
with
multiplication given by
xy=(\sigma^{q}x)\circ y
for
x\in A_{p}
and

y\in A_{q}
. The assignment
X\mapsto\Gamma_{*}(X)
yields an equivalence

\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{grproj}A
and
T\colon\operatorname{grmod}A\to\mathcal{A}
is the right exact
functor extending

\operatorname{grproj}A\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{C}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}.

Thus we have the following
diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{grmod}A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{GrMod}A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{*}}

and
\Gamma_{*}
corestricts to a right adjoint of
T
if and only if

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is contravariantly finite so that
\Gamma_{*}(X)
is a
finitely presented
A
-module for each
X\in\mathcal{A}
; see
Remark
10
. Nonetheless, we have for all
X\in\operatorname{grmod}A

and
Y\in\mathcal{A}
the adjointness
isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(T(X),Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,\Gamma_{*}(Y)).

The conditions (A1)–(A2) express the ‘ampleness’ of the pair

(C,\sigma)
. The prototypical example is a theorem of Serre

[
21
]
and we refer to
[
1
,
18
]
for non-commuative
analogues.

Example 2

(Serre)
.

Consider the projective variety
\mathbb{P}^{r}(K)
given by an
algebraically closed field
K
and an integer
r\geq 1
. Let

\operatorname{coh}\mathbb{P}^{r}(K)
denote the category of coherent sheaves on

\mathbb{P}^{r}(K)
. The assignment

\mathscr{F}\mapsto\Gamma_{*}(\mathscr{F})=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{O},\mathscr{F}(n))

yields a
functor
\operatorname{coh}\mathbb{P}^{r}(K)\to\operatorname{grmod}A
to the category of graded modules
over the orbit algebra of the structure sheaf

A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{O},\mathscr{O}(n))\cong K[t_{0},\ldots,t_{r}].

Taking a module
M
to its associated sheaf

\widetilde{M}
provides a left ajoint functor, which is exact and
annihilates all modules of finite length. In

[
21
, N
o
65]
it is shown that the
canonical morphism
\widetilde{\Gamma_{*}(\mathscr{F})}\to\mathscr{F}
is
an isomorphism, while kernel and cokernel of the canonical morphism

M\to\Gamma_{*}(\widetilde{M})
are of finite length. An equivalent
statement is that
M\mapsto\widetilde{M}
induces an
equivalence

(\operatorname{grmod}A)/(\operatorname{grmod}_{0}A)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{coh}\mathbb{X}.

Let us comment on the notions ‘finite length’ and
‘torsion’ for graded modules, because it is common to work
modulo the category of torsion modules in the context of Serre’s theorem.

Remark 3
.

Let
A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
be a graded ring and suppose that the ring

A_{0}=A/A_{\geq 1}
is semisimple. Then a graded
A
-module
M
has finite
length if and only if
M
is finitely generated and torsion, so
M_{n}=0
for

n\gg 0
. To see this fix a finitely
generated module
M
and consider the sequence

\cdots\twoheadrightarrow M/M_{\geq 2}\twoheadrightarrow M/M_{\geq 1}\twoheadrightarrow M/M_{\geq 0}\twoheadrightarrow M/M_{\geq-1}\twoheadrightarrow\cdots

which stabilises in the right
direction since
M
is finitely generated. It stabilises in both
directions if and only if
M
has finite length, since each
subquotient has finite length (using the assumption

\operatorname{grmod}_{0}A\subseteq\operatorname{grmod}A
so that each
subquotient is finitely generated over
A_{0}
).

3.
A relative Auslander formula

The proof of the analogue of Serre’s theorem is based on a relative
version of Auslander’s formula; it is established in this section
and may be of independent interest. For instance, the
Gabriel–Popescu theorem for Grothendieck categories is another
result that can be explained in terms of this Auslander formula.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an additive category. Following Auslander
[
3
]

an additive functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}
into the category of abelian
groups is called
finitely presented

or
coherent
if it admits a presentation

(3.1)

\operatorname{Hom}(-,X)\to\operatorname{Hom}(-,Y)\to F\to 0.

In that case
X\to Y
is called the
presenting morphism
.
Let
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
denote the category of finitely presented functors

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}
. The assignment
X\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(-,X)
yields the fully
faithful
Yoneda functor

\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
. We collect some basic
facts from
[
3
]
which will be used throughout without
further reference. These properties reflect the fact that

\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
is nothing but the completion of
\mathcal{C}
under finite
colimits.

A morphism
X\to Y
is a
weak kernel

of a morphism
Y\to Z
if the induced sequence

\operatorname{Hom}(C,X)\to\operatorname{Hom}(C,Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}(C,Z)
is exact for all objects
C
.

Lemma 4
.

For an additive category
\mathcal{C}
we have the following.

(1)

The category
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
is additive and every morphism has a
cokernel; it is abelian iff every morphism
in
\mathcal{C}
has a weak kernel.

(2)

The Yoneda functor
\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
admits an exact left
adjoint if
\mathcal{C}
is abelian.

(3)

An additive functor
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
into a category with
cokernels extends to a right exact functor
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
.

(4)

An additive functor

f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
extends to a right exact functor

f^{*}\colon\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{D}
, and
f^{*}
is fully faithful if and only
if
f
is fully faithful.

Proof.

See
[
3
]
or Sections 2.1–2.3 in
[
12
]
.
∎

Example 5
.

For a graded ring
A
the inclusion
\operatorname{grproj}A\to\operatorname{grmod}A
induces
an equivalence
\operatorname{mod}(\operatorname{grproj}A)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{grmod}A
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category. A functor
F\in\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}
with
presentation (
3.1
) is called
effaceable
if it belongs to
the kernel of the left adjoint of the Yoneda functor
\mathcal{A}\to\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}
. An
equivalent condition is that the presenting morphism

\phi\colon X\to Y
is an epimorphism, because the left adjoint
preserves cokernels, so sends

F=\operatorname{Coker}\operatorname{Hom}(-,\phi)
to
\operatorname{Coker}\phi
. We write
\operatorname{eff}\mathcal{A}
for the full
subcategory of effaceable functors and note that it is a Serre
subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition 6

(Auslander)
.

The right exact functor
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
extending the identity

\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
is exact and induces an equivalence

(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A})/(\operatorname{eff}\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{A}.\qed

This result from
[
3
]
is also known as
Auslander’s
formula

[
16
]
. We need the following relative version.
Let
i\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
denote the inclusion of
a full additive subcategory. We view the induced functor

i^{*}\colon\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}
as an inclusion and set

\operatorname{eff}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C}):=\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\cap\operatorname{eff}\mathcal{A}.

The subcategory
\mathcal{C}

generates

\mathcal{A}
if every object
X\in\mathcal{A}
admits an
epimorphism
C\to X
with
C\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
a full additive
subcategory such that
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
is abelian. Then
\mathcal{C}
generates

\mathcal{A}
if and only if the right exact functor
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
extending
the inclusion
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
is exact and induces an equivalence

(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C})/(\operatorname{eff}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C}))\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{A}.

Proof.

We write the right exact functor
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
as composite

\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{i^{*}}\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{A}

with
i^{*}
induced by the inclusion
i\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
. The functor
i^{*}
is fully
faithful, and it is exact when any weak kernel sequence in
\mathcal{C}
is exact
in
\mathcal{A}
. The latter property follows when
\mathcal{C}
generates

\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, the condition that
\mathcal{C}
generates is equivalent to
the property of
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
to be essentially surjective.

Now suppose that
\mathcal{C}
generates
\mathcal{A}
. Then the exact functor
i^{*}

induces a functor

(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C})/(\operatorname{eff}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C}))\to(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A})/(\operatorname{eff}\mathcal{A})

by the definition of

\operatorname{eff}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C})
. The morphisms in the
localised categories are computed via a calculus of fractions. From
this it follows that the functor is fully faithful when the following
cofinality condition holds: Every
F\in\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}
admits a morphism

F^{\prime}\to F
such that
F^{\prime}\in\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
and the image under

\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{A}
is an isomorphism; cf.
[
12
, Lemma 1.2.5]
. For this cofinality see Lemma
8
below,
and it remains to compose this functor with the
equivalence
(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A})/(\operatorname{eff}\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{A}
.
∎

.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category and

a full additive

\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
is abelian and
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{A}
. Then every
F\in\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}

F^{\prime}\to F

F^{\prime}\in\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
and the image under

\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{A}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{A}

Let
X\to Y

. We find objects

Remark 9
.

One may think of the relative Auslander formula as a variation of
the Popescu–Gabriel theorem
[
19
]
which says for a
Grothendieck category
\mathcal{A}
and a generator
G
that the left adjoint
of
\operatorname{Hom}(G,-)\colon\mathcal{A}\to\operatorname{Mod}A
with
A=\operatorname{End}(G)
induces an
equivalence

(\operatorname{Mod}A)/\mathcal{L}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{A}

for some appropriate localising
subcategory
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}A
. In fact, we may take for

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
the full subcategory all coproducts of copies of

G
. Then the colimit preserving composite

\operatorname{Mod}A\to\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
that identifies
A
with
G
induces equivalences

(\operatorname{Mod}A)/\mathcal{L}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C})/(\operatorname{eff}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C}))\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{A}.

Remark 10
.

For a full additive subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
the right exact functor
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}

admits a right adjoint if and only if the subcategory
\mathcal{C}
is

contravariantly finite
, so each object
X\in\mathcal{A}
admits a
morphism
\pi\colon C\to X
such that
C\in\mathcal{C}
and each morphism

C^{\prime}\to X
with
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
factors through
\pi
. This condition means
that
\operatorname{Hom}(-,X)|_{\mathcal{C}}
belongs to
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
for all
X\in\mathcal{A}
, so

X\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(-,X)|_{\mathcal{C}}
provides the right adjoint.

4.
Serre’s theorem via Auslander’s techniques

We use the relative Auslander formula from
Proposition
7
to prove Theorem
1
. We
need some preparations and fix an additive category
\mathcal{C}
that is Krull–Schmidt. Let

\operatorname{mod}_{0}\mathcal{C}
denote the category of all additive functors
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}

that are of finite length. For an object
X\in\mathcal{C}

set

S_{X}=\operatorname{Hom}(-,X)/\operatorname{Rad}(-,X).

We note that
S_{X}
is simple when

\operatorname{End}(X)
is local, and
S_{X}\cong\bigoplus_{i}S_{X_{i}}
for any finite
decomposition
X=\bigoplus_{i}X_{i}
.

Given an indecomposable object
X
, a radical morphism
X^{\prime}\to X
is
called
right almost split
if all radical morphisms terminating
at
X
factor through
X^{\prime}\to X
. Simple functors and their connection
to almost split morphisms are discussed in great detail in

[
4
, Chapter II]
. In our context the following is needed.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Krull–Schmidt category. Then

\operatorname{mod}_{0}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
holds if and only if every indecomposable
object in
\mathcal{C}
admits a right almost split morphism.

Proof.

A functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}
is simple if and only if

F\cong S_{X}
for some indecomposable object
X\in\mathcal{C}
; see

[
4
, Proposition II.1.8]
. Here one uses that
\mathcal{C}
is
Krull–Schmidt. On the other hand, for an indecomposable object

X\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a right almost split morphism
X^{\prime}\to X
if
and only if
S_{X}
belongs to
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
, because a right almost split morphism

X^{\prime}\to X
amounts to an epimorphism
\operatorname{Hom}(-,X^{\prime})\to\operatorname{Rad}(-,X)
.
∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Krull–Schmidt category such that
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
is
abelian and
\operatorname{mod}_{0}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
. A functor
F\in\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}

with presentation (
3.1
)
belongs to
\operatorname{mod}_{0}\mathcal{C}
if and only if the
morphism
X\to Y
in
\mathcal{C}
has finite length.

Proof.

We write
\operatorname{rad}F
for the intersection of all maximal subobjects of

F
and set
\operatorname{rad}^{n+1}F=\operatorname{rad}(\operatorname{rad}^{n}F)
for all
n\geq 0
. For

H_{X}=\operatorname{Hom}(-,X)
observe that
\operatorname{rad}^{n}H_{X}=\operatorname{Rad}^{n}(-,X)
for all

n\geq 0
; see
[
4
, Proposition II.1.8]
. Thus the presentation of
F
induces an epimorphism

S_{Y}\to F/(\operatorname{rad}F)
. Then the radical filtration

\cdots\subseteq\operatorname{rad}^{2}F\subseteq\operatorname{rad}^{1}F\subseteq\operatorname{rad}^{0}F=F

lies in
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
and each subquotient

(\operatorname{rad}^{n}F)/(\operatorname{rad}^{n+1}F)

has
finite length. It follows that
F
has finite length if and only if

\operatorname{rad}^{n}F=0
for
n\gg 0
. The presenting morphism
X\to Y
has by definition
length at most
n
if and only if the epimorphism
H_{Y}\to F
factors
through
H_{Y}\to H_{Y}/(\operatorname{rad}^{n}H_{Y})
. On the other hand,
H_{Y}\to F
maps

\operatorname{rad}^{n}H_{Y}
onto
\operatorname{rad}^{n}F
, and therefore it factors
through
H_{Y}\to H_{Y}/(\operatorname{rad}^{n}H_{Y})
if and only
if
\operatorname{rad}^{n}F=0
.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
a full additive
subcategory. Suppose that
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
is abelian and that

\operatorname{mod}_{0}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
. Then the right exact functor

\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
extending the inclusion
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
is exact and induces
an equivalence

(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C})/(\operatorname{mod}_{0}\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{A}

if and only if
\mathcal{C}
generates
\mathcal{A}
and
\operatorname{mod}_{0}\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{eff}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

The assertion is an immediate consequence of Proposition
7
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We recall the equivalences

\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{grproj}A\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{grmod}A.

Our
assumption
\operatorname{grmod}_{0}A\subseteq\operatorname{grmod}A
implies that every
indecomposable object in
\mathcal{C}
admits a right almost split morphism;
see Lemma
11
.

Now we apply Lemma
13
and need to show that conditions
(A1)–(A2) in Theorem
1
are equivalent to the
conditions in Lemma
13
. This is clear for the first pair
of conditions expressing the fact that
\mathcal{C}
is generating
\mathcal{A}
. It
follows from Lemma
12
that (A2) holds if and only
if

\operatorname{eff}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\cap\operatorname{eff}\mathcal{A}\subseteq\operatorname{mod}_{0}\mathcal{C}.

For the other inclusion it suffices that all simple objects in

\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
belong to
\operatorname{eff}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C})
. This means that for every
indecomposable
X\in\mathcal{C}

the right almost split morphism
X^{\prime}\to X
is an
epimorphism. But this is precisely the extra condition in (A1) that
there is an epimorphism
X^{\prime}\to X
in
\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{C}
.
∎

5.
Hereditary algebras

Let
K
be a field and
\Lambda
a hereditary finite dimensional

K
-algebra. This means
\operatorname{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n}(-,-)=0
for all
n>1
. We consider
the category
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
of finitely presented
\Lambda
-modules. This
category admits a canonical decomposition. For an additive category

\mathcal{C}
we use the notation
\mathcal{C}=\bigvee_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}
and call this a

decomposition
when each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a full additive subcategory
such that each object in
\mathcal{C}
can be written as a coproduct

\coprod_{i}C_{i}
with
C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
for all
i
, and

\mathcal{C}_{i}\cap\big(\bigvee_{j\neq i}\mathcal{C}_{j}\big)=0
for all
i
.

Assume that
\Lambda
is connected and of infinite
representation type. Then there is a decomposition

\operatorname{mod}\Lambda=\mathcal{P}\vee\mathcal{R}\vee\mathcal{I}

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the full subcategory of
preprojective

\Lambda
-modules,
\mathcal{R}
denotes the full subcategory of
regular

\Lambda
-modules, and
\mathcal{I}
denotes the full subcategory of

postinjective

\Lambda
-modules
[
5
, VIII]
. Because

\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
is a hereditary abelian category we have a decomposition
of the bounded derived category

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})=\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}[n]

where
\mathcal{A}[n]
denotes the full subcategory of complexes with cohomology
concentrated in degree
-n
. Note that this category has Serre duality
which extends the Auslander–Reiten duality for
\mathcal{A}
. Thus there is a
functor
\tau\colon\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})
such that for all objects

X,Y
there is a natural isomorphism

D\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y[1])\cong\operatorname{Hom}(Y,\tau X)

where
D=\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(-,K)
; see
[
12
]
for details.

In
[
15
]
Lenzing proposes a geometric approach and introduces
the following full additive subcategory

\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{I}[-1]\vee\mathcal{P}[0]\vee\mathcal{R}[0]\subseteq\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda).

He shows that
\mathcal{H}
is a hereditary abelian category with Serre duality
given by the Auslander–Reiten translate
\tau\colon\mathcal{H}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{H}
. One way
of seeing this is that the torsion pair
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{P}\vee\mathcal{R})
for
\mathcal{A}

induces a t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})
such that
\mathcal{H}
identifies with its
heart
[
10
]
. Moreover,
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}[0]
is a tilting object and
\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}(\Lambda,-)

provides a triangle equivalence

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda).

The

preprojective algebra
of
\Lambda
is the orbit algebra

\Pi:=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\tau^{-n}\Lambda)

and the following
theorem is implicit in Lenzing’s geometric treatment of hereditary
algebras; cf. Theorem 4.10 in
[
15
]
. We deduce this from
Theorem
1
. Note that
\Pi
is noetherian if and only if

\Lambda
is of tame type
[
6
,
7
]
.

Theorem 14
.

For a connected hereditary algebra
\Lambda
of infinite representation type the
assignment

X\mapsto\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\tau^{-n}X)

induces an equivalence

\mathcal{H}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}(\operatorname{grmod}\Pi)/(\operatorname{grmod}_{0}\Pi).

Proof.

We set
\sigma=\tau^{-}
and the distinguished object is
C=\Lambda
. We need to
check the conditions in Theorem
1
for the triple

(\mathcal{H},C,\sigma)
and note that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{I}[-1]\vee\mathcal{P}[0]
. The preprojective
algebra
\Pi
is semiperfect since each homogeneous component is
finite dimensional over a field. The algebra is right coherent
because the category
\mathcal{C}
has kernels. Here one uses that

\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{grmod}\Pi
. Serre duality for
\mathcal{H}
provides for each
indecomposable
X\in\mathcal{C}
an almost split sequence

0\to\tau X\to X^{\prime}\to X\to 0

[
20
, Theorem I.3.3]
. By the
definition of an almost split sequence, the morphism
X^{\prime}\to X
is
right almost split. The condition (A1) is clear because we have for
each regular module
X\in\mathcal{R}[0]
an epimorphism
X^{\prime}\to X
from a
projective module
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}[0]
. For an indecomposable object

X\in\mathcal{C}
one uses the right hand morphism
X^{\prime}\to X
from the
corresponding almost split sequence. To check (A2) consider an
epimorphism
Y\to Z
in
\mathcal{C}
which yields an exact sequence

0\to X\to Y\to Z\to 0
. We may apply a power of
\tau
and assume
that the sequence lies in
\mathcal{I}[-1]
. Consider the induced exact
sequence

0\to\operatorname{Hom}(-,X)\to\operatorname{Hom}(-,Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}(-,Z)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(-,X)

in
\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{C}
.
We need to show that all morphisms
Z^{\prime}\to Z
from
\operatorname{Rad}^{n}\mathcal{C}
factor
through
Y\to Z
for
n\gg 0
. For this it suffices to show that

\operatorname{Rad}^{n}\mathcal{C}
annihilates the functor
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(-,X)|_{\mathcal{I}[-1]}
for

n\gg 0
, because
\operatorname{Hom}(P,Z)=0
for all
P\in\mathcal{P}[0]
. We
have

D\operatorname{Hom}(\tau^{-}X,-)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(-,X)

and from this the claim
follows, because for any postinjective
\Lambda
-module
M
we have

\operatorname{Rad}^{n}(M,-)=0
for
n\gg 0
in
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
.
∎

When passing to derived categories we have the following immediate
consequence; see also
[
17
, Corollary 5.4]
.

Corollary 15
.

The quotient functor
\operatorname{grmod}\Pi\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{H}

induces a triangle equivalence

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}((\operatorname{grmod}\Pi)/(\operatorname{grmod}_{0}\Pi))\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda).\qed

The following example connects the results of Serre and Lenzing; see
[
15
, Proposition 6.3]
.

Example 16
.

Consider the Kronecker algebra
\Lambda=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}K&0\\
K^{2}&K\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. In that
case
\mathcal{H}
identifies with the category of coherent shaves on the
projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. More precisely, the category
\operatorname{coh}\mathbb{P}^{1}

admits a tilting object
T=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
such that
\operatorname{End}(T)\cong\Lambda
.
Then the functor
\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}(T,-)
yields a triangle equivalence

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{coh}\mathbb{P}^{1})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda)

which restricts to an equivalence
\operatorname{coh}\mathbb{P}^{1}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{H}
. Note that this
identifies the twist
\mathscr{F}\mapsto\mathscr{F}(2)
with

X\mapsto\sigma X
. We have

R:=K[x,y]\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n))

and therefore

\Pi\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}R_{2n}&R_{2n-1}\\
R_{2n+1}&R_{2n}\end{smallmatrix}\right].

The assignment

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}\mapsto\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(M_{2n}\oplus M_{2n-1})

provides an
equivalence
\operatorname{grmod}R\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{grmod}\Pi
which identifies the equivalence
from Serre’s theorem in Example
2
with the equivalence
in Theorem
14
, though their twisting objects are
different (
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
in
\operatorname{coh}\mathbb{P}^{1}
versus
\Lambda
in
\mathcal{H}
).

The example suggests that there are other and arguably better choices
for a distinguished object
C\in\mathcal{H}
and a twist
\sigma\colon\mathcal{H}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{H}

(different from
C=\Lambda
and
\sigma=\tau^{-}
), in particular when
\Lambda
is a
tame hereditary algebra; see
[
13
,
14
]
for a detailed discussion.

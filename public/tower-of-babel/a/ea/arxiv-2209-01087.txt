Abstract
. Let ω ∗ ​ ( n ) \omega^{*}(n) be the number of primes p p such that p − 1 p-1 divides n n . Assuming the Elliott–Halberstam Conjecture, we prove a conjecture posted by M. R. Murty and V. K. Murty in 2021 which states that ∑ n ⩽ x ω ∗ ​ ( n ) 2 ∼ 2 ​ ζ ⁡ ( 2 ) ​ ζ ​ ( 3 ) ζ ⁡ ( 6 ) ​ x ​ log ⁡ x , as x → ∞ . \sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}\sim 2\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}x\log x,\quad\text{as}\quad x\rightarrow\infty. 1.
Introduction

The investigations of the normal order of certain arithmetic functions started from the paper of Hardy and Ramanujan
[
8
]
, proving that almost all integers
n
satisfy
\omega(n)\sim\log\log n
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, where
\omega(n)
is the number of distinct prime divisors of
n
. Later, Turán
[
14
]
simplified the proof significantly, by showing the following two asymptotic formulae:

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)=x\log\log x+Bx+O(x/\log x)

and

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)^{2}=x(\log\log x)^{2}+O(x\log\log x),

where
B
is an absolute constant.

Let
\omega^{*}(n)
be the number of primes
p
such that
p-1
divides
n
, which is a variant of the arithmetic function
\omega
. Nöbauer
[
12
]
studied this arithmetic function while investigating the number of a special group. Later, Prachar
[
13
]
considered the arithmetic properties of
\omega^{*}
similar to Turán
[
14
]
’s contributions on
\omega
and proved that

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)=x\log\log x+Bx+O(x/\log x)

as well as

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}=O\left(x(\log x)^{2}\right).

Prachar moreover showed that

\omega^{*}(n)>\exp\left(a\log n/(\log\log n)^{2}\right)

for infinitely many integers
n
, where
a
is an absolute constant. Adleman, Pomerance and Rumely
[
1
]
improved this to

\omega^{*}(n)>\exp\left(a\log n/\log\log n\right).

Prachar’s work relies on the estimation of a function counting the number of pairs of primes
p
and
q
so that the least common multiple
[p-1,q-1]\leqslant x
. Erdős and Prachar
[
6
]
proved that the counting function is bounded by
O(x\log\log x)
and claimed that the estimation can be improved in a remark of their article. Murty and Murty
[
10
]
followed the remark and improved the bound to
O(x)
. As a consequence, they showed that

x(\log\log x)^{3}\ll\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}\ll x\log x.

As remarked by Murty and Murty, the above lower bound means that
\omega^{*}(n)
does not have a normal order. Moreover, Murty and Murty conjectured that there is some positive constant
C
such that

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}\sim Cx\log x

as
x\rightarrow\infty
, or equivalently,

\sum_{[p-1,q-1]\leqslant x}\frac{1}{[p-1,q-1]}\sim C\log x.

In a former note, Ding
[
3
]
showed that

a_{1}x\log x\leqslant\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}\leqslant a_{2}x\log x,

where
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are two absolute constants.

In this article, we give a conditional proof of the conjecture by Murty and Murty. Precisely, we confirm the conjecture under the Elliott–Halberstam Conjecture
[
5
]
, which is of the following.

Conjecture 1.1

(Elliott–Halberstam)
.

For any fixed positive real numbers
A
and
\epsilon
, we have the following estimate

\sum_{d\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\left|\pi(y;d,1)-\frac{\mathrm{li}y}{\varphi(d)}\right|\ll_{A,\epsilon}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}},

where
\pi(y;d,1)
is the number of primes
p\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)
up to
y
, and
\mathrm{li}y=\int_{2}^{y}\frac{1}{\log t}dt.

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assuming that the Elliott–Halberstam Conjecture is true, then we have

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}\sim 2\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}x\log x

as
x\rightarrow\infty
.

2.
Auxiliary results

Throughout our paper, the number
x
is supposed to be sufficiently large whereas
\epsilon
is a given number which was supposed to be sufficiently small. The symbol
p
will always be a prime.
In this section, we provide some preliminary results to be used later.

We need firstly the following asymptotic formulae of Landau
[
9
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For positive number
y
we have

\sum_{m\leqslant y}\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}=\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}\log y+O(1)

and

\sum_{m\leqslant y}\frac{m}{\varphi(m)}=\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}y+O(\log y).

A classical inequality below involving primes in arithmetic progressions will be used frequently.

Lemma 2.2

(Brun–Titchmarsh inequality
[
11
]
)
.

Let
x
be a positive real number, and let
k,a
be relatively prime positive integers. Then

\pi(x;k,a)\leqslant\frac{2x}{\varphi(k)\log(x/k)},

provided that
x>k
.

The following result is obtained directly from Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
x
be a positive real number, and let
m,a
be relatively prime positive integers. Then

\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\ll\frac{\log(\log x-\log m)}{\varphi(m)}

provided that
x>6m
, where the implied constant is absolute.

Proof.

Trivially, we have

\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\ll\int_{m}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{t^{2}}dt.

We separate the integral above into the following three parts

\int_{m}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{t^{2}}dt=\left(\int_{m}^{em}+\int_{em}^{x/2}+\int_{x/2}^{x}\right)\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{t^{2}}dt.

Using the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality for the second part as well as the trivial estimates for the other two parts, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\ll\int_{m}^{em}\frac{dt}{mt}+\int_{em}^{x/2}\frac{dt}{\varphi(m)t\log(t/m)}+\int_{x/2}^{x}\frac{dt}{mt}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{m}+\frac{\log(\log x-\log m)}{\varphi(m)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\log(\log x-\log m)}{\varphi(m)}.

(2.1)

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

The whole proof of our article is based on the following substantial identity of Murty and Murty
[
10
, equation (4.10)]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For positive number
x
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}=x\sum_{m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p-1}\bigg)^{2}+O(x).

The next lemma is deduced from partial summations and elementary estimates.

Lemma 2.5
.

For positive number
x\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}=x\sum_{1<m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\bigg(\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\bigg)^{2}+O\left(x(\log\log x)^{3}\right).

Proof.

From Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}=x\sum_{m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p-1}\bigg)^{2}+O(x).

(2.2)

It is clear that the contribution from term
m=1
is bounded by
O(x\log\log x)
.

Integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p-1}=\frac{\pi(x;m,1)}{x-1}+\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt.

(2.3)

Inserting Eq. (
2.3
) into Eq. (
2.2
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}=S_{1}(x)+S_{2}(x)+S_{3}(x)+O\left(x\right),

(2.4)

where

S_{1}(x)=x\sum_{1<m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\bigg(\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\bigg)^{2},

S_{2}(x)=\frac{2x}{x-1}\sum_{m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\pi(x;m,1)\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt

and

S_{3}(x)=\frac{x}{(x-1)^{2}}\sum_{m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\pi(x;m,1)^{2}.

Then we will show that
S_{2}(x)
and
S_{3}(x)
provide the error terms.

We first deal with the sum
S_{3}(x)
as it is easy to be bounded. By the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality (i.e., Lemma
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{x}{(x-1)^{2}}\sum_{m\leqslant x/\log x}\varphi(m)\pi(x;m,1)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll x\sum_{m\leqslant x/\log x}\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\frac{1}{\log^{2}(x/m)}

\displaystyle\ll x\sum_{m\leqslant x/\log x}\frac{\log\log m}{m}\frac{1}{\log^{2}(x/m)}

\displaystyle\ll x\log\log x,

where, in the last but one estimation above, we employed the well–known estimate
\varphi(m)\gg m/\log\log m
.
Thus, by the trivial estimate we obtain

\displaystyle S_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{(x-1)^{2}}\sum_{x/\log x<m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\pi(x;m,1)^{2}+O\left(x\log\log x\right)

\displaystyle\ll x\sum_{x/\log x<m\leqslant x}\frac{\varphi(m)}{m^{2}}+x\log x\log x

\displaystyle\ll x\log\log x.

(2.5)

Next, we bound the sum
S_{2}(x)
. Since
\pi(t;m,1)=0
for
t\leqslant m
, from the trivial estimate we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x/16<m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\pi(x;m,1)\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\ll x\sum_{x/16<m\leqslant x}\frac{\varphi(m)}{m}\int_{m}^{x}\frac{1}{mt}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant x\sum_{x/16<m\leqslant x}\frac{\log x-\log m}{m}

\displaystyle\ll x.

We then prove that

\sum_{m\leqslant x/16}\varphi(m)\pi(x;m,1)\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\ll x(\log\log x)^{3}.

From the estimate of Lemma
2.3
together with the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leqslant x/16}\varphi(m)\pi(x;m,1)\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{m\leqslant x/16}\varphi(m)\frac{x}{\varphi(m)\log(x/m)}\frac{\log\log x}{\varphi(m)}

\displaystyle\ll x\log\log x\sum_{m\leqslant x/16}\frac{\log\log m}{m\log(x/m)}

\displaystyle\ll x(\log\log x)^{3},

where, in the last but one estimate above, we used the estimate
\varphi(m)\gg m/\log\log m
again. Thus, we proved that

\displaystyle S_{2}(x)\ll x(\log\log x)^{3}.

(2.6)

Now, the lemma follows immediately from Eqs. (
2.2
), (
2.4
), (
2
) and (
2.6
).
∎

3.
Applications of the sieve results

The aims of this section are the establishments of the following Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
which will be used later in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\epsilon
be a sufficiently small positive number. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\bigg(\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\bigg)^{2}\ll\epsilon\log x.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\epsilon
be a sufficiently small positive number. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\varphi(m)\bigg(\int_{2}^{m^{1+\epsilon}}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\bigg)^{2}\ll\epsilon\log x+\log\log x

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\epsilon
be a sufficiently small positive number. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\varphi(m)\int_{2}^{m^{1+\epsilon}}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\ll\epsilon(\log\epsilon^{-1})^{2}\log x+(\log\log x)^{2}.

In order to prove the propositions, we need some more lemmas. The following one is quoted from a recent article of Ding and Zhao.

Lemma 3.6
.

[
4
, Proposition 2.1]

For any given positive integers
g\geqslant 2
and
\ell
, we have

\sum_{1<h_{1}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<h_{g}<z}\frac{1}{h_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot h_{g}}\prod_{p|E_{h_{1},...,h_{g}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\ell}\ll(\log z)^{g},

provided that
z
is sufficiently large, where

E_{h_{1},...,h_{g}}=h_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot h_{g}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant g}(h_{j}-h_{i})

and the implied constant depends only on
g
and
\ell
.

We also need a weak form of the standard result
[
7
, Theorem 2.3, Page 70]
deduced from the Brun sieve.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
g
be a natural number, and let
a_{i},b_{i}~(i=1,...,g)
be integers satisfying

(a_{i},b_{i})=1\quad(i=1,...,g)

and

E:=\prod_{i=1}^{g}a_{i}\prod_{1\leqslant r<s\leqslant g}(a_{r}b_{s}-a_{s}b_{r})\neq 0.

Then

\#\{n\leqslant y:a_{i}n+b_{i}\in\mathcal{P},i=1,...,g\}\ll\prod_{p|E}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\rho(p)-g}\frac{y}{(\log y)^{g}},

where
\rho(p)
denotes the number of solutions of

\prod_{i=1}^{g}(a_{i}n+b_{i})\equiv 0\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p),

and the constant implied by the
\ll-
symbol depends on
g
and
b_{1},...,b_{g}
.

We are ready to offer the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Let

\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)=\sum_{x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p-1}\bigg)^{2}.

Expanding the sum, we have

\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)=\sum_{x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x}\varphi(m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},~p_{2}\leqslant x\\
p_{1}\equiv 1\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)\\
p_{2}\equiv 1\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(p_{1}-1)(p_{2}-1)}.

Suppose that
p_{i}=m\ell_{i}+1~(i=1,2)
, then
\ell_{i}<x/m
. Changing orders of the sums above, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}<x^{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(m)}{m^{2}}\leqslant\sum_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}<x^{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m},

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of primes.
Separating the case of
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}
with
\ell_{1}\neq\ell_{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\ll\sum_{\ell_{1}<x^{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{1}+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+\sum_{\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<x^{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}.

(3.1)

Trivially, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell_{1}<x^{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{1}+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}\leq\sum_{\ell_{1}<x^{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}\ll\epsilon\log x.

(3.2)

Conveniently, we denote

\mathcal{W}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant t\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}1.

Integrating by parts again, we find that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}=\frac{\mathcal{W}(x)}{x}-\frac{\mathcal{W}(x^{1-\epsilon})}{x^{1-\epsilon}}+\int_{x^{1-\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\mathcal{W}(t)}{t^{2}}dt.

By Lemma
3.7
with
g=2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle\ll\prod_{p|\ell_{1}\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1})}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-2}\Big(\frac{1}{\log^{2}x}+\int_{x^{1-\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{1}{t(\log t)^{2}}dt\Big)

\displaystyle\ll\prod_{p|\ell_{1}\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1})}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\log x},

which clearly means that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<x^{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1-\epsilon}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}\ll\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<x^{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\prod_{p|\ell_{1}\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1})}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}.

(3.3)

Employing Lemma
3.6
with
g=\ell=2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<x^{2\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\prod_{p|\ell_{1}\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1})}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}\ll\epsilon\log^{2}x

(3.4)

Thus, we obtain by Eqs. (
3.1
), (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\ll\epsilon\log x

The sum to be estimated is clearly bounded by
\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)
due to the partial summations

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p-1},

(3.5)

which ends up the proof of our proposition.
∎

We next provide the proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

On noting Eq. (
3.5
), we easily find that the sum to be bounded is not larger than

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\varphi(m)\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant m^{1+2\epsilon}\\
p\equiv 1\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p-1}\Bigg)^{2}:=\mathcal{A}_{2}(x),~\text{say}.

By expanding the sums, it is clear that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)=\sum_{m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\varphi(m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{i}\leqslant m^{1+2\epsilon}\\
p_{i}\equiv 1\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m),~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(p_{1}-1)(p_{2}-1)}.

Suppose that
p_{i}=m\ell_{i}+1~(i=1,2)
, then

\ell_{i}<m^{2\epsilon}\leqslant x^{2\epsilon},

and hence
m>\ell_{i}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}
. Changing orders of the sums above, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\leqslant\sum_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\leqslant x^{2\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{i}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(m)}{m^{2}}\leqslant 2\sum_{\ell_{1}\leqslant\ell_{2}\leqslant x^{2\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}.

Separating the case of
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}
with
\ell_{1}\neq\ell_{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)\ll\!\sum_{\ell_{1}\leqslant x^{2\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{1}+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}\leqslant x^{2\epsilon}\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}:=\mathcal{A}_{2,1}(x)+\mathcal{A}_{2,2}(x),

(3.6)

say.
It is plain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2,1}(x)=\sum_{\ell_{1}\leqslant e}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{1}+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+\sum_{e<\ell_{1}\leqslant x^{2\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{1}+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}

and

\sum_{\ell_{1}\leqslant e}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{1}+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant x\\
m+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant x\\
2m+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}<2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant 2x+1,~p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{3}{p}\ll\log\log x

via the Mertens’ formula.

Obviously, we are in a position to employ Lemma
3.7
. Conveniently, we denote

\mathcal{W}_{1}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant t\\
~m\ell_{1}+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{W}_{2}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant t\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}1.

Integrating by parts, we find (for
\ell_{1}>e
) that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{1}+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}=\frac{\mathcal{W}_{1}(x)}{x}-\frac{\mathcal{W}_{1}(\ell_{1})}{\ell_{1}}+\int_{\ell_{1}}^{x}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{1}(t)}{t^{2}}dt.

By Lemma
3.7
with
g=1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{1}+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle\ll\prod_{p|\ell_{1}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\log\ell_{1}}+\int_{e}^{x}\frac{1}{t\log t}dt\right)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\ell_{1}}{\varphi(\ell_{1})}\left(\frac{1}{\log\ell_{1}}+\log\log x\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2,1}(x)\ll\sum_{\ell_{1}<x^{2\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{1}+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+\log\log x\ll\log\log x.

(3.7)

Similar to
\mathcal{A}_{2,1}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2,2}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e<\ell_{2}\leqslant x^{2\epsilon}\\
\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+O(\log\log x).

(3.8)

Integrating by parts again, we find (for

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}=\frac{\mathcal{W}_{2}(x)}{x}-\frac{\mathcal{W}_{2}\left(\ell_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}\right)}{\ell_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}}+\int_{\ell_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}}^{x}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{2}(t)}{t^{2}}dt.

By Lemma
3.7
with

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell_{i}+1\in\mathcal{P},~i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle\ll\prod_{p|\ell_{1}\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1})}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-2}\Big(\frac{1}{(\log x)^{2}}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{(\log\ell_{2})^{2}}+\int_{\ell_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}}}^{x}\frac{1}{t(\log t)^{2}}dt\Big)

\displaystyle\ll\prod_{p|\ell_{1}\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1})}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\log x}+\frac{\epsilon}{\log\ell_{2}}\right).

(3.9)

Inserting Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2,2}(x)\ll\log\log x+\epsilon\log x+\epsilon\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e<\ell_{2}\leqslant x^{2\epsilon}\\
\ell_{1}\leqslant\ell_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\log\ell_{2}}\prod_{p|\ell_{1}\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1})}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}

(3.10)

via Lemma

(3.11)

and

\displaystyle\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt

via Lemma
2.2

\mathcal{A}_{3,2}(x)\ll\sum_{m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\int_{2}^{m^{1+\epsilon}}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt.

Hence, on putting
p-1=\ell m

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,2}(x)\ll\sum_{m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant m^{1+\epsilon}\\

For

\sum_{\ell\leqslant e}\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant x\\
m+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant x\\

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,2}(x)\ll\sum_{e<\ell\leqslant x^{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+\log\log x.

(3.16)

\mathcal{W}_{1}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant t\\
~m\ell+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}1.

Integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{W}_{1}(x)}{x}-\frac{\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\ell\right)}{\ell}+\int_{\ell}^{x}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{1}(t)}{t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\ll\prod_{p|\ell}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\log x}+\frac{1}{\log\ell}+\int_{\ell}^{x}\frac{1}{t\log t}dt\right)

\displaystyle\ll\prod_{p|\ell}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\log\ell}+\log\log x-\log\log\ell\right)

via Lemma
3.7
for
\ell>e
. Taking the estimate above into Eq. (
3.16

3.6

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,2}(x)\ll\sum_{e<\ell\leqslant x^{\epsilon}}\frac{\log\log x-\log\log\ell}{\varphi(\ell)}+\log\log x+\log\epsilon^{-1}.

(3.17)

Again, integrating by parts, we would have

\displaystyle\sum_{e<\ell\leqslant x^{\epsilon}}\frac{\log\log x-\log\log\ell}{\varphi(\ell)}=K\left(x^{\epsilon}\right)(\log\log x-\log\log x^{\epsilon})+\int_{e}^{x^{\epsilon}}\frac{K(t)}{t\log t}dt,

Since

from Lemma
2.1

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,2}(x)\ll\epsilon\log\epsilon^{-1}\log x+\log\log x+\log\epsilon^{-1}.

(3.18)

Now, by trivial adjustments of the arguments to

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,1}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}(\log\log x-\log\log m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant m^{1+\epsilon}\\
p\equiv 1\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p-1}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{e<\ell\leqslant x^{\epsilon}}\frac{\log\log x-\log\log\ell}{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell<m\leqslant x\\
m\ell+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+(\log\log x)^{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,1}(x)\ll\epsilon(\log\epsilon^{-1})^{2}\log x+(\log\log x)^{2}+\left(\log\epsilon^{-1}\right)^{2}.

\displaystyle\sum_{1<m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\varphi(m)\bigg(\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\bigg)^{2}=\sum_{1<m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\varphi(m)\bigg(\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\bigg)^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}/x=\sum_{1<m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\varphi(m)\bigg(\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\bigg)^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{1}(x).

(4.1)

On putting

\displaystyle\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt=\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t)}{\varphi(m)(t-1)^{2}}dt+\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)-\pi(t)/\varphi(m)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}/x=\sum_{1<m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\bigg(\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\!\bigg)^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{1}(x)+\mathcal{R}_{2}(x)+\mathcal{R}_{3}(x),

(4.2)

where

\mathcal{R}_{2}(x)\ll\sum_{1<m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\varphi(m)\bigg(\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)-\pi(t)/\varphi(m)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\bigg)^{2}

and

\mathcal{R}_{3}(x)\ll\sum_{1<m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\varphi(m)\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t)}{\varphi(m)(t-1)^{2}}dt\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\left|\pi(t;m,1)-\pi(t)/\varphi(m)\right|}{(t-1)^{2}}dt.

We are in a position to employ the Elliott–Halberstam Conjecture.
By Conjecture

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{3}(x)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{1<m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{1}{t\log t}dt\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\left|\pi(t;m,1)-\frac{\pi(t)}{\varphi(m)}\right|\frac{1}{t^{2}}dt

(4.3)

Again, by Conjecture
1.1
with
A=2
and the trivial estimates, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{3}(x)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{1<m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\varphi(m)\bigg(\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t;m,1)-\pi(t)/\varphi(m)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\log\log x\sum_{1<m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\left|\pi(t;m,1)-\frac{\pi(t)}{\varphi(m)}\right|\frac{1}{t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon}\log\log x

(4.4)

by the same argument with

Combing Eqs. (

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}/x=\sum_{1<m\leqslant x^{1-\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\bigg(\!\int_{m^{1+\epsilon}}^{x}\frac{\pi(t)}{(t-1)^{2}}dt\bigg)^{2}+\mathcal{R}(x),

(4.5)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}/(x\log x)=f(\epsilon)+O\left(\frac{(\log\log x)^{3}}{\log x}+\frac{\left(\log\epsilon^{-1}\right)^{2}}{\log x}\right)+O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\right),

\displaystyle 2\kappa+O(\epsilon(\log\epsilon^{-1})^{2})\leqslant\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum\limits_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}}{x\log x}\leqslant\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum\limits_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}}{x\log x}\leqslant 2\kappa+O(\epsilon(\log\epsilon^{-1})^{2}).

2\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}\leqslant\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum\limits_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}}{x\log x}\leqslant\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum\limits_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}}{x\log x}\leqslant 2\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}

since

\epsilon(\log\epsilon^{-1})^{2}\rightarrow 0

as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. Therefore,

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}\sim 2\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}x\log x,\quad(\text{as~}x\rightarrow\infty).

1.1
.

5.
Final remarks

It is clear that we have the following corollary

\sum_{p,q\leqslant x}\frac{1}{[p-1,q-1]}\sim 2\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}\log x

as
x\rightarrow\infty
under the Elliott–Halberstam Conjecture due to (see
[
10
, page 6, last line]
)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega^{*}(n)^{2}=\sum_{p,q\leqslant x}\frac{x}{[p-1,q-1]}+O(x)

and Theorem
1.1
.

We cannot obtain explicit error terms in Theorem
1.1
since it is certain that there is an oscillation of
\epsilon
–proportion of the main term which comes from the Elliott–Halberstam Conjecture naturally.

We believe that an unconditional proof of our main theorem is widely open and should be considered of independent interest. However, this seems unreachable under the current circumstance.

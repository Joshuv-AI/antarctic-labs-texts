A stability result for the first Robin-Neumann eigenvalue: a double perturbation approach
Simone Cito 1 , Gloria Paoli 2 , Gianpaolo Piscitelli 3

Abstract
. Let Ω = Ω 0 ∖ Θ ¯ ⊂ ℝ n \Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , where Ω 0 \Omega_{0} and Θ \Theta are two open, bounded and convex sets such that Θ ¯ ⊂ Ω 0 \overline{\Theta}\subset\Omega_{0} and let β < 0 \beta<0 be a given parameter. We consider the eigenvalue problem for the Laplace operator associated to Ω \Omega , with Robin boundary condition on ∂ Ω 0 \partial\Omega_{0} and Neumann boundary condition on ∂ Θ \partial\Theta . In [ 47 ] it is proved that the spherical shell is the only maximizer for the first Robin-Neumann eigenvalue in the class of domains Ω \Omega with fixed outer perimeter and volume. We establish a quantitative version of the afore-mentioned isoperimetric inequality; the main novelty consists in the introduction of a new type of hybrid asymmetry, that turns out to be the suitable one to treat the different conditions on the outer and internal boundary. Up to our knowledge, in this context, this is the first stability result in which both the outer and the inner boundary are perturbed. MSC 2020: 35J25, 35P15, 47J30. Keywords and phrases : Laplace operator; Robin-Neumann eigenvalue; Quantitative spectral inequality.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
be a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\geq 2
, where
\Omega_{0}
is an open bounded convex set,
\Theta
is a finite union of open sets homeomorphic to balls such that
\overline{\Theta}\subset\Omega_{0}
and let
\beta<0
be a given parameter.

In this paper, we study the first Robin-Neumann eigenvalue of the the Laplace operator, defined as:

(1)

\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\\
\psi\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}R_{\Omega}(\psi),

where

(2)

R_{\Omega}(\psi)=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\;dx+\beta\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\psi^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi^{2}\;dx}.

If
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is a minimizer of (
1
), then it satisfies

(3)

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)w&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}+\beta w=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{0}\\
\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Theta,\end{cases}

where we denote by
\nu
the unit outer normal to the boundary and
\beta
is usually called the Robin boundary parameter associated to the problem.

In
[
37
]
, the authors interpret
\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)
, in the case
\Theta=\emptyset
, as
the growth rate of particles arising from branching
of Brownian motions with respect to the local time and the splitting rate
-\beta
.
In our case, we are considering, in addition to this situation, an insulating internal hole
\Theta
.
Moreover, for the physical interpretation in the superconductivity context see also
[
42
]
and the references therein.

We denote by
B_{R}
the open ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of radius
R
centered at the origin and we set
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}:=B_{R_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{1}}}
, with
0<R_{1}<R_{2}
. Moreover, we denote, as usual, by
P(\cdot)
and
|\cdot|
, respectively, the perimeter and the volume of a set.
In
[
47
]
it has been proved that

(4)

\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\leq\lambda_{1}(\beta,A),

where
A=A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
is the spherical shell such that
P(B_{R_{2}})=P(\Omega_{0})
and
|\Omega|=|A|
and
equality holds if and only if
\Omega=A
.

The aim of the present paper is to prove a quantitative estimate of the isoperimetric inequality (
4
).
More precisely, we want to improve (
4
), obtaining an inequality of the following type:

\lambda_{1}(\beta,A)-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\geq g(\alpha(\Omega)),

where
g(\cdot)
is a modulus of continuity, i.e. a positive continuous increasing function, vanishing only at
0
and
\alpha(\cdot)
is a suitable asymmetry functional, i.e. a non negative functional such that
\alpha(\Omega)=0
if and only if
\Omega=A
.

In order to get this kind of quantitative result, we need to introduce the following class of sets, in which (
4
) turns out to be stable. Let us fix
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<+\infty
and let us denote by
\vartheta_{R_{1},R_{2}}
a positive constant such that
\vartheta_{R_{1},R_{2}}\leq\min\{R_{1},R_{2}-R_{1}\}/2
. We set

(5)

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}=\{\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}:&\ \Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta},\ \Omega_{0},\Theta\ \text{convex},\ |\Omega|=|A_{R_{1},R_{2}}|,\ P(\Omega_{0})=P(B_{R_{2}}),\\
&d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Theta,\Omega_{0})\geq\vartheta_{R_{1},R_{2}}\ \text{and}\ \rho(\Theta)\geq\vartheta_{R_{1},R_{2}}\},\end{split}

where
\rho(\cdot)
is the inradius of a set and
d_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Hausdorff distance between two convex sets. More precisely, given
E,F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
convex sets, the Hausdorff distance is defined as follows

d_{\mathcal{H}}(E,F)=\inf\{\varepsilon>0\ :\ E\subset F+B_{\varepsilon},\ F\subset E+B_{\varepsilon}\}.

Thus, as a direct consequence of (
4
), we have that the spherical shell
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
is the only maximizer for the shape optimization problem

(6)

\max\left\{\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega):\Omega\in\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}\right\}.

In order to study the stability of the reverse Faber-Krahn type inequality (
4
) in the class
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
, we need to introduce a suitable asymmetry functional, that takes into account both the perturbations on the outer and inner boundary with the different boundary conditions.

To treat the perturbation of the outer boundary, we use this standard notion of asymmetry for a convex set:

(7)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0})=\inf_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0},B_{R}(x_{0}))}{\omega_{n}R^{n}}\ :\ P(\Omega_{0})=P(B_{R}(x_{0}))\right\},

where
\omega_{n}
is the measure of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

On the other hand, we also need to take into account the perturbation of the inner hole
\Theta
. In order to do that, we recall the definition of the test function
w_{\Omega_{0}}
used in
[
47
]
to prove (
4
):

(8)

w_{\Omega_{0}}(x):=\begin{cases}G(d(x))\quad&\text{if}\ d(x)<R_{2}-R_{1}\\
z_{m}&\text{if}\ d(x)\geq R_{2}-R_{1}\end{cases}\ x\in\Omega_{0},

where
z
be the first positive eigenfunction on the spherical shell
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
with
\|z\|_{L^{2}(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})}=1
,
d(x)
is the distance function from the boundary of
\Omega_{0}
and
G
is defined as

G^{-1}(t)=\int_{t}^{z_{M}}\dfrac{1}{\ell(\tau)}\;d\tau,

with
\ell(t)=|\nabla z|_{z=t}
, determined for
z_{m}<t\leq z_{M}
with
z_{m}:=\min_{A}z
and
z_{M}:=\max_{A}z
.
We point out that
w_{\Omega_{0}}
is a map depending only on the distance from the outer boundary (see Section
2.3
for further details) and, moreover, it is constant on the boundaries of the inner parallels to
\Omega_{0}
. This type of functions are usually called
web-function
and this name appeared for the first time in
[
36
]
, since their level lines recall the web of a spider.

Therefore,
w_{\Omega_{0}}
can be used as a test function for every admissible holed set with outer box
\Omega_{0}
.
Now, we are in position to give the following definition (see also Figure
1
).

Definition 1.1

(Hybrid asymmetry)
.

We define the hybrid asymmetry of
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
as:

(9)

\alpha(\Omega):=\max\left\{g(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0})),\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0})\right\}.

Here,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)
is the Hausdorff asymmetry defined in (
7
) and
g(\cdot)
is the positive increasing function defined as

g(s)=\begin{cases}s^{2}&\text{if $n=2$}\\
f^{-1}(s^{2})&\text{if $n=3$}\\
s^{\frac{n+1}{2}}&\text{if $n\geq 4$},\end{cases}

with
f(t)=\sqrt{t\log\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)}
for
0<t<e^{-1}
.

The term
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0})
is the weak weighted Fraenkel-type asymmetry of
\Theta
relative to the set
\Omega_{0}
, defined as

(10)

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0}):=\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}(|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}+w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}(x)-z_{m}^{2})\>dx,

where

•

w_{\Omega_{0}}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{0})
is the test function defined in (
8
);

•

K_{\Omega_{0}}
is the inner parallel set relative to
\Omega_{0}
, i.e. the set

K_{\Omega_{0}}=\{x\in\Omega_{0}\;:\;d(x)>t_{\Omega_{0}}\},

with
t_{\Omega_{0}}\in(0,\rho(\Omega_{0}))
such that
|K_{\Omega_{0}}|=|\Theta|
;

•

z
is the first positive eigenfunction of (
1
) on the
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
with
\|z\|_{L^{2}(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})}=1
;

•

z_{m}
is the minimum of
z
on
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
.

Figure 1.
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0})
is drawn in orange; the green set is the integration domain in the definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0})
.

We point out that the hybrid asymmetry just introduced is zero if and only if
\Omega=A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
, and so, (
9
) actually quantifies a deviation of an admissible set from the optimal set.
The term
g(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0}))
quantifies the deviation with respect to the Hausdorff distance of the domain
\Omega_{0}
from the optimal exterior shape
B_{R_{2}}
;
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0})
is necessary to estimate the total deviation of
\Omega
from
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
.
Indeed, if
\Theta
has a low Hausdorff distance from
K_{\Omega_{0}}
, then the larger term in the hybrid asymmetry is
g(\mathcal{A}_{H}(\Omega_{0}))
; on the other hand, if
\Omega_{0}
is the ball
B_{R_{2}}
, any admissible hole
\Theta\neq B_{R_{1}}
satisfies
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;B_{R_{2}})>0
and thus in that case the largest term is
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;B_{R_{2}})
(see Remark
2.10
).

Moreover,
\alpha(\Omega)
is a proper asymmetry and in
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
it holds (see Proposition
2.11
) that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{j},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})=0\iff\lim_{j\to+\infty}\alpha(\Omega_{j})=0.

The key idea that inspired our definition of asymmetry
is the following:
provided that
\Omega_{0}
is the outer box, the most natural hole to compare
\Theta
with is neither a ball with the same measure as
\Theta
nor
B_{R_{1}}
, but the inner parallel
K_{\Omega_{0}}
having the same measure as
\Theta
. This behaviour seems to depend on the fact that
K_{\Omega_{0}}
makes the set
\Omega_{0}\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}
a "shell" with outer "box"
\Omega_{0}
and fixed distance of the inner boundary
\partial K_{\Omega_{0}}
from the outer boundary
\partial\Omega_{0}
. Moreover, the test function built via web function is constant on both
\partial K_{\Omega_{0}}
and
\partial\Omega_{0}
. Nevertheless, the set
K_{\Omega_{0}}
coincides with
B_{R_{1}}
if and only if
\Omega_{0}=B_{R_{2}}
.

The main results of the paper are the following. The first result is the quantitative version of (
4
).

Main Theorem 1

(Quantitative isoperimetric inequality)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
, with
\Omega_{0}
convex and
\Theta
is a finite union of open sets homeomorphic to balls. Let
A=A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
the spherical shell such that
P(B_{R_{2}})=P(\Omega_{0})
and
|\Omega|=|A|
.
There exist a positive constant
C(n,R_{1},R_{2},\beta)
such that, if
\Omega_{0}
is
R_{2}
-nearly spherical, then

(11)

\lambda_{1}(\beta,A)-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\geq C(n,R_{1},R_{2},\beta)\alpha(\Omega).

The second result is the stability of inequality (
4
) in the class
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
.

Main Theorem 2

(Stability in
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
)
.

Let
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<+\infty
. There exist two positive constants
C(n,R_{1},R_{2},\beta)
and
\delta_{0}(n,R_{1},R_{2},\beta)
such that, for every
\Omega\in\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
, if
\lambda_{1}(\beta,A)-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\leq\delta_{0}
, then

(12)

\lambda_{1}(\beta,A)-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\geq C(n,R_{1},R_{2},\beta)\alpha(\Omega),

where
A=A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
is the spherical shell in
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
and
\alpha(\Omega)
is the asymmetry defined in (
9
).

The proofs are based on two estimates. Since on
\partial\Omega_{0}
a Robin boundary condition holds, we can use a Fuglede type technique on an auxiliary Steklov type problem to obtain the first estimate relative to the perturbation of the outer boundary.
For this type of approach we refer to
[
29
,
31
]
for the stability of the isoperimetric inequality and to
[
26
,
32
,
23
]
for the quantitative estimates of some spectral functionals.
Then, once the outer boundary is fixed, we can estimate the deviation of
\Theta
from
K_{\Omega_{0}}
using the weak asymmetry
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0})
, defined in (
10
) and inspired by
[
13
,
3
]
.

In conclusion, the main novelty of the paper is the introduction of an hybrid type of asymmetry related to the fact that we have to deal with an holed domain with two different boundary conditions and subject to both a perimeter and a volume constraint.
More specifically, as far as the asymmetry of the outer boundary, when treating the auxiliary Steklov problem in the radial case, instead of having only one Bessel function as in
[
26
,
23
]
, we have a linear combination of two Bessel functions and so we have to face the related technical difficulties, such as the validity of all the recurrence formulas, the derivation rules, and the estimate from below of some integral quantities linked to the quantitative estimate.
Moreover, the inner hole with Neumann boundary condition cannot be treated as in
[
29
]
, since a cancellation of the inner boundary integrals occurs due to the boundary condition. So, we cannot use the standard Fuglede approach and we need to introduce the weighted Fraenkel type term that appears in (
9
).

The structure of the paper is the following. In Section
2
, we give some historical insights, we fix the notation, we recall some known results on the Robin-Neumann eigenvalue problem and recall some useful results on the nearly spherical sets. In Section
3
we study an auxiliary problem of Steklov type; in Section
4
, we provide some quantitative estimates for
\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)
and we prove of the main result of the paper. Finally, in Section
5
, a list of open problems is presented.

2.
Background and Preliminary results

We start this Section by describing the historical background on problems with different boundary conditions on the outer and inner boundary. Then, we fix the notation, we recall some results on the Robin-Neumann eigenvalue problem and some properties of nearly spherical sets.

2.1.
A glimpse of history

The study of spectral problems for the Robin-Laplace operator with negative parameter
\beta
on holed domains arises from the fact that the optimal shape for the first eigenvalue, among domains with fixed volume, is expected to be the spherical shell for
{\left|\beta\right|}
large enough. More precisely, in 1977, Bareket
[
9

conjectured that, among all smooth bounded domains of given volume, the first eigenvalue is maximized when
\Omega
is a ball and, in
[
26
]

topology. Subsequently, in
[
27
,
41
]
, it has been proved that the conjecture is true for small values of
\left|\beta\right|
; on the other hand, for
\left|\beta\right|
great enough, the first Robin eigenvalue on the spherical shell is greater than the eigenvalue on the disk, among domains of fixed volume.

More recently, many authors have investigated the Laplace eigenvalue problem with different boundary conditions on the outer and inner boundary: for instance positive Robin-Neumann
[

49
]

[

]

[

,
33
,

40
,
46
,
52
]
, Steklov-Robin
[

]
.

In particular, in this paper we study a stability result for this kind of problems.

To the best of our knowledge, the quantitative improvement of spectral inequalities firstly appeared in

2.3.
The Robin-Neumann eigenvalue problem

We collect now the definitions and the basic properties of the first Robin-Neumann eigenvalue; for the proofs refer to
[

We start by recalling the weak formulation of problem (
3

Definition 2.1
.

A real number
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of (
3
) if and only if there exists a function
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, not identically zero, such that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi\nabla\varphi\;dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\psi\varphi\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\psi\varphi\;dx

for every

.

In the following definition, we list the main properties of (
1
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\beta<0

•

There exists a minimizer

of (

•

\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)
is simple, i.e. all the associated eigenfunctions are scalar multiple of each other and can be taken to be positive. Moreover,

The map

•

Let

\displaystyle(w_{\Omega_{0}})_{m}:=\min_{\Omega_{0}}w_{\Omega_{0}}\geq z_{m},

\displaystyle(w_{\Omega_{0}})_{M}:=\max_{\Omega_{0}}w_{\Omega_{0}}=z_{M}=G(0).

Moreover, we recall the expression of the eigenfunction
z
associated to
\lambda=\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})
:

z(x)=|x|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\left(Y_{\frac{n}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}R_{1})J_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(\sqrt{\lambda}|x|)-J_{\frac{n}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}R_{1})Y_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(\sqrt{\lambda}|x|)\right).

So, by the radiality of
z
, we have
z(x)=\Psi(|x|)
and

\Psi(r)=r^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\delta(r),\quad\Psi^{\prime}(r)=r^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\gamma(r),

once having set

\begin{split}&\delta(r)=Y_{\frac{n}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}R_{1})J_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)-J_{\frac{n}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}R_{1})Y_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(\sqrt{\lambda}r),\\
&\gamma(r)=-Y_{\frac{n}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}R_{1})J_{\frac{n}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)-J_{\frac{n}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}R_{1})Y_{\frac{n}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}r),\end{split}

and using (
15
)-(
16
).

Therefore, substituting
z
in (
1
), we obtain

(21)

\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{R_{1}}^{R_{2}}r\gamma^{2}(r)dr+\beta R_{2}\delta^{2}(R_{2})}{\displaystyle\int_{R_{1}}^{R_{2}}r\delta^{2}(r)dr}.

Finally, we stress that it is possible to write the explicit expression of (
21
) by using the following relations for Bessel functions (see
[
53
, eq. (10)-(11) pag. 134]
):

\begin{split}&\int rF_{\nu}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)G_{\nu}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)dr=\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\left(2F_{\nu}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)G_{\nu}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)-F_{\nu-1}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)G_{\nu+1}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)-F_{\nu+1}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)G_{\nu-1}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)\right),\\
&\int rF^{2}_{\nu}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)dr=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\left(F^{2}_{\nu}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)-F_{\nu-1}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)F_{\nu+1}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)\right),\end{split}

where
F_{\nu}
and
G_{\nu}
are Bessel functions of any kind of order
\nu
.

2.4.
Nearly spherical sets

In this Section, we give some definitions and useful properties related to nearly spherical sets (see
[
29
,
30
]
).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded set with
0\in\Omega_{0}
. The set
\Omega_{0}
is said a
R-
nearly spherical set parametrized by
u
if there exist a constant
R>0
and
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, with
||u||_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq R/2
, such that

\partial\Omega_{0}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon y=\xi(R+u(\xi)),\ \xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right\}.

Let us observe that the volume of a
R-
nearly spherical set is given by

|\Omega_{0}|=\dfrac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(R+u(\xi)\right)^{n}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

and the perimeter by

P(\Omega_{0})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(R+u(\xi)\right)^{n-1}\sqrt{1+\dfrac{|\nabla_{\tau}u(\xi)|^{2}}{(R+u(\xi))^{2}}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
\nabla_{\tau}u
denotes the tangential gradient of
u
and the tangential Jacobian of the map

\phi:\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to y=R\xi(1+u(\xi))
is

J_{\phi}(\xi)=(R+u(\xi))^{n-2}\sqrt{(R+u(\xi))^{2}+|\nabla_{\tau}u(\xi)|^{2}}.

Moreover, the unit outer normal to
\partial\Omega_{0}
at
y=\xi(R+u(\xi))
is given by

\nu(\xi)=\dfrac{\xi(R+u(\xi))-\nabla_{\tau}u(\xi)}{\sqrt{(R+u(\xi))^{2}+|\nabla_{\tau}u(\xi)|^{2}}}.

In the following, we deal with sets that are, in a suitable sense, close to a spherical shell
and here we set the needed notation.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\Theta
, where
\Omega_{0}
and
\Theta
are two convex sets such that
\Theta\subset\subset\Omega_{0}
and
0\in\Theta
. We say that
\Omega
is a
(R_{1},R_{2})-
nearly annular set
if there exist
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<+\infty
and
u,v\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, with
||u||_{W^{1,\infty}}<R_{2}/2
,
||v||_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq R_{1}/2
, such that

\partial\Omega_{0}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon y=\xi(R_{2}+u(\xi)),\,\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right\}

and

\partial\Theta=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon y=\xi(R_{1}+v(\xi)),\,\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right\}.

This definition and the outer perimeter and volume constraints yield to the following Lemma, based on the Taylor expansions.

Lemma 2.6
.

There exists
C=C(n)>0
such that, for any parametrization couple
u,v\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
of a
(R_{1},R_{2})-
nearly annular set and for every
0<\varepsilon<R_{1}/2
such that
||u||_{W^{1,\infty}},

||v||_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq\varepsilon
, the following estimates hold:

(22)

\left|\left(R_{2}+u\right)^{n-1}-\left(R_{2}^{n-1}+(n-1)R_{2}^{n-2}u+(n-1)(n-2)R_{2}^{n-3}\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)\right|\leq C\varepsilon R_{2}^{n-3}u^{2};

(23)

\left|\left(R_{1}+v\right)^{n-1}-\left(R_{1}^{n-1}+(n-1)R_{1}^{n-2}v+(n-1)(n-2)R_{1}^{n-3}\frac{v^{2}}{2}\right)\right|\leq C\varepsilon R_{1}^{n-3}v^{2};

(24)

1+\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}}{2R_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{1+\dfrac{|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}}{(R_{2}+u)^{2}}}\leq C\varepsilon\left(u^{2}+|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}\right).

Moreover,

(25)

\left|R_{2}^{n-2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\dfrac{R^{n-3}_{2}}{2(n-1)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\dfrac{(n-2)}{2}R_{2}^{n-3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right|\leq C\varepsilon,

and

(26)

\begin{split}\left|R_{1}^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}v\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\right.&\dfrac{(n-1)}{2}R_{1}^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}v^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\left.-R_{2}^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\dfrac{(n-1)}{2}R_{2}^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right|\leq C\varepsilon.\end{split}

Proof.

The proofs of (
22
)-(
26
) can be immediately adapted from
[
29
]
. Inequalities (
22
) and (
23
) easily follow, respectively, from

(27)

(R_{2}+u)^{n-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}u^{k}R_{2}^{n-1-k}\quad\text{and}\quad(R_{1}+v)^{n-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}v^{k}R_{1}^{n-1-k}.

Inequality (
24
) is a consequence of inequality
\sqrt{1+x}\geq 1+\frac{x}{2}-\frac{x^{2}}{4}
.
Furthermore, using the outer perimeter constraint

\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(R_{2}+u)^{n-1}\sqrt{1+\dfrac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{(R_{2}+u)^{2}}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\omega_{n}R_{2}^{n-1},

the first equality in (
27
) and (
24
), we obtain (
25
).
Finally, inequality (
26
) follows by using the volume constraint

\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(R_{2}+u)^{n-1}-(R_{1}+v)^{n-1}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\omega_{n}(R_{2}^{n-1}-R_{1}^{n-1})

and the second equality in (
27
).
∎

The Poincaré inequality in this setting holds with constant
(n-1)
, but, since we are working with functions parametrizing nearly spherical sets, this constant can be improved to
2n
. For the proof we refer to
[
29
]
and
[
26
, Lem. 2.4]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that, for any parametrization
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
of a
R-
nearly spherical set and for every
0<\varepsilon<R/2
with
||u||_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq\varepsilon
, it holds

(28)

||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq 2n||u||_{L^{2}}^{2}-\varepsilon C.

Finally, these last two Lemmata provide key estimates for the norms of functions parametrizing the boundary of a nearly spherical set, see
[
29
,
30
]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
such that
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0
, then

\displaystyle||u||_{L^{\infty}}^{n-1}\leq\begin{cases}\pi\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}}&n=2\\
4||\nabla_{\tau}u||^{2}_{L^{2}}\log\frac{8e||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{\infty}}^{n-1}}{||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{2}}^{2}}&n=3\\[5.69046pt]
C(n)||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{2}}^{2}||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{\infty}}^{n-3}\,\,&n\geq 4.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
be a parametrization of a
R-
nearly spherical set such that
||u||_{W^{1,\infty}}<\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon<R/2
,
then

||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{\infty}\leq 3||u||_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

2.5.
Some properties of the hybrid asymmetry

In this Section we focus on some properties of the hybrid asymmetry
\alpha(\Omega)
, see Definition
1.1
. First of all, we show that
\alpha(\Omega)=0
if and only if
\Omega=A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
.

Remark 2.10
.

We point out that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0})\geq 0
, but it could be zero even if
\Omega_{0}\neq B_{R_{2}}
and
\Theta\neq K_{\Omega_{0}}
. Indeed, if

d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Theta,K_{\Omega_{0}})<t_{\Omega_{0}}-(R_{2}-R_{1}),

then
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0})=0
. Nevertheless, in that case,
\Omega_{0}\neq B_{R_{2}}
, thus the global asymmetry satisfies

\alpha(\Omega)=\max\left\{g(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0})),\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0})\right\}=g(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0}))>0.

In other words, if
\Theta
has a low Hausdorff distance from
K_{\Omega_{0}}
, then the larger term in the computation of
\alpha(\Omega)
is
g(\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{0}))
. In particular we point out that, provided that
\Theta\neq K_{\Omega_{0}}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0})
could be zero if and only if
\Omega_{0}\neq B_{R_{2}}
.

On the other hand, we stress the fact that
t_{\Omega_{0}}=R_{2}-R_{1}
if and only if
\Omega_{0}=B_{R_{2}}
. So, in this case,
K_{\Omega_{0}}=B_{R_{1}}
and any admissible hole
\Theta\neq B_{R_{1}}
satisfies
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;B_{R_{2}})>0
, since
w_{\Omega_{0}}=z\gneq z_{m}
on
\Theta\setminus B_{R_{1}}
. Consequently, we have

\alpha(\Omega)=\max\left\{g(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(B_{R_{2}})),\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;B_{R_{2}})\right\}=\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;B_{R_{2}})>0.

So, it actually holds
\alpha(\Omega)=0
if and only if
\Omega=A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
.

Now, we justify the fact that
\alpha(\Omega)
is actually an asymmetry, namely that the set
\Omega
tends to the spherical shell
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
in some suitable sense if and only if the hybrid asymmetry
\alpha(\Omega)
tends to zero.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<+\infty
and
\Omega_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the class
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
. Then

\lim_{j\to+\infty}d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{j},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})=0\iff\lim_{j\to+\infty}\alpha(\Omega_{j})=0.

Proof.

The necessary implication directly follows from Definition
1.1
. Therefore, it only remains to prove the converse condition.

Let
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence such that
\alpha(\Omega_{j})\to 0
.
Since
\Omega_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\Omega_{j}=(\Omega_{0})_{j}\setminus\Theta_{j}
. Consequently, from Definition
1.1
, it is clear that
d_{\mathcal{H}}({\Omega_{0}}_{j},B_{R_{2}})\to 0
.

By contradiction, let us assume that
d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Theta j,B_{R_{1}})>c
. We have two possibilities: the difference between
\Theta
and
B_{R_{1}}
is more concentrated either inside or outside
B_{R_{1}}
. Specifically

(i)

there exists
x_{j}\in\Theta_{j}\cap B_{R_{1}}
such that
d(x_{j},B_{R_{1}})>c
and
d_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial\Theta_{j}\setminus B_{R_{1}},B_{R_{1}})\to 0
;

(ii)

there exists
x_{j}\in\Theta_{j}\setminus B_{R_{1}}
such that
d(x_{j},B_{R_{1}})>c
.

In the case (i), the volume constraint gives the contradiction. Indeed, there exists
\bar{j}
such that
|\Theta_{j}|<|B_{R_{1}}|-c
, for any
j>\bar{j}
. From the volume constraint, we have an absurd, since it would be
|\Omega_{j}|\geq|A_{R_{1},R_{2}}|+c
.

Now, we consider the case (ii), where a key role is played by the set
({\Omega_{0}}_{j})_{R_{2}-R_{1}}
, in which, by the definition (
8
), the test function
w_{\Omega_{0}}
is constant.

We point out that, in view of the behavior of the outer asymmetry, we have
d_{\mathcal{H}}(B_{R_{1}},({\Omega_{0}}_{j})_{R_{2}-R_{1}})\to 0
; in particular, there exists
c>0
such that
d_{\mathcal{H}}(({\Omega_{0}}_{j})_{R_{2}-R_{1}},B_{R_{1}})<c/2
.
This inequality, together with the assumption (ii), implies that there exists two positive constants
c
and
c^{\prime}
such that
d_{\mathcal{H}}(({\Omega_{0}}_{j})_{R_{2}-R_{1}},\Theta_{j})>c/2
and, in view of the inradius constraint in the class
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
, it holds
|\Theta_{j}\setminus({\Omega_{0}}_{j})_{R_{2}-R_{1}}|>c^{\prime}/2
.

Since, by construction and by the volume constraint, we have that
K_{(\Omega_{0})_{j}}\subseteq({\Omega_{0}}_{j})_{R_{2}-R_{1}}
, then

d(x)<R_{2}-R_{1}-c/2
and hence
w_{(\Omega_{0})_{j}}(x)>G(R_{2}-R_{1}-c/2)
on
\Theta_{j}\setminus K_{(\Omega_{0})_{j}}
(and
|\nabla w_{(\Omega_{0})_{j}}|
is uniformly bounded from below by a positive constant as well on
\Theta_{j}\setminus K_{(\Omega_{0})_{j}}
). Consequently, there exists
c^{\prime\prime}>0
such that

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta_{j},{\Omega_{0}}_{j})=\int_{\Theta_{j}\setminus K_{(\Omega_{0})_{j}}}(|\nabla w_{(\Omega_{0})_{j}}^{2}|^{2}+w_{(\Omega_{0})_{j}}^{2}-z_{m}^{2})\ dx>c^{\prime\prime}>0,

that is absurd.

Therefore, in both cases we contradict the assumption
d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Theta j,B_{R_{1}})>c
. Hence we conclude.
∎

More generally, one can observe that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\alpha(\Omega_{j})=0\iff\lim_{j\to+\infty}d_{\mathcal{H}}({\Omega_{0}}_{j},B_{R_{2}})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{j\to+\infty}|\Theta_{j}\Delta B_{R_{1}}|=0.

Let us observe that, if we denote by
\chi_{E}
the characteristic function of a set
E
, then the weak weighted Fraenkel asymmetry (
10
) can be also written as

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta,{\Omega_{0}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}(|\nabla w_{(\Omega_{0})}|^{2}+w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}-z_{m}^{2})dx=Vol_{(|\nabla w_{(\Omega_{0})}|^{2}+w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}-z_{m}^{2})}(\Theta\Delta K_{\Omega_{0}}).

Finally, it is worth noticing that

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta,{\Omega_{0}})\geq 2z_{m}||w_{\Omega_{0}}-z_{m}||_{L^{1}(\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}})}.

3.
A quantitative result for a Steklov-type problem

The quantitative results for the Robin eigenvalue problem with negative boundary parameter are often solved through Steklov type problems (see
[
7
,
23
,
26
]
).
This strategy is suggested by a similar behavior of the functionals in terms of monotonicity and of eigenfunctions. Indeed, as a first trivial link between the two functionals, one can observe that the Rayleigh quotients of both the Robin and the Steklov first eigenvalues are monotonically decreasing with respect to the boundary integral.

The strategy of the proof is inspired by
[
23
,
26
]
, where the authors relate the Raylegh quotient of a suitable Steklov-type eigenvalue problem with a ratio involving only the boundary integral terms. Subsequently, the suitable representations of the outer and inner boundary are useful to estimate the difference between the eigenvalues on the radial and on the quasi-radial shape.

3.1.
A Steklov-type problem

The auxiliary
problem we deal with is the following:

(29)

\sigma_{1}(\Omega)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\\
\psi\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\psi^{2}\;dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\psi^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}.

If
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is a minimizer of (
30
), then it satisfies:

(30)

\begin{cases}-\Delta w+w=0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=\sigma_{1}(\Omega)w&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{0}\\
\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0&\text{on }\partial\Theta.\end{cases}

If
\Omega
is the spherical shell
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}=B_{R_{2}}\setminus\overline{B}_{R_{1}}
, the unique solution (up to multiplicative constant) of problem (
29
) is radial and it is given by

\mathcal{Z}(x)=\Psi(|x|),

where

(31)

\Psi(r)=r^{1-\frac{n}{2}}K_{\frac{n}{2}}(R_{1})I_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(r)+r^{1-\frac{n}{2}}I_{\frac{n}{2}}(R_{1})K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(r)\quad\forall r\in[R_{1},R_{2}].

The starting point of our analysis is the following upper bound for
\sigma_{1}(\Omega)
, only involving outer boundary integral terms.

Lemma 3.1
.

It holds that

(32)

\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\leq\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\mathcal{Z}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{Z}}{\partial\nu}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\mathcal{Z}^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}=:\dfrac{N(\Omega)}{D(\Omega)},

where
\mathcal{Z}(x)=\Psi(|x|)
is defined in (
31
). Moreover, equality in (
32
) holds iff
\Omega=A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
, that is the spherical shell with the same volume as
\Omega
and such that
P(\Omega_{0})=P(B_{R_{2}})
.

Proof.

Let
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be a solution of (
29
). For every
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, we have that

\displaystyle 0=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi w\;dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi\Delta w\;dx=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi w\;dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\nabla w\;dx-\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\varphi\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi w\;dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\nabla w\;dx-\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\varphi w\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Therefore, we have

\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\varphi w\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi w\;dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\nabla w\;dx.

If we choose
\varphi=w
, the divergence theorem and the fact that
w
is a solution of (
30
) lead to

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w^{2}\;dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\;dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}w^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}=

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w^{2}\;dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w\Delta w\;dx+\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}w\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}w^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}w\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}w^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}.

In this way, we have obtained the variational characterization of
\sigma_{1}(\Omega)
:

(33)

\sigma_{1}(\Omega)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\\
\psi\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\psi\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\psi^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}.

Finally, let
\mathcal{Z}
be the solution of (
29
) when
\Omega
is the spherical shell; the claimed result follows by testing the problem (
33
) with
z
.
∎

3.2.
The weighted quotient

Now, our aim is to write the ratio in the right-hand side of (
32
) in the case when
\Omega_{0}
is a
R_{2}
-nearly spherical set. It is worth noticing that, since in (
32
) an interior boundary term is not appearing, the same variational characterization holds when
\Omega
is a
(R_{1},R_{2})-
nearly annular set.

Keeping in mind the radial solution (
31
),
we define

(34)

\eta(r)=\left[K_{\frac{n}{2}}(R_{1})I_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(r)+I_{\frac{n}{2}}(R_{1})K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(r)\right]\quad\forall r\in[R_{1},R_{2}],

(35)

\mu(r)=\left[K_{\frac{n}{2}}(R_{1})I_{\frac{n}{2}}(r)-I_{\frac{n}{2}}(R_{1})K_{\frac{n}{2}}(r)\right]\quad\forall r\in[R_{1},R_{2}],

where the second relation is obtained by deriving the first one with the use of the derivative rules (
17
) and (
18
).

We observe that both the quantities
\eta(r)
and
\mu(r)
are strictly positive. Indeed,
\eta>0
is trivially verified and, as far as
\mu
is concerned, for any
\nu>-1
, we have that

\dfrac{I_{\nu+1}}{K_{\nu+1}}(R_{1})<\dfrac{I_{\nu+1}}{K_{\nu+1}}(r)

for any
r>R_{1}
, since

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\dfrac{I_{\nu}}{K_{\nu}}(r)\right]=\dfrac{\left(I_{\nu-1}(r)+I_{\nu+1}(r)\right)K_{\nu}(r)+I_{\nu}(r)\left(K_{\nu-1}(r)+K_{\nu+1}(r)\right)}{2K_{\nu}(x)^{2}}>0.

Furthermore, the monotonicity of
I_{\nu}
and
K_{\nu}
with respect to the index (see as a reference
[
24
]
) implies that
\eta(r)>\mu(r)
.
Let us observe that, by (
17
) and (
18
), the following derivation rules also hold:

(36)

\left(r^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\eta(r)\right)^{\prime}=r^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\mu(r);\quad\left(r^{-\frac{n}{2}}\mu(r)\right)^{\prime}=r^{-\frac{n}{2}}\eta(r);

(37)

\eta^{\prime}(r)=\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)\frac{\eta(r)}{r}+\mu(r);\qquad\mu^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{n}{2}\frac{\mu(r)}{r}+\eta(r).

Hence, the functions
\eta
and
\mu
, defined in (
34
) and (
35
) respectively, lead to the definitions of the integrand functions in the quotient (
32
), that are

(38)

h(r)=r^{2-n}\eta^{2}(r)\quad\forall r\in[R_{1},R_{2}],

(39)

f(r)=\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{2}=r^{2-n}\eta(r)\mu(r)\quad\forall r\in[R_{1},R_{2}].

Finally, using the parametrization of
R_{2}-
nearly spherical sets (see Definition (
2.4
)), we get:

(40)

N(\Omega)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(R_{2}+u)\left(R_{2}+u\right)^{n-1}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

(41)

D(\Omega)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h(R_{2}+u)\left(R_{2}+u\right)^{n-1}\sqrt{1+\dfrac{|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}}{\left(R_{2}+u\right)^{2}}}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Let us notice that, in the case
\Omega=A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
, we have

\sigma_{1}(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})=\dfrac{f(R_{2})}{h(R_{2})}.

The following Lemma is a consequence of the fact that
the functions
h
and
f
defined in (
38
) and (
39
) are analytic.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any parametrization couple
u,v\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
of a
(R_{1},R_{2})-
nearly annular set and for every
0<\varepsilon<R_{1}/2
such that
||u||_{W^{1,\infty}},||v||_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq\varepsilon
, there exists
C=C(n,R_{1},R_{2})>0
such that, on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, we have:

(42)

\left|h(R_{2}+u)-h(R_{2})-h^{\prime}(R_{2})u-h^{\prime\prime}(R_{2})\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right|\leq K\varepsilon u^{2},

(43)

\left|h(R_{1}+v)-h(R_{1})-h^{\prime}(R_{1})v-h^{\prime\prime}(R_{1})\frac{v^{2}}{2}\right|\leq K\varepsilon v^{2},

(44)

\left|f(R_{2}+u)-f(R_{2})-f^{\prime}(R_{2})u-f^{\prime\prime}(R_{2})\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right|\leq K\varepsilon u^{2}.

The following monotonicity result will be useful in proving the stability result for
\sigma_{1}(\Omega)
. The proof generalizes a result contained in
[
4
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
R_{1}>0
, we have that

\left(\frac{\eta(r)}{\mu(r)}\right)^{\prime}<0\quad\forall\ r\geq R_{1},

where
\eta
and
\mu
are defined in (
34
) and (
35
).

Proof.

Since
\eta(r)
is a linear combination of the modified Bessel functions
I_{\nu}
and
K_{\nu}
, it satisfies the following equation:

\eta^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{r}\eta^{\prime}-\frac{r^{2}+(\frac{n}{2}-1)^{2}}{r^{2}}\eta=0,

that is

r^{2}\eta^{\prime}\eta^{\prime\prime}+r\eta^{\prime 2}-\left(r^{2}+\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)^{2}\right)\eta\eta^{\prime}=0,

i.e.

r\eta^{\prime}(r\eta^{\prime})^{\prime}-\left(r^{2}+\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)^{2}\right)\eta\eta^{\prime}=0.

Therefore, dividing by
r\eta^{\prime}
, we obtain:

(45)

(r\eta^{\prime})^{\prime}=\frac{r^{2}+(\frac{n}{2}-1)^{2}}{r}\eta.

In the same way, we also gain:

(46)

(r\mu^{\prime})^{\prime}=\frac{r^{2}+(\frac{n}{2})^{2}}{r}\mu.

By using (
45
) and (
46
), we are able to write the following equality

\int_{0}^{r}[\eta(s\mu^{\prime})^{\prime}-\mu(s\eta^{\prime})^{\prime}]ds=(n-1)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\eta(s)\mu(s)}{s}ds>0.

By applying the Green Theorem on the l.h.s, we have

s[\eta\mu^{\prime}-\mu\eta^{\prime}]_{0}^{r}=(n-1)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\eta(s)\mu(s)}{s}ds>0,

that leads to

(47)

\frac{\mu^{\prime}(r)}{\mu(r)}-\frac{\eta^{\prime}(r)}{\eta(r)}=\frac{n-1}{r\eta(r)\mu(r)}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\eta(s)\mu(s)}{s}ds>0\quad\forall r>0.

By using the relation (
47
), the thesis follows by observing that

\left(\dfrac{\eta(r)}{\mu(r)}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{\eta^{\prime}(r)\mu(r)-\eta(r)\mu^{\prime}(r)}{\mu^{2}(r)}=\frac{\eta^{\prime}(r)\mu(r)-\eta(r)\mu^{\prime}(r)}{\mu(r)\eta(r)}\frac{\eta(r)}{\mu(r)}=\left(\frac{\eta^{\prime}(r)}{\eta(r)}-\frac{\mu^{\prime}(r)}{\mu(r)}\right)\frac{\eta(r)}{\mu(r)}<0.

∎

We end this section with a very technical Lemma establishing the sign of a certain quantity, that will be useful to obtain the stability result. The proof is based on the second part of
[
26
, Prop. 2.5]
. For sake of simplicity, we set:

(48)

\begin{split}A_{0}=&R_{2}^{n-1}f(R_{2})h(R_{2}),\\
A_{1}=&R_{2}^{n-1}[f(R_{2})h^{\prime}(R_{2})-f^{\prime}(R_{2})h(R_{2})],\\
A_{2}=&R_{2}^{n-1}[f(R_{2})h^{\prime\prime}(R_{2})-f^{\prime\prime}(R_{2})h(R_{2})].\end{split}

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
R_{2}>0
, we have that

(49)

(n-1)R_{2}A_{1}+R_{2}^{2}A_{2}+2nA_{0}>0.

Proof.

If we put in evidence
R_{2}^{2}
in (
49
), we obtain
R_{2}^{2}[\frac{(n-1)}{R_{2}}A_{1}+A_{2}+\frac{2n}{R_{2}^{2}}A_{0}]>0
and, so, we have to prove that

\left[\frac{(n-1)}{R_{2}}A_{1}+A_{2}+\frac{2n}{R_{2}^{2}}A_{0}\right]=[r^{n-1}(f(r)h^{\prime}(r)-h(r)f^{\prime}(r)]^{\prime}+2nr^{n-3}h(r)f(r)>0

for
r=R_{2}
. By using the definitions of
h
and
f
in (
38
)-(
39
) and the derivation rules (
36
), the last relation becomes

[r^{2-n}(r\eta^{2}(r)\mu^{2}(r)-r\eta^{4}(r)+(n-1)\eta^{3}(r)\mu(r))]^{\prime}+2nr^{1-n}\eta^{3}(r)\mu(r)>0

and hence

r^{1-n}\eta(r)\mu(r)[2r^{2}\mu^{2}(r)+2(n-1)r\eta(r)\mu(r)+(n+1-2r^{2})\eta^{2}(r)]>0.

If we set

\zeta(r):=2r^{2}\mu^{2}(r)+2(n-1)r\eta(r)\mu(r)+(n+1-2r^{2})\eta^{2}(r),

we need to show that
\zeta(r)>0
.
We observe that

\zeta^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{r}\bigl(\zeta(r)+4r\eta(r)\mu(r)+(n^{2}-2n-3)\eta^{2}(r)\bigr).

If
n\geq 3
, since
n^{2}-2n-3\geq 0
and
\eta(r),\mu(r)>0
for any
r>0
, we deduce that
r\zeta^{\prime}(r)\geq\zeta(r)
and hence that
\zeta(r)>0
for any
r>0
. Otherwise, if
n=2
, we observe that the function
\xi(r)=r\zeta^{\prime}(r)
is increasing for
r>0
because, by using (
37
), we have that

\xi^{\prime}(r)=(r\zeta^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}=6r\mu^{2}(r)+2r\eta^{2}(r)>0\quad\forall r>0.

Moreover, it is immediate that
\xi^{\prime}(0)=\xi(0)=0
. This means that
\xi(r)>0
and hence
\zeta^{\prime}(r)>0
for any
r>0
. Finally, since
\zeta(0)=0
, this implies that
\zeta(r)>0
for any
r>0
.
∎

3.3.
The stability result

A crucial result to obtain the stability with respect to the outer boundary is the following stability issue for the functional
\Omega\mapsto\frac{N(\Omega)}{D(\Omega)}
. Up to some necessary technical modification, the proof follows the scheme in
[
23
,
26
]
.

From now on, we say that
\Omega
is an admissible set if
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
with
\Omega_{0}
convex, bounded and open and
\Theta\subset\subset\Omega_{0}
a finite union of open sets homeomorphic to balls.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<+\infty
.
There exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,R_{1},R_{2})>0
such that, for any admissible set
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
with
\Omega_{0}
a
R_{2}
-nearly spherical set and the outer boundary
\partial\Omega_{0}
parametrized by
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, and for every
0<\varepsilon<R_{2}/2
such that
||u||_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq\varepsilon
, the following quantitative inequality holds true:

\frac{N(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})D(\Omega)-D(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})N(\Omega)}{n\omega_{n}}\geq C_{1}(n,R_{1},R_{2})||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

We start by performing a suitable “Taylor expansion” in terms of the deformations of
\partial\Omega_{0}
compared to
\partial B_{R_{2}}
. By using the characterization of
N(\Omega)
and
D(\Omega)
in (
40
) and (
41
), we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{N(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})D(\Omega)-D(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})N(\Omega)}{n\omega_{n}}&=f(R_{2})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h(R_{2}+u)(R_{2}+u)^{n-1}\sqrt{1+\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}}{(R_{2}+u)^{2}}}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\quad-h(R_{2})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(R_{2}+u)(R_{2}+u)^{n-1}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

Then, by using the Lemmata
2.6
and
3.2
, we get

(50)

\begin{split}\frac{N(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})D(\Omega)-D(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})N(\Omega)}{n\omega_{n}}&\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}R_{2}^{2n-2}[f(R_{2})h^{\prime}(R_{2})-f^{\prime}(R_{2})h(R_{2})]u\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}R_{2}^{2n-3}[2(n-1)(f(R_{2})h^{\prime}(R_{2})-f^{\prime}(R_{2})h(R_{2}))]\frac{u^{2}}{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}R_{2}^{2n-2}[f(R_{2})h^{\prime\prime}(R_{2})-f^{\prime\prime}(R_{2})h(R_{2})]\frac{u^{2}}{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}R_{2}^{2n-4}f(R_{2})h(R_{2})\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}}{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\varepsilon C_{1}\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\\
\end{split}

Now, using the notation in (
48
), inequality (
50
) becomes

(51)

\begin{split}\frac{N(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})D(\Omega)-D(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})N(\Omega)}{n\omega_{n}}&\geq R_{2}^{n-1}A_{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+R_{2}^{n-2}[2(n-1)A_{1}+R_{2}A_{2}]\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{u^{2}}{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\quad+R_{2}^{n-3}A_{0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}}{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\varepsilon C_{1}\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Moreover, by the perimeter constraint given by (
25
), then (
51
) becomes

(52)

\begin{split}\frac{N(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})D(\Omega)-D(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})N(\Omega)}{n\omega_{n}}&\geq R_{2}^{n-2}\frac{nA_{1}+R_{2}A_{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&+R_{2}^{n-3}\left(\frac{A_{0}}{2}-\frac{R_{2}A_{1}}{2(n-1)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\varepsilon C_{1}\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Firstly, let us note that the term in round parenthesis is positive because it is the sum of two positive terms. Indeed,
A_{0}
, defined in (
48
), is positive,
since
h>0
and
f>0
(defined respectevely in (
38
) and (
39
));
meanwhile

(53)

A_{1}=R_{2}^{n-1}[f(R_{2})h^{\prime}(R_{2})-f^{\prime}(R_{2})h(R_{2})]=f^{2}(R_{2})\left[\frac{h(r)}{f(r)}\right]^{\prime}_{r=R_{2}}=f^{2}(R_{2})\left[\frac{\eta(r)}{\mu(r)}\right]^{\prime}_{r=R_{2}}<0,

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
3.3
.

Therefore, since it is possible to take
\varepsilon C_{1}
arbitrarily small, where the quantity
C_{1}
does not depend on
\varepsilon
, the proof is concluded if we verify that

(54)

\frac{nA_{1}+R_{2}A_{2}}{2}\geq 0.

Otherwise, if (
54
) does not hold (i.e. if
\frac{nA_{1}+R_{2}A_{2}}{2}<0
), we go on with the estimate from below. More precisely, by using the Poincaré inequality (
28
), from (
52
), we get

\begin{split}\frac{N(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})D(\Omega)-D(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})N(\Omega)}{n\omega_{n}}&\geq\frac{R_{2}^{n-3}}{4n}\left(\frac{n^{2}-3n}{n-1}R_{2}A_{1}+R_{2}^{2}A_{2}+2nA_{0}\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\quad-\varepsilon C_{1}\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Finally, in order to conclude the proof, it remains to show that

\left(\frac{n^{2}-3n}{n-1}R_{2}A_{1}+R_{2}^{2}A_{2}+2nA_{0}\right)>0.

This term can be written as the sum of two positive quantities in the following way:

\frac{n^{2}-3n}{n-1}R_{2}A_{1}+R_{2}^{2}A_{2}+2nA_{0}=\left[(n-1)R_{2}A_{1}+R_{2}^{2}A_{2}+2nA_{0}\right]-\frac{n+1}{n-1}R_{2}A_{1}.

Indeed, the positive sign of the first addendum of the right hand side of the last equality is proved in Lemma
3.4
and the sign of the second one follows from (
53
).
∎

We point out that the previous result holds in particular if
\Omega
is a
(R_{1},R_{2})-
nearly annular set.

An immediate consequence of Proposition
3.5
is the following stability result for
\sigma_{1}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<+\infty
.
There exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,R_{1},R_{2})>0
such that, for any for any admissible set
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
with
\Omega_{0}
a
R_{2}
-nearly spherical set and the outer boundary parametrized by
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, and for every
0<\varepsilon<R_{2}/2
such that
||u||_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq\varepsilon
, the following stability inequality holds true:

\sigma_{1}(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\geq C_{1}(n,R_{1},R_{2})||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Moreover, the constant
C_{1}(n,R_{1},R_{2})
depends continuously (actually analytically) on
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
.

Proof.

The proof follows by combining Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.5
.
∎

4.
Proof of the Main Results

In this section we provide the proof of the main results. First of all, we use the results of Section
3.3
related to the auxiliary problem (
29
) to gain the outer stability result. The strategy follows an idea developed in
[
23
,
26
]
, linking the stability of the Steklov-Neumann eigenvalue (
29
) to the stability of the Robin-Neumann eigenvalue (
1
).

Furthermore, in the second part of the section, we develop the key idea of considering an additional term to take into account the asymmetry of the inner boundary.

Finally, in order to complete the proof of Main Theorem 2, we show that we can obtain the stability result in
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
reducing our study to the nearly annular sets.

4.1.
Outer asymmetry

At this stage, we are able to restrict our study to the class of nearly annular sets. We will use the quantitative estimate given through the Steklov-Neumann eigenvalues in Section
3.3
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any admissible set
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
with
\Omega_{0}
a
R_{2}
-nearly spherical set, with
P(\Omega_{0})=n\omega_{n}R_{2}^{n-1}
and
|\Omega|=|A_{R_{1},R_{2}}|
, having the outer boundary parametrized by
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, and for every
0<\varepsilon<R_{2}/2
such that
||u||_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq\varepsilon
, it holds

(55)

\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\geq C(n,\beta,R_{1},R_{2})\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
and let
\Omega
be chosen as in the statement. The map
\beta\mapsto\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)
is continuous and monotonically increasing from
(-\infty,0)
onto
(-\infty,0)
. Then, there exists
\bar{\beta}\leq\beta
such that

\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega)=\lambda_{1}({\bar{\beta}},A_{R_{1},R_{2}}).

Hence,

\lambda_{1}({\beta},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega)=\lambda_{1}({\beta},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}({\bar{\beta}},A_{R_{1},R_{2}}).

Let us consider the positive constant

\kappa:=\sqrt{|\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega)|}=\sqrt{|\lambda_{1}({\bar{\beta}},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})|};

for the rescaled sets
\kappa\Omega
and
\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
it holds that

\displaystyle-\kappa^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega)

\displaystyle=\kappa^{2}\lambda_{1}\left({\frac{\beta}{\kappa}},\kappa\Omega\right)\leq\kappa^{2}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\kappa\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\>dx+\frac{\beta}{\kappa}\int_{\partial(\kappa\Omega_{0})}\psi^{2}\>d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\kappa\Omega}\psi^{2}\>dx}\quad\forall\psi\in H^{1}(\kappa\Omega)

and

\displaystyle-\kappa^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}({\bar{\beta}},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})

\displaystyle=\kappa^{2}\lambda_{1}\left({\frac{\bar{\beta}}{\kappa}},\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}\right)\leq\kappa^{2}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}|\nabla\Psi|^{2}\>dx+\frac{\bar{\beta}}{\kappa}\int_{\partial(\kappa B_{R_{2}})}\Psi^{2}\>d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}\Psi^{2}\>dx}\quad\forall\Psi\in H^{1}(\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}),

with equality holding if
\psi
and
\Psi
are, respectively, the eigenfunctions for the Robin-Neumann problem on
\kappa\Omega
with parameter
\beta
and on
\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
with boundary parameter
\bar{\beta}
. Thus we get

-\frac{\beta}{\kappa}\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\kappa\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\>dx+\int_{\kappa\Omega}\psi^{2}\>dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\partial(\kappa\Omega_{0})}\psi^{2}\>d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}\quad\forall\psi\in H^{1}(\kappa\Omega)

and

-\frac{\bar{\beta}}{\kappa}\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}|\nabla\Psi|^{2}\>dx+\int_{\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}\Psi^{2}\>dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\partial(\kappa B_{R_{2}})}\Psi^{2}\>d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}\quad\forall\Psi\in H^{1}(\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}).

It follows that the infimum for the Steklov-Neumann problem (
30
) is achieved on
\kappa\Omega
and
\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
if
\psi
and
\Psi
are, respectively, the eigenfunctions for the Robin-Neumann problem on
\kappa\Omega
with parameter
\beta
and on
\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
with boundary parameter
\bar{\beta}
. Therefore, we obtain

\sigma_{1}(\kappa\Omega)=-\frac{\beta}{\kappa},\quad\sigma_{1}(\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}})=-\frac{\bar{\beta}}{\kappa}.

If we denote with
z_{\beta}
the first eigenfunction of
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
for the Robin-Neumann problem with parameter
\beta
, using the variational characterization of
\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)
and
\lambda_{1}({\bar{\beta}},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})
we have that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}({\beta},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}({\bar{\beta}},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})\geq({\beta}-\bar{\beta})\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{R_{2}}}z_{\beta}^{2}\>d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}z_{\beta}^{2}\>dx}

\displaystyle=C_{2}(n,\beta,R_{1},R_{2})(\beta-\bar{\beta})\geq\kappa C_{2}(n,\beta,R_{1},R_{2})(\sigma_{1}(\kappa A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\sigma_{1}(\kappa\Omega))

\displaystyle\geq\kappa C_{2}(n,\beta,R_{1},R_{2})C_{1}(n,\kappa^{\frac{1}{n}}R_{1},\kappa^{\frac{1}{n}}R_{2})||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
C_{1}
is the constant found in Proposition
3.5
. The conclusion follows by setting
C:=\kappa C_{2}C_{1}
.
∎

Now we are in position to prove the main stability result regarding the outer boundary.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<+\infty
. There exist a positive constant
C(n,R_{1},R_{2},\beta)
such that, for every admissible set
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
with
\Omega_{0}
a
R_{2}
-nearly spherical set, we have

\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\geq C(n,R_{1},R_{2},\beta)g(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0}))

where
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}
is the Hausdorff asymmetry defined in (
7
) and
g
is the positive increasing function defined by

g(s)=\begin{cases}s^{2}&\text{if $n=2$}\\
f^{-1}(s^{2})&\text{if $n=3$}\\
s^{\frac{n+1}{2}}&\text{if $n\geq 4$},\end{cases}

with
f(t)=\sqrt{t\log\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)}
for
0<t<e^{-1}
.

Proof.

We have that

\partial\Omega_{0}=\{\xi(R_{2}+u(\xi)),\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}

for some
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
with
\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}<R_{2}/2
. Let
\rho_{2}
such that
|B_{\rho_{2}}|=|\Omega_{0}|
; then, we have that

(56)

\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(R_{2}+u)^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\omega_{n}\rho_{2}^{n}.

Now, let us define the function
h:=(R_{2}+u)^{n}-\rho_{2}^{n}
.
By a straightforward expansion of the left hand side of (
56
), we get

R_{2}^{n}-\rho_{2}^{n}=-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}u^{k}R_{2}^{n-k}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

which immediately implies
|R_{2}-\rho_{2}|<C(n)\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
.
Hence, it holds

(57)

C_{2}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{3}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}},

where
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
are constant depending only on the dimension.
Moreover, since

\nabla_{\tau}h=n(R_{2}+u)^{n-1}\nabla_{\tau}u,

then

C_{4}\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\nabla_{\tau}h\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{5}\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}},

where
C_{4},C_{5}
depend on the dimension and on
R_{2}
.
Moreover, from (
56
), we know that
h
has zero integral, thus we can apply Lemma
2.8
to
h
and use (
57
) to infer

(58)

\displaystyle||u||_{L^{\infty}}^{n-1}\leq\begin{cases}\pi\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}}&n=2\\
4||\nabla_{\tau}u||^{2}_{L^{2}}\log\frac{8e||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{\infty}}^{n-1}}{||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{2}}^{2}}&n=3\\[5.69046pt]
C(n)||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{2}}^{2}||\nabla_{\tau}u||_{L^{\infty}}^{n-3}\,\,&n\geq 4.\end{cases}

For the sake of brevity, we show the conclusion of the proof only for
n\geq 4
, since for
n=2,3
the argument is the same. Inequality (
58
) and Lemma
2.9
lead to

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{n-1}\leq C(n)\|D_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{\frac{n-3}{2}},

and hence we have

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\leq C(n)\|D_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}.

Recalling now that
R_{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}=d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0},B_{R_{2}})
, we get

\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\geq C_{6}(n,R_{2})g(d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0},B_{R_{2}})).

Plugging the previous estimate in (
55
) and recalling that
\delta_{0}
depends only on
n,\beta,R_{1},R_{2}
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq C_{7}(n,\beta,R_{1},R_{2},\delta_{0})\|\nabla_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\geq C(n,\beta,R_{1},R_{2})g(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{0})).

∎

4.2.
Inner asymmetry

In this section we explain the key idea to keep into account both inner and outer perturbations of the optimal set
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
. As previously explained, the Neumann condition on the inner boundary and the measure constraint on
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
do not seem to allow a Fuglede type approach, since we do not have any control on
d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Theta,B_{R_{1}})
. Intuitively, this is due to lack of a constraint on
P(\Theta)
and to the fact that in the Rayleigh quotient (
2
), there is no boundary integral on
\partial\Theta
. Moreover, if
\Omega
is nearly annular and one tries to improve the quantitative result of Proposition
3.5
, for example by using the measure constraint (
26
), only the terms involving the outer boundary profile
u
remain and the ones containing the inner boundary profile
v
cancel each other out.

By using the weak asymmetry relative to the outer box defined in (
10
), we prove the following result.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<+\infty
. Then, for every
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\Omega_{0}
convex and
\Theta
an admissible hole such that
\overline{\Theta}\subset\Omega_{0}
,
P(\Omega_{0})=P(B_{R_{2}})
and
|\Omega|=|A_{R_{1},R_{2}}|
,
it holds

\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\geq\min\{1,|\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})|\}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0}),

where
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta;\Omega_{0})
is defined in (
10
).

Proof.

Let
z\in H^{1}(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})
be the first positive eigenfunction of
\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})
with
\|z\|_{L^{2}(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})}=1
, let
w_{\Omega_{0}}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{0})
the test function defined in (
8
) and
t_{\Omega_{0}},K_{\Omega_{0}}
as in the Definition
1.1
.

We observe that
t_{\Omega_{0}}\geq R_{2}-R_{1}
in view of the isoperimetric inequality; it follows
w_{\Omega_{0}}\geq z_{m}
in
\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{K_{\Omega_{0}}}
and
w_{\Omega_{0}}=z_{m}
in
K_{\Omega_{0}}
.

We point out that the choice of
w_{\Omega_{0}}
depends only on
\Omega_{0}
and thus it is a suitable web function for both
\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
and
\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{K_{\Omega_{0}}}
. From
[
47
, eq.(3.9),(3.10),(3.11)]
applied to the admissible set
\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{K_{\Omega_{0}}}
, we have that

(59)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx\leq\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}|\nabla z|^{2}\>dx,&\quad\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}w^{2}_{\Omega_{0}}\>d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial B_{R_{2}}}z^{2}\>d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\\
\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}w^{2}_{\Omega_{0}}\>dx&\leq\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}z^{2}\>dx.\end{split}

Moreover, it holds the following

(60)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{0}}w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}\>dx&=\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus\Theta}w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}\>dx+\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}\>dx+\int_{K_{\Omega_{0}}\cap\Theta}w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}\>dx\\
\end{split}

and, by using (
59
), it holds

(61)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{0}}w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}\>dx&=\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}\>dx+\int_{K_{\Omega_{0}}\setminus\Theta}w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}\>dx+\int_{K_{\Omega_{0}}\cap\Theta}w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}\>dx\\
&\leq\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}z^{2}\>dx+z_{m}^{2}|\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}|+\int_{K_{\Omega_{0}}\cap\Theta}w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}\>dx,\end{split}

since
|K_{\Omega_{0}}\setminus\Theta|=|\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}|
, which follows from the fact that
|K_{\Omega_{0}}|=|\Theta|
.
Therefore, by combining (
60
) and (
61
), we get

(62)

\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus\Theta}w^{2}_{\Omega_{0}}\>dx\leq\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}z^{2}\>dx-\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}(w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}-z_{m}^{2})\>dx.

Now, by splitting in the same way also
\int_{\Omega_{0}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx
and recalling that
w_{\Omega_{0}}
is constant in
K_{\Omega_{0}}
, we get

(63)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{0}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx&=\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus\Theta}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx+\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx+\int_{K_{\Omega_{0}}\cap\Theta}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus\Theta}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx+\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx\end{split}

and, in view of (
59
),

(64)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{0}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx&=\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx+\int_{K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx\leq\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}|\nabla z|^{2}\>dx.\end{split}

By combining (
63
) and (
64
) we get

(65)

\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus\Theta}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx\leq\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}|\nabla z|^{2}\>dx-\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx.

Now, by using
w_{\Omega_{0}}
as a test function for
\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)
, we have, in view of (
59
), (
62
) and (
65
)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus\Theta}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}w^{2}_{\Omega_{0}}\>d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{0}\setminus\Theta}w^{2}_{\Omega_{0}}\>dx}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}|\nabla z|^{2}\>dx+\beta\int_{\partial A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}z^{2}\>d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx}{\displaystyle\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}z^{2}\>dx-\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}(w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}-z_{m}^{2})\>dx}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx}{\displaystyle 1-\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}(w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}-z_{m}^{2})\>dx},

and thus, from
-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\geq-\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})=|\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})|
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}(w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}-z_{m}^{2})\>dx+\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx

\displaystyle\geq|\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})|\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}(w_{\Omega_{0}}^{2}-z_{m}^{2})\>dx+\int_{\Theta\setminus K_{\Omega_{0}}}|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{2}\>dx,

achieving the thesis.
∎

4.3.
Towards the nearly annular sets

We want to show that the stability result in the class
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
, defined in (
5
), gets meaningful if we reduce to nearly annular sets. To do that, the first step is to provide a uniform bound on the diameters of the sets whose eigenvalues are "not so far" from the optimum. This kind of isodiametric control of the eigenvalues is common when dealing with maximization problems in shape optimization and it often provides extra compactness when working on existence problems without a bounded design region (see for instance the isodiametric control of the Robin spectrum proved in
[
18
]
or for the Steklov spectrum proved in
[
12
]
, both valid also for higher eigenvalues in a wider class of sets).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<+\infty
. There exists a positive constant
C(R_{1},R_{2},n,\beta)
such that, for every
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\Omega_{0}
and
\Theta
convex sets such that
\overline{\Theta}\subset\Omega_{0}
,
P(\Omega_{0})=P(B_{R_{2}})
and
|\Omega|=|A_{R_{1},R_{2}}|
, if

\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega)>2\lambda_{1}({\beta},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})

then

\text{diam}(\Omega)<C(R_{1},R_{2},n,\beta).

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward adaptation to our context of
[
23
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Let us argue by contradiction and suppose that there exists a sequence
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of domains of the form
\Omega_{j}={\Omega_{0}}_{j}\setminus\overline{\Theta_{j}}
such that

\lambda_{1}({\beta},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})\geq\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega_{j})>2\lambda_{1}({\beta},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})\ \text{and}\ \text{diam}(\Omega_{j})=\text{diam}({\Omega_{0}}_{j})\to+\infty.

In view of the convexity of
{\Omega_{0}}_{j}
and of the constraint
P({\Omega_{0}}_{j})=m
, the sequence
\{\rho_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of the inradii of
\{{\Omega_{0}}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is necessarily vanishing. Recalling that, for any convex set
A
with inradius
\rho
, it holds

|A|\leq\rho P(A)

(see, for instance,
[
17
, Prop. 2.4.3]
), we deduce that
|{\Omega_{0}}_{j}|
vanishes as
j
goes to
+\infty
. Now, using the charachteristic function
\chi_{\Omega_{j}}
as a test for
\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega_{j})
, we obtain

\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega_{j})\leq-\beta\frac{P({\Omega_{0}}_{j})}{|\Omega_{j}|}\leq-\beta\frac{P({\Omega_{0}}_{j})}{{|{\Omega_{0}}_{j}}|}\to-\infty,

in contradiction with the lower bound on
\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega_{j})
.
∎

We point out that the previous result is immediate if we restrict to the class
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
.

Now, we provide two useful semicontinuity results: the first one gives the lower semicontinuity of the boundary integral term and the second one give the upper semicontinuity of the map
\Omega\mapsto\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)
.

Lemma 4.5

(
[
22
]
, Proposition 2.1)
.

Let
E_{j},E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be convex domains such that
E_{j}\to E
in the sense of Hausdorff and in measure. Let
w_{j},w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If
w_{j}\rightharpoonup w
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then it holds

\int_{\partial E}w^{2}\>d\sigma\leq\liminf_{j}\int_{\partial E_{j}}w_{j}^{2}\>d\sigma

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\Omega_{j},\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be in the class
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
, with
\Omega_{j}\to\Omega
in the sense of Hausdorff and in measure. Then

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega_{j})\leq\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega).

Proof.

We proceed similarly to
[
22
, Prop. 3.1]
, being careful to the fact that the boundary integral keeps into account only
\partial\Omega_{0}
. Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
an eigenfunction for
\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)
, and let
\overline{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
an extension of
u
to the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let us notice that, in view of the hypotheses, the convergence of the convex sets
{\Omega_{0}}_{j}\to\Omega_{0}
in the sense of Hausdorff and in measure holds. Thus we have

\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\overline{u}^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\partial{\Omega_{0}}_{j}}\overline{u}^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

as a consequence of Lemma
4.5
. Moreover, both volume integrals are continuous in view of the convergence in measure
\Omega_{j}\to\Omega
and this implies the upper semicontinuity of the Rayleigh quotients, defined in (
2
). Therefore, we have

\lambda_{1}({\beta},\Omega)=R_{\Omega}(u)=R_{\Omega}(\overline{u})\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}R_{\Omega_{j}}(\overline{u})\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega_{j}).

∎

Since
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
is the unique maximizer of
\lambda_{1}(\beta,\cdot)
in our class of admissible sets, as a consequence of the upper semicontinuity of
\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)
and of the isodiametric control in Lemma
4.4
, the following convergence result holds.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
be a maximizing sequence for Problem (
6
) with
\Omega_{j}
is barycentered at the origin. Then

d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{j},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})\to 0.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
4.4
, the diameters of the convex sets
{\Omega_{0}}_{j}
and
\Theta_{j}
are uniformly bounded.

The uniform upper bound on the diameters and the uniform lower bound
\rho(\Theta_{j})>\vartheta_{R_{1},R_{2}}
imply that, up to subsequences, there exist an open bounded convex set
\Omega_{0}
and an open bounded convex set
\Theta\subset\subset\Omega_{0}
such that
\Omega:=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}\in\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
and
d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{j},\Omega)\to 0
. Since
\lambda_{1}(\beta,\cdot)
is upper semicontinuous in view of Lemma
4.6
, we obtain that

\max_{\mathcal{A}_{R_{1},R_{2}}}\lambda_{1}(\beta,\cdot)=\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega_{j})\leq\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega).

So, since the spherical shell
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
is the unique maximizer for
\lambda_{1}(\beta,\cdot)
in
\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
, we get that
\Omega=A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
necessarily.
∎

As a consequence of the previous lemma, we can actually restrict our main stability result to nearly spherical sets barycentered in the origin.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<+\infty
. There exists a positive constant
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,\beta,R_{1},R_{2})
such that, if
\Omega\in\mathcal{T}_{R_{1},R_{2}}
and

\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\leq\delta_{0}

then, up to a translation,
\Omega
is a
(R_{1},R_{2})-
nearly annular set.

Proof.

The proof is based on a straightforward contradiction argument. Indeed, let us suppose that, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an admissible set
\Omega_{j}={\Omega_{0}}_{j}\setminus\overline{\Theta_{j}}
such that

(66)

\lambda_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega_{j})\leq\frac{1}{j}

and any translations of
\Omega_{j}
is not a
(R_{1},R_{2})-
nearly annular set, i.e.

(67)

\text{either}\quad d_{\mathcal{H}}({\Omega_{0}}_{j},B_{R_{2}})>\frac{R_{2}}{2}\quad\text{or}\quad d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Theta_{j},B_{R_{1}})>\frac{R_{1}}{2}.

By (
66
),
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a maximizing sequence for Problem (
6
); if we consider the sequence (still denoted by
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
) of translated sets with barycenter at the origin, we are under the hypotheses of Lemma
4.7
and thus we get
d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega_{j},A_{R_{1},R_{2}})\to 0
, that is in contradiction with (
67
).
∎

4.4.
Conclusion

Now we are in position to prove both the Main Theorems stated in the introduction.

Proof of the Main Theorem 1.

It is a straightforward combination of Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
4.3
.
∎

Proof of the Main Theorem 2.

It is a straightforward combination of Theorem
4.2
, Theorem
4.3
and Lemma
4.8
, which ensures us that, if
\delta_{0}
is small enough, then we can suppose without loss of generality that
\Omega

(R_{1},R_{2})-
annular set with barycenter at the origin,

∎

Remark 4.9
.

We point out that, as usual when dealing with negative Robin boundary conditions, the constant

5.
Open problems

In this Section, we collect some open problems.

Open Problem 5.1
.

By repeating the very same computations as in Theorem
4.3
for the
p
-Laplace Robin-Neumann eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{(p)}(\beta,\Omega)
, we get

\lambda^{(p)}_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})-\lambda_{1}^{(p)}(\beta,\Omega)\geq\min\{1,|\lambda^{(p)}_{1}(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})|\}\int_{\Theta\setminus K}(|\nabla w_{\Omega_{0}}|^{p}+w_{\Omega_{0}}^{p}-z_{m}^{p})\>dx.

To get a complete stability result also for the nonlinear case we need an estimate for the asymmetry of
\Omega_{0}
which at the moment does not seem available, since our argument based on a Fuglede type approach does not seem to apply in the nonlinear framework due to the lack of an explicit expression for
z
.

Moreover, we ask whether the method used in this paper may be applied in more general frameworks, as e.g. the Minkowski spaces, in which the standard Euclidean norm is replaced by a Finsler norm
[
51
]
.
As far as Robin boundary conditions in the anisotropic setting, we refer e.g to
[
48
,
8
]
, where isoperimetric inequalities, involving possibly nonlinear elliptic operators, are proved. It remains open the problem of the generalisation to holed domains with Robin- Neumann anisotropic boundary conditions and the relative quantitative estimates.

Open Problem 5.2
.

It would be interesting to consider the case
\beta>0
. In
[
47
]
, it is proved that the spherical shell such that
|A|=|\Omega|
and
P(B_{R_{2}})=P(\Omega_{0})
is maximizing:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\beta,\Omega)\leq\lambda_{1}(\beta,A).

A stability result in this sense requires different methods because, for example, it is not possible to consider the auxiliary Steklov-type problem.

Open Problem 5.3
.

Prove the analogous of the isoperimetric inequality (
4
), contained in
[
47
]
, in the case when
\beta
is a positive or negative function satisfying suitable regularity assumptions, and prove the associated quantitative inequality.

Open Problem 5.4
.

It remains open the problem of proving the sharpness of the our quantitative results.

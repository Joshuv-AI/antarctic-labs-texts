Non-cutoff Boltzmann equation with soft potentials in the whole space
Kleber Carrapatoso, Pierre Gervais

Abstract
. We prove the existence, uniqueness and convergence of global solutions to the Boltzmann equation with non-cutoff soft potentials in the whole space when the initial data is a small perturbation of a Maxwellian with polynomial decay in velocity. Our method is based in the decomposition of the desired solution into two parts: one with polynomial decay in velocity satisfying the Boltzmann equation with only a dissipative part of the linearized operator; the other with Gaussian decay in velocity verifying the Boltzmann equation with a coupling term.



1.
Introduction

Consider the Boltzmann equation for the unknown
F=F(t,x,v)
, with
t\geqslant 0
,
x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
, and
v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F=Q(F,F)

complemented with an initial data
F_{0}=F_{0}(x,v)
.
The collision operator
Q
is bilinear and acts only on the velocity variable
v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
, which represents the fact that collisions are supposed to be localized in space, and it reads

(1.2)

Q(f,g)(v)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbf{S}^{2}}B(v-v_{*},\sigma)\left[f(v^{\prime}_{*})g(v^{\prime})-f(v_{*})g(v)\right]\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}.

The pre- and post-collision velocities
(v^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{*})
and
(v,v_{*})
are given by

(1.3)

v^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma\quad\text{and}\quad v^{\prime}_{*}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma

which is one possible parametrization of the conservation of momentum and energy in an elastic collision

v^{\prime}+v^{\prime}_{*}=v+v_{*}\quad\text{and}\quad|v^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}_{*}|^{2}=|v|^{2}+|v_{*}|^{2}.

The collision kernel
B(v-v_{*},\sigma)
encodes the physics of the interaction between particles. It is assumed to be nonnegative and to depend only on the relative velocity
|v-v_{*}|
and the angle
\cos\theta=\sigma\cdot\frac{(v-v_{*})}{|v-v_{*}|}
as

B(v-v_{*},\sigma)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}b(\cos\theta),

where
-3<\gamma\leqslant 1
and the angular part
b
is a smooth function (except maybe at
\theta=0
). As it is standard now, we may suppose, without loss of generality, that
\theta\in[0,\pi/2]
by replacing
B
by its symmetrized version if necessary.

In this paper we shall consider the case of
non-cutoff soft potentials
, more precisely we assume that
b
is an implicit function that is locally smooth and has a non-integrable singularity at
\theta=0
as

(1.4)

\sin\theta\,b(\cos\theta)\,\underset{\theta\sim 0}{\approx}\,C_{b}\,\theta^{-1-2s}\quad\text{with}\quad s\in(0,1),

for some constant
C_{b}>0
, and

(1.5)

-1<\gamma+2s<0\quad\text{and}\quad-3/2-s<\gamma<0.

Let
\mu(v)=(2\pi)^{-3/2}e^{-|v|^{2}/2}
be the standard Maxwellian and define the perturbation

F=\mu+f

which satisfies

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\mathscr{L}f+Q(f,f)\\
&f_{|t=0}=f_{0}=F_{0}-\mu\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mathscr{L}
is the linearized collision operator given by

\mathscr{L}f=Q(\mu,f)+Q(f,\mu).

We also denote by
\Lambda
the full linearized operator

\Lambda:=\mathscr{L}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}.

It is well known (see for instance
[
40
]
) that
\mathscr{L}
is a nonnegative self-adjoint operator on the space
L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})=\left\{g:\mathbf{R}^{3}\to\mathbf{R}\mid\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|g|^{2}\mu^{-1}\,\,\mathrm{d}v<\infty\right\}
with kernel given by

\mathrm{ker}(\mathscr{L})=\Span\{\mu,v_{1}\mu,v_{2}\mu,v_{3}\mu,|v|^{2}\mu\}.

We define
\pi
to be the orthogonal projection onto
\mathrm{ker}(\mathscr{L})
so that we can decompose

f=\pi f+f^{\perp},\quad f^{\perp}:=f-\pi f

with

(1.7)

\pi f=\left\{\rho[f]+u[f]\cdot v+\theta[f]\frac{(|v|^{2}-3)}{2}\right\}\mu

where

\rho[f]=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}f\,\mathrm{d}v,\quad u[f]=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}vf\,\mathrm{d}v,\quad\theta[f]=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{(|v|^{2}-3)}{3}f\,\mathrm{d}v.

1.1.
Main result

Before stating our main result we shall introduce the functional spaces we work with. If
X
is a function space and
w
a non-negative function, we define the weighted space
X(w)
as the space associated to the norm

\|f\|_{X(w)}:=\|wf\|_{X}.

In particular, for a weight function
m=m(v)
, we consider the weighted Lebesgue space
L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(m)
as the space associated to the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(m)}:=\langle mf,mg\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

and the corresponding norm

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(m)}:=\|mf\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}
denote the usual inner product and norm of
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbf{R}^{3}_{v})
.

We consider polynomial weight functions
m(v)=\langle v\rangle^{k}:=(1+|v|^{2})^{k/2}
with
k>0
, and we introduce the anisotropic dissipation space in velocity
H^{s,*}_{v}(m)
, inspired from the one presented in
[
28
]
, as the space associated to the norm

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}^{2}+\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}\times\mathbf{R}^{3}\times\mathbf{S}^{2}}b\left(\cos\theta\right)\mu(v_{*})\langle v_{*}\rangle^{\gamma}\left(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v,

where we use the shorthand
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(v):=m(v)\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f(v)
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}(v^{\prime})
recalling that
v^{\prime}
is defined in (
1.3
), and which satisfies the following bound (see Lemma
2.7
):

\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{H^{s}_{v}(m)}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}f\|_{H^{s}_{v}(m)}.

For functions
f=f(x,v)
depending on the position
x
and velocity
v
variables, we also define the polynomially weighted spaces
\mathbf{X}(m)
,
\mathbf{X^{*}}(m)
and
\mathbf{Y}(m)
as the spaces associated to the norms

(1.10)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathbf{X}(m)}

\displaystyle:=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}(m)}+\|\langle v\rangle^{-6s}\nabla_{x}^{3}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}(m)}^{2},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m)}

\displaystyle:=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}+\|\langle v\rangle^{-6s}\nabla_{x}^{3}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2},

and

(1.12)

\|f\|^{2}_{\mathbf{Y}(m)}:=\|f^{\perp}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m)}+\|\nabla_{x}\pi f\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}.

respectively.

Finally, we denote the Fourier transform
f\mapsto\widehat{f}
with respect to the space variable
x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
defined as

\widehat{f}(\xi,v):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}f(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{3}.

We can now state our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) hold. Consider
k>13/2+7|\gamma|/2+8s
and define the weight function
m=\langle v\rangle^{k}
. There exists
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough such that any initial data
f_{0}\in\mathbf{X}(m)
satisfying
\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0}
gives rise to a unique global weak solution
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+};\mathbf{X}(m))\cap L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+};\mathbf{Y}(m))
to (
1.6
), which satisfies the energy estimate

(1.13)

\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\|f(t)\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f(t)\|_{\mathbf{Y}(m)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}.

If moreover the initial data
\widehat{f}_{0}\in L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{-8s}m)
satisfies
\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{-8s}m)}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0}
with
p\in(2,\infty]
, then for any
0<\vartheta<\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)
and
k_{0}<k-|\gamma|
we have the decay estimate

(1.14)

\|f(t)\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\vartheta}\left(\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{-8s}m)}\right),

for all
t\geqslant 0
, where
m_{0}=\langle v\rangle^{k_{0}}
.

We now briefly review the known results for the Boltzmann equation near Maxwellian in the torus
\mathbf{T}^{3}
and the whole space
\mathbf{R}^{3}
.

We start by considering the case of cutoff potentials, which corresponds to angular kernels
b
for which the singularity in (
1.4
) is removed by assuming
b
integrable. By working near equilibrium, Grad
[
22
]
constructed in 1965 the first spatially inhomogeneous solutions for short times. Ukai
[
37
,
38
]
gave in the 1970’s a new impulse to the Cauchy problem and established, in the case of hard potentials
\gamma\in[0,1]
, the existence of global solutions in
L^{\infty}_{v}H^{s}_{x}\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}\mu^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}v\right)
, first in the periodic box
\mathbf{T}^{3}
in 1974
[
37
]
, then in the whole space
\mathbf{R}^{3}
in 1976
[
38
]
, by relying on spectral studies of the linearized equation
[
37
,
20
,
40
]
(let us mention also
[
32
]
). The case of soft potentials was then treated in 1980 by Caflisch
[
11
]
, then in 1982 by Asano and Ukai
[
40
]
only for
\gamma\in(-1,0)
, but this approach was recently extended to the full range
\gamma\in(-3,0)
by Sun and Wu
[
42
]
in 2021 and then Deng
[
16
]
in 2022. These results were then proven using energy methods in spaces of the form
L^{2}_{v}H^{s}_{x}\left(\mu^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x\right)
by Kawashima
[
29
]
, Liu, Yang and Yu
[
30
]
, Guo
[
26
]
and Guo and Strain
[
35
,
36
]
, as well as Duan
[
17
]
.

Concerning the non-cutoff case, the first existence result near equilibrium attributed to Ukai
[
39
]
; he constructed local solutions for analytic initial data in
(x,v)
having Gaussian decay using the Cauchy-Kowalewski theorem. Between 2011 and 2012, Gressman and Strain
[
24
,
23
]
(in the torus), and Alexandre, Morimoto, Ukai, Xu, Yang
[
6
,
4
,
7
]
(in the whole space) constructed the first global solutions in spaces of the form
H^{s}_{x,v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}\mu^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v\right)
by working with anisotropic norms. In the whole space framework, Strain
[
34
]
obtained the optimal time-decay for solutions in the whole space. Later Sohinger and Strain
[
33
]
extended these results to some Besov spaces in 2014, and Fang and Wang
[
41
]
relaxed some technical regularity and integrability assumptions in 2022. Recently, in the case of the torus, Duan, Liu, Sakamoto and Strain
[
18
]
obtained the existence of small-amplitude solutions, that is, in the space
L^{1}_{\xi}L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)
where
\xi
denotes the Fourier variable in space.
Let us also mention two very recent works in the case of the whole space: Deng
[
15
]
, in the case of hard potentials, who constructec global solutions by working with an anisotropic norm defined from the pseudo-differential study of Alexandre, Hérau and Li
[
2
]
; and also Duan, Sakamoto and Ueda
[
19
]
who constructed low regularity solutions (as in
[
18
]
) in the case of hard and moderately soft potentials, obtaining also obtained the decay in time of solutions.

All the above results concern solutions with Gaussian decay in velocity, that is, they hold in functional spaces with a weight in velocity of the form
\mu^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}v
.
In 2017, Gualdani, Mischler and Mouhot
[
25
]
, in the line of
[
31
]
, constructed solutions with polynomial decay in velocity. More precisely they relaxed the integrability conditions of previous results and constructed solutions in
W^{\ell,p}_{v}W^{s,q}_{x}\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x\right)
, in the case of hard spheres in the torus
\mathbf{T}^{3}
. In the same framework, the case of non-cutoff hard potentials was treated in
[
28
,
9
]
, and that of non-cutoff soft potentials in
[
13
]
.
Very recently, still in the torus and also in spaces with polynomial weights, the case of cutoff soft potentials was studied by Cao
[
12
]
.

Our result in Theorem
1.1
gives then, up to our knowledge, the first result of existence of global solutions with polynomial decay in velocity to the non-cutoff Boltzmann equation in the whole space. Moreover we also establish the convergece of solution to the Maxwellian. Inspired by the strategy of
[
10
]
, we shall construct a solution
f
to (
1.6
) by considering a decomposition of the form
f=h+g
, where
h(t)\in\mathbf{X}(m)
has polynomial decay in velocity and satisfies a “nice” semilinear equation in which only a dissipative part of the linearized operator
\Lambda
is present, and
g(t)
has Gaussian decay in velocity and evolves according to the Boltzmann equation plus some coupling term coming from
h
, with convenient decay properties in time and velocity. This system will then be solved using an iterative scheme and an energy method, where it its crucial to have better decay properties for
h
in order to treat the coupling term in the equation for
g
. We will then combine the energy estimate from the well-posedness theory with dispersive-type estimates for the equation associated to
g
, similarly as in
[
19
]
, in order to obtain the decay estimate.
In Section
2
we prove the necessary nonlinear homogeneous estimates on
Q
, and in Section
3
we prove the necessary coercive-type estimate on the linear part of the equation for
h
and recall those related to the equation for
g
. In Section
4
we prove nonlinear inhomogeneous estimates associated to
Q
. We then proceed to prove our main result in Section
5
.

2.
Estimates on the collision operator

This section is devoted to spatially homogeneous estimates on the collision operator
Q
. We present some auxiliary results in Section
2.1
which we will use to prove estimates on
Q
in polynomially weighted spaces in Section
2.2
.

2.1.
Auxiliary results

We state a few results that will be useful in the sequel.
This first lemma will be used to estimates integrals against the kinetic part
|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}
of the collision kernel
B(v-v_{*},\sigma)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,3)
and
s\in(0,1]
. For any smooth enough function
f=f(v)
one has:

(1)

If
0<\alpha<\frac{3}{2}
then for any
\ell>\frac{3}{2}
there holds, for any
v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
,

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|v-v_{*}|^{-\alpha}|f(v_{*})|\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\lesssim\langle v\rangle^{-\alpha}\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

(2)

If
0<\alpha<\frac{3}{2}+s
then for any
\ell>\frac{3}{2}+s
there holds, for any
v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
,

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|v-v_{*}|^{-\alpha}|f(v_{*})|\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\lesssim\langle v\rangle^{-\alpha}\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f\|_{H^{s}_{v}}.

Proof.

From
[
14
, Lemma 3.3]
for instance one has, for any
0<\beta<3
and
\vartheta>3
,

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|v-v_{*}|^{-\beta}\langle v_{*}\rangle^{-\vartheta}\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\lesssim\langle v\rangle^{-\beta},\quad\forall v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}.

We now write for
p\in[1,\infty]
, thanks to Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|v-v_{*}|^{-\alpha}|f(v_{*})|\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|v-v_{*}|^{-\alpha\frac{p}{p-1}}\langle v_{*}\rangle^{-\ell\frac{p}{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f\|_{L^{p}_{v}}.

We then conclude by using (
2.3
) with:
p=2
if
0<\alpha<\frac{3}{2}
; and
p=\frac{6}{3-2s}
if
0<\alpha<\frac{3}{2}+s
by using the Sobolev embedding
H^{s}(\mathbf{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{6}{3-2s}}(\mathbf{R}^{3})
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\varphi=\varphi(v)
be a Schwartz function and
\alpha\in(0,3)
. For any
\eta\in(0,1)
and
\ell>0
there is
C>0
such that there holds, for any
v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
,

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|v-v_{*}|^{-\alpha}\varphi(v_{*})\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\leqslant\eta\langle v\rangle^{-\alpha}+C\langle v\rangle^{-\ell}.

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
be fixed. We split the integral for some
M\geqslant 1
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|v-v_{*}|^{-\alpha}|\varphi(v_{*})|\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\int_{|v-v_{*}|\geqslant M}|v-v_{*}|^{-\alpha}|\varphi(v_{*})|\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}+\int_{|v-v_{*}|\leqslant M}|v-v_{*}|^{-\alpha}|\varphi(v_{*})|\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}

\displaystyle\qquad=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{2}
we write
\langle v_{*}\rangle^{-\ell}\leqslant\langle v-v_{*}\rangle^{\ell}\langle v\rangle^{-\ell}
for some arbitrary
\ell\geqslant 0
, thus

I_{2}\lesssim\langle v\rangle^{-\ell}\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\int_{|v-v_{*}|\leqslant M}|v-v_{*}|^{-\alpha}\langle v-v_{*}\rangle^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\lesssim\langle v\rangle^{-\ell}.

For
I_{1}
we take
p>3/(3-\alpha)
, so that
p/(p-1)<3/\alpha
, and apply Hölder’s inequality to get

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|v-v_{*}|^{-\alpha\frac{p}{p-1}}\langle v_{*}\rangle^{-q\frac{p}{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\mathbf{1}_{|v-v*|\geqslant M}\langle v_{*}\rangle^{qp}|\varphi(v_{*})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle v\rangle^{-\alpha}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\mathbf{1}_{|v-v*|\geqslant M}\,\langle v_{*}\rangle^{qp}|\varphi(v_{*})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
q>0
is such that
q\frac{p}{p-1}>3
. We now observe that for
M\geqslant 2|v|
, if
|v-v_{*}|\geqslant M
then
|v_{*}|\geqslant|v-v_{*}|-|v|\geqslant M/2
, and thus we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\mathbf{1}_{|v-v_{*}|\geqslant M}\,\langle v_{*}\rangle^{qp}|\varphi(v_{*})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\mathbf{1}_{|v_{*}|\geqslant M/2}\,\langle v_{*}\rangle^{qp}|\varphi(v_{*})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-p}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\langle v_{*}\rangle^{(q+1)p}|\varphi(v_{*})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-p}.

We then conclude taking
M\geqslant\max(1,2|v|)
large enough.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

The following change of variables formulas hold:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int b(\cos\theta)|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}

\displaystyle f(v,v^{\prime},\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}

\displaystyle\approx\int

\displaystyle b\left(\cos(\pi- 2 \theta)\right)|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right)^{-2-\gamma}f\left(v,v_{*},\pi-2\theta\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int b(\cos\theta)|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}f(v^{\prime},v_{*},\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v\approx\int b(\cos 2\theta)|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}f\left(v,v_{*},2\theta\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int B(v-v_{*},\sigma)f(v,v_{*},v^{\prime},v_{*}^{\prime},\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v=\int B(v-v_{*},\sigma)f(v^{\prime},v_{*}^{\prime},v,v_{*},\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v.

Proof.

The pre-post collisional change of variables (
2.6
) is known to be involutive with Jacobian
1
and it is easy to check that
|v-v_{*}|=|v^{\prime}-v_{*}^{\prime}|
. We only deal with the first two change of variables. Recall the definition of
v^{\prime}
:

v^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\sigma\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}.

Denote
\displaystyle k:=\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}
and recall that
\theta
is the angle
(k,\sigma)
. The differentials of
v^{\prime}
with respect to
v
and
v_{*}
writes in the basis
(k,\sigma,w)
where
w\perp k,\sigma
(at least when
k
and
\sigma
are not colinear)

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}}{\,\mathrm{d}v}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\id+\langle\,\cdot\,,k\rangle\sigma\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0\\
1&1+\cos\theta&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}}{\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\id-\langle\,\cdot\,,k\rangle\sigma\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0\\
1&1-\cos\theta&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right).

Thus, the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}}{\,\mathrm{d}v}\right|=\frac{1}{8}(1+\cos\theta)=\frac{1}{4}\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right),

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}}{\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}}\right|=\frac{1}{8}(1-\cos\theta)=\frac{1}{4}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right).

Furthermore, the definition of
v^{\prime}
also implies

\displaystyle|v^{\prime}-v_{*}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2}|v-v_{*}|^{2},

\displaystyle|v^{\prime}-v|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2}|v-v_{*}|^{2}.

The angle
\varphi
formed by
v^{\prime}-v_{*}
and
\sigma
and the angle
\psi
formed by
v^{\prime}-v
and
\sigma
are related to
\theta
by

\displaystyle\varphi=\frac{\theta}{2},\quad\psi=\frac{\pi-\theta}{2},

thus the integrals are estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\int b(\cos\theta)|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}

Proof.

We start by splitting the Dirichlet form using commutators:

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{\delta}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}

\displaystyle=\langle Q_{\delta}(\mu,f),f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}+\langle Q_{\delta}(f,\mu),f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}

\displaystyle=\langle Q_{\delta}(\mu,F),F\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}+R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3},

where we denoted

\displaystyle R_{1}:=\langle Q_{\delta}(f,m\mu),F\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}},

\displaystyle R_{2}:=\langle Q_{\delta}(\mu,f),f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}-\langle Q_{\delta}(\mu,F),F\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}},

\displaystyle R_{3}:=\langle Q_{\delta}(f,\mu),f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}-\langle Q_{\delta}(f,m\mu),F\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}.

The first term is estimated using Lemma
2.9
(where we choose
w_{1}=\gamma/2+2s
,
w_{2}=\gamma/2
for the weights,
a=2s
and
b=0
for the derivatives):

R_{1}\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}^{2},

and the two other ones using Lemma
2.10
and Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle R_{2}+R_{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}^{2}+\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}\|f\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\varepsilon}\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}^{2}+{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2}.

We then focus on the first term which provides the anisotropic dissipation
\dot{H}^{s,*}_{v}(m)
:

\displaystyle\langle Q_{\delta}(\mu,F),F\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}=

\displaystyle\int B_{\delta}(v-v_{*},\sigma)(\mu^{\prime}_{*}F^{\prime}-\mu_{*}F)F\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int B_{\delta}(v-v_{*},\sigma)(2\mu^{\prime}_{*}F^{\prime}F-\mu_{*}F^{2}-\mu_{*}^{\prime}F^{2})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int B_{\delta}(v-v_{*},\sigma)(\mu^{\prime}_{*}-\mu_{*})F^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v.

We use (
2.6
) to change the term
\mu^{\prime}_{*}F^{2}
of the first integral into
\mu_{*}(F^{\prime})^{2}
, and the cancellation lemma (Proposition
2.4
) in the second integral:

\displaystyle\langle Q_{\delta}(\mu,F),F\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}=-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int B_{\delta}(v-v_{*},\sigma)\mu^{\prime}_{*}(F^{\prime}-F)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+C_{\delta}\int|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\mu_{*}F^{2}\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v,

where
C_{\delta}\lesssim 1
. We thus have in virtue of (
2.1
)

\langle Q_{\delta}(\mu,F),F\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}+\frac{1}{2}\int B_{\delta}(v-v_{*},\sigma)\mu^{\prime}_{*}(F^{\prime}-F)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}.

Next, using (
2.6
), and then
|v-v_{*}|\leqslant\langle v\rangle\langle v_{*}\rangle
combined with the fact that
\gamma\leqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle-\int B_{\delta}(v-v_{*},\sigma)\mu^{\prime}_{*}

\displaystyle(F^{\prime}-F)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int b_{\delta}(\cos\theta)|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\mu_{*}(F^{\prime}-F)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-\int b_{\delta}(\cos\theta)\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}\left(\mu\langle v\rangle^{-\gamma}\right)_{*}(F^{\prime}-F)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v.

One shows as in the proof of
(1)\approx(2)
from Lemma
2.7
that

\displaystyle-\int B_{\delta}(v-v_{*},\sigma)\mu^{\prime}_{*}(F^{\prime}-F)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v+\frac{1}{2}\int b_{\delta}(\cos\theta)

\displaystyle\left(\mu\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}\right)_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}-\mathcal{F})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}.

Together with the previous estimates, we conclude that

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{\delta}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}\leqslant

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int b_{\delta}(\cos\theta)\left(\mu\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}\right)_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}-\mathcal{F})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+{\varepsilon}\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}.

The second term being non-positive, we conclude that (
3.6
) holds.

Furthermore, this proof works when replacing
b_{\delta}
by
b
(which corresponds, in a way, to taking
\delta
large), thus for
\mathscr{L}
we get

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}\leqslant

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int b(\cos\theta)\left(\mu\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}\right)_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}-\mathcal{F})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+{\varepsilon}\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}.

Recalling the definition of the norm
\dot{H}^{s,*}_{v}(m)
in (
1.9
), we therefore deduce that (
3.7
) also holds by taking
{\varepsilon}
small enough.
∎

We are now able to complete the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We get from Lemmas
3.2
and (
3.6
) that for
\delta
small enough

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathscr{L}_{\delta}^{c}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}+\langle\mathscr{L}_{\delta}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}

\displaystyle\leqslant{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}-c\delta^{-2s}\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}^{2}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\,{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2}-\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}^{2}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}^{2}.

We interpolate this estimate with (

\displaystyle\leqslant\left[\theta{\varepsilon}-(1-\theta)c\right]\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left[-\theta+(1-\theta)C\right]\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}+\theta C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2}.

We deduce (

) by taking

3.2.
Estimates on
\mathscr{L}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}
in exponentially weighted spaces

We present here dissipativity estimates for the full linearized operator
\Lambda=\mathscr{L}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}
in the space
L^{2}_{v}\left(\mu^{-1/2}\right)
and its weighted counterpart
L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)
. These estimates were initially proved in the works
[
34
,
7
]
, but we formulate them in a manner compatible with our approach.

As in
[
34
]
let us introduce the following bilinear symmetric form using the notations introduced in (
1.7
):

\displaystyle\Psi[f,g](\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\eta_{1}\mathrm{i}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\xi\theta[\widehat{f}(\xi)]\cdot\Upsilon[\widehat{g}^{\perp}(\xi)]+\frac{\eta_{1}\mathrm{i}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\xi\theta[\widehat{g}(\xi)]\cdot\Upsilon[\widehat{f}^{\perp}(\xi)]

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{2}\mathrm{i}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}(\xi\otimes u[\widehat{f}(\xi)])^{\mathrm{sym}}:\left\{\Theta[\widehat{g}^{\perp}(\xi)]+\theta[\widehat{g}(\xi)]\id\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{2}\mathrm{i}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}(\xi\otimes u[\widehat{g}(\xi)])^{\mathrm{sym}}:\left\{\Theta[\widehat{f}^{\perp}(\xi)]+\theta[\widehat{f}(\xi)]\id\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{3}\mathrm{i}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\xi\rho[\widehat{f}(\xi)]\cdot u[\widehat{g}(\xi)]+\frac{\eta_{3}\mathrm{i}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\xi\rho[\widehat{g}(\xi)]\cdot u[\widehat{f}(\xi)]

with
0<\eta_{3}\ll\eta_{2}\ll\eta_{1}\ll 1
, for any
\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
, where
\id
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix and

\Upsilon[f]=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}v(|v|^{2}-5)f\,\mathrm{d}v,\qquad\Theta[f]=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\left(v\otimes v-\id\right)f\,\mathrm{d}v,

and where for vectors
a,b\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
and matrices
A,B\in\mathbf{R}^{3\times 3}
, we denoted

(a\otimes b)^{\mathrm{sym}}=\frac{1}{2}(a_{j}b_{k}+a_{k}b_{j})_{1\leqslant j,k\leqslant 3},\qquad A:B=\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}A_{jk}B_{jk}.

We use
\Psi
to define the following inner product
\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}\cdot,\cdot\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}_{L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2})}
(with respect to the variable
v
) for some small enough
\kappa>0
for any functions
f=f(x,v)
and
g=g(x,v)
, and any
\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
as

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}\widehat{f}(\xi),\widehat{g}(\xi)\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}:=

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{f}(\xi),\widehat{g}(\xi)\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\mu^{-1/2}\right)}+\real\Psi[f,g](\xi)

\displaystyle+\kappa\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\leqslant 1}\langle\widehat{f}^{\perp}(\xi),\widehat{g}^{\perp}(\xi)\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}

\displaystyle+\kappa\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\geqslant 1}\langle\widehat{f}(\xi),\widehat{g}(\xi)\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}.

The next lemma states dissipativity estimates for this inner product.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
q\geqslant 0
be fixed. Denote by
|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|\cdot|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}
the norm associated with the inner product
\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}\cdot,\cdot\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}
. The following holds for some constants
C_{q}>0
and
\lambda_{q}>0
.

(1)

The norm
|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|\cdot|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}
is equivalent to the natural one, and more precisely in Fourier variables, there holds uniformly in
\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{3}

\frac{1}{C_{q}}\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}\leqslant|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}\leqslant C_{q}\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}.

(2)

The full linearized operator in Fourier variables satisfies the dissipativity estimate

\real\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}(\mathscr{L}-iv\cdot\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}\leqslant-\lambda_{q}\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}^{2}

where we defined the (
\xi
-dependent) norm
H^{s,**}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)
as

(3.9)

\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}^{2}=\|\widehat{f}^{\perp}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}+\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\|\pi\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}.

Proof.

The norm it induces is equivalent to the natural one of
L^{2}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)
since

\left|\Psi[f,f](\xi)\right|\lesssim\eta_{1}\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}^{2},

uniformly in
\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
, where we recall that
\eta_{1}
is assumed to be small, and also because

\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\mu^{-1/2}\right)}\geqslant\|\pi\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\mu^{-1/2}\right)}\approx\|\pi\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}.

The dissipativity comes from estimates established in
[
34
, Section 2.2]
, which we recall below in a crude but sufficient form; the hypocoercive estimate
[
34
, (2.20)]
for some
\alpha>0
:

\displaystyle\forall\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{3},\quad\real\langle\big(\mathscr{L}-iv\cdot\xi\big)\widehat{f}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi)\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\mu^{-1/2}\right)}+\real\Psi\left[\left(\mathscr{L}-iv\cdot\xi\right)\widehat{f}(\xi),\widehat{f}(\xi)\right](\xi)

\displaystyle\leqslant-\alpha\|\widehat{f}^{\perp}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}\left(\mu^{-1/2}\right)}^{2}-\alpha\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\|\pi\widehat{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\mu^{-1/2}\right)}.

as well as the weighted degenerate estimates
[
34
, (2.9)]
and
[
34
, (2.12)]
(with
g=0
)

\displaystyle\forall|\xi|\leqslant 1,\quad\real\langle\left\{\big(\mathscr{L}-iv\cdot\xi\big)\widehat{f}(\xi)\right\}^{\perp},\,

\displaystyle\widehat{f}^{\perp}(\xi)\Big\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}+\alpha\|\widehat{f}^{\perp}(\xi)\|^{2}_{H^{s,*}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\widehat{f}^{\perp}(\xi)\|^{2}_{H^{s,*}_{v}\left(\mu^{-1/2}\right)}+C|\xi|^{2}\|\pi\widehat{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\mu^{-1/2}\right)},

\displaystyle\forall\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{3},\quad\real\langle\big(\mathscr{L}-iv\cdot\xi\big)\widehat{f}(\xi),\widehat{f}(\xi)\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}

\displaystyle+\alpha\|\widehat{f}^{\perp}(\xi)\|^{2}_{H_{v}^{s,*}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{H^{s,*}_{v}\left(\mu^{-1/2}\right)}.

It is clear that for
\kappa
small enough, the dissipativity estimate for
\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}\cdot,\cdot\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}_{L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2})}
holds:

\real\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}(\mathscr{L}-iv\cdot\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}_{L^{2}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}\leqslant-\lambda_{q}\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}\left(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2}\right)}^{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

We now introduce the exponentially weighted Sobolev spaces

3.3.
Estimates on
\mathcal{B}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}
in polynomially weighted spaces

We already know from above subsection that in the gaussian spaces
\mathbf{E}_{q}
, the full linearized operator
\Lambda=\mathscr{L}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}
dissipates the
\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}
-norm. Concerning the polynomial space
\mathbf{X}(m)
, we will rely on the following splitting of the linearized collision operator
\mathscr{L}
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=M\chi_{R},\quad\mathcal{B}:=\mathscr{L}-\mathcal{A},

with constants
M,R>0
and
\chi_{R}(v)=\chi(v/R)
, where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbf{R}^{3})
is a smooth function satisfying
\mathbf{1}_{|v|\leqslant 1}\leqslant\chi\leqslant\mathbf{1}_{|v|\leqslant 2}
. The parameters
M,R>0
will be tuned later (to be chosen large enough) in order to make
\mathcal{B}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}
dissipative.

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume
k>13/2+2|\gamma|
and define
m=\langle v\rangle^{k}
. There are
M_{0},R_{0}>0
large enough such that for any
M\geqslant M_{0}
and
R\geqslant R_{0}
there holds, for any smooth enough function
f
,

\langle(\mathcal{B}-v\cdot\nabla_{x})f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}(m)}\lesssim-\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2}.

Proof.

We write

\langle(\mathcal{B}-v\cdot\nabla_{x})f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}(m)}=\langle\mathscr{L}-\mathcal{A}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}(m)}-\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}(m)}.

The second term vanishes in virtue of its gradient structure
(\nabla_{x}f)\,f=\nabla_{x}(|f|^{2})
, thus by Proposition
3.1
, we have for some constants
c,C>0

\displaystyle\langle(\mathcal{B}-v\cdot\nabla_{x})f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}(m)}

\displaystyle\leqslant-c\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}-M\|\chi_{R}(v)f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(m)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{c}{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}^{2}-\int\left(\frac{c}{2}\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}-Cm^{-2}+M\chi_{R}(v)\right)|f|^{2}m^{2}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x,

by using that
\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{s,*}_{v}(m)}\geqslant\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(m)}
.
For large values of
|v|
, we have that
\frac{c}{2}\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}-Cm^{-2}>0
by the assumption
k>|\gamma|/2
, thus there are
M_{0},R_{0}>0
large enough such that for all
M\geqslant M_{0}
and
R\geqslant R_{0}
we have

\frac{c}{2}\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}-Cm^{-2}+M\chi_{R}(v)>0,

from which we deduce the desired estimate.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.6
and the fact that
\mathcal{B}
commutes with
\nabla_{x}
, we obtain the following dissipative estimate for
\mathcal{B}
in spaces of the type
\mathbf{X}(m)
and
\mathbf{X}^{*}(m)
, recalling the definition in (
1.10
) and (
1.11
), respectively:

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume
k>13/2+2|\gamma|+6s
and define
m=\langle v\rangle^{k}
. There are
M_{0},R_{0}>0
large enough such that for any
M\geqslant M_{0}
and
R\geqslant R_{0}
there holds, for any smooth enough function
f
,

\langle(\mathcal{B}-v\cdot\nabla_{x})f,f\rangle_{\mathbf{X}(m)}\lesssim-\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}^{*}(m)}^{2}.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|h_{N+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}+\lambda\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}\left(\|h_{N}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}+\|g_{N}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}\right),

which, using the bounds (
5.9
) and assuming
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough in order to absorb the first term in the right-hand side by the left-hand side, simplifies as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|h_{N+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}\left(\|h_{N}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}+\|g_{N}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}\right),

Moreover, since we have
\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}h\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}\leqslant\|h\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}
, the following interpolation inequality holds for any
R>0
:

(5.11)

\langle R\rangle^{-|\gamma|}\|h\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}^{2}\leqslant\|h\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}^{2}+\langle R\rangle^{-|\gamma|-2(k-k_{0})}\|h\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2},

thus, taking
\langle R\rangle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{4\theta}\right)^{1/|\gamma|}(1+t)^{1/\gamma|}
, we have for all
t\geqslant 0

\frac{4\theta}{\lambda}(1+t)^{-1}\|h\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}^{2}\leqslant\|h\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}^{2}+\left(\frac{4\theta}{\lambda}\right)^{1+\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}}(1+t)^{-1-\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}}\|h\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}.

We now plug this control in the energy estimate:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|h_{N+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}+\frac{\lambda}{4}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}^{2}+\theta(1+t)^{-1}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(1+t)^{-1-\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}\left(\|h_{N}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}+\|g_{N}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}\right),

and then multiply both sides by
(1+t)^{2\theta}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big\{(1+t)^{2\theta}\|h_{N+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}\Big\}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(1+t)^{2\theta}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(1+t)^{2\theta-1-\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(1+t)^{2\theta}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}\left(\|h_{N}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}+\|g_{N}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}\right).

Integrating in time and using (
5.9
), we get

\displaystyle\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(m_{0})}^{2}\lesssim\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{X}(m)}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{0}\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(m_{0})}^{2}+\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}^{2},

where we used the fact that
2\theta-1-\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}<-1
so that
(1+t)^{2\theta-1-\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}}
is integrable. Assuming
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough and plugging in (
5.10
), we finally get

(5.12)

\|h_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(m_{0})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0}.

This concludes this step.

|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|\cdot|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g_{N+1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}^{2}+\lambda\|g_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g_{N}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}\|g_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}^{2}+\|g_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g_{N+1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}\|g_{N}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}

\displaystyle+\|h_{N}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g_{N+1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}},

which, using (
5.9

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g_{N+1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|g_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}^{2}\lesssim\|g_{N+1}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g_{N+1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}\|g_{N}\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}+\|h_{N}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m_{0})}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g_{N+1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}.

\displaystyle\|g_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}}^{2}

) for

5.2.

Consider the successive differences of

, that of
(g_{N})_{N=0}^{\infty}
by

(5.15)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}d_{N+1}=(\mathcal{B}-v\cdot\nabla_{x})d_{N+1}&+Q(h_{N},d_{N+1})+Q(d_{N},h_{N})\\
&+Q(g_{N},d_{N+1})+Q(e_{N},h_{N})\\

in norm

5.2.4.

We first prove (
5.17
) by using previous estimates. It is clearly true for
N=0
, so we assume that (
5.17
) holds for all integers up to some
N\geqslant 0
, and we shall deduce it for
N+1
.
Thanks to estimates (
5.18
), (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) we have obtained, for all

,

\displaystyle\|d_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{0}\left(\|d_{N}\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m})}+\|e_{N}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}}\right)

\displaystyle\|d_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(\underline{m_{0}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{0}\left(\|d_{N}\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m})}+\|d_{N}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(\underline{m_{0}})}+\|e_{N}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}}\right)

\displaystyle\|e_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{0}\|e_{N}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}}+\|d_{N}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(\underline{m_{0}})}.

This implies that

\|d_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m})}+\|d_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(\underline{m_{0}})}+\|e_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{0}(\|d_{N-1}\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m})}+\|d_{N-1}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(\underline{m_{0}})}+\|e_{N-1}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}})

and thus, using (

) for

, we deduce

\|d_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m})}+\|d_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(\underline{m_{0}})}+\|e_{N+1}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{0}(C_{0}{\varepsilon}_{0})^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\lesssim(C_{0}{\varepsilon}_{0})^{\frac{N+1}{2}},

which proves (

Therefore, assuming
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough, the sequence
(h_{N},g_{N})_{N\geqslant 0}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathscr{X}(\underline{m})\times\mathscr{E}_{q}
and thus converges to some limit
(h,g)
in
\mathscr{X}(\underline{m})\times\mathscr{E}_{q}
. In virtue of the stability estimates, the limit thus satisfies the bounds

(5.21)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathscr{X}(m)}=\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\|h(t)\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|h(t)\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2},

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(m_{0})}=\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\,(1+t)^{2\theta}\|h(t)\|_{\mathbf{X}\left(m_{0}\right)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+t)^{2\theta}\|h(t)\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}\left(m_{0}\right)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2},

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}}=\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\|g(t)\|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|g(t)\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{q}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}.

The solution thus constructed to the original perturbation equation (
1.6
) is given by letting
f:=h+g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+};\mathbf{X}(m))\cap L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+};\mathbf{Y}(m))
, which thus satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\|f(t)\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\|f^{\perp}(t)\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}\pi f(t)\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}.

together with the energy estimate (

5.3.
Uniqueness of the solution

Consider two solutions
f,\widetilde{f}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+};\mathbf{X}(m))\cap L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+};\mathbf{Y}(m))
to (
1.6
) with the same initial condition

and verifying

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\|f(t)\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f(t)\|_{\mathbf{Y}(m)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0}^{2},

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\|\widetilde{f}(t)\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\widetilde{f}(t)\|_{\mathbf{Y}(m)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{2}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0}^{2},

with
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough. Denote

the difference of these solutions, which satisfies

\partial_{t}d=(\mathcal{B}-v\cdot\nabla_{x})d+\mathcal{A}d+Q(d,f)+Q(\widetilde{f},d).

Arguing as in the previous steps, one gets for some
\lambda>0
the control

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|d\|^{2}_{\mathbf{X}(\underline{m})}+\lambda\|d\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(\underline{m})}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|d\|^{2}_{\mathbf{X}(\underline{m})}+\|d\|^{2}_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(\underline{m})}\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}(\underline{m})}+\|d\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(\underline{m})}\|d\|_{\mathbf{X}(\underline{m})}\|f\|_{\mathbf{Y}(m)}

\displaystyle+\|d\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(\underline{m})}^{2}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathbf{X}(\underline{m})}+\|d\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(\underline{m})}\|d\|_{\mathbf{X}(\underline{m})}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(\underline{m})},

which, once integrated from
t=0
to
t=T<\infty
, gives (with obvious notation)

\displaystyle\|d\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m};T)}^{2}\lesssim\,\Big(T

\displaystyle+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathbf{X}(m)\right)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathbf{X^{*}}(m)\right)}

\displaystyle+\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathbf{X}(m)\right)}+\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathbf{X^{*}}(m)\right)}\Big)\|d\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m};T)}^{2}.

Observing that

\|\phi\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m)}\lesssim\|\pi\phi\|_{H^{3}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m)}\lesssim\|\pi\phi\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|\phi\|_{\mathbf{Y}(m)}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}+\|\phi\|_{\mathbf{Y}(m)}

we have

\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathbf{X^{*}}(m))}\lesssim\sqrt{T}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbf{X}(m))}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathbf{Y}(m))}\lesssim\sqrt{T}{\varepsilon}_{0}+{\varepsilon}_{0}

and similarly for
\widetilde{f}
. Using the uniform bounds on
f
and
\widetilde{f}
, this becomes

\displaystyle\|d\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m};T)}^{2}\lesssim\,\Big(T+{\varepsilon}_{0}+\sqrt{T}{\varepsilon}_{0}\Big)\|d\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m};T)}^{2}.

Assuming
T>0
small enough and
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough, we have (for instance)

\displaystyle\|d\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m};T)}^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\|d\|_{\mathscr{X}(\underline{m};T)}^{2},

which means that
d=0
, or equivalently
f=\widetilde{f}
, on interval
[0,T]
. By continuing this argument, we deduce that
f_{0}
gives rise to a unique (global) solution, namely,
f
. This concludes the proof of uniquenees in Theorem
1.1
.

5.4.
Decay estimate

This subsection is devoted to the proof of the decay estimate (
1.14
) in Theorem
1.1
.
We therefore consider the unique solution
f=h+g
of (
1.6
) constructed in Sections
5.1
,
5.2
and
5.3
above, where
(h,g)\in\mathscr{X}(m)\times\mathscr{E}_{q}
is a solution to the system (
5.4
) and satisfies the estimates (
5.21
), where we fix
q>\vartheta|\gamma|
and recall that
0<\vartheta<\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
with
p\in(2,\infty]
. We shall obtain in the sequel the a priori estimates that implies the decay estimate (
1.14
), recalling that we suppose

\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{-8s}m)}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0}

small enough.

We observe that we have already obtained a decay estimate for the polynomial part of the solution
h
in (
5.21
).
It therefore remains to obtain a decay estimate for
g
in the space
\mathscr{E}_{0}
, which will be done by obtaining estimates in the Sobolev-type space
\mathscr{E}_{0,\star}
defined by

(5.22)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathscr{E}_{0,\star}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t\geqslant 0}(1+t)^{2\vartheta}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g(t)|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}_{0}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+s)^{2\vartheta}\|g(t)\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}_{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

This will require us to obtain uniform estimates in time of
h
and
g
in some Fourier-based spaces (defined below in (
5.24
), (
5.25
) and (
5.26
)).

Taking the Fourier transform in space of the system (
5.4
) gives, for all
\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
,

(5.23)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\widehat{h}(\xi)=(\mathcal{B}-iv\cdot\xi)\widehat{h}(\xi)+\widehat{Q}(h,h)(\xi)+\widehat{Q}(g,h)(\xi)+\widehat{Q}(h,g)(\xi),\end{aligned}\\
\partial_{t}\widehat{g}(\xi)=(\mathscr{L}-iv\cdot\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi)+\widehat{Q}(g,g)(\xi)+\mathcal{A}\widehat{h}(\xi),\\
\widehat{h}(0,\xi,v)=\widehat{f}_{0}(\xi,v),\quad\widehat{g}(0,\xi,v)=0,\end{cases}

where we recall

\widehat{Q}(f,g)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}Q\left(\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta),\widehat{g}(\eta)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

Recalling that the weight functions
m=\langle v\rangle^{k}
and
m_{0}=\langle v\rangle^{k_{0}}
are defined in (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), respectively, we define the weights
\tilde{m}=\langle v\rangle^{-8s}m
and
\tilde{m}_{0}=\langle v\rangle^{-8s}m_{0}
. Observe that we can apply Propositions
3.6
and
4.3
with the weight functions
\tilde{m}_{0}
and
\tilde{m}
. We also remark that the
\langle v\rangle^{-8s}
appearing in the definition of
\tilde{m}
comes from our definition of the space
\mathbf{X}(m)
and the weight loss in the nonlinear estimate of Proposition
4.3
, which allows us to control some Fourier-based norm with the energy estimate (see (
5.27
)).

We then define the Fourier-based functional spaces with polynomial weights
\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})
and
\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})
as the spaces associated to the norms, respectively:

(5.24)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}

\displaystyle:=\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}+\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m})},

(5.25)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}

\displaystyle:=\|(1+t)^{\theta}\widehat{h}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+\|(1+t)^{\theta}\widehat{h}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})},

where the decay parameter
\theta>0
is defined in (
5.2
). Moreover we also define the exponentially weighted space
\mathscr{G}^{p}
as the space associated to the norm:

(5.26)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}

\displaystyle:=\|\widehat{g}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}+\|\widehat{g}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}.

For later use, we already observe that

(5.27)

\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{2s}\tilde{m})}\lesssim\|\langle\xi\rangle^{3}\widehat{h}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{2s}\tilde{m})}\approx\|h\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{3}_{x}H^{s,*}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{2s}\tilde{m})}\lesssim\|h\|_{\mathscr{X}(m)},

and similarly

(5.28)

\|\widehat{g}\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}\lesssim\|\langle\xi\rangle^{3}\widehat{g}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}\approx\|g\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{3}_{x}H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}},

with
\|h\|_{\mathscr{X}(m)}
and
\|g\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}}
being controlled thanks to (
5.21
).

We split the proof of (
1.14
) into four steps.

Step 1: Estimate of
h
in norm
\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})
.

We start with the first equation of (
5.5
). The weak coercivity estimate on
\mathcal{B}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}
from Proposition
3.6
gives for some
\lambda>0

\displaystyle\real\left\langle(\mathcal{B}-iv\cdot\xi)\widehat{h}(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}\leqslant-\lambda\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m})}^{2},

thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}+\lambda\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left\langle\widehat{Q}(h,h)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}+\left\langle\widehat{Q}(g,h)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}+\left\langle\widehat{Q}(h,g)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}.

Integrating in time then taking the supremum in time in both sides gives

\displaystyle\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}^{2}+\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[h,h,h](\xi)^{2}+\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[g,h,h](\xi)^{2}+\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[h,g,h](\xi)^{2}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}^{2}

and hence

(5.29)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}+\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[h,h,h](\xi)+\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[g,h,h](\xi)+\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[h,g,h](\xi)+\|\widehat{f}_{0}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}.

Observe that thanks to Proposition
4.3
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[h,h,h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{2s}\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m})}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})},

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[g,h,h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}+\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[h,g,h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widehat{g}\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}+\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{1/2}\|\langle v\rangle^{2s}\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m})}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}^{1/2}.

Thanks to (
5.27
)–(
5.28
) and the bounds on
h
and
g
from estimate (
5.21
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[h,h,h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}+\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[g,h,h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}+\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}}[h,g,h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}+\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}^{1/2},

therefore taking the
L^{p}_{\xi}
norm of (
5.29
) yields

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}+\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}^{1/2}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}.

Recalling that
\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{-8s}m)}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0}
and taking
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough we deduce

(5.30)

\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}.

Step 2: Estimate of
h
in norm
\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})
.
As for the estimates with the weight
\tilde{m}
in Step 1, thanks to Proposition
3.6
we have for some
\lambda>0

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+\lambda\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left\langle\widehat{Q}(h,h)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+\left\langle\widehat{Q}(g,h)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left\langle\widehat{Q}(h,g)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}.

Moreover, since we have
\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}h\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}\leqslant\|h\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}
, using a similar interpolation inequality as in (
5.11
) we have for all
t\geqslant 0

(5.31)

\frac{2\theta}{\lambda}(1+t)^{-1}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{2}\leqslant\|h\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{2}+\left(\frac{2\theta}{\lambda}\right)^{1+\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}}(1+t)^{-1-\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}^{2}.

We now plug this control in the previous energy estimate:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{2}+\theta(1+t)^{-1}\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left\langle\widehat{Q}(h,h)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+\left\langle\widehat{Q}(g,h)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(m_{0})}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left\langle\widehat{Q}(h,g)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+(1+t)^{-1-\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}^{2},

and then multiply both sides by
(1+t)^{2\theta}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\{(1+t)^{2\theta}\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}\right\}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(1+t)^{2\theta}\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(1+t)^{2\theta}\left\langle\widehat{Q}(h,h)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+(1+t)^{2\theta}\left\langle\widehat{Q}(g,h)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(1+t)^{2\theta}\left\langle\widehat{Q}(h,g)(\xi),\widehat{h}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+(1+t)^{2\theta-1-\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}}\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}.

Integrating in time then taking the supremum in time in both sides gives

\displaystyle\|(1+t)^{\theta}\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{2}+\|(1+t)^{\theta}\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[h,(1+t)^{\theta}h,(1+t)^{\theta}h](\xi)^{2}+\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[g,(1+t)^{\theta}h,(1+t)^{\theta}h](\xi)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[(1+t)^{\theta}h,g,(1+t)^{\theta}h](\xi)^{2}+\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(m)}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m_{0})}^{2},

where for the fourth term in the right-hand side we have used the fact that
\theta<(k-k_{0})/|\gamma|
so that
(1+t)^{2\theta-1-\frac{2(k-k_{0})}{|\gamma|}}
is integrable. Hence we deduce

(5.32)

\displaystyle\|(1+t)^{\theta}\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+\|(1+t)^{\theta}\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[h,(1+t)^{\theta}h,(1+t)^{\theta}h](\xi)+\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[g,(1+t)^{\theta}h,(1+t)^{\theta}h](\xi)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[(1+t)^{\theta}h,g,(1+t)^{\theta}h](\xi)+\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}.

Using Propositions
4.3
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[h,(1+t)^{\theta}h,(1+t)^{\theta}h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{2s}(1+t)^{\theta}\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{1/2},

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[g,(1+t)^{\theta}h,(1+t)^{\theta}h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}+\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[(1+t)^{\theta}h,g,(1+t)^{\theta}h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widehat{g}\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{1/2}\|\langle v\rangle^{2s}(1+t)^{\theta}\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,*}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{1/2}.

Arguing as in previous step, using the bounds on
h
and
g
from estimate (
5.21
) together with (
5.27
)–(
5.28
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[h,(1+t)^{\theta}h,(1+t)^{\theta}h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}+\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[g,(1+t)^{\theta}h,(1+t)^{\theta}h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}+\|\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{m}_{0}}[(1+t)^{\theta}h,g,(1+t)^{\theta}h]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{1/2}+\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{1/2},

therefore taking the
L^{p}_{\xi}
norm in the above estimate implies

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{1/2}+\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{1/2}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}(\xi)\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}.

Recalling that
\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{-8s}m)}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0}
and taking
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough yields

(5.33)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}+\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}

\displaystyle+\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}(\xi)\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}.

Step 3: Estimate of
g
in norm
\mathscr{G}^{p}
.

We now turn to the second equation of (
5.5
). The weak coercivity estimate from Lemma
3.4
gives us, for some
\lambda>0
,

\real\left\langle\!\left\langle(\mathscr{L}-iv\cdot\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi),\widehat{g}(\xi)\right\rangle\!\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}\leqslant-\lambda\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2},

thus

(5.34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|\widehat{g}(\xi)|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|^{2}_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}+\lambda\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle\!\!\left\langle\widehat{Q}(g,g)(\xi),\widehat{g}(\xi)\right\rangle\!\!\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}+\|\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})},

where we have used that
|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|\cdot|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}
are equivalent and that the operator
\mathcal{A}
has compact support in velocity.

Integrating in time then taking the supremum in time in both sides gives

\displaystyle\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}+\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{0}[g,g,g](\xi)^{2}+\|(1+t)^{\theta}\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2})},

where we have used the fact that
\theta>1
so that
(1+t)^{-\theta}
is integrable in the last term. Hence we deduce

(5.35)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}+\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{0}[g,g,g](\xi)+\|(1+t)^{\theta}\widehat{h}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m}_{0})}.

Observe that, since
p\in(2,\infty]
, we have by Proposition
4.5

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{q}[g,g,g]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{3/2}+\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}\|\hat{g}\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}L^{2}_{t}H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{1/2},

so that arguing as in previous steps, using the bounds on
g
from estimate (
5.21
) together with (
5.28
), we deduce

\|\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{0}[g,g,g]\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{3/2}+\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}.

Therefore taking the
L^{p}_{\xi}
norm of (
5.35
) implies

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{3/2}+\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}^{1/2}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}+\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})},

thus taking
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough, recalling that
\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{-8s}m)}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0}
, gives

(5.36)

\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{3/2}+\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}.

Therefore gathering (
5.30
)–(
5.33
)–(
5.36
) and taking
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough we obtain

(5.37)

\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}(\tilde{m})}+\|h\|_{\mathscr{F}^{p}_{\star}(\tilde{m}_{0})}+\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0}.

Step 4: Estimate of
g
in
\mathscr{E}_{0,\star}
.

We finally turn to the estimation of
g
in
\mathscr{E}_{0,\star}
, which is the most delicate one. Arguing similarly as for obtaining (
5.13
), we get for some
\lambda>0

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}^{2}+\lambda\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}}^{2}\lesssim|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}}^{2}+\|h\|_{\mathbf{X}(\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}m_{0})}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}.

where we observe that, in comparison with (
5.13
), we have a weight
\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}m_{0}
instead of
m_{0}
for
h
on the second term in the right-hand side, which is possible since
\mathcal{A}
has compact support in velocity. Thanks to estimate (
5.21
) and the fact that
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
is small enough, we deduce

(5.38)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}}^{2}\lesssim\|h\|_{\mathbf{X}(\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}m_{0})}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}.

We shall now bound by below the dissipation term
\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}}
, by observing that

\frac{1}{c_{0}}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}^{2}\leqslant\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}(1+|\xi|^{6})\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leqslant c_{0}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}^{2}

and

\frac{1}{c_{1}}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}}^{2}\leqslant\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}(1+|\xi|^{6})\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leqslant c_{1}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}}^{2}

for some constants
c_{0},c_{1}>0
. We therefore shall focus below on the quantity
\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}
, that we recall is given by

(5.39)

\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}=\|\widehat{g}^{\perp}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}+\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\|\pi\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}.

First of all, for the first term in the right-hand side of (
5.39
) we can argue similarly as in Step 2 above in order to obtain the interpolation inequality (
5.31
), therefore we obtain, using that
\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}\leqslant\|\cdot\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}
, that for all
\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{3}
and
t\geqslant 0
there holds

(5.40)

\displaystyle\frac{4\vartheta c_{0}c_{1}}{\lambda}(1+t)^{-1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|\widehat{g}^{\perp}(\xi)|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\widehat{g}^{\perp}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}+C(1+t)^{-1-\frac{2q}{|\gamma|}}\|\widehat{g}^{\perp}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2})}^{2},

for some constant
C>0
(depending only on
\lambda,\vartheta,q,\gamma
).

We now investigate the second term in the right-hand side of (
5.39
) by splitting the analysis into two parts : low frequencies
|\xi|\leqslant 1
and high frequencies
|\xi|\geqslant 1
.
For high frequencies
|\xi|\geqslant 1
we observe that
\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}
, which implies

\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\geqslant 1}\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}\leqslant\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\geqslant 1}\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}\leqslant\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\geqslant 1}\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,*}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}.

Therefore arguing as in (
5.40
) we obtain

(5.41)

\displaystyle\frac{4\vartheta c_{0}c_{1}}{\lambda}(1+t)^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\geqslant 1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|\widehat{g}(\xi)|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\geqslant 1}\|\widehat{g}_{N+1}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}+C(1+t)^{-1-\frac{2q}{|\gamma|}}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\geqslant 1}\|\widehat{g}_{N+1}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}.

We now turn to the the case of low frequencies
|\xi|\leqslant 1
.
Recalling that
2<p\leqslant\infty
, we fix a parameter
r>1
such that

(5.42)

1+\frac{2p}{3(p-2)}<r<1+\frac{1}{2\vartheta},

which is possible because
0<\vartheta<\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
. Remarking that, since
|\xi|\leqslant 1
, there holds
|\xi|^{2}\leqslant\frac{2|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}
, we obtain by Young’s inequality that for any
\eta>0
there is
C_{\eta}>0
such that

\forall|\xi|\leqslant 1,~\forall t\geqslant 0,\quad 1\leqslant\eta(1+t)\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}+C_{\eta}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{r-1}}|\xi|^{-\frac{2}{r-1}}.

We thus deduce

(5.43)

\displaystyle\eta^{-1}(1+t)^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\leqslant 1}\|\pi\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\leqslant 1}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\|\pi\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}+C^{\prime}_{\eta}(1+t)^{-1-\frac{1}{r-1}}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\leqslant 1}|\xi|^{-\frac{2}{r-1}}\|\pi\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}.

Gathering (
5.40
)–(
5.41
)–(
5.43
) and choosing
\eta>0
appropriately yields

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{4c_{1}}\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{H^{s,**}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{0}\vartheta(1+t)^{-1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|\widehat{g}(\xi)|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}-C(1+t)^{-1-\frac{2q}{|\gamma|}}\|\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\langle v\rangle^{q}\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-C(1+t)^{-1-\frac{1}{r-1}}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\leqslant 1}|\xi|^{-\frac{2}{r-1}}\|\pi\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{4}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\vartheta(1+t)^{-1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}^{2}-C(1+t)^{-1-\frac{2q}{|\gamma|}}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-C(1+t)^{-1-\frac{1}{r-1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\leqslant 1}|\xi|^{-\frac{2}{r-1}}\langle\xi\rangle^{6}\|\pi\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Applying Hölder’s inequality for the last term we get

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\leqslant 1}|\xi|^{-\frac{2}{r-1}}\langle\xi\rangle^{6}\|\pi\widehat{g}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi\lesssim\|\pi\widehat{g}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}

since
r>1+\frac{2p}{3(p-2)}
. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{4}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\vartheta(1+t)^{-1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}^{2}-C(1+t)^{-1-\frac{2q}{|\gamma|}}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}^{2}-C(1+t)^{-1-\frac{1}{r-1}}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{2},

which gives, coming back to (
5.38
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|^{2}_{\mathbf{E}}+\frac{\lambda}{4}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}}^{2}+\vartheta(1+t)^{-1}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|h\|_{\mathbf{X}(\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}m_{0})}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}+(1+t)^{-1-\frac{2q}{|\gamma|}}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}^{2}+(1+t)^{-1-\frac{1}{r-1}}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{2}.

Multiplying by
(1+t)^{2\vartheta}
we thus get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\{(1+t)^{2\vartheta}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|^{2}_{\mathbf{E}}\right\}+\frac{\lambda}{4}(1+t)^{2\vartheta}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}^{*}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{2\vartheta}\|h\|_{\mathbf{X}(\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}m_{0})}|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|g|\hskip-1.13791pt|\hskip-1.13791pt|_{\mathbf{E}}+(1+t)^{2\vartheta-1-\frac{2q}{|\gamma|}}\|g\|_{\mathbf{E}_{q}}^{2}+(1+t)^{2\vartheta-1-\frac{1}{r-1}}\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{2}.

Integrating in time last estimate implies (recall the definition of
\mathscr{E}_{0,\star}
from (
5.22
))

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathscr{E}_{0,\star}}^{2}\lesssim\|h\|_{\mathscr{X}_{\star}(m_{0})}\|g\|_{\mathscr{E}_{0,\star}}+\|g\|_{\mathscr{E}_{q}}^{2}+\|g\|_{\mathscr{G}^{p}}^{2},

where for the first term in the right-hand side we have used that
\theta>\vartheta+1/2
so that
(1+t)^{2(\vartheta-\theta)}
is integrable and that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{X}(\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}m_{0}))}\leqslant\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{X^{*}}(m_{0})}
; for the second one that
q>|\gamma|\vartheta
so that
(1+t)^{2\vartheta-1-\frac{2q}{|\gamma|}}
is integrable; and for the third one that
r<1+1/(2\vartheta)
so that
(1+t)^{2\vartheta-1-\frac{1}{r-1}}
is integrable.

Using the bounds (
5.21
) and (
5.37
) yields

(5.44)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathscr{E}_{0,\star}}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathbf{X}(m)}+\|\widehat{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}L^{2}_{v}(\tilde{m})}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{0},

which together with the second estimate of (
5.21
) concludes the proof of (
1.14
).

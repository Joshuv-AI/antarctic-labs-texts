Jack Littlewood-Richardson Coefficients and the Nazarov-Sklyanin Lax Operator
Ryan Mickler

Abstract
. We continue the work begun by Mickler-Moll [ 10 ] investigating the properties of the polynomial eigenfunctions of the Nazarov-Sklyanin quantum Lax operator. By considering products of these eigenfunctions, we produce a novel generalization of a formula of Kerov relating Jack Littlewood-Richardson coefficients and residues of certain rational functions. Precisely, we derive a system of constraints on Jack Littlewood-Richardson coefficients in terms of a simple multiplication operation on partitions.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Lambda
be the ring of symmetric functions and
{{\mathbb{C}}_{{\bm{{\varepsilon}}}}}={\mathbb{C}}({\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2})
. For
\lambda
a partition, we let
s\in\lambda
be a box of its corresponding Young diagram. For such a box, we write
s=(s_{1},s_{2})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
, labelling the grid position of its bottom corner, and we define the content map
[s]:=s_{1}{\varepsilon}_{1}+s_{2}{\varepsilon}_{2}
. Let
j_{\lambda}\in\Lambda\otimes{{\mathbb{C}}_{{\bm{{\varepsilon}}}}}
be the homogenous versions (c.f.
21
) of the integral Jack symmetric functions
J_{\lambda}
from
[
9
]
, which we review in section
2.1
. The Jack Littlewood-Richardson (LR) coefficients
c_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda}
are defined as the coefficients of a product of Jack functions expanded in the basis of Jacks:

(1)

j_{\mu}\cdot j_{\nu}=\sum_{\lambda}c_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda}\,j_{\lambda}.

In this paper, we prove the following ‘sum-product’ combinatorial identity that captures deep structure of these Jack Littlewood-Richardson coefficients:

Main Result

(Theorem
4.28
)
.

For any partitions
\mu,\nu
, the Jack Littlewood-Richardson coefficients
c_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma}
satisfy the following identity of rational functions in the variable
u
,

(2)

\sum_{\gamma\supset\mu\cup\nu}c_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma}\frac{\varpi_{\gamma}}{\varpi_{\mu}\varpi_{\nu}}\left(\sum_{s\in\gamma/(\mu\cup\nu)}\frac{1}{u-[s]}\right)=T_{\mu{\star}\nu}(u)-1,

where
\mu\cup\nu
is the union as sets of boxes,
\varpi_{\lambda}\coloneqq\prod_{s\in\lambda\setminus(0,0)}[s]
, and

(3)

T_{\mu{\star}\nu}(u):=\prod_{x\in\mu,y\in\nu}N(u-[x+y]),\qquad N(u):=\frac{(u-[0,0])(u-[1,1])}{(u-[1,0])(u-[0,1])}.

In the case
\mu=1
, this theorem recovers a well known result of Kerov
[
8
]
:

(4)

\sum_{\nu+s\supset\nu}c_{1\nu}^{\nu+s}\left(\frac{1}{u-[s]}\right)=u^{-1}T_{\nu}(u).

By expanding at various poles in
u
, the identity
2
gives a family of relations amongst the
c_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma}
. We provide a simple yet illustrative example in
4.29
. Although these equations are underdetermined, they do provide explicit closed form expressions for large families of Jack LR coefficients, which we investigate in a follow up article w/ P. Alexandersson
[
1
]
along with connections to various conjectures of Stanley on the structure of these coefficients
[
15
]
.

We repackage and interpret the above result in terms of a simple map:

Interpretation

(Theorem
5.6
)
.

Consider the following ‘basic’ evaluation map on symmetric functions
\Delta:\Lambda\to{{\mathbb{C}}_{{\bm{{\varepsilon}}}}}(u)
, defined on the basis of homogeneous Jack symmetric functions
j_{\lambda}
as

(5)

{\Delta}(j_{\lambda}):=\varpi_{\lambda}\,\sum_{s\in\lambda}\frac{1}{u-[s]}.

For two partitions
\mu,\nu
of arbitrary size, this evaluation map satisfies

(6)

{\Delta}(j_{\mu}\cdot j_{\nu})=\varpi_{\mu}\varpi_{\nu}\left(T_{\mu{\star}\nu}(u)-1\right).

Note that the map
\Delta
is
not
a ring homomorphism, and furthermore it degenerates in the Schur case (
{\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{2}=0
) as in this case it vanishes on all non-hook partitions.

This paper is the sequel to
[
10
]
, where a spectral theorem was proven for the quantum Lax operator
{\mathcal{L}}
introduced by Nazarov-Sklyanin
[
11
]
. The polynomial eigenfunctions
\psi_{\lambda}^{s}\in\Lambda[w]
of
{\mathcal{L}}
depend on a partition
\lambda
and a choice of location
s
where a box can be added to
\lambda
.

The central idea of this second paper is to consider product expansions of these Lax eigenfunctions

(7)

\psi_{\lambda}^{s}\cdot\psi_{\nu}^{t}=\sum_{\gamma,u}c_{\lambda,\nu;u}^{s,t;\gamma}\psi_{\gamma}^{u},

and analyse their structure.
Here, we introduce a new object, the
Jack-Lax Littlewood-Richardson coefficients
,
c_{\lambda,\nu;u}^{s,t;\gamma}
, the structure of which will be illuminated throughout this paper. Indeed, these Jack-Lax LR coefficients reproduce the Jack LR coefficients (
1
) under summation,

(8)

c_{\lambda,\nu}^{\gamma}=\sum_{u}c_{\lambda,\nu;u}^{s,t;\gamma}.

We will demonstrate that in many ways this refined algebra of eigenfunctions is
easier to understand
than the algebra of Jack functions due to the action of
{\mathcal{L}}
, and leads ultimately to a proof of the Main Result
4.28
.

1.1.
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
, we review some preliminary material, and recall the main results of the previous paper in this series
[
10
]
. We introduce the Nazarov-Skylanin Lax operator, and describe is spectrum.

In Section
3
, we begin the task of understand the structure of the basis of Lax eigenfunctions. Here, we lay out the central new focus of this work, which is the algebra of products of these Lax eigenfunctions. We introduce a family of linear maps, the Trace functionals, that help to explore the properties of the Lax eigenfunction products. These traces are associated with three different decompositions of the Hilbert space. We describe a cohomological approach to the understanding of the combined Trace map and compute its kernel and cokernel.

In Section
4
, we produce special elements of the algebra, the
\beta
and
\theta
elements, and show a key relation between their traces. We then use this relation to compute the traces of these elements, demonstrating a connection to the Jack Littlewood-Richardson coefficients. We conclude this section with the main theorem (
4.28
).

In Section
5
, we reinterpret the main results in terms of the language of double affine Hecke algebras, motivated by the results of Bourgine-Matsuo-Zhang
[
4
]
.

In Section
6
, we close out this work with some conjectures on the deeper structure of the algebra of Lax eigenfunctions. These conjectures would give a more direct explanation of the central results of this article.

2.1.3.
Jack Functions

Define the real
deformation parameter

\alpha=-{\varepsilon}_{2}/{\varepsilon}_{1}
. The (integral form) Jack symmetric functions
J_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}
, indexed by partitions
\lambda
, are a family of symmetric functions depending on the deformation parameter
\alpha
, introduced in
[
7
]
. When
\alpha=1
, these reduce to the standard Schur symmetric functions
s_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 2.1

(Jack Functions
[
9
]
Chap. VI, (4.5))
.

There exists a unique family of symmetric functions
J_{\lambda}\in\Lambda[\alpha]
, indexed by partitions
\lambda
, which satisfy the following three properties

•

Orthogonality:

(11)

\langle J_{\lambda},J_{\mu}\rangle_{\alpha}=0\text{ when }\lambda\neq\mu.

•

Triangularity:

(12)

J_{\lambda}=\sum_{\mu<_{d}\lambda}c_{\lambda\mu}m_{\mu},

where
<_{d}
indicates dominance order on partitions.

•

Normalization:

(13)

[m_{1^{n}}]J_{\lambda}=n!.

This normalization is known as the
integral
form of the Jack symmetric functions, as they have the property that
J_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\alpha,p_{1},p_{2},\ldots]
in the basis of the power-sum symmetric functions with the expansion

(14)

J_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}=1.p_{1}^{|\lambda|}+\ldots

For example, for partitions of size
n=3
, the Jack functions are

(15)

\displaystyle J_{\{1^{3}\}}^{(\alpha)}(p)=

\displaystyle p_{1}^{3}-3p_{2}p_{1}+2p_{3}

\displaystyle=6m_{1^{3}},

(16)

\displaystyle J_{\{1,2\}}^{(\alpha)}(p)=

\displaystyle p_{1}^{3}+(\alpha-1)p_{2}p_{1}-\alpha p_{3}

\displaystyle=6m_{1^{3}}+(\alpha+2)m_{1,2},

(17)

\displaystyle J_{\{3\}}^{(\alpha)}(p)=

\displaystyle p_{1}^{3}+3\alpha p_{2}p_{1}+2\alpha^{2}p_{3}

\displaystyle=6m_{1^{3}}+3(\alpha+1)m_{1,2}+(\alpha+1)(2\alpha+1)m_{3}.

The triangularity of the Jack functions in the monomial basis is evident.

2.1.4.
Fock Module

We use the ring of coefficients
{{\mathbb{C}}_{{\bm{{\varepsilon}}}}}={\mathbb{C}}({\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2})
. Out of the two deformation parameters, we build two secondary parameters: The
quantum
parameter
\hbar=-{\varepsilon}_{1}{\varepsilon}_{2}=-[(1,0)][(0,1)]
, and the
dispersion
parameter
{\overline{\varepsilon}}={\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{2}=[(1,1)]
. We consider a
\hat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{1}
Heisenberg current
V(z)=\sum_{k}V_{k}z^{k}
, with
V_{0}=0
and
[V_{n},V_{m}]=\hbar n\delta_{n+m,0}
. This current acts on the Fock module
{\mathcal{F}}={{\mathbb{C}}_{{\bm{{\varepsilon}}}}}[V_{1},V_{2},\cdots]
, via

(18)

V_{-k}=\hbar k\partial_{V_{k}},\quad k>0.

In this paper, we work with an alternate presentation of the ring of symmetric functions by embedding them into the Fock module via
p_{k}\to(-{\varepsilon}_{1})^{-1}V_{k}
.
In this basis, the Hall inner product becomes

(19)

||V_{1}^{d_{1}}V_{2}^{d_{2}}\cdots||_{\hbar}^{2}=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(\hbar k)^{d_{k}}d_{k}!.

With this, we have

(20)

V_{-k}=V_{k}^{\dagger}.

This ring has the natural grading operator
{\mathcal{N}}
, where
V_{k}
has degree
k
.

2.1.5.
Homogeneous Integral Normalization

We will use

(21)

j_{\lambda}(V|{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}):={(-{\varepsilon}_{1})^{|\lambda|}}\cdot J^{(\alpha=-{\varepsilon}_{2}/{\varepsilon}_{1})}_{\lambda}(p=(-{\varepsilon}_{1})^{-1}V)\in{\mathcal{F}}.

With this normalization, the three homogeneous Jack functions for

\displaystyle j_{\{1^{3}\}}=

(23)

\displaystyle j_{\{1,2\}}=

\displaystyle V_{1}^{3}+({\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{2})V_{1}V_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}{\varepsilon}_{2}V_{3},

(24)

Note that these are all homogenous integral polynomials in

For a partition

2.1.6.
Jack Littlewood-Richardson (LR) Coefficients

Much of the work in this paper will be concerning the
Jack Littlewood-Richardson coefficients
,
c_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda}
, defined as the expansion coefficients in the product of Jack functions,

(28)

j_{\mu}\cdot j_{\nu}=\sum_{\lambda}c_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda}\,j_{\lambda}.

In the literature, these are often denoted as
c_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda}(\alpha)
to indicate the dependence on the deformation parameter
\alpha
. In general, it is very difficult to find explicit closed-form expressions for these coefficients, instead most formulas involving them are recursive in nature. In this paper, we find new families of relations between these coefficients. In the sequel paper,
[
1
]
, we make progress towards finding explicit closed form expressions.

The most well known explicit formula for Jack LR coefficients is given by

Proposition 2.3

(Pieri Rule - Stanley ’89
[
15
]
Thm 6.1)

Let
\mu\subset\lambda
, and
\lambda/\mu
be a
horizontal
r
-strip
, i.e. no two boxes in the quotient shape
\lambda/\mu
are adjacent in a row. Then

(29)

c_{1^{r},\mu}^{\lambda}=\frac{\left(\prod_{s\in\{1^{r}\}}h^{L}_{1^{r}}(s)\right)\left(\prod_{t\in\mu}A_{\mu}(t)\right)}{\left(\prod_{v\in\lambda}A_{\lambda}(v)\right)},

where

(30)

A_{\sigma}(b)=h^{U}_{\sigma}(b)\text{ if $\lambda/\mu$ does not intersect the row of $b$, $h^{L}_{\sigma}(b)$ otherwise. }

2.2.1.
Preliminaries

We enlarge our Hilbert space and work in the extended Fock module
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[w]
, where
w
is of degree 1. The inner product is

(31)

\langle V_{\mu}w^{m},V_{\nu}w^{n}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}\langle V_{\mu},V_{\nu}\rangle_{\hbar}.

In this way, the inclusion
{\mathcal{F}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
is an isometry.
The total grading operator for
{\mathcal{H}}
is

(32)

{\mathcal{N}}^{*}:={\mathcal{N}}+w\partial_{w}=\hbar^{-1}\sum_{k>0}V_{k}V_{-k}+w\partial_{w}.

This gives the graded decomposition

(33)

{\mathcal{H}}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}{\mathcal{H}}_{n}.

On this space, we have several important projections on
{\mathcal{H}}
. Firstly,
\pi_{0}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
projects just onto the
w^{0}
component.
\pi_{+}
is its complement, projecting only onto positive powers of
w
.
\pi_{w}:F[w,w^{-1}]\to F[w]
is the map that projects onto non-negative powers of
w
.

2.2.2.
Lax Operator

We at last come to introducing the main actor in our story.

Definition 2.4
.

The
Nazarov-Sklyanin Lax Operator

[
11
]
is the linear operator on
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{F}}[w]
given by

(34)

{\mathcal{L}}=\pi_{w}\sum_{k>0}\left(w^{-k}V_{k}+w^{k}V_{-k}\right)+{\overline{\varepsilon}}w\partial_{w}

In the basis
{\mathcal{H}}=\oplus_{k}(w^{k}{\mathcal{F}})
, we can express
{\mathcal{L}}
as the semi-infinite matrix operator, with coefficients in
\mathop{\rm End}({\mathcal{F}})

(35)

{\mathcal{L}}:=\left(\begin{matrix}0&V_{1}&V_{2}&V_{3}&\cdots\\
V_{-1}&{\overline{\varepsilon}}&V_{1}&V_{2}&\cdots\\
V_{-2}&V_{-1}&2{\overline{\varepsilon}}&V_{2}&\cdots\\
V_{-3}&V_{-2}&V_{-1}&3{\overline{\varepsilon}}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{matrix}\right)

One can check that
{\mathcal{L}}
commutes with grading operator
32
, so let
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}={\mathcal{L}}|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{n}}
.
Furthermore, let
{\mathcal{L}}^{+}=\pi_{+}{\mathcal{L}}|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+}}
be the restrictions of
{\mathcal{L}}
to only the positive powers of
w
.

Corollary 2.5

(Shift property)

(36)

w^{-1}{\mathcal{L}}_{n+1}^{+}w={\mathcal{L}}_{n}+{\overline{\varepsilon}}

Note that from the definition

acts as derivation if either of the factors is in
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}:=\pi_{0}{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{F}}
, i.e.

(37)

2.2.5.
Transition Measures

The influential work of Kerov
[

introduces the following objects.

Definition 2.7

(
[
8
]

For

\tau_{\lambda}^{s}:=\mathop{\rm Res}_{u=[s]}u^{-1}T_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{\prod_{t\in\mathcal{R}^{+}_{\lambda}}[s-t]}{\prod_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda},s^{\prime}\neq s}[s-s^{\prime}]}.

Note that for

Lemma 2.8

(Kerov ’97
[
8
]

The simplest Jack LR coefficient coincides with the co-transition measure,

(43)

c_{1,\lambda}^{\lambda+s}=\tau_{\lambda}^{s}.

That is, Jack functions satisfy the following simple multiplication formula (‘Pieri’ rule)

(44)

j_{1}\cdot j_{\lambda}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}}\tau_{\lambda}^{s}\,j_{\lambda+s}.

From this, we can write

(45)

u^{-1}T_{\lambda}(u)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}}c_{1,\lambda}^{\lambda+s}\frac{1}{u-[s]}.

One of the main results (Theorem
4.28
) of this paper is a generalization this Kerov relation between Jack LR coefficients and residues of certain ‘spectral’ rational functions.

Kerov also introduces the
transition measures
, for

,

(46)

\tilde{\tau}^{t}_{\lambda}=\mathop{\rm Res}_{u=[t]}u\,T_{\lambda}(u)^{-1}.

Spectral Theorem

Here we recall the results of Mickler-Moll

We can now state the main result of the first paper in this series.

Theorem 2.11

(Spectral Decomposition
[

•

Under the action of the Nazarov-Skylanin Lax operator

We use this to compute its dimension. We do this by looking at the horizontal sub-complexes:

(179)

K^{\ell+\bullet}_{k}:=\cdots\rightarrow K^{\ell+2}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}K^{\ell+1}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}K^{\ell}_{k}.

In terms of these sub-complexes, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\dim(\ker{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n})x^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\chi(K^{3+i+\bullet}_{n-(i+1)})\right)x^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\chi(K^{3+i+\bullet}_{n})x^{n}\right)x^{i+1}.

Consider just the sub-complex contribution of a partition
\eta\,\vdash\,k
with
r
minima. At each stage of the complex we choose the appropriate number of those corners
S\subset\mathcal{A}(\eta)
to include in the symbol
\Psi_{\eta}^{S}
, and thus the contribution from
\eta
is

(180)

K^{\ell+\bullet}_{k,r}:=\cdots\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\binom{r}{\ell+2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathbb{C}}^{\binom{r}{\ell+1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathbb{C}}^{\binom{r}{\ell}}.

The dimension of this sub-complex is

(181)

\chi(K^{\ell+\bullet}_{k,r})=\sum_{s=\ell}^{r}(-1)^{s-\ell}\binom{r}{s}=\binom{r-1}{\ell-1}=\left[\left(\frac{1}{(\ell-1)!}(\partial_{t})^{\ell-1}t^{-1}\right)t^{r}\right]_{t=1}.

We then have

(182)

K^{\ell+\bullet}_{k}\cong\bigoplus_{r}\left(K^{\ell+\bullet}_{k,r}\right)^{p(k,r)}.

Thus, the dimension of the horizontal complex is

(183)

\chi(K^{\ell+\bullet}_{k})=\sum_{r>0}p(k,r)\chi(K^{\ell+\bullet}_{k,r})=\sum_{r>0}p(k,r)\binom{r-1}{\ell-1}=\left[\frac{1}{(\ell-1)!}(\partial_{t})^{\ell-1}t^{-1}\sum_{r>0}p(k,r)t^{r}\right]_{t=1}

where
p(k,r)
is the number of partitions of
k
with
r
minima.
Thus, the generating function for the dimension of the kernel is

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\dim(\ker{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n})x^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\chi(K^{3+i+\bullet}_{n})x^{n}\right)x^{i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}x^{i+1}\left[\frac{1}{(3+i-1)!}(\partial_{t})^{3+i-1}(t^{-1}P(x,t))\right]_{t=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{-1}\left[\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-x)^{i}}{i!}(\partial_{t})^{i}(t^{-1}P(x,t))\right]_{t=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{-1}\left(\left[\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-x)^{i}}{i!}(\partial_{t})^{i}(t^{-1}P(x,t))\right]_{t=1}-\left[-x\partial_{t}(t^{-1}P(x,t))\right]_{t=1}-P(x,1)\right).

Using Taylor’s formula, and the properties
A.2
of
P(x,t)
, we find

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{-1}\left((1-x)^{-1}P(x,1-x)+(1-x)P(x,1)+P_{t}(x,1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{-1}(1-x)^{-1}+x^{-1}(1-x)P(x)+x^{-1}(1-x)^{-1}P(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\left(x^{2}+x-1\right)P(x)}{x(1-x)}.

∎

3.7.4.
Back to the Cokernel

Using the calculation of the dimension of the kernel (proposition
3.35
), we can conclude that:

Lemma 3.36
.

The set of relations
160
exhaust the cokernel of the total trace, i.e.

(184)

\mathrm{coker}\,\,{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n}=Span\{R^{s}_{n}:s\in{\Omega}(n)\}.

Proof.

The vanishing Euler characteristic of the exact sequence
158
is

(185)

0=\chi\left(\ker{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\rightarrow\mathrm{coker}\,{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n}\right).

The generating function of this yields

(186)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}(\dim\mathrm{coker}{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n})x^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}(\dim\mathrm{ker}{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n}-\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{n}+\dim{\mathcal{W}}_{n})x^{n}

(188)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{1+\left(x^{2}+x-1\right)P(x)}{x(1-x)}\right)-\left(\tfrac{P(x)}{1-x}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\tfrac{P(x)-1}{x}+\left(Q(x)-\tfrac{1}{1-x}\right)+P(x)\right).

With this we confirm that the dimension of the cokernel is given by

(189)

\sum_{n\geq 0}(\dim\mathrm{coker}{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n})x^{n}=Q(x).

∎

The goal of this section will be to produce interesting algebra elements

To work towards the construction of these interesting elements, we provide more results on the structure of the algebra

4.1.
Homological algebra

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a graded associative algebra. We recall the Hochschild cochain complex

(191)

{\mathcal{C}}^{k}=\mathop{\rm Hom}(\otimes^{k}{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{A}})[-k+1]

Equipped with the Gerstenhaber bracket
\{,\}
, c.f.
[
6
]
. In particular let
\mu:\otimes^{2}{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
be the multiplication map
\mu(a,b)=ab
. The differential
\partial=\{\cdot,\mu\}
, which satisfies
\partial^{2}=0
, turns
(C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{A}}),\partial)
into a differential graded (dg) algebra - the Hochschild cochain complex.

In particular, for any linear map
T:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
, we call the map
\partial T:\otimes^{2}{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
the
derivator
of
T
, since

(192)

(\partial T)(\zeta,\xi)=T(\zeta\cdot\xi)-(T\zeta)\cdot\xi-\zeta\cdot(T\xi),

clearly vanishes if

is a derivation of the algebra, i.e.
\ker\partial_{1}=\{\text{Derivations of }{\mathcal{A}}\}
.
For the next section, the following expression of
\partial^{2}T=0
will be important:

Corollary 4.1
.

The derivator
\partial T
satisfies the following identity

(193)

\partial T(a\cdot b,c)-\partial T(a,b\cdot c)=a\cdot\partial T(b,c)-\partial T(a,b)\cdot c.

4.2.
\beta
Elements

We know that the NS Lax operator
{\mathcal{L}}
is not a derivation, however its derivator will play a fundamental role in the analysis to follow.
We use the notation

(194)

\beta(\xi,\zeta):=\partial{\mathcal{L}}(\xi,\zeta)={\mathcal{L}}(\zeta\cdot\xi)-({\mathcal{L}}\zeta)\cdot\xi-\zeta\cdot({\mathcal{L}}\xi).

Lemma 4.2
.

The derivator
\beta:=\partial{\mathcal{L}}
of the NS Lax operator has the following properties

1)

\beta
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-bilinear, that is, for

(195)

\beta(\zeta,\eta\cdot\xi)=\eta\cdot\beta(\zeta,\xi).

In particular,
\beta

(196)

\beta(\zeta,\xi)=\beta(\zeta,\pi_{+}\xi).

2)

\beta
is Hochschild exact, that is,

where

{\mathcal{F}}
-linear. Note that this means that

{\overline{\varepsilon}}
,

{\varepsilon}_{i}

Proof.

(1) follows because

is a derivation on

.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For any

(197)

Proof.

(198)

\displaystyle=

\nu=1

(346)

\sum_{s\in\mathcal{A}_{\mu}}\hat{c}_{\mu,1}^{\mu+s}\left(\frac{1}{u-[s]}\right)=T_{\mu}(u)-1.

It can be checked that Theorem

determines all Jack LR coefficients

for

. However, the number of poles on the right-hand side grows like

, whereas the number of LR coefficients on the left grows as the partition number

, so in general this system of equations for the Jack LR coefficients is undetermined.

Example 4.29
.

Let
\mu=1^{2},\nu=2

\hat{c}_{1^{2},2}^{1^{4}}\left(\frac{1}{u-[3,0]}+\ldots\right)+\hat{c}_{1^{2},2}^{1^{2}2}\left(\frac{1}{u-[2,0]}+\ldots\right)+\hat{c}_{1^{2},2}^{2^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{u-[1,1]}+\ldots\right)

(350)

+\hat{c}_{1^{2},2}^{13}\left(\frac{1}{u-[0,2]}+\ldots\right)+\hat{c}_{1^{2},2}^{4}\left(\frac{1}{u-[0,3]}+\ldots\right).

We can read off the only non-zero (up to transposition) hatted LR coefficient

which gives the regular Jack LR coefficient

6.
Structure of twists

In this final section, we outline some conjectures that hope to further illuminate the structure of the algebra of Lax eigenfunctions, in particular we seek to provide a natural explanation of the trace twist property (
293
), that is,

{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n}(\beta(\zeta,\xi))=\rho_{*}\circ{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{n-1}(\theta(\zeta,\xi)),

where as before
\rho_{*}:(\{x_{\gamma}\},\{y^{s}\},\{0\})\mapsto(\{0\},\{y^{s}\},\{-x_{\gamma}\})
.

6.1.
The
\rho
operator

We begin by introducing a peculiar operator.

Definition 6.1
.

Consider the following operator, defined for
s\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}
,

(408)

\rho_{\lambda}^{s}:{{Z}}_{\lambda}^{0}\to\bigoplus_{\gamma\nearrow(\lambda+s)}{{X}}_{\gamma}^{0}\,\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{|\lambda|+1},\qquad\rho_{\lambda}^{s}:{\hat{\psi}}_{\lambda}^{t}-{\hat{\psi}}_{\lambda}^{s}\mapsto{\hat{\psi}}_{\lambda+s}^{t}-{\hat{\psi}}_{\lambda+t}^{s}.

The operator
\rho_{\lambda}^{s}
is expressed in the basis of
{{Z}}_{\lambda}^{0}
given by
\{{\hat{\psi}}_{\lambda}^{t}-{\hat{\psi}}_{\lambda}^{s}\}_{t\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda},t\neq s}
.

The next result follows directly from the definition.

Corollary 6.2
.

For
\zeta\in{{Z}}_{\lambda}^{0}
, i.e.
\zeta\in{{Z}}_{\lambda}
and
{z}_{\lambda}(\zeta)=0
, the map
\rho_{\lambda}^{s}
has the properties

(409)

{y}_{u}(\rho_{\lambda}^{s}\zeta)={y}_{u}(\zeta),\qquad{z}_{\gamma}(\rho_{\lambda}^{s}\zeta)=-{x}_{\gamma}(\zeta),\qquad{x}_{\gamma}(\rho_{\lambda}^{s}\zeta)=0,

for all
\gamma\,\vdash\,|\lambda|+1
. In other words,
\rho_{\lambda}^{s}
induces the trace twist (
261
)

One point functions in large N N vector models at finite chemical potential
Justin R. David, Srijan Kumar

Abstract
We evaluate the thermal one point function of higher spin currents in the critical model of U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) complex scalars interacting with a quartic potential and the U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) Gross-Neveu model of Dirac fermions at large N N and strong coupling using the Euclidean inversion formula. These models are considered in odd space time dimensions d d and held at finite temperature and finite real chemical potential μ \mu measured in units of the temperature. We show that these one point functions simplify both at large spin and large d d . At large spin, the one point functions behave as though the theory is free, the chemical potential appears through a simple pre-factor which is either cosh ⁡ μ \cosh\mu or sinh ⁡ μ \sinh\mu depending on whether the spin is even or odd. At large d d , but at finite spin and chemical potential, the 1-point functions are suppressed exponentially in d d compared to the free theory. We study a fixed point of the critical Gross-Neveu model in d = 3 d=3 with 1-point functions exhibiting a branch cut in the chemical potential plane. The critical exponent for the free energy or the pressure at the branch point is 3 / 2 3/2 which coincides with the mean field exponent of the Lee-Yang edge singularity for repulsive core interactions.

2.3.1
Thermal 1-pt functions

At finite temperature the twisted 2-point function for complex

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(\tau,\vec{x})=\langle\phi^{*}(\tau,\vec{x})\phi(0,0)\rangle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{e^{i(\tilde{\omega}_{n}\tau+\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x})}}{\tilde{\omega}_{n}^{2}+\vec{k}^{2}+m_{th}^{2}},

The thermal mass will be determined by demanding that the thermal 1-point function of

This defines the conformal fixed points of the theory. We
sum over Matsubara frequencies using Poisson re-summation, which transforms the two point function to a
sum over images in the imaginary time direction. This results in

\displaystyle G(\tau,\vec{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\mu\tau}\int\frac{d\omega d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}e^{i\omega(\tau-n)}}{(\omega-\mu)^{2}+\vec{k}^{2}+m_{\rm th}^{2}},

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-in\mu}\int\frac{d\omega d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}e^{i\tilde{\omega}(\tau-n)}}{\tilde{\omega}^{2}+\vec{k}^{2}+m_{\rm th}^{2}}.

\displaystyle{G}(\tau,\vec{x})=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-im\mu}}{(2\pi)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}m_{\rm th}^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{K_{k-\frac{1}{2}}(m_{\rm th}\sqrt{(m-z)(m-\bar{z})}\,)}{[(m-z)(m-\bar{z})]^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}.

\displaystyle{\rm Disc}\bigg[\frac{K_{k-\frac{1}{2}}(m_{\rm th}\sqrt{(m-z)(m-\bar{z})}\,)}{[(m-z)(m-\bar{z})]^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}\bigg]=\pi\frac{J_{\frac{1}{2}-k}(m_{\rm th}\sqrt{(z-m)(m-\bar{z})})}{[(z-m)(m-\bar{z})]^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}.

4
Conclusions

In this paper we have generalised the use of the Euclidean inversion formula to large
N
vector models at finite
values of chemical potentials.
We studied the resulting one point functions of bi-linear in fields at the non-trivial fixed point of these models
at both large spins and large dimensions and
showed that they simplify. At large spins they are proportional to the one point functions of the free theory, while at large
dimensions they vanish in comparison with the free theory.
We studied both the bosonic and fermionic model in detail for the special case of
d=3
space-time dimensions.
For the Gross-Neveu model in
d=3
we studied a fixed point which is argued to exhibit the Yang-Lee edge singularity.
The exponent of the branch cut of the pressure at the singularity in the chemical potential plane is found to be

\frac{3}{2}
which coincides with mean field theory description of systems which have repulsive core interactions.
As a byproduct of our investigations, we have found non-trivial identities satisfied by certain sums of Bernoulli polynomials.

There has been several interesting works which have developed the program of constraining both one
point functions and two point functions in thermal CFT’s using symmetries
[
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
.
It will be interesting to see how the results obtained for CFT’s from vector models fit these general observations.
One specific direction is to study the perturbative expansion of thermal one point functions for general
CFT’s at large spin obtained in
[
2
]
to see if it can be generalised to situations when the chemical potential
is turned on.
A more ambitious program would be to see if a solution dual to a thermal state can be constructed in
Vasiliev theories and these simplifications and large spin can be seen in the dual descriptions.

Another direction is to develop the inversion formula for vector models on geometries
S^{1}\times S^{d-1}
or
S^{1}\times AdS_{d-1}

and connect with the general results of the ambient space formalism developed in
[
56
,
57
]
.
Finally, it is important to study how the results of this paper generalise to large
N
Chern-Simmons theories with matter.
The recent development in this subject
[
58
]

of evaluating the partition function in temporal gauge should be useful
in obtaining the Euclidean inversion formula in these theories.

The results from the analysis at large dimensions suggest that there exists a unique function of dimension that describes
one point functions of large
N
vector models both bosonic and fermionic.
It will be interesting to obtain this for arbitrary odd dimensions, rather than just
in the asymptotic limit.

Appendix A
Gap equation and stress tensor from the partition function

In this appendix we discuss the derivation of the gap equation as a saddle point equation of the partition function at large
N
for both the case of complex scalars and Gross-Neveu model at finite chemical potential. The partition function can be computed by linearising the theory at the leading order in
N
. We also compute the stress tensor and the spin-1 conserved currents from the partition function in this appendix.

A.1
Complex Scalars at finite density

Consider the action for
N
massless complex scalar fields with imaginary chemical potential given by,

\displaystyle S=\int d^{2k+1}x\bigg[|(\partial_{\tau}-i\hat{\mu}){\tilde{\phi}}|^{2}+\partial_{i}\tilde{\phi}\partial_{i}\tilde{\phi}^{*}+\frac{\lambda}{N}(|{\tilde{\phi}}|)^{4}\bigg].

(186)

where
{\tilde{\phi}}
is
N
dimensional vector of complex scalars with
\tilde{\phi}^{*}\tilde{\phi}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\tilde{\phi}^{*}_{a}\tilde{\phi}_{a}
and
i=1,2,\cdots,2k
denoting the spatial directions.
At finite temperature the partition function can be obtained by the following path integral over
N
scalar fields with the imaginary time direction being compactified on a circle of length
\beta.

\tilde{Z}=\int{\cal D}\tilde{\phi}^{*}{\cal D}\tilde{\phi}e^{-S(\tilde{\phi},\tilde{\phi}^{*})}.

(187)

Using the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation, one obtains a quadratic action in
\phi
by introducing an auxiliary field
\zeta
,

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\cal D}\tilde{\phi}^{*}{\cal D}\tilde{\phi}\exp\left[{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\int d\tau d^{2k}x\Big(|(\partial_{\tau}-i\hat{\mu}){\tilde{\phi}}|^{2}+|\partial_{\vec{x}}{\tilde{\phi}}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{N}(|{\tilde{\phi}}|)^{4}\Big)}\right],

(188)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\cal D}\tilde{\phi}^{*}{\cal D}\tilde{\phi}{\cal D}\zeta\left[-\int_{0}^{\beta}\int d\tau d^{2k}x\Big(|(\partial_{\tau}-i\hat{\mu}){\tilde{\phi}}|^{2}+|\partial_{\vec{x}}{\tilde{\phi}}|^{2}+\frac{\zeta^{2}N}{4\lambda}+i\zeta|{\tilde{\phi}}|^{2}\Big)\right].

By isolating the zero mode
\zeta_{0}
from the auxiliary field
\zeta
,

\displaystyle\zeta=\tilde{\zeta}+\zeta_{0}.

(189)

\tilde{\zeta}
is the non-zero mode, we substitute this into the partition function to obtain,

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}=\int d\zeta_{0}{\cal D}{\tilde{\phi}^{*}}{\cal D}\tilde{\phi}\left[\exp\Big(-\frac{\zeta_{0}^{2}N\beta V_{2k}}{4\lambda}\Big)\exp(-S_{0}-S_{I})\right],\qquad{\rm where,}

(190)

\displaystyle S_{0}=\int_{0}^{\beta}\int d\tau d^{2k}x\Big(|(\partial_{\tau}-i\hat{\mu}){\tilde{\phi}}|^{2}+|\partial_{\vec{x}}{\tilde{\phi}}|^{2}+\frac{\zeta^{2}N}{\lambda}+i\zeta_{0}|{\tilde{\phi}}|^{2}\Big),\quad S_{I}=\int_{0}^{\beta}\int d\tau d^{2k}x\;i\tilde{\zeta}|{\tilde{\phi}}|^{2}.

Now, at the leading order in large
N
we can ignore
S_{I}
as it is easy to show that the perturbative corrections due to this term appear in the order
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}
when the non-zero mode of the auxiliary field is integrated out.
Thus the non-zero mode of
\zeta
is present as an Gausssian path integral in the leading order at large
N
and this integral is easily performed resulting in an ordinary integral on the zero mode,

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\zeta_{0}\exp\left[-\beta V_{2k}N\Big(\frac{\zeta_{0}^{2}}{4\lambda}-\frac{1}{\beta}\log Z(i\zeta_{0})\Big)\right],

(191)

where,

\displaystyle\log Z(i\zeta_{0})=-\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\frac{d^{2k}p}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\log\left[\frac{(2\pi n-\hat{\mu}\beta)^{2}}{\beta^{2}}+\vec{p}^{2}+i\zeta_{0}\right].

(192)

Now we can evaluate the partition function by looking for the saddle point of the above integral in
\zeta_{0}.
If the saddle point value of
\zeta_{0}
is
\zeta_{0}^{*}
, the thermal mass can be expressed as,

\displaystyle m_{\rm th}^{2}=i\zeta_{0}^{*}.

(193)

The saddle point equation of the integral (
191
) is given by,

m_{\rm th}^{3}=-\lambda\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial m_{\rm th}}\log Z(m_{\rm th}).

(194)

After doing the matsubara sum,
Z(m_{\rm th})
is evaluated as follows ,

\displaystyle\log{Z}(m_{\rm th})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\beta^{d-1}}\int\frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\Big[\sqrt{\vec{k}^{2}+m_{\rm th}^{2}\beta^{2}}+2{\rm Re}\Big(\log\big[1-e^{-\sqrt{\vec{k}^{2}+m_{\rm th}^{2}\beta^{2}}+i\beta\hat{\mu}}\big]\Big)\Big],

\displaystyle=\beta\frac{m_{\rm th}^{2k+1}\Gamma\left(-k-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{2^{2k+1}\pi^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{(k+1-n)_{2n}(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{k-n}{\rm Re}[\text{Li}_{n+k+1}\left(e^{-\beta(m_{\rm th}+i\hat{\mu})}\right)]}{n!\pi^{k}2^{n+k-1}\beta^{2k}}.

(195)

The integral of the first term from the parenthesis is evaluated by analytically continuing the following standard integral,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\;\frac{x^{2k-1}}{(x^{2}+1)^{a}}=\frac{\Gamma(a-k)\Gamma(k)}{2\Gamma(a)}.

(196)

Integral of the 2nd term is convergent and one of the standard approaches is to use the infinite series expansion of
\log(1+x)
for small
x
and each term is integrated in term of BesselK functions of half integer order and finally one can resum the infinite number of terms using the fact that BesselK of half integer order can be represented by finite order polynomial. And it can be expressed in terms of the Polylogarithm functions as given in (
195
).

Using (
195
) and (
194
), the gap equation at the leading order in large
\lambda
is computed to be,

\displaystyle\frac{(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{k}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-k\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}}+\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\frac{(k-n)_{2n}{\rm Re}\left[\text{Li}_{n+k}\left(e^{-\beta(m_{\rm th}+i\hat{\mu})}\right)\right]}{(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{n}2^{n-k-1}\Gamma(n+1)}=0.

(197)

This model behaves as a CFT at the critical value of the thermal mass
m_{\rm th}
which satisfies the above equation. The stress tensor at the critical point can be evaluated from the partition function by,

T_{00}=\frac{1}{NV_{2k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\log\tilde{Z}(m_{\rm th})\bigg|_{\hat{\mu}\beta={\rm fixed}}.

(198)

Note that in presence of the chemical potential the stress tensor can be evaluated by differentiating the partition function keeping
\hat{\mu}\beta=
fixed, this definition is consistent with the more general definition of the stress tensor by metric variation of the action. Finally the stress tensor can be given by,

\displaystyle T_{00}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\rm th}^{2k+1}\Gamma\left(-k-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{2^{2k+1}\pi^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\sum_{n=0}^{k+1}\frac{m_{\rm th}\left((k+n)^{2}+k-n\right)(k-n+2)_{2n-2}{\rm Re}\left[\text{Li}_{n+k}\left(e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta+i\hat{\mu}\beta}\right)\right]}{n!\pi^{k}2^{n+k-1}\beta^{2k}(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{n-k}}.

(199)

The stress tensor at the critical point or the conformal fixed point characterised by the thermal mass can be further simplified on the use of the gap equation (
197
) to have,

\displaystyle T_{00}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta^{2k+1}\pi^{k}}\Big(\frac{k}{2k+1}\Big)\sum_{n=0}^{k+1}\frac{(k-n+2)_{2n}}{n!2^{n+k-2}}(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{k+1-n}{\rm Re}[\text{Li}_{n+k}(e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta+i\hat{\mu}\beta})].

(200)

As
(m_{\rm th}\beta)
is a dimensionless number, the conformal stress tensor scales with the powers of temperature which is the only scale present in the critical theory.
Also the use of gap equation in the partition function (
195
) gives the free energy density for the critical theory to be,

\displaystyle F=-\frac{1}{\beta NV_{2k}}\log\tilde{Z}=-\frac{1}{\beta^{2k+1}\pi^{k}}\sum_{n=0}^{k+1}\frac{(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{k-n+1}(k-n+2)_{2n}}{2^{n+k-1}(2k+1)n!}{\rm Re}[\text{Li}_{n+k}(e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta+i\hat{\mu}\beta})].

(201)

And from equations (
200
) and (
201
), it is easy to observe the signature relationship between the stress tensor and the free energy density of a conformal field theory at finite temperature is being satisfied,

\displaystyle T_{00}=(d-1)F.

(202)

By putting
l=2
in the equation (
84
), we see that the thermal one point function of the conformal spin-2 current evaluated using the OPE inversion formula agrees with the stress tensor
T_{00}
up to an overall constant factor given by,

\displaystyle a_{\mathcal{O}}[0,2]=-\frac{1}{4k(2k-1)}T_{00}.

(203)

The spin-1 conserved current can also be obtained form the partition function by,

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\hat{\mu}}}\log Z(m_{\rm th})

(204)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{(k-n+1)_{2n}(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{k-n}\Big[\text{Li}_{k+n}\left(e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}-i\beta\hat{\mu}}\right)-\text{Li}_{k+n}\left(e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}+i\beta\hat{\mu}}\right)\Big]}{\pi^{k}\beta^{2k}2^{k+n}n!}.

(205)

Now, the thermal expectation of conformal spin-1 current is obtained by putting
l=1
in the OPE inversion formula answer (
84
), and this also agrees with
J
up to a constant factor given as follows,

\displaystyle a_{\mathcal{O}}[0,1]=-\frac{1}{2(2k-1)}J.

A.1.1

The evaluation of the partition in (
195
), involved using the analytical continuation of the integral in (
196
).
This approach was introduced in
[
16
]
, but it is illustrative to relate it with the approach of introducing
a cutoff scale or using a dimensional regulator which is found in the literature
[
45
,
33
,
15
,
59
]
.
For this purpose it is sufficient to
restrict our discussions to
d=3,4
which are well studied in the literature and to bring out the differences
between even and odd dimension.

d=3

Consider the integral in (
195
), with
d=3,k=1
, it is the first term that needs a regulator.
Let us perform the integral using the momentum cut off
\Lambda
, the first term in (
195
) which needs a regulator.
This can be found by using the result

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}k\sqrt{k^{2}+m_{\rm th}^{2}}\,dk=\frac{\Lambda^{3}}{3}+\frac{\Lambda m_{\rm th}^{2}}{2}-\frac{m_{\rm th}^{3}}{3}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\Lambda}\Big).

(207)

Substituting this in (
195

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\log Z(m_{\rm th})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Bigg[\beta\left(\frac{\Lambda^{3}}{3}+\frac{\Lambda m_{\rm th}^{2}}{2}-\frac{m_{\rm th}^{3}}{3}\right)

(208)

\displaystyle-2{\rm Re}\frac{\left(\beta m_{\rm th}\text{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta+i\mu\beta}\right)+\text{Li}_{3}\left(e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta+i\mu\beta}\right)\right)}{\beta^{2}}\Bigg]+O\Big(\frac{1}{\Lambda}\Big).

Note that the second integral in (
195
) is convergent as the cut off is taken to infinity, which results the expression involving
poly-logarithims.
Let us also obtain the derivative of this partition function,

\displaystyle\partial_{m_{\rm th}}\log Z=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big(\beta\left(\Lambda m_{\rm th}-m_{\rm th}^{2}\right)-2m_{\rm th}{\rm Re}\log(1-e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}+i\mu\beta})\Big).

(209)

We can now substitute this result in the gap equation (
194
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{m_{\rm th}^{2}}{\lambda}-\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi}=-\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}-\frac{1}{\pi\beta}{\rm Re}\log(1-e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}+i\mu\beta}).

(210)

Let us now define the renormalized coupling
\lambda_{R}
in the following manner

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi m_{\rm th}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{R}}.

(211)

And the gap equation (
210
) at zero temperature relates the renormalized coupling
\lambda_{R}
to the mass of theory at zero temperature
m_{(0)}
which represents a physical length scale in the theory, given as

\displaystyle\lambda_{R}=-2\pi m_{(0)}.

(212)

Now using (
211
) and (
212
) we can rewrite the gap equation (
210
) for the thermal mass
m_{\rm th}
in terms of the physical mass scale
m_{(0)}
and the inverse temperature
\beta
as

\displaystyle\frac{m_{\rm th}^{2}}{2\pi m_{0}}=\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}+\frac{1}{\pi\beta}{\rm Re}\log(1-e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}+i\mu\beta}).

(213)

By solving this gap equation we see that the dimensionless combination
m_{\rm th}\beta
depends on another scale

m_{(0)}
and therefore not a CFT.
When the value of this scale is set to a critical value such that

m_{(0)}\beta\to\infty
the above equation coincides with the gap equation in (
16
) obtained using
the analytical continuation of the integral (
196
) and setting the coupling to infinity.
This results in the thermal mass given by (
17
).
Therefore we conclude that the approach developed by
[
16
]
in evaluating the partition function in odd dimensions

It is interesting to observe that demanding the vanishing of the expectation value of
|\phi|^{2}
obtained using the inversion
formula also results in the gap equation for the critical theory.
We have checked that this reasoning works for other odd dimensions and also for the Gross-Neveu model in odd dimensions.

d=4

The relevant divergent integral in the partition function
given in (

). with

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}k^{2}\sqrt{k^{2}+m_{\rm th}^{2}}\,dk=\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{4}+\frac{\Lambda^{2}m_{\rm th}^{2}}{4}-\frac{1}{32}m_{\rm th}^{4}\left(\log\frac{16\Lambda^{4}}{m_{\rm th}^{4}}-1\right)+O\Big(\frac{1}{\Lambda}\Big).

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\log Z(m_{\rm th})=-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\Bigg(\beta\left[\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{4}+\frac{\Lambda^{2}m_{\rm th}^{2}}{4}-\frac{1}{32}m_{\rm th}^{4}\left(\log\frac{16\Lambda^{4}}{m_{\rm th}^{4}}-1\right)\right]

\displaystyle-2{\rm Re}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\mu\beta n}m_{\rm th}^{2}K_{2}(m_{\rm th}n\beta)}{\beta n^{2}}\Bigg).

\displaystyle\partial_{m_{\rm th}}\log Z=-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{1}{8}\beta m_{\rm th}\left(4\Lambda^{2}-m_{\rm th}^{2}\log\frac{16\Lambda^{4}}{m_{\rm th}^{4}}+2m_{\rm th}^{2}\right)+2{\rm Re}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\mu\beta n}m_{\rm th}^{2}K_{1}(m_{\rm th}n\beta)}{n}\Bigg].

(216)

Finally substituting this result in the gap equation (

\displaystyle m_{\rm th}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{4\pi^{2}m_{\rm th}^{2}}\right)=-\frac{m_{\rm th}}{8\pi^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{\rm th}^{2}e}\Big)+\frac{1}{\pi^{2}\beta}{\rm Re}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\mu\beta n}K_{1}(m_{\rm th}n\beta)}{n}.

(217)

We now define renormalized coupling

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{R}(\Lambda)}=\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{4\pi^{2}m_{\rm th}^{2}}.

(218)

From the gap equation (

) at zero temperature, we get the relation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{R}(\Lambda)}=-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{(0)}^{2}e}\Big),

(219)

where
m_{(0)}
is a physical length scale.
Replacing the expression on the RHS of (

\displaystyle m_{\rm th}\left(\frac{8\pi^{2}}{\lambda_{R}(\Lambda)}+\log\Big(\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{\rm th}^{2}e}\Big)\right)=\frac{4}{\beta}{\rm Re}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\mu\beta n}K_{1}(m_{\rm th}n\beta)}{n}.

(220)

Observe that the cut off dependence on cancels using the relation (

) and we obtain the gap equation which depends on the physical length
m_{0}

\displaystyle m_{\rm th}\log\Big(\frac{m_{(0)}}{m_{\rm th}}\Big)=\frac{2}{\beta}{\rm Re}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\mu\beta n}K_{1}(m_{\rm th}n\beta)}{n}.

(221)

Similar equations have been obtained using dimensional regularization in

the thermal mass

A.2
Gross-Neveu model with chemical potential

We repeat the same large

analysis for
U(N)
Gross-Neveu model with four fermion interaction at finite temperature in presence of an imaginary chemical potential characterised by
\hat{\mu}
. We start with the following Euclidean action with
N
massless Dirac fermions
\psi_{a}
’s where
a=1,\cdots,N
.

S=\int d^{2k+1}x\left[{\tilde{\psi}}_{a}^{\dagger}(\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu})\tilde{\psi}_{a}-i\hat{\mu}{\tilde{\psi}}_{a}^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\tilde{\psi}_{a}-\frac{\lambda}{N}({\tilde{\psi}}_{a}^{\dagger}\tilde{\psi}_{a})^{2}\right].

(222)

The interaction strength is controlled by the coupling constant
\lambda
.
In Euclidean signature we have
\bar{\tilde{\psi}}=\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}
and the gamma matrices satisfy the following anti-commutation relation,

\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2\delta^{\mu\nu}.

(223)

Again the partition function can be expressed as a path integral of a quadratic action in
\psi
by introducing an auxiliary field
\zeta
applying the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation,

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\cal D}{\tilde{\psi}}^{\dagger}{\cal D}\tilde{\psi}\exp\left[{-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau d^{2k}x\Big({\tilde{\psi}}^{\dagger}_{a}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\psi}_{a}-i\hat{\mu}{\tilde{\psi}}^{\dagger}_{a}\gamma^{0}\tilde{\psi}_{a}-\frac{\lambda}{N}({\tilde{\psi}}^{\dagger}_{a}\tilde{\psi}_{a})^{2}\Big)}\right],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\cal D}{\tilde{\psi}}^{\dagger}{\cal D}\tilde{\psi}{\cal D}\zeta\left[-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau d^{2k}x\Big({\tilde{\psi}}^{\dagger}_{a}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\psi}_{a}-i\hat{\mu}{\tilde{\psi}}^{\dagger}_{a}\gamma^{0}\tilde{\psi}_{a}+\frac{\zeta^{2}N}{4\lambda}+\zeta({\tilde{\psi}}^{\dagger}_{a}\tilde{\psi}_{a})\Big)\right].

Again path integral over all fermionic fields is denoted above.
Normalisation constant of the Gaussian path integral is absorbed in the measure. Identical to the previous case we separate out the zero mode of the auxiliary field as
\zeta_{0}
and call the rest to be
\tilde{\zeta}
,

\displaystyle\zeta=\tilde{\zeta}+\zeta_{0}.

(225)

Plugging this definition into (
A.2
), we get,

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}=\int d\zeta_{0}{\cal D}\bar{\psi}{\cal D}\psi\left[\exp\Big(-\frac{\zeta_{0}^{2}N\beta V_{2k}}{4\lambda}\Big)\exp(-S_{0}-S_{I})\right],

(226)

\displaystyle S_{0}=\int d\tau d^{2k}x\Big[\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\psi}-i\hat{\mu}\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\tilde{\psi}+\zeta_{0}\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}\tilde{\psi}+\frac{\tilde{\zeta}^{2}N}{4\lambda}\Big],\qquad\qquad S_{I}=\int d\tau d^{2k}x\;\tilde{\zeta}\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}\tilde{\psi}.

Using the same argument as before, the contribution due to
S_{I}
in the partition function is suppressed by
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}
and can be dropped off in our calculation restricted to the leading order in
N
. Thus ignoring
S_{I}
, we are left with the gaussian path integral in the non-zero mode of the auxiliary field which is evaluated trivially and the computation of the partition function is reduced to evaluating an ordinary integral over
\zeta_{0}
as given below,

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\zeta_{0}\exp\left[-\beta V_{2k}N\Big(\frac{\zeta_{0}^{2}}{4\lambda}-\frac{1}{\beta}\log Z(\zeta_{0})\Big)\right],

(227)

where

\displaystyle\log Z(\zeta_{0})=2^{k-1}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\frac{d^{2k}p}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\log\left[\frac{\big((2n+1)\pi-\hat{\mu}\beta\big)^{2}}{\beta^{2}}+\vec{p}^{2}+\zeta_{0}^{2}\right].

(228)

Note that
2^{k-1}
factor is due to the fact that the Dirac operator is a
2^{k}\times 2^{k}
dimensional matrix.
After evaluating the Matsubara sum, we obtain

\displaystyle\log Z(\zeta_{0})=\frac{2^{k-1}}{\beta^{2k}}\int\frac{d^{2k}p}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\left[\sqrt{\vec{p}^{2}+\zeta_{0}^{2}\beta}+2{\rm Re}\log\Big(1+e^{-\sqrt{\vec{p}^{2}+\zeta_{0}^{2}\beta^{2}}+i\hat{\mu}\beta}\big)\right].

(229)

And using similar steps as in the case of complex scalars this can be evaluated to give

\displaystyle\log Z(\zeta_{0})=-\frac{\zeta_{0}^{2k+1}\beta}{\pi^{k}2^{k+2}}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{(k-n+1)_{2n}(\zeta_{0}\beta)^{-k-n-1}{\rm Re}[{\rm Li}_{k+n+1}\left(-e^{-\zeta_{0}\beta-i\hat{\mu}\beta}\right)]}{2^{n-k-2}n!}+\frac{\Gamma\left(-k-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right].

Again we compute the partition function by approximating the integral (
227
) by the value of the integrand at the saddle point
\zeta_{0}=\zeta_{0}^{*}
, and it is related to the thermal by
m_{\rm th}=\zeta_{0}^{*}.
The saddle point equation is given by,

m_{\rm th}=2\lambda\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial m_{\rm th}}Z(m_{\rm th}).

(231)

At the leading order in large
\lambda
it reduces to,

\frac{\partial}{\partial m_{\rm th}}Z(m_{\rm th})=0.

(232)

The above saddle point condition at large
\lambda
leads to the following gap equation in thermal mass
m_{\rm th}
,

\displaystyle 2(2m_{\rm th}\beta)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\frac{(k-n)_{2n}}{(2m_{\rm th}\beta)^{n}n!}{\rm Re}[\text{Li}_{k+n}(-e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta-i\hat{\mu}\beta})]+\frac{(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{2k}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}-k\big)=0.

(233)

The theory at the critical value of the thermal
m_{\rm th}
satisfying the gap equation is a CFT. The stress
tensor is given by

T_{00}=\frac{1}{V_{2k}N}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\log\tilde{Z}(m_{\rm th})\bigg|_{\hat{\mu}\beta={\rm fixed}}.

(234)

\displaystyle T_{00}=-\frac{m_{\rm th}^{2k+1}\Gamma\left(-k-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{2^{k+2}\pi^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+\sum_{n=0}^{k+1}\frac{\left((k+n)^{2}+k-n\right)(k-n+2)_{2n-2}{\rm Re}[\text{Li}_{k+n}\left(-e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta-i\mu\beta}\right)]}{(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{n-k-1}2^{n}\beta^{2k+1}\pi^{k}n!}.

(235)

The above equation together with the gap equation results in

\displaystyle T_{00}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2k+1}\pi^{k}}\Big(\frac{k}{2k+1}\Big)\sum_{n=0}^{k+1}\frac{(k-n+2)_{2n}(\beta m_{\rm th})^{k-n+1}}{2^{n-1}n!}{\rm Re}[\text{Li}_{k+n}\big(-e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta-i\beta\hat{\mu}}\big)].

(236)

This expression is the stress tensor or the energy density per fermion at the conformal fixed point and again it scales with the powers of inverse temperature.
Using the gap equation in the partition function it is straightforward to see that the stress tensor is proportional free energy density

\displaystyle T_{00}=(d-1)F,\qquad{\rm where}\ \ F=-\frac{1}{\beta V_{2k}N}\log\tilde{Z}.

(237)

In the Hamiltonian picture the expectation value of the spin-1 current is given by

J=\langle\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\gamma^{\mu}\tilde{\psi}\rangle_{(\beta,\mu)},

(238)

Here
\tilde{\psi}
is an operator,
\gamma^{0}
is Hermitian. Therefore as an operator, the current is Hermitian.
We have also used the fact that it is only the time component that
gets non-trivial expectation value. From the path integral, this expectation value is obtained by the following

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{\mu}}\log Z(m_{\rm th})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{(k-n+1)_{2n}(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{k-n}\Big[\text{Li}_{k+n}\left(-e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}-i\hat{\mu}\beta}\right)-\text{Li}_{k+n}\left(-e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}+i\hat{\mu}\beta}\right)\Big]}{\beta^{2k}\pi^{k}2^{n+1}n!}.

(239)

Observe that this charge is purely imaginary when the chemical potential is purely imaginary and real when the
chemical potential is real as it should be.

We can also consider the expectation of the spin-1 current in the class
{\cal O}_{0}[0,1]
.
In the Hamiltonian picture it is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{J}=\frac{i}{2}\Big\langle\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\big(\partial_{\nu}-i\mu\delta_{\nu 0}\big)\tilde{\psi}-\big(\partial_{\nu}+i\mu\delta_{\nu 0}\big)\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\;\tilde{\psi}\Big\rangle_{(\beta,\mu)},

(240)

Note that the current is a Hermitian operator.
Furthermore, the expectation value of the sum of the terms occurring in the above equation vanishes

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\Big\langle\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\big(\partial_{\nu}-i\mu\delta_{\nu 0}\big)\tilde{\psi}+\big(\partial_{\nu}+i\mu\delta_{\nu 0}\big)\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\;\tilde{\psi})\Big\rangle_{(\beta,\mu)}=0

(241)

This is because the operator involved is a descendant of a primary,
note here the chemical potential term cancels and the operator is just a total derivative
of the primary
\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\psi
.
Therefore we can write
\tilde{J}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{J}=i\Big\langle\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\big(\partial_{\nu}-i\mu\delta_{\nu 0}\big)\tilde{\psi}\Big\rangle_{(\beta,\mu)}.

(242)

Now to obtain this expectation value from the path integral we deform the Euclidean action
(
222
) in the following manner,

S[\alpha]=\int d^{2k+1}x\left[\tilde{\psi}_{a}^{\dagger}(\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu})\tilde{\psi}_{a}-i\hat{\mu}\tilde{\psi}_{a}^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\tilde{\psi}_{a}-\frac{\lambda}{N}(\tilde{\psi}_{a}^{\dagger}\psi_{a})^{2}-\alpha\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}_{a}(\partial_{\tau}-i\hat{\mu})\tilde{\psi}_{a}\right].

(243)

The thermal one point function
J

at large
\lambda
can be evaluated by taking the derivative of the free energy of the above action with respect to

\alpha
evaluated at
\alpha=0
,

\displaystyle\tilde{J}=\langle\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}(\partial_{\tau}-i\hat{\mu})\tilde{\psi}\rangle=\frac{1}{\beta V_{2k}}\partial_{\alpha}\log Z[m_{\rm th},\alpha]\Big|_{\alpha=0}.

(244)

The partition function is evaluated by repeating the similar steps of Hubbard-Stratanovich transformation shown earlier in this appendix,

\displaystyle\log Z(\alpha,\zeta_{0})=2^{k-1}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\frac{d^{2k}p}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\log\Big[\frac{1-\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\Big((2n+1)\pi-\frac{i\zeta_{0}\alpha}{1-\alpha^{2}}-\hat{\mu}\Big)^{2}+\vec{p}^{2}+\frac{\zeta_{0}^{2}}{1-\alpha^{2}}\Big],

(245)

where
\zeta_{0}
is the zero mode of the auxiliary field.
Now, we evaluate
\log Z(\alpha,\zeta_{0})
following similar steps as shown before, and finally from (
244
) we obtain,

\displaystyle\tilde{J}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2k+1}}\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{(k-n+1)_{2n}\big[\text{Li}_{k+n}\left(-e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta+i\beta\hat{\mu}}\right)-\text{Li}_{k+n}\left(-e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}-i\hat{\mu}\beta}\right)\big]}{(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{n-k-1}\pi^{k}2^{n+1}n!}.

(246)

And it is easy to see from (
246
) and (
239
),

\displaystyle\tilde{J}=-m_{\rm th}J.

A.2.1
Evaluation of the partition function using a cut off

The divergent integral in (
229
) can also be regularised using a cutoff in the similar approach used for
U(N)
complex scalars in
A.1.1
. We restrict the discussion here for
d=3
but it can be straightforwardedly generalised for any higher dimension for both odd and even
d.
For
d=3
the 1st term in (
229
) involves the same diverging integral as it was for the
U(N)
complex scalars given in (
207
) with a momentum cut off
\Lambda
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}k\sqrt{k^{2}+m_{\rm th}^{2}}\,dk=\frac{\Lambda^{3}}{3}+\frac{\Lambda m_{\rm th}^{2}}{2}-\frac{m_{\rm th}^{3}}{3}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\Lambda}\Big).

(248)

Now the integral in the 2nd term of (
229
)
is convergent and adding the 1st term regularised using the cutoff with it and finally differentiating the resulting expression w.r.t
m_{\rm th}
we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{m_{\rm th}}\log Z(m_{\rm th})=\frac{\beta\left(\Lambda m_{\rm th}-m_{\rm th}^{2}\right)-2m_{\rm th}{\rm Re}\log\left(1+e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}+i\mu}\right)}{2\pi}.

(249)

Now plugging this in the gap equation (
231
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\lambda}-\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi}=-\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}-\frac{1}{\beta\pi}{\rm Re}\log\left(1+e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}+i\mu}\right).

(250)

Now we define the renormalised coupling
\lambda_{R}
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\lambda}-\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{R}}.

(251)

Using this in (
250
) at zero temperature we have,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{R}}=-\frac{m_{(0)}}{2\pi},

(252)

where
m_{(0)}
is the mass of the theory at zero temperature which serves as a physical scale in the theory. And finally combining (
250
),(
251
) and (
252
) we can express the gap equation for the thermal mass
m_{\rm th}
in terms of the physical scale
m_{(0)}
and the inverse temperature
\beta
as given below.

\displaystyle\frac{m_{(0)}}{2\pi}-\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{\beta\pi}{\rm Re}\log(1+e^{-\beta m_{\rm th}+i\mu}).

(253)

The gap equation in this form was also obtained in
[
45
]
, using the similar approach used here. Now setting the physical length scale at its critical value
m_{(0)}=0
, the dimensionless combination
m_{\rm th}\beta
turns out to be completely independent of any length scale and thus the theory behaves as a thermal CFT.

Appendix B
m_{\rm th}\to-m_{\rm th}
symmetry in the Gross-Neveu model

In this appendix we show that the one point functions of
higher spin currents in the class
a_{\mathcal{O}_{+}}[0,l]
for Gross-Neveu model in any odd space-time dimension are even functions of the thermal mass
m_{\rm th}
in the complex
m_{\rm th}
plane with
-\pi<{\rm Im}(m_{\rm th})<\pi\ {\rm for}\ \mu\in R.

The proof of this statement boils down to proving a pair of non-trivial mathematical identities of Bernoulli polynomials.
Thermal one point functions of the spin-
l
operators of the class
a_{\mathcal{O}_{+}}[0,l]
was given in (
138
), replacing
m_{\rm th}
by
-m_{\rm th}
we have,

\displaystyle a_{{\cal O}_{+}}[n=0,l]\Big|_{m_{\rm th}\to-m_{\rm th}}=

\displaystyle\frac{l}{4\pi^{k}(k-\frac{1}{2})_{l}}\left(-\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2}\right)^{l+k-1}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{l+k-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}(l+k-n)_{2n}}{(2m_{\rm th})^{n}n!}[{\rm Li}_{k+n}(-e^{m_{\rm th}+\mu})+(-1)^{l}{\rm Li}_{k+n}(-e^{m_{\rm th}-\mu})].

(254)

Now we have to show that the expression above is equal to the original expression of the one point function in equation (
138
).
Now to do so we will use the following standard polylogarithm identity,

\displaystyle i^{-s}{\rm Li}_{s}(e^{2\pi iz})+i^{s}{\rm Li}_{s}(e^{-2\pi iz})=\frac{(2\pi)^{s}\zeta(1-s,z)}{\Gamma(s)},

(255)

for
0\leq{\rm Re}(z)<1
if
{\rm Im}(z)\geq 0
and
0<{\rm Re}(z)\leq 1
if
{\rm Im}(z)<0
; and
\zeta(1-s,z)
refers to the Hurwitz zeta function.
Using this identity we can recast (
254
) into the following form,

\displaystyle a_{\mathcal{O}_{+}}

\displaystyle[n=0,l]\Big|_{m_{\rm th}\to-m_{\rm th}}

\displaystyle=\frac{lm_{\rm th}^{l+k-1}}{2^{l+k+1}\pi^{k}(k-\frac{1}{2})_{l}}\sum_{n=0}^{l+k-1}\frac{(l+k-n)_{2n}}{(2m_{\rm th})^{n}n!}[{\rm Li}_{k+n}(-e^{-m_{\rm th}+\mu})+(-1)^{l}{\rm Li}_{k+n}(-e^{-m_{\rm th}-\mu})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{(-i)^{k}l{m_{\rm th}}^{k+l-1}}{2^{l+1}\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{l}}[(-1)^{l}g_{k,l}(m_{\rm th},\mu)+g_{k,l}(m_{\rm th},-\mu)],

(256)

Where,

\displaystyle g_{k,l}(m_{\rm th},\mu)=\sum_{n=0}^{k+l-1}\frac{\pi^{n}(k+l-n)_{2n}\zeta\left(1-k-n,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi i}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi i}\right)}{i^{n}n!m_{\rm th}^{n}\Gamma(k+n)},

(257)

with
-\pi<{\rm Im}(m_{\rm th})<\pi
and
\mu\in R.

Note that the term in the first line of (
256
) is exactly the original expression for the thermal one point function
a_{\mathcal{O}_{+}}[0,l]
given in (
138
), thus for
a_{\mathcal{O}_{+}}[0,l]
to be an even function of
m_{\rm th}
the rest of the terms in (
256
) should vanish.

We will see that indeed
[(-1)^{l}g_{k,l}(m_{\rm th},\mu)+g_{k,l}(m_{\rm th},-\mu)]
is zero for all values of
m_{\rm th}
and
\mu
,
based on the existence of the non-trivial mathematical identities as given below. Using the relation between Hurwitz zeta functions and Bernoulli polynomials
\zeta(1-n,x)=-\frac{B_{n}(x)}{n}
, the
[(-1)^{l}g_{k,l}(m_{\rm th},\mu)+g_{k,l}(m_{\rm th},-\mu)]

vanishes if the following identity is true

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{k+l-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}(k+l-n)_{2n}}{n!(2x)^{n}\Gamma(k+n+1)}\Big[B_{k+n}\Big(x+y+\frac{1}{2}\Big)+(-1)^{l}B_{k+n}\Big(x-y+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\Big]=0,

(258)

for
k,l=1,2,3,\ldots
. In the absence of chemical potential, which relates to
y=0
we must have
the following identity for
l
even

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{k+l-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}(k+l-n)_{2n}}{n!(2x)^{n}\Gamma(k+n+1)}B_{k+n}\Big(x+\frac{1}{2}\Big)=0.

(259)

These identities imply that
for
\mu=0
using (
259
) we obtain
g_{k,l}(m_{\rm th},\mu)=0
and for
\mu>0
using (
258
) one gets that
[(-1)^{l}g_{k,l}(m_{\rm th},\mu)+g_{k,l}(m_{\rm th},-\mu)]=0
. This implies the
a_{\mathcal{O}_{+}}[0,l]
is shown to be an even function of
m_{\rm th}
in the complex
m_{\rm th}
plane
with
-\pi<{\rm Im}(m_{\rm th})<\pi\ {\rm for}\ \mu\in R
given that the identities (
258
) and (
259
) hold true.
We have first verified in Mathematica that these identities are true by substituting
k=1,2,3,4
and various values of
l
.

We will now prove the identity (
259
), we have also proved the identity (
258
), but
the proof is more tedious and can be reproduced using the same approach.

Proof of identity (
259
)

B_{k+n}(x+\frac{1}{2})
can be expanded as a polynomial in powers of
x
using the following finite expansion of the
Bernoulli polynomial,

\displaystyle B_{n}\left(x+h\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}B_{k}\left(x\right)h^{n-k}.

(260)

Substituting the above identity in the LHS of (
259
) gives,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{k+l-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}(k+l-n)_{2n}}{n!(2)^{n}\Gamma(k+n+1)}\sum_{m=0}^{n+k}{n+k\choose m}B_{m}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)x^{k-m}.

(261)

We organise the expansion in powers of
x
by interchanging the order of
n
and
m
sums. Thus the expression (
261
) becomes,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{k}x^{k-m}B_{m}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)\sum_{n=0}^{k+l-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}(k+l-n)_{2n}}{2^{n}n!\Gamma(k+n+1)}{n+k\choose m}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=k+1}^{2k+l-1}x^{k-m}B_{m}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)\sum_{n=m-k}^{k+l-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}(k+l-n)_{2n}}{2^{n}n!\Gamma(k+n+1)}{n+k\choose m}.

(262)

If the equation (
259
) holds, the coefficients at each order in
x
in (
262
) must vanish, we will show that this indeed is true. For
m
being any odd integer
B_{m}(\frac{1}{2})
is zero, thus from now on we will restrict our attention for even integer values of
m
. The coefficients of
x^{k-m}
in the first
k+1
terms given in the first line of (
262
) can be identified as
a Gauss Hypergeometric function at special values of both its argument and the order.
This function further reduces to the associated Legendre polynomial at zero argument as shown below,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{k+l-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}(k+l-n)_{2n}}{2^{n}n!\Gamma(k+n+1)}{n+k\choose m}=\frac{P_{k+l-1}^{m-k}(0)}{m!}.

(263)

The associated Legendre polynomials obey the following parity relation

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}^{\beta}(x)=(-1)^{\alpha-\beta}P_{\alpha}^{\beta}(x).

(264)

Since both
l
and
m
are even integers it is easy to see that,

\displaystyle P_{k+l-1}^{m-k}(0)=0.

(265)

This implies the coefficients in the first
k+1
terms in (
262
) vanish.
Now the coefficients of
x^{k-m}
in the rest of terms given in the second line of (
262
) can also
be evaluated to be a Gauss Hypergeometric function with specific arguments, as given below,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=m-k}^{k+l-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}(k+l-n)_{2n}}{2^{n}n!\Gamma(k+n+1)}{n+k\choose m}=\frac{2^{k+l-1}(-1)^{m-k}\Gamma\left(\frac{l+m}{2}\right)}{m!\Gamma(2k+l-m)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(-2k-l+m+2)\right)}.

(266)

Now as both
m
and
l
are even integers and
m\leq 2k+l-2
, due to the last gamma function in the denominator of the above expression it vanishes.

Thus we have shown that the coefficient in each power of
x
in the expression (
262
) vanishes.
this concludes the proof of (
259
).

The same procedure can be repeated for the proof of the identity in (
258
), we need to use a similar expansion in
y
as
(
260
)
to write the Bernoulli polynomial in terms of a finite sum, and then perform the expansion in
x
.
The steps are tedious, therefore we refrain from presenting the proof here.

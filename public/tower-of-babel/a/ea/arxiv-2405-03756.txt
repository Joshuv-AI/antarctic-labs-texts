Differential spinors and Kundt three-manifolds with skew-torsion
C. S. Shahbazi

Abstract
. We develop the theory of spinorial polyforms associated with bundles of irreducible Clifford modules of non-simple real type, obtaining a precise characterization of the square of an irreducible real spinor in signature ( p − q ) ≡ 8 1 (p-q)\equiv_{8}1 as a polyform belonging to a semi-algebraic real set. We use this formalism to study differential spinors on Lorentzian three-manifolds, proving that in this dimension and signature, every differential spinor is equivalent to an isotropic line preserved in a given direction by a metric connection with prescribed torsion. We apply this result to investigate Lorentzian three-manifolds equipped with a skew-torsion parallel spinor, namely a spinor parallel with respect to a metric connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion. We obtain several structural results about this class of Lorentzian three-manifolds, which are necessarily Kundt, and in the compact case, we obtain an explicit differential condition that guarantees geodesic completeness. We further elaborate on these results to study the supersymmetric solutions of three-dimensional NS-NS supergravity, which involve skew-torsion parallel spinors whose torsion is given by the curvature of a curving on an abelian bundle gerbe. In particular, we obtain a correspondence between NS-NS supersymmetric solutions and null coframes satisfying an explicit exterior differential system that we solve locally.

Introduction

Background and context

The main purpose of this article is to introduce and explore the notion of differential spinor, which we consider here in signature
(p-q)\equiv_{8}1
, as a unifying notion naturally containing as particular cases the different types of special parallel spinors that have been considered in the literature, as well as some that are yet to appear or be discovered. This includes parallel spinors, Killing spinors, Codazzi spinors, Cauchy spinors, skew-torsion parallel spinors, skew-Killing spinors or generalized Killing spinors, just to name a few, see for instance

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented pseudo-Riemannian manifold and let

a bundle of Clifford modules over the bundle of Clifford algebras of
(M,g)
. Given a connection
\mathcal{D}
on
S
a differential spinor is a section
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
such that
\mathcal{D}\varepsilon=0
. In this generality, the notion of differential spinor is in principle too general to yield explicit results, mainly because the connection
\mathcal{D}
is abstractly defined and is not tied
a priori
to the geometry of
(M,g)
. In signatures
(p-q)\equiv_{8}0,1,2
however, the results of
[

imply that a bundle of irreducible Clifford modules exists if and only if
(M,g)
is spin, in which case there exists a spin structure
Q_{g}
on
(M,g)
such that

is associated to

via the standard
spinorial
representation of the spin group. Hence, in this case the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
lifts to
S
and equation
\mathcal{D}\varepsilon=0
reduces to:

(1)

\nabla^{g}\varepsilon=\mathcal{A}(\varepsilon)

where
\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathrm{End}(S))
is a uniquely determined one-form on

with values in the endomorphism bundle
\mathrm{End}(S)
. Equation (

parallelicity condition
that one can impose on an irreducible real spinor in signatures
(p-q)\equiv_{8}0,1,2
. To the best of our knowledge it does not seem to have been studied in this generality in the literature, especially not in Lorentzian signature.

Our main reason for introducing the notion of differential spinor is to develop a general spinorial geometric framework that can be applied to the study of the differential conditions satisfied by supersymmetric solutions and configurations in supergravity and string theory, see

The method that we develop here, and that originated in the early supergravity literature on supersymmetric solutions

Despite the fact that supersymmetric solutions are of the utmost importance in the theoretical physics community, which has devoted thousands of papers to their study, their mathematical theory, as well as the mathematical theory of supergravity, is yet to be established as a mathematical discipline, see

With this motivation in mind, and as explained in more detail below, we apply the general theory of spinorial polyforms to the systematic study of three-dimensional supersymmetric solutions in NS-NS supergravity

Supersymmetric solutions of NS-NS supergravity involve irreducible spinors parallel with respect to a metric connection with totally skew torsion given by the curvature of a curving on a bundle gerbe. Although the study of Riemannian metric connections with totally skew torsion is classical in the mathematics literature

Main results and outline

We summarize in the following bullet points the main results of the article and the contents of each of its sections.

•

In Section
2
we study the algebraic square of a real irreducible Clifford module in signature
(p-q)\equiv_{8}1
and in Theorem
2.8
we characterize it as a solution of a semi-algebraic system of equations in a truncated model of the Kähler-Atiyah algebra underlying the given quadratic vector space. This characterization lies at the heart of the spinorial polyform formalism, since it is the first step towards achieving a complete description of spinors in terms of polyforms. The main novelty of this section with respect to
[
21
]
, where similar results are obtained in signature
(p-q)\equiv_{8}0,2
, is that in the present case an irreducible Clifford module does not define an isomorphism of unital and associative real algebras. This leads to the use of the aforementioned
truncated model
as already proposed in
[
53
,
53
]
.

•

In Section
3
we introduce the notion of differential spinor and we develop the theory of spinorial polyforms associated to differential spinors in signature
(p-q)\equiv_{8}1
, obtaining a complete equivalence in Theorem
3.6
, which can now be applied to the study of every differential spinor in these signatures. In particular, we apply it to the three-dimensional Lorentzian case, proving in Theorem
3.9
that every differential spinor in this dimension and signature is equivalent to a trivialized isotropic line preserved by a metric connection with torsion given explicitly in terms of
\mathcal{A}
and relative to a direction also explicitly given in terms of the latter.

•

In Section
4
we apply the previous formalism to the study of a very specific type of differential spinor in three Lorentzian dimensions, namely spinors parallel with respect to a connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion, to which we refer as
skew-torsion parallel spinors
. We obtain a geometric characterization of this type of spinors via a class of adapted parallelisms that we call
null coframes
, showing that they are necessarily Kundt
[
11
,
52
]
, and we apply it to completely solve the problem locally. Equivalently, a Lorentzian three-manifold admits a skew-torsion spinor if and only if it admits an isotropic one-form
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
parallel under a metric connection with totally skew-torsion, possibly non-parallel. Although the case of parallel torsion has been recently studied in
[
24
,
25
]
, the non-parallel case seems to no have been considered before in the literature. Several natural open problems arise in this context, for instance the geodesic completeness and classification problems of compact Lorentzian three-manifolds equipped with a skew-torsion parallel spinor, or equivalently, an isotropic vector parallel with respect to a connection with skew-symmetric torsion. The methods and results of
[
48
]
seem to be particularly promising to study this case. In this direction, we prove we obtain a simple condition that guarantees that the Levi-Civita connection of
(M,g)
defines an affine structure on the leaves of the foliation canonically determined by the kernel of
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, encountering thus one of the cases considered in
[
48
]
. Furthermore, in the compact we case we obtain in Proposition
4.20
an explicit differential condition which, if it does not admit a solution, guarantees the geodesic completeness of the underlying Lorentzian three-manifold.

•

In Section
5
we consider the particular type of skew-torsion spinors that occurs in the study of three-dimensional NS-NS supersymmetric solutions. These are Lorentzian three-manifolds that satisfy a natural curvature condition, namely the Einstein equation of NS-NS supergravity, and are in addition equipped with a skew-torsion spinor relative to a metric connection with torsion given by the curvature of a bundle gerbe. This section exemplifies the main motivation behind our interest on differential spinors and the theory of spinorial polyforms: the study of supersymmetric solutions in supergravity, in this case NS-NS supergravity. Whereas NS-NS supergravity is formulated on a ten-dimensional manifold, we consider it here on a three-dimensional manifold as a first step towards the mathematical understanding of the differential geometry, topology and moduli of supersymmetric solutions in this ten-dimensional supergravity theory. Our main result in this section is Theorem
5.15
, which characterizes globally every three-dimensional supersymmetric solution in terms of an explicit differential system that involves a cohomological condition and for which the dilaton decouples. We apply this theorem to easily obtain the most general NS-NS supersymmetric solution in local adapted coordinates. The study of the Cauchy problem of these supersymmetric solutions, which is work in progress and will be consider elsewhere, provides a natural framework to generalize the results of
[
3
,
46
,
77
,
78
,
79
]
on initial data sets for spinors on globally hyperbolic Lorentzian manifolds.

Proposition 2.4
.

We have
\mathcal{S}_{l}(\alpha)=2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\alpha^{(0)}
for every
\alpha\in\wedge^{<}V^{\ast}

Proof.

Let
\left\{e^{i}\right\}_{i=1,\ldots,d}
be an orthonormal basis of
(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
. For

we have
e^{i}\vee_{h}e^{j}=-e^{j}\vee_{h}e^{i}
and hence
(e^{i})^{-1}\vee_{h}e^{j}\vee_{h}e^{i}=-e^{j}
. Let
1\leq k\leq(d-1)/2
and
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq d
. If
k
is even, then:

\mathcal{S}_{l}(e^{i_{1}}\vee_{h}\cdots\vee_{h}e^{i_{k}})=\mathcal{S}_{l}(e^{i_{k}}\vee_{h}e^{i_{1}}\vee_{h}\cdots\vee_{h}e^{i_{k-1}})=-\mathcal{S}_{l}(e^{i_{1}}\vee_{h}\cdots\vee_{h}e^{i_{k}})

and hence
\mathcal{S}(e^{i_{1}}\vee_{h}\cdots\vee_{h}e^{i_{k}})=0
. Here we have used cyclicity of the Kähler-Atiyah trace and the fact that
e^{i_{k}}
anticommutes with
e^{i_{1}}\vee_{h}\cdots\vee_{h}e^{i_{k-1}}
. If
k
is odd, let
j\in\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}
be such that
j\not\in\left\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\right\}
, which exists since

. We have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l}(e^{i_{1}}\vee_{h}\cdots\vee_{h}e^{i_{k}})=-\mathcal{S}((e^{j})^{-1}\vee_{h}e^{i_{1}}\vee_{h}\cdots\vee_{h}e^{i_{k}}\vee_{h}e^{j})

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{S}_{l}(e^{i_{1}}\vee_{h}\cdots\vee_{h}e^{i_{k}})=0

and hence we conclude.
∎

Definition 2.7

Let

We will say that a polyform

or

\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
on the source

(p-q)\equiv_{8}1

Theorem 2.8

Let

\sigma

E\circ E=\mathrm{Tr}(E)E\,,\qquad E^{t}=sE\,.

Signature

3.
Differential spinors in signature
(p-q)\equiv_{8}1

To define and study differential spinors on pseudo-Riemannian manifolds of signature
(p-q)\equiv_{8}1
, we
globalize
the algebraic theory of spinors and polyforms laid out in Section
2
to bundles of real irreducible Clifford modules equipped with an arbitrary connection.

3.1.
General theory

Let
(M,g)
denote a connected and oriented pseudo-Riemannian manifold of signature
(p,q)
and odd dimension
d=p+q
, where
p-q\equiv_{8}1
. Since
M
is connected, the pseudo-Euclidean vector bundle
(TM,g)
is modeled on a fixed quadratic vector space that we denote by
(V,h)
. For any point
m\in M
, we thus have an isomorphism of quadratic spaces
(T_{m}M,g_{m})\simeq(V,h)
. Accordingly, the cotangent bundle
T^{\ast}M
endowed with the dual metric
g^{\ast}
is modeled on the dual quadratic space
(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
. We denote by
\mathrm{Cl}(M,g)
the bundle of real Clifford algebras of the
cotangent
bundle
(T^{\ast}M,g^{\ast})
, which is modeled on the real Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
. We use the notation introduced in Section
2
to denote the corresponding objects naturally extended on
M
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
bundle of real Clifford modules
on
(M,g)
is a pair
(S,\Gamma)
, where
S
is a real vector bundle on
M
and

\Gamma:\mathrm{Cl}(M,g)\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(S)
is a unital morphism of bundles of unital and associative real algebras.

Given a bundle of Clifford modules
(S,\Gamma)
, for every point

the unital morphism of associative algebras
\Gamma_{m}:\mathrm{Cl}(T_{m}^{\ast}M,g_{m}^{\ast})\to\mathrm{End}(S_{m})
defines a Clifford module that we denote by
(S_{m},\Gamma_{m})
. Since
M
is connected, it follows that there exists a Clifford module
(\Sigma,\gamma)
, unique modulo isomorphism, such that that
(S_{m},\Gamma_{m})
is isomorphic to
(\Sigma,\gamma)
via an
unbased
isomorphism of Clifford modules
[
61
]
. In this situation we say that
(S,\Gamma)
is a bundle of real Clifford modules of
type

(\Sigma,\gamma)
.

Definition 3.2
.

An
paired spinor bundle
of type
(\Sigma,\gamma)
is a triple
(S,\Gamma,\Scr B)
consisting of a bundle of real Clifford modules
(S,\Gamma)
of type
(\Sigma,\gamma)
equipped with an admissible bilinear pairing
\Scr B
on
S
of symmetry type
s
and adjoint type
\sigma
. An
irreducible paired spinor bundle
is a paired spinor bundle of irreducible type
(\Sigma,\gamma)
. In the latter case, a global section
\epsilon\in\Gamma(S)
is an
irreducible spinor
on
(M,g)
.

In the signature
p-q\equiv_{8}1
considered in this note, an irreducible spinor bundle has rank
2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
, where
d
is the dimension of
M
. References
[
61
,
60
]
prove that
(M,g)
admits an irreducible real spinor bundle if and only if it admits a
real Lipschitz structure
of irreducible type. As mentioned in the introduction, in signature
p-q\equiv_{8}1
, a real Lipschitz structure of irreducible type corresponds to a standard spin structure
Q_{g}
such that (
S,\Gamma)
is the standard spinor bundle associated to
Q_{g}
via the tautological representation of the spin group
[
61
]
.

Remark 3.3
.

Admissible bilinear pairings on an irreducible spinor bundle may be obstructed. They are however guaranteed to exist if
(M,g)
is
strongly spin
, that is, if every spin structure on
(M,g)
admits a reduction to the identity component of the spin group in the given signature.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(S,\Gamma,\Scr B)
be a paired spinor bundle on
(M,g)
and let

be a connection on

\mathcal{D}\varepsilon=0

Such

relative to
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{Q})

\mathcal{Q}(\varepsilon)=0

\mathcal{Q}\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}(S))

.

Remark 3.5
.

Supersymmetric solutions of supergravity theories are generally characterized as manifolds admitting certain systems of constrained differential spinors, see for instance
[

. This extends the notion of generalized

Let

Once Theorem

3.2.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented and time-oriented Lorentzian three-manifold. Denote by
\nabla^{g}
the Levi-Civita connection on
(M,g)

\nabla

(M,g)

\nabla_{w_{1}}w_{2}=\nabla^{g}_{w_{1}}w_{2}+A^{g}(w_{1},w_{2})\,,\qquad w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

A^{g}(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})+A^{g}(w_{1},w_{3},w_{2})=0

Hence
A^{g}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes\wedge^{2}T^{\ast}M)

as the

of the connection
\nabla
on
(M,g)

(M,g)
identifies with all sections
\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes\wedge^{2}M)
of the bundle
T^{\ast}M\otimes\wedge^{2}M
.

Every paired spinor bundle
(S,\Gamma,\Scr B)
on
(M,g)
is of rank two and equipped with a skew-symmetric non-degenerate bilinear pairing of negative adjoint type. Given
(S,\Gamma,\Scr B)
and
\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathrm{End}(S))
, we denote by
\mathfrak{a}\in\Omega^{1}(M,\wedge^{<}M)
the dequantization of the latter, which we expand as follows:

(14)

\mathfrak{a}=\frac{1}{2}(\theta+\chi)

where

and

on the first entry of

relative to which differential spinors can exist on
(M,g)
.

Theorem 3.9
.

A spin Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g)

(S,\Gamma,\Scr B)
and an endomorphism-valued one-form
\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathrm{End}(S))
with dequantization
\mathfrak{a}\in\Omega^{1}(M,\wedge^{<}M)
if and only if it admits a isotropic vector field
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
satisfying:

\mathcal{D}^{g,\chi}u=\theta\otimes u

where
\mathcal{D}^{g,\chi}
is the unique metric connection on
(M,g)
with contorsion tensor given by:

A^{g}_{w_{1}}(w_{2})=l\ast_{g}(\chi_{w_{1}}\wedge w_{2})

where
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

Remark 3.10
.

The one-form
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, or its metric dual
u^{\sharp_{g}}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, is usually referred to as the
Dirac current
of
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.6

(M,g)
admits a differential spinor if and only if Equations (
11
) and (
12
) (equivalently, Equation (
13
)) are satisfied. Equation (
12
) is satisfied if and only if
\alpha=u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
for an isotropic one-form on
(M,g)
. Equation (
11
) then becomes:

\nabla^{g}_{w}u=\mathfrak{a}_{w}\vee_{g}u+u\vee_{g}(\pi\circ\tau)(\mathfrak{a}_{w})=\frac{1}{2}(\theta_{w}+\chi_{w})\vee_{g}u+\frac{1}{2}u\vee_{g}(\theta_{w}-\chi_{w})=\theta_{w}u+\frac{1}{2}(\chi_{w}\vee_{g}u-u\vee_{g}\chi_{w})

We further compute:

\chi_{w}\vee_{g}u=l\ast_{g}(u\wedge\chi_{w})+g(\chi_{w},u)

and thus:

\frac{1}{2}(\chi_{w}\vee_{g}u-u\vee_{g}\chi_{w})=l\ast_{g}(u\wedge\chi_{w})

from which the result follows.
∎

In other words, a differential spinor on
(M,g)
defines a bundle of isotropic lines trivialized by
u
and preserved by
\mathcal{D}^{g,\chi}
in the
direction
prescribed by
\theta
. Hence, every differential spinor defines an
optical structure
with torsion that can be studied within the general framework developed in
[
31
]
. By Lemma
2.5
it immediately follows that
\mathcal{A}
is skew, that is, the connection
(\mathcal{D}-\mathcal{A})
on
S
preserves
\Scr B
, if and only if
\theta=0
in the decomposition (
14
), namely, if and only if the Dirac current
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
is actually parallel with respect to
\mathcal{D}^{g,\chi}
. In this case, the optical structure determined by
\varepsilon
reduces to a Bargmannian structure with torsion as described in
[
30
]
.

Remark 3.11
.

If a differential spinor is constrained to be in the kernel an endomorphism
\mathcal{Q}\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}(S))
with fixed dequantization
\mathfrak{q}\in\Omega^{1}(\wedge^{<}M)
then we simply need to add to the statement of the previous theorem the condition
\mathfrak{q}\vee_{g}u=0
, where
u
is the Dirac current associated to the given differential spinor.

Alternatively, we can rephrase the previous theorem in the following form, which is sometimes more convenient for computations.

Corollary 3.12
.

A strongly spin Lorentzian Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g)
admits a differential spinor if and only if there exists a isotropic one-form
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
satisfying:

\nabla^{g}_{w}u=\theta_{w}u+\ast_{g}(u\wedge\chi_{w})\,,\qquad\forall\,\,w\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

for a one-form
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and a tensor
\chi\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes T^{\ast}M)
.

Note that, without loss of generality, in the previous corollary we have absorbed the sign
l
into
\chi
. Theorem
3.9
characterizes all possible differential spinors on an oriented and spin Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g)
through an adequate choices of
\theta
and
\chi
, and hence recovers well-known types of special spinors on
(M,g)
as particular cases. More precisely:

•

If
\theta=0
and
\chi=0
both vanish identically then
\varepsilon
is a parallel spinor and we conclude that
(M,g)
admits a parallel irreducible real spinor field if and only if it admits a isotropic vector field parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection. Note that this does not longer hold in higher dimensions
[
77
,
78
]
.

•

If
\theta=0
and
\chi=\lambda g
for a non-zero real constant
\lambda
then
\varepsilon
is a standard Killing spinor and we conclude that
(M,g)
admits an irreducible real Killing spinor if and only if it admits a isotropic vector field
u\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
satisfying:

(15)

\nabla^{g}u=\lambda\,\ast_{g}u

The sign factor
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
has been absorbed in
\lambda
without further consequence.

•

If
\theta=0
and
\chi\in\mathrm{End}(TM)
is an endomorphism of
TM
symmetric with respect to
g
then
\varepsilon
is formally an irreducible Lorentzian generalized Killing spinor, namely a type of Lorentzian analog of the notion of generalized Killing spinor introduced in
[
72
,
73
,
74
]
for Riemannian manifolds. In particular,
(M,g)
admits a Lorentzian generalized Killing spinor if and only if it admits a isotropic vector field satisfying:

\nabla^{g}_{w}u=\ast_{g}(u\wedge\chi_{w})\,,\qquad\forall\,\,w\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

However, this is not, strictly speaking, a generalized Killing spinor as it was originally introduced
[
5
,
34
,
36
]
, namely it is not the restriction of an irreducible parallel spinor to a hypersurface. This is because a real irreducible spinor in four Lorentzian dimensions does not descend to an irreducible spinor in three Lorentzian dimensions. Hence, a generalized Killing spinor in three Lorentzian dimensions would correspond a
faithful
real spinor satisfying differential equation of the type above. To the best of our knowledge, this type of generalized Killing spinors, which cannot occur in Riemannian signature, have not been studied in the literature.

•

If
\theta=0
and
\chi\in\Omega^{2}(M)
is a two-form, then
\varepsilon
is a
skew-torsion parallel spinor
, namely a spinor parallel under a connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion
H\in\Omega^{3}(M)
given by:

\displaystyle H(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})=-2(\ast_{g}\chi_{w_{1}})(w_{2},w_{3})\,,\qquad w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

In particular,
(M,g)
admits a skew-torsion parallel spinor with respect to the spinorial lift of a metric connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion
\nabla^{g,H}
if and only if it admits a isotropic vector field parallel under the very same connection
\nabla^{g,H}
. We will further explore this case in Section

, since it is the main object of study in this note.

•

If
\chi=0
then the corresponding
\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathrm{End}(S))
takes values in the line spanned by the identity isomorphism, that is,
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
satisfies:

\nabla^{g}\varepsilon=\theta\otimes\varepsilon

for a one-form
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Hence, Theorem

implies in this case that
(M,g)
admits such a differential spinor if and only if:

\nabla^{g}u=\theta\otimes u

that is, if and only if
(M,g)
admits a recurrent isotropic line, namely a bundle of isotropic lines preserved by the Levi-Civita connection of
(M,g)
. Manifolds equipped with such a recurrent isotropic line, called

in
[
12
]
and
weakly abelian
in
[
69
]
, have been already considered in the literature, see for instance
[
14
,
39
,
37
]
and their references and citations. Interestingly enough, general differential spinors are characterized similarly in Theorem
3.9
in terms of a bundle of isotropic lines preserved by
\mathcal{D}^{g,\chi}
instead of the Levi-Civita connection.

Remark 3.13
.

For dimensional reasons a real Killing spinor in three Lorentzian dimensions is a particular case of a skew-torsion parallel spinor. Indeed, using the identity
\ast_{g}u=u\lrcorner_{g}\nu_{g}
it follows that Equation (
15
) is equivalent to:

\nabla^{g}_{w}u+\lambda\,u\lrcorner_{g}w\lrcorner_{g}\nu_{g}=0

Hence, the Dirac current
u
associated to a Killing spinor is parallel with respect to the unique metric connection on
(M,g)
with completely skew torsion given by
2\lambda\nu_{g}\in\Omega^{3}(M)
, where
\nu_{g}
is the Lorentzian volume form on
(M,g)
. In contrast, a general skew-torsion parallel spinor is parallel with respect to the spinorial lift of a metric connection with torsion
\mathfrak{f}\nu_{g}\in\Omega^{3}(M)
for a possibly not constant function
\mathfrak{f}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. As we will see in the following, the theory of skew-torsion parallel spinors with non-parallel torsion is markedly more complicated than the parallel torsion case.

Given
u\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
we choose a conjugate isotropic vector field
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, which by definition satisfies
g(u,v)=1
. We call such a pair
(u,v)
a
conjugate pair
. Given a conjugate pair
(u,v)
, we obtain a canonical, unit norm, space-like vector field:

\displaystyle n=-\ast_{g}(u\wedge v)

making the triple
(u,v,n)
positively oriented. We will refer to such a triplet
(u,v,n)
as a
null coframe
of
M
. The line spanned by
n
in
T^{\ast}M
is the
screen distribution
determined by
(u,v)
, which in this case reduces to a rank-one distribution and is therefore always integrable. For further reference we note the following identities, which are useful in computations:

(16)

\ast_{g}u=-u\wedge n\,,\qquad\ast_{g}v=v\wedge n\,,\qquad\ast_{g}n=u\wedge v\,,\qquad\ast_{g}\nu_{g}=-1\,.

Isotropic vector fields conjugate to a fixed isotropic vector field
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
are unique modulo transformations of the form:

v\mapsto v-\frac{F^{2}}{2}u+fn

for a function
F\in C^{\infty}(M)
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{u}
the set of null coframes associated to a given isotropic one-form
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{u}
is a torsor over
C^{\infty}(M)
with the following action:

F\cdot(u,v,n)=(u,v-\frac{F^{2}}{2}u+Fn,n-Fu)

where
F\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
. Note that for every pair of null coframes
(u,v,n),(u,v^{\prime},n^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
we have:

g=u\odot v+n\otimes n=u\odot v^{\prime}+n^{\prime}\otimes n^{\prime}\,.

We will make extensive use of null coframes in the following sections.

4.
Skew-Torsion parallel spinors

In this section we focus on Lorentzian three-manifolds equipped with a particular class of differential spinors that we call
skew-torsion parallel spinors
. These are irreducible real spinors parallel under the lift to the spinor bundle of a metric connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion.

4.1.
General characterization

In this subsection we use Theorem
3.9
to describe skew-torsion parallel spinors in terms of an equivalent differential system for a null coframe associated to the corresponding Dirac current.

Proposition 4.1
.

A three-dimensional strongly spin Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
admits a skew-torsion parallel spinor if and only if it admits an isotropic one-form
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
satisfying:

(17)

\nabla^{g}u=\mathfrak{f}\ast_{g}u

for a function
\mathfrak{f}\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Remark 4.2
.

Equivalently,
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
is parallel under the unique metric connection on
(M,g)
with totally skew-torsion
2\,\mathfrak{f}\,\nu_{g}\in\Omega^{3}(M)
. In particular, it immediately follows that if
(M,g)
is a Lorentzian three-manifold equipped with a skew-torsion parallel spinor with associated Dirac current
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, then
(M,g,u)
is a Kundt three-dimensional space-time
[
11
,
52
]
. Hence, this defines a particular class of Kundt three-manifolds that, to the best of our knowledge, has not been studied in the literature and certainly deserves more attention.

Proof.

The result follows directly from Theorem
3.9
, but for completeness we prove it directly by means of Theorem
3.4
. Let
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
be a spinor parallel under the lift to the spinor bundle of a metric connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion. Then,
\varepsilon
satisfies:

\nabla^{g}_{w}\varepsilon+\frac{1}{4}\Gamma\circ\Psi^{-1}(H_{w})(\varepsilon)=0\,,\qquad w\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

where
H\in\Omega^{3}(M)
is a three-form. Hence,
\varepsilon
is a differential spinor relative to an endomorphism-valued one-form
\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathrm{End}(S))
given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{w}=-\frac{1}{4}\Gamma\circ\Psi^{-1}(H_{w})\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}(S))\,,\qquad w\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

Therefore:

\mathfrak{a}_{w}=(\Psi_{\Gamma}^{<})^{-1}(\mathcal{A}_{w})=-\frac{1}{8}(\mathcal{P}_{l}|_{\wedge_{l}M})^{-1}(H_{w}+l\nu_{g}\diamond_{g}H_{w})=-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{P}_{<}(H_{w}-l\ast_{g}H_{w})=\frac{l}{4}\ast_{g}H_{w}

which, by Theorem
3.9
, implies:

\nabla^{g}_{w}u=\frac{l}{4}(\ast_{g}H_{w}\vee_{g}u-u\vee_{g}\ast_{g}H_{w})=\frac{1}{2}H(w,u)

Equivalently,
u
is parallel with respect to the unique metric connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion given by
-H\in\Omega^{3}(M)
. Setting
H=-2\,\mathfrak{f}\,\nu_{g}
in terms of a uniquely determined function
\mathfrak{f}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we have:

H(w,u)=-2\,\mathfrak{f}\,u\lrcorner_{g}w\lrcorner_{g}\nu_{g}=2\,\mathfrak{f}\,w\lrcorner_{g}\ast_{g}u

and hence we conclude.
∎

By the previous result, it immediately follows that the Dirac current associated to a skew-torsion parallel spinor is Killing and its orthogonal distribution is integrable and defines a codimension one smooth foliation by null hypersurfaces. Furthermore, we have
\nabla^{g}_{u}u=0
whence the integral curves of
u
define a non-expanding, non-twisting and non-shear null geodesic congruence. Therefore, a Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g)
equipped with a torsion parallel spinor defines an idealized gravitational wave that belongs to the Kundt class of space-times. Projecting Equation (
17
) to its symmetric and skew-symmetric components we immediately obtains the following corollary.

Corollary 4.3
.

A three-dimensional strongly spin Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
admits a skew-torsion parallel spinor if and only if it admits an isotropic Killing vector field
u^{\sharp_{g}}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
whose dual one-form
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
satisfies:

(18)

\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}u=\mathfrak{f}\ast_{g}u

for a function
\mathfrak{f}\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

For further reference we introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.4
.

A skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold is a tuple
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
consisting of a strongly spin Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g)
equipped with an isotropic one-form
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
satisfying Equation (
17
) with respect to the function
\mathfrak{f}\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

By Proposition
4.1
, every skew-torsion Lorentzian carries a skew-torsion parallel spinor, which is canonical modulo a global sign.

Lemma 4.5
.

The covariant derivative of a null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
on a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
is given by:

(19)

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}u=\mathfrak{f}\ast_{g}u=\mathfrak{f}\,n\wedge u\,,\qquad\nabla^{g}v=\kappa\otimes n-\mathfrak{f}\,n\otimes v\,,\qquad\nabla^{g}n=\mathfrak{f}\,u\otimes v-\kappa\otimes u

for a one-form
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

Proof.

A direct calculation shows that
\ast_{g}u=n\wedge u
and therefore the first equation in (
19
) follows from Proposition
4.1
. For the remaining equations, we compute by taking the covariant derivative of (
16
) with respect to any
w\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
:

\displaystyle\ast_{g}\nabla_{w}^{g}u=-\nabla_{w}^{g}u\wedge n-u\wedge\nabla_{w}^{g}n\,,\qquad\ast_{g}\nabla_{w}^{g}v=\nabla_{w}^{g}v\wedge n+v\wedge\nabla_{w}^{g}n

(20)

\displaystyle\ast_{g}\nabla_{w}^{g}n=\nabla_{w}^{g}u\wedge v+u\wedge\nabla_{w}^{g}v

Plugging
\nabla^{g}_{w}u=\mathfrak{f}\,w\lrcorner\ast_{g}u=\mathfrak{f}\ast_{g}(u\wedge w)=\mathfrak{f}\,n(w)u-\mathfrak{f}\,u(w)n
into the first equation above yields:

\mathfrak{f}\,u\wedge w=u\wedge(\mathfrak{f}\,n(w)n+\nabla^{g}_{w}n)

and thus:

\nabla^{g}n=\mathfrak{f}\,u\otimes v+(\kappa_{o}+\mathfrak{f}\,v)\otimes u

for a one-form
\kappa_{o}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Plugging this equation together with the covariant derivative of
u
into the second line of (
20
), we obtain:

\nabla^{g}v=-(\kappa_{o}+\mathfrak{f}\,v)\otimes n-\mathfrak{f}\,n\otimes v

Setting
\kappa:=-(\kappa_{o}+\mathfrak{f}\,v)
we conclude.
∎

Let
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
be a null coframe satisfying the differential system (
19
) with respect to a one-form
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Then, any other null coframe
(u,v^{\prime},n^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
satisfies again the (
19
) with respect to the one-form:

(21)

\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa+\mathrm{d}F+\mathfrak{f}\,Fn-\frac{\mathfrak{f}F^{2}}{2}u

and hence, the choice of null coframe in
\mathcal{P}_{u}
is a matter of taste or convenience.

Proposition 4.6
.

A null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
satisfies equations (
19
) with respect to
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
if and only if:

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}u=\mathfrak{f}\ast_{g}u=\mathfrak{f}\,n\wedge u\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}v=(\mathfrak{f}v+\kappa)\wedge n\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}n=u\wedge(\mathfrak{f}v+\kappa)

for the same one-form
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

Proof.

The skew-symmetrization of equations (
19
) yields equations (
22
) for the same one-form
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. On the other hand, the symmetrization of equations (
19
) is equivalent to:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{u^{\sharp_{g}}}g=0\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{v^{\sharp_{g}}}g=\kappa\odot n-\mathfrak{f}\,n\odot v\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{n^{\sharp_{g}}}g=\mathfrak{f}\,u\odot v-\kappa\odot u

Assuming equation (
22
), this equations are automatically satisfied and hence we conclude.
∎

By the first equation in (
22
), the distribution
u^{\perp_{g}}\subset TM
determined by the kernel of
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
in
TM
is integrable and defines a foliation
\mathcal{X}_{u}
of
M
whose leaves are immersed two-dimensional submanifolds. We will refer to
\mathcal{X}_{u}
as the
canonical foliation
of the given skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
. By the first equation in (
22
), the Godbillon-Vey class of
\mathcal{X}_{u}
is given explicitly by:

\sigma_{u}=4[\mathfrak{f}n\wedge\mathrm{d}(\mathfrak{f}n)]=4[\mathfrak{f}^{2}n\wedge u\wedge(\mathfrak{f}v+\kappa)]\in H^{3}(M,\mathbb{R})

in terms of any null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
. We will refer to
\sigma_{u}\in H^{3}(M,\mathbb{R})
as the
Godbillon-Vey class
of the given skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
. Taking the exterior derivative of equations (
22
) we immediately the following exterior differential system:

\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}\wedge u\wedge n=0\,,\qquad n\wedge(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}\wedge v+\mathrm{d}\kappa)=0\,,\qquad(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)+\mathfrak{f}^{2}+\kappa(u)\mathfrak{f})\,u\wedge v\wedge n=u\wedge\mathrm{d}\kappa

to which we will refer as the
integrability conditions
of the null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}\in u
be a null coframe satisfying equations (
19
) with respect to
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Then:

\mathrm{d}\kappa=\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(v)v\wedge u+(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)+\mathfrak{f}^{2}+\kappa(u)\mathfrak{f})v\wedge n+\mathfrak{l}\,u\wedge n

in terms of a function
\mathfrak{l}\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Proof.

We expand:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\kappa=\mathrm{d}\kappa(u,v)v\wedge u+\mathrm{d}\kappa(u,n)v\wedge n+\mathrm{d}\kappa(v,n)u\wedge n

in terms of the given null coframe
(u,v,n)
. Substituting this expression into the integrability equations and isolating for the coefficients of the expansion we obtain the expression in the statement of the proposition.
∎

We end this subsection summarizing the equivalent descriptions of skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifolds contained in propositions
4.1
and
4.6
.

Corollary 4.8
.

A three-dimensional strongly spin Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
admits a skew-torsion parallel spinor if and only if it admits a null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
satisfying equations (
19
), or, equivalently, equations (
22
), for some one-form
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

4.2.
Curvature

The curvature tensor of a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g)
is highly constrained. We compute in the following both the Riemmann and Ricci curvatures of
g
, which in three-dimensions are equivalent curvature tensors. Denote by
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{g}}\colon\Omega^{1}(M,\wedge^{1}M)\to\Omega^{2}(M,\wedge^{1}M)
the exterior covariant derivative associated to the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
. Using equations (
19
) together with the integrability conditions of the given null coframe
(u,v,n)
, we compute:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{g}}\nabla^{g}u=\mathfrak{f}^{2}u\wedge v\otimes u+u\wedge n\otimes(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}+\mathfrak{f}^{2}n)=(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}\wedge n+\mathfrak{f}^{2}u\wedge v)\otimes u-(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}\wedge u+\mathfrak{f}^{2}n\wedge u)\otimes n

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{g}}\nabla^{g}v=(\mathrm{d}\kappa+\mathfrak{f}\,n\wedge\kappa)\otimes n-(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}\wedge n+\mathfrak{f}^{2}u\wedge v)\otimes v

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{g}}\nabla^{g}n=(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}\wedge u+\mathfrak{f}^{2}n\wedge u)\otimes v-(\mathrm{d}\kappa+\mathfrak{f}\,n\wedge\kappa)\otimes u

These expressions immediately imply that the Riemann tensor
{\mathcal{R}}^{g}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}M\otimes\wedge^{2}M)
of the Lorentzian metric
g=u\odot v+n\otimes n
is given by:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{g}=(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}\wedge n+\mathfrak{f}^{2}u\wedge v)\otimes v\wedge u-(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}\wedge u+\mathfrak{f}^{2}n\wedge u)\otimes v\wedge n+(\mathrm{d}\kappa+\mathfrak{f}\,n\wedge\kappa)\otimes u\wedge n

Written in this form, the celebrated symmetries of the Riemann tensor are not apparent. Using the integrability conditions of the given null coframe,
{\mathcal{R}}^{g}
can be equivalently written as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{g}=\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(v)(u\wedge n\otimes v\wedge u+v\wedge u\otimes u\wedge n)+\mathfrak{f}^{2}u\wedge v\otimes v\wedge u

\displaystyle-(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)+\mathfrak{f}^{2})(n\wedge u\otimes v\wedge n+v\wedge n\otimes n\wedge u)+(\mathfrak{f}\,\kappa(v)-\mathfrak{l})n\wedge u\otimes u\wedge n

from which the Ricci tensor of
g=u\odot v+n\otimes n
can be computed to be:

(23)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{g}=-\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(v)u\odot n-(2\mathfrak{f}^{2}+\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n))g-\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)\,n\otimes n+(\mathfrak{f}\,\kappa(v)-\mathfrak{l})u\otimes u

From this formula we immediately obtain that the scalar curvature of a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold is given by:

\displaystyle\mathrm{s}^{g}=-(6\mathfrak{f}^{2}+4\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n))

These explicit expressions for the Ricci and scalar curvatures of a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold imply the following corollary.

Corollary 4.9
.

A skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold is Einstein if and only if and only if
\mathfrak{f}
is constant and
\mathfrak{f}\,\kappa(v)=\mathfrak{l}
.

The fact that
(M,g)
is Einstein only if the torsion is parallel, points out to the Einstein condition not being natural for general skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifolds. In Section
5
we will encounter a curvature condition naturally compatible with skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifolds in the context of supersymmetric solutions of supergravity.

Example 4.10
.

The Godbillon-Vey class of a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold is only relevant if
M
is compact, since otherwise
H^{3}(M,\mathbb{R})=\left\{0\right\}
. Setting
\kappa=\mathfrak{f}v
, the differential system (
22
) reduces to:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}u=\mathfrak{f}\,n\wedge u\,,\qquad\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}v=\mathfrak{f}\,v\wedge n\,,\qquad\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}n=\mathfrak{f}\,u\wedge v\,.

Assuming now that
\mathfrak{f}
is in addition a non-zero constant, the first line in the previous differential system is satisfied by every right invariant, appropriately normalized, coframe on
\mathrm{Sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Hence, every right-invariant coframe on
\mathrm{Sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
can be normalized as to define a null coframe
(u,v,n)
associated to skew-torsion parallel spinor on
\mathrm{Sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
with respect to the metric
g=u\odot v+n\otimes n
. In particular,
\mathrm{Sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
, as well as its many discrete quotients, admit plenty of skew-torsion parallel spinors with respect to left-invariant Lorentzian metrics. The corresponding Godbillon-Vey invariant is given by:

\displaystyle\sigma_{u}=8\mathfrak{f}^{3}[u\wedge v\wedge n]\in H^{3}(M,\mathbb{R})

and hence by appropriately choosing the constant
\mathfrak{f}
we can realize every possible Godbillon-Vey invariant.

Remark 4.11
.

Going beyond the Godbillon-Vey class, there is a class of
secondary invariants
associated to codimension-one foliations, such as the invariant introduced by Losik
[
66
,
67
,
7
]
. It would be interesting to study these secondary invariants for the codimension-one foliations ocurring in skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifolds.

4.3.
Local adapted coordinates

In the following we solve the differential system (

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
be a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold. Then, there exist local coordinates
(x_{u},x_{v},z)
in which the metric

\displaystyle g=\mathcal{H}\,\mathrm{d}x_{u}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{u}+e^{\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{d}x_{u}\odot\mathrm{d}x_{v}+\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z

in terms of local functions
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
depending only on the coordinates
x_{u}
and
z

Proof.

Choose local coordinates
(x^{\prime}_{v},y_{1},y_{2})

where the coefficients depend only on
(y_{1},y_{2})

\mathcal{F}
and
\kappa

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{d}\kappa=g_{1x^{\prime}_{v}}\mathrm{d}y_{1}+g_{2x^{\prime}_{v}}\mathrm{d}y_{2}

Since

, we can assume, perhaps after relabelling the coordinates, that
g_{1x^{\prime}_{v}}\neq 0
locally around
m\in M
. Hence,
(x^{\prime}_{v},\kappa,y_{2})
are local functions around
m\in M
with linearly independent differentials and thus define a local coordinate system around
m\in M
. Setting

the metric is locally given by:

\displaystyle g=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x_{u}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{u}+e^{\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{d}x_{u}\odot\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}_{v}+F\,\mathrm{d}x_{u}\odot\mathrm{d}y_{2}+e^{2\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{d}y_{2}\otimes\mathrm{d}y_{2}

for a local functions
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{K}
and

depending only on
x_{u}
and
y_{2}
. Defining now the coordinate:

we obtain:

\displaystyle g=\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x_{u}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{u}+e^{\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{d}x_{u}\odot\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}_{v}+F^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x_{u}\odot\mathrm{d}z+\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z

x_{u}
and

\displaystyle x_{v}:=x^{\prime}_{v}+\int e^{-\mathcal{F}}F^{\prime}\mathrm{d}z

crossed term

u^{\sharp_{g}}=\partial_{x^{\prime}_{v}}=\partial_{x_{v}}
.
∎

Since the isotropic vector field

in local adapted coordinates is given by

, we have

whence

(24)

(u=e^{\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{d}x_{u},v=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}e^{-\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{d}x_{u}+\mathrm{d}x_{v},n=e^{\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{d}z)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}

is a local null coframe associated to

. In particular, we have:

n^{\sharp_{g}}=e^{-\mathcal{K}}\partial_{z}

for the metric dual of

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
be a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold. Then, the local null coframe (
24

(25)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\mathcal{F}=2\mathfrak{f}\,,\qquad\partial_{z}\mathcal{H}-\mathfrak{f}\,\mathcal{H}+2\kappa_{u}e^{\mathcal{F}}=0\,,\qquad\kappa_{z}=0

Proof.

(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
is a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold if and only if the null coframe (

∎

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
be a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold. Around every point

there exist local coordinates
(x_{u},x_{v},z)
in which the metric

is given by:

\displaystyle g=\mathcal{H}\,\mathrm{d}x_{u}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{u}+e^{2\int\mathfrak{f}\,\mathrm{d}z+c(x_{u})}\mathrm{d}x_{u}\odot\mathrm{d}x_{v}+\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z

for a function
\mathcal{H}
of
x_{u}
and
z
and a function
c(x_{u})
depending only on

.

Proof.

The general solution to the first equation in (

) is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=2\int\mathfrak{f}\,\mathrm{d}z+c(x_{u})

for a function
c(x_{u})
of the coordinate

. Then, since

needs to be chosen as to solve the equations (

\displaystyle\kappa_{u}=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{f}\,\mathcal{H}-\partial_{z}\mathcal{H})\,,\qquad\kappa_{z}=0

Taking the coordinates

that admit skew-torsion parallel spinors.

Corollary 4.15
.

Lorentzian metrics admitting skew torsion parallel spinors are locally parametrized by one function of two variables and one function of one variable.

4.4.
The lightlike foliation and the dynamics of
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

Let
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
skew-torsion parallel spinor. Then, the kernel
u^{\perp_{g}}\subset TM
of
u
defines an integrable distribution in
TM
whose corresponding foliation we have denoted earlier by
\mathcal{X}_{u}\subset M
. This foliation is
lightlike
in the sense that the restriction of the Lorentzian metric
g
to the tangent spaces of the leaves of
\mathcal{X}_{u}
is degenerate. Equations (
19
) immediately imply that the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
preserves
u^{\perp_{g}}\subset TM
and therefore defines by restriction a connection on each of the leaves of
\mathcal{X}_{u}
. This connection, which we denote by
D
momentarily, satisfies:

(26)

\displaystyle Du^{\sharp_{g}}=\mathfrak{f}\,n\otimes u^{\sharp_{g}}\,,\qquad Dn=-\kappa\otimes u^{\sharp_{g}}

where we are using that
u^{\perp_{g}}\subset TM
is spanned by
u^{\sharp_{g}}
and
n^{\sharp_{g}}
. A direct computation gives the following formulas for the curvature tensor of
D
:

\displaystyle R^{D}_{un}u^{\sharp_{g}}=D_{u^{\sharp_{g}}}D_{n^{\sharp_{g}}}u^{\sharp_{g}}-D_{n^{\sharp_{g}}}D_{u^{\sharp_{g}}}u^{\sharp_{g}}-D_{[u^{\sharp_{g}},n^{\sharp_{g}}]}u^{\sharp_{g}}=0

\displaystyle R^{D}_{un}n^{\sharp_{g}}=D_{u^{\sharp_{g}}}D_{n^{\sharp_{g}}}n^{\sharp_{g}}-D_{n^{\sharp_{g}}}D_{u^{\sharp_{g}}}n^{\sharp_{g}}-D_{[u^{\sharp_{g}},n^{\sharp_{g}}]}n^{\sharp_{g}}=-(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)+\mathfrak{f}^{2})u^{\sharp_{g}}

What we find interesting about this expression is that it does not depend on
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, that is, it does not depend on the one-form that determines the specific form of the differential system that each null coframe
(u,v,n)
satisfies, but only on the null coframe itself and on the underlying fixed torsion. This allows for a simple criteria that guarantees that the leaves of
\mathcal{X}_{u}
carry a flat connection and therefore an affine structure.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
be a skew-torsion Lorenztian three-manifold such that:

\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)+\mathfrak{f}^{2}=0

for any, and hence for all, null coframes associated to
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Then the foliation
\mathcal{X}_{u}\subset M
associated to
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
carries an affine structure induced by the Levi-Civita connection on
\nabla^{g}
.

The condition given in the previous proposition brings us to one of the cases considered by the authors of
[
48
]
in their study of completeness of foliated structures of compact Lorentzian manifolds, see also
[
64
,
69
]
, and supposes an excellent starting point to study the geodesic completeness of compact skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifolds. Since the latter are in particular equipped with a nowhere vanishing isotropic Killing vector field, their completeness depends on the dynamics of
u
: if the dynamics of
u
is non-equicontinuous then
(M,g)
is geodesically complete, as proven by Zeghib in
[
88
]
, where all such non-equicontinuous flows are classified. On the other hand, the geodesic completeness of a compact Lorentzian manifold equipped with a lightlike Killing vector field with equicontinuous flow seems to be a completely open problem
[
48
, Page 3]
.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
(M,g,\mathfrak{f},u)
be a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold. Then, the flow of
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
is equicontinuous if and only if there exists a null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
and a function
F
satisfying the following differential system:

(27)

\kappa(u)+\mathrm{d}F(u)+\mathfrak{f}=0

where
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
is the one-form respect to which
(u,v,n)
satisfies (
22
).

Proof.

By
[
48
, Lemma 7.4]
, the flow of
u
is equicontinuous if and only if there exists a null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
that is preserved by
u
. We compute:

\mathcal{L}_{u}u=0\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{u}v=(\mathfrak{f}+\kappa(u))n\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{u}n=-(\mathfrak{f}+\kappa(u))u

Every other null coframe satisfies an analogous equation with respect to
\kappa^{\prime}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
as prescribed by Equation (
21
). Choose a new null coframe of the form:

(u,v^{\prime},n^{\prime})=F\cdot(u,v,n)=(u,v-\frac{F^{2}}{2}u+Fn,n-Fu)

where
F\in C^{\infty}(M)
satisfies the differential equation:

\kappa(u)+\mathrm{d}F(u)+\mathfrak{f}=0

Then, by Equation (
21
) it follows that
\kappa^{\prime}(u)=-\mathfrak{f}
and hence
\mathcal{L}_{u}u=0
,
\mathcal{L}_{u}v^{\prime}=0
and
\mathcal{L}_{u}n^{\prime}=0
, implying that the flow of
u
preserves a Riemannian metric and is therefore equicontinous.
∎

Solutions to Equation (
27
) may be obstructed. Consider first the following
weaker
form of Equation (
27
):

\alpha(u)=-(\kappa(u)+\mathfrak{f})

for a one-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Solutions to this equation always exist: simply set
\alpha=-(\kappa(u)+\mathfrak{f})v
. It follows that every solution is of the form:

\alpha=-(\kappa(u)+\mathfrak{f})v+\rho_{1}u+\rho_{2}n

for functions
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Hence, the space of solutions is an affine space
\mathbb{A}_{u,\kappa}
modelled on the sections of the distribution
u^{\sharp_{g}}\subset TM
given by the kernel of
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

Corollary 4.18
.

Equation (
27
) admits a solution if and only if
\mathbb{A}_{u,\kappa}
contains an exact one-form.

Hence, by the previous corollary it is natural to first impose
\alpha=-(\kappa(u)+\mathfrak{f})v+\rho_{1}u+\rho_{2}n
to be closed for appropriately chosen functions
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and then evaluate if this closed one-form is exact, which would lead to a cohomological obstruction. In particular, we obtain the following criteria for the geodesic completeness of a compact skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g,u\mathfrak{f})
.

Corollary 4.19
.

If
\mathbb{A}_{u,\kappa}
does not contain an exact one-form then
(M,g)
is geodesically complete.

To end this subsection we recall now the following result, which, as communicated by Hanounah and Mehidi and holds more generally for compact Lorentzian three-manifolds equipped with a nowhere vanishing null Killing vector.

Proposition 4.20
.

Let
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
be a compact skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold that is not diffeomorphic to torus bundle over
S^{1}
. Then
(M,g)
is geodesically complete.

Proof.

If the flow of
u
is not equicontinuous then
(M,g)
is geodesically complete by the results of
[
88
]
. Assume then that the flow of
u
is equicontinuous. Then, by Proposition
4.17
, there exists a null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
satisfying:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}u=\mathfrak{f}\ast_{g}u=\mathfrak{f}\,n\wedge u\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}v=\kappa(v)u\wedge n\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}n=\kappa(n)u\wedge n

In particular, the distribution defined by the kernel of
n\in\Omega^{1}(M)
is integrable. Denote by
\mathcal{X}_{n}\subset M
a given leaf of the corresponding foliation, which has vanishing Godbillon-Vey invariant since:

\displaystyle\kappa(n)u\wedge\mathrm{d}(\kappa(n)u)=\kappa(n)^{2}u\wedge\mathrm{d}u=0

The orthogonal projection of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
to the distribution determined by the kernel of

defines a connection
D^{n}
on
T\mathcal{X}_{n}
that satisfies:

\displaystyle D^{n}u^{\sharp_{g}}=0\,,\qquad D^{n}v^{\sharp_{g}}=0\

and has vanishing torsion. Hence,

defines a flat connection with vanishing torsion on
\mathcal{X}_{n}
that in addition preserves the Riemannian metric
q
defined on
T\mathcal{X}_{n}
by
q:=u\otimes u+v\otimes v
. This implies that

admits a codimension-one foliation with flat leaves, that can only be planes, cylinders or tori. Furthermore, we have:

\displaystyle[u^{\sharp_{g}},v^{\sharp_{g}}]=\nabla^{g}_{u^{\sharp_{g}}}v^{\sharp_{g}}-\nabla^{g}_{v^{\sharp_{g}}}u^{\sharp_{g}}=(\mathfrak{f}+\kappa(u))n^{\sharp_{g}}=0

and therefore,
(u^{\sharp_{g}},v^{\sharp_{g}})
defines a non-singular action of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
on

whose leaves are precisely the leaves
\mathcal{X}_{n}\subset M
of the foliation defined by the kernel of
n

is diffeomorphic to a torus bundle over
S^{1}
and hence we conclude
[
4
,
15
,
83
]
given by a suspension of
T^{2}
over
\mathbb{R}
by an element in
\mathrm{Sl}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

The class of examples in
4.10
gives numerous instances of compact skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifolds that are not diffeomorphic to a torus bundle over
S^{1}
and that are geodesically complete, as it can be independently verified by noticing that they are compact quotients of AdS

(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
is a compact skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold with equicontinuous

then

5.
Supersymmetric solutions in NS-NS supergravity

In this section we consider the type of skew-torsion parallel spinors that occur as supersymmetric configurations and solutions of NS-NS supergravity. This requires introducing the notion of a
abelian gerbe
, which we proceed to briefly review together with other geometric preliminaries needed to establish NS-NS supergravity together with its three-dimensional Killing spinor equations.

5.1.
The NS-NS system

In the following
\mathcal{C}
will denote a bundle gerbe
\mathcal{C}:=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{A},Y)

[
80
,
8
]
, with underlying submersion
Y\to M
, equipped with a fixed connective structure
\mathcal{A}
defined on a fixed three-dimensional manifold
M
, and
\mathcal{X}
will denote a principal
\mathbb{Z}
defined on
M
. Given a curving
b\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
on
\mathcal{C}
we denote its curvature by
H_{b}\in\Omega^{3}(M)
. A given equivariant function
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
defined on
\mathcal{X}
does not necessarily descend to
M
. Nonetheless, its exterior derivative does descend to
M
and defines the
curvature

\varphi_{\phi}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
of
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
. A pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
determines a unique NS-NS supergravity on
M
, as prescribed in the following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

The
NS-NS supergravity system
, or
NS-NS system
for short, determined by
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
on
M
is the following differential system
[
81
,
87
]
:

(28)

\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+\nabla^{g}\varphi_{\phi}-\frac{1}{2}H_{b}\circ_{g}H_{b}=0\,,\qquad\nabla^{g\ast}H_{b}+\varphi_{\phi}\lrcorner_{g}H_{b}=0\,,\qquad\nabla^{g\ast}\varphi_{\phi}+|\varphi_{\phi}|_{g}^{2}=|H_{b}|^{2}_{g}

for triples
(g,b,\phi)
consisting of a Lorentzian metric
g
on
M
, a curving
b\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
on
\mathcal{C}
, and an equivariant function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
, where
H_{b}\in\Omega^{3}(M)
and
\varphi_{\phi}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
are the curvatures of
b
and
\phi
, respectively.

Remark 5.2
.

Solutions
(g,b,\phi)
to equations (
28
) are
NS-NS solutions
. The first equation in (
28
) is the so-called
Einstein equation
, whereas the second equation in (
28
) is called the
Maxwell equation
and the third equation in (
28
) is referred to as the
dilaton equation
in the literature. In this context, an equivariant function on
\mathcal{X}
corresponds to the
dilaton
of the theory, and therefore we will refer to equivariant functions on
\mathcal{X}
as
dilatons
.

Given a NS-NS solution
(g,b,\phi)
, we will refer to the cohomology class
\sigma=[\varphi_{\phi}]\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
determined by
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
as the
Lee class
of
(g,b,\phi)
. Clearly, different dilatons on
\mathcal{X}
define the same Lee class through their curvature. Given
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
, we will denote by
\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
the
configuration space
of the NS-NS system defined on
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
, namely the set of triples
(g,b,\phi)
consisting of a Lorentzian metric
g
on
M
, a curving
b
on
\mathcal{C}
and an equivariant function
\phi
on
\mathcal{X}
. Similarly, we will denote by
\mathrm{Sol}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})\subset\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
the solution set of NS-NS supergravity on
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
. The notion of NS-NS system that we have introduced in Definition
5.1
is valid in any dimension. Since in our case
M
is three-dimensional, we can simplify the NS-NS system accordingly. Given a curving
b\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
with curvature
H_{b}\in\Omega^{3}(M)
we set
H_{b}=-2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\nu_{g}
for a unique function
\mathfrak{f}_{b}\in C^{\infty}(M)
determined by:

\mathfrak{f}_{b}=\frac{1}{2}\ast_{g}H_{b}

A quick computation gives the following formulae:

H_{b}\circ_{g}H_{b}=-4\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}\,g\,,\qquad|H_{b}|^{2}_{g}=-4\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}\,,\qquad\varphi_{\phi}\lrcorner_{g}H_{b}=-2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\ast_{g}\varphi_{\phi}\,,\qquad\nabla^{g\ast}H_{b}=2\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}

Hence, the three-dimensional NS-NS system is equivalent to the following system of equations:

(29)

\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+\nabla^{g}\varphi_{\phi}+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}g=0\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}=\mathfrak{f}_{b}\varphi_{\phi}\,,\qquad\nabla^{g\ast}\varphi_{\phi}+|\varphi_{\phi}|_{g}^{2}+4\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}=0

for triples
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
.

Remark 5.3
.

Left-invariant solutions on three-dimensional Lie groups to the first two equations in the NS-NS system (
29
) have been studied in
[
22
]
in the context of generalized geometry, since they appear naturally as the conditions for a generalized metric to be generalized Ricci flat
[
41
]
. Hence, if also satisfying the dilaton equation in (
29
), these solutions give examples of NS-NS supergravity solutions.

Definition 5.4
.

A triple
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
is
flux-less
if
b
is flat, namely if
\mathfrak{f}_{b}=0
identically on
M
, and is
flux
otherwise. A triple
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
is
trivial
if its flux-less and
g
is flat.

For a trivial triple
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Sol}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
the NS-NS system reduces to:

\nabla^{g}\varphi_{\phi}=0\,,\qquad|\varphi_{\phi}|_{g}^{2}=0

and thus it follows that
\varphi_{\phi}
is a parallel light-like one-form on
M
and consequently
(M,g,\varphi_{\phi})
defines a three-dimensional flat Brinkmann space-time
[
69
]
. Note that this class of space-times can be very
non-trivial
as Lorentzian manifolds
[
13
]
.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
(g,b,\varphi)\in\mathrm{Sol}(\mathcal{C},M)
non-trivial. Then
\nabla^{g}\varphi_{\phi}\neq 0
at some point in
M
.

Proof.

Let
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Sol}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
be non-trivial and assume that
\nabla^{g}\varphi_{\phi}=0
identically. The norm of
\varphi_{\phi}
is then constant, which by the third equation in (
29
) implies that
\mathfrak{f}_{b}
is also constant. Hence, either
\mathfrak{f}_{b}\neq 0
or
\mathfrak{f}_{b}=0
identically on
M
. If
\mathfrak{f}_{b}\neq 0
, the second equation in (
29
) implies
\varphi_{\phi}=0
, which plugged back into the third equation of (
29
) gives a contradiction. Therefore,
\mathfrak{f}_{b}=0
and the NS-NS system reduces to:

\mathrm{Ric}^{g}=0\,,\qquad|\varphi_{\phi}|_{g}^{2}=0

We conclude that
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Sol}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
defines a three-dimensional Ricci-flat Brinkmann space-time which is therefore flat and thus
(g,b,\phi)
is trivial.
∎

In the following we will be interested in non-trivial NS-NS solutions.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Sol}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
. Then, either
\mathfrak{f}_{b}=0
or else:

\varphi_{\phi}=\mathrm{d}\phi\,,\qquad\mathfrak{f}_{b}=ce^{\phi}\,,\qquad c\in\mathbb{R}^{\ast}

for a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
unique modulo an additive constant. In particular
\mathfrak{f}_{b}\in C^{\infty}(M)
is nowhere vanishing.

Proof.

Let
(g,b,\varphi_{\phi})\in\mathrm{Sol}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
. If
\mathfrak{f}_{b}
is nowhere vanishing, then the second equation in (
29
) is equivalent to:

\varphi_{\phi}=\mathrm{d}\log(|\mathfrak{f}_{b}|)

and thus there exists a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
\varphi_{\phi}=\mathrm{d}\phi
and
\mathfrak{f}_{b}=ce^{\phi}
for a non-zero real constant
c
. Suppose now that
\mathfrak{f}_{b}
has a zero on
m\in M
. Every smooth path
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
in
M
passing through
m\in M
at
t=0
satisfies:

\partial_{t}(\mathfrak{f}_{b}(\gamma_{t}))=\mathfrak{f}_{b}(\gamma_{t})\gamma^{\ast}_{t}\varphi_{\phi}=\mathfrak{f}_{b}(\gamma_{t})F_{t}

for a certain real function
F_{t}
of
t
. This equation becomes an ordinary differential equation for the real function
\mathfrak{f}_{b}(\gamma_{t})
which, if it has a zero at
t=0
then it is identically zero. Since this property holds for every such smooth curve
\gamma
and
M
is connected, we conclude that if
\mathfrak{f}_{b}
is zero at some point then it is identically zero.
∎

By the previous lemma, the study of NS-NS solutions splits into the study of flux-less NS-NS solutions, namely solutions with flat
b
-field, and
flux
NS-NS solutions, namely solutions which have nowhere vanishing curvature
\mathfrak{f}_{b}
. For flux-less triples
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{C},M)
the NS-NS system reduces to:

\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+\nabla^{g}\varphi=0\,,\qquad\nabla^{g\ast}\varphi+|\varphi|_{g}^{2}=0

and thus it effectively reduces to differential system for pairs
(g,\varphi)
, where
g
is a Lorentzian metric on
M
and
\varphi
is a closed one-form. Note that solutions to the first equation above correspond to steady Lorentzian Ricci solitons
[
43
]
. On the other hand, for triples
(g,b,\varphi)
with
\mathfrak{f}_{b}\neq 0
the NS-NS system reduces to a family of differential systems:

(30)

\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\phi+2c^{2}e^{2\phi}g=0\,,\qquad\nabla^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}\phi+|\mathrm{d}\phi|_{g}^{2}+4c^{2}e^{2\phi}=0\,,\qquad\mathfrak{f}_{b}=ce^{\phi}

parametrized by the non-zero real constant
c\in\mathbb{R}^{\ast}
. Hence, the NS-NS system decouples: the first two equations in (
30
) can be
solved
independently and, once a solution
(g,\phi)
has been found, a curving
b
solving the third equation can be found as soon as
\mathfrak{f}_{b}
defines an integral class in de Rham cohomology.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
M
be an oriented compact three-manifold without boundary. Then
M
admits no flux NS-NS solutions.

Proof.

Integrate the second equation in (
30
).
∎

Three-dimensional NS-NS flux solutions can however be globally hyperbolic with compact Cauchy hypersurface, and therefore can be studied from the point of view of the celebrated program initiated by Mess
[
71
,
10
]
.

5.2.
Supersymmetric configurations

The notion of
supersymmetric configuration
originated in supergravity and, for the three-dimensional NS-NS supergravity that we consider in this note, it involves skew-torsion parallel spinors. Given an element
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
we denote by
\nabla^{g,b}
the unique metric connection on
(M,g)
with totally skew-symmetric torsion given by
H_{b}\in\Omega^{3}(M)
, the curvature of
b\in\Omega^{2}(Y\times_{M}Y)
. This is the natural way in which supersymmetry realizes geometrically the notion of completely skew torsion of a metric connection. For ease of notation we denote by the same symbol
\nabla^{g,b}
its lift to any irreducible spinor bundle defined on
(M,g)
.

Definition 5.8
.

A
supersymmetric configuration
on
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
is triple
(g,b,\phi)
consisting of a Lorentzian metric
g
on
M
, an equivariant function
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
, and a curving
b\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
satisfying the following differential system:

(31)

\nabla^{g,b}\varepsilon=0\,,\qquad(\varphi_{\phi}+H_{b})\cdot\varepsilon=0

for a non-zero spinor
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
of an irreducible paired spinor bundle
(S,\Gamma,\Scr B)
defined on
(M,g)
. Such spinor
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
is a
supersymmetry parameter
or
supersymmetry generator
for the supersymmetric configuration
(g,\varphi,b)
.

Therefore, the supersymmetry parameter of a supersymmetric configuration is in particular a skew-torsion parallel spinor. We denote by
\mathrm{Conf}_{s}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})\subset\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
the set of supersymmetric configurations on
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
.

Proposition 5.9
.

A triple
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Conf}_{s}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
is a supersymmetric configuration if and only if there exists an isotropic one-form
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
on
(M,g)
and a function
\mathfrak{f}_{b}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that:

(32)

\nabla^{g}u=\mathfrak{f}_{b}\ast_{g}u\,,\qquad\varphi_{\phi}=\mathfrak{c}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n

for a function
\mathfrak{c}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, where
H_{b}=-2\,\mathfrak{f}_{b}\nu_{g}
is the curvature of the curving
b\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
, the first equation in (
31
) is satisfied if and only if there exists an isotropic vector field
u\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
satisfying the first equation in (
32
). On the other hand, by Theorem
3.6
and Remark
3.11
, the second equation in (
31
) is equivalent to:

\displaystyle(\Psi_{\Gamma}^{<})^{-1}(\Gamma\circ\Psi^{-})(\varphi_{\phi}+H_{b})\vee_{g}u=(\varphi_{\phi}-2l\mathfrak{f}_{b})\vee_{g}u=\varphi_{\phi}(u)+l\ast_{g}(u\wedge\varphi_{\phi})-2l\mathfrak{f}_{b}u

which is solved precisely by
\varphi_{\phi}=\mathfrak{c}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n
for a function
\mathfrak{c}\in C^{\infty}(M)
.
∎

Hence, every supersymmetric configuration on
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
defines a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f}_{b})
. We cannot expect however the converse to be true since, for a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold to be part of a supersymmetric configuration the one-form
(\mathfrak{c}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n)\in\Omega^{1}(M)
needs to be not only closed, but exact.

Proposition 5.10
.

A skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g,u,\mathfrak{f})
defines a supersymmetric configuration if and only if there exists a function
\mathfrak{c}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that:

[\mathfrak{c}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n]\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})

defines a integral class.

Proof.

The
only if
condition follows directly from Proposition
5.9
. To prove the converse, simply note that since
\mathfrak{c}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n
is an integral one-form, it follows that there exists a principal
\mathbb{Z}
bundle
\mathcal{X}
and an equivariant function
\phi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi_{\phi}=\mathfrak{c}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n
.
∎

We end this subsection by computing the covariant derivative of the curvature
\varphi_{\phi}
of the dilaton.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
(g,b,\phi,\varepsilon)\in\mathrm{Conf}_{s}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
be a supersymmetric configuration with associated Dirac current
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and let
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
be any null coframe. Then:

(33)

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}\varphi_{\phi}=(2\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(v)-\mathfrak{c}\,\mathfrak{f}_{b})u\odot n+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}u\odot v+2\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(n)n\otimes n+(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}(v)-2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,\kappa(v))u\otimes u

where
\varphi_{\phi}=\mathfrak{c}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n
. Furthermore,
(u,v,n)
satisfies the differential system (
22
) with respect to a one-form
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
that satisfies the following relations:

(34)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}(u)=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}(\mathfrak{f}_{b}+\kappa(u))\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}(n)=2(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(v)+\mathfrak{f}_{b}\kappa(n)-\mathfrak{c}\,\mathfrak{f}_{b})

in terms of
\mathfrak{c}\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.9
we have
\varphi_{\phi}=\mathfrak{c}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n
in any given null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
. Since
\varphi
must be exact, it is necessarily closed, hence:

\displaystyle 0=\mathrm{d}\varphi_{\phi}=\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}\wedge u+\mathfrak{c}\,\mathrm{d}u+2\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}\wedge n+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\mathrm{d}n=\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}\wedge u+\mathfrak{c}\,\mathfrak{f}_{b}n\wedge u+2\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}\wedge n+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}u\wedge(\mathfrak{f}_{b}v+\kappa)

Solving the previous equation by using the integrability conditions for
(u,v,n)
, we obtain the given relations (
34
). Applying now Equation (
19
) to compute the covariant derivative of
n
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}\varphi_{\phi}=\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}\otimes u+\mathfrak{c}\nabla^{g}u+2\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}\otimes n+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\nabla^{g}n

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}\otimes u+\mathfrak{c}\,\mathfrak{f}_{b}(n\otimes u-u\otimes n)+2\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}\otimes n+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}(\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,u\otimes v-\kappa\otimes u)

Plugging Equation (
34
) into the previous equation and expanding we obtain (
33
) and we conclude.
∎

We will need the previous lemma to study supersymmetric solutions in the following subsection.

5.3.
Supersymmetric solutions

We consider now the supersymmetric solutions of the NS-NS system, which satisfy the natural curvature condition on skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifolds that we will consider in this article.

Definition 5.12
.

A
supersymmetric solution
is a supersymmetric configuration
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Conf}_{s}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
that satisfies the NS-NS system on
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
.

We denote by
\mathrm{Sol}_{s}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
the set of supersymmetric solutions on
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
. By the results of Section
5.1
, we have to distinguish between flux-less and flux supersymmetric NS-NS solutions. A quick inspection of equations (
29
) and (
32
) reveals that flux-less supersymmetric NS-NS solutions consist of flat bundle gerbes over flat Brinkmann space-times equipped with a parallel isotropic vector field given by the curvature of the dilaton. We focus then on flux solutions.

Proposition 5.13
.

A supersymmetric configuration
(g,b,\phi,\varepsilon)\in\mathrm{Conf}_{s}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
satisfies the Einstein equation of the NS-NS system if and only if:

(35)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(v)=\mathfrak{c}\,\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(n)=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}(v)=\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,\kappa(v)

where
\varphi_{\phi}=\mathfrak{c}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n
in terms of any null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
associated to the Dirac current
u
of
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
.

Proof.

Let
(g,b,\phi,\varepsilon)
be a supersymmetric configuration with associated Dirac current
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and fix a null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
. Then, by Proposition
5.9
we have
\varphi_{\phi}=\mathrm{d}\phi=\mathfrak{c}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n
in terms of a function
\mathfrak{c}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Using equations (
23
) and (
33
), we compute:

\displaystyle 0=\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+\nabla^{g}\varphi_{\phi}+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}=-\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(v)u\odot n-\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)g-\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)\,n\otimes n+(\mathfrak{f}\,\kappa(v)-\mathfrak{l})u\otimes u

(36)

\displaystyle+(2\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(v)-\mathfrak{c}\,\mathfrak{f}_{b})u\odot n+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}u\odot v+2\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(n)n\otimes n+(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}(v)-2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,\kappa(v))u\otimes u

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}(v)-\mathfrak{l}-\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,\kappa(v))u\otimes u+(\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(v)-\mathfrak{c}\,\mathfrak{f}_{b})u\odot n+(2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}-\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n))u\odot v

Hence
\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(v)=\mathfrak{c}\,\mathfrak{f}_{b}
,
\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(n)=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}
,
\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{c}(v)=\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,\kappa(v)
which together with Equation (
34
) we conclude.
∎

By the previous proposition, we introduce the following notation:

\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b}:=v(\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b}))=\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v)

that we will use in the following for simplicity in the exposition. Let
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Sol}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
be a NS-NS solution. Then, by the third equation in (
30
), we can equivalently write
(g,b,\phi)=(g,b,\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})+c)
for a certain constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
which is uninteresting to our purposes. Hence, the pair
(g,b)
already determines
(g,b,\phi)
modulo the aforementioned constant, so in the following we will occasionally refer to a NS-NS solution simply by
(g,b)\in\mathrm{Sol}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})
.

Proposition 5.14
.

A tuple
(g,b,\phi,\varepsilon)
is a supersymmetric solution if and only if
(M,g,\varepsilon)
is a skew-torsion Lorentzian manifold with respect to a nowhere vanishing
\mathfrak{f}_{b}
, the one-form:

\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v)\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n\in\Omega^{1}(M)

is exact and
(g,b,\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b}))
satisfies the Einstein equation of the NS-NS system.

Proof.

The
only if
direction follows directly from equations (
30
) together with propositions
5.9
,
5.10
and
5.13
. To prove the
if
direction we consider a skew-torsion Lorentzian three-manifold
(M,g,\mathfrak{f}_{b},u)
such that
\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v)\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n\in\Omega^{1}(M)
is exact and
(g,b,\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b}))
satisfies the Einstein equation in (
29
). Since the one-form
\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v)\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n\in\Omega^{1}(M)
is exact, there exists a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\phi=\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v)\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n

Evaluating on
(u,v,n)
we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}f(u)=0\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}f(v)=\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v)\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}f(n)=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}(n)=\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(n)

where we have used Proposition
5.13
by virtue of the Einstein equation being satisfied by
(g,b,\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b}))
. Since
\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(u)=0
by the integrability conditions (
4.1
) of
(u,v,n)
, we obtain
\mathrm{d}\phi=\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})
and consequently
\mathfrak{f}_{b}=ce^{\phi}
for a non-zero constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, setting
\varphi_{\phi}=\mathrm{d}\phi=\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v)\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n
, the tuple
(g,b,\phi,\varepsilon)
becomes a supersymmetric configuration that satisfies the Einstein and Maxwell equations of the NS-NS system. Hence, only the dilaton equation needs to be checked. We compute:

\displaystyle 0=\nabla^{g\ast}\varphi_{\phi}+|\varphi_{\phi}|^{2}_{g}+4\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}=-4\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}-2\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(n)+4\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}+4\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}=2(2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}-\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n))

and therefore we conclude since by Proposition
5.13
we have
2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}=\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)
.
∎

Theorem 5.15
.

A NS-NS flux configuration
(g,b)\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
is a supersymmetric solution of the NS-NS system if and only if there exists a nowhere vanishing isotropic vector
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
satisfying:

(37)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}u=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,n\wedge u\,,\qquad 2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\mathrm{d}v=(\mathfrak{K}\,u+u(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})v)\wedge n\,,\qquad 2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\mathrm{d}n=u\wedge(u(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})v+n(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})n)

\displaystyle\frac{n(\mathfrak{K})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}}=\frac{u(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})}{4\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}}\mathfrak{K}+v(\frac{n(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}})+\frac{n(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})^{2}}{4\mathfrak{f}^{2}_{b}}-v(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(n)=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}\,,\qquad 0=[\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v}u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n]\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})

for a function
\mathfrak{K}\in C^{\infty}(M)
and for any, and hence for all, null coframes
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
associated to
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

Remark 5.16
.

Supersymmetric configurations and solutions of ten-dimensional NS-NS supergravity have been studied before in the supergravity literature under the assumption of existence of a compatible absolute parallelism
[
26
,
27
,
51
]
. In our framework, this case requires setting
\kappa=\mathfrak{f}_{b}v
, which imposes strong constraints on the underlying geometry.

Remark 5.17
.

By Remark
4.2
, we can refer to three-dimensional NS-NS supersymmetric solutions, and in particular to the Lorentzian three-manifolds characterized in the previous theorem as
supersymmetric Kundt three-manifolds
. This defines an interesting class of Kundt three-manifolds that has not been studied in the mathematics literature.

Proof.

Let
(g,b)\in\mathrm{Sol}_{s}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
be a supersymmetric NS-NS solution with supersymmetry parameter
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
and associated Dirac current
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Then, by Lemma
4.5
and Proposition
5.9
, every null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
associated to
u
satisfies equations (
19
) for a certain
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Equations (
19
) imply in turn that
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
satisfies the exterior differential system (
22
) for the same
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. On the other hand, by Proposition
5.13
we have:

\varphi_{\phi}=\mathrm{d}\phi=v(\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b}))u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}

as well as
\mathfrak{f}_{b}=ce^{\phi}
for a non-zero real constant in
c
by Lemma
5.6
. Furthermore, again by Proposition
5.13
together with Lemma
5.11
, see equations (
34
), we have:

\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b}=v(\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b}))\,,\quad v(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})=\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,\kappa(v)\,,\quad u(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}(\mathfrak{f}_{b}+\kappa(u))\,,\quad n(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\kappa(n)\,,\quad 2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}=\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)

which are satisfied by any given null coframe
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
associated to the Dirac current
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
corresponding to the underlying skew-torsion parallel spinor. These equations imply that the one-form
\kappa
respect to which
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
satisfies the exterior differential system (
22
) is given by:

(38)

\displaystyle\kappa=\kappa(v)u+(\frac{u(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}}-\mathfrak{f}_{b})v+\frac{n(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}}n=\kappa(v)u+(\frac{u(v(\mathfrak{f}_{b}))}{2\mathfrak{f}^{2}_{b}}-\mathfrak{f}_{b})v+(\frac{n(v(\mathfrak{f}_{b}))}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}}-\frac{v(\mathfrak{f}_{b})}{\mathfrak{f}_{b}})n

From this equation, we compute:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\kappa=(v(\kappa(u))+\kappa(n)(\mathfrak{f}_{b}+\kappa(u))-u(\kappa(v)))u\wedge v

\displaystyle+(\kappa(u)\kappa(v)+v(\kappa(n))+\kappa(n)^{2}-n(\kappa(v))-2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\kappa(v))u\wedge n+(-n(\kappa(u))+\kappa(u)(\mathfrak{f}_{b}+\kappa(u))+u(\kappa(n)))v\wedge n

Comparing this expression for
\mathrm{d}\kappa\in\Omega^{2}(M)
with the expression obtained in Proposition
4.7

\displaystyle v(\kappa(u))+\kappa(n)(\mathfrak{f}_{b}+\kappa(u))-u(\kappa(v))+v(\mathfrak{f}_{b})=0

\displaystyle\kappa(u)\kappa(v)+v(\kappa(n))+\kappa(n)^{2}-n(\kappa(v))-2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\kappa(v)=\mathfrak{l}

\displaystyle\kappa(u)^{2}-n(\kappa(u))+u(\kappa(n))=3\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}

where:

\displaystyle\kappa(u):=\frac{u(v(\mathfrak{f}_{b}))}{2\mathfrak{f}^{2}_{b}}-\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,,\qquad\kappa(n)=\frac{n(v(\mathfrak{f}_{b}))}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}}-\frac{v(\mathfrak{f}_{b})}{\mathfrak{f}_{b}}

Note that the only unknown in the previous system of equations is
\kappa(v)\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Of this system of equations we are interested in the equation that determines
\mathfrak{l}
, which plugged back into the second equation in (

) gives the explicit expression for the last equation that we need to consider, which is at this point equivalent to the Einstein equation in the NS-NS system, namely:

\displaystyle v(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})=\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,\kappa(v)=-n(\kappa(v))-2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\kappa(v)+\frac{u(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}}\kappa(v)+v(\frac{n(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}})+(\frac{n(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}})^{2}

This is the equation in the statement of the theorem after setting
\mathfrak{K}:=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\kappa(v)
.

Conversely, let
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{X})
and assume that we have a nowhere vanishing isotropic vector field
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
on
(M,g)
. Let
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
be a null coframe that satisfies all equations in (
37
) with respect to a given function
\mathfrak{K}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. By assumption,
(u,v,n)
satisfies (
22
) with respect to a one-form
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
of the form:

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{\mathfrak{K}}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}}u+(\frac{u(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}}-\mathfrak{f}_{b})v+\frac{n(\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}}n

This in turn implies that
(u,v,n)
satisfies equations (
19
) with respect to the metric
g=u\odot v+n\otimes n
and the same one-form
\kappa\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Hence
(M,g)
admits a skew-torsion parallel spinor
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
with respect to a metric connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion
H=-2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\nu_{g}
. Furthermore, since
0=[\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n]\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
there exists a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\phi=\mathfrak{c}^{v}_{b}\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n

Hence we obtain that
\mathfrak{f}_{b}=ce^{\phi}
for a non-zero real constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
upon use of
\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(u)=0
by the integrability conditions of
(u,v,n)
. Then, by Proposition
5.9
, the tuple
(g,\mathfrak{f},\phi,\varepsilon)
is in fact a supersymmetric configuration of NS-NS supergravity. Furthermore, condition
2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}=\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}(n)
implies that the dilaton equation holds by the same computation as in the proof of Proposition
5.13
. On the other hand, by the same computation as in (
36
), the Einstein equation evaluated on
(g,\mathfrak{f}_{b},\phi)
reduces to:

\displaystyle v(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})=\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,k

After identifying
\mathfrak{l}
in terms of
\mathfrak{f}_{b}
and its derivatives with respect to the given parallelism
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
this equation reduces to the first equation in the second line of (
37
) and therefore is satisfied by assumption.
∎

Remark 5.18
.

It is remarkable that if
(u,v,n)\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
satisfies the differential system of the statement of the previous theorem, then any other null coframe
(u,v^{\prime},n^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}_{u}
also satisfies it, perhaps for a differente
\mathfrak{K}
. In particular, for the curvature of the dilaton, we have:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\phi=\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v^{\prime})\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n^{\prime}=\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v-\frac{F^{2}}{2}u+Fn)\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n-2\mathfrak{f}_{b}F\,u

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v)\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n+F\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(n)\,u-2\mathfrak{f}_{b}F\,u=\mathrm{d}\log(\mathfrak{f}_{b})(v)\,u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n

where
(u,v^{\prime},n^{\prime})=(u,v-\frac{F^{2}}{2}u+Fu,n-Fu)
for a function
F\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

To the best of our knowledge the previous theorem provides what seems to be one of the first global characterizations of the supersymmetric NS-NS solutions on manifolds of general topology, and as such can be further refined for different particular classes of supersymmetric solutions, for instance, static or stationary supersymmetric solutions, which we plan consider elsewhere. If in Theorem
5.15
we are not interested in the relation between the given supersymmetry parameter
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
and the associated Dirac current and null coframes, then we can reformulate it as follows, which is sometimes more convenient for applications.

Corollary 5.19
.

A three-manifold
M
admits a supersymmetric NS-NS solution if and only if it admits a global coframe
(u,v,n)
satisfying the following differential system

(39)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}u=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\,n\wedge u\,,\qquad 2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\mathrm{d}v=(\mathfrak{K}\,u+u(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})v)\wedge n\,,\qquad 2\mathfrak{f}_{b}\mathrm{d}n=u\wedge(u(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})v+n(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})n)

\displaystyle\frac{n(\mathfrak{K})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}}=\frac{u(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})}{4\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}}\mathfrak{K}+v(\frac{n(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})}{2\mathfrak{f}_{b}})+\frac{n(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})^{2}}{4\mathfrak{f}^{2}_{b}}-v(\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v})\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{f}_{b}(n)=2\mathfrak{f}_{b}^{2}\,,\qquad 0=[\mathfrak{c}_{b}^{v}u+2\mathfrak{f}_{b}n]\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})

for a function

and a curving

Remark 5.20

In a supergravity context, the supersymmetry parameter is many times irrelevant in itself, only the geometric and topological consequences of its existence being important. In this situation, the previous corollary captures the if and only if conditions for a supersymmetry solution to exist with no mention of the underlying Lorentzian metric or supersymmetry parameter. Corollary 5.21

Let

Corollary 5.22

Supersymmetric solutions of three-dimensional NS-NS supergravity are locally parametrized by two real functions of one variable.

The Poiseuille flow of the full Ericksen-Leslie model for nematic liquid crystals: The general Case
Geng Chen, Weishi Liu, Majed Sofiani

Abstract
. In this work, we study the Cauchy problem of Poiseuille flow of the full Ericksen-Leslie model for nematic liquid crystals. The model is a coupled system of two partial differential equations (PDEs): One is a quasi-linear wave equation for the director field representing the crystallization of the nematics, and the other is a parabolic PDE for the velocity field characterizing the liquidity of the material. We extend the work in [Chen, et. al. Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 236 (2020), 839-891] for a special case to the general physical setup. The Cauchy problem is shown to have global solutions beyond singularity formation. Among a number of progresses made in this paper, a particular contribution is a systematic treatment of a parabolic PDE with only Hölder continuous diffusion coefficient and rough (maybe unbounded) nonhomogeneous terms.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider existence and regularity for Poiseuille flow of nematic liquid crystals. Liquid crystals have many forms and a particular form is the nematic whose molecules can be viewed as
rod-like/thread-like. Macroscopically, the state of a nematic liquid crystal is characterized by its velocity field
{\bf u}
for the flow and its director field
{\bf n}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
for the alignment of the rod-like feature. These two characteristics interact with each other so that any distortion of the director
{\bf n}
causes
a motion
{\bf u}
and, likewise, any flow
{\bf u}
affects the alignment
{\bf n}
. Using the convention to denote
\dot{f}=f_{t}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla f
the material derivative, the full Ericksen-Leslie model for nematics is given as follows

\begin{cases}\rho\dot{\bf u}+\nabla P=\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\nabla{\bf n}}\otimes\nabla{\bf n}\right),\\
\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=0,\\
\nu{\ddot{\bf n}}=\lambda{\bf n}-\frac{\partial W}{\partial{\bf n}}-{\bf g}+\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\nabla{\bf n}}\right),\\
|{\bf n}|=1.\\
\end{cases}

(1.1)

In (
1.1
),
P
is the pressure,
\lambda
is the Lagrangian multiplier of the constraint
|{\bf n}|=1
,
\rho
is the density,
\nu
is the inertial coefficient of the director
{\bf n}
, and
W
,

{\bf g}
and
\sigma
are the Oseen-Frank energy, the kinematic transport and the viscous stress tensor, respectively (see, e.g.,
[
16
,
18
,
21
,
9
,
30
,
31
,
35
]
for details).

1.1.
Poiseulle flow of nematics

For Poiseulle flows of nematics with a choice of coordinates system,

{\bf u}(x,t)=(0,0,u(x,t))^{T}\;\mbox{ and }\;{\bf n}(x,t)=(\sin\theta(x,t),0,\cos\theta(x,t))^{T},

where the motion

[

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(g(\theta)u_{x}+h(\theta)\theta_{t}\right)_{x},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\theta_{tt}+\gamma_{1}\theta_{t}

\displaystyle=c(\theta)\big(c(\theta)\theta_{x}\big)_{x}-h(\theta)u_{x}.

(1.3)

We will be interested in Cauchy problem of the Poiseulle flow
with initial data

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\;\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x)\in H^{1},\;\theta_{t}(x,0)=\theta_{1}(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

(1.4)

We further impose an assumption

\begin{split}u^{\prime}_{0}(x)+\frac{h(\theta_{0}(x))}{g(\theta_{0}(x))}\theta_{1}(x):=J_{0}&\in H^{1}(x)\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}).\end{split}

To this end, we make an important comment on condition (

and
\theta_{t}
can approach as
(x,t)\to(x^{*},t^{*})
for some

turns out to be in

for any

reveals a cancellation of finite time singularities of

and plays crucial role for the study of existence after singularity formation. This key observation was first made in
[
12
]
for a special case.
We further need
J_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that an important auxiliary function
A_{0}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}J_{0}(z)dz
is well defined. The condition

is needed for estimates in (

The functions

,

and
h(\theta)
in the model are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}g(\theta):=&\alpha_{1}\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta+\frac{\alpha_{5}-\alpha_{2}}{2}\sin^{2}\theta+\frac{\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{6}}{2}\cos^{2}\theta+\frac{\alpha_{4}}{2},\\
h(\theta):=&\alpha_{3}\cos^{2}\theta-\alpha_{2}\sin^{2}\theta=\frac{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\cos(2\theta)}{2},\\
c^{2}(\theta):=&K_{1}\cos^{2}\theta+K_{3}\sin^{2}\theta,\end{split}

where, depending on the nematic material and temperature,

and

are Frank’s coefficients for splay and bending energies, and
\alpha_{j}
’s
(1\leq j\leq 6)
are the Leslie dynamic coefficients. The following relations are assumed in the literature.

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{2},\quad\gamma_{2}=\alpha_{6}-\alpha_{5},\quad\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}=\alpha_{6}-\alpha_{5}.

(1.7)

The first two relations are compatibility conditions, while the third relation is called a Parodi’s relation, derived from Onsager reciprocal relations expressing the equality of certain relations between flows and forces in thermodynamic systems out of equilibrium (cf.
[
43
]

They also satisfy the following empirical relations (p.13,
[

]

\displaystyle\alpha_{4}>0,\quad 2\alpha_{1}+3\alpha_{4}+2\alpha_{5}+2\alpha_{6}>0,\quad\gamma_{1}=\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{2}>0,

(1.8)

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{5}+\alpha_{6}>0,\quad 4\gamma_{1}(2\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{5}+\alpha_{6})>(\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+\gamma_{2})^{2}.

Note that the fourth relation is implied by the third together with the last relation and the last can be rewritten as

\gamma_{1}(2\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{5}+\alpha_{6})>\gamma_{2}^{2}

Very importantly, relations (

\displaystyle g(\theta)\geq g(\theta)-\frac{h^{2}(\theta)}{\gamma_{1}}>\overline{C}.

4.
A brief review of parabolic differential operators with non-constant Hölder coefficients.

In this section we summarise relevant results from first chapter in

Let the differential operator

where

is a strictly positive smooth function and
\theta

with respect to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\,\omega

\displaystyle=0.

(4.1)

Note that, for any fixed

\displaystyle H^{\xi,\tau}(x-\xi,t-\tau)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{g(\theta(\xi,\tau))}\sqrt{t-\tau}}e^{-\frac{(x-\xi)^{2}}{4g(\theta(\xi,\tau))(t-\tau)}}.

(4.2)

.

The superscripts in

and

indicate the dependence on

via

Several results established in Chapter 1 of
[
20
]
will be recalled and used. These include Theorems 8-11, displays (4.15), (6.12) and (6.13) in
[
20
]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists a function
\Phi
such that
\Gamma
given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,t,\xi,\tau)

\displaystyle=H^{\xi,\tau}(x-\xi,t-\tau)+\int_{\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}H^{y,s}(x-y,t-s)\Phi(y,s;\xi,\tau)\,dy\,ds

satisfies (
4.1
). Moreover, one has

\displaystyle|\Phi(y,s;\xi,\tau)|\leq\frac{const}{(s-\tau)^{5/4}}e^{\frac{-d(y-\xi)^{2}}{4(s-\tau)}},

(4.4)

where

is a constant depending on
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
and
\gamma_{1}.

Set
\displaystyle\Omega_{T}:=\mathbb{R}\times(0,T]
for some

and consider the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\,\omega(x,t)=f(x,t),\quad\text{on}\quad\Omega_{T}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\omega(x,0)=\phi(x),\quad\text{at}\quad t=0

where

is Hölder continuous on
\overline{\Omega}_{T}
and
\phi
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
. It is shown in

\displaystyle\omega(x,t)=\int_{R}\Gamma(x,t,\xi,0)\phi(\xi)\,d\xi+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{R}\Gamma(x,t;\xi.\tau)f(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau

solution of the Cauchy problem (

\displaystyle|\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-\tau}}e^{-\frac{d(x-\xi)^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\approx H(x-\xi,t-\tau),

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{x}(x,t;\xi,\tau)|\lesssim\frac{1}{{t-\tau}}e^{-\frac{d(x-\xi)^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-\tau}}H(x-\xi,t-\tau),

We now apply the above results from

]

Set

\displaystyle M_{f}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)f(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau,

\displaystyle M_{f,x}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{x}(x,t;\xi,\tau)f(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
f(x,t)\in{L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))},

then

\displaystyle\|M_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})}\lesssim T^{1/4}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})},\;

\displaystyle\;\|M_{f,x}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})}\lesssim T^{1/4}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))},

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|M_{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\lesssim T\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})},\;

\displaystyle\mbox{ and }\;\|M_{f,x}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\lesssim\sqrt{T}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}.

To estimate the

\displaystyle|M_{f}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{{t-\tau}}e^{-\frac{d(x-\xi)^{2}}{2(t-\tau)}}\,dx\,d\tau\bigg)^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\lesssim T^{1/4}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}.

\displaystyle|M_{f,x}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{{|t-\tau|}^{2-2r}}e^{-\frac{d(x-\xi)^{2}}{2(t-\tau)}}\,dx\,d\tau\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{{|t-\tau|}^{2r}}f^{2}\,dx\,d\tau\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\|M\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-\tau}}e^{-\frac{d(x-\xi)^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}|f(\xi,\tau)|\,d\xi\,d\tau\bigg\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\|M_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim

Proposition 4.4
.

If
f(x,t)\in{L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))},

\displaystyle M_{f}\,,\,M_{f,x}\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega_{T}),

\displaystyle M_{f}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)f(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}H^{\xi,\tau}(x-\xi,t-\tau)f(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg[\int_{\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}H^{y,s}(x-y,t-s)\Phi(y,s;\xi,\tau)\,dy\,ds\bigg]f(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau

and

Taking

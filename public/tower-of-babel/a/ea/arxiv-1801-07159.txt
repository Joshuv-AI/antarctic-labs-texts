Timelets on causal sets
Romàn Zapatrin

Abstract
Dual structures on causal sets called timelets are introduced, being discrete analogs of global time coordinates. Algebraic and geometrical features of the set of timelets on a causal set are studied. A characterization of timelets in terms of incidence matrix of causal set is given. The connection between timelets and preclusive coevents is established, it is shown that any timelet has a unique decomposition over preclusive coevents. The equivalence classes of timelets with respect to reascaling are shown to form a simplicial complex.



A foreword

Causal set approach is intended to represent spacetime structure as a discrete one. It has promising application in both quantum gravity and foundations of quantum mechanics. On the other hand, causal sets may be treated as just Regge-like approximations of spacetime, which is thought of as being ‘indeed continuous’. This paper has a technical character, it has nothing to do with interpretation, I just introduce a dual structure on causal sets. Roughly speaking, the elements of a causal set (causet in the sequel) are events, while timelets I introduce are dual structure on the set of events, respecting the causal order. Timelets generalize the notion of coevents
[
3
]
, sequential growth parameter
[
2
]
, they look like analogs of global time.

The paper is organized as follows. First, the definition of timelets is provided along with their matrix representation and the monotonicity condition in terms of the incidence matrix is formulated. The classes of monotonically equivalent timelets are described and the representation of generic timelets as maximal extensions of the partial order on the causet is provided. In section
3
the most degenerate, two-valued timelets are described, they are associated with preclusive coevents and the decomposition of timelets over preclusive coevents is provided. Then the equivalence classes of timelets are shown to form simplicial complexes, two examples are elaborated in detail.

4
Simplicial representation of timelets

In this section I introduce an analog of Lorenz transformation: time shift and boost. This make it possible to represent equivalence classes of generic timelets as simplices, so the set of all timelets acquire the structure of simplicial complex.

Affine reduction of timelets.

Let us introduce an equivalence relation
\simeq
on timelets being stronger than themonotonicity gauge
\sim
defined in (
9
). Rescaling means affine transformation: for any real
A
and positive
B
.

T\equiv T^{\prime}\;\Leftrightarrow\;T^{\prime}=A+BT

Adding appropriate constant and rescaling timelets by appropriate positive multiple we can always normalize them to fit into a simplex
\mathcal{S}
spanned on unit vectors in
\mathcal{L}
and satisfy the following property

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\sum_{x\in C}\limits T(x)=1\\
\forall x\in C\quad x\geq 0\\
\exists x\in C\quad x=0\end{array}\right.

(13)

It follows from (
13
) that any such timelet is inside the total simplex
\mathcal{S}
. Furthermore, considering its barycentric coordinates, we see, as it follows from the third condition (
13
) that it is always on a face of
\mathcal{S}
rather than in its interior (because its minimal value is 0). The support of timelets is concentrated on simplices, which are faces opposite to vertices asoociated with minimal elements of the causet
C
.

Decomposition of timelets.

Consider a generic timelet. Normalize it in such way that it has all different values on the elements of the causet
C
and the minimal value is 0, the other values are positive integers. It follows immediately that the minimal zero value is the least, and it is achieved on a particular element
x_{0}\in C
. Consider the coevent
\kappa_{0}
such that its support is the support of the timelet
T

\kappa_{1}(x)\;=\;\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{if $x=x_{0}$}\\
1&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

The value 1 is achieved on a unique element
x_{1}\in C
(since all the values of
T
are different). Define

T_{1}\;=\;T_{0}-\kappa_{1}

T_{1}
is a timelet whose all non-zero values are positive and different; however it is not normalized by the sum of its elements. Let
a_{2}=\min_{x\in\supp(T_{1})}T_{1}(x)
achieved on an element
x_{2}\in C
. Introduce

\kappa_{2}(x)\;=\;\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{if $x\not\in\supp(T_{1})$}\\
1&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

and define

T_{2}\;=\;T_{1}-\kappa_{2}

and so on. Finally we get

T_{N-1}=\kappa_{N-1}

where
N
is the cardinality of the causet
C
and the initial timelet
T
decomposes into a sum

T\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\kappa_{k}

(14)

Each step of this decomposition is unique and unambiguous, hence the whole decomposition (
14
) is unique.

That is, any generic timelet can be uniquely decomposed in this way into a convex combination of exactly
N-1
preclusive timelets.

The total simplex.

Given the linear span
\mathcal{L}
of the causet
C
, consider the simplex
\mathcal{S}
spanned on unit vectors associated with elements of
causet
, call it
total simplex

\mathcal{S}\;=\;\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid x_{i}\geq 0,\sum x_{i}=1\}

It follows from the above that each equivalence class (
13
) is associated with a unique point of
\mathcal{S}
, but not the vice versa. In the rest of the paper we explore the geometry of the set of so-normalized timelets. It follows from the third condition in (
13
) that the points of
\mathcal{S}
associated with normalized timelets reside on the faces of
\mathcal{S}
of dimension up to
N-1
, where
N
is the cardinality of the causet
C
. Viewing coevents as real-valued (not necessarily monotone) functions on
C
provides their natural representation in terms points of the simplex
\mathcal{S}
.

Barycentric representation of coevents.

Using the affine transformation (
13
) any coevent can be normalized as a timelet and given the form like
00\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{N}\ldots\frac{1}{N}0\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{N}
.

Statement 5

There is 1–1 correspondence between nonzero coevents and faces (and barycenters as well) of the simplex
\mathcal{S}
.

In fact, any coevent can be transformed into a point of the form
00\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{N}\ldots\frac{1}{N}0\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{N}
, which is a barycenter of the appropriate face of the simplex
\mathcal{S}
, while the faces of a simplex are comletely determined by their barycenters. Using this, from now on we will write for brevity

0011\ldots 1011\quad\simeq\quad 00\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{N}\ldots\frac{1}{N}0\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{N}

(15)

For a given normalized coevents
\kappa

on which
\kappa

support
of
\kappa

\supp\left(0011\ldots 1011\vphantom{\int}\right)\;=\;\supp\left(00\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{N}\ldots\frac{1}{N}0\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{N}\right)

Preclusive coevents are also have barycentric representation, denote the set of points of
\mathcal{S}
associated with preclusive by
\mathcal{P}

Simplicial representation of timelets.

So, every coevent is (associated with) a barycenter of some face of the total simplex
\mathcal{S}
. Any convex combination of timelets (and, in particular, preclusive coevents) is, in turn, a timelet. As it follows from the above, any timelet with nonnegative different values starting with zero can be uniquely decomposed over
N-1
points in the total simplex
\mathcal{S}
. So, all equivalence (with respect to the monotonic gauge) classes of timeles form a simplicial complex in the space
\mathcal{L}
spanned on the causal set
C
.

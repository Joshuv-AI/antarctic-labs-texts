Three-dimensional chiral Veselago lensing
S. Tchoumakov, J. Cayssol, A. G. Grushin

Abstract
The effect by which light focuses upon entering a medium with a negative refractive index, known as Veselago lensing, may enable optical imaging below the diffraction limit. Similarly, focusing electrons across a p ​ n pn -junction could realize a technologically promising electronic Veselago lens. However, its scope remains limited by the lack of three-dimensional platforms and its insensitivity to computational degrees of freedom, like spin or chirality. Here we propose a single-material three-dimensional electronic Veselago lens that selectively focuses electrons of a given chirality. Using the chiral anomaly of topological semimetals it is possible to create a sharp p ​ n pn -junction for a single chirality, a chiral Veselago lens, and tune it with a magnetic field to an ideal lensing condition. We estimate that chiral Veselago lensing is observable in non-local transport and spectroscopy experiments. In particular we show that the chiral Veselago lens leads to giant non-local magnetoresistance.

In optics, the trajectory of light is deflected at the interface between two media, allowing to focus, guide and disperse light controllably.

The prospect of creating useful devices based on electronic Veselago lenses faces three fundamental problems.
The first problem is that we lack a three-dimensional (3D) Veselago lens. Theoretically, a

The second problem is creating a clean interface. A

These three problems materialize in the challenge to realize a single-material 3D Veselago lens that could select specific electronic degrees of freedom.

In this work we take a significant step to solve these problems by proposing how to Veselago-lens a single chirality of 3D Weyl quasiparticles controllably.
The chirality is a quantum mechanical degree of freedom of 3D Weyl quasiparticles, labelled by

By locally activating the chiral anomaly, an effect that overpopulates one chirality with respect to the other in the presence of colinear electric and magnetic fields, it is possible to create a sharp

The resulting chiral Veselago lensing can be detected either by spectroscopic probes, like scanning-tunneling microscopy (STM), or non-local transport.

Because of the chiral anomaly, the lensing can be tuned with electric and magnetic fields to modulate the intensity of the image charge in STM or the image current in non-local transport. We also show that ordinary, quadratically dispersing electrons can Veselago-lens stronger than Weyl electrons, albeit without the degree of control offered by the chiral anomaly and suffering from a charging layer at the

Model.
Weyl semimetals host pairs of linearly dispersing bands separated in momentum space, known as Weyl cones. Each cone can be described by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{W}=(v_{F}{\bf k}-{\bf b})\cdot\hat{\bm{\sigma}}-\mu,

(1)

where
\bm{\sigma}
is a vector of Pauli matrices,
\mu
is the chemical potential,
v_{F}
is the Fermi velocity, and
{\bf b}
determines the momentum space separation between Weyl cones. The eigenstates of
\hat{H}_{W}
depend on the sign of
v_{F}
, which defines the chirality
\mathrm{sgn}(v_{F})=\chi=\pm
. Weyl cones come in pairs of opposite
\chi
and, in certain materials, they can be tilted
[
21
]
or anisotropic
[
17
]
. Here we focus on materials where these effects are negligible, as discussed at the end.

In presence of electric (
\mathbf{E}
) and magnetic (
{\bf B}
) fields, the transport of Weyl quasiparticles depends on their respective chirality, as reflected by the continuity equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial n_{\chi}}{\partial t}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\bf j}_{\chi}=\chi\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}\mathbf{E}\cdot{\bf B},

(2)

where
n_{\pm}
and
{\bf j}_{\pm}
are, respectively, the charge and current distributions of carriers with
\chi=\pm
. The source term, on the right-hand side, creates an imbalance
\Delta n
between the chiral charges
n_{+}
and
n_{-}
, without altering the total charge
n=n_{+}+n_{-}
. This term, known as the chiral anomaly, is responsible for anomalous transport responses, such as the enhancement of the magneto-conductance of Weyl semimetals
[
22
,
17
]
.

The chiral anomaly competes with inter-valley scattering, leading to a charge imbalance between Weyl nodes with opposite chiralities in the steady-state. The inter-valley scattering time
\tau
can range from a picosecond in TaAs to a nanosecond in Na
3
Bi
[
23
,
24
]
. The chiral charge imbalance
\Delta n=n_{+}-n_{-}=\frac{\tau e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}\mathbf{E}\cdot{\bf B}
equals the carrier density
n
for a magnetic field
B_{c}\equiv 2\mu^{3}/(3v^{3}e^{2}\tau E)
. For
n=10^{21}
cm
-3
,
\tau=10^{-9}
s
[
24
,
25
,
23
]
and
E\approx 10^{6}
V.m
-1
, this crossover field is
B_{c}=1
T. Beyond
B_{c}
, the chiral anomaly reverses the type of carriers of one of the Weyl nodes, e.g. from electrons to holes. Hence, if
B>B_{c}
is applied homogeneously and the
E
is not homogeneous, e.g. stepwise as illustrated in Fig.
1
, the chiral anomaly creates an effective
pn
junction for a single Weyl chirality.

The main goal of this work is to explain how to realize and detect a single-chirality Veselago lens, under the above conditions. To this end, we discuss spectroscopy and non-local transport responses of a single Weyl cone to determine the ideal conditions for single-chirality Veselago lensing. Combining the contributions from the two chiralities we discuss how chiral Veselago lensing can be detected using the simple set-up in Fig.
1

[
20

we propose two related, albeit less practical, devices to detect directly chiral Veselago lensing in spectroscopy and in non-local transport.

We model the change in the carrier density with a step-wise chemical potential, where
\mu_{L}=\left(\mu^{3}\pm\frac{3}{2}v^{3}e^{2}\tau\mathbf{E}\cdot{\bf B}\right)^{1/3}

Spectroscopic signatures of chiral Veselago lensing.

To visualize and understand the ideal conditions for Veselago lensing, we calculate the charge distribution created by a local potential due to an impurity, or the tip of a STM.
For a local potential

\displaystyle\Pi(z,z^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d\omega~{\rm Tr}\left(\hat{G}(z,z^{\prime})\hat{G}(z^{\prime},z)\right),

(3)

We compare the case of a single Weyl chirality with a 3D electron gas, with a quadratic band structure

In a

However, a drawback of using a 3D electron gas is that the electron density accross the

While the image charge is convenient to understand how Veselago lensing can be enhanced (see Supplemental Material

A bulk, non-local transport measurement, such as that depicted in Fig.

As with the image Veselago charge, the image Veselago current is larger for a 3D electron gas than for a Weyl semimetal, where it oscillates close to zero, but negative on average. These oscillations are absent in the 3D electron gas which is a single-band model, and thus we attribute them to interband excitations that lead to hole-like and electron-like regions. Lastly, we find that ideal lensing is achieved when both sides of the

3D Chiral Veselago lens.

We have previously considered the transport of each chirality individually, while in the set-up of Fig.

Discussion.
We have determined how the chiral anomaly of Weyl semimetals can be used to create a Veselago lens that distinguishes electrons by their chirality. We have uncovered that the chiral Veselago lens based on Weyl semimetals is advantageous for several reasons. First, a chiral Veselago lens eliminates the charge build up at the interface, since it requires a single material, and the total charge remains constant along the sample. Second, the chiral anomaly allows to control the
k_{F}
of each chirality using a magnetic field. This allows to detect chiral Veselago lensing by measuring non-local conductivity and tune it to the ideal condition for lensing. This property goes beyond current graphene-based proposals, since the chiral anomaly is unique to 3D Weyl semimetals.

We have neglected several effects in our computations. First, we neglected the orbital localization in a magnetic field, since this occurs over the magnetic length scale
\ell_{B}\approx 25.6
nm
/\sqrt{B{\rm[T]}}\gg z,\lambda_{F}
. Second, tilted Weyl cones
[
21
]
could prevent a perfect matching of
k_{F}
. However, the chiral anomaly is often observed in systems where the Weyl cones are generated by magnetic field from a lightly doped semimetal, e.g. GdPtBi, ZrTe
5
, or KZnBi
[
40
,
41
,
17
,
42
]
where the effect of tilting is negligible. Lastly, Fermi arcs will be negligible in non-local transport, as it is a bulk probe.

The estimates we provide indicate that chiral Veselago lensing is observable under moderate electric and magnetic fields. Non-local transport experiments have probed the chiral anomaly, albeit without
pn
-junctions and lensing
[
23
,
43
]
, suggesting that our proposal can be realized using current technology. Our work opens the possibility to realize a device that can control a computational degree of freedom using Veselago lensing in 3D materials, enabled by the chiral anomaly.

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\frac{k^{2}}{2m}-\mu,

(6)

Since the mass term is also position dependent, we symmetrize this Hamiltonian and use

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\left(\frac{1}{m(z)}\nabla\right)-\mu.

\displaystyle G_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z,z^{\prime})=G^{\infty}_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z-z^{\prime})+RG^{\infty}_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z+z^{\prime}),

(15)

\displaystyle G_{R}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z,z^{\prime})=TG^{\infty}_{R}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z-z^{\prime}),

(16)

with
R
and
T
constants in
z^{\prime}
but functions in
z
. The boundary condition (
14
) then implies

\displaystyle G^{\infty}_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z)+RG^{\infty}_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z)=TG^{\infty}_{R}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z)

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{L}}\partial_{z}G^{\infty}_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z)-R\frac{1}{m_{L}}\partial_{z}G^{\infty}_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z)=T\frac{1}{m_{R}}\partial_{z}G^{\infty}_{R}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z),

(18)

which leads to

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{L}^{\infty}/G_{L}^{\infty}-J_{R}^{\infty}/G_{R}^{\infty}}{J_{L}^{\infty}/G_{L}^{\infty}+J_{R}^{\infty}/G_{R}^{\infty}},

(19)

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\frac{2J_{L}^{\infty}/G_{R}^{\infty}}{J_{R}^{\infty}/G_{R}^{\infty}+J_{L}^{\infty}/G_{L}^{\infty}},

(20)

where we introduce the current function
J^{\infty}=\frac{1}{m}\partial_{z}G^{\infty}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z)
. More explicitly this leads to reflection and transmission coefficients that remind of Fresnel coefficients in optics

\displaystyle R=\frac{q_{L}(k_{\parallel})/|m_{L}|-q_{R}(k_{\parallel})/|m_{R}|}{q_{L}(k_{\parallel})/|m_{L}|+q_{R}(k_{\parallel})/|m_{R}|},

(21)

\displaystyle T=\frac{2q_{R}(k_{\parallel})/|m_{R}|}{q_{L}(k_{\parallel})/|m_{L}|+q_{R}(k_{\parallel})/|m_{R}|}e^{i{\rm sign}(\omega_{n})({\rm sign}(m_{L})q_{L}(k_{\parallel})-{\rm sign}(m_{R})q_{R}(k_{\parallel}))|z|},

(22)

with
q_{i}(k_{\parallel})=\sqrt{2m_{i}(\mu_{i}+i\omega_{n})-k_{\parallel}^{2}}
.

We obtain the Green’s function for
z>0
following the same procedure and obtain similar expression with a permutation of the
L\leftrightarrow R
indices.

We compute Friedel oscillations and non-local conductivity by performing a numerical Fourier transform of the above Green’s function, which is in momentum space, to have the Green’s function in real space. In order to simplify the calculation, we perform part of the Fourier transform analytically, using rotation invariance, for example for
z<0
we have

\displaystyle G_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf r},z,z^{\prime})=G^{\infty,1}(i\omega_{n},{\bf r},z-z^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int dk_{\parallel}k_{\parallel}RG^{\infty,1}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z+z^{\prime})J_{0}(kr_{\parallel}),

(23)

\displaystyle G_{R}(i\omega_{n},{\bf r},z,z^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int dk_{\parallel}k_{\parallel}TG^{\infty,2}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z-z^{\prime})J_{0}(kr_{\parallel}).

(24)

A.3
Surface states

We can look for the surface states solution as poles of the Green’s function and, more specifically, as poles of the reflection and transmission coefficients. The denominators of
R
and
T
are proportional to

\displaystyle D(i\omega_{n},k_{\parallel})=\sqrt{2m_{L}(\mu_{L}+i\omega_{n})-k_{\parallel}^{2}}/|m_{L}|+\sqrt{2m_{R}(\mu_{R}+i\omega_{n})-k_{\parallel}^{2}}/|m_{R}|,

(25)

and the poles are obtained as a solution to
D(\omega+i0^{+},k_{\parallel})=0
. The solution is obtained only if the argument of both square roots is negative and with opposite imaginary parts, that is if we satisfy the two conditions

\bullet

{\rm sgn}(m_{L}m_{R})<0
: we have a
pn
junction,

\bullet

2m_{L}(\mu_{L}+\omega)<k_{\parallel}^{2}
and
2m_{R}(\mu_{R}+\omega)<k_{\parallel}^{2}
: which does not imply any condition on
\mu_{L}
and
\mu_{R}
.

Then one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{k_{\parallel}^{2}-2m_{L}(\mu_{L}+\omega)}{m_{L}^{2}}=\frac{k_{\parallel}^{2}-2m_{R}(\mu_{R}+\omega)}{m_{R}^{2}}

(26)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\omega_{k_{\parallel}}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{m_{L}}+\frac{1}{m_{R}}\bigg)k_{\parallel}^{2}-\frac{\mu_{R}/m_{R}-\mu_{L}/m_{L}}{1/m_{R}-1/m_{L}}.

(27)

We observe that we have a flat band behaviour (no dispersion of the surface states, diverging density of states) if
m_{L}=-m_{R}
, which is the case of perfect Veselago lensing, so the surface states do not disperse. Note that the effective chemical potential of surface states is an average of the chemical potential on each side of the interface.

The consistency condition for a pole in
R
and
T
is valid only for in-plane wavevector such that

\displaystyle k_{\parallel}^{2}>{\rm min}\bigg(m_{L}\frac{\mu_{L}/m_{L}-\mu_{R}/m_{R}}{1/m_{L}-1/m_{R}}-2\mu_{L},-m_{R}\frac{\mu_{L}/m_{L}-\mu_{R}/m_{R}}{1/m_{L}-1/m_{R}}-2\mu_{R}\bigg),

(28)

so the spectrum of surface states has a hole at small wavevectors. Also the decay of surface states in each bulk is given by
q_{1,2}(\omega)=\sqrt{2m_{1,2}(\mu_{1,2}+\omega_{k_{\parallel}}+i0^{+})-k_{\parallel}^{2}}
, with opposite signs in each bulk.

Appendix B
Green’s function for a junction between two Weyl semimetals

We consider a Dirac electron gas with Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{W}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}v_{i}(k_{i}-\delta k_{i})\hat{\sigma}_{i}-\mu,

(29)

which depends on the three Dirac velocities
v_{x},v_{y},v_{z}
, the three momentum shifts
\delta k_{x},\delta k_{y},\delta k_{z}
and the chemical potential
\mu
. Note that in this equation we relate units of time and energy by setting
\hbar=1
. Here, we define the current operator as

\displaystyle\hat{J}=ev_{F}\Psi^{\dagger}({\bf r})\hat{\sigma}\Psi({\bf r}).

(30)

B.1
Bulk Green’s function

It is useful to write three forms of the Green’s function: in momentum space
G^{\infty}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k})
, in real space
G^{\infty}(i\omega_{n},{\bf x},z)
and in mixed representation
G^{\infty}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},k_{z})
. As for the 3D electron gas we define the mixed representation by considering momentum space along the interface and real space transverse to it. The
\infty
symbol on the Green’s functions is used to remind that it correspond to the solution for a bulk sample, where parameters are constant over all space.

(i)

in momentum space

\displaystyle G^{\infty}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k};m,\mu)=\frac{\mu+i\omega_{n}+\hat{H}_{0}}{\sum_{i}h_{i}^{2}-(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}}.

(31)

(ii)

in real space

\displaystyle G^{\infty}(i\omega_{n},{\bf r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}{\bf k}~\frac{\mu+i\omega_{n}+\hat{H}_{0}}{\sum_{i}h_{i}^{2}-(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}

(32)

\displaystyle=(\mu+i\omega_{n})I_{0}({\bf r})\hat{\mathbbm{1}}+v_{x}(-i\partial_{x}-\delta k_{x})I_{0}({\bf r})\hat{\sigma}_{x}+v_{y}(-i\partial_{y}-\delta k_{y})I_{0}({\bf r})\hat{\sigma}_{y}-iv_{z}\partial_{z}I_{0}({\bf r})\hat{\sigma}_{z},

(33)

with

\displaystyle I_{0}({\bf r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}{\bf k}~\frac{e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}}{\sum_{i}h_{i}^{2}-(\mu+i\omega_{n})^{2}}=\frac{e^{i(\delta k_{x}x+\delta k_{y}y)}}{4\pi|v_{x}v_{y}v_{z}|\tilde{r}}e^{i{\rm sgn}(\omega_{n})(\mu+i\omega_{n})\tilde{r}},

(34)

where
\tilde{r}=\sqrt{(x/v_{x})^{2}+(y/v_{y})^{2}+(z/v_{z})^{2}}
. From this we obtain,

\displaystyle G^{\infty}(i\omega_{n},{\bf r})=\left[(\mu+i\omega_{n})\hat{\mathbbm{1}}+\frac{i}{\tilde{r}^{2}}\left(1-i{\tilde{r}}{\rm sgn}(\omega_{n})(\mu+i\omega_{n})\right){\tilde{\bf r}\cdot\hat{\sigma}}\right]I_{0}({\bf r}).

(35)

(iii)

in mixed representation

\displaystyle G^{\infty}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}dk_{z}~\frac{i\omega_{n}+\hat{H}_{D}}{\sum_{i}v_{i}^{2}k_{i}^{2}-(\mu+i\omega_{n})^{2}}e^{ik_{z}z}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}dk_{z}~\frac{\mu+i\omega_{n}+v_{x}k_{x}\hat{\sigma}_{x}+v_{y}k_{y}\hat{\sigma}_{y}+v_{z}k_{z}\hat{\sigma}_{z}}{v_{z}^{2}k_{z}^{2}-((\mu+i\omega_{n})^{2}-(v_{x}^{2}k_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2}k_{y}^{2}))}e^{ik_{z}z},

(36)

which gives

\displaystyle G^{\infty}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z)=\frac{i}{2v_{z}}\left(\frac{(\mu+i\omega_{n})+v_{x}k_{x}\hat{\sigma}_{x}+v_{y}k_{y}\hat{\sigma}_{y}}{v_{z}q({\bf k}_{\parallel})}+{\rm sgn}(z)\hat{\sigma}_{z}\right)e^{iq({\bf k}_{\parallel})|z|},

(37)

where
q({\bf k}_{\parallel})={\rm sgn}(\omega_{n}\mu)\sqrt{(\mu+i\omega_{n})^{2}-(v_{x}^{2}k_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2}k_{y}^{2})}\big/|v_{z}|
.

B.2
Junction Green’s function

In the case the location of cones in momentum space and the chemical potential change abruptly at
z=0
, from
(v_{L},\delta{\bf k}_{L},\mu_{L})
for
z<0
to
(v_{R},\delta{\bf k}_{R},\mu_{R})
for
z>0
, the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{W}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}v_{i}(k_{i}-\delta k_{i}(z))\hat{\sigma}_{i}-\mu(z),

(38)

can be studied separately for
z<0
and
z>0
. For this reason, we introduce the Green’s functions
G_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z,z^{\prime})
and
G_{R}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z,z^{\prime})
defined for
z^{\prime}<0
and
z^{\prime}>0
respectively,
i.e.
on the left and right side of the junction.

The integration of the Weyl equation (
38
) on a small segment around the interface, at
z=0
, leads to the following boundary condition between
G^{L}
and
G^{R}

\displaystyle G^{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z,0)=G^{R}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z,0).

(39)

The Green’s function of the junction can be expanded over the bulk Green’s functions,
G_{L}^{\infty}
and
G_{R}^{\infty}
, that are respectively the Green’s functions for a bulk material with parameters
(v_{L},\delta{\bf k}_{L},\mu_{L})
and
(v_{R},\delta{\bf k}_{R},\mu_{R})
. This is similar to method of images in electromagnetism and we use it to satisfy the boundary condition (
39
). In the following we illustrate this expansion for
z<0

\displaystyle G_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z,z^{\prime})=G^{\infty}_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z-z^{\prime})+\hat{R}_{-}G^{\infty}_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z+z^{\prime})=G^{\infty}_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z-z^{\prime})+G^{\infty}_{L}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z+z^{\prime})\hat{R}_{+},

(40)

\displaystyle G_{R}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z,z^{\prime})=\hat{T}_{-}G^{\infty}_{R}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z-z^{\prime})=G^{\infty}_{R}(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z-z^{\prime})\hat{T}_{+},

(41)

where we introduce the reflection and transmission operators
\hat{R}_{\pm}
and
\hat{T}_{\pm}
. We introduce the notation
\pm
in order to account for the fact that, in general,
\hat{R}
and
\hat{T}
do not commute with
G^{\infty}
.

We simplify our calculation by expanding the reflection and transmission operators as

\displaystyle R_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0\pm}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}+\alpha_{1\pm}{\bf h}_{\parallel,L}\cdot\hat{\sigma},

(42)

\displaystyle T_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\big[\beta_{0\pm}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}+\beta_{1\pm}{\bf h}_{\parallel,R}\cdot\hat{\sigma}\big]\frac{v_{z,L}}{v_{z,R}}e^{i(q_{L}({\bf k}_{\parallel})-q_{R}({\bf k}_{\parallel}))z},

(43)

where (
\alpha_{0\pm},\alpha_{1\pm},\beta_{0\pm},\beta_{1\pm})
are solve from the boundary condition (
39
). Here we also introduce
{\bf h}_{\parallel}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}v_{i}(k_{i}-\delta k_{i}){\bf e}_{i}
, the in-plane component of the Hamiltonian.

The boundary condition is satisfied for the following set of coefficients

\displaystyle\alpha_{0+}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\tilde{\bf h}_{L}-\tilde{\bf h}_{R})\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}-\tilde{q}_{R}i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}{\tilde{\bf h}_{L}^{2}\tilde{\bf h}_{R}^{2}-(1+\tilde{q}_{L}\tilde{q}_{R})\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}+(\tilde{q}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{R})i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}\tilde{\bf h}_{L}^{2}v_{z,L},

(44)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1+}

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{q_{L}(0)}\frac{(\tilde{\bf h}_{L}-\tilde{\bf h}_{R})\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}-\tilde{q}_{R}i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}{\tilde{\bf h}_{L}^{2}\tilde{\bf h}_{R}^{2}-(1+\tilde{q}_{L}\tilde{q}_{R})\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}+(\tilde{q}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{R})i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}\tilde{\bf q}_{L}v_{z,L},

(45)

\displaystyle\beta_{0+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\tilde{q}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{R})\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}-(1+\tilde{q}_{L}\tilde{q}_{R})i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}{\tilde{\bf h}_{L}^{2}\tilde{\bf h}_{R}^{2}-(1+\tilde{q}_{L}\tilde{q}_{R})\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}+(\tilde{q}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{R})i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}\tilde{\bf q}_{R}v_{z,R},

(46)

\displaystyle\beta_{1+}

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{q_{R}(0)}\frac{(\tilde{\bf h}_{L}-\tilde{\bf h}_{R})\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{L}i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}{\tilde{\bf h}_{L}^{2}\tilde{\bf h}_{R}^{2}-(1+\tilde{q}_{L}\tilde{q}_{R})\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}+(\tilde{q}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{R})i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}\tilde{\bf q}_{R}v_{z,R},

(47)

or for

\displaystyle\alpha_{0-}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\tilde{\bf h}_{L}-\tilde{\bf h}_{R})\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}+\tilde{q}_{R}i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}{\tilde{\bf h}_{L}^{2}\tilde{\bf h}_{R}^{2}-(1+\tilde{q}_{L}\tilde{q}_{R})\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}-(\tilde{q}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{R})i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}\tilde{\bf h}_{L}^{2}v_{z,L},

(48)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1-}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{q_{L}(0)}\frac{(\tilde{\bf h}_{L}-\tilde{\bf h}_{R})\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}+\tilde{q}_{R}i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}{\tilde{\bf h}_{L}^{2}\tilde{\bf h}_{R}^{2}-(1+\tilde{q}_{L}\tilde{q}_{R})\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}-(\tilde{q}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{R})i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}\tilde{\bf q}_{L}v_{z,L},

(49)

\displaystyle\beta_{0-}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\tilde{q}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{R})\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}+(1+\tilde{q}_{L}\tilde{q}_{R})i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}{\tilde{\bf h}_{L}^{2}\tilde{\bf h}_{R}^{2}-(1+\tilde{q}_{L}\tilde{q}_{R})\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}-(\tilde{q}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{R})i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}\tilde{\bf q}_{R}v_{z,R}

(50)

\displaystyle\beta_{1-}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{q_{R}(0)}\frac{(\tilde{\bf h}_{L}-\tilde{\bf h}_{R})\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{L}-\tilde{q}_{L}i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}{\tilde{\bf h}_{L}^{2}\tilde{\bf h}_{R}^{2}-(1+\tilde{q}_{L}\tilde{q}_{R})\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\cdot\tilde{\bf h}_{R}-(\tilde{q}_{L}+\tilde{q}_{R})i\tilde{\bf h}_{L}\times\tilde{\bf h}_{R}}\tilde{\bf q}_{R}v_{z,R}.

(51)

where the tilde notation implies that a quantity is normalized by
q_{0,L/R}=\mu_{L/R}+i\omega_{n}
(for example
\tilde{\bf h}_{L}={\bf h}_{L}/q_{0,L}
or
\tilde{\bf h}_{R}={\bf h}_{R}/q_{0,R}
). We also use the simplified notation
{\bf h}_{L}\times{\bf h}_{R}\equiv({\bf h}_{L}\times{\bf h}_{R})\cdot{\bf e}_{z}
. All these expressions are obtained for
z<0
; if instead
z>0
then we should switch

.

The Fourier transforms of

to position-space are performed numerically and we use the expressions above to draw Figs. 2 and 3.

B.3
Smooth junction Green’s function in the WKB approximation

In the previous section we solved the Green’s function of an abrupt interface between regions with different parameter
(v,\delta{\bf k},\mu)
in the Hamiltonian (
29
). We now consider the situation where the parameters smoothly vary from
(v,\delta{\bf k},\mu)_{L}
for
z\rightarrow-\infty
to
(v,\delta{\bf k},\mu)_{R}
for
z\rightarrow\infty
. The parameters are supposed to be only
z
dependent and in the following we solve for the Green’s function of the Weyl equation (
29
) in the WKB approximation following the method in
[
46
]
.

We start by introducing the inverse of the thermal Green’s function with smoothly varying parameters

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=h_{x}(z)\hat{\sigma}_{x}+h_{y}(z)\hat{\sigma}_{y}+\bigg(-iv(z)\partial_{z}-\frac{i}{2}v^{\prime}(z)-\delta k_{z}(z)\bigg)\hat{\sigma}_{z}-(\mu(z)+i\omega_{n}+i{\rm sign}(\omega_{n})/(2\tau(z)))\hat{\mathbbm{1}},

(52)

to which we search for an eigensolution of the form
\psi(z)=\Psi(z)e^{iS(z)}
where the amplitude
\Psi(z)
varies slowly compared to
e^{iS(z)}
. This then satisfies

\displaystyle\left(h_{x}(z)\hat{\sigma}_{x}+h_{y}(z)\hat{\sigma}_{y}+v(z)\bigg(k-\delta k_{z}(z)\bigg)\hat{\sigma}_{z}-(\tilde{\mu}(z)+\lambda)\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\right)\Psi(z)=0,

(53)

where
\tilde{\mu}(z)=\mu(z)+i\omega_{n}+i{\rm sign}(\omega_{n})/(2\tau(z))
,
k=\partial S/\partial z
. We can diagonalize this equation on the states
\phi_{\sigma=\pm}
defined by the spinors

\displaystyle\phi_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\left(1+\sigma\frac{h_{z}}{\delta h}\right)^{1/2}\\
\sigma\left(1-\sigma\frac{h_{z}}{\delta h}\right)^{1/2}e^{i\theta},\end{array}\right)

with
e^{i\theta}=(h_{x}+ih_{y})/|h_{\parallel}|
which leads to the projectors

\displaystyle\phi_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(z^{\prime})\phi_{\sigma}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(1+\sigma\frac{h_{z}}{\delta h}\right)^{1/2}\left(1+\sigma\frac{h_{z}^{\prime}}{\delta h^{\prime}}\right)^{1/2}&\sigma\left(1+\sigma\frac{h_{z}}{\delta h}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\sigma\frac{h_{z}^{\prime}}{\delta h^{\prime}}\right)^{1/2}e^{-i\theta^{\prime}}\\
\sigma\left(1-\sigma\frac{h_{z}}{\delta h}\right)^{1/2}\left(1+\sigma\frac{h_{z}^{\prime}}{\delta h^{\prime}}\right)^{1/2}e^{i\theta}&\left(1-\sigma\frac{h_{z}}{\delta h}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\sigma\frac{h_{z}^{\prime}}{\delta h^{\prime}}\right)^{1/2}e^{i(\theta-\theta^{\prime})}\end{array}\right),

where
\delta h=(h_{\parallel}^{2}+h_{z}^{2})^{1/2}
with
h_{z}=v_{z}(\partial_{z}S-\delta k_{z})
. We then have for each eigensolution of Eq. (
53
)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S_{\sigma}}{\partial z}=k_{\sigma}=\delta k_{z}(z)+\frac{h(z)}{v_{z}(z)}=\delta k_{z}+\frac{1}{v_{z}}\left((\tilde{\mu}+\lambda_{\sigma})-h_{\parallel}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(58)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle S(z)=\int^{z}_{z_{0}}du~\left(\delta k_{z}(u)+\frac{1}{v_{z}(u)}((\tilde{\mu}(u)+\lambda_{\sigma})^{2}-h_{\parallel}^{2}(u))^{1/2}\right).

(59)

The phase factor
S(z)
is defined up to a constant of integration, associated to the starting position
z_{0}
, and that we replace with parameter

\displaystyle a_{\sigma}\equiv k_{\sigma}(z_{0})=\delta k_{0}+\frac{1}{v_{z,0}}((\tilde{\mu}_{0}+\lambda_{\sigma})^{2}-h_{\parallel,0}^{2})^{1/2},

(60)

and write the eigenvalues
\lambda_{\sigma}
as a function of
a_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\lambda_{\sigma}=-\tilde{\mu}_{0}+\sigma\delta h_{0}=-\tilde{\mu}_{0}+\sigma(h_{x,0}^{2}+h_{y,0}^{2}+v_{z,0}^{2}(a_{\sigma}^{2}-\delta k_{0})^{2})^{1/2}.

(61)

In this approximation we discard the trajectories that cycle,
i.e.
the trajectory is uniquely defined by the parameter
a
. Also, since there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\lambda
and
a
, we can use either parameter to define the trajectory.

It can be shown that the amplitude
\Psi(z)
is given by the van Vleck determinant, usually related to the local density of states of semiclassical trajectories
[
46
]
. When parametrizing the trajectory with the eigenvalue
\lambda
, we thus have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\sigma}(z)=\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial z\partial\lambda_{\sigma}}\right)^{1/2}=\left(\frac{1}{v_{z}(z)}\frac{\tilde{\mu}(z)+\lambda_{\sigma}}{\left((\tilde{\mu}(z)+\lambda_{\sigma})^{2}-h_{\parallel}^{2}(z)\right)^{1/2}}\right)^{1/2}.

(62)

Then the WKB Green’s function is obtained by integrating the previous solution over all eigenvalues
\lambda

\displaystyle G(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z,z^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\int\frac{d\lambda_{\sigma}}{2\pi}~\frac{1}{\lambda_{\sigma}}\phi_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(z^{\prime})\phi_{\sigma}(z)\Psi_{\sigma}(z^{\prime})\Psi_{\sigma}(z)e^{i\int^{z^{\prime}}_{z}du~\left(\delta k_{z}(u)+\frac{1}{v_{z}(u)}((\tilde{\mu}(u)+\lambda_{\sigma})^{2}-h_{\parallel}^{2}(u))^{1/2}\right)},

(63)

where the factor
1/2
appears because we consider a bi-spinor
[
46
]
. We can compute the above integral with the Cauchy theorem by choosing a contour such that the integrrand is negligible at large
|\lambda|
, that is such that
{\rm Im}(S(\lambda))>0\implies\partial S/\partial z\sim{\rm sign}((z^{\prime}-z)v_{z}(u)\mu(u)\omega_{n})
. We obtain

\displaystyle G(i\omega_{n},{\bf k}_{\parallel},z,z^{\prime})=\frac{i}{2}e^{i\int_{z}^{z^{\prime}}du~\left(\delta k_{z}(u)+{\rm sign}\left[(z^{\prime}-z)\omega_{n}v_{z}(u)\mu(u)\right]\frac{1}{v_{z}(u)}\left(\tilde{\mu}^{2}(u)-h_{\parallel}^{2}(u)\right)^{1/2}\right)}\times

(64)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(a(z,z^{\prime})/v_{z}^{\prime}\right)^{1/2}\left(b(z,z^{\prime})/v_{z}\right)^{1/2}&\left(1/(a(z,z^{\prime})v_{z}^{\prime}h_{z}^{\prime 2})\right)^{1/2}\left(b(z,z^{\prime})/{v_{z}}\right)^{1/2}\left(h_{x}^{\prime}-ih_{y}^{\prime}\right)\\
\left(a(z,z^{\prime})/v_{z}^{\prime}\right)^{1/2}\left(1/(b(z,z^{\prime})v_{z}h_{z}^{2})\right)^{1/2}\left(h_{x}+ih_{y}\right)&\left(1/(a(z,z^{\prime})v_{z}^{\prime}h_{z}^{\prime 2})\right)^{1/2}\left(1/(b(z,z^{\prime})v_{z}h_{z}^{2})\right)^{1/2}\left(h_{x}^{\prime}-ih_{y}^{\prime}\right)\left(h_{x}+ih_{y}\right)\end{array}\right),

where

\displaystyle h_{z}(z)={\rm sign}(\omega_{n}\mu(z)v_{z}(z))\left(\tilde{\mu}^{2}(z)-h_{\parallel}^{2}(z)\right)^{1/2},

(67)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}(z)=\mu(z)+i\omega_{n}+i{\rm sign}(\omega_{n})/(2\tau(z)),

(68)

\displaystyle a(z,z^{\prime})=\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{z^{\prime}}}{h_{z}^{\prime}}+{\rm sign}(z^{\prime}-z),

(69)

\displaystyle b(z,z^{\prime})=\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{z}}{h_{z}}+{\rm sign}(z^{\prime}-z).

(70)

Note that we cannot further simplify these expressions since the square root has a branch cut on
(-\infty,0]
so
\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}
is only defined if

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lc}{\rm(1):}&{\rm Im}(a){\rm Im}(b)\leq 0,\\
{\rm(2):}&{\rm Im}(a){\rm Im}(b)>0~{\rm and}~{\rm Im}(a){\rm Im}(ab)>0.\end{array}

Appendix C
Current response in the bulk

In this section we compute the non-local conductivity in various situation for a planar electric field
{\bf E}({\bf x})={\bf E}_{0}\delta(z-z_{0})
and measuring the current in a plane
j_{\mu}({\bf x})=j_{\mu}(z_{1})\delta(z-z_{1})
, such that
j_{\mu}(z_{1})=\sigma_{\mu\mu}(z_{1},z_{0})E_{0\mu}
.

C.1
Non-local conductivity and time-reversal symmetry

The conductivity tensor can always be decomposed over a symmetric,
\sigma_{1}
, and an antisymmetric,
\sigma_{2}
, component with respect to time-reversal symmetry
[
38
]

\displaystyle\sigma_{1,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{0}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}d\epsilon~\left(-\frac{df}{d\epsilon}\right){\rm Tr}\left[\hat{J}_{\mu}\left(G_{+}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})-G_{-}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\right)\hat{J}_{\nu}\left(G_{+}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})-G_{-}({\bf x},{\bf x})\right)\right]

(73)

\displaystyle\sigma_{2,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}d\epsilon~\left(-\frac{df}{d\epsilon}\right)\left\{{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{J}_{\mu}G_{+}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\hat{J}_{\nu}G_{-}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})\right]-{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{J}_{\mu}G_{-}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\hat{J}_{\nu}G_{+}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})\right]\right\}

\displaystyle-\sigma_{0}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}d\epsilon~f(\epsilon)\left\{{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{J}_{\mu}\partial_{\epsilon}G_{+}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\hat{J}_{\nu}G_{+}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})\right]+{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{J}_{\mu}G_{-}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\hat{J}_{\nu}\partial_{\epsilon}G_{-}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})\right]\right.~

\displaystyle\left.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{J}_{\mu}\partial_{\epsilon}G_{-}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\hat{J}_{\nu}G_{-}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})\right]-{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{J}_{\mu}G_{+}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\hat{J}_{\nu}\partial_{\epsilon}G_{+}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})\right]\right\},

(74)

with
G_{\pm}=(\hat{H}\pm i0^{+})^{-1}
. In general
\sigma_{2}
is not zero but one can check that, for
\mu=\nu
, there is the symmetry

\displaystyle\sigma_{2,\mu\mu}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})=-\sigma_{2,\mu\mu}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x}),

(75)

so

\displaystyle\sigma_{1,\mu\mu}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma_{\mu\mu}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})+\sigma_{\mu\mu}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})\right).

(76)

That is, longitudinal components of the conductivity tensor can be obtained by averaging the longitudinal conductivities obtained when permuting the two probes. In the main text and below we focus on
\sigma_{1,\mu\mu}
and drop the reference to the index
1
.

C.2
Kubo formalism

The expression of the non-local conductivity is obtained in the Kubo formalism in
[
37
,
38
]
. In the situation of a planar electric field
{\bf E}({\bf x})={\bf E}_{0}\delta(z-z_{0})
and measuring the current in a plane
j_{\mu}({\bf x})=j_{\mu}(z_{1})\delta(z-z_{1})
, such that
j_{\mu}(z_{1})=\sigma_{\mu\mu}(z_{1},z_{0})E_{0\mu}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mu\nu}(z,z^{\prime})=\int\dfrac{dS_{z}dS_{z^{\prime}}}{\pi\mathcal{A}}{\rm Tr}\left(\hat{j}_{\mu}{\rm Im}\hat{G}(z,z^{\prime})\hat{j}_{\nu}{\rm Im}\hat{G}(z,z^{\prime})\right).

(77)

where the current operator for the Weyl equation is
\hat{\bf j}=v(z)\hat{\bm{\sigma}}
and where
{\rm Im}\hat{G}(z,z^{\prime})\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{G}(i0^{+},{\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime})-\hat{G}(i0^{-},{\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime})\right)
with

\displaystyle\hat{G}(i0^{+},{\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime})-\hat{G}(i0^{-},{\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4\pi v^{2}|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle\left[\tilde{\mu}e^{i\tilde{\mu}|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|/|v|}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}-\tilde{\mu}^{*}e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|/|v|}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}+i\frac{v({\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime})}{|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|^{2}}\cdot\hat{\sigma}\left(e^{i\tilde{\mu}|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|/|v|}-e^{-i\tilde{\mu}|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|/|v|}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+{\rm sign}(v)\frac{{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}}{|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|}\cdot\hat{\sigma}\left(\tilde{\mu}e^{i\tilde{\mu}|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|/|v|}+\tilde{\mu}^{*}e^{-i\tilde{\mu}|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|/|v|}\right)\right],

(78)

where we use the notation
\tilde{\mu}=\mu+i/(2\tau)
.
Since the Green’s function is translation invariant in the
(x,y)
plane, we can easily get rid of one of the surface integrals in Eq. (
77
). We perform the second surface integral in polar coordinates and obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}\pi}\int\frac{dR}{R}d\theta~\left[\left(\tilde{\mu}e^{i\tilde{\mu}R/|v|}-\tilde{\mu}^{*}e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}R/|v|}\right)^{2}+\frac{-X^{2}+Y^{2}+Z^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\left(\tilde{\mu}+\frac{i|v|}{R}\right)e^{i\tilde{\mu}R/|v|}+\left(\tilde{\mu}^{*}-\frac{i|v|}{R}\right)e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}R/|v|}\right)^{2}\right].

(79)

where
(X,Y,Z)={\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}
and
X=R\cos(\theta)
,
Y=R\cos(\theta)
. This expression shows that
\sigma_{xx}=\sigma_{yy}
so we can write
\sigma_{xx}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy})
and get rid of the angular integral by removing the
X^{2}-Y^{2}
term. We then have

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}}\int\frac{dR}{R}~\left[\left(\tilde{\mu}e^{i\tilde{\mu}R/|v|}-\tilde{\mu}^{*}e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}R/|v|}\right)^{2}+\frac{Z^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\left(\tilde{\mu}+\frac{i|v|}{R}\right)e^{i\tilde{\mu}R/|v|}+\left(\tilde{\mu}^{*}-\frac{i|v|}{R}\right)e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}R/|v|}\right)^{2}\right]

(80)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u}~\left[\left(\tilde{\mu}e^{i\tilde{\mu}Zu/|v|}-\tilde{\mu}^{*}e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Zu/|v|}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{u^{2}}\left(\left(\tilde{\mu}+\frac{i|v|}{Zu}\right)e^{i\tilde{\mu}Zu/|v|}+\left(\tilde{\mu}^{*}-\frac{i|v|}{Zu}\right)e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Zu/|v|}\right)^{2}\right]

(81)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}{du}~\left(\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{2}e^{i(2\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|)u}+\tilde{\mu}^{*2}e^{i(2\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|)u}-2|\tilde{\mu}|^{2}e^{i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|u}}{u}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{2}e^{i(2\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|)u}+\tilde{\mu}^{*2}e^{i(2\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|)u}+2|\tilde{\mu}|^{2}e^{i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|u}}{u^{3}}+\frac{2i|v|}{Z}\frac{\tilde{\mu}e^{2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|u}-\tilde{\mu}^{*}e^{-2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|u}+(\tilde{\mu}^{*}-\tilde{\mu})e^{i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|u}}{u^{4}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{v^{2}}{Z^{2}}\frac{e^{2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|u}+e^{-2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|u}-2e^{i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|u}}{u^{5}}\right)

(82)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}}\left[\tilde{\mu}^{2}(E_{1}(-2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|)+E_{3}(-2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|))+\tilde{\mu}^{*2}(E_{1}(2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|)+E_{3}(2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|))\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2|\tilde{\mu}|^{2}(E_{3}(-i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|)-E_{1}(-i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|))+\frac{2i|v|}{Z}\left(\tilde{\mu}E_{4}(-2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|)-\tilde{\mu}^{*}E_{4}(2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(\tilde{\mu}^{*}-\tilde{\mu})E_{4}(-i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|))-\frac{v^{2}}{Z^{2}}\left(E_{5}(-2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|)+E_{5}(2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|)-2E_{5}(-i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|)\right)\right].

(83)

We can also compute
\sigma_{zz}
as a function of
Z=|z-z^{\prime}|
and obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{zz}=-\frac{2\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}}\int\frac{dR}{R}~\left[\left(\tilde{\mu}e^{i\tilde{\mu}R/|v|}-\tilde{\mu}^{*}e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}R/|v|}\right)^{2}+\left(1-2\frac{Z^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)\left(\left(\tilde{\mu}+\frac{i|v|}{R}\right)e^{i\tilde{\mu}R/|v|}+\left(\tilde{\mu}^{*}-\frac{i|v|}{R}\right)e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}R/|v|}\right)^{2}\right]

(84)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u}~\left[\left(\tilde{\mu}e^{i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|u}-\tilde{\mu}^{*}e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|u}\right)^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2}{u^{2}}\right)\left(\left(\tilde{\mu}+\frac{i|v|}{Zu}\right)e^{i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|u}+\left(\tilde{\mu}^{*}-\frac{i|v|}{Zu}\right)e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|u}\right)^{2}\right]

(85)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}du~\left(\left({\tilde{\mu}^{2}e^{2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|u}+\tilde{\mu}^{*2}e^{-2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|u}}\right)\left(\frac{2}{u}-\frac{2}{u^{3}}\right)-\frac{4\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\mu}^{*}e^{-i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|u}}{u^{3}}\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{2i|v|}{Z}\left({\tilde{\mu}e^{i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|u}-\tilde{\mu}^{*}e^{-i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|u}+(\tilde{\mu}^{*}-\tilde{\mu})e^{i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|u}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{u^{2}}-\frac{2}{u^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{v^{2}}{Z^{2}}\left({e^{2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|u}+e^{-2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|u}-2e^{i\delta\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|u}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{u^{3}}-\frac{2}{u^{5}}\right)\right)

(86)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}}\left(2\tilde{\mu}^{2}(E_{1}(-2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|)-E_{3}(-2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|))+2\tilde{\mu}^{*2}(E_{1}(2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|)-E_{3}(2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|))-2|\tilde{\mu}|^{2}E_{3}(-i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{2i|v|}{Z}\left[\tilde{\mu}(E_{2}(2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|)-2E_{4}(2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|))-\tilde{\mu}^{*}(E_{2}(-2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|)-2E_{4}(-2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|))\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+(\tilde{\mu}^{*}-\tilde{\mu})(E_{2}(-i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|)-2E_{4}(-i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|))\right]-\frac{v^{2}}{Z^{2}}(E_{3}(-2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|)-2E_{5}(-2i\tilde{\mu}Z/|v|)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+E_{3}(2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|)-2E_{5}(2i\tilde{\mu}^{*}Z/|v|)-2E_{3}(-i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|))+2\times 2E_{5}(-i(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\mu}^{*})Z/|v|))\right).

(87)

C.3
Semiclassical limit.

In the semiclassical limit the wave-like nature of quasiparticles is neglected such that one can approximate the exponentials with complex arguments with a functions with the same decay length and same volume integral but without the quantum oscillations

\displaystyle e^{i\alpha Zu}\rightarrow\frac{i{\rm Im}(\alpha)}{\alpha}e^{-{\rm Im}(\alpha)Zu}

(88)

from which we also deduce the semiclassical limits of terms involving powers of
1/Z=1/|z-z^{\prime}|
by integrating this equation over
u
to have

\displaystyle\frac{e^{i\alpha Zu}}{Z^{n}}\rightarrow\left(\frac{\alpha}{i{\rm Im}(\alpha)}\right)^{n-1}\frac{e^{-{\rm Im}(\alpha)Zu}}{Z^{n}}.

(89)

This semi-classical limit is evaluated for a non-relativistic electron gas in Ref.
[
37
]
, and for a Weyl electron gas we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}^{\rm cl.}(Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}}\left(\left({\rm Re}(\tilde{\mu})^{2}+2{\rm Im}(\tilde{\mu})^{2}\right)E_{1}(2{\rm Im}(\tilde{\mu})Z/|v|)-{\rm Re}(\tilde{\mu})^{2}E_{3}(2{\rm Im}(\tilde{\mu})Z/|v|)\right),

(90)

\displaystyle\sigma_{zz}^{\rm cl.}(Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\hbar v_{x}^{2}}{(4\pi v^{2})^{2}}\left({\rm Im}(\tilde{\mu})^{2}E_{1}(2{\rm Im}(\tilde{\mu})Z/|v|)+{\rm Re}(\tilde{\mu})^{2}E_{3}(2{\rm Im}(\tilde{\mu})Z/|v|)\right),

(91)

that indeed match the conductivity in the Kubo formula but without the oscillating behaviour (see Fig.
S1
).

C.4
Total conductivity

The global conductivity tensor is obtained after performing the integral over all the volume

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{\mu\mu}\rangle=\frac{1}{L_{z}}\int dzdz^{\prime}~\sigma_{\mu\mu}(z,z^{\prime}).

(92)

In the configuration with an electric field in the
z
direction, we obtain

1.

For the non-relativistic electron gas:

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}=\sigma_{yy}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{{\rm Re}(q)^{2}-m\mu/3}{{\rm Im}(q)}\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar},

(93)

\displaystyle\sigma_{zz}=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}\frac{\mu}{{\rm Im}(q)}\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}

(94)

so the total conductivity is

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}=\frac{1}{3}(\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy}+\sigma_{zz})=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\frac{{\rm Re}(q)^{2}}{{\rm Im}(q)}\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}

(95)

which is obtained in Ref.
[
37
]
.

2.

For the Weyl electron gas:

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}=\sigma_{yy}=\sigma_{zz}=\frac{3+4\mu^{2}\tau^{2}}{6\pi^{2}|v|\tau}\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}

(96)

so the total conductivity is

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}=\frac{1}{3}(\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy}+\sigma_{zz})=\frac{3+4\mu^{2}\tau^{2}}{6\pi^{2}|v|\tau}\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}.

(97)

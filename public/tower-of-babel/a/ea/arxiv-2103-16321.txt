Abstract
. We study the Hilbert scheme ℋ d , g , r ℒ \mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r} parametrizing smooth, irreducible, non-degenerate and linearly normal curves of degree d d and genus g g in ℙ r \mathbb{P}^{r} whose complete and very ample hyperplane linear series 𝒟 \mathcal{D} have relatively small index of speciality i ⁡ ( 𝒟 ) = g − d + r i(\mathcal{D})=g-d+r . In particular we show the existence (and non-existence as well in some sporadic cases) of every Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves with i ⁡ ( 𝒟 ) = 4 i(\mathcal{D})=4 . We also determine the irreducibility of ℋ 2 ​ r + 4 , r + 8 , r ℒ \mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{2r+4,r+8,r} for 3 ≤ r ≤ 8 3\leq r\leq 8 , which are rather peculiar families in a certain sense.

1.
An overview, preliminaries and basic set-up

Let
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
be the Hilbert scheme of smooth, irreducible and non-degenerate curves of degree
d
and genus
g
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
the union of those components of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
whose general element is linearly normal.
By abuse of terminology, we say that a component of the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
has index of speciality
\alpha
, if
the hyperplane series
\mathcal{D}=g^{r}_{d}
of a general element of the component has index of speciality
\alpha
, i.e.

h^{1}(C,\mathcal{D})=g-d+\dim|\mathcal{D}|=\alpha\geq g-d+r.

In case
\alpha\gneq g-d+r
, the linear series
\mathcal{D}
is incomplete and it is possible that there may exist a component of the Hilbert scheme which has index of speciality strictly greater than
g-d+r
; cf.
[
12
]
and references therein, especially in the paragraph before the statement of Main Theorem in the Introduction, in which the authors also provide useful remarks on connections between Hilbert scheme business and several other areas in algebraic geometry. However the index of speciality of any component of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
is
g-d+r
, which one may define as the
index of speciality of a Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves
.

We recall the following modified assertion of Severi, which has been given attention by some authors recently; cf.
[
5
,
19
]
.

Modified Assertion of Severi
.

A nonempty
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
is irreducible for any triple
(d,g,r)
in the Brill-Noether range

\rho(d,g,r)=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)\geq 0,

where
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
is the union of those components of
{\mathcal{H}}_{d,g,r}
whose general element is linearly normal.

We note that the modified assertion of Severi makes sense only if the index of speciality of Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
is non-negative. Through a preliminary attempt to settle down the Modified Assertion of Severi, which is still in its infancy, one has quite extensive knowledge about the Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves of small index of speciality
\alpha\leq 3
which can be summarized as follows; cf.
[
16
,
5
,
19
]
.

•

\alpha=0
:
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r,g,r}\neq\emptyset
and irreducible.

•

\alpha=1
:
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-1,g,r}\neq\emptyset
and irreducible for
g\geq r+1
and is empty for
g\leq r
.

•

\alpha=2
:
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-2,g,r}\neq\emptyset
and irreducible for every
g\geq r+3
and is empty if

g\leq r+2
.

•

\alpha=3
:
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-3,g,r}=\emptyset
for
g\leq r+4
.
For
r\geq 5
and
g\geq r+5
,
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-3,g,r}\neq\emptyset
unless
g=r+6
and
r\geq 10
. Moreover
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-3,g,r}
is irreducible for every
g\geq 2r+3
and is reducible for almost all
g
in the range
r+5\leq g\leq 2r+2
.

One obvious advantage in considering the Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves according to its index of speciality
\alpha
is that the residual series of the very ample hyperplane series corresponding to a general element of any component of the Hilbert scheme has fixed dimension
\alpha-1
regardless of values of genus
g
and
degree
d
. Therefore one may work more effectively in exploring out several properties of Hilbert schemes under consideration by looking at the family of curves in a fixed projective space
\mathbb{P}^{\alpha-1}
induced by the residual series of hyperplane series. Especially when
\alpha
is low, approaching this way becomes somewhat useful.

In case
\alpha=3
,
the residual series of hyperplane series are nets and it is possible to derive the basic properties such as irreducibility, existence as well as the number of moduli of the Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves by considering the corresponding property of the Severi variety of plane curves, which has been proven to be effective to a certain extent in some previous works on the subject;
[
16
,
5
,
18
,
19
]
. In this paper we consider Hilbert scheme of linear normal curves with index of speciality
\alpha=4
. Equivalently we study the Hilbert schemes of non-degenerate, smooth and linearly normal curves in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
(
r\geq 3
) of degree
d=g+r-4
. We show the existence as well as non-existence of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
for every triple

(d,g,r)
with
g-d+r=4
. We also determine the irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
for specific triples
(d,g,r)=(2r+4,r+8,r)
with
3\leq r\leq 8
which is of some particular interest. We also study
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
for the triples
(d,g,r)=(2r+5,r+9,r)
(
3\leq r\leq 11
) and give a partial irreducibility result.

For notation and conventions, we usually follow those in
[
3
,
4
,
13
]
; e.g.
\pi(d,r)
is the maximal possible arithmetic genus of an irreducible and non-degenerate curve of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
\pi_{1}(d,r)
is the so-called the second Castelnuovo genus bound of an irreducible and non-degenerate curve of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
not lying on a surface of degree
r-1
; cf.
[
13
, Theorem 3.13, 3.15]
. In particular if
r=3
,
\pi_{1}(d,3)
is the maximal possible arithmetic genus of an irreducible and non-degenerate curve of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
not lying on a quadric surface.
Following classical terminology, a linear series of degree
d
and dimension
r
on a smooth irreducible curve
C
is usually denoted by
g^{r}_{d}
.
A base-point-free linear series
g^{r}_{d}
(
r\geq 2
) on a smooth curve
C
is called birationally very ample when the morphism

C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{r}
induced by
g^{r}_{d}
is generically one-to-one (or birational) onto its image. A base-point-free linear series
g^{r}_{d}
on
C
is said to be compounded of a covering (compounded for short) if the morphism induced by
g^{r}_{d}
gives rise to a non-trivial covering map
C\rightarrow C^{\prime}
of degree
k\geq 2
. For a complete linear series
\mathcal{E}
on a smooth curve
C
, the residual series
|K_{C}-\mathcal{E}|
is denoted by
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
.
We work over the field of complex numbers.

The organization of this paper is as follows. We briefly recall some terminologies and basic preliminaries in the remainder of this section. In the second section we determine all the triples
(d,g,r)
,
r\geq 3
for which
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}\neq\emptyset
.

In subsequent sections, we determine the irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}
for

by using the irreducibility of Severi varieties of nodal curves on a non-singular quadric surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. In the final section – with an open end – we make a discussion on a couple of related aspects of our study, such as the next case
d=g+r-5
for further investigation, in addition to the existence and partial irreducibility result of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}
for

(
3\leq r\leq 11
).

We briefly recall several fundamental facts for our study of Hilbert schemes which are well-known; cf.
[
4
]
or
[
2
, §1 and §2]
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the moduli space of smooth curves of genus
g
. Given an isomorphism class
[C]\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
corresponding to a smooth irreducible curve
C
, there exist a neighborhood
U\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
of the class
[C]
and a smooth connected variety
\mathcal{M}
which is a finite ramified covering
h:\mathcal{M}\to U
, as well as varieties
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{W}^{r}_{d}
and
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
proper over
\mathcal{M}
with the following properties:

(1)

\xi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{M}
is a universal curve, i.e. for every
p\in\mathcal{M}
,
\xi^{-1}(p)
is a smooth curve of genus
g
whose isomorphism class is
h(p)
,

(2)

\mathcal{W}^{r}_{d}
parametrizes the pairs
(p,L)
where
L
is a line bundle of degree
d
and
h^{0}(L)\geq r+1
on
\xi^{-1}(p)
,

(3)

\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
parametrizes the couples
(p,\mathcal{D})
, where
\mathcal{D}
is possibly an incomplete linear series of degree
d
and dimension
r
on
\xi^{-1}(p)
.

Given an irreducible family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
with some geometric meaning, if a general element of
\mathcal{F}
is complete, the closure of the family
\{\mathcal{E}^{\vee}|\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{F},~\mathcal{E}\textrm{ is complete}\}\subset\mathcal{G}^{g-d+r-1}_{2g-2-d}
is sometimes denoted by
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
(
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}
, respectively) be the union of components of
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
whose general element
(p,\mathcal{D})
corresponds to a very ample (very ample and complete, respectively) linear series
\mathcal{D}
on the curve
C=\xi^{-1}(p)
. By recalling that an open subset of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
consisting of elements corresponding to smooth irreducible and non-degenerate curves is a
\mathbb{P}\textrm{GL}(r+1)
-bundle over an open subset of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, the irreducibility of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
guarantees the irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
. Likewise, the irreducibility of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}
ensures the irreducibility of

\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}^{\mathcal{L}}
.
We recall the following regarding the schemes
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
which is also well-known; cf.
[
13
, 2.a]
and
[
4
, Ch. 21, §3, 5, 6, 11, 12]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

For non-negative integers
d
,
g
and
r\geq 1
, let

\rho(d,g,r):=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)

be the Brill-Noether number.
The dimension of any component of
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
is at least

\lambda(d,g,r):=3g-3+\rho(d,g,r),

hence the minimal possible dimension of any component of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
is

\mathcal{X}(d,g,r):=\lambda(d,g,r)+\dim\mathbb{P}GL(r+1).

Moreover, if
\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0
, there exists a unique component (called the principal component)
\mathcal{G}_{0}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
which dominates
\mathcal{M}
(or
\mathcal{M}_{g}
).

2.
Existence and non-existence of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}

In this section we prove the existence of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}
for every
g\geq r+7
(
r\geq 5
) unless
g=r+8
(
r\geq 9
) or
g=r+9
(
r\geq 12)
, in which cases we have
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}=\emptyset
.
For

, we quote the existence of

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}
which are known to some people and to the author (or as folklores) as follows.

Remark 2.1
.

(i) For
r=3,
it is easy to see that

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,3}=\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g-1,g,3}\neq\emptyset\textrm{ if and only if }g\geq r+6=9.

Note that by Castelnuovo genus bound,
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g-1,g,3}=\emptyset
for
g\leq 8
. For
g=9
, (extremal) curves of type
(4,4)
on quadrics form the only irreducible component of degree
d=8
and genus
g=9
of dimension

\dim\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}(4,4))+\dim\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2))=33\gneq 4\cdot 8=\mathcal{X}(8,g,3).

For
g=10
, there exist curves of type
(3,6)
on smooth quadrics which form an irreducible family of dimension

\dim\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}(3,6))+\dim\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2))=36.

Moreover, complete intersections of two irreducible cubics form another family of the same expected dimension

\dim\mathbb{G}(1,\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(3)))=\dim\mathbb{G}(1,19)=36.

Hence
\mathcal{H}_{9,10,3}=\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{9,10,3}\neq\emptyset
and is reducible. We further note that the inequality

g\leq\frac{(g-2)(g-3)}{6}=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{6}\leq\pi_{1}(d,3)

is valid for every pair
(d,g)=(g-1,g)
with
g\geq 11
, which guarantees the existence of a smooth curve of degree
g-1
and genus
g
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
; cf.
[
11
, Theorem 2.4]
.

(ii) For the existence of smooth curves of genus
g
and degree
d=g+r-4=g
in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
, one may deduce that

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}=\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g,g,4}\neq\emptyset\textrm{ if and only if }g\geq 11=r+7

as follows. In general, in the range
d\leq g+r
inside the Brill-Noether range
\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0
, the principal component
\mathcal{G}_{0}
which has the expected dimension

is one of the components of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}
(cf.
[
13
, 2.1 page 70]
), and hence
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}
or

\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}^{\mathcal{L}}
is non-empty. In particular for
g\geq 20
,
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g,g,4}\neq\emptyset
.
For
(d,g,4)=(g,g,4)
outside the Brill-Noether range

, we refer
[

with negative Brill-Noether number. For

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=g^{3}_{e}
(with

) be a complete and special linear series (possibly with non-empty base locus

) on a smooth curve

of genus

. Suppose

is compounded. Then

– the residual series of
\mathcal{E}
– is not very ample.

Proof.

Let
k
be the degree of the morphism
C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\rightarrow}}E\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
induced by
the base-point-free part of
\mathcal{E}
. Since
e\geq k\cdot\deg\phi(C)
and
\deg\phi(C)\geq 3
, the following three cases are possible;

(a)

k=3
and
C
is
trigonal with
\mathcal{E}=|3g^{1}_{3}+\Delta|
,
\Delta\neq\emptyset
.

(b)

k=2
and
C
is bielliptic with
\mathcal{E}=|\phi^{*}(g^{3}_{4})+\Delta|
, where
C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\rightarrow}}E
is a double cover of an elliptic curve
E
.

(c)

k=2
and
C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\rightarrow}}E
is a double cover of a curve
E
of genus
h=2
,
\mathcal{E}=|\phi^{*}(g^{3}_{5})+\Delta|
.

(a) If
C
is trigonal, let
q\in\Delta
and let
q+t+s\in g^{1}_{3}
be the unique trigonal divisor containing
q
. We then have

\displaystyle\dim|\mathcal{E}+t+s|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim|3g^{1}_{3}+\Delta+t+s|=\dim|3g^{1}_{3}+(q+t+s)+(\Delta-q)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim|4g^{1}_{3}+(\Delta-q)|\geq\dim\mathcal{E}+1.

(b) If

\dim|\mathcal{E}+\phi^{*}(u)|=\dim|\phi^{*}(g^{3}_{4}+u)+\Delta|=\dim|\phi^{*}(g^{4}_{5})+\Delta|\geq\dim\mathcal{E}+1

(c) For any

Definition 3.1

(i)

Let

\mathcal{M}
.

(ii)

Given an integer
g
such that

, we set

. We denote by

\Sigma_{\mathcal{M},\delta}\subset|\mathcal{M}|=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{M}))

the (equi-singular) Severi variety which is the closure of the locus of integral curves in the linear system
\mathcal{M}
whose singular locus consists of exactly
\delta
nodes.

(iii)

Let
\Sigma_{\mathcal{M},g}
be the (equi-generic) Severi variety which is the closure of the locus of integral curves of geometric genus

\mathcal{M}
.

We shall make use the following well-known results adopted for our current specific situation. The results quoted below are known to be true in a more general context. Readers are advised to refer
[
8
]

Remark 3.2

(i)

A general member of every irreducible component of the equi-generic Severi variety

is a nodal curve; cf.
[

(ii)

The equi-singular Severi variety

[

, Proposition 2.11, Theorem 3.1]

(i)

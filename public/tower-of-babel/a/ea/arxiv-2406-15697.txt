1.
Introduction

Classification of the manifolds with a given cohomology ring (up to diffeomorphism, homeomorphism, or homotopy) is one of the central problems in geometric topology. The Poincaré Theorem is a special case, i.e. for
n\geq 3
, if a
n
-dimensional manifold has the same homology groups as the
n
-dimensional sphere
S^{n}
, then it is homeomorphic to
S^{n}
. For a more complicated cohomology ring, there also exist many investigations. In the general case, Wall
[
33
,
35
]
investigated
(s-1)
-connected
2s
-manifolds and
(s-1)
-connected
(2s+1)
-manifolds. For concrete cases with specified dimensions, we also list some results. Barden
[
5
]
classified simply connected 5-manifolds. Kreck and Su
[
25
]
considered certain nonsimply connected 5-manifolds. One of their results is the classification for closed oriented
5
-manifolds
M
with
\pi_{1}(M)
a free group and
H_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})=0
. In
[
34
]
Wall classified closed, oriented, simply connected, spin
6
-manifolds with torsion-free homology. Jupp extended the Wall’s classification in nonspin case
[
18
]
. In dimension
7
, there is a family of smooth manifolds with positive sectional curvature, namely Aloff-Wallach spaces
[
3
]
. Kreck and Stolz
[
23
,
24
]
gave a classification for the
7
-manifolds modeled on Aloff-Wallach spaces. Subsequently, a homotopy classification of Aloff-Wallach spaces was given by Kruggel
[
26
]
. Besides, there are other classifications for
7
-manifolds with certain cohomology rings, such as
[
11
,
22
]
.

In this paper, we consider simply connected, closed, smooth
13
-dimensional manifolds, namely
\mathcal{M}
, with cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{\ast}(\mathrm{CP}^{3}\times S^{7};\mathbb{Z})
, where
\mathrm{CP}^{3}
is the complex projective space. Many examples of these
13
-manifolds arise from the following construction
[
37
]
:
From an
S^{1}
action on
S^{7}\times S^{7}
given by

\theta\circ(x,y)=(e^{il\theta}x,e^{-ik\theta}y)
with
\mathrm{gcd}(k,l)=1
,
we get the
S^{1}
-bundle
S^{1}\to S^{7}\times S^{7}{\to}\mathcal{M}
.
From
[
12
]
, we can see some interesting geometric properties that there are infinitely many circle quotients
\mathcal{M}
of
S^{7}\times S^{7}
admitting a metric of
\mathrm{Ric}_{2}>0
.
In addition, Kerin
[
19
]
gave an example

with almost positive sectional curvature, which is an
S^{7}
-bundle over
\mathrm{CP}^{3}
.

To investigate the geometric properties of these manifolds
\mathcal{M}
further, we need to classify them up to diffeomorphism first. However, for the classification of all manifolds
\mathcal{M}
,
there are still many unsolved technical issues that will be pointed out in Section
4

Fortunately, making some constraints to the topology of manifolds
\mathcal{M}
, we have some interesting conclusions.

Throughout the rest of the paper, let
H^{i}(-)
(or

) denote the integral cohomology (or homology) group

(or
H_{i}(-;\mathbb{Z})
) unless other statements are given. We always choose the cup product
x^{2}
as the generator of
H^{4}(\mathcal{M})
, where

is a generator of
H^{2}(\mathcal{M})
. Hence we only use an integer to denote the first Pontrjagin class

of

.

By [
[
15
]
, Thoerem 4.57], the cohomology classes in

are in one-to-one correspondence to the homotopy classes
[\mathcal{M},\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{Z},2)]
of maps
\mathcal{M}\to\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{Z},2)
where
\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{Z},2)
is the Eilenberg-MacLane space. Indeed
\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{Z},2)
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathrm{CP}^{\infty}
that is the colimit over the sequence
\mathrm{CP}^{1}\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow\mathrm{CP}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{CP}^{n+1}\hookrightarrow\cdots
where
\mathrm{CP}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{CP}^{n+1}
is the natural inclusion. So we can regard the
x
as a map
x:\mathcal{M}\to\mathrm{CP}^{\infty}
.

Definition 1.1
.

\mathcal{M}
admits a

if there exists a map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathrm{CP}^{4}
such that the following diagram is homotopy commutative

\textstyle{\mathrm{CP}^{4}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{eb}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\mathrm{CP}^{\infty}}

i.e.

is the natural inclusion,

Obviously, if

is homotopy equivalent to a manifold

.

Let

be the total space of an

-bundle over

is one of considered manifolds, the composition of the bundle projection

of

Until now, I have not found any manifold

that really has no

admit

can be completed by the main theorem of this paper.

Before presenting the main theorem, we introduce
13
-dimensional homotopy spheres, namely

is a smooth manifold and homotopy equivalent to the standard sphere

. By Poincaré Theorem,

is homeomorphic to

. We say that

. Up to diffeomorphism, there are three

-dimensional homotopy spheres

. Moreover, the set of the diffeomorphism classes of

-dimensional homotopy spheres and the connected sum
\#
as addition form an abelian group that is isomorphic to

. The standard sphere

represents the
0

represents a generator of the group.

Now, we are ready to give the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
be nonspin and admit restriction lifts.

(1) There exists a homotopy sphere
\Sigma^{13}
so that
\mathcal{M}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\#\Sigma^{13}
if and only if
p_{1}(\mathcal{M})=p_{1}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})
;

(2) If
p_{1}(\mathcal{M})=p_{1}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\neq 0\mod 3
,
\mathcal{M}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.

(3)
\mathcal{M}
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
if and only if
p_{1}(\mathcal{M})=p_{1}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})
.

(4)
\mathcal{M}
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
if and only if
p_{1}(\mathcal{M})=p_{1}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\mod 24
.

By computing the first Pontrjagin classes of the total spaces of certain
S^{7}
-bundles over
\mathrm{CP}^{3}
(see Section

), we know that for every integer

, there exists an
S^{7}
-bundle over
\mathrm{CP}^{3}
so that the first Pontrjagin class of its total space

equals to
nx^{2}\in H^{4}(\overline{\mathcal{M}})
. Thus, by applying (1), (2) of Theorem
1.2
, we have

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be nonspin and admit a restriction lift, then there exists a homotopy sphere
\Sigma^{13}
so that
\mathcal{M}\#\Sigma^{13}
is diffeomorphic to the total space of an
S^{7}
-bundle over
\mathrm{CP}^{3}
. If
\mathcal{M}
additionally satisfies

, then it is diffeomorphic to the total space of an
S^{7}
-bundle over
\mathrm{CP}^{3}
.

In Riemannian geometry, many well-known results show that the curvature of a manifold can control its partial topological properties, such as the most fundamental theorems, Bonnet-Myers Theorem and Synge Theorem.
On the other hand,
one of the most interesting problems in Riemannian geometry is whether a smooth manifold admits a metric of non-negative sectional curvature or positive sectional curvature. Here, we use the topology of manifolds to investigate the metrics.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be nonspin and admit a restriction lift with
p_{1}(\mathcal{M})\geq 4
, then there exists a homotopy sphere
\Sigma^{13}
so that
\mathcal{M}\#\Sigma^{13}
admits a metric of non-negative sectional curvature. If
\mathcal{M}
additionally satisfies

, it admits a metric of non-negative sectional curvature.

For the general spin case, the same theorem as Theorem
1.2
is unclear. For the spin manifolds

where
\Sigma^{7}
is a homotopy
7
-sphere, we know that

is diffeomorpic to

where

is the standard
7
-sphere, which has been proved in [
[
9
]
, the proof of Theorem 6.1, pp.37]. Thus,
for every homotopy sphere

,

admits a metric of non-negative sectional curvature.

We mainly use surgery theory

7.
A metric of non-negative sectional curvature

In Riemannian geometry, there is an interesting question:

Question 7.1
.

Let
E
be the total space of a vector bundle over a compact manifold
B
with non-negative sectional curvature. Does E admit a metric of non-negative curvature?

From
[
14
]
, we know that: for arbitrary real (resp. complex) vector bundle
\eta
over
\mathrm{CP}^{3}
, there exists
k\geq 0
such that the total space of
\eta\oplus\epsilon^{k}
admits a metric of non-negative curvature where
\epsilon^{k}
is the
k
-dimensional trivial bundle. In this section, we will prove that the total spaces of certain real (resp. complex) vector bundles over
\mathrm{CP}^{3}
admit metrics of non-negative curvature.

Recall that
\mathrm{CP}^{3}=\mathrm{U}(4)/(\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(3))
. Now we consider the following bundle, namely
\eta_{\mathbb{q}}
(
\mathbb{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})
,
q_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
)

\eta_{\mathbb{q}}:\mathbb{C}^{4}\to\mathrm{U}(4)\times_{(\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(3))}\mathbb{C}^{4}\to\mathrm{CP}^{3}

where the
\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(3)
action on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
is given by, for
(z,A)\in\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(3)
,

(z,A)\curvearrowright(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}):=(z^{q_{1}}c_{1},z^{q_{2}}c_{2},z^{q_{3}}c_{3},z^{q_{4}}c_{4})

We can endow
\mathrm{U}(4)\times\mathbb{C}^{4}
with the product metric of
\langle,\rangle_{\mathrm{U}(4)}
and the standard Euclidean metric. By O’Neill’s Theorem on Riemannian submersions,
\mathrm{U}(4)\times_{(\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(3))}\mathbb{C}^{4}
admits a metric of non-negative curvature. On the other hand, by the
\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(3)
action on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
, we have
\mathrm{U}(4)\times_{(\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(3))}\mathbb{C}^{4}=S^{7}\times_{S^{1}}\mathbb{C}^{4}
where
\mathrm{U}(1)=S^{1}
. Moreover, the bundle
\eta_{\mathbb{q}}
has the form

\mathbb{C}^{4}\to S^{7}\times_{S^{1}}\mathbb{C}^{4}\to\mathrm{CP}^{3}.

Lemma 7.2
.

For every integer
n\geq 0
, there exists
\mathbb{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})
such that the first Pontrjagin class of the underlying real bundle of
\eta_{\mathbb{q}}
equals to
nx^{2}
where
x\in H^{2}(\mathrm{CP}^{3})
is a generator.

Proof.

We first present a bundle, namely
\theta_{\mathbb{q}}
over
\mathrm{CP}^{\infty}

\mathbb{C}^{4}\to S^{\infty}\times_{S^{1}}\mathbb{C}^{4}\to\mathrm{CP}^{\infty}

where the
S^{1}
action on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
is given by, for
z\in S^{1}
,

z\curvearrowright(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}):=(z^{q_{1}}c_{1},z^{q_{2}}c_{2},z^{q_{3}}c_{3},z^{q_{4}}c_{4}).

By the natural inclusion
i:\mathrm{CP}^{3}\to\mathrm{CP}^{\infty}
, we have
\eta_{\mathbb{q}}=i^{\ast}\theta_{\mathbb{q}}
.

Note that
\theta_{\mathbb{q}}
is the associated bundle of a principal
\mathrm{U}(4)
-bundle, namely
\Theta_{\mathbb{q}}:\mathrm{U}(4)\to S^{\infty}\times_{S^{1}}\mathrm{U}(4)\to\mathrm{CP}^{\infty}
, i.e. there is a bundle isomorphism so that
(S^{\infty}\times_{S^{1}}\mathrm{U}(4))\times_{\mathrm{U}(4)}\mathbb{C}^{4}\cong S^{\infty}\times_{S^{1}}\mathbb{C}^{4}
where the
\mathrm{U}(4)
action on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
is standard, the
S^{1}
action on
\mathrm{U}(4)
is induced by the embedding
S^{1}\to\mathrm{U}(4):z\to\mathrm{diag}(z^{q_{1}},z^{q_{2}},z^{q_{3}},z^{q_{4}})
.

Let
\kappa_{\mathbb{q}}:\mathrm{CP}^{\infty}\to\mathrm{BU}(4)
be the classifying map of
\Theta_{\mathbb{q}}
. By the following homotopy fibration,

S^{1}\to\mathrm{U}(4)\to\mathrm{U}(4)/S^{1}\simeq S^{\infty}\times_{S^{1}}\mathrm{U}(4)\to\mathrm{B}S^{1}=\mathrm{CP}^{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\kappa_{\mathbb{q}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{BU}(4)

{\kappa}_{\mathbb{q}}
is induced by the above embedding
S^{1}\to\mathrm{U}(4).

Now we recall the classical characteristic theory for the sake of convenience.
Let
G
be a Lie group, and
T^{n}\subset G
be a maximal torus in
G
with induced map
\kappa:\mathrm{BT}^{n}\to\mathrm{BG}
. Let
I_{G}
be the ring of polynomials in
H^{\ast}(\mathrm{BT}^{n})
invariant under the Weyl group
W(G)
.

Theorem 7.3

(Borel’s Theorem
[
8
]
)
.

If
H^{\ast}(G)
and
H^{\ast}(G/T^{n})
are torsion-free, then
\kappa^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(\mathrm{BG})\to H^{\ast}(\mathrm{BT}^{n})
is a monomorphism with range
I_{G}
.

As was shown in
[
8
]
, the conditions of Borel’s Theorem are satisfied for all classical groups.
Recall that
H^{\ast}(\mathrm{BU}(4))\cong\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\cdots,c_{4}]
a polynomial rings on the Chern classses
c_{1},\cdots,c_{4}
. Applying Borel’s Theorem to
\mathrm{U}(4)
, we have that
the homomorphism

H^{\ast}(\mathrm{BU}(4))\to H^{\ast}(\mathrm{BT}^{4})

sends
c_{i}
to the elementary symmetric polynomial
\sigma_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{4})
of degree
i
where
x_{j}\in H^{2}(\mathrm{BT}^{4})
,
1\leq j\leq 4
, are the generators.
Given a circle subgroup
S^{1}
in the maximal torus parameterized
\mathbb{q}
as above,
\kappa_{\mathbb{q}}^{*}(c_{i})=\sigma_{i}(q_{1},\cdots,q_{4})x^{i}
where
x\in H^{2}(\mathrm{B}{S}^{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}
is a generator.

Therefore, the first and second Chern classes of the complex vector bundle
\eta_{\mathbb{q}}
are
c_{1}(\eta_{\mathbb{q}})=\sigma_{1}(q_{1},\cdots,q_{4})x
,
c_{2}(\eta_{\mathbb{q}})=\sigma_{2}(q_{1},\cdots,q_{4})x^{2}
. Thus, the first Pontryagin class of the underlying real bundle of
\eta_{\mathbb{q}}
equals to
p_{1}=(\sigma_{1}(q_{1},\cdots,q_{4}))^{2}-2\sigma_{2}(q_{1},\cdots,q_{4})=\Sigma_{i=1}^{4}q_{i}^{2}
.

Recall Lagrange’s four-square theorem: every integer
n\geq 0
, there exist
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
so that
n=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}
, which has been proved by Lagrange and Euler respectively. This finishes our proof.
∎

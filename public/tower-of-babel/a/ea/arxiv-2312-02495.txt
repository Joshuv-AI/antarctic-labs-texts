( n , k ) (n,k) -Besicovitch sets do not exist in ℤ p n {\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n} and ℤ ^ n \hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n} for k ≥ 2 k\geq 2
Manik Dhar

Abstract
Besicovitch showed that a compact set in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} which contains a unit line segment in every direction can have measure 0 0 . These constructions also work over other metric spaces like the p p -adics and profinite integers. It is conjectured that it is impossible to construct sets with measure 0 0 which contain a unit 2 2 -disk in every direction in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} . We prove that over the p p -adics and profinite integers any set which contains a 2-flat in every direction must have positive measure. The main ingredients are maximal Kakeya estimates for ( ℤ / N ​ ℤ ) n ({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n} proven in [ Dha22 ] and adapting Fourier analytic arguments by Oberlin [ Obe05 ] . In general, we prove L n − 1 L^{n-1} to L n − 1 L^{n-1} estimates for the maximal operator corresponding to 2 2 -flats.

1.1

(

where

is a constant depending on

and
{\mathbb{F}}
, and
\mu
and
\nu
are normalized Haar measure over
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
and

The conjecture in this setting is to have

1.2
Proof overview

We just talk about
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
here. Given a function
f:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
, we project it / take a quotient along direction
u\in\operatorname{Gr}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n},1)
to get
f_{u}
(we integrate along preimages to get the value of the function).
f_{u}
as a function over
\operatorname{Gr}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n},1)\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n-1}
is referred to as the X-ray transform of
f
. We can show that
{\mathcal{N}}^{1}f_{u}
is the same as
{\mathcal{N}}^{2}f
for the subspaces
U\in\operatorname{Gr}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n},2)
containing
u
.

If we had
{\mathcal{N}}^{1}
operator bounds over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n-1}
, we could apply those on
f_{u}
for a fixed
u
and take average over
u
to complete the proof. The problem is that no good
{\mathcal{N}}^{1}
bounds hold (because of the existence of
0
measure Kakeya sets). But we do have
{\mathcal{N}}^{1}
bounds when the function
f
is locally constant over balls of fixed radius (from
[
Dha22
]
). If
f_{i}
is constant over all balls of radius
p^{-i}
(recall
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
is made of
p^{in}
disjoint balls of radius
p^{-i}
) we have (the term
i^{n}
is simplified and not exactly correct but captures the growth rate),

\int{\mathcal{N}}^{1}f_{i,u}^{n-1}\,d\mu\leq i^{n}\int f_{i,u}^{n-1}\,d\mu.

We use the above statement by decomposing
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}f_{i}
where
f_{i}
is locally constant over balls of radius
p^{-i}
(this is done by a Littlewood-Paley decomposition where the Fourier transform of
f_{i}
is the Fourier transform of
f
at a fixed scale).
Using a simple Fourier analytic argument we can show,

\iint_{u,x}f_{i,u}(x)^{n-1}\,d\mu d\mu\leq p^{-i}\int f^{n-1}d\mu.

Combining the previous two equations and adding them up for all
i
gives us,

\left(\int{\mathcal{N}}^{2}f^{n-1}\,d\mu\right)^{1/n}\leq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\iint{\mathcal{N}}^{1}f_{i,u}^{n-1}\,d\mu d\mu\right)^{1/n}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}ip^{-i/n}\right)\left(\int f^{n-1}d\mu\right)^{1/n}.

As
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}ip^{-i/n}<\infty
we are done.

We let

\operatorname{Gr}(({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n},k)=\operatorname{Gr}(({\mathbb{Z}}/q_{1}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n},k)\times\ldots\times\operatorname{Gr}(({\mathbb{Z}}/q_{r}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n},k).

For a given
u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
, we let
Q_{u}
be the quotient
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\langle u\rangle
which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
. We choose representative vectors in
{\mathbb{P}}Q_{u}
such that they are a subset of
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
.

2.2
Fourier Analysis over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}

To adapt the Fourier analytic argument of Oberlin
[
Obe05
]
we cover some
simple facts about Fourier Analysis over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
e
be the map from
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
to
{\mathbb{C}}
which maps
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
to
e^{2\pi ix}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Given
u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n-1}
,we let
u^{\perp}=\{a\in({\mathbb{Z}}[1/p]/{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}|\langle u,a\rangle=0\}.
We similarly define
u^{\perp}
for
u\in\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n-1}
.

We will need a simple lemma about the density of vectors in
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
orthogonal to a fixed
v\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
where
{\mathbb{D}}
is the dual of
{\mathbb{F}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
{\mathbb{D}}={\mathbb{Z}}[1/p]/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
respectively. For a given
a\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
, the set of
u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
such that
a\in u^{\perp}
has measure

\frac{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-2}|}{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|},

where
N=v(a)
(
v=\hat{v}
for
{\mathbb{F}}=\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
v=v_{p}
for
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
).

Proof.

We give the proof for
{\mathbb{F}}=\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
{\mathbb{D}}={\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
. The proof is the same for
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}[1/p]/{\mathbb{Z}}
(with
v_{p}
used instead of
\hat{v}
).

For
a\in({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
, say
\hat{v}(a)=N
. This means
a=a^{\prime}/N
where
a^{\prime}\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^{n}
and
ta^{\prime}\mod N\neq 0
for
t=1,\ldots,N-1
. We also use
a^{\prime}
to refer to
a^{\prime}\mod N\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
. We now see that for any
u\in{\mathbb{P}}\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n-1},\langle u,a\rangle=0
if and only if
\langle u\mod N,a^{\prime}\rangle=0\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
. For
u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
,
u+N\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n}
partitions
{\mathbb{P}}\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n-1}
into
|({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|
many disjoint isomorphic pieces. Therefore
u+N\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n-1}
has measure
1/|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|
.

As
ta^{\prime}\mod N\neq 0
for
t\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
then the set of
b\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
such that
\langle b,a^{\prime}\rangle=0
is isomorphic to
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
. This means that the number of
u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
such that
\langle u,a^{\prime}\rangle=0
is
|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-2}|
.
∎

Definition 2.4

(Fourier transform over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
)
.

{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
{\mathbb{D}}={\mathbb{Z}}[1/p]/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
respectively. Given a
f:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
, the Fourier transform
\hat{f}:{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is defined as

\hat{f}(a)=\int\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}e(\langle x,u\rangle)f(x)\,d\mu,

where
\mu
is the normalized Haar measure on
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. The inverse of the above is,

f(x)=\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}}e(-\langle x,v\rangle)\hat{f}(a).

We have Plancherel’s Theorem

\int_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}\,d\mu=\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}}|\hat{f}(a)|^{2},

where
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
{\mathbb{D}}={\mathbb{Z}}[1/p]/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
respectively.

We also need to define the X-ray transform which looks at the pushforward of a function on quotients along every direction.

Definition 2.5

(X-ray transform)
.

Given a
f:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
(where
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
) we define
f_{u}:Q_{u}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
for
u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
as,

f_{u}(x)=\int_{t\in{\mathbb{F}}}f(x+tu)\,d\mu,

where
\mu
is the normalized Haar measure over
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
.

We next prove a key lemma which will connect the
\ell^{2}
norm of the X-ray transform with a Sobolev norm of
f
(analogous to Lemma 3.1 in
[
Obe05
]
).

Lemma 2.6
.

Given
f:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
(where
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
or
\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
{\mathbb{D}}={\mathbb{Z}}[1/p]/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
v=v_{p},\hat{v}
respectively) ,

\int\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{x\in Q_{u}}|f_{u}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu\,d\nu=\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}}\frac{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/v(a){\mathbb{Z}})^{n-2}|}{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/v(a){\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|}|\hat{f}(a)|^{2},

where
\mu
is the normalized Haar measure on
{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
and
\nu
is the normalized Haar measure on
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
.

Proof.

As usual
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
{\mathbb{D}}={\mathbb{Z}}[1/p]/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
respectively.

Claim 2.7
.

For
u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
we have,

\int\limits_{x\in Q_{u}}|f_{u}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu=\sum\limits_{a\in u^{\perp}}|\hat{f}(a)|^{2}.

Proof.

We prove this claim for
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{F}}^{n})
which suffices as these functions are dense in the space of
L_{2}
functions over
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Let
f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1/N}({\mathbb{F}}^{n})
(
N
will be a power of
p
for
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and a positive integer for
{\mathbb{F}}=\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
).

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{a\in u^{\perp}}|\hat{f}(a)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{a\in u^{\perp}}\iint_{x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}e(\langle x-y,a\rangle)f(x)\overline{f(y)}\,d\mu d\mu

\displaystyle=N^{-2n}\sum\limits_{a\in u^{\perp}}\sum\limits_{x,y\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}\iint_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}e(\langle x-y+N(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}),a\rangle)f(x)\overline{f(y)}\,d\mu d\mu,

as
f
is constant over
x+N{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. We see that if
Na\neq 0
then
\iint_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}e(\langle x-y+N(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}),a\rangle)=0
and otherwise it equals
e(\langle x-y,a\rangle)
. The set of
a\in u^{\perp}
such that
Na=0
is finite. We therefore get,

\sum\limits_{a\in u^{\perp}}|\hat{f}(a)|^{2}=N^{-2n}\sum\limits_{a\in u^{\perp},Na=0}\sum\limits_{x,y\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}e(\langle x-y,a\rangle)f(x)\overline{f(y)}.

We see that if
x-y
is not a multiple of
u
then
\sum\limits_{a\in u^{\perp},Na=0}e(\langle x-y,v\rangle)=0
and if
x-y
is a multiple of
u
then
\sum_{a\in u^{\perp},Na=0}e(\langle x-y,a\rangle)=N^{n-1}
. Using this in the above equation gives us,

\sum\limits_{a\in u^{\perp}}|\hat{f}(a)|^{2}=N^{-n-1}\sum\limits_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}\sum\limits_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}}f(x)\overline{f(x+tu)}=\iint\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n},t\in{\mathbb{F}}}f(z)\overline{f(z+tu)}.

We also have,

\int\limits_{x\in Q_{u}}|f_{u}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu=\iiint\limits_{x\in Q_{u},t_{1},t_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}}f(x+t_{1}u)\overline{f(x+t_{2}u)}=\iint\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n},t\in{\mathbb{F}}}f(z)\overline{f(z+tu)}.

∎

Using the above claim we have,

\int\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{x\in Q_{u}}|f_{u}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu\,d\nu=\int\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}}\sum\limits_{v\in u^{\perp}}|\hat{f}(v)|^{2}.

To simplify the above sum for a given
v\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
, we want the measure of the set of
u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
such that
v\in u^{\perp}
. Lemma
2.3
does exactly that completing the proof.
∎

We want to use complex interpolation with the above lemma. We will use the Riesz-Thorin theorem and we state it for the case we need.

Theorem 2.8

(Riesz-Thorin interpolation (See Chapter 4 in
[
Kat04
]
))
.

Let
W
be a linear operator mapping complex valued functions
f
over
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
(where
{\mathbb{F}}=\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) to complex valued functions over some other compact measurable domain
G
with measure
\nu
(for us
G={\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
). If we have

\int\limits_{y\in G}|Wf(y)|^{p_{1}}\,d\nu\leq B\int\limits_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p_{1}}\,d\mu,

and

\sup\limits_{y\in G^{\prime}}|Wf(y)|\leq\sup\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}|f(x)|

for some constant
B>0
and
1\leq p<\infty
we have

\int\limits_{y\in G}|Wf(y)|^{p_{\theta}}\,d\nu\leq B\int\limits_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p_{\theta}}\,d\mu,

where
p_{\theta}=p/\theta
for any
\theta\in[0,1]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
or
\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
{\mathbb{D}}={\mathbb{Z}}[1/p]/{\mathbb{Z}}
or
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
respectively. Let
f:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
we have the following inequality,

\int\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{x\in Q_{u}}|f_{M_{1},M_{2},u}(x)|^{p}\,d\mu\,d\nu\leq\frac{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/M_{1}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-2}|}{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/M_{1}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|}\int\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\mu,

for all
p\geq 2
,
M_{1}<M_{2}
are positive integers for
{\mathbb{F}}=\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and powers of
p
for
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, and
f_{M_{1},M_{2},u}
is the X-ray transform of

f_{M_{1},M_{2}}(x)=\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n},M_{1}\leq v(a)<M_{2}}e(-\langle x,v\rangle)\hat{f}(a).

Proof.

The X-ray transform is a linear map on functions over
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
to functions over
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Going from
f
to
f_{M_{1},M_{2}}
is also a linear map. Therefore mapping
f
to
f_{M_{1},M_{2},u}:{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
is a linear map. Lemma
2.3
and Plancherel’s theorem gives us,

\int\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{x\in Q_{u}}|f_{M,u}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu\,d\nu\leq\frac{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/M_{1}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-2}|}{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/M_{1}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|}\int\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}\,d\mu.

By the triangle inequality also have

\sup\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1},x\in Q_{u}\cong{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}}|f_{M,u}(x)|\leq\sup\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}|f(x)|.

Riesz-Thorin interpolation now gives us the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6

Throughout
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}},{\mathbb{D}}={\mathbb{Z}}[1/p]/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
v=v_{p},\hat{v}

f_{i}

We note that
f_{i}

We now consider the X-ray transform

of

, where

and

such that
u,w\in U
.
u,w\in U
and
w\in{\mathbb{P}}Q_{u}

u
and
w
span
U
. For a fixed
u
and
U
containing
u
,

has to be unique and always exists. We then have

{\mathcal{N}}^{2}f_{i}(U)={\mathcal{N}}^{1}f_{i,u}(w).

(1)

Let
Q_{i,u}
be
Q_{u}\mod M_{i+1}
(quotient by
M_{i+1}Q_{u}
).
Q_{u}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
and as before

is constant over cosets of

so

. It easily follows that
f^{\prime}_{i,u}
is the X-ray transform of

\int\limits_{w\in{\mathbb{P}}Q_{u}}{\mathcal{N}}^{1}f_{i,u}(w)^{n-1}\,d\nu=\underset{w\in{\mathbb{P}}Q_{i,u}}{{\mathbb{E}}}[{\mathcal{N}}^{1}f^{\prime}_{i,u}(w)^{n-1}],

where

is the normalized Haar measure on

\underset{v\in{\mathbb{P}}Q_{i,u}}{{\mathbb{E}}}[{\mathcal{N}}^{1}f^{\prime}_{i,u}(v)^{n-1}]C_{M_{i+1},n-1}\leq\underset{x\in Q_{i,u}}{{\mathbb{E}}}f^{\prime}_{i,u}(x)^{n-1}=\int\limits_{x\in Q_{u}}f_{i,u}(x)^{n-1}\,d\mu,

C_{M_{i+1},n-1}\int\limits_{U\in\operatorname{Gr}({\mathbb{F}}^{n},2)}{\mathcal{N}}^{2}f_{i}(U)^{n-1}\,d\nu_{2}\leq\iint\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1},x\in Q_{u}}f_{i,u}(x)^{n-1}\,d\nu d\mu,

\int\limits_{U\in\operatorname{Gr}({\mathbb{F}}^{n},2)}{\mathcal{N}}^{2}f(U)^{n-1}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/M_{i}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-2}|^{1/(n-1)}\over C_{M_{i+1},n-1}^{1/(n-1)}|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/M_{i}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|^{1/(n-1)}}\right)^{n-1}\int\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}f(x)^{n-1}\,d\mu.

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/M_{i}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-2}|^{1/(n-1)}\over C_{M_{i+1},n-1}^{1/(n-1)}|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/M_{i}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|^{1/(n-1)}}<\infty

The sum above is clearly bounded for

Definition A.2

N
coprime and
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
we define the
p
-maximal weight
\textsf{mweight}(f,p)
as follows:

Let
L(u)=\{a_{u}+tu|t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}N{\mathbb{Z}}\}
be a line such that
\sum_{x\in L(u)}f(x)=f^{*}(u)
. Using Fact
A.1
we note that the line
L(u)
can be written as a product of lines
L_{p}(u)\subseteq({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
and
L_{1}(u)\subseteq({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
. We define,

\textsf{mweight}(f,p)=\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1},z\in L_{1}(u)}\sum\limits_{x\in L_{p}(u)}f((x,z)).

Note,
\textsf{mweight}(f,p)
for
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
is simply
\max_{u}f^{*}(u)
.

We can finally state the main theorem from
[
Dha22
]
.
We also let

f^{*}(U)=N{\mathcal{N}}^{1}f(U)=\sup\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in U}|f(a+x)|.

Theorem A.3

(Maximal Kakeya bounds over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
for general
N
)
.

Let
n>0
be an integer and
N=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
with
p_{i}
primes and
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For any function
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
we have the following bound,

\sum\limits_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}|f(x)|^{n}\geq C_{N,n}\underset{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}{{\mathbb{E}}}[|f^{*}(u)|^{n}]=\frac{C_{N,n}}{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|}\left(\sum\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}|f^{*}(u)|^{n}\right),

where

\displaystyle C_{N,n}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2(\log(\textsf{mweight}(f,p_{1}))+1)\lceil\log_{p_{1}}(\textsf{mweight}(f,p_{1}))+\log_{p_{1}}(n)\rceil}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2(k_{r}+\lceil\log_{p_{r}}(n)\rceil)}\prod\limits_{i=2}^{r-1}\frac{1}{2(k_{i}\log(p_{i})+1)(k_{i}+\lceil\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rceil)}\right)^{n}

.

Proof of Theorem
2.10
.

By a scaling and limiting argument we see that it suffices to prove the theorem for
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}
and
\sum_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}|f(x)|^{n}=1
.
In this case we see that
0\leq f(x)\leq 1
. We divide
[0,1]
into
N
parts of length
1/N
. Let
g(x)
be the function obtained by rounding the value of
f(x)
to
\lceil Nf(x)\rceil/N
. We see that
g(x)\geq f(x)
which implies
g^{*}(u)\geq f^{*}(u)
for all
u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
. We also see that if
f(x)\geq 1/(2N)
then
g(x)\leq 2f(x)
. Let
S
be the set of values of
x
for which
f(x)\leq 1/(2N)
. We see that
\sum_{x\in S}g(x)^{n}\leq 1=\sum_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}f(x)^{n}
. This gives us,

\sum\limits_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}g(x)^{n}=\sum_{x\in S}g(x)^{n}+\sum_{x\in S^{c}}g(x)^{n}\leq(2^{n}+1)\sum_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}f(x)^{n}.

We note that
Ng
is a function which takes values in
\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
. Hence, we can apply Theorem
A.3
(note,
\textsf{mweight}(Ng,p_{1})\leq Np\leq N^{2}
).

\displaystyle(2^{n}+1)\sum_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}f(x)^{n}

\displaystyle\geq N^{-n}\sum\limits_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}(Ng(x))^{n}

\displaystyle\geq D_{N,n}\underset{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}{{\mathbb{E}}}[|g^{*}(u)|^{n}]\geq D_{N,n}\underset{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}{{\mathbb{E}}}[|f^{*}(u)|^{n}],

where

\displaystyle D_{N,n}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2(2\log N+1)(2\log_{p_{1}}N+\log_{p_{1}}n+1)}\right)^{n}\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2(k_{r}+\lceil\log_{p_{r}}(n)\rceil)}\prod\limits_{i=2}^{r-1}\frac{1}{2(k_{i}\log(p_{i})+1)(k_{i}+\lceil\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rceil)}\right)^{n}.

The expression
\prod_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}
is upper bounded by the number of divisors
\tau(N)
of
N
which satisfies
\log(\tau(N))=O(\log N/\log\log N)
. The bound for
\tau(n)
is Theorem 317 in
[
HW75
]
. We now see that the constant
D_{N,n}/(2^{n}+1)
is lower bounded by
N^{-Cn\log(n)/\log\log N}
where
C
is a universal constant.
∎

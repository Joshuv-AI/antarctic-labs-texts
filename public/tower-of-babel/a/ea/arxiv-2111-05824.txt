Very weak solution for the exterior stationary Stokes equations with Navier slip boundary condition
Anis Dhifaoui

Abstract
In some problems of fluid mechanics, it is possible to be confronted with data that are not regular, that is why we are interested here in the search for the so-called very weak solutions for the stationary Stokes problem with Navier-type boundary conditions in a three-dimensional exterior domain. The problem describes the flow of a viscous and incompressible fluid past an obstacle where we assume that the fluid may slip on the boundary of the obstacle. Because the flow domain is unbounded, we set the problem in weighted Sobolev spaces in order to control the behavior at infinity of the solutions. Our purpose is to prove the existence and the uniqueness of a very weak solution in a Hilbertian framework. Keywords : Stokes equations, Navier boundary condition, exterior domain, weighted spaces, strong solutions, very weak solutions.

Introduction

We are interested in this paper on the existence of very weak solutions for the (linear stationary) Stokes problem in a domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The set
\Omega
is simply connected exterior domain, namely
the complement of a simply connected bounded domain which represents the obstacle. The system of stationary Stokes be written as follows:

\displaystyle-\Delta\textbf{{u}}+\nabla{\pi}=\textbf{{f}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{u}}=0\quad\mathrm{\text{ in }}\Omega,

(1.1)

where the unknowns are

following Navier’s type slip boundary conditions:

\textbf{{u}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=0,\quad 2[\mathrm{\textbf{D}}(\textbf{{u}})\textbf{{n}}]_{\tau}+\alpha\textbf{{u}}_{\tau}=\boldsymbol{0}\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma,

\Gamma

[

]
. The first condition in (

2.2
Weighted Hilbert spaces

Let
\textit{{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
be a typical point in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and let
r=|\textit{{x}}|=\left(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
denotes its distance to the origin. In order to control the behaviour at infinity of our functions and distributions we use for basic weights the quantity

\rho(\textit{{x}})=\left(1+r^{2}\right)^{1/2}
which is equivalent to
r
at infinity. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we introduce

W_{k}^{0,2}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega),\,\rho^{k}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\Big\},

which is a Hilbert space equipped with the norm:

\|u\|_{W_{k}^{0,2}(\Omega)}=\|\rho^{k}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Let
m\geqslant 1
be an integer. We define the weighted Hilbert space:

W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega);\,\forall\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{N}^{3}:\,0\leq|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|\leq m,\,\rho^{k-m+|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|}\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\Big\},

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{0\leqslant|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|\leqslant m}\|\rho^{k-m+|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|}\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/2}.

We define the semi-norm

|u|_{W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|=m}\|\rho^{k}\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/2}.

Let us give an examples of such spaces that will be often used in the remaining of the paper.
For
m=1
, we have

W_{k}^{1,2}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega),\,\rho^{k-1}u\in L^{2}(\Omega),\,\rho^{k}\nabla u\in{L}^{2}(\Omega)\Big\}.

For
m=2
, we have

W_{k+1}^{2,2}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in{W_{k}^{1,2}(\Omega)},\rho^{k+1}\nabla^{2}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\right\}.

For the sake of simplicity, we have defined these spaces with integer exponents on the weight function.
But naturally, these definitions can be extended to real number exponents with eventually some slight modifications (see
[
6
]
for more details).

We shall now give some basic properties of those spaces:

Properties 2.1
.

1.

The space
\mathcal{D}(\overline{\Omega})
is dense
in
W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega).

2.

For any
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, the mapping

u\in W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}u\in W_{k}^{m-|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|,2}(\Omega)

(2.1)

is continuous.

3.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have the following continuous imbedding:

W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W_{k-1}^{m-1,2}(\Omega).

(2.2)

4.

The space
\mathcal{P}_{m-2-k}
is the space of all polynomials included in
W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)
and the following Poincaré-type inequality holds:

\forall u\in W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega),\quad\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{j^{\prime}}}\|u+\mu\|_{W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)}\leqslant C|u|_{W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)},

(2.3)

where
j^{\prime}=\min(m-2-k,m-1)
. In other words the semi-norm
|\cdot|_{W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)}
is a norm on
W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)/\mathcal{P}_{j^{\prime}}
.
In particular
|\cdot|_{W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)}
is a norm on
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

For more details on the above properties and the ones that we shall present in the following,
the reader can refer to
[
24
,
27
,
6
,
7
]
and references therein.

Note that all the local properties of the space

coincide with those of the

H^{m,2}(\Omega)

Therefore, the dual space of

W_{k}^{-m,2}(\Omega)\subset W_{k-1}^{-m-1,2}(\Omega).

(2.4)

Moreover, we have the following Poincaré-type

\forall u\in\mathring{W}_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega),\quad\|u\|_{W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)}\leq C|u|_{W_{k}^{m,2}(\Omega)}.

(2.5)

The closure of

2.3
Density and trace results

In this work, we are going to study the existence and uniqueness of very weak solutions for the stationary Stokes problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). Let us recall some notations related to the boundary condition. First, for any vector field

\textbf{{v}}=\textbf{{v}}_{\tau}+(\textbf{{v}}\cdot\textbf{{n}})\textbf{{n}},

where
\textbf{{v}}_{\tau}
is the projection of

\Gamma
. Next, for any point

\Gamma
, one may choose an open neighbourhood

of

\textbf{{v}}_{\tau}=(\textbf{{v}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1}+(\textbf{{v}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{2})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{2}.

As a result for any

the following formula holds (see

\displaystyle 2[\mathrm{\textbf{D}}(\textbf{{v}})\textbf{{n}}]_{\tau}=\nabla_{\tau}(\textbf{{v}}\cdot\textbf{{n}})+\left(\frac{\partial\textbf{{v}}}{\partial\textbf{{n}}}\right)_{\tau}-\Lambda\textbf{{v}}\qquad\text{ on }\quad\Gamma,

where

\displaystyle\Lambda\,\textbf{{v}}=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\left(\textbf{{v}}_{\tau}\cdot\frac{\partial\textbf{{n}}}{\partial s_{k}}\right)\boldsymbol{\tau}_{k}.

Now, we need to introduce some functional spaces. Otherwise, it is necessary to establish some Green formulas which are deduce from density lemmas. We start by the following space:

\displaystyle T_{k+1}(\Omega)=\Big\{\textbf{{v}}\in\mathring{H}_{k-1}(\mathrm{div},\Omega);\,\,\,\,\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{v}}\in\mathring{W}^{1,2}_{k+1}(\Omega)\Big\},

\displaystyle\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{T_{k+1}(\Omega)}=\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{{W}^{0,2}_{k-1}(\Omega)}+\|\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{v}}\|_{{W}^{1,2}_{k+1}(\Omega)}

The following Lemma state some properties related to the space

\displaystyle\langle\Delta\,\textbf{{u}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{(T^{2}_{k+1}(\Omega))^{\prime}\times T^{2}_{k+1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\textbf{{u}}\cdot\Delta\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}d\textbf{x}+\langle 2[\textbf{D}(\textbf{{u}})\textbf{{n}}]_{\tau}+\alpha\textbf{{u}}_{\tau},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{H^{-3/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{3/2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle-\langle 2[\textbf{D}(\boldsymbol{\varphi})\textbf{{n}}]\cdot\textbf{{n}},\textbf{{u}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}.

\displaystyle-2\langle[\textbf{D}(\boldsymbol{\varphi})\textbf{{n}}]\cdot\textbf{{n}},\textbf{{u}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}.

Conclusion

In this paper, we solved the exterior Stokes problem with the Navier slip boundary conditions with a positive friction term. To prescribe the growths or decay of functions at infinity, we set the problem in weighted Sobolev spaces. Our study is based on an
L^{2}
-theory. We established the existence and the uniqueness of the very weak solution to the problem. The main idea consists in the use of a duality argument with the strong solutions obtained in
[
19
]
. In a forthcoming work, we will study the very weak solution to the problem in weighted
L^{p}
spaces.

1.
Notations and Intorduction

The aim of this paper is to generalize the Brezis–Kato theorem
[
3
]
for higher order elliptic differential operators with variable coefficients.
It is intended to make the least assumptions possible concerning the coefficients of the operator so that it can serve for future references.

We consider a linear differential operator
L=(-1)^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2m}a_{\alpha}(x)D^{\alpha}
with variable complex-valued coefficients defined in an arbitrary open subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Here for every multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})
we set
D^{\alpha}=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\partial x_{N}^{\alpha_{N}}}
and
\xi^{\alpha}=\xi_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\xi_{N}^{\alpha_{N}}
, for a vector
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{N})
.
The characteristic form
l(x,\xi)
of
L
is defined by
l(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|=2m}a_{\alpha}(x)\xi^{\alpha}
for
x\in\Omega
and real vectors
\xi
.
We say that
L
is
strongly elliptic
if there exists
\lambda>0
such that

(1.1)

\mathfrak{Re}(l(x,\xi))\geq\lambda|\xi|^{2m}

for
x\in\Omega
and all real vectors
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{N})
.
We make standing assumptions:

(i)

all coefficients
a_{\alpha}
belong to the space
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
,

(ii)

top-order coefficients
a_{\alpha}
for
|\alpha|=2m
are continuous on
\Omega
.

We denote by
C^{k}(\Omega)
(resp.
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
) the class of complex valued functions defined in
\Omega
which has continuous derivatives up to order
k
(resp. which are smooth and have compact support).
We consider Hölder’s spaces
C^{k,h}(\Omega)
for nonnegative integers
k
and
0<h<1
which consists of all functions
u\in C^{k}(\Omega)
such that
D^{\alpha}u
with
|\alpha|=k
satisfies the (uniform) Hölder condition of exponent
h
.
The symbol
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
for
1\leq p<\infty
denotes the Sobolev space of all complex valued
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for which all the distributional derivatives
D^{\alpha}u
are also in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for
|\alpha|\leq k
.
We consider the norm
\|u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|D^{\alpha}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/p}
for
u\in W^{k,p}(\Omega)
.
We use the symbol
W^{k,p}_{0}(B)
to denote the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in the
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
norm.
The local Sobolev space
W^{k,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
consists of functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
such that
u\in W^{k,p}(U)
for every open subset
U\subset\subset\Omega
(the latter means that
\overline{U}
is compact and
\overline{U}\subset\Omega
).
Similarly,
C^{k,h}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
consists of
u\in C^{k}(\Omega)
such that
u\in C^{k,h}(U)
for every open
U\subset\subset\Omega
.
We see with the aid of the partition of unity that a function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
belongs to
W^{k,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
if and only if
u\in W^{k,p}(B)
for every ball
B\subset\subset\Omega
.

We use
\lesssim
to denote the inequality up to a constant whenever it is convenient.
Throughout what follows,
g:\Omega\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
stands for a Carathéodory function, i.e., measurable with respect to the first variable and continuous with respect to the second one.

The Brezis–Kato theorem — Thm 2.3 in
[
3
]
— asserts that if
u\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of

-\Delta u=g(x,u)\ \text{in }\Omega,

that is for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}g(x,u)\varphi\,dx

and there exists a non-negative
a\in L^{\frac{N}{2}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
such that

|g(x,u(x))|\leq a(x)(1+|u(x)|)\ \text{a.e. in }\Omega,

then
u\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
See also Lemma B.3 in Struwe’s book
[
10
]
for a direct exposition of this result.

Notice that the regularity of solutions which the Brezis–Kato theorem asserts is higher than the one obtained through the elliptic regularity theory.
This has numerous important consequences, let us name just two.
The Brezis–Kato theorem has proved to be useful in the study of elliptic equations through the variational approach.
It enables one to show that critical points of certain functionals are classical
C^{2}
-solutions, see
[
10
]
and
[
12
]
.
For another example, the Brezis–Kato theorem turns out to be crucial in proving the so-called Pohozaev identity, cf.
[
2
]
.

The Brezis–Kato theorem has been extended to the case of the nonlocal right hand side in
[
9
]
and generalized to the case of the fractional Laplacian in
[
7
]
.
The generalization to the bilaplacian operator has recently been proved in
[
8
]
.
To the best of the author’s knowledge these are the only results present in the literature.

In this paper we generalize the Brezis–Kato theorem in two directions.
Firstly, we consider the general elliptic differential operator with variable coefficients.
Secondly, we allow the elliptic operator to be of any even order.
Results presented here seems to be new even for the second order differential operator.

The proof of the main result Theorem
2.1
is inspired by
[
11
]
.
It was developed and adjusted to the bilaplacian case in
[
8
]
and is presented here in a full generality.

The organization of the paper is the following.
In Section
2
we provide the proof of the main result — Theorem
2.1
— divided into some number of lemmata.
Section
3
contains Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
which are just versions of Theorem
2.1
adjusted to the weaker notions of solutions occurring in the literature.

2.
The Main Result

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
N>2m
be positive integers,
p>1
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open.
Let
u\in W^{2m,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
be a
strong solution
of
Lu=g(x,u)
, i.e.,

(2.1)

Lu=g(x,u(x))\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,

If there exists a non-negative
a\in L^{\frac{N}{2m}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle|g(x,u(x))|\leq a(x)(1+|u(x)|),\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,

then
u\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
If
g
has a polynomial rate of growth, namely, there exists
r\geq 1
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle|g(x,s)|\lesssim 1+|s|^{r}\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega\text{ and }s\in\mathbb{C},\;\text{where }r=\begin{cases}\frac{N}{N-2mp}&\text{if }p<\frac{N}{2m},\\
\text{ is arbitary}&\text{if }p\geq\frac{N}{2m},\end{cases}

then
u\in W^{2m,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}\cap C^{2m-1,h}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for all
1\leq q<\infty
and
0<h<1
.

Remark 2.2
.

Observe that the hypothesis
N>2m
in Theorem
2.1
is the only interesting case, for otherwise the Sobolev embedding
W^{2m,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\subset\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
immediately implies the assertion for any strong solution.
Form now on we confine our attention to
N>2m
without further comment.

Let us also emphasize that without stronger assumptions Theorem
2.1
is false in general for
p=1
, as there is a counterexample, see Theorem 3 in
[
5
]
.

Before proving Theorem
2.1
, we need some preparations first.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
s\geq\frac{N}{N-2m}
.
If for some
u\in L^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
there is a non-negative
a\in L^{\frac{N}{2m}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
such that

|g(x,u(x))|\leq a(x)(1+|u(x)|),\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,

then
g(\cdot,u)\in L^{\frac{Ns}{N+2ms}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

For any ball
B\subset\subset\Omega
, we have

\int_{B}|g(x,u)|^{\frac{Ns}{N+2ms}}\,dx\lesssim\int_{B}a^{\frac{Ns}{N+2ms}}\,dx+\int_{B}\left(a|u|\right)^{\frac{Ns}{N+2ms}}\,dx.

Since
\frac{Ns}{N+2ms}<\frac{N}{2m}
, the first integral
\int_{B}a^{\frac{Ns}{N+2ms}}\,dx
is finite.
We use the Hölder inequality with the exponents

\frac{1}{\frac{N+2ms}{2ms}}+\frac{1}{\frac{N+2ms}{N}}=1

to get

\int_{B}\left(a|u|\right)^{\frac{Ns}{N+2ms}}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{B}a^{\frac{N}{2m}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2ms}{N+2ms}}\left(\int_{B}|u|^{s}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N}{N+2ms}}<\infty.

Therefore
\int_{B}|g(x,u)|^{\frac{Ns}{N+2ms}}\,dx
is finite what completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma is based on Lemma B.2 from
[
11
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a measurable set with
0<|U|<\infty
, where
|U|
denotes the measure of
U
.
We assume that
a\in L^{p}(U)
and
v\in L^{1}(U)
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
q_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(U)
and
f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(U)
such that
\|q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(U)}<\varepsilon
and

a(x)v(x)=q_{\varepsilon}(x)v(x)+f_{\varepsilon}(x)\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in U.

Proof.

We define the sets

A_{k}=\left\{x\in U\mid|a(x)|<k\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{k}=\left\{x\in U\mid|v(x)|<k\right\}.

Since
a
,
v\in L^{1}(U)
, we have
|A_{k}^{c}|
,
|B_{k}^{c}|\to 0
.
Observe that

\displaystyle|A_{k}^{c}\cup B_{k}^{c}|=|A_{k}^{c}|+|B_{k}^{c}|-|A_{k}^{c}\cap B_{k}^{c}|\leq|A_{k}^{c}|+|B_{k}^{c}|\to 0,

so there is
k_{1}
such that for
k\geq k_{1}

|A_{k}\cap B_{k}|=|U|-|A_{k}^{c}\cup B_{k}^{c}|>0.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
By the absolute continuity of the measure
\mu(C):=\int_{C}|a(x)|^{p}\,dx
, we find
k_{2}\geq k_{1}
such that

(2.4)

\int_{A_{k}^{c}\cup B_{k}^{c}}|a(x)|^{p}\,dx<\left(\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\right)^{p},\quad\text{for }k\geq k_{2}.

We are in a position to define

\displaystyle q_{\varepsilon}(x):

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{k}a(x),&\text{for }x\in A_{k}\cap B_{k},\\
a(x),&\text{for }x\in(A_{k}\cap B_{k})^{c},\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=(a(x)-q_{\varepsilon}(x))v(x).

We estimate using the definition of
q_{\varepsilon}
and (
2.4
), for
k\geq k_{2}
,

\displaystyle\|q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(U)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k}\left(\int_{A_{k}\cap B_{k}}|a(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}+\left(\int_{(A_{k}\cap B_{k})^{c}}|a(x)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\,dx<\frac{1}{k}\|a\|_{L^{p}(U)}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon.

If
k\geq\max\left\{\frac{2\|a\|_{L^{p}(U)}}{\varepsilon},k_{2}\right\}
, then

\|q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(U)}<\varepsilon.

Observe that
f_{\varepsilon}=0
on
(A_{k}\cap B_{k})^{c}
, hence we get

|f_{\varepsilon}(x)|=\left|a(x)-\frac{1}{k}a(x)\right||v(x)|\mathds{1}_{A_{k}\cap B_{k}}(x)=\frac{k-1}{k}|a(x)||v(x)|\mathds{1}_{A_{k}\cap B_{k}}(x)\leq(k-1)k,

in view of the definitions of
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
.
∎

The next lemma is crucial. It is a modification of Lemma 2.2 in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
u\in W^{2m,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, with
p>1
, is a strong solution of

Lu=g(x,u)\quad\text{in }\Omega,

and (
2.2
) holds, then

u\in\begin{cases}L^{\frac{Np}{N-(2m+1)p}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),&\text{if }p<\frac{N}{2m+1},\\
\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),&\text{if }p\geq\frac{N}{2m+1}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Observe that if
2mp\geq N
, then we are done due to the Sobolev embedding
W^{2m,p}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
for
1\leq q<\infty
.
We therefore assume that
2mp<N
.

Take some ball
B_{0}\subset\subset\Omega
and choose another ball
B
such that
B_{0}\subset\subset B\subset\subset\Omega
.
Take a smooth cut-off function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B)
satisfying
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{0}
and
0\leq\eta\leq 1
.
Let us define

\displaystyle\tilde{a}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{g(x,u(x))}{u(x)}\mathds{1}_{\left\{x\mid|u(x)|>1\right\}}(x),

\displaystyle b(x)

\displaystyle:=g(x,u(x))\mathds{1}_{\left\{x\mid|u(x)|\leq 1\right\}}(x),

for
x\in B
, where it is understood that
\tilde{a}(x)=0
whenever
u(x)=0
.
We then have

g(x,u(x))=\tilde{a}(x)u(x)+b(x),\quad x\in B,

and by (
2.2
)

\displaystyle|\tilde{a}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a(x)(1+|u(x)|)}{|u(x)|}\mathds{1}_{\left\{x\mid|u(x)|>1\right\}}(x)\leq\frac{a(x)(2|u(x)|)}{|u(x)|}\mathds{1}_{\left\{x\mid|u(x)|>1\right\}}(x)\leq 2a(x),

\displaystyle|b(x)|

\displaystyle\leq a(x)(1+|u(x)|)\mathds{1}_{\left\{x\mid|u(x)|\leq 1\right\}}(x)\leq 2a(x),

so
\tilde{a}
,
b\in L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)
.
Moreover,
u
is a strong solution of

(2.5)

Lu=\tilde{a}(x)u+b(x)\quad\text{in }B.

Let us consider

v:=u\eta\in W^{2m,p}(B)\cap W^{m,p}_{0}(B).

By the Leibniz formula for any multi-index
|\alpha|\leq 2m
we get

D^{\alpha}v=\left(D^{\alpha}u\right)\eta+\sum_{\beta<\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}D^{\beta}uD^{\alpha-\beta}\eta,

and so

(2.6)

\displaystyle Lv

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2m}a_{\alpha}(x)D^{\alpha}v=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2m}a_{\alpha}(x)\left(\left(D^{\alpha}u\right)\eta+\sum_{\beta<\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}D^{\beta}uD^{\alpha-\beta}\eta\right)

\displaystyle=\left(Lu\right)\eta+F(u,\eta),

where

F(u,\eta)=\sum_{\beta<\alpha,\,|\alpha|=2m}c_{\alpha,\beta}(x)D^{\beta}uD^{\alpha-\beta}\eta,

for some coefficients
c_{\alpha,\beta}\in L^{\infty}(B)
.
Since
u\in W^{2m,p}(B)\subset W^{2m-1,\frac{Np}{N-p}}(B)
, we estimate

(2.7)

\left\|F(u,\eta)\right\|_{L^{\frac{Np}{N-p}}(B)}\lesssim\|u\|_{W^{2m-1,\frac{pN}{N-p}}(B)}\|\eta\|_{W^{2m,\infty}(B)}\lesssim\|u\|_{W^{2m,p}(B)}.

By Lemma
2.4
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
q_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)
and
\widehat{f}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(B)
such that

(2.8)

\tilde{a}(x)v(x)=q_{\varepsilon}(x)v(x)+\widehat{f}_{\varepsilon}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\|q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)}<\varepsilon.

We use (
2.6
), (
2.5
) and (
2.8
)

\displaystyle Lv

\displaystyle=\left(\tilde{a}(x)u+b(x)\right)\eta+F(u,\eta)=\tilde{a}(x)v+b(x)\eta+F(u,\eta)

\displaystyle=q_{\varepsilon}(x)v+\underbrace{\widehat{f}_{\varepsilon}(x)+b(x)\eta}_{f_{\varepsilon}}+F(u,\eta)=q_{\varepsilon}(x)v+f_{\varepsilon}(x)+F(u,\eta),

with

(2.9)

f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B).

We therefore arrive at

(2.10)

Lv-q_{\varepsilon}v=f_{\varepsilon}+F(u,\eta).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
1<p<q<\frac{N}{2m}
and
v\in W^{2m,p}(B)\cap W^{m,p}_{0}(B)
.
If for every
\varepsilon>0
we have
Lv-q_{\varepsilon}v\in L^{q}(B)
whith
\|q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)}<\varepsilon
, then
v\in W^{2m,q}(B)
.

Proof.

We recall some theory about differential and unbounded operators, see
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
for details and proofs.
Fix
1<s<\infty
.
Let
A_{s}
be the
L^{s}(B)
–realization of the elliptic differential operator
L=(-1)^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2m}a_{\alpha}(x)D^{\alpha}
satisfying (i) and (ii) under null Dirichlet boundary conditions.
The domain
D_{s}
of
A_{s}
equals to
W^{2m,s}(B)\cap W^{m,s}_{0}(B)
.
For
u\in D_{s}
we set
A_{s}u=Lu=(-1)^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2m}a_{\alpha}(x)D^{\alpha}u
and the latter is a well-defined element of
L^{s}(B)
.
There exists a constant
\tilde{k}_{s}>0
such that for every
u\in D_{s}

(2.11)

\|u\|_{W^{2m,s}(B)}\leq\tilde{k}_{s}\left(\|Lu\|_{L^{s}(B)}+\|u\|_{L^{s}(B)}\right),

see formula (1) in
[
4
]
.

By Thm 2 of
[
4
]
, the inverse operator
(A_{s}+\zeta I)^{-1}:L^{s}(B)\to D_{s}\subset L^{s}(B)
exists (notice that
D(A_{s}+\zeta I)=D_{s}
) for large positive
\zeta
.
We fix such a large
\zeta>0
and consider the invertible operator
\tilde{A}_{s}:=A_{s}+\zeta I
.
For
f\in L^{s}(B)
we set
u=\tilde{A}_{s}^{-1}f\in D_{s}
.
Then

\|u\|_{L^{s}(B)}\leq\|\tilde{A}_{s}^{-1}\|\|f\|_{L^{s}(B)}=c_{s}\|\tilde{A}_{s}u\|_{L^{s}(B)}

The above and the inequality (
2.11
) applied to the operator
L+\zeta I
yields

(2.12)

\|u\|_{W^{2m,s}(B)}\leq k_{s}\|\tilde{A}_{s}u\|_{L^{s}(B)},\quad u\in D_{s}.

Let us also consider for
1<s<\frac{N}{2m}
the multiplication operator
Q_{\varepsilon,s}
considered as an unbounded operator from
L^{s}(B)
into
L^{s}(B)
defined by

Q_{\varepsilon,s}u=q_{\varepsilon}u

for
u
in the domain
D(Q_{\varepsilon,s})
.
Observe that for any
u\in L^{\frac{Ns}{N-2ms}}(B)
we have

(2.13)

\|Q_{\varepsilon,s}\|_{L^{s}(B)}\leq\|q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{Ns}{N-2ms}}(B)},

where we used the Hölder inequality with the exponents

\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{\frac{N}{2m}}+\frac{1}{\frac{Ns}{N-2ms}}.

For
u\in D_{s}
we utilize (
2.13
), the Sobolev embedding
W^{2m,s}(B)\subset L^{\frac{Ns}{N-2ms}}(B)
and (
2.12
) to deduce

\|Q_{\varepsilon,q}u\|_{L^{q}(B)}\leq c_{\mathrm{Sobolev}}\|q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)}\|u\|_{W^{2m,q}(B)}\leq c_{\mathrm{Sobolev}}k_{s}\|q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)}\|\tilde{A}_{s}u\|_{L^{q}(B)}.

Therefore
Q_{\varepsilon,s}
is relatively bounded with respect to
\tilde{A}_{s}
, see §4 of
[
6
]
for the definitions and details.
If
\|q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)}
is small enough so that
\|q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)}c_{\mathrm{Sobolev}}k_{s}<1
, then
(\tilde{A}_{s}-Q_{\varepsilon,s})^{-1}:L^{s}(B)\to D_{s}\subset L^{s}(B)
exists in view of Thm 1.16 in §4.4 of
[
6
]
.
By (
2.8
) we can always assume this is the case.

Case I:

q\leq\frac{Np}{N-2mp}

In this case
v\in W^{2m,p}(B)\subset L^{\frac{Np}{N-2mp}}(B)\subset L^{q}(B)
and so

\underbrace{Lv-q_{\varepsilon}v}_{\in L^{q}(B)}+\underbrace{\zeta v}_{\in L^{q}(B)}=:f\in L^{q}(B),

where
\zeta
is large enough so that both
\tilde{A}_{q}^{-1}
and
\tilde{A}_{p}^{-1}
exist.
We define

y:=(\tilde{A}_{q}-Q_{\varepsilon,q})^{-1}f\in D_{q},

then

(\tilde{A}_{q}-Q_{\varepsilon,q})y=f.

On the other hand, we notice that
v\in D_{p}
so
(\tilde{A}_{p}-Q_{\varepsilon,p})v
is well-defined and equals to
f
.
Since
q>p
and
D_{q}\subset D_{p}
, we may consider
\tilde{A}_{q}
as a restriction of
\tilde{A}_{p}
and thus

(\tilde{A}_{q}-Q_{\varepsilon,q})y=(\tilde{A}_{p}-Q_{\varepsilon,p})y.

This yields

(\tilde{A}_{p}-Q_{\varepsilon,p})(y-v)=0.

Since
\tilde{A}_{p}-Q_{\varepsilon,p}
is invertible, we get

v=y\in D^{q}\subset W^{2m,q}(B)

as claimed.

Case II:

q>\frac{Np}{N-2mp}

We proceed similarly as in the previous case, but this time

Lv-q_{\varepsilon}v+\zeta v\in L^{\frac{Np}{N-2mp}}(B),\quad\zeta>0,

and, reasoning as above, we can conclude that
v\in D_{\frac{Np}{N-2mp}}
.
We define
p_{i}:=\frac{Np}{N-2mip}
for
i=0,\ldots,k+1
, where
k\geq 1
is the integer satisfying

k<\frac{N(q-p)}{2mpq}\leq k+1.

Repeating this procedure
k
times we see that
v\in D_{p_{k}}
where
p_{k}
satisfies
p_{k}<q\leq p_{k+1}
, and so
v\in W^{2m,p_{k}}(B)\subset L^{p_{k+1}}(B)\subset L^{q}(B)
.
We then have

Lv-q_{\varepsilon}v+\zeta v\in L^{q}(B),\quad\zeta>0,

and proceeding as in
Case I
we show that
u\in W^{2m,q}(B)
.
∎

We continue the proof of Lemma
2.5
by considering two cases.
If
p<\frac{N}{2m+1}
, then for
q=\frac{Np}{N-p}
we estimate using (
2.7
)

\|f_{\varepsilon}+F(u,\eta)\|_{L^{q}(B)}\lesssim\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)}+\|u\|_{W^{2m,p}(B)},

By (

) and the hypothesis the

norm of the right hand side of (

) is finite.
By Proposition
2.6

v\in W^{2m,q}(B)\subset L^{\frac{Np}{N-(2m+1)p}}(B)
.
Since

on
B_{0}
and

was arbitrary, we infer that

.

If

Fix any

and define

.
Then we get the following relations

We estimate as above using (

\|f_{\varepsilon}+F(u,\eta)\|_{L^{r}(B)}\lesssim\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2m}}(B)}+\|u\|_{W^{2m,p}(B)}.

We observe that the right hand side of (
2.10

Once again it implies that

∎

We need the following observation which bears great resemblance to Proposition
2.6
.
We present the proof for the sake of the clarity.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
u\in W^{2m,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
satisfy
Lu\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for some
1<p<q<\infty
.
Then
u\in W^{2m,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The proof is similar in spirit to that of Proposition
2.6
.
Fix
B_{0}\subset\subset\Omega
and choose another ball
B
such that
B_{0}\subset\subset B\subset\subset\Omega
.
Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B)
be smooth cut–off function such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\eta=1
on
B_{0}
.
We consider the function

v:=\eta u\in W^{2m,p}(B)\cap W^{m,p}_{0}(B)

which satisfies (as in (
2.6
))

Lv=(Lu)\eta+\sum_{\beta<\alpha,\,|\alpha|=2m}c_{\alpha,\beta}(x)D^{\beta}uD^{\alpha-\beta}\eta=(Lu)\eta+F(u,\eta).

We denote by
\tilde{A}_{s}
the
L^{s}(B)
–realization of
L+\zeta I
.
Observe we only need to show that
v\in W^{2m,q}(B)
.

We consider two cases.
If
W^{2m,p}(B)\subset W^{2m-1,q}(B)
(that is either
p>N
or
q\leq\frac{Np}{N-p}
), then

\|F(u,\eta)\|_{L^{q}(B)}\lesssim\|u\|_{W^{2m-1,q}(B)}\|\eta\|_{W^{2m,\infty}(B)}\lesssim\|u\|_{W^{2m,p}(B)},

what yields

Lv\in L^{q}(B).

We choose
\zeta>0
large enough so that both
\tilde{A}_{p}^{-1}
and
\tilde{A}_{q}^{-1}
exist and set
f:=Lv+\zeta v\in L^{q}(B)
.
We consider

y=A_{q}^{-1}f\in D_{q}=W^{2m,q}\cap W^{m,q}_{0}(B)\subset D_{p}.

Then we have
y-v\in D_{p}
and

\tilde{A}_{p}(y-v)=(L+\zeta)(y-v)=0

Since
\tilde{A}_{p}:D_{p}\to L^{p}(B)
is a bijection, we obtain

and so
v\in W^{2m,q}(B)
.

If
W^{2m,p}(B)\not\subset W^{2m-1,q}(B)
(that is
p<N
and

), then as in (
2.6
)

\|F(u,\eta)\|_{L^{\frac{Np}{N-p}}(B)}\lesssim\|u\|_{W^{2m,p}(B)}.

Hence

Lv\in L^{\frac{Np}{N-p}}(B)

and, similarly as above, this implies that
v\in D_{\frac{Np}{N-p}}
.
After finite number of such steps we find
s<N
such that
v\in D_{s}\subset W^{2m,s}(B)\subset L^{q}(B)
.
Then the same argument as in the previous case applies to show that
v\in W^{2m,q}(B)
.

Summarizing, in both cases
v\in W^{2m,q}(B)
and the proof is completed.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
p>1
and
u\in W^{2m,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
If
p\geq\frac{N}{2m+1}
, then
u\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
due to Lemma
2.5
.
We thus assume that
p<\frac{N}{2m+1}
.

First, we show that whenever
u\in W^{2m,s}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
with some
1<s<\frac{N}{2m+1}
is the strong solution of

Lu=g(x,u)\quad\text{in }\Omega,

then
u\in W^{2m,\frac{Ns}{N-s}}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
.
Indeed, Lemma
2.5
implies that
u\in L^{\frac{Ns}{N-(2m+1)s}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
g(\cdot,u)\in L^{\frac{Ns}{N-s}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
in view of Proposition
2.3
.
Since
Lu=g(x,u)\in L^{\frac{Ns}{N-s}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, Proposition
2.7
yields
u\in W^{2m,\frac{Ns}{N-s}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
as claimed.

Let
k\geq 1
be the integer satisfying

k-1<\frac{N-(2m+1)p}{p}\leq k

and define

p_{i}:=\frac{Np}{N-ip},\quad\text{for }i=0,\ldots,k.

Notice that
k
is chosen in such a way that
p_{k-1}<\frac{N}{2m+1}
and
p_{k}\geq\frac{N}{2m+1}
.
The first part of the proof shows that if
u\in W^{2m,p_{i}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, then
u\in W^{2m,p_{i+1}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
After
k
such steps we obtain
u\in W^{2m,p_{k}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and Lemma
2.5
yields the assertion.

We now proceed with the proof of the second assertion. Let
u\in W^{2m,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
with
p>1
be the strong solution of
Lu=g(x,u)
in
\Omega
with
g
of polynomial growth (
2.3
).
If
p\geq\frac{N}{2m}
, then the Sobolev embedding implies that
u\in\bigcap_{1\leq 1<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
Hence the superposition
g(\cdot,u)\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for any
q\geq 1
and Proposition
2.7
yields
u\in W^{2m,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
Then
u\in C^{2m-1,h}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
due to the Sobolev embeddings for any
0<h<1
.

We now consider the case
p<\frac{N}{2m}
.
We claim that there exists
a\in L^{\frac{N}{2m}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
such that

|g(x,u(x))|\leq a(x)(1+|u(x)|),\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega.

Indeed, defining

(2.14)

a(x)=\frac{|g(x,u(x))|}{1+|u(x)|}

and fixing some

we have by (

)

\displaystyle\int_{B}a^{\frac{N}{2m}}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B}\left(\frac{1+|u|^{\frac{N}{N-2mp}}}{1+|u|}\right)^{\frac{N}{2m}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B\cap\left\{x\mid|u(x)|\leq 1\right\}}2^{\frac{N}{2m}}\,dx+\int_{B\cap\left\{x\mid|u(x)|>1\right\}}\left(\frac{2|u|^{\frac{N}{N-2mp}}}{|u|}\right)^{\frac{N}{2m}}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\int_{B}{|u|^{\frac{2mp}{N-2mp}\frac{N}{2m}}}\,dx<\infty,

since

We apply the already proved first part of Theorem

to infer that

.
This and the polynomial rate of growth (

Once again Proposition

∎

3.
(Very) Weak Solutions

This section is devoted to the Brezis–Kato theorem for certain types of weak solutions.
We make stronger assumptions on the coefficients of the differential operator
L
to deal with less regular solutions.
We consider the differential operator in the divergence form as well.

Let us recall that
L
is a strongly elliptic linear differential operator satisfying the hypothesis stated in Introduction.
We additionally assume here that the coefficients
a_{\alpha}
of
L
are more regular, namely,

(3.1)

a_{\alpha}\in C^{|\alpha|}(\Omega),\quad\text{for}\;0<|\alpha|\leq 2m.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
.
We say that
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
is a
very weak solution
of

Lu=f,

if for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)

\int_{\Omega}u(x)\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2m}(-1)^{m+|\alpha|}D^{\alpha}\left(a_{\alpha}(x)\varphi(x)\right)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\varphi(x)\,dx.

The above formula arises in a usual manner from formally multiplying the differential equation
Lu=f
by a test function
\varphi
, integrating over
\Omega
and using integration by parts.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<p,\,q<\infty
.
If
u\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is a very weak solution of
Lu=f\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, then
u\in W^{2m,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Take any ball
B_{0}\subset\subset\Omega
and choose another ball
B
such that
B_{0}\subset\subset B\subset\subset\Omega
.
We introduce the adjoint
A
of
L
in
B
by the formula

Au:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2m}(-1)^{m+|\alpha|}D^{\alpha}(\overline{a_{\alpha}(x)}u).

Due to the Leibniz formula and (
3.1
) the operator
A
is itself an elliptic differential operator, i.e.,
Au=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2m}b_{\alpha}(x)D^{\alpha}u
for some coefficients
b_{\alpha}
.
Note that
A
and
u
fulfill all the hypotheses of Thm 7.1 in
[
1
]
and therefore
u\in W^{2m,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B)\subset W^{2m,p}(B_{0})
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
L
by strongly elliptic operator with the coefficients satisfying (
3.1
).
Let
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for some
p>\frac{N}{N-2m}
be a very weak solution of

(3.2)

Lu=g(x,u)\quad\text{in }\Omega.

If
g
satisfies (
2.2
), then
u\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
If
g
has a polynomial rate of growth, namely,

(3.3)

|g(x,s)|\lesssim 1+|s|^{\frac{N+2mp}{N}},\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega\text{ and }s\in\mathbb{C},

then
u\in W^{2m,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap C^{2m-1,h}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, for all
1\leq q<\infty
and
0<h<1
.

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
be a very weak solution of
Lu=g(x,u)
.
By Proposition
2.3
,
Lu=g(\cdot,u)\in L^{\frac{Np}{N+2mp}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
u\in W^{2m,\frac{Np}{N+2mp}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
in view of Lemma
3.1
.
Such a regularity implies that
u
is a strong solution of (
3.2
) and Theorem
2.1
yields
u\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

If
g
satisfies (
3.3
), then we define

a(x)=\frac{|g(x,u(x))|}{1+|u(x)|}

and proceed similarly as in (
2.14
) and below to show that
a\in L^{\frac{N}{2m}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
g
satisfies (
2.2
).
By the first part of the proof
u\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and the polynomial rate of growth (
3.3
) then implies that
Lu=g(\cdot,u)\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
We deduce utilizing Lemma
3.1
that
u\in W^{2m,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for every

.
The last assertion follows by the Sobolev embeddings
W^{2m,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\subset C^{2m-1,h}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
∎

We now consider a differential operator in a
divergence form

(3.4)

Lu=(-1)^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|,\,|\beta|\leq m}D^{\beta}\left(a_{\alpha\beta}(x)D^{\alpha}u\right),

where
a_{\alpha\beta}\in C^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}(\Omega)
.
We say that
L
is elliptic provided that

\mathfrak{Re}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=2m}a_{\alpha\beta}(x)\xi^{\alpha+\beta}\right)\geq\lambda|\xi|^{2m},\quad\text{for }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
We say that
u\in W^{m,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is a
weak solution
of

Lu=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,

if for every test function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)

\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\alpha|,\,|\beta|\leq m}(-1)^{m+|\beta|}a_{\alpha\beta}(x)D^{\alpha}u(x)D^{\beta}\varphi(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\varphi(x)\,dx.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
L
be the elliptic differential operator in the divergence form (
3.4
) with the coefficients
a_{\alpha\beta}\in C^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}(\Omega)
.
Let
u\in W^{m,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
with
p>\frac{N}{N-m}
be a weak solution of

Lu=g(x,u)\quad\text{in }\Omega.

If
g
satisfies (
2.2
), then
u\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
If
g
has the polynomial rate of growth: there is
r>0
such that

(3.5)

|g(x,s)|\lesssim 1+|s|^{r},\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega\text{ and }s\in\mathbb{C},\text{ where }r=\begin{cases}\frac{N+mp}{N-mp},&\text{if }p<\frac{N}{m},\\
\text{is arbitrary},&\text{if }p\geq\frac{N}{m},\end{cases}

then
u\in W^{2m,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap C^{2m-1,h}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for all
1\leq q<\infty
and
0<h<1
.

Proof.

If
p\geq\frac{N}{m}
, then
u\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
due to the Sobolev embedding.
Hence we assume that
p<\frac{N}{m}
.
Fix
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in the definition of a weak solution.
Using integration by parts and ordering the terms one may show that
u
is also a very weak solution to some differential operator in the non-divergence form
\tilde{L}u=(-1)^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2m}c_{\alpha}(x)D^{\alpha}u=g(x,u)
in
\Omega
.
The smoothness assumption on the coefficients

implies that
c_{\alpha}\in C^{|\alpha|}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, we have
\sum_{|\alpha|=2m}c_{\alpha}(x)\xi^{\alpha}=\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}a_{\alpha\beta}(x)\xi^{\alpha+\beta}
, so
\tilde{L}
is elliptic as well.
The Sobolev embedding

where
\frac{Np}{N-mp}>\frac{N}{N-2m}
and Theorem
3.2
proves the assertion.

Let us now assume that (
3.5
) holds.
If
p\geq\frac{N}{m}
, then the Sobolev embedding yields
u\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
If

, then in view of (

)

satisfies (
2.2
) with

what may be shown as above.
Therefore, in any case we obtain

.
The polynomial rate of growth implies that

, so
\tilde{L}u\in\bigcap_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
Lemma
3.1
and the Sobolev embeddings finishes the proof.
∎

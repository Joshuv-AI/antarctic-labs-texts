Quantum Curved Tetrahedron, Quantum Group Intertwiner Space, and Coherent States
Chen-Hung Hsiao, Qiaoyin Pan

Abstract
In this paper, we construct the phase space of a constantly curved tetrahedron with fixed triangle areas in terms of a pair of Darboux coordinates called the length and twist coordinates, which are in analogy to the Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates for flat connections, and their quantization. The curvature is identified to the value of the cosmological constant, either positive or negative. The physical Hilbert space is given by the 𝒰 q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 2 ) ) {{\mathcal{U}}_{q}({\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{u}}(2))} intertwiner space. We show that the quantum trace of quantum monodromies, defining the quantum length operators, form a fusion algebra and describe their representation theory. We also construct the coherent states in the physical Hilbert space labeled by the length and twist coordinates. These coherent states describe quantum curved tetrahedra and peak at points of the tetrahedron phase space. This works is closely related to 3+1 dimensional Loop Quantum Gravity with a non-vanishing cosmological constant. The coherent states constructed herein serve as good candidates for the application to the spinfoam model with a cosmological constant.

Introduction

Quantum tetrahedron is the building block in the theory of Loop Quantum Gravity (LQG).
It is essential for the construction of both the canonical formalism and covariant formalism, the latter also called the spinfoam model. In the canonical formalism, quantum tetrahedra build up the spacial quantum geometries, which evolve dynamically under the Wheeler-DeWitt equation. In the spinfoam model, five quantum tetrahedra are included on the boundary of a four-simplex, whose amplitude describes the elementary dynamics of a discretized four-manifold.

{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{u}}(2)
with a deformation parameter

In this case,
{{\mathcal{U}}_{q}({\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{u}}(2))}
has finitely many

The collection of the matrix elements of the quantum monodromy around one single puncture form an algebra called the

IV
Fusion algebra from quantum monodromies around two punctures

In the previous section, we have only considered the quantum monodromies around single punctures. However, the length coordinate that we are interested in in this paper is the trace of the monodromy around two punctures, whose quantization is still a quantum monodromy but the algebra it forms needs to be further investigated. Quite neatly, we will see that the matrix element of this quantum monodromy still forms a loop algebra and its gauge-invariant elements form a fusion algebra, also called a Verlinde algebra.

and
\mathbb{y^{*}}
is the same as that between
\mathbb{x}
and
\mathbb{y}
:

\mathbb{x^{*}}\mathbb{y^{*}}=q\mathbb{y^{*}}\mathbb{x^{*}}.

(87)

To end this section, let us point out an observation: the eigenvalue of
\mathbb{x}
has a periodicity condition. This implies that the quantum states in the auxiliary Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\text{aux}}
are those defined on the torus. This motivates our construction of coherent states in the next section.

VII
Coherent states in the auxiliary Hilbert space

We list the action of operators
\mathbb{x},\mathbb{y}
and
\mathbb{x^{*}},\mathbb{y^{*}}
on states in the sub-space of each representation label
J
:

\begin{split}\mathbb{x}(\Psi_{J})=e^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}(2J+1)}\Psi_{J}\quad&,\quad\mathbb{y}(\Psi_{J})=\Psi_{J+\frac{1}{2}}\;,\\
\mathbb{x^{*}}(\Psi_{J})=e^{-\frac{\pi i}{k+2}(2J+1)}\Psi_{J}\quad&,\quad\mathbb{y^{*}}(\Psi_{J})=\Psi_{J-\frac{1}{2}}\;,\end{split}

(88)

where
\Psi_{J}
is any state in the sub-space
W^{J}(K_{1},K_{2})\otimes W^{\bar{J}}(K_{3},K_{4})
for each representation label
J
. It is easy to check that (
83
) and (
87
) are indeed realized.

As mentioned in the end of the preceding section, the auxiliary space is the quantization of the torus as the phase space. Classically, a torus is viewed as a two-dimensional phase space with canonical coordinates
(q,p)\in[0,a)\times[0,b)
. Since they are on the torus, it is naturally equipped them with periodic conditions. In quantum theory, the periodic condition yields the quantization condition for any states on the torus:

ab=2\pi\hbar N\;,

(89)

where
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
For more details on quantum states on the torus, we refer to
[
10
]
as well as Appendix
C
.

For the auxiliary Hilbert space, the irreducible representations
J\in{\mathbb{N}}/2
fall within the range of
0
and
(k+\frac{3}{2})
, as this range corresponds to the periodicity of the eigenvalue of the operator
\mathbb{x}
as shown in (
82
).

Following
[
10
]
, we define a set of coherent states, denoted as
\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}
, as an
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
function on a torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{2}=S^{1}\times S^{1}
with parameters
(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})\in[0,2\pi)\times[0,2\pi)
being the angle coordinates on
{\mathbb{T}}_{2}
. (The derivation of the coherent states can be found in Appendix
C
.) When viewing
{\mathbb{T}}_{2}
as a phase space,
(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})
is a pair of canonical coordinates.
\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(x)
is defined as the superposition of basis
e_{l}(x)
with coefficient
\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(l)
:

\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(x):=\sum_{l=0}^{2k+3}\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(l)e_{l}(x)\,,

(90)

where

\displaystyle\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(l)

\displaystyle:=\left(\frac{1}{k+2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{i\tilde{Y}_{0}\tilde{X}_{0}(k+2)}{2\pi}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac{i(k+2)}{\pi}\tilde{Y}_{0}\left(l\frac{\pi}{k+2}-2\pi m^{\prime}-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)}e^{-\frac{k+2}{2\pi}(l\frac{\pi}{k+2}-2\pi m^{\prime}-\tilde{X}_{0})^{2}}\,,

(91)

\displaystyle e_{l}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{k+2}}\delta(x-2\pi\left(\frac{l}{2(k+2)}-j\right))\;.

Here
\delta(x-2\pi\left(\frac{l}{2(k+2)}-j\right))
is a Dirac distribution and the basis
e_{l}(x)
is given by the Dirac combs and is an orthonormal basis in
\mathbb{C}
.
Therefore,
\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(x)
takes non-trivial value only at discrete
x
’s.
The resolution of identity for coherent states is
[
10
]

\frac{k+2}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{2}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{X}_{0}\mathrm{d}\tilde{Y}_{0}|\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}\rangle\langle\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}|=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{H}_{\text{aux}}}\;,

(92)

where
|\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}\rangle
is defined as in (
90
) and
\langle\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}|=\sum_{l=0}^{2k+3}\bar{\xi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(l)e_{l}(x)
.
By definition, the inner product of the coherent state is defined as

\langle\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}|\psi^{\prime}_{(\tilde{X}_{0}^{\prime},\tilde{Y}_{0}^{\prime})}\rangle=\sum^{2k+3}_{l,\tilde{l}=0}\bar{\xi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(l)\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0}^{\prime},\tilde{Y}_{0}^{\prime})}(\tilde{l})\,.

(93)

The final expression of the coherent state
\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}
in the
\mathcal{H}_{\text{aux}}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{2(k+2)}
is:

\sum^{2k+3}_{l=0}\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(l)|l\rangle\;,

(94)

where we have used
|l\rangle\equiv e_{\ell}(x)
to denote the orthonormal basis in
\mathbb{C}^{2(k+2)}
. This notation will be used in the next section. From our construction of coherent states on the torus, the
2(k+2)-
dimensional auxiliary Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\text{aux}}
is decomposed as follows:
\mathcal{H}_{\text{aux}}=\bigoplus^{2k+3}_{l=0}V^{l}
.

VIII
Projection into
\mathcal{H}
and the expectation value

In the preceeding section, we have constructed the coherent state in the auxiliary Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\text{aux}}
, which can be decomposed into subspace
V^{l}
, and where the eigenvector of
{\bf x}
is associated to the eigenvalue
e^{\frac{i\pi}{k+2}(l+1)}
. However, the coherent state that corresponds to our quantum system are in the
\mathcal{H}
rather than
\mathcal{H}_{\text{aux}}
. The physical Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is decomposed into
\sum_{J}W^{J}\otimes W^{\bar{J}}
, where
J
can only fall within the range of
\max\left(|K_{1}-K_{2}|,|K_{3}-K_{4}|\right)\leq J\leq\min\left(u(K_{1},K_{2}),u(K_{3},K_{4})\right)
and the difference between closest
J
’s is
1
instead of
\frac{1}{2}
.

Let us recall that the action of operators
\mathbb{x}
and
\mathbb{y}
on the sub-space
V^{l}
, where
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
falls within the range of
0
and
2k+3
, is defined as:

\mathbb{x}(\Psi_{l})=e^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}(l+1)}\Psi_{l}\quad,\quad\mathbb{y}(\Psi_{l})=\Psi_{l+1}\,,\quad\forall\,\Psi_{l}\in V^{l}\,.

(95)

The actions of
\mathbb{x}
and
\mathbb{y}
indeed admit the commutation relation (
83
).
Denote
u(K_{\nu})\equiv\min\left(u(K_{1},K_{2}),u(K_{3},K_{4})\right)
and
m(K_{\nu})\equiv\max\left(|K_{1}-K_{2}|,|K_{3}-K_{4}|\right)
.
The physical Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
is the subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm aux}
as each
J
subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
is identical to the subspace
V^{2J}
of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm aux}
due to the fact that
\bigoplus^{u(K_{\nu})}_{J=m(K_{\nu})}V^{J}
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus^{2u(K_{\nu})}_{2J=2m(K_{\nu})}V^{2J}
and both have the same eigenvalue as
e^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}(2J+1)}
. We define a projection map
P_{n}:{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm aux}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}

P_{n}=\sum^{2u(K_{\nu})}_{2J=2m(K_{\nu})}|2J\rangle\langle 2J|\,,

(96)

where
n
denotes the dimension of
\mathcal{H}
, i.e.
n=2u(K_{\nu})-2m(K_{\nu})+1
and
|2J\rangle
is an orthonormal basis in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm aux}
.

Proposition VIII.1
.

P_{n}
are the projectors that project the space
\mathcal{H}_{\text{aux}}
onto
\mathcal{H}
, they satisfy the following relations:

P_{n}P_{n^{\prime}}=P_{\min(n,n^{\prime})}\quad,\quad(P_{n})^{*}=P_{n}\,.

(97)

Proof.

\displaystyle P_{n}P_{n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{2J}\sum_{2J^{\prime}}|2J\rangle\langle 2J|2J^{\prime}\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime}|=\delta_{J,J^{\prime}}\sum_{2J}|2J\rangle\langle 2J|=P_{\min(n,n^{\prime})}\,,

(98)

\displaystyle(P_{n})^{*}

\displaystyle=(\sum_{2J}|2J\rangle\langle 2J|)^{*}=\sum_{2J}|2J\rangle\langle 2J|=P_{n}\,.

∎

Given a coherent state
|\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}\rangle=\sum_{l=0}^{2k+3}\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(l)|l\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\text{aux}}
, the projected coherent state
|\widetilde{\psi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}\rangle\equiv P_{n}|\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}\rangle
is defined as

|\widetilde{\psi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}\rangle=\sum_{2J=2m(K_{\nu})}^{2u(K_{\nu})}|2J\rangle\langle 2J|\psi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}\rangle=\sum_{2J=2m(K_{\nu})}^{2u(K_{\nu})}\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(2J)\,|2J\rangle\,.

(99)

Proposition VIII.2
.

Given elements
\mathbb{x}
and
\mathbb{y}
that form a Weyl algebra that quantizes a torus satisfying
\mathbb{xy}=q^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{yx}
, the projected elements
\mathbb{\tilde{x}}:=P_{n}\mathbb{x}P_{n}
and
\mathbb{\tilde{y}}:=P_{n}\mathbb{y}^{2}P_{n}
satisfy the same commutation relation of a Weyl algebra with
q^{\frac{1}{2}}\to q
. That is,

\mathbb{\tilde{x}\tilde{y}}=q\mathbb{\tilde{y}\tilde{x}}\,.

(100)

Proof.

We prove it by direct calculation for both sides of (
100
).
For the
l.h.s.
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\bf x}\tilde{\bf y}

\displaystyle:=P_{n}{\bf x}P_{n}P_{n}{\bf y}^{2}P_{n}=P_{n}{\bf x}P_{n}{\bf y}^{2}P_{n}=\sum_{J,J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime}}|2J\rangle\langle 2J|{\bf x}|2J^{\prime}\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime}|{\bf y}^{2}|2J^{\prime\prime}\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime\prime}|

(101)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}\left(2J^{\prime}+1\right)}\sum_{J,J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime}}|2J\rangle\langle 2J|2J^{\prime}\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime}|2J^{\prime\prime}+2\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime\prime}|=qe^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}\left(2J^{\prime}+1\right)}\sum_{J,J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime}}\delta_{J,J^{\prime}}\delta_{J^{\prime\prime}+1,J^{\prime}}|2J^{\prime\prime}+2\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime\prime}|

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}\left(2J^{\prime\prime}+3\right)}\sum_{J^{\prime\prime}}|2J^{\prime\prime}+2\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime\prime}|\equiv qe^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}\left(2J^{\prime\prime}+1\right)}\sum_{J^{\prime\prime}}|2J^{\prime\prime}+2\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime\prime}|\,,

where the summations are from
m(K_{\nu})
to
u(K_{\nu})
.
For the
r.h.s.
,

\displaystyle q\tilde{\bf y}\tilde{\bf x}

\displaystyle:=qP_{n}{\bf y}^{2}P_{n}P_{n}{\bf x}P_{n}=qP_{n}{\bf y}^{2}P_{n}{\bf x}P_{n}=q\sum_{J,J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime}}|2J\rangle\langle 2J|{\bf y}^{2}|2J^{\prime}\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime}|{\bf x}|2J^{\prime\prime}\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime\prime}|

(102)

\displaystyle=qe^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}\left(2J^{\prime\prime}+1\right)}\sum_{J,J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime}}|2J\rangle\langle 2J|2J^{\prime}+2\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime}|2J^{\prime\prime}\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime\prime}|=qe^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}\left(2J^{\prime\prime}+1\right)}\sum_{J,J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime}}\delta_{J,J^{\prime}+1}\delta_{J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime}}|2J^{\prime\prime}+2\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime\prime}|

\displaystyle=qe^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}\left(2J^{\prime\prime}+1\right)}\sum_{J^{\prime\prime}}|2J^{\prime\prime}+2\rangle\langle 2J^{\prime\prime}|\equiv l.h.s.\,.

∎

This shows that the operators
\tilde{\bf x}
and
\tilde{\bf y}
introduced in
VI
can be defined by projection of operators
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
, which is the reason why we use the same notation to denote these operators here.

The resolution of identity (
92
) for coherent states in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm aux}

induces the resolution of identity for coherent states in
{\mathcal{H}}
, which reads

\frac{k+2}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{X}_{0}\mathrm{d}\tilde{Y}_{0}|\widetilde{\psi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}\rangle\langle\widetilde{\psi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}|=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{H}}\,.

(103)

The coherent state labels
(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})
encode the classical length-twist coordinates, which can be seen by calculating the expectation values of the operators
\tilde{\bf x}
and
\tilde{\bf y}
in the coherent state representation as shown in the following proposition.

Proposition VIII.3
.

In the semi-classical limit, where
k
and the four representations
K_{1},K_{2},K_{3},K_{4}
increase at the same rate (i.e.,
k=\lambda k
,
K_{\nu}=\lambda K_{\nu}
, and
\lambda\to\infty
), the expectation values of
\tilde{\bf x}
and
\tilde{\bf y}
in the projected coherent state representation behave as

\langle\tilde{\mathbb{x}}\rangle=e^{i\tilde{X}_{0}}+O\left(e^{-\lambda}/\sqrt{\lambda}\right)\,,\quad\langle\tilde{\mathbb{y}}\rangle=e^{2i\tilde{Y}_{0}}+O\left(e^{-\lambda}/\sqrt{\lambda}\right)\;,

(104)

given that the position coordinate
\tilde{X}_{0}
labeling the coherent state
\widetilde{\psi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}
satisfies the triangle inequality:
2\max\left(\frac{|K_{1}-K_{2}|}{k+2},\frac{|K_{3}-K_{4}|}{k+2}\right)\leq\frac{\tilde{X}_{0}}{\pi}\leq 2\min\left(\frac{u(K_{1},K_{2})}{k+2},\frac{u(K_{3},K_{4})}{k+2}\right)
with
u(K_{1},K_{2})=\min(K_{1}+K_{2},k-K_{1}-K_{2})
. Otherwise, the expectation values of
\tilde{\mathbb{x}}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{y}}
exponential decay as

\langle\tilde{\mathbb{x}}\rangle=O\left(e^{-\lambda}/\sqrt{\lambda}\right)\;,\quad\langle\tilde{\mathbb{y}}\rangle=O\left(e^{-\lambda}/\sqrt{\lambda}\right)\;.

(105)

Proof.

We first calculate that

\begin{split}\langle\tilde{\bf x}\rangle:=\langle\widetilde{\psi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}|\tilde{\bf x}|\widetilde{\psi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}\rangle&=\sum_{2J,2J^{\prime}}\bar{\xi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(2J)\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(2J^{\prime})\langle 2J|\tilde{{\bf x}}|2J^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{2J}e^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}(2J+1)}\bar{\xi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(2J)\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(2J)\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{1}{k+2}}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-i2(k+2)\tilde{Y}_{0}(s-s^{\prime})}\sum_{2J}e^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}(2J+1)}e^{-\frac{k+2}{2\pi}\left[\left(\pi\frac{2J}{k+2}-2\pi s-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}+\left(\pi\frac{2J}{k+2}-2\pi s^{\prime}-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}\right]}\,,\end{split}

(106)

where the summation over
2J
is from
2m(K_{\nu})
to
2u(K_{\nu})
as before. To proceed, we first apply the Poisson resummation formula

\sum_{l=l_{i}}^{l_{f}}f(l)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{l_{i}-\delta}^{l_{f}+1-\delta}{\rm d}l\,f(l)e^{2\pi inl}\,,\quad\delta>0\text{ arbitrarily small}

(107)

to rewrite
\sum_{2J}
in (
106
) in into an integral.
Denote
m:={2m(K_{\nu})}
and
u:={2u(K_{\nu})+1}
for simplicity.
Then (
106
) becomes

\langle\tilde{{\bf x}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(k+2)}}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-i2(k+2)\tilde{Y}_{0}(s-s^{\prime})}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{{m}-\delta}^{u-\delta}{\rm d}(2J)\,e^{\frac{\pi i}{k+2}(2J+1)+4\pi iJn}e^{-\frac{k+2}{2\pi}\left[\left(\pi\frac{2J}{k+2}-2\pi s-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}+\left(\pi\frac{2J}{k+2}-2\pi s^{\prime}-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}\right]}\,.

(108)

We observe that the integration can be written in terms of the error function whose asymptotic expansion behaves as

{\rm erf}(x):=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-t^{2}}{\rm d}t=1-\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}x}\left(1+O(1/x^{2})\right),\quad x\gg 1\,.

(109)

Therefore, the asymptotic expansion of the integration
\int_{b}^{a}e^{-cx^{2}}{\rm d}x
with
a>b
and
c>0
decays exponentially unless
a,b
have different signs, or equivalently, the integrand
e^{-cx^{2}}
peaks within the range
[a,b]
. More precisely,

\int_{a}^{b}e^{-cx^{2}}{\rm d}x=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{4c}}\left({\rm erf}(b\sqrt{c})-{\rm erf}((a\sqrt{c})\right)\,\xrightarrow[\lambda\rightarrow\infty]{a\rightarrow\lambda a,\ b\rightarrow\lambda b}\ O\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\lambda}\right)&{\rm if\ }0\leq a<b\\
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{4c}}\left({\rm erf}(b\sqrt{c})+{\rm erf}((a\sqrt{c})\right)\>\xrightarrow[\lambda\rightarrow\infty]{a\rightarrow\lambda a,\ b\rightarrow\lambda b}\ \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{c}}+O\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\lambda}\right)&{\rm if\ }a<0<b\\
-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{4c}}\left({\rm erf}(b\sqrt{c})-{\rm erf}((a\sqrt{c})\right)\>\xrightarrow[\lambda\rightarrow\infty]{a\rightarrow\lambda a,\ b\rightarrow\lambda b}\ O\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\lambda}\right)&{\rm if\ }a<b\leq 0\end{cases}\,.

(110)

The integrand of (
108
) peaks at
\frac{2J}{k+2}=\frac{i(n(k+2)+\frac{1}{2})}{k+2}+(s+s^{\prime})+\frac{x}{\pi}
, where it reads

\exp\left[(k+2)\left(-\pi n^{2}-\pi(s-s^{\prime})^{2}+2in(\pi(s+s^{\prime})+\tilde{X}_{0})\right)-\frac{\pi\left(\frac{1}{4}-i\frac{1}{2}\right)}{k+2}+(-\pi n+i(\pi(s+s^{\prime})+\tilde{X}_{0}))\right]\,.

(111)

At large
k
, the dominant contribution comes from the term
n=0,s^{\prime}=s
of the summation. In this case, the peak is
\frac{2J}{k+2}=\frac{\tilde{X}_{0}}{\pi}+2s+i\frac{1}{2(k+2)}\xrightarrow[\lambda\rightarrow\infty]{k\rightarrow\lambda k}\frac{\tilde{X}_{0}}{\pi}+2s
, which is within the range
[\tilde{m},\tilde{u}]
only if
s=0
. Therefore, (
108
) can be simplified at the
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
approximation (taking into account that the constant
c
takes the value
\pi(k+2)
when using (
110
))

\langle\tilde{{\bf x}}\rangle\xrightarrow[\lambda\rightarrow\infty]{K_{\nu}\rightarrow\lambda K_{\nu},k\rightarrow\lambda k}e^{i\tilde{X}_{0}}+O\left(e^{-\lambda}/\sqrt{\lambda}\right)\,

(112)

where
2\max\left(\frac{|K_{1}-K_{2}|}{k+2},\frac{|K_{3}-K_{4}|}{k+2}\right)\leq\frac{\tilde{X}_{0}}{\pi}\leq 2\min\left(\frac{u(K_{1},K_{2})}{k+2},\frac{u(K_{3},K_{4})}{k+2}\right)
. Otherwise,
\langle\tilde{{\bf x}}\rangle=O\left(e^{-\lambda}/\sqrt{\lambda}\right)
.

The expectation value of
\tilde{\bf y}
is calculated in the same way:

\begin{split}\langle\tilde{{\bf y}}\rangle&:=\langle\tilde{\psi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}|\tilde{{\bf y}}|\tilde{\psi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}\rangle=\sum_{2J,2J^{\prime}}\bar{\xi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(2J^{\prime})\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(2J)\langle 2J|\tilde{{\bf y}}|2J^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{2J}\bar{\xi}_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(2J+2)\xi_{(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{Y}_{0})}(2J)\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{k+2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-i2(k+2)\tilde{Y}_{0}(s-s^{\prime})}e^{i2\tilde{Y}_{0}}\sum_{2J}e^{-\frac{k+2}{2\pi}\left[\left(\pi\frac{2J+2}{k+2}-2\pi s-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}+\left(\pi\frac{2J}{k+2}-2\pi s^{\prime}-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}\right]}\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{k+2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-i2(k+2)\tilde{Y}_{0}(s-s^{\prime})+i2\tilde{Y}_{0}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2m-\delta}^{2u-\delta}{\rm d}(2J)\,e^{-\frac{k+2}{2\pi}\left[\left(\pi\frac{2J+2}{k+2}-2\pi s-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}+\left(\pi\frac{2J}{k+2}-2\pi s^{\prime}-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}\right]}\,,\end{split}

(113)

where we have used the Poisson resummation in the third line.
The peak of the integrand in the last line is at
\frac{2J}{k+2}=(s+s^{\prime})+\frac{\tilde{X}_{0}}{\pi}-\frac{1}{k+2}\xrightarrow[\lambda\rightarrow\infty]{k\rightarrow\lambda k}(s+s^{\prime})+\frac{\tilde{X}_{0}}{\pi}
, which is within the range
[\frac{m}{k+2},\frac{u}{k+2}]
only if
s+s^{\prime}=0
then the peak is at
\frac{2J}{k+2}=\frac{\tilde{X}_{0}}{\pi}
. At the peak, the integrand takes the form

\begin{split}e^{-\frac{k+2}{2\pi}\left[\left(\pi\frac{2J}{k+2}+\frac{2\pi}{k+2}-2\pi s-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}+\left(\pi\frac{2J}{k+2}-2\pi s^{\prime}-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}\right]}&\xrightarrow{\frac{2J}{k+2}=\frac{\tilde{X}_{0}}{\pi}}e^{-\frac{k+2}{2\pi}\left[\left(\tilde{X}_{0}+\frac{2\pi}{k+2}-2\pi s-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}+\left(\tilde{X}_{0}-2\pi s^{\prime}-\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}\right]}\\
&\xrightarrow{k\rightarrow\infty}e^{-\frac{k+2}{2\pi}\left[\left(2\pi s\right)^{2}+\left(2\pi s^{\prime}\right)^{2}\right]}\,,\end{split}

(114)

which decays exponentially at large
k
unless
s=s^{\prime}=0
. We therefore conclude that

\langle\tilde{{\bf y}}\rangle\xrightarrow[\lambda\rightarrow\infty]{K_{\nu}\rightarrow\lambda K_{\nu},k\rightarrow\lambda k}e^{2i\tilde{Y}_{0}}+O\left(e^{-\lambda}/\sqrt{\lambda}\right),

(115)

when
2\max\left(\frac{|K_{1}-K_{2}|}{k+2},\frac{|K_{3}-K_{4}|}{k+2}\right)\leq\frac{\tilde{X}_{0}}{\pi}\leq 2\min\left(\frac{u(K_{1},K_{2})}{k+2},\frac{u(K_{3},K_{4})}{k+2}\right)
. Otherwise,
\langle\tilde{{\bf y}}\rangle=O\left(e^{-\lambda}/\sqrt{\lambda}\right)
.
∎

So far, we have only calculated the expectation values of
\tilde{\mathbb{x}}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{y}}
. However, in general, one can find the expectation value of a polynomial in
\tilde{\mathbb{x}}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{y}}
. The fact that the sum

has an upper limit restricts the power order
\mu

can have. Acting

(116)

where sums are from

to
u(K_{\nu})\equiv\min\left(u(K_{1},K_{2}),u(K_{3},K_{4})\right)
.
From the calculation above, we obtain the range that

must fall within:

0\leq\mu\leq u(K_{\nu})-m(K_{\nu})\;.

(117)

The state is set to

if
\mu
is greater than the allowed value.

We have, therefore, seen that the coherent state

provides a complete basis spanning the intertwiner space of a quantum curved tetrahedron as a Hilbert space, and that the operators
\tilde{\bf x}
and
\tilde{\bf y}
quantize the length coordinate
x
and twist coordinate

respectively as multiplication and derivative operators on the Hilbert space. Let the coherent state labels

defined in (

). Then the expectation values of
\tilde{\bf x}
and
\tilde{\bf y}
correspond to their classical counterparts,

and

respectively. Both
\tilde{X}_{0}
and
\tilde{Y}_{0}
indeed fall within the range from

to
\pi
, considering that the triangle inequality is satisfied. This inequality,

, where

, is considered as the quantum counterpart of (

IX
Conclusion and discussion

In this work, we have constructed the algebra generated by the quantum monodromies
{\bf M}^{I}_{\ell}
, where the loop
\ell
encloses a pair of punctures. We have proved that the algebra generated by
{\bf M}^{I}_{\ell}
forms a loop algebra and that the
q
-deformed Wilson loop operators
c^{I}_{\ell}
constructed from the
{\bf M}^{I}_{\ell}
form a fusion algebra. The quantum diagonal length operator is obtained from
c^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\ell}
. A set of coherent states is constructed directly in the intertwiner space and the expectation value of length and twist operators in the semi-classical limit peak at points of the phase space, each describing the shape of a constantly curved tetrahedron.
We have also shown that not all coherent states are geometrical states for technical reasons. Firstly, the coherent state labels
\tilde{X}_{0}
and
\tilde{Y}_{0}
label a point on the torus, but the phase space of the shape of a tetrahedron is only the subspace of the torus phase space. Furthermore, the integration of the resolution of the identity is over the entire torus, which means that the coherent intertwiner is not in one-to-one correspondence with the classical tetrahedron.

The coherent states constructed in this paper may be adapted to construct the spinfoam model with a non-zero cosmological constant
\Lambda
similar to the one introduced in
[
18
]
, where the coherent state labels were chosen to be the Fock-Goncharov coordinates. These coordinates are also coordinates of the tetrahedron shape phase space, but are not Darboux coordinates and do not have natural holonomy interpretation as the length variables used in this paper, which makes it more difficult to connect to the canonical quantization approach of LQG (with
\Lambda
). We expect that the adjustment of coherent state coupling to the partition function in building the spinfoam model could lead to a more feasible model, which is easier for applications.

The Guillemin-Sternberg theorem
[
19
]
guarantees that the quantization commutes with reduction as illustrated in the following commuting diagram:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}(\mathrm{SU}(2))^{\otimes 4}&\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\text{ quantization }\hskip 8.19447pt}&\begin{array}[]{c}V^{K_{1}}\otimes V^{K_{2}}\otimes V^{K_{3}}\otimes V^{K_{4}}\end{array}\\
\phantom{\text{quantization}}\left\downarrow\rule{0.0pt}{28.45274pt}\right.\text{\small symplectic reduction}&&\phantom{\text{quantization}}\left\downarrow\rule{0.0pt}{28.45274pt}\right.\text{\small quantum reduction}\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{flat}}(\Sigma_{0,4},\mathrm{SU}(2))&\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\text{ quantization }\hskip 8.19447pt}&\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Inv}_{q}(K_{1},K_{2},K_{3},K_{4})\end{array}\,.\end{array}

(118)

In our approach, we proceed the quantization first, and the reduction after. However, we may try the other route as in
[
5
]
, i.e. to proceed the geometric quantization directly on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{flat}}(\Sigma_{0,4},\mathrm{SU}(2))
. The Hilbert space should be the conformal blocks of the WZW model, which is identified with the Hilbert space constructed by the combinatorial quantization
[
9
]
. One may define the coherent intertwiners in this manner. It would be interesting to investigate the isomorphism between those states and the coherent states we constructed in this paper.

Appendix C
Quantum State on the torus and coherent state

In this appendix, we briefly review the construction and features of the generic quantum states and coherent states on the torus
[
10
]
and give the derivation explicitly. The auxiliary space
\mathcal{H}_{\text{aux}}
is the quantization of the torus as the phase space. Suppose that we have a pair of operators
(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{P})
with the commutation relation
[\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{P}]=i\hbar
. Since they are on the torus it is natural to impose the periodic condition. The periodic condition is defined in
[
10
]
as

U(a,0)\Psi=e^{-i\kappa_{1}a}\Psi\quad,\quad U(0,b)\Psi=e^{-i\kappa_{2}b}\Psi,

(139)

where the operator is defined as
U(\alpha,\beta)=e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}(\beta\mathbb{Q}-\alpha\mathbb{P})}
and
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})\in

[0,\frac{2\pi}{a})\times[0,\frac{2\pi}{b})
.
A state on the torus can be obtained from arbitrary Schwarz function by the map
\mathrm{P}_{\kappa}

[
10
]
. Given any Schwarz function
\psi(x),\;x\in\mathbb{R}
, one can construct a state on the torus from it as
[
10
]
:

\displaystyle\psi(x)\mapsto\mathrm{P}_{\kappa}\psi(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{Nmn}e^{i\left(\kappa_{1}ma-\kappa_{2}nb\right)}U(ma,nb)\psi(x)=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{Nmn}e^{i\left(\kappa_{1}ma-\kappa_{2}nb\right)}e^{-\frac{i}{2\hbar}mnab}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}nbx}\psi(x-ma)

(140)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i\kappa_{1}ma}\psi(x-ma)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}nb\left(x-\kappa_{2}\hbar-\frac{1}{2}ma\right)+i\pi Nmn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i\kappa_{1}ma}\psi(x-ma)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i2\pi n\left(\frac{N}{a}(x-\kappa_{2}\hbar-\frac{1}{2}ma)+\frac{1}{2}Nm\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i\kappa_{1}ma}\psi(x-ma)\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta\left(\frac{N}{a}(x-\kappa_{2}\hbar-\frac{1}{2}ma)+\frac{1}{2}Nm-l\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i\kappa_{1}ma}\psi(x-ma)\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{a}{N}\delta\left(x-\kappa_{2}\hbar+\frac{1}{2}ma-\frac{1}{2}ma-l\frac{a}{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i\kappa_{1}ma}\psi(\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}+l\frac{a}{N}-ma)\frac{a}{N}\delta\left(x-\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}-l\frac{a}{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}e^{i\kappa_{1}ma}\psi(\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}+l\frac{a}{N}-ma)\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}\delta\left(x-\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}-l\frac{a}{N}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{l}(\psi)\delta_{l}(x)\,,

where
c_{l}(\psi):=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}e^{i\kappa_{1}ma}\psi(\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}+l\frac{a}{N}-ma)
and
\delta_{l}(x):=\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}\delta\left(x-\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}-l\frac{a}{N}\right)
.
The state
\mathrm{P}_{\kappa}\psi(x)
is in an
N
dimensional vector space denoted as
S^{\prime}(\mathbf{\kappa},N)

[
10
]
. By definition, the inner product is defined as

\langle\mathrm{P}_{\kappa}\psi|\mathrm{P}_{\kappa}\psi^{\prime}\rangle=\sum^{N-1}_{l=0}\bar{c_{l}}(\psi)c_{l}(\psi^{\prime}).

(141)

By applying the periodicity condition on the state, one has the periodicity condition for the coefficients
c_{l}(\psi)
, which we now show. Firstly,

\displaystyle U(a,0)\delta_{l}(x)

\displaystyle=U(a,0)\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}\delta\left(x-\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}-l\frac{a}{N}\right)=e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}aP}\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}\delta\left(x-\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}-l\frac{a}{N}\right)

(142)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}\delta\left(x-\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}-(l+N)\frac{a}{N}\right)\,.

Let
l^{\prime}=l+N
, then

\displaystyle U(a,0)\delta_{l}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}e^{i\kappa_{1}ma}\psi(\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}+l\frac{a}{N}-ma)\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}\delta\left(x-\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}-(l+N)\frac{a}{N}\right)\right)

(143)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}e^{i\kappa_{1}ma}\psi(\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}+(l^{\prime}-N)\frac{a}{N}-ma)\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{a}{N}}\delta\left(x-\frac{ab}{2\pi N}\kappa_{2}-(l^{\prime})\frac{a}{N}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{l-N}(\psi)\delta_{l}(x)\,.

Therefore,

U(a,0)\psi=e^{-i\kappa_{1}a}\psi=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-i\kappa_{1}a}c_{l}(\psi)\delta_{l}(x)\,.

(144)

We then obtain the periodicity condition on the coefficients themselves:

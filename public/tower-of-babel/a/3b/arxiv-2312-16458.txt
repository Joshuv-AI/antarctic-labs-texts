Abstract
. We provide convergence in the quantum Gromov-Hausdorff propinquity of Latrémolière of some sequences of infinite-dimensional Leibniz compact quantum metric spaces of Rieffel given by AF algebras and Christensen-Ivan spectral spaces. The main examples are convergence of Effros-Shen algebras and UHF algebras.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 46L89, 46L30, 58B34.

1.
Introduction and Background

The first example of convergence of sequences of infinite-dimensional quantum metric spaces was established by Rieffel
[
22
]
, where he showed that the quantum tori converged with respect their parameters that defined their anti-commutation relation. This was accomplished by the introduction of the theory of quantum metric spaces and a noncommutative analogue to the Gromov-Hausdorff distance both introduce by Rieffel in
[
21
,
22
]
, respectively. This introduced an new field of study known as noncommutative metric geometry, which has its roots from work of Connes in
[
6
,
5
]
and Gromov
[
9
]
.

Since the introduction of Rieffel’s noncommutative analogue to the Gromov-Hausdorff distance, there has been much progress in developing noncommutative analogues of the Gromov-Hausdorff distance to capture the C*-algebraic structure of the quantum metric space
[
19
,
11
,
24
,
15
,
14
]
. In particular, in
[
13
]
, Latrémolière proved convergence of the quantum tori in this stronger sense. Moreoever,
the Gromov-Hausdorff propinquity of Latrémolière first introduced in
[
15
]
has been adapted to capture more structure such as module structure
[
12
]
and spectral triple structure
[
18
,
17
]
.

Another example of convergence of infinite-dimensional quantum metric spaces appeared in
[
2
]
, where it was shown that the Effros-Shen algebras
[
8
]
are continuous in Gromov-Hausdorff propinquity with respect to their natural parameter space of irrationals in
(0,1)
with the usuual topology. It was also show that UHF algebras are continuous with respect to their natural parameter space of multiplicity sequences metrized by the Baire space. This was accomplished by introducing new quantum metrics on AF algebras equipped with faithful tracial state motivated by work of Christensen and Ivan in
[
4
]
. Now, in
[
4
]
, Christensen and Ivan did introduce quantum metrics on these infinite-dimensional algebras, but at the time it wasn’t clear how to provide convergence of these algebras in any noncommutative analogue to the Gromov-Hausdorff distance, which is one reason why the quantum metrics of
[
2
]
were introduced which are not defined using spectral triples. But, in an effort, to bring the realms of noncommutative geometry and noncommutative metric geometry closer, it is important to provide the convergence results of these infinite-dimensional algebras of
[
2
]
with the quantum metrics induced by the spectral triples of
[
4
]
, which is exactly what is accomplished in this article.

The main hurdles to overcome in proving this arise from two issues that were circumvented by the quantum metrics introduced in
[
2
]
. First, the spectral triples of
[
4
]
are constructed using equivalence constants which are only provided by existence and not explicitly given, which cause an issue when providing continuous fields of
L
-seminorms as it is difficult to control these non-explicit constants. Second, providing continuous fields of
L
-seminorms provided by faithful tracial states is difficult when relying on convergence in various operator norms given by different GNS represenations for each spectral triple rather than a fixed C*-norm. The first issue is overcome by an application of
[
1
, Lemma 3.9]
, and the second issue is overcome by a generalization of
[
1
, Lemma 3.9]
. Both of these issues are overcome in Section
2
, and we apply these results in the last section to provide convergence of these infinite-dimensional algebras using quantum metrics induced by Christensen-Ivan spectral triples. We only define what we mean by a Leibniz compact quantum metric space as things can get quite overwhelming as more definitions are provided, but for references regarding quantum metric spaces, propinquity and propinquity in the context of AF algebras see
[
23
,
15
,
2
,
16
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

[

,
15
]

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital C*-algebra with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
and unit
1_{\mathcal{A}}
. Let

(2)

(3)

(4)

the metric on the state space

of

metrizes the weak* topology,

then we call

The following fact is stated after
[
3

Proposition 2.3
.

Using the setting of Theorem
2.2
, we have for every
n\in{\mathds{N}}
and for every
a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
that

L^{\tau}_{\beta}(a)=\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]\|_{B({\mathcal{A}}^{\tau}_{n})},

where
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{\tau}={\mathcal{A}}_{n}
but for
B({\mathcal{A}}^{\tau}_{n})
we are considering bounded operators with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
on
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathds{N}}
. Let
a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
. By definition, we have that

\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]\|_{B({\mathcal{A}}_{n})}\leqslant L^{\tau}_{\beta}(a).

Next, let
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. We have since
\pi_{\tau}(a)
commutes with
P^{\tau}_{n}
by the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
or the proof of Step 1 of
[
2
, Theorem 3.5]
. Moreover,
P_{n}P_{k}=P_{k}P_{n}=P_{k}
and
P_{n}P_{k-1}=P_{k-1}P_{n}=P_{k-1}
by construction. Thus

\displaystyle P^{\tau}_{n}[Q^{\tau}_{k},\pi_{\tau}(a)]P^{\tau}_{n}

\displaystyle=P^{\tau}_{n}(Q^{\tau}_{k}\pi_{\tau}(a)-\pi_{\tau}(a)Q^{\tau}_{k})P^{\tau}_{n}

\displaystyle=P^{\tau}_{n}((P^{\tau}_{k}-P^{\tau}_{k-1})\pi_{\tau}(a)-\pi_{\tau}(a)(P^{\tau}_{k}-P^{\tau}_{k-1}))P^{\tau}_{n}

\displaystyle=(P^{\tau}_{k}-P^{\tau}_{k-1})\pi_{\tau}(a)P^{\tau}_{n}-P^{\tau}_{n}\pi_{\tau}(a)(P^{\tau}_{k}-P^{\tau}_{k-1})

\displaystyle=(P^{\tau}_{k}-P^{\tau}_{k-1})P^{\tau}_{n}\pi_{\tau}(a)-\pi_{\tau}(a)P^{\tau}_{n}(P^{\tau}_{k}-P^{\tau}_{k-1})

\displaystyle=(P^{\tau}_{k}-P^{\tau}_{k-1})\pi_{\tau}(a)-\pi_{\tau}(a)(P^{\tau}_{k}-P^{\tau}_{k-1})

\displaystyle=Q^{\tau}_{k}\pi_{\tau}(a)-\pi_{\tau}(a)Q^{\tau}_{k}

\displaystyle=[Q^{\tau}_{k},\pi_{\tau}(a)]

Thus

\displaystyle[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a^{\tau}_{\beta,k}[Q^{\tau}_{k},\pi_{\tau}(a)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a^{\tau}_{\beta,k}P^{\tau}_{n}[Q^{\tau}_{k},\pi_{\tau}(a)]P^{\tau}_{n}

\displaystyle=P^{\tau}_{n}[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]P^{\tau}_{n}.

Hence, since
(P^{\tau}_{n})^{2}=P^{\tau}_{n}
and
P^{\tau}_{n}
is contractive with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
and
P^{\tau}_{n}(h)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
for every
h\in H_{\tau}
, we have

\displaystyle L^{\tau}_{\beta}(a)

\displaystyle=\|P^{\tau}_{n}[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]P^{\tau}_{n}\|_{B(H_{\tau})}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\|P^{\tau}_{n}[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]P^{\tau}_{n}(h)\|_{\tau}:h\in H_{\tau},\|h\|_{\tau}\leqslant 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\|P^{\tau}_{n}[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)](P^{\tau}_{n})^{2}(h)\|_{\tau}:h\in H_{\tau},\|h\|_{\tau}\leqslant 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\|P^{\tau}_{n}[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]P^{\tau}_{n}(P^{\tau}_{n}(h))\|_{\tau}:h\in H_{\tau},\|h\|_{\tau}\leqslant 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)](P^{\tau}_{n}(h))\|_{\tau}:h\in H_{\tau},\|h\|_{\tau}\leqslant 1\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup\left\{\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]\|_{\tau}:h\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n},\|h\|_{\tau}\leqslant 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]\|_{B({\mathcal{A}}_{n})}.

Therefore
\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]\|_{B({\mathcal{A}}_{n})}\leqslant L^{\tau}_{\beta}(a)\leqslant\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(a)]\|_{B({\mathcal{A}}_{n})}
as desired.
∎

[
20

(1)

{\mathcal{B}}_{0}={\mathds{C}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n_{n}}{\mathfrak{M}}_{d_{n,k}}({\mathds{C}})

n\in{\mathds{N}}\setminus\{0\}

n\in{\mathds{N}}
,
set

{\mathcal{A}}_{n}=\alpha^{(n)}({\mathcal{B}}_{n}).

As above, for each
n\in{\mathds{N}}
, let

denote the unique
\tau

For each

\tau_{n}=\tau\circ\alpha^{(n)},

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
n\in{\mathds{N}}
. It holds that

L^{\tau}_{\beta}\circ\alpha^{(n)}(a)=\sup\{\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))](\alpha^{(n)}(b))\|_{\tau}:b\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n},\|b\|_{\tau_{n}}\leqslant 1\}=L^{\tau_{n}}_{\beta}(a)

for every
a\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathds{N}}
and let
a\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
, then by Proposition
2.3

\displaystyle L^{\tau}_{\beta}\circ\alpha^{(n)}(a)

\displaystyle=L^{\tau}_{\beta}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))

\displaystyle=\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))]\|_{B({\mathcal{A}}^{\tau}_{n})}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))](c)\|_{\tau}:c\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n},\|c\|_{\tau}\leqslant 1\}.

Consider
c\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
, then there exists a unique
b\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
such that
\alpha^{(n)}(b)=c
. We have

\displaystyle\|c\|_{\tau}^{2}=\tau(c^{*}c)=\tau\left(\alpha^{(n)}(b)^{*}\alpha^{(n)}(b)\right)=\tau\left(\alpha^{(n)}(b^{*}b)\right)=\tau_{n}(b^{*}b)=\|b\|^{2}_{\tau_{n}}.

Hence

L^{\tau}_{\beta}\circ\alpha^{(n)}(a)=\sup\{\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))](\alpha^{(n)}(b))\|_{\tau}:b\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n},\|b\|_{\tau_{n}}\leqslant 1\}

Let
b\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
.
Let
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
. Then a similar argument as the beginning of the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 3.5]
provides

E^{\tau}_{k}\circ\alpha^{(n)}=\alpha^{(n)}\circ E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}

and

E^{\tau}_{k-1}\circ\alpha^{(n)}=\alpha^{(n)}\circ E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}

by Expression (
2.3
).
Next, we have

\displaystyle[Q^{\tau}_{k},\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))](\alpha^{(n)}(b))

\displaystyle\quad=((E^{\tau}_{k}-E^{\tau}_{k-1})\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))-\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))(E^{\tau}_{k}-E^{\tau}_{k-1}))(\alpha^{(n)}(b))

Now

\displaystyle\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))(E^{\tau}_{k}-E^{\tau}_{k-1})(\alpha^{(n)}(b))

\displaystyle=\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))(\alpha^{(n)}(E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(b))-\alpha^{(n)}(E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}(b)))

\displaystyle=\alpha^{(n)}(a)(\alpha^{(n)}(E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(b))-\alpha^{(n)}(E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}(b)))

\displaystyle=\alpha^{(n)}(aE^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(b)-aE^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}(b))

and similarly

(E^{\tau}_{k}-E^{\tau}_{k-1})\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))(\alpha^{(n)}(b))=\alpha^{(n)}(E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(ab)-E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}(ab)).

Thus

\displaystyle[Q^{\tau}_{k},\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))](\alpha^{(n)}(b))

\displaystyle\quad=\alpha^{(n)}\left(E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(ab)-E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}(ab)-(aE^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(b)-aE^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}(b))\right).

However,

\displaystyle E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(ab)-E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}(ab)-(aE^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(b)-aE^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}(b))

\displaystyle\quad=E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)(b))-E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}(\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)(b))

\displaystyle\quad\quad-(\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)(E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(b))-\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)(E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1}(b)))

\displaystyle\quad=(E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}-E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1})(\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)(b))-\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)((E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}-E^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k-1})(b))

\displaystyle\quad=Q^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)(b))-\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)((Q^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k})(b))

\displaystyle\quad=(Q^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}(\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a))-\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)(Q^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k}))(b)

\displaystyle\quad=[Q^{\tau_{n}}_{n,k},\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)](b).

Hence

[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))](\alpha^{(n)}(b))=\alpha^{(n)}([D^{\tau_{n}}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)](b))

and so as above

\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))](\alpha^{(n)}(b))\|_{\tau}=\|[D^{\tau_{n}}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau_{n}}(a)](b)\|_{\tau_{n}}.

Therefore

L^{\tau}_{\beta}\circ\alpha^{(n)}(a)=\sup\{\|[D^{\tau}_{\beta},\pi_{\tau}(\alpha^{(n)}(a))](\alpha^{(n)}(b))\|_{\tau}:b\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n},\|b\|_{\tau_{n}}\leqslant 1\}=L^{\tau_{n}}_{\beta}(a)

of Expression (
2.5
)
as desired.
∎

Now that we have an expression for the L-seminorms on the terms of a given inductive sequence, we would like to show that these form a continuous field of L-seminorms with respect to weak* convergence of the faithful tracial state. However, since the norms defining our L-seminorms are operator norms this takes some care, which is why we need some tools from metric geometry. The following result might be known in metric geometry, but we cannot find a proof and so we provide one here. The following result also serves as a generalization of
[
1
, Lemma 3.9]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. Let
(C_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
be a sequence of compact subsets of
X
that converges in the Hausdorff distance with respect to
d
,
Haus_{d},
to a compact
C\subseteq X
. Let
C^{\prime}\subseteq X
be a compact set such that
C\cup(\cup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}C_{n})\subseteq C^{\prime}
. Let
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
be a sequence of real-valued continuous functions on
X
and let
f:X\rightarrow{\mathds{R}}
be continuous.

If
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges uniformly to
f
on
C^{\prime}
, then
(\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
converges to
\sup_{x\in C}f(x)
in the usual topology on
{\mathds{R}}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By uniform convergence, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
a,b\in C^{\prime}
and
n\in{\mathds{N}}
, we have

|f_{n}(a)-f_{n}(b)|<\varepsilon/2.

Let
N\in{\mathds{N}}
such that for every
n\geqslant N

Haus_{d}(C_{n},C)<\delta/3

and

|\sup_{x\in C}f_{n}(x)-\sup_{x\in C}f(x)|<\varepsilon/2

by
[
1
, Lemma 3.9]
.

Let
n\geqslant N
. By compact, there exists
x^{\prime}\in C
such that

\sup_{x\in C}f_{n}(x)=f_{n}(x^{\prime}).

Now consider
\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x).
Assume by way of contradiction that
|\sup_{x\in C}f_{n}(x)-\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x)|>\varepsilon/2.
Assume first that

\sup_{x\in C}f_{n}(x)-\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x)>\varepsilon/2

f_{n}(x^{\prime})-\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x)>\varepsilon/2

\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x)<f_{n}(x^{\prime})-\varepsilon/2.

Hence

f_{n}(x)<f_{n}(x^{\prime})-\varepsilon/2

for every
x\in C_{n}
. Now there exists
x\in C_{n}
such that
{\mathsf{d}}(x,x^{\prime})<\delta
by definition of the Hausdorff distance. Hence

|f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(x^{\prime})|<\varepsilon/2.

And so

f_{n}(x^{\prime})-\varepsilon/2<f_{n}(x)<f_{n}(x^{\prime})-\varepsilon/2,

contradiction.

On the other hand, if
\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x)-\sup_{x\in C}f_{n}(x)>\varepsilon/2
. Then

\varepsilon/2<\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(x^{\prime}).

Now, by compact, there exists
z\in C_{n}
such that
\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x)=f_{n}(z)
. Hence

f_{n}(x^{\prime})<f_{n}(z)-\varepsilon/2

and so

\sup_{x\in C}f_{n}(x)<f_{n}(z)-\varepsilon/2.

Thus

f_{n}(x)<f_{n}(z)-\varepsilon/2

for every
x\in C
. This leads to a similar contradiction. Hence,

|\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x)-\sup_{x\in C}f_{n}(x)|\leqslant\varepsilon/2.

Finally,

\displaystyle|\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x)-\sup_{x\in C}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\sup_{x\in C_{n}}f_{n}(x)-\sup_{x\in C}f_{n}(x)|+|\sup_{x\in C}f_{n}(x)-\sup_{x\in C}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon/2+|\sup_{x\in C}f_{n}(x)-\sup_{x\in C}f(x)|<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon.\qed

\displaystyle=\sigma^{n}(y^{*}y)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{n}\left(\frac{\mu^{\infty}_{1}}{\mu^{n}_{1}}a_{1}^{*}a_{1},\frac{\mu^{\infty}_{2}}{\mu^{n}_{2}}a_{2}^{*}a_{2},\ldots,\frac{\mu^{\infty}_{N}}{\mu^{n}_{N}}a_{N}^{*}a_{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\|a\|_{\sigma^{\infty}}^{2}\leqslant 1.

\displaystyle=\max\{\|a_{1}-y_{1}\|_{M_{n_{1}}({\mathds{C}})},\|a_{2}-y_{2}\|_{M_{n_{2}}({\mathds{C}})},\ldots,\|a_{N}-y_{N}\|_{M_{n_{N}}({\mathds{C}})}\}

\displaystyle\|a_{k}-y_{k}\|_{M_{n_{k}}({\mathds{C}})}

\displaystyle Haus_{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}}(D_{n},D_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\Bigg\{\max\left\{\left|1-\frac{\sqrt{\mu^{\infty}_{k}}}{\sqrt{\mu^{n}_{k}}}\right|\cdot\frac{\sqrt{m_{k}}}{\sqrt{\mu^{\infty}_{k}}}:k\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}\right\}\ ,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \max\left\{\left|1-\frac{\sqrt{\mu^{n}_{k}}}{\sqrt{\mu^{\infty}_{k}}}\right|\cdot\frac{\sqrt{m_{k}}}{\sqrt{\mu^{n}_{k}}}:k\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}\right\}\Bigg\}

converges to

By construction of

and since

On Sharpness of L ​ log ⁡ L L\log L Criterion for Weak Type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) boundedness of rough operators
Ankit Bhojak

Abstract
. In this note, we show that the L ​ log ⁡ L L\log L hypothesis is the strongest size condition on a homogeneous rough function on the sphere which ensures the weak type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) boundedness of the corresponding singular integral T Ω T_{\Omega} , provided T Ω T_{\Omega} is bounded in L 2 L^{2} .

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Omega(\theta)d\theta=0
, where
d\theta
is the surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Calderón and Zygmund
[
2
]
considered the rough singular integrals defined as,

T_{\Omega}f(x)=p.v.\int\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\Omega\Bigl(\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\Big)f(y)\;dy,

They showed that
\Omega\in L\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
i.e.
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|\Omega(\theta)|\log(e+|\Omega(\theta)|)<\infty
implies that
T_{\Omega}
is bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for
1<p<\infty
. The singular integral
T_{\Omega}
was shown to be of weak type
(1,1)
using
TT^{*}
arguments by Christ and Rubio de Francia
[
3
]
in dimension
d=2
(and independently by Hofmann
[
7
]
). The case of general dimensions was resolved by Seeger
[
11
]
by showing that
T_{\Omega}
is of weak type
(1,1)
for
\Omega\in L\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
assuming the
L^{2}
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
.

It is of interest to know other sufficient conditions on
\Omega
that ensures the weak type boundedness of the operator
T_{\Omega}
. In fact, during the inception of this problem, Calderón and Zygmund
[
2
]
showed that
\Omega\in L\log L
is “almost” a necessary size condition for
T_{\Omega}
to be
L^{2}
bounded. If we drop the condition that
\Omega\in L\log L
, then Calderón and Zygmund
[
2
]
pointed out that
T_{\Omega}
may even fail to be
L^{2}
bounded. Infact, the examples of
\Omega
constructed in
[
13
]
lies outside the space
L\log L
and the corresponding operator
T_{\Omega}
is unbounded on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Later on, it was shown in
[
5
,
9
]
that
\Omega\in H^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
in the sense of Coifman and Weiss
[
4
]
implies
T_{\Omega}:L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\;1<p<\infty
. It is still an open problem if
T_{\Omega}
is of weak type
(1,1)
for
\Omega\in H^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
. A partial result assuming additional conditions on
H^{1}
-atoms in dimension two was obtained by Stefanov
[
12
]
.

In
[
6
,
8
]
, it was shown that
T_{\Omega}
distinguishes
L^{p}
spaces by considering a suitable quantity based on the Fourier transform of
\Omega
. However, we would like to know if there exists an Orlicz space
X\supsetneq L\log L
which would ensure that the
L^{2}
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
implies the weak
(1,1)
boundeness of
T_{\Omega}
when
\Omega\in X
. We will show that no such
X
exists. To state our main result, we introduce the Orlicz spaces and discuss some of its basic properties.

Definition 1.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a Young’s function i.e. there exists an increasing and left continuous function
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\phi(0)=0
such that
\Phi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\phi(u)\;du
and
\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}\to\infty,\text{ as }t\to\infty
. We say
\Omega\in\Phi(L)({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
, if the quantity

(1.1)

\|\Omega\|_{\Phi(L)}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\Phi(|\Omega(\theta)|)\;d\theta

is finite.

The quantity in (
1.1
) fails to be a norm and
\Phi(L)({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
is not even a linear space. To remedy that, we define the set

L^{\Phi}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})=\{\Omega:{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}:\exists k>0\text{ such that }\lVert k^{-1}\Omega\rVert_{\Phi(L)}<\infty\}.

We define the Luxemburg norm as

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Omega\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Phi(L)}=\inf\{k>0:\|k^{-1}\Omega\|_{\Phi(L)}<\infty\}.

It is well known that the Orlicz space
L^{\Phi}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
forms a Banach space with this norm. We state the following fact that compares this norm with the quantity in (
1.1
), see Lemma
8.8
in
[
1
]
.

Lemma 1.2
.

If
\|\Omega\|_{\Phi(L)}<{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Omega\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Phi(L)}
, then
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Omega\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Phi(L)}\leq 1
.

2.
Main result

We state our main result for dimension two but the same also holds for higher dimensions using the methods in
[
13
,
6
]
. Our main result is the following,

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Phi
be a Young’s function such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Psi(t)=\frac{t\log(e+t)}{\Phi(t)}

\displaystyle\to\infty,\;\text{as}\;t\to\infty,

Then there exists an
\Omega\in\Phi(L)(\mathbb{S}^{1})
such that
T_{\Omega}
is
L^{p}
bounded iff
p=2
. In particular,
T_{\Omega}
does not map
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
to
L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

We note that using the geometric construction in
[
8
]
, one can obtain the above theorem for the space
L(\log L)^{1-\epsilon}({\mathbb{S}}^{1}),\;0<\epsilon\leq 1
. To obtain the general case, we will employ the construction in
[
6
]
with a suitable modification to ensure that the resulting
\Omega
lies in the required Orlicz space.

3.
Proof of
Theorem

2.1

To prove
Theorem

2.1
, we will rely on a transference principle
[
6
]
. The space of
L^{p}
multipliers
M^{p}({\mathbb{T}})
is defined as

M^{p}({\mathbb{T}})=\{\textbf{a}=\{a_{n}\}\in l^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}):\;T_{\textbf{a}}f(x)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}\widehat{f}(n)e^{2\pi inx}\text{ is bounded on }L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})\},

with
\lVert\textbf{a}\rVert_{M^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}=\lVert T_{\textbf{a}}\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}
.

The idea is to construct a sequence of
\{\Omega_{n}\}
such that the
L^{p}
norm of
T_{\Omega_{n}}
is large for
p\neq 2
. We achieve this by employing the fact that
\{e^{2\pi ikx},\;k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
is not an unconditional basis for
L^{p}([0,1]),\;p\neq 2
. We have,

Lemma 3.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

For
p\neq 2
and fixed
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists finite sequences
\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
and
\{\epsilon_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
(depending on
n
) with
\epsilon_{k}\in\{-1,1\}
such that

\Big\lVert\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\epsilon_{k}a_{k}e^{2\pi ikx}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{[}0,1])}\geq c_{p}n^{\lvert\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\rvert}\Big\lVert\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}e^{2\pi ikx}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{[}0,1])},

where
c_{p}>0
depends only on
p
. Consequently,
\lVert\{\dots,0,\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\dots,\epsilon_{n},0,\dots\}\rVert_{M^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\gtrsim n^{\lvert\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\rvert}
. Moreever, we can choose
\epsilon_{k}
such that

\lVert\{\dots,0,\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\dots,\epsilon_{n},0,\dots\}\rVert_{M^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}=\sup\{\lVert\{\dots,0,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots,\delta_{n},0,\dots\}\rVert_{M^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}:\;|\delta_{k}|\leq 1\}.

Proof.

The inquality follows from the unconditionality of basis
\{e^{2\pi ikx},\;k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
for
p\neq 2
with constant
K(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. We justify the choice of constant
c_{p}n^{\lvert\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\rvert}
. Indeed, we invoke Theorem 1 from
[
10
]
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists
\{\epsilon_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
with
\epsilon_{k}=\pm 1
such that
\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}\epsilon_{k}e^{2\pi ikx}\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}\leq 5n^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By using the well known fact that
L^{p}
norm of the Dirichlet kernel satisfies the following estimate:

\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}\ e^{2\pi ikx}\rVert_{L^{p}([0,1])}\sim n^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

we have, for
p>2
and
a_{k}=\epsilon_{k}
,

\Big\lVert\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\epsilon_{k}^{2}e^{2\pi ikx}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{[}0,1])}\geq c_{p}n^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\Big\lVert\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\epsilon_{k}e^{2\pi ikx}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{[}0,1])}.

The analogous inequality for
p<2
follows from duality.
∎

If we have a seqeunce of multiplier
\{\gamma_{n}\}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that
\gamma_{n}|_{{\mathbb{Z}}}=\{\dots,0,\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\dots,\epsilon_{n},0,\dots\}
where
\epsilon_{k}
are from the above lemma, then by classical transference
\lVert T_{\gamma_{n}}\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\gtrsim n^{\lvert\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\rvert}
. Our aim is to produce
\gamma_{n}
such that
T_{\gamma_{n}}=T_{\Omega_{n}}
. Towards this, the following observation is important.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\gamma\in M^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
be continuous on an arithmetic progression
\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
(i.e. there exists vector
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that
x_{k}-x_{k-1}=v
). Then there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\lVert\gamma\rVert_{M^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\geq C_{p}\lVert\{\dots,0,\gamma(x_{1}),\gamma(x_{2}),\dots,\gamma(x_{n}),0,\dots\}\rVert_{M^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}.

The proof of above lemma can be found in
[
6
]
. We now begin the proof of
Theorem

2.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

2.1
.

We fix a large
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
n=\left[\Psi\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)\right]
. By hypothesis, we have
n\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
.

Let
s_{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
be a large number such that there exists natural numbers
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{2n}
satisfying

•

The numbers
t_{k}
are in arithmetic progression, i.e.
t_{k+1}-t_{k}=t_{k}-t_{k-1}
.

•

Let
x_{k}=(t_{k},s_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Then
x_{k},\;k=1,\dots,2n
lies in the second quadrant between the lines
y
-axis and
y=-x
.

•

\lvert\frac{x_{k+1}}{|x_{k+1}|}-\frac{x_{k}}{|x_{k}|}\rvert\sim\frac{1}{n}
.

We denote
\tilde{x}_{k}
to be the point on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
obtained by rotating the point
\frac{x_{k}}{|x_{k}|}
by
\frac{\pi}{2}
radians clockwise. We consider
I_{k},\;k=1,\dots,2n
to be the arc on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with centre
\tilde{x}_{k}
and arc length
N^{-1}
and denote
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}(I_{k})
to be the arc obtained by rotating
I_{k}
by
\alpha
radians counterclockwise.

We define
w_{k}
as

w_{k}(\theta)=c_{I_{k}}(-\chi_{{}_{I_{k}}}(\theta)+\chi_{{}_{\mathfrak{R}_{\frac{\pi}{2}}(I_{k})}}(\theta)-\chi_{{}_{\mathfrak{R}_{\pi}(I_{k})}}(\theta)+\chi_{{}_{\mathfrak{R}_{\frac{3\pi}{2}}(I_{k})}}(\theta)),

where we choose
c_{I_{k}}
as follows. We recall that the Fourier transform of the kernel in
T_{\Omega}
for any even
\Omega
with mean value zero is given by

\widehat{K}_{\Omega}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\Omega(\theta)\log\frac{1}{|\langle\xi,\theta\rangle|}\;d\theta.

We define the larger quantity
m(\Omega)
which will be useful for our purpose.

m(\Omega)(\xi):=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|\Omega(\theta)|\log\frac{1}{|\langle\xi,\theta\rangle|}\;d\theta.

Clearly,
|\widehat{K}_{\Omega}(\xi)|\leq m(\Omega)(\xi)
.

We choose
c_{I_{k}}
such that
m(w_{k})(\frac{x_{k}}{|x_{k}|})=1
. It is not difficult to see that
c_{I_{k}}
and
\widehat{K}_{w_{k}}(\frac{x_{k}}{|x_{k}|})
are independent of
k
and satisfies,

\displaystyle c_{I_{k}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{N}{\log N}.

\displaystyle 1\lesssim\sup_{x}|\widehat{K}_{w_{k}}(x)|=\Bigl|\widehat{K}_{w_{k}}

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{x_{k}}{|x_{k}|}\Bigr)\Bigr|\leq\sup_{x}m(w_{k})(x)=1.

We now set

\Omega_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{2n}(-1)^{k}\epsilon_{\left[\frac{k+1}{2}\right]}w_{k},

where
[\;]
denotes the integer part and
\epsilon_{[.]}
is as in
Lemma

3.1
.

By the disjointness of the arcs
I_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\Omega_{n}\rVert_{\Phi(L)(\mathbb{S}^{1})}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{k=1}^{2n}\int_{I_{k}}\Phi(c_{I_{k}})

\displaystyle\sim nN^{-1}\Phi\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)

\displaystyle\sim 1.

Moreover, since
\lVert\Omega_{n}\rVert_{\Phi(L)(\mathbb{S}^{1})}>1
, by
Lemma

1.2
, we have
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Omega_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Phi(L)(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim 1
.

To calculate the
L^{2}
norm of
T_{\Omega_{n}}
, we will need the following estimate. Let
J_{k}
be the arc of length
\frac{1}{100n}
and whose bisector passes though the point
\frac{x_{k}}{|x_{k}|}
. Then for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
lying in second quadrant between the lines
y
-axis and
y=-x
with
x\notin\bigcup_{i=0}^{3}\mathfrak{R}_{\frac{i\pi}{2}}(J_{k})
, we have

(3.1)

m(w_{k})(x)\lesssim\frac{\log n}{\log N}.

The equation (
3.1
) follows from the fact that for
\gamma\in(2I_{k})^{c}\cap(0,\frac{\pi}{4})
, we have

|m(w_{I_{k}})(e^{i\gamma})|\lesssim\frac{|\log|\gamma-\tilde{x}_{k}||}{|\log|I_{k}||}.

Indeed, for
\theta\in I_{k}
, we have
|\gamma-\tilde{x}_{k}|<|\theta-\tilde{x}_{k}|+|\theta-\gamma|<\frac{|I_{k}|}{2}+|\theta-\gamma|<|\gamma-\tilde{x}_{k}|/2+|\theta-\gamma|
. Thus
\frac{|\tilde{x}_{k}-\gamma|}{2}<|\theta-\gamma|
and it follows that

\displaystyle|m(w_{I_{k}})(e^{i\gamma})|

\displaystyle\lesssim-c_{I_{k}}\int_{I_{k}}\log|\sin(\theta-\gamma)|\;d\theta

\displaystyle\leq c_{I_{k}}|I_{k}||\log\left|\sin\left(\frac{|\gamma-\tilde{x}_{k}|}{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\log|\gamma-\tilde{x}_{k}||}{|\log|I_{k}||}.

Since
\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
, we have
\frac{\Psi(t)}{\log t}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. Therefore,

(3.2)

\lVert m(\Omega_{n})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim 1+\frac{n\log n}{\log N}\lesssim\frac{\Psi\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)}{\log N}\log\Psi\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)\lesssim\log n.

Now we turn to the estimate of
L^{p}
bounds of
T_{\Omega_{n}}
, We claim

(3.3)

\lVert T_{\Omega_{n}}\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\gtrsim n^{\lvert\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\rvert}.

To acheive the above claim, we need to prove that for
1\leq k\leq n
and
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
lying in second quadrant between the lines
y
-axis and
y=-x
with
x\notin\bigl(\bigcup_{i=0}^{3}\mathfrak{R}_{\frac{i\pi}{2}}(J_{2k})\bigr)\cup\bigl(\bigcup_{i=0}^{3}\mathfrak{R}_{\frac{i\pi}{2}}(J_{2k-1})\bigr)
, we have

(3.4)

|\widehat{K}_{w_{2k}}(x)-\widehat{K}_{w_{2k-1}}(x)|\lesssim\left(n\log N\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-\frac{x_{2k}}{|x_{2k}|}\right|\right)^{-1}.

Indeed, let
e^{i\theta_{2k}}=\frac{x_{2k}}{|x_{2k}|},\;e^{i\gamma}=\frac{x}{|x|}
and
A_{2k}
be the interval in

I_{2k}-\frac{x_{2k}}{|x_{2k}|}=\{e^{i\theta}:\;\theta\in A_{2k}\}
. By using mean value theorem twice and the fact that

is small, we have

\displaystyle|\widehat{K}_{w_{2k}}(x)-\widehat{K}_{w_{2k-1}}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim c_{I_{2k}}\int_{A_{2k}}\left(\log\frac{1}{|\tan(\theta+\theta_{2k}-\gamma)|}-\log\frac{1}{|\tan(\theta+\theta_{2k-1}-\gamma)|}\right)\;d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim c_{I_{2k}}\int_{A_{2k}}\frac{|\tan(\theta+\theta_{2k}-\gamma)-\tan(\theta+\theta_{2k-1}-\gamma)|}{|\tan(\theta+\theta_{2k}-\gamma)|}\;d\theta

where we have used
|\gamma-\theta_{2k}|\leq 2|\theta+\theta_{2k}-\gamma|
and
\tan\theta\sim\theta
away from odd multiples of
\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Now, we return to the proof of the claim (

). For

\widehat{K}_{\Omega_{n}}(x_{2k})=(-1)^{2k}\widehat{K}_{w_{2k}}(x_{2k})\epsilon_{k}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i\neq 2k\leq 2n}(-1)^{i}\epsilon_{\left[\frac{i+1}{2}\right]}\widehat{K}_{w_{i}}(x_{2k})=D\epsilon_{k}+\delta_{k},

Lemma

3.1

\frac{1}{2}\lVert\{\dots,0,\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\dots,\epsilon_{n},0,\dots\}\rVert_{M^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\geq\Big\lVert\Bigl\{\dots,0,\frac{\delta_{1}}{D},\frac{\delta_{2}}{D},\dots,\frac{\delta_{n}}{D},0,\dots\Bigr\}\Big\rVert_{M^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Since
\widehat{K}_{\Omega_{n}}(\theta)
is a circular convolution of a

\displaystyle\lVert T_{\Omega_{n}}\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lVert\widehat{K}_{\Omega_{n}}\rVert_{M^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\lVert\{\dots,0,\widehat{K}_{\Omega_{n}}(x_{2}),\widehat{K}_{\Omega_{n}}(x_{4}),\dots,\widehat{K}_{\Omega_{n}}(x_{2n}),0,\dots\}\rVert_{M^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}

\mathfrak{B}:=\{\Omega:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}\text{ is even}:\int\Omega=0\text{ and }\|\Omega\|_{\mathfrak{B^{\prime}}}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Omega\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Phi(L)(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\|m(\Omega)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}<\infty\}.

The space
\mathfrak{B}

Fix

. For
\mathfrak{F}=\{f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):\|f\|_{p}=1\}
, we define a collection of operators

. Suppose we have

\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathfrak{F^{\prime}}}\|T_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<\infty,\;\forall\Omega\in\mathfrak{B}.

Then by uniform boundedness principle, there exists

\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathfrak{F}}\|\varTheta_{f}(\Omega)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<M\|\Omega\|_{\mathfrak{B}}.

) and (

\displaystyle n^{\lvert\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\rvert}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert T_{\Omega_{n}}\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Omega_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Phi(L)(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\|m(\Omega_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\log n.

which is a contradiction for large

and

and that concludes the proof of

.

The Cauchy problem for the (2+1) integrable nonlinear Schrödinger equation
L. P. Nizhnik

Abstract
We study the Cauchy problem for the (2+1) integrable nonlinear Schrödinger equation by the inverse scattering transform (IST) method. This Cauchy problem with given initial data and boundary data at infinity is reduced by IST to the Cauchy problem for the linear Schrödinger equation, in which the potential is expressed in terms of boundary data. The results on direct and inverse scattering problems for a two-dimensional Dirac system with special potentials are used and refined. The Cauchy problem admits an explicit solution if the IST of the solution is an integral operator of rank 1. We give one such solution.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=A(t)v
, where the operator

due to the fact that the function

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

The Cauchy problem for a linear equation

\Bigl[i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+\varepsilon_{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}+p(y,t)+q(x,t)\Bigr]u(x,y;t)=0,

(3.6)

where

\varepsilon_{2}=\pm 1,

satisfying the initial conditions

. The solution of the Cauchy problem for the equation (

U(x,y;t)=U_{1}(t)U_{2}(t)U_{0}(x,y),

(3.7)

where the semigroup unitary operators

act on the variable

and the unitary operators

act on the variable

from the equations

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\Bigl[i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+q(x,t)\Bigr]u(x,y;t)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\Bigl[i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\varepsilon_{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}+p(y,t)\Bigr]u(x,y;t)=0.\end{array}

If the equality (

and

as kernels of the integral operators

and

, then this equality admits representation

A(t)=U_{1}(t)A_{0}JU_{2}^{*}(t)J,

(3.8)

, that is, this norm does not depend on

Proof.

The formula (

. The formula (

) follows from the representation of action of the integral operator

with kernel

on an arbitrary function

in the form

where

. Therefore, the operator

acting on the second variable of the function

can be transferred in the scalar product to

. The kernel

generates an integral operator

represented as:

\hat{U}_{2}\varphi=<\hat{u}_{2}(x,\cdot),J\varphi>_{L_{2}}=<u_{0}(x,\cdot),U_{2}^{*}J\varphi>_{L_{2}}=\int\,u_{0}(x,y)JU_{2}^{*}(t)J\varphi(y)\,dy.

In other words,
\hat{U}_{2}=A_{0}JU_{2}^{*}(t)J.
Acting on the last equality by the operator

, we get the equality (

). Since the operators

,

∎

4
Results on the inverse scattering problem for the Dirac system in the case when the integral operator of scattering data has rank 1

The following simple facts from the theory of integral operators are required in order to obtain explicitly all elements of the scattering operator and the potential from the scattering data,

4.1
Constructive facts from the theory of Hilbert–Schmidt integral equations

An integral operator
K
with the Hilbert–Schmidt kernel
k(x,y)\in L^{2}(R^{2})
has the trace
\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\,K=\int\,k(x,x)\,dx.
An operator
K
has rank 1 if its kernel
k(x,y)
can be represented as a product of two functions, each of them depends on only one argument
k(x,y)=f(x)\cdot g(y)
, where
f,g\in L^{2}(R^{1})
. If an integral operator
K_{+}
can be represented as

K_{+}\psi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\,k(x,y)\psi(y)\,dy,

then this is the Volterra operator of positive polarity. If an integral operator
K_{-}
can be represented as

K_{-}\psi(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\,k(x,y)\psi(y)\,dy,

then this is the Volterra operator of negative polarity. If the kernel of the Volterra operator can be represented as
k(x,y)=f(x)\cdot g(y)
, then we say that the Volterra operator has the kernel of rank 1. Note that the product of two integral operators of rank 1 is an integral operator of rank 1. The product of an integral operator of rank 1 and a Volterra operator with a kernel of rank 1 is an integral operator of rank 1. The product of two Volterra operators with kernels of rank 1 will no longer be an operator of rank 1, and will not even have a kernel of rank 1. We need such simple results.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
K
be an integral operator with the Hilbert–Schmidt kernel of rank 1. Then
(I-K)^{-1}
exists if and only if the trace
\mathrm{tr}\,K\neq 1
. In this case,

(I-K)^{-1}=I+\frac{1}{1-\mathrm{tr}\,K}K.

(4.1)

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
K
be a Hilbert–Schmidt integral operator of rank 1 with kernel
k(x,y)=f(x)\cdot g(y)
, and norm
||K||=||f||_{L_{2}}\cdot||g||_{L_{2}}<1.
Then the operator
I-K
admits two-sided factorization into the Volterra integral operators:

I-K=(I+A_{+})^{-1}(I+A_{-})^{-1}=(I+B_{-})^{-1}(I+B_{+})^{-1},

(4.2)

where the integral operators
A_{\pm}
,
B_{\pm}
have kernels

A_{+}(x,y)=\frac{f(x)}{1-\mathrm{tr}\,KQ_{x}}g(y)\theta(x-y),\quad A_{-}(x,y)=f(x)\frac{g(y)}{1-\mathrm{tr}\,KQ_{y}}\theta(y-x),

B_{+}(x,y)=f(x)\frac{g(y)}{1-\mathrm{tr}\,KP_{y}}\theta(x-y),\quad B_{-}(x,y)=\frac{f(x)}{1-\mathrm{tr}\,KP_{x}}g(y)\theta(y-x),

(4.3)

where

\mathrm{tr}\,KQ_{x}=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\,f(s)g(s)\,ds,

\mathrm{tr}\,KP_{x}=\int_{x}^{\infty}\,f(s)g(s)\,ds.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
K_{+}
be the Hilbert–Schmidt Volterra integral operator of positive polarity with a kernel of rank 1 and
k(x,y)=f(x)\cdot g(y)
. Then there is an operator

(I-K_{+})^{-1}=I+\hat{K}_{+},

(4.4)

where
\hat{K}_{+}
is the Volterra Hilbert–Schmidt integral operator of positive polarity with a kernel of rank 1, whose kernel
\hat{K}_{+}(x,y)
has the form

\hat{K}_{+}(x,y)=\hat{f}(x)\hat{g}(y)\theta(x-y),

(4.5)

where

\hat{f}(x)=f(x)e^{\mathrm{tr}\,K_{+}Q_{x}},\quad\hat{g}(y)=g(y)e^{-\mathrm{tr}\,K_{+}Q_{y}},

(4.6)

where
Q_{\lambda}
- projector

Q_{\lambda}\psi(x)=\theta(\lambda-x)\psi(x),\quad\mathrm{tr}\,(K_{+}Q_{x})=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\,f(s)g(s)\,ds.

Lemma
4.2
contains various Volterra operators
(I+A_{+})^{-1}
and
(I+B_{+})^{-1}
. Let us take a look at their explicit forms. Lemma
4.2
implies

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
K_{+}
be a Hilbert–Schmidt Volterra integral operator with a kernel of rank 1 and a kernel of the form

K_{+}(x,y)=\frac{f(x)g(y)\theta(x-y)}{1-T(x)},\quad T(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\,f(s)g(s)\,ds.

(4.7)

Then the operator

(I+K_{+})^{-1}=I-\hat{K}_{+},\quad\hat{K}_{+}(x,y)=\frac{f(x)g(y)\theta(x-y)}{1-T(y)}.

(4.8)

In the simplest case, when we have
g(y)=\overline{f}(y)
in lemma
4.4
a nonlinear operator arises that maps the function
f(x)
into the function

\displaystyle F(x)=\frac{f(x)}{1-\int_{-\infty}^{x}\,|f(s)|^{2}\,ds}
.
Such an operator has important properties. This type of nonlinear operators can play an important independent role.

Lemma 4.5
.

In the space
L_{2}(R^{1})
, the nonlinear operator
\mathcal{A}_{k}
depending on the positive parameter
k<1
and acting according to the formula

\mathcal{A}_{k}f(x)=\frac{\sqrt{1-k^{2}}f(x)}{1-\frac{k^{2}}{||f||_{L_{2}}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\,|f(s)|^{2}\,ds}.

(4.9)

The operator
\mathcal{A}_{k}
maps each function
f\in L_{2}
,
||f||\neq 0
into the function
F(x)=\mathcal{A}_{k}f(x),

L_{2}
– norm is equal to
||f||_{L_{2}}:

||F||_{L_{2}}=||f||_{L_{2}}.
The inverse operator
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-1}
has the form

\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-1}F(x)=\frac{\sqrt{1-k^{2}}F(x)}{1-\frac{k^{2}}{||F||_{L_{2}}^{2}}\int_{x}^{+\infty}\,|F(s)|^{2}\,ds}.

(4.10)

In the case of
||f||=0
and
||F||=0
, we set
\mathcal{A}_{k}0=0
,
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-1}0=0
.

As a special case of lemma
4.5
is the following statement.

Lemma 4.6
.

A nonlinear operator
\mathcal{A}
defined on functions
f\in L_{2}(R^{1})
with condition
||f||<1
by equality

\mathcal{A}f(x)=\frac{f(x)\sqrt{1-||f||^{2}}}{1-\int_{-\infty}^{x}\,|f(s)|^{2}\,ds}

(4.11)

preserves the norm
||\mathcal{A}f||=||f||.
The inverse operator
\mathcal{A}^{-1}
has the form

\mathcal{A}^{-1}g(y)=\frac{g(y)\sqrt{1-||g||^{2}}}{1-\int_{x}^{\infty}\,|g(s)|^{2}\,ds}.

(4.12)

The proof of all lemmas is reduced to a simple verification of constructive assertions.

5
Explicit Solutions with Scattering Data of Rank 1

If
u(x,y;t)
is a solution of the equation (
1.1
) with pseudopotentials

v_{1}
,
v_{2}
representable as (
1.5
) with boundary data

p_{-}(y,t)
,
q_{+}(x,t)
, which are real, continuous, and bounded functions of their arguments, then the IST of such a solution is an integral operator
F(t)
with the Hilbert–Schmidt kernel
f(x,y;t)
, and this kernel satisfies the linear differential equation (
1.8
). Here norm
||F(t)||=||F(0)||<1
. If the integral operator
F(t)
has rank 1, then
f(x,y;t)=f(x,t)\cdot g(y,t).
Moreover,
||F(t)||=||f||_{L_{2}}\cdot||g||_{L_{2}}.
The solution
u(x,y;t)
, as the potential of the two-dimensional Dirac system, is uniquely and explicitly expressed in terms of the scattering data
F(t)
in the form:

u(x,y;t)=\frac{\overline{f}(x,t)\cdot\overline{g}(y,t)}{1-F(x)G(y)},

(5.1)

where

F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\,|f(s,t)|^{2}\,ds,\quad G(y)=\int_{y}^{+\infty}\,|g(s,t)|^{2}\,ds.

(5.2)

The opposite is also true:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
f(x,t)
and
g(y,t)
be solutions of linear equations

\begin{array}[]{c}\Bigl(i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\Bigr)\overline{f}(x,t)+q_{+}(x,t)\overline{f}(x,t)=0,\\
\Bigl(i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}\Bigr)\overline{g}(y,t)+p_{-}(y,t)\overline{g}(y,t)=0,\end{array}

(5.3)

where
f(x,0)
,
g(y,0)
are arbitrary functions from
L_{2}
such that

||f(x,0)||_{L_{2}}\cdot||g(y,0)||_{L_{2}}<1,
and functions
p_{-}(y,t)
,
q_{+}(x,t)
are real, continuous, bounded functions of their arguments. Then the formula (
5.1
) is a solution of the equation (
1.1
) for
t>0
the representations (
1.5
) are satisfied, and for
t=0
the solution
u(x,y;t)=u(x,y;0)
.

Proof.

The correctness of this statement follows from the direct substitution of
u
of the form (
5.1
) into the equation (
1.1
). We only note that, in view of the accepted conditions

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F(x)=\overline{f}(x,t)\cdot f_{x}^{\prime}(x,t)-\overline{f}_{x}^{\prime}(x,t)\cdot f(x,t),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(y)=\overline{g}_{y}^{\prime}(y,t)\cdot g(y,t)-\overline{g}(y,t)\cdot g_{y}^{\prime}(y,t),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle v_{1}+v_{2}=p_{-}(y,t)+q_{+}(x,t)+2\Bigl[\frac{F(|g|^{2})_{y}^{\prime}-G(|g|^{2})_{x}^{\prime}}{1-F\cdot G}-\frac{G^{2}|f|^{4}+F^{2}|g|^{4}}{(1-F\cdot G)^{2}}\Bigr].\end{array}

The other calculations are very simple.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

Since the pseudopotentials
v_{1}
and

) has the integral of motion

independent of

(5.4)

Let us give an example of solving linear equations (

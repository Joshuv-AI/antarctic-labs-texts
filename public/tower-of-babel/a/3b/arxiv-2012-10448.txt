KdV solves BKP
Alexander Alexandrov

Abstract
. In this note, we prove that any tau-function of the KdV hierarchy also solves the BKP hierarchy after a simple rescaling of times.



Keywords: tau-functions, BKP hierarchy, KdV hierarchy, Hirota bilinear identity

In this paper we answer the question about the relation between the KdV and BKP hierarchies, raised in
[
Ale20
]
. Namely, in
[
Ale20
]
it was observed that several families of the KdV tau-functions important in enumerative geometry and theoretical physics also solve the BKP hierarchy after a simple rescaling of times. Here we prove that
any
tau-function of KdV solves the BKP hierarchy.

In terms of tau-function
\tau_{KP}({\bf t})
the KP hierarchy, introduced in
[
SS83
]
, is described by the Hirota bilinear identity

(1)

\oint_{\infty}e^{\xi({\bf t-t^{\prime}},z)}\tau_{KP}({\bf t}-[z^{-1}])\tau_{KP}({\bf t^{\prime}}+[z^{-1}])dz=0.

This bilinear identity encodes all nonlinear equations of the KP hierarchy.
Here we use the standard short-hand notations

(2)

{\bf t}\pm[z^{-1}]:=\bigl\{t_{1}\pm z^{-1},t_{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}z^{-2},t_{3}\pm\frac{1}{3}z^{-3},\ldots\bigr\}

and

(3)

\xi({\bf t},z)=\sum_{k>0}t_{k}z^{k}.

If a tau-function of the KP hierarchy does not depend on even time variables,

(4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2k}}\tau_{KdV}({\bf t})=0\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,k>0,

than it is a tau-function of the KdV hierarchy. Since we still have arbitrary
t_{2k}
and
t_{2k}^{\prime}
in the first factor of the integrand in (
1
),
for the KdV hierarchy the Hirota bilinear identity is given by

(5)

\oint_{\infty}e^{\xi^{o}({\bf t-t^{\prime}},z)}\,f(z)\,\tau_{KdV}({\bf t}-[z^{-1}])\tau_{KdV}({\bf t^{\prime}}+[z^{-1}])dz=0

for arbitrary series
f(z)\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![z^{2}]\!]
. Here we introduce

(6)

\xi^{o}({\bf t},z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}t_{k}z^{k}.

In terms of the
Hirota derivatives

(7)

P(D_{1},D_{2},\dots)f\cdot g=\left.P\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}},\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}},\dots\right)f({\bf t+y})g({\bf t-y})\right|_{{\bf y}={\bf 0}},

the first few equations of the KdV hierarchy can be represented as

(8)

\begin{split}\left(D_{1}^{4}-4D_{1}D_{3}\right)\tau_{KdV}\cdot\tau_{KdV}=0,\\
\left(D_{1}^{6}+4D_{1}^{3}D_{3}-32D_{3}^{2}\right)\tau_{KdV}\cdot\tau_{KdV}=0,\\
\left(D_{1}^{6}-20D_{1}^{3}D_{3}-80D_{3}^{2}+144D_{1}D_{5}\right)\tau_{KdV}\cdot\tau_{KdV}=0.\\
\end{split}

The BKP hierarchy was introduced by Date, Jimbo, Kashiwara and Miwa in
[
DJKM82*
]
.
It can be represented in terms of tau-function
\tau_{BKP}({\bf t})
by the Hirota bilinear identity similar to (
1
):

(9)

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\infty}e^{\xi^{o}({\bf t}-{\bf t}^{\prime},z)}\tau_{BKP}({\bf t}-2[z^{-1}])\tau_{BKP}({\bf t^{\prime}}+2[z^{-1}])\frac{dz}{z}=\tau_{BKP}({\bf t})\tau_{BKP}({\bf t^{\prime}}).

The first equation of the BKP hierarchy in terms of the Hirota derivatives is given by

(10)

\left(D_{1}^{6}-5D_{1}^{3}D_{3}-5D_{3}^{2}+9D_{1}D_{5}\right)\tau_{BKP}\cdot\tau_{BKP}=0.

Note, that if we map
D_{k}\mapsto D_{k}/2
then this equation coincides with the last equation in (
8
). Let us show that this is more than just a coincidence, and all equations of the BKP hierarchy follow from the KdV hierarchy. The main result of this note is

Theorem 1
.

For any KdV tau-function

(11)

\tau_{BKP}({\bf t})=\tau_{KdV}({\bf t}/2)

is a tau-function of the BKP hierarchy.

Proof.

Let us consider

(12)

f(z)=\frac{e^{\xi^{o}({\bf t}-{\bf t}^{\prime},z)}-e^{-\xi^{o}({\bf t}-{\bf t}^{\prime},z)}}{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![z^{2}]\!],

then the Hirota bilinear identity (
5
) for the KdV hierarchy leads to

(13)

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\infty}e^{2\xi^{0}({\bf t-t^{\prime}},z)}\tau_{KdV}({\bf t}-[z^{-1}])\tau_{KdV}({\bf t^{\prime}}+[z^{-1}])\frac{dz}{z}=\tau_{KdV}({\bf t})\tau_{KdV}({\bf t^{\prime}}).

Now we substitute the times
{\bf t}\mapsto{\bf t}/2
,
{\bf t}^{\prime}\mapsto{\bf t}^{\prime}/2
:

(14)

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\infty}e^{\xi^{o}({\bf t}-{\bf t}^{\prime},z)}\tau_{KdV}({\bf t}/2-[z^{-1}])\tau_{KdV}({\bf t^{\prime}}/2+[z^{-1}])\frac{dz}{z}=\tau_{KdV}({\bf t}/2)\tau_{KdV}({\bf t^{\prime}}/2).

This equation coincides with the Hirota bilinear identity for the BKP hierarchy (
9
) if we identify tau-functions by (
11
). This completes the proof.
∎

It is easy to see that the converse statement is false, that is, the KdV hierarchy is a certain reduction of the BKP hierarchy. This reduction, as well as similar description of the higher Gelfand–Dickey hierarchies, will be discussed elsewhere.

This theorem partially explains the results of
[
Ale20
,
MM20
]
. Indeed, in
[
MM20
]
it was noted that the Kontsevich-Witten tau-function - one of the most important solutions of the KdV hierarchy - has a simple expansion in terms of the Schur Q-functions. This result was generalized in

[
Ale20
]
to a family of KdV tau-functions related to the Brézin–Gross–Witten model. On the basis of these expansions the author has conjectured (for the Kontsevich-Witten tau-function) and proved (for the Brézin–Gross–Witten tau-function) that these KdV tau-functions also solve the BKP hierarchy. From Theorem
1
it follows, that this part of the conjectures in
[
Ale20
]
trivially follows from a general relation between the KdV and BKP hierarchies. Moreover, this theorem makes the Schur Q-functions a natural basis for expansion of the KdV tau-functions.

There are several different ways to relate KdV and BKP hierarchies. In particular, in
[
DJKM82
]
the authors describe an identification of KdV hierarchy with the 4-reduction of BKP, see Remark on page 1098. Another reduction from BKP to KdV (so-called 1-constrained BKP) was described in
[
Orl91
,
Orl93
,
Che92
]
. To the best of our understanding, these reductions do not coincide with the one considered in this paper.

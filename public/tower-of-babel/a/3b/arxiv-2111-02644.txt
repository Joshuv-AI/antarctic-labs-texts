A Concentration Bound for LSPE( λ \lambda )
Siddharth Chandak, Vivek S Borkar, Harsh Dolhare

Abstract
The popular LSPE( λ \lambda ) algorithm for policy evaluation is revisited to derive a concentration bound that gives high probability performance guarantees from some time on.

Introduction

The LSPE(
\lambda

\lambda\geq 0
. It typically gives faster convergence in terms of number of iterates at the expense of a somewhat higher per iterate computational budget. Its convergence properties have been extensively analyzed in

n_{0}

n

2
Background and Main Result

The LSPE(
\lambda
) algorithm is for policy evaluation, i.e., we fix the policy a priori and do not optimize over policies. This gives us a time-homogeneous uncontrolled Markov chain
\{X_{n}\}
with transition probabilities
p(\cdot|\cdot)
. Let
S
be the state space of this chain and
s=|S|
. We assume that the chain is irreducible with a unique stationary distribution denoted by
\pi=[\pi(1),\pi(2),\dots,\pi(s)]
. We denote by
D
the
s\times s
diagonal matrix whose
i^{th}
diagonal entry is
\pi(i)
. When a transition from state
i
takes place, we incur a cost
k(i)
. We want to evaluate the long term discounted cost for each state given by the ‘value function’

V(i)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{m}k(X_{m})\Big|X_{0}=i\right],i\in S.

Here
\alpha
is a discount factor with
0<\alpha<1
. Using ‘one step analysis’,
V(\cdot)
is seen to satisfy the linear Bellman equation

V(i)=k(i)+\alpha\sum_{j\in S}p(j|i)V(j),i\in S.

This suffers from the ‘curse of dimensionality’ when
|S|>>1
. To alleviate it, we may approximate
V
as a linear combination of
M
prescribed basis function
\phi_{i}:S\to\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq i\leq M
. Thus,
V(i)\approx\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}\phi_{j}(i)
, written in vector form as
V=\Phi r
where
r=[r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{M}]^{T}
and
\Phi
is a
s\times M
matrix whose
i^{th}
column is
\phi_{i}
. The approximate Bellman equation then is

\Phi r\approx k+\alpha P\Phi r,

where
P\coloneqq[[p(j|i)]]
is the transition matrix.

The LSPE(
\lambda
), as described in
[
1
]
, iteratively updates the weight vector
r_{n}
to minimize the squared norm error between the right and left hand sides of the approximate Bellman equation. Let
\varphi(i)
denote the
i
th row of
\Phi
, transposed to make it a column vector. The update can be written as

r_{n+1}=r_{n}+a(n)B_{n}^{-1}(A_{n}r_{n}+b_{n}),

(2.1)

where, for a small
\varrho>0
and
I:=
the identity matrix,

B_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\varrho I+\sum_{m=0}^{n}\varphi(X_{m})\varphi(X_{m})^{T}\right),

A_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{m=0}^{n}z_{m}(\alpha\varphi(X_{m+1})-\varphi(X_{m}))^{T},

(2.2)

b_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{m=0}^{n}z_{m}k(X_{m}),

\mbox{where}\ \ z_{m}=\sum_{t=0}^{m}(\alpha\lambda)^{m-t}\varphi(X_{t}).

(2.3)

Observe that our definitions of
A_{n},B_{n},b_{n}
are normalized versions of those in
[
1
]
.
\{a(n)\}
denote the stepsizes which are assumed to satisfy the usual Robbins-Monro conditions
\sum_{n}a(n)=\infty,\sum_{n}a(n)^{2}<\infty
. We additionally assume that
a(n)<1
for all
n
. It should be mentioned here that
((n+1)B_{n})^{-1}
is usually computed recursively using the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula
[
31
]
. This recursion is initiated with initial condition given by a small constant
\varrho
times the identity matrix
I
to ensure invertibility. The error caused by this choice gets washed away asymptotically because of convergence of the iterates and averaging thereof.

It is shown in
[
1
]
that LSPE(
\lambda
) converges to
r^{*}
defined as the solution to the linear system

Ar^{*}+b=0,

(2.4)

where
A
and
b
are given by

A=\Phi^{T}D(\alpha P-I)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\alpha\lambda P)^{j}\Phi,

b=\Phi^{T}D\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\alpha\lambda P)^{j}\bar{k}.

(2.5)

for
\bar{k}(i)\coloneqq
the vectorized
k(\cdot)
and
P=[[p(j|i)]]_{i,j}
.

Let
B=\Phi^{T}D\Phi
and define
N=I+B^{-1}A
. It is proved in
[
2
]
that magnitude of all eigenvalues of N is less than 1.

Define
H\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
as the unique positive definite solution to the Lyapunov equation

N^{T}HN-H=-I.

Then the matrix
N
is a contraction w.r.t. the norm induced by
H
, i.e.,
\|Nx\|_{H}\leq\beta\|x\|_{H}
, where
\beta\in(0,1)
is the contraction factor. The existence of such a matrix
H
and the contractivity are shown in Lemma
6
in
A
.

We make an additional assumption on the stepsize sequence
a(n)
, viz.,

\frac{\mu_{1}}{n}\leq a(n)\leq\mu_{3}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\mu_{2}}

for all
n\geq 1
, where
\mu_{1},\mu_{3}>0
and
\frac{1}{2}+\theta<\mu_{2}\leq 1
. Here
\theta\in(0,1/2)
. For
n\geq m\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
, we define:

\displaystyle b_{m}(n)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{n}a(k),

\displaystyle\xi_{n}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\epsilon(n-1)^{1/2+\theta-\mu_{2}}+(n-1)^{-\mu_{2}}.

Note that for a fixed
\epsilon
,
\xi_{n}(\epsilon)\downarrow 0
as
n\uparrow\infty
.

Let
\lambda_{max}(\cdots)
,
\lambda_{min}(\cdots)
and
\sigma_{max}(\cdots)
denote the largest eigenvalue, the smallest eigenvalue, and the largest singular value of the matrix ‘
\cdots
’, respectively. Further, let us denote by
k_{max}=\|k\|_{\infty}
and
\Phi_{max}\coloneqq\|\Phi\|_{\infty}
. For any matrix
A
, we define the matrix norm
\|A\|
as the operator norm, i.e.,
\|A\|=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\|Ax\|
.

We now state our main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n_{0},\epsilon>0
and
0<\delta<1
. Then there exist finite positive constants
K_{1},\ldots,K_{4}
, such that for
n_{0}
large enough and
\delta
small enough to satisfy
\beta+\delta+\frac{K_{1}}{n_{0}+1}<1
and
n\geq n_{0}
, the inequality

\displaystyle\|r_{n}-r^{*}\|_{H}\leq e^{-(1-\beta)b_{n_{0}}(n-1)}\|r_{n_{0}}-r^{*}\|_{H}+\frac{K_{3}\left(\|r_{n_{0}}\|_{H}+1\right)\xi_{n}(\epsilon)}{1-(\beta+\delta+\frac{K_{1}}{n_{0}+1})},

holds with probability exceeding

1-8M^{2}\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{n-1}\left(\exp\left(\frac{-(k+1)\delta^{2}}{M^{2}K_{2}}\right)+\exp\left(\frac{-(k+1)^{2\theta}\epsilon^{2}}{M^{2}K_{4}}\right)\right).

In particular,

\displaystyle\|r_{n}-r^{*}\|_{H}\leq e^{-(1-\beta)b_{n_{0}}(n-1)}\|r_{n_{0}}-r^{*}\|_{H}+\frac{K_{3}\left(\|r_{n_{0}}\|_{H}+1\right)\xi_{n}(\epsilon)}{1-(\beta+\delta+\frac{K_{1}}{n_{0}+1})},

(2.6)

\forall\;n\geq n_{0}
with probability exceeding

1-8M^{2}\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{\infty}\left(\exp\left(\frac{-(k+1)\delta^{2}}{M^{2}K_{2}}\right)+\exp\left(\frac{-(k+1)^{2\theta}\epsilon^{2}}{M^{2}K_{4}}\right)\right).

(2.7)

We conclude this section with some observations concerning the above theorem and its proof that follows.

1.

Note that our result is not a traditional sample complexity result that gives a high probability bound at some time
n
sufficiently large starting from time zero, but a ‘tail’ bound that is a high probability bound for
n\geq n_{0}
, for
every

n_{0}
from a prescribed value onwards. Admittedly, the bound depends on
r_{n_{0}}
, but this is a.s. bounded because the iteration is a.s. convergent and a basic bound on its norm can be easily obtained using the discrete Gronwall inequality. A better result is to stitch our result with a classical sample complexity bound to get a ‘
for all time
’ result, which has been done for other algorithms in our earlier works
[
23
,
24
]
. Unfortunately a classical sample complexity bound seems unavailable for LSPE(
\lambda
).

2.

\|r_{n_{0}}\|_{H}
has a bound depending on
\|r_{0}\|_{H}
which can be derived using discrete Gronwall’s inequality. As an example, consider a stepsize of the form
a(n)=c/n
for some
c>0
. Then for a large enough
\varrho
, we have
\|r_{n_{0}}\|_{H}=O(n_{0}^{c})
. Even though our bound on
\|r_{n_{0}}\|_{H}
increases with
n_{0}
, the bound on
\|r_{n}-r^{*}\|_{H}
goes to
0
as
n\uparrow\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Define
y_{n}
for
n\geq n_{0}
by

y_{n+1}=y_{n}+a(n)B^{-1}\left(Ay_{n}+b\right),

(3.1)

where

\displaystyle r_{n+1}-y_{n+1}=

\displaystyle\;(1-a(n))(r_{n}-y_{n})

\displaystyle+a(n)\left((I+B^{-1}A)(r_{n}-y_{n})\right)

\displaystyle+a(n)\Big(\left(B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\right)A_{n}+B^{-1}\left(A_{n}-A\right)\Big)(r_{n}-y_{n})

\displaystyle+a(n)\Big(B_{n}^{-1}\left((A_{n}-A)y_{n}+(b_{n}-b)\right)+\left(B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\right)(Ay_{n}+b)\Big).

Define

\delta(n)\coloneqq(B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1})A_{n}+B^{-1}(A_{n}-A),

(3.2)

\varepsilon_{1}(n)\coloneqq B_{n}^{-1}\left(A_{n}-A\right)+\left(B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\right)A,

(3.3)

\varepsilon_{2}(n)\coloneqq B_{n}^{-1}(b_{n}-b)+\left(B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\right)b.

(3.4)

Also define
\varepsilon(n)=\varepsilon_{1}(n)y_{n}+\varepsilon_{2}(n)
.

For
n,m\geq 0
, let
\chi(n,m)=\prod_{k=m}^{n}(1-a(k))
if
n\geq m
and 1 otherwise.
Note that
r_{n_{0}}=y_{n_{0}}
.
Then iterating the above equation for
n_{0}\leq m\leq n
, we obtain,

\displaystyle r_{m+1}-y_{m+1}=\;

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\chi(m,k+1)a(k)(I+B^{-1}A)(r_{k}-y_{k})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\chi(m,k+1)a(k)\delta(k)(r_{k}-y_{k})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\chi(m,k+1)a(k)\varepsilon(k).

By Lemma
6
of Appendix A,
I+B^{-1}A
is a contraction in the
\|\cdot\|_{H}
with contraction factor
\beta\in(0,1)
. Therefore we can write

\displaystyle\|r_{m+1}-y_{m+1}\|_{H}\ \leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\chi(m,k+1)a(k)\beta\|r_{k}-y_{k}\|_{H}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\chi(m,k+1)a(k)\|\delta(k)(r_{k}-y_{k})\|_{H}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\chi(m,k+1)a(k)\|\varepsilon(k)\|_{H}.

In what follows, the constants
\{K_{i}\}
will be defined later. Lemma
4
, stated at the end of this section, gives concentration bounds on
\|\epsilon(k)\|_{H}
and
\|\delta(k)\|_{H}
. It shows that
\|\delta(k)\|_{H}\leq\delta+\frac{K_{1}}{k+1}
holds with probability exceeding some
1-p_{1_{k}}
where

p_{1_{k}}=4M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(k+1)\delta^{2}}{M^{2}K_{2}}\right)

for some constants
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
. Similarly it also shows that
\|\varepsilon_{1}(k)\|_{H}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{(k+1)^{1/2-\theta}}+\frac{K_{5}}{k+1}
and
\|\varepsilon_{2}(k)\|_{H}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{(k+1)^{1/2-\theta}}+\frac{K_{6}}{k+1}
together hold with probability greater than some

where

p_{2_{k}}=8M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(k+1)^{2\theta}\epsilon^{2}}{M^{2}K_{4}}\right)

for some constants
K_{4},K_{5}
and
K_{6}
. Recall that
\theta\in(0,1/2]
. This implies that, for some constant
K_{3}^{\prime}
,

\|\varepsilon(k)\|_{H}\leq K_{3}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{(k+1)^{(1/2-\theta)}}+\frac{1}{k+1}\right)(\|r_{n_{0}}\|_{H}+1)

holds with probability greater than
1-p_{2_{k}}
,
where we use the result from Lemma
2
. Then, for some constant
K_{3}
,

\max_{n_{0}\leq k\leq n-1}\|\delta(k)\|_{H}\leq\delta+\frac{K_{1}}{n_{0}+1}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\chi(m,k+1)a(k)\|\varepsilon(k)\|_{H}\leq K_{3}\left(\|r_{n_{0}}\|_{H}+1\right)\left(\epsilon m^{1/2+\theta-\mu_{2}}+m^{-\mu_{2}}\right),\;\forall\;n_{0}\leq m\leq n-1

(3.5)

with probability exceeding
1-\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{n-1}(p_{1_{k}}+p_{2_{k}})
. The second statement here uses the assumption that
\frac{\mu_{1}}{n}\leq a(n)\leq\mu_{3}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\mu_{2}}
and is shown in Lemma

\delta
and
\varepsilon
such that
\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{n-1}(p_{1_{k}}+p_{2_{k}})<1
. The values for these are specified in the statement of Lemma

Then with probability exceeding

\displaystyle\|r_{m+1}-y_{m+1}\|_{H}\leq\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\chi(m,k+1)a(k)\left(\beta+\delta+\ \frac{K_{1}}{n_{0}+1}\right)\|r_{k}-y_{k}\|_{H}+\zeta_{m}.

(3.6)

Let

\displaystyle e^{-(1-\beta)b_{n_{0}}(n-1)}\|r_{n_{0}}-r^{*}\|_{H}.

4
Convergence

We show that Theorem
1

Corollary 1
.

r_{n}\to r^{*}\;a.s.

Proof.

Fix
\delta

1
. Also fix
\epsilon>0
. Define
\{\hat{b}(m)\}
to be a sequence such that

. Then choose
n_{0}=n_{0}(m)
sufficiently large such that the probability in (
2.7
) exceeds
1-\frac{1}{m^{2}}
. Pick
n_{1}(m)>n_{0}(m)
large enough such that

e^{-(1-\beta)b_{n_{0}(m)}(n_{1}(m)-1)}\|r_{n_{0}(m)}-r^{*}\|_{H}\leq\frac{\hat{b}(m)}{2},

and

\frac{K_{3}\left(\|r_{n_{0}(m)}\|_{H}+1\right)\xi_{n_{1}(m)}(\epsilon)}{1-(\beta+\delta+\frac{K_{1}}{n_{0}(m)+1})}\leq\frac{\hat{b}(m)}{2}.

This implies that

\sum_{m}P\left(\sup_{n\geq n_{1}(m)}\|r_{n}-r^{*}\|_{H}>\hat{b}(m)\right)\leq\sum_{m}\frac{1}{m^{2}}<\infty.

By the Borel-Cantelli lemma,
\|r_{n_{1}(m)}-r^{*}\|_{H}\leq\hat{b}(m)
for
m
sufficiently large, a.s. Since
\hat{b}(m)\downarrow 0
, it follows that
r_{m}\rightarrow r^{*}
a.s.
∎

Thus
\{\hat{b}(m)\}
also serves as a regret bound for the ‘cost’
\|r_{n}-r^{*}\|_{H}
, though possibly not the tightest one.

5
Conclusions

In this work we provided a rigorous high probability concentration bound for the error in LSPE
(\lambda)
algorithm for all time, from some time

onwards, chosen suitably (essentially, so that the decreasing step sizes have become sufficiently small). The proof uses some novel tools including a concentration inequality from
[

. It seems very promising that analogous results would be possible for other algorithms of a similar flavor, such as zap Q-learning
[

]

Then for any

\mathbb{P}(|f(X)-\mathbb{E}(f(X))|\geq t)\leq 2\exp\left(\frac{-2t^{2}}{\|c\|_{2}^{2}\tau_{min}}\right).

(A.1)

The following lemma proves existence of the matrix
H
above and shows that

Lemma 6
.

For
N=I+B^{-1}A
, there exists a unique positive definite matrix
H\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
such that
H
satisfies
N^{T}HN-H=-I
. Also, the matrix

is a contraction under the norm induced by the matrix

Proof.

We know that
N
has all eigenvalues less than

[

) that a matrix
N\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
is asymptotically stable (i.e., all eigenvalues lie within the open unit disc) if and only if for every positive definite matrix
R\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
, there exits a unique positive definite matrix
H\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
satisfying the Lyapunov equation

N^{T}HN-H=-R.

(A.2)

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
,

\displaystyle x^{T}N^{T}HNx=x^{T}Hx-x^{T}Rx

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\|Nx\|^{2}_{H}=\|x\|^{2}_{H}-x^{T}Rx.

Here
\|\cdot\|_{H}
for a positive definite matrix
H
denotes the norm
\|x\|_{H}=\sqrt{x^{T}Hx}
. Let
R=I
in above equation and let
(\lambda_{i}>0,v_{i})
denote the eigenvalue-eigenvector pairs of

. Then since

in the usual order for positive definite matrices (as is obvious from (

) with

), we have
\lambda_{i}\geq 1\ \forall i
. Hence

\displaystyle\beta\coloneqq\max_{\|x\|\neq 0}\frac{\|Nx\|_{H}}{\|x\|_{H}}=\max_{\|x\|\neq 0}\frac{\sqrt{x^{T}N^{T}HNx}}{\sqrt{x^{T}Hx}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\|x\|\neq 0}\frac{\sqrt{x^{T}(H-I)x}}{\sqrt{x^{T}Hx}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\|x\|\neq 0}\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}-1)\langle x,v_{i}\rangle^{2}}}{\sqrt{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\langle x,v_{i}\rangle^{2}}}\ <\ 1.

We conclude that

is a contraction w.r.t. the norm induced by

∎

Next, we present a lemma containing some linear algebraic preliminaries, which are used to bound the norm induced by

Lemma 7

For a positive-definite matrix
H

1.

, then

2.

Proof.

1.

For any vector

\implies\ \ \|u\|_{H}\ \leq\ \sqrt{\lambda_{max}(H)M}a.

2.

For any non-zero vector

\displaystyle\frac{x^{T}V^{T}HVx}{x^{T}Hx}\leq\frac{\lambda_{max}(H)\|Vx\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{min}(H)\|x\|_{2}^{2}}\leq\frac{\lambda_{max}(H)}{\lambda_{min}(H)}\|V\|_{2}^{2}\ \leq\ \frac{\lambda_{max}(H)}{\lambda_{min}(H)}\|V\|_{F}^{2}.

∎

m_{1}=\max_{i\in S}\|\varphi(i)\|_{H}

\|z_{m}\|_{H}\leq\max_{i\in S}\|\varphi(i)\|_{H}\sum_{t=0}^{m}(\alpha\lambda)^{m-t}=m_{1}\frac{1-(\alpha\lambda)^{m}}{1-\alpha\lambda}.

Therefore
\|z_{m}\|_{H}\leq\frac{m_{1}}{1-\alpha\lambda}\;\;\;\forall m
. Using this in (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\|A_{n}\|_{H}\

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{m_{1}}{1-\alpha\lambda}\|(\alpha\varphi(X_{m+1})-\varphi(X_{m}))\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{m_{1}}{1-\alpha\lambda}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(\alpha+1)\max_{i\in S}\|\varphi(i)\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq m_{1}^{2}\frac{(1+\alpha)}{1-\alpha\lambda}\eqqcolon C_{5}.

∎

Next we present the proof for the bound on
\delta(n)
.

Proof for Lemma
4
(a)
.

We have

\displaystyle\|\delta(n)\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\|(B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1})A_{n}\|_{H}+\|B^{-1}(A_{n}-A)\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\|B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\|_{H}\|A_{n}\|_{H}+\|B^{-1}\|_{H}\|A_{n}-A\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\|B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\|_{H}C_{5}+\|B^{-1}\|_{H}\|A_{n}-A\|_{H}.

By using part (c) of Lemma
3
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\|B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\|_{H}\leq\frac{\delta}{2C_{5}}+\frac{C_{4}}{n+1}\Big)\geq 1-2M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(n+1)\delta^{2}}{4M^{2}C_{5}^{2}(C^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}\|B^{-1}\|_{H}^{2}\gamma_{3}}\right)

Using part (b) of Lemma
3
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\|A_{n}-A\|_{H}\leq\frac{\delta}{2\|B^{-1}\|_{H}}+\frac{C_{2}}{n+1}\Big)\geq 1-2M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(n+1)\delta^{2}}{4M^{2}\|B^{-1}\|_{H}^{2}\gamma_{2}}\right).

Combining the above inequalities using the union bound,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\delta(n)\|_{H}\leq\delta+\frac{K_{1}}{(n+1)}\Big)\

\displaystyle\geq 1-2M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(n+1)\delta^{2}}{4M^{2}C_{5}^{2}(C^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}\|B^{-1}\|_{H}^{2}\gamma_{3}}\right)-2M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(n+1)\delta^{2}}{4M^{2}\|B^{-1}\|_{H}^{2}\gamma_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-4M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(n+1)\delta^{2}}{M^{2}K_{2}}\right),

where
K_{1}\coloneqq C_{5}C_{4}+\|B^{-1}\|_{H}C_{2}
and

K_{2}\coloneqq\max\left(4C_{5}^{2}(C^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}\|B^{-1}\|_{H}^{2}\gamma_{3},4\|B^{-1}\|_{H}^{2}\gamma_{2}\right).

∎

Now we prove the bound on
\varepsilon(n)
.

Proof for Lemma
4
(b)
.

Recall that

\varepsilon_{1}(n)\coloneqq B_{n}^{-1}\left((A_{n}-A)\right)+\left(B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\right)A,

and

\varepsilon_{2}(n)\coloneqq B_{n}^{-1}(b_{n}-b)+\left(B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\right)b.

Then using Lemma
3
(b) to bound
\|A_{n}-A\|_{H}
, and Lemma
3
(c) to bound
\|B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\|_{H}
, we get

P\left(\|\epsilon_{1}(n)\|_{H}\leq a+\frac{K_{5}}{n+1}\right)\geq 1-4M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(n+1)a^{2}}{M^{2}K_{7}}\right)

where
K_{5}=C_{2}C^{\prime}+C_{4}\|A\|_{H}
and

K_{7}=\max\left\{4C^{\prime 2}\gamma_{2},4C^{\prime 2}\|B^{-1}\|_{H}^{2}\gamma_{3}\|A\|_{H}^{2}\right\}.

Similarly, using Lemma
3
(a) to bound
\|b_{n}-b\|_{H}
and Lemma
3
(c) to bound
\|B_{n}^{-1}-B^{-1}\|_{H}
, we get

P\left(\|\epsilon_{2}(n)\|_{H}\leq a+\frac{K_{6}}{n+1}\right)\geq 1-4M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(n+1)a^{2}}{M^{2}K_{8}}\right)

where
K_{6}=C_{1}C^{\prime}+C_{4}\|b\|_{H}
and

K_{8}=\max\left\{4C^{\prime 2}\gamma_{1},4C^{\prime 2}\|B^{-1}\|_{H}^{2}\gamma_{3}\|b\|_{H}^{2}\right\}.

These imply that

\|\epsilon_{1}(n)\|_{H}\leq a+\frac{K_{5}}{n+1}\;\textrm{and}\;\|\epsilon_{2}(n)\|_{H}\leq a+\frac{K_{6}}{n+1}

together hold with probability greater than

1-8M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(n+1)a^{2}}{M^{2}K_{4}}\right),

where
K_{4}=\max\{K_{7},K_{8}\}
. Substituting
a=\epsilon/(n+1)^{1/2-\theta}
completes the proof.
∎

We now give a proof for the final part of this lemma.

Proof of Lemma
4
(c)
.

Note that Lemma
4
(b) implies that

\|\varepsilon(k)\|_{H}\leq K_{3}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{(k+1)^{(1/2-\theta)}}+\frac{1}{k+1}\right)(\|r_{n_{0}}\|_{H}+1)

holds with probability greater than

1-8M^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-(k+1)^{2\theta}\epsilon^{2}}{M^{2}K_{4}}\right),

where

K_{3}^{\prime}=\max\Big\{2,2\|B^{-1}b\|_{H}/(1-\beta),K_{5},K_{5}\|B^{-1}b\|_{H}/(1-\beta)+K_{6}\Big\}.

Then under the assumption on stepsize
\{a(n)\}
, we have

\displaystyle\chi(m,k+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=k+1}^{\ell=m}(1-a(\ell))\leq\exp\left(-\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{m}a(\ell)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{m}\frac{\mu_{1}}{\ell}\right)\leq\left(\frac{k+1}{m}\right)^{\mu_{1}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\frac{\chi(m,k+1)a(k)}{(k+1)^{1/2-\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{3}\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\left(\frac{k+1}{m}\right)^{\mu_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{\mu_{2}}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{1/2-\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{3}^{*}m^{1/2+\theta-\mu_{2}},

where
K_{3}^{*}
is some positive constant. Similarly, for some positive
K_{3}^{**}
,

\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\frac{\chi(m,k+1)a(k)}{k+1}\leq K_{3}^{**}m^{-\mu_{2}}.

Then, for
K_{3}=K_{3}^{\prime}\max\{K_{3}^{*},K_{3}^{**}\}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\chi(m,k+1)a(k)\|\varepsilon(k)\|_{H}\leq K_{3}\left(\|r_{n_{0}}\|_{H}+1\right)\left(\epsilon m^{1/2+\theta-\mu_{2}}+m^{-\mu_{2}}\right),

holds with probability greater than

1-8M^{2}\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{m}\exp\left(\frac{-(k+1)^{2\theta}\epsilon^{2}}{M^{2}K_{4}}\right).

∎

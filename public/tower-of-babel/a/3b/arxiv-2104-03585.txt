1.
Introduction

The dyadic maximal operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a useful tool in analysis and is defined by the formula

{\mathcal{M}}_{d}\phi(x)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{|S|}\int_{S}|\phi(u)|du:x\in S,\;S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \text{ is a dyadic cube}\right\},

(1)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the dyadic cubes are those formed by the grids
2^{-N}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, for
N=0,1,2,\ldots
.

It is well known that the operator defined above satisfies the following weak type
(1,1)
inequality

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:{\mathcal{M}}_{d}\phi(x)>\lambda\right\}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\left\{{\mathcal{M}}_{d}\phi>\lambda\right\}}|\phi(u)|\,du

(2)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and every
\lambda>0
,
from which it is easy to get the following
L^{p}
-inequality

\|{\mathcal{M}}_{d}\phi\|_{p}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|\phi\|_{p}

(3)

for every
p>1
, and every
\phi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It is easy to see that the weak type inequality (
2
) is the best possible. For refinements of this inequality see
[
7
]
.

It has also been proved that (
3
) is best possible (see
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
for general martingales and
[
10
]
for dyadic ones).
An approach for the study of the behaviour of this maximal operator in more depth is the introduction of the so called Bellman functions which play the role
of generalized norms of
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
. Such functions related to the
L^{p}
-inequality (
3
) have been precisely evaluated in
[
3
]
,

[
4
]
and
[
6
]
. For the study of the Bellman functions of
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
, we use the notation
\Av_{E}(\psi)=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\psi
,
whenever
E
is a Lebesgue measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of positive measure and
\psi
is a real valued integrable function
defined on
E
. For a fixed dyadic cube
Q
the localized maximal operator
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{d}\phi
is defined as in (
1
)
but with the dyadic cubes
S
being assumed to be contained in
Q
. Then for every
p>1
let

B_{p}(f,F)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}({\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{d}\phi)^{p}:\;\phi\geq 0,\;\Av_{Q}(\phi)=f,\;\Av_{Q}(\phi^{p})=F\right\}

(4)

where the variables
f,F
satisfy
0<f^{p}\leq F
.
This is the well known Bellman function of two integral variables of the dyadic maximal operator.
By a scaling argument it is easy to see that (
4
) is independent of the choice of
Q
, so we may choose

Q
to be the unit cube
[0,1]^{n}
.
In
[
3
]
, the function (
4
) has been precisely evaluated for the first time. The proof has been given in a much more general setting of tree-like structures on probability spaces.

More precisely for a non-atomic probability space
(X,{\mathcal{A}},\mu)
and
{\mathcal{T}}
a family of measurable subsets of
X
that has a tree-like structure similar to the one of the dyadic case (the exact definition is given in Section
2
)
the dyadic maximal operator associated to
{\mathcal{T}}
is defined by

{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi(x)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}|\phi|d\mu:\;x\in I\in{\mathcal{T}}\right\}

(5)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}(\mu)
and
x\in X
.

This operator is related to the theory of martingales and satisfies essentially the same inequalities as
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
does. Now we define the corresponding Bellman function of three variables of
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}
, by

B_{p}^{{\mathcal{T}}}(f,F,k)=\sup\left\{\int_{K}({\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi)^{p}d\mu:\;\phi\geq 0,\;\int_{X}\phi d\mu=f,\right.\\
\left.\int_{X}\phi^{p}d\mu=F,\ K\subset X\ \text{measurable with}\ \mu(K)=k\right\},

(6)

the variables
f,F,k
satisfying
0<f^{p}\leq F
and
k\in(0,1]
.
The exact evaluation of (
6
) is given in
[
3
]
.

It is well known that in general
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi
does not belong to
L^{1}(\mu)
when
\phi\in L^{1}(\mu)
. In
[
11
]
it is proved
that if
\phi
satisfies the condition
\int\limits_{X}|\phi|\log^{+}|\phi|d\mu<+\infty
then
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi\in L^{1}(\mu)
.
In
[
9
]
it is shown that this condition is also necessary for the integrability of
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi
.
In
[
5
]
the corresponding to (
6
) function with respect to certain
L\log L
conditions has been precisely evaluated.
As a matter of fact in
[
5
]
more general conditions on
\phi
have been considered.
An application of this result is the evaluation of the following Bellman type function

B_{1}^{{\mathcal{T}}}(f,M)=\sup\left\{\int_{X}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu:\;\phi\geq 0,\;\int_{X}\phi d\mu=f,\;\|\phi\|_{\infty}=M\right\}

(7)

when
0<f\leq M
.

In the subsequent sections we provide proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
that are stated right below. The results that we present are
special cases of deep results concerning the study of more general Bellman type functions that are considered in
[
4
]
by A. Melas. However the approach that
we give in the present paper is more simple and elementary and thus easily accessible to the reader.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all real variables
f,M_{1},M_{2}
with
M_{1}\geq f>M_{2}\geq 0
the following holds:

\sup\Big\{\int\limits_{X}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu:\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\mbox{ is measurable, }\\[2.84526pt]
\int\limits_{X}\phi d\mu=f,\;\|\phi\|_{\infty}=M_{1},\;\essinf_{X}(\phi)=M_{2}\Big\}\\[2.84526pt]
=f+(f-M_{2})\log\Big(\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}}{f-M_{2}}\Big)

(8)

For the proof of the above theorem we study the respective Hardy operator problem which is connected to the dyadic
maximal operator problem and we use a symmetrization principle which appears in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For all
f,M,k
that satisfy
0<f\leq M
and
k\in(0,1]
it holds that

\sup\Big\{\int\limits_{K}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu:\;\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\mbox{ is measurable},\;\int\limits_{X}\phi d\mu=f,\;\|\phi\|_{\infty}=M,\\[2.84526pt]
\;K\mbox{ measurable, }\mu(K)=k\Big\}\\[2.84526pt]
=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad} l}kM&\mbox{ if }0<k\leq\frac{f}{M}\\[11.38109pt]
f+f\log(\frac{Mk}{f})&\mbox{ if }\frac{f}{M}<k\leq 1\end{array}\right.

(9)

The values of the supremums
that appear in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are
independent
of the probability measure space
(X,{\mathcal{A}},\mu)
and the tree
{\mathcal{T}}
(see also
[
4
]
).

At this points we should comment on the methods that we use in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
compared to
the methods used in the proofs of more general results in
[
4
]
by A. Melas.

In
[
4
]
A. Melas studies a more general problem by considering integrals of
\phi
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi
related to two increasing
convex functions
G
and
H
that satisfy certain growth conditions. His approach is given in several steps.
In the first one he provides a combinatorial rearrangement inequality on subtrees of the initial tree
{\mathcal{T}}
, and several technical lemmas that
uses in the sequel.
In the second step he applies a linearization for
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi
which permits him to study this maximal function on a certain subtree
of
{\mathcal{T}}
related to
\phi
.
By using the rearrangement inequality proved in the first step he reduces the evaluation of the Bellman function of interest to the evaluation
of a respective Bellman type function involving decreasing functions.
The proofs of his results involve techniques from ODE’s and from the theory of calculus of variations. At the third step he finds extremals
for the Bellman functions that he studies, again using ODE’s and several techniques on extremization of integral expressions.
Finally he provides examples, considering specific functions
G
and
H
.

In our approach we use independent results (appearing in
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
) that allow us to reduce the evaluation
of the Bellman type functions that we study, to the corresponding problem for the Hardy operator acting on decreasing functions on
(0,1]
.
We determine the upper bound that is described in (
8
) (see Theorem
1.1
) by using Riemann-Stieljes integrals in a direct way.
The sharpness of this upper bound in (
8
) is proved by using the results in
[
8
]
. Then we prove Theorem
1.2
by considering integrals of
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi
on certain subsets of
X
(related to the distribution function of
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi
)
which can be decomposed as pairwise almost disjoint unions of elements of
{\mathcal{T}}
. Then applying Theorem
1.1
we reach the upper
bound that is stated in (
9
). Finally we prove the sharpness of this upper bound by providing functions
\phi

which satisfy the conditions that are settled on our problem, for which the value of
\int\limits_{K}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu
on certain
suitable subsets
K
of
X
is arbitrarily close to the right side of (
9
).

2.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{A}},\mu)
be a non-atomic probability measure space.
We recall that a collection of measurable sets
{\mathcal{T}}
is called a tree in
{\mathcal{A}}
provided that the following
conditions are satisfied:

(i)

X\in{\mathcal{T}}
and every
I\in{\mathcal{T}}
has positive measure.

(ii)

To every
I\in{\mathcal{T}}
corresponds a countable (finite or infinite) family
C(I)\subset{\mathcal{T}}
, containing at least two elements,
such that:

(a)

The elements of
C(I)
are almost pairwise disjoint, i.e. for
J,J^{\prime}\in C(I)
with
J\neq J^{\prime}
we have that

\mu(J\cap J^{\prime})=0
.

(b)

I=\cup C(I)
.

(iii)

If we define
{\mathcal{T}}_{(0)}=\{X\}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{(n+1)}=\cup\{C(I):\;I\in{\mathcal{T}}_{(n)}\}
for all
n

then
{\mathcal{T}}=\bigcup\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{T}}_{(n)}
.

(iv)

For
({\mathcal{T}}_{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as defined above,

\lim\limits_{n}\big[\sup\{\mu(I):I\in{\mathcal{T}}_{(n)}\}\big]=0
.

The maximal operator
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}
associated to the tree
{\mathcal{T}}
corresponds to every measurable function

\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}

the function
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi
defined by the formula

{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi(x)=\sup\big\{\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int\limits_{I}|\phi|d\mu:\;\;x\in I\in{\mathcal{T}}\big\}.

We also recall that for every measurable function
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
,
defining

\phi^{*}:(0,1]\to[0,+\infty)
by the formula

\phi^{*}(t)=\inf\{y>0:\;\mu([|\phi|>y])<t\}

we have that
\phi^{*}
is the unique decreasing and left continuous function on
(0,1]
that is equimeasurable to
\phi
.
The following is proved in
[
3
]
:

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
I\in{\mathcal{T}}
and every
\alpha
such that
0<\alpha<1
, there exists a subfamily
{\mathcal{F}}
of
{\mathcal{T}}

consisting of almost pairwise disjoint subsets of
I
such that

\mu\big(\bigcup\limits_{J\in{\mathcal{F}}}J\big)=\sum\limits_{J\in{\mathcal{F}}}\mu(J)=(1-\alpha)\mu(I).

In
[
8
]
the following is proved:

Lemma 2.3
.

For any integrable
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
it holds that

({\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}f)^{*}(t)\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{*}(u)du\mbox{ for every }t\in(0,1].

We will also need the following symmetrization principle which appears in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
g:(0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be decreasing and let
G_{1},G_{2}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be two increasing functions.
Then for every
k\in(0,1]
the following holds:

\sup\big\{\int_{K}(G_{1}\circ{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi)(G_{2}\circ\phi)d\mu:\;\phi^{*}=g,\;\mu(K)=k\big\}\\[2.84526pt]
=\int_{0}^{k}G_{1}\big(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(u)du\big)G_{2}\big(g(t)\big)dt.

Let us fix some notation. For
M_{1}\geq f>M_{2}\geq 0
, we set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{X,{\mathcal{T}}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\text{ measurable, }\|\phi\|_{\infty}=M_{1},

\displaystyle\;\int\limits_{X}\phi d\mu=f,\;\essinf_{X}(\phi)=M_{2}\}

and

{\mathcal{C}}_{X,{\mathcal{T}}}(M_{1},f)=\{\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\text{ measurable, }\|\phi\|_{\infty}=M_{1},\;\int\limits_{X}\phi d\mu=f\}.

In Theorem
1.1
we will compute the quantity

\sup\big\{\int\limits_{X}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu:\;\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{X,{\mathcal{T}}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})\big\}

for all
for all
M_{1}\geq f>M_{2}\geq 0
while in Theorem
1.2
we will compute the quantity

\sup\big\{\int\limits_{K}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu,\;\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{X,{\mathcal{T}}}(M_{1},f),\;K\in{\mathcal{A}},\;\mu(K)=k\big\}

for all
M_{1}\geq f>0
and all
k\in(0,1]
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
we work as follows.
Fix
M_{1}\geq f>M_{2}\geq 0

and let
{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})
be the class of functions

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{g:[0,1)\to[0,+\infty):\;\;g\mbox{ is decreasing,}

\displaystyle\mbox{left continuous, continuous at }0,

\displaystyle g(0)=M_{1},\;\lim\limits_{t\to 1}g(t)=M_{2}\mbox{ and }\int_{0}^{1}g(t)dt=f\Big\}

For each
g\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})
we set

I_{g}=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(u)du\Big)dt.

Our goal is to maximize
I_{g}
over all
g\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})
.

We observe that since
\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\Big(\log(t)\int_{0}^{t}g(u)du\Big)=0
for every

g\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})
,
when we integrate by parts we get that

I_{g}=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(u)du\Big)dt=-\int_{0}^{1}\log(t)g(t)dt.

We consider the function
h:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
defined by the formula

h(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad} l}t-t\log(t)&\mbox{ if }\;\;0<t\leq 1\\[11.38109pt]
0&\mbox{ if }\;\;t=0.\end{array}\right.

Since
h(0)=0=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}h(t)
,
h^{\prime}(t)=-\log(t)>0
and
h^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{t}<0
for every
t\in(0,1)
,

h
is continuous, strictly increasing and strictly concave, thus
for every
g\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})
the value
I_{g}=\int_{0}^{1}g(t)\big(-\log(t)\big)dt
may be expressed as the Riemann-Stieltjes integral
I_{g}=\int_{0}^{1}g(t)dh(t).

Proposition 3.1
.

The quantity
\sup\{I_{g}:\;g\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})\}
is equal to

f+(f-M_{2})\log(\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}}{f-M_{2}})
.
Moreover the supremum is uniquely attained by the function
g_{0}
defined as

g_{0}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad} l}M_{1}&\mbox{ if }\;\;0\leq t\leq c\\[11.38109pt]
M_{2}&\mbox{ if }\;\;c<t\leq 1\end{array}\right.

where
c=\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}}
.

Proof.

We start with the second part. A direct calculation shows that

\int_{0}^{1}g_{0}(t)dt=M_{1}\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}}+M_{2}(1-\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}})=f,

while
g_{0}(0)=M_{1}
,
g_{0}(1)=M_{2}
and
g_{0}
is left continuous; thus
g_{0}\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})
.
We also have that

\displaystyle I_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\log(t)g_{0}(t)dt=\ -\int_{0}^{c}M_{1}\log(t)dt-\int_{c}^{1}M_{2}\log(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{1}\big(h(c)-h(0)\big)+M_{2}\big(h(1)-h(c)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{1}\big(c-c\log(c))+M_{2}\big(1-(c-c\log(c)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{2}+(M_{1}-M_{2})\;c\;\big(1-\log(c)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{2}+(M_{1}-M_{2})\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}}\big(1-\log(\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f+(f-M_{2})\log(\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}}{f-M_{2}}).

Thus
\sup\{I_{g}:\;g\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})\}\geq f+(f-M_{2})\log(\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}}{f-M_{2}})
.

We will show now that the number
f+(f-M_{2})\log(\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}}{f-M_{2}})
is an upper
bound of the set
\{I_{g}:\;g\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})\}
.

Let
g\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})
.
We will calculate the Riemman Stieltjes integral
I_{g}=\int_{0}^{1}g(t)dh(t)

as the limit of the Riemman Stieltjes sums over the net of all partitions
{\mathcal{P}}=\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}
equipped with the right boundaries of the intervals
[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
as intermediate points.

\displaystyle I_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g(t)dh(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}g(t_{i})\big(h(t_{i})-h(t_{i-1})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{P}}}\big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}g(t_{i})h(t_{i})-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}g(t_{i})h(t_{i-1})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{P}}}\;\;\Big[-g(t_{1})h(t_{0})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}h(t_{i})\big(g(t_{i})-g(t_{i+1})\big)+g(t_{n})h(t_{n})\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{P}}}\;\;\Big[M_{2}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}h(t_{i})\big(g(t_{i})-g(t_{i+1})\big)\Big]

(we took into account that
h(t_{0})=h(0)=0
,
h(t_{n})=h(1)=1
and
g(t_{n})=g(1)=M_{2}
).

Since
g
is decreasing, for every partition
{\mathcal{P}}=\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}
we have that
g(t_{i})-g(t_{i+1})\geq 0

for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-1
while
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\big(g(t_{i})-g(t_{i+1})\big)=g(0)-g(1)=M_{1}-M_{2}

and thus the numbers
\big(\frac{g(t_{i})-g(t_{i+1})}{M_{1}-M_{2}}\big)_{i=0}^{n-1}
serve as coefficients of a convex combination. Since

I_{g}=M_{2}+(M_{1}-M_{2})\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}h(t_{i})\frac{g(t_{i})-g(t_{i+1})}{M_{1}-M_{2}}

the fact that the function
h
is concave and continuous yields

I_{g}\leq M_{2}+(M_{1}-M_{2})h\Big(\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{g(t_{i})-g(t_{i+1})}{M_{1}-M_{2}}t_{i}\Big)

(10)

But also

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\big(g(t_{i})-g(t_{i+1})\big)t_{i}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{P}}}\Big(g(t_{0})t_{0}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}g(t_{i})(t_{i}-t_{i-1})-g(t_{n})t_{n-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=M_{1}\cdot 0+\int_{0}^{1}g(t)dt-M_{2}\cdot 1=f-M_{2}

and thus

\displaystyle I_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{2}+(M_{1}-M_{2})h(\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{2}+(M_{1}-M_{2})\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}}(1-\log\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{2}+(f-M_{2})+(f-M_{2})\log\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}}{f-M_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f+(f-M_{2})\log\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}}{f-M_{2}}.

Up to this pont we have shown that

\sup\{I_{g}:g\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})\}=f+(f-M_{2})\log\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}}{f-M_{2}}

and that the
supremum is attained by the function
g_{0}
. It remains to prove that
g_{0}
is the unique function in
{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})
with this property.
It is enough to show that for each
g\in{\mathcal{A}}(M_{1},f,M_{2})
with
g\neq g_{0}
we have that
I_{g}<f+(f-M_{2})\log\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}}{f-M_{2}}
.

Consider such a
g
. Since
g\neq g_{0}
there exists
y_{0}
a point of continuity of
g
such that
M_{1}>g(y_{0})>M_{2}
. We set
M=g(y_{0})
.
In the proof that is presented above we may consider only partitions of
[0,1]
containing the point
y_{0}
i.e. partitions of the form

{\mathcal{P}}=\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{k-1}<t_{k}=y_{0}<t_{k+1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}.

Then

\displaystyle I_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{2}+(M_{1}-M_{2})\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{P}}}\Big(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}h(t_{i})\frac{g(t_{i})-g(t_{i+1})}{M_{1}-M_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{2}+(M_{1}-M_{2})\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{P}}}\Big(\frac{M_{1}-M}{M_{1}-M_{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}h(t_{i})\frac{g(t_{i})-g(t_{i+1})}{M_{1}-M}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g_{1},g_{2}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
and
0<k\leq 1
such that:

(a)
g_{1},g_{2}
are decreasing.

(b)
g_{1}(t)\leq g_{2}(t)
for every

.

(c)
g_{1}
is right continuous and
g_{2}
is left continuous.

(d)
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}g_{2}(t)=0
and
g_{2}(0)=1
.
Then there exists
u\in[0,+\infty)
such that
g_{1}(u)\leq k\leq g_{2}(u)
.

Proof.

We set
u=\sup\;g_{2}^{-1}\Big([k,+\infty)\Big)
.
Since
g_{2}(0)=1
the set
g_{2}^{-1}\Big([k,+\infty)\Big)
is nonempty, while it is bounded from above since
g_{2}
is decreasing
and
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}g_{2}(t)=0
. Thus
u
is a well defined real number.

The left continuity of
g_{2}
yields
g_{2}(u)\geq k
, thus it suffices to show that
g_{1}(u)\leq k
.
If
g_{1}(u)>k
from the right continuity of
g_{1}
we get that
g_{1}(t)>k
for all
t\in[u,u+\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0

Thus
k<g_{1}(t)\leq g_{2}(t)
for all

u
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{A}},\mu)
be a measure space, let
g:X\to[0,+\infty)
be a measurable function and
u\geq 0
.
Let also
D
be a measurable set such that
[g>u]\subset D\subset[g\geq u]
.
Then for every measurable set
K
such that
\mu(D)=\mu(K)

\int\limits_{K}gd\mu\leq\int\limits_{D}gd\mu
.

Proof.

Let
\nu
denote the indefinite integral of
g
with respect to
\mu
, i.e. the measure defined by the formula

\nu(A)=\int\limits_{A}gd\mu
for all
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

We set
V_{1}=[g>u]
and
V_{2}=[g\geq u]
.
We have that

\nu(K)=\nu(K\cap V_{1})+\nu(K\setminus V_{1})=\nu(V_{1})-\nu(V_{1}\setminus K)+\nu(K\setminus V_{1})
and taking into account

V_{1}\subset D\subset V_{2}
we get that

\nu(K)-\nu(D)=-\nu(D\setminus V_{1})-\nu(V_{1}\setminus K)+\nu(K\setminus V_{1})

(11)

Since for every

we have that

and hence

and since

we get that

-\nu(D\setminus V_{1})\leq-u\mu(D\setminus V_{1})=-u\mu(D)+u\mu(V_{1})

(12)

For every
x\in V_{1}\setminus K
we have that
g(x)>u
, thus

-\nu(V_{1}\setminus K)\leq-u\mu(V_{1}\setminus K)=-u\mu(V_{1})+u\mu(K\cap V_{1})

(13)

Finally for every
x\in K\setminus V_{1}
we have that

thus

\nu(K\setminus V_{1})\leq u\mu(K\setminus V_{1})=u\mu(K)-u\mu(K\cap V_{1})

Therefore from (

\nu(K)-\nu(D)\leq u\mu(K)-u\mu(D)=0

i.e.
\int\limits_{K}gd\mu\leq\int\limits_{D}gd\mu

.
The function
h_{1}
is increasing, since
h_{1}^{\prime}(t)=\log(\frac{Mk}{t})>0
for every
t\in(0,Mk)
.
Since from (
16
) we have that
k\Gamma\leq f
we get that

h_{1}(k\Gamma)\leq h_{1}(f)
(remember that we are studying the case where
\frac{f}{M}<k\leq 1
), thus

k\Gamma+k\Gamma\log(\frac{Mk}{k\Gamma})\leq f+f\log(\frac{Mk}{f})
.

Therefore

\int\limits_{K}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu\leq f+f\log(\frac{Mk}{f}).

Up to this point we have shown that the left hand side of (
9
) in the statement of Theorem
1.2
is less than or equal to the right side.
It remains to show that equality holds. We will again discern the cases
0<k\leq\frac{f}{M}
and
\frac{f}{M}<k\leq 1
.

Let’s first consider the case
0<k\leq\frac{f}{M}
. Using Lemma
2.2
we select a family
(I_{i})_{i\in A}
of almost pairwise disjoint
sets in
{\mathcal{T}}
such that
\sum\limits_{i\in A}\mu(I_{i})=k
.
We consider the function
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

\phi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad} l}M&\mbox{ if }\;\;x\in\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i}\\[11.38109pt]
\frac{f-Mk}{1-k}&\mbox{ if }\;\;x\in X\setminus\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i}\end{array}\right.

The function
\phi
is measurable, while since
k\leq\frac{f}{M}
we have
\phi\geq 0
.
Also, since
f\leq M
we have
\frac{f-Mk}{1-k}\leq M
thus
\|\phi\|_{\infty}=M
.
Finally

\displaystyle\int\limits_{X}\phi d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M\mu(\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i})+\frac{f-Mk}{1-k}\mu(X\setminus\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Mk+\frac{f-Mk}{1-k}(1-k)=f

thus
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{X,{\mathcal{T}}}(M,f)
.

Since

\int\limits_{\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i}}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu=Mk

the proof in the case where
0<k\leq\frac{f}{M}
is complete.

We treat now the case
\frac{f}{M}\leq k\leq 1
.
In this case it is enough to show that for every
\delta>0
there exists
a
K\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
\mu(K)=k
and a
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{X,{\mathcal{T}}}(M,f)
such that

\int\limits_{K}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu\geq(1-\delta)\big(f+f\log(\frac{Mk}{f})\big).

As in the first case, using again Lemma
2.2
, we select a family
(I_{i})_{i\in A}
of almost pairwise disjoint
sets in
{\mathcal{T}}
such that
\sum\limits_{i\in A}\mu(I_{i})=k
.
We set
K=\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i}
,
f^{\prime}=\frac{f}{k}
(notice that
f^{\prime}\leq M
) and for every
i\in A

we set
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=\{I\in{\mathcal{A}}:\;I\subset I_{i}\}
,
{\mathcal{T}}_{i}=\{I\in{\mathcal{T}}:\;I\subset I_{i}\}
and
we define
\mu_{i}(I)=\frac{1}{\mu(I_{i})}\mu(I)
for every
I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
. For each
i\in A
, Theorem
1.1

yields the
existence of a
\phi_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{I_{i},{\mathcal{T}}_{i}}(M,f^{\prime},0)
such that

\int{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}_{i}}\phi_{i}d\mu_{i}>(1-\delta)(f^{\prime}+f^{\prime}\log(\frac{M}{f^{\prime}}))
.

We consider
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
with
\phi|I_{i}=\phi_{i}
for each
i\in I
and
\phi|(X\setminus\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i})=0
.
Then
\phi\geq 0
,
\|\phi\|_{\infty}=M
and

\int_{X}\phi d\mu=\sum\limits_{i\in A}\int\limits_{I_{i}}\phi d\mu=\sum\limits_{i\in A}\mu(I_{i})f^{\prime}=k\frac{f}{k}=f

hence
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{X,{\mathcal{T}}}(M,f)
.

Finally we have that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{K}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in A}\int\limits_{I_{i}}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in A}\int\limits_{I_{i}}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}_{i}}\phi_{i}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in A}\mu(I_{i})\int\limits_{I_{i}}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}_{i}}\phi_{i}d\mu_{i}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in A}\mu(I_{i})(1-\delta)(f^{\prime}+f^{\prime}\log(\frac{M}{f^{\prime}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(1-\delta)(\frac{f}{k}+\frac{f}{k}\log(\frac{Mk}{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\delta)(f+f\log(\frac{Mk}{f})).

Letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
we obtain our result.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

In the case that
f<M
we may add the condition
\essinf_{X}(\phi)=0
in the statement of Theorem
1.2
without affecting the value of the supremum.

Proof.

We distinguish the following three cases

Case 1.

k=1
.

This case is obviously a special case of Theorem
1.1
for
M_{2}=0
.

Case 2.

\frac{f}{M}\leq k<1
.

In this case the extremal function
\phi
that we constructed in the proof of Theorem
1.2

satisfies the desired condition.

Case 3.

0<k<\frac{f}{M}
.

In this case the extremal function
\phi
that we constructed in the proof of Theorem
1.2
does not satisfy the condition
\essinf_{X}(\phi)=0
.
In order to overcome this difficulty, for every sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
(namely
\varepsilon<1-\frac{f}{M}
) we construct a function
\phi_{\varepsilon}
as follows.
Keeping the notation that was used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, for fixed
i_{0}\in A
, we choose a set
J_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathcal{T}}

such that
0<\mu(J_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon\mu(I_{0})<1-\frac{f}{M}
and
\mu(J_{\varepsilon})<\frac{\varepsilon}{M}
. We define
\phi_{\varepsilon}
by the formula

\phi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad} l}M&\mbox{if }x\in\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i}\setminus J_{\varepsilon}\\[8.53581pt]
0&\mbox{ if }\;\;x\in J_{\varepsilon}\\[8.53581pt]
\frac{f-M(k-\mu(J_{\varepsilon}))}{1-k}&\mbox{ if }\;\;x\in X\setminus\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i}.\end{array}\right.

We notice that for this choice of
\varepsilon
and
J_{\varepsilon}
we have that
\frac{f-M(k-\mu(J_{\varepsilon}))}{1-k}<M
and thus
\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}=M
,
while
\int\phi_{\varepsilon}d\mu=f
,
\essinf_{X}(\phi_{\varepsilon})=0
and

\int\limits_{\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i}}{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\varepsilon}d\mu\geq\int\limits_{\bigcup\limits_{i\in A}I_{i}\setminus J_{\varepsilon}}{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\varepsilon}d\mu=M(k-\mu(J_{\varepsilon}))\geq Mk-\varepsilon.

and this completes the proof of the remark.
∎

An application of Theorem
1.2
is the following.

Corollary 4.4
.

For
0\leq M_{2}<f<M_{1}
and
0\leq k\leq 1

\sup\Big\{\int\limits_{K}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\phi d\mu:\;\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\mbox{ is measurable},\;\int\limits_{X}\phi d\mu=f,\;\|\phi\|_{\infty}=M_{1},\\[2.84526pt]
\essinf_{X}(\phi)=M_{2},\;K\mbox{ measurable, }\mu(K)=k\Big\}\\[2.84526pt]
=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad} l}kM_{1}&\mbox{ if }0<k\leq\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}}\\[11.38109pt]
f-M_{2}(1-k)+(f-M_{2})\log(\frac{(M_{1}-M_{2})k}{f-M_{2}})&\mbox{ if }\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}}<k\leq 1\end{array}\right.

(17)

Proof.

Fix
M_{2},f,M_{1}
and
k
as in the statement, and let
A
be the value of the supremum.
Then

A=kM_{2}+\sup\Big\{\int\limits_{K}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(\phi-M_{2})d\mu:\;\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\mbox{ is measurable},\\[2.84526pt]
\int\limits_{X}(\phi-M_{2})d\mu=f-M_{2},\;\;\|\phi-M_{2}\|_{\infty}=M_{1}-M_{2},\\[2.84526pt]
\essinf_{X}(\phi-M_{2})=0,\;\;K\mbox{ measurable, }\mu(K)=k\Big\}\\[2.84526pt]
=kM_{2}+\sup\Big\{\int\limits_{K}{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\psi d\mu:\;\psi:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\mbox{ is measurable},\;\int\limits_{X}\psi d\mu=f-M_{2},\\[2.84526pt]
\|\psi\|_{\infty}=M_{1}-M_{2},\;\;\essinf_{X}(\psi)=0,\;\;K\mbox{ measurable, }\mu(K)=k\Big\}\\[2.84526pt]

The latest supremum is evaluated using Remark
4.3
.
In the case that
0<k\leq\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}}
we get that

A=kM_{2}+k(M_{1}-M_{2})=kM_{1}

while in the case that
0<k\leq\frac{f-M_{2}}{M_{1}-M_{2}}

we get that

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kM_{2}+(f-M_{2})+(f-M_{2})\log(\frac{(M_{1}-M_{2})k}{f-M_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f-M_{2}(1-k)+(f-M_{2})\log(\frac{(M_{1}-M_{2})k}{f-M_{2}})

and this completes the proof.
∎

Unraveling Functional Equations in Composition Algebra: Resolving Conjectures and Examining Implications Thanks: The first author was supported by FAPESP number 2022/14579-0.
Daniel Kawai, Bruno Leonardo Macedo Ferreira

Abstract
. We address the conjectures left by the recent article by Ferreira et al. titled “Commuting maps and identities with inverses on alternative division rings.” We also present an example showing the necessity of the conditions of the results that answer the conjectures. Keywords : Functional identity, division algebras, additive functions, split octonion algebras. 2020 MSC : 16K20; 16W10.



1.
Introduction

In 1987, Vukman
[
4
]
studied a result about Cauchy functional equations in associative division rings, and he showed that if
D
is a noncommutative associative division ring with characteristics different from
2
and
f:D\rightarrow D
is an additive function satisfying:

(1)

f(x)=-x^{2}f(x^{-1})

for every
x\in D^{\times}
, where
D^{\times}
is the set of invertible elements of
D
, then
f(x)=0
for all
x\in D
.

In 2024, Ferreira et al.
[
2
]
studied the functional equation (
1
) in alternative division rings and proved the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
D
be a noncommutative alternative division ring with characteristic different from
2
, and let
f:D\rightarrow D
be an additive map satisfying the identity (
1
):

f(x)=-x^{2}f(x^{-1})

for for every
x\in D^{\times}
. Then
f(x)=0
for all
x\in D
.

They also posed the following conjectures:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Is Theorem
1.1
true if
D=O
is a split-octonion algebra?

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
D
be an alternative division ring and suppose
f,g:D\rightarrow D
are additive maps satisfying:

(2)

f(x)+x^{2}g(x^{-1})=0

for all
x\in D^{\times}
. Then there exists a fixed element
q\in D
such that
f(x)=xq

and
g(x)=-xq
for all
x\in D
.

Note that the articles
[
4
]
and
[
2
]
deal with the particular case of equation (
2
) where
f=g
. The central focus of this work is to provide answers to these conjectures. Our approach aims to characterize the additive mappings that preserve that identity, exploring both theoretical aspects and practical implications.

We hope that this study will contribute not only to the understanding of functional equation theory but also to the advancement of knowledge in various areas of mathematics.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
R
be a ring (not necessarily associative or commutative) and consider the following notational convention for its multiplication operation: that
xy\cdot z=(xy)z
and
x\cdot yz=x(yz)
for
x,y,z\in R
, simply to reduce the number of necessary parentheses. We denote the associator of
R
by
(x,y,z)=xy\cdot z-x\cdot yz
for
x,y,z\in R
.

A ring
R
is said to be
alternative
if
(x,x,y)=0=(y,x,x)
for all
x,y\in R
. One easily sees that any associative ring is an alternative ring. It is also well-known that every alternative ring satisfies the flexible identity
(x,y,x)=0
, for all
x,y\in R
. Alternative rings also satisfy the Moufang identities

(xax)y=x[a(xy)],

y(xax)=[(yx)a]x,

(xy)(ax)=x(ya)x

for all
x,y,a\in R
. If parentheses or the symbol
\cdot
are not needed to write an expression, we omit them.

In any ring
R
with
1
, an element
x
is said to have an inverse
x^{-1}
if there is an element
x^{-1}
in
R
satisfying
xx^{-1}=x^{-1}x=1
. The set of all invertible elements of
R
is denoted
R^{\times}
. Just as in the associative case, inverses are unique in alternative rings. If every nonzero element in an alternative ring
R
with
1
has an inverse, then
R
is called an alternative division ring. If
R
is an alternative ring with
1
, then a subset
S\subseteq R
is said to be a
division subring
if
S
is a subring of
R
,
1\in S
and for every
x\in S\cap R^{\times}
we have
x^{-1}\in S
.

It is well known that, if
R
is an alternative division ring, then for every
x,y\in R
the division subring generated by
\{x,y\}
is associative, as the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

In an alternative ring with
1
, the division subring generated by any two elements is associative.

Proof.

Let
A
be an alternative ring with
1
. We will show that if
(a,b,c)=0
and
a\in R^{\times}
, then
(a^{-1},b,c)=0
. Indeed, we have:

(a^{-1},b,c)=(a^{-1},a^{-1}(ab),c)=(a^{-1},ab,c)a^{-1},

but we have:

(a^{-1},ab,c)=-(b,aa^{-1},c)+b(a^{-1},a,c)+a^{-1}(b,a,c)=0,

so
(a^{-1},b,c)=0
.

Let
u,v\in A
be two elements. Let
R_{k}
be the submodule generated by non-associative words of the form
w(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
where there exist
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1
such that
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}\leq k
and
a_{i}\in\{u,v\}
if
k_{i}=1
and
a_{i}\in(R_{k_{i}-1}\cap R^{\times})^{-1}
if
k_{i}\geq 2
. Let
S=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}R_{k}
. Then
R_{1}\subseteq R_{2}\subseteq\cdots
and also
S
is a submodule. For
x\in R_{k}
and
y\in R_{l}
, then
x
is a linear combination of
w_{x}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
where there exist
k_{1},\dots,k_{s},l_{1},\dots,l_{s}\geq 1
such that
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{s}\leq k+l
and
a_{i}\in\{u,v\}
if
k_{i}=1
and
a_{i}\in(R_{k_{i}-1}\cap R^{\times})^{-1}
if
k_{i}\geq 2
, and similarly for
y_{j}
. Therefore
xy
is a linear combination of words
w_{x}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})w_{y}(b_{1},\dots,b_{s})
where there exist
k_{1},\dots,k_{s},l_{1},\dots,l_{s}\geq 1
such that
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{s}\leq k+l
and
a_{i}\in\{u,v\}
if
k_{i}=1
and
a_{i}\in(R_{k_{i}-1}\cap R^{\times})^{-1}
if
k_{i}\geq 2
, and similarly for
b_{j}
. Hence
xy\in R_{k+l}
. Finally, if
x\in R_{k}\cap R^{\times}
, then
x^{-1}\in(R_{k}\cap R^{\times})^{-1}
, so
x^{-1}\in R_{k+1}
. Also, it is easy to see that
u,v\in R_{1}
. Therefore
S
is the division subring generated by
u
and
v
.

We will show by induction on
d(x)+d(y)+d(z)
that
(x,y,z)=0
for any
x,y,z\in S
, where
d(x)
is the smallest
k
such that
x\in R_{k}
. If
d(x)+d(y)+d(z)=3
, then
d(x)=d(y)=d(z)=1
, so
x
,
y
, and
z
are linear combinations of
u
and
v
, so
(x,y,z)=0
. Now if
d(x)+d(y)+d(z)>3
, we can assume that
x
,
y
, and
z
are words
w_{x}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
,
w_{y}(b_{1},\dots,b_{s})
, and
w_{z}(c_{1},\dots,c_{t})
and that
x_{1},\dots,x_{r},y_{1},\dots,y_{s},z_{1},\dots,z_{t}\geq 1
satisfy
x_{1}+\dots+x_{r}\leq d(x)
and
a_{i}\in\{u,v\}
if
x_{i}=1
and
a_{i}\in(R_{x_{i}-1}\cap R^{\times})^{-1}
if
x_{i}\geq 2
, and similarly for
y_{j}
and
z_{k}
. Since
n=d(x)+d(y)+d(z)
, then
d(x),d(y),d(z)<n
, so by the induction hypothesis, we can assume that
w_{x}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})=(\cdots(a_{1}a_{2})a_{3}\cdots)a_{r}
and so on. We have two cases:

Case 1:
If
x_{r}\geq 2
, then
a_{r}=a^{-1}
for some
a\in R_{x_{r}-1}\cap R^{\times}
.

(1)

If
r=1
, then
(x,y,z)=(a^{-1},y,z)=0
, because
d(a)\leq x_{r}-1\leq d(x)-1
and so
(a,y,z)=0
.

(2)

If
r\geq 2
, let
\overline{a}=(\cdots(a_{1}a_{2})a_{3}\cdots)a_{r-1}
, then:

\displaystyle(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=(\overline{a}a,y,z)

\displaystyle=-(\overline{a}y,a_{r},z)+a_{r}(\overline{a},y,z)+y(a,\overline{a},z),

but
d(\overline{a})\leq d(a_{1})+\cdots+d(a_{r-1})\leq x_{1}+\cdots+x_{r-1}
and
d(a_{r})\leq x_{r}
, so
d(\overline{a})<d(x)
, hence:

\displaystyle(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=-(\overline{a}y,a_{r},z)

\displaystyle=-u(\overline{a},\overline{b}z,u)=0,

since
(\overline{a}y,a,z)=0
as
d(a)\leq x_{r}-1<x_{r}
so
d(\overline{a}y)+d(a)+d(z)<n
.

Case 2:
If
x_{r}=y_{s}=z_{t}=1
, then
a_{r},b_{s},c_{t}\in\{u,v\}
, so we can assume without loss of generality that
a_{r}=b_{s}=u
, then:

\displaystyle(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=(\overline{a}u,\overline{b}u,z)

\displaystyle=-(\overline{a}u,\overline{b}z,u)+u(\overline{a}u,\overline{b},z)+z(\overline{a}u,\overline{b},u)

\displaystyle=-(\overline{a}u,\overline{b}z,u)

\displaystyle=-u(\overline{a},\overline{b}z,u)=0.

∎

It is also pertinent to revisit the well-known identity attributed to Hua, an important element for the development of this paper. Let
R
be an unital alternative ring and let
a
,
b
be any two elements of
R
with
a,b,ab-1\in R^{\times}
. Then Hua’s identity holds:

a-(a^{-1}+(b^{-1}-a)^{-1})^{-1}=aba.

3.
Alterntative Division Rings

In this section, we present theorems that address the Conjecture
1.2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
D
satisfy one of the following properties:

a)

D
is a noncommutative alternative division ring with
\mathrm{Char}(D)\neq 2
.

b)

D
is a perfect field with
\mathrm{Char}(D)=2
.

Suppose
f,g:D\rightarrow D
are additive maps satisfying (
2
):

f(x)+x^{2}g(x^{-1})=0

for all
x\in D^{\times}
. Then there exists a fixed element
q\in D
such that
f(x)=xq
and
g(x)=-xq
for all
x\in D
.

Proof.

We proceed with the proof of Theorem
3.1
by delineating several steps.

Step 1:

Consider an unital alternative ring
D
, and let
f,g:D\rightarrow D
be additive maps satisfying (
2
) for all
x\in D^{\times}
. Choose
a\in D
such that
a,a-1\in D^{\times}
. By invoking Hua’s identity and exploiting the associativity of the division subring generated by
\{a,x\}
for every
x\in D
, we derive:

(3)

a^{2}=a-(a^{-1}+(1-a)^{-1})^{-1}

and consequently:

\displaystyle f(a^{2})

\displaystyle=f(a-(a^{-1}+(1-a)^{-1})^{-1})

\displaystyle=f(a)-f((a^{-1}+(1-a)^{-1})^{-1})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{vukmana}}{=}f(a)+(a^{-1}+(1-a)^{-1})^{-2}g(a^{-1}+(1-a)^{-1})

\displaystyle=f(a)+a^{2}(1-a)^{2}g(a^{-1}+(1-a)^{-1})

\displaystyle=f(a)+a^{2}(1-a)^{2}(g(a^{-1})+g((1-a)^{-1}))

\displaystyle=f(a)+a^{2}(1-a)^{2}g(a^{-1})+a^{2}(1-a)^{2}g((1-a)^{-1})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{vukmana}}{=}f(a)-a^{2}(1-a)^{2}a^{-2}f(a)-a^{2}(1-a)^{2}(1-a)^{-2}f(1-a)

\displaystyle=f(a)-(1-a)^{2}f(a)-a^{2}f(1-a)

\displaystyle=f(a)-(1-a)^{2}f(a)-a^{2}(f(1)-f(a))

\displaystyle=f(a)-(1-a)^{2}f(a)-a^{2}f(1)+a^{2}f(a)

\displaystyle=(1+a^{2}-(1-a)^{2})f(a)-a^{2}f(1)

\displaystyle=2af(a)-a^{2}f(1),

which can be succinctly expressed as:

(4)

f(a^{2})=2af(a)-a^{2}f(1).

Step 2:
Assume
D
satisfies either (a) or (b), and let
f,g:D\rightarrow D
be additive maps satisfying (
2
) for all
x\in D^{\times}
. In this scenario,
D
qualifies as an alternative division ring. Consequently, for any
a\in D
, if
a\neq 0
and
a\neq 1
, then
a\in D^{\times}
and
a-1\in D^{\times}
, allowing us to apply (
4
). Moreover, (
4
) remains valid for
a=0
or
a=1
. Define
h(x)=f(x)-xf(1)
for all
x\in D
. Then
h:D\rightarrow D
is additive and satisfies:

(5)

h(x^{2})=2xh(x)

for every
x\in D
.

a)

In case (a), leveraging the Bruck and Kleinfeld Theorem,
D
manifests as either an associative division algebra or an octonion division algebra over its center. We can adopt the proof strategy from the Main Theorem of
[
4
]
for the associative case and Theorem 5.1 of
[
2
]
for the octonion case to establish
h(x)=0
for all
x\in D
.

b)

In case (b), for every
x\in D
, there exists
r\in D
such that
r^{2}=x
, implying
h(x)=h(r^{2})=2rh(r)=0
.

Consequently, in both scenarios, we infer:

(6)

f(x)=xf(1)

for all
x\in D
. Thus, for every
x\neq 0
, by invoking (
2
) with
x^{-1}
instead of
x
, we deduce:

f(x^{-1})+x^{-2}g(x)=0,

which, in turn, yields:

(7)

g(x)=-xf(1).

Equation (
7
) holds for
x=0
as well.
∎

Now, we will prove the necessity of the condition of perfectness in Theorem
3.1
, case (b), by illustrating that in the non-perfect field
D=\mathbb{Z}_{2}(t)
of rational functions with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, there exist numerous solutions
(f,g)
to the functional equation (
2
), where
f
and
g
are additive, aside from the standard one.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
D=\mathbb{Z}_{2}(t)
be the field of rational functions with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. For any
A,B\in D
, there is exactly one pair
(f,g)
consisting of additive functions
f,g:D\rightarrow D
that satisfy
f(1)=A
,
f(t)=B
and the identity (
2
):

f(x)+x^{2}g(x^{-1})=0

for every
x\in D
such that
x\neq 0
.

Proof.

1) Uniqueness:

Let
f,g:D\rightarrow D
be additive functions that satisfy
f(1)=A
,
f(t)=B
and (
2
) for all
x\neq 0
. Then, for every
a,b\in D
such that
ab\neq 0,1
, by Hua identity and the fact that
D
is a field with
\mathrm{Char}(D)=2
:

a^{2}b=a+(a^{-1}+(b^{-1}+a)^{-1})^{-1}

we have:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}f(a^{2}b)&=&f(a+(a^{-1}+(b^{-1}+a)^{-1})^{-1})\\
&=&f(a)+f((a^{-1}+(b^{-1}+a)^{-1})^{-1})\\
&\overset{\eqref{vukmana}}{=}&f(a)+(a^{-1}+(b^{-1}+a)^{-1})^{-2}g(a^{-1}+(b^{-1}+a)^{-1})\\
&=&f(a)+(a^{2}b+a)^{2}g(a^{-1}+(b^{-1}+a)^{-1})\\
&=&f(a)+(a^{2}b+a)^{2}(g(a^{-1})+g((b^{-1}+a)^{-1}))\\
&=&f(a)+(a^{2}b+a)^{2}g(a^{-1})+(a^{2}b+a)^{2}g((b^{-1}+a)^{-1})\\
&\overset{\eqref{vukmana}}{=}&f(a)+(a^{2}b+a)^{2}a^{-2}f(a)+(a^{2}b+a)^{2}(b^{-1}+a)^{-2}f(b^{-1}+a)\\
&=&f(a)+(ab+1)^{2}f(a)+a^{2}b^{2}(a+b^{-1})^{2}(b^{-1}+a)^{-2}f(b^{-1}+a)\\
&=&f(a)+(a^{2}b^{2}+1)f(a)+a^{2}b^{2}f(b^{-1}+a)\\
&=&a^{2}b^{2}f(a)+a^{2}b^{2}(f(b^{-1})+f(a))\\
&=&a^{2}b^{2}f(a)+a^{2}b^{2}f(b^{-1})+a^{2}b^{2}f(a)\\
&\overset{\eqref{vukmana}}{=}&a^{2}b^{2}f(a)+a^{2}b^{2}b^{-2}g(b)+a^{2}b^{2}f(a)\\
&=&a^{2}b^{2}f(a)+a^{2}g(b)+a^{2}b^{2}f(a)\\
&=&a^{2}g(b),\end{array}

that is:

(8)

f(a^{2}b)=a^{2}g(b).

By
f(0)=g(0)=0
and (
2
), the above equation (
8
) is also true for
a,b\in D
such that
ab=0
or
ab=1
. So (
8
) holds for all
a,b\in D
. By (
8
) with
a=1
, for all
b\in D
we have
f(b)=g(b)
. So
f=g
and for all
a\in D
we have:

(9)

f(a^{2}b)=a^{2}f(b).

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, by (
9
) with
a=t
and
b=t^{n}
, we have:

(10)

f(t^{n+2})=t^{2}f(t^{n}).

Therefore, using induction in
n
and (
10
), for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have:

f(t^{2n})=t^{2n}A,\quad\quad f(t^{2n+1})=t^{2n}B.

Also, if
P(t)
is a polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, then it is easy to see that:

(11)

f(P(t^{2})t^{2p})=P(t^{2})t^{2p}A,\quad\quad f(P(t^{2})t^{2p+1})=P(t^{2})t^{2p}B

and also:

(12)

P(t^{2})=P(t)^{2}.

Every element of
x\in D
can be represented by the form:

x=\frac{P(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t},

where
P(t),Q(t),R(t),S(t)
are polynomials with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, so, by representing:

P(t)=\sum_{p=0}^{\hat{p}}P_{p}t^{p},\quad\quad Q(t)=\sum_{q=0}^{\hat{q}}Q_{q}t^{q},

where
P_{p},Q_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
we have:

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=f\left(\frac{P(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\hat{p}}P_{p}f\left(\frac{t^{2p}}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)+\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\hat{q}}Q_{q}f\left(\frac{t^{2q+1}}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\eqref{vukmana}}}{=}\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\hat{p}}\frac{P_{p}t^{4p}}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}f\left(\frac{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}{t^{2p}}\right)+\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\hat{q}}\frac{Q_{q}t^{4q+2}}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}f\left(\frac{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}{t^{2q+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\hat{p}}\frac{P_{p}t^{4p}f(R(t^{2})t^{-2p}+S(t^{2})t^{-2p+1})}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}+\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\hat{q}}\frac{Q_{q}t^{4q+2}f(R(t^{2})t^{-2q-1}+S(t^{2})t^{-2q})}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\eqref{18}}}{=}\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\hat{p}}\frac{P_{p}t^{4p}(R(t^{2})t^{-2p}A+S(t^{2})t^{-2p}B)}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}+\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\hat{q}}\frac{Q_{q}t^{4q+2}(R(t^{2})t^{-2q-2}B+S(t^{2})t^{-2q}A)}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\hat{p}}\frac{P_{p}t^{2p}(R(t^{2})A+S(t^{2})B)}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}+\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\hat{q}}\frac{Q_{q}t^{2q}(R(t^{2})B+S(t^{2})t^{2}A)}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}

\displaystyle=\frac{P(t^{2})(R(t^{2})A+S(t^{2})B)}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}+\frac{Q(t^{2})(R(t^{2})B+S(t^{2})t^{2}A)}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{P(t^{2})R(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})S(t^{2})t^{2}}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}A+\frac{P(t^{2})S(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})R(t^{2})}{(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)^{2}}B

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{P(t)R(t)+Q(t)S(t)t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}A+\left(\frac{P(t)S(t)+Q(t)R(t)}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}B,

therefore:

(13)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}f\left(\frac{P(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)&=&\left(\frac{P(t)R(t)+Q(t)S(t)t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}A+\left(\frac{P(t)S(t)+Q(t)R(t)}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}B\end{array}

2) Existence:

It remains now to show that for any
A,B\in D
there exists a function
f:D\rightarrow D
such that (
13
) holds for any polynomials
P(t),Q(t),R(t),S(t)
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and to show that in fact
f
is additive,
f(1)=A
,
f(t)=B
and, if
g=f
, then
f
and
g
satisfy (
2
) for all
x\neq 0
. We will divide this task into steps.

2,1)
We will show that there exists a function
f:D\rightarrow D
satisfying (
13
) for any polynomials
P(t),Q(t),R(t),S(t)
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In other words, we will show that the function
f
as described in (
13
) is well defined.

Let
P(t),\hat{P}(t),Q(t),\hat{Q}(r),R(t),\hat{R}(t),S(t),\hat{S}(t)
be polynomials such that:

\frac{P(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}=\frac{\hat{P}(t^{2})+\hat{Q}(t^{2})t}{\hat{R}(t^{2})+\hat{S}(t^{2})t}

Then:

(P(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})t)(\hat{R}(t^{2})+\hat{S}(t^{2})t)=(\hat{P}(t^{2})+\hat{Q}(t^{2})t)(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)

Hence:

(P(t^{2})\hat{R}(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})\hat{S}(t^{2})t^{2})+(P(t^{2})\hat{S}(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})\hat{R}(t^{2}))t

=(\hat{P}(t^{2})R(t^{2})+\hat{Q}(t^{2})S(t^{2})t^{2})+(\hat{P}(t^{2})S(t^{2})+\hat{Q}(t^{2})R(t^{2}))t

Both sides of the above equality are polynomials over
t
, so we can compare their coefficients and obtain the following equalities:

(14)

P(t)\hat{R}(t)+Q(t)\hat{S}(t)t=\hat{P}(t)R(t)+\hat{Q}(t)S(t)t

(15)

P(t)\hat{S}(t)+Q(t)\hat{R}(t)=\hat{P}(t)S(t)+\hat{Q}(t)R(t)

Multiplying (
14
) by
R(t)\hat{R}(t)
and by
S(t)\hat{S}(t)t
, and multiplying (
15
) by
R(t)\hat{S}(t)t
and by
S(t)\hat{R}(t)t
, we obtain respectively:

P(t)R(t)\hat{R}(t)^{2}+Q(t)R(t)\hat{R}(t)\hat{S}(t)t=\hat{P}(t)\hat{R}(t)R(t)^{2}+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{R}(t)R(t)S(t)t

P(t)S(t)\hat{R}(t)\hat{S}(t)t+Q(t)S(t)\hat{S}(t)^{2}t^{2}=\hat{P}(t)\hat{S}(t)R(t)S(t)t+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{S}(t)S(t)^{2}t^{2}

P(t)R(t)\hat{S}(t)^{2}t+Q(t)R(t)\hat{R}(t)\hat{S}(t)t=\hat{P}(t)\hat{S}(t)R(t)S(t)t+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{S}(t)R(t)^{2}t

P(t)S(t)\hat{R}(t)\hat{S}(t)t+Q(t)S(t)\hat{R}(t)^{2}t=\hat{P}(t)\hat{R}(t)S(t)^{2}t+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{R}(t)R(t)S(t)t

By summing up the above four equations, and using
\mathrm{Char}(D)=2
and (
12
), we obtain:

(P(t)R(t)+Q(t)S(t)t)(\hat{R}(t^{2})+\hat{S}(t^{2})t)=(\hat{P}(t)\hat{R}(t)+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{S}(t)t)(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)

so that:

(16)

\frac{P(t)R(t)+Q(t)S(t)t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}=\frac{\hat{P}(t)\hat{R}(t)+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{S}(t)t}{\hat{R}(t^{2})+\hat{S}(t^{2})t}

Now, multiplying (
14
) by
S(t)\hat{R}(t)
and by
R(t)\hat{S}(t)
, and multiplying (
15
) by
S(t)\hat{S}(t)t
and by
R(t)\hat{R}(t)
, we obtain respectively:

P(t)S(t)\hat{R}(t)^{2}+Q(t)S(t)\hat{R}(t)\hat{S}(t)t=\hat{P}(t)\hat{R}(t)R(t)S(t)+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{R}(t)S(t)^{2}t

P(t)R(t)\hat{R}(t)\hat{S}(t)+Q(t)R(t)\hat{S}(t)^{2}t=\hat{P}(t)\hat{S}(t)R(t)^{2}+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{S}(t)R(t)S(t)t

P(t)S(t)\hat{S}(t)^{2}t+Q(t)S(t)\hat{R}(t)\hat{S}(t)t=\hat{P}(t)\hat{S}(t)S(t)^{2}t+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{S}(t)R(t)S(t)t

P(t)R(t)\hat{R}(t)\hat{S}(t)+Q(t)R(t)\hat{R}(t)^{2}=\hat{P}(t)\hat{R}(t)R(t)S(t)+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{R}(t)R(t)^{2}

By adding the above four equations, and using
\mathrm{Char}(D)=2
and (
12
), we have:

(P(t)S(t)+Q(t)R(t))(\hat{R}(t^{2})+\hat{S}(t^{2})t)=(\hat{P}(t)\hat{S}(t)+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{R}(t))(R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t)

so that:

(17)

\frac{P(t)S(t)+Q(t)R(t)}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}=\frac{\hat{P}(t)\hat{S}(t)+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{R}(t)}{\hat{R}(t^{2})+\hat{S}(t^{2})t}

We now square both sides of (
16
) and multiply by
A
, square both sides of (
17
) and multiply by
B
, and add the equations thus obtained, to obtain:

\left(\frac{P(t)R(t)+Q(t)S(t)t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}A+\left(\frac{P(t)S(t)+Q(t)R(t)}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}B

=\left(\frac{\hat{P}(t)\hat{R}(t)+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{S}(t)t}{\hat{R}(t^{2})+\hat{S}(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}A+\left(\frac{\hat{P}(t)\hat{S}(t)+\hat{Q}(t)\hat{R}(t)}{\hat{R}(t^{2})+\hat{S}(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}B

2,2)
Let us show that
f
is additive. Let
x,y\in D
, then, by bringing the fractions to a common denominator, we can have polynomials
P(t),\hat{P}(t),Q(t),\hat{Q}(t),R(t),S(t)
such that:

x=\frac{P(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t},\quad\quad y=\frac{\hat{P}(t^{2})+\hat{Q}(t^{2})t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t},

thus:

x+y=\frac{(P(t^{2})+\hat{P}(t^{2}))+(Q(t^{2})+\hat{Q}(t^{2}))t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}

therefore, using the fact that
x\mapsto x^{2}
is additive in fields of characteristic
2
:

\displaystyle f(x+y)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{17}}{=}\left(\frac{(P(t)+\hat{P}(t))R(t)+(Q(t)+\hat{Q}(t))S(t)t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}A

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{(P(t)+\hat{P}(t))S(t)+(Q(t)+\hat{Q}(t))R(t)}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}B

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{P(t)R(t)+Q(t)S(t)t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}A+\left(\frac{P(t)S(t)+Q(t)R(t)}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}B

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\hat{P}(t)R(t)+\hat{Q}(t)S(t)t}{\hat{R}(t^{2})+\hat{S}(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}A+\left(\frac{\hat{P}(t)S(t)+\hat{Q}(t)R(t)}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}B

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{17}}{=}f(x)+f(y).

2,3)
Let us show that
f(1)=A
and
f(t)=B
. Indeed, we have:

1=\frac{1+0t}{1+0t}

so that:

f(1)=\left(\frac{1\cdot 1+0\cdot 0t}{1+0t}\right)^{2}A+\left(\frac{1\cdot 0+0\cdot 1}{1+0t}\right)^{2}B=A

Also, we have:

t=\frac{0+1t}{1+0t}

thereby:

f(t)=\left(\frac{0\cdot 1+1\cdot 0t}{1+0t}\right)^{2}A+\left(\frac{0\cdot 0+1\cdot 1}{1+0t}\right)^{2}B=B

2,4)
Let us show that, if
g=f
, then
(f,g)
satisfy (
2
) for all
x\neq 0
. Let
x\neq 0
, then we have polynomials
P(t),Q(t),R(t),S(t)
such that:

x=\frac{P(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t},

hence:

x^{-1}=\frac{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}{P(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})t},

therefore:

\displaystyle x^{2}f(x^{-1})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{17}}{=}\left(\frac{P(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}\left(\left(\frac{R(t)P(t)+S(t)Q(t)t}{P(t^{2})+Q(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}A+\left(\frac{R(t)Q(t)+S(t)P(t)}{P(t^{2})+R(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}B\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{P(t)R(t)+Q(t)S(t)t}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}A+\left(\frac{P(t)S(t)+Q(t)R(t)}{R(t^{2})+S(t^{2})t}\right)^{2}B

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{17}}{=}f(x),

so that, by
\mathrm{Char}(D)=2
, we obtain:

f(x)+x^{2}f(x^{-1})=0.

Therefore, if
g=f
, then
(f,g)
satisties (
2
) for all
x\neq 0
.

Conclusion
:
Thus, given any
A,B\in D
, we have proven both the uniqueness and existence of the additive functions
f,g:D\rightarrow D
satisfying (
2
) for every
x\neq 0
. Therefore, the theorem is established.
∎

Split Octonion Algebras

In this section, we delve into investigating Conjecture
1.1
, which questions whether the outcome of Theorem 3.1 extends to split octonion algebras. However, before delving into this inquiry, let’s revisit some key points, including definitions and crucial results, concerning this particular class of algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

A
composition algebra
is an
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
\mathcal{C}
equipped with a nondegenerate quadratic form
N:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
over
\mathbb{F}
that satisfies
N(xy)=N(x)N(y)
for any
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
. The function
N
is referred to as the
norm
of
\mathcal{C}
.

An unital composition algebra is also known as a
Hurwitz algebra
. It’s important to differentiate between Hurwitz algebras that have every element invertible and those that do not. If a composition algebra contains non-zero isotropic elements (non-invertible), or equivalently, if it possesses a non-zero element with zero norm, it is termed
split
. Conversely, a composition algebra without such elements is termed
non-split
. Furthermore, it’s worth noting that a Hurwitz algebra is split if and only if it is not a division algebra. An important subsequent result concerning composition algebras can be found on page 19 in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

There exists a unique (up to isomorphism) split Hurwitz algebra over
\mathbb{F}
for each dimension
2
,
4
, and
8
.

4.1

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

be a split octonion algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
, and suppose

are additive maps satisfying the identity (

f(x)+x^{2}g(x^{-1})=0

for all
x\in D^{\times}
. Then there exists a fixed element
q\in D
such that
f(x)=xq
and
g(x)=-xq
for all
x\in D
.

We will divide it into some cases. First we consider the case where
\mathrm{Char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
.

Proof.

Let
h(x)=f(x)-xf(1)
. Then
h
is additive,
h(1)=0
and, by Step 1 of the proof of Theorem
3.1
, for all
x\in D
such that
x\in D^{\times}
and
1-x\in D^{\times}
we have:

(18)

h(x^{2})=2xh(x).

Now we divide it into some steps.

Step 1:
We will prove that
h(\alpha e_{i})=0
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
and
i\in\{\pm 1,\pm\omega,\pm\bar{\omega}\}
. Indeed, let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
,
i\in\{\pm 1,\pm\omega,\pm\bar{\omega}\}
and:

x=e_{0}-e_{-0}+e_{i\omega}-e_{-i\omega}+\alpha e_{i}.

Then:

1-x=2e_{-0}-e_{i\omega}+e_{-i\omega}-\alpha e_{i}.

Thus
T(x)=0
,
N(x)=-2
and
N(1-x)=-1
, so, by
\mathrm{Char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
, (
18
) holds. But:

x^{2}=T(x)x-N(x)=2.

Hence by (
18
) we have
h(2)=2xh(x)
. But
h(1)=0
,
\mathrm{Char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
and
x\in D^{\times}
, so
h(x)=0
, in other words:

(19)

h(e_{0}-e_{-0}+e_{i\omega}-e_{-i\omega}+\alpha e_{i})=0.

The above equation holds for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
, in particular:

(20)

h(e_{0}-e_{-0}+e_{i\omega}-e_{-i\omega})=0.

Therefore, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
, we have (
19
) and (
20
), so we conclude that
h(\alpha e_{i})=0
.

Step 2:
We will prove that
h(e_{i})=0
for
i\in\{\pm 0\}
. Indeed, by (
20
) and Step 1 we obtain
h(e_{0})-h(e_{-0})=0
. Also
h(1)=0
, so we have
h(e_{0})+h(e_{-0})=0
. Because
\mathrm{Char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
, we obtain
h(e_{0})=h(e_{-0})=0
.

Step 3:
We will prove that
h(\alpha)=0
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
. Indeed
h(0)=0
, and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}

Thus

, so (
18
) holds. But:

x^{2}=T(x)x-N(x)=x-\alpha.

Hence, by (
18
), we have
h(x-\alpha)=2xh(x)
, but by Steps 1 and 2 we have
h(x)=0
, so we obtain
h(\alpha)=0
.

Step 4:
We will prove that
h(\alpha e_{i})=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
and
i\in\{\pm 0\}
. Indeed let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
and:

x=\alpha e_{0}-\alpha e_{-0}+(\alpha^{2}+1)e_{1}+e_{-1}.

Then:

1-x=-(\alpha-1)e_{0}+(\alpha+1)e_{-0}-(\alpha^{2}+1)e_{1}-e_{1}.

Thus
T(x)=0
,
N(x)=1
and
N(1-x)=2
, so, by
\mathrm{Char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
, (
18
) holds. But:

x^{2}=T(x)x-N(x)=-1.

Hence, by (
18

h(-1)=2xh(x)

h(1)=0
,
\mathrm{Char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
and
x\in D^{\times}
, so
h(x)=0
. Thus by Step 1 we obtain
h(\alpha e_{0})-h(\alpha e_{-0})=0
. Also by Step 3 we have
h(\alpha e_{0})+h(\alpha e_{-0})=0
. Because
\mathrm{Char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
, we obtain
h(\alpha e_{0})=h(\alpha e_{-0})=0

We will prove that
f(x)=xf(1)
for all

. Indeed, for every element

is of the form

for all

Proof.

h(e_{0}+e_{i\omega}+e_{-i\omega}+\alpha e_{i})=0.

h(\alpha e_{i})=0

Step 2:
We will prove that
h(e_{i})=0

Step 3:

Hence, by (

) we have

, but by Steps 1 and 2, we have

, so we obtain

.

Step 4:
For every
x\in D^{\times}
, then by (
21
) with
x^{-1}
in place of

we have:

h(x^{-1})+x^{-2}k(x)=0,

so we obtain:

This is (
21
) with

and

that are analogous to Steps 1 to 3.

Step 5:
We will prove that
h(\alpha e_{i})=k(\alpha e_{i})=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
and
i\in\{\pm 0\}
. By Steps 2 and 4, we have

and

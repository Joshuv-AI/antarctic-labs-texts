Complexity in Bolza Surface
Bhola Nath Saha, Bidyut Sanki

Abstract
. A surface in the Teichmüller space, where the systole function attains its maximum, is called a maximal surface. For genus two there exists a unique maximal surface which is called the Bolza surface. In this article, we study the complexity of the set of systolic geodesics on the Bolza surface. We show that any non-systolic geodesic intersects the systolic geodesics in 2 ​ n 2n points, where n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 . Furthermore, we show that there are 12 12 second systolic geodesics on the Bolza surface and they form a triangulation of the surface.



1.
Introduction

A Riemann surface of constant curvature
-1
is called a
hyperbolic surface
. A compact surface with empty boundary is called a
closed surface
. In this article, by a surface we always mean a closed hyperbolic surface. For
g\geq 2
, let
\mathcal{T}_{g}
denote the Teichmüller space of hyperbolic surfaces of genus
g
(see Section 10.1
[
4
]
). A shortest essential closed geodesic on a surface
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
is called a
systolic geodesic
of
X
. The function
\mathrm{sys}:\mathcal{T}_{g}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where
\mathrm{sys}(X)
is the length of a systolic geodesic of
X
(see Section 1
[
9
]
), is called the systole function on
\mathcal{T}_{g}
and
\mathrm{sys}(X)
is called the systole of
X
.

The function
\mathrm{sys}
has been studied extensively by P. Schmutz
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
H. Akrout
[
1
]
and others. In Theorem A
[
9
]
, Schmutz has shown that
\mathrm{sys}
is a topological Morse function and studied the properties of its critical points. It is an interesting and difficult problem to find the critical points of the systole function explicitly. These are the surfaces where
\mathrm{sys}
attains its local maximum. For
g=2
, there is a unique surface (up to isometry), namely the Bolza surface in
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, where the systole function attains a global maximum (see Section 5
[
10
]
). The Bolza surface can be obtained by identifying the opposite sides of a regular hyperbolic octagon with interior angle
\frac{\pi}{4}
(Secttion 5
[
10
]
). It has
12
systolic geodesics and this is the genus two hyperbolic surface with the biggest automorphism group. In Theorem 3.1
[
8
]
, Sanki has given an explicit description of the systolic geodesics on the Bolza surface and shown that the set of all systolic geodesics forms a triangulation of the surface. Furthermore, he has studied the complexity of the set of systolic geodesics to some extent.

The
k
-th complexity of a given system of closed geodesics on a surface is the number of simple closed geodesics which intersect the collection exactly at
k
many points. In this article, we further extend the study of complexity of the systolic geodesics of the Bolza surface. We show that the
k
-th complexity of systollic geodesics is zero when either
k<10
or
k
is an odd integer. Furthermore, for any
k\geq 10
even, the
k
-th complexity is finite (see Theorem
3.1
).

The set
\mathrm{Spec(X)}
of lengths of all simple closed geodesics on a surface
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
is called the
length spectrum
of
X
. The length spectrum of a surface is a discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(see Lemma 12.4
[
4
]
) and hence its elements can be arranged in an ascending order

\mathrm{Spec(X)}=\{l_{i}\mid l_{i+1}>l_{i},\text{ for all }i\in\mathbf{N}\}.

The
i
-th element

of
\mathrm{Spec}(X)
is called the
i
-th systole of
X
. A simple closed geodesic of length
l_{i}
is called
i
-th systolic geodesic. Note that,
l_{1}=\mathrm{sys}(X)
. It is a very difficult problem to find the length spectrum of a given hyperbolic surface, in general. The length spectrum of the Bolza surface has been extensively studied by R. Aurich, F. Steiner and E. B. Bogomolny (see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
). In particular, the authors have evaluated
i
-th systole on the Bolza surface, for certain values of
i
. In Section 2 of
[
3
]
, Aurich-Steiner have shown that the length of the second systolic geodesic in the Bolza surface is
2\cosh^{-1}{\left(3+2\sqrt{2}\right)}
. In this article, we show that there are only
12
second systolic geodesics on the Bolza surface and they form a triangulation of the surface (see Theorem
4.1
).

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a curve system on a hyperbolic surface

. Then the

-th complexity
\mathcal{T}_{k}(\Gamma)
is finite if and only if
\Gamma
is a filling.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose,
\Gamma
is a filling system in
X
. Let

, where

is a Fuchsian group and

is the upper half plane. Let

be a polygonal fundamental domain for

determined by the lifts of
\Gamma

\delta
be a simple closed curve on

be a path lift of

(

\Gamma

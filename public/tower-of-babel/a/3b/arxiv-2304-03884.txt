Rayleigh quotients of Dillon’s functions
Aditi Kar Gangopadhyay, Mansi, Bimal Mandal, Aleksandr Kutsenko, Sugata Gangopadhyay

Abstract
The Walsh–Hadamard spectrum of a bent function uniquely determines a dual function. The dual of a bent function is also bent. A bent function that is equal to its dual is called a self-dual function. The Hamming distance between a bent function and its dual is related to its Rayleigh quotient. Carlet, Danielsen, Parker, and Solé [ 2 ] studied Rayleigh quotients of bent functions in 𝒫 ​ S a ​ p {\mathcal{P}S}_{ap} , and obtained an expression in terms of a character sum [ 6 ] . We use another approach comprising of Desarguesian spreads to obtain the complete spectrum of Rayleigh quotients of bent functions in 𝒫 ​ 𝒮 a ​ p \mathcal{PS}_{ap} .

1.1
Bent functions, duality, and Rayleigh quotients

Any Boolean function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
satisfies Parseval’s identity
\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}|W_{f}({\bf u})|^{2}=2^{2n}
. In other words, the average of the squares of the Walsh–Hadamard coefficients is
2^{n}
. Since the maximum value in a data set of non–negative integers is always greater than or equal to the average, i.e.,
\max_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}|W_{f}({\bf u})|\geq 2^{\frac{n}{2}}
. Thus, nonlinearity of any Boolean function is bounded above by
2^{n-1}-2^{\frac{n}{2}-1}
, i.e., for any
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}

nl_{f}\leq 2^{n-1}-2^{\frac{n}{2}-1}.

When
n
is even, this bound is attainable by a class of functions which are called bent Boolean functions.

Definition 2

Let
n
be a positive even integer. A Boolean function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
that has nonlinearity

nl_{f}=2^{n-1}-2^{\frac{n}{2}-1}
is said to be bent.

Equivalently, a Boolean function in
n
variables is called bent if and only if

|W_{f}({\bf u})|=2^{\frac{n}{2}}
, for all
{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
. Thus, bent functions are defined over an even number of variables and its Walsh–Hadamard spectrum consists of two values only.

Definition 3

If
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
is a bent function, then there exists a unique function
\widetilde{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
such that

W_{f}({\bf u})=(-1)^{\widetilde{f}({\bf u})}2^{\frac{n}{2}},\mbox{ for all }{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}.

The function
\widetilde{f}
is said to be the dual of
f
, and it is also a bent function in
n
variables.

In particular, if
f=\widetilde{f}
then
f
is called self-dual, and if
f=\widetilde{f}\oplus 1
, i.e.,
\widetilde{f}
is complement of
f
then
f
is called anti-self-dual.
Rothaus
[
13
]
first constructed a class of bent functions, called Maiorana–McFarland bent functions. Let
n=2k
. A function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{k}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{k}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
is in Maiorana–McFarland bent class if

f({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf x}\cdot\pi({\bf y})\oplus g({\bf y}),

for all
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{k}
, where
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{k}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{k}
is permutation and
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{k}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
is any Boolean function in
k
variables. The dual of Maiorana–McFarland function
f
is also of the same type given as:

\widetilde{f}({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf x}\cdot\pi^{-1}({\bf y})\oplus g(\pi^{-1}({\bf y})),

for all
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{k}
.
Dillon
[
4
,
5
]
constructed another class of bent functions, called partial spread (
\mathcal{PS}
), whose support is the union of elements of partial spreads of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
. Further, Dillon constructed a subclass of partial spread functions consisting of elements from a spread. We call these functions Dillon’s functions
[
6
]
.

Definition 4

Let
n=2k
be an even integer. A partial spread of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
consists of pairwise supplementary
k
-dimensional subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
. A partial spread is a full spread if the union of its elements equals
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
.

The
\mathcal{PS}
class is divided into two disjoint class of functions, called
\mathcal{PS^{-}}
and
\mathcal{PS^{+}}
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}^{-}\cup{\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}^{+}
. We first define the indicator function of a set
S
as

\phi_{S}({\bf x})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if }{\bf x}\in S;\\
0&\mbox{ if }{\bf x}\notin S.\end{array}\right.

Let
U
and
V
be two subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
. We refer to
U
and
V
as “disjoint” if
U\cap V=\{{\bf 0}\}
, where
{\bf 0}
is the all-zero vector of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
. Let
E_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,2^{k-1}
are mutually “disjoint”
k
-dimensional subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
. A bent function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
is said to be in
{\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}^{-}
class if it is
of the form

f({\bf x})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2^{k-1}}\phi_{E_{i}}({\bf x})-2^{k-1}\phi_{\{{\bf 0}\}}({\bf x}).

The dual of
f\in{\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}^{-}
is of the form

\widetilde{f}({\bf x})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2^{k-1}}\phi_{E_{i}^{\perp}}({\bf x})-2^{k-1}\phi_{\{{\bf 0}\}}({\bf x})
.
A bent function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
is said to be in
{\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}^{+}
class if it is
of the form

f({\bf x})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2^{k-1}+1}\phi_{E_{i}}({\bf x})-2^{k-1}\phi_{\{{\bf 0}\}}({\bf x}),

where
E_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,2^{k-1}+1
are mutually “disjoint”
k
-dimensional subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
. The dual of
f\in{\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}^{+}
is of the form

\widetilde{f}({\bf x})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2^{k-1}+1}\phi_{E_{i}^{\perp}}({\bf x})-2^{k-1}\phi_{\{{\bf 0}\}}({\bf x}).

The distance between a bent function and its dual is an interesting research problem. Danielsen et al.
[
6
]
introduced the normalized Rayleigh quotient of a bent Boolean function that measures the distance of the bent function to its dual.

Definition 5

The Rayleigh quotient
S_{f}
of a Boolean function
f
in

n
variables is

S_{f}=\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x},{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf x})\oplus f({\bf y})\oplus{\bf x}\cdot{\bf y}}=\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf x})}W_{f}({\bf x}).

For bent functions, we use the normalized version of the Rayleigh quotient. The normalized Rayleigh quotient
N_{f}
of a bent function
f
in
n
variables is

N_{f}=\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf x})\oplus\widetilde{f}({\bf x})}=2^{-\frac{n}{2}}S_{f}.

For an
n
-variable bent Boolean function
f
,
N_{f}=2^{n}
is
f
is self-dual, and
N_{f}=-2^{n}
if
f
is anti-self-dual.
The Hamming distance between a bent function
f
and its dual
\widetilde{f}
is

\displaystyle{\rm dist}(f,\widetilde{f})=|\{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f({\bf x})\neq\widetilde{f}({\bf x})\}|

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{N_{f}}{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{ if }f\mbox{ is self-dual}\\
2^{n}&\mbox{ if }f\mbox{ is anti self-dual}\end{array}\right.

Now, we are going to discuss some results related to Rayleigh’s quotients of symmetric bent functions.

1.2
Distribution of Rayleigh quotients of symmetric bent functions

A Boolean function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
is called a symmetric Boolean function if its output remains the same for any permutation of its inputs. In 1994, Savický
[
9
]
derived the following characterization for symmetric bent functions.

Theorem 1.1

[
9
, Theorem 3.3]

If
f({\bf x})=c_{k}
,
{\rm wt}({\bf x})=k
, is a symmetric function of an even number of variable
n
, then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

The function
f
is a bent function;

2.

For all
k=0,1,\dots,n-2
, the identity
c_{k+2}=c_{k}\oplus 1
is satisfied;

3.

There exist
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
,
f({\bf x})=\oplus_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x_{i}x_{j}\oplus\varepsilon_{1}(\oplus_{i=1}^{n}x_{i})\oplus\varepsilon_{2}
, for all
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
.

We first derive the dual of symmetric bent functions explicitly. Then the possible Rayleigh quotient of symmetric bent functions are calculated. It is observed that the dual of a symmetric bent function is either self-dual or anti-self-dual or exactly half of the output of dual function is the same as the output of the function and the other half is different. These cases are depended on the number of variables and the coefficient related to the constant and linear terms. The results are given in the following Propositions.

Proposition 1

Let
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
be a symmetric bent function then the dual of
f
is

\displaystyle\tilde{f}({\bf x})=\begin{cases}{\rm wt}({\bf x})\oplus c_{{\rm wt}({\bf x})}\oplus\frac{n}{4}&\text{if $\frac{n}{2}$ is even}\\
&\\
\begin{cases}{\rm wt}({\bf x})\oplus c_{{\rm wt}({\bf x})+1}\oplus\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor&\text{if ${\rm wt}({\bf x})\leq n-1$}\\
n\oplus 1\oplus c_{1}\oplus\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor&\text{if ${\rm wt}({\bf x})=n$}\end{cases}&\text{if $\frac{n}{2}$ is odd}\end{cases}.

Proposition 2

Let
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
be a symmetric bent Boolean function, then the Rayleigh quotient of
f
is

N_{f}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\frac{n}{2}$ is even}\\
&\\
\begin{cases}2^{n}&\begin{cases}\text{either $\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor$ is even and $c_{0}=c_{1}$}\\
\text{or $\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor$ is odd and $c_{0}=c_{1}\oplus 1$}\end{cases}\\
&\\
-2^{n}&\begin{cases}\text{either $\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor$ is odd and $c_{0}=c_{1}$}\\
\text{or $\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor$ is even and $c_{0}=c_{1}+1$}\end{cases}\end{cases}&\text{if $\frac{n}{2}$ is odd}\end{cases},

where
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
are the output value of function for input of weight
0
and
1
, respectively, and
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
is the floor function.

We refer
[
12
,
9
]
to get details of these propositions. From above, we have a complete spectrum of Rayleigh quotients for symmetric bent Boolean functions. By ease, without doing many calculations, if we are given a symmetric bent Boolean function we can find out its Rayleigh quotient. But it is not the case with Dillon’s functions. The method of finding the Rayleigh quotient of Dillon’s function is given in
[
2
]
. We are interested in finding the possible distance between Dillon’s functions and their duals using another approach. So, we partition the bent functions into distinct sets based on the distance between them and their duals. At this point, it is worth noting that Kolomeec
[
7
]
proved that the minimum Hamming distance between two bent functions is
2^{\frac{n}{2}}
. Therefore, the largest possible Hamming distance between two bent functions is
2^{n}-2^{\frac{n}{2}}
, except for anti-self-dual bent functions.

2
Properties of bent functions and their duals

In this section, we study some metrical properties of bent functions and their duals. These expressions can help us characterise the distance between a bent function and its dual. Let us denote
E_{e}=\{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\rm wt}({\bf x})\mbox{ is even}\}
and
E_{o}=\{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\rm wt}({\bf x})\mbox{ is odd}\}
. The restricted Walsh–Hadamard transform over
A\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
of a Boolean function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
at
{\bf a}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
is denoted by
W_{f}({\bf a})|_{A}
, defined as
W_{f}({\bf a})|_{A}=\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in A}(-1)^{f({\bf x})\oplus{\bf a}\cdot{\bf x}}
.

Proposition 3

Let
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
, where
n=2k\geq 4
, be a bent function and its dual

\widetilde{f}
. The distance between
f
and
\widetilde{f}
can be written as

\begin{split}{\rm dist}(f,\widetilde{f})&=2^{n-1}-2^{k-1}(-1)^{f({\bf 0})}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\rm supp}(f)}W_{f}({\bf u})\\
&=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}W_{D_{{\bf u}}f}({\bf u})+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}W_{D_{{\bf u}}f}({\bf u})|_{E_{o}}.\end{split}

Proof

Let
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
be a bent function,
n=2k\geq 4
, and
W_{f}({\bf u})=2^{k}(-1)^{\widetilde{f}({\bf u})}
, for all
{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
. The Hamming distance between
f
and
\widetilde{f}
is

\displaystyle{\rm dist}(f,\widetilde{f})

\displaystyle=|\{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f({\bf x})\neq\widetilde{f}({\bf x})\}|=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf x})\oplus\widetilde{f}({\bf x})}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(1-2f({\bf x}))(-1)^{\widetilde{f}({\bf x})}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{\widetilde{f}({\bf x})}+\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}f({\bf x})(-1)^{\widetilde{f}({\bf x})}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}W_{f}({\bf x})+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\rm supp}(f)}W_{f}({\bf x})

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf y})\oplus{\bf y}\cdot{\bf x}}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\rm supp}(f)}W_{f}({\bf x})

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf y})}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{{\bf y}\cdot{\bf x}}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\rm supp}(f)}W_{f}({\bf x})

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-2^{k-1}(-1)^{f({\bf 0})}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\rm supp}(f)}W_{f}({\bf x}),

and

\displaystyle{\rm dist}(f,\widetilde{f})

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf x})\oplus\widetilde{f}({\bf x})}=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf x})}W_{f}({\bf x})

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf x})\oplus f({\bf y})\oplus{\bf y}\cdot{\bf x}}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf x})\oplus f({\bf y})}(-1)^{{\bf y}\cdot{\bf x}}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf x})\oplus f({\bf x}\oplus{\bf u})}(-1)^{({\bf x}\oplus{\bf u})\cdot{\bf x}}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{D_{{\bf u}}f({\bf x})}(-1)^{{\rm wt}({\bf x})}(-1)^{{\bf u}\cdot{\bf x}}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}\bigg\{\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in E_{e}}(-1)^{D_{{\bf u}}f({\bf x})\oplus{\bf u}\cdot{\bf x}}-\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in E_{o}}(-1)^{D_{{\bf u}}f({\bf x})\oplus{\bf u}\cdot{\bf x}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}\bigg\{\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{D_{{\bf u}}f({\bf x})\oplus{\bf u}\cdot{\bf x}}-2\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in E_{o}}(-1)^{D_{{\bf u}}f({\bf x})\oplus{\bf u}\cdot{\bf x}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}W_{D_{{\bf u}}f}({\bf u})+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}W_{D_{{\bf u}}f}({\bf u})|_{E_{o}}.

Corollary 1

For any bent function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
where
n\geq 4

is a positive even integer, we have

2\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\rm supp}(f)}W_{f}({\bf u})+\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}W_{D_{{\bf u}}f}({\bf u})-2\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}W_{D_{{\bf u}}f}({\bf u})|_{E_{o}}-2^{n}(-1)^{f({\bf 0})}=0.

The set of bent functions can be partitioned using the distance between bent functions and their duals. Let us define the equivalence classes of bent functions.

Definition 6

Two bent functions
f,g\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
are called distance equivalent if
{\rm dist}(f,\widetilde{f})={\rm dist}(g,\widetilde{g})
, and are denoted by
f\sim g
.

For any bent functions
f,g,h\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
,
f\sim f
, and if
f\sim g
then
g\sim f
, and if
f\sim g
and

then

. Thus, “
\sim
” is an equivalence relation on the set of bent functions. Let us define equivalence classes of bent functions corresponding to this equivalence relation as

cl(i)=\{f\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f\mbox{ is bent and }{\rm dist}(f,\widetilde{f})=i\},\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq 2^{n}.

Equivalence classes of self-dual and anti-self-dual functions are
cl(0)
and
cl(2^{n})
, respectively. Except for the anti-self-dual function,
{\rm dist}(f,\widetilde{f})\leq 2^{n}-2^{\frac{n}{2}}
. For every
0\leq i\neq j\leq 2^{n},cl(i)\cap cl(j)=\emptyset
. Thus, the set of all bent functions in
n
variables is
\cup_{i=0}^{2^{n}}cl(i)
. Here, the two obvious questions arise, as below:

•

Are the bent functions of the same class, i.e.,
cl(i)
, for a fixed
0\leq i\leq 2^{n}
, affine equivalent? Or is it possible to find a non–singular affine transformation such that both bent functions belong to the same class?

•

What are the possible distances for a particular class of bent functions?

We are going to address both questions in this paper. Now, we prove that these distance classes are, in general, not affine invariant. In the following section, we determine the specifics of potential classes of a class of bent functions.

Let
f
be a bent function in
n=2k
variables and
g({\bf x})=f({\bf x}A\oplus{\bf b})
, for all
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
, where
A
is a non–singular binary matrix of order
n
and
{\bf b}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
. Then
g({\bf 0})=f({\bf b})
,
{\rm supp}(g)={\rm supp}(f)B\oplus{\bf b}B=\{{\bf x}B\oplus{\bf b}B\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f({\bf x})=1\}
, where
B=A^{-1}
, and for any
{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
,

\begin{split}W_{g}({\bf u})&=\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf x}A\oplus{\bf b})\oplus{\bf u}\cdot{\bf x}}=\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf y})\oplus{\bf u}\cdot({\bf y}B\oplus{\bf b}B)}\\
&=(-1)^{{\bf u}B^{T}\cdot{\bf b}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f({\bf y})\oplus{\bf u}B^{T}\cdot{\bf y}}=(-1)^{{\bf u}B^{T}\cdot{\bf b}}W_{f}({\bf u}B^{T}).\end{split}

Thus,

\begin{split}{\rm dist}(g,\widetilde{g})&=2^{n-1}-2^{k-1}(-1)^{g(0)}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\rm supp}(g)}W_{g}({\bf u})\\
&=2^{n-1}-2^{k-1}(-1)^{f({\bf b})}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}\in{\rm supp}(f)B+{\bf b}B}(-1)^{{\bf u}B^{T}\cdot{\bf b}}W_{f}({\bf u}B^{T})\\
&=2^{n-1}-2^{k-1}(-1)^{f({\bf b})}\\
&+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}^{\prime}\in{\rm supp}(f)}(-1)^{({\bf u}^{\prime}\oplus{\bf b})BB^{T}\cdot{\bf b}}W_{f}(({\bf u}^{\prime}\oplus{\bf b})BB^{T})\\
&\neq{\rm dist}(f,\widetilde{f}),\text{ in general}.\end{split}

Let
A
be an orthogonal binary matrix. Then

{\rm dist}(g,\widetilde{g})=2^{n-1}-2^{k-1}(-1)^{f({\bf b})}+\frac{(-1)^{{\rm wt}({\bf b})}}{2^{k}}\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf u}^{\prime}\in{\rm supp}(f)}(-1)^{{\bf u}^{\prime}\cdot b}W_{f}({\bf u}^{\prime}\oplus{\bf b}).

In particular if

. We get the next results directly from the above expressions.

Let

Lemma 1

For any
E\in\mathfrak{D}
, its dual
E^{\perp}\in\mathfrak{D}
.

Proof

First, consider the case when
E=E_{a}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}^{*}
.
Let
(y,z)\in E_{a}^{\perp}
, so that

\braket{(y,z),(x,xa)}=0,

for all
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}
. That is

{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(yx)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(zxa)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}((y+za)x)=0,

for all
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}
.

This implies
y+za=0
, that is,

z=ya^{-1}
. So

E_{a}^{\perp}=\{(y,ya^{-1})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}\}=E_{a^{-1}}\in\mathfrak{D}.

We complete the proof by observing that
E_{0},E_{\infty}\in\mathfrak{D}
are mutually orthogonal.

The direct count for self-dual
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
is given in
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]
. But our next theorem characterizes self-dual
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent functions along with their count. Our approach to this problem lets us count the total number of self-dual
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
functions as well as obtain the complete distribution of the Rayleigh quotients for functions in
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
.

Theorem 3.5

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be the Desarguesian spread defined in

). A
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
class of bent function

f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
defined in (
4
)
is self-dual if and only if
the following conditions are satisfied

1.

E_{1}\notin\mathfrak{C}
;

2.

\{E_{0},E_{\infty}\}\cap\mathfrak{C}\in\{\emptyset,\{E_{0},E_{\infty}\}\}
;

3.

For all
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
,
E_{a}\in\mathfrak{C}
if
and only if
E_{a^{-1}}\in\mathfrak{C}
.

The number of self-dual
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
-bent functions is
{2^{k-1}-1}\choose 2^{k-2}
.

Proof

The dual of
f
defined in (
4
) is

\widetilde{f}({\bf x})=\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathfrak{C}}\phi_{E^{\perp}}({\bf x})-2^{k-1}{\bf 1}_{\{{\bf 0}\}}({\bf x}).

Let
\mathfrak{I}=\{E_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}\cup\{\infty\},a\neq 1\}
. For
f
to be a self-dual bent function

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathfrak{C}\cap\mathfrak{I}\right\rvert}
is even. This is because

E_{a}\in\mathfrak{C}
if and only if
E_{a^{-1}}\in\mathfrak{C}
,
where
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
, and

E_{0}\in\mathfrak{C}
if and only if
E_{\infty}\in\mathfrak{C}
.
So the subspaces in
\mathfrak{I}
appear or do not appear in pairs. If
E_{1}\in\mathfrak{C}
, then
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathfrak{I}\right\rvert}=2^{k-1}-1
. This is not possible.

If
f
is self-dual either both

E_{0},E_{\infty}\in\mathfrak{C}
or

E_{0},E_{\infty}\notin\mathfrak{C}
. Further, for all
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
,
E_{a}\in\mathfrak{C}
if
and only if
E_{a^{-1}}\in\mathfrak{C}
.
Therefore, the three conditions are satisfied. It is not difficult to check that these conditions are sufficient for self-duality.

If
\{E_{0},E_{\infty}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{C}
, then the remaining subspaces appear in pairs from the set

\mathfrak{D}\setminus\{E_{0},E_{1},E_{\infty}\}
.
The number of elements
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathfrak{D}\setminus\{E_{0},E_{1},E_{\infty}\}\right\rvert}=2^{k}-2
. Therefore, the number of subspace pairs
that may be selected from

\mathfrak{D}\setminus\{E_{0},E_{1},E_{\infty}\}
is

2^{k-1}-1
. To construct
\mathfrak{C}

we need
2^{k-2}-1
pair of subspaces, since

\{E_{0},E_{\infty}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{C}
.
This can be done in
{2^{k-1}-1}\choose{2^{k-2}-1}

ways.

In case
\{E_{0},E_{\infty}\}\cap\mathfrak{C}=\{\emptyset\}
,
we have to choose all
2^{k-2}
pairs in
\mathfrak{C}
from

\mathfrak{D}\setminus\{E_{0},E_{1},E_{\infty}\}
. This can be
done in
{2^{k-1}-1}\choose{2^{k-2}}
. Thus, the total number of
self-dual
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
-bent functions is

{{2^{k-1}-1}\choose{2^{k-2}-1}}+{{2^{k-1}-1}\choose{2^{k-2}}}={{2^{k-1}}\choose{2^{k-2}}}

We can calculate the distribution of the distance between a
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent function and it’s dual when we consider the Desarguesian spread in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}
defined in (
3
).
Let
\mathfrak{C}^{\perp}=\{E^{\perp}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\in\mathfrak{C}\}
where
\mathfrak{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{D}

Theorem 3.6

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be the Desarguesian spread defined in (
3
).
A
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
defined in (
4
) with its dual
\widetilde{f}
of the form
\widetilde{f}({\bf x})=\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathfrak{C}^{\perp}}{\bf 1}_{E}({\bf x})-2^{k-1}{\bf 1}_{\{{\bf 0}\}}({\bf x})
.

1.

If
E_{1}\notin\mathfrak{C}
and
|\mathfrak{C}\cap\mathfrak{C}^{\perp}|=i
,
0\leq i\leq 2^{k-1}
, then
N_{f}=2^{n}-4(2^{k-1}-i)(2^{k}-1)
.

2.

If
E_{1}\in\mathfrak{C}
and
|\mathfrak{C}\cap\mathfrak{C}^{\perp}|=1+j
,
0\leq j\leq 2^{k-1}-1
, then
N_{f}=2^{n}-4(2^{k-1}-j-1)(2^{k}-1)
.

Proof

Let
E_{1}\notin\mathfrak{C}
and
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathfrak{C}\cap\mathfrak{C}^{\perp}\right\rvert}=i
where

i\in\{0,1,\cdots,2^{k-1}\}
. The Hamming distance between
f
and
\widetilde{f}
is

{\rm dist}(f,\widetilde{f})=2(2^{k-1}-i)(2^{k}-1)
.
Therefore

N_{f}=2^{n}-4(2^{k-1}-i)(2^{k}-1).

Let
E_{1}\in\mathfrak{C}
and
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathfrak{C}\cap\mathfrak{C}^{\perp}\right\rvert}=1+j
where

j\in\{0,1,\cdots,2^{k-1}-1\}
. The Hamming distance between
f
and
\widetilde{f}
is

{\rm dist}(f,\widetilde{f})=2(2^{k-1}-j-1)(2^{k}-1)
.
Therefore

N_{f}=2^{n}-4(2^{k-1}-j-1)(2^{k}-1).

From the above theorem, we get the explicit expression for
N_{f}

\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
class. The expression of Rayleigh quotients on
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
in Danielsen, Parker, and Solé
[

Proposition 4

There is no anti-self-dual bent function in
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}.

Proof

We will prove this by the method of contradiction. Let

n=2k
and
n\geq 4
. Let
f\in\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
be a
n
-variable anti-self-dual bent function such that
E_{1}\notin\mathfrak{C}
and
|\mathfrak{C}\cap\mathfrak{C}^{\perp}|=i
,
0\leq i\leq 2^{k-1}
, then

N_{f}=2^{n}-4(2^{k-1}-i)(2^{k}-1).

Thus,
f
is a anti-self-dual function
\Rightarrow N_{f}=-2^{n}
, for some
i,~0\leq i\leq 2^{k-1}
.

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle 2^{n}-4(2^{k-1}-i)(2^{k}-1)=-2^{n}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle 2^{n+1}=4(2^{k-1}-i)(2^{k}-1)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle 2^{n-1}=(2^{k-1}-i)(2^{k}-1)

But this is a contradiction, since
n\geq 4\Rightarrow k\geq 2\Rightarrow(2^{k}-1)>1
, so, the right-hand side product has an odd factor other than
1
but the left-hand side has no such factor.

Similarly, we can show that the same when
E_{1}\in\mathfrak{C}

Hence, for any function

Conclusion

To the best of our knowledge, it is an open problem to check the distribution of the Rayleigh quotient of bent functions in
\mathcal{PS}
. Our results above demonstrate that we could approach this problem by checking different spreads and partial spreads over finite fields.

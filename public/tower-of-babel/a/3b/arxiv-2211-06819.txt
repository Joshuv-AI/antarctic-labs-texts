On the Mod p p Cohomology of Pro- p p Iwahori Subgroups of SL n ⁡ ( ℚ p ) \SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) Thanks: UC San Diego, dkongsga@ucsd.edu Thanks: I would like to thank Claus Sørensen for giving me interesting number theory research topics, and for his input and ideas during my research.
Daniel Kongsgaard

Abstract
. This paper can be seen as an update to part of the author’s dissertation. We study the mod p p cohomology of the pro- p p Iwahori subgroups I I of SL n ⁡ ( ℚ p ) \SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) (and GL n ⁡ ( ℚ p ) \GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) ) for n = 2 n=2 and n = 3 n=3 . Here we use the spectral sequence E 1 s , t = H s , t ​ ( 𝔤 , 𝔽 p ) ⟹ H s + t ​ ( I , 𝔽 p ) E_{1}^{s,t}=H^{s,t}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F}_{p})\Longrightarrow H^{s+t}(I,\mathbb{F}_{p}) due to Sørensen, and we do explicit calculations with an ordered basis of I I , which gives us a basis of 𝔤 = 𝔽 p ⊗ 𝔽 p ​ [ π ] gr ⁡ I \mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\pi]}\gr I that we use to calculate H s , t ​ ( 𝔤 , 𝔽 p ) H^{s,t}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F}_{p}) . We note that the multiplicative spectral sequence E 1 s , t = H s , t ​ ( 𝔤 , 𝔽 p ) E_{1}^{s,t}=H^{s,t}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F}_{p}) collapses on the first page by noticing that all maps on each page are necessarily trivial, and this allows us to describe the above group cohomology groups and all cup products. Finally we note some connections to cohomology of central division algebras over ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} and point out some future research directions.



1.
Introduction

The cohomology of Lie groups has a long history. In the late forties Chevalley and Eilenberg found that
H^{*}(G,\mathbb{R})\cong H^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{R})
for a connected compact Lie group
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(cf.
[
CE48
]
), and since then there has been much research into different types of Lie group cohomology. In particular, the mod
p
cohomology of a connected compact real Lie group has been well understood by Kac since the eighties (cf.
[
Kac85
]
), and the continuous mod
p
cohomology
H^{*}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
of an equi-
p
-valued compact
p
-adic Lie group
G
was already described by Lazard in the sixties (cf.
[
Laz65
]
). We note here that (except for Lazard’s work)
H^{*}(G,\mathbb{R})
and
H^{*}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
indicate the cohomology of
G
as a topological space, and not continuous group cohomology, which can be thought of as the cohomology of the classifying space
BG
.

This paper’s main interest is the continuous mod
p
cohomology
H^{*}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
of compact
p
-adic Lie groups
G
for some specific
G
. Since
p
-adic Lie groups are totally disconnected, working with them requires very different methods than what Chevalley and Eilenberg or Kac used for real Lie groups, and we have to follow the ideas of Lazard (see
[
Laz65
]
) and Serre. In particular we need a
p
-valuation on
G
(and on the completed group algebras associated with
G
), and we work with the graded
Lazard
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\pi]}\gr G
attached to
G
. We will repeatedly use that Sørensen (in
[
Sør21
]
) showed that
H^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F}_{p})
determines
H^{*}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
via a multiplicative spectral sequence

E_{1}^{s,t}=H^{s,t}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F}_{p})\Longrightarrow H^{s+t}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}).

When
G
is equi-
p
-valuable, we get that
\mathfrak{g}
is concentrated in a single degree, and Lazard showed that
H^{*}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\bigwedge H^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F}_{p})
, while Sørensen showed that this also follows from the above spectral sequence. We are interested in cases where
G
is
not
equi-
p
-valuable, and we note that the spectral sequence of Sørensen allows us to work purely with
G
and
\mathfrak{g}
without having to worry about the completed group algebras
\Lambda(G)=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
and
\Omega(G)=\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
(though these do show up in Sørensen’s arguments).

Before describing our particular results in the following paragraph, we emphasize the following remark of Sørensen from
[
Sør21
]
: It is known (due to Lazard) that any compact
p
-adic Lie group contains an open equi-
p
-valuable subgroup (see
[
Laz65
, Chap. V 2.2.7.1]
), which gives the impression that the distinction between
p
-valued and equi-
p
-valued groups is somewhat nuanced, which is true for some questions. But there are many examples of naturally occurring
p
-valuable groups
G
which are not equi-
p
-valuable, where detailed information about
H^{*}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is important. For example unipotent groups (i.e., the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-points of the unipotent radical of a Borel in a split reductive group), Serre’s standard groups with
e>1
as in
[
HKN11
, Lem. 2.2.2]
, pro-
p
Iwahori subgroups for large enough
p
, and
1+\mathfrak{m}_{D}
where
D
is the quaternion division algebra over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for
p>3
(or more generally a central division algebra over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
). Sørensen explicitly calculates
H^{*}\bigl((1+\mathfrak{m}_{D})^{\Nrd=1},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)
for
p>3
and uses it to describe
H^{*}(1+\mathfrak{m}_{D},\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and he notes that
1+\mathfrak{m}_{D}
plays an important role both in number theory (in the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence for instance, see
[
JL70
]
) and algebraic topology, where
1+\mathfrak{m}_{D}
is known as the (strict) Morava stabilizer in stable homotopy theory, and
H^{*}(1+\mathfrak{m}_{D},\mathbb{F}_{p})
somehow controls certain localization functors with respect to Morava
K
-theory (see e.g.
[
Hen07
]
).

In this paper we focus on the case of pro-
p
Iwahori subgroups of
\SL_{n}
(and to a lesser extend
\GL_{n}
) over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for
n=2
or
n=3
, and our work will build on ideas of Lazard and Serre from their more general (but not yet finished) description of the case when
G
is not equi-
p
-valued. We especially rely on the refinement of these ideas as described by Sørensen and Schneider in
[
Sør21
]
and
[
Sch11a
]
. (We note that the cases of
n=4
and the case over quadratic extensions
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for
n=2
are also investigated in
[
Kon22a
]
.) We explicitly calculate the algebra structure of
H^{*}(I,\mathbb{F}_{p})
for the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroups
I_{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\subseteq\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and
I_{\SL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\subseteq\SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, and we note that the first of these is isomorphic as algebras to
H^{*}\bigl((1+\mathfrak{m}_{D})^{\Nrd=1},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)
while the second is at least isomorphic as graded vector spaces (and we conjecture also as algebras). Here
D/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is the central division algebra of dimension
n^{2}
for
n=2
and
n=3
, respectively. We finish the paper by mentioning some future research directions and a conjecture on the connection between the mod
p
cohomology of
(1+\mathfrak{m}_{D})^{\Nrd=1}
(resp.
1+\mathfrak{m}_{D}
) for central division algebras and
I_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
(resp.
I_{\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
).

1.1.
Background and layout of the paper

This work can be seen as a continuation of the recent work on the mod
p
cohomology of pro-
p
Iwahori subgroups. E.g. the work by Schneider and Olivier (see
[
OS19
,
OS18
,
Sch15
]
) working with pro-
p
Iwahori-Hecke modules and the work by Koziol (see
[
Koz17
]
) computing
H^{1}(I,\pi)
as a
\mathcal{H}
-algebra (where
\mathcal{H}
is the pro-
p
Iwahori-Hecke algebra and
\pi
is a mod
p
principal series representation of
\GL_{n}(F)
for some
p
-adic field

). Work by Cornut and Ray (cf.
[
CR16
]
) finding a minimal set of topological generators of the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of a split reductive group over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is also relevant, since the number of generators can be used to find the cohomological dimension of
H^{1}(I,\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Overall all of this work can be seen as part of the search for a mod
p
and
p
-adic local Langlands program.

We start the paper by introducing the setup and notation in the next subsection, after which we briefly note that
H^{*}(I_{\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})},\mathbb{F}_{p})
can be fully described if we know
H^{*}(I_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})},\mathbb{F}_{p})
. We give an overview of the techniques we use throughout the paper in the next section. We then explicitly calculate the algebra structure of
H^{*}(I,\mathbb{F}_{p})
for the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroups
I=I_{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\subseteq\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and (using computer calculations)
I=I_{\SL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\subseteq\SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, and we note that the first of these is isomorphic as algebras to
H^{*}\bigl((1+\mathfrak{m}_{D})^{\Nrd=1},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)
for
n=2
and the second at least as graded vector spaces for
n=3
. We note that these calculations heavily rely on results of Sørensen and Fuks (see
[
Sør21
]
and
[
Fuk86
]
). In particular the multiplicative spectral sequence

E_{1}^{s,t}=H^{s,t}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F}_{p})\Longrightarrow H^{s+t}(I,\mathbb{F}_{p})

of Sørensen (see
[
Sør21
]
) collapses on the first page in both cases, which allows us to calculate the continuous group cohomology via the Lie algebra cohomology of
\mathfrak{g}
. Here
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\pi]}\gr I
is the graded Lazard Lie algebra associated with
I
.

We note that this paper in large parts are repeating material from the authors dissertation (see
[
Kon22a
]
), but some parts have been refined since then. In particular we give a small correction (not to a calculation, but to a comment) and add new calculations in the
\SL_{3}
-case, which fully describes the cup proudcts of the cohomology now, where before only the dimensions where calculated.

Let

Given a finite field extension

are two mutually inverse isomorphisms (and homeomorphisms)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{m}_{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{F}^{(1)}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\log}

Big-

For elements of

Matrices.

Let

denote the matrix with

(i,j)

Dual basis.

Let

-vector space with basis

\mathcal{B}^{*}=(e_{1}^{*},\dotsc,e_{d}^{*})

\mathcal{B}_{V}
and
\mathcal{B}_{W}

Let

\frac{1}{e}
. In particular,
I_{\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}=(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})\times I_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
(which will be discussed in more detail later).

Cohomology.

We denote (using the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex) the mod
p
Lie algebra cohomology of any
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F}_{p})
, while we write
H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
for the mod
p
continuous group cohomology of a topological group
G
. We introduce filtrations and then gradings on the cohomology and use the notation
H^{s,t}=\gr^{s}H^{s+t}
for any type of cohomology
H
.

Spectral sequences.

A cohomological spectral sequence in this paper is a choice of
r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and a collection of

(1)

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules
E_{r}^{s,t}
for each
s,t\in\mathbb{Z}
and all integers
r\geq r_{0}

(2)

differentials
d_{r}^{s,t}\colon E_{r}^{s,t}\to E_{r}^{s+r,t+1-r}
such that
d_{r}^{2}=0
and
E_{r+1}
is isomorphic to the homology of
(E_{r},d_{r})
, i.e.,

E_{r+1}^{s,t}=\frac{\kernel(d_{r}^{s,t}\colon E_{r}^{s,t}\to E_{r}^{s+r,t+1-r})}{\image(d_{r}^{s-r,t+r-1}\colon E_{r}^{s-r,t+r-1}\to E_{r}^{s,t})}.

For a given
r
, the collection
(E_{r}^{s,t},d_{r}^{s,t})_{s,t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called the
r
-th page. A spectral sequence
converges
if
d_{r}
vanishes on
E_{r}^{s,t}
for any
s,t
when
r\gg 0
. In this case
E_{r}^{s,t}
is independent of
r
for sufficiently large
r
, we denote it by
E_{\infty}^{s,t}
and write

E_{r}^{s,t}\Longrightarrow E_{\infty}^{s+t}.

Also, we say that the spectral sequence collapses at the
r^{\prime}
-th page if
E_{r}=E_{\infty}
for all
r\geq r^{\prime}
, but not for
r<r^{\prime}
. Finally, when we have terms
E_{\infty}^{n}
with a natural filtration
F^{\bullet}E_{\infty}^{n}
(but no natural double grading), we set
E_{\infty}^{s,t}=\gr^{s}E_{\infty}^{s+t}=F^{s}E_{\infty}^{s+t}/F^{s+1}E_{\infty}^{s+t}
.

1.3.
A note on the
\GL_{n}
-case

Before continuing we will give a short note on this paper’s connection with the mod
p
continuous group cohomology of the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. We do this by comparing with a similar result for subgroups of central division algebras, so let’s briefly recall the setup from
[
Sør21
]
.

Let
D
be the central division algebra over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of dimension
n^{2}
and invariant
\frac{1}{n}
. Recall the following setup from
[
Sør21
, Sect. 6.3]
: The valuation
v_{p}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
extends uniquely to a valuation
\tilde{v}\colon D^{\times}\to\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}
by the formula
\tilde{v}(x)=\frac{1}{n}v\bigl(\Nrd_{D/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(x)\bigr)
, and the valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{D}=\{x:\tilde{v}(x)>0\}
is the maximal compact subring of
D
. It is local with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{D}=\{x:\tilde{v}(x)>0\}
and residue field
\mathbb{F}_{D}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Furthermore, we can pick
\varpi_{D}
such that
\tilde{v}(\varpi_{D})=\frac{1}{n}
,
\mathfrak{m}_{D}=\varpi_{D}\mathcal{O}_{D}=\mathcal{O}_{D}\varpi_{D}
and
p=\varpi_{D}^{n}

Proposition 1.1
.

Assume that
p>n+1
and let
I_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
and
I_{\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
be the pro-

Iwahori subgroup of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

H^{*}\bigl(I_{\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)\cong H^{*}\bigl(I_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[\varepsilon],

where
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\varepsilon]
denotes the dual numbers (
\varepsilon^{2}=0
). Similarly

H^{*}\bigl(1+\mathfrak{m}_{D},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)\cong H^{*}\bigl((1+\mathfrak{m}_{D})^{\Nrd=1},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[\varepsilon].

Proof.

We note that
[
Sør21
, Sect. 6.3]
proves the second part of the proposition as follows.

Consider the short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(1+\mathfrak{m}_{D})^{\Nrd=1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1+\mathfrak{m}_{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Nrd}

and note that

implies that
\frac{1}{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

\displaystyle H^{*}\bigl(1+\mathfrak{m}_{D},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong H^{*}\bigl((1+\mathfrak{m}_{D})^{\Nrd=1},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)\otimes H^{*}\bigl(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)

Here the identification

can be viewed as a very special case of Lazard’s isomorphism.

A similar argument applies to the first part of the proposition:

Consider the short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\det}

implies that

, so

makes sense on

viewed as a central subgroup of

I_{\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\cong I_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\times(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, and by Künneth

\displaystyle H^{*}\bigl(I_{\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong H^{*}\bigl(I_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)\otimes H^{*}\bigl(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong H^{*}\bigl(I_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})},\mathbb{F}_{p}\bigr)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[\varepsilon].

We note that here

is the center of

since
I_{\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\subseteq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}=\mu_{p-1}\times(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, so when discussing pro-
p

2.
Techniques

In this section we will describe how to calculate information about the cohomology of a
p
-valuable group by using its Lazard Lie algebra.

Let
(G,\omega)
be a
p
-valuable group and let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p
(later we will assume that
k=\mathbb{F}_{p}
). In this section we will describe how the spectral sequence

(4)

E_{1}^{s,t}=H^{s,t}(\mathfrak{g},k)\Longrightarrow H^{s+t}(G,k)

from
[
Sør21
, §6.1]
can be used to calculate information about the dimensions of
H^{n}(G,k)
for varying
n
and information about the cup product on
H^{*}(G,k)
. After this, we will then discuss how this applies to pro-
p
Iwahori subgroups
I
of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
for
n=2
and
n=3
.

Recall that
\mathfrak{g}
in the above spectral sequence is given by
\mathfrak{g}=k\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\pi]}\gr G
, so to describe
\mathfrak{g}
, we first need a good description of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\pi]
-Lie algebra
\gr G
. To get this description, suppose that we have an ordered basis
(g_{1},\dotsc,g_{d})
of
G
, so that
\omega(g)=\min_{i=1,\dotsc,d}\bigl(\omega(g_{i})+v_{p}(x_{i})\bigr)
for
g=g_{1}^{x_{1}}\dotsb g_{d}^{x_{d}}
, and recall that
\bigl(\sigma(g_{1}),\dotsc,\sigma(g_{d})\bigr)
is a basis of
\gr G
, where
\sigma(g)=gG_{\omega(g)+}\in\gr G
for
g\neq 1
.

To understand the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\pi]
-Lie algebra, we need to find
[\sigma(g_{i}),\sigma(g_{j})]=\sigma\bigl([g_{i},g_{j}]\bigr)
for all
i,j=1,\dotsc,d
. We recall from
[
Sch11
]
that
\sigma(g^{x})=\overline{x}\pi^{v_{p}(x)}\,.\,\sigma(g)
for
g\in G\setminus\{1\}
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
, where
\overline{x}=p^{-v_{p}(x)}x\pmod{p}
. Now, calculating
[g_{i},g_{j}]
for all
i,j=1,\dotsc,d
, we can find
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that

[g_{i},g_{j}]=g_{1}^{x_{1}}\dotsb g_{d}^{x_{d}},

and thus

\bigl[\sigma(g_{i}),\sigma(g_{j})\bigr]=\sigma\bigl([g_{i},g_{j}]\bigr)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\overline{x}_{\ell}\pi^{v_{p}(x_{\ell})}\,.\,\sigma(g_{\ell}).

See the proofs of
[
Sch11
, Lem. 26.4 and Prop. 26.5]
for more details, and note that it is enough to only consider the
i<j
cases.

Let
\{\ell_{1},\dotsc,\ell_{r}\}
be the subset of
\{1,\dotsc,d\}
such that
v_{p}(x_{\ell_{s}})=0
and
v_{p}(x_{\ell})>0
for
\ell\notin\{\ell_{1},\dotsc,\ell_{r}\}
, and recall that
\mathfrak{g}=k\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\pi]}\gr G
has basis
\xi_{i}=1\otimes\sigma(g_{i})
. Since
\pi
acts trivially on
k
here, we see that

[\xi_{i},\xi_{j}]=\bigl[1\otimes\sigma(g_{i}),1\otimes\sigma(g_{j})\bigr]=\sum_{s=1}^{r}\overline{x}_{\ell_{s}}\xi_{\ell_{s}}.

Now we have a basis
(\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{d})
of
\mathfrak{g}=k\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\pi]}\gr G
, and we know all the structure constants.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the structure constants are in
\mathbb{F}_{p}\subseteq k
by the above, so we can lift them to structure constants in
\{0,1,\dotsc,p-1\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, which will be useful later. Also note that we will often (but not always) be able to lift
\mathfrak{g}
to a
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}}
with
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes k
.

Assume from now on that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is unitary, i.e., that
[\xi_{i},\xi_{j}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{ij\ell}\xi_{\ell}
has
\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{ijj}=0
. This will be the case for all Lie algebras, we will work with in this paper. Suppose furthermore that
\mathfrak{g}
is a graded Lie algebra, graded by finitely many positive integers,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{2}\oplus\dotsb\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{m}
, which will also be the case for all Lie algebras we work with in this paper.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that any
p
-valuable group
G
admits a
p
-valuation
\omega
with values in
\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, cf.
[
Sch11
, Cor. 33.3]
. Thus we can reindex the filtration of
G
by letting
G^{i}=G_{\frac{i}{m}}
for
i=0,1,\dotsc
, and this translates to
\gr^{i}G=\gr_{\frac{i}{m}}G
and
\mathfrak{g}^{i}=\mathfrak{g}_{\frac{i}{m}}
in general. In the cases we care about there will be no zero graded part, which allows us to make the above assumption.

Then
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}
is graded as well by letting

\gr^{j}\Bigl(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\Bigr)=\bigoplus_{j_{1}+\dotsb+j_{n}=j}\mathfrak{g}^{j_{1}}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\mathfrak{g}^{j_{n}}.

We note that, since
\mathfrak{g}
is finite dimensional, there are only finitely many non-zero
\gr^{j}\bigl(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\bigr)
we are interested in, and we can find a basis with elements of the form
\xi_{i_{1}}\wedge\xi_{i_{2}}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\xi_{i_{n}}
of each of these using our basis
(\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{d})
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 2.3
.

When ordering the basis of
\gr^{j}\bigl(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\bigr)=\bigoplus_{j_{1}+\dotsb+j_{n}=j}\mathfrak{g}^{j_{1}}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\mathfrak{g}^{j_{n}}
, we will do it as follows. First we order the
\mathfrak{g}^{j_{1}}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\mathfrak{g}^{j_{n}}
by the lexicographical order on
(j_{1},\dotsc,j_{n})
. Then we order the basis of each
\mathfrak{g}^{j_{1}}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\mathfrak{g}^{j_{n}}
by the lexicographical order on equal
j_{\ell}
’s, e.g., if
\mathfrak{g}^{1}=\Span_{k}(\xi_{1},\xi_{3})
and
\mathfrak{g}^{2}=\Span_{k}(\xi_{2},\xi_{4})
, then
\mathfrak{g}^{1}\wedge\mathfrak{g}^{1}\wedge\mathfrak{g}^{2}
has basis
\xi_{1}\wedge\xi_{3}\wedge\xi_{2},\xi_{1}\wedge\xi_{3}\wedge\xi_{4}
.

Assuming furthermore that
k
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded (concentrated in degree
0
), the space
\Hom_{k}\bigl(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},k\bigr)
inherits the
\mathbb{Z}
-grading

\Hom_{k}\Bigl(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},k\Bigr)=\bigoplus_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{k}^{s}\Bigl(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},k\Bigr),

where
\Hom_{k}^{s}
denotes the homogeneous
k
-linear maps of degree
s
, cf.
[
FF74
, Lem. 4.2]
. We note, by
[
Fuk86
, Chap. 1 §3.7]
, that these gradings on the chain and cochain complexes transfer to gradings on the homology and cohomology. We write

H^{s,t}=H^{s,t}(\mathfrak{g},k)=H^{s+t}\Bigl(\gr^{s}\Hom_{k}\bigl(\bigwedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g},k\bigr)\Bigr),

and we let
e_{i_{1},i_{2},\dotsc,i_{n}}
denote the dual of
\xi_{i_{1}}\wedge\xi_{i_{2}}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\xi_{i_{n}}
and note that we get a dual basis of
\Hom_{k}^{s}(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},k)
consisting of such elements.

Remark 2.4
.

We do not spend effort to describe the homology for a few reasons. First, we need the cohomology, not the homology, in our spectral sequence. Second, by
[
Fuk86
, Chap. 1 §3.6]
, we have a version of Poincaré duality for Lie algebra cohomology, i.e.,
H^{n}(\mathfrak{g},k)\cong H_{n-d}(\mathfrak{g},k)
, so we can easily describe the homology using the cohomology. Third, we will care about the cup product later, and we do not get a nice product in homology, cf.
[
Fuk86
, Chap. 1 §3.2]
.

Now we have bases of all
\gr^{j}\bigl(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\bigr)
, and by
[
Fuk86
, Chap. 1 §3.7]
we get graded chain complexes that we can use to find the homology of
\mathfrak{g}
. Here the boundary maps of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigwedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{4}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{1}}

are given by

d_{n}(x_{1}\wedge\dotsb\wedge x_{n})=\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}[x_{i},x_{j}]\wedge x_{1}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\widehat{x}_{i}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\widehat{x}_{j}\wedge\dotsb\wedge x_{n},

and the coboundary maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{k}\Bigl(\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},k\Bigr)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{k}(\mathfrak{g},k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{k}(k,k)=k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{1}}

are the dual maps of the boundary maps (see
[
Fuk86
, Chap. 1 §3.1]
for more details). Thus, if we use the dual basis, of our basis of
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}
, in
\Hom_{k}\bigl(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},k\bigr)
, we get that
\partial_{n}=d_{n}^{\top}
as matrices, where
(\>\cdot\>)^{\top}
is the transpose. Since we know bases and linear maps explicitly, and we know that the linear maps restrict to graded linear maps, we can now find matrices describing all graded linear maps

\gr^{j}\Bigl(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\Bigr)\to\gr^{j}\Bigl(\bigwedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\Bigr),

and thus we can find matrices describing all graded linear maps

\Hom_{k}^{s}\Bigl(\bigwedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},k\Bigr)\to\Hom_{k}^{s}\Bigl(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},k\Bigr),

which allows us to exactly calculate the cohomology and find a basis for each cohomology space.

Remark 2.5
.

We note here that when the structure constants can be lifted to
\mathbb{Z}
, it clear that the above matrices describing the (co)boundary maps can be lifted to
\mathbb{Z}
by looking at the formula for the boundary maps. This allows us to do calculations over
\mathbb{Z}
and just reduce mod
p
in the end.

Suppose now that we have found the cohomology
H^{s,t}=H^{s,t}(\mathfrak{g},k)
for all
s,t
. To get information about the (continuous) group cohomology
H^{n}(G,k)
, we need to use the multiplicative spectral sequence (
4
), i.e.,

E_{1}^{s,t}=H^{s,t}(\mathfrak{g},k)\Longrightarrow H^{s+t}(G,k)

and information about spectral sequences in general. We already know from
[
Sør21
]
that this spectral sequence collapses at a finite page, and one can hope that it will actually collapse at the first page. One way we can verify that the spectral sequence (in certain cases) collapses at the first page, is by considering the exact bidegrees of the differentials. We know that the differentials
d_{r}^{s,t}\colon E_{r}^{s,t}\to E_{r}^{s+r,t+1-r}
have bidegree
(r,1-r)
, so if the non-zero modules on the first pages is distributed in such a way that all differentials
d_{r}^{s,t}
are trivial for all
s,t
and
r\geq 1
, then we can be sure that the spectral sequence collapses on the first page. This will become clearer when we look at examples in the next few sections.

Remark 2.6
.

We note here that the spectral sequence will collapse on the first page for the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroups of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
\SL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
by the above argument, but in
[
Kon22a
]
it is shown that this argument does not work for the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of
\SL_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

Now note, by
[
Fuk86
, Chap. 1 §3.7]
, that the cup product is compatible with the gradings on the Lie algebra cohomology, in particular

(5)

H^{s,t}\cup H^{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\subseteq H^{s+s^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}},

where
H^{s,t}=H^{s,t}(\mathfrak{g},k)
. Thus, since the spectral sequence is multiplicative, we can describe the cup product on
H^{*}(G,k)
when the spectral sequence collapses on the first page. Some cup products will be trivially zero by (
5
), and for the rest of the cup products, we can calculate them with an explicit basis using the following.

For
f\in\Hom_{k}\bigl(\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{g},k\bigr)
and
g\in\Hom_{k}\bigl(\bigwedge^{q}\mathfrak{g},k\bigr)
, we know from
[
CE56
, Chap. XIII, Sect. 8]
, that the cup product in cohomology is induced by:
f\cup g\in\Hom_{k}\bigl(\bigwedge^{p+q}\mathfrak{g},k\bigr)
defined by

(6)

(f\cup g)(x_{1}\wedge\dotsb\wedge x_{p+q})=\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{p+q}\\
\sigma(1)<\dotsb<\sigma(p)\\
\sigma(p+1)<\dotsb<\sigma(p+q)\end{subarray}}}\sign(\sigma)f(x_{\sigma(1)}\wedge\dotsb\wedge x_{\sigma(p)})g(x_{\sigma(p+1)}\wedge\dotsb\wedge x_{\sigma(p+q)}).

With our basis and dual basis this clearly reduces to

(7)

(e_{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{p}}\cup e_{j_{1},\dotsc,j_{q}})(\xi_{k_{1}}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\xi_{k_{p+q}})=\sign(\sigma)\bm{1}_{\{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{p}\}\cup\{j_{1},\dotsc,j_{q}\}=\{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{p+q}\}},

where
\bm{1}_{B}
is the indicator function that is
1
if
B
is true and
0
otherwise and
\sigma\in S_{p+q}
is the permutation with
\sigma(k_{\ell})=i_{\ell}
and
\sigma(k_{p+\ell})=j_{\ell}
. (Here we assume that the wedge product
\xi_{k_{1}}\wedge\dotsc\wedge\xi_{k_{p+q}}
are already ordered in the lexicographical order we are working with.) This equation clearly simplifies calculations a lot, and as long as we work with explicit bases for everything, it makes it quite possible to calculate cup products explicitly. In particular, we will by the above have an explicit basis
(v_{1}^{(s,t)},\dotsc,v_{m}^{(s,t)})
of each
H^{s,t}
, where each
v_{m}^{(s,t)}
can be lifted to a linear combination of the
e_{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{n}}
’s, which makes the calculations easier.

Finally note that even when the spectral sequence does not (necessarily) collapse on the first page, we can still get some bounds on the dimensions of
H^{n}(G,k)
that will allow us to draw some conclusions about the structure of
H^{*}(G,k)
. This is investigated in more detail in
[
Kon22a
]
, but we will not explore it in this paper.

In the following two sections of this paper we will focus on using the techniques described in this section to get as much as possible information about the cohomology of
H^{*}(I,k)
, where

is the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
for
n=2
or
n=3
.

3.
H^{*}(I,\mathbb{F}_{p})
for the pro-
p
Iwahori
I\subseteq\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

In this section we will describe the continuous group cohomology of the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup
I
of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

When

is the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup in
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, we know by

that we can take it to be of the form

I=\linespread\begin{pNiceMatrix}^{\!\!\det=1}\subseteq\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

In this case, an obvious guess for an ordered basis (using that
(1+p)^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}=1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
) is

Becausewewanttobeabletodescribethecommutatorsusingthisorderedbasis
,

5.3.
Serre spectral sequence

Another interesting research direction is to try to work with the Serre spectral sequence in the following way.

Assume we have the
“standard”
setup with
\mathcal{G}=\SL_{n}
,
\mathcal{U}
unipotent upper triangular matrices and
\mathcal{T}
diagonal matrices with determinant
1
. Let also
I\subseteq\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
which is upper triangular and unipotent modulo
p
, and let

K\coloneqq\kernel\bigl(\red\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\bigr),

where
\red\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is the reduction map. (Note that
I=\{g\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}):\red(g)\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\}
in this case, cf.
[
CR16
]
.) Then

I/K\cong\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),

and thus we get the Serre spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}=H^{i}\bigl(\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),H^{j}(K,\mathbb{F}_{p})\bigr)\Longrightarrow H^{i+j}(I,\mathbb{F}_{p}),

which is also a multiplicative spectral sequence. Since
K
is a uniformly powerful group (cf.
[
OS19
, Prop. 7.6]
), we know by
[
Laz65
, p. 183]
that

H^{j}(K,\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\bigwedge^{j}\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(K,\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Now we can let
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
act by

(g\,.\,f)(x)=f(g^{-1}xg)

for
g\in\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
,
f\colon K\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
x\in K
, and hope to split
\bigwedge^{j}\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(K,\mathbb{F}_{p})
into a direct sum of Verma modules
\bigoplus_{\lambda}V(\lambda)
for
p
-restricted
\lambda
(
\lambda
with
0\leq\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\leq p-1
), similarly to what is done in
[
PT18
]
. This description might be easier to compare with a similar spectral sequence for
(1+\mathfrak{m}_{D})^{\Nrd=1}
, but it is harder to get started with since the spectral sequence is more complicated. One can hope that the difference in the spectral sequence might make it so that it will always collapse on the second page (the starting page in this case). We note that the author has recently been attempting to use this approach, but the fact that even
H^{*}(\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),\mathbb{F}_{p})
is not very well understood for
n>3
makes this approach hard to generalize. The hope is that we can connect it directly with a spectral sequence for
(1+\mathfrak{m}_{D})^{\Nrd=1}
without discussing where the spectral sequence collapses.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to study the generalize notion of bounded variation. The paper can be subdivided into two parts. In the first part, we focused on power oriented bounded variation. While on the second half we studied distance dependent variation. At the beginning of each section, a brief discussion about structural characterization and various inclusion properties are also provided.

Let
r\in]0,\infty[
be fixed. A function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be satisfying
r
-bounded variation if for any partition
P=\{a=x_{0},\cdots,x_{n}=b\}
with
x_{0}<\cdots<x_{n};
the expression
\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}
is bounded. It can be easily observed that for the case
r=1
, this notion implies the well-known definition of bounded variation.

The term functions of bounded variation was first introduced by Jordan in his paper
[
3
]
. Later the concept was generalized by Wiener in the paper
[
4
]
where he investigated several topics related to Fourier Analysis. His generalized form is widely known
p
-variation and the definition of it is same as above mentioned
r
-variation with
r\geq 1
. Since then many more generalized forms in this direction were introduced including well known Young’s generalized bounded variation in
[
5
]
.

One of our main objectives in this paper is to find a possible decomposition for the functions satisfying
r
-bounded variation. We established that for
r\in]0,1[
, if a function equipped with
r
-bounded variation then
f
is nothing but a function of ordinary bounded variation. In other words, in such cases
f
can be expressed as the difference of two monotone functions. We generalized the Jordan’s decomposition theorem and showed that a function
f:I(\subseteq R_{+})\to\mathbb{R}
with
0\in I
satisfying Wiener variation/p-variation (
r
-variation with
r\geq 1
) can be treated as approximately monotone. Moreover with a minimal assumption on the error term, such a function
f
can be precisely approximated by a nondecreasing function
g
that satisfies the following inequality

\Phi(x)\leq g(x)-f(x)\leq 2\Phi\Big(\dfrac{x}{2}\Big)\quad\mbox{where}

\displaystyle\Phi(u):=\sup_{x,x+u\in I}\Big\{{{}^{r}}v_{f}(x+u)-{{}^{r}}v_{f}(x)\Big\}^{\dfrac{1}{r}},\quad\quad\mbox{(${{}^{r}v}_{f}$ is $r$-variation function of $f$)}.

We also proved that for
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
, a function satisfying
r_{1}
-bounded variation is also a function of
r_{2}
-bounded variation. We showed that if
\Phi^{r}
is superadditive, then in that scenario a
\Phi
-Hölder function satisfies
r
-bounded variation. The definition and other essentials for
\Phi
-Hölder functions are discussed below.

Let
p\geq 0
be fixed and
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following functional inequality

f(x)\leq f(y)+(y-x)^{p}\quad\mbox{for all $x,y\in I$}\quad\mbox{with $x<y$}.

For

, it is clearly visible that
f(t)+t
is nondecreasing in

. While for

possesses usual monotonicity(increasingness). In the case

,

can be treated as

-monotone function. Using the result related to the decomposition of such functions which was mentioned by Páles in his paper
[

]
;

is expressible as

, where

is nondecreasing and
||h||\leq\dfrac{1}{2}
. The decomposition of

when

is still unknown. In this paper we have shown that if

with

, then for any

, the function

can be tightly approximated with a nondecreasing majorant

that satisfies

Motivated from this concept, we studied
\Phi
-monotonicity in depth in our papers
[
2
]
and
[

]
.
A function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
\Phi
-monotone if for any
x,y\in I
with
x<y
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle f(x)\leq f(y)+\Phi(y-x)\quad\mbox{where}\quad\mbox{$\Phi:[0,\ell(I)]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}$ }.

On the other hand a function

is termed as
\Phi
-Hölder
if both

and
-f
are
\Phi
-monotone, That is for any

, the following inequality must satisfies

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\Phi(|y-x|).

Let

be fixed and

such that for any

with

;
\Phi(y-x)=0
holds. Then the notion of
\Phi
-monotonicity for

can be treated as distance dependent monotone(increasing) function.

Inspired by this, we introduce the following concept. For a fixed

, a function

is said to be increasing by a period

(or

In our recently submitted paper, we have gone through the fundamental structural properties of such functions. For any given function

we give a precise formula to obtain the largest

-periodically increasing function by an ordinary monotone function. Besides basic characterization and a conditional decomposition of

-monotone function was also provided.

The main objective in the last section of the paper to establish a relationship between bounded functions and

-periodically increasing function. For this establishment, we need to introduce a weaker concept of boundedness, named as

Let

and the interval is partitioned as follows

Then for any function

\displaystyle\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}(f,P):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(x_{i}-f(x_{i-1})|\quad\mbox{with}\quad x_{i}-x_{i-1}\geq d\quad(i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}).

If for such possible partitions of

is a function of

-bounded variation. As mentioned before, we will later see that a function of

-bounded variation satisfies boundedness and vice verse. We also showed that the variation function

is non decreasing. Moreover, superadditive characteristic of

2.
On approximately monotone and Hölder Functions

Before proceeding, we need to recall several notions and terminologies. We consider
I
be a non empty and non singleton interval.
\ell(I)(\in\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}})
denotes the length of the interval in the extended real line.
Then the function
\Phi:[0,\ell(I)]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is said to be a error function.

Based upon
\Phi
, we can formulate the concept of
\Phi
-monotonicity. A function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
\Phi
-monotone(increasing) if for any
x,y\in I
with
x\leq y
the following functional inequality holds

f(x)\leq f(y)+\Phi(y-x).

On the other hand if both
f
and
-f
are
\Phi
-monotone, we will say that
f
is
\Phi
-Hölder. In other words a function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
\Phi
Hölder if for any
x,y\in I
; it satisfies the inequality below

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\Phi(|y-x|).

Now we will go through some inequalities associated with the error function
\Phi
provided it has some structural properties .

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
\Phi:[0,\ell(I)[\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be subadditive and concave. Then for any
x,y\in[0,\ell(I)]
with
x\leq y
, the following inequality holds

(1)

\displaystyle\Phi(y)-\Phi(x)\leq\Phi(y-x)\leq 2\Phi\Big(\dfrac{y}{2}\Big)-\Phi(x).

Additionally, under the pre-mentioned conditions
(-\Phi)
is
\Phi
-monotone.

Proof.

By using the subadditivity of
\Phi
, we get

\displaystyle\Phi(y)=\Phi(y-x+x)\leq\Phi(y-x)+\Phi(x).

This is the first inequality of (
1
).

Now applying concavity property of
\Phi
, we can compute the following inequality

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Phi(y-x)}{2}+\dfrac{\Phi(x)}{2}\leq\Phi\Big(\dfrac{(y-x)+x}{2}\Big)=\Phi\Big(\dfrac{y}{2}\Big).

Multiplying the above inequality by
2
and rewriting it, we will obtain the remaining part of inequality of (
1
).

The second assertion can be easily seen from the first part of the inequality (
1
). By rearranging the terms of it we get

(-\Phi)(x)\leq(-\Phi)(y)+\Phi(y-x).

This yields the
\Phi
-monotonicity of
(-\Phi)
and completes the proof of first assertion.
∎

Based upon the above theorem, we can propose the following corollary related to power functions.

Corollary 2.2
.

For any
0\leq x\leq y
; the following inequality will hold true for any
p\in[0,1]

(2)

\displaystyle y^{p}-x^{p}\leq(y-x)^{p}\leq 2^{1-p}y-x^{p}.

Proof.

For
u\geq 0
and for any fixed
p\in[0,1]
, the function
u\to u^{p}
is subadditive and concave. Hence, the result directly follows from Proposition
2.1
.
∎

We are now ready to present a possible decomposition for
\Phi
-monotone function. The following theorem shows the tight approximation of a
\Phi
-monotone function by an ordinary monotone function.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a non-empty and non-singleton interval such that
0\in I
.

\Phi:[0,\ell(I)]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a subadditive error function. If
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
is non-decreasing then
f:=g-\Phi
is a
\Phi
-monotone function in
I
.
Conversely if,
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is
\Phi
-monotone where
\Phi
is subadditive and concave. Then there exists a non decreasing majorant
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
of
f
, that satisfies the following inequality

(3)

\displaystyle\Phi(x)\leq g(x)-f(x)\leq 2\Phi\Big(\dfrac{x}{2}\Big).

Proof.

To prove the first part of the theorem, we assume
x,y\in I
with
x\leq y
. By using the first inequality of (
1
), we get the following for
f

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=g(x)-\Phi(x)

\displaystyle\leq g(y)-\Phi(y)+\big(\Phi(y)-\Phi(x)\big)

\displaystyle=f(y)+\Phi(y-x).

This establishes that
f
is
\Phi
-monotone.
To prove the converse assertion,
we use the inequality associated with
\Phi
-monotonicity of
f
. Applying the last inequality of (
1
) there; we observe that the following holds

(4)

\displaystyle f(x)+\Phi(x)\leq f(y)+2\Phi\bigg(\dfrac{y}{2}\bigg)\qquad(\mbox{ for all }x,y\in I\,\,\mbox{ with }\,x\leq y).

We can define
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
as as follows

g(u):=\inf_{z\in I,u\leq z}\bigg(f(z)+2\Phi\Big(\dfrac{z}{2}\Big)\bigg).

We assume
x,y\in I
with
x\leq y
. Then we can formulate the following inequality

\displaystyle g(x)=\inf_{z\in I,x\leq z}\bigg(f(z)+2\Phi\Big(\dfrac{z}{2}\Big)\bigg)\leq\inf_{z\in I,y\leq z}\bigg(f(z)+2\Phi\Big(\dfrac{z}{2}\Big)\bigg)=g(y).

This shows that
g
is nondecreasing. Now from (
4
), we obtain the following

\displaystyle f(x)+\Phi(x)\leq g(x)\leq f(x)+2\Phi\bigg(\dfrac{x}{2}\bigg).

Subtracting
f
from all part of above inequality, we will get (
3
) and it validates the result.
∎

From the above theorem, we can establish a conditional decomposition of the function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following inequality

(5)

\displaystyle f(x)\leq f(y)+c(y-x)^{p}\quad x,y\in I\quad\mbox{with}\quad x\leq y.\quad(p\in]0,1[).

We heve already discussed about such type of functions in the Introduction Section of this paper.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let

be a nonempty and nonsingleton interval with

. Then for

satisfying

, there exists a nondecreasing majorant

Proof.

One can easily show that for a fixed

, the function

(

) is subadditive and concave. Hence, the result is a direct implication of Theorem

and Corollary

.
∎

3.
On functions of power dependent variation

Through out this section
I
will denote the non empty and non singleton interval
[a,b]
. Let
P
be a partition of
I
as follows

(6)

\displaystyle P=\{x_{0},\cdots,x_{n}\}\quad\mbox{with}\quad a=x_{0}<\cdots<x_{n}=b

Let
r\in]0,\infty[
be fixed. For a real valued function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
, we define
r
-bounded variation
\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f,P)
for the partition
P
of
I
as below

\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f,P):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}.

If the above mentioned expression is bounded with respect to all such possible partitions of
I
, we say that
f
possesses
r
-bounded variation. The supremum of all
r
-variations of
f
is called total
r
-variation which is denoted by

\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V^{r}}}(f):=\sup\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f,P).

We term
{{}^{r}v}_{f}:I\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by
{{}^{r}v}_{f}(x):=\overset{x}{\underset{a}{V^{r}}}(f)
as
r
-variation function for
f
.
It can be clearly observed that if a function is unbounded then it can’t be a function of
r
-bounded variation. Also, there are bounded functions which can not be categorized as function satisfying
r
-bounded variation for any
r\in]0,\infty[
. For example

f:I\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

f(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad\mbox{if $x$ is rational}\\
0\quad\mbox{if $x$ is irrational}\end{cases}

is bounded but not a function of
r
-bounded variation.
We will utilize the following subadditive (superadditive) property in the upcoming result extensively.

For any
r\leq 1
and
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the following inequality satisfies

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\bigg)^{r}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{r}.

For
r\geq 1
under the same assumptions on
{a_{i}}^{\prime}s
, the reserve inequality holds.

Now we are going to show that the
r
- variation function possesses monotonicity.

Proposition 3.1
.

{{}^{r}v}_{f}
is a nondecreasing function.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in I
with
x\leq y
. For any
\epsilon>0
, we will have a partition
P
of
I
as
defined in (
6
) satisfying the following inequality

\displaystyle{{}^{r}v}_{f}(x)

\displaystyle<\sum_{i=0}^{n}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}+|f(y)-f(x)|^{r}+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq{{}^{r}v}_{f}(y)+\epsilon.

Upon taking
\epsilon\to 0
, we get the monotonicity of
{{}^{r}v}_{f}
.
∎

The next proposition will show that total
r
-bounded variation of a function defined in a compact interval of

possesses characteristics similar to superadditivity.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

be fixed and

is a function of

, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\overset{c}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f)+\overset{b}{\underset{c}{V}^{r}}(f)\leq\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f).

Additionally, in case of

the above inequality turns into equality.

Proof.

To establish the inequality, we assume that
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary. Then there must exists two partitions

and
P_{2}=\{c=x_{k},\cdots,x_{n}=b\}
respectively for

and
[c,b]
with

satisfying the following two inequalities

\displaystyle\overset{c}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f)<\overset{c}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f,P_{1})+\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overset{c}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f)<\overset{b}{\underset{c}{V}}(f,P_{2})+\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}.

Upon adding up the two inequalities side by side, we obtain

\displaystyle\overset{c}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f)+\overset{b}{\underset{c}{V}^{r}}(f)

\displaystyle<\overset{c}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f,P_{1})+\overset{c}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f,P_{2})+\epsilon

\displaystyle=\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f,P_{1}\cup P_{2})+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V}}(f)+\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, taking
\epsilon\to 0
, we obtain (
7

To prove the second assertion it will be enough to show that for any

, the reverse inequality of (
7
) also holds. For any arbitrary
\epsilon>0
, there must exists a partition like
P
of
I
, as defined in (
6
) satisfying the following inequality

\displaystyle\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f)<\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}+\epsilon.

Then for any
c\in I^{\circ}
there must exists a

such that

where

. Together with this, by using the subadditive property for

; we can expand the above inequality as follows

\displaystyle\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f)

\displaystyle<\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}+|f(x_{k-1}-f(c)+f(c)-f(x_{k})|^{r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=k-1}^{n}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}+\epsilon

\displaystyle<\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}+\Big(|f(x_{k-1}-f(c)|+|f(c)-f(x_{k})|\Big)^{r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=k-1}^{n}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}+|f(x_{k-1}-f(c)|^{r}+|(f(c)-f(x_{k+1})|^{r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=k}^{n}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|^{r}+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\overset{c}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f)+\overset{b}{\underset{c}{V}^{r}}(f)+\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, considering it negligibly small we obtain

\displaystyle\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f)\leq\overset{c}{\underset{a}{V}^{r}}(f)+\overset{b}{\underset{c}{V}^{r}}(f).

This proves the validity of assertion and completes the proof.
∎

The next proposition will show that that algebraic multiplication of two

and

4.
On functions of
d
-bounded variation

Throughout this section
d
will be a fixed positive number and
I=[a,b]
will represent a non-empty and non singleton closed interval with
\ell(I)\geq 2d
. However for some of the results this condition can be relaxed to
\ell(I)\geq d
and it is very much self explanatory. A function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
d
-periodically increasing if for any
x,y\in I
, with
y-x\geq d
,
f(x)\leq f(y)
holds. It is evident that the class of
d
-periodically increasing functions is a convex cone as it is closed under addition and multiplication with non negative scalars. Moreover if
f
is
d
-periodically increasing and non negative then for any
r(\in\mathbb{R}_{+})
the function
f^{r}
is also periodically increasing with the same period
d
. It can also be shown that the class of
d
-periodically increasing functions is also closed under pointwise limit operations.

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a real valued function. We consider an arbitrary partition of
I
as

P=\{x_{0},\cdots,x_{n}\}
with
a=x_{0}<\cdots<x_{n}=b
satisfying
x_{i}-x_{i-1}\geq d
where
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
.
Based on the partition
P
, we derive the
d
-variation of
f
in
I
as follows

(11)

\displaystyle\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}(f,P):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|.

The supremum of (
11
) with respect to all such
d
-variation of
f
, is termed as total
d
-variation of
f
.
In other words, we can define total
d
-variation of
f
as follows

\displaystyle\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}(f):=\sup\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}(f,P).

By using the notion of total
d
-variation of
f
we can formulate the
d
-variation function
{}_{d}v_{f}
as follows

\displaystyle{}_{d}v_{f}(x):=\begin{cases}0\quad\qquad\mbox{if}\quad x\in[a,a+d[\\
\overset{x}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}(f)\quad\mbox{if}\quad x\in[a+d,b]\end{cases}

It is evident that any bounded function
f
will satisfy
d
- bounded variation since

{\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}}(f,P)\leq\bigg\lceil{\dfrac{b-a}{d}}\bigg\rceil\sup_{u,v\in[a,b]}|f(u)-f(v)|

holds. Moreover from the inequalities

\displaystyle|f(x)|-|f(b)|\leq|f(b)-f(x)|\leq{\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}}(f)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in[a,b-d]

\displaystyle|f(x)|-|f(a)|\leq|f(x)-f(a)|\leq{\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}}(f)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in[a+d,b]

and from our pre assumption on
I
that
\ell(I)\geq 2d
, it can be easily seen that

(12)

\displaystyle|f(x)|\leq\max\big\{|f(a)|,|f(b)|\big\}+{\overset{b}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}}(f).

Which yields that
any function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
d
-bounded variation is bounded as well.
Therefore, we can use the terms bounded function and function satisfying
d
-bounded variation interchangeably.
The following results give a structural overview of this newly defined variation. The proposition below shows the
d
-periodic monotonicity of a
d
- variation function.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded function. Then

-variation function
{}_{d}v_{f}
is
d
-periodically increasing.

Proof.

To validate the statement we consider two cases. first let
x\in[a,a+d[
and

such that

. Then the

-periodical monotonicity of
{{}_{d}v}_{f}
is clearly evident from the following inequality

{{}_{d}v}_{f}(x)=0\leq\overset{y}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}(f)={{}_{d}v}_{f}(y).

Next we assume

such that

. Let
\epsilon\geq 0
be arbitrary. Then there must exists a partition

{{}_{d}v}_{f}(x)=\overset{x}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}(f)<\overset{x}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}(f,P)+\epsilon.

Using the above inequality we obtain the following

\displaystyle\overset{y}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}(f)+\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})|+|f(y)-f(x)|\bigg)+\epsilon

\displaystyle=\overset{x}{\underset{a}{V_{d}}}(f,P)+\epsilon+|f(y)-f(x)|

Taking

It proves that

is

-periodically increasing in the interval

.
∎

Now we are ready to state a possible decomposition for any bounded function in terms of a monotone and

Theorem 4.4
.

A function of

Conversely difference of two

-periodically increasing functions is a function of
d
-bounded variation.

Proof.

First we assume that
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is a function of
d
-bounded variation.
Now we construct a function
{{}_{d}w}_{f}(x):I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
as

{{}_{d}w}_{f}:=\begin{cases}0\qquad\mbox{if}\quad x\in[a,a+d[\\
\underset{z\in[a,x-d]}{\sup}\overset{x}{\underset{z}{V_{d}}}(f)\,\,\mbox{if}\quad x\in[a+d,b]\end{cases}

and rewrite
f
in the following way

f:={{}_{d}}w_{f}-({{}_{d}}w_{f}-f).

To validate the theorem it will be enough to show that
{{}_{d}}w_{f}
is increasing while
{{}_{d}}w_{f}-f
is
d
periodically increasing.

Let
x,y\in I
with
x<y
. If
x,y\in[a,a+d[
then there is nothing to show. For
x\in[a,a+d[
and
y\in[a+d,b]
, the monotonicity follows from the definition of
{{}_{d}}w_{f}
. On the other hand if both
x,y\in[a+d,b]
, then the following inequality yields that
{{}_{d}}w_{f}
is non decreasing

{{}_{d}}w_{f}(x)=\underset{z\in[a,x-d]}{\sup}\overset{x}{\underset{z}{V_{d}}}(f)\leq\underset{z\in[a,y-d]}{\sup}\overset{y}{\underset{z}{V_{d}}}(f)={{}_{d}w}_{f}(y).

To show
{{}_{d}}w_{f}-f
is
d
-periodically increasing, we consider
x,y\in I
such that
y-x\geq d.
This implies
y\in[a+d,b]
and we have to consider consider two cases. For
x\in[a,a+d[
, by definition
{{}_{d}}w_{f}(x)=0
.
On the other hand if
x\in[a+d,b]
then
{{}_{d}}w_{f}(x)=\underset{z\in[a,x-d]}{\sup}\overset{x}{\underset{z}{V_{d}}}(f)
. For both of these cases we can compute the following inequality

{{}_{d}}w_{f}(y)\geq{{}_{d}}w_{f}(x)+|f(y)-f(x)|\geq{{}_{d}}w_{f}(x)+f(y)-f(x).

Rearranging the terms of the above inequality we see that
{{}_{d}}w_{f}-f
is
d
-periodically increasing. This proves the first part of the theorem.

To show the converse assertion, let
g
and
h
are two
d
-periodically increasing functions and hence by Proposition
4.3
, both of these functions satisfies
d
-bounded variation. Therefore, from (
12
), we have that both
g
and
h
are bounded. Thus
g-h
will also be bounded. In other words
g-h
is a function of
d
-bounded variation. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

The occurrence of
d
-periodically increasing function often noticeable in the stock market prices of a reputed company’s share. In most of the cases it varies in a short time frame but eventually it gets a upward trend after a distinct time interval. The similar type of periodical monotonicity is observable in the population growth graph of most East European nations.

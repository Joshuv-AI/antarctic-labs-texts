Ends of large scale groups
Yuankui Ma, Hussain Rashed, Jerzy Dydak

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to unify the theory of ends of finitely generated groups with that of ends of locally compact, metrizable and connected topological groups. In both theories one proves that, if the number of ends is finite, then it must be at most 2 2 . In both theories groups of two ends are characterized as having an infinite cyclic subgroup of either finite index or such that its coset space is compact. Our generalization amounts to defining the space of ends of any coarse space and then applying it to large scale groups, a class of groups generalizing both finitely generated groups and locally compact, metrizable and connected topological groups. Additionally, we prove a version of Svarc-Milnor Lemma for large scale groups and we prove that coarsely hyperbolic large scale groups have finite asymptotic dimension provided they have bounded geometry.

1.
Introduction

Historically, as noted in
[
8
]
on p.287,
ends are the oldest coarse topological notion. Here is their internal description:

Definition 1.1
.

A
Freudenthal end
is a decreasing sequence
(U_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of components of
X\setminus K_{i}
, where
(K_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is an
exhausting sequence
, i.e.

is compact,

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}K_{i}=X.

The set of ends of

is denoted by

.

Ends were used by Freudenthal in 1930 in his famous compactification (see
[
17
]
for information about theorems in this section and see
[
12
]
for results related to the theory of dimension):

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
X
is a
\sigma
-compact, locally compact, connected and locally connected Hausdorff space. It has a compactification
\bar{X}
such that

\bar{X}\setminus X
is of dimension
0
and
\bar{X}
dominates any compactification

\hat{X}
of

whose corona is of dimension
0
.

Notation 1.3
.

Given a
\sigma
-compact, locally compact, connected and locally connected Hausdorff space

. If

, we define the subset
U_{end}:=\{(U_{i})\in Ends(X)|U_{i}\subset U
for some

and

.

The family

is a basis for a topology

on

. The topological space

is a compactification of

called the

. The space of ends
Ends(X)=\bar{X}\setminus X
is of dimension 0, and
\bar{X}
dominates any compactification

\hat{X}
of

whose corona is of dimension
0
. Moreover, the number of ends of

is the supremum of

where

is the number of all mutually disjoint unbounded components of

, for all

.

Initially, ends were useful as properties of topological groups:

Theorem 1.4
.

(Freudenthal) A path connected topological group has at most two ends.

Theorem 1.5
.

(Leo Zippin
[

If a locally compact, metrizable, connected topological group

contains a closed subgroup

is compact; moreover, the space

is the topological product of the axis of reals by a compact connected set homeomorphic to the space

Theorem 1.6
.

(H. Hopf) Let

be a finitely generated discrete group acting on a space

(i) The end space of

(ii) If

Conclusion (ii) suggests to regard the end space of

as an invariant of the group

Definition 1.7
.

Let
p:X\to B
be a covering map with compact base
B
and the group of covering transformations
G
. The

of

is

Ends(G):=Ends(X).

When applied to a Cayley graph of

[
8
]
, p.295). See
[
13
]
for basic results in this theory and see
[
14
]
for more general facts in coarse geometry related to groups.
[
16
]
contains interesting results for ends of finitely generated groups.

In this paper we will define ends of arbitrary countable groups by generalizing the construction of the Higson corona. In the case of coarse spaces we generalize Freudenthal’s method to define their space of ends.

E. Specker
[
18
]
defined ends of arbitrary groups using Stone’s duality theorem. See a very nice paper
[
6
]
of Yves Cornulier describing properties of the space of ends of infinitely generated groups. We consider Specker’s approach highly non-geometric. Additionally, our way of defining ends of spaces leads directly to view them as coronas of certain compactifications (large scale compactifications in case of coarse spaces). A future paper will demonstrate the equivalence of Specker’s definition of ends of groups and our definition of them.

W. Dicks and M. J. Dunwoody
[
1
]
also consider ends of non-finitely generated groups. In particular, they prove the following result that is a generalization of the famous theorem of Stallings

:

Theorem 1.8

A group

has infinitely many ends if and only if
one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

is countably infinite and locally finite,

(i)

can be expressed as an amalgamated free product
A\ast_{C}B
or an HNN extension

, where

and

such that

2.
Ends of coarse spaces

In this section we generalize the concept of Freudenthal ends to arbitrary coarse spaces. See
[
10
]
for other ways to introduce ends in coarse spaces.

We follow a description of coarse spaces (quite often our terminology is that of
large scale spaces
) as in
[
9
]
. It is equivalent to Roe’s definition of those spaces in
[
20
]
.

Recall that a
star

st(x,U)
of
x\in X
with respect to a family
\mathcal{U}
of subsets of
X
is defined as the union of
U\in\mathcal{U}
containing
x
. If
A\subset X
, then
st(A,\mathcal{U}):=\bigcup\limits_{x\in A}st(x,\mathcal{U})
. Given two families
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
of subsets of
X
,

st(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is defined as the family
st(A,\mathcal{V})
,
A\in\mathcal{U}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
large scale space
is a set
X
equipped with a family
\mathbb{LSS}
of covers (called
uniformly bounded
covers) satisfying the following two conditions:

1.

st(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\in\mathbb{LSS}
if
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}\in\mathbb{LSS}
.

2. If
\mathcal{U}\in\mathbb{LSS}
and every element of
\mathcal{V}
is contained in some element of
\mathcal{U}
, then
\mathcal{V}\in\mathbb{LSS}
.

Sets which are contained in an element of
\mathcal{U}\in\mathbb{LSS}
are called
bounded
.

In this paper we consider only large scale spaces that have the
lowest form of coarse connectivity
. Namely, the union of two bounded subsets of
X
is always bounded.

Definition 2.2
.

The subsets
A
and
C
of a large scale space
X
are
coarsely disjoint
if for every uniformly bounded cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
the set
st(A,\mathcal{U})\cap st(C,\mathcal{U})
is bounded.
A
is
coarsely clopen
if
A
and
A^{c}
are coarsely disjoint.

A
non-trivial coarsely clopen
subset
A
of a large scale space
X
(an NCC-set for short) is one that is not bounded and
A^{c}
is not bounded.

Lemma 2.3
.

st(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\mathcal{U})\cap st((A_{1}\cap A_{2})^{c},\mathcal{U})\subset st(A_{1},\mathcal{U})\cap st((A_{1})^{c},\mathcal{U})\cup st(A_{2},\mathcal{U})\cap st((A_{2})^{c},\mathcal{U})
.

Proof.

Suppose
x\in st(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\mathcal{U})\cap st((A_{1}\cap A_{2})^{c},\mathcal{U})
. There is
y\in A_{1}\cap A_{2}
satisfying
x\in st(y,\mathcal{U})

and there is
z\in A_{1}^{c}\cup A_{2}^{c}
satisfying
x\in st(z,\mathcal{U})
.
Thus either
x\in st((A_{1})^{c},\mathcal{U})
or
x\in st((A_{2})^{c},\mathcal{U})

and we are done.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

The intersection of two coarsely clopen subsets of
X
is coarsely clopen.

Proof.

Apply
2.3
. Notice that it is exactly here we need the union of two bounded subsets of
X
be always bounded.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

A topology on
X
is
compatible
with the large scale structure on
X
if there is a uniformly bounded cover of

consisting of open subsets of
X
.

Observation 2.6
.

The simplest non-trivial topology compatible with a large scale structure is the discrete topology.

Definition 2.7
.

A
topological large scale space
is a set equipped with large scale structure and with a compatible topology. Additionally, we assume that the coarse structure is
coarsely connected
, i.e. the union of two bounded subsets of

is bounded.

Example 2.8
.

Every metric space
(X,d)
has a natural topological large scale structure
\mathbb{LSS}_{d}=\{\mathcal{U}_{r}|r>0\}
, where
\mathcal{U}_{r}
is the family of all subsets of
X
whose diameter is at most

. Notice that for any subset

of
X
and any uniformly bounded cover
\mathcal{U}_{r}
, one has

of

is coarsely clopen if one of the following conditions holds:

1. For all

B(A,r)\cap A^{c}
is bounded,

2. For all

3. For all

of

4. For all

Lemma 2.9
.

If
A
is a (non-trivial) coarsely clopen subset of

, then
st(A,\mathcal{U})
is a (non-trivial) coarsely clopen subset of

for each uniformly bounded cover
\mathcal{U}
of

Proof.

Observe that if
A
is a non-trivial coarsely clopen subset of
X
, then
C:=(st(A,\mathcal{U}))^{c}
cannot be bounded. Indeed, in that case

would be bounded.

Notice
st(st(A,\mathcal{U}),\mathcal{V})\subset st(A,st(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))
for any two covers
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}

Therefore
st(st(A,\mathcal{U}),\mathcal{V})\cap st(st(A^{c},\mathcal{U}),\mathcal{V})\subset st(A,st(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))\cap st(A^{c},st(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))

∎

Lemma 2.10
.

If
A
is a coarsely clopen subset of
X
, then a subset

of

for some uniformly bounded cover

of

Proof.

Observe

Let

3.
Comparison of coarse ends and Freundenthal ends

In this section we are concentrating on a relationship between Freudenthal ends and coarse ends. More specifically, we are interested in cases where there is a one-to-one correspondence between those ends in the following sense: each Freudenthal end is contained in a unique coarse end and each coarse end contains a Freudenthal end. To accomplish it, we need a large scale analog of local connectedness. Also, we need to generalize Freundenthal ends to non-locally compact spaces. See
[
7
]
for a theory of Freundenthal compactifications for general topological spaces.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose
X
is a locally connected topological space that is a union of an increasing sequence of closed subspaces
\{K_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}

such that
K_{i}\subset int(K_{i+1})
for each
i\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be the bornology generated by
\{K_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
, i.e. all subsets

of

such that

for some

.
A

of

with respect to

is a decreasing sequence
\{C_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
, where each
C_{i}
is a non-empty component of

.

Notice each
C_{i}
is unbounded as otherwise it is contained in some
K_{k}
,
k>i
, and
C_{k}\subset C_{i}\cap(X\setminus K_{k})=\emptyset
, a contradiction.

Definition 3.2
.

A large scale space

is

if there is a uniformly bounded cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
such that every uniformly bounded cover
\mathcal{V}
of

is a refinement of a uniformly bounded cover
\mathcal{W}
that consists of
\mathcal{U}
-connected sets (that means any two points

can be connected by a chain of points

with the property that for any

there is

containing both

and
x_{i+1}
).

Definition 3.3
.

Suppose
X
is a large scale space that is large scale chain-connected via a uniformly bounded cover
\mathcal{U}
and the bornology
\mathcal{B}
of

has an increasing sequence
\{B_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
of bounded subsets of

that serves as a basis of it. A
Freundenthal end
of
X
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
is a decreasing sequence
\{C_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
, where each
C_{i}
is a non-empty
\mathcal{U}
-component of

.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose

is a large scale space that is large scale chain-connected via a uniformly bounded cover
\mathcal{U}
and has the property that for any bounded subset

of

the union of all bounded

-components of

-components of

Proof.

Claim 1:

\mathcal{U}
-components of

for some bounded subset

of

, then

is coarsely clopen.

Proof of Claim 1

of

-connected subsets of

Proof of

4.
Large scale groups

In this section we introduce the concept of a large scale group that generalizes the following classes of groups:

1. Finitely generated groups with word metrics,

2. Countable groups with proper left-invariant metrics,

3. Locally compact topological groups.

Ideally, the name of those groups should be coarse groups but it has been already used in literature for similar but different objects (see
[
15
]
).

See
[
5
]
for a discussion of large scale structures on a group induced by right-invariant metrics versus left-invariant metrics.

Recall that a
bornology
on a set
X
is a cover of
X
that is stable under inclusion and is stable under finite unions.

Definition 4.1
.

A
large scale group
is a group

equipped with a large scale structure
\mathcal{LSS}
induced by a bornology
\mathcal{B}
. That means every uniformly bounded cover in
\mathcal{LSS}
is a refinement of

for some

.

Lemma 4.2
.

A bornology
\mathcal{B}
on a group

induces a large scale structure on

if and only if
\mathcal{B}
is stable under inverses and products.

Proof.

Notice
\mathcal{B}
is stable under inverses and products if and only if for all non-empty

the set
(B_{1}\cdot B_{2}^{-1})\cdot B_{2}
belongs to
\mathcal{B}
.

Let
\mathcal{U}:=\{g\cdot B_{2}\}_{g\in G}
. Notice
(B_{1}\cdot B_{2}^{-1})\cdot B_{2}=\bigcup\limits_{g\in G}B_{1}\cap(g\cdot B_{2})=st(B_{1},\mathcal{U})
.
Conversely, if
B_{3}:=\bigcup\limits_{g\in G}B_{1}\cap(g\cdot B_{2})\in\mathcal{B}
,
then
st(h\cdot B_{1},\mathcal{U})\subset h\cdot B_{3}
for all
h\in G
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Large scale groups include the following classes of groups:

1. Finitely generated groups with word metrics,

2. Countable groups with proper left-invariant metrics,

3. Locally compact topological groups.

Proof.

In cases 1) and 2) the bornologies consist of all finite subsets of
G
.

In case 3) the bornology
\mathcal{B}
consist of all subsets of compact sets in
G
. Indeed, given
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
the set

(B_{1}\cdot B_{2}^{-1})\cdot B_{2}
is pre-compact (its closure is compact).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Given a subgroup
H
of a group

the restriction of a cover

to

is a refinement of the cover
\{h\cdot((B^{-1}\cdot B)\cap H)\}_{h\in H}
of

.

Proof.

Since

is non-empty, so is

. For each

such that

pick

.
If

,
then

and

, so

.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Given a subgroup
H
of a large scale group

the induced large scale on

equals the large scale generated by the restriction of the bornology of
G
to

.

.

See
[

for a discussion of asymptotic dimension

Choose a uniformly bounded cover

8.

In this section we introduce the concept of bounded geometry for large scale groups. Typically, bounded geometry is defined for metric spaces by requiring that for each

such that every

Definition 8.1
.

A large scale space

if it is coarsely equivalent to a large scale space

with the property that for each uniformly bounded cover
\mathcal{U}
of

.

Suppose

and

are large scale spaces and

has bounded geometry. If there is a coarse embedding

, then

Proof.

Pick a coarse equivalence

, where

has the property that for each uniformly bounded cover
\mathcal{U}
of

there is
N(\mathcal{U})\in\mathbb{N}
such that every element of
\mathcal{U}
contains at most
N(\mathcal{U})
elements. Observe that

is coarsely equivalent to

.
∎

Proposition 8.3

A large scale group

of

there are elements

Proof.

Suppose

\mathcal{U}
of

Suppose there is a bounded set

such that for every bounded subset

of

there are elements

,

, so that

of

Definition 9.1

Proposition 9.2

Suppose the bornology of a large scale group

If

Proof.

Express

is a union of a strictly increasing sequence

To show

i\geq 1
. We will show that there exists a strictly increasing sequence
H_{n}
of subgroups of
G
satisfying the following conditions:

1.
H_{1}
is infinite cyclic,

2.
H_{n}
is of bounded index in
H_{n+1}
for each
n\geq 1
,

3.
G
is the union of all
H_{n}
,
n\geq 1
.

Given a bounded subset
B
of
G
we can find using
9.4
a boundedly generated subgroup
H_{B}
of
G

containing
B
such that both
A\cap H_{B}
and
A^{c}\cap H_{B}
are NCC sets in
H_{B}
.
By the first case,
H_{B}
has an infinite cyclic subgroup of bounded index. Call this group
H_{1}
.

In particular, if we construct two subgroups

H_{B}\subset H_{B^{\prime}}
that way, then
H_{B}
is of bounded index in
H_{B^{\prime}}
. To this end, notice that
H_{B^{\prime}}
has an infinite cyclic subgroup
H^{\prime}_{1}
of bounded index. Since
H_{1}
and
H^{\prime}_{1}
are infinite cyclic subgroups of
H_{B^{\prime}}
and
H^{\prime}_{1}
of bounded index in
H_{B^{\prime}}
, by
9.12
,
H_{1}
must be of bounded index in
H_{B^{\prime}}
and hence
H_{B}
is of bounded index in
H_{B^{\prime}}
. Using these facts it is easy to construct the required sequence
H_{n}
of subgroups of
G
.

A
cannot be contained in any
H_{m}
. Indeed, suppose there is
m\geq 1
such that

A\subset H_{m}
and choose
z\in H_{m+1}\setminus H_{m}
. On one hand, if
A\cap A\cdot z\neq\emptyset
, then
z\in A^{-1}\cdot A\subset H_{m}
, a contradiction. On the other hand, if
A\cap A\cdot z=\emptyset
, then
A\Delta(A\cdot z)=A\cup A\cdot z
which is unbounded, a contradiction.

A\cap H_{1}
and
A^{c}\cap H_{1}
are both unbounded. Let
t
be a generator of
H_{1}
. Since both
A\Delta(A\cdot t)
and
A^{c}\Delta(A^{c}\cdot t)
are bounded,
there is
k>m
such that both these sets are contained in
H_{k}
.
Given
x\in A\cap H_{k+1}\setminus H_{k}
, then
x\cdot t\notin H_{k}
. One has
x\cdot t\in A
as otherwise
x\cdot t\in A\Delta(A\cdot t)\subset H_{k}
. Consequently,
x\cdot t^{n}\in A
for all integers
n
.
As
G
acts trivially on
A
,
x^{-1}\cdot A\setminus A
is bounded.
That implies
H_{1}\cap A^{c}
is bounded, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 9.14
.

Let
G
be a locally compact
\sigma
-compact topological group with bornology consisting of pre-compact subsets. If
G
is not compactly generated, then either
G
is 1-ended or it has infinitely many ends.

Theorem 9.15
.

If

is a large scale group of bounded geometry with

1.

is boundedly generated of bounded geometry and has
2

2.

is boundedly generated of bounded geometry,
\sigma

Proof.

1)
\implies

\implies

.

∎

Theorem 9.16
.

Let
G
be a coarse group whose bornology consists of all finite subsets. If
G
has
2

is finitely generated. Therefore it contains an infinite cyclic subgroup of finite index.

Proof.

G
has a subgroup

of finite index that has
2
ends and
G_{1}
acts on its ends trivially. If
G_{1}
is countable, then we are done, so assume it is uncountable.
Given a countable subgroup
G_{2}
of

, a non-trivial coarsely clopen subset on which

acts trivially. Let

and choose infinite countable subsets
C_{i}
of

,

. The group generated by

has two ends since the countable group
<G_{2}\cup C_{1}\cup C_{2}>

, by

, it is finitely generated. That means every countable subgroup

is contained in a finitely generated subgroup

.

∎

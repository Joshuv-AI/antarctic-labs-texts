Universality of periodic points in bounded discrete time series
Chikara Nakayama, Tsuyoshi Yoneda

Abstract
. We consider arbitrary bounded discrete time series originating from dynamical system. Without any use of the Fourier transform, we find periodic points which suitably characterizes (i.e. independent of Lyapunov exponent) the corresponding time series. In particular, bounded discrete time series generated by the autoregressive model (without the white noise) is equivalent to a quasi periodic function.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider universality of periodic points from algebraic point of view.
More precisely,
without any use of the Fourier transform,
we show that bounded discrete time series originating from
dynamical system
can be characterized by (i.e. independent of Lyapunov exponent) periodic points.
This characterization might be helpful for the further study of “chaos” (see
[
5
, Chapter 15.4]
),
since the definition of it requires existence of a dense set of periodic points in the corresponding domain.
Furthermore, we point out that bounded discrete time series generated by the autoregressive (AR) model is
equivalent to a quasi periodic function.
Let us formulate them more precisely.
For
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
let
\Phi:[-1,1]^{L}\to[-1,1]^{L}
(
(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{L})^{T}\mapsto(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{L})^{T}
) be such that

(1)

w_{1}=x_{2},\quad w_{2}=x_{3},\cdots,w_{L-1}=x_{L}

(i.e. dynamical system in a delay coordinate, see
[
7
]
).
By using this
\Phi
, we generate a time series
y:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-L+1}\to[-1,1]
as follows:

Y_{t}=\underbrace{\Phi\circ\Phi\circ\cdots\circ\Phi}_{t\ \text{times}}(Y_{0}),

where

Y_{t}:=(y(t),y(t-1),\cdots,y(t-L+1))^{T}

and
Y_{0}
is the initial data.
From this
\Phi
, we define
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
to be the maximum Lyapunov exponent, and assume it is finite, as follows:

e^{\lambda}:=\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle W,W^{\prime}\in[-1,1]^{L},}}{{W\not=W^{\prime}}}}\frac{|\Phi(W)-\Phi(W^{\prime})|}{|W-W^{\prime}|}<\infty.

Now we briefly explain the typical models of the dynamical system
\Phi
, that is, functional-coefficient regression models (see
[
1
]
) without the white noise, as follows:

•

AR model:

(2)

y(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}p_{\ell}y(t-\ell)

for
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
\{p_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}\subset\mathbb{R}
,

•

FAR model:

(3)

y(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}p_{\ell}(Y)y(t-\ell)

for
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,

p_{\ell}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
(
\ell=1,2,\cdots,L
) and

Y=(y(t-i_{1}),y(t-i_{2})\cdots,y(t-i_{k}))
with
\{i_{k^{\prime}}\}_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

•

EXPAR model (here we describe the simplest case, see
[
6
]
)

(4)

y(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\left\{\alpha_{\ell}+\exp(-\beta_{\ell}(y(t-1))^{2})\right\}y(t-\ell)

for
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
\{\alpha_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
,
\{\beta_{\ell}\}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}
.

These models have been applied extensively. See
[
2
,
4
]
for example.
However, as far as the authors are aware, none of the numerous works to date
attempted to investigate the universality of periodic points of the functional-coefficient regression models,
and in this paper we initiate it.
The main theorem is as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
y:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-L+1}\to[-1,1]
be a time series generated by
\Phi:[-1,1]^{L}\to[-1,1]^{L}
(
Y_{t-1}\mapsto Y_{t}
) satisfying (
1
) with nonnegative Lyapunov exponent
\lambda\geq 0
.
Then
there exist
approximating functions:
\{y^{*}_{K}\}_{K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}
, constants:
\{L_{K}\}_{K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
\{T_{K}\}_{K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
M_{K}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

such that

y^{*}_{K}(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{M_{K}}a_{m}\sin\left(\frac{mt}{L_{K}}\right)+b_{m}\cos\left(\frac{mt}{L_{K}}\right)\quad(\{a_{m}\}_{m},\{b_{m}\}_{m}\subset\mathbb{R})

for
t\geq T_{K}
and

(5)

\displaystyle|y^{*}_{K}(t)-y(t)|\leq(2t+1)e^{\lambda t}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\quad\text{for}\quad t\geq-L+1.

Note that
\lambda
is independent of
K
.

Remark 1
.

We can provide a conditional characterization of the periodic points.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{K,K^{\prime}}
be the least common multiple of
L_{K}
and
L_{K^{\prime}}
.
For
\{K_{j}\}_{j}
(
K_{1}<K_{2}<\cdots<K_{j}<\cdots
),
we re-select
T_{K_{1}},T_{K_{2}},\cdots,T_{K_{j}},\cdots
such that

T_{K_{1}}\leq T_{K_{2}}\leq\cdots\leq T_{K_{j}}\leq\cdots\quad\text{and}\quad T_{K_{j+1}}-T_{K_{j}}\ \text{is divisible by}\ L_{K_{j}}.

If

(6)

\sum_{j}(2T_{K_{j+1}}+2\mathcal{L}_{K_{j+1},K_{j}}+1)\frac{e^{\lambda(T_{K_{j+1}}+\mathcal{L}_{K_{j+1},K_{j}})}}{K_{j}}<^{\exists}\!\!C^{*}<\infty,

then there exists an almost periodic function
ap(t)

such that the following convergence holds:

(7)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{L}_{K_{j+1},K_{j}}}|y(t+T_{K_{j}})-ap(t)|\to 0\quad(j\to\infty).

Indeed,

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\geq 0}|y^{*}_{K_{j+1}}(t+T_{K_{j+1}})-y^{*}_{K_{j}}(t+T_{K_{j}})|\\
=&\sup_{t\geq 0}|y^{*}_{K_{j+1}}(t+T_{K_{j+1}})-y^{*}_{K_{j}}(t+T_{K_{j+1}})|\\
=&\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{L}_{{K_{j+1},K_{j}}}}|y^{*}_{K_{j+1}}(t+T_{K_{j+1}})-y^{*}_{K_{j}}(t+T_{K_{j+1}})|\\
\leq&\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{L}_{{K_{j+1},K_{j}}}}|y^{*}_{K_{j+1}}(t+T_{K_{j+1}})-y(t+T_{K_{j+1}})|\\
&\qquad+\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{L}_{K_{j+1},K_{j}}}|y^{*}_{K_{j}}(t+T_{K_{j+1}})-y(t+T_{K_{j+1}})|\\
&\leq(2T_{K_{j+1}}+2\mathcal{L}_{K_{j+1},K_{j}}+1)e^{\lambda(T_{K_{j+1}}+\mathcal{L}_{K_{j+1},K_{j}})}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K_{j+1}}\\
&\qquad+(2T_{K_{j+1}}+2\mathcal{L}_{K_{j+1},K_{j}}+1)e^{\lambda(T_{K_{j+1}}+\mathcal{L}_{K_{j+1},K_{j}})}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K_{j}}\\
\end{split}

due to (
5
).
By (
6
),

\{y^{*}_{K_{j}}(\cdot+T_{K_{j}})\}_{j}
is uniform convergence on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
and by Corduneanu
[
3
, (1.2)]
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}|y^{*}_{K_{j}}(t+T_{K_{j}})-ap(t)|\to 0\quad(j\to\infty).

Combining the following convergence:

\begin{split}&\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{L}_{K_{j+1},K_{j}}}|y(t+T_{K_{j}})-y^{*}_{K_{j}}(t+T_{K_{j}})|\\
=&\sup_{T_{K_{j}}\leq t\leq T_{K_{j}}+\mathcal{L}_{K_{j+1},K_{j}}}|y(t)-y^{*}_{K_{j}}(t)|\to 0\quad(j\to\infty),\end{split}

we have the desired (
7
).

Proposition 2
.

Furthermore,
if
\Phi
is the AR model (
2
), we see that

y\equiv ap
. It is an open question whether
this
ap
coincides with
ap
that is in (
7
) (or, before that, whether the condition (
6
) holds).

See Appendix
3
.

2.
Proof of main theorem

First let us discretize the range
[-1,1]
as follows:
For
K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we choose
\{a_{k}^{K}\}_{k=0}^{K+1}\subset[-1,1]
such that

•

-1=a_{0}^{K}<a_{1}^{K}<a_{2}^{K}<\cdots<a_{K+1}^{K}=1
,

•

\sup_{1\leq k\leq K+1}|a_{k-1}^{K}-a_{k}^{K}|\leq^{\exists}\!\!C/K
.

We now discretize
y
as follows:

(8)

\bar{y}_{K}(t):=\argmin_{a\in\{a^{K}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}}|y(t)-(a-0)|,

where
a-0:=a-\varepsilon
for any sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
We classify patterns in
\bar{y}
.
Let
\sigma_{n}
(
n=1,2,\cdots,N
) be a permutation operator, namely,

\sigma_{n}^{K}:\{1,2,\cdots,L\}\to\{a_{1}^{K},a_{2}^{K},\cdots,a_{K-1}^{K},a_{K}^{K}\}

(
\ell\mapsto\sigma_{n}^{K}(\ell)
) with
\sigma_{n}^{K}\not=\sigma_{n^{\prime}}^{K}
(
n\not=n^{\prime}
),
and we impose the following two conditions for determining
N
:

(9)

\begin{cases}\text{For any $t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-L+1}$, there is $n\in\{1,\cdots,N\}$ such that}\\
\qquad\text{$\sigma_{n}^{K}(\ell)=\bar{y}_{K}(t-\ell)$ for $\ell=1,2,\cdots,L$},\\
\text{For any $n\in\{1,\cdots,N\}$ there is $t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-L+1}$ such that}\\
\qquad\text{$\sigma_{n}^{K}(\ell)=\bar{y}_{K}(t-\ell)$ for $\ell=1,2,\cdots,L$.}\end{cases}

Note that
N\leq K^{L}
due to the sequence with repetition.
We define a next value
a_{k(n)}^{K}
of
\sigma_{n}^{K}
as follows:
For any
K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
n\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
, we choose a
k(n)\in\{1,2,\cdots,K\}
such that

(10)

a_{k(n)}^{K}=\bar{y}_{K}(t)\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{n}^{K}(\ell)=\bar{y}_{K}(t-\ell)\quad(\ell=1,2,\cdots,L)

for some
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-L+1}
.
Now we construct approximating functions

\{y^{*}_{K}(t)\}_{t\geq-L+1}
inductively
from initial data
\{\bar{y}_{K}(1-\ell)\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
.
First, let

y^{*}_{K}(1-\ell)=\bar{y}_{K}(1-\ell)\quad\text{for}\quad\ell=1,2,\cdots,L.

We now apply the induction argument. Assume that, for
t(>-L+1)
,

y^{*}_{K}(t-\ell)

(1\leq\ell\leq L)
is already defined. Then
there is
t^{\prime}
such that

(11)

y^{*}_{K}(t-\ell)=\bar{y}_{K}(t^{\prime}-\ell)\quad\text{for}\quad\ell=1,2,\cdots,L.

Then by (
9
),
there exists
n_{t^{\prime}}\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\}

such that

(12)

\bar{y}_{K}(t^{\prime}-\ell)=\sigma^{K}_{n_{t^{\prime}}}(\ell)\quad\text{for}\quad\ell=1,2,\cdots,L,

and by (
10
), we generate the next value
y^{*}_{K}(t)
such that

y^{*}_{K}(t):=a^{K}_{k(n_{t^{\prime}})}.

This means that, there exists
t^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

y^{*}_{K}(t-\ell)=\bar{y}_{K}(t^{\prime\prime}-\ell)\quad\text{for}\quad\ell=0,1,2,\cdots,L-1,

and the condition (
11
) holds also for the
t+1
case.
Therefore the induction argument goes through, and then we obtain the desired sequence

\{y^{*}_{K}(t)\}_{t=-L+1}^{\infty}
.
Next we show (
5
).
Let

\begin{split}\overline{Y}_{t}&:=(\bar{y}_{K}(t),\bar{y}_{K}(t-1),\cdots,\bar{y}_{K}(t-L+1))^{T},\\
Y^{*}_{t}&:=(y^{*}_{K}(t),y^{*}_{K}(t-1),\cdots,y^{*}_{K}(t-L+1))^{T}.\end{split}

Then we see that, for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-L+1}
, there exists
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-L+1}
such that

(13)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{t^{\prime}}=Y^{*}_{t},\quad|Y_{t^{\prime}}-Y^{*}_{t}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K},\\
Y_{t^{\prime}}=\Phi(Y_{t^{\prime}-1}),\\
\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{t^{\prime}-1}=Y^{*}_{t-1},\quad|Y_{t^{\prime}-1}-Y^{*}_{t-1}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}.\end{cases}

Recall
e^{\lambda}\geq 1
, and then we have

\begin{split}|y^{*}_{K}(t)-y(t)|\leq&|Y^{*}_{t}-Y_{t}|\\
\leq&|Y_{t^{\prime}}-Y_{t}|+\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\\
\leq&e^{\lambda}|Y_{t^{\prime}-1}-Y_{t-1}|+\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\\
\leq&e^{\lambda}|Y^{*}_{t-1}-Y_{t-1}|+e^{\lambda}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}+\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\\
\leq&e^{\lambda}|Y^{*}_{t-1}-Y_{t-1}|+2e^{\lambda}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\leq\cdots\\
\leq&e^{\lambda t}\left|Y^{*}_{0}-Y_{0}\right|+2te^{\lambda t}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\\
\leq&(2t+1)e^{\lambda t}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}.\end{split}

Therefore we have (
5
).
Now we show that
y^{*}_{K}(t)
is a discretized periodic function on
t\geq T_{K}
for some
T_{K}
.
By the construction of
y^{*}_{K}
,
for each
K
, we have a chain of the pair of permutation operator and the corresponding next value:
(\sigma_{j}^{K},a_{k(j)}^{K})
as follows:

(\sigma_{j_{1}}^{K},a_{k(j_{1})}^{K})\rightarrow(\sigma_{j_{2}}^{K},a_{k(j_{2})}^{K})\rightarrow(\sigma_{j_{3}}^{K},a_{k(j_{3})}^{K})\rightarrow\cdots.

Since the number of permutation operators
\{\sigma_{n}^{K}\}_{n=1}^{N}
is finite,
then there exist

m,M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
(
m<M
) such that
(\sigma_{j_{m}}^{K},a_{k(j_{m})}^{K})=(\sigma_{j_{M}}^{K},a_{k(j_{M})}^{K})
.
This means that there exists
T_{K}
such that
y^{*}_{K}(t)
is a periodic chain for
t\geq T_{K}
.
This is the desired result.

3.
Appendix

Let
L\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
In this appendix, we focus on the AR model (
2
).
First we figure out the corresponding characteristic equation, that is,
we plug the following:

y(t-\ell)=\mu^{L-\ell},\quad(\mu\in\mathbb{R},\ \ell=0,1,\cdots,L)

into (
2
).
After factorization of the
L
-th degree polynomial, then, we see that
\{p_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
satisfies the following equality (at least we can easily figure out that
p_{L}=-1
):

(14)

\prod_{j=1}^{L/2}(\mu-e^{i\xi_{j}})(\mu-e^{-i\xi_{j}})=\mu^{L}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}p_{\ell}\mu^{L-\ell}=0

if and only if
y
is a quasi-periodic function:

(15)

y(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{L/2}a_{j}\sin\left(\xi_{j}(t-b_{j})\right),\quad t\in\mathbb{Z}

for
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{L/2}
,
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{L/2}
,
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{L/2}\subset\mathbb{R}
.
We emphasize that all absolute values of the roots of the polynomial (
14
) are
1
.
To the contrary, if
\{p_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
does not satisfy (
14
), the time series is not bounded (exponentially growing or decaying).

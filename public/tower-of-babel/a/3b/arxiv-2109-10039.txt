On Reid’s recipe for non abelian groups
Álvaro Nolla de Celis

Abstract
. This paper presents two new explicit examples of Reid’s recipe for non-abelian groups in SL ⁡ ( 3 , ℂ ) \SL(3,\mathbb{C}) , namely the dihedral group 𝔻 5 , 2 \mathbb{D}_{5,2} and a trihedral group of order 39.



1.
Introduction

If
G
is a finite subgroup of
\SL(2,\mathbb{C})
, the
classical McKay correspondence
establish a one to one bijection between non-trivial irreducible representations of
G
and the irreducible components of the exceptional locus
E
in the minimal resolution
Y
of the du Val singularity
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
. It is well known that in this case the exceptional locus
E\subset Y
consists of a chain of rational curves
E_{i}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
E_{i}^{2}=-2
and intersection graph of ADE type.

On the other hand, McKay
[
14
]
observed that if we take the set of irreducible representations of
G
to be
\Irr G=\{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{n}\}
and
V:G\to\SL(2,\mathbb{C})
the natural representation, then the
McKay graph
formed by a vertex for each
\rho\in\Irr G
and
a_{ij}
edges between
\rho_{i}
and
\rho_{j}
such that
V\otimes\rho_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{ij}\rho_{j}
, has the same (extended) ADE type. Thus, the correspondence can be stated as the bijection:

(1)

\{
Irreducible components
E_{i}\subset Y\}\longleftrightarrow\{
Non-trivial
\rho_{i}\in\Irr G\}

Remark 1
.

The choice of notation
\rho_{0}^{\pm}
and
\rho_{2}^{\pm}
comes from the fact that irreducible representations of
D_{4}
can be induced from the irreducible representations of the cyclic group
A_{4}=\left<\alpha\right>
. In this group
\Irr A_{4}=\{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}\}
where
\rho_{i}(\alpha)=i^{k}
for
i=1,\ldots,3
. The action of
D_{4}/A_{4}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
on
\Irr A_{4}
fixes
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{2}
, producing
\rho_{0}^{\pm}
and
\rho_{2}^{\pm}
, and the orbit
\{\rho_{1},\rho_{3}\}
produces the 2-dimensional representation
\rho_{1}
. Similar situation and notation is present in the examples of Sections
3
and
4
.

In this sense, we can assign a non-trivial irreducible representation
\rho\in\Irr G
to every component
E_{\rho}\subset Y
in the minimal resolution, so the McKay correspondence reflects how the geometry of
Y
is determined by the representation theory of
G
.

The geometric interpretation of the correspondence was done by Gonzalez-Springer and Verdier in
[
10
]
as follows: for each nontrivial
\rho_{i}\in\Irr G
there is a vector bundle
\mathcal{R}_{i}
on
Y
such that

(2)

\deg(c_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{i}|_{E_{j}}))=\delta_{ij},\hskip 28.45274ptj=1,\ldots,n.

The bundles
\mathcal{R}_{i}
can be constructed in the following way. From the work of Ito and Nakamura
[
12
]
it is know that the
G
-equivariant Hilbert scheme
G\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, the fine moduli space of scheme-theoretic orbits or
G
-clusters
, is the minimal resolution
Y
. Recall that a
G
-cluster
is a
G
-invariant 0-dimensional subscheme
\mathcal{Z}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}}\cong\mathbb{C}[G]
the regular representation as
G
-modules. The interpretation of
Y\cong G\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
as a moduli space allows us to consider the tautological bundle
\mathcal{R}
on
Y
, where the fibre over a point
y\in Y
is
H^{0}(Z(y),\mathcal{O}_{Z(y)})
where
Z(y)
is the orbit parametrised by
y
. By definition of
G
-cluster
H^{0}(Z(y),\mathcal{O}_{Z(y)})\cong\mathbb{C}[G]\cong\bigoplus_{\rho\in\Irr G}\rho^{\dim\rho}
. This fact imply that
\mathcal{R}
decomposes as a direct sum of globally generated vector bundles:

\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{R}_{i}\otimes\rho_{i}

where
\mathcal{R}_{i}=\Hom_{G}(\rho_{k},\mathcal{R})
are the
tautological bundles
of
Y
.

Notice that
\mathcal{R}_{i}\in\Pic Y
, and
\mathcal{R}_{0}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
generates
H^{0}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
. Also, since
E_{i}
for
i>0
form a basis of
H_{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
, dual to a basis of
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
, we can use the Gonzalez-Springer and Verdier result to state the
integral McKay correspondence
as the bijection:

(3)

\{
Irreducible representations of
G\}\longleftrightarrow
basis of
H^{*}(Y,\mathbb{Z})

Reid
[
16
]
conjectured that statement (
3
) should hold for any finite subgroup
G\subset\SL(n,\mathbb{C})
as long as a crepant resolution
\pi:Y\to\mathbb{C}^{n}/G
exists. Under this circumstances, the (tautological) bundles
\mathcal{R}_{\rho}
form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of the
K
-theory of
Y
, and certain cookery with the Chern classes
c_{i}(\mathcal{R}_{\rho})
would lead to a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
H^{*}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.

When
n=3
it is known that there always exists such a resolution. In fact, by
[
2
]
it is know that
Y=G\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
is a crepant resolution of
\mathbb{C}^{3}/G
for any finite subgroup of
\SL(3,\mathbb{C})
. By definition, a
G
-cluster contains all the information of the representation theory of
G
, so even though crepant resolutions are no longer unique the
G\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
is in this sense “distinguished”, and becomes a suitable resolution to test the conjecture.

For
G=A\subset\SL(3,\mathbb{C})
an abelian subgroup, Reid
[
16
]
verified the correspondence (
3
) with a series of examples defining a decoration or
marking
of the junior simplex of the toric resolution
Y=A\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
which leads to (i) the relations between the tautological bundles
\mathcal{R}_{\rho}
in
\Pic Y
inducing a basis for
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
, and (ii) the definition of certain virtual bundles
\mathcal{F}_{E}
for each compact surface
E\subset Y
whose second Chern class
c_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{E})
form a basis of
H^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
. The marking and cookery is the so called
Reid’s recipe
. The extension to every abelian subgroup in
SL(3,\mathbb{C})
was completed by Craw
[
3
]
, which can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1

(
[
3
]
)
.

The conjecture
3
holds for any finite abelian subgroup
A\subset\SL(3,\mathbb{C})
and
Y=A\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
.

The correspondence can be described precisely as follows:

•

\mathcal{R}_{\rho_{0}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
generates
H^{0}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.

•

\{[c_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\rho})]\}
such that
\rho
either marks a curve in
Y
or is the second representation marking a del Pezzo surface
dP_{6}\subset Y
forms a basis of
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.

•

\{[c_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{E})]\}
such that
E\subset Y
is a compact surface forms a basis of
H^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.

See Example
2.1
for explicit computations.

Using the language of derived categories, a generalized version of the correspondence was introduced by Cautis-Logvinenko
[
8
]
and completed in Cautis-Craw-Logvinenko
[
7
]
for
A\subset\SL(3,\mathbb{C})
, giving rise to the so called
derived Reid’s recipe
. This interpretation also remains open for the non-abelian case. Finally, for the dimer model case, which covers every three-dimensional toric Gorenstein singularity, the recipe has been studied in
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
.

There are very few examples of this explicit correspondence when the group
G
is non-abelian, mainly due to the lack of examples of explicit descriptions of crepant resolutions away from the toric case. One can find the case of finite
G\subset\SO(3)
in
[
15
]
, where
\mathbb{C}^{3}\text{-Hilb}(G)
is computed. In this case the exceptional fibre
\pi^{-1}(0)
is 1-dimensional, so the exceptional locus does not contain compact divisors, and there is a one-to-one correspondence between (non-trivial) irreducible exceptional curves in
E
and (non-trivial) irreducible representations of
G
. With the presence of compact divisors, Gomi-Nakamura-Shinoda
[
9
]
computed the exceptional locus of
G\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
for
G_{60}
and
G_{168}
, the only two non-abelian simple groups in
\SL(3,\mathbb{C})
. The resolution of
\mathbb{C}^{3}/G_{60}
has 1-dimensional fiber over
\pi^{-1}(0)
whereas for
G_{168}
consists of a smooth rational curve and a doubly blown-up projective plane intersecting transversally at a unique point.

In this paper we include the explicit description for two new non-abelian examples in
\SL(3,\mathbb{C})
, namely the dihedral group
\mathbb{D}_{5,2}
(Section
3
) and the trihedral group
\left<\frac{1}{13}(1,3,9),T\right>
(Section
4
), in order to define an analogue of Reid’s recipe and check the correspondence (
3
) on them. Although there are some differences from the abelian case, most of the features and spirit of the recipe seem to hold. Both examples are contained in two interesting families of subgroups of
\SL(3,\mathbb{C})
, and may constitute the first step to generalize Reid’s recipe to this cases.

2.
Marking the exceptional locus

The
marking
of the irreducible components of the exceptional locus
E\subset Y=G\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
for
G\subset\SL(3,\mathbb{C})
finite is done as follows. Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
be an open cover of
Y
, and denote by
r_{i,\rho}
the basis of sections of
\mathcal{R}_{\rho}
at
U_{i}
(there are
\dim(\rho)
elements in
r_{i,\rho}
). Note that
r_{i,\rho}\in S_{\rho}
. Then
\mathcal{Z}_{i}=\{r_{i,\rho}~|~\rho\in\Irr G\}
form a basis for any
G
-cluster in
U_{i}
, also known as a
G
-graph
.

Let
C\subset Y
be a rational curve. Define the submodule
G
-igMod
{}_{C}(U_{i}):=\{r_{i,\rho}~|~\deg(\mathcal{R}_{i}|_{C})\neq 0\}
of
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
. This submodule contains the
G
-igsaw transformation
along the curve
C
among the basis elements in
\mathcal{R}
. Now, the curve
C\subset Y
is covered by two open sets,
C\subset U_{i}\cap U_{j}
and we say that

\rho
marks
C\iff r_{\alpha,\rho}
generates
G
-igMod
{}_{C}(U_{\alpha})
for
\alpha=i
or
\alpha=j

Let
D\subset Y
be a compact divisor, and let
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be an open covering of
D
. Then

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\text{$\rho$ marks $D$}&\iff&\text{$r_{i,\rho}$ is in the socle of $\mathcal{Z}_{i}$ at the }\\
&&\text{origin of $U_{i}$, for $i=1,\ldots,k$}\end{array}

Remark 2
.

Notice that it may happen that a curve
C
is marked by two representations (see the curve
C_{1}
in Example
4

[

{}_{C}(U_{i})

Proposition 1
.

Let
G=\left<\frac{1}{12}(1,7,4),\beta\right>\subset\SL(3,\mathbb{C})
,
\pi:Y=G\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{3})\to\mathbb{C}^{3}
crepant and let
\{\mathcal{R}_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in\Irr G}
be the set of tautological bundles of
Y
. Then,

(a)

The following relations hold in
\Pic Y
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{-}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{-}_{8}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{-}_{6}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{-}_{2}

\displaystyle\det\mathcal{R}_{5}=\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}\otimes\det\mathcal{R}_{9}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{+}_{8}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{+}_{6}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{+}_{2}

(b)

The one-to-one correspondence (
3
) holds, that is, there exists a bijection between irreducible representations of
G
and a basis of
H^{*}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

(a)
The statement comes directly from Table
8
. It can be computed that the five relations hold in every open set
U_{i}\subset G\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
.

(b)
We know that
\mathcal{R}_{\rho}
, for non-trivial
\rho\in\Irr G
, generates
\Pic Y
but the relations in
(a)
allows us to reduce the list of generators to 9 by removing from the list
\mathcal{R}^{-}_{0}
,
\mathcal{R}^{+}_{8}
,
\mathcal{R}^{-}_{6}
,
\mathcal{R}^{+}_{6}
and
\mathcal{R}_{5}
. There are 5 relations in
(a)
, precisely the number of compact divisors in
Y
. By
[
13
]
we know that
\rk\Pic Y=|\Irr G\backslash\{\rho_{0}^{+}\}|-5=9
so the classes of the first Chern class of the 9 remaining tautological bundles form a basis of
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.

The tautological bundle
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
generates
H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{Z})
and, as in the original Reid’s recipe, the following virtual bundles

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-}_{0}

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{-}_{8})\ominus(\mathcal{R}^{-}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y})

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-}_{6}

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{-}_{2})\ominus(\mathcal{R}^{-}_{6}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y})

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{+}_{8}

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4})\ominus(\mathcal{R}^{+}_{8}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y})

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{+}_{6}

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{+}_{2})\ominus(\mathcal{R}^{+}_{6}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y})

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{5}

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{R}^{+}_{4}\otimes\det\mathcal{R}_{9})\ominus(\det\mathcal{R}_{5}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y})

Remark 3

(ii)

Every irreducible representation of

[

).

(iii)

The trihedral group

A trihedral group

in this case consists of 3 rational curves in a Dynkin
A_{3}
configuration, each of them corresponding to the three non-trivial irreducible representations of
G
(see
[
15
]
for the computations). It is the only trihedral case where this fibre is
1
-dimensional.

\rho_{0}^{\prime}
and
\rho_{0}^{\prime\prime}

and
\omega^{2}

\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
-action producing

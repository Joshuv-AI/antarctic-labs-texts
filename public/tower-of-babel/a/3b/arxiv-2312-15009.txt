1.
Introduction and Main Results

In this paper, we are concerned with the nonlinear fractional Helmholtz equation

(1.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}u-k^{2}u=Q(x)|u|^{p-2}u,\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{in}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\penalty\ \penalty\ N\geq 3,

where
\frac{N}{N+1}<s<\frac{N}{2}
,
\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}<p<\frac{2N}{N-2s}
are two real exponents,
Q
is a bounded continuous function.

When

, the Helmholtz equation

(1.2)

-\Delta u-k^{2}u=f(x,u)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},

k^{2}>0
is contained in the essential spectrum of negative Laplace
-\Delta

1.2

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Therefore, the direct variational approach is invalid.

To overcome this difficult, a dual invariant method has been proposed, which is based on the “Limiting Absorption Principle”. By constructing the auxiliary problems

(1.3)

-\Delta u-(\lambda+i\varepsilon)u=f(x,u)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{in}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},

one can obtain the boundedness estimate for the resolvent operator

\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}=(-\Delta-(\lambda+i\varepsilon))^{-1},

and as
\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0^{+}
, one can also obtain the boundedness estimate for the resolvent
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}=(-\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}
, see
[
27
, Theorem 6]
or see
[
32
,
35
,
39
,
14
,
33
]
. Based on the boundedness estimate, Evéquoz and Weth
[
20
]
(see
[
22
]
) set up a dual variational framework for (
1.2
). Correspondingly, the nontrivial real-valued solutions of equation (
1.2
) with

are detected via the mountain pass argument, where

is a positive weight function, see also
[
24
,
21
,
34
,
25
,
31
]
for the other cases. By a similar way, Shen and the second author
[
10

also obtained the real valued solutions for the fractional Helmholtz equation (
1.1
) in the case that

and

is assumed to be a periodic or decay function.

Recently, Evéquoz
[

considered (

is assumed to be a bounded continuous function,

as the frequency

where

is a potential function. Basically, there are two main routes have been pursued to investigate the concentrating solutions. One is the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction scheme proposed by Floer and Weinstein
[
26

, which has been further extended and combined with variational arguments by Ambrosetti et al.
[

it was proved in
[

that a ground state (i.e., positive least-energy solution) of (

showed that sequences of ground states concentrate at a global minimum point

These results are also extended to the fractional Shrödinger equation, that is

(1.7)

\varepsilon^{2s}(-\Delta)^{s}u+V(x)u=f(x,u),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

Motivated by these works, we also consider in this paper that the existence and concentrating phenomenon of the solutions of (

There is no uniqueness and nondegeneracy result for the real valued solutions, therefore, the classical methods of reduction may be not available to construct the concentrating solutions. This impels us to consider the another method what proposed by Rabinowitz. Actually, the variational method also can not be directly adapt in our case, since there is no natural concept of ground state associated the direct variational formulation.

Follow the idea in
[
23
]
, we define the dual ground state for (

\varepsilon=k^{-1}

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}=Q_{\varepsilon}(x)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}u_{\varepsilon}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

Furthermore, setting

|v|^{p-2}v=Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{p}}[\mathbf{R}^{s}\ast(Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{p}}v)]

2.
The variational framework

2.1.
Dual Functional

Before we compare the energy functional, we recall some properties of the dual functional (
1.10
). Since
p^{\prime}<2
and since the kernel of the operator

is positive close to the origin, the geometry of the functional

is of mountain pass type:

(2.1)

\exists\alpha>0\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \rho>0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{such\penalty\ \penalty\ that}\penalty\ \penalty\ J_{\varepsilon}(v)\geq\alpha>0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\penalty\ v\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{with}\penalty\ \penalty\ ||v||_{p^{\prime}}=\rho.

(2.2)

\exists v_{0}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{such\penalty\ \penalty\ that}\penalty\ \penalty\ ||v_{0}||_{p^{\prime}}>\rho\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ J_{\varepsilon}(v_{0})<0.

As a consequence, the Nehari set associated to

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}:=\{v\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}:J^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(v)v=0\},

is not empty. More precisely, by (

U^{+}_{\varepsilon}:=\{v\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{p}}v\mathbf{R}^{s}\big(Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{p}}v\big)\mathrm{d}x>0\}

is not empty and for each

there is a unique

such that

holds. It is given by

t_{v}^{2-p^{\prime}}=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{p}}v\mathbf{R}^{s}\big(Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{p}}v\big)\mathrm{d}x}.

In addition,

is the unique maximum point of

,

. Using (

. Hence,

as follow. Fix

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}^{(1)}\mathbf{R}^{s}\big(Q^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}^{(1)}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q^{\frac{1}{p}}w_{n}^{(1)}\mathbf{R}^{s}\big(Q^{\frac{1}{p}}w_{n}^{(1)}\big)\mathrm{d}x+o(1),

as
n\longrightarrow\infty
. Moreover, by the Brézis-Lieb Lemma
[
9
]
,

(4.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{n}^{(2)}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{n}^{(1)}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w^{(1)}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x+o(1),\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{as}\penalty\ n\longrightarrow\infty.

These properties and the translation invariance of
J
together give

(4.7)

J(v_{n}^{(2)})=J(v_{n}^{(1)})-J(w^{(1)})+o(1)=d-J(w^{(1)})+o(1),\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{as}\penalty\ n\longrightarrow\infty.

Since by Lemma
2.1
,
1_{B_{r}}v_{n}^{(1)}\longrightarrow 1_{B_{r}}w^{(1)}
strongly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
r>0
, we find

(4.8)

1_{B_{r}}|v_{n}^{(2)}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}^{(2)}-1_{B_{r}}|v_{n}^{(1)}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}^{(1)}+1_{B_{r}}|w^{(1)}|^{p^{\prime}-2}w^{(1)}\longrightarrow 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{in}\penalty\ L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{as}\penalty\ n\longrightarrow\infty.

On the other hand, since
||a|^{q-1}a-|b|^{q-1}b|\leq 2^{1-q}|a-b|^{q}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<q<1
, it follows that

(4.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\big||v_{n}^{(2)}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}^{(2)}-|v_{n}^{(1)}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}^{(1)}\big|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq 2^{(2-p^{\prime})p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}|w^{(1)}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\longrightarrow 0,

as
n\longrightarrow\infty
, uniformly in
n
. The both estimates then give the strong convergence

(4.10)

|v_{n}^{(2)}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}^{(2)}-|v_{n}^{(1)}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}^{(1)}+|w^{(1)}|^{p^{\prime}-2}w^{(1)}\longrightarrow 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{in}\penalty\ L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{as}\penalty\ n\longrightarrow\infty,

and therefore,

(4.11)

J^{\prime}(v_{n}^{(2)})=J^{\prime}(v_{n}^{(1)})-J^{\prime}(w^{(1)})+o(1),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{as}\penalty\ n\longrightarrow\infty.

This implies that
(v_{n}^{(2)})_{n}
is a (PS)-sequence for
J
at level
d-J(w^{(1)})>0
. Thus, the nonvanishing theorem again gives the existence of
R_{1}
,
\zeta_{1}>0
and of a sequence
(y_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that, going to a subsequence

(4.12)

\int_{B_{R_{1}}(y_{n})}|v_{n}^{(2)}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\geq\zeta_{1}>0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{for\penalty\ all}\penalty\ n.

By Lemma
2.1
, there is a critical point
w^{(2)}
of
J
such that (taking a further subsequence)

v_{n}^{(2)}(\cdot+y_{n})\rightharpoonup w^{(2)}
weakly and
1_{B}v_{n}^{(2)}(\cdot+y_{n})\longrightarrow 1_{B}w^{(2)}
strongly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, for all bounded and measurable set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In particular,
w^{(2)}\neq 0
and since
v_{n}^{(2)}\rightharpoonup 0
, we see that
|y_{n}|\longrightarrow\infty
as
n\longrightarrow\infty
.

Setting
x_{n}^{(2)}=x_{n}^{(1)}=y_{n}
, we obtain
|x_{n}^{(2)}-x_{n}^{(1)}|\longrightarrow\infty
as
n\longrightarrow\infty
, and

(4.13)

v_{n}-\big(w^{(1)}(\cdot+x_{n}^{(1)})+w_{n}^{(2)}(\cdot+x_{n}^{(2)})\big)=v_{n}^{(2)}(\cdot+y_{n}-x_{n}^{(2)})-w^{(2)}(\cdot-x_{n}^{(2)})\rightharpoonup 0,

weakly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In addition, the same argument as before show that

(4.14)

J(w^{(2)})\leq\mathop{\mathrm{lim\penalty\ inf}}\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}J(v_{n}^{(2)})=d-J(w^{(1)})

with equality if and only

. If the inequality is strict, we can iterate the procedure. Since for every nontrivial critical point

of

we have

, the iterate has to stop after finitely many steps, and we obtain the desired result.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n}\subset(0,\infty)
satisfy
\varepsilon_{n}\longrightarrow 0
as
n\longrightarrow\infty
. Consider for each
n
some
v_{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{n}}
and assume that
J_{\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{n})\longrightarrow c_{0}
as
n\longrightarrow\infty
. Then, there is
x_{0}\in M
, a critical point
w_{0}
of
J_{0}
at level
c_{0}
and a sequence
(y_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that (up to a subsequence)

(4.15)

\varepsilon_{n}y_{n}\longrightarrow x_{0}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ ||v_{n}(\cdot+y_{n})-w_{0}||_{p^{\prime}}\longrightarrow 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{as}\penalty\ n\longrightarrow\infty.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set
v_{0,n}:=(\frac{Q_{\varepsilon_{n}}}{Q_{0}})^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}
. It follows that
|v_{0,n}|\leq|v_{n}|
a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and that

(4.16)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q_{0}^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{0,n}\mathbf{R}^{s}\big(Q_{0}^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{0,n}\big)\mathrm{d}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}\mathbf{R}^{s}(Q_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n})\mathrm{d}x>0.

Therefore, setting

(4.17)

t_{0,n}^{2-p^{\prime}}=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{0,n}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q_{0}^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{0,n}\mathbf{R}^{s}\big(Q_{0}^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{0,n}\big)\mathrm{d}x}

we find that
t_{0,n}v_{0,n}\in\mathcal{N}_{0}
and
0<t_{0,n}\leq 1
. As a consequence, we can write

(4.18)

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle\leq J_{0}(t_{0,n}v_{0,n})=(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2})t_{0,n}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q_{0}^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{0,n}\mathbf{R}^{s}\big(Q_{0}^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{0,n}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2})t_{0,n}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}\mathbf{R}^{s}\big(Q_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=t_{0,n}^{2}J_{\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{n})\leq J_{\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{n})\longrightarrow c_{0}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{as}\penalty\ n\longrightarrow\infty.

In particular, we find

(4.19)

\mathop{\mathrm{lim}}\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\penalty\ t_{0,n}=1,

and
(t_{0,n}v_{0,})_{n}\subset\mathcal{N}_{0}
is thus a minimizing sequence for
J_{0}
on
\mathcal{N}_{0}
. Using Ekeland’s variational principle and the fact that
\mathcal{N}_{0}
is a natural constraint, we obtain the existence of a
(\mathrm{PS})_{c_{0}}
-sequence
(w_{n})_{n}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
J_{0}
with the property that
||v_{0,n}-w_{n}||_{p^{\prime}}\longrightarrow 0
, as
n\longrightarrow\infty
.

By Lemma
4.1
, there exists a critical point
w_{0}
for
J_{0}
at level
c_{0}
and a sequence
(y_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that (up to a subsequence)
||w_{n}(\cdot+y_{n})-w_{0}||_{p^{\prime}}\longrightarrow 0
, as

. Therefore,

(4.20)

v_{0,n}(\cdot+y_{n})\longrightarrow w_{0}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{strongly\penalty\ in\penalty\ }L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{as}\penalty\ n\longrightarrow\infty.

We are going to show that
(\varepsilon_{n}y_{n})_{n}
is bounded. Suppose not, there exist some subsequence (which we still call
(\varepsilon_{n}y_{n})_{n})
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{lim}}\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}|\varepsilon_{n}y_{n}|=\infty
. We consider the following two cases.

(1)
Q_{\infty}=0
. In this case, by the assumption on
Q
, we have
Q(\varepsilon_{n}\cdot+\varepsilon_{n}y_{n})\longrightarrow 0
, as
n\longrightarrow\infty
, holds uniformly on bounded sets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. From the definition of

(2)

(4.21)

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{lim}}\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}J_{\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{n})=\mathop{\mathrm{lim}}\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x

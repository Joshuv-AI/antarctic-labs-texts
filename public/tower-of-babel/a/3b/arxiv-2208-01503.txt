Abstract
. We prove global well-posedness of the 3 ​ d 3d Yang-Mills equation in the temporal gauge in H σ H^{\sigma} for σ > 5 6 \sigma>\frac{5}{6} . Unlike related equations, Yang-Mills is not directly amenable to the method of almost conservation laws (I-method) in its Fourier and global version. We propose a modified energy which: (1) Is gauge-invariant and easy to localize (2) Provides local gauges which give control of local Sobolev norms (through an Uhlenbeck-type lemma for fractional regularities) (3) Is slightly smoother in time compared to the classical I-method energy for related systems. The spatial smoothing is realized via the Yang-Mills heat flow instead of the multiplier I I . Due to the temporal condition and its finite speed of propagation, the local gauge selection is compatible with recent initial data extension results. Therefore, smoothened energy differences ℰ ⁡ ( t 1 , s ) − ℰ ⁡ ( t 0 , s ) \mathcal{E}(t_{1},s)-\mathcal{E}(t_{0},s) can be partitioned into local pieces whose (appropriately extended) bounds can be square summed. After revealing the null structure within the trilinear integrals, these can be estimated using known methods. In an appendix we show how an invariant modified energy for Maxwell-Klein-Gordon can extend previous results to regularities σ > 5 6 \sigma>\frac{5}{6} .

To obtain a global in time result for (

(1)

Heuristic remarks and other works

Remark 1.12

(Smoothing heuristics and connection to Littlewood-Paley theory)
.

The heat flows solutions to (
cYMHF
), (
dYMHF
) satisfy the covariant parabolic equations (
6.1
). Therefore, in the first approximation one can think of the nonlinear
F_{\alpha\beta}(s)
as roughly
F_{\alpha\beta}(s)\approx e^{s\Delta}F_{\alpha\beta}(0)
. Expanding (
cYMHF
) it is seen that in general, the evolution of
A_{i}(s)
is only
degenerately parabolic. With an appropriate choice of gauge (De Turck
A_{s}=\partial^{\ell}A_{\ell}
) one can make the
A_{i}
equations genuinely parabolic, specifically (
5.1
). Then the approximation
A_{i}(s)\approx e^{s\Delta}A_{i}(0)
is still expected.

The Gaussian multipliers
e^{s\Delta}
make the bulk of the frequencies to become concentrated to
\left|\xi\right|\lesssim s^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. In connection to Littlewood-Paley projections this can be stated as
e^{s\Delta}\approx P_{\leq k(s)}
and
\partial_{x}e^{s\Delta}\approx 2^{k(s)}P_{k(s)}
where
2^{k(s)}=s^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Since
F_{\alpha\beta}
contains derivatives, in a certain gauge one heuristically has
F(s)\approx P_{k(s)}F(0)
.

Now to decompose
A_{i}(0)
one can set the caloric condition
A_{s}=0
, turning (
cYMHF
) into
\partial_{s}A_{i}=\mathbf{D}^{\ell}F_{\ell i}
, which integrates to

(1.34)

A_{i}(s)=A_{i}(s_{0})+\int_{s}^{s_{0}}s\mathbf{D}^{\ell}F_{i\ell}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}s}{s}

Since
\,\mathrm{d}k\simeq-\,\mathrm{d}s/s
,
2^{2k}s=1
and taking
s_{0}=N^{-2}
one can interpret (
1.34
) as a continuous Littlewood-Paley decomposition

(1.35)

A(0)\approx P_{\leq N}A(0)+\int_{\log N}^{\infty}2^{-k}P_{k}F(0)\,\mathrm{d}k,\qquad P_{k}A_{i}(0)\approx s\mathbf{D}^{\ell}F_{i\ell}(s)

Remark 1.13

\mathcal{IE}(t)

The I-method suggests applying the Fourier multiplier

2.
Preliminaries

2.0.1.
Space-time

We will work on the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
equipped with the Minkowski metric, according to which indices will be raised and lowered. We adopt the Einstein summation convention. Greek letters run over the space-time variables, while latin indices are used only for spatial variables.

We use
A[t]
as a short-hand for the pair
(A(t),\partial_{t}A(t))
.

2.0.2.
Lie theory

Let
G
be a Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}
its associated Lie algebra. We assume the existence of a bi-invariant inner product
(\cdot,\cdot):\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to[0,\infty)
. This means

(2.1)

([A,B],C)=(A,[B,C])\quad\text{or equivalently},\quad(UAU^{-1},UBU^{-1})=(A,B)

for all
A,B,C\in\mathfrak{g}
,
U\in G
. The Leibniz rule

(2.2)

\partial_{\alpha}(B,C)=\left(\mathbf{D}_{\alpha}B,C\right)+\left(B,\mathbf{D}_{\alpha}C\right)

holds, as well as Bianchi’s identity

(2.3)

\mathbf{D}_{\alpha}F_{\beta\gamma}+\mathbf{D}_{\beta}F_{\gamma\alpha}+\mathbf{D}_{\gamma}F_{\alpha\beta}=0.

Covariant derivatives are commuted using

(2.4)

\mathbf{D}_{\alpha}\mathbf{D}_{\beta}-\mathbf{D}_{\beta}\mathbf{D}_{\alpha}=[F_{\alpha\beta},\cdot]

The covariant laplacian is denoted by
\Delta_{A}\vcentcolon=\mathbf{D}^{\ell}\mathbf{D}_{\ell}
.

Due to (
2.1
), the inner-product can be used to define the
L^{p}
norms of
\mathfrak{g}
-valued functions in a gauge-invariant way.

For concreteness, it is common to take
G
to be a matrix group such as
SO(n,\mathbb{R})
or
SU(n,\mathbb{C})
, with the associated Lie Algebras
\mathfrak{so}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathfrak{su}(n)
. In these cases the Lie bracket is
[A,B]=AB-BA
given by matrix multiplication, while the bi-invariant scalar products are
(A,B)=\textrm{tr}(AB^{T})
, respectively
(A,B)=\textrm{tr}(AB^{*})
.

2.0.3.
Regular functions

A function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(such as an initial data
\bar{A}_{i}
) is called
regular
if
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\vcentcolon=\bigcap_{n\geq 0}H^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Solutions
A_{\alpha}(t,x)
or
A_{i}(x,s)
defined on intervals are called regular if
A_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}_{t}H^{\infty}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, respectively
A_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{s}H^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times J)
. Similarly for

A_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}_{t,s}H^{\infty}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times J)
.
Gauge transformations are called regular if
\nabla U,\nabla U^{-1}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

2.0.4.
Boundedness notations

We use
A\lesssim B
to denote
A\leq CB
and
A\ll B
to denote
AC\leq B
, where
C
is a large constant. We use
A+
or
A-
to denote
A+\varepsilon
and
A-\varepsilon
where

For high-high-to-low frequency interactions we argue exactly the same as for (

), only the power obtained from Bernstein’s inequality changes by a factor of
2^{-\frac{3}{2}k}
. The same is true for low-high and high-low

interactions.

For the remaining high-low interactions place
A
(high frequency) in
L^{\infty}L^{2}
and
\partial_{t}A
(low frequency) in
L^{2}L^{\infty}
using (
4.14
).

For low-high interactions, place
A^{df}
(low) in
L^{2}L^{\infty}
and
\partial_{t}A
(high) in
L^{\infty}L^{2}
. It remains to deal with

for

. This term is responsible for the

factor in (

), rather than

(which can probably be fully obtained). Place

in

using (

) and Bernstein and place

in

using Bernstein again.
∎

5.
Yang-Mills Heat Flow solutions

This section is devoted to the Yang-Mills heat flow. We consider deTurck’s and caloric gauges including: well-posedness in
I^{-1}H^{1}
and
H^{\rho}
, control of the modified energy (
1.6
) from the initial data and the change between the two gauges.

5.1.
Local well-posedness in DeTurck’s gauge

In the covariant (
cYMHF
) equation, setting the DeTurck gauge condition
A_{s}=\partial^{\ell}A_{\ell}
one obtains the parabolic equation for
A_{i}

(5.1)

(\partial_{s}-\Delta)A_{i}=2\left[A^{\ell},\partial_{\ell}A_{i}\right]-\left[A^{\ell},\partial_{i}A_{\ell}\right]+\left[A^{\ell},\left[A_{\ell},A_{i}\right]\right]

In short, this equation can be written schematically as
(\partial_{s}-\Delta)A=A\nabla A+A^{3}
.

One can separate the linear and nonlinear parts of
A_{i}
:

(5.2)

A_{i}(s)=e^{s\Delta}A_{i}(0)+A^{bil}_{i}(s)

where the bilinear term is written schematically as

(5.3)

A^{bil}_{i}(s_{1})\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{s_{1}}e^{(s_{1}-s)\Delta}(A\nabla A+A^{3})\,\mathrm{d}s

and will enjoys more favorable estimates, such as (
5.14
).

This equation is locally well-posed in
I^{-1}H^{1}
:

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose the initial data satisfies
I\bar{A}_{i}\in\varepsilon H^{1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\varepsilon>0
is small enough. Then there exists a solution to (
5.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,1]
with
A_{i}(s=0)=\bar{A}_{i}
which satisfies

(5.4)

IA_{i}\in\varepsilon L^{\infty}_{s}H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,1])

and is the unique limit of regular solutions.

This provides a solution to (
cYMHF
) with
A_{s}=\partial^{\ell}A_{\ell}
.
If the initial data is regular then also the solution is.
Moreover,

(5.5)

A_{s}\in\varepsilon L^{1}_{s}L^{\infty}_{x},\qquad\nabla A_{s}\in\varepsilon L^{1}_{s}L^{3}_{x},\qquad A_{s}\in\varepsilon L^{1}_{s}H^{\frac{3}{2}+}

and for any
s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,1]
, one has

(5.6)

\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}I\Delta A_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\in\varepsilon L^{2}_{x}

Proof.

This is proved using standard methods, so we only sketch the proof. One sets up a contraction argument or Picard iteration,
looking for a fixed point for the functional

\Phi A_{i}(s_{1})=e^{s_{1}\Delta}\bar{A}_{i}+\int_{0}^{s_{1}}e^{(s_{1}-s)\Delta}(A\nabla A+A^{3})\,\mathrm{d}s

in a suitable space
S
where one has the estimates

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{s_{1}}e^{(s_{1}-s)\Delta}(A\nabla B)\,\mathrm{d}s\|_{S}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|A\|_{S}\|B\|_{S}

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{s_{1}}e^{(s_{1}-s)\Delta}(A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}))\,\mathrm{d}s\|_{S}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|A_{1}\|_{S}\|A_{2}\|_{S}\|A_{3}\|_{S}

One may use the space

(5.7)

\|A\|_{S}\vcentcolon=\|\|\left\langle 2^{2k}s\right\rangle^{10}\|P_{k}IA(s)\|_{H^{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{s}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}}

to check these estimates using basic Littewood-Paley theory.

One may also use the following parabolic energy estimates,
[
37
, Proposition 3.12]

(5.8)

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{s_{1}}e^{(s_{1}-s)\Delta}\mathcal{N}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\|_{\tilde{S}}\lesssim\|s\mathcal{N}\|_{L^{1}_{\frac{\,\mathrm{d}s}{s}}\cap L^{2}_{\frac{\,\mathrm{d}s}{s}}(L^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\tilde{S}}\vcentcolon=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{s}L^{2}_{x}}+\|s^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{\frac{\,\mathrm{d}s}{s}}(L^{2}_{x})}+\|s\partial_{x}^{2}f\|_{L^{2}_{\frac{\,\mathrm{d}s}{s}}L^{2}_{x}}

used in the proof of
[
37
, Proposition 5.2]
which is the corresponding well-posedness statement for
\dot{H}^{1}
. Here one writes the equation as

(5.9)

(\partial_{s}-\Delta)I\left\langle D_{x}\right\rangle A=I\left\langle D_{x}\right\rangle(A\nabla A+A^{3})

One defines
\|A\|_{S}\vcentcolon=\|I\left\langle D_{x}\right\rangle A\|_{\tilde{S}}
.
The needed estimates to check for this case of
I^{-1}H^{1}
, as opposed to
\dot{H}^{1}
, become

(5.10)

\displaystyle\|I\left\langle D\right\rangle(f\nabla g)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|If\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+(1-\sigma)+}}\|Ig\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\|I\left\langle D\right\rangle(f_{1}f_{2}f_{3})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|If_{1}\|_{H^{2-\frac{2}{3}\sigma}}\|If_{2}\|_{H^{2-\frac{2}{3}\sigma}}\|If_{3}\|_{H^{2-\frac{2}{3}\sigma}}

which follow easily from (
2.19
) or from Littewood-Paley, by splitting into different cases depending on whether the inputs have frequencies higher or lower than
N
.

By similar arguments for the differentiated equation one obtains persistence of regularity.
We refer to
[
37
, Proposition 5.2]
for more details.

The bounds (
5.5
) follow from the
\|A\|_{S}
bound and the definition of
A_{s}=\partial^{\ell}A_{\ell}
.

Denoting by
\mathcal{N}
the nonlinear RHS of (
5.1
), for our solution, the bounds above show

(5.11)

\|I\left\langle D_{x}\right\rangle\mathcal{N}\|_{L^{1}_{s}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}

We use this to prove (
5.6
). The equation for
IA_{s}
is of the type
(\partial_{s}-\Delta)IA_{s}=I\partial_{x}\mathcal{N}
. Integrating the equation and taking
L^{2}_{x}
norms one obtains

\|\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}I\Delta A_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq\|IA_{s}(s_{1})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|IA_{s}(s_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|I\partial_{x}\mathcal{N}\|_{L^{1}_{s}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\varepsilon.

∎

As a consequence one obtains gauge-invariant bounds for the curvature, such as control of the modified energy (
1.6
).

Corollary 5.2
.

Suppose
A_{i}(t,x)
is a regular solution of the (
YM
) equation in the temporal gauge on
[t_{0},t_{1}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

(IA_{i},IF_{0i})\in\varepsilon L^{\infty}_{t}(H^{1}\times L^{2})([t_{0},t_{1}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})

Suppose

As a consequence, for (

) one has the gauge-invariant bound
\mathcal{IE}(t)\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]

Fix a time

We begin with

.

Now consider

in the norm

. The estimates we need now are

\displaystyle\|I(A\nabla B)\|_{L^{2}}+\|I(B\nabla A)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|IA\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+(1-\sigma)+}}\|IB\|_{H^{1+}}

\displaystyle\|I(A^{2}B)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|IA\|_{H^{2-\sigma}}^{2}\|IB\|_{H^{2-\sigma}}

which follow from (

For

.

Suppose

obeys the conditions of Proposition

.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let

be given by Proposition

be the solution of the following ODE

Then one has

and

obeys the same bounds. If

is a regular solution then

is a regular gauge transformation.

Proof.

Write the integral form

U(s_{1})=1+\int_{0}^{s_{1}}UA_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s

The first two bounds in (

[

[

The first two terms in the integral are handled by Gronwall’s inequality and by

\displaystyle\|I(fg)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|If\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+}}

(5.23)

\displaystyle\|I(fg)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|If\|_{H^{1}}\|g\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+}}

for

For the third term one plugs in

\int_{0}^{s_{1}}I\Delta A_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+I\int_{0}^{s_{1}}U(s^{\prime})A_{s}(s^{\prime})\left(\int_{s^{\prime}}^{s_{1}}\Delta A_{s}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\,\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}

Bound this in

using (

)
for

and

by (

). Note that

by using the algebra property for

.
∎

Here one denotes the covariant derivative
D_{\alpha}
and the curvature
F_{\alpha\beta}

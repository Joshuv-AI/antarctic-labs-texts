Abstract
. We provide a complete characterization of those non-elliptic semigroups of holomorphic self-maps of the unit disc for which the linear span of eigenvectors of the generator of the corresponding semigroup of composition operators is weak-star dense in H ∞ H^{\infty} . We also give some necessary and some sufficient conditions for completeness in H p H^{p} . This problem is equivalent to the completeness of the corresponding exponential functions in H ∞ H^{\infty} (in the weak-star sense) or in H p H^{p} of the Koenigs domain of the semigroup. As a tool needed for the results, we introduce and study discontinuities of semigroups of holomorphic self-maps of the unit disc.

1.
Introduction

The investigation into the density of a natural class of functions in classical
H^{p}
-spaces represents a significant problem, akin to a broader exploration of classical approximation theorems such as those by Runge and Mergelyan
[
16
]
. Notably, this inquiry serves as fundamental extensions of classical approximation theorems. Several notable contributions have been made to this field, as evidenced by the works of
[
2
,
3
,
9
,
14
,
26
,
32
,
33
]
, particularly concerning polynomial approximation. Nikolskii’s monograph
[
23
]
stands out as a valuable source of results in this regard.

One compelling avenue of investigation involves the study of weak-star density of polynomials in
H^{\infty}(G)
for simply connected domains
G
, leading to the conceptualization of “weak-star generators of
H^{\infty}
”. This notion, fully characterized in topological terms by Sarason
[
28
,
29
]
, has proven to be instrumental not only in understanding the density of polynomials but also in the broader context of operator theory (see
[
6
]
). The exploration of this topic is of paramount importance, linking classical approximation theorems to modern operator theory developments.

In this paper, we extend the ongoing investigation by exploring approximation achieved through the frequencies associated with holomorphic flows (semigroups), providing a complete picture for the weak-star topology of
H^{\infty}
and sufficient conditions in
H^{p}
.

In order to better describe our results, we recall that every continuous semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of holomorphic self-maps of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, or, for short, a semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
, gives rise to a semigroup
\{C_{\varphi_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
of strongly continuous composition operators in the classical Hardy space of the unit disc
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
,
p\in[1,\infty)
.

In case the semigroup is non-elliptic, the eigenvalues of the generator
\Gamma
of
\{C_{\varphi_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
are those
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, where
h
is the Koenigs function of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. In other words, if
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
is the Koenigs domain of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is an eigenvalue of
\Gamma
if and only if
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
. Such
\lambda
’s will be called
frequencies
. We refer the reader to Section
2
for definitions and details.

Betsakos
[
5
]
conducted a meticulous study, employing harmonic measure theory and considering the nature of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
(whether hyperbolic, parabolic with a positive hyperbolic step, or parabolic with a zero hyperbolic step), aimed to determine the values of
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
for which
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
and provided detailed estimates.

The aim of this paper is to study
completeness
of such exponentials. More precisely, for
p\in[1,+\infty]
, let

\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega):=\hbox{span}\{e^{\lambda z}:\lambda\in\mathbb{C},e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)\}.

The precise questions we address here are:

•

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
, when is
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
?

•

For
p=\infty
, when is
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
?

In case
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
(or
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
) we say that the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has
p
-complete (or
\infty
-weak-star complete) frequencies.

In this paper we completely characterize when
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
, in terms of the topological properties of the Koenigs domain, its geometrical properties and the dynamical properties of the (non-elliptic) semigroup. We also provide sufficient conditions, and some necessary, for
p
-completeness.

It is here worth emphasizing that when
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is elliptic, similar queries consistently yield negative results. This is due to the existence of a common fixed point for all the functions associated to frequencies of the infinitesimal generator of the composition operator semigroups.

In order to address the previous questions, we start this paper with a careful study of the set
\mathcal{S}(h)\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
of boundary discontinuities of the Koenigs function
h
of the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
(see Subsection
6.3
and Subsection
7.2
for details). Here
\partial\mathbb{D}
denotes the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
. Except at the Denjoy-Wolff point, the discontinuities of
h
correspond to the discontinuities of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Among such discontinuities, we focus on the
unbounded ones
,
\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)
for which (with the exception of the Denjoy-Wolff point)
the impression of the corresponding prime end contains
\infty
.
The Denjoy-Wolff point can be a discontinuity in the hyperbolic and parabolic of positive hyperbolic step cases. If this is the case, and only in the hyperbolic case, the impression in
\mathbb{C}
of the corresponding prime end under
h
, is contained in the union of two lines. We say that the Denjoy-Wolff point is a
simple discontinuity
if the impression does not coincide with the union of the two lines.

Also, we introduce
Cantor combs
of discontinuities, which are discontinuities on
\partial\mathbb{D}
which form a Cantor set and whose image under the Koenigs function looks like a comb (see Section
6
).

An important role in our characterization is also played by maximal contact arcs (see Subsection
6.2
), introduced and studied in
[
7
]
. Roughly speaking, a maximal contact arc is an open arc on
\partial\mathbb{D}
such that the semigroup extends holomorphically through it and flows the arc from the starting point to the final point. A maximal contact arc is exceptional if its final point is the Denjoy-Wolff point.

Every Koenigs domain
\Omega
of a non-elliptic semigroup can be described as

\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:x>\psi(y)\},

for some upper semicontinuous function
\psi:I\to[-\infty,+\infty)
defined on an open interval
I\subseteq(-\infty,+\infty)
. We let
\tilde{\psi}
to be the upper semicontinuous regularization of the lower semicontinuous regularization of
\psi
(see Definition
6.15
). The functions
\psi
and
\tilde{\psi}
encode the geometry of
\Omega
.

With these definitions at hand, we can state our main results. We start with the hyperbolic case:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a hyperbolic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be the Koenigs function,
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the Koenigs domain and let
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the defining function of
\Omega
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has
\infty
-weak-star complete frequencies, that is,
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

(2)

\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components,

(3)

\psi(y)=\tilde{\psi}(y)
for every
y\in I
and either
\liminf_{y\to y_{0}}\psi(y)>-\infty
for all
y_{0}\in\overline{I}
or there exist
a\leq b
such that
[a,b]\subset\overline{I}
,
\liminf_{y\to y_{0}}\psi(y)=-\infty
for all
y_{0}\in[a,b]
and
\liminf_{I\ni y\to y_{1}}\psi(y)>-\infty
for all
y_{1}\in\overline{I}\setminus[a,b]
,

(4)

the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

if
A,B
are non-exceptional maximal contact arcs for
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with final points
x_{1}(A)
and
x_{1}(B)
then
x_{1}(A)\neq x_{1}(B)
,

(b)

\mathcal{S}(h)
does not contain any Cantor comb,

(c)

one of the following (mutually excluding) conditions holds:

(c.1)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has at most one boundary fixed point other than
\tau
and no unbounded discontinuities,

(c.2)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has one and only one boundary fixed point
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
,
\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)=\{\xi\}
with
\xi\neq\tau
, and there exists a maximal contact arc starting from
\sigma
and ending at
\xi
,

(c.3)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has one and only one boundary fixed point
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
which is a boundary regular fixed point,
\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)=\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\}
with
\xi_{j}\neq\tau
, and for
j=1,2
,
there is a maximal contact arc starting from
\sigma
and ending at
\xi_{j}
.

(c.4)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has no boundary fixed points other than
\tau
and
\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)=\{\xi\}
, with
\xi\neq\tau
,

(c.5)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has no boundary fixed points other than
\tau
and
\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)=\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\}
, with
\xi_{j}\neq\tau
,
j=1,2
and there exists a maximal contact arc starting from
\xi_{1}
and ending at
\xi_{2}
,

(c.6)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has no boundary fixed points other than
\tau
,
\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)=\{\tau\}
and
\tau
is a simple unbounded discontinuity.

Moreover, if one—and hence any—of the above conditions
(1)–(4) holds, then
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has
p
-complete frequencies, that is,
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

The result looks rather surprising and somewhat unexpected, because, on one hand, condition (2) says essentially that
\Omega
is a Carathéodory domain in the strip, that is to say, the composition of
h
with the conformal mapping of the strip onto
\mathbb{D}
is a weak-star sequential generator of
H^{\infty}
in the sense of Sarason. However, there exist weak-star generators of
H^{\infty}
such that their images are not Carathéodory domains. On the other hand, one might expect that discontinuities of
h
represent a serious constraint to completeness, but, apart from those which are forbidden,
h
can have “serious” discontinuities (like a Cantor set which is not a Cantor comb of discontinuities) and still enjoy completeness. Finally, the presence of boundary fixed points is very limited.

For the case of parabolic semigroups of positive hyperbolic step, we have:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup of positive hyperbolic step in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be the Koenigs function,
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the Koenigs domain and let
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the defining function of
\Omega
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has
\infty
-weak-star complete frequencies, that is,
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

(2)

\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected,

(3)

\psi(y)=\tilde{\psi}(y)
for every
y\in I
and
\liminf_{I\ni y\to y_{0}}\psi(y)>-\infty
for every
y_{0}\in\overline{I}\setminus\overline{I_{\infty}}
, where
I_{\infty}
is the (possibly empty) unbounded connected component of
\psi^{-1}(-\infty)
,

(4)

the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

if
A,B
are non-exceptional maximal contact arcs for
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with final points
x_{1}(A)
and
x_{1}(B)
then
x_{1}(A)\neq x_{1}(B)
,

(b)

\mathcal{S}(h)
does not contain any Cantor comb,

(c)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has no boundary fixed points other than
\tau
and
\mathcal{S}(h)
contains only bounded discontinuities except, at most, one unbounded discontinuity
\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
which is the final point of a maximal contact arc starting at
\tau
.

Moreover, if one—and hence any—of the above conditions (1)–(4) holds, then
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has
p
-complete frequencies, that is,
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Finally, for parabolic semigroups of zero hyperbolic step, the situation is simpler:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup of zero hyperbolic step in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be the Koenigs function,
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the Koenigs domain and let
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the defining function of
\Omega
. If
\Omega
is not contained in any half-plane, then
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)=\mathbb{C}
and in particular
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is not weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

If there exists a half-plane
H
such that
\Omega\subset H
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has
\infty
-weak-star complete frequencies, that is,
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

(2)

\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
,

(3)

\psi(y)=\tilde{\psi}(y)
for every
y\in I
,

(4)

the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

if
A,B
are non-exceptional maximal contact arcs for
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with final points
x_{1}(A)
and
x_{1}(B)
then
x_{1}(A)\neq x_{1}(B)
,

(b)

\mathcal{S}(h)
does not contain any Cantor comb.

Moreover, if one—and hence any—of the above conditions (1)–(4) holds, then
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has
p
-complete frequencies, that is,
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

The proof of the previous theorems, which is based on the materials developed in the next sections, goes as follows. By a functional analysis argument, the
\infty
-weak-star completeness of frequencies implies that the same frequencies are also
p
-complete. Thus, we just consider
\infty
-frequencies. We show that for some basic domain like the half-plane and the strip, frequencies are
\infty
-weak-star complete. This is done using suitable Laplace transforms and approximation of rational functions. Then we show that a Koenigs domain
\Omega
contained in a strip or in a half-plane has
\infty
-weak-star complete frequencies if and only if
H^{\infty}
of the strip or the half-plane is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
. We study then such a condition and, using some results of Sarason and properties of starlike at infinity domains, we come up with the characterization (2) in the previous theorems. The rest of the arguments are then devoted to
state the topological condition on
\Omega
in terms of dynamical properties of the semigroup, and this brings to the study of discontinuities.

In the final Section
10
, we turn our attention to
p
-completeness of frequencies. We do not know whether the equivalent conditions (2)-(3)-(4) in the previous theorems are also necessary for
p
-completeness of frequencies. This seems to be an interesting problem, related to polynomial approximation in
H^{p}
spaces of the so-called “crescents”.

However, we can prove that certain conditions are necessary for
p
-completeness of frequencies (for instance, we show that (4).(a) is a necessary condition, and also the fact that
p
-frequencies are not contained in an interval is a necessary condition).

We also provide some positive result for parabolic semigroups of zero hyperbolic step which are not contained in any half-plane (and for which then there are no
\infty
-frequencies, so that the
\infty
-weak-star completeness is not an issue). In such a case, we define
logarithmic starlike at infinity domains
, which, roughly speaking, are logarithmic perturbations of a half-plane. If such a domain has
p
-frequencies (see (
4.4
)),
then these frequencies are
p
-complete (see Theorem
4.6
). Using such logarithmic domains as a “pivot”, we prove:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup of zero hyperbolic step in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be the Koenigs function,
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the Koenigs domain and let
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the defining function of
\Omega
. Suppose
\Omega\subseteq D
, where
D
is the rotation of logarithmic starlike at infinity domain with
p
-frequencies (that is, satisfying (
4.4
)). Then conditions (2)-(3) and (4) of Theorem
1.2
are equivalent and if one—and hence any—of them holds, then
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has
p
-complete frequencies, that is,
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
for all

p\in[1,\infty)
.

It is worth mentioning that there is some relation between the subject of our paper and an extensive study of the possibility and the uniqueness of the representation of a function
f(z)
, holomorphic in a domain
\Omega
, as a series
\sum c_{k}\exp(\lambda_{k}z)
. See, for instance, the book by Leont’ev
[
21
]
or its account in
[
22
]
and Krasichkov-Ternovskii
[
20
]
. In the setting of these studies, the domain
\Omega
is convex and the sequence of exponents
\{\lambda_{k}\}
is fixed. Typically it is a zero sequence of an entire function with some known growth properties. Explicit formulas for coefficients
c_{k}
are given. In most of these works,
\Omega
is assumed to be bounded, but in the book
[
21
]
, the case when
\Omega
is unbounded and its boundary contains two half-lines and the case when
\Omega=\mathbb{C}
are also considered (in the setting of the locally uniform convergence). As it is proved in
[
20
, Sections 7 and 8]
, the so-called spectral synthesis for invariant subspaces of the space of holomorphic functions in
\Omega
holds for all unbounded convex domains and does not hold in general for bounded convex domains. Relations with convolution equations for holomorphic functions and differential equations of infinite order can also be found in those works.

The plan of the paper is the following. In Section
2
we recall some definitions and prove some preliminary facts about semigroups, semigroups of composition operators and Hardy spaces. In Section
3
, we define frequencies and prove some basic properties of them. In Section
4
, we prove the completeness results for strips, half-planes and logarithmic starlike at infinity domains. In Section
5
, we prove that
\infty
-weak-star completeness of frequencies is equivalent to the topological conditions on
\Omega
studying the holomorphic hulls of starlike at infinity domains. In Section

, we initiate our study of discontinuities of Koenigs functions, introducing the Cantor combs, and characterize which Koenigs domains
\Omega
have the property that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
. In Section

These are the key results to prove the dynamical characterization of completeness of frequencies. In the next Section
8
we characterize those Koenigs domains
\Omega
such that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components. Section

-frequencies.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
denote the unit disc of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. The Riemann sphere will be denoted by
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. A
half-plane
is the open set
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,\langle w,v\rangle<c\}
, where
v\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. The right half-plane is
\mathbb{C}_{+}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,z>0\}
. A
strip
is the open set
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:a<|{\sf Im}\,w|<b\}
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
a<b
.

If
D\subset\mathbb{C}
is a domain (open and connected set), its Euclidean closure is denoted by
\overline{D}
and its boundary by
\partial D
. Likewise,
\overline{D}^{\infty}
and
\partial_{\infty}D
denote, respectively, the closure of
D
and the boundary of
D
in the Riemann sphere. For any set
D\subset\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
we denote by
\hbox{Int}(D)
its interior.

2.1.
Semigroups in the unit disc, Koenigs functions and Koenigs domains

In this subsection we collect the relevant results regarding semigroups of holomorphic functions. For details we refer the reader to the monographs
[
8
,
31
]
.

A continuous semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
, or, for short, a
semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
, is a continuous semigroup homomorphism from
[0,+\infty)
endowed with the Euclidean topology to
\hbox{Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
, the space of holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
endowed with the topology of uniform convergence on compacta. Hence, for every
t\geq 0
,

(1)

\varphi_{t}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is holomorphic,

(2)

\varphi_{0}={\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}
,

(3)

\varphi_{t}\circ\varphi_{s}=\varphi_{t+s}
for all
s,t\geq 0
, and

(4)

\varphi_{t}
converges uniformly on compacta of
\mathbb{D}
to
\varphi_{s}
when
t\to s
,
s,t\geq 0
.

A semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is
elliptic
if there exists a point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
such that
\varphi_{t}(z_{0})=z_{0}
for some
t>0
. It is
non-elliptic
otherwise.

If
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is non-elliptic, then there exists a unique point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
converges uniformly on compacta to the constant map
z\mapsto\tau
when
t\to+\infty
. Such a point is called the
Denjoy-Wolff point
of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Moreover, for all
t\geq 0
,
\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(z)
has non-tangential limit at
\tau
given by
e^{t\alpha}
for some
\alpha\leq 0
. If
\alpha<0
, the semigroup is called
hyperbolic
while it is
parabolic
if
\alpha=0
.

A parabolic semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is
of positive hyperbolic step
if for some—and hence any—
z\in\mathbb{D}
it holds

\lim_{t\to+\infty}k_{\mathbb{D}}(\varphi_{t}(z),\varphi_{t+1}(z))>0

(here
k_{\mathbb{D}}
is the hyperbolic distance in
\mathbb{D}
), otherwise it is
of zero parabolic step
.

For every semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
there exists
the infinitesimal generator of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, namely, the unique holomorphic function
G:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\frac{\partial\varphi_{t}(z)}{\partial t}=G(\varphi_{t}(z)),\qquad t\geq 0,\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

We recall the following theorem (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Thm. 9.3.5]
):

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}

h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
univalent such that

(1)

h(\varphi_{t}(z))=h(z)+t

(2)

\bigcup_{t\geq 0}(h(\mathbb{D})-t)=U
,

where
U=\mathbb{C}
or
U=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,z>0\}
, or
U=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,z<0\}
or
U=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:a<{\sf Im}\,z<b\}
for some

.

Moreover,
[

, if

such that

(and, actually, in case

,

).

The semigroup is parabolic of zero hyperbolic step if and only if
U=\mathbb{C}
, it is parabolic of positive hyperbolic step if and only if

or

We call

of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
\Omega:=h(\mathbb{D})

of

.

By definition, the Koenigs domain

is
starlike at infinity

There is a simple way to define starlike at infinity domains. Recall that, if

is an open interval, a function

\limsup_{t\to t_{0}}u(t)\leq u(t_{0}).

Note that we allow the function

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

be an open interval and let
\psi:I\to[-\infty,+\infty)
be an upper semicontinuous function. Let

\Omega_{\psi}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,z>\psi({\sf Im}\,z),{\sf Im}\,z\in I\}.

Then
\Omega_{\psi}
is a domain starlike at infinity. Conversely, if
\Omega
is a domain starlike at infinity, then there exist an open interval
I\subseteq(-\infty,+\infty)
and an upper semicontinuous function
\psi:I\to[-\infty,+\infty)
such that
\Omega=\Omega_{\psi}
. Moreover,
\Omega_{\psi}=\mathbb{C}
if and only if

and
\psi\equiv-\infty

Proof.

Since

is upper semicontinuous, then
\Omega_{\psi}
is a domain and clearly it is starlike at infinity.

Conversely, let

be a domain starlike at infinity.
Then the projection

of

onto the

(finite or infinite).
For

Since

is starlike at infinity, it follows easily that

it is easy to see that the point

belongs to the closure of

It follows that
\limsup_{y\to y_{0}}\psi(y)\leq y_{0},

so that

Definition 2.3

If

2.2.
Hardy spaces and weak-star topology

If
D\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
is a simply connected domain, for
p\in[1,\infty)
we let

H^{p}(D)
denote the set of holomorphic functions
f
in
D
such that
|f|^{p}
has a harmonic majorant in
D
. Given
z_{0}\in D
, we let
\|f\|_{p}:=\inf(u(z_{0}))^{1/p}
, where the infimum is taken among all harmonic majorants
u
of
|f|^{p}
. This is indeed a norm that makes
H^{p}(D)
into a Banach space. If
g:D\to G
is a biholomorphism, then the map
\Phi_{g}:H^{p}(D)\to H^{p}(G)
defined as
f\mapsto f\circ g^{-1}
induces a continuous isomorphism. If
D=\mathbb{D}
, the Poisson representation formula implies that
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is the usual Hardy space
H^{p}
of holomorphic functions with bounded
p
-mean. See,
e.g.
,
[
15
,
27
]
, for details.

Also, we denote by
H^{\infty}(D)
the set of all bounded holomorphic functions, which is a Banach space when endowed with the norm
\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup\{|f(z)|,z\in D\}
.

Given
p\in[1,\infty]
, a classical result due to Fatou states that every function
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
has radial limit
f^{\ast}
almost everywhere on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. The map
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})\ni f\mapsto f^{\ast}\in L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
induces an isomorphism on a subspace
H^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
of
L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
(with respect to the normalized Lebesgue measure)—see,
e.g.
,
[
15
, Chapter 7]
.

For
f,g
functions we let, whenever it makes sense,

\langle f,g\rangle:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{i\theta})g(e^{i\theta})d\theta.

Then
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:L^{1}(\partial\mathbb{D})\times L^{\infty}(\partial\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{C}
defines an isometrical isomorphism

\iota_{\infty}:L^{\infty}(\partial\mathbb{D})\to(L^{1}(\partial\mathbb{D}))^{\ast},

the dual space of
L^{1}(\partial\mathbb{D})
.
Thus, one can endow
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
with the topology induced by the weak-star topology of
(L^{1}(\partial\mathbb{D}))^{\ast}
on
\iota_{\infty}(H^{\infty}(\partial\mathbb{D}))
. Such topology is the
weak-star topology
on
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
.

For
p\in(1,+\infty)
, let
q\in(1,+\infty)
be such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. The dual pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})\times L^{q}(\partial\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{C}

defines an isometrical isomorphism
\iota_{p}:L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})\to(L^{q}(\partial\mathbb{D}))^{\ast}
. Hence, we can define the weak-star topology of
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
as the one induced on
\iota_{p}(H^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D}))
by the weak-star topology of
(L^{q}(\partial\mathbb{D}))^{\ast}
. The dual pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
allows one also to define an isomorphism (not isometrical) between
H^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
and
(H^{q}(\partial\mathbb{D}))^{\ast}
. In particular, the space
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is reflexive, and the weak-star topology in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
coincides with the weak topology in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
.

Using a Riemann map, one can then define the weak-star topology on
H^{\infty}(D)
and
H^{p}(D)
,
p>1
, for every simply connected domain
D\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
.

It is well-known (see
[
28
]
) that a sequence
\{f_{n}\}
converges to
f
in the weak-star topology on

H^{\infty}(D)
if and only if it converges pointwise in
D
to
f
and the norms of
f_{n}
in
H^{\infty}(D)

are uniformly bounded.

For the sake of completeness (and partly because of the lack of direct references), we prove some facts about Hardy spaces that we would need later.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected domain and let
\mathcal{G}\subset H^{\infty}(D)
be a linear set. If
\mathcal{G}
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(D)
then
\mathcal{G}
is dense in
H^{p}(D)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Using a Riemann map from
\mathbb{D}
onto
D
, we can assume
D=\mathbb{D}
and
\mathcal{G}\subset H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, and prove that
\mathcal{G}
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Fix
p
,
1<p<\infty
, and let
q
be such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.
Since the inclusion
(L^{1}(\partial\mathbb{D}))^{\ast}\subset(L^{q}(\partial\mathbb{D}))^{\ast}
is weak-star continuous, it follows that the inclusion
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\subset H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is weak-star continuous as well.

Since polynomials are dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, it follows that
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
(with respect to the strong topology in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
), thus, it is dense also in the weak topology of
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
. But
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is reflexive, hence
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
in the weak-star topology of
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is dense in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
in the weak-star topology, it follows from the previous observations that
\mathcal{G}
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
in the weak-star topology of
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, thus,
\mathcal{G}
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
in the weak topology of
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
. By Mazur’s Theorem (see,
e.g.
,
[
13
, Corollary 3, Chapter 2]
),
\mathcal{G}
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
(with respect to the strong topology in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
).

Therefore, we proved that
\mathcal{G}
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for every
p>1
.

Since
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
embeds densely and continuously in
H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
, it follows immediately that
\mathcal{G}
is also dense in
H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{D}
be a simply connected domain such that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{G})=G
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{G}
is connected. Then
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(G)
and
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is dense in
H^{p}(G)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of all holomorphic polynomials. The conditions that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{G})=G
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{G}
is connected imply (see,
e.g.
,
[
14
, Prop. 1]
) that
G
is a Carathéodory domain. Hence,
[
28
, Proposition 2]
implies that holomorphic polynomials are weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(G)
. Since
\mathcal{P}\subset H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, we have that
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(G)
.

The last statement follows from Lemma
2.4
.
∎

2.3.
Infinitesimal generators of composition operators semigroups

Associated to any semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{D}
, there is a family of composition operators
\{C_{\varphi_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
, defined
on the space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
by

C_{\varphi_{t}}f=f\circ\varphi_{t}.

Obviously,
\{C_{\varphi_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
has the semigroup property:

(1)

C_{\varphi_{0}}=I
(the identity operator);

(2)

C_{\varphi_{t}}C_{\varphi_{s}}=C_{\varphi_{t+s}}
for all
t,s\geq 0
.

Recall that an operator semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
acting on a Banach space
X
is called
strongly continuous
or
C_{0}
-semigroup
if it satisfies

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}T_{t}f=f

for
f\in X
. A remarkable result of Berkson and Porta
[
4
]
states that any semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
always induces a strongly continuous semigroup on the classical Hardy spaces
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
,
1\leq p<\infty
.

Given a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on a Banach space
X
, recall that its generator is the closed and
densely defined linear operator
\Gamma
given by

\Gamma f=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{T_{t}f-f}{t}

with domain
{\mathcal{D}}\Gamma=\{f\in X:\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{T_{t}f-f}{t}\kern 5.0pt\text{exists}\}
. It is well-known that the semigroup is
determined uniquely by its generator (see,
e.g.
,
[
12
,
25
]
).

In the particular instance of
\{C_{\varphi_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
, if
G
is the infinitesimal generator of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, the generator
\Gamma
of the operator semigroup
\{C_{\varphi_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
,
p\in[1,\infty)
is given by

\Gamma f=G^{\prime}f

for any
f\in{\mathcal{D}}\Gamma=\{f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D}):\,G^{\prime}f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})\}
.

In
[
30
]
Siskakis showed that a complex number
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\Gamma
if and only if
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, where
h
is the Koenigs function of the semigroup. At this regard, observe that if
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
,
h
its Koenigs function and
\Omega:=h(\mathbb{D})
the Koenigs domain, for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,

e^{\lambda h(\varphi_{t}(z))}=e^{\lambda t}e^{\lambda h(z)},\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

that is,
e^{\lambda h}
, is a
semi-model
of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
By conformal invariance of
H^{p}
spaces, we get that

\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\Gamma
if and only if
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
; this is the criterion which we will mostly use.

Recently, Betsakos
[
5
]
has studied the point spectrum of
\Gamma
in terms of the geometry of the Koenigs domain of the semigroup. As we mentioned in the introduction, our aim is to study the completeness of eigenfunctions of
\Gamma
.

3.
Frequencies of semigroups in Hardy spaces

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be the Koenigs domain of a semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. For
p\in[1,+\infty]
, we define the set of
p
-frequencies
of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to be

\Lambda_{p}(\Omega):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)\}.

To each
\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
we associate the
linear space of
p
-frequencies
of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
defined as

\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega):=\hbox{span}\{e^{\lambda z}:\lambda\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)\}.

If
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p\in[1,\infty)
, we say that
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has
p
-complete frequencies
.

If
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
we say that
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has
\infty
-weak-star complete frequencies
.

Frequencies of semigroups have been studied by D. Betsakos
[
5
]
. We state here his results in this form:

Theorem 3.2

(Betsakos)
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup of holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
, with associated Koenigs domain
\Omega
.

(1)

If
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is hyperbolic and
p\in[1,\infty)
then there exist
c_{1}\in(0,+\infty]
,
c_{2}\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\frac{c_{1}}{p}<{\sf Re}\,\lambda<\frac{c_{2}}{p}\right\}\subseteq\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)\subseteq\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\frac{c_{1}}{p}<{\sf Re}\,\lambda\leq\frac{c_{2}}{p}\right\}.

In particular,
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,\lambda=0\}\subseteq\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
.

(2)

If
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is parabolic and
p\in[1,\infty)
then

\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)\subseteq\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,\lambda\leq 0\right\}.

We examine some easy properties of frequencies.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
be the Koenigs domain of a semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Then:

(1)

\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
is a convex set of
\mathbb{C}
and
0\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
.

(2)

If
p,q\in[1,+\infty)
then
\Lambda_{q}(\Omega)=\frac{p}{q}\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
.

(3)

If
1\leq p\leq q\leq+\infty
then
\Lambda_{q}(\Omega)\subseteq\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
and hence
\mathcal{E}_{q}(\Omega)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
. In particular,
\Lambda_{\infty}(\Omega)\subseteq\bigcap_{1\leq p<\infty}\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)\subseteq\bigcap_{1\leq p<\infty}\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
.

(4)

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{\infty}(\Omega)
if and only if
\Omega\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,(\lambda z)<M\}
for some
M\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, if
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\infty}(\Omega)
then
t\lambda\in\Lambda_{\infty}(\Omega)
for all
t\geq 0
.

(5)

If
D
is the Koenigs domain of another semigroup and
\Omega\subset D
, then
\Lambda_{p}(D)\subseteq\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{E}_{p}(D)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
.

Proof.

(1) It is clear that
0\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
. Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
.

Hence, there exist two harmonic functions
u_{1},u_{2}
in
\Omega
such that
|e^{\lambda_{j}z}|^{p}\leq u_{j}(z)
for
z\in\Omega
and
j=1,2
.

Let
t\in(0,1)
. We have to prove that
e^{t\lambda_{1}z+(1-t)\lambda_{2}z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
. Simple considerations show that

|e^{t\lambda_{1}z+(1-t)\lambda_{2}z}|^{p}=e^{pt{\sf Re}\,(\lambda_{1}z)}e^{p(1-t){\sf Re}\,(\lambda_{2}z)}\leq e^{p{\sf Re}\,(\lambda_{1}z)}+e^{p{\sf Re}\,(\lambda_{2}z)}\leq u_{1}(z)+u_{2}(z),

and we are done.

(2) Assume
\lambda\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
, that is,
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
, and let
u
be an harmonic majorant of
|e^{\lambda z}|^{p}
. Hence,

|e^{\frac{p}{q}\lambda z}|^{q}=|e^{\lambda z}|^{p}\leq u(z),

which proves that
\frac{p}{q}\lambda\in\Lambda_{q}(\Omega)
.

(3) It follows immediately from the inclusion
H^{q}(\Omega)\subset H^{p}(\Omega)
.

(4) It is obvious since
|e^{\lambda z}|=e^{{\sf Re}\,(\lambda z)}
.

(5) The restriction map from
D
to
\Omega
induces a continuous injective linear map from
H^{p}(D)
to
H^{p}(\Omega)
, hence, if
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(D)
then
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
, and the result follows.
∎

Frequencies are not conformal invariant. However, they well behave under affine maps, we state here the lemma we need:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
T(z)=az+b
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\Omega
be a Koenigs domain of a semigroup, and let
D=T^{-1}(\Omega)
. Then for every
p\in[1,+\infty]
,

\Lambda_{p}(D)=a\Lambda_{p}(\Omega).

Moreover,
\overline{\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)}^{H^{p}(\Omega)}=H^{p}(\Omega)
(
respectively
,
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
) if and only if
\overline{\mathcal{E}_{p}(D)}^{H^{p}(D)}=H^{p}(D)
(respectively,
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(D)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(D)
).

Proof.

If
\lambda\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
then
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
. Hence,
e^{\lambda T(z)}\in H^{p}(D)
, that is,
e^{\lambda b}e^{a\lambda z}\in H^{p}(D)
and thus
e^{a\lambda z}\in H^{p}(D)
. Therefore,
a\lambda\in\Lambda_{p}(D)
. A similar argument proves that if
\mu\in\Lambda_{p}(D)
then
\frac{\mu}{a}\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
, and the first statement follows.

In order to prove the last assertion, let
1\leq p<\infty
(the argument for
p=\infty
is similar) and note that
H^{p}(D)=H^{p}(\Omega)\circ T
. Hence,
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)\circ T
is dense in
H^{p}(D)
.
Since

\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)\circ T=\hbox{span}\{e^{a\lambda z}:\lambda\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)\}=\hbox{span}\{e^{\mu z}:\mu\in\Lambda_{p}(D)\}=\mathcal{E}_{p}(D),

we have the result.
∎

\hbox{span}\{e^{i\lambda z}:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{S})
.

The proofs of the two results are similar, so we only give the proof of the second theorem.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and observe that
|e^{itz}|\leq e^{|t|\frac{\pi}{2}}
. Hence,
e^{itz}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
.

Now, let
t\geq 0
. For
z\in\mathbb{S}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{C},{\sf Re}\,\beta>\frac{\pi}{2}
, let

\Phi_{\beta}(z):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{itz}e^{-t\beta}dt.

Since, for every
z\in\mathbb{S}
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}|e^{itz}e^{-t\beta}|dt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t({\sf Re}\,\beta-\frac{\pi}{2})}dt,

the integral converges uniformly for
z\in\mathbb{S}
, and

\Phi_{\beta}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{itz}e^{-t\beta}dt=\frac{1}{-iz+\beta}.

This also proves that
\Phi_{\beta}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
.

Now, for
R>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{C},{\sf Re}\,\beta>\frac{\pi}{2}
,
z\in\mathbb{S}
, let

\Phi_{\beta}^{R}(z):=\int_{0}^{R}e^{itz}e^{-t\beta}dt.

By the same token as before, we see that
\{\Phi_{\beta}^{R}\}
converges uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{S}
to
\Phi_{\beta}
as
R\to+\infty
, hence,
\{\Phi_{\beta}^{R}\}
converges to
\Phi_{\beta}
in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
.

Now, fix
R>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
,
{\sf Re}\,\beta>\frac{\pi}{2}
. Hence,
\Phi_{\beta}^{R}
can be seen as the Laplace transform of the complex Borel measure compactly supported in
[0,+\infty)
defined by
\mu:=\chi_{[0,R]}e^{-t\beta}dt
.

For
n=1,2,\ldots
, we define
\mu_{n}
as

(4.1)

\mu_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu([\frac{(j-1)R}{n},\frac{jR}{n}))\delta_{\frac{jR}{n}}.

For each
n
, the measure
\mu_{n}
has support on a finite number of points, and
\{\mu_{n}\}
converges to
\mu
in the sense of measures, for
n\to\infty
. Let

P_{n}(z):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{itz}d\mu_{n}(t).

By definition,
P_{n}(z)\in\hbox{span}\{e^{itz}:t\geq 0\}
for all
n
. Also, since the support of
\mu_{n}
is contained in
[0,R]
for all
n
, there exists
C>0
such that
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{S}}|P_{n}(z)|\leq C
.

Thus, for every
z\in\mathbb{S}
fixed,

\lim_{n\to\infty}|P_{n}(z)-\Phi_{\beta}^{R}(z)|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big|\int_{0}^{R}e^{itz}\,d\mu_{n}(t)-e^{itz}e^{\beta t}\,dt\Big|=0,

where the last limit is
0
because the latter integral converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
for
z
fixed since
\{\mu_{n}\}
converges to
\mu
in the sense of measures. Thus,
\{P_{n}\}
converges weak-star in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
to
\Phi_{\beta}^{R}
.

This proves that rational functions with simple poles in

\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,z>\frac{\pi}{2}\}

can be approximated in the weak-star topology of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
by elements of
\hbox{span}\{e^{itz},t\geq 0\}
.

A similar argument shows that rational functions with simple poles in

\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,z<-\frac{\pi}{2}\}

can be approximated in the weak-star topology of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
by elements of
\hbox{span}\{e^{itz},t\leq 0\}
.

Therefore, rational functions with simple poles outside
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
are contained in the closure of
\hbox{span}\{e^{itz}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
in weak-star topology of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
. Each rational function in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
can be uniformly approximated in
\mathbb{S}
by rational functions with simple poles.
Since
\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}
is a compact finitely connected proper subset of the Riemann sphere and

\hbox{Int}(\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty})=\mathbb{S}
, rational functions in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})

are weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
by
[
19
, Theorem 5.3]
(note that this theorem is formulated in
[
19
]
only for compact subsets of the complex plane, but by applying Möbius transformations, one can extend it to
compact proper subsets of the Riemann sphere).
Thus
\hbox{span}\{e^{i\lambda z}:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
.

The case
p\in[1,\infty)
follows at once from Lemma
2.4
.
∎

4.2.
Logarithmic starlike at infinity domains

In this section we consider domains which might not be contained in any half-plane (so that they have no
\infty
-frequencies), for which however there is density in
H^{p}
of linear combinations of exponentials.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a a function such that

(1)

\psi
is differentiable and there exists
K>0
such that
|\psi^{\prime}|\leq K
on
\mathbb{R}
,

(2)

there exist
R>0
and
0<a<1
such that
\psi(y)\geq-a\log|y|
for
|y|\geq R
.

We call the corresponding domain

\Omega:=\{x+iy:x>\psi(y)\}

a
logarithmic starlike at infinity domain
.

It is easy to see that any logarithmic starlike at infinity domain
\Omega
is a Jordan domain in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
and that, if
b\in\mathbb{R}
then
\Omega+b
is still a logarithmic starlike at infinity domain.

We show now that every logarithmic starlike at infinity domain is biholomorphic to a bounded Jordan domain via a particular map. For
z\neq 0
, let

\alpha(z):=\int_{-1}^{0}(1+\lambda)^{2}e^{\lambda z}\,d\lambda=\frac{-2e^{-z}-2z+z^{2}+2}{z^{3}}.

Also, for
b>0
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
let

T_{b}(z)=z+b.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a logarithmic starlike at infinity domain. Then there exists
b>0
such that the function
\alpha\circ T_{b}
is a biholomorphism from
\Omega
onto a bounded Jordan domain
G
, and, in particular it extends as a homeomorphism from
\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
to
\overline{G}
.

Proof.

Let
\psi
be the defining function of
\Omega
. Let
b>0
and let
\Omega_{b}:=T_{b}(\Omega)
. The defining function of
\Omega_{b}
is
\psi_{b}:=\psi+b
. Choose
b>0
so large that
\psi_{b}(y)\geq-a\log|y|
whenever
|y|\geq 1
and
\psi_{b}(y)\geq 1
if
|y|\leq 1
.
Then the closure of
\Omega_{b}
does not contain
0
.
We observe that

(4.2)

|e^{-z}|\leq|z|^{a}\quad\text{for any }z=x+iy\in\overline{\Omega_{b}}.

Indeed,
if
|y|\leq 1
, then
x\geq\psi_{b}(y)\geq 1
, and so

|e^{-z}|=e^{-x}\leq 1\leq|z|^{a}
. If
|y|>1
, then

|e^{-z}|=e^{-x}\leq e^{-\psi_{b}(y)}\leq|y|^{a}\leq|z|^{a}
.

All these conclusions remain true if
b
is replaced by a even larger constant.

Let
\eta(z)=1/\alpha(z)
. A direct differentiation gives that

\eta^{\prime}(z)=\frac{z^{4}+\varkappa_{1}(z)}{z^{4}+\varkappa_{2}(z)},

where

\varkappa_{1}(z)=-4z^{3}+6z^{2}-(2z^{3}+6z^{2})e^{-z},\quad z^{4}+\varkappa_{2}(z)=(z^{2}-2z+2-2e^{-z})^{2}.

Choose a small
\delta>0
. By (
4.2
), one can find a large
b>0
such that
for all
z\in\partial\Omega_{b}
, one has

|\varkappa_{j}(z)|\leq\delta|z|^{4},\quad j=1,2.

Hence

(4.3)

|\eta^{\prime}(z)-1|=\bigg|\frac{1+\varkappa_{1}(z)z^{-4}}{1+\varkappa_{2}(z)z^{-4}}\,-1\bigg|\leq\frac{2\delta}{1-\delta}.

We parametrize
\partial\Omega_{b}
by
z(t):=\psi(t)+b+it
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since
z^{\prime}(t)=\psi^{\prime}(t)+i
and
|\psi^{\prime}(t)|\leq K
, we have

\varepsilon\leq\arg z^{\prime}(t)\leq\pi-\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon=\arg(K+i)\in(0,\pi/2)
.
Take
\delta
so small that (
4.3
) implies
that

|\arg\eta^{\prime}(z)|<\varepsilon/2

for all
z\in\partial\Omega_{b}
. Then we get

\varepsilon/2\leq\arg\frac{d}{dt}\eta(z(t))=\arg\big(\eta^{\prime}(z(t))\cdot z^{\prime}(t)\big)\leq\pi-\varepsilon/2

for all
t
. This implies that
\eta(z(t))
parametrizes a curve without self-intersections. Moreover,
{\sf Im}\,\eta(z(t))>0
for

t>0
and
{\sf Im}\,\eta(z(t))<0
for
t<0
. Note that
|\eta(z(t))|\to\infty
as
t\to\pm\infty
. Since
\Omega_{b}
is a Jordan domain in the Riemann sphere
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, it follows that
\alpha=1/\eta
maps the boundary
of this domain onto a positively oriented curve
\partial G
, containing
0
, where

G
is a Jordan domain.
By the argument principle,
\alpha
is univalent on
\Omega_{b}
and maps
\Omega_{b}
onto
G
. By Carathéodory extension theorem,
\alpha
extends as a homeomorphism on the boundaries.
∎

Let
M_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
denote the set of compactly supported (finite) complex Borel measures on
[0,+\infty)
and let

\mathcal{LM}=\{\mathcal{L}\mu:\;\mu\in M_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))\}

to be the set of Laplace transforms of these measures. That is,

\mathcal{L}\mu(z):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-sz}d\mu(s).

The set
\mathcal{LM}
is an algebra, consisting of entire functions. The restrictions of functions in
\mathcal{LM}
to
\mathbb{C}_{+}
belong to
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.

Let
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
be algebra of continuous functions on
\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}

that are holomorphic on
\Omega
and have a finite limit at infinity.

We prove the following density result:

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a logarithmic starlike at infinity domain. Let
p_{0}\geq 1
and suppose that

(4.4)

e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\quad\forall\lambda\leq 0.

Then,

(1)

\mathcal{LM}\cap\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
is dense in
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
,

(2)

e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
for all
\lambda\leq 0
, and

\overline{\hbox{span}\{e^{\lambda z}:\lambda\leq 0\}}^{H^{p}(\Omega)}=H^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

(1) First observe that it is enough to prove the result for
T_{b}(\Omega)
for some
b>0
. Hence, by Lemma
4.5
, we can assume that
\alpha:\Omega\to G
is a biholomorphism. Note that, by definition,
\alpha\in\mathcal{LM}\cap\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
and let
\tilde{f}=f\circ\alpha^{-1}
.
Then
\tilde{f}
is continuous on
\overline{G}
and hence, by Mergelyan’s approximation theorem there exists a sequence of holomorphic polynomials
\{p_{n}\}
such that

\|\tilde{f}-p_{n}\|_{\infty,\overline{G}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore,
\{p_{n}\circ\alpha\}
approximates
f
uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
.

Since the Laplace transform of the convolution of two measures is the product of the Laplace transforms of these measures, it follows that
p_{n}\circ\alpha\in\mathcal{LM}\cap\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
for all
n
, and (1) follows.

(2) By Proposition
3.3
(2),
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
for all
\lambda\leq 0
and
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
and let
\mu\in M_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, whose support is contained in
an interval
[0,R]
. For every
n=1,2,\ldots
we define
\mu_{n}
as in (
4.1
).

Each
\mu_{n}
is a measure supported on a finite set. Moreover, by definition,
\mathcal{L}\mu_{n}
is a finite combination of exponentials of type
e^{tz}
,
t\leq 0
, hence, by (
4.4
),
\mathcal{L}\mu_{n}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
.

Now, we claim that
\{\mathcal{L}\mu_{n}\}
converges to
\mathcal{L}\mu
in
H^{p}(\Omega)
.

To this aim, note that the map
[0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto e^{tz}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
is continuous, thus uniformly continuous on
[0,R]
. Hence, given
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|e^{-\lambda z}-e^{-\tilde{\lambda}z}\|_{H^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon
for every
\lambda,\tilde{\lambda}\in[0,R]
such that
|\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}|<\delta
. Let
I_{j}:=[\frac{(j-1)R}{n},\frac{jR}{n})
, with
n
sufficiently large so that
\frac{1}{n}<\delta
. Hence,

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{L}\mu-\mathcal{L}\mu_{n}\|_{H^{p}(\Omega)}&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\int_{I_{j}}e^{-\lambda z}d\mu(\lambda)-\mu(I_{j})e^{-\frac{jR}{n}z}\|_{H^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\int_{I_{j}}(e^{-\lambda z}-e^{-\frac{jR}{n}z})d\mu(\lambda)\|_{H^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{I_{j}}\|e^{-\lambda z}-e^{-\frac{jR}{n}z}\|_{H^{p}(\Omega)}|d\mu(\lambda)|\leq\varepsilon|\mu([0,R])|.\end{split}

Therefore, each function in
\mathcal{LM}
belongs to the closure of linear combination of exponentials
in
H^{p}(\Omega)
.

Now,
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
is dense in

H^{p}(\Omega)
and by (1), each function in
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
can be approximated uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
by functions in
\mathcal{LM}\cap\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
, which belong to the closure of the span of exponentials in

H^{p}(\Omega)
. Since the topology of
H^{p}(\Omega)
is weaker than the topology of
{\mathcal{A}}(\Omega)
, assertion (2) follows.
∎

Definition 4.7
.

A logarithmic starlike at infinity domain
\Omega
which satisfies (
4.4
) is a logarithmic starlike at infinity domain
with
p
-frequencies
.

Further positive and negative results on the
p
-completeness of frequencies in logarithmic starlike domains at infinity
are given in Theorem
5.10
and
in Section
10
(where, in particular,
Theorem
1.4
is proved).

5.
Weak-star density for Koenigs domains

The aim of this section is to extend the results of the previous section to more general Koenigs domains.

To this aim, assume that
\Omega,D
are Koenigs domains of two non-elliptic semigroups. Suppose
\Omega\subseteq D
. Then the inclusion
\iota:D\to\Omega
induces the restriction map

H^{p}(D)\to H^{p}(\Omega)

which is a continuous injective, for every
p\in[1,+\infty]
.

The following statement follows at once from the conformal invariance of Hardy space and recalling that
\mathcal{E}_{p}(D)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
for
p\in[1,+\infty]
:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\Omega,D
be Koenigs domains of two non-elliptic semigroups. Suppose
\Omega\subseteq D
.

•

Assume
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(D)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(D)
. Then
H^{\infty}(D)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(D)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
. If this is the case,
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is also weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

•

Assume
\mathcal{E}_{p}(D)
is dense in
H^{p}(D)
for some
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then
H^{p}(D)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{p}(D)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
. If this is the case,
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is also dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
.

In our context, the domain
D
of the previous lemma will be either a strip, or a half-plane or a logarithmic starlike at infinity domain (for the
p\in[1,\infty)
case). Thus, we are aimed to understand when
H^{\infty}(D)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
in case
D
is either a strip or a half-plane.

In order to see this, we need the following proposition, which is a simple consequence of the work of Sarason
[
28
,
29
]
on weak-star generators of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
:

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Omega,D
be simply connected domains in
\mathbb{C}
and assume
\Omega\subset D
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

H^{\infty}(D)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

(2)

For every domain
E\subseteq D
such that
\Omega\subsetneq E
there exist
f\in H^{\infty}(E)
and
z_{0}\in E\setminus\Omega
such that
|f(z_{0})|>\sup_{z\in\Omega}|f(z)|
.

Proof.

Using a Riemann map of
D
, we can assume that
D=\mathbb{D}
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{D}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of all holomorphic polynomials. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
be a Riemann map. Then by
[
28
, Corollary 2]
,
\mathcal{P}\circ\varphi
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if (2) holds. By the conformal invariance of Hardy spaces, it follows that (2) is equivalent to
\mathcal{P}
being weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Since
\mathcal{P}\subset H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, it follows that (1) is equivalent to (2).
∎

As a first corollary we have

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\Omega,D
be simply connected domains in
\mathbb{C}
and assume
\Omega\subset D
. Assume that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{D})=D
and that
H^{\infty}(D)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
.

Proof.

Recall that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})
is the union of all open sets
A\subset\overline{\Omega}
. Since
\overline{\Omega}\subseteq\overline{D}
, it follows that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})\subseteq\hbox{Int}(\overline{D})=D
.

Let
E:=\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})\subseteq D
. Let
f\in H^{\infty}(E)
and
z_{0}\in E
. Then there exists a sequence
\{z_{n}\}\subset\Omega
converging to
z_{0}
. Thus,

|f(z_{0})|=\lim_{n\to\infty}|f(z_{n})|\leq\sup_{z\in\Omega}|f(z)|.

Since
H^{\infty}(D)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we get
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
by Proposition
5.2
.
∎

As a matter of notation, we say that a Jordan arc
\Gamma
with end points
a,b
,
a\neq b
is a
crosscut
in
\mathbb{D}
if
\Gamma\setminus\{a,b\}\subset\mathbb{D}
and
a,b\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. We stress that, by definition, a crosscut has
different
end points.
In what follows, for
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we set

L_{y}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,z=y\}.

We need the following lemma which is a simple consequence of
[
28
, Proposition 4]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{D}
be a simply connected domain. Suppose that every crosscut of
\mathbb{D}
intersects
G
. Then for every
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
and every
z\in\mathbb{D}

|f(z)|\leq\sup_{\zeta\in G}|f(\zeta)|.

Now we are in good shape to characterize Koenigs domains contained either in a half-plane or a strip for which linear combinations of exponentials are weak-star dense. We start with domains contained in a half-plane.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\Omega
be the Koenigs domain of a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
. Suppose
\Omega\subseteq H
, where
H
is a half-plane. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(H)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

(2)

\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected.

Proof.

We first show that (1) implies (2). By Lemma
5.1
and Corollary
5.3
,
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
.

In order to show that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected, we first note that by
Lemma
5.1
and Proposition
5.2
, (1) is equivalent to

(
\star
) For every domain
E\subseteq H
such that
\Omega\subsetneq E
there exist
f\in H^{\infty}(E)
and
z_{0}\in E
such that
|f(z_{0})|>\sup_{z\in\Omega}|f(z)|
.

Suppose by contradiction that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is not connected.

Note that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has a connected component,
U_{0}
such that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{H}\subset U_{0}
. Let
E:=H\setminus\overline{U_{0}}
. Since we are assuming that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is not connected, it follows that
\Omega\subsetneq E\subset H
.

Now, let
f\in H^{\infty}(E)
. Let
z_{0}\in E
. If
z_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
then
|f(z_{0})|\leq\sup_{z\in\Omega}|f(z)|
.

If
z_{0}\in E\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, then
z_{0}
belongs to a connected component
U_{1}
of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
different from
U_{0}
. Since
\Omega
is starlike at infinity,
z_{0}-t\not\in\overline{\Omega}
for all
t\geq 0
. It follows that
z_{0}-t\in U_{1}
for all
t\geq 0
. Hence

z_{0}-t\notin U_{0}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Since
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{H}\subset U_{0}
, this implies that the boundary of
H
has to be parallel to the real axis.
Up to a translation and reflection we can assume that
H=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>0\}
.

Thus,
z_{0}:=x+iy
for some
y>0
. We claim that there exists
y_{0}\in[0,y)
such that, setting
Q:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>y_{0}\}
, we have

(5.1)

L_{y_{0}}\subset\overline{\Omega}\cap\overline{Q}.

Indeed, let
t\geq 0
. Note that if
x-t+ir\not\in\overline{\Omega}
for all
r\in[0,y]
, then
[-1,y]\ni r\mapsto x-t+ir
would connect
U_{1}
with
U_{0}
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, a contradiction. Thus, for every
t\geq 0
there exists
r(t)\in[0,y]
such that
x-t+ir\not\in\overline{\Omega}
for all
r\in(r(t),y]
and
x-t+ir(t)\in\overline{\Omega}
. Since
\Omega
is starlike at infinity, then
a-t+ir(t)\in\overline{\Omega}
for all
a\geq x
. Hence,
[0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto r(t)
is non-increasing. Let
y_{0}:=\lim_{t\to+\infty}r(t)
. Since
a-t+ir(t)\in\overline{\Omega}
for all
a\geq x
and
t\geq 0
, it follows that
L_{y_{0}}\subset\overline{\Omega}\cap\overline{Q}
, and (
5.1
) holds.

It follows from (
5.1
) that
L_{y_{0}}\cap U_{0}=\emptyset
.
Since
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{H}\subset U_{0}
and
U_{0}
is connected,
it follows that
\overline{U_{0}}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus Q
. Hence
Q\subset E
.
Note also that
Q\cap\Omega
is a domain starlike at infinity, in particular, it is simply connected.

Recall that
f\in H^{\infty}(E)
, thus
f|_{Q}\in H^{\infty}(Q)
. We claim

(5.2)

\sup_{\zeta\in Q}|f(\zeta)|=\sup_{\zeta\in\Omega\cap Q}|f(\zeta)|.

In order to prove (
5.2
), we denote by
T
the Cayley transform which maps
Q
onto
\mathbb{D}
and let
G:=T(Q\cap\Omega)
. We show that every crosscut of
\mathbb{D}
intersects
G
. By Lemma
5.4
, this implies that
|f(T^{-1}(\zeta))|\leq\sup_{\xi\in G}|f(T^{-1}(\xi))|
for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
and thus (
5.2
) follows.

Assume by contradiction that there is a crosscut
\tilde{\Gamma}
in
\mathbb{D}
which does not intersect
G
. Let
\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
,
\tilde{a}\neq\tilde{b}
be the end points of
\tilde{\Gamma}
. Then
\tilde{\Gamma}\setminus\{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\}\subset\mathbb{D}\setminus G
. Hence,
\Gamma:=T^{-1}(\tilde{\Gamma})
is a Jordan arc in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
with end points
a:=T^{-1}(\tilde{a})
and
b:=T^{-1}(\tilde{b})
such that
a,b\in\partial_{\infty}Q
,
a\neq b
and
\Gamma\setminus\{a,b\}\subset Q\setminus\Omega
. In particular, either
a
or
b
is different from
\infty
. We assume
a\in L_{y_{0}}
. Let
u\in\Gamma\setminus\{a,b\}
and let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be the Jordan arc given by the part of
\Gamma
connecting
a
and
u
. Since
\Omega
is starlike at infinity,
\cup_{t\geq 0}(\Gamma^{\prime}-t)\cap\Omega=\emptyset
. This implies easily that
L_{y_{0}}\not\subset\overline{\Omega}\cap\overline{Q}
, contradicting (
5.1
). Thus, (
5.2
) holds.

Since
z_{0}\in Q
by construction, (
5.2
) implies

|f(z_{0})|\leq\sup_{\zeta\in Q}|f(\zeta)|=\sup_{\zeta\in\Omega\cap Q}|f(\zeta)|\leq\sup_{\zeta\in\Omega}|f(\zeta)|.

Summing up, we proved that for every
z_{0}\in E
and
f\in H^{\infty}(E)
we have
|f(z_{0})|\leq\sup_{\zeta\in\Omega}|f(\zeta)|
, contradicting (
\star
). Hence
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected. Thus (2) holds.

Now, we show that (2) implies (1). By Lemma
5.1

and Theorem
4.2
, it is enough to show that
H^{\infty}(H)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
. To this aim, let
T:H\to\mathbb{D}
be a Riemann map and let
G:=T(\Omega)
. Hence, it is enough to prove that
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(G)
. Since

is a Moebius transformation, hence a homeomorphism of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, and by hypothesis (2), it follows easily that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{G})=G
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{G}
is connected. Thus the result follows from Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The previous theorem shows that Theorem
4.3
cannot be improved, in the sense that
\hbox{span}\{e^{it}:t\leq 0\}
(or
\hbox{span}\{e^{it}:t\geq 0\}
) is not weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
.

Now we consider the case when
\Omega
is contained in a strip.
Notice that the set
{\sf Re}\,\Omega=\{{\sf Re}\,z:z\in\Omega\}
is not bounded from below if and only if
\overline{\Omega}
contains some horizontal line
L_{y}
. Indeed,
if
{\sf Re}\,\Omega
is not bounded from below, then one can find a sequence of points
w_{n}\in\Omega

such that
{\sf Re}\,w_{n}\to-\infty
and
{\sf Im}\,w_{n}
tend to a real number
y
. Since
\Omega
is starlike at infinity,
w_{n}+\mathbb{R}_{+}\subseteq\Omega
for all
n
, and it follows easily that
L_{y}
is contained in
\overline{\Omega}
.

We need a preliminary lemma:

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\Omega
be the Koenigs domain of a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
. Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{S}
, where
\mathbb{S}
is a strip. Then

(1)

\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has exactly one connected component
if and only if
{\sf Re}\,\Omega
is bounded from below.

(2)

Now assume that
{\sf Re}\,\Omega
is not bounded from below.
Set

y_{0}=\inf\{y:L_{y}\subset\overline{\Omega}\},\quad y_{1}=\sup\{y:L_{y}\subset\overline{\Omega}\}.

Then
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has exactly two connected components if

\{z\in\mathbb{C}:y_{0}\leq{\sf Im}\,z\leq y_{1}\}\subset\overline{\Omega}
, and has more than two
connected components if

\{z\in\mathbb{C}:y_{0}\leq{\sf Im}\,z\leq y_{1}\}\not\subset\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

(1) Since
\Omega
is starlike at infinity, it follows that if
p,q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, then
p-t,q-t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
for all
t\geq 0
. Thus, if
\Omega\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,z>R\}
, then
p-t,q-t\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,z<R\}
for
t
large enough, and hence
p,q
can be joined
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}

by a Jordan arc, showing that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has one connected component.

Conversely, if

\overline{\Omega}
contains a line
L_{y}
, then this line separates

\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
into two open nonempty sets, so that

\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at least two connected components.

(2) Suppose that
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:y_{0}\leq{\sf Im}\,z\leq y_{1}\}\not\subset\overline{\Omega}
.
Notice that
the lines
L_{y_{0}}
,
L_{y_{1}}
are contained in
\overline{\Omega}
.
Hence
y_{0}\neq y_{1}
.

The set
\mathbb{C}\setminus(L_{y_{0}}\cup L_{y_{1}})

has three connected components. Clearly, each of these components contains a non-empty part of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. Therefore

\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at least three connected components.

Now consider the remaining case when

\{z\in\mathbb{C}:y_{0}\leq{\sf Im}\,z\leq y_{1}\}\subset\overline{\Omega}
.
Then
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is a disjoint union
of open sets
A_{-}:=\{z:{\sf Im}\,z<y_{0}\}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
and

A_{+}:=\{z:{\sf Im}\,z>y_{1}\}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.
We assert that
A_{-}
and
A_{+}
are the two connected components of

\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. We need only to check that these two sets are connected.
Notice that for any sequence of points
\{w_{n}\}\subseteq\overline{\Omega}

such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\sf Re}\,w_{n}=-\infty
and the limit
y:=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\sf Im}\,w_{n}
exists, one has

y_{0}\leq y\leq y_{1}
.

Let
a<b
be real numbers such that

\mathbb{S}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:a<{\sf Im}\,z<b\}.

Fix any point
z_{0}
with
{\sf Im}\,z_{0}<a
, then
z_{0}\in A_{-}
.
Take any
z_{1}\in A_{-}
with
a\leq{\sf Im}\,z_{0}<y_{0}
.
We assert that there exists
t<\min({\sf Re}\,z_{0},{\sf Re}\,z_{1})
such that the interval

[t+i{\sf Im}\,z_{0},t+i{\sf Im}\,z_{1}]
is contained in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.
Indeed, if it were not the case, then
one could find a sequence of points
t_{n}+is_{n}\in\overline{\Omega}
such
that
t_{n}\to-\infty
and
{\sf Im}\,z_{0}\leq s_{n}\leq{\sf Im}\,z_{1}
for all
n
.
As in the argument preceding the formulation of Lemma, this would imply
that there is a line
L_{y}\subset\overline{\Omega}
with

{\sf Im}\,z_{0}\leq y\leq{\sf Im}\,z_{1}<y_{0}
. This contradicts the definition of
y_{0}
.

If
[t+i{\sf Im}\,z_{0},t+i{\sf Im}\,z_{1}]
is contained in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
,
then the union of three segments
[z_{0},t+i{\sf Im}\,z_{0}]
,

[t+i{\sf Im}\,z_{0},t+i{\sf Im}\,z_{1}]
,
[t+i{\sf Im}\,z_{1},z_{1}]
connects
z_{0}
with

z_{1}
inside
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. It follows that
A_{-}
is connected.

A similar argument shows that
A_{+}
is connected.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\Omega
be the Koenigs domain of a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
. Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{S}
, where
\mathbb{S}
is a strip. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

(2)

\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components.

Proof.

Let
a<b
be real numbers such that
\mathbb{S}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:a<{\sf Im}\,w<b\}
.

Suppose (1) holds. By Lemma
5.1
and by Corollary
5.3
,
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
.

Also, by Lemma
5.1
and Proposition
5.2
, (1) is equivalent to

(
\star\star
) For every domain
E\subseteq\mathbb{S}
such that
\Omega\subsetneq E
there exist
f\in H^{\infty}(E)
and
z_{0}\in E
such that
|f(z_{0})|>\sup_{z\in\Omega}|f(z)|
.

Assume now by contradiction that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has more than two connected components.
Define
y_{0},y_{1}
as in Lemma
5.7
, and
let

Q:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:y_{0}<{\sf Im}\,w<y_{1}\}.

By Lemma
5.7
,

L_{y_{0}},L_{y_{1}}\subset\overline{\Omega}
and

Q\not\subset\overline{\Omega}
.
Let

E:=\Omega\cup Q.

It is easy to see that, by construction,
E
is a domain starlike at infinity,
\Omega\subsetneq E\subseteq\mathbb{S}
.

Now let
f\in H^{\infty}(E)
,

M:=\sup_{\zeta\in\Omega}|f(\zeta)|
and
z\in E
. If
z\in\overline{\Omega}\cap E
then clearly
|f(z)|\leq M
. If
z\in E\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, then by construction,
z\in Q
.

Let
g
be a Riemann map from
Q
onto
\mathbb{D}
and let
G:=g(Q\cap\Omega)
. Note that
Q\cap\Omega
is a (simply connected) starlike at infinity domain, so
G
is simply connected. We claim that every crosscut of
\mathbb{D}
intersects
G
. Once the claim is proved, by Lemma
5.4
, this implies that
|f(g^{-1}(\zeta))|\leq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{D}}|f(g^{-1}(\xi))|=\sup_{\xi\in G}|f(g^{-1}(\xi))|
for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
and thus

|f(z)|\leq\sup_{\zeta\in Q}|f(\zeta)|=\sup_{\zeta\in Q\cap\Omega}|f(\zeta)|\leq M.

Hence,
|f(z)|\leq\sup_{\zeta\in\Omega}|f(\zeta)|
for every
z\in E
, contradicting (
\star\star
).

So we have to prove that every crosscut of
\mathbb{D}
intersects
G
. Assume by contradiction that there is a crosscut
\tilde{\Gamma}
in
\mathbb{D}
which does not intersect
G
. Let
\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
,
\tilde{a}\neq\tilde{b}
be the end points of
\tilde{\Gamma}
. Then
\tilde{\Gamma}\setminus\{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\}\subset\mathbb{D}\setminus G
.

Now,
by applying translations and dilations, we can assume that

y_{0}=-\pi/2,y_{1}=\pi/2
.
Then we can take
g(w)=\frac{e^{w}-1}{e^{w}+1}
. In particular,
\lim_{{\sf Re}\,w\to-\infty}g(w)=-1
,
\lim_{{\sf Re}\,w\to+\infty}g(w)=1
and
g
extends continuously and injectively on
\partial Q
, with values different from
\pm 1
.

Hence,
\Gamma:=g^{-1}(\tilde{\Gamma})
is a Jordan arc in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
with end points
a:=g^{-1}(\tilde{a})
and
b:=g^{-1}(\tilde{b})
such that
a,b\in\partial_{\infty}Q
, and
\Gamma\setminus\{a,b\}\subset Q\setminus\Omega
. Moreover, if either
a\in\partial Q
or
b\in\partial Q
then
a\neq b
.

In case
a\in\partial Q
or
b\in\partial Q
, we get a contradiction arguing as in Theorem
5.5
.

In case
a=b=\infty
, let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous injective parametrization of
\Gamma\cap\mathbb{C}
. Hence
y_{0}<{\sf Im}\,\gamma(t)<y_{1}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\gamma(t)=\infty
in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. Now, the curve
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto g(\gamma(t))
parametrizes
\tilde{\Gamma}\cap\mathbb{D}
, and, by the previous discussion on
g
, we can assume
\lim_{t\to+\infty}g(\gamma(t))=1
and
\lim_{t\to-\infty}g(\gamma(t))=-1
. It follows that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}{\sf Re}\,\gamma(t)=+\infty
and
\lim_{t\to-\infty}{\sf Re}\,\gamma(t)=-\infty
. Then
\Gamma\cap\mathbb{C}
is a Jordan arc outside
\Omega
which divides
\mathbb{C}
into two connected components, one, say
V_{1}
containing
L_{y_{1}}
, and the other, say
V_{0}
, containing
L_{y_{0}}
. Then
\Omega
is contained either in
V_{0}
or in
V_{1}
. If
\Omega\subset V_{0}
then clearly
L_{y_{1}}\not\subset\overline{\Omega}
, while, if
\Omega\subset V_{1}
then
L_{y_{0}}\not\subset\overline{\Omega}
. In both cases, we have a contradiction and the claim is proved.

Assume (2) holds. Let
g:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{D}
be a Riemann map. As before, we assume that
y_{0}=-\pi/2,y_{1}=\pi/2
and
g(w)=\frac{e^{w}-1}{e^{w}+1}
.

Let
G:=g(\Omega)
. We claim that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{G}
is connected and
\hbox{Int}(\overline{G})=G
. As soon as the claim is proved, arguing as in the proof of “(2) implies (1)” of Theorem
5.5
, one can show that (1) holds.

Let us prove the claim. It is clear that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{G})=G
because
g:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{D}
is a homeomorphism and
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
.

Next, note that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
.
If
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
—and hence
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
—has one connected component, then by Lemma
5.7
,
\Omega\subset H:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,z>R\}
, for some
R\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
H\cap\mathbb{S}
is a Jordan domain in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, it follows by the Jordan-Schoenflies theorem that
g|_{H\cap\mathbb{S}}
extends to a homeomorphism
\tilde{g}:\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. Taking into account that
G=g(\Omega)=\tilde{g}(\Omega)
, we have that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{G}
is connected.

If
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has two connected components, by Lemma
5.7
, it follows that if
p\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega})\cap\mathbb{S}
then there exists a Jordan arc
\Gamma
in
\mathbb{C}
with one end point
p
and the other end point
\xi\in\partial\mathbb{S}
such that
\Gamma\setminus\{\xi\}\subset\mathbb{S}
and

\Gamma\cap\overline{\Omega}=\emptyset
. Therefore,
g(\Gamma)
is a Jordan arc in
\mathbb{D}\setminus\overline{G}
which connects
g(p)
to a point of
\partial\mathbb{D}
, from which it follows immediately that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{G}
is connected.
∎

The next corollary complements the previous theorems:

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
\Omega
be the Koenigs domain of a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
.

(1)

Suppose there is a strip
\mathbb{S}
such that

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{S}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1.a)

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

(1.b)

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

(2)

Suppose there is a half-plane
H
such that

\Omega\subseteq H
and
\Omega
is not contained in any strip. Then the following are equivalent:

(2.a)

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(H)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

(2.b)

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\mathbb{S})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
, it is clear that (1.a) implies (1.b) (
respectively
, (2.a) implies (2.b)), so we are left to show that (1.b) implies (1.a) (
respect.
, (2.b) implies (2.a)).

Let
D=\mathbb{S}
or

, according to hypothesis (1) or (2).

If
\Lambda_{\infty}(\Omega)=\Lambda_{\infty}(D)
, there is nothing to prove. Thus, we assume

\Lambda_{\infty}(D)\subsetneq\Lambda_{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in

.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the family of all half-planes which contain
\Omega
. Note that each
V\in\mathcal{H}
is starlike at infinity and convex. Then let
Q
be the intersection of all

. It is clear that
Q
is a convex domain, starlike at infinity and
\Omega\subseteq Q\subseteq D
.

By Proposition
3.3
.(4),
\Lambda_{\infty}(Q)=\Lambda_{\infty}(\Omega)
. Thus
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(Q)=\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
, and, since we are assuming that
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in

,
Lemma
5.1
implies that
H^{\infty}(Q)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Moreover, since

is convex, then
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{Q}
is either connected or has two connected components, and the latter possibility occurs if and only if

is a strip.

Therefore, if
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{Q}
is connected, by either Theorem
5.8
or Theorem
5.5
(depending whether we are in case (1) or (2)), we have that
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(D)
is weak-star dense in

, and since the latter is weak-star dense in

, we have that (1.b) implies (1.a) (

, (2.b) implies (2.a)).

If
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{Q}
has two connected components, then

is a strip. Hence, we are in case (1), and arguing as before using Theorem

∎

In case

has no

.

Let

, where

and

. By Lemma

it is enough to prove that

.

6.
Characterization of Koenigs domains
\Omega
such that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega

The aim of this section is to give a characterization of Koenigs domains of non-elliptic semigroups for which the interior of the closure in the Riemann sphere coincide with the domain. To this aim, we need some definitions and preliminary results.

6.1.
Prime ends and impressions

Let
h
be the Koenigs function of non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
and let
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
.

Let
\widehat{\Omega}
denote the Carathéodory compactification of
\Omega
, endowed with the Carathéodory topology, and let
\partial_{C}\Omega
be the set of prime ends of
\Omega
. Moreover, let
\hat{h}:\widehat{\mathbb{D}}\to\widehat{\Omega}
be the homeomorphism induced by
h
and, for
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, let
\underline{\sigma}
be the prime end defined by
\sigma
—more precisely, the identity map
{\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
extends as a homeomorphism
{\Phi_{\mathbb{D}}}
from
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
onto
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}
, and we let
\underline{\sigma}:={\Phi_{\mathbb{D}}}(\sigma)
. If
(C_{n})
,
n\geq 0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is a null-chain, every
C_{n}
divides
\Omega
in two simply connected domains. The interior part
V_{n}
of
C_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, is the connected component which does not contain
C_{0}
. The impression of the prime end
[(C_{n})]
represented by
(C_{n})
is then

I([(C_{n})])=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\overline{V_{n}}^{\infty}\subset\partial_{\infty}\Omega.

See,
e.g.

[
8
, Chapter 4]
for details about Carathéodory’s prime ends and Carathéodory’s topology.

Recall (see,
e.g.

[
8
, Corollary 11.1.7]
) that, if
h
is the Koenigs function of a semigroup, then for every
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, there exists

h^{\ast}(\sigma):=\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}h(z).

Therefore (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Prop. 4.4.4, Thm. 4.4.9]
), if
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, the
principal part
of
\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma})
(that is, the set of points which are accumulation points of any null chain representing
\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma})
) is
\{h^{\ast}(\sigma)\}
, while

(6.1)

I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma}))=\{p\in\partial_{\infty}\Omega:\exists\{z_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D},\lim_{n\to\infty}z_{n}=\sigma,\lim_{n\to\infty}h(z_{n})=p\}.

In particular,
h
is continuous at
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
if and only if the impression of
I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma}))
is a point.

6.2.
Maximal contact arcs

We are going to recall briefly the theory of maximal contact arcs for semigroups of the unit disc (we refer the reader to
[
8
, Cap 14.2]
for details).

If
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
, then for every
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and for every
t\geq 0
, there exists the non-tangential limit

\varphi^{\ast}_{t}(\sigma):=\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\varphi_{t}(z).

If for some
t_{0}>0
(and hence any
0\leq t\leq t_{0}
)
\varphi^{\ast}_{t_{0}}(\sigma)\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, then the curve
[0,t_{0})\ni t\mapsto\varphi^{\ast}_{t}(\sigma)
is a continuous injective curve on
\partial\mathbb{D}
, with the natural orientation.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
. A
contact arc
is an open arc
A\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
such that for every
\sigma\in A
there exists
t_{\sigma}>0
such that
\varphi^{\ast}_{t_{\sigma}}(\sigma)\in\partial\mathbb{D}
.

A contact arc is
maximal
if there exists no contact arc
B
such that
A\subsetneq B
.

It is clear that every contact arc is contained in a maximal contact arc. Let
A
be a maximal contact arc for a semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. As we remarked above,
A
has a natural orientation given by flowing
A
with
\varphi^{\ast}_{t}
and thus we can define the
starting point

x_{0}(A)
of
A
to be the end point of
A
where the flow starts and the
final point

x_{1}(A)
of
A
to be the end point of
A
where the flow ends.

A maximal contact arc
A
is
exceptional
if
x_{1}(A)
is the Denjoy-Wolff point of the semigroup.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a semigroup with Koenigs function
h
and
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
. If
A
is a maximal contact arc for
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, with initial point
x_{0}(A)
and final point
x_{1}(A)
then for
j=0,1
we have (see
[
8
, Proposition 14.2.13]
)

(6.2)

\lim_{A\ni\xi\to x_{j}(A)}h(\xi)=h^{\ast}(x_{j}(A)).

Moreover, either
x_{1}(A)
is the Denjoy-Wolff point of the semigroup, or
h^{\ast}(x_{1}(A))\in\mathbb{C}
and
h^{\ast}(x_{1}(A))+t\in\Omega
for all
t>0
(see
[
8
, Proposition 14.2.6]
).

For our aims, we also need the following lemma (which follows easily from
[
8
, Thm. 14.2.10]
):

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, Koenigs function
h
and Koenigs domain
\Omega
. An open arc
A\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
is a contact arc if and only if there exist
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
-\infty\leq a<b\leq+\infty
such that

h^{\ast}(A)=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=y_{0},a<{\sf Re}\,w<b\}.

If this is the case,
h
extends holomorphically through
A
. Moreover, there exist two maximal contact arcs
A,B
with final point
\sigma=x_{1}(A)=x_{1}(B)\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
if and only if
h^{\ast}(\sigma)\in\mathbb{C}
and there exists
r>0
such that

D(h^{\ast}(\sigma),r)\setminus\{z:{\sf Re}\,z\leq{\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma),{\sf Im}\,z={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)\}=\Omega\cap D(h^{\ast}(\sigma),r).

Also,
h
extends holomorphically through
A\cup B
and continuously through
\sigma
.

From this lemma it follows immediately:

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and Koenigs domain
\Omega
and let
\psi:I\to[-\infty,+\infty)
be the upper semicontinuous function defining
\Omega
, that is,
\Omega=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,z>\psi({\sf Im}\,z),{\sf Im}\,z\in I\}
. Then there exist two maximal contact arcs
A,B
with final point
\sigma=x_{1}(A)=x_{1}(B)\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
if and only if there exist
q\in\mathbb{R}
, an open interval
J\subset I
and
c_{0}\in J
such that
\psi(c_{0})>q
and
\psi(c)\leq q
for all
c\in J\setminus\{c_{0}\}
.

6.3.
Discontinuities of Koenigs functions

Definition 6.4
.

Let
h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
be the Koenigs function of a non-elliptic semigroup. The set of points of continuity of
h
is

\mathcal{C}(h):=\{\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}:\lim_{z\to\sigma}h(z)\ \hbox{exists in $\mathbb{C}_{\infty}$}\}.

The set of points of discontinuity is

\mathcal{S}(h):=\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathcal{C}(h).

By Collingwood maximality theorem (see,
e.g.
,
[
24
, Prop. 2.23]
) and since the Koenigs function
h
has non-tangential limit everywhere on
\partial\mathbb{D}
, the set
\mathcal{S}(h)
is a set of Baire first category, that is, it is the countable union of nowhere dense subsets of
\partial\mathbb{D}
. In particular
\hbox{Int}(\mathcal{S}(h))=\emptyset
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. If
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
then
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(h)
if and only if
\varphi_{t_{0}}
does not extend continuously at
\sigma
for some
t_{0}>0
. This is the case if and only if
\varphi_{t}
does not extend continuously at
\sigma
for all
t>0
(see,
[
8
, Proposition 11.3.2]
).

For
-\infty\leq a<b<+\infty
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
we let

(6.3)

L_{a,b;y}:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:a\leq{\sf Re}\,w\leq b,{\sf Im}\,w=y\}.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
be the Koenigs function of a non-elliptic semigroup with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and let
\psi:I\to[-\infty,\infty)
be the defining function of
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
.
If
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
then
b:={\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}
and
y={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)\in\overline{I}
and there exists
a\in[-\infty,b)
such that
I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma}))=L_{a,b;y}
. Moreover, one of the two following two cases (not mutually disjoint) are possible:

(1)

(y-\varepsilon,y)\subset I
for some
\varepsilon>0
,
a=\liminf_{t\to y^{-}}\psi(t)

and
b=\limsup_{t\to y^{-}}\psi(t)
;

(2)

(y,y+\varepsilon)\subset I
for some
\varepsilon>0
,
a=\liminf_{t\to y^{+}}\psi(t)

and
b=\limsup_{t\to y^{+}}\psi(t)
.

Conversely, if there exist
y\in\overline{I}
,
b\in(-\infty,+\infty)
, and
a\in[-\infty,b)
such that (1) or (2) (or both) hold, then there exists
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
such that
h^{\ast}(\sigma)=b+iy
.

The previous lemma is a straightforward reinterpretation, using the defining function of the Koenigs domain, of the next lemma. The proof of the latter immediately ensues from the depiction of prime ends impressions in starlike at infinity domains (see
[
8
, Thm. 11.1.4]
and its proof):

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
be the Koenigs function of a non-elliptic semigroup with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
. Let
p:=h^{\ast}(\sigma)
, and, if
p\in\mathbb{C}
, set
b:={\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)
and
y={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)
.

Then
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(h)
if and only if
p\in\mathbb{C}
and there exists
a\in[-\infty,b)
such that the impression of
\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma})
is
I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma}))=L_{a,b;y}
.

If this is true,
the two following cases (not mutually disjoint) are possible:

(1)

The following conditions hold:

(1.a)

there exists a sequence
\{z^{-}_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
converging to
p
such that

\{{\sf Im}\,z^{-}_{n}\}
is strictly increasing,

, and

for all

,

(1.b)

\delta>0
such that

,

(1.c)

for any
\alpha>a
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.d)

if
a\neq-\infty
,
for any
\beta<a
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\{w\in\mathbb{C}:y-\delta<{\sf Im}\,w<y,{\sf Re}\,w<\beta\}\cap\Omega=\emptyset
.

(2)

The following conditions hold:

(2.a)

there exists a sequence
\{z^{+}_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
converging to
p
such that

\{{\sf Im}\,z^{+}_{n}\}
is strictly decreasing,

, and

for all

,

(2.b)

for any
x>b
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:y<{\sf Im}\,w<y+\delta,{\sf Re}\,w>x\}\subset\Omega
,

(2.c)

\{w\in\mathbb{C}:y<{\sf Im}\,w<y+\varepsilon,{\sf Re}\,w<\alpha\}\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
for any
\alpha>a
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(2.d)

if
a\neq-\infty
,
for any
\beta<a
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\{w\in\mathbb{C}:y<{\sf Im}\,w<y+\delta,{\sf Re}\,w<\beta\}\cap\Omega=\emptyset
.

Conversely, if there exist
p\in\partial\Omega
,
-\infty\leq a<b<+\infty
and

which verify (1) or (2) (or both), then there exists
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}

and

.

Remark 6.7
.

Although we are not going to use the following, for the sake of precision, the complete “converse” conclusion of Lemma

is the following. If there exist

Definition 6.8
.

Let
h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
be the Koenigs function of a non-elliptic semigroup with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
\sigma\in S(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
. We say that

if

for some

, where

a<b
and (1) of Lemma
6.5
holds.
While, we say that
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
if
I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma}))=L_{a,b;y}
for some

a,b\in[-\infty,\infty)
, where

a<b
and (2) of Lemma
6.5
holds.

Note that
\mathcal{S}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}=\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\cup\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
. In general,
\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\cap\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\neq\emptyset
.

Remark 6.9
.

Let
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
.
Notice that the condition (1) in Lemma
6.5

is equivalent to the condition (1) in Lemma
6.6
; the conditions (2)
in these two lemmas are also mutually equivalent. Moreover,

\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
if and only if for some

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
h
be the Koenigs function of a non-elliptic semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau
and let
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
be the Koenigs domain. Let
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the defining function of
\Omega
. The fibres of the map

\mathcal{S}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}\ni\sigma\mapsto{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)\in\overline{I}

have at most two points.
Moreover if
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
,
\sigma_{1}\neq\sigma_{2}
are such that
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})
then
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})\in I
and,

(1)

if
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})
, then

•

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\cap\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)

•

there exists
t>0
such that
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})+t\in\partial\Omega
,

•

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has two non-exceptional maximal contact arcs starting, respectively, at
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
and having the same final point (different from
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
).

(2)

If
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})\neq h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})
, then

\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{\pm}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\mp}(h)
,
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{\mp}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\pm}(h)
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
. We can assume that
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
(the case
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
is similar). Let
x_{0}+iy_{0}:=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})\in\mathbb{C}
. Hence, by Lemma
6.5
, there exists
a_{0}\in[-\infty,x_{0})
such that
(y_{0}-\varepsilon,y_{0})\subset I
for some
\varepsilon>0
,
a_{0}=\liminf_{t\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(t)
and
x_{0}=\limsup_{t\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(t)
.

Assume now
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
,
\sigma_{2}\neq\sigma_{1}
, and
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})=y_{0}
. It follows again by Lemma
6.5
that
{\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})=\limsup_{t\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(t)=x_{0}
. That is,
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})
.

Consider the continuous injective curve
\gamma:(0,1)\to\mathbb{D}
defined by
\gamma(t)=(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})t+\sigma_{1}
. The closure of the image of
\gamma
is the segment
T
joining
\sigma_{1}
with
\sigma_{2}
. Hence,
\Gamma:=h(T)
is a Jordan curve,
p:=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})\in\Gamma
and
\Gamma\setminus\{p\}\subset\Omega
. Let
U
be the bounded connected component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma
and let
V=h^{-1}(U)
. Hence,
\partial V\cap\partial\mathbb{D}
is a closed arc
A
(of positive measure) in
\partial\mathbb{D}
joining
\sigma_{1}
with
\sigma_{2}
. Since
h
is proper, it follows that
h^{\ast}(\zeta)\in\partial\Omega
for all
\zeta\in A
. Taking into account that
\Gamma
is a continuous curve and
\Omega
is starlike at infinity, it follows easily that

h^{\ast}(A)\subset\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=y_{0},{\sf Re}\,w\geq x_{0}\}.

If it were
h^{\ast}(A)=\{p\}
, then

is constant. In particular, this means that

cannot be contained in either
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>y_{0}\}

.

Therefore, taking again into account that
\Omega
is starlike at infinity and
\Gamma
is continuous, there exists
t_{0}>0
such that

for all
\delta>0
,

(6.4)

\{w\in\mathbb{C}:x_{0}\leq{\sf Re}\,w\leq x_{0}+t_{0},{\sf Im}\,w=y_{0}\}=h^{\ast}(A),

and for every
t>0
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

(6.5)

\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,w>x_{0}+t,0<|{\sf Im}\,w-y_{0}|<\varepsilon\}\subset\Omega.

Now, we can assume that
{\sf Im}\,h(\gamma(t))<y_{0}
for
t
close to
0
and
{\sf Im}\,h(\gamma(t))>y_{0}
for
t
close to
1
. Let

be the interior of

,

.

For

W_{n}^{-}:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w-(x_{0}+iy_{0})|<\frac{1}{n},{\sf Im}\,w<y_{0},{\sf Re}\,w\geq{\sf Re}\,w_{n}^{-}\},

the interior part
V^{-}_{n}
of
C^{-}_{n}
is given by
V^{-}_{n}=\Omega\cap(R^{-}_{n}\cup W^{-}_{n})
,
n>n_{0}
.
Note that, in particular,
\{C^{+}_{n}\}_{n\geq n_{0}}
and
\{C^{-}_{n}\}_{n\geq n_{0}}
are not equivalent. Let
\zeta_{1}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\{C_{n}^{-}\}
represents the prime end
\hat{h}(\underline{\zeta_{1}})
and let
\zeta_{2}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\{C_{n}^{+}\}
represents the prime end
\hat{h}(\underline{\zeta_{2}})
.

Now, if
\eta:[0,1)\to\Omega
is a continuous curve such that
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\eta(t)=x_{0}+iy_{0}
, by (
6.4
) it follows that either
{\sf Im}\,\eta(t)>y_{0}
or
{\sf Im}\,\eta(t)<y_{0}
for
t
close enough to
1
. Suppose that
{\sf Im}\,\eta(t)>y_{0}
for every
t>t_{0}
, for some fixed
t_{0}\in[0,1)
. Fix
n>n_{0}
. Since
\overline{R^{+}_{n}\cup W^{+}_{n}}
is a neighborhood of
x_{0}+iy_{0}
in
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w\geq y_{0}\}
, it follows that
\eta(t)\in R^{+}_{n}\cup W^{+}_{n}
eventually. But
\eta(t)\in\Omega
for all
t\in[0,1)
, hence,
\eta(t)\in V^{+}_{n}
eventually, by (
6.6
). This implies that
\eta(t)
converges to
\hat{h}(\underline{\zeta_{2}})
in the Carathéodory topology of
\Omega
as
t\to 1
. Therefore,
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}h^{-1}(\eta(t))=\zeta_{2}
. But, if
\gamma:(0,1)\to\mathbb{D}
is the continuous curve defined above such that
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\gamma(t)=\sigma_{2}
, we have that
h(\gamma(t))
is a continuous curve in
\Omega
converging to
x_{0}+iy_{0}
as
t\to 1^{-}
such that
{\sf Im}\,h(\gamma(t))>y_{0}
for
t
close to
1
. Hence,
\zeta_{2}=\sigma_{2}
. A similar argument shows that if
{\sf Im}\,\eta(t)<y_{0}
for every
t>t_{0}
, for some fixed
t_{0}\in[0,1)
then
h^{-1}(\eta(t))
converges to
\sigma_{1}
as
t\to 1
. This proves the first part of
(\star)
. As for the last part, assume
h^{\ast}(\sigma)=x_{0}+iy_{0}
. Hence, the continuous curve
[0,1)\ni t\mapsto h(t\sigma)
converges to
x_{0}+iy_{0}
for
t\to 1^{-}
. Thus
t\sigma=h^{-1}(h(t\sigma))
converges to either
\sigma_{1}
or
\sigma_{2}
as
t\to 1^{-}
, that is, either
\sigma=\sigma_{1}
or
\sigma=\sigma_{2}
.

Now we show that
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are the starting points of two non-exceptional maximal contact arcs
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
with the same final point.

To this aim, (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) and Schwarz reflection principle imply that
h^{-1}
restricted to
\Omega\cap\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>y_{0}\}
extends holomorphically through
B:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:x_{0}<{\sf Re}\,w<x_{0}+t_{0},{\sf Im}\,w=y_{0}\}=h^{\ast}(A)
. Hence, there exists an open arc
A_{1}\subset A
such that
h
extends holomorphically through
A_{1}
and
h(A_{1})=B
. Similarly, considering
\Omega\cap\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w<y_{0}\}
, one can show that there exists an open arc
A_{2}\subset A
such that
h
extends holomorphically through
A_{2}
and
h(A_{2})=B
. By Lemma
6.2
,
A_{1},A_{2}
are (different) contact arcs with the same final point different from the Denjoy-Wolff point.

Since
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\cap\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
, it follows from Lemma
6.6
that for every
x<x_{0}
the point
x+iy_{0}
is not accessible from
\Omega
by any injective continuous curve, and thus cannot belong to the image via
h
of any point of
A_{1},A_{2}
. On the other hand, if
x_{0}<x<x_{0}+t_{0}
, then
x+t+iy_{0}\in B
for all
t\in[0,x_{0}+t_{0}-x]
. Therefore, the image of the starting point of
A_{j}
under
h^{\ast}
,
j=1,2
, is either
x_{0}+iy_{0}
or
x_{0}+t_{0}+iy_{0}
. Since the flow is given by
z\mapsto z+t
,
t\geq 0
, it follows that the starting point of
A_{j}
has image
x_{0}+iy_{0}
via
h^{\ast}
,
j=1,2
. Hence, by (
6.2
) and
(\star)
we have that the starting points of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
.

By
(\star)
we also see that there cannot be three different points
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},q\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
such that
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(q)
. Indeed, as we already noticed at the beginning of the proof, this would imply that
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})=h^{\ast}(q)
, contradicting
(\star)
.

Summing up, we have proved that for every
y\in\overline{I}
, the pre-image of

under the map
\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\ni\sigma\mapsto{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)
has at most two points. Moreover, if
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
,
\sigma_{1}\neq\sigma_{2}
are such that
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})=y
then

,
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})
,
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
and there exist two maximal contact arcs starting, respectively, from
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
and ending at the same final point. A similar conclusion holds for
\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
.

Since
\mathcal{S}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}=\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\cup\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
, it follows that the fibres of
\mathcal{S}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}\ni\sigma\mapsto{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)\in\overline{I}
have at most two points.
Moreover, if a fiber of this map has two points, then either one point is in
\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
and the other in
\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
or they are both in
\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\cap\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
.

In order to end the proof, we have to show that if, say,
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
are such that

, then
y\in I
and
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})\neq h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})
.
But this follows immediately from the very definitions of
\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
and
\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
.
∎

Now we examine certain special discontinuities. To this aim, we need a definition:

Definition 6.11
.

Let
H=\mathbb{R}
or
H=\partial\mathbb{D}
. A subset
A\subset H
is a
Cantor set
if every point of
A
is an accumulation point of
A
and
A
has empty interior.

Note that, by definition, we are not assuming that a Cantor set is closed.

Now, for discontinuities of a Koenigs function we give a couple of definitions that depend on their impressions.

Given
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C},\delta>0,\varepsilon>0
an
open box
is

B(z_{0},\varepsilon,\delta):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|{\sf Re}\,z-{\sf Re}\,z_{0}|<\varepsilon,|{\sf Im}\,z-{\sf Im}\,z_{0}|<\delta\}.

Definition 6.12
.

Let
h
be a Koenigs function of a non-elliptic semigroup and let
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
.

A set
\{\sigma_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathcal{S}(h)
is a
Cantor comb of discontinuities
if it forms a Cantor set on
\partial\mathbb{D}
and if there exists an open box
B\subset\overline{\Omega}
such that
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{\alpha})\in B
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}
and

(6.7)

\begin{split}\Omega\cap B&=B\setminus\cup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}}I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma_{\alpha}}))\\
&=B\setminus\cup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,z={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{\alpha}),{\sf Re}\,z\leq{\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{\alpha})\}.\end{split}

We are now going to give some better description of Cantor combs of discontinuities, also in terms of the defining function of the Koenigs domain (see Proposition
2.2
).

As a matter of notation, if
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, we let

L_{x,y}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,z\leq x,{\sf Im}\,z=y\}.

Proposition 6.13
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup, with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
,
h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
its Koenigs function and
\Omega:=h(\mathbb{D})
. Let
\psi:I\to[-\infty,+\infty)
be the defining function of
\Omega
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists
\{\sigma_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in C_{1}}\subset\mathcal{S}(h)
a Cantor comb of discontinuities.

(2)

There exist an open box
B\subset\overline{\Omega}
, a Cantor set
\mathcal{C}_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}
and a set of real numbers
\{x_{c}\}_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}}
such that
x_{c}+ic\in B
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
,

(6.8)

\Omega\cap B=B\setminus\cup_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}}L_{x_{c},c},

and
\limsup_{\mathcal{C}_{2}\ni c\to c_{0}}x_{c}=x_{c_{0}}
for every
c_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
.

(3)

There exist an interval
J\subset I
, a Cantor set
\mathcal{C}_{3}\subset J
and
q\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

\psi(c)>q
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}_{3}
and
\psi(y)\leq q
for all
y\in J\setminus\mathcal{C}_{3}
and
\limsup_{\mathcal{C}_{3}\ni c\to c_{0}}\psi(c)=\psi({c_{0}})
for every
c_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{3}
.

Proof.

(1) implies (2).
Let
\mathcal{C}_{2}:=\{{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{\alpha}):\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\}
. By definition of Cantor comb of discontinuities, (
6.8
) holds.

Now we show that
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is a Cantor set.
Since by definition of Cantor comb of discontinuities, for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
t>0
we have
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{\alpha})+t\in\Omega
, it follows by
Proposition
6.10

that

x_{c}:=\max\{{\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{\alpha}):\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{1},{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{\alpha})=c\}

is well defined. Note that, since the impressions of prime ends are contained in
\partial\Omega
, we have
L_{x_{c},c}\cap B\subset\partial\Omega
.

First we show that the interior of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is empty. If
R_{0}=\inf\{x:x+iy\in B\}
, by definition of Cantor comb, we have
x_{c}>R_{0}
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Let
n=1,2\ldots
and set

\mathcal{K}(n):=\{c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}:x_{c}>R_{0}+\frac{1}{n}\}.

Clearly
\mathcal{C}_{2}=\cup_{n}\mathcal{K}(n)
. We claim that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\mathcal{K}(n)})=\emptyset
for all
n
, from which it follows from Baire category theorem that
\mathcal{C}_{2}
has empty interior.

Assume there exists
n
and an open set
A
such that
A\subset\overline{\mathcal{K}(n)}
. Thus, any point
a\in A
is a limit of points in
\mathcal{K}(n)
. Since for every point
c\in K(n)

one has
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=c,R_{0}\leq{\sf Re}\,w\leq R_{0}+\frac{1}{n}\}\subset\partial\Omega
and
\partial\Omega
is closed, it follows that also
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=a,R_{0}\leq{\sf Re}\,w\leq R_{0}+\frac{1}{n}\}\subset\partial\Omega
.
Therefore, the open set
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w\in A,R_{0}<{\sf Re}\,w<R_{0}+\frac{1}{n}\}\subset\partial\Omega
, which is a contradiction.

Since
\partial\Omega
is closed, we have
\limsup_{\mathcal{C}_{2}\ni\tilde{c}\to c}x_{\tilde{c}}\leq x_{c}
for every
c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
.

Therefore, in order to prove (2), we are left to show that every point in
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is an accumulation point in
\mathcal{C}_{2}
and that for every
c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
there exists a sequence
\{c_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{2}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{c_{n}}=x_{c}
. To this aim, given
c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
, let
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
be such that
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{\alpha})=x_{c}+ic
. By Lemma
6.6
, we can assume that there exists a sequence
\{z_{n}^{-}\}\subset\partial\Omega
such that
\{{\sf Im}\,z_{n}^{-}\}
is strictly increasing,
z_{n}^{-}+t\in\Omega
for
t>0
and
\{z_{n}^{-}\}
converges to
x_{c}+ic
(the case of a sequence
\{z_{n}^{+}\}\subset\partial\Omega
such that
\{{\sf Im}\,z_{n}^{+}\}
is strictly increasing is similar). For
n
sufficiently large,
\{z_{n}^{-}\}
is contained in the open box
B
given by the definition of Cantor comb of discontinuities. Therefore, since
z_{n}^{-}+t\in\Omega
for
t>0
, from (
6.7
) we obtain that
z_{n}^{-}=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{\alpha_{n}})
for some
\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
. Letting
x_{c_{n}}+ic_{n}=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{\alpha_{n}})
, it is clear that
\{c_{n}\}
accumulates to
c
and that
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{c_{n}}=x_{c}
.

(2) implies (3).
Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
,

\varepsilon>0,\delta>0
be such that
B=B(z_{0},\varepsilon,\delta)
. Let
J:=({\sf Im}\,z_{0}-\delta,{\sf Im}\,z_{0}+\delta)
. Note that, since
x_{c}+ic\in B
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
, it follows that
\mathcal{C}_{2}\subset J
. By the very definition of
\psi
and (
6.8
), we have
\psi(c)=x_{c}
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Take
c_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
and let

{\sf Re}\,z_{0}-\varepsilon<q<x_{c_{0}}
.
Note that
q+ic_{0}\in B
. Then define
\mathcal{C}_{3}:=\{c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}:x_{c}>q\}
. For every
c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
there exists a sequence
\{c_{n}\}

of points of
C_{2}
such that
c_{n}\neq c
for all
n
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}=c
. It follows that
\mathcal{C}_{3}
is a Cantor set. By construction,
\psi(c)>q
for all
c\in C_{3}
and
\psi(y)\leq q
for all
q\in J\setminus\mathcal{C}_{3}
.

(3) implies (1). Let
c_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{3}
be a point in the interior of
J
. Since
\psi
is upper semicontinuous, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\psi(t)<\psi(c_{0})+\varepsilon
for every
t
such that
|t-c_{0}|<\delta
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
such that
\psi(c_{0})-2\varepsilon>q
and let
\delta>0
as before. We can assume, up to taking
\delta
smaller, that
[c_{0}-\delta,c_{0}+\delta]\subset J
. Let

\mathcal{C}_{1}:=\mathcal{C}_{3}\cap(c_{0}-\delta,c_{0}+\delta)
.
Note that
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a Cantor set.
Let
B:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|{\sf Re}\,z-\psi(c_{0})|<2\varepsilon,|{\sf Im}\,z-c_{0}|<\delta\}
. By construction and hypothesis on
\psi
, we have

(6.9)

B\cap\Omega=B\setminus\cup_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{1}}L_{\psi(c),c},

and
\psi(c)+ic\in B
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

Therefore, for every
c\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
the Jordan arc
\gamma_{c}:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
defined as
\gamma_{c}(t):=\psi(c)+t+ic
is contained in
\Omega
.
Hence, (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Prop. 3.3.3]
), for every
c\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
there exists
\sigma_{c}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
\sigma_{c}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}h^{-1}(\gamma_{c}(t))
and

\psi(c)+ic=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{c}).

By Lemma
6.6
, it follows that
L_{\psi(c),c}\cap B
contains the impression of the prime-end
\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma_{c}})
.
Hence,
\sigma_{c}\in\mathcal{S}(h)
. By (
6.9
), we are left to prove that
\{\sigma_{c}\}_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{1}}
forms indeed a Cantor set in
\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Fix
c\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
. Let
\{c_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{1}
be a sequence converging to
c
such that
\psi(c_{m})\to\psi(c)
. We prove that
\{\sigma_{c_{m}}\}
converges, up to subsequences, to
\sigma_{c}
.

Indeed, assume by contradiction that there exists
C>0
such that
|\sigma_{c_{m}}-\sigma_{c}|\geq C
for all
m
. Let
\{r_{n}\}
be a sequence of positive numbers strictly decreasing to
0
. Since
\psi
is upper semicontinuous, for every
n
there exists
\delta_{n}>0
such that
\psi(y)<\psi(c)+r_{n}
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|y-c|<\delta_{n}
. Thus, if
c_{m}
is such that
|c_{m}-c|<\delta_{n}
, the Jordan arc

\Gamma_{n}:=([\psi(c_{m}),\psi(c)+r_{n}]+ic_{m})\cup(\psi(c)+r_{n}+i[c_{m},c])\cup([\psi(c),\psi(c)+r_{n}]+ic)

connects
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{c})
to
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{c_{m}})
and
\Gamma_{n}\setminus\{h^{\ast}(\sigma_{c}),h^{\ast}(\sigma_{c_{m}})\}\subset\Omega
.

It follows that
h^{-1}(\Gamma_{n})
is a Jordan arc connecting
\sigma_{c}
and
\sigma_{c_{m}}
and
A_{n}:=h^{-1}(\Gamma_{n})\setminus\{\sigma_{c},\sigma_{c_{m}}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
. By hypothesis, the diameter of
A_{n}
is bounded from below by
C
(since
|\sigma_{c_{m}}-\sigma_{c}|\geq C
for all
m
). However,
\{h(A_{n})\}
converges to
h^{\ast}(\sigma_{c})
—in the sense that for every
w_{n}\in h(A_{n})
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n}=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{c})
. Thus,
\{A_{n}\}
is a sequence of Koebe arcs for
h
, against the “no Koebe arcs Theorem” (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.4]
.

Thus,
\{\sigma_{c_{m}}\}
converges, up to subsequences, to
\sigma_{c}
—actually, using the same token for every subsequence, one can prove that
\{\sigma_{c_{m}}\}
converges to
\sigma_{c}
, although this is not needed here. By the arbitrariness of
c
, it follows that
\{\sigma_{c}\}
is a Cantor set, and we are done.
∎

Example 6.14
.

We construct a non-elliptic semigroup such that its Koenigs function has a Cantor comb of discontinuities. To this aim, let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a closed Cantor set. Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be defined as
\psi(c)=1
for
c\in\mathcal{C}
and
\psi(y)=0
for
y\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathcal{C}
. By construction
\psi
is upper semicontinuous. Then

\Omega_{\psi}
is the Koenigs domain of a parabolic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
. By Proposition
6.13
such a semigroup has a Cantor comb of discountinuities.

6.4.
Characterization

Now we characterize those Koenigs domain
\Omega
such that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
. To this aim we give the following:

Definition 6.15
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an open interval and let
\psi:I\to[-\infty,+\infty)
be an upper semicontinuous function. The lower semicontinuous regularization
\psi_{\ast}:I\to[-\infty,+\infty)
of
\psi
is defined, for
x\in I
, as

\psi_{\ast}(x):=\liminf_{t\to x}\psi(t).

Then we let
\tilde{\psi}:I\to[-\infty,+\infty)
be the upper semicontinuous regularization of
\psi_{\ast}
, defined for
x\in I
as

\tilde{\psi}(x):=\limsup_{t\to x}\psi_{\ast}(t).

Note that, since
\psi
is upper semicontinuous, it follows that
\psi_{\ast}(x)\leq\psi(x)
—and hence
\tilde{\psi}(x)\leq\psi(x)
—for all
x\in I
.

Theorem 6.16
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup, with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
,
h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
its Koenigs function and
\Omega:=h(\mathbb{D})
. Let
\psi:I\to[-\infty,+\infty)
be the defining function of
\Omega
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

If
A,B
are non-exceptional maximal contact arcs for
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with final points
x_{1}(A)
and
x_{1}(B)
then
x_{1}(A)\neq x_{1}(B)
, and
\mathcal{S}(h)
does not contain any Cantor comb.

(2)

\psi(y)=\tilde{\psi}(y)
for every
y\in I
.

(3)

\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
.

Proof.

First, we extend
\psi_{\ast}
to
\overline{I}
by setting
\psi_{\ast}(a):=\liminf_{I\ni y\to a}\psi(y)
for
a\in\partial I
. Then it is easy to see that

\overline{\Omega}=\overline{\Omega}_{\psi}=\{x+iy:y\in\overline{I},\,x\geq\psi_{\ast}(y)\},

where, if
y\in\partial I
and
\psi_{\ast}(y)=+\infty
, the inequality
x\geq+\infty
means that
x+iy\not\in\overline{\Omega}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Using the previous equality, one can easily check that

\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\{x+iy:y\in I,\,x>\tilde{\psi}(y)\}.

Hence (2) is equivalent to (3).

Now let us see that (1) implies (2). Arguing by contradiction, we assume that there exists
y_{0}\in I
such that
\psi(y_{0})>\tilde{\psi}(y_{0})
. Let
q\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\psi(y_{0})>q>\tilde{\psi}(y_{0})
. Now, for every
\varepsilon>0
such that
(y_{0}-\varepsilon,y_{0}+\varepsilon)\subset I
let

C_{\varepsilon}(q):=\{y\in(y_{0}-\varepsilon,y_{0}+\varepsilon):\psi(y)>q\}.

If for every
\varepsilon>0
the set
C_{\varepsilon}(q)
contains some open interval
(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})
, it would follow that
\psi_{\ast}(a_{\varepsilon})>q
, and hence, since
a_{\varepsilon}\to y_{0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we would have
\tilde{\psi}(y_{0})\geq q
. Hence, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
\hbox{Int}(C_{\varepsilon}(q))=\emptyset
for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
.

If there exists
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
such that
C_{\varepsilon}(q)=\{y_{0}\}
, then by Corollary
6.3
the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has two non-exceptional maximal contact arcs
A,B
with final points
x_{1}(A)=x_{2}(B)
, contradicting (1).

Therefore, we can assume that for every
\varepsilon>0
the set
C_{\varepsilon}(q)
contains infinitely many points. If some point in
C_{\varepsilon}(q)
is isolated, repeating the previous argument at such a point, we would get again two non-exceptional maximal contact arcs
A,B
with final points
x_{1}(A)=x_{2}(B)
, contradicting (1). Therefore,
C_{\varepsilon}(q)
is a Cantor set in
(y_{0}-\varepsilon,y_{0}+\varepsilon)
for every
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
.

Let
\{q_{n}\}
be a increasing sequence converging to
\psi(y_{0})
and let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}
be a decreasing sequence of positive numbers converging to
0
. Repeating the previous argument, for every
n
large, we can find
y_{n}\in C_{\varepsilon_{n}}(q_{n})\subset C_{\varepsilon_{0}}(q)
. Thus,

\limsup_{C_{\varepsilon_{0}}(q)\ni y\to y_{0}}\psi(y)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\psi(y_{n})\geq\psi(y_{0}).

Since
\psi
is upper semicontinuous, it actually follows that
\limsup_{C_{\varepsilon_{0}}(q)\ni y\to y_{0}}\psi(y)=\psi(y_{0})
. Hence, by Proposition
6.13
,
\mathcal{S}(h)
contains a Cantor comb, against (1). Therefore, (1) implies (2).

Next, if (3) holds, it follows from Lemma
6.2
and the second equivalent condition in Proposition
6.13
that (1) holds.
∎

Example 6.17
.

We construct a semigroup such that its Koenigs function has a Cantor set of discontinuities which is not a Cantor comb of discontinuities. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the ternary Cantor set in
[0,1]
.
Define a function
\psi
on
(0,1)
as follows.
If
(a,b)\subset(0,1)
is one of the complementary intervals
of
\mathcal{C}
and
x\in(a,b)
, then we set
\psi(x)=\sin((b-x)^{-1}(x-a)^{-1})
. If
x\in(0,1)\cap C
, then we set
\psi(x)=1
. It is easy to see that
\psi
is upper semicontinuous and its set of discontinuities is
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
.

Consider a hyperbolic semigroup whose
Koenigs domain is
\Omega_{\psi}
.
To each point
c\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
there corresponds a prime end whose impression is
[-1,1]+ic
. Hence the Koenigs function of the semigroup has a Cantor set of discontinuities, but there are no Cantor combs.

Observe that by Theorem
5.8
, Corollary
5.9
, and Theorem
6.16
, for the domain
\Omega
of the previous example,
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\Omega)
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

7.
Boundary fixed points, unbounded discontinuities, and contact arcs

The aim of this section is to study the interplay between boundary fixed points, a type of discontinuities that we call “unbounded” and maximal contact arcs, also in relation with the defining function of the Koenigs’ domain.

7.1.
Boundary fixed points

As we already recalled, if
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
, then for every
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and for every
t\geq 0
, the non-tangential limit
\varphi^{\ast}_{t}(\sigma):=\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\varphi_{t}(z)

exists.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then
\sigma
is a
boundary fixed point
of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if
\varphi_{t}^{\ast}(\sigma)=\sigma
for every
t\geq 0
.

It can be proved that a point
\sigma
is a boundary fixed point of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if and only if there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
\varphi_{t_{0}}^{\ast}(\sigma)=\sigma
. Moreover, if
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is non-elliptic, the Denjoy-Wolff point is a boundary fixed point (see
e.g.
,
[
8
, Chapter 11]
for details).

In the sequel we will use some facts, that we are going to collect in the following lemmas. We start with the following (see,
e.g.

[
8
, Prop. 13.6.1]
)

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Koenigs function
h
and Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau
. Let
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then
\sigma
is a boundary fixed point of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if and only if

(7.1)

h^{\ast}(\sigma)=\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}h(z)=\infty.

Moreover, if
\sigma\neq\tau
, then
\lim_{z\to\sigma}h(z)=\infty
.

Next, (see,
e.g.

[
8
, Theorem 13.5.5 and Theorem 13.6.6]
):

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Koenigs function
h
and Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau
. Let
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
. Then
\sigma
is a boundary fixed point if and only if

(7.2)

\lim_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Re}\,h(z)=-\infty,\quad-\infty<a:=\liminf_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h(z)\leq b:=\limsup_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h(z)<+\infty.

Moreover, if
a<b
, then
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:a<{\sf Im}\,w<b\}\subset\Omega
and
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:a-\delta<{\sf Im}\,w<b\}\not\subset\Omega
,
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:a<{\sf Im}\,w<b+\delta\}\not\subset\Omega
for all
\delta>0
. Also,
\lim_{t\to+\infty}h^{-1}(\gamma(t))=\sigma
for every continuous curve
\gamma:[0,+\infty)\to\Omega
such that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}{\sf Im}\,\gamma(t)\in(a,b)

and
\lim_{t\to+\infty}{\sf Re}\,\gamma(t)=-\infty
.

While, if
a=b
, then
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:a-\delta<{\sf Im}\,w\leq a\}\not\subset\Omega
and
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:a\leq{\sf Im}\,w<a+\delta\}\not\subset\Omega
for all
\delta>0
.

The previous lemma allows us to give the following definition:

Definition 7.4
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Koenigs function
h
and Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial D
. Let
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
be a boundary fixed point for
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Let

(7.3)

a_{-}(\sigma):=\liminf_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h(z),\quad a_{+}(\sigma):=\limsup_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h(z).

If
a_{-}(\sigma)<a_{+}(\sigma)
, the point
\sigma
is a
boundary regular fixed point
. We denote by
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{R}
the set of boundary regular fixed points
\sigma\neq\tau
. If
a_{-}(\sigma)=a_{+}(\sigma)
, the point
\sigma
is a
super-repelling boundary fixed point
and we denote by
{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}}_{N}
the set of super-repelling boundary fixed points.

Remark 7.5
.

It is known (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Chapters 12 and 13]
) that
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{R}
if and only if
\lim_{z\to\sigma}\varphi_{t}(z)=\sigma
and
\lim_{z\to\sigma}|\varphi^{\prime}_{t}(z)|<+\infty
for every
t>0
, while
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{N}
if and only
\lim_{z\to\sigma}\varphi_{t}(z)=\sigma
and
\lim_{z\to\sigma}|\varphi^{\prime}_{t}(z)|=+\infty
for every
t>0
.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be its Koenigs function,
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the associated Koenigs domain. Let
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the defining function of
\Omega
.

(1)

Let
\Theta_{\infty}
be the (possibly empty) union of the open unbounded connected components of
\psi^{-1}(-\infty)
.
Then
\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{-1}(w_{n})=\tau
for every sequence
\{w_{n}\}\subset\Omega
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\sf Re}\,w_{n}=-\infty
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\sf Im}\,w_{n}=y_{0}
for some
y_{0}\in\Theta_{\infty}
.

(2)

Let
I_{R}
be the set of all bounded open connected components of
\psi^{-1}(-\infty)
. There is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{R}
and
I_{R}
given by

\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{R}\ni\sigma\mapsto(a_{-}(\sigma),a_{+}(\sigma))\subset\mathbb{R}.

(3)

Assume that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
. Let
J_{\infty}
be the union of the open connected components of
\psi^{-1}(-\infty)
and let
I_{N}
be the set of points
y_{0}\in\overline{I}\setminus\overline{J_{\infty}}
such that either
\lim_{y\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(y)=-\infty
or
\lim_{y\to y_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)=-\infty
. There is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{N}
and
I_{N}
given by

\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{N}\ni\sigma\mapsto a_{-}(\sigma).

Proof.

In the sequel we will use the following classical result about boundary behaviour of univalent functions (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Prop. 3.3.5]
):

(\star)
Let
\gamma:(-\infty,+\infty)\to\Omega
be an injective continuous curve such that
\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\gamma(t)=\infty
. Let
U
be a connected component of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\overline{\gamma((-\infty,+\infty))}^{\infty}
. If
U\subset\Omega
then there exists
\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}h^{-1}(\gamma(t))=\xi
.

(1) The result follows from the behaviour of
h
on the so-called “parabolic petals” (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Chapter 13]
). However, we give here a direct proof. Assume
\Theta_{\infty}\neq\emptyset
. Note that
\Theta_{\infty}
has one or two connected components. We assume
(-\infty,c)
is one of such component for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
(the other case is similar and we omit it). Let
\{w_{n}\}\subset\Omega
be such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\sf Re}\,w_{n}=-\infty
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\sf Im}\,w_{n}=y_{0}
for some
y_{0}<c
. Let
y_{1}\in(y_{0},c)
. We can assume that
{\sf Im}\,w_{n}<y_{1}
for all
n
. Let
\gamma(t):=t+iy_{1}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
U:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w<y_{1}\}\subset\Omega
, it follows by
(\star)
that
\Gamma:=\overline{h^{-1}(\gamma((-\infty,+\infty))}
is a Jordan curve such that
\Gamma\cap\partial\mathbb{D}=\{\tau\}
. Note also that
h^{-1}(U)
is the bounded connected component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma
. Since
\{h^{-1}(w_{n})\}\subset h^{-1}(U)
and it converges to the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
, it follows that
\{h^{-1}(w_{n})\}
converges to
\tau
.

(2) follows at once from Lemma
7.3
.

(3) First, we show that if
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{N}
then
a_{-}(\sigma)\in I_{N}
. Indeed,
let
\alpha_{1}(t)=h(t\sigma)
and let

M_{1}:={\sf Im}\,\alpha_{1}([0,1))
. By Lemma
7.3
,
M_{1}\subset I
is either an interval or a point such that
a_{-}(\sigma)\in\overline{M_{1}}
. If
M_{1}
is an interval, since
\psi({\sf Im}\,\alpha_{1}(t))<{\sf Re}\,\alpha_{1}(t)
for every
t\in[0,1)
and
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}{\sf Re}\,\alpha_{1}(t)=-\infty
, it follows easily that either
\lim_{y\to a_{-}(\sigma)^{-}}\psi(y)=-\infty
or
\lim_{y\to a_{-}(\sigma)^{+}}\psi(y)=-\infty
(or both). If
M_{1}
is a point, then necessarily
M_{1}=\{a_{-}(\sigma)\}
and
\alpha_{1}(t)\in\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=a_{-}(\sigma)\}
for all
t\in[0,1)
—in particular,
a_{-}(\sigma)\in I
.
Then choose any
\xi
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
\xi\overline{\sigma}\notin\mathbb{R}
. Put
\alpha_{2}(t)=h(t\sigma+(1-t)\xi)
. Since
h
is injective, it is easy to see that
M_{2}:={\sf Im}\,\alpha_{2}([0,1))

has to be an interval and cannot be a point. By repeating
the above argument for the curve
\alpha_{2}
in place of

\alpha_{1}
, we conclude that
a_{-}(\sigma)\in I_{N}
.

Next, we show that
\mathcal{B}_{N}\ni\sigma\mapsto a_{-}(\sigma)\in I_{N}
is injective. Assume by contradiction that there exist
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{N}
,
\sigma_{1}\neq\sigma_{2}
, such that
a_{-}(\sigma_{1})=a_{-}(\sigma_{2})
. Let
\gamma_{j}(t):=h(t\sigma_{j})
,

t\in[0,1)
and
j=1,2
. Let
\Gamma
be the closure in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
of
\gamma_{1}([0,1))\cup\gamma_{2}([0,1))
. Then by Lemma
7.3
,
\Gamma
is a Jordan curve in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. By the same lemma, it follows that there exists
R\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Gamma\cap\mathbb{C}
is contained in
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,w<R\}
. Let
U
be the connected component of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\Gamma
which is contained in
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,w<R\}
.

Since we are assuming that
\sigma_{1}\neq\sigma_{2}
, by
(\star)
we have
U\not\subset\Omega
. We show that, if this is the case, then
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})\neq\Omega
, reaching a contradiction.

Indeed, assume
w\in U\setminus\Omega
. We have that
w-t\in U\setminus\Omega
for all
t>0
, since
\Omega
is starlike at infinity and
\partial U=\Gamma\cap\mathbb{C}\subset\Omega
.

Taking into account Lemma
7.3
, we see that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
R_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
so that

U\cap\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,w<R_{\varepsilon}\}\subset\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|{\sf Im}\,w-a_{-}(\sigma_{1})|<\varepsilon\}.

Hence, if
{\sf Im}\,w\neq a_{-}(\sigma_{1})
, it follows that there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
w-t_{0}\in\Gamma\cap\mathbb{C}\subset\Omega
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
{\sf Im}\,w=a_{-}(\sigma_{1})
. In particular
\psi(a_{-}(\sigma_{1}))>-\infty
. Also, since
{\sf Re}\,w+ia_{-}(\sigma_{1})-t\in U
for all
t>0
, we have that either
{\sf Im}\,\gamma_{1}(t)>a_{-}(\sigma_{1})
and
{\sf Im}\,\gamma_{2}(t)<a_{-}(\sigma_{1})
or
{\sf Im}\,\gamma_{1}(t)<a_{-}(\sigma_{1})
and
{\sf Im}\,\gamma_{2}(t)>a_{-}(\sigma_{1})
, for
t
close to
1
. In both cases,
\lim_{y\to a_{-}(\sigma_{1})}\psi(y)=-\infty<\psi(a_{-}(\sigma_{1}))
. Thus, by Theorem
6.16
we have
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})\neq\Omega
, contradicting our hypothesis. Hence the map is injective.

Finally, we prove that
\mathcal{B}_{N}\ni\sigma\mapsto a_{-}(\sigma)\in I_{N}
is surjective. Let
a\in I_{N}
. We assume
\lim_{y\to a^{+}}\psi(y)=-\infty
(the other case is similar). Therefore, for every
n=1,2,\dots
there exists
\varepsilon_{n}>0
such that
\psi(y)<-n
for all
y\in I
such that

0<y-a<\varepsilon_{n}
.
It follows that there exists a continuous injective curve

\theta:[1,+\infty)\to\Omega
such that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}{\sf Re}\,\theta(t)=-\infty
,

{\sf Im}\,\theta
strictly decreases
and
\lim_{t\to+\infty}{\sf Im}\,\theta(t)=a
. Hence (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Prop. 3.3.3]
), there exists
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}h^{-1}(\theta(t))=\sigma
and
h^{\ast}(\sigma)=\infty
. Therefore,
\sigma
is a boundary fixed point of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

Now, let us prove that
\sigma\neq\tau
. Indeed, if this were the case, we can define the injective continuous curve
\eta:(-\infty,+\infty)\to\Omega
by setting
\eta(t):=\theta(t)
for
t\geq 1
and
\eta(t):=\theta(1)-(t-1)
for
t<1
. By construction, and since we are assuming
\sigma=\tau
, it follows that
L:=\overline{h^{-1}(\theta((-\infty,+\infty))}
is a Jordan curve such that
L\setminus\{\tau\}\subset\mathbb{D}
and
L\cap\partial\mathbb{D}=\{\tau\}
. Let
U\subset\mathbb{D}
be the bounded connected component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus L
.
Then
h\big|_{U}
extends to a continuous function from
\overline{U}
to
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
.
Thus
h(U)\subset\Omega
is one of the connected components of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\eta(-\infty,+\infty)}
. But then
a
belongs to the closure of one open unbounded connected component of
\psi^{-1}(-\infty)
, against the hypothesis
a\in I_{N}
. Thus,
\sigma\neq\tau
.

Finally,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}{\sf Im}\,h(h^{-1}(\theta(t)))=a
, and
\lim_{t\to+\infty}h^{-1}(\theta(t))=\sigma
. Therefore,

\liminf_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h(z)\leq a\leq\limsup_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h(z).

Thus, by Lemma
7.3
and since
a\in I_{N}
, we have
\lim_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h(z)=a
.
This implies that
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{N}
,
a=a_{-}(\sigma)
, and we are done.
∎

7.2.
Unbounded discontinuities

Definition 7.7
.

Let
h
be the Koenigs function of a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
. We say that
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(h)
is
bounded
, provided there exists
R\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,w<R\}\cap I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma}))=\emptyset
. If
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(h)
is not bounded, we say it is
unbounded
. We let
\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)
denote the set of all unbounded discontinuities.

Except for the Denjoy-Wolff point, unbounded discontinuities can be deduced by analytic properties of the semigroup:

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be its Koenigs function, and
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
. Let
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\varphi_{t}^{\ast}(\sigma)=\tau
, and for every
t_{0}>0
there exists a sequence
\{w_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
converging to
\sigma
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{t_{0}}(w_{n})=\sigma
.

(2)

\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)
.

Proof.

(1) implies (2). It is clear that
\sigma
is not a boundary fixed point.
Moreover,
\varphi_{t}
does not extend continuously at
\sigma
for
t>0
by hypothesis. Hence, as we already remarked, it follows that
h
is not continuous at
\sigma
, thus
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(h)
. The cluster set of
h
at
\sigma
is the impression of
\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma})
—which is either a segment or a half-line parallel to the real axis (see Lemma
6.6
). We have to prove that
\sigma
is an unbounded discontinuity, that is,
I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma}))
is a half-line.

Suppose by contradiction that
K:=I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma}))=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:a\leq{\sf Re}\,w\leq b,{\sf Im}\,w=y\}
for some
-\infty<a<b={\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)<+\infty
. Fix
t>b-a
. Recall that
\varphi_{t}(z)=h^{-1}(h(z)+t)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Let
\{w_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
be the sequence converging to
\sigma
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{t}(w_{n})=\sigma
given by hypothesis (1). Up to subsequences, we can assume that
\{h(w_{n})\}
converges to some
p\in K
. Let
\xi_{n}:=h(w_{n})+t
. Hence,
\{\xi_{n}\}
converges to
p+t
. Since the identity map
\sf{id}_{\mathbb{D}}
extends as a homeomorphism between
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}
(the Carathéodory closure of
\mathbb{D}
) and
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, it follows that
\{\varphi_{t}(w_{n})\}
converges to
\sigma
if and only if
\{h^{-1}(\xi_{n})\}
converges to
\underline{\sigma}\in\partial_{C}\mathbb{D}
in the Carathéodory topology. Moreover, taking into account that
\hat{h}
is a homeomorphism between
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}
and
\widehat{\Omega}
, it follows that
\{h^{-1}(\xi_{n})\}
converges to
\underline{\sigma}\in\partial_{C}\mathbb{D}
if and only if
\{\xi_{n}\}
converges to
\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma})
in the Carathèodory topology. Let
(C_{n})
,
n\geq 0
be a null-chain representing
\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma})
and let
V_{n}
,
n\geq 1
denotes the interior part of
C_{n}
. Fix
s\in(b,t)
. Since
\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\overline{V_{n}}=K
and
V_{n+1}\subset V_{n}
it follows that there exists
m
such that
\overline{V_{m}}\subset\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,w<s\}
. But this implies that
\{\xi_{n}\}
is not eventually contained in
V_{m}
, and hence (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Remark 4.2.2]
),
\{\xi_{n}\}
does not converge to
\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma})
in the Carathéodory topology, contradiction. Therefore (1) implies (2).

(2) implies (1). Recall that, by the so-called “boundary Denjoy-Wolff Theorem” (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Theorem 14.1.1]
), for every
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
which is not a boundary fixed point of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, the curve
[0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto\phi_{t}^{\ast}(\sigma)
is a continuous injective curve converging to
\tau
. Thus
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\varphi_{t}^{\ast}(\sigma)=\tau
. Finally, since
I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma}))
is a half-line, using again that
\varphi_{t}(z)=h^{-1}(h(z)+t)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
t\geq 0
, that
I(\hat{h}(\underline{\sigma}))
is the cluster set of
h
at
\sigma
and a similar argument as before, we have the conclusion.
∎

Now we relate unbounded discontinuities to the defining function of the Koenigs domain.

Proposition 7.9
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be its Koenigs function,
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the associated Koenigs domain . Let
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the defining function of
\Omega
. Let
D_{U}
be the set of points
y_{0}\in\overline{I}
such that either

\liminf_{y\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(y)=-\infty,\quad-\infty<\limsup_{y\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(y)<+\infty,

or

\liminf_{y\to y_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)=-\infty,\quad-\infty<\limsup_{y\to y_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)<+\infty.

Then the map
\mathcal{U}:\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}\to D_{U}
defined by

\mathcal{U}(\sigma)={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)
is surjective. Moreover, for each
y_{0}\in I\cap D_{U}
the fibre
\mathcal{U}^{-1}(y_{0})
contains at most two points, while, if
y_{0}\in\partial I\cap D_{U}
the fibre
\mathcal{U}^{-1}(y_{0})
contains only one point.

Finally, assume
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and let
y_{0}\in I\cap D_{U}
. Then, there exist
\sigma_{1}\neq\sigma_{2}
such that
\mathcal{U}^{-1}(y_{0})=\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\}
if and only if either
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\cap(\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{+}(h))
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\cap(\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{-}(h))
or
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\cap(\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{-}(h))
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\cap(\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{+}(h))
and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are the end points of a non-exceptional maximal contact arc of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

Proof.

Given
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)
, it follows easily from
Lemma
6.5

that
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)\in D_{U}
. Hence, the map
\mathcal{U}
is well defined.

In order to see that
\mathcal{U}
is surjective, take
y_{0}\in D_{U}
. We can assume without loss of generality that
\liminf_{y\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(y)=-\infty
and
-\infty<M:=\limsup_{y\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(y)<+\infty
. By definition of
\psi
and since
\psi
is upper semicontinuous, it is clear from the reverse implication of Lemma
6.6
that
L_{-\infty,M;y_{0}}

is the impression of a prime end corresponding to a point
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
such that
\mathcal{U}(\sigma)=y_{0}
.

By Proposition
6.10
, the fibre
\mathcal{U}^{-1}(y_{0})
contains at most two points for every
y_{0}\in D_{U}
and only one if
y_{0}\in\partial I\cap D_{U}
,

Assume now
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
. By Theorem
6.16
, every two non-exceptional maximal contact arcs of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
have different final points. Thus, by Proposition
6.10
, if
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
are such that
\sigma_{1}\neq\sigma_{2}
and
y_{0}:=\mathcal{U}(\sigma_{1})=\mathcal{U}(\sigma_{1})
then
y_{0}\in I
and, either
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
or
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
. Moreover,

{\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})\neq{\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})
.

We assume
x_{1}:={\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})<x_{2}:={\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma_{2})
and
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
,
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
(the other cases are similar).

We need to show that there exists a maximal contact arc for
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
starting at
\sigma_{1}
and ending at
\sigma_{2}
. The proof is very similar to the one for the proof of the existence of the two maximal contact arcs in Proposition
6.10
, so we only sketch it.

Since
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
, using (1.b) of Lemma
6.6
, by Schwarz reflection principle,
h^{-1}
restricted to
\Omega\cap\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w<y_{0}\}
extends holomorphically through
B:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:x_{1}<{\sf Re}\,w<x_{2},{\sf Im}\,w=y_{0}\}
. Hence, there exists an open arc
A\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
h
extends holomorphically through
A
and
h(A)=B
. By Lemma
6.2
,
A
is a contact arc.

Since
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
, properties (1.a) and (1.b) of Lemma
6.2
show that for every
x<x_{1}
the point
x+iy_{0}
is not accessible with injective continuous curves in
\Omega
and thus cannot belong to the image of any contact arc. Since
x_{1}+iy_{0}\in\overline{B}
, it follows then that the starting point
\xi_{1}
of
A
satisfies
h^{\ast}(\xi_{1})=h^{\ast}(\sigma_{1})
.

Now, arguing exactly as in the proof of Proposition
6.10
one can show that
\xi_{1}=\sigma_{1}
.

In order to show that
\sigma_{2}
is the final point of
A
, it is enough to prove that
\psi(y_{0})=x_{2}
. Indeed, by construction,
\psi(y_{0})\geq x_{2}
. Since
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
(here we use (2.a) and (2.b) of Lemma
6.6
), for every
\varepsilon>0
we can find
\delta>0
such that
\liminf_{t\to y}\psi(y)\leq x_{2}+\varepsilon
for all
|y-y_{0}|<\delta
. This clearly implies that
\tilde{\psi}(y_{0})\leq x_{2}
and hence, by Theorem
6.16
,
\psi(y_{0})=\tilde{\psi}(y_{0})=x_{2}
.

Finally, if
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are two unbounded discontinuities which are the end points of a non-exceptional maximal contact arc
A
of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
then clearly
\mathcal{U}(\sigma_{1})=\mathcal{U}(\sigma_{2})
since
h^{\ast}(A)
is contained in a line parallel to the real axis.
∎

7.3.
Boundary fixed points and unbounded discontinuities

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
, with Koenigs’ function
h
, Koenigs’ domain
\Omega
and Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the defining function of
\Omega
. In the previous sections, we introduced the following sets:

•

\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{R}\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
, the set of all boundary regular fixed points of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
different from
\tau
,

•

\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{N}\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
, the set of all boundary super-repelling fixed points of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,

•

\mathcal{S}^{+}(h),\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\subset\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
, the sets of “upper”/“lower” discontinuities,

•

\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
, the set of all unbounded discontinuities,

•

I_{R}\subset I
, the set of all bounded open connected components of
\psi^{-1}(-\infty)
,

•

J_{\infty}\subset I
, the union of all open connected components of
\psi^{-1}(-\infty)
,

•

I_{N}\subset\overline{I}
, the set of all points
y_{0}\in\overline{I}\setminus\overline{J_{\infty}}
such that either
\lim_{y\to y_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)=-\infty
or
\lim_{y\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(y)=-\infty
(or both),

•

D_{U}\subset\overline{I}
, the set of all points
y_{0}\in\overline{I}
such that either

\liminf_{y\to y_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)=-\infty<\limsup_{y\to y_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)<+\infty,

or

\liminf_{y\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(y)=-\infty<\limsup_{y\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(y)<+\infty.

Assuming
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
, we proved that there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{R}
and
I_{R}
; a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{N}
and
I_{N}
; and a surjective map from
\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)
to
D_{U}
, so that, if the fibre above
y_{0}
contains two points
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
, then
y_{0}\in I
and there is a maximal contact arc starting from
\sigma_{1}
and ending at
\sigma_{2}
.

Let us now explore what happens at those points of intersection between
D_{U}
and
I_{N}
or those points of
D_{U}
which belong to the closure of an element of
I_{R}
.

Note that, by definition, if
(a,b)\in I_{R}
then
x\not\in D_{U}
for every
x\in(a,b)
. However, it might happen that
a
or
b
belongs to
D_{U}
.

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be its Koenigs function,
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the associated Koenigs domain and assume that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
. Let
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the defining function of
\Omega
.

(1)

y_{0}\in D_{U}\cap I_{N}
if and only there exist a unique
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{N}
and a unique
\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
such that
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\xi)=y_{0}
.

(2)

Let
(y_{0},b)\in I_{R}
. Then
y_{0}\in D_{U}
if and only if there exist a unique
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{R}
and a unique
\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\cap\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
such that
\liminf_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(z)={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\xi)=y_{0}
.

(3)

Let
(a,y_{0})\in I_{R}
. Then
y_{0}\in D_{U}
if and only if there exist a unique
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{R}
and a unique
\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\cap\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
such that
\limsup_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(z)={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\xi)=y_{0}
.

Moreover, in the hypothesis of (1), (2) or (3),
y_{0}\in I
and there exists a maximal contact arc
A
starting from
\sigma
and ending at
\xi
such that
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(A)=y_{0}
.

Proof.

(1) Assume
y_{0}\in D_{U}\cap I_{N}
. Then, by Proposition
7.6
there exists a unique
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{N}
such that

{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)=\lim_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h(z)=y_{0}
. By Proposition
7.9
, either there exist a unique
\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
such that
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\xi)=y_{0}
or there are
\xi^{+},\xi^{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
such that
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\xi^{\pm})=y_{0}
and
\xi^{+}\in S^{+}(h)
,
\xi^{-}\in S^{-}(h)
. By (1.a) and (1.b) of Lemma
6.6
, the existence of such
\xi^{+}
and
\xi^{-}

implies that there exist two sequences
\{z_{n}^{+}\}
and
\{z_{n}^{-}\}
such that
{\sf Im}\,z_{n}^{+}>y_{0}
,
{\sf Im}\,z_{n}^{+}<y_{0}
,
\{z_{n}^{\pm}\}
converges to
h^{\ast}(\xi^{\pm})
and
z_{n}^{\pm}-t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega
for all
t\geq 0
. Since the continuous curve
[0,1)\ni t\mapsto h(t\sigma)

in
\Omega

has the property that
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}{\sf Re}\,h(t\sigma)=-\infty
and
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}{\sf Im}\,h(t\sigma)=y_{0}
, this immediately leads to a contradiction.

Therefore, there exists a unique
\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
such that
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\xi)=y_{0}
. By the same argument as before, it follows that either
\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
and
{\sf Im}\,h(t\sigma)>y_{0}
for all
t
close to
1

or
\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
and
{\sf Im}\,h(t\sigma)<y_{0}
for all
t
close to
1
. In both cases,
y_{0}\in I
.

The converse implication of (1) is straightforward from Proposition
7.6
and Proposition
7.9
.

(2) If
(y_{0},b)\in I_{R}
then
\psi(y)=-\infty
for all
y\in(y_{0},b)
. Hence, one can argue as in the proof of (1) showing the existence of a unique
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}_{R}
and a unique
\xi\in S^{-}(h)
such that
\liminf_{z\to\sigma}{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(z)={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\xi)=y_{0}
, and that
y_{0}\in I
. The reverse implication follows again by Proposition
7.6
and Proposition
7.9
.

(3) is similar to (2).

We are left to show that there exists a maximal contact arc starting from
\sigma
and ending at
\xi
. Since the argument is quite similar to the one for proving the existence of the two maximal contact arcs in Proposition
6.10
, we omit it and leave details to the reader.
∎

7.4.
Discontinuities and the Denjoy-Wolff point

For the Denjoy-Wolff point, recalling the notation introduced in (
6.3
) and setting
L_{y}:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=y\}
for
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have (see
[
8
, Thm.11.1.4]
),

Lemma 7.11
.

Let
h
be the Koenigs function of a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau
and let
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the Koenigs domain. If
\tau\in\mathcal{S}(h)
then

(1)

either there exists
y\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Omega\subset\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>y\}
or
\Omega\subset\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w<y\}
and
I(\hat{h}(\underline{\tau}))=\overline{L_{a,+\infty;y}}^{\infty}
,
where
a\in[-\infty,\infty)
,

(2)

or, there exist real numbers
y_{1}<y_{2}
such that
\Omega\subset\{w\in\mathbb{C}:y_{1}<{\sf Im}\,w<y_{2}\}
and

I(\hat{h}(\underline{\tau}))=\overline{L_{a_{1},+\infty;y_{1}}}^{\infty}\cup\overline{L_{a_{2},+\infty;y_{2}}}^{\infty}
,
where
a_{1},a_{2}\in[-\infty,\infty)
.

Moreover, in the first case, there are no
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
h^{\ast}(\sigma)\in L_{y}
, while in the second case there are no
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
h^{\ast}(\sigma)\in L_{y_{1}}\cup L_{y_{2}}
.

Such lemma suggests the following definition:

Definition 7.12
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and Koenigs function
h
. Suppose
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)
. If
I(\hat{h}(\underline{\tau}))=L_{y_{1}}\cup L_{y_{2}}\cup\{\infty\}
, for some
-\infty<y_{1}<y_{2}<+\infty
, we say that
\tau
is a
double
unbounded discontinuity. In all other cases, we say that
\tau
is a
simple
unbounded discontinuity.

Remark 7.13
.

By Lemma
7.11
it follows that
if
\tau\in\mathcal{S}(h)
then the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is either hyperbolic or parabolic with positive hyperbolic step.

In particular,
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)
if and only if, given
\psi:(a_{0},a_{1})\to\mathbb{R}
the defining function of the associated Koenigs domain,
-\infty\leq a_{0}<a_{1}\leq+\infty
, then, either
a_{0}>-\infty
and

\liminf_{y\to a_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)=-\infty,\quad\limsup_{y\to a_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)=+\infty,

or,
a_{1}<+\infty
and

\liminf_{y\to a_{1}^{-}}\psi(y)=-\infty,\quad\limsup_{y\to a_{1}^{-}}\psi(y)+\infty,

Moreover,
\tau\in\mathcal{S}(h)
is a double unbounded discontinuity if and only if
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is hyperbolic (that is,
-\infty<a_{0}<a_{1}<+\infty
) and both the two previous conditions hold.

For future aims, we need the following proposition (recall the definition of
a_{\pm}(\sigma)
from (
7.3
)):

Proposition 7.14
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be the Koenigs function and
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the Koenigs domain. Assume that the defining function
\psi
of
\Omega
is defined in
(a_{0},a_{1})
for some

(1)

\tau
is a simple unbounded discontinuity,

(2)

one and only one of the following two conditions holds:

(2.a)

\liminf_{y\to a_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)=-\infty
,
\limsup_{y\to a_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)=+\infty
and, either
\limsup_{y\to a_{1}^{-}}\psi(y)<+\infty
or
\liminf_{y\to a_{1}^{-}}\psi(y)>-\infty
(or both).

(2.b)

\liminf_{y\to a_{1}^{-}}\psi(y)=-\infty
,
\limsup_{y\to a_{1}^{-}}\psi(y)=+\infty
and, either
\limsup_{y\to a_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)<+\infty
or
\liminf_{y\to a_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)>-\infty
(or both).

Moreover, if (2.a) holds, then

(a1)

for every boundary fixed point
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
we have
a_{-}(\sigma)>a_{0}
,

(a2)

for every
\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}

we have
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\xi)\neq a_{0}
.

While,
if (2.b) holds, then

(b1)

for every boundary fixed point
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
we have

a_{+}(\sigma)<a_{1}
,

(b2)

for every
\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}

we have
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\xi)\neq a_{1}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
7.11
it follows immediately that (1) and (2) are equivalent. Moreover, by the same token, if (2.a) (
respectively (2.b)
holds then (a2) (
respect.
(b2)) holds.

As for (a1),
if
\sigma
is a boundary fixed point different from
\tau
, and
a_{0}=a_{-}(\sigma)
, then for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Re}\,w>x-\delta,a_{0}<{\sf Im}\,w<a_{0}+\delta\}\subset\Omega
. This is immediate if

, and it follows from the fact that
\Omega
is starlike at infinity and the continuous injective curve
[0,1)\ni t\mapsto h(t\sigma)
has the property that
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}{\sf Re}\,h(t\sigma)=-\infty
and
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}{\sf Im}\,h(t\sigma)=a_{0}
in case

. But this implies that for every

there exists
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that

(the point
\zeta
being the limit of

), against Lemma

.

∎

For our aims, we need also the following (see Proposition
7.9
for the definition of the map
\mathcal{U}
):

Lemma 7.15
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be the Koenigs function and
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the Koenigs domain. Assume that the defining function
\psi
of
\Omega
is defined in
(a_{0},a_{1})
for some
-\infty<a_{0}<a_{1}\leq+\infty
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a maximal contact arc for
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with starting point
\tau
and final point
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)
.

(2)

a_{1}=+\infty
and there exists

a\in(a_{0},+\infty)
such that

\psi(y)=-\infty
for all
y>a
and

-\infty=\liminf_{y\to a^{-}}\psi(y)<\limsup_{y\to a^{-}}\psi(y)=\psi(a).

Moreover, if either (1) or (2)—and hence both—holds, then
\mathcal{U}(\sigma)=a
,
\mathcal{U}^{-1}(a)=\{\sigma\}
and
h^{\ast}(\sigma)=\psi(a)+ia
.

Proof.

(1) implies (2). Let
A\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
be a maximal contact arc starting at
\tau
and ending at
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)
. If
\sigma=\tau
, then
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a parabolic group (see
[
8

), in particular,
\sigma\not\in\mathcal{S}(h)
. Hence,
\sigma\neq\tau
and, since it is not a boundary fixed point of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, it follows that
h^{\ast}(\sigma)\in\mathbb{C}
. Since
x_{0}(A)=\tau
, a fixed point of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, by Lemma

and (
6.2
), there exists
a\geq a_{0}
and
b\in(-\infty,+\infty]
such that
h(A)=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=a,{\sf Re}\,w<b\}

\lim_{A\ni\xi\to\sigma}h(\xi)=h^{\ast}(\sigma)

a={\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)
and
b={\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
\mathcal{U}(\sigma)=a
.

Moreover, we already recalled (see the discussion on maximal contact arcs) that

h^{\ast}(\sigma)+t\in\Omega
for all
t>0
.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
be the closure of the image of the continuous injective curve given by
(0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto h^{-1}(h^{\ast}(\sigma)+t)

\varphi_{t}(w)=h^{-1}(h(w)+t)
for all

and

\Gamma_{1}
is a Jordan arc with end points
\sigma
and
\tau
and
\Gamma_{1}\setminus\{\sigma,\tau\}\subset\mathbb{D}
. Hence,
\Gamma:=\overline{A}\cup\Gamma_{1}
is a Jordan curve. Let

be the bounded connected component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma
. Note that

is a Jordan domain and hence

extends as a homeomorphism to
\partial V
. Since by construction
h(\Gamma\setminus\{\sigma,\tau\})=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=a\}\setminus\{b+ia\}
, it follows that
h(\partial V)=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=a\}\cup\{\infty\}
. Thus,
h(V)=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w<a\}
or
h(V)=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>a\}
. But

and
\Omega\subset\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>a_{0}\}
, therefore,
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>a\}=h(V)\subset\Omega
. This implies that
\psi
is defined on
(a_{0},+\infty)
and
\psi(y)=-\infty
for all
y>a
.

This also implies that
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{-}(h)
and there cannot be
\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(h)
such that
{\sf Im}\,h^{\ast}(\xi)=a
. Therefore, Proposition
7.9
implies that
\mathcal{U}^{-1}(a)=\{\sigma\}
. Finally, Lemma
6.6
shows that

-\infty<{\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)=\limsup_{y\to a^{-}}\psi(y)\leq\psi(a).

Since
{\sf Re}\,h^{\ast}(\sigma)+t\in\Omega
for all
t>0
, we have actually
\limsup_{y\to a^{-}}\psi(y)=\psi(a)
and hence
h^{\ast}(\sigma)=\psi(a)+ia
.

Thus (2) and the last part of the proposition are proved.

(2) implies (1). By Proposition
7.9
, and arguing as before one can see that there exists a unique
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{u}(h)\setminus\{\tau\}
such that
h^{\ast}(\sigma)=\psi(a)+ia
.

Next, the hypothesis on
\psi
implies that
H:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>a\}\subset\Omega
,
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=a,{\sf Re}\,w\leq\psi(a)\}\cap\Omega=\emptyset
and
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=a,{\sf Re}\,w>\psi(a)\}\subset\Omega
. Since for every sequence
\{w_{n}\}\subset H
converging to any point of
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=a,{\sf Re}\,w\leq\psi(a)\}\cup\{\infty\}
the cluster set of
h^{-1}(w_{n})
is contained in
\partial\mathbb{D}
, by Schwarz reflection principle it follows that
h^{-1}
extends holomorphically through
\partial_{\infty}H
. Let
V:=h^{-1}(H)
. Then
V\subset\mathbb{D}
is a Jordan domain and
\partial V
is the union of an open arc
A\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
and a Jordan arc
\Gamma
with the same end points as
A
and such that
\Gamma
minus its end points is contained in
\mathbb{D}
. By construction,
h^{\ast}(A)=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w=a,{\sf Re}\,w<\psi(a)\}
. Therefore,
A
is a contact arc for
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
(see Lemma
6.2
). The order on
A
is given by the direction of the flow
(h|_{\partial H})^{-1}(w+t)
,
t>0
,
w\in\mathbb{C}
with
{\sf Im}\,w=a,{\sf Re}\,w<\psi(a)
. Hence, if
\zeta\in\partial A
is such that
h^{\ast}(\zeta)=\psi(a)+ia
, it follows that
\zeta
is the final point of
A
. Using an argument similar to the one in the proof of Proposition
6.10
one can easily show that
\zeta=\sigma
.

We are left to prove that
\tau
is the initial point of
A
.
Denote by
\tilde{h}
the homeomorphic extension of
h|_{V}
to
\overline{V}
. By construction,
\tilde{h}(\overline{V}\setminus\{x_{0}(A)\})=\overline{H}
. Since
\tilde{h}
is a homeomorphism from
\overline{V}
onto
\overline{H}^{\infty}
, it follows that
\tilde{h}(x_{0}(A))=\infty
. Thus
h^{\ast}(x_{0}(A))=\lim_{A\ni\xi\to x_{0}(A)}h(\xi)=\infty
. Therefore,
x_{0}(A)=\lim_{t\to+\infty}h^{-1}(\gamma(t))
for any continuous curve
\gamma:[0,+\infty)\to H
such that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\gamma(t)=\infty
(see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Proposition 3.3.3]
). Fix
w_{0}\in H
and let
\gamma(t):=w_{0}+t
. Hence

\lim_{t\to+\infty}h^{-1}(\gamma(t))=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\varphi_{t}(h^{-1}(w_{0}))=\tau,

and therefore
\tau=x_{0}(A)
. Thus (1) holds.
∎

8.
Koenigs domains
\Omega
such that
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components

In this section we characterize Koenigs domains whose complementary in
\mathbb{C}
is connected or doubly connected (and contained in a strip).

Remark 8.1
.

Suppose
\Omega
is the Koenigs domain of a non-elliptic semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Let
\psi:I\to[-\infty,+\infty)
be the defining function of
\Omega
. If
\Omega\subset H
, where
H
is a half-plane not parallel to
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>0\}
, then
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected.
This follows from the fact that for any
p\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
there exists
t_{0}\geq 0
such that
p-t_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{H}
and
p-t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Moreover, it is clear that in such a case
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has neither boundary fixed points other than the Denjoy-Wolff point nor unbounded discontinuities. Also
\liminf_{y\to y_{0}}\psi(y)>-\infty
for all
y_{0}\in I
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let
h
be the Koenigs function,
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
the Koenigs
domain and let
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the defining function of
\Omega
. Suppose
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\Omega\subset\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>0\}
. Let
I_{\infty}
denotes the unbounded connected component of
\psi^{-1}(-\infty)
(possibly empty). Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected.

(2)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has no boundary fixed points other than
\tau
and
\mathcal{S}(h)
contains only bounded discontinuities except, at most, one unbounded discontinuity
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
which is the final point of a maximal contact arc starting at
\tau
.

(3)

\liminf_{y\to y_{0}}\psi(y)>-\infty

for every
y_{0}\in\overline{I}\setminus\overline{I_{\infty}}
.

Proof.

(2) implies (3). Let
W\subset I
be the set of points
y
such that either
\liminf_{y\to y_{0}^{+}}\psi(y)=-\infty
or
\liminf_{y\to y_{0}^{-}}\psi(y)=-\infty
. Using the subsets of
\overline{I}
we introduced so far (see the beginning of Subsection
7.3
), we have that

W\setminus\overline{I_{\infty}}=(I_{N}\cap I)\cup I_{R}\cup(\mathcal{D}_{U}\cap I).

Since, by hypothesis,
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has no boundary fixed points other than
\tau
, Proposition
7.6
implies that
I_{R}=I_{N}=\emptyset
. Also, by hypothesis,
\mathcal{S}(h)
contains no unbounded discontinuity except, at most, one unbounded discontinuity
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
which is the final point of a maximal contact arc starting at
\tau
. Therefore, Proposition
7.9
(together with Lemma
7.15
in case of the existence of such a
\sigma
) implies that
\mathcal{D}_{U}\cap I=\emptyset
. Thus,
W\setminus\overline{I_{\infty}}=\emptyset
, and (3) follows.

(3) implies (2). Indeed, if either
I_{\infty}=\emptyset
or

I_{\infty}=[a,+\infty)
, where
a>0
,
it follows from Proposition
7.6
and Proposition
7.9
that (2) holds. Similarly, if

I_{\infty}=(a,+\infty)
for some
a>0
and
\liminf_{y\to a^{-}}\psi(y)>-\infty
.

Thus, we assume that (3) holds, that
I_{\infty}=(a,+\infty)
for some
a>0
,
\psi(a)>-\infty
and
\liminf_{y\to a^{-}}\psi(y)=-\infty
.
Notice that
\limsup_{y\to a^{-}}\psi(y)=\limsup_{y\to a}\psi(y)\leq\psi(a)
.
If
M:=\limsup_{y\to a^{-}}\psi(y)<\psi(a)
,
then the interval
(M,\psi(a)]+ia
is contained in
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})\setminus\Omega
,
which contradicts our hypothesis. Therefore
\limsup_{y\to a^{-}}\psi(y)=\psi(a)
.
By Lemma
7.15
, (2) holds.

(1) implies (3). Arguing by contradiction we assume that there exists
y_{0}\in\overline{I}\setminus\overline{I_{\infty}}
such that
\liminf_{I\ni y\to y_{0}}\psi(y)=-\infty
.

By the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
5.7
,
L_{y_{0}}\subset\overline{\Omega}
.

Since by hypothesis
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w<0\}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
and we are assuming that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected, it follows that
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>y_{0}\}\subset\overline{\Omega}
. But
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>y_{0}\}
is open, thus
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w>y_{0}\}\subset\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
, by hypothesis. Therefore,
(y_{0},+\infty)\subset I
and
\psi(y)=-\infty
for all
y>y_{0}
. In other words,
y_{0}\in\overline{I_{\infty}}
, a contradiction. Thus (3) holds.

(3) implies (1). Notice that

\Pi=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\sf Im}\,w<0\}
is contained in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.
The condition (3) implies that
\inf_{y\in J}\psi(y)>-\infty
for any compact interval
J\subset\overline{I}\setminus\overline{I_{\infty}}
.
It follows that any point in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
can be connected with a point in
\Pi
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
by a union of a horizontal
interval (going to the left) and a vertical one (going down). Therefore

\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected.

∎

As a direct corollary of Lemma
7.15
and Theorem
8.2
, we have:

Corollary 8.3
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a non-elliptic semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Let

be the Koenigs function,

is connected.

(2)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has no boundary fixed points other than

and

.

Now we examine the case

.

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}

,

(3.4)

, with

(3.5)

, with

,

10.1.
Logarithmic starlike domains at infinity with frequencies and the proof of Theorem

We start with the following proposition:

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}

\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point

the Koenigs domain and let

be the defining function of

. Suppose
\hbox{Int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega

denotes the unbounded connected component of

\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected.

(2)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}

\tau
and
\mathcal{S}(h)
contains only bounded discontinuities except, at most, one unbounded discontinuity
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}

for every
y_{0}\in\overline{I}\setminus\overline{I_{\infty}}

Observe that if
\Omega
is contained in

and

there exists

so that

If

is pure imaginary, then

parallel to the real axis which is contained in

and such that

Then the same argument as in the proof of Theorem

.

\beta(t):={\sf Im}\,\eta(it)
. Since
\beta^{\prime}(t)={\sf Re}\,\eta^{\prime}(it)>0
for all
t
and
\beta(t)
tends to
\pm\infty
when
t\to\pm\infty
, it follows that
\beta
is an increasing diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
.

Hence the function
\psi=\alpha\circ\beta^{-1}
is differentiable, and
\Omega_{0}=\eta(\mathbb{C}_{+})
has the form
\{x>\psi(y):y\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Therefore,
\Omega_{0}
is a starlike at infinity domain and clearly it is not contained in any half-plane, since
\psi(y)\to-\infty
as
y\to\pm\infty
.

Since
\beta^{\prime}(t)={\sf Re}\,\eta^{\prime}(it)\in(1-a,1+a)
for all
t
, it follows that
\psi
satisfies (1) of Definition
4.4
. By the same token and since
\beta(0)=0
, we have for all
t

|\beta^{-1}(t)|=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{ds}{\beta^{\prime}(\beta^{-1}(s))}\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{ds}{{\sf Re}\,\eta^{\prime}(i\beta^{-1}(s))}\right|\leq\frac{|t|}{1-a}.

Now, there exists some
C>0
and
R>1
such that
\alpha(t)\geq-C\big(\log|t|\big)^{a}
for
|t|>R
.
Hence the previous displayed formula implies

\psi(t)=\alpha(\beta^{-1}(t))\geq-C\big(\log\frac{|t|}{1-a}\big)^{a},\quad|t|>R,

that is,
\psi
satisfies (2) of Definition
4.4
. Therefore,
\Omega_{0}
is a logarithmic starlike at infinity domain.

Finally, let
\lambda\leq 0
. By the conformal invariance of the Hardy spaces,
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega_{0})
if and only if
e^{\lambda\eta(z)}\in H^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. Take any
b\in(a,1)
. It is easy to see that there exists some
R>0

such that

p\lambda{\sf Re}\,\eta(w)\leq p|\lambda|\,{\sf Re}\,\big(\log(w+3)\big)^{a}\leq\log{\sf Re}\,(w+3)^{b},\quad{\sf Re}\,w\geq 0,|w|>R.

Hence for some
C>0
,

p\lambda{\sf Re}\,\eta(w)\leq C+\log{\sf Re}\,(w+3)^{b},\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

which implies that
e^{p\lambda{\sf Re}\,\eta(w)}\leq e^{C}\cdot{\sf Re}\,(w+3)^{b}

in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. Since the function
{\sf Re}\,(w+3)^{b}
is harmonic in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

|e^{\lambda\eta(w)}|^{p}
has a harmonic majorant in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
, which proves that
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega_{0})
. Thus,
\Omega_{0}
has
p
-frequencies, and we are done. ∎

As a direct application of Theorem
1.4
and Proposition
10.2
, we have:

Corollary 10.3
.

Let
\psi
be any continuous function on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\psi(y)>C_{1}-\big(\log(|y|+3)\big)^{a}
for all
y
, where
C_{1}
and
a\in(0,1)
are constants. Then
\Omega:=\{x+iy:x>\psi(y)\}
is a Koenigs domain of a non-elliptic semigroup, and
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

10.2.
Necessary conditions for completeness of
p
-frequencies

The aim of this section is to give some necessary conditions for completeness of frequencies in
H^{p}(\Omega)
. We first show that a necessary condition is that the frequencies are not reduced to an interval:

Proposition 10.4
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a Koenigs domain of a non-elliptic semigroup such that
\mathbb{C}_{+}+c\subset\Omega
for some

c\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that for some
p_{0}\in[1,+\infty)
and
M>0
,

\Lambda_{p_{0}}(\Omega)\subset[-M,0].

Then
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is not dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
for every
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Notice that by Proposition
3.3
.(1), the condition
\Lambda_{p_{0}}(\Omega)\subset[-M,0]
implies that
either
\Lambda_{p_{0}}(\Omega)=[-N,0]
for some
N\geq 0
or

\Lambda_{p_{0}}(\Omega)=(-N,0]
for some
N>0
. Then, by Proposition
3.3
.(2), for any
q\in[1,\infty)
,

\Lambda_{q}(\Omega)
is also a finite interval.

We show that, if
\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
is a finite interval then
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is not dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
.

We may assume that
c=0
. Arguing by contradiction, we assume that
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega)
.

Let
h:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
be a Riemann map. We claim that
h^{-1}
extends continuously and injectively on
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}^{\infty}
. Indeed, if
ir\in\Omega
for some
r\in\mathbb{R}
then clearly
h^{-1}
extends continuously at
ir
. If
ir\in\partial\Omega
, then (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Proposition 3.3.3]
) there exists a point
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}h^{-1}(t+ir)=\sigma
and
h^{\ast}(\sigma)=ir
. Now, let
\{z_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{C}_{+}
be a sequence converging to
ir
. We can assume that
\{{\sf Re}\,z_{n}\}
is strictly decreasing. Let
\Gamma_{n}
be the segment joining
z_{n}
to
z_{n+1}
. Hence,
\Gamma=\cup_{n}\Gamma_{n}
is a Jordan arc contained in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
(since it is convex) converging to
ir
. It is easy to show (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Proposition 3.3.5]
) that the preimage of
\Gamma
via
h^{-1}
converges to
\sigma
. Thus
h^{-1}
extends univocaly at every
ir
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
. It is also easy to show (by the same token) that there exists
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
\lim_{\mathbb{C}_{+}\ni z\to\infty}h^{-1}(z)=\tau
. We set
h^{-1}(\infty)=\tau
. In order to show that
h^{-1}
is continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
, it is enough to show that if
\{ir_{n}\}
is a sequence converging to
ir
(with
r\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
) then
\{h^{-1}(ir_{n})\}
converges to
h^{-1}(ir)
. Indeed, let
\Gamma_{n}
be a Jordan arc in
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}^{\infty}
such that
ir_{n},ir\in\Gamma_{n}
and
\Gamma_{n}\setminus\{ir_{n},ir\}\subset\mathbb{C}_{+}
. We can take
\Gamma_{n}
in such a way that its spherical diameter tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Thus, if
\{h^{-1}(ir_{n})\}
does not converge to
h^{-1}(ir)
, there is a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}
such that
h^{-1}(\Gamma_{n_{k}})\cap\mathbb{D}
has diameter bounded from below by a positive constant. By this violates the no Koebe arcs theorem (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.4]
). Thus
h^{-1}
extends continuously on
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
. Finally, it is easy to see that
h^{-1}
is injective on
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
(see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Proposition 3.3.4]
).

Therefore,
G:=h^{-1}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is a Jordan domain in
\mathbb{D}
. By Mergelyan’s approximation theorem, polynomials are dense in the algebra of holomorphic functions which are continuous up to
\overline{G}
, since this last space is dense in
H^{p}(G)
, it follows that
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is dense in
H^{p}(G)
and thus
H^{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.

By Lemma
5.1
, the density of
H^{p}(\Omega)
in
H^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
implies that
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.

So to finish the proof it suffices to check that the last statement is false. To this end, choose a nonzero
C^{\infty}
function
s
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which vanishes on
[0,M]
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
be the Hardy-Smirnov space of the semiplane
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. By the Paley-Wiener theorem,
(2\pi)^{-1/2}\,\mathcal{L}
is an isometric isomorphism of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dx)
onto
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
(here
\mathcal{L}
is the Laplace transform).
Set
u=\mathcal{L}s
. Then
u\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
,
and, moreover,
(z+1)u\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.
The function
u
gives rise to
a bounded linear functional on
H^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, defined by

\tilde{u}(v)=\int_{i\mathbb{R}}u(-z)v(z)|dz|,\quad v\in H^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

By the Fourier inversion formula,

\tilde{u}(e^{\lambda z})=2\pi s(-\lambda),\quad\lambda\leq 0.

Since
s
is nonzero,

\tilde{u}
is a nonzero functional on
H^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. On the other hand,

s(-\lambda)=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
, which implies that
\tilde{u}
vanishes on exponentials
e^{\lambda z}
for

\lambda\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
. Hence the space of linear combinations of these exponentials is not dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.
∎

Example 10.5
.

Let
\eta(w)=w-\log(w+3)
. The arguments given in Proposition
10.2
show that
\eta
is univalent on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
and the domain
\Omega_{0}=\eta(\mathbb{C}_{+})
has the form
\Omega_{0}=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:x>\psi_{0}(y)\}
.

We claim that
\Lambda_{1}(\Omega_{0})=(-1,0]
. Hence, by Proposition
10.4
, we have that
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega_{0})
is not dense in
H^{p}(\Omega_{0})
, for all
p\geq 1
.

In order to prove the claim, let
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{0})
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Since
\bigcup_{t\geq 0}(\Omega_{0}-t)=\mathbb{C}
, it follows that the semigroup associated to the Koenigs domain
\Omega_{0}
is parabolic. Hence,
{\sf Re}\,\lambda\leq 0
by Theorem
3.2
.

Now,
|e^{\lambda\eta(w)}|\asymp|e^{\lambda w}||w|^{|\lambda|}
as

w\in\partial\mathbb{C}_{+}
goes to infinity. Taking into account that the harmonic measure
of
\mathbb{C}^{+}
is given by
d\omega_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}(w)=(|w|^{2}+1)^{-1}/\pi
,
one gets that
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{0})
if and only if
e^{\lambda\eta(w)}\asymp|e^{\lambda w}||w|^{|\lambda|}\in L^{1}(d\omega_{\mathbb{C}_{+}})
. From this it follows easily that if
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{0})
then
{\sf Im}\,\lambda=0
and
|\lambda|<1
. On the other hand, if
\lambda\in(-1,0]
, the estimate

\lambda{\sf Re}\,\eta(w)\leq|\lambda|\log|w+3|

implies that

|e^{\lambda\eta(w)}|\leq|w+3|^{|\lambda|}\leq K{\sf Re}\,(w+3)^{|\lambda|}

in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. Hence
{\sf Re}\,(w+3)^{|\lambda|}
is a harmonic majorant of
|e^{\lambda\eta}|
in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
and
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{0})
.

Note that
\Omega_{0}
is not a logarithmic starlike at infinity domain.

Next, we see that the existence of two non-exceptional maximal contact arcs with the same final point is an obstruction to
p
-completeness of frequencies (and not only to
\infty
-weak-star completeness):

Proposition 10.6
.

Assume
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a non-elliptic semigroup with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. If there exist two contact arcs
A,B
with final point
\sigma=x_{1}(A)=x_{1}(B)\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
then
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has no
p
-complete frequencies for every
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
h
be the Koenigs function of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. By Lemma
6.2
, we can find
\varepsilon>0
,
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and two non-empty open arcs
A^{\prime}\subset A
and
B^{\prime}\subset B
such that
h
extends holomorphically through
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
,

\Omega\cap\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\zeta-z_{0}|<\varepsilon\}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\zeta-z_{0}|<\varepsilon\}\setminus L,

where

L:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|{\sf Re}\,w-{\sf Re}\,z_{0}|<\varepsilon,{\sf Im}\,w={\sf Im}\,z_{0}\},

and
h(A^{\prime})=h(B^{\prime})=L
.

We claim that
h^{-1}\not\in\overline{\mathcal{E}(\Omega)}^{H^{p}(\Omega)}
. Since
h^{-1}\in H^{\infty}(\Omega)\subset H^{p}(\Omega)
, this proves the statement.

The claim, using conformal invariance, is equivalent to
z\not\in\overline{\hbox{span}\{e^{\lambda h(z)}:\lambda\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)\}}^{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})}
.

Indeed, for every
\lambda\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)
, it follows that
e^{\lambda h(A^{\prime})}=e^{\lambda h(B^{\prime})}
. Hence, any function
f
in
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
extends holomorphically through
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
and
f(A^{\prime})=f(B^{\prime})
.

Now, if
\{P_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{E}_{p}(\Omega)
converges in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
to
z
, since the sequence of boundary values
\{P_{n}^{\ast}\}
converges in
L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
to the boundary values of
z
, it follows that
\{P_{n}^{\ast}(e^{i\theta})\}
converges to
e^{i\theta}
for almost every
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
.

Recall that
P_{n}
extends holomorphically through
A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
and let
A^{\prime\prime}\subset A^{\prime}
be the set of full measure such that
\{P_{n}(e^{i\theta})\}
converges to
e^{i\theta}
and similarly define
B^{\prime\prime}\subset B^{\prime}
.

Let
V
be the (bounded) Jordan domain whose boundary is given by
A^{\prime}
and the segment joining the two end points of
A^{\prime}
. By the Riesz-Privalov theorem, applied to
h|_{V}
(see,
e.g.
,
[
24
, Theorem 6.8]
), we see that
h(A^{\prime\prime})
has full measure in
h(A^{\prime})=L
. Similarly,
h(B^{\prime\prime})
has full measure in
h(B^{\prime})=L
. Therefore, there exist
q\in A^{\prime\prime}
and
p\in B^{\prime\prime}
such that
P_{n}(q)=P_{n}(p)
for all
n
, but
\{P_{n}(p)\}
converges to
p
and
\{P_{n}(q)\}
converges to
q
, a contradiction.
Therefore,
z\not\in\overline{\hbox{span}\{e^{\lambda h(z)}:\lambda\in\Lambda_{p}(\Omega)\}}^{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})}
, and we are done.
∎

Anisotropic Weighted Levin-Cochran-Lee type inequalities on homogeneous Lie groups
Michael Ruzhansky, Anjali Shriwastawa, Bankteshwar Tiwari

Abstract
. In this paper, we first prove the weighted Levin-Cochran-Lee type inequalities on homogeneous Lie groups for arbitrary weights, quasi-norms, and L p L^{p} -and L q L^{q} -norms. Then, we derive a sharp weighted inequality involving specific weights given in the form of quasi-balls in homogeneous Lie groups. Finally, we also calculate the sharp constants for the aforementioned inequalities.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
26D10, 22E30, 45J05.

1.
History and Introduction

In 1984, Cochran and Lee rediscovered an exponential weighted inequality in their paper
[
3
]
, which was proved earlier in an unnoticed paper of V. Levin
[
13
]
in 1938 written in the Russsian language. We recall the following exponential weighted inequalities proved in the papers of Levin
[
13
]
and Cochran and Lee
[
3
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\epsilon
and
a
be two real numbers. Suppose that
f
is a positive function such that the function
t^{\epsilon-1}\log f(t)
is locally integrable on
(0,\infty).
Then the inequality

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\exp\left(\epsilon\,x^{-\epsilon}\int_{0}^{x}t^{\epsilon-1}\log\,f(t)\,dt\right)\right]x^{a}\,dx\leq\left(\exp\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a}\,f(x)\,dx

holds for
\epsilon>0,
and

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\exp\left(-\epsilon\,x^{-\epsilon}\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{\epsilon-1}\log\,f(t)\,dt\right)\right]x^{a}\,dx\leq\left(\exp\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a}\,f(x)\,dx

holds for
\epsilon>0.
Moreover, the constant
\exp\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)
is the best possible constant.

The inequality (
1.1
) is called the Levin-Cochran-Lee type
inequality and its complementary inequality (
1.2
) was proved by E. R. Love in
[
14
]
, which was again
reproved by G.-S. Yang and Y. J. Lin
[
22
]
. It is worth noting that inequality (
1.1
) is a generalization of the famous Knopp inequality
[
10
]
, which can be obtained by setting
a=0
and
\epsilon=1
in (
1.1
).
Thereafter, several works have been devoted to the study of these exponential type inequalities in different forms and in
different settings such as higher dimensional Euclidean spaces and Euclidean balls, by many authors. It is clearly impossible to give a complete
overview of the available literature, therefore we refer to the books, surveys and papers
[
1
,
3
,
2
,
4
,
8
,
10
,
11
]
and references
therein.

Čižmešija et al.
[
2
]
investigated an
n
- dimensional analogue of (
1.1
) by replacing the intervals
(0,\infty)
by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the means are considered over the balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered as origin. We state this inequality as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f
be a positive function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)
be the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with radius
|x|,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
centered at the origin, with its volume (with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) denoted by
|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|
. Then we have the following inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Bigg[\exp\Bigg(\epsilon\,{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}^{-\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|y|)|}^{\epsilon-1}\log\,f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\Bigg)\Bigg]|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{a}\,dx

(1.3)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\exp{\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{a}dx,

where
a
and
\epsilon>0
are two real numbers. Moreover, the constant
\exp{\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}}
appearing in (
1.2
) is a sharp constant.

The inequality (
1.2
) was further generalized by Jain et al.
[
8
]
to a more general situation involving general weight functions on the Euclidean space.

The main objective of this paper is to prove a Levin-Cochran-Lee type inequality involving general weight functions on homogeneous (Lie) groups equipped with a quasi-norm

|\cdot|
and a family of
dilations compatible with the group law. For a detailed description of analysis on homogeneous groups, we refer to
[
6
,
5
,
18
]
.
Particular examples of homogeneous groups are the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(in which case
Q=n
), the
Heisenberg group, as well as general stratified groups (homogeneous Carnot groups) and graded groups. Recently, Hardy type inequalities and their best constants have been extensively investigated in non-commutative settings (e.g. Heisenberg groups, graded groups, homogeneous groups); we cite
[
7
,
18
,
21
,
17
,
16
]
just to a mention a few of them.

Recently, the first author and Verma
[
19
]
obtained several characterizations of weights for two-weight integral Hardy inequalities to hold on general metric measure spaces possessing polar decompositions for the range
1<p\leq q<\infty
(see,
[
20
]
for the case
0<q<p
and
1<p<\infty
). Using this, one deduced the weighted integral Hardy inequality on homogeneous groups, hyperbolic spaces and Cartan-Hadamard manifolds. In particular, one proved the following theorem
[
19
]
which will be useful to establish results of the present paper.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group with the homogeneous dimension
Q,
and let
1<p\leq q<\infty.

Suppose that
u
and
v
are two weight functions on
\mathbb{G}.
Then the inequality

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}f(y)\,dy\right)^{q}u(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)\,v(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for all non-negative functions
f
on
\mathbb{G}
if and only if

\displaystyle\textbf{A}_{Q}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{1}{1-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}<\infty,

and the best constant
C
in
(
1.4
) can be estimated in the following way:

\displaystyle\textbf{A}_{Q}\leq C\leq\textbf{A}_{Q}\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}p^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Very recently, we have proved a sharp version of Theorem
1.3
in
[
15
]
. In fact, we have also calculated the precise value of sharp constants in respective inequalities on homogeneous groups. Using Theorem
1.3
we prove the following result which is one of the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group with the homogeneous dimension
Q
equipped with a quasi norm
|\cdot|
and
let
0<p\leq q<\infty.
Suppose that
u
and
v
are two positive weight functions on
\mathbb{G}.
Then, there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for all positive functions
f
on
\mathbb{G}
, the following inequality holds

(1.5)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log f(y)\,dy\right)\right]^{q}u(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)\,v(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

provided that

(1.6)

D_{Q}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}u_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log\frac{1}{v(y)}\,dy\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Here
u_{1}
is the spherical average of
u,
given by

(1.7)

\displaystyle u_{1}(x):=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}|}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}u(|x|\sigma)\,d\sigma,

where
\mathfrak{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{G}:|x|=1\}\subset\mathbb{G}
is the unit sphere with respect to the quasi-norm
|\cdot|
.

Moreover, the optimal constant
C
in (
3.1
) can be estimated as follows:

(1.8)

0<C\leq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}e^{\frac{1}{p}}D_{Q}.

We will also prove a conjugate version (see Theorem
3.3
) of Theorem
1.4
. Furthermore, we establish some stronger exponential inequalities on the quasi-balls on homogeneous Lie groups (see Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.4
).
In fact, we will prove the following result:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group with the homogeneous dimension
Q
equipped with a quasi norm
|\cdot|.

Let
0<p\leq q<\infty
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}.
Then for any
\epsilon>0
and for any arbitrary positive function
f
on the homogeneous Lie group
\mathbb{G},
the following inequality

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\Bigg[\exp\Bigg(\epsilon|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{-\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}

\displaystyle|\mathbb{B}(0,|y|)|^{\epsilon-1}\log\,f(y)dy\Bigg)\Bigg]^{q}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{a}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(1.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{b}\,dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for a positive finite constant
C
if and only if

(1.10)

\displaystyle p\left(a+1\right)-q\left(b+1\right)=0.

Moreover, the best constant
C
in (
1.5
) satisfies

(1.11)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\,\exp\bigg({\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg)\leq C\leq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\,\exp\bigg({\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg).

For the proof, we follow the method developed in
[
2
,
8
]
in the (isotropic and abelian) setting of Euclidean spaces. We note that also in the abelian (both isotropic and anisotropic) cases of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, our results provide new insights in view of the arbitrariness of the quasi-norm
|\cdot|
which does not necessarily have to be the Euclidean norm.

Apart from Section
1
, this manuscript is divided in two sections. In the next section, we will recall the basics of homogeneous Lie groups and some other useful concepts. The last section is devoted to presenting proofs of the main results of this paper.

Throughout this paper, the symbol
A\asymp B
means
\exists\,C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
C_{1}A\leq B\leq C_{2}A
.

2.
Preliminaries: Basics on homogeneous Lie groups

In this section, we recall the basics of homogeneous groups. For more details on homogeneous groups as well as several functional inequalities on homogeneous groups, we refer to monographs
[
5
,
6
,
18
]
and references therein.

A Lie group
\mathbb{G}
(identified with
(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ)
) is called a homogeneous group if it is equipped with the dilation mapping

D_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\lambda>0,

defined as

(2.1)

D_{\lambda}(x)=(\lambda^{v_{1}}x_{1},\lambda^{v_{2}}x_{2},\ldots,\lambda^{v_{N}}x_{N}),\quad v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{N}>0,

which is an automorphism of the group
\mathbb{G}
for each
\lambda>0
. At times, we will denote the image of
x\in\mathbb{G}
under
D_{\lambda}
by
\lambda(x)
or, simply
\lambda x
.
The homogeneous dimension
Q
of the homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
is defined by

Q=v_{1}+v_{2}+\dots+v_{N}.

It is well known that a homogeneous group is necessarily nilpotent and unimodular. The Haar measure
dx
on
\mathbb{G}
is nothing but the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Let us denote the volume of a measurable set
\omega\subset\mathbb{G}
by
|\omega|
. Then we have the following consequences: for
\lambda>0

(2.2)

|D_{\lambda}(\omega)|=\lambda^{Q}|\omega|\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(\lambda x)dx=\lambda^{-Q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)dx.

A quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
is any continuous non-negative function
|\cdot|:\mathbb{G}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

|x|=|x^{-1}|
for all
x\in\mathbb{G},

(ii)

|\lambda x|=\lambda|x|
for all
x\in\mathbb{G}
and
\lambda>0,

(iii)

|x|=0\iff x=0.

If
\mathfrak{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{G}:|x|=1\}\subset\mathbb{G}
is the unit sphere with respect to the quasi-norm
|\cdot|
, then there is a unique Radon measure
\sigma
on
\mathfrak{S}
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
, we have the following polar decomposition

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}f(ry)r^{Q-1}d\sigma(y)dr.

Firstly, let us consider metric spaces
\mathbb{X}
with a Borel measure
dx
allowing for the following
polar decomposition
at
a\in{\mathbb{Y}}
: we assume that there is a locally integrable function
\lambda\in L^{1}_{loc}
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
we have

(2.4)

\int_{\mathbb{Y}}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}f(r,\omega)\lambda(r,\omega)d\omega_{r}dr,

for the set
\Sigma_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{Y}:d(x,a)=r\}\subset\mathbb{Y}
with a measure on it denoted by
d\omega_{r}
, and
(r,\omega)\rightarrow a
as
r\rightarrow 0
.

The examples of such metric measure spaces are the Euclidean space, homogeneous Lie groups, hyperbolic spaces and, more generally, Cartan-Hadamard manifolds.

Here we fix some notation which be used in the sequel. The letters
u
and
v
will be always used to denote the weights on homogeneous groups
\mathbb{G}.
A quasi-ball in the homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
with radius
|x|,

x\in\mathbb{G},
and centred at the origin will be denoted by
\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)
. We denote the Haar measure of the unit sphere
\mathfrak{S}
in
\mathbb{G}
by
|\mathfrak{S}|.
The Haar measure of the unit quasi-ball
\mathbb{B}(0,|x|),
denoted by
|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|,
can be calculated by using (
2.3
) as

\displaystyle|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}dy=\int_{0}^{|x|}r^{Q-1}\left(\int_{\mathfrak{S}}d\sigma\right)\,dr

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\left(\int_{0}^{|x|}r^{Q-1}dr\right)d\sigma=\frac{|x|^{Q}|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}.

For a given function
u
on
\mathbb{G},
the spherical average
u_{1}
of
u
is defined by

(2.6)

\displaystyle u_{1}(x):=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}|}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}u(|x|\sigma)\,d\sigma,

where
\mathfrak{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{G}:|x|=1\}\subset\mathbb{G}
is the unit sphere with respect to the quasi-norm
|\cdot|
.

3.
Main Results

In this section, we prove the weighted Levin-Cochran-Lee type inequalities on a homogeneous Lie group equipped with a quasi-norm for arbitrary weights. We will derive sharp weighted inequalities on quasi-balls in homogeneous (Lie) groups involving specific weights and also calculate the sharp constant for these inequalities.

We begin this section with the following Levin-Cochran-Lee inequality on homogeneous groups involving general weights.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group with the homogeneous dimension
Q
equipped with a quasi-norm
|\cdot|,
and
let
0<p\leq q<\infty.
Suppose that
u
and
v
are two non-negative functions on
\mathbb{G}.
Then, there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for all positive functions
f
on
\mathbb{G}
, the following inequality holds

(3.1)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log f(y)\,dy\right)\right]^{q}u(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)\,v(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

provided that

(3.2)

D_{Q}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}u_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log\frac{1}{v(y)}\,dy\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Here
u_{1}
is the spherical average of
u,
given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle u_{1}(x):=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}|}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}u(|x|\sigma)\,d\sigma,

where
\mathfrak{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{G}:|x|=1\}\subset\mathbb{G}
is the unit sphere with respect to the quasi-norm
|\cdot|
.

Moreover, the optimal constant
C
in (
3.1
) can be estimated as follows:

(3.4)

0<C\leq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\exp\bigg({\frac{1}{p}}\bigg)D_{Q}.

Proof.

We begin with the proof by rewriting Theorem
1.3
by replacing
\frac{p}{\alpha},\,\frac{q}{\alpha},\,u(x)|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{-q}{\alpha}}
and
f^{\alpha}
in the places of
p,\,q,\,u(x)
and
f,
respectively, where
0<\alpha<p
. Indeed, we get the following inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}f^{\alpha}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{q}{\alpha}}u(x)|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{-q}{\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\leq C_{\alpha}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{\alpha\frac{p}{\alpha}}(x)\,v(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}},

which in turn implies that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}f^{\alpha}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{q}{\alpha}}u(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)\,v(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

holds for all non-negative functions
f\in\mathbb{G}
if

\displaystyle\textbf{A}_{Q,\alpha}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\,|\mathbb{B}(0,|y|)|^{-\frac{q}{\alpha}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}<\infty,

and the constant
C_{\alpha}
satisfies the following estimate:

(3.6)

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq\textbf{A}_{Q,\alpha}\left(\frac{p}{p-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}.\left(\frac{p}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

We note that

\displaystyle\textbf{A}_{Q,\alpha}\left(\frac{p}{p-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}.\left(\frac{p}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=A_{Q,\alpha}\left(\frac{p}{p-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}\times p^{\frac{1}{q}},

with

(3.7)

\displaystyle A_{Q,\alpha}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{-q}{\alpha}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}.

Therefore, from (
3.6
), we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq A_{Q,\alpha}\left(\frac{p}{p-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}\times p^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Since

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\frac{p}{p-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}=e^{\frac{1}{p}},

using (
3.9
) in (
3.8
), we get

(3.10)

\displaystyle C\leq A_{Q}\,p^{\frac{1}{q}}\,e^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

A_{Q}:=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}A_{Q,\alpha}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad C:=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}C_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Recall that by (
2
) we have,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{B}(0,|y|)|:=\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|y|)}dx=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\left(\int_{0}^{|y|}r^{Q-1}dr\right)d\sigma=\frac{|y|^{Q}|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}.

Now, let us calculate the first integral from (
3.7
). We get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\,|\mathbb{B}(0,|y|)|^{\frac{-q}{\alpha}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\left(\frac{|y|^{Q}}{Q}|\mathfrak{S}|\right)^{\frac{-q}{\alpha}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{-q}{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\frac{1}{|y|^{\frac{Qq}{\alpha}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{-q}{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{\frac{Qq}{\alpha}}|x|^{\frac{-Qq}{\alpha}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{-q}{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{\frac{Qq}{\alpha}}|x|^{Q}\,|x|^{-Q}\,|x|^{\frac{-Qq}{\alpha}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{-1}{\alpha}}|x|^{\frac{-Q}{\alpha}}\,|x|^{\frac{Q}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{\frac{Qq}{\alpha}}|x|^{-Q}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Also, calculating the second integral of (
3.7
), we get that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}=\left(\frac{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|x|^{Q}}{Q}|\mathfrak{S}|\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}|x|^{Q\left(\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}\right)}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}.

Next, substituting the values from (
3
) and (
3
) in (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle A_{Q,\alpha}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{-1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}|x|^{\frac{-Q}{\alpha}}|x|^{\frac{Q}{q}}|x|^{Q\left(\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}\right)}\times

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{\frac{Qq}{\alpha}}|x|^{-Q}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}.

Thus we find,

(3.13)

\displaystyle A_{Q,\alpha}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|x|^{\frac{Q}{q}-\frac{Q}{p}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{-1}{p}}\times

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{\frac{Qq}{\alpha}}|x|^{-Q}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}.

Performing the variable transformation
y=|x|z
in the first integral of (
3.13
) and then using the polar decomposition by setting
z=r\omega,
we have

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}u(y)\left(\frac{|x|}{|y|}\right)^{\frac{Qq}{\alpha}}|x|^{-Q}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,1)}u(|x|z)\,|z|^{\frac{-Qq}{\alpha}}dz

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{1}^{\infty}u\left(|x|r\omega\right)r^{Q-\frac{Qq}{\alpha}-1}dr\,d\omega.

We observe that

\displaystyle\chi_{(1,\infty)}(r)\,Q\left(\frac{q}{\alpha}-1\right)\,r^{Q-\frac{Qq}{\alpha}-1}\longrightarrow\delta_{1}(r)\quad\text{as}\quad\alpha\to{0}^{+},

where
\delta_{1}(r)
is the Dirac delta function at
r=1.
Indeed, by choosing a test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
we see that
\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\langle\chi_{(1,\infty)}(r)\,Q\left(\frac{q}{\alpha}-1\right)\,r^{Q-\frac{Qq}{\alpha}-1},\phi\rangle=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{1}^{\infty}Q\left(\frac{q}{\alpha}-1\right)\,r^{Q-\frac{Qq}{\alpha}-1}\phi(r)\,dr=-\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}(r^{Q-\frac{Qq}{\alpha}}\phi(r)|_{r=1}^{\infty})+\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{Q-\frac{Qq}{\alpha}}\phi^{\prime}(r)dr=\phi(1)=\langle\delta_{1},\phi\rangle.
This implies that

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(r)\,Q\left(\frac{q}{\alpha}-1\right)\,r^{Q-\frac{Qq}{\alpha}-1}=\delta_{1}(r).

Indeed, for
r\in(1,\infty)
and for
\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+},
we have
\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}r^{Q-\frac{Qq}{\alpha}}=0,

which shows that

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(r)\,\frac{1}{\alpha}\,r^{Q-\frac{Qq}{\alpha}-1}=\frac{\delta_{1}(r)}{Qq}.

Thus, from (
3
), we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(x)\rightarrow\frac{1}{Qq}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}u\left(|x|\omega\right)\,d\omega=\frac{1}{Qq}\,|\mathfrak{S}|\,u_{1}(x)\quad\text{as}\quad\alpha\to{0}^{+}.

A simple calculation gives, as
\beta\to{0}^{+},
that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}f^{\beta}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\rightarrow\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log\,f(y)\,dy\right).

Next, using (
3.16
) in the second integral of (
3.13
), we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}v^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-p}}(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}\longrightarrow\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log\frac{1}{v(y)}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{as}\quad\alpha\to{0}^{+}.

Substituting (
3.15
) and (
3.17
) in (
3.13
) as
\alpha\to{0}^{+}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{Q}=\lim_{\alpha\to{0}^{+}}A_{Q,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\frac{Q}{q}-\frac{Q}{p}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{-1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{Qq}|\mathfrak{S}|u_{1}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log\frac{1}{v(y)}dy\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}q^{\frac{-1}{q}}|x|^{\frac{Q}{q}-\frac{Q}{p}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}u^{\frac{1}{q}}_{1}(x)\left(\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log\frac{1}{v(y)}dy\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}q^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left(|x|^{Q}\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\,u_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)\left(\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log\frac{1}{v(y)}dy\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad=q^{-\frac{1}{q}}D_{Q},

which is finite by the hypothesis of the theorem.

Thus, putting the value of
A_{Q}
from (
3
) in (
3.10
), we get

(3.19)

\displaystyle 0<C\leq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{p}\bigg)\,D_{Q},

which is same as (
3.4
).

Finally, using (
3.16
) in (
3.5
), we obtain

(3.20)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log f(y)\,dy\right)\right]^{q}u(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)\,v(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

completing the proof of the theorem.
∎

In the next Theorem

, we will prove the following stronger inequality for the special case
u(x)=|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{a}
and
v(x)=|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{b}
:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group with the homogeneous dimension
Q
equipped with a quasi norm
|\cdot|.

Let
0<p\leq q<\infty
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}.
Then for any
\epsilon>0
and for any arbitrary positive function
f
on the homogeneous Lie group
\mathbb{G},
the following inequality

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\Bigg[\exp\Bigg(\epsilon|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{-\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}

\displaystyle|\mathbb{B}(0,|y|)|^{\epsilon-1}\log\,f(y)dy\Bigg)\Bigg]^{q}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{a}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{b}\,dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for a positive finite constant
C
if and only if

(3.22)

\displaystyle p\left(a+1\right)-q\left(b+1\right)=0.

Moreover, the best constant
C
in (
3.2
) satisfies

(3.23)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\,\exp\bigg({\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg)\leq C\leq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\,\exp\bigg({\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg).

Proof.

Assume that equality (
3.22
) holds. Let
f
be an arbitrary positive function on the homogeneous Lie group
\mathbb{G}
equipped with a quasi norm
|\cdot|
. To prove (
3.2
) we first obtain its equivalent form using polar decomposition on
\mathbb{G}
and then we use
Theorem
3.1
to establish it. Indeed, using the polar decomposition on
\mathbb{G}
, by setting
x=r\sigma
and
y=t\tau
in (
3.2
) on
\mathbb{G},
we get an equivalent inequality of (
3.2
):

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{a}

\displaystyle r^{Qa+Q-1}\Bigg[\exp\bigg(\epsilon r^{-Q\epsilon}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{-\epsilon}\times

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{r}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\epsilon-1}t^{Q\epsilon-1}\log f(t\tau)dt\,d\tau\bigg)\Bigg]^{q}dr\,d\sigma\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.24)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq C\left(\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{Qb+Q-1}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{b}f^{p}(r\sigma)\,dr\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Next, we do variable transformation
r=r_{1}^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}
and
t=t_{1}^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}
in (
3
) to obtain

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{a}r_{1}^{Q\left({\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1}\right)}r_{1}^{Q-1}\bigg[\exp\Bigg(\frac{Qq}{|\mathfrak{S}|r_{1}^{Q}}\quad\times

\displaystyle\quad\quad\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{r_{1}}t_{1}^{Q-1}\,\times\log f\left(t_{1}^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\tau\right)\,dt_{1}\,d\tau\Bigg)\bigg]^{q}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,dr_{1}d\sigma\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.25)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\leq C\left(\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{b}r_{1}^{Q\left({\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1}\right)}r_{1}^{Q-1}\,f^{p}(r_{1}^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\sigma)\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,dr_{1}\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Recalling the volume of
|\mathbb{B}(0,|z|)|
in
\mathbb{G}
from (
2
), that is,

|\mathbb{B}(0,|z|)|=\frac{|z|^{Q}|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}

and
using this in (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathbb{B}(0,r_{1})|^{\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}\,\,\times

\displaystyle\quad\quad\bigg[\exp\left(\frac{q}{|\mathbb{B}(0,r_{1})|}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{r_{1}}\log\,F(t_{1}\tau)t_{1}^{Q-1}\,dt_{1}d\tau\right)\bigg]^{q}r_{1}^{Q-1}dr_{1}d\sigma\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\left(\frac{b+1}{p}-\frac{a+1}{q}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}\left(\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathbb{B}(0,r_{1})|^{\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}F^{p}(r_{1}\sigma)r_{1}^{Q-1}\,dr_{1}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we have written
F(r\sigma)=f(r^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\sigma).

Again using the polar decomposition in
\mathbb{G}
with
t_{1}\tau=z
and
r_{1}\sigma=w
, the inequality (
3
) yields that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}\left[\exp\bigg(\frac{q}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)}\log\,F(z)\,dz\bigg)\right]^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\left(\frac{b+1}{p}-\frac{a+1}{q}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}F^{p}(w)\,dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Recalling the assumption from (
3.22
) that,
\frac{b+1}{p}-\frac{a+1}{q}=0,
we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}\left[\exp\bigg(\frac{q}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)}\log\,F(z)\,dz\bigg)\right]^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.28)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}F^{p}(w)\,dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Now, we note that the above inequality (
3
) is equivalent to the inequality (
3.2
). Therefore, to prove (
3.2
), it is enough to show that
D_{Q}
(ref. (
3.2
)) is finite.
Thus, we apply Theorem
3.1
with the corresponding weights
u(w)=|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}
and
v(w)=|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}

on
\mathbb{G}.
For this case,

(3.29)

\displaystyle D_{Q}=\sup_{w\in\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\,u^{\frac{1}{q}}_{1}(w)\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)}\log\frac{1}{v(s)}ds\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
u_{1}(w)=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}|}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}u(|w|\sigma)\,d\sigma.

Let us now calculate the value of
u_{1}.
In fact, we have

\displaystyle u^{\frac{1}{q}}_{1}(w)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}|}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}u(|w|\sigma)\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathfrak{S}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.30)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{1}{q}\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}\left(\int_{\mathfrak{S}}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{1}{q}\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}.

Now, we calculate the value of the integral in right hand side of (
3.29
) by using the polar decomposition
s=(t,w)
with
|s|=t
as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)}\log\frac{1}{v(s)}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)}\log|\mathbb{B}(0,|s|)|^{-\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{|w|}\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)t^{Q-1}\log\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}t^{Q}\right)dt\,dw

(3.31)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{0}^{|w|}|\mathfrak{S}|\,t^{Q-1}\log\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}t^{Q}\right)dt.

Observe that,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}}\log UdU=\log U\times U\bigg|_{0}^{\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}}-\int_{0}^{\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}}\frac{d}{dU}(\log U)\times U

(3.32)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\left(\log\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}\right)\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}\right)-\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}\right).

Using (
3
) in (
3
) along with the change of variable
\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}t^{Q}=U
and using
|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|=\frac{|w|^{Q}|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q},
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)}\log\frac{1}{v(s)}\,ds

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{0}^{\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}}\log UdU

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)\left[\left(\log\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}\right)\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}\right)-\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}\right)\left[\log\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}|w|^{Q}\right)-1\right]

(3.33)

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|\left(\log|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)}+\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right).

Substituting the values from (
3
) and (
3
) in (
3.29
), we obtain

\displaystyle D_{Q}=\sup_{w\in\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\,|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{1}{q}\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}

\displaystyle\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|}\,|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|\left(\log|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)}+\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{w\in\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{a+1}{q\epsilon}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\left[\exp\left(\log|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}}+\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

(3.34)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}\sup_{w\in\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{a+1}{q\epsilon}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}},

which implies that

(3.35)

\displaystyle D_{Q}=\exp\bigg({\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}\bigg)\sup_{w\in\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{a+1}{q}-\frac{b+1}{p}\right)}.

Thus, using the assumption (
3.22
) in (
3.35
), we have

(3.36)

\displaystyle D_{Q}=\exp\bigg({\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}\bigg),

which is finite.
Therefore, by Theorem
3.1
inequality (
3.2
) holds for each positive function
f
defined on
\mathbb{G}.

Moreover, by (
3.4
) and using the explicit value of
D_{Q}
from (
3.36
), we get

(3.37)

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}C\leq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\exp\bigg({\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg)\,\,\text{implies that}\,\,C\leq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\,\exp\bigg({\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg).

Conversely, we assume that (
3.2
) holds for all positive functions
f
on
\mathbb{G}
. Again we will use the equivalent form (
3
) of (
3.2
). It is clear from (
3
) that the following inequality is true for any ball
\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)
of radius
x\in\mathbb{G}:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}\left[\exp\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)}\log\,F(z)\,dz\right]^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.38)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\left(\frac{b+1}{p}-\frac{a+1}{q}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}F^{p}(w)\,dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

So, we will test the inequality (
3
) (and therefore, equivalently, inequality (
3.2
)) with the test function, for
x\in\mathbb{G}
as chosen above,

\displaystyle F_{x}(z)=|\mathbb{B}(0,|z|)|^{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)}\,\chi_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)},\quad\quad z\in\mathbb{G},

where
\chi_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}
is the characteristic function of
\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)
in
\mathbb{G}.
Indeed, we obtain by noting
|z|\leq|w|\leq|x|
that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)}\left[\exp\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)}\log|\mathbb{B}(0,|z|)|^{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)}\,dz\right]^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.39)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\left(\frac{b+1}{p}-\frac{a+1}{q}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p}}=\tilde{C}\,|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

(3.40)

\displaystyle\tilde{C}=C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\left(\frac{b+1}{p}-\frac{a+1}{q}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}.

Again, using the polar decomposition on
\mathbb{G}
and doing calculations similar to (
3
), we get

(3.41)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)}\log|\mathbb{B}(0,|z|)|^{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)}dz=|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|\left(\log|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)}+\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)\right).

Using (
3.41
) in (

) we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1}\Bigg(\exp\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|}\Bigg\{|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|\times

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left(\log|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)}+\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)\right)\Bigg\}\Bigg)^{q}dw\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\tilde{C}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1}\left(|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)}\,\exp\left(\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}-1\right)\right)\right)^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.42)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\leq\tilde{C}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Therefore, from (
3
), we can rewrite (
3
) in the following form

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}-\frac{1}{p}\right\}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}-1+\frac{q}{p}\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}\right)}\,dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\tilde{C}\,|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p}},

which further can be rewritten as

(3.43)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\left(\frac{q}{p}\left(\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}\frac{p}{q}-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}+1\right)-1\right)}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\tilde{C}\,\exp\left\{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}\right\}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Therefore, the inequality (
3.43
) implies that

(3.44)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\tilde{C}\,\exp\left\{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}\right\}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

(3.45)

\displaystyle p_{0}=\frac{p}{\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}\frac{p}{q}-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}+1}.

Now, again using the polar decomposition on
\mathbb{G},
from (
3.44
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}|w|^{Q(\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1)}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\left(\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{0}^{|x|}r^{Q-1}\,r^{Q(\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1)}dr\,d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\left(\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1}\int_{0}^{|x|}|\mathfrak{S}|\,r^{Q\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1}|\mathfrak{S}|\,|x|^{Q\frac{q}{p_{0}}}\left(\frac{p_{0}}{Qq}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad=\left(\frac{p_{0}}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|x|^{Q}\,|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}=|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\left(\frac{p_{0}}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Thus we have

(3.46)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|^{\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\left(\frac{p_{0}}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Therefore, using (
3.46
) in (
3.44
), we deduce that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\left(\frac{p_{0}}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\tilde{C}\,\exp\bigg({\frac{1}{p}-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg)\,|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p}},

which implies that

(3.47)

\displaystyle|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq\tilde{C}\,\exp\bigg({\frac{1}{p}-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg)\left(\frac{q}{p_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Since (
3.47
) holds for every
x\in\mathbb{G},
this implies that
p_{0}=p,
that is, from (
3.45
),

\frac{a+1}{\epsilon}\frac{p}{q}-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon}+1=1.

Hence

(3.48)

\displaystyle\frac{a+1}{q}-\frac{b+1}{p}=0,

which is same as (
3.22
).

Again substituting the value of
\tilde{C}

from (
3.40
) in (
3.47
), we estimate that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq\exp\bigg({\frac{1}{p}-\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg)\left(\frac{q}{p_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q}\right)^{\left(\frac{b+1}{p}-\frac{a+1}{q}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}

which with the fact that
p=p_{0}
and (
3.48
) gives that

(3.49)

\displaystyle C\geq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\exp\bigg({\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg)\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}.

This completes the proof of the theorem. ∎

Next, we also state the following result which is conjugate of Theorem

3.1
:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
u
and
v
be weight functions on a homogeneous Lie group
\mathbb{G}
of homogeneous dimension
Q,
equipped with an arbitrary quasi norm
|\cdot|,
and let
0<p\leq q<\infty
and
\epsilon>0.
Then, there exist a positive constant
C
such that, for all positive functions
f
on
\mathbb{G}
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left[\exp\left(\epsilon\left|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)\right|^{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\left|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)\right|^{-\epsilon-1}\log f(y)\,dy\right)\right]^{q}u(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.50)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)\,v(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

provided that

(3.51)

\tilde{D}_{Q}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{G}}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\,\tilde{u}^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}\log\frac{1}{\tilde{v}(y)}\,dy\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

where
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{v}
are the spherical average of
u
and
v
respectively, given by,

\tilde{u}(s)=u(s^{-\frac{1}{\epsilon}})\frac{1}{\epsilon}|s|^{-Q\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}\quad\quad\tilde{v}(s)=v(s^{-\frac{1}{\epsilon}})\frac{1}{\epsilon}|s|^{-Q\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}.

Moreover, the optimal constant
C
in (
3.3
) can estimated as follows:

(3.52)

0<C\leq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\exp\bigg({\frac{1}{p}}\bigg)\tilde{D}_{Q}.

Proof.

Let
f
be a positive function on the homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
with an arbitrary quasi norm
|\cdot|
. Now, using the polar decomposition of (
3.3
) on the homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
similar to (
3
) in Theorem
3.2
and again, making some variable transformations similar to (
3
) and using (
2
), (
3.3
) can be written as

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,|w|)}\log\,f(z^{-\frac{1}{\epsilon}})\,dz\right)\right]^{q}\tilde{u}(w)\,dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq

(3.53)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(w^{-\frac{1}{\epsilon}})\,\tilde{v}(w)\,dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Again, applying Theorem
3.1
, using polar decomposition, (
2
) and some variable transformations
we get the required result (
3.52
).
∎

Next, we will define the conjugate of Theorem
3.2
for the power weights
u(x)=|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{a}
and
v(x)=|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{b}
. We have the following inequality.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group with the homogeneous dimension
Q
equipped with a quasi norm
|\cdot|.

Let
0<p\leq q<\infty,
and let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon>0.
Then for all positive functions
f
on
\mathbb{G}
the inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left[\exp\left(\epsilon|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)}|\mathbb{B}(0,|y|)|^{(-\epsilon-1)}\log f(y)\,dy\right)\right]^{q}|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{a}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq

(3.54)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)|\mathbb{B}(0,|x|)|^{b}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for some finite constant
C,
if and only if

\displaystyle p\left(a+1\right)-q\left(b+1\right)=0.

Moreover, the least possible constant
C
such that (
3.4
) holds can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\,\exp\bigg({-\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}\bigg)-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\leq C\leq\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\,\exp\bigg({-\left(\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}\right)}\bigg).

Proof.

The proof of this theorem
follows exactly same lines as the proof of Theorem

3.2
and therefore, we omit the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

If we take
p=q
and so that
a=b,
then the inequality (
3.4
) holds with the constant
C=\exp\bigg(-{\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg).
By using the test function in inequality (
3.4
)

f_{\delta}(x)=\begin{cases}\exp\bigg({\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg)|\mathbb{B}(0,1)|^{-(b+1)}|x|^{-\frac{Q}{p}(b+1-\epsilon\delta)},\quad x\in\mathbb{B}(0,1),\\
\exp\bigg({\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg)|\mathbb{B}(0,1)|^{-(b+1)}|x|^{-\frac{Q}{p}(b+1+\epsilon\delta)},\quad x\in\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,1),\end{cases}

and suppose that
\delta\rightarrow 0^{+},
it can be shown that the constant
C=\exp\bigg(-{\frac{b+1}{\epsilon p}}\bigg)
is sharp.

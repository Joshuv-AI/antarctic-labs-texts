Spontaneous breaking of U(1) symmetry at zero temperature in one dimension
Haruki Watanabe, Hosho Katsura, Jong Yeon Lee

Abstract
The Hohenberg–Mermin–Wagner theorem states that there is no spontaneous breaking of continuous symmetries in spatial dimensions d ≤ 2 d\leq 2 at finite temperature. At zero temperature, the classical/quantum mapping further implies the absence of continuous internal symmetry breaking in one dimension, which is also known as Coleman’s theorem in the context of relativistic quantum field theories. One route to violate this “folklore” is requiring an order parameter to commute with a Hamiltonian, as in the classic example of the Heisenberg ferromagnet and its variations. However, a systematic way of understanding the spontaneous breaking of internal U(1) symmetries has been lacking. In this Letter, we propose a family of one-dimensional models that display spontaneous breaking of a U(1) symmetry at zero temperature, although the order parameter does not commute with the Hamiltonian, unlike the Heisenberg ferromagnet. We argue that a more general condition for this behavior is that the Hamiltonian is frustration-free.



Introduction.—

In the study of condensed matter physics, understanding symmetry and how it can be spontaneously broken stands as a foundational pillar underpinning the elucidation of a diverse spectrum of emergent phenomena.
A pivotal theorem in this domain, known as the Hohenberg–Mermin–Wagner (HMW) theorem
Hohenberg 1967
;
Mermin and Wagner 1966
, states that any continuous symmetry cannot be spontaneously broken in spatial dimension
d\,{\leq}\,2
at a finite temperature
T\,{>}\,0
. The theorem has been recently extended to higher-form
Lake 2018
and multipole symmetries
Stahl et al. 2022
;
Kapustin and Spodyneiko 2022
, offering insights into highly constrained systems. With quantum-classical mapping
Sondhi et al. 1997
, the theorem further implies the absence of continuous symmetry breaking in one-dimensional quantum systems at zero temperature.

Notably, an exception has been known to the
T\,{=}\,0
version of the HMW theorem: the
\textrm{SO}(3)
symmetric Heisenberg ferromagnet and its variations
Anderson 2018
;
Zvonarev et al. 2007
;
Beekman et al. 2019
;
Watanabe 2020
. At a conceptual level, this exception has been understood to arise from the fact that an order parameter commutes with the Hamiltonian as it is simultaneously the generator of the global symmetry, which is possible only for non-abelian symmetry.
However, it was recently shown that a family of frustration-free Hamiltonians naturally occurring in the description of dynamical quantum systems may exhibit spontaneous breaking of the abelian
\textrm{U}(1)
symmetry in all dimensions
Ogunnaike et al. 2023
.

In this Letter, we propose and examine a new series of one-dimensional spin models that display spontaneous breaking of U(1) symmetry at
T\,{=}\,0
, whose order parameter
does not
commutes with the Hamiltonian. Then, we argue that the general criterion for this behavior is that the Hamiltonians is
frustration-free
: The Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}
is frustration-free if and only if there exists a simultaneous eigenstate of
\hat{H}_{i}
with its lowest eigenvalue
\alpha_{i}
for every
i

Tasaki 2020
1
1

1

Without loss of generality, we set
\alpha_{i}=0
by properly choosing the constant in
\hat{H}_{i}
.
. In other words, the ground state of
\hat{H}
simultaneously minimizes all
\hat{H}_{i}

\hat{H}_{i}

Leveraging the understanding that excitations in gapless frustration-free systems are softer than linearly dispersive modes

;
Anshu 2020

Spin Chain Model.—

The Hamiltonian for the first class of our new examples is given by
\hat{H}^{\text{(chain)}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(chain)}}

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(chain)}}

\displaystyle=J\big[s(s+1)-(\hat{s}_{i}^{z})^{2}\big]\big[s(s+1)-(\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z})^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{J}{2}(\hat{s}_{i}^{2+}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{2-}+\hat{s}_{i}^{2-}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{2+})-J\hat{s}_{i}^{z}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z},

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{i}^{2\pm}|m\rangle_{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[s(s+1)-m(m\pm 1)]|m\pm 1\rangle_{i},

(5)

which should be compared to the standard spin-raising/lowering operators
\hat{s}_{i}^{\pm}|m\rangle_{i}=\sqrt{s(s+1)-m(m\pm 1)}|m\,\,{\pm}\,\,1\rangle_{i}
.
Here,

is the state at the
i

.
For
s\,{=}\,1/2
,
\hat{s}^{2\pm}
coincides with
\hat{s}^{\pm}

discussed above.
For

\hat{s}_{i}^{2\pm}

. When
s\,\,{\geq}\,\,1
, the model has the following symmetries: a
\mathrm{U}(1)
spin rotation about

The ground states of the model are given by the uniform superposition of all orthonormal states in the

\displaystyle|M\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}=\frac{1}{N_{M}^{\prime}}\sum_{\{m_{i}\}|\sum_{i}m_{i}=M}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle.

(6)

Here,
|\{m_{i}\}\rangle
represents the normalized state satisfying
\hat{s}_{i}^{z}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle=m_{i}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle

In the summation,

For the U(1) symmetry generated by

Spin Ladder Model.—

The second class of our new examples is a spin ladder model. The Hamiltonian takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\text{(ladder)}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(ladder)}}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\big(\hat{h}_{i,1}-\hat{h}_{i,2}\big)^{2},

(7)

where
\hat{h}_{i,\alpha}
describes short-range interactions among spins on the
\alpha
th leg (
\alpha=1,2
), satisfying
\hat{h}^{\vphantom{T}}_{i,2}=\hat{h}_{i,1}^{T}
. This model is discussed in Ref.
Ogunnaike et al. 2023
as the effective Hamiltonian of Brownian random circuits
Lashkari et al. 2013
;
Gharibyan et al. 2018
;
Zhou and Chen 2019
;
Xu and Swingle 2019
;
Chen and Zhou 2019
. The model in Eq.(
7
) has
(i)
two U(1) symmetries for each leg and
(ii)
the anti-unitary
\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{2}
symmetry generated by the leg exchange accompanied by complex conjugation.

Although the choice of
\hat{h}_{i,\alpha}
can be quite arbitrary, for concreteness here we assume a typical XXZ Hamiltonian with Zeeman field:

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{i,\alpha}

\displaystyle=J_{x}\big(\hat{s}_{i,\alpha}^{x}\hat{s}_{i+1,\alpha}^{x}+\hat{s}_{i,\alpha}^{y}\hat{s}_{i+1,\alpha}^{y}\big)+J_{z}\hat{s}_{i,\alpha}^{z}\hat{s}_{i+1,\alpha}^{z}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{B_{z}}{2}\big(\hat{s}_{i,\alpha}^{z}+\hat{s}_{i+1,\alpha}^{z}\big),

(8)

where two U(1) symmetries are generated by
\hat{S}_{\alpha}^{z}\,{\coloneqq}\,\sum_{i}\hat{S}_{i,\alpha}^{z}
for
\alpha\,{=}\,1,2
. This model has an additional symmetry which is the bare complex-conjugation.

The groundstates of Eq.(
7

\displaystyle|M\rangle^{\text{(ladder)}}=\frac{1}{N_{M}^{\prime}}\sum_{\{m_{i}\}|\sum_{i}m_{i}=M}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle_{1}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle_{2}

(9)

for
M\,{=}\,-sL,-sL+1,\cdots,sL
,
where
|\{m_{i}\}\rangle_{\alpha}
represents the state satisfying
\hat{s}_{i,\alpha}^{z}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle_{\alpha}\,{=}\,m_{i}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle_{\alpha}
for the
\alpha
th leg.
Using the relation
\hat{h}^{\vphantom{T}}_{i,2}\,{=}\,\hat{h}_{i,1}^{T}

\hat{H}^{\text{(ladder)}}
is frustration-free.
There is no other groundstate as far as
\hat{h}_{i,\alpha}
is sufficiently general, e.g., Eq.(

and
B_{z}\neq 0

To diagnose the spontaneous breaking of the diagonal
\mathrm{U}(1)

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(s_{i,1}^{+}s_{i,2}^{+}+s_{i,1}^{-}s_{i,2}^{-})=\hat{s}_{i,1}^{x}\hat{s}_{i,2}^{x}-\hat{s}_{i,1}^{y}\hat{s}_{i,2}^{y},

(10)

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{i}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2i}(s_{i,1}^{+}s_{i,2}^{+}-s_{i,1}^{-}s_{i,2}^{-})=\hat{s}_{i,1}^{x}\hat{s}_{i,2}^{y}+\hat{s}_{i,1}^{y}\hat{s}_{i,2}^{x}.

Under the

\displaystyle e^{i\theta\hat{Q}}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{n}_{i}^{(1)}\\
\hat{n}_{i}^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}e^{-i\theta\hat{Q}}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos 2\theta&-\sin 2\theta\\
\sin 2\theta&\cos 2\theta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{n}_{i}^{(1)}\\
\hat{n}_{i}^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}.

(12)

We set
\hat{\mathcal{O}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{n}_{i}^{(1)}
as the order parameter. Again, the order parameter does not commute with the Hamiltonian, but the plot in Fig.

Perturbation.—
To elucidate the mechanism of the spontaneous symmetry breaking in proposed examples, we add the following perturbation to
\hat{H}^{\text{(chain)}}
:

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{\textrm{(chain)}}

\displaystyle=-\delta\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{s}^{z}_{i}\cdot\hat{s}^{z}_{i+1},

(13)

which preserves the symmetries of
\hat{H}^{\text{(chain)}}
.

In Fig.
1
(d), we plot the order parameter when
\delta\,{>}\,0
, showing that
\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}m(h)\,{=}\,0
and the
\mathrm{U}(1)
symmetry is unbroken. This observation aligns with the identified gap when
\delta\,{>}\,0
. When
\delta\,{<}\,0
, the system stays gapless, but its dispersion changes from a quadratic to linear form without long-range ordering (Sec.II of SM). The perturbed Ladder model, which preserves all symmetries yet breaks frustration-freeness, also exhibits an identical behavior.
Interestingly, this behavior mirrors that of perturbed ferromagnets, alluding to a potential link between the Chain and Ferromagnet models.
More importantly, at any value of
\delta\,{\neq}\,0
, the perturbed Chain Hamiltonian is no longer frustration-free, which suggests that the frustration-free nature plays a critical role in symmetry breaking here. We elaborate on these two aspects in the following sections.

Relations among three models.—

The three models discussed above have several similarities:
(i)
they are frustration-free.
(ii)
there is one ground state in each
\hat{S}^{z}\,{=}\,M
sector and the ground state degeneracy is
2sL+1
-fold.
(iii)
Anderson’s tower of states

, which commonly appear in systems with continuous symmetry breaking, is absent and the large

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}m(h)
. See Table
1
for the comparison of these examples. Here we explain the relation among these models.

First,
\hat{H}^{\text{(chain)}}
can be obtained from
\hat{H}^{\text{(ladder)}}
by projecting the
(2s+1)^{2L}

(2s+1)^{L}

\displaystyle|\{m_{i}\}\rangle=|\{m_{i}\}\rangle_{1}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle_{2}.

(14)

We denote this projection by
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
. This relation explains the similarity between the ground states in Eqs. (
6
) and (
9
). Also, the relation
\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(chain)}}=\hat{\mathcal{P}}\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(ladder)}}\hat{\mathcal{P}}
proves the positive-semidefinite property of
\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(chain)}}
. The coupling constant
J
in
\hat{H}^{\text{(chain)}}
is solely determined by
J_{x}
in
\hat{H}^{\text{(ladder)}}
as
J=J_{x}^{2}
and does not depend on
J_{z}
or
B_{z}
.

The relation between
\hat{H}^{\text{(FM)}}
and
\hat{H}^{\text{(chain)}}
is more subtle.
When
s\,{=}\,1/2
,
\hat{H}^{\text{(chain)}}\,{=}\,\hat{H}^{\text{(FM)}}
as explained above.
Although
\hat{H}^{\text{(ladder)}}
has only
\mathrm{U}(1)\,{\times}\,\mathrm{U}(1)
symmetry, an exact

spin rotation symmetry as well as the time-reversal symmetry emerge in the low-energy subspace specified by the projection
\hat{\mathcal{P}}

;
Gharibyan et al. 2018
;

Consequently, the low-energy effective Lagrangian for

When

\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(chain)}}

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}(\Delta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\frac{J}{\Delta}(\hat{s}_{i}^{x}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{x}+\hat{s}_{i}^{y}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{y}+\Delta\hat{s}_{i}^{z}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z})

where

for

. To elucidate, we introduce an invertible operator

.
We find that
\hat{\mathcal{S}}^{\pm}\coloneqq\hat{\mathcal{M}}\hat{S}^{\pm}\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}
connect degenerate ground states of
\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(chain)}}
and satisfy the standard commutation relation of spin-raising/lowering operators, i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{S}}^{\pm}|M\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}\propto|M\pm 1\rangle^{\text{(chain)}},\quad[\hat{\mathcal{S}}^{+},\hat{\mathcal{S}}^{-}]=2\hat{S}^{z},

(16)

similar to the
\hat{S}^{\pm}
operators for
\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(FM)}}
. See Sec. III of SM for the derivation. However, it is crucial that
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
is

, and thus,

and

.
Therefore, the chain Hamiltonian emerges as an intriguing system characterized by the spontaneous breaking of abelian continuous symmetry in one dimension. In fact, through Witten’s conjugation, we can create an infinite family of Hamiltonians with spontaneously broken internal U(1) symmetry starting from the ferromagnetic Hamiltonian.

In summary, we have the following relations among the three models:

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\text{(ladder)}}\xrightarrow{\text{Projection $\hat{\mathcal{P}}$}}\hat{H}^{\text{(chain)}}\xrightarrow{\text{Conjugation by $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$}}\hat{H}^{\text{(FM)}}.

(17)

Note that the first arrow connects the

physics between two, and the second arrow connects the
groundstate manifold
between the two.

Hohenberg–Mermin–Wagner theorem.—

Having seen that Abelian continuous symmetries may be spontaneously broken even in one dimension at zero temperature, let us confirm that this result does not contradict any existing no-go theorems. The HMW theorem for finite temperature was originally derived based on the Bogoliubov inequality which is nothing but the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for a correlation function
Hohenberg 1967
;
Mermin and Wagner 1966
.
This approach was extended to
T\,{=}\,0
in Ref.
Takada 1975
;
Shastry 1992
. There is also a proof of Nambu–Goldstone theorem from this direction
Wagner 1966
;
Stringari 1994
. Here we reproduce these results with some generalizations.

Let us consider a system defined on a
d
-dimensional lattice
\Lambda
whose Hamiltonian is given by
\hat{H}\coloneqq\sum_{\bm{i}\in\Lambda}\hat{H}_{\bm{i}}

Suppose that a continuous symmetry generated by

Gapless Excitations in Frustration-Free Systems.—

There are several recent general results on low-energy excitations in gapless frustration-free systems.
Suppose that
\hat{H}\,{=}\,\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}
is frustration-free and translation invariant with zero groundstate energy.
Let us take a length
\ell
(
3\leq\ell<L/2
) and define subsystem Hamiltonian by
\hat{H}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}\,{\coloneqq}\,\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}
.
We write the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of
\hat{H}
and

and
\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}

\lim_{L\to\infty}\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}}\,{=}\,0

such that

0\,{<}\,\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}\,{<}\,C(\ell^{2}+\ell)^{-1}

;
Gosset and Mozgunov 2016
;
Anshu 2020
.
For readers’ convenience, we sketch the proof of these results in Sec. VII of SM.
Therefore, assuming there is no weak edge zero mode
Verresen et al. 2021
, a spontaneously broken continuous symmetry for the frustration-free Hamiltonian is always accompanied by an excitation whose dispersion is quadratic or softer (
n\,{\geq}\,2
).
This is why spontaneous breaking of continuous symmetries is not prohibited for frustration-free Hamiltonians even in one dimension.

Conclusion and Outlook.—

In this Letter, we discussed illuminating spin models in which a
\mathrm{U}(1)
symmetry is spontaneously broken, where the spontaneous symmetry breaking implies the divergence of the uniform charge susceptibility
Momoi 1996
;
Kapustin and Spodyneiko 2022
. Unlike the case of the Heisenberg ferromagnet, the order parameter does not commute with the Hamiltonian.
We remark that in our models, symmetry generators are written by an unweighted sum of local operators unlike the orientational order in 2d at finite temperature
Halperin 2019
or dipolar symmetry breaking in 1d at zero temperature
Stahl et al. 2022
, whose mechanism behind of symmetry breaking is conceptually different.
Furthermore, we have identified the origin of interesting features shared among these models, which is the hidden
\mathrm{SO}(3)
symmetry realized in a non-unitary manner. This hints at a deeper connection between proposed models with the SSB of abelian U(1) symmetry and the Heisenberg ferromagnet with the SSB of nonabelian SO(3) symmetry breaking. Beyond proposing concrete models, our discussion clarifies that continuous symmetries are allowed to be broken in one dimension as far as the Hamiltonian is frustration-free.
The field theoretic understanding of these exotic behaviors would be an exciting future direction.

\displaystyle|M\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N_{M}}(\hat{S}^{-})^{sL-M}|\Phi_{0}\rangle,

(S9)

\displaystyle|M\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}=\frac{1}{N_{M}^{\prime}}\sum_{\{m_{i}\}|\sum_{i}m_{i}=M}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle.

(S10)

for
M=-sL,-sL+1,\cdots,sL
. We find

\displaystyle N_{M}=\sqrt{\frac{(2sL)!(sL-M)!}{(sL+M)!}},

(S11)

\displaystyle N_{M}^{\prime}=\sqrt{\sum_{\{m_{i}\}|\sum_{i}m_{i}=M}1}.

(S12)

Here we discuss their relations.

\displaystyle|M\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{M}}\sum_{\{k_{i}\}|\sum_{i=1}^{L}k_{i}=sL-M,0\leq k_{i}\leq 2s}\frac{(sL-M)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{L}k_{i}!}\prod_{i=1}^{L}(\hat{s}_{i}^{-})^{k_{i}}|\Phi_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(sL-M)!}{N_{M}}\sum_{\{k_{i}\}|\sum_{i=1}^{L}k_{i}=sL-M,0\leq k_{i}\leq 2s}\prod_{i=1}^{L}\binom{2s}{k_{i}}^{1/2}|\{m_{i}=s-k_{i}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(sL-M)!}{N_{M}}\sum_{\{m_{i}\}|\sum_{i}m_{i}=M}\prod_{i=1}^{L}\binom{2s}{s+m_{i}}^{1/2}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(sL-M)!}{N_{M}}\sum_{\{m_{i}\}|\sum_{i}m_{i}=M}\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}|\{m_{i}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(sL-M)!N_{M}^{\prime}}{N_{M}}\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}|M\rangle^{\text{(chain)}},

(S13)

where we used
(\hat{s}_{i}^{-})^{k}|s\rangle_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{(2s)!k!}{(2s-k)!}}|s-k\rangle_{i}
.

Similarly, starting from the ‘all down’ state
|\Phi_{0}^{\prime}\rangle
, we can write the groundstates of
\hat{H}^{\text{(FM)}}
as

\displaystyle|M\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}=\frac{1}{N_{-M}}(\hat{S}^{+})^{sL+M}|\Phi_{0}^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{(sL+M)!N_{M}^{\prime}}{N_{-M}}\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}|M\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}.

(S14)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{M}}|M\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}=\frac{(sL-M)!N_{M}^{\prime}}{N_{M}}|M\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}=\frac{(sL+M)!N_{M}^{\prime}}{N_{-M}}|M\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}.

(S15)

Since
\hat{\mathcal{M}}|\Phi_{0}\rangle=|\Phi_{0}\rangle
and
\hat{\mathcal{M}}|\Phi_{0}^{\prime}\rangle=|\Phi_{0}^{\prime}\rangle
, we obtain

\displaystyle|M\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}=\frac{1}{(sL-M)!N_{M}^{\prime}}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}\hat{S}^{-}\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{-1})^{sL-M}|\Phi_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{(sL+M)!N_{M}^{\prime}}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}\hat{S}^{+}\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{-1})^{sL+M}|\Phi_{0}^{\prime}\rangle.

(S16)

IV
Interpolating Hamiltonian

In this section, we discuss the interpolating Hamiltonian between the ferromagnetic Heisenberg model

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\text{(FM)}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(FM)}},

(S17)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(FM)}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq Js^{2}-\frac{J}{2}(\hat{s}_{i}^{+}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{-}+\hat{s}_{i}^{-}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{+})-J\hat{s}_{i}^{z}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z}

(S18)

and our spin chain model

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\text{(chain)}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(chain)}},

(S19)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(chain)}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq J\big[s(s+1)-(\hat{s}_{i}^{z})^{2}\big]\big[s(s+1)-(\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z})^{2}\big]-\frac{J}{2}(\hat{s}_{i}^{2+}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{2-}+\hat{s}_{i}^{2-}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{2+})-J\hat{s}_{i}^{z}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z}.

(S20)

Let
|\Phi_{0}\rangle=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}|s\rangle_{i}
be the fully polarized state.
The coherent state defined by

\displaystyle|\alpha\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}\coloneqq e^{\alpha\hat{S}^{-}}|\Phi_{0}\rangle=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}e^{\alpha\hat{s}_{i}^{-}}|s\rangle_{i}

(S21)

is a groundstate of
\hat{H}^{\text{(FM)}}
regardless of
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, because
\hat{S}^{-}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{s}_{i}^{-}
commutes with
\hat{H}^{\text{(FM)}}
. Indeed, this state can be written as
|M\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}
as

\displaystyle|\alpha\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{k=0}^{2s}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}(\hat{s}_{i}^{-})^{k}|s\rangle_{i}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{m_{i}=-s}^{s}\alpha^{s-m_{i}}\binom{2s}{s+m_{i}}^{1/2}|m_{i}\rangle_{i}=\sum_{M=-sL}^{sL}\alpha^{sL-M}\binom{2sL}{sL+M}^{1/2}|M\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}.

(S22)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle|\alpha\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{m_{i}=-s}^{s}\alpha^{s-m_{i}}|m_{i}\rangle_{i}=\sum_{M=-sL}^{sL}\alpha^{sL-M}N_{M}^{\prime}|M\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}.

(S23)

is a groundstate of
\hat{H}^{\text{(chain)}}
regardless of
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, because it is given as a superposition of
|M\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}
. The normalization factor
N_{M}^{\prime}
is defined in Eq.(
S11
).

To interpolate
|\alpha\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}
to
|\alpha\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}
, we introduce
q
-bracket,
q
-factorial, and
q
-binomial, respectively, by

\displaystyle[m]_{q}\coloneqq\frac{1-q^{m}}{1-q}=1+q+\cdots+q^{m-1},

(S24)

\displaystyle[n]_{q}!\coloneqq[n]_{q}[n-1]_{q}\cdots[2]_{q}[1]_{q},

(S25)

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}_{q}\coloneqq\frac{[n]_{q}!}{[k]_{q}![n-k]_{q}!}.

(S26)

We introduce an operator
\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)
by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{i}(q),

(S27)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{i}(q)|m\rangle_{i}=\binom{2s}{s+m}_{q}^{-1/2}|m\rangle_{i}

(S28)

and define

\displaystyle|\alpha(q)\rangle\coloneqq\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)|\alpha\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}.

(S29)

Since the
q
-binomial satisfies

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}_{q=1}=\binom{n}{k},

(S30)

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}_{q=0}=1,

(S31)

|\alpha(q)\rangle
coincides with
|\alpha\rangle^{\text{(FM)}}
at
q=0
and with
|\alpha\rangle^{\text{(chain)}}
at
q=1
.

We postulate the following form of the interpolating Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\hat{H}(q)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}(q),

(S32)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}(q)

\displaystyle\coloneqq J\Big[\sum_{l=0}^{s}c_{l}(q)(\hat{s}_{i}^{z})^{2l}\Big]\Big[\sum_{l=0}^{s}c_{l}(q)(\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z})^{2l}\Big]-\frac{J}{2}(\hat{s}_{i}^{+}(q)\hat{s}_{i+1}^{-}(q)+\text{h.c.})-J\hat{s}_{i}^{z}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z},

(S33)

where
\hat{s}_{i}^{-}(q)
is a generalized lowering operator defined by

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{i}^{-}(q)|m\rangle_{i}=w_{m}(q)|m-1\rangle_{i}

(S34)

for
m=-s+1,-s+2,\cdots,s
and
\hat{s}_{i}^{+}(q)\coloneqq(\hat{s}_{i}^{-}(q))^{\dagger}
. Furthermore, we assume
w_{s-(m-1)}(q)=w_{m-s}(q)
for
1\leq m\leq s
.
We require that
\hat{H}_{i}(q)
to be positive semi-definite. With this condition, one can determine the coefficients
c_{0}(q),c_{1}(q),\cdots,c_{s}(q)
and
w_{s}(q),w_{s-1}(q),\cdots
by solving that
|\alpha(q)\rangle
vanishes under the application of
\hat{H}(q)
regardless of
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. In practice, we can establish a system of equations by imposing
\hat{H}_{i}(q)
to vanish for all two-site states obtained by projecting
|\alpha(q)\rangle
onto each magnetization-
M
sector.
Note that
\hat{H}_{i}(q)|\alpha(q)\rangle=0
if and only if
\hat{H}_{i}(q){\cal P}_{M}|\alpha(q)\rangle=0
for all
M
due to U(1) symmetry, where
{\cal P}_{m}
is a projection onto a sector with
z
-magnetization
M
.

Below we present the solution for
s=1/2,1,3/2,2,5/2
, and
3
.

IV.0.1
s=1/2

We find

\displaystyle c_{0}(q)=\frac{1}{2},

(S35)

and

\displaystyle w_{\frac{1}{2}}(q)=1.

(S36)

The resulting Hamiltonian is the ferromagnetic Heisenberg model:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}(q)=\frac{J}{4}-\frac{J}{2}(\hat{s}_{i}^{+}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{-}+\text{h.c.})-J\hat{s}_{i}^{z}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z}

(S37)

We find

(S38)

\displaystyle c_{1}(q)=-q,

and

\displaystyle w_{1}(q)=\sqrt{2}\sqrt{q+1}.

(S40)

The resulting interpolating Hamiltonian

\displaystyle=J\Big[(q+1)-q(\hat{s}_{i}^{z})^{2}\Big]\Big[(q+1)-q(\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z})^{2}\Big]-\frac{J}{2}(q+1)(\hat{s}_{i}^{+}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{-}+\text{h.c.})-J\hat{s}_{i}^{z}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z}

(S41)

agrees with
\hat{H}_{i}(\Delta)
in the main text if
\Delta
is replaced with
1/(q+1)
.

IV.0.3
s=3/2

We find

\displaystyle c_{0}(q)=\frac{3}{8}\left(3q^{2}+3q+4\right),

(S42)

\displaystyle c_{1}(q)=-\frac{1}{2}q(q+1),

(S43)

and

\displaystyle w_{\frac{3}{2}}(q)=\sqrt{3}\sqrt{q^{2}+q+1},

(S44)

\displaystyle w_{\frac{1}{2}}(q)=q^{2}+q+2.

(S45)

IV.0.4
s=2

We find

\displaystyle c_{0}(q)=\frac{2\left(q^{5}+2q^{4}+3q^{3}+5q^{2}+4q+3\right)}{3(q+1)},

(S46)

\displaystyle c_{1}(q)=\frac{q\left(-5q^{4}-2q^{3}+q^{2}-9q+3\right)}{6(q+1)},

(S47)

\displaystyle c_{2}(q)=\frac{q\left(q^{4}-q^{2}+q-1\right)}{6(q+1)},

(S48)

and

\displaystyle w_{2}(q)=\sqrt{4}\sqrt{q^{3}+q^{2}+q+1},

(S49)

\displaystyle w_{1}(q)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\sqrt{\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{q+1}}\left(q^{3}+q^{2}+q+3\right).

(S50)

IV.0.5
s=5/2

We find

\displaystyle c_{0}(q)=\frac{5}{128}\left(15q^{6}+15q^{5}+25q^{4}+25q^{3}+70q^{2}+10q+64\right),

(S51)

\displaystyle c_{1}(q)=\frac{1}{48}q\left(-17q^{5}-17q^{4}+5q^{3}+5q^{2}-46q+22\right),

(S52)

\displaystyle c_{2}(q)=\frac{1}{24}q\left(q^{5}+q^{4}-q^{3}-q^{2}+2q-2\right),

(S53)

and

\displaystyle w_{\frac{5}{2}}(q)=\sqrt{5}\sqrt{q^{4}+q^{3}+q^{2}+q+1},

(S54)

\displaystyle w_{\frac{3}{2}}(q)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\frac{q^{3}+q^{2}+q+1}{q+1}}\left(q^{4}+q^{3}+q^{2}+q+4\right),

(S55)

\displaystyle w_{\frac{1}{2}}(q)=\frac{1}{2}\left(q^{6}+q^{5}+2q^{4}+2q^{3}+5q^{2}+q+6\right).

(S56)

IV.0.6
s=3

We find

\displaystyle c_{0}(q)=\frac{3\left(q^{11}+2q^{10}+4q^{9}+6q^{8}+8q^{7}+13q^{6}+13q^{5}+16q^{4}+19q^{3}+17q^{2}+11q+10\right)}{10\left(q^{2}+q+1\right)},

(S57)

\displaystyle c_{1}(q)=-\frac{q\left(49q^{11}+75q^{10}+78q^{9}+58q^{8}+56q^{7}+219q^{6}+68q^{5}-55q^{4}+95q^{3}+212q^{2}-130q-5\right)}{120(q+1)\left(q^{2}+q+1\right)},

(S58)

\displaystyle c_{2}(q)=\frac{q\left(7q^{11}+8q^{10}+3q^{9}-8q^{8}-9q^{7}+21q^{6}-2q^{5}-23q^{4}-7q^{3}+24q^{2}-18q+4\right)}{60(q+1)\left(q^{2}+q+1\right)},

(S59)

\displaystyle c_{3}(q)=-\frac{q\left(q^{11}+q^{10}-2q^{8}-2q^{7}+3q^{6}-3q^{4}-q^{3}+4q^{2}-2q+1\right)}{120(q+1)\left(q^{2}+q+1\right)},

(S60)

and

\displaystyle w_{3}(q)=\sqrt{6}\sqrt{q^{5}+q^{4}+q^{3}+q^{2}+q+1},

(S61)

\displaystyle w_{2}(q)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}\sqrt{\frac{q^{4}+q^{3}+q^{2}+q+1}{q+1}}\left(q^{5}+q^{4}+q^{3}+q^{2}+q+5\right),

(S62)

\displaystyle w_{1}(q)=\frac{\sqrt{3}\left(q^{11}+2q^{10}+4q^{9}+6q^{8}+8q^{7}+13q^{6}+13q^{5}+16q^{4}+19q^{3}+17q^{2}+11q+10\right)}{5\sqrt{q^{5}+2q^{4}+3q^{3}+3q^{2}+2q+1}}.

(S63)

We remark that in all the above examples the local Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{i}(q)
is positive semi-definite for
q\geq 0
. The proof is as follows. Since
\hat{H}_{i}(q)
commutes with
{\hat{S}}^{z}_{i,i+1}\coloneqq{\hat{s}}^{z}_{i}+{\hat{s}}^{z}_{i+1}
, it is block-diagonal with respect to the eigenspaces of
{\hat{S}}^{z}_{i,i+1}
. Let
{\cal V}_{M}
be the eigenspace spanned by the basis states
|m_{i}\rangle_{i}|m_{i+1}\rangle_{i+1}
with
m_{i}+m_{i+1}=M
. Since
w_{m}(q)>0
for all
m
, it is clear that the restriction of
\hat{H}_{i}(q)
to
{\cal V}_{M}
is
(i)
a matrix whose off-diagonal entries are all nonpositive and
(ii)
irreducible, in the basis we work with. Thus we can apply the Perron-Frobenius theorem to conclude that the ground state within
{\cal V}_{M}
is unique and can be written as

\displaystyle|\Psi_{M}\rangle_{i,i+1}=\sum_{(m_{i},m_{i+1})|m_{i}+m_{i+1}=M}c_{m_{i},m_{i+1}}|m_{i}\rangle_{i}|m_{i+1}\rangle_{i+1},

(S64)

where
c_{m_{i},m_{i+1}}>0
for all
(m_{i},m_{i+1})
such that
m_{i}+m_{i+1}=M
.

By the construction, the zero-energy state of
\hat{H}_{i}(q)
in
{\cal V}_{M}
is given as the projection of the local coherent state onto a sector with magnetization
M
denoted as
{\cal P}_{M}|\alpha(q)\rangle_{i,i+1}
, where

\displaystyle|\alpha(q)\rangle_{i,i+1}

\displaystyle={\hat{\cal M}}_{i}(q){\hat{\cal M}}_{i+1}(q)\,|\alpha\rangle^{({\rm FM})}_{i,i+1}={\hat{\cal M}}_{i}(q){\hat{\cal M}}_{i+1}(q)\,e^{\alpha({\hat{s}}^{-}_{i}+{\hat{s}}^{-}_{i+1})}\,|s\rangle_{i}|s\rangle_{i+1}.

(S65)

Since coefficients of
{\cal P}|\alpha(q)\rangle_{i,i+1}
in the
z
-basis are all positive as well,
\langle\Psi_{M}|{\cal P}_{M}|\alpha(q)\rangle_{i,i+1}>0
.
Since the Perron-Frobenius theorem tells that there can be no other eigenstate having only positive coefficients,

|\Psi_{M}\rangle_{i,i+1}
must coincide with the zero-energy state
{\cal P}_{M}|\alpha(q)\rangle_{i,i+1}
apart from an overall factor. Since the groundstate energy is zero, the eigenvalues of
\hat{H}_{i}(q)
are nonnegative in each
{\cal V}_{M}
, which proves that
\hat{H}_{i}(q)
is positive semi-definite.

IV.1
Witten’s conjugation I

The interpolating Hamiltonian constructed above is inspired by Witten’s conjugation
Witten 1982
;
Wouters et al. 2021
. Here we explain the connection.

The local Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(FM)}}=Js^{2}-J\hat{\vec{s}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\vec{s}}_{i+1}=Js(2s+1)-\frac{J}{2}(\hat{\vec{s}}_{i}+\hat{\vec{s}}_{i+1})^{2}

(S66)

can be decomposed into the summation of the projector onto the spin-
s^{\prime}
subspace (
s^{\prime}=0,1,\cdots,2s
):

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(FM)}}=J\sum_{s^{\prime}=0}^{2s}\frac{(2s-s^{\prime})(2s+1+s^{\prime})}{2}\hat{P}_{s^{\prime}}.

(S67)

Hence,
\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(FM)}}
can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(FM)}}=J\hat{L}_{i}^{2}=J\hat{L}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{L}_{i},

(S68)

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{i}\coloneqq\sum_{s^{\prime}=0}^{2s}\sqrt{\frac{(2s-s^{\prime})(2s+1+s^{\prime})}{2}}\hat{P}_{s^{\prime}}.

(S69)

Now we construct a one-parameter family of Hamiltonians of the following form:

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\prime}(q)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}^{\prime}(q),

(S70)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}^{\prime}(q)

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)^{-1}\hat{L}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{i}(q)\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)\hat{L}_{i}\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)^{-1}.

(S71)

for which
|\alpha(q)\rangle
in Eq.(
S29
) is a groundstate regardless of
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. Here,
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{i}(q)
describes a local operator around the
i
th spin,
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{i}(q)
which is assumed to be positive definite. As far as
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{i}(q=0)=1
and
\hat{H}_{i}^{\prime}(q=1)=\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(chain)}}
,
\hat{H}_{i}^{\prime}(q)
can be used as an interpolating Hamiltonian.

For example, for
s=1
,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{i}(q)=\frac{3(1+q)^{2}}{3+4q}-\frac{q(1+q)}{3+4q}\frac{1}{2}(\hat{s}_{i}^{+}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{-}+\text{h.c.})+\frac{q(2+3q)}{3+4q}\hat{s}_{i}^{z}\hat{s}_{i+1}^{z}.

(S72)

satisfies all the assumptions including the positive definite property for
0\leq q\leq 1
. However, we find that
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{i}(q)
can become quite complicated for higher
s
. To avoid such complication, we directly constructed the interpolating Hamiltonian without constructing
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{i}(q)
.

IV.2
Witten’s conjugation II

In this subsection, we discuss another class of frustration-free Hamiltonians obtained by Witten’s conjugation. We start with the
s=1/2
ferromagnetic Heisenberg model
\hat{H}^{\text{(FM)}}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(FM)}}
with the local Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}^{\text{(FM)}}=J\hat{L}^{2}_{i}=J\hat{L}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{L}_{i},

(S73)

\displaystyle{\hat{L}}_{i}\coloneqq\frac{1}{4}-\hat{{\bm{s}}}_{i}\cdot\hat{{\bm{s}}}_{i+1},

(S74)

where the number of sites
L
is even and
\hat{L}_{i}
is a projection operator (
\hat{L}^{2}_{i}=\hat{L}_{i}
). To deform this model, we introduce the operator
\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)
via

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)=q^{\hat{s}^{z}_{1}/2}q^{-\hat{s}^{z}_{2}/2}\cdots q^{-(-1)^{i}\hat{s}^{z}_{i}/2}\cdots q^{-\hat{s}^{z}_{L}/2},

(S75)

with the inverse

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)^{-1}=q^{-\hat{s}^{z}_{1}/2}q^{\hat{s}^{z}_{2}/2}\cdots q^{(-1)^{i}\hat{s}^{z}_{i}/2}\cdots q^{\hat{s}^{z}_{L}/2}.

(S76)

With this
\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)
, we get

\displaystyle\hat{L}^{\prime\prime}_{i}\coloneqq\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)\hat{L}_{i}\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)^{-1}=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}q^{-(-1)^{i}}\hat{s}^{+}_{i}\hat{s}^{-}_{i+1}-\frac{1}{2}q^{(-1)^{i}}\hat{s}^{-}_{i}\hat{s}^{+}_{i+1}-\hat{s}^{z}_{i}\hat{s}^{z}_{i+1},

(S77)

where we used
\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)\hat{s}^{\pm}_{i}\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)^{-1}=q^{\mp(-1)^{i}/2}\hat{s}^{\pm}_{i}
and
\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)\hat{s}^{z}_{i}\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)^{-1}=\hat{s}^{z}_{i}
. The construction of the deformed model proceeds along the same lines as in the previous subsection. We define a one-parameter family of Hamiltonians by

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\text{(XXZ)}}\coloneqq\sum^{L}_{i=1}\hat{H}^{\text{(XXZ)}}_{i},

(S78)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\text{(XXZ)}}_{i}\coloneqq(\hat{L}^{\prime\prime}_{i})^{\dagger}\hat{L}^{\prime\prime}_{i}.

(S79)

A straightforward calculation shows that

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\text{(XXZ)}}_{i}=\frac{q+q^{-1}}{2}\left[-\hat{s}^{x}_{i}\hat{s}^{x}_{i+1}-\hat{s}^{y}_{i}\hat{s}^{y}_{i+1}+\frac{q+q^{-1}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\hat{s}^{z}_{i}\hat{s}^{z}_{i+1}\right)+\frac{q-q^{-1}}{4}(-1)^{i}(\hat{s}^{z}_{i}-\hat{s}^{z}_{i+1})\right],

(S80)

which is, up to a prefactor, the local Hamiltonian of the frustration-free model studied in previous work
Alcaraz and Malvezzi 1995
;
Karadamoglou and Papanicolaou 1999
;
Wouters et al. 2018
.

Since Witten’s conjugation does not change the number of zero-energy states, the ground state degeneracy of
\hat{H}^{\text{(XXZ)}}
is
L+1
-fold. By construction it is clear that these degenerate ground states are obtained by acting with a deformed lowering operator on the fully polarized state
|\Phi_{0}\rangle=\bigotimes^{L}_{i=1}|\frac{1}{2}\rangle_{i}
:

\displaystyle|M\rangle^{(\text{XXZ})}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N^{\prime\prime}_{M}}(\hat{\mathcal{S}}^{-})^{\frac{L}{2}-M}|\Phi_{0}\rangle,\quad M=-\frac{L}{2},-\frac{L}{2}+1,\cdots,\frac{L}{2},

(S81)

where
\hat{\mathcal{S}}^{-}=\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)\hat{S}^{-}\hat{\mathcal{M}}(q)^{-1}
is the deformed lowering operator and
N^{\prime\prime}_{M}
is a normalization prefactor.

Interestingly, the
s=1/2
model in Eq. (
S80
) is deeply connected to a seemingly unrelated model – an anisotropic extension of the
s=1
ferromagnetic biquadratic model
Shi et al. 2022
;
Zhou et al. 2023
. The Hamiltonian of the model is given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(\text{BQ})}(J_{x},J_{y},J_{z})\coloneqq\sum^{L}_{i=1}\hat{H}^{(\text{BQ})}_{i}(J_{x},J_{y},J_{z}),

(S82)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(\text{BQ})}_{i}(J_{x},J_{y},J_{z})\coloneqq(J_{x}\hat{s}^{x}_{i}\hat{s}^{x}_{i+1}+J_{y}\hat{s}^{y}_{i}\hat{s}^{y}_{i+1}+J_{z}\hat{s}^{z}_{i}\hat{s}^{z}_{i+1})^{2},

(S83)

where the number of sites
L
is even. In the following, we focus on the special line in the parameter space where
J_{x}/J_{y}>0
and
J_{z}=0
, and argue that the model in a particular subspace can be mapped to the
s=1/2
model (
S78
). Note that the same argument applies to the lines obtained by cyclic permutations of the indices
x
,
y
, and
z
.

To proceed, we first note that the subspace consisting of
|\psi\rangle
such that
\hat{s}^{z}_{i}|\psi\rangle=\pm|\psi\rangle
for all
i
is an invariant subspace of the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}^{(\text{BQ})}(J_{x},J_{y},0)
. In other words,
\hat{H}^{(\text{BQ})}_{i}(J_{x},J_{y},0)
does not create
|0\rangle
when acting on a product state of the form
|m_{i}\rangle_{i}|m_{i+1}\rangle_{i+1}
, where
m_{i}m_{i+1}=\pm 1
.
We denote by
\mathcal{W}
the invariant subspace and introduce effective Pauli operators:

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}^{+}_{i}=|1\rangle_{i}\langle-1|,\quad\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{i}=|-1\rangle_{i}\langle 1|,\quad\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i}=|1\rangle_{i}\langle 1|-|-1\rangle_{i}\langle-1|.

(S84)

Let us denote by
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{W}
. A tedious but straightforward calculation shows that

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(\text{eff})}(J_{x},J_{y},0)

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{P}}\hat{H}^{(\text{BQ})}(J_{x},J_{y},0)\hat{\mathcal{P}}=\sum^{L}_{i=1}\hat{H}^{(\text{eff})}_{i}(J_{x},J_{y},0),

(S85)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(\text{eff})}_{i}(J_{x},J_{y},0)

\displaystyle=\sum^{L}_{i=1}\left[\frac{J^{2}_{x}+J^{y}_{2}}{4}\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{i}\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{i+1}+\frac{J_{x}J_{y}}{2}\hat{\sigma}^{y}_{i}\hat{\sigma}^{y}_{i+1}-\frac{J_{x}J_{y}}{2}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i+1}+\frac{J^{2}_{x}-J^{2}_{y}}{4}(\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{i}+\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{i+1})+\frac{J^{2}_{x}+J^{2}_{y}}{4}\right],

(S86)

where
\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{i}=\hat{\sigma}^{+}_{i}+\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{i}
and
\hat{\sigma}^{y}_{i}=(\hat{\sigma}^{+}_{i}-\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{i})/\mathrm{i}
. To make the link to the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
S80
), we perform a unitary transformation such that
\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{i}\to(-1)^{i}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i}
,
\hat{\sigma}^{y}_{i}\to(-1)^{i}\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{i}
, and
\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i}\to\hat{\sigma}^{y}_{i}
in Eq. (
S86
). As a result, we obtain the local Hamiltonian of
\hat{H}^{(\text{eff})}(J_{x},J_{y},0)
in the new basis:

\displaystyle\hat{\widetilde{H}}^{(\text{eff})}_{i}(J_{x},J_{y},0)=\frac{J_{x}J_{y}}{2}\left[-\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{i}\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{i+1}-\hat{\sigma}^{y}_{i}\hat{\sigma}^{y}_{i+1}+\frac{\frac{J_{x}}{J_{y}}+\frac{J_{y}}{J_{x}}}{2}(1-\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i+1})+(-1)^{i}\frac{\frac{J_{x}}{J_{y}}-\frac{J_{y}}{J_{x}}}{2}(\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i}-\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i+1})\right].

(S87)

With the identification
J_{x}/J_{y}\leftrightarrow q
, this local Hamiltonian is, up to a multiplicative constant, equivalent to
\hat{H}^{\text{(XXZ)}}_{i}
in Eq. (
S80
). This correspondence between the
s=1
and
s=1/2
models clearly explains the presence of highly degenerate ground states on the special line
J_{x}/J_{y}>0
and
J_{z}=0
found in Refs.
Shi et al. 2022
;
Zhou et al. 2023
. Moreover, since the ground states of
\hat{H}^{(\text{BQ})}(J_{x},J_{y},0)
are annihilated by
J_{x}\hat{s}^{x}_{i}\hat{s}^{x}_{i+1}+J_{y}\hat{s}^{y}_{i}\hat{s}^{y}_{i+1}
for all
i
, they are zero-energy ground states of a more general Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(2n)}(J_{x},J_{y},0)\coloneqq\sum^{L}_{i=1}(J_{x}\hat{s}^{x}_{i}\hat{s}^{x}_{i+1}+J_{y}\hat{s}^{y}_{i}\hat{s}^{y}_{i+1})^{2n},

(S88)

where
n=2,3,\cdots
. This is in accord with a recent study by Y-W. Dai et al.
Dai et al. 2023
, where the
n=2
case was studied in detail.

V
The Bogoliubov inequality

V.1
T>0

To derive the Bogoliubov inequality, we introduce the correlation function of two operators
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
:

\displaystyle(\hat{A},\hat{B})=(\hat{B}^{\dagger},\hat{A}^{\dagger}):=\beta\int_{0}^{1}dx\Big\langle e^{\beta x\hat{H}}\hat{A}e^{-\beta x\hat{H}}\hat{B}^{\dagger}\Big\rangle=\sum_{n,m}\langle n|\hat{A}|m\rangle\langle m|\hat{B}^{\dagger}|n\rangle\frac{e^{-\beta E_{m}}-e^{-\beta E_{n}}}{Z}\frac{1}{E_{n}-E_{m}},

(S89)

where the expectation value is taken by the Gibbs state of
\hat{H}
. Since this operation is a well-defined inner product between two operators
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality tells that
(\hat{A},\hat{A})(\hat{B},\hat{B})\geq|(\hat{A},\hat{B})|^{2}
.
By replacing
\hat{H}
with
\hat{H}(h)
and plugging in
\hat{A}=\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}
and
\hat{B}=[\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}},\hat{H}(h)]
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\hat{A},\hat{A}):=\beta\int_{0}^{1}dx\Big\langle e^{\beta x\hat{H}(h)}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}e^{-\beta x\hat{H}(h)}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\Big\rangle,

(S90)

\displaystyle(\hat{A},\hat{B}):=\beta\int_{0}^{1}dx\Big\langle e^{\beta x\hat{H}(h)}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}e^{-\beta x\hat{H}(h)}[\hat{H}(h),\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]\Big\rangle=\langle[\hat{X}_{\bm{k}},\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]\rangle,

(S91)

\displaystyle(\hat{B},\hat{B}):=\beta\int_{0}^{1}dx\Big\langle e^{\beta x\hat{H}(h)}[\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}},\hat{H}(h)]e^{-\beta x\hat{H}(h)}[\hat{H}(h),\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]\Big\rangle=\langle[[\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}},\hat{H}(h)],\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]\rangle.

(S92)

Also, we have

\displaystyle(\hat{A},\hat{A})=\sum_{n,m}|\langle n|\hat{A}|m\rangle|^{2}\frac{e^{-\beta E_{m}}-e^{-\beta E_{n}}}{Z}\frac{1}{E_{n}-E_{m}}\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{n,m}|\langle n|\hat{A}|m\rangle|^{2}\frac{e^{-\beta E_{m}}+e^{-\beta E_{n}}}{Z}=\frac{\beta}{2}\langle\hat{A}\hat{A}^{\dagger}+\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hat{A}\rangle,

(S93)

where we used

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\beta E_{m}}-e^{-\beta E_{n}}}{E_{n}-E_{m}}<\frac{\beta}{2}(e^{-\beta E_{m}}+e^{-\beta E_{n}}),

(S94)

which follows from
\tanh(x)\leq x
. Therefore, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\langle\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}+\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\rangle\geq\frac{2T|\langle[i\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger},\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}]\rangle|^{2}}{\langle[[\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}},\hat{H}(h)],\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]\rangle}.

(S95)

V.2
T=0

To obtain the zero-temperature version of the Bogoliubov inequality, we define the following operator inner product:

\displaystyle(\hat{A},\hat{B}):=\Big\langle\hat{A}\frac{\hat{P}}{\hat{H}-E_{\textrm{GS}}}\hat{B}^{\dagger}+\hat{B}^{\dagger}\frac{\hat{P}}{\hat{H}-E_{\textrm{GS}}}\hat{A}\Big\rangle,

(S96)

where the expectation value is taken in the groundstate
|\Phi_{\textrm{GS}}\rangle
of
\hat{H}
,
E_{\textrm{GS}}
is the groundstate energy,
\hat{P}
is the projection onto excited states.
By plugging in
\hat{A}=\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}
and
\hat{B}=[\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}},\hat{H}(h)]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\langle\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\frac{\hat{P}}{\hat{H}(h)-E_{\mathrm{GS}}}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}+\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\frac{\hat{P}}{\hat{H}(h)-E_{\mathrm{GS}}}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\Big\rangle\langle[\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}},[\hat{H}(h),\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]]\rangle\geq\big|\big\langle\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\hat{P}\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}-\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{P}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\big\rangle\big|^{2}=\big|\langle[\hat{X}_{\bm{k}},\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]\rangle\big|^{2}.

(S97)

By the definition of
\omega_{\bm{k}}(h)
, we find

\displaystyle\Big\langle\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\frac{\hat{P}}{\hat{H}(h)-E_{\mathrm{GS}}}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}+\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\frac{\hat{P}}{\hat{H}(h)-E_{\mathrm{GS}}}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\Big\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{\bm{k}}(h)}\Big\langle\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\hat{P}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}+\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{P}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\Big\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{\bm{k}}(h)}\Big\langle\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}+\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\Big\rangle.

(S98)

Combining this with the Bogoliubov inequality (
S97
), we obtain the expression we used in the main text:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}+\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\rangle\geq\frac{\omega_{\bm{k}}(h)\big|\langle[i\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger},\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}]\rangle\big|^{2}}{\langle[\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}},[\hat{H}(h),\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]]\rangle}.

(S99)

VI
The Nambu–Goldstone theorem

The Nambu–Goldstone theorem guarantees the presence of a gapless excitation when a continuous symmetry is spontaneously broken, i.e.,
m(0^{+})=\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\lim_{V\to\infty}m(h)\neq 0
. Here we review the proof based on the Bogoliubov inequality, following Ref.
Wagner 1966
;
Stringari 1994
.
To this end, instead of inequality in (
S98
), we use

\displaystyle\Big\langle\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\frac{\hat{P}}{\hat{H}(h)-E_{\mathrm{GS}}}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}+\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\frac{\hat{P}}{\hat{H}(h)-E_{\mathrm{GS}}}\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{\bm{k}}(h)^{2}}\langle\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}(\hat{H}(h)-E_{\mathrm{GS}})\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}+\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(\hat{H}(h)-E_{\mathrm{GS}})\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{\bm{k}}(h)^{2}}\langle[\hat{X}_{\bm{k}},[\hat{H}(h),\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]]\rangle.

(S100)

Combining this with the Bogoliubov inequality (
S97
), we find

\displaystyle\omega_{\bm{k}}(h)^{2}\leq\frac{\langle[\hat{X}_{\bm{k}},[\hat{H}(h),\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]]\rangle\langle[\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger},[\hat{H}(h),\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}]]\rangle}{\big|\langle[i\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger},\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}]\rangle\big|^{2}}.

For
|\bm{k}|\ll 1
, one can take

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{1}{V}\langle[i\hat{Q}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}},\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}]\rangle=m(0^{+}),

(S101)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{1}{V}\langle[\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger},[\hat{H}(h),\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}}]]\rangle\sim C|\bm{k}|^{2n_{0}},

(S102)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{1}{V}\langle[\hat{X}_{\bm{k}},[\hat{H}(h),\hat{X}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]]\rangle\sim C^{\prime}.

(S103)

Then, we can show that

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\lim_{V\to\infty}\omega_{\bm{k}}(h)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{C^{\prime}C}{|m(0^{+})|^{2}}}|\bm{k}|^{n_{0}}.

(S104)

VII
Theorems on frustration-free Hamiltonians

Here, following Refs.
Knabe 1988
;
Gosset and Mozgunov 2016
, we review the derivation of the bound for excitation energies in frustration-free translation-invariant Hamiltonians.

Suppose that
\hat{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}
is frustration-free. We assume the invariance under translation operator
\hat{T}
, i.e.,
\hat{T}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{T}^{\dagger}=\hat{H}_{i+1}
for all
i
. Without loss of the generality, we can assume that (i)
\hat{H}_{i}
acts nontrivially only on the site
i
and
i+1
and (ii)
\hat{H}_{i}
is a projection operator (
\hat{H}_{i}^{2}=\hat{H}_{i}
) onto excited states.
If the first assumption is not satisfied, we can combine several sites together (i.e., coarse-graining) until this assumption holds.
Furthermore, if the second assumption is not automatically fulfilled, we modify eigenvalues of
\hat{H}_{i}
, without changing eigenvectors, in such a way that the smallest eigenvalue is
0
and other eigenvalues are
1
. This process keeps all essential features such as whether the system is gapless or gapped, as well as the scaling of the gapless excitation. Without loss of generality, after shifting
H_{i}
by a proper constant,
\hat{H}_{i}=\sum_{n}a_{n}\mathcal{P}_{i,n}
, where
a_{n}>0
and
\mathcal{P}_{i,n}
is a projector onto an
n
-th eigenvector. The groundstate of
\hat{H}=\sum_{i}\hat{H}_{i}
has a zero energy. Among non-zero eigenvalues of
\hat{H}_{i}
, denote
a_{\textrm{min/max}}
as the smallest/largest eigenvalue of
\hat{H}_{i}
. Now, define
\hat{Q}_{i}:=\sum_{n}\mathcal{P}_{i,n}
and
\hat{H}^{Q}:=\sum_{i}\hat{Q}_{i}
. Then, due to the positive semidefiniteness of projector operators, it is straightforward that

\displaystyle a_{\textrm{min}}\gamma_{Q}(L)\leq\gamma(L)\leq a_{\textrm{max}}\gamma_{Q}(L)

(S105)

where
\gamma(L)
and
\gamma_{Q}(\L)
are gaps for
\hat{H}
and
\hat{H}^{Q}
respectively for system size
L
. Therefore, by studying
\hat{H}^{Q}
, one can rigorously study the scaling form of the excitation gap of
\hat{H}
.

VII.1
Knabe’s theorem

We set the groundstate energy to be
0
. Let us take a length
\ell
(
3\leq\ell<L/2
) and define subsystem Hamiltonian by

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}\,{\coloneqq}\,\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}.

(S106)

We write the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of
\hat{H}
and
\hat{H}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}
as
\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}}
and
\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}
, respectively.

Knabe showed
Knabe 1988

\displaystyle\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}}\geq\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}\left(\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}-\frac{1}{\ell-1}\right).

(S107)

This relation can be shown by the property
\hat{H}_{i}^{2}=\hat{H}_{i}
of the projector.

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{\ell-2}\hat{H}-\frac{1}{\ell-2}\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}(\hat{H}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{j}+\frac{1}{\ell-2}\hat{H}-\frac{1}{\ell-2}\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{i,j=0}^{\ell-2}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+j}

\displaystyle=\left(\hat{H}+2\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{i+1}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq L,\,2\leq|i-j|}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{\ell-2}\hat{H}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{\ell-2}\left(\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}+2\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-3}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i+1}+\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq\ell-2,\,2\leq|i-j|}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq L,\,2\leq|i-j|}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{j}-\frac{1}{\ell-2}\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq\ell-2,\,2\leq|i-j|}\hat{H}_{x_{0}}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+(j-i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq L,\,\ell-1\leq|i-j|}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{j}+2\sum_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{d=2}^{\ell-2}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{i+d}-\frac{2}{\ell-2}\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{d=2}^{\ell-2}\left(\sum_{i,j=0}^{\ell-2}\delta_{d,j-i}\right)\hat{H}_{x_{0}}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq L,\,\ell-1\leq|i-j|}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{j}+2\sum_{d=2}^{\ell-2}\frac{d-1}{\ell-2}\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{i+d}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

(S108)

In the second last line, we used
\sum_{i,j=0}^{\ell-2}\delta_{d,j-i}=\ell-1-d
.
The last inequality follows because the products of commuting projectors are positive semidefinite.

Also, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}(\hat{H}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}})^{2}\geq\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}=(\ell-1)\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}\hat{H}.

(S109)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{\ell-2}\hat{H}\geq\frac{1}{\ell-2}\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}(\hat{H}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}})^{2}\geq\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}\hat{H}

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{2}\geq\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}\left(\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}-\frac{1}{\ell-1}\right)\hat{H}.

(S110)

This gives the inequality (
S107
).

VII.2
Gosset–Mozgunov’s improvement

Gosset–Mozgunov improved the bound in (
S107
) by modifying the OBC Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}c_{i}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}

(S111)

with parameters
c_{i}=(\ell-1)+(\ell-2)i-i^{2}=\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}-(i+1-\frac{\ell}{2})^{2}
.
We will use

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-3}c_{i}c_{i+1}=\frac{\ell(\ell^{2}-1)(\ell^{2}-4)}{30},\quad\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}c_{i}^{2}=\frac{\ell(\ell^{4}-1)}{30},\quad\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}c_{i}=\frac{\ell(\ell^{2}-1)}{6}.

(S112)

This time we find

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{2}+\beta\hat{H}-\alpha\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}(\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{j}+\beta\hat{H}-\alpha\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{i,j=0}^{\ell-2}c_{i}c_{j}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+j}

\displaystyle=\left(\hat{H}+2\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{i+1}+\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq L,\,2\leq|i-j|}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{j}\right)+\beta\hat{H}

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha\left(\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}c_{i}^{2}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}+2\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-3}c_{i}c_{i+1}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i+1}+\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq\ell-2,\,2\leq|i-j|}c_{i}c_{j}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+j}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\beta-\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}c_{i}^{2}\right)\hat{H}+2\left(1-\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-3}c_{i}c_{i+1}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{j}\hat{H}_{j+1}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq L,\,2\leq|i-j|}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{j}-\alpha\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq\ell-2,\,2\leq|i-j|}c_{i}c_{j}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq L,\,\ell-1\leq|i-j|}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{j}+2\sum_{d=2}^{\ell-2}\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-3}c_{i}c_{i+1}-\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2-d}c_{i}c_{i+d}}{\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-3}c_{i}c_{i+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{j}\hat{H}_{j+d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq L,\,\ell-1\leq|i-j|}\hat{H}_{i}\hat{H}_{j}+2\sum_{d=2}^{\ell-2}\frac{(d^{2}-1)(5\ell^{3}-5d\ell^{2}-5\ell+d^{3}+d)}{\ell(\ell^{2}-1)(\ell^{2}-4)}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\hat{H}_{j}\hat{H}_{j+d}

\displaystyle\geq 0

(S113)

In the derivation, we set
\alpha
and
\beta
in such a way that

\displaystyle 1-\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-3}c_{i}c_{i+1}=0,\quad 1+\beta-\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}c_{i}^{2}=0,\quad\text{i.e.},\quad\alpha=\frac{30}{\ell(\ell^{2}-1)(\ell^{2}-4)},\quad\beta=\frac{5}{\ell^{2}-4}.

(S114)

Also, we used the fact that
5\ell^{3}-5d\ell^{2}-5\ell+d^{3}+d>0
for all
2\leq d\leq\ell-2
.

Let us consider the eigenspace of
\hat{H}
with eigenvalue
\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}}
.
As we show in the next subsection, there exists a state
|\phi\rangle
in this space such that

\displaystyle\langle\phi|(\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}})^{2}|\phi\rangle\geq\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{6}\langle\phi|\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle.

(S115)

Hence,

\displaystyle\langle\phi|(\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}})^{2}|\phi\rangle\geq\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{6}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}c_{i}\langle\phi|\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}|\phi\rangle,

(S116)

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\langle\phi|(\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}})^{2}|\phi\rangle\geq\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}}\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{6}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}\hat{c}_{i}=\frac{\ell^{2}(\ell+1)^{2}(\ell-1)}{36}\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}}.

(S117)

Combining these results, we find

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}})^{2}+\beta\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}}\geq\alpha\sum_{x_{0}=1}^{L}\langle\phi|(\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}})^{2}|\phi\rangle\geq\frac{5\ell(\ell+1)}{6(\ell^{2}-4)}\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}}.

(S118)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}}\geq\frac{5\ell(\ell+1)}{6(\ell^{2}-4)}\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}-\frac{5}{\ell^{2}-4}=\frac{5\ell(\ell+1)}{6(\ell^{2}-4)}\left(\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}-\frac{6}{\ell(\ell+1)}\right).

(S119)

VII.3
Proof of Eq.(
S115
)

We choose
|\phi\rangle
to be a simultaneous eigenstate of the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
and the translation operator
\hat{T}
with eigenvalues
\epsilon_{L}^{\text{PBC}}
and
e^{i\theta}
, respectively.

Let
{\cal W}_{0}
denote the groundstate subspace of
\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}
and let
\hat{P}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}
denote the projector onto
{\cal W}_{0}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}=\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}\hat{P}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}=0

(S120)

for
0\leq i\leq\ell-2
. It follows that

\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}\coloneqq 1-\hat{P}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}
,
the projector onto the orthogonal complement of
{\cal W}_{0}
, satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}=\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}=\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}

(S121)

for
0\leq i\leq\ell-2
.

If
\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle=0
then Eq.(
S115
) trivially holds. So we assume
\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle\neq 0
. Then we define

\displaystyle|\phi^{\prime}\rangle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\|\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle\|}\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle.

(S122)

By these definitions, we have

\displaystyle\langle\phi|(\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}})^{2}|\phi\rangle=\langle\phi^{\prime}|(\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}})^{2}|\phi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\phi|\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle\geq\langle\phi^{\prime}|\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi^{\prime}\rangle^{2}\langle\phi|\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle=\langle\phi^{\prime}|\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\phi|\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle.

(S123)

Also, by the translation invariance of
|\phi\rangle
,

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{\prime}|\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}|\phi^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{\langle\phi|\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle}{\langle\phi|\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle}=\frac{\langle\phi|\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}|\phi\rangle}{\langle\phi|\hat{Q}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi\rangle}

(S124)

is independent of
0\leq i\leq\ell-2
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{\prime}|\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}c_{i}\langle\phi^{\prime}|\hat{H}_{x_{0}+i}|\phi^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{1}{\ell-1}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}c_{i}\langle\phi^{\prime}|\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\ell,x_{0}}^{\text{OBC}}|\phi^{\prime}\rangle\geq\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{6}\epsilon_{\ell}^{\text{OBC}}.

(S125)

Combining (
S123
) and (
S125
), we obtain Eq.(
S115
).

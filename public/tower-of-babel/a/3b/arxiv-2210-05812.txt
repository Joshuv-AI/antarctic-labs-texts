Cramér–Rao Lower Bound Optimization for Hidden Moving Target Sensing via Multi-IRS-Aided Radar
Zahra Esmaeilbeig, Kumar Vijay Mishra, Arian Eamaz, Mojtaba Soltanalian

Abstract
Intelligent reflecting surface (IRS) is a rapidly emerging paradigm to enable non-line-of-sight (NLoS) wireless transmission. In this paper, we focus on IRS-aided radar estimation performance of a moving hidden or NLoS target. Unlike prior works that employ a single IRS, we investigate this problem using multiple IRS platforms and assess the estimation performance by deriving the associated Cramér-Rao lower bound (CRLB). We then design Doppler-aware IRS phase shifts by minimizing the scalar A-optimality measure of the joint parameter CRLB matrix. The resulting optimization problem is non-convex, and is thus tackled via an alternating optimization framework. Numerical results demonstrate that the deployment of multiple IRS platforms with our proposed optimized phase shifts leads to a higher estimation accuracy compared to non-IRS and single-IRS alternatives.

IV
Doppler-Aware Phase Shift Design

Since we are estimating a vector of parameters, the CRLB is a matrix. In such a situation, to obtain a scalar objective function,
[
31
]
introduced several CRLB-based scalar metrics for optimization. In this paper, we choose the objective function as
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{C}_{{}_{\textrm{CRLB}}}\right)
based on an average sense or the A-optimality criterion.
The A-optimality criterion has a low computational complexity for optimization
[
31
]
and directly targets the minimization of the parameters of interest. From
[
32
, Proposition 1]
,
\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}^{-1}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{-1}\right)
is both a lower bound and an approximation of
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{C}_{{}_{\textrm{CRLB}}}\right)
. In particular, we consider the following CRLB approximation-based optimization problem

\displaystyle\underset{\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{k},\;k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}{\textrm{minimize}}\hskip 9.24994pt

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}^{-1}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{-1}\right)

subject to

\displaystyle\left|\left[\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{k}\right]_{ii}\right|=1,\;\hskip 9.24994pt\left[\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{k}\right]_{ij}=0,i\neq j.

(20)

IV

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{C}_{{}_{\textrm{CRLB}}}\right)

[

\displaystyle h_{k}=\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v}_{k},\;k\in\left\{1,\ldots,K\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\operatorname{Re}\left(\left[1\;\mathrm{j}\right]^{\mathrm{H}}\otimes\left[1\;\mathrm{j}\right]\otimes\left((\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{H}})^{\top}\odot\mathbf{G}\right)\right),

(22)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\operatorname{Re}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{K}\right)^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{H}}\left((\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{H}})^{\top}\odot\dot{\mathbf{G}}\right)\mathbf{Z}\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{K}\right)\right),

(23)

where
\mathbf{R}=\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N}
. For the unimodularity constraint in (
IV
), we write
\{\mathbf{v}_{k}\}
as
\left\{e^{\textrm{j}\phi_{k,1}},\ldots,e^{\textrm{j}\phi_{k,M}}\right\}
and optimizing (
IV
) with respect to
\left\{\phi_{k,1},\ldots,\phi_{k,M}\right\}
instead of
\mathbf{v}_{k}
. Denote the Lagrangian function and multipliers by
f\left(\mathbf{h}\right)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}^{-1}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{-1}\right)
and
\left\{\eta_{k}\right\}
. The Lagrangian formulation of (
IV
) is

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{h},\phi_{k,m},\\
k\in\{1,\ldots,K\},m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}\end{subarray}}{\textrm{minimize}}f\left(\mathbf{h}\right)+\sum^{K}_{k=1}\eta_{k}\left|h_{k}-\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v}_{k}\right|^{2}.

(24)

The non-convex problem (
24
) may be solved via a generic nonlinear programming solver by employing multiple random initial points
but with increased computational complexity. To avoid the high computational complexity, we resort to a task-specific
alternating optimization
(AO) procedure, wherein we optimize (
24
) over
\mathbf{h}
and
\{\phi_{k,m}\}
cyclically. Algorithm
1
summarizes the steps of this procedure.

Algorithm 1

IRS phase shift design via A-optimality.

1:

Input:
Initialization values
\mathbf{h}^{(0)}
, and
\left\{\phi_{k,m}^{(0)}\right\}
, the total number of iterations
\mathcal{E}
, and
\varepsilon
as a small positive number.

2:

Output:
Optimized phase shifts
\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{*}_{k}
,
k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
.

3:

Set
\mathbf{h}^{(i)}
, and
\left\{\phi_{k,m}^{(i)}\right\}
, the parameters at the
i
th iteration.

4:

Evaluate
\mathbf{F}_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}^{-1}
and
\mathbf{F}_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{-1}
as in (
22
) and (
23
).

5:

\mathbf{v}_{k}\leftarrow\mathrm{diag}\left(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{k}\right)
, where
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{k}=\text{{Diag}}\left(e^{\textrm{j}\phi_{k,1}},\ldots,e^{\textrm{j}\phi_{k,M}}\right)
.

6:

Set the objective function as

g\left(\mathbf{h},\left\{\phi_{k,m}\right\}\right)\leftarrow\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}^{-1}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{-1}\right)+\sum^{K}_{k=1}\eta_{k}\left|h_{k}-\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v}_{k}\right|^{2}

7:

for

i=0:\mathcal{E}-1

do

8:

\mathbf{h}^{(i+1)}\leftarrow\underset{\mathbf{h}}{\textrm{argmin}}\quad g\left(\mathbf{h},\left\{\phi^{(i)}_{k,m}\right\}\right)
.

9:

\left\{\phi^{(i+1)}_{k,m}\right\}\leftarrow\underset{\left\{\phi_{k,m}\right\}}{\textrm{argmin}}\quad g\left(\mathbf{h}^{(i+1)},\left\{\phi_{k,m}\right\}\right)
.

10:

Choose Lagrangian multipliers
\left\{\eta_{k}\right\}
such that
g\left(\mathbf{h}^{(\mathcal{E})},\left\{\phi^{(\mathcal{E})}_{k,m}\right\}\right)\leq\varepsilon
.

11:

return

\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{*}_{k}\leftarrow\text{{Diag}}\left(e^{\textrm{j}\phi^{(\mathcal{E})}_{k,1}},\ldots,e^{\textrm{j}\phi^{(\mathcal{E})}_{k,M}}\right)
.

We used a nonlinear programming solver based on interior-point method
[
33
,
34
]
to solve the subproblems in Steps 6 and 7. Since the objective function in steps 6 and 7 decreases monotonically, the convergence of AO is guaranteed.
The number of constraints in (
IV
) increases with the number of IRS platforms, making it more likely to capture the desired local optima inside the feasible set with the objective value meeting the threshold i.e.
g\left(\mathbf{h}^{(\mathcal{E})},\left\{\phi^{(\mathcal{E})}_{k,m}\right\}\right)\leq\varepsilon
.

VI
Conclusion

Drawing on the recent advances in IRS-aided communications and radar, we investigated a pulse-Doppler multi-IRS-aided radar system with the goal of improving the estimation accuracy of a moving target. We carried out the theoretical estimation accuracy based on Fisher information analysis, aimed at designing the Doppler-aware phase shifts. Our approach improves the estimation accuracy of the Doppler shifts and target complex reflectivity over a single IRS-aided radar.

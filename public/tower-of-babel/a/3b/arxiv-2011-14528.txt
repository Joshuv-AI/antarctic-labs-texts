Abstract
. In the past two decades, many researchers have studied index 2 2 Gauss sums, where the group generated by the characteristic p p of the underling finite field is of index 2 2 in the unit group of ℤ / m ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}} for the order m m of the multiplicative character involved. A complete solution to the problem of evaluating index 2 2 Gauss sums was given by Yang and Xia (2010). In particular, it is known that some nonzero integral powers of the Gauss sums in this case are in quadratic fields. On the other hand, Chowla (1962), McEliece (1974), Evans (1977, 1981) and Aoki (1997, 2004, 2012) studied pure Gauss sums, some nonzero integral powers of which are in the field of rational numbers. In this paper, we study Gauss sums, some integral powers of which are in quadratic fields. This class of Gauss sums is a generalization of index 2 2 Gauss sums and an extension of pure Gauss sums to quadratic fields.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime,
h
be a positive integer, and
q=p^{h}
. The canonical additive character
\psi
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is defined by

\psi\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast},\qquad\psi(x)=\zeta_{p}^{{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q/p}(x)},

where
\zeta_{p}={\rm exp}(\frac{2\pi i}{p})
and
{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q/p}
is the trace from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. For a multiplicative character

\eta_{m}
of order
m
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, we define the
Gauss sum

G_{q}(\eta_{m})=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}\eta_{m}(x)\psi(x),

which belongs to the ring of integers in the field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m},\zeta_{p})
.

The Gauss sum is one of important and fundamental objects in number
theory. The concept of the Gauss sums was introduced by Gauss in 1801
[
12
]
, who evaluated the
quadratic
Gauss sums.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
12
]
)
Let
\eta
be the quadratic character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}={\mathbb{F}}_{p^{h}}
. Then, it holds that

G_{q}(\eta)=(-1)^{h-1}\left(\sqrt{(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}p}\right)^{h}.

After Gauss’ work, many researchers have tried to evaluate Gauss sums for larger
m
.
However, in general, the explicit evaluation of Gauss sums is a very difficult problem.
There are only a few cases where the Gauss sums have been completely evaluated.
For example, the Gauss sums for
m=3,4,5,6,8,12,16,24
have been
evaluated (but not explicit in some cases). See
[
5
]
for more details.

The next important case is the so-called

semi-primitive case
(also referred to as uniform cyclotomy or supersingular), where there
exists an integer
s
such that
p^{s}\equiv-1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{m})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
5
]
)
Suppose that
m>2
and
p
is semi-primitive modulo
m
,
i.e., there exists an
s
such that
p^{s}\equiv-1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{m})
. Choose

s
minimal and write

h=2st
. Let
\eta_{m}
be a multiplicative character of order
m
.
Then,

p^{-h/2}G_{p^{h}}(\eta_{m})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{t-1}&\mbox{if $p=2$;}\\
(-1)^{t-1+(p^{s}+1)t/m}&\mbox{if $p>2$. }\end{array}\right.

The next interesting case is the
index
2
case
, where the subgroup
\langle p\rangle
generated by
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}

has index
2
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
.
In this case, it is known that
m
can have at most two distinct odd
prime divisors. Furthermore, some nonzero integral powers of index
2
Gauss sums are in quadratic fields. Many authors have studied this case, see, e.g.,
[
14
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
22
]
.
In particular, a complete solution to the problem of evaluating Gauss sums in this case was given in
[
22
]
. As a large generalization, Aoki
[
3
]
studied Gauss sums such that
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle p\rangle
is an elementary abelian
2
-group. The
index
4
case
including the case where
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle p\rangle
is cyclic was also studied in
[
10
,
11
,
21
]
.

On the other hand, there were studies on Gauss sums from another point of view. Chowla
[
6
,
7
]
showed that if a Gauss sum defined in a prime field has the form
\epsilon p^{\frac{1}{2}}
with
\epsilon
a root of unity, it is in the quadratic case. McEliece
[
16
]
studied for which
(m,p,h)
, some nonzero integral power of the corresponding Gauss sum is an integer, i.e.,
p^{-h/2}G_{p^{h}}(\eta_{m})
is a root of unity, related to weight distribution of irreducible cyclic codes. Such Gauss sums are called
pure
.
It is clear that the semi-primitive Gauss sums are examples of pure Gauss sums.
Evans
[
8
]
showed that pure Gauss sums for prime powers
m
are in the semi-primitive case.
Furthermore, he
[
9
]
gave the following nontrivial families of pure Gauss sums which are not semi-primitive.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
m=cd
with
\gcd{(c,d)}=\gcd{({\mathrm{ord}}_{c}(p),{\mathrm{ord}}_{d}(p))}=1
and let
f={\mathrm{ord}}_{m}(p)
, where
{\mathrm{ord}}_{n}(x)
is the order of
x
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
. Then,
G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})
is pure if any of the following holds.

(1)

{\mathrm{ord}}_{c}(p)=\phi(c)
and
\ell\in\langle p\rangle\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{d})
for some prime
\ell\,|\,c
.

(2)

-1\not\in\langle p\rangle\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{c})
,
2{\mathrm{ord}}_{c}(p)=\phi(c)
,
\ell\in\langle p\rangle\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{d})
for some prime
\ell\,|\,c
, and all of them hold with
c
and
d
interchanged.

(3)

2||m
,
2+m/2\not\in\langle p\rangle\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{c})
,
2{\mathrm{ord}}_{c}(p)=\phi(c)
,
-1
or
\ell
is in
\langle p\rangle\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{d})
for some prime
\ell\,|\,c
, and all of them hold with
c
and
d
interchanged.

Here,
\phi
is Euler’s totient function.

In particular, Aoki
[
2
, Theorem 7.2]
proved that the converse of the assertion of Theorem
1.3
also holds if
c
and
d
are both odd prime powers.

As a remarkable work, Aoki
[
2
,
4
]
gave the following
necessary and sufficient condition for a Gauss sum to be pure.

Theorem 1.4
.

(
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
)
Let
f={\mathrm{ord}}_{m}(p)
. Then,

G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})
is pure if and only if

\prod_{\ell\,|\,m}(1-\chi(\ell))=0
for any odd Dirichlet character
\chi

modulo
m
such that
\chi(p)=1
, where
\ell
runs over the prime divisors of
m
not
dividing the conductor of
\chi
.

Based on this result, Aoki proved the following theorem on the finiteness of pure Gauss sums.

Theorem 1.5
.

(
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
)
For a fixed
f
, the set of pairs
(m,[p]_{m})
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{m}(p)=f
and
G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})
is pure but not semi-primitive is finite, where
[p]_{m}
is an integer
such that
[p]_{m}\equiv p\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{m})
and
1\leqslant[p]_{m}\leqslant m-1
.

Furthermore, he completely classified such pairs
(m,[p]_{m})
for
f=1,2,3,4

[
1
]
. This result was extended to
f=5,7,9,11,13,17,19,23
in
[
19
]
.

As mentioned above, previous studies on evaluating Gauss sums fall into the following
cases.

(1)

m=2,3,4,5,6,8,12,16,24
.

(2)

\phi(m)/f=2,4
or
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle p\rangle
is an elementary
abelian
2
-group.

(3)

G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})/p^{f/2}
is a root of unity.

In this paper, we consider a new class of Gauss sums, some nonzero integral
powers of which are in quadratic fields.
This class of Gauss sums is a generalization of index
2
Gauss sums and
an extension of pure Gauss sums to quadratic fields.
Thus, this class of Gauss sums unifies them.
In particular, we are interested in classifying triples
(m,p,f)
such that

G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})^{\ell}
is in a quadratic field for some nonzero integer
\ell

but not concerned with explicitly evaluating the corresponding Gauss sums.

The objectives of this paper are three-fold.
First, we give a general necessary condition for some nonzero integral
power of a Gauss sum to be in an extension field of minimal degree
e
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
for a fixed
e\geqslant 1
in terms of Dirichlet characters modulo
m
. See Lemma
3.2
and Proposition
3.5
in Section
3
. The approach is based on the Stickelberger theorem on ideal factorization of
Gauss sums and evaluating the exponent of each prime ideal factor of the Gauss sum by
using
1
st generalized Bernoulli numbers, which was already applied by Aoki for pure Gauss sums. In this paper, we generalize this approach to general
e
. In particular, we show that the necessary condition is also sufficient if
e=2
.
The following is one of our main theorems.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
f={\mathrm{ord}}_{m}(p)
.
Some nonzero integral power of

G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})
is in a quadratic field but any power of which is not in
{\mathbb{Q}}

if and only if there exists a subgroup
E_{0}
of index
2
of
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
containing
\langle p\rangle
such that

(1)

the unique nontrivial annihilator
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}
(as a Dirichlet character modulo
m
) of
E_{0}
is an odd character and
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}(\ell)=-1
for any prime divisor
\ell
of
m
not dividing the conductor of
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}
, and

(2)

\prod_{\ell|m}(1-\chi(\ell))=0
for any odd character
\chi
modulo
m
such that
\chi(p)=1
except for
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}
, where
\ell
runs over all prime divisors of
m
not dividing the conductor of
\chi
.

Second, we prove the
following theorem in Section
4
,
which is the quadratic field version of Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 1.7
.

For each fixed
f
, the set of pairs
(m,[p]_{m})
such that

{\mathrm{ord}}_{m}(p)=f
and some nonzero integral power of

G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})
is in a quadratic field but any power of which is not in
{\mathbb{Q}}
is finite.

To prove Theorem
1.5
,
Aoki
[
2
]
studied for which
m
and
p
, the set of
odd primitive characters modulo
m
being trivial on
\langle p\rangle

is empty in relation to Diophantine equations of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}/d_{i}\equiv 0\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{1})
. On the other hand, we give a short proof for
Theorem
1.7
based on the pigeonhole principle. Furthermore, we give a relatively sharp upper bound for
m
in the case where
f
is odd, and completely classify such pairs
(m,[p]_{m})
for
f=1,3,5,7
.
However, we do not know whether this approach can be applied to general extension degree
e>2
.

Third, we give a complete characterization for the new class of Gauss sums when

m
is a prime power or
m
has exactly two distinct prime divisors with
8\not|m
corresponding to Theorem
1.3
and the converse of its assertion shown by Aoki
[
2
]
. Then, we find infinitely many nontrivial examples of such Gauss sums not belonging to the index
2
case.

At last of this section, we mention again that we will not try to explicitly evaluate Gauss sums in this paper. To evaluate the Gauss sums in our case, one may need to deal with many cases individually as in the index
2
case. Furthermore, we need to determine the minimum
\ell
such that

G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})^{\ell}\in F
for some quadratic field
F
, which seems to be a difficult problem. Hence, this problem will be left as an open problem. (See
Problem
6.4
in the last section.)

2.
Preliminaries

Let
p
be a prime and let
q=p^{h}
for a positive integer
h
. Let
m>1
be a positive integer such that
m\,|\,q-1
and
\eta_{m}
be a multiplicative character of order
m
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Let
\sigma_{a,b}
be the automorphism of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m},\zeta_{p})
determined
by

\sigma_{a,b}(\zeta_{m})=\zeta_{m}^{a},\qquad\sigma_{a,b}(\zeta_{p})=\zeta_{p}^{b}

for
\gcd{(a,m)}=\gcd{(b,p)}=1
.
Below are basic properties of Gauss sums
[
15
]
:

(i)

G_{q}(\eta_{m})\overline{G_{q}(\eta_{m})}=q
if
\eta_{m}
is nontrivial;

(ii)

G_{q}(\eta_{m}^{p})=G_{q}(\eta_{m})
;

(iii)

G_{q}(\eta_{m}^{-1})=\eta_{m}(-1)\overline{G_{q}(\eta_{m})}
;

(iv)

G_{q}(\eta_{m})=-1
if
\eta_{m}
is trivial;

(v)

\sigma_{a,b}(G_{q}(\eta_{m}))=\eta_{m}^{-a}(b)G_{q}(\eta_{m}^{a})
.

We give an important formula, known as

the Davenport-Hasse lifting formula
, on Gauss sums.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
5
, Theorem 11.5.2]
)
Let
\eta_{m}
be a nontrivial multiplicative character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
let
\eta_{m}^{\prime}
be the lifted character of
\eta_{m}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{s}}
, i.e.,
\eta_{m}^{\prime}(\alpha):=\eta_{m}({\mathrm{Norm}}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{s}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\alpha))
for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{s}}
.
Then, it holds that

G_{q^{s}}(\eta_{m}^{\prime})=(-1)^{s-1}(G_{q}(\eta_{m}))^{s}.

Hereafter, fix an integer
m>1
, and let
p
be a prime such that

\gcd{(p,m)}=1
.
We will identify the quotient ring
{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
with the ring of integers modulo
m
. Let
G=({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
be the unit group of
{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
let
f
be the order of
p
in
G
. Let
q=p^{f}
.

Define

K={\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m}),\,M=K(\zeta_{p})={\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m},\zeta_{p}),

and
let
O_{K}
and
O_{M}
denote their respective rings of integers.
For

, define
\sigma_{j}\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(M/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p}))

\sigma_{j}(\zeta_{m})=\zeta_{m}^{j}
. Let

lying over

. Then, there is a prime ideal

of

.
Denote
P_{j}=\sigma_{j}(P)
and

, and

P_{j}O_{M}={\mathfrak{p}}_{j}^{p-1}
. Let
T
be a set of representatives

. Then,

,

Define the character
\eta_{P}
of order

\eta_{P}(\alpha+P)
denote the

\zeta_{m}

\eta_{P}(\alpha+P)\equiv\alpha^{(q-1)/m}\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{P}),

\eta_{P}(\alpha+P)=0

\eta_{P}

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[

For a prime ideal

of

G_{q}(\eta_{P}^{-1})O_{M}={\mathfrak{p}}^{\frac{p-1}{m}\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}[tp^{i}]_{m}\sigma_{t}^{-1}}.

This theorem is known as

On the other hand, the following is also known.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[

)
It holds that

G_{q}(\eta_{m})^{m}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m})
.

Then, by the Stickelberger theorem and Theorem

, we have

(2.1)

G_{q}(\eta_{P}^{-1})^{m}O_{K}=P^{\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}[tp^{i}]_{m}\sigma_{t}^{-1}}.

3.
Necessary and sufficient conditions

In this section, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for some nonzero integral power of a Gauss sum to be in some quadratic field.
To do this, we define

(3.1)

\mu_{p,f,m}:=\min\{[F:{\mathbb{Q}}]\,|\,\mbox{$G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{P}^{-1})^{\ell}\in F$ for some $\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}$ and some field $F/{\mathbb{Q}}$}\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
2
,
8
,
13
]
)

\mu_{p,f,m}=1
if and only if
\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}[tp^{j}]_{m}=fm/2
for any integer
t
prime to
m
.

We give a general necessary condition below.

Lemma 3.2
.

There is a subgroup
E_{0}
of index
\mu_{p,f,m}
of
G
containing
H=\langle p\rangle
such that for each
i=0,1,\ldots,\mu_{p,f,m}-1
,
\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}[tp^{j}]_{m}
is constant for any
t\in E_{i}
. Here,

R=\{s_{i}\,|\,i=0,1,\ldots,e-1\}
is the set of coset representatives of

G/E_{0}
and
E_{i}:=s_{i}E_{0}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,\mu_{p,f,m}-1
.

Proof:
We can take
\ell
so that
m\,|\,\ell
. Then,
G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{P}^{-1})^{\ell}\in K
. This implies that
F
is a subfield of
K
. Furthermore,
since
G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{P}^{-1})^{\ell}
is invariant under
\sigma_{p}\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/{\mathbb{Q}})
,
F
is fixed by
\sigma_{p}
.
Then, by (

), we have

\displaystyle P^{\frac{\ell}{m}\sum_{t\in G/H}\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}[tp^{j}]_{m}\sigma_{t}^{-1}}=

\displaystyle\,(G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{P}^{-1})^{\ell})O_{K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\tau(G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{P}^{-1})^{\ell})O_{K}=P^{\frac{\ell}{m}\sum_{t\in G/H}\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}[tp^{j}]_{m}\tau\sigma_{t}^{-1}}

for any
\tau\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/F)
. Then, by comparing the exponents of

,

, of both sides, there is a subgroup
E_{0}
of index

of

is constant for any

contains

is fixed by

.
∎

Let
\mathsf{D}(m)
be the set of Dirichlet characters modulo

\mathsf{D}(m)
can be viewed as the character group of

B_{1,\chi}:=\frac{1}{c_{\chi}}\sum_{x=0}^{c_{\chi}-1}x\chi(x)
, where

is the conductor of
\chi
. We use the following fact, see, e.g.,
[

Lemma 3.3
.

For any nontrivial
\chi\in\mathsf{D}(m)
, it holds that

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{x\in G}x\chi(x)=B_{1,\chi}\prod_{\ell|m}(1-\chi(\ell)),

where
\ell
runs over all prime divisors of
m
not dividing the conductor of
\chi
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
E_{0}
be a subgroup of index
e
of
G
containing
\langle p\rangle
.
For each
i=0,1,\ldots,e-1
,
if
\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}[tp^{j}]_{m}
is constant, say

, for any

, then
A_{i}=\frac{ef}{\phi(m)}\sum_{t\in E_{i}}t
.

Proof:
The condition
\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}[tp^{j}]_{m}=A_{i}
implies that

|E_{i}|A_{i}=\sum_{t\in E_{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}[tp^{j}]_{m}=f\sum_{t\in E_{i}}t.

This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in E_{i}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}[tp^{j}]_{m}-A_{i}\right)\chi(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}\sum_{t\in E_{i}}[tp^{j}]_{m}\chi(t)-A_{i}\sum_{t\in E_{i}}\chi(t)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}\chi(p^{-j})\right)\left(\sum_{t\in E_{i}}t\chi(t)\right)-A_{i}\sum_{t\in E_{i}}\chi(t).

Note that the indicator function of each

(3.5)

Then, by (

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:tra2}=

\displaystyle\,\left(\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}\chi(p^{-j})\right)\sum_{t\in G}t\chi(t)g_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{m}{e}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}\chi(p^{-j})\right)\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\mathsf{A}(E_{0})}{\chi^{\prime}}^{-1}(s_{i})B_{1,\chi\chi^{\prime}}\prod_{\ell\,|\,m}(1-\chi\chi^{\prime}(\ell)).

If

\eqref{eq:tra2}=0

\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\mathsf{A}(E_{0})}{\chi^{\prime}}^{-1}(s_{i})F_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}=0,\quad\forall i=0,1,\ldots,e-1,\,\forall\chi\in\mathsf{D}(m)\setminus\mathsf{A}(E_{0})\mbox{ s.t. $\chi(p)=1$},

We now assume that (

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\mathsf{A}(E_{0})}{\chi^{\prime}}^{-1}(s_{i})F_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}\chi^{\prime\prime}(s_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\mathsf{A}(E_{0})}F_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\chi^{\prime\prime}{\chi^{\prime}}^{-1}(s_{i})\right)=eF_{\chi\chi^{\prime\prime}}.

In this subsection, we characterize

For two positive integers

,
let

Similarly, we define
A^{+}(d_{1},d_{2})
by replacing

in the definition of

by
a_{1}+a_{2}\equiv 0\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{2})
. Observe that when either one of
d_{i}
,

, is odd,

if and only if

and
d_{2}
are both odd,

.

We can choose
\chi_{i}
so that
\chi_{i}(p)=\zeta_{f}^{f/f_{i}}
.
For
\chi\in\mathsf{D}(m)
, we write
\chi=\chi_{1}^{a_{1}}\chi_{2}^{a_{2}}
for some integers
a_{i}
. Then, we have

\chi(p)=\zeta_{f}^{f(\frac{a_{1}}{f_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{f_{2}})}\mbox{ and }\chi(-1)=(-1)^{a_{1}+a_{2}}.

Hence, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
m=m_{1}m_{2}
with
m_{i}
odd or
m_{1}=2^{u_{1}}
with
u_{1}\in\{1,2\}
.
Assume that
(m,\overline{p},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
. Then,
\mathsf{X}^{-}(m,p)
consists of only characters of the form
\chi_{1}^{i_{1}f_{1}}\chi_{2}^{i_{2}f_{2}}
for some integers

if and only if
A^{-}(f_{1},f_{2})=\emptyset
.

In
[
2
]
, the following is known.

Lemma 5.4
.

(
[
2
, Lemma 7.1]
)
The following hold.

(i)

Assume that

is even. Then,

.

(ii)

Assume that
\gcd{(d_{1},d_{2})}
is odd. Then,
A^{-}(d_{1},d_{2})=\emptyset
if and only if
\gcd{(d_{1},d_{2})}=1
.

By using Lemma
5.4
, we have the following.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
f_{1},f_{2}\equiv 0\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{2})
,
f_{1}/2\equiv 1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{2})
and

f_{2}/2\equiv 0\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{2})
. Let
g_{1}=f_{1}/2
and
g_{2}=f_{2}/2
. Then,

A^{-}(f_{1},f_{2})=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\}
if and only if
\gcd{(g_{1},g_{2})}=1
.

Proof:
Define

\displaystyle S_{1}=

\displaystyle\,\left\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\mid\frac{a_{1}}{g_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{g_{2}}\in 2{\mathbb{Z}},0<a_{i}<f_{i},a_{i}\not=g_{i}\,(i=1,2),a_{1}+a_{2}\equiv 1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{2})\right\},

\displaystyle S_{2}=

\displaystyle\,\left\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\mid\frac{a_{1}}{g_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{g_{2}}\in{\mathbb{Z}},0<a_{i}<f_{i},a_{i}\not=g_{i}\,(i=1,2),a_{1}+a_{2}\equiv 1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{2})\right\},

\displaystyle S_{3}=

\displaystyle\,\left\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\mid\frac{a_{1}}{g_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{g_{2}}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\,0<a_{i}<g_{i}\,(i=1,2)\right\}.

Assume that
A^{-}(f_{1},f_{2})=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\}
. This condition is equivalent to

S_{1}=\emptyset
.
Furthermore, this implies that
S_{2}=\emptyset
.
In fact, if there is
(a_{1},a_{2})\in S_{2}
,
either
(a_{1},a_{2})
,
(a_{1},a_{2}-g_{2})
or
(a_{1},a_{2}+g_{2})
is in

. Then, by restricting the range

of

to

for

, we obtain
A^{-}(g_{1},g_{2})=\emptyset
. Finally,
by Lemma
5.4
, it holds that
\gcd{(g_{1},g_{2})}=1
.

Conversely, assume that
\gcd{(g_{1},g_{2})}=1
. Then, by the Chinese remainder theorem,
S_{3}=\emptyset
.
Furthermore, this implies that
S_{1}=\emptyset
.
In fact, if there is
(a_{1},a_{2})\in S_{1}
,
either
(a_{1},a_{2})
,
(a_{1},a_{2}-g_{2})
,
(a_{1}-g_{1},a_{2})
or
(a_{1}-g_{1},a_{2}-g_{2})
is in
S_{3}
. Hence,
A^{-}(f_{1},f_{2})=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\}
follows.
∎

We now characterize
(m=m_{1}m_{2},\overline{p},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
. We first consider the case where

. In the following theorem, we allow that

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume that
m=m_{1}m_{2}
with

. Then,
(m,\overline{p},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
and

(1)

and
f_{2}=\phi(m_{2})/4
are odd,
\gcd{(f_{1},f_{2})}=1
,
p_{2}
is quadratic modulo
p_{1}
, and
p_{1}
is quartic modulo
p_{2}
.

(2)

f_{1}=\phi(m_{1})/2
is odd,
f_{2}=\phi(m_{2})/2
is even,
\gcd{(f_{1},f_{2})}=1
and
p_{2}
is quadratic modulo
p_{1}

(3)

,
f_{2}=\phi(m_{2})
,
f_{1}/2
is odd,
f_{2}/2
is even and
\gcd{(f_{1}/2,f_{2}/2)}=1
.

Proof:
First, assume that
(m,\overline{p},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
and
\mathsf{X}^{-}(m,p)=\{\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}\}
. Consider the following three cases:
(i) both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are odd; (ii)
f_{1}
is odd and
f_{2}
is even;
(iii) both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are even. Note that

is impossible since

.

Case (i):
If

for some

and
f_{2}=\phi(m_{2})/4
. Furthermore, by Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
,

1.6
.

Case (ii):

, we have

, a contradiction. Hence,

\chi_{1}^{\phi(m_{1})/2}(-1)=-1

5.3
and

\chi_{1}^{f_{1}}(p_{2})=1

. This shows the assertion (2).

f_{i}<\phi(m_{i})/2

or

On the other hand, since

\phi(m_{1})/2
or
\phi(m_{2})/2

\phi(m_{1})/2
is odd and
\phi(m_{2})/2
is even. Hence, we have

. Furthermore, since
\chi_{1}^{\phi(m_{1})/2}(p)=-1
, we have
\chi_{2}^{\phi(m_{2})/2}(p)=-1
, i.e.,

. Since

\mathsf{D}^{-}(m,p)=\{\chi_{1}^{f_{1}}\chi_{2}^{2f_{2}},\chi_{1}^{f_{1}},\chi_{2}^{f_{2}}\}

Remark 5.7
.

The Gauss sums in the case (1) of Theorem
5.6
are out of the framework of previous studies in literature. In fact,
\phi(m)/f=8
and the group
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle p\rangle
is not elementary abelian. Hence,
we have infinitely many examples of Gauss sums, some nonzero integral powers of which are in quadratic fields but not belong to the index
2
case. For example, let
m_{1}=p_{1}\equiv 1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{10})
and
m_{2}=5
. It is clear that

\gcd{(\phi(m_{1})/2,\phi(m_{2})/4)}=1
and
p_{1}
is quartic modulo
p_{2}
. Furthermore, by the quadratic reciprocity law,
p_{2}
is quadratic modulo
p_{1}
. Take a prime
p
such that
p\equiv 1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{5})
and
p\equiv g^{2}\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{p_{1}})
for a primitive element
g
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{1}}
. Then,
(m,\overline{p},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
, which is in the case (1) of Theorem
5.6
.

We next consider the case where
\mathsf{X}^{-}(m,p)=\emptyset
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Assume that
m=2m_{2}=2p_{2}^{u_{2}}
with
p_{2}
odd.
Then,

Assume that

. It is clear that

\mathsf{X}^{-}(m,p)=\emptyset
.
Since
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}=\chi_{2}^{\phi(m_{2})/2}
,
\phi(m_{2})/2
is odd and
f=f_{2}\,|\,\phi(m_{2})/2
. Since
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}(2)=-1
, it follows that
p_{2}\equiv 3\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{8})
by the supplementary law of quadratic reciprocity. Furthermore,
f_{2}
is odd. If
f_{2}<\phi(m_{2})/2
, we have
\chi_{2}^{f_{2}}(2)=1
but
\chi_{2}^{\phi(m_{2})/2}(2)=-1

.

1.6
since

.
∎

,
p_{2}
is nonquadratic modulo
p_{1}
, and

p_{1}
is quadratic modulo
p_{2}
.

(2)

f_{1}=\phi(m_{1})/2
is odd,
f_{2}=\phi(m_{2})
,
\gcd{(f_{1},f_{2})}=1
and
p_{2}
is nonquadratic modulo
p_{1}
.

Proof:
First, assume that
(m,\overline{p},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
and
\mathsf{X}^{-}(m,p)=\emptyset
. Consider the following three cases:
(i) both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are odd; (ii)
f_{1}
is odd and
f_{2}
is even;
(iii) both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are even.

Case (i):
If
f_{1}<\phi(m_{1})/2
, we have
\chi_{1}^{2f_{1}}\chi_{2}^{f_{2}}\in\mathsf{X}^{-}(m,p)
, a contradiction. Hence,
f_{1}=\phi(m_{1})/2
. Similarly,
f_{2}=\phi(m_{2})/2
. Here, we can assume that
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}=\chi_{1}^{f_{1}}
. Then, we have

\chi_{1}^{f_{1}}(p_{2})=-1
and
\chi_{2}^{f_{2}}(p_{1})=1
since
\chi_{i}^{f_{i}}\in\mathsf{D}^{-}(m,p)
.
Furthermore, by Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
, it follows that
\gcd{(f_{1},f_{2})}=1
. This shows the assertion (1).

Case (ii):
If
f_{2}\not=\phi(m_{2})
, we have
\chi_{1}^{f_{1}}\chi_{2}^{f_{2}}\in\mathsf{X}^{-}(m,p)
, a contradiction. Hence,
f_{2}=\phi(m_{2})
. Then, the characters in
\mathsf{D}^{-}(m,p)
are only
\chi_{1}^{if_{1}}
for odd
i
. Hence,
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}=\chi_{1}^{jf_{1}}
for some odd
j
. If
\chi_{1}^{f_{1}}(p_{2})=1
, we have
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}(p_{2})=1
, a contradiction. This implies that
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}=\chi_{1}^{f_{1}}
and
\chi_{1}^{f_{1}}(p_{2})=-1
. Since
\chi_{\mathsf{ann}}
is of order
2
, we have

f_{1}=\phi(m_{1})/2
. Furthermore, by Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
, it follows that
\gcd{(f_{1},f_{2})}=1
. This shows the assertion (2).

Case (iii):
Since
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are even, we have
p^{f_{i}/2}\equiv-1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{m_{i}})
for
i=1,2
. Furthermore, by Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
, it follows that
v_{2}(f_{1})=v_{2}(f_{2})=v_{2}(f)
. Then, we obtain

p^{f/2}\equiv-1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{m})
, i.e.,
(m,\overline{p},f)
is in the semi-primitive case.
Hence, this case is impossible.

Finally, the converses are true due to Theorem
1.6
since
\mathsf{D}^{-}(m,p)=\{\chi_{1}^{f_{1}},\chi_{2}^{f_{2}}\}
and
\mathsf{D}^{-}(m,p)=\{\chi_{1}^{f_{1}}\}
in the cases (1) and (2), respectively.
∎

6.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we gave a general necessary condition for some nonzero integral power of a Gauss sum to be in an extension field of minimal degree
e
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
for a fixed
e\geqslant 1
in terms of Dirichlet characters modulo
m
. In particular, we proved that the set of pairs
(m,\overline{p})
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{m}(p)=f
and some nonzero integral power of
G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})
is in a quadratic field
F/{\mathbb{Q}}
is finite for each
f
.
However, we could not prove this claim for extension fields of degree
e>2
. Hence,
the following problem naturally arises.

Problem 6.1
.

Determine whether the set of pairs
(m,\overline{p})
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{m}(p)=f
and some nonzero integral power of
G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})
is in an extension field of minimal degree
e
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
is finite for fixed
e>2
and
f\geqslant 1
.

We give a list of
(m,{\overline{p}},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
for
m\leqslant 1000
except for those in index
2
case in Tables
1
and
2
. The computation is based on the claim of Lemma
3.2
for
\mu_{p,f,m}=2
and its converse shown in the proof of Theorem
1.6
as follows. For each

m\leqslant 1000
and
1\leqslant\overline{p}\leqslant m-1
such that
\gcd{(\overline{p},m)}=1
, check whether
\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}[x\overline{p}^{i}]_{m}
for representatives
x
of
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle\overline{p}\rangle
take exactly two values, where
f
is the order of
\overline{p}
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
. If the set of
x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}[xp^{i}]_{m}=\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}[p^{i}]_{m}
forms a subgroup of index
2
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
, then return
[m,\overline{p},f,|({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle\overline{p}\rangle|]
except for the case where
\langle\overline{p}\rangle
is of index
2
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
.
As seen from the tables, the index
|({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle p\rangle|
is a power of
2
for all listed examples. Indeed, this is correct when
f
is odd and
v_{2}(m)\not=1
by Proposition
4.2
and Theorem
4.4
or when
m
has at most two distinct prime divisors by the results in Section
5
.
However, it remains unsolved in general. Furthermore, one may be interested in the structure of
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle p\rangle
. Hence, we give the following open problem.

Problem 6.2
.

Determine the structure of the group
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle p\rangle
for
(m,\overline{p},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
.

When
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle p\rangle
is an elementary abelian
2
-group, the corresponding Gauss sums were already evaluated in
[
3
]
.
However,
({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}/\langle p\rangle
for
(m,\overline{p},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
is not necessarily elementary abelian as seen in Remark
5.7
. Thus, the problem treated in this paper is out of the framework of the study in
[
3
]
.

The condition for
(m,p)
such that
\mathsf{X}^{-}(m,p)=\emptyset
was studied in
[
2
,
4
]
in relation to Diophantine equations
of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}/d_{i}\equiv 0\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{1})
. On the other hand, in Proposition
4.3
,
we gave a short proof for that the set of
(m,\overline{p})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}^{\ast}
such that
\mathsf{X}^{-}(m,p)=\emptyset
is finite without using this argument.
One may use the results in
[
2
,
4
]
to derive a good bound
for
m
in the case where
f
is even.
Hence, we give the following problem for future work.

Problem 6.3
.

Give a sharp upper bound for
m
such that
(m,\overline{p})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}^{\ast}
when
f
is even. Furthermore, classify
(m,\overline{p})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}^{\ast}
for small even
f
.

Finally, we give one more important problem.
We studied the triples
(m,\overline{p},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
but did not give an explicit evaluation for the corresponding Gauss sums. To do this, we need to determine the minimum
\ell
such that
G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})^{\ell}\in F
. Hence, we give the following problem.

Problem 6.4
.

For
(m,\overline{p},f)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\ast}
, determine the minimum
\ell
such that
G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})^{\ell}
is in some quadratic field
F
. Furthermore, explicitly evaluate
G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})
and
G_{p^{f}}(\eta_{m})^{\ell}
in this case.

Restricted Variable Chevalley-Warning Theorems
Anurag Bishnoi, Pete L. Clark

Abstract
. We pursue various restricted variable generalizations of the Chevalley-Warning theorem for low degree polynomial systems over a finite field. Our first such result involves variables restricted to Cartesian products of the Vandermonde subsets of 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} defined by Gács-Weiner and Sziklai-Takáts. We then define an invariant ω ¯ ​ ( X ) \underline{\omega}(X) of a nonempty subset of 𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} . Our second result involves X X -restricted variables when the degrees of the polynomials are small compared to ω ¯ ​ ( X ) \underline{\omega}(X) . We end by exploring various classes of subsets for which ω ¯ ​ ( X ) \underline{\omega}(X) can be bounded from below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

Proposition 1.5
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.4

X_{i}
is a coset: that is,
there is a subgroup
G_{i}\subseteq(\mathbb{F}_{q},+)

such that
X_{i}=a_{i}+G_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n

p\mid\#Z_{X}

Proof.

If for all

such that for all

we have
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(x_{i})=c_{i}
, then we have

\sum_{x\in Z_{X}}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(x_{i})}=\sum_{x\in Z_{X}}\frac{1}{c_{1}\cdots c_{n}}=\frac{\#Z_{X}}{c_{1}\cdots c_{n}},

so when we apply Theorem
1.4
the conclusion is
0=\frac{\#Z_{X}}{c_{1}\cdots c_{n}}
and thus
p\mid\#Z_{X}
.

For any finite nonempty subset
X
of a field

, if
\varphi_{X}(t)\coloneqq\prod_{x\in X}(t-x)
, then for all
x\in X
we have

\varphi_{X}^{\prime}(x)=\prod_{y\in X\setminus\{x\}}(x-y).

Taking
X=X_{i}=a_{i}+G_{i}
, for all

we get

\varphi_{X_{i}}^{\prime}(x_{i})=\prod_{y\in(a_{i}+G_{i})\setminus\{x_{i}\}}(x_{i}-y)=\prod_{x\in G_{i}\setminus\{0\}}x\eqqcolon c_{i};

that is to say, the value of

does not depend upon the choice of

Remark 1.8
.

The main observation of this paper is that a Restricted Variable Chevalley Theorem for a subset
X\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
should take into
account the “structure” of
X
via an invariant that measures the vanishing of certain symmetric functions summed over
X
. This observation is also the point of departure of a recent preprint of Nica
[
Ni21
]
. The main difference is that while we are interested
in restricted variable theorems whose conclusion is

, Nica is interested in improvements of the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz and the Coefficient Formula that take the structure of the “grid”
X=\prod_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
into account.

His work and ours were done independently, and despite the similarity of content, have virtually no overlap.

2.1.
Vandermonde Sets and their Polynomials

Let
Y
be a finite subset of
F
of cardinality
r\geq 1
. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we put

\pi_{k}(Y)\coloneqq\sum_{y\in Y}y^{k}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{0\}\neq Y\subset F
be a finite subset of size
r\geq 1
.

a)

If
p\mid r
then
\pi_{k}(Y)\neq 0
for some
1\leq k\leq r-1
.

b)

In general we have
\pi_{k}(Y)\neq 0
for some
1\leq k\leq r
.

Proof.

Write
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}\}
.

a) Consider the
r\times r
Vandermonde matrix
V=V(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r})
associated to
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
, where the
i
-th row of
V
is
(y_{1}^{i-1},\ldots,y_{r}^{i-1})
.
Say
\pi_{k}(Y)=0
for
all
1\leq k\leq r-1
.
Then
V
evaluated at the column vector
(1,\ldots,1)^{T}
is
0
, since the the first entry of the
product is
r=0\in F
as
p\mid r
and rest of the entries are
\pi_{k}(Y)
for
1\leq k\leq r-1
. This contradicts the nonsingularity of
V
.

b) Suppose first that
0\notin Y
, and consider the matrix
\tilde{V}
obtained from
V
by multiplying the
j
th column by
y_{j}
, so

\det\tilde{V}=y_{1}\cdots y_{r}\det(V)\neq 0
. Again we must have that
\tilde{V}(1,\ldots,1)^{T}\neq 0
which means that

\pi_{k}(Y)\neq 0
for some
1\leq k\leq r
. If
0\in Y
, let
Y^{\bullet}\coloneqq Y\setminus\{0\}
. As we have just shown,
there is
1\leq k\leq r-1
such that
\pi_{k}(Y^{\bullet})\neq 0
, so then
\pi_{k}(Y)=\pi_{k}(Y^{\bullet})\neq 0
.
∎

For the rest of this section
Y
will denote a finite subset of
F
of size
r\geq 2
.

We denote by
\omega(Y)
the least
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
\pi_{k}(Y)\neq 0
. Lemma
2.1
gives

\omega(Y)\leq\begin{cases}r&\text{always}\\
r-1&\text{if }p\mid\#Y\end{cases}.

Following Sziklai-Takáts
[
ST08
]
we say that

is
Vandermonde
if
\omega(Y)=r-1
and is
super-Vandermonde
if
\omega(Y)=r
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
Y\subset F
be finite of cardinality
r\geq 2
.

a)

For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\alpha\in F^{\times}
we have
\pi_{k}(Y)=0\iff\pi_{k}(\alpha Y)=0
. Thus

\omega(Y)=\omega(\alpha Y)
and
Y
is Vandermonde
\iff

\alpha Y
is Vandermonde.

b)

Suppose
Y
is Vandermonde. Then for
\beta\in F^{\times}
the translate
Y+\beta
is Vandermonde iff
p\mid r
.

Proof.

a) Indeed
\pi_{k}(\alpha Y)=\alpha^{k}\pi_{k}(Y)
, and the rest is clear.

b) If
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}\}
then for all
1\leq k\leq r-2
, expanding out each term in

\pi_{k}(Y+\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(y_{i}+\beta)^{k}

gives a linear combination of
\pi_{j}(Y)
for
j\leq k
together with the final term
r\beta^{k}
, which is zero iff

p\mid r
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic
p>0
, let
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}\}\subset F
be finite of cardinality
r\geq 2
, and write

\varphi_{Y}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(t-y_{i})=t^{r}+\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}a_{i}t^{i}.

a)

The following are equivalent:

(i)

The subset
Y
is Vandermonde or super-Vandermonde.

(ii)

For
2\leq i\leq r-1
, if
p\nmid i

then
a_{i}=0
.

b)

The following are equivalent:

(i)

The subset
Y
is super-Vandermonde.

(ii)

For
1\leq i\leq r-1
, if
p\nmid i
then
a_{i}=0
.

Proof.

For independent indeterminates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
and
0\leq k\leq r
let
s_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
be the
k
th elementary symmetric function and let
\pi_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{k}
be the
k
th power sum.
Note that
a_{i}=(-1)^{r-i}s_{r-i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})
, for
0\leq i\leq r-1
.
In the ring
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
we have
Newton’s identities
(see e.g.
[
Ze84
]
for a short proof): for all
1\leq k\leq r
we have

ks_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}s_{k-i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\pi_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})

and

\pi_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})=(-1)^{k-1}ks_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{k-1+i}s_{k-i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\pi_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}).

From these we see that for elements
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
in a field of characteristic
p
, for any
1\leq k\leq r
, the vanishing of the power sums

\pi_{i}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r})
for
1\leq i\leq k
is equivalent to the vanishing of the elementary symmetric functions
s_{i}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r})
for
1\leq i\leq k
and
p\nmid i
. The result follows easily.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. For a nonempty subset
Y\subset F
or size
r\geq 2
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

There is
g\in F[t]
and

such that
\varphi_{Y}=g(t^{p})+ct
.

(ii)

The polynomial
\varphi_{Y}^{\prime}
is a nonzero constant (i.e., has degree zero).

(iii)

The polynomial
\varphi_{Y}^{\prime}
is constant on
Y
.

(iv)

The set
Y
is Vandermonde of size a multiple of

.

Proof.

(i)
\iff

Y
is Vandermonde of size divisible by

if and only if
\varphi_{Y}=g(t^{p})+ct
for some

. Since

, by Lemma

the set

is

.

(i)
\implies
(ii): We have
(g(t^{p})+ct)^{\prime}=c
.

(ii)
\implies
(iii): This is immediate.

(iii)
\implies
(i): Let

, and let

be the constant value of
\varphi_{Y}^{\prime}
on

\varphi_{Y}(X)
is separable, for all

\varphi_{Y}^{\prime}(y)\neq 0
, so

Theorem 2.5

let
X_{i}\subset F
be a finite nonempty Vandermonde
subset of cardinality
d_{i}+1
divisible by
p
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
c_{i}=\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\in F^{\times}
. Put
d=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
and
X=\prod_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
. Let
f\in F[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
be
d
-topped – this means that the monomial
t_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{d_{n}}

does not divide any other monomial appearing in
f
with a nonzero coefficient, and this holds when
\deg(f)\leq d
–
and let
c_{d}(f)
be the coefficient of
t_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{d_{n}}
in
f
. Then we have

\left(c_{1}\cdots c_{n}\right)c_{d}(f)=\sum_{x\in X}f(x).

Proof.

This follows from a suitable version of the Coefficient Formula: e.g. from
[
Cl14
, Thm. 3.9]
.
∎

2.2.
More on Cosets

Suppose
Y\subset F
is a coset of a finite subgroup of
(F,+)
. Then
\varphi_{Y}^{\prime}
is constant on
Y
: this was shown in the proof of Proposition
1.5
when
F
is finite, but the argument holds verbatim. Applying Theorem
2.4
, we deduce:

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
Y
be a coset of a finite subgroup of
(F,+)
. Then
Y
is a Vandermonde set.

Corollary
2.6
was shown for additive subgroups in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{a}}
by Gács-Weiner
[
GW03
, Ex. 2.3(i)]
and for cosets of additive subgroups in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by Sziklai-Takáts
[
ST08
, Prop. 1.8(i)]
. Our proof is different from both of theirs, which draw on the theory of
additive polynomials. With this approach one can deduce more about
\varphi_{Y}
when
Y
is a coset, as we now explain.

Suppose
F
contains the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A polynomial
f\in F[t]
is
functionally
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear
if the induced map
E(f):F\rightarrow F
given by
x\mapsto f(x)
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space endomorphism. In characteristic zero, if the induced map
E(f):F\rightarrow F
were
even a group homomorphism then
f
would have to be of the form
f(t)=at
for some
a\in F
; however in positive characteristic
the functionally
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear polynomials are precisely those of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}t^{q^{i}}
for
a_{i}\in F
. We say that a polynomial
f\in F[t]
is
additive
if it is functionally
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear: equivalently,
E(f):(F,+)\rightarrow(F,+)
is a group homomorphism.
A polynomial
f\in F[t]
is
functionally
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-affine
if
f-f(0)
is functionally
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
Y\subset F
be a finite nonempty subset.

a)

The following are equivalent:

(i)

The subset
Y
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
F
.

(ii)

The polynomial
\varphi_{Y}
is functionally
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear.

b)

The following are equivalent:

(i)

The subset
Y
is a coset of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
F
.

(ii)

The polynomial
\varphi_{Y}
is functionally
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-affine.

Proof.

When
F
is finite, these results are special cases of
[
LN97
, Thms. 3.56 and 3.57]
. The proofs given there do not use
the finiteness of
F
.
∎

In particular, if
Y
is a coset of a finite subgroup of
(F,+)
then
\varphi_{Y}-\varphi_{Y}(0)
is an additive polynomial, so there
are
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n},b\in F
such that

\varphi_{Y}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}t^{p^{i}}+b.

Notice that this is stronger than
\varphi_{Y}
just being of the form
g(t^{p})+ct
. If
F\supseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
Y
is a coset of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace, then there are
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n},b\in F
such that

\varphi_{Y}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}t^{q^{i}}+b,

a stronger conclusion still.

2.3.
More on Vandermonde Sets

Theorem
1.6
is a generalization of Proposition
1.5
. How much of an improvement is it? This comes down to asking how many more Vandermonde subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of cardinality divisible by
p
there are than cosets of additive subgroups. In
[
ST08
, Prop. 1.8(ii)]
, Sziklai and Takáts construct a family
of such Vandermonde sets over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with
p=2
that are, in general, not cosets of additive subgroups.
In particular, certain geometrical objects in finite projective planes known as hyperovals (see
[
BCP06
]
and the references therein for the list of known infinite families of hyperovals), give rise to such Vandermonde sets.
There are further examples of Vandermonde sets known for small fields (see for example
[
AH19
, Example 7]
), but a full classification is out of reach (simply because a full classification of hyperovals appears to be out of reach
[
Va19
]
).

We ask the following question related to the enumeration of Vandermonde sets.

Question 2.8
.

For a prime number
p
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, let
V(p,n)
be the number of subsets
X\subset\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
that are Vandermonde of size divisible by
p
, and let
C(p,n)
be the number of subsets
X\subset\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
that are cosets of subgroups of
(\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}},+)
. Straightforward calculation gives

C(p,n)=\sum_{d=0}^{n}p^{(1-d)(n-d)}\frac{\#\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}{\#\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\#\operatorname{GL}_{n-d}(\mathbb{F}_{p})},

where

\#\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(p^{n}-p^{n-i+1}).

Is it true that for each fixed
p
we have
C(p,n)=o(V(p,n))
as
n\rightarrow\infty
?

3.
The Invariant
\underline{\omega}(X)

Again let
F
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. For a function
f:F^{n}\rightarrow F
and a finite subset
X\subset F^{n}
we put

\int_{X}f\coloneqq\sum_{x\in X}f(x)\in F.

A polynomial
P\in F[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
determines a function
E(P):x\in F^{n}\mapsto P(x)\in F
. We put
\int_{X}P\coloneqq\int_{X}E(P)
.

For
\underline{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we put
|\underline{k}|\coloneqq k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}
. For a finite nonempty subset
X\subseteq F^{n}
and
\underline{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, let

\pi_{\underline{k}}(X)\coloneqq\int_{X}t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{k_{n}}=\sum_{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in X}x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}}.

Thus

(6)

\pi_{\underline{0}}(X)=\int_{X}1=\#X\in F.

We put

\underline{\omega}(X)\coloneqq\inf\{|\underline{k}|\ \big|\ \pi_{\underline{k}}(X)\neq 0\}.

Thus for
d\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\underline{\omega}(X)\geq d+1
iff
\int_{X}P=0
for every
P\in F[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
of degree at most
d
.
In particular, by (
6
) we have
\underline{\omega}(X)\geq 1
iff
\pi_{\underline{0}}(X)=0
iff
p\mid\#X
.

Our definition allows
\underline{\omega}(X)
to be infinite, which would occur if and only if
\pi_{\underline{k}}(X)=0
for all
\underline{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. But the following result shows that this is never the case.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X\subset F^{n}
be finite and nonempty. For
1\leq i\leq n
, let

X_{i}\coloneqq\{a\in F\mid\exists\ (x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},a_{,}x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n})\in X\}

be the projection of
X
onto its
i
th coordinate. Then we have

(7)

\underline{\omega}(X)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\#X_{i}-1\right).

Proof.

Choose
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in X
, and put

\delta_{X,x}\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{y_{i}\in X_{i}\setminus\{x_{i}\}}\frac{t_{i}-y_{i}}{x_{i}-y_{i}}.

Then
\deg\delta_{X,x}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\#X_{i}-1\right)
. For
y\in X
we have
\delta_{X,x}(y)=\begin{cases}1&y=x\\
0&y\neq x\end{cases}
, so and
\int_{X}\delta_{X,x}=1
.
∎

To be sure: when
n=1
and
Y\subseteq F
is finite of size
r\geq 1
, we have
\underline{\omega}(Y)=\omega(Y)
if
p\mid\#Y
,
while if
p\nmid\#Y
we have
\underline{\omega}(Y)=0
and
\omega(Y)\geq 1
. (In fact, we did not define
\omega(Y)
for subset of size

1
: extending the definition we gave, we would have
\omega(\{x\})=1
if
x\neq 0
, while
\omega(\{0\})
would be infinite.)

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

Once again, let
\chi\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{r}(1-f_{i}^{q-1})
be Chevalley’s polynomial, so for
x\in X
we have

\chi(x)=\begin{cases}1&x\in Z_{X}\\
0&x\notin Z_{X}\end{cases}.

Moreover
\deg\chi=(q-1)\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}<\underline{\omega}(X)
, so in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
we have

\#Z_{X}=\int_{X}\chi(x)=0\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has characteristic
p
, this yields
p\mid\#Z_{X}
.

3.2.
Optimal Subsets

A finite nonempty subset
X\subseteq F^{n}
is
optimal
if equality holds in (
7
):

\underline{\omega}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\#X_{i}-1\right).

Thus when
n=1
we get that an optimal subset of
F
is precisely a set that either has size
1
or is a Vandermonde set of
size divisible by
p
.

Remark 3.2
.

That
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is optimal is the crux of Ax’s “Quick Proof of the Chevalley-Warning Theorem”
[
Ax64
, §2]
,
[
CGS21
, Thm. 1.1]
.
In some sense the proof of Theorem
1.7
corresponds to
the rest
of Ax’s proof…which is why it is so short.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
X_{i}\subseteq F
be finite nonempty, and put

X\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
.

a)

For all
\underline{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
we have
\pi_{\underline{k}}(X)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{k_{i}}(X_{i})
.

b)

Let
J\coloneqq\{1\leq i\leq n\mid\#X_{i}\neq 1\}
. Then we have

\underline{\omega}(X)=\sum_{j\in J}\omega(X_{j}).

c)

If each
X_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
is Vandermonde of size divisible by
p
, then

\underline{\omega}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\#X_{i}-1\right),

so
X
is optimal.

Proof.

a) We have

\pi_{\underline{k}}(X)=\sum_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}}x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{x_{i}\in X_{i}}x_{i}^{k_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{k_{i}}(X_{i}).

Part b) follows in the case where
\#X_{i}\geq 2
for all
i
, as does part c).
If
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus J
and
\underline{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is such that
\pi_{\underline{k}}(X)\neq 0
, then
\pi_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{j-1},0,k_{j+1},\ldots,k_{n})}(X)\neq 0
. If
\underline{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is such that
\underline{k}_{j}=0
for all
j\notin J
, then let
\hat{\underline{k}}\in\mathbb{N}^{J}
be the corresponding tuple with the indices outside of
J
removed. We have

\pi_{\underline{k}}(X)=\pi_{\hat{\underline{k}}}(\prod_{j\in J}X_{j})=\sum_{j\in J}\omega(X_{j}).\qed

Lemma
3.3
and Theorem
1.7
together imply Theorem
1.6
.

3.3.
The invariant
\underline{\omega}(X)
of a coset
X

Now we look more closely at
\underline{\omega}(X)
for a coset
X
of a finite additive subgroup of
F
. The following result reduces us to studying finite subgroups
G\subset(F^{n},+)
.

Lemma 3.4

(Affine Invariance)
.

Let
\operatorname{AGL}_{n}(F)=F^{n}\rtimes\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F)
be the group of affine transformations of
F^{n}
. For all
\sigma\in\operatorname{AGL}_{n}(F)
and all finite nonempty
X\subset F^{n}
we have
\underline{\omega}(\sigma(X))=\underline{\omega}(X)
.

Proof.

The group
\operatorname{AGL}_{n}(F)
acts on polynomials: if

\sigma\in\operatorname{AGL}_{n}(F):(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\mapsto(a_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{1,j}x_{j},\ldots,a_{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{n,j}x_{j})

and
P\in F[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
then

P_{\sigma}=P(a_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{1,i}t_{i},\ldots,a_{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{n,j}t_{i}).

This action preserves the degree. So if
\deg(P)<\underline{\omega}(X)
then

\int_{\sigma(X)}P=\int_{X}P_{\sigma}=0,

so
\underline{\omega}(\sigma(X))\leq\underline{\omega}(X)
. Applying this with
\sigma^{-1}
and
\sigma(X)
in place of
\sigma
and
X
we deduce that
\underline{\omega}(\sigma(X))=\underline{\omega}(X)
.
∎

Example 3.5
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
be a nontrivial, proper coset, so
\#X=p
. The group
\operatorname{AGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
acts transitively on all nontrivial proper cosets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
, so by Lemma
3.4
all such cosets have the same invariant
\underline{\omega}(X)
; taking

X=\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\{0\}
and applying Lemma
3.3
we find that this common value is
p-1
. However, for the subgroup

\Delta\coloneqq\{(a,a)\mid a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}

we have
\underline{\omega}(\Delta)=(p-1)<2(p-1)=(\#\mathbb{F}_{p}-1)+(\#\mathbb{F}_{p}-1)
. Thus optimality is
not

\operatorname{AGL}_{n}(F)
-invariant. More precisely optimality is translation-invariant and is not generally
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F)
-invariant.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be an additive subgroup.

a)

If
G
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace, then
\underline{\omega}(G)=(\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(G))(q-1)
.

b)

If
q=p
, then
\underline{\omega}(G)=(\log_{p}(\#G))(p-1)
.

Proof.

a) Let
e\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(G)
. Since
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
acts transitively on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of dimension
e
, we have
\underline{\omega}(G)=\underline{\omega}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{e}\times\{0\}^{n-e})=e(q-1)
.

b) Every finite subgroup of a field of characteristic
p
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspace, of dimension
\log_{p}(\#G)
. So part a) applies.
∎

Thus we can compute
\omega(G)
when
n=1
or
F=\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The remaining case seems more interesting.

Example 3.7
.

There are subgroups
A_{1},B_{1},B_{2}
of
(F,+)
with

A_{1}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2},\ B_{1}\cong B_{2}\cong\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}.

We put

A\coloneqq A_{1}\times\{0\}^{n-1},\ B=B_{1}\times B_{2}\times\{0\}^{n-2}.

Then

\underline{\omega}(A)=\underline{\omega}(A_{1})=p^{2}-1>2(p-1)=\underline{\omega}(B_{1}\times B_{2})=\underline{\omega}(B),

even though
A\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2}\cong B
and thus there is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear automorphism
\sigma:F\rightarrow F
such that

B=\sigma(A)
.

In light of Examples
3.5
and
3.7
it is not clear to us what form the determination of
\underline{\omega}(G)

for an arbitrary finite subgroup
G\subset(F,+)
should take. The following is the best possible lower bound that takes only
the isomorphism class of
G
into account:

Proposition 3.8

(Aichinger-Moosbauer)
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic
p>0
, and let
G\subset(F^{n},+)
be a finite subgroup of order
p^{e}
. Then

(8)

\underline{\omega}(G)\geq e(p-1).

Proof.

This follows from
[
AM21
, Lemma 8.2 and Lemma 12.1]
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Combining Proposition
3.8
and Theorem
1.7
, we find that if
X\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is a coset of a subgroup of
order
p^{e}
and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
have positive degree, then if

(q-1)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(f_{j})\right)<d(p-1),

then
p\mid\#Z_{X}
. When
q=p
, this result implies Proposition
1.5
. If
q=p^{a}
with
a>1
, then a better
bound follows from
[
AM21
, Thm. 12.2]
: we have
p\mid\#Z_{X}
if

(9)

a\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(f_{j})\right)<p-1.

The low degree condition (
9
) has a different form than the low degree condition (
2
) of Proposition

1.5
, but using an elementary convexity argument, Aichinger and Moosbauer show that if (
2
) holds then so does (
9
).

3.4.
Graphs of Functions

We end by giving a class of subsets
X
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
for which
\underline{\omega}(X)
can be bounded from below.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1}]
of degree
d\geq 1
, let

X_{f}\coloneqq\{(x,f(x))\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\}

be the graph of the associated function
E(f):\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then:

a)

We have
\omega(X_{f})\geq\frac{(n-1)(q-1)}{d}
.

b)

If
\omega(X_{f})=\frac{(n-1)(q-1)}{d}
then
d\mid(n-1)(q-1)
.

c)

If
n-1\mid d\mid(n-1)(q-1)
and the only monomial of degree
d
in the support of

f
is
t_{1}^{\frac{d}{n-1}}\cdots t_{n-1}^{\frac{d}{n-1}}
, then
\omega(X_{f})=\frac{(n-1)(q-1)}{d}
.

d)

If
n=2
, then
\omega(X_{f})=\frac{q-1}{d}
iff
d\mid q-1
.

Proof.

a) For
\underline{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\pi_{\underline{k}}(X_{f})=

\sum_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n-1}}x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{k_{n-1}}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})^{k_{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n-1}}t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{n-1}^{k_{n-1}}f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1})^{k_{n}}.

Since
\mu(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n-1})=(n-1)(q-1)
, if
\pi_{\underline{k}}(X)\neq 0
then

k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n-1}+dk_{n}=\deg(t^{k_{1}}\cdots t^{k_{n-1}}f^{k_{n}})\geq(n-1)(q-1),

so

|\underline{k}|=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}\geq\frac{k_{1}}{d}+\ldots+\frac{k_{n-1}}{d}+k_{n}\geq\frac{(n-1)(q-1)}{d}.

b) Clearly equality can only hold if
d\mid(n-1)(q-1)
.

c) If the hypotheses hold, then the only monomial of degree
(n-1)(q-1)
in the support of
f^{\frac{(n-1)(q-1)}{d}}
is

t_{1}^{q-1}\cdots t_{n-1}^{q-1}
, so

\pi_{(0,\ldots,0,\frac{(n-1)(q-1)}{d})}X_{f}=\int_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n-1}}f^{\frac{(n-1)(q-1)}{d}}\neq 0.

d) When
n=2
and
d\mid q-1
, the conditions of part c) hold.
∎

When
d=1
, the subset
X_{f}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is an affine
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-hyperplane, so it follows from Proposition
3.6
a) that
\omega(X_{f})=(n-1)(q-1)
, so we get another case in which the bound of Proposition
3.10
a) is sharp.

On the Asymptotics of a Prime Spin Relation, II
Christine McMeekin

Abstract
. For K K a cyclic cubic number field with odd class number containing a unit ω \omega such that Norm K / ℚ ⁡ ( ω ) = Norm K / ℚ ⁡ ( 1 − ω ) = − 1 \operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\omega)=\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(1-\omega)=-1 , we prove that the density of rational primes p p that satisfy the spin relation, spin ⁡ ( 𝔭 , σ ) = spin ⁡ ( 𝔭 , σ − 1 ) for all ​ σ ≠ 1 ∈ Gal ⁡ ( K / ℚ ) , \operatorname{spin}(\mathfrak{p},\sigma)=\operatorname{spin}(\mathfrak{p},\sigma^{-1})\quad\text{for all }\sigma\neq 1\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}), where 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} is a prime of K K above p p is equal to 1 / 2 1/2 . Furthermore, we prove that this density restricted to rational primes that are 1 mod 4 1\bmod 4 is 1 / 4 1/4 and this density restricted to rational primes that are − 1 mod 4 -1\bmod 4 is 3 / 4 3/4 .

We say a modulus

Let

By Lemma

Now write the minimal polynomial of

Let

. Furthermore, since

of

.

By Lemma

, since

, there exists a totally positive generator

of

.
There exists some

because all signatures are represented by units since

in

.

is a square (element) in

.

If

. By uniqueness of

.
Write

. Squaring both sides yields

, which implies

since

. Thus

. Since

is totally positive,

and

have the same signature. Since

in

.
∎

\iota^{-1}(\omega)\neq 0
. That is,
\iota^{-1}(\omega)=1
. If in addition
\iota^{-1}(1-\omega)=1
then we would have
\iota^{-1}(1)=\iota^{-1}(\omega)+\iota^{-1}(1-\omega)=0
, a contradiction. Therefore

\iota^{-1}(1-\omega)=0
. This implies
2|\operatorname{Norm}(1-\omega)
so
1-\omega
is not a unit.
∎

Proposition 4.4

Proof.

U/U^{2}

\operatorname{Norm}(\omega)=-1

-\sigma^{2}(\omega)\equiv\pm 1\text{ in }U/U^{2}.

Applying
\sigma
, this implies
\omega\equiv\pm 1
in
U/U^{2}

(2)

\{\omega,\sigma(\omega),\sigma^{2}(\omega)\}\quad\text{generates $U/U^{2}$ as a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-vector space.}

Since
\omega\in\mathcal{O}_{K}

1-\omega\in\mathcal{O}_{K}

\operatorname{Norm}(1-\omega)=-1

1-\omega

a_{1}\\
a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\\
1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\

Suppose (for contradiction) that

a_{1}\\
a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\\
1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\

Then in

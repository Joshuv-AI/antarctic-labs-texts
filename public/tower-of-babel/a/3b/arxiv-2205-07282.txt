Random matrix theory and moments of moments of L L -functions
J. C. Andrade, C. G. Best

Abstract
. We give an analytic proof of the asymptotic behaviour of the moments of moments of the characteristic polynomials of random symplectic and orthogonal matrices. We therefore obtain alternate, integral expressions for the leading order coefficients previously found by Assiotis, Bailey and Keating. We also discuss the conjectures of Bailey and Keating for the corresponding moments of moments of L L -functions with symplectic and orthogonal symmetry. Specifically, we show that these conjectures follow from the shifted moments conjecture of Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein and Snaith.

Introduction

Let
G(N)\in\{U(N),Sp(2N),SO(2N)\}
, where
U(N)
is the group of
N\times N
unitary matrices,
Sp(2N)
is the the group of
2N\times 2N
unitary symplectic matrices and
SO(2N)
is the group of
2N\times 2N
orthogonal matrices with determinant
+1
. Also, let

P_{G(N)}(\theta;A)=\det\left(I-Ae^{-i\theta}\right),

(1.1)

be the characteristic polynomial of a matrix
A\in G(N)
on the unit circle. Recently, the
moments of moments
of these characteristic polynomials have been the object of much study. The moments of moments consist of an average over the unit circle first and then an average through the group, hence the name. Specifically, they are defined as

\text{MoM}_{G(N)}(k,\beta):=\int_{G(N)}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|P_{G(N)}(\theta;A)|^{2\beta}d\theta\right)^{k}dA,

(1.2)

where
dA
is the Haar measure on
G(N)
.

One particular motivation for the study of the moments of moments is their link to the maximum value of the characteristic polynomials on the unit circle. For example, in the case of the unitary group
U(N)
, Fyodorov, Hiary and Keating
[
10
]
and Fyodorov and Keating
[
11
]
, using heuristics involving the moments of moments, made conjectures for the maximum value of
|P_{U(N)}(\theta;A)|
for
0\leq\theta<2\pi
. For an in depth discussion of the conjectures of
[
10
,
11
]
and work in their direction, see
[
5
]
.

Concerning the moments of moments, one of the conjectures of
[
11
]
is that as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\text{MoM}_{U(N)}(k,\beta)\sim\begin{cases}\Big(\frac{\mathcal{G}(1+\beta)^{2}}{\mathcal{G}(1+2\beta)\Gamma(1-\beta^{2})}\Big)^{k}\Gamma(1-k\beta^{2})N^{k\beta^{2}}&\text{ if }k<1/\beta^{2},\\
c(k,\beta)N^{k^{2}\beta^{2}-k+1}&\text{ if }k>1/\beta^{2},\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
\mathcal{G}(s)
is the Barnes
\mathcal{G}
-function and
c(k,\beta)
is some unspecified function of
k
and
\beta
. At the transition point
k=1/\beta^{2}
, the moments of moments are conjectured to grow like
N\log N
. The above conjecture was proven in the case that
k,\beta\in\mathbb{N}
by Bailey and Keating
[
6
]
with the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Bailey-Keating
[
6
]
)
.

For
k,\beta\in\mathbb{N}
,

\textup{MoM}_{U(N)}(k,\beta)=c(k,\beta)N^{k^{2}\beta^{2}-k+1}\big(1+O\big(\tfrac{1}{N}\big)\big),

(1.4)

where
c(k,\beta)
can be written explicitly in the form of an integral. Furthermore,
\textup{MoM}_{U(N)}(k,\beta)
is a polynomial in
N

.

The proof of theorem
1.1
uses the fact that for

, one can change the order of integration to obtain

\text{MoM}_{U(N)}(k,\beta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dotsi\int_{0}^{2\pi}I_{k,\beta}(U(N),\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k})\,d\theta_{1}\dots d\theta_{k},

(1.5)

where

I_{k,\beta}(U(N),\underline{\theta})=\int_{U(N)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|P_{U(N)}(\theta_{j};A)|^{2\beta}dA.

(1.6)

The function

is an autocorrelation function of the characteristic polynomials and was computed by Conrey et al.
[
8
]
. Two equivalent expressions for

are given in
[
8
]
; one takes the form of a combinatorial sum and the other is as a multiple contour integral. The first of these was used in
[
6
]
to prove that
\text{MoM}_{U(N)}(k,\beta)
is a polynomial and then a complex analytic argument using the integral representation was used to determine the asymptotic behaviour.

Assiotis and Keating
[
3
]
gave an alternate proof of the asymptotic formula in theorem
1.1
using a combinatorial approach involving constrained Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns. They therefore obtained a different expression for the leading order coefficient

as the volume of a certain region. It is remarked in
[
3
]
that their expression for

appears to be very difficult to obtain from the expression obtained in
[

]
.

The approach used in

was then extended by Assiotis, Bailey and Keating
[
4
]
to the symplectic and special orthogonal groups to determine the asymptotic behaviour of the moments of moments for

Theorem 1.2

(Assiotis et al.
[

Let

\textup{MoM}_{Sp(2N)}(k,\beta)=\mathfrak{c}_{Sp}(k,\beta)N^{k\beta(2k\beta+1)-k}\left(1+O\left(\tfrac{1}{N}\right)\right),

(1.7)

Theorem 1.3

(Assiotis et al.
[

]
)
.

Let

\textup{MoM}_{SO(2N)}(k,\beta)
is a polynomial function in

\textup{MoM}_{SO(2N)}(1,1)=2(N+1)

(1.8)

otherwise,

\textup{MoM}_{SO(2N)}(k,\beta)=\mathfrak{c}_{SO}(k,\beta)N^{k\beta(2k\beta-1)-k}\left(1+O\left(\tfrac{1}{N}\right)\right),

Theorem 1.5

2.
The symplectic group
Sp(2N)

In this section we will prove theorem
1.4
. The argument follows that used in
[
6
]
and makes use of the complex analytic techniques deployed in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
. The eigenvalues of matrices in
Sp(2N)
lie on the unit circle and come in complex conjugate pairs
e^{i\phi_{1}},e^{-i\phi_{1}},e^{i\phi_{2}},e^{-i\phi_{2}},\dots,e^{i\phi_{N}},e^{-i\phi_{N}}
. Hence,

\overline{P_{Sp(2N)}(\theta;A)}=P_{Sp(2N)}(-\theta;A).

(2.1)

For
k,\beta\in\mathbb{N}
, we can change the order of integration by Fubini’s theorem and use (
2.1
) to see that

\text{MoM}_{Sp(2N)}(k,\beta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cdots\int_{0}^{2\pi}I_{k,\beta}(Sp(2N),\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k})\,d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{k},

(2.2)

where

I_{k,\beta}(Sp(2N),\underline{\theta}):=\int_{Sp(2N)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}P_{Sp(2N)}(\theta_{j};A)^{\beta}P_{Sp(2N)}(-\theta_{j};A)^{\beta}dA.

(2.3)

The autocorrelation function
I_{k,\beta}(Sp(2N),\underline{\theta})
was also calculated by Conrey et al.
[
8
]
. In particular, it can written in the form of a multiple contour integral as

\displaystyle I_{k,\beta}(Sp(2N),\underline{\theta})=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k\beta}2^{2k\beta}}{(2\pi i)^{2k\beta}(2k\beta)!}\oint\cdots\oint\prod_{1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta}\left(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{n}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k\beta}^{2})^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}z_{n}}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\prod_{m=1}^{k}(z_{n}-i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}(z_{n}+i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}}e^{N\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}z_{n}}dz_{1}\cdots dz_{2k\beta},

(2.4)

where
\Delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\prod_{i<j}(z_{j}-z_{i})
is the Vandermonde determinant and the contours encircle the poles at
\pm i\theta_{m}
for
1\leq m\leq k
.

Each of the
2k\beta
contours in (
2
) can be deformed into
2k
small circles around each of the poles
\pm i\theta_{m}
with connecting straight lines whose contributions will cancel out. The multiple integral
I_{k,\beta}(Sp(2N),\underline{\theta})
can therefore be written as a sum of
(2k)^{2k\beta}
integrals. For
j\in\{\pm 1,\dots,\pm k\}
, let
C_{j}
denote a small circular contour around
i\theta_{j}
if

\displaystyle J_{k,\beta}(\underline{\theta};\underline{\varepsilon})=

\displaystyle\int_{C_{\varepsilon_{2k\beta}}}\cdots\int_{C_{\varepsilon_{1}}}\prod_{1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta}\left(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{n}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k\beta}^{2})^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}z_{n}}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\prod_{m=1}^{k}(z_{n}-i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}(z_{n}+i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}}e^{N\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}z_{n}}dz_{1}\cdots dz_{2k\beta}.

(2.6)

Many of the summands in (
2.5
) are in fact zero as the following lemma demonstrates.

Lemma 2.1
.

For a choice of contours
\underline{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{2k\beta})
in

)
and for

, let
m_{j}
and

be the number of occurrences of

and

\underline{\varepsilon}
. Then, if

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of lemma 3.2 in
[
6
]
and lemma 4.11 in
[
14
]
but we include it for the sake of completeness. Suppose without loss of generality that
m_{1}+n_{1}=2\beta+1
and that

\underline{\varepsilon}=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{m_{1}},\underbrace{-1,\dots,-1}_{2\beta+1-m_{1}},\underbrace{2,\dots,2}_{\beta-1},\underbrace{-2,\dots,-2}_{\beta},\underbrace{3,\dots,3}_{\beta},\underbrace{-3,\dots,-3}_{\beta},\dots,\underbrace{k,\dots,k}_{\beta},\underbrace{-k,\dots,-k}_{\beta}).

(2.7)

All other cases can be proven in the same way. For simplicity, we assume that
m_{1}=0
. If this is not the case, then to
J_{k,\beta}(\underline{\theta};\underline{\varepsilon})
, we would make the change of variables
z_{j}\mapsto-z_{j}

Making the change of variables

\frac{G(z_{1},\dots,z_{2\beta+1})\Delta\left((z_{1}-i\theta_{1})^{2},\dots,(z_{2k\beta}-i\theta_{1})^{2}\right)dz_{1}\cdots dz_{2k\beta}}{\prod_{n=1}^{2\beta+1}z_{n}^{2\beta}},

(2.8)

where

\displaystyle G(z_{1},\dots,z_{2\beta+1})=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta}\left(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{n}+2i\theta_{1}}\right)^{-1}\Delta\left((z_{1}-i\theta_{1})^{2},\dots,(z_{2k\beta}-i\theta_{1})^{2}\right)\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}(z_{n}-i\theta_{1})e^{N\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}(z_{n}-i\theta_{1})}}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\prod_{m=2}^{k}\left(z_{n}-i(\theta_{1}+\theta_{m})\right)^{2\beta}\left(z_{n}+i(\theta_{m}-\theta_{1})\right)^{2\beta}\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}(z_{n}-2i\theta_{1})^{2\beta}\prod_{n=2\beta+2}^{2k\beta}z_{n}^{2\beta}}

is zero and hence by the residue theorem, so is the integral. We have seen that

\displaystyle\Delta\left((z_{1}-i\theta_{1})^{2},\dots,(z_{2k\beta}-i\theta_{1})^{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\Delta\left((z_{1}^{2}-2i\theta_{1}z_{1}),\dots,(z_{2k\beta}^{2}-2i\theta_{1}z_{2k\beta})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2k\beta}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}(z_{n}^{2}-2i\theta_{1}z_{n})^{\sigma(n)-1}.

(2.10)

For each permutation
\sigma\in S_{2k\beta}

\sigma(n)-1\geq 2\beta
for at least one

. It follows that there are no terms in the expansion of the Vandermonde of the form

. Thus, as
G(z_{1},\dots,z_{2\beta+1})
is analytic around zero, the coefficient of
\prod_{n=1}^{2\beta+1}z_{n}^{-1}
in the integrand of
J_{k,\beta}(\underline{\theta};\underline{\varepsilon})
is zero which completes the proof.

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}(i\mu_{n})\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}(i\mu_{m}+i\mu_{n})^{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}-\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}(i\mu_{m}-i\mu_{n})^{2}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=0\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{N}{v_{n}+v_{m}}\Big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}-\mu_{n}=0\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{N}{v_{n}-v_{m}}\Big)^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\big(\tfrac{v_{n}}{N}\big)^{-2\beta}}{\underset{\mu_{n}-\theta_{m}\neq 0}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\prod_{m=1}^{k}}(i\mu_{n}-i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}\underset{\mu_{n}+\theta_{m}\neq 0}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\prod_{m=1}^{k}}(i\mu_{n}+i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}}\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}dv_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+O\left(\tfrac{1}{N}\right)\right)(-1)^{k\beta}N^{4k\beta^{2}-2k\beta}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\right)^{-1}e^{\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}v_{n}}e^{iN\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mu_{n}\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m<n\\
\mu_{m}^{2}=\mu_{n}^{2}\end{subarray}}(2i\mu_{n})^{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m<n\\
\mu_{m}^{2}\neq\mu_{n}^{2}\end{subarray}}(\mu_{m}^{2}-\mu_{n}^{2})^{2}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=0\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{N}{v_{n}+v_{m}}\Big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}-\mu_{n}=0\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{N}{v_{n}-v_{m}}\Big)^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{1}{\underset{\mu_{n}^{2}=\theta_{m}^{2}}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\prod_{m=1}^{k}}(2i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}\underset{\mu_{n}^{2}\neq\theta_{m}^{2}}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\prod_{m=1}^{k}}(\mu_{n}^{2}-\theta_{m}^{2})^{2\beta}}\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\frac{dv_{n}}{v_{n}^{2\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+O\left(\tfrac{1}{N}\right)\right)\frac{(-1)^{\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}}}{2^{2k\beta}}N^{4k\beta^{2}-2k\beta}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\right)^{-1}e^{\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}v_{n}}e^{iN\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{v_{n}+v_{m}}{N}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}-\mu_{n}=0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{v_{n}-v_{m}}{N}\right)^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\frac{dv_{n}}{v_{n}^{2\beta}}.

(2.18)

The power of
N
coming from the products in the second line of (
2
) is determined by the size of the following sets:

\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:=\{(m,n):1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta,\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=0\},

(2.19)

\mathcal{B}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:=\{(m,n):1\leq m<m\leq 2k\beta,\mu_{m}-\mu_{n}=0\}.

(2.20)

Using the definition of
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{2k\beta}
, we have that

|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|=\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}(2\beta-l_{m}),

(2.21)

and

|\mathcal{B}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|=k\beta(2\beta-1)+\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}(l_{m}-2\beta).

(2.22)

In particular,

|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|+|\mathcal{B}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|=\#\{(m,n):1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta,\mu_{m}^{2}=\mu_{n}^{2}\}=k\beta(2\beta-1),

(2.23)

and the power of
N
in the second line of (
2
) is

-|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|-2|\mathcal{B}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|=|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|-2k\beta(2\beta-1).

(2.24)

Therefore the integrand of
J_{k,\beta}(\underline{\theta};\underline{l})
as
N\to\infty
is

\left(1+O\left(\tfrac{1}{N}\right)\right)\frac{(-1)^{\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}}}{2^{2k\beta}}N^{|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\right)^{-1}e^{iN\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mu_{n}}f(\underline{v};\underline{l})\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}dv_{n},

(2.25)

where

f(\underline{v};\underline{l}):=\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=0\end{subarray}}(v_{n}+v_{m})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}-\mu_{n}=0\end{subarray}}(v_{n}-v_{m})^{2}}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}v_{n}^{2\beta}}e^{\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}v_{n}}

(2.26)

does not depend on
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}
. Hence, as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle I_{k,\beta}(Sp(2N),\underline{\theta})\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}=0}^{2\beta}\frac{(-1)^{k\beta+\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}}c_{\underline{l}}(k,\beta)}{(2\pi i)^{2k\beta}(2k\beta)!}N^{|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|}e^{iN\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{C_{0}}\cdots\int_{C_{0}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\right)^{-1}f(\underline{v};\underline{l})\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}dv_{n},

(2.27)

where
C_{0}
denotes a small circular contour around the origin.

∎

We can now obtain an asymptotic formula for
\textup{MoM}_{Sp(2N)}(k,\beta)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

As
N\to\infty
, we have

\textup{MoM}_{Sp(2N)}(k,\beta)\sim\gamma_{Sp}(k,\beta)N^{k\beta(2k\beta+1)-k},

(2.28)

where
\gamma_{Sp}(k,\beta)
is given in the form of an integral and is defined on the proof, see
(
2.43
)
.

Proof.

By lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\text{MoM}_{Sp(2N)}(k,\beta)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cdots\int_{0}^{2\pi}I_{k,\beta}(Sp(2N),\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k})d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{k}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\sum_{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}=0}^{2\beta}\frac{(-1)^{k\beta+\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}}c_{\underline{l}}(k,\beta)}{(2\pi i)^{2k\beta}(2k\beta)!}N^{|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dotsi\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{iN\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{C_{0}}\dotsi\int_{C_{0}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\Big)^{-1}f(\underline{v};\underline{l})\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}dv_{n}\prod_{m=1}^{k}d\theta_{m}.

(2.29)

Changing the order of integration, we have that

\displaystyle\text{MoM}_{Sp(2N)}(k,\beta)

\displaystyle\qquad\sim\sum_{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}=0}^{2\beta}\frac{(-1)^{k\beta+\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}}c_{\underline{l}}(k,\beta)}{(2\pi)^{k}(2\pi i)^{2k\beta}(2k\beta)!}N^{|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|}\int_{C_{0}}\dotsi\int_{C_{0}}f(\underline{v};\underline{l})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dotsi\int_{0}^{2\pi}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\Big)^{-1}e^{iN\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mu_{n}}\prod_{m=1}^{k}d\theta_{m}\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}dv_{n},

(2.30)

and we now seek to determine the
N
dependence of the inner integrals over
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}
. The first step is to write the integrand explicitly in terms of
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}
using the definition of
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{2k\beta}
. The exponential term is

\exp\left(iN\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mu_{n}\right)=\exp\left(2iN\sum_{m=1}^{k}(l_{m}-\beta)\theta_{m}\right).

(2.31)

For the product of
(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{n}})^{-1}
terms, we define the set

\mathcal{T}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:=\{(m,n):1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta,\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\}=\{(m,n):1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta\}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}},

(2.32)

and the following disjoint subsets of
\mathcal{T}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}
for
1\leq\sigma\leq\tau\leq k
:

\mathcal{U}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}:=\{(m,n)\in\mathcal{T}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=\theta_{\sigma}+\theta_{\tau}\},

(2.33)

\mathcal{U}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}:=\{(m,n)\in\mathcal{T}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=-(\theta_{\sigma}+\theta_{\tau})\},

(2.34)

and

\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}:=\{(m,n)\in\mathcal{T}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=\theta_{\sigma}-\theta_{\tau}\},

(2.35)

\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}:=\{(m,n)\in\mathcal{T}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=-(\theta_{\sigma}-\theta_{\tau})\}.

(2.36)

Note that
\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}=\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}=\emptyset
for
\sigma=\tau
. The product of
(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{n}})^{-1}
terms can then be written as

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad=\prod_{1\leq\sigma\leq\tau\leq k}\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{U}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\theta_{\sigma}+\theta_{\tau})}\Big)^{-1}\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{U}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}+i(\theta_{\sigma}+\theta_{\tau})}\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\theta_{\sigma}-\theta_{\tau})}\Big)^{-1}\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}+i(\theta_{\sigma}-\theta_{\tau})}\Big)^{-1}.

(2.37)

Now, we make the change of variables
t_{m}=N\theta_{m}
. As
N\to\infty
, by the Laurent expansion of
(1-e^{-s})^{-1}
about
s=0
, the above product is then

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\sim\prod_{1\leq\sigma\leq\tau\leq k}\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{U}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}\frac{N}{v_{m}+v_{n}+i(t_{\sigma}+t_{\tau})}\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{U}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}\frac{N}{v_{m}+v_{n}-i(t_{\sigma}+t_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}\frac{N}{v_{m}+v_{n}+i(t_{\sigma}-t_{\tau})}\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}\frac{N}{v_{m}+v_{n}-i(t_{\sigma}-t_{\tau})}.

(2.38)

The power of
N
coming from this product is

|\mathcal{T}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|=k\beta(2k\beta+1)-|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|.

(2.39)

We therefore have that as
N\rightarrow\infty
, the integrals over
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}
are

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dotsi\int_{0}^{2\pi}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\right)^{-1}e^{iN\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mu_{n}}\prod_{m=1}^{k}d\theta_{m}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2N\pi}\dotsi\int_{0}^{2N\pi}\frac{N^{k\beta(2k\beta+1)-k-|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|}e^{2i\sum_{m=1}^{k}(l_{m}-\beta)t_{m}}}{\prod_{1\leq\sigma\leq\tau\leq k}\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{U}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}(v_{m}+v_{n}+i(t_{\sigma}+t_{\tau}))\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{U}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}(v_{m}+v_{n}-i(t_{\sigma}+t_{\tau}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{dt_{1}\cdots dt_{k}}{\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}(v_{m}+v_{n}+i(t_{\sigma}-t_{\tau}))\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}(v_{m}+v_{n}-i(t_{\sigma}-t_{\tau}))}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\,N^{k\beta(2k\beta+1)-k-|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|}\Psi_{k,\beta}(\underline{v};\underline{l}),

(2.40)

where

\displaystyle\Psi_{k,\beta}(\underline{v};\underline{l})

\displaystyle\quad:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2i\sum_{m=1}^{k}(l_{m}-\beta)t_{m}}}{\prod_{1\leq\sigma\leq\tau\leq k}\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{U}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}(v_{m}+v_{n}+i(t_{\sigma}+t_{\tau}))\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{U}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}(v_{m}+v_{n}-i(t_{\sigma}+t_{\tau}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{dt_{1}\cdots dt_{k}}{\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}(v_{m}+v_{n}+i(t_{\sigma}-t_{\tau}))\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}(v_{m}+v_{n}-i(t_{\sigma}-t_{\tau}))}.

(2.41)

Returning now to (
2
) and using (
2
), we have that

\text{MoM}_{Sp(2N)}(k,\beta)\sim\gamma_{Sp}(k,\beta)N^{k\beta(2k\beta+1)-k},

(2.42)

where

\gamma_{Sp}(k,\beta):=\sum_{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}=0}^{2\beta}\frac{(-1)^{k\beta+\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}}c_{\underline{l}}(k,\beta)}{(2\pi)^{k}(2\pi i)^{2k\beta}(2k\beta)!}\int_{C_{0}}\cdots\int_{C_{0}}f(\underline{v};\underline{l})\Psi_{k,\beta}(\underline{v};\underline{l})\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}dv_{n},

(2.43)

and this completes the proof.

∎

1.4
.

2.1.
A symplectic family of
L
-functions

For an example of a family of
L
-functions with symplectic symmetry, Bailey and Keating
[
7
]
considered the family of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions. For
d
a fundamental discriminant, let
\chi_{d}(n)=(\tfrac{d}{n})
be the quadratic character defined by the Kronecker symbol. The associated
L
-function is defined for
\text{Re}(s)>1
by

L(s,\chi_{d})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{s}},

(2.44)

and has an analytic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
. The
L
-function satisfies the functional equation

L(s,\chi_{d})=X_{d}(s)L(1-s,\chi_{d}),

(2.45)

where
X_{d}(s)=|d|^{1/2-s}X(s,a)
with
a=0
if
d>0
and
a=1
if
d<0
, and

X(s,a)=\pi^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+a-s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+a}{2}\right)^{-1}.

(2.46)

The moments of moments of the family of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions are defined as

\text{MoM}_{L_{\chi_{d}}}(k,\beta)=\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{|d|\leq D}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}L(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\theta,\chi_{d})^{2\beta}d\theta\right)^{k},

(2.47)

where the sum is only over fundamental discriminants and
D^{*}
is the number of terms in the sum. The conjecture of
[
7
]
in this case is the following.

Conjecture 2.1

(Bailey-Keating
[
7
]
)
.

For
k,\beta\in\mathbb{N}
, as
D\to\infty
,

\textup{MoM}_{L_{\chi_{d}}}(k,\beta)=\eta(k,\beta)\mathfrak{c}_{Sp}(k,\beta)(\log D)^{k\beta(2k\beta+1)-k}\left(1+O_{k,\beta}(\log^{-1}D)\right),

(2.48)

where
\mathfrak{c}_{Sp}(k,\beta)
corresponds to the leading order coefficient in
(
1.7
)
and
\eta(k,\beta)
contains the arithmetic information.

By adapting the proof of theorem
1.4
, we can relatively easily prove that conjecture
2.1
follows from the shifted moment conjecture of
[
9
]
. Changing the order of integration and summation gives

\text{MoM}_{L_{\chi_{d}}}(k,\beta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dotsi\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{|d|\leq D}\prod_{m=1}^{k}L(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\theta_{m},\chi_{d})^{2\beta}d\theta_{1}\dots d\theta_{k},

(2.49)

and the relevant shifted moment conjecture is the following.

Conjecture 2.2

(Conrey et al.
[
9
]
)
.

Let
k,\beta\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\underline{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Then,

\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{|d|\leq D}\prod_{m=1}^{k}L(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\theta_{m},\chi_{d})^{2\beta}=\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{|d|\leq D}\prod_{m=1}^{k}X_{d}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\theta_{m})^{\beta}Q_{k,\beta}(\log|d|,\underline{\theta})+O(D^{-\delta}),

(2.50)

for some
\delta>0
, where

\displaystyle Q_{k,\beta}(x,\underline{\theta})=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k\beta}2^{2k\beta}}{(2\pi i)^{2k\beta}(2k\beta)!}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\oint\dotsi\oint\frac{G(z_{1},\dots,z_{2k\beta})\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k\beta}^{2})^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}z_{n}}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\prod_{m=1}^{k}(z_{n}-i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}(z_{n}+i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}}e^{\frac{x}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}z_{n}}dz_{1}\dots dz_{2k\beta},

(2.51)

in which the path of integration encloses the poles at
\pm i\theta_{m}
for
1\leq m\leq k
. Also,

G(z_{1},\dots,z_{2k\beta})=A_{k\beta}(z_{1},\dots,z_{2k\beta})\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}X(\tfrac{1}{2}+z_{n},a)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta}\zeta(1+z_{m}+z_{n}),

(2.52)

where
A_{k\beta}
is the Euler product, absolutely convergent for
|\textup{Re}(z_{n})|<1/2
, defined by

\displaystyle A_{k\beta}(z_{1},\dots,z_{2k\beta})=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\prod_{1\leq m\leq n\leq 2k\beta}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{1+z_{m}+z_{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{1/2+z_{n}}}\right)^{-1}+\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{1/2+z_{n}}}\right)^{-1}\right)+\frac{1}{p}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}.

(2.53)

We therefore define

\text{MoM}_{Q_{k,\beta}}(D):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dotsi\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{|d|\leq D}\prod_{m=1}^{k}X_{d}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\theta_{m})^{\beta}Q_{k,\beta}(\log|d|,\underline{\theta})\,d\theta_{1}\dots d\theta_{k},

(2.54)

which should approximate
\text{MoM}_{L_{\chi_{d}}}(k,\beta)
up to a power saving in
D
.

Comparing the integral
Q_{k,\beta}(x,\underline{\theta})
with the integral expression for
I_{k\beta}(Sp(2N),\underline{\theta})
in (
2
), we immediately see the similarity on identifying
N
with
x/2
. In particular, the product of
\zeta(1+z_{m}+z_{n})
terms replaces the product of
(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{n}})^{-1}
terms with both having the same analytic structure with simple poles at
z_{m}+z_{n}=0
. This means that the same analysis we applied to
I_{k\beta}(Sp(2N),\underline{\theta})
can be applied to
Q_{k,\beta}(x,\underline{\theta})
to yield an asymptotic formula for
\text{MoM}_{Q_{k,\beta}}(D)
. The function
G(z_{1},\dots,z_{2k\beta})
also contains arithmetic information in the Euler product
A_{k\beta}
and the
X(s,a)
factors. However, these factors do not present any additional difficulties. Using the fact that
A_{k\beta}
is analytic in a neighbourhood of zero and
X(s,a)
is analytic around
s=1/2
and
X(\tfrac{1}{2},a)=1
, one can show that

\displaystyle\text{MoM}_{Q_{k,\beta}}(D)\sim

\displaystyle A_{k\beta}(0,\dots,0)\gamma_{Sp}(k,\beta)\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sum_{|d|\leq D}\left(\frac{\log|d|}{2}\right)^{k\beta(2k\beta+1)-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{k\beta}(0,\dots,0)\gamma_{Sp}(k,\beta)\left(\frac{\log D}{2}\right)^{k\beta(2k\beta+1)-k}\left(1+O(\log^{-1}D)\right),

(2.55)

where
\gamma_{Sp}(k,\beta)
is as defined in (
2.43
). Thus,
\text{MoM}_{Q_{k,\beta}}(D)
satisfies the asymptotic formula conjectured for
\text{MoM}_{L_{\chi_{d}}}(k,\beta)
in conjecture
2.1
.

3.
The special orthogonal group
SO(2N)

In this section we turn to the orthogonal case and prove theorem
1.5
. From now on we assume that
k,\beta\in\mathbb{N}
with
k,\beta
not both 1. As in the symplectic case, the eigenvalues of matrices in
SO(2N)
lie on the unit circle and come in complex conjugate pairs so

\overline{P_{SO(2N)}(\theta;A)}=P_{SO(2N)}(-\theta;A).

(3.1)

Then, as usual, we change the order of integration to write

\text{MoM}_{SO(2N)}(k,\beta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cdots\int_{0}^{2\pi}I_{k,\beta}(SO(2N),\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k})\,d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{k},

(3.2)

where

I_{k,\beta}(SO(2N),\underline{\theta}):=\int_{SO(2N)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}P_{SO(2N)}(\theta_{j};A)^{\beta}P_{SO(2N)}(-\theta_{j};A)^{\beta}dA.

(3.3)

From
[
8
]
, we have the following contour integral expression for
I_{k,\beta},(SO(2N),\underline{\theta})

\displaystyle I_{k,\beta}(SO(2N),\underline{\theta})=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k\beta}2^{2k\beta}}{(2\pi i)^{2k\beta}(2k\beta)!}\oint\dotsi\oint\prod_{1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta}(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{n}})^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k\beta}^{2})^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}z_{n}}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\prod_{m=1}^{k}(z_{n}-i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}(z_{n}+i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}}e^{N\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}z_{n}}dz_{1}\dots dz_{2k\beta},

(3.4)

where again the contours enclose the poles at
\pm i\theta_{m}
for
1\leq m\leq k
. We note the similarity between the above expression for
I_{k,\beta}(SO(2N),\underline{\theta})
and that for
I_{k,\beta}(Sp(2N),\underline{\theta})
in (
2
). Specifically, the only difference is in the product of
(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{n}})^{-1}
terms; in the symplectic case, the product is over
m\leq n
rather than
m<n
. The proof of theorem
1.5
will therefore mirror that of theorem
1.4
but with this slight difference.

First, by using lemma
2.1
and then following the proof of lemma
2.2
, we get that

\displaystyle I_{k,\beta}(SO(2N),\underline{\theta})\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}=0}^{2\beta}\frac{(-1)^{k\beta+\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}}c_{\underline{l}}(k,\beta)}{(2\pi i)^{2k\beta}(2k\beta)!}N^{|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|}e^{iN\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{C_{0}}\cdots\int_{C_{0}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\right)^{-1}f(\underline{v};\underline{l})\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}dv_{n},

(3.5)

where the
\mu_{n}
, the set
\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}
and the function
f(\underline{v};\underline{l})
are as defined in (
2.16
), (
2.19
) and (
2.26
) respectively. We then proceed as in the proof of lemma
2.3
with the change being that we will replace the set
\mathcal{T}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}
by

\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{T}}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:=\{(m,n):1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta,\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\}=\{(m,n):1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}},

(3.6)

and for
1\leq\sigma\leq\tau\leq k
, define the subsets

\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{U}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}:=\{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{T}}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=\theta_{\sigma}+\theta_{\tau}\}

(3.7)

\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{U}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}:=\{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{T}}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=-(\theta_{\sigma}+\theta_{\tau})\},

(3.8)

and

\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{V}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}:=\{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{T}}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=\theta_{\sigma}-\theta_{\tau}\}

(3.9)

\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{V}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}:=\{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{T}}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}:\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}=-(\theta_{\sigma}-\theta_{\tau})\}.

(3.10)

After making the same change of variables
t_{m}=N\theta_{m}
, the product of
(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{n}})^{-1}
terms will be

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta\\
\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{(v_{m}+v_{n})}{N}-i(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\sim\prod_{1\leq\sigma\leq\tau\leq k}\prod_{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{U}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}\frac{N}{v_{m}+v_{n}+i(t_{\sigma}+t_{\tau})}\prod_{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{U}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}\frac{N}{v_{m}+v_{n}-i(t_{\sigma}+t_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{V}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}\frac{N}{v_{m}+v_{n}+i(t_{\sigma}-t_{\tau})}\prod_{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{V}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}\frac{N}{v_{m}+v_{n}-i(t_{\sigma}-t_{\tau})}.

(3.11)

The power of
N
coming from this product is

|\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{T}}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|=k\beta(2k\beta-1)-|\mathcal{A}_{k,\beta;\underline{l}}|.

(3.12)

Taking into account this difference, we see that in this case, we will obtain

\text{MoM}_{SO(2N)}(k,\beta)\sim\gamma_{SO}(k,\beta)N^{k\beta(2k\beta-1)-k},

(3.13)

where

\gamma_{SO}(k,\beta):=\sum_{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}=0}^{2\beta}\frac{(-1)^{k\beta+\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}}c_{\underline{l}}(k,\beta)}{(2\pi)^{k}(2\pi i)^{2k\beta}(2k\beta)!}\int_{C_{0}}\cdots\int_{C_{0}}f(\underline{v};\underline{l})\Omega_{k,\beta}(\underline{v};\underline{l})\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}dv_{n},

(3.14)

and

\displaystyle\Omega_{k,\beta}(\underline{v};\underline{l})

\displaystyle\quad:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2i\sum_{m=1}^{k}(l_{m}-\beta)t_{m}}}{\prod_{1\leq\sigma\leq\tau\leq k}\prod_{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{U}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}(v_{m}+v_{n}+i(t_{\sigma}+t_{\tau}))\prod_{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{U}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}(v_{m}+v_{n}-i(t_{\sigma}+t_{\tau}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{dt_{1}\cdots dt_{k}}{\prod_{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{V}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{+}}(v_{m}+v_{n}+i(t_{\sigma}-t_{\tau}))\prod_{(m,n)\in\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{V}}_{\sigma,\tau;\underline{l}}^{-}}(v_{m}+v_{n}-i(t_{\sigma}-t_{\tau}))}.

(3.15)

Finally, comparing the asymptotic formula (
3.13
) to the result of theorem
1.3
shows that

\gamma_{SO}(k,\beta)=\mathfrak{c}_{SO}(k,\beta)>0
which completes the proof of theorem
1.5
.

3.1.
An orthogonal family of
L
-functions

An example of a family of
L
-functions with orthogonal symmetry is the family of quadratic twists of an elliptic curve
L
-function. Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with conductor
M
. The
L
-function attached to
E
is defined for
\text{Re}(s)>1
by

L_{E}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s+1/2}}=\prod_{p|M}(1-a_{p}p^{-s-\frac{1}{2}})^{-1}\prod_{p\nmid M}(1-a_{p}p^{-s-\frac{1}{2}}+p^{-2s})^{-1}:=\prod_{p}\mathcal{L}_{p}(p^{-s}),

(3.16)

where the
a_{p}
are related to the number of points on the reduction of
E
mod
p
.
L_{E}(s)
can be analytically continued to
\mathbb{C}
and satisfies the functional equation

L_{E}(s)=w_{E}Y(s)L_{E}(1-s),

(3.17)

where
w_{E}=\pm 1
is the sign of the functional equation and

Y(s)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{M}}{2\pi}\right)^{1-2s}\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}-s\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+s\right)^{-1}.

(3.18)

For
d
a fundamental discriminant with
(d,M)=1
, the twist of
L_{E}(s)
by the quadratic character
\chi_{d}(n)=(\tfrac{d}{n})
is

L_{E}(s,\chi_{d})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{s+1/2}}.

(3.19)

These twisted
L
-functions can also be analytically continued to
\mathbb{C}
and they satisfy the functional equation

L_{E}(s,\chi_{d})=w_{E}\chi_{d}(-M)Y_{d}(s)L_{E}(1-s,\chi_{d}),

(3.20)

where
Y_{d}(s)=|d|^{1-2s}Y(s)
. The set of
L_{E}(s,\chi_{d})
for which the sign
w_{E}\chi_{d}(-M)
of the functional equation equals
+1
forms a family with even orthogonal symmetry and so we use the special orthogonal group
SO(2N)
for comparison.

The moments of moments of this family are defined as

\text{MoM}_{L_{E}}(k,\beta)=\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq D\\
w_{E}\chi_{d}(-M)=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}L_{E}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\theta,\chi_{d})^{2\beta}d\theta\right)^{k},

(3.21)

where the sum is only over fundamental discriminants and
D^{*}
is the number of terms in the sum. The conjecture made in
[
7
]
for this family, based on theorem
1.3
, is

Conjecture 3.1

(Bailey-Keating
[
7
]
)
.

For
k,\beta\in\mathbb{N}
and
k,\beta
not both 1, as
D\rightarrow\infty
,

\textup{MoM}_{L_{E}}(k,\beta)=\xi(k,\beta)\mathfrak{c}_{SO}(k,\beta)(\log D)^{k\beta(2k\beta-1)-k}\big(1+O_{k,\beta}\big(\log^{-1}D\big)\big),

(3.22)

where
\mathfrak{c}_{SO}(k,\beta)
corresponds to the leading order coefficient in
(
1.9
)
and
\xi(k,\beta)
contains the arithmetic information.

Once again, we can change the order of integration and summation to write

\text{MoM}_{L_{E}}(k,\beta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cdots\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq D\\
w_{E}\chi_{d}(-M)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=1}^{k}L_{E}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\theta_{m},\chi_{d})^{2\beta}d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{k},

(3.23)

and we have the following conjecture of
[
9
]
for the shifted moments in the integrand.

Conjecture 3.2

(Conrey et al.
[
9
]
)
.

Let
k,\beta\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\underline{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Then,

\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq D\\
w_{E}\chi_{d}(-M)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=1}^{k}L_{E}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\theta_{m},\chi_{d})^{2\beta}=\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq D\\
w_{E}\chi_{d}(-M)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=1}^{k}Y_{d}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\theta_{m})^{\beta}\Upsilon_{k,\beta}(\log|d|,\underline{\theta})+O(D^{-\delta}),

(3.24)

for some
\delta>0
, where

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{k,\beta}(x,\underline{\theta})=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k\beta}2^{2k\beta}}{(2\pi i)^{2k\beta}(2k\beta)!}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\oint\dotsi\oint\frac{H(z_{1},\dots,z_{2k\beta})\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k\beta}^{2})^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}z_{n}}{\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\prod_{m=1}^{k}(z_{n}-i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}(z_{n}+i\theta_{m})^{2\beta}}e^{x\sum_{n=1}^{2k\beta}z_{n}}dz_{1}\dots dz_{2k\beta},

(3.25)

in which the path of integration encloses the poles at
\pm i\theta_{m}
for
1\leq m\leq k
. Also,

H(z_{1},\dots,z_{2k\beta})=B_{k\beta}(z_{1},\dots,z_{2k\beta})\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}Y(\tfrac{1}{2}+z_{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta}\zeta(1+z_{m}+z_{n}),

(3.26)

where
B_{k\beta}
is the Euler product, absolutely convergent for
|\textup{Re}(z_{n})|<1/2
, defined by

\displaystyle B_{k\beta}(z_{1},\dots,z_{2k\beta})=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\prod_{1\leq m<n\leq 2k\beta}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{1+z_{m}+z_{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mathcal{L}_{p}\left(\frac{1}{p^{1/2+z_{n}}}\right)+\prod_{n=1}^{2k\beta}\mathcal{L}_{p}\left(\frac{-1}{p^{1/2+z_{n}}}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{p}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}.

(3.27)

Naturally, we define

\text{MoM}_{\Upsilon_{k,\beta}}(D):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cdots\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq D\\
w_{E}\chi_{d}(-M)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=1}^{k}Y_{d}(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\theta_{m})^{\beta}\Upsilon_{k,\beta}(\log|d|,\underline{\theta})\,d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{k},

(3.28)

which should approximate
\text{MoM}_{L_{E}}(k,\beta)
up to a power saving in
D
. Similarly to the symplectic case considered earlier, we can clearly see the similarity between the integral expressions for
\Upsilon_{k,\beta}(x,\underline{\theta})
above and
I_{k,\beta}(SO(2N),\underline{\theta})
in (
3
). Hence, by following the proof of theorem
1.5
and taking into account the arithmetic factors just as in the case of the quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions, one can show that

\displaystyle\text{MoM}_{\Upsilon_{k,\beta}}(D)\sim

\displaystyle B_{k\beta}(0,\dots,0)\gamma_{SO}(k,\beta)\frac{1}{D^{*}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq D\\
w_{E}\chi_{d}(-M)=1\end{subarray}}(\log|d|)^{k\beta(2k\beta-1)-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{k\beta}(0,\dots,0)\gamma_{SO}(k,\beta)(\log D)^{k\beta(2k\beta-1)-k}\left(1+O(\log^{-1}D)\right),

(3.29)

where
\gamma_{SO}(k,\beta)
is as defined in (
3.14
). Therefore, conjecture
3.1
also follows from the shifted moment conjecture of
[
9
]
.

On the reducibility of affine models with dependent Lévy factors
Michał Barski

Abstract
The paper is devoted to the study of the short rate equation of the form d ​ R ​ ( t ) = F ⁡ ( R ⁡ ( t ) ) ​ d ​ t + ∑ i = 1 d G i ​ ( R ⁡ ( t − ) ) ​ d ​ Z i ​ ( t ) , R ⁡ ( 0 ) = x ≥ 0 , t > 0 , \displaystyle\textnormal{d}R(t)=F(R(t))\textnormal{d}t+\sum_{i=1}^{d}G_{i}(R(t-))\textnormal{d}Z_{i}(t),\quad R(0)=x\geq 0,\quad t>0, with deterministic functions F , G 1 , … , G d F,G_{1},...,G_{d} and a multivariate Lévy process Z = ( Z 1 , … , Z d ) Z=(Z_{1},...,Z_{d}) with possibly dependent coordinates. The equation is supposed to have a nonnegative solution which generates an affine term structure model. Under some assumptions on spherical decomposition, a precise form of the generator of R R is determined and it is shown that the resulted term structure model is identical to that generated by the equation d ​ R ​ ( t ) = ( a ​ R ​ ( t ) + b ) ​ d ​ t + C ⋅ ( R ⁡ ( t − ) ) 1 / α ​ d ​ Z α ​ ( t ) , R ⁡ ( 0 ) = x , \textnormal{d}R(t)=(aR(t)+b)\textnormal{d}t+C\cdot(R(t-))^{1/\alpha}dZ^{\alpha}(t),\quad R(0)=x, with some constants a , b , C a,b,C and a one dimensional α \alpha -stable Lévy process Z α Z^{\alpha} , where α ∈ ( 1 , 2 ) \alpha\in(1,2) . 1
Introduction

Let us consider a bond market with a family of stochastic processes describing zero coupon bond prices

P(t,T),t\in[0,T],

parametrized by the maturity time
T>0
and the short rate process

R(t),t\geq 0.

The processes are defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
with a filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t}),t\geq 0
.
The bond with maturity
T
pays to its owner at time
T
a nominal value assumed here to be
1
, i.e.
P(T,T)=1
.
The discounted value of
1
paid at time
t>0
equals

D(t)=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}R(s)ds}.

The short rate process
R
is supposed to satisfy, for each
T>0
, the condition

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{-\int_{t}^{T}R(s)ds}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=e^{-A(T-t)-B(T-t)R(t)},\qquad t\in[0,T],

(1.1)

with some deterministic functions
A(\cdot),B(\cdot)
. Interpreting
\mathbb{P}
as a risk neutral measure, one recognizes in the left side of (
1.1
) the price at time
t
of the bond with maturity
T
, that is
P(t,T)
. Thus (
1.1
) means that
the short rate
R

generates
an
affine term structure
.

The concept of modelling bond prices in the affine fashion was introduced by Filipović
[
12
]
and Duffie, Filipović and
Schachermeyer
[
8
]
. It was motivated by the results of Kawazu and Watanabe
[
14
]

on continuous state branching processes with immigration. Further developments on regularity of affine processes
are due to Cuchiero, Filipović and Teichmann
[
5
]
and Cuchiero and Teichmann

[
6
]
. The aforementioned results are settled in the general Markovian setting and the description of affine processes
is given in the form of their generators. A class of particular interest are short rates given by stochastic equations.
We call
a generating equation
an equation with solution which generates an affine model. The precursors of generating equations are two classical equations due to
Cox-Ingersoll-Ross (CIR)
[
10
]

\displaystyle\textnormal{d}R(t)=(aR(t)+b)\textnormal{d}t+c\sqrt{R(t)}\textnormal{d}W(t),

(1.2)

with
a\in\mathbb{R},b\geq 0,c>0

and due to Vasiček
[
15
]

\displaystyle\textnormal{d}R(t)=(aR(t)+b)\textnormal{d}t+c\ \textnormal{d}W(t),

(1.3)

with
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
, both driven by a one dimensional Wiener process
W
. To make the behaviour of the short rate process more realistic and to improve the accuracy of calibration to market data more involved equations are considered in the literature.
Dai and Singleton
[
7
]
consider factorial models perturbed by correlated Wiener processes and examine the influence of the correlation structure on the resulting affine model. It is shown in
[
7
]
, in particular, that different equations may generate identical affine models.
Replacing
W
by a Lévy process it was shown in Barski and Zabczyk

[
3
]
that the generalization of (
1.2
) must be of the form

\displaystyle\textnormal{d}R(t)=(aR(t)+b)\textnormal{d}t+C\cdot(R(t-))^{1/\alpha}dZ^{\alpha}(t),

(1.4)

with
a\in\mathbb{R},b\geq 0,C>0
, where
Z^{\alpha}
is an
\alpha
-stable process with index
\alpha\in(1,2]
. It was also shown in
[
3
]
that the counterpart of (
1.3
) in the Lévy setting allows to preserve the positivity of
R
, which clearly is not preserved in (
1.3
). Models driven by a multivariate Lévy process with independent coordinates appear, among others, in Duffie and Gârleanu
[
9
]
, Barndorff-Nielsen and Shephard
[
1
]
, Jiao, Ma and Scotti
[
13
]
, Barski and Łochowski
[
2
]
. Similarly as in
[
7
]
, it is noticed in
[
2
]
that different equations may generate identical affine models. This fact motivated a classification of all generating equations into several classes which are representable by the so called canonical equations having tractable forms.
The case when the coordinates of the multivariate Lévy process are dependent is an unexplored field entered by this paper.

We consider an equation of the form

\displaystyle\textnormal{d}R(t)=F(R(t))\textnormal{d}t+\sum_{i=1}^{d}G_{i}(R(t-))\textnormal{d}Z_{i}(t),\quad R(0)=x\geq 0,\quad t>0,

(1.5)

where
F,G:=(G_{1},...,G_{d})
are deterministic functions and
Z=(Z_{1},...,Z_{d})
is a multivariate Lévy process. As the coordinates of
Z
may be dependent, the Lévy measure
\nu
of
Z
is not necessarily concentrated on axes and it is assumed to admit
a
spherical decomposition
which means that

\nu(A)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{1}_{A}(r\xi)\gamma_{\xi}(\textnormal{d}r)\ \lambda(\textnormal{d}\xi),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.6)

Here
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\mid x\mid=1\}
is a unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\lambda
is a finite measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
called a
spherical part
of
\nu
and
\{{\gamma_{\xi}};{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\}
is a family of measures on
(0,+\infty)
called
radial measures
of
\nu
. The spherical decomposition may seem a very particular feature of
\nu

at first glance, but let us recall a well known example of an
\alpha
-stable process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with index
\alpha\in(1,2)
. Its jump measure admits a spherical decomposition with identical radial measures given by the density

\displaystyle\gamma_{\xi}(\textnormal{d}r)=\gamma(\textnormal{d}r):=\frac{1}{r^{1+\alpha}}\textnormal{d}r,\quad r>0,\ \xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

(1.7)

and with any finite spherical measure
\lambda
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. In fact, the spherical decomposition can be determined, for instance, when
\nu
has a density with respect to the Lebesgue measure. In this case the radial measures are of the form

\gamma_{\xi}(\textnormal{d}r)=g(r\xi)r^{d-1}\sqrt{1-\xi_{1}^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{1-(\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})}\cdot...\cdot\sqrt{1-(\xi_{1}^{2}+...+\xi_{d-2}^{2})}\ \textnormal{d}r.

for
\xi=(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},r>0
, where
g
stands for the density of
\nu
. The spherical part
\lambda
is then the image of the Lebesgue measure by the polar transformation, see Section
3.1.1
for details. We examine the question which affine models can be generated by equation (
1.5
)
when (
1.6
) holds. In Example
2.3
we show that
if (
1.5
) is a generating equation and
Z
is an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued
\alpha
-stable process then the resulted affine model is identical with the model generated by (
1.4
).
This means that (
1.4
) can replace the initial equation, which may be of a complicated form, preserving the bond prices unchanged. In this case we call the initial equation to have the
reducibility property
. This extends the observations from
[
7
]
and
[
2
]
to the case with dependent noise coordinates. The main result of this paper, Theorem
3.1
, provides conditions for (
1.5
) driven by
Z
with Lévy measure of the form (
1.6
) to have the reducibility property. We prove that if
G
is a continuous function for which the limit

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{G(x)}{\left|G(x)\right|}

exists and the Laplace exponents related to radial measures

J_{\gamma_{\xi}}(b):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(e^{-br}-1+br)\gamma_{\xi}(\textnormal{d}r),\quad b\geq 0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

satisfy the condition

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in\text{supp}(\lambda)}J_{\gamma_{\xi}}(b)\leq K\cdot\inf_{\xi\in\text{supp}(\lambda)}J_{\gamma_{\xi}}(b),\quad b\geq 0,

(1.8)

with some
1\leq K<+\infty
, then any equation with such
G
and
Z
can generate only the same affine model as that one generated by (
1.4
) with some
\alpha\in(1,2)
. In particular, this takes place, for instance, when
Z
is
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued and its jump measure has a density
g
such that the functions

\underline{g}(r):=\inf_{\left|x\right|=r}g(x),\qquad\bar{g}(r):=\sup_{\left|x\right|=r}g(x),\quad r\geq 0,

satisfy the integrability conditions

0<\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min\{r^{2},r^{3}\}\underline{g}(r)\textnormal{d}r\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min\{r^{2},r^{3}\}\bar{g}(r)\textnormal{d}r<+\infty,

and

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}r^{2}\bar{g}(r)\textnormal{d}r}{\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}r^{2}\underline{g}(r)\textnormal{d}r}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{\int_{1}^{1/\varepsilon}r^{3}\bar{g}(r)\textnormal{d}r}{\int_{1}^{1/\varepsilon}r^{3}\underline{g}(r)\textnormal{d}r}<+\infty,

for details and extensions for the cases
d>2
see Section
3.1.1
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present some basic facts on Lévy processes and
the Markovian characterization of generating equations. The main Section
3
on the reducibility problem is divided into three sections. The first contains the formulation of the main results including Theorem
3.1
and illustrative analysis of the case when
\nu
has a density resulting in Theorem
3.7
. The next section consists of a sequence of auxiliary results and in the final one we present the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Basic facts on Lévy processes

Let
Z:=(Z_{1},Z_{2},...,Z_{d})
be a Lévy process in
\mathbb{R}^{d},d\geq 1
on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
with a filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t},t\geq 0\}
. If
Z
is a martingale, then it admits the following unique representation

Z(t)=W(t)+X(t),\quad t\geq 0,

where
W
is a Wiener process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a covariance matrix
Q
and
X
is the so called
jump martingale part
of
Z
. It is independent of
W
and can be described in terms of the
jump measure
of
Z
defined by

\displaystyle\pi(t,A):=\sharp\{s\in[0,t]:\triangle Z(s)\in A\},\quad t\geq 0,

(2.1)

where
\triangle Z(s):=Z(s)-Z(s-)
and
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a set separated from zero, i.e.
0
does not belong to the closure of
A
.
With (
2.1
) at hand one defines the
Lévy measure
of
Z
by

\nu(A):=\mathbb{E}\Big[\pi(1,A)\Big].

Then
X
can be written as

X(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y\ (\pi(\textnormal{d}s,\textnormal{d}y)-\textnormal{d}s\ \nu(\textnormal{d}y)),\quad t\geq 0,

and its properties can be formulated in terms of the measure
\nu
. The integrability of
X
is equivalent
to the condition

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\left|y\right|^{2}\wedge\left|y\right|)\nu(\textnormal{d}y)<+\infty,

(2.2)

while the
variation
of
X
is finite if and only if

\displaystyle\int_{\left|y\right|<1}\left|y\right|\nu(\textnormal{d}y)<+\infty.

(2.3)

In our notation
\left|\cdot\right|
stands for the standard norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the standard scalar product.

By the independence of
X
and
W
we see that, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\langle\lambda,Z(t)\rangle}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\langle\lambda,W(t)\rangle}\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\langle\lambda,X(t)\rangle}\right],

so the Laplace exponent
J_{Z}
of
Z
defined by

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\langle\lambda,Z(t)\rangle}\right]=e^{tJ_{Z}(\lambda)},

exists at
\lambda
if and only if
J_{X}(\lambda)
finite. The latter property
is equivalent to the condition

\displaystyle\int_{\mid y\mid>1}e^{-\langle\lambda,y\rangle}\nu(\textnormal{d}y)<+\infty.

(2.4)

If (
2.4
) holds, then

J_{X}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(e^{-\langle\lambda,y\rangle}-1+\langle\lambda,y\rangle)\nu(\textnormal{d}y),

(2.5)

and, consequently,

\displaystyle J_{Z}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=J_{W}(\lambda)+J_{X}(\lambda)

\displaystyle={{\frac{1}{2}\langle Q\lambda,\lambda\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(e^{-\langle\lambda,y\rangle}-1+\langle\lambda,y\rangle)\nu(\textnormal{d}y)}}.

(2.6)

It follows, in particular, that the process
Z
is uniquely determined by the pair
(Q,\nu)
.

2.2
Markovian characterization of generating equations

It was shown in
[
12
, Theorem 5.3]
that the generator of a general positive Markovian short rate generating an affine model is of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(x)=

\displaystyle cxf^{\prime\prime}(x)+(\beta x+\gamma)f^{\prime}(x)

(2.7)

\displaystyle+\int_{(0,+\infty)}\Big(f(x+v)-f(x)-f^{\prime}(x)(1\wedge v)\Big)(m(\textnormal{d}v)+x\mu(\textnormal{d}v)),\quad x\geq 0,

for
f\in\mathcal{L}(\Lambda)\cup C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, where

\mathcal{L}(\Lambda)
is the linear hull of
\Lambda:=\{f_{\lambda}:=e^{-\lambda x},\lambda\in(0,+\infty)\}

and
C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
stands for the set of twice continuously differentiable functions with compact support in
[0,+\infty)
.
Above
c,\gamma\geq 0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
m(\textnormal{d}v)
,
\mu(\textnormal{d}v)
are nonnegative Borel measures on
(0,+\infty)
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{(0,+\infty)}(1\wedge v)m(\textnormal{d}v)+\int_{(0,+\infty)}(1\wedge v^{2})\mu(\textnormal{d}v)<+\infty.

(2.8)

Moreover, the functions
A(\cdot),B(\cdot)
in (
1.1
) are uniquely determined by the form of the generator
(
2.7
), for details see
[
12
]
.

Application of the characterization above to the case when
R
is given by (
1.5
) leads to necessary and sufficient conditions
making (
1.5
) a generating equation, for the proof see Proposition 2.2 in
[
2
]
. For their formulations we need a family of measures related to the pair
(G,Z)
. For
x\geq 0
we define the measure

\nu_{G(x)}(A):=\nu\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle G(x),y\rangle\in A\},\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}).

which is the image of the Lévy measure
\nu
under the linear transformation
y\mapsto\langle G(x),y\rangle
.
This measure may have an atom at zero and therefore its restriction
\nu_{G(x)}(\textnormal{d}v)\mid_{v\neq 0}
is used below.
The aforementioned conditions are as follows.

•

The drift is affine

\displaystyle F(x)=ax+b,\ \text{where}\ a\in\mathbb{R},\ b\geq\int_{(1,+\infty)}(v-1)\nu_{G(0)}(\textnormal{d}v).

(2.9)

•

The covariance matrix of the Wiener part of
Z
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle QG(x),G(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=cx,\quad x\geq 0,

(2.10)

with some
c\geq 0
.

•

The jumps of
Z
and the function
G
are such that

\displaystyle\langle G(x),\triangle Z(t)\rangle\geq 0,\quad x\geq 0,t\geq 0,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\nu_{G(0)}(\textnormal{d}v)=m(\textnormal{d}v)\ \text{and}\ \int_{(0,+\infty)}v\ \nu_{G(0)}(\textnormal{d}v)<+\infty,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{(0,+\infty)}(v\wedge v^{2})\mu(\textnormal{d}v)<+\infty,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\nu_{G(x)}(\textnormal{d}v)\mid_{(0,+\infty)}

\displaystyle=\nu_{G(0)}(\textnormal{d}v)\mid_{(0,+\infty)}+x\mu(\textnormal{d}v),\quad x\geq 0.

(2.14)

Moreover, (
2.7
) reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(x)=cxf^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle+\Big[ax+b+\int_{(1,+\infty)}(1-v)\{\nu_{G(0)}(\textnormal{d}v)+x\mu(\textnormal{d}v)\}\Big]f^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle+\int_{(0,+\infty)}[f(x+v)-f(x)-f^{\prime}(x)(1\wedge v)]\{\nu_{G(0)}(\textnormal{d}v)+x\mu(\textnormal{d}v)\}.

(2.15)

In particular, with the parameters
a,b,c
and the measures
\nu_{G(0)}(\textnormal{d}v),\mu(\textnormal{d}v)
from (
2.15
) at hand one can determine the zero coupon bond prices, for details see
[
12
]
.

Note stronger integrability requirements (
2.12
), (
2.13
) for the measures
m(\textnormal{d}v),\mu(\textnormal{d}v)
than in (
2.8
). They appear due to the fact that
Z
is a martingale.

Remark 2.1

Conditions (
2.10
)-(
2.14
) describe the law of the family of one-dimensional Lévy processes
Z^{G(x)}(t):=\langle G(x),Z(t)\rangle,x\geq 0
. Conditions (
2.10
) and (
2.14
) can be reformulated
in terms of their Laplace exponents

\displaystyle J_{Z^{G(x)}}(b)=J_{Z}(bG(x))=cb^{2}+J_{\nu_{G(0)}}(b)+xJ_{\mu}(b),\quad b\geq 0,

(2.16)

where
J_{\mu}(b):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(e^{-bv}-1+bv)\mu(\textnormal{d}v)
and
J_{\nu_{G(0)}}
is defined analogously.

Remark 2.2

For the equation (
1.4
) with
\alpha\in(1,2)
one can show that

c=0,\ \nu_{G(0)}=0,\ \mu(\textnormal{d}v)=\mathbf{1}_{\{v>0\}}\frac{1}{v^{1+\alpha}}\textnormal{d}v,

hence
\mu(\textnormal{d}v)
is an
\alpha
-stable measure, for details see
[
2
]
or
[
4
]
.

We start with an example of (
1.5
) where
Z
is an
\alpha
-stable martingale in
\mathbb{R}^{d},d>1
, with
\alpha\in(1,2)
. Recall, its radial measure is given by (
1.7
). Since
Z
has no Wiener part, the Laplace exponent of the jump part
X
of
Z
is identical with the Laplace exponent of
Z
and admits the following representation:

\displaystyle J_{X}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\lambda(\textnormal{d}\xi)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(e^{-\langle z,r\xi\rangle}-1+\langle z,r\xi\rangle\right)\frac{1}{r^{1+\alpha}}\textnormal{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\lambda(\textnormal{d}\xi)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(e^{-r\langle z,\xi\rangle}-1+r\langle z,\xi\rangle\right)\frac{1}{r^{1+\alpha}}\textnormal{d}r

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\langle z,\xi\rangle^{\alpha}\lambda(\textnormal{d}\xi),

(2.17)

where
c_{\alpha}:=\Gamma(2-\alpha)/(\alpha(\alpha-1))
and
\Gamma
stands for the Gamma function.
Above we used the formula

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\Big(e^{-uv}-1+uv\Big)\frac{1}{v^{1+\alpha}}\textnormal{d}v=c_{\alpha}u^{\alpha}.

In the following example, assuming that
Z
is an
\alpha
-stable martingale in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we compute the condition for the function
G
in (
1.5
) so that this equation
generates an affine model.

Example 2.3

Let
Z
be an
\alpha
-stable martingale in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the
Laplace exponent (
2.17
) and
G:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)^{d}
,
G(0)=0
. Then the equation

\displaystyle\textnormal{d}R(t)=(aR(t)+b)\textnormal{d}t+\langle G(R(t-)),Z(t)\rangle,

(2.18)

with
a\in\mathbb{R},b\geq 0
generates an affine model if and only if the function
G
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\langle G(x),\xi\rangle^{\alpha}\lambda(\textnormal{d}\xi)=\frac{C}{c_{\alpha}}x,\quad x\geq 0,

(2.19)

with
C\geq 0
. To prove this fact we need to show that (
2.19
) is equivalent to
(
2.16
) with some measure
\mu(\textnormal{d}v)
. Since
Z
has no Wiener part and

\nu_{G(0)}(\textnormal{d}v)\equiv 0
, we see that (
2.16
) takes the form

J_{Z}(bG(x))=J_{X}(bG(x))=xJ_{\mu}(b),\quad x,b\geq 0.

By (
2.17
)

J_{X}(bG(x))=c_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\langle bG(x),\xi\rangle^{\alpha}\lambda(\textnormal{d}\xi)=c_{\alpha}b^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\langle G(x),\xi\rangle^{\alpha}\lambda(\textnormal{d}\xi).

Consequently,

c_{\alpha}b^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\langle G(x),\xi\rangle^{\alpha}\lambda(\textnormal{d}\xi)=xJ_{\mu}(b),

holds if and only if

J_{\mu}(b)=Cb^{\alpha},\quad\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\langle G(x),\xi\rangle^{\alpha}\lambda(\textnormal{d}\xi)=\frac{C}{c_{\alpha}}x,

for some
C\geq 0
. Hence,
\mu
is an
\alpha
-stable measure and
G
can be any function satisfying (
2.19
).
It follows from Remark
2.2
and (
2.15
) that the generators of
(
2.18
) and that of the equation (
1.4
)
are identical, so are the related bond markets.

3
Reducibility of equations with multivariate noise

In this section we specify conditions for the equation (
1.5
), written now for convenience in the form

\displaystyle\textnormal{d}R(t)=(aR(t)+b)\textnormal{d}t+\langle G(R(t-)),\textnormal{d}Z(t)\rangle,\quad R(0)=x\geq 0,\quad t>0,

(3.1)

to have the reducibility property. The affine form of drift above is justified by (
2.9
). This means that (
3.1
) is supposed to generate
the same bond prices as the equation

\displaystyle\textnormal{d}R(t)=(aR(t)+b)\textnormal{d}t+C\cdot R(t-)^{1/\alpha}\textnormal{d}Z^{\alpha}(t),

(3.2)

with
a\in\mathbb{R},b\geq 0,C>0
and an
\alpha
-stable real valued Lévy process
Z^{\alpha}
with some

\displaystyle\int_{\mid y\mid\leq 1}\left|y\right|\nu(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{1}r\ \gamma_{\xi}(\textnormal{d}r)\ \lambda(\textnormal{d}\xi)=+\infty.

3.1
Main results

For
\xi\in\text{supp}(\lambda)
let us consider the Laplace exponent related to the measure

We need the condition that there exists

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in\text{supp}(\lambda)}J_{\gamma_{\xi}}(b)\leq K\cdot\inf_{\xi\in\text{supp}(\lambda)}J_{\gamma_{\xi}}(b),\quad b\geq 0.

The main result of the paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1

Let
Z
be a Lévy martingale with a covariance matrix
Q
of the Wiener part and a Lévy measure
\nu

admitting the decomposition (
3.3
) with a spherical measure
\lambda
satisfying (
3.11
) and radial measures
\{\gamma_{\xi};\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\}
satisfying (

) and (
3.12

3.7
) and (
3.8
) are satisfied or that (
3.9
) holds. Moreover, let
G:[0,+\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a continuous function such that

\displaystyle G_{0}:=\lim_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{G(x)}{|G(x)|},

(3.13)

exists.

Then if (
3.1
) generates an affine model, then the measure
\mu(\textnormal{d}v)
in (

)-(

) is
\alpha
-stable with

.

The proof of Theorem

is presented in Subsection

and is preceded by
some auxiliary results presented in Subsection

.

From Theorem

the following corollary and remarks follow.

Corollary 3.2

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
be satisfied. If
(
3.1
) is a generating equation then (
2.10
) is satisfied with
c=0
. This means that
the continuous (Wiener) part of the process

Proof:
It follows from (
2.10
) that the Wiener part of

satisfies

\frac{1}{2}\langle QG(x),G(x)\rangle=cx,\ x\geq 0\ \text{for some}\ c\geq 0.

(3.14)

Either directly by assumption (
3.9
) or by the assumptions (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) we get that
G(0)=0
. By Theorem
3.1
, the Laplace transform of the jump part of

satisfies

J_{X}(bG(x))=J_{\nu_{G(0)}}(b)+xJ_{\mu}(b)=\gamma xb^{\alpha},\quad x\geq 0\ \text{for some}\ \gamma>0,\alpha\in(1,2).

(3.15)

Condition (

) guarantees that
\langle G_{0},y\rangle\geq 0
for any

and condition
(
3.11
) guarantees that

From this,
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0+}{|G(x)|}=0
and from (

\langle QG_{0},G_{0}\rangle=\lim_{x\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\langle QG(x),G(x)\rangle}{|G(x)|^{2}}=\lim_{x\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\gamma x}{|G(x)|^{\alpha}}\frac{2c/\gamma}{|G(x)|^{2-\alpha}}=\begin{cases}0\ \ \ \ \ \text{if }c=0;\\
+\infty\ \text{if }c>0.\end{cases}

The following result provides sufficient conditions for the condition (

Proposition 3.5

Let
\gamma(\textnormal{d}r)
and
\Gamma(\textnormal{d}r)
be two measures on
(0,+\infty)
such that for any
\xi\in\text{supp}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\gamma(A)\leq\gamma_{\xi}(A)\leq\Gamma(A),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}((0,+\infty)),

(3.16)

and

\displaystyle 0<\int_{0}^{+\infty}(r^{2}\wedge r)\ \gamma(\textnormal{d}r)\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}(r^{2}\wedge r)\ \Gamma(\textnormal{d}r)<+\infty.

(3.17)

If
\left(\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}r\gamma(\textnormal{d}r)\right)\wedge\left(\int_{1}^{1/\varepsilon}r^{2}\gamma(\textnormal{d}r)\right)>0
for all
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently close to
0
and there exist the limits

\displaystyle q_{0}:=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}r\Gamma(\textnormal{d}r)}{\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}r\gamma(\textnormal{d}r)},\qquad q_{\infty}:=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\int_{1}^{1/\varepsilon}r^{2}\Gamma(\textnormal{d}r)}{\int_{1}^{1/\varepsilon}r^{2}\gamma(\textnormal{d}r)},

and both are finite, then (
3.12
) is satisfied.

Example 3.6
(Spherically balanced Lévy measure)

Let us consider
the case when the radial measures satisfy

\displaystyle\gamma(A)\leq\gamma_{\xi}(A)\leq K\cdot\gamma(A),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}((0,+\infty)),

with some finite constant
K\geq 1
and a measure
\gamma
such that

0<\int_{0}^{+\infty}(r^{2}\wedge r)\ \gamma(\textnormal{d}r)<+\infty

and
\left(\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}r\gamma(\textnormal{d}r)\right)\wedge\left(\int_{1}^{1/\varepsilon}r^{2}\gamma(\textnormal{d}r)\right)>0
for all
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently close to
0
.
Then
q_{0}\leq K
and
q_{\infty}\leq K
, so by Proposition
3.5
condition (
3.12
) is satisfied.

Proof of Proposition
3.5
:
Under (
3.16
) we clearly have

J_{\gamma}(b)\leq\inf_{\xi\in\text{supp}(\lambda)}J_{\gamma_{\xi}}(b)\leq\sup_{\xi\in\text{supp}(\lambda)}J_{\gamma_{\xi}}(b)\leq J_{\Gamma}(b),\quad b\geq 0,

where

\displaystyle J_{\gamma}(b):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(e^{-br}-1+br)\ \gamma(\textnormal{d}r),\quad J_{\Gamma}(b):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(e^{-br}-1+br)\ \Gamma(\textnormal{d}r),\quad b\geq 0.

Therefore (
3.12
) is implied by the condition

\displaystyle J_{\Gamma}(b)\leq K\cdot J_{\gamma}(b),\quad b\geq 0.

(3.18)

Since the functions
J_{\gamma}(\cdot),J_{\Gamma}(\cdot)
are continuous, hence bounded on compacts, (
3.18
) is satisfied with some
K\geq 1
if and only if

p_{\infty}:=\limsup_{b\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J_{\Gamma}(b)}{J_{\gamma}(b)}<+\infty

(3.19)

and

p_{0}:=\limsup_{b\rightarrow 0+}\frac{J_{\Gamma}(b)}{J_{\gamma}(b)}<+\infty.

(3.20)

In what follows we show that (
3.19
) and (
3.20
) indeed hold.

Let us notice that for
x\geq 0

e^{-x}-1+x\sim x\wedge x^{2},

where the relation
\sim
means that there exist universal positive
numbers
k
and
K
such that

k\cdot x\wedge x^{2}\leq e^{-x}-1+x\leq K\cdot x\wedge x^{2}.

Thus, to prove (
3.19
) it is sufficient to prove that

\displaystyle\limsup_{b\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min(br,b^{2}r^{2})\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min(br,b^{2}r^{2})\gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{b\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{1/b}b^{2}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}x)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}br\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}{\int_{0}^{1/b}b^{2}r^{2}\gamma(\mathrm{d}x)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}br\gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}<+\infty.

Let us define the functions

G(y):=\int_{(y,+\infty)}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r),\quad g(y):=\int_{(y,+\infty)}r\gamma(\mathrm{d}r),\quad y>0.

By integration by parts,

\int_{0}^{1/b}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)=\int_{0}^{1/b}r(-\mathrm{d}G(r))=-r\cdot G(r)|_{0}^{1/b}+\int_{0}^{1/b}G(r)\mathrm{d}r.

(3.21)

We fix
\varepsilon>0
and for
y\in(0,\varepsilon)
estimate

\displaystyle y\cdot G(y)

\displaystyle=y\int_{y}^{+\infty}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)=\int_{y}^{\varepsilon}yr\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)+y\cdot G(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)+y\cdot G(\varepsilon).

From this it follows

\limsup_{y\rightarrow 0+}y\cdot G(y)\leq\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)

and by the finiteness of
\int_{0}^{1}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)
and
arbitrary choice of
\varepsilon
, we get

\limsup_{y\rightarrow 0+}yG(y)=0.

Thus, (
3.21
) takes the form

\int_{0}^{1/b}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)=\int_{0}^{1/b}r(-\mathrm{d}G(r))=-\frac{1}{b}\cdot G\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)+\int_{0}^{1/b}G(r)\mathrm{d}r.

Similarly,

\int_{0}^{1/b}r^{2}\gamma(\mathrm{d}r)=-\frac{1}{b}\cdot g\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)+\int_{0}^{1/b}g(r)\mathrm{d}r.

Now we calculate

\displaystyle\limsup_{b\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{1/b}b^{2}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}x)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}br\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}{\int_{0}^{1/b}b^{2}r^{2}\gamma(\mathrm{d}x)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}br\gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{b\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{b^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{b}\cdot G\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)+\int_{0}^{1/b}G(r)\mathrm{d}r\right)+b\cdot G\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)}{b^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{b}\cdot g\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)+\int_{0}^{1/b}g(r)\mathrm{d}r\right)+b\cdot g\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{b\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{1/b}G(r)\mathrm{d}r}{\int_{0}^{1/b}g(r)\mathrm{d}r}<+\infty,

where the last estimate follows from the assumption

q_{0}=\limsup_{y\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\int_{y}^{1}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}{\int_{y}^{1}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}<+\infty

and the finiteness of
\int_{1}^{+\infty}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r),
which
yields that the ratio
G(r)/g(r)
is separated from
+\infty
for

r
sufficiently close to
0
.

To prove (
3.20
) we will proceed in a similar way. To prove
(
3.19
) it is sufficient to prove that

\displaystyle\limsup_{b\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min(br,b^{2}r^{2})\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min(br,b^{2}r^{2})\gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{b\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\int_{0}^{1/b}b^{2}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}x)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}br\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}{\int_{0}^{1/b}b^{2}r^{2}\gamma(\mathrm{d}x)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}br\gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}<+\infty.

We define

Q(y):=\int_{(0,y]}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r),\quad q(y):=\int_{(0,y]}r^{2}\gamma(\mathrm{d}r),\quad y>0.

By integration by parts,

\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)=\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{r}\mathrm{d}Q(r)=\frac{1}{r}Q(r)|_{1/b}^{+\infty}+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}\frac{Q(r)}{r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r.

(3.22)

We fix
M>0
and for
y\in(M,+\infty)
estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y}Q(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{y}\int_{0}^{y}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)=\frac{1}{y}\int_{0}^{M}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)+\int_{M}^{y}\frac{r}{y}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{y}Q(M)+\int_{M}^{+\infty}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r).

From this it follows

\limsup_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{y}Q(y)\leq\int_{M}^{+\infty}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)

and by the finiteness of
\int_{1}^{+\infty}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)

and arbitrary choice of
M
, we get

\limsup_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{y}Q(y)=0.

Thus, (
3.22
) takes the form

\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}r\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)=-bQ\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}\frac{Q(r)}{r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r.

Similarly,

\int_{0}^{1/b}r^{2}\gamma(\mathrm{d}r)=-bq\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}\frac{q(r)}{r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r.

Now we calculate

\displaystyle\limsup_{b\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\int_{0}^{1/b}b^{2}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}x)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}br\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}{\int_{0}^{1/b}b^{2}r^{2}\gamma(\mathrm{d}x)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}br\gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{b\rightarrow 0+}\frac{b^{2}Q\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)+b\left(-bQ\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}Q(r)\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r^{2}}\right)}{b^{2}q\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)+b\left(-bq\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)+\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}q(r)\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r^{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{b\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}Q(r)\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r^{2}}}{\int_{1/b}^{+\infty}q(r)\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r^{2}}}<+\infty,

where the last estimate follows from the assumption

q_{\infty}=\limsup_{y\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\int_{1}^{1/y}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}{\int_{1}^{1/y}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r)}<+\infty

and the finiteness of
\int_{0}^{1}r^{2}\Gamma(\mathrm{d}r),
which
yields that the ratio
Q(r)/q(r)
is separated from
+\infty
for
sufficiently large
r
.

\square

Lemma 3.9

The function
H:[0,+\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

H(z)=e^{-z}-1+z,

is convex, strictly increasing and

\displaystyle\min\{1,t^{2}\}\cdot H(z)\leq H(tz)\leq\max\{1,t^{2}\}\cdot H(z),\quad z\geq 0,t>0.

(3.34)

Proof:
Since
H^{\prime}(z)=1-e^{-z}
the monotonicity and convexity of
H
follows. For
t\geq 1
it follows from the monotonicity of
H
that

H(tz)\geq H(z)=\min\{1,t^{2}\}H(z).

Let us notice that the function

t\longrightarrow\frac{(1-e^{-t})t}{e^{-t}-1+t},\quad t\geq 0,

is strictly decreasing, with limit
2
at zero and
1
at infinity. This implies that

(e^{-t}-1+t)<(1-e^{-t})t<2(e^{-t}-1+t),\quad t\in(0,+\infty).

(3.35)

From (
3.35
) we obtain

\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}s}\ln H(s)=\frac{H^{\prime}(s)}{H(s)}=\frac{1-e^{-s}}{e^{-s}-1+s}\leq\frac{2}{s},\quad s>0,

and, consequently, we obtain that for
t\geq 1
:

\ln H(tz)-\ln H(z)\leq\int_{z}^{tz}\frac{2}{s}\textnormal{d}s=\ln t^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle\min\{1,t^{2}\}H(z)=H(z)\leq H(tz)\leq t^{2}H(z)=\max\{1,t^{2}\}H(z).

(3.36)

Using the monotonicity of
H
and (
3.36
) we see that for
t\in(0,1)
:

H(tz)\leq H(z)=H\left(\frac{1}{t}tz\right)\leq\frac{1}{t^{2}}H(tz),

so also for

\min\{1,t^{2}\}H(z)=t^{2}H(z)\leq H(tz)\leq H(z)=\max\{1,t^{2}\}H(z).

\square

(3.56)

and then, by (
3.37
) and (
3.55
), we obtain that

\displaystyle e^{-2\delta}\leq\frac{e^{m\ln\eta}}{e^{n\ln\theta}}=\frac{J_{\rho}(e^{m\ln\beta})}{J_{\rho}(e^{n\ln\gamma})}\leq e^{2\delta}.

(3.57)

It follows from (
3.56
) that

\left|\frac{\ln\beta}{\ln\gamma}-\frac{n}{m}\right|\leq\frac{\delta}{\mid m\mid\ln\gamma},

and from (
3.57
) that

\left|\frac{\ln\eta}{\ln\theta}-\frac{n}{m}\right|\leq\frac{2\delta}{\mid m\mid\ln\theta}.

Consequently,

\left|\frac{\ln\beta}{\ln\gamma}-\frac{\ln\eta}{\ln\theta}\right|\leq\frac{\delta}{\mid m\mid\ln\gamma}+\frac{2\delta}{\mid m\mid\ln\theta}\leq\frac{\delta}{\ln\gamma}+\frac{2\delta}{\ln\theta}.

Letting
\delta\longrightarrow 0
yields

\frac{\ln\beta}{\ln\gamma}=\frac{\ln\eta}{\ln\theta}.

Let us define

\alpha:=\frac{\ln\eta}{\ln\beta}=\frac{\ln\theta}{\ln\gamma}>0,

and put
b=1
in (
3.55
). This gives

J_{\rho}(e^{m\ln\beta+n\ln\gamma})=J_{\rho}(1)\left(e^{m\ln\beta+n\ln\gamma}\right)^{\alpha},

which means that
J_{\rho}(b)=J_{\rho}(1)b^{\alpha}
for
b
from the set
e^{D}

which is dense in
[0,+\infty)
. As
J_{\rho}
is continuous, (
3.54
) follows.
Finally, by Proposition 3.4 in
[
2
]
it follows that
\alpha\in(1,2)
.

\square

The following result is strictly related to Weyl’s equidistribution theorem, see
[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.14

Let
p,q>0
be such that
p/q\notin\mathbb{Q}
. Let us define the set

G:=\{mp+nq;\quad m,n,=1,2,...\}.

Then for each
\delta>0
there exists a number
M(\delta)>0
such that

\forall x\geq M(\delta)\quad\exists\ g\in G\quad\text{such that}\ \mid x-g\mid\leq\delta.

Moreover, the set

D:=\{mp+nq;\quad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\},

is dense in
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof:
Since
p/q\notin\mathbb{Q}
, at least one of
p,q
, say
q
, is irrational. For simplicity assume that
p=1
and consider the sequence

r(jq),j=1,2,...\quad\text{where}\ r(x):=x\ \text{mod}\ 1,

of fractional parts of the numbers
jq,j=1,2,...
. Recall, Weyl’s equidistribution theorem states that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\longrightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sharp\{j\leq N:r(jq)\in[a,b]\}}{N}=b-a

(3.58)

for any
[a,b]\subseteq[0,1)
if and only if
q
is irrational.

For fixed
\delta>0
and
n
such that
1/n<\delta
let us consider a partition of
[0,1)
of the form

[0,1)=\bigcup_{k=0}^{n-1}A_{k},\quad A_{k}:=[k/n,(k+1)/n).

For a natural number
N
let us consider the set
R_{N}:=\{r(jq):j=1,2,...,N\}
. By (
3.58
), for each
k=0,1,...,n-1
, there exists
N_{k}
such that

R_{N_{k}}\cap A_{k}\neq\emptyset.

Then for
\bar{N}:=\max\{N_{0},N_{1},...,N_{n-1}\}
we have

R_{\bar{N}}\cap A_{k}\neq\emptyset,\quad k=0,1,...,n-1.

Let
M=M(\delta):=\bar{N}q
. Then, for
x\geq M
, there exists a number
N_{x}\leq\bar{N}
such that

\displaystyle\mid r(N_{x}q)-r(x)\mid\leq\frac{1}{n}.

(3.59)

Then for the number

g:=\lfloor x\rfloor-\lfloor N_{x}q\rfloor+N_{x}g\in G

the following holds

\displaystyle\mid x-g\mid

\displaystyle=\mid x-(\lfloor x\rfloor-\lfloor N_{x}q\rfloor+N_{x}q)\mid

\displaystyle=\mid\lfloor x\rfloor+r(x)-\lfloor x\rfloor+\lfloor N_{x}q\rfloor-N_{x}q\mid

\displaystyle=\mid r(x)-r(N_{x}q)\mid\leq 1/n<\delta,

where the last inequality follows from (
3.59
).

The density of
D
is an immediate consequence of the first part of the Lemma. Indeed, for
x<M(\delta)
and
g\in G
such that
x+g>M(\delta)
there exists
\tilde{g}\in G
such that
\mid x+g-\tilde{g}\mid<\delta
.

The general case with
p\neq 1
can be proven in the same way but requires a generalized version of Weyl’s theorem, which says that the numbers
r_{p}(nq),n=1,2,...
, where
r_{p}(x):=x\ mod\ p
, are equidistributed on
[0,p)
if and only if
p/q\notin\mathbb{Q}
. This can be proven by a straightforward modification of the original arguments of Weyl.

\square

(3.61)

,

.

,
such that for any

from the inequality

\left|\frac{G(x)}{\left|G(x)\right|}-G_{0}\right|\leq\delta(\varepsilon).

follows that for any

\varepsilon>0

,

\left|\frac{G(x)}{\left|G(x)\right|}-G_{0}\right|\leq\delta(\varepsilon),

Let us fix

and take

,

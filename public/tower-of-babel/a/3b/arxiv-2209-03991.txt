Bound states and electromagnetic radiation of relativistically rotating cylindrical wells
Matteo Buzzegoli, Kirill Tuchin

Abstract
We compute the effect of rigid rotation on the non-relativistic bound states. The energy levels of the bound states increase with the angular velocity of rotation until at certain value of the angular velocity they are completely pushed out into the continuum which corresponds to dissociation of the bound states. When the angular velocity exceeds the critical value at which the ground state disappears into the continuum, no bound state is possible. This effect should have important consequences for the phenomenology of the quark-gluon plasma. One of the ways to study it experimentally is to observe the electromagnetic radiation emitted by a rotating bound state. We compute the corresponding intensity of electromagnetic radiation and show that it strongly depends on the angular velocity of rotation.

One of the plasma signatures is dissociation of the bound states immersed into it through the mechanism of the Debye screening. The ability of plasma to break up the bound states depends on the relationship between the Debye radius which is a decreasing function of temperature and the linear size of the bound state. On the other hand, the rotating plasma drags the bound state along. As a result, the rotating bound state possesses extra centrifugal energy that makes it more fragile. Thus, both the Debye screening and rotation induce the break up of bound states. The main goal of this paper is to investigate the effect of rotation on the bound state spectrum.

To this end we consider a model consisting of a cylindrical well of radius
R
and constant depth
U_{0}
, rotating with constant angular velocity
\Omega
about an axis passing through its center-of-mass. We neglect all plasma effects apart from being the source of rotation. Furthermore, we treat the bound state non-relativistically which allows us to retain essential qualitative features while keeping algebra simple. Our results thus apply literally to a heavy quarkonium immersed into and coaxial with a vortex of the rotating plasma. The critical ingredient of our calculation is the causal boundary condition at
r=1/\Omega

\Omega R
is larger or smaller than unity. We refer to this two cases as rapid and slow rotation. Our main observation is that the energy levels of the rotating quarkonium increase with
\Omega

\Omega

III

The dependence of the spectrum on
\Omega

\Omega>1/R

in comparison with the non-rotating well. This is studied it Sec
V

\Omega

II
Bound states of stationary cylindrical well

First, for the future reference, consider a non-rotating cylindrical well as shown in Fig.
1
. We are interested in the solutions with
E<0

r<R
, reads

\displaystyle H_{0}\psi_{0}=-\frac{1}{2M}\nabla^{2}\psi_{0}-U_{0}\psi_{0}=E_{0}\psi_{0}\,.

(1)

The corresponding set of eigenfunctions, regular at the origin and bound in the radial direction, is

\displaystyle\psi_{0}({\bm{x}})=AJ_{m}(kr)e^{im\phi}e^{ik_{z}z}\,,

(2)

where
J_{m}(z)
is the Bessel function of the first kind,
A
is the normalization constant and

\displaystyle k=\sqrt{2M(U_{0}-k_{z}^{2}/2M-|E_{0}|)}\,.

(3)

Clearly the bound states exist only if
|E_{0}|\leq U_{0}-k_{z}^{2}/2M
.

Outside the well the functions that vanish at
r\to\infty
are

\displaystyle\psi_{0}({\bm{x}})=BK_{m}(\kappa r)e^{im\phi}e^{ik_{z}z}\,,

(4)

where
K_{m}(z)
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind and

\displaystyle\kappa=\sqrt{2M(k_{z}^{2}/2M+|E_{0}|)}\,.

(5)

Eq. (
4

\psi\to 0
as
r\to\infty
.

The boundary conditions at
r=R
are the requirements of continuity of the wave function and its first derivative:

\displaystyle AJ_{m}(kR)

\displaystyle=BK_{m}(\kappa R)\,,

(6)

\displaystyle AJ_{m}^{\prime}(kR)k

\displaystyle=BK_{m}^{\prime}(\kappa R)\kappa\,.

(7)

The solution exists only if the determinant vanishes:

\displaystyle J_{m}(kR)K_{m}^{\prime}(\kappa R)\kappa=K_{m}(\kappa R)J_{m}^{\prime}(kR)k\,.

(8)

This equation along with

\displaystyle k=\sqrt{2MU_{0}-\kappa^{2}}

(9)

gives the spectrum.

The Hamiltonian of the rotating system is

The determinant of the set of linear equations (

As

Using the convenient notation:

\displaystyle I_{m}

\displaystyle-K_{m}(y/\lambda)\left[-J_{m}(x)I_{m}^{\prime}(y)y+I_{m}(y)J_{m}^{\prime}(x)x\right]=0\,,

where

Denoting the roots of (

\displaystyle E_{mnk_{z}}=-\frac{1}{2MR^{2}}y_{mn}^{2}-\frac{m\lambda}{R}+\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{2M}\,.

III.2
Rapid rotation

If the rotation is fast
R>1/\Omega
, all the region
r>1/\Omega
is outside of the rotating spacetime. Thus there is only solution inside the well; its functional form is (
2

\displaystyle J_{m}(k/\Omega)=0\,.

(20)

Denoting zeros of the

\displaystyle E_{mak_{z}}=\frac{1}{2M}(\Omega^{2}x^{2}_{ma}+k_{z}^{2})-U_{0}-m\Omega\,,

(21)

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle E_{mak_{z}}=\frac{1}{2MR^{2}}\left(\lambda^{2}x^{2}_{ma}-\beta^{2}\right)+\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{2M}-\frac{m\lambda}{R}\,.

(22)

Fig.

19

, and (

\lambda>1
, with

,
k_{z}=0

As the angular velocity of rotation increases, the level increases (i.e. its absolute value decreases) until it reaches

. The discontinuity at

III.3
Shallow and slowly rotating cylindrical well

Consider states with
m=0
,
k_{z}=0
in the shallow well limit
\beta^{2}\ll 1
, we can expand the Bessel functions in (
17
) at
x,y\ll 1
which yields

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{2}(\beta^{2}-y^{2})\ln\frac{2}{\tilde{\gamma}y}=-\frac{\pi\beta^{2}}{2}\exp\left(-\frac{2y}{\lambda}\right)\,,

(23)

where
\tilde{\gamma}=\exp\gamma\approx 1.78
, with
\gamma
being Euler-Mascheroni constant.
Considering the right-hand-side of (
23
) a perturbation we can write at the leading “non-rotating” approximation

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{2}(\beta^{2}-y_{0}^{2})\ln\frac{2}{\tilde{\gamma}y_{0}}=0\,.

(24)

The only solution is obtained by neglecting
y_{0}
compared to
\beta

\displaystyle y_{0}=\frac{2}{\tilde{\gamma}}\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}\right)\,.

(25)

Clearly,

is much smaller than any positive power of (small)
\beta
. In usual units

\displaystyle E_{0}=-\frac{2}{\tilde{\gamma}^{2}MR^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{2}{MU_{0}R^{2}}\right)\,.

(26)

The second iteration of (
23
) is obtained by expanding
y=y_{0}+y_{1}
at small

:

\displaystyle y_{1}=-\pi y_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{4}{\lambda\tilde{\gamma}}e^{-\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}}\right)\,.

(27)

Converting this back to the dimensional quantities gives the correction due to rotation

\displaystyle\Delta E=-2\pi E_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{4}{R\Omega\tilde{\gamma}}e^{-\frac{2}{MU_{0}R^{2}}}\right)\,.

(28)

As
\Omega
increases,
\Delta E
also increases with the result that the energy level moves up.

The level crosses into the continuum when
E_{0}+\Delta E=0
. This happens at the value
\Omega_{c}
given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{c}=\frac{2\sqrt{2M|E_{0}|}}{\ln(2\pi)}\,.

(29)

Bound states of rotating 2D
1/r

Another instructive model of a rotating well with cylindrical symmetry is an attractive

V
Electromagnetic radiation by rotating cylindrical well

A rotating well binding oppositely charged particles emits electromagnetic radiation. The intensity of radiation can be used as an effective diagnostic tool.

The
S
-matrix element describing photon radiation when the system transitions from the initial state
i
to the final state
f
is

\displaystyle S_{fi}=-ie\int dt\int d^{3}x\,{\bm{j}}_{fi}(x)\cdot{\bm{A}}^{*}(x)\,,

(35)

where the transition current is

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}_{fi}=\frac{i}{2M}\left(\psi_{i}{\bm{\nabla}}\psi^{*}_{f}-\psi^{*}_{f}{\bm{\nabla}}\psi_{i}\right)\,.

(36)

We will distinguish the quantum numbers of the final state with the prime, i.e.
i=\{m,k_{z},\ldots\}
,
f=\{m^{\prime},k_{z}^{\prime},\ldots\}
.
The well wave functions, including their time dependence, read

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}X(r)e^{im\phi}e^{ik_{z}z}e^{-iEt}\,,

(37)

where
X(r)
is an appropriate radial function derived in the previous section. The wave functions are normalized by
\int\psi^{*}\psi d^{3}x=1
which implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/\Omega}drrX^{2}=1\,.

(38)

The photon wave function is

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}^{*}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega V}}{\bm{\epsilon}}^{*}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}e^{-i{\bm{p}}\cdot{\bm{x}}+i\omega t}\,,

(39)

where
{\bm{\epsilon}}^{*}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}
is the polarization vector,
\lambda=\pm 1
.

Since we treat the well in non-relativistic approximation, the leading contribution to the radiation intensity is the electric dipole radiation corresponding to wave lengths much larger than the system size, i.e.
pR\ll 1
. Thus neglecting the phase
{\bm{p}}\cdot{\bm{r}}
in the exponent in (
39
) and substituting it along with (
37
) into (
36
) and (
35
) produces

\displaystyle S_{fi}=\frac{e(2\pi)^{2}}{2M\sqrt{2\omega V}}{\bm{\epsilon}}^{*}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}\cdot{\bm{I}}_{fi}\delta(E-E^{\prime}-\omega)\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\delta_{k_{z}k_{z}^{\prime}}\,,

(40)

where we defined

\displaystyle{\bm{I}}_{fi}

\displaystyle=I_{r,fi}\hat{{\bm{r}}}+I_{\phi,fi}\hat{{\bm{\phi}}}+I_{z,fi}\hat{{\bm{z}}}\,

\displaystyle=\hat{{\bm{r}}}\int_{0}^{1/\Omega}(X_{f}^{\prime}X_{i}-X_{f}X_{i}^{\prime})rdr+2m\hat{{\bm{\phi}}}\int_{0}^{1/\Omega}X_{f}X_{i}dr+2k_{z}\hat{{\bm{z}}}\int_{0}^{1/\Omega}X_{f}X_{i}rdr\,.

(41)

The total radiation intensity is given by dividing the emission probability
|S|^{2}
by the observation time
\Delta t
, multiplying by the photon energy
\omega
and summing and integrating over the photon phase space:

\displaystyle W_{fi}=\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{|S|^{2}}{\Delta t}\omega\frac{do_{\hat{{\bm{p}}}}\omega^{2}d\omega V}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,,

(42)

where
do_{\hat{{\bm{p}}}}
is the solid angle element in the direction of the photon momentum. Substituting (
42
) into (
40
) and using

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}|{\bm{\epsilon}}^{*}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}\cdot{\bm{I}}_{fi}|^{2}=\frac{|{\bm{I}}_{fi}\times{\bm{p}}|^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\,,

(43)

we obtain

\displaystyle W_{fi}=\frac{1}{24\pi}\frac{e^{2}\omega^{2}}{M^{2}}|{\bm{I}}_{fi}|^{2}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\delta_{k_{z}k_{z}^{\prime}}\,.

(44)

Eqs. (
44
) and (
V
) give the intensity of the electromagnetic radiation in the dipole approximation. The photon energy
\omega
is fixed by the energy and momentum conservation and depends on the angular velocity
\Omega
.

V.1
Radiation by rapidly rotating cylindrical well

Using the model we developed in the previous section we can compute the intensity of electromagnetic radiation of the rapidly rotating cylindrical well

\displaystyle X(r)=\frac{\sqrt{2}\Omega}{J_{m+1}(x_{ma})}J_{m}(x_{ma}\Omega r)\,.

Using these in (

\displaystyle W_{fi}=\frac{1}{96\pi}\frac{e^{2}\Omega^{6}}{M^{4}}(x_{ma}^{2}-x_{ma^{\prime}}^{2})^{2}\Omega^{2}(\tilde{I}_{r,fi}^{2}+\tilde{I}_{\phi,fi}^{2})\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\,,

where the photon energy is, using (

\displaystyle\omega=E_{ma0}-E_{ma^{\prime}0}=\frac{\Omega^{2}}{2M}(x_{ma}^{2}-x_{ma^{\prime}}^{2})\,,

(47)

and the functions

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{r,fi}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2\rho d\rho}{J_{m+1}(x_{ma})J_{m+1}(x_{ma^{\prime}})}\left[J_{m}^{\prime}(x_{ma^{\prime}}\rho)J_{m}(x_{ma}\rho)x_{ma^{\prime}}-J_{m}(x_{ma^{\prime}}\rho)J_{m}^{\prime}(x_{ma}\rho)x_{ma}\right],

(48)

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{\phi,fi}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{4md\rho}{J_{m+1}(x_{ma})J_{m+1}(x_{ma^{\prime}})}J_{m}(x_{ma^{\prime}}\rho)J_{m}(x_{ma}\rho)\,

V.2
Radiation by rotating infinite cylindrical well

The calculation of the radiation intensity for slowly rotating cylindrical well
R<1/\Omega
can also be done analytically. However it leads to bulky expressions involving Bessel functions. A more instructive model is an
infinite
cylindrical well, i.e. the potential
U=0
if

and

\displaystyle X(r)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\ell J_{m+1}(x_{ma})}J_{m}(x_{ma}r/\ell)\,,

(50)

where

\displaystyle W_{fi}=\frac{1}{96\pi}\frac{e^{2}}{M^{4}\ell^{6}}(x_{ma}^{2}-x_{ma^{\prime}}^{2})^{2}(\tilde{I}_{r,fi}^{2}+\tilde{I}_{\phi,fi}^{2})\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\,.

(51)

Thus, in intensity is

the intensity increases rapidly in proportion to

:

\displaystyle W_{fi}=(R\Omega)^{6}W_{fi}^{\Omega=0}\,,\qquad\text{if}\quad\Omega R>1\,.

(52)

While the particular functional form of

depends on the form of the potential

VI
Discussion

We computed the energy spectrum of a particle in the non-relativistic cylindrical potential well rotating with constant angular velocity
\Omega
. The energy levels increase with the angular velocity
\Omega
until they are pushed out of the well at some critical value
\Omega_{c}
as can be seen in Fig.
2
. The particular form of the functional dependence
E(\Omega)
is model-dependent. The discontinuity at
R\Omega=1
is an artifact of the cylindrical well approximation; it disappears for a smoother potential such as the attractive
1/r
potential discussed in Sec.
IV
.

To investigate the effect of rotation in a phenomenologically interesting case of charmonium one would have to consider rotation of the spherical symmetric potential, which has to be dealt with numerically. Nevertheless, we can make a ballpark estimate using (
34
). The binding energy of
J\mskip-2.0mu/\mskip-0.5mu\psi
is
M(\psi^{\prime})-M(J\mskip-2.0mu/\mskip-0.5mu\psi)=\frac{16}{9}\alpha^{2}M=0.59
GeV. The reduced mass is
M=m_{c}/2=0.63
GeV which implies that
\alpha=0.72
. Using the values
\ell_{1,0}
and
\ell_{2,0}
listed at the end of Sec.
IV
we derive that
J\mskip-2.0mu/\mskip-0.5mu\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
dissociate into the continuum when they get swirled by a vortex rotating with angular velocity
\Omega=3
fm
-1
and
\Omega=0.6
fm
-1
respectively. These estimates of
\Omega
are somewhat larger than the vorticity achievable at a (relatively) low energy relativistic heavy ion collisions
Deng:2016gyh
. We stress that at the above values of vorticity, the bound states completely dissolve, however the effect of rotation — the decrease of the binding energy with
\Omega
— is essential even at sub-critical angular velocities. Needless to say that these estimates should be taken only as a motivation for further investigation with more accurate quarkonium models.

We also computed the intensity of radiation emitted by a rotating bound state and found that when
R\Omega<1
it increases by a factor
(R\Omega)^{6}
. This indicates an increase in transition probability from the excited to the ground state.

Our results indicate that plasma rotation has significant effect on the hadron spectra in relativistic heavy-ion which deserves a dedicated quantitative study.

Connection Problem for an Extension of 𝒒 \bm{q} -Hypergeometric Systems
Takahiko NOBUKAWA

Abstract
We give an example of solutions of the connection problem associated with a certain system of linear q q -difference equations recently introduced by Park. The result contains a connection formulas of the q q -Lauricella hypergeometric function φ D \varphi_{D} and those of the q q -generalized hypergeometric function φ N N + 1 {}_{N+1}\varphi_{N} as special cases.

Introduction

In
[
24
]
, a hypergeometric function
F_{L,N}
, which is a certain generalization of hypergeometric functions, was defined by Tsuda.
He also obtained a Hamiltonian system
\mathcal{H}_{L,N}
, which describes an isomonodromic deformation of an
L\times L
Fuchsian system on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
N+3
regular singularities, and which has particular solutions in terms of the function
F_{L,N}
.
In
[
20
,
21
]
, a
q
-analog of Tsuda’s result was obtained by Park. Namely, she defined a
q
-hypergeometric function
\mathcal{F}_{N,M}
, which is given by (
2.2
) below, and a system
\mathcal{P}_{N,M}
as a
q
-analog of the function
F_{N+1,M}
and the system
\mathcal{H}_{N+1,M}
, respectively.
The function
\mathcal{F}_{N,M}
converges locally and satisfies linear
q
-difference equations, given by (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) below.
Also the function
\mathcal{F}_{N,M}
has the Euler-type integral representation, given by (
2.4
).
Thus the function
\mathcal{F}_{N,M}
must be continued analytically to
\mathbb{C}^{M}
.

Our aim is to solve the connection problem related with the function
\mathcal{F}_{N,M}
, that is to give connection matrices among fundamental solutions of the
q
-difference equations (
2.5
) and (
2.6
).
The main result is Theorem
3.8
in Section
3.2
.
Proposition
3.6
gives fundamental solutions for the equation, and Theorem
3.8
gives connection matrices among these fundamental solutions.
Our method to calculate the connection matrices is to use a connection formula of the generalized
q
-hypergeometric function
{}_{N+1}\varphi_{N}
many times.
The connection matrices are given by the product of some matrices.

In the general theory of
q
-difference systems in several variables, local solutions should be characterized by the asymptotic behavior near the singularity in some prescribed sector.
For the general theory of
q
-difference systems in several variables, see
[
2
,
3
]
.
Our solutions are defined in the region
\{|t_{1}|\ll\cdots\ll|t_{L}|\ll 1\ll|t_{L+1}|\ll\cdots\ll|t_{M}|\}
where
0\leq L\leq M
, and characterized by the asymptotic behavior of the following form

\displaystyle t_{1}^{\delta_{1}}\cdots t_{M}^{\delta_{M}}(1+O(||x||)),

at
x=(t_{1}/t_{2},\dots,t_{L-1}/t_{L},t_{L},1/t_{L+1},t_{L+1}/t_{L+2},\dots,t_{M-1}/t_{M})=(0,\dots,0)
.
Here
\delta=(\delta_{1},\dots,\allowbreak\delta_{M})
is a suitable parameter.
For more details, see Definition
3.1
, Proposition
3.2
, Remark
3.4
.

The function
\mathcal{F}_{N,M}
is a generalization both of the
q
-Lauricella function
\varphi_{D}
and of the generalized
q
-hypergeometric function
{}_{N+1}\varphi_{N}
.
Thus our results contain solving the connection problem related with
\varphi_{D}
and also related with
{}_{N+1}\varphi_{N}
.
The equality (
3.12
) below can be considered as a part of the connection relations for
{}_{N+1}\varphi_{N}
, which was first studied by Thomae
[
23
]
and was proved by Watson
[
25
]
.
Also the
q
-Lauricella function has a Jackson integral representation of Jordan–Pochhammer type, and the connection problem related with the Jackson integral of Jordan–Pochhammer type was solved by Mimachi
[
16
]
.
In differential case, fundamental solutions of the equation related with the Lauricella function
F_{D}
were obtained by Gelfand–Kapranov–Zelevinsky
[
9
]
as an application of the theory of the GKZ hypergeometric function.
Our solutions are generalizations of the Gelfand–Kapranov–Zelevinsky’s solutions (see Remark
3.3
).

The contents of this paper are as follows.
In Section
2
, we give notations and the properties of the function
\mathcal{F}_{N,M}
and the system of
q
-difference equations satisfied by
\mathcal{F}_{N,M}
.
In Section
3
, we solve the connection problem of the system.
In Section
3.1
, we show fundamental solutions of the
q
-difference system.
In Section
3.2
, we give the matrices which connect the fundamental solution with the other fundamental solution.
In Section
4
, we obtain an elliptic solution of the Yang–Baxter equation as an application of Section
3.2
.

5
Summary and discussion

A summary of our results is as follows.
The main result of this paper is Theorem
3.8
, which gives the connection matrices for fundamental solutions
\bm{u}^{L,\sigma}
of the
q
-difference system
E_{N,M}
(
2.5
) and (
2.6
).
The fundamental solution
\bm{u}^{L,\sigma}
converges in the region
\{|t_{\sigma(1)}|\ll\cdots\ll|t_{\sigma(L)}|\ll 1\ll|t_{\sigma(L+1)}|\ll\cdots\ll|t_{\sigma(M)}|\}
.
The component of the fundamental solution
\bm{u}^{L,\mathrm{id}}
has the asymptotic behavior of the form
t_{1}^{\delta_{1}}\cdots t_{M}^{\delta_{M}}(1+O(||x||))
at
x=(t_{1}/t_{2},\dots,t_{L-1}/t_{L},t_{L},1/t_{L+1},t_{L+1}/t_{L+2},\dots,\allowbreak t_{M-1}/t_{M})=(0,\dots,0)
for some
\delta
.
More precisely, see Remark
3.4
.
The way to get the connection matrices is to calculate “easy” connection matrices many times, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{L_{1},\sigma_{1}}\to\bm{u}^{L_{1}+1,\sigma_{1}}\to\cdots\to\bm{u}^{M,\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\bm{u}^{L_{1},\sigma_{1}}}{}\to\bm{u}^{M,s_{r_{1}}\sigma_{1}}\to\bm{u}^{M,s_{r_{2}}s_{r_{1}}\sigma_{1}}\to\cdots\to\bm{u}^{M,s_{r_{I}}\cdots s_{r_{1}}\sigma_{1}}=\bm{u}^{M,\sigma_{2}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\bm{u}^{L_{1},\sigma_{1}}}{}\to\bm{u}^{M-1,\sigma_{2}}\to\bm{u}^{M-2,\sigma_{2}}\to\cdots\to\bm{u}^{L_{2},\sigma_{2}}.

Here,
s_{r}=(r,r+1)\in\mathfrak{S}_{M}
.
Each step can be calculated by using Thomae–Watson’s formula
[
23
,
25
]
, which is a connection formula of the function
{}_{N+1}\varphi_{N}
.
In addition, as an application of Theorem
3.8
, we obtained a solution of the Yang–Baxter equation by considering the connection matrix between
\bm{u}^{M,(r,r+2)}
and
\bm{u}^{M,\mathrm{id}}
.
Also we showed that our matrix (
4.3
) is identified as Jimbo, Miwa, Okado’s matrix (
4.2
), and our solution and Aomoto, Kato, Mimachi’s solution
[
4
]
are conjugate.

There are many problems related to our results.
We mention four of them here.

(i)

By taking the limit
q\to 1
with
a_{j}=q^{\alpha_{j}}
,
b_{i}=q^{\beta_{i}}
,
c_{j}=q^{\gamma_{j}}
, we obtain fundamental solutions of Tsuda’s hypergeometric equations
[
24
]

\displaystyle\left\{t_{s}(\beta_{s}+\mathcal{D}_{s})\prod_{j=1}^{N}(\alpha_{j}+\mathcal{D})-\mathcal{D}_{s}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(\gamma_{j}-1+\mathcal{D})\right\}y=0,\qquad 1\leq s\leq M,

\displaystyle\{t_{r}(\beta_{r}+\mathcal{D}_{r})\mathcal{D}_{s}-t_{s}(\beta_{s}+\mathcal{D}_{s})\mathcal{D}_{r}\}y=0,\qquad 1\leq r<s\leq M,

where
\mathcal{D}_{s}=t_{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{s}}
and
\mathcal{D}=\sum_{s=1}^{M}\mathcal{D}_{s}
.
Similar to the method of Theorem
3.8
, it is expected that the connection problem of Tsuda’s equations will be solved.
In this case, it must be noted that solutions are multivalued functions.
The connection formula of Tsuda’s hypergeometric function
F_{N+1,M}
, which is a solution of Tsuda’s equations, depends on the path on
X=\big\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{M})\in\mathbb{C}^{M};\,t_{i}\neq t_{j},\,i\neq j,\,t_{i}\neq 0,1\big\}
.
In
[
15
]
, a path of connection for solutions of GG system
[
8
]
was discussed by Matsubara-Heo.
Thus the connection problem of Tsuda’s equations will also be solved by Matsubara’s method.
Note that in the case of
N=1
, some of connection formulas related with
F_{1}
were obtained by Olsson
[
18
]
, and related with
F_{D}
were obtained by Mimachi
[
17
]
from the viewpoint of the Jordan–Pochhammer integral and the intersection theory.

(ii)

The function is a generalization of the
q
-Lauricella function
\varphi_{D}
.
In differential case and two variable case, i.e.,

\displaystyle[t_{s}(\beta_{s}+\mathcal{D}_{s})(\alpha+\mathcal{D}_{s}+\mathcal{D}_{r})-\mathcal{D}_{s}(\gamma-1+\mathcal{D}_{s}+\mathcal{D}_{r})]F=0,\qquad(i,j)=(1,2),\,(2,1),

there are power series solutions of the equation which converge near
(t_{1},t_{2})=(0,0)
,
(0,1)
,
(0,\infty)
,
(1,0)
,
(1,1)
,
(1,\infty)
,
(\infty,0)
,
(\infty,1)
,
(\infty,\infty)
.
Here we show two solutions as examples:

\displaystyle F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\alpha;\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\\
\alpha+\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+1-\gamma\end{matrix};1-t_{1},1-t_{2}\right),

\displaystyle t_{1}^{-\beta_{1}}t_{2}^{-\beta_{2}}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+1-\gamma;\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\\
\alpha+\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+1-\gamma\end{matrix};\frac{t_{1}-1}{t_{1}},\frac{t_{2}-1}{t_{2}}\right).

60 solutions like these series were obtained by Appell
[
5
]
, Le Vavasseur
[
14
]
, and other solutions which are given by the Horn’s
G_{2}
function were obtained by Erdélyi
[
6
]
.
In this paper we get power series solutions for the system
E_{N,M}
, however in the case
N=1
and
M=2
, the series are
q
-analogs of only a part of Appell, Le Vavasseur, Erdélyi’s solutions.
In case of one variable, Hahn
[
10
]
obtained
q
-analogs of Kummer’s 24 solutions.
In future we hope that other solutions for
E_{N,M}
, which contain
q
-analogs of Appell, Le Vavasseur, Erdélyi’s solutions will be obtained.
It is expected that these solutions will be generalizations of Hahn’s solutions.

(iii)

Our fundamental solutions
\bm{u}^{L,\sigma}
are given by series.
On the other hand, a solution
\mathcal{F}_{N,M}
has the Euler-type Jackson integral representation (
2.4
).
It is also expected that other solutions of the
q
-difference system
E_{N,M}
have the Euler-type Jackson integral representation with suitable domain of integration.

(iv)

In recent years, the theory of elliptic difference equations has progressed.
For example, considering discrete isomonodromic deformations of a linear difference system, an elliptic Garnier system which is a generalization of elliptic Painlevé equation defined by Sakai
[
22
]
was obtained by Ormerod, Rains
[
19
]
and Yamada
[
26
]
.
Also, by using representation theory of the elliptic quantum group

, an explicit formula for elliptic hypergeometric integral solutions of the face type elliptic

[
13
]
.
We hope that an elliptic analog of the hypergeometric function
\mathcal{F}_{N,M}
and its related isomonodromic system will be obtained and our result will be extended to the elliptic hypergeometric function.

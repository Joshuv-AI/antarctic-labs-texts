Introduction

Let
f(z)
be a modular form of weight
k\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}

and level
N
with Fourier expansion

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)e^{2\pi inz},\quad\Im(z)\geq 0,

where
a(n)
is the
n^{th}
Fourier coefficient. In this article, we assume that all the Fourier coefficients
a(n)\in\mathbb{Q}.
J.P. Serre showed in
[
17
]
that,
if there is a prime
p\nmid 2N
such that
a(p)\not\equiv 0\pmod{m},
we then have an asymptotic expansion

\#\{n\leq x\mid a(n)\not\equiv 0\pmod{m}\}=c\frac{x}{\log^{\alpha(m)}x},

for some
c>0,~0<\alpha(m)<1.
On the other hand, he also showed that if there exists a prime
p
such that
a(p)\equiv 0\pmod{m}
for all
p\nmid 2N,
there exists
c^{\prime}>0
such that

\#\{n\leq x\mid a(n)\not\equiv 0\pmod{m}\}=c^{\prime}\sqrt{x}.

These results essentially follow from Delange’s generalization of Wiener-Ikehara Tauberian theorem
[
4
]
, and considering the corresponding
L
-function over the ring
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}.
In particular, this says that
a(n)\equiv 0\pmod{m}
for almost all
n
and other residue classes appear rather rarely. However, we do not know whether each non-zero residue class
a\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
can be written as
a(n)\pmod{m}
with equal proportion. It was mentioned by Serre in page 20 of
[
17
]
that, for any odd
m
and non-zero
a\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},

\#\{n\leq x\mid a(n)\equiv a\pmod{m},\ \omega(n)=M\}\gg\frac{x}{\log x}(\log\log x)^{M}.

Proof of these arguments were based on showing that, for a positive density of primes
p\equiv 1\pmod{m}
and
q\equiv-1\pmod{m}
the corresponding Hecke operators
T_{p}
and
T_{q}
acts respectively as
2
and
0
on the
\mathbb{Z}
-module of all holomorphic modular forms with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}.

In this regard, we study the distribution of
\{a(n)\pmod{m}\}
over the integers with
O(1)
prime factors and prove the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
M\geq 1
be any integer, and
f
be any newform without CM, and with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}.
Then under the certain assumptions on
m
, the following asymptotic formula holds for any tuple

\frac{\#\{n\leq x\mid a(n)\equiv a\pmod{m},~\omega(n)=M\}}{\#\{n\leq x\mid\omega(n)=M\}}\sim d_{a}(m)\frac{1}{m^{M}},

for some
d_{a}(m)>0
, which is an effectively computable constant.

We shall write a more precise version in Theorem
4
, and for a much broader family of cuspforms. Even though this guarantees the existence of a solution to
a(n)\equiv a\pmod{m},
any such
n
could be large. First of all, a solution to
a(n)\pmod{m}
is also a solution to
a(n)\pmod{\ell}
for any prime
\ell\mid m.
The reader may refer to
[
20
]
for a bound on least such
n
when
m
is a prime. Thorner and Zaman assumed that
f(z)
is a newform without CM, when weight
k
satisfies
(k-1,m-1)=1,
and the associated Galois representation has image
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}).
Thorner and Zaman gave a bound for least prime
n
which satisfies
a(n)\equiv a\pmod{m}.
However, this is indeed equivalent to the general case. For instance if
n_{0}
is the least solution, then writing
n_{0}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}p^{e_{i}}_{i,0},
we have
\prod_{i=1}^{t}a(p^{e_{i}}_{i,0})\equiv a\pmod{m}.
Setting
a(p^{e_{i}}_{i,0})=a_{i}
and writing
a(p^{e_{i}}_{i,0})
as a polynomial in
a(p_{i,0})
and (since
p_{i,0}
is fixed), we see that
a(p_{i,0})\pmod{\ell},\ \ell\mid m
is a root of a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}[x].
Then we look for the least prime
p_{i}
for which
a(p_{i})\equiv a(p_{i,0})\pmod{\ell},
and this shows that it is enough to look for the least prime.

In particular, we find that any such
n
with
a(n)\equiv a\pmod{m}
could grow exponentially as a function of
m.
However, it turns out that we can have a polynomial bound if we allow a few more terms. More precisely, we study the equation

\sum_{i=1}^{O(1)}a(n_{i})\equiv a\pmod{m},~n_{i}=m^{O(1)}.

In
[
19
]
, Shparlinski proved that for the Ramanujan-Tau function
\tau
the set
\{\tau(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
is an additive basis modulo any prime
\ell,
that is, there exists an absolute constant
s
such that the Waring-type congruence

\tau(n_{1})+\cdots+\tau(n_{s})\equiv a\hskip-8.5359pt\pmod{\ell}

is solvable for any residue class
a\pmod{\ell}.
Moreover, Shparlinski showed that each such
n_{i}
is at most
O(\ell^{4}),
and has at most
4
prime factors. His argument relied on studying the exponential sum

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}\in U_{1},u_{2}\in U_{2}\\
a(n)=u_{1}u_{2}\pmod{\ell}\end{subarray}}{\mathbf{e}}_{\ell}\left({\lambda(u-a)}\right),~\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{*},

over a suitably large subset
U_{1}\times U_{2}
of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\times\mathbb{F}_{\ell},
which satisfies
\#U_{1}\#U_{2}>\ell^{1+c}
for some
c>0.

Recently, Bajpai, García, and the first author showed in
[
1
]
that when
f
is a newform without CM and with rational Fourier coefficients, then there is an absolute constant
s_{0}
such that any element of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
can be written as a sum of at most
s_{0}
elements of the set
\{a(p^{n})\}_{n\geq 0},
for almost all primes
p
and
\ell
. Moreover,
s_{0}
does not depend on the choice of
f.
To prove that they studied the exponential sum

\sum_{n\leq\Gamma}{\mathbf{e}}_{\ell}\left({\lambda a(p^{n})}\right),~\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{*},

where
\Gamma
is a suitable parameter depending on the sequence
\{a(p^{n})\pmod{\ell}\}.
Although we are getting more solutions, in this case, the coefficients might grow exponentially as a function of
\ell.

In this article, we prove an improvement over the main result of Shparlinski
[
19
]
in the following form.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f(z)
be any cuspform with rational coefficients. Let
0<\epsilon,\gamma<1
be any given positive real numbers. Let
S_{1},S_{2}
be any set of primes having positive density with
S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\phi.
Then there exists an integer
N_{S_{1},S_{2}}
such that for any integer
m
with all prime factors larger than
N_{S_{1},S_{2}},
and
L^{\gamma}\geq m/L,
where
L
is the largest prime factor of
m,
we have the following.

(a)

If
f(z)
is a Hecke eigenform then for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},
we can write

\sum_{i=1}^{s}a(n_{i})\equiv a\pmod{m},~n_{i}\leq m^{4\epsilon},~\forall 1\leq i\leq s,

for some
s=O_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(1)
. Moreover, all the prime factors of any such
n_{i}
are
O(m^{\epsilon}),
and in
S_{1}\cup S_{2}.

(b)

In general if
f(z)
is of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}f_{i},\ c_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},f_{i}
are newforms without CM and
\sum\sigma_{i}c_{i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{m},\sigma_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}.
If none of the associated Galois representations
\rho_{f_{i_{1}},f_{i_{2}}\cdots,f_{i_{s}},m}
does not have image
\Delta_{k}^{(s)}(m)
for any subset

of
\{1,2,\cdots,r\}
with
\#I\geq 2.
Then for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},
we can write

\sum_{i=1}^{s}a(n_{i})\equiv a\pmod{m},~n_{i}\leq m^{4\epsilon},~\forall 1\leq i\leq s,

where
s
is the same as in part (a), all the prime factors of any such
n_{i}
are
O(m^{\epsilon})
, and are in
S_{1}\cup S_{2}.

One of the key tools to prove Theorem
2
is the following estimate on the bilinear exponential sums, which says

\mathrm{max}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\left|\sum_{a_{1}\in A_{1}}\sum_{a_{2}\in A_{2}}{\mathbf{e}}_{m}\left({\lambda a_{1}a_{2}}\right)\right|\leq\sqrt{m\#A_{1}\#A_{2}},

for any subsets
A_{1},A_{2}
of
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
. We shall briefly discuss the bilinear and multilinear exponential sums in Section
3
. Applying this unconditional exponential bound above and the
sum-product estimate
for subsets of finite fields, we can make everything explicit in the proof of Theorem
2
, and obtain the following.

Corollary 1
.

Let
f(z)
be any Hecke eigenform with rational coefficients, and
S_{1},S_{2}
be any set of primes having positive density with
S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\phi.
Then there exists an integer
N_{S_{1},S_{2}}
such that for any integer
m
with all prime factors larger than
N_{S_{1},S_{2}},
and
L^{1/77}\geq m/L,
where

is the largest prime factor of
m,
and for any

we can write

\sum_{i=1}^{s}a(n_{i})\equiv a\pmod{m},~n_{i}\leq m^{130/33},~\forall 1\leq i\leq 13,

for some

. Moreover, all the prime factors of any such

are
O(m^{65/66}),
and in
S_{1}\cup S_{2}.

Distribution over small sets

In this section, we will discuss the preliminary facts needed to prove the main results.

2.1.
Galois representations and image

Let
f(z)
be any newform of weight
k
and level
N
. From Deligne-Serre correspondence, we have an associated Galois representation

\rho_{f}^{(\ell)}:\text{Gal}\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\right),

such that
a(p)\pmod{\ell}\equiv\text{tr}\left(\rho_{f}^{(\ell)}(\text{Frob}_{p})\right)
for any prime
p\nmid N\ell.
It is clear that the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f}^{(\ell)}(\text{Frob}_{p})\pmod{\ell}
is same as
x^{2}-a(p)x+p^{k-1}\hskip-2.84544pt\pmod{\ell}
. When
f
is without CM it follows from Ribet
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
that, the image of this representation is given by

\mathrm{Im}\left(\rho_{f}^{\ell}\right)=\Delta_{k}(\ell)=\left\{A\in\text{GL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\right)\mid\det(A)\in(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{*})^{k-1}\right\},

except possibly for finitely many primes
\ell.
For any integer
e\geq 1,
we have a Galois representation

\rho_{f,\ell^{e}}:\text{Gal}\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{e}}\right)

satisfying that
a(p)\pmod{\ell^{e}}\equiv\text{tr}\left(\rho_{f,\ell^{e}}(\text{Frob}_{p})\right),
and

\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{f,\ell^{e}})=\Delta_{k}(\ell^{e}):=\{A\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{e}}\right)\mid\det(A)\in(\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{e}}^{*})^{k-1}\}.

Now, given any composite number
m
, we can naturally associate a Galois representation

\rho_{f,m}:=\prod_{\ell^{e}\mid\mid m}\rho_{f,\ell^{e}},

and denote
\mathrm{G}_{f,m}
to be its image. Due to Ribet’s result, we immediately have the following.

Corollary 2
.

Suppose that
f(z)
is any newform without CM. Then, there exists a finite set of primes
S_{f}
such that

\mathrm{G}_{m}=\Delta_{k}(m)=\{A\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\right)\mid\det(A)\in((\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*})^{k-1}\},

for any integer
m
co-prime to any prime from
S_{f}.

Consider the prime factorization
m=\prod_{\ell\mid m}\ell^{e}.
Take any residue class
a\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{e}\mathbb{Z}
and
\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{e}\mathbb{Z})^{*}.
Denote

be the number of matrices in
\Delta_{k}(\ell^{e})
of trace

and determinant
\lambda.
We know from
[

(1)

N_{a,\lambda}(\ell^{e})=\begin{cases}\ell^{2e},~\mathrm{if}~a^{2}-4\lambda=0.\\
\ell^{e}(\ell^{e}+1),~\mathrm{if}~a^{2}-4\lambda~\mathrm{is~a~non}\text{-}\mathrm{zero~square~in~\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{e}\mathbb{Z}}.\\
\ell^{e}(\ell^{e}-1)~\mathrm{if}~a^{2}-4\lambda~\mathrm{is~not~a~square~in~\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{e}\mathbb{Z}}.\end{cases}

Lemma 1
.

Let
m=\prod_{\ell\mid m}\ell^{e}
be any integer, and

By the Chinese remainder theorem, we can write

\Delta_{k}(m)=\prod_{\ell^{e}\mid\mid m}\Delta_{k}(\ell^{e}).

Therefore, it is enough to prove the result when

that
\mathrm{G}_{f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{r},m}
contains
(I,I,\cdots,\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}),I,\cdots,I)
and this completes the proof.
∎

Now we need one more lemma to prove the main result of this section.

Lemma 4
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{r}
by any pairwise twist-inequivalent newforms without CM of the same weight
k
. Then there exists a finite set of primes
S_{f}
such that,

\mathrm{G}_{f_{1},\cdots,f_{r},m}=\Delta_{k}^{(r)}(m):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&&\\
&A_{2}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&A_{r}\\
\end{pmatrix}\mid\det(A_{1})=\cdots=\det(A_{r})\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}^{*})^{k-1}\right\}.

for any integer
m
co-prime to any prime from
S_{f}.

Proof.

It follows from Corollary
2
, and by induction that each projection

\pi_{i}:\mathrm{G}_{f_{1},\cdots,f_{r},m}\to\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})

has the property that
\text{Im}(\pi_{i})=\Delta_{k}(m),~\forall 1\leq i\leq r.
For
r=2,
the result follows from the proof of Lemma 3.3 in
[
8
]
, combining with Lemma
3
. Note that we are using Lemma
3
to rule out the case (b) of Lemma 3.3 in
[
8
]
.

Now, by induction,

\mathrm{G}_{f_{1},\cdots,f_{r-1},m}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&&\\
&A_{2}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&A_{r}\\
\end{pmatrix}\mid\det(A_{1})=\cdots=\det(A_{r-1})\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}^{*})^{k-1}\right\}

and
\mathrm{G}_{f_{r},m}=\Delta_{k}(m).
If
\mathrm{G}_{f_{1},\cdots,f_{r},m}
does not have the desired image, then by Goursat’s lemma (Lemma 3.2 in
[
8
]
) there exists a normal subgroup
N_{1}
of
\mathrm{G}_{f_{1},\cdots,f_{r-1},m}
and a normal subgroup
N_{2}
of
\mathrm{G}_{f_{r},m}
and an isomorphism
\psi:\mathrm{G}_{f_{1},\cdots,f_{r-1},m}/N_{1}\to\mathrm{G}_{f_{r},m}/N_{2}
such that

\mathrm{G}_{f_{1},\cdots,f_{r},m}=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\mid\psi(g_{1}N_{1})=g_{2}N_{2}\}.

If
N_{2}
contains
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})
then clearly
N_{1}
is contained in
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{r-1}
, because

N_{1}\times\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\subseteq N_{1}\times N_{2}\subseteq\Delta_{k}^{(r)}(m).

Due to the isomorphism
\psi
, we have
N_{1}=\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{r-1}
and
N_{2}=\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}).
In particular,

\mathrm{G}_{f_{1},\cdots,f_{r-1},m}/N_{1}=\mathrm{G}_{f_{r},m}/N_{2}=(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}.

This implies that
\psi
must be the identity map, because
\mathrm{G}_{f_{1},\cdots,f_{r},m}
is the graph of
\psi,
which of course lies inside
\Delta^{(r)}_{k}(m).
The proof is now complete.
∎

With this, we are done with all the preparations to prove the main result of this section.

On the residue classes

Let
f(z)
be a cuspform with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q},
and
m
be any integer. In this section, we give a lower bound for the number of elements in the set
\{a(n)\pmod{m}\}_{n\in I},
where

is some small set. We know from section

\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},
when

is of a certain type. In this section, we shall consider a small set
I.
Shparlinski in
[
19

considered this for the Ramanujan-tau function. Arguing along the same lines, we first have the following generalization.

Lemma 6
.

Let
f(z)
be any Hecke eigenform, and
m
be any integer. For any set of primes
S,
consider

is the largest prime factor of

More precisely,

\#\{a(p)\pmod{m}\mid p\in S,~p\leq\sqrt{x}\}\gg_{S}x^{1/4+o(1)}

N_{f,L,S}(m^{2\varepsilon})\gg_{S}m^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+o(1)},

The proof is essentially the same as in
[
19
]
. It follows from the Hecke relation
a(p^{2})=a(p)^{2}-p^{k-1},

p^{k-1}\pmod{m},~p\leq\sqrt{x}\leq L
is
\gg\sqrt{x}
. Given any integer
m,

f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{r}

Lemma 7

If all of the

Corollary 3
.

Suppose that

are all newforms without CM. Let

If the set

in (

for a set of primes

On the other hand for any prime

P(\pm 1,\pm 1,\cdots,\pm 1)\not\equiv 0.
Note that the factor

is coming because an equation

.

Lemma 8

Let us consider

\displaystyle a_{i}(p^{2\beta})

Lemma 9

Let

4.
Waring-type for problems with modular forms

The main goal of this section is to extend the main result of Shparlinski for a large class of cusp forms and produce many other

s for the solutions. In certain cases, we also study the same problem modulo composite numbers. Let us first discuss the tools that will be used throughout.

4.1.
Exponential sums and Waring’s type problems.

Let

be any given integers, and
A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{\omega}

We then have the following counting formula,

T_{s}(a)=\frac{(\#A_{1}\#A_{2}\cdots\#A_{\omega})^{s}}{m}+O\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\lambda=1}^{m-1}\left|\sum_{a^{(1)}\in A_{1},\cdots,a^{(\omega)}\in A_{\omega}}{\mathbf{e}}_{m}\left({\lambda a^{(1)}a^{(2)}\cdots a^{(\omega)}}\right)\right|^{s}\right).

When

an old result mentioned in Exercise 14.a in Chapter 6 of
[

says that by replacing in the proof of Lemma 7 in
[
2
]

9
) in (

For
\omega=2

the sum

for any sufficiently large

When

Theorem 5

Let

, mentioned in the preamble of this section.

Now, for a proof of part
(b),
it follows from Lemma
4

f_{i},f_{j}
differ by a quadratic character. In particular, the result now follows the same argument as in the proof of Corollary
6

d=1

P=\sum c_{i}x_{i}
.

4.6.
Proof of Theorem
3

Let us first prove
(a).
Take
\varepsilon>0
be any given real, and
S_{1},S_{2},\cdots,S_{\omega}
be any pairwise disjoint set of primes of positive density, with
\varepsilon(2+\frac{\omega-2}{81})>2
. We studied the case
\omega=2
in Theorem
2
. The proof for
\omega>2
case follows similarly. It follows from Lemma
6
that for each
1\leq i\leq\omega,
we have

\#\{a(p)\pmod{L},p\in S_{i},p\leq m^{\varepsilon}\}~\mathrm{or}~\#\{a(p^{2})\pmod{L},p\in S_{i},p\leq m^{\varepsilon}\}\gg_{S_{i}}L^{\varepsilon/2+o(1)}.
Denote
A_{i}
to be one of the corresponding sets with larger cardinality, we have

\#A_{1}\#A_{2}(\prod_{3\leq i\leq\omega}\#A_{i})^{1/81}\gg_{S_{1},S_{2},\cdots,S_{\omega}}L^{\varepsilon(2+\frac{\omega-2}{81})/2+o(1)}.

Denoting

5.
Further questions and remarks

5.1.
Solution with primes

We are having some assumptions on the composite number
m
in both Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
. We would like to see if it is possible to remove them. We also ask if it is possible to obtain a solution to the equation

\sum_{i=1}^{O(1)}a(p_{i})\equiv a\pmod{m},~p_{i}\leq m^{O(1)},

where each
p_{i}
is a prime. Or at least, if
\{a(n)\pmod{m}\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega(n)=1\\
n\leq m^{O(1)}\end{subarray}}
is an additive basis for
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}.
Recall that Bajpai, García, and the first author studied this in
[
1
]
; however, their method does not give polynomial growth of the solutions. Note that we have obtained solutions with polynomial growth and
\omega
many prime factors for certain
m
.

5.2.
Sum of the polynomial values

Following the arguments in Section
3
one can study solvability of the equation
\sum\limits_{i=1}\limits^{O(1)}a(n_{i})^{d}\equiv a~\pmod{m},
as remarked by Shparlinski in
[
19
]
. However, Proposition
2
is giving the hope that it is also possible to study
\sum\limits_{i=1}\limits^{O(1)}p(a(n_{i}))\equiv a~\pmod{m},
for any polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x].
The only obstacle is that
p(f(n))
may not be multiplicative for any multiplicative function
f(n).
We also ask if there is a way to overcome this. Perhaps the most interesting situation is when
P
is of degree
1.
In that case,
a(n):=P(a_{1}(n),a_{2}(n),\cdots,a_{r}(n))
is Fourier coefficient of some modular form.

5.3.
On a larger family of cuspforms

We expect that it is possible to work with a larger class of cuspforms in Proposition
2
, at least when
m
is a prime
\ell
. We covered some other families in Corollary
3
. In this section, we shall discuss our heuristics for extending these families. This is because we expect the following to hold, under some suitable conditions, perhaps.

Question 1
.

Let
\ell
be any prime, and
L
be its any power. Is it true that for any tuples
(c_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r}\in\mathbb{F}_{L}^{r},~(a_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r+1}\in\mathbb{F}_{L}^{r+1},
the number of solutions to the equations

c_{1}x_{1}^{i}+c_{2}x_{2}^{i}+\cdots c_{r}x_{r}^{i}=a_{i},~\forall 1\leq i\leq r+1,

is at most
O_{r}(1)?

For instance, this is easily seen to be true when all the
c_{i}
are the same, using Newton’s identity. In general, we have a partial answer due to the following.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\ell
be any prime, and
L
be its any power. For any
(c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,c_{r})\in\mathbb{F}_{L}^{r},
consider
f_{i}=c_{1}x_{1}^{i}+c_{2}x^{i}\cdots c_{r}x_{r}^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{L}[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots x_{r}],
and
V
be the projective variety generated by
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{r}
. Then
\mathrm{dim}(V)=0
provided that
\sum\limits_{i\in S}c_{i}\neq 0
in
\mathbb{F_{\ell}}
for any
S\subseteq\{1,2,\cdots,r\}.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that there is no non-trivial prime ideal
I
in the coordinate ring of
V.
Suppose there is such a non-trivial prime ideal
I
. It follows from the identity

f_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}f_{r-i}
that
(\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i})x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{r}
in
I
, where
q_{i}
is
i^{\text{th}}
elementary symmetric polynomial in
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{r}
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\neq 0,
we may assume that
x_{r}\in I.
Then repeating the same argument, and keeping in mind that any sum
\sum_{i\in S}c_{i}\neq 0,
we have that
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{r}
all are in
I.
In particular, this shows that,

I=(f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{r})=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{r}),

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 7
.

The condition
\sum\limits_{i\in S}c_{i}\neq 0
is important. For instance,
\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq r}c_{i}=0,
implies that
V
contains the variety
(x_{1}-x_{r},x_{2}-x_{r},\cdots x_{r-1}-x_{r}).
In particular,
V
is of dimension at least
1.

As a consequence of Lemma
10
, we have a positive answer to Question
1
when all the
a_{i}
are equal. For proof, one may use
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
. We shall now see how helpful it is to have a complete answer to Question
1
. Let
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{r}
be any set of Hecke eigenforms and set

a_{P,\vec{f}}(n)=P(a_{1}(n),a_{2}(n),\cdots,a_{r}(n)),

where
P(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{r})
is a polynomial with
r
number of varriables.

Corollary 7
.

Let
\ell
be any given prime, then for any set of primes
S,
consider quantity,

N_{S,P,\vec{f}}(x)=\#\{a_{P,\vec{f}}(n)\pmod{\ell}\mid p~\mathrm{divides}~n\implies p\in S,~n\leq x\}.

Suppose that
S
has positive density, then we have the following estimate for any sufficiently large prime
\ell
, and any
\delta>0

N_{S,P,\vec{f}}(x)\geq\mathrm{min}\left\{\ell^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta},x^{\frac{1}{4r^{2}}-\delta}\right\}.

Proof.

Let us start with writing
a_{i}(p^{n})\pmod{\ell}=c_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n}+d_{i}\beta_{i}^{n},
where
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{2}}
, and suppose that
P
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
. Then
a_{f}(p^{n})
is a linear combination of

\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{nt_{i}}\beta_{i}^{n(d_{i}-t_{i})}\right\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t_{i}\leq d_{i}\\
\sum d_{i}=d\end{subarray}}.
Let
\vec{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{r})
appear as degrees of a monomial in
P
. For a fixed tuple
(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{r+1})\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{r+1},
let us now consider the number of primes
p
for which

(a_{P,\vec{f}}(p),a_{P,\vec{f}}(p^{2})\cdots,a_{P,\vec{f}}(p^{r+1}))\pmod{\ell}=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{r+1}).

It follows from our expectation in Question
1
that,
\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{t_{i}}\beta_{i}^{d_{i}-t_{i}}\right\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t_{i}\leq d_{i}\\
\sum d_{i}=d\end{subarray}},
is
O(1).
In particular,

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum d_{i}=d\\
\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{0\leq t_{i}\leq d_{i}}\alpha_{i}^{t_{i}}\beta_{i}^{d_{i}-t_{i}}=O(1).

Recall that
\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}=p^{k_{i}-1}\pmod{\ell}
, and hence

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum d_{i}=d\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{0\leq t_{i}\leq d_{i}}\alpha_{i}^{t_{i}}\beta_{i}^{d_{i}-t_{i}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum d_{i}=d\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}p^{(k_{i}-1)\frac{d_{i}(d_{i}+1)}{2}}\pmod{\ell}=O(1).

This is impossible since
S
is infinite, and any
k_{i}-1
is strictly positive. In particular, this shows that the number of primes
p
for which

(a_{P,\vec{f}}(p),a_{P,\vec{f}}(p^{2})\cdots,a_{P,\vec{f}}(p^{r+1}))\pmod{\ell}=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{r+1})

is
O(1).
The proof now follows, arguing similarly as in the proof of Proposition
2

∎

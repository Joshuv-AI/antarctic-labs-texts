Gravitational-wave tails of memory
David Trestini, Luc Blanchet

Abstract
Gravitational-wave tails are linear waves that backscatter on the curvature of space-time generated by the total mass-energy of the source. The non-linear memory effect arises from gravitational waves sourced by the stress-energy distribution of linear waves themselves. These two effects are due to quadratic multipolar interactions (mass-quadrupole and quadrupole-quadrupole) and are well known. Also known are the tails generated by tails themselves (cubic “tails-of-tails”) and the tails generated by tails-of-tails or vice versa (quartic “tails-of-tails-of-tails”). In this work, we focus on the cubic “tails-of-memory” corresponding to the mass-quadrupole-quadrupole interaction, as well as the “spin-quadrupole tails”, which are due to the cubic interaction between the mass, the total angular momentum and the quadrupole. The tails-of-memory and the spin-quadrupole tails contribute to the asymptotic waveform at the fourth-post-Newtonian (4PN) order beyond quadrupolar radiation.

Introduction

Due to the non-linear nature of general relativity, non-linear effects in the propagation of gravitational waves from their source to a distant detector play an important role in predictions for gravitational waves generated by compact binary systems

;
Thorne 1980
;

Blanchet and Sathyaprakash 1994
;
Blanchet and Sathyaprakash 1995
;
Arun et al. 2006a
;
Arun et al. 2006b
.

At leading order the tail effect is a quadratic coupling between the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (ADM) total mass of space-time and the mass quadrupole moment of the source. This effect appears at one-and-a-half post-Newtonian (1.5PN) order in the waveform beyond the Einstein quadrupole formula

;
Poisson 1993
;
Wiseman 1993
;
Blanchet and Schäfer 1993
;
Foffa and Sturani 2012
;

tail-of-tail
effect which is a cubic coupling between two ADM masses and the quadrupole
Blanchet 1998a
;
Faye et al. 2015
. The previous effects have been included into the PN templates for compact binary inspiral that are routinely used by the LIGO-Virgo detectors. Furthermore, at quartic order there is the
tail-of-tail-of-tail
which arises at 4.5PN order and involves three masses and a quadrupole
Marchand et al. 2016
;
Messina and Nagar 2017
.

The non-linear memory effect,
i.e.
the permanent change in the wave amplitude after the passage of a gravitational wave burst, is due to the re-radiation of quadrupole gravitational waves by a linear quadrupole wave and corresponds to the coupling between two quadrupole moments

The oscillatory (“AC”) piece of this interaction is routinely used by the LIGO-Virgo detector, but the genuine, secular memory effect (which is almost zero frequency or “DC”) has not yet been detected experimentally, but could be observed in the coming years with ground-based gravitational wave detectors

We shall compute the tails-of-memory using the multipolar-post-Minkowskian (MPM) formalism

;
Blanchet 1987

Finally, despite the fact that we are mainly motivated by the case of quasi-circular compact binaries, the results presented in this paper are valid for a generic isolated source described in terms of its multipole moments; the source could be a general hydrodynamical fluid with compact support, and it needs not even be a post-Newtonian source.

II
The radiative MPM algorithm

The vacuum field equations of general relativity are recast in the form of a wave like equation by defining the gothic metric deviation
h^{\mu\nu}\equiv\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}
, where
\eta^{\mu\nu}
is the flat Minkowski metric (in the mostly plus signature),
g^{\mu\nu}

\Box h^{\mu\nu}-\partial H^{\mu\nu}=\Lambda^{\mu\nu}\bigl[h,\partial h,\partial^{2}h\bigr]\,,

(1)

where
\Box

H^{\mu}\equiv\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}
and we introduce the useful shorthand notation

\partial\theta^{\mu\nu}\equiv 2\partial^{(\mu}\theta^{\nu)}-\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\rho}\theta^{\rho}

\theta^{\mu}
. The non-linear source term in vacuum reads explicitly

\Lambda^{\mu\nu}=\Lambda_{\text{harm}}^{\mu\nu}+2h^{\rho(\mu}\partial_{\rho}H^{\nu)}-\partial_{\rho}\left(h^{\mu\nu}H^{\rho}\right)\,,

(2)

where
\Lambda_{\text{harm}}^{\mu\nu}
is the source term in a harmonic gauge satisfying
H^{\mu}=0
and is given explicitly by Eq. (24) of
Blanchet 2014

1

\partial_{\nu}\partial H^{\mu\nu}=\Box H^{\mu}
, we find that the source term is identically divergenceless in any coordinate system,
i.e.

\partial_{\nu}\Lambda^{\mu\nu}=0
, which is equivalent to the contracted Bianchi identity.

Once the linearized solution is defined, the non-linear corrections are readily obtained by injecting the PM expansion into the field equations and solving these equations iteratively over the PM order. At any order

, the general equation to solve is

\Box h_{n}^{\mu\nu}-\partial H^{\mu\nu}_{n}=\Lambda_{n}^{\mu\nu}\,,

(7)

where
\Lambda_{n}^{\mu\nu}\equiv\Lambda^{\mu\nu}[h_{1},\cdots,h_{n-1}]

\Box v_{n}^{\mu\nu}=0

\partial_{\nu}v_{n}^{\mu\nu}=-w_{n}^{\mu}

\xi_{n}^{\mu\nu}=\mathop{\mathrm{FP}}_{B=0}\Box^{-1}_{\text{ret}}\biggl[\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\frac{k^{\mu}}{2r^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\sigma_{n}(u-\tau,\mathbf{n})\biggr]\,.

(11)

With this choice of gauge vector, one can prove by induction that the metric is free of far-zone logarithms at all post-Minkowskian orders

;
Trestini et al. 2022

III
Results at quadratic order

In the present paper we shall compute from first principles the following three
cubic
non-linear multipole interactions (
i.e.
,
n=3
in the PM expansion):

\mathrm{M}^{2}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\,,\qquad\d{M}\times\mathrm{S}_{i}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\quad\text{and}\quad\d{M}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\,.

The first one is known as the quadrupole “tail-of-tail”
Blanchet 1998a

\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{M}\,,\qquad\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{S}_{i}\,,\qquad\mathrm{M}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\,,\qquad\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{S}_{i}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\,,

which we shall compute at any distance
r
from the source, while the cubic interactions will just be computed asymptotically when
r\to+\infty
.

IV
Solution of the wave equation at cubic order

IV.1
General multipolar solution

To solve the problem of tails-of-memory we need to develop new techniques for integrating the wave equation when the source term is a complicated non-local functional of the moments such as (
23
). We first consider in a general way the wave equation whose source term admits a definite multipolarity
\ell
in STF guise, thus takes the general form

\Box\Psi_{L}=\hat{n}_{L}\,S(r,t-r)\,,

(26)

where
S(r,u)
is an arbitrary function of
r=|\mathbf{x}|
and
u=t-r
that verifies straightforward smoothness properties, and tends sufficiently rapidly to zero when
r\to 0
, namely

S(r,u)=\mathcal{O}(r^{\ell+5})\quad\text{(when $r\to 0$ with $u$ or $t$ held fixed)}\,.

(27)

See Theorem 6.1 of
Blanchet and Damour 1986
for more details on the required conditions we impose. Under these conditions, we know how to solve the wave equation (
26
). We first define

R_{\alpha}(\rho,s)\equiv\rho^{\ell}\int_{\alpha}^{\rho}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\frac{(\rho-\lambda)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\left(\frac{2}{\lambda}\right)^{\ell-1}S(\lambda,s)\,,

(28)

where
\alpha
is an arbitrary constant. Then the solution of (
26
) can be written as

\Psi_{L}=\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\,\hat{\partial}_{L}\left[\frac{R_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2},s\right)-R_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t+r-s}{2},s\right)}{r}\right]\,,

(29)

see Eq. (6.4) in
Blanchet and Damour 1986
. This solution is the unique retarded solution of (
26
), thus satisfying the no-incoming radiation condition when
r\to+\infty
with
t+r=
const. Furthermore it is independent from the constant
\alpha
. To see this we consider separately the two terms in (
29
), which we decompose as
\Psi_{L}=\Psi^{[1]}_{L}+\Psi^{[2]}_{L}
with

\displaystyle\Psi^{[1]}_{L}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\,\hat{\partial}_{L}\left[\frac{1}{r}R_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2},s\right)\right]\,,

(30a)

\displaystyle\Psi^{[2]}_{L}

\displaystyle\equiv-\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\,\hat{\partial}_{L}\left[\frac{1}{r}R_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t+r-s}{2},s\right)\right]\,.

(30b)

We see that
\Psi^{[1]}_{L}
is a retarded homogeneous solution of the wave equation,
\Box\Psi^{[1]}_{L}=0
, but that
\Psi^{[2]}_{L}
has a unwieldy dependency on the advanced time
v=t+r
, and represents a particular retarded solution of the wave equation,
i.e.
,
\Box\Psi^{[2]}_{L}=\hat{n}_{L}\,S(r,t-r)
. However note that the latter two wave equations satisfied by
\Psi^{[1]}_{L}
and
\Psi^{[2]}_{L}
separately are valid only in the sense of ordinary functions; only the wave equation (
26
) for the full solution
\Psi_{L}
is satisfied in the sense of distributions.
2
2

2

If
f
is a smooth function, then
\Box\left[r^{-1}f(t-r)\right]=-4\pi f(t)\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{x})
in the sense of distributions, but
\Box\left[r^{-1}f(t-r)\right]=0
in the sense of ordinary functions.

Plugging Eq. (
28
) into (
30
) we obtain

\displaystyle\Psi^{[1]}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell!}\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\,\hat{\partial}_{L}\!\left[\frac{1}{r}\int_{\alpha}^{\frac{t-r-s}{2}}\!\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2}\right)^{\ell}\!\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2}-\lambda\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{2}{\lambda}\right)^{\ell-1}\!\!S(\lambda,s)\right]\,,

(31a)

\displaystyle\Psi^{[2]}_{L}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\ell!}\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\,\hat{\partial}_{L}\!\left[\frac{1}{r}\int_{\alpha}^{\frac{t+r-s}{2}}\!\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\left(\frac{t+r-s}{2}\right)^{\ell}\!\left(\frac{t+r-s}{2}-\lambda\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{2}{\lambda}\right)^{\ell-1}\!\!S(\lambda,s)\right]\,.

(31b)

Next we remark that in both terms (
31
), one can commute the partial differential
operator
\hat{\partial}_{L}
with the integral over
\lambda
, since the terms coming from the differentiation of the bound of the integral,
i.e.

(t\pm r-s)/2
, have to be evaluated at
s=t\pm r
and will clearly vanish. Hence we can rewrite

\displaystyle\Psi^{[1]}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\ell-1}}{\ell!}\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\!\int_{\alpha}^{\frac{t-r-s}{2}}\!\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-\ell+1}S(\lambda,s)\,\hat{\partial}_{L}\!\left[\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2}\right)^{\ell}\,\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2}-\lambda\right)^{\ell}\right]\,,

(32a)

\displaystyle\Psi^{[2]}_{L}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2^{\ell-1}}{\ell!}\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\!\int_{\alpha}^{\frac{t+r-s}{2}}\!\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-\ell+1}S(\lambda,s)\,\hat{\partial}_{L}\!\left[\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{t+r-s}{2}\right)^{\ell}\!\left(\frac{t+r-s}{2}-\lambda\right)^{\ell}\right]\,.

(32b)

Then, using Eq. (A36) of
Blanchet and Damour 1986

\hat{\partial}_{L}\!\left[\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2}\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2}-\lambda\right)^{\ell}\right]=\hat{\partial}_{L}\!\left[\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{t+r-s}{2}\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{t+r-s}{2}-\lambda\right)^{\ell}\right]\,,

(33)

\Psi_{L}=-\frac{2^{\ell-1}}{\ell!}\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\!\int_{\frac{t-r-s}{2}}^{\frac{t+r-s}{2}}\!\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-\ell+1}S(\lambda,s)\,\hat{\partial}_{L}\!\left[\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2}\right)^{\ell}\,\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2}-\lambda\right)^{\ell}\right]\,.

(34)

This form is clearly independent of any choice for the constant
\alpha
. It was also derived in a different way, using a direct multipolar expansion of the Green function of the d’Alembertian operator, in Appendix D of
Blanchet and Damour 1986
.

By using Eq. (A35a) of
Blanchet and Damour 1986
we can expand explicitly the last factor containing the multipolar derivative operator. Introducing the following coefficients, which will become ubiquitous in our practical computations,

C_{ij}^{\ell}\equiv\frac{(\ell+i)!(\ell+j)!}{2^{i+j}i!j!(\ell-i)!(\ell-j)!(i+j)!}\\
\qquad\text{(for $0\leqslant i,j\leqslant\ell$)}\,,

(35)

we conveniently write the general solution as

\Psi_{L}=-\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\,\frac{(-)^{j}C_{ij}^{\ell}}{r^{i+1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\rho^{i+j}\int_{\frac{\rho}{2}}^{\frac{\rho}{2}+r}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-j+1}\,S(\lambda,u-\rho)\,.

(36)

Note that although the coefficient
C_{ij}^{\ell}
has been defined to be symmetric in

and
j
, these two indices play a different role. In particular the index

rules the behavior of the solution at infinity, when
r\to+\infty
with
t-r=
const, depending on the fall-off properties at infinity of the integrals in (
36
). A useful fact is that for
i=0
, the coefficient is straightforwardly linked to the
j
-th derivative of the usual Legendre polynomial evaluated at 1, namely

C_{0j}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{j!}P_{\ell}^{(j)}(1)\,.

(37)

In addition to the general formula (
36
), we also have an independent result which directly provides the leading behavior of the solution at infinity, when
r\to+\infty
with
u=
const
, depending on the fall-off properties of the source
S(r,u)
. Namely, suppose that
S(r,u)
has the following asymptotic behavior at infinity:

S(r,u)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\right)\quad\text{(when $r\to+\infty$ with $u$ fixed)}\,.

(38)

More precisely the rigourous fall-off conditions of the source term are given in Lemma 7.2 of
Blanchet and Damour 1986
. Then the corresponding solution will behave dominantly like
1/r
at infinity, with leading term explicitly given by

\Psi_{L}=\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}}\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{r}\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\,R_{\infty}^{(\ell)}\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2},s\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,.

(39)

Here the function
R_{\infty}(\rho,s)
is defined by (
28
) with the explicit choice
\alpha=+\infty
, and the superscript
(\ell)
means
\ell
partial derivatives with respect to
\rho
. We shall use the latter result as a consistency check of the derivation of the leading behavior of the solution at infinity.

B
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}B}\!\underset{k,m}{\ \ S^{B}_{L}}=\ln\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)\!\!\underset{k,m}{\ \ S^{B}_{L}}\,.

(43)

Applying the formulae of the previous section the solution for any
B\in\mathbb{C}
reads

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \Psi^{B}_{L}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{(-)^{j}C_{ij}^{\ell}}{r^{i+1}}\underset{k,m}{\phi}\!_{ij}^{B}\,,

(44a)

where

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\phi}\!_{ij}^{B}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}\rho\,\rho^{i+j}G(u-\rho)\int_{\frac{\rho}{2}}^{\frac{\rho}{2}+r}\!\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\left(\frac{\lambda}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\lambda^{-k-j+1}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)F\bigl[u-\rho-\lambda(x-1)\bigr]\,.

(44b)

Note that this solution is “exact”, valid at any radial distance
r
except
r=0
. But in the following, we shall mostly be interested in the asymptotic limit when
r\to+\infty
with
u=
const
. Permuting the integrals, introducing the change of variable
\lambda\longrightarrow\tau=\lambda(x-1)
, and factorizing out the expected leading behavior
1/r
of the solution (in anticipation of the limit
r\to+\infty
), we find that

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \Psi^{B}_{L}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{2r}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,G(u-\rho)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,F(u-\rho-\tau)\underset{k,m}{\ K^{B}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)\,,

(45a)

where we have introduced for convenience the kernel function (with
x=y+1
)

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ K^{B}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\tau}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\frac{1}{r^{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}(-)^{j}C_{ij}^{\ell}\,\rho^{i+j}\,\tau^{-k-j+1}\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{k+j-2-B}Q_{m}(y+1)\,,

\displaystyle=\tau^{1-k}\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\left(\frac{\tau}{yr_{0}}\right)^{B}y^{k-2}Q_{m}(y+1)\,\Pi_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau},1+\frac{\rho}{r}\right)\,.

(45b)

In the second line we have introduced the following symmetric bivariate polynomial

\Pi_{\ell}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}C^{\ell}_{ij}(x-1)^{i}(y-1)^{j}\,,

(46)

which is related to the Legendre polynomial, recalling (
37
), by

P_{\ell}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}C^{\ell}_{0j}\left(x-1\right)^{j}=\Pi_{\ell}(x,1)=\Pi_{\ell}(1,x)\,.

(47)

Similarly, the quantity
{}_{k,m}\chi_{L}^{B}
defined in (
42
) reads

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \chi^{B}_{L}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{2r}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,G(u-\rho)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,F(u-\rho-\tau)\underset{k,m}{\ L^{B}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)\,,

(48a)

where the kernel is defined as

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ L^{B}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}B}\biggl[\underset{k,m}{\ K^{B}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)\biggr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\frac{1}{r^{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}(-)^{j}C_{i,j}^{\ell}\,\rho^{i+j}\,\tau^{-k-j+1}\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\left(\frac{\tau}{yr_{0}}\right)^{B}\,\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{r_{0}y}\right)y^{k+j-2}Q_{m}(x)\,,

\displaystyle=\tau^{1-k}\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\left(\frac{\tau}{yr_{0}}\right)^{B}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{yr_{0}}\right)y^{k-2}Q_{m}(y+1)\,\Pi_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau},1+\frac{\rho}{r}\right)\,.

(48b)

When
r\to+\infty
the above kernels are dominated by the contribution
i=0
, and the property (
47
) allows us to relate the asymptotic limit to the Legendre polynomial as

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ K^{B}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{1-k}\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\left(\frac{\tau}{yr_{0}}\right)^{B}y^{k-2}Q_{m}(y+1)\,P_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau}\right)+o\left(1\right)\,,

(49a)

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ L^{B}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{1-k}\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\left(\frac{\tau}{yr_{0}}\right)^{B}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{yr_{0}}\right)y^{k-2}Q_{m}(y+1)\,P_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau}\right)+o\left(1\right)\,.

(49b)

We employ the Landau symbol
o
for remainders, hence
o(1)
means terms that behave as
\sim\ln^{p}r/r
with uncontrolled powers of
\ln r
as
r\rightarrow+\infty
.

IV.2.1
Cases where
k=1
and
k=2

In this subsection, we assume that
k\in\{1,2\}
. As proven in Appendix
B
,
{}_{k,m}\,\Psi^{B}_{L}
and
{}_{k,m}\,\chi^{B}_{L}
have a well-defined limit when
B\rightarrow 0
, so we can drop the finite part prescription and simply set
B=0
in (
45
) and (
48
). Therefore we obtain the expression for the kernels, only valid for
k\in\{1,2\}
,

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ K_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)

\displaystyle\equiv\tau^{1-k}\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{k-2}Q_{m}(y+1)\,\Pi_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau},1+\frac{\rho}{r}\right)\,,

(50a)

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ L_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)

\displaystyle\equiv\tau^{1-k}\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{yr_{0}}\right)y^{k-2}Q_{m}(y+1)\,\Pi_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau},1+\frac{\rho}{r}\right)\,.

(50b)

When restricting our interest to the asympotic limit
r\rightarrow+\infty
, we must treat separately the cases
k=1
and
k=2
.

For
k=2
, the integrands in the kernels (
50
) are clearly integrable at the
y\rightarrow 0
bound, therefore the kernels converge in the
r\rightarrow+\infty
limit, such that we are allowed to define the “asymptotic” kernels:

\displaystyle\underset{2,m}{\ \overline{K}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau)

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{r\to+\infty}\underset{2,m}{\ K_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)=\tau^{-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,Q_{m}(y+1)P_{\ell}\Bigl(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau}\Bigr)\,,

(51a)

\displaystyle\underset{2,m}{\ \overline{L}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau)

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{r\to+\infty}\underset{2,m}{\ L_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)=\tau^{-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{yr_{0}}\right)Q_{m}(y+1)P_{\ell}\Bigl(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau}\Bigr)\,.

(51b)

We have verified the above asymptotic limit in the case where
k=2
using the general statement in Eq. (
39
), where the relevant function is defined by Eq. (
28
) with
\alpha=+\infty
.

In the case
k=1
, the situation is more complicated since the integrands of the kernels are no longer integrable when
y\to 0
, so it is not possible to simply take the limit
r\to+\infty
like in (
51
). Instead the kernels exhibit a logarithmic behavior as
r\to+\infty
. Since the radiative construction will not exhibit any logarithmic behavior, the logarithms should cancel out in the final metric, and it is crucial to verify this by controlling the logarithmic limit of the kernel functions. We detail the case of the “
K
”-kernel

\underset{1,m}{\ K_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)=\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\,Q_{m}(y+1)\,\Pi_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau},1+\frac{\rho}{r}\right)\,.

(52)

To extract the logarithmic behavior, we integrate by parts. First, we introduce the regular part of the Legendre function of the second type when
x\to 1^{+}
, which is defined as

R_{m}(x)\equiv Q_{m}(x)+\frac{1}{2}P_{m}(x)\ln\left(\frac{x-1}{2}\right)\,.

(53)

A useful observation is that
R_{m}(1)=-H_{m}
, where
H_{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}k^{-1}
is the harmonic number. Substituting
Q_{m}
by its expression in terms of
R_{m}
and integrating by parts, we find

\displaystyle\underset{1,m}{\ K_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)

\displaystyle=\Bigg[\ln\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\biggl(R_{m}(y+1)-\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)P_{m}(y+1)\biggr)\Pi_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau},1+\frac{\rho}{r}\right)\Bigg]_{y=\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{y=\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}

\displaystyle-\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\ln\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\bigg[R_{m}(y+1)\,\Pi_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau},1+\frac{\rho}{r}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\ln^{2}\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\bigg[P_{m}(y+1)\,\Pi_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau},1+\frac{\rho}{r}\right)\bigg]\,,

(54)

where the all-integrated terms are shown in the first line. Since
P_{m}(x)
,
R_{m}(x)
and
\Pi_{\ell}(x,y)
as well as all their derivatives are perfectly integrable as
x\rightarrow 1^{+}
, we can safely take the
r\to+\infty
expansion, and we obtain the explicit “polylogarithmic” structure

\displaystyle\underset{1,m}{\ K_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)\bigg[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+2H_{m}\bigg]+\underset{1,m}{\ \overline{K}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau)+o\left(1\right)\,,

(55a)

where
{}_{1,m}\overline{K}_{\ell}(\rho,\tau)
does not exhibit any
r
-dependence. We obtain its explicit expression as

\displaystyle\underset{1,m}{\ \overline{K}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+H_{m}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{4}\left[\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)-2\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\ln\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)+\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)\right]P_{m}\left(1+\frac{2\tau}{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle-(-)^{\ell}\left[\ln\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)-\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\right]R_{m}\left(1+\frac{2\tau}{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\ln^{2}\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\bigg[P_{m}(y+1)\,P_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\ln\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\bigg[R_{m}(y+1)\,P_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau}\right)\bigg]\,.

(55b)

The same reasoning applied to the “
L
”-kernel also gives a polylogarithmic structure but in this case with powers of the logarithm up to three:

\displaystyle\underset{1,m}{\ L_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\ln^{3}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)\left[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+2H_{m}\right]+\underset{1,m}{\ \overline{L}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau)+o\left(1\right)\,,

(56a)

and where
{}_{1,m}\overline{L}_{\ell}(\rho,\tau)
is explicitly given by

\displaystyle\underset{1,m}{\ \overline{L}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}\ln^{3}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}H_{m}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)

(56b)

\displaystyle-\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{12}\left[\ln^{3}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)-3\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)+2\ln^{3}\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)\right]P_{m}\left(1+\frac{2\tau}{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{2}\left[\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)-\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\right]R_{m}\left(1+\frac{2\tau}{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\ln^{2}\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\left[\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)-\frac{1}{6}\ln\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\bigg[P_{m}(y+1)\,P_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\ln\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\left[-\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\bigg[R_{m}(y+1)\,P_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{\rho y}{\tau}\right)\bigg]\,.

Again, a beautiful check of the radiative construction of the metric in Sec.
II
, is that all these explicitly determined far zone logarithms will be compensated by those induced by the applied gauge transformations, notably the one described in (
19
).

In practical computations, it is easier to compute the full kernels
{}_{1,m}K_{\ell}(\rho,\tau,r)
and
{}_{1,m}L_{\ell}(\rho,\tau,r)
, and then to remove the logarithmic dependencies to obtain the reduced asymptotic kernels
{}_{1,m}\overline{K}_{\ell}(\rho,\tau)
and
{}_{1,m}\overline{L}_{\ell}(\rho,\tau)
. However, computing the two later quantities directly from their explicit expressions (
55
) and (
56b
) yields, of course, the same result.

IV.2.2
Cases where
k\geqslant 3

In the previous subsection we could integrate the source term (
40
) when
k\in\{1,2\}
.
A priori
, the cases
k\geqslant 3
are more difficult and (when
G
is not constant) analytic closed-form expressions for the retarded integral seem non trivial. Hence we proceed differently and prove that we can retrieve the cases
k\geqslant 3
from the known cases
k\in\{1,2\}
. This will show that analytic closed-form expressions also exist in the cases
k\geqslant 3
.

Given a source term with
k\geqslant 3
, endowed with the associated regularization factor
r^{B}
(posing here
r_{0}=1
), we reduce it to a fully integrated part and new source terms with decreased values of
k
by two steps at most:
k-1
and
k-2
, by means of the identity:

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{L}r^{B-k}G(t-r)\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}xQ_{m}(x)F(t-rx)

\displaystyle\qquad=\Box\left[\frac{\hat{n}_{L}r^{B-k+2}}{(k+\ell-2-B)(k-\ell-3-B)}G(t-r)\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)F(t-rx)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{2(k-3-B)\hat{n}_{L}r^{B-k+1}}{(k+\ell-2-B)(k-\ell-3-B)}\bigg(\overset{(1)}{G}(t-r)\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)F(t-rx)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\ G(t-r)\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,x\,Q_{m}(x)\overset{(1)}{F}(t-rx)\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{\hat{n}_{L}r^{B-k+2}}{(k+\ell-2-B)(k-\ell-3-B)}\bigg(2\,\overset{(1)}{G}(t-r)\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,(x-1)Q_{m}(x)\overset{(1)}{F}(t-rx)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+G(t-r)\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,(x^{2}-1)\,Q_{m}(x)\overset{(2)}{F}(t-rx)\bigg)\,.

(57)

This formula, when applied iteratively, allows us to reduce any case
k\geqslant 3
in terms of the cases when
k=1
and
2
, modulo a series of all-integrated terms (
i.e.
, inside the d’Alembertian operator). Note that we introduced a new type of integral,
\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{n}\,Q_{m}(x)H(t-rx)
where
n\in\mathbb{N}
, but which can easily be recast in the previous form (
i.e.

n=0
) by recursively applying Bonnet’s recursion formula for the Legendre function, namely

\displaystyle x\,Q_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{m+1}{2m+1}Q_{m+1}(x)+\frac{m}{2m+1}Q_{m-1}(x)

\displaystyle\text{if $m\geqslant 1$}\,,

(58a)

\displaystyle x\,Q_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=Q_{1}(x)+1

\displaystyle\text{if $m=0$}\,.

(58b)

An important point is that we keep the Hadamard regulator
r^{B}
“alive” in (
IV.2.2
). Indeed, by applying the inverse d’Alembertian operator on both sides of (
IV.2.2
), we see that the first term in the right side being the d’Alembertian of a source term containing the regulator
r^{B}
, will directly yield that source term proportional to
r^{B}
without any additional homogeneous solution (since no homogeneous solution can be proportional to
r^{B}
). Hence the iteration with
r^{B}
can be done blindly, ignoring homogeneous solutions, and only at the end do we apply the finite part when
B\to 0
.

However a price we have to pay is that the
B
-dependent coefficients in (
IV.2.2
) can generate a simple pole when
B\to 0
which will compete with the higher contribution
\propto B
in the retarded integral, and
vice versa
, a term
\propto B
in the coefficient will be compensated by a pole coming from the retarded integral. A useful fact to remember in this respect is that the integrals are convergent for
k\in\{1,2\}
and hence no poles
\propto 1/B
can be generated in these cases. Furthermore, the structure of the identity (
IV.2.2
) can only generate simple poles
\propto 1/B
, and no double poles
\propto 1/B^{2}
or any poles of higher order. The presence of these simple poles is the main reason why we introduced the
{}_{k,m}\chi_{L}
integral, as is clear by its first definition in (
42
).

Finally, although we now have all the needed formulae to integrate the tails-of-memory, our recursion formula (
IV.2.2
) coupled to the
m=0
case of the Bonnet formula (
58b
) can generate instantaneous terms in the source, which must also be integrated. Although the integration of such terms is well known using standard integration techniques
Blanchet and Damour 1992
;
Blanchet 1998b
, its generalization to the case where simple poles
\propto 1/B
can appear in the source was unknown.

IV.3
Application to tails-of-tails

The previous observation motivates us to extend our formalism developed for complicated tails-of-memory to the easier cases of tails-of-tails, tails and even instantaneous terms, such that we can treat the entire problem consistently, using one single formalism. When comparing with previous works
Blanchet 1998a
;
Marchand et al. 2016
this will provide important tests of the results of Sec.
IV.2
. Thus, we specialize the formulae of the previous section to the case where
G\equiv 1
, since in the case of the tails-of-tails two of the moments are just the mass
\mathrm{M}
.

When
k\geqslant 3
, we can again reduce the source to the cases
k=1
and
k=2
using (
IV.2.2
), with now
G^{(1)}(u)=0
. Then, after lengthy computations where we perform two of the three integrations of the tails-of-memory (one on the
y
variable in the kernel and one on the time variable
\tau
), we find drastically simpler expressions when
k\in\{1,2\}
, which read

\displaystyle\underset{2,m}{\ \overline{\Psi}_{\ell}}{\bigg|}_{G=1}

\displaystyle=2\,\underset{{2,m}}{\alpha_{\ell}}\overset{(-1)}{F}(u)\,,

(59a)

\displaystyle\underset{2,m}{\ \overline{\chi}_{\ell}}{\bigg|}_{G=1}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\left[\underset{{2,m}}{\beta_{\ell}}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\underset{{2,m}}{\gamma_{\ell}}\right]F(u-\tau)\,,

(59b)

\displaystyle\underset{1,m}{\ \overline{\Psi}_{\ell}}{\bigg|}_{G=1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overset{(-1)}{F}(u-\tau)\left[\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+4H_{m}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)-8\underset{{1,m}}{\delta_{\ell}}\right]\,,

(59c)

\displaystyle\underset{1,m}{\ \overline{\chi}_{\ell}}{\bigg|}_{G=1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overset{(-1)}{F}(u-\tau)\bigg[\ln^{3}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+6H_{m}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)-24\underset{{1,m}}{\delta_{\ell}}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+12\underset{{1,m}}{\varepsilon_{\ell}}\bigg]\,.

(59d)

where
{}_{k,m}{\overline{\Psi}_{L}}\equiv-\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{2r}\ {}_{k,m}{\overline{\Psi}_{\ell}}
and
{}_{k,m}{\overline{\chi}_{L}}\equiv-\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{2r}\ {}_{k,m}{\overline{\chi}_{\ell}}
.
The numerical constants
{}_{2,m}\alpha_{\ell}
,
{}_{2,m}\beta_{\ell}
and
{}_{2,m}\gamma_{\ell}
are defined by the integrals

\displaystyle\underset{2,m}{\ \alpha_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\equiv\underset{2,m}{\ \beta_{\ell}}\equiv\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)Q_{\ell}(x)\,,

(60a)

\displaystyle\underset{2,m}{\ \gamma_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2^{\ell+1}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{(1-z^{2})^{\ell}}{(x-y)^{\ell+1}}\left(-\ln\left(\frac{x-y}{2}\right)+H_{\ell}\right)\,.

(60b)

Note that
{}_{2,m}\alpha_{\ell}
is a special case of
\,{}_{k,m}\alpha_{\ell}
defined for
2\leqslant k\leqslant\ell+2
in (A16) of
Blanchet 1998a
, whereas
\,{}_{2,m}\beta_{\ell}
and
\,{}_{2,m}\gamma_{\ell}
are an extension of (A20) of
Blanchet 1998a
, in which these constants were defined only for
k\geqslant\ell+3
. In the case
k=2
, we find it natural to define
{}_{2,m}\alpha_{\ell}
and
{}_{2,m}\beta_{\ell}
to be equal.

Similarly one can define
{}_{1,m}\delta_{\ell}
and
{}_{1,m}\varepsilon_{\ell}
using formal integrals but at the price of introducing a Hadamard “partie finie” (pf) to cure the bound of the integrals at
x=1
:

\displaystyle\underset{1,m}{\ \delta_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathrm{pf}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}Q_{\ell}}{\mathrm{d}x}\,,

(61a)

\displaystyle\underset{1,m}{\ \varepsilon_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathrm{pf}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}S_{\ell}}{\mathrm{d}^{2}x}\,.

(61b)

The Hadamard partie finie is defined in the usual way by removing the divergent part of the integral (and using for our purpose here a Hadamard scale equal to 1):

\displaystyle\mathrm{pf}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}Q_{\ell}}{\mathrm{d}x}\equiv

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\left\{\int_{1+\eta}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}Q_{\ell}}{\mathrm{d}x}+\frac{1}{8}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}H_{m}\ln\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\right\}\,,

(62a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{pf}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}S_{\ell}}{\mathrm{d}^{2}x}\equiv

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\left\{\int_{1+\eta}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}S_{\ell}}{\mathrm{d}^{2}x}+\frac{1}{6}\ln^{3}\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}H_{m}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\right\}\,.

(62b)

In the definition of
{}_{1,m}\varepsilon_{\ell}
the function
S_{\ell}

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{L}r^{B-k}F(t-r)

\displaystyle=\Box\left[\frac{\hat{n}_{L}r^{B-k+2}}{(k+\ell-2-B)(k-\ell-3-B)}F(t-r)\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ -\frac{2(k-3-B)\hat{n}_{L}r^{B-k+1}}{(k+\ell-2-B)(k-\ell-3-B)}\overset{(1)}{F}(t-r)\,.

\mathop{\mathrm{FP}}_{B=0}\Box^{-1}_{\text{ret}}\biggl[\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{r^{2}}F(t-r)\biggr]=\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{2r}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,F(u-\tau)\Bigl[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r}\right)+2H_{\ell}\Bigr]+o\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\,,

(69)

V
Implementing the calculation of tails-of-memory

V.1
Explicit integration of the asymptotic kernels

Up to this point, the kernels in the asymptotic limit
r\to+\infty
were defined only in an integral form, given by (
51
) in the relatively easy case where
k=2
, and by the more complex forms (
55
) and (
56b
) when
k=1
. These integrals are too complicated to seek a general explicit formula valid for arbitrary
m
and
\ell
. However, we can easily compute all these integrals on a case-by-case basis. For this it suffices to insert into them the explicit expressions of the Legendre polynomial
P_{m}(x)
and the Legendre function of the second kind
Q_{m}(x)
, which are simply polynomials multiplied by some logarithms, see (
16
).

In this way we find that the general structure of the kernels
{}_{k,m}K_{\ell}(\rho,\tau,r)
and
{}_{k,m}L_{\ell}(\rho,\tau,r)
for
k=1
and
2
, up to
o(1)
precision, is of the type

\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ K_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)\\[19.91684pt]
\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ L_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau,r)\end{array}\right)=\sum_{s,p,q}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \mathcal{X}}\!\!{}_{\,s,p,q}\\[19.91684pt]
\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \mathcal{Y}}\!\!{}_{\,s,p,q}\end{array}\right)\int_{\frac{2\tau}{\rho+2r}}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{s}\ln^{p}\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\ln^{q}\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right)\,,

(70)

where
{}_{k,m}\mathcal{X}_{s,p,q}
and
{}_{k,m}\mathcal{Y}_{s,p,q}
denote some numerical coefficients, with
s
an integer such that
s\geqslant k-2
, and where we can restrict our attention only to the values
p=0,1,2,3
and
q=0,1
. When
s\geqslant 0
, the lower bound of the integral can be set to
0
in the
r\to+\infty
limit, but not when
s=-1
which can happen when
k=1
. In the latter case, the integral will develop the logarithmic behavior when
r\to+\infty
which has already been obtained in (
55a
) and (
56a
), and we now only consider the finite part.

When
q=0
, these integrals are of course well-known (see
e.g.
2.721-722 in
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 1980
), so we now restrict our discussion to the case
q=1
. Let us first examine the case
q=1
and
s\geqslant 0
; we thus set the lower bound to zero, in the limit
r\to+\infty
. We perform an integration by parts using 2.722 in
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 1980
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}y\,y^{s}\ln^{p}\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\ln\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right)

(71)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\frac{(-)^{i}p!}{(p-i)!(s+1)^{i+1}}\Bigg\{\left(\frac{2\tau}{\rho}\right)^{s+1}\!\ln^{p-i}\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)\ln\left(1+\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)-\int_{0}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{y^{s+1}}{y+2}\ln^{p-i}\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\Bigg\}\,.

The rational fraction
y^{s+1}(y+2)^{-1}
in the remaining integral can be expanded as a polynomial, plus a function proportional to
(y+2)^{-1}
. From this result we see that the only non trivial integrals left are of the type
\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{d}y\,(y+2)^{-1}\ln^{j}\left(y/2\right)
, and these are simply related to the polylogarithm functions, for instance
3
3

3

The polylogarithm, or Jonquière’s function, is defined for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z>0
as

\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)\equiv\frac{(-)^{n-1}}{(n-2)!}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\,\ln^{n-2}\left(\frac{s}{z}\right)\ln(1-s)\,.

In particular, the dilogarithm, or Spence’s function, is defined as
\mathrm{Li}_{2}(z)=-\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\,\ln(1-s)
. We also find that
\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z)=-\ln(1-z)
.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y+2}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{Li}_{3}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)+2\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)-\mathrm{Li}_{1}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)\,.

(72)

Now, the only remaining cases are when
q=1
and
s=-1
, but in these cases too the integrals are related to polylogarithms, for instance

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{2\tau}{\rho}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\ln\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\ln\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right)=\mathrm{Li}_{3}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)-\,\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)\,.

(73)

To summarize we have obtained the following general structure of the kernel functions in the asymptotic limit
r\to+\infty
, discarding the leading logarithmic behavior in the
k=1
case — recall (
55a
) and (
56a
) —, as

\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \overline{K_{\ell}}}(\rho,\tau)\\[19.91684pt]
\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \overline{L_{\ell}}}(\rho,\tau)\end{array}\right)=\tau^{1-k}\sum_{\scriptsize{\begin{matrix}j\in\mathbb{Z}\\
p,q,n\in\mathbb{N}\end{matrix}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \kappa}{}_{j,p,q,n}\\[19.91684pt]
\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \lambda}{}_{j,p,q,n}\end{array}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)^{j}\ln^{p}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\ln^{q}\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)\,\overline{\mathrm{Li}}_{n}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)\,,

(74)

with some numerical coefficients
{}_{k,m}\kappa_{j,p,q,n}
and
{}_{k,m}\lambda_{j,p,q,n}
that depend on the relative integer
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and natural integers
p,q,n\in\mathbb{N}
, and where we have defined for notational convenience
\overline{\mathrm{Li}}_{0}(z)\equiv 1
and
\overline{\mathrm{Li}}_{n}(z)\equiv\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)
for
n\geqslant 1
. We also recall that
\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z)=-\ln(1-z)
.

The coefficients
{}_{k,m}\kappa_{j,p,q,n}
and
{}_{k,m}\lambda_{j,p,q,n}
represent six-dimensional matrices of numerical coefficients and cannot be presented. Instead, we provide the explicit expressions of all the kernels needed for the computation of the tails-of-memory in the Supplementary Material
Sup
. For the purpose of clarity, we give two explicit examples of such kernels, where we choose to look at
\overline{K}
for
m=2
,
\ell=4
and
k=1,2
:

\displaystyle\underset{1,2}{\ \overline{K_{4}}}(\rho,\tau)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)+\frac{7}{12}\,\mathrm{Li}_{1}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{7}{12}\ln\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{25}{12}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{9}{4}-\frac{7}{12}\,\frac{\rho}{\tau}+\frac{35}{24}\left(\frac{\rho}{\tau}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\rho}{\tau}\right)^{3}\left[\frac{7}{3}\,\mathrm{Li}_{1}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)+\frac{7}{4}\right]+\frac{7}{4}\,\left(\frac{\rho}{\tau}\right)^{4}\mathrm{Li}_{1}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)\,,

(75a)

\displaystyle\underset{2,2}{\ \overline{K_{4}}}(\rho,\tau)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{\tau}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{6}-\frac{5}{6}\,\frac{\rho}{\tau}+\left(\frac{\rho}{\tau}\right)^{2}\left[-3\,\mathrm{Li}_{1}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)-5\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\frac{\rho}{\tau}\right)^{3}\left[-7\,\mathrm{Li}_{1}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)-4\right]-4\left(\frac{\rho}{\tau}\right)^{4}\,\mathrm{Li}_{1}\left(-\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)\Bigg\}\,.

(75b)

Notice that despite the structure in
\rho/\tau
of these kernels, one can check explicitly that they are indeed integrable in a vicinity of
(\rho,\tau)=(0,0)
.

V.2
Raw expression of the radiative quadrupole in the radiative construction

We have applied the MPM construction of the metric in radiative gauge as described in Sec.
II
, together with the previous integration techniques, to the computation of the three cubic interactions
\mathrm{M}^{2}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}
,
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{S}_{i}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}
and
\mathrm{M}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}
.

The first interaction,
\mathrm{M}^{2}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}
, is the tail-of-tail which enters at 3PN order and was already known in the harmonic construction
Blanchet 1998a
. We have, on the one hand, computed the asymptotic waveform for this interaction in the radiative construction using the integration machinery developed in Sec.
IV
, and, on the other hand, used standard integration techniques
Blanchet 1998b
;
Blanchet 1998a
;
Faye et al. 2015
;
Marchand et al. 2016
applied to the radiative algorithm, and checked that both methods yielded
identical results. We then verified that these results, performed in the radiative construction, could be independently retrieved from the known result in the harmonic construction
Blanchet 1998a
solely using a moment redefinition, as explained in Ref.
Trestini et al. 2022
. In the radiative gauge, we find

\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\mathrm{M}^{2}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}}=\frac{2G^{2}\mathrm{M}^{2}}{c^{6}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}\tau\left[\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{107}{42}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{40037}{8810}\right]\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}^{(5)}(u-\tau)\,.

(76)

For the
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{S}_{i}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}
interaction, which only enters at 4PN, the fact that the current dipole moment
\mathrm{S}_{i}
(or total angular momentum) is constant greatly simplifies the computation, and
a priori
allows for the use of standard techniques
Blanchet 1998b
;
Blanchet 1998a
;
Marchand et al. 2016
. However, in some cases, those techniques break down, since some of the numerical constants introduced are ill-defined, as has been mentioned in
Marchand et al. 2016
. Therefore, we have computed for the first time the full
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{S}_{i}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}
waveform using our new integration method, which reads in radiative gauge,

\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{S}_{i}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}}=-\frac{2G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{c^{8}}\,\mathrm{S}_{a|\langle i}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\left[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{5381}{5670}\right]\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(6)}(u-\tau)\,.

(77)

Finally, the
\mathrm{M}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}
interaction is the genuine tail-of-memory entering at 4PN; the double time-dependence within the two associated quadrupole moments strongly complicates the situation. We have performed this calculation only in the asymptotic limit
r\to+\infty
. Since we are using the radiative construction
Blanchet 1987
the waveform is automatically free of far-zone logarithms, and can straightforwardly be projected in a TT gauge, where the radiative quadrupole moment
\mathcal{U}_{ij}
is extracted in a standard way. Here we first present the “raw” result, obtained in a direct manner from our integration formulae, in terms of the radiative canonical moments. In Sec.
V.3
we describe a very efficient simplification method, and the resulting radiative quadrupole will be presented in Sec.
VI
in terms of the harmonic canonical moments. The “raw” result is however important and worthy to be presented, as it is the direct result of our integration scheme.

In order to present the raw result, we define the following functionals of two time-derivatives of quadrupole moments
F
and
G
for the cases
k=1,2
:

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \overline{\Psi}_{\ell}}[F,G]

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,G(u-\rho)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,F(u-\rho-\tau)\underset{k,m}{\ \overline{K}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau)\,,

(78a)

\displaystyle\underset{k,m}{\ \overline{\chi}_{\ell}}[F,G]

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,G(u-\rho)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,F(u-\rho-\tau)\underset{k,m}{\ \overline{L}_{\ell}}(\rho,\tau)\,,

(78b)

where the kernels are given by (
51
) in the case
k=2
and by Eqs. (
55
) and (
56b
) in the case
k=1
. As explained in Sec.

we do not need the cases
k\geqslant 3
.

With these definitions in hand, we decompose the cubic interaction
\mathrm{M}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}
as follows: we separate terms which are purely instantaneous (
i.e.
local-in-time), from terms whose non-locality is rather simple and looks like that for the ordinary tails or tails-of-tail, and finally from the genuine and much more intricated tail-of-memory (ToM) integrals. We thus write our raw result, expressed in terms of the radiative canonical moments, as

(79)

1.

The instantaneous terms (depending only on the current time
u

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\mathrm{M}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}}{\bigg|}_{\text{inst}}

\displaystyle=\frac{G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{c^{8}}\biggl[\frac{3362032}{165375}\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}^{(3)}\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(3)}+\frac{2014871}{165375}\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}^{(2)}\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(4)}

(80a)

2.

The non-local tail like terms involve some logarithmic kernels, and naturally the quadrupole moment of the usual tail terms is here replaced by a combination

(80b)

3.

The genuine tail-of-memory part is fully specified by the bilinear functionals of the two quadrupole moments defined by (

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\mathrm{M}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}}{\bigg|}_{\text{ToM}}

\displaystyle=\frac{G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{c^{8}}\sum_{m,\ell,n}\bigg\{\mathcal{A}_{m,\ell}^{n}\,\underset{1,m}{\ \overline{\Psi}_{\ell}}\bigl[\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}^{(n)},\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(8-n)}\bigr]+\mathcal{B}_{m,\ell}^{n}\,\underset{2,m}{\ \overline{\Psi}_{\ell}}\bigl[\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}^{(n)},\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(7-n)}\bigr]

(80c)

where the purely numerical coefficients
\mathcal{A}_{m,\ell}^{n}
,
\mathcal{B}_{m,\ell}^{n}
,
\mathcal{C}_{m,\ell}^{n}
and
\mathcal{D}_{m,\ell}^{n}
in front of each of these integrals are provided in the Tables

{}_{k,m}\overline{\Psi}_{\ell}
and

V.3
Simplification method

In this Section, we implement a method for simplifying the expression of the pure
tail-of-memory
part of the radiative quadrupole, given by (
3.
). The idea is to rexpress everything as only one double integral over the two quadrupoles and a single kernel, modulo some easy surface terms. We thus alternatively integrate by parts the
\rho
and
\tau
variables of (
78
), so as to transfer all the time derivatives on the quadrupole moment represented by
F(u-\rho-\tau)
,
i.e.
the left slot in the functionals (
78
).

First we observe in (
3.
) that when
k=1
, the two quadrupoles have respectively
n
and
8-n
time derivatives, while in the case
k=2
, they instead have
n
and
7-n
derivatives. We first uniformize this by transforming the case
k=2
with the formula

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}^{(n)}(u-\rho)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(7-n)}(u-\rho-\tau)\,f(\rho,\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}^{(n)}(u-\rho)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(8-n)}(u-\rho-\tau)\,\bigl(\partial_{\tau}^{-1}f\bigr)(\rho,\tau)\,,

(81)

where we have introduced the
\tau
-antiderivative which vanishes at
\tau=0
, defined for any function
f(\rho,\tau)
with adequate regularity properties as

\bigl(\partial_{\tau}^{-1}f\bigr)(\rho,\tau)\equiv\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,f(\rho,\lambda)\,.

(82)

After performing this operation, we are left with integrals of the moments that only have
n
and
8-n
time derivatives, and the only possible cases are
n\in[\![0,4]\!]
. Next we integrate by parts so as to be left with integrals with only
0
and
8
time derivatives. However this operation yields some integrals over
\rho
that are separately divergent at the lower bound
\rho=0
. To cure this, we introduce a regularization and replace the
0
at the lower bound by some small
\epsilon
, and restrict attention to the
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
expansion. In the end, we will verify that the final result has a finite
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
limit. With this caveat in mind, we will simplify all our integrals with the formula (valid for
n\in[\![0,4]\!]
)

\displaystyle\int_{\epsilon}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}^{(n)}(u-\rho)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(8-n)}(u-\rho-\tau)\,f(\rho,\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\epsilon}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}(u-\rho)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(8)}(u-\rho-\tau)\,\mathcal{O}_{\rho,\tau}^{\,n}\bigl[f(\rho,\tau)\bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}^{(k)}(u-\epsilon)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(7-k)}(u-\tau-\epsilon)\bigg[\mathcal{O}_{\rho,\tau}^{\,n-1-k}\bigl[f(\rho,\tau)\bigr]\bigg]_{\rho=\epsilon}\,,

(83)

where moments involving
\epsilon
should be Taylor-expanded when
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, the second term is evaluated at
\rho=\epsilon
, and we have introduced the differential operator
\mathcal{O}_{\rho,\tau}
defined by

\mathcal{O}_{\rho,\tau}\bigl[f(\tau,\rho)\bigr]\equiv\partial_{\tau}^{-1}\partial_{\rho}\bigl[f(\tau,\rho)\bigr]-f(\tau,\rho)\,,

(84)

together with its iterations
\mathcal{O}^{\,n}_{\rho,\tau}\equiv\mathcal{O}_{\rho,\tau}\cdots\mathcal{O}_{\rho,\tau}
. Applying the formula (
V.3
) to all the terms composing the tails-of-memory (
3.
), we arrive at a unique master double integral:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\mathrm{M}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}}{\bigg|}_{\text{ToM}}=\mathrm{M}\int_{\epsilon}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}(u-\rho)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(8)}(u-\rho-\tau)\,\Omega(\rho,\tau)+\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}\,,

(85)

where
\Omega(\rho,\tau)
denotes some new kernel function and
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
are all the surface terms coming from the second line of (
V.3
), which carry at most one integral and that simplify drastically in the
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
expansion. Since the expression is long we do not show here the result for
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
.

At this stage, we would
a priori
expect
\Omega(\rho,\tau)
to have a very complicated structure akin to (
74
), and in particular to involve many polylogarithms. But instead, we find the following simple expression without any polylogarithms:

\displaystyle\Omega(\rho,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{7613764}{165375}-\frac{1024076}{18375}\,\frac{\tau}{\rho}-\frac{2074}{63}\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)^{2}-\frac{104}{15}\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho}\right)^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{634076}{55125}\ln\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{384}{175}\,\frac{\tau}{\rho}\ln\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)-\frac{144}{175}\ln\left(\frac{\rho}{2r_{0}}\right)^{2}+\frac{8}{7}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\,.

(86)

Note that if we changed even a single coefficient in Table
1(d)
, the cancellation of polylogarithms would not occur in general, and we would be left with a much more complicated expression for
\Omega(\rho,\tau)
.
When inserting
\Omega(\rho,\tau)
into the first term in (
85
), we can integrate by parts so as to remove all the powers of
\tau/\rho
. This introduces poles in
\epsilon
, and powers of the logarithms of
\epsilon
, but we have checked that these poles and divergences exactly cancel when adding the surface terms
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
, namely the second term in (
85
). Putting all of this together and taking the
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
limit, we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\mathrm{M}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}}{\bigg|}_{\text{ToM}}

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{7}\,\mathrm{M}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}^{(4)}(u-\rho)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(4)}(u-\rho-\tau)

\displaystyle+\frac{48}{175}\,\mathrm{M}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(7)}_{j\rangle a}(u-\tau)\left[\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{1243}{420}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{192}{175}\,\mathrm{M}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(1)}_{a\langle i}\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(6)}_{j\rangle a}(u-\tau)\left[\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{186743}{15120}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1084}{315}\,\mathrm{M}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(2)}_{a\langle i}\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(5)}_{j\rangle a}(u-\tau)

\displaystyle-\frac{104}{5}\,\mathrm{M}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(3)}_{a\langle i}\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(4)}_{j\rangle a}(u-\tau)

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{7}\,\mathrm{M}\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(7)}_{j\rangle a}(u-\tau)\left[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{15667}{10500}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{20}{7}\,\mathrm{M}\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(1)}_{a\langle i}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(6)}_{j\rangle a}(u-\tau)\left[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)-\frac{3590791}{472500}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{16}{7}\,\mathrm{M}\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(2)}_{a\langle i}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(5)}_{j\rangle a}(u-\tau)\left[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)+\frac{981149}{94500}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{3901382}{165375}\mathrm{M}\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(3)}_{a\langle i}\overline{\mathrm{M}}^{(3)}_{j\rangle a}\,.

(87)

There is only one doubly-integrated term left,
cf.
the first line of (
V.3
), which can be seen as the tails-of-memory contribution properly speaking, whereas the structure of the other singly-integrated terms are more akin to simpler tails-of-tails.

The terms proportional to
\ln^{2}(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}})
are exactly compensated by those arising in the tail part of
\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\mathrm{M}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}}
given by (
2.
). Notice that not all possible terms allowed by a dimensionality argument are present in (
V.3
): for instance there is no term

\displaystyle\propto\mathrm{M}\,\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{a\langle i}^{(4)}(u)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{j\rangle a}^{(3)}(u-\tau)\,,

(88)

which can be understood from the structure of the cubic source term (
23
) corresponding to the diagram of Fig.
1(a)
.

V.4
Testing the integration method

The simplication method in Sec
V.3
is also very useful to test our integration method. Indeed, let us consider the integration of a typical tail-of-memory source term, but which we multiply by the factor
B
. For simplicity, we choose
k=3
,
m=0
and
\ell=0
:

\mathcal{I}\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{FP}}_{B=0}\Box^{-1}_{\text{ret}}\biggl[B\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\frac{G(t-r)}{r^{3}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{0}(x)F(t-rx)\biggr]\,.

(89)

This can be computed asymptotically when
r\to+\infty
using the arsenal of techniques developed in Sec.
IV
, and we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\frac{1}{r}\biggl[\Delta-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)G(u-\tau)F^{(1)}(u-\tau)\biggr]+o\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\,,

(90a)

with the rather cumbersome combination of the functionals (
78a
):

\displaystyle\Delta\equiv-\frac{1}{3}\,\underset{1,2}{\overline{\Psi}_{0}}[F^{(2)},G]+\frac{1}{3}\,\underset{1,0}{\overline{\Psi}_{0}}[F^{(2)},G]+\underset{1,1}{\overline{\Psi}_{0}}[F^{(1)},G^{(1)}]-\underset{1,0}{\overline{\Psi}_{0}}[F^{(1)},G^{(1)}]\,.

(90b)

But, on the other hand, the presence of the factor
B
has the effect of only selecting the pole in the
B
-expansion of the integrated source. This allows us to perform first the near-zone expansion
r\rightarrow 0
of the source, and then to integrate term by term this expansion using the formulae in
Blanchet et al. 2022
;
Trestini et al. 2022
. Performing the integration in this manner, we find instead

\mathcal{I}=-\frac{G(u)}{r}\bigg[F(u)+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)F^{(1)}(u-\tau)\biggr]+o\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\,.

(91)

The two expressions (
90
) and (
91
) must be identical, therefore we have found a non-trivial relationship between the functionals
{}_{k,m}{\overline{\Psi}_{\ell}}[F,G]
which must absolutely be satisfied if our integration method is correct. Applying the simplification method described in Sec
V.3
, we can prove that these two expressions are indeed identical. This constitutes a strong test confirming simultaneously the soundness of our integration method and of our simplification method. We have repeated this test with many other such sources; all were successful.

VI
Results

We present our final result, which is the radiative quadrupole moment
\mathcal{U}_{ij}
parametrizing the asymptotic waveform, expressed in terms of the harmonic canonical moments up
to 4.5PN order
, including the tail-of-memory contribution.

Recall that in the previous sections, we have only worked in the radiative construction, and hence with radiative canonical moments denoted
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{L}
and
\overline{\mathrm{S}}_{L}
. This is perfectly legitimate, but until now, all the results in the literature
Blanchet and Damour 1992
;
Blanchet 1998a
;
Blanchet 1998b
;
Faye et al. 2012
;
Marchand et al. 2016
were presented in terms of the harmonic construction and hence of harmonic canonical moments
\mathrm{M}_{L}
and
\mathrm{S}_{L}
. Even more crucially, it is the harmonic canonical moments that were computed in previous works in terms of source parameters
Marchand et al. 2020
;
Larrouturou et al. 2022a
;
Larrouturou et al. 2022b
;
Blanchet et al. 2022
. For these reasons, we have to express our final result in terms of the harmonic canonical moments
\mathrm{M}_{L}
and
\mathrm{S}_{L}
. At 4PN order, this can be done thanks to the moment redefinition derived in
Trestini et al. 2022
, namely

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{ij}=\mathrm{M}_{ij}

\displaystyle+\frac{G\,\mathrm{M}}{c^{3}}\,\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(1)}\left[-\frac{26}{15}+2\ln\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)\right]+\frac{G^{2}\mathrm{M}^{2}}{c^{6}}\,\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(2)}\left[\frac{124}{45}-\frac{52}{15}\ln\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)+2\ln^{2}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)\right]

(92)

\displaystyle+\frac{G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{c^{8}}\bigg[-\frac{8}{21}\,\mathrm{M}_{a\langle i}\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(4)}-\frac{8}{7}\,\mathrm{M}^{(1)}_{a\langle i}\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(3)}-\frac{8}{9}\,\mathrm{M}^{(3)}_{a\langle i}\mathrm{S}_{j\rangle|a}\bigg]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{9}}\right)\,,

where
b_{0}
is the arbitrary gauge constant introduced in Eq. (
6
), and where
c
is henceforth reintroduced. The moment redefinition (
92
) should be inserted into the linear and quadratic contributions to the radiative quadrupole written in terms of the radiative metric (
20
), so as to yield a correction at cubic order.

This correction should be understood in the following sense: let
\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{cubic,rad}}[\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{L},\overline{\mathrm{S}}_{L}]
be the functional expression for the cubic part of the radiative quadrupole in terms of the radiative moments, as worked out in the present paper. Then its counterpart in terms of the harmonic moments, simply denoted
\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{cubic}}[\mathrm{M}_{L},\mathrm{S}_{L}]
, is given by

\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{cubic}}[\mathrm{M}_{L},\mathrm{S}_{L}]=\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{cubic,rad}}[\mathrm{M}_{L},\mathrm{S}_{L}]+\delta\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{cubic,rad}}[\mathrm{M}_{L},\mathrm{S}_{L}]\,,

(93)

where
\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{cubic,rad}}[\mathrm{M}_{L},\mathrm{S}_{L}]
means that we simply substituted the radiative moments with the harmonic moments in the radiative functional, and where
\delta\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{cubic,rad}}[\mathrm{M}_{L},\mathrm{S}_{L}]
represents the correction to be applied due to the moment redefinition (
92
). We find

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{cubic,rad}}

\displaystyle=\frac{G^{2}\mathrm{M}^{2}}{c^{6}}\Bigg\{\left[-\frac{52}{15}+4\ln\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)\right]\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(5)}(u-\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left[-\frac{257}{75}+\frac{11}{3}\ln\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)+2\ln^{2}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)\right]\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle+\frac{G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{c^{8}}\Biggl\{\left[-\frac{16}{105}-\frac{8}{7}\ln\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)\right]\mathrm{M}_{a\langle i}^{(3)}\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(3)}+\left[-\frac{14}{15}-2\ln\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)\right]\mathrm{M}_{a\langle i}^{(2)}\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(4)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left[-\frac{32}{35}-\frac{8}{7}\ln\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)\right]\mathrm{M}_{a\langle i}^{(1)}\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(5)}+\left[-\frac{22}{35}+\frac{2}{7}\ln\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)\right]\mathrm{M}_{a\langle i}\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(6)}\Biggr\}

\displaystyle+\frac{G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{c^{8}}\mathrm{S}_{a|\langle i}\left[\frac{22}{15}-\frac{2}{3}\ln\left(\frac{r_{0}}{b_{0}}\right)\right]\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(5)}\,.

(94)

We finally present the full radiative quadrupole, parametrized by the harmonic canonical moments
\mathrm{M}_{L}
and
\mathrm{S}_{L}
, including all quadratic, cubic and quartic contributions that contribute up to the 4.5PN order. It reads

\mathcal{U}_{ij}=\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{linear}}+\,\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{quadratic}}+\,\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{cubic}}+\,\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{quartic}}\,,

(95)

where the linear contribution is just
\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{linear}}=\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(2)}

can be entirely found in Eqs. (4.4-5-6) of
Faye et al. 2012

\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{quartic}}

(96)

Blanchet 1998a
;
Faye et al. 2015

(97c)

) is the genuine tail-of-memory. However, when computing the flux, we must actually compute the time-derivative of the radiative quadrupole. Blanchet 1998b
.

We can perform three important tests of our end result:

•

The constant
b_{0}
can be eliminated at the level of the full radiative quadrupole at 4PN order by the shift in the time coordinate

\displaystyle u^{\prime}=u-\frac{2G\mathrm{M}}{c^{3}}\ln b_{0}\,,

(98)

along with a Taylor expansion of the canonical quadrupole moment. This shows that
b_{0}
is just associated to a choice for the origin of time in the asymptotic radiative coordinate system and is clearly unphysical.

•

When isolating the contribution of
\ln r_{0}
to the radiative quadrupole for the three cubic interactions (
97
), we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{cubic}}

\displaystyle=\ln r_{0}\bigg[\frac{214}{105}\frac{G^{2}\mathrm{M}^{2}}{c^{6}}\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{G^{2}M}{c^{8}}\Big(4\mathrm{M}_{a\langle i}^{(2)}\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(4)}+\frac{32}{7}\mathrm{M}_{a\langle i}^{(1)}\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(5)}+\frac{4}{7}\mathrm{M}_{a\langle i}\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(6)}+\frac{4}{3}\mathrm{S}_{a|\langle i}\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(5)}\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(\text{terms independent of $r_{0}$})\,.

(99)

This expression exactly cancels the
\ln r_{0}
terms arising in the second time derivative of Eq. (6.1) in
Larrouturou et al. 2022b
, which accounts for the contribution of the dimensional regularization of the cubic interactions to the renormalized canonical quadrupole moment for compact binaries. This cancellation of the
\ln r_{0}
terms with those in the end result of
Larrouturou et al. 2022b
is a strong indication of the correctness of our results (
97
).

•

In the MPM approach, the leading-order quadratic memory term, see
e.g.
(5.10) in
Blanchet 1998b
, reads

\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{\text{mem}}=-\frac{2G}{7c^{5}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{M}_{a\langle i}^{(3)}(u-\rho)\mathrm{M}_{j\rangle a}^{(3)}(u-\rho)+\mathcal{O}(G^{2})\,.

(100)

We find here that the genuine tail-of-memory given by the first term of (
97
) can be obtained simply by replacing in (
100
) the canonical quadrupole moment
\mathrm{M}_{ij}
by the radiative quadrupole moment itself, including the dominant tail effect,
i.e.

\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(2)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{ij}=\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(2)}+\frac{2G\mathrm{M}}{c^{3}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}\left[\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)+\frac{11}{12}\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{5}}\right)\,,

(101)

along with a reexpansion at cubic order and an integration by parts (the constant
11/12
is irrelevant here). With our result (
97
), we thus explicitly retrieve at this order the expression of memory effects computed using the radiative moments defined at future null infinity, see
e.g.

Favata 2009a
;
Favata 2011
.

Appendix A
Extracting
N_{L}
,
P_{L}
,
Q_{L}
and
R_{L}

We describe the practical method we use to extract four sets of STF moments
N_{L}
,
P_{L}
,
Q_{L}
and
R_{L}
parametrizing a vector quantity satisfying
\Box w^{\mu}=0
. Here
w^{\mu}\equiv w_{n}^{\mu}
represents the divergence
w_{n}^{\mu}=\partial_{\nu}u_{n}^{\mu\nu}
of the quantity (
8
) following the MPM algorithm. Having extracted these moments we can then construct the tensor
v_{n}^{\mu\nu}\equiv\mathcal{V}^{\mu\nu}[N_{L},P_{L},Q_{L},R_{L}]
using the formulae (48) in
Blanchet 2014
, which satisfies at once
\Box v_{n}^{\mu\nu}~=~0
and
\partial_{\nu}v_{n}^{\mu\nu}=-w_{n}^{\mu}
. By definition, see (47) in
Blanchet 2014
,

\displaystyle w^{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\partial_{L}\left[r^{-1}N_{L}(t-r)\right]\,,

(102a)

\displaystyle w^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\partial_{iL}\left[r^{-1}P_{L}(t-r)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\partial_{L-1}\left[r^{-1}Q_{iL}(t-r)\right]+\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\epsilon_{iab}\partial_{aL-1}\left[r^{-1}R_{bL-1}(t-r)\right]\,.

(102b)

Next, we define the auxiliary quantities

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}^{i}

\displaystyle\equiv w^{i}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\partial_{iL}\left[r^{-1}P_{L}(t-r)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\partial_{L-1}\left[r^{-1}Q_{iL}(t-r)\right]+\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\epsilon_{iab}\partial_{aL-1}\left[r^{-1}R_{bL-1}(t-r)\right]\,,

(103a)

\displaystyle\overset{\approx}{w}{}^{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\widetilde{w}^{i}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\partial_{L-1}\left[r^{-1}Q_{iL}(t-r)\right]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\epsilon_{iab}\partial_{aL-1}\left[r^{-1}R_{bL-1}(t-r)\right]\,.

(103b)

Using formulae from Appendix A in
Blanchet and Damour 1986
, we express the angular integrals

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,\hat{n}_{L}w^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell!}{(2\ell+1)!!}\,r^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{\ell}\bigg[r^{-1}N_{L}(t-r)\bigg]\,,

(104a)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,\hat{n}_{iL}w^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\ell+1)\ell!}{(2\ell+1)(2\ell+1)!!}\,r^{\ell+1}\left(\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{\ell+1}\bigg[r^{-1}P_{L}(t-r)\bigg]\,,

(104b)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,n_{i}\hat{n}_{L}\widetilde{w}^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell!}{(2\ell+1)!!}\,r^{\ell-1}\left(\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{\ell-1}\bigg[r^{-1}Q_{L}(t-r)\bigg]\,,

(104c)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{ab\langle i}\!\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,n_{L-1\rangle}n_{b}\overset{\approx}{w}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\ell+1)(\ell-1)!}{(2\ell+1)!!}\,r^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{\ell}\bigg[r^{-1}R_{iL-1}(t-r)\bigg]\,.

(104d)

If
w^{\mu}
is known exactly to all order in
r
, we find that the multipole moments can be computed using the near zone limit
r\to 0
(with
t-r=
const) as

\displaystyle N_{L}(t-r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell}(2\ell+1)}{\ell!}\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg[r^{\ell+1}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,\hat{n}_{L}w^{0}\bigg]\,,

(105a)

\displaystyle P_{L}(t-r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell+1}(2\ell+1)}{(\ell+1)!}\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg[r^{\ell+2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,\hat{n}_{iL}w^{i}\bigg]\,,

(105b)

\displaystyle Q_{L}(t-r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell-1}(2\ell+1)(2\ell-1)}{\ell!}\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg[r^{\ell}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,n_{i}\hat{n}_{L}\widetilde{w}^{i}\bigg]\,,

(105c)

\displaystyle R_{iL-1}(t-r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell}(2\ell+1)}{(\ell+1)(\ell-1)!}\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg[r^{\ell+1}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,\epsilon_{ab\langle i}n_{L-1\rangle}n_{b}\overset{\approx}{w}{}^{a}\bigg]\,.

(105d)

If we only know the leading order of the asymptotic expansion of
w^{\mu}
as
r\to+\infty
, the previous expressions cannot be used. In that case, we get equivalent expressions for the time derivatives of the multipole moments:

\displaystyle\overset{(\ell)}{N_{L}}(t-r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell}(2\ell+1)!!}{\ell!}\lim_{r\to+\infty}\bigg[r\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,\hat{n}_{L}w^{0}\bigg]\,,

(106a)

\displaystyle\overset{(\ell+1)}{P_{L}}(t-r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell}(2\ell+1)(2\ell+1)!!}{(\ell+1)!}\lim_{r\to+\infty}\bigg[r\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,\hat{n}_{iL}w^{i}\bigg]\,,

(106b)

\displaystyle\overset{(\ell-1)}{Q_{L}}(t-r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell-1}(2\ell+1)!!}{\ell!}\lim_{r\to+\infty}\bigg[r\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,n_{i}\hat{n}_{L}\widetilde{w}^{i}\bigg]\,,

(106c)

\displaystyle\overset{(\ell)}{R}_{iL-1}(t-r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell}(2\ell+1)!!}{(\ell+1)(\ell-1)!}\lim_{r\to+\infty}\bigg[r\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{4\pi}\,\epsilon_{ab\langle i}n_{L-1\rangle}n_{b}\overset{\approx}{w}{}^{a}\bigg]\,.

(106d)

Appendix B
Proof of convergence when
k=1
and
k=2

In this Appendix, we assume that
k\in\{1,2\}
. We shall prove that in these cases one can set
B=0
in the general solution (
44
), for arbitrary smooth functions
F
and
G\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
which vanish identically in the remote past, for
t\leqslant-\mathcal{T}
. Although it should be possible to check this point using the expression of the three-dimensional retarded integral of the source term (
40
), we present here a detailed analysis directly based on the structure of the solution (
44
).

We must first do some manipulation so as to transfer the boundary conditions of
F
and
G
into the bounds of the integrals. Thanks to the regularization factor
\lambda^{B}
, we can always manipulate these integrals safely, since they are defined by analytic continuation for any
B\in\mathbb{C}
except at some integer values including the value of interest
B=0
, at which we apply the finite part in the end.

B.0.1
Case
k+j\leqslant 1

In this case, we can perform the change of variable
\lambda=\mu+\rho/2
on Eq. (
44b
) and expand using the binomial formula:

\displaystyle\phi_{ij}^{B}(u,r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{-k-j+1}\begin{pmatrix}-k-j+1\\
p\end{pmatrix}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\rho^{i+j}\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right)^{-k-j+1-p}G(u-\rho)\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}\mu\,\left(\frac{\mu+\rho/2}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\mu^{p}\,F\left(u-\frac{\rho(x+1)}{2}-\mu(x-1)\right)\,.

(107)

We then set
\tau=\mu(x-1)
with
\mathrm{d}\tau=(x-1)\mathrm{d}\mu
, invert the integrals, and find

\displaystyle\phi_{ij}^{B}(u,r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{-k-j+1}2^{-k+j-1+p}\begin{pmatrix}-k-j+1\\
p\end{pmatrix}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\tau^{p}\int_{1+\tau/r}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,\frac{Q_{m}(x)}{(x-1)^{p+1}}\left(\frac{\frac{\tau}{x-1}+\rho/2}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\rho^{-k+i+1-p}G(u-\rho)F\left(u-\frac{\rho(x+1)}{2}-\tau\right)\,.

(108)

Next we introduce

\Upsilon_{k,i,j,p}(\tau)\equiv\tau^{p}\int_{1+\tau/r}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,\frac{Q_{m}(x)}{(x-1)^{p+1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\rho^{-k+i+1-p}G(u-\rho)F\left(u-\frac{\rho(x+1)}{2}-\tau\right)\,,

(109)

which is well defined, since
-k+i+1-p\geqslant i+j\geqslant 0
ensures that the integral in
\rho
converges at
0
, and the behavior of
Q_{m}(x)/(x-1)^{p+1}\sim x^{-m-p-2}
when
x\to+\infty
ensures that the integral in
x
converges at infinity.

Since
F
vanishes identically in the remote past, so does
\Upsilon_{k,i,j,p}(\tau)
, thus it is clearly integrable at
\tau\rightarrow\infty
. We then bound this quantity with

\left|\Upsilon_{k,i,j,p}(\tau)\right|\leqslant\tau^{p}\int_{1+\tau/r}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,\frac{Q_{m}(x)}{(x-1)^{p+1}}=\mathcal{O}_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\left(\ln^{2}\tau\right)\,,

(110)

which proves integrability at the bound
\tau\rightarrow 0
.

B.0.2
Case
k+j\geqslant 2

We first shuffle the order of the integrals in Eq. (
44b
) and obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{ij}^{B}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\left(\frac{\lambda}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\lambda^{-k-j+1}\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\,\rho^{i+j}G(u-\rho)F\bigl[u-\rho-\lambda(x-1)\bigr]

\displaystyle-\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\int_{r}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\left(\frac{\lambda}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\lambda^{-k-j+1}\int_{0}^{2(\lambda-r)}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\,\rho^{i+j}G(u-\rho)F\bigl[u-\rho-\lambda(x-1)\bigr]\,.

(111)

Taking advantage of the conditions on
F
and
G
, we can now restrict the bounds of the integrals to
\lambda\leqslant\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{x-1}
. This yields

\displaystyle\phi_{ij}^{B}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\int_{0}^{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{x-1}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\left(\frac{\lambda}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\lambda^{-k-j+1}\int_{0}^{\min(u+\mathcal{T},\,2\lambda)}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\,\rho^{i+j}G(u-\rho)F\bigl[u-\rho-\lambda(x-1)\bigr]

\displaystyle-\int_{1}^{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{r}}\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{m}(x)\int_{r}^{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{x-1}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\left(\frac{\lambda}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\lambda^{-k-j+1}\int_{0}^{\min(u+\mathcal{T},\,2(\lambda-r))}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\,\rho^{i+j}G(u-\rho)F\bigl[u-\rho-\lambda(x-1)\bigr]\,.

(112)

We introduce

\Xi_{k,i,j}(x)\equiv\int_{0}^{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{x-1}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-k-j+1}\int_{0}^{\min(u+\mathcal{T},\,2\lambda)}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\,\rho^{i+j}G(u-\rho)F\bigl[u-\rho-\lambda(x-1)\bigr]\,.

(113)

Let us bound this quantity for any
x\in\ ]1,\infty]
:

\displaystyle\big|\Xi_{k,i,j}(x)\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{x-1}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-k-j+1}\int_{0}^{\min(u+\mathcal{T},\,2\lambda)}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\,\rho^{i+j}\Big|G(u-\rho)F(u-\rho-\lambda(x-1))\Big|

(114)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{K}\int_{0}^{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{x-1}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-k-j+1}\int_{0}^{\min(u+\mathcal{T},\,2\lambda)}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\,\rho^{i+j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{K}}{i+j+1}\int_{0}^{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{x-1}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-k-j+1}\Big[\min(u+\mathcal{T},\,2\lambda)\Big]^{i+j+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{K}}{i+j+1}\Bigg\{\int_{0}^{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{2}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-k-j+1}(2\lambda)^{i+j+1}+\theta(3-x)\int_{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{2}}^{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{x-1}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-k-j+1}(u+\mathcal{T})^{i+j+1}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{K}}{i+j+1}\Bigg\{\frac{2^{j+k-2}}{i+(3-k)}(u+\mathcal{T})^{i+(3-k)}+\theta(3-x)(u+\mathcal{T})^{i+j+1}\int_{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{2}}^{\frac{u+\mathcal{T}}{x-1}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\lambda^{-k-j+1}\Bigg\}\,,

where

\mathcal{K}\equiv\sup_{(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\in[-\mathcal{T},u]^{2}}\Big[F(\tau)G(\tau^{\prime})\Big]\,.

(115)

We now distinguish the case
j=2-k
, which yields

\big|\Xi_{2,i,0}(x)\big|\leqslant\frac{\mathcal{K}}{i+1}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{i+1}(u+\mathcal{T})^{i+1}+\theta(3-x)(u+\mathcal{T})^{i+1}\ln\left(\frac{2}{x-1}\right)\Bigg\}\,,

(116)

and the more general case
k+j\geqslant 2
, in which we find

\big|\Xi_{k,i,j}(x)\big|\leqslant\frac{\mathcal{K}(u+\mathcal{T})^{i+(3-k)}}{i+j+1}\Bigg\{\frac{2^{j+k-2}}{i+(3-k)}+\frac{\theta(3-x)}{j+(k-2)}\Big(2^{j+(k-2)}-(x-1)^{k+j-2}\Big)\Bigg\}\,.

(117)

Since
Q_{m}(x)=\mathcal{O}_{x\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left(\ln\left(x-1\right)\right)
and
Q_{m}(x)=\mathcal{O}_{x\to+\infty}\left(x^{-m-1}\right)
, we can now look at the asymptotic behavior of our integrand. In the case
(k,j)=(2,0)
, we find

Q_{m}(x)\,\Xi_{2,i,0}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{x\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left(\ln(x-1)^{2}\right)\,,\\[8.5359pt]
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{x\to+\infty}\left(x^{-m-1}\right)\,.\end{cases}

(118)

In the general case
k+j\geqslant 2
, we instead have

Q_{m}(x)\,\Xi_{k,i,j}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{x\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left(\ln(x-1)(x-1)^{j+(k-2)}\right)\,,\\[8.5359pt]
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{x\to+\infty}\left(x^{-m-1}\right)\,.\end{cases}

(119)

It is now clear that
Q_{m}(x)\,\Xi_{k,i,j}(x)
is integrable at both bounds
x\rightarrow 1^{+}
and
x\to+\infty
as soon as
k\in\{1,2\}
. After doing a similar (and much easier) analysis on the second member of Eq. (
B.0.2
), we find that Eqs. (
B.0.2
)–(
B.0.2
), and consequently Eq. (
44b
), have a convergent limit as
B\rightarrow 0
,
i.e.
, they do not develop poles in
1/B
. Repeating the analysis of this section with an extra factor
\ln(r/r_{0})
in the integrand shows that we can also safely compute the
1/B
coefficient in the Laurent series.

Local topology for periodic Hamiltonians and fuzzy tori
Nora Doll, Terry Loring, Hermann Schulz-Baldes

Abstract
A variety of local index formulas is constructed for quantum Hamiltonians with periodic boundary conditions. All dimensions of physical space as well as many symmetry constraints are covered, notably one-dimensional systems in Class DIII as well as two- and three-dimensional systems in Class AII. The constructions are based on several periodic variations of the spectral localizer and are rooted in the existence of underlying fuzzy tori. For these latter, a general invariant theory is developed. MSC 2010: 81R60, 37B30, 46L80, 81V70, 47S40

Overview

In several works
[
14
,
15
,
5
]
it was shown that the index pairing between
K
-theory and
K
-homology elements can be computed as the half-signature of a suitably constructed finite dimensional matrix, called the spectral localizer. The main motivation for these works is the application to topological insulators for which the bulk topological invariants (Chern numbers and winding numbers) then become readily accessible in numerical computations. As will be described below, the technique is based on the principle of placing the physical system in a linearly growing Dirac trap and hence the spectral localizer is an intrinsically non-periodic object. On the other hand, it is well-known that periodic approximations often provide stable algorithms for bulk quantities in solid state systems (for invariants, this is described in
[
17
]
). This work constructs new periodic versions of the spectral localizer, for sake of conciseness referred to as
periodic spectral localizers
, which also allow to compute the topological invariants numerically, possibly in a more stable manner than with the non-periodic spectral localizer used in other works. Apart from this practical aspect, a further more theoretical insight is that the periodic spectral localizers can be understood as the
K
-theory representatives of associated fuzzy tori. Furthermore, the periodic spectral localizers may inspire extensions to interacting systems (with periodic boundary conditions) with a computable gapped ground state.

Let us directly describe the periodic spectral localizer for a bounded tight-binding Hamiltonian
H
on the Hilbert space
{\cal H}=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},{\mathbb{C}}^{L})
over an even dimensional lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with
L
local degrees of freedom. This Hamiltonian is supposed to be of finite range
R
and periodic in space, namely the
L\times L
matrices
\langle x|H|y\rangle
vanish for
|x-y|>R
and
\langle x+p_{j}e_{j}|H|y+p_{j}e_{j}\rangle=\langle x|H|y\rangle
for some periods
p_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
in the direction of the unit vectors
e_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,d
. The final assumption on
H
is that it describes an insulator,
i.e.
it has a spectral gap at the Fermi level
\mu
. After an energy shift,
\mu=0
and the Fermi projection is
P=\chi(H<0)
. For any such insulator it is well-known (
e.g.

[
18
]
) that there is an associated (even strong) Chern number
{\rm Ch}_{d}(P)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. This paper provides yet another way to compute this topological invariant. Moreover, the formula that is proven to work in the asymptotic regime of large volumes allows to associate numerical topological invariants to rather small systems.

For that purpose, let
\rho\in{\mathbb{N}}
be a system size such that
2\rho
is an integer multiple of each of the
p_{j}
. Then let
H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}
be the restriction of
H
to
{\cal H}_{\rho}=\ell^{2}(({\mathbb{Z}}/(2\rho{\mathbb{Z}}))^{d},{\mathbb{C}}^{L})
with periodic boundary conditions. Then the associated (even)
periodic spectral localizer
is a finite dimensional matrix on
({\cal H}_{\rho}\oplus{\cal H}_{\rho})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}
defined by

L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}\,=\,\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big({\bf 1}-\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}\,X_{j})\big)&\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}\,X_{j})\,\hat{\gamma}_{j}^{*}\\
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}\,X_{j})\,\hat{\gamma}_{j}&-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big({\bf 1}-\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}\,X_{j})\big)\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}-H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}&0\\
0&H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}\;,

(1)

where
X_{j}
are the components
j=1,\ldots,d
of the position operators on the lattice, furthermore
\hat{\gamma}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\gamma}_{d-1}
is a selfadjoint irreducible representation on
{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}
of the Clifford algebra with
d-1
generators, namely
\hat{\gamma}_{i}\hat{\gamma}_{j}+\hat{\gamma}_{j}\hat{\gamma}_{i}=2\,\delta_{i,j}
for
i,j=1,\ldots,d-1
, and
\hat{\gamma}_{d}=\imath{\bf 1}
with
\imath=\sqrt{-1}
. Finally,
\eta>0
is a parameter that should roughly be chosen as
\|H\|
, see Remark
3
below. Note that the first summand in (
1
) is the restriction of a diagonal operator onto
({\cal H}_{\rho}\oplus{\cal H}_{\rho})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}
and it is periodic in all
d
directions of the discrete torus
({\mathbb{Z}}/(2\rho{\mathbb{Z}}))^{d}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\cap[-\rho+1,\rho]^{d}
.

Theorem 1

Let
d
be even and
H
be a finite-range periodic operator on
{\cal H}=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},{\mathbb{C}}^{L})
. Also let
\rho\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\rho
is an integer multiple of the periods and
\rho\geq 2R
. Suppose

\displaystyle\rho\;\geq\;\frac{C\,d^{4}\,M\,\|H\|^{3}\,\eta^{2}}{g^{6}}\;,

(2)

where
g=\|H^{-1}\|^{-1}
,
M=\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}\|[X_{j},H]\|
, and finally
C=15\cdot 10^{6}
. Moreover,
\eta\geq\frac{g}{4}
is such that

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{g}{\|H\|}\Big)^{2}+4\Big(1-\frac{\eta}{\|H\|}\Big)\,\leq\,\frac{g^{2}}{4\,d\,\eta\,\|H\|}\;.

(3)

Then the periodic spectral localizer is gapped with lower bound

(L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho})^{2}\;\geq\;\frac{g^{2}}{600\,d\,\eta^{2}}\,{\bf 1}\;,

(4)

and the strong invariant given by the
d
-th Chern number of
P=\chi(H<0)
is equal to the half-signature of the periodic spectral localizer, namely

{\rm Ch}_{d}(P)\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,{\rm Sig}(L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho})\;.

(5)

Remark 2

Let us start out by comparing the periodic spectral localizer
L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}
with the spectral localizer
L_{\kappa,\rho}
used in prior works
[
14
,
15
,
5
]
, and also explain the connection between the two of them. The latter matrix
L_{\kappa,\rho}
is defined on the same finite-dimensional Hilbert space
({\cal H}_{\rho}\oplus{\cal H}_{\rho})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}
by

L_{\kappa,\rho}\;=\;\kappa\begin{pmatrix}0&\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}\hat{\gamma}_{j}^{*}\\
\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}\hat{\gamma}_{j}&0\end{pmatrix}\,+\,\begin{pmatrix}-H_{\rho}&0\\
0&H_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}\;.

(6)

Here the boundary condition on
H_{\rho}
merely needs to be local, notably it can be either periodic or simply be Dirichlet. The first matrix, without the factor
\kappa
, is called the Dirac operator
D
and is off-diagonal as the dimension
d
is even. The spectral localizer
L_{\kappa,\rho}
is clearly not periodic in the above sense because the position operators take large positive and negative values at the boundaries of the discrete cube
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\cap[-\rho+1,\rho]^{d}
. The main result of
[
15
]
(see also
[
5
]
) is that the equality (
5
) holds with
L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}
on the r.h.s. replaced by
L_{\kappa,\rho}
, provided conditions on
\kappa
and
\rho
hold that are quantitatively weaker than (
2
). In the latter regime, the spectral asymmetry of both operators
L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}
and
L_{\kappa,\rho}
is acquired near the center of the finite volume where both operators are roughly the same which gives an intuitive understanding why Theorem
1
should hold (based on the earlier results
[
14
,
15
]
). Indeed, the proof of Theorem
1
consists of constructing a homotopy from
L_{\kappa,\rho}
to
L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}
inside the finite-dimensional invertible selfadjoint matrices. The essential step is a deformation of the first summand in (
1
), which is explicitly given in (
25
) below. It further results from the strategy of proof in Section
2
that the Hamiltonian can be tapered down, see (
23
). This means that in the regime of (
2
) the contribution to the signature results merely from the central part of the finite volume. Hence the half-signature in (
5
) is a
local topological marker
in this regime, just as the half-signature of the spectral localizer of
[
14
,
15
]
. However, one can use the r.h.s. of (
5
) also for much smaller
\rho
for which periodic boundary conditions
are
relevant so that the signature invariant is a
global or bulk topological invariant
. All of this is numerically confirmed in Remark
6
on the example of a one-dimensional topological system, but there is definitely a need for further investigations.

\diamond

Remark 3

Besides being the volume, the parameter
\rho
sets the length scale of the position operator close to the origin because
\sin(\frac{\pi}{\rho}x_{j})\sim\frac{\pi}{\rho}x_{j}
and
1-\cos(\frac{\pi}{\rho}x_{j})\sim\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\pi}{\rho}x_{j})^{2}
. Comparing with the (non-periodic) spectral localizer (spelled out in (
6
)),
\frac{1}{\rho}
hence plays the same role as the parameter
\kappa
in prior works
[
14
,
15
,
5
]
. Having this in mind, the condition in (
2
) is a more stringent version of the main hypothesis in these works. Note that, given a gapped Hamiltonian
H
, it can always be guaranteed by choosing
\rho
sufficiently large. The second bound (
3
) is a new supplementary condition. For a flat band Hamiltonian which by definition satisfies
g=\|H\|
, the condition becomes
\eta\geq\frac{g}{2}(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{4d}})
. In particular,
\eta=1
is allowed for a flat band Hamiltonian with
g=\|H\|=1
. As will be explained in second part of this introduction and Section
5
, this is reminiscent of the fact that the flat band Hamiltonian together with suitable functions of the position operators forms a
graded fuzzy torus
. On the other hand, if one chooses
\eta=\|H\|
(without imposing the flat band condition), then the bound (
3
) becomes
\frac{\|H\|}{g}-1\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{d}}
which means that
H
has to be relatively close to a flat band Hamiltonian in the sense that
\|H\|
is not allowed to be much bigger than
g=\|H^{-1}\|^{-1}
. Furthermore, (
3
) is always satisfied if
\eta\geq\frac{5}{4}\|H\|
(simply because then the l.h.s. becomes negative due to
g\leq\|H\|
). Note, however, that for large
\eta
,
(
2
) enforces
\rho
to be larger, and furthermore the gap of
L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}
closes, see the bound (
4
), and then its signature may not be numerically stable any more. Hence from a numerical perspective, it may be best to choose
\eta
of the order of
\|H\|
and actually somewhat smaller than
\|H\|
so that the periodic spectral localizer is associated to a fuzzy torus of small width (in the sense of Definition
19
below). In conclusion, the discussion shows that one should chose
\eta\approx\|H\|
and in the sequel not consider it as a free parameter.

\diamond

Remark 4

The bounds (
2
) and (
3
) have an intrinsic scale invariance. Actually, replacing
H
,
M
,
g
and
\eta
by
\lambda H
,
\lambda M
,
\lambda g
and
\lambda\eta
respectively where
\lambda>0
is a scaling parameter, leaves the conditions invariant. All four quantities are expressed in energy units, while
\rho
is a space unit. From a quantitative aspect, we believe that hypothesis (
3
) is relatively close to optimal, while the condition (
2
) is certainly off by several orders of magnitude and even the dependence on
g
is likely much worse than needed. Let us stress that once
\eta
is chosen as in Remark
3
, there is no further parameter other than
\rho
. One can then analyze numerically the behavior for small
\rho
and safely use the half-signature as local topological marker, as long as it is stable.

\diamond

Remark 5

As pointed out in Remark
3
, the condition (
3
) is easiest to satisfy if
H
is already somewhat close to a flat band Hamiltonian. This can be attained by replacing a given initial gapped finite-range periodic Hamiltonian
H^{\prime}
by a suitable polynomial
H=q(H^{\prime})
which is then also periodic and of finite range, even though the range is increased by a factor given by the degree of the polynomial
q
. The polynomial
q
should be chosen odd with
q(x)>0
for
x>0
so that
H
also has a spectral gap at
0
, and, moreover, to have a degree as small as possible. Based on the spectral information of
H^{\prime}
, it is straightforward to construct a suitable polynomial.

\diamond

Remark 6

As already discussed in Remarks
2
and
4
, we expect the signature of
L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}
to be stable for much smaller system sizes
\rho
. Let us support this belief by some numerics in the numerically most simple situation of a chiral model in dimension
d=1
, namely the so-called SSH model (see
[
18
]
for a detailed description of this much studied object). The chiral Hamiltonian is then an off-diagonal
2\times 2
matrix with off-diagonal entry
A
given by an invertible tight-binding operator on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
which in Dirac Bra-Ket notation is given by
A|x\rangle=(m+m_{x})||x\rangle+(1+t_{x})|x+1\rangle
, where
m>0
,
(m_{x})_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
(t_{x})_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
are independent and identically distributed random variables in
[-\lambda,\lambda]
with
\lambda<1
. For
\lambda
and
m
sufficiently small, the operator
A
has a non-commutative winding number equal to
-1
in the present situation. According to Theorem
15
(the odd-dimensional equivalent to Theorem
1
), it can be computed using the following odd periodic spectral localizer discussed in Section
3
:

{L}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\sin(\frac{\pi}{\rho}X)&{\bf 1}-\cos(\frac{\pi}{\rho}X)\\
{\bf 1}-\cos(\frac{\pi}{\rho}X)&-\sin(\frac{\pi}{\rho}X)\end{pmatrix}\,-\,\frac{1}{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}0&A^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}\\
(A^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho})^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\;.

(7)

Here
X=X_{1}
is the position operator and the right summand is the periodized SSH Hamiltonian
H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}
in finite volume. Numerics readily show that its half-signature is
-1
for a large span of
\rho
and
\lambda
, simply confirming Theorem
15
. If the periodic boundary conditions are used, this reliably works for
\rho
as small as
4
. Also interesting is that drastic modifications of the Hamiltonian do not alter the signature index if
\rho
is sufficiently large: instead of
H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}
, one can use
\widetilde{H}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}
obtained from
H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}
by setting all matrix elements
\langle x|H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}|y\rangle=0
for either
|x|>{(1-s)}\rho
or
|y|>{(1-s)}\rho
where
s\in[0,1)
. Any
s>0
will eliminate the periodic boundary conditions and, moreover, leads to a large kernel of
H
, roughly of dimension
{4\rho s}
. Nevertheless, this kernel results from regions where the first summand in (
7
) has large off-diagonal parts and hence does not lead to a kernel of
{L}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}
. In the central region
[-{(1-s)}\rho,{(1-s)}\rho]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
where
{L}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}
extracts the topology by means of its spectral asymmetry, the modified Hamiltonian
\widetilde{H}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}
coincides with the
{H}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}
. Indeed, numerics clearly show that the half-signature is still
-1
as long as
{(1-s)\rho\approx 30}
. This clearly shows that the periodic spectral localizer in the regime of large
\rho
reads out the topology locally close to the origin (where
\sin
is linear and
1-\cos
vanishes).

Remark 9

From a
KK
-theoretic perspective (explained in some detail elsewhere), both
L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}
and

are representatives of a Kasparov product of two even
KK
-cycles
[P]\in KK^{0}({\mathbb{C}},{\cal A})
and
[D]\in KK^{0}({\cal A},{\mathbb{C}})
for a suitable algebra
{\cal A}
. The two gradings are apparent in (

), but once the Kasparov product is computed, one can deform it without respecting the grading of the separate Fredholm modules

Let us conclude this introduction with a brief discussion of the notion of a fuzzy
d
-torus in connection with the periodic spectral localizer. This is discussed in detail in Section
5
which we believe to be of considerable independent interest, possibly serving as a guideline to the construction of numerically computable local index formulas for other fuzzy versions of classical geometric objects. Abstract index formulas (not suitable for numerical implementation) have been known for a long time
[
8
,
6
,
7
]
, and invariants for the special case of fuzzy spheres were already studied in other works, in particular
[
11
,
15
]
. More specifically, a fuzzy
d
-torus consists by definition of
d
operators
A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}
which are almost unitary and almost commute, see Definition
19
. Motivated by standard models of topological insulators (see Chapter 2 in
[
18
]
) and the work
[
12
]
, let us associate a selfadjoint operator to the fuzzy torus:

G\;=\;\frac{\imath}{2}\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}(A_{j}^{*}-A_{j})\otimes\gamma_{j}+\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}(A_{j}^{*}+A_{j})\right)\otimes\gamma_{d+1}\;,

(8)

where
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d+1}
is an irreducible representation of the Clifford algebra with
d+1
generators.
In Section
5
it is shown that
G
is gapped and hence defines an even
K
-theory class which in the case of a matrix torus of even dimension
d
can be read out as half-signature. At the root of the construction of (
8
) is a classical map from the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
to the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
of mapping degree
1
. This map is analyzed in detail in Appendix
B
. Using variations of this map, one can construct a large set of invariants associated to the fuzzy torus, see Section
5
. In the context of Theorem
1
, there are two fuzzy tori of matrices, namely
e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{1}},\ldots,e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{d}},H_{\rho}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}
and
P_{\rho}e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{1}}P_{\rho},\ldots,P_{\rho}e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{d}}P_{\rho}
where
P_{\rho}=\chi(H_{\rho}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}<0)
. The first one is a
(d+1)
-torus consisting of
d+1
operators, but the last operator
H_{\rho}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}
in the list is selfadjoint; such a fuzzy
(d+1)
-torus is called a graded
d
-torus (see again Definition
19
). Essentially the
G
-operator associated to the graded fuzzy
d
-torus
e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{1}},\ldots,e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{d}},H_{\rho}^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}
is the periodic spectral localizer. On the other hand,
P_{\rho}e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{1}}P_{\rho},\ldots,P_{\rho}e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{d}}P_{\rho}
is an un-graded fuzzy
d
-torus. The latter is the reduced out version of the former and both have the same topological content (see Proposition
33
). Indeed, for
d=2
, this second fuzzy torus already played a role in
[
6
,
7
]
and the recent work by Toniolo on quantum Hall systems
[
23
]
. Combined with Theorem
1
one obtains:

Theorem 10

Let
G=G(P_{\rho}e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{1}}P_{\rho},\ldots,P_{\rho}e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{d}}P_{\rho})
be constructed as in (
8
). For
\rho
sufficiently large, one has

{\rm Ch}_{d}(P)\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,{\rm Sig}(G)\;.

(9)

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1
. Section
3
describes the odd dimensional version of the periodic spectral localizer. Then Section
4
shows how to modify the periodic spectral localizer so that it can be used to compute
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-invariants in systems with real symmetries such as time-reversal and particle-hole symmetry. Finally Section
5
introduces the general notion of a fuzzy torus and shows how to extract
K
-theoretic topological invariants from it. Finally Appendix
A
recalls the tight connection between mapping degree and Chern number, which is then applied in Appendix
B
in order to analyze the classical maps behind the index construction of the periodic spectral localizer.

2
Periodic spectral localizer in even dimension

This section provides the proof of Theorem
1
. Let
H=H^{*}
be a bounded selfadjoint operator on
{\cal H}=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},{\mathbb{C}}^{L})
of finite range
R
which is periodic in all
d
directions with periods
{\bf p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
. As above let
\rho\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\rho
is an integer multiple of all these
p_{j}
. Then
H
is
2\rho
periodic in each of the
d
directions. It is well-known that such an operator can be partially diagonalized by a Bloch-Floquet transformation
{\cal F}_{\rho}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},{\mathbb{C}}^{L})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{\rho},{\mathbb{C}}^{(2\rho)^{d}L})
where
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{\rho}=({\mathbb{R}}/4\pi{\rho}{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}
:

{\cal F}_{\rho}\,H\,{\cal F}_{\rho}^{*}\;=\;\int^{\oplus}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{\rho}}dk\;H(k)\;.

(10)

Then the periodic Hamiltonian appearing in (
1
) is
H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}=H(0)
. Actually, any fiber
H(k)
could be used as well and Theorem
1
remains valid. The first key observation, following directly from the direct integral representation is that

{\rm spec}(H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho})\,\subset\,{\rm spec}(H)\;.

In particular,
H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}
also has a gap around the Fermi level
\mu=0
of size at least
g=\|H^{-1}\|^{-1}
.

Next let us introduce the periodic function
\xi:{\mathbb{R}}\to[-1,1]
by

\xi(x)\;=\;\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\,x)\;,

(11)

and then set
\xi_{\rho}(x)=\xi(\frac{x}{\rho})
. Due to the addition theorems, one then has

e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{\rho}x}\;=\;1-2\,\xi_{\rho}(x)^{2}+\imath\,2\,\xi_{\rho}(x)\sqrt{1-\xi_{\rho}(x)^{2}}\;

(12)

for
x\in[-\rho,\rho]
.
The main estimates of the next lemma are folklore (
e.g.

[
1
]
), but for the convenience of the reader a full proof is nevertheless provided.

Lemma 11

For
j=1,\ldots,d
and
\rho\in{\mathbb{R}}
, one has

\big\|\big[\xi_{\rho}(X_{j}),H\big]\big\|\;\leq\;\frac{\pi}{2\rho}\,\|[X_{j},H]\|\;.

(13)

Furthermore if
\rho\in{\mathbb{N}}
is such that
2\rho
is an integer multiple of the periods of
H
one has the following commutator bounds for
H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}
:

\displaystyle\big\|\big[\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}X_{j}),H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}\big]\big\|\;\leq\;\frac{\pi}{\rho}\,\|[X_{j},H]\|\;,

(14)

\displaystyle\big\|\big[\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}X_{j}),H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}\big]\big\|\;\leq\;\frac{\pi}{\rho}\,\|[X_{j},H]\|\;,

(15)

\displaystyle\big\|\big[|\xi_{\rho}(X_{j})|,H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}\big]\big\|^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{25\,\pi}{32\,\rho}\,\|H\|\,\|[X_{j},H]\|\;.

(16)

Proof.
Let us start out by noting that
\xi_{\rho}(X_{j})
is a linear combination of
e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{2\rho}\,X_{j}}
and
e^{-\imath\frac{\pi}{2\rho}\,X_{j}}
, see (

). Therefore DuHamel’s formula implies

\|[\xi(X_{j}),H]\|\;\leq\;\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|[e^{\imath\frac{\pi}{2\rho}X_{j}},H]\|\,+\,\|[e^{-\imath\frac{\pi}{2\rho}X_{j}},H]\|\Big)\;\leq\;\frac{\pi}{2\rho}\,\|[X_{j},H]\|\;,

which shows (
13
). As
\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}X_{j})
is a periodic multiplication operator one has

\|[\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}X_{j}),H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}]\|\;\leq\;\|[\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}X_{j}),H]\|

and then (
14
) follows from DuHamel’s formula as above. Further, (
15
) holds by the same argument. To show (
16
) note that

|\xi_{\rho}(X_{j})|^{2}\;=\;\frac{{\bf 1}-\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}X_{j})}{2}\;.

Then using the main theorem in
[
16
]
stating that for any positve semidefinite bounded operator
T
on
{\cal H}_{\rho}
and any bounded operator
S
on
{\cal H}_{\rho}

\|[T^{\frac{1}{2}},S]\|\;\leq\;\frac{5}{4}\|S\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|[T,S]\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\;,

one obtains

\displaystyle\big\|\big[|\xi_{\rho}(X_{j})|,H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}\big]\big\|\;\leq\;\frac{5}{4}\|H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\left[\tfrac{1}{2}({\bf 1}-\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{\rho}X_{j})),H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}\right]\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\leq\;\frac{5\sqrt{\pi}}{4\sqrt{2\rho}}\|H\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|[X_{j},H]\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\;,

where in the last step (
14
) was used. The square of this bound is precisely (
16
).

Proposition 12

If all conditions of
Theorem

hold, then the periodic spectral localizer satisfies the bound (
4
). Moreover, if two parameter sets
(\eta,\rho)
and
(\eta^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
both satisfy all conditions, then

{\rm Sig}(L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho})\;=\;{\rm Sig}(L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}})\;.

Proof.
Let us start out with several preliminaries. To shorten notations let us denote
H^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\rho}
simply by
H_{\rho}
,
\sin(\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{j})
by
s_{\rho,j}
and
\cos(\frac{\pi}{\rho}X_{j})
by
c_{\rho,j}
. Further let us introduce the Clifford representation
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d+1}
by

\gamma_{1}=\hat{\gamma}_{1}\otimes\sigma_{1}\;\;,\;\ldots\,,\;\;\gamma_{d-1}=\hat{\gamma}_{d-1}\otimes\sigma_{1}\,,\;\;\gamma_{d}={\bf 1}\otimes\sigma_{2}\,,\;\;\gamma_{d+1}={\bf 1}\otimes\sigma_{3}\;,

where
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}

are the Pauli matrices. Finally let us set
\tilde{H}_{\rho}=\frac{1}{\eta}H_{\rho}
. Then the periodic spectral localizer as given in (

) becomes

\displaystyle L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho,j}\,\gamma_{j}\;+\;\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}(1-c_{\rho,j})-\tilde{H}_{\rho}\Big)\gamma_{d+1}\;.

(17)

Next let us introduce a tapering function as in
[
15
,
5
]
by setting
G(x)=\frac{1}{2}({\chi}(4x+3)-{{\chi}}(4x-3))
with
{\chi}:{\mathbb{R}}\to[-1,1]
being be the odd non-decreasing switch function with
{\chi}(\pm x)=\pm 1
for
x\geq 1
given by
{\chi}(x)=x(2-|x|)
for
x\in[-1,1]
. Then set
G_{\rho}(x)=G(\frac{x}{\rho})
. One finds that
G_{\rho}
satisfies by construction
G_{\rho}(x)=1
for
|x|\leq\frac{\rho}{2}
and
G_{\rho}(x)=0
for
|x|\geq\rho
, and, moreover, it is an even function. Furthermore, by Lemma 4 in
[
14
]
one has
\|[G_{\rho}(D),H\oplus H]\|\leq\tfrac{8}{\rho}\,\|[D,H\oplus H]\|
where
D=\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}\gamma_{j}
(see also
[
5
]
). As
G_{\rho}
is even and
D_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}{\hat{\gamma}}_{j}
is normal, the operator
G_{\rho}(D)
is diagonal with diagonal entry
G_{\rho}(|D_{0}|)=G_{\rho}(|D^{*}_{0}|)
. Therefore the commutator bound can also be stated as

\|[G_{\rho}(|D_{0}|),H]\|\;\leq\;\frac{8}{\rho}\,\|[D_{0},H]\|\;.

(18)

Then introduce an interpolating function
G_{\rho,t}:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,1]
by
G_{\rho,t}(x)=tG_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x)+(1-t)
.
Finally let us also set

G_{t}\;=\;G_{\rho,t}(|D_{0}|)\;,\qquad\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}\;=\;G_{t}\,\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}\,G_{t}\;,

where
\rho^{\prime}\in[\rho,2\rho]
satisfies all conditions of Theorem
1
.
The path

t\in[0,1]\mapsto L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho,\rho^{\prime}}(t)\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}\,\gamma_{j}\;+\;\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})-\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}\Big)\gamma_{d+1}

(19)

connects
L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho,\rho^{\prime}}(0)=L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho^{\prime}}
to an operator on
({\cal H}_{\rho^{\prime}}\oplus{\cal H}_{\rho^{\prime}})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}
that can be restricted to
({\cal H}_{\rho}\oplus{\cal H}_{\rho})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}
easily. After these preparation, let us now start by computing the square

\displaystyle L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho,\rho^{\prime}}(t)^{2}

\displaystyle\;=\;\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})-\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}\right)^{2}\right)\otimes{\bf 1}\,+\,\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq d}[s_{\rho^{\prime},j},s_{\rho^{\prime},l}]\otimes\gamma_{j}\gamma_{l}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big[s_{\rho^{\prime},j},\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})-\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}\big]\otimes\gamma_{j}\gamma_{d+1}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}(1-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})-\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}\right)^{2}\right)\otimes{\bf 1}\,-\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|[s_{\rho^{\prime},j},\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}]\|{\bf 1}\;.

(20)

because
[s_{\rho^{\prime},j},s_{\rho^{\prime},l}]=[s_{\rho^{\prime},j},c_{\rho^{\prime},l}]=0
for all
j,l=1,\ldots,d
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})-\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\Big)^{2}+(\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t})^{2}\,-\,G_{\rho,t}\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big(({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}+\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\big)\,G_{\rho,t}

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\Big)^{2}+(\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t})^{2}\,-\,\lambda\,G_{\rho,t}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big(({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}+\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\big)\,G_{\rho,t}\;,

where
\lambda>0
is a parameter to be chosen later and
\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}=\frac{\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}}{\lambda}
.
Using
s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+c_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}={\bf 1}
for all
j=1,\ldots,d
one directly checks

\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}(1-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\Big)^{2}\;\geq\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}(1-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})^{2}\;=\;2\sum_{j=1}^{d}(1-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\;.

Replacing in the above gives

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})-\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}\right)^{2}\;=\;2(1\,-\,\lambda\,G_{\rho,t}^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\,+\,(\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t})^{2}

\displaystyle\,+\,\lambda\,G_{\rho,t}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big(({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}})+({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}})({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\big)G_{\rho,t}\;.

Now let us use the elementary identity

{\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2})\;,

(21)

which implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})-\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;2(1\,-\,\lambda\,G_{\rho,t}^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\,+\,(\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t})^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;+\,\frac{\lambda}{2}\,G_{\rho,t}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}(({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})^{2}+(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})^{2}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j}))\Big)G_{\rho,t}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;+\frac{\lambda}{2}\,G_{\rho,t}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}(({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2})+({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2})({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j}))\Big)G_{\rho,t}\;.

Both of the last two summands require a detailed analysis. In order to deal with the first of them, let us use

({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})^{2}\;=\;\big((\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})+[{\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j},\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1}]\big)(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})

and

(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})^{2}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\;=\;(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})\big(({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})-[{\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j},\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1}]\big)

as well as
(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})\geq 0
. One gets

\displaystyle({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})^{2}+(\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1})^{2}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})

\displaystyle\;\geq\;-2\|[{\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j},\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1}]\|\|\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}-{\bf 1}\|{\bf 1}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;-2\|[c_{\rho^{\prime},j},\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}]\|(\|\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}\|+1){\bf 1}\;.

\displaystyle\;\geq\;-4\max\{\|\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}\|,1\}\,\|[c_{\rho^{\prime},j},\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}]\|\,{\bf 1}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;-\frac{4\pi}{\rho^{\prime}\lambda^{2}\eta^{2}}\,\max\{\|H\|,\lambda\eta\}\,\|[X_{j},H]\|\,{\bf 1}\;,

where the last step follows from Lemma
11
.
This combined with
G_{t}^{2}\leq 1
leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})-\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;2(1\,-\,\lambda\,G_{\rho,t}^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\,+\,(\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t})^{2}\,-\,\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\eta^{2}\rho^{\prime}}\,\max\{\|H\|,\lambda\eta\}\,d\,M\,{\bf 1}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;+\frac{\lambda}{2}\,G_{\rho,t}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}(({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2})+({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2})({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j}))\Big)G_{\rho,t}\;.

For the last summand, let us use that (
12
) implies
\frac{{\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j}}{2}=|\xi_{\rho^{\prime},j}|^{2}
where
\xi_{\rho^{\prime},j}=\sin(\frac{\pi}{2\rho^{\prime}}X_{j})
. Thus

\displaystyle({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})

\displaystyle({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2})+({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2})({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})

\displaystyle\;=\;{4}\,|\xi_{\rho^{\prime},j}|({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2})|\xi_{\rho^{\prime},j}|-{2}\,|\xi_{\rho^{\prime},j}|[({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}),|\xi_{\rho^{\prime},j}|]-{2}\,[|\xi_{\rho^{\prime},j}|,({\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2})]|\xi_{\rho^{\prime},j}|

\displaystyle\;\geq\;-\,2\,\|{\bf 1}-\hat{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}\|\,({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\,-\,8\frac{5\sqrt{\pi}}{\lambda^{2}\eta^{2}4\sqrt{2\rho^{\prime}}}\,\|[X_{j},H]\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|H\|^{\frac{3}{2}}\,{\bf 1}

Replacing in the above, one then gets

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{\rho^{\prime},j}^{2}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})-\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t}\right)^{2}\;\geq\;

\displaystyle\Big(2\,-\,\big(2\lambda+\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}\|\big)\,G_{\rho,t}^{2}\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})

\displaystyle\;\,+\,(\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t})^{2}\,-\,\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\eta^{2}\rho^{\prime}}\,\max\{\|H\|,\lambda\eta\}\,d\,M\,{\bf 1}

\displaystyle\,-\,\frac{5\sqrt{\pi}}{\lambda\eta^{2}\sqrt{2\rho^{\prime}}}\,\|H\|^{\frac{3}{2}}d\sqrt{M}\,{\bf 1}\;.

Now let us focus on the summand
(\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t})^{2}
. Due to
(\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t})^{2}\geq g^{2}\,{\bf 1}
and again
G_{\rho,t}^{2}\leq{\bf 1}

\displaystyle(\tilde{H}_{\rho,\rho^{\prime},t})^{2}

\displaystyle\;=\;\tfrac{1}{\eta^{2}}\big(G_{\rho,t}^{2}H_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}G_{\rho,t}^{2}\,+\,G_{\rho,t}^{2}H_{\rho^{\prime}}[G_{\rho,t},H_{\rho^{\prime}}]G_{\rho,t}\,+\,G_{\rho,t}[H_{\rho^{\prime}},G_{\rho,t}]G_{\rho,t}H_{\rho^{\prime}}G_{\rho,t}\big)

\displaystyle\;\geq\;\tfrac{g^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\,G_{\rho,t}^{4}\,-\,\tfrac{2}{\eta^{2}}\|H\|\,\|[G_{\rho,t},H_{\rho^{\prime}}]\|\,{\bf 1}\,.

The commutator can be bounded using (
18

\|[G_{\rho,t},H_{\rho^{\prime}}]\|\;=\;t\|[G_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(|D_{0}|),H_{\rho^{\prime}}]\|\;=\;t\|[G_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(|D_{0}|),H]\|\;\leq\;\frac{16}{\rho}\,\|[D_{0},H]\|,

where
[G_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(|D_{0}|),H_{\rho^{\prime}}\oplus H_{\rho^{\prime}}]=[G_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(|D_{0}|),H]

R+\frac{\rho}{2}\leq\rho\leq\rho^{\prime}
so that periodic boundary conditions do not interfere in the commutator.
Replacing this, one concludes from (
20

\displaystyle L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho,\rho^{\prime}}(t)^{2}\;\geq\;

\displaystyle\Big(2\,-\,\big(2\lambda+\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}\|\big)\,G_{\rho,t}^{2}\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\,+\,\,\frac{g^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\,G_{\rho,t}^{4}

\displaystyle\,-\,\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\eta^{2}\rho^{\prime}}\,\max\{\|H\|,\lambda\eta\}\,d\,M\,{\bf 1}\,-\,\frac{5\sqrt{\pi}}{\lambda\eta^{2}\sqrt{2\rho^{\prime}}}\,\|H\|^{\frac{3}{2}}d\sqrt{M}\,{\bf 1}

\displaystyle-\frac{32\|H\|}{\rho\eta^{2}}\,\|[D_{0},H]\|\,{\bf 1}\,-\,\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|[s_{\rho^{\prime},j},H]\|\,{\bf 1}\;.

Using
\sum_{j=1}^{d}(1-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\leq 2d
and also bounding
\|[s_{\rho^{\prime},j},H_{\rho^{\prime}}]\|\leq\frac{\pi}{\rho^{\prime}}M
by Lemma
11

\|[D_{0},H]\|\leq dM
, this implies

\displaystyle L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho,\rho^{\prime}}(t)^{2}\;\geq\;

\displaystyle\Big(2\,-\,\big(2\lambda+\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}\|\big)\,G_{\rho,t}^{2}\,+\,\frac{g^{2}}{4d\eta^{2}}\,G_{\rho,t}^{4}\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\,+\,\frac{g^{2}}{2\eta^{2}}\,G_{\rho,t}^{4}

\displaystyle\,-\,\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\eta^{2}\rho^{\prime}}\,\max\{\|H\|,\lambda\eta\}\,d\,M\,{\bf 1}\,-\,\frac{5\sqrt{\pi}}{\lambda\eta^{2}\sqrt{2\rho^{\prime}}}\,\|H\|^{\frac{3}{2}}d\sqrt{M}\,{\bf 1}

\displaystyle-\frac{32\|H\|}{\rho\eta^{2}}\,d\,M\,-\,\frac{\pi}{\eta\rho^{\prime}}\,d\,M\,{\bf 1}\;.

Now the parenthesis in the first summand seen as a function of
G_{\rho,t}^{2}
has a negative derivative for all
G_{\rho,t}^{2}\in[0,1]
as long as

2\lambda+\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}\|\;\geq\;\frac{g^{2}}{2d\eta^{2}}\;,

which, after discarding the summand
\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}\|
, actually always holds for
\lambda\geq\frac{g}{\eta}
and
\eta\geq\frac{g}{4d}
(which is required in Theorem
1
). Then the minimum of the parenthesis is taken at
G_{t}^{2}=1
. Hence

\displaystyle L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho,\rho^{\prime}}(t)^{2}\;\geq\;

\displaystyle\Big(2\,-\,\big(2\lambda+\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}\|\big)\,+\,\frac{g^{2}}{4d\eta^{2}}\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\,+\,\frac{g^{2}}{2\eta^{2}}\,G_{\rho,t}^{4}

\displaystyle\,-\,\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\eta^{2}\rho^{\prime}}\,\max\{\|H\|,\lambda\eta\}\,d\,M{\bf 1}\,-\,\frac{5\sqrt{\pi}}{\lambda\eta^{2}\sqrt{2\rho^{\prime}}}\,\|H\|^{\frac{3}{2}}d\sqrt{M}{\bf 1}

\displaystyle-\frac{32\|H\|}{\rho\eta^{2}}\,d\,M{\bf 1}\,-\,\frac{\pi}{\eta\rho^{\prime}}\,d\,M{\bf 1}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;

\displaystyle\Big(2\,-\,\big(2\lambda+\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}\|\big)\,+\,\frac{g^{2}}{8d\eta^{2}}\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{d}({\bf 1}-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\,+\,\frac{g^{2}\pi^{2}}{2560d\eta^{2}}\,{\bf 1}

\displaystyle\,-\,\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\eta^{2}\rho^{\prime}}\,\max\{\|H\|,\lambda\eta\}\,d\,M{\bf 1}\,-\,\frac{5\sqrt{\pi}}{\lambda\eta^{2}\sqrt{2\rho^{\prime}}}\,\|H\|^{\frac{3}{2}}d\sqrt{M}{\bf 1}

\displaystyle-\frac{32\|H\|}{\rho\eta^{2}}\,d\,M{\bf 1}\,-\,\frac{\pi}{\eta\rho^{\prime}}\,d\,M{\bf 1}\;,

because
\sum_{j=1}^{d}(1-c_{\rho^{\prime},j})\geq\frac{1}{5}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\pi^{2}}{(\rho^{\prime})^{2}}X_{j}^{2}\geq\frac{\pi^{2}\rho^{2}}{80(\rho^{\prime})^{2}}({\bf 1}-G_{t}^{4})\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{320}({\bf 1}-G_{t}^{4})
where the second step holds as
({\bf 1}-G_{t}^{4})\chi\big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}^{2}\leq\frac{\rho^{2}}{16}\big)=0
and the final step used the bound
\rho^{\prime}\leq 2\rho
. Because
{\rm spec}(H_{\rho^{\prime}})\subset{\rm spec}(H)

\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}\|\;\leq\;\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\tfrac{H^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\|\;.

Thus let us minimize
f(\lambda)=2\lambda+\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\frac{H^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\|
over
\lambda\in[\frac{g}{\eta},\frac{\|H\|}{\eta}]
. By spectral calculus and elementary analysis one finds

\min_{\eta\lambda\in[g,\|H\|]}\;f(\lambda)\;=\;\min_{\eta\lambda\in[g,\|H\|]}\;\lambda\Big(2+\max\Big\{1-\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}\eta^{2}}\,,\,\frac{\|H\|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}\eta^{2}}-1\Big\}\Big)\;=\;f(\lambda_{c})

(\lambda_{c})^{2}=\frac{\|H\|^{2}+g^{2}}{2\eta^{2}}
. Thus

\displaystyle f(\lambda_{c})

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{1}{\eta}\,\frac{g^{2}+3\|H\|^{2}}{\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{g^{2}+\|H\|^{2}}}\;=\;\frac{\|H\|}{\eta}\,\Big(\sqrt{1-\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\tfrac{g^{2}}{\|H\|^{2}})}\;+\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\tfrac{g^{2}}{\|H\|^{2}})}}\Big)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\frac{\|H\|}{\eta}\Big(2+\frac{1}{8}\big(1-\frac{g^{2}}{\|H\|^{2}}\big)^{2}\Big)\;\leq\;\frac{\|H\|}{\eta}\Big(2+\frac{1}{2}\big(1-\frac{g}{\|H\|}\big)^{2}\Big)\;,

where the first inequality follows from
\sqrt{1+\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon}}\leq 2+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{2}
holding for
\epsilon\in[-\frac{1}{2},1]
. Then the term in the parenthesis satisfies

2-\big(2\lambda+\|\lambda\,{\bf 1}-\lambda^{-1}\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{2}\|\big)+\frac{g^{2}}{4d\eta^{2}}\,\geq\,2-f(\lambda_{c})+\frac{g^{2}}{8d\eta^{2}}\,\geq\,2-\frac{\|H\|}{\eta}\Big(2+\frac{1}{2}\big(1-\frac{g}{\|H\|}\big)^{2}\Big)+\frac{g^{2}}{8d\eta^{2}}\,\geq\,0\,,

where the last inequality is precisely the bound (
3
) divided by
\frac{\|H\|}{2\eta}
. Due to
\|H\|\geq\eta\lambda_{c}\geq g
and the equality
\max\{\|H\|,\lambda_{c}\eta\}=\|H\|
one hence deduces

\displaystyle L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho,\rho^{\prime}}(t)^{2}\,\geq\,\frac{g^{2}\pi^{2}}{2560d\eta^{2}}\,{\bf 1}

\displaystyle\,-\,\frac{2\pi}{g\eta\rho^{\prime}}\|H\|\,d\,M{\bf 1}\,-\,\frac{5\sqrt{\pi}}{g\eta\sqrt{2\rho^{\prime}}}\,\|H\|^{\frac{3}{2}}d\sqrt{M}{\bf 1}

4
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-invariants via periodic spectral localizer

This section addresses the real cases of the CAZ (Cartan-Altland-Zirnbauer) classification. They all impose a symmetry property on the Hamiltonian that involves a complex conjugation (real structure, denoted by an overline) on the complex Hilbert space. There are 64 such cases, stemming from an
8
-periodicity in both dimension
d
and the CAZ classes (both routed in Bott periodicity). Only
16
of these cases are known to lead to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-valued strong invariants
[
19
,
10
]
. In previous works
[
13
,
4
]
it was shown that a real skew-adjoint version of the spectral localizer, the so-called skew localizer, can be used to compute these
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-indices. In this section the associated skew periodic localizer is introduced for the physically most relevant low-dimensional cases, and it is shown that the sign of its Pfaffian is connected to the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-invariants. An exhaustive treatment of all cases as in
[
4
]
is not provided here.

Let us now sketch the general common scheme. Like in
[
4
]
, the skew periodic localizer is constructed from the periodic localizer by a basis change and multiplication by
\imath
. More explicitly, in each of the relevant CAZ classes in even dimension
d
, there is a unitary
R:({\cal H}_{\rho}\oplus{\cal H}_{\rho})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}\to({\cal H}_{\rho}\oplus{\cal H}_{\rho})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}
such that the skew periodic localizer given by

L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny skew,per}}_{\eta,\rho}\;=\;\imath\,R^{*}L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny per}}_{\eta,\rho}R

is a real and skew-adjoint operator on
({\cal H}_{\rho}\oplus{\cal H}_{\rho})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}
. For odd
d
, the only modification is that
R:{\cal H}_{\rho}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}\to{\cal H}_{\rho}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}
and
L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny skew,per}}_{\eta,\rho}
then acts on
{\cal H}_{\rho}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}}
. Cleary
R
depends on
\rho
, but as
R
is local, this dependence is suppressed in the notation. For even
d
and
(\eta,\rho)
as in Theorem
1
and for odd
d
and
(\eta,\rho)
as in Theorem
15
this operator is invertible and therefore has a non-vanishing Pfaffian. Moreover, let
\widetilde{D}
be a suitable perturbation of the first summand of the periodic spectral localizer (
1
) or (
27
) by a term localized at the origin such that
\widetilde{D}
is invertible and such that
D^{\mbox{\rm\tiny skew}}=\imath\,R^{*}\widetilde{D}R
is real and skew-adjoint. Then its Pfaffian is well-defined, does not vanish and the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-index associated to the Hamiltonian
H
is in each case proven to be given by

{\rm Ind}_{2}(T)\;=\;{\rm sgn}({\rm Pf}(L^{\mbox{\rm\tiny skew,per}}_{\eta,\rho}))\,{\rm sgn}({\rm Pf}(D^{\mbox{\rm\tiny skew}}_{\rho}))\,\in\,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\;,

(30)

where
{\rm Ind}_{2}(T)=\dim({\rm Ker}(T))\,\mbox{\rm mod}\,2
in the
16
relevant cases is defined as in
[
10
]
using the Fredholm operators

or
T=EAE+({\bf 1}-E)
with
F
being the Dirac phase and

5
Fuzzy tori and their invariants

This section develops a general theory of fuzzy tori and their invariants. Here the terminology of fuzzy geometric object is meant in the following sense: consider a classical geometric object (compact or non-compact) as a subset of an Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
defined by a set of equations in the components of
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
; replace these coordinates or functions of them by operators in some algebra and ask the defining equations to be satisfied only approximately, namely up to errors in operator norm; then these operators are called a fuzzy geometric object. As this is a particular case of algebras defined by relation, an abstract study of the
K
-theoretic invariants of such fuzzy objects has been known for a long time
[
8
,
6
,
7
]
. The construction of these invariants was essentially based on the replacement of the fuzzy object into classical maps from the geometric object to a sphere
[
11
]
. This leads to correct, but not very practical maps. Here we rather use relatively easy polynomial maps into the dotted Euclidean space and show that they do allow to construct the invariants, actually in a much easier manner that can be implemented numerically. The focus is only on fuzzy tori, because of their great relevance for solid state systems and hence connections to the first part of the paper. More precisely, it is shown how these abstract constructions applied to natural fuzzy tori associated to the situations analyzed in Sections
1
to
3
directly lead to the periodic spectral localizers.

Definition 19

∗

(36)

Proposition 29

For unitaries
U_{1},\ldots,U_{d}\in{\mathbb{U}}({\cal H})
, the operator

satisfies

G^{2}\;\geq\;\left(1-7\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\|[U_{j},U_{i}]\|\right){\bf 1}\;.

(41)

In particular,
G
is invertible if the unitaries
U_{1},\ldots,U_{d}\in{\mathbb{U}}({\cal H})
form a fuzzy
d
-torus of width
\delta
satisfying

1-\binom{d}{2}7\delta>0
.

Proof.

The argument is essentially identical to the one leading to Proposition 4.3 in
[
12
]
. Using the Clifford relations, one finds

\displaystyle G^{2}\;=\;

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Im m(U_{j})^{2}+\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(U_{j})\right)^{2}\right)\otimes{\bf 1}+\,\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}[\Im m(U_{j}),\Im m(U_{i})]\otimes\gamma_{j}\gamma_{i}

\displaystyle\;\;+\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}[\Im m(U_{j}),(d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Re e(U_{i})]\otimes\gamma_{j}\gamma_{d+1}\;.

Using that

\|[\Im m(U_{j}),\Im m(U_{i})]\|\;\leq\;\|[U_{j},U_{i}]\|\;,\qquad\|[\Im m(U_{j}),\Re e(U_{i})]\|\;\leq\;\|[U_{j},U_{i}]\|

and that
\gamma_{j}
is unitary for all
j
one obtains

\displaystyle G^{2}\;\geq\;

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Im m(U_{j})^{2}+\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(U_{j})\right)^{2}\right)\otimes{\bf 1}\,-\,3\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\|[U_{j},U_{i}]\|{\bf 1}\;.

(42)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}

\displaystyle\Im m(U_{j})^{2}+\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(U_{j})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Im m(U_{j})^{2}+(d-1)^{2}{\bf 1}-2(d-1)\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(U_{j})+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(U_{j})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle d{\bf 1}+(d-1)^{2}{\bf 1}-2(d-1)\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(U_{j})+\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}^{d}(\Re e(U_{j})\Re e(U_{i})+\Re e(U_{i})\Re e(U_{j}))\;,

where the last step follows from
\Im m(U_{j})^{2}+\Re e(U_{j})^{2}={\bf 1}
. This simplifies to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}

\displaystyle\Im m(U_{j})^{2}+\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(U_{j})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;=\;{\bf 1}+\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\big(({\bf 1}-\Re e(U_{j}))({\bf 1}-\Re e(U_{i}))+({\bf 1}-\Re e(U_{i}))({\bf 1}-\Re e(U_{j}))\big)\;.

(43)

One directly checks that
{\bf 1}-\Re e(U_{j})=\frac{1}{2}(U_{j}-{\bf 1})(U_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*}
and therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Im m(U_{j})^{2}+\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(U_{j})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;=\;{\bf 1}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\Big((U_{j}-{\bf 1})(U_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*}(U_{i}-{\bf 1})(U_{i}-{\bf 1})^{*}+(U_{i}-{\bf 1})(U_{i}-{\bf 1})^{*}(U_{j}-{\bf 1})(U_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*}\Big)

\displaystyle\;=\;{\bf 1}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\!\!\Big((U_{j}-{\bf 1})(U_{i}-{\bf 1})(U_{i}-{\bf 1})^{*}(U_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*}+(U_{j}-{\bf 1})[(U_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*},(U_{i}-{\bf 1})](U_{i}-{\bf 1})^{*}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+(U_{j}-{\bf 1})(U_{i}-{\bf 1})[(U_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*},(U_{i}-{\bf 1})^{*}]+(U_{i}-{\bf 1})(U_{j}-{\bf 1})(U_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*}(U_{i}-{\bf 1})^{*}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+(U_{i}-{\bf 1})[(U_{i}-{\bf 1})^{*},(U_{j}-{\bf 1})](U_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*}+(U_{i}-{\bf 1})(U_{j}-{\bf 1})[(U_{i}-{\bf 1})^{*},(U_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*}]\Big).

Using that the first and fourth summand are non-negative and

\|(U_{j}-{\bf 1})\|\leq 2
,
\|(U_{i}-{\bf 1})\|\leq 2
, one obtains

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Im m(U_{j})^{2}+

\displaystyle\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(U_{j})\right)^{2}\;\geq\;\big(1-4\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\|[U_{j},U_{i}]\|\big){\bf 1}\;.

Combining with (
42
) the claim (
41
) follows.

Proposition 32

form a fuzzy
d

the selfadjoint operator

\displaystyle G^{2}\;\geq\;\big(1\,-\,33\,d^{2}\delta\big){\bf 1}\;.

(44)

In particular,
G
is invertible if
\delta<\frac{1}{33\,d^{2}}
.

Proof.

Using the Clifford relations one has

\displaystyle G^{2}

\displaystyle\;=\;\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Im m(A_{j})^{2}+\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(A_{j})\right)^{2}\right)+\,\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}[\Im m(A_{j}),\Im m(A_{i})]\otimes\gamma_{j}\gamma_{i}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}[\Im m(A_{j}),(d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Re e(A_{i})]\otimes\gamma_{j}\gamma_{d+1}

\displaystyle\geq\;\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Im m(A_{j})^{2}+\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(A_{j})\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-\,\left(\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\|[\Im m(A_{j}),\Im m(A_{i})]\|\,-\,\sum_{j,i=1}^{d}\|[\Im m(A_{j}),\Re e(A_{i})]\|\right)\;.

(45)

Using that
{\rm spec}(|A_{j}|)\subset[(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}},(1+\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}]
and
\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
, one checks that

\|A_{j}\|\,=\,\||A_{j}|\|\,\leq\,(1+\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\leq\,1+\frac{\delta}{2}\;,\qquad\|A_{j}^{-1}\|^{2}\,\leq\,(1-\delta)^{-1}\,\leq\,1+2\delta\;.

(46)

Therefore

\displaystyle\|[A_{i},A_{j}^{*}]\|

\displaystyle\;=\;\|A_{j}^{-1}(A_{j}A_{i}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}-A_{j}A_{j}^{*}A_{i}A_{j})A_{j}^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\|A_{j}^{-1}\|^{2}\,\|A_{j}A_{i}(A_{j}^{*}A_{j}-{\bf 1})+[A_{j},A_{i}]+({\bf 1}-A_{j}A_{j}^{*})A_{i}A_{j}\|

\displaystyle\;\leq\;(1+2\delta)\big(2(1+\delta)\delta+\delta\big)\;\leq\;8\,\delta\;.

(47)

This leads to

\displaystyle\|[\Im m(A_{j}),\Im m(A_{i})]\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(\big\|[A_{j},A_{i}]\big\|+\big\|[A_{i},A_{j}^{*}]\big\|+\big\|[A_{i}^{*},A_{j}]\big\|+\big\|[A_{j}^{*},A_{i}^{*}]\big\|\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\delta+8\delta)=5\,\delta\;.

In the same way one shows the bound

\displaystyle\|[\Im m(A_{j}),\Re e(A_{i})]\|\;\leq\;\frac{1}{4}\left(\big\|[A_{j},A_{i}]\big\|+\big\|[A_{i},A_{j}^{*}]\big\|+\big\|[A_{j},A_{i}^{*}]\big\|+\big\|[A_{i}^{*},A_{j}^{*}]\big\|\right)\;\leq\;5\,\delta\;,

for
j\neq i
. For
j=i
a slightly better estimate holds

\|[\Im m(A_{j}),\Re e(A_{j})]\|\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\|A_{j}A_{j}^{*}-A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\|\;\leq\;\delta\;.

Inserting this into (
45
) leads to

\displaystyle G^{2}\,-\,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Im m(A_{j})^{2}+\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(A_{j})\right)^{2}\right)\,\geq\,-\left(d\delta+15\binom{d}{2}\delta\right){\bf 1}\,\geq\,-9\,d^{2}\,\delta\,{\bf 1}\;.

(48)

It thus remains to prove a lower bound on the term in the parenthesis. Using

\Im m(A_{j})^{2}+\Re e(A_{j})^{2}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}(A_{j}A_{j}^{*}+A_{j}^{*}A_{j})\;\geq\;{\bf 1}-\frac{1}{2}\|(A_{j}A_{j}^{*}-{\bf 1})+(A_{j}^{*}A_{j}-{\bf 1})\|{\bf 1}\;\geq\;(1-\delta){\bf 1}\,,

one finds

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Im m(A_{j})^{2}+\left((d-1){\bf 1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(A_{j})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;d(1-\delta){\bf 1}+(d-1)^{2}{\bf 1}-2(d-1)\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Re e(A_{j})\,+\!\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}^{d}\!\big(\Re e(A_{j})\Re e(A_{i})+\Re e(A_{i})\Re e(A_{j})\big)

\displaystyle\;=\;{\bf 1}+\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\big(({\bf 1}-\Re e(A_{j}))({\bf 1}-\Re e(A_{i}))+({\bf 1}-\Re e(A_{i}))({\bf 1}-\Re e(A_{j}))\big)-d\delta{\bf 1}\;.

(49)

One directly checks that

\frac{1}{2}(A_{j}-{\bf 1})(A_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*}-({\bf 1}-\Re e(A_{j}))\;=\;\frac{1}{2}(A_{j}A_{j}^{*}-{\bf 1})

and therefore, by the first part of (
36
),

\big\|\frac{1}{2}(A_{j}-{\bf 1})(A_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*}-({\bf 1}-\Re e(A_{j}))\big\|\;\leq\;\frac{1}{2}\,\delta\;.

Moreover, using (
46
)

\|{\bf 1}-\Re e(A_{j})\|\;\leq\;1+(1+\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\leq\,\frac{5}{2}\;,\qquad\|A_{j}-{\bf 1}\|^{2}\;\leq\;\big(1+(1+\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}\,\leq\,5\;.

Using the last two bounds one gets

\displaystyle\big\|\frac{1}{4}(A_{j}-{\bf 1})(A_{j}-{\bf 1})^{*}(A_{i}-{\bf 1})(A_{i}-{\bf 1})^{*}-({\bf 1}-\Re e(A_{j}))({\bf 1}-\Re e(A_{i}))\big\|

Appendix A
Mapping degree versus Chern number

For the convenience of the reader, this appendix provides a detailed proof of the connection between mapping degree of a differentiable function
f:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
on an even-dimensional torus and the Chern number of an associated matrix-valued projection
P_{f}:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}
with
d^{\prime}=2^{\frac{d}{2}}
. This fact is used in Appendix
B
which is crucial for an understanding the motivation for the periodic spectral localizer and the
G
-operators in Section
5
. While the main statement, Corollary
37
below, is certainly well-known in the community, we could not localize a detailed proof.

For
d
be even and let denote the restrictions of the euclidean coordinate functions to
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
by
x_{j}:{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,d+1\}
. More precisely,

x_{j}(y)\;=\;y_{j}\;,\qquad y\,=\,\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\\
\vdots\\
y_{d+1}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\;.

Furthermore, let
f_{j}=x_{j}\circ f:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be
j
th component of the function
f
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,d+1\}
. Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d+1}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}
be an irreducible self-adjoint representation of the Clifford algebra with the convention that

\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d+1}\;=\;\imath^{\frac{d}{2}}{\bf 1}\;.

(50)

Then let us define the map
P_{f}:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}
by

P_{f}(k)\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}f_{j}(k)\gamma_{j}+{\bf 1}\Big)\;.

Then
P_{f}(k)
is an (orthogonal) projection for all
k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Its exterior derivative is
dP_{f}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\gamma_{j}df_{j}
, a matrix-valued
1
-form on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Then let us set

\omega_{f}\;=\;\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(P_{f}(dP_{f}\wedge dP_{f})^{\wedge\frac{d}{2}}\big)\;,

which is a
d
-form on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Using (
50
) as
\mbox{\rm Tr}({\bf 1})=2^{\frac{d}{2}}
, it is explicitly given by

\displaystyle\omega_{f}

\displaystyle\;=\;C_{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}(-1)^{j+1}f_{j}df_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge df_{j-1}\wedge df_{j+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge df_{d+1}\;,\qquad C_{d}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2^{d}}d!2^{\frac{d}{2}}\imath^{\frac{d}{2}}

Similarly, let us define another projection-valued map
P^{W}:{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}
(called the Weyl projection) by

P^{W}(p)\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}x_{j}(p)\gamma_{j}+{\bf 1}\Big)\;.

Similar as above, there is an associated
d
-form on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
given by

\displaystyle\omega^{W}\,=\,\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(P^{W}(dP^{W}\wedge dP^{W})^{\wedge\frac{d}{2}}\big)\,=\,C_{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}(-1)^{j+1}x_{j}dx_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx_{j-1}\wedge dx_{j+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx_{d+1}\;.

Proposition 36

For any differentiable map
f:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
, the differential form
\omega_{f}
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
equals the pullback of the differential form
\omega^{W}
by
f
:

\omega_{f}\;=\;f^{*}\omega^{W}\;.

Proof.

For a point
k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
let
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{d}:(-a,a)\to{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
for
a>0
represent tangent vectors of
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
at the point
k
, namely
\eta_{j}
is a differentiable curve fulfilling
\eta_{j}(0)=k
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. The tangent vector represented by
\eta_{j}
is denoted by
[\eta_{j}]
. Then one has to show

\omega_{f}([\eta_{1}],\ldots,[\eta_{d}])\;=\;\omega^{W}(df_{k}([\eta_{1}]),\ldots,df_{k}([\eta_{d}]))\;,

(51)

where
df_{k}
denotes the differential of
f
at the point
k
. A direct computation shows

\displaystyle\omega_{f}([\eta_{1}],\ldots,[\eta_{d}])

\displaystyle\;=\;C_{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}(-1)^{j+1}f_{j}df_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge df_{j-1}\wedge df_{j+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge df_{d+1}([\eta_{1}],\ldots,[\eta_{d}])

\displaystyle\;=\;C_{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}(-1)^{j+1}f_{j}(k)\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}df_{1}([\eta_{\sigma(1)}])\cdots df_{j-1}([\eta_{\sigma(j-1)}])df_{j+1}([\eta_{\sigma(j)}])\cdots df_{d+1}([\eta_{\sigma(d)}])

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{C_{d}}{d!}\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}(-1)^{j+1}f_{j}(k)\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}(f_{1}\circ\eta_{\sigma(1)})^{\prime}(0)\cdots(f_{j-1}\circ\eta_{\sigma(j-1)})^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt(f_{j+1}\circ\eta_{\sigma(j)})^{\prime}(0)\cdots(f_{d+1}\circ\eta_{\sigma(d)})^{\prime}(0)\;.

In a similar manner one checks

\displaystyle\omega^{W}(df_{k}([\eta_{1}]),\ldots,df_{k}([\eta_{d}]))

\displaystyle\;=\;C_{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}(-1)^{j+1}x_{j}dx_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx_{j-1}\wedge dx_{j+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx_{d+1}(df_{k}([\eta_{1}]),\ldots,df_{k}([\eta_{d}]))

\displaystyle\;=\;C_{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}(-1)^{j+1}x_{j}(f(k))\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}dx_{1}(df_{k}[\eta_{\sigma(1)}])\cdots dx_{j-1}(df_{k}[\eta_{\sigma(j-1)}])

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652ptdx_{j+1}(df_{k}[\eta_{\sigma(j)}])\cdots dx_{d+1}(df_{k}[\eta_{\sigma(d)}])

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{C_{d}}{d!}\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}(-1)^{j+1}f_{j}(k)\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}(x_{1}\circ f\circ\eta_{\sigma(1)})^{\prime}(0)\cdots(x_{1}\circ f\circ\eta_{\sigma(j-1)})^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt(x_{j+1}\circ f\circ\eta_{\sigma(j)})^{\prime}(0)\cdots(x_{d+1}\circ f\circ\eta_{\sigma(d)})^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{C_{d}}{d!}\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}(-1)^{j+1}f_{j}(k)\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}(f_{1}\circ\eta_{\sigma(1)})^{\prime}(0)\cdots(f_{j-1}\circ\eta_{\sigma(j-1)})^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt(f_{j+1}\circ\eta_{\sigma(j)})^{\prime}(0)\cdots(f_{d+1}\circ\eta_{\sigma(d)})^{\prime}(0)\;.

Therefore (
51
) holds and the claim follows.

\Box

Now by a well-known pullback formula (
e.g.
tom Dieck’s lecture notes
[
22
]
contain a detailed proof) one has

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\omega_{f}\;=\;\deg(f)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}\omega^{W}\;,

where
\deg(f)
is the mapping degree of
f
, generically defined as the sum over all preimages (of a fixed point) of the signs of the determinants of the Jacobians. Next recall (
e.g.

[
18
]
) the definition of the
d
th Chern number of a differentiable projection-valued map
P:{\cal M}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{L\times L}
on a
d
-dimensional manifold
{\cal M}
:

{\rm Ch}_{d}(P)\;=\;(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}(\tfrac{1}{2\pi\imath})^{\frac{d}{2}}\frac{1}{\frac{d}{2}!}\int_{{\cal M}^{d}}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(P(dP)^{d}\big)\;.

Then for
\omega_{f}=\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(P_{f}(dP_{f})^{d}\big)
as above, one obtains

{\rm Ch}_{d}(P_{f})\;=\;\deg(f)(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}(\tfrac{1}{2\pi i})^{\frac{d}{2}}\frac{1}{\frac{d}{2}!}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}\omega^{W}\;=\;\deg(f)\,{\rm Ch}_{d}(P^{W})\;.

It hence remains to compute
{\rm Ch}_{d}(P^{W})
which is again well-known:

{\rm Ch}_{d}(P^{W})\;=\;(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}\;.

(
E.g.

[
3
,
21
]
contains a detailed computation.) Summing up, one concludes:

Corollary 37

For
d
even and a smooth map
f:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
, the
d
th Chern number of
P_{f}
is

{\rm Ch}_{d}(P_{f})\;=\;(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}\deg(f)\;.

{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
is equipped with the orientation inherited from the atlas
(U_{j},\varphi_{j})_{j\in\{1,2\}}
and
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
is equipped with the orientation inherited from the atlas
(V_{j},\psi_{j})_{j\in\{1,2\}}
the degree of the map
f_{d,m}
defined by (
53
) is given by

\deg(f_{d,m})\;=\;\begin{cases}\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{d-n-1}{2}}(-1)^{k}\binom{d}{k}\,,&n\in(0,d+1)\cap 2{\mathbb{N}}+1,m\in(n-1,n+1)\;,\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{d-n-1}{2}}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{d}{k}\,,&n\in(0,d+1)\cap 2{\mathbb{N}}+1,m\in(-n-1,-n+1)\;,\\
0\,,&m\in(-\infty,-d)\cup(d,\infty)\,.\end{cases}

Proof.
By the homotopy invariance of the mapping degree, it is sufficient to consider the case
m\in\{-d-1,-d+1,\ldots,d+1\}
. For the case
m=d+1
, one can consider the maps

g_{d,d+1,t}\big(e^{\imath{\bm{\theta}}}\big)\;=\;\Big(t\sin(\theta_{1}),\ldots,t\sin(\theta_{d}),1-t\sum_{n=1}^{d}(\cos(\theta_{n})-1)\Big)\;,

where
t\in[0,1]
. Then
g_{d,d+1,1}=g_{d,d+1}
and
g_{d,d+1,0}(e^{\imath{\bm{\theta}}})=p_{N}
where
p_{N}=(0,\ldots,0,1)\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
is the north pole. Now the norm satisfies

\|g_{d,d+1,t}\big(e^{\imath{\bm{\theta}}}\big)\|^{2}\;=\;t^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\theta_{j})+1+2t\sum_{j=1}^{d}(1-\cos(\theta_{j}))+t^{2}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}(1-\cos(\theta_{j}))\Big)^{2}\;\geq\;1\;.

This implies that
t\in[0,1]\mapsto f_{d,d+1,t}=\|g_{d,d+1,t}\|^{-1}g_{d,d+1,t}
is a homotopy from
f_{d,d+1}
to a constant map. Hence the mapping degree vanishes. (Note that this argument is essentially reproduced for fuzzy tori in Lemma
34
.) Let us next focus on the case
m\in\{1,3,\ldots,d-1\}
. The other points are dealt with in a similar manner (with the north instead of the south pole used in the argument below). Consider the south pole given by
p_{S}=(0,\ldots,0,-1)\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
. Its inverse image is

\displaystyle f_{d,m}^{-1}(p_{S})\;=\;\big\{e^{\imath{\bm{\theta}}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}:\theta_{n}\in\{0,\pi\}\;\text{for}\;n\in\{1,\ldots,d\},\theta_{j}=\pi\;\text{for at most}\;\tfrac{d-m-1}{2}\;\text{many}\;j\big\}\;.

Then

\psi_{1}\circ f_{d,m}\circ\varphi_{2}^{-1}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\big\{\hat{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{n}\in\{\pm 1\},\#\{j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:x_{j}=-1\}>\tfrac{d-m-1}{2}\big\}\;\to\;{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

is given by

\displaystyle(\psi_{1}\circ f_{d,m}\circ\varphi_{2}^{-1})\big(\hat{\bm{x}}\big)\;=\;\left(\tfrac{1-x_{1}^{2}}{x_{1}^{2}+1}\big(1-m+2\sum_{n=1}^{d}\tfrac{x_{n}}{x_{n}^{2}+1}\big)^{-1},\ldots,\tfrac{1-x_{d}^{2}}{x_{d}^{2}+1}\big(1-m+2\sum_{n=1}^{d}\tfrac{x_{n}}{x_{n}^{2}+1}\big)^{-1}\right).

Its
k
th component is

(\psi_{1}\circ f_{d,m}\circ\varphi_{2}^{-1})\big(\hat{\bm{x}}\big)_{k}\;=\;\frac{(1-x_{k}^{2})\prod_{n\neq k}(x_{n}^{2}+1)}{(1-m)\prod_{n=1}^{d}(x_{n}^{2}+1)+2\sum_{n=1}^{d}x_{n}\prod_{j\neq n}(x_{j}^{2}+1)}\;.

(54)

Let us denote the Jacobian matrix of this map by

J=J_{\psi_{1}\circ f_{d,m}\circ\varphi_{2}^{-1}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\big\{\hat{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{n}\in\{\pm 1\},\#\{j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:x_{j}=-1\}>\tfrac{d-m-1}{2}\big\}\;\to\;{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;.

To determine the mapping degree of
f
(at the point
p_{S}
) it is sufficient to compute the restriction of
J
to
{\mathbb{M}}=\varphi_{2}(f^{-1}(p_{S}))
explicitly given by

{\mathbb{M}}\;=\;\big\{\hat{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{n}\in\{-1,1\}\;\text{for}\;n\in\{1,\ldots,d\},x_{j}=-1\;\text{for at most}\;\tfrac{d-m-1}{2}\;\text{many}\;j\big\}\;.

One directly checks that the off-diagonal entries of
J|_{\mathbb{M}}
vanish, namely
J(\hat{\bm{x}})_{k,l}=0
for all
\hat{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{M}}
and
k,l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
k\neq l
. The diagonal entries of
J|_{\mathbb{M}}
are

J(\hat{\bm{x}})_{k,k}\;=\;\frac{-x_{k}2^{d}}{((1-m)2^{d}+2^{d}\sum_{n=1}^{d}x_{j})^{2}}

(55)

for
\hat{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{M}}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Therefore
{\rm sgn}(J(\hat{\bm{x}})_{k,k})=-{\rm sgn}(x_{k})
and

{\rm sgn}(\det(J(\hat{\bm{x}})))\;=\;(-1)^{\#\{j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:x_{j}=1\}}\;=\;(-1)^{\#\{j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:x_{j}=-1\}}\;,

where the last equality holds because
d
is even. Because

\#\big\{\hat{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{n}\in\{-1,1\}\;\text{for}\;n\in\{1,\ldots,d\},x_{j}=-1\;\text{for}\;k\;\text{many}\;j\big\}\;=\;\binom{d}{k}\;,

one has

\deg(f_{d,m},p_{S})\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{d-n-1}{2}}(-1)^{k}\binom{d}{k}\;.

As the mapping degree is independent of the point at which the preimage is taken (provided it has a finite preimage), this shows the claim for
m\in\{1,3,\ldots,d-1\}
.

\Box

An upper bound and a characterization for Gini’s mean difference based on correlated random variables
Roberto Vila, Narayanaswamy Balakrishnan, Helton Saulo

Abstract
In this paper, we obtain an upper bound for the Gini mean difference based on mean, variance and correlation for the case when the variables are correlated. We also derive some closed-form expressions for the Gini mean difference when the random variables have an absolutely continuous joint distribution. We then examine some particular examples based on elliptically contoured distributions, and specifically multivariate normal and Student- t t distributions. Keywords: Gini’s mean difference ⋅ \cdot Gini index ⋅ \cdot Correlation ⋅ \cdot Elliptically contoured distributions ⋅ \cdot Exchangeable variables ⋅ \cdot Maximum ⋅ \cdot Minimum ⋅ \cdot Skew symmetric distribution.



1
Introduction

Based on the random variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
, the Gini mean difference (GMD) is defined as

\displaystyle GMD_{n}=\dfrac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}{\mathbb{E}(|X_{i}-X_{j}|)},

(1)

provided the involved expectations exist. When
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
is a random sample (sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables), we obtain the classical GMD
(
La Haye and Zizler, 2019
;
Schezhtman and Yitzhaki, 1987
, see)
, given by

\displaystyle GMD=\mathbb{E}(|X_{1}-X_{2}|).

(2)

The GMD is a very useful measure of variability in the presence of non-normality.
In these cases, the GMD is superior to variance, in three different aspects: stochastic dominance, exchangeability and stratification; see
Yitzhaki, 2003
and
Yitzhaki, 2015
for details on other interesting features of the GMD. This measure has also been reported to be superior than Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients for some distributions; see
Schezhtman and Yitzhaki, 1999
and
Kattumannil et al., 2022
. Based on the GMD,
Schmid and Semeniuk, 2021
introduced tests on positive correlation and methods for monitoring the correlation structure of a process. Interesting applications of the GMD can also be found in survival analysis; as discussed by
Bonetti et al., 2009
, for example.

This work explores some properties of the GMD. First, in Section
2
, we derive an upper bound for the GMD which holds for all distributions with finite second moment. This upper bound is a refinement of the one derived by
Cerone and Dragomir, 2005
. In addition, the derived upper bound does not require independent random variables, as required in the works of
Cerone and Dragomir, 2005
and
Yin et al., 2022
. Next, in Section
3
, we provide more informative results and present in particular methods for the exact calculation of GMD for jointly distributed absolutely continuous random variables. Finally, in Section
4
, we derive the GMD for the multivariate normal and Student-
t
distributions to illustrate the results, developed here.

2
Upper bound for GMD

The following theorem presents an upper bound for the GMD that holds for all distributions with finite second moment.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be random variables with finite second moments, and
let
\mu_{i}=\mathbb{E}(X_{i})
,
\sigma_{i}^{2}={\rm Var}(X_{i})
and
\rho_{i,j}={\rm Corr}(X_{i},X_{j})
, for
i,j=1,\ldots,n
. Then, we have

\displaystyle GMD_{n}\leqslant\dfrac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\left[\sqrt{(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j}\rho_{i,j})^{2}+\sigma_{j}^{2}(1-\rho_{i,j}^{2})}+|\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}|\right].

Proof.

Writing
|X_{i}-X_{j}|=|Y_{i}-Y_{j}+\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}|
, where
Y_{i}=X_{i}-\mu_{i}
and
Y_{j}=X_{j}-\mu_{j}
, and then applying triangular inequality, we get

|X_{i}-X_{j}|\leqslant|Y_{i}-Y_{j}|+|\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}|
.
Now, by Jensen’s inequality, we then have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(|X_{i}-X_{j}|)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\mathbb{E}(|Y_{i}-Y_{j}|^{2})}+|\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{i}-Y_{j})}+|\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}|=\sqrt{{\rm Var}(X_{i}-X_{j})}+|\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}|,

where we have used the invariance with regard to translations of the variance in the last equality. Upon
using the known identity that
{\rm Var}(X_{i}-X_{j})=\sigma_{i}^{2}+\sigma_{j}^{2}-2{\rm Cov}(X_{i},X_{j})
, the expression on the right-hand side can be rewritten as

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sigma_{i}^{2}+\sigma_{j}^{2}-2{\rm Cov}(X_{i},X_{j})}+|\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}|=\sqrt{\sigma_{i}^{2}+\sigma_{j}^{2}-2\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rho_{i,j}}+|\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}|.

Then, from the definition of the GMD in (
1
), the proof gets completed.
∎

In Theorem
1
, upon taking

\mu_{1}=\mathbb{E}(X_{i})
,
\sigma_{1}^{2}={\rm Var}(X_{i})

and
\rho_{i,j}={\rm Corr}(X_{i},X_{j})
, for
i,j=1,\ldots,n
, we deduce

\displaystyle GMD_{n}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\sigma_{1}\,\dfrac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\sqrt{1-\rho_{i,j}}.

(3)

The above inequality has appeared in
Schmid and Semeniuk, 2021
.
Furthermore, if we take
n=2
in (
3
), we obtain

GMD_{2}={\mathbb{E}(|X_{1}-X_{2}|)}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\sigma_{1}\sqrt{1-\rho_{1,2}}
. So, for
\rho_{1,2}=0
,

\displaystyle GMD_{2}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\sigma_{1}.

(4)

We emphasize that the upper bound in (
4
) refines the upper bound of
(4/\sqrt{3})\sigma_{1}
in
Cerone and Dragomir, 2005
, but not the one of
(2/\sqrt{3})\sigma_{1}
presented in

Yin et al., 2022
.
Another point worth mentioning is that the bound in (
4
)
is for variables that
are not necessarily independent, as required in the results of
Cerone and Dragomir, 2005
and
Yin et al., 2022
.

Let us now suppose that
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},
are independent and identically distributed random variables with mean
\mu_{1}
and variance
\sigma_{1}^{2}
, and that the variable
Z_{1}
, the standardized version of
X_{1}
, has a norm in
L^{p}
, denoted by
\lVert Z\rVert_{p}=[\mathbb{E}(|Z|^{p})]^{1/p}
,
p>1
. Moreover, let us denote
X_{(1)}=\min\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
and
X_{(2)}=\max\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
for the smallest and largest of the variables
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, respectively.
Then, from
Barry, 1985
, it follows that

\mathbb{E}(X_{(2)})\leqslant\mu_{1}+\lVert Z_{1}\rVert_{p}[({p-1})/({2p-1})]^{(p-1)/p}\sigma_{1},\ p>1,

and consequently, the GMD in (
2
) is such that

\displaystyle GMD=\mathbb{E}(X_{(2)}-X_{(1)})=2[\mathbb{E}(X_{(2)})-\mu_{1}]\leqslant C_{p}\sigma_{1},

(5)

with

C_{p}=2\lVert Z_{1}\rVert_{p}[(p-1)/(2p-1)]^{(p-1)/p}.

If we take
p=2
in (
5
), the upper bound
C_{2}\sigma_{1}=(2/\sqrt{3})\sigma_{1}
of
Yin et al., 2022
is obtained.
Further, depending on the choice of distribution of
X_{1}
, it is possible to find values of
1<p<2
such that the upper bound
C_{p}\sigma_{1}
is slightly better than
(2/\sqrt{3})\sigma_{1}
.
For example, it is not difficult to verify that, for
X_{1}\sim N(0,1)
and
p=3/2
, we have
C_{3/2}\sigma_{1}={[\Gamma({1/4})]^{2/3}}\sigma_{1}/({\sqrt{2}\sqrt[3]{\pi}})\approx 1.14\sigma_{1}<C_{2}\sigma_{1}=({2/\sqrt{3}})\sigma_{1}\approx 1.15\sigma_{1}.

3
Closed-form for GMD

In this section, we assume that the random variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are absolutely continuous and are not necessarily independent. To state the main result of this section (in Theorem
2
), we write

\displaystyle h_{i,j}(x)=f_{X_{j}}(x)\,{\pi_{i,j}(x)\over R_{i,j}},

(6)

where
\pi_{i,j}(x)=F_{X_{i}|(X_{j}=x)}(x)
denotes the conditional cumulative distribution function (CDF) of
X_{i}|(X_{j}=y)
evaluated at
y=x
,
and
R_{i,j}=\mathbb{P}(X_{i}\leqslant X_{j})=\mathbb{E}[\pi_{i,j}(X_{j})]
is the stress-strength reliability.

Proposition 1
.

The function
h_{i,j}
in (
6
) is a probability density function (PDF).

Proof.

A simple algebraic manipulation shows that

\displaystyle h_{i,j}(x)=f_{X_{i}}(x)\,\dfrac{\pi_{j,i}(x)}{R_{j,i}}

\displaystyle=f_{X_{i}}(x)\,\dfrac{\mathbb{P}(X_{i}\geqslant X_{j}|X_{i}=x)}{R_{j,i}}

(7)

\displaystyle=f_{X_{i}}(x)\,\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{X_{i}-X_{j}|(X_{i}=x)}(y){\rm d}y}{R_{j,i}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{X_{i},X_{i}-X_{j}}(x,y){\rm d}y}{R_{j,i}}=f_{X_{i}|(X_{i}\geqslant X_{j})}(x).

(8)

So, from the above identities, it is evident that
h_{i,j}
is indeed a PDF.
∎

Let
H_{i,j}
be the CDF corresponding to
h_{i,j}
. From here on, we assume that all involved expected values exist.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\bm{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{\top}
be an absolutely continuous random vector. Then the GMD in (
1
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle GMD_{n}=\dfrac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(2R_{j,i}\mu_{H_{j,i}}+2R_{i,j}\mu_{H_{i,j}}-\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}),

where
\mu_{i}=\mathbb{E}(X_{i})
and
\mu_{H_{i,j}}
denotes the expected value with respect to the distribution
H_{i,j}
.

Proof.

Let
m_{ij}=\min\{X_{i},X_{j}\}
and
M_{ij}=\max\{X_{i},X_{j}\}
,
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
.
Then the GMD in (
1
) can be written as

\displaystyle GMD_{n}=\dfrac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\mathbb{E}(M_{ij}-m_{ij}).

(9)

In order to find a closed-form expression for the GMD, from (
9
), it is essential to know the PDFs of
M_{ij}
and
m_{ij}
. Indeed, the law of total probability gives

\displaystyle F_{M_{ij}}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(X_{i}\leqslant x|X_{i}\geqslant X_{j})R_{j,i}+\mathbb{P}(X_{j}\leqslant x|X_{j}\geqslant X_{i})R_{i,j},

where
R_{i,j}
is as given in (
6
).
Then, by using (
8
), the PDF of
M_{ij}
is

\displaystyle f_{M_{ij}}(x)

\displaystyle=f_{X_{i}|(X_{i}\geqslant X_{j})}(x)R_{j,i}+f_{X_{j}|(X_{j}\geqslant X_{i})}(x)R_{i,j}

\displaystyle=f_{X_{i}}(x)\pi_{j,i}(x)+f_{X_{j}}(x)\pi_{i,j}(x),

(10)

where
\pi_{j,i}
is as defined in (
6
).

Now, upon using the well-known identities that
\min\{x,y\}=-\max\{-x,-y\}
,
f_{-X_{i}}(-x)=f_{X_{i}}(x)
and
F_{-X_{j}|(-X_{i}=-x)}(-x)=1-\pi_{j,i}(x)
,
we can write the PDF of
m_{ij}=\min\{X_{i},X_{j}\}
as

\displaystyle f_{m_{ij}}(x)

\displaystyle=f_{\max\{-X_{i},-X_{j}\}}(-x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pdf-2}}}{{=}}f_{-X_{i}}(-x)F_{-X_{j}|(-X_{i}=-x)}(-x)+f_{-X_{j}}(-x)F_{-X_{i}|(-X_{j}=-x)}(-x)

\displaystyle=f_{X_{i}}(x)[1-\pi_{j,i}(x)]+f_{X_{j}}(x)[1-\pi_{i,j}(x)]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pdf-2}}}{{=}}f_{X_{i}}(x)+f_{X_{j}}(x)-f_{M_{ij}}(x).

(11)

Therefore, by (
3
) and (
3
), the GMD in (
9
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle GMD_{n}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\,\big[f_{M_{ij}}(x)-f_{m_{ij}}(x)\big]{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\,\big[2f_{X_{i}}(x)\pi_{j,i}(x)+2f_{X_{j}}(x)\pi_{i,j}(x)-f_{X_{i}}(x)-f_{X_{j}}(x)\big]{\rm d}x

(12)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\,\big[2R_{j,i}h_{j,i}(x)+2R_{i,j}h_{i,j}(x)-f_{X_{i}}(x)-f_{X_{j}}(x)\big]{\rm d}x,

where
R_{i,j}
and
h_{i,j}
are as given in (
6
). This completes the proof of theorem.
∎

A similar derivation of the PDF of the maximum of
n
random variables has also been given by
Arellano-Valle and Genton, 2008
.

In order to state the next result, we adopt the notation

g_{i,j}(x)=2f_{X_{j}}(x)\pi_{i,j}(x)
,
where

\pi_{i,j}(x)=F_{X_{i}|(X_{j}=x)}(x)
,
\pi_{i,j}
is a skewing function, i.e., it satisfies
0\leqslant\pi_{i,j}(x)\leqslant 1
and
\pi_{i,j}(-x)=1-\pi_{i,j}(x)
, and
f_{X_{j}}
is symmetric around
0
.
Under these conditions, it is clear that
g_{i,j}

is a skew-symmetric PDF, and let

G_{i,j}
be the corresponding CDF.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\bm{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{\top}
be an absolutely continuous random vector.
If
\pi_{i,j}
is a skewing function and
f_{X_{j}}
is symmetric around
0
, then
the GMD is given by

\displaystyle GMD_{n}=\dfrac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(\mu_{G_{j,i}}+\mu_{G_{i,j}}-\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}),

with
\mu_{i}=\mathbb{E}(X_{i})
and
\mu_{G_{i,j}}
being the expected value with respect to the distribution
G_{i,j}
.

Proof.

The proof follows directly from the identity in (
12
).
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
\bm{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{\top}
be an absolutely continuous exchangeable random vector. If
\pi_{1,2}
is a skewing function and
f_{X_{1}}
is symmetric around
0
, then
the GMD is given by

\displaystyle GMD_{n}=\dfrac{2}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(\mu_{G^{*}_{j,i}}-\mu_{1}),

where
\mu_{1}=\mathbb{E}(X_{1})
and
\mu_{G^{*}_{j,i}}
denotes the expected value with respect to the skew-symmetric CDF
G^{*}_{j,i}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}2f_{X_{i}}(t)F_{X_{j}}(t){\rm d}t
.

Proof.

Because
\bm{X}
is exchangeable, we have
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{\top}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{D}}}{{=}}(X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{n}})^{\top}
, for each permutation
(i_{1}\ldots,i_{n})
of
(1,\ldots,n)
. Here,
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{D}}}{{=}}
means that
the two sides of the equality have the same distribution. Consequently,

\mu_{G_{j,i}}=\mu_{G_{i,j}}
and

\mu_{i}=\mu_{j}=\mu_{1}
, for
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
. Hence, by applying Corollary
2
, the required result follows.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
2
, the following result readily follows.

Proposition 3
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be independent random variables with identical distributions
F_{X_{1}}
. Then, the classical GMD in (
2
) is simply

\displaystyle GMD=\mu_{G_{1}}-\mu_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}(2u-1)F_{X_{1}}^{-1}(u){\rm d}u,

(13)

where
\mu_{1}=\mathbb{E}(X_{1})
and
\mu_{G_{1}}
is the expected value with respect to the skew-symmetric CDF
G_{1}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}2f_{X_{1}}(t)F_{X_{1}}(t){\rm d}t
.

Furthermore, the Gini index, defined by
G=GMD/(2\mu_{1})
, is simply

G=\left[({\mu_{G_{1}}/\mu_{1}})-1\right]/2
.

Observe that the formula in (
13
) has appeared recently in the work of
Yin et al., 2022
.

4
Some examples

In this section, we consider the class of elliptically contoured (EC) distributions for the
n\times 1
random vector
\bm{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{\top}
. First, we recall that a random vector
\bm{X}

has an EC distribution, also called an elliptically symmetric distribution, if
\bm{X}
has joint PDF
(
Fang et al., 1990
, see)
as

\displaystyle f(\bm{x})=k_{n}|\bm{\Sigma}|^{-1/2}g^{(n)}[(\bm{x}-\bm{\mu})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}(\bm{x}-\bm{\mu})],\quad\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We use the notation
\bm{X}\sim EC_{n}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\Sigma},g^{(n)})
, where
\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a location parameter,
\bm{\Sigma}
is a scale matrix (positive-definite real
n\times n
matrix),
g^{(n)}
is the PDF generator and
k_{n}
is a normalizing constant.

As EC distributions are invariant under marginalization
and conditioning
(
Fang et al., 1990
, Theorem 2.16)
, and our results only require marginal and conditional laws involving the variables
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
, for
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
, our analysis is restricted to the marginalization
and conditioning corresponding to the bivariate case. In particular, we now discuss in detail the multivariate normal and Student-
t
cases.

4.1
Multivariate normal distribution

Let
\bm{X}\sim EC_{n}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\Sigma},g^{(n)})
, where
g^{(n)}(x)=\exp(-x/2)
is the PDF generator of
the multivariate normal distribution.
It is well-known that the conditional distribution of

X_{i}
, given
X_{j}=x
, is

N(\mu_{i}+\rho_{i,j}(x-\mu_{j})\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j},\sigma_{i}^{2}(1-\rho_{i,j}^{2}))

and that its unconditional distribution is
X_{j}\sim N(\mu_{j},\sigma_{j}^{2})
.
Let
\phi
and
\Phi
be the PDF and CDF of the standard normal distribution, respectively.
By using the standardization of
X_{i}|(X_{j}=x)
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{i,j}(x)=F_{X_{i}|(X_{j}=x)}(x)=\Phi\left({1\over\sqrt{1-\rho_{i,j}^{2}}}\,\left[{{x-\mu_{i}\over\sigma_{i}}-\rho_{i,j}\left({x-\mu_{j}\over\sigma_{j}}\right)}\right]\right).

Hence, by (
6
),

\displaystyle h_{i,j}(x)={1\over R_{i,j}}\,{1\over\sigma_{j}}\,\phi\left({x-\mu_{j}\over\sigma_{j}}\right)\Phi\left({1\over\sqrt{1-\rho_{i,j}^{2}}}\,\left[{{x-\mu_{i}\over\sigma_{i}}-\rho_{i,j}\left({x-\mu_{j}\over\sigma_{j}}\right)}\right]\right).

(14)

By using (
14
) and making the change of variable
z=(x-\mu_{j})/\sigma_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{H_{i,j}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x{\rm d}H_{i,j}(x)={1\over R_{i,j}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\sigma_{j}z+\mu_{j})\,\phi(z)\Phi\left({1\over\sqrt{1-\rho_{i,j}^{2}}}\,\left[{{\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}\over\sigma_{i}}+\left({\sigma_{j}\over\sigma_{i}}-\rho_{i,j}\right)z}\right]\right){\rm d}z.

Using the well-known
formulas
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\phi(x)\Phi(a+bx){\rm d}x=(b/\sqrt{1+b^{2}})\phi(a/\sqrt{1+b^{2}})
and
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(x)\Phi(a+bx){\rm d}x=\Phi(a/\sqrt{1+b^{2}})
,
the integral on the RHS of the above identity is

\displaystyle={1\over R_{i,j}}\left[{\dfrac{\sigma_{j}}{c_{i,j}}\left(\dfrac{\sigma_{j}}{\sigma_{i}}-\rho_{i,j}\right)}\,\phi\left(\dfrac{1}{c_{i,j}}\left(\dfrac{\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right)+\mu_{j}\Phi\left(\dfrac{1}{c_{i,j}}\left(\dfrac{\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right)\right],

where

c_{i,j}=\sqrt{1-\rho_{i,j}^{2}+[({\sigma_{j}}/{\sigma_{i}})-\rho_{i,j}]^{2}}.

Thus, by applying Theorem
2
, we obtain the following closed-form expression for the GMD:

\displaystyle GMD_{n}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\left[{\dfrac{\sigma_{j}}{c_{i,j}}\left(\dfrac{\sigma_{j}}{\sigma_{i}}-\rho_{i,j}\right)}\,\phi\left(\dfrac{1}{c_{i,j}}\left(\dfrac{\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right)+\mu_{j}\Phi\left(\dfrac{1}{c_{i,j}}\left(\dfrac{\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right)-{\mu_{j}\over 2}\right]

\displaystyle+\dfrac{2}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\left[{\dfrac{\sigma_{i}}{c_{j,i}}\left(\dfrac{\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{j}}-\rho_{j,i}\right)}\,\phi\left(\dfrac{1}{c_{j,i}}\left(\dfrac{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}{\sigma_{j}}\right)\right)+\mu_{i}\Phi\left(\dfrac{1}{c_{j,i}}\left(\dfrac{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}{\sigma_{j}}\right)\right)-{\mu_{i}\over 2}\right].

(15)

Proposition 4
.

Let
\bm{X}\sim EC_{n}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\Sigma},g^{(n)})
be an exchangeable random vector, where
g^{(n)}(x)=\exp(-x/2)
.
The GMD is then simply

\displaystyle GMD_{n}={2\over\sqrt{\pi}}\,{\sigma_{1}}\,\dfrac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}{\sqrt{1-\rho_{i,j}}},

where
\mu_{1}=\mathbb{E}(X_{1})
and
\sigma_{1}^{2}={{\rm Var}(X_{1})}
.

Proof.

As
\bm{X}
is an exchangeable vector, we have
\mu_{i}=\mu_{j}=\mu_{1}
,
\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{1}
,
\rho_{i,j}=\rho_{j,i}
,
R_{i,j}=R_{j,i}
and

c_{i,j}=c_{j,i}=\sqrt{2(1-\rho_{i,j})}
. Then, the required result follows directly from (
4.1
).
∎

The expression in Proposition
4
has appeared in the work of
Schmid and Semeniuk, 2021
.

4.2
Multivariate Student-
t
distribution

Let
\bm{X}\sim EC_{n}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\Sigma},g^{(n)})
, where
g^{(n)}(x)=(1+x/\nu)^{-(\nu+n)/2}
is the PDF generator of
the multivariate Student-
t
distribution with
\nu
degrees of freedom.
It is well-known that
(
Saulo et al., 2023
, see Remark 1 of )

\displaystyle X_{i}|(X_{j}=x)\sim t_{\nu+1}\Biggl(\mu_{i}+\rho_{i,j}\sigma_{i}\Big({x-\mu_{j}\over\sigma_{j}}\Big),{\nu+\left({x-\mu_{j}\over\sigma_{j}}\right)^{2}\over\nu+1}\,\sigma_{i}^{2}(1-\rho_{i,j}^{2})\Biggr),

and that its unconditional distribution is
X_{j}\sim t_{\nu}(\mu_{j},\sigma_{j}^{2})
. By
using the standardization of
X_{i}|(X_{j}=x)
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{i,j}(x)=F_{X_{i}|(X_{j}=x)}(x)

\displaystyle=F_{\nu+1}\left(\sqrt{(\nu+1)/(1-\rho_{i,j}^{2})\over\nu+\left({x-\mu_{j}\over\sigma_{j}}\right)^{2}}\,\left[{{x-\mu_{i}\over\sigma_{i}}-\rho_{i,j}\left({x-\mu_{j}\over\sigma_{j}}\right)}\right]\right).

Hence, by (
6
), we find

\displaystyle h_{i,j}(x)={1\over R_{i,j}}\,{1\over\sigma_{j}}\,f_{\nu}\left({x-\mu_{j}\over\sigma_{j}}\right)F_{\nu+1}\left(\sqrt{(\nu+1)/(1-\rho_{i,j}^{2})\over\nu+\left({x-\mu_{j}\over\sigma_{j}}\right)^{2}}\,\left[{{x-\mu_{i}\over\sigma_{i}}-\rho_{i,j}\left({x-\mu_{j}\over\sigma_{j}}\right)}\right]\right).

(16)

Here,
f_{\nu}
and
F_{\nu}
, respectively, denote the PDF and CDF of a Student-
t
distribution with
\nu
degrees of freedom.

So, from the identity in (
16
), by making the change of variable
z=(x-\mu_{j})/\sigma_{j}
, it follows that

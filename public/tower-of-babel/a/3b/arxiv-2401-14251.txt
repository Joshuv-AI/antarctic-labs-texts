On quasi-local spin-angular momentum and the construction of axial vector fields
István Rácz

Abstract
A novel procedure is presented which allows the construction of all axial vector fields on Riemannian two-spheres. Using these axial vector fields and the centre-of-mass unit sphere reference systems, a constructive definition of quasi-local spin-angular momentum is introduced. Balance relations are also derived, with respect to arbitrary Lie-propagated unit sphere reference systems, to characterize the angular momentum transports in spacetimes without symmetries.

(ii)

Condition

As we proceed, note first that the future-pointing null normals
\mathcal{k}^{a}
and
\ell^{a}
are not unique on
\mathscr{S}
, since they can be rescaled by using an arbitrary smooth real function

\mathscr{S}
such that for
\mathcal{k}^{\prime}{}^{a}=e^{f}\,\mathcal{k}^{a}
and
\ell^{\prime}{}^{a}=e^{-f}\,\ell{}^{a}

\mathcal{k}^{\prime}{}^{a}\ell^{\prime}_{a}=-1

9

\omega^{\prime}_{a}=\omega_{a}-\widehat{D}_{a}f
. This leads to

\phi^{a}\omega^{\prime}_{a}=\phi^{a}[\omega_{a}-\widehat{D}_{a}f]=\phi^{a}\omega_{a}-\widehat{D}_{a}(f\phi^{a})+f(\widehat{D}_{a}\phi^{a})\,,

(1.3)

which, together with the vanishing of

In light of the construction recalled above, it is also worth considering the following alternative formulation. Suppose
\mathscr{S}
is embedded in a spacelike hypersurface
\Sigma
in an ambient spacetime
(M,g_{ab})
. Let
n^{a}
and
\widehat{n}^{a}
be unit vector fields on
\mathscr{S}
such that they are both orthogonal to
\mathscr{S}
, and also orthogonal and parallel to
\Sigma
, respectively. Assume also that
n^{a}
is future-directed. The combinations
\mathcal{k}^{a}=(n^{a}-\widehat{n}^{a})/\sqrt{2}
and
\ell^{a}=(n^{a}+\widehat{n}^{a})/\sqrt{2}
are then properly scaled future-directed null normals. We also have

\omega_{a}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,(n_{b}-\widehat{n}_{b})\nabla_{a}(n^{b}+\widehat{n}^{b})=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,(n_{b}\nabla_{a}\widehat{n}^{b}-\widehat{n}_{b}\nabla_{a}n^{b})=\widehat{n}^{b}\nabla_{a}n_{b}\,,

(1.4)

which implies

\widehat{\omega}_{a}=\widehat{\gamma}_{a}{}^{e}\widehat{n}^{b}\nabla_{e}n_{b}=\widehat{\gamma}_{a}{}^{e}K_{eb}\widehat{n}^{b}={\rm\bf k}_{a}\,,

(1.5)

where
K_{ab}
and
{\rm\bf k}_{a}
denote the extrinsic curvature of
\Sigma
and its vector projection, respectively. Note that since
\phi^{a}
is tangent to
\mathscr{S}
while
\widehat{n}^{a}
is normal to it, we also have that
\phi^{a}{\rm\bf k}_{a}=\phi^{a}(K_{ab}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,h_{ab}K)\,\widehat{n}^{b}

J[\phi]=-(8\,\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathscr{S}}\phi^{a}(K_{ab}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,h_{ab}K)\,\widehat{n}^{b}\,\widehat{{\bm{\epsilon}}}=-(8\,\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathscr{S}}\phi^{a}{\rm\bf k}_{a}\,\widehat{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\,.

(1.6)

The integral expression in the middle also verifies the equivalence of the quasi-local angular momentum expression proposed by Brown and York (given in
[
7
]
under the assumption that
\phi^{a}
is an axial Killing vector field) and that given by (

\phi^{a}
. This observation also suggests that the quasi-local angular momentum given either by the integral expression (
1.2

tends to an asymptotic Killing vector field and also suitable admissible spherical coordinates can be introduced, since the first integral expression in (
1.6

A few comments are in order. Obviously, the unit vector field
n^{a}
is well defined in a neighborhood of
\mathscr{S}
in
\Sigma
. Note, however, that the extendibility of
\widehat{n}^{a}
within
\Sigma

\mathscr{S}

n^{a}
and
\widehat{n}^{a}
corresponds to the rescaling ambiguity in the future-pointing null normals
\mathcal{k}^{a}
and
\ell^{a}
discussed above. Following an analogous argument, the vanishing of the divergence
\widehat{D}_{a}\phi^{a}
guarantees the invariance of the resulting quasi-local angular momentum.

\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}^{a}=\nu_{i}\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i}{}^{a}
. The absolute value of
J[{\phi}]
, given by the integral expression (
2.7
), can be interpreted as the magnitude, while the unit vector
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
can be interpreted as the direction of
\vec{J}\,[{\phi}]
.

3
The quasi-local spin-angular momentum

In this section we will explore the freedom we have in choosing spherical coordinates and unit sphere metrics on
\mathscr{S}
. By restricting attention to the centre-of-mass unit sphere reference systems, we fix this freedom, which allows the introduction of the notion of intrinsic quasi-local spin-angular momentum.

The Poincaré uniformization theorem guarantees that for any Riemannian metric
{\widehat{\gamma}}_{ab}
on
\mathscr{S}
there always exists a diffeomorphism
\Psi:\mathscr{S}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
, a positive real function
\Omega
and spherical coordinates
(\vartheta,\varphi)
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
so that the unit sphere metric
{\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}_{ab}
takes the form

ds^{2}=d\vartheta^{2}+\sin\vartheta^{2}\,d\varphi^{2}\,,

(3.12)

and the Riemannian metrics
{\widehat{\gamma}}_{ab}
and
{\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}_{ab}
are conformally related such that the relation

\Psi^{*}{\widehat{\gamma}}_{ab}=\Omega^{2}\,{\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}_{ab}\,.

(3.13)

holds. Note that we then also have that
\Omega^{2}=\sqrt{(\Psi{}^{*}{\widehat{\gamma}})/{\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}}
.
It is well-known that neither the conformal factor nor the underlying spherical coordinates
(\vartheta,\varphi)
are unique
[
18
]
. It is also known that the stereographic counterparts,
z=\cot(\vartheta/2)\cdot e^{\mathbbm{i}\,\varphi}
and
z^{\prime}=\cot(\vartheta^{\prime}/2)\cdot e^{\mathbbm{i}\,\varphi^{\prime}}
, of the conformally related spherical coordinates
(\vartheta,\varphi)
and
(\vartheta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, respectively, are related by the Möbius transformations

z^{\prime}=\frac{a\,z+b}{c\,z+d}\,,

(3.14)

where
a,b,c,
and
d
are arbitrary complex numbers subject to the condition
ad-bc=1

[
18
,
19
]
.
Correspondingly, the composition of two successive (orientation preserving) conformal transformations of the unit sphere onto itself can be represented by the composition of fractional linear transformations of the form (
3.14
). Thus the conformal transformations of the unit sphere,
({\mathbb{S}^{2}},\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{ab})
, onto itself can be seen to be isomorphic to the six-(real)-parameter restricted Lorentz group which has
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
as its double covering
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
.

These finite transformations on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
can also be seen to be generated by the three axial Killing vector fields
\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{1}{}^{a},\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{2}{}^{a},\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{3}{}^{a}
, given by (
2.8
)-(
2.10
),
together with the three proper conformal Killing vector fields

\displaystyle\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{1}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=-\cos\vartheta\cos\varphi\,(\partial_{\vartheta})^{a}+\frac{\sin\varphi}{\sin\vartheta}\,(\partial_{\varphi})^{a}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{2}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=-\cos\vartheta\sin\varphi\,(\partial_{\vartheta})^{a}-\frac{\cos\varphi}{\sin\vartheta}\,(\partial_{\varphi})^{a}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{3}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=\sin\vartheta\,(\partial_{\vartheta})^{a}\,.

(3.17)

These six vector fields are also known to satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle[\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i},\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{j}]=-\epsilon_{ijk}\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}

(3.18)

\displaystyle[\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i},\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{j}]=\epsilon_{ijk}\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}

(3.19)

\displaystyle[\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i},\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{j}]=[\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i},\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{j}]=-\epsilon_{ijk}\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}\,,

(3.20)

so they form the Lie algebra
so(1,3)
, where
\epsilon_{ijk}
is the Levi-Civita antisymmetric symbol
[
19
]
(see also
[
12
]
).

It is known that any linear combination of the form
\nu_{i}\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i}{}^{a}
, where the coefficients
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})
form a unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, is an axial vector field (in the sense defined above), while no linear combination of the proper conformal Killing vector fields is axial. Moreover, while the orbit of
\nu_{i}\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i}{}^{a}
with respect to the adjoint action reads as

\displaystyle\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}\,^{\prime}_{i}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}\,(\nu_{k}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a})+\cos\theta\Big[\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i}{}^{a}-\nu_{i}\,(\nu_{k}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a})\,\Big]-\sin\theta\,\big(\epsilon_{ijk}\nu_{j}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a}\big)\,,

(3.21)

\displaystyle\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}\,^{\prime}_{i}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}\,(\nu_{k}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a})+\cos\theta\Big[\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i}{}^{a}-\nu_{i}\,(\nu_{k}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a})\,\Big]-\sin\theta\,\big(\epsilon_{ijk}\nu_{j}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a}\big)\,,

(3.22)

the integrated action of a proper conformal transformation with respect to
\nu_{i}\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i}{}^{a}
is given by

\displaystyle\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}\,^{\prime}_{i}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}\,(\nu_{k}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a})+\cosh\theta\Big[\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i}{}^{a}-\nu_{i}\,(\nu_{k}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a})\,\Big]-\sinh\theta\,\big(\epsilon_{ijk}\nu_{j}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a}\big)\,,

(3.23)

\displaystyle\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}\,^{\prime}_{i}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}\,(\nu_{k}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a})+\cosh\theta\Big[\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i}{}^{a}-\nu_{i}\,(\nu_{k}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a})\,\Big]+\sinh\theta\,\big(\epsilon_{ijk}\nu_{j}\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{k}{}^{a}\big)\,,

(3.24)

where
\theta
is the group additive parameter.
These relations clearly show that while
SO(3)
transformations, see (
3.21
) and (
3.22
), do not mix the generators
\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i}{}^{a}
and
\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\xi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}_{i}{}^{a}
, by virtue of (
3.23
) and (
3.24
), the proper conformal transformations do. This simple observation will serve as an important guideline in the following.

As we proceed, first recall that our main goal here is to define the quasi-local spin-angular momentum. In doing so we have to take into account that the group
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
, which was found to be isomorphic to the group of conformal transformations of the unit sphere onto itself, is a non-compact Lie group. In fact, the non-compactness of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
is the main obstacle we have to face on the way to define the quasi-local spin-angular momentum. It is also known that if
\Phi
is an element of the group of conformal transformations of the unit sphere onto itself it admits the decomposition (see, e.g., Lemma 3.4 in
[
17
]
)

\Phi=R_{1}\cdot B_{N,\tau}\cdot R_{2}\,,

(3.25)

where
R_{1},R_{2}
are “rotations” that can be represented by the
SU(2)
elements

R_{i}=\left(\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}&\beta_{i}\\
-\overline{\beta}_{i}&\overline{\alpha}_{i}\\
\end{matrix}\right)\,,

(3.26)

with
i=1,2
, while
B_{N,\tau}
corresponds to the scaling (boost) transformation
[
18
]

\cot(\vartheta^{\prime}/2)=\tau\cdot\cot(\vartheta/2)\quad{\rm and}\quad\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi\,,

(3.27)

with
\tau>0
and such that
N
is at the “north pole”, which can also be represented by the special
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
matrix

B_{N,\tau}=\left(\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\tau}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\\
\end{matrix}\right)\,.

(3.28)

Note also that
SU(2)
is the universal cover of
SO(3)
rotations, and, most importantly, it is a compact subgroup of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
.
This observation raises the question whether there is a viable selection rule that leads to the desired subset of conformal transformations. The existence of such a selection rule has been demonstrated by several authors, while focusing on different, usually more complex research topics
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
. For the sake of completeness, we give below a short, self-contained, and simple synthesis of the relevant arguments.

Once some spherical coordinates
(\vartheta,\varphi)
are chosen on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
consider the three-vector
\vec{X}
defined by the integral

\vec{X}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\Omega^{2}\;\vec{x}\;\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}\,,

(3.29)

where
\vec{x}=(\cos\varphi\sin\vartheta,\sin\varphi\sin\vartheta,\cos\vartheta)
. Note that here
\vec{x}
can be viewed either as a vector pointing from the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to the points of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, or as the unit normal vector field on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Note also that since
\vec{x}
is a unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, by virtue of (
3.13
) and (
3.29
), using the relation
\Omega^{2}\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}=\sqrt{(\Psi^{*}\widehat{\gamma})/\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}=\Psi^{*}\widehat{{\bm{\epsilon}}}
as well,
\vec{X}
must also be an interior point of the ball of radius
\mathscr{A}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\Psi^{*}\widehat{{\bm{\epsilon}}}=\int_{{\mathscr{S}}}\widehat{{\bm{\epsilon}}}
, i.e.,
\|\vec{X}\|<\mathscr{A}
. If
\vec{X}
happens to be the zero vector in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
then the underlying
(\vartheta,\varphi)
coordinates will be referred to as a centre-of-mass unit sphere reference system (CMUSRS).

Correspondingly, the desired type of selection rule exists if there exists a conformal diffeomorphism
\Phi
of the unit sphere onto itself which transforms
\vec{X}
to the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Recall that a map
\Phi:{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
, with
(\vartheta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})=\Phi\big((\vartheta,\varphi)\big)
is a conformal diffeomorphism of the unit sphere onto itself if there exist a positive real function
\omega
such that both
\Phi^{*}\big(\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}{}_{ab}{(\vartheta,\varphi)}\big)=\omega^{2}{(\vartheta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})}\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}{}_{ab}{(\vartheta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})}
and
\omega^{2}=\det(d\Phi)
holds, where
\det(d\Phi)
is the Jacobian of the map
\Phi
. Accordingly, our task reduces to finding a conformal diffeomorphism
\Phi:{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}(\Phi^{-1})^{*}\big(\Omega^{2}\big)\;\vec{x}\;\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\big(\Omega^{2}\,\circ\Phi^{-1}\,\big)\det(d\Phi)\;\vec{x}\;\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\Omega^{2}\;(\vec{x}\,\circ\,\Phi)\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}=\vec{0}\,.

(3.30)

where the variable change formula was applied in the second step. Since conformal diffeomorphisms of the unit sphere onto itself are in one-to-one correspondence with the Möbius transformations, our task reduces to finding an element
g
in
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
such that

{\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\Omega^{2}\hskip 0.56917pt(z)\,\vec{x}\hskip 0.56917pt(gz)\;\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}=\vec{0}\,.

(3.31)

To see that such a
g\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
really exists, consider the map

\vec{\chi}:SL(2,\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\,,\quad{\rm with}\quad\vec{\chi}\hskip 0.56917pt(q)={\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\Omega^{2}\hskip 0.56917pt(z)\,\vec{x}\hskip 0.56917pt(gz)\;\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}

(3.32)

which is continuous by construction, maps the unit element
e\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
to
\vec{X}=\vec{\chi}(e)
, and, as follows from the above discussion, it maps
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
to the interior of the ball with radius
\mathscr{A}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, i.e.,
\|\vec{\chi}\|<\mathscr{A}
.

Now consider the foliation of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
by spheres defined as

\mathscr{B}_{\tau}=\{\,B_{p,\tau}\,|\,p\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\,\}\,,

(3.33)

where
\tau
takes its value from the interval
1\leq\tau\leq\infty
, while
B_{p,\tau}
denotes a scale transformation where
p
now plays the role of the “north pole” running through the individual points of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Correspondingly,
B_{p,\tau}
can also be given by the product

B_{p,\tau}=R_{p\to N}^{\dagger}\cdot B_{N,\tau}\cdot R_{p\to N}\,,

(3.34)

where
R_{p\to N}
is the rotation that takes
p
to the north pole
N
and
R_{p\to N}^{\dagger}
is its adjoint, which is also the inverse of
R_{p\to N}
.
It follows from (
3.34
) that the foliation degenerates and gives the unit element
e={\mathscr{B}}_{\tau=1}
of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
for
\tau=1
and hence
\vec{\chi}\,[\,{\mathscr{B}}_{\tau=1}\,]=\vec{X}
holds. More importantly, since the unit normal vector field
\vec{x}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is invariant under rotations, thus these transformations do not affect the value of the integral in (
3.32
), and since
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\vec{x}\,(B_{N,\tau}\,z)=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\vec{x}\circ B_{N,\tau}=(0,0,1)^{T}
it also follows that
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\vec{\chi}\,[\,{\mathscr{B}}_{\tau}\,]
gives the sphere with radius
\mathscr{A}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which is actually the boundary of the open ball
\vec{\chi}\,[\,SL(2,\mathbb{C})\,]<\mathscr{A}
. Therefore, by continuity of the map
\vec{\chi}:SL(2,\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we also have that for a sufficiently large
\bar{\tau}
value of
\tau
the sphere
\vec{\chi}\,[\,{\mathscr{B}}_{\bar{\tau}}\,]
is close to this boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Since, as noted above, the foliation
\{\,\mathscr{B}_{\tau}\,|\,1\leq\tau\leq\infty\,\}
of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
is contractible to the unit element
e
, there must exist
\widetilde{\tau}\in[1,\infty)
such that
\vec{\chi}\,[\,{\mathscr{B}}_{\widetilde{\tau}}\,]
contains the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Since
\{\mathscr{B}_{\tau}\}
provides a one-parameter foliation of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
, one may expect that the level set
{\mathscr{B}}_{\widetilde{\tau}}
is unique up to
SO(3)
rotations. This kind of uniqueness was verified by Lemma 3.5 in
[
17
]
, which can be reformulated in the notation used here as follows. Suppose that we already have a centre-of-mass unit sphere reference system, i.e.,
\vec{X}=\vec{0}
in (
3.29
). The level set
{\mathscr{B}}_{\widetilde{\tau}}
can be unique only if for a
B_{N,\tau}
scaling transformation

{\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\Omega^{2}\,\Big[\,\vec{x}\circ B_{N,\tau}-\vec{x}\,\Big]\;\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}=\vec{0}\,

(3.35)

holds, then
B_{N,\tau}
must be trivial, i.e.,
\tau=1
must also hold. To see that this is indeed the case, recall that
\vec{x}=(\cos\varphi\sin\vartheta,\sin\varphi\sin\vartheta,\cos\vartheta)
, which together with the substitutions
\vartheta^{\prime}=2\,{\rm arccot}\big(\tau\cdot\cot(\vartheta/2)\big)
and
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi
(the last two relations follow from (
3.27
)), can be used to derive

\displaystyle x_{1}\circ B_{N,\tau}-x_{1}

\displaystyle=(\tau-1)\,\frac{[\tau\,(1+\cos\vartheta)-(1-\cos\vartheta)]\,\sin\vartheta\cos\varphi}{\tau^{2}\,(1+\cos\vartheta)+(1-\cos\vartheta)}

(3.36)

\displaystyle x_{2}\circ B_{N,\tau}-x_{2}

\displaystyle=(\tau-1)\,\frac{[\tau\,(1+\cos\vartheta)-(1-\cos\vartheta)]\,\sin\vartheta\sin\varphi}{\tau^{2}\,(1+\cos\vartheta)+(1-\cos\vartheta)}

(3.37)

\displaystyle x_{3}\circ B_{N,\tau}-x_{3}

\displaystyle=(\tau^{2}-1)\,\frac{\sin^{2}\vartheta}{\tau^{2}\,(1+\cos\vartheta)+(1-\cos\vartheta)}\,.

(3.38)

Inspecting now the integrand relevant for the third component of (
3.35
), since, for any
\tau\in[1,\infty)
, all the terms in (
3.38
) are non-negative and it is regular, thus it is straightforward to see that the corresponding integral can only vanish if
\tau=1
. When this happens the integrands of the other two components also vanish automatically which completes the proof of our claim that the centre-of-mass unit sphere reference systems are unique up to
SO(3)
rotations.

By economizing on this uniqueness property of centre-of-mass unit sphere reference systems we can now define the intrinsic spin-angular momentum as follows. Taking into account (
3.21
) we know that
SO(3)
always maps the set of axial Killing vector fields of unit sphere reference systems onto itself. Hence we define the intrinsic quasi-local spin-angular momentum as the maximum of the quasi-local integral expressions given by (
2.7
) over the compact set of axial Killing vector fields over any of the centre-of-mass unit sphere reference systems

J=\max\Big\{J[\phi]\,\,\big|\,\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}\,\in\,CMUSRS\,\Big\}\,.

(3.39)

Note that since the centre-of-mass unit sphere reference systems are related by
SO(3)
transformations, as is the set of axial Killing vector fields, the maximum is well defined.

It is important to have good guesses about the approximate value of
J
, at least in certain special cases. It is worth noting that the closer the metric
\widehat{\gamma}_{ab}
is to the round sphere metric on
{\mathscr{S}}
, the closer the value of the average
\underline{\sqrt{{\widehat{\gamma}}/{\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}}}_{\,[{\tiny{\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.25832pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-0.64583pt}}}]}
, relevant for an axial Killing vector field
\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}{}^{a}
, is to the value of the function
\sqrt{\widehat{\gamma}/\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}

3
3

3

By slightly abusing our notation, as we did in section
2
, in the rest of this paper instead of
\sqrt{{\widehat{\gamma}}/(\Psi^{-1}){}^{*}{\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}}
we will simply write
\sqrt{{\widehat{\gamma}}/{\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}}}
.
. In such a special case, the quasi-local spin angular momentum
J
, as defined by (
3.39
), can be well approximated as

J\sim\max\bigg\{-(8\,\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathscr{S}}\big(\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}{}^{a}{\rm\bf k}_{a}\big)\,\widehat{{\bm{\epsilon}}}\,\,\,\Big|\,\,\,\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\phi}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}\,\in\,CMUSRS\,\bigg\}\,,

5
Final remarks

It has been known for long that in the absence of well-defined axial vector fields on generic Riemannian two-spheres no viable definition of quasi-local spin-angular momentum can be given. Our aims in this paper were two-folded. First, we introduced a method that enable us to construct all the possible axial vector fields on generic Riemannian two-spheres. Then by restricting attention to the centre-of-mass reference systems we also introduced a novel definition of the quasi-local spin-angular momentum that gets well-defined for any three-dimensional spatial domain bounded by a sufficiently smooth topological two-sphere. By referring to the canonical embedding of the underlying unit sphere
(\mathscr{S},\accentset{\smash{\raisebox{-0.36165pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{-1.29167pt}{}_{ab})
into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
the quasi-local spin-angular momentum associated with the domain bounded by
\mathscr{S}
can also be represented as a triple vector in the three-dimensional Euclidean space.

We have also derived balance relations for quasi-local angular momentum expressions, with respect to some suitably chosen axial vector fields, on spacelike hypersurfaces foliated by topological two-spheres. These balance relations will allow to characterize the angular momentum transport in dynamical processes. It seems plausible that these balance relations can be extended to include hypersurfaces of arbitrary causal character. We also expect that these balance relations will prove to be essential monitoring tools in numerical investigations of highly dynamical processes of coupled gravity-matter systems in generic spacetimes without symmetries.

Not surprisingly, a new construction raises many new issues. It remains to be seen whether the expectations regarding the global angular momentum charges (see references
[
25
,
26
]
), as discussed in the last paragraphs of section
3
, can be verified. In addition, there are several practical issues that need to be addressed. First, given a two-metric
{\widehat{\gamma}}_{ab}
on
\mathscr{S}
, one must determine a centre-of-mass unit sphere reference system. Once this is done, the maximum of the integral expressions used in (
3.39
) must be determined. These and many other questions are left for future investigations. Nevertheless, we believe that progress in research areas such as dynamical black hole thermodynamics and various aspects of Penrose inequalities will be greatly stimulated by the novel constructions and results presented in this paper.

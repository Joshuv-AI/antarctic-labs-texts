1
Introduction

A continuum is a nonempty compact connected metric space. Given a continuum
X
,
2^{X}
denotes the hyperspace of all nonempty closed subsets of
X
topologized with the Hausdorff metric. The set
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)
denotes the family of all one-point subsets of
X
.

Let
X
be a continuum and let
B\in 2^{X}
. We say that
B
does not block
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)
provided that for any
x\in X\setminus B
, the union of all subcontinua of
X
containing
x
and contained in
X\setminus B
is a dense subset of
X
. The collection of all
B
in
2^{X}
such that
B
does not block
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)
is denoted by
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
.

The notion of nonblocker is introduced by A. Illanes and P. Krupski in
[
5
]
. Since
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))\subseteq 2^{X}
,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
has the subspace topology and, it is natural to ask, over what conditions is the hyperspace
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
a continuum?

In
[
4
, Theorem 4.4]
, it is proved that if
X
is locally connected, then,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum if and only if
X
is a simple closed curve. Also, in
[
4
]
, the authors showed that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum when
X
is the circle of pseudoarcs. In
[
2
]
, the simple closed curve is characterized as the unique continuum
X
such that the hyperspace
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)
. In
[
3
]
, the authors show examples of continua
X
such that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum. Recently, A. Illanes and B. Vejnar showed in
[
7
]
that for any completely metrizable and separable space
X
, there exists a continuum
Y
such that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(Y))
is homeomorphic to
X
.

The following question was taken from
[
4
, Question 4.5]
.

Question 1.1
.

For which nonlocally connected continua
X
is
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
a continuum?

We note that in all the examples presented in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
or
[
7
]
, if
X
is not a simple closed curve and
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum, then
X
contain an infinite number of indecomposable continua. Hence, we rise the following question:

Question 1.2
.

Let
X
be an hereditarily decomposable continuum such that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum. Then, does it follow that
X
is the simple closed curve?

The main result in this paper is Theorem
5.3
, where we characterize the simple closed curve as the unique hereditarily decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley
X
, such that the hyperspace of nonblockers
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum.

The reader can see
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
, or
[
14
]
for additional information about nonblockers in hyperspaces.

2
Preliminaries

The symbols
\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
denote the set of positive integers, reals numbers and complex numbers, respectively. In this paper, every map will be a continuous function. Given a metric space
X
and
A\subseteq X
,
\mathrm{Int}(A)
,
\mathrm{Cl}(A)
and
\mathrm{Bd}(A)
denote the interior, the closure and the boundary of
A
, respectively. For
x\in X
and
r>0
, we represent
B(x,r)=\{y\in X:d(x,y)<r\}
where
d
is a metric on
X
. A
continuum
is a nonempty compact connected metric space. A
subcontinuum
is a continuum contained in some metric space. We say that a continuum
X
is
irreducible between two of its points
if no proper subcontinuum of
X
contains both points. A continuum is
irreducible
if it is irreducible between two of its points. A subcontinuum
Z
of a continuum
X
is said to be
terminal
if each subcontinuum
Y
of
X
that intersects
Z
satisfies either
Z\subseteq Y
or
Y\subseteq Z
.
An
arc
is any continuum homeomorphic to
[0,1]
, and a
simple closed curve
is any continuum homeomorphic to
S^{1}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
.

A continuum is
decomposable
provided that it is the union of two of its proper subcontinua. A continuum that is not decomposable is said to be
indecomposable
. A continuum is
hereditarily decomposable
if every subcontinuum is decomposable.

Let
X
be a continuum and let
\mathcal{D}
be a decomposition of
X
. We say that
\mathcal{D}
is an
upper semicontinuous decomposition
provided that whenever
D\in\mathcal{D}
,
U
open subset of
X
, and
D\subseteq U
, there exists an open subset
V
of
X
with
D\subseteq V
such that if
A\in\mathcal{D}
and
A\cap V\neq\emptyset
, then
A\subseteq U
. We say that
\mathcal{D}
is
lower semicontinuous
provided that for each
D\in\mathcal{D}
any two points
x
and
y
of
D
and each open subset
U
of
X
such that
x\in U
, there exists an open subset
V
of
X
such that
y\in V
and such that if
A\in\mathcal{D}
and
A\cap V\neq\emptyset
, then
A\cap U\neq\emptyset
. Finally, we say that
\mathcal{D}
is
continuous
if
\mathcal{D}
is both upper and lower semicontinuous. If
\mathcal{D}
is a decomposition of
X
, we denote
\rho\colon X\to\mathcal{D}
by the natural function such that
\rho(x)=D_{x}
where
D_{x}
is unique
D_{x}\in\mathcal{D}
with
x\in D_{x}
. The following result is not difficult to prove.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a continuum, let
\mathcal{D}
be a decomposition of
X
such that
D
is closed for each
D\in\mathcal{D}
. Then,
\mathcal{D}
is upper semicontinuous (lower semicontinuous) if and only if for each sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
X
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x
for some
x\in X
, we have that
\limsup\rho(x_{n})\subseteq\rho(x)
(
\rho(x)\subseteq\liminf\rho(x_{n})
, respectively).

Given a map between continua
f\colon X\to Y
, we say that
f
is
monotone
provided that
f^{-1}(D)
is a continuum for each subcontinuum
D
of
Y
. We say that
f
is
open
provided that
f(U)
is open for every open subset
U
of
X
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a continuum, and let
p,q\in X
. We say that
X
is
aposyndetic at
p
with respect to
q
provided that there exists a subcontinuum
W
of
X
such that
p\in\mathrm{Int}(W)\subseteq W\subseteq X\setminus\{q\}
. Now,
X
is
aposyndetic at
p
if
X
is aposyndetic at
p
with respect to each point of
X\setminus\{p\}
. We say that
X
is
aposyndetic
provided that
X
is aposyndetic at each of its points.

A continuum
X
is
locally connected
provided that for every
p\in X
and each open
V
such that
p\in V
, there exists an open connected set
U
such that
p\in U\subseteq V
. We say that
X
is
semi-locally connected
provided that for every
p\in X
and each open
V
such that
p\in V
, there exists an open
U
such that
p\in U\subseteq V
and
X\setminus U
has a finite number of components.

Let
X
be a continuum. Let
2^{X}=\{A\subseteq X:A\neq\emptyset\text{ and }\mathrm{Cl}(A)=A\}
. It is known that the collection of sets
\langle U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{l}\rangle
form a base on
2^{X}
(
Vietoris topology
, see
[
6
, p.3]
), where
U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{l}
are open sets in
X
and

\langle U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{l}\rangle=\{A\in 2^{X}:A\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{l}U_{i}\text{ and }A\cap U_{i}\neq\emptyset\text{ for each }i\}.

We denote
\mathcal{C}(X)=\{A\in 2^{X}:A\text{ is connected}\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)=\{\{x\}:x\in X\}
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{C}(X)\subseteq 2^{X}
, the sets
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)
and
\mathcal{C}(X)
are considered as subspaces of
2^{X}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be defined for each
A,B\in 2^{X}
by

\mathcal{H}(A,B)=\inf\{r>0:A\subseteq N(B;r)\text{ and }B\subseteq N(A;r)\},

where
N(D,s)=\{x\in X:d(x,y)<s\text{ for some }y\in D\}
, for any
D\in 2^{X}
and
s>0
.
\mathcal{H}
is a metric on
2^{X}
and it is known as the
Hausdorff metric

[
12
, Theorem 0.2]
. It is well known that the topology induced by the Hausdorff metric and the Vietoris topology are the same
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Let
A,B\in 2^{X}
such that
A\cap B=\emptyset
. We use the notation

\kappa_{X\setminus A}(B)=\bigcup\{L\in\mathcal{C}(X):B\cap L\neq\emptyset\text{ and }L\subseteq X\setminus A\}.

If
B=\{b\}
for some
b\in X
, we write
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(b)
instead of
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(\{b\})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a continuum and let
A,B\in 2^{X}
such that
A\cap B=\emptyset
. If
B
is connected, then there exists a sequence of subcontinua
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
such that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(B)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
U_{n}=N(A,1/n)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
A
and
B
are compact and
A\cap B=\emptyset
, we may suppose that
U_{n}\cap B=\emptyset
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
K_{n}
be the component of
X\setminus U_{n}
such that
B\subseteq K_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is not difficult to see that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(B)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}
.
∎

A subset
W
of a continuum
X
is said to be
connected by continua
provided for any two points
x,y\in W
there exists a subcontinuum
L
of
W
such that
\{x,y\}\subseteq L\subseteq W
. Observe that for
A\in 2^{X}
,
X\setminus A
is connected by continua if
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)=X\setminus A
for every
x\in X\setminus A
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a continuum and let
A,B\in 2^{X}
such that
A\cap B=\emptyset
. We say that
A
does not block
B
provided that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(B)
is a dense subset of
X
. In another way we say that
A
blocks
B
; i.e.,
A
blocks
B
provided that
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(B))\neq X
.

In this paper, we will focus on the following set:

\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))=\{A\in 2^{X}:A\text{ does not block }\{x\}\text{ for each }x\in X\setminus A\}.

The sets
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X),\mathcal{C}(X),\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
and
2^{X}
are called
hyperspaces
of
X
. In particular,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is called
the hyperspace of nonblockers of
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)
.
In
[
4
, Theorem 4.4]
, it is proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
X
be a locally connected continuum. Then,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum if and only if
X
is a simple closed curve.

Let
X
be a continuum. We say that
X
has the
property of Kelley
provided that for any sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x
for some
x\in X
, and for any
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)
such that
x\in A
, there exists a sequence on subcontinua
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
such that
x_{n}\in A_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}=A
.

The proof of the next result can be found in
[
9
, Corollary 4.2]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X
be a continuum, let
A\in 2^{X}
and let
K
be a subcontinuum of
X\setminus A
such that
\mathrm{Int}(K)\neq\emptyset
. If
X
has the property of Kelley, then there exists a subcontinuum
L
of
X
such that
K\subseteq\mathrm{Int}(L)\subseteq L\subseteq X\setminus A
.

3
On minimal nonblockers

In
[
14
]
, it is introduced the function
\pi\colon X\to\mathcal{C}(X)
defined by

\pi(x)=\begin{cases}\bigcap\{A\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)):x\in A\},&\hbox{ if }x\in\bigcup\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X));\\
X,&\hbox{ if }x\notin\bigcup\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)).\end{cases}

(3.1)

By Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 3.3 of
[
14
]
,
\pi
is well defined and
\pi(x)\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
for each
x\in X
where
x\in\bigcup\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. We prove in Theorem
3.6
that
\pi
is a map whenever
X
is a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley such that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum, giving a partial answer to Question 3.6 of
[
14
]
. Furthermore, we show in Theorem
3.9
that
\{\pi(x):x\in X\}
is a continuous decomposition of
X
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley. If
L
is a proper subcontinuum of
X
, then there exists
A\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
such that
A\subseteq X\setminus L
.

Proof.

Let
L
be a proper subcontinuum of
X
and let
M
and
N
be proper subcontinua of
X
such that
X=M\cup N
. We consider two cases:

1.

\mathrm{Int}(L)=\emptyset
. Note that either
M\cap L\neq\emptyset
or
N\cap L\neq\emptyset
. Suppose that
M\cap L\neq\emptyset
. Since
X\setminus M
is a nonempty open subset of
X
and
X\setminus M\nsubseteq L
, we have that
M\cup L\neq X
. Let
L_{1}=M\cup L
. Since
\mathrm{Int}(M)\neq\emptyset
,
\mathrm{Int}(L_{1})\neq\emptyset
. By Theorem
2.6
, there exists a proper subcontinuum
R
of
X
such that
L_{1}\subseteq\mathrm{Int}(R)
. By
[
6
, Theorem 14.6]
, there exists an order arc in
\mathcal{C}(X)
from
R
to
X
. Let
\alpha
be an order arc from
R
to
X
and let
f\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal{C}(X)
be an embedding such that
f(0)=R,f(1)=X
and
f([0,1])=\alpha
. Since
f
is a map, there exists
t\in(0,1)
such that
\mathcal{H}(f(t),X)<\frac{1}{2}
. Let
L_{2}=f(t)
. Note that
L_{1}\subseteq\mathrm{Int}(L_{2})
. Hence inductively, we may construct an increasing sequence of continua
(L_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
L_{n}\subseteq\mathrm{Int}(L_{n+1})
,
L_{n}\neq X
and
\mathcal{H}(L_{n},X)<\frac{1}{n}
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
A=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(X\setminus\mathrm{Int}(L_{n}))
. Since
\mathrm{Int}(L_{n})\subseteq\mathrm{Int}(L_{n+1})
,
X\setminus\mathrm{Int}(L_{n+1})\subseteq X\setminus\mathrm{Int}(L_{n})
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore,
X\setminus\mathrm{Int}(L_{n})\neq\emptyset
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,
A
is a nonempty compact subset of
X
. Observe that
X\setminus A=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{Int}(L_{n})
and, since
\mathrm{Int}(L_{n})\subseteq L_{n+1}
,
X\setminus A=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L_{n}
. Also,
X\setminus A
is dense, because
\mathcal{H}(L_{n},X)<\frac{1}{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore,
A\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
and
A\subseteq X\setminus L
. Similarly, we construct
A
if
N\cap L\neq\emptyset
.

2.

\mathrm{Int}(L)\neq\emptyset
. We define
L_{1}=L
and continue the same argument as in case
1
to construct
A
and complete the proof.
∎

With the next theorem, we have that
\pi(x)\neq X
for each
x\in X
when
X
is a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley such that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley. If
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum, then
X=\bigcup\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
.

Proof.

By
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))\cap\mathcal{C}(X)\neq\emptyset
. Since
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum,
\bigcup\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a subcontinuum of
\mathcal{C}(X)
, by
[
6
, Exercise 15.9 (2)]
. Suppose that
\bigcup\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a proper subcontinuum of
\mathcal{C}(X)
. Thus, there exists
A\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
such that
A\subseteq X\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
, by Lemma
3.1
. A contradiction. Therefore,
X=\bigcup\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
.
∎

In this section we use the following notation:

B(x)=\{y\in X:x\text{ blocks }y\}=\{y\in X:\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus\{x\}}(y))\neq X\},\text{ for }x\in X.

Observe that
X\setminus\pi(x)\subseteq X\setminus B(x)
for each
x\in X
. Thus,
B(x)\subseteq\pi(x)
for each
x\in X
. The following remark is not difficult to prove.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
X
be a continuum and let
x\in X
. If
L\in\mathcal{C}(X)
is such that
L\cap B(x)\neq\emptyset
and
L\cap(X\setminus B(x))\neq\emptyset
, then
x\in L
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum such that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum. If
X
has the property of Kelley, then
\pi(x)=B(x)
for each
x\in X
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
. Note that there exists
A\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
such that
x\in A
, by Theorem
3.2
. Thus,
\pi(x)\neq X
. Since
B(x)\subseteq\pi(x)
, we need to show that
\pi(x)\subseteq B(x)
. Observe that if
B(x)
is closed, then
B(x)\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
, and hence,
B(x)\in\{A\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)):x\in A\}
and
\pi(x)\subseteq B(x)
(see (
3.1
)).

We see that
B(x)
is closed. Let
(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
B(x)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n}=w
for some
w\in X
. Suppose that
w\in\mathrm{Cl}(B(x))\setminus B(x)
. Since
B(x)\subseteq\pi(x)
,
\pi(x)
is closed and
\mathrm{Int}(\pi(x))=\emptyset
, we have that there exists a nonempty open subset
U
of
X
such that
U\cap\mathrm{Cl}(B(x))=\emptyset
.
Furthermore, there exists
L\in\mathcal{C}(X)
such that
w\in L
,
x\notin L
and
L\cap U\neq\emptyset
, because
w\notin B(x)
. Since
X
has the property of Kelley, there exists a sequence
(L_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathcal{C}(X)
such that
w_{n}\in L_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}=L
. Note that
L\in\langle X\setminus\{x\},U\rangle
. Thus, there exists a positive number
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
L_{i}\in\langle X\setminus\{x\},U\rangle
for each
i\geq k
. Let
k_{0}>k
. Note that
L_{k_{0}}\cap B(x)\neq\emptyset
,
L_{k_{0}}\cap U\neq\emptyset
and
x\notin L_{k_{0}}
. This contradicts Remark
3.3
. Therefore,
B(x)
is closed and
\pi(x)=B(x)
.
∎

Question 3.5
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum. If
X
has the property of Kelley, then does it follow that
B(x)
is closed for each
x\in X
?

It is easy to verify that
B(x)
is one composant of
X
, whenever
X
is an hereditarily indecomposable continuum. Thus, if
X
is hereditarily indecomposable, then
X
has the property of Kelley and
B(x)
is not closed (see
[
6
, 20.6, p.168]
).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley. If
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum, then
\pi
is a map.

Proof.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
X
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x
for some
x\in X
. We prove that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi(x_{n})=\pi(x)
. To see this, we show that
\limsup\pi(x_{n})\subseteq\pi(x)
and
\pi(x)\subseteq\liminf\pi(x_{n})
.

We see that
\limsup\pi(x_{n})\subseteq\pi(x)
. Let
z\in\limsup\pi(x_{n})
. Let
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of positive numbers such that, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
z_{n_{k}}\in\pi(x_{n_{k}})
where
\lim_{k\to\infty}z_{n_{k}}=z

Note that

Given a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley

(3.2)

Proposition 3.8
.

Let

be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley such that

and

\mathcal{M}

.

2.

\pi(x)
is terminal, for each

.

\mathcal{N}
is compact.

4.

\bigcup\mathcal{N}

.

We see that

. Let

),

. We see that

(\rho(x_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in

and
\mathcal{L}

, and

,

, by Proposition

. We have proved

.

Let

be such that
\pi(x)\in\mathcal{N}
. We prove that
\pi(x)
is terminal. Since

). Since

. Let

be a subcontinuum of

. Suppose that

. Let

. Note that

. We know that

, by Proposition

. Thus,

, by Remark

. Since

,

. Therefore,
\pi(x)
is terminal. The proof of
2

We see
3
. Suppose that
\mathcal{N}
is not compact, that is, there exists a sequence
(\pi(x_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{N}

,

(see

17
, Corollary 25.4]
). Let

be a nonempty open subsets of

. Since

, there exists a continuum

such that

,

is an open subset of

such that

. Since

, and

in

for each

, because

is terminal, for each

(see

in this proposition). Therefore,

. This contradicts the facts that

and

.

We prove
4
. Since

is compact,

is compact, by

6
, Exercise 11.5]
. We suppose that

and

Theorem 3.9
.

Let

be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley such that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum. Let
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
be as (
3.2
) and (

). Then,
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{N}
is a continuous decomposition of

.

Proof.

In order to prove that
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{L}
, we show first that
\bigcup\mathcal{N}=X
. We know that
\bigcup\mathcal{N}
is a continuum, by Proposition
3.8
(
4
). Suppose that
\bigcup\mathcal{N}\neq X
.
By Lemma
3.1
, there exists

such that

. Note that

for each

. Hence, there exists
z\in X
such that
\pi(z)\in\mathcal{N}
and
\pi(z)\subseteq B
. A contradiction. Therefore,
\bigcup\mathcal{N}=X
.

We see that
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{L}
. Let
x\in X
. Since
\bigcup\mathcal{N}=X
, there exists

such that
\pi(z)\in\mathcal{N}
and

. Hence,

and, since
\pi(z)
is minimal,
\pi(x)=\pi(z)
. Thus,
\pi(x)\in\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{L}
.

Observe that if every
\pi(x)
is minimal, then every
\pi(x)
is maximal; i.e.,
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}
. Thus,
\mathcal{N}
is a decomposition, by Proposition
3.8
(

). Finally, we have that

is a continuous decomposition because

is a map (see Proposition

and Theorem

).
∎

We know that
\mathcal{N}
is a continuous decomposition of

, and
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(X)
. Let

denote the decomposition topology on
\mathcal{N}
induced by the surjective map

defined by

for each

, and let

denote the topology on
\mathcal{N}
as a subspace of

. Then, by

, Theorem 13.10]
,

is an open map, and

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley such that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum. Let
\mathcal{N}=\{\pi(x):x\in X\}
be the continuous decomposition of
X
and let
q\colon X\to\mathcal{N}
be the quotient map. Then,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
, and
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N})\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
.

Proof.

Let
D\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. By (
3.1
),
\pi(x)\subseteq D
for each
x\in D
. Hence,
D=\bigcup\{\pi(x):x\in D\}
, for each
D\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. Thus, we have that
D=q^{-1}(q(D))
for each
D\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. Since
q
is an open monotone map, by
[
4
, Lemma 5.1]
, we have that

q(D)\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))\text{ for each }D\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)).

(3.4)

Let
g\colon\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))\to\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
be defined by
g(D)=q(D)
for each
D\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. Note that
g
is well defined, by (
3.4
). Furthermore,
g=2^{q}|_{\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))}
where
2^{q}\colon 2^{X}\to 2^{\mathcal{N}}
is the induced map given by
2^{q}(A)=q(A)
for each
A\in 2^{X}
. Since

is a map,
g
is a map (see
[
6
, p.188]
).

We see that
g
is one-to-one. Let
A,B\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
be such that
A\neq B
. Without loss of generality, we may suppose that there exists
x\in A\setminus B
. Hence,
\pi(x)\in q(A)
. If
\pi(x)\in q(B)
, then there exists
z\in B
such that
\pi(z)=\pi(x)
. Since
\pi(z)\subseteq B
,
x\in B
. A contradiction. Thus,
\pi(x)\notin q(B)
. Therefore,
g(A)\neq g(B)
and

is one-to-one.

Let
B\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
. By
[
4
, Lemma 5.1]
,
q^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. Since
q
is surjective,
q(q^{-1}(B))=B
. Thus,
g(q^{-1}(B))=B
and
g
is surjective. Since
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is compact and
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
is a Hausdorff space,
g
is a homeomorphism (see
[
17
, Theorem 17.14]
). Therefore,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
.

Finally, if
D\in\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N})
, then there exists
x\in X
such that

. Since
\pi(x)\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
,
g(\pi(x))\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
. Therefore,
D\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
and
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N})\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
.
∎

4
More about the property of Kelley

In this section, the show some extra properties related to a continuum with the property of Kelley and its hyperspace of nonblockers. The main result is Theorem
4.8
when we show that if

is decomposable with the property of Kelley,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum and

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley. Let
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and let

be such that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)=\emptyset

\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))\neq\emptyset
, then
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))
.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and let
x,y\in X\setminus A
be such that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)=\emptyset
and
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))\neq\emptyset
. Let
z\in\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))
. Suppose that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))\neq\emptyset
. Let
w\in\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))
. Since
\{z,w\}\subseteq\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)
, there exists a continuum

L
such that
\{z,w\}\subseteq L
and
L\cap A=\emptyset
. Let
U
be an open subset of
X
such that
w\in U
and
U\cap\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))=\emptyset
. Note that
L\in\langle X\setminus A,U\rangle
where
\langle X\setminus A,U\rangle
is an open subset of
2^{X}
. Since
z\in\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))\setminus\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)
, there exists a sequence
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}z_{n}=z
. Since
X
has the property of Kelley, there exists a sequence of subcontinua
(L_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
such that
z_{n}\in L_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}=L
. Hence, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
L_{i}\in\langle X\setminus A,U\rangle
for each
i\geq k
. Let
m>k
. Hence,
L_{m}\cap\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)\neq\emptyset
and
L_{m}\cap A=\emptyset
. Thus,
L_{m}\subseteq\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)
. This contradicts that
L_{m}\cap U\neq\emptyset
and
U\cap\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))=\emptyset
. Therefore,
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X
be a continuum and let
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pairwise disjoint subcontinua of

such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}=A
for some

. Then, there exists a countable set

such that

,

for each

, and

.

Proof.

Let
E=\{e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a dense subset of
A
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(x_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence such that
x_{i}^{n}\in A_{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\lim_{i\to\infty}x_{i}^{n}=e_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2}\ldots
be a sequence of pairwise disjoint subsets of
\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{N}_{n}
is infinite for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{N}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{N}_{n}
. Let
D=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}D_{n}
where
D_{n}=\{x_{i}^{n}:i\in\mathcal{N}_{n}\}
. It is not difficult to see that
D
satisfies all the conditions in our lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X
be a continuum and let
A
be a proper subcontinuum of
X
such that
X\setminus A
is not connected by continua. Let
B\in 2^{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}(X)
be such that
\mathrm{Int}(B)=\emptyset
and
B\subseteq X\setminus A
. Suppose that
B
satisfies the following conditions:

1.

D\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
for every component
D
of
B
;

2.

\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))\cap\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x^{\prime})=\emptyset
for every
x,x^{\prime}\in X\setminus A
such that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x^{\prime})=\emptyset

\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))\cap B\neq\emptyset
and
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x^{\prime}))\cap B\neq\emptyset
; and

3.

B\cap\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)
is a component of
B
for each
x\in X\setminus A
such that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap B\neq\emptyset
.

Then,
B\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
.

Proof.

We prove that
X\setminus B
is connected by continua. To this end, we show that for every
x\in X\setminus B
, there exists a subcontinuum
L_{x}
of
X\setminus B
such that
x\in L_{x}
and
L_{x}\cap A\neq\emptyset
. Let
x\in X\setminus B
. If
x\in A
, then we define
L_{x}=A
and clearly
x\in L_{x}
,
L_{x}\cap B=\emptyset
and
L_{x}\cap A\neq\emptyset
. Hence, we suppose that
x\in X\setminus A
. Note that if
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))\cap B=\emptyset
, then
L_{x}=\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))
is a continuum satisfying the conditions. Thus, assume that
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))\cap B\neq\emptyset
. Let
z\in\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))\cap B
. It is clear that
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(z))\cap B\neq\emptyset
. Thus, by
2
,
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap\kappa_{X\setminus A}(z)\neq\emptyset
and
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)=\kappa_{X\setminus A}(z)
. Observe that we showed that

\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))\cap B=\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap B.

(4.1)

Furthermore,
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap B
is a component of
B
, by
3
. Since
B
is not connected, there exists
b\in B
such that
b\in X\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))
(see (
4.1
)). Hence,
X\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))\neq\emptyset
. Let
U=X\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))
and let
D=\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)\cap B
. We know that
D\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
, by
1
. Since
x\notin D
, there exists a subcontinuum
H
of
X\setminus D
such that
x\in H
and
H\cap U\neq\emptyset
. Note that
H\cap A\neq\emptyset
. Let
L_{x}
be the component of
H\cap\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))
such that
x\in L_{x}
. If
L_{x}\cap A=\emptyset
, then there exists a subcontinuum
J
of
H\setminus A
such that
L_{x}\subseteq J
and
J\neq L_{x}
, by
[
11
, Corollary 5.5]
. Thus,
J\subseteq\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)
contradicting that
L_{x}
is a component of
H\cap\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))
; i.e.,
L_{x}\cap A\neq\emptyset
. Since
H\cap D=\emptyset
and
L_{x}\subseteq H
,
L_{x}\cap D=\emptyset
. Furthermore,
L_{x}\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x))
and hence,
L_{x}\cap B=\emptyset
, by (
4.1
). Therefore, for every
x\in X\setminus B
, there exists a subcontinuum
L_{x}
of
X\setminus B
such that
x\in L_{x}
and
L_{x}\cap A\neq\emptyset
.

If
x,y\in X\setminus B
, then there exist subcontinua
L_{x}
and
L_{y}
of
X\setminus B
such that
x\in L_{x},y\in L_{y}
,
L_{x}\cap A\neq\emptyset
and
L_{y}\cap A\neq\emptyset
. Thus,
L_{x}\cup A\cup L_{y}
is a proper subcontinuum of
X\setminus B
containing both
x
and
y
. Therefore,
X\setminus B
is connected by continua and
B\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley such that
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
and
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is compact. If
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)
is such that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(w)
is dense for some
w\in X\setminus A
, then there exists
L\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
such that
A\subseteq L
.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and let
w\in X\setminus A
be such that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(w)
is dense in
X
. If
A\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
, then
L=A
. Hence, suppose that
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)\setminus\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. Let
y\in X\setminus A
be such that
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))\neq X
. We define

L=A\cup\{y\in X\setminus A:\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))\neq X\}.

(4.2)

We see that
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))\subseteq L
for each
y\in L\setminus A
. Let
y\in L\setminus A
and let
z\in\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))
. Note that if
z\in\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))\setminus A
, then
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(z)\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))
, by Lemma
4.1
. Hence,
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(z))\neq X
and
z\in L
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))\subseteq L
for each
y\in L\setminus A
.

We prove that
L
is a continuum. Since
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y))\cap A\neq\emptyset
for each
y\in L\setminus A
, and
L=A\cup(\bigcup\{\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)):y\in L\setminus A\})
, we have that
L
is connected. We show that
L
is closed. Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
L
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x
for some
x\in X
. We see that
x\in L
. Observe that if there exist
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{k}
in
L\setminus A
such that

\mathcal{N}=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:x_{n}\in A\cup\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y_{1}))\cup\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y_{2}))\cup\cdots\cup\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y_{k}))\right\}

is infinite, then
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{N}}
converges to some point of
A\cup\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y_{1}))\cup\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y_{2}))\cup\cdots\cup\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y_{k}))
. Hence,
x\in L
. Thus, by Lemma
4.1
, we may assume that there exists a sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
L\setminus A
such that
x_{n}\in\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y_{n})
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and

\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y_{i}))\cap\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y_{j})=\emptyset\text{ whenever that }i\neq j.

(4.3)

Suppose that
x\notin L
. We will have a contradiction, showing that
X\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. Let
U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}
be open subsets of
X
such that
X\in\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}\rangle
. Since
x\notin L
,
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)
is dense. Thus, there exists a subcontinuum
M
of
X\setminus A
such that
x\in M
and
M\in\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}\rangle
. Notice that
M\in\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}\rangle\cap\langle X\setminus A\rangle
. Since
X
has the property of Kelley, there exists a sequence of subcontinua
(M_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
such that
x_{n}\in M_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}M_{n}=M
. Hence, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M_{n}\in\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}\rangle\cap\langle X\setminus A\rangle
for each
n\geq k
. Since
M_{n}\cap A=\emptyset
and
x_{n}\in M_{n}
, we have that
M_{n}\subseteq\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y_{n})
for each
n\geq k
. Thus,
(M_{n})_{n\geq k}
is a sequence of pairwise disjoint subcontinua of
X
, by (
4.3
). By Lemma
4.2
, there exists a countable set
D\subseteq\bigcup_{n\geq k}M_{n}
such that
|D\cap M_{n}|=1
for each
n\geq k
and
M\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(D)
. We know that
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. If
F
is a finite subset of
D
, then
F\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
, by Lemma
4.3
. Let
R_{m}=(\bigcup_{k\leq i\leq m}M_{i})\cap D
, for each
m\geq k
. Note that
R_{n}\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
for each
n\geq m
. Furthermore, since
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is compact, we may suppose that
\lim_{m\to\infty}R_{m}=Q
for some
Q\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. Since
M\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(D)
and
\mathrm{Cl}(D)\subseteq Q
,
M\subseteq Q
. Thus,
Q\in\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}\rangle
. Therefore,
X
is a limit point of
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. This contradicts the compactness of
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
and the fact that
X\notin\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. We have that
L
is a subcontinuum of
X
.

Finally,
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)
is dense and
L\cap\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)=\emptyset
. Hence,
\mathrm{Int}(L)=\emptyset
. Furthermore,
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(z)
is dense, for every
z\in X\setminus L
(see definition of
L
in (
4.2
)). Therefore,
L\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
and
A\subseteq L
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley such that
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
and
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is compact. If
A\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
, then
X\setminus A
is connected by continua.

Proof.

Let
x\in X\setminus A
. We prove that
X\setminus A=\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)
. Suppose that there exists
y\in X\setminus A
such that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)\cap\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)=\emptyset
. Note that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)
is dense, because
A\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. Since
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)
is dense, there exists a sequence of subcontinua
(L_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)
such that
L_{n}\subseteq L_{n+1}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}=X

[
4
, Proposition 2.2]
. Since
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)\cap L_{n}=\emptyset
and
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)
is dense, we have that
\kappa_{X\setminus A}(y)\subseteq\kappa_{X\setminus L_{n}}(y)
; i.e.,
\kappa_{X\setminus L_{n}}(y)
is dense for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, there exists a continuum
K_{n}\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
such that
L_{n}\subseteq K_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by Theorem
4.4
. Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}=X
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}K_{n}=X
. Thus,
X\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
, a contradiction. Therefore,
X\setminus A=\kappa_{X\setminus A}(x)
and
X\setminus A
is connected by continua.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley such that
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
and
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum. If
W
is a proper subcontinuum of
X
, then each component of
X\setminus W
is open.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that there exist a component
C
of
X\setminus W
and
p\in C
such that
p\in\mathrm{Cl}(X\setminus C)
.

Claim 4.7
.

\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p))\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(X\setminus C)
.

To prove the Claim
4.7
, we need to show that
\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p)\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(X\setminus C)
.
Let
q\in\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p)
. Hence, there exists a subcontinuum
P
of
X\setminus W
such that
\{p,q\}\subseteq P
.
Let
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
X\setminus C
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n}=p
. Since
X
is compact, we may suppose that
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
X\setminus(W\cup C)
. Since
X
has the property of Kelley, there exists a sequence of subcontinua
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
such that
p_{n}\in P_{n}
for all
n
, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{n}=P
.
Notice that
\langle X\setminus W\rangle
is an open subset of
2^{X}
, and
P\in\langle X\setminus W\rangle
. Hence, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
P_{n}\in\langle X\setminus W\rangle
for each
n\geq k
. Since
p_{n}
belongs to a different component to
C
in
X\setminus W
, and
P_{n}\cap W=\emptyset
for each
n\geq k
, we have that
P_{n}\cap C=\emptyset
for all
n\geq k
. Hence,
P_{n}\subseteq X\setminus(W\cup C)
for each
n\geq k
. Thus,
P\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(X\setminus(W\cup C))\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(X\setminus C)
. Therefore,
q\in\mathrm{Cl}(X\setminus C)
and
\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p)\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(X\setminus C)
. We have proved the Claim
4.7
.

We prove that
2^{\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p))}\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. Let
F=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}\}
be a finite subset of
\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p)
. By Claim
4.7
, for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, there exists a sequence
(z^{j}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
X\setminus C
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{j}_{n}=w_{j}
. Since
W
is compact, we may suppose that
(z^{j}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
X\setminus(C\cup W)
for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Furthermore, observe that if
D
is a component of
X\setminus W
and
D\neq C
, then
\{n\in\mathbb{N}:z^{j}_{n}\in D\}
is a finite set for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Hence, if necessary, we can take a subsequence
(z^{j}_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(z^{j}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, such that
|\{z_{n_{k}}^{j}:k\in\mathbb{N},j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\}\cap D|\leq 1
for each component
D
of
X\setminus W
. Let
F_{i}=\{z^{1}_{n_{i}},\ldots,z^{m}_{n_{i}}\}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
,
F_{i}\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, by Lemma
4.3
. It is not difficult to see that
\lim_{i\to\infty}F_{i}=F
. Since
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is compact,
F\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. We have that
F\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
for every finite subset
F
of
\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p)
. It is easy to see that the collection of all finite subsets of
\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p)
is dense of
2^{\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p))}
.
Hence, since
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is compact,
2^{\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p))}\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
.

Let
Q=W\cap\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p))
. Since
Q\in 2^{\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p))}
,
Q\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. Note that
\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p)\subseteq C
and
C
is a component of
X\setminus W
. Hence,
\mathrm{Cl}(\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p))\setminus\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p)\subseteq W
. Thus,
p\notin Q
and
\kappa_{X\setminus Q}(p)\subseteq\kappa_{X\setminus W}(p)\subseteq C
. This clearly is a contradiction, because
\kappa_{X\setminus Q}(p)
has to be dense and
C
is not dense. Therefore, each component of
X\setminus W
is open.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley such that
\mathcal{F}_{1}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
. If
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum, then
X
is aposyndetic.

Proof.

Let
p\in X
. We show that
X
is aposyndetic at
p
with respect to each point of
X\setminus\{p\}
(see Definition
2.2
). Let
q\in X\setminus\{p\}
. Since
\{q\}\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
,
X\setminus\{q\}
is connected by continua, by Theorem
4.5
. Furthermore, there exists a sequence of subcontinua
(L_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X\setminus\{q\}
such that
X\setminus\{q\}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L_{n}
, by Lemma
2.3
. Since
X
is a Baire space, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathrm{Int}(L_{k})\neq\emptyset

[
17
, Corollary 25.4]
. Since

X\setminus\{q\}
is connected by continua, there exists a subcontinuum
R
of
X\setminus\{q\}
such that
p\in R
and
R\cap L_{k}\neq\emptyset
. Since
X
has the property of Kelley, there exists a continuum
W
such that
L_{k}\cup R\subseteq\mathrm{Int}(W)\subseteq W\subseteq X\setminus\{q\}
, by Theorem
2.6
. Thus,
p\in\mathrm{Int}(W)
and
q\notin W
. Therefore,
X
is aposyndetic at
p
with respect to
q
and hence,
X
is aposyndetic.
∎

5
The simple closed curve

In this section, we present our main results. We show two characterizations in theorems

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

Suppose that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))

4.8
,
X
is aposyndetic. Hence,

is semi-locally connected, by
[
8
, Theorem 1.7.17]
. We prove that

is locally connected. Let
p\in X
and let
V
be an open subset of
X
such that

. Since

is semi-locally connected, there exists an open subset
U
of
X
such that
p\in U\subseteq V
and

has a finite number of components. Let
L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{k}
be the components of

. Since
\{p\}\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
,
X\setminus\{p\}
is connected by continua, by Theorem
4.5
. Thus, for each
i\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
, there exists a subcontinuum
M_{i}
of
X\setminus\{p\}
such that
M_{i}\cap L_{1}\neq\emptyset
and
M_{i}\cap L_{i}\neq\emptyset
.

Let

W=(L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup\cdots\cup L_{k})\cup(M_{2}\cup M_{3}\cup\cdots\cup M_{k}).

Observe that
W
is a continuum and
p\notin W
. Let
J
be the component of
X\setminus W
such that
p\in J
. By Theorem
4.6
,
J
is open. Furthermore,
J\subseteq X\setminus W\subseteq U\subseteq V
. Thus,
X
is locally connected at
p
. Therefore,
X
is a simple closed curve, by Theorem
2.5
.

Conversely,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(S^{1}))=\mathcal{F}_{1}(S^{1})
. Therefore,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(S^{1}))
is a continuum.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X
be a decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley. If
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum, then there exists a map
f\colon X\to S^{1}
such that
f
is open, monotone and
f^{-1}(z)
is a terminal subcontinuum of
X
for every
z\in S^{1}
.

Proof.

We know that
\mathcal{N}=\{\pi(x):x\in X\}
is a continuous decomposition of
X
such that
\pi(x)
is a terminal subcontinuum of
X
for every
x\in X
, by Proposition
3.8
and Theorem
3.9
. Let
\rho\colon X\to\mathcal{N}
be the quotient map. Since
\rho
is monotone,
\mathcal{N}
has the property of Kelley, by
[
15
, Theorem 4.3]
. It is not difficult to see that
\mathcal{N}
is decomposable. Furthermore,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
is a continuum and
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N})\subseteq\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{N}))
, by Theorem
3.10
. Thus,
\mathcal{N}
is a simple closed curve, by Theorem
5.1
. Let
g\colon\mathcal{N}\to S^{1}
be a homeomorphism. It is clear that
f=g\circ\rho
is a map from
X
to
S^{1}
monotone, open and
f^{-1}(z)
is terminal for each
z\in S^{1}
.
∎

The following result give us a partial answer to Question
1.2
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X
be an hereditarily decomposable continuum with the property of Kelley. Then,
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum if and only if
X
is a simple closed curve.

Proof.

We assume that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
is a continuum. By Theorem
5.2
, there exists a map
f\colon X\to S^{1}
such that
f
is open, monotone and
f^{-1}(z)
is a terminal subcontinuum of
X
for each
z\in S^{1}
. We show that
|f^{-1}(z)|=1
for every
z\in S^{1}
. Let
z_{0}\in S^{1}
and let
\alpha
be an arc in
S^{1}
such that
z_{0}\in\alpha
. Let
L=f^{-1}(\alpha)
. Since
f
is monotone,
L
is a continuum. Let
p,q\in L
be such that
\alpha
is irreducible between
f(p)
and
f(q)
. We see that
L
is irreducible between
p
and
q
. Let
K
be a subcontinuum of
L
such that
p,q\in K
. Hence,
f(K)=\alpha
. Since
f^{-1}(z)
is terminal for each
z\in\alpha
and
f^{-1}(z)\cap K\neq\emptyset
, we have that
f^{-1}(z)\subseteq K
for each
z\in\alpha
. Thus,
K=f^{-1}(\alpha)
and
K=L
. Therefore,
L
is irreducible.

Let
g=f|_{L}\colon L\to\alpha
. Since
f
is open,
g
is open, by
[
16
, Theorem 4.31]
. Furthermore,
g
monotone and
g^{-1}(z)
is terminal for each
z\in\alpha
. Thus,
D=\{x\in L:g^{-1}(g(x))=x\}
is a dense subset of
L
, by
[
13
, Corollary 9, p.130]
. Since
g
is open,
\{g^{-1}(z):z\in\alpha\}
is a continuous decomposition of
L
(see
[
16
, Theorem 4.32, p.130]
). Thus,
g^{-1}(g(x))=x
for each
x\in L
. In particular,
|f^{-1}(z_{0})|=1
. Therefore,
f
is injective, and
f
is a homeomorphism.

It is clear that
\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(S^{1}))
is a continuum. Therefore, we complete the proof of our theorem.
∎

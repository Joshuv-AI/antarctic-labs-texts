Pseudo-ovals of elliptic quadrics as Delsarte designs of association schemes

Abstract
A pseudo-oval of a finite projective space over a finite field of odd order q q is a configuration of equidimensional subspaces that is essentially equivalent to a translation generalised quadrangle of order ( q n , q n ) (q^{n},q^{n}) and a Laguerre plane of order q n q^{n} (for some n n ). In setting out a programme to construct new generalised quadrangles, Shult and Thas [ 17 ] asked whether there are pseudo-ovals consisting only of lines of an elliptic quadric Q − ​ ( 5 , q ) {Q}^{-}(5,q) , non-equivalent to the classical example , a so-called pseudo-conic . To date, every known pseudo-oval of lines of Q − ​ ( 5 , q ) {Q}^{-}(5,q) is projectively equivalent to a pseudo-conic. Thas [ 18 ] characterised pseudo-conics as pseudo-ovals satisfying the perspective property, and this paper is on characterisations of pseudo-conics from an algebraic combinatorial point of view. In particular, we show that pseudo-ovals in Q − ​ ( 5 , q ) Q^{-}(5,q) and pseudo-conics can be characterised as certain Delsarte designs of an interesting five-class association scheme. These association schemes are introduced and explored, and we provide a complete theory of how pseudo-ovals of lines of Q − ​ ( 5 , q ) Q^{-}(5,q) can be analysed from this viewpoint.



Keywords: Association scheme, Elliptic quadric, Pseudo-oval

subspaces corresponding to the points of a non-degenerate conic of

[
18
]
characterised pseudo-conics as pseudo-ovals of

q

Let

[

]

[
18
]
.
In the quest to construct new generalised quadrangles, Shult and Thas
[

To date, every known pseudo-oval of lines of

Under the Klein correspondence, pseudo-ovals contained in

are mapped onto

of

[
16
]

of the Hermitian surface

is a set

of
q^{2}+1
points such that any point of

not in

is orthogonal to 0 or 2 points of
\mathcal{S}

[

]
.
From a result by De Soete and Thas

]
,

Bader, O’Keefe, Penttila in
[

]

whose polarity commutes with the given unitary one. The special sets in this class are called

This paper is on characterisations of pseudo-conics from an algebraic combinatorial point of view. In particular, we will show (see Theorem
5.6
) that pseudo-ovals and pseudo-conics in

can be characterised as certain Delsarte designs of an interesting five-class association scheme. These association schemes are introduced and explored, and we provide a complete theory of how pseudo-ovals of lines of

The paper is organised as follows. Section
2
contains some notation and introductory material on projective geometry, classical polar geometries and association schemes. In Section
3
we investigate the perspective property for lines of

{\rm PG}(5,q)

3.5
).
In Section

[

Section

Q^{-}(5,q)

In this setting, from comparing the characteristic vector of a pseudo-oval with the common eigenspaces of the scheme, we provide a characterisation of pseudo-conics in terms of the configurations introduced in Section

Association schemes are important objects in algebraic combinatorics that generalise distance-regular graphs, linear codes, and combinatorial designs. As we shall see, the theory of association schemes can be a powerful tool when applied to some problems in finite geometry. An association scheme
\mathfrak{X}=(X,\{R_{i}\}_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d})
is a set of
vertices

X
and binary
relations

R_{i}
on
X
satisfying the following:

1.

R_{0}
is the diagonal relation, that is,
R_{0}=\{(x,x):x\in X\}
.

2.

\{R_{i}\}
is closed under taking the opposite relation; that is,
R_{j}^{*}:=\{(x,y)\colon(y,x)\in R_{j}\}
is in
\{R_{i}\}
, for each
j
.

3.

For each
i,j,k\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
, there exist constants
p_{i,j}^{k}
, such that if
(x,y)\in R_{k}
, then there are
p_{i,j}^{k}
vertices
z
such that
(x,z)\in R_{i}
and
(z,y)\in R_{j}
. The
p_{i,j}^{k}
are called

.

We will say that the association scheme is
symmetric
if each relation is equal to its opposite. Let
\mathfrak{X}=(X,\{R_{i}\}_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d})
be an association scheme with
d
classes. For
0\leqslant i\leqslant d
, let
A_{i}
be the adjacency matrix of the relation
R_{i}
, and

the
i-
th primitive idempotent of the Bose-Mesner algebra of
\mathfrak{X}
which projects onto the
i-
th maximal common eigenspace of
A_{0},\ldots,A_{d}
. The matrices
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
defined by

(A_{0}\ A_{1}\ \ldots\ A_{d})=(E_{0}\ E_{1}\ \ldots\ E_{d})\mathcal{P}

and

(E_{0}\ E_{1}\ \ldots\ E_{d})=|X|^{-1}(A_{0}\ A_{1}\ \ldots\ A_{d})\mathcal{Q}

are the
first
and the
second eigenmatrix
of
\mathfrak{X}
, respectively. The reader is referred to
[
2
,
3
,
7
]
for additional information on association schemes.

\chi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\cdot v=\chi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\cdot\chi_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}-\chi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\cdot\chi_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}=|\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{O}_{1}|-|\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{O}_{2}|.

Since
\mathcal{S}
is a pseudo-oval, we have
|\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{O}_{1}|,|\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{O}_{2}|\leqslant 1
. Furthermore,

|\mathcal{S}\cap(\mathcal{O}_{1}\cup\mathcal{O}_{2})|=|\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{O}_{1}|+|\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{O}_{2}|,

because
\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
are disjoint sets of lines.
Hence,
\chi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\cdot v=0
, for all
v\in\mathcal{V}_{l}
, if and only if each
\mathcal{U}_{p_{1},p_{2}}
meets
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
in
0
or
2
elements.
∎

Theorem
5.13
and the following proposition provide an additional way to characterise pseudo-conics.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a pseudo-oval of
Q^{-}(5,q)
and
l\in\mathcal{S}
. Let
A
be the average
number of
\mathcal{U}_{p_{1},p_{2}}
over all flags
(B,l)
containing two distinct elements of
\mathcal{S}\setminus\{l\}
.
Then,
A=q+1
if and only if each
\mathcal{U}_{p_{1},p_{2}}
meets
\mathcal{S}\backslash\{l\}
in
0
or
2
elements.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{i}
be the number of lines of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}\setminus\{l\}
contained in the
i-
th set of type
\mathcal{U}_{p_{1},p_{2}}
.
We count in two ways the number of pairs
(m,\mathcal{U}_{p_{1},p_{2}})
such that
m\in\mathcal{U}_{p_{1},p_{2}}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. From Lemma
5.10
(b), this number is

\sum_{i}\mu_{i}=|\mathcal{S}^{\prime}|(q+1)(q^{2}-1)=q^{2}(q+1)(q^{2}-1).

If we double count triples
(m,n,\mathcal{U}_{p_{1},p_{2}})
where
m
and
n
are distinct elements of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
lying in
\mathcal{U}_{p_{1},p_{2}}
, we see that

\sum_{i}\mu_{i}(\mu_{i}-1)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\backslash\{m\}}|\{\mathcal{U}_{p_{1},p_{2}}\colon m,n\in\mathcal{U}_{p_{1},p_{2}}\}|=q^{2}(q^{2}-1)A.

Hence,

\sum_{i}\mu_{i}(2-\mu_{i})=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}-\sum_{i}\mu_{i}(\mu_{i}-1)=q^{2}(q^{2}-1)(q+1-A).

Therefore,
A=q+1
precisely when each
\mu_{i}
is
0
or
2
.
∎

\begin{array}[]{lrcl}\textrm{Maximise:}&0&&\\
\textrm{subject to:}&\boldsymbol{x}B-2\boldsymbol{y}&=&0\\
&\sum_{i}x_{i}&=&q^{2}\\
&x_{i},y_{j}&\in&\{0,1\}.\end{array}

(18)

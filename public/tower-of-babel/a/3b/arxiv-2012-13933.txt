Anisotropic p p -capacity and anisotropic Minkowski inequality Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 53C21, 31C15, 52A39, 49Q10. Thanks: CX was supported by NSFC (Grant No. 11871406).
Chao Xia, Jiabin Yin

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a sharp anisotropic L p L^{p} Minkowski inequality involving the total L p L^{p} anisotropic mean curvature and the anisotropic p p -capacity, for any bounded domains with smooth boundary in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . As consequences, we obtain an anisotropic Willmore inequality, a sharp anisotropic Minkowski inequality for outward F F -minimising sets and a sharp volumetric anisotropic Minkowski inequality. For the proof, we utilize a nonlinear potential theoretic approach which has been recently developed in [ 3 ] .



1.
Introduction

The Minkowski inequality in the theory of convex geometry says that if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex domain with smooth boundary, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{H}{n-1}d\sigma\geq\left(\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{\omega_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}},

(1.1)

with equality holding if and only if
\Omega
is a round ball. Here
H
is the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
with respect to the outward unit normal and
\omega_{n-1}=|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|,
the area of unit
(n-1)
-sphere.

In recent decades, many mathematicians are interested in the question whether (
1.1
) holds for non-convex domains.
By using smooth solutions to inverse mean curvature flow, Guan-Li
[
20
]
proved that (
1.1
) holds for star-shaped domains with mean convex boundary. Huisken-Ilmanen
[
23
]
developed a level set weak formulation for inverse mean curvature flow, which was adopted by Huisken
[
22
]
to prove (
1.1
) for outward minimising sets (see also
[
19
]
).
Quite recently, a powerful and elegant approach based on nonlinear potential theory has been developed by Agostiniani-Fogagnolo-Mazzieri in
[
3
]
(see also related papers
[
2
,
6
,
18
]
), which enables them to establish a sharp
L^{p}
version of Minkowski inequality involving the
p
-capacity of
\Omega
(
1<p<n
)
for general bounded domain with smooth boundary

\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\Big|\frac{H}{n-1}\Big|^{p}d\sigma\geq\left[\big(\frac{p-1}{n-p}\big)^{p-1}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}{\rm Cap}_{p}(\Omega)\right]^{\frac{n-p-1}{n-p}}.

(1.2)

Here
{\rm Cap}_{p}(\Omega)
is called by the
p
-capacity of
\Omega
, given by

{\rm Cap}_{p}(\Omega)=\inf\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx:u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),u\geq 1\hbox{ on }\Omega\Big\}.

We remark that various geometric inequalities involving the mean curvature integral, the capacity and the surface area have been considered by Xiao
[
40
]
. In particular, (
1.2
) has been proved in
[
40
, Theorem 3.1]
in the case of star-shaped, mean convex domains.
As
p\to 1
in (
1.2
), Agostiniani-Fogagnolo-Mazzieri have showed that the limit version of (
1.2
) recovers (
1.1
) for outward minimising sets by Huisken
[
22
]
. Moreover, they are able to obtain a volumetric version of Minkowski inequality

\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\Big|\frac{H}{n-1}\Big|d\sigma\geq\left(\frac{|\Omega|}{|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}.

(1.3)

for any bounded domains. Previously, (
1.3
) has been shown by Trudinger
[
33
]
and Chang-Wang
[
14
]
(see also
[
27
]
) for bounded domains with
2
-convex boundary.
The idea of Agostiniani-Fogagonolo-Mazzieri in
[
3
]
is based on a conformal change of the Euclidean
metric and a monotone integral over level sets of
p
-capacitary potentials under the conformal metric. Actually, it turns out that the results can be obtained by working directly on the Euclidean
metric rather than the conformal metric. It is worth highlighting that their approach can be used to obtain sharp geometric inequalities for hypersurfaces in manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature
[
2
,
6
,
18
]
as well as for static manifolds
[
7
,
11
,
12
]
, see also
[
13
,
39
]
.

In this paper, we are concerned with an anisotropic version of Minkowski inequality.
Let
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be a Minkowski norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The unit Wulff ball
\mathcal{W}_{F}
with respect to
F
(centered at the origin) is given by

\mathcal{W}_{F}=\{F^{o}(x)<1\},

where
F^{o}
is the dual norm of
F
.
For a bounded domain
\Omega
with smooth boundary, the
anisotropic area
of
\partial\Omega
is defined by

|\partial\Omega|_{F}=\int_{\partial\Omega}F(\nu)d\sigma.

The well-known Wulff theorem (see e.g Theorem 20.8 in
[
25
]
) says that Wulff balls are the only minimizers for the
anisotropic isoperimetric problem
. Equivalently,
the
Wulff inequality
holds true:

|\partial\Omega|_{F}\geq n|\mathcal{W}_{F}|^{\frac{1}{n}}|\Omega|^{1-\frac{1}{n}}.

(1.4)

Equality in (
1.4
) holds if and only if
\Omega
is a Wulff ball.

The anisotropic mean curvature
H_{F}
of
\partial\Omega
arises from the first variational formula for
|\partial\Omega|_{F}
.
The anisotropic Minkowski inequality
says that if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
convex
domain with smooth boundary, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{H_{F}}{n-1}F(\nu)d\sigma\geq\left(\frac{|\partial\Omega|_{F}}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}},

(1.5)

with equality holding if and only if
\Omega
is a Wulff ball. Here
\kappa_{n-1}=n|\mathcal{W}_{F}|
. When
F
is the Euclidean norm
F(\xi)=|\xi|
, then (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) reduce to the classical isoperimetric inequality and the Minkowski inequality (
1.1
) respectively.
Inequality (
1.5
) is in fact equivalent to the following Minkowski inequality for mixed volumes

\displaystyle V_{(2)}(\Omega,\mathcal{W}_{F})\geq V_{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{W}_{F})^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}|\mathcal{W}_{F}|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

(1.6)

where
V_{i}(\Omega,\mathcal{W}_{F})
is the mixed volume for two convex bodies
\bar{\Omega}
and
\bar{\mathcal{W}_{F}}
, which is given by

\left|\Omega+t\mathcal{W}_{F}\right|=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}t^{k}V_{(k)}(\Omega,\mathcal{W}_{F}).

Inequality (
1.6
) is a special one for a class of Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for mixed volumes in the theory of convex bodies, see e.g. Schneider’s celebrated book
[
30
]
Section 7.3, or the Introduction in
[
38
]
.

Regarding (
1.5
), the first named author
[
38
]
showed that (
1.5
) holds for star-shaped domains with
F
-mean convex (
H_{F}\geq 0
) boundary, by using the smooth solution to the inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow, in the same spirit of Guan-Li
[
20
]
.
Della Pietra, Gavitone and the first named author
[
15
]
has considered the level set formulation of the inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow, in the spirit of Huisken-Ilmanen
[
23
]
, and proved the existence of the weak solutions by using
L^{p}
-type approximation. However, such approximation seems not sufficient to prove (
1.5
) for outward
F
-minimising sets.

In this paper, we will use the method developed in
[
3
,
6
,
18
]
to study (
1.5
) for non-convex domains. For
1<p<n
, the
anisotropic
p
-capacity
of
\Omega
is defined by

{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)=\inf\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{p}(\nabla u)dx:u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),u\geq 1\hbox{ on }\Omega\Big\}.

We remark that there are early consideration for basic properties of the anisotropic
p
-capacity, see e.g. Maz’ya’s book
[
26
]
, Section 2.2. Also, the Brunn-Minkowski theory has been extended to the anisotropic
p
-capacity
[
4
,
5
]
.

In the following we generalize (
1.2
) to the anisotropic case.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 3)
be a bounded domain with smooth
boundary. Then, for every
1<p<n
, the following inequality holds

\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\Big|\frac{H_{F}}{n-1}\Big|^{p}F(\nu)d\sigma\geq\left[\big(\frac{p-1}{n-p}\big)^{p-1}\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)\right]^{\frac{n-p-1}{n-p}}.

(1.7)

Moreover, equality holds in (
1.7
) if and only if
\Omega
is a Wulff ball.

As a direct consequence (with
p=n-1
), we get the
anisotropic Willmore inequality
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 3)
be a bounded domain with smooth
boundary. Then

\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\Big|\frac{H_{F}}{n-1}\Big|^{n-1}F(\nu)d\sigma\geq\kappa_{n-1}.

(1.8)

Moreover, equality holds in (
1.8
) if and only if
\Omega
is a Wulff ball.

Theorem
1.1
are a special case of Theorem
5.4
. Consider the anisotropic
p
-capacitary potential
u
, which satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta_{F,p}u=&0\ \ {\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\Omega}\\
u=&1\ \ {\rm on}\ \ \partial\Omega\\
u(x)\rightarrow&0\ \ {\rm as}\ \ |x|\rightarrow\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Delta_{F,p}
is the anisotropic
p
-Laplace operator (see Section
2
). It is not hard to see that

{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\Omega}}F^{p}(\nabla u)dx=\int_{\partial{\Omega}}F^{p-1}(\nabla u)F(\nu)d\sigma.

Let
1<p<n
and
q\geq 1+\frac{1}{p^{*}}
, where
p^{*}:=\frac{(n-1)(p-1)}{n-p}
. Let
\Phi_{p,q}:[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\Phi_{p,q}(\tau):=\tau^{(q-1)p^{*}}\int\limits_{\{u=1/\tau\}}F^{q(p-1)}(\nabla u)F(\nu)d\sigma.

The asymptotic behavior of
\Phi_{p,q}
corresponds to
{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)
and the key ingredient to prove Theorem
5.4
is that
\Phi_{p,q}
is an (essentially) monotone non-increasing function. See Sections
4
and
5
.

Next by letting
p\to 1
in (
1.7
), we get the following extended anisotropic Minkowski Inequality.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 3)
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary, then

\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\Big|\frac{H_{F}}{n-1}\Big|F(\nu)d\sigma\geq\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset U\\
\partial U\hbox{smooth}\end{subarray}}|\partial U|_{F}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

(1.9)

Moreover, equality in (
1.9
) is achieved by Wulff balls.

As consequences, we are able to deduce a sharp anisotropic Minkowski inequality for outward
F
-minimising sets (see Definition
6.2
) and a sharp volumetric anisotropic Minkowski inequality.

Corollary 1.1
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 3)
is a bounded outward
F
-minimising set with smooth boundary, then

\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\frac{H_{F}}{n-1}F(\nu)d\sigma\geq\left(\frac{|\partial\Omega|_{F}}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

(1.10)

Moreover, equality in (
1.10
) is achieved by Wulff balls. Conversely, if the equality holds in (
1.10
) for some
bounded strictly outward
F
-minimising open set with smooth and strictly
F
-mean-convex boundary,
then
\Omega
is a Wulff ball.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 3)
be a bounded set with smooth boundary. Then

\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\Big|\frac{H_{F}}{n-1}\Big|F(\nu)d\sigma\geq\left(\frac{n|\Omega|}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}.

(1.11)

Moreover, equality holds in (
1.11
) if and only if
\Omega
is a Wulff ball.

To end the introduction, we make some remark on the
strictly outward
F
-minimising hull
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary.
It is well-known that the infimum

\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset U\\
\partial U\hbox{smooth}\end{subarray}}|\partial U|_{F}

may not be achieved in general. In the setting of set of finite perimeter, the infimum

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset E\\
\ E\hbox{ set of perimeter}\end{subarray}}P_{F}(E)

(1.12)

can be achieved, by the direct method in Calculus of Variations. Here

P_{F}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E}F(\nu_{E})d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

is the
anisotropic perimeter
of
E
,
where
\partial^{*}E
is the reduced boundary of
E
and
\nu_{E}
is the outward measure theoretical unit normal to
E
, see e.g.
[
25
, Chapter 20]
.
There is a so-called strictly outward
F
-minimising hull corresponding to
\Omega
which attains the infimum, see
[
3
,
17
,
23
]
. Similarly as in
[
17
, Definition 2.12]
, the strictly outward
F
-minimising hull of
\Omega
denoted by
\Omega^{*}
, can be reformulated as
the measure theoretic interior Int
(E)
of any set
E
that solves the minimisation problem

|E|=\inf\limits_{G\in{\rm SOFMBE(\Omega)}}|G|

where

{\rm SOFMBE}(\Omega)=\{G\,|\,\Omega\subseteq G\ \hbox{ and }\ G\hbox{ is bounded and strictly outward}\ F-{\rm minimising}\}

is the Strictly Outward
F
-Minimizing Bounded Envelopes of
\Omega
.
It turns out that
\Omega^{*}
is the unique maximal volume solution to the least anisotropic perimeter problem with obstacle
\Omega
, up to zero-measure modification, see
[
17
, Theorem 2.16]
(In our case, the assumption on the existence of an exhaustion of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by a sequence of strictly outward
F
-minimising sets is automatically satisfied. In fact, such exhaustion is given by the Wulff balls). In particular,
\Omega^{*}
attains the infimum in (
1.12
), namely,

P_{F}(\Omega^{*})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset E\\
\ E\hbox{ set of perimeter}\end{subarray}}P_{F}(E).

It is direct to see that

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset U\\
\partial U\hbox{smooth}\end{subarray}}|\partial U|_{F}\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset E\\
\ E\hbox{ set of perimeter}\end{subarray}}P_{F}(E)=P_{F}(\Omega^{*}).

Thus it follows from (
1.9
) that it holds

\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\Big|\frac{H_{F}}{n-1}\Big|F(\nu)d\sigma\geq\left(\frac{P_{F}(\Omega^{*})}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

(1.13)

Remark 1.1
.

In
[
3
]
, the isotropic version of (
1.13
) has been shown. In their argument, a regularity result in
[
31
]
and an exterior smooth approximation in
[
29
]
for the strictly outward minimising hull
\Omega^{*}
have to play a role. In this paper, by showing a stronger inequality (
1.9
), we can avoid of using the regularity and an exterior smooth approximation for the anisotropic case, which seems unavailable in the reference.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review the basic concepts in anisotropic setting, including the anisotropic mean curvature for level sets and the anisotropic
p
-laplace operator. In particular, we prove a Kato-type identity for anisotropic
p
-harmonic functions.
In Section
3
we make a systematic study of anisotropic
p
-capacitary potential, focusing on its asymptotic behavior. In Sections
4
and
5
, we establish the essential and effective monotonicity properties for the crucial function
\Phi_{p,q}(\tau)
, and in turn, prove the
L^{p}
anisotropic Minkowski inequality, Theorem
1.1
. In Section
6
, we study the outward
F
-minimising sets, the limit of
{\rm Cap}_{F,p}
as
p\to 1^{+}
and finally prove Theorem
1.3
and Corollaries
1.1
and
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Minkowski norm, Wulff shape, anisotropic area

Let
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be a
Minkowski norm
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in the sense that

(i)

F
is a norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
F
is a convex, even,
1
-homogeneous function satisfying
F(x)>0
when
x\neq 0
and
F(0)=0
;

(ii)

F
satisfies a uniformly elliptic condition:
\nabla^{2}(\frac{1}{2}F^{2})
is positive definite in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

The
dual norm

F^{o}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,+\infty[

of
F
is defined as

F^{o}(x)=\sup_{\xi\neq 0}\frac{\langle\xi,x\rangle}{F(\xi)}.

F^{o}
is also a Minkowski norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Furthermore,

F(\xi)=\sup_{x\neq 0}\frac{\langle\xi,x\rangle}{F^{o}(x)}.

We remark that, throughout this paper, we use conventionally
\xi
as the variable for
F
and
x
as the variable for
F^{o}
.

Denote

\mathcal{W}_{F}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon F^{o}(x)<1\}.

For the simplicity of notations, we will denote by
\mathcal{W}_{F}=\mathcal{W}
.
We call
\mathcal{W}
the
unit Wulff ball
centered at the origin, and
\partial\mathcal{W}
the
Wulff shape
.

More generally, we denote

\mathcal{W}_{r}(x_{0})=r\mathcal{W}+x_{0},

and call it the
Wulff ball of radius
r
centered at
x_{0}
. We simply denote
\mathcal{W}_{r}=\mathcal{W}_{r}(0)
.

The following properties of
F
and
F^{o}
hold true and will be frequently used in this paper (see e.g.
[
15
,
37
]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a Minkowski norm. Then for any
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, the
following holds:

(1)

\langle F_{\xi}(\xi),\xi\rangle=F(\xi),\quad\langle F^{o}_{x}(x),x\rangle=F^{o}(x).

(2)

\sum_{j}F_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}(\xi)\xi_{j}=0
for any
i=1,\ldots,n
.

(3)

F(F_{x}^{o}(x))=F^{o}(F_{\xi}(\xi))=1.

(4)

F^{o}(x)F_{\xi}(F_{x}^{o}(x))=x,\quad F(\xi)F_{x}^{o}\left(F_{\xi}(\xi)\right)=\xi.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
\nu
be its unit outward normal of
\partial\Omega
. The
anisotropic area

|\partial\Omega|_{F}
of
\Omega
is defined by

|\partial\Omega|_{F}=\int_{\partial\Omega}F(\nu)d\sigma.

(2.1)

Note that when
\Omega=\mathcal{W}
, the unit Wulff ball, one can check by the divergence theorem that

|\partial\mathcal{W}|_{F}=\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}}\frac{1}{|\nabla F^{o}|}d\sigma=\int_{\mathcal{W}}{\rm div}(x)dx=n|\mathcal{W}|.

(2.2)

For notation simplicity, we denote

\kappa_{n-1}=|\partial\mathcal{W}|_{F}=n|\mathcal{W}|.

2.2.
Anisotropic
p
-Laplacian

Let
u
be twice continuous differentiable at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We denote by
F_{i},F_{ij},\ldots
the partial derivatives of
F
and by
u_{i},u_{ij},\ldots
the partial derivatives of
u
,

F_{i}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial\xi_{i}},\ F_{ij}=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\xi_{j}},\ u_{i}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}},\ u_{ij}=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}.

For
x
such that
\nabla u(x)\neq 0
, denote

\displaystyle a_{ij}(\nabla u)(x):=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\xi_{j}}(\frac{1}{2}F^{2})(\nabla u(x))=(F_{i}F_{j}+FF_{ij})(\nabla u(x)),

(2.3)

\displaystyle a_{ij,p}(\nabla u)(x):=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\xi_{j}}(\frac{1}{p}F^{p})(\nabla u(x))=F^{p-2}(a_{ij}+(p-2)F_{i}F_{j})(\nabla u(x)).

The
anisotropic Laplacian and
p
-Laplacian
of
u
at regular points (
|\nabla u|\neq 0
) is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{F}u:=

\displaystyle a_{ij}(\nabla u)u_{ij},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Delta_{F,p}u:=

\displaystyle a_{ij,p}(\nabla u)u_{ij}=F^{p-2}(\Delta_{F}u+(p-2)F_{i}F_{j}u_{ij}),

See e.g.
[
10
,
34
]
.
For notation simplicity, we also introduce

V(\xi)=\frac{1}{p}F^{p}(\xi).

and

\displaystyle W_{ij}=\partial_{x_{j}}\left(\partial_{\xi_{i}}V(\nabla u)\right)=a_{ik,p}(\nabla u)u_{kj}.

(2.5)

One sees easily that

{\rm tr}(W)=\Delta_{F,p}u.

Let
1\leq k\leq n
, be an integer. For a
n
-vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
, the
k
-th elementary symmetric function on
\lambda
is defined by

\sigma_{k}(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}.

Given a real matrix
B=(b_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, we denote by
S_{k}(B)
the sum of all
the principal minors of
B
of order
k
. In particular,
S_{1}(B)={\rm tr}(B)
, the trace of
B
, and

S_{n}(B)={\rm det}(B)
, the determinant of
B
.
When the eigenvalues
\lambda_{B}
of
B
are all real, it is clear that

S_{k}(B)=\sigma_{k}(\lambda_{B}).

For more properties on
S_{k}(B)
, we refer to
[
16
,
28
]
.

For our purpose, we consider
k=2
. By setting

S^{ij}_{2}(B)={\rm tr}B\,\delta_{ij}-b_{ji},

then

S_{2}(B)=\frac{1}{2}S^{ij}_{2}(B)b_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\big(({\rm tr}B)^{2}-{\rm tr}B^{2}\big).

(2.6)

Of particular interest for our purpose is
S_{2}(W)
, where
W
is given by (
2.5
).

Since
{\rm tr}W=\Delta_{F,p}u
, it holds

S^{ij}_{2}(W)=\Delta_{F,p}u\,\delta_{ij}-a_{jk,p}(\nabla u)u_{ki}.

(2.7)

Moreover, in this setting
S^{ij}_{2}(W)
is divergence free, see e.g.
[
8
,
10
,
16
]

\partial_{x_{j}}S^{ij}_{2}(W)=0.

(2.8)

It follows from (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) that if
\Delta_{F,p}u=0
, then

\partial_{x_{j}}(S^{ij}_{2}(W)V_{\xi_{i}}(\nabla u))=2S_{2}(W)=-a_{ik,p}a_{jl,p}u_{kj}u_{il}.

(2.9)

2.3.
Anisotropic curvature for level sets

We recall the concept of anisotropic curvature for a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. See e.g.
[
34
,
38
]
.

Let
M
be a smooth embedded hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\nu
be one unit
normal of
M
. The corresponding anisotropic normal of
M
is defined by

\nu_{F}=\nabla F(\nu).

The anisotropic principal curvatures
\kappa_{F}=(\kappa_{1}^{F},\ldots,\kappa_{n-1}^{F})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
are defined as the eigenvalues of the map

d\nu_{F}\colon T_{x}M\to T_{\nu_{F}(x)}\partial\mathcal{W}.

The mean curvature (with respect to
\nu
) is defined to be

H_{F}=\sigma_{1}(\kappa_{F})=\sum_{i}\kappa_{i}^{F}.

The second mean curvature (with respect to
\nu
) is defined to be

\sigma_{2}(\kappa_{F})=\sum_{i<j}\kappa_{i}^{F}\kappa_{j}^{F}.

A well-known variational characterization for
H_{F}
is that it arises from the first variation
of the anisotropic area functional
|\partial\Omega|_{F}
, (see e.g. Proposition 2.1 in
[
38
]
), namely,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}F(\nu)d\sigma=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}H_{F}\langle Y,\nu\rangle d\sigma,

where
Y
is the variation vector filed for
\partial\Omega_{t}
.

In this paper we are interested in the case when
M
is given by a regular level set of a smooth
function
u
, that is
M=\{u=t\}
for some regular value
t
. For our purpose, we choose the unit normal
\nu=-\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
and

\nu_{F}=-F_{\xi}(\nabla u),\ \ H_{F}=-{\rm div}(F_{\xi}(\nabla u)).

In this case, we have that

H_{F}=-{\rm div}(F_{\xi}(\nabla u))=-F_{ij}u_{ij},

(2.10)

Here
\rm div
is the Euclidean divergence. See e.g.
[
16
]
.

From Theorem 2.5 in
[
16
]
, we also have that

\sigma_{2}(\kappa_{F})=S_{2}(F_{ik}u_{kj}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{n-1}S_{1}^{2}(F_{ik}u_{kj})-2S_{2}(F_{ik}u_{kj})=

\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sigma_{1}^{2}(\kappa_{F})-2\sigma_{2}(\kappa_{F}).

(2.11)

We remark that the right hand side of (
2.11
) is exactly the square of traceless part of anisotropic second fundamental form, which is nonnegative.

Next we give two propositions to the connection between an anisotropic
p
-harmonic function
u
and the anisotropic curvatures of regular level sets of
u
. The first one is a formula for the anisotropic mean curvature of regular level set of
u
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
u
satisfy
\Delta_{F,p}u=0
. Then the anisotropic mean curvature of regular level set of
u
is given by

H_{F}=(p-1)F^{-1}F_{i}F_{j}u_{ij}.

(2.12)

Proof.

Note that
\Delta_{F,p}u=0
implies
FF_{ij}u_{ij}+(p-1)F_{i}F_{j}u_{ij}=0
. The assertion follows from (
2.10
).
∎

The second is a Kato-type identity for anisotropic
p
-harmonic functions, which generalizes Proposition 4.4 in
[
18
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
u
satisfy
\Delta_{F,p}u=0
. Let
\kappa_{F}
denote the anisotropic principal curvature of regular level set of
u
. Then, at any point where
F(\nabla u)\not=0
, the following
identity holds true:

\displaystyle a_{ij}a_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}=

\displaystyle F^{2}(\nabla u)\left[\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sigma_{1}^{2}(\kappa_{F})-2\sigma_{2}(\kappa_{F})\right]

(2.13)

\displaystyle+(1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{n-1})|\nabla(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}

\displaystyle+(1-\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{n-1})|\nabla^{T}(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}},

where

|\nabla(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}:=a_{ij}(F_{k}u_{ki})(F_{l}u_{lj})\geq 0,

(2.14)

and

|\nabla^{T}(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}:=FF_{ij}(F_{k}u_{ki})(F_{l}u_{lj})\geq 0.

(2.15)

Remark 2.1
.

|\cdot|_{a_{F}}
can be viewed as the norm with respect to the positive definite matrix
(a_{ij})
. Also by the definition, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}=|\nabla^{T}(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}+(F_{i}F_{j}u_{ij})^{2}.

(2.16)

Proof.

The proof is inspired by
[
37
]
. Note that
a_{ij}=FF_{ij}+F_{i}F_{j}
, we can separate
\Delta_{F}u=a_{ij}u_{ij}=A+B
where

A=F_{i}F_{j}u_{ij}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ B=FF_{ij}u_{ij}.

Since

a_{ij}a_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}=A^{2}+2FF_{kl}F_{i}F_{j}u_{ik}u_{jl}+F^{2}F_{ij}F_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl},

and

\frac{(A+B)^{2}}{n}+\frac{n}{n-1}\Big(\frac{B}{n}-\frac{n-1}{n}A\Big)^{2}=A^{2}+\frac{1}{n-1}B^{2},

we have

\displaystyle a_{ij}a_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}=

\displaystyle\frac{(A+B)^{2}}{n}+\frac{n}{n-1}\Big(\frac{B}{n}-\frac{n-1}{n}A\Big)^{2}-\frac{1}{n-1}B^{2}

(2.17)

\displaystyle+2FF_{kl}F_{i}F_{j}u_{ik}u_{jl}+F^{2}F_{ij}F_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(a_{ij}u_{ij})^{2}}{n}+\frac{n}{n-1}\Big(\frac{FF_{ij}u_{ij}}{n}-\frac{n-1}{n}F_{i}F_{j}u_{ij}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n-1}(FF_{ij}u_{ij})^{2}+2FF_{kl}F_{i}F_{j}u_{ik}u_{jl}+F^{2}F_{ij}F_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}.

On the on hand, it follows
\Delta_{F,p}u=0
that

a_{ij}u_{ij}=-(p-2)F_{i}F_{j}u_{ij}.

(2.18)

On the other hand, we have the following computations

2FF_{kl}F_{i}F_{j}u_{ik}u_{jl}=2a_{kl}F_{i}F_{j}u_{ik}u_{jl}-2F_{i}F_{j}F_{k}F_{l}u_{ik}u_{jl}.

(2.19)

Substituting (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) into (
2.17
) yields to

\displaystyle a_{ij}a_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{n-1}-1\Big)(F_{i}F_{j}u_{ij})^{2}+2a_{kl}F_{i}F_{j}u_{ik}u_{jl}

(2.20)

\displaystyle+F^{2}F_{ij}F_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}-\frac{1}{n-1}(FF_{ij}u_{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{n-1}-1\Big)(F_{i}F_{j}u_{ij})^{2}+(1-\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{n-1})|\nabla(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}

\displaystyle+F^{2}F_{ij}F_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}-\frac{1}{n-1}(FF_{ij}u_{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{n-1}\right)|\nabla(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{n-1}\right)|\nabla^{T}(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}+\left(1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{n-1}\right)|\nabla(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}

\displaystyle+F^{2}F_{ij}F_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}-\frac{1}{n-1}(FF_{ij}u_{ij})^{2},

where the last equality we have used (
2.16
).
Finally using (
2.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle F_{ij}F_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}-\frac{1}{n-1}(F_{ij}u_{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{n-1}S_{1}^{2}(F_{ik}u_{kj})-2S_{2}(F_{ik}u_{kj})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sigma_{1}^{2}(\kappa_{F})-2\sigma_{2}(\kappa_{F}).

This completed the proof of Proposition
2.3
.
∎

We also have some facts as following

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
u
satisfy
\Delta_{F,p}u=0
. Then, at any point where
F(\nabla u)\not=0
, the following
identity holds true:

\displaystyle F^{2-p}a_{jk,p}F_{i}u_{ik}F_{l}u_{lj}=

\displaystyle|\nabla^{T}F(\nabla u)|^{2}_{a_{F}}+\frac{1}{p-1}H_{F}^{2}F^{2}(\nabla u),

(2.21)

\displaystyle a_{jk,p}a_{il,p}u_{ik}u_{lj}F^{4-2p}=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-1}H_{F}^{2}F^{2}+2(p-1)|\nabla^{T}(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}

(2.22)

\displaystyle+F^{2}(\nabla u)\left[\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sigma_{1}^{2}(\kappa_{F})-2\sigma_{2}(\kappa_{F})\right].

Proof.

From Proposition
2.3
, we have that

\displaystyle a_{ij}a_{kl}u_{ik}u_{jl}=

\displaystyle F^{2}(\nabla u)\left[\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sigma_{1}^{2}(\kappa_{F})-2\sigma_{2}(\kappa_{F})\right]

(2.23)

\displaystyle+\Big(1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{n-1}\Big)\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}F^{2}H_{F}^{2}+2|\nabla^{T}(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}.

By using (

) and (
2.23
), directing computation gives that

\displaystyle a_{jk,p}a_{il,p}u_{ik}u_{lj}F^{4-2p}=

\displaystyle(a_{jk}+(p-2)F_{j}F_{k})(a_{il}+(p-2)F_{i}F_{l})u_{ik}u_{lj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{jk}a_{il}u_{ik}u_{lj}+2(p-2)|\nabla(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}+\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{(p-1)^{2}}H_{F}^{2}F^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-1}H_{F}^{2}F^{2}+2(p-1)|\nabla^{T}(F(\nabla u))|^{2}_{a_{F}}

\displaystyle+F^{2}(\nabla u)\left[\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sigma_{1}^{2}(\kappa_{F})-2\sigma_{2}(\kappa_{F})\right].

This completes the assertion.
∎

3.
Anisotropic
p
-capacitary potential

In this section, we will introduce the anisotropic
p
-capacity and its related properties.

Definiton 3.1

(anisotropic
p
-capacity)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with smooth boundary.
For
p\in[1,n)
, the anisotropic
p
-capacity of
\Omega
is defined as

{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)={\rm inf}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{p}(\nabla u)dx\,\Big|\,u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,u\geq 1\ \ {\rm on}\ \ \Omega\Big\},

(3.1)

It is easy to check that (see e.g.
[
26
]
)

{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)={\rm inf}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{p}(\nabla u)\,dx\,\Big|\,u\geq\chi_{\Omega},\,u\in{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big\}.

(3.2)

Next we consider the case
p\in(1,n)
.
The variational structure of the above definition leads naturally to the formulation of the problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta_{F,p}u=&0\ \ {\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\Omega}\\
u=&1\ \ {\rm on}\ \ \partial\Omega\\
u(x)\rightarrow&0\ \ {\rm as}\ \ |x|\rightarrow\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

we say a function
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(U)
is a
weak solution of
\Delta_{F,p}u=0
in an open set
U
if

\int_{U}\langle F^{p-1}(\nabla u)F_{\xi}(\nabla u),\nabla\psi\rangle dx=0.

for any
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
.
A weak solution satisfying (
3.3
) is called the
anisotropic
p
-capacitary potential
associated with
\Omega
.

We collect the existence and regularity results for the anisotropic
p
-capacity potentials in the following theorem, see e.g.
[
8
,
9
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Existence and regularity of anisotropic
p
-capacity potentials)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
open set with smooth boundary, and let
1<p<n
. Then

(1)

There exists a unique weak solution
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\Omega})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)
to (
3.3
);

(2)

u
is smooth away from
{\rm Crit}(u):=\{\nabla u=0\}
;

(3)

The solution u fullfills

{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\Omega}}F^{p}(\nabla u)dx.

(3.4)

Remark 3.1
.

Note that since
\partial\Omega
is assumed to be smooth, by the Hopf Lemma for
anisotropic
p
-harmonic functions, we have that
\nabla u\not=0
in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
.
In particular,
u
is smooth in
\partial\Omega
. Coupled with this fact, the asymptotic
expansions below imply that
{\rm Crit}(u)=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\Omega}\,|\,Du(x)=0\big\}

is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\Omega}

(generically depending on
p
), and in turn that
u
is analytic outside this set.

For
1<p<n
, let

\Gamma_{F,p}(x)=\frac{p-1}{n-p}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}F^{o}(x)^{\frac{p-n}{p-1}}.

One can check that

\Delta_{F,p}\Gamma_{F,p}(x)=\delta_{0}\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac Delta function about the origin. We call

\Gamma_{F,p}
the fundamental solution to
\Delta_{F,p}u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. See
[
36
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with
smooth boundary, and
1<p<n
. Let
u
is a
weak solution of
\Delta_{F,p}u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. Then
u
satisfies

(1)

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u(x)}{\Gamma_{F,p}(x)}={{\rm Cap}_{F,p}}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

(2)

\nabla u(x)=\,{{\rm Cap}_{F,p}}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\nabla\Gamma_{F,p}(x)+o(|x|^{-\frac{n-1}{p-1}}),\;{\rm as}\;|x|\to+\infty.

Proof.

If
u
is solution of (
3.3
), it is a standard argument by using comparison theorem to show that there exists two positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

C_{1}\Gamma_{F,p}\leq u\leq C_{2}\Gamma_{F,p}.

Following the argument of
[
24
]
, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.5, (see
[
36
]
, Theorem 4.1 and Remark 4.1 for anisotropic case), we conclude that there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u(x)}{\Gamma_{F,p}(x)}=\gamma,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}(F^{o}(x))^{\frac{n-1}{p-1}}\left(\nabla u-\gamma\nabla\Gamma_{F,p}\right)=0.

(3.6)

It remains to show

\gamma={\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Through the following integration by parts that
holds true by the anisotropic
p
-harmonicity of
u
:

\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}F^{p-1}(\nabla u)F(\nu)d\sigma=-\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int\limits_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}}F^{p-1}(\nabla u)\langle F_{\xi}(\nabla u),\nu_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}}\rangle d\sigma,

(3.7)

where
\nu=-\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
on
\partial\Omega
and
\nu_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}}
is the outward unit normal to
\mathcal{W}_{R}
,
we shall see in Proposition
3.2
below that the left-hand side of (
3.7
) is
exactly
{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)
.
We next compute the limit in the right-hand side of (
3.7
).
Since

\nabla u=\gamma\nabla\Gamma_{F,p}+o(|x|^{-\frac{n-1}{p-1}}),

we have

F(\nabla u)=\gamma F(\nabla\Gamma_{F,p})+o(|x|^{-\frac{n-1}{p-1}})=\gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}F^{o}(x)^{-\frac{n-1}{p-1}}+o(|x|^{-\frac{n-1}{p-1}})

On
\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}
,

\nu_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}}=\frac{\nabla F^{o}}{|\nabla F^{o}|}=-\kappa_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}F^{o}(x)^{\frac{n-1}{p-1}}\frac{\nabla\Gamma_{F,p}}{|\nabla F^{o}|}=-\kappa_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}F^{o}(x)^{\frac{n-1}{p-1}}\frac{\gamma^{-1}\nabla u}{|\nabla F^{o}|}+o(1).

It follows that on
\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}

\displaystyle\langle F_{\xi}(\nabla u),\nu_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\kappa_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}F^{o}(x)^{\frac{n-1}{p-1}}\frac{\gamma^{-1}F(\nabla u)}{|\nabla F^{o}|}+o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{|\nabla F^{o}|}+o(1).

Hence

\displaystyle F^{p-1}(\nabla u)\langle F_{\xi}(\nabla u),\nu_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}}\rangle=-\gamma^{p-1}\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\frac{(F^{o}(x))^{1-n}}{|\nabla F^{o}|}+o(|x|^{1-n}).

Combining the fact that

\int\limits_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}}\frac{(F^{o}(x))^{1-n}}{|\nabla F^{o}|}d\sigma=\kappa_{n-1},

we deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int\limits_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}}F^{p-1}(\nabla u)\langle F_{\xi}(\nabla u),\nu_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{R}}\rangle d\sigma=-\gamma^{p-1}.

Thus
\gamma={\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
, and

(1),(2)
of Proposition
3.1
follows.
∎

We need the following expression for
{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)
in terms of an integral on
\partial\Omega
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with smooth boundary, and let
1<p<n
.
Then, the solution
u
to (
3.3
) satisfies

{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)=\int_{\partial{\Omega}}F^{p-1}(\nabla u)F(\nu)d\sigma.

(3.8)

where
\nu=-\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
on
\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
U_{\varepsilon}
be the
\varepsilon
-tubular neighborhood of Crit
(u)
, namely

U_{\varepsilon}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\Omega}\,\big|\,{\rm dist}(x,{\rm Crit(u)})<\varepsilon\Big\},

where dist
(x,{\rm Crit}(u))
is the distance of
x
from
{\rm Crit}(u)
. By the compactness of Crit
(u)

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\Omega}
, we have that
U_{\varepsilon}\subset\{u\geq t\}
, for
\varepsilon>0
and
t>0
small enough. Since
F(\nabla u)=0
on
Crit
(u)
, we have from identity (
3.4
) and by Monotone Convergence Theorem that

{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int\limits_{\{u\geq t\}\setminus U_{\varepsilon}}F^{p}(\nabla u)dx.

Then, for
\varepsilon
and
t
small enough, by intergration by parts,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\{u\geq t\}\setminus U_{\varepsilon}}F^{p}(\nabla u)dx=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\{u\geq t\}\setminus U_{\varepsilon}}{\rm div}(uF^{p-1}(\nabla u)F_{\xi}(\nabla u))dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t\int\limits_{\{u=t\}}\frac{F^{p}(\nabla u)}{|\nabla u|}d\sigma+\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}F^{p-1}(\nabla u)F(\nu)d\sigma

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\partial U_{\varepsilon}}uF^{p-1}(\nabla u)\langle F_{\xi}(\nabla u),\nu_{\partial U_{\varepsilon}}\rangle d\sigma.

By the computation in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we see
\int\limits_{\{u=t\}}\frac{F^{p}(\nabla u)}{|\nabla u|}d\sigma
is bounded when

, which implies

-t\int\limits_{\{u=t\}}\frac{F^{p}(\nabla u)}{|\nabla u|}d\sigma\to 0\hbox{ as }t\to 0.

On the other hand, we have

\int\limits_{\partial U_{\varepsilon}}uF^{p-1}(\nabla u)\langle F_{\xi}(\nabla u),\nu_{\partial U_{\varepsilon}}\rangle d\sigma\to 0\hbox{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

since
F(\nabla u)
tends to zero as
x
approaches Crit
(u)
. The assertion follows.
∎

4.
Essential monotonicity

In this section, we assume
u
is anisotropic
p
-harmonic, that is,
\Delta_{F,p}u=0
. We will establish some Lemmas for later using.

Let

p^{*}:=\frac{(n-1)(p-1)}{n-p}.

\Lambda=\Big\{(p,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\Big|\,1<p<n,\ {\rm and}\ q\geq 1+\frac{1}{p^{*}}\Big\}.

For
(p,q)\in\Lambda

\Phi_{p,q}:[1,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

\Phi_{p,q}(\tau):=\tau^{(q-1)p^{*}}\int\limits_{\{u=1/\tau\}}F^{q(p-1)}(\nabla u)F(\nu)d\sigma.

(4.1)

By the asymptotic behavior given in Lemma
3.1
, it is direct to check the limits of
\Phi_{p,q}(\tau)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow+\infty}\Phi_{p,q}(\tau)=\big(\frac{n-p}{p-1}\big)^{(q-1)p^{*}}\big(\kappa_{n-1}\big)^{\frac{(q-1)(p-1)}{n-p}}({\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega))^{1-\frac{(q-1)(p-1)}{n-p}}.

Proof.

From the asymptotic behavior of
u

3.1
, we know that
For
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\tau_{0}

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\gamma\Gamma_{F,p}\leq u(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\gamma\Gamma_{F,p},

From this one deduces that on such

\displaystyle(1-\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma^{-\frac{(q-1)(p-1)^{2}}{n-p}}\big(\frac{n-p}{p-1}\big)^{(q-1)p^{*}}\big(\kappa_{n-1}\big)^{\frac{(q-1)(p-1)}{n-p}}\tau^{-(q-1)p^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq F^{(q-1)(p-1)}(\nabla u(x))

\displaystyle\leq(1+\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma^{-\frac{(q-1)(p-1)^{2}}{n-p}}\big(\frac{n-p}{p-1}\big)^{(q-1)p^{*}}\big(\kappa_{n-1}\big)^{\frac{(q-1)(p-1)}{n-p}}\tau^{-(q-1)p^{*}}.

where

Arguing as in Proposition

, we see that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\{u=1/\tau\}}F^{p-1}F(\nu)d\sigma={\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega).

Since

\tau^{(q-1)p^{*}}\int\limits_{\{u=1/\tau\}}F^{q(p-1)}F(\nu)d\sigma=\tau^{(q-1)p^{*}}\int\limits_{\{u=1/\tau\}}F^{(q-1)(p-1)}(F^{p-1}F(\nu)d\sigma

It follows that

\displaystyle(1-\tilde{\varepsilon})\big(\frac{n-p}{p-1}\big)^{(q-1)p^{*}}\big(\kappa_{n-1}\big)^{\frac{(q-1)(p-1)}{n-p}}({\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega))^{1-\frac{(q-1)(p-1)}{n-p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau^{(q-1)p^{*}}\int\limits_{\{u=1/\tau\}}F^{q(p-1)}F(\nu)d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\tilde{\varepsilon})\big(\frac{n-p}{p-1}\big)^{(q-1)p^{*}}\big(\kappa_{n-1}\big)^{\frac{(q-1)(p-1)}{n-p}}({\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega))^{1-\frac{(q-1)(p-1)}{n-p}}.

The assertion follows by letting
\tau\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

For
(p,q)\in\Lambda
. Let us consider the vector field
X
with component

\displaystyle X^{j}=

\displaystyle-(q-1)u^{-{(q-1)p^{*}}+2}F^{q(p-1)-1}\left(a_{jk,p}F^{1-p}F_{i}u_{ik}-{p^{*}}\frac{FF_{j}}{u}\right)

(4.3)

.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded set of finite perimeter. We say that
E
is outward
F
-minimising if

\displaystyle P_{F}(E)\leq P_{F}(G)\hbox{ for }G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\hbox{ bounded s.f.p. with }E\subset G.

(6.1)

We say that
E
is strictly outward
F
-minimising if it is outward
F
-minimising and equality in (
6.1
) holds only if
|G\setminus E|=0
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an outward
F
-minimising set with smooth boundary. Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset U\\
\partial U\hbox{smooth}\end{subarray}}|\partial U|_{F}=|\partial\Omega|_{F}.

(6.2)

Proof.

It follows directly by the definition.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an outward
F
-minimising set with smooth boundary. Then
H_{F}\geq 0
.

Proof.

It follows from (
6.2
) and the first variational formula of
|\partial U|_{F}
.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with smooth boundary. Then

\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)={\rm Cap}_{F,1}(\Omega)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset U\\
\partial U\hbox{smooth}\end{subarray}}|\partial U|_{F}.

(6.3)

Proof.

The second equality can be found in
[
26
]
, Lemma 2.2.5. See also
[
2
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we provide a proof here.

Step 1.

{\rm Cap}_{F,1}(\Omega)\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset U\\
\partial U\hbox{smooth}\end{subarray}}|\partial U|_{F}.

(6.4)

By co-area formula, for any
u\in{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
u\geq\chi_{\Omega}

we have that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(\nabla u)dx\geq\int^{1}_{0}|\partial\{u>t\}|_{F}dt\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset U\\
\partial U\hbox{smooth}\end{subarray}}|\partial U|_{F}.

In particular, taking the infimum over any such

u
, we get (
6.4
).

Step 2.

{\rm Cap}_{F,1}(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega).

(6.5)

For every
u\in{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
u\geq\chi_{\Omega}
and any
q>0
, by the definition of
{\rm Cap}_{F,1}(\Omega)
and the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Cap}_{F,1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(\nabla u^{q})dx

\displaystyle\leq q\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{\frac{(q-1)p}{p-1}}dx\Big)^{(p-1)/p}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{p}(\nabla u)dx\Big)^{1/p}.

(6.6)

Choose
q=\frac{p(n-1)}{n-p}>1
, then
\frac{(q-1)p}{p-1}=\frac{np}{n-p}

which is the Sobolev critical exponent of
p
.
The anisotropic Sobolev inequality with best constant
[
1
]
says that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq\left(\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}T_{n,p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{p}(\nabla u)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(6.7)

where
T_{n,p}
is the best constant in the classical Sobolev inequality, due to Talenti
[
32
]
.

It follows from (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) that

\displaystyle{\rm Cap}_{F,1}(\Omega)\leq\frac{p(n-1)}{n-p}\left(\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{n-p}}T_{n,p}^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n-p}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{p}(\nabla u)dx\Big)^{\frac{n-1}{n-p}},

(6.8)

Taking the infimum over any
u\in{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

u\geq\chi_{\Omega}
in (
6.8
), we obtain

{\rm Cap}_{F,1}(\Omega)\leq\frac{p(n-1)}{n-p}\left(\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{n-p}}T_{n,p}^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n-p}}{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{n-p}}.

(6.9)

Passing to the limit in (
6.9
) as
p\to 1^{+}
, since
\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}T_{n,p}={n}^{\frac{1}{n}-1}\omega_{n-1}^{-\frac{1}{n}}
, we conclude (
6.5
).

Step 3.

\limsup_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset U\\
\partial U\hbox{smooth}\end{subarray}}|\partial U|_{F}.

(6.10)

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any open and bounded set with smooth boundary such that
\Omega\subset U
. Let
d_{U}
be the distance function
d_{U}(x)={\rm dist}(x,U)
. It is well-known that
d_{U}
is smooth in a neighborhood of
U
. Introduce a smooth cut-off function

\chi_{\varepsilon}
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} \chi_{\varepsilon}&=1\ \ {\rm in}\ t<\varepsilon,\\
-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}<&\chi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)<0\ \ {\rm in}\ \varepsilon\leq t\leq 2\varepsilon,\\
\chi_{\varepsilon}&=0\ \ {\rm in}\ t>2\varepsilon,\end{aligned}\right.

and set
\eta_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{\varepsilon}(d_{U})
. Choosing
\varepsilon
small enough so that
\eta_{\varepsilon}
is smooth. Hence

{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{p}(\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon})dx

for any
p\geq 1
. Letting
p\rightarrow 1^{+}
, by using the co-area formula and the mean value theorem, we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon})dx=\int^{2\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon}|\chi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)|\int_{\{d_{U}=t\}}F(\nu)d\sigma dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{d_{U}=r_{\varepsilon}\}}F(\nu)d\sigma,

for some
r_{\varepsilon}\in(\varepsilon,2\varepsilon)
. Let
r_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0^{+}
,
we conclude that

\limsup_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}{\rm Cap}_{F,p}(\Omega)\leq|\partial U|_{F}

for any bounded set
U
with smooth boundary such that
\Omega\subset U
. (
6.10
) follows.

Step 4.
The assertion (
6.3
) follows from (
6.4
), (
6.5
) and (
6.10
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
. The inequality (
1.9
) follows from Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
6.3
by letting
p\to 1^{+}
.

When
\Omega
is a Wulff ball, then in view of (
6.13
), we see that

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset U\\
\partial U\hbox{smooth}\end{subarray}}|\partial U|_{F}=|\partial\Omega|_{F}.

Hence the equality in (
1.9
) holds.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.1
. The first assertion follows directly from Theorem
1.3
, Propositions
6.1
and
6.2
.

Next we consider the equality case in (
1.10
) for some strictly outward
F
-minimising set
\Omega

with smooth and strictly
F
-mean-convex boundary.
Let
\{\partial\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T)}
be the evolution
of
\partial\Omega
under smooth inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow, for some
T>0
. By
[
15
]
, we know that the weak inverse anisotropic mean curvature flow starting
at
\partial\Omega
coincides with the smooth flow
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T^{*})}
for some
0<T^{*}\leq T
.
In view of Proposition 3.4 of
[
15
]
(see also Lemma 2.4 of
[
23
]
),

\Omega_{t}
is strictly outward
F
-minimising and strictly
F
-mean-convex for every
t\in[0,T^{*})

. Then (
1.10
) holds for every
\partial\Omega_{t}
with
t\in[0,T^{*})
. For
t\in[0,T^{*})
, we can define
monotonic quantity as following

\Psi(t)=|\partial\Omega_{t}|_{F}^{-\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}H_{F}F(\nu)d\sigma.

by using Proposition 2.1 of
[
38
]
,

\Psi^{\prime}(0)=-|\partial\Omega|_{F}^{-\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{H_{F}}\left|h_{F}-\frac{H_{F}}{n-1}g_{F}\right|^{2}d\sigma\leq 0.

(6.11)

Here
h_{F}-\frac{H_{F}}{n-1}g_{F}
is the traceless anisotropic second fundamental form.
If the strict inequality
\Psi^{\prime}(0)<0
holds, it follows that

\Psi(t)<\Psi(0)=(n-1)\kappa_{n-1}^{1/(n-1)}\hbox{ for some }t\in(0,T^{*}),

which contradicts (
1.10
) for some outward anisotropic minimising
\Omega_{t}
with strictly anisotropic mean-convex boundary. Therefor
\Psi^{\prime}(0)=0
. It follows from (
6.11
) that
\partial\Omega
is totally anisotropic umbilical and in turn,
\partial\Omega
is of a Wulff shape.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
. For any
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
bounded with smooth boundary and
\Omega\subset U
, by the Wulff inequality (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\partial U|_{F}}{\kappa_{n-1}}\geq\left(\frac{n|U|}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\geq\left(\frac{n|\Omega|}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

(6.12)

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subset U\\
\partial U\hbox{smooth}\end{subarray}}|\partial U|_{F}\geq\left(\frac{n|\Omega|}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

(6.13)

The inequality follows from Theorem
1.3
and (
6.13
). The equality classification for (
1.11
) follows from that of the Wulff inequality.
∎

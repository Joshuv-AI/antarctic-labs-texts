Lagrangian Skeleta and Koszul Duality on Bionic Symplectic Varieties
Gwyn Bellamy, Christopher Dodd, Kevin McGerty, Thomas Nevins

Abstract
. We consider the category of modules over sheaves of Deformation-Quantization (DQ) algebras on bionic symplectic varieties. These spaces are equipped with both an elliptic 𝔾 m {\mathbb{G}}_{m} -action and a Hamiltonian 𝔾 m {\mathbb{G}}_{m} -action, with finitely many fixed points. On these spaces one can consider geometric category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} : the category of (holonomic) modules supported on the Lagrangian attracting set of the Hamiltonian action. We show that there exists a local generator in geometric category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} whose dg endomorphism ring, cohomologically supported on the Lagrangian attracting set, is derived equivalent to the category of all DQ-modules. This is a version of Koszul duality generalizing the equivalence between 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -modules on a smooth variety and dg-modules over the de Rham complex.



1.
Introduction

The Riemann-Hilbert correspondence provides a fundamental link between the algebra of systems of differential equations and the topology of complex
algebraic varieties. Its extension to an equivalence between categories of algebraic
{\mathcal{D}}
-modules on a smooth quasiprojective variety
X
and dg modules over the de Rham complex
\Omega_{X}
in
[
Kap
]
represents a deep example of Koszul duality for noncommutative quadratic algebras.
The present paper extends the duality between
{\mathcal{D}}
-modules and
\Omega
-modules to a more flexible symplectic context, and deduces a locality result—“sheafification over the Lagrangian skeleton”—that expresses a fundamental Morse-theoretic property of the category of modules over a deformation quantization.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a smooth, complex algebraic variety with algebraic symplectic form
\omega
. Such varieties that appear in geometric representation theory typically come equipped with an action of the multiplicative group
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
of invertible complex numbers that rescales the symplectic form with positive weight:

t^{*}\omega=t^{\ell}\omega
for some
\ell>0
. We call such an action
elliptic
when the limit
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\cdot x
exists for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
. Typical examples of symplectic varieties with elliptic
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action include cotangent bundles of flag varieties and more general projective spherical varieties, and Nakajima quiver varieties.

Many symplectic varieties that arise in nature can be equipped not only with an elliptic
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action but also a compatible
Hamiltonian

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action, i.e. one preserving the symplectic form. When the two actions have the same, finite, set of fixed points, we call the variety
\mathfrak{X}

bionic symplectic
: a precise definition appears in Section
2.2
. Standard examples are, again, cotangent bundles of projective spherical varieties, and Hilbert schemes of points on minimal resolutions of cyclic Kleinian singularities (among other quiver varieties).
Such a variety comes equipped with a canonical Lagrangian “skeleton”
\Lambda^{c}
.

Suppose now that a symplectic variety
\mathfrak{X}
comes equipped with a deformation quantization
{\mathcal{W}}
compatible with an elliptic
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action.
Taking
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules
equivariant for the elliptic
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action, one arrives at a category useful for geometric representation theory. When the action is the scaling action on fibers of
\mathfrak{X}=T^{*}X
, this category reproduces a category of twisted
{\mathcal{D}}
-modules on
X
; more generally such categories are closely related (depending on the geometry of
\mathfrak{X}
) to representations of Cherednik algebras, finite
W
-algebras, and more. When the variety is bionic symplectic and the deformation quantization is compatible with both
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-actions, there is a good geometric analogue of category
\mathcal{O}

[
BLPW1
,
BLPW2
,
Lo1
,
Web
]
inside the category of
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules, consisting of modules supported on the skeleton
\Lambda^{c}
.

The present paper establishes two basic features of the category of
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules on a bionic symplectic variety. We build a sheaf of proper dg algebras
\Omega
that plays the role for
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules analogous to that played by the de Rham complex for
{\mathcal{D}}_{X}
-modules, i.e.
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules on
\mathfrak{X}=T^{*}X
. The sheaf
\Omega
is supported (cohomologically) on a Lagrangian
\Lambda\supset\Lambda^{c}
in
\mathfrak{X}
; while for general bionic symplectic varieties we do not have an explicit description of
\Lambda
, in the case where
\mathfrak{X}
is a symplectic resolution (which is the case in essentially all examples of import to geometric representation theory), we have
\Lambda=\Lambda^{c}
(c.f.
1.4
below).

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that locally free resolutions exist for coherent
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules. Then the bounded derived category of coherent
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules is equivalent to the derived category of coherent dg
\Omega
-modules:

D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}D(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}).

As in
[
BDMN
]
, we define the category of ”quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules”
{\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}
to be the ind-category of good
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules. We show that Theorem
1.1
extends to the derived category
D({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})
, though not in the obvious way. We introduce the ”exotic” derived category
D_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
as a certain quotient of
K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
, admitting a quotient functor
D_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})\to D(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
to the usual derived category of dg
\Omega
-modules.

Theorem 1.2
.

The category
D(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
is a full (triangulated) subcategory of
D_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
such that the equivalence of Theorem
1.1
extends to an equivalence

D({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}D_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}).

In fact, we first establish the equivalence of Theorem
1.2
and then show that this restricts to the equivalence given in Theorem
1.1
.

Since any good
{\mathcal{W}}
-module locally admits a resolution by free
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules, we derive, as a formal consequence, the following “sheafification over the Lagrangian skeleton”:

Theorem 1.3
.

The bounded derived category of coherent
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules on
\mathfrak{X}
is the category of global sections of a sheaf of dg categories over
\Lambda
.

Beyond
[
Kap
]
, the paper draws inspiration from the structure of Fukaya categories of Weinstein manifolds in real symplectic geometry
as layed out by
Kontsevich
[
Ko
]
, explored in important works by several authors (including, among others, Abouzaid, Seidel, Soibelman, Tamarkin, Tsygan, Zaslow)
and given flesh in a categorical context in work of
Nadler
[
Nad
]
. Links between Fukaya categories and deformation quantizations form an established theme in the field. Theorems

1.1
and, especially,
1.3
may be viewed as furthering those connections.

1.1.
Symplectic resolutions

In the case where
\mathfrak{X}
is a symplectic resolution of its affinization
X:=\mathfrak{X}^{\mathrm{aff}}
, it was shown in
[
BLPW2
]
that, possibly after shifting by a quantized line bundle, we may assume localization holds. This implies that bounded locally free resolutions exist for coherent
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules. Therefore, the assumptions of Theorem

are satisfied. Moreover, in this situation we have greater control over the cohomological support of
\Omega
. Namely,

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{X}\to X
is a symplectic resolution. Then the sheaf of dg algebras
\Omega
is cohomologically supported on the canonical Lagrangian skeleton
\Lambda^{c}
.

The key to Theorem
1.4
is the fact that one can choose a local generator
\mathcal{L}
(whose dg endomorphism ring is
\Omega
) supported on
\Lambda^{c}
; see Proposition
3.16
.

C

C

C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\mathfrak{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookleftarrow}}U.

Let

[

]
, we show that:

Theorem 1.5
.

There is a functor
\mathbb{R}i^{!}\colon D^{b}_{\mathrm{Hol}}({\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X}})\to D^{b}_{\mathrm{Hol},C}({\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X}})

\mathbb{R}j_{*}

\mathbb{R}j_{*}\colon D^{b}_{\mathrm{Hol}}({\mathcal{W}}_{U})\to D^{b}_{\mathrm{Hol}}({\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X}})

If

.

2.
Bionic Symplectic Varieties

In this section we introduce a class of elliptic symplectic varieties which come naturally equipped with a Lagrangian skeleton, and for which we will be able to show the existence of a local generator in the sense of Definition
4.7
.

2.1.
Lagrangian Skeletons

We begin by recalling from
[
BDMN
]
the notion of an elliptic symplectic variety.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{X},\omega)
be a smooth symplectic variety equipped with an action
\lambda\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
of the multiplicative group. We say that
\mathfrak{X}
is
elliptic symplectic
if

i)

The symplectic form
\omega
has weight
\ell>0
,
i.e.

\lambda(t)^{*}(\omega)=t^{\ell}\omega
, (
t\in\mathbb{G}_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
),

ii)

For each
x\in\mathfrak{X}
the limit
\lim_{t\to\infty}\lambda(t).x
exists.

Given an elliptic symplectic variety, the fixed point locus
Y
of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action can be written as a union of connected components
Y=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}Y_{i}
and this induces a partition
\mathfrak{X}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}C_{i}
of
X
, where
C_{i}=\{x\in X:\lim_{t\to\infty}\lambda(t).x\in Y_{i}\}
. We call the
C_{i}
the
coisotropic cells
of the partition. As in Lemma 2.4 of
[
BDMN
]
, the collection of coisotropic cells are partially ordered by the relation defined by
C_{i}\geq C_{j}
if
\overline{C}_{j}\cap C_{i}\neq\emptyset
. Moreover, for any choice of refinement of this order (to a partial or total order), and any index
i
, the subsets
K_{\geq i}=\bigsqcup_{j\geq i}C_{j}
and
K_{>i}=\bigsqcup_{j>i}C_{j}
are closed coisotropic subsets of
\mathfrak{X}
.

The following is one of the main results of
[
BDMN
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{X},\omega)
be an elliptic symplectic variety with associated partition
\mathfrak{X}=\bigsqcup C_{i}
. Then each
C_{i}
is a smooth locally-closed
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant coisotropic subvariety of
X
with a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant coisotropic reduction
(S_{i},\omega_{i})
. That is, there is an equivariant morphism
\pi_{i}\colon C_{i}\to S_{i}
where
S_{i}
is an elliptic symplectic variety, and
\pi_{i}^{*}(\omega_{i})=\omega_{|C_{i}}
.

One of the goals of the present paper is to understand to what extent the category of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant modules for a quantization
\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{X}}
of
\mathfrak{X}
can be described in terms of objects supported on Lagrangians in
\mathfrak{X}
. The basic notion we use for this is that of a Lagrangian skeleton.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{X},\omega)
be an elliptic symplectic variety, and
\Lambda
a closed (possibly singular) Lagrangian subvariety of
\mathfrak{X}
. We say that
\Lambda
is a
Lagrangian skeleton
for
\mathfrak{X}
if

i)

\Lambda
is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant.

ii)

For each
i\in I
,
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda\cap C_{i}
is a nonempty smooth closed subvariety of
C_{i}
.

We will refer to
\Lambda_{i}
as a
Lagrangian cell
. Using the partial ordering of the coisotropic cells (and any refinement of it) we obtain closed Lagrangian subvarieties
\Lambda_{\geq i}=\bigsqcup_{j\geq i}\Lambda_{j}
and
\Lambda_{>i}=\bigsqcup_{j>i}\Lambda_{j}
. Note that under our assumptions the closures of the
\Lambda_{i}
are the irreducible components of
\Lambda
.
The next lemma shows that a Lagrangian skeleton is compatible with the collection of symplectic reductions
\pi_{i}\colon C_{i}\to S_{i}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
(\mathfrak{X},\omega)
is elliptic symplectic, and
\Lambda
is a closed
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant Lagrangian subvariety such that
\Lambda\cap C_{i}=\Lambda_{i}
is smooth, then
\pi_{i}(\Lambda_{i})
is a smooth Lagrangian in
S_{i}
and
\Lambda_{i}=\pi_{i}^{-1}(\pi_{i}({\Lambda}_{i}))
.

Proof.

We must check that
\Lambda_{i}=\pi_{i}^{-1}(\pi_{i}(\Lambda_{i}))
, and that
\pi_{i}(\Lambda_{i})
is Lagrangian in
S_{i}
. This can be checked locally on
S_{i}
, and indeed since it is shown in
[
BDMN
, §2]
that
\pi_{i}
factors the projection map
p_{i}\colon C_{i}\to Y_{i}
, it is enough to check this locally on
Y_{i}
. We thus fix
y\in Y_{i}
. Now
p_{i}
is an affine bundle over
Y_{i}
, and by
[
BB
]
we may equivariantly trivialize it near
y
, so that we may assume
C_{i}=Y_{i}\times V
where
V
is a linear
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-representation with strictly negative weight. If
U<V
denotes the subspace of vectors with weight
k
where
-\ell\leq k<0
and
W
denotes the subspace of
V
consisting of vectors with weights strictly less than
-\ell
, then it is shown in Lemma 2.5 of
[
BDMN
]
that in this trivialization we have
S_{i}=Y_{i}\times U
, where
\pi_{i}
is the projection along
W
.

Now consider
\Lambda_{i}
: since
\Lambda_{i}
is closed in
C_{i}
we may assume
y\in\Lambda_{i}
. Then we may identify
T_{y}C_{i}=T_{y}Y_{i}\times U\times W
, and since
\Lambda_{i}
is Lagrangian we must have
W\subseteq T_{y}\Lambda_{i}
. But then it follows that (locally)
\pi_{i}(\Lambda_{i})
is
(Y\cap\Lambda_{i})\times(U\cap\Lambda_{i})
which is Lagrangian in
S_{i}
, and
\Lambda_{i}=\pi_{i}^{-1}(\pi_{i}(\Lambda_{i}))
.
∎

2.2.
Better…Stronger…Faster

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a smooth, connected, complex algebraic symplectic variety with symplectic form
\omega
. We say that
\mathfrak{X}
is a
conic (or conical) symplectic variety
if
\mathfrak{X}
comes equipped with a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action satisfying:

(C1)

the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action is elliptic.

Now, suppose that
\mathfrak{X}
comes equipped with a

\mathsf{T}={\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{2}
action, such that:

(L1)

There is a choice of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\rightarrow\mathsf{T}
that satisfies (C1) above.

(L2)

There is a different choice of
\mathsf{H}\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\rightarrow\mathsf{T}
that is Hamiltonian: i.e., it preserves the symplectic form.

(L3)

Both actions have the same, finite, set
{\mathcal{S}}
of fixed points.

Definition 2.5
.

We will call a variety with
\mathsf{T}={\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{2}
-action satisfying (C1), (L1), (L2), (L3) above a
bionic symplectic variety
.

Remark 2.6
.

When one has a bionic symplectic variety, there is a bit of freedom in the choice of the torus
\mathsf{T}
. More precisely, this definition really only requires the action of the product
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
of the tori giving the elliptic and Hamiltonian actions. This torus will be isogenous to
\mathsf{T}
, but may not be equal to it; for our purposes replacing
\mathsf{T}
by this torus would be completely harmless. However, the natural torus actions occurring in the examples below often use a
\mathsf{T}
which is not simply the product of the tori giving the elliptic and Hamiltonian actions, so it seemed sensible to allow this extra flexibility in the definition.

We call the action in (L1) the
elliptic
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action
, and the action in (L2) the
Hamiltonian
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action
. Equipping
\mathfrak{X}
with only the elliptic action it clearly becomes an elliptic symplectic variety, and hence has a decomposition
X=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}C_{i}
into coisotropic pieces. However, since the elliptic action has isolated fixed points, each
C_{i}
is in this case an affine space
\mathbb{A}^{d_{i}}
, and as is shown
[
BDMN
, §2.6]
, in this case the symplectic reductions
S_{i}
are symplectic vector spaces equipped with an elliptic linear
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action. Indeed, since the constructions of
[
BDMN
]
can be carried out equivariantly, the
C_{i}
are in fact
\mathsf{T}
-invariant and the
S_{i}
are
\mathsf{T}
-representations.

2.3.
The Lagrangian
\Lambda^{c}

Suppose that
\mathfrak{X}
is bionic symplectic. Let
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}(s)\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
denote the Hamiltonian
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action. We define the
canonical Lagrangian skeleton

\Lambda^{c}
of
\mathfrak{X}
to be:

(2.1)

\Lambda^{c}=\Big\{x\in\mathfrak{X}\,\Big|\,\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\mathsf{H}(s)\cdot x\,\,\text{exists in $\mathfrak{X}$}\Big\}.

Lemma 2.7
.

The subset
\Lambda^{c}
is a Lagrangian subset of
\mathfrak{X}
.

For each
i
, let
\Lambda^{c}_{i}=C_{i}\cap\Lambda^{c}
, where
C_{i}
is a coisotropic cell as in Section
2.1
.

Proposition 2.8
.

(1)

Under the isomorphism
C_{i}\cong\mathbb{A}^{d_{i}}
given above, each
\Lambda^{c}_{i}
is a vector subspace of the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-representation
C_{i}
.

(2)

Moreover, the projection
\pi_{i}(\Lambda^{c}_{i})\subset S_{i}
is a

\mathsf{T}
-stable Lagrangian subspace of the symplectic vector space
S_{i}
, and
\Lambda^{c}_{i}=\pi^{-1}\big(\pi(\Lambda^{c}_{i})\big)
.

The next corollary is immediate:

Corollary 2.9
.

The variety
\Lambda^{c}
is a Lagrangian skeleton of
\mathfrak{X}
.

Example 2.10
.

An illustrative example is provided by the minimal resolution of the Kleinian singularity of type
A
; this example is discussed further in
2.5
and
3.17
below.

Let
Y_{n}
denote the Kleinian singularity of type
A_{n}
and let
X_{n}
be its minimal resolution, a smooth symplectic surface. (The symplectic form is induced from volume form on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
). There is a natural two-torus
\mathsf{T}
acting on
X_{n}
, and it has
n
fixed points
\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}\}
all lying in the exceptional fibre of the resolution: indeed if the exceptional locus is
D_{1}\cup D_{2}\ldots\cup D_{n-1}
where each
D_{i}
is a projective line then
D_{i}\cap D_{i+1}=\{p_{i+1}\}
for
1\leq i\leq n-2
and the other fixed points
p_{1}
and
p_{n}
lie on
D_{1}\backslash\{p_{1}\}
and
D_{n-1}\backslash\{p_{n-1}\}
respectively.

Picking a suitable generic one-parameter subgroup, one obtains an elliptic
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action, which has
n
coisotropic affine cells: the fixed point
p_{n}
attracts a two-dimensional affine space
C_{n}=\mathbb{A}^{2}
, while each of the others has attracting locus isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{1}
: for
p_{i}
where
i>1
this is the affine line
C_{i}=D_{i}\backslash\{p_{i-1}\}
, while for
p_{0}
the affine line
C_{0}
lies outside the exceptional locus, and is tangent to
C_{1}
at
p_{0}
.

The Hamiltonian
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action
\mathsf{H}\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathsf{T}
is induced from a Hamiltonian action on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, and the locus of points
\Lambda^{c}
which have a limit under it is the union of the exceptional locus and an affine line tangent to
C_{n-1}
at
p_{n}
. On the open cell, one of course already has a symplectic variety, so it is its own reduction, while on each of the other cells, the reduction is just a point.

We list below standard examples of bionic symplectic varieties.

2.4.
Cotangent Bundles of Spherical Varieties

Let
G
be a connected reductive group and
B
a Borel subgroup.

Lemma 2.11
.

If
X
is a complete
B
-spherical variety, then
T^{*}X
has a
\mathsf{T}
-action making it bionic symplectic.

Proof.

We may write
X
as the disjoint union
X=\bigsqcup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{O}_{s}
of its
B
-orbits, where
|\mathcal{S}|<\infty
. To show that
T^{*}X
has the structure of a bionic symplectic variety, it suffices to exhibit a
1
-parameter subgroup
\gamma\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to B
such that
\gamma(\mathbb{G}_{m})
has finitely many fixed points on
\mathcal{O}_{s}
for each
s\in\mathcal{S}
. Indeed assuming such a
\gamma
exists, it induces a Hamiltonian action on
T^{*}X
, and by modifying this action by a sufficiently high power of the scaling action on the fibres of
T^{*}X
we may ensure that the resulting action on
T^{*}X
is fibrewise convergent.

Fix a maximal torus
T
of
B
and, for each
s\in\mathcal{S}
, let
K_{s}
be the stabilizer of a point in
\mathcal{O}_{s}
chosen so that
T_{s}=K_{s}\cap T
is a maximal torus of
K_{s}
(this is possible since the maximal tori in
B
are all conjugate). If
\gamma\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to T
then

bK_{s}\in(B/K_{s})^{\gamma(\mathbb{G}_{m})}\iff\forall t\in\mathbb{G}_{m},\gamma(t).bK_{s}=bK_{s},\iff b^{-1}\gamma b\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to K_{s}.

Let
r\colon B\to B/U
be the quotient of
B
by its unipotent radical, and let
\mathcal{S}^{\text{max}}=\{s\in\mathcal{S}:\text{rank}(K_{s})=\text{rank}(B)\}
. If
s\notin\mathcal{S}^{\text{max}}
, then

is a proper subtorus of

and, since

is abelian, for any

and

generic,

and hence any such

. On the other hand, if

, then

, so that
\mathcal{O}_{s}
is a

-orbit, and hence is an affine space. But

acts with positive weights on

, and hence a generic

-parameter subgroup

contracts

to

and hence

to

. Thus for

suitably generic we see that

will have exactly

fixed points on

.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

If
X
is either a partial flag manifold or a toric variety then
T^{*}X
is bionic symplectic.

Throughout, we follow the exposition and conventions of

A sheaf of

\mathcal{A}

is a sheaf of

-algebras on

called the

-algebra associated to

. If

is any

-module (or sheaf) then

will denote
{\mathbb{C}}[\hbar^{\pm 1}]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[\hbar]}M=M[{\hbar}^{-1}]

We assume throughout that our algebra
{\mathcal{W}}
carries an action of
\mathsf{T}
. Since the symplectic form

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action and is invariant with respect to

with respect to

. Therefore,

with respect to
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. After making this replacement, the Poisson bracket on

\{\overline{a},\overline{b}\}=\frac{1}{\hbar^{\ell}}[a,b]\ \mathrm{mod}\ \hbar\mathcal{A}.

because the bracket

is now a multiple of

for all

Assumption 3.1
.

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant

-modules are always defined with respect to the elliptic

We will occasionally refer explicitly to

Coherent modules over

3.2.

By Gabber’s theorem, the support of any good

. The category of holonomic

-modules is denoted

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a holonomic
{\mathcal{W}}
-module. Then
\mathscr{M}
has finite length.

This paper is concerned mainly with those
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant holonomic objects which are supported on
\Lambda^{c}
. Consider an open subset
U=\mathfrak{X}\smallsetminus K_{>i}
for a closed union
K_{>i}
of coisotropic cells. Then
C_{i}
is closed in
U
and
\Lambda^{c}_{i}
is closed in
C_{i}
. Recall that
\overline{\Lambda^{c}}_{i}=\pi_{i}(\Lambda^{c}_{i})
where
\pi_{i}:C_{i}\rightarrow S_{i}
is the coisotropic reduction. In
[
BDMN
]
it is shown that if
\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{X}}
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant quantization of
\mathfrak{X}
then each

has a natural
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant quantization
\mathcal{W}_{S_{i}}
and there is a reduction functor
\mathbb{H}_{i}
giving an equivalence

\mathbb{H}_{i}\colon{\mathcal{W}}_{U}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}}_{C_{i}}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}{\mathcal{W}}_{S_{i}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}}

between the category of good modules supported on
C_{i}
and the category of good modules for
\mathcal{W}_{S_{i}}
. The analogous statement holds in the dg context as well.

Proposition 3.5
.

(1)

There is a simple
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant holonomic object
\delta_{\Lambda^{c}_{i}}
of
{\mathcal{W}}_{U}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}}
supported on
\Lambda^{c}_{i}
.

(2)

Such an object
\delta_{\Lambda^{c}_{i}}
is unique up to twisting by a character of the Hamiltonian
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action.

(3)

every
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant holonomic object of
{\mathcal{W}}_{U}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}}
supported on
\Lambda^{c}_{i}
is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of twists (for the Hamiltonian
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action) of copies of
\delta_{\Lambda^{c}_{i}}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{S_{i}}
denote the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant
{\mathcal{W}}
-algebra on
S_{i}
.
By Theorem 1.5 of
[
BDMN
]
, checking that each statement of the proposition holds on
\Lambda^{c}_{i}\subset U
is equivalent to checking the corresponding statement on
\overline{\Lambda^{c}}_{i}\subset S_{i}
. By
[
BDMN
, Corollary 1.6]
, the category of good
{\mathcal{W}}_{S_{i}}
-modules is equivalent to the category of coherent
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{A}}^{d_{i}})
-modules where

. The additional equivariance with respect to the Hamiltonian action on
\mathfrak{X}
translates to monodromicity of
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{A}}^{d_{i}})
-modules. Hence the claim is that there is a unique simple monodromic
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{A}}^{d_{i}})
-module supported (microlocally) on the “attracting locus” in
T^{*}V
where
V
is a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-representation with positive weights. The attracting locus is the zero section
V\subset T^{*}V
and we need to show that there is a unique simple
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-monodromic local system on a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-representation with positive weights.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{A}^{d_{i}})
denote the Weyl algebra equipped with the filtration
F_{\bullet}
induced by the elliptic action on
\mathcal{W}_{S_{i}}
. The Hamiltonian action
\mathsf{H}
induces a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\mathcal{D}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}_{n}
where
\deg x_{i}>0
and
\deg\partial_{i}=-\deg x_{i}
. Note that
\mathcal{D}_{0}={\mathbb{C}}\langle x_{i}\partial_{i}\rangle
is a commutative subalgebra. Since the elliptic and Hamiltonian actions commute, the filtration and grading are compatible. Moreover, since
F_{k}\mathcal{D}
is finite dimensional, so is
F_{k}\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{D}_{n}
for every
n
. Suppose that
M
is a simple monodromic
\mathcal{D}
-module supported on
V
. Let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
be the grading on
M
given by the Hamiltonian
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action. Since
M
is supported on
V
, we claim that
M_{n}=0
for all

sufficiently negative. To see this, note that since the associated graded of
M
(with respect to the filtration

) is supported on
V
, the generators of
\text{gr}_{F}\mathcal{D}
with negative grading must act nilpotently. Since
\mathrm{gr}_{F}M
is finitely generated, the result follows.

Let
M_{l}\neq 0
and
M_{k}=0
for all

. Since
\partial_{i}
has negative degree,
\partial_{i}m=0
for all

. Thus, choosing a non-zero

defines a non-zero morphism

of
\mathcal{D}
-modules. Since
M
is simple, this is an isomorphism.

∎

3.3.
Duality and Hamiltonian Reduction

In this subsection we will briefly discuss the duality functor on
quantizations and its application to the construction of the (partially
defined) functor
i^{*}
. We will concern ourselves only with a local
situation, as that is all that we need for this paper. In particular,
following the notation of
[
BDMN
]
, Section 4, we assume
\mathfrak{X}=\text{Spec}(R)
is a smooth affine
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant symplectic variety, and
I
is the ideal in
R
generated by the homogeneous elements of negative degree. The closed subvariety
C=\text{Spec}(R/I)
is co-isotropic, and the symplectic variety
S=\mathrm{Spec}(R/I)^{\{I,\cdot\}}
is obtained via Hamiltonian reduction as in Theorem
2.2
. We let
\mathfrak{C}
denote the formal
completion of
C
in
\mathfrak{X}
. There is a (non-canonical)
equivariant isomorphism

\mathfrak{C}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}S\times\widehat{T^{*}V}

where
V
is a vector space and
\widehat{T^{*}V}
is the completion
of
T^{*}V
along
V
. Under this isomorphism, we have
C{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}S\times V
.

If
Q
is an algebra then
Q\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}
denotes the category of left
Q
-modules and
Q\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
the category of finitely generated left
Q
-modules. We identify the category of right
Q
-modules with
Q^{\mathrm{opp}}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}
.

If
A
denotes our given quantization of
R
, then let
J
be
the left ideal generated by homogeneous elements of negative degree.
Let
B
denote the Hamiltonian reduction algebra

B=\text{End}(A/J)^{\mathrm{opp}}.

Then we have:

Proposition 3.6
.

The ideal
J
is generated by a graded vector space
\mathfrak{u}
of dimension

\text{dim}(V)
. Let
\widehat{A}
denote the completion of
A

along
J
. Then there is an equivariant isomorphism of algebras

\widehat{A}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}B\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{\widehat{D}}

where
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
denotes the completion of the Weyl
algebra
\mathcal{D}
of
V
along the left ideal generated by
\mathfrak{u}
.
Under this isomorphism,
\widehat{J}
corresponds to
B\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{\widehat{D}}\mathfrak{u}

and
\widehat{A}/\widehat{J}
corresponds to
B\widehat{\otimes}(\mathcal{\widehat{D}}/\widehat{\mathcal{D}}\mathfrak{u})
.

Proof.

First, we note that in
[
BDMN
, Section 4]
, the algebra
\widehat{A}
was defined to be the completion of
A
along the two-sided ideal
K
, which is the preimage of
I
under the quotient
A\to A/\hbar A\cong R
. But this is isomorphic, as topological algebras, to
\widehat{A}
(as defined in the statement of the proposition) by
[
BDMN
, Lemma 4.15(1)]
. Then the first statement is Lemma 4.10 of
[
BDMN
]
, as is the identification
\widehat{J}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}B\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{\widehat{D}}\mathfrak{u}
. The second statement is Proposition 4.9 of
[
BDMN
]
. Finally, the proof of
[
BDMN
, Proposition 4.12]
explains that
\widehat{A}/\widehat{J}
is mapped isomorphically to
B\widehat{\otimes}(\mathcal{\widehat{D}}/\widehat{\mathcal{D}}\mathfrak{u})
.
∎

We require a better understanding of the relation between
\widehat{A}
-modules
and
A
-modules. Certainly, there is a flat morphism of algebras

A\to\widehat{A}
, and the corresponding functor
M\to\widehat{A}\otimes_{A}M
,
which agrees with the naive completion when
M
is finite over
A
.
In certain cases we can can actually recover
M
from its completion.
To state the relevant result, recall the adjoint pair of functors

\mathbb{H}\colon A\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}\to B\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}
and
\mathbb{H}^{\perp}\colon B\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}\to A\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}

given by

\mathbb{H}(M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(A/J,M)

and

\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(N)=A/J\otimes_{B}N.

We have also the adjoint pair of functors

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}(M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{A}}(\widehat{A}/\widehat{J},M)

from
\widehat{A}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}
to
B\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}
and

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{\perp}(N)=\widehat{A}\otimes_{A}(A/J\otimes_{B}N)=\widehat{A}/\widehat{J}\otimes_{B}N

from
B\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}
to
\widehat{A}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}
. We use the same
letters to denote the corresponding functors for
B_{\hbar}
-modules,
resp.
A_{\hbar}
and
\widehat{A}_{\hbar}
-modules.

As explained in
[
BDMN
, Proposition 4.14, Lemma 4.17]
, we have isomorphisms

(3.3)

\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{A}_{\hbar}}(\widehat{A}/\widehat{J}_{\hbar},\widehat{A}/\widehat{J}_{\hbar}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(A/J_{\hbar},A/J_{\hbar})\cong B^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\hbar}.

From this we deduce:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
N
be a finite
B_{\hbar}
-module, and let
M=\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(N)
.
Then there is a canonical isomorphism

N{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\widehat{\mathbb{H}}\circ\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{\perp}(N)

and therefore a canonical isomorphism

M{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{H}^{\perp}\circ\widehat{\mathbb{H}}\circ\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{\perp}(N).

In particular, if
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are two finite
A_{\hbar}
-modules,
both of which are in the image of
\mathbb{H}^{\perp}
, then
\widehat{M}_{1}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\widehat{M}_{2}

implies
M_{1}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}M_{2}
.

Proof.

By
[
BDMN
, Theorem 4.21]
, there is an isomorphism
N{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{H}\circ\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(N)=\mathbb{H}(M)
.
So we only have to show that the canonical map
M\to\widehat{M}

induces

\mathbb{H}(M){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\widehat{\mathbb{H}}(\widehat{M}).

As
M=A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar}\otimes_{B_{\hbar}}N
for a finite
B_{\hbar}
-module
N
, one reduces immediately by adjunction to showing

\mathbb{H}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\widehat{\mathbb{H}}(\widehat{A}_{\hbar}/\widehat{J}_{\hbar}),

but this follows directly from equation (
3.3
).
∎

Remark 3.8
.

By
[
BDMN
, Theorem 4.21]
, the image of
B_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
under
\mathbb{H}^{\perp}
is the full subcategory of
A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
supported on
C
. Moreover,
\mathbb{H}^{\perp}
is exact and hence extends to a functor between the derived categories associated to these abelian categories.

With this in hand, let us turn to duality functors. To save ourselves
from writing the same definition many times, let
Q
denote any of

A
,
\widehat{A}
, or
B
. As these are
algebras of finite homological dimension, we may define

\mathbb{D}(M)=\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{Q_{\hbar}}(M,Q_{\hbar})[d]

as a contravariant functor
D_{f.g.}^{b}(Q_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})\to D_{f.g.}^{b}(Q^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
; where
2d=\dim\mathfrak{X}
if
Q=A
or
2d=\dim S

if
Q=B
. Replacing
Q
by
Q^{\text{opp }}
, we obtain
a functor
D_{f.g.}^{b}(Q^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})\to D_{f.g.}^{b}(Q_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
,
also denoted by
\mathbb{D}
. Both of these functors are equivalences
of categories, and we have
\mathbb{D}\circ\mathbb{D}=\text{Id}
.
Note that
A^{\mathrm{opp}}
is a quantization of
\mathfrak{X}
(but with negative symplectic form) and
B^{\mathrm{opp}}

is a quantization of
S
; so the above discussion, and the construction
of
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{H}^{\perp}
, applies verbatim to the
these algebras as well.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
N
be a finite
B_{\hbar}
-module. Then there is an isomorphism

\mathbb{D}\circ\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(N){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{H}^{\perp}\circ\mathbb{D}(N)

in
D^{b}(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
.

Proof.

We shall first show the isomorphism

(3.4)

\mathbb{D}(A/J_{\hbar}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar},A_{\hbar}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}(J^{\prime}\backslash A)_{\hbar}[-d]

where
J^{\prime}
is the right ideal of
A
generated by
\mathfrak{u}
,
and
d=\text{dim}(V)
. To show this isomorphism, we first tackle
the completed version

(3.5)

\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{A}_{\hbar}}(\widehat{A}_{\hbar}/\widehat{J}_{\hbar},\widehat{A}_{\hbar}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}(\widehat{J^{\prime}}\backslash\widehat{A})_{\hbar}[-d]

For this we recall that under the isomorphism
\widehat{A}\cong B\widehat{\otimes}\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
,

\widehat{A}/\widehat{J}
corresponds to
B\widehat{\otimes}(\mathcal{\widehat{D}}/\widehat{\mathcal{D}}\mathfrak{u})
.
As in the proof of
[
BDMN
, Proposition 4.14]
, we can consider the Koszul complex
B\widehat{\otimes}K(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},\mathfrak{u})
which is a finite free resolution of
\widehat{A}/\widehat{J}
. We have

H^{i}(\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{A}_{\hbar}}(\widehat{A}/\widehat{J}_{\hbar},\widehat{A}_{\hbar})){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}H^{i}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\widehat{A}_{\hbar}}(B_{\hbar}\widehat{\otimes}K(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},\mathfrak{u})_{\hbar},\widehat{A}_{\hbar}))

{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}H^{i}(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{u}^{*}\otimes\widehat{A}_{\hbar}),

where we have used adjunction in the last step. The complex of right
modules so obtained is then the Koszul resolution for
(\widehat{J^{\prime}}\backslash\widehat{A})_{\hbar}
,
placed in degree
d
; which implies the above isomorphism.

Now, to obtain the uncompleted version, we note that, as the duality
functor commutes with localization, the complex
\mathbb{D}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar})

is supported along
C
. Therefore, by
[
BDMN
, Theorem 4.21]
, each

H^{i}(\mathbb{D}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar}))
is in the image of
\mathbb{H}^{\perp}
.
Therefore we may apply the previous proposition (for the algebra
A^{\mathrm{opp}}
)
to deduce that, since

H^{d}(\mathbb{D}(\widehat{A}_{\hbar}/\widehat{J}_{\hbar})){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}(\widehat{J^{\prime}}\backslash\widehat{A})_{\hbar}

and

H^{i}(\mathbb{D}(\widehat{A}_{\hbar}/\widehat{J}_{\hbar}))=0

for all
i\neq d
, we must also have

H^{d}(\mathbb{D}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar})){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}(J^{\prime}\backslash A)_{\hbar}

and
H^{i}(\mathbb{D}(A/J_{\hbar}))=0
for all
i\neq d
as claimed.

Now, we compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\circ\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(N)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar}\otimes_{B_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}N,A_{\hbar})[\dim(\mathfrak{X})/2]

\displaystyle{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{\hbar}}(N,\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar},A_{\hbar}))[\dim(\mathfrak{X})/2]

\displaystyle{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{\hbar}}(N,J^{\prime}_{\hbar}\backslash A_{\hbar})[\dim(\mathfrak{X})/2-d]

\displaystyle{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{\hbar}}(N,B_{\hbar})[\dim(S)/2]\otimes_{B_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}J_{\hbar}^{\prime}\backslash A_{\hbar}=\mathbb{H}^{\perp}\circ\mathbb{D}(N)

as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

There is an isomorphism of functors

\mathbb{D}\circ\mathbb{H}^{\perp}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{H}^{\perp}\circ\mathbb{D}\colon D^{b}_{f.g.}(B_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})\to D^{b}_{f.g.}(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}).

Now we can define the desired functor
\mathbb{H}^{*}
. Recall (see
[
KS1
, Chapter 6]
) that we have the category
\text{Pro}(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})

the category of pro-objects on
A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
,
and its derived category
D(\text{Pro}(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}))
. If
P
is any projective object in
A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}
, then, writing
P
as an inductive limit of finite
A_{\hbar}
-modules,
the module
\operatorname{Hom}(P,A_{\hbar})
is naturally an element of
\text{Pro}(A_{\hbar}^{\mathrm{opp}}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
. Therefore
the duality
\mathbb{D}
extends to a contravariant functor

\mathbb{D}\colon D(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})\to D(\text{Pro}(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}))

which is easily seen to be an equivalence of categories; indeed, its inverse is given by the cocontinuous extension of
\mathbb{D}\colon D(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})\to D(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})

to
D(\text{Pro}(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}))
; which we will
also denote by
\mathbb{D}
. Similarly, the functor
\mathbb{H}^{\perp}

admits a cocontinious extension to a functor
\mathbb{H}^{\perp}:D(\text{Pro}(B_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}))\to D(\text{Pro}(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}))
. Then we have:

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
N\in D(\mathrm{Pro}(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}))
. Then we
define

\mathbb{H}^{*}(N):=\mathbb{D}\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{D}N)\in D(\mathrm{Pro}(B_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}))

We have a functorial isomorphism

\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(N,\mathbb{H}^{\perp}M){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{B_{\hbar}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}N,M)

for all
N\in D(\mathrm{Pro}(A_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}))
and

M\in D(\mathrm{Pro}(B_{\hbar}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}))
.

Proof.

Let
N=\mathbb{D}N^{\prime}
and
M=\mathbb{D}M^{\prime}
. Then

\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(N,\mathbb{H}^{\perp}M){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(\mathbb{D}N^{\prime},\mathbb{H}^{\perp}\mathbb{D}M^{\prime})

{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(\mathbb{D}N^{\prime},\mathbb{D}\mathbb{H}^{\perp}M^{\prime}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\hbar}}(\mathbb{H}^{\perp}M^{\prime},N^{\prime})

{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{B^{opp}_{\hbar}}(M^{\prime},\mathbb{H}N^{\prime}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{\hbar}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}N,M)

as claimed.
∎

3.4.
Preservation of Holonomicity

Let
U\subset U^{\prime}
be open subsets of
\mathfrak{X}
which are unions of coisotropic cells and write
Z=U^{\prime}\setminus U
. We form the usual diagram
Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\hookrightarrow}}U^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookleftarrow}}U
, as in (
3.1
). Denote by
D_{\mathrm{Hol}}^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})
the derived category of quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules with holonomic cohomology. The following key result is crucial in proving our main result that there exists a holonomic local generator in
D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
. The proof is given in the next subsection.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\mathscr{M}\in D_{\mathrm{Hol}}^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{U}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})
. Then
j_{*}\mathscr{M}\in D_{\mathrm{Hol}}^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{U^{\prime}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})
.
Equivalently, if
\mathscr{N}\in D_{\mathrm{Hol}}^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{U^{\prime}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})
,
then
\mathbb{R}i^{!}\mathscr{N}\in D_{\mathrm{Hol}}^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{Z}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})
.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.12
, we may define

\displaystyle j_{!}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\circ\mathbb{R}j_{*}\circ\mathbb{D}\colon D_{\mathrm{Hol}}^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{U}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})\to D_{\mathrm{Hol}}^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{U^{\prime}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}),

\displaystyle i^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\circ\mathbb{R}i^{!}\circ\mathbb{D}\colon D_{\mathrm{Hol}}^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{U^{\prime}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})\to D_{\mathrm{Hol},Z}^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{U^{\prime}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}).

and get a recollment pattern

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}_{\mathrm{Hol},Z}({\mathcal{W}}_{U^{\prime}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}_{\mathrm{Hol}}({\mathcal{W}}_{U^{\prime}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}_{\mathrm{Hol}}({\mathcal{W}}_{U}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}i^{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}j_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{!}}

This means that
i^{*}
is left adjoint (resp.
\mathbb{R}i^{!}
right adjoint) to
i_{*}
and
j_{!}
left adjoint (resp.
\mathbb{R}j_{*}
right adjoint) to
j^{*}
. Moreover,
j^{*}\circ i_{*}=0,i^{*}\circ j^{!}=0
and
\mathbb{R}i^{!}\circ\mathbb{R}j_{*}=0
. These functors give rise to the usual distinguished triangles

i_{!}\circ\mathbb{R}i^{!}(\mathscr{M})\to\mathscr{M}\to\mathbb{R}j_{*}\circ j^{*}(\mathscr{M})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[1]}}{{\rightarrow}}

i_{*}\circ i^{*}(\mathscr{M})\to\mathscr{M}\to j_{!}\circ j^{!}(\mathscr{M})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[1]}}{{\rightarrow}},

noting that
i_{*}=i_{!}
and
j^{!}=j^{*}
. Finally, the functors
i_{*},\mathbb{R}j_{*}
and
j_{!}
are fully faithful.

3.5.
Proof of Theorem
3.12

The fact that the two statements of the theorem are equivalent follows from the triangle (
3.2
):

i_{*}\mathbb{R}i^{!}\mathscr{N}\to\mathscr{N}\to j_{*}(\mathscr{N}|_{U}),

as well as the fact that every holonomic module in
{\mathcal{W}}_{U}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}

admits a holonomic extension to
{\mathcal{W}}_{U^{\prime}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}
by
[
BDMN
, Corollary 3.33]
.

To proceed, note that by induction on the number of cells, we may
suppose that
Z=U^{\prime}\setminus U
is a single cell, called
C
. We shall
prove the second statement in this context. As the statement is local,
we can suppose we are in the affine situation, and
\Gamma(\mathfrak{X},{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X}})=A_{\hbar}
.
Let
\widehat{A}_{\hbar}
denote the completion of
A_{\hbar}
along
the ideal
J
(notation as in Section
3.3
). By Proposition
3.6
, we have the isomorphism

\widehat{A}=\widehat{\mathcal{D}}\widehat{\otimes}B

where
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
is the standard quantization of
\widehat{T^{*}V}

and
B
is the quantization of
S
, the coisotropic reduction of

C
. It is explained in
[
BDMN
, Section 2.6]
that the fact that
S
has a single fixed point for the elliptic
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action implies that the algebra
B_{\hbar}
is isomorphic to the
\hbar
-Weyl algebra
\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}
, of rank
m=(1/2)\dim S
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
M
be a good
A_{\hbar}
-module. Then

\mathbb{R}i^{!}M[n]\cong J_{\hbar}^{\prime}\backslash A_{\hbar}\otimes_{A_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}M\cong\widehat{J}_{\hbar}^{\prime}\backslash\widehat{A}_{\hbar}\otimes_{\widehat{A}_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}\widehat{M},

where
n=\dim V
and
\widehat{M}=\widehat{A}_{\hbar}\otimes_{A_{\hbar}}M
.

Proof.

Recall that
i^{!}M=\mathbb{H}\circ\Gamma_{C}(M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar},\Gamma_{C}(M))
, where
\Gamma_{C}(M)
is the largest submodule of
M
supported on
C
. If
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar},M)
then
\mathrm{Im}\,\phi
is a quotient of
A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar}
and hence supported on
C
. Thus,
\mathrm{Im}\,\phi\subset\Gamma_{C}(M)
meaning that
i^{!}M=\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar},M)
.

Passing to derived functors,

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}i^{!}M

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar},M)

\displaystyle{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\hbar}}(A_{\hbar}/J_{\hbar},A_{\hbar})\otimes_{A_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}M{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}J_{\hbar}^{\prime}\backslash A_{\hbar}\otimes_{A_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}M[-n],

where we applied the isomorphism (
3.4
). This establishes the first isomorphism.

The proof of
[
BDMN
, Theorem 4.15]
shows that
J_{\hbar}^{\prime}\backslash A_{\hbar}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\widehat{J}_{\hbar}^{\prime}\backslash\widehat{A}_{\hbar}
as
(B_{\hbar},A_{\hbar})
-bimodules. Since
\widehat{A}_{\hbar}
is flat over
A_{\hbar}
,

\widehat{J}_{\hbar}^{\prime}\backslash\widehat{A}_{\hbar}\otimes_{\widehat{A}_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}\widehat{M}\cong\widehat{J}_{\hbar}^{\prime}\backslash\widehat{A}_{\hbar}\otimes_{\widehat{A}_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}\widehat{A}_{\hbar}\otimes_{A_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}M\cong\widehat{J}_{\hbar}^{\prime}\backslash\widehat{A}_{\hbar}\otimes_{A_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}M,

proving the second isomorphism.
∎

Again, let
Q
be any one of
A,\widehat{A}
or
B
. Recall that if
M
is a finite
Q_{\hbar}
-module then the
grade

j(M)
of
M
is the smallest
j\geq 0
such that
\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{Q_{\hbar}}(M,Q_{\hbar})\neq 0
. Since
Q_{\hbar}
is Auslander regular and
Q/\hbar Q
is Cohen-Macaulay,

j(M)+\dim\mathrm{Supp}\,M=\dim\operatorname{Spec}(Q/\hbar Q).

Moreover, by Gabber’s Theorem,
\dim\mathrm{Supp}M\,\leq(1/2)\dim\operatorname{Spec}(Q/\hbar Q)
. Thus,

j(M)\leq(1/2)\dim\operatorname{Spec}(Q/\hbar Q)

and
j(M)=(1/2)\dim\operatorname{Spec}(Q/\hbar Q)
if and only if
M
is holonomic.

Since
\widehat{A}_{\hbar}
is flat over
A_{\hbar}
, we have

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(M,A_{\hbar})\otimes_{A_{\hbar}}\widehat{A}_{\hbar}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(\widehat{A}_{\hbar}\otimes_{A_{\hbar}}M,\widehat{A}_{\hbar}),

which shows that if
M
is a holonomic
A_{\hbar}
-module then
\widehat{A}_{\hbar}\otimes_{A_{\hbar}}M
is a holonomic
\widehat{A}_{\hbar}
-module. Therefore, by Lemma
3.13
, it suffices to prove that if
N
is a holonomic
\widehat{A}_{\hbar}
-module then the cohomology groups

H^{k}\left(\widehat{J}_{\hbar}^{\prime}\backslash\widehat{A}_{\hbar}\otimes_{\widehat{A}_{\hbar}}^{\mathbb{L}}N\right)

are holonomic
B_{\hbar}
-modules.

Choose local coordinates
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
for
V
. Then the
algebra
(\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar})^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
can be
explicitly described as follows: let
\deg(\partial_{i})=a_{i}>0

and
\deg(x_{i})=-b_{i}<0
, so that
a_{i}-b_{i}=\ell>0
because
[\partial_{i},x_{i}]=\hbar^{\ell}
. Let
A=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

and
B=(b_{1},\dots b_{n})
be the associated multi-indices. Then

(\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar})^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}

-linear sheaf of dg-rings on
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{Eq}}
. If
\mathscr{N}
is a dg
\Omega
-module then

is a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant dg
\Omega_{\hbar}
-module. This establishes an equivalence
\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}(\Omega_{\hbar},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
, given by
\mathscr{N}\mapsto\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}
, between the category of coherent dg
\Omega
-modules and coherent
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant dg
\Omega_{\hbar}
-modules, with inverse
\mathscr{F}\mapsto\mathscr{F}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{E}
be as above, with
\mathcal{L}
supported on
\Lambda
. Then

(1)

\Omega_{\hbar}\cong\mathcal{E}\otimes_{{\mathcal{W}}}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
, as sheaves of dg-algebras.

(2)

Suppose that the object
\mathcal{L}
is nonzero. Then
{\mathcal{W}}\cong\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes_{\Omega_{\hbar}}\mathcal{E}
as dg-algebras, where the differential on
{\mathcal{W}}
is trivial.

(3)

\Omega
and
\Omega_{\hbar}
are cohomologically supported on
\Lambda
: that is, the total cohomology
\mathcal{H}^{{\>\raisebox{2.0pt}{\text{\circle*{1.5}}}}}(\Omega)
is supported on
\Lambda
, and the same is true for
\Omega_{\hbar}
.

(4)

\mathcal{E}
is a finite flat left dg
\Omega_{\hbar}
-module.

Proof.

Part (1) is immediate.

Part (2). The evaluation map is a morphism of dg-algebras
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes_{\Omega_{\hbar}}\mathcal{E}\to{\mathcal{W}}
. To show that it is an isomorphism, it suffices to check locally that this is an isomorphism of sheaves (forgetting the grading and differential). In this case we may assume that
\mathcal{E}={\mathcal{W}}^{\oplus k}
for some
k>0
and the claim is clear.

Part (3) follows from (
4.1
) since
\mathcal{L}
assumed to be supported on
\Lambda
.

Part (4) is a local statement, so we may assume that
\mathcal{E}
is a finite complex of free
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules of finite rank. Moreover, we may forget the differential and the (homological) grading when checking that
\mathcal{E}
is finite and flat. Thus, we are reduced to the fact that
\mathcal{E}={\mathcal{W}}^{\oplus k}
is a finite flat (in fact projective) module over
\mathcal{E}nd_{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathcal{W}}^{\oplus k})
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a bounded complex of locally free good right
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules.

(1)

It is essential in Lemma
4.1
to work sheaf-theoretically. If
P
is a finitely generated projective module over a (say regular, Noetherian) ring then it is not usually flat over
\operatorname{End}_{R}(P)
.

(2)

\mathcal{E}
will
not
in general be
K
-flat (in the sense of Spaltenstein) as a left dg
\Omega_{\hbar}
-module.

Let
\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
denote the category of sheaves of coherent right dg
\Omega
-modules and
\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}=\Ind\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
. For
\mathscr{M}\in C^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
and
\mathscr{N}\in\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
, we define

G(\mathscr{M})=\underline{\mathcal{H}om}_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{X}}}(\mathcal{E},\mathscr{M})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}},\quad F(\mathscr{N})=\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}\otimes_{\Omega_{\hbar}}\mathcal{E}.

Lemma 4.3
.

The functors
G,F
define equivalences
C^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
and
\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}C^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
. These induce equivalences

K^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}),\quad K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}K^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})

and

K({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}),\quad K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}K({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}).

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{N}\in\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
. Then the adjunction
\Id\to GF
applied to
\mathscr{N}
is

\displaystyle\mathscr{N}\to GF(\mathscr{N})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{E},\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}\otimes_{\Omega_{\hbar}}\mathcal{E})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}

\displaystyle\cong(\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}\otimes_{\Omega_{\hbar}}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}))^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}

\displaystyle\cong(\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}\otimes_{\Omega_{\hbar}}\Omega_{\hbar})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}\cong\mathscr{N},

where we have used Lemma
4.1
(1). Similarly, if
\mathscr{M}\in C^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
then

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}\leftarrow FG(\mathscr{M})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{E},\mathscr{M})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}}\otimes_{\Omega_{\hbar}}\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{E},\mathscr{M})\otimes_{\Omega_{\hbar}}\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\cong\mathscr{M}\otimes_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{E},{\mathcal{W}})\otimes_{\Omega_{\hbar}}\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\cong\mathscr{M}\otimes_{{\mathcal{W}}}{\mathcal{W}}=\mathscr{M},

where we have used the fact that if
\mathscr{P}
is a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant dg
\Omega_{\hbar}
-module then the adjunction
\widetilde{\mathscr{P}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}}\to\mathscr{P}
is an isomorphism and we have used Lemma
4.1
(2). Since
F,G
are additive functors they induce equivalences of homotopy categories.

Identifying
C({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})=\Ind C^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
and using the fact that
G
and
F
are cocontinuous on
C^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
and
\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
respectively, the above proof shows that

and

extend to equivalences
C({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}
and
\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}C({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})
respectively. Passing to homotopy categories gives the final claim of the lemma.
∎

If
\mathscr{F}
is a complex of sheaves on
\mathfrak{X}
then we say that
\mathscr{F}
is

if the sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{i}(\mathscr{F})
are zero for all

. Since
\mathcal{E}
is a bounded complex of locally free
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules, the functor
G
is
K
-projective i.e. if
\mathscr{M}\in K({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})
is an acyclic complex then so too is
G(\mathscr{M})
. However,
\mathcal{E}
will almost never be
K
-flat as an
\Omega
-module and so, even if
\mathscr{N}
is acyclic,

will not in general be acyclic. An object

is said to be

if

is acyclic.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
\mathscr{N}\in K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
is
F
-acyclic then
\mathscr{N}
is acyclic.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that the adjunction
\mathscr{N}\to GF(\mathscr{N})
is an isomorphism in
K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
and
G
sends acyclic objects to acyclic objects.
∎

Let
\mathcal{N}_{F}
denote the full subcategory of
K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
consisting of

-acyclic complexes. Then
\mathcal{N}_{F}
is a thick strictly triangulated subcategory of

and we call the Verdier quotient

the ”exotic derived category”

.

Since
G,F
are equivalences on the level of homotopy categories, they induce equivalences

(4.2)

G\colon D({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}D_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}),\quad F\colon D_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}D({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}}).

Let
D^{b}_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
denote the quotient of
K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
by
\mathcal{N}_{F}\cap K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
. Then

restrict to equivalences

G\colon D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}D_{ex}^{b}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}),\quad F\colon D_{ex}^{b}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}}).

Note that Lemma
4.4
implies that there are quotient functors
D_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})\to D(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
and

.

Remark 4.5
.

This construction can be very degenerate. For instance, consider the two-term complex

. Then every
\Omega
-module is acyclic and

to the

-invariants of the cone

. Then one can easily check that

is

-acyclic if and only if

is acyclic.

.

.

This follows from the fact that we can form the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})}

∎

Definition 4.7
.

The object
\mathcal{L}\in D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
is a
local generator
if given
\mathscr{M}\in D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
such that
\mathbb{R}Hom_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})
is an acyclic sheaf, we must have
\mathscr{M}\cong 0
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M}\in D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
. Under the elliptic
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action on
\mathfrak{X}
, the complex of sheaves
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})
is acyclic if and only if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
is acyclic on
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{Eq}}
if and only if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}_{y}
is an acyclic complex of vector spaces for all
y\in Y
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})
is acyclic if and only if the stalks
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})_{x}
are acyclic for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
. The locus in
\mathfrak{X}
where
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})
is not acyclic is closed and
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-stable. Since the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
action on
\mathfrak{X}
is elliptic, any such closed subset contains a fixed point. Therefore, if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})
is not acyclic then there exits
y\in Y
such that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})_{y}
is not acyclic. Now,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})_{y}
is a complex of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant
{\mathbb{C}}(\!(\hbar)\!)
-modules with
{\mathbb{C}}(\!(\hbar)\!)
-linear differential. Since
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
acts with weight one on
\hbar
, the complex
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})_{y}
is acyclic if and only if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})_{y}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
is acyclic. Thus, if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})
is not acyclic then there exists
y\in Y
such that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})_{y}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
is not acyclic. Conversely, if the complex
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})
is acyclic then clearly
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})_{y}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
is acyclic for all
y\in Y
.

A similar argument shows that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
is acyclic if and only if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}_{y}
is an acyclic complex of vector spaces for all
y\in Y
.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
be a local generator. Then
D_{ex}^{b}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}D^{b}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
.

Proof.

We must show that
\mathscr{N}\in K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
is
F
-acyclic if and only if
\mathscr{N}
is acyclic. One implication always holds by Lemma
4.4
. Therefore, we need to show that if
\mathscr{N}
is acyclic then it is
F
-acyclic. By Lemma
4.3
, we may assume that
\mathscr{N}=G(\mathscr{M})
for some
\mathscr{M}\in K^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
. This means that
F(\mathscr{N})\cong\mathscr{M}
. Moreover,

\mathcal{H}^{i}(\mathscr{N})=\mathcal{H}^{i}(\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{E},\mathscr{M})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}})=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om^{i}_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathscr{L},\mathscr{M})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}.

Therefore, if
\mathcal{H}^{i}(\mathscr{N})=0
for all
i
then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathscr{L},\mathscr{M})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
is an acyclic complex of sheaves. By Lemma
4.8
,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathscr{L},\mathscr{M})
is acyclic and we deduce that
\mathscr{M}
is zero since
\mathscr{L}
is a local generator.
∎

Note that when
\mathcal{E}
is a resolution of a local generator
\mathcal{L}
, Lemma
4.9
shows that
\mathcal{E}
is
K
-flat as an object in
K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
, even though it is not
K
-flat in
K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
.

We have shown in the proof of Lemma
4.9
that
\mathcal{N}_{F}\cap K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
is the category
\mathrm{Acyc}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
of acyclic objects in
K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
. The following shows that one can characterize
\mathcal{N}_{F}
entirely in terms of these acyclic complexes.

Lemma 4.10
.

Every acyclic complex
\mathscr{M}\in K({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})
is the filtered colimit of a sequence of coherent (bounded) acyclic complexes
\mathscr{M}_{i}\in K^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
.

Conversely, if
\mathscr{M}\in K({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{qcoh}})
is the filtered colimit of a sequence of coherent (bounded) acyclic complexes
\mathscr{M}_{i}\in K^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
then
\mathscr{M}
is acyclic.

Proof.

The first claim follows from the fact that
{\mathcal{W}}
is a coherent sheaf of rings.

The converse follows from
[
S
, Lemma 10.8.8]
.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

The category
\mathcal{N}_{F}
is the smallest thick strictly triangulated subcategory of the homotopy category
K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
containing
\mathrm{Acyc}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
and closed under coproducts.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{N}
be
F
-acyclic. Then
\mathscr{M}=F(\mathscr{N})
is an acyclic complex of
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules. By Lemma
4.10
, we may write
\mathscr{M}=\lim_{\to}\mathscr{M}_{i}
as a filtered colimit of acyclic objects in
K^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
. Since
G(-)=\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{E},-)
and
\mathcal{E}
is a bounded complex of (coherent) locally free
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules,
G
preserves filtered colimits (see e.g.
[
CH
, Theorem 4.5]
; in fact, by
[
CH
, Lemma 3.4]
, the functor

preserved arbitrary colimits). Therefore,
\mathscr{N}=\lim_{\to}\mathscr{N}_{i}
, where
\mathscr{N}_{i}=G(\mathscr{M}_{i})
is acyclic in

. Therefore, each

.

Conversely, if
\mathscr{N}=\lim_{i}\mathscr{N}_{i}
with each

[

. ∎

4.2.

Fix a total ordering on

K_{<r}:=\bigsqcup_{i<r}C_{i}

of Section

By Theorem

is a bounded complex with holonomic cohomology.

Our goal is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.12
.

The object
\mathcal{L}
is a local generator.

The proof is by induction on the number of coisotropic cells in
\mathfrak{X}
. We begin by assuming that
\mathfrak{X}
itself is a coisotropic cell. This means that
\mathfrak{X}=T^{*}V
for some vector space
V
. In this case the elliptic
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action scales the fibers of

and hence has fixed points

. Let
\rho\colon T^{*}V\to V
be the projection map. By
[
BDMN
, Proposition 4.33]
, the functor
\mathscr{M}\mapsto(\rho_{{\>\raisebox{2.0pt}{\text{\circle*{1.5}}}}}\mathscr{M})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}

, the latter being the category of coherent right

-modules.

Lemma 4.13
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{X}=T^{*}V
. Let
x\in V\subset T^{*}V
and
\mathscr{M}_{i}\in D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X},x}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
for

. Then
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X},x}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}=\mathcal{D}_{V,x}
and

(4.3)

\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X},x}}(\mathscr{M}_{1},\mathscr{M}_{2})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}\cong\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{V,x}}(\mathscr{M}_{1}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}},\mathscr{M}_{2}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}).

Proof.

In this case,
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X}}
can be identified as a sheaf of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant
{\mathbb{C}}(\!(\hbar)\!)
-modules with
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\!(\hbar)\!)
, where the product is given by the Moyal-Weyl product on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\!(\hbar)\!)
. Any section

is defined on some
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-invariant open neighbourhood of
x
and shrinking this if necessary, we may assume
s\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(U\times V^{*})\widehat{\otimes}{\mathbb{C}}(\!(\hbar)\!)
, where
x\in U\subset V
. It is easily checked that any such invariant section is of the form

s=\hbar^{-N}D_{-N}(x,y)+\hbar^{-N+1}D_{-N+1}(x,y)+\cdots+D_{0}(x,y),

where each
D_{-i}(x,y)=\sum_{|\alpha|=i}f_{i}(x)y^{\alpha}
is a polynomial homogeneous in the

and
f_{i}(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{V}(U)
. Since the map
\mathcal{D}_{V,x}\to{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X},x}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
sends
\partial_{i}
to
\hbar^{-a_{i}}y_{i}
, it follows that this is an isomorphism.

Since
\mathscr{M}_{1}\in D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X},x}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
, it is isomorphic to a finite complex of finite rank projective
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{X},x}
-modules, where the differentials are
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant. Then the identification (
4.3
) reduces to the case where
\mathscr{M}_{1}={\mathcal{W}}_{X,x}\{j\}
for some
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

We continue to assume that
\mathfrak{X}=T^{*}V
. Let
n=\dim V
and let
\Omega_{V,\hbar}^{n}
denote the good right
{\mathcal{W}}
-module corresponding to
\Omega^{n}_{V}
under the equivalence
{\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathcal{D}_{V}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
. Note that
\Omega_{V,\hbar}^{n}
is a simple
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant holonomic object supported on

. Therefore, Proposition
3.5

is the unique such simple object.

Proposition 4.14
.

The sheaf
\delta_{V}
is a local generator in
D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{M}\in D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
. By definition, we must show that if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\delta_{V},\mathscr{M})
is acyclic then
\mathscr{M}\cong 0
. The complex of sheaves is acyclic if and only if the complex of vector spaces
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\delta_{V},\mathscr{M})_{x}
is acyclic for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
. Since
{\mathcal{W}}
is a coherent sheaf of algebras and
\delta_{V}
a coherent
{\mathcal{W}}
-module,
[

says that

(4.4)

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\delta_{V},\mathscr{M})_{x}=\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{x}}(\delta_{V,x},\mathscr{M}_{x}).

Take
x\in V\subset T^{*}V=\mathfrak{X}
. Then
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
acts on the spaces of (
4.4
). Taking
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-invariants of the right hand side gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{x}}(\delta_{V,x},\mathscr{M}_{x})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{x}}((\Omega_{V,\hbar}^{n})_{x},\mathscr{M}_{x})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{x}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}}((\Omega_{V,\hbar}^{n})_{x}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}},\mathscr{M}_{x}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}})

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{V,x}}(\Omega_{V,x}^{n},\mathscr{M}_{x}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{D}_{V}}(\Omega_{V}^{n},\mathscr{M}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}})_{x}

having applied Lemma
4.13
. This implies that

\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{V}}(\Omega_{V}^{n},\mathscr{M}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}})\cong\mathscr{M}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}\otimes_{\mathcal{D}_{V}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}_{V}

is an acyclic complex of sheaves on
V
. Hence, by
[
BD1
, Lemma 7.2.6(iii)]
or
[
BD2
, Lemma 2.1.6]
,
\mathscr{M}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}\cong 0
. But taking
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-invariants is an equivalence, hence
\mathscr{M}\cong 0
.
∎

Now, we prove by induction on the strata that
\mathcal{L}
is a local generator. Let
C\subset\mathfrak{X}
be a closed coisotropic cell and
U
its complement. Assume that
\mathscr{M}\in D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
such that the complex
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})
is acyclic. Then

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})|_{U}=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{U}}(\mathcal{L}|_{U},\mathscr{M}|_{U})

is acyclic and hence we may assume by induction that
\mathscr{M}|_{U}\cong 0
. Therefore,
\mathscr{M}
is supported on
C
and Kashiwara’s Theorem
[
BDMN
, Theorem 4.28]
says that there exists
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
on
S
such that
\mathscr{M}=\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime})
. Then

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime})).

Pick a point
x\in C
. Then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime}))_{x}
is an acyclic sheaf of vector spaces.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
y\in Y\subset C
and
s=\pi(y)\in S
. Then

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime}))_{y}\cong\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})_{s}.

Proof.

Let us say that an open subset
U\subset\mathfrak{X}
is
C
-good if (a)
U
is a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-stable affine open set and (b)
C\cap U_{i}=\{u\in U_{i}\,|\,\lim_{t\to\infty}t\cdot u\in Y\}
. By
[
BDMN
, Lemma 4.25]
,
\mathfrak{X}
admits a cover by
C
-good open sets. Thus, we may assume that
\mathfrak{X}
is itself
C
-good.

Moreover, we can fix a filtered system
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
C
-good neighbourhoods of
y
, meaning that
U_{j}\subset U_{i}
if
i\to j
and
\bigcap_{i\in I}U_{i}=\{y\}
. Indeed, Sumihiro’s Theorem implies that we can choose a filtered system
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of affine
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-stable open neighbourhoods of
y
. Then it suffices to note that if
C=\{x\in\mathfrak{X}\,|\,\lim_{t\to\infty}t\cdot x\in Y\}
then
C\cap U=\{u\in U\,|\,\lim_{t\to\infty}t\cdot u\in Y\}
for all
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-stable open subsets of
\mathfrak{X}
.

Next, we claim that
\{S_{i}:=\pi(C\cap U_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a filtered system of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-stable affine open neighbourhoods of
s
in
S
. It follows from the explicit construction of
\pi
given in Section 2.4 of
[
BDMN
]
that
S_{i}=\mathrm{Spec}((R/I)^{\{I,-\}})
, where
R=\mathcal{O}(U_{i})
and
I
is the radical ideal in
R
defining
C
. Thus, each
S_{i}
is an affine
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-stable open open neighbourhood of
s
. If
s\in U\subset S
then there exists an open set
U^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{X}
containing
y
such that
U^{\prime}\cap C=\pi^{-1}(U)
. If
i\in I
such that
U_{i}\subset U^{\prime}
then
S_{i}\subset U
.

Finally, we compute:

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime}))_{y}

\displaystyle=\lim_{I}\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{X}}(\mathcal{L},\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime}))(U_{i})

\displaystyle\cong\lim_{I}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{X}(U_{i})}(\mathcal{L}(U_{i}),\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime})(U_{i}))

\displaystyle\cong\lim_{I}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{X}(U_{i})}(\mathcal{L}(U_{i}),\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime}(S_{i})))

\displaystyle\cong\lim_{I}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}(S_{i})}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L})(S_{i}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime}(S_{i}))

\displaystyle\cong\lim_{I}\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})(S_{i})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})_{s}

where we have applied the adjunction of Proposition
3.11
in the fourth line.

∎

Proposition 4.16
.

If
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime}))
is acyclic then so too is
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Note that the action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
on
S
is elliptic. Therefore, Lemma
4.9
says that the complex
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})
is acyclic if and only if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})_{s}
is an acyclic complex of vector spaces for all
s\in S^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
. Fix such a point
s
and let
y\in Y\subset C
such that
\pi(y)=s
. Then Lemma
4.15
says that

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime}))_{y}\cong\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})_{s}.

Since we have assumed that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime}))_{y}
is acyclic, the proposition follows.
∎

We deduce that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})
is an acyclic complex of sheaves.

Lemma 4.17
.

\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L})
is a local generator on
S
.

Proof.

Let
N=|I|
. If, for
r<N
,
j_{r}^{\prime}\colon U_{r}\hookrightarrow U
then
j_{r}=j\circ j_{r}^{\prime}
. Then

\mathcal{L}=\delta_{\mathsf{\Lambda}_{|I|}}\oplus\mathbb{R}j_{*}\left(\bigoplus_{r<N}\mathbb{R}(j_{r}^{\prime})_{*}\delta_{\mathsf{\Lambda}_{r}}\right).

Since
\mathbb{R}i^{!}\circ\mathbb{R}j_{*}=0
, we deduce that
\mathbb{R}i^{!}(\mathcal{L})=\delta_{\mathsf{\Lambda}_{N}}
and hence
\mathbb{H}^{*}\mathcal{L}=\delta_{\mathsf{\Lambda}_{N}}
on
S=S_{N}
. Then the claim follows from Proposition
4.14
applied to
S\cong T^{*}V_{N}
.
∎

We deduce from Proposition
4.16
and Lemma
4.17
that
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}=0
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.12
.

Remark 4.18
.

Since
\mathcal{L}\in D^{b}_{\mathrm{Hol}}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
, equation (
4.1
) implies that
\Omega
is a proper dg algebra.

4.3.
The coderived category

This subsection is an aside, and not used elsewhere in the paper.

Implicitly, the proof of Proposition
4.14
is based on classical
\mathcal{D}
-
\Omega
-duality, which is an equivalence
D^{b}(\mathcal{D}_{V}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}D_{ex}(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}})
, originally considered by Kapranov
[
Kap
]
and later Saito
[
Sa
]
and Beilinson-Drinfeld
[
BD1
,
BD2
]
. Here
\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}
is the (sheaf) de Rham dg-algebra of poly-differential forms on
V
. More recently, Positselski
[
Po
, Appendix B]
has given a beautiful reformulation (and generalization) of this result in terms of coderived categories. Namely, one first considers the smallest thick triangulated subcategory
\text{Acyc}^{\text{co}}(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}})
of
K(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
that is cocomplete and contains the total complex of every exact triple in
C(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}})
. Then Positselski defines the coderived category
D^{\operatorname{co}}(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}})
as the Verdier quotient of
K(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
by
\text{Acyc}^{\text{co}}(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}})
. He proves that the
(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}},\mathcal{D}_{V})
-bimodule
\mathcal{T}:=\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{D}_{V}
(equipped with the de Rham differential) induces an equivalence

G_{\mathsf{DR}}\colon D(\mathcal{D}_{V}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}}){\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}D^{\operatorname{co}}(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}),\quad G_{\mathsf{DR}}(\mathscr{M})=\underline{\mathcal{H}om}_{\mathcal{D}_{V}}(\mathcal{T},\mathscr{M})

with quasi-inverse
F_{\mathsf{DR}}(\mathscr{N})=\mathscr{N}\otimes_{\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}}\mathcal{T}
.

If we define
\Omega=\underline{\mathcal{E}nd}_{\mathcal{D}_{V}}(\mathcal{T})
then there is a quasi-isomorphism
\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\Omega
of sheaves of dg-algebras on
V
. If we define the thick subcategories
\mathcal{N}_{F}\subset K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
and
\mathcal{N}_{F_{\mathsf{DR}}}\subset K(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
as in Section
4.1
, then Positselski’s result implies that
\mathcal{N}_{F_{\mathsf{DR}}}=\text{Acyc}^{\text{co}}(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}})
. Moreover, one can show that:

Proposition 4.19
.

Positselski’s equivalences
G_{\mathsf{DR}},F_{\mathsf{DR}}
can be factored as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(\mathcal{D}_{V})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})/\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\operatorname{co}}(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Res}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ind}

where all functors are equivalences.

We omit the proof since the result is not needed elsewhere.

Based on Proposition
4.19
one would expect that
K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})/\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}=D^{\operatorname{co}}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
. However, just as in Lemma
4.1
, one can check that the sheaf
\Omega
has finite global dimension since
\mathcal{T}
is a finite free complex of
\mathcal{D}_{V}
-modules. Therefore, it follows from
[
Po
, Theorem 3.6(a)]
that
D^{\operatorname{co}}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})=D(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
is the usual derived category. Since
\Omega
and
\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}}
are quasi-isomorphic, we would then conclude from Proposition
4.19
that
D^{\operatorname{co}}(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}})=D(\Omega_{\mathsf{DR}})
too. But this cannot be the case, as explained for instance in
[
BD1
, Section 7]
. Thus,
K(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})/\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}
is not the coderived category. The key point is that quasi-isomorphic dg-algebras need not have equivalent coderived categories.

Globally, if we no longer assume
\mathfrak{X}=T^{*}V
then one might similarly assume that the exotic derived category
D_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
, appearing in the equivalences of (
4.2
), is just the coderived category. But, again, Lemma
4.1
implies that the coderived category of
\Omega
equals the usual derived category
D(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
and hence is a proper quotient of
D_{ex}(\Omega\text{-}{\mathsf{Mod}})
.

4.4.
Symplectic resolutions

In this section we assume once again that
\mu\colon\mathfrak{X}\to X
is a symplectic resolution. We first note that locally free resolutions exist. Indeed, by
[
BLPW2
, Corollary B.1]
we may assume, up to twisting by a quantized line bundle, that localization holds for our quantization. In this case, every module over the ring of global sections
\Gamma(\mathfrak{X},{\mathcal{W}})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
admits a finite resolution by finitely generated projective modules since the ring has finite global dimension. These projective modules localize to finite rank locally free
{\mathcal{W}}
-modules.

By Proposition
3.16
, for each
1\leq r\leq N=|I|
, there exists a simple holonomic
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant (good)
{\mathcal{W}}
-module
\mathcal{L}_{r}
that is supported on
\Lambda^{c}
and
\mathcal{L}_{r}|_{U(r)}\cong\delta_{\Lambda^{c}_{r}}
. We take now

\mathcal{L}:=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{r}.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a locally free resolution of
\mathcal{L}
then
\Omega=\underline{\mathcal{E}nd}_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{E})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
is cohomologically supported on
\Lambda^{c}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We just need to show that
\mathcal{L}
is a local generator for
D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
. Assume
\mathscr{M}\in D^{b}({\mathcal{W}}\text{-}{\mathsf{good}})
with
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathcal{L},\mathscr{M})
acyclic. If
U
is the open coisotropic cell then
\mathcal{L}|_{U}=\mathcal{L}_{1}|_{U}=\delta_{\Lambda^{c}_{1}}
is a local generator by Proposition
4.14
. As in the proof of Theorem
4.12
, we may therefore assume by induction that there is a closed coisotropic cell
C=C_{N}
with complement
U
such that
\mathscr{M}|_{U}\cong 0
. Again,
\mathscr{M}=\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\mathscr{M}^{\prime})
for some good
{\mathcal{W}}_{S}
-module
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
. By Proposition
4.16
, we are reduced to showing that
\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L})
is a local generator on
S
. Note that

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{N}\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{r}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})

and if the left hand side is acyclic then every summand
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{r}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})
of the right hand side must be acyclic. In particular,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{W}}_{S}}(\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{N}),\mathscr{M}^{\prime})
is acyclic. But since
C_{N}
is closed in
\mathfrak{X}
,
\mathcal{L}_{N}=\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\delta_{V_{N}})
if we identify
S=S_{N}=T^{*}V_{N}
. Then
\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{N})=\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathbb{H}^{\perp}(\delta_{V_{N}}))\cong\delta_{V_{N}}
is a local generator by Proposition
4.14
. This forces
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}\cong 0
.
∎

Minimum degree conditions for rainbow triangles
Victor Falgas-Ravry, Klas Markström, Eero Räty

Abstract
Let 𝐆 := ( G 1 , G 2 , G 3 ) \mathbf{G}:=(G_{1},G_{2},G_{3}) be a triple of graphs on a common vertex set V V of size n n . A rainbow triangle in 𝐆 \mathbf{G} is a triple of edges ( e 1 , e 2 , e 3 ) (e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}) with e i ∈ G i e_{i}\in G_{i} for each i i and { e 1 , e 2 , e 3 } \{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\} forming a triangle in V V . In this paper we consider the following question: what triples of minimum degree conditions ( δ ⁡ ( G 1 ) , δ ⁡ ( G 2 ) , δ ⁡ ( G 3 ) ) (\delta(G_{1}),\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{3})) guarantee the existence of a rainbow triangle? This may be seen as a minimum degree version of a problem of Aharoni, DeVos, de la Maza, Montejanos and Šámal on density conditions for rainbow triangles, which was recently resolved by the authors. We establish that the extremal behaviour in the minimum degree setting differs strikingly from that seen in the density setting, with discrete jumps as opposed to continuous transitions. Our work leaves a number of natural questions open, which we discuss.



Keywords:
extremal graph theory, rainbow triangles, Gallai colourings, Mantel’s theorem, min degree

Introduction

One of the foundational results in extremal graph theory is Mantel’s celebrated 1907 theorem
[
17
]
, which asserts that a triangle-free graph
G
on
n
vertices can have at most
\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor
edges, with equality if and only if
G
is (isomorphic to) the complete balanced bipartite graph
T_{2}(n)
. Mantel’s theorem is equivalent to its minimum-degree variant, which states that a triangle-free on
n
vertices has minimum degree at most
n/2
, which is tight (again, by considering
T_{2}(n)
). In this paper we will investigate a rainbow version of the latter result: given a triple of graphs
(G_{1},G_{2},G_{3})
on a common vertex set
V
, what minimum degree conditions on the
G_{i}
guarantee the existence of a
rainbow triangle
, that is, a triangle with edges
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
such that
e_{i}\in G_{i}
for each
i
?

We begin by introducing the requisite terminology and background for our problem.

Definition 1.1
.

[Colouring templates, colourings]
An
r
-colouring template on
V
is an
r
-tuple
\mathbf{G}^{(r)}=(G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{r})
, where each of the
G_{i}
is a graph on
V
. Whenever
r
is clear from context, we omit the superscript
r
and write
\mathbf{G}
for
\mathbf{G}^{(r)}
. An
r
-coloured graph
(H,c)
is a graph
H=(V(H),E(H))
together with an
r
-colouring of its edges
c:\ E(H)\rightarrow\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
. (Note that an
r
-coloured graph may be identified with an
r
-colouring template where the colour classes
G_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq r
, are pairwise edge-disjoint.)

Definition 1.2

(Coloured and rainbow subgraphs)
.

Given an
r
-coloured graph
(H,c)
, we say that an
r
-colouring template
\mathbf{G}^{(r)}
on a vertex set
V
contains a copy of
(H,c)
as a subgraph if there is an injection
f:\ V(H)\rightarrow V
such that for each edge
e=\{x,y\}\in E(H)
we have
\{f(x),f(y)\}\in G_{c(e)}
. Further, given a graph
H
, we say that
\mathbf{G}
contains a rainbow copy of
H
if
\mathbf{G}
contains
(H,c)
for some
r
-colouring
c:\ E(H)\rightarrow\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
assigning distinct colours to distinct edges.

Gallai
[
10
]
initiated the study of
r
-colourings with no rainbow triangles, proving a structure theorem that was subsequently re-discovered and extended by a number of other researchers
[
4
,
12
]
;
r
-coloured graphs and
r
-colouring templates containing no rainbow triangles are accordingly referred to as
Gallai colourings
and
Gallai colouring templates
respectively.

One of the first rainbow variants of Mantel’s theorem was obtained by Keevash, Saks, Sudakov and Verstraëtewho considered the following problem: how large does the arithmetic mean of the size of the colour classes
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots G_{r}
have to be to guarantee the existence of a rainbow
H
in an
r
-colouring template? As a special case of more general results, they showed
[
16
, theorem 1.2]
that when
H
is a triangle, it is best to take
G_{i}=T_{2}(n),\forall i
when
r\geq 4
, while for
r=3
it is best to take
G_{1}=G_{2}=K_{n}
and take
G_{3}
to be the empty graph. As the latter construction features an empty colour class, it is natural to ask what happens in the
r=3
case if all three of the colour classes are large. This was done by Diwan and Mubayi
[
6
]
, who asked: what is the least
\alpha>0
such that for all
n
sufficiently large, every
3
-colouring template
\mathbf{G}
on an
n
-set
V
with
\min\{|E(G_{i})|:\ 1\leq i\leq 3\}>\alpha n^{2}
contains a rainbow triangle? Their question was answered by Aharoni, DeVos, de la Maza, Montejanos and Šámal
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
, who determined the optimal value for
\alpha
. Aharoni
et al

[
1
, Problem 1.3]
suggested the more general problem of determining which triples of edge densities
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
guarantee the existence a rainbow triangle, where
\alpha_{i}
denotes the edge-density of
G_{i}
. Later Frankl, Györi, He, Lv, Salia, Tompkins, Varga and Zhu
[
9
]
considered the related problem of determining the maximum of the geometric mean of the sizes of the colour classes
\left(e(G_{1})e(G_{2})e(G_{3})\right)^{1/3}
over all
n
-vertex Gallai
3
-colouring templates
\mathbf{G}
, and provided a certain construction which they conjectured to be asymptotically extremal for this problem.

In
[
8
]
the authors resolved the question of Aharoni
et al
, and, as a by-product, proved the aforementioned conjecture of Frankl
et al
. However as we noted in that paper, the two families of extremal examples of Gallai colouring templates for the various triples of critical densities
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
given in
[
8
, Theorem 1.13]
have as a common feature that at least one of the colour classes
G_{i}
,
i\in\{1,2,3\}
contains isolated vertices. Motivated by this observation, we investigate in this paper the minimum degree variant of Aharoni
et al
’s problem. As we show, the extremal behaviour in the minimum degree setting differs starkly from that seen in the edge-density setting. In particular, unlike what holds for Mantel’s classical theorem, whose edge-density and minimum-degree versions are essentially equivalent, determining edge-density and minimum-degree conditions for rainbow triangles are two genuinely different problems. To set our result in its proper context, we first make the following trivial observation.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a
3
-colouring template on
n
vertices. Suppose
\delta(G_{3})>0
. If
\delta(G_{1})+\Delta(G_{2})>n
, then
\mathbf{G}
contains a rainbow triangle.

Proof.

Suppose
\delta(G_{3})>0
and
\delta(G_{1})+\Delta(G_{2})>n
. Let
v
be such that
d_{G_{2}}(v)=\Delta(G_{2})
, and let
\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E(G_{3})
. By the pigeonhole principle, there exists
v^{\prime\prime}
such that
\{v,v^{\prime\prime}\}\in E(G_{2})
and
\{v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\}\in E(G_{1})
. Thus
\{v,v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\}
is a rainbow triangle in
\mathbf{G}
.
∎

In particular, Proposition
1.3
implies that if
\mathbf{G}
is a Gallai
3
-colouring template on
n
vertices with
\delta(G_{i})>0
for
i\in\{2,3\}
and
\delta(G_{1})>\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)n
, then
\min\left(\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{3})\right)<\frac{n}{r}
. Our main result is a substantial improvement of this trivial bound: we show that in fact
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})\leq\frac{2n}{r+1}
. As a consequence, it follows that in
n
-vertex Gallai
3
-colouring templates, the maximum value of
\min\left\{\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{3})\right\}
that can be attained jumps down from
\frac{n}{r}
to
\frac{n}{r+1}
when
\delta_{1}(G)
increases from
n-\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil
to
\lceil n-\frac{n}{r}\rceil+1
. This stands in sharp contrast to the more continuous behaviour seen in the edge-density case
[
8
, Theorem 1.13]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a
3
-colouring template on
n
vertices, and let
r\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\mathbf{G}
contains no rainbow triangle and satisfies
\delta(G_{1})>\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)n
and
\delta(G_{2})\geq\delta(G_{3})>0
, then
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})\leq\frac{2n}{r+1}
. In particular, we must have
\delta(G_{3})\leq\frac{n}{r+1}
.

The upper bounds on the sum
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})
and on the value of
\delta(G_{3})
in Theorem
1.4
are easily seen to be tight up to an additive term of
1
, by considering the following Turán like construction.

Construction 1.5
.

Given
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{T}(r,n)
denote the
3
-colouring template in which

is a complete, balanced
r
-partite graph on

Clearly, for every

, while
\delta(T_{1}(r,n))=\lfloor(1-\frac{1}{r})n\rfloor
and
\delta(T_{2}(r,n))=\delta(T_{3}(r,n))=\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor-1>\frac{n}{r+1}
. Many other near extremal constructions are possible, however. Indeed, consider the following general family of constructions.

.

Let

, and let

2.1
Proof strategy

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We shall prove Theorem
1.4
by contradiction: suppose its statement is false. Let
r
be the least natural number for which it fails to be true, and let
\mathbf{G}
be a Gallai colouring template on
N
vertices that provides a counterexample to Theorem
1.4
for that value of
r
.

It is easy to see that
r\geq 2
. Indeed, for any Gallai
3
-colouring template
\mathbf{G}
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G_{1})>0
, Proposition
1.3
(with colours
1
and
3
interchanged), implies that
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})<n
.

If
r\geq 3
, then we shall exploit the minimum degree conditions
\delta(G_{1})>(1-\frac{1}{r})N
and
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})>\frac{2N}{r+1}
together with the absence of rainbow triangles to infer structural information about
\mathbf{G}
, from which we will eventually derive a contradiction. More specifically, we will first show that there are no bi-chromatic edges in colours
23
(Lemma
2.7
), and that
G_{2}
is a bipartite graph (Lemma
2.15
) while
G_{3}
is well-behaved with respect to
G_{2}
’s bipartition (Lemma
2.20
). From there, we shall be able to derive a contradiction and show
\mathbf{G}
cannot both satisfy our minimum degree conditions and fail to contain a rainbow triangle.

Our approach to the case
r=2
is similar in spirit (we rule out bi-chromatic edges in colour
23
in Lemma
2.35
and show one of
G_{2}
,
G_{3}
must be bipartite in Lemma
2.42
, before deriving a contradiction); however for
r=2
, the key inequality

\displaystyle\delta(G_{1})+2\min(\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{3}))>N

(2.1)

which drives many of the proofs in the
r\geq 3
case fails. Indeed, since
\delta(G_{3})>0
and
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{r-1}{r}N
, Proposition
1.3
implies that
\delta(G_{2})\leq\Delta(G_{2})\leq N-\delta(G_{1})<\frac{N}{r}
. Thus

\displaystyle\delta(G_{3})\geq\frac{2N}{r+1}-\delta(G_{2})>\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N,

(2.2)

which is greater or equal to
\frac{N}{2r}
(and thus implies (
2.1
)) if and only if
r\geq 3
. If
r\geq 3
, thanks to (
2.1
) we are able to treat the colours
2
and
3
interchangeably. This is no longer true when
r=2
, and we must come up with alternative and much longer arguments that are specially tailored to that setting and do not rely on (
2.1
). We deal with the
r\geq 3
and
r=2
cases in Section
2.2
and Section
2.3
respectively, proving the following theorems:

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\mathbf{G}
is a Gallai
3
-colouring template on
N
vertices with
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{N}{2}
and
\delta(G_{2})\geq\delta(G_{3})>0
. Then
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})\leq\frac{2N}{3}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
\mathbf{G}
is a Gallai
3
-colouring template on
N
vertices with
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{(r-1)N}{r}
, for some
r\geq 3
,
\delta(G_{2})>0
and
\delta(G_{3})>0
. Then
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})\leq\frac{2N}{r+1}
.

Theorem
1.4
follows immediately from these two results together with Proposition

. ∎

2.2
Proof of the case
r\geq 3

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Having outlined our proof strategy in the subsection above, we now carry it out. By Proposition
1.3
and Theorem
2.1
, we may assume the statement of Theorem
1.4
is true for
r=1
and
r=2
. Suppose Theorem
2.2
is false, and let
r\geq 3
be the least natural number for which it fails. We let
\mathbf{G}
be a putative counterexample on
N
vertices for this minimum value of
r
, and we denote its vertex set by
V
.

Note that in this proof (as in the statement of Theorem
2.2
), the colours
2
and
3
play a symmetrical role. Given a colour
c\in\{2,3\}
, we shall denote by
\overline{c}
the complementary colour,
\{\overline{c}\}:=\{2,3\}\setminus\{c\}
. We observe that, by applying Proposition
1.3
as in the proof of (
2.2
), the bounds

\displaystyle\frac{N}{r}>\Delta(G_{c})\geq\delta(G_{c})\geq\frac{2N}{r+1}-\Delta(G_{\overline{c}})>\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N

(2.3)

hold for each
c\in\{2,3\}
. Further, the bound
\delta(G_{1})+2\delta(G_{c})>\delta(G_{1})+2\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N>N
holds (since
r\geq 3
).

We begin by showing that there is no non-trivial bipartition of
V
with no edge in colour
2
or
3
going across it; this simple fact will be used several times in the argument.

Definition 2.3

(
1
-cut)
.

A bipartition
V=A\sqcup B
is a
1
-cut if
|G_{2}[A,B]|=|G_{3}[A,B]|=0
, i.e. there are no edges in colours
2
or
3
across the partition. A
1
-cut is non-trivial if both
A
and
B
are non-empty.

Lemma 2.4
.

The Gallai colouring template
\mathbf{G}
does not contain a non-trivial
1
-cut.

Proof.

Suppose
A\sqcup B
is a non-trivial
1
-cut with
\left|A\right|=\frac{\alpha N}{r+1}
and
\left|B\right|=\frac{\beta N}{r+1}
,
where
\alpha+\beta=r+1
. Our minimum degree condition
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})>\frac{2N}{r+1}
together with the non-triviality of the
1
-cut
A\sqcup B
implies
1<\alpha,\beta<r
. Note that for any vertex
v\in A
,
d_{G_{2}[A]}(v)=d_{G_{2}}(v)\geq\delta(G_{2})
and
d_{G_{3}[A]}(v)=d_{G_{3}}(v)\geq\delta(G_{3})
. Further, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d_{G_{1}[A]}\left(v\right)}{\left|A\right|}\geq\frac{d_{G_{1}}(v)-(N-|A|)}{|A|}>\frac{\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)N-\left(N-\frac{\alpha N}{r+1}\right)}{\frac{\alpha N}{r+1}}=1-\frac{r+1}{\alpha r}.

Suppose that
\alpha r\geq(r+1)\left(\lceil\alpha\rceil-1\right)
. The inequality above then tells us that
\delta(G_{1}[A])>\left(1-\frac{1}{\lceil\alpha\rceil-1}\right)|A|
. Since the statement of Theorem
1.4
holds for
r^{\prime}=\lceil\alpha\rceil-1<r
and since
\mathbf{G}[A]
is a Gallai colouring template, this implies that

\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})\leq\delta(G_{2}[A])+\delta(G_{3}[A])\leq\frac{2|A|}{\lceil\alpha\rceil}\leq\frac{2N}{r+1},

contradicting our assumption on
\mathbf{G}
. Thus we must have
\alpha r<(r+1)\left(\lceil\alpha\rceil-1\right)
and, by a symmetric argument for
v\in B
,
\beta r<(r+1)\left(\lceil\beta\rceil-1\right)
. Summing these two inequalities, and using
\alpha+\beta=r+1
, we get a contradiction:

\displaystyle(r+1)r=(\alpha+\beta)r<(r+1)\left(\lceil\alpha\rceil+\lceil\beta\rceil-2\right)\leq(r+1)r.

∎

Next we prove another useful result, namely that whenever a vertex is incident with at least one bi-chromatic edge in colours
ij
, it is incident to many such edges. To make this more precise, we define the
minimum positive degree

\delta^{+}(G)
of a non-empty graph

over all non-isolated vertices

.

Lemma 2.5
.

For every pair
i,j
of distinct colours from
\{1,2,3\}
, either
\delta^{+}(G_{i}\cap G_{j})>\frac{(r-1)}{r(r+1)}N
or
G_{i}\cap G_{j}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Suppose
u
is a vertex with
d_{G_{i}\cap G_{j}}(u)>0
. Let
\{k\}=\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i,j\}
, and let
v
be any vertex in
N_{G_{i}\cap G_{j}}(u)
. Since
\mathbf{G}
is a Gallai colouring template,
N_{G_{k}}(v)
is disjoint from
N_{G_{i}\cup G_{j}}(u)
. In particular,

\displaystyle d_{G_{i}\cap G_{j}}(u)=|N_{G_{i}}(u)|+|N_{G_{j}}(u)|-|N_{G_{i}\cup G_{j}}(u)|

\displaystyle\geq\delta(G_{i})+\delta(G_{j})-\left(N-|N_{G_{k}}(v)|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\delta(G_{1})+\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})-N>\frac{(r-1)}{r(r+1)}N.

∎

Remark 2.6

Both Lemma

Lemma 2.7

G_{2}\cap G_{3}=\emptyset

Claim 2.8

Z=\emptyset

Proof.

Suppose this is not the case. Since

2.4

z\in Z
sending an edge in a colour

By construction of

Claim 2.9

|X|\geq r

|X|\geq 1+\delta^{+}(G_{2}\cap G_{3})>1+\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N

Claim 2.10
.

G_{1}[X]
is
K_{r}
-free.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(d_{G_{1}}(x_{i})-(r-1)\right)>(r-1)(N-r)

\{x_{i}:\ i\in[r]\}\cup\{u\}

in

Now, by the minimum degree condition for colours
2
and
3

d_{G_{2}}(u)+d_{G_{3}}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(d_{G_{2}}(x_{i})+d_{G_{3}}(x_{i})\right)>2N,

whence there exists
v

into
\{x_{i}:\ i\in[r]\}\cup\{u\}

x_{1}v
and
x_{2}v

G_{c}

Since
\mathbf{G}

N_{G_{2}\cap G_{3}}(x_{1})

contradicting the fact our three sets are pairwise disjoint subsets of

∎

Now for every

|N_{G_{1}}(y)\cap X|\leq|X|-|N_{G_{2}\cap G_{3}}(x)|<|X|-\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N.

It follows that
|G_{1}[X,Y]|<(N-|X|)\left(|X|-\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N\right)

[

\displaystyle\frac{r-1}{r}N|X|<\delta(G_{1})|X|\leq 2|G_{1}[X]|+|G_{1}[X,Y]|<\frac{r-2}{r-1}|X|^{2}+(N-|X|)\left(|X|-\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N\right).

Corollary 2.11

There are no rainbow edges in
\mathbf{G}
.∎

Lemma 2.12

. Then either every vertex in

(if

). Note that if

\delta^{+}(G_{1}\cap G_{2})>\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N

Claim 2.13
.

Claim 2.14
.

G_{2}[X,Y]=\emptyset

Proof.

Recall that by Lemma

\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N

Thus there are no edges in colour

or

Since
\delta(G_{1})+\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})>N

In the remainder of the proof, we shall assume without loss of generality that

\displaystyle\delta(G_{1}\cap G_{3})=\delta^{+}(G_{1}\cap G_{3})>\frac{(r-1)N}{r(r+1)}.

We are now ready to prove our second key structural lemma and show that

is bipartite.

Lemma 2.15
.

The graph
G_{2}
is bipartite.

Proof.

We first show
G_{2}
is triangle-free and that the neighbourhoods in
G_{3}
are independent sets in
G_{2}
.

Claim 2.16
.

The graph
G_{2}
is triangle-free.

Proof.

Suppose
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
induces a triangle in
G_{2}
. As
\mathbf{G}
is a Gallai colouring template,
N_{G_{1}\cap G_{3}}(v_{1})
,
N_{G_{1}}(v_{2})
and
N_{G_{3}}(v_{3})
must be pairwise disjoint subsets of
N
.
Since
\delta(G_{1})+\delta(G_{3})+\delta(G_{1}\cap G_{3})>\left(\frac{r-1}{r}+\frac{2(r-1)}{r(r+1)}\right)N>N
, this is
a contradiction.
∎

Claim 2.17
.

For every
v\in V
,
N_{G_{3}}(v)
is an independent set in
G_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose
u,u^{\prime}\in N_{G_{3}}(v)
and
uu^{\prime}\in G_{2}
. By Claim
2.16
,
N_{G_{2}}(u)
and
N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})
are disjoint sets. Further, since there are no rainbow triangles, both of these sets are disjoint from
N_{G_{1}}(v)
. Since
\delta(G_{1})+2\delta(G_{2})>N
, this is a contradiction.
∎

Suppose now that
G_{2}
is not bipartite. Since
G_{2}
is triangle-free by Claim
2.16
, it must contain a shortest odd cycle
C
of length
|C|=(2\ell+1)
, for some integer
\ell\geq 2
. Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{2\ell+1}
denote the vertices of
C
. By the minimality of
C
, every vertex
v\in V
(including the ones in
C
) can send at most two edges in
G_{2}
to
C
, for otherwise we would have a shorter odd cycle. Furthermore, if a vertex
v\in V
sends two edges of
G_{2}
into
C
, these two vertices must lie at distance exactly
2
apart on the cycle. Hence
N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})
is disjoint from
N_{G_{2}}(v_{j})
for any
j\not\in\left\{i-2,i,i+2\right\}
.

Fix an index
i
and consider the sets
N_{G_{2}}(v_{i-2})\cap N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})
and
N_{G_{2}}(v_{i+2})\cap N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})
. Since they are disjoint subsets of
N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})
, at least one of these two sets contains fewer than
\frac{1}{2}|N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})|
elements, and so we may assume without loss of generality that
|N_{G_{2}}(v_{i-2})\cap N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})|\leq\frac{1}{2}|N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})|
. Hence

\displaystyle|N_{G_{2}}(v_{i-2})\cup N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})|\geq\frac{3}{2}|N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})|\geq\frac{3\delta(G_{2})}{2}

(2.4)

Now,
N_{G_{3}}(v_{i})\cup N_{G_{3}}(v_{i-2})
and
N_{G_{1}}(v_{i-1})
are disjoint sets (else we have a rainbow triangle), and since
\delta(G_{1})+2\delta(G_{3})>N
(by (
2.3
)), it follows that the sets
N_{G_{3}}(v_{i})
and
N_{G_{3}}(v_{i-2})
must have a non-empty intersection. Thus there exists
w
for which we have
wv_{i-2}
,
wv_{i}\in G_{3}
. Since we have no rainbow triangles in
\mathbf{G}
,
N_{G_{1}}(w)
is disjoint from
N_{G_{2}}(v_{i-2})\cup N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})
; combining this information with (
2.4
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\delta_{2}(G)\leq|N_{G_{2}}(v_{i-2})\cup N_{G_{2}}(v_{i})|\leq N-|N_{G_{1}}(w)|\leq N-\delta(G_{1})<\frac{N}{r},

whence
\delta(G_{2})<\frac{2N}{3r}
. Our next aim is to show that the same bound holds for
\delta(G_{3})
as well. To do this, in the next two claims we shall bound the number of edges in the various colours a vertex
v\in V
can send into
C
.

Claim 2.18
.

For any
v\in V
, there are at most
2
edges of
G_{3}
between
v
and
C
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that there exist
v\in V
and
v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},v_{i_{3}}\in C
so that
vv_{i_{j}}\in G_{3}
for every
j\in\left\{1,2,3\right\}
. Since
C
contains
2\ell+1\geq 5
vertices, there exists at least one pair of indices from
\{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}\}
which do not differ by
2
modulo
2\ell+1
, say
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
. Hence
N_{G_{2}}(v_{i_{1}})
and
N_{G_{2}}(v_{i_{2}})
are disjoint sets, and they are both disjoint from
N_{G_{1}}(v)
. This contradicts the fact that
\delta(G_{1})+2\delta(G_{2})>N
(by (
2.3
)). ∎

Claim 2.19
.

For every
v\in V
,
|G_{1}[\{v\},C]|+|G_{3}[\{v\},C]|\leq|C|
. Moreover this can be attained if and only if
v\notin C
and
v
sends no edge in colour
3
to
C
.

Proof.

Observe that
v
can send in total at most two edges in colours
1
or
3
to a neighbouring pair of vertices
\left\{v_{i-1},v_{i}\right\}
on
C
, as otherwise we have a rainbow triangle.

If
v
sends an edge in
G_{1}\cap G_{3}
to
v_{i}
for some
i
, then
v
does not send any edge in colours
1
or
3
to
v_{i-1}
or
v_{i+1}
. By the averaging argument above,
v
sends at most a total of
|C|-3
edges in colours
1
or
3
to the remaining
|C|-3
vertices, as required.

Otherwise, every pair
vv_{i}
can belong to at most one of
G_{1}
or
G_{3}
. To attain the upper bound of
|C|
on the total number of edges in colours
1
or
3
sent by
v
into
C
, each such pair must be present in at least one of the graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{3}
, which implies that all
|C|
edges must be of the same colour (as otherwise we have a rainbow triangle). However,
v
cannot send an edge of
G_{3}
to every vertex on

, as neighbourhoods in
G_{3}
are independent sets in
G_{2}
(by Claim
2.17
). This completes the proof.

∎

Now let
A:=\left\{v\in V:|G_{3}[\{v\},C]|>0\right\}
. Claim
2.19
implies that we have

\displaystyle\left(\delta(G_{1})+\delta(G_{3})\right)|C|\leq|C|\left(N-|A|\right)+\left(|C|-1\right)|A|=|C|N-|A|.

(2.5)

Since every
v\in V
sends most two edges of
G_{3}
to
C
(by Claim
2.18
), we have by averaging over the vertices of

that
|A|\geq\frac{|C|\delta(G_{3})}{2}
. Combining this lower bound on

with (

), we obtain that
\delta(G_{1})+\frac{3\delta(G_{3})}{2}\leq N
, which implies that
\delta(G_{3})<\frac{2N}{3r}
and hence
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})<\frac{4N}{3r}
. However, since

, this contradicts the fact that
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})>\frac{2N}{r+1}
. From this final contradiction we deduce that
G_{2}
contains no odd cycle, and hence that
G_{2}
is bipartite as desired.
∎

Let
A\sqcup B
be a bipartition of

such that
G_{2}\subseteq(A,B)^{(2)}
. We now prove our third key structural lemma and show

is well-behaved with respect to this bipartition: either

is a bipartite graph with the same bipartition, or it has no edges going from

.

Lemma 2.20
.

Either
G_{3}\subseteq(A,B)^{(2)}
or
G_{3}\subseteq A^{(2)}\cup B^{(2)}
.

Proof.

We first show neighbourhoods in
G_{3}
are well-behaved with respect to the bipartition

.

Claim 2.21
.

For every
v\in V
,
N_{G_{3}}(v)
has non-empty intersection with at most one of

and

.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that
av,bv\in G_{3}
for some

,
b\in B
. Since
G_{2}
is bipartite,
N_{G_{2}}(a)
and
N_{G_{2}}(b)
are disjoint. Furthermore, both of these sets are disjoint from
N_{G_{1}}(v)
(as otherwise we would have a rainbow triangle). Since
\delta(G_{1})+2\delta(G_{2})>N
, this is a contradiction.
∎

With this claim in hand, we may define a new bipartition

by setting

(i.e. the vertices incident with an edge of

.

Proof.

By Claim

G_{3}
in
(C,D)^{(2)}
. Suppose now

,
v\in D
and

. Since

is bipartite, we may assume without loss of generality that

and

. Let

be a neighbour of

in
G_{3}
, which must lie inside

(since

). Now as
\mathbf{G}
contains no rainbow triangle,
N_{G_{1}}(v)
must be disjoint from
N_{G_{3}}(u)
and
N_{G_{2}}(w)
. Further
N_{G_{2}}(w)\subseteq A
(since
G_{2}
is bipartite and
w\in B
) and
N_{G_{3}}(u)\subseteq B
(since

). Thus the three sets

,

and
N_{G_{3}}(u)
are pairwise disjoint — however

, so this gives a contradiction. It follows there are no edges in

from

to

, and that

The Lemma now follows immediately from Lemma
2.4
: either

or

.
∎

As a corollary to Lemma

, we can rule out the case

; knowing

will simplify the final analysis.

Corollary 2.23
.

We must have
r\geq 4
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that
r=3
. We may assume without loss of generality that
|A|\leq N/2\leq|B|
. We separate into two cases.

If
G_{3}\subseteq(A,B)^{(2)}
, then
\delta(G_{1})>N/2
implies that there is at least one edge
uv\in G_{1}
with
u,v\in B
. Now both
N_{G_{2}}(u)
and
N_{G_{3}}(v)
are subsets of
A
and the sum of their sizes is strictly greater than
N/2
. Hence by the pigeon-hole principle,
|N_{G_{2}}(u)\cap N_{G_{3}}(v)|>0

On the other hand if
G_{3}\subseteq A^{(2)}\cup B^{(2)}
, then
\delta(G_{1})>N/2
implies that there is at least one edge
uv\in G_{1}
with

and
v\in B
. Now both
N_{G_{3}}(u)
and
N_{G_{2}}(v)
are subsets of
A
and the sum of their sizes is strictly greater than
N/2
. Again, by the pigeon-hole principle, this implies the existence of a rainbow triangle, a contradiction. ∎

It turns out that analysing the two alternatives for
G_{3}
’s structure provided by Lemma
2.20
can be essentially done simultaneously if
G_{2}
happened to be disconnected. This follows from the fact that in such a case, the only way to avoid a non-trivial

to also be bipartite, with

edges joining vertices in different connected components of

(this is proved in Lemma
2.30
below). In particular, the cases
G_{3}\subseteq A^{(2)}\cup B^{(2)}
and
G_{3}\subseteq\left(A,B\right)^{(2)}
are symmetric under switching the vertex classes of one connected component of
G_{2}
in the bipartition.

Hence our next task is to prove that
G_{2}
cannot be connected. Our approach is as follows: we show that if
x
and
y
are two vertices on the same side of the bipartition which are also in the same connected component of
G_{2}
, then they have many joint neighbours in
G_{2}
(Lemma
2.25
below). This in turn can be shown (Lemma
2.26
) to contradict the upper bound on
\Delta(G_{2})
from (
2.3
). Unfortunately, this approach does not quite work when
r=4
and
\delta(G_{2})
is small, and we need to apply a more ad hoc argument (Lemma
2.27
) to deal with this special case.

Having sketched how our final analysis will go, we now make it concrete. We begin by recording a useful lemma which shows neighbourhoods in
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
have large intersections if they are non-empty.

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
\{i_{1},j_{1}\}
and
\{i_{2},j_{2}\}
be two copies of
\{2,3\}
. Let
u,u^{\prime}
be two distinct vertices in
V
. If
N_{G_{i_{1}}}(u)\cap N_{G_{i_{2}}}(u^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, then both
N_{G_{i_{1}}}(u)\cap N_{G_{i_{2}}}(u^{\prime})
and
N_{G_{j_{1}}}(u)\cap N_{G_{j_{2}}}(u^{\prime})
have size strictly larger than
\frac{(r-3)}{r(r+1)}N
. Furthermore, if
\max\left(\delta(G_{j_{1}}),\delta(G_{j_{2}})\right)=\max\left(\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{3})\right)
, then we have the stronger bound
|N_{G_{j_{1}}}(u)\cap N_{G_{j_{2}}}(u^{\prime})|>\frac{(r-1)}{r(r+1)}N
.

Proof.

Let
v\in N_{G_{i_{1}}}(u)\cap N_{G_{i_{2}}}(u^{\prime})
. Since
\mathbf{G}
is a Gallai colouring template,
N_{G_{1}}(v)
is disjoint from
N_{G_{j_{1}}}(u)\cup N_{G_{j_{2}}}(u^{\prime})
. Then by our minimum degree assumptions for our three colour classes and the lower bound on
\delta(G_{c})
,
c\in\{2,3\}
from (
2.3
), we have:

\displaystyle|N_{G_{j_{1}}}(u)\cap N_{G_{j_{2}}}(u^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\geq|N_{G_{j_{1}}}(u)|+|N_{G_{j_{2}}}(u^{\prime})|-\left(N-|N_{G_{1}}(v)|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\delta(G_{1})+\delta(G_{j_{1}})+\delta(G_{j_{2}})-N>\frac{(r-3)}{r(r+1)}N,

as required. Thus the intersection is non-empty, and the lower bound on
|N_{G_{i_{1}}}(u)\cap N_{G_{i_{2}}}(u^{\prime})|
follows symmetrically. Further, if at least one of
\delta(G_{j_{1}})
or
\delta(G_{j_{2}})
is equal to
\max\left(\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{3})\right)
, then
\delta(G_{j_{1}})+\delta(G_{j_{2}})
is at least
\frac{2N}{r+1}
, which, substituted in the displayed inequality above, yields an improved lower bound

\displaystyle|N_{G_{j_{1}}}(u)\cap N_{G_{j_{2}}}(u^{\prime})|>\frac{(r-1)}{r(r+1)}N.

∎

The next lemma is the key tool we employ to derive a contradiction from our structural information; it guarantees that vertices from
A
in the same component of
G_{2}
have a large number of common neighbours in
G_{2}
. This in turn implies the existence of vertices in
B
with large degree in
G_{2}
, violating the upper bound
\Delta(G_{2})\leq\frac{N}{r}
from (
2.3
).

Lemma 2.25
.

Suppose that
\delta(G_{2})\geq\frac{2}{3r}N
. If
u,u^{\prime\prime}
are both in
A
or both in
B
and in addition lie in the same connected component of
G_{2}
, then they have at least
\frac{r-3}{r(r+1)}N
neighbours in common in both
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
u,u^{\prime\prime}\in A
. Note it is enough to show that if there is a path of length
4
in
G_{2}
with edges
uv
,
vu^{\prime}
,
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}
joining
u
to
v
, then
u
and
u^{\prime\prime}
have at least one common neighbour in
G_{2}
— indeed by Lemma
2.24
it then follows that
u
and
u^{\prime\prime}
have at least
\frac{r-3}{r(r+1)}N
common neighbours in both
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
N_{G_{2}}(u)
and
N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime\prime})
are disjoint sets. Since
N_{G_{2}}(u)\cap N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})
and
N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})\cap N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime\prime})
are both non-empty, it follows from Lemma
2.24
that
N_{G_{3}}(u)\cap N_{G_{3}}(u^{\prime})
and
N_{G_{3}}(u^{\prime})\cap N_{G_{3}}(u^{\prime\prime})
are both non-empty as well. Now, for any
w\in N_{G_{3}}(u)\cap N_{G_{3}}(u^{\prime})
,
N_{G_{1}}(w)
is a set of size at least
\delta(G_{1})>N-\frac{N}{r}
, which is disjoint from
N_{G_{2}}(u)\cup N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})
; thus
|N_{G_{2}}(u)\cup N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})|<\frac{N}{r}
, and similarly we have
|N_{G_{2}(u^{\prime})}\cup N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime\prime})|<\frac{N}{r}
.

Since
N_{G_{2}}(u)
and
N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime\prime})
are disjoint sets, at least one of the inequalities
|N_{G_{2}}(u)\cap N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})|\leq\frac{1}{2}|N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})|
and
|N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime\prime})\cap N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})|\leq\frac{1}{2}|N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})|
must be satisfied — without loss of generality, let us assume it is the first of these two inequalities. Then it follows that

\displaystyle|N_{G_{2}}(u)\cup N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})|\geq|N_{G_{2}}(u)|+\frac{1}{2}|N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})|\geq\frac{3\delta(G_{2})}{2}.

Since
\delta(G_{2})\geq\frac{2}{3r}N
, it follows that
|N_{G_{2}}(u)\cup N_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})|\geq\frac{N}{r}
, a contradiction. The lemma follows.
∎

We can now prove that
G_{2}
is not connected. We split the proof into two lemmas, the first of which deals with the general case
r\geq 5
and
r=4
,
\delta(G_{2})\geq\frac{2}{3r}N
, and the second of which deals with special case
r=4
,
\delta(G_{2})<\frac{2}{3r}N
.

Lemma 2.26
.

Suppose
\delta(G_{2})\geq\frac{2}{3r}N
. Then
G_{2}
is not connected.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that
G_{2}
is connected. Assume without loss of generality that
|A|\leq|B|
.

Case 1:
\delta(G_{2})\geq\delta(G_{3})
.
Lemma
2.25
together with the ‘furthermore’ part of Lemma
2.24
implies that for every pair of vertices
b,b^{\prime}\in B
, the joint neighbourhood
N_{G_{2}}(b)\cap N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})
is a subset of
A
of size at least
\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N
. Combining this piece of information with the upper bound on
\Delta(G_{2})
from (
2.3
) and double counting, we get

\displaystyle\frac{(r-1)N^{3}-2(r-1)N^{2}}{8r(r+1)}\leq\binom{|B|}{2}\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N

\displaystyle<\sum_{bb^{\prime}\in B^{(2)}}|N_{G_{2}}(b)\cap N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A}\binom{d_{G_{2}}(a)}{2}\leq|A|\binom{\Delta_{2}(G)}{2}<\frac{N^{3}-rN^{2}}{4r^{2}}.

(2.6)

Rearranging terms, this yields the inequality
\left(r(r-3)-2\right)N^{3}+4rN^{2}<0
, which is a contradiction since
r\geq 4
.

Case 2:
\delta(G_{3})>\delta(G_{2})
.
By Lemma
2.20
we have
G_{3}\subseteq\left(A,B\right)^{(2)}
or
G_{3}\subseteq A^{(2)}\cup B^{(2)}
. If
G_{3}\subseteq\left(A,B\right)^{(2)}
, the proof follows by using exactly the same argument as in the previous case (with colours
2
and
3
interchanged), as Lemma
2.25
implies that
G_{3}
is also a connected bipartite graph with respect to the same vertex-partition as
G_{2}
.

Hence it suffices to consider the case when
G_{3}\subseteq A^{(2)}\cup B^{(2)}
. By Lemma
2.25
, for every pair of vertices
b,b^{\prime}\in B
the joint neighbourhood
N_{G_{2}}(b)\cap N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})
is non-empty, and thus by the ‘furthermore’ part of Lemma
2.24
the joint neighbourhood
N_{G_{3}}(b)\cap N_{G_{3}}(b^{\prime})
is a subset of
B
of size at least
\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N
. Hence in a similar fashion to the previous case, using the upper bound on
\Delta(G_{3})
from (
2.3
) and double counting, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|B|N}{2r}\right)\frac{\left(|B|-1\right)(r-1)}{r+1}=\binom{|B|}{2}\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N

\displaystyle<\sum_{bb^{\prime}\in B^{(2)}}|N_{G_{3}}(b)\cap N_{G_{3}}(b^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in B}\binom{d_{G_{3}}(b)}{2}\leq|B|\binom{\Delta_{3}(G)}{2}<\left(\frac{|B|N}{2r}\right)\frac{N-r}{r}.

Rearranging the inequality above and using
|B|\geq N/2
then yields the desired contradiction:

0>|B|(r-1)r-(r+1)N+2r\geq\frac{r(r-3)-2}{2}N+2r>0.

∎

Lemma 2.27
.

Suppose that
r=4
and
\delta(G_{2})<\frac{N}{6}=\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{N}{4}
. Then
G_{2}
is not connected.

Proof.

If
G_{3}
is also bipartite with respect to the vertex classes
A
and
B
, we are done as in Case 1 in the proof of Lemma
2.26
(with colours
2
and
3
interchanged):
G_{2}
being connected implies
G_{3}
is a bipartite connected graph with minimum degree
\delta(G_{3})>\frac{2N}{5}-\delta(G_{2})>\frac{N}{6}
, which leads to a contradiction as in (
2.6
).

Hence we may assume that
G_{3}\subseteq A^{(3)}\cup B^{(3)}
. Set
|A|=\alpha N
and
|B|=\beta N
, and assume without loss of generality that
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}\leq\beta
. We claim that
|A|
and
|B|
have almost balanced sizes, i.e. that
\beta
is not much larger than
1/2
.

Claim 2.28
.

\beta<\frac{15}{29}

Proof.

Since
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{3N}{4}
, by averaging there exists a vertex
b\in B
sending at least

\displaystyle\frac{|G_{1}\left[A,B\right]|}{|B|}\geq\frac{\left(\delta(G_{1})-|A|\right)|A|}{|B|}>\frac{\left(\frac{3N}{4}-\alpha N\right)\alpha N}{\beta N}=\frac{\left(\beta-\frac{1}{4}\right)(1-\beta)}{\beta}N.

edges of
G_{1}
into
A
. Let
a\in N_{G_{2}}(b)
. Then

N_{G_{3}}(a)
and
N_{G_{1}}(b)\cap A
are disjoint subsets of
A
(otherwise we have a rainbow triangle), and so

\displaystyle|A|=(1-\beta)N\geq|N_{G_{3}}(a)|+|N_{G_{1}}(b)\cap A|\geq\delta(G_{3})+\frac{\left(\beta-\frac{1}{4}\right)(1-\beta)}{\beta}N.

Since
\delta(G_{3})>\frac{2N}{5}-\frac{N}{6}=\frac{7N}{30}
, solving the inequality above yields
\beta<\frac{15}{29}
as desired.
∎

Claim 2.29
.

For any two vertices
a,a^{\prime}\in A
, there exist
b\in B
and
a^{\prime\prime}\in A
such that
aa^{\prime\prime}
,
a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime}
both lie in
G_{3}
and
ab
,
a^{\prime}b
both lie in
G_{2}
.

Proof.

Fix
a,a^{\prime}\in A
. By Lemma
2.24
, these two vertices have a joint neighbour in
G_{2}
(which must lie in
B
) if and only if they have a joint neighbour in
G_{3}
(which must lie in
A
). Observe that by (
2.3
)
\Delta(G_{3})<\frac{N}{r}=\frac{N}{4}
.

Suppose for a contradiction that
N_{G_{2}}(a)
,
N_{G_{2}}(a^{\prime})
are disjoint subsets of
B
. Since
|A|\leq\frac{N}{2}
and
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{3N}{4}
,
|N_{G_{1}}(a)\cap N_{G_{1}}(a^{\prime})|>|A|
, and hence there exists
b\in B
such that
ab
,
a^{\prime}b
both lie in
G_{1}
. But then
N_{G_{3}}(b)
,
N_{G_{2}}(a)
and
N_{G_{2}}(a^{\prime})
must be pairwise disjoint subsets of
B
(otherwise we have a rainbow triangle). However, as
\Delta(G_{3})\leq\frac{N}{4}
, we have
\delta(G_{2})>\frac{2N}{5}-\frac{N}{4}=\frac{3N}{20}
; together with the bound
|B|\leq\frac{15N}{29}
from Claim
2.28
, this yields a contradiction:

\displaystyle|B|\geq|N_{G_{3}}(b)|+|N_{G_{2}}(a)|+|N_{G_{2}}(a^{\prime})|\geq 2\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})>\frac{11N}{20}>\frac{15}{29N}\geq|B|.

∎

Thus any two vertices
a,a^{\prime}\in A
have a common neighbour in
G_{3}[A]
, and hence strictly more than
\frac{3N}{20}
such neighbours by Lemma
2.24
. Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
2.26
we double count edges of
G_{3}[A]
and use the upper bound on
\Delta(G_{3})
from (
2.3
) to get

\displaystyle\binom{|A|}{2}\frac{3N}{20}<|A|\binom{\Delta(G_{3})}{2}\leq\frac{|A|}{2}\frac{N}{4}\left(\frac{N}{4}-1\right).

Dividing through by
|A|N/40
and rearranging terms, we get
|A|<\frac{5}{12}N-\frac{2}{3}<\frac{14N}{29}
, contradicting Claim
2.28
.
It follows that
G_{2}
cannot be connected.
∎

By (
2.3
), for
r\geq 5
we have
\delta(G_{2})\geq\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}\geq\frac{2}{3r}
. Thus Lemma
2.26
and Lemma
2.27
together imply that
G_{2}
is disconnected. In particular, there exist partitions
A=V_{1}\sqcup V_{4}
and
B=V_{2}\sqcup V_{3}
of
A
and
B
into a total of four disjoint non-empty sets
V_{1},\dots,V_{4}
so that there are no edges of colour
2
in
(V_{1}\cup V_{2},V_{3}\cup V_{4})^{(2)}
. Furthermore, we may assume without loss of generality that
G_{2}\left[V_{1}\cup V_{2}\right]
is connected.

Our next aim is to show that
G_{3}
is also bipartite with edges only in
\left(V_{1},V_{4}\right)^{(2)}
and
\left(V_{2},V_{3}\right)^{(2)}
or only in
\left(V_{1},V_{3}\right)^{(2)}
and
\left(V_{2},V_{4}\right)^{(2)}
. Of course, these two cases are identical up to interchanging the labels of
V_{3}
and
V_{4}
, which will allow us to treat the two alternatives
G_{3}\subseteq\left(A,B\right)^{(2)}
and
G_{3}\subseteq A^{(2)}\cup B^{(2)}
from Lemma
2.20
simultaneously.

Lemma 2.30
.

There exists a choice
\left\{i,j\right\}=\left\{3,4\right\}
so that
G_{3}\subseteq\left(V_{1},V_{i}\right)^{(2)}\cup\left(V_{2},V_{j}\right)^{(2)}
. Furthermore, each of the graphs
G_{2}\left[V_{1},V_{2}\right]
,
G_{2}\left[V_{3},V_{4}\right]
,
G_{3}\left[V_{1},V_{i}\right]
and
G_{3}\left[V_{2},V_{j}\right]
is connected.

Proof.

We first show neighbourhoods in colours
2
and
3
are well-behaved with respect to the partition
V=\sqcup_{i=1}^{4}V_{i}
.

Claim 2.31
.

For every vertex
v\in V
and every colour
c\in\{2,3\}
,
v
sends edges in colour
c
to exactly one of the four parts
V_{i}
,
i\in[4]
.

Proof.

Our assumptions on
G_{2}
ensure that there is no vertex sending an edge of
G_{2}
to two distinct parts
V_{i}
,
i\in[4]
, and thus no pair of vertices in distinct parts have a common neighbour in
G_{2}
. By Lemma
2.24
, this implies that no pair of vertices in distinct parts have a common neighbour in
G_{3}
, and hence that every vertex sends edges in colour
3
to exactly one of the four parts
V_{i}
,
i\in[4]
.
∎

Let
W_{1}=\bigcup_{v\in V_{1}}N_{G_{3}}(v)
and
W_{2}=\bigcup_{v\in V_{2}}N_{G_{3}}(v)
.

Claim 2.32
.

There exist distinct
i,j
with
\left\{i,j\right\}=\left\{1,2\right\}
or
\left\{i,j\right\}=\left\{3,4\right\}
so that
W_{1}\subseteq V_{i}
and
W_{2}\subseteq V_{j}
.

Proof.

Claim
2.31
implies that
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are disjoint. Further, by Lemma
2.24
, if two vertices have a common neighbour in
G_{2}
, then they have a common neighbour in
G_{3}
and hence, by Claim
2.31
again, they must send edges of
G_{3}
to the same part.

Since
G_{2}[V_{1},V_{2}]
is connected, it follows that
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are subsets of two distinct parts
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
respectively,
i,j\in[4]
. Now picking any edges
v_{1}v_{2}\in G_{2}
and
v_{2}w_{2}\in G_{3}
with
v_{1}\in V_{1}
,
v_{2}\in V_{2}
and
w_{2}\in W_{2}
, we see by yet another application of Lemma
2.24
that there exists
w_{1}\in N_{G_{3}}(v_{1})\cap N_{G_{2}}(w_{2})
. Since
w_{1}\in W_{1}\subseteq V_{i}
and
w_{2}\in V_{j}
,
w_{1}w_{2}\in G_{2}
implies that
\{i,j\}=\{1,2\}
or
\{3,4\}
.
∎

Set
X=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup W_{1}\cup W_{2}
and
Y=V\setminus X
.

Claim 2.33
.

X\sqcup Y
is a
1
-cut.

Proof.

By Claims
2.31
and
2.32
, we have that for every vertex
w_{1}\in W_{1}
,
N_{G_{3}}(w_{1})\subseteq V_{1}
, and similarly
N_{G_{3}}(w_{2})\subseteq V_{2}
for every
w_{2}\in W_{2}
. Thus there are no edges in colour
3
from
X
to
Y
.

Let
w_{1}\in W_{1}
and let
w_{2}
be such that
w_{1}w_{2}\in G_{2}
. Then by construction there exists
v_{1}\in V_{1}
such that
v_{1}w_{1}\in G_{3}
. Applying Lemma
2.24
, we see that
N_{G_{2}}(v_{1})\cap N_{G_{3}}(w_{2})
is non-empty, which implies
w_{2}
send an edge in colour
3
to some

, and hence that
w_{2}\in W_{2}
. Thus all edges in colour
2
from
W_{1}
lie in
W_{2}
; a symmetric argument shows that all edges in colour
2
from
W_{2}
lie in
W_{1}
. By construction, we already know all edges in colour
2
from
V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
are to
V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
. Thus there are no edges in colour
2
from
X
to
Y
, and
X\sqcup Y
is a
1
-cut as claimed.
∎

If
W_{1},W_{2}\subseteq V_{1}\cup V_{2}
, then
X=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
and
Y\neq\emptyset
, which in turn implies that the
1
-cut
V=X\sqcup Y
is non-trivial, contradicting Lemma
2.4
. Thus
W_{1},W_{2}\subseteq V_{3}\cup V_{4}
. By symmetry we may assume that
W_{1}\subseteq V_{4}
, whence
W_{2}\subseteq V_{3}
. For this
1
-cut to be trivial, we must then have
Y=\emptyset
and so
W_{1}=V_{4}
and
W_{2}=V_{3}
.
It then follows from Lemma
2.20
that
G_{3}\subseteq\left(V_{1},V_{4}\right)^{(2)}\cup\left(V_{2},V_{3}\right)^{(2)}
.

To complete the proof of the lemma, we need to verify that the graphs
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
are connected when restricted to the appropriate vertex class pairs. Let

, and consider a pair of vertices

. Since
G_{2}\left[V_{1},V_{2}\right]
is connected, there exists a path

in
G_{2}
between them, with

for each

. Since

and

have a common

implies that they also have a common

in

. Thus

, and a symmetrical argument proves that

is also connected.
∎

We are at last in a position to obtain a contradiction, in a similar spirit to the proof of Lemma
2.26
. We first show

is large, and then double count edges to conclude the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Claim 2.34
.

We must have
r\geq 7
.

Proof.

Note our minimum degree conditions imply each of the four parts

V_{1},\dots,V_{4}
are non-empty and have size at least
\max\left(\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{3})\right)>\frac{N}{r+1}
. Assume without loss of generality that
V_{1}
is the largest of the four parts, so that in particular
|V_{1}|\geq\frac{N}{4}
. Since
\delta(G_{1})>(1-\frac{1}{r})N
and since

, for any
u\in V_{3}
there must be a vertex
v\in V_{1}
such that

.

Now,
N_{G_{2}}(v)
and
N_{G_{3}}(u)
are disjoint subsets of
V_{2}
, whence
|V_{2}|\geq\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})>\frac{2N}{r+1}
. Similarly
|V_{4}|>\frac{2N}{r+1}
. As
|V_{1}|\geq|V_{2}|
and
|V_{3}|>\frac{N}{r+1}
, this implies

N=|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|+|V_{3}|+|V_{4}|>\frac{7N}{r+1},

whence

, as claimed.
∎

We may assume that
\delta(G_{2})\geq\delta(G_{3})
, that
|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|=N^{\prime}\geq N/2
and that
|V_{1}|\geq|V_{2}|
. By Lemma
2.25
, for every pair of vertices
b,b^{\prime}\in V_{1}
we have that
N_{G_{2}}(b)\cap N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})
is a subset of
V_{2}
of size at least
\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N
. On the other hand, we know from (
2.3

receives at most
\Delta(G_{2})<N/r
edges in colour

from

. By double-counting as in the proof of Lemma

, we get

\displaystyle\frac{(r-1)(N^{\prime})^{2}N-2(r-1)N^{\prime}N}{8r(r+1)}\leq\binom{|V_{1}|}{2}\frac{r-1}{r(r+1)}N\leq|V_{2}|\binom{\Delta(G_{2})}{2}<\frac{N^{\prime}N^{2}-rN^{\prime}N}{4r^{2}}.

Rearranging terms, this yields the inequality

N^{\prime}N\left(N^{\prime}(r^{2}-r)-N(2r+2)+4r\right)<0.

Since
N^{\prime}(r^{2}-r)-N(2r+2)\geq\frac{N}{2}\left(r^{2}-5r-4\right)>0
for

(which holds by Claim
2.34
), this is the final contradiction.

Proof of the case

Proof of Theorem

We now turn our attention to the

Suppose
\mathbf{G}
is a Gallai
3
-olouring template on

,
\delta(G_{2})\geq\delta(G_{3})>0

\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})>\frac{2N}{3}

(which holds for

We begin as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
by proving that

.

Lemma 2.35
.

G_{2}\cap G_{3}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Again, set
X
to be the set of vertices in
V
incident with a bi-chromatic edge in colours
23
from the graph
G_{2}\cap G_{3}
. Suppose for a contradiction that
X\neq\emptyset

such that

sends at least one edge in colour
2
or
3
to

. Finally, let

denote the rest of the vertices in

. We will analyse the structure of the edges between and inside these three sets in a succession of claims.

Claim 2.36
.

G_{1}[X]
is empty.

Proof.

Suppose there exists
x,x^{\prime}\in X
with
xx^{\prime}\in G_{1}
. Since
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{N}{2}
, there exists
v
such that
xx^{\prime}v
induces a triangle in
G_{1}
. Further, since
3\left(\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})\right)>2N
, there exist
w\in V
and
c\in\left\{2,3\right\}
so that
\left\{x,x^{\prime},v\right\}\subseteq N_{G_{c}}(w)
. As we have no rainbow triangles in
\mathbf{G}
, the sets
N_{G_{1}}(w)
,
N_{G_{2}\cap G_{3}}(x)
,
N_{G_{2}\cap G_{3}}(x^{\prime})
and
N_{G_{\overline{c}}}(v)
are pairwise disjoint (and non-empty, since
x,x^{\prime}\in X
). Since

\delta(G_{1})+2\delta^{+}(G_{2}\cap G_{3})+\min(\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{3}))>\frac{N}{2}+2\cdot\frac{N}{6}+\frac{N}{6}>N,

this gives a contradiction.
∎

Claim 2.37
.

|Z|>\frac{N}{3}
.

Proof.

For any
y\in Y
, there exists
x\in X
so that
xy\in G_{2}\cup G_{3}
. Since
N_{G_{2}\cap G_{3}}(x)
contains at least
\delta^{+}(G_{2}\cap G_{3})>\frac{N}{6}
elements and is a subset of
X
disjoint from
N_{G_{1}}(y)
, it follows that
|G_{1}[X,Y]|\leq|Y|\left(|X|-\frac{N}{6}\right)
. Since
X
contains no edge of
G_{1}
by Claim
2.36
, counting edges in colour
1
from

\displaystyle|X|\frac{N}{2}<|X|\delta(G_{1})<\left|G_{1}[X,Y\cup Z]\right|\leq|Y|\left(|X|-\frac{N}{6}\right)+|X|\cdot|Z|.

Using
|Y|=N-|X|-|Z|
and rearranging terms, we obtain the bound

\displaystyle|Z|>\frac{|X|^{2}-\frac{2N|X|}{3}+\frac{N^{2}}{6}}{N/6}=\frac{\left(|X|-\frac{N}{3}\right)^{2}}{N/6}+\frac{N}{3}.

The claim follows immediately.
∎

Claim 2.38
.

G_{3}\subseteq X^{(2)}\sqcup Y^{(2)}\sqcup Z^{(2)}
.

Proof.

By construction, there are no edges in colour
3
from
X
to
Z
. If there exist
y\in Y
,
z\in Z
with

, then by construction there exist
x\in X
with
xy\in G_{c}
for some
c\in\{2,3\}
. Since we have no rainbow triangles,
N_{G_{2}\cap G_{3}}(x)
(which by construction is a subset of
X
),
N_{G_{1}}(y)
and
N_{G_{2}}(z)
(which by construction is a subset of
Y\cup Z
) must be pairwise disjoint. However the sum of the sizes of these three sets is at least
\delta^{+}(G_{2}\cap G_{3})+\delta(G_{1})+\delta(G_{2})>N
, a contradiction. Thus there are no edges in colour
3
from
X\cup Y
to
Z
.

Suppose now there exist
xy\in G_{3}
with
x\in X
and
y\in Y
. Since
|Z|>\frac{N}{3}
(by Claim
2.37
), since
N_{G_{2}}(y)\cap N_{G_{3}}(y)=\emptyset
(by construction of

) and since

(as shown in the previous paragraph), it follows from our minimum degree assumption
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})>\frac{2N}{3}
that there exists

such that

N_{G_{2}}(x)
and

are disjoint, as the first one is contained in

(else we have a rainbow triangle). This contradicts the fact that

, and the claim follows.
∎

Claim 2.39
.

G_{2}[Y]
and
G_{1}[X,Y]
are both empty.

Proof.

Note that for any
y\in Y
there exist
x\in X
and
z\in Z
so that

. Indeed, the existence of

follows from the construction of

and Claim
2.38
, while the existence of

N_{G_{2}}(y)\cap N_{G_{3}}(y)=\emptyset
and
|Z|>\frac{N}{3}>N-\left(\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})\right)
(by Claim
2.37
) together with Claim
2.38
. Since
N_{G_{3}}(x)\subseteq X
and
N_{G_{3}}(z)\subseteq Z
have size at least
\delta(G_{3})
, and are both disjoint from
N_{G_{1}}(y)
(else we have a rainbow triangle), we must have that

|N_{G_{1}}(y)\cap Y|\geq|N_{G_{1}}(y)|-|X\setminus N_{G_{3}}(x)|-|Z\setminus N_{G_{3}}(z)|\geq\delta(G_{1})+2\delta(G_{3})-\left(N-|Y|\right)>|Y|+2\delta(G_{3})-\frac{N}{2}.

Since

\displaystyle\delta(G_{1}[Y])+\delta(G_{3}[Y])\geq|Y|+3\delta(G_{3})-\frac{N}{2}>|Y|,

which in turn implies that there cannot be any edge of

inside

(by the pigeon-hole principle: any such edge would yield a rainbow triangle in

For the second part, suppose that there exist
y\in Y
,
x\in X
and

. Then
N_{G_{3}}(x)\subseteq X

can send edges of

to at most one of

.

By construction and Claim
2.38
, for any

and the

are disjoint subsets of

(which is either at least

or zero by the previous paragraph) is at most

|X|-\delta(G_{3})<N-|Y|-|Z|-\frac{N}{6}<\frac{N}{2}-|Y|<\delta(G_{1})-|Y|,

where in the penultimate inequality we used the bound

Claims

Claim 2.40

Since

Consider now a new partition of

The following hold:

(i)

(ii)

suppose that

N_{G_{1}\cap G_{3}}(y^{\prime})

Our next aim is to prove that

Lemma 2.42

Proof.

Suppose that
G_{3}[X]
is not bipartite, and let

must have length at least
5
. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
be the vertices of

in cyclic order. Since

has minimal length,
N_{G_{3}}(x_{i})
and
N_{G_{3}}(x_{j})
may intersect only when

and
j
differ by
2
modulo

. We split the proof into two cases, depending on the size of

Case 1:
\delta(G_{3})>\frac{N}{4}
.

Claim 2.43
.

Suppose that
\delta(G_{3})\geq\frac{N}{4}
. Then every vertex
v\in V
sends at most two edges of
G_{2}
to
C
, and furthermore does not send edges in colour
2
to adjacent vertices on the cycle.

Proof.

We use a similar argument to the proof of Claim
2.18
.
Let
v\in V
, and suppose that
i,j
and
k
are distinct indices chosen so that
\left\{x_{i},x_{j},x_{k}\right\}\subseteq N_{G_{2}}(v)
. Since

is a cycle of length at least

, two of these indices do not differ by exactly
2
modulo
|C|
, say
i
and

N_{G_{3}}(x_{i})
,
N_{G_{3}}(x_{j})
and

are disjoint, contradicting the fact that

modulo
|C|
.
∎

Since every vertex
v\in V
sends at most two edges of

into

, it follows by averaging over

that
\delta(G_{2})\leq\frac{2N}{|C|}
. As

.

We say that a pair

\displaystyle|N_{G_{2}}(x_{i-2})\cap N_{G_{2}}(x_{i})|=|N_{G_{2}}(x_{i-2})|+|N_{G_{2}}(x_{i})|-|N_{G_{2}}(x_{i})\cup N_{G_{2}}(x_{i+2})|

\displaystyle>|N_{G_{2}}(x_{i})|-\frac{N}{6}

must be bipartite.
∎

Lemma 2.46
.

If
X=\emptyset
, then
G_{2}=G_{2}[Y]
is bipartite.

Proof.

Assume
X=\emptyset
(and thus
Y=V
). Suppose for a contradiction that
G_{2}
is not bipartite, and let
C
be a shortest odd cycle in
G_{2}
. By Lemma
2.41
,
G_{2}
is triangle-free, and so
|C|\geq 5
. Further, since
G_{2}
is not bipartite, the Andrasfai–Erdős –Sós theorem
[
3
]
implies that
\delta(G_{2})\leq\frac{2N}{5}
and hence that
\delta(G_{3})>\frac{4N}{15}
. As
C
is a shortest cycle, every vertex
v\in V
can send at most two edges of
G_{2}
into
C
. Thus in particular
|C|\delta(G_{2})\leq 2N
, and since
\delta(G_{2})>\frac{N}{3}>\frac{2N}{7}
we must have
|C|=5
.

Let
y_{1},\ldots y_{5}
denote the vertices of
C
. Considering indices modulo
5
, we have that for every
i\in[5]
,
N_{G_{1}}(y_{i})
is disjoint from
N_{G_{3}}(y_{i-1})\cup N_{G_{3}}(y_{i+1})
(otherwise we have a rainbow triangle). Since
\delta(G_{1})+2\delta(G_{3})>\frac{31N}{30}
, this implies that there exist vertices
v_{i}
,
i\in[5]
, with
v_{i}y_{i-1},v_{i}y_{i+1}\in G_{3}
for all
i
.

Now
N_{G_{1}}(v_{4})
is disjoint from
N_{G_{2}}(y_{3})\cup N_{G_{2}}(y_{5})
, whence we have
|N_{G_{2}}(y_{3})\cup N_{G_{2}}(y_{5})|<\frac{N}{2}
and

\displaystyle|N_{G_{2}}(y_{3})\cap N_{G_{2}}(y_{5})|\geq|N_{G_{2}}(y_{3})|+\delta(G_{2})-|N_{G_{2}}(y_{3})\cup N_{G_{2}}(y_{5})|>|N_{G_{2}}(y_{3})|-\frac{N}{6}>\frac{1}{2}|N_{G_{2}}(y_{3})|.

Similarly, we have
|N_{G_{2}}(y_{1})\cap N_{G_{2}}(y_{3})|>\frac{1}{2}|N_{G_{2}}(y_{3})|
. However this implies that the triple intersection
N_{G_{2}}(y_{1})\cap N_{G_{2}}(y_{3})\cap N_{G_{2}}(y_{5})
is non-empty. In particular
y_{1}
and
y_{5}
have a common neighbour in
G_{2}
, contradicting the fact that
G_{2}
is triangle-free. Thus
G_{2}
must be bipartite as claimed.
∎

We now show
X=V
, i.e. that every vertex is incident with bi-chromatic edges in colours
12
.

Lemma 2.47
.

X=V
.

Proof.

We first show
X
is non-empty.

Claim 2.48
.

X\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Suppose
X=\emptyset
(and thus
Y=V
). By Lemma
2.46
,
G_{2}=G_{2}[Y]
is bipartite. Let
V=V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
be a bipartition of
V
such that
G_{2}\subseteq\left(V_{1},V_{2}\right)^{(2)}
.

We claim that every vertex sends edges of
G_{3}
to at most one of
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
. Indeed, suppose this is not the case, and there exist
u,v
and
w
with
u,v\in V_{i}
and
w\in V_{3-i}
with
uv,uw\in G_{3}
. Then
N_{G_{1}}(u)
,
N_{G_{2}}(v)
and
N_{G_{2}}(w)
are disjoint sets (as the latter two are contained in distinct vertex classes of the bipartition), which contradicts the fact that
\delta(G_{1})+2\delta(G_{2})>N
.

Assume without loss of generality that
|V_{1}|\geq\frac{N}{2}
. Suppose that there exists
u\in V_{1}
with
N_{G_{3}}(u)\subseteq V_{2}
. Since
|V_{1}|\geq\frac{N}{2}>N-\delta(G_{1})
, there exists
v\in V_{1}
with
uv\in G_{1}
. As we have no rainbow triangles,
N_{G_{3}}(u)
and
N_{G_{2}}(v)
are disjoint subsets of
V_{2}
, which contradicts the fact that
|V_{2}|\leq\frac{N}{2}<\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})
. Hence no
v\in V_{1}
can send an edge in colour
3
to
V_{2}
.

Finally, given
u\in V_{2}
, there exists
v\in V_{1}
with
uv\in G_{1}
(since
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{N}{2}\geq|V_{2}|
). As we have no rainbow triangles,
N_{G_{3}}(u)
and
N_{G_{2}}(v)
are disjoint subsets of
V_{2}
, contradicting the fact that
|V_{2}|<\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})
, which completes the proof.
∎

Let
X=A\sqcup B
be a bipartition of
G_{3}[X]
(which exists by Lemma
2.42
).

Claim 2.49
.

\forall v\in V
and
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
,
v
sends an edge of
G_{i}
to at most two of the sets
A
,
B
and
Y
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that there exist
v\in V
and vertices
a\in A
,
b\in B
and
y\in Y
with
\left\{a,b,y\right\}\subseteq N_{G_{i}}(v)
. Then the sets
N_{G_{3}}(a)
,
N_{G_{3}}(b)
and
N_{G_{3}}(y)
are disjoint, as they are subsets of
B
,
A
and
Y
respectively. Furthermore, they are pairwise disjoint with
G_{3-i}(v)
, which contradicts the fact that
\min\left(\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{1})\right)+3\delta(G_{3})>\frac{2N}{3}+2\delta(G_{3})>N
(since
\delta(G_{3})>\frac{N}{6}
by (
2.3
)). The claim follows.
∎

Since
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{N}{2}
, there must be an edge of
G_{1}
between
Y
and
X=A\cup B
. Assume without loss of generality that
a\in A
and
y\in Y
are such that
ay\in G_{1}
. As we observed in the paragraph above Lemma
2.41
,
y
is incident with at least one bi-chromatic edge in colours
13
, which by Lemma
2.41
(i) must be to some vertex
y^{\prime}\in Y
. By Claim
2.49
, this implies
y
sends no edge in colour
1
to the set
B
. Further, since
y\in Y
, there is no bi-chromatic edge in colours
12
incident with
y
, and its neighbourhoods in
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are disjoint

Thus
N_{G_{1}}(y)\subseteq V\setminus B
,
N_{G_{3}}(a)\subseteq B
and
N_{G_{2}}(y)
are disjoint subsets (otherwise we have a rainbow triangle or a bi-chromatic edge in colours
12
incident with
y
), which contradicts the fact that
\delta(G_{1})+\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})>N
. The lemma follows.
∎

By Lemma
2.47
,
X=V
(i.e. every vertex is incident with at least
N/6
bi-chromatic edges in colours
12
), and by Lemma
2.42
,
G_{3}=G_{3}[X]
is a bipartite graph with bipartition
A\sqcup B
. Assume without loss of generality that
|A|\geq|B|
. Our next aim is to verify that every vertex sends an edge in colour
1
to both of
A
and
B
.

Claim 2.50
.

For every
v\in V
there exist
a\in A
and
b\in B
with
va,vb\in G_{1}
.

Proof.

Fix
v\in V
. Since
|A|\geq\frac{N}{2}>N-\delta(G_{1})
, it is clear that there exists
a\in A
with
av\in G_{1}
. Suppose for a contradiction that
N_{G_{1}}(v)\subseteq A
. Let
u
be chosen so that
uv\in G_{3}
, and consider
N_{G_{2}}(u)\cap B
. Since
N_{G_{2}}(u)\cap A
and
N_{G_{1}}(v)
are disjoint subsets of
A
, it follows that

\displaystyle|N_{G_{2}}(u)\cap B|\geq\delta(G_{2})-|N_{G_{2}}(u)|\geq\delta(G_{2})-\left(|A|-\delta(G_{1})\right)>|B|-\delta(G_{3}),

as we have
\delta(G_{1})+\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})>N=|A|+|B|
. Thus for any
w\in A
we must have
N_{G_{3}}(w)\cap\left(N_{G_{2}}(u)\cap B\right)\neq\emptyset
, which implies that
uw\not\in G_{1}
. Hence
N_{G_{1}}(u)\subseteq B
, which contradicts the fact that
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{N}{2}\geq|B|
. The claim follows.
∎

Claim 2.51
.

\delta(G_{3})<\frac{N}{4}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\delta(G_{3})\geq\frac{N}{4}
. Fix
a\in A
. Since
G_{2}\cap G_{3}=\emptyset
and
|B|<\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})
, there exists
a^{\prime}\in A
with
aa^{\prime}\in G_{2}
. Suppose there exists
b\in B
with
ab\in G_{2}
. Then
N_{G_{1}}(a)
,
N_{G_{3}}(a^{\prime})\subseteq B
and
N_{G_{3}}(b)\subseteq A
are disjoint sets, which contradicts the fact that
\delta(G_{1})+2\delta(G_{3})>N
whenever
\delta(G_{3})\geq\frac{N}{4}
. Hence no such
b\in B
can exist, and we must have
|G_{2}[A,B]|=0
.

Since
\delta(G_{2})>\frac{N}{3}
, this implies
|B|>\frac{N}{3}
. For
b\in B
, there exists
a\in A
with
ab\in G_{1}
as
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{N}{2}
. As
\mathbf{G}
does not contain rainbow triangles, the sets
N_{G_{2}}(a)
and
N_{G_{3}}(b)
are disjoint subsets of
A
. Hence
|A|>\frac{2N}{3}
, which contradicts the fact that
|B|>\frac{N}{3}
. Thus we must have
\delta(G_{3})<\frac{N}{4}
, as claimed.
∎

By Claim
2.51
,
\delta(G_{3})<\frac{N}{4}
, whence by our minimum degree assumption
\delta(G_{2})>\frac{5N}{12}
.

Claim 2.52
.

Let
u,v\in V
be vertices both in
A
or both in
B
. Then there exists
w
such that
uw,vw\in G_{3}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
u,v\in V
are vertices from the same vertex-class of the bipartition
A\sqcup B
, and for simplicity assume that
u,v\in A
. (Our argument will not rely on the relative sizes of
A
and
B
, so we may make such an assumption without loss of generality.) Since
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{N}{2}
, there exists
w\in V
so that
uw,vw\in G_{1}
. Furthermore, Claim
2.50
implies that there exists
b\in B
with
wb\in G_{1}
.

Suppose that
N_{G_{3}}(u)
and
N_{G_{3}}(v)
are disjoint. Since
\mathbf{G}
does not contain a rainbow triangle, the sets
N_{G_{2}}(w)
,
N_{G_{3}}(b)\subseteq A
and
N_{G_{3}}(u)\cup N_{G_{3}}(v)\subseteq B
are disjoint. This contradicts the fact that

\displaystyle|N_{G_{2}}(w)|+|N_{G_{3}}(b)|+|N_{G_{3}}(u)\cup N_{G_{3}}(v)|\geq\delta(G_{2})+3\delta(G_{3})>\frac{2N}{3}+2\frac{N}{6}=N.

The claim follows.
∎

Fix
b\in B
, and consider the set
C:=N_{G_{2}}(b)\cap A
. By Claim
2.50
, there exists
a\in A
with
ab\in G_{1}
. Since
N_{G_{3}}(a)
and
N_{G_{2}}(b)\cap B
are disjoint subsets of
B
, it follows that
|C|\geq\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})-|B|>\frac{N}{6}
. Our next aim is to prove by a simple averaging argument that there exists a vertex
c\in C
which sends many edges in colour
2
to
B
, which we will use to derive a final contradiction and conclude the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Claim 2.53
.

There exists
c\in C
with
|N_{G_{2}}(c)\cap B|>|B|-\delta(G_{3}).

Proof.

We first prove that for
b^{\prime}\in B
we have
|N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})\cap C|>|C|-\left(\frac{N}{2}-\delta(G_{2})\right)
. Indeed, suppose that there exists a vertex
b^{\prime}\in B
which violates this condition. Let
a\in A
be chosen so that
ab,ab^{\prime}\in G_{3}
, which exists by Claim
2.52
.

Since
b^{\prime}
satisfies the condition
|N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})\cap C|\leq|C|-\left(\frac{N}{2}-\delta(G_{2})\right)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|N_{G_{2}}(b)\cup N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})|\geq|C\cup N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})|\geq|N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})|+|C|-|N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})\cap C|\geq\frac{N}{2}>N-|N_{G_{1}}(a)|.

However, this contradicts the fact that the sets
N_{G_{2}}(b)\cup N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})
and
N_{G_{1}}(a)
are disjoint. Hence for any
b^{\prime}\in B
we have
|N_{G_{2}}(b^{\prime})\cap C|>|C|-\left(\frac{N}{2}-\delta(G_{2})\right)
. By averaging, it follows that there exists a vertex
c\in C
with

\displaystyle|N_{G_{2}}(c)\cap B|>\frac{\left(|C|+\delta(G_{2})-\frac{N}{2}\right)|B|}{|C|}.

(2.8)

Now,

\displaystyle\left(|C|+\delta(G_{2})-\frac{N}{2}\right)|B|-\left(|B|-\delta(G_{3})\right)|C|=\left(\delta(G_{2})-\frac{N}{2}\right)|B|+\delta(G_{3})|C|.

(2.9)

Since
\frac{5N}{12}<\delta(G_{2})<\frac{N}{2}
,
|B|\leq\frac{N}{2}
and
|C|>\frac{N}{6}
, the right-hand side of (
2.9
) is at least

\displaystyle\delta(G_{2})\frac{N}{2}-\frac{N^{2}}{4}+\delta(G_{3})\frac{N}{6}\geq\frac{2N}{3}\cdot\frac{N}{6}+\frac{5N}{12}\cdot\frac{N}{3}-\frac{N^{2}}{4}=0.

Thus combining equations (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we obtain that
|N_{G_{2}}(c)\cap B|>|B|-\delta(G_{3})
.
∎

We are ready ar last to complete the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Let
c\in C\subseteq A
be chosen so that
|N_{G_{2}}(c)\cap B|>|B|-\delta(G_{3})
. Let
a\in A
be chosen so that
ac\in G_{1}
(such an
a
exists since
|A|\geq\frac{N}{2}>N-\delta(G_{1})
). As
\mathbf{G}
does not contain a rainbow triangle, the sets
N_{G_{2}}(c)\cap B
and
N_{G_{3}}(a)
must be disjoint subsets of
B
. However, this contradicts the fact that
|N_{G_{3}}(a)|+|N_{G_{2}}(c)\cap B|>|B|
. This final contradiction completes the proof.
∎

3
Concluding remarks

Our work leaves a number of questions open. To begin with, one could ask for a generalisation of Theorem
1.4
in the style of
[
8
, Theorem 1.13]
. Explicitly, say that a triple
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3})
with
\delta_{1}\geq\delta_{2}\geq\delta_{3}
is a
mindegree forcing triple
if for all
n
sufficiently large, every
n
-vertex
3
-colouring template
\mathbf{G}
with
\delta(G_{i})>\delta_{i}n
for every
i\in[3]
must contain a rainbow triangle.

Problem 3.1
.

Determine the set of mindegree forcing triples.

As a slightly easier question, we ask whether the bound in Theorem
1.4
can be significantly improved if
\max\left(\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{3})\right)>\frac{N}{r+1}
:

Question 3.2
.

Fix
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. Let
\mathbf{G}
be an
n
-vertex Gallai
3
-colouring template with
\delta(G_{1})\geq\delta(G_{2})\geq\delta(G_{3})>0
. Suppose
\delta(G_{1})>\frac{r-1}{r}n
and
\delta(G_{2})>\frac{n}{r+1}
. Does there exist a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(r)>0
such that
\delta(G_{2})+\delta(G_{3})\leq\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\frac{2n}{r+1}
?

If such constants
\varepsilon(r)
exist, it would naturally be interesting to determine their optimal value for each
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. We expect that the proof of Theorem
1.4
could be adapted to yield stability versions of our results (albeit with an unusual flavour: for each
r
,we expect near-extremal constructions must be close to one of the
\mathbf{C_{P,c}}
templates given in Construction
1.6
), but we had not explored this further due to the length of the paper. We suspect such stability results may be a useful tool for tackling Question
3.2
.

In a different direction, there has been considerable interest in rainbow versions of Dirac’s theorem since the work of Joos and Kim on the subject
[
15
]
(see notably
[
11
]
for some very recent progress on these questions). One could consider analogues of our results in this setting. Explicitly, suppose one has an
n
-vertex
n
-colouring template
\mathbf{G}^{(n)}
. Let
\mathbf{\delta}:=\frac{1}{n}\left(\delta(G_{1}),\delta(G_{2}),\ldots,\delta(G_{n})\right)
. Which vectors
\mathbf{\delta}
guarantee the existence of a Hamilton cycle? Previous results have focussed on the value of
\min_{i}\delta(G_{i})
required for this. But perhaps if many of the
\delta(G_{i})
are large, then we can afford for the smallest one to be quite a bit smaller. For example, suppose we know
\delta(G_{1})\geq\delta(G_{2})\geq\delta(G_{k})=\alpha n
and
\delta(G_{k+1})\geq\delta(G_{k+2})\geq\ldots\delta(G_{n})\geq\beta n
. For a given
k<n
, what values of
\alpha\geq\beta>0
guarantee the existence of a rainbow Hamilton cycle?

Another natural question in the style of Joos and Kim, given that the existence of rainbow triangles is now fairly well understood, is the existence of triangle factors in colouring templates.

Question 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{G}^{(3)}
be an
3
-colouring template on
3n
vertices. What triples
(\delta(G_{1}),\delta(G_{2}),\delta(G_{3}))
guarantee the existence of
n
vertex-disjoint rainbow triangles in
\mathbf{G}
?

Finally, we focused in this work on colouring
templates
, in which colour classes may overlap. Following Erdős and Tuza
[
7
]
, one could instead consider problems for
colourings
of
K_{n}
or of subgraphs of
K_{n}
, in which the colour classes must be disjoint. Can one obtain analogues of Theorem
1.4
in these settings?

In particular a conjecture of Aharoni
[
2
]
(which implies the famous Cacceta-Häggkvist conjecture) states that every
n
-vertex
n
-coloured graph in which each colour class has size at least
r
has
rainbow girth
at most
\lceil n/r\rceil
(i.e. contains a rainbow cycle of length at most
\lceil n/r\rceil
); see Hompe and Huynh
[
14
]
for recently announced progress on this problem. Motivated by Aharoni’s rainbow cycle conjecture, Clinch, Goerner, Huynh and Illingworth
[
5
, Question 2.5]
proposed the more general problem of finding the maximum value of the rainbow girth in an
n
-vertex
t
-colouring template in which every colour class has size at least
r
, while Hompe, Qu and Spirkl
[
13
]
studied the set of pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
such that any
\alpha n
-coloured graph in which every colour class has size at least
\beta n
must contain a rainbow triangle. In a similar spirit to the results in this paper, one could consider variants of Aharoni’s conjecture of the following form: given a lower bound on the rainbow girth, how large could (a) the sum of the colour classes, or (b) the minimum degree across the colour classes
be in an
n
-vertex
t
-colouring template?

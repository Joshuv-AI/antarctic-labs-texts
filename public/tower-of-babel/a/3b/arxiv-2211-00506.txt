Uniqueness of meromorphic functions that share Two Sets
Amit Kumar Pal 1 , Bikash Chakraborty 2 and Sudip Saha 3

Abstract
. In this note, we introduce a new kind of pair of finite range sets in ℂ \mathbb{C} for meromorphic functions corresponding to their uniqueness, i.e., how two meromorphic functions are uniquely determined by their two finite shared sets.

1.
Introduction

We use
M(\mathbb{C})
to denote the field of all meromorphic functions in
\mathbb{C}
. Let
S\subset\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
be a non-empty set with distinct elements and
f\in M(\mathbb{C})
. We set

E_{f}(S)=\bigcup\limits_{a\in S}\{z~:~f(z)-a=0\},

where a zero of
f-a
with multiplicity
m
counts
m
times in
E_{f}(S)
. Let
\overline{E}_{f}(S)
denote the collection of distinct elements in
E_{f}(S)
.

Let
g\in M(\mathbb{C})
. We say that two functions
f
and
g
share the set
S
CM (resp. IM) if
E_{f}(S)=E_{g}(S)
(resp.
\overline{E}_{f}(S)=\overline{E}_{g}(S)
).

Let
f
and
g
be two nonconstant entire functions and
S_{1}=\{1\}
,
S_{2}=\{-1\}
and
S_{3}=\{a,b\in\mathbb{C}~:~a\not=\pm 1,b\not=\pm 1,b\not=\frac{1}{a},b\not=1+\frac{4}{a-1}\}
. F. Gross (
[
11
]
) proved that if
E_{f}(S_{j})=E_{g}(S_{j})
(for
j=1,2,3
), then
f=g
.

In 1982, F. Gross and C. C. Yang (
[
13
]
) first proved that if two nonconstant entire functions
f
and
g
share the set
S=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:e^{z}+z=0\}
, then
f\equiv g
.

Moreover, this type of set was termed as a unique range set for entire functions. Later, similar definition for meromorphic functions was also introduced in the literature.

Definition 1.1
.

(
[
16
]
)
Let
S\subset\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
;
f
and
g
be two nonconstant meromorphic (resp. entire) functions. If
E_{f}(S)=E_{g}(S)
implies
f\equiv g
, then
S
is called a unique range set for meromorphic (resp. entire) functions or in brief URSM (resp. URSE).

The introduction of the unique range set for meromophic function (URSM in brief) influenced many researchers to to find finite URSM’s (e.g., see,
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
etc.)

2.
Functions sharing two sets

In continuation with the notion of unique range sets, in 2003, the following question was asked by Lin and Yi in (
[

Can one find two finite sets

E_{f}(S_{j})=E_{g}(S_{j})

To answer the Question

In this direction, in 2012, B. Yi and Y. H. Li (
[

(with

and

.

.

(
[

Suppose

\alpha_{i}\not=\alpha_{j}

(2.2)

Is it possible to take

In (
[

Theorem 2.2
.

[

Let

be a strong uniqueness polynomial of the form (

But, in literature, the examples of strong uniqueness polynomials are few. Thus the motivation of this paper is to present some uniqueness theorems which show how two meromorphic functions are uniquely determined by their two shared sets.

Let us consider the following polynomial which was introduced in (
[
3
]
) by A. Banerjee and B. Chakraborty.

(2.4)

P(z):=Q(z)+c:=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}\binom{m}{i}\binom{n}{j}\frac{(-1)^{i+j}}{n+m+1-i-j}z^{n+m+1-i-j}a^{j}b^{i}+c,

where

be two complex numbers such that

,

and

The above mentioned polynomial is the generalization of the unique range set generating polynomial introduced in (
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
). Clearly

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}\binom{m}{i}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{i+j}z^{m+n-i-j}a^{j}b^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}z^{m-i}b^{i}\big)\big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{j}z^{n-j}a^{j}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(z-b)^{m}(z-a)^{n}

Thus by the assumptions on
c
,
P(z)
has only simple zeros.

Next, we can write

P(z)-P(b)=(z-b)^{m+1}R_{1}(z)
where
R_{1}(b)\not=0
, and

P(z)-P(a)=(z-a)^{n+1}R_{2}(z)
where
R_{2}(a)\not=0
.

If
P(a)=P(b)
, then
(z-b)^{m+1}R_{1}(z)=(z-a)^{n+1}R_{2}(z)
, i.e.,
R_{1}(z)
has a factor
(z-a)^{n+1}
which implies the polynomial

is of degree at least
m+1+n+1
, a contradiction. Thus
P(a)\not=P(b)
.

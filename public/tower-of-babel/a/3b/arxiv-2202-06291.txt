1.
Introduction

All rings are commutative noetherian with unity and all projective modules are finitely generated of constant rank.

Let
A
be a ring of dimension
d
and
P
be a projective
A[T]
-module of rank
n
. We say that
p\in P
is a unimodular element if there exist
\phi\in Hom(P,A)
such that
\phi(p)=1
. We write
Um(P)
for the set of unimodular elements of
P
. When
n>d
, then Plumstead
[
17
]
proved that
Um(P)\neq\varnothing
. Further, there are well known examples in the case
n\leq d
with
Um(P)=\varnothing
. For example, let
d=2m
,
R=\mathbb{R}[X_{0},\ldots,X_{d}]/(X_{0}^{2}+\ldots+X_{d}^{2}-1)
,
\phi:R^{d+1}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow R
defined by
e_{i}\mapsto X_{i}

and
Q=ker(\phi)
. Then
Q
is stably free
R
-module of rank
d
and
Um(Q)=\varnothing
. Hence if
P=Q[T]
, then
Um(P)=\varnothing
. Thus we need some further conditions in the case
n\leq d
to get
Um(P)\neq\varnothing
.

Let
f\in A[T]
be a monic polynomial and
P_{f}=P\otimes A[T]_{f}
.
Roitman
[
20
, Lemma 10]
proved that if
A
is a local ring, then
Um(P_{f})\neq\varnothing
implies
Um(P)\neq\varnothing
.
He asked whether this result holds for arbitrary ring
A
.

Question 1.1

(Roitman’s question)
.

Let
A
be a ring of dimension
d
and
P
be a projective
A[T]
-module of rank
n\leq d
.
Let

be a monic polynomial such that
Um(P_{f})\neq\varnothing
. Does this imply
Um(P)\neq\varnothing
?

If
A
contains an infinite field
k
, then
an affirmative answer is given in the following cases.

(1)

Bhatwadekar
[
2
, Proposition 3.3]
for
n=2
, arbitrary
d
and
A
need not contain any field.

(2)

Bhatwadekar-Sridharan
[
4
, Theorem 3.4]
for
n=d
.

(3)

Bhatwadekar-Keshari
[
3
, Theorem 5.3]
for
2n\geq d+3
when
P
is extended from
A
.

(4)

Bhatwadekar-Keshari
[
3
, Corollary 5.4]
for
2n\geq d+3
when

is regular.

We will follow the proof of Bhatwadekar-Keshari

.

Let

(2)

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A
be a ring of dimension
d
containing an infinite field
k
and
P
be a projective
A[T]
-module of rank
n
with
2n\geq d+3
. Assume that the singular locus of Spec
(A)
is a closed set
V(\mathcal{J})
with ht
(\mathcal{J})\geq d-n+2
. Let
f\in A[T]
be a monic polynomial such that
Um(P_{f})\neq\varnothing
. Then
Um(P)\neq\varnothing
.

Proof

Since
Um(P_{f})\neq\varnothing
and
A
contains an infinite field
k
, by (
2.4
), we get a surjection
\Phi:P\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I
, where
I
is an ideal of
A[T]
of height
\geq n
containing a monic polynomial. If ht
\,I>n
, then
I=A[T]
. Hence
Um(P)\neq\varnothing
and we are done. So we assume ht
\,I=n
. The map
\Phi
induces two surjections
\phi:P\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I/(I^{2}T)
and
\overline{\Phi}:P/TP\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I(0)
.
Let
J=(I\cap A)\cap\mathcal{J}
and write

and
\widetilde{P}=P_{1+J}
.

Step 1.
We will show that
Um(\widetilde{P})\neq\varnothing
by
verifying the conditions of (
2.1
) for the ideal
JR
of
R
.

(1)

For any
s\in J
,
A_{s}
is a regular ring containing an infinite field
k
. Thus by Popescu
[
18
]
,
\widetilde{P}_{s}
is extended from
R_{s}
.

(2)

Since
n-1\geq d-n+2
, height of
J
is
\geq d-n+2
, so dim
(R/J)\leq d-(d-n+2)=n-2
. By Plumstead
[
17
]
,
\widetilde{P}/J\widetilde{P}
has a unimodular element.

(3)

Since dim
(R/J)\leq n-2
, any ideal of
(R/J)[T]
of height
\geq n-1
contains a monic polynomial in
T
.

(4)

EL(\widetilde{P}/(T,J)\widetilde{P})
acts transitively on
Um(\widetilde{P}/(T,J)\widetilde{P})
, by Bass cancellation theorem
[
1
]
.

(5)

By hypothesis, there exists a monic polynomial
f\in R[T]
such that
Um(\widetilde{P}_{f})\neq\varnothing
.

By (
2.1
),
Um(\widetilde{P})\rightarrow Um(\widetilde{P}/T\widetilde{P})
is surjective.
Since
\widetilde{P}/T\widetilde{P}
is a projective
R
-module of rank
n
and
J
is contained in the Jacobson radical of

with dim
(R/J)\leq n-2
, we get
Um(\widetilde{P}/T\widetilde{P})\neq\varnothing
. Therefore,
Um(\widetilde{P}=P_{1+J})\neq\varnothing
.

Step 2.
Write
P_{1+J}=Q\oplus A_{1+J}[T]
. By (
2.6
), the surjection

\phi\otimes A_{1+J}[T]:Q\oplus A_{1+J}[T]\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I_{1+J}/(I^{2}T)_{1+J}
can be lifted to a surjection

\Psi=(\psi,h(T)):Q\oplus A_{1+J}[T]\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I_{1+J}

such that
h(T)
is a monic polynomial in
A_{1+J}[T]
. The surjection
\Psi
induces the surjection

\overline{\Psi}:Q/TQ\oplus A_{1+J}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I(0)_{1+J}
. Hence
\overline{\Phi}\otimes A_{1+J}=\overline{\Psi}
. We can find
a\in J
such that if
b=1+a
, then there exist a projective
A_{b}[T]
-module
Q_{1}
with
h(T)\in A_{b}[T]
monic such that

(1)

Q_{1}\otimes A_{1+J}=Q
,

(2)

P_{b}=Q_{1}\oplus A_{b}[T]
,

(3)

\Psi=(\psi,h(T)):P_{b}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I_{b}
is a surjection,

(4)

\overline{\Phi}\otimes A_{b}=\Psi\otimes A_{b}[T]/(T)
.

Let
pr_{2}:Q_{1}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow A_{b}[T]\rightarrow A_{b}[T]
be the second projection. Since

,
I(0)_{a}=A_{a}
and hence
\overline{\Phi}_{a}:P_{a}/TP_{a}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow A_{a}
is a surjection. Further, since

is a regular ring containing a field

, by Popescu
[
18
]
,
(Q_{1})_{a}
is extended from

. Thus there exist a projective

Consider two unimodular elements

and
(pr_{2})_{a}
of

. Since

are isotopically connected. Since

is regular, by Popescu
[
18
]

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
be an affine
\overline{\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}}_{p}
-algebra of dimension
d\geq 2
and
I,L
be ideals of
A
with

. Let
P
be a projective

-module of rank

and
\phi:P\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I/L
be a surjection. Then
\phi
can be lifted to a surjection
\Phi:P\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I
.

Proof

If
R
is a ring and
Q
is a projective
R
-module, then
Um(Q)\neq\varnothing
if and only if
Um(Q_{red})\neq\varnothing
, where
Q_{red}=Q\otimes A_{red}
and
A_{red}=A/nil(A)
is the reduced ring. Therefore, it is enough to assume that the ring
A
is reduced. We will give the proof in steps.

Step 1.

If
\phi^{\prime}:P\rightarrow I
is a lift of
\phi
, then
\phi^{\prime}(P)+L=I
. There exist
c\in L
such that
c(1-c)\in\phi^{\prime}(P)
and
(\phi^{\prime}(P),c)=I.
By Swan Bertini’s theorem
[
25
, Theorem 1.3, 1.4]
, there exist
\psi\in P^{*}
such that ht
(\phi^{\prime}+c\psi)(P)_{c}=d
and
(A/(\phi^{\prime}+c\psi)(P))_{c}
is reduced. Replacing
\phi^{\prime}
with
\phi^{\prime}+c\psi
, we may assume that ht
(\phi^{\prime}(P)_{c})=d
and
(A/\phi^{\prime}(P))_{c}
is reduced. If
J=(\phi^{\prime}(P),1-c)
, then
\phi^{\prime}(P)=I\cap J=IJ
. Since
J_{c}=\phi^{\prime}(P)_{c}
and
(J,c)=A
, thus we get ht
J=
ht
J_{c}=d
and
A/J
is reduced. Hence

J
is a product of distinct maximal ideals, say
J=\prod_{1}^{n}\mathfrak{m}_{i}
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
’s are maximal ideals of
A.

The surjection
\phi^{\prime}:P\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow IJ
induces a surjection
\overline{\phi}^{\prime}:P/JP\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow J/J^{2}
. If
\mathfrak{p}\supset J
is a prime ideal, then
\overline{\phi}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{p}}
lifts to a surjection
P_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow J_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since
A
is a reduced ring, using prime avoidance lemma, we may assume that
J_{p}
is generated by a regular sequence of
d
elements. Consequently,
J
is a local complete intersection ideal.
Hence
J
is a product of distinct smooth maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}_{i}.
If
\mathcal{J}
is the ideal defining the singular locus of
A,
then
J+\mathcal{J}=A.

By Mohan Kumar-Murthy-Roy
[
15
, Theorem 3.6]
for
d\geq 3
and Murthy
[
16
, Corollary 3.4]
for
d=2
,
J
is a complete intersection ideal. By Mohan Kumar-Murthy-Roy
[
15
, Theorem 3.7]
for
d\geq 3
and Krishna-Srinivas
[
10
]
for
d=2
,
P
has a unimodular element, i.e.
P\simeq P^{\prime}\oplus Ap_{d}
with rank
P^{\prime}=d-1.
As
\phi^{\prime}
induces an isomorphism of
P/JP\simeq J/J^{2},
we have
\phi^{\prime}(p_{1}),\cdots,\phi^{\prime}(p_{d-1}),\phi^{\prime}(p_{d})
is a base for
J
modulo
J^{2}
for some
p_{1},\cdots,p_{d-1}\in P^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
denotes the image of the map
p_{1}\wedge p_{2}\ldots\wedge p_{d}:\wedge^{n}P^{*}\rightarrow A
. Then
\mathcal{O}/(J\cap\mathcal{O})\simeq A/J.
Thus
J+\mathcal{O}=A.
Since
J
is comaximal with
L,\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{J}
, if
\mathcal{T}=L\mathcal{O}\mathcal{J}
, then
J+\mathcal{T}=A.

Step 2.

We will prove the followings.

(1)

There exist
h\in J
with
h-1\in\mathcal{T}
such that
A/Ah
is a smooth of dimension
d-1.

(2)

If bar denote going modulo the ideal
(h)
, then
\overline{J}
is a complete intersection ideal of height

9.1.

R[T]
-module

Let
R
be a ring of dimension
d
containing a field
k
,

Assume the singular locus of

with

Let

be a stably free
\mathcal{R}
-module of rank
n
.
We will define the Euler class
e(P)\in E^{n}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})
of
P
and prove that
Um(P)\neq\varnothing
if and only if
e(P)=0
in
E^{n}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})
.

Let

and
Um_{r,n+r}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})
be the set of all
r\times(n+r)
matrices
\sigma
in

which has a right inverse, i.e. there exists
\tau\in M_{n+r,r}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})
such that

.
For any

we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}^{r}\rightarrow^{\sigma}\,\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}^{n+r}\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0

where
\sigma(v)=v\sigma
for
v\in\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}^{r}
and
P
is a stably free
\mathcal{R}
-module of rank
n.
Hence every element of
Um_{r,n+r}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})
corresponds to a stably free
\mathcal{R}
-module of rank
n
and conversely, any stably free
\mathcal{R}
-module
P
of rank
n
will give rise to an element of
Um_{r,n+r}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})
for some
r.
We will define a map

e:Um_{r,n+r}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})\rightarrow E^{n}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})

which is a natural generalization of the map
Um_{n+1}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})\rightarrow E^{n}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})
defined in
[
6
]
.
Let
\sigma
be an element of
Um_{r,n+r}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}),
then

\sigma=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1,1}&\cdots&a_{1,n+r}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{r,1}&\cdots&a_{r,n+r}\\
\end{bmatrix}

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n+r}
be the standard basis of
\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}^{n+r}
and let

P=\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}^{n+r}/\left(\sum^{n+r}_{i=1}a_{1,i}e_{i},\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{n+r}a_{r,i}e_{i}\right)\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}.

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n+r}
be the images of
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n+r}
respectively in
P.
Then

P=\sum_{i=1}^{n+r}\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}p_{i}~\mbox{ with relations }~\sum_{i=1}^{n+r}a_{1,i}p_{i}=0,\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{n+r}a_{r,i}p_{i}=0.

To the triple
(P,(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n+r}),\sigma),
we associate an element
e(P,(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n+r}),\sigma)
of
E^{n}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})
as follows:
Let
\lambda:P\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow J
be a generic surjection, i.e.
J\subset\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}
is an ideal of height
n.
Since
P\oplus\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}^{r}=\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}^{n+r}
and dim
\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}/J=d+1-n\leq n-2
, we get
P/JP
is a free
\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}/J

[
1
]
. Since

is surjective image of

is generated by
n

Let “bar” denote reduction modulo
J
. By Bass
[
1
]
, there exist
\Theta\in E_{n+r}(\overline{\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}})
such that

[\overline{a_{1,1}},\ldots,\overline{a_{1,n+r}}]\Theta=[1,0,\ldots,0]

i.e.
the first row of
\Theta^{-1}
is
[\overline{a_{1,1}},\ldots,\overline{a_{1,n+r}}]
. Let
\overline{\sigma}\circ\Theta
be given by

\overline{\sigma}\circ\Theta=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
\overline{b_{2,1}}&\overline{b_{2,2}}&\cdots&\overline{b_{2,n+r}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\overline{b_{r,1}}&\overline{b_{r,2}}&\cdots&\overline{b_{r,n+r}}\end{bmatrix}.

Note that
[\overline{b_{2,2}},\ldots,\overline{b_{2,n+r}}]\in Um_{n+r-1}(\overline{\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}}).
By Bass
[
1
]

\Theta_{1}\in E_{n+r-1}(\overline{\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}})

[\overline{b_{2,2}},\ldots,\overline{b_{2,n+r}}]\Theta_{1}=[1,0,\ldots,0].

If
\Phi\in E_{m}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})
, then
\begin{bmatrix}Id_{t}&0\\
0&\Phi\\
\end{bmatrix}\in E_{m+t}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})

\overline{b_{2,1}}&1&\cdots&0\\
\overline{b_{3,1}}&\overline{c_{3,2}}&\cdots&\overline{c_{3,n+r}}\\

\overline{b_{r,1}}&\overline{c_{r,2}}&\cdots&\overline{c_{r,n+r}}\\

\overline{b_{2,1}}&1&0&0&\cdots&0\\

\hfill\square

E_{n+r}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}/J^{\prime}),
then
(J,\omega_{J})=(J^{\prime},\omega_{J}^{\prime})\in E^{n}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}).

By Bhatwadekar-Sridharan
[
6
, Lemma 5.1]
, there exist an ideal
I\subset\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}[W]
of height
n
and a surjection
\alpha(W):P[W]\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I
such that
I(0)=J,\alpha(0)=\lambda
and
I(1)=J^{\prime},\alpha(1)=\lambda^{\prime}.
Let
N=(I\cap\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}\cap\mathcal{J})^{2}
.

Note that
\mathcal{J}\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}
is the ideal defining singular locus of
\mathcal{R}
. Since
ht
N\geq d-n+2
and dim
\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$}/N\leq d+1-(d-n+2)\leq n-1
, by Bass
[
1
]
,
P/NP
is free.

Using (
9.1
), rest of the proof of well definedness of
e(P,(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n+r}),\sigma)
is same as in
[
6
]
. We denote the element
e(P,(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n+r}),\sigma)
of
E^{n}(\mbox{$\mathcal{R}$})
by
e(P)
or
e(\sigma).

Following the above arguments for a stably free
R
-module
Q
, we get a well defined element
e(Q)
of
E^{n}(R)
.
Therefore, we have proved the following result.

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
R
be a ring of dimension
d
containing a field
k
and
n
be an integer with
2n\geq d+3.
Assume the singular locus of
Spec(R)
is a closed set
V(\mathcal{J})
with
ht\,\mathcal{J}\geq d-n+2.

Then we have well defined maps

e:Um_{r,n+r}(R)\to E^{n}(R),\hskip 36.135pte:Um_{r,n+r}(R[T])\to E^{n}(R[T])

In particular, given stably free
R
(resp.
R[T]
) module
Q
(resp.
P
) of rank
n
, we can associate an element
e(Q)\in E^{n}(R)
(resp.
e(P)\in E^{n}(R[T])
).

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
R
be a ring of dimension
d
containing a field
k
and
n
be an integer with

2n\geq d+3
. Assume
the singular locus of
Spec(R)
is a closed set
V(\mathcal{J})
with ht
\mathcal{J}\geq d-n+2
. Let
Q
(resp.
P
) be stably free
R
(resp.
R[T]
)-modules of rank
n
. Then

(1)

Um(Q)\neq\varnothing
if and only if
e(Q)=0\in E^{n}(R)
.

(2)

Assume
k
is infinite. Then
Um(P)\neq\varnothing
if and only if
e(P)=0\in E^{n}(R[T])
.

Proof

(1) Following the proof of Bhatwadekar-Sridharan
[
6
, Theorem 5.4]
.

(2) Assume
e(P)=0
. Then
e(P\otimes R(T))=0
in
E^{n}(R(T)).
By (1),
Um(P\otimes R(T))\neq\varnothing
. By (
3.1
),
Um(P)\neq\varnothing
.

Conversely, assume
Um(P)\neq\varnothing
. Let
e(P)=(I,\omega_{I})
for some height
n
ideal
I\subset R[T]
and
\omega_{I}:(R[T]/I)^{n}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I/I^{2}
a surjection. Since
R
contains an infinite field, by Bhatwadekar-Sridharan
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
, we may assume that either
I(0)=R
or
I(0)
is height
n
ideal of
R
.

When
I(0)=R
,
\omega_{I}
can be lifted to a surjection
\overline{\phi}:(R[T]/I)^{n}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I/I^{2}T
.

When
I(0)
has height
n
, then using
Um(P/TP)\neq\varnothing
and
e(P/TP)=(I(0),\omega_{I(0)})=0
in
E^{n}(R)
, by first part of these theorem. Thus by Bhatwadekar-Sridharan
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
, the surjection
\omega_{I(0)}:(R/I(0))^{n}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I(0)/I(0)^{2}
can be lifted to a surjection
\phi_{1}:R^{n}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I(0)
. Patching
\phi_{1}
and
\omega_{I}
, we get a surjection
\overline{\phi}:(R[T]/I)^{n}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I/I^{2}T
which is a lift of
\omega_{I}
.

Since
Um(P\otimes R(T))\neq\varnothing
and
dim\,R(T)=d
, by Bhatwadekar-Sridharan
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
,
\omega_{I}\otimes R(T)
can be lifted to a surjection
\Phi:R(T)^{n}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow IR(T)
which is also a lift of
\overline{\phi}
. By (
7.4
),
\overline{\phi}
has a surjective lift
\theta:R[T]^{n}\rightarrow\!\!\!\rightarrow I
which is also a lift of
\omega_{I}
. Thus
e(P)=(I,\omega_{I})=0
in
E^{n}(R[T])
.

\hfill\square

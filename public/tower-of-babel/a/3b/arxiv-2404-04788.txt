Abstract
. We study projections in the bidual of a C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra B B that are null with respect to a subalgebra A A , that is projections p ∈ B ∗ ⁣ ∗ p\in B^{**} satisfying | φ | ​ ( p ) = 0 |\varphi|(p)=0 for every φ ∈ B ∗ \varphi\in B^{*} annihilating A A . In the separable case, A A -null projections are precisely the peak projections in the bidual of A A at which the subalgebra A A interpolates the entire C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra B B . These are analogues of null sets in classical function theory, on which several profound results rely. This motivates the development of a noncommutative variant, which we use to find appropriate ‘quantized’ versions of some of these classical facts. Through a delicate generalization of a theorem of Varopoulos, we show that, roughly speaking, sufficiently regular interpolation projections are null precisely when their atomic parts are. As an application, we give alternative proofs and sharpenings of some recent peak interpolation results of Davidson and Hartz for algebras on Hilbert function spaces, also illuminating thereby how earlier noncommutative peak-interpolation theory may be applied. In another direction, given a convex subset of the state space of B B , we characterize when the associated Riesz projection is null. This is then applied to various important topics in noncommutative function theory, such as the F.& M. Riesz property, the existence of Lebesgue decompositions, the description of Henkin functionals, and Arveson’s noncommutative Hardy spaces (maximal subdiagonal algebras).

The current paper is a contribution to this general program. Herein we focus on a subclass of the projections in

We close this introduction by mentioning some notation and background facts. An operator algebra is a norm closed algebra of operators on a Hilbert space,
or equivalently a closed subalgebra of a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. We refer to

[

]
for their theory. We will use silently the fact from basic functional analysis that

is the weak* closure in

of

, and is isometric to

. For us a

is always an orthogonal projection. The bidual of an operator algebra

is an operator algebra, indeed is a von Neumann algebra
if

is selfadjoint.
We write

for
the characteristic function of a set

. In the
case that

in

may be viewed as an element of

in a natural way since

is a certain space of measures on

. Thus if

the open or closed projections are precisely the characteristic functions of open or closed sets.
Open projections arise naturally in functional analysis. For example, they come naturally out of the ‘spectral theorem/functional calculus’: the spectral projections in

B^{**}
of a self-adjoint operator

corresponding to open sets in the spectrum of

are open projections.
The nullspace of a normal state on a von Neumann algebra is closed under ‘joins’ and ‘meets’ of projections.

2.
Null projections

Let
B
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
M\subset B
be a subspace. Recall that a projection
p\in B^{**}
is
M
-null
if
|\varphi|(p)=0
for every

In this section, we collect some general results on null projections.
Before proceeding, we record an elementary inequality that will be used numerous times throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
B
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
\varphi\in B^{*}
. Let
p\in B^{**}
be a projection. Then, for every
x\in B^{**}
we have

|\varphi(px)|^{2}\leq\|\varphi\|\|x\|^{2}|\varphi|(p).

Proof.

Choose a partial isometry
v\in B^{**}
such that
\varphi=|\varphi|(\cdot v)
. Thus, by the Schwarz inequality we obtain that

|\varphi(px)|^{2}=||\varphi|(pxv)|^{2}\leq\|\varphi\||\varphi|(pxv(pxv)^{*})\leq\|\varphi\|\,\|x\|^{2}\,|\varphi|(p)

for
x\in B^{**}
. ∎

Recall that a closed projection
p\in B^{**}
is
compact
if there is a contraction
b\in B
such that
p=pb
. When
B
is unital, this is clearly the same notion as that of being closed.
We note that if
B
is a nonunital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and
A\subset B
be a norm-closed subalgebra, then the
generalized
A
-peak projections are the compact projections that happen to lie in
A^{\perp\perp}
. See
[
18
,
16
]
for this generalization of Glicksberg’s theorem.
Thus in this case our “Basic principle” stated in the introduction informs us that compact
A
-null projections are exactly the
generalized
A
-peak projections which are also interpolation projections.

The next development hinges on the following basic fact.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
B
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. Then, the compact projections in
B^{**}
span a weak-
*
dense subspace.

Proof.

Let
D\subset B^{**}
denote the subspace generated by all compact projections in
B^{**}
. Let
\varphi\in B^{*}
be a functional annihilating
D
. We claim that
\varphi=0
. To see this, it suffices to fix a positive element
b\in B
and to show that
\varphi(b)=0
. Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}
denote the spectrum of
b
.
We recall that

\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega\setminus\{0\})
is
*
-isomorphic to the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by
b
. Then there exists a regular Borel measure
\mu
on
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
such that

(1)

\varphi(f(b))=\int_{\Omega}fd\mu

for every
f\in\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega)
with
f(0)=0
.
If
K\subset\Omega\setminus\{0\}
is compact then
\chi_{K}(b)\in B^{**}
by functional calculus, and
\chi_{K}(b)
is closed with respect to the unitization of
B
, hence it
is a compact projection in
B^{**}
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
. Hence,
\chi_{K}(b)\in D
and
\varphi(\chi_{K}(b))=0
.
On the other hand, by a dominated convergence argument applied to a sequence of continuous functions decreasing to
\chi_{K}
and vanishing at
0
, we find by virtue of (

) that
\varphi(\chi_{K}(b))=\mu(K)
so that
\mu(K)=0
. By regularity of the measure

, we conclude that
\mu=0
, and thus
\varphi=0
.
∎

Proposition 2.3

Let

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
M\subset B
be a norm-closed subspace. Then, the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

M=B
.

(ii)

The unit of
B^{**}
is
M
-null.

(iii)

Any compact projection in
B^{**}
lies in
M^{\perp\perp}
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): By assumption, we see that
M^{\perp}=\{0\}
, so that, vacuously, the unit of
B^{**}
is
M
-null.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): Let
\varphi\in M^{\perp}
and

be a

denote the unit. Then,

so that

. This shows that

is

by Lemma

.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i): The assumption combined with Lemma

implies that

. In turn, by the Hahn–Banach theorem, we find

since

is norm-closed.
∎

A natural question that arises from Proposition
2.3
is whether there is a similar
characterization of nullity for proper projections, as opposed to the unit of
B^{**}
in that result.
As was communicated to us by Alexander Izzo, this is indeed the case for subalgebras of commutative
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, at least for Borel projections – this conversation with Izzo inspired several results in this section; see also the remark at the end of Section 6.

More precisely, let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and let
A\subset\mathrm{C}(X)
be a unital norm-closed subalgebra. Let
E\subset X
be a Borel measurable subset, and denote by
\chi_{E}\in\mathrm{C}(X)^{**}
its characteristic function. Then,
\chi_{E}
is
A
-null if and only if
\chi_{K}\in A^{\perp\perp}
for every closed subset
K\subset E
.
Our next goal is to establish such a characterization in the non-commutative context.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
B
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
M\subset B
be a norm-closed subspace. Let
p\in B^{**}
be a projection. Then, the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

pB^{**}\subset M^{\perp\perp}
.

(ii)

For every compact projection
q\in B^{**}
, the element
pq
lies in
M^{\perp\perp}
.

(iii)

The projection
p
is
M
-null.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): This is trivial.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): Fix
\varphi\in M^{\perp}
. By assumption,
\varphi(pq)=0
for every compact projection
q\in B^{**}
. By Lemma
2.2
, it follows that
\varphi(px)=0
for every
x\in B^{**}
. In particular,
|\varphi|(p)=0
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Let
\varphi\in M^{\perp}
.
Then
|\varphi|(p)=0
, so that
\varphi(px)=0
for each
x\in B^{**}
by Lemma
2.1
. We conclude that
pB^{**}\subset M^{\perp}
.
∎

When
B
is unital and commutative and
p
is closed, statement (ii) above is equivalent to the stipulation that every closed projection dominated by
p
lies in
M^{\perp\perp}
. Next, we give an alternate version of Proposition
2.4
that is more aligned with this formulation. For this purpose, we require some ideas from
[
20
]
.

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
q\in B^{**}
be a closed projection. Denote by
B_{q}\subset B
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of elements in
B
commuting with
q
. We say that a projection
r\in B_{q}^{\perp\perp}
is
closed with respect to
B_{q}
if there is a net
(b_{i})
of positive contractions in
B_{q}
decreasing to
r
in the weak-
*
topology of
B^{**}
.
Equivalently, using the weak-
*
homeomorphic
*
-isomorphism
B_{q}^{**}\cong B_{q}^{\perp\perp}
, this means that

is closed when viewed as an element of
B_{q}^{**}
. It is easily verified that

itself lies in
B_{q}^{\perp\perp}
, and it is closed with respect to

(see for instance
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
).
Indeed a projection

is closed with respect to

if and only if it is closed with respect to

.

Next, let
A\subset B
be a norm-closed subalgebra and put
A_{q}=A\cap B_{q}
. Then,
A_{q}
may be viewed as a subalgebra of both
B^{**}
and
B_{q}^{**}
. Via the isomorphism
B_{q}^{**}\cong B_{q}^{\perp\perp}
, the double annihilator of
A_{q}
inside of
B_{q}^{**}
and its double annihilator inside of

are identified with one another. In what follows, we will reserve the notation
A_{q}^{\perp\perp}
for the double annihilator of
A_{q}
inside of
B^{**}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, and let
A\subset B
be a unital norm-closed subalgebra.
Let
q\in B^{**}
be a closed projection lying in
A^{\perp\perp}
.
Then, the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

Every closed subprojection
p
of
q
in

B_{q}^{\perp\perp}
is a generalized
A_{q}
-peak projection (that is,

p\in A_{q}^{\perp\perp}
).

(ii)

q
is
A_{q}
-null with respect to
B_{q}

(that is,
|\varphi|(q)=0
for all
\varphi\in(A_{q})^{\perp}
).

(iii)

q
is
A_{q}
-interpolating with respect to
B_{q}

(that is,
qA_{q}=qB_{q}
).

Proof.

By Lemma 2.1 in
[
20
]
we automatically have

.
Then the equivalence of (ii) and (iii) follows from the
“Basic principle” stated in the introduction.
Note that by definition of
B_{q}
the projection

commutes with the elements here.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
Let
\varphi:B_{q}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a continuous linear functional annihilating

. Let

be a partial isometry such that
|\varphi|=\varphi(\cdot v)
. The goal is to show that
\varphi(qv)=0
. By virtue of Lemma

, it suffices to show that
\varphi(qp)=0
for every closed projection

. Any such projection

necessarily commutes with

in

is closed in

[

, Theorem II.7]
and is dominated by

as a closed subprojection of

, the assumption gives

, so that as a projection in

,

. In particular,

as desired.

(ii)
\Rightarrow

be any projection lying in

and dominated by

. Then,

may be viewed as a projection in

dominated by

. Our assumption then implies that

for every

, we infer that

for all such functionals

inside of

∎

3.
Measurably indistinguishable projections

This short section contains a preliminary discussion on some non-commutative topological notions for projections. These are used in the subsequent section.

We start with a motivating discussion. Given a topological space
X
satisfying the
T_{1}
separation axiom and distinct subsets
E,F\subset X
, there always exists an open subset
U\subset X
containing
E
but not containing
F
. In Akemann’s non-commutative topology for projections, more diverse phenomena can arise, even in commutative contexts.

Example 1
.

Let
{\mathbb{T}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
denote the unit circle and let
B=\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}})
. For each
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
, consider the closed projection
p_{t}=\chi_{\{t\}}\in B^{**}
. Let
\lambda\in B^{*}
denote the state of integration against arc length measure.
Let
e\in B^{**}
be the projection satisfying

(1-e)B^{**}=\{x\in B^{**}:\lambda(x^{*}x)=0\}.

Since
\{t\}
has measure zero, we see that
\lambda(p_{t})=0
so that
p_{t}\leq 1-e
for each
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
, and thus
\bigvee_{t\in{\mathbb{T}}}p_{t}\leq 1-e
.
Fix a Borel measurable subset
E\subset{\mathbb{T}}
with non-zero measure. We clearly have
\bigvee_{t\in E}p_{t}\leq\chi_{E}
. On the other hand,
\chi_{E}(\lambda)>0
so
\chi_{E}
is not dominated by
1-e
, and in particular,
\chi_{E}\neq\bigvee_{t\in E}p_{t}
.

Nevertheless, the projections
\chi_{E}
and
\bigvee_{t\in E}p_{t}
are nearly identical: their differences cannot be detected by means of certain Borel projections. More precisely, let
F\subset{\mathbb{T}}
be a Borel subset. Assume that
\bigvee_{t\in E}p_{t}\leq\chi_{F}
, so that
\chi_{\{t\}}\leq\chi_{F}
and
t\in F
for each
t\in E
. This means that
E\subset F
, or
\chi_{E}\leq\chi_{F}
. In other words, while
\bigvee_{t\in E}p_{t}
and
\chi_{E}
are distinct projections, they are dominated by precisely the same projections arising from Borel subsets.
∎

Let
B
be a

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. By considering the usual universal representation of
B
, we find a Hilbert space
H_{u}
such that
B^{**}\subset B(H_{u})
.
As in 2.4.1 in
[
37
]
let
(B_{\text{sa}})^{m}\subset B^{**}
denote the set of elements in
B^{**}
that can be obtained as the increasing limit in the SOT of
B(H_{u})
of a self-adjoint net from
B
. A self-adjoint element
x\in B^{**}
is said to be
universally measurable
if, for every
\varepsilon>0
and state
\varphi
on
B
, there are elements
y,z\in(B_{\text{sa}})^{m}
such that
-y\leq x\leq z
and
\varphi(y+z)<\varepsilon
.

Projections in
B^{**}
that are either open or closed are universally measurable
[
37
, Proposition 3.11.9]
. More generally, the class of universally measurable projections also contains the Borel projections in the sense of
[
30
]
; see
[
21
]
for more detail.

Let
p,q\in B^{**}
be two projections. We say that
p
and
q
are
measurably indistinguishable
if they are dominated by the same universally measurable projections in
B^{**}
. We will be concerned with the issue of measurable indistinguishability between a projection and the supremum of all minimal projections that it dominates. This is best framed using the following device.

Let
z_{\operatorname{at}}\in B^{**}
denote the supremum of all minimal projections in
B^{**}
. It is well known that
z_{\operatorname{at}}
is central: indeed, it coincides with the support projection for the unique weak-
*
continuous extension to
B^{**}
of the
atomic representation
of
B

[
37
, Paragraph 4.3.7]
. In particular, by virtue of
[
37
, Lemma 4.3.8]
, we see that there is a collection
\{H_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
of Hilbert spaces such that
z_{\operatorname{at}}B^{**}\cong\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}B(H_{\lambda})
. Consequently, if
p\in B^{**}
is a projection, we have

(2)

pz_{\operatorname{at}}=\bigvee\{r:r\text{ minimal projection in }B^{**},r\leq p\}.

Henceforth, we will refer to
pz_{\operatorname{at}}
as the
atomic part
of
p
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Universally measurable projections in
B^{**}
are measurably indistinguishable from their atomic parts.

Proof.

Let
p,e\in B^{**}
be universally measurable projections with
e\geq pz_{\operatorname{at}}
. Since
z_{\operatorname{at}}
is central, we find
(e-p)z_{\operatorname{at}}\geq 0
. In turn, because
e-p
is universally measurable
[
37
, Proposition 4.3.13]
, we infer that
e\geq p
by virtue of
[
37
, Theorem 4.3.15]
.
∎

Let us further analyze the case of closed projections, as this is what we will focus on in the next section. The previous proposition can be interpreted as saying that, roughly speaking, a closed projection in
B^{**}
essentially coincides with the supremum of all the minimal projections that it dominates. Of course, as illustrated in Example
1
, this is not strictly true. Nevertheless, the following does hold.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
q\in B^{**}
be a closed projection. Let
C\subset B^{**}
denote the collection of all closed projections dominating the same minimal projections as
q
does. Then,
q=\bigwedge_{r\in C}r
.

Proof.

Since
z_{\operatorname{at}}
is central, we have
q\geq qz_{\operatorname{at}}
, so that
q\in C
and
q\geq\bigwedge_{r\in C}r
. Conversely, fix
r\in C
and an open projection
u\in B^{**}
with
u\geq r
. Then,
u\geq qz_{\operatorname{at}}
by (
2
), so by Proposition
3.1
, it follows that
u\geq q
. Since this holds for every such open projection
u
, applying
[
35
, Proposition 2.3]
, we find that
q\leq r
. We conclude that
q\leq\bigwedge_{r\in C}r
, so in fact
q=\bigwedge_{r\in C}r
as desired.
∎

4.
The non-commutative Varopoulos phenomenon

We begin by revisiting Example
1
to illustrate that a closed projection may have a null atomic part without being null itself, despite both projections being measurably indistinguishable by Proposition
3.1
.

Example 2
.

Let
{\mathbb{T}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
denote the unit circle, let
B=\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}})
. For each
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
, consider the closed projection
p_{t}=\chi_{\{t\}}\in B^{**}
. Let
\lambda\in B^{*}
denote the state of integration against arc length measure. Let
K\subset{\mathbb{T}}
be a closed subset with positive measure. The atomic part of the closed projection
\chi_{K}\in B^{**}
is easily seen to be
\bigvee_{t\in K}p_{t}
by (
2
).

Next, let
A\subset B
denote the disc algebra, i.e. the norm-closed subalgebra generated by analytic polynomials. It is well known that each point of
{\mathbb{T}}
is an
A
-peak point, so that
p_{t}
is
A
-null for each
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
. It follows that
\bigvee_{t\in E}p_{t}
is also
A
-null. On the other hand,
\lambda(\chi_{K})>0
, and hence
\chi_{K}
is not an
A
-null
[
41
, Theorem 10.1.2]

and not in
A^{\perp\perp}
, so that
K
is not a peak set.
∎

The goal of this section is to exhibit conditions under which such a pathology cannot occur. This will be accomplished by proving a non-commutative analogue of Varopoulos’ theorem
[
41
, Theorem 10.2.2]
. First, we record a topological preliminary.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
q\in B^{**}
be a closed projection. Let
\{u_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
be a collection of open projections in
B^{**}
that commute with
q
and satisfy
q\leq\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}u_{\lambda}
. Then, there is a finite set
F\subset\Lambda
such that
q\leq\bigvee_{\lambda\in F}u_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

By assumption, we see that
q\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(1-u_{\lambda})\right)=0
. Invoking
[
2
, Proposition II.10]
, we find a finite subset
F\subset\Lambda
such that
q\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{\lambda\in F}(1-u_{\lambda})\right)=0
or
q\wedge\left(1-\bigvee_{\lambda\in F}u_{\lambda}\right)=0
. Equivalently, using the commutation assumption, we see that
q\leq\bigvee_{\lambda\in F}u_{\lambda}
as desired.
∎

The previous result is stated to hold with no requirement of commutativity in
[
35
]
, but this is not valid in general.
Thankfully, the main claims of that paper appear to be unaffected by this.

We also need the following technical fact. Given a vector space
X
, we denote by
{\mathbb{M}}_{m,n}(X)
the space of
m\times n
matrices with entries in
X
. When
m=n
, we simply write
{\mathbb{M}}_{n}(X)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
B
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, let
q\in B^{**}
be a closed projection and let
n\geq 1
. Let
R\in{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}(qB^{**})
and
C\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n,1}(B^{**}q)
. Fix
\Delta\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}(B)
, a functional
\varphi\in B^{*}
and a number
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there are
S\in{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}(qB)
,
T\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n,1}(Bq)
such that
\|S\|\leq\|R\|
,
\|T\|\leq\|C\|
and

|\varphi(R\Delta C-S\Delta T)|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
J=B\cap(1-q)B^{**}
. Consider the map
\Phi:B/J\to qB
defined as

\Phi(b+J)=qb,\quad b\in B.

By
[
35
, Proposition 3.1]
, it follows that
\Phi
is a completely isometric isomorphism. Hence,
\Phi^{**}:(B/J)^{**}\to(qB)^{**}
is a completely isometric weak-
*
homeomorphism. In turn, by
[
15
, Paragraph 1.4.4]
, there is a completely isometric weak-
*
homeomorphism
\Psi:(B/J)^{**}\to B^{**}/J^{\perp\perp}
such that

\Psi(b+J)=b+J^{\perp\perp},\quad b\in B.

Next, since
q
is closed,
and is the support projection of

[
2
]
,
we obtain that
J^{\perp\perp}=(1-q)B^{**}
. Hence, the map
\Theta:B^{**}/J^{\perp\perp}\to qB^{**}
defined as

\Theta(x+J^{\perp\perp})=qx,\quad x\in B^{**}

is also a completely isometric weak-
*
homeomorphism. Therefore, we obtain a completely isometric weak-
*
homeomorphism
\Omega=\Theta\circ\Psi\circ(\Phi^{**})^{-1}:(qB)^{**}\to qB^{**}
acting as the inclusion on

.

Invoke now
[
15
, Theorem 1.4.11]
to find an isometric isomorphism

\Gamma:{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}(qB)^{**}\to{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}((qB)^{**})

acting as the inclusion on
{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}(qB)
. Then, the map
\Xi=\Omega^{(n)}\circ\Gamma:{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}(qB)^{**}\to{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}(qB^{**})
is an isometric weak-
*
homeomorphism acting as the inclusion on
{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}(qB)
.
Consider the weak-
*
continuous linear functional
\psi
on
{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}(qB)^{**}
defined as

\psi(X)=\varphi(\Xi(X)\Delta C),\quad X\in{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}(qB)^{**}.

By Goldstine’s lemma
(namely that the unit ball of a Banach space is weak* dense in the ball of the bidual), we can find

with
\|S_{0}\|\leq\|\Xi^{-1}(R)\|=\|R\|
such that

|\psi(S_{0}-\Xi^{-1}(R))|<\varepsilon/2.

Put

, which then satisfies
\|S\|\leq\|R\|
and

(3)

|\varphi(S\Delta C-R\Delta C)|<\varepsilon/2.

To find

, we argue in a similar fashion. First, proceed as above to find an isometric weak-
*
homeomorphism
\Xi^{\prime}:{\mathbb{M}}_{n,1}(Bq)^{**}\to{\mathbb{M}}_{n,1}(B^{**}q)
acting as the inclusion on
{\mathbb{M}}_{n,1}(Bq)
. Consider the weak-
*
continuous linear functional
\psi^{\prime}
on
{\mathbb{M}}_{n,1}(Bq)^{**}
defined as

\psi^{\prime}(X)=\varphi(S\Delta\Xi^{\prime}(X)),\quad X\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n,1}(Bq)^{**}.

By Goldstine’s lemma, we can find

with
\|T_{0}\|\leq\|(\Xi^{\prime})^{-1}C\|=\|C\|
such that

|\psi^{\prime}(T_{0}-(\Xi^{\prime})^{-1}(C))|<\varepsilon/2.

Put

, which then satisfies

and

(4)

|\varphi(S\Delta T-S\Delta C)|<\varepsilon/2.

Finally, combining (

) and (

|\varphi(R\Delta C-S\Delta T)|<\varepsilon

as desired.
∎

Let
B
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let

be a subalgebra. A projection

is said to be

if

for each

. Further,

is said to be

if

is

-invariant. We can now state and prove one of the main results of the paper.

Let

be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let

be a unital norm-closed subalgebra. Let

be a projection with the following properties:

(i)

is closed,

(ii)

there is

for which

is a complete

-interpolation projection,

(iii)

commutes with

, and

(iv)

the atomic part of

.

|\varphi|(q)\leq\kappa^{3}\sqrt{|\varphi|(1)\,|\varphi|(u-q)}.

Let

. We may assume without loss of generality that

, so that

is a state. Throughout, we put

. There is a partial isometry

and

Because

-interpolation projection, there is

. In particular, we have

Let

be the set of minimal projections in

dominated by

for every

to find a contraction
a_{\lambda}\in A
such that
a_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}=p_{\lambda}
and

(6)

\|(1-u)a_{\lambda}\|<\varepsilon.

Consider the open projection
f_{\lambda}=\chi_{[0,\varepsilon)}(|1-a_{\lambda}|)\in B^{**}
. Note that
q
commutes with
A
, and hence with
f_{\lambda}
as well.
Since
\|a_{\lambda}\|=1
and
a_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}=p_{\lambda}
, we infer
|1-a_{\lambda}|p_{\lambda}=0
and thus

(1-f_{\lambda})p_{\lambda}=\chi_{[\varepsilon,1]}(|1-a_{\lambda}|)p_{\lambda}=0.

Therefore,
p_{\lambda}\leq f_{\lambda}
.
It follows that the open projection
\bigvee_{\lambda}f_{\lambda}
dominates
\bigvee_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}
, and hence it also dominates
q
by Proposition
3.1
. We may apply Lemma
4.1
to find finitely many indices
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
such that
q\leq f_{\lambda_{1}}\vee\ldots\vee f_{\lambda_{n}}
.

Put
e_{1}=f_{\lambda_{1}}
, and for
2\leq j\leq n
, let
e_{j}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{j}f_{\lambda_{i}}-\bigvee_{i=1}^{j-1}f_{\lambda_{i}}
. It follows that the collection
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is pairwise orthogonal and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{n}=\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}f_{\lambda_{j}}
.
Invoking
[

, Proposition V.1.6]
, we find for each
2\leq j\leq n
a partial isometry
v_{j}\in B^{**}
such that
v_{j}^{*}v_{j}=e_{j}
and
v_{j}v_{j}^{*}=e^{\prime}_{j}
, where

e_{j}^{\prime}=f_{\lambda_{j}}-\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^{j-1}f_{\lambda_{i}}\right)\wedge f_{\lambda_{j}}.

For convenience, put
v_{1}=1
and
e_{1}^{\prime}=e_{1}
.
We compute, for
1\leq j\leq n
, that

\displaystyle v_{j}^{*}a_{\lambda_{j}}v_{j}e_{j}-e_{j}

\displaystyle=v_{j}^{*}a_{\lambda_{j}}v_{j}v_{j}^{*}v_{j}-v_{j}^{*}v_{j}v_{j}^{*}v_{j}

\displaystyle=v_{j}^{*}(a_{\lambda_{j}}e^{\prime}_{j}-e^{\prime}_{j})v_{j}

whence

\|v_{j}^{*}a_{\lambda_{j}}v_{j}e_{j}-e_{j}\|\leq\|(a_{\lambda_{j}}-1)e_{j}^{\prime}\|\leq\||1-a_{\lambda_{j}}|f_{\lambda_{j}}\|

since
e^{\prime}_{j}\leq f_{\lambda_{j}}
.
We conclude, for each
1\leq j\leq n
, that

(7)

\|v_{j}^{*}a_{\lambda_{j}}v_{j}e_{j}-e_{j}\|\leq\varepsilon.

Consider
V=\begin{bmatrix}qe_{1}v_{1}^{*}&\ldots&qe_{n}v_{n}^{*}\end{bmatrix}
and
E=\begin{bmatrix}qe_{1}&\ldots&qe_{n}\end{bmatrix}
as elements in
{\mathbb{M}}_{1,n}(qB^{**})
. We find

VV^{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}qe_{j}v_{j}^{*}v_{j}e_{j}q=qEE^{*}q

and

EE^{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}qe_{j}q=q\left(\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}f_{\lambda_{j}}\right)q=q

whence
\|V\|=\|E\|=1
.
If we set
\Delta=\operatorname{diag}(a_{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,a_{\lambda_{n}})
, then

\displaystyle V\Delta V^{*}-q

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}qe_{j}v_{j}^{*}a_{\lambda_{j}}v_{j}e_{j}q-\sum_{j=1}^{n}qe_{j}q

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}qe_{j}(v_{j}^{*}a_{\lambda_{j}}v_{j}e_{j}-e_{j})e_{j}q

\displaystyle=E\operatorname{diag}(v_{j}^{*}a_{\lambda_{j}}v_{j}e_{j}-e_{j})E^{*}.

By (
7
) we infer

(8)

\|V\Delta V^{*}-q\|\leq\varepsilon.

By Lemma
4.2
, we may now choose
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in B
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\in B
such that, upon setting

S=\begin{bmatrix}s_{1}&\ldots&s_{n}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad T=\begin{bmatrix}t_{1}\\
\vdots\\
t_{n}\end{bmatrix}

we find

\|qS\|\leq\|V\|=1\quad\text{and}\quad\|Tq\|\leq\|V^{*}\|=1

along with

(9)

\left|\tau\left((V\Delta V^{*}-qS\Delta Tq)xy^{*}\right)\right|<\varepsilon.

Since
q
is a complete
(A,\kappa)
-interpolation projection, we can find
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in A
along with
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\in A
such that, if we put

R=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}&\ldots&\alpha_{n}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad C=\begin{bmatrix}\beta_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\beta_{n}\end{bmatrix},

then

(10)

qS=qR\quad\text{and}\quad Tq=Cq

while

\|R\|\leq\kappa\|qS\|\leq\kappa\quad\text{and}\quad\|C\|\leq\kappa\|Tq\|\leq\kappa.

We define
d=R\Delta C=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}a_{\lambda_{i}}\beta_{i}\in A
and note that

(11)

\|d\|\leq\|R\|\|\Delta\|\|C\|\leq\kappa^{2}.

Since
u
is
A
-invariant, we find
(1-u)d=(1-u)\sum_{j=1}\alpha_{j}(1-u)a_{\lambda_{j}}\beta_{j}
so

\|(1-u)d\|\leq\|R\|\|C\|\|\operatorname{diag}((1-u)a_{\lambda_{j}})\|.

Using (
6
), we thus find

(12)

\|(1-u)d\|\leq\kappa^{2}\varepsilon.

On the other hand, using that
q
commutes with
A
we may write
qd=qdq
, and by (
8
),(
9
) and (
10
) we find that

\displaystyle|\tau((qd-q)xy^{*})|

\displaystyle=|\tau((qdq-q)xy^{*})|=|\tau((qR\Delta Cq-q)xy^{*})|

\displaystyle=|\tau((qS\Delta Tq-q)xy^{*})|<\varepsilon+|\tau((V\Delta V^{*}-q)xy^{*})|

\displaystyle<(1+\|x\|)\varepsilon

whence

(13)

|\tau((qd-q)xy^{*})|<(1+\kappa)\varepsilon.

Note that
dx\in A
so that
\varphi(dx)=0
since
\varphi\in A^{\perp}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\varphi(qx)

\displaystyle=\varphi((q-d)x)=\tau((q-d)xy^{*})

\displaystyle=\tau((q-qd)xy^{*})-\tau((u-q)dxy^{*})-\tau((1-u)dxy^{*})

so by Lemma

Combined with (

\displaystyle\tau(q)

\displaystyle<\varepsilon+|\varphi(qx)|\leq\varepsilon+(1+\kappa)\varepsilon+\kappa^{3}\tau(u-q)^{1/2}+\kappa^{3}\varepsilon.

Letting

∎

4.1.
Atomic parts and double annihilators

Theorem
4.3
makes it advantageous to determine when the atomic part of a projection lies in the double annihilator of a subalgebra. As far as we know, there are no available general criteria guaranteeing this. We thus set out to identify a class of examples where this property is satisfied.

Our class of examples will involve tensor products. We recall some relevant facts here, since several preliminaries are needed.
Let
B
and
D
be
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras such that
B
is nuclear. It follows from
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
that there is an injective
*
-homomorphism
\sigma:B^{**}\otimes_{\min}D^{**}\to(B\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
with the following properties.

(i)

\sigma(b\otimes d)=b\otimes d
for each
b\in B
and
d\in D
.

(ii)

Let
\sigma_{B}:B^{**}\to(B\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
and
\sigma_{D}:D^{**}\to(B\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
be the restriction
*
-homomorphisms arising from
\sigma
(see for instance
[
22
, Theorem 3.2.6]
). Then,
\sigma_{B}
and
\sigma_{D}
are both weak-
*
continuous.

Via this map
\sigma
, we will view
B^{**}\otimes_{\min}D^{**}
as a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
(B\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
. In particular, given projections
q\in B^{**}
and
r\in D^{**}
,
q\otimes r
is a projection in
(B\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
. For the following, recall that if
A\subset B
is a subalgebra, then

A\otimes_{\min}D\subset B\otimes_{\min}D

by injectivity of the minimal tensor product.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
B
and
D
be

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras such that
B
is nuclear. Let
q\in B^{**}
and
r\in D^{**}
be projections, with
r

open or closed. Let
A\subset B
be a
closed subalgebra and assume that
q\in A^{\perp\perp}
. Then,
q\otimes r\in(A\otimes D)^{\perp\perp}
.

Proof.

We first assume that
r
is open.
Let
(d_{j})
be a net of positive contractions in
D
increasing to
r
in the weak-
*
topology of
D^{**}
. Then
(\sigma_{D}(d_{j}))
increases to
\sigma_{D}(r)
in the weak-
*
topology of
(B\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
.
In the universal representation of
B\otimes_{\min}D
, this implies that in fact
(\sigma_{D}(d_{j}))
increases to
\sigma_{D}(r)
in the strong operator topology. On the other hand,
by Goldstine’s lemma there is a net
(a_{i})
of contractions in
A
converging to
q
in the weak-
*
topology of
B^{**}
. We see that the net
(\sigma_{B}(a_{i})\sigma_{D}(d_{j}))_{(i,j)}
converges to
\sigma(q\otimes r)
in the weak-
*
topology of
(B\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
,
being the product of a
bounded weak-
*
converging net and a bounded strongly converging net. Each
\sigma_{B}(a_{i})\sigma_{D}(d_{j})=\sigma(a_{i}\otimes d_{j})
lies in the image of
A\otimes D
inside of
(B\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
, and therefore
q\otimes r\in(A\otimes_{\min}D)^{\perp\perp}
.

If
r
is closed, then by the above
q\otimes(1-r)
lies in
(A\otimes_{\min}D)^{\perp\perp}
, and hence so does
q\otimes r
.
∎

Before proving the final preliminary result, recall that for a projection
p\in B^{**}
, the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

The projection
p
is minimal in
B^{**}
.

(ii)

pB^{**}p={\mathbb{C}}p
.

(iii)

There is a pure state
\omega
on
B
such that
\omega(b)p=pbp
for each
b\in B
.

Using this equivalence, it is easily verified that if
p\in B^{**}
and
q\in D^{**}
are minimal projections, then the projection
p\otimes q
is also minimal in
(B\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
B
and
D
be
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras such that
B
is commutative. Let
p\in(B\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
be a minimal projection. Then, there are minimal projections
e\in B^{**},f\in D^{**}
such that
p=e\otimes f
.

Proof.

There is a pure state
\omega
on
B\otimes_{\min}D
with support projection equal to
p
. By
[
43
, Theorem IV.4.14]
, there are pure states
\varphi
of
B
and
\psi
of
D
such that
\omega=\varphi\otimes\psi
. Let
e\in B^{**}
and
f\in D^{**}
denote the support projections of
\varphi
and
\psi
, respectively. These are both minimal, so that
e\otimes f
is minimal as well. On the other hand, we have
\omega(e\otimes f)=1
, so
e\otimes f\geq p
, which then forces
p=e\otimes f
.
∎

We now arrive at our class of examples where all projections have their atomic parts in the double annihilator of a subalgebra.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and let
A\subset\mathrm{C}(X)
be a unital norm-closed subalgebra separating the points of
X
. Assume that
X
is the Choquet boundary of
A
. Let
D
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. Then, every minimal projection in
(\mathrm{C}(X)\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
lies in
(A\otimes_{\min}D)^{\perp\perp}
. In particular, the atomic part of any projection in
(\mathrm{C}(X)\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
lies in
(A\otimes_{\min}D)^{\perp\perp}
.

Proof.

Let
q\in(\mathrm{C}(X)\otimes_{\min}D)^{**}
be a minimal projection. By Lemma
4.5
, there are minimal projections
e\in\mathrm{C}(X)^{**}
and
f\in D^{**}
such that
q=e\otimes f
.
As is well-known,
e
is simply the characteristic function of a point
x\in X
. By
[
33
, Theorem II.11.3]
,
we see that
x\in X
is an
A
-peak point, since it lies in the Choquet boundary. Hence
e\in A^{\perp\perp}
, so by Lemma
4.4
, we conclude that

q\in(A\otimes_{\min}D)^{\perp\perp}
. This proves the first statement. The second statement immediately follows from the first along with (
2
).
∎

4.2.
Recovering Varopoulos’ null projections

We identify special cases where the conclusion of Theorem
4.3
can be strengthened to recover the fact that
q
is
A
-null, as in Varopoulos’ original result. More precisely, we impose a commutation condition between
A
and the net approximating the closed projection
q
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
A\subset B
be a unital norm-closed subalgebra. Let
q\in B^{**}
be a projection with the following properties:

(i)

there is a net
(b_{i})
of positive contractions in
B
commuting with
A
and decreasing to
q
in the weak-
*
topology of
B^{**}
,

(ii)

there is
\kappa>0
for which
q
is a complete
(A,\kappa)
-interpolation projection, and

(iii)

the atomic part of
q
lies in
A^{\perp\perp}
.

Then,
q
is
A
-null.

Proof.

First note that assumption (i) implies that
q
is closed and commutes with
A
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\varphi\in A^{\perp}
with
\|\varphi\|=1
. Let
C\subset B
denote the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra generated by
1
and the elements
b_{i}
. Hence
A
and
C
commute. By virtue of
[
2
, Proposition II.3]
(and its proof), there is an open projection
u\in B^{**}
lying in
C^{\perp\perp}
such that
|\varphi|(u-q)<\varepsilon
. In particular,
u
commutes with
A
, and hence is
A
-invariant.
Theorem
4.3
then gives
|\varphi|(q)\leq\kappa^{3}\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, and the proof is complete upon letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

Next, we show how the previous result implies Varopoulos’ original statement. Before proceeding with the proof, we record a useful fact about interpolation projections, inspired by the trick used in
[
31
, Lemma 10.1]
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
B
be a unital nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
A\subset B
be a unital norm-closed subalgebra. Let
q\in B^{**}
be a central closed projection such that
Aq=Bq
as subspaces in
B^{**}
. Then, there is
\kappa>0
such that
q
is a complete
(A,\kappa)
-interpolation projection.

Proof.

Consider the surjective completely contractive map
\pi:A\to Bq
defined as
\pi(a)=aq
for each
a\in A
. Since
q
is central,
Bq
is a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and
\pi
is a homomorphism. Therefore, there is a bounded homomorphism
\rho:Bq\to A/\ker\pi
such that
\rho\circ\pi:A\to A/\ker\pi
is simply the quotient map. Next, since
B
is nuclear,
so is
Bq

[
22
, Corollary 9.4.4]
. In addition, because
A/\ker\pi
is an operator algebra (see
[
15
, Proposition 2.3.4]
), we may
apply
[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
to see that
\rho
is in fact completely bounded. This immediately implies the existence of
\kappa>0
satisfying the required property.
∎

We note in passing that in view of
[
36
]
, one-sided module maps on nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras do not have to be completely bounded.

We can now show how to recover Varopoulos’ original theorem from our general machinery.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and let
A\subset\mathrm{C}(X)
be a unital norm-closed subalgebra. Let
K\subset X
be a subset with the following properties:

(i)

K
is closed,

(ii)

A\chi_{K}=\mathrm{C}(X)\chi_{K}
as subspaces in
\mathrm{C}(X)^{**}

(that is,
K
is an interpolation set), and

(iii)

\chi_{\{x\}}\in A^{\perp\perp}
for each
x\in K

(that is, every point in
K
is an
A
-peak point).

Then,
\chi_{K}
is
A
-null (that is,
K
is a peak set).

Proof.

Let
q=\chi_{K}
, which is a closed projection in
\mathrm{C}(X)^{**}
. Invoking (
2
), we see that the atomic part of
\chi_{K}
is simply
\bigvee_{x\in K}\chi_{\{x\}}
. Given
x\in K
, we know that
\chi_{\{x\}}\in A^{\perp\perp}
.
Hence, the atomic part of
\chi_{K}
lies in
A^{\perp\perp}
. Next, it follows from Proposition
4.8
that there is
\kappa>0
such that
q
is a complete
(A,\kappa)
-interpolation projection.
By virtue of Corollary
4.7
, we conclude that
q
is
A
-null.
∎

5.
Application to algebras of analytic functions

In this section, we illustrate how our general theorem can be used to recover some recent results pertaining to operator algebras of analytic functions on the unit ball
[
31
]
. This will be done through another special case of Theorem
4.3
, which we tackle first.

5.1.
Adapted projections

Let
B
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
A\subset B
be a subalgebra. We say that a projection
e\in B^{**}
is
A
-adapted
if there is a net
(e_{i})
of closed
A
-coinvariant
projections in
B^{**}
increasing to
e
in the weak-
*
topology of
B^{**}
. The linchpin technical result of this section is the following refinement of Theorem
4.3
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
A\subset B
be a unital norm-closed subalgebra. Assume that there is a central closed projection
z\in B^{**}
such that
B^{**}z
is commutative and
1-z
is
A
-adapted. Let
q\in B^{**}
be a projection with the following properties:

(i)

q
is closed,

(ii)

there is
\kappa>0
for which
q
is a complete
(A,\kappa)
-interpolation projection,

(iii)

q\leq z
,

(iv)

the atomic part of
q
lies in
A^{\perp\perp}
.

Then,
q
is
A
-null.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\varphi\in A^{\perp}
with
\|\varphi\|=1
. Let
\tau=|\varphi|
, which is a state
on
B
. By
[
2
, Proposition II.3]
, there is an open projection
u\in B^{**}
dominating
q
such that
\tau(u-q)<\varepsilon.
Since
z
is central and
q\leq z
, we find
q=qz\leq uz\leq u
so that

(14)

\tau(uz-q)<\varepsilon.

Further, because
1-z
is
A
-adapted, there is an
A
-coinvariant closed projection
r\leq 1-z
such that

(15)

\tau((1-z)-r)<\varepsilon.

Because
z
is closed, so is
z(1-u)

[
2
, Proposition II.5]
. Moreover, we have
r\leq 1-z
so that
r^{\prime}=r+z(1-u)
is closed by
[
2
, Theorem II.7]
. The projection

u^{\prime}=1-r^{\prime}=((1-z)-r))+uz

is then seen to be open with
u^{\prime}\geq uz\geq q
.
We compute

\displaystyle\tau(u^{\prime}-q)

\displaystyle=\tau(uz-q)+\tau((1-z)-r)\leq 2\varepsilon

by (
14
) and (
15
).

Observe next that since
B^{**}z
is commutative,
q=qz
is
A
-coinvariant and
uz
is
A
-invariant. On the other hand,
1-z
is central and
r
is
A
-coinvariant, so that
(1-z)-r
is
A
-invariant. It follows that
u^{\prime}
is
A
-invariant as well. By virtue of Theorem
4.3
, we see that

\tau(q)\leq\kappa^{3}\sqrt{\tau(u^{\prime}-q)}\leq\kappa^{3}\sqrt{2\varepsilon},

so the proof is complete upon letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

5.2.
Hilbert function spaces and their multipliers

Our goal is to show how Corollary
5.1
can be used to recover
[
31
, Theorem 10.3]
.
Along the way, we will also recover a few more results from
[
31
]
.
Let us recall some background material and terminology; for more detail the reader may consult
[
31
]

or e.g.
[
28
, Section 5]
.

Let
d\geq 1
be a positive integer and let
{\mathbb{B}}_{d}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
denote the open unit ball. Let
{\mathbb{S}}_{d}=\partial{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
denote the unit sphere. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert space of analytic functions on
{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
admitting a reproducing kernel that is unitarily invariant and regular. Examples include the Dirichlet space on the disc, the Hardy and Bergman spaces, as well as the Drury–Arveson space. For each
\lambda\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
, we let
k_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{H}}
denote the corresponding reproducing kernel vector. We then have that
\{k_{\lambda}:\lambda\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}\}
spans a norm-dense subspace in
{\mathcal{H}}
(see
[
34
]
).

A multiplier is a function
\varphi:{\mathbb{B}}_{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying
\varphi f\in{\mathcal{H}}
for every
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
. The corresponding multiplication operator
M_{\varphi}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is then bounded. The space of all multiplication operators induced by a multiplier is denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{H}})
, and it is weak-
*
closed subalgebra of
B({\mathcal{H}})
. If
\varphi
is a multiplier, then

(16)

M_{\varphi}^{*}k_{\lambda}=\overline{\varphi(\lambda)}k_{\lambda},\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}.

All polynomials are multipliers, and we let
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{H}})\subset{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{H}})
denote the norm-closure of these polynomial multipliers. A standard argument
shows that if
M_{\varphi}\in{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{H}})
, then
\varphi
is continuous on
\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
.
Indeed, for any multiplier
\varphi
, (
16
) shows that
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq\|M_{\varphi}\|
; hence functions inducing the multiplication operators in
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{H}})
are uniform limits of polynomials on
\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
B({\mathcal{H}})
generated by
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{H}})
. It follows from
[
34
, Theorem 4.6]
that
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})
contains the ideal
K
of compact operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
. Let
z_{K}\in{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})^{**}
denote the closed central projection satisfying
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})^{**}(1-z_{K})=K^{\perp\perp}
. This projection has the following crucial property.

Lemma 5.2
.

The projection

5.3.
The Davidson–Hartz theorems

The previous result has bearing on the topic of peak-interpolation. Indeed, it allows us to provide alternative proofs (and even some refinements) of some of the results from

Theorem 5.6

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert space of analytic functions on
{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
admitting a reproducing kernel that is unitarily invariant and regular. Let

\|M_{\varphi}\|=\|g\|

\varphi|_{E}=g

on

Proof.

Let

Next, by Lemma
5.3

there is a closed projection

such that

is central in
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})^{**}

-null by Theorem

\displaystyle\Theta(d^{2}q)=k_{1}^{2}\chi_{E}\geq|g_{0}|^{2}\chi_{E}=\Theta(qb^{*}bq)

whence

6.
Null projections and the Riesz projection

In this section, we consider certain projections arising from convex sets of states on a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, and aim to understand when they are null,
or more generally when projections annihilating the ‘absolutely continuous’ functionals are null. Let us be more precise.

Let
B
be a

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be continuous linear functionals on
B
. We say that
\varphi
is
absolutely continuous
with respect to
\psi
if
|\varphi|(x^{*}x)=0
for every
x\in B^{**}
satisfying
|\psi|(x^{*}x)=0
. Equivalently, this means that the support projection of
|\varphi|
is dominated by the support projection of
|\psi|
. Here, by the support projection of a state
\theta
on
B
, we mean the unique projection
s_{\theta}\in B^{**}
such that

\{x\in B^{**}:\theta(x^{*}x)=0\}=B^{**}(1-s_{\theta}).

When the support projections of
|\varphi|
and
|\psi|
are instead orthogonal, we say that
\varphi
and
\psi
are
singular
. The reader may consult
[
28
, Section 2]
for more detail on absolute continuity and singularity for states.

Let now
\Delta
be a norm-closed convex set of states on
B
. We let
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)\subset B^{*}
denote the set of those functionals that are absolutely continuous with respect to some element of
\Delta
. Similarly, we let
\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
denote the set of those functionals that are mutually singular with respect to every element of
\Delta
. By
[
28
, Theorem 3.5]
, there is a projection
r_{\Delta}\in B^{**}
with the property that
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)=((1-r_{\Delta})B^{**})_{\perp}
and
\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)=(r_{\Delta}B^{**})_{\perp}
. We sometimes refer to
r_{\Delta}
as the
Riesz projection
corresponding to
\Delta
. The following easy observation will be used several times below.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in B^{*}
such that
\varphi
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\psi
. Then, the following statements hold.

(i)

If
\psi\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
, then
\varphi\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
.

(ii)

If
\psi\in\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
, then
\varphi\in\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
.

Proof.

(i) is trivial. To see (ii), assume that
\psi\in\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
. Fix
b\in B
and put
\rho=\psi(\cdot b)
. Then,
\rho(r_{\Delta}x)=\psi(r_{\Delta}xb)=0
for each
x\in B^{**}
, since
\psi\in(r_{\Delta}B^{**})_{\perp}
. This shows that
\rho\in(r_{\Delta}B^{**})_{\perp}=\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
. Further, the equality
(r_{\Delta}B^{**})_{\perp}=\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
shows that
\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
is a norm-closed subspace, so from the previous argument we conclude that
\varphi\in\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
upon invoking
[
28
, Theorem 2.7]
.
∎

Let
A\subset B
be a subalgebra. Henceforth we assume that this inclusion is unital for simplicity.
A unifying motif behind this section
is to determine when the Riesz projection
r_{\Delta}
is
A
-null. In doing so, we will highlight connections with many different properties of interest from the point of view of noncommutative function theory.

First, we tackle the issue of determining when
r_{\Delta}
lies in
A^{\perp\perp}
. For this purpose, we need the following notion. We say that
A
has the
F.&M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Delta
if, whenever
\varphi\in A^{\perp}
, we have that
\varphi(r_{\Delta}\cdot)\in A^{\perp}
. In this case, we also have
\varphi((1-r_{\Delta})\cdot)\in A^{\perp}
. In other words, this property says that the absolutely continuous and singular parts (with respect to
\Delta
) of a functional on
B
annihilating
A
must also annihilate
A
. This can be reformulated as follows.

Lemma 6.2
.

The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

A
has the F.&M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Delta
.

(ii)

The Riesz projection
r_{\Delta}
lies in
A^{\perp\perp}
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
\varphi\in A^{\perp}
. Then,
\varphi(r_{\Delta}\cdot)\in A^{\perp}
by
the F.&M. Riesz property. Evaluating at
1\in A
, we obtain in particular
\varphi(r_{\Delta})=0
. We conclude that
r_{\Delta}\in A^{\perp\perp}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Let
\varphi\in A^{\perp}
. Then,
\varphi
annihilates
A^{\perp\perp}
, and in particular it annihilates
r_{\Delta}A
since
r_{\Delta}\in A^{\perp\perp}=\bar{A}^{w*}
. This implies in turn that
\varphi(r_{\Delta}\cdot)
annihilates
A
.
∎

Notice that if

(17)

A^{\perp}\subset\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)

then trivially
A
has the F.&M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Delta
. We therefore refer to
condition (

strong F.&M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Delta
for
A

For example, the original F.&M. Riesz theorem shows that the disk algebra

has the strong F.&M. Riesz property in
\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}})
with respect to arclength measure.
This can also be encoded in terms of the Riesz projection, as we show next.

Lemma 6.3
.

The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

A
has the strong F.&M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Delta
.

(ii)

The Riesz projection
1-r_{\Delta}
is
A
-null.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): The assumption immediately implies that

\{|\varphi|:\varphi\in A^{\perp}\}\subset\operatorname{AC}(\Delta).

Given
\varphi\in A^{\perp}
, we thus find
|\varphi|=|\varphi|(r_{\Delta}\cdot)
and
|\varphi|(1-r_{\Delta})=0
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
Let
\varphi\in A^{\perp}
. By assumption,
|\varphi|(1-r_{\Delta})=0
, so that
\varphi((1-r_{\Delta})\cdot)=0
by Lemma
2.1
. This shows that
\varphi\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
.
∎

6.2
and
6.3
. For this purpose, we need a technical result from

. A projection

will be said to be of

. If the collection can be chosen to be countable, we say that

is of
type
F_{\sigma}
. If the collection can be chosen to be finite, we say that

is of
type
F_{0}
. If

[
2

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume that
\Delta
is closed in the weak-
*
topology of

. Let

be a state in
\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
. Then, there is a type
F_{\sigma}
projection

annihilating
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
and satisfying
\psi(q)=1
.

Proof.

By
[
28

, there a type
F_{\sigma}
projection
q\in B^{**}
that annihilates
\Delta

annihilates
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)

We say that
A
has the
Forelli property in

with respect to

if
for every type

projection

lying in
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}

in

-Forelli property in

has the

These properties are inspired by a result of Forelli (see for instance
[

), which explains our choice of terminology. We now arrive at another main result of the paper, which connects nullity of the Riesz projection with the various properties introduced above.

Let

Remark.
The reader may notice that there is a notable omission in the long list in the statement of the last theorem, namely that all closed projections in AC
(\Delta)^{\perp}
are in
A^{\perp\perp}
. This seems to be a very interesting condition. It is equivalent to (iv) in the commutative case, but we are not sure about the general case.
Indeed if
B=\mathrm{C}(X)
for some compact Hausdorff space
X
,
then
this condition is simply saying that a closed set
E
in
X
is a generalized
A
-peak set if each of its closed subsets has
\mu
-measure zero for every probability measure
\mu
in
\Delta
.
That is, a closed set
E\subset X
is a generalized
A
-peak set if

|\mu|(E)=0

for every probability measure
\mu
in
\Delta
. This clearly implies that
every closed subset of such a set
E
is a generalized peak set, which implies by Proposition
2.4
or the discussion above it that
\chi_{E}
is
A
-null.
That is,

E
is a peak-interpolation set.

When
\Delta=\{\mu\}
for some regular Borel probability measure
\mu
on
X
, and every closed set of
\mu
-measure zero is a peak set for

, it follows that every closed set of
\mu
-measure zero is
A
-null. This was showed to us by Alexander Izzo, and it inspired several of the results in Section 2.

7.1.
Von Neumann algebraic preliminaries

We begin with some general facts about von Neumann algebras that will be needed throughout the remainder of the paper.

Given a von Neumann algebra

M_{*}\subset M^{*}
denote the norm-closed subspace of weak-
*
continuous linear functionals on
M
. The following is actually a general principle for dual Banach spaces
that is well known in certain quarters. It is a necessary and sufficient condition for a kind of ‘Kaplansky density’.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra. Let
A\subset M
be a subspace and let
W\subset M
denote its weak-
*
closure. Let
\rho:M_{*}\to A^{*}
denote the restriction map. Then,
\rho
has closed range if and only if there is a constant
r>0
such that the closed unit ball of
W
is contained in the weak-
*
closure of
\{a\in A:\|a\|\leq r\}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\rho
has closed range. By the open mapping theorem, there is
r>0
such that for every
\omega\in M_{*}
, there is
\omega^{\prime}\in M_{*}
with
\rho(\omega)=\rho(\omega^{\prime})
and
\|\omega^{\prime}\|\leq r\|\rho(\omega)\|
. Then, for each
\omega\in M_{*}
we see that

\displaystyle\sup\{|\omega(a)|:a\in A,\|a\|\leq r\}

\displaystyle=r\|\rho(\omega)\|\geq\|\omega^{\prime}\|\geq\|\omega^{\prime}|_{W}\|=\|\omega|_{W}\|

\displaystyle=\sup\{|\omega(a)|:a\in W,\|a\|\leq 1\}.

By the convex separation theorem, we conclude that the weak-
*
closure of
\{a\in A:\|a\|\leq r\}
contains the closed unit ball of
W
.

Conversely, assume that there is
r>0
such that the closed unit ball of
W
is contained in the weak-
*
closure of
\{a\in A:\|a\|\leq r\}
. Let
\omega\in M_{*}
. It follows that

r\|\rho(\omega)\|=\sup\{|\omega(a)|:a\in A,\|a\|\leq r\}\geq\|\omega|_{W}\|.

On the other hand, the predual of
W
is isometrically isomorphic to
M_{*}/W_{\perp}
, so that there is
\omega^{\prime}\in M_{*}
such that
\omega^{\prime}|_{W}=\omega|_{W}
and
\|\omega^{\prime}\|\leq 2\|\omega|_{W}\|
. In particular, we find
\rho(\omega^{\prime})=\rho(\omega)
and
\|\omega^{\prime}\|\leq 2r\|\rho(\omega)\|
, so the range of
\omega
is closed.
∎

There is a central projection
z\in M^{**}
such that
M_{*}=M^{*}z

[
43
, Theorem III.2.14]
. An important fact is that there is a normal
*
-isomorphism
\Omega:M\to M^{**}z
such that
\Omega(a)=az
for every
a\in M
. Next, let
\varphi
be a normal state on
M
,
and let
\widehat{\varphi}:M^{**}\to{\mathbb{C}}
denote its unique normal extension. Consider the weak-
*
closed left ideals

L_{\varphi}=\{a\in M:\varphi(a^{*}a)=0\}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{\widehat{\varphi}}=\{x\in M^{**}:\widehat{\varphi}(x^{*}x)=0\}.

Then, it is readily verified that
\varphi=\widehat{\varphi}\circ\Omega
and that

L_{\widehat{\varphi}}=\Omega(L_{\varphi})\oplus M^{**}(1-z).

In particular, this gives a simple relationship between what we call the support projection
s_{\varphi}
of
\varphi
, and another notion of support found in the von Neumann algebras literature.
Indeed, our convention in this paper is that

L_{\widehat{\varphi}}=M^{**}(1-s_{\varphi}).

On the other hand, there is a projection
e_{\varphi}\in M
such that
L_{\varphi}=M(1-e_{\varphi})
. It follows that
s_{\varphi}=ze_{\varphi}
. Furthermore, given another normal state
\psi
on
M
, we see that
L_{\varphi}\subset L_{\psi}
if and only if
L_{\widehat{\varphi}}\subset L_{\widehat{\psi}}
, so that our notion of absolute continuity coincides with the corresponding notion from von Neumann algebra theory for such states.

We say that a normal state
\varphi
is
faithful
if
L_{\varphi}=\{0\}
. We now give an alternative description of
z
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state
\varphi
.
Then,
s_{\varphi}=z
and
M_{*}=\operatorname{AC}(\varphi)
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is normal, we have
\varphi(z)=1
so
z\geq s_{\varphi}
. Next, by the discussion above we have
L_{\widehat{\varphi}}z=\Omega(L_{\varphi})=\{0\}
since
\varphi
is faithful. In particular,
(1-s_{\varphi})z=0
, so that
z\leq s_{\varphi}
. Therefore,
z=s_{\varphi}
.
It follows immediately from this that
M_{*}\subset{\rm AC}(\varphi)
. Conversely, if
\psi\in{\rm AC}(\varphi)
then
|\psi|(1-z)=0
since
\varphi(1-z)=0
, so indeed
\psi\in M_{*}=M^{*}z
by Lemma
2.1
.
∎

More generally, we can describe
M_{*}
in terms of absolute continuity, as follows.
We denote by
S_{n}(M)
the set of normal states on
M
. Assume now that
M
contains a weak-
*
dense unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
B
. Let
\rho:M^{*}\to B^{*}
denote the restriction map. By the Kaplansky density theorem we see that
\rho
is isometric on
M_{*}
. In particular, this means that the sets
(M_{*})|_{B}=\rho(M_{*})
and
\rho(S_{n}(M))
are norm-closed. Put
\Delta=\rho(S_{n}(M))
; this is the norm-closed convex set
in
B^{*}

consisting of those elements of the form
\omega|_{B}
, where
\omega
is a normal state on
M
. We now record a simple fact.

Lemma 7.3
.

We have that
\operatorname{AC}(S_{n}(M)))=M_{*}
and
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)=(M_{*})|_{B}
.

Proof.

Given
\omega\in M_{*}
and
a\in M
, we have that
\omega(\cdot a)\in M_{*}
. Because both
M_{*}
and
(M_{*})|_{B}
are norm-closed, we then find
\operatorname{AC}(S_{n}(M))\subset M_{*}
and
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)\subset(M_{*})|_{B}
by virtue of
[
28
, Theorem 2.7]
. Conversely, let
\varphi\in M_{*}
with
\|\varphi\|=1
. We have
\varphi(z\cdot)=\varphi
. Now, there is a partial isometry
v\in M
such that
|\varphi|=\varphi(\cdot v)
. It follows that
|\varphi|(z\cdot)=|\varphi|
, whence
|\varphi|\in S_{n}(M)
and
\varphi\in\operatorname{AC}(S_{n}(M))
. We conclude that
M_{*}\subset\operatorname{AC}(S_{n}(M))
, which in turn plainly implies
(M_{*})|_{B}\subset\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
.
∎

7.2.
Lebesgue decompositions

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
A\subset B
be a unital subalgebra. Given a subset
X\subset B^{*}
, we let
X|_{A}
denote the set of restrictions to
A
of functionals in
X
. Following
[
28
]
, we say that
X
is a
left band
if, whenever a functional
\varphi\in B^{*}
is absolutely continuous with respect to some element of
X
, then it must be that
\varphi
itself lies in
X
.

Next, let
M
be a von Neumann algebra containing
B
as a weak-
*
dense unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra.
We say that
A
admits a
Lebesgue decomposition
relative to
M
if
(M_{*})|_{A}
is norm-closed and there is a (necessarily unique) subspace
\Sigma\subset A^{*}
such that
A^{*}=(M_{*})|_{A}\oplus_{1}\Sigma
.

It follows from
[
25
, Lemma 3.4]
that
B
itself always admits a Lebesgue decomposition relative to
M
. By contrast,
there is a sense in which the existence of such a decomposition for
A
is somewhat rare (see
[
25
]
for more detail). Combining Lemma
7.3
with
[
25
, Theorem 2.4]
, we see that if
A
has the F.& M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to the convex set of restrictions to
B
of the normal states on
M
, then
A
admits a Lebesgue decomposition relative to
M
that is ‘compatible’ (in a precise sense) with that of
B
. We can now give a different sufficient condition, where we don’t insist on this compatibility condition.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra. Let
B\subset M
be a weak-
*
dense unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra, and let
A\subset B
be a unital commutative subalgebra. Let

X=\{\psi\in B^{*}:\psi|_{A}\in(M_{*})|_{A}\}.

If
X
is a
norm-closed left band, then
A
admits a Lebesgue decomposition relative to
M
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma\subset B^{*}
denote the norm-closure of the convex hull of
\{|\psi|:\psi\in X,\|\psi\|=1\}
. Let
r_{\Gamma}\in B^{**}
denote the corresponding Riesz projection
[
28
, Theorem 3.5]
. By assumption,
X
is norm-closed and a left band, so we see that
X=\operatorname{AC}(\Gamma)
. On the other hand, it is clear that
A^{\perp}\subset X
, so in fact
A^{\perp}\subset\operatorname{AC}(\Gamma)
. We conclude that
A
has the strong F.& M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Gamma
. Hence, Theorem
6.5
implies that
r_{\Gamma}\in A^{\perp\perp}
. In particular,
r_{\Gamma}
commutes with
A
since
A
is assumed to be commutative. It thus follows that
A^{*}=(A^{**}r_{\Gamma})_{\perp}\oplus_{1}(A^{**}(1-r_{\Gamma}))_{\perp}
. Hence, it suffices to check that
(A^{**}(1-r_{\Gamma}))_{\perp}=(M_{*})|_{A}
.

Let
\varphi\in(M_{*})|_{A}
and choose
\omega\in M_{*}
such that
\omega|_{A}=\varphi
. Plainly,

\omega|_{B}\in X=\operatorname{AC}(\Gamma)
, so by construction of the Riesz projection, we have
\omega((1-r_{\Gamma})\cdot)=0
. Using that
r_{\Gamma}\in A^{\perp\perp}
, we find

\varphi((1-r_{\Gamma})a)=\omega((1-r_{\Gamma})a)=0,\quad a\in A

so that
\varphi\in(A^{**}(1-r_{\Gamma}))_{\perp}
. Conversely let
\varphi\in A^{*}
such that
\varphi((1-r_{\Gamma})\cdot)=0
. By the Hahn–Banach theorem, there is
\psi\in B^{*}
such that
\psi|_{A}=\varphi
. Since
r_{\Gamma}\in A^{\perp\perp}
, this implies that
\psi(r_{\Gamma}\cdot)|_{A}=\varphi(r_{\Gamma}\cdot)=\varphi
. Put
\psi^{\prime}=\psi(r_{\Gamma}\cdot)\in B^{*}
, which then lies in
\operatorname{AC}(\Gamma)=X
. Hence, there is
\omega\in M_{*}
such that
\omega|_{A}=\psi^{\prime}|_{A}=\varphi
, so that
\varphi\in(M_{*})|_{A}
as desired.
∎

We now illustrate how this result applies in some classical settings.

Example 3
.

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer, let
{\mathbb{B}}_{d}\subset{\mathbb{C}}_{d}
be the open unit ball, and let
{\mathbb{S}}_{d}
denote its topological boundary, the unit sphere. Let
\sigma
denote surface measure on
{\mathbb{S}}_{d}
. Let
M=L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}_{d},\sigma),B=\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{S}}_{d})
and let
A\subset B
denote the ball algebra, i.e. the norm-closure of the polynomials.
It is well known that the weak-
*
closure of
A
in
M
is simply
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{d})
, by making the usual identification via radial boundary values. Further, given
f\in H^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{d})
and
0<r<1
, we define
f_{r}:\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
as
f_{r}(z)=f(rz)
. Then,
f_{r}\in A
,
\|f_{r}\|\leq\|f\|
and the net
(f_{r})
converges to
f
in the weak-
*
topology of
M
. We infer that the set

X=\{\psi\in B^{*}:\psi|_{A}\in(M_{*})|_{A}\}

is norm-closed by Lemma
7.1
. In turn,
X

is a left band by Henkin’s theorem
[
41
, Theorem 9.3.1]
,
Thus,
A
admits a Lebesgue decomposition relative to
M
by Corollary
7.4
; this also follows from
[
41
, Section 9.8]
.

Next, we note that
B
admits a Lebesgue decomposition relative to
M
by
[
25
, Lemma 3.4]
. Nevertheless, the Lebesgue decompositions of
A
and
B
relative to
M
are not compatible, by
[
25
, Theorem 4.8 and Proposition 6.3]
. ∎

Example 4
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert function space of the type considered in Subsection
5.2
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{H}})\subset{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})\subset B({\mathcal{H}})
be the operator algebras defined therein. Consider the set

X=\{\psi\in{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})^{*}:\psi|_{{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{H}})}\in(B({\mathcal{H}})_{*})|_{{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{H}})}\}.

This is the set of
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{H}})
-functionals examined in Section
5
. As explained there, this coincides with the set
\mathscr{B}
from
[
28
, Theorem 5.7]
, so we see that
X
is a norm-closed left band. Thus,
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{H}})
admits a Lebesgue decomposition relative to
B({\mathcal{H}})
by Corollary
7.4
.

Next, recall that
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})
contains the ideal of compact operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
, whence it admits a Lebesgue decomposition relative to
B({\mathcal{H}})
by
[
25
, Lemma 3.4]
. Nevertheless, the Lebesgue decompositions of

{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{H}})
and
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})
relative to
B({\mathcal{H}})
are not compatible, by
[
25
, Theorem 4.8 and Proposition 6.1]
.
∎

We emphasize again that the incompatibility of the Lebesgue decompositions in the previous two examples shows in particular that Corollary
7.4
is applicable in some cases where
[
25
, Theorem 2.4]
is not.

8.
The F.& M. Riesz properties

It is a natural question whether the statements in Theorem
6.5
are ever all equivalent, perhaps under some additional assumptions. One way of answering this question is to determine when the F.& M. Riesz property is equivalent to its stronger variant. We explore this issue in this section.

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
A\subset B
be a unital subalgebra.
Let
\Delta\subset B^{*}
be a norm-closed, convex set of states on
B
. Define

E(\Delta)=\{\psi\in B^{*}:\psi|_{A}\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)|_{A}\}.

This set will play an analogous role to the set of functionals whose restrictions to
A
are “Henkin” (see e.g. Section 8.1 below).
We now show that the F.& M. Riesz property implies a certain rigidity property for left bands containing
\Delta
.
Its relation to the
classical Cole-Range theorem
[
41
, Theorem 9.6.1]
will be thoroughly
explained at the end of this section.

Theorem 8.1
.

Assume that
A
has the F.& M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Delta
. If
X\subset B^{*}
is a left band satisfying
\Delta\subset X\subset E(\Delta)
, then
X=\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
.

Proof.

Since
X
is assumed to be a left band containing
\Delta
, we necessarily have
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)\subset X
. Conversely, fix
\varphi\in X
. We see that
\varphi(r_{\Delta}\cdot)\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)\subset X
, so it suffices to show that the functional
\psi=\varphi((1-r_{\Delta})\cdot)
is zero. To see this, fix
b\in B
. Since
X
is a subspace,
\psi=\varphi-\varphi(r_{\Delta}\cdot)\in X
. Further, since
X
is a left band again, we see that
\psi(\cdot b)\in X
by

[
28
, Lemma 2.7]
. By assumption, we have
X\subset E(\Delta)
. Using the definition of
E(\Delta)
we may find

\nu\in A^{\perp}
and
\alpha\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
such that
\psi(\cdot b)=\alpha+\nu
. On the other hand,
\psi(\cdot b)\in\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
by Lemma
6.1
. Since we have
\nu=-\alpha+\psi(\cdot b)
, the F.& M. Riesz property implies
that
\psi(\cdot b)\in A^{\perp}
, so in particular
\psi(b)=0
. Consequently,
\psi=0
as desired.
∎

We can now reformulate the strong F.& M. Riesz property.

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
A\subset B
be a unital subalgebra. Let
\Delta
be a norm-closed convex subset of the state space of
B
. Assume that
A
has the F.& M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Delta
.
Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

A
has the strong F.& M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Delta
.

(ii)

E(\Delta)=\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
.

(iii)

E(\Delta)
is a left band.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): The inclusion
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)\subset E(\Delta)
is trivial. Conversely, assume that
\psi\in E(\Delta)
. By definition, this means that there is
\varphi\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
such that
\psi|_{A}=\varphi|_{A}
. Now,
\psi-\varphi\in A^{\perp}
so by assumption
\psi-\varphi\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
, which forces
\psi\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): This is trivial.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i): This follows immediately from Theorem
8.1
.
∎

We remark that by using the F.& M. Riesz property, it is easy to see that the strong F.& M. Riesz property with respect to
\Delta
is also equivalent to
\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)\cap A^{\perp}=\{0\}
.

We illustrate this result by applying it to a classical example.

Example 5
.

Let
\lambda
denote arclength measure on the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
. By means of radial boundary values, we may embed the algebra
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
of bounded holomorphic functions on the open unit disc as a unital norm-closed subalgebra of
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
. Choosing
\Delta=\{\lambda\}
,
Lemma
7.2
implies that
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)
coincides with the weak-
*
continuous linear functionals on
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
.
Hence,
E(\Delta)
coincides with the set
X
appearing in Corollary
7.4
.

Consider now the Douglas algebra
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})+\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}})\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
, which is known to be proper and norm-closed (see for instance
[
32
, Chapter 6]
). Hence, there is a non-zero bounded linear functional
\varphi
on
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
that annihilates
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})+\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}})
. Since
\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}})
is weak-
*
dense in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
, we see that
\varphi
is not weak-
*
continuous, so that
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
does not have the strong F.& M. Riesz property in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
with respect to
\Delta
. On the other hand, it is known
that
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
has the F.& M. Riesz property in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
with respect to
\Delta
(see
[
6
]
). We may thus use Corollary
8.2
to see that the set
E(\Delta)
is not a left
band. ∎

8.1.
Henkin functionals and the Cole-Range phenomenon

We now wish to connect Corollary
8.2
with the main objects studied in
[
28
]
. This will be accomplished via an appropriate choice of
\Delta
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra. Let
B\subset M
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra that is weak-
*
dense in
M
. Let
A\subset B
be a unital subalgebra. A bounded linear functional
\varphi:A\to{\mathbb{C}}
is said to be
Henkin
relative to
M
if it extends weak-
*
continuously to the weak-
*
closure of
A
in
M
. This notion is in agreement with our terminology used in Section
5
.
Equivalently,
\varphi
is Henkin if it extends weak-
*
continuously to
M

[
40
, Theorem 3.6]
, so the space of Henkin functionals is simply
(M_{*})|_{A}
.

Let
\Delta
denote the set of restrictions to
B
of normal states on
M
. Let
X\subset B^{*}
denote the subspace consisting of functionals
\varphi\in B^{*}
such that
\varphi|_{A}
is Henkin – this is precisely the set considered in Theorem
7.4
. This subspace is closed whenever
(M_{*})|_{A}
is closed, which can be ascertained with the help of Lemma
7.1
. Further, by virtue of Lemma
7.3
, we see that
X=E(\Delta)
. In particular, Corollary
8.2
can serve as a tool for checking when
X
forms a left band,
which was a central question in
[
28
]
, and shown to be useful in Corollary
7.4
.

Furthermore, Theorem
8.1
can be interpreted as a unifying principle underlying previous known results, as we illustrate next.

Example 6
.

Assume that
X
, as defined in the last paragraph, is norm-closed (see Lemma
7.1
).
Clearly,
A^{\perp}\subset X
. Next, let
\Omega\subset B^{*}
denote the norm closure of the convex hull of
\{|\varphi|:\varphi\in A^{\perp},\|\varphi\|=1\}
. In this case,
A^{\perp}\subset\operatorname{AC}(\Omega)
.
If
\psi\in E(\Omega)
then as before using the definition of
E(\Omega)
we may find

\nu\in A^{\perp}
and
\alpha\in\operatorname{AC}(\Omega)
such that
\psi=\alpha+\nu\in\operatorname{AC}(\Omega)
. Thus

E(\Omega)=\operatorname{AC}(\Omega)
.
Clearly
A
has the strong F.& M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Omega
.

Let us now examine the assumption
\Omega\subset X\subset E(\Omega)
from Theorem
8.1
.
The inclusion
X\subset E(\Omega)
is equivalent to
X|_{A}\subset\operatorname{AC}(\Omega)|_{A}
, which can then be phrased in terms of weak topologies on
A
,
matching the condition of
analyticity
in
[
28
, Theorem 4.4]
, and
recovering that theorem.
Next, since
A^{\perp}\subset X
, if
X
is a left band then
\Omega\subset X
.
This follows because if
\varphi\in A^{\perp}\subset X
then
|\varphi|\in X
, and because
X
is convex and assumed to be closed.
Theorem
8.1
implies in turn that
X=\operatorname{AC}(\Omega)
, assuming analyticity. This gives a
variant of
[
28
, Theorem 4.5]
.
∎

Example 7
.

We go back to the setting of Example
3
, so that
M=L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}_{d},\sigma)
,
B=\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{S}}_{d})
and
A
is the ball algebra. Again,
X\subset B^{*}
is the space of functionals whose restriction to
A
is Henkin relative to
M
. Next, let
\Omega\subset B^{*}
denote the norm-closed convex set of states
\rho
such that

\rho(a)=a(0),\quad a\in A.

Clearly, integration against
\sigma
is in
\Omega
by Cauchy’s formula. Furthermore,
\Omega\subset X
.
Since Henkin functionals on
A
admit a weak-
*
continuous extension to
M
, it follows that
X\subset A^{\perp}+\operatorname{AC}(\sigma)\subset A^{\perp}+\operatorname{AC}(\Omega)=E(\Omega)
.

It is known that
A
has the F.& M. Riesz property in
B
with respect to
\Omega
by
[
41
, Theorem 9.5.6]
. In addition,
X
is a left band by Henkin’s theorem
[
41
, Theorem 9.3.1]
. Hence, applying Theorem
8.1
, we find
X=\operatorname{AC}(\Omega)
, and we recover the classical characterization of Henkin measures for the ball algebra due to Cole and Range
[
41
, Theorem 9.6.1]
. ∎

9.
The Amar–Lederer property

Section
8
studied the interplay between the F.& M. Riesz properties as a mechanism for understanding when all the statements appearing in Theorem
6.5
are equivalent. In this section, we tackle this question in different direction, focusing instead on the implication (vi)
\Rightarrow
(iv) therein. As we shall see, this is a rather delicate issue.

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
A\subset B
be a unital norm-closed subalgebra. Let
\Delta
be a norm-closed convex subset of the state space of
B
. We say that
A
has the
Amar–Lederer property in
B
with respect to
\Delta
if all closed projections in
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}
are dominated by an
A
-
peak
projection in
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}
. The reason for this terminology is made clear in the next example, which incidentally also shows that the implication (vi) implies (iv) generally fails in Theorem
6.5
, even for commutative
B
and
\Delta
a singleton, and even if we then strengthen (vi) to insist that
all projections there are closed.

Example 8
.

Consider
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
as in Example
5
. Let
X
denote the character space of
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
. Then, via the Gelfand transform, we know that
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)\cong\mathrm{C}(X)
. Let
A\subset\mathrm{C}(X)
denote the unital norm-closed subalgebra corresponding to
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
under this identification. Let
\tau
denote the state on
\mathrm{C}(X)
corresponding to integration against
\lambda
on
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
. Let
\Delta=\{\tau\}
. In this case,
A
has the Amar–Lederer property in
B
with respect to
\Delta
by a classical result of Amar and Lederer
[
5
]
.
Of course understanding this requires also the dictionary between closed sets and closed projections, and
between peak sets and closed projections in
A^{\perp\perp}
,
that we have discussed earlier. On the other hand, properties (iv) and (v) in Theorem
6.5
fail.
To see this note that the latter properties, in the specific case of this example, imply that closed ‘Lebesgue-null’ sets are generalized peak sets.
Yet
[
38
, Proposition 6.3]
gives a closed ‘Lebesgue-null’
G_{\delta}
set in this context which is not a peak set.
Hence, it is not a generalized peak set, since
G_{\delta}
generalized peak sets are peak sets
[
33
, Lemma II.12.1]
.

For
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
as above, the Amar–Lederer property was established in
[
5
]
as a tool in characterizing the exposed points of the unit ball of
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
. Subsequently, it also played a key role in Ando’s proof of uniqueness for the predual of
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})

[
6
]
, and in establishing a Lebesgue decomposition for
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
. In light of these developments, it stands to reason that the Amar–Lederer property should also be meaningful in the context of certain noncommutative analogues of the inclusion
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda)
. Indeed the Amar–Lederer property has inspired several deep papers in noncommutative function theory, some of them concerned with the topic of uniqueness of predual.
Ueda and the first author with Labuschagne have proved in
[
44
,
14
]
a partial Amar–Lederer theorem for maximal subdiagonal algebras in
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebras, and used
this to give several powerful applications such as a Lebesgue decomposition for such algebras.
We say ‘partial’ because of a severe restriction imposed in those papers on the projections in the theorem.
Accordingly, we set out to remove this restriction and establish the full noncommutative Amar–Lederer theorem for the class of maximal subdiagonal algebras.
The first part of argument has little to do with those algebras, but instead is purely von Neumann algebraic in nature.

9.1.
Von Neumann algebraic preliminaries

Let
B
be a

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
q\in B^{**}
be a projection. Let
\varphi
be a state on
B
.
We say that
\varphi
is
regular at
q
if for all projections
p\in B
dominating
q
and for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an open projection
u\in B^{**}
such that
p\geq u\geq q
and
\varphi(u)<\varphi(q)+\varepsilon
.
(Note that we are writing here and below, as is common,
\hat{\varphi}(u)
as
\varphi(u)
, where

\hat{\varphi}
is the extension of
\varphi
to the bidual.)

For example, let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and let
B=\mathrm{C}(X)
. Let
\mu
be regular Borel measure on
X
, and let
\varphi
be the state on
B
of integration against
\mu
. Let
q=\chi_{E}\in B^{**}
for some Borel subset
E\subset X
. Then,
\varphi
is regular at
q
. Indeed, given
\varepsilon>0
, by the regularity property of the measure
\mu
, we may choose an open projection
u\in B^{**}
such that
u_{0}\geq q
and
\varphi(u_{0})<\varphi(q)+\varepsilon
. If we are also given a projection
p\in B
such that
p\geq q
, then we may simply take
u=u_{0}p
, which is still open and has the required property.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
q\in B^{**}
be a projection. Let
\varphi
be a state on
B
.
Two sufficient conditions for regularity of
\varphi
at
q
are the following:

(i)

q
is open or closed.

(ii)

q
is of type
F_{\sigma}
and
\varphi
is tracial with
\varphi(q)=0
.

Proof.

(i) The ‘open’ case is trivial, so assume
q
closed. We adapt

[
2
, Proposition II.3]
. Write
q^{\perp}=1-q
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
p
be a projection in
B
with
p\leq q^{\perp}
. The desired conclusion is equivalent to showing that there is a closed projection
r\in B^{**}
such that
p\leq r\leq q^{\perp}
and
\varphi(r)>\varphi(q^{\perp})-\varepsilon
. Because
q
is closed and is orthogonal to
p
,
p+q
is closed
[
2
, Theorem II.7]
, and hence
q^{\perp}-p=1-(q+p)
is open. Thus, there is an increasing net of contractions
(b_{i})
in
B
converging to
q^{\perp}-p
in the weak-
*
topology of
B^{**}
. Consider the positive element
a_{i}=b_{i}+p\in B
. The increasing net
(a_{i})
converges to
q^{\perp}
in the weak-
*
topology of
B^{**}
. Choose an index
i
large enough so that
\varphi(q^{\perp}-a_{i})<\varepsilon/2
. Note that
a_{i}p=p
since
pb_{i}p\leq p(q^{\perp}-p)p=0
. Thus for any polynomial
f
we have
f(a_{i})p=f(1)p
, and by uniform approximation the same is true for any continuous function on
[0,1]
. The proof of
[
2
, Proposition II.3]
constructs a sequence
(f_{n})
of continuous functions on
[0,1]
with
f_{n}(1)=1
such that
(f_{n}(a_{i}))
increases in the weak-
*
topology to the closed projection
r=\chi_{[\varepsilon/2,1]}(a_{i})\in B^{**}
. Because
f_{n}(a_{i})p=p
for each
n
, we find
rp=p
or
r\geq p
. Finally, arguing as in
[
2
, Proposition II.3]
, we see that
\varphi(q^{\perp}-r)<\varepsilon
as desired.

(ii) Since
q
is of type
F_{\sigma}
, there is a countable collection
\{q_{k}:k\geq 1\}
of closed projections in
B^{**}
such that
q=\bigvee_{k}q_{k}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
, and
let
p
be a projection in
B
dominating
q
. For each
k\geq 1
, we apply (i) to find an open projection
u_{k}\in B^{**}

with
\varphi(u_{k})<\varepsilon/2^{k}
and
q_{k}\leq u_{k}\leq p
. The projection
u=\bigvee_{k}u_{k}
is open and clearly dominates
q

and is dominated by
p
. Furthermore, since
\varphi
is tracial on
B
, it is also tracial on
B^{**}
so we see that
\varphi(u)\leq\sum_{k}\varphi(u_{k})
by
[
43
, Proposition V.1.6]
. Therefore
\varphi(u)<\varepsilon
.
∎

Remark.
Proposition
9.1
is true for nonunital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras with ‘closed’ in (i) replaced by ‘compact’. This follows almost immediately by applying the unital case in the unitization
B^{1}
,
since
q
is compact if it is closed in
B^{1}
.

We also record a fact found in the proof of
[
2
, Proposition II.14]
.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra. Let
u\in M^{**}
be an open projection and
\varphi
be a normal state on
M
. Then, there is a projection
p\in M
dominating
u
such that
\varphi(u)=\varphi(p)
.

We now establish the pivotal technical tool that we require.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra, let
\varphi
be a normal state on
M
and let
q\in M^{**}
be a projection.
Then
\varphi
is regular at
q
if and only if there is a decreasing sequence of projections
(p_{n})
in
M
dominating
q
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(p_{n})=\varphi(q)
.

Proof.

Suppose that there is a decreasing sequence of projections
(p_{n})
in
M
dominating
q
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(p_{n})=\varphi(q)
, and that

p
is a projection in
M
dominating
q
. For each
n
, we let
u_{n}=p\wedge p_{n}\in M
, which is clopen. Since
q\leq u_{n}\leq p_{n}
we have that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(u_{n})=\varphi(q)
.

For the other direction, put
p_{0}=1
. Assume that we have constructed projections
p_{0},\ldots,p_{n}\in M
dominating
q
such that
p_{j}\leq p_{k}
and
\varphi(p_{j})\leq\varphi(q)+1/(j+1)
for every
0\leq k\leq j\leq n
. Consider the von Neumann algebra
N=p_{n}Mp_{n}
. By regularity, there is an open projection
u=p_{n}up_{n}\in M^{**}
such that
u\geq q
and
\varphi(u)<\varphi(q)+1/(n+2)
. Also
u
is open in
N^{**}
.
Apply Lemma
9.2
to find a projection
p_{n+1}\in N
such that
p_{n+1}\geq u\geq q
and
\varphi(p_{n+1})=\varphi(u)<\varphi(q)+1/(n+2)
. Since
p_{n+1}\in N
, we must have that
p_{n+1}\leq p_{n}
. The existence of the desired sequence then follows by induction.
∎

9.2.
Subdiagonal algebras

Let us recall the setting of Arveson’s subdiagonal algebras, referring the reader to
[
13
,
44
,
14
]
for additional detail and references.

Let
M
be a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state
\varphi
. Let
A\subset M
be a unital weak-
*
closed subalgebra such that
A+A^{*}
is weak-
*
dense in
M
. Put
D=A\cap A^{*}
and let
E:M\to D
be a (normal faithful) conditional expectation. We say that
A
is
subdiagonal
in
M
with respect to
E
if
E
is multiplicative on
A
. Such an algebra

A
is said to be a
maximal subdiagonal algebra
if
A
is properly contained in no larger subdiagonal algebra in
M
with respect to
E
.

We now motivate the main result of this section. Let
\Delta\subset M^{*}
denote the set of normal states on
M
. Recall that there is a central projection
z\in M^{**}
such that
M_{*}=M^{*}z

[
43
, Theorem III.2.14]
. Let
\psi
be a state on
M
and consider its support projection
s_{\psi}\in M^{**}
, which is defined by

\{x\in M^{**}:\psi(x^{*}x)=0\}=M^{**}(1-s_{\psi}).

If we assume that
\psi\in\operatorname{SG}(\Delta)
, then by definition
s_{\psi}r_{\Delta}=0
or
s_{\psi}\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}
, which by Lemma
7.3
is equivalent to
s_{\psi}\leq 1-z
. Under the additional requirement that the given faithful normal state
\varphi
be tracial, Ueda showed in
[
44
]
that there always exists a closed projection
p\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}\cap A^{\perp\perp}
such that
s_{\psi}\leq p
.
This is a ‘partial Amar–Lederer theorem’. At the cost of great technical difficulty, the tracial condition on
\varphi
was removed in
[
14
]
.

We now improve this result, where we replace the support projection of a singular state by any
\varphi
-null regular projection. Fortunately, the very lengthy and
delicate argument found in
[
14
]
can be adapted verbatim to our situation, so we simply need to verify that the required conditions for its application are met.

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
M
be a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state
\varphi
. Let
\Delta
denote the set of normal states on
M
. Let
A\subset M
be a maximal subdiagonal algebra. Then, the following statements hold.

(i)

A
has the Amar–Lederer property in
M
with respect to
\Delta
.
Indeed every regular projection that is
\varphi
-null (or in
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}
) is dominated by a closed peak
projection in
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}\cap A^{\perp\perp}
.

(ii)

A
has the Forelli property in
M
with respect to
\Delta
.

Proof.

(i)
Note that
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)=\operatorname{AC}(\varphi)
by virtue of Lemmas
7.3
and
7.2
. In particular, it follows from
[
28
, Lemma 2.4]
that a projection
p\in M^{**}
lies in
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}
if and only if
\varphi(p)=0
. Invoking Proposition
9.1
, it thus suffices to fix a given projection
q\in M^{**}
such that
\varphi(q)=0
and with the property that
\varphi
is regular at
q
, and to show that there then exists a closed projection
p\in M^{**}
lying in
A^{\perp\perp}
such that
\varphi(p)=0
and
p\geq q
. By Proposition
9.3

there is a decreasing sequence of projections
(p_{n})
in
M
dominating
q
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(p_{n})=0
. It follows that
p_{n}\searrow 0
weak* in
M
(since
if
p_{n}\searrow r
then
\varphi(r)=0
and so
r=0
). Let
p_{0}
be the
infimum (and weak* limit) of the
p_{n}
in
M^{**}
, a closed projection with
\varphi(p_{0})=0
.

We now have completed our generalization of the first (von Neumann algebraic) part of the main theorem in
[
44
]
or
[
14
]
. Fortunately
the above is all that is needed to be changed in the rest of the proofs of the latter.
By the very technical proof comprising pages 8223–8228 in
[
14
]
we obtain
a contraction
b\in A
with
bp_{0}=p_{0}=p_{0}b
. By the basic theory of peak projections
[
35
,
19
]
the real positive element

a=(1+b)/2
peaks at a projection
p\geq p_{0}
, with
a^{n}\to p
weak* in
M^{**}
.
As in
[
44
]
we have
a^{n}\to 0
weak* in
M
, so that
p\in\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}\cap A^{\perp\perp}
. By construction
p
is a peak projection.

For the last assertion, simply note that (vi) in Theorem
6.5
implies the Forelli property.
∎

Thus for example if we assume in addition that
\varphi
is tracial then every type
F_{\sigma}
projection in
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}
is dominated by a closed
projection in
\operatorname{AC}(\Delta)^{\perp}\cap A^{\perp\perp}
.

As noted in the proof we may replace
\Delta
by
\{\varphi\}
with no change in the conclusions.

Finally, we remark that statement (i) above
is almost certainly not provable for general (even commutative) von Neumann algebras. Indeed there is a set theoretic
obstruction as is noted in the last section of
[
14
]
.

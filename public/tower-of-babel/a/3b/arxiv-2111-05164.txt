1.
Introduction

This article follows the recent investigation of the Littlewood-Paley-Stein theory carried out by Xu. In his paper
[
14
]
, Xu has developed a new powerful method to study the vector-valued Littlewood-Paley-Stein theory for semigroups. One interesting benefit of this new method is the fact that it often yields the optimal orders of the relevant best constants.

Let
\{\mathsf{T}_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a symmetric diffusion semigroup on a measure space
(\Omega,\mathscr{A},\mu)
and
\{\mathsf{P}_{t}\}_{t>0}
its subordinated Poisson semigroup defined as follows

\mathsf{P}_{t}(f)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-s}}{\sqrt{s}}{\mathsf{T}}_{\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}(f)ds.

Let
G^{\mathsf{P}}(f)
denote the Littlewood-Paley
g
-function of
f\in L_{p}(\Omega)
defined by

(1.1)

G^{\mathsf{P}}(f)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathsf{P}_{t}(f)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then Stein’s celebrated Littlewood-Paley inequality states that for every
1<p<\infty
there exist two positive constants
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}
and
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}
such that

\big(\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}\big)^{-1}\big\|f-\mathrm{F}(f)\big\|_{p}\leq\big\|G^{\mathsf{P}}(f)\big\|_{p}\leq\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}\big\|f\big\|_{p}\,,\quad\ \forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega),

where
\mathrm{F}
is the projection from
L_{p}(\Omega)
onto the fixed point subspace of
\{\mathsf{P}_{t}\}_{t>0}
of
L_{p}(\Omega)
.

Note that Stein’s proof does not give the optimal orders of the constants
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}
and
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}
. Although the principal objective of
[
14
]
concerns the vector-valued case, Xu has proved the following estimates on
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}
and
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}
:

\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}\lesssim\max\{p,p^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}\}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}\lesssim\max\{p^{\frac{1}{2}},p^{\prime}\}.

Moreover, he has shown that the estimate above on
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}
is optimal as
p\rightarrow 1
and
p\rightarrow\infty
since it is so already for the classical Poisson semigroup on
\mathbb{R}
.

However, the corresponding problem for
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}
is left open in
[
14
]
. Note that when
\mathsf{P}
is the classical Poisson semigroup on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one has
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}\approx 1
as
p\rightarrow 1
(see
[
15
]
).

In this article, we show that Xu’s above estimate on
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}
is in fact optimal as
p\rightarrow\infty
, namely,
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}\gtrsim p
as
p\rightarrow\infty
. In fact, we will
prove the stronger inequality
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{T}}_{p}\gtrsim p
as
p\rightarrow\infty
for a symmetric diffusion semigroup
\{{\mathsf{T}}_{t}\}_{t>0}
. Thus it only remains to determine the optimal order of
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{p}
as
p\rightarrow 1
.

Our proof relies on the deep relationship between martingale inequalities and Littlewood-Paley-Stein inequalities inspired by a result of Neveu. He establishes a profound link between the martingale theory and the ergodic theory in
[
6
]
. Indeed, Neveu shows that Doob’s maximal inequality for martingale can be obtained from Dunford-Schwartz’s maximal ergodic theorem. We will prove the analogous result for the square function.

We recall the definition of symmetric diffusion semigroups in Stein’s sense
[
12
, Chapter 3, Section 1]
. Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{A},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space.
\left\{\mathsf{T}_{t}\right\}_{t>0}
is called a symmetric
diffusion semigroup on
(\Omega,\mathscr{A},\mu)
if
\left\{\mathsf{T}_{t}\right\}_{t>0}
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)
\mathsf{T}_{t}
is a contraction on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
;

(b)
\mathsf{T}_{t}\mathsf{T}_{s}=\mathsf{T}_{t+s}
for positive
t
and
s
;

(c)
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathsf{T}_{t}(f)=f
in
L_{2}(\Omega)

for every
f\in L_{2}(\Omega)
;

(d)
\mathsf{T}_{t}
is positive (i.e. positivity preserving);

(e)
\mathsf{T}_{t}
is selfadjoint on
L_{2}(\Omega)
;

(f)
\mathsf{T}_{t}(1)=1
.

Recall that any symmetric diffusion semigroup
\left\{\mathsf{T}_{t}\right\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for
1<p<\infty
which implies that
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathsf{T}_{t}(f)
is well-defined (see
[
12
, Chapter 3, Section 2]
). As a result, we define
G^{\mathsf{T}}(f)
by

G^{\mathsf{T}}(f)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathsf{T}_{t}(f)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We often call
G^{\mathsf{T}}(f)
the Littlewood-Paley
g
-function of
f
, or call
G^{\mathsf{T}}(f)
the square function for semigroups for simplicity.

We will use the following convention:
A\lesssim B
(resp.
A\lesssim_{\alpha}B
) means that
A\leq CB
(resp.
A\leq C_{\alpha}B
) for some absolute positive constant
C
(resp. a positive constant
C_{\alpha}
depending only on a parameter
\alpha
).
A\approx B
or
A\approx_{\alpha}B
means that these inequalities as well as their inverses hold.

It is a classical fact that the orthogonal projection
F
from
L_{2}(\Omega)
onto the fixed point subspace of
\left\{\mathsf{T}_{t}\right\}_{t>0}
extends to a contractive projection on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then
\mathrm{F}
is also positive and
\mathrm{F}\left(L_{p}(\Omega)\right)
is the fixed point subspace of
\left\{\mathsf{T}_{t}\right\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega)
. Stein’s celebrated extension of the classical Littlewood-Paley inequality
[
12
]
asserts that for every symmetric diffusion semigroup

\left\{\mathsf{T}_{t}\right\}_{t>0}
and every
1<p<\infty

(1.2)

\|f-\mathrm{F}(f)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\approx_{p}\left\|G^{\mathsf{T}}(f)\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)},\quad\ \forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega).

Stein proves the above inequality (
1.2
) by virtue of Burkholder-Gundy’s martingale inequality and complex interpolation. He establishes a close connection between semigroup theory and martingale theory and studies semigroups through martingale theory via Rota’s dilation. We will proceed in a reverse way by studying martingales by semigroups.

We consider the square function in the martingale setting. Throughout this article we will work with a fixed probability
space
(\Omega,\mathscr{A},\mu)
and a sequence of
\sigma
-algebras

\mathscr{A}_{1}\subset\mathscr{A}_{2}\subset\dots\subset\mathscr{A}_{n}\subset\dots\subset\mathscr{A}

such that
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{A}_{n}=\mathscr{A}
.
For a random variable
f\in L_{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{A},\mu)
we will set
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

E_{n}(f)=E\left(f\mid{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\right),\quad dE_{n}(f)=E_{n}(f)-E_{n-1}(f),

where
E_{0}=0
as convention.
The square function of the martingale
\{E_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is

S(f)=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|dE_{n}(f)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

See
[
2
]
for more information on martingale inequalities.

We introduce the most important operator of the article associated to martingales. Given a strictly increasing sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
with
a_{0}=0
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}=1
, define

(1.3)

T=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_{n}-a_{n-1})E_{n}.

Then
T
is a positive contraction on
L_{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{A},\mu)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. In particular, for
p=2
,
T
is positive and selfadjoint on
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathscr{A},\mu)
. We use
T
to produce a symmetric diffusion semigroup.

Theorem 1.1
.

If we denote by
T^{t}
the operator obtained by continuous functional calculus, then for
t>0

(1.4)

T^{t}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-a_{n-1})^{t}d{E}_{n}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[(1-a_{n-1})^{t}-(1-a_{n})^{t}\right]{E}_{n},

and
T^{t}
is a positive contraction for any
t>0
on
L_{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{A},\mu)\ (1\leq p\leq\infty)
. Moreover,
\{T^{t}\}_{t>0}
is a symmetric diffusion semigroup.

The above semigroup
\{T^{t}\}_{t>0}
is exactly our desired semigroup generated by martingale. Then we select some specific sequence of
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and use this particular semigroup to obtain the best order of
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{T}}_{p}
. Below is one of our principal theorem, and it is the square function analogue of Neveu’s result
[
6
]
. Note that
\mathrm{F}=E_{1}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
a_{n}=1-\mathrm{e}^{-16^{n+1}}
for
n\geq 1
, then the semigroup
\{T^{t}\}_{t>0}
defined in Theorem
1.1
satisfies

\sqrt{\dfrac{7}{60}}\ S(f-\mathrm{F}(f))\leq G^{T}(f)\leq\sqrt{\dfrac{23}{60}}\ S(f-\mathrm{F}(f)),\ \ \ \forall f\in L_{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{A},\mu).

Consequently, for
1\leq p\leq\infty

\sqrt{\dfrac{7}{60}}\ \|S(f-\mathrm{F}(f))\|_{p}\leq\|G^{T}(f)\|_{p}\leq\sqrt{\dfrac{23}{60}}\ \|S(f-\mathrm{F}(f))\|_{p},\ \ \ \forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{A},\mu).

Remark 1.3
.

The choice of
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is not unique. There are many other sequences of
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfying the above pointwise inequalities with different constants, of course.

Theorem
1.2
shows that for any martingale, there exists a symmetric diffusion semigroup such that their corresponding square functions are equivalent. In this way, we can use the Littlewood-Paley-Stein inequality (
1.2
) to show the analogous inequality for martingale square function. This means that the Burkholder-Gundy square function inequality for martingales can be deduced from the Littlewood-Paley-Stein inequality for semigroups. In this way, the optimal constants in the martingale square function inequality can be applied to the setting of semigroups.

Recently, Xu has shown the vector-valued Littlewood-Paley-Stein inequality for analytic symmetric diffusion semigroups via holomorphic functional calculus (see
[
14
]
). His method is optimal in the sense that it offers the optimal orders of the best constants. For the martingale cotype case, he shows that the classical Poisson semigroup meets the optimal orders (see
[
14
, Proposition 8.5]
). Unfortunately, the optimal order of the reverse Littlewood-Paley-Stein inequality was left unsolved in
[
14
]
. In the following, we will utilize Theorem
1.2
to give the optimal order of
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{T}}_{p}
as
p\rightarrow\infty
.

To this end, we recall the following Burkholder-Gundy inequality.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
p^{*}=\max\{p,\frac{p}{p-1}\}
. Then

\left({p}^{*}-1\right)^{-1}\|S({f})\|_{{p}}\leq\|{f}\|_{{p}}\leq\left({p}^{*}-1\right)\|S({f})\|_{{p}}.

In particular,

\begin{array}[]{l}\|f\|_{{p}}\geq({p}-1)\|S(f)\|_{{p}}\ \text{ if }\quad 1<{p}\leq 2,\\
\|f\|_{{p}}\leq({p}-1)\|S(f)\|_{{p}}\ \text{ if }\quad 2\leq{p}<\infty.\end{array}

Moreover, the constant
p-1
is best possible.

Using Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
, we immediately get the following corollary, which solves a problem left open by Xu in
[
14
]
.

Corollary 1.5
.

For any symmetric diffusion semigroup
\{\mathsf{T}_{t}\}_{t>0}
, and for
p\geq 2

(1.5)

\|f-\mathrm{F}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim p\|G^{\mathsf{T}}(f)\|_{p},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega),

and
p
is the optimal order of
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{T}}_{p}
.

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

Indeed, (
1.5
) has already been proved in
[
14
, Theorem 8.1]
. Then it suffices to show that
p
is the optimal order of
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{T}}_{p}
, which is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
.
∎

can be extended to the vector-valued and noncommutative settings. We first consider the vector-valued case. Given a Banach space

, let
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
denote the

-space of strongly measurable

-integrable functions from

. Thus

is also a semigroup of contractions on

for any Banach space

For an
X
-valued

-martingale

, define the corresponding

-variant of the martingale square function for

as follows

with

and

be defined by (

).

Then

extended to

remains to be a strongly continuous semigroup of contractions on

, and

remains valid in the

-valued case. Define for

The following theorem is the vector-valued analogue of Theorem
1.2
:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
1\leq p,\ q<\infty
. Then there exist a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and two universal positive constants,

and

such that

(1.6)

cS_{q}\left(f-{\rm F}(f)\right)\leqslant G_{q}^{T}(f)\leqslant CS_{q}\left(f-{\rm F}(f)\right).

for any

.

\mathcal{M}
denote a finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a normal faithful normalized trace

, and
\left(\mathcal{M}_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}

\mathrm{w}^{*}

. For

, or simply

-space associated with

. As usual,

. It is well-known that there exists a unique normal faithful conditional expectation

from

Moreover,

extends to a contractive projection from

, which is still denoted by

. Similarly, we denote by

for

A noncommutative martingale with respect to

The difference sequence of

(with

-martingales and bounded

-martingales, as usual.
In the sequel, we will fix

.

Recall that

stands for the usual (right) modulus of operators, i.e.
|a|=

\left(a^{*}a\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Define the column and row square functions respectively for

S_{c}(x)=\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\left|dx_{n}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ S_{r}(x)=\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\left|dx_{n}^{*}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We refer to
[

]
,
[
9
]
,
[

]
and

]

Let
\left({\mathsf{T}}_{t}\right)_{t>0}
be a semigroup of operators on

. We say that
\left({\mathsf{T}}_{t}\right)_{t>0}
is a noncommutative symmetric diffusion semigroup if it satisfies the following conditions:

(\mathsf{a})
each
{\mathsf{T}}_{t}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is a unital, normal and completely positive;

(\mathsf{b})
for any

in the

-topology of
\mathcal{M}
when

;

(\mathsf{c})

is selfadjoint. Namely, for any

(\mathsf{d})
the extension of each

from

to

It is well-known that such a semigroup extends to a semigroup of contractions on

for any

Define

G^{\mathsf{T}}_{c}(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t\left|\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}{\mathsf{T}}_{t}(x)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

G^{\mathsf{T}}_{r}(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t\left|\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}{\mathsf{T}}_{t}(x)^{*}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We call

Note that

.

If

2.
Proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2

We will need the following elementary lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
S
is a positive injective continuous operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, then for
x\in\mathcal{H}

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|S^{t}(x)-x\|=0.

Proof.

By the spectral decomposition, there exists a resolution of the identity
\left\{E_{\lambda}\right\}_{0\leq\lambda\leq\|S\|}
such that

S=\int_{0}^{\|S\|}\lambda\mathrm{d}E_{\lambda}.

Since
S
is injective,
E_{0}=0
. Hence for
x\in\mathcal{H}

x=\int_{(0,\|S\|]}\mathrm{d}E_{\lambda}(x).

Thus

\displaystyle\|S^{t}(x)-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{(0,\|S\|]}(\lambda^{t}-1)\mathrm{d}E_{\lambda}(x)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\|S\|]}|\lambda^{t}-1|^{2}\mathrm{d}\langle E_{\lambda}(x),x\rangle.

By the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem and the fact that
|\lambda^{t}-1|\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
for
\lambda\neq 0
, we deduce the desired limit.
∎

Lemma 2.2

(Gershgorin circle theorem)
.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
be a complex
n\times n
matrix. If
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
A
, then

\lambda\in\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}D(a_{kk},\sum\limits_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}|a_{kj}|),

where
D(a_{kk},\sum\limits_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}|a_{kj}|)
is the closed disc of the complex plane centered at
a_{kk}
with radius
\sum\limits_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}|a_{kj}|
.

Proof.

Assume that
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
A
. Then there exists a nonzero vector
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
such that
Ax=\lambda x
. Choose
i
such that
|x_{i}|=\text{max}_{1\leq j\leq n}|x_{j}|>0
. Since

\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}x_{j}=\lambda x_{i},

we obtain by the triangle inequality

|\lambda-a_{ii}|=\left|\sum\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}a_{ij}\dfrac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\right|\leq\sum\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}|a_{ij}|,

which implies the desired result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

It is easy to prove the first part of Theorem
1.1
. Indeed, we can rewrite
T
as

T=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-a_{n-1})dE_{n},

since

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(a_{k}-a_{k-1})E_{k}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(a_{k}-a_{k-1})\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}dE_{i}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}dE_{i}\sum\limits_{k=i}^{n}(a_{k}-a_{k-1})=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(a_{n}-a_{i-1})dE_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-a_{n-1})dE_{n}.

Then by the continuous functional calculus and
dE_{p}dE_{q}=0
for
p\neq q
, we obtain

(2.1)

T^{t}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-a_{n-1})^{t}dE_{n},\quad t>0.

Hence we conclude that

is a positive contraction for any

on

(

). Actually, for

(2.2)

It suffices to show that

is a symmetric diffusion semigroup.
T^{t}
satisfies the conditions
(a)
and
(d)
since

is a positive contraction on

(

For the condition

In order to show that the semigroup

Proof of Theorem

.

We will calculate the derivative

since

on

.

if

. Then for

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=2}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}t(1-a_{i-1})^{t}(1-a_{j-1})^{t}\ln(1-a_{i-1})\ln(1-a_{j-1})dE_{i}(f)dE_{j}(f)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=2}^{n}\ln(1-a_{i-1})\ln(1-a_{j-1})dE_{i}(f)dE_{j}(f)\int_{0}^{\infty}t(1-a_{i-1})^{t}(1-a_{j-1})^{t}\mathrm{d}t

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=2}^{n}\dfrac{\ln(1-a_{i-1})\ln(1-a_{j-1})}{\ln^{2}\left[(1-a_{i-1})(1-a_{j-1})\right]}dE_{i}(f)dE_{j}(f).

We write

. Then

. Let

We need to determine the lower and upper bounds of the eigenvalues of

.

Let

be an eigenvalue of

. By Lemma

we have

, thus for

Thus for every

\dfrac{7}{60}\leq\lambda\leq\dfrac{23}{60}.

satisfies (

\dfrac{7}{60}I\leq B\leq\dfrac{23}{60}I.

Therefore by (
2
),

which implies

∎

Considering the interval
\left(t_{n},t_{n-1}\right)

\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}
, by the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}tb_{k}e^{-tb_{k}}dE_{k+1}(f)\right\|_{L_{q}(\left(t_{n},t_{n-1}\right),\frac{{\rm d}t}{t};X)}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left|\left\|tb_{n}e^{-tb_{n}}dE_{n+1}(f)\right\|_{L_{q}(\left(t_{n},t_{n-1}\right),\frac{{\rm d}t}{t};X)}-\left(\sum_{k\neq n,k\geq 1}\left\|tb_{n}e^{-tb_{n}}dE_{n+1}(f)\right\|_{L_{q}(\left(t_{n},t_{n-1}\right),\frac{{\rm d}t}{t};X)}\right)\right|

Thus we deduce

\displaystyle G_{q,M}^{T}(f)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}R_{n}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geqslant\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|R_{n,n}-\left(\sum_{k\neq n,k\geq 1}R_{n,k}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

To get the desired control, we are concerned with making
\sum\limits_{k\neq n,k\geq 1}R_{n,k}
sufficiently small.

We take
l_{k}
and
m_{k}

(k\geqslant 1)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{l_{k}}t^{q-1}e^{-t}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{q^{2}(k+2)}}\Gamma(q),

\Gamma(q)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q-1}e^{-t}{\rm d}t,\ \text{as }\ q\geq 1
.

\displaystyle t_{k}b_{k}q=l_{k},\quad t_{k-1}b_{k}q=m_{k},\qquad k\geqslant 1.

By elementary calculations, it follows that

\displaystyle t_{k}

\displaystyle=M\prod_{j=0}^{k}\dfrac{l_{j}}{m_{j}}\ \ \ \ \ \ {\rm for}\ k\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle b_{k}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{Mq}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\dfrac{m_{j}}{l_{j-1}}\ \ \ {\rm for}\ k\geqslant 1\ {\rm and}\ b_{0}=0.

\displaystyle|R_{n,n}|^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n-1}}\left\|tb_{n}e^{-tb_{n}}dE_{n+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{n}b_{n}q}^{t_{n-1}b_{n}q}t^{q-1}e^{-t}{\rm d}t\frac{\left\|dE_{n+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}}{q^{q}}

On the other hand, for

\displaystyle|R_{n,k}|^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{n}b_{k}q}^{t_{n-1}b_{k}q}t^{q-1}e^{-t}{\rm d}t\frac{\left\|dE_{k+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}}{q^{q}}

Similarly, for
k>n

\displaystyle|R_{n,k}|^{q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{m_{k}}^{\infty}t^{q-1}e^{-t}{\rm d}t\frac{\left\|dE_{k+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}}{q^{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(q)}{2^{q^{2}(k+2)}q^{q}}\left\|dE_{k+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}.

r_{n},r_{n,k}>0
be defined as follows

\displaystyle|r_{n}|^{q}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{2}{2^{q^{2}(n+2)}}\right)\frac{\Gamma(q)}{q^{q}}\left\|dE_{n+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q},

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(q)}{2^{q^{2}(n+1)}q^{q}}\left\|dE_{k+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}\ {\rm when}\ n>k\geq 1,

\displaystyle|r_{n,k}|^{q}

\displaystyle r_{k,k}

\displaystyle=0.

and

. By the definition of

It remains to estimate
\left\|\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}^{\infty}r^{(k)}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}
. We consider the measure space
(\mathbb{N}^{2},2^{\mathbb{N}^{2}},\mu)

\mu\left(\{(n,k)\}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\left(\frac{\Gamma(q)}{2^{q^{2}(n+1)}q^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}&,\ \text{if}&1\leq k<n\\
0&,\ \text{if}&k=n\\
\left(\frac{\Gamma(q)}{2^{q^{2}(k+2)}q^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}&,\ \text{if}&k>n\end{array}\right..

It is very easy to verify that

is a finite measure. Assume that

is the counting measure on

. Define

\displaystyle f:\mathbb{N}^{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

\displaystyle(n,k)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left\|dE_{k+1}(f)\right\|_{X}.

Then we deduce by the Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}^{\infty}r^{(k)}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{n,k}\right|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{k\in\mathbb{N}}f(n,k){\rm d}\mu\right|^{q}{\rm d}\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{k\in\mathbb{N}}f(n,k)^{q}{\rm d}\mu\right|\left(\int_{k\in\mathbb{N}}1{\rm d}\mu\right)^{q-1}{\rm d}\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

However, for

\displaystyle\left(\int_{k\in\mathbb{N}}1{\rm d}\mu\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(n\left(\frac{\Gamma(q)}{2^{q^{2}(n+1)}q^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\sum_{k>n}\left(\frac{\Gamma(q)}{2^{q^{2}(k+2)}q^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{\Gamma(q)}{q^{q}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q^{2}}}\left(\dfrac{n}{2^{q(n+1)}}+\sum_{k>n}\dfrac{1}{2^{q(k+2)}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}\left(\dfrac{\Gamma(q)}{q^{q}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q^{2}}}.

Moreover, in the same way

\displaystyle\left(\int_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{k\in\mathbb{N}}f(n,k)^{q}{\rm d}\mu{\rm d}\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle G_{q,M}^{T}(f)

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|R_{n,n}-\left(\sum_{k\neq n,k\geq 1}R_{n,k}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geqslant\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|r_{n}-\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}r_{n,k}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left\|r-\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{(k)}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{q}}\geqslant\left\|r\right\|_{\ell_{q}}-\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{(k)}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\left(1-\frac{2}{2^{3q^{2}}}\right)\frac{\Gamma(q)}{q^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}S_{q}(f-\mathrm{F}(f))-\frac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{\Gamma(q)}{q^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}S_{q}(f-\mathrm{F}(f))

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{4}\left(\dfrac{\Gamma(q)}{q^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}S_{q}(f-\mathrm{F}(f)).

Since

for all

, it is easy to deduce that

(3.3)

G_{q}^{T}(f)\geqslant G_{q,M}^{T}(f)\gtrsim S_{q}(f-{\rm F}(f)).

For the reversing inequality, we have by the convexity of norms

\displaystyle G_{q}^{T}(f)^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}tb_{k}e^{-tb_{k}}dE_{k+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q-1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}e^{-tb_{k}}\right)^{q-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}e^{-tb_{k}}\left\|dE_{k+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}{\rm d}t.

To this end, we choose a positive number
N>2
such that
b_{k+1}/b_{k}=m_{k+1}/l_{k}>N
for all
k\geq 1
. Then we have
b_{k}\leqslant\frac{N}{N-1}(b_{k}-b_{k-1})
, so

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}e^{-tb_{k}}\leqslant\frac{N}{N-1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{b_{k-1}}^{b_{k}}e^{-tx}{\rm d}x\leq\frac{N}{N-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tx}{\rm d}x\leqslant\frac{N}{(N-1)t}.

Then

\displaystyle G_{q}^{T}(f)^{q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{N}{N-1}\right)^{q-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[\left\|dE_{k+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}b_{k}e^{-tb_{k}}{\rm d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{N}{N-1}\right)^{q-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|dE_{k+1}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{N}{N-1}\right)^{q-1}S_{q}(f-{\rm F}(f))^{q}.

Combine (
3.3
) and (
3
), and we finally obtain

(3.5)

S_{q}(f-{\rm F}(f))\approx G_{q}^{T}(f).

This completes the proof.
∎

Recall that a Banach space
X
is of martingale cotype
q
(
q\geq 2
) if there exists a positive constant
c
such that every finite
X
-valued
L_{q}
-martingale
\left\{E_{n}(f)\right\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the following inequality

(3.6)

\mathbb{E}[S_{q}(f)^{q}]\leq c^{q}\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left\|E_{n}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q},

where
\mathbb{E}
denotes the underlying expectation. See
[
8
]
for more information about martingale cotype. Pisier’s famous renorming theorem asserts that
X
is of martingale cotype
q
iff
X
admits an equivalent norm that is
q
-convex. Besides, he also proved that
X
is of martingale cotype
q
iff all
X
-valued Walsh-Paley martingales satisfy (
3.6
). See
[
7
]
for more details.

Corollary 3.1
.

Assume
2\leq q<\infty
. If for every symmetric diffusion semigroup
\left\{T_{t}\right\}_{t>0}
and for every
1<p<\infty
there exists a constant
c
such that

(3.7)

\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}T_{t}(f)\right\|_{X}^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq c\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X),

then
X
is of martingale cotype
q
.

Proof.

It is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.6
and the definition of martingale cotype.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7

We end this article with the proof of Theorem
1.7
. Although it is similar to that of Theorem
1.2
, we give some details for reader’s convenience.

Note that for
1<p<\infty
, any noncommutative symmetric diffusion semigroup
\{{\mathsf{T}}_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic. See
[
4
]
for details. Then the column and row square functions are well-defined.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

At first, we show that
\{T^{t}\}_{t>0}
is a noncommutative symmetric diffusion semigroup by the same method as Theorem
1.1
. More precisely, the condition
(\mathsf{a})
follows from the fact that all conditional expectations on
{\mathcal{M}}
are unital normal and completely positive. As for the condition
(\mathsf{b})
, observe that
T_{t}(x)\rightarrow x
for
x\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\ (\forall n\geq 1)
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
in the
w^{*}
-topology of
{\mathcal{M}}
, and then use the fact that
\cup_{n\geq 1}{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is
w^{*}
-dense in
{\mathcal{M}}
.

The condition
(\mathsf{c})
is due to the fact that all conditional expectations on
{\mathcal{M}}
are selfadjoint. The condition
(\mathsf{d})
follows from the fact that any conditional expectation can extend to a completely contractive operator from
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
to
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Hence, we conclude that
\{T^{t}\}_{t>0}
is a noncommutative symmetric diffusion semigroup.

Then it suffices to prove (
1.8
) and (
1.9
). Observe that if we have

(4.1)

\dfrac{7}{60}S_{c}(x-\mathrm{F}(x))^{2}\leq G^{T}_{c}(x)^{2}\leq\dfrac{23}{60}S_{c}(x-\mathrm{F}(x))^{2},

then the desired inequality follows immediately since the square root function is operator increasing. We only need to consider the case where
x\in L_{p}({\mathcal{M}}_{n})
for a fixed arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By the same calculation as (
2
), we obtain

G^{T}_{c}(x)^{2}=\sum_{i,j=2}^{n}\dfrac{\ln(1-a_{i-1})\ln(1-a_{j-1})}{\ln^{2}\left[(1-a_{i-1})(1-a_{j-1})\right]}dx_{i}^{*}dx_{j}.

Since
dx_{i}^{*},dx_{j}
and
dx_{i}^{*}dx_{j}

(2\leq i,j\leq n)
are all measurable operators affiliated to
{\mathcal{M}}
, they have a common domain denoted by
\mathcal{K}
which is a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
.

For any
\xi\in\mathcal{K}
,

\displaystyle\langle G^{T}_{c}(x)^{2}(\xi),\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=2}^{n}\dfrac{\ln(1-a_{i-1})\ln(1-a_{j-1})}{\ln^{2}\left[(1-a_{i-1})(1-a_{j-1})\right]}\langle dx_{i}^{*}dx_{j}(\xi),\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=2}^{n}\dfrac{\ln(1-a_{i-1})\ln(1-a_{j-1})}{\ln^{2}\left[(1-a_{i-1})(1-a_{j-1})\right]}\langle dx_{j}(\xi),dx_{i}(\xi)\rangle,

which implies that

\dfrac{7}{60}\sum_{i=2}^{n}\langle dx_{i}(\xi),dx_{i}(\xi)\rangle\leq\langle G^{T}_{c}(x)^{2}(\xi),\xi\rangle\leq\dfrac{23}{60}\sum_{i=2}^{n}\langle dx_{i}(\xi),dx_{i}(\xi)\rangle.

Using the fact that
\langle dx_{i}(\xi),dx_{i}(\xi)\rangle=\langle dx_{i}^{*}dx_{i}(\xi),(\xi)\rangle
, we obtain the desired inequality (
4.1
). As for the row square function, it can be deduced by the same way.
∎

We introduce the norms in the Hardy spaces of martingales defined in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
x=\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
be an
L_{p}
-martingale. Set, for
p\geq 2

\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}=\max\left\{\left\|S_{c}(x)\right\|_{p},\left\|S_{r}(x)\right\|_{p}\right\}

and for
p<2

\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}=\inf\left\{\left\|S_{c}(y)\right\|_{p}+\left\|S_{r}(z)\right\|_{p}\right\},

where the infimum runs over all decompositions
x=y+z
of
x
as sums of two
L_{p}
martingales.

Similarly, we introduce the semigroup analogues of these norms. Let
\|\cdot\|_{p,F}
denote the
p
-norm of semigroup square function defined as follows: for
x\in L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})

\quad\|x\|_{p,F}=\max\left\{\|G_{c}^{T}(x)\|_{p},\ \|G_{r}^{T}(x)\|_{p}\right\}\ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ 2\leq p<\infty,

and

\|x\|_{p,F}=\inf\left\{\|G_{c}^{T}(y)\|_{p}+\|G_{r}^{T}(z)\|_{p}:x=y+z\right\}\ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ 1\leq p<2.

Note that our definition of
\|x\|_{p,F}
is different from that of
\|x\|_{p,F}
in
[
4
, Chapter 6]
when
1\leq p<2
.

Remark 4.1
.

It should be noted that when
1\leq p<\infty
, we have the same equivalence for the
p
-norm of their corresponding square functions like the classical setting for the noncommutative symmetric diffusion semigroup in Theorem
1.7
, i.e.

\sqrt{\dfrac{7}{60}}\ \|x\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p}}\leq\|x\|_{p,F}\leq\sqrt{\dfrac{23}{60}}\ \|x\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p}},\ \ \ \forall x\in L_{p}({\mathcal{M}}),

which follows from (
1.8
) and (
1.9
).

This means that for any fixed

In this paper, we give a slightly more general result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider
N\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
a Dirichlet character modulo

This result is not particularly surprising, but we prove it here for lack of reference in the literature. We also give the complete list of triples
(N,k,\chi)

,

Now, in order to prove Theorems

and

\displaystyle\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=\sum_{f\mid M\mid N}\beta(N/M)\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(M),\chi),

In the case of trivial character, (

For comparison, first consider the full space

Theorem 1.5

Consider

,

One might guess that a similar equidistribution property holds for the newspace

But we are still able prove an equidistribution property if we only consider characters of a given conductor.

Theorem 1.6

Consider

, and

a Dirichlet character modulo

Lemma 2.2

3.
The dimension of
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)

Next, we turn our attention to the newspace. We start by giving the classical dimension formula for
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
.

Formula 3.1

(
[
2
, Corollary 13.3.7]
)
.

Let
\beta
be the multiplicative function defined by

\beta(p^{r})=\begin{cases}-2&\text{if }r=1\\
1&\text{if }r=2\\
0&\text{if }r\geq 3,\end{cases}

(3.1)

and let
N\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
of conductor
f={\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
.
Then

\displaystyle\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=\sum_{f\mid M\mid N}\beta(N/M)\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(M),\chi).

(3.2)

We then write each of the
\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
terms appearing in Formula
3.1
as a linear combination of multiplicative functions. In the following,
v_{p}(\cdot)
denotes
p
-adic valuation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
be a primitive character modulo
f
with
f\mid N
and where
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. Factor
N=p_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{r_{t}}
, and write
f=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{\alpha_{t}}
(the
\alpha_{i}
are possibly
0
here). Then factor
\chi
as
\chi=\chi_{p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}\cdots\chi_{p_{t}^{\alpha_{t}}}
where each
\chi_{p^{\alpha}}
is a primitive character modulo
p^{\alpha}

[
13
, p. 71]
. We also consider
\chi_{p^{0}}
to be the trivial character modulo
1
even for
p\nmid N
. Then define the three arithmetic functions
\rho
,
\rho^{\prime}
, and
\sigma
on prime powers as

\displaystyle\rho(p^{r})

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\mod p^{r}\\
x^{2}+x+1\equiv 0\mod p^{r}\end{subarray}}\mspace{-35mu}\chi_{p^{\alpha}}(x)&\text{ if }r\geq\alpha\ \ (\text{where }\alpha=v_{p}(f))\\
1&\text{ if }1\leq r<\alpha\end{cases}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(p^{r})

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\mod p^{r}\\
x^{2}+1\equiv 0\mod p^{r}\end{subarray}}\mspace{-35mu}\chi_{p^{\alpha}}(x)&\text{ if }r\geq\alpha\ \ (\text{where }\alpha=v_{p}(f))\\
1&\text{ if }1\leq r<\alpha\end{cases}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sigma(p^{r})

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid p^{r}\\
\gcd(d,p^{r}/d)\mid p^{r-\alpha}\end{subarray}}\mspace{-20mu}\phi(\gcd(d,p^{r}/d))&\text{ if }r\geq\alpha\ \ (\text{where }\alpha=v_{p}(f))\\
1&\text{ if }1\leq r<\alpha,\end{cases}

(3.5)

and extend these three functions multiplicatively.
Then

\displaystyle\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=\frac{k-1}{12}\psi(N)-c_{3}(k)\rho(N)-c_{4}(k)\rho^{\prime}(N)-\frac{1}{2}\sigma(N)+c_{0}(k,\chi).

(3.6)

Proof.

Recall that the space
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
is unchanged whether we consider
\chi
to be a character modulo
N
or modulo
f
. And none of the terms in Formula
2.1
are affected in either case. So it is perfectly well-defined to ask about
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
here and use Formula
2.1
to compute its dimension.

Now, comparing (
3.3
) with Formula
2.1
, we first need to show that

\displaystyle\rho(N)=\mspace{-30mu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\mod N\\
x^{2}+x+1\equiv 0\mod N\end{subarray}}\mspace{-35mu}\chi(x).

(3.7)

Recall that
N=p_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{r_{t}}
, and observe that
r_{i}\geq\alpha_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq t
. Thus by (
3.3
) and the Chinese Remainder Theorem,

\displaystyle\rho(N)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\rho(p_{i}^{r_{i}})

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}\mod p_{i}^{r_{i}}\\
x_{i}^{2}+x_{i}+1\equiv 0\mod p_{i}^{r_{i}}\end{subarray}}\mspace{-35mu}\chi_{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}(x_{i})

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}+1\equiv 0\mod p_{1}^{r_{1}}\\
\vskip-9.3894pt\\
\vdots\\
x_{t}^{2}+x_{t}+1\equiv 0\mod p_{t}^{r_{t}}\end{subarray}}\ \prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\chi_{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}(x_{i})

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\mod N\\
x^{2}+x+1\equiv 0\mod N\end{subarray}}\ \prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\chi_{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}(x)

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\mod N\\
x^{2}+x+1\equiv 0\mod N\end{subarray}}\chi(x).

(3.12)

By an identical argument, we also have that

\rho^{\prime}(N)=\mspace{-30mu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\mod N\\
x^{2}+1\equiv 0\mod N\end{subarray}}\mspace{-35mu}\chi(x)\,.

(3.13)

Then comparing (
3.5
) with Formula
2.1
, we also need to show that

\sigma(N)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid N\\
\gcd(d,N/d)\mid N/f\end{subarray}}\mspace{-20mu}\phi(\gcd(d,N/d)).

(3.14)

By (
3.5
) and the Chinese Remainder Theorem,

\displaystyle\sigma(N)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\sigma(p_{i}^{r_{i}})

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{i}\mid p_{i}^{r_{i}}\\
\gcd(d_{i},p_{i}^{r_{i}}/d_{i})\mid{p_{i}}^{r_{i}-\alpha_{i}}\end{subarray}}\mspace{-20mu}\phi(\gcd(d_{i},p_{i}^{r_{i}}/d_{i}))

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}\mid p_{1}^{r_{1}}\\
\gcd(d_{1},p_{1}^{r_{1}}/d_{1})\mid{p_{1}}^{r_{1}-\alpha_{1}}\\
\vskip-9.3894pt\\
\vdots\\
d_{t}\mid p_{t}^{r_{t}}\\
\gcd(d_{t},p_{t}^{r_{t}}/d_{t})\mid{p_{t}}^{r_{t}-\alpha_{t}}\\
\end{subarray}}\mspace{-10mu}\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\phi(\gcd(d_{i},p_{i}^{r_{i}}/d_{i}))

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}\mid p_{1}^{r_{1}}\\
\gcd(d_{1},p_{1}^{r_{1}}/d_{1})\mid{p_{1}}^{r_{1}-\alpha_{1}}\\
\vskip-9.3894pt\\
\vdots\\
d_{t}\mid p_{t}^{r_{t}}\\
\gcd(d_{t},p_{t}^{r_{t}}/d_{t})\mid{p_{t}}^{r_{t}-\alpha_{t}}\\
\end{subarray}}\mspace{-10mu}\phi\left(\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\gcd(d_{i},p_{i}^{r_{i}}/d_{i})\right)

(3.18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid N\\
\gcd(d,N/d)\mid N/f\end{subarray}}\phi(\gcd(d,N/d)).

(3.19)

Then substituting these three identities into Formula
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=\frac{k-1}{12}\psi(N)-c_{3}(k)\rho(N)-c_{4}(k)\rho^{\prime}(N)-\frac{1}{2}\sigma(N)+c_{0}(k,\chi),

(3.20)

as desired.
∎

4.
A theory of partial Dirichlet convolutions

Now that we have written
\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
as a linear combination of multiplicative functions, observe that the summation in Formula
3.1
resembles a Dirichlet convolution with some of the terms missing. We will refer to sums of this form as “partial Dirichlet convolutions”. We then give a lemma to compute these partial Dirichlet convolutions.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\geq 1
be a fixed positive integer. Then for multiplicative functions
\beta
and
\theta
with
\theta(f)\neq 0
, define the function
\theta_{f}
by
\theta_{f}(N):=\theta(fN)/\theta(f)
. Then
\theta_{f}
is also a multiplicative function, where

\displaystyle\theta_{f}(p^{r})=\begin{cases}\theta(p^{r})&\text{if }v_{p}(f)=0\\
\frac{\theta(p^{r+v_{p}(f)})}{\theta(p^{v_{p}(f)})}&\text{in general}.\\
\end{cases}

(4.1)

Furthermore, the partial convolution of
\beta
with
\theta
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{f\mid M\mid N}\beta(N/M)\theta(M)=\theta(f)\cdot\beta{*}\theta_{f}(N/f).

(4.2)

Here,
v_{p}(\cdot)
denotes
p
-adic valuation, and
*
denotes ordinary Dirichlet convolution.

Proof.

First, we show that
\theta_{f}(N)
actually is a multiplicative function of the claimed form. We have,

\displaystyle\theta_{f}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta(fN)}{\theta(f)}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\mid fN}\frac{\theta(p^{v_{p}(fN)})}{\theta(p^{v_{p}(f)})}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\mid fN}\begin{cases}\frac{\theta(p^{v_{p}(f)+v_{p}(N)})}{\theta(p^{v_{p}(f)})}&\text{ if }v_{p}(N)\geq 1\\
\frac{\theta(p^{v_{p}(f)})}{\theta(p^{v_{p}(f)})}&\text{ if }v_{p}(N)=0\end{cases}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\mid N}\frac{\theta(p^{v_{p}(f)+v_{p}(N)})}{\theta(p^{v_{p}(f)})}

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\mid N}\begin{cases}\theta(p^{v_{p}(N)})&\text{if }v_{p}(f)=0\\
\frac{\theta(p^{v_{p}(N)+v_{p}(f)})}{\theta(p^{v_{p}(f)})}&\text{in general},\end{cases}

(4.7)

as claimed.

Next, observe that we can convert the desired summation over
f\mid M\mid N
into a summation over
L\mid N/f
where
M=f\cdot L
. This yields

\displaystyle\sum_{f\mid M\mid N}\beta(N/M)\theta(M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\mid N/f}\beta(N/(fL))\theta(fL)

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\theta(f)\cdot\sum_{L\mid N/f}\beta((N/f)/L)\theta_{f}(L)

(4.9)

\displaystyle=\theta(f)\cdot\beta{*}\theta_{f}(N/f),

(4.10)

as desired.
∎

We note here that the condition
\theta(f)\neq 0
in the above lemma is not restrictive. If
\theta(M)=0
for all
f\mid M\mid N
, then
\sum_{f\mid M\mid N}\beta(N/M)\theta(M)=0
. Otherwise, we can always rewrite this partial
convolution as
\sum_{f^{\prime}\mid M\mid N}\beta(N/M)\theta(M)
where
\theta(f^{\prime})\neq 0
. Factor
N=p_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{r_{t}}
, and write
f=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{\alpha_{t}}
(the
\alpha_{i}
are possibly
0
here). Then for
1\leq i\leq t
, let
\delta_{i}
be the minimal integer in the range
\alpha_{i}\leq\delta_{i}\leq r_{i}
such that
\theta(p_{i}^{\delta_{i}})\neq 0
. (These
\delta_{i}
are guaranteed to exist, since otherwise, we would have
\theta(M)=0
for all
f\mid M\mid N
.) Then let
f^{\prime}:=p_{1}^{\delta_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{\delta_{t}}
. Observe that
f^{\prime}
is constructed here to be the minimum element (with respect to the divisibility ordering) such that
\theta(f^{\prime})\neq 0
; if
f^{\prime}\nmid M
for some
f\mid M\mid N
, then
\theta(M)=0
. Thus we can rewrite the partial convolution of
\beta
with
\theta
as

\displaystyle\sum_{f\mid M\mid N}\beta(N/M)\theta(M)=\sum_{f^{\prime}\mid M\mid N}\beta(N/M)\theta(M),

(4.11)

where
\theta(f^{\prime})\neq 0
.

For the particular function
\beta
given in Formula
3.1
, we also note how to compute an ordinary Dirichlet convolution of
\beta
with
\theta
. This lemma follows immediately from (
3.1
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\beta
be the multiplicative function defined in Formula
3.1
, and
\theta
be any other multiplicative function. Then
\beta{*}\theta
is the multiplicative function defined by

\displaystyle\beta{*}\theta(p^{r})=\begin{cases}\theta(p)-2&\text{ if }r=1\\
\theta(p^{r})-2\theta(p^{r-1})+\theta(p^{r-2})&\text{ if }r\geq 2.\\
\end{cases}

(4.12)

5.
An explicit dimension formula for the newspace

Throughout this entire section, let
N\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
of conductor
f={\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}

\beta

3.1

Combining Formula

\displaystyle\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)

First,

Additionally, observe that

\displaystyle\beta{*}\psi_{f}(N/f)=\beta{*}\sigma_{f}(N/f)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho(f)=\rho^{\prime}(f)=0

as well,

.

From Theorem

\displaystyle\ \ -c_{4}(k)\cdot\rho^{\prime}(f)\cdot\beta{*}\rho_{f}^{\prime}(N/f)-\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2^{\omega(f)}\cdot\beta{*}\sigma_{f}(N/f)+c_{0}(k,\chi)\cdot\mu(N/f).

First, we show that

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k-1}{12}\cdot\frac{N}{\nu(N)}-\frac{1}{3}\cdot 2\cdot 2^{\omega(N)}

(6.23)

7.
Disproof of a conjecture from Martin

In
[
7
, Conjecture 27]
, Greg Martin conjectured that
\dim S_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
takes on all possible non-negative integers. We show that this is in fact not true. The smallest non-negative integer not achieved by
\dim S_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
is
67846
. In order to prove this fact, we use the same method and bounds as in (
6.26
) and (
6.29
).

We take
B=67846
. Then following (
6.26
) and (
6.29
),

\displaystyle\dim S_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))-67846\geq\frac{N}{\nu(N)}\left[\frac{k-1}{12}-E(N)\right]

(7.1)

where

\displaystyle E(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{3}\cdot\frac{\nu(N)2^{\omega(N)}}{N}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\cdot\frac{\nu(N)}{\sqrt{N}}+(67846+1)\cdot\frac{\nu(N)}{N}

(7.2)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{3}\cdot\frac{21.234\cdot N^{1/16}\cdot 4.862\cdot N^{1/4}}{N}+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\cdot\frac{21.234\cdot N^{1/16}}{\sqrt{N}}+67847\cdot\frac{21.234\cdot N^{1/16}}{N},

(7.3)

which will be
<\frac{1}{12}
for all
N\geq 58260767
.
This means that for
N\geq 58260767
, we will have
\dim S_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))>67846
. Then checking all the
N<58260767
by computer shows that we never have
\dim S_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))=67846
; see
[
10
]
for the code.

We observe here that this is in the trivial character case, so the tools in Martin’s paper (e.g.
[
7
, Proposition 26]
) are sufficient to prove this result.
It appears the main difference is just that we are using slightly better bounds and computed up to larger values of
N
.

8.
A character equidistribution property

We also obtain another result for free from the explicit dimension formula given in Theorem
1.4
. In
[
8
]
, Kimball Martin considered the decomposition of
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
into
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))=S_{k}^{{\textnormal{new}},+}(\Gamma_{0}(N))\oplus S_{k}^{{\textnormal{new}},-}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
. He then used an explicit dimension formula for
S_{k}^{{\textnormal{new}},\pm}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
to show the asymptotic equidistribution of the two terms in this direct sum
[
8
, Corollary 2.3]
.

In our case, recall that
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
can be decomposed as
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(N))=\bigoplus_{\chi}S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)

[
2
, Theorem 7.3.4]
.
We can then similarly use our explicit dimension formula for
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
to ask about character equidistribution in this direct sum.

For comparison, first consider the full space
S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))=\bigoplus_{\chi}S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
. Then in the notation of Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=\frac{k-1}{12}\psi(N)-c_{3}(k)\rho(N)-c_{4}(k)\rho^{\prime}(N)-\frac{1}{2}\sigma(N)+c_{0}(k,\chi).

(8.1)

Here, we have directly from Formulas
5.1
-
5.4
that

\displaystyle\psi(N)

\displaystyle\geq N\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\rho(N)

\displaystyle=O(N^{\varepsilon})

(8.2)

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(N)

\displaystyle=O(N^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\sigma(N)

\displaystyle=O(N^{1/2+\varepsilon})

for any
\varepsilon>0
.
Note that
\rho
,
\rho^{\prime}
, and
\sigma
depend on
\chi
, but the largest term
\psi
does not. This immediately gives the following result.

Theorem
1.5
.

Consider
N\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
a Dirichlet character modulo
N
with
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
.
Recall that
S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))=\bigoplus_{\chi}S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
.
Then asymptotically in
N
, the terms in this direct sum are equidistributed by character.

We note here precisely what “asymptotic equidistribution by character” means.
Consider
N\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
a Dirichlet character modulo
N
with
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
.
Let
s(N,k,\chi)
denote
\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
,
s(N,k)
denote
\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))=\sum_{\chi}\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
, and
n(N,k)
denote the number of
\chi
in this sum. Further restrict to
N,k
where
s(N,k)\neq 0
. Then Theorem
1.5
is saying that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{s(N,k,\chi)}{s(N,k)}-\frac{1}{n(N,k)}\right\lvert<\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }k,\chi,

(8.3)

for sufficiently large
N
.

One might guess that a similar equidistribution property holds for the newspace
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(N))=\bigoplus_{\chi}S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
.
However, this is wildly untrue; recall from Proposition
6.1
that
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
is trivial for any characters
\chi
with
2\mid f
and
2\parallel N/f
. Even for characters not in this infinite family, one can see from Theorem
1.4
that
\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
depends heavily on the conductor
f={\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
.

However, since the main term
\psi(f)\cdot\beta{*}\psi_{f}(N/f)
of Theorem
1.4
depends only on the conductor of
\chi
(i.e. not on
\chi
itself), we still have an equidistribution property if we only consider characters of a given conductor. We have from (
5.39
) that

\displaystyle\psi(f)\cdot\psi_{f}(N/f)

\displaystyle=\Omega(N^{1-\varepsilon})\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\rho(f)\cdot\beta{*}\rho_{f}(N/f)

\displaystyle=O(N^{\varepsilon})

(8.4)

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(f)\cdot\rho_{f}^{\prime}(N/f)

\displaystyle=O(N^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle 2^{\omega(f)}\cdot\sigma_{f}(N/f)

\displaystyle=O(\sqrt{N})

for any
\varepsilon>0
.
The following result then follows immediately from Theorem
1.4
(with the second claim using the facts that
c_{3}(k)=c_{4}(k)=0
when
k\equiv 1\mod 12
, and that
2^{\omega(f)}\cdot\sigma_{f}(N/f)
depends only on the conductor of
\chi
).

Theorem
1.6
.

Consider
N\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
a Dirichlet character modulo
N
with
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
.
Recall that
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(N))=\bigoplus_{\chi}S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
.
Then asymptotically in
N
, the terms in this direct sum corresponding to characters of a given conductor are equidistributed by character.
Furthermore, if
k\equiv 1\mod 12
, then for all
N
, the terms corresponding to characters of a given conductor are exactly equidistributed by character.

More precisely, let
s^{\prime}(N,k,\chi)
denote
\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
,
n_{f}^{\prime}(N,k)
denote the number of
\chi
with conductor
f
, and
s_{f}^{\prime}(N,k)
denote the sum of
\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
over the
n_{f}^{\prime}(N,k)
characters
\chi
of conductor
f
. Further restrict to
N,k,\chi
where
s_{f}^{\prime}(N,k)\neq 0
. Then Theorem
1.6
is saying that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{s^{\prime}(N,k,\chi)}{s_{f}^{\prime}(N,k)}-\frac{1}{n_{f}^{\prime}(N,k)}\right\lvert<\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }k,\chi,

(8.5)

for sufficiently large
N
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{s^{\prime}(N,k,\chi)}{s_{f}^{\prime}(N,k)}=\frac{1}{n_{f}^{\prime}(N,k)}\qquad\text{for all }k,\chi\text{ where }k\equiv 1\!\!\!\!\mod 12,

(8.6)

for all
N
.

9.
A decomposition of certain
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)

In Proposition
6.1
, we showed that if
2\mid f
and
2\parallel N/f
, then
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
is trivial. However, one might ask for an intuitive reason why this is the case. In particular, since all of
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
is old, one could ask where each of the forms in
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
is coming from. By Atkin-Lehner-Li theory (e.g.
[
2
, Corollary 13.3.6]
), we have that
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N/2),\chi)\oplus S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N/2),\chi)|_{k}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\subseteq S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
. And one can verify directly by Formula
2.1
that in this case, we have
\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=2\cdot\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N/2),\chi)
. This means that in fact
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N/2),\chi)\oplus S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N/2),\chi)|_{k}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. This yields the following result.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
of conductor
f={\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. Also assume that
2\mid f
and
2\parallel N/f
. Then each
g\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
can be decomposed uniquely as
g=g_{1}+g_{2}|_{k}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where
g_{1},g_{2}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N/2),\chi)
.

This proposition provides some additional understanding as to why
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
is trivial when
2\mid f
and
2\parallel N/f
. However, one might still ask if there is a nice way to find the decomposition promised in Proposition
9.1
. Of course, for specific
N
,
k
, and
\chi
, one can calculate bases for each of the spaces involved and find the decomposition computationally. However, we surmise that there might be a more natural way to find this decomposition.

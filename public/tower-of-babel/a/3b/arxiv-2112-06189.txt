An original model for multi-target learning of logical rules for knowledge graph reasoning
Yuliang Wei, Haotian Li, Guodong Xin, Yao Wang, Bailing Wang

Abstract
Large-scale knowledge graphs provide structured representations of human knowledge. However, as it is impossible to collect all knowledge, knowledge graphs are usually incomplete. Reasoning based on existing facts paves a way to discover missing facts. In this paper, we study the problem of learning logical rules for reasoning on knowledge graphs for completing missing factual triplets. Learning logical rules equips a model with strong interpretability as well as the ability to generalize to similar tasks. We propose a model able to fully use training data which also considers multi-target scenarios. In addition, considering the deficiency in evaluating the performance of models and the quality of mined rules, we further propose two novel indicators to help with the problem. Experimental results empirically demonstrate that our model outperforms state-of-the-art methods on five benchmark datasets. The results also prove the effectiveness of the indicators.

Preliminaries and two novel indicators

2.1
Knowledge graph reasoning

Knowledge graph
can be modeled as a collection of
factual triplets
\mathcal{G}=\{(s,p,o)\ |\ s,o\in\mathcal{E},p\in\mathcal{P}\}
,
with
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{P}

(

.

.

The subgraph relating to a particular predicate

being the predicate:

\mathcal{G}(\texttt{p}_{i})=\{(s,p,o)\ |\ s,o\in\mathcal{E},\texttt{p}_{i}\in\mathcal{P},p=\texttt{p}_{i}\}

Definition 1

(Directed Labeled Multigraph)
.

A

Because of its graph structure, a knowledge graph can be regarded as a directed labeled multigraph

[
39
]
. In this paper, ”graph” is used to refer to ”directed labeled multigraph”
for the sake of simplicity.
G(\texttt{p})=(V(\texttt{p}),E(\texttt{p}))

\mathcal{G}(\texttt{p})
.

m=|V|

Particularly in a KG,

In a graph

and

\displaystyle deg^{+}(v)

\displaystyle=|\{(u,v)\ |\ \exists u\in V,(u,v)\in E\}|

(1)

\displaystyle deg^{-}(v)

\displaystyle=|\{(v,u)\ |\ \exists u\in V,(v,u)\in E\}|

(2)

Furthermore in KGs, the

\displaystyle deg^{+}_{q}(v)

\displaystyle=|\{(u,v)\ |\ \exists u\in V,(u,q,v)\in\mathcal{G}\}|

\displaystyle deg^{-}_{q}(v)

\displaystyle=|\{(v,u)\ |\ \exists u\in V,(v,q,u)\in\mathcal{G}\}|,

(4)

which are exactly the number of entities connected by relation

In this work, the task of knowledge graph reasoning is regarded as a

,

For example, the rule

All rule patterns of length

A

Traditionally, the logic reasoning problem is to

The task here is considered to be composed of a

q

through inducing over the whole query
(q,h,T)
.
Thus, given maximum length
L
, we assign a single

i
.
e

to a set of rule paths

that connects
h
and
t

1
1

1

In Neural LP framework, they view

query

the answer to the query.
Then a confidence

is assigned to one particular path

.

:

\{p_{i}(h,t)\Rightarrow q(h,t)\ |\ p_{i}\rhd\textbf{p},\textbf{p}\in\mathbb{H}^{L},t\in T\}\ :\ \alpha

(6)

During inference, given an entity

2.2
Novel indicators for reasoning performance

In the following, we propose two novel metrics,
saturation
and
bifurcation
,

to help evaluate a model for knowledge graph reasoning. More specifically, we analyze the reasoning complexity
from the inherent attributes of the graph structure
G
corresponding to a KG

.

Definition 2

(Macro Reasoning Saturation)
.

Given a query
q\in\mathcal{P}
and the maximum length
L
of a rule pattern
\textbf{p}_{l}\in\mathbb{H}^{L}
,
the
macro reasoning saturation
of
\textbf{p}_{l}
in relation to predicate
q
, i.e.
\gamma^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}
,
is the percentage of triplets
(h_{i},q,t_{j})
in subgraph
\mathcal{G}(q)

such that
\textbf{p}_{l}(h_{i},t_{j})\Rightarrow q(h_{i},t_{j})
.

We compute the macro reasoning saturation
\gamma^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}
using the following equation:

\gamma^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}=\frac{|\{(h,q,t)\ |\ (h,q,t)\in\mathcal{G}(q),\textbf{p}_{l}(h,t)\Rightarrow q(h,t)\}|}{n^{q}},

(7)

with
n^{q}=|\mathcal{G}(q)|
being the number of edges (
i
.
e
. the number of triplets) in
G(q)
.
We can reasonably say that the larger
\gamma^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}
grows, the more likely
\textbf{p}_{l}

can be as a proper inference of the query
q
. When
\gamma^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}
equals 1, it means
we can reason out every factual triplets in
\mathcal{G}(q)
through at least one rule path
following the pattern
\textbf{p}_{l}
.

Definition 3

(Micro Reasoning Saturation)
.

Given the maximum length
L
of a rule pattern, we define the
micro reasoning saturation of pattern
\textbf{p}_{l}\in\mathbb{H}^{L}
as following.
Firstly, for a specific triplet
\texttt{tri}=(h,q,t)\in\mathcal{G}
,
i.e.
\delta^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{\texttt{tri}}
, is the percentage of the number of paths

\textbf{p}_{l_{i}}\rhd\textbf{p}_{l}
such that
\textbf{p}_{l}(h,t)\Rightarrow q(h,t)

as to all paths from
h
to
t
.

The equation to compute
\delta^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{\texttt{tri}}
is

\delta^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{\texttt{tri}}=\frac{|\{\textbf{p}_{l_{i}}\ |\ \textbf{p}_{l_{i}}\rhd\textbf{p}_{l},(h,q,t)\in\mathcal{G},\textbf{p}_{l}(h,t)\Rightarrow q(h,t)\}|}{|\{\textbf{p}_{k_{j}}\ |\ \textbf{p}_{k_{j}}\rhd\textbf{p}_{k},(h,q,t)\in\mathcal{G},\forall\textbf{p}_{k}\in\mathbb{H}^{L},\textbf{p}_{k}(h,t)\Rightarrow q(h,t)\}|}

(8)

Then, we average
\delta^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{\texttt{tri}}
on all triplets
(h,q,t)\in\mathcal{G}(q)

and get the
micro reasoning saturation
of pattern
\textbf{p}_{l}\in\mathbb{H}^{L}

for query
q
:

\delta^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}=\frac{1}{n^{q}}\sum_{\texttt{tri}\in\mathcal{G}(q)}{\delta^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{\texttt{tri}}}

(9)

In Eqs. (
7
) and (
9
),

\gamma^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}
and
\delta^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}
assess how easy it is to

q
following the pattern
\textbf{p}_{l}
respectively from a macro and a micro perspective.
The higher the two indicators are, the easier we are to gain the inference
that
\textbf{p}_{l}(h,t)\Rightarrow q(h,t)
.
In order to obtain an overall result, we define the comprehensive reasoning saturation

\eta^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}
by combining the two indicators through multiplication.

\eta^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}=\gamma^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}\times\delta^{\textbf{p}_{l}}_{q}

(10)

The other indicator,

, is proposed as follows.

Definition 4

(Bifurcation)
.

Given a query
q
, the
\lambda

forward bifurcation
is the proportion of head entity
h\in V(q)

with

within all head entities in
\mathcal{G}(q)
.
Likewise, the
\lambda

\mathcal{G}(q)

Bifurcation(s) can be computed on both forward and backward reasoning directions and are formulated as follows:

\displaystyle fw\mbox{-}bifur^{q}(\lambda)=\frac{|\{h\ |\ h\in V(q),deg^{-}_{q}(h)\geq\lambda\}|}{|\{h\ |\ h\in V(q)\}|}

(11)

\displaystyle bw\mbox{-}bifur^{q}(\lambda)=\frac{|\{t\ |\ t\in V(q),deg^{+}_{q}(t)\geq\lambda\}|}{|\{t\ |\ t\in V(q)\}|}

(12)

fw\mbox{-}bifur^{q}(\lambda)
and

indicate the problem scale
when performing backward and forward reasoning in case that there are multiple targets.
As is shown in Fig.

Combining learning structures and parameters, Neural LP

3.1
Neural LP for logic reasoning

Since Neural LP originally borrows the idea of the work of TensorLog

[

,
19
]

that connects inference using logical rules with sparse matrix multiplication.
In a KG involving a set of entities
\mathcal{E}

\mathcal{P}

Then

[

]

unique paths following the pattern

from

to
e_{j}

[
14
]
.

For example, every KG entity

in Fig.
1

vector of length

. For every predicate

and
every pair of entities

0&0&0&0&0&0&x_{2}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1}&0&0&0&0&0&z_{1}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1}&0&0&0&0&0&z_{2}\\

By setting
\mathrm{v}_{z_{1}}=[0,0,1,0,0,0]^{\top}
as the one-hot vector of

\mathrm{v}_{z_{1}}^{\top}
on the left,

The resultant

Neural LP

[
52
]
inherits the idea of TensorLog. Given a query

q(h,t)
, after
L
steps of reasoning, the score of the query induced through rule pattern

\textbf{p}_{s}
of length
L
is computed as

\text{score}(t\ |\ q,h,\textbf{p}_{s})=\mathrm{v}^{T}_{h}\prod_{l=1}^{L}\mathrm{M}^{l}\cdot\mathrm{v}_{t},

(13)

where
\mathrm{M}^{l}
is the adjacency matrix of the predicate used at
l
-th hop.

The operators above are used to learn for query
q
by calculating the weighted sum
of all possible patterns:

\sum_{s}{\alpha_{s}\prod_{k\in\beta_{s}}{\mathrm{M}_{p_{k}}}},

(14)

where
s
indexes over all potential patterns with maximum length of
L
,

\alpha_{s}
is the confidence score associated with the rule
\textbf{p}_{s}

and
\beta_{s}
is the ordered list of predicates appearing in
\textbf{p}_{s}
.

To summarize, we update the score function in Eq. (
13
)
by finding an appropriate
\alpha
in

\varphi(t\ |\ q,h)=\mathrm{v}^{T}_{h}\sum_{s}{\alpha_{s}\cdot\left(\prod_{k\in\beta_{s}}{\mathrm{M}_{p_{k}}}\cdot\mathrm{v}_{t}\right)},

(15)

and the optimization objective is

\max_{\alpha_{s}}{\sum_{(h,q,t)\in\mathcal{G}}{\varphi(t\ |\ q,h)}},

(16)

where
\alpha_{s}
is to be learned.

Whereas the searching space of learnable parameters is exponentially large,

i
.
e
.
O(|\mathcal{P}|^{L})
, direct optimization of Eq. (
16
)
may fall in the dilemma of over-parameterization. Besides,
it is difficult to apply gradient-based optimization. This is because each variable
\alpha_{s}
is
bound with a specific rule pattern, and it is obviously a discrete work to enumerate rules.
To overcome these defects, the parameter of rule
\textbf{p}_{s}
can be reformulated by distributing
the confidence to its containing predicate at each hop, resulting in a differentiable score function:

\phi_{L}(t\ |\ q,h)=\left(\mathrm{v}^{T}_{h}\prod_{l=1}^{L}{\sum_{k=0}^{|\mathcal{P}|}{a_{k}^{l}\mathrm{M}_{p_{k}}}}\right)\cdot\mathrm{v}_{t},

(17)

where
L
is a hyperparameter denoting the maximum length of patterns and
|\mathcal{P}|
is the
number of predicates in KG.
\mathrm{M}_{p_{0}}
is an identity matrix
I
that enables the model
to include all possible rule patterns of length
L
or smaller
[
38
]
.
The key difference of parameterization between Eq. (
15
)
and Eq. (
17
) is illustrated in Fig.
2
. Fig.
2a

shows that the rule
brotherOf

\wedge

fatherOf
gains more plausibility against

workIn

\wedge

hasStudent
facing the query
uncleOf
, so
\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{2}
.
In the latter, the score of
\texttt{brotherOf}\wedge\texttt{fatherOf}
is obtained by multiplying
the weight of
brotherOf
at first hop
a_{1}^{1}
and
fatherOf
at second hop
a_{2}^{2}
.

To perform training and prediction over the Neural LP framework, we should first construct a KG from
a large subset of all triplets. Then we remove the edge
(h,t)
from the graph
when facing the query
(h,q,t)
, so that the score of
t
can get rid of the influence imposed
by passing from head entity
h
directly through edge
(h,t)
for the correctness of reasoning.

Our
MPLR model

In this section, we propose our MPLR model as an improvement to Neural LP

As aforementioned in Section
3.1
, all edges starting from the head entity
h

to
t_{i}
should be removed from the graph in a multi-target query, thus more edges in a batch of
queries will be removed, which would break the graph structure to a considerable extent.
For example, in Fig.

, suppose the query is
(z_{1},\texttt{sisterOf},\{z_{2},z_{4}\})
,
so that two edges
(z_{1},z_{2})
and
(z_{1},z_{4})

sisterOf
(

,
z_{2}
)
\wedge

sisterOf
(
z_{2}
,
z_{4}
)
\Rightarrow

daughterOf
(
z_{1}
,
z_{4}

Therefore, we update Eq. (
17
) to address the limitation of Neural LP in multi-target scenario,
where the bonus on the score of
t_{i}
from edge
(h,t_{i})
is avoided without removing the edge.
For each sub-query

(h,q,t),\forall t\in T
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{u}_{0}=\mathrm{v}_{h},\ \mathrm{\varepsilon}_{1}=a_{q}^{1}\cdot\mathrm{v}_{t}

(18)

\displaystyle\mathrm{u}_{l}^{\top}=\mathrm{u}_{l-1}^{\top}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{|\mathcal{P}|}{a_{k}^{l}\mathrm{M}_{p_{k}}},\ l=1,2,\ldots,L

(19)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\varepsilon}_{l}^{\top}=\mathrm{\varepsilon}_{l-1}^{\top}(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{|\mathcal{P}|}{a_{k}^{l}\mathrm{M}_{p_{k}}})+a_{q}^{l}\mathrm{u}_{l-1}^{\top}\mathrm{M}_{t},\ l=2,3,\ldots,L

(20)

\displaystyle\varPhi_{L}(t\ |\ q,h)=(\mathrm{u}_{L}^{\top}-\mathrm{\varepsilon}_{L}^{\top}\mathrm{M}_{t})\cdot\mathrm{v}_{t},

(21)

where
a_{q}^{l}
is the attention score of predicate
q
at
l
-th hop,
and
\mathrm{M}_{t}\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{E}|}
is the matrix
with only its
(t,t)
-th element being 1, otherwise 0.

\mathrm{\varepsilon}_{1}^{\top}
is actually vector
\mathrm{u}_{1}^{\top}

with all elements reduced to 0 except its
t
-th value.
Also, vector
\mathrm{u}^{\top}_{l-1}\mathrm{M}_{t}
keeps only the
t
-th value of
\mathrm{u}^{\top}_{l-1}
.

Eq. (
21
) eliminates redundant gain (
i
.
e
.
\mathrm{\varepsilon}_{L}^{\top}\mathrm{M}_{t}
)
of
t
-th value in vector
\mathrm{u}_{L}^{\top}

passed from
h
to
t
directly through edge
(h,t)
, but retains the approach to affect other
nodes except

through this edge. That is to say, in query (
z_{1}
,
sisterOf
,
\{z_{2},z_{4}\}
)
the score of entity
z_{2}
should not involve that from edge
(z_{1},z_{2})
, but the score of
z_{4}

can be increased by path
sisterOf
(
z_{1}
,
z_{2}
)
\wedge

sisterOf
(
z_{2}
,
z_{4}
).

In addition, considering there might be multiple tail entities in relation to the given head entity in a query,
and as shown in Eq. (
6
), only one score should be allocated to a set of rule paths.
We modify the representation of the
tail
vector to a multi-hot one.
Given a query
q
, a head entity
e_{i}
and a set of tails

the target vector
\mathrm{v}_{T}\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{E}|}
is also a

For example, in the KG displayed in Fig.

Finally, the confidence scores are learned over the bidirectional LSTM
[

]

followed by the attention using Eqs (

) and (

) for the temporal dependency
among several consecutive steps. The

in Eq. (

\displaystyle\textbf{h}_{i},\textbf{h}^{\prime}_{L-i+1}=\text{BiLSTM}(\textbf{h}_{i-1},\textbf{h}^{\prime}_{L-i},\text{input})

(22)

\displaystyle[a_{i,1},\ldots,a_{i,|\mathcal{P}|}]=f_{\theta}\left([\textbf{h}_{i}\ ||\ \textbf{h}^{\prime}_{L-i}]\right),

(23)

where

and the subscripts denote their time step.

3.3
Optimization of the model

As our work extends the fully differentiable framework, Neural LP
[
52
]
,
a gradient-based algorithm ADAM
[
20
]
is applied to optimize our model, which is
widely used in a large number of deep learning models. In this section, loss function and an optimization
method of tensor called lower-rank approximation are introduced respectively.

Loss construction.
In general, we treat this task as a multi-label classification
to handle multiple outcomes.
For each query
q(h,T)
in KG, we first split the objective function Eq. (
21
) into
two parts: target vector
\mathrm{v}_{T}
and prediction vector

\mathrm{s}^{\top}=\mathrm{u}_{L}^{\top}-\mathrm{\varepsilon}_{L}^{\top}\mathrm{M}_{t},

(24)

and then we construct the loss function for
\mathrm{v}_{T}
and
\mathrm{s}^{\top}

using the Bernoulli negative log-likelihood with logits:

\ell_{q}(h,T)=-\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{E}|}\left\{\mathrm{v}_{T}[i]\cdot\log{\left(\sigma(\mathrm{s}[i])\right)}+(1-\mathrm{v}_{T}[i])\cdot\log{\left(\sigma(1-\mathrm{s}[i])\right)}\right\},

where
i
indexes elements in vector
\mathrm{v}_{T}
and
\mathrm{s}
, and
\sigma(\cdot)
is the
sigmoid function
\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{(-z)}}
. To ensure numerical stability,
the above equation can be reformulated into the equivalent Eq. (
25
) through log-sum-exp method.

\ell_{q}(h,T)=\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{E}|}\left\{\max(\mathrm{s}[i],0)-\mathrm{v}_{T}[i]\cdot\mathrm{s}[i]+\log{(1+e^{-|\mathrm{s}[i]|})}\right\}

(25)

Low-rank approximation.
It can be shown that the final confidences obtained by expanding

\varPhi_{L}
are a rank one estimation of the
confidence value tensor

[
38
]
,
and a low-rank approximation is a popular method for tensor approximation. Hence we follow the work
of
[
38
]
and rewrite Eq. (
21
) using rank
R
approximation,
as shown in Eq. (
26
).

\varPhi_{L}(t\ |\ q,h)=\sum_{r=1}^{R}{\left(\mathrm{u}_{L}^{\top}-\mathrm{\varepsilon}_{L}^{\top}\mathrm{M}_{t}\right)}\cdot\mathrm{v}_{t}

(26)

More concretely, we update Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
),
as is shown in Eqs. (
27
) and (
28
),
by deploying number of
R
BiLSTMs of the same network structure,
each of which can extract features from various dimensions.

\displaystyle\textbf{h}_{i}^{(r)},\textbf{h}_{L-i+1}^{{}^{\prime}(r)}=\text{BiLSTM}_{r}(\textbf{h}_{i-1}^{(r)},\textbf{h}_{L-i}^{{}^{\prime}(r)},\text{input})

(27)

\displaystyle[a_{i,1}^{(r)},\ldots,a_{i,|\mathcal{P}|}^{(r)}]=f_{\theta}\left([\textbf{h}_{i}{{}^{\prime}(r)}\ ||\ \textbf{h}_{L-i}^{{}^{\prime}(r)}]\right),

(28)

where the superscripts of the hidden states identify their bidirectional LSTM.

4.4
Discussion on Neural LP framework

Despite the fact that Neural LP is an end-to-end gradient-based KG reasoning framework and
fills in the gap between traditional KG reasoning models (
e
.
g
. embedding methods)
and interpretability, through close observation on datasets and analysis on formulas,
we discover that there also exist some restrictions for Neural LP-based algorithms:

1.

As proved in
[
38
]
, current framework inevitably mines incorrect rules
with high confidences,
i.e.
, if there are several rules sharing one or more predicates,
confidences of rules would be coupled mutually. Intuitively, this is because Eq. (
17
)
distributes the score of a rule to the predicates that constitute the rule at different hops.
For instance,
brotherOf

\wedge

sonOf
and
brotherOf

\wedge

sisterOf
share
brotherOf
at first hop.
However, in case our query is
sonOf
, if
brotherOf
wins high confidence at first hop,
the score of the second rule may not be too low, which is absolutely an incorrect result.
This reduces the interpretability of the output rules.

2.

Present models are faced with the dilemma where there would be invertible relation
pairs and rules of varied lengths mixing up.
(i)
A relation pair
(R_{1},R_{2})

is invertible if there simultaneously exist two triplets
(h,R_{1},t)
and
(t,R_{2},h)

in a KG.
(ii)
The KG example shown in Fig.
4
consists of
candidate rules of length 2, 3 and 4 for query
brotherOf
(
x
,
y
).
These two factors jointly may cause invalid induction results, under the condition that
we choose an improper hyperparameter
L
as the maximum length of rules, e.g., if we set
L=4
,
the rule path
sonOf
(
x
,
v
)
\wedge

motherOf
(
v
,
x
)
\wedge

sonOf
(
x
,
v
)
\wedge

motherOf
(
v
,
y
)
\Rightarrow

brotherOf
(
x
,
y
) is possible but meaningless. This is also
essentially due to the distribution of confidences brought by Eq. (
17
),
and thus impedes the way for multi-hop reasoning over long rules.

3.

A high ranking of an entity results not solely from a top-scored rule,
but also a number of relatively low-scored rules. As formulated in Section
3.1
,
the product of vector-matrix multiplication is a scalar representing the number of unique paths,
and the final score of the entity is computed by summing up the confidences of all paths.
Again, metrics like MRR and Hit@
k
only assess models in terms of the ranking of the desired entity,
rather than the quality of mined reasoning rules.
Thus, models following the Neural LP framework with high MRR and Hit@
k
may be better suited to
tasks like question answering or relation completion
[
18
]
,
but this may not be applied to rule mining.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we firstly propose novel indicators that help to understand knowledge graph
reasoning tasks and to serve as a supplement to the existing metrics (
e
.
g
. Hit@
k
) for evaluating models.
The
saturation
measures the possibility of a rule being a plausible inference for a relation,
which fills in the blanks of judging the interpretability of mined rules.
While the
bifurcation
, computing the proportion of instances with multiple reasoning destinations,
is useful for enhancing the power of MMR and Hit@
k
. Then we address the problem of learning rules
from knowledge graphs in multi-target cases where a model called MPLR is proposed.
MPLR improves the Neural LP framework in order to allow more queries fed in one batch,
thus fits in multi-target scenarios. Experiment results have shown that our proposed method
improves performance on several knowledge graph reasoning datasets and owns strong interpretability,
under the evaluation of traditional metrics and our newly suggested ones. In the future,
we would like to break the limitation of our current model for multi-hop reasoning where the rules
are much longer.

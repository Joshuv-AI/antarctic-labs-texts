On Free products of graphs
Max Carter, Stephan Tornier, George Willis

Abstract
. We define a free product of connected simple graphs that is equivalent to several existing definitions when the graphs are vertex-transitive but differs otherwise. The new definition is designed for the automorphism group of the free product to be as large as possible, and we give sufficient criteria for it to be non-discrete. Finally, we transfer Tits’ classification of automorphisms of trees and simplicity criterion to free products of graphs.

Every t.d.l.c. group can be viewed as a directed union of compactly generated (c.g.) open subgroups. Among c.g.t.d.l.c. groups, those acting on (regular) graphs stand out due to the Cayley-Abels graph construction

Groups acting on graphs are studied extensively

]

[

In this article, we define a free product
\Gamma=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
of connected simple graphs
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}

(n\in\bbN)
, which may be (locally) infinite. This product is associative and commutative with unit a single vertex. It realises the Cayley graph of a free product of finitely generated groups as the free product of the Cayley graphs of the factors. We compare our definition with several existing ones and see that all are equivalent when
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
are vertex-transitive. The vertices and edges of
\Gamma
are defined directly, rather than by gluing copies of
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
as done in the other definitions, which facilitates computation in the graphs and also, when the graphs are not vertex-transitive, yields a free product with a larger automorphism group. We give sufficient criteria for

to be non-discrete: Corollary

states that
\Aut(\Gamma)
is non-discrete as soon as one of
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}

(n\geq 2)
has some non-trivial vertex-stabiliser, and Proposition
6.8
states that
\Aut(\Gamma)
is non-discrete whenever two of the factors
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}

(n\geq 3)
are isomorphic.

Finally, we consider the subgroup
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
of

which preserves the sheet structure of

Definition

In Sections

3.1.
A new definition

Let
\Gamma_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1}),\ldots,\Gamma_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n})
be connected graphs. The
free product
of
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
, denoted
\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
, is the following graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
. Its vertex set consists of
admissible
words in the alphabet
V_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_{n}
and its edge set is defined in terms of the last vertices in such admissible words.

Vertices

The set of admissible words of length
l
is denoted by
V^{(l)}
and defined by induction on
l
. Its definition involves an
update function

\mathbf{u}:V\to V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{n}
defined inductively alongside
V^{(l)}
. Choose
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{n}
and set

(1)

V^{(0)}:=\{\emptyset\}\mbox{ and }\mathbf{u}(\emptyset):=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{n}.

Assume that
V^{(k)}
and
\mathbf{u}|_{V^{(k)}}
are defined for
k\in\{0,\dots,l\}
. For
\tilde{v}=v_{1}\ldots v_{k}\in V^{(k)}
, let
\mathbf{u}(\tilde{v})=\left(u_{1}(\tilde{v}),\dots,u_{n}(\tilde{v})\right)
. Then
V^{(l+1)}
consists of all words of the form
\tilde{v}v_{l+1}
where
\tilde{v}=v_{1}\ldots v_{l}\in V^{(l)}
and, supposing that
v_{l}\in V_{i}
and

, we have

(2)

j\neq i\mbox{ and }v_{l+1}\neq u_{j}(\tilde{v}).

The update function is defined on

by

(3)

u_{h}(\tilde{v}v_{l+1}):=\begin{cases}u_{h}(\tilde{v})&\mbox{ if }h\neq j\\
v_{l+1}&\mbox{ if }h=j\end{cases}.

Edges

The edge set
E
consists of pairs of admissible words
\{\tilde{v},\tilde{v}v_{l+1}\}
as follows. The edges incident on the empty word are

(4)

\{\emptyset,v_{1}\}\mbox{ where }\{u_{i}(\emptyset),v_{1}\}\in E_{i}\mbox{ for some }i\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

The edges incident on the non-empty word

(5)

\displaystyle\{v_{1}\dots v_{l-1}v_{l},v_{1}\dots v_{l-1}v^{\prime}_{l}\}\mbox{ for all }\{v_{l},v_{l}^{\prime}\}\in E_{i}

(6)

and

\displaystyle\{\tilde{v},\tilde{v}v_{l+1}\}\mbox{ for all }\{u_{j}(\tilde{v}),v_{l+1}\}\in E_{j}\mbox{ and }j\neq i.

]
, Pisanski-Tucker define a free product of rooted graphs. Let
\Gamma_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1},v_{1})
and
\Gamma_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2},v_{2})
be rooted graphs. The free product
\Gamma_{1}\ast\Gamma_{2}
is the graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
with vertex set all finite words in the alphabet
V_{1}\backslash\{v_{1}\}\cup V_{2}\backslash\{v_{2}\}
whose letters alternate between
V_{1}\backslash\{v_{1}\}
and
V_{2}\backslash\{v_{2}\}
. Two vertices
w
and
w^{\prime}
are adjacent if either
w^{\prime}=wx
for a neighbour
x
of the respective root, or
w=ux
and
w^{\prime}=uy
for adjacent
x,y
in
\Gamma_{1}
or
\Gamma_{2}
.

Thus multiple copies of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are connected by identifying each vertex in a copy of
\Gamma_{1}
with the root in a copy of
\Gamma_{2}
, and each vertex in a copy of
\Gamma_{2}
with the root in a copy of
\Gamma_{1}
.

3.4.
Quenell

In
[
23
]
, Quenell defines a free product of rooted graphs. Let
\Gamma_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1},v_{1}),\ldots,\Gamma_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n},v_{n})
be rooted graphs. Recursively define graphs
B_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
by setting

B_{i}=\underset{v\in V_{i}\backslash\{v_{i}\}}{\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\#$}}}}(\Gamma_{i},v)\#(B_{2},v_{2})\#\cdots\#\widehat{(B_{i},v_{i})}\#\cdots\#(B_{n},v_{n}),

where
\widehat{(B_{i},v_{i})}
means that
(B_{i},v_{i})
is omitted from the connected sum. Finally, set
\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}:=(B_{1},v_{1})\#\cdots\#(B_{n},v_{n})
.

When
n=2
, the graph
B_{1}
in the Quenell construction corresponds to those words in the Pisanski-Tucker construction that are empty or begin with a letter in
V_{1}\backslash\{v_{1}\}
, and
B_{2}
corresponds to those words that are empty or begin with a letter in
V_{2}\backslash\{v_{2}\}
. The Quenell construction connects
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
at their respective vertices corresponding to the empty word.

4.
Uniqueness and Quotients

Based on Remark
3.1
we define the following class of quotients of the free product
\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
. It includes the free product itself as well as the cartesian product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
, …,
\Gamma_{n}
be graphs. A
mashup
of
\Gamma_{1}
, …,
\Gamma_{n}
is a connected graph
M\!=\!(V,E)
with embeddings
\varphi_{(x,i)}:\Gamma_{i}\to M
(
x\!\in\!V,\ i\!\in\!\{1,\ldots,n\}
) such that

(1)

x\in\varphi_{(x,i)}(\Gamma_{i})
, the
\Gamma_{i}
-sheet at
x
,

(2)

E=\bigcup\left\{\varphi_{(x,i)}(E(\Gamma_{i}))\mid i\in\{1,\dots,n\},\ x\in V\right\}
,

(3)

for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
x,y\in V
the subgraphs
\varphi_{(x,i)}(\Gamma_{i})
and
\varphi_{(y,j)}(\Gamma_{j})
are either identical and
i=j
, have empty intersection, or intersect at a single vertex and
i\neq j
; and

(4)

for all
i,\,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
with
i\neq j
,
x\in V
and
y\in V(\varphi_{(x,i)}(\Gamma_{i}))
the maps

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}\to M\to\Gamma_{i}:

\displaystyle\ v\mapsto\varphi_{(x,i)}(v)\mapsto\varphi^{-1}_{(y,i)}(\varphi_{(x,i)}(v))

\displaystyle\hbox to0.0pt{\hfil}\Gamma_{i}\to M\to\Gamma_{j}:

\displaystyle\ v\mapsto\varphi_{(x,i)}(v)\mapsto\varphi^{-1}_{(\varphi_{(x,i)}(v),j)}(\varphi_{(x,i)}(v))

are constant.

Remark 4.2
.

Retain the notation of Definition
4.1
.

(1)

Conditions
1
and
4
imply that every vertex of
M
belongs to a unique
\Gamma_{i}
-sheet for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(2)

Conditions
2
and
3
imply that every edge of
M
belongs to a unique
\Gamma_{i}
-sheet for a unique
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(3)

Conditions
1
and
4
provide a map
\mathbf{u}:V\to V(\Gamma_{1})\times\dots\times V(\Gamma_{n})
defined by
\smash{\mathbf{u}(x)=(\varphi^{-1}_{(x,1)}(x),\dots,\varphi^{-1}_{(x,n)}(x))}
such that all coordinates
u_{j}
of
\mathbf{u}
except the
i
-th one are constant on
\varphi_{(x,i)}(\Gamma_{i})
.

(4)

By conditions
2
,
3
and
4
, every
x\in V
has
|N_{\Gamma_{i}}(u_{i}(x))|
neighbours in its
\Gamma_{i}
-sheet. The valency of
x
in
M
is therefore given as in Remark
3.1
(
2
).

(5)

Conditions
1
and
4
imply
3
.

Example 4.3
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be connected graphs. Examples of mashups are:

(1)

The free product
\Gamma:=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
. For
x\in V(\Gamma)
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
define the map
\varphi_{(x,i)}:\Gamma_{i}\to\Gamma
using the sheet structure of
\Gamma
given in Remark
3.1
.

(2)

The cartesian product
\Gamma:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
. Recall that
V(\Gamma)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}V(\Gamma_{i})
. For
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in V(\Gamma)
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, define the map
\varphi_{(x,i)}:\Gamma_{i}\to\Gamma
by
\varphi_{(x,i)}(y):=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},y,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n})\in V(\Gamma)
for every

. In particular,
\mathbf{u}=\id
in this case.

(3)

Parts
1
and
2
can be combined via the following construction which resembles that of right-angled Artin groups, see
e.g.

[
6
]
. Let
B
be a non-empty, finite graph with vertex set
V(B)=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and let
\Gamma_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})
be a connected graph for every
i\in V(B)
. We outline the construction of a mash-up
M_{B}=(V,E)
of the
\Gamma_{i}

(i\in V(B))
such that
M_{B}=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i\in V(B)}\Gamma_{i}
when
B
has no edges, and
M_{B}=\prod_{i\in V(B)}\Gamma_{i}
when
B
is complete. The vertex set of
M_{B}
consists of certain admissible words in the alphabet
V_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_{n}
, forming a strict subset of those defined in Section
3.1
.

Vertices
. The set of admissible words of length
l
is denoted by
V^{(l)}
and defined by induction on
l
. It involves an update function
\mathbf{u}\!:V\!\to V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{n}
defined inductively alongside
V^{(l)}
. Choose
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{n}
. Set

\hbox to0.0pt{\hfil}V^{(0)}:=\{\emptyset\}\text{ and }\mathbf{u}(\emptyset):=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{n}.

Now, assume that
V^{(k)}
and
\mathbf{u}|_{V^{(k)}}
are defined for

. Given a vertex
\tilde{v}=v_{1}\ldots v_{k}\in V^{(k)}
, let
\mathbf{u}(\tilde{v})=(u_{1}(\tilde{v}),\ldots,u_{n}(\tilde{v}))
. The set
V^{(l+1)}
consists of all words of the form
\tilde{v}v_{l+1}
where
\tilde{v}=v_{1}\ldots v_{l}\in V^{(l)}
and, supposing that
v_{l}\in V_{i}
and
v_{l+1}\in V_{j}
we have

\hbox to0.0pt{\hfil}j\neq i,\ v_{l+1}\neq u_{j}(\tilde{v})\text{ and }j>i\text{ whenever }\{i,j\}\in E(B).

The update function is defined on
V^{(l+1)}
by

\hbox to0.0pt{\hfil}u_{h}(\tilde{v}v_{l+1}):=\begin{cases}u_{h}(\tilde{v})&\text{if }h\neq j\\
v_{l+1}&\text{if }h=j\end{cases}.

Sheets
. To define the edges of
M_{B}
and show that it is a mash-up of the
\Gamma_{i}

(i\in V(B))
, we define its sheets: For
\tilde{v}=v_{1}\ldots v_{l}\in V
with
v_{l}\in V_{i}
and

, the
\Gamma_{j}
-sheet at
\tilde{v}
has the following vertex set
S\subseteq V
, which is naturally turned into a copy of
\Gamma_{j}
.

(a)

If
j=i
, let
S:=\{v_{1}\dots v_{l-1}\}\cup\left\{v_{1}\dots v_{l-1}v^{\prime}_{l}\mid v^{\prime}_{l}\in V_{j}\setminus\{u_{j}(v_{1}\dots v_{l-1})\}\right\}
.

(b)

If
j>i
and
\{i,j\}\in E(B)
, or
\{i,j\}\not\in E(B)
, set

\hbox to0.0pt{\hfil}S:=\{\tilde{v}\}\cup\{\tilde{v}v_{l+1}\mid v_{l+1}\in V_{j}\backslash\{u_{j}(\tilde{v})\}\}.

(c)

If
j<i
and
\{i,j\}\in E(B)
, define
\iota(v_{m})\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

(m\in\{1,\ldots,l\})
as the unique index such that
v_{m}\in\Gamma_{\iota(v_{m})}
. Now, let
k\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
be minimal such that
\iota(v_{k})\geq j
and
\{\iota(v_{r}),j\}\in E(B)
for all

. If
\iota(v_{k})=j
, then
k\neq l
and we set

\hbox to0.0pt{\hfil}S:=\{\tilde{v}\}\cup\{v_{1}\ldots v_{k-1}v^{\prime}v_{k+1}\ldots v_{l}\mid v^{\prime}\in V_{j}\backslash\{u_{j}(\tilde{v})=v_{k}\}\}.

Otherwise, if
\iota(v_{k})>j
, then
k\leq l
and we set

\hbox to0.0pt{\hfil}S:=\{\tilde{v}\}\cup\{v_{1}\ldots v_{k-1}v^{\prime}v_{k}\ldots v_{l}\mid v^{\prime}\in V_{j}\backslash\{u_{j}(\tilde{v})\}\}.

In each of the above cases, the set
S
is in natural bijection with
V_{j}
. The edges of the
\Gamma_{j}
-sheet at
\tilde{v}
are then defined using
E_{j}
via said bijection.

For example, one can see that the Cayley graph of the right-angled Artin group
A:=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\mid\{[s_{i},s_{j}]\mid\{i,j\}\in E(B)\}\}
defined via the base graph

is isomorphic to
M_{B}
where
\Gamma_{i}=T_{2}
for all

.

Now, let
M
be a mashup of
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
and let
\gamma=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})
be a path in
M
. By Remark
4.2
(
2
), there is a unique index
c(e_{m})\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
for every edge
e_{m}
in
\gamma
such that
e_{m}
belongs to a
\Gamma_{c(e_{m})}
-sheet of
M
. Write
e_{m}=\{x_{m-1},x_{m}\}\in E(\Gamma_{c(e_{m})})
. We say that
\gamma

transitions from
\Gamma_{i}
to
\Gamma_{j}
at
m
if
c(e_{m})=i
,
c(e_{m+1})=j
and
i\neq j
. Then
x_{m}
is a
transition point in
M
and
\smash{\varphi_{(x_{m},i)}^{-1}(x_{m})}
is a
transition point in
\Gamma_{i}
.

In the case
\smash{M=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}}
, there is a path from the empty word
\emptyset
to
\tilde{v}=v_{1}\dots v_{l}
with transitition points
v_{k}
(
k\in\{1,\dots,l-1\}
). If
v_{k}\in V(\Gamma_{i_{k}})
, then the path goes from
v_{1}\dots v_{k-1}
to
v_{1}\dots v_{k}
in a
\Gamma_{i_{k}}
-sheet and transitions into a
\Gamma_{i_{k+1}}
-sheet at
v_{1}\ldots v_{k}
. Denote
v_{1}\dots v_{k}
by
x_{k}
. The length of the shortest path from
\emptyset
to
\tilde{v}
now is

d(\emptyset,\tilde{v})=\sum_{k=1}^{l}d_{\Gamma_{i_{k}}}(\varphi^{-1}_{(x_{k},i_{k})}(x_{k-1}),\varphi^{-1}_{(x_{k},i_{k})}(x_{k}))=\sum_{k=1}^{l}d_{\Gamma_{i_{k}}}(u_{i_{k}}(x_{k-1}),u_{i_{k}}(x_{k})),

where, it may be recalled,
u_{i_{k}}(x_{k})=v_{k}
. Moreover, we have the following.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be connected graphs,
\Gamma:=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
, and
\tilde{v},\tilde{w}\in V(\Gamma)
. Then all paths of minimal length from
\tilde{v}
to
\tilde{w}
have the same transition points.

Proof.

If
\gamma
transitions at
m
then
\tilde{x}_{m}
is a cut vertex and deleting
\tilde{x}_{m}
disconnects
\Gamma
so that
\tilde{v}
lies in one component and
\tilde{w}
in the other. Indeed, if
\tilde{w}
lay in the same component as
\tilde{v}
, then
\gamma
would return to that component through
\tilde{x}_{m}
and could be shortened. Every path from
\tilde{v}
to
\tilde{w}
must therefore pass through
\tilde{x}_{m}
.
∎

The following proposition states in particular that every mashup is a quotient of its associated free product.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
M
be a mashup of
\Gamma_{1}
, …,
\Gamma_{n}
. Further, let
x_{0}\in V(M)
and
\smash{u_{i}:=\varphi^{-1}_{(x_{0},i)}(x_{0})}
(
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
). Initialise
\Gamma:=\smash{\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}}
by
\mathbf{u}(\emptyset)=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
. Then there is a surjective graph homomorphism
\smash{\psi:\Gamma\to M}
with
\psi(\emptyset)=x_{0}
and
\mathbf{u}(\psi(\tilde{v}))=\mathbf{u}(\tilde{v})
for all
\tilde{v}\in V(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on the natural number
l
in
V(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{l\in\bbN_{0}}V^{(l)}
. Define
\psi(\emptyset)=x_{0}
and extend
\psi
to
V^{(1)}
by setting

(8)

\psi(v_{1}):=\varphi_{(x_{0},i)}(v_{1})\mbox{ if }v_{1}\in\Gamma_{i}\setminus\{u_{i}=\varphi^{-1}_{(x_{0},i)}(x_{0})\}.

Then
\psi
is a morphism from
\mathrm{span}_{\Gamma}(V^{(0)}\cup V^{(1)})
to
M
since
\psi(\emptyset)=x_{0}=\varphi_{(x_{0},i)}(u_{i})
, by Definition
3.1
(
4
) of the neighbours of
\emptyset\in V(\Gamma)
, and since
\varphi_{(x_{0},i)}
is a morphism.

By Definition
4.1
4
, the map
\smash{y\mapsto\varphi^{-1}_{(y,j)}(y)}
is constant on
\psi(S_{(\emptyset,i)})=\varphi_{(x_{0},i)}(\Gamma_{i})
for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\backslash\{i\}
. The definition of the initial value of
\mathbf{u}
implies that this constant is equal to
\smash{\varphi^{-1}_{(x_{0},j)}(x_{0})=u_{j}=u_{j}(\emptyset)}
.

Now, suppose
\psi
is defined on
\mathrm{span}_{\Gamma}(\bigcup_{k=0}^{l}V^{(k)})
and for
y=\psi(v_{1}\dots v_{k})
satisfies

(9)

\varphi^{-1}_{(y,i)}(y)=\begin{cases}v_{k}&\mbox{ if }v_{k}\in V(\Gamma_{i})\\
u_{i}(v_{1}\dots v_{k})&\mbox{ if }v_{k}\in V(\Gamma_{j}),\ j\neq i\end{cases},\qquad(k\in\{1,\dots,l\}).

This holds for
l=1
by the above. Now, consider
\tilde{v}v_{l+1}=v_{1}\dots v_{l}v_{l+1}\in V^{(l+1)}
, where
v_{l}\in V_{i}
,
v_{l+1}\in V_{j}
and
i\neq j
. In particular,
v_{l+1}\neq u_{j}(\tilde{v})
. Set
y:=\psi(\tilde{v})
and

\psi(\tilde{v}v_{l+1}):=\varphi_{(y,j)}(v_{l+1}).

Since (
9
) implies that
\varphi_{(y,j)}(u_{j}(\tilde{v}))=y
, it follows that
\psi
is a morphism from
S_{(\tilde{v},j)}
to the
\Gamma_{j}
-sheet of
M
containing
y
. By (
6
) of
E(\Gamma)
, this extension of
\psi
is a morphism
\mathrm{span}_{\Gamma}(\smash{\bigcup_{k=0}^{l+1}V^{(k)})\to M}
. To see that (
9
) extends to the case
k=l+1
, observe that
\smash{\varphi^{-1}_{(z,j)}(z)=v_{l+1}}
for
z=\psi(\tilde{v}v_{l+1})
by definition and, for
h\neq j
,

\varphi^{-1}_{(z,h)}(z)=\varphi^{-1}_{(y,h)}(y)=\begin{cases}v_{l}=u_{i}(\tilde{v})&\mbox{ if }h=i\\
u_{h}(\tilde{v})&\mbox{ if }h\neq i\end{cases},

where the first equality holds by Definition
4.1
4
and the second row of the second equality holds by (
9
) with
k=l
. Since
u_{h}(\tilde{v}v_{l+1})=u_{h}(\tilde{v})
for
h\neq j
, (
9
) extends.

Continuing recursively produces the claimed morphism
\psi
, which is surjective by Lemma
2.1
, the connectedness of
M
, and conditions
1
and
2
in Definition
4.1
.
∎

Using Example

, the following is a consequence of Proposition

.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be connected graphs. Then
\mathbf{u}:\!\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}\!\to\!\Gamma_{1}\times\dots\times\Gamma_{n}
is a graph homomorphism. ∎

We now show that the isomorphism class of a free product is independent of the initialisation of its update function.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be connected graphs and let
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
be the free products of
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
with respect to
(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
and
(u_{1}^{\prime},\dots,u_{n}^{\prime})
in
\prod_{i=1}^{n}V(\Gamma_{i})
. Then
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}
denote the update functions on
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
respectively. Choose
\tilde{v}_{0}\in V(\Gamma^{\prime})
such that
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{0})=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
. Since
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a mashup of
\Gamma_{1}
, …,
\Gamma_{n}
, there is, by Proposition
4.5
, a surjective homomorphism
\psi:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
with
\psi(\emptyset)=\tilde{v}_{0}
.

To see that
\psi
is injective, we construct a left inverse: Interchanging the roles of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
, Proposition
4.5
also yields a surjective homomorphism
\psi^{\prime}:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Gamma
. Since
\psi^{\prime}
is surjective, there is
\tilde{v}^{\prime}_{0}\in V(\Gamma^{\prime})
such that
\psi^{\prime}(\tilde{v}^{\prime}_{0})=\emptyset\in V(\Gamma)
. Proposition
4.5
ensures that
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(\tilde{v}^{\prime}_{0})=\mathbf{u}(\psi^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{0}^{\prime}))=\mathbf{u}(\emptyset)=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
. Hence
\tilde{v}_{0}
from the previous paragraph may be chosen to be
\tilde{v}^{\prime}_{0}
. Then the composition
\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi:\Gamma\to\Gamma
is surjective and satisfies
\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi(\emptyset)=\emptyset
as well as
\mathbf{u}(\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi(\tilde{v}))=\mathbf{u}(\tilde{v})
for every
\tilde{v}\in V(\Gamma)
.

It remains to show that
\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi
is the identity homomorphism. We argue by induction on the distance of a vertex from the empty word, given that
\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi(\emptyset)=\emptyset
. Suppose
\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi(\tilde{v})=\tilde{v}
for every
\tilde{v}
with
d(\emptyset,\tilde{v})\leq d
. Consider
\tilde{w}
with
d(\emptyset,\tilde{w})=d+1
. Then there is a neighbour,
\tilde{v}
of
\tilde{w}
with
d(\emptyset,\tilde{v})=d
and a unique
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\{\tilde{v},\tilde{w}\}
belongs to
S_{(\tilde{v},i)}
. Since
\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi(\tilde{v})=\tilde{v}
and
u_{i}(\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi(\tilde{w}))=u_{i}(\tilde{w})
, it follows that
\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi(\tilde{w})
equals
\tilde{w}
.
∎

The proof of Corollary
4.7
entails that if
\tilde{v}\in V(\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i})
satisfies
\mathbf{u}(\tilde{v})=\mathbf{u}(\emptyset)
, then there is an automorphism
\alpha\in\Aut(\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i})
such that
\alpha(\emptyset)=\tilde{v}
and

(10)

\mathbf{u}(\alpha(\tilde{w}))=\mathbf{u}(\tilde{w})\mbox{ for every }\tilde{w}\in V(\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}).

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\Gamma_{3}
be connected graphs and
\Gamma:=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{3}\Gamma_{i}
. Then

(\Gamma_{1}\ast\Gamma_{2})\ast\Gamma_{3}\cong\Gamma\cong\Gamma_{1}\ast(\Gamma_{2}\ast\Gamma_{3}).

Proof.

The graph
\Gamma^{\prime}:=(\Gamma_{1}\ast\Gamma_{2})\ast\Gamma_{3}
is naturally a mashup of the graphs
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\Gamma_{3}
, where the embeddings
\varphi_{(x,i)}:\Gamma_{i}\to\Gamma^{\prime}
(
x\in V(\Gamma^{\prime})
,
i\in\{1,2,3\}
) come from the structure of
\Gamma^{\prime}
as a free product of
\Gamma_{1}\ast\Gamma_{2}
and
\Gamma_{3}
, and the structure of
\Gamma_{1}\ast\Gamma_{2}
as a free product of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
. Hence, by Proposition
4.5
, there is a surjective graph homomorphism
\psi:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
intertwining the update functions. As a consequence,
\psi
is completely determined, and in particular injective, on balls of arbitrary radius around
\emptyset\in V(\Gamma)
by induction. Hence it is injective overall. Proceed analogously in the case of
\Gamma_{1}\ast(\Gamma_{2}\ast\Gamma_{3})

5.
Comparison of definitions of the free product

Here, we show that the definitions presented in Sections
3.1
to
3.4
, which we will denote, respectively, by
\ast_{1}
up to
\ast_{4}
in this section, are equivalent when the graphs are vertex-transitive. First, we highlight the obvious differences, namely:
\ast_{2}
is defined only for pairs of graphs that are vertex-transitive; and
\ast_{3}
and
\ast_{4}
are defined only for rooted graphs and produce rooted graphs. The equivalence of
\ast_{3}
and
\ast_{4}
with
\ast_{1}
arises because all choices of root vertex are equivalent when a graph is vertex-transitive.

We now turn to the various constructions. In all cases, vertex-transitivity of the factors is required to account for the fact that in the genesis of all graphs, new sheets are attached to the existing part at different vertices.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}

Proof.

Let

Recall that

6.
Automorphisms of Free Product Graphs

It was seen in Equation
10
that free product graphs admit many automorphisms which preserve the update function. Three additional types of automorphisms of free product graphs are described in this section.

The first proposition shows that further automorphisms arise from automorphisms of the free product’s factors.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be connected graphs and initialise
\Gamma:=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
with
\mathbf{u}(\emptyset)=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}V(\Gamma_{i})
. Given

and
\alpha\!\in\!\Aut(\Gamma_{j})
define

\tilde{\alpha}:\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}V(\Gamma_{i})\to\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}V(\Gamma_{i}),\ v\mapsto\begin{cases}v&\text{if }v\in V(\Gamma_{i}),\ i\neq j\\
\alpha(v)&\text{if }v\in V(\Gamma_{j})\end{cases}.

\tilde{v}\!=\!v_{1}\ldots v_{l}\in V(\Gamma)
. When
\alpha(u_{j})\!=\!u_{j}

\hat{\alpha}(\tilde{v})\!:=\!\tilde{\alpha}(v_{1})\dots\tilde{\alpha}(v_{l})
. Otherwise, set

\tilde{\alpha}(v_{2})\dots\tilde{\alpha}(v_{l})&\text{if }v_{1}\in\Gamma_{j}\mbox{ and }\alpha(v_{1})=u_{j}\\
\tilde{\alpha}(v_{1})\dots\tilde{\alpha}(v_{l})&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Then
\hat{\alpha}\!\in\!\Aut(\Gamma)
and the map
\Aut(\Gamma_{j})\!\to\!\Aut(\Gamma),\ \alpha\mapsto\hat{\alpha}
is a group monomorphism. Moreover,
\mathbf{u}\circ\hat{\alpha}=\overline{\alpha}\circ\mathbf{u}
, where
\overline{\alpha}=\id\times\dots\times\alpha\times\dots\times\id\in\Aut(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i})
.

Proof.

We first show that
\hat{\alpha}(\tilde{v})\!\in\!V(\Gamma)
. Since
\tilde{\alpha}
preserves
V(\Gamma_{i})
for all
i\!\in\!\{1,\ldots,n\}
, any two consecutive letters in the word
\hat{\alpha}(\tilde{v})
belong to distinct graphs. As to the other half of Equation (
2
), note that only the
j
-th component of
\mathbf{u}
is affected by
\hat{\alpha}
. Let
k_{1}<\cdots<k_{r}\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
be the indices for which

(
s\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
).

First, consider the case when
\alpha(u_{j})=u_{j}
. Since
\tilde{v}\in V(\Gamma)
, we have
v_{k_{1}}\neq u_{j}
and
v_{k_{s+1}}\neq v_{k_{s}}
for
s\geq 1
. Then
\alpha(v_{k_{1}})\neq u_{j}
and
\alpha(v_{k_{s+1}})\neq\alpha(v_{k_{s}})
for
s\geq 1
because
\alpha(u_{j})=u_{j}
and
\alpha
is a bijection. Hence
\hat{\alpha}(\tilde{v})\in V(\Gamma)
. The assertion
\mathbf{u}\circ\hat{\alpha}=\overline{\alpha}\circ\mathbf{u}
is immediate from the definition of
\hat{\alpha}
.

Now suppose
\alpha(u_{j})\neq u_{j}
. There are several cases to consider: If
l=0
or
v_{1}\not\in\Gamma_{j}
, then the first letter of
\hat{\alpha}(\tilde{v})
is
\alpha(u_{j})
, which belongs to
\Gamma_{j}
and is not equal to
u_{j}
. The remaining letters from
\Gamma_{j}
are
\alpha(v_{k_{s}})
(
s\geq 1
) and satisfy
\alpha(v_{k_{1}})\neq\alpha(u_{j})
and
\alpha(v_{k_{s+1}})\neq\alpha(v_{k_{s}})
(
s\geq 1
) because
\alpha
is a bijection. If
v_{1}\in\Gamma_{j}
and
\alpha(v_{1})=u_{j}
, then the letters from
\Gamma_{j}
are
\alpha(v_{k_{s}})
(

\alpha(v_{k_{2}})\neq u_{j}

(

and
\alpha

As a consequence of Corollary

\Gamma(\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}G_{i},\sqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i})\cong\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma(G_{i},S_{i})

The question of how

[

Question 6.9

Does every finitely generated group

, admit a finite symmetric generating set

In case of a positive answer to Question

7.
Sheet-preserving automorphisms

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be connected graphs and
\Gamma:=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
. We define two subgroups of
\Aut(\Gamma)
that preserve the set of sheets
\calS:=\{S_{(v,i)}\mid v\in V(\Gamma),\ i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\}
. Recall that the colour
c(S)\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
of a sheet
S\in\calS
is well-defined. We set

\displaystyle\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle:=\{\alpha\in\Aut(\Gamma)\mid\forall S\in\calS:\ \alpha(S)\in\calS\},

\displaystyle\Aut_{\calS,c}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle:=\{\alpha\in\Aut(\Gamma)\mid\forall S\in\calS:\ \alpha(S)\in\calS\text{ and }c(\alpha(S))=c(S)\}.

Note that
\Aut_{\calS,c}(\Gamma)\leq\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)\leq\Aut(\Gamma)
. If the factors of
\Gamma
are
2
-connected then the set
\calS
coincides with the maximal
2
-connected subgraphs of
\Gamma
, also known as “blocks”, or “lobes”, see e.g.
[
15
]
and
[
11
]
. In this case,
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)=\Aut(\Gamma)
. If, in addition, the factors of
\Gamma
are pairwise non-isomorphic we conclude that
\Aut_{\calS,c}(\Gamma)=\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)=\Aut(\Gamma)
.

Example 7.1
.

The following examples illustrate the non-
2
-connected case.

(1)

Let
\Gamma:=T_{1}\ast T_{1}
. Then
\Gamma\cong T_{2}
and
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)=\Aut(\Gamma)=D_{\infty}
. Furthermore,
[\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma):\Aut_{\calS,c}(\Gamma)]=[\Aut(\Gamma):\Aut_{\calS,c}(\Gamma)]=2
.

(2)

Let
\Gamma:=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{d}T_{1}\cong T_{d}
. Then
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)=\Aut(\Gamma)
. Following the notation of Burger–Mozes
[
3
, Section 3.2]
and Tornier
[
25
, Definition 3.1]
, we have
\smash{\Aut_{\calS,c}(\Gamma)=\mathrm{U}_{1}^{(c)}(\{\id\})}
, which is discrete. In particular, we obtain
[\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma):\Aut_{\calS,c}(\Gamma)]=[\Aut(\Gamma):\Aut_{\calS,c}(\Gamma)]=\infty
.

(3)

Let
\Gamma:=T_{1}\ast T_{2}\cong T_{3}
. In this case,
\smash{\Aut_{\calS,c}(\Gamma)=\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)=\mathrm{U}_{1}^{(l)}(\langle\tau\rangle)}
where
\tau:=(12)\in S_{3}
and

is the legal labelling of

arising from the labellings
-0.1,-0.075)(1.1,0)
and

. We deduce

.

Example 7.2
.

In
[

, Example 3.7]
, Caprace and De Medts consider the free product
\Gamma:=T_{2}\ast T_{2}
of two lines and the group

.

Every graph that is not

-connected has a cut vertex. Therefore, the following proposition contrasts the case where all factors of

are

-connected.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be connected graphs and
\Gamma\!:=\!\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
. If
\Gamma
has two isomorphic factors which contain a cut vertex then
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)\lneq\Aut(\Gamma)
. Conversely, if
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)\!\lneq\!\Aut(\Gamma)
then some factor of
\Gamma
contains a cut vertex.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:\Gamma_{i}\to\Gamma_{j}
be an isomorphism for some distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Pick a cut vertex
u_{i}\!\in\!V(\Gamma_{i})
and a decomposition
\smash{\Gamma_{i}\!=\!(\Gamma_{i}^{(1)},u_{i})\#(\Gamma_{i}^{(2)},u_{i})}
of
\Gamma_{i}
associated to it. Let
\smash{\Gamma_{j}=(\Gamma_{j}^{(1)},u_{j})\#(\Gamma_{j}^{(2)},u_{j})}
be the image of the decomposition of
\Gamma_{i}
under
\varphi
. Making arbitrary choices of
u_{k}\in V(\Gamma_{k})
(
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\backslash\{i,j\}
), we may assume without loss of generality that
\textbf{u}(\emptyset)=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},\ldots,u_{n})
. Define an automorphism
\hat{\varphi}\in\Aut(\Gamma)\backslash\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
as follows. Let
\smash{B_{i}^{(1)},B_{j}^{(1)}\leq\Gamma}
be the subgraphs of
\Gamma
spanned by the sets of vertices
\tilde{v}=v_{1}\ldots v_{l}
with
\smash{v_{1}\in\Gamma_{i}^{(1)}}
and
\smash{v_{1}\in\Gamma_{j}^{(1)}}
respectively, and note that these subgraphs are two components of
\Gamma\setminus\{\emptyset\}
. Define

\tilde{\varphi}:\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{n}V(\Gamma_{k})\to\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{n}V(\Gamma_{k}),\ v\mapsto\begin{cases}\varphi(v)&\text{if }v\in V(\Gamma_{i})\\
\varphi^{-1}(v)&\text{if }v\in V(\Gamma_{j})\\
v&\text{if }v\notin V(\Gamma_{i})\sqcup V(\Gamma_{j})\end{cases}.

Now define
\hat{\varphi}(\emptyset)=\emptyset
and, for
\tilde{v}=v_{1}\ldots v_{l}\in V(\Gamma)
,

\hat{\varphi}(v_{1}\ldots v_{l}):=\begin{cases}\tilde{\varphi}(v_{1})\ldots\tilde{\varphi}(v_{l})&\text{if }\tilde{v}\in V(B_{i}^{(1)})\sqcup V(B_{j}^{(1)})\\
v_{1}\ldots v_{l}&\text{if }\tilde{v}\not\in V(B_{i}^{(1)})\sqcup V(B_{j}^{(1)})\end{cases}.

Then
\hat{\varphi}
is the identity on all components of
\Gamma\setminus\{\emptyset\}
except
B_{i}^{(1)}
and
B_{j}^{(1)}
and interchanges those components because it interchanges
i
and
j
in Equation (
2
). It also preserves edges because
\varphi
is an isomorphism. Hence,
\hat{\varphi}\in\Aut(\Gamma)
but not in
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
as it does not map the
\smash{\Gamma_{i}}
- and
\smash{\Gamma_{j}}
-sheets at
\emptyset\in V(\Gamma)
to other sheets.

Now, assume
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)\lneq\Aut(\Gamma)
. If no factor of
\Gamma
contains a cut vertex, then all factos of
\Gamma
are
2
-connected and hence
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)=\Aut(\Gamma)
, as discussed above, in contradiction to the assumption.
∎

7.1.
Structure Tree

To analyse
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
further, we associate a
structure tree

T
to
\Gamma
which captures the fact that the graph
\Gamma
is tree-like. Define

\displaystyle V(T):=V(\Gamma)\sqcup\calS\text{ and }E(T):=\left\{\{v,S\}\mid v\in V(\Gamma),\ S\in\calS\text{ and }v\in V(S)\right\}.

It is immediate from the definition that
T
is a bipartite graph with parts
V(\Gamma)
and
\calS
. Then, since any two distinct vertices in
V(\Gamma)
can belong to at most one sheet, and any two distinct sheets in
\calS
intersect in at most one vertex,
T
is a tree. When all factors of
\Gamma
are
2
-connected the tree
T
is precisely the block-cut tree of
\Gamma
, see
[
13
, Section 4]
. Note that
T
is locally finite if and only if all factors of
\Gamma
are finite. Also, the actions of
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
on the sets
V(\Gamma)
and
\calS
induce an injective homomorphism
\tau:\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)\to\Aut(T)
.

Remark 7.4
.

The map
\tau:\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)\to\Aut(T)
is in fact a topological embedding: To check continuity, let
F\subseteq V(T)
be a finite set and decompose
F=F_{V}\sqcup F_{\calS}
into vertices
F_{V}
coming from vertices of
\Gamma
, and vertices
F_{\calS}
coming from sheets of
\Gamma
. For every
S\in F_{\calS}
, choose
e_{S}\in E(S)
. Then
\tau^{-1}(\Aut(T)_{F})
contains the open set
\smash{\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)_{F_{V}\cup\{e_{S}\mid S\in F_{\calS}\}}}
. To see that
\tau
is open onto its image, note that
V(\Gamma)\subseteq V(T)
and therefore
\tau(\Aut(\Gamma)_{F})=\Aut(T)_{F}\cap\image\tau
for any finite set
F\subseteq V(\Gamma)
.

Using the standard metric
d^{\prime}
on
V(T)
, define a metric
d
on
\calS\subseteq V(T)
by
d:=\frac{1}{2}d^{\prime}
, as well as the map
\|\cdot\|:\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)\to\bbN_{0},\ \alpha\mapsto\min\{d(S,\alpha(S))\mid S\in\calS\}
.

Let
\alpha\in\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
. If
\|\alpha\|=0
then
\alpha
leaves a sheet invariant. For example, this applies to the automorphisms of Propositions
6.1
and
6.6
. The automorphisms in the following proposition satisfy either
\|\alpha\|=0
or
\|\alpha\|=1
.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be connected graphs,
\Gamma\!:=\!\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
and
\varphi:\Gamma_{i}\to\Gamma_{j}
an isomorphism for distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. If
\tilde{v}_{0}\!\in\!V(\Gamma)
satisfies
\varphi(u_{i}(\tilde{v}_{0}))\!=\!u_{j}(\tilde{v}_{0})
then there is
\hat{\varphi}\in\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)_{\tilde{v}_{0}}
with
\hat{\varphi}(S_{(\tilde{v}_{0},i)})=S_{(\tilde{v}_{0},j)}
and
\hat{\varphi}(S_{(\tilde{v}_{0},j)})=S_{(\tilde{v}_{0},i)}
.

Proof.

First, assume
\tilde{v}_{0}=\emptyset
and set

\tilde{\varphi}:\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{n}V(\Gamma_{k})\to\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{n}V(\Gamma_{k}),\ v\mapsto\begin{cases}\varphi(v)&\text{if }v\in V(\Gamma_{i})\\
\varphi^{-1}(v)&\text{if }v\in V(\Gamma_{j})\\
v&\text{if }v\notin V(\Gamma_{i})\sqcup V(\Gamma_{j})\end{cases}.

Now, set
\hat{\varphi}(\emptyset)=\emptyset
. For
\tilde{v}=v_{1}\ldots v_{l}\in V(\Gamma)
, define

\hat{\varphi}(v_{1}\ldots v_{l}):=\begin{cases}\tilde{\varphi}(v_{1})\ldots\tilde{\varphi}(v_{l})&\text{if }v_{1}\in V(\Gamma_{i})\cup V(\Gamma_{j})\\
v_{1}\ldots v_{l}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Note that
\hat{\varphi}(S_{(\tilde{v}_{0},i)})=S_{(\tilde{v}_{0},j)}
and
\hat{\varphi}(S_{(\tilde{v}_{0},j)})=S_{(\tilde{v}_{0},i)}
by definition, that it maps sheets to sheets, and that it exchanges the roles of
i
and
j
in Equation (
2
). It also preserves edge relations, because
\varphi
is an automorphism, and
\hat{\varphi}(\emptyset)=\emptyset
. Hence
\hat{\varphi}
belongs to
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)_{\tilde{v}_{0}}
.

If
\tilde{v}_{0}\neq\emptyset
, consider the free product
\Gamma^{\prime}:=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
initialised with
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(\emptyset)=\mathbf{u}(\tilde{v}_{0})
. Let
\psi:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Gamma
be the isomorphism with
\psi(\emptyset)=\tilde{v}_{0}
provided by Corollary
4.7
. Then
\psi\circ\hat{\varphi}\circ\psi^{-1}
, where
\hat{\varphi}
is applied to
\Gamma^{\prime}
serves.
∎

The following classification of sheet-preserving automorphisms of
\Gamma
is analogous to
[
24
, Proposition 3.2]
. A
bi-infinite line of sheets
in
\Gamma
is a map
\gamma:\bbZ\to\calS
such that
d(\gamma(n),\gamma(m))=|n-m|
for all

. An automorphism
\alpha\in\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
acts as a

along

if
\alpha(\gamma(n))=\gamma(n+l)
for all

.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be connected graphs,
\Gamma:=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
and
\alpha\in\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
. Then
\alpha
satisfies exactly one of the following conditions. Either, it

(
\|\alpha\|=0
)

leaves a sheet invariant, or

(
\|\alpha\|=1
)

fixes a vertex without leaving a sheet invariant, or

(
\|\alpha\|\geq 1
)

is a translation of length
\|\alpha\|
along a unique bi-infinite line of sheets.

Furthermore, if
\|\alpha\|\leq 1
then
\alpha=\sigma\circ\beta
for some automorphisms
\sigma,\beta\in\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
that leave a sheet invariant and stabilise a vertex respectively.

Proof.

Consider the automorphism
\tau(\alpha)\in\Aut(T)
induced by
\alpha\in\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
. By
[
24
, Proposition 3.2]
, the automorphism
\tau(\alpha)
either fixes a vertex, inverts an edge or acts as a translation along a unique bi-infinite line in

. If
\tau(\alpha)
fixes a vertex in
\calS\subseteq V(T)
then
\alpha
is of the first type. If
\tau(\alpha)
fixes a vertex in
V(\Gamma)\subseteq V(T)
, but no vertex in
\calS\subseteq V(T)
, then
\alpha
is of the second type. The case where
\tau(\alpha)
is an inversion does not occur because
\alpha
preserves the bipartition

. The final option is that
\tau(\alpha)
acts as a translation of length

along a unique bi-infinite line
\gamma^{\prime}:\bbZ\to V(T)
in

. Without loss of generality,
\gamma^{\prime}(0)\in\calS\subseteq V(T)
. Then
\alpha
is a translation of length

along
\gamma:\bbZ\to\calS
, where
\gamma(t)=\gamma^{\prime}(2t)
, and
\|\alpha\|=l/2
.

Now consider the case
\|\alpha\|\leq 1
. If
\|\alpha\|=0
or
\alpha
fixes a vertex then
\alpha
decomposes trivially. We may therefore assume that
\alpha
is a translation of length

along a bi-infinite line
\gamma:\bbZ\to\calS
of sheets. Let
i_{k}:=c(\gamma(k))\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
for all

. Since
\{1,\ldots,n\}
is finite there are

with
k<l
and
i_{k}=i_{l}
. Moreover, we may choose

minimal with this property. Put

. Note that the fact that

\gamma(k)\cap\gamma(k+1)=\{v\}
for some

and that
\gamma(k)
and
\gamma(k+1)
belong to sheets of different colours, so that

. Let
\mathbf{u}(v)=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
. Then
u_{i_{k}}(\alpha^{l-k-1}(v))=u_{i_{k}}(v)=v_{i_{k}}
as no minimal path from

to
\alpha^{l-k-1}(v)
passes through a sheet of type

. Hence the isomorphism
\smash{\varphi_{(v,i_{k+1})}^{-1}\circ\alpha^{-(l-k-1)}\circ\varphi_{(\alpha^{(l-k-1)}(v),i_{k})}:\Gamma_{i_{k}}\to\Gamma_{i_{k+1}}}

are the embeddings introduced in Example
4.3
1
, maps

to
v_{i_{k+1}}
. Proposition

now provides an automorphism

which fixes

and swaps

and

and

∎

7.2.
Simplicity

Here, we apply Tits’ simplicity criterion for groups acting on trees to subgroups of
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
via the injective homomorphism
\tau:\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)\to\Aut(T)
.

First, we briefly recall this criterion. See e.g. (
[
8
, Section 6.3]
) for more detail. Let
C
denote a finite or (bi)infinite path in
T
. For every
x\in V(C)
, the pointwise stabilizer
H_{C}
induces an action
\smash{H_{C}^{(x)}\leq\Aut(\pi^{-1}(x))}
on the subtree spanned by those vertices of
T
whose closest vertex in
C
is
x
; here,
\pi:V(T)\to V(C)
denotes the closest point projection. We therefore obtain an injective homomorphism

\varphi_{C}:H_{C}\to\prod\nolimits_{x\in V(C)}H_{C}^{(x)}.

A subgroup
H\leq\Aut(T)
satisfies
Property
P
if
\varphi_{C}
is an isomorphism for every path
C
in
T
. Also recall that Tits defines the subgroup
G^{+}
of
G
to be that generated by the pointwise stabilisers of the edges of the tree
T
.

Theorem

(
[
24
, 4.5 Théorème]
)
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(T)
. Suppose
G
neither fixes an end nor preserves a proper subtree of
T
setwise, and that
G
satisfies Property
P
. Then the group
G^{+}
is either trivial or simple.

Now, let
H\leq\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
. Given a sheet
S\in\calS
and
v\in V(S)
, we define the subgroup
H_{(S,v)}:=\{h\in H\mid h(v)=v\text{ and }h(S)=S\}
and put

H^{+_{\calS}}:=\langle\{H_{(S,v)}\mid S\in\calS,\ v\in V(S)\}\rangle(=\tau^{-1}(\tau(H)^{+})).

If
\tau(H)\leq\Aut(T)
preserves no proper subtree, fixes no end of
T
, and satisfies Property (P) then
\tau(H)^{+}
is either trivial or simple by
[
24
, 4.5 Théorème]
, and hence so is
H^{+_{\calS}}\cong\tau(H)^{+}
.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be connected, vertex-transitive graphs and let
\Gamma:=\operatorname{\scalebox{1.7}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\ast$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
. Then
\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)^{+_{\calS}}
is either trivial or simple.

Proof.

Let
G:=\Aut_{\calS}(\Gamma)
. We first show that
\tau(G)\leq\Aut(T)
is geometrically dense: Since the automorphisms of Corollary
6.4
preserve the set of sheets
\calS
, the group
\tau(G)
acts transitively on
V(\Gamma)\subseteq V(T)
. Hence it preserves no proper subtree of
T
. Proposition
6.1
and vertex-transitivity of the factors

imply that the stabiliser,
\tau(G)_{S}
, of a sheet

\tau(G)

\tau(G)

\tau(\alpha)

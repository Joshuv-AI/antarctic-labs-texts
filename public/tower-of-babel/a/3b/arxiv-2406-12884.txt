Automorphisms of free metabelian Lie algebras, I


Abstract
. We show that all Chein automorphisms (or one-row transformations) of lower degree ≥ 4 \geq 4 of a free metabelian Lie algebra M n M_{n} of rank n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 over an arbitrary field K K of characteristic ≠ 3 \neq 3 are tame. We then show that all exponential automorphisms of M n M_{n} of lower degree ≥ 5 \geq 5 are also tame under the same conditions. The same results hold for fields of any characteristic when n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 . These results contradict some long-standing results in the area. We also prove that a large class of automorphisms of M n M_{n} of rank n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 that move only two variables are almost tame, that is, they can be expressed as a product of Chein automorphisms.

1.
Introduction

The well known Jung–van der Kulk Theorem
[
16
,
20
]
states that every automorphism of the polynomial algebra
K[x,y]
in two variables
x,y
over an arbitrary field
K
is tame. An analogue of this result for free associative algebras in two variables was proven by Czerniakiewicz and Makar-Limanov
[
12
,
24
]
. The same result is true for free Poisson algebras in two variables over a field of characteristic zero
[
26
]
(see also
[
25
,
27
,
65
]
). Moreover, the automorphism groups of polynomial algebras, free associative algebras, and free Poisson algebras in two variables are isomorphic. The tameness of automorphisms of free right-symmetric algebras in two variables was proven in
[
18
]
.

For any free algebra
A
in the free variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
denote by
\phi=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
the endomorphism of
A
determined by
\phi(x_{i})=f_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

The automorphism groups of commutative and associative algebras generated by three elements are much more complicated.
The well-known Nagata automorphism
[
31
]

\displaystyle(x+2y(zx-y^{2})+z(zx-y^{2})^{2},y+z(zx-y^{2}),z)

of the polynomial algebra
K[x,y,z]
over a field
K
of characteristic
0
is proven to be wild
[
46
,
60
]
. The well-known Anick automorphism (see
[
11
, p. 398]
)

\displaystyle(x+z(xz-zy),\,y+(xz-zy)z,\,z)

of the free associative algebra
K\langle x,y,z\rangle
over a field
K
of characteristic
0
is also proven to be wild
[
61
,
63
]
.
The Nagata
automorphism gives an example of a wild
automorphism of free Poisson algebras in three variables. Recently Shestakov and Zhang
[
48
]
constructed an analogue of the Anick automorphism for free Poisson algebras in three variables.

It is well known
[
55
]
that the Nagata and Anick automorphisms are stably tame, that is, they become tame after adding one more variable.

A classical theorem of combinatorial group theory asserts that every subgroup of a free group is free. Nielsen established this remarkable result in 1921 for finitely generated subgroups
[
37
]
; in 1927, it was extended to all subgroups by Schreier
[
52
]
.
A variety of universal algebras is called Nielsen-Schreier, if any subalgebra of a free algebra of this variety is free. In 1953 Shirshiv and in 1956 Witt proved that
[
49
,
67
]
the varieties of Lie algebras and Lie
p
-algebras over a field are Nielsen-Schreier. The varieties of all non-associative algebras
[
21
]
,
commutative and anti-commutative algebras
[
50
]
, Lie superalgebras
[
28
,
54
]
, and Lie
p
-superalgebras
[
29
]
over a field are also Nielsen-Schreier. It was recently shown
[
13
]
that the varieties of pre-Lie (also known as right-symmetric) algebras and Lie-admissible algebras over a field of characteristic zero are Nielsen-Schreier.
Some other examples of Nielsen-Schreier
varieties can be found in
[
9
,
30
,
45
,
57
,
59
]
.

In 1924, Nielsen also proved
[
38
]
that the automorphism group of a finitely generated free group is generated by Nielsen transformations, and represented this group as a finitely defined group.
In 1964 P. Cohn proved
[
10
]
that all automorphisms of finitely generated free
Lie algebras over a field are tame. Later this result was extended to free algebras of Nielsen-Schreier varieties
[
22
]
.
Defining relations between the elementary automorphisms of free algebras of Nielsen-Schreier varieties are given in
[
64
]
.

One of the classical and well-studied varieties of groups is the variety of metabelian groups. In 1965 Bachmuth
[
2
]
proved that all automorphisms of the free metabelian group of rank
2
are tame. The first example of a non-tame automorphism of the free metabelian group of rank
3
was constructed by Chein
[
8
]
.
Bachmuth-Mochizuki proved
[
3
]
that the group of automorphisms of the free metabelian group of rank
3
is not even finitely generated. Strengthening these results, Roman’kov proved
[
41
]
that the free metabelian group of rank
3
contains non-tame primitive elements and gave a method of constructing of such elements. The well known Bachmuth-Mochizuki-Roman’kov Theorem
[
4
,
40
]
states that every automorphism of the free metabelian group of rank
n\geq 4
is tame. More information about automorphisms of free metabelian groups can be found in
[
44
]
.

The variety of metabelian Lie algebras is also a classical and well-studied variety of Lie algebras.
Throughout this paper,
M_{n}
denotes the free metabelian Lie algebra of rank
n
over a field
K
generated by
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
It is well known that any nontrivial exponential automorphism of
M_{2}
is wild (see, for example
[
1
,
51
]
).
In 1992, Drensky
[
14
]
proved that the exponential automorphism
\mathrm{exp}(\mathrm{ad}[x_{1},x_{2}])
of
M_{3}
is wild. More examples of wild automorphisms of
M_{3}
are given in
[
17
,
32
,
43
]
.

In 1992 Bahturin and Nabiyev
[
5
]
claimed that every nontrivial exponential automorphism of the free metabelian Lie algebra
M_{n}
of any rank
n\geq 2
over a field of characteristic zero is wild. More examples of wild automorphisms of
M_{n}
of rank
n\geq 4
were given in 2008 by Özcurt and Ekici
[
39
]
. For a long time, the area specialists believed these results were true. Unfortunately, both articles have fatal errors
[
66
]
.

We show that all Chein automorphisms (or one-row transformations) of lower degree
\geq 4
of a free metabelian Lie algebra
M_{n}
of rank
n\geq 4
over an arbitrary field
K
of characteristic
\neq 3
are tame. We then show that all exponential automorphisms of
M_{n}
of lower degree
\geq 5
are also tame under the same conditions. The same results hold for fields of any characteristic when
n\geq 5
. These results contradict those of
[
5
,
39
]
.

We also prove that a large important class of automorphisms of
M_{n}
of rank
n\geq 4
that move only two variables are almost tame, that is, they can be expressed as a product of Chein automorphisms.

More precise formulations of the results are given in the following sections.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we define some important automorphisms of
M_{n}
and give precise formulations some of our results. In Section 3, using a representation of
M_{n}
via Magnus embedding and Fox derivatives, we describe Chein autoimorphisms of
M_{n}
. In Section 4 we prove that a huge part of Chein and exponential automorphisms are tame. In Section 5 we prove that a huge class of automorphisms that move only two variables are almost tame.

2.
Automorphisms of
M_{n}

Let
M_{n}
be the free metabelian Lie algebra over a field
K
with free generators
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
The set of all right normed Lie monomials of the form

(1)

\displaystyle[\ldots[[x_{i},x_{i_{1}}],x_{i_{2}}],\ldots,x_{i_{k}}],\ i>i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\ldots\leq i_{k},

is a linear basis of
M_{n}^{\prime}=[M_{n},M_{n}]
(see, for example
[
6
, p. 139]
).

Let
\mathrm{Aut}(M_{n})
be the group of all automorphisms of
M_{n}
.
An automorphism of
M_{n}
of the form

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i-1},\alpha x_{i}+f,x_{i+1},\ldots x_{n}),

where
0\neq\alpha\in K
and
f
does not contain
x_{i}
, is called
elementary
. The subgroup
\mathrm{TAut}(M_{n})
of
\mathrm{Aut}(M_{n})
generated by all
elementary automorphisms is called the
tame automorphism group
,
and the elements of this subgroup are called
tame automorphisms

of
M_{n}
. Nontame automorphisms of
M_{n}
are called
wild
.

Defining relations among elementary automorphisms have been described for polynomial algebras in three variables
[
61
,
62
]
, for finitely generated free Lie algebras
[
64
]
, and for free metabelian Lie algebras in three variables
[
33
]
.

As usual, the group of all linear automorphisms of
M_{n}
will be identified with the group of all invertible matrices
GL_{n}(K)
.
For every pair of integers
s,t
with
1\leq s\neq t\leq n
, let
(st)
denote the automorphism that simply permutes the variables
x_{s}

and
x_{t}
and leaves all other variables unchanged. The group generated by all
(st)
is isomorphic to the symmetric group
S_{n}
.

In 2015, Nauryzbaev
[
35
]
proved that the group of tame automorphisms of
M_{n}
is generated by all linear automorphisms
GL_{n}(K)
and the quadratic automorphism

(2)

\displaystyle(x_{1}+[x_{2},x_{3}],x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})

if either
n\geq 4
and
K
is a field of characteristic
\neq 3
, or
n\geq 5
and
K
is a field of arbitrary characteristic.

If
\phi=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}),\psi=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})\in\mathrm{Aut}(M_{n})
, then the composition
\phi\psi
is defined as

\displaystyle\phi\psi=(g_{1}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}),\ldots,g_{n}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})).

We also adopt the standard notation
[\phi,\psi]=\phi\psi\phi^{-1}\psi^{-1}
for the commutator, and
\phi^{\psi}=\psi\phi\psi^{-1}
for conjugation.

Let
0\neq z\in[M_{n},M_{n}]
. Then
\mathrm{ad}\,z:M_{n}\to M_{n}(m\mapsto[z,m])
is a nilpotent derivation and
(\mathrm{ad}\,z)^{2}=0
. The
exponential
automorphism

\exp(\mathrm{ad}\,z)
of
M_{n}
can be written as

\displaystyle\exp(\mathrm{ad}\,z)=\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{ad}\,z=(x_{1}+[z,x_{1}],\ldots,x_{n}+[z,x_{n}]).

It is known that the exponential automorphism
\exp(\mathrm{ad}\,[x_{1},x_{2}])
of
M_{3}
is wild
[
14
]
.

Recall that in 1993 Bryant and Drensky proved
[
7
]
that every automorphism of
M_{n}
of rank
n\geq 4
over a field
K
of characteristic zero is
approximately tame
( see the definitions in
[
47
]
); that is, it can be approximated by tame automorphisms with respect to power series topology
[
7
,
47
]
. In contrast, all wild automorphisms of
M_{3}
are
absolutely wild

[
47
]
, meaning that they cannot be approximated by tame automorphisms.

Free metabelian Lie algebras admit automorphisms of the form

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},f_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}),\ 1\leq i\leq n,

which include all elementary automorphisms but not necessarily elementary ones. They are called
Chein automorphisms
or
one-row transformations

[
15
,
42
]
. These automorphisms move only one variable and fix the others. The subgroup
\mathrm{ATAut}(M_{n})
of
\mathrm{Aut}(M_{n})
generated by all
Chein automorphisms is called the
almost tame automorphism group
,
and the elements of this subgroup is called
almost tame automorphisms

of
M_{n}

[
32
,
34
]
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{TAut}(M_{n})\subseteq\mathrm{ATAut}(M_{n})\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(M_{n}).

Obviously, the group of almost tame automorphisms
\mathrm{ATAut}(M_{n})
is generated by all linear automorphisms
GL_{n}(K)
and all Chein automorphisms of the form

(3)

\displaystyle(x_{1}+f,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),\ f\in[M_{n},M_{n}].

Chein automorphisms of this form are described in Section 3.

The lowest-degree example of a non-elementary Chein automorphism is

(4)

\displaystyle(x_{1}+[[x_{2},x_{3}],x_{1}],x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}).

This automorphism is wild if
n=3

[
32
,
43
]
, and is the first candidate for wildness for all
n\geq 4
, but it is still unknown whether it is tame or not. It was recently shown
[
36
]
that an analogue of this automorphism for free metabelian anticommutative algebras is absolutely wild for all
n\geq 3
.

In 2008, Roman’kov
[
43
]
proved that the group of automorphisms of
M_{3}
cannot be finitely generated modulo the subgroup generated by all exponential and Chein automorphisms. Kabanov and Roman’kov
[
17
]
constructed strictly nontame primitive elements of
M_{3}
. Defining relations for Chein automorphisms of
M_{3}
are given in
[
34
]
.

We introduce the notion of the lower degree of an automorphism. Consider the standard degree grading of

\displaystyle M_{n}=L_{1}\oplus L_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus L_{k}\oplus\ldots,

where
L_{k}
is the linear span of all Lie monomials of degree
k
in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. Every nonzero
m\in M_{n}
can be uniquely written in the form

\displaystyle m=l_{s}+l_{s+1}+\ldots+l_{t},

where
l_{j}\in L_{j}
for all
s\leq j\leq t
,
l_{s}\neq 0
, and
l_{t}\neq 0
. We call
\mathrm{ldeg}(m)=s
the
lower degree
of
m
, while
\deg(m)=t
denotes the degree of
m
. If
\mathrm{ldeg}(m)=\deg(m)=t
, then
m
is
homogeneous
of degree
t
. Set also
\mathrm{ldeg}(0)=\infty
and
\deg(0)=-\infty
.

Let

\displaystyle\phi=(l_{1}+m_{1},\ldots,l_{n}+m_{n})

be an automorphism of
M_{n}
such that
l_{i}\in L_{1}
and
m_{i}\in[M_{n},M_{n}]
for all
i
. Set

\displaystyle\mathrm{ldeg}(\phi)=\mathrm{min}\{\mathrm{ldeg}(m_{i})|1\leq i\leq n\}

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\phi)=\mathrm{max}\{\mathrm{deg}(m_{i})|1\leq i\leq n\}.

If
\mathrm{ldeg}(\phi)=\mathrm{deg}(\phi)=t
, then we say that
\phi
is
homogeneous
of degree
t
. In particular, (
2
) is quadratic and (
4
) is cubic.
Clearly, every nonlinear automorphism has a finite lower degree and degree.

We show that all Chein automorphisms of lower degree
\geq 4
are tame. This means that all Chein automorphisms ”except” (
4
) are tame. We also show that all exponential automorphisms of lower degree
\geq 5
are tame. These results contradict the results of
[
5
,
39
]
.

3.
Chein automorphisms

In this section, using an analogue of the well known Magnus embedding
[
19
,
23
]
, we give another representation of the free metabelian algebra
M_{n}
, and give direct definition of the Fox derivatives for
M_{n}
. This construction for Lie algebras is a particular case of Shmel’kin’s wreath products
[
51
]
.

Let
Y_{n}=M_{n}/[M_{n},M_{n}]
be the abelian Lie algebra with a linear basis
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}
, where
y_{i}=x_{i}+[M_{n},M_{n}]
.
The universal enveloping algebra
U(Y_{n})
of
Y_{n}
is the polynomial algebra
U=U(Y_{n})=K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
in the variables
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}
. Let

\displaystyle U=U_{0}\oplus U_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus U_{k}\oplus\ldots

be the degree grading of
U
, i.e.,
U_{k}
is the linear span of all homogeneous monomials in
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}
of degree
k
.
If

\displaystyle a=a_{s}+a_{s+1}+\ldots+a_{t}\in U,a_{j}\in U_{j},a_{s}\neq 0,a_{t}\neq 0,

then
\deg(a)=t
is the degree and
\mathrm{ldeg}(a)=s
is the lower degree of
a
in the variables
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}
.
Set
\deg(0)=-\infty
and
\mathrm{ldeg}(0)=\infty
as usual.

Let
T_{n}
be the free right
U
-module with basis
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
. Turn the direct sum

\displaystyle M=Y_{n}\oplus T_{n}

into a Lie algebra by

\displaystyle[a+t,b+s]=tb-sa,

where
a,b\in Y_{n}
and
t,s\in T_{n}
. Then the subalgebra of
M
generated by

\displaystyle x_{i}=y_{i}+t_{i},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

is the free metabelian Lie algebra with free generators
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}

[
19
,
23
,
51
]
. We identify this algebra with
M_{n}
.

Notice that
M_{n}^{\prime}=[M_{n},M_{n}]\subseteq T_{n}
and for any
m\in M_{n}^{\prime}
and
y_{i}
we get

\displaystyle my_{i}=[m,x_{i}].

Every element
m\in M_{n}^{\prime}
can be written in the form

\displaystyle m=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}[x_{i},x_{j}]a_{ij},\ \ a_{ij}\in U.

Let
f\in M_{n}
be an arbitrary element
M_{n}
. Then
f
is uniquely represented as

\displaystyle f=y+t,y\in Y_{n},t\in T_{n}.

Moreover,
t
is uniquely represented as

\displaystyle t=t_{1}d_{1}+\ldots+t_{n}d_{n},\ \ \ \ d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\in K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]=U.

Set
d_{i}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and

\displaystyle\partial(f)=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})^{t}=\big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\big)^{t},

where
t
means the transpose. We call
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}
the Fox derivatives of
f
(see
[
53
,
56
]
).

Let
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}
be the columns of the identity matrix
I=I_{n}
of order
n
.

The statement of the next lemma is well known
[
56
,
58
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})^{t}\in(U^{n})^{t}
be an arbitrary column of dimension
n
over
U
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

a=\partial(f)
for some
f\in[M_{n},M_{n}]
;

(b)

Ya=y_{1}a_{1}+\ldots+y_{n}a_{n}=0
where
Y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
;

(c)

a=\sum_{i<j}(e_{i}y_{j}-e_{j}y_{i})a_{ij},\ a_{ij}\in U
.

Proof.
If
i<j
and
a\in U
, then

\displaystyle\partial([x_{i},x_{j}]a)=e_{i}y_{j}a-e_{j}y_{i}a=(e_{i}y_{j}-e_{j}y_{i})a.

Consequently,
(a)
implies
(c)
. Obviously,
(c)
implies
(b)
since
Y(e_{i}y_{j}-e_{j}y_{i})=0
for all
i<j
.

Suppose that
(b)
holds. Let
a_{i}=-y_{n}b_{i}+a_{i}^{0}
, where
a_{i}^{0}\in K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1}]
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
Then
(b)
implies that
a_{n}=y_{1}b_{1}+\ldots+y_{n-1}b_{n-1}
. Set
g=[x_{n},x_{1}]b_{1}+\ldots+[x_{n},x_{n-1}]b_{n-1}
. We get

\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{n}}=a_{n}
and the last component of
a^{\prime}=a-\partial(g)
is zero. Moreover, if
a^{\prime}=(a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{n-1}^{\prime},0)
then

y_{1}a_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+y_{n-1}a_{n-1}^{\prime}=0
. Leading an induction on the number of nonzero components of
a
, we may assume that there exists
h\in[M_{n},M_{n}]
such that
a^{\prime}=\partial(h)
. Then
a=\partial(g+h)
.

\Box

Every endomorphism
\phi
of
M_{n}
induces an endomorphism of
Y_{n}=M_{n}/[M_{n},M_{n}]
, which can be uniquely extended to an endomorphism of
U=U(Y_{n})
. This endomorphism will be denoted again by
\phi
for simplicity of notation. If
f\in U
, then the image
\phi(f)
will be denoted by
f^{\phi}
for convenience.

We define the Jacobian matrix of
\phi=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
by

\displaystyle J(\phi)=\left[\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}=\left[\partial(f_{1})\ \ldots\ \partial(f_{n})\right].

If
\phi=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
and
\psi=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
then

\displaystyle\phi\psi(x_{i})=g_{i}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})

for all
1\leq i\leq n
. The corresponding Chain Rule gives
[
53
,
56
,
58
]
that

\displaystyle J(\phi\psi)=J(\phi)J(\psi)^{\phi}.

Consequently, if
\phi
is an automorphism, then
J(\phi)
is invertible. The converse is also true
[
53
,
56
]
.

Lemma 2
.

An endomorphism
\phi
of
M_{n}
is an automorphism if and only if the Jacobian matrix
J(\phi)
is invertible.

Proof.
Suppose that
J(\phi)
is invertible. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

\displaystyle\phi=(x_{1}+f_{1},\ldots,x_{n}+f_{n}),\ \ f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in[M_{n},M_{n}].

Then

\displaystyle J(\phi)=I+A,\ \ A=[\partial(f_{1}),\ldots,\partial(f_{n})].

We get

\displaystyle J(\phi)^{-1}=I+B,B=\sum_{i\geq 1}(-A)^{i}.

By Lemma
1
, we have
YA=0
. Consequently,
YB=0
. Again, by Lemma
1
, there exist
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in[M_{n},M_{n}]
such that

\displaystyle B=[\partial(g_{1}),\ldots,\partial(g_{n})].

Let
\psi=(x_{1}+g_{1},\ldots,x_{n}+g_{n})
. Then

\displaystyle J(\psi)=I+B=J(\phi)^{-1}

and

\displaystyle J(\phi\psi)=J(\phi)J(\psi)^{\phi}=J(\phi)J(\psi)=I.

Consequently,
\phi\psi=\mathrm{Id}
and
\phi
is an automorphism.
\Box

We now give a description of Chein automorphisms of the form (
3
).

Lemma 3
.

An endomorphism of
M_{n}
of the form (
3
)
is an automorphism if and only if
f
belongs to the ideal generated by all
[x_{s},x_{t}]
, where
s,t\neq 1
.

Proof.
Let
\phi=(x_{1}+f,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
where
f\in[M_{n},M_{n}]
. The Jacobian matrix of
\phi
is

\displaystyle J(\phi)=I+[\partial(f)\ 0\ldots 0].

This matrix is invertible if and only if
1+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}
is invertible in
U
or, equivalently,

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}=0
. We want to show that
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}=0
if and only if
f
belongs to the ideal generated by all
[x_{s},x_{t}]
, where
s,t\neq 1
.

The basis (
1
) of
[M_{n},M_{n}]
is convenient to work with
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}=0
. Any
f\in[M_{n},M_{n}]
can be written in the form

\displaystyle f=f^{\prime}+[x_{n},x_{1}]a_{1}+[x_{n},x_{2}]a_{2}+\ldots+[x_{n},x_{n-1}]a_{n-1},

where

An accelerated minimax algorithm for convex-concave saddle point problems with nonsmooth coupling function
Radu Ioan Boţ, Ernö Robert Csetnek, Michael Sedlmayer

Abstract
In this work we aim to solve a convex-concave saddle point problem, where the convex-concave coupling function is smooth in one variable and nonsmooth in the other and not assumed to be linear in either. The problem is augmented by a nonsmooth regulariser in the smooth component. We propose and investigate a novel algorithm under the name of OGAProx , consisting of an optimistic gradient ascent step in the smooth variable coupled with a proximal step of the regulariser, and which is alternated with a proximal step in the nonsmooth component of the coupling function. We consider the situations convex-concave, convex-strongly concave and strongly convex-strongly concave related to the saddle point problem under investigation. Regarding iterates we obtain (weak) convergence, a convergence rate of order 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 K ) \mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{K}) and linear convergence like 𝒪 ⁡ ( θ K ) \mathcal{O}(\theta^{K}) with θ < 1 \theta<1 , respectively. In terms of function values we obtain ergodic convergence rates of order 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 K ) \mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{K}) , 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 K 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{K^{2}}) and 𝒪 ⁡ ( θ K ) \mathcal{O}(\theta^{K}) with θ < 1 \theta<1 , respectively. We validate our theoretical considerations on a nonsmooth-linear saddle point problem, the training of multi kernel support vector machines and a classification problem incorporating minimax group fairness. Key words. saddle point problem, convex-concave, minimax algorithm, convergence rate, acceleration, linear convergence



1
Introduction

Saddle point – or minimax – problems have witnessed increased interest due to many relevant and challenging applications in the field of machine learning, with the most prominent being the training of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)
[
10
]
. Even though the problems in reality are often not of this form, in the classical setting the minimax objective comprises a smooth convex-concave coupling function with Lipschitz continuous gradient and a (potentially nonsmooth) regulariser in each variable, leading to a convex-concave objective in total.

One well established method in practice due to its simplicity and computational efficiency is
Gradient Descent Ascent
(GDA), either in a
simultaneous
or in an
alternating
variant (for a recent comparison of the convergence behaviour of the two schemes we refer to
[
23
]
). However, naive application of GDA is known to lead to oscillatory behaviour or even divergence already in simple cases such as bilinear objectives. Most algorithms with convergence guarantees in the general convex-concave setting make use of the formulation of the first order optimality conditions as monotone inclusion or variational inequality, treating both components in a symmetric fashion. For example we have the
Extragradient method

[
12
]
whose application to minimax problems has been studied in
[
19
]
under the name of
Mirror Prox
, and the
Forward-Backward-Forward method
(FBF)
[
22
]
with application to saddle point problems in
[
3
]
. Both algorithms have even been successfully applied to the training of GANs (see
[
9
,
3
]
), but, though being single-loop methods, suffer in practice from requiring two gradient evaluations per iteration. A possible way to avoid this is to reuse previous gradients. Doing this for FBF – as shown in
[
3
]
– recovers the
Forward-Reflected Backward method

[
15
]
which was applied to saddle point problems under the name of
Optimistic Mirror Descent
and to GAN training under the name of
Optimistic Gradient Descent Ascent

[
6
,
5
,
14
]
.

The first method treating general coupling functions with an asymmetric scheme is the
Accelerated Primal-Dual Algorithm
(APD) by
[
11
]
, involving an optimistic gradient ascent step in one component which is followed by a gradient descent step in the other one. In the special case of a bilinear coupling function APD recovers the
Primal-Dual Hybrid Gradient Method
(PDHG)
[
4
]
. In the case of the minimax objective being strongly convex-concave acceleration of PDHG is obtained in
[
4
]
, which is also done for APD in
[
11
]
, however only under the rather limiting assumption of linearity of the coupling function in one component.

In this paper we introduce a novel algorithm
OGAProx
for solving a convex-concave saddle point problem, where the convex-concave coupling function is smooth in one variable and nonsmooth in the other, and it is augmented by a nonsmooth regulariser in the smooth component. OGAProx consists of an optimistic gradient ascent step in the smooth component of the coupling function combined with a proximal step of the regulariser, and which is followed by a proximal step of the coupling function in the nonsmooth component. We will be also able to accelerate our method in the convex-strongly concave setting
without
linearity assumption on the coupling function. Furthermore we prove linear convergence if the problem is strongly convex-strongly concave, yielding similar results as for PDHG
[
4
]
in the bilinear case.

So far in most works nonsmoothness is only introduced via regularisers, as the coupling function is typically accessed through gradient evaluations. Recently there is another development, although with the saddle point problem
not
being convex-concave, where the assumption on differentiability of the coupling function in both components is weakened to only one component
[
2
]
. As the evaluation of the proximal mapping does not require differentiability we will assume the coupling function to be smooth in only one component, too.

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. Next we will introduce the precise problem formulation and the setting we will work with, formulate the proposed algorithm OGAProx and state our contributions. This will be followed by preliminaries in Section
2
. Afterwards we will discuss the properties of our algorithm in the convex-concave and convex-strongly concave setting and state respective convergence results in Section
3
. After that we will investigate the convergence of the method under the additional assumption of strong convexity-strong concavity in Section
4
. The paper will be concluded by numerical experiments in Section
5
, where we treat a simple nonsmooth-linear saddle point problem, the training of multi kernel support vector machines and a classification problem taking into account minimax group fairness.

1.1
Problem description

Consider the saddle point problem

\min_{x\in{\mathcal{H}}}\max_{y\in{\mathcal{G}}}\Psi(x,y):=\Phi(x,y)-g(y),

(1)

where
{\mathcal{H}},\,{\mathcal{G}}
are real Hilbert spaces,
\Phi:{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a coupling function with

\dom\Phi:=\{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}\ |\ \Phi(x,y)<+\infty\}\neq\emptyset
and
g:{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
a regulariser. Throughout the paper (unless otherwise specified) we will make the following assumptions:

\bullet

g
is proper, lower semicontinuous and convex with modulus
\nu\geq 0
, i.e.
g-\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert\,\cdot\,\right\rVert^{2}
is convex (notice that we also allow and consider the situation
\nu=0
, in which case
g
is convex; otherwise
g
is strongly convex);

\bullet

for all
y\in\dom g
,
\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y):{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous;

\bullet

for all
x\in\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi):=\{u\in{\mathcal{H}}\ |\ \exists y\in{\mathcal{G}}\ \mbox{such that}\ (u,y)\in\dom\Phi\}
we have that
\dom\Phi(x,\,\cdot\,)={\mathcal{G}}
and
\Phi(x,\,\cdot\,):{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
is concave and Fréchet differentiable. Moreover,
\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)
is closed;

\bullet

there exist
L_{yx},\,L_{yy}\geq 0
such that for all
(x,y),\,(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)\times\dom g
it holds

\left\lVert\nabla_{y}\Phi(x,y)-\nabla_{y}\Phi(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right\rVert\leq L_{yx}\left\lVert x-x^{\prime}\right\rVert+L_{yy}\left\lVert y-y^{\prime}\right\rVert.

(2)

By convention we set
+\infty-(+\infty):=+\infty
. Thus, the situation can be summarised by

\Psi(x,y)=\begin{cases}-\infty&\text{if }x\in\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)\text{ and }y\notin\dom g,\\
\Phi(x,y)-g(y)&\text{if }x\in\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)\text{ and }y\in\dom g,\\
+\infty&\text{if }x\notin\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi).\end{cases}

(3)

We are interested in finding a
saddle point
of (
1
), which is a point
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
that fulfils the inequalities

\Psi(x^{\ast},y)\leq\Psi(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\leq\Psi(x,y^{\ast})\quad\forall(x,y)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}.

(4)

For the remainder we assume that such a saddle point exists.

The assumptions considered above ensure that for any saddle point
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
we have

x^{\ast}\in\pr\nolimits_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi),\quad y^{\ast}\in\dom g\ \mbox{and}\ \Psi(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})=\Phi(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})-g(y^{\ast})\in\mathbb{R}.

Finding a saddle point of (
1
) amounts to solving the necessary and sufficient first order optimality conditions, given by the following coupled inclusion problems

0\in\partial\left[\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y^{\ast})\right](x^{\ast})\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt0\in\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\nabla_{y}\Phi(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})+\partial g(y^{\ast}).

(5)

Remark 1
.

In case
\Phi
and
g
have full domain,
\Psi
is a convex-concave function with full domain and the set
\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)
is obviously closed. However, in order to allow more flexibility and to cover a wider range
of problems (see also the last section with numerical experiments), our investigations are carried out in the more general setting given by the assumptions described above. Furthermore, these assumptions allow us to stay in the rigorous setting of the theory of convex-concave saddle functions as described by Rockafellar in
[
21
]
(see Definition
3
and Proposition
4
below).

1.2
Algorithm

The algorithm we investigate performs an optimistic gradient ascent step of
\Phi
followed by an evaluation of the proximal mapping
g
in the variable
y
, while it carries out a purely proximal step of
\Phi
in
x
. We will call this method
Optimistic Gradient Ascent – Proximal Point algorithm
(OGAProx) in the following. For all
k\geq 0
we define

\displaystyle\raisebox{-3.75pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\\
\end{array}\right.\kern-1.2pt$}}y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{prox}_{\sigma_{k}g}\left(y_{k}+\sigma_{k}\left[(1+\theta_{k})\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})-\theta_{k}\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\right]\right),

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{prox}_{\tau_{k}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y_{k+1})}\left(x_{k}\right),

(8)

with the conventions
x_{-1}:=x_{0}
and
y_{-1}:=y_{0}
for starting points
x_{0}\in\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)
and
y_{0}\in\dom g
. The particular choices of the sequences
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 0},\,(\tau_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
and
(\theta_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subseteq\left(0,1\right]
will be specified later.

2
Preliminaries

We recall some basic notions in convex analysis and monotone operator theory (see for example
[
1
]
). The real Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
are endowed with inner products
\left\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\right\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
and
\left\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\right\rangle_{{\mathcal{G}}}
, respectively. As it will be clear from the context which one is meant, we will drop the index for ease of notation and write
\left\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\right\rangle
for both. The norm induced by the respective inner products is defined by
\left\lVert\,\cdot\,\right\rVert:=\sqrt{\left\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\right\rangle}
.

A function
f:{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is said to be
proper
if
\dom f:=\{x\in{\mathcal{H}}:f(x)<+\infty\}\neq\emptyset
. The
(convex) subdifferential
of the function
f:{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
at
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
is defined by
\partial f(x):=\{u\in{\mathcal{H}}\hskip 5.69054pt|\,\left\langle y-x,u\right\rangle+f(x)\leq f(y)\ \forall y\in{\mathcal{H}}\}
if
f(x)\in\mathbb{R}
and by
\partial f(x):=\emptyset
otherwise. If the function
f
is convex and Fréchet differentiable at
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
, then
\partial f(x)=\{\nabla f(x)\}
. For the sum of a proper, convex and lower semicontinuous function
f:{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and a convex and Fréchet differentiable function
h:{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}
we have
\partial(f+h)(x)=\partial f(x)+\nabla h(x)
for all
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
. The subdifferential of the
indicator function

\delta_{C}
of a nonempty closed convex set
C\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
, defined as
\delta_{C}(x)=0
for
x\in C
and
\delta_{C}(x)=+\infty
otherwise, is denoted by
N_{C}:=\partial\delta_{C}
and is called the
normal cone
the set
C
.

Let
f:{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper, convex and lower semicontinuous. The
proximal operator
of
f
is defined by

\textup{prox}_{f}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}},\quad\operatorname{prox}_{f}\left(x\right):=\argmin_{y\in{\mathcal{H}}}\left\{f(y)+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert y-x\right\rVert^{2}\right\}.

(9)

The proximal operator of the indicator function
\delta_{C}
of a nonempty closed convex set
C\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
is the
orthogonal projection

P_{C}:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow C
of the set
C
.

A set-valued operator
A:{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is said to be
monotone
if for all
(x,u)
,
(y,v)\in\gra A:=\{(z,w)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\>|\>w\in Az\}
we have
\left\langle x-y,u-v\right\rangle\geq 0
. Furthermore,
A
is said to be
maximal monotone
if it is monotone and there exists no monotone operator
B:{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\gra A\subsetneqq\gra B
. The graph of a maximal monotone operator
A:{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is
sequentially closed
in the
strong

\times

weak
topology, which means that if
(x_{k},u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a sequence in
\gra A
such that
x_{k}\to x
and
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
, then
(x,u)\in\gra A
. The notation
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
is used to denote convergence of the sequence
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
to
u
in the weak topology.

To show weak convergence of sequences in Hilbert spaces we use the following so-called
Opial Lemma
.

Lemma 2
.

(Opial Lemma
[
20
]
)
Let
C\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
be a nonempty set and
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
a sequence in
{\mathcal{H}}
such that the following two conditions hold:

(a)

for every
x\in C
,
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left\lVert x_{k}-x\right\rVert
exists;

(b)

every weak sequential cluster point of
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
belongs to
C
.

Then
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
converges weakly to an element in
C
.

In the following definition we adjust the term
proper
to the saddle point setting and refer to
[
21
]
for further considerations related to saddle functions.

Definition 3
.

A function
\Psi:{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
is called a
saddle function
, if
\Psi(\,\cdot\,,y)
is convex for all
y\in{\mathcal{G}}
and
\Psi(x,\,\cdot\,)
is concave for all
x\in{\mathcal{G}}
. A saddle function
\Psi
is called
proper
, if there exists
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\Psi(x^{\prime},y)<+\infty
for all
y\in{\mathcal{G}}
and
-\infty<\Psi(x,y^{\prime})
for all
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
.

We conclude the preliminary section with a useful result regarding the minimax objective from (
1
).

Proposition 4
.

The function
\Psi:{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
defined via (
3
) is a proper saddle function such that
\Psi(\,\cdot\,,y)
is lower semicontinuous for each
y\in{\mathcal{G}}
and
\Psi(x,\,\cdot\,)
is upper semicontinuous for each
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Consequently, the operator

(x,y)\mapsto\partial[\Psi(\,\cdot\,,y)](x)\times\partial[\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\Psi(x,\,\cdot\,)](y)

is maximal monotone.

Proof.

We choose
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
and distinguish four cases.

Firstly, we look at the case
y^{\prime}\notin\dom g
. Then

\Psi(x,y^{\prime})=\begin{cases}-\infty&\text{if }x\in\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi),\\
+\infty&\text{if }x\notin\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi),\end{cases}

(10)

thus
x\mapsto\Psi(x,y^{\prime})
is convex and lower semicontinuous, since
\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)
is convex and closed. Secondly, if
y^{\prime}\in\dom g
, then
g(y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}
and

\Psi(x,y^{\prime})=\Phi(x,y^{\prime})-g(y^{\prime})\ \forall x\in{\mathcal{H}},

(11)

which means that
x\mapsto\Psi(x,y^{\prime})
is convex and lower semicontinuous. This proves that
\Psi(\,\cdot\,,y)
is convex and lower semicontinuous for all
y\in{\mathcal{G}}
.

On the other hand, if
x^{\prime}\notin\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)
, then

\Psi(x^{\prime},y)=+\infty\ \forall y\in{\mathcal{G}},

(12)

which means that
y\mapsto\Psi(x^{\prime},y)
is upper semicontinuous and concave. Finally, if
x^{\prime}\in\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)
, then

\Phi(x^{\prime},y)\in\mathbb{R}\ \mbox{and}\ -\Psi(x^{\prime},y)=-\Phi(x^{\prime},y)+g(y)\ \forall y\in{\mathcal{G}}.

(13)

Hence
y\mapsto-\Psi(x^{\prime},y)
is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous, and so
y\mapsto\Psi(x^{\prime},y)
is concave and upper semicontinuous. This proves that
\Psi(x,\,\cdot\,)
is concave and upper semicontinuous for all
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
.

Moreover,
\Psi
is a proper saddle function. By assumption we have
g(y)>-\infty
for all
y\in{\mathcal{G}}
and there exists
x^{\prime}\in\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)\neq\emptyset
such that
\dom\Phi(x^{\prime},\,\cdot\,)={\mathcal{G}}
. Thus

\Psi(x^{\prime},y)=\Phi(x^{\prime},y)-g(y)<+\infty\ \forall y\in{\mathcal{G}}.

(14)

Furthermore, by assumption there exist
y^{\prime}\in\dom g\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}
such that
g(y^{\prime})<+\infty
and for all
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
we have
\Phi(x,y^{\prime})>-\infty
. Hence,

\Psi(x,y^{\prime})=\Phi(x,y^{\prime})-g(y^{\prime})>-\infty\ \forall x\in{\mathcal{H}}.

(15)

The maximal monotonicity of
(x,y)\mapsto\partial[\Psi(\,\cdot\,,y)](x)\times\partial[\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\Psi(x,\,\cdot\,)](y)

follows from Corollary 1 and Theorem 3 in
[
21
, pages 248-249]
.
∎

3
Convex-(strongly) concave setting

First we will treat the case when the coupling function
\Phi
is convex-concave and
g
is convex with modulus
\nu\geq 0
. In the case
\nu=0
this corresponds to
\Psi(x,y)=\Phi(x,y)-g(y)
being convex-concave, while for
\nu>0
the saddle function
\Psi(x,y)
is convex-strongly concave.

We will start with stating two assumptions on the step sizes of the algorithm which will be needed in the convergence analysis. These will be followed by a unified preparatory analysis for general
\nu\geq 0
that will be the base to show convergence of the iterates as well as of the minimax gap. After that we will introduce a choice of parameters that satisfy the aforementioned assumptions. The section will be closed by convergence results for the convex-concave (
\nu=0
) and the convex-strongly concave (
\nu>0
) setting.

Assumption 1
.

We assume that the step sizes
\tau_{k}
,
\sigma_{k}
and the momentum parameter
\theta_{k}
satisfy

\tau_{k+1}\geq\frac{\tau_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sigma_{k+1}\geq\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}(1+\nu\sigma_{k})}\quad\text{for all }k\geq 0.

(16)

Furthermore we assume that there exist
\delta>0
and
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
such that

\frac{1-\delta}{\tau_{k}}\geq\frac{L_{yx}}{\alpha_{k+1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1-\delta}{\sigma_{k}}\geq L_{yx}\alpha_{k}\theta_{k}+L_{yy}(1+\theta_{k})\quad\text{for all }k\geq 0,

(17)

where
\theta_{0}:=1
.

3.1
Preliminary considerations

In this subsection we will make some preliminary considerations that will play an important role when proving the convergence properties of the numerical scheme given by (
1.2
)-(
8
). For all
k\geq 0
we will use the notations

q_{k}:=\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})-\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\ \mbox{and}\ s_{k}:=\theta_{k}q_{k}+\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k},y_{k}).

(18)

We take an arbitrary
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
and let
k\geq 0
be fixed.
From (
1.2
) we derive

0\in\partial g(y_{k+1})+\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}(y_{k+1}-y_{k})-s_{k},

(19)

and, as
g
is convex with modulus
\nu
, this implies

\begin{split}g(y)&\geq g(y_{k+1})+\left\langle s_{k},y-y_{k+1}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}\left\langle y_{k}-y_{k+1},y-y_{k+1}\right\rangle+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&=g(y_{k+1})+\left\langle s_{k},y-y_{k+1}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{k}}\left(\left\lVert y_{k}-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert y-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}.\end{split}

(20)

From (
8
) we get

0\in\partial[\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y_{k+1})](x_{k+1})+\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}(x_{k+1}-x_{k}),

(21)

hence the convexity of
\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y)
for
y\in\dom g
yields

\begin{split}\Phi(x,y_{k+1})&\geq\Phi(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})+\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\left\langle x_{k}-x_{k+1},x-x_{k+1}\right\rangle\\
&=\Phi(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})+\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\left(\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert x-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert x-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right).\end{split}

(22)

Combining (
20
) and (
22
) we obtain

\begin{split}\Psi(x_{k+1},y)-\Psi(x,y_{k+1})&=\Phi(x_{k+1},y)-g(y)-\Phi(x,y_{k+1})+g(y_{k+1})\\
&\leq\Phi(x_{k+1},y)-\Phi(x,y_{k+1})+\left\langle s_{k},y_{k+1}-y\right\rangle-\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{k}}\left(\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\left\lVert y_{k}-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert y-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\\
&\leq\Phi(x_{k+1},y)-\Phi(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})+\left\langle s_{k},y_{k+1}-y\right\rangle-\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\left(\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert x-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert x-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{k}}\left(\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\left\lVert y_{k}-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert y-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right),\end{split}

(23)

which, together with the concavity of
\Phi
in the second variable and (
18
), gives

\begin{split}\Psi(x_{k+1},y)-\Psi(x,y_{k+1})\leq&\ \theta_{k}\left\langle q_{k},y_{k+1}-y\right\rangle-\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&-\left\langle\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k+1},y_{k+1}),y_{k+1}-y\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k},y_{k}),y_{k+1}-y\right\rangle\\
&+\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\left(\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert x-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert x-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{k}}\left(\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\left\lVert y_{k}-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert y-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\\
=&\ \scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\left\langle q_{k+1},y_{k+1}-y\right\rangle+\theta_{k}\left\langle q_{k},y_{k}-y\right\rangle-\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&+\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\left(\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert x-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert x-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{k}}\left(\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\left\lVert y_{k}-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert y-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\\
&+\theta_{k}\left\langle q_{k},y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(24)

By using (
2
) we can evaluate the last term in the above expression as follows

\begin{split}|\left\langle q_{k},y-y_{k}\right\rangle|&\leq\left\lVert q_{k}\right\rVert\,\left\lVert y-y_{k}\right\rVert\leq\left(L_{yx}\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rVert+L_{yy}\left\lVert y_{k}-y_{k-1}\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert y-y_{k}\right\rVert\\
&\leq\frac{L_{yx}}{2}\left(\alpha_{k}\left\lVert y-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}}\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rVert^{2}\right)+\frac{L_{yy}}{2}\left(\left\lVert y-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert y_{k}-y_{k-1}\right\rVert^{2}\right),\end{split}

(25)

with
\alpha_{k}>0
chosen such that (
17
) holds.

Writing (
25
) for
y:=y_{k+1}
and combining the resulting inequality with (
24
) we derive

\Psi(x_{k+1},y)-\Psi(x,y_{k+1})\leq a_{k}(x,y)-b_{k+1}(x,y)-c_{k},

(26)

where

\begin{split}a_{k}(x,y)&:=\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\left\lVert x-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{k}}\left\lVert y-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\theta_{k}\left\langle q_{k},y_{k}-y\right\rangle+\theta_{k}\frac{L_{yx}}{2\alpha_{k}}\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\theta_{k}\frac{L_{yy}}{2}\left\lVert y_{k}-y_{k-1}\right\rVert^{2},\end{split}

(27)

\begin{split}b_{k+1}(x,y)&:=\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\left\lVert x-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}+\nu\right)\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\langle q_{k+1},y_{k+1}-y\right\rangle\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{L_{yx}}{2\alpha_{k+1}}\left\lVert x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L_{yy}}{2}\left\lVert y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2},\end{split}

(28)

and

\begin{split}c_{k}&:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}-\frac{L_{yx}}{\alpha_{k+1}}\right)\left\lVert x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}-L_{yy}-\theta_{k}(L_{yx}\alpha_{k}+L_{yy})\right)\left\lVert y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}.\end{split}

(29)

Now, let us define for all
k\geq 0

t_{k}:=\frac{\theta_{0}}{\theta_{0}\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{k}}

(30)

and notice that

\frac{t_{k}}{t_{k+1}}=\theta_{k+1}.

(31)

Relation (
16

1

\frac{t_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\geq\frac{t_{k+1}}{\tau_{k+1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad t_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}+\nu\right)\geq\frac{t_{k+1}}{\sigma_{k+1}},

(32)

which will be used in telescoping arguments in the following.

Let
K\geq 1
and denote

T_{K}:=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t_{k},\hskip 14.22636pt\bar{x}_{K}:=\frac{1}{T_{K}}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t_{k}x_{k+1},\hskip 14.22636pt\bar{y}_{K}:=\frac{1}{T_{K}}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t_{k}y_{k+1}.

(33)

Multiplying both sides of (

) by
t_{k}>0
as defined in (
30
), followed by summing up the inequalities for
k=0,\ldots,K-1
gives

\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t_{k}\left(\Psi(x_{k+1},y)-\Psi(x,y_{k+1})\right)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t_{k}\left(a_{k}(x,y)-b_{k+1}(x,y)-c_{k}\right).

(34)

By Jensen’s inequality, as
\Psi(\,\cdot\,,y)-\Psi(x,\,\cdot\,)
is a convex function, we obtain

T_{K}\left(\Psi(\bar{x}_{K},y)-\Psi(x,\bar{y}_{K})\right)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t_{k}\left(\Psi(x_{k+1},y)-\Psi(x,y_{k+1})\right),

(35)

and thus

T_{K}\left(\Psi(\bar{x}_{K},y)-\Psi(x,\bar{y}_{K})\right)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t_{k}\left(a_{k}(x,y)-b_{k+1}(x,y)-c_{k}\right).

(36)

Furthermore, using (
32
), we get for all
k\geq 0

\begin{split}t_{k}b_{k+1}(x,y)&=\frac{t_{k}}{2\tau_{k}}\left\lVert x-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{t_{k}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}+\nu\right)\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+t_{k}\left\langle q_{k+1},y_{k+1}-y\right\rangle\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+t_{k}\frac{L_{yx}}{2\alpha_{k+1}}\left\lVert x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+t_{k}\frac{L_{yy}}{2}\left\lVert y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{t_{k+1}}{2\tau_{k+1}}\left\lVert x-x_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{t_{k+1}}{2\sigma_{k+1}}\left\lVert y-y_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}+t_{k+1}\theta_{k+1}\left\langle q_{k+1},y_{k+1}-y\right\rangle\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+t_{k+1}\theta_{k+1}\frac{L_{yx}}{2\alpha_{k+1}}\left\lVert x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+t_{k+1}\theta_{k+1}\frac{L_{yy}}{2}\left\lVert y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&=t_{k+1}a_{k+1}(x,y).\end{split}

(37)

Notice that by (
17
) in Assumption
1
there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
k\geq 0

c_{k}\geq\delta\left(\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\left\lVert x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{k}}\left\lVert y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\geq 0.

(38)

For the following recall that
x_{-1}=x_{0}
and
y_{-1}=y_{0}
, which implies
q_{0}=0
. By using the above two inequalities in (
36
) and writing (
25
) for
k=K
we obtain

\begin{split}\Psi(\bar{x}_{K},y)-\Psi(x,\bar{y}_{K})&\leq\frac{1}{T_{K}}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\left(t_{k}a_{k}(x,y)-t_{k+1}a_{k+1}(x,y)\right)=\frac{1}{T_{K}}\left(t_{0}a_{0}(x,y)-t_{K}a_{K}(x,y)\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{T_{K}}\left(\frac{t_{0}}{2\tau_{0}}\left\lVert x-x_{0}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{t_{0}}{2\sigma_{0}}\left\lVert y-y_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\right)-\frac{t_{K}}{T_{K}}\left(\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\left\lVert x-x_{K}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{K}}\left\lVert y-y_{K}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{t_{K}\theta_{K}}{T_{K}}\left(\left\langle q_{K},y_{K}-y\right\rangle+\frac{L_{yx}}{2\alpha_{K}}\left\lVert x_{K}-x_{K-1}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L_{yy}}{2}\left\lVert y_{K}-y_{K-1}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{T_{K}}\left(\frac{t_{0}}{2\tau_{0}}\left\lVert x-x_{0}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{t_{0}}{2\sigma_{0}}\left\lVert y-y_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{t_{K}}{T_{K}}\left(\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\left\lVert x-x_{K}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}}-\theta_{K}(L_{yx}\alpha_{K}+L_{yy})\right)\left\lVert y-y_{K}\right\rVert^{2}\right).\end{split}

(39)

By definition we have
t_{0}=1
and by (

) that the last term of the above inequality is nonpositive, hence the following estimate for the minimax gap function evaluated at the ergodic sequences holds

\Psi(\bar{x}_{K},y)-\Psi(x,\bar{y}_{K})\leq\frac{1}{T_{K}}\left(\frac{1}{2\tau_{0}}\left\lVert x-x_{0}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{0}}\left\lVert y-y_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\ \forall K\geq 1.

(40)

With these considerations at hand – in specific we want to point out (

), (

) and (

) – we will be able to obtain convergence statements for the two settings
\nu=0
and

3.2
Fulfilment of step size assumptions

In this subsection we will investigate a particular choice of parameters to fulfil Assumption
1
which is suitable for both cases of
\nu=0
and
\nu>0
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\nu\geq 0
,
c_{\alpha}>L_{yx}\geq 0
,
\theta_{0}=1
and
\tau_{0},\,\sigma_{0}>0
such that

\left(c_{\alpha}L_{yx}\tau_{0}+2L_{yy}\right)\sigma_{0}<1.

(41)

We define

\theta_{k+1}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\nu\sigma_{k}}},\hskip 14.22636pt\tau_{k+1}:=\frac{\tau_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}},\hskip 14.22636pt\sigma_{k+1}:=\theta_{k+1}\sigma_{k}\quad\text{for all }k\geq 0.

(42)

Then the sequence
(\tau_{k})_{k\geq 0}
,
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(\theta_{k})_{k\geq 0}
fulfil (
16
) in Assumption
1
with equality and (
17
) for

\alpha_{k}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c_{\alpha}\tau_{0}&\text{if }k=0,\\
c_{\alpha}\tau_{k-1}&\text{if }k\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

(43)

and

\delta:=\min\left\{1-\frac{L_{yx}}{c_{\alpha}},\,1-\left(c_{\alpha}L_{yx}\tau_{0}+2L_{yy}\right)\sigma_{0}\right\}>0.

(44)

Furthermore, for
(t_{k})_{k\geq 0}
defined as in (
30
) we have

t_{k}=\frac{\theta_{0}}{\theta_{0}\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{k}}=\frac{\tau_{k}}{\tau_{0}}\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(45)

Proof.

First, we show that the particular choice (
42
) fulfils (
16
) in Assumption
1
with equality.
We see that for all
k\geq 0

\tau_{k+1}=\frac{\tau_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}},

(46)

as well as

\sigma_{k+1}=\theta_{k+1}\sigma_{k}=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}\frac{1}{\theta_{k+1}^{2}}}=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}(1+\nu\sigma_{k})},

(47)

follow straight forward by definition.

Next, we show that (
17
) in Assumption
1
holds for
\delta
defined in (
44
)
with the choices (
42
) and (
43
).
The first inequality of (
17
) is equivalent to

1-\delta\geq\frac{L_{yx}}{\alpha_{k+1}}\tau_{k}=\frac{L_{yx}}{c_{\alpha}}\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(48)

which clearly is fulfilled as

\delta\leq 1-\frac{L_{yx}}{c_{\alpha}}.

(49)

On the other hand, the second inequality of (
17
) is equivalent to

1-\delta\geq L_{yx}\alpha_{k}\theta_{k}\sigma_{k}+L_{yy}(1+\theta_{k})\sigma_{k}\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(50)

By definition of the step size parameters (
42
) we have for all
k\geq 0

\tau_{k+1}\sigma_{k+1}=\tau_{0}\sigma_{0},\hskip 14.22636pt\theta_{k+1}\leq 1=\theta_{0},\hskip 14.22636pt\sigma_{k+1}\leq\sigma_{0},\hskip 14.22636pt\theta_{k+1}\tau_{k+1}=\tau_{k},

(51)

and thus

\begin{split}1-\delta&\geq L_{yx}\alpha_{0}\theta_{0}\sigma_{0}+L_{yy}(1+\theta_{0})\sigma_{0}=c_{\alpha}L_{yx}\tau_{0}\sigma_{0}+2L_{yy}\sigma_{0}\geq c_{\alpha}L_{yx}\theta_{k+1}^{2}\tau_{k+1}\sigma_{k+1}+L_{yy}(1+\theta_{k+1})\sigma_{k+1}\\
&=L_{yx}c_{\alpha}\tau_{k}\theta_{k+1}\sigma_{k+1}+L_{yy}(1+\theta_{k+1})\sigma_{k+1}=L_{yx}\alpha_{k+1}\theta_{k+1}\sigma_{k+1}+L_{yy}(1+\theta_{k+1})\sigma_{k+1}.\end{split}

(52)

This chain of inequalities holds since

\delta\leq 1-\left(c_{\alpha}L_{yx}\tau_{0}+2L_{yy}\right)\sigma_{0}.

(53)

Finally, using the definition of
t_{k}
and (
42
) we conclude that for all
k\geq 0

t_{k}=\frac{\theta_{0}}{\theta_{0}\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{k}}=\frac{\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau_{0}}}{\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau_{0}}\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau_{1}}\cdots\frac{\tau_{k-1}}{\tau_{k}}}=\frac{\tau_{k}}{\tau_{0}}.

(54)

∎

Remark 6
.

The choice
L_{yy}=0
in (
2
) which was considered in
[
11
]
in the convex-strongly concave setting corresponds to the case when the coupling function
\Phi
is linear in
y
. We will prove convergence also for
L_{yy}
positive, which makes our algorithm applicable to a much wider range of problems, as we will see in the section with the numerical experiments.

When the coupling function
\Phi:{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
is bilinear, that is
\Phi(x,y)=\left\langle y,Ax\right\rangle
for some nonzero continuous linear operator
A:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{G}}
then we are in the setting of
[
4
]
. In this situation one can choose
L_{yy}=0
and
L_{yx}=\|A\|
, and (
44
) yields

\delta=\min\left\{1-\frac{\|A\|}{c_{\alpha}},\,1-c_{\alpha}\|A\|\tau_{0}\sigma_{0}\right\},

(55)

with
c_{\alpha}>\|A\|
.
To guarantee
\delta>0
we fix
0<\varepsilon<1
and set

c_{\alpha}=(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\|A\|.

(56)

Hence, we need to satisfy

\tau_{0}\sigma_{0}\|A\|^{2}<1-\varepsilon,

(57)

which heavily resembles the step size condition of
[
4
, Algorithm 2]
. Since
\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma\Phi(\cdot,y)}\left(x\right)=x-\gamma A^{*}y
for all
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
and all
\gamma>0
, our OGAProx scheme becomes the primal-dual algorithm PDHG from
[
4
]
.

3.3
Convergence results

In this subsection we combine the preliminary considerations with the choice of parameters (
42
) from Proposition
5
.

We will start with the case
\nu=0
and constant step sizes, which gives weak convergence of the iterates to a saddle point
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
and convergence of the minimax gap evaluated at the ergodic iterates to zero like
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{K})
. Afterwards we will consider the case
\nu>0
, which leads to an accelerated version of the algorithm with improved convergence results. In this setting we obtain convergence of
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
to
y^{\ast}
like
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{K})
and convergence of the minimax gap evaluated at the ergodic iterates to zero like
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{K^{2}})
.

3.3.1
Convex-concave setting

For the following we assume that the function
g
is convex with modulus
\nu=0
, meaning it is merely convex. Using the results of the previous subsection we will show that with the choice (
42
) all the parameters are constant.

Proposition 7
.

Let
c_{\alpha}>L_{yx}\geq 0
and
\tau,\,\sigma>0
such that

\left(c_{\alpha}L_{yx}\tau+2L_{yy}\right)\sigma<1.

(58)

If
\nu=0
, then the sequences
(\tau_{k})_{k\geq 0}
,
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(\theta_{k})_{k\geq 0}

\tau_{k}=\tau_{0}:=\tau,\hskip 14.22636pt\sigma_{k}=\sigma_{0}:=\sigma,\hskip 14.22636pt\theta_{k}=\theta_{0}=1\quad\text{for all }k\geq 0.

(59)

Proof.

As
\nu=0
, (
42
) gives for all

\theta_{k+1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\nu\sigma_{k}}}=1,\hskip 14.22636pt\tau_{k+1}=\frac{\tau_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}=\tau_{0},\hskip 14.22636pt\sigma_{k+1}=\theta_{k+1}\sigma_{k}=\sigma_{0}.

(60)

∎

Next we will state and prove the convergence results in the convex-concave case.

Theorem 8
.

Let
c_{\alpha}>L_{yx}\geq 0
and
\tau,\,\sigma>0
such that

\left(c_{\alpha}L_{yx}\tau+2L_{yy}\right)\sigma<1.

Then the sequence
(x_{k},y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by OGAProx with the choice of constant parameters as in Proposition
7

\tau_{k}=\tau_{0}:=\tau,\hskip 14.22636pt\sigma_{k}=\sigma_{0}:=\sigma,\hskip 14.22636pt\theta_{k}=\theta_{0}=1\quad\text{for all }k\geq 0,

(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}

1

(64)

Proof.

First we will show weak convergence of the sequence of iterates
(x_{k},y_{k})_{k\geq 0}

(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
of (
1
). For this we will use the Opial Lemma (see Lemma

Let

and

since

\begin{split}a_{k}(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})&=\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\lVert x^{\ast}-x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\lVert y^{\ast}-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\langle q_{k},y_{k}-y^{\ast}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{yx}}{2\alpha}\left\lVert x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L_{yy}}{2}\left\lVert y_{k}-y_{k-1}\right\rVert^{2}\\

) and (

Next we will show that all weak cluster points of

1

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{1}{\tau}(x_{k_{n}}-x_{k_{n}+1}),\frac{1}{\sigma}(y_{k_{n}}-y_{k_{n}+1})+q_{k_{n}}-q_{k_{n}+1}\right)\\

Proposition 10

(91)

(95)

\begin{split}T_{K}\theta^{K}\big(\Psi(\bar{x}_{K},y)-\Psi(x,\bar{y}_{K})\big)+&\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\lVert x-x_{K}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tilde{\sigma}}\left\lVert y-y_{K}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\theta^{K}\left(\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\lVert x-x_{0}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\lVert y-y_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\right),\end{split}

(119)

which we will use to obtain our convergence results in the following.

4.2
Fulfilment of step size assumptions

In this subsection we will investigate a particular choice of parameters
\tau
,
\sigma
and
\theta
such that Assumption
2
holds.

Proposition 12
.

For
\alpha>0
define

\tilde{\theta}:=\max\left\{\frac{L_{yx}}{\alpha\mu+L_{yx}},\,\frac{\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy}}{\nu+\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy}}\right\}.

(120)

Let
\theta>0
such that

0\leq\tilde{\theta}<\theta<1,

(121)

and set

\tau=\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sigma=\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}.

(122)

Then
\tau
,
\sigma
and
\theta
fulfil Assumption
2
.

Proof.

If
L_{yx}=L_{yy}=0
, then the conclusion follows immediately. Assume that
L_{yx}+L_{yy}>0
. It is easy to verify that definition (
120
) yields

0<\tilde{\theta}<1

(123)

and that (
122
) is equivalent to (
107
) where (
108
) is ensured by (
121
). Furthermore, plugging the specific form of the step sizes (
122
) into (
109
) we obtain for the first inequality of (
109
)

\frac{L_{yx}}{\alpha}\leq\frac{\mu\theta}{1-\theta},

(124)

which is equivalent to

\theta\geq\frac{L_{yx}}{\alpha\mu+L_{yx}}.

(125)

Note that by (
121
) we have

0\leq\frac{L_{yx}}{\alpha\mu+L_{yx}}\leq\tilde{\theta}<\theta<1.

(126)

Similarly, the second inequality of (
109
) is equivalent to the following quadratic inequality

\theta^{2}-\frac{\alpha L_{yx}-\nu}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}\theta-\frac{L_{yy}}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}\geq 0.

(127)

The non negative solution of the associated quadratic equation reads

\rho:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha L_{yx}-\nu}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{\alpha L_{yx}-\nu}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}\right)^{2}+\frac{4L_{yy}}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}}\right)\geq 0.

(128)

Since

0\leq\rho<\frac{\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy}}{\nu+\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy}}\leq\tilde{\theta}<\theta<1,

(129)

the second inequality of (
109
) is also fulfilled. In order to see that

\rho<\frac{\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy}}{\nu+\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy}},

we notice that this inequality is equivalent to

\left(\frac{\alpha L_{yx}-\nu}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}\right)^{2}+\frac{4L_{yy}}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}<\frac{\big(\nu^{2}+2\nu L_{yy}+(\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy})^{2}\big)^{2}}{(\nu+\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy})^{2}(\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy})^{2}},

(130)

which holds if and only if

\displaystyle\big((\alpha L_{yx}-\nu)^{2}+4L_{yy}(\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy})\big)(\nu+\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy})^{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\ (\nu^{2}+2\nu L_{yy})^{2}+2(\nu^{2}+2\nu L_{yy})(\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy})^{2}+(\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy})^{4}

or, equivalently,

0<4\nu^{2}(\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy})^{2}.

For the remaining condition (
110
) to hold we need to ensure

\theta>\frac{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}-\nu}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}.

(131)

For this we observe that

\begin{split}\rho&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha L_{yx}-\nu}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy}-\nu}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}\right)^{2}}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\alpha L_{yx}-\nu+\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy}-\nu}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}=\frac{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}-\nu}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}.\end{split}

(132)

In conclusion, we obtain the following chain of inequalities

\frac{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}-\nu}{\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy}}\leq\rho<\frac{\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy}}{\nu+\alpha L_{yx}+2L_{yy}}\leq\tilde{\theta}<\theta<1,

(133)

which is satisfied by (
121
).
∎

4.3
Convergence results

Now we can combine the previous results and prove the convergence statements in the strongly convex-strongly concave setting.

Theorem 13
.

Let
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
be a saddle point of (
1

(x_{k},y_{k})_{k\geq 0}

(134)

(135)

\alpha>0
, we denote for
K\geq 1

(136)

for which the following holds

0\leq\theta\big(\Psi(\bar{x}_{K},y^{\ast})-\Psi(x^{\ast},\bar{y}_{K})\big)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\lVert x^{\ast}-x_{K}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tilde{\sigma}}\left\lVert y^{\ast}-y_{K}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\theta^{K}\left(\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\lVert x^{\ast}-x_{0}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\lVert y^{\ast}-y_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\right),

where
\tilde{\sigma}:=\frac{\sigma}{1-\theta\sigma(\alpha L_{yx}+L_{yy})}
.

Proof.

Let
K\geq 1
and
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
be an arbitrary but fixed saddle point of (

(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
we get

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\ T_{K}\theta^{K}\big(\Psi(\bar{x}_{K},y^{\ast})-\Psi(x^{\ast},\bar{y}_{K})\big)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\lVert x^{\ast}-x_{K}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tilde{\sigma}}\left\lVert y^{\ast}-y_{K}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \theta^{K}\left(\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\lVert x^{\ast}-x_{0}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\lVert y^{\ast}-y_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\right).

5
Numerical experiments

In this section we will treat three numerical applications of our method. The first one is of rather simple structure and has the purpose to highlight the convergence rates we obtained in the previous sections. The second one concerns multi kernel support vector machines to validate OGAProx on a more relevant application in practice, even though there are no theoretical guarantees for the “metric” reported there. The third numerical application addresses a classification problem incorporating minimax group fairness, which traces back to the solving of a minimax problem with nonsmooth coupling function.

5.1
Nonsmooth-linear problem

The first application we treat is to showcase the convergence rates we obtained in the previous sections and make a simple proof of concept. We look at the following nonsmooth-linear saddle point problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Psi(x,y):=\left\langle[x]_{+},Ay\right\rangle-\left(\delta_{C}(y)+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y\right\rVert^{2}\right),

(138)

with
\nu\geq 0
and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
,
[\,\cdot\,]_{+}
being the component-wise positive part,

[x]_{+}={\big(\max\{0,x_{i}\}\big)}_{i=1}^{d},

(139)

and
C
being the following convex polytope

C:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ Ay\geqq 0\}.

(140)

For
u=(u_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
,
v=(v_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the relation
u\geqq v
denotes component-wise inequalities, namely,

u\geqq v\>\Leftrightarrow\>u_{i}\geq v_{i}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d.

(141)

Then
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with

g(y):=\delta_{C}(y)+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y\right\rVert^{2}

(142)

is proper, lower semicontinuous and convex with modulus
\nu\geq 0
and
\dom g=C
. Moreover,
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\Phi(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\max\{0,x_{i}\}(Ay)_{i}

(143)

has full domain, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have that
\Phi(x,\,\cdot\,)
is linear and for all
y\in\dom g=C
the function
\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y)
is convex and continuous.

Furthermore, we obtain for all
(x,y),\,(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\dom g

\left\lVert\nabla_{y}\Phi(x,y)-\nabla_{y}\Phi(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right\rVert=\left\lVert A^{T}\left([x]_{+}-[x^{\prime}]_{+}\right)\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert x-x^{\prime}\right\rVert,

(144)

hence (
2
) holds with
L_{yx}=\left\lVert A\right\rVert
and
L_{yy}=0
.

The algorithm (
1.2
)-(
8
) iterates for
k\geq 0

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}v_{k}&=&y_{k}+\sigma_{k}\left[(1+\theta_{k})\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})-\theta_{k}\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\right]=y_{k}+\sigma_{k}A^{T}\big((1+\theta_{k})[x_{k}]_{+}-\theta_{k}[x_{k-1}]_{+}\big),\\
y_{k+1}&=&\operatorname{prox}_{\sigma_{k}g}\left(v_{k}\right)=P_{C}\left(\frac{1}{1+\nu\sigma_{k}}v_{k}\right),\\
x_{k+1}&=&\operatorname{prox}_{\tau_{k}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y_{k+1})}\left(x_{k}\right),\end{array}\right.

where the calculation of the orthogonal projection on the set
C
is a simple quadratic program and

\operatorname{prox}_{\tau\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y)}\left(x\right)=\left(\operatorname{prox}_{\tau(Ay)_{i}\max\{0,\,\cdot\,\}}\left(x_{i}\right)\right)_{i=1}^{d},

(145)

where, for
i=1,...,d
,

\operatorname{prox}_{\tau(Ay)_{i}\max\{0,\,\cdot\,\}}\left(x_{i}\right)=\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{if }x_{i}\leq 0,\\
0&\text{if }0<x_{i}\leq\tau(Ay)_{i},\\
x_{i}-\tau(Ay)_{i}&\text{if }x_{i}>\tau(Ay)_{i}.\end{cases}

(146)

By writing the first order optimality conditions and using Lagrange duality we obtain the following characterisation.

\begin{split}(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\text{ is a saddle point of\penalty\ \eqref{nslSP}}&\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0\in\partial\left(\left\langle[\,\cdot\,]_{+},Ay^{\ast}\right\rangle-\delta_{C}(y^{\ast})-\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y^{\ast}\right\rVert^{2}\right)(x^{\ast})\\

A^{T}[x^{\ast}]_{+}-\nu y^{\ast}\in N_{C}(y^{\ast})\end{array}\right.\\
&\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\forall i=1,\ldots,d:\big((Ay^{\ast})_{i}>0\text{ and }x^{\ast}_{i}\leq 0\big)\text{ or }\\
\hskip 14.22636pt\big((Ay^{\ast})_{i}=0\text{ and }x^{\ast}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\big)\\

&\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\forall i=1,\ldots,d:\big((Ay^{\ast})_{i}>0\text{ and }x^{\ast}_{i}\leq 0\big)\text{ or }\\
\hskip 14.22636pt\big((Ay^{\ast})_{i}=0\text{ and }x^{\ast}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\big)\\

(147)

This means, that for

(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\text{ is a saddle point of\penalty\ \eqref{nslSP}}\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\forall i=1,\ldots,d:\big((Ay^{\ast})_{i}>0\text{ and }x^{\ast}_{i}\leq 0\big)\text{ or }\\
\hskip 14.22636pt\big((Ay^{\ast})_{i}=0\text{ and }x^{\ast}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\big)\\
0\in A^{T}\left([x^{\ast}]_{+}+\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\right)\end{array},\right.

(148)

whereas for
\nu>0

0\in A^{T}\left([x^{\ast}]_{+}+\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\right)\end{array}.\right.

(149)

If

has full row rank the inclusion

(151)

5.2
Multi kernel support vector machine

The second application to test our method in practice is to learn a combined kernel matrix for a multi kernel
support vector machine
(SVM). We have a set of labelled training data

S_{n}=\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},b_{n})\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\{-1,1\},

(152)

where we call
b=(b_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, and a set of unlabelled test data

T_{l}=\{a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n+l}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(153)

We consider embeddings of the data according to a kernel function
\kappa:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
with the corresponding symmetric and positive semidefinite kernel matrix

\mathcal{K}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{K}^{tr}&\mathcal{K}^{tr,t}\\
\mathcal{K}^{t,tr}&\mathcal{K}^{t}\end{array}\right),

(154)

where
\mathcal{K}_{ij}=\kappa(a_{i},a_{j})
for
i,\,j=1,\ldots,n,n+1,\ldots,n+l
.

In the following
e
is a vector of appropriate size consisting of ones. According to
[
13
]
the problem of interest is

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{K}\in\mathbb{K}\\
\tr(\mathcal{K})=c\end{subarray}}\hskip 5.69054pt\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqq\alpha\leqq C\\
\left\langle\alpha,b\right\rangle=0\end{subarray}}\,\alpha^{T}e-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{T}G(\mathcal{K}^{tr})\alpha-\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert^{2}_{2},

(155)

where
\mathbb{K}
is the model class of kernel matrices,
c\in(0,+\infty)
,
C\in(0,+\infty]
and
\nu\in[0,+\infty)
are model parameters and we define
G(\mathcal{K}^{tr}):=\diag(b)\mathcal{K}^{tr}\diag(b)
.

The set
\mathbb{K}
is restricted to be the set of positive semidefinite matrices that can be written as a non negative linear combination of kernel matrices
\mathcal{K}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{K}_{d}
, i.e.,

\mathbb{K}=\left\{\mathcal{K}\in S^{m}_{+}\ \bigg|\ \mathcal{K}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\eta_{i}\mathcal{K}_{i},\,\eta_{i}\geq 0\text{ for }i=1,...,d\right\}.

(156)

With this choice (
155

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left\langle\eta,r\right\rangle=c\\
\eta\geqq 0\end{subarray}}\hskip 5.69054pt\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqq\alpha\leqq C\\
\left\langle\alpha,b\right\rangle=0\end{subarray}}\,\alpha^{T}e-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\eta_{i}\alpha^{T}G(\mathcal{K}^{tr}_{i})\alpha-\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert^{2},

(157)

where
\eta=(\eta_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
and

with

for
i=1,...,d
. Assume
(\eta^{\ast},\alpha^{\ast})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

157

\mathcal{K}^{\ast}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\eta^{\ast}_{j}\mathcal{K}_{j}.

(158)

Following the considerations of
[
11

we compute for

with

\mathcal{L}(a_{k})=\sgn\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\alpha^{\ast}_{i}\mathcal{K}^{\ast}_{ik}+\gamma\right)=\sgn\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{i}\alpha^{\ast}_{i}\eta^{\ast}_{j}\left(\mathcal{K}_{j}\right)_{ik}+\gamma\right),

(159)

with

\gamma=b_{j_{0}}(1-\nu\alpha^{\ast}_{j_{0}})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\alpha^{\ast}_{i}\mathcal{K}^{\ast}_{ij_{0}}=b_{j_{0}}(1-\nu\alpha^{\ast}_{j_{0}})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{i}\alpha^{\ast}_{i}\eta^{\ast}_{j}\left(\mathcal{K}_{j}\right)_{ij_{0}},

(160)

for some

.

After writing

for

and augmenting the objective with an additional (strongly) convex penalisation term, we obtain

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,\delta_{\Delta}(x)+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}y^{T}M_{i}y+y^{T}e-\left(\delta_{Y}(y)+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y\right\rVert^{2}\right),

(161)

where
\mu\geq 0
and
M_{i}:=\frac{c}{r_{i}}G(\mathcal{K}_{i}^{tr})
for
i=1,...,d
,

\Delta:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ |\ x\geqq 0,\,\left\langle x,e\right\rangle=1\}

(162)

is the
m
-dimensional unit simplex and

Y:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ 0\leqq y\leqq C,\,\left\langle y,b\right\rangle=0\}

(163)

is the intersection of a box and a hyperplane.

In the notation of (
1

\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

\Phi(x,y)=\delta_{\Delta}(x)+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}y^{T}M_{i}y+y^{T}e,

(164)

and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
given by

g(y)=\delta_{Y}(y)+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\lVert y\right\rVert^{2}.

(165)

We see that
\Phi
and

satisfy the assumptions considered for problem (
1
).

The algorithm (
1.2
)-(
8
) iterates as follows for
k\geq 0

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}v_{k}&=&y_{k}+\sigma_{k}\left[(1+\theta_{k})\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})-\theta_{k}\nabla_{y}\Phi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\right],\\

(166)

where

\nabla_{y}\Phi(x,y)=\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}(M_{i}+M_{i}^{T})\right)y+e=\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}M_{i}\right)y+e\ \mbox{ for }(x,y)\in\Delta\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

(167)

and

\xi^{y}:=\left(\frac{1}{2}y^{T}M_{i}y\right)_{i=1}^{d}.

(168)

To determine the correct step sizes and momentum parameter, we need to find Lipschitz constants for
\nabla_{y}\Phi
, i.e.,
L_{yx}
,
L_{yy}\geq 0
such that (
2
) holds. Recall, that we require for all
(x,y),\,(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)\times\dom g

\left\lVert\nabla_{y}\Phi(x,y)-\nabla_{y}\Phi(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right\rVert\leq L_{yx}\left\lVert x-x^{\prime}\right\rVert+L_{yy}\left\lVert y-y^{\prime}\right\rVert,

(169)

with
\pr_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\dom\Phi)=\Delta
and
\dom g=Y
.

Let
(x,y),\,(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\Delta\times Y
. Then

\begin{split}\left\lVert\nabla_{y}\Phi(x,y)-\nabla_{y}\Phi(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right\rVert&=\left\lVert-\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}M_{i}y+e+\sum_{i=1}^{d}x^{\prime}_{i}M_{i}y^{\prime}-e\right\rVert\\
&=\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}M_{i}y^{\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}M_{i}y+\sum_{i=1}^{d}x^{\prime}_{i}M_{i}y^{\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}M_{i}y^{\prime}\right\rVert\\
&\leq\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}M_{i}(y-y^{\prime})\right\rVert+\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i})M_{i}y^{\prime}\right\rVert\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lvert x_{i}\rvert\left\lVert M_{i}\right\rVert\left\lVert y-y^{\prime}\right\rVert+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lvert x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i}\rvert\left\lVert M_{i}\right\rVert\left\lVert y^{\prime}\right\rVert\\
&\leq\left(\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{1}\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\left\lVert M_{i}\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert y-y^{\prime}\right\rVert+\left(\left\lVert y^{\prime}\right\rVert\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\left\lVert M_{i}\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert x-x^{\prime}\right\rVert_{1}\\
&\leq\left(\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{1}\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\left\lVert M_{i}\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert y-y^{\prime}\right\rVert+\left(\left\lVert y^{\prime}\right\rVert\sqrt{d}\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\left\lVert M_{i}\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert x-x^{\prime}\right\rVert.\end{split}

(170)

As
x\in\Delta
, we have
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{1}=1
and since
y^{\prime}\in Y
we get
\left\lVert y^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq C\sqrt{n}
. Thus we obtain

\left\lVert\nabla_{y}\Phi(x,y)-\nabla_{y}\Phi(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right\rVert\leq L_{yx}\left\lVert x-x^{\prime}\right\rVert+L_{yy}\left\lVert y-y^{\prime}\right\rVert,

(171)

with

L_{yx}=C\sqrt{dn}\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\left\lVert M_{i}\right\rVert,\hskip 14.22636ptL_{yy}=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\left\lVert M_{i}\right\rVert.

(172)

For our experiments we use four different data sets from the “UCI Machine Learning Repository”
[
8
]
: the (original) Wisconsin
breast cancer
dataset
[
16
]
(699 total observations including 16 incomplete examples; 9 features), the Statlog

Furthermore we take
d=3
given kernel functions, namely a polynomial kernel function
k_{1}(a,a^{\prime})=(1+a^{T}a^{\prime})^{2}
of degree 2 for
\mathcal{K}_{1}
, a Gaussian kernel function
k_{2}(a,a^{\prime})=\exp(\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\frac{1}{2}(a-a^{\prime})^{T}(a-a^{\prime})/\frac{1}{10})
for
\mathcal{K}_{2}
and a linear kernel function
k_{3}(a,a^{\prime})=a^{T}a^{\prime}
for
\mathcal{K}_{3}
. The resulting kernel matrices are normalised according to
[
13
, Section 4.8]
, giving

r_{i}=\tr(\mathcal{K}_{i})=n+l.

(173)

The model parameter
c>0
is chosen to be

c=\sum_{i=1}^{d}r_{i}=d(n+l),

(174)

and we set

.

On this application we test the three proposed versions of OGAProx. We refer to the version of OGAProx with constant parameters from Section

as OGAProx-C1, to the one with adaptive parameters from Section

as OGAProx-A and to the one from Section

[
11
]
. In their experiments on multi kernel SVMs they showed superiority of their method compared to
Mirror Prox
by
[

]
in terms of accuracy, runtime and relative error. They also argued that with APD they are able to obtain decent approximations of solutions of (

) by interior point methods such as MOSEK
[

\nu>0

x
and

x_{0}=x_{-1}=\frac{1}{d}e,\hskip 14.22636pty_{0}=y_{-1}=0.

(175)

5.3
Classification incorporating minimax group fairness

We want to classify labelled data
(a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{\pm 1\}
, additionally taking into account so-called
minimax group fairness

[
17
,
7
]
. The data is divided into
m
groups
G_{1},...,G_{m}
, such that for
i\in[m]:=\{1,...,m\}
we have
G_{i}=(a_{i_{j}},b_{i_{j}})_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\subseteq(a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
with
n_{i}:=\left\lvert G_{i}\right\rvert
and
i_{j}\in[n]
for all
i\in[m]
and all
j\in[n_{i}]
.

Fairness
is measured by worst-case outcomes across the considered groups. Hence we consider the following problem,

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max_{i\in[m]}f_{i}(x),

(176)

with

f_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{n_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}L(h_{x}(a_{i_{j}}),b_{i_{j}}),

(177)

where
h_{x}
is a function parametrised by
x
, mapping features to predicted labels, and
L
is a loss function measuring the error between the predicted and true labels.

It is easy to see that (
176
) is equivalent to

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max_{y\in\Delta_{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}f_{i}(x),

(178)

where
\Delta_{m}:=\{(v_{1},...,v_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\ |\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}=1,\;v_{i}\geq 0\text{ for }i=1,...,m\}
denotes the probability simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
We will work with a linear (affine) predictor
h_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

h_{x}(a)=a^{T}x,

(179)

with
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
L:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
being the hinge loss, i.e.,

L(r,s)=\max\{0,1-sr\},

(180)

for
r
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Combining all of the above we get

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Phi(x,y)-g(y),

(181)

with
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\Phi(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\frac{1}{n_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\max\{0,1-b_{i_{j}}a_{i_{j}}^{T}x\},

(182)

and
g:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
given by

g(y)=\delta_{\Delta_{m}}(y).

(183)

The function
g
is proper, lower semicontinuous and convex (with modulus
\nu=0
). Furthermore we observe that
\Phi(\cdot,y):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous for all
y\in\dom g=\Delta_{m}
and for all
x\in\Pr_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\dom\Phi)=\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
\dom\Phi(x,\cdot)=\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\Phi(x,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is concave and Fréchet differentiable. However, note that
\Phi
is not differentiable in its first component.

Moreover the Lipschitz condition on the gradient is fulfilled as well. Indeed, for
(x,y),\,(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Delta_{m}
we have

\left\lVert\nabla_{y}\Phi(x,y)-\nabla_{y}\Phi(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right\rVert\leq L_{yx}\left\lVert x-x^{\prime}\right\rVert+L_{yy}\left\lVert y-y^{\prime}\right\rVert,

(184)

with

L_{yx}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{n_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\left\lVert a_{i_{j}}\right\rVert^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad L_{yy}=0.

(185)

Additionally, with
\tau>0
and
y\in\dom g
, we have for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\operatorname{prox}_{\tau\Phi(\cdot,y)}\left(x\right)=\argmin_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\frac{1}{n_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\max\{0,1-b_{i_{j}}a_{i_{j}}^{T}u\}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u-x\right\rVert^{2}\right\}.\\

(186)

By introducing slack variables for the pointwise maximum, we see that the above minimisation problem is equivalent to the following quadratic program

\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
r_{ij}\in\mathbb{R},\\
i\in[m],\;j\in[n_{i}]\end{subarray}}&\quad\left\{\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}y_{i}\frac{1}{n_{i}}r_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u-x\right\rVert^{2}\right\}.\\
\textrm{s.t.}\quad&r_{ij}\geq 0\quad\forall\,i\in[m],\,\forall\,j\in[n_{i}]\\
&r_{ij}+b_{i_{j}}a_{i_{j}}^{T}u\geq 1\quad\forall\,i\in[m],\,\forall\,j\in[n_{i}]\\
\end{aligned}\end{split}

(187)

For our practical applications we consider the Statlog
heart disease
data set (270 observations; 13 features) from the “UCI Machine Learning Repository”
[
8
]
and consider two different groupings; one consisting of the sex of the patients, while the other one is regarding the patients’ age. For “sex” we have two groups, that is female patients (Group S1) and male patients (Group S2), whereas for “age” we consider three groups, that is patients that are younger than 50 years old (Group A1), patients that are younger than 60 but at least 50 years old (Group A2), and patients that are 60 years of age or older (Group A3). The data set is randomly partitioned into 80 % training data and 20 % test data. The results in Table
4
and Table
5

Every time we report the results obtained by iterates of OGAProx governed by solving the minimax problem (
181
) taking into account the considered groups (“with fairness”), as well as the results obtained by not taking into account minimax group fairness (“without fairness”), i.e., solving the problem for a single extensive group
G_{1}=(a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
with
n_{1}=n
, yielding the minimisation of the average loss over the whole population and leading to an “ordinary” minimisation problem.

The graded Betti numbers of truncation of ideals in polynomial rings
Chwas Ahmed, Ralf Fröberg and Mohammed Rafiq Namiq

Abstract
. Let R = 𝕂 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] , a graded algebra S = R / I S=R/I satisfies N k , p N_{k,p} if I I is generated in degree k k , and the graded minimal resolution is linear the first p p steps, and the k k -index of S S is the largest p p such that S S satisfies N k , p N_{k,p} . Eisenbud and Goto have shown that for any graded ring R / I R/I , then R / I ≥ k R/I_{\geq k} , where I ≥ k = I ∩ M k I_{\geq k}=I\cap M^{k} and M = ( x 1 , … , x n ) M=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}) , has a k k -linear resolution (satisfies N k , p N_{k,p} for all p p ) if k ≫ 0 k\gg 0 . For a squarefree monomial ideal I I , we are here interested in the ideal I k I_{k} which is the squarefree part of I ≥ k I_{\geq k} . The ideal I I is, via Stanley-Reisner correspondence, associated to a simplicial complex Δ I \Delta_{I} . In this case, all Betti numbers of R / I k R/I_{k} for k > min ⁡ { deg ⁡ ( u ) ∣ u ∈ I } k>\min\{\deg(u)\mid u\in I\} , which of course is a much finer invariant than the index, can be determined from the Betti diagram of R / I R/I and the f f -vector of Δ I \Delta_{I} . We compare our results with the corresponding statements for I ≥ k I_{\geq k} . (Here I I is an arbitrary graded ideal.) In this case we show that the Betti numbers of R / I ≥ k R/I_{\geq k} can be determined from the Betti numbers of R / I R/I and the Hilbert series of R / I ≥ k R/I_{\geq k} .

2.
The graded Betti numbers of
I_{k}

In this section, let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal of
R
.
Define
J_{k}=\{u\in M_{k}\mid u\notin I_{k}\}
. Let
e_{u}=\supp(u)
where
\supp(u)=\{x_{i}\mid u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots,x_{n}^{a_{n}},a_{i}\neq 0\}
and
G(I)
be the set of minimal generators of
I
.
The Stanley-Reisner complex of
I
is the simplicial complex consisting of faces which correspond to the squarefree monomials not in
I
, that is

\Delta_{I}=\{e_{u}\subset\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\mid u\text{ is a squarefree monomial not in }I\}.

An element
F
of
\Delta_{I}
is a face of
\Delta_{I}
of dimension
|F|-1
. A maximal face (with respect to inclusion) of
\Delta_{I}
is a facet. Denote the set of the facets of
\Delta_{I}
by
\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I})
. The dimension of
\Delta_{I}
is
\dim\Delta_{I}=\max\{|F|\mid F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I})\}-1
. The pure
k
-skeleton of
\Delta_{I}
is a simplicial complex
\Delta_{I}^{[k]}
whose facets are in
\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I})
with
|F|=k+1
.

Note that
I_{k+1}=\left(ux_{i}\mid\deg(u)=k,u\in I_{k},x_{i}\notin e_{u}\right)+\left(v\mid\deg(v)>k,v\in I_{k}\right).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal of
R
.
Then for
k\geq\min\{\deg(u)\mid u\in I\}
we have

u\in J_{k+1}
if and only if
e_{u}\in\Delta_{I_{k}}^{[k]}
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
u\in J_{k+1}
. Assume that
e_{u}\notin\Delta_{I_{k}}^{[k]}
. Then
u\in I_{k}
and
u
is a squarefree monomial of degree
k+1
. Thus
u\in I_{k+1}
which is a contradiction.

(
\Leftarrow
) We have
u\in G(M_{k+1})
since
e_{u}\in\Delta^{[k]}_{I_{k}}
. We claim
u\notin I_{k+1}
. Suppose to the contrary that
u\in I_{k+1}
. Then there exists
x_{t}\in e_{u}
such that
\hat{u}_{t}\in I_{k}
. Hence
\hat{u}_{t}\notin J_{k}
for some
x_{t}\in e_{u}
, a contradiction. The claim follows.
∎

In the following Proposition we find the facets, i.e. maximal faces, of
\Delta_{I}
, then later use these facets to compute the Betti numbers of
I_{k}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal of
R
. Then for
k\geq\min\{\deg(u)\mid u\in I\}
we have

\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I_{k+1}})=\{e_{u}\mid u\in G(I_{k}),\deg(u)=k\}\cup\{e_{v}\mid e_{v}\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I_{k}}),\deg(v)\geq k\}.

Proof.

Let
P_{k}:=\{e_{u}\mid u\in G(I_{k}),\deg(u)=k\}
and
Q_{k}:=\{e_{v}\mid e_{v}\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I_{k}}),\deg(v)\geq k\}
.
Let
F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I_{k+1}})
. Then
F=e_{u}
for some
u\notin I_{k+1}
. We need to consider the following three cases.

•

The case when
|F|>k
. If
e_{u}
is not a face of
\Delta_{I_{k}}
, then
u\in I_{k}
. But
\deg(u)\geq k+1
so
u\in I_{k+1}
, and this contradicts
e_{u}\in\Delta_{I_{k+1}}
.
It remains to show that
F
is maximal in
\Delta_{I_{k}}
. Suppose
e_{ux}\in\Delta_{I_{k}}
for some
x\notin e_{u}
. Note that
ux\not\in I_{k+1}
, otherwise having
\deg(ux)>k+1
implies
ux\in I_{k}
. Hence
e_{ux}\in\Delta_{I_{k+1}}
, a contradiction. Therefore
F\in Q_{k}
.

•

The case when
|F|=k
. If
u\notin I_{k}
, then
u\in J_{k}
. To obtain a contradiction assume that
e_{ux}\in\Delta_{I_{k}}
for some
x\notin e_{u}
. Then
\deg({ux})=k+1
, and Lemma
2.1
implies
ux\in J_{k+1}
. Hence
e_{ux}\in\Delta_{I_{k+1}}
, again this contradicts the assumption
e_{u}\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I_{k+1}})
. Therefore
e_{u}\in P_{k}
or
e_{u}\in Q_{k}
.

•

The case when
|F|<k
. We have
e_{ux}\notin\Delta_{I_{k+1}}
. Hence
ux\in I_{k+1}
with
\deg({ux})\leq k
which is a contradiction.

Conversely, let
e_{u}\in P_{k}\cup Q_{k}
, we show that
e_{u}\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I_{k+1}})
by considering the following three cases:

•

When
|e_{u}|=k
. If
e_{u}\in P_{k}
, then
ux\in I_{k+1}
for all
x\notin e_{u}
. It follows that
e_{u}\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I_{k+1}})
.
Now if
e_{u}\in Q_{k}
, then
u\not\in I_{k}
. Suppose that
ux\not\in I_{k+1}
for some
x\notin e_{u}
. Then
ux\notin I_{k}
otherwise
ux\in I_{k+1}
. Hence
e_{ux}\in\Delta_{I_{k}}
which is a contradiction. Therefore
ux\in I_{k+1}
for all
x\notin e_{u}
. Hence
e_{u}\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{I_{k+1}})
.

•

If
|e_{u}|=k+1
, then
e_{u}\in\Delta_{I_{k}}
. By Lemma
2.1
we have
u\in J_{k+1}
, hence
e_{u}\in\Delta_{I_{k+1}}
. If
e_{u}
is not a facet of
\Delta_{I_{k+1}}
, then there an
x\notin e_{u}
such that
e_{ux}\in\Delta_{I_{k+1}}
. Then
ux\notin I_{k+1}
, and so
ux\notin I_{k}
. Hence
e_{ux}\in\Delta_{I_{k}}
some
x\notin e_{u}
which is a contradiction.

•

When
|e_{u}|\geq k+2
. If
u\in I_{k+1}
then
u\in I_{k}
, and so
e_{u}\notin\Delta_{I_{k}}
, which is also a contradiction. The face
e_{u}
is maximal in
\Delta_{I_{k+1}}
by the same argument used in the case
|e_{u}|=k+1
.

∎

For a fixed
t
, we shall write
(f_{-1}^{t},f_{0}^{t},\dots,f_{d-1}^{t})
for the
f
-vector of
\Delta_{I_{t}}
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal of
R
and
f(\Delta_{I})=(f_{-1},f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{d-1})
. Then for
k\geq\min\{|e_{u}|\mid u\in I\}
we have

f(\Delta_{I_{k+1}})=\begin{cases}\left(\binom{n}{0},\binom{n}{1},\binom{n}{2},\dots,\binom{n}{k},f_{k},\dots,f_{d-1}\right)\quad&\text{ if }k\leq d,\\
\left(\binom{n}{0},\binom{n}{1},\binom{n}{2},\dots,\binom{n}{k}\right)\quad&\text{ if }d<k<n.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
, we have

f^{k+1}_{k-1}=f^{k}_{k-1}+|\{e_{u}\mid u\in G(I_{k}),\deg(u)=k\}|=|\{e_{u}\mid u\in J_{k}\}|+|\{e_{u}\mid u\in G(I_{k}),\deg(u)=k\}|=\binom{n}{k}
. Then the result follows by induction.
∎

The following Lemma of Hochster provides a very useful explanation of the graded Betti numbers of a Stanley-Reisner ring (see
[
8
, Theorem 5.1]
or
[
4
, Lemma 9]
). Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal of
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. We write

\Delta_{W}
for the simplicial complex on the vertex set
W\subseteq\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
, whose faces are
F\in\Delta_{I}
with
F\subseteq W
.

Lemma 2.4

(
Hochster
)
.

Let
W\subseteq\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and
K_{R(W)}
be the part of the Koszul complex
K_{R}
which is of degree
\delta(W)=(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
, where
d_{i}=1
if
x_{i}\in W
and
d_{i}=0
otherwise. Then
H_{i,\delta(W)}=H_{i}(K_{R(W)})\cong\tilde{H}_{|W|-i-1}\left(\Delta_{W};\mathbb{K}\right)
.

Now we come to our main result. We have a precise formula for the graded Betti numbers of the graded algebra
R/I_{k}
for
k>\min\{\deg(u)\mid u\in I\}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal of
R
. Then for
k>\min\{|e_{u}|\mid u\in I\}
we have

\beta_{i,i+j}(R/I_{k})=\begin{cases}1\quad&\mbox{ if }i=0,j=0,\\
0\quad&\mbox{ if }i\neq 0,0\leq j\leq k-2,\\
\alpha_{i,i+k-1}\quad&\mbox{ if }i\neq 0,j=k-1,\\
\beta_{i,i+j}(R/I)\quad&\mbox{ if }i\neq 0,j\geq k.\end{cases}

where

(2.1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,i+j}=\sum_{r=0}^{i+j}(-1)^{j-r}\binom{n-r}{i+j-r}f_{r-1}^{k}+\sum_{\scriptstyle\ell+m=i+j\atop\scriptstyle\ell<i}(-1)^{\ell-i-1}\beta_{\ell,\ell+m}(R/I).

Proof.

Let the
f
-vector of
\Delta_{I}
be
(f_{-1},f_{0},f_{1}\dots,f_{d-1})
. Then Corollary
2.3
implies that for
0\leq i\leq k-1
,
f^{k}_{i-1}=\binom{n}{i}
. Then
\beta_{i,i+j}=0
for all
0\leq j\leq k-2
except
\beta_{0,0}=1
. Hence by Hochster’s formula, Lemma
2.4
, the only graded Betti numbers that change are

\beta_{i,i+k-1}(R/I_{k})=\sum_{\scriptstyle|W|=k-1,\atop W\subseteq\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\tilde{H}_{k-i-2}\left(\Delta_{W};\mathbb{K}\right)=\alpha_{i,i+k-1}.

where
\Delta_{W}
is the simplicial complex on the vertex set
W
, whose faces are
F\in\Delta_{I_{k}}
with
F\subseteq W
. Thus
\beta_{i,i+j}(R/I_{k})=\beta_{i,i+j}(R/I)
for
j\geq k
. The Hilbert series of
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{I}]
is

H_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{I}]}(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{d}\frac{f_{r-1}t^{r}}{(1-t)^{r}}=\frac{\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\sum_{j}\beta_{i,i+j}t^{i+j}}{(1-t)^{n}}.

We have that

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{d}\frac{f_{r-1}t^{r}}{(1-t)^{r}}\times(1-t)^{n}=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sum_{r=0}^{s}(-1)^{s-r}\binom{n-r}{s-r}f_{r-1}t^{s}.

Then from the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{I}]
we have

\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sum_{r=0}^{s}(-1)^{s-r}\binom{n-r}{s-r}f_{r-1}t^{s}=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\sum_{j}\beta_{i,i+j}t^{i+j}

If
s=i+j
, then

\sum_{r=0}^{s}(-1)^{s-r}\binom{n-r}{s-r}f_{r-1}=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\sum_{i+j=s}\beta_{i,s},\quad s=0,1,\dots,n.

Let
j=k-1
. Since
\beta_{i,i+j}(R/I_{k})\neq 0
whenever
j\geq k-1
, we have

\beta_{i,i+j}(R/I_{k})=\sum_{r=0}^{i+j}(-1)^{j-r}\binom{n-r}{i+j-r}f_{r-1}^{k}+\sum_{\scriptstyle\ell+m=s\atop\scriptstyle\ell<i}(-1)^{\ell-i+1}\beta_{\ell,\ell+m}(R/I).

∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal of
R
. If
I
has a linear resolution, then

has a linear resolution for any positive integer

.

Proof.

Let
I
be generated in degree

. If
k\leq d
, then
I_{k}=I_{d}=I
. For the case

, one can apply Theorem

.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal of
R
and
\reg(I)=d
. Then for any positive integer

\reg(I_{k})=\begin{cases}d\quad\mbox{ if }d\geq k,\\

Proof.

We have
\beta_{i,i+d}(I)\neq 0
for some

. Theorem
2.5
implies that
\reg(I_{k})=d
whenever

and
\reg(I_{k})=k

∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let

be a squarefree monomial ideal of

. Then

if and only if

is the smallest integer such that

has a linear resolution for all

We have
\beta_{i,i+d}(I)\neq 0

. Hence Theorem

implies that

has a

-linear resolution but

\beta_{i,i+d}(I)=\beta_{i,i+d}(I_{d-m})=\beta_{i,i+d}(I_{d})

.

3.
Polarizations

Let
I=(u_{1},\dots,u_{t})
be a monomial ideal of
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with
u_{j}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a_{i,j}}
for
1\leq j\leq t
. For
{1\leq i\leq n}
, let
a_{i}=\max\{a_{i,j}\mid 1\leq j\leq t\}
. The polarization of
I
, denoted by
\mathcal{P}(I)
, is a squarefree monomial ideal of a polynomial ring

S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1,1},x_{1,2},\dots,x_{1,a_{1}},x_{2,1},x_{2,2},\dots,x_{2,a_{2}},\dots,x_{n,1},x_{n,2},\dots,x_{n,a_{n}}]

with minimal generators
\mathcal{P}(u_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{P}(u_{t})
where

\mathcal{P}(u_{j})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{l=1}^{a_{i,j}}x_{i,l},1\leq j\leq t.

In 1982, Fröberg proved the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
I=(u_{1},\dots,u_{t})
be a monomial ideal of
R
such that for each
i
, the variable
x_{i,a_{i}}
appears in at least on of the monomials
\mathcal{P}(u_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{P}(u_{t})
. Then for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1<j\leq a_{i}
,

f_{i,j}=x_{i,1}-x_{i,j}
forms a regular sequence of degree one in
S/\mathcal{P}(I)
. Moreover,
R/I\cong S/(\mathcal{P}(I)+J)
where
J=(f_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,1<j\leq a_{i})
.

For any quotient ring, factoring out with a regular sequence of degree one does not change the Betti numbers. That is, the graded Betti numbers of
R/I
and
S/\mathcal{P}(I)
are the same (see
[
3
]
). Hence
R/I
has a linear resolution if and only if
S/\mathcal{P}(I)
has a linear resolution. We show that it is also valid for componentwise linear ideals.

Herzog and Hibi in
[
7
]
defined the concept of (squarefree) componentwise linear.
A graded ideal
I\subseteq R
is componentwise linear if
I_{<j>}
has a linear resolution for all
j
where
I_{<j>}
is the ideal generated by all homogeneous polynomials of degree
j
belonging to
I
. An ideal
I\subseteq R
is squarefree componentwise linear if
I_{[j]}
has a linear resolution for all
j
where
I_{[j]}
is the ideal generated by all squarefree homogeneous polynomials of degree
j
belonging to
I
. The following Theorem is standard but we include for the sake of self-containment.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
R/I
be a graded algebra with all minimal generators of
I
of degree
\geq k
. Then
R/I
has a
k
-linear resolution if and only if
\reg(I)=k
.

Proof.

Suppose
R/I
has a
k
-linear resolution

0\longleftarrow R/I\longleftarrow R\longleftarrow R^{\beta_{1}}[-k]\longleftarrow R^{\beta_{2}}[-k-1]\longleftarrow\cdots\longleftarrow R^{\beta_{m}}[-k-m+1]\longleftarrow 0

Thus reg
(R/I)=\max\{k+i-i-1\}=k-1
, so reg
(I)=k
. If all generators of
I
are of degree
\geq k
a resolution of
R/I

looks like this:

0\longleftarrow R/I\longleftarrow R\longleftarrow\oplus_{j=1}^{j_{1}}R^{\beta_{1,k-1+j}}[-k+1-j]\longleftarrow\oplus_{j=1}^{j_{2}}R^{\beta_{2,k+j}}[-k-j]\longleftarrow\cdots

\cdots\longleftarrow\oplus_{j=1}^{j_{m}}R^{\beta_{m,k+m-2+j}}[-k-m+2-j]\longleftarrow 0

If reg
(I)=k
(so reg
(R/I)=k-1
) then
\{k+i-2+j_{i}-i\}\leq k-1
for all
i
, so
j_{i}=1
for all
i
, so
the resolution is linear.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
I
be monomial ideal
R
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

i)

I
is componentwise linear;

ii)

\mathcal{P}(I)
is componentwise linear;

iii)

\mathcal{P}(I)
is squarefree componentwise linear.

Proof.

(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
Let
k
be a positive integer. By Lemma
3.1
,
\reg(I)=\reg(\mathcal{P}(I))
and
I
has a linear resolution if and only if
\mathcal{P}(I)
has a linear resolution. Then it follows from Theorem
3.2
that

\mathcal{P}(I_{<k>})
has a linear resolution if and only if
\reg(I_{<k>})=k
. But Theorem
3.2
also implies that
\mathcal{P}(I)_{<k>}
has a linear resolution if and only if
\reg(\mathcal{P}(I)_{<k>})=k
. Hence
\mathcal{P}(I_{<k>})
has a linear resolution if and only if
\mathcal{P}(I)_{<k>}
has a linear resolution. Then the proof follows from the definition of componentwise linear.

(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
See
[
7
, Proposition 1.5]
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

i)

I_{\geq k}
has a linear resolution;

ii)

\mathcal{P}(I)_{\geq k}
has a linear resolution;

iii)

\mathcal{P}(I)_{k}
has a linear resolution.

Proof.

Note that by the argument in the proof of Proposition
3.3
we have
\mathcal{P}(I_{\geq k})
has a linear resolution if and only if
\mathcal{P}(I)_{\geq k}
has a linear resolution. Then the proof is an immediate consequence of the statement of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

4.
The graded Betti numbers of
I_{\geq k}

In this section we consider quotients of
I_{\geq k}=I\cap M^{k}
, where
M
is the graded maximal ideal, for any graded, not necessarily monomial, ideal.
We show that we can determine all graded Betti numbers of
R/I_{\geq k}
if we know the Betti numbers of
R/I
and the Hilbert series of
R/I_{\geq k}
.
The following theorem is probably well known, but we haven’t found any reference.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
I
be a graded ideal in
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Let
k=\min\{\deg(u)\mid u\in I\}
. If
j<i+k-1
, then
\beta_{i,j}(R/I_{\geq k})=0
. If
j>i+k-1
,
then
\beta_{i,j}(R/I_{\geq k})=\beta_{i,j}(R/I)
. If we know the graded Betti numbers of
R/I
and the Hilbert series of
R/I_{\geq k}
, we can
determine the graded Betti numbers of
R/I_{\geq k}
.

Proof.

We think about the Betti numbers as the dimension of the homology of the Koszul complex of
R/I
. A “monomial”
mT_{l_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{l_{i}}

is of degree
(i,i+j)
if
m\in R/I
is of degree
j
, and elements of degree
(i,i+j)
are linear combinations of such monomials. Now
\beta_{1,j}(R/I_{\geq k})=0

if
j<k
since the generators of
I_{\geq k}
have degree
\geq k
, and then
\beta_{i,j}(R/I_{\geq k})=0
if
j<i+k-1
, since if
j_{i}
is the smallest
j

for which
\beta_{i,j}(R/I_{\geq k})\neq 0
, then
j_{i}<j_{i+1}
because we have a minimal resolution. In degrees
j\geq i-k-1

R/I_{\geq k}
is identical
with
R/I
, so if
j>i+k-1

\beta_{i,j}(R/I_{\geq k})=\beta_{i,j}(R/I)
. Since the Hilbert series of
R/I_{\geq k}
equals
\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\beta_{i,i+j}(R/I_{\geq k})t^{i+j}/(1-t)^{n}
, we can determine
the remaining Betti numbers
\beta_{i,i+k-1}(R/I_{\geq k})
from the Hilbert series.
∎

We first give some corollaries.

Corollary 4.2
.

We have that
k
-index of
R/I_{\geq k}
is smaller or equal to the
(k+1)
-index. In particular, if
R/I_{\geq k}
has a linear resolution,
then
R/I_{\geq k+1}
has a linear resolution.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
I
be a graded ideal of
R
and
\reg(I)=d
. Then for any positive integer
k
we have

\reg(I_{\geq k})=\begin{cases}d\quad\mbox{ if }d\geq k,\\
k\quad\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We have
\beta_{i,i+d}(I)\neq 0
for some
i
. Theorem
4.1
implies that
\reg(I_{\geq k})=d
whenever
d\geq k
and
\reg(I_{\geq k})=k
otherwise.
∎

For a complete intersection generated in degree 2, the result is easy to describe.

Corollary 4.4
.

If
I
is an artinian complete intersection generated in degree 2, then the
k
-index of
R/I_{\geq k}
is
k-1
if
2\leq k\leq n
and the
k
-index is
\infty
if
k\geq n+1
.

Corollary 4.5
.

If
S=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\cap M^{k})
,
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
a complete intersection in degree
d
, then
S
has a linear resolution exactly for
k\geq nd-n+1
.

Corollary 4.6
.

If
S=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
, where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
is a complete intersection in degree
d
, and
T=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\cap M^{k})
.
Suppose that
k\leq n(d-1)
. Then
\beta_{i,j}(T)\neq 0
only if
(i,j)=(0,0)
,
(i,j)=(i,k+i-1)
for
1\leq i\leq n
, and for
(i,j)=(r,dr)
for
\frac{k-1}{d-1}<r\leq n
. Furthermore,

\beta_{r,dr}={n\choose r}
for those
r
.

Example 4.7
.

Let
S=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{8}]/\left((x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{8}^{2})\cap M^{5}\right)
. In
S
we have all monomials
of degree
<5
, but only the squarefree in degrees 6,7, and 8, and nothing in degrees
>8
.
Thus the Hilbert series is

1+{8\choose 1}t+{9\choose 2}t^{2}+{10\choose 3}t^{3}+{11\choose 4}t^{4}+{8\choose 3}t^{5}+{8\choose 2}t^{6}+{8\choose 1}t^{7}+{8\choose 0}t^{8}=

\frac{1-736t^{5}+4200t^{6}-10528t^{7}+14910t^{8}-12832t^{9}+6720t^{10}-2016t^{11}+288t^{12}-8t^{14}+t^{16}}{(1-t)^{8}}.

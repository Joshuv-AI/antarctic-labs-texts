Spectrum of the Neumann-Poincaré operator and optimal estimates for transmission problems in presence of two circular inclusions Thanks: This work was supported by NRF (of S. Korea) grants No. 2019R1A2B5B01069967.
Yong-Gwan Ji, Hyeonbae Kang

Abstract
We consider the field concentration for the transmission problems of the homogeneous and inhomogeneous conductivity equations in the presence of closely located circular inclusions. We revisit these well-studied problems by exploiting the spectral nature residing behind the phenomenon of the field concentration. The spectral approach enables us not only to recover the existing results with new insights but also to produce significant new results. We show that when relative conductivities of inclusions have different signs, then the gradient of the solution is bounded regardless of the distance between inclusions, but the second and higher derivatives may blow up as the distance tends to zero if one of conductivities is 0 0 and the other ∞ \infty . This result holds for both homogenous and inhomogeneous problems. We prove these results by precise quantitative estimates of the derivatives of the solution. We also show by examples that the estimates are optimal.

\Lambda-\mathbb{K}_{\partial D}^{*}
is invertible on

.

(iii)

\mathbb{K}_{\partial D}^{*}
corresponding to the eigenvalue

such that

is constant on

and

Proof.

(i) is well-known (see, for example,
[

, Chapter XI, Section 11]
).

Since
\mathbb{K}_{\partial D}^{*}
is compact, (ii) follows from the injectivity of
\Lambda-\mathbb{K}_{\partial D}^{*}
on
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{-1/2}(\partial D)
by Fredholm alternative. If
\left(\Lambda-\mathbb{K}_{\partial D}^{*}\right)[\varphi]=0
, then

is the solution to (
1.10
) with
\eta=0
and
u(x)=O(|x|^{-1})
as
|x|\to\infty
. It implies that
u\equiv 0
and hence
\varphi=0
.

If
\psi\in E_{1/2}
, then
(\varphi,\mathbb{S}_{\partial D}[\psi])=0
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{-1/2}
. Thus
\mathbb{S}_{\partial D}[\psi]
is constant, that is,

is constant on

and

and hence

Let

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\int_{\partial D_{i}}\partial_{\nu}q^{1}|_{+}\,d\sigma=(-1)^{i+1},\quad&i=1,2,\\

(2.19)

The existence of the solution

Let

Let

\psi^{i}:=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{\nu}q^{i}|_{\partial D_{1}+}\\

One can easily see that

3
NP spectrum on two circles

From now on we suppose that
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are disks of radii
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
, respectively. In this section we recall a complete spectrum of the NP operator on
\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}_{0}
using the NP spectrum on concentric disks and Möbius invariance of the NP spectrum (see, for example,
[
30
]
). NP spectrum on two disks is derived in
[
11
]
and we follow the method there. However, we need to review the method in some detail since we need to prove Möbius invariance of the inner product (Lemma
3.2
(ii)), and to derive the solution formula for the integral equation (
2.9
) (Lemma
3.5
).

3.1
NP spectrum on concentric circles

Let
D_{j}^{*}
,
j=1,2
be the disk of radius
R_{j}
centered at
0
, where
R_{1}<R_{2}
, and let
\mathbb{K}_{\partial D^{*}}
be the NP operator on
\partial D^{*}
.
The complete spectrum of
\mathbb{K}_{\partial D^{*}}
on
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{-1/2}(\partial D^{*})
is known (see
[
2
]
) as we describe in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\rho:=R_{1}/R_{2}
. The eigenvalues of
\mathbb{K}_{\partial D^{*}}
on
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{-1/2}(\partial D^{*})
are
\pm\frac{1}{2}\rho^{|n|}
and the corresponding eigenfunctions are
f^{|n|,\pm}
and
f^{-|n|,\pm}
where

f^{n,\pm}=\left(\frac{1}{R_{1}}e^{in\theta},\pm\frac{1}{R_{2}}e^{in\theta}\right)^{T},\quad n=\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots.

(3.1)

Actually Lemma
3.1
is a consequence of the following formula whose proof can be found in
[
2
]
: if
\Omega
is the disk of radius
R
(centered at
0
), then for
n\neq 0

\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{R}e^{in\theta}\right](r,\theta)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2|n|}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{|n|}e^{in\theta}\quad&\text{if }r\leq R,\\
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2|n|}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{|n|}e^{in\theta}\quad&\text{if }r>R.\end{cases}

(3.2)

Using (
3.2
), one can see that the following formula holds:
if
n>0
, then

\mathcal{S}_{\partial D^{*}}[f^{n,\pm}](\zeta)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2n}(R_{1}^{-n}\pm R_{2}^{-n})\zeta^{n}\quad&\text{if }|\zeta|\leq R_{1},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2n}(R_{1}^{n}\bar{\zeta}^{-n}\pm R_{2}^{-n}\zeta^{n})\quad&\text{if }R_{1}<|\zeta|\leq R_{2},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2n}(R_{1}^{n}\pm R_{2}^{n})\bar{\zeta}^{-n}\quad&\text{if }R_{2}<|\zeta|,\end{cases}

(3.3)

and if
n<0
, then

\mathcal{S}_{\partial D^{*}}[f^{n,\pm}](\zeta)=\overline{\mathcal{S}_{\partial D^{*}}[f^{-n,\pm}](\zeta)},

(3.4)

where
\zeta=re^{i\theta}
. These formulas play a crucial role in what follows.

3.2
Möbius invariance and NP spectrum on two circles

By translating and rotating
D
if necessary, we may assume that their centers are located at
(c_{1},0)
and
(c_{2},0)
, where

c_{1}=\frac{r_{2}^{2}-r_{1}^{2}-(r_{1}+r_{2}+\epsilon)^{2}}{2(r_{1}+r_{2}+\epsilon)}-\frac{\beta}{2},\quad c_{2}=c_{1}+r_{1}+r_{2}+\epsilon

(3.5)

with

\beta=\sqrt{\epsilon}\frac{\sqrt{(2r_{1}+\epsilon)(2r_{2}+\epsilon)(2r_{1}+2r_{2}+\epsilon)}}{r_{1}+r_{2}+\epsilon},

(3.6)

where
\epsilon
is the separation distance between
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
. These numbers are chosen so that
\partial D_{1}
and
\partial D_{2}
are mapped onto two concentric circles by an inversion in a circle and a translation:
if we let

z^{*}=Tz:=\frac{\beta}{z}+1,

(3.7)

then
T(\partial D_{j})
(
j=1,2
) is the circle of the radius
R_{j}
centered at
0
, where
R_{j}
is given by

R_{1}^{2}=1+\frac{\beta}{c_{1}},\quad R_{2}^{2}=1+\frac{\beta}{c_{2}}.

(3.8)

Let

D_{1}^{*}:=T(D_{1})=\{|\zeta|<R_{1}\},\quad D_{2}^{*}:=T(D_{2})=\{|\zeta|>R_{2}\}.

(3.9)

One can see from (
3.6
) that

\beta=\frac{4}{r_{*}}\sqrt{\epsilon}+O(\epsilon),

(3.10)

where
r_{*}
is defined by (
1.6
).
Since
c_{1}=-r_{1}+O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
and
c_{2}=r_{2}+O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
, we see from (
3.8
) that

R_{1}=1-\frac{\beta}{2r_{1}}+O(\epsilon),\quad R_{2}=1+\frac{\beta}{2r_{2}}+O(\epsilon),

(3.11)

which together with (
3.10
) yields

\rho:=\frac{R_{1}}{R_{2}}=1-r^{*}\sqrt{\epsilon}+O(\epsilon),

(3.12)

as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Under the change of variables (
3.7
) the line and area elements are respectively transformed as

ds(z^{*})=\frac{\beta}{|z|^{2}}ds(z)

(3.13)

and

dA(z^{*})=\frac{\beta^{2}}{|z|^{4}}dA(z).

(3.14)

Define the transformation
U
from
\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\partial D)
to
\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\partial D^{*})
by

\displaystyle(U\varphi)(z^{*})=\varphi^{*}(z^{*}):=\frac{|z|^{2}}{\beta}\varphi(z),\quad z^{*}\in\partial D^{*}.

(3.15)

We obtain the following lemma (a similar lemma has been proved in
[
17
]
for the case with a single inclusion).

Lemma 3.2
.

(i)

The following transformation formula holds for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial D)
:

\mathcal{S}_{\partial D^{*}}[\varphi^{*}](z^{*})=\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi](z)-\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi](0),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

(3.16)

(ii)

U
is a unitary transform from
\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial D)
to
\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial D^{*})
, namely,

\langle\varphi^{*},\psi^{*}\rangle_{\partial D^{*}}=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\partial D}

(3.17)

for any
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial D)
.

(iii)

\mathbb{K}^{*}_{\partial D}[\varphi]=\lambda\varphi
(
|\lambda|<1/2
) if and only if
\mathbb{K}^{*}_{\partial D^{*}}[\varphi^{*}]=-\lambda\varphi^{*}
. In fact, we have

\mathbb{K}^{*}_{\partial D^{*}}\circ U=-U\circ\mathbb{K}^{*}_{\partial D}.

(3.18)

Proof.

From the definition (
3.7
) of
z^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle|z^{*}-w^{*}|=\frac{\beta}{|z||w|}|z-w|,

(3.19)

and hence

\displaystyle\Gamma(z^{*}-w^{*})=\Gamma(z-w)-\Gamma(w)-\Gamma(z)+\Gamma(\beta).

(3.20)

Here
\Gamma(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|z|
. Hence for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial D)
, we have from (
3.13
) and (
3.20
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\partial D^{*}}[\varphi^{*}](z^{*})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D^{*}}\Gamma(z^{*}-w^{*})\,\varphi^{*}(w^{*})\,d\sigma(w^{*})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\left(\Gamma(z-w)-\Gamma(w)-\Gamma(z)+\Gamma(\beta)\right)\,\varphi(w)\,d\sigma(w)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi](z)-\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi](0),

which proves (
3.16
).

That
U
is a unitary transform follows from (
3.16
) immediately. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\langle\varphi^{*},\psi^{*}\rangle_{\partial D^{*}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial D^{*}}\varphi^{*}(z^{*})\,\overline{\mathcal{S}_{\partial D^{*}}[\psi^{*}](z^{*})}\,d\sigma(z^{*})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial D}\varphi(z)\;\overline{\left(\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\psi](z)-\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\psi](0)\right)}\,d\sigma(z)

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\partial D},

where the last equality holds since
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial D)
.

Let
n_{z}
and
n_{z^{*}}
be the complexified outward unit normal vectors on
\partial D_{j}
and
\partial D^{*}_{j}
, respectively, namely,

n_{z}=\frac{z-c_{j}}{r_{j}}\quad\mbox{if }z\in\partial D_{j},

and

n_{z^{*}}=(-1)^{j+1}\frac{z^{*}}{R_{j}^{*}}\quad\mbox{if }z^{*}\in\partial D^{*}_{j},\quad j=1,2.

Then the following relation holds:

\displaystyle n_{z^{*}}=\frac{\bar{z}}{z}n_{z}.

(3.21)

In fact, since
|T(z)|^{2}=R_{j}
if
z\in\partial D_{j}
, we see that
{\bar{\partial}_{z}}|T(z)|^{2}={T(z)}\overline{\partial_{z}T(z)}
is normal to
\partial D_{j}
. Thus,

\frac{{\bar{\partial}_{z}}|T(z)|^{2}}{|{\bar{\partial}_{z}}|T(z)|^{2}|}=-\frac{T(z)z}{R_{j}\bar{z}}=cn_{z},\quad\mbox{for }z\in\partial D_{j},

where
c
is either
1
or
-1
. One can easily see that
c=-1
, and hence (
3.21
) holds.

Note that the outward normal derivatives
\nu_{z^{*}}
and
\nu_{z}
are given by

\partial_{\nu_{z^{*}}}=2\Re(n_{z^{*}}\partial_{z^{*}}),\quad\partial_{\nu_{z}}=2\Re(n_{z}\partial_{z}).

We thus have from (
3.7
) and (
3.21
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu_{z^{*}}}=2\Re(n_{z^{*}}\partial_{z^{*}})=-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\beta}2\Re(n_{z}\partial_{z})=-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\beta}\partial_{\nu_{z}}.

(3.22)

Hence we have from (
3.20
) and (
3.22
) the following identity

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}^{*}_{\partial D^{*}}[\varphi^{*}](z^{*})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D^{*}}\partial_{\nu_{z^{*}}}\Gamma(z^{*}-w^{*})\,\varphi^{*}(w^{*})\,d\sigma(w^{*})

\displaystyle=-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\beta}\int_{\partial D}\partial_{\nu_{z}}\left(\Gamma(z-w)-\Gamma(z)\right)\,\varphi(w)\,d\sigma(w)

\displaystyle=-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\beta}\mathbb{K}^{*}_{\partial D}[\varphi](z)

which proves (
3.18
). This completes the proof.
∎

Let
f^{n,\pm}
be the eigenfunctions of
\mathbb{K}_{\partial D^{*}}^{*}
as given in (
3.1
) and let
\varphi^{n,\pm}
,

be functions such that

4.1
Vanishing integral condition

We first deal with the case when the integrals of
f
over
D_{1}
and

vanish, namely,

\int_{D_{1}}f=\int_{D_{2}}f=0.

(4.1)

In this case,
\partial_{\nu}F\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial D_{j})
for
j=1,2
because of the condition (
4.1
). In fact, since
\Delta F=k_{j}^{-1}f
on
D_{j}
(
j=1,2
), we have

\int_{\partial D_{j}}\partial_{\nu}Fds=\frac{1}{k_{j}}\int_{D_{j}}f=0.

In particular, the constants
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
given in (
2.29
) and (
2.30
) become
0
. It thus follows from (
2.35
) that
the solution to (
1.2
) admits the following representation:

u(x)=F(x)+\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi](x)

(4.2)

where
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial D)
is the solution to the integral equation

\left(\Lambda-\mathbb{K}_{\partial D}^{*}\right)[\varphi]=\partial F.

(4.3)

Since
\partial_{\nu}F\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial D_{j})
for
j=1,2
, the following Neumann boundary value problem admits a unique solution which we denote by
H_{j}
:

\begin{cases}\Delta H_{j}=0\quad&\text{in }D_{j},\\
\partial_{\nu}H_{j}=\partial_{\nu}F\quad&\text{on }\partial D_{j}.\end{cases}

(4.4)

Let
h_{j}
be the analytic function in
D_{j}^{*}
such that
h_{1}(0)=0
,
\lim_{|\zeta|\to\infty}h_{2}(\zeta)=0
, and

H_{j}(z)=\Re(h_{j}\circ T)(z)+C_{j},\quad z\in D_{j}

(4.5)

for some constant
C_{j}
, where
T
is the transformation defined in (
3.7
) and
\Re
denotes the real part.

We look into the integral equation (
4.3
). We have from the Green’s identity, the jump formula (
2.5
) and the relations (
3.13
), (
3.23
), (
3.24
) that

\displaystyle\langle\partial F,\varphi^{n,\pm}\rangle_{\partial D}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\partial D_{j}}\partial_{\nu}F\;\overline{\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi^{n,\pm}]}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\partial D_{j}}H_{j}\;\overline{\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi^{n,\pm}]|_{-}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\rho^{|n|}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\partial D_{j}}H_{j}\;\overline{\varphi_{j}^{n,\pm}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(1\pm\rho^{|n|}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\partial D^{*}_{j}}(h_{j}+\overline{h_{j}})\;\overline{f_{j}^{n,\pm}}.

It then follows from (
3.25
) that

\frac{\langle\partial F,\varphi^{n,\pm}\rangle_{\partial D}}{\|\varphi^{n,\pm}\|_{\partial D}^{2}}=\frac{|n|}{8\pi}C_{n,\pm}:=\frac{|n|}{8\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\partial D^{*}_{j}}(h_{j}+\overline{h_{j}})\;\overline{f_{j}^{n,\pm}}.

(4.6)

Thus we have

\partial F=\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{|n|}{8\pi}\big(C_{n,+}\varphi^{n,+}+C_{n,-}\varphi^{n,-}\big).

(4.7)

By Lemma
3.5
, the solution
\varphi
is given by

\varphi=\sum_{n\neq 0}(a_{n,+}\varphi^{n,+}+a_{n,-}\varphi^{n,-}),

(4.8)

where

\displaystyle a_{n,+}

\displaystyle=\frac{|n|}{4\pi(4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\rho^{2|n|})}\big((\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\rho^{|n|})C_{n,+}+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})C_{n,-}\big),

(4.9)

\displaystyle a_{n,-}

\displaystyle=\frac{|n|}{4\pi(4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\rho^{2|n|})}\big(-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})C_{n,+}+(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\rho^{|n|})C_{n,-}\big).

(4.10)

The transformed function
\varphi^{*}=U(\varphi)
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{|n|}{4\pi(4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\rho^{2|n|})}\Big[C_{n,+}\Big((\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\rho^{|n|})f^{n,+}-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})f^{n,-}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{n,-}\Big((\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})f^{n,+}+(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\rho^{|n|})f^{n,-}\Big)\Big].

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\partial D^{*}}[\varphi^{*}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{|n|}{4\pi(4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\rho^{2|n|})}\Big[C_{n,+}\Big((\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\rho^{|n|})H_{n,+}-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})H_{n,-}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{n,-}\Big((\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})H_{n,+}+(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\rho^{|n|})H_{n,-}\Big)\Big].

Here we use notation

for simplicity of expressions. Here and afterwards, we use
\zeta
for

.

Observe from the definition (

) that
\overline{C_{n,\pm}}=C_{-n,\pm}
, and from (

) that

. We then have

\mathcal{S}_{\partial D^{*}}[\varphi^{*}](\zeta)=\frac{V(\zeta)+\overline{V(\zeta)}}{2},

(4.11)

where

\displaystyle V(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{2\pi(4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\rho^{2n})}\Big[C_{n,+}\Big((\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\rho^{n})H_{n,+}(\zeta)-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})H_{n,-}(\zeta)\Big)

h_{1}
is analytic in

and

(4.13)

(4.14)

where

\displaystyle=a_{1,n}R_{1}^{n}\Big((2\lambda_{1}-\rho^{n})(-2nH_{n,+}(\zeta))+(2\lambda_{2}+\rho^{n})(-2nH_{n,-}(\zeta))\Big),

(4.15)

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})(R_{1}^{2}\bar{\zeta}^{-1})^{n}+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\rho\zeta)^{n}-(\rho^{2}\zeta)^{n},\quad&R_{1}<|\zeta|\leq R_{2},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})(R_{1}^{2}\bar{\zeta}^{-1})^{n}+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(R_{1}R_{2}\bar{\zeta}^{-1})^{n}-(R_{1}^{2}\bar{\zeta}^{-1})^{n},\quad&R_{2}<|\zeta|,\end{cases}

(4.17)

and

\displaystyle B_{2,n}(\zeta)=2\overline{a_{2,n}}\times

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})(R_{2}^{2}\zeta^{-1})^{-n}-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(R_{1}R_{2}\zeta^{-1})^{-n}-(R_{2}^{2}\zeta^{-1})^{-n},\quad&|\zeta|\leq R_{1},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})(R_{2}^{2}\zeta^{-1})^{-n}-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\rho^{-1}\bar{\zeta})^{-n}-(\rho^{-2}\bar{\zeta})^{-n},\quad&R_{1}<|\zeta|\leq R_{2},\\

(4.18)

Let, for

A_{j}(\zeta):=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{j,n}(\zeta)}{4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\rho^{2n}}.

(4.19)

Then we have from (
4.14

(4.20)

It follows from (
4.17
) that

A_{1}(\zeta)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})w_{1}(\zeta)&\\
\qquad+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})w_{1}(\rho\zeta)-w_{1}(\rho^{2}\zeta),\quad&|\zeta|\leq R_{1},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})w_{1}(R_{1}^{2}\bar{\zeta}^{-1})&\\
\qquad+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})w_{1}(\rho\zeta)-w_{1}(\rho^{2}\zeta),\quad&R_{1}<|\zeta|\leq R_{2},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})w_{1}(R_{1}^{2}\bar{\zeta}^{-1})&\\
\qquad+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})w_{1}(R_{1}R_{2}\bar{\zeta}^{-1})-w_{1}(R_{1}^{2}\bar{\zeta}^{-1}),\quad&R_{2}<|\zeta|,\end{cases}

(4.21)

where

w_{1}(\zeta)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{1,n}\zeta^{n}}{4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\rho^{2n}},\quad|\zeta|\leq R_{1},

(4.22)

and from (
4.18

A_{2}(\zeta)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})w_{2}(R_{2}^{2}\zeta^{-1})&\\
\qquad-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})w_{2}(R_{1}R_{2}\zeta^{-1})-w_{2}(R_{2}^{2}\zeta^{-1}),\quad&|\zeta|\leq R_{1},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})w_{2}(R_{2}^{2}\zeta^{-1})&\\
\qquad-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})w_{2}(\rho^{-1}\bar{\zeta})-w_{2}(\rho^{-2}\bar{\zeta}),\quad&R_{1}<|\zeta|\leq R_{2},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})w_{2}(\bar{\zeta})&\\

(4.23)

where

w_{2}(\zeta)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{2,n}\zeta^{-n}}{4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\rho^{2n}},\quad|\zeta|\geq R_{2}.

(4.24)

Likewise we have

(4.26)

(6.3)

\displaystyle=-\rho^{2}w_{1}^{\prime}(\rho^{2}\zeta)-R_{2}^{2}\zeta^{-2}w_{2}^{\prime}(R_{2}^{2}\zeta^{-1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\rho^{2l+2}h_{1}^{\prime}(\rho^{2l+2}\zeta)-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\rho^{-2l}R_{2}^{2}\zeta^{-2}h_{2}^{\prime}(\rho^{-2l}R_{2}^{2}\zeta^{-1}).

\frac{\partial^{k}\zeta}{\partial z^{k}}=(-1)^{k}\frac{k!\beta}{z^{k+1}}=(-1)^{k}k!\frac{(\zeta-1)^{k+1}}{\beta^{k}},\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

(6.5)

\big|\partial_{z}(V\circ T)(T^{-1}(-1))\big|=\Big|\partial_{\zeta}V(-1)\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial z}\Big|_{\zeta=-1}\Big|\gtrsim\frac{1}{\beta},

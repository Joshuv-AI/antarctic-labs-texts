On linear-quadratic optimal control of implicit difference equations
Daniel Bankmann, Matthias Voigt

Abstract
In this work we investigate explicit and implicit difference equations and the corresponding infinite time horizon linear-quadratic optimal control problem. We derive conditions for feasibility of the optimal control problem as well as existence and uniqueness of optimal controls under certain weaker assumptions compared to the standard approaches in the literature which are using algebraic Riccati equations. To this end, we introduce and analyze a discrete-time Lur’e equation and a corresponding Kalman-Yakubovich-Popov inequality. We show that solvability of the Kalman-Yakubovich-Popov inequality can be characterized via the spectral structure of a certain palindromic matrix pencil. The deflating subspaces of this pencil are finally used to construct solutions of the Lur’e equation. The results of this work are transferred from the continuous-time case. However, many additional technical difficulties arise in this context.

Introduction

In this article we revisit the discrete-time linear-quadratic optimal control problem, that is minimizing a quadratic cost functional given by

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\begin{pmatrix}x_{j}\\
u_{j}\end{pmatrix}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}Q&S\\
S^{*}&R\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{j}\\
u_{j}\end{pmatrix}}

subject to the
implicit difference equation

E\sigma x_{j}=Ax_{j}+Bu_{j},

(1)

with the initial condition
Ex_{0}=Ex^{0}
and the stabilization condition
\lim_{j\to\infty}Ex_{j}=0
. Here
\sigma
denotes the shift operator, i. e.,
\sigma x_{j}=x_{j+1}
. Moreover,
(x_{j})_{j}\in(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is the
state sequence
, and
(u_{j})_{j}\in(\mathbb{K}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is the
input sequence
. Throughout this work we will further assume that the matrix pencil
zE-A\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{n\times n}
is regular, i. e.,
{\det(zE-A)\not\equiv 0}
. Such discrete-time systems often appear during the time-discretization
[
6
]
or discrete-time lifting
[
19
]
of continuous-time differential-algebraic equations, but many problems can also be directly modeled as implicit difference equations
[
27
,
32
]
.

There is a large body of work concerning the linear-quadratic optimal control problem for differential-algebraic equations, see, e. g.,
[
29
,
24
,
1
,
23
,
36
]
, just to mention a few.

So far, the discrete-time optimal control problem has only been discussed in a few works, most of which treat the case
E=I_{n}
. However, several technical assumptions are usually made. In
[
25
]
, the case of an invertible
A
with
Q\succeq 0
and
R\succ 0
is discussed. The invertibility is needed to form a symplectic matrix that is associated with a discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation

A^{*}XA-X-(A^{*}XB+S)(B^{*}XB+R)^{-1}(B^{*}XA+S^{*})+Q=0,\quad X=X^{*}

(2)

and the necessary optimality conditions. The invertibility condition is relaxed in
[
31
,
26
]
where instead of the symplectic matrix, a symplectic matrix pencil is considered. Another difficulty arises, if
R
is not invertible. Then also the symplectic pencil cannot be formed and one has to turn to an extended symplectic pencil
[
18
]
, which is essentially what we will later call the BVD matrix pencil. However, for the analysis it is still assumed that this pencil is regular.

The relation of the optimal control problem to a certain linear matrix inequality (the Kalman-Yakubovich-Popov inequality) and so-called Popov functions is discussed in
[
38
]
. Here a generalized algebraic Riccati equation is considered, where
B^{*}XB+R
is not assumed to be invertible. It is shown that the solutions of this equation fulfill a certain rank-minimization property of the associated linear matrix inequality which, in contrast to the continuous-time case, do not need to be solutions of the algebraic Riccati equation (
2
).

The case of optimal control problems for implicit difference equations, i. e., the case where
E
might be singular, has only been briefly considered in the literature. In
[
3
]
, a discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation similar to (
2
) is derived by transforming the system into SVD coordinates and modifying the cost functional accordingly. This analysis needs an index-1 condition on the system to ensure the solvability of the optimality system. The monograph
[
29
]
treats the problem numerically, i. e., structure-preserving algorithms for symplectic matrix pencils are devised.

The goal of this work is a full theoretical analysis of the infinite time horizon linear-quadratic optimal control problem for implicit difference equations. In contrast to most other works, we do not impose any definiteness conditions on the cost functional nor the index of the system. Also, our notion of rank-minimality turns out to be more general than in
[
38
]
. The results obtained in this paper are motivated by recent achievements for the continuous-time case
[
35
,
36
,
41
]
.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we recap basic matrix and control theoretic notations and results. In Section
3
we introduce a variant of the Kalman-Yakubovich-Popov inequality for implicit difference equations
given by

\mathcal{M}(P):=\begin{bmatrix}A^{*}PA-E^{*}PE+Q&A^{*}PB+S\\
B^{*}PA+S^{*}&B^{*}PB+R\end{bmatrix}\succeq_{\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}}0,\qquad P=P^{*},

a discrete-time version of the inequality introduced in
[
36
]
, where
\succeq_{\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}}
denotes an inequality projected on a certain subspace
\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
, i. e.,
V^{*}\mathcal{M}(P)V\succeq 0
holds for any basis matrix
V
of
\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
. We show statements which relate the solvability of this inequality
to the non-negativity of the Popov function on the unit circle, a certain rational matrix function defined by

\Phi(z):=\begin{bmatrix}(zE-A)^{-1}B\\
I_{m}\end{bmatrix}^{\sim}\begin{bmatrix}Q&S\\
S^{*}&R\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}(zE-A)^{-1}B\\
I_{m}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{K}(z)^{m\times m},

where
G^{\sim}(z):=G\left(\overline{z}^{-1}\right)^{*}
for a rational matrix
G(z)\in\mathbb{K}(z)^{n\times n}
.

In Sections
4
and
5
we introduce the notion of inertia for palindromic matrix pencils evaluated on the unit circle and provide spectral characterizations regarding positivity of the Popov function, similar to the characterizations which were obtained in
[
35
]
and
[
41
]
for even matrix pencils in the continuous-time case.

In Section
6
we investigate the Lur’e equation for the discrete-time optimal control problem which is a generalization of the algebraic Riccati equation (
2
). This means that we seek solution triples
(X,\,K,\,L)\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{q\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{q\times m}
fulfilling

\begin{bmatrix}A^{*}XA-E^{*}XE+Q&A^{*}XB+S\\
B^{*}XA+S^{*}&B^{*}XB+R\end{bmatrix}=_{\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}}\begin{bmatrix}K^{*}\\
L^{*}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}K&L\end{bmatrix},\quad X=X^{*},

where
q:=\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{K}(z)}\Phi(z)
.

We show that solvability of this equation can be related to the existence of certain deflating subspaces of a palindromic matrix pencil of the form

z\begin{bmatrix}0&E&0\\
A^{*}&Q&S\\
B^{*}&S^{*}&R\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}0&A&B\\
E^{*}&Q&S\\
0&S^{*}&R\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{(2n+m)\times(2n+m)}.

The explicit construction of these deflating subspaces turns out to be much more demanding compared to the continuous-time setting.
Furthermore, solutions of the Lur’e equation can be obtained from these subspaces. Due to the symmetry of the above pencil, robust and efficient structure-exploiting numerical methods
[
9
,
37
]
can be used.

Finally, in Section
7
we apply these results to the optimal control problem. Here we consider the stabilizing solutions of the Lur’e equations which can be used to construct optimal controls (in case of existence) and to determine the optimal value. In particular, we show that under some weak conditions, the existence of stabilizing solutions is equivalent to the feasibility of the optimal control problem. We further characterize existence and uniqueness of optimal controls in terms of the zero dynamics of the closed-loop system. Finally, we discuss how the deflating subspaces of the palindromic and BVD pencils appear in the solution of the corresponding boundary value problems.

Mathematical Preliminaries

2.1
Matrix Pencils

In this section we briefly discuss some basic notions of matrix pencils
zE-A\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{m\times n}
.

Definition 2.1

(Equivalence of matrix pencils)
.

Two matrix pencils
zE_{1}-A_{1},\,zE_{2}-A_{2}\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{m\times n}
are called equivalent if there exist invertible matrices
W\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
and
T\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
such that

zE_{2}-A_{2}=W(zE_{1}-A_{1})T.

Each matrix pencil
zE-A\in\mathbb{C}[z]^{m\times n}
can be transformed to Kronecker canonical form via equivalence transformations. This is made precise in the next theorem.

Theorem 2.2

(Kronecker canonical form (KCF))
.

[
13
]

For every matrix pencil
zE-A\in\mathbb{C}[z]^{m\times n}
, there exist invertible matrices
W\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
and
T\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
such that

W(zE-A)T=\operatorname{diag}\left({K}_{1}(z),\,\ldots,\,{K}_{l}(z)\right),\qquad l\in\mathbb{N},

where each block
{K}_{j}(z)
is in one of the forms in Table
1
and

N_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&1\\
&&&0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{k\times k},\,K^{R}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&\\
0&\ddots&\\
&\ddots&1\\
&&0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{(k+1)\times k},\,K^{L}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
0&&0&1\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{k\times(k+1)}.

Table 1
:
Blocks in Kronecker canonical form

type

size

{K}_{j}(z)

parameters

K1

k\times k

(z-\lambda)I_{k}-N_{k}

k\in\mathbb{N},\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

K2

k\times k

zN_{k}-I_{k}

k\in\mathbb{N}

K3

k\times(k+1)

z(K_{k}^{R})^{T}-K_{k}^{L}

k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

K4

(k+1)\times k

zK_{k}^{R}-(K_{k}^{L})^{T}

The KCF is unique up to permutations of the blocks.

Equivalent matrix pencils share the same spectral structure which can be read off the KCF. Here blocks of type K1 and K2 correspond to finite eigenvalues and infinite eigenvalues, respectively. Blocks of these types and combinations of them are regular. Blocks of types K3 and K4 are rectangular and thus not regular. Note that we allow for blocks of type K3 or K4 to have zero rows or zero columns, respectively. Such blocks represent a zero row or zero column, respectively, in the KCF of
zE-A
. Based on the KCF we define the
index
of the pencil
zE-A\in\mathbb{C}[z]^{m\times n}
as the size
k
of the largest block of type K2 or K4 in its KCF
[
5
]
.

When characterizing the eigenstructure of matrices
A\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
, often invariant subspaces are involved, i. e., subspaces
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
such that
A\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
. The generalization of invariant subspaces to matrix pencils
zE-A\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{m\times n}
are so-called deflating subspaces. Here, we are using a general definition which is also suitable for singular matrix pencils, see
[
41
,
40
]
.

Definition 2.3

(Basis matrix, deflating subspaces)
.

Let
zE-A\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{n\times n}
and some subspace
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
be given.

(a)

A matrix
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
with full column rank such that
\mathcal{Y}=\operatorname{im}Y
is called
basis matrix
of
\mathcal{Y}
.

(b)

If for a basis matrix
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
of
\mathcal{Y}
there exist
W\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times p}
and
z\hat{E}-\hat{A}\in\mathbb{C}[z]^{p\times n}
such that

(zE-A)V=W(z\hat{E}-\hat{A})

and
\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{C}(z)}(z\hat{E}-\hat{A})=p
, then
\mathcal{Y}
is called
deflating subspace
of
zE-A
.

Indeed, every invariant subspace
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
of
A\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
with basis matrix
V\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times k}
describes a deflating subspace for the associated matrix pencil
zI_{n}-A
by setting
W=V
and
(z\hat{E}-\hat{A})=zI_{k}-\Lambda
, where
\Lambda\in\mathbb{K}^{k\times k}
fulfills
AV=V\Lambda
.

An important property that deflating subspaces might have is
E
-neutrality.

Definition 2.4

(
E
-neutrality)
.

[
14
,
35
]

Let
E\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
and some subspace
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
be given. Then
\mathcal{Y}
is called
E
-neutral
if for all

it holds that

x^{*}Ey=0.

It is called
maximally

E
-neutral if every proper superspace
\mathcal{W}\supsetneq\mathcal{Y}
is not
E
-neutral.

For a subspace
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
we can check

-neutrality by testing whether

,
where
Y\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times k}
is given such that

.

2.2
Feedback Equivalence

Let
\Sigma_{m,n}(\mathbb{K})
denote the set containing all the system triples
(E,\,A,\,B)\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n\times m}
with regular
zE-A
, i. e.,
\det(zE-A)\neq 0
. Later, we will also use the set
\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
containing all systems
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n\times m}\times\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n\times m}\times\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
, where
(E,\,A,\,B)\in\Sigma_{m,n}(\mathbb{K})
and
Q
and
R
are Hermitian.
Furthermore, we call the space of all
{\lparen\,x,\,u\,\rparen}\in(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}\times(\mathbb{K}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
that solve the IDE (
1
) the
behavior
of the system
(E,\,A,\,B)
. The behavior is denoted by
\mathfrak{B}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
.
In this subsection we introduce an equivalence relation on the set
\Sigma_{m,n}(\mathbb{K})
which will be particularly useful in Sections
3
and
6
. This subsection is mainly based on
[
36
, Section 2.3]
.

Definition 2.5

(Feedback equivalence)
.

Two systems
(E_{i},\,A_{i},\,B_{i})\in\Sigma_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}),\,i=1,2
, are said to be
feedback equivalent
if there exist invertible matrices
W,\,T\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
and a feedback matrix
F\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n}
such that

\begin{bmatrix}zE_{2}-A_{2}&-B_{2}\end{bmatrix}=W\begin{bmatrix}zE_{1}-A_{1}&-B_{1}\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{T}_{F},

where

\mathcal{T}_{F}=\begin{bmatrix}T&0\\
FT&I_{m}\end{bmatrix}.

If this is the case we say that
(E_{1},\,A_{1},\,B_{1})
is feedback equivalent to
(E_{2},\,A_{2},\,B_{2})
via
W
and
\mathcal{T}_{F}
.

Note that in the behavior sense, i. e., looking at the system defined by
z\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}
, where

\mathcal{E}:=\begin{bmatrix}E&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{A}:=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\end{bmatrix},

feedback equivalence corresponds to strong equivalence as introduced in
[
20
]
.
In particular, this means that feedback equivalence is indeed an equivalence relation, see
[
20
, Lemma 2.2.]
.

Given such an equivalence relation, one is usually interested in some condensed form. The following result provides such a form.

Theorem 2.6

(Feedback equivalence form)
.

[
17
, Proposition 2.12]

Let the system
(E,\,A,\,B)\in\Sigma_{m,n}(\mathbb{K})
be given. Then
(E,\,A,\,B)
is feedback equivalent to
(E_{F},\,A_{F},\,B_{F})
via some
W
and
\mathcal{T}_{F}
, where

\begin{bmatrix}zE_{F}-A_{F}&-B_{F}\end{bmatrix}=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{} ccc|c@{}}zI_{n_{1}}-A_{11}&0&0&-B_{1}\\
0&-I_{n_{2}}&zE_{23}&-B_{2}\\
0&0&zE_{33}-I_{n_{3}}&0\end{array}\right],

(3)

n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
E_{33}
is nilpotent.

A similar form has also been achieved in
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
via unitary transformations.

Example 2.7
.

Consider the system given by

E=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad A=\begin{bmatrix}-1&1\\
1&0\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

(4)

We obtain that the system is feedback equivalent to

\begin{bmatrix}zE_{F}-A_{F}&-B_{F}\end{bmatrix}=W\begin{bmatrix}zE-A&-B\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{T}_{F}=\begin{bmatrix}z-1&0&1\\
0&-1&-1\end{bmatrix}

(5)

via zero feedback, i. e.,
F=0
and

W=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathcal{T}_{F}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

Thus, we have
n_{1}=n_{2}=1
and
n_{3}=0
in (
3
).

Proposition 2.8
.

Let the system
(E,\,A,\,B)\in\Sigma_{m,n}(\mathbb{K})
be feedback equivalent to the system
(E_{F},\,A_{F},\,B_{F})\in\Sigma_{m,n}(\mathbb{K})
in feedback equivalence form (
3
). Further, denote by

(I_{n_{1}},\,A_{11},\,B_{1})\in\Sigma_{m,n_{1}}(\mathbb{K})

the associated
explicit difference equation (EDE)
system.
Then for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
we have
\det(\lambda E_{F}-A_{F})\neq 0
if and only if
\det(\lambda I_{n_{1}}-A_{11})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Note that every nilpotent matrix
E_{33}\in\mathbb{K}^{n_{3}\times n_{3}}
has only eigenvalues zero and thus
\det(zE_{33}-I_{n_{3}})=(-1)^{n_{3}}
. Then the assertion follows immediately from the block-triangular structure of

.
∎

In this subsection we consider implicit difference equations with an output of the form

E\sigma x_{j}=Ax_{j}+Bu_{j},\quad y_{j}=Cx_{j}+Du_{j},

(8)

where

2.6
Linear-Quadratic Optimal Control

One main goal of this work is to provide tools for analyzing the discrete-time infinite horizon linear-quadratic control problem
[
23
,
1
,
29
]
. It is given by:

For
x^{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
find
{\lparen\,x,\,u\,\rparen}\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
such that
Ex_{0}=Ex^{0}
,
\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}Ex_{j}=0,
and the
objective function

\mathcal{J}(x,u):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\begin{pmatrix}x_{j}\\
u_{j}\end{pmatrix}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}Q&S\\
S^{*}&R\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{j}\\
u_{j}\end{pmatrix}}

(9)

is minimized. In other words, we are interested in the value of the functional
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})\!:E\mathcal{W}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
defined by

\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0}):=\inf\left\{\mathcal{J}(x,u)\,\middle|\,{\lparen\,x,\,u\,\rparen}\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,\,A,\,B)},\,Ex_{0}=Ex^{0},\,\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}Ex_{j}=0\right\}.

The problem is called
feasible
if
\infty>\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})>-\infty
. It is called
solvable
if the infimum is actually a minimum. Note that for
x^{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
the existence of
{\lparen\,x,\,u\,\rparen}\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
such that
Ex_{0}=Ex^{0}
is guaranteed by the definition of
\mathcal{W}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
.
If further
(E,\,A,\,B)
is stabilizable we can choose
u
such that in addition
\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}Ex_{j}=0
, i. e.,
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})<\infty
.

It can be easily seen that the objective function
\mathcal{J}(x,u)
does not change if the system
(E_{F},\,A_{F},\,B_{F})\in\Sigma_{m,n}(\mathbb{K})
is equivalent to
(E,\,A,\,B)
via
W
and
\mathcal{T}_{F}
and we use the modified weights

\begin{bmatrix}Q_{F}&S_{F}\\
S_{F}^{*}&R_{F}\end{bmatrix}:=\begin{bmatrix}T^{*}(Q+F^{*}S^{*}+SF+F^{*}RF)T&T^{*}(S+F^{*}R)\\
(S^{*}+RF)T&R\end{bmatrix}.

If we assume that the system
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
is I-controllable and that

\begin{bmatrix}Q&S\\
S^{*}&R\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0

(10)

then it is well-known that in this case solutions of the optimal control problem can be characterized via certain structured matrix pencils, see,
[
28
,
9
,
21
,
29
]
.
One main contribution of this work is that we actually can drop these assumptions.

In the discrete-time case, applying Pontryagin’s maximum principle
[
33
,
29
]
leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix}0&E&0\\
A^{*}&0&0\\
B^{*}&0&0\end{bmatrix}\sigma\begin{pmatrix}\mu\\
x\\
u\end{pmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&A&B\\
E^{*}&Q&S\\
0&S^{*}&R\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mu\\
x\\
u\end{pmatrix},\quad Ex_{0}=Ex^{0},\quad\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}{E^{*}\mu_{j}}=0,\end{split}

(11)

where
{\lparen\,x,\,u\,\rparen}\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
,
x^{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
, and
\mu\in{(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
denote some Lagrange multipliers. This IDE can be analyzed by means of the matrix pencil

z\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}=\begin{bmatrix}0&zE-A&-B\\
zA^{*}-E^{*}&-Q&-S\\
zB^{*}&-S^{*}&-R\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{(2n+m)\times(2n+m)},

(12)

a so-called
BVD-pencil
; here BVD is an acronym for
B
oundary
V
alue problem for the optimal control of

D
iscrete systems
. The structure of this pencil is not invariant under unitary transformations which leads to problems in the numerical treatment
[
9
]
. In
[
9
,
37
]
it is shown how we can achieve a more structured version if we introduce new variables

m_{j}:=\mu_{j}-\mu_{j+1}.

This reformulation yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix}0&E&0\\
A^{*}&Q&S\\
B^{*}&S^{*}&R\end{bmatrix}\sigma\begin{pmatrix}\mu\\
x\\
u\end{pmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&A&B\\
E^{*}&Q&S\\

with the corresponding matrix pencil

Kalman-Yakubovich-Popov Lemma

Consider the weighted system
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
and corresponding system space
\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
. In this section we relate positive semi-definiteness on the unit circle of the Popov function – a specific rational matrix function – to the solvability of a certain matrix inequality, namely the Kalman-Yakubovich-Popov inequality. We will see in Section
7
that positive semi-definiteness on the unit circle of the Popov function is necessary for feasibility of the optimal control problem (
9
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
be given. Consider
P=P^{*}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
and

\mathcal{M}(P):=\begin{bmatrix}A^{*}PA-E^{*}PE+Q&A^{*}PB+S\\
B^{*}PA+S^{*}&B^{*}PB+R\end{bmatrix}.

(15)

If
\mathcal{M}(P)\succeq_{\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}}\!0
, then
P
is called solution of the discrete-time
Kalman-Yakubovich-Popov (KYP)
inequality

\mathcal{M}(P)\succeq_{\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}}\!0,\qquad P^{*}=P.

(16)

Throughout this chapter we will make use of the system
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
being transformed to
(E_{F},\,A_{F},\,B_{F},\,Q_{F},\,S_{F},\,R_{F})
via feedback equivalence, i. e., we have invertible
W,\,T\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
and a feedback matrix
F\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n}
such that

\begin{matrix}E_{F}=WET,\qquad A_{F}=W(A+BF)T,\qquad B_{F}=WB,\\
Q_{F}=T^{*}(Q+SF+F^{*}S^{*}+F^{*}RF)T,\qquad S_{F}=T^{*}(S+F^{*}R),\qquad R_{F}=R.\end{matrix}

(17)

These transformations will allow us to extract an EDE formulation from the IDE problem.
The next results are crucial for the proof of the KYP Lemma in the IDE case and are mainly adaptions of the corresponding results in
[
36
, Section 4]
.

.

Let
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})

By using (

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}(zE-A)^{-1}B\\

I_{m}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:sysspace}}{=}

Structure of Palindromic Matrix Pencils

In this section we are concerned with palindromic matrix pencils
z\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{n\times n}
. For the investigation of these palindromic matrix pencils we first introduce so-called quasi-Hermitian matrices. Then we show characterizations of the inertia of palindromic matrix pencils similar to what was done in
[

in the case of so-called even matrix pencils.
The concept of quasi-Hermitian matrices is an extension to the notion of Hermitian and skew-Hermitian matrices. These are matrices
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
with the property
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}\mathcal{A}^{*}
for some
\omega\in[0,2\pi)
. They have the special property that every eigenvalue lies on the line with angle
\omega/2
through the origin.

We can extend the notion of inertia for Hermitian matrices to quasi-Hermitian matrices.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}\mathcal{A}^{*}
be quasi-Hermitian with
\omega\in[0,2\pi)
. Then the inertia of
\mathcal{A}
along
\frac{\omega}{2}
is

\operatorname{In}(\mathcal{A}):=\operatorname{In}_{\frac{\omega}{2}}(\mathcal{A}):=(n_{+},\,n_{0},\,n_{-}),

where
n_{+}
,
n_{0}
, and
n_{-}
denote the number of eigenvalues
\lambda=r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\omega}{2}}
where
r
is positive, zero, or negative, respectively. We omit the subscript
\frac{\omega}{2}
in
\operatorname{In}_{\frac{\omega}{2}}(\mathcal{A})
if the angle is clear from the context.

Similar to the Hermitian case, also in the quasi-Hermitian case we have a canonical form under congruence transformations.

Theorem 4.2
.

The inertia of a quasi-Hermitian matrix is invariant under congruence transformations. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}

\omega\in[0,2\pi)
, then there exists some invertible

such that

i. e.,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are congruent.

See
[
16
]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
be given with corresponding Popov function
\Phi(z)\in\mathbb{K}(z)^{m\times m}
and
\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{K}(z)}\Phi(z)=q
for some
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Assume that
(E,\,A,\,B)
has no uncontrollable modes on the unit circle. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

The Popov function
\Phi(z)
is positive semi-definite on the unit circle, i. e.,
\Phi(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega})\succeq 0
for all
\omega\in[0,2\pi)
.

(b)

The following conditions for the PKCF of
z\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}
as in (
21
) hold:

(i)

There are no blocks of type P2 corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta},\,\theta\neq 0
, and all blocks of type P3 have negative sign-characteristic.

(ii)

The number of blocks of type P2 corresponding to an eigenvalue
\lambda=1
with positive sign-characteristic is greater by
q
than the number of those with negative sign-characteristic.

(c)

The following conditions for the PKCF of
z\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}
as in (
21
) hold:

(i’)

There are no blocks of type P2 corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta},\,\theta\neq 0
.

(ii’)

The number of blocks of type P2 corresponding to an eigenvalue
\lambda=1
with positive sign-characteristic is greater by
q
than the number of those with negative sign-characteristic.

Proof.

The strategy of the proof is similar to the one in
[
41
, Theorem 3.4.2]
for the continuous-time case. First note that since
(E,\,A,\,B)
has no uncontrollable modes on the unit circle we can find a feedback matrix
F\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n}
such that
zE-(A+BF)\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{n\times n}
has no eigenvalues on the unit circle. Then by Lemma
3.3
and the fact that the palindromic pencil
z\mathcal{A}_{F}^{*}-\mathcal{A}_{F}
corresponding to
(E,\,A+BF,\,B)
is connected to
z\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}
via
\mathcal{A}_{F}=U_{F}^{*}\mathcal{A}U_{F}
, where

U_{F}:=\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}&0&0\\
0&I_{n}&0\\
0&F&I_{m}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{K}^{(2n+m)\times(2n+m)},

we can assume without loss of generality that
(E,\,A)
has no eigenvalues on the unit circle.

Now we show that
(a)
implies
(b)
. Therefore, assume that
\Phi(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega})\succeq 0
for all
\omega\in[0,2\pi)
. Then in particular we have

\operatorname{In}(\Phi(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}))=\left(q-a(\omega),m-q+a(\omega),0\right)

for all
\omega\in(0,2\pi)
, where
a:(0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
is some function which is zero for almost all
\omega\in(0,2\pi)
. Hence, by Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
4.2
we obtain

\operatorname{In}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}\right)=\left(n+q-a(\omega),m-q+a(\omega),n\right)

for
\omega\in(0,2\pi)
. Again, by Theorem
4.2
the inertia of
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}
coincides with the inertia of the PKCF of
z\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}
as in (
21
) evaluated at
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}
. Since by Theorem
4.3
the block structure of the PKCF is uniquely determined, we can proceed by identifying blocks by their inertia patterns.

Note that
\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{K}(z)}(z\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A})=2n+q
, since
z\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}
can only have a finite amount of rank drops and due to Lemma
5.1
and the regularity of
zE-A
there exist infinitely many values
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
for which
\operatorname{rk}(\lambda\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A})=2n+q
.
From Lemma
4.6
we can infer that we have exactly
2n+m-(2n+q)=m-q
blocks of type P5 in the PKCF of
z\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}
, since these are the only rank deficient blocks.

Thus, since
\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A})=2r
, where
r:=\operatorname{rk}\left[E-A\kern 5.0ptB\right]
, the number of blocks of type P2 or P4 corresponding to an eigenvalue
\lambda=1
is exactly
2(n-r)+q
.
Then, removing the blocks of type P5 from the PKCF of
z\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}
yields a matrix pencil
z\mathcal{A}^{*}_{1}-\mathcal{A}_{1}\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{(2n_{1}+q)\times(2n_{1}+q)}
in PKCF with full normal rank and inertia

\operatorname{In}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}\mathcal{A}^{*}_{1}-\mathcal{A}_{1})=\left(n_{1}+q-a(\omega),a(\omega),n_{1}\right)

on
(0,2\pi)
.
Then, by Lemma
4.6
, there are
q
blocks of type P2 with corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda=1
and positive sign-characteristic, since these are
present in every combination of blocks with an inertia pattern of the form

(k+1,0,k)

independent of
\omega>0
. Removing these blocks leads to the pencil
z\mathcal{A}^{*}_{2}-\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{2n_{2}\times 2n_{2}}
in PKCF with inertia

\operatorname{In}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}\mathcal{A}^{*}_{2}-\mathcal{A}_{2})=\left(n_{2}-a(\omega),a(\omega),n_{2}\right)

on
(0,2\pi)
.
Furthermore, from Lemma
4.6
we deduce that there are no blocks of type P2 corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta},\,\theta\neq 0
. Thus, all blocks of type P3 have negative sign-characteristic, since these are
the only blocks with an inertia pattern of the form

(k-1,1,k)

for exactly one value of
\omega>0
. This shows statement
(b)(i)
.
Removing these blocks, we obtain a matrix pencil
z\mathcal{A}^{*}_{3}-\mathcal{A}_{3}\in\mathbb{K}[z]^{2n_{3}\times 2n_{3}}
in PKCF with inertia

\operatorname{In}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}\mathcal{A}^{*}_{3}-\mathcal{A}_{3})=\left(n_{3},0,n_{3}\right)

on
(0,2\pi)
.
The inertia pattern of
z\mathcal{A}_{3}^{*}-\mathcal{A}_{3}
together with Lemma
4.6

reveals that the remaining blocks of type P2 corresponding to an eigenvalue
\lambda=1
are split up equally into those with positive and those with negative sign-characteristic. This shows
(b)(ii)
and thus statement
(b)
.

The proof that
(c)
follows from
(b)
is clear, since condition
(c)(i’)
follows immediately from condition
(b)(i)
and conditions
(b)(ii)
and
(c)(ii’)
coincide.

Now let the conditions
(c)(i’)
, and
(c)(ii’)
hold.
Again, by Lemma
5.1

and Theorem
4.2
, for
\omega\in(0,2\pi)
we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{In}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{A}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(n,0,n\right)+\operatorname{In}\left(\Phi(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(n+m_{1}-a_{1}(\omega),m-m_{1}-m_{2}+a_{1}(\omega)+a_{2}(\omega),n+m_{2}-a_{2}(\omega)\right)

and functions
a_{i}:(0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
i=1,2
, which are zero for almost all
\omega\in(0,2\pi)
such that
m_{1}+m_{2}=q
.
We now have to show that
m_{2}=0=a_{2}(\omega)
. Then the blocks of type P2 with positive sign-characteristic are the only ones leading to an inertia pattern of the form

(k+1,0,k)

for
\omega>\theta
. The only blocks that could compensate the additional positive eigenvalue for
\omega>\theta
are
blocks of type P2 with negative sign-characteristic. By condition
(c)(i’)
we are only allowed to take such blocks with
\theta=0
. By condition
(c)(ii’)
then we obtain that
n+m_{1}=(n+m_{2})+q
and thus
m_{2}=0
,
m_{1}=q
.
Hence, we have

\operatorname{In}(\Phi(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega}))=\left(q-a_{1}(\omega),m-q+a_{1}(\omega)+a_{2}(\omega),-a_{2}(\omega)\right).

Since the inertia of a quasi-Hermitian matrix by definition is a triple of non-negative integers, this implies
a_{2}\equiv 0
and thus
\Phi(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega})\succeq 0
for all
\omega\in(0,2\pi)
. Then, by continuity, we also have that
{\Phi(1)\succeq 0}
.

6
Lur’e Equations

In this section we characterize solvability of Lur’e equations for explicit as well as for implicit difference equations in a similar way as in
[
36
]
for continuous-time systems.
Finding a solution of the Lur’e equation means finding
X=X^{*}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
,
K\in\mathbb{K}^{q\times n}
, and
L\in\mathbb{K}^{q\times m}
such that

\mathcal{M}(X)=\begin{bmatrix}A^{*}XA-E^{*}XE+Q&A^{*}XB+S\\
B^{*}XA+S^{*}&B^{*}XB+R\end{bmatrix}=_{\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}}\begin{bmatrix}K^{*}\\
L^{*}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}K&L\end{bmatrix},

(25)

where
q:=\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{K}(z)}\Phi(z)
.

If
X
is a solution of the KYP inequality (
16
), then we can always find
K\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times n}
and
L\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times m}
for some
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that (
25
) holds.
The next result shows that for such solutions it holds that
p\geq q
. Thus, in other words, we are interested in the existence of solutions of (
25
) with minimal rank
q
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
be given and let
q=\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{K}(z)}\Phi(z)
. Further, let

(X,\,K,\,L)\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{q\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{q\times m}

be a solution of the Lur’e equation (
25
) and assume, that for
M\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times n}
and
N\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times m}
also the triple
(X,\,M,\,N)
fulfills (
25
).
Then we have
q\leq p
and

\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{K}(z)}\begin{bmatrix}zE-A&-B\\
(z-1)K&(z-1)L\end{bmatrix}=n+q.

(26)

Proof.

See
[
2
, Proposition 5.1]
.
∎

Note that in the continuous-time case
[
36
]
instead of rank minimality the condition (

) was used to define solutions fo the Lur’e equation (

7
Application to Optimal Control

In this section we discuss the structure of solutions of the discrete-time optimal control problem (
9
) corresponding to the system
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
based on the results from the previous subsection. First we show relations between so-called stabilizing solutions of the Lur’e equation (
25
) and feasibility of the optimal control problem. Then we show characterizations for the existence and uniqueness of the optimal control.

7.1
Stabilizing solutions

In this subsection we state several discrete-time versions of results from
[
17
]
. If not explicitly stated otherwise, these results can be proven analogously, i. e., by using the same algebraic transformations and the identical properties of the solution operators.

Definition 7.1

(Stabilizing solution)
.

Let
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
be given and assume that a solution
(X,\,K,\,L)\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{p\times n}\times\mathbb{K}^{p\times m}
of the corresponding Lur’e equation (
25
) exists.
Then
(X,\,K,\,L)
is also called
stabilizing
solution if in addition it holds

\operatorname{rk}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda E-A&-B\\
K&L\end{bmatrix}=n+q

(49)

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|\geq 1
.

The following is an adaptation of
[
17
, Proposition 6.4(b)]
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
(E,\,A,\,B)\in\Sigma_{m,n}(\mathbb{K})
be I-controllable. If
for every
\varepsilon>0
and every
x^{0}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
there exists a
{\lparen\,x,\,u\,\rparen}\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}\cap(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{K}^{n})\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{K}^{m}))
such that
Ex_{0}=Ex^{0}
and

\|Kx+Lu\|_{\ell^{2}}<\varepsilon,

then we have

\operatorname{rk}\begin{bmatrix}E&A&B\\
0&K&L\end{bmatrix}=\operatorname{rk}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda E-A&-B\\
K&L\end{bmatrix}

(50)

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|>1
.

Proof.

The major part of the proof is completely analogous to the continuous-time case, since only algebraic operations and the linearity of the shift operator is used.
The basic idea is that the relation (
50
) is equivalent to the fact that the Kronecker canonical form of the matrix pencil

\begin{bmatrix}zE-A&-B\\
K&L\end{bmatrix}

consists only of blocks of type K1 with
|\lambda|\leq 1
and blocks of type K4 of size
1\times 0

[
17
, Remark 2.8(b)]
. Also note, that due to the block structure of the KCF we have that for every
\varepsilon>0
and every
v^{j,0}\in\mathbb{C}^{k_{j}}
and corresponding block
K_{j}(z)=zF_{j}-G_{j}
in the KCF there exists
v^{j}\in\ell_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{k_{j}})
such that

F_{j}v_{0}^{j}=F_{j}v^{j,0},\quad\lim_{i\to\infty}F_{j}v^{j}_{i}=0,\quad\left\|F_{j}\sigma v^{j}-G_{j}v^{j}\right\|_{\ell_{2}}<\varepsilon.

Here we show, that all blocks of type K1 fulfill
|\lambda|\leq 1
. To this end we thus proof the following fact: Let a block of the KCF of the form
zF-G:=K(z)=zI_{k}-(\lambda I_{k}+N_{k})\in\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}[z]^{k\times k}
be given. If for any
v^{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
and any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
v\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{k})
such that
v_{0}=v^{0}
and
\|\sigma v-Gv\|<\varepsilon
, then
|\lambda|\leq 1
.

For the sake of a contradiction, assume that
|\lambda|>1
. Then the eigenvalue
\frac{1}{\lambda}
of
G^{-1}
fulfills
\frac{1}{|\lambda|}<1
and thus there exists positive definite
P
such that

P-G^{*}PG=-I_{k}.

Set
\tilde{\varepsilon}=(v^{0})^{*}Pv^{0}>0
and
\varepsilon:=\|(P+P^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}G^{-1}\|_{2}^{-2}\tilde{\varepsilon}
. Then for any
v^{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
and
w:=\sigma v-Gv
with
\|w\|^{2}_{\ell_{2}}<\varepsilon
one finds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{0}^{*}Pv_{0}-v_{j}^{*}Pv_{j}&=-\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}{\sigma(v_{k}^{*}Pv_{k})-v_{k}^{*}Pv_{k}}=-\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}{({Gv_{k}+w_{k})}^{*}P{(Gv_{k}+w_{k})}-v_{k}^{*}Pv_{k}}\\
&=-\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}{\begin{pmatrix}v_{k}\\
w_{k}\end{pmatrix}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}G^{*}PG-P&G^{*}P\\
PG&P\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_{k}\\
w_{k}\end{pmatrix}}\\
&=-\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}{\begin{pmatrix}v_{k}+(I+P)G^{-1}w_{k}\\
G^{-1}w_{k}\end{pmatrix}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&-P-P^{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_{k}+(I+P)G^{-1}w_{k}\\
G^{-1}w_{k}\end{pmatrix}}\\
&\leq\|(P+P^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}G^{-1}\|_{2}^{2}\|w\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}<\tilde{\varepsilon}\end{split}

and thus in the limit case
\tilde{\varepsilon}=\lim_{j\to\infty}v_{0}^{*}Pv_{0}-v_{j}^{*}Pv_{j}<\tilde{\varepsilon}
.
∎

.

Let

We characterize feasibility and structure of the optimal control problem with the existence of a stabilizing solution of the Lur’e equation. First we show that the existence of a stabilizing solution implies feasibility and an explicit characterization of the optimal value function
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})
for given
x^{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
be given with no uncontrollable modes on the unit circle and assume there exists a stabilizing solution

49

for all

and
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})=(x^{0})^{*}E^{*}XEx^{0}
.

Proof.

First, since
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
has no uncontrollable modes on the unit circle and
(X,\,K,\,L)
is a stabilizing solution we obtain that

\operatorname{rk}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda E-A&-B\end{bmatrix}+q=\operatorname{rk}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda E-A&-B\\
K&L\end{bmatrix}=n+q

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|\geq 1
. Thus,
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)
is stabilizable.

Let
x^{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
. Then, we have
{\lparen\,x,\,u\,\rparen}\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
with
Ex_{0}=Ex^{0}
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}Ex_{j}=0
. By the definition of the system space
\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n+m}
, we obtain
{\big\lparen\,x_{j}^{*}\;\;u_{j}^{*}\,\big\rparen}^{*}\in\mathcal{V}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Thus, for

we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&x_{j_{2}}^{*}E^{*}XEx_{j_{2}}-x_{j_{1}}^{*}E^{*}XEx_{j_{1}}=\sum_{k=j_{1}}^{j_{2}-1}{({Ax_{k}+Bu_{k})}^{*}X{(Ax_{k}+Bu_{k})}-x_{k}^{*}E^{*}XEx_{k}}\\
=&\sum_{k=j_{1}}^{j_{2}-1}{\begin{pmatrix}x_{k}\\
u_{k}\end{pmatrix}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}A^{*}XA-E^{*}XE&A^{*}XB\\

u_{k}\end{pmatrix}}\geq-\sum_{k=j_{1}}^{j_{2}-1}{\begin{pmatrix}x_{k}\\
u_{k}\end{pmatrix}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}Q&S\\
S^{*}&R\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{k}\\
u_{k}\end{pmatrix}}.\end{split}

(52)

For

we thus obtain for the objective function
\mathcal{J}(x,u)
that

x_{0}^{*}E^{*}XEx_{0}\leq\mathcal{J}(x,u)

and thus

x_{0}^{*}E^{*}XEx_{0}\leq\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})<\infty.

(53)

Furthermore, since
(X,\,K,\,L)
is a stabilizing solution of the Lur’e equation (
25
), for every
x^{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
and
{\lparen\,x,\,u\,\rparen}\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}\cap\left(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{K}^{n})\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{K}^{m})\right)
with

we obtain in (

\displaystyle\begin{split}-x_{0}^{*}E^{*}XEx_{0}=&\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\begin{pmatrix}x_{k}\\
u_{k}\end{pmatrix}^{*}\left(\mathcal{M}(X)-\begin{bmatrix}Q&S\\
S^{*}&R\end{bmatrix}\right)\begin{pmatrix}x_{k}\\
u_{k}\end{pmatrix}}\\
=&\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\begin{pmatrix}x_{k}\\
u_{k}\end{pmatrix}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}K^{*}\\
L^{*}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}K&L\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{k}\\
u_{k}\end{pmatrix}}-\mathcal{J}(x,u)\end{split}

and thus

x_{0}^{*}E^{*}XEx_{0}+\|Kx+Lu\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}=\mathcal{J}(x,u).

(54)

Since
(X,\,K,\,L)
is a stabilizing solution, using Lemma

for all

.
∎

Next we show, that the opposite implication is also true, i. e., that feasibility implies the existence of a stabilizing solution of the Lur’e equation.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
(E,\,A,\,B,\,Q,\,S,\,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
be given with no uncontrollable modes on the unit circle. Assume that the optimal control problem is feasible, i. e.,
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a stabilizing solution of the Lur’e equation.

Proof.

First we have to show, that
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})=(Ex^{0})^{*}XEx^{0}
for some Hermitian
X\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
. This can be done in an analogous way as in
[
41
, Theorem 3.8.3]
.
With similar steps as in
[
41
, Theorem 3.8.3]
we can also show, that
X
solves the KYP inequality (
16
).

It remains to show, that
X
also induces a stabilizing solution of the Lur’e equation. Since
X
solves the KYP inequality there exist
\tilde{K}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times n}
and
\tilde{L}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times m}
such that (
25
) and (
51
) hold. Thus, by (
54
) we obtain that for every
x^{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
{\lparen\,x,\,u\,\rparen}\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,\,A,\,B)}
such that
\|\tilde{K}x+\tilde{L}u\|_{\ell^{2}}<\varepsilon
. Then, by Lemma
7.3
we also obtain a stabilizing solution of the Lur’e equation.
∎

7.3
Existence and Uniqueness of Optimal Controls

In this section we discuss conditions for the existence and uniqueness of optimal controls. Based on the considerations
of the previous subsections we pose these conditions in terms of the zero dynamics
\mathfrak{ZD}_{(E,\,A,\,B,\,K,\,L)}

\left[\begin{smallmatrix}zE-A&-B\\

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
(E,A,B,Q,S,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})
be I-controllable and assume that

(a)

For every

with
Ex_{0}=Ex^{0}
such that
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})=\mathcal{J}(x,u)
if and only if
\mathfrak{ZD}_{(E,\,A,\,B,\,K,\,L)}
is strongly stabilizable.

(b)

For every
x^{0}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
there exists a unique trajectory
(x,u)\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,A,B)}
with
Ex_{0}=Ex^{0}
such that
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})=\mathcal{J}(x,u)
if and only if
\mathfrak{ZD}_{(E,\,A,\,B,\,K,\,L)}
is strongly asymptotically stable.

Proof.

(a)

In view of (
54
), we see that for every
x^{0}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
there exists a trajectory
(x,u)\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,A,B)}
with
Ex_{0}=Ex^{0}
such that
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})=\mathcal{J}(x,u)
, if and only for each
x^{0}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
there exists a
(x,u)\in\mathfrak{ZD}_{(E,\,A,\,B,\,K,\,L)}(x^{0})
with
\lim_{j\to\infty}Ex_{j}=0
. Using Proposition
2.15
(b), this is equivalent to
\mathfrak{ZD}_{(E,\,A,\,B,\,K,\,L)}
being strongly stabilizable.

(b)

For every
x^{0}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
there exists a unique trajectory
(x,u)\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,A,B)}
with
Ex_{0}=Ex^{0}
such that
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})=\mathcal{J}(x,u)
, if and only for each
x^{0}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
there exists a unique
(x,u)\in\mathfrak{ZD}_{(E,\,A,\,B,\,K,\,L)}(x^{0})
with
\lim_{j\to\infty}Ex_{j}=0
. With Proposition
2.15
(d), this is equivalent to
\mathfrak{ZD}_{(E,\,A,\,B,\,K,\,L)}
being strongly asymptotically stable.

∎

Using the results of Proposition
2.15
, we directly obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 7.8
.

Let
(E,A,B,Q,S,R)\in\Sigma_{m,n}^{w}(\mathbb{K})

For every

(x,u)\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,A,B)}
with

For every

(x,u)\in\mathfrak{B}_{(E,A,B)}
with
Ex_{0}=Ex^{0}
such that
\mathcal{W}_{+}(Ex^{0})=\mathcal{J}(x,u)

\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{R}(\lambda)=n+m
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|\geq 1

\mathcal{R}(z)

8
Conclusions and Outlook

We have discussed several problems arising in the discrete-time linear-quadratic optimal control problem and we have seen their relations to the results that have been obtained in the continuous-time setting. In Section
3
we have discussed an extension of the Kalman-Yakubovich-Popov inequality for standard difference equations to the case of implicit difference equations. The characterizations are analogous to what was obtained in
[
41
,
36
]
in the continuous-time case. Nonetheless, some more technical difficulties had to be tackled. For an analogous relaxation of the controllability assumption to sign-controllability we would need the discrete-time analog of of
[
11
, Theorem 6.1]
.

In Section
4
we further related the spectral properties of the palindromic pencil associated to the discrete-time optimal control problem (
9
) to the positivity of the Popov function on the unit circle. To this end, we introduced the notion of quasi-Hermitian matrices which allows for a generalization of the concept of inertia.

In Section
6
we introduced Lur’e equations for explicit as well as for implicit difference equations. We have shown that solvability of these equations is equivalent to the existence of certain deflating subspaces of the BVD and palindromic pencil arising in the discrete-time control problem (
9
). In the palindromic case we needed the additional assumption that the given system is controllable at the eigenvalue one, which can always be achieved for discrete-time systems originating from discretization. It is an open question whether this condition can be dropped if the latter is not the case.

In Section
7
we have seen how we can use these results to characterize feasibility of the optimal control problem as well as existence and uniqueness of optimal controls. Furthermore, we have shown how the deflating subspaces are related to the solutions of the related two-point boundary value problems.

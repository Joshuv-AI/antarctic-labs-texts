Constraint energy minimizing generalized multiscale finite element method for convection diffusion equation
Lina Zhao, Eric Chung

Abstract
In this paper we present and analyze a constraint energy minimizing generalized multiscale finite element method for convection diffusion equation. To define the multiscale basis functions, we first build an auxiliary multiscale space by solving local spectral problems motivated by analysis. Then constraint energy minimization performed in oversampling domains is exploited to construct the multiscale space. The resulting multiscale basis functions have a good decay property even for high contrast diffusion and convection coefficients. Furthermore, if the number of oversampling layer is chosen properly, we can prove that the convergence rate is proportional to the coarse mesh size. Our analysis also indicates that the size of the oversampling domain weakly depends on the contrast of the heterogeneous coefficients. Several numerical experiments are presented illustrating the performances of our method.



Keywords:

Multiscale method, convection diffusion equation, local multiscale basis function, local spectral problem

1
Introduction

In this paper we consider the following convection diffusion equation: Find
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\begin{split}-\nabla\cdot(\kappa\nabla u)+\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla u&=f\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,\\
u&=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\partial\Omega,\end{split}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the computational domain and
\bm{\beta}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{2}
. We assume that both
\kappa
and
\bm{\beta}
are heterogeneous coefficients with multiple scales and very high contrast, in addition, the velocity field
\bm{\beta}
is incompressible, i.e.,
\nabla\cdot\bm{\beta}=0
. Further, we assume that there exist
\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1}
such that
\kappa_{0}\leq\kappa\leq\kappa_{1}
, where
\kappa_{1}/\kappa_{0}
could be large. Moreover, we let
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{0}
represent the supremum and minimum of
|\bm{\beta}|
over
\Omega
, respectively. For simplicity, we assume that
\beta_{0}\geq 1
and
\kappa_{0}\geq 1
.

There are a large number of works devoted to solving the convection diffusion equation (
1.1
). This problem becomes even harder to solve when the Péclet number is large. To overcome this issue, numerous multiscale methods have been developed such as variational multiscale method
[
20
,
21
,
26
,
29
]
, multiscale finite element method
[
25
]
, multiscale discontinuous Galerkin method
[
22
,
13
]
, heterogeneous multiscale method
[
18
]
,
variational multiscale stabilization
[
23
]
and multiscale stabilization
[
1
,
10
]
. The aforementioned methods are based on special construction of basis functions, which typically resolve fine scale information on relatively coarse meshes.

In this paper, our purpose is to study constraint energy minimizing generalized multiscale finite element method (CEM-GMsFEM) for convection diffusion equation. CEM-GMsFEM is based on GMsFEM
[
14
,
7
,
11
,
15
,
6
,
12
,
2
]
and have been successfully applied to a wide range of partial differential equations
[
9
,
24
,
27
,
4
,
3
,
16
,
17
]
.
The key steps of CEM-GMsFEM used in this paper can be summarized as follows. First, we need to build an auxiliary space. Specially, we define a suitable spectral problem over each coarse cell, and the first few eigenfunctions corresponding to small eigenvalues that contain important features about the multiscale coefficients
\kappa
and
\bm{\beta}
are used in the definition of the local auxiliary multiscale space. Second, we solve an appropriate energy subject to some constraints over the oversampling domain by using the local auxiliary multiscale space. We emphasize that the choice of spectral problem is very important and can ensure the good performances of the method. We prove that the multiscale basis functions are localizable. In addition, we prove the convergence rate
H/\Lambda
if the number of oversampling layer is chosen suitably, where
\Lambda
is the minimal eigenvalue that the corresponding eigenvector is not included in the space. Our analysis also shows that the size of the oversampling domain depends on the contrast of the heterogeneous coefficients weakly (logarithmically). We present several numerical experiments to verify the performances of CEM-GMsFEM. In particular, we exploit one example where velocity is obtained by solving Darcy law with SPE benchmark heterogeneous field.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we provide some preliminaries. Then in section
3
, we present in detail the construction of our multiscale basis functions. Specially, we introduce the spectral problem that is used to define the auxiliary multiscale basis and the energy minimization that will be used to construct the multiscale space. The decay property of the multiscale basis function and the error estimates are presented in section
4
. Several numerical experiments are carried out in section
5
to test the performances of our method. Finally, a conclusion is given.

\mathcal{T}_{H}

\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Note that
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is a refinement of the coarse grid
\mathcal{T}_{H}
with the meshsize
h
. It is assumed that the fine grid is fine enough to resolve the solution. Here we use triangular grid as the fine grid and extension to other shapes of grids such as quadrilateral is straightforward. For each coarse element, we define an oversampling domain
K_{i,m}\subset\Omega
by enlarging
K_{i}
by
m
coarse grid layers, where
m\geq 1
is an integer, see Figure
1

The weak solution of (

3
Construction of multiscale basis function

In this section we present the construction of the multiscale basis functions. To this end, we first define the auxiliary space which is obtained by solving local spectral problems. Then we solve an appropriate energy subject to some constraints over the oversampling domain, which delivers the multiscale space.

Let
K_{i}
be the
i
-th coarse block and let
V(K_{i})
be the restriction of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
on
K_{i}
. We define a local spectral problem which is defined as follows: Find
\lambda_{j}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi_{j}^{(i)}\in V(K_{i})
such that

a_{i}(\phi_{j}^{(i)},v)=\lambda_{j}^{(i)}s_{i}(\phi_{j}^{(i)},v)\quad\forall v\in V(K_{i}).

(3.1)

We remark that the above problem is solved on the fine mesh in the actual computations. According to our analysis, we can choose

\displaystyle a_{i}(u,v)=\int_{K_{i}}\kappa\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\;dx,\quad s_{i}(u,v)=\int_{K_{i}}H^{-2}\kappa|\bm{\beta}|^{2}uv\;dx.

(3.2)

We let
\lambda_{j}^{(i)}
be the eigenvalues of (
3.1
) arranged in ascending order. We will use the first
l_{i}
eigenfunctions to construct our local auxiliary multiscale space
V_{aux}^{(i)}
, where
V_{aux}^{(i)}=\text{span}\{\phi_{j}^{(i)}|j\leq l_{i}\}
. The global auxiliary multiscale space
V_{aux}
is the sum of these local auxiliary multiscale space, namely
V_{aux}=\oplus_{i=1}^{N}V_{aux}^{(i)}
. We will use this space to construct the multiscale basis functions which are
\phi
-orthogonal to the auxiliary space defined above.

For the local auxiliary space
V_{aux}^{(i)}
, the bilinear form
s_{i}
in (
3.2
) defines an inner product with norm
\|v\|_{s(K_{i})}=s_{i}(v,v)^{1/2}
. These local inner products and norms provide natural definitions of inner product and norm for the global auxiliary multiscale space
V_{aux}
, which are defined by

\displaystyle s(v,w)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}s_{i}(v,w),\quad\|v\|_{s}=s(v,v)^{1/2}\quad\forall v,w\in V_{aux}.

We note that
s(v,w)
and
\|v\|_{s}
are also an inner product and norm for the space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Using the above inner product, we can define the notion of
\phi
-orthogonality in the space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
(cf.
[
8
]
). Given a function
\phi_{j}^{(i)}\in V_{aux}
, we say that a function
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is
\phi_{j}^{(i)}
-orthogonal if

\displaystyle s(\psi,\phi_{j}^{(i)})=1,\quad s(\psi,\phi_{j^{\prime}}^{(i^{\prime})})=0\quad\mbox{if}\;j^{\prime}\neq j,i^{\prime}\neq i.

Now, we define
\pi_{i}:L^{2}(K_{i})\rightarrow V_{aux}^{(i)}
to be the projection with respect to the inner product
s_{i}(v,w)
. More precisely, it is defined by

\displaystyle\pi_{i}(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}\frac{s_{i}(u,\phi_{j}^{(i)})}{s_{i}(\phi_{j}^{(i)},\phi_{j}^{(i)})}\phi_{j}^{(i)}\quad\forall u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

In addition, we let
\pi
be the projection with respect to the inner product
s(v,w)
. Hence,
\pi
is defined by

\displaystyle\pi(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}\frac{s_{i}(u,\phi_{j}^{(i)})}{s_{i}(\phi_{j}^{(i)},\phi_{j}^{(i)})}\phi_{j}^{(i)}\quad\forall u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

which satisfies
\pi(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}(u)
.

We now present the construction of our multiscale basis functions. For each coarse element
K_{i}
and an oversampling domain
K_{i,m}\subset\Omega
, we define the multiscale basis function
\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\in V_{0}(K_{i,m})
by

\displaystyle\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}=\mbox{argmin}\{a(\psi,\psi)\;|\;\psi\in V_{0}(K_{i,m}),\quad\psi\;\mbox{is}\;\phi_{j}^{(i)}-\mbox{orthogonal}\},

(3.3)

where
V(K_{i,m})
is the restriction of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
in
K_{i,m}
and
V_{0}(K_{i,m})
is the subspace of
V(K_{i,m})
with zero trace on
\partial K_{i,m}
. Our multiscale finite element space
V_{ms}
is defined by

\displaystyle V_{ms}=\mbox{span}\{\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\;|\;1\leq j\leq l_{i},1\leq i\leq N\}.

The existence of the solution of the minimization problem (
3.3
) will be proved in Lemma
4.2
. Moreover, we illustrate the importance of the auxiliary space on the decay of the multiscale basis functions. Here we take
H=1/10
,
\kappa=1
and
\bm{\beta}=(\cos(18\pi y)\sin(18\pi x),-\cos(18\pi x)\sin(18\pi y))^{T}
, where
\bm{\beta}
is a highly oscillatory vectorized function and its profile is shown in Figure
2
. In Figure
3
, we display the first four nonzero eigenvalues obtained from solving the local spectral problem (
3.1
), and a multiscale basis function with one eigenfunction and four eigenfunctions in the local auxiliary space. We can observe that if enough number of eigenfunctions are exploited in solving the energy minimization problem, then the multiscale basis functions have a fast decay outside of the coarse block.

With the above preparations, the multiscale solution
u_{ms}
is defined as the solution of the following problem: Find
u_{ms}\in V_{ms}
such that

\displaystyle A(u_{ms},v)=(f,v)\quad\forall v\in V_{ms}.

(3.4)

Remark 3.1
.

The minimization problem (
3.3
) is implicit, we can recast it into the explicit form by introducing the lagrange multiplier. The equivalent explicit form reads as follows: Find
\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\in V_{0}(K_{i,m}),\lambda\in V_{aux}^{(i)}(K_{i,m})
such that

\displaystyle a(\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)},p)+s(p,\lambda)

\displaystyle=0\quad\forall p\in V_{0}(K_{i,m}),

\displaystyle s(\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}-\phi_{j}^{(i)},q)

\displaystyle=0\quad\forall q\in V_{aux}^{(i)}(K_{i,m}),

where
V_{aux}^{(i)}(K_{i,m})
is the union of all local auxiliary spaces for
K_{j}\subset K_{i,m}
. One can numerically solve the above continuous problem on fine scale mesh.

Profile of the first component of
\bm{\beta}
(left) and the second component of
\bm{\beta}
(right).

A plot of the first four nonzero eigenvalues (left), a multiscale basis function using one eigenfunction in each local auxiliary space (middle) and a multiscale basis function using 4 eigenfunctions in each local auxiliary space (right).

The local multiscale basis construction is motivated by the global basis construction defined below. Since the global multiscale basis functions will be exploited for later analysis, we present its construction here. The global multiscale basis function
\psi_{j}^{(i)}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is defined by

\displaystyle\psi_{j}^{(i)}=\textnormal{argmin}\{a(\psi,\psi)\;|\;\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\psi\;\mbox{is}\;\phi_{j}^{(i)}-\textnormal{orthogonal}\},

(3.5)

Thereby, the global multiscale finite element space
V_{glo}
is defined by

\displaystyle V_{glo}=\textnormal{span}\{\psi_{j}^{(i)}\;|\;1\leq j\leq l_{i},1\leq i\leq N\}.

To facilitate later analysis, we define the projection
\tilde{u}\in V_{glo}
of
u
by

a(\tilde{u},v)=a(u,v)\quad\forall v\in V_{glo},

(3.6)

where
a(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\;dx
. The above problem is well posed thanks to the Lax-Milgram lemma.
Notice that we have
V=V_{glo}\oplus\tilde{V}
, where
\tilde{V}
is the kernel of the projection
\pi:L^{2}\rightarrow V_{aux}
with respect to the inner product
s(\cdot,\cdot)
.
Interested readers can refer to
[
8
]
for more discussions regarding this.
We can infer from (
3.6
) that
u-u_{glo}\in V_{glo}^{\perp}=\tilde{V}
, thereby
\pi(u-\tilde{u})=0
.

4
Analysis

In this section we first prove the convergence for the projection defined in (
3.6
), which will be served as an intermediate tool for the convergence analysis of the multiscale solution. Then we show the decay property of the multiscale basis functions. Finally, we prove the convergence of the multiscale solution. Before proving the convergence of the proposed method, we introduce some notations that will be used later. We define
a
-norm by
\|u\|_{a}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}\;dx
. For a given subdomain
\Omega_{i}\subset\Omega
, we define the local
a
-norm and
s
-norm by
\|u\|_{a(\Omega_{i})}^{2}=\int_{\Omega_{i}}\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}\;dx
and
\|u\|_{s(\Omega_{i})}^{2}=H^{-2}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\kappa|\bm{\beta}|^{2}u^{2}\;dx
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u
be the solution of (
2.1
) and
\tilde{u}
satisfy (
3.6
). We have
u-\tilde{u}\in\tilde{V}
and

\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{a}\leq H\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big),

where
\Lambda=\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\lambda_{l_{i}+1}^{(i)}
.

Proof.

We have from (
3.6
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\begin{split}\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{a}^{2}&=a(u-\tilde{u},u-\tilde{u})=a(u,u-\tilde{u})=(f,u-\tilde{u})-(\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla u,u-\tilde{u})\\
&\leq\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|(u-\tilde{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|(u-\tilde{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq(H\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+H\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{s}.\end{split}

Since
\pi(u-\tilde{u})=0
, we have from the spectral problem (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{s}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{s(K_{i})}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|(I-\pi_{i})(u-\tilde{u})\|_{s(K_{i})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\Lambda}\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{a}^{2}.

Therefore, the following estimate holds

\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{a}\leq H\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big).

∎

The above lemma shows the convergence of the projection (cf. (
3.6
)) defined by using global multiscale basis functions. Next, we are going to show that the global multiscale basis functions are localizable. For this purpose, we introduce some concepts that will be used later. For each coarse block
K_{i}
, we define
B
to be a bubble function and
B\mid_{\tau}=\frac{\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\varphi_{3}}{27},\forall\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(K_{i})
, where
\varphi_{i}
is barycentric coordinate and
\mathcal{T}_{h}(K_{i})
denotes the collection of fine grids restricted to
K_{i}
, and more information regarding the bubble function
B
can be found in
[
28
]
.
We define the constant by

\displaystyle C_{\pi}=\sup_{K_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{H},\mu\in V_{aux}}\frac{\int_{K_{i}}|\bm{\beta}|^{2}\mu^{2}\;dx}{\int_{K_{i}}|\bm{\beta}|^{2}B\mu^{2}\;dx}.

The following lemma considers the following minimization problem defined on a coarse block
K_{i}

\displaystyle v=\text{argmin}\{a(\psi,\psi)\;|\;\psi\in V_{0}(K_{i}),\quad s_{i}(\psi,v_{aux})=1,\quad s_{i}(\psi,w)=0\quad\forall w\in V_{aux}^{\perp}\}.

(4.1)

for a given
v_{aux}\in V_{aux}^{(i)}
with
\|v_{aux}\|_{s(K_{i})}=1
, where
v_{aux}^{\perp}\subset V_{aux}^{(i)}
is the orthogonal complement of
\text{span}\{v_{aux}\}
with respect to the inner product
s_{i}
.

The next lemma shows the existence of the solution to the minimization problem (
4.1
), which follows similar line to that of
[
8
]
. We also provide the proof here for the readers’ convenience.

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
v_{aux}\in V_{aux}
there exists a function
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\pi(v)=v_{aux},\quad\|v\|_{a}^{2}\leq C\|v_{aux}\|_{s}^{2},\quad supp(v)\subset supp(v_{aux}).

Proof.

Let
v_{aux}\in V_{aux}^{(i)}
. The minimization problem (
4.1
) is equivalent to the following variational problem: Find
v\in V_{0}(K_{i})
and
\mu\in V_{aux}^{(i)}
such that

\displaystyle a_{i}(v,w)+s_{i}(w,\mu)

\displaystyle=0\quad\forall w\in V_{0}(K_{i}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle s_{i}(v,\phi)

\displaystyle=s_{i}(v_{aux},\phi)\quad\forall\phi\in V_{aux}^{(i)}.

(4.3)

Note that, the well-posedness of the minimization problem (
4.1
) is equivalent to the existence of a function
v\in V_{0}(K_{i})
such that

\displaystyle s_{i}(v,v_{aux})\geq C\|v_{aux}\|_{s}^{2},\quad\|v\|_{a(K_{i})}\leq C\|v_{aux}\|_{s(K_{i})},

where
C
is independent of the meshsize but possibly depends on the problem parameters.

Note that
v_{aux}
is supported in
K_{i}
. We let
v=Bv_{aux}
, it then follows from the definition of
s_{i}
that

\displaystyle s_{i}(v,v_{aux})=H^{-2}\int_{K_{i}}\kappa|\bm{\beta}|^{2}Bv_{aux}^{2}\geq C_{\pi}^{-1}\|v_{aux}\|_{s(K_{i})}^{2}.

Since
\nabla(Bv_{aux})=v_{aux}\nabla B+B\nabla v_{aux}
,
|B|\leq 1
and
|\nabla B|^{2}\leq CH^{-2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{a(K_{i})}^{2}=\|Bv_{aux}\|_{a(K_{i})}^{2}\leq C\|v\|_{a(K_{i})}\Big(\|v_{aux}\|_{a(K_{i})}+\|v_{aux}\|_{s(K_{i})}\Big).

Finally, using the spectral problem (
3.1
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\|v_{aux}\|_{a(K_{i})}\leq(\max_{1\leq j\leq l_{i}}\lambda_{j}^{(i)})\|v_{aux}\|_{s(K_{i})}.

This proves the unique solvability of the minimization problem (
4.1
). So
v
and
v_{aux}
satisfy (
4.2
)-(
4.3
). Then we can obtain
\pi_{i}(v)=v_{aux}
from (
4.3
). Therefore, the preceding arguments complete the proof.

∎

Next, we will show that the multiscale basis functions have a decay property. To this end, we define the cutoff function with respect to the oversampling domains.
For each
K_{i}
, we recall that
K_{i,m}
is the oversampling coarse region by
enlarging
K_{i}
by
m
coarse grid layers. For
M>m
, we
define
\chi_{i}^{M,m}\in\mbox{span}\{\chi_{i}^{ms}\}
such that
0\leq\chi_{i}^{M,m}\leq 1

and

\displaystyle\chi_{i}^{M,m}

\displaystyle=1\quad\mbox{in}\;K_{i,m},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\chi_{i}^{M,m}

\displaystyle=0\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega\backslash K_{i,M}.

(4.5)

Note that we have
K_{i,m}\subset K_{i,M}
and
\{\chi_{i}^{ms}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are the standard multiscale finite element (MsFEM) basis functions (cf.
[
19
]
).

Lemma 4.3
.

We consider the oversampling domain
K_{i,k}
with
k\geq 2
. That is,
K_{i,k}
is an oversampling region by enlarging
K_{i}
by
k
coarse grid layers. Let
\phi_{j}^{(i)}
be a given auxiliary multiscale basis function. Let
\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}
be the multiscale basis function achieved from (
3.3
) and let
\psi_{j}^{(i)}
be the global multiscale basis function obtained from (
3.5
). Then we have

\displaystyle\|\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\|_{a}^{2}\leq E\|\phi_{j}^{(i)}\|_{s(K_{i})}^{2},

where
E=8D^{2}(1+\Lambda^{-1})(1+\frac{\Lambda^{1/2}}{2D^{1/2}})^{1-k}
.

Proof.

For the given
\phi_{j}^{(i)}\in V_{aux}
, it follows from Lemma
4.2
that there exists a
\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)}
such that

\displaystyle\pi(\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)})=\phi_{j}^{(i)},\quad\|\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)}\|_{a}^{2}\leq C\|\phi_{j}^{(i)}\|_{s}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\text{supp}(\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)})\subset K_{i}.

(4.6)

We let
\eta=\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)}
. Note that
\eta\in\tilde{V}
since
\pi(\eta)=0
. By using the resulting variational forms of the minimization problems, we can obtain

\displaystyle a(\psi_{j}^{(i)},v)+s(v,\mu_{j}^{(i)})=0\quad\forall v\in V

(4.7)

and

\displaystyle a(\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)},v)+s(v,\mu_{j,ms}^{(i)})=0\quad\forall v\in V_{0}(K_{i,k})

(4.8)

for some
\mu_{j}^{(i)},\mu_{j,ms}^{(i)}\in V_{aux}
. Subtracting the above two equations and restricting
v\in\tilde{V}_{0}(K_{i,k})
leads to

\displaystyle a(\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)},v)=0\quad\forall v\in\tilde{V}_{0}(K_{i,k}).

Here, we have
\tilde{V}_{0}(K_{i,k})=\{v\in H^{1}_{0}(K_{i,k})\;|\;\pi(v)=0\}
. Therefore,
for
v\in\tilde{V}_{0}(K_{i,k})
, we can infer that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\|_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle=a(\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)},\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=a(\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)},\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}+\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)})=a(\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)},\eta-v),

where
-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}+\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)}\in\tilde{V}_{0}(K_{i,k})
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\|_{a}\leq\|\eta-v\|_{a}.

(4.9)

To estimate
\|\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\|_{a}
, we need to derive the upper bound for
\|\eta-v\|_{a}
. We consider the
i
-th coarse
block
K_{i}
. For this block, we consider two oversampling regions
K_{i,k-1}
and
K_{i,k}
.
Using these two overampling regions, we define the cutoff function
\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}

with the properties in (
4.4
)-(
4.5
), where we take
m=k-1
and
M=k
.
For any coarse block
K_{j}\subset K_{i,k-1}
,
we have
\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\equiv 1
on
K_{j}
by using (
4.4
). Since
\eta\in\tilde{V}
, it holds that

\displaystyle s_{j}(\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\eta,\phi_{n}^{(j)})=s_{j}(\eta,\phi_{n}^{(j)})=0\quad\forall n=1,2,\cdots,l_{j}.

From the above result and the fact that
\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\equiv 0
in
\Omega\backslash K_{i,k}
, we have

\displaystyle\mbox{supp}(\pi(\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\eta))\subset K_{i,k}\backslash K_{i,k-1}.

By Lemma
4.2
, for the function
\pi(\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\eta)
, there is
\mu\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\mbox{supp}(\mu)\subset K_{i,k}\backslash K_{i,k-1}
and
\pi(\mu-\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\eta)=0
. Moreover, it also follows from Lemma
4.2
that

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{a(K_{i,k}\backslash K_{i,k-1})}\leq D^{1/2}\|\pi(\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\eta)\|_{s(K_{i,k}\backslash K_{i,k-1})}\leq D^{1/2}\|\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\eta\|_{s(K_{i,k}\backslash K_{i,k-1})}.

(4.10)

Hence, taking
v=-\mu+\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\eta
in (
4.9
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\|\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\|_{a}\leq\|\eta-v\|_{a}\leq\|(1-\chi_{i}^{k,k-1})\eta\|_{a}+\|\mu\|_{a(K_{i,k}\backslash K_{i,k-1})}.

(4.11)

Next, we will estimate the two terms on the right hand side of (
4.11
).

Step 1: We first estimate the first term in (
4.11
). By a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\|(1-\chi_{i}^{k,k-1})\eta\|_{a}^{2}\leq 2\Big(\int_{\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1}}\kappa(1-\chi_{i}^{k,k-1})^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\int_{\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1}}\kappa|\nabla\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}|^{2}\eta^{2}\Big).

Note that, we have
1-\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\leq 1
. For the second term on the righ hand side of the above inequality, we will use the fact that
\eta\in\tilde{V}
and the spectral problem (
3.1
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1}}\kappa|\nabla\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}|^{2}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1}}H^{-2}\kappa|\nabla\eta|^{2}\leq C\beta_{0}^{-2}\int_{\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1}}H^{-2}\kappa|\bm{\beta}|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}=C\beta_{0}^{-2}\|\eta\|_{s(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda^{-1}\beta_{0}^{-2}\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1})}^{2}.

Therefore, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|(1-\chi_{i}^{k,k-1})\eta\|_{a}^{2}\leq C(1+\Lambda^{-1})\int_{\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1}}\kappa|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

We will estimate the right hand side in Step 3.

Step 2: In this step we will estimate the second term on the right hand side of (
4.11
). By (
4.10
), the fact that
|\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}|\leq 1
and the spectral problem (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{a(K_{i,k}\backslash K_{i,k-1})}^{2}\leq D\|\chi_{i}^{k,k-1}\eta\|_{s(K_{i,k}\backslash K_{i,k-1})}^{2}\leq\frac{D}{\Lambda}\int_{K_{i,k}\backslash K_{i,k-1}}\kappa|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Combining Steps 1 and 2, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\|_{a}^{2}\leq 2D(1+\frac{1}{\Lambda})\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1})}^{2}.

(4.12)

Step 3: Finally, we will estimate the term
\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1})}
. We will first show that the following recursive inequality holds

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1})}^{2}\leq(1+\frac{\Lambda^{1/2}}{2D^{1/2}})^{-1}\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-2})}^{2},

(4.13)

where
k-2\geq 0
. Using (
4.13
) in (
4.12
), we can get

\displaystyle\|\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\|_{a}^{2}\leq 2D(1+\frac{1}{\Lambda})(1+\frac{\Lambda^{1/2}}{2D^{1/2}})^{-1}\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-2})}^{2}.

(4.14)

By using (
4.13
) again in (
4.14
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\|\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\|_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2D(1+\frac{1}{\Lambda})(1+\frac{\Lambda^{1/2}}{2D^{1/2}})^{1-k}\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2D(1+\frac{1}{\Lambda})(1+\frac{\Lambda^{1/2}}{2D^{1/2}})^{1-k}\|\eta\|_{a}^{2}.

By employing the definition of
\eta
, the energy minimizing property of
\psi_{j}^{(i)}
and Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{a}=\|\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)}\|_{a}\leq 2\|\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)}\|_{a}\leq 2D^{1/2}\|\phi_{j}^{(i)}\|_{s(K_{i})}.

Step 4: We will prove the estimate (
4.13
). Let
\xi=1-\chi_{i}^{k-1,k-2}
. Then we see that
\xi\equiv 1
in
\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1}
and
0\leq\xi\leq 1
otherwise. Then we have

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1})}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\kappa\xi^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla(\xi^{2}\eta)-2\int_{\Omega}\kappa\xi\eta\nabla\xi\nabla\eta.

(4.15)

We estimate the first term in (
4.15
). For the function
\pi(\xi^{2}\eta)
, using Lemma
4.2
, there exists
\gamma\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\pi(\gamma)=\pi(\xi^{2}\eta)
and
\mbox{supp}(\gamma)\subset\mbox{supp}(\pi(\xi^{2}\eta))
. For any coarse elements
K_{m}\subset\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1}
, since
\xi\equiv 1
on
K_{m}
, we have

\displaystyle s_{m}(\xi^{2}\eta,\phi_{n}^{(m)})=0\quad\forall n=1,\ldots,l_{m}.

On the other hand, since
\xi\equiv 0
in
K_{i,k-2}
, there holds

\displaystyle s_{m}(\xi^{2}\eta,\phi_{n}^{(m)})=0\quad\forall n=1,\ldots,l_{m},\;\forall K_{m}\subset K_{i,k-2}.

From the above two conditions, we see that
\mbox{supp}(\pi(\xi^{2}\eta))\subset K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2}
and consequently
\mbox{supp}(\gamma)\subset K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2}
. Note that, since
\pi(\gamma)=\pi(\xi^{2}\eta)
, we have
\xi^{2}\eta-\gamma\in\tilde{V}
. We also note that
\mbox{supp}(\xi^{2}\eta-\gamma)\subset\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-2}
. By (
4.6
), the functions
\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)}
and
\xi^{2}\eta-\gamma
have disjoint supports, so
a(\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{(i)},\xi^{2}\eta-\gamma)=0
. Then, by the definition of
\eta
, we have

\displaystyle a(\eta,\xi^{2}\eta-\gamma)=a(\psi_{j}^{(i)},\xi^{2}\eta-\gamma).

By the construction of
\psi_{j}^{(i)}
, we have
a(\psi_{j}^{(i)},\xi^{2}\eta-\gamma)=0
. Then we can estimate the first term in (
4.15
) by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Lemma
4.2

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla(\xi^{2}\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla\gamma

\displaystyle\leq D^{1/2}\|\eta\|_{a(K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2})}\|\pi(\xi^{2}\eta)\|_{s(K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2})}.

For all coarse elements
K\subset K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2}
, since
\pi(\eta)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\|\pi(\xi^{2}\eta)\|_{s(K)}^{2}\leq\|\xi^{2}\eta\|_{s(K)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int_{K}\kappa|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Summing the above over all coarse elements
K\subset K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2}
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|\pi(\xi^{2}\eta)\|_{s(K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2})}\leq(\frac{1}{\Lambda})^{1/2}\|\eta\|_{a(K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2})}.

To estimate the second term in (
4.15
), we have from the spectral problem (
3.1
)

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}\kappa\xi\eta\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla\eta\leq 2\beta_{0}^{-1}\|\eta\|_{s(K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2})}\|\eta\|_{a(K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2})}\leq\frac{2}{\beta_{0}\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\eta\|_{a(K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2})}^{2}.

Hence, the preceding arguments yield the upper bound for (
4.15
)

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1})}^{2}\leq\frac{2D^{1/2}}{\Lambda^{1/2}}\|\eta\|_{a(K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2})}^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-2})}^{2}=\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1})}^{2}+\|\eta\|_{a(K_{i,k-1}\backslash K_{i,k-2})}^{2}\geq(1+\frac{\Lambda^{1/2}}{2D^{1/2}})\|\eta\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K_{i,k-1})}^{2}.

∎

Following
[
8
]
, we can prove the following lemma. The proof is omitted here for simplicity.

Lemma 4.4
.

With the same assumptions as in Lemma
4.3
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)})\|_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(k+1)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\|_{a}^{2}.

Now we are ready to prove the following theorem, which gives an estimate of the error between the weak solution
u
and the multiscale solution
u_{ms}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u
be the solution of (
2.1
) and
u_{ms}
be the solution of (
3.4
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\|u-u_{ms}\|_{a}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big((1+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})H\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\;+\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)+(1+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})(1+k)E^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{s}\Big).

where
\tilde{u}
is the projection obtained from (
3.6
). Moreover, if the number of oversampling layers
k=\mathcal{O}(log(\frac{\beta_{1}\kappa_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{H\kappa_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}))
and
H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq C
,
we have

\displaystyle\|u-u_{ms}\|_{a}\leq CH\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big).

Proof.

Since
\bm{\beta}
is divergence free, it holds that

\|u-u_{ms}\|_{a}^{2}=a(u-u_{ms},u-u_{ms})=a(u-u_{ms},u-u_{ms})+\int_{\Omega}\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla(u-u_{ms})\,(u-u_{ms}).

For any
v\in V_{H}
, we can infer from Galerkin orthogonality that

\begin{split}\|u-u_{ms}\|_{a}^{2}&=a(u-u_{ms},u-v)+\int_{\Omega}\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla(u-u_{ms})\,(u-v)\\
&\leq\|u-u_{ms}\|_{a}\|u-v\|_{a}+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\|u-u_{ms}\|_{a}\|u-v\|_{s}.\end{split}

(4.16)

We assume that
\tilde{u}
obtained from (
3.6
) can be written as
\tilde{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}c_{j}^{(i)}\psi_{j}^{(i)}
. Then we define a function
v=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}c_{j}^{(i)}\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)}\in V_{ms}
.
We can infer from the spectral problem (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\|u-v\|_{s}\leq\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{s}+\|\tilde{u}-v\|_{s}\leq\Lambda^{-1/2}\Big(\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{a}+\|\tilde{u}-v\|_{a}\Big),

which coupling with (
4.16
) yields

\displaystyle\|u-u_{ms}\|_{a}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-v\|_{a}+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{a}+\|\tilde{u}-v\|_{a}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{a}+\|\tilde{u}-v\|_{a}+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{a}+\|\tilde{u}-v\|_{a}\Big).

Then it follows from Lemma
4.1
that

\displaystyle\|u-u_{ms}\|_{a}

\displaystyle\leq(1+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{a}+(1+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})\|\tilde{u}-v\|_{a}

\displaystyle\leq\Big((1+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})H\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\;+(1+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})\|\tilde{u}-v\|_{a}\Big).

We can infer from Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}-v\|_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}c_{j}^{(i)}(\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)})\|_{a}^{2}\leq C(1+k)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}c_{j}^{(i)}(\psi_{j}^{(i)}-\psi_{j,ms}^{(i)})\|_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+k)^{2}E\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}c_{j}^{(i)}\phi_{j}^{(i)}\|_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+k)^{2}E\|\tilde{u}\|_{s}^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\|u-u_{ms}\|_{a}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big((1+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})H\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\;+(1+H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})(1+k)E^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{s}\Big).

Now we show the error estimate for
\|\tilde{u}\|_{s}
. By the definition of
s
-norm, we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}\|_{s}^{2}=H^{-2}\int_{\Omega}\kappa|\bm{\beta}|^{2}|\tilde{u}|^{2}\;dx\leq H^{-2}\kappa_{1}\beta_{1}^{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq H^{-2}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{0}^{-1}\beta_{1}^{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{a}^{2}.

(4.17)

Recall that
\nabla\cdot\bm{\beta}=0
, thereby integration by parts yields
(\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u},\tilde{u})=0
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}\|_{a}^{2}=(\kappa\nabla\tilde{u},\nabla\tilde{u})=(f,\tilde{u})-(\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u},\tilde{u})=(f,\tilde{u})\leq H\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\tilde{u}\|_{s},

which together with (
4.17
) yields

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}\|_{s}\leq H^{-2}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{0}^{-1}\beta_{1}^{2}H\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq H^{-1}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{0}^{-1}\beta_{1}^{2}\|\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\bm{\beta}|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In order to deliver convergent solution, we require that
H\kappa_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq C
and
H^{-1}\beta_{1}^{2}(1+k)E^{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{0}^{-1}
is bounded, i.e.,

\displaystyle H^{-1}\beta_{1}^{2}(1+k)E^{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{0}^{-1}=\mathcal{O}(1).

Thus, we need to take
k=\mathcal{O}(log(\frac{\beta_{1}\kappa_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{H\kappa_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}))
. Therefore, the proof is completed.

∎

6
Conclusion

In this paper we have developed constraint energy minimizing generalized multiscale finite element method for convection diffusion equation. The decay property of the multiscale basis functions is proved. In line of this, we prove the convergence of the multiscale solution. Our theories indicate that if the overampling layer is taken properly, then the resulting multiscale basis functions have a decay property. Several numerical experiments are presented to verify the performances of our method. In the future we aim to develop a novel method in the framework of CEM-GMsFEM to solve convection dominated diffusion problem that exhibits interior or boundary layers.

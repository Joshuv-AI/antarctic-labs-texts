Energy dissipation in elasto-capillary fluid-structure interaction systems involving three immiscible fluids
S. R. Bhopalam, H. Gomez

Abstract




ABSTRACT

Here, we consider the elasto-capillary fluid-structure interaction problem studied in
Bhopalam et al. 2022
, i.e., three immiscible fluids in contact with an elastic solid. In this article, we show that the solution of this fluid-structure interaction problem satisfies an energy dissipation law.

Governing equations

Preliminaries:
We use the open sets
\Omega_{t}\in\mathcal{R}^{d}
and
\Omega_{0}\in\mathcal{R}^{d}
respectively, to denote the spatial and referential domains occupied by a continuum body, where
d
is the number of spatial dimensions. We hereby refer to
\Omega_{t}
and
\Omega_{0}
as Eulerian and Lagrangian domains, respectively. We assume
\Omega_{0}
to be fixed in time and its points to be parameterized by the reference coordinates
\bm{X}
. We define a function
\bm{\phi}
as a mapping from the Lagrangian to Eulerian domains at time
t
as
\bm{\phi}(\cdot,t):\Omega_{0}\longmapsto\Omega_{t}=\bm{\phi}(\Omega_{0},t)
such that
\bm{X}\longmapsto\bm{x}=\bm{\phi}(\bm{X},t)\ \forall\bm{X}\in\Omega_{0}
where
\bm{x}
denotes the coordinates of the spatial domain. We define the referential displacement and referential velocity as
\bm{u}(\bm{X},t)\coloneq\bm{\phi}(\bm{X},t)-\bm{X}
and
\bm{v}\coloneq\partial_{t}\bm{\phi}=\partial_{t}\bm{u}
, respectively, where the operator
\partial_{t}
denotes partial time differentiation. We define the deformation gradient as
\bm{F}\coloneq\frac{\partial\bm{\phi}}{\partial\bm{X}}
and the Jacobian determinant as
J\coloneq\det\bm{F}
. In what follows, we use subscripts in the definition of spatial and time derivatives. For example, the subscript
\bm{X}
in
\left.\partial_{t}\bm{u}\right|_{\bm{X}}
indicates that the time derivative has been computed by holding
\bm{X}
fixed. When no subscript is specified in the time derivative, we assume that the derivative has been computed by holding
\bm{x}
fixed. Similarly, in the context of spatial derivatives, the subscript
\bm{X}
in
\nabla_{\bm{X}}\bm{u}
, for example, indicates that the spatial derivative has been computed with respect to
\bm{X}
. When no subscript is specified in the spatial derivative, we assume that the derivative has been taken with respect to
\bm{x}
.

In the Fluid-Structure Interaction (FSI) problem we consider here, we decompose
\Omega_{t}
into two open sets,
\Omega_{t}^{f}
and
\Omega_{t}^{s}
, such that
\Omega_{t}\coloneq\Omega_{t}^{f}\cup\Omega_{t}^{s}
, and
\Omega_{t}^{f}\cap\Omega_{t}^{s}=\emptyset
. Here,
\Omega_{t}^{f}
and
\Omega_{t}^{s}
refer to the spatial configurations of the fluid and solid, respectively. We also define a similar decomposition of
\Omega_{0}
into
\Omega_{0}^{f}
and
\Omega_{0}^{s}
, which denote the referential configurations of the fluid and solid. In what follows, we denote the fluid-solid interface in the spatial domain as
\Gamma_{t}^{sf}\coloneq\partial\Omega_{t}^{f}\cap\partial\Omega_{t}^{s}
.

Fluids:
The governing equations of the fluids here constitute the continuity and the linear momentum balance equations written in the Eulerian domain. We use a thermodynamically consistent phase-field model — a ternary Navier-Stokes-Cahn-Hilliard model
Boyer and Lapuerta 2006
;
Boyer et al. 2010
to describe the dynamics of the three immiscible fluids. In what follows, we make two assumptions: a) we use constant density and viscosity for the fluids, and b) we use a single velocity field to describe the motion of the fluids. We define the Ginzburg-Landau free energy density of the fluids as
\Psi^{f}=\frac{12}{\epsilon}\Psi^{f}_{\text{bulk}}+\Psi^{f}_{\text{int}}
, where
\Psi^{f}_{\text{bulk}}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\varsigma_{i}}{2}c_{i}^{2}\left(1-c_{i}\right)^{2}
and
\Psi^{f}_{\text{int}}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{3}{8}\epsilon\varsigma_{i}\lvert\nabla c_{i}\rvert^{2}
represent the bulk and interfacial components of the free energy density. Here,
\epsilon
is the diffuse interface length scale,
c_{i}\in\left[0,1\right]
for
i=1,2,3
is the phase field denoting the volume fraction of the
i\textsuperscript{th}
fluid, and
\varsigma_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
is the spreading coefficient defined as
\varsigma_{1}=\gamma_{12}+\gamma_{13}-\gamma_{23}
,
\varsigma_{2}=\gamma_{12}+\gamma_{23}-\gamma_{13}
and
\varsigma_{3}=\gamma_{13}+\gamma_{23}-\gamma_{12}
where
\gamma_{ij}
denotes the surface tension at the interface between fluids
i
and
j
.

Solid:
The governing equations for the solid here are given by the linear momentum balance equation in the Lagrangian domain. In what follows, we assume the solid to be homogeneous, isotropic and nonlinearly elastic.

We state the strong form of the ternary FSI problem as follows: find

Energy dissipation relation

The energy functional of the fluid-structure interaction problem can be given by

\mathcal{E}=\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\frac{1}{2}\rho\lvert\bm{v}\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\Psi^{f}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Gamma_{t}^{sf}}\gamma_{sf}\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma+\int_{\Omega_{0}^{s}}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}^{s}\lvert\partial_{t}{\bm{u}}\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Omega_{0}^{s}}W\ \mathrm{d}\Omega,

(2)

where
W
denotes the strain energy density of the solid. The terms on the right hand side of Eq. (
2
) are as follows: first term represents the kinetic energy of the fluids, the second term represents the free energy associated with the mixing of the fluids, the third term represents the energetic contribution of the solid-fluid surface tension, the fourth term represents the kinetic energy of the solid and the fifth term represents the strain energy of the solid. In what follows, we show that the solution variables that satisfy Eq. (
1
) satisfy an energy dissipation law. To derive this energy dissipation law, we independently evaluate the time derivative of all the terms in Eq. (
2
) and assemble them eventually.

Using the Reynolds transport theorem
Scovazzi and Hughes 2007
, we show that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\Psi^{f}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\frac{12}{\epsilon}\frac{\partial F}{\partial c_{i}}\partial_{t}c_{i}+\frac{3}{4}\epsilon\varsigma_{i}\nabla c_{i}\cdot\nabla\left(\partial_{t}c_{i}\right)\right)\ \mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Gamma_{t}^{sf}}\Psi^{f}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}^{f}\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma,

(3)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\frac{12}{\epsilon}\frac{\partial F}{\partial c_{i}}-\frac{3}{4}\epsilon\varsigma_{i}\Delta c_{i}\right)\partial_{t}c_{i}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega}_{T_{1}^{GL}}+\underbrace{\int_{\Gamma_{t}^{sf}}\Psi^{f}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}^{f}\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{T_{2}^{GL}}+\underbrace{\int_{\Gamma_{t}^{sf}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{3}{4}\epsilon\varsigma_{i}\partial_{t}c_{i}\nabla c_{i}\cdot\bm{n}^{f}\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{T_{3}^{GL}}.

In deriving Eq. (
3
), we have used Eqs. (
1f
) and (
1h
). To derive the second step of Eq. (
3
), we also use the divergence theorem. For convenience, we split Eq. (
3

T_{1}^{GL}
,
T_{2}^{GL}
and
T_{3}^{GL}
, each of which we evaluate independently.
T_{1}^{GL}
can be re-written as,

\displaystyle T_{1}^{GL}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\mu_{i}-\beta\right)\left(\frac{M}{\varsigma_{i}}\Delta\mu_{i}-\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{v}c_{i}\right)\right)\ \mathrm{d}\Omega,

(4)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{M}{\varsigma_{i}}\lvert\nabla\mu_{i}\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega-\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mu\bm{v}\cdot\nabla c_{i}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega-\beta\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\frac{M}{\varsigma_{i}}\Delta\mu_{i}-\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{v}c_{i}\right)\right)\ \mathrm{d}\Omega,

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{M}{\varsigma_{i}}\lvert\nabla\mu_{i}\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega-\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mu\bm{v}\cdot\nabla c_{i}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega,

where we substitute for
\left(\frac{12}{\epsilon}\frac{\partial F}{\partial c_{i}}-\frac{3}{4}\epsilon\varsigma_{i}\Delta c_{i}\right)
and
\partial_{t}c_{i}
from Eqs. (
1c
) and Eq. (
1d

4

1a
) and (
1g
). The last step in Eq. (
4

T_{2}^{GL}
can be re-written as

\displaystyle T_{2}^{GL}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\Psi^{f}\nabla\cdot\bm{v}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\Psi^{f}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega,

(5)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\Psi^{f}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega,

where we use the divergence theorem and product rule in the first step of Eq. (

We use standard tensor-calculus operations to derive the following identity:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{3}{4}\epsilon\varsigma_{i}\nabla\cdot\left(\nabla c_{i}\otimes\nabla c_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{3}{4}\epsilon\varsigma_{i}\nabla c_{i}\Delta c_{i}\ +\ \sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{3}{8}\epsilon\varsigma_{i}\nabla\left(\nabla c_{i}\cdot\nabla c_{i}\right),

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\nabla\left(\frac{12}{\epsilon}\frac{\partial F}{\partial c_{i}}+\frac{3}{4}\epsilon\varsigma_{i}\nabla c_{i}\cdot\nabla c_{i}\right)\ -\ \sum_{i=1}^{3}\mu_{i}\nabla c_{i}\ +\ \sum_{i=1}^{3}\beta\nabla c_{i},

1h

Scovazzi and Hughes 2007

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\frac{1}{2}\rho\lvert\bm{v}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}^{f}}\rho\partial_{t}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{v}\ \mathrm{d}\Omega\ +\ \int_{\Gamma_{t}^{sf}}\frac{1}{2}\rho\lvert\bm{v}\rvert^{2}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}^{f}\ \mathrm{d}\Gamma,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega_{0}^{s}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}^{s}\lvert\partial_{t}{\bm{u}}\rvert^{2}+W\right)\ \mathrm{d}\Omega

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{0}^{s}}\left(\rho_{0}^{s}\partial_{t}\left.\bm{u}\right|_{\bm{X}}\cdot\partial_{t}^{2}\left.\bm{u}\right|_{\bm{X}}\ +\ \partial_{t}\left.W\right|_{\bm{X}}\right)\ \mathrm{d}\Omega,

where

We follow

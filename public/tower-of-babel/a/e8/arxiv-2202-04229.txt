Matter radii and skins of 6,8 He from reaction cross section of proton+ 6,8 He scattering based on the Love-Franey t t -matrix model
Tomotsugu Wakasa, Maya Takechi, Shingo Tagami, Masanobu Yahiro

Abstract
Background: For 4,6,8 He, Tanihata et al. determined matter radii r m ​ ( σ I ) = 1.57 ​ ( 4 ) , 2.48 ​ ( 3 ) , 2.52 ​ ( 3 ) r_{m}(\sigma_{\rm I})=1.57(4),2.48(3),2.52(3) fm from interaction cross sections σ I \sigma_{\rm I} for 4,6,8 He scattering on Be, C Al targets at 790 MeV/nucleon. Lu et al. measured the atomic isotope shifts (AIS) for 4,6,8 He and determined proton radii r p ​ ( AIS ) r_{p}({\rm AIS}) for 4,6,8 He. As for p+ 4,6,8 He scattering, reaction cross sections σ R ​ ( exp ) \sigma_{\rm R}({\rm exp}) are available at 700 MeV with high accuracy. Aim: Our aim is to determine matter radii r m r_{m} and skins r skin r_{\rm skin} for 6,8 He from the σ R ​ ( exp ) \sigma_{\rm R}({\rm exp}) and the r p ​ ( AIS ) r_{p}({\rm AIS}) . Method: Our model is the Love-Franey t t -matrix folding model, since the model is better than the optical limit of Glauber model. Results: Our results for 6,8 He are r m ​ ( exp ) = 2.48 ​ ( 3 ) , 2.53 ​ ( 2 ) r_{m}({\rm exp})=2.48(3),2.53(2) fm and r skin = r_{\rm skin}= 0.78(3), 0.82(2) fm. Conclusion: For 6,8 He, our results r m ​ ( σ R ) r_{m}(\sigma_{\rm R}) agree with those of Tanihata et al. . For 8 He, the distance between 4 He and the center of mass of valence four neutrons is 2.367 fm.

II
Model

We use the folding model based on Lovey-dovey (LF)
t
-matrix
LF
.

We show the formulation on the LF folding
t
-matrix model below.
For proton-nucleus scattering, the potential
U({\mbox{\boldmath$R$}})

between a projectile (P) and a target (
{\rm T}
) has the direct and exchange parts,

U^{\rm DR}
and
U^{\rm EX}
, as

\displaystyle U^{\rm DR}({\mbox{\boldmath$R$}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu,\nu}\int\rho^{\nu}_{\rm T}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\rm T})t^{\rm DR}_{\mu\nu}(s;\rho_{\mu\nu})d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\rm T}\ ,

(1a)

\displaystyle U^{\rm EX}({\mbox{\boldmath$R$}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu,\nu}\int\rho^{\nu}_{\rm T}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\rm T},{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\rm T}+{\mbox{\boldmath$s$}})

(1b)

\displaystyle\times t^{\rm EX}_{\mu\nu}(s;\rho_{\mu\nu})\exp{[-i{\mbox{\boldmath$K$}}({\mbox{\boldmath$R$}})\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$s$}}/M]}d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\rm T}\,~~

where
R
is the relative coordinate between P and T,

{\mbox{\boldmath$s$}}=-{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\rm T}+{\mbox{\boldmath$R$}}
, and
{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\rm T}
is
the coordinate of the interacting nucleon from T.
Each of
\mu
and
\nu
denotes the
z
-component of isospin.
The nonlocal
U^{\rm EX}
has been localized in Eq. (
1b
)
with the local semi-classical approximation
Brieva-Rook

where
K
(
R
) is the local momentum between P and T,
and
M=A/(1+A)
for the target mass number
A
;
see Ref.
Minomo:2009ds
for the validity of the localization.

The direct and exchange parts,
t^{\rm DR}_{\mu\nu}
and

t^{\rm EX}_{\mu\nu}
, of the
t
matrix are described by

\displaystyle t_{\mu\nu}^{\rm DR}(s)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4}\sum_{S}}\hat{S}^{2}t_{\mu\nu}^{S1}(s)\hskip 2.84544pt\hskip 2.84544pt{\rm for}\hskip 2.84544pt\mu+\nu=\pm 1,

(2)

\displaystyle t_{\mu\nu}^{\rm DR}(s)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{8}\sum_{S,T}}\hat{S}^{2}t_{\mu\nu}^{ST}(s)\hskip 2.84544pt\hskip 2.84544pt{\rm for}\hskip 2.84544pt\mu+\nu=0,

(3)

\displaystyle t_{\mu\nu}^{\rm EX}(s)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4}\sum_{S}}(-1)^{S+1}\hat{S}^{2}t_{\mu\nu}^{S1}(s)\hskip 2.84544pt\hskip 2.84544pt{\rm for}\hskip 2.84544pt\mu+\nu=\pm 1,

(4)

\displaystyle t_{\mu\nu}^{\rm EX}(s)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{8}\sum_{S,T}}(-1)^{S+T}\hat{S}^{2}t_{\mu\nu}^{ST}(s)\hskip 2.84544pt\hskip 2.84544pt{\rm for}\hskip 2.84544pt\mu+\nu=0,

(5)

where
\hat{S}={\sqrt{2S+1}}
and
t_{\mu\nu}^{ST}
are
the spin-isospin components of the
t
-matrix interaction.
We apply the LF
t
-matrix folding model for p+
4,6,8
He scattering
at
E_{\rm in}=700
MeV.

As proton and neutron densities,
\rho^{\nu=-1/2}_{\rm T}
and
\rho^{\nu=1/2}_{\rm T}
,
we use the densities calculated with D1S-Gogny HFB (D1S-GHFB)

As a way of taking the center-of-mass correction to the densities,
we use the method of Ref.
Sumi:2012fr

We scale D1S-GHFB proton and neutron densities, as mentioned below.

We consider proton and neutron densities calculated with D1S-GHFB as
the original density
\rho({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
.
The scaled density
\rho_{\rm scaling}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
is determined
from the original density
\rho({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
as

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm scaling}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\equiv\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\rho({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}/\alpha),~{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\rm scaling}\equiv{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}/\alpha

(6)

with a scaling factor

\displaystyle\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{\langle{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}^{2}\rangle_{\rm scaling}}{\langle{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}^{2}\rangle}}.

(7)

In Eq. (
6
), we have replaced
r
by
{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}/\alpha
in the original density.
Eventually,
r
dependence
of
\rho_{\rm scaling}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
is different from that of
\rho({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
.
We have multiplied the original density by
\alpha^{-3}

in order to normalize the scaled density.
The symbol means
\sqrt{\langle{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}^{2}\rangle_{\rm scaling}}
is the root-mean-square
radius of
\rho_{\rm scaling}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})

For later convenience, we refer to the proton (neutron) radius of the scaled proton (neutron) density

(8)

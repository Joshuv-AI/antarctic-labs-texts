Affine Laumon spaces and iterated 𝒲 {\mathcal{W}} -algebras
Thomas Creutzig, Duiliu-Emanuel Diaconescu, Mingyang Ma

Abstract
A family of vertex algebras whose universal Verma modules coincide with the cohomology of affine Laumon spaces is found. This result is based on an explicit expression for the generating function of Poincaré polynomials of these spaces. There is a variant of quantum Hamiltonian reduction that realizes vertex algebras which we call iterated 𝒲 {\mathcal{W}} -algebras and our main conjecture is that the vertex algebras associated to the affine Laumon spaces are subalgebras of iterated 𝒲 {\mathcal{W}} -algebras.

Introduction

Affine Laumon spaces provide a rigorous mathematical construction for the surface defects introduced by Gukov and Witten in
[
GW08
]
. In the context of AGT correspondence

[
AGT10
]
, this predicts a vertex operator algebra action on their equivariant
Borel-Moore homology.
As shown by Schiffmann and Vasserot
[
SV13
]
and by
Maulik and Okounkov
[
MO19
]
,
for the moduli space of rank
r
unramified instantons, this vertex algebra is the

{\mathcal{W}}
-algebra associated to a principal nilpotent element in
{\mathfrak{g}l}_{r}
.
More precisely, the equivariant Borel-Moore homology of the instanton moduli space is identified to a highest weight
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}l}_{r})
-module. This action is constructed using an algebraic
relation between the
{\mathcal{W}}
-algebra and the Yangian of affine
{\mathfrak{g}l}_{1}
.
The latter is then shown to act on the equivariant Borel-Moore homology via Hecke
operators. The analogous construction of moduli spaces of
G
-instantons was
carried out by Braverman, Fikelberg and Nakajima
[
BFN16
]
.
Earlier results on the representation theoretic structure of the equivariant Borel-Moore homology
of affine Laumon spaces of type
(1,\ldots,1)
include
[
FFNR11
,
Neg18
,
Neg11
]
.

A similar program for ramified instantons was initiated by Finkelberg and Tsymbaliuk
[
FT19
]
and Negut
[
Neg19
]
. Using Hecke transformations, they constructed a
shifted affine Yangian action on the equivariant Borel-Moore homology of affine Laumon
spaces, respectively a shifted quantum toroidal action on their equivariant K-theory.
The main missing piece of the puzzle is the construction of the associated
vertex operator algebras. This is understood only for the rectangular case i.e. affine Laumon spaces of type
(s,\ldots,s)
,
which is treated in
[
Neg20
]
. Similar relations between affine Yangians and rectangular

{\mathcal{W}}
-algebras were established in
[
Ued22
,
Ued21
]
.

In the physics literature, the relation between the Nekrasov partition functions
of affine Laumon spaces and
{\mathcal{W}}
-characters was studied in

[
KPW10
,
Wyl11b
,
Wyl11a
,
KT11
]
. Most of these results are restricted to the case
to affine Laumon spaces of type
(1,\ldots,1)
, with the exception of
Kanno and Tachikawa
[
KT11
]
who address some aspects
of the partition function for Euler characteristics in the general case.

The present paper presents a conjectural construction of
vertex operator algebras associated to affine Laumon spaces
of type
{\bf r}=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{\ell-1})
,
\ell\geq 2
, subject to the conditions

r_{0}\geq r_{1}\geq\ldots\geq r_{\ell-1}\geq 1
. The main result identifies the
generating function
for Poincaré polynomials of affine Laumon spaces of type
{\bf r}
as above to the conformal weight refinement of the universal Verma module character of the associated
vertex algebra. These vertex operator algebras are obtained as natural subalgebras of iterated
{\mathcal{W}}
-algebras.

In more detail, recall that the affine Laumon space of type
(r_{0},\ldots,r_{\ell})
is
a moduli space of framed parabolic torsion free sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
The parabolic structure is specified by a flag of subsheaves of ranks

r_{0},\ldots,r_{\ell}
along

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\{0\}
, while the framing condition
rigidifies the parabolic sheaves along
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\{\infty\}\,\cup\,\{\infty\}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. These spaces were studied in detail in
[
FR14
]
. In particular, as shown in
loc. cit, each such space is naturally identified to a connected component
of the fixed locus for a certain
{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}_{\ell}
-action on the moduli space of rank

r=r_{0}+\cdots+r_{\ell}
instantons. Moreover, using the ADHM construction,
the affine Laumon spaces are identified to moduli spaces of cyclic framed
representations of the chain-saw quiver. Section
2
provides a summary
of all these results.

For fixed
type
{\bf r}=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{\ell})
, the moduli space of framed parabolic sheaves
consists of infinitely many smooth components
{\mathcal{M}}({\bf r},{\bf n})
. The index
{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\times\ell}
encodes the parabolic second Chern class.
The generating function

Z_{\bf r}=\sum_{{\bf n}}P_{y}({\mathcal{M}}({\bf r},{\bf n}))\prod_{a=0}^{\ell-1}q_{a}^{n_{a}}

for Poincaré polynomials is computed in Section
3
using Atiyah-Bott-Morse
localization. This computation generalizes the results obtained by Nakajima and Yoshioka

[
NY04
]
for unramified instantons, and employs similar techniques. Similar results were obtained by Bruzzo et al in
[
BPT11
]
for
framed torsion free sheaves on Hirzebruch surfaces.

A

Most of section

Affine Laumon spaces

This section is a review of affine Laumon space following
[
FR14
]
. The presentation will emphasize their construction in terms of representations of the chain-saw quiver.

2.1
ADHM orbifolds

Given two vector spaces
V
and
W
, the moduli space
of framed representations of the ADHM quiver is a GIT quotient
{\mathcal{A}}//GL(V)
where

{\mathcal{A}}=\{\,(A_{1},A_{2},I,J)\in{\rm End}(V)^{\times 2}\times{\rm Hom}(W,V)\times{\rm Hom}(W,V)\,|\,[A_{1},A_{2}]+IJ=0\}.

The general linear group
GL(V)
acts
naturally on
{\mathcal{A}}
,

g\times(A_{1},A_{2},I,J)\mapsto(gA_{1}g^{-1},gA_{2}a^{-1},gI,Jg^{-1}).

The stable locus
{\mathcal{A}}^{s}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is defined by
ruling out
A_{i}
-invariant subspaces
V^{\prime}\subsetneq V
,
1\leq i\leq 2
, so that
{\rm Im}(I)\subset V^{\prime}
. The resulting moduli space is a smooth quasi-projective variety of dimension
2rn
,
where
r={\rm dim}(W)
and
n={\rm dim}(V)
. In the following we will fix a basis
(w_{\alpha})
in
W
,
1\leq\alpha\leq r
, and
write
(I_{\alpha})
,
(J_{\alpha})
for the associated components of
I,J
.

Any ordered partition
{\bf r}=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{\ell-1})
, where

r_{0}+\cdots+r_{\ell-1}=r
,
r_{a}\geq 0
,
\ell\geq 2
, determines a
cyclic group action
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
as follows.
Let
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}\to{\rm End}(W)
, be the linear action which maps the
canonical generator
\omega=e^{2i\pi/\ell}
to the diagonal matrix

\omega\mapsto M=\rm{diag}(\ \underbrace{1,\ldots 1}_{r_{0}},\underbrace{\omega,\ldots,\omega}_{r_{1}},\ldots,\underbrace{\omega^{\ell-1},\ldots,\omega^{\ell-1}}_{r_{\ell-1}}\ ).

(2.1)

Then one defines the linear action
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
,

\omega\times(A_{1},A_{2},I,J)\mapsto(A_{1},\omega A_{2},IM^{-1},\omega MJ).

This action commutes with the
GL(V)
-action on
{\mathcal{A}}
and preserves the stable locus, hence it descends to a
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
-action on the moduli space.

The components of the
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
-fixed locus in the moduli space are indexed by
ordered partitions
{\bf n}=(n_{0},\cdots,n_{\ell-1})
,

n_{0}+\cdots+n_{\ell-1}=n
,

n_{a}\geq 0
,
0\leq a\leq\ell-1
.
The fixed component
{\mathcal{M}}({\bf r},{\bf n})
corresponding to an ordered partition
{\bf n}
consists of isomorphism classes of ADHM data
(A_{1},A_{2},I,J)
satisfying

A_{1}=U_{\bf n}A_{1}U_{\bf n}^{-1},\hskip 20.00003pt\omega A_{2}=U_{\bf n}A_{2}U_{\bf n}^{-1},\hskip 20.00003ptIM^{-1}=U_{\bf n}^{-1}I,\hskip 20.00003pt\omega MJ=JU_{\bf n}

(2.2)

where

U_{\bf n}=\rm{diag}(\ \underbrace{1,\ldots 1}_{n_{0}},\underbrace{\omega,\ldots,\omega}_{n_{1}},\ldots,\underbrace{\omega^{\ell-1},\ldots,\omega^{\ell-1}}_{n_{\ell-1}}\ ).

As explained in
[
FR14
, Section. 2.3]
, each component
{\mathcal{M}}({\bf r},{\bf n})
is identified to a moduli space of stable representations of the chain-saw quiver. Moreover, it is a smooth quasi-projective variety.

2.2
Torus fixed points

The torus
{\bf T}=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{\times r}

(t_{1},t_{2},\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{r})\times(A_{1},A_{2},I,J)\mapsto(t_{1}A_{1},t_{2}A_{2},\zeta_{\alpha}^{-1}I_{\alpha},t_{1}t_{2}\zeta_{\alpha}J),\hskip 20.00003pt1\leq\alpha\leq r.

-fixed point

3
The generating function of Poincaré polynomials

The goal of this section is to find a closed expression for the generating function of the
Poincaré polynomials of the affine Laumon spaces
{\mathcal{M}}({\bf r},{\bf n})
for
fixed invariants
{\bf r}=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{\ell-1})
. The computation
will employ Atiyah-Bott-Morse localization by analogy to
[
NY04
, Theorem 3.8]
.

Let
\lambda:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to{\bf T}
be a one parameter group of the form

t\mapsto(t^{M_{1}},t^{M_{2}},t_{\alpha}^{N_{\alpha}})

where

M_{2}>>N_{1}>\cdots>N_{r}>>M_{1}>0.

As shown in the proof of
[
NY04
, Theorem 3.7]
, the resulting
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action
on the ADHM moduli space is compact. The same holds for the induced action on
{\mathcal{M}}({\bf r},{\bf n})
since the latter is a closed subscheme of the ADHM moduli space. Therefore the first step is the computation of the Morse index for an arbitrary fixed point
{\underline{\mu}}\in{\mathcal{M}}({\bf r},{\bf n})
.

3.1
The Morse index

Let
{\underline{\mu}}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
be a
{\bf T}
-fixed point in
{\mathcal{M}}({\bf r},{\bf n})
. Recall that any integer
1\leq\beta\leq r
determines a unique
0\leq a(\beta)\leq\ell-1
so that

r_{0}+\cdots+r_{a(\beta)-1}+1\leq\beta\leq r_{1}+\cdots+r_{a(\beta)-1}+r_{a(\beta)}.

As explained in Section
2.2
, this
determines the coloring function for the partition
\mu_{\beta}
to be

c_{a(\beta)}(i,j)\equiv a(\beta)-j+1\ (\ell).

For any
0\leq c\leq\ell-1
and any
1\leq\beta\leq r
let
n_{c}(\mu_{\beta})
be the
total number of
c
-marked boxes in
\mu_{\beta}
. Let also

{\rm col}(\mu_{\beta})
denote the total number of columns of
\beta
.
Then the main result of this section is the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

The Morse index associated to the fixed point
{\underline{\mu}}\in{\mathcal{M}}({\bf r},{\bf n})^{\bf T}
is given by

w({\underline{\mu}})=\sum_{\beta=1}^{r}w(\mu_{\beta})

(3.1)

where

w(\mu_{\beta})=\sum_{c=0}^{\ell-1}n_{c}(\mu_{\beta})r_{c}-{\rm col}(\mu_{\beta})(r_{0}+\cdots+r_{a(\beta)}-\beta+1).

(3.2)

Proof
.
The tangent space to

{\mathcal{M}}({\bf r},{\bf n})
at the fixed point
{\underline{\mu}}
is the
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
-fixed part of
the right hand side of equation (
2.8
),

T_{{\underline{\mu}}}^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}=\sum_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq r}T^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}_{\alpha,\beta}~.

Using equation (
2.7
), one obtains

\displaystyle T^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}_{\alpha,\beta}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in\mu_{\alpha}\\
\mu^{\prime}_{\alpha,i}-j+1+a(\beta)-a(\alpha)\equiv 0\,(\ell)\end{subarray}}\ T_{1}^{-\mu_{\beta,j}+i}T_{2}^{\mu^{\prime}_{\alpha,i}-j+1}

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in\mu_{\beta}\\
-\mu^{\prime}_{\beta,i}+j+a(\beta)-a(\alpha)\equiv 0\,(\ell)\end{subarray}}\ T_{1}^{\mu_{\alpha,j}-i+1}T_{2}^{-\mu^{\prime}_{\beta,i}+j}

As in the proof of
[
NY04
, Theorem 3.8]
, the Morse index of the fixed point

{\underline{\mu}}
is obtained by counting the number of monomials in the expression of

T_{{\underline{\mu}}}^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

If
\alpha\geq\beta
then the weight of
T_{2}
is negative.

(ii)

If
\alpha<\beta
then the weight of
T_{2}
is nonpositive.

Note that all such monomials are contained in the second term in the right hand side of equation (
3.3
). All monomials in the first term have positive
T_{2}
-degree.
Equation (
3.3
) shows that

w({\underline{\mu}})
has a decomposition

w({\underline{\mu}})=\sum_{\beta=1}^{r}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}w_{\beta,\alpha}({\underline{\mu}})

where

w_{\beta,\alpha}({\underline{\mu}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sharp\{(i,j)\in\beta\,|\,\mu^{\prime}_{i}-j\equiv a(\beta)-a(\alpha)\ (\ell)\},&{\rm for}\ \alpha<\beta,\\
&\\
\sharp\{(i,j)\in\beta\,|\,j\leq\mu_{i}^{\prime}-1,\ \mu^{\prime}_{i}-j\equiv a(\beta)-a(\alpha)\ (\ell)\},&{\rm for}\ \alpha\geq\beta.\end{array}\right.

As shown in Lemma
A.1
, equation (
A.5
),

\sharp\{(i,j)\in\beta\,|\,\mu^{\prime}_{i}-j\equiv a(\beta)-a(\alpha)\ (\ell)\}=n_{a(\alpha)}(\mu_{\beta}).

(3.4)

Furthermore, Lemma
A.2
, equation (
A.3
), shows that

\sharp\{(i,j)\in\beta\,|\,j\leq\mu_{i}^{\prime}-1,\ \mu^{\prime}_{i}-j\equiv a(\beta)-a(\alpha)\ (\ell)\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n_{a(\alpha)}(\mu_{\beta}),&{\rm if}\ a(\alpha)\neq a(\beta)\\
&\\
n_{a(\alpha)}(\mu_{\beta})-{\rm col}(\mu_{\beta}),&{\rm if}\ a(\alpha)=a(\beta).\\
\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

Then

w({\underline{\mu}})=\sum_{\beta=1}^{r}\left(w_{<}(\mu_{\beta})+w_{0}(\mu_{\beta})+w_{>}(\mu_{\beta})\right),

where

\displaystyle w_{<}(\mu_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r_{0}+\cdots+r_{a(\beta)-1}}n_{a(\alpha)}(\mu_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{c=0}^{a(\beta)-1}n_{c}(\mu_{\beta})r_{c}~,

\displaystyle w_{0}(\mu_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=r_{0}+\cdots+r_{a(\beta)-1}+1}^{\beta-1}n_{a(\alpha)}(\mu_{\beta})+\sum_{\alpha=\beta}^{r_{0}+\cdots+r_{a(\beta)}}\left(n_{a(\alpha)}(\mu_{\beta})-{\rm col}(\mu_{\beta})\right)

\displaystyle=n_{a(\beta)}(\mu_{\beta})r_{a(\beta)}-{\rm col}(\mu_{\beta})(r_{0}+\cdots+r_{a(\beta)}-\beta+1)~,

\displaystyle w_{<}(\mu_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=r_{0}+\cdots+r_{a(\beta)}+1}^{r}n_{a(\alpha)}(\mu_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{c=a(\beta)+1}^{\ell-1}n_{c}(\mu_{\beta})r_{c}~.

Then

\displaystyle w(\mu_{\beta})

\displaystyle=w_{<}(\mu_{\beta})+w_{0}(\mu_{\beta})+w_{>}(\mu_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{c=0}^{\ell-1}n_{c}(\mu_{\beta})r_{c}-{\rm col}(\mu_{\beta})(r_{0}+\cdots+r_{a(\beta)}-\beta+1).

\Box

4
{\mathcal{W}}
-algebras

{\mathcal{W}}
-algebras are obtained by affine vertex algebras via quantum Hamiltonian reduction. In this section we will study an iterated version.

4.1
Affine Lie algebras

This section is a brief reminder of universal Verma modules for affine Lie algebras.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
a Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{h}
a Cartan subalgebra of rank
r+1
and
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}
a set of positive simple roots.
Let

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-}

the usual triangular decomposition where
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is the sum of all positive root spaces and similarly
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
is the sum of all negative ones.
Let
R=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{r},x_{0}]
and
K=\mathbb{C}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r},x_{0})
. The universal Verma module of
\mathfrak{g}
is a usual Verma module over
R
and so after base change over
K
. This means, let
h_{0},\dots,h_{n}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{h}
, e.g. the one corresponding to the simple roots
\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{r}
.
Then one considers the one dimensional module
\mathbb{C}_{x}
over
\mathfrak{h}
on which
h_{i}
acts by multiplication by
x_{i}
. This extends to a

\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+}
-module by letting
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
act trivially. The universal Verma module is then

V:=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0})}\mathbb{C}_{x}.

In particular as a
\mathfrak{h}
-graded vector space
M
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[B_{-}]
for
B_{-}
a homogeneous basis (every basis vector is an
\mathfrak{h}
-eigenvector) of
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
.

Let us now specialize to
\mathfrak{g}
being the affinization of a finite dimensional reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{0}
.
Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}
be a set of positive simple roots for the finite dimensional subalgebra, let
\theta
be the longest root and let
\delta
be the imaginary root of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\alpha_{0}=\delta-\theta
. Let
\Delta_{\pm}^{0},\Delta^{0}
denote the sets of positive, negative roots and roots of
\mathfrak{g}^{0}
.
Then the positive root space is

\Delta_{+}=\Delta_{+}^{0}\cup\{\alpha+n\delta,n\delta|n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\alpha\in\Delta^{0}\}

and similarly

\Delta_{+}=\Delta_{-}^{0}\cup\{\alpha+n\delta,n\delta|n\in\mathbb{Z}_{<0},\alpha\in\Delta^{0}\}

The dimension of the root space for
\alpha+n\delta
is one and the one of
n\delta
is the rank of
\mathfrak{g}^{0}
.
It follows that the formal character of the universal Verma module is given by the Weyl denominator, that is

\begin{split}\text{ch}[V]&=\frac{\text{ch}[\mathbb{C}_{x}]}{D}\\
D&=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{-n\delta})^{r}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}^{0}}(1-e^{-\alpha}e^{(1-n)\delta})(1-e^{\alpha}e^{-n\delta}).\end{split}

(4.1)

This is an equation as formal power series with the short hand notation

\frac{1}{1-y}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y^{n}.

Here

\text{ch}[\mathbb{C}_{x}]=\prod_{i=0}^{r}e^{x_{i}\omega_{i}}

with
\omega_{i}
dual to our basis of the Cartan subalgebra, that is
\omega_{i}(h_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
.
The term universal is due to that for a ring homomorphism
f:R\rightarrow R
(or
\mathbb{C}
if desired) one can compose the action of
\mathfrak{g}
with
f
to get a module with character

\text{ch}[V_{f}]=\prod_{i=0}^{r}e^{f(x_{i})\omega_{i}}\frac{1}{D}.

in particular any ordinary Verma module over
\mathbb{C}
can be realized in this way.
If
f
is an automorphism, then we call
M_{f}
a universal Verma module as well.
An example is that
f
maps each
x_{i}
to
x_{i}+\lambda_{i}
for certain constants
\lambda_{i}
.

We note that any ordinary Verma module is simple for generic highest-weight. Moreover it is also projective and injective in the category of weight Verma modules, that is semisimple action of the Cartan subalgebra.

Set

\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{i+1}

for

n

. Similarly the elements corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
are the

for

and either negative integer
n
or

and
i>j
.
Let

acts by multiplication with

Let

This converges to a meromorphic function on

]
and using the summary of
[
CL22
, Section 3]
. For this let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie algebra,

(\ \ |\ \ ):{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

(4.4)

a nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
and

\{q^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in S}
be a basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
indexed by the set
S
.
The structure constants are defined by

[q^{\alpha},q^{\beta}]=\sum_{\gamma\in S}{f^{\alpha\beta}}_{\gamma}q^{\gamma}.

The affine vertex algebra
V^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
at level
k\in\mathbb{C}
is strongly and freely generated by
\{X^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in S}
with the usual operator products

X^{\alpha}(z)X^{\beta}(w)\sim\frac{k(q^{\alpha}|q^{\beta})}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\sum_{\gamma\in S}{f^{\alpha\beta}}_{\gamma}X^{\gamma}(w)}{(z-w)}.

We assume that
k
is generic. It is more precise to denote
V^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})
by
V^{k(\ \ |\ \ )}({\mathfrak{g}})
to emphazise the given bilinear form and we will do so later.
Set
X_{\alpha}
to be the field corresponding to
q_{\alpha}
where
\{q_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in S}
is the dual basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with respect to
(\ \ |\ \ )
.

Let
f
be a nilpotent element of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. By the Jacobson-Morozov theorem,
f
is part of an
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{2}
triple
\{f,x,e\}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfying the relations
[x,e]=e,[x,f]=-f,[e,f]=2x
.
In particular
{\mathfrak{g}}
decomposes as an
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{2}
-module

{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{k},\hskip 20.00003pt{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}=\{a\in{\mathfrak{g}}|[x,a]=ka\}.

Let
S_{k}
be a basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
and take
S=\bigcup_{k}S_{k}
as basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Let
us also set

{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}=\bigoplus_{k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{k},\hskip 20.00003pt{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}=\bigoplus_{k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{<0}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}

with corresponding basis
S_{+}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}
;
{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}
is naturally identified with the dual of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}
.
On
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
one defines the antisymmetric bilinear form

\langle a,b\rangle:=(f|[a,b]).

Let
F({\mathfrak{g}}_{+})
be the vertex superalgebra associated to the vector superspace
{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}^{*}
. This vertex superalgebra is strongly generated by odd fields
\{\varphi_{\alpha},\varphi^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in S_{+}}
. The conformal weight of
\varphi_{\alpha}
is
j
if
q^{\alpha}
is in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
and the one of
\varphi^{\alpha}
is
1-j
.
The operator products are

\varphi_{\alpha}(z)\varphi^{\beta}(w)\sim\frac{\delta_{\alpha,\beta}}{(z-w)},\hskip 20.00003pt\varphi_{\alpha}(z)\varphi_{\beta}(w)\sim 0\sim\varphi^{\alpha}(z)\varphi^{\beta}(w).

Let
F({\mathfrak{g}}_{\frac{1}{2}})
be the neutral vertex algebra associated to
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
with bilinear form
\langle\ \ ,\ \ \rangle
. This has even strong generators
\{\Phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in S_{\frac{1}{2}}}
with operator products

\Phi_{\alpha}(z)\Phi_{\beta}(w)\sim\frac{\langle q^{\alpha},q^{\beta}\rangle}{(z-w)}\sim\frac{(f|[q^{\alpha},q^{\beta}])}{(z-w)},

(4.5)

and fields corresponding to the dual basis with respect to
\langle\ \ ,\ \ \rangle
are denoted by
\Phi^{\alpha}
.
Set

F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k):=F({\mathfrak{g}}_{+})\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}}_{\frac{1}{2}}).

The complex is

C({\mathfrak{g}},f,k):=V^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k).

It is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded by ghost number, that is the
\varphi_{\alpha}
have charge minus one, the
\varphi^{\alpha}
charge one and all others charge zero.
One further defines the odd field
d(z)
of charge minus one by

\begin{split}d(z)&=\sum_{\alpha\in S_{+}}:X^{\alpha}\varphi^{\alpha}:-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in S_{+}}{f^{\alpha\beta}}_{\gamma}:\varphi_{\gamma}\varphi^{\alpha}\varphi^{\beta}:+\sum_{\alpha\in S_{+}}(f|q^{\alpha})\varphi^{\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha\in S_{\frac{1}{2}}}:\varphi^{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}:.\end{split}

(4.6)

The zero-mode
d_{0}
is a differential since
[d(z),d(w)]=0
by
[
KRW03
, Thm. 2.1]
. The
\mathcal{W}
-algebra is defined to be its homology

{\mathcal{W}}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}},f):=H\left(C({\mathfrak{g}},f,k),d_{0}\right).

The homology is non-trivial only in degree zero
[
KW04
]
.

4.4
Properties

1.

Set

I^{\alpha}:=X^{\alpha}+\sum_{\beta,\gamma\in S_{+}}{f^{\alpha\beta}}_{\gamma}:\varphi_{\gamma}\varphi^{\beta}:+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\beta\in S_{\frac{1}{2}}}{f^{\beta\alpha}}_{\gamma}\Phi^{\beta}\Phi_{\gamma}

(4.12)

for
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
. Denote by
{\mathfrak{g}}^{f}
the centralizer of
f
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and set
{\mathfrak{b}}:={\mathfrak{g}}^{f}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
. It is a Lie subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
[
KW04
, Thm 2.1]
says in particular
that
d_{0}(I^{\alpha})=0
for
q^{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{b}}
and

I^{\alpha}{}(z)I^{\beta}(w)\sim\frac{B(q^{\alpha}|q^{\beta})}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{{f^{\alpha\beta}}_{\gamma}I^{\gamma}}{(z-w)},

with the bilinear form
B
on
{\mathfrak{a}}
given by

B(q^{\alpha}|q^{\beta})=k(q^{\alpha}|q^{\beta})+\frac{1}{2}\left(\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}(q^{\alpha},q^{\beta})-\kappa_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}(q^{\alpha},q^{\beta})-\kappa_{\frac{1}{2}}(q^{\alpha},q^{\beta})\right)

with
\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\kappa_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}
the usual Killing form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
and

\kappa_{\frac{1}{2}}
the trace of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Thus
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}},f)
contains
V^{B}({\mathfrak{b}})
as subalgebra for some bilinear form depending on
({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
.

2.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}^{k}
be some vertex algebra containing
V^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})
as subalgebra, then

H\left({\mathcal{A}}^{k}\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k),d_{0}\right)

is a vertex algebra that for generic
k
contains the
{\mathcal{W}}
-algebra
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}},f)
as subalgebra.
If
{\mathcal{A}}^{k}
is an object in the category of ordinary
V^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules, usually denoted by
{\rm{KL}}_{k}({\mathfrak{g}})
or
{\rm{KL}}_{k(\ \ |\ \ )}({\mathfrak{g}})
, then for generic level the homology is concentrated in degree zero since Theorem 6.2. of
[
KW04
]
applies. In particular the graded character of the homology is just the Euler-Poincaré character, that is the supercharacter of the complex.

3.

Let
M
be an object in
{\rm{KL}}_{k}({\mathfrak{g}})
, then
M\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
is a

V^{B}({\mathfrak{b}})
-module. The zero-modes of the
I^{\alpha}
for
q^{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{b}}
give rise to an action of
{\mathfrak{b}}
on
M\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
.
This action commutes with the action of the Virasoro zero-mode of
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}},f)
by
[
KW04
, Thm 2.1]
.
Since conformal weight spaces of
M\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
are finite-dimensional (the conformal weight of the
\Phi^{\alpha}
is
1/2
) it follows that each conformal weight space of
M\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
is an integrable
{\mathfrak{b}}
-module and hence
M\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
must be an object in a completion of
{\rm{KL}}_{B}({\mathfrak{b}})
.

4.

Let
V
be the universal Verma module as discussed in section
4.1
. As discussed in that section, we can twist the action of
R
on
V
by an automorphism
\varphi
of
R
, we call the resulting module still a universal Verma module. Let
\mathcal{V}^{k}
be a possibly infinite direct sum of universal Verma modules (all possibly twisted).
Then the homology

H\left(\mathcal{V}^{k}\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k),d_{0}\right)

is concentrated in degree zero, since Theorem 6.2. of
[
KW04
]
applies as well.

5.

Let
V
be the universal Verma module, then
V\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
is a

V^{B}({\mathfrak{b}})
-module (over
R
). As before, the zero-modes of the
I^{\alpha}
for
q^{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{b}}
give rise to an action of
{\mathfrak{b}}
on
V\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
.
This action commutes with the action of the Virasoro zero-mode of
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}},f)
.
Any conformal weight space
V_{n}
of
V
is a direct sum of universal Verma modules for
{\mathfrak{b}}
. Conformal weight spaces
F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)_{m}
of
F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
are integrable
{\mathfrak{b}}
-modules and the
tensor product of an integrable module with a universal Verma module is a direct sum of universal Verma modules.
As a
{\mathfrak{b}}
-module the weight space of conformal weight
N
is just the finite direct sum
\bigoplus_{n}V_{n}\otimes F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)_{N-n}

and hence a direct sum of universal Verma modules for
{\mathfrak{b}}
. The action of the Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{b}}
is semisimple on
V
and
F({\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
and so it also is on the tensor product of the two, i.e.

. is an object in the category
\mathcal{O}
.
Let

be an indecomposable

-submodule of

{\mathfrak{b}}

It follows that

-module, but universal Verma modules are simple, projective and injective in the category

[
Hum08
]

V^{B}({\mathfrak{b}})
-module.

\text{ch}_{\rm ref}[V_{\lambda}]=q^{-\frac{c}{24}}q^{\frac{(\lambda|\lambda+2\rho)}{2(k+h^{\vee})}-\lambda(x+y)}\ \prod_{i=1}^{L}X_{i}\ \prod_{1\leq i<j\leq L}X_{i,j}

(4.34)

where

\displaystyle X_{i}=

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{s_{i}}(y^{-2t}z)_{\infty}^{-m_{i}}\prod_{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i-1}+1\leq a<c\leq m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i}}\ \ (y^{-2t}zu_{a}u_{c}^{-1})^{-1}_{\infty}\ (y^{-2t}u_{a}^{-1}u_{c})^{-1}_{\infty}

(4.35)

and

\displaystyle X_{i,j}=

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{s_{i}}\ \ \prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i-1}+1\leq a\leq m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i}\\
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{j-1}+1\leq c\leq m_{1}+\cdots+m_{j}\end{subarray}}\ \ (y^{-2t}y^{2(s_{i}-s_{j})}zu_{a}u_{c}^{-1})^{-1}_{\infty}(y^{-2t}u_{a}^{-1}u_{c})^{-1}_{\infty}~.

(4.36)

In conclusion, note that the conformal weight refinement of identity (
4.28
) reads

\displaystyle{\rm ch}^{V}_{\rm ref}[{\mathcal{W}}_{L}^{k}

\displaystyle({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N},({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N_{1}},\dots,{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N_{L}}),(f_{1},\dots,f_{L}))]={\rm ch}_{\rm ref}^{V}[{\mathcal{W}}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N},f)]\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq L}{\rm ch}_{\rm ref}[\beta\gamma^{m_{i}m_{j}(s_{j}-s_{i})}].

(4.37)

where

{\rm ch}_{\rm ref}^{V}[{\mathcal{W}}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N},f)]=\prod_{i=1}^{L}X_{i}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq L}X_{i,j}~.

(4.38)

5
Comparison to partition functions of affine Laumon spaces

The goal of this section is to prove equation (
1.7
), which relates the partition
(
1.3
) to the refined universal Verma character
associated to the
\beta
-truncation of the vertex algebra

{\mathcal{W}}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N},f)\otimes\bigotimes\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq L}\beta\gamma^{m_{i}m_{j}(s_{j}-s_{i})}.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose the numerical invariants
{\bf r}=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{\ell-1})
are given by

(r_{0},\ldots,r_{\ell-1})=\big(\underbrace{s_{L},\ldots,s_{L}}_{m_{L}}\,,\,\underbrace{s_{L-1},\ldots,s_{L-1}}_{m_{L-1}},\ldots,\underbrace{s_{1},\ldots,s_{1}}_{m_{1}}\big)

(5.1)

for some
m_{1},\ldots,m_{L}>0
and
0<s_{1}<\cdots<s_{L}
,
L\geq 1
.
Then

\displaystyle Z_{\bf r}={\rm ch}_{\rm ref}^{V}[{\mathcal{W}}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N},f)]\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq L}\ {\rm ch}_{\rm ref}[B(m_{i},m_{j},s_{j}-s_{i})].

(5.2)

Proof
.
As shown in Proposition
3.4
, the partition function

Z_{\bf r}
is given by

\displaystyle Z_{\bf r}=

\displaystyle\prod_{a=0}^{\ell-1}\bigg(\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a}}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq a<c\leq\ell}\ \bigg(\prod_{t=1}^{r_{\ell-c}}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k}u_{a}u_{c}^{-1}}\bigg)\ \bigg(\prod_{t=1}^{r_{\ell-a}}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k-1}u_{a}^{-1}u_{c}}\bigg).

The data
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{L})
determines a partition

\{1,\ldots,\ell\}\simeq\bigcup_{i=1}^{L}\{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i-1}+1,\ldots,m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i}\}

where the sets in the right hand side are pairwise disjoint. By convention,
m_{0}=0
.
Moreover, given
1\leq c\leq\ell
, one has

r_{\ell-c}=s_{i}

if and only if
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i-1}+1\leq c\leq m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq L
.
Then the above expression for
Z_{\bf r}
is equivalent to

Z_{\bf r}=\prod_{i=1}^{L}Z_{i}\ \prod_{1\leq i<j\leq L}Z_{i,j}

(5.3)

where

\displaystyle Z_{i}=

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{s_{i}}{(y^{-2t}z)_{\infty}^{m_{i}}}\prod_{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i-1}+1\leq a<c\leq m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i}}\ \ (y^{-2t}zu_{a}u_{c}^{-1})^{-1}_{\infty}\ (y^{-2t}u_{a}^{-1}u_{c})^{-1}_{\infty}

(5.4)

and

\displaystyle Z_{i,j}=

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{s_{j}}\prod_{v=1}^{s_{i}}\ \ \prod_{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i-1}+1\leq a\leq m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i}}\ \ \prod_{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{j-1}+1\leq c\leq m_{1}+\cdots+m_{j}}(y^{-2t}zu_{a}u_{c}^{-1})^{-1}_{\infty}(y^{-2v}u_{a}^{-1}u_{c})^{-1}_{\infty}~.

(5.5)

Note that the
Z_{i}
coincide with the factors
X_{i}
defined in equation (
4.35
). Furthermore, note that

\displaystyle Z_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\prod_{(a,c)}\prod_{t=1}^{s_{j}}\prod_{v=1}^{s_{i}}\prod_{(a,c)}(y^{-2t}zu_{a}u_{c}^{-1})^{-1}_{\infty}(y^{-2v}u_{a}^{-1}u_{c})^{-1}_{\infty}

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\prod_{(a,c)}\prod_{t=1}^{s_{j}-s_{i}}(y^{-2t}zu_{a}u_{c}^{-1})^{-1}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\prod_{(a,c)}\prod_{t=1}^{s_{i}}\prod_{v=1}^{s_{i}}(y^{-2(t+s_{j}-s_{i})}zu_{a}u_{c}^{-1})^{-1}_{\infty}(y^{-2v}u_{a}^{-1}u_{c})^{-1}_{\infty}~.

where
\prod_{(a,c)}
stands for

\prod_{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i-1}+1\leq a\leq m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i}}\ \ \prod_{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{j-1}+1\leq c\leq m_{1}+\cdots+m_{j}}~.

By comparison to equations (
4.30
) and (
4.36
)
one obtains

Z_{i,j}=X_{i,j}\,{\rm ch}_{\rm ref}[B(m_{i},m_{j},s_{j}-s_{i})].

In conclusion, using equation (
4.38
), one obtains,

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{L}X_{i}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq L}\ X_{i,j}\ {\rm ch}_{\rm ref}[B(m_{i},m_{j},s_{j}-s_{i})]

(5.7)

\displaystyle={\rm ch}_{\rm ref}^{V}[{\mathcal{W}}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N},f)]\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq L}\ {\rm ch}_{\rm ref}[B(m_{i},m_{j},s_{j}-s_{i})].

\Box

Appendix A
Some combinatorial identities

This section proves equations (
3.4
) and (
3.5
).

Given a Young diagram
\mu
and an integer
-\ell+1\leq c\leq\ell-1
, let

\displaystyle{\sf N}^{\geq 0}_{1}(\mu;c)

\displaystyle=\{(i,j)\in\mu\,|\,\mu^{\prime}_{i}-j\equiv c\ {\rm mod}\ \ell\,\}

\displaystyle{\sf N}^{>0}_{1}(\mu;c)

\displaystyle=\{(i,j)\in\mu\,|\,\mu^{\prime}_{i}-j>0,\ \mu^{\prime}_{i}-j\equiv c\ {\rm mod}\ \ell\,\}

\displaystyle{\sf N}^{\geq 0}_{2}(\mu;c)

\displaystyle=\{(i,j)\in\mu\,|\,j-1\equiv c\ {\rm mod}\ \ell\,\}

Moreover, for fixed
1\leq i\leq{\rm col}(\mu)
let

\displaystyle{\sf N}^{\geq 0}_{1}(i;c)

\displaystyle=\{1\leq j\leq\mu_{i}^{\prime}\,|\,\mu^{\prime}_{i}-j\equiv c\ {\rm mod}\ \ell\,\}

\displaystyle{\sf N}^{>0}_{1}(i;c)

\displaystyle=\{1\leq j\leq\mu_{i}^{\prime}-1\,|\,\mu^{\prime}_{i}-j\equiv c\ {\rm mod}\ \ell\,\}

\displaystyle{\sf N}^{\geq 0}_{2}(i;c)

\displaystyle=\{1\leq j\leq\mu_{i}^{\prime}\,|\,j-1\equiv c\ {\rm mod}\ \ell\,\}

where
{\rm col}(\mu)
denotes the number of columns of
\mu
and
\mu_{i}^{\prime}
denotes the height of the
i
-th column.

As in equation (
2.3
) a partition
\mu
will be said to be
a
-colored if and only if the box
(i,j)\in\mu
is marked by
a-j+1
mod
\ell
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
n_{a-c}
denote the number
of boxes of
\mu
marked by
a-c
mod
\ell
. Then

n_{a-c}=|{\sf N}_{1}^{\geq 0}(\mu;c)|

(A.1)

for any
-\ell+1\leq c\leq\ell-1
.

Proof
.
By definition,
n_{a-c}=|{\sf N}_{2}^{\geq 0}(\mu;c)|
. Hence
it suffices to show that

|{\sf N}_{1}^{\geq 0}(\mu;c)|=|{\sf N}_{2}^{\geq 0}(\mu;c)|

(A.2)

for any
-\ell+1\leq c\leq\ell-1
.
Setting
j^{\prime}=\mu_{i}^{\prime}-j
, note that

\displaystyle{\sf N}^{\geq 0}_{1}(i;c)

\displaystyle=\{1\leq j\leq\mu_{i}^{\prime}\,|\,\mu^{\prime}_{i}-j\equiv c\ {\rm mod}\ \ell\,\}

\displaystyle=\{0\leq j^{\prime}\leq\mu_{i}^{\prime}-1\,|\,j^{\prime}\equiv c\ {\rm mod}\ \ell\,\},

which coincides with
{\sf N}^{\geq 0}_{2}(i;c)
by definition.
In conclusion, one obtains

|{\sf N}_{1}^{\geq 0}(i;c)|=|{\sf N}_{2}^{\geq 0}(i;c)|

for any
1\leq i\leq{\rm col}(\mu)
, hence equation (
A.5
) holds.

\Box

Lemma A.2
.

The following identity holds for any partition
\mu
:

|{\sf N}_{1}^{>0}(\mu;c)|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|{\sf N}_{2}^{\geq 0}(\mu;c)|&{\rm for}\ -\ell+1\leq c\leq\ell-1,\ c\neq 0\\
&\\
|{\sf N}_{2}^{\geq 0}(\mu;c)|-{\rm col}(\mu)&{\rm for}\ c=0,\\
\end{array}\right.

(A.3)

where
{\rm col}(\mu)
denotes the number of columns of
\mu
.

Proof
.
The defining conditions of
{\sf N}_{1}^{>0}(i;c)
are equivalent to

1\leq j\leq\mu^{\prime}_{i}-1,\hskip 20.00003pt\mu^{\prime}_{i}-j=k\ell+c,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Suppose
\mu^{\prime}_{i}=q_{i}\ell+a_{i}
,
0\leq a_{i}\leq\ell-1
. Then these conditions are equivalent to

1\leq(q_{i}-k)\ell+a_{i}-c\leq q_{i}\ell+a_{i}-1~,

hence also to

{1-c\over\ell}\leq k\leq q_{i}+{a_{i}-c-1\over\ell}~.

Since
\ell\geq 2
, and
k,c,q_{i},a_{i}
are integers so that

-\ell+1\leq c\leq\ell-1,\hskip 20.00003pt0\leq a_{i}\leq\ell-1~,

these conditions are further equivalent to

\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq q_{i}-1

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle if}\ 1\leq c\leq\ell-1\ {\rm and}\ 0\leq a_{i}\leq c

(A.4)

\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq q_{i}

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle if}\ 1\leq c\leq\ell-2\ {\rm and}\ c+1\leq a_{i}\leq\ell-1

\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq q_{i}-1

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle if}\ c=0\ {\rm and}\ a_{i}=0

\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq q_{i}

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle if}\ c=0\ {\rm and}\ 1\leq a_{i}\leq\ell-1

\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq q_{i}

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle if}\ -\ell+1\leq c\leq-1\ {\rm and}\ 0\leq a_{i}\leq\ell+c

\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq q_{i}+1

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle if}\ -\ell+1\leq c\leq-2\ {\rm and}\ \ell+c+1\leq a_{i}\leq\ell-1

On the other hand, as shown in the proof of Lemma

A.1
, the defining conditions of
{\sf N}_{2}^{\geq 0}(i;c)

are

-{c\over\ell}\leq k\leq q_{i}+{a_{i}-c-1\over\ell}~.

(A.5)

These conditions are similarly equivalent to

\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq q_{i}-1

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle if}\ 0\leq c\leq\ell-1\ {\rm and}\ 0\leq a_{i}\leq c

(A.6)

\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq q_{i}

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle if}\ 0\leq c\leq\ell-2\ {\rm and}\ c+1\leq a_{i}\leq\ell-1

\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq q_{i}

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle if}\ -\ell+1\leq c\leq-1\ {\rm and}\ 0\leq a_{i}\leq\ell+c

\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq q_{i}+1

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle if}\ -\ell+1\leq c\leq-2\ {\rm and}\ \ell+c+1\leq a_{i}\leq\ell-1.

By comparison with (
A.4
), one obtains the following table

\begin{array}[]{cccc}&&|{\sf N}_{1}^{>0}(i;c)|&|{\sf N}_{2}^{\geq 0}(i;c)|\\
&&\\
1\leq c\leq\ell-1,\ 0\leq a_{i}\leq c&&q_{i}&q_{i}\\
&&\\
1\leq c\leq\ell-2,\ c+1\leq a_{i}\leq\ell-1&&q_{i}+1&q_{i}+1\\
&&\\
c=0,\ a_{i}=0&&q_{i}-1&q_{i}\\
&&\\
c=0,\ 1\leq a_{i}\leq\ell-1&&q_{i}&q_{i}+1\\
&&\\
-\ell-1\leq c\leq-1,\ 0\leq a_{i}\leq\ell+c&&q_{i}&q_{i}\\
&&\\
-\ell-1\leq c\leq-2,\ \ell+c+1\leq a_{i}\leq\ell-1&&q_{i}+1&q_{i}+1\\
&&\\
\end{array}

In conclusion,

|{\sf N}_{1}^{>0}(i;c)|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|{\sf N}_{2}^{\geq 0}(i;c)|&{\rm for}\ -\ell+1\leq c\leq\ell-1,\ c\neq 0\\
&\\
|{\sf N}_{2}^{\geq 0}(i;c)|-1&{\rm for}\ c=0.\\
\end{array}\right.

This yields equation (
A.3
).

\Box

Appendix B
Some product identities

This section proves equation (
3.19
).

Lemma B.1
.

The following identity holds:

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a,c\leq\ell-1\\
a\neq c\end{subarray}}\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k}({\widetilde{q}}_{a-c})^{-1}({\widetilde{q}}_{a-c-1})^{-1}\cdots({\widetilde{q}}_{a-\ell+1})^{-1}}=

(B.1)

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq a<c\leq\ell-1}\bigg(\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k}({\widetilde{q}}_{a-c})^{-1}({\widetilde{q}}_{a-c-1})^{-1}\cdots({\widetilde{q}}_{a-\ell+1})^{-1}}

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a-c+\ell}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k-1}{\widetilde{q}}_{a-c}{\widetilde{q}}_{a-c-1}\cdots{\widetilde{q}}_{a-\ell+1}}\bigg).

Proof
.
Note the set isomorphism

\{1\leq c<a\leq\ell-1\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\{1\leq a<c\leq\ell-1\}

(a,c)\mapsto(a-c,\ell-c)

Let
a^{\prime}=a-c
and
c^{\prime}=\ell-c
.
Since

z={\widetilde{q}}_{a}{\widetilde{q}}_{a-1}\cdots{\widetilde{q}}_{a-\ell+1}

for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
one has

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq c<a\leq\ell-1}\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k}({\widetilde{q}}_{a-c})^{-1}({\widetilde{q}}_{a-c-1})^{-1}\cdots({\widetilde{q}}_{a-\ell+1})^{-1}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq c<a\leq\ell-1}\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k-1}{\widetilde{q}}_{a}{\widetilde{q}}_{a-c-1}\cdots{\widetilde{q}}_{a-c+1}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq c<a\leq\ell-1}\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k-1}{\widetilde{q}}_{a-\ell}{\widetilde{q}}_{a-c-1-\ell}\cdots{\widetilde{q}}_{a-c+1-\ell}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq a^{\prime}<c^{\prime}\leq\ell-1}\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a^{\prime}-c^{\prime}+\ell}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k-1}{\widetilde{q}}_{a^{\prime}-c^{\prime}}{\widetilde{q}}_{a^{\prime}-c^{\prime}-1}\cdots{\widetilde{q}}_{a^{\prime}-\ell+1}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq a<c\leq\ell-1}\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a-c+\ell}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k-1}{\widetilde{q}}_{a-c}{\widetilde{q}}_{a-c-1}\cdots{\widetilde{q}}_{a-\ell+1}}

\Box

Recall that the variables (
3.13
) are given by

u_{a}=({\widetilde{q}}_{-a}\cdots{\widetilde{q}}{\widetilde{q}}_{-\ell+1})^{-1},\ 1\leq a\leq\ell-1.

Then one has:

Lemma B.2
.

Identity (
B.1
) can be further written as

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a,c\leq\ell-1\\
a\neq c\end{subarray}}\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k}({\widetilde{q}}_{a-c})^{-1}({\widetilde{q}}_{a-c-1})^{-1}\cdots({\widetilde{q}}_{a-\ell+1})^{-1}}=

(B.2)

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq a<c\leq\ell-1}\ \bigg(\prod_{t=1}^{r_{\ell-c}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k}u_{a}u_{c}^{-1}}\bigg)\ \bigg(\prod_{t=1}^{r_{\ell-a}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k-1}u_{a}^{-1}u_{c}}\bigg)

Proof
.
Again, note the set isomorphism

\{1\leq a<c\leq\ell-1\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\{1\leq a<c\leq\ell-1\}

(a,c)\mapsto(c-a,\ell-a)

Let
a^{\prime}=c-a
and
c^{\prime}=\ell-a
.
This yields

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq a<c\leq\ell-1}\bigg(\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k}({\widetilde{q}}_{a-c})^{-1}({\widetilde{q}}_{a-c-1})^{-1}\cdots({\widetilde{q}}_{a-\ell+1})^{-1}}

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{r_{a-c+\ell}}{1\over 1-y^{-2t}z^{k-1}{\widetilde{q}}_{a-c}{\widetilde{q}}_{a-c-1}\cdots{\widetilde{q}}_{a-\ell+1}}\bigg)=

\Box

Perturbation Method in Orlicz Sequence Spaces
H. Topalova, N. Zlateva

Abstract
We develop a new perturbation method in Orlicz sequence spaces ℓ M \ell_{M} with Orlicz function M M satisfying Δ 2 \Delta_{2} condition at zero. This result allows one to support from below any bounded below lower semicontinuous function with bounded support, with a perturbation of the defining function σ M \sigma_{M} . We give few examples how the method can be used for determining the type of the smoothness of certain Orlicz spaces. Key words: Orilcz spaces, perturbation space, variational principle 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 46N10; Secondary 35A15, 49J45, 90C48



1
Introduction

Variational principles are well established and widely used tools in renorming theory of Banach spaces and especially so after the prominent monograph
[
2
]
.

In this article we establish a new perturbation tool – or variational principle – fit to the structure of Orlicz sequence spaces. If the Orlicz function
M
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero then for each proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function
f
on the Orlicz space
\ell_{M}
and each
\varepsilon>0
there is a perturbation

g_{a}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}M(x_{n})

of the defining function
\sigma_{M}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(x_{n})
such that
0\leq a_{n}<\varepsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f+g_{a}
attains a minimum, see for details Theorem
4.3
.

This result is proved by adapting some ideas from
[
9
,
10
]
.

The benefit of such perturbation, i.e. a perturbation keeping the form of
\sigma_{M}
, is that we can adapt the proof of
[
2
, Lemma II.5.4]
, see also
[
16
]
, to finer situations. That is, we apply the above result for the function
f-\sigma_{M}
, where
f
is a proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function with bounded domain (and, therefore,
f-\sigma_{M}
is bounded below) to get the following statement, see for details Theorem
4.4
.

If
f
is a proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function with bounded domain then there are
a_{n}\in[1,2]
such that the function

x\to f(x)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}M(x_{n})

attains a minimum.

In other words, we can support
f
from below with a function very similar to
\sigma_{M}
. So, if
M
, and thus
\sigma_{M}
, is “too rough upwards” then
f
can not be “too smooth” inside its domain. This idea applied to
f=b^{-2}
, where
b
is a nontrivial bump on
\ell_{M}
, may prove the non-existence of a bump with certain smoothness properties.

When applied to
\ell_{1}
the latter gives that there is no nontrivial Fréchet differentiable bump on
\ell_{1}
, see Proposition
5.1
. We can use this simple example to illustrate an important point. Another way to prove the nonexistence of nontrivial
Lipschitz
Fréchet differentiable bump on
\ell_{1}
is to use as a perturbation space the Banach space of all bounded Lipschitz continuous and Fréchet differentiable functions and apply
[
2
, Lemma I.2.5]
. But for doing this one needs global Lipschitz continuity of the bump. Therefore, it is more beneficial – here and in general – to perturb
\sigma_{M}
, which in this case is the norm. (Note that the proof works for any Orlicz function
M
satisfying
\limsup_{t\to 0}M(t)/t>0
, but from convexity it follows that such function is equivalent at zero to
|\cdot|
, so the space
\ell_{M}
is isomorph to
\ell_{1}
.)

In a similar simple way we prove in Proposition
5.2
that if
M
has
\Delta_{2}
at zero and
\limsup_{t\to 0}M(t)/t^{p}=\infty
for some
p\in(1,2]
then on
\ell_{M}
there is no nontrivial bump
b
with the estimate
b(x+h)=b(x)+\langle b^{\prime}(x),h\rangle+O(\|h\|^{p})
at each point
x
. In particular, on
\ell_{p}
,
p\in(1,2]
there is no nontrivial bump
b
with the estimate
b(x+h)=b(x)+\langle b^{\prime}(x),h\rangle+O(\|h\|^{q})
for
q>p
. In
[
2
, Chapter V]
it is proved that there is no nontrivial bump with locally
(q{-}1)
-Hölder derivative, so our result apparently is bit more general. Our approach is also more direct, because in
[
2
]
a deep result is used to regularise the bump to one which has globally
(q{-}1)
-Hölder derivative.

Our next example, Proposition
5.3
, is somewhat different. From the previous example it immediately follows that on
l_{p}
for
p\in(1,2)
there is no twice Freéchet differentiable bump, because two times Freéchet differentiability implies the estimate
b(x+h)=b(x)+\langle b^{\prime}(x),h\rangle+O(\|h\|^{2})
. It is known, see
[
8
]
, but not easy to prove that there is even no twice Gâteaux differentiable bump. We show that if
M
has
\Delta_{2}
at zero and
\lim_{t\to 0}M^{\prime\prime}(t)=\infty
, then there is no twice Gâteaux differentiable bump on
l_{M}
. In particular, there is no twice Gâteaux differentiable bump on
l_{p}
for
p\in(1,2)
.

These applications can be proved by Stegall Variational Principle (
[
17
,
18
]
and
[
6
, Theorem 11.6]
) as for example in
[
14
]
, but the approach here is more straightforward and explicit.

The article is organised as follows. In Section
2
we define the perturbation space, see Definition
2.2
and use it to prove an abstract variational principle in Theorem
2.3
. Even more abstract variant is given in Theorem
2.5
. In Section
3
we give some facts about Orlicz sequence spaces we need in the sequel and prove Lemma
3.1
which is of independent interest. In Section
4
we find a perturbation space in Orlicz sequence space where the Orlicz function
M
is non-degenerate and satisfies
\Delta_{2}
condition at
0
, see Lemma
4.2
. Theorem
4.3
is a variational principle in such a Orlicz space. The latter is used for proving Theorem
4.4
which is our tool for determining the type of the smoothness of certain Orlicz spaces. Such results are presented in the last Section
5
.

2
Abstract perturbation technique

We need to refine the main technique of
[
9
]
, because the axioms as formulated there will not do in our context here. We use the opportunity to also somewhat simplify the proof. We start with recalling some definitions.

Let
(X,\|.\|)
be a Banach space and let
S
be a nonempty
subset in
X
.
By
\alpha(S)
we denote the Kuratowski index of non-compactness of
S
(see
[
11
]
), that is the infimum of all
\varepsilon>0
for which
S

admits finite (or compact)
\varepsilon
net.

For a function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
we set

\Omega_{f}^{S}(\varepsilon)=\left\{x\in S:f(x)\leq\inf_{S}f+\varepsilon\right\}.

Clearly,
\Omega_{f}^{S}(0)=\mathrm{argmin\,}_{S}f
.

We will need the following simpler version of
[
9
, Lemma 4]
: for any
f,g:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, bounded below on
S
, and
\delta>0
,

\Omega_{f}^{S}(\delta)\cap\Omega_{g}^{S}(\delta)\neq\varnothing\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Omega_{f+g}^{S}(\delta)\subset\Omega_{f}^{S}(3\delta)\cap\Omega_{g}^{S}(3\delta).

(2.1)

Indeed, let
x\in\Omega_{f}^{S}(\delta)\cap\Omega_{g}^{S}(\delta)
and
y\in\Omega_{f+g}^{S}(\delta)
. Then

\delta\geq f(y)+g(y)-\inf(f+g)\geq f(y)+g(y)-(f(x)+g(x))\geq(f(y)-\inf_{S}f-\delta)+(g(y)-\inf_{S}g-\delta)
. Since
g(y)-\inf_{S}g\geq 0
, we derive
\delta\geq f(y)-\inf_{S}f-2\delta
. So,
y\in\Omega_{f}^{S}(3\delta)
. In the same way,
y\in\Omega_{g}^{S}(3\delta)
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
10
]
, Definition 4.1)
.

Let
S\subset X
and
f:S\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. We say that the minimization
problem

(f,S)\quad\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}f(x)\to\min\\
x\in S\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

is
well-posed modulus compact
, abbreviated as
wpmc
, if
\inf_{S}f\in\mathbb{R}
and

\Omega_{f}^{S}(0)
is nonempty and compact set such that if

\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a minimizing sequence:

f(x_{n})\to\inf_{S}f,\quad n\to\infty,

then

d(x_{n},\Omega_{f}^{S}(0))\to 0.

The meaning of the term wpmc is quite natural. If
\Omega_{f}^{S}(0)
is a singleton, then we get the well-known notion of Tikhonov well-posedness (for details
see e.g.
[
4
, Chapter I]
).
From Kuratowski Lemma we have, see
[
10
, Proposition 4.2]
:
for a closed set
S\subset X
and a proper lower semicontinuous function
f:S\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
bounded below on
S

(f,S)\text{ is wpmc }\iff\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\alpha(\Omega_{f}^{S}(t))=0.

(2.3)

Clearly, if
X
is finite dimensional and
S
is closed then for any proper lower semicontinuous function
f:S\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
bounded below on
S
the problem
(f,S)
is wpmc. The idea for general
X
is to perturb
f
by a suitable function so that the perturbed problem is wpmc. Here we differ from the usual variational principles, as those considered in
[
2
]
, where the problem is perturbed to become Tikhonov well posed.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space and let
S\subseteq X
be a non-empty set. The space of

(\cal{P},\|\cdot\|_{\cal{P}})
of real continuous and bounded on
S
functions, is called a
perturbation space on

S
if

\mathrm{(\char 16\relax)}

\cal{P}
is a convex cone;

\mathrm{(\char 16\relax\char 16\relax)}

\cal{P}
is complete with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\cal{P}}
defined on
\mathrm{span\,}\cal{P}
, that dominates the uniform convergence on
S
, that is, for some
c>0

\sup_{x\in S}|g(x)|\leq c\|g\|_{\cal{P}},\quad\forall g\in\mathrm{span\,}\cal{P}.

(2.4)

In other words,
(\mathrm{span\,}\cal{P},\|\cdot\|_{\cal{P}})
is a Banach space of bounded on
S
continuous functions, and
\cal{P}
is its positive cone.

\mathrm{(\char 16\relax\char 16\relax\char 16\relax)}

For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x\in S
there exists
g\in\cal{P}
(depending on
x
) such that

\|g\|_{\cal{P}}\leq\varepsilon,\ x\in\Omega_{g}^{S}(\delta),\text{ and }\ \alpha(\Omega_{g}^{S}(3\delta))\leq\varepsilon.

(2.5)

Note that in the axioms used in
[
9
]
it is required that
x\in\Omega_{g}^{S}(0)
, which is too strong. That is why we weaken this requirement.

Here is the promised refinement of
[
9
, Theorem 10]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space and let
S\subset X
be a non-empty closed set in
X
. Let
(\cal{P},\|\cdot\|_{\cal{P}})
be a perturbation space on
S
. Let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper, lower semicontinuous and bounded below on
S
function.

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
g\in\cal{P}
such that
\|g\|_{\cal{P}}<\varepsilon
and

(f+g,S)
is wpmc. In particular,
f+g
attains its minimum on
S
.

Proof.

Consider for
n\in\mathbb{N}
the subset
A_{n}
of
\cal{P}
defined by

A_{n}:=\left\{g\in{\cal{P}}:\exists t>0:\ \alpha\left(\Omega_{f+g}^{S}(t)\right)<\frac{1}{n}\right\}.

(2.6)

We will show that
A_{n}
is dense and open in
(\cal{P},\|\cdot\|_{\cal{P}})
. Then by Baire Category Theorem there will be

g\in\cap A_{n}
such that
\|g\|_{\cal{P}}<\varepsilon
and by (
2.3
) the problem
(f+g,S)
will be wpmc.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
g\in A_{n}
be arbitrary and let
\beta>0
be such that

\alpha\left(\Omega_{f+g}^{S}(3\beta)\right)<\frac{1}{n}.

For any
h\in\mathrm{span\,}\cal{P}
such that
\|h\|_{\cal{P}}<\beta/2c
it follows from (
2.4
) that
h(x)-h(y)<\beta
for any
x,y\in S
and, therefore,
\Omega_{h}^{S}(\beta)=S
. From (
2.1
) for
(f+g)
and
h
it follows that

\Omega^{S}_{f+g+h}(\beta)\subset\Omega_{f+g}^{S}(3\beta),

thus
\alpha(\Omega^{S}_{f+g+h}(\beta))<1/n
and
g+h\in A_{n}
. So,
A_{n}
is open.

Let now
h\in\cal{P}
be arbitrary. Fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon\in(0,1/n)
.
Let
\delta>0
be provided by
\mathrm{(\char 16\relax\char 16\relax\char 16\relax)}
of Definition
2.2
. Fix

x\in\Omega_{f+h}^{S}(\delta),

and let
g\in\cal{P}
satisfy (
2.5
). The latter implies that
x\in\Omega_{g}^{S}(\delta)
. From (
2.1
) it follows that

\Omega_{f+h+g}^{S}(\delta)\subset\Omega_{g}^{S}(3\delta)\Longrightarrow\alpha(\Omega_{f+h+g}^{S}(\delta))\leq\varepsilon.

As
\varepsilon<1/n
, this means that
h+g\in A_{n}
. Since
\|g\|_{\cal{P}}\leq\varepsilon
, see (
2.5
), the distance from
h
to
A_{n}
is smaller than
\varepsilon
. In other words,
A_{n}
is dense in
\cal{P}
.
∎

For the purpose of this article we made the axioms in Definition
2.2
more immediate to check rather than most general. It is worth however to point out how our approach relates to the more general topological context of
[
12
]
where variational principles are derived from the properties of the
\varepsilon
-argmin map

g\to\Omega_{f+g}(\varepsilon).

In such context the abstract of our approach is as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
S
be a non-empty closed subset of the Banach space
X
. We say that the positive function
\gamma
defined on the closed subsets of
S
is
Cantor measure
if

\mathrm{(\char 16\relax)}

\gamma
is monotone with respect to inclusion, that is,

A\subset B\subset S\Rightarrow\gamma(A)\leq\gamma(B).

\mathrm{(\char 16\relax\char 16\relax)}

\gamma
satisfies the Cantor Lemma in the sense that if the non-empty subsets
(A_{n})_{1}^{\infty}
of
S
are nested and closed, that is,
A_{n+1}\subset A_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma(A_{n})=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\neq\varnothing.

Examples of Cantor measures are e.g. the classical one
\mathrm{diam}\,A
, the Kuratovski index of non-compactness
\alpha(A)
with which we predominantly work here, and the similar De Blasi index of non-weak-compactness
\beta(A)=\inf\{\varepsilon>0:\ A\subset K+\varepsilon B_{X},
where
K\subset X
is weakly compact set
\}
, see
[
1
]
.

We can call the problem
(f,S)
, see (
2.2
),
\gamma
-well-posed if

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\gamma(\Omega^{S}_{f}(\varepsilon))=0,

where
\gamma
is a Cantor measure on
S
. Obviously, a
\gamma
-well-posed problem
(f,S)
has a solution, that is,
\Omega^{S}_{f}(0)\neq\varnothing
.

The even more abstract version of Theorem
2.3
is:

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
S
be a non-empty closed subset of the Banach space
X
and let
\gamma
be a Cantor measure on
S
.

Let
(\mathcal{P},+,\rho)
be a compete metric semigroup with zero. Let
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\mathcal{P}\cup\{f\}
and let
(\mathcal{P}_{1},+)
be a semigroup. Let the maps

T_{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{P}_{1}\to 2^{S},

where
\varepsilon>0
, satisfy

\ T_{\delta}(u)\subset T_{\varepsilon}(u),\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{P}_{1},\ \forall 0<\delta<\varepsilon,

(2.7)

\forall u,v\in\mathcal{P}_{1},\ \forall\delta>0:T_{\delta}(u)\cap T_{\delta}(v)\neq\varnothing\Rightarrow\\
T_{\delta}(u+v)\subset T_{3\delta}(u)\cap T_{3\delta}(v),

(2.8)

\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0:\ \rho(0,g)<\delta\Rightarrow T_{\varepsilon}(g)=S,

(2.9)

\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0:\ \forall x\in S\ \exists g\in\mathcal{P}:\\
\rho(0,g)\leq\varepsilon,\ x\in T_{\delta}(g)\text{ and }\gamma(T_{3\delta}(g))\leq\varepsilon.

(2.10)

Then the set
U\subset\mathcal{P}
defined as

g\in U\iff\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}T_{f+g}(\varepsilon)\neq\varnothing

is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
\mathcal{P}
.

Clearly, if
{\cal P}
is a perturbation space, and
f
is a proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below on
S
function, Theorem
2.5
implies Theorem
2.3
.

Theorem
2.5
can be proved following exactly the proof of Theorem
2.3
, that is, by showing that the sets

A_{n}:=\left\{g\in{\cal{P}}:\exists s>0:\ \gamma\left(T_{s}\right)<\frac{1}{n}\right\}

are dense and open. For the openness we have to add only that, because the addition is continuous, the set
\{g+h:\ \rho(0,h)<\delta\}
is a neighbourhood of
g
in
\mathcal{P}
.

3
Some facts about Orlicz sequence spaces

Here we recall some well known facts about Orlicz sequence spaces, see e.g.
[
13
,
7
]
.

An Orlicz function
M
is a continuous non-decreasing and convex
function defined for
t\geq 0
such that
M(0)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}M(t)=\infty
. If
M(t)=0
for
some
t>0
,
M
is said to be a degenerate Orlicz function.

We represent the elements of the space
\ell_{\infty}
of bounded sequences as
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},\ldots)
or

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}e_{n},

where
(e_{n})_{1}^{\infty}
is the canonical basis of
c_{0}
– the space of tending to zero sequences. Below we will clarify the exact convergence meaning of this sum in our context. We denote for
x\in\ell_{\infty}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

P_{N}x:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}e_{i},\quad P_{N}^{\bot}x:=x-P_{N}x=\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}x_{i}e_{i}.

(3.1)

The even convex function
\sigma_{M}:\ell_{\infty}\to[0,\infty]
is defined
by

\sigma_{M}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(|x_{n}|).

(3.2)

The Orlicz sequence space
\ell_{M}
is the linear subspace of
\ell_{\infty}
consisting of all
x\in\ell_{\infty}
satisfying

\sigma_{M}(x/\rho)<\infty
for some
\rho>0
. Equipped with the norm given by the Minkowski
functional of
\{x\in\ell_{\infty}:\sigma_{M}(x)\leq 1\}
, i.e.

\|x\|:=\inf\left\{\rho>0:\sigma_{M}\left(\frac{x}{\rho}\right)\leq 1\right\},

the space
\ell_{M}
is a Banach space. If
M
is degenerate then
\ell_{M}\equiv\ell_{\infty}
and this case is not interesting for us. If
M
is non-degenerate then, of course,
\ell_{M}\subset c_{0}
.

From the
convexity of
\sigma_{M}
we have

\sigma_{M}(x)\leq\|x\|,\text{ if }\|x\|\leq 1;\quad\sigma_{M}(x)>\|x\|,\text{ if }\|x\|>1.

(3.3)

The non-degenerate Orlicz function
M

satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero
if

\liminf_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{M(t)}{M(2t)}>0.

(3.4)

By the end of this section we work with a non-degenerate Orlicz function
M
satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero.

Fix
\overline{t}
such that

\overline{t}>1,\text{ and }M(\overline{t})>1.

(3.5)

Since the function
M(t)/M(2t)
is continuous on
(0,\overline{t}]
, from (
3.5
) it follows that
\displaystyle\inf_{0<t\leq\overline{t}}M(t)/M(2t)>0
and, therefore, there is
C\in(0,1)
such that

M(t)\geq CM(2t),\quad\forall t\in[0,\overline{t}].

(3.6)

Note that from (
3.2
), (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and
B_{\ell_{M}}=\{x:\ \sigma_{M}(x)\leq 1\}
, see (
3.3
), it immediately follows that

\sigma_{M}(x)\geq C\sigma_{M}(2x),\quad\forall x\in B_{\ell_{M}}.

(3.7)

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
x\in\ell_{M}
be such that
\|x\|\leq 2^{m}
, that is,
\sigma_{M}(2^{-m}x)\leq 1
. In particular,
M(2^{-m}|x_{n}|)\leq 1
for all
n
, so (
3.5
) gives
|x_{n}|\leq 2^{m}\overline{t}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
A\subset\mathbb{N}
be the set of those
n
for which
|x_{n}|\geq\overline{t}
. Because
x\in c_{0}
the set
A
is finite. Let
|A|
be the number of elements of
A
. Clearly,
1\geq\sigma_{M}(2^{-m}x)\geq\sum_{n\in A}M(2^{-m}|x_{n}|)\geq|A|M(2^{-m}\overline{t})
.
So,
|A|\leq 1/M(2^{-m}\overline{t})
, and
\sum_{n\in A}M(|x_{n}|)\leq M(2^{m}\overline{t})/M(2^{-m}\overline{t})
.
On the other hand, (
3.6
) iterated gives
1\geq\sigma_{M}(2^{-m}x)\geq\sum_{n\not\in A}M(2^{-m}|x_{n}|)\geq C^{m}\sum_{n\not\in A}M(|x_{n}|)
. So,
\sum_{n\not\in A}M(|x_{n}|)\leq C^{-m}
and
\sigma_{M}(x)\leq C^{-m}+M(2^{m}\overline{t})/M(2^{-m}\overline{t})
for all
x\in 2^{m}B_{\ell_{M}}
.
Consequently,

\sup_{KB_{\ell_{M}}}\sigma_{M}<\infty,\quad\forall K>0.

(3.8)

Also, provided
M
is non-degenerate and satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero,
\sigma_{M}
is convex and bounded on the bounded sets and, therefore, continuous (see e.g. Lemma
4.1
). In this case, (
3.3
) implies that

\|x\|=1\iff\sigma_{M}(x)=1.

(3.9)

We need the
\Delta_{2}
condition mostly because of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a non-degenerate Orlicz function. Then
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero
if and only if
\sigma_{M}
has a strong minimum at zero, or, in other words, the problem

\sigma_{M}(x)\to\min,\quad x\in\ell_{M}

is Tikhonov well posed.

Proof.

Let
M
be a non-degenerate Orlicz function that satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero. Iterating on (
3.7
) we get

\sigma_{M}\left(\frac{x}{2^{m}}\right)\geq C\sigma_{M}\left(\frac{x}{2^{m-1}}\right)\geq\ldots\geq C^{m}\sigma_{M}(x),\quad\forall x\in B_{\ell_{M}},\ \forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

So, if
y\in\ell_{M}
is such that
\|y\|>2^{-m}
, then using (
3.9
),

\sigma_{M}(y)>\sigma_{M}\left(\frac{y}{2^{m}\|y\|}\right)\geq C^{m}\sigma_{M}\left(\frac{y}{\|y\|}\right)=C^{m}.

Therefore,

\Omega_{\sigma_{M}}(C^{m})\subset 2^{-m}B_{\ell_{M}},

and

\mathrm{diam}\,\Omega_{\sigma_{M}}(t)\to 0,\text{ as }t\to 0.

(3.10)

Assume now that
M
fails (
3.4
), that is, there is a sequence
t_{k}\searrow 0
such that

\frac{M(t_{k})}{M(2t_{k})}<\frac{1}{k}.

(3.11)

We may, of course, assume that
2t_{k}<1
and
M(2t_{k})<1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Define

i_{k}:=[M(2t_{k})^{-1}],\quad x^{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{i_{k}}t_{k}e_{i}.

(3.12)

By definition,

\sigma_{M}(2x^{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{i_{k}}M(2t_{k})=i_{k}M(2t_{k}).

But
i_{k}\leq 1/M(2t_{k})
, so
\sigma_{M}(2x^{k})\leq 1
. Also,
i_{k}>1/M(2t_{k})-1
and, because
M(2t_{k})\to 0
, we have
\sigma_{M}(2x^{k})\to 1
. From (
3.3
) it follows that

\|2x^{k}\|\to 1,\text{ as }k\to\infty\iff\|x^{k}\|\to 1/2.

On the other hand, from (
3.11
)

\sigma_{M}(x^{k})=i_{k}M(t_{k})\leq\left(\frac{1}{M(2t_{k})}+1\right)M(t_{k})<\frac{1}{k}+M(t_{k})\to 0.\qed

If
M
is non-degenerate and satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero we can derive one more important implication from (
3.10
).
Let
x\in\ell_{M}
. From (
3.8
) it follows that
\sigma_{M}(P_{N}^{\bot}x)\to 0
as
N\to\infty
and from (
3.10
) it follows that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|P_{N}^{\bot}x\|=0\iff\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|x-\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}e_{i}\right\|=0,

that is,
(e_{n})_{1}^{\infty}
is a basis of
\ell_{M}
.

4
Main results

We are going to state and proof our main results concerning a very specific perturbation method for Orlicz sequence spaces with
\Delta_{2}
. To ease the annotation, we fix throughout this section a non-degenerate Orlicz function
M
satisfying
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero, and we denote
\sigma(\cdot):=\sigma_{M}(\cdot)
. We also set
X:=\ell_{M}
. For the same reason of simplifying the annotation, let

\nu(t):=\sup\{\sigma(x):\ \|x\|\leq t\}<\infty,

(4.1)

see (
3.8
), and

\varphi(t):=\mathrm{diam}\,\Omega_{\sigma}(t),\quad\text{ so }\varphi(t)\to 0,\text{ as }t\to 0,

(4.2)

see (
3.10
).

For
a\in\ell_{\infty}
we define the real function
g_{a}
on
X
by

g_{a}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}M(|x_{n}|).

In this way,
\sigma=g_{(1,1,1,\ldots)}
. Recall that
\|a\|_{\infty}=\sup_{n}|a_{n}|
for
a\in\ell_{\infty}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The function
g_{a}
is bounded and Lipschitz on
KB_{X}
for each
K>0
. More precisely,

\sup_{KB_{X}}|g_{a}|\leq\|a\|_{\infty}\nu(K),

(4.3)

and

|g_{a}(x)-g_{a}(y)|\leq 2\|a\|_{\infty}\nu(K+1)\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in KB_{X}.

(4.4)

Proof.

Assume first that
a\in\ell_{\infty}^{+}
, that is
a_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
g_{a}\geq 0
and

g_{a}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}M(|x_{n}|)\leq\|a\|_{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(|x_{n}|)=\|a\|_{\infty}\sigma(x),

so (
4.3
) is fulfilled. Since
g_{a}
is convex, it has directional derivatives
g_{a}^{\prime}(x;h)
. If
\|x\|\leq 1
and
\|h\|=1
then by convexity
g_{a}(x+h)\geq g_{a}(x)+g_{a}^{\prime}(x;h)
. But
x+h\in(K+1)B_{X}
, so
g_{a}(x+h)\leq\|a\|_{\infty}\nu(K+1)
, and
g_{a}(x)\geq 0
; thus
g_{a}^{\prime}(x;h)\leq\|a\|_{\infty}\nu(K+1)
for all
x\in KB_{X}
and all
h
of norm
1
, meaning that
g_{a}
is Lipschitz on
KB_{X}
with Lipschitz constant
\|a\|_{\infty}\nu(K+1)
.

Now, for arbitrary
a\in\ell_{\infty}^{+}
we use the canonical representation
a=a_{+}-a_{-}
, where
(a_{+})_{n}=\max\{a_{n},0\}
, so
(a_{-})_{n}=-\min\{a_{n},0\}
. Accordingly,
g_{a}=g_{a_{+}}-g_{a_{-}}
and, since
a_{\pm}\in\ell_{\infty}^{+}
and
\|a\|_{\infty}=\max\{\|a_{+}\|,\|a_{-}\|\}
, the claim follows.
∎

Set

\mathcal{P}:=\left\{g_{a}\ :\ a\in\ell_{\infty}^{+}\right\},

the latter being the cone of positive bounded sequences. We consider
\mathrm{span}\,\mathcal{P}
, that is, the set of all functions
g_{a}
. Let
\|g\|_{\cal P}=\|a\|_{\infty}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The so defined
(\cal{P},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{P}})
is a perturbation space on each non-empty, closed and bounded subset of
X
.

Proof.

It is clear that it is enough to check the axioms of Definition
2.2
for arbitrary ball. Thus, let
S\subset KB_{X}
for some
K>0
. That the functions from
\mathrm{span}\,\mathcal{P}
are continuous on
S
is checked in Lemma
4.1
. The axiom
\mathrm{(\char 16\relax)}
is clear and
\mathrm{(\char 16\relax\char 16\relax)}
follows from (
4.3
).

To check
\mathrm{(\char 16\relax\char 16\relax\char 16\relax)}
, let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Due to (
4.2
) there is
\delta>0
such that

\varphi\left(\frac{3\delta}{\varepsilon}\right)<\varepsilon.

(4.5)

Let
\theta\in(0,\varepsilon)
be such that

\theta\nu(K)<\delta/2.

(4.6)

Fix an arbitrary
x\in S
. Hence,
\|x\|\leq K
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

\varepsilon\sigma(P_{N}^{\bot}x)<\delta/2.

(4.7)

Define

\bar{a}:=\theta\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}+\varepsilon\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}e_{i},

and

g(y):=g_{\bar{a}}(y)=\theta\sigma(P_{N}y)+\varepsilon\sigma(P_{N}^{\bot}y).

We will check that this
g
satisfies (
2.5
) for the fixed
x
.

Obviously,
\|g\|_{\mathcal{P}}=\|\bar{a}\|_{\infty}=\varepsilon
.

Also,
\sigma(P_{N}x)\leq\sigma(x)\leq\nu(K)
and
\theta\sigma(P_{N}x)<\delta/2
by (
4.6
), so using (
4.7
), we get
g(x)<\delta
. Since
g(0)=0
and, therefore,
\min_{S}g=0
, we get
x\in\Omega_{g}^{S}(\delta)
.

Let
y\in\Omega_{g}^{S}(3\delta)
, that is,
\|y\|\leq K
and
g(y)\leq 3\delta
. In particular,
\varepsilon\sigma(P_{N}^{\bot}y)\leq 3\delta\iff P_{N}^{\bot}y\in\Omega_{\sigma}(3\delta/\varepsilon)
. From (
4.5
), (
4.2
) and
0\in\Omega_{\sigma}(3\delta/\varepsilon)
it follows that
\|P_{N}^{\bot}y\|<\varepsilon
. Since
P_{N}y\in P_{N}S
and, of course,
y=P_{N}y+P_{N}^{\bot}y
, it follows that the distance of
y
to the compact set
P_{N}S
is less than
\varepsilon
.

Since
y\in\Omega_{g}^{S}(3\delta)
was arbitrary, we get
\alpha(\Omega_{g}^{S}(3\delta))\leq\varepsilon
and (
2.5
) is verified.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
M
be a non-degenerate Orlicz function satisfying
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero and let
f:\ell_{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function. Then for each
\varepsilon>0
there is
a\in\ell_{\infty}
such that
\|a\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
and
the function
f+g_{a}

attains its minimum on
\ell_{M}
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Assume first that

\lim_{\|x\|\to\infty}f(x)=\infty.

(4.8)

Fix
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
and
K>\|x_{0}\|
be so big that

\|x\|\geq K\Rightarrow f(x)>f(x_{0})+\sigma(x_{0}).

Set
S:=KB_{X}
. From Lemma
4.2
and Theorem
2.3
there is
a\in\ell_{\infty}^{+}
such that
\|a\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
and
f+g_{a}
attains its minimum on
S
. Let
\bar{x}\in S
be such that

f(x)+g_{a}(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+g_{a}(\bar{x}),\quad\forall x\in S.

But for
x\not\in S
, that is,
\|x\|>K
, we have
f(x)+g_{a}(x)\geq f(x)>f(x_{0})+\sigma(x_{0})
. Since
a_{n}\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\sigma\geq g_{a}
, so
f(x)+g_{a}(x)\geq f(x_{0})+g_{a}(x_{0})\geq f(\bar{x})+g_{a}(\bar{x})
, because
x_{0}\in S
. That is,
\bar{x}
is a global minimum of
f+g_{a}
.

Now, if
f
is only bounded below, fix
\theta\in(0,\varepsilon/2)
and consider

f_{1}(x):=f(x)+\theta\sigma(x).

From (
3.3
) it follows that
\sigma(x)\to\infty
as
\|x\|\to\infty
, so
f_{1}
satisfies (
4.8
) and from the first part of this proof there is
a^{\prime}\in\ell_{\infty}^{+}
such that
\|a^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\theta
and
f_{1}+g_{a^{\prime}}
attains its minimum. Then
a:=(\theta+a_{n}^{\prime})_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the claim.
∎

We are now ready to present our new tool for no-renorming results.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
M
be a non-degenerate Orlicz function satisfying
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero and let
f:\ell_{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper, lower semicontinuous and bounded below function with bounded domain.

Then for any
0<\delta<\varepsilon
there is
a\in\ell_{\infty}
such that

\delta\leq a_{n}\leq\varepsilon,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

(4.9)

and
the function
f-g_{a}

attains its minimum on
\ell_{M}
.

Proof.

Consider

f_{1}(x):=f(x)-\varepsilon\sigma(x).

Since
\sigma(x)
is bounded on the bounded set
\mathrm{dom}\,f
, see (
3.8
), the function
f_{1}
is bounded below. From Theorem
4.3
there is
a^{\prime}\in\ell_{\infty}^{+}
such that
\|a^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon-\delta
and
f_{1}+g_{a^{\prime}}
attains its minimum. Set

a:=(\varepsilon-a_{n}^{\prime})_{n=1}^{\infty}.

Obviously,
a
satisfies (
4.9
) and

f-g_{a}=f_{1}+g_{a^{\prime}}.\qed

Note that a variant of Stegall Variational Principle for an Orlicz space such that
M
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero (it is well known that such space is dentable, or, which is equivalent, has Radon-Nikodym property, see
[
15
]
) follows almost immediately from Theorem
4.4
. Indeed, if
\bar{x}
is the point of minimum of
f-g_{a}
then, assuming that
f(\bar{x})=g_{a}(\bar{x})
, we have
f\geq g_{a}
and so a subdifferential of
g_{a}
at
\bar{x}
, say
p
, will be a supporting functional to
f
, that is,
f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+p(x-\bar{x})
. From (
4.4
) it follows that
\|p\|<2\varepsilon\nu(\sup\|\mathrm{dom}\,f\|+2)
, which can be made arbitrary small.

Of course, we will not get that
\bar{x}
is a strong minimum of
f-p
, but even this weaker version of Stegall Variational Principle is still equivalent to the dentability of the space, see
[
5
]
. If one wants to get directly a strong minimum, one can regularise in advance
M
to be strictly convex, see e.g.
[
13
]
.

The important point here is that the
\Delta_{2}
assumption can not be just dropped in Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
.

5
Applications

In this section we will demonstrate on several examples how Theorem
4.4
can be applied. We start with the simplest of our cases, see e.g.
[
2
, Theorem II.5.3]
. Note that most part of the proof is just checking that the canonical norm of
\ell_{1}
is nowhere Fréchet differentiable.

Proposition 5.1
.

On
\ell_{1}
there is no non-trivial Fréchet differentiable bump.

Proof.

Suppose
b
is such a bump. Consider

f(x):=\begin{cases}b^{-2}(x),\quad b(x)\neq 0,\\
\infty,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(5.1)

It is clear that
f
is proper, lower semicontinuous function which is positive and Fréchet differentiable at each point of
\mathrm{dom}\,f
and the latter is bounded set.

Applying Theorem
4.4
with
\delta=1
and
\varepsilon=2
we get

a\in\ell_{\infty}^{+}
such that
1\leq a_{n}\leq 2
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and

\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
such that
f-g_{a}
attains its minimum at
\bar{x}
. In particular

f(x)-f(\bar{x})\geq g_{a}(x)-g_{a}(\bar{x}),

(5.2)

for all
x\in\ell_{1}
.
In particular, for any
h\in\ell_{1}
,
h\neq 0
,

\frac{f(\bar{x}+h)+f(\bar{x}-h)-2f(\bar{x})}{\|h\|}\geq\frac{g_{a}(\bar{x}+h)+g_{a}(\bar{x}-h)-2g_{a}(\bar{x})}{\|h\|}.

Letting
h
to
0
, the left hand side of the above tends to zero, because
f
is Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
, whilst the right hand side is always positive, because
g_{a}
is convex. Therefore,

\lim_{\|h\|\rightarrow 0}\frac{g_{a}(\bar{x}+h)+g_{a}(\bar{x}-h)-2g_{a}(\bar{x})}{\|h\|}=0.

(5.3)

We get a contradiction by checking that

\limsup_{\|h\|\rightarrow 0}\frac{g_{a}(\bar{x}+h)+g_{a}(\bar{x}-h)-2g_{a}(\bar{x})}{\|h\|}\geq 2.

Indeed, fix
t>0
. For all
n
large enough
|\bar{x}_{n}|<t
, so
|\bar{x}_{n}+t|+|\bar{x}_{n}-t|-2|\bar{x}_{n}|=2(t-|\bar{x}_{n}|)
, and
g_{a}(\bar{x}+te_{n})+g_{a}(\bar{x}-te_{n})-2g_{a}(\bar{x})=a_{n}(|\bar{x}_{n}+t|+|\bar{x}_{n}-t|-2|\bar{x}_{n}|)\geq 2(t-|\bar{x}_{n}|)
. That is,
\sup_{n}(g_{a}(\bar{x}+te_{n})+g_{a}(\bar{x}-te_{n})-2g_{a}(\bar{x}))\geq 2t
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
M
be non-degenerate Orlicz function satisfying
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero, and such that for some
p\in(1,2]

\limsup_{t\searrow 0}\frac{M(t)}{t^{p}}=\infty.

(5.4)

Then on
\ell_{M}
there is no nontrivial Fréchet differentiable bump with estimate

b(x+h)=b(x)+\langle b^{\prime}(x),h\rangle+O(\|h\|^{p})

(5.5)

for all
x\in\ell_{M}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
5.1
we define
f
by (
5.1
). It is immediate to check that (
5.5
) implies similar estimate for
f
. Consequently,

\limsup_{\|h\|\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)+f(x-h)-2f(x)}{\|h\|^{p}}<\infty,\quad\forall x\in\ell_{M}.

(5.6)

Further we get
g_{a}
with
a_{n}\geq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that (
5.2
) is fulfilled. Then from (
5.6
),

\limsup_{\|h\|\rightarrow 0}\frac{g_{a}(\bar{x}+h)+g_{a}(\bar{x}-h)-2g_{a}(\bar{x})}{\|h\|^{p}}<\infty

(5.7)

at some point
\bar{x}\in\ell_{M}
.

On the other hand, let
t_{k}\to 0
be such that
M(t_{k})>kt_{k}^{p}
, see (
5.4
). Then

\sup_{\|h\|=t_{k}}\frac{g_{a}(\bar{x}+h)+g_{a}(\bar{x}-h)-2g_{a}(\bar{x})}{\|h\|^{p}}\geq\\
\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_{n}\frac{M(|\bar{x}_{n}+t_{k}|)+M(|\bar{x}_{n}-t_{k}|)-2M(|\bar{x}_{n}|)}{t_{k}^{p}}\\
\geq 2\frac{M(t_{k})}{t_{k}^{p}}>2k,

which yields a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

If
M
is a non-degenerate twice differentiable Orlicz function satisfying
\Delta_{2}
condition at
0
, and such that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\searrow 0}M^{\prime\prime}(t)=\infty
, then on
\ell_{M}
there is no twice Gâteaux differentiable bump.

Proof.

Again assume the contrary and let
f
,
g_{a}
and
\bar{x}
be like in (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). Then
f
is twice Gatêaux differentiable on
\bar{x}
. Let
K:=\|f^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x})\|
. In particular, the second directional derivatives of
f
at
\bar{x}
in the co-ordinate directions

f^{\prime\prime}_{e_{n}^{2}}(\bar{x})\leq K,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.8)

Since
f^{\prime\prime}_{e_{n}^{2}}(\bar{x})=\lim_{t\to 0}(f(\bar{x}+t)+f(\bar{x}-t)-2f(\bar{x}))/t^{2}
, from (
5.2
) and (
5.8
) it follows that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{g_{a}(\bar{x}+te_{n})+g_{a}(\bar{x}-te_{n})-2g_{a}(\bar{x})}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{M(|\bar{x}_{n}+t|)+M(\bar{x}_{n}-t|)-2M(|\bar{x}_{n}|)}{t^{2}}.

If
\bar{x}_{n}=0
the latter equals
2M(t)/t^{2}
. However,
\limsup_{t\to 0}M(t)/t^{2}=\infty
. Indeed, if
c:=\limsup_{t\to 0}M^{\prime}(t)>0
then by convexity
M(t)\geq ct
. Otherwise by L’Hôpitale’s rule,
\lim_{t\to 0}2M(t)/t^{2}=\lim_{t\to 0}M^{\prime\prime}(t)=\infty
, by assumption. This means that
\bar{x}_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. But then the last limit superior above is simply
M^{\prime\prime}(|\bar{x}_{n}|)
. So,

K\geq M^{\prime\prime}(|\bar{x}_{n}|),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

which yields a contradiction to
\bar{x}_{n}\to 0
.
∎

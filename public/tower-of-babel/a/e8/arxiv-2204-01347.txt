Zhonghua Li and Zhenlu Wang

Abstract
In 2015, S. Hong and C. Wang proved that none of the elementary symmetric functions of 1 , 1 / 3 , … , 1 / ( 2 ​ n − 1 ) 1,1/3,\ldots,1/(2n-1) is an integer when n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . In 2017, Kh. Pilehrood, T. Pilehrood and R. Tauraso proved that the multiple harmonic sums H n ​ ( s 1 , … , s r ) H_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}) are never integers with exceptions of H 1 ​ ( s 1 ) = 1 H_{1}(s_{1})=1 and H 3 ​ ( 1 , 1 ) = 1 H_{3}(1,1)=1 . They also proved that the multiple harmonic star sums are never integers when n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . In this paper, we consider the odd multiple harmonic sums and the odd multiple harmonic star sums and show that none of these sums is an integer with exception of the trivial case. Besides, we give evaluations of the odd (alternating) multiple harmonic sums with depth one.



Keywords
odd multiple harmonic sums, harmonic series.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers. For any
r,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\leq n
and
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, the multiple harmonic sum and the multiple harmonic star sum are defined respectively by

\displaystyle H_{n}(\mathbf{s})=H_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}):=\sum\limits_{1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots<k_{r}\leq n}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{k_{j}^{s_{j}}},

(1.1)

and

\displaystyle H_{n}^{\star}(\mathbf{s})=H_{n}^{\star}(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}):=\sum\limits_{1\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\cdots\leq k_{r}\leq n}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{k_{j}^{s_{j}}}.

(1.2)

The odd multiple harmonic sum and the odd multiple harmonic star sum are defined respectively by

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(\mathbf{s})=\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}):=\sum\limits_{0\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots<k_{r}\leq n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{(2k_{j}+1)^{s_{j}}},

(1.3)

and

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}^{\star}(\mathbf{s})=\overline{H}_{n}^{\star}(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}):=\sum\limits_{0\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\cdots\leq k_{r}\leq n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{(2k_{j}+1)^{s_{j}}}.

(1.4)

We call
r
the depth and
\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{s}):=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}s_{j}
the weight. Besides, we denote the sequence of
a
with
r
repetitions as
\left(\{a\}^{r}\right)
.

The multiple harmonic sums (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) are of certain interest because by taking the limit as
n
goes to
\infty
when
s_{r}>1
, we get

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\zeta(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\quad\text{and}\quad\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=t(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}),

which are the so-called multiple zeta value
[
4
,
9
]
and Hoffman’s multiple
t
-value
[
5
]
, respectively. Also by taking the limit, the star versions (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) deduce the multiple zeta-star value
[
4
]
and Hoffman’s multiple
t
-star value
[
5
]
respectively.

A well-known result in elementary number theory says that for any positive integers
n>1
, the harmonic sum
H_{n}(1)
is not an integer. In 1946, P. Erdős and I. Niven
[
3
]
proved that the nested multiple harmonic sums
H_{n}(\{1\}^{r})
can take integer values only for a finite number of positive integers
n
, and they also mentioned that there are only finitely many integers
n
for which one or more of the elementary symmetric functions of
1/a,1/(a+b),\ldots,1/(a+bn)
are integers, where
a
and
b
are given positive integers. Y. Chen and M. Tang
[
1
]
proved that
H_{n}(\{1\}^{r})
is an integer only for
(n,r)=(1,1)
and
(n,r)=(3,2)
. Kh. Pilehrood, T. Pilehrood and R. Tauraso
[
7
]
got a more general result which says that for
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
,
H_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is never an integer with exceptions of
H_{1}(s_{1})=1
and
H_{3}(1,1)=1
, and they also proved that
H_{n}^{\star}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is never an integer with exception of
H_{1}^{\star}(s_{1})=1
. For the odd multiple harmonic sums, S. Hong and C. Wang
[
6
]
proved that
\overline{H}_{n}(\{1\}^{r})
is not an integer for any integer
n\geq 2
. For other works such as the binomial identities and the congruences involving the (odd) multiple harmonic sums, one can refer to
[
8
]
or the book
[
10
]
of J. Zhao and the references therein.

In this paper, using similar method as in
[
7
]
, we prove the following results indicates the integrality of the odd multiple harmonic star sums and the odd multiple harmonic sums.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
r,n,s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
r\leq n
. Then
\overline{H}_{n}^{\star}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is never an integer when
n\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
r,n,s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
r\leq n
. Then
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is never an integer when
n\geq 2
.

More generally, one can define the odd alternating multiple harmonic sums. Let
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{N}\cup\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, where
\overline{\mathbb{N}}=\{\overline{s}\mid s\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Define the absolute value function
|\cdot|
on
\mathbb{D}
by
|s|=|\overline{s}|=s
for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
and the sign function by
\operatorname{sgn}(s)=1
and
\operatorname{sgn}(\overline{s})=-1
for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\leq n
and
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{D}^{r}
, the odd alternating multiple harmonic sum
\overline{H}_{n}(\mathbf{s})
is defined by

\overline{H}_{n}(\mathbf{s})=\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}):=\sum\limits_{0\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots<k_{r}\leq n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(s_{j})^{k_{j}}}{(2k_{j}+1)^{|s_{j}|}}.

A formula known to Euler says that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds

H_{n}(1)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\binom{n}{k}.

In this paper, we generalize the above binomial identity and give the evaluations of
\overline{H}_{n}(s)
and
\overline{H}_{n}(\overline{s})
for any
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
. In the evaluation formulas, the special values of the generalized hypergeometric functions appear.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we first give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Then we show several lemmas which are needed for the proof of Theorem
1.2
and finally complete the proof. In Section
3
, we give the evaluations of
\overline{H}_{n}(s)
and
\overline{H}_{n}(\overline{s})
for any
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
.

In this paper, all the numerical computations were performed by using Matlab.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2

For a prime
p
, let
v_{p}(a)
be the
p
-adic valuation of the rational number
a
, that is, if
a=p^{n}q_{1}/q_{2}
with
n,q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
q_{1},q_{2}
are coprime with
p
, then
v_{p}(a)=n
. It is a fact that the
p
-adic valuation satisfies the inequality

v_{p}(a+b)\geq\min(v_{p}(a),v_{p}(b)),

where the equality holds for
v_{p}(a)\neq v_{p}(b)
.

The integrality of the odd multiple harmonic star sums is simple to prove.

Let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})
. Since
n\geq 2
, by Bertrand’s postulate, there exists a prime
p
such that
n<p<2n
. Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}^{\star}(\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{0\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\cdots\leq k_{r}\leq n-1\atop\exists j,p\neq 2k_{j}+1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{(2k_{j}+1)^{s_{j}}}+\sum\limits_{0\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\cdots\leq k_{r}\leq n-1\atop\forall j,p=2k_{j}+1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{(2k_{j}+1)^{s_{j}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{bp^{t}}+\frac{1}{p^{\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{s})}},

where
a,b
are positive integers with
(b,p)=1
,
t
is a nonnegative integer with
t<\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{s})
. Since
v_{p}\left(\frac{a}{bp^{t}}\right)>v_{p}\left(\frac{1}{p^{\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{s})}}\right)
, we have
v_{p}\left(\overline{H}_{n}^{\star}(\mathbf{s})\right)=-\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{s})<0
, which implies
\overline{H}_{n}^{\star}(\mathbf{s})
is not an integer.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is more complicated. We need several lemmas. In order to compare values of odd multiple harmonic sums of the same depth, the following definition and lemma will be useful.

Definition 2.1

(
[
7
, Definition 1]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
and
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
be two
r
-tuples of positive integers. If
\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{s})\geq\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{t})
, and
s_{1}\leq t_{1},\ldots,s_{l}\leq t_{l},s_{l+1}\geq t_{l+1},\ldots,s_{r}\geq t_{r}
for some
0\leq l\leq r-1
, we say that
\mathbf{s}\geq\mathbf{t}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
r,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
r\leq n
, if
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
and
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
are two
r
-tuples of positive integers such that
\mathbf{s}\geq\mathbf{t}
, then it holds

\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\leq\overline{H}_{n}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}).

Proof.
Let
0\leq l\leq r-1
such that
s_{1}\leq t_{1},\ldots,s_{l}\leq t_{l},s_{l+1}\geq t_{l+1},\ldots,s_{r}\geq t_{r}
. For fixed
0\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{r}\leq n-1
, we have

\displaystyle(2k_{1}+1)^{t_{1}-s_{1}}\cdots(2k_{l}+1)^{t_{l}-s_{l}}

\displaystyle\leq(2k_{l}+1)^{t_{1}+\cdots+t_{l}-(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{l})}

\displaystyle=(2k_{l}+1)^{t_{1}+\cdots+t_{r}-(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r})}\cdot(2k_{l}+1)^{s_{l+1}-t_{l+1}+\cdots+s_{r}-t_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq(2k_{l}+1)^{s_{l+1}-t_{l+1}+\cdots+s_{r}-t_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq(2k_{l+1}+1)^{s_{l+1}-t_{l+1}}\cdots(2k_{r}+1)^{s_{r}-t_{r}}.

Therefore, we get

\frac{1}{(2k_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}\cdots(2k_{r}+1)^{s_{r}}}\leq\frac{1}{(2k_{1}+1)^{t_{1}}\cdots(2k_{r}+1)^{t_{r}}},

which implies the desired result.∎

We then show that the odd multiple harmonic sum is not an integer if the depth is large enough.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
r,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

e\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(2n-1)+1\right)\leq r\leq n.

Then for any
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is not an integer.

Proof.
Notice that

\overline{H}_{n}(1)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2i+1}<1+\int_{0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2x+1}dx=\frac{1}{2}\log(2n-1)+1.

By using Lemma
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\leq\overline{H}_{n}(\{1\}^{r})\leq\frac{\left(\overline{H}_{n}(1)\right)^{r}}{r!}\leq\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(2n-1)+1\right)^{r}}{r!}.

(2.1)

Since
e\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(2n-1)+1\right)\leq r
and
\frac{r^{r}}{r!}<e^{r}
, we have
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})<1
, which implies the result.
∎

The following lemma is useful in the proof of Theorem
1.2

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
r,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
r\leq n
. Suppose that there exists a prime
p>r+1
satisfying that

\frac{2n}{r+1}\leq p<\frac{2n}{r},

then for any
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}

\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is not an integer.

Proof.
Since
0<p<\cdots<rp<2n\leq(r+1)p<p^{2}
and

are positive integers, we have

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{0\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{r}\leq n-1\atop\exists j,p\nmid 2k_{j}+1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{(2k_{j}+1)^{s_{j}}}+\sum\limits_{0\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{r}\leq n-1\atop\forall j,p\mid 2k_{j}+1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{(2k_{j}+1)^{s_{j}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{bp^{t}}+\frac{1}{cp^{\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{s})}},

where

,

are positive integers with
(b,p)=(c,p)=1
,
t
is a nonnegative integer with

.
Therefore,

, which implies
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Given
2\leq r\leq n
and
(s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r-1}
, there exists an integer
N
(which depends on
(s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})
and
n
) such that
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is not an integer for any positive integer
s_{1}>N
.

Proof.
Let
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. If
r=n
, it is obvious that
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is not an integer for all
s_{1}\geq 1
. If
r<n
, we set

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\sum\limits_{k_{1}=0}^{n-r}\frac{c_{k_{1}}}{(2k_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}}\quad\text{where}\quad c_{k_{1}}=\sum\limits_{k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots<k_{r}\leq n-1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{r}\frac{1}{(2k_{j}+1)^{s_{j}}}.

By Bertrand’s postulate, there exists at least one prime in
(n-r+1,2n-2r+2)
. Let
p
be such largest prime, then
2p>2n-2r+2
. We get

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\sum\limits_{k_{1}=0\atop p=2k_{1}+1}^{n-r}\frac{c_{k_{1}}}{(2k_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}}+\sum\limits_{k_{1}=0\atop p\neq 2k_{1}+1}^{n-r}\frac{c_{k_{1}}}{(2k_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{\frac{p-1}{2}}}{p^{s_{1}}}+\sum\limits_{k_{1}=0\atop p\neq 2k_{1}+1}^{n-r}\frac{c_{k_{1}}}{(2k_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}}.

Define

N(n,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}):=\operatorname{max}\left(v_{p}(c_{\frac{p-1}{2}}),v_{p}(c_{\frac{p-1}{2}})-\min\limits_{0\leq k_{1}\leq n-r\atop p\neq 2k_{1}+1}v_{p}(c_{k_{1}})\right).

Assume that
s_{1}>N(n,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})
, then we have

\displaystyle s_{1}>v_{p}(c_{\frac{p-1}{2}})

(2.6)

and

\displaystyle s_{1}>v_{p}(c_{\frac{p-1}{2}})-\min\limits_{0\leq k_{1}\leq n-r\atop p\neq 2k_{1}+1}v_{p}(c_{k_{1}}).

(2.7)

By (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle v_{p}\left(\sum\limits_{k_{1}=0\atop p\neq 2k_{1}+1}^{n-r}\frac{c_{k_{1}}}{(2k_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}}\right)\geq\min\limits_{0\leq k_{1}\leq n-r\atop p\neq 2k_{1}+1}v_{p}\left(\frac{c_{k_{1}}}{(2k_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}}\right)=\min\limits_{0\leq k_{1}\leq n-r\atop p\neq 2k_{1}+1}v_{p}(c_{k_{1}})>v_{p}\left(\frac{c_{\frac{p-1}{2}}}{p^{s_{1}}}\right).

Hence, by (
2.6
), we get

v_{p}\left(\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\right)=\min\left(v_{p}\left(\frac{c_{\frac{p-1}{2}}}{p^{s_{1}}}\right),v_{p}\left(\sum\limits_{k_{1}=0\atop p\neq 2k_{1}+1}^{n-r}\frac{c_{k_{1}}}{(2k_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}}\right)\right)=v_{p}\left(\frac{c_{\frac{p-1}{2}}}{p^{s_{1}}}\right)<0,

which implies
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is not an integer.
∎

e\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(2n-1)+1\right)<18
and Remark
2.7
, it is enough to consider the integrality of
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
for
2\leq r\leq 17
and
n<n_{r}
.

Let
11\leq r\leq 17
. For
n<n_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})<\overline{H}_{n_{r}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\leq\overline{H}_{n_{r}}(\{1\}^{r})\leq\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(2n_{r}-1)+1\right)^{r}}{r!}<1,

where the last inequality can be verified numerically. Therefore,
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is not an integer.

Let
5\leq r\leq 10
. We have the following estimations

\overline{H}_{n_{10}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{10})\leq\overline{H}_{n_{10}}(\{1\}^{10})<0.06243<1,

\overline{H}_{n_{9}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{9})\leq\overline{H}_{n_{9}}(\{1\}^{9})<0.17796<1,

\overline{H}_{n_{8}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{8})\leq\overline{H}_{n_{8}}(\{1\}^{8})<0.12835<1,

\overline{H}_{n_{7}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{7})\leq\overline{H}_{n_{7}}(\{1\}^{7})<0.20280<1,

\overline{H}_{n_{6}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{6})\leq\overline{H}_{n_{6}}(\{1\}^{6})<0.51825<1,

\overline{H}_{n_{5}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{5})\leq\overline{H}_{n_{5}}(\{1\}^{5})<0.85442<1.

Hence, for
n<n_{r}
,
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is not an integer.

Let
r=2,2\leq n<n_{2}=12
. We verify numerically that
\overline{H}_{n}(1,1)
is not an integer. Since
\overline{H}_{12}(1,2)<0.27273<1
, we get

\overline{H}_{n}(1,s_{2})\leq\overline{H}_{n}(1,2)<\overline{H}_{12}(1,2)<1

for all
s_{2}>1
, which implies
\overline{H}_{n}(1,s_{2})
is not an integer if
s_{2}>1
. We can verify that the upper bound
N(n,1)=1
for all
3\leq n\leq 11
in the proof of Lemma
2.8
. Hence
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},1)
is not an integer for
s_{1}>1
and
3\leq n\leq 11
by Lemma
2.8
. Therefore we proved that
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2})
is never an integer for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
r=3,3\leq n<n_{3}=17
. We verify numerically that
\overline{H}_{n}(1,1,1)
is not an integer. Since
\overline{H}_{17}(1,2,1)<0.22216<1
, we get

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\leq\overline{H}_{n}(1,2,1)<\overline{H}_{17}(1,2,1)<1

for all
s_{1},s_{3}\geq 1
and
s_{2}>1
. Since
\overline{H}_{17}(1,1,2)<0.08552<1
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\leq\overline{H}_{n}(1,1,2)<\overline{H}_{17}(1,1,2)<1,

for all
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 1
and
s_{3}>1
. As we can verify
\operatorname{max}\limits_{4\leq n\leq 16}N(n,1,1)=1
, we find from Lemma
2.8
that
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},1,1)
is not an integer for
s_{1}>1
. Thus, we proved that
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
is never an integer for all
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
r=4,4\leq n<n_{4}=59
. We verify numerically that
\overline{H}_{n}(1,1,1,1)
is not an integer. Since
\overline{H}_{59}(1,2,1,1)<0.28433<1
, we get

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})\leq\overline{H}_{n}(1,2,1,1)<\overline{H}_{59}(1,2,1,1)<1

for all
s_{1},s_{3},s_{4}\geq 1
and
s_{2}>1
. Since
\overline{H}_{59}(1,1,2,1)<0.10452<1
, we get

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})\leq\overline{H}_{n}(1,1,2,1)<\overline{H}_{59}(1,1,2,1)<1

for all
s_{1},s_{2},s_{4}\geq 1
and
s_{3}>1
. Since
\overline{H}_{59}(1,1,1,2)<0.04060<1
, we get

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})\leq\overline{H}_{n}(1,1,1,2)<\overline{H}_{59}(1,1,1,2)<1

for all
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\geq 1
and
s_{4}>1
. As we have
\operatorname{max}\limits_{5\leq n\leq 58}N(n,1,1,1)=1
, we get that
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},1,1,1)
is not an integer for
s_{1}>1
from Lemma
2.8
. Therefore, we have
\overline{H}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})
is never an integer for all
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4}\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

3
Some evaluations

In this section, we give evaluations of
\overline{H}_{n}(s)
and
\overline{H}_{n}(\overline{s})
for any
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
. The generalized hypergeometric functions are needed in the following discussion. For
a_{1},\ldots,a_{s+1}
,
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\in\mathbb{C}
with none of
b_{i}
is zero or a negative integer, the generalized hypergeometric function is defined by

{}_{s+1}F_{s}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s+1};b_{1},\ldots,b_{s};x):=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{i}\cdots(a_{s+1})_{i}}{(b_{1})_{i}\cdots(b_{s})_{i}}\frac{x^{i}}{i!},

where

(a)_{i}:=\begin{cases}1,&i=0,\\
a(a+1)\cdots(a+i-1),&i>0.\end{cases}

The above series is absolutely and uniformly convergent if
|x|<1
and the convergence also extends over the unit circle if
\Re\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s+1}a_{i}\right)>0
.

We have the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k+1)^{s}}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}{{}_{s+1}F_{s}}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}^{s},1-k;\left\{\frac{3}{2}\right\}^{s};x^{2}\right).

(3.1)

Proof.
We prove the lemma by induction on
s
. Let

f_{s}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k+1)^{s}}.

If
s=1
, then

f_{1}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^{2k}}{2k+1},

and we get that

(xf_{1}(x))^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}x^{2k}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}(1-x^{2})^{k-1}.

Integrating both sides from
0
to
x
, we find the left-hand side becomes
xf_{1}(x)
and the right-hand side turns to be

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\int_{0}^{x}(1-t^{2})^{k-1}dt

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\int_{0}^{x}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{l}\binom{k-1}{l}t^{2l}dt

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-1)^{l}(k-1)\cdots(k-l)x^{2l+1}}{(2l+1)l!}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k-1}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{l}\cdot(1-k)_{l}\cdot x^{2l+1}}{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)_{l}\cdot l!}

\displaystyle=x\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}{{}_{2}}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2},1-k;\frac{3}{2};x^{2}\right).

Thus, we prove the case of
s=1
. Now Assume that (
3.1
) holds for
s
. Integrating both sides of (
3.1
) from
0
to
x
, we find the left-hand side becomes
xf_{s+1}(x)
and the right-hand side turns to be

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{l}\cdots\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{l}\cdot(1-k)_{l}\cdot t^{2l}}{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)_{l}\cdots\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)_{l}\cdot l!}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{l}\cdots\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{l}\cdot(1-k)_{l}\cdot x^{2l+1}}{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)_{l}\cdots\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)_{l}\cdot(2l+1)\cdot l!},

which is

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\cdot x\cdot{{}_{s+2}F_{s+1}}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}^{s+1},1-k;\left\{\frac{3}{2}\right\}^{s+1};x^{2}\right).

Hence, we prove that (
3.1
) holds for
s+1
.
∎

Setting
x=1
in Lemma
3.1
, we get the evaluations of
\overline{H}_{n}(s)
. In the evaluation formulas, the values of the generalized hypergeometric functions at
1
appear.

Corollary 3.2
.

For
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}{{}_{s+1}F_{s}}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}^{s},1-k;\left\{\frac{3}{2}\right\}^{s};1\right).

Note that if
s=1
, using the Chu-Vandermonde summation formula

{}_{2}F_{1}(-n,b;c;1)=\frac{(c-b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}},

we find that

\overline{H}_{n}(1)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-2)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(k-1)!}{(2k-1)!!}.

Similarly as Lemma
3.1
, we can prove the following result. And we omit the proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}x^{2k}}{(2k+1)^{s}}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}{{}_{s+1}F_{s}}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}^{s},1-k;\left\{\frac{3}{2}\right\}^{s};-x^{2}\right).

Let
x=1
in Lemma
3.3
, we get the evaluations of
\overline{H}_{n}(\overline{s})
. In the evaluation formulas, the values of the generalized hypergeometric functions at
-1
appear.

Corollary 3.4
.

For
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(\overline{s})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}{{}_{s+1}F_{s}}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}^{s},1-k;\left\{\frac{3}{2}\right\}^{s};-1\right).

As a variant of the odd multiple harmonic sums, for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

G_{m,n}:=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(2k+1)(2k+2)\cdots(2k+m)}.

Then we can represent
G_{m,n}
by the special values of the hypergeometric function at
-1
.

Proposition 3.5
.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle G_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(m-1)!(m+k-1)}\binom{n}{k}{{}_{2}}F_{1}(1,1-k;m+k;-1).

Proof.
Let

h_{m}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^{2k+m}}{(2k+1)(2k+2)\cdots(2k+m)}.

Then we get

h^{(m)}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}x^{2k}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}(1-x^{2})^{k-1}.

Integrating of both sides, we get

\displaystyle G_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t_{m-1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{t_{1}}(1-x^{2})^{k-1}dxdt_{1}\cdots dt_{m-1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\,\int\limits_{x<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{m-1}<1}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m-1}\right)(1-x^{2})^{k-1}dx

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(m-1)!}\binom{n}{k}\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{m+k-2}(1+x)^{k-1}dx

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(m-1)!}\binom{n}{k}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{l}\int_{0}^{1}x^{l}(1-x)^{m+k-2}dx

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(m-1)!}\binom{n}{k}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{l}B(l+1,m+k-1),

where
B(a,b)
denotes the beta function. Since
B(a,b)=\frac{(a-1)!(b-1)!}{(a+b-1)!}
for positive integers
a,b
, we have

\displaystyle G_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(m-1)!}\binom{n}{k}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{l}\frac{l!(m+k-2)!}{(m+k+l-1)!}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(m-1)!(m+k-1)}\binom{n}{k}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k-1}\frac{(1)_{l}(1-k)_{l}(-1)^{l}}{l!(m+k)_{l}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(m-1)!(m+k-1)}\binom{n}{k}{{}_{2}}F_{1}(1,1-k;m+k;-1).

Hence, we complete the proof.
∎

As applications, using the binomial inversion

f(n)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}g(k)\Longleftrightarrow g(n)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}f(k),

we obtain the following summation formulas of the (generalized) hypergeometric functions.

Corollary 3.6
.

For
m,n,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle{{}_{s+1}}F_{s}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}^{s},1-n;\left\{\frac{3}{2}\right\}^{s};1\right)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\overline{H}_{k}(s),

\displaystyle{{}_{s+1}}F_{s}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}^{s},1-n;\left\{\frac{3}{2}\right\}^{s};-1\right)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\overline{H}_{k}(\overline{s}),

and

{{}_{2}}F_{1}(1,1-n;m+n;-1)=(m-1)!(m+n-1)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}G_{m,k}.

Remark 3.7
.

Similarly, we can obtain evaluations of the (alternating) multiple harmonic sums of depth one. For any
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{n}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}{{}_{s+1}}F_{s}\left(\left\{1\right\}^{s},1-k;\left\{2\right\}^{s};1\right),

\displaystyle H_{n}(\overline{s})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}{{}_{s+1}}F_{s}\left(\left\{1\right\}^{s},1-k;\left\{2\right\}^{s};-1\right),

where
H_{n}(\overline{s})
is the alternating harmonic sum defined by

H_{n}(\overline{s})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k^{s}}.

Using the binomial inversion formula, we may obtain the summation formulas

\displaystyle{{}_{s+1}}F_{s}\left(\left\{1\right\}^{s},1-n;\left\{2\right\}^{s};1\right)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}(s),

\displaystyle{{}_{s+1}}F_{s}\left(\left\{1\right\}^{s},1-n;\left\{2\right\}^{s};-1\right)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}(\overline{s}).

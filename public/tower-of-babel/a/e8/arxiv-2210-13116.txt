Variations of the mutual curvature of two orthogonal non-complementary distributions
Vladimir Rovenski, Tomasz Zawadzki

Abstract
On a smooth manifold with distributions 𝒟 1 {\cal D}_{1} and 𝒟 2 {\cal D}_{2} having trivial intersection, we consider the integral of their mutual curvature, as a functional of Riemannian metrics that make the distributions orthogonal. The mutual curvature is defined as the sum of sectional curvatures of planes spanned by all pairs of vectors from an orthonormal basis, such that one vector of the pair belongs to 𝒟 1 {\cal D}_{1} and the second vector belongs to 𝒟 2 {\cal D}_{2} . As such, it interpolates between the sectional curvature of a plane field (if both distributions are one-dimensional), and the mixed scalar curvature of a Riemannian almost product structure (if both distributions together span the tangent bundle). We derive Euler-Lagrange equations for the functional, formulated in terms of extrinsic geometry of distributions, i.e., their second fundamental forms and integrability tensors. We give examples of critical metrics for distributions defined on domains of Riemannian submersions, twisted products and f f - K K -contact manifolds. Keywords : Riemannian manifold, distribution, foliation, mutual curvature, variation, twisted product, f f - K K -contact manifold Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) 53C15; 53C12; 53C40



Introduction

Distributions on a smooth manifold (i.e., subbundles of the tangent bundle) appear in various topics of mathematics and theoretical physics,
e.g.,
[
1
,
3
]
, and are used to build up notions of integrability, and specifically of a foliation (i.e., a partition of a manifold by submanifolds of a constant dimension).
In
[
12
,
13
]
we were guided by the following approach to variational problems in the geometry of distributions
(developed in
[
11
]
for codimension one foliations):

given a distribution
{\cal D}
on a smooth manifold and a property
Q
of
the second fundamental form or the curvature associated with a distribution,
find critical Riemannian metrics for the integral
Q
in the class of general
or adapted
(
i.e., with fixed orthogonal complement to
{\cal D})
variations of metrics.

Many canonical metrics are critical points of some variational problems, e.g., Einstein metrics are critical points of the integral of the scalar curvature, and their classification is a deep problem
[
2
]
.

In the presence of an additional structure (e.g., a contact structure
[
3
]
,
an almost product structure
[
7
]
, or a foliation
[
1
]
),
one can consider an analogue of Einstein-Hilbert action consistent with this structure
and, instead of the scalar curvature, use another suitable curvature invariant.
This approach was implemented in our previous papers, e.g.,
[
12
,
13
]
,
where the integrated scalar curvature of a smooth manifold
M
with a Riemannian metric
g
was replaced by the integrated
mixed scalar curvature

{S}_{\,{\cal D},{\cal D}^{\bot}}

of an almost product structure
({\cal D},{\cal D}^{\bot})
.
The mixed scalar curvature
{S}_{\,{\cal D},{\cal D}^{\bot}}
is an averaged sum of sectional curvatures of the planes that non-trivially intersect
with the distribution
{\cal D}
and its orthogonal complement
{\cal D}^{\bot}
, see
[
14
]
.

A natural generalization of the above approach is to consider curvatures defined by a pair of orthogonal, but not necessarily complementary distributions
{\cal D}_{1}
and
{\cal D}_{2}
. Then, their sum
{\cal D}
is a distribution on
M
with orthogonal decomposition:

{\cal D}={\cal D}_{1}\oplus{\cal D}_{2},

(1)

which can be regarded as an almost product structure
associated with a foliated manifold (if
{\cal D}
is everywhere integrable) or with a non-holonomic manifold (if
{\cal D}
is nowhere integrable), which is the central object of sub-Riemannian geometry, e.g.,
[
1
]
.
In
[
9
,
10
]
, some integral formulae were proved for a scalar curvature-type invariant of such
(M,{\cal D})
.

In this article, we consider a manifold of dimension
\dim M>2
, with two distributions
{\cal D}_{1}
and
{\cal D}_{2}
having trivial intersection, and investigate critical points of their integrated mutual curvature, among Riemannian metrics that make
{\cal D}_{1}
and
{\cal D}_{2}
orthogonal. For local orthonormal bases
\{E_{a}\}

and
\{{\cal E}_{i}\}

of orthogonal distributions
{\cal D}_{1}
and
{\cal D}_{2}
, respectively,
the

is given by

{S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2}}=\sum\nolimits_{\,a}\sum\nolimits_{\,i}g(R(E_{a},{\cal E}_{i}){\cal E}_{i},E_{a}),

(2)

where

is the curvature tensor of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla

(M,g)

For
\dim{\cal D}_{1}=\dim{\cal D}_{2}=1
, the mutual curvature
{S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2}}
reduces to the sectional curvature of the plane field
{\cal D}
.
On the other hand, if
{\cal D}=TM
, then
{S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2}}
reduces to the mixed scalar curvature of the almost-product structure
({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2})
.
The relation of
{S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2}}
to sectional curvature means that many curvatures can be expressed by linear combinations of formulas (
2
) for suitably chosen distributions.
On the other hand, with an appropriate choice of some auxiliary distributions,
{S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2}}
can be expressed in terms of their mixed scalar curvatures, which will allow us to develop variational formulas using results from
[
13
]
.

We introduce and study the following action:

J_{{\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2}}:g\mapsto\int_{M}{S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2}}\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g},

(3)

where
metrics
g
satisfy

g(X,Y)=0
for all
X\in{\cal D}_{1},\ Y\in{\cal D}_{2}
.
If
M
is non-compact, we integrate in (
3
)
over an arbitrary relatively compact domain
\Omega\subset M
, which contains supports of variations of
g
.
We derive the Euler-Lagrange equations for (
3
),
for variations of metric keeping orthogonality of the two given
distributions
{\cal D}_{1}
and
{\cal D}_{2}
. These equations
are formulated using notions of the extrinsic geometry, i.e., the second fundamental forms and integrability tensors of

{\cal D}_{1}
,
{\cal D}_{2}
,
{\cal D}
and their orthogonal complements, and correspond to variations of metric along those distributions.

We present examples of critical points of (
3
)
for the following cases: one-dimensional distributions, twisted products, Riemannian submersions and
f
-
K
-contact manifolds.
The first case gives a new perspective on sectional curvature from the point of view of extrinsic geometry of distributions.
In the second case, we examine a simple but non-trivial property of distributions to be mixed integrable and mixed totally geodesic, arising on twisted products of manifolds; and use examples obtained in
[
13
]
to find critical points of action (
3

The remaining cases deal with
non-integrable and not mixed integrable distributions
on domains of Riemannian submersions,
and manifolds with
f
-
K
-structures, e.g.,
[
4
,
6
]
, and

K
-contact structures
[
3
]
.

The article consists of an Introduction and three sections.
Section
1

{\cal D}_{1}
and
{\cal D}_{2}

In Section

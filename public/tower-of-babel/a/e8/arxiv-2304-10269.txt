Lattices in ℝ n ⋊ SL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) \mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
M. M. Radhika, Sandip Singh

Abstract
. We determine the existence of cocompact lattices in groups of the form V ⋊ SL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) \mathrm{V}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}) , where V \mathrm{V} is a finite dimensional real representation of SL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}) . It turns out that the answer depends on the parity of dim ( V ) \dim(\mathrm{V}) when the representation is irreducible.

1.
Introduction

A lattice in a Lie group
G
is a discrete subgroup
\Gamma
which has a finite covolume with respect to the Haar measure on
G
. Lattices in semisimple Lie groups have been extensively studied. It is known that (linear) semisimple Lie groups contain both cocompact and non-cocompact lattices (cf.
[
1
]
;
[
2
]
;
[
16
]
). While non-arithmetic lattices are known to exist in certain orthogonal and unitary groups (see
[
10
, Appendix C]
), for instance, in
\mathrm{SO}(1,n)
(
[
9
,
17
]
for
n\leq 5
,
[
6
]
) and
\mathrm{SU}(1,n)
(
[
13
]
for
n=2
,
[
5
]
for
n=3
), Margulis arithmeticity theorem asserts that, in most cases, lattices in classical groups are arithmetic subgroups and are obtained by taking the integral points. Arithmeticity allows one to use algebraic and number theoretic techniques to study these lattices, whereas non arithmetic lattices are often studied using geometric techniques.

In solvable groups, lattices (if they exist) are cocompact
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
. Both cocompact and non cocompact arithmetic lattices are known to exist in non-compact semisimple groups.
It is a natural question to ask whether a semidirect product of a solvable group and a semisimple group contains a cocompact lattice or not; whether the semidirect product contains an arithmetic lattice or not; and if such lattices exist, what they are. In this article we will be investigating these questions for the simplest group of this type,
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an algebraic representation space for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, write
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma\cap{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and let
\Gamma_{2}
be the projection of
\Gamma
on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It follows from
[
15
, Corollary 8.28]
(see Appendix A) that
\Gamma_{1}
is a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Gamma_{2}
is a lattice in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
We show that the lattice
\Gamma_{2}
is necessarily an arithmetic lattice in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
(see Proposition
4.1
). It is a little surprising that for the irreducible action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
the existence of a cocompact lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
depends on the parity of
n
(see Theorem
1.1
).
When the action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is irreducible, one obvious way of exhibiting lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is to take finite index subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. When
n
is even, it turns out that up to conjugates and commensurability these are the only lattices. In particular, for an even
n
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
does not contain cocompact lattices. However, when
n
is odd,
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
does possess cocompact lattices.

We now state the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
n\geq{2}
, and the irreducible action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ (\simeq{\rm{Sym}}^{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, the following are true:

(1)

\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
contains a cocompact lattice if and only if
n
is odd. Moreover, the cocompact lattices are of the form
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
upto conjugates and commensurability, where
D
is a quaternion division algebra over a totally real number field
k
.

(2)

If
n
is even, all lattices are isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
up to conjugates and commensurability.

Part (1) of the above theorem is proved in Theorem
5.1
. Although part (1) seems to be a purely Lie theoretic statement, the proof depends on considerations of number theoretic nature. More importantly, the ‘only if’ assertion is achieved using Proposition
4.4
which gives the necessary condition for the existence of a cocompact lattice in terms of the field of definition of the algebraic representation of the associated algebraic group. Part (2) of the above theorem is dealt with in Theorem
5.2
. We note here that to exhibit cocompact lattices, we use the classification results for the arithmetic subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
which is described, for instance, in
[
12
, Section 18.5]
.

For a non-trivial and non-irreducible action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we prove the following theorem which is a consequence of Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.2
.

The group
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
contains a cocompact lattice if and only if the multiplicity of the even dimensional representations occurring in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is even.

2.
Notation

In this section we set down the notation relevant to the study of arithmetic lattices in semisimple groups, in particular
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, which will be used repeatedly in this article. We begin with stating the notion of arithmetic subgroups of an algebraic group.

2.1.
Generalities on algebraic groups and arithmetic subgroups

We let
G
denote a semisimple algebraic group defined over a number field
k
. The notion of arithmetic subgroup for a linear algebraic groups is as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

For a linear algebraic group
G
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, a subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
is said to be an arithmetic subgroup, if
\Gamma
is commensurable with the subgroup
G(\mathbb{Z})
.

In view of studying the lattices in Lie groups, we will be assuming a more general definition of arithmetic subgroups that respects commensurability, isomorphisms, and allows to ignore compact subgroups. We state here the definition of arithmetic subgroup as given in
[
12
, Def. 5.1.19]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A subgroup
\Gamma
of a semisimple real Lie group
H
is an arithmetic subgroup if and only if there exists a connected semisimple algebraic group
G^{\prime}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, compact normal subgroups
K
and
K^{\prime}
respectively of the identity connected component
H^{\circ}
of
H
and
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, and an isomorphism
\phi:H^{\circ}/K\rightarrow G^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})/K^{\prime}
such that
\phi(\overline{\Gamma})
is commensurable to
\overline{G^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})}
, where
\overline{\Gamma}
and
\overline{G^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})}
are respectively the images of
\Gamma
and
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
in
H^{\circ}/K
and
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})/K^{\prime}
.

The cocompactness of arithmetic subgroups in semisimple groups is given by the compactness criterion due to Godement which states that for a semisimple algebraic group
G
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
,
G(\mathbb{R})/G(\mathbb{Z})
is compact if and only if
G(\mathbb{Z})
has no nontrivial unipotent elements.

For the classical groups, a complete classification of the
\mathbb{Q}
-forms
[
14
, Chapter 2]
, and hence of their arithmetic subgroups, is available in the literature. We refer the reader to the table in
[
12
, Section 18.5]
to know the arithmetic subgroups of a simple Lie group
G(\mathbb{R})
. In particular, for
G=\mathbf{SL}_{n+1}
the classification of arithmetic subgroups is achieved using central simple algebras defined over number fields. We collect a few related facts here for completeness.

Using Definition
2.2
we see that the Weil restriction of scalars functor provides a way to obtain more arithmetic subgroups of simple Lie groups as explained below. For a number field
k
, we let
\Sigma_{k}^{\infty}
denote the set of distinct archimedean valuations of
k
and
\mathcal{O}_{k}
be the ring of integers in
k
. For any faithful embedding of
G
in
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for some

, we define
G(\mathcal{O}_{k})=G\cap\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
.
If

is an embedding of

in
\mathbb{C}
, we let
G_{\sigma}
denote the algebraic group defined over
\sigma(k)
.
The restriction of scalars to
\mathbb{Q}
gives the algebraic group

\mathrm{Res}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}(G)
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
\mathrm{Res}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}(G)(\mathbb{C})=\prod_{\sigma}G_{\sigma}
, for
\sigma
varying over all archimedean embeddings of

. Let

G^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{Res}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}(G)
. We direct the reader to
[

, Cor. 6.1.4]
,
[
12
, Prop. 5.5.8]
and
[

, Prop. 5.5.10]

Proposition 2.3
.

Let

be a semisimple algebraic group defined over an algebraic number field

and
G^{\prime}=\mathrm{Res}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}(G)
. Then the following holds.

(1)
{G}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
embeds as an arithmetic subgroup of

via the diagonal embedding of

in
\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{k}^{\infty}}k_{v}
.

(2) If

is
k

(3) If

2.2.
Groups of type
A_{1}
:
\mathbb{Q}
-forms and Lattices

Simple algebraic groups defined over a number field
k
of type
A_{n}
are the groups associated to the reduced norm
1
elements of a central simple algebra defined over
k
(see
[
14
, Section 2.3.1]
). The
\bar{k}
-rational points of these groups are the special linear groups
\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}
. Given our interest in arithmetic subgroups of the simple Lie group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we restrict

to be a totally real number field unless specified otherwise and consider quaternion central simple algebras defined over
k

[
7
, Chapter 5]
. The classification of arithmetic subgroups of the classical groups is described in
[
12
, Section 18.5]
.

Let
A
be a quaternion central simple algebra defined over
k
. By Artin-Wedderburn theorem, when
\dim_{k}(A)=4
, there are the following two possibilities: (1)
A=D
, a quaternion division algebra, or (2)
A=\mathrm{M}_{2}(k)
, the algebra of split quaternions. In the former case, the left regular representation of
D
enables us to construct an algebraic group defined over
k
, which is denoted
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}
, such that

\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(k)=\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{b\in D^{*}:\rm{Nrd}(b)=1\},

where
\mathrm{Nrd}:D^{*}\rightarrow k^{*}
is the
reduced norm
map. Using the isomorphism
D\otimes_{k}\bar{k}\simeq\mathrm{M}_{2}(\bar{k})
, we see that
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}
is a simply connected simple group of type
A_{1}
defined over
k
.

[
12
, Prop. 6.2.6 ]
tells that an arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is
cocompact (uniform)
if and only if the corresponding central simple algebra is a quaternion
division
algebra

defined over a totally real number field

and which is ramified at all but one place in
\Sigma_{k}^{\infty}
.
In this case
\Gamma
is commensurable to
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
, where
\mathcal{O}_{D}
is a maximal order in

and the cocompactness of
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is proved using Godement criterion. Moreover, non-isomorphic quaternion central simple algebras define different commensurability classes of arithmetic subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and all such classes arise in this way.

When

, we appeal to Proposition
2.3
to obtain arithmetic subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
as described below. By the assumption on

,

D\otimes_{k}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\underbrace{\mathbb{H}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}}_{(m-1)\mbox{ times}},

where
\mathbb{H}
is the algebra of Hamilton’s quaternions over
\mathbb{R}
. Using Proposition
2.3
we see that
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
embeds as an arithmetic lattice in

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D\otimes_{k}\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\underbrace{\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}}_{(m-1)\ \mbox{times}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\underbrace{\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\cdots\times\mathrm{SU}(2).}_{(m-1)\ \mbox{times}}

(1)

Thus, going modulo the compact factors of
\mathrm{SU}(2)
in (
1
) we obtain a cocompact arithmetic lattice in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. In the case when the quaternion central simple algebra is split, we get the non-cocompact arithmetic lattice
\Gamma\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. In fact, from
[
12
, Prop. 6.1.5]
we see that it is the only non-cocompact arithmetic subgroup in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
up to commensurability and conjugates. We summarize the above discussions in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

For the simple group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, up to conjugates and commensurability,
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
, where
D
is a quaternion central division algebra defined over
k
and split at exactly one real place of

, are all the arithmetic subgroups. Moreover, the cocompact arithmetic subgroups are of the form
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
.

2.3.
Representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
on
D

We end this section with an important observation about the left multiplicative action of
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
on
D
. Observe that a quaternion central division algebra
D
defined over
k
necessarily splits over the algebraic closure
\bar{k}
of

so that,
D\otimes_{k}{\bar{k}}\simeq\mathrm{M}_{2}(\bar{k})
. Thus, for any embedding
\psi:D\hookrightarrow\mathrm{M}_{2}(\bar{k})
, the norm 1 elements
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
of

embeds inside
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\bar{k})
. The left regular action of
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
on

is given by restricting the left matrix multiplication action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\bar{k})
on
\mathrm{M}_{2}(\bar{k})
to the respective images of
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
and

. Note that the latter representation is not irreducible. Indeed, under the embedding

,
\mathrm{M}_{2}(\bar{k})\simeq{\bar{k}}^{2}\oplus\bar{k}^{2}
as
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\bar{k})
-representation, where
\bar{k}^{2}
denotes the standard two-dimensional representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\bar{k})
.
We denote the standard
2
-dimensional representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\bar{k})
on the space
\bar{k}^{2}
by

. This is an algebraic representation defined over
\bar{k}
. The group
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\bar{k})
also acts algebraically on

for

and we denote these representations by

. The representation

and its symmetric powers

are irreducible.

3.
Representations of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\bar{k})

In order to prove Theorem

\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\rho
of the algebraic

-group
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\bar{k})
on

Lemma 3.1
.

The standard representation

\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\bar{k})

.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that

-basis with respect to which

, which is also an
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
-invariant subspace of

as an algebra, giving a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.2

The representation

Proof.

The proof proceeds by exhibiting a

Define the representation

on the

of trace

Let

be the subspace of trace 0 elements in

. As

\bar{k}
,

. Then

is a

We will extend the dichotomy provided by the Lemmas

on
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{m}(\bar{k}^{2})=\bar{k}^{m+1}
is defined over
{k}
if and only if
m
is even.

Proof.

By the highest weight theory, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have up to isomorphism a unique irreducible representation
\mathrm{Sym}^{\ell}\rho
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
with highest weight
\ell
. If the representations
\mathrm{Sym}^{r}\rho
and
\mathrm{Sym}^{s}\rho
are both defined over
k
, then it is immediately seen that the tensor product representation
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}\rho\otimes_{\bar{k}}{\mathrm{Sym}}^{s}\rho
is defined over
k
. The complete reducibility of this tensor product representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\bar{k})
is explicitly given by the Clebsch-Gordan formula
[
3
, Prop. 5.5]
:

{\rm{Sym}}^{r}(\bar{k}^{2})\otimes_{\bar{k}}{\rm{Sym}}^{s}(\bar{k}^{2})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\min\{r,s\}}{\rm{Sym}}^{r+s-2i}(\bar{k}^{2}).

(2)

Note that since
\dim_{\bar{k}}{\rm{Sym}}^{r+s-2i}(\bar{k}^{2})=(r+s-2i)+1
, each irreducible subrepresentations has multiplicity one.
Then, by Proposition
3.3
, the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\bar{k})
on
{\rm{Sym}}^{r}(\bar{k}^{2})\otimes_{\bar{k}}{\rm{Sym}}^{s}(\bar{k}^{2})
is defined over
k
if and only if each irreducible subrepresentation
\mathrm{Sym}^{r+s-2i}\rho
, for
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,\mbox{min}\{r,s\}\}
, is defined over
k
.

We first prove that when
m=2n
is even,
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\rho
is defined over
k
. The strategy is to use induction on
n
. The trivial representation is vacuously defined over
k
. When
n=1
, Lemma
3.2
upholds the result. Let
n>1
. Assume that the representations
\mathrm{Sym}^{2i}\rho
are all defined over
k
for
i
varying over the nonnegative integers up to
n-1
. Choose nonnegative even integers
r,s
such that
0<r,s<m
and
r+s>m=2n
. Observe that
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\bar{k}^{2})
appears as a direct summand in (
2
) when
i=(r+s-m)/2
. By induction hypothesis,
\mathrm{Sym}^{j}(\bar{k}^{2})
is defined over
k
for
j=r,s
so that the tensor product on the left hand side is defined over
k
. Now, Proposition
3.3
forces each irreducible representation on the right hand side of (
2
) to be defined over
k
. Hence,
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\rho
is defined over
k
.

To prove the only if part, it is enough to show that when
m=2n+1
is an odd integer the representation
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\rho
is not defined over
k
. On the contrary, if possible, suppose that
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\rho
is defined over
k
, so that the tensor product representation
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\rho\otimes_{\bar{k}}\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\rho
is defined over
k
. The decomposition in (
2
) together with Proposition
3.3
then implies that the representations
\mathrm{Sym}^{2n+3}\rho
and
\mathrm{Sym}^{2n-1}\rho
are both defined over k. Reiterating the above procedure, we eventually get that
\mathrm{Sym}^{1}\rho=\rho
is defined over
k
.
This contradicts Lemma
3.1
.
Thus,
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\bar{k}^{2})
for
m
odd, is not defined over
k
. Hence the proposition.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We note here that the Clebsh-Gordon formula for decomposition of tensor product of representation is known only for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
and hence this limits the extension of the present methods to higher rank.

4.
Lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

In this section we detail on the discussions surrounding Theorem
1.1
stated in the Introduction. Notation from the previous sections are used here without further definition. Let
\Gamma
be a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\varphi
is not necessarily an irreducible representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Gamma_{2}
be the projection of
\Gamma
on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. By
[
18
, Prop. 1.3]
both
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are lattices. It is interesting to see that the very existence of the lattice
\Gamma
necessitates
\Gamma_{2}
to be an arithmetic subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
n\geq{2}
, let
\varphi
be any nontrivial representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
be a lattice. Then, the lattice
\Gamma_{2}
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is an arithmetic lattice.

Proof.

Suppose the representation
\varphi:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is faithful. Since
\Gamma_{1}
is a normal subgroup in
\Gamma
,
\Gamma_{2}
is contained in
\Lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}):\varphi(g)\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{1}\}
which is easily seen to be an arithmetic subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed,
\varphi(\Lambda)=\varphi(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap{a\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})a^{-1}}
for some
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
which conjugates
\Gamma_{1}
to the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
given by the standard
\mathbb{Q}
-structure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since,
\varphi(\Gamma_{2})
is a lattice in
\varphi(\Lambda)
, this implies that the Zariski closure of
\varphi(\Lambda)
determines an algebraic subgroup
G
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
of
\mathbf{SL}_{n}
such that,
\varphi(\Gamma_{2})\subset G(\mathbb{Q})
and
G(\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It follows that
\Gamma_{2}
is an arithmetic subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Now, given any nontrivial representation
\varphi
, either
\varphi
is faithful or
\mathrm{Ker}(\varphi)=\{\pm{\mathrm{I}}\}
which is a compact subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The latter case gives a faithful representation of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In the light of the arguments in the preceding paragraph, we obtain an algebraic group
G
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
G(\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and the image of
\Gamma_{2}
in
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is arithmetic subgroup with respect to this
\mathbb{Q}
-structure. Consider the simply connected cover
\widetilde{G}
of
G
. Since
G
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-form of
\mathbf{PSL}_{2}
, we know that its (algebraically) simply connected cover will be a
\mathbb{Q}
-form of the group
\mathbf{SL}_{2}
. Since
\widetilde{G}\rightarrow G
is an isogeny we get that
\Gamma_{2}
is an arithmetic subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the
\mathbb{Q}
-structure given by
\widetilde{G}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

A careful reading of the proof of Proposition
4.1
reveals that regardless of the parity of
n
, if
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is a lattice, we get a
\mathbb{Q}
-form of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
determined by an algebraic subgroup
G
of
\mathbf{SL}_{n}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, which contains the image of
\Gamma_{2}
in
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. We emphasize here that the existence of such a
\mathbb{Q}
-form of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
depends only on the fact that
\Gamma_{2}
stabilizes a lattice
\Gamma_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and is irrespective of the action being irreducible or not.

Proposition 4.3
.

If a lattice
\Gamma_{2}
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
stabilizes a lattice
\Gamma_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n
, then
\Gamma_{2}
is an arithmetic lattice in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
determined by a quaternion central simple algebra defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
, and Remark
4.2
yields that
\Gamma_{2}
is an arithmetic lattice with respect to a
\mathbb{Q}
-form
G
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Since the
\mathbb{Q}
-forms of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
are precisely
\mathbf{SL}_{2}
and
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}
for quaternion division algebras
D
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, we conclude that
G=\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}
or
\mathbf{SL}_{2}
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

If
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is a cocompact lattice, then the nontrivial representation
\varphi
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{2}
be the projection of
\Gamma
on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Since
\Gamma_{2}
is a cocompact arithmetic lattice in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, by Proposition
4.3

\Gamma_{2}=\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
for some quaternion central division algebra
D
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Being a lattice,
\Gamma_{2}
is Zariski dense in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
by Borel density theorem. If
F=\mathbb{Q}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})
is an extension of
\mathbb{Q}
by the coefficients of the polynomials
f
defining
\varphi
, then for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})
,
\Gamma_{2}
intersects the Zariski open set
U_{\sigma}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{p\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}):\sigma{(f)}(p)\neq{f(p)}\}
nontrivially if
U_{\sigma}\neq\emptyset
. This gives a contradiction as
f(p)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
p\in\Gamma_{2}
. Thus,
U_{\sigma}=\emptyset
so that
F=\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For a quaternion division algebra
D
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, if a representation
\varphi:\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
has a cocompact lattice.

Proof.

Lemma 4.1 of
[
14
]
shows the existence of a finite index subgroup
H
of
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
when
\varphi
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, such that
\varphi(\mathrm{H})\subset\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with respect to an
\mathbb{R}
-basis
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. That is,
H
stabilizes an arithmetic lattice
\Gamma_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence, there exists a cocompact lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\varphi}{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}
projecting onto
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

At this point we need to be mindful about the parity of
n
when the action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is irreducible. The necessary condition stated in the Proposition
4.4
does not hold true when
n
is an even integer. In Lemma
4.6
,
D
continues to be a quaternion division algebra defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and split over
\mathbb{R}
. We let
G=\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}
so that
G(\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 4.6
.

For
n\geq{2}
, the irreducible representation of
G(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if
n
is odd.

Proof.

This result follows from Proposition
3.4
if we restrict the representation defined over the completion
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{Q}
and see that the induced irreducible representation of
G(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
on
\mathrm{Sym}^{n-1}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^{2})
, which is a priori defined over the algebraic closure
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
, is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if
n
is odd.

∎

5.
Proof of main theorems

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
stated in the Introduction. With the detailed analysis on
\mathbb{Q}
-structures done in the preceding section, we need only combine the relevant results to prove Theorem
1.1
.

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Theorem 5.1

(Part (1), Theorem
1.1
)
.

For
n\geq{2}
, and the irreducible action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ (\simeq{\rm{Sym}}^{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, the group
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
contains a cocompact lattice if and only if
n
is odd. Moreover, the cocompact lattices are of the form
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
upto conjugates and commensurability, where
D
is a quaternion division algebra over a totally real number field
k
.

Proof.

A
\mathbb{Q}
-structure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
invariant under an irreducible action of a
\mathbb{Q}
-structure
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}
on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
exists only for an odd dimensional representation is seen from Lemma
4.6
. The result now follows from Lemma
4.5
.

When
\Gamma
is a cocompact lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the projection
\Gamma_{2}
of
\Gamma
onto
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is a cocompact arithmetic lattice in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and has the form
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
for a quaternion division algebra
D
over a totally real number field
k
. Then the structure of
\Gamma
follows from the arguments in Theorem
5.2
below.
∎

Note that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
contains a
non-cocompact
lattice of the form
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. In the following theorem we prove that for an irreducible representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
of even dimension
n
, all lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
are of the form
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. This will complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 5.2

(Part (2), Theorem
1.1
)
.

For a positive even integer
n
and the irreducible action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, every lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
up to conjugates and commensurability.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
denote the projection map. Then, for a lattice
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma\cap{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
\Gamma_{2}=\pi(\Gamma)
are lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, respectively. We have the exact sequence of lattices

0\longrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\longrightarrow\Gamma\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\Gamma_{2}\longrightarrow{1}.

When
n
is even, by Lemma
4.6
and the notation therein, the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is not defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. By Theorem
5.1
this implies that
\Gamma_{2}
is a
non-cocompact
arithmetic lattice in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and hence, is the group of
\mathbb{Z}
-points of the split central simple algebra
\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
over
\mathbb{Q}
(see Section
2
). Therefore,
\Gamma_{2}
is commensurable with
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and thus, contains a free subgroup
\Lambda
having a finite index in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Indeed, for any free subgroup
\tilde{\Lambda}
of finite index in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
\Lambda=\Gamma_{2}\cap\tilde{\Lambda}
is a free subgroup of finite index in
\Gamma_{2}\cap\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
The inverse image
\pi^{-1}(\Lambda)
of
\Lambda
, contained inside
\Gamma
, gives the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\longrightarrow{\pi^{-1}(\Lambda)}\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\Lambda\longrightarrow{1}.

(3)

Now, it can easily be seen that the map
\pi^{*}:\Gamma/\pi^{-1}(\Lambda)\longrightarrow\Gamma_{2}/\Lambda
, defined as
\pi^{*}([\gamma])\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[\pi(\gamma)]
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, is a bijection. Therefore
\pi^{-1}(\Lambda)
is a subgroup of finite index in
\Gamma
and hence, a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Since
\Lambda
is a free group, the sequence (
3
) splits, giving
\pi^{-1}(\Lambda)\cong\Gamma_{1}\rtimes\Lambda
. Up to conjugates and commensurability, every lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is of the form
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Thus,
\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
up to conjugation and commensurability.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We now proceed to consider a non-trivial and not necessarily irreducible representation
\varphi
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We continue with the notation from the previous sections. If the lattice
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is cocompact, then the lattice
\Gamma_{2}
is cocompact in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. By Proposition
4.3
,
\Gamma_{2}=\mathrm{SL}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
for a quaternion division algebra
D
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and split over
\mathbb{R}
. Then by Proposition
4.4
, the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. A converse to this statement is true with a certain restriction on the multiplicities of the irreducible subrepresentations of even dimension occurring in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

A representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if each irreducible subrepresentation of even dimension in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has even multiplicity.

Proof.

To prove the sufficiency assertion, we need only show that for any even integer
m
, the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathrm{Sym}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\oplus\mathrm{Sym}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. By Proposition
4.4
the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
D\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. The discussion towards the end of Section
2.2
shows that
\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
decomposes into
\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}
as an
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
-module. Thus, the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\oplus\mathrm{Sym}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

By Lemma
4.6
the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\cong\rm{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Following the isomorphism of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
-modules using the Clebsch-Gordon formula in (
2
),

\underbrace{(\rm{Sym}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\oplus\rm{Sym}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\rm{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}_{\mbox{defined over }\mathbb{Q}}\cong\underbrace{\rm{Sym}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\oplus\rm{Sym}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\oplus\underbrace{\rm{Sym}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\oplus\rm{Sym}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}_{\mbox{defined over }\mathbb{Q}},

the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathrm{Sym}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\oplus\mathrm{Sym}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cong\mathbb{R}^{4}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{4}
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Now, use induction on the dimension
m
to see that the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{m}\cong\rm{Sym}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\oplus\rm{Sym}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
when
m
is an even integer.

For the necessary assertion, assume that the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let

\mathbb{R}^{n}\cong\underbrace{(\oplus_{i=1}^{s_{1}}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2l_{i}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\oplus\mathrm{Sym}^{2l_{i}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})))}_{\mbox{defined over }\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\underbrace{(\oplus_{i=1}^{s_{2}}\mathrm{Sym}^{2m_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}_{\mbox{defined over }\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\ (\oplus_{i=1}^{s_{3}}\mathrm{Sym}^{2n_{i}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))

be the decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into irreducible
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
-submodules, where
n_{i}\neq{n_{j}}
for
i\neq{j}
. From the arguments in the ‘if’ part above and Lemma
4.6
, the terms in the first two parenthesis are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Assuming the
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
-submodule
\oplus_{i=1}^{s_{3}}\mathrm{Sym}^{2n_{i}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is non-zero, we get that the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\oplus_{i=1}^{s_{3}}\mathrm{Sym}^{2n_{i}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Hence, by Proposition
3.3
, we see that the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathrm{Sym}^{2n_{i}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, for each
i
, is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. This is a contradiction to Lemma
4.6
. Thus,
\oplus_{i=1}^{s_{3}}\mathrm{Sym}^{2n_{i}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is trivial. That is, each irreducible representation of even dimension which occurs in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has even multiplicity.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

If the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
contains a cocompact lattice.

Proof.

Proof of Lemma
4.5
does not depend on the irreducibility of the representation of
\mathbf{SL}_{1,D}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

Proposition
4.4
states that, if
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
contains a cocompact lattice, then
\varphi
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. The converse assertion follows from Propositions
5.3
and
5.4
.
∎

Although the cocompact lattices are of primary interest in this article, we observe that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and any representation
\varphi
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on

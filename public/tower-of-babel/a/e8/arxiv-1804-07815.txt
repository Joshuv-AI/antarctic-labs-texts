Decidability of Timed Communicating Automata
Lorenzo Clemente

Abstract
We study the reachability problem for networks of timed communicating processes. Each process is a timed automaton communicating with other processes by exchanging messages over unbounded FIFO channels. Messages carry clocks which are checked at the time of transmission and reception with suitable timing constraints. Each automaton can only access its set of local clocks and message clocks of sent/received messages. Time is dense and all clocks evolve at the same rate. Our main contribution is a complete characterisation of decidable and undecidable communication topologies generalising and unifying previous work. From a technical point of view, we use quantifier elimination and a reduction to counter automata with registers.

The original name

In order to stress that we consider timed channels, we speak about timed communicating automata.

Messages are additionally equipped with densely-valued clocks which elapse at the same rate as local
ta

When a message is sent, a logical constraint between local and message clocks specifies the initial values for the latter;
if multiple values are allowed, a satisfying one is chosen nondeterministically.
Symmetrically, when a message is received, a logical constraint on local and message clocks
specifies whether the reception is possible.

We consider three kinds of clocks:

classical clocks
over the rationals
\mathbb{Q}
,

integral clocks
over the nonnegative integers
\mathbb{N}
,
and
fractional clocks
over the unit interval
\mathbb{I}:=\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1)

All clocks evolve at the same rate;
an integral clock behaves the same as a classical clock,
except that in constraints it evaluates to the underlying integral part;
when a fractional clock reaches value
1
,
its value is wrapped around
0
.
Integral and fractional clocks are complementary in the sense that they express two perpendicular features of time:
Integral clocks are unbounded but discrete,
and fractional clocks are bounded but dense.
For classical and integral clocks
\mathtt{x},\mathtt{y}
,
we consider
inequality

\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{y}\sim k
and
modulo

\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{y}\equiv_{m}k

for fractional clocks

\mathtt{x}\sim\mathtt{y}

where
\sim\in\left\{<,\leq,\geq,>\right\}

In the presence of fractional clocks, constraints on classical and integral clocks are inter-reducible.
Nevertheless, we consider separately classical, integral, and fractional clocks, mainly for two reasons.
First, in our main result below we can point out with greater precision what makes the model computationally harder.
Second, from a technical standpoint
it is sometimes more convenient to manipulate classical clocks—their constraints are invariant w.r.t. the elapse of time;
sometimes integral clocks—they reduce the impedance when converting to counters.

The
non-emptiness problem
asks whether there exists an execution of the

where all processes start and end in predefined control locations,
with empty channels both at the beginning and at the end of the execution.
It is long-known that already in the untimed setting of communicating automata (

the model is Turing-powerful
[
15
]
,
and thus all verification questions such as non-emptiness are undecidable.
Decidability can be regained by restricting the

i.e., the graph where vertices are processes
\mathtt{p}
,
and there is an edge
\mathtt{p}\to\mathtt{q}

\mathtt{p}
to process
\mathtt{q}

A

is a disjoint union of polytrees.
Our main result is a complete characterisation of the decidable

Non-emptiness of

More precisely, it was shown there that,

Regarding decidability,

In particular, combining timed channels with diagonal constraints on message and local clocks

Our characterisation completes the picture of decidable

While our undecidability results are essentially inherited from

Our second technical contribution is the encoding of fractional clocks into

Timed communicating automata.

Main result

We characterise completely which

\thmmain

Remark 1

(Inequality vs. emptiness tests)
.

A similar characterisation for
untimed
channels appeared previously in
[
19
]
,
where channels can be tested for emptiness.
In that setting, it was shown that non-emptiness of discrete-time
tca
with untimed channels
is decidable precisely for polyforest topologies
where in each polytree there is at most one channel which can be tested for emptiness.
Since a timed channel with inequality tests
can simulate an untimed channel with emptiness tests,
our decidability result generalises
[
19
]

to the more general case of timed channels,
and our undecidability result follows from their characterisation.
The simulation is done as follows.
Suppose processes
\mathtt{p},\mathtt{q}
want to cooperate
in such a way that
\mathtt{q}
can test whether the channel
\mathtt{pq}
is empty.
Time instants are split between even and odd instants.
All standard operations of
\mathtt{p},\mathtt{q}
are performed at odd instants.
At even time instants,
\mathtt{p}
sends to
\mathtt{q}
a special message
\hat{\mathtt{m}}
with initial age
0

by performing
{\mathsf{send}(\mathtt{pq},\hat{\mathtt{m}}:\mathtt{x}^{\mathtt{pq}}=0)}
.
Process
\mathtt{q}
simulates an emptiness test on
\mathtt{pq}

by receiving message
\hat{\mathtt{m}}
with the same age
0

{\mathsf{receive}(\mathtt{pq},\hat{\mathtt{m}}:\mathtt{x}^{\mathtt{pq}}=0)}
.
This is indeed correct because if some other message
\mathtt{m}
was sent by
\mathtt{p}
afterwards,
then
\hat{\mathtt{m}}
would have age
\geq 1
,
since all other operations happen at odd instants.

Proof of the “only if” direction.

If the topology is not a polyforest, i.e., it contains an undirected cycle,
then it is well-known that non-emptiness is undecidable already in the untimed setting

[
15
,
37
]
.
If the topology is a polyforest, but it contains a polytree with more than one timed channel with integral inequality tests,
then undecidability follows from
[
19
, Theorem 3]
already in discrete time,
since non-emptiness tests (on the side of the receiver) can be simulated by timed channels with inequality tests
as remarked above.
∎

4
Simple
tca

A
tca
is
simple
if: it contains only integral and fractional clocks;
send constraints are of the form
\mathtt{x}^{\mathtt{c}}=0
(for
\mathtt{x}^{\mathtt{c}}
a channel clock);
receive constraints of the form
\mathtt{x}^{\mathtt{c}}\sim k
,
\mathtt{x}^{\mathtt{c}}\equiv_{M}k

for an integral clock
\mathtt{x}^{\mathtt{c}}:\mathbb{N}
,
and of the form
\mathtt{y}^{\mathtt{pq}}=\mathtt{y}^{\mathtt{q}}

for fractional clocks
\mathtt{y}^{\mathtt{pq}},\mathtt{y}^{\mathtt{q}}:\mathbb{I}
.
We present a non-emptiness preserving transformation of a given
tca
into a simple one.

Remove integral clocks.

We remove integral clocks,
by expressing their constraints as combinations of classical and fractional constraints.
Unlike integral and fractional constraints,
classical constraints
\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{y}\sim k
with
\mathtt{x},\mathtt{y}:\mathbb{Q}

are invariant under the elapse of time.
For every integral clock
\mathtt{x}:\mathbb{N}
,
we introduce a classical
\mathtt{x}_{\mathbb{Q}}:\mathbb{Q}
and a fractional clock
\mathtt{x}_{\mathbb{I}}:\mathbb{I}

which are reset at the same moment as
\mathtt{x}
.
A constraint
\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{y}\leq k
on clocks
\mathtt{x},\mathtt{y}:\mathbb{N}

is replaced by the equivalent

(\mathtt{x}_{\mathbb{Q}}-\mathtt{y}_{\mathbb{Q}}\leq k\wedge\mathtt{x}_{\mathbb{I}}\leq\mathtt{y}_{\mathbb{I}})\vee\mathtt{x}_{\mathbb{Q}}-\mathtt{y}_{\mathbb{Q}}\leq k-1
.
The same technique can handle modulo constraints and channel clocks.

Copy-send.

A
tca
is
copy-send
if channel clocks are always copies of local clocks of the sender process,
i.e.,
\mathtt{X}^{\mathtt{pq}}=\left\{\hat{\mathtt{x}}_{i}^{\mathtt{pq}}\;\middle|\;\mathtt{x}_{i}^{\mathtt{p}}\in\mathtt{X}^{\mathtt{p}}\right\}
,
and all send constraints of process
\mathtt{p}
are equal to

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathsf{copy}}^{\mathtt{p}}\ \equiv\ \bigwedge_{\mathtt{x}^{\mathtt{p}}_{i}\in\mathtt{X}^{\mathtt{p}}}\hat{\mathtt{x}}^{\mathtt{pq}}_{i}=\mathtt{x}^{\mathtt{p}}_{i}.

Euclidean sets with only one distance modulo a prime ideal
Hiroshi Nozaki

Abstract
Let X X be a finite set in the Euclidean space ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . If the squared distance between any two distinct points in X X is an odd integer, then the cardinality of X X is bounded above by d + 2 d+2 , as shown by Rosenfeld (1997) or Smith (1995). They proved that there exists a ( d + 2 ) (d+2) -point set X X in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} having only odd integral squared distances if and only if d + 2 d+2 is congruent to 0 0 modulo 4 4 . The distances can be interpreted as an element of the finite field ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} . We generalize this result for a local ring ( A 𝔭 , 𝔭 ​ A 𝔭 ) (A_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}) as follows. Let K K be an algebraic number field that can be embedded into ℝ \mathbb{R} . Fix an embedding of K K into ℝ \mathbb{R} , and K K is interpreted as a subfield of ℝ \mathbb{R} . Let A = 𝒪 K A=\mathcal{O}_{K} be the ring of integers of K K , and 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} a prime ideal of 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} . Let ( A 𝔭 , 𝔭 ​ A 𝔭 ) (A_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}) be the local ring obtained from the localization ( A ∖ 𝔭 ) − 1 ​ A (A\setminus\mathfrak{p})^{-1}A , which is interpreted as a subring of ℝ \mathbb{R} . If the squared distances of X ⊂ ℝ d X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} are in A 𝔭 A_{\mathfrak{p}} and each squared distance is congruent to some constant k ≢ 0 k\not\equiv 0 modulo 𝔭 ​ A 𝔭 \mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}} , then | X | ≤ d + 2 |X|\leq d+2 , as shown by Nozaki (2023). We continue to use the same notation as before.
Throughout this paper, let

It is well known that for a prime ideal

\mathbb{Z}

The following theorem can be proved using the same method as presented in

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p
be a prime number in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
A
be the ring of integers of an algebraic number field.
Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset A
be a prime ideal lying above
p\mathbb{Z}
.
If there exists a
1
-distance set modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d+2
points, then
d+2\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a tight 1-distance set modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We may suppose
D_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)=\{1\}
.
Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
be the points in
X
, where
n=d+2=|X|
. For
u_{i}=x_{i}-x_{1}
(
2\leq i\leq n
), we consider the Gram matrix
M=(2\langle u_{i},u_{j}\rangle)_{i,j=2,\ldots,n}
, where
\langle,\rangle
is the usual inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The rank of
M
is the dimension of the subspace spanned by
\{u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}\}
, which is at most
d
.
Thus, the rank of
M
is less than
n-1
, and hence the determinant of
M
is 0.
From our assumption,
||u_{i}||^{2}=||x_{i}-x_{1}||^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and
||u_{i}-u_{j}||^{2}=||x_{i}-x_{j}||^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, and hence

2\langle u_{i},u_{j}\rangle=||u_{i}||^{2}+||u_{j}||^{2}-||u_{i}-u_{j}||^{2}\equiv\begin{cases}2\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\text{ if $i=j$},\\
1\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\text{ if $i\neq j$}.\end{cases}

Therefore,

0=\det(M)\equiv\det(J+I)=n=d+2\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}},

and
d+2\equiv 0\pmod{p}
because of
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap\mathbb{Z}=p\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A
be the ring of integers of an algebraic number field, and let
\mathfrak{p}
be the prime ideal of
A
lying above
2\mathbb{Z}
. Let
n
be an odd positive integer, and suppose that a symmetric matrix
M=(m_{ij})\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
satisfies

\displaystyle m_{ij}\equiv\begin{cases}2\pmod{2\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}&\text{if }i=j,\\
1\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

(2.2)

Then
\det M\equiv n+1\pmod{2\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.

Proof.

Since
\det(J+I)=n+1
, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\det M\equiv\det(J+I)\pmod{2\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}.

(2.3)

In general, for a square matrix
N=(n_{ij})\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
and a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
, define

N(\sigma)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}n_{k,\sigma(k)}.

If
N
is symmetric, then

N(\sigma)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}n_{k,\sigma(k)}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}n_{\sigma^{-1}(k),k}=N(\sigma^{-1}).

Define
M^{\prime}=(m^{\prime}_{ij})\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
by

m^{\prime}_{ij}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}m_{ii}&\text{if }i=j,\\
m_{ij}&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

Then

m^{\prime}_{ij}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\quad(1\leq i,j\leq n).

Let
f_{\sigma}
be the number of fixed points of
\sigma\in S_{n}
.
For
\sigma\in S_{n}
with
\sigma=\sigma^{-1}
,
f_{\sigma}
is nonzero since

is odd. Since
M^{\prime}(\sigma)\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
(equivalently
2M^{\prime}(\sigma)\equiv 2\pmod{2\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
) by the above congruence, we have

M(\sigma)=2^{f_{\sigma}}M^{\prime}(\sigma)=2^{f_{\sigma}-1}\cdot 2M^{\prime}(\sigma)\equiv 2^{f_{\sigma}}=(J+I)(\sigma)\pmod{2\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}.

For
\sigma\in S_{n}
with
\sigma\neq\sigma^{-1}

M(\sigma)\equiv(J+I)(\sigma)\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, we have

M(\sigma)+M(\sigma^{-1})=2M(\sigma)\equiv 2(J+I)(\sigma)=(J+I)(\sigma)+(J+I)(\sigma^{-1})\pmod{2\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}.

(2.4)

Using the definition of the determinant together with (

is odd. Note that
8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap\mathbb{Z}=16\mathbb{Z}
. The matrix entries satisfy

2\langle u_{i},u_{j}\rangle=||u_{i}||^{2}+||u_{j}||^{2}-||u_{i}-u_{j}||^{2}\equiv\begin{cases}2\pmod{8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}&\text{if }i=j,\\
1,9\pmod{8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}&\text{if }i\neq j,\end{cases}

since
||u_{i}||^{2}\equiv||u_{j}||^{2}\equiv 1,9\pmod{8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and

||u_{i}-u_{j}||^{2}\equiv\begin{cases}0\pmod{8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}&\text{if }i=j,\\
1,9\pmod{8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

Let
f_{\sigma}
be the number of fixed points of
\sigma\in S_{n}
.
From
2=2\cdot 1\equiv 2\cdot 9\pmod{8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
,
one has
2M(\sigma)\equiv 2\cdot 2^{f_{\sigma}}\pmod{8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
Moreover, if
f_{\sigma}
is nonzero, then
M(\sigma)=2^{f_{\sigma}}
.

For
\sigma\in S_{n-1}
with
\sigma=\sigma^{-1}
,

\displaystyle M(\sigma)\equiv 2^{f_{\sigma}}\equiv(J+I)(\sigma)\pmod{8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}

since
f_{\sigma}
is nonzero.
For
\sigma\in S_{n-1}
with
\sigma\neq\sigma^{-1}
,

\displaystyle M(\sigma)+M(\sigma^{-1})=2M(\sigma)\equiv 2\cdot 2^{f_{\sigma}}=2(J+I)(\sigma)=(J+I)(\sigma)+(J+I)(\sigma^{-1})\pmod{8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}.

Therefore, one has

0=\det(M)\equiv\det(J+I)=n=d+2\pmod{8\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}},

and
d\equiv 2\pmod{16}
.
∎

3
Tight 1-distance set modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}

In this section, we provide examples of tight 1-distance sets modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
We use the same notation
K
,
\mathcal{O}_{K}=A
,
\mathfrak{p}
, and
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
as in the previous sections.
Initially, we will verify whether the number of modular distances decreases upon field extension
L\supset K
.
Let
P\subset B=\mathcal{O}_{L}
be a prime ideal lying above a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset A=\mathcal{O}_{K}
, namely
\mathfrak{p}=P\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}
holds.
If
D(X)\subset A_{\mathfrak{p}}
holds, then
D(X)\subset B_{P}
and
|D_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)|=|D_{P}(X)|
hold.
Indeed, the natural homomorphism
\alpha+\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\mapsto\alpha+PB_{P}
is injective.
From this discussion, if every element of
D(X)=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
is an algebraic number, then it is sufficient to select the field
K=\mathbb{Q}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s})
for the reduction of the number of modular distances.

An
s
-distance set modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
can be modified to a non-similar
s
-distance set modulo
PB_{P}
for some ring
B\supset A
and a prime ideal
P\subset B
with
\mathfrak{p}=P\cap A
.
From the following lemma, for an
s
-distance set
X
modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{p}
,
we may suppose that the coordinates of each point in
X
are contained in some finite extension field
L\supset K
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a finite set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose squared distances
D(X)
are contained in an algebraic number field
K
.
Then, there exist a congruence transformation
\sigma
and a finite extension field
L
of
K
such that
L\subset\mathbb{R}
and
L
contains all coordinates of each point of
\sigma(X)
.

Proof.

Let
M
be the squared-distance matrix
M=(||x-y||^{2})_{x,y\in X}
of
X
.
From our assumption, each element of
M
belongs to
K
. Let
P
be the matrix
P=I-(1/|X|)J
.
By direct calculation,
N=(-1/2)PMP
is the Gram matrix of
\sigma(X)
with some congruence transformation
\sigma

[
12
]
.
Note that each entry in the Gram matrix
N
belongs to
K
.
By the Cholesky factorization algorithm
[
8
, Theorem 10.9]
, there exists an
n\times d
matrix
F
such that
N=FF^{\top}
.
The computation of each entry of
F
, as defined in the algorithm, establishes that each entry belongs to a certain finite extension field
L
of
K
. The row vectors in
F
can be regarded as the coordinates of
\sigma(X)
.
This implies the lemma.
∎

From the following theorem,
infinitely many non-similar modular
s
-distance sets can be obtained from a given
s
-distance set modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
while maintaining the cardinality.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an
s
-distance set modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
A=\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal of
A
.
From Lemma
3.1
, we may suppose that there exists a finite extension field
L\supset K
such that
L
contains all coordinates
x_{ij}
of each point
x_{i}=(x_{i1},\ldots,x_{id})\in X
.
Let
P\subset B=\mathcal{O}_{L}
be a prime ideal lying above
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
Let
t=\max\{0,-\min_{i,j}{\rm ord}_{P}(2x_{ij})\}
. Then,
for any
a_{i}=(a_{i1},\ldots,a_{id})
with
a_{ij}\in(PB_{P})^{t+1}
, the set
X^{\prime}=\{x_{1}+a_{1},\ldots,x_{n}+a_{n}\}
is
an
s
-distance set modulo
PB_{P}
.

Proof.

For any
x_{i}+a_{i},x_{j}+a_{j}\in X^{\prime}
, the squared distance between them is

\displaystyle||(x_{i}+a_{i})-(x_{j}+a_{j})||^{2}

\displaystyle=||(x_{i}-x_{j})+(a_{i}-a_{j})||^{2}

\displaystyle=||x_{i}-x_{j}||^{2}+2\langle x_{i}-x_{j},a_{i}-a_{j}\rangle+||a_{i}-a_{j}||^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv||x_{i}-x_{j}||^{2}\pmod{PB_{P}}

from our assumption because of
{\rm ord}_{P}(2(x_{ik}-x_{jk}))\geq\min\{{\rm ord}_{P}(2x_{ik}),{\rm ord}_{P}(2x_{jk}))\}\geq-t
and
{\rm ord}_{P}(a_{ik}-a_{jk})\geq t+1
. Thus, the assertion follows.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

From the tight 1-distance set modulo
p\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
defined in (
2.1
),
we can obtain infinitely many non-similar tight modular 1-distance sets.

In the remaining part of this section, we provide tight 1-distance sets modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
having only two Euclidean distances.
For
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
D(X)=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
, the following value
t_{i}
is called the
LRS ratio
:

t_{i}=\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{j}-a_{i}}

for
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}

[
9
,
13
]
. Note that
\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}=1

[
11
]
.
If
|X|>\binom{d+s-1}{s-1}+\binom{d+s-2}{s-2}
holds,
then the values
t_{i}
are algebraic integers
[
14
, Corollary 4.2]
.
If
t_{i}
is an algebraic integer for each
i
, then there does not exist
K
such that

|D_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)|<s
for some
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}

[
14
, Equation (4.1)]
. The converse is not true in general, but it is true for
s=2
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a 2-distance set with
D(X)=\{1,a\}
. Suppose the squared distance
a
is an algebraic number. Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(a)
and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
the ring of integers of
K
.
The LRS ratio
t=1/(1-a)
is not an algebraic integer if and only if there exists a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
|D_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)|=1
.

Proof.

We only prove that
t=1/(1-a)\not\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
implies that there exists
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
|D_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)|=1
.
Let
(1-a)\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the fractional ideal generated by
1-a
. We can obtain the prime factorization
(1-a)\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}^{c_{r}}
.
Since
t=1/(1-a)\not\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, there exists
i
such that
c_{i}\geq 1
. Put
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for such
i
, and then

(1-a)A_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{p}^{c_{i}}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
This implies

a\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, and
D_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)=\{1\}
.
∎

A 2-distance set is identified with a representation of a simple graph. If a 2-distance set
X
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has size at least
d+2
, then
X
is a minimal-dimensional representation of some simple graph
[
5
,
19
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a 2-distance set with
D(X)=\{1,a\}
. If
|X|\geq d+2
, then
a
is an algebraic number.

Proof.

Let
A
be the adjacency matrix of the simple graph corresponding to the 2-distance set
X
, and
the squared-distance matrix of
X
is equal to
M=aA+A^{\prime}
, where
A^{\prime}=J-I-A
.
Let
P=I-(1/|X|)J
, which is the orthogonal projection matrix onto the space
j^{\perp}
, where
j^{\perp}
is the space perpendicular to the all-ones vector
j
.
Then,
(-1/2)PMP
is the Gram matrix of
\sigma(X)
with some congruence transformation
\sigma
and the rank of
-PMP
is at most
d

[
12
]
.
Given that
|X|\geq d+2
, the smallest eigenvalue of
-PMP=-(aPAP+PA^{\prime}P)
on
j^{\perp}
is 0.
Since
PAP
and
PA^{\prime}P=-P-PAP
have the same eigenspaces, the distance
a
is expressed as
a=(\lambda+1)/\lambda
, where
\lambda
is the smallest eigenvalue of
PAP
on
j^{\perp}
.
All entries of
PAP
are rational, and hence
\lambda
and
a
are algebraic numbers.
∎

From Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
,
a tight modular 1-distance set with two Euclidean distances is characterized by the property that its LRS ratio is not an algebraic integer.
If
X
is on a sphere and
|D_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)|=1
for some prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, then
|X|\leq d+1
, which is proved by the same manner as in
[
14
]
.
A non-spherical 2-distance set with
d+2
points is characterized as Type (5), see the definition of Type (5) in
[
15
, Theorem 2.4]
.
There are many simple graphs of Type (5) whose LRS ratio is not an algebraic integer.
It is easy to obtain such graphs from random graphs.

A tight 1-distance set modulo
2\mathbb{Z}
given in
[
7
,
18
]
is a regular simplex and its center, which is a 2-distance set.
Nozaki and Shinohara
[
16
]
investigated a 2-distance set that contains a regular simplex.
Let
\mathcal{R}
be the
d
-dimensional regular simplex.
The set
\mathcal{R}
can be expressed by
\mathcal{R}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d+1}\}\subset H_{d}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\mid\langle j,x\rangle=1\}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
j
is the all-ones vector and
e_{i}
is the vector with the
i
-th entry 1 and the other entries 0.
It is proved in
[
16
]
that
\mathcal{R}\cup\{x\}
is a 2-distance set in
H_{d}
if and only if
x\in T_{d}(k,\beta)=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d+1})\in H_{d}\mid\forall i,x_{i}\in\{c,c+\beta\},|N(x,c)|=k\}
, where
N(x,c)=\{i\mid x_{i}=c\}
,
k\in\{1,\ldots,d+1\}
,

c=\frac{1}{d+1}-\frac{d+1-k}{d+1}\beta,\text{ and }\beta=\begin{cases}\frac{k\pm\sqrt{k(d+1)(d+2-k)}}{k(d+1-k)}\text{ if $2\leq k\leq d$},\\
1+\frac{2}{d}\text{ if $k=1$},\\
-\frac{d+2}{2(d+1)}\text{ if $k=d+1$}.\end{cases}

We determine when a 2-distance set containing the regular simplex
\mathcal{R}
is a tight
1
-distance set modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d+1}\}
be the
d
-dimensional regular simplex in
H_{d}
, and
x\in T_{d}(k,\beta)
.
Let
X=(1/\sqrt{2})(\mathcal{R}\cup\{x\})
, which is a 2-distance set.
Let
K
be an algebraic number field that contains the distances
D(X)
.
If
K\neq\mathbb{Q}
,
then the following are equivalent.

(1)

There exists a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset A=\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
X
is a tight
1
-distance set modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(2)

k\neq(d+2)/2
or
d\not\equiv 0\pmod{4}
.

Proof.

By Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
, it suffices to determine when the LRS ratio is an algebraic integer. Note that
D(X)=\{1,\beta+1\}
. Then, the LRS ratio is
t=-1/\beta
.
From our assumption
K\neq\mathbb{Q}
, we have
k\neq 1,d+1
.
For
2\leq k\leq d
, we can express

t=-\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{k}{d+2}\pm\frac{\sqrt{k(d+1)(d+2-k)}}{d+2}.

It is well known that for
L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{r})
with a square-free integer
r
, one has

\mathcal{O}_{L}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1+\sqrt{r}}{2}\mathbb{Z}\text{\quad if $r\equiv 1\pmod{4}$},\\
\mathbb{Z}+\sqrt{r}\mathbb{Z}\text{\quad if $r\equiv 2,3\pmod{4}$}.\end{cases}

If
\sqrt{k(d+1)(d+2-k)}
is not an integer and
t\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
then
k/(d+2)\in(1/2)\mathbb{Z}
.
From
2\leq k\leq d
, one has
k=(d+2)/2
and
d
is even. Moreover, we have

\frac{\sqrt{k(d+1)(d+2-k)}}{d+2}=\frac{\sqrt{d+1}}{2}

with
k=(d+2)/2
. Since
d+1
is an odd integer,
d+1
should be congruent to 1 modulo 4 to satisfy
t\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
even if
d+1
is not square-free.
Therefore,
t\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
if and only if
k=(d+2)/2
and
d\equiv 0\pmod{4}
.
From Theorem
3.4
, this theorem follows.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

For
k=1
, it follows that
K=\mathbb{Q}
and the LRS ratio
-1/\beta=-d/(d+2)
is not an integer. Thus, for
k=1
, there exists a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
|D_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)|=1
by Theorem
3.4
. This is the example (
2.1
).
For
k=d+1
, it follows that
K=\mathbb{Q}
and the LRS ratio
-1/\beta=2(d+1)/(d+2)
is not an integer. Thus, for
k=d+1
, there exists a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
|D_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)|=1
by Theorem
3.4
.
This corresponds to the regular simplex and its center.

Remark 3.8
.

A tight 1-distance set
X
modulo
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
presented in Theorem
3.6
can be obtained in the manner described in Theorem
3.2
from the regular simplex and its center.
Indeed, for the coordinates

X=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d+1},(c,\ldots,c,c+\beta,\ldots,c+\beta)\},

one has
D(X)=\{1,\beta+1\}
and hence
\beta\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. From Theorems
2.2
and
2.4
,

\mathfrak{p}
contains
d+2
but does not contain
d+1
. Therefore,

c+\beta\equiv c=\frac{1}{d+1}-\frac{d+1-k}{d+1}\beta\equiv\frac{1}{d+1}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}},

and
(1/(d+1),\ldots,1/(d+1))
corresponds the center of the regular simplex
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d+1}\}
.

Every
(d+2)
-point non-spherical 2-distance set with a non-algebraic integer LRS ratio is a tight modular 1-distance set.
Many tight modular 1-distance sets can easily be derived from the 2-distance sets through Theorem
3.2
.
Our goal is to classify tight modular 1-distance sets while exploring possible frameworks for this classification.

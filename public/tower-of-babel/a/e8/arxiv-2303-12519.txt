More on Modulus Based Iterative Method for Solving Implicit Complementarity Problem
Bharat kumar, Deepmala, A.K. Das

Abstract
This article presents a class of modified new modulus-based iterative methods to process the large and sparse implicit complementarity problem (ICP). By using two positive diagonal matrices, we formulate a fixed-point equation which is equivalent to an ICP and based on a fixed-point equation, an iterative method is presented to solve the ICP. We provide some convergence conditions for the proposed methods when the system matrix is a P P -matrix or an H + H_{+} -matrix.

3
Main results

In this section, we present a class of modified new modulus-based splitting methods for solving ICP
(q,A,\psi)
. First, we construct a new equivalent expression of the ICP
(q,A,\psi)
using the Lemma
2.1
. The details are as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
are two positive diagonal matrices. Then the ICP
(q,A,\psi)
is equivalent to

(\phi_{1}A+\phi_{2})z=\lvert{(\phi_{1}A-\phi_{2})z+\phi_{1}q+\phi_{2}\psi(z)}\rvert-\phi_{1}q+\phi_{2}\psi(z).

(2)

Proof.

The ICP
(q,A,\psi)
can be rewritten as

\phi_{1}(Az+q)\geq 0,~~~~~~\phi_{2}(z-\psi(z))\geq 0,~~~~~~~(\phi_{2}(z-\psi(z))^{T}(\phi_{1}(Az+q))=0.

Let
x=\phi_{1}(Az+q)
and
y=\phi_{2}(z-\psi(z))
. Then by using the Lemma
2.1
, we write

\phi_{1}(Az+q)+\phi_{2}(z-\psi(z))=\lvert\phi_{1}(Az+q)-\phi_{2}(z-\psi(z))\rvert.

This implies that

(\phi_{1}A+\phi_{2})z=\lvert{(\phi_{1}A-\phi_{2})z+\phi_{1}q+\phi_{2}\psi(z)}\rvert-\phi_{1}q+\phi_{2}\psi(z).

∎

According to the statement in the Theorem
3.1
, we are able to find the solution to ICP
(q,A,\psi)
with the use of an implicit fixed-point Equation (
2
). Now we provide an iterative method for solving the ICP
(q,A,\psi)
by using a new matrix splitting of the system matrix
A
. Suppose
A=(M_{1}+\phi)-(N_{1}+\phi)
as a matrix
splitting of
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, where
\phi
is a diagonal matrix of order
n
. Now from Equation (
2
), we obtain

\begin{split}(\phi_{1}M_{1}+\phi_{1}\phi+\phi_{2})z&=(\phi_{1}N_{1}+\phi_{1}\phi)z+\lvert{(\phi_{1}A-\phi_{2})z+\phi_{1}q+\phi_{2}\psi(z)}\rvert\\
&-\phi_{1}q+\phi_{2}\psi(z).\end{split}

(3)

Let
\phi_{1}M_{1}=M_{\phi_{1}},~\phi_{1}N_{1}=N_{\phi_{1}},~\phi_{1}A=A_{\phi_{1}}
and
\phi_{1}\phi=\phi_{\phi_{1}}

\begin{split}(M_{\phi_{1}}+\phi_{\phi_{1}}+\phi_{2})z&=(N_{\phi_{1}}+\phi_{\phi_{1}})z+\lvert(A_{\phi_{1}}-\phi_{2})z+\phi_{1}q+\phi_{2}\psi(z)\rvert\\
&-\phi_{1}q+\phi_{2}\psi(z).\end{split}

(4)

In order to solve the ICP
(q,A,\psi)

Method 3.1

Let

Let residual be the Euclidean norm of the error vector which is defined in

Assessing the QGP speed of sound in ultra-central heavy-ion collisions with ALICE
*

Abstract
Ultrarelativistic heavy-ion collisions produce a state of hot, dense, strongly interacting QCD matter known as quark–gluon plasma (QGP). On an event-by-event basis, the volume of the QGP in ultra-central collisions is mostly constant, while its total entropy can vary significantly, leading to variations in the temperature of the system. Exploiting this unique feature of ultra-central collisions allows us to interpret the correlation between the mean transverse momentum ( ⟨ p T ⟩ ) (\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle) of produced charged hadrons and the number of charged hadrons as a measure of the the speed of sound, c s c_{s} . The speed of sound, c s c_{s} , which relates to the speed at which compression waves travel in a medium (in this case the QGP), is determined by fitting the relative increase of ⟨ p T ⟩ \langle p_{\rm T}\rangle with respect to the relative change of the average charged-particle density ( ⟨ d ​ N ch / d ​ η ⟩ ) (\langle\mathrm{d}N_{\mathrm{ch}}/\mathrm{d}\eta\rangle) measured at midrapidity. This study reports the ⟨ p T ⟩ \langle p_{\rm T}\rangle of charged particles in ultra-central Pb–Pb collisions at a center-of-mass energy of 5.02 TeV per nucleon pair, using the ALICE detector. Different centrality estimators based on charged-particle multiplicity or the transverse energy of the event are employed to select ultra-central collisions. By ensuring a pseudorapidity gap between the region used to define the centrality and the region used for measurement, the influence of biases and their potential effects on the rise of the mean transverse momentum are tested. The measured c s 2 c_{s}^{2} is found to strongly depend on the type of the centrality estimator, ranging from 0.113 ± 0.003 ​ ( stat ) ± 0.007 ​ ( syst ) 0.113\pm 0.003\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm 0.007\,\mathrm{(syst)} to 0.438 ± 0.001 ​ ( stat ) ± 0.019 ​ ( syst ) 0.438\pm 0.001\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm 0.019\,\mathrm{(syst)} in natural units.

Introduction

High-energy heavy ion collisions produce a quark–gluon plasma (QGP)
[
1
]
, a state of matter in which quarks and gluons are deconfined and not bound to hadrons. On an event-by-event basis, the volume of the QGP in ultra-central collisions is mostly constant, while the charged-particle multiplicity
(N_{\mathrm{ch}})
can vary significantly
[
2
,
3
]
. Charged-particle multiplicity variations are interpreted as fluctuations in the entropy, which is generated early in the collision primarily through interactions of the sea gluons of the colliding nuclei. Given the QGP’s fixed volume, the corresponding rise in the entropy density leads to higher temperatures
(T)
, as the entropy density is approximately proportionally to
T^{3}
for the QCD equation of state of high temperature deconfined matter
[
4
]
. The equation of state determines how gradients in the energy density profile give rise to pressure gradients. A fundamental quantity that characterizes the expansion is the speed of sound,
c_{s}
, which is the velocity at which a compression wave travels in a medium. In a relativistic fluid,
c_{s}^{2}=\mathrm{d}\,P/\mathrm{d}\,\epsilon=\mathrm{d\,ln}\,T/\mathrm{d\,ln}\,s
, where
P,\epsilon
, and
s
are the pressure, energy density, and entropy density, respectively. Assuming that the mean transverse momentum (
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
) and the
N_{\mathrm{ch}}
are proportional to
T
and
s
of the QGP
[
5
]
, respectively, the speed of sound can be determined experimentally as,
c_{s}^{2}=\mathrm{d\,ln}\,\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle/\mathrm{d\,ln}\,N_{\mathrm{ch}}

[
6
]
. This manuscript describes the analysis to extract the
c_{s}^{2}
with ALICE
[
7
]
.

2
Data analysis

This study uses data from
Pb–Pb
collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}~=~5.02
TeV collected by the ALICE detector
[
8
]
during the Run 2 data-taking period of the LHC. The relevant subdetectors for this study are the VZERO (V0), the Inner Tracking System (ITS), the Time Projection Chamber (TPC), and the Zero Degree Calorimeters (ZDC). The V0 provides the interaction trigger and is employed for centrality assessment. The ITS and TPC detectors are also employed for centrality classification, using the number of tracklets (
N_{\mathrm{tracklets}}
) and
N_{\mathrm{ch}}
, respectively. A proxy for the event transverse energy (
E_{\mathrm{T}}
) is also used for centrality estimation, quantified as the summed transverse mass
(m_{\mathrm{T}}=\sqrt{p_{\rm T}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}})
, where the pion mass is assumed for all particles. The ZDC is utilized to measure the energy of the spectator nucleons, thereby providing an estimate of the average number of participating nucleons
(\langle N_{\mathrm{part}}\rangle)
. The aim of this analysis is not only to measure the speed of sound (
c_{s}
) but also to investigate dependence of the extracted
c_{s}
on the acceptance, kinematic cuts, and the actual observable used to quantify the event activity for centrality classification
[
9
]
. To this end, different centrality estimators sensitive to the event activity in different regions of the phase–space are defined. Table
1
lists the different estimators along with the kinematic cuts on the particles to estimate centrality, as well as the
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
and
\langle\mathrm{d}N_{\mathrm{ch}}/\mathrm{d}\eta\rangle
. The
N_{\mathrm{tracklets}}
-based centrality estimators include particles with transverse momentum down to
p_{\rm T}\approx 0.03~\mathrm{GeV}/c
and do not impose an upper
p_{\rm T}
cut. In contrast, the
N_{\mathrm{ch}}
-based centrality estimators use particles with
0.15\leq p_{\rm T}<50~\mathrm{GeV}/c
. This also applies to the
E_{\mathrm{T}}
-based centrality estimators. The charged particles reaching the V0 neither have a lower nor an upper
p_{\rm T}
cut.

4
Conclusions

The speed of sound in ultra-central
Pb–Pb
collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}~=~5.02
TeV is extracted through a fit to the correlation between
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle/\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle^{0-5\%}
and
\langle\mathrm{d}N_{\mathrm{ch}}/\mathrm{d}\eta\rangle/\langle\mathrm{d}N_{\mathrm{ch}}/\mathrm{d}\eta\rangle^{0-5\%}
. The rise of the normalized
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
demonstrates a strong dependence on the definition of the centrality estimator. The slope of the
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle/\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle^{0-5\%}
is steeper when using the
E_{\mathrm{T}}
-based centrality estimators compared to the results from the
N_{\mathrm{ch}}
-based centrality estimators. This difference is mainly attributed mainly to the effects of short- and long-range
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
–
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
correlations. These features confirm a prediction from the Trajectum hydrodynamic model
[
9
]
and highlight the need for a reevaluation of how the speed of sound can be extracted from heavy-ion data.

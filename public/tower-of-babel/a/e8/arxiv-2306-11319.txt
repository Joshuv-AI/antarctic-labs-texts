Primordial Density Perturbations from Magnetic Fields
Tal Adi, Hector Afonso G. Cruz, Marc Kamionkowski

Abstract
Perturbations to the cosmic baryon density—and thus to the total-matter density—can be induced by magnetohydronamic forces if there are primordial magnetic fields. The power spectrum for these density perturbations was first provided in 1996, but without much in the way of detail in the derivation, and there has been confusion in the intervening years about this calculation. In this brief note, we re-derive this power spectrum using modern conventions, provide a simplified result, and identify some of the discrepancies in the literature.



I
Introduction

Although no current observations or measurements indicate the existence of magnetic fields in the early Universe, there is an abundance of models for new physics that predict primordial magnetic fields
Widrow 2002
;
Subramanian 2016
. In 1978, Ref.
Wasserman 1978
showed that if there are primordial magnetic fields (PMFs), then magnetohydrodynamic effects induce density perturbations in the cosmic baryon density, and thus in the total-matter density. The power spectrum for these density perturbations, for a given spectrum of magnetic fields, was then calculated in Ref.
Kim et al. 1996
. This result was then reproduced in subsequent papers
Tashiro and Sugiyama 2006a
;
Gopal and Sethi 2003
;
Fedeli and Moscardini 2012
;
Pandey and Sethi 2013
;
Shibusawa et al. 2014
;
Camera et al. 2014
exploring various empirical consequences, and the results also used for numerical work in yet other papers
Pandey et al. 2015
;
Sanati et al. 2020
;
Sethi and Subramanian 2005
;
Pandey and Sethi 2012
;
Varalakshmi and Nigam 2017
;
Cheera and Nigam 2018
.

In this brief note, we re-derive the original result
Kim et al. 1996
for the magnetic-field power spectrum using modern conventions and provide calculational details left out of the original work. We also derive a simpler (and more easily evaluated) expression, and identify errors in some of the intervening literature.

II
Equations of motion

We surmise a primordial magnetic field
{\bf B}({\bf x},t)
, as a function of comoving position
{\bf x}
and time
t
. Prior to recombination, the tight coupling of the baryons to photons, as well as the large photon energy density, suppresses the dynamical effects of the magnetic fields. After recombination, the baryons experience a magnetohydrodynamic force that provides a source to the linearized equation of motion for the fractional total-matter-density perturbation
\delta_{m}({\bf x},t)
. Following Refs.
Wang et al. 2020
;
Kim et al. 1996
;
Adi et al. 2023
, this equation is

\ddot{\delta}_{m}+2H\dot{\delta}_{m}-4\pi G\bar{\rho}_{m}\delta_{m}=f_{b}\frac{v({\bf x})}{\mu_{0}\bar{\rho}_{b,0}\left[a(t)\right]^{3}},

(1)

where
f_{b}=\bar{\rho}_{b}/\bar{\rho}_{m}
is the fraction of the mean total-matter density
\bar{\rho}_{m}
contributed by baryons;
\bar{\rho}_{b,0}
the mean baryon density today;
a(t)
the scale factor (normalized to unity today);
H=\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter; and
t_{0}
the cosmic time today. We recognize this as the usual equation for matter perturbations with a source, with

v({\bf x})\equiv\nabla\cdot\left\{{\bf B}({\bf x})\times\left[\nabla\times{\bf B}({\bf x})\right]\right\},

(2)

where we invoke the shorthand
{\bf B}({\bf x})\equiv{\bf B}({\bf x},t_{0})
, and hereafter take the magnetic field
{\bf B}({\bf x})
and its Fourier
\tilde{\bf B}({\bf k})
without an explicit time argument to be evaluated at
t_{0}
. We note that the scaling
{\bf B}({\bf x},t)={\bf B}({\bf x})/a^{2}(t)
, of the magnetic field with scale factor, as been used in the derivation of Eq. (
1
).
We work in SI units, with
\mu_{0}
the magnetic permeability of the vacuum, and the energy density in the magnetic field is
\rho_{B}={\bf B}^{2}/(2\mu_{0})
.
1
1

1

If Gaussian units are used, then
\mu_{0}\to 4\pi
and
\rho_{B}={\bf B}^{2}/(8\pi)
.

The fractional matter perturbation is then obtained as the special solution of this differential equation as

\delta_{m}({\bf x},t)=M(t)\alpha v({\bf x}).

(3)

where we define
\alpha\equiv f_{b}/\left(\mu_{0}\bar{\rho}_{b,0}\right)
to declutter.
The time dependence
M(t)
satisfies,

\ddot{M}(t)+2H(t)\dot{M}(t)-4\pi G\bar{\rho}_{m}(t)M(t)=\frac{1}{a^{3}(t)},

(4)

with initial conditions
M(t_{r})=\dot{M}(t_{r})=0
imposed at the time
t_{r}
of recombination. Under the approximation that the Universe is fully matter dominated at recombination, this becomes

M(t)\to\frac{9}{10}t_{r}^{2}\frac{D_{+}(t)}{D_{+}(t_{r})},

(5)

at
t\gg t_{r}
, where
D_{+}(t)
is the linear-theory growth function (
\propto t^{2/3}
during matter domination). In practice, though, in current work the equation is solved numerically to take into account the fact that the Universe is not fully matter dominated at
t_{r}
.

III
The matter power spectrum

The matter power spectrum
P(k,t)
is defined as

\left\langle\tilde{\delta}_{m}({\bf k},t)\tilde{\delta}_{m}^{*}({\bf k}^{\prime},t)\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime})P(k,t),

(6)

where
\delta_{D}({\bf k})
is the Dirac delta function,

\tilde{\delta}_{m}({\bf k},t)=\int d^{3}x\,e^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}}\delta_{m}({\bf x},t),

(7)

is the Fourier transform of the density field, and

\delta_{m}({\bf x},t)=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\tilde{\delta}_{m}({\bf k},t)e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}},

(8)

is the inverse Fourier transform.

Given Eq. (
3
), the total matter power spectrum can be written,

P(k,t)=D_{+}^{2}(t)P(k,t_{0})+M^{2}(t)\Pi(k),

(9)

where
M^{2}(t)\Pi(k)
is the magnetic-field-induced matter power spectrum.

IV
The mode-coupling integral

We now calculate
\Pi(k)
assuming some spectrum of PMFs.
We define the power spectrum
P_{B}(k)
of the magnetic field (today) by,

\left\langle{B_{p}({\bf k})B_{p^{\prime}}^{*}({\bf k}^{\prime})}\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime})\delta_{pp^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}P_{B}(k),

(10)

where
B_{p}({\bf k})
are coefficients in the Fourier expansion,

{\bf B}({\bf x})=\sum_{p}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}}B_{p}({\bf k}){\bf{\hat{\epsilon}}}_{p}({\bf k}),

(11)

of the magnetic field today.
The two polarization (unit) vectors
{\bf{\hat{\epsilon}}}_{p}({\bf k})
,
p=\{1,2\}
, are orthogonal to
{\bf k}
and to each other. Given the completeness relation,

\sum_{p}{\bf{\hat{\epsilon}}}_{p,\alpha}({\bf k}){\bf{\hat{\epsilon}}}^{*}_{p,\beta}({\bf k})=\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}}{k^{2}},

(12)

for the polarization vectors (where the subscripts
\alpha,\beta
denote Cartesian components), the Cartesian components
B_{\alpha}({\bf k})
of the magnetic field satisfy,

\left\langle{B_{\alpha}({\bf k})B_{\beta}^{*}({\bf k}^{\prime})}\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime})\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}}{k^{2}}\right)P_{B}(k),

(13)

as in prior literature.

From Eq. (
3
), it follows that

\Pi(k)=\alpha^{2}P_{v}(k),

(14)

where
P_{v}(k)
is the power spectrum for
v({\bf x})
,
defined by

\left\langle\tilde{v}({\bf k})\tilde{v}^{*}({\bf k}^{\prime})\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime})P_{v}(k),

(15)

{\bf k}_{2}

\Pi(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha k}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}k_{1}^{2}\,dk_{1}\int_{-1}^{1}d\mu\,

\displaystyle\times P_{B}(k_{1})P_{B}\left(\sqrt{k^{2}+k_{1}^{2}-2kk_{1}\mu}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[k^{2}+(k^{2}-2kk_{1}\mu)\mu^{2}\right],

in the integrand in Eq. (

\displaystyle\Pi(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\right)^{2}k^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}k_{1}^{3}\,dk_{1}\int_{-1}^{1}d\mu\,

(22)

\displaystyle\times\frac{P_{B}(k_{1})P_{B}\left(\sqrt{k^{2}+k_{1}^{2}-2kk_{1}\mu}\right)}{k^{2}+k_{1}^{2}-2kk_{1}}

), avoids a quantity that goes to zero in the denominator of the integrand and is thus a bit more easily evaluated numerically.

V
Discussion

Our analytic result for the matter power spectrum is smaller by a factor of
(4\pi)^{2}
than the analytic expressions in several prior papers
Gopal and Sethi 2003
;
Tashiro and Sugiyama 2006a
;
Tashiro and Sugiyama 2006b
;
Fedeli and Moscardini 2012
(and an earlier version of Ref.
Adi et al. 2023
), as we visualize in Fig.
3
. We have not been able to trace the origin of these discrepancies, but in at least one case
Gopal and Sethi 2003
we believe it is due partially to inconsistencies in Fourier conventions. We have also checked that the errors are propagated in the numerical work and also in other papers
Pandey and Sethi 2013
;
Pandey et al. 2015
;
Sanati et al. 2020
;
Sethi and Subramanian 2005
;
Pandey and Sethi 2012
that use the results.

We advocate using Eq. (
21
) in future work on density perturbations from PMFs.

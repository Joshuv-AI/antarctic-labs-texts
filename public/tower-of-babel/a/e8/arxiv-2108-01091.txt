Coherence properties of a spin in a squeezed resonator
Inbar Shani, Emanuele G. Dalla Torre, Michael Stern

Abstract
A promising venue for hybrid quantum computation involves the strong coupling between impurity spins and superconducting resonators. One strategy to control and enhance this coupling is to prepare the resonator in a non-classical state, such as a squeezed state. In this work, we theoretically study the effects of these states on the coherence properties of the spin. We develop an analytic approach based on the Schrieffer-Wolf transformation that allows us to quantitatively predict the coupling and the dephasing rate of the spin, and we numerically confirm its validity. We find that squeezing can enhance the coupling between the resonator and the spin. However, at the same time, it amplifies the photon noise and enhances the spin decoherence. Our work demonstrates a major impediment in using squeezing to reach the strong-coupling limit.



I
Introduction

Impurity spins in semiconductors are quantum entities with a long coherence time, which enables them to store safely quantum information
Muhonen et al. 2014
. Unfortunately, the weakness of their interaction
with the environment hinders our ability to control them directly. An appealing road towards a spin-based quantum processor consists of combining the impurity spins with superconducting circuits
Kurizki et al. 2015
;
Clerk et al. 2020
.
To realize this kind of hybrid system, one needs to reach the strong coupling regime, where the coupling between the spins and the superconducting circuit is much larger than the decoherence rates. In recent years, such a regime was reached for large ensemble of spins
Kubo et al. 2010
;
Bienfait et al. 2016
, and for spin-like macroscopic structures
Viennot et al. 2015
;
Borjans et al. 2020
. Yet, the coupling constant of a single microscopic spin with a superconducting resonator is extremely small (of the order of a few kHz
Haikka et al. 2017
), and reaching the strong coupling, in these conditions, remains a great challenge
Lee 2019
. Recently, Ref.
Leroux et al. 2018
suggested to increase artificially the coupling by squeezing the resonator. In a squeezed state, the fluctuations of the electromagnetic field in a given quadrature can be controlled and made arbitrarily large
Caves 1981
;
Abadie et al. 2011
;
Aasi et al. 2013
. Thus, increasing the coupling by squeezing seems interesting and even promising. On the other hand, the large number of photons that characterizes a squeezed state leads to large fluctuations, and may compromise the coherence of the spin. In this work, we study the interplay between these two effects and show that the beneficial effects of squeezing are strongly suppressed by the noise in the photon number.

II
Physical model

We consider the quantum circuit illustrated in

In order to characterize the effective coupling between the spin and the squeezed resonator, we diagonalize the latter using a Bogoliubov transformation. To perform this task, we introduce the canonical operators
\gamma

\gamma^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\gamma=a\cosh(r)-a^{\dagger}\sinh(r)\\

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}\gamma=\cosh(2r)a^{\dagger}a-\tfrac{1}{2}\sinh(2r)\left(a^{2}+{a^{\dagger}}^{2}\right).

(8)

The Hamiltonian (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{H}/\hbar=&\Omega_{r}\gamma^{\dagger}\gamma+\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\omega}_{s}\sigma_{z}+\tfrac{1}{2}ge^{r}(\gamma^{\dagger}+\gamma)(\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-})\\
&-\tfrac{1}{2}ge^{-r}(\gamma^{\dagger}-\gamma)(\sigma_{+}-\sigma_{-}).\end{split}

from

Leroux et al. 2018

To study the interplay between squeezing and decoherence, we consider the combined action of the Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}
and the decay of photons from the resonator, described by the quantum master equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rho=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\tilde{H},\rho\right]+L\rho L^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(L^{\dagger}L\rho+\rho L^{\dagger}L\right),

(10)

where

is the density matrix and

is a Lindblad superoperator. The operator
L
originates from the coupling between the superconducting resonator and the external environment, and drives the resonator to its vacuum (zero photons) state.

IV
Schrieffer-Wolff Transformation for a Squeezed resonator

Motivated by the numerical results of the previous section, we now introduce a systematic approach to describe the effects of the squeezed resonator on the spin. Our approach generalizes the Schrieffer-Wolf (SW) transformation
Schrieffer and Wolff 1966
to a squeezed resonator and allow us to compute the energy shift and the decoherence of the spin analytically. As a first step, we write the Hamiltonian (
6
) as
\tilde{H}=\tilde{H}_{0}+V
, with

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{0}/\hbar

\displaystyle=\tilde{\omega}_{r}a^{\dagger}a+\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\omega}_{s}\sigma_{z}-\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda\left(a^{2}+{a^{\dagger}}^{2}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle V/\hbar

\displaystyle=g(a^{\dagger}\sigma_{-}+a\sigma_{+}).

(14)

Next, we apply the unitary transformation
U=e^{S}
, where
S
is the anti-Hermitian operator

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=C_{1}(a\sigma_{+}-a^{\dagger}\sigma_{-})+C_{2}(a\sigma_{-}-a^{\dagger}\sigma_{+}),\penalty\ {\rm with}\penalty\

(15)

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{\Delta}\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{s}+\tilde{\omega}_{r}}{\tilde{\omega}_{s}+\Omega_{r}},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ C_{2}=\frac{g}{\Delta}\frac{\lambda}{\tilde{\omega}_{s}+\Omega_{r}}

(16)

and
\Delta=\tilde{\omega}_{s}-\Omega_{r}
. This transformation satisfies the condition
[\tilde{H}_{0},S]=V
and leads to a transformed Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}_{\rm SW}=e^{S}\tilde{H}e^{-S}
that does not include linear terms in
V
. By expanding this expression to second order in
V
, we obtain
\tilde{H}_{\rm SW}/\hbar=\tilde{H}_{0}/\hbar+\delta\tilde{\omega}_{s}\sigma_{z}/2
, with a dispersive shift

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{\omega}_{s}

\displaystyle=\chi_{r}(2\tilde{n}+1),

(17)

where we defined
\chi_{r}={g}^{2}/\Delta
, and

\displaystyle\tilde{n}+\tfrac{1}{2}=\frac{\left(\tilde{\omega}_{s}+\tilde{\omega}_{r}\right)\left(2a^{\dagger}a+1\right)-\lambda\left(a^{2}+{a^{\dagger}}^{2}\right)}{2(\tilde{\omega}_{s}+\Omega_{r})}

(18)

For
\lambda=0
,
Eqs.

17
and
18
recover the well known Lamb shift of a spin coupled to a resonator

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{\omega}_{s}=

\displaystyle\chi_{0}\left(2a^{\dagger}a+1\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ {\rm and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \chi_{0}=\frac{g^{2}}{\tilde{\omega}_{s}-\tilde{\omega}_{r}}.

(19)

The SW transformation creates an effective channel of dissipation for the spin, in addition to its intrinsic dissipation. In order to consider this effect, one needs to transform the dissipation operator
L
using the SW transformation. Up to second order in
V
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tilde{L}_{\rm SW}=e^{S}Le^{-S}=L+\left[S,L\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\left[S,\left[S,L\right]\right]+...\end{split}

(20)

Using
Eq.

15
, we obtain

\displaystyle L_{\rm SW}=\sqrt{\kappa}a+\sqrt{\kappa}C_{1}\sigma_{-}+\sqrt{\kappa}C_{2}\sigma_{+}+{\rm O}\left(\frac{g^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}\right)

(21)

After the transformation, the resonator and the spin are decoupled, such that the coherence of the spin is entirely dictated by the second and third terms of
Eq.

21
. If we consider these two terms as independent channels, we obtain an effective decoherence rate

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Gamma_{\rm Purcell}&=\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}=\kappa\left(C_{1}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(22)

In the limit of
\lambda=0
, this expression reproduces the well-known Purcell decay,
\Gamma_{\rm Purcell}=g^{2}\kappa/\Delta^{2}

Purcell 1995
;
Bienfait et al. 2016
.

V
Photon noise

In addition to the Purcell effect, the squeezing of the resonator also affects the pure dephasing rate of the spin. Stochastic fluctuations in the number of photons in the resonator create a random dispersive shift which translates into a dephasing of the spin. The rate of this process can be determined by the decay of the expectation value
\langle\sigma_{x}(t)\rangle
in a system initialized in the
|+\rangle=\left(|0\rangle+|1\rangle\right)/\sqrt{2}
state
Ithier et al. 2005
,

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x}(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{i\int_{0}^{t}\delta\tilde{\omega}_{s}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}+e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}\delta\tilde{\omega}_{s}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\right).

(23)

Assuming that
\tilde{n}(t)=\tilde{n}_{0}+\delta\tilde{n}{(t)}
, where the fluctuation
\delta\tilde{n}(t)
is a random variable with zero average, one can rewrite the exponent as

\displaystyle e^{\pm i\int_{0}^{t}\delta\tilde{\omega}_{s}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}=e^{\pm i\chi_{r}\left(2{\tilde{n}_{0}}+1\right)t}e^{\pm i2\chi_{r}\int_{0}^{t}\delta\tilde{n}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}.

(24)

By expanding the exponent to a Taylor series, one obtains

\displaystyle\langle e^{\pm i\chi_{r}\int_{0}^{t}\delta\tilde{n}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle\approx 1-2\chi_{r}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime\prime}\penalty\ \langle\delta\tilde{n}(t^{\prime})\delta\tilde{n}(t^{\prime\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle\approx 1-2\chi_{r}^{2}t\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle\delta\tilde{n}(\tau)\delta\tilde{n}(0)\rangle_{\infty}d\tau.

(25)

Here, the last identity is valid in the limit of
t\to\infty
, under the assumption that the resonator is found in a steady state, where two-time correlations depend on the time difference only. In the case of Gaussian fluctuations, the higher order terms can be re-summed exactly leading to

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x}(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle\cos\left[\chi_{r}\left({2\tilde{n}_{0}}+1\right)t\right]e^{-\Gamma_{\phi}^{\rm photon}t},

(26)

where we defined

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi}^{\rm photon}=2\chi_{r}^{2}\tilde{\eta},\penalty\ {\rm and}\penalty\ \tilde{\eta}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle\delta\tilde{n}(\tau)\delta\tilde{n}(0)\rangle d\tau.

(27)

In
Appendix

D
, we compute the correlations
\langle\delta n(\tau)\delta n(0)\rangle
for a thermal state and for a coherent state and reproduce known results for the photon noise dephasing of the spin.

VI
Steady state correlations of a squeezed resonator

In the previous sections we expressed the dispersive shift and the decoherence rates of the spin in terms of physical observables of the resonator, see
Eqs.

17
,
22
and
27
. In the following, we compute these quantities in the steady state of a squeezed resonator described by the Hamiltonian (
13
) (with
g=0
). According to the Lindblad master equation (
10

is determined by the differential equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left<O(t)\right>&=\frac{i}{\hbar}\left<\left[H,O(t)\right]\right>+\left<L^{\dagger}O(t)L\right>\\
&-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\left<L^{\dagger}LO(t)\right>+\left<O(t)L^{\dagger}L\right>\right).\end{split}

(28)

This equation can be explicitly solved if one finds a set of variables
\vec{O}=(O_{1},O_{2},...)
whose expectation values satisfy the closed-form recursive relation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left<O_{i}(t)\right>=\sum_{j}G_{i,j}\left<O_{j}(t)\right>,

(29)

where
G_{i,j}
is a time independent scalar. For a squeezed resonator, this condition is satisfied by the operator
\vec{O}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\dagger}a,\ a^{2},\ {a^{\dagger}}^{2},\ \mathds{1}\end{pmatrix}^{T}
with

\displaystyle G=\left(\begin{matrix}-\kappa&-i\lambda&i\lambda&0\\
i2\lambda&-i2\tilde{\omega}_{r}-\kappa&0&i\lambda\\
-i2\lambda&0&i2\tilde{\omega}_{r}-\kappa&-i\lambda\\
0&0&0&0\end{matrix}\right).

(30)

The steady-state expectation values of
\vec{O}
can be found by equating the left-hand side of
Eq.

29

\sum_{j}G_{i,j}\left<O_{j}(t)\right>_{\infty}=0

\displaystyle\langle a^{\dagger}a\rangle_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{4\Omega_{r}^{2}+\kappa^{2}}

(31)

\displaystyle\langle a^{2}\rangle_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda(2\tilde{\omega}_{r}+i\kappa)}{4\Omega_{r}^{2}+\kappa^{2}}

(32)

\displaystyle\langle{a^{\dagger}}^{2}\rangle_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda(2\tilde{\omega}_{r}-i\kappa)}{4\Omega_{r}^{2}+\kappa^{2}}.

(33)

Plugging these results in

VIII
Discussion and Conclusion

In conclusion, we studied the coherence properties of a spin embedded in a resonator under parametric drive. We found out that such a drive gives rise to an enhanced effective coupling between the spin and the resonator but also increases relaxation and decoherence rates of both systems. These non-unitary effects are related to the relatively large number of photons in the squeezed resonator. In particular, the number of photons in a squeezed resonator modifies the relaxation rate of the spin. This effect limits the relative enhancement of the coupling by squeezing to approximately
20\%
. This situation is in contrast to what usually occurs for coherent or thermal states, where the relaxation of the spin via the resonator - the so-called Purcell rate - is fixed by the intrinsic properties of the system and is independent of the photon occupation. Here, the squeezing terms change both the coupling between the two systems and their loss rates. The theoretical methods developed in this work enabled us to consider these two effects on equal footing and could be extended further to more ‘exotic’ quantum states like cat states
Vlastakis et al. 2013
or Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) states
Gottesman et al. 2001
;
Campagne-Ibarcq et al. 2020
. Our analytical approach based on generalized Schrieffer Wolf transformations and quantum regression theorem are valid for squeezed cavities on a large parameter scale and were found to be in quantitative agreement with the numerical calculations.

Appendix C
Quantum Regression Theorem

In this appendix we offer a simple derivation of the quantum regression theorem for Markovian master equations of the form

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rho=\mathcal{L}\rho,

(39)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the Liouvillian superoperator and
\rho
is the density matrix. For time-independent
\mathcal{L}
, the time evolution of
\rho
is given by

\displaystyle\rho(t+\tau)=e^{\mathcal{L}\tau}\rho(t).

(40)

The expectation value of an operator
A
is defined as

\displaystyle\langle A(t)\rangle={\rm Tr}\left[A\rho(t)\right].

(41)

Its time derivative is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle A(t)\rangle=\frac{d}{dt}{\rm Tr}\left[A\rho(t)\right]={\rm Tr}\left[A\frac{d}{dt}\rho(t)\right].

(42)

Plugging-in
Eq.

39
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle A(t)\rangle={\rm Tr}\left[A\mathcal{L}\rho(t)\right].

(43)

The quantum regression theorem allows one to compute the two-time correlation function

\displaystyle\langle A(t+\tau)O(t)\rangle={\rm Tr}\left[Ae^{\mathcal{L}\tau}O\rho(t)\right]

(44)

for systems where the time derivative of
d\langle A(t)\rangle/dt
has a linear dependence on the system’s operators
B_{j}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle A(t)\rangle=\sum_{j}G_{j}\langle B_{j}(t)\rangle.

(45)

Because the trace of sum of matrices is the sum of their traces, we can rewrite
Eq.

45
as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle A(t)\rangle=\sum_{j}G_{j}{\rm Tr}\left[B_{j}\rho(t)\right]=\sum_{j}{\rm Tr}\left[G_{j}B_{j}\rho(t)\right]

(46)

Because Eq. (
46
) and Eq. (
43
) are satisfied for any
\rho(t)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle A\mathcal{L}=\sum_{j}G_{j}B_{j}

(47)

This result allows us to compute the time derivative of
Eq.

44
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\langle A(t+\tau)O(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\tau}{\rm Tr}\left[Ae^{\mathcal{L}\tau}O\rho(t)\right]

(48)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left[A\mathcal{L}e^{\mathcal{L}\tau}O\rho(t)\right].

(49)

Using
Eq.

47
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\langle A(t+\tau)O(t)\rangle

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left[\sum_{j}G_{j}B_{j}e^{\mathcal{L}\tau}O\rho(t)\right]

(50)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}G_{j}{\rm Tr}\left[B_{j}e^{\mathcal{L}\tau}O\rho(t)\right]

(51)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}G_{j}\langle B_{j}(t+\tau)O(t)\rangle

(52)

Eq. (
52
) is the quantum regression theorem used in Eq. (
35
).

Lusztig correspondence and the finite Gan-Gross-Prasad problem
Zhicheng Wang

Abstract
. The Gan-Gross-Prasad problem is to describe the restriction of representations of a classical group G G to smaller groups H H of the same kind. In this paper, we solved the Gan-Gross-Prasad problem over finite fields completely. In previous work [ LW1 , LW2 , LW3 , Wang ] , we study the Gan-Gross-Prasad problem for unipotent representations of finite classical groups. The main tools used are the Lusztig correspondence as well as a formula of Reeder [ R ] for the pairings of Deligne-Lusztig characters. We give a reduction decomposition of Reeder’s formula, and deduce the Gan-Gross-Prasad problem for arbitrary representations from the unipotent representations by Lusztig correspondence.

1.
Introduction

In
[
GP1
]
, Gross and Prasad formulated conjectures which relate the periods of automorphic forms on
\mathrm{SO}_{n+1}\times\mathrm{SO}_{n}
along the diagonal subgroup
\mathrm{SO}_{n}
with central
L
-values, where
\mathrm{SO}_{n+1}
and
\mathrm{SO}_{n}
are special orthogonal groups over a local field. In
[
GGP1
]
, Gan, Gross, and Prasad extended these local conjectures to all classical groups. The local Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture has been resolved for orthogonal groups by J.-L. Waldspurger and C. Mœglin
[
Wal2
,
Wal3
,
Wal4
,
MW
]
, for unitary groups by R. Beuzart-Plessis
[
BP1
,
BP2
]
and W. T. Gan and A. Ichino
[
GI
]
, and for symplectic-metaplectic groups by H. Atobe
[
Ato
]
.

The main goal of this paper is to consider the Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture over finite fields. Partial results can be proved via known results for local fields
[
GGP2
, section 4,5]
. However, that way does not allow one to get the complete answer to the finite Gan-Gross-Prasad problem. In previous works
[
LW1
,
LW3
]
, we studied the Gan-Gross-Prasad problem of unipotent representations of finite unitary groups and in
[
LW2
,
Wang
]
for finite orthogonal groups and finite symplectic groups. This paper will give a formula reducing the Gan-Gross-Prasad problem of arbitrary representations to unipotent representations.

There are two types of periods in the Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture: the Bessel periods and the Fourier-Jacobi periods. One can deduce the Fourier-Jacobi case from the Bessel case by the standard arguments of theta correspondence and see-saw dual pairs, which are used in the proof of the local Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture (see
[
GI
,
Ato
]
). Theta correspondence over finite fields is described explicitly in
[
AMR
,
P1
,
P2
]
. So in this paper, we only focus on the Bessel case.

1.1.
Representation of finite reductive groups

Let
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be an algebraic closure of a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is of characteristic
p>2
. Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with Frobenius map
F
, and let
Z
be the center of
G^{F}
. We assume that
q
is large enough such that the main theorem in
[
Sr2
]
holds, namely we assume that for every
F
-stable maximal torus
T
of
G
,
T^{F}/Z
has at least two Weyl group orbits of regular characters.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
, and
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{F})
and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Irr}(H^{F})
. We write

\langle\pi,\sigma\rangle_{H^{F}}=\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{H^{F}}(\pi,\sigma).

Let
P
be an F-stable parabolic subgroup of
G
with Levi factor
L
. Let
\sigma\in\mathrm{Irr}(L^{F})
. We write the parabolic induction

I^{G}_{P}(\sigma):=\mathrm{Ind}^{G^{F}}_{P^{F}}(\sigma).

For an
F
-stable maximal torus
T
of
G
and a character
\theta
of
T^{F}
, let
R_{T,\theta}^{G}
be the virtual character of
G^{F}
defined by Deligne and Lusztig in
[
DL
]
. We say a complex irreducible representation is uniform if its character is a linear combination of the Deligne-Lusztig characters. Let
G^{*}
be the dual group of
G
. For simplicity, we still denote the Frobenius endomorphism of
G^{*}
by
F
. Then there is a natural bijection between the set of
G^{F}
-conjugacy classes of
(T,\theta)
and the set of
G^{*F}
-conjugacy classes of
(T^{*},s)
where
T^{*}
is a
F
-stable maximal torus in
G^{*}
and
s\in T^{*F}
. If
(T,\theta)
corresponds to
(T^{*},s)
, we denote
R_{T,\theta}^{G}
by
R_{T^{*},s}^{G}
.

From now on, we will consider the following three cases: general linear groups, unitary groups, and special orthogonal groups. Let
a\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
and

[a]:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\{a^{{(-q)}^{k}}|k\in\mathbb{Z}\}&\textrm{ if }G\textrm{ is a unitary group};\\
&\{a^{{q}^{k}}|k\in\mathbb{Z}\}&\textrm{ otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

For a pair
(T^{*},s)
corresponding to the pair
(T,\theta)
, assume that
s
has eigenvalues
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
. If
[a]\nsubseteq\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}
, then we say
[a]\notin s
or
[a]\notin\theta
. If there exists an
[a]
such that
x_{i}\in[a]
for each
i
, then we say
s\sim[a]
or
\theta\sim[a]
. For abbreviation, we write
\pm 1\notin s
,
s\sim\pm 1
and
\theta\sim\pm 1
instead of
[\pm 1]\notin s
,
s\sim[\pm 1]
and
\theta\sim[\pm 1]
, respectively. Here
-1
is the unique element in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
(-1)^{2}=1
and
-1\neq 1
.

For a semisimple element
s\in G^{*F}
, define the Lusztig series as follows:

\mathcal{E}(G^{F},s)=\{\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{F}):\langle\pi,R_{T^{*},s}^{G}\rangle\neq 0\textrm{ for some }T^{*}\textrm{ containing }s\}.

And it is known that
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{F})
is partitioned into Lusztig series

\mathrm{Irr}(G^{F})=\coprod_{(s)}\mathcal{E}(G^{F},s),

where
(s)
runs over the conjugacy classes of semisimple elements. Moreover, there is a bijection

\mathcal{L}_{s}:\mathcal{E}(G^{F},s)\to\mathcal{E}(C_{G^{*F}}(s),1).

Note that usually, the correspondence
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is not uniquely determined. For unitary groups and general linear groups, every representation is uniform. For special orthogonal groups, in
[
Wang
, Theorem 1.4]
, we reduce the Gan-Gross-Prasad problem of arbitrary representations to the representation
\pi\in\mathcal{E}(G^{F},s)
with
\pm 1\notin s
. Note that in the special orthogonal groups case,
\pm 1\notin s
implies that
\pi
is uniform. So we only consider the Lusztig correspondence for the uniform case, and
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is uniquely determined by (
4.1
).

It is well known that the centralizer
C_{G^{*}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(s)
is a product of classical groups. We adopt the notations of
[
AMR
, subsection 1.B]
. Let
T^{*}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\cong\overline{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}_{q}\times\cdots\times\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
be a rational maximal torus of
G^{*}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, and
s=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{l})\in T^{*F}
. For
a\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
, let
\nu_{a}(s):=\#\{i|x_{i}=a\}
. The group
C_{G^{*}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(s)
has a natural decomposition:

C_{G^{*}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(s)=\prod_{[a]\subset\{x_{i}\}}G^{*}_{[a]}(s)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})

where
G^{*}_{[a]}(s)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is a reductive quasi-simple group of rank equal to
\#[a]\cdot\nu_{a}(s)
. Now we have the following situations:

•

If
a=\pm 1
and
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
,
\mathrm{U}_{n}
,
\mathrm{SO}^{\epsilon}_{2n}
or
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}
, then
G_{[a]}(s)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is
\mathrm{GL}_{m}
,
\mathrm{U}_{m}
,
\mathrm{SO}^{\epsilon}_{2m}
or
\mathrm{Sp}_{2m}
, respectively, for some
m\leq n
;

•

If
a\neq\pm 1
and
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, then
G_{[a]}(s)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is a general linear group;

•

If
a\neq\pm 1
and
G\neq\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, then
G_{[a]}(s)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is either a general linear group or a unitary group.

For
[a]\nsubseteq\{x_{i}\}
, we set
G_{[a]}(s):=1
. Then we have

C_{G^{*F}}(s)=\prod_{[a]}G^{*F}_{[a]}(s).

Hence, we can rewrite the Lusztig correspondence as follows:

(1.1)

\cal{L}_{s}(\pi)=\prod_{[a]}\pi_{[a]}

where
\pi_{[a]}
is a unipotent representation of
G^{*F}_{[a]}(s)
for each
[a]
.

1.2.
The main result

Let
V_{n}
be an
n
-dimensional space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with a nondegenerate symmetric (resp. Hermitian) bilinear form
(,)
, which defines the special orthogonal group
\mathrm{SO}(V_{n})
(resp. unitary group
\mathrm{U}_{n}
), and let
V_{n-m}\subset V_{n}
. Assume that

is odd if
(,)
is symmetric. Let
\pi
and
\sigma
be complex irreducible representations of
\mathrm{SO}(V_{n})
and
\mathrm{SO}(V_{n-m})
(resp.
\mathrm{U}(V_{n})
and
\mathrm{U}(V_{n-m})
), respectively.
The Gan-Gross-Prasad problem is concerned with the multiplicity

(1.2)

m(\pi,\sigma):=\langle\pi\otimes\bar{\nu},\sigma\rangle_{H^{F}}=\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{H^{F}}(\pi\otimes\bar{\nu},\sigma)

where the data
(H,\nu)
is defined as (1.2) in
[
LW1
]
and (1.2) in
[
LW2
]
.
According to the parity of

, the above Hom space is called the Bessel model or the Fourier-Jacobi model. We call the above two models the basic cases if
n-m\leq 1
.

Now we focus on the Bessel case. For unitary groups,
\pi
and
\sigma
are always uniform. For special orthogonal groups, in
[
Wang
, Theorem 1.4]
, we reduce the finite Gan-Gross-Prasad problem of arbitrary representations to the case

and
\sigma
in the Lusztig series associated with the semisimple elements

and

such that
\pm 1\notin s
and
\pm 1\notin s^{\prime}
, respectively. So the finite Gan-Gross-Prasad problem is solved if one can solve the above case. Note that in the special orthogonal groups case,
\pm 1\notin s
and
\pm 1\notin s^{\prime}
imply
\pi
and
\sigma
are uniform.
We can calculate the multiplicity by Reeder’s formula (
[
R
,
LW1
]
). This formula in
[
R
]
is used to calculate
\langle R^{G}_{T,\chi},R^{H}_{S,\eta}\rangle_{H^{F}}
for some groups

and

with regular characters

and

, and verify some conjectures in

]
for depth-zero supercuspidal representations of special orthogonal groups over a local field. In general, explicit calculation with Reeder’s formula is still quite involved. The first main theorem of this paper is the following reduction decomposition of Reeder’s formula:

.

2.1.
Partitions

Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\cdots,\mu_{k})
be a partition of
n
, and
|\mu|:=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}
. For simplicity of notations, we write
\mu=(a_{1}^{k_{1}},\cdots,a_{l}^{k_{l}})
where
a_{j}
runs over
\{\mu_{i}\}
and
k_{j}=\#\{i|\mu_{i}=a_{j}\}
.

If there exists an
h\in\mathbb{N}
such that
h|\mu_{i}
for each
i
, then we say
h|\mu
and set
\frac{\mu}{h}:=(\frac{\mu_{1}}{h},\cdots,\frac{\mu_{k}}{h})
. If
b\in\{\mu_{i}\}
, then we say
b\in\mu
. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots\lambda_{n})
be a partition of
m
with
m\leq n
. We say
\lambda\subset\mu
if
\{\lambda_{i}\}\subset\{\mu_{i}\}
. Here
\{\lambda_{i}\}\subset\{\mu_{i}\}
means the containment of multisets of integers.
Let
(\lambda,\mu)
be a pair of partitions. We say
(\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})\subset(\mu,\lambda)
if
\mu^{\prime}\subset\mu
and
\lambda^{\prime}\subset\lambda
.

For two partitions
\mu^{\prime}\subset\mu
, we write

(2.1)

\mu=[n_{1}^{a_{1}},\ldots,n_{l}^{a_{l}}]\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mu^{\prime}=[n_{1}^{b_{1}},\ldots,n_{l}^{b_{l}}],

where
n_{i}
’s are distinct so that
0\leq b_{i}\leq a_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,l
. We set

(2.2)

C_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}=\prod_{i=1}^{l}{a_{i}\choose b_{i}}.

For a finite set of partitions
\{\mu^{i}=(\mu^{i}_{1},\cdots,\mu^{i}_{k_{1}})\}
with
i=1,\cdots l
, let
\mu
be a partition such that
\{\mu_{i}\}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{l}\{\mu_{i}^{j},\cdots,\mu^{j}_{k_{j}}\}
. Here
\{\mu_{i}\}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{l}\{\mu_{i}^{j},\cdots,\mu^{j}_{k_{j}}\}
means the equivalence of multisets of integers. We say
\mu=\bigcup_{i}\mu^{i}
or
\mu=\mu^{1}\bigcup\mu^{2}\bigcup\cdots\bigcup\mu^{l}
.

Clearly, if

a^{\prime}\in\{x_{i}\}
and
\nu_{a^{\prime}}(s)=\nu_{a}(s)
.
The group
C_{G(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(s)
has a natural decomposition with the eigenvalues of
s
:

C_{G(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(s)=\prod_{[a]\subset\{x_{i}\}}G_{[a]}(s)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})

where
G_{[a]}(s)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is a reductive quasi-simple group of rank equal to
\#[a]\cdot\nu_{a}(s)
. Now we have the
following situations:

•

If
a=\pm 1
and
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
,
\mathrm{U}_{n}
,
\mathrm{SO}^{\epsilon}_{2n}
or
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}
, then
G_{[a]}(s)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is
\mathrm{GL}_{m}
,
\mathrm{U}_{m}
,
\mathrm{SO}^{\epsilon}_{2m}
or
\mathrm{Sp}_{2m}
, respectively, for some
m\leq n
.

•

If
a\neq\pm 1
and
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, then
G_{[a]}(s)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is a general linear group.

•

If
a\neq\pm 1
and
G\neq\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, then
G_{[a]}(s)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is a general linear group or unitary group.

For
[a]\nsubseteq\{x_{i}\}
, we set
G_{[a]}(s):=1
. Then we rewrite

C_{G^{F}}(s)=\prod_{[a]}G^{F}_{[a]}(s).

3.
Remarks on maximal tori

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
F
be a Frobenius morphism of
G
. Fix an
F
-stable maximal torus
T_{0}
in
G
which is contained in an
F
-stable Borel subgroup of
G
, and let
W=W_{G}(T_{0})=N_{G}(T)/T
be the Weyl group of
G
.

For any
F
-stable maximal torus
T
, there is
g\in G
such that
{}^{g}T=T_{0}
. Since
T
is
F
-stable, we have
gF(g^{-1})\in N_{G}(T_{0})
. If
w
is the image of
gF(g^{-1})
in
W
, then we denote
T
by
T_{w}
. Hence there is a bijection between the set of
G^{F}
-conjugacy classes of
F
-stable maximal tori of
G
and the set of
F
-conjugacy classes of
W
(
[
C
, Proposition 3.3.3]
). It is known that
T_{0}^{wF}:=\{t\in T_{0}:F(t)={}^{w^{-1}}t\}
is isomorphic to
T_{w}^{F}
(
[
C
, the proof of Proposition 3.3.6]
), and
W_{G}(T_{w})^{F}\cong C_{W,F}(w)
where
C_{W,F}(w):=\{w^{\prime}\in W|w^{\prime-1}wF(w^{\prime})=w\}
.

3.1.
The general linear groups

We first consider
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
. In this case,
W\cong\mathbb{S}_{n}
, where the symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{n}
acts on the set
\{1,\cdots,n\}
. So the conjugacy classes of
W
are parameterized by partitions
\mu=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{k})
of
n
. Since the action of the Frobenius morphism
F
on the Weyl group
W
is trivial, the
F
-conjugacy classes of
W
are also parameterized by partitions. We choose a canonical representative
w_{\mu}
of the conjugacy class corresponding to
\mu
such that

w_{\mu}=(1,2,\cdots,\mu_{1})(\mu_{1}+1,\cdots,\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})\cdots(\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k-1}+1,\cdots,\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k}).

The torus
T_{0}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
can be chosen to be the group of diagonal matrices. So for each
t_{0}\in T_{0}
, we write
t_{0}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
, where
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
. The Weyl group
W
acts on
T_{0}
by permuting
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
in a similar way, that is,

w_{\mu}\cdot t_{0}=(x_{\mu_{1}},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\cdots,x_{\mu_{1}-1},x_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}},x_{\mu_{1}+1},x_{\mu_{1}+2},x_{\mu_{1}+3},\cdots,x_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-1},x_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}}\cdots).

For a partition
\mu=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{k})=(n_{1}^{k_{1}},n_{2}^{k_{2}},\cdots,n_{r}^{k_{r}})
, we write
w_{\mu}
as a product of cycles:

w_{\mu}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k_{i}}c_{n_{i},\ell}.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{S}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{S}_{k_{r}}\to\mathbb{S}_{n}
such that for any
\tau=\tau_{1}\times\cdots\times\tau_{r}\in\mathbb{S}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{S}_{k_{r}}
, the permutation
\psi(\tau)
sends
c_{n_{i},\ell}
to
c_{n_{i},\tau_{i}(\ell)}
for each index
1\leq i\leq r
and
1\leq\ell\leq k_{i}
. It is well known that

(3.1)

C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})=\left\langle\psi(\mathbb{S}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{S}_{k_{r}}),\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k_{i}}\langle c_{i,\ell}\rangle\right\rangle

and

\#C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})=\prod_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{k_{i}}k_{i}!.

For abbreviation, we write
T_{\mu}
instead of
T_{w_{\mu}}
. Let
t\in T_{\mu}^{F}
be a semisimple element. There is a natural embedding:

\begin{matrix}T_{\mu}=\prod_{[a]}T_{\mu[t,[a]]}\hookrightarrow C_{G^{F}}(t)=\prod_{[a]}G_{[a]}(t).\par\end{matrix}

where
T_{\mu[t,[a]]}=T_{\mu}\bigcap G_{[a]}(t)
is an
F
-stable maximal torus of
G_{[a]}(t)
. On the other hand, there is a natural decomposition of the torus
T_{\mu}
as follows:

\begin{matrix}T_{\mu}=T_{\mu_{1}}\times\cdots\times T_{\mu_{k}}\par\end{matrix}

where
T_{\mu_{i}}^{F}\cong\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\mu_{i}}})
. Clearly, for each
\mu_{i}
, there is a unique index
[a]
such that
T_{\mu_{i}}\subseteq T_{\mu[t,[a]]}
, and

T_{\mu[t,[a]]}=\prod_{i}T_{\mu_{i}}

where the product runs over the integers
i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
such that
T_{\mu_{i}}\subseteq T_{\mu[t,[a]]}
. In other words, we get a map
f_{t}
from the set
\{\mu_{i}\}
to the set
\{[a]\}
, and then we can regard the index
\mu[t,[a]]
as a partition
\mu[t,[a]]=(\mu_{j})\subset\mu
where
\mu_{j}\in\{\mu_{i}\}
with
f_{t}(\mu_{j})=[a]
. If
[a]
is not in the image of
f_{t}
, then we set
\mu[t,[a]]=0
. Hence, we have
\#[a]\big|\mu[t,[a]]
. For abbreviation, we write
\mu[t,a]
instead of
\mu[t,[a]]
. It is easy to check that
\mu[t,a]=\mu[t^{\prime},a]
for any
[a]
if and only if
t
and
t^{\prime}
are in the same
W_{G}(T_{\mu})^{F}
-conjugacy class.

Let
\mu^{\prime}
and
\mu^{\prime\prime}
be two partitions such that
|\mu^{\prime}|+|\mu^{\prime\prime}|=n
and
\mu=\mu^{\prime}\bigcup\mu^{\prime\prime}
. We fix an embedding

(3.2)

T_{\mu}=T_{\mu^{\prime}}\times T_{\mu^{\prime\prime}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}\times\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime\prime}|}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}.

It is easy to check that

w_{\mu}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k_{i}}\langle c_{i,\ell}\rangle\subset\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}\times\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime\prime}|},

which implies that

(3.3)

C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})=\left\langle C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})\bigcap\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|},C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})\bigcap\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime\prime}|},S\right\rangle

where
S
is a certain subgroup of
\psi(\mathbb{S}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{S}_{k_{r}})
such that
S\bigcap\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}=S\bigcap\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime\prime}|}=1
.
Note that
W_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}(T_{\mu})^{F}=C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})
,
W_{\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}})^{F}=C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})\bigcap\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and
W_{\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime\prime}})^{F}=C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})\bigcap\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime\prime}|}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. We get

(3.4)

|W_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}(T_{\mu})^{F}|=|C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})|=C_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\cdot|W_{\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}})^{F}|\cdot|W_{\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime\prime}})^{F}|.

From now on, we fix a
t\in T_{\mu}^{F}
. We now turn to consider the set

D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t):=\{t^{\prime}\in T_{\mu^{\prime}}^{F}\textrm{ such that }t^{\prime}=^{w^{\prime}}t|_{T_{\mu^{\prime}}}\textrm{ for some }w^{\prime}\in W_{G}(T_{\mu})^{F}\}.

If
t^{\prime}\in D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
, then it is easy to check that

•

{}^{w^{\prime}}t^{\prime}\in D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
for each
w^{\prime}\in W_{\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}})^{F}
;

•

\mu^{\prime}[t^{\prime},a]\subset\mu[t,a]
for each
[a]
;

•

\mu^{\prime}[t^{\prime},a]=\mu^{\prime}[t^{\prime\prime},a]
for any
[a]
if and only if
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime\prime}
are in the same
W_{\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}})^{F}
-conjugacy class.

Hence, we get an injection from the set of
W_{\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}})^{F}
-conjugacy classes in
D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
to the set

(3.5)

P(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t):=\left\{f:A\to P\textrm{ such that }\#[a]\big|f([a]),\ \bigcup_{[a]}f([a])=\mu^{\prime},\textrm{ and }f([a])\subset\mu[t,a]\right\}

where
A
is the set of indexes
[a]
, and
P
is the set of partitions of any integers. To show this is actually a bijection, we shall associate every map
f\in P(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
to a semisimple
t^{\prime}\in D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
as follows. We have a decomposition of
T_{\mu^{\prime}}
:

T_{\mu^{\prime}}=\prod_{[a]}T_{f([a])}

where
T_{f([a])}
is an
F
-stable maximal torus of
\mathrm{GL}_{|f([a])|}
. Let
T_{0,f([a])}
be the
F
-stable maximal torus of
\mathrm{GL}_{|f([a])|}
consisting of diagonal matrices. Put

t_{0,f([a])}=\mathrm{diag}\left(a,a^{q},a^{q^{2}}\cdots,a^{q^{|f([a])|-1}}\right)\in T_{0,f([a])}.

Let
w_{f([a])}
be the element of the Weyl group of
\mathrm{GL}_{|f([a])|}
corresponding to the partition
f[a]
. Since
\#[a]\big|f([a])
and
a^{q^{\#[a]}}=a
, we have
F(w_{f([a])}\cdot t_{0,f([a])})=t_{0,f([a])}
, or equivalently
t_{0,f([a])}\in T_{0,f([a])}^{w_{f([a])}F}
. Let
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{|f([a])|}
such that the image of
gF(g^{-1})
in the Weyl group is
w_{f([a])}
. Then
t_{f([a])}:={}^{g^{-1}}t_{0,f([a])}\in T_{f([a])}^{F}
. Put

t^{\prime}:=\prod_{[a]}t_{f([a])}.

Clearly,
t^{\prime}\in D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
and
\mu[t^{\prime},a]=f([a])
for each
[a]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Keep the notations and assumptions as above, there is a bijection from the set
P(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
to the
W_{\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}})^{F}
-conjugacy classes in
D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
.

Set

M(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t,t^{\prime}):=\{w^{\prime}\in W_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}(T_{\mu})^{F}:t^{\prime}=^{w^{\prime}}t|_{T_{\mu^{\prime}}}\}.

Recall that the action of
W_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}})^{F}
on
T_{\mu^{\prime}}
is equal to the action of
C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})
on
T_{0}^{w_{\mu}F}
. By our discussion of the action of
C_{W,F}(w_{\mu})
in (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), we have

(3.6)

\#M(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &|W_{\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}},t^{\prime})^{F}|\cdot|W_{\mathrm{GL}_{|\mu^{\prime\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime\prime}})^{F}|\cdot\prod_{[a]}C_{\mu[t,a],\mu^{\prime}[t^{\prime},a]}&\textrm{ if }t^{\prime}\in D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t);\\
&0&\textrm{ otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

where
W_{G}(T,t):=\{w\in W_{G}(T)|{}^{w}t=t\}
.

3.2.
The unitary groups

In this subsection, we assume that
G=\mathrm{U}_{n}
. We may choose the hermitian form that defines the unitary group to be represented by the identity matrix so that

\mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})|g\cdot{}^{t}\bar{g}=I_{n}\},

where
\bar{g}
is obtained from raising matrix entries in
g
to the
q
-th power.
Then the action of the Frobenius endomorphism
F
is given by

g\mapsto{}^{t}\bar{g}^{-1}.

Take an
F
-stable maximal torus
T_{0}
of
G
that is contained in an
F
-stable Borel subgroup. As before, we have
W=W_{G}(T_{0})\cong\mathbb{S}_{n}
. It is easy to check that with this identification, the action of
F
on
W
is given by

F(w)=(w_{0}ww_{0})

where
w_{0}
is the longest element in
\mathbb{S}_{n}
.
Note that
w
and
w^{\prime}
are
F
-conjugate if and only if
ww_{0}
and
w^{\prime}w_{0}
are conjugate. So the
F
-conjugacy classes in
W
are parametrized by partitions of
n
. We write
T_{\mu}
instead of
T_{w}
if
w
is in the
F
-conjugacy class corresponding to
\mu
or equivalently
ww_{0}
is in the
W
-conjugacy class corresponding to
\mu
.

Let
\mu^{\prime}
and
\mu^{\prime\prime}
be two partitions such that
|\mu^{\prime}|+|\mu^{\prime\prime}|=n
. Suppose that
\mu=\mu^{\prime}\bigcup\mu^{\prime\prime}
. Then we have

(3.7)

|W_{\mathrm{U}_{n}}(T_{\mu})^{F}|=C_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\cdot|W_{\mathrm{U}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}})^{F}|\cdot|W_{\mathrm{U}_{|\mu^{\prime\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime\prime}})^{F}|.

Similar to subsection
3.1
, we define
\mu[t,a]
,
D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
and
P(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Keep the notations and assumptions as above, there is a bijection from
P(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
to the
W_{\mathrm{U}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}})^{F}
-conjugacy classes in
D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t)
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to Lemma
3.1
. The only difference between the unitary groups case and the general linear groups case is the construction of
t_{0,f([a])}
. In the unitary groups case, we take
t_{0,f([a])}=\mathrm{diag}\left(a,a^{-q},a^{{-q}^{2}}\cdots,a^{{-q}^{|f([a])|-1}}\right)
instead of
\mathrm{diag}\left(a,a^{q},a^{q^{2}}\cdots,a^{q^{|f([a])|-1}}\right)
, and that is why the definitions of
[a]
differ between these two cases.
∎

As before, we set

M(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t,t^{\prime}):=\{w^{\prime}\in W_{\mathrm{U}_{n}}(T_{\mu})^{F}:t^{\prime}=^{w^{\prime}}t|_{T_{\mu^{\prime}}}\}.

Then

(3.8)

\#M(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &|W_{\mathrm{U}_{|\mu^{\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime}},t^{\prime})^{F}|\cdot|W_{\mathrm{U}_{|\mu^{\prime\prime}|}}(T_{\mu^{\prime\prime}})^{F}|\cdot\prod_{[a]}C_{\mu[t,a],\mu^{\prime}[t^{\prime},a]}&\textrm{ if }t^{\prime}\in D(\mu,\mu^{\prime},t);\\
&0&\textrm{ otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

3.3.
The symplectic and odd special orthogonal groups

Let G be an odd special orthogonal group
G=\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
or a symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}
. The Weyl group
W
of
G
is isomorphic to the semidirect product of
\mathbb{S}_{n}
with an abelian normal subgroup of order
2^{n}
. Clearly,
F
acts trivially on
W
. Then the conjugacy classes of
W
are equal to the
F
-conjugacy classes of
W
. It is well known that the conjugacy classes of
W
are parameterized by bipartitions
(\mu,\lambda)
, where
|\mu|+|\lambda|=n
. We allow
\mu=0
or
\lambda=0
. For abbreviation, we write
T_{\mu,\lambda}
instead of
T_{w_{\mu,\lambda}}
.

The canonical form of an element
w_{\mu,\lambda}\in W
can be described as follows. Suppose that
\mu=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{k})
,
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{l})
. Put

N_{\mu_{i}}:=\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{i}\textrm{ and }N_{\lambda_{j}}:=|\mu|+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{j}.

Let
T_{0}
be the group of diagonal matrices.
Let
t=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in T_{0}
and
w_{\mu,\lambda}\cdot t=t^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,x^{\prime}_{n})\in T_{0}
.
Then

(3.9)

(x^{\prime}_{N_{\mu_{i}}+1},x^{\prime}_{N_{\mu_{i}}+2},\cdots,x^{\prime}_{N_{\mu_{i+1}}})=(x_{N_{\mu_{i+1}}},x_{N_{\mu_{i}}+1},x_{N_{\mu_{i}}+2},\cdots,x_{N_{\mu_{i+1}}-1})

and

(3.10)

(x^{\prime}_{N_{\lambda_{j}}+1},x^{\prime}_{N_{\lambda_{j}}+2},\cdots,x^{\prime}_{N_{\lambda_{j+1}}})=((x_{N_{\lambda_{j+1}}})^{-1},x_{N_{\lambda_{j}}+1},x_{N_{\lambda_{j}}+2},\cdots,x_{N_{\lambda_{j+1}}-1}).

From now on, consider the symplectic group
G=\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}
.
Let
t\in T_{\mu,\lambda}^{F}
be a semisimple element. There is a natural embedding:

\begin{matrix}T_{\mu,\lambda}=\prod_{[a]}T_{\mu[t,[a]],\lambda[t,[a]]}\hookrightarrow C_{G^{F}}(t)=\prod_{[a]}G_{[a]}(t).\end{matrix}

where
T_{\mu[t,[a]],\lambda[t,[a]]}=T_{\mu,\lambda}\bigcap G_{[a]}(t)
is an
F
-stable maximal torus of
G_{[a]}(t)
. On the other hand, there is a natural decomposition of the torus
T_{\mu,\lambda}
as follows:

\begin{matrix}T_{\mu,\lambda}=T_{\mu_{1}}\times\cdots\times T_{\mu_{k}}\times T_{\lambda_{1}}\times\cdots\times T_{\lambda_{l}}\end{matrix}

where
T_{\mu_{i}}^{F}\cong\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\mu_{i}}})
and
T_{\lambda_{i}}^{F}\cong\mathrm{U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\lambda_{i}}})
. Clearly, for each
\mu_{i}
(resp.
\lambda_{j}
), there is a unique index
[a]
such that
T_{\mu_{i}}\subseteq G_{[a]}(t)
(resp.
T_{\lambda_{j}}\subseteq G_{[a]}(t)
), and

T_{\mu[t,[a]],\lambda[t,[a]]}=T_{\mu[t,[a]]}\times T_{\lambda[t,[a]]}=\prod_{i}T_{\mu_{i}}\times\prod_{j}T_{\lambda_{j}}

where the product of
i
(resp.
j
) runs over the integers
i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
(resp.
j\in\{1,\cdots,l\}
) such that
T_{\mu_{i}}\subseteq G_{[a]}(t)
(resp.
T_{\lambda_{j}}\subseteq G_{[a]}(t)
). In other words, we get a map
f_{t}
from the set
\{\mu_{i}\}\cup\{\lambda_{j}\}
to the set
\{[a]\}
, and then we can regard the index
(\mu[t,[a]],\lambda[t,[a]])
as a bipartition
(\mu[t,[a]],\lambda[t,[a]])=((\mu_{i^{\prime}}),(\lambda_{j^{\prime}}))\subset(\mu,\lambda)
where
\mu_{i^{\prime}}\in\{\mu_{i}\}
with
f_{t}(\mu_{i^{\prime}})=[a]
and
\lambda_{j^{\prime}}\in\{\lambda_{j}\}
with
f_{t}(\lambda_{j^{\prime}})=[a]
. If
[a]
is not in the image of
f_{t}
, then we set
(\mu[t,[a]],\lambda[t,[a]])=0
. Hence, we have

\#[a]\big|\mu[t,[a]]\ \textrm{ and }\ \#[a]\big|2\lambda[t,[a]].

For abbreviation, we write
\mu[t,a]
(resp.
\lambda[t,a]
) instead of
\mu[t,[a]]
(resp.
\lambda[t,[a]]
). It is easy to check that
(\mu[t,a],\lambda[t,a])=(\mu[t^{\prime},a],\lambda[t^{\prime},a])
for any
[a]
if and only if
t
and
t^{\prime}
are in the same
W_{G}(T_{\mu,\lambda})^{F}
-conjugacy class.

Let
(\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
and
(\mu^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime})
be two partitions such that
\mu=\mu^{\prime}\bigcup\mu^{\prime\prime}
and
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}\bigcup\lambda^{\prime\prime}
. Then we fix an embedding

(3.11)

T_{\mu,\lambda}=T_{\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\times T_{\mu^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sp}_{2(|\mu^{\prime}|+|\lambda^{\prime}|)}\times\mathrm{Sp}_{2(|\mu^{\prime\prime}|+|\lambda^{\prime\prime}|)}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}.

In a similar way to the general linear groups case, we have

(3.12)

|W_{G}(T_{\mu,\lambda})^{F}|\cong|C_{W,F}(w_{\mu,\lambda})|=C_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\cdot C_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\cdot|W_{\mathrm{Sp}_{2(|\mu^{\prime}|+|\lambda^{\prime}|)}}(T_{\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})^{F}|\cdot|W_{\mathrm{Sp}_{2(|\mu^{\prime\prime}|+|\lambda^{\prime\prime}|)}}(T_{\mu^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}})^{F}|.

From now on, we fix a
t\in T_{\mu}^{F}
. With the embedding (
3.11
), we now turn to describe the set

D(\mu,\lambda,\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},t):=\{t^{\prime}\in T_{\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}^{F}\textrm{ such that }t^{\prime}=^{w^{\prime}}t|_{T_{\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}}\textrm{ for some }w^{\prime}\in W_{\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}}(T_{\mu,\lambda})^{F}\}.

It is easily seen that

•

{}^{w^{\prime}}t^{\prime}\in D(\mu,\lambda,\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},t)
for each
w^{\prime}\in W_{\mathrm{Sp}_{2(|\mu^{\prime}|+|\lambda^{\prime}|)}}(T_{\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})^{F}
.

•

\mu^{\prime}[t^{\prime},a]\subset\mu[t,a]
for each
[a]
.

•

\lambda^{\prime}[t^{\prime},a]\subset\lambda[t,a]
for each
[a]
.

•

(\mu[t^{\prime},a],\lambda[t^{\prime},a])=(\mu[t^{\prime\prime},a],\lambda[t^{\prime\prime},a])
for any
[a]
if and only if
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime\prime}
are in the same
W_{\mathrm{Sp}_{2(|\mu^{\prime}|+|\lambda^{\prime}|)}}(T_{\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})^{F}

\epsilon_{t,s}\cdot(-1)^{|\lambda^{\prime}[t^{\prime\prime},a]|}\cdot(-1)^{\mathrm{rk}(G)+\mathrm{rk}(H)}=(-1)^{\mathrm{rk}\left(G^{\prime}[a]_{\frac{\mu^{\prime}[a]}{\#[a]}\bigcup\frac{\lambda^{\prime}[a]}{\frac{1}{2}\#[a]}}\right)+\mathrm{rk}\left(H^{\prime}[a]_{\frac{\mu^{\prime}[a]}{\#[a]}\bigcup\frac{\lambda^{\prime}[a]}{\frac{1}{2}\#[a]}}\right)}.

In summarizing, we have the following result.

Proposition 7.4
.

Keep the notations and assumptions as above. Then

(7.15)

\epsilon_{t,s}\langle R^{G}_{T,\chi},R_{S,\eta}^{H}\rangle_{H^{F}}=\prod_{[a]}\epsilon_{[a]}\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{T^{\prime}[a],\theta[a]\otimes 1},R^{H^{\prime}[a]}_{S^{\prime}[a],1}\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}

where the product runs over
[a]
such that
[a]\in\chi
and
[a]\in\eta
, and it is a finite product.

Proof.

It follows immediately from Proposition
7.1
, (
7.10
), Lemma
7.2
and Lemma
7.3
.

8.
Decomposition of the Gan-Gross-Prasad problem

Let
G
and
H
be the classical groups in section
6
and section

7
. Let
t\in G^{F}
and
s\in H^{F}
be semisimple elements. Let
\pi\in\cal{E}(G^{F},t)
and
\sigma\in\cal{E}(H^{F},s)
. Additional assumptions
\pm 1\notin t
and
\pm 1\notin s
if
G=\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
. Then
\pi
and
\sigma
are uniform, i.e. they are linear combinations of the Deligne-Lusztig characters as follows:

\pi=\sum_{(T^{*},t)\ \textrm{mod }G^{F}}C_{\pi,T^{*}}R^{G}_{T^{*},t}

and

\sigma=\sum_{(S^{*},s)\ \textrm{mod }H^{F}}C_{\sigma,S^{*}}R^{H}_{S^{*},s}

where the sums run over the geometric conjugacy of
(T^{*},t)
and
(S^{*},s)
, respectively. We can therefore calculate the pairing
\langle\pi,\sigma\rangle_{H^{F}}
by Reeder’s formal.

Consider the irreducible representations
\pi[a]
and
\sigma[a]
defined in section
4
. They are also linear combinations of the Deligne-Lusztig characters as follows:

\pi[a]=\sum_{(T^{*}_{[a]},1)\ \textrm{mod }G^{*}_{[a]}(t)^{F}}C_{\pi,T^{*}_{[a]}}R^{G^{*}_{[a]}(t)}_{T^{*}_{[a]},1}

and

\sigma[a]=\sum_{(S^{*}_{[a]},1)\ \textrm{mod }H^{*}_{[a]}(s)^{F}}C_{\sigma,S^{*}_{[a]}}R^{H^{*}_{[a]}(s)}_{S^{*}_{[a]},1}

where the sums run over the geometric conjugacy of
(T^{*}_{[a]},1)
and
(S^{*}_{[a]},1)
, respectively.

By (
4.2
), Proposition
6.4
, and Proposition
7.4
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\pi,\sigma\rangle_{H^{F}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}(T^{*},t)\ \textrm{mod }G^{F}\\
(S^{*},s)\ \textrm{mod }H^{F}\\
\end{array}$}}C_{\pi,T^{*}}\cdot C_{\sigma,S^{*}}\left\langle R^{G}_{T^{*},t},R^{H}_{S^{*},s}\right\rangle_{H^{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}(T^{*},t)\ \textrm{mod }G^{F}\\
(S^{*},s)\ \textrm{mod }H^{F}\\
\end{array}$}}\epsilon_{t,s}\cdot C_{\pi,T^{*}}\cdot C_{\sigma,S^{*}}\prod_{[a]}\epsilon_{[a]}\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{T^{\prime}[a],\theta[a]\otimes 1},R^{H^{\prime}[a]}_{S^{\prime}[a],1}\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{t,s}\epsilon_{G}\epsilon_{H}\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}(T,t)\ \textrm{mod }G^{F}\\
(S,s)\ \textrm{mod }H^{F}\\
\end{array}$}}\prod_{[a]}\epsilon_{[a]}\epsilon_{G^{*}_{[a]}(t)}\epsilon_{H^{*}_{[a]}(t)}C_{\pi,T^{*}_{[a]}}\cdot C_{\sigma,S^{*}_{[a]}}\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{T^{\prime}[a],\theta[a]\otimes 1},R^{H^{\prime}[a]}_{S^{\prime}[a],1}\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{t,s}\epsilon_{G}\epsilon_{H}\prod_{[a]}\epsilon_{[a]}\epsilon_{G^{*}_{[a]}(t)}\epsilon_{H^{*}_{[a]}(t)}\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}((T^{*}_{[a]},1)\ \textrm{mod }G^{*}_{[a]}(t)^{F}\\
(S^{*}_{[a]},1)\ \textrm{mod }H^{*}_{[a]}(s)^{F}\\
\end{array}$}}C_{\pi,T^{*}_{[a]}}\cdot C_{\sigma,S^{*}_{[a]}}\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{T^{\prime}[a],\theta[a]\otimes 1},R^{H^{\prime}[a]}_{S^{\prime}[a],1}\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}.

As in (
6.12
) and
(\ref{taso})
, we write
(T^{\prime}[a],\theta[a]\otimes 1)
as
(T^{\prime\prime}[a]\times T^{1}[a],\theta[a]\otimes 1)
. Since
G^{\prime}[a]=\mathrm{GL}_{n[a]+1}
or
\mathrm{U}_{n[a]+1}
, there is an
F
-stable levi subgroup
G^{\prime\prime}[a]\times G^{1}[a]\subset G^{\prime}[a]
such that

\begin{matrix}T^{\prime\prime}[a]&\times&T^{1}[a]&&\\
\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\\
\\
G^{\prime\prime}[a]&\times&G^{1}[a]&\subset&G^{\prime}[a],\end{matrix}

and
T^{\prime\prime}[a]
and
T^{1}[a]
are
F
-stable maximal torus of
G^{\prime\prime}[a]
and
G^{1}[a]
, respectively.
By the definition of
H^{\prime}[a]
and
G^{1}[a]
, we know that

H^{\prime}[a]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}G^{*}_{[a]}(t)&\textrm{ if }\nu_{[a]}(t)\geq\nu_{[a]}(s);\\
H^{*}_{[a]}(s)&\textrm{ otherwise};\end{array}\right.

and

G^{1}[a]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}H^{*}_{[a]}(t)&\textrm{ if }\nu_{[a]}(t)\geq\nu_{[a]}(s);\\
G^{*}_{[a]}(s)&\textrm{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Hence

\displaystyle\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{T^{\prime}[a],\theta[a]\otimes 1},R^{H^{\prime}[a]}_{S^{\prime}[a],1}\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}=

\displaystyle\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{G^{\prime\prime}[a]\times G^{1}[a]}\left(R^{G^{\prime\prime}[a]}_{T^{\prime\prime}[a],\theta[a]}\otimes R^{G^{1}[a]}_{T^{1}[a],1}\right),R^{H^{\prime}[a]}_{S^{\prime}[a],1}\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}.

For our purpose, it is convinced to set
\theta[a]
to be a regular character for each
[a]
. Assume that
(T^{\prime}[a],\theta[a]\otimes 1)
corresponds to
(T^{\prime}[a],x[a])=(T^{\prime\prime}[a],x^{\prime\prime}[a])\times(T^{1}[a],1)
. Then the geometric conjugacy of
(T^{\prime\prime}[a],x^{\prime\prime}[a])
is a single, and there exists an irreducible representation
\tau\in\cal{E}(G^{\prime\prime}[a]^{F},x^{\prime\prime}[a])
such that
\tau=\epsilon^{\prime\prime}_{[a]}R^{G^{\prime\prime}[a]}_{T^{\prime\prime}[a],\theta[a]}
for some
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}_{[a]}\in\{\pm 1\}
.

Assume that
\nu_{[a]}(t)<\nu_{[a]}(s)
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}((T^{*}_{[a]},1)\ \textrm{mod }G^{*}_{[a]}(t)^{F}\\
(S^{*}_{[a]},1)\ \textrm{mod }H^{*}_{[a]}(s)^{F}\\
\end{array}$}}C_{\pi,T^{*}_{[a]}}\cdot C_{\sigma,S^{*}_{[a]}}\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{G^{\prime\prime}[a]\times G^{1}[a]}\left(R^{G^{\prime\prime}[a]}_{T^{\prime\prime}[a],\theta[a]}\otimes R^{G^{1}[a]}_{T^{1}[a],1}\right),R^{H^{\prime}[a]}_{S^{\prime}[a],1}\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}((T^{*}_{[a]},1)\ \textrm{mod }G^{*}_{[a]}(t)^{F}\\
(S^{*}_{[a]},1)\ \textrm{mod }H^{*}_{[a]}(s)^{F}\\
\end{array}$}}\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{G^{\prime\prime}[a]\times G^{1}[a]}\left(R^{G^{\prime\prime}[a]}_{T^{\prime\prime}[a],\theta[a]}\otimes C_{\pi,T^{*}_{[a]}}R^{G^{1}[a]}_{T^{1}[a],1}\right),C_{\sigma,S^{*}_{[a]}}R^{H^{\prime}[a]}_{S^{\prime}[a],1}\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\prime\prime}_{[a]}\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{G^{\prime\prime}[a]\times G^{1}[a]}\left(\tau\otimes\pi[a]\right),\sigma[a]\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}.

If
G^{\prime}[a]
is a general linear group, then by the definition of
m(\pi[a],\sigma[a])
and Lemma
6.5
, we have

\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{G^{\prime\prime}[a]\times G^{1}[a]}\left(\tau\otimes\pi[a]\right),\sigma[a]\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}=m(\pi[a],\sigma[a]).

Suppose that
G^{\prime}[a]
is a unitary group. Then
H^{\prime}[a]
and
G^{1}[a]
are also unitary groups. Assume that
H^{\prime}[a]=\mathrm{U}_{n[a]}
and
G^{1}[a]=\mathrm{U}_{m[a]}
. Recall that
\pi[a]
and
\sigma[a]
are unipotent representations. If
n[a]-m[a]
is odd, then by
[
LW3
, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3]
, we get

\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{G^{\prime\prime}[a]\times G^{1}[a]}\left(\tau\otimes\pi[a]\right),\sigma[a]\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}=m(\pi[a],\sigma[a])

where
m(\pi[a],\sigma[a])
is the multiplicity in the Bessel case of the Gan-Gross-Prasad problem. Assume that
n[a]-m[a]
is even.
By
[
LW3
, Corollary 5.3 (ii)]
, for any irreducible generic representation
\tau^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime\prime F}[a]
, we have

\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{G^{\prime\prime}[a]\times G^{1}[a]}\left(\tau\otimes\pi[a]\right),\sigma[a]\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}=\pm\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{G^{\prime\prime}[a]\times G^{1}[a]}\left(\tau^{\prime}\otimes\pi[a]\right),\sigma[a]\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}.

In particular, by Proposition
4.3
, we can pick

\tau^{\prime}=\epsilon_{\tau^{\prime}}R^{\mathrm{U}_{n[a]+1-m[a]}}_{\mathrm{U}_{1}\times\mathrm{U}_{n[a]-m[a]}}(\tau^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\tau^{\prime}_{2})

where
\epsilon_{\tau^{\prime}}\in\{\pm\}
and
\tau^{\prime}_{1}
and
\tau^{\prime}_{2}
are certain irreducible generic representations of
\mathrm{U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{h[a]}})
and
\mathrm{U}_{n[a]-m[a]}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{h[a]}})
with
h[a]=\frac{\#[a]}{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, respectively. Thus

\displaystyle\left\langle R^{G^{\prime}[a]}_{G^{\prime\prime}[a]\times G^{1}[a]]}\left(\tau\otimes\pi[a]\right),\sigma[a]\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\left\langle R^{\mathrm{U}_{n[a]+1}}_{\mathrm{U}_{m[a]+1}\times\mathrm{U}_{n[a]-m[a]}}\left(\tau^{\prime}_{2}\otimes R^{\mathrm{U}_{m[a]+1}}_{\mathrm{U}_{1}\times\mathrm{U}_{m[a]}}(\tau^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\pi[a])\right),\sigma[a]\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}

Since we already assume that is
q
large enough such that the main theorem in
[
Sr2
]
holds, we can pick
\tau^{\prime}_{1}\in\cal{E}(\mathrm{U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),s^{\prime})
such that
1\notin s^{\prime}
. By Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 in
[
LW3
]
, we have

\displaystyle\pm\left\langle R^{\mathrm{U}_{n[a]+1}}_{\mathrm{U}_{m[a]+1}\times\mathrm{U}_{n[a]-m[a]}}\left(\tau^{\prime}_{2}\otimes R^{\mathrm{U}_{m[a]+1}}_{\mathrm{U}_{1}\times\mathrm{U}_{m[a]}}(\tau^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\pi[a])\right),\sigma[a]\right\rangle_{H^{\prime}[a]^{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\left(R^{\mathrm{U}_{m[a]+1}}_{\mathrm{U}_{1}\times\mathrm{U}_{m[a]}}(\tau^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\pi[a]),\sigma[a]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(\pi[a],\sigma[a])

where
m(R^{\mathrm{U}_{m[a]+1}}_{\mathrm{U}_{1}\times\mathrm{U}_{m[a]}}(\tau^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\pi[a]),\sigma[a])
is the multiplicity in the Bessel case, but
m(\pi[a],\sigma[a])
is in the Fourier-Jacobi case.

For the
\nu_{[a]}(t)\geq\nu_{[a]}(s)
case, we have a similar result. In summarizing, we have

\displaystyle\langle\pi,\sigma\rangle_{H^{F}}=\pm\prod_{[a]}m(\pi[a],\sigma[a]).

Note that both sides are not negative, so we can remove the sign in the RHS, which completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

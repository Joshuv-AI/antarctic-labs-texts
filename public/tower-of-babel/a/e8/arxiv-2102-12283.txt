Quantum decorated character stacks
David Jordan, Ian Le, Gus Schrader, Alexander Shapiro

Abstract
. We initiate the study of decorated character stacks and their quantizations using the framework of stratified factorization homology. We thereby extend the construction by Fock and Goncharov of (quantum) decorated character varieties to encompass also the stacky points, in a way that is both compatible with cutting and gluing and equivariant with respect to canonical actions of the modular group of the surface. In the cases G = SL 2 , PGL 2 G=\operatorname{SL}_{2},\operatorname{PGL}_{2} we construct a system of categorical charts and flips on the quantum decorated character stacks which generalize the well–known cluster structures on the Fock–Goncharov moduli spaces.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we introduce
decorated character stacks
and their quantizations using the framework of
stratified factorization homology
, as recently developed by Ayala, Francis, and Tanaka
[
AFT17

G

B

decorated surface
is a bipartite surface with open regions colored
G

T
and codimension one defects between them labelled
B
. The decorated character stack
\operatorname{Ch}(\mathbb{S})
is the moduli stack of
G
- and
T
-local systems over each corresponding region of
\mathbb{S}
, together with additional
B
-reduction data along the defect lines. The quantum decorated character stack is a quantization

of the category
\mathcal{QC}(\operatorname{Ch}_{G}(\mathbb{S}))
, obtained by integration over
\mathbb{S}
with coefficients in a

, which prescribes the ribbon braided tensor categories
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)
and
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(T)
in their respective regions, and their central tensor category
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(B)
along line defects.

The resulting quantum decorated character stacks form a basic ingredient in the study of Betti quantum geometric Langlands, a mathematical model for the Kapustin-Witten twist of
\mathcal{N}=4
SUSY
4d
Yang-Mills theory compactified on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(that is, evaluated on decorated surfaces times

). In mathematical terms, the structure of the parabolic induction algebra is simply that of a
1
-morphism between
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)
and

, these categories being regarded as objects in the 4-category of braided tensor categories

defines a natural transformation between the

and

between the
G
- and
T

G
and

In this paper we develop a number of tools which allow for very concrete computations with quantum decorated character stacks. Our lodestar is the following proposal of David Ben-Zvi:

\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})

1.2.
Construction via factorization homology

Our starting point is the following fundamental result, which is a direct corollary of Theorem
[
BFN10
, Theorem 1.2]
:

Theorem 1.12
.

For any
n
-manifold
X
, we have an equivalence of categories,

\mathcal{QC}(\operatorname{Ch}(X))=\int_{X}\mathrm{Rep}(G).

On the left we denote the category of quasi-coherent sheaves on the (ordinary, not decorated) character stack. The integral notation on the righthand side denotes the factorization homology of
X
with coefficients in the symmetric monoidal category
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, which is here regarded as an
E_{n}
-algebra. The theorem reconstructs the character stack
\operatorname{Ch}(X)
on any
n
-manifold
X
in terms of its factorization homology.

We may treat decorated character stacks analogously in the framework of
stratified factorization homology
. Whereas an ordinary surface has only a single basic open disk (to which is attached
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
above), a decorated surface has three basic open disks: one contained in
\mathbb{S}_{G}
, one contained in
\mathbb{S}_{T}
, and one straddling a wall.

Accordingly, factorization homology of decorated surfaces requires us to specify coefficients for each type of basic open disk, together with a
\mathbb{D}\mathrm{isk}
-algebra
— a system of functors and higher coherences relating the coefficients. For instance, in the undecorated case we were required to specify not only
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, but also its
E_{2}
(= braided monoidal) structure. In order to recover
decorated
character stacks, we will specify the so-called “parabolic induction algebra”, being the triple of categories

\mathrm{Rep}(T\leftarrow B\rightarrow G):=(\mathrm{Rep}(G),\mathrm{Rep}(B),\mathrm{Rep}(T)),

together with the pullback functors along
i\colon B\to G
and
\pi\colon B\to T
. The following theorem again follows as a corollary of
[
BFN10
, Theorem 1.2]
:

Theorem 1.13
.

We have an equivalence of categories,

\mathcal{QC}(\operatorname{Ch}(\mathbb{S}))=\int_{S}\mathrm{Rep}(T\leftarrow B\rightarrow G).

In the works
[
BBJ18
,
BBJ18a
]
,
Theorem

1.12
was taken as the starting place for a
quantization procedure.
Namely, replacing
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
with
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)
, yields a functorial deformation quantization of
\operatorname{Ch}(S)
. Such quantizations of character stacks via factorization homology were subsequently related to combinatorial Chern–Simons theory and Alekseev–Grosse–Schomerus algebras
[
AGS96
]
in
[
BBJ18
]
, double affine Hecke algebras
[

]
in
[

]
, and to skein algebras and skein categories
[

,

]
in
[

]

In the present paper we extend this paradigm to the study of decorated surfaces by replacing
\mathrm{Rep}(T\leftarrow B\rightarrow G)
with the “quantum parabolic induction algebra”. This consists of the triple,

\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(T\leftarrow B\rightarrow G):=\left(\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G),\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(B),\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(T)\right)

along with
the ribbon braided tensor structure on
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)
and
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(T)
, the tensor structure on
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(B)
, and a braided tensor functor

\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)\boxtimes\overline{\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(T)}\longrightarrow Z(\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(B)),

where

denotes the

of the monoidal category

\overline{\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(T)}
carries the opposite of its standard braiding. This data defines a
local coefficient system
for stratified factorization homology of
[

, see also
[
BJS18

Definition 1.14
.

The

is the stratified factorization homology,

\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S}):=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(T\leftarrow B\rightarrow G).

\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})=\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S}_{1})\underset{\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S}_{12})}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S}_{2}).

A final important ingredient of stratified factorization homology is the existence of a canonical
distinguished object

\mathrm{Dist}_{\mathbb{S}}
in each category
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})
. The assignment
\mathbb{S}\mapsto\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})
is functorial for stratified embeddings, and the morphism in factorization homology induced by the empty embedding
\emptyset\hookrightarrow\mathbb{S}
determines a functor
\operatorname{Vect}\to\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})
; its image on the one-dimensional vector space is, by definition, the distinguished object. Classically, the distinguished object is precisely the structure sheaf of
\operatorname{Ch}(\mathbb{S})
, hence the distinguished object quantizes the structure sheaf to
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})
, and the functor
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathrm{Dist}_{\mathbb{S}},-)
quantizes the global sections functor.

1.3.
Aside on noncommutative algebraic geometry

Our main results are phrased in the language of noncommutative algebraic geometry, for which we follow
[
Smi00
]
. The main idea is to treat an arbitrary abelian category
\mathcal{C}
, such as
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})
, as the category of sheaves on some putative noncommutative algebraic variety (more generally, stack), and to study
\mathcal{C}
functorially drawing on geometric intuition coming from the commutative case. Hence, each definition below, when applied in the commutative case
q=1
, will recover precisely the usual geometric notion, and for generic
q
will give its deformation.

In this context, a full subcategory
i\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is called
open
if the inclusion functor
i
admits an exact left adjoint. The name “open” comes from the following basic example: given an inclusion
i\colon U\hookrightarrow X
of an open subvariety (more generally, an open substack), the pushforward
i_{*}\colon\mathcal{QC}(U)\to\mathcal{QC}(X)
is fully faithful with exact left adjoint
i^{*}
. For this reason, we will refer to
i^{L}
as the
restriction functor
, and talk about restricting objects to open subcategories. Important examples of open subcategories arise from Ore localizations: the inclusion
A[S^{-1}]\mathrm{-mod}\subset A\mathrm{-mod}
has an exact left adjoint — the localization functor — given by tensoring over
A
with
A[S^{-1}]
.

Dually, we may view an open subcategory
\mathcal{C}
as a quotient of
\mathcal{D}
. Indeed
\ker(i^{L})
is a Serre subcategory, hence
i^{L}
induces an equivalence
\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathcal{D}/\ker(i^{L})
. Classically,
\ker(i^{*})
consists of the sheaves supported on the complement of
U
. The
geometric union
of open subcategories
i_{1}\colon\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}
and
i_{2}\colon\mathcal{C}_{2}\to\mathcal{D}
is defined as

\mathcal{C}_{1}\dot{\cup}\mathcal{C}_{2}=\mathcal{D}/(\ker i_{1}\cap\ker i_{2})
. We emphasize that this is not simply the full subcategory obtained by taking unions of their objects.

We say that an open subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{D}
is a
chart
(alternatively, is
affine
) if
\mathcal{C}
admits a compact projective generator
X
, and hence an equivalence
\mathcal{C}\simeq\operatorname{End}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\mathrm{-mod}
with the category of modules for a (typically, noncommutative) algebra. Classically, this would mean that the corresponding open set is affine. We say that the chart is
toric
if, moreover, the algebra
\operatorname{End}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}
is a quantum torus, i.e. if there exists an isomorphism of algebras,

\operatorname{End}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbb{C}\left\langle x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{m}^{\pm 1}\right\rangle/\left\langle x_{i}x_{j}=q^{m_{ij}}x_{j}x_{i}\right\rangle,

for some skew symmetric integer matrix
M=(m_{ij})
.

Given two charts
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{D}
, the
transition functor

\phi_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is the composite exact functor,

\phi_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\colon\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{i_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{D}\xrightarrow{i_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{L}}\mathcal{C}^{\prime},

where
i_{\mathcal{C}},i_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
are inclusion functors, and
i_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{L}
is left adjoint to
i_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
.
In the case
\mathcal{C}=A\mathrm{-mod}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=A^{\prime}\mathrm{-mod}
are affine charts, the transition functor
\phi_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is given by tensoring with some
A-A^{\prime}
bimodule
M
. Classically, if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{QC}(U)
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{QC}(U^{\prime})
with
U,U^{\prime}
being open affine subvarieties of some variety
X
, the bimodule
M
is merely the algebra of functions on the intersection
U\cap U^{\prime}
.

An additional feature in our story is the presence of quantum categorical
G
- and
T
-actions coming from disk insertions through
G
and
T
-gates. We will say that a subcategory is a
\mathcal{G}
-chart
(alternatively, is
\mathcal{G}
-affine
) if it admits a compact projective
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(\mathcal{G})
-generator
X
, hence an equivalence,

\mathcal{C}\simeq\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal{G}}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\mathrm{-mod}_{\mathcal{G}},

where the notation
A\mathrm{-mod}_{\mathcal{G}}
is a shorthand for the category of
A
-modules internal to
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(\mathcal{G})
– i.e. the category of
A
-modules equipped with compatible actions of the quantum group attached to
\mathcal{G}
.

Classically for a quotient stack
X/\mathcal{G}
,
QC(X)
being
\mathcal{G}
-affine means that the map
X\to\bullet/\mathcal{G}
is affine. For instance
\bullet/\mathcal{G}
itself is not affine, since
\mathcal{QC}(\bullet/\mathcal{G})=\mathrm{Rep}(\mathcal{G})
does not admit a compact projective generator; however it is
\mathcal{G}
-affine, since the trivial representation is tautologically a compact projective
\mathcal{G}
-generator. See
Section

3
for a discussion of monadic reconstruction and internal endomorphism algebras, and
Section

3.3
for discussion of familiar classical examples in this language.

1.4.
Charts and flips on
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})

As explained in
Section

2
, the framework of stratified factorization homology may be understood as the specification of a universal property to be satisfied by an desired invariant of surfaces, and a theorem stating that this universal property can be solved in suitable settings. The universal property itself is phrased in
\infty
-categorical terms, and its
a priori
solution is expressed via colimits in higher categories.

At first encounter, such a definition involving
\infty
-colimits may sound hopelessly abstract. However, a number of techniques — most notably the excision formula, and its monadic reformulations — allow us to wrangle this abstract definition into a concrete enough form from which we can extract the calculus of quantum cluster algebras. Our strategy is roughly as follows; we will focus the exposition on
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}
, however, we will explain the modifications for the
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}
-case as well.

To begin, we treat the case of the
n
-gon
\mathbb{D}_{n}
, with a unique gate in the
G
-region and in each
T
-region. We compute the internal endomorphism algebra
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\operatorname{Conf}^{fr}_{n})=\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{G\times T^{n}}(\mathrm{Dist}_{\mathbb{S}})
, identifying it with the braided tensor product of
n
copies of an algebra
\mathcal{F}_{q}(N\backslash G)
. The functor
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{G\times T^{n}}(\mathrm{Dist}_{\mathbb{S}},-)
, of
G\times T^{n}
-equivariant global sections, defines an open embedding,

i\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{D}_{n})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}(\operatorname{Conf}^{fr}_{n})\mathrm{-mod}_{G\times T^{n}}

In
3.51
we compute the orthogonal of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{D}_{n})
to be the Serre subcategory of
torsion
modules (see
3.42
).

We consider triangulations
{\underline{\Delta}}
of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
. We define
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant Ore sets

in
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\operatorname{Conf}^{fr}_{n})

{\underline{\Delta}}
, and which quantize corresponding cluster
\mathcal{A}
-coordinates on
G\backslash(N\backslash G)^{\times n}
. In fact, the most important cases are

, which each have a unique triangulation. We denote these special triangulations by
\underline{\mathbb{D}_{2}}
(a degenerate triangulation with one edge, but no triangles) and

as an edge of

In

, we define an open subcategory

act invertibly.
We show that the restriction

of the distinguished object to

is a

), hence yielding an equivalence of categories

and sandwiching

between

-affine categories:

\mathcal{F}_{q}(\operatorname{Conf}^{fr}_{n})[S_{\underline{\Delta}}^{-1}]\mathrm{-mod}_{G\times T^{n}}\subset\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{D}_{n})\subset\mathcal{F}_{q}(\operatorname{Conf}^{fr}_{n})\mathrm{-mod}_{G\times T^{n}},

Crucially, we also show that the functor of taking

-invariants is conservative on

(we note again, it is not so for

\mathcal{Z}({\underline{\Delta}})\simeq\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{T^{n}}(\mathrm{Dist}_{\underline{\Delta}})\mathrm{-mod}_{T^{n}}\cong\left(\mathcal{F}_{q}(\operatorname{Conf}^{fr}_{n})[S_{\underline{\Delta}}^{-1}]\right)^{U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}\mathrm{-mod}_{T^{n}}.

Henceforth let us abbreviate
\zeta({\underline{\Delta}}):=\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{T^{n}}(\mathrm{Dist}_{\underline{\Delta}})\mathrm{-mod}_{T^{n}}
. Returning to the special cases

, we calculate isomorphisms,

\zeta(\underline{\mathbb{D}_{2}})\cong\mathbb{C}_{q}\left\langle D_{12}^{\pm 1}\right\rangle\qquad\text{and}\qquad\zeta(\underline{\mathbb{D}_{3}})\cong\mathbb{C}_{q}\left\langle D_{12}^{\pm 1},D_{23}^{\pm 1},D_{13}^{\pm 1}\right\rangle/I,

We then turn to the construction of charts on a general simple decorated surface

(1.1)

\mathbb{S}=(\mathbb{D}_{3})^{\sqcup t}\bigsqcup_{(\mathbb{D}_{2})^{\sqcup 2\ell}}(\mathbb{D}_{2})^{\sqcup\ell}.

(1.2)

\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})\simeq\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{D}_{3})^{t}\bigboxtimes_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{D}_{2})^{2\ell}}\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{D}_{2})^{\ell},

\mathcal{Z}({\underline{\Delta}})\subset\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})

(1.3)

\mathcal{Z}({\underline{\Delta}}):=\mathcal{Z}(\underline{\mathbb{D}_{3}})^{t}\bigboxtimes_{\mathcal{Z}(\underline{\mathbb{D}_{2}})^{2\ell}}\mathcal{Z}(\underline{\mathbb{D}_{2}})^{\ell},

Remark 1.16

The reader is encouraged to view

1.6.
Outlook

The most self-evident challenge which presents itself is to extend the results of this paper to groups of higher rank, namely to discover cluster charts and flips within quantum decorated character stacks for higher rank groups. While the general construction from this paper applies to arbitrary reductive groups, we expect the detailed determination of cluster charts beyond rank one will involve many interesting and challenging aspects, and will not be a straightforward modification of our computations here. In higher rank, decorated character stacks admit richer algebraic structure — an arbitrary parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
with Levi quotient
P\to L
determining a domain wall between
G
and
L
. These domain walls can not only stack (i.e, compose as 1-morphisms in
\operatorname{BrTens}
), but they may also overlap — this is modelled by the 2-morphisms in
\operatorname{BrTens}
, and hence involves stratified surfaces with point defects. We expect a correspondence between these interesting additional structures on the lattice of Levi subgroups, and the rich combinatorial structure of the cluster charts in higher rank.

Let us mention a number of more immediate applications which we intend to pursue:

•

Combining our work with that of
[

and
[

. We will give an invariant construction of such embeddings in a forthcoming work.

•

Alekseev–Grosse–Schomerus have obtained
[

]
a generators-and-relations presentation of certain algebras, now commonly called AGS algebras, by quantizing the Poisson structure described in
[

by Fock and Rosly on framed character varieties (in the absence of

and

. We will explore this systematically in a forthcoming work.

•

While this paper focuses primarily on construction of the subcategory
\dot{\cup}\mathcal{Z}({\underline{\Delta}})
and its relation to quantum cluster algebras, an important direction of future inquiry is to understand precisely the
complement
, which describes the stacky points of decorated character stacks and their quantizations.

•

By appealing to the 4-category

-dimensional TFT. In particular, we may consider extension of our work to decorated 3-manifolds,

•

We have largely restricted attention to

3.
Monadic reconstruction of module categories

Monadic reconstruction for module categories is a center-piece in the higher representation theory of tensor categories. It was developed the furthest in the fusion, and in the finite non-semisimple, settings; a comprehensive reference is
[
EGNO16
]
. Monadic reconstruction was developed also in the locally presentable framework in
[
BBJ18
,
BBJ18a
,
BJS18
]
. We recall those constructions here, with an additional wrinkle: in contrast to the usual situation, generators we encounter will not be projective, even in the relative sense; this reflects that the framed moduli spaces at the classical level are only quasi-affine rather than affine. For this reason, in place of the equivalences of categories featuring in usual monadic reconstruction we will instead obtain open embeddings of categories, and we will compute their orthogonal complements.

L
. Then we have an identification,

R(\mathcal{D})=(\ker(L))^{\perp},

between the essential image of
R
and the right orthogonal to the kernel of
L
.

Proof..

Let
X\in\mathcal{E}
. By the Yoneda embedding,
L(X)=0
if, and only if,
\operatorname{Hom}(L(X),Y)=0
for all
Y\in\mathcal{D}
, which is if, and only if,
\operatorname{Hom}(X,R(Y))=0
for all
Y\in\mathcal{D}
. The first condition is that
X\in\ker(L)
, while the second condition is that
X\in\,^{\perp}R(\mathcal{D})
. Hence
\ker L=\,^{\perp}R(\mathcal{D})
, hence
(\ker L)^{\perp}=(^{\perp}R(\mathcal{D}))^{\perp}=R(\mathcal{D})
.
∎

3.2.
Internal homomorphisms

The preceding discussion of monadicity is especially useful in analyzing rigid tensor categories and their module categories. We recall these applications here.

Let
\mathcal{A}

\operatorname{Hom}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(n,o)\otimes\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(m,n)\otimes m,o)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(n,o)\otimes\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(m,n),\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(m,o))

Definition 3.23
.

The

Remark 3.24
.

Let

in the preceding section. Then we have a natural isomorphism

We have the following translation of

3.3.
Monadic reconstruction: classical examples

Let us recall some very classical and well-known facts in geometric representation theory of flag varieties, recast in the categorical framework of noncommutative algebraic geometry and treated by monadic reconstruction.

Example 3.29
.

Let us start with a simple, almost trivial, application of the Barr-Beck theorem. Let
R\rightarrow S
be commutative algebras. Let
X=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
,
Y=\operatorname{Spec}(S)
so that we have the corresponding map of varieties
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
. We have
\mathcal{QC}(X)=R\mathrm{-mod}
and
\mathcal{QC}(Y)=S\mathrm{-mod}
.

There is a natural functor
L:=\pi^{*}\colon\mathcal{QC}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{QC}(Y)
given by
M\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}S
. The right adjoint is
R:=\pi_{*}\colon\mathcal{QC}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{QC}(X)
. Note that
\mathcal{QC}(Y)
is a module category for
\mathcal{QC}(X)
. Barr-Beck reconstruction realizes
\mathcal{QC}(Y)
as the category of objects of
\mathcal{QC}(X)
equipped with an action of the monad
RL
.

The monad for the adjunction is
-\otimes_{R}S
. An algebra for this monad is exactly an
R
-module which has a compatible
S
-module structure. Clearly such an object in
\mathcal{QC}(X)
corresponds to an object in
\mathcal{QC}(Y)
.

The examples we consider throughout this paper build on these examples in three distinct directions.

(1)

X
and
Y
will be replaced by stacks. Hence we will work internally to a monoidal category which carries the symmetries of the stack.

(2)

The map
Y\rightarrow X
will not be affine, but quasi-affine. Hence applying Barr-Beck will only give open embeddings of categories rather than equivalences.

(3)

We will be interested in quantizations; hence the symmetric monoidal categories expressing classical symmetries will be replaced systematically by their braided monoidal
q
-deformations.

In the remainder of this section, we will illustrate the first two points with classical examples. In the next section, we treat the quantum case.

Example 3.30
.

Let
X=\bullet/(G\times G)
and let
Y=\bullet/G
be the stack quotients of the one-point variety, by the trivial group action – these are sometimes called
classifying stacks
, and denoted
B(G\times G)
,
BG
. By definition, we have

\mathcal{QC}(X)=\mathrm{Rep}(G)\boxtimes\mathrm{Rep}(G),\qquad\mathcal{QC}(Y)=\mathrm{Rep}(G).

The group homomorphism
g\rightarrow(g,g^{-1})
induces a natural map
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
. Pullback along
\pi^{*}
defines a
\mathrm{Rep}(G)\boxtimes\mathrm{Rep}(G)
-module structure on
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, and we can apply Barr-Beck techniques to describe it. Let
\mathds{1}_{G}
be the trivial
G
-module. The functor
L=\pi^{*}
is given by
\mathrm{act}_{\mathds{1}_{G}}
, or

(V\boxtimes W)\longmapsto V\otimes W.

The right adjoint can be computed,

R=\pi_{*}V\longmapsto\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P_{+}}(V\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*})\boxtimes V_{\lambda}.

One then computes an isomorphism of algebras,

\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{G\times G}(\mathds{1}_{G})=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P_{+}}V_{\lambda}^{*}\boxtimes V_{\lambda}\cong\mathcal{O}(G).

Hence, Barr-Beck asserts that
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
is equivalent to the category
\mathcal{O}(G)\mathrm{-mod}_{G\times G}
. In other words,
\mathcal{O}(G)
is an algebra object in
\mathrm{Rep}(G)\boxtimes\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, and
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
can be realized as
\mathrm{Rep}(G)\boxtimes\mathrm{Rep}(G)
-modules which have the structure of an algebra over the
\mathrm{Rep}(G)\boxtimes\mathrm{Rep}(G)
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(G)
. The equivalence in this case expresses the obvious isomorphism of stacks,
G/(G\times G)\simeq\bullet/G
.

Note that the map
Y\rightarrow X
is a fibration with fiber
G
. Barr-Beck for the map of varieties
G\rightarrow\bullet
is easy–this is just a map of affine varieties as in the previous example. Thus we are really just revisiting
Example

3.29
in a family over the stack
X=\bullet/(G\times G)
.

Example 3.31
.

Let
Y=N\backslash G
and let
X
be a point. The left adjoint
L=\pi^{*}
takes a vector space
V
to the trivial vector bundle over
Y
with fiber
V
. The right adjoint
R=\pi_{*}
is the functor of global sections. Barr-Beck does not allow us to reconstruct
\mathcal{QC}(Y)
inside
\mathcal{QC}(X)
, because
\mathcal{QC}(Y)
does not have a compact-projective generator. The structure sheaf is a generator, but the global sections functor
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O},-)
does not preserve colimits (it is not right exact). Therefore, we have an open embedding

\mathcal{QC}(N\backslash G)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(N\backslash G)\mathrm{-mod}.

Geometrically,
N\backslash G
is quasi-affine, so it sits as an open set in
\overline{N\backslash G}=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(N\backslash G))
, and the orthogonal complement to
\mathcal{QC}(N\backslash G)
in
\mathcal{QC}(\overline{N\backslash G})
is the Serre subcategory of torsion sheaves supported on the complementary closed subvariety.

Example 3.32

(Classical parabolic induction)
.

Let
X=\bullet/(G\times T)
and let
Y=\bullet/B
. The map
B\rightarrow G\times T
induces a natural map
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
. Recall that
\pi^{*}
equips
\mathcal{QC}(Y)=\mathrm{Rep}(B)
with the structure of a
\mathcal{QC}(X)=\mathrm{Rep}(G\times T)
-module category. Let
\mathds{1}_{B}
denote the trivial representation of
B
. It is a
\mathrm{Rep}(G\times T)
-generator, but not
\mathrm{Rep}(G\times T)
-compact projective.
Then we have the module functor,

L=\mathrm{act}_{\mathds{1}_{B}}\colon\mathrm{Rep}(G\times T)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Rep}(B),\qquad V\boxtimes\chi\longmapsto i^{*}V\otimes\pi^{*}\chi.

We then have

\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{G\times T}(\mathds{1}_{B})\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P_{+}}V_{\lambda}\boxtimes\mathbb{C}_{-\lambda}\simeq\mathcal{O}(N\backslash G),

with the latter isomorphism being one of algebras in
\mathrm{Rep}(G\times{T})
.
Thus
\mathrm{Rep}(B)
can be realized as an open subcategory of
G\times T
-equivariant
\mathcal{O}(N\backslash G)
-modules. By the classical
\mathrm{Proj}
-construction applied to the
T
-graded ring
\mathcal{O}(N\backslash G)
, this open subcategory is further equivalent to the category of
G
-equivariant sheaves on the flag variety
B\backslash G
. Thus the monadic comparison functor is nothing but the classical parabolic induction equivalence

\mathrm{Rep}(B)\simeq\mathcal{QC}_{G}(B\backslash G),\qquad M\longmapsto M\times_{B}G,

sending a
B
-module
M
to the
G
–equivariant vector bundle
M\times_{B}G
on
B\backslash G
. Note that the left adjoint to this functor is given by taking the fiber
\mathcal{F}_{e}
of a
G
–equivariant sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
B\backslash G
over the identity element
e\in G
; this fiber carries a
B
-action since the identity coset
Be
is a fixed point for the action of
B
on
B\backslash G
by right translation.

3.4.
Monadic reconstruction:
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)
and
\mathcal{F}_{q}(G)

As we shall now explain, the monadic approach to the classical parabolic induction construction carries over equally well to the quantum setting.

Notation 3.33
.

In what follows we set

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{H}(V,W):=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(H)}(V,W)

for
H
being
G
,
B
, or
T
. We do the same for
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(H)^{\mathrm{op}}
and
\overline{\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(H)}
, so that e.g.

\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{G\boxtimes\overline{G}}(\mathds{1}_{G})=\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)\boxtimes\overline{\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)}}(\mathds{1}_{G}).

Example 3.34
.

Consider the rigid tensor category
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)
. Then
\mathcal{M}=\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)
is a module category for
\mathcal{A}
with the module structure given by

\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M},\qquad(V\boxtimes W)\otimes U\longmapsto V\otimes U\otimes W.

Writing
\mathds{1}_{G}\in\mathcal{M}
for the unit
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module, we obtain the functor

\mathrm{act}_{\mathds{1}_{G}}\colon\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M},\qquad V\boxtimes W\longmapsto V\otimes W.

Note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{G^{\mathrm{op}}\times G}\left(V_{\lambda}\boxtimes V_{\mu},\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{G^{\mathrm{op}}\times G}(\mathds{1}_{G})\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{G^{\mathrm{op}}\times G}\left(V_{\lambda}\boxtimes V_{\mu},\mathrm{act}^{R}_{\mathds{1}_{G}}(\mathds{1}_{G})\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{G}\left(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu},\mathds{1}_{G}\right).

Since the category
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)
is semisimple, and

\operatorname{Hom}_{G}\left(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu},\mathds{1}_{G}\right)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\text{if}\;V_{\lambda}\simeq V_{\mu}^{*},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

it follows that

\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{G^{\mathrm{op}}\times G}(\mathds{1}_{G})\simeq\bigoplus_{V\in\mathrm{Irr(\mathcal{M})}}V^{*}\boxtimes V=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}}V^{*}_{\lambda}\boxtimes V_{\lambda},

where the first direct sum is taken over the isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{M}
. The algebra structure on
\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{G\times G^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathds{1}_{G})
is given by

(\xi\boxtimes v)\otimes(\zeta\boxtimes w)=(\xi\otimes^{\mathrm{op}}\zeta)\boxtimes(v\otimes w)=(\zeta\otimes\xi)\boxtimes(v\otimes w).

It follows that
\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{G^{\mathrm{op}}\times G}(\mathds{1}_{G})
is isomorphic as an algebra in
\mathcal{A}
to the Faddeev–Reshetikhin–Takhtadjan quantum coordinate ring
\mathcal{F}_{q}(G)
, the Hopf algebra of matrix coefficients of finite-dimensional type I representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. The algebra
\mathcal{F}_{q}(G)
is a
q
–deformation of the Poisson algebra of functions on the simple Lie group
G
equipped with its standard Poisson-Lie structure
\pi=r^{L}-r^{R}
given by the difference of the left and right translates of the classical
r
–matrix, see
[
Sem92
]
.

Remark 3.35
.

Let
l_{\lambda}\subset V_{\lambda}
and
l_{-\lambda}\subset V_{\lambda}^{*}
be the highest and lowest weight lines respectively, and consider the
principal minor

f_{\lambda}=v^{*}_{-\lambda}\boxtimes v_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{F}_{q}(G)
, where
v^{*}_{-\lambda},v_{\lambda}
are any elements of
l_{-\lambda},l_{\lambda}
normalized such that
\left\langle v^{*}_{-\lambda},v_{\lambda}\right\rangle=1
. Then since

appears with multiplicity one in

and

spans the corresponding highest weight line, we have
f_{\lambda}f_{\mu}=f_{\lambda+\mu}
. In particular, the subalgebra of
\mathcal{F}_{q}(G)
generated by the principal minors
\{f_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}}
is commutative, and isomorphic to the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}(q)[\Lambda_{+}]
.

Example 3.36
.

Let us now consider the rigid tensor category
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)\boxtimes{\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)}

\mathcal{M}=\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G)
again has the structure of an
\mathcal{A}
–module category, with the module structure now being given by

\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{G^{2}}(\mathds{1}_{G})\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}}V^{*}_{\lambda}\boxtimes V_{\lambda},

but in view of (

\displaystyle(\xi\boxtimes v)\otimes(\zeta\boxtimes w)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{V^{*},W^{*}}(\xi\otimes\zeta)\boxtimes(v\otimes w)

The algebra

[
Sem92

Remark 3.37
.

Both algebras

3.5.

\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(B)
and
\mathcal{F}_{q}(N\backslash G)

We are now ready to give a monadic description of the category
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(B)
in terms of the quantized flag variety, by analogy with the classical parabolic induction equivalence described in

Example 3.39
.

Recall that
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(B)
is a module category for
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G\times\overline{T})
.
We write
\mathds{1}_{B}
for the monoidal unit in
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(B)

\mathrm{act}_{\mathds{1}_{B}}\colon\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G\times\overline{T})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(B),\qquad V\boxtimes\chi\longmapsto i^{*}V\otimes\pi^{*}\chi.

Its right adjoint is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{act}^{R}_{\mathds{1}_{B}}\colon\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(B)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(G\times\overline{T}),

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\longmapsto\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}\\
\mu\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Hom}_{B}\left(V_{\lambda}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\mu},M\right)\otimes V_{\lambda}\boxtimes\mathbb{C}_{\mu},

which implies

\mathrm{act}^{R}_{\mathds{1}_{B}}(M)\simeq\left(M\otimes\mathcal{F}_{q}(G\times T)\right)^{U_{q}(\mathfrak{b})},

where we have set

\mathcal{F}_{q}(G\times T)=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}\\
\mu\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}\left(\mathbb{C}_{\mu}^{*}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*}\right)\otimes V_{\lambda}\boxtimes\mathbb{C}_{\mu},

with

acting diagonally in the
\mathbb{C}_{-\mu}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*}
tensor factor, and
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\boxtimes U_{q}(\mathfrak{t})
acting in

.
Let us now turn our attention to the braided-commutative algebra
\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{G\times\overline{T}}(\mathds{1}_{B}).
Setting
M=\mathds{1}_{B}

Definition 3.42
.

An
\mathcal{F}_{q}(N\backslash G)
-module

is

. We denote the collection of torsion modules by

Lemma 3.43

We have the following characterization of

-invariant ideals in

∎

Proof..

The map

is surjective onto
\chi_{\operatorname{PGL}_{2}}({\underline{\Delta}})

. Given a puncture

Then, for a triangle

{\underline{\Delta}}

for all edges

4.9.
Topological remarks

In this final section we collect a number of remarks concerning the topological invariance built into our construction.

Let
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{S})
denote the 2-group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{S}
in
\mathbb{S}\mathrm{urf}
, i.e. the category with a single object, whose 1-morphisms are self-diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{S}
which respect the stratification and the labeling by
G
and
T
, and whose 2-morphisms are stratified isotopies of such (equivalently
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{S})
may be regarded as a monoidal category in which all objects and morphisms are invertible). We note that the
1
-truncation
\pi_{\leq 1}\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{S})
is the precisely the marked mapping class group, since by definition this means that we take isotopy equivalence classes. Given a set
\mathcal{G}
of
G
- and
T
-gates, let us also denote by
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{S})
the sub
2
-group of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{S})
of diffeomorphisms and isotopies which fix
\mathcal{G}
componentwise.

Simply because
\mathcal{Z}
is a functor, the category
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})
inherits a canonical action of the 2-group
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{S})
. This data can be understood as an action of the mapping class group, in which functors compose by the group law only up to a coherent isomorphism, encoded by a 2-cocycle with values in the center of the category. We note that the distinguished object, being defined by the empty embedding is preserved canonically (i.e. up to a canonical isomorphism) by any diffeomorphism, so that it obtains a canonical structure of a
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{S})
-fixed point of the category. In particular the mapping class group acts strictly on the vector space
\operatorname{End}(\mathrm{Dist}_{\mathbb{S}})
, and in such a way that it intertwines the cluster charts coming from different triangulations.

Suppose we are given two triangulations
{\underline{\Delta}}
and
{\underline{\Delta}}^{\prime}
related by an isotopy
\gamma
. We may assume
\gamma
at time
t=0
to be the identity automorphism of
\mathbb{S}
, so that as
t
runs to
t=1
, it induces isomorphisms between objects of
\mathcal{Z}({\underline{\Delta}})
and
\mathcal{Z}({\underline{\Delta}}^{\prime})
. Since both categories are closed under isomorphisms, it means two subcategories related by an isotopy are simply equal.

An interesting extra feature comes from the
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(\mathcal{G})
-action at the gates. In the presence of gates
\mathcal{G}
, any functor arising coming from
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{S})
is canonically promoted to a
\mathcal{G}
-module functor, and any isotopy to a
\mathcal{G}
-module natural isomorphism. This additional naturality has a consequence for the charts
\zeta({\underline{\Delta}}_{\bullet})
and their mutations. The algebras
\zeta({\underline{\Delta}}_{\bullet})
are defined with respect to a notched triangulation, yet their presentation, as encoded by the fencing graph, depended only on the associated notched ideal triangulation. We note that two isotopy classes of notched triangulations give rise to the same notched ideal triangulation if and only if they differ by iterated application of the Dehn twist implementing
2\pi
rotation at some puncture. This Dehn twist preserves the notching, hence it gives a
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(T)
-module auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S})
. However, the natural isotopy undoing the Dehn twist
does not
preserve the notch.

This means that the Dehn twist
is isomorphic
to the identity functor as a plain functor of categories, but is
not isomorphic
as a
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(T)
-module category. Hence the Dehn twist does not act trivially on internal endomorphism algebras such as
\zeta({\underline{\Delta}}_{\bullet})
. Indeed, a direct computation implies that the Dehn twist induces a homomorphism
\widetilde{\zeta}({\underline{\Delta}}_{\bullet})\to\widetilde{\zeta}({\underline{\Delta}}_{\bullet}^{\prime})
given by
Z_{e}\mapsto qZ_{e}\alpha_{p}
, for all edges
e
incident to the puncture.

And additional interesting subtlety comes when we consider the appearance of topological framings of
\mathbb{S}
. In

In our approach, this is reversed, and ordinary local systems are what naturally appear.

5.1.
The cylinder

We have

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]\left\langle Z_{1}^{\pm 1},Z_{2}^{\pm 1},Z_{3}^{\pm 1},Z_{4}^{\pm 1}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]\big\langle Z_{1}^{\pm 1},Z_{2}^{\pm 1},{Z^{\prime}_{3}}^{\pm 1},Z_{4}^{\pm 1}\big\rangle,

where the elements
Z_{1}
and
Z_{4}
are central in both tori, the other relations read

Z_{2}Z_{3}=qZ_{3}Z_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Z^{\prime}_{3}Z_{2}=qZ_{2}Z^{\prime}_{3},

and the flip
\mu
takes form

Z^{\prime}_{3}Z_{3}=Z_{1}Z_{4}+qZ_{2}^{2}.

The
X
-variables of the torus
\chi_{1}
then read

X_{1}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{1}Z_{2}Z_{3}^{-1}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,\qquad X_{2}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{3}^{2}Z_{1}^{-1}Z_{4}^{-1}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,\qquad X_{3}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{1}Z_{4}Z_{2}^{-2}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,\qquad X_{4}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{4}Z_{2}Z_{3}^{-1}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,

and satisfy the relations

\displaystyle X_{1}X_{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}X_{2}X_{1},

\displaystyle X_{1}X_{3}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}X_{3}X_{1},

\displaystyle X_{1}X_{4}

\displaystyle=X_{4}X_{1},

\displaystyle X_{4}X_{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}X_{2}X_{4},

\displaystyle X_{4}X_{3}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}X_{3}X_{4},

\displaystyle X_{2}X_{3}

\displaystyle=q^{4}X_{3}X_{2}.

Similarly, the
X
-variables of the torus
\chi_{2}
are given by

X^{\prime}_{1}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{1}Z^{\prime}_{3}Z_{2}^{-1}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,\qquad X^{\prime}_{2}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{1}Z_{4}{Z^{\prime}_{3}}^{-2}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,\qquad X^{\prime}_{3}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{2}^{2}Z_{1}^{-1}Z_{4}^{-1}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,\qquad X^{\prime}_{4}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{4}Z^{\prime}_{3}Z_{2}^{-1}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,

\displaystyle X^{\prime}_{1}X^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle X^{\prime}_{1}X^{\prime}_{4}

\displaystyle=X^{\prime}_{4}X^{\prime}_{1},

\displaystyle X^{\prime}_{4}X^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=q^{-2}X^{\prime}_{2}X^{\prime}_{4},

\displaystyle X^{\prime}_{4}X^{\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=q^{2}X^{\prime}_{3}X^{\prime}_{4},

5.2.
The punctured torus

In this case, we get

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]\left\langle Z_{1}^{\pm 1},Z_{2}^{\pm 1},Z_{3}^{\pm 1},\alpha^{\pm 1}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]\big\langle Z_{1}^{\pm 1},Z_{2}^{\pm 1},{Z^{\prime}_{3}}^{\pm 1},\alpha^{\pm 1}\big\rangle.

The generators satisfy relations

\displaystyle Z_{2}Z_{1}

\displaystyle=qZ_{1}Z_{2},

\displaystyle Z_{2}Z_{3}

\displaystyle=qZ_{3}Z_{2},

\displaystyle Z_{3}Z_{1}

\displaystyle=qZ_{1}Z_{3},

\displaystyle Z_{2}Z^{\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=qZ^{\prime}_{3}Z_{2},

\displaystyle Z_{1}Z^{\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=qZ^{\prime}_{3}Z_{1},

as well as

\alpha Z_{x}=q^{2}Z_{x}\alpha

for any edge
x\in\left\{1,2,3,3^{\prime}\right\}
. The flip
\mu
reads

Z^{\prime}_{3}Z_{3}=q^{2}Z_{1}^{2}\alpha+qZ_{2}^{2}.

The
X
-generators of the torus
\chi_{1}
read

X_{1}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{2}^{2}Z_{3}^{-2}\alpha\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,\qquad X_{2}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{3}^{2}Z_{1}^{-2}\alpha\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,\qquad X_{3}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{1}^{2}Z_{2}^{-2}\alpha^{-1}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,

and satisfy the relations

X_{i}X_{i+1}=q^{4}X_{i+1}X_{i},

for
i\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
. Similarly, the
X
-generators of the torus
\chi_{2}
take form

X^{\prime}_{1}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997pt{Z^{\prime}_{3}}^{2}Z_{2}^{-2}\alpha\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,\qquad X^{\prime}_{2}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{1}^{2}{Z^{\prime}_{3}}^{-2}\alpha^{-1}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,\qquad X^{\prime}_{3}=\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997ptZ_{2}^{2}Z_{1}^{-2}\alpha\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt,

and satisfy the relations

X^{\prime}_{i}X^{\prime}_{i+1}=q^{-4}X^{\prime}_{i+1}X^{\prime}_{i},

for
i\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
. Finally, the flip of
X
-variables is

X^{\prime}_{1}=X_{1}(1+qX_{3})(1+q^{3}X_{3}),\qquad X^{\prime}_{2}=X_{2}(1+qX_{3}^{-1})^{-1}(1+q^{3}X_{3}^{-1})^{-1},\qquad X^{\prime}_{3}=X_{3}^{-1}.

5.3.
The 3-punctured sphere

We have

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]\left\langle Z_{1}^{\pm 1},Z_{2}^{\pm 1},Z_{3}^{\pm 1},\alpha_{1}^{\pm 1},\alpha_{2}^{\pm 1},\alpha_{3}^{\pm 1}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]\big\langle Z_{1}^{\pm 1},Z_{2}^{\pm 1},{Z^{\prime}_{3}}^{\pm 1},\alpha_{1}^{\pm 1},\alpha_{2}^{\pm 1},\alpha_{3}^{\pm 1}\big\rangle.

The generators satisfy relations

\displaystyle Z_{2}Z_{1}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{1}{2}}Z_{1}Z_{2},

\displaystyle Z_{3}Z_{2}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{1}{2}}Z_{2}Z_{3},

\displaystyle Z_{3}Z_{1}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{1}{2}}Z_{1}Z_{3},

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}_{3}Z_{2}

\displaystyle=Z_{2}Z^{\prime}_{3},

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}_{3}Z_{1}

\displaystyle=qZ_{1}Z^{\prime}_{3},

as well as

[\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}]=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha_{i}Z_{j}=q^{1-\delta_{ij}}Z_{j}\alpha_{i},

for any
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant 3
. The flip
\mu
reads

Z^{\prime}_{3}Z_{3}=q^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997pt\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}Z_{1}Z_{2}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt+q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\hskip 1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip-1.99997pt\alpha_{3}Z_{1}Z_{2}\hskip-1.99997pt\boldsymbol{:}\hskip 1.99997pt.

The torus
\chi_{1}
is commutative and is generated by the elements

X_{j}=\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}},

for
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant 3
. The torus
\chi_{2}
is commutative as well, and is generated by

X^{\prime}_{1}=X_{1},\qquad X^{\prime}_{2}=X_{2},\qquad X^{\prime}_{3}=X_{3}^{-1}.

Introduction

Well-Quasi-Orders

(wqo for short) are a
notion from order theory
[
29
,
41
]
that has proven
very effective in many areas of mathematics, logic,
combinatorics, and computer science in order to establish finiteness
statements. For instance, in the field of formal verification, they
provide the termination arguments for the generic algorithms
for
well structured transition
systems

[
1
,
22
]
, notably the
backward
coverability algorithm
for deciding safety
properties
[
3
,
1
,
22
]
.

In full generality, one cannot extract complexity bounds from
wqo-powered termination proofs. Nevertheless, in an algorithmic
setting, one can “instrument” wqos by considering
so-called
controlled sequences

[
41
,
39
]
, and
new tight complexity upper bounds for wqo-based algorithms now appear
on a regular basis
[e.g.,
40
,
4
,
6
,
5
,
26
, for a few recent
examples]
.

Those complexity upper bounds are however astronomically high, and
sometimes actually way too high for the problem at hand. An
emblematic illustration of this phenomenon is the backward
coverability algorithm for vector addition systems (VAS), which was
shown to run in double exponential time by
Bozzelli and Ganty 2011
based on
an original analysis due to
Rackoff 1978
: the corresponding
bounds over the wqo
\mathbb{N}^{d}
are Ackermannian
[
20
]
.

Descending Chains.

One way pioneered by
Lazić and Schmitz 2021

to close such complexity gaps while retaining some of the wide
applicability of wqos and well structured transition systems is to
focus on the descending chains of downwards closed sets over the wqo
at hand. Indeed, one of the equivalent characterisations of wqos is
the
descending chain condition

[
29
,
41
]
, which
guarantees that those descending chains are finite.

In themselves, descending chains are no silver bullet: e.g., the
controlled descending chains over
\mathbb{N}^{d}
are also of Ackermannian
length
[
32
, Thm. 3.10]
. Nevertheless, these chains
sometimes exhibit a form of “monotonicity,” which yields vastly
improved upper bounds. When applied to a
dual
version of the
backward coverability algorithm in well structured transition systems,
this allows to recover the same double exponential time upper bound as
in
[
13
,
37
]
for the VAS coverability problem,
along with tight upper bounds for coverability in several VAS
extensions. The same framework was also the key to establishing tight
bounds for coverability in
\nu
-Petri nets
[
31
]
. As a
further testimony to the versatility of the approach,

Benedikt et al. 2017
use it in
[
7
]
to derive
original upper bounds for problems on invertible polynomial automata
and invertible affine nets, in a setting that is not strictly speaking
one of well structured transition systems.

Fine-grained Bounds for VAS Coverability.

The coverability
problem in VAS is well-known to be
\mathsf{EXPSPACE}
-complete, thanks
to
Rackoff 1978
’s
Rackoff 1978
upper bound matching
a
Lipton 1976
lower bound by
Lipton 1976
. The main
parameter driving this complexity is the dimension of the system: the
problem is in pseudo-polynomial time in fixed dimension
d
; more
precisely,
Rackoff 1978
’s analysis yields a
n^{2^{O(d\lg d)}}
deterministic time upper bound for
d
-dimensional VAS encoded
in unary
[
38
]
, by proving the same bound on the length of
a covering execution of minimal length. Here, there is a discrepancy
with the
n^{2^{\Omega(d)}}
lower bound on the length of that
execution in
Lipton 1976
’s construction—a discrepancy that
was already highlighted as an open problem in the early 1980’s
by
Mayr and Meyer 1982
, and settled in the specific case of reversible
systems by
Koppenhagen and Mayr 2000
. The upper bounds of
both
Bozzelli and Ganty 2011
and
Lazić and Schmitz 2021
on the complexity of the
backward coverability algorithm inherit from
Rackoff 1978
’s

n^{2^{O(d\lg d)}}
bound and suffer from the same discrepancy.

This was the situation until
Künnemann et al. 2023
showed an

n^{2^{O(d)}}
upper bound on the length of minimal covering
executions of unary encoded
d
-dimensional VAS,
matching
Lipton 1976
’s lower
bound
[
30
, Thm. 3.3]
. This directly translates into a
deterministic algorithm with the same upper bound on the running
time
[
30
, Cor. 3.4]
. Furthermore, assuming the
exponential time hypothesis,
Künnemann et al. 2023
also show that
there does not exist a deterministic
n^{o(2^{d})}
time algorithm
deciding coverability in unary encoded
d
-dimensional
VAS
[
30
, Thm. 4.2]
.

Thinness.

The improved upper bound relies on the notion
of a
thin
vector in
\mathbb{N}^{d}

[
30
, Def. 3.6]

(somewhat reminiscent of the “extractors” of
Leroux 2013
). The
proof of
[
30
, Thm. 3.3]
works by induction on the
dimension
d
. By splitting a covering execution of minimal length at
the first non-thin configuration,
Künnemann et al. 2023
obtain a
prefix made of distinct thin configurations (which must then be of
bounded length), and a suffix starting from a configuration with some
components high enough to be disregarded, hence that can be treated as
an execution in a VAS of lower dimension, on which the induction
hypothesis applies.

Contributions.

In this paper, we show that the improved

n^{2^{O(d)}}
upper bound of
Künnemann et al. 2023
also applies to the
number of iterations of the backward coverability algorithm for

d
-dimensional VAS encoded in unary (see
theorem

4.2
). In order to
do so, one could reuse the approach of
Bozzelli and Ganty 2011
to lift the
improved bound from the length of minimal covering executions to the

for the generality of the framework of
[
32
]
.

Our main contribution is thus to show in
section

3
that the

—which include those

3.6
) when focusing

), rather than an

We apply our results to the coverability problem in vector addition

—thus providing as promised an alternative

Bozzelli and Ganty 2011
’s approach
to
Künnemann et al. 2023
’s results—and show that the backward

(see

4.5
) and is therefore conditionally optimal

[
30
, Thm. 4.2]
.

As a further demonstration of the versatility of our results, we show

how to apply them to invertible affine nets, a

by
Benedikt et al. 2017
, and a good showcase for our techniques. We
obtain the same bounds for their coverability problem as in the case
of vector addition systems (see
theorems

4.11
and
4.12
), and thereby
improve on the
\mathsf{2EXPSPACE}
upper bound
of
[
7
]
by showing that the problem is
actually
\mathsf{EXPSPACE}
-complete (see

4.13
).
Along the way, we will see that the improved upper bounds also
apply for other VAS extensions, for which Rackoff’s proof scheme had
been successfully adapted (see

), namely

]
, branching
VAS
[
16
]
, and alternating VAS
[

]
.

Well-Quasi-Orders.

A

{\downarrow}S\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\{x\in X\mid\exists y\in S\mathbin{.}x\leq y\}

{\downarrow}y
for this set. A subset

if
S={\downarrow}S
.
A

(X,{\leq})

(1)

D_{0}\supsetneq D_{1}\supsetneq D_{2}\supsetneq\cdots

[

,

Conversely, the

of a subset

{\uparrow}S\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\{x\in X\mid\exists y\in S\mathbin{.}y\leq x\}

is

if

. The complement

of a downwards-closed set

is upwards-closed (and
conversely), hence wqos have the
ascending chain condition
for

of upwards-closed sets:

Furthermore, any upwards-closed set

[

,

]

Order Ideals.

An
order ideal
of
X
is a downwards-closed
subset
I\subseteq X
, which is
directed
: it is non-empty, and
if
x,x^{\prime}
are two elements of
I
, then there exists
y
in
I
with

x\leq y
and
x^{\prime}\leq y
. Alternatively, order ideals are characterised as
the
irreducible
non-empty downwards-closed sets of
X
: an
order ideal is a non-empty downwards-closed set
I
with the property that,
if
I\subseteq D_{1}\cup D_{2}
for two downwards-closed sets
D_{1}

and
D_{2}
, then
I\subseteq D_{1}
or
I\subseteq D_{2}
.

Over a wqo
(X,{\leq})
, any downwards-closed set

has a
canonical decomposition as a finite union of order ideals

D=I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{n}
, where the
I_{j}
’s are mutually incomparable
for inclusion
[
11
,
25
]
. We write

if
I

is an order ideal appearing in the canonical decomposition of
D
, i.e., if
it is a maximal order ideal included in
D
. Then
D\subseteq D^{\prime}
if and only if,
for all
I\in D
, there exists
I^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
such that
I\subseteq I^{\prime}
.

Main Result

In this section, we establish a new bound on the length of controlled

generalisation of the notion of

from

3.1.
Thinness

Fix a control function
g
, an initial size
n_{0}
, and a
dimension

(N_{i})_{0\leq i\leq d}
and lengths

\displaystyle N_{0}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}n_{0}\;,

\displaystyle N_{i+1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}g^{L_{i}+1}(n_{0})\;,

\displaystyle L_{0}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}0\;,

\displaystyle L_{i+1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}L_{i}+\prod_{1\leq j\leq i+1}\hskip-8.99994pt(d-j+1)(N_{j}+1)\;.

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Idl}^{\mathsf{thin}}(\mathbb{N}^{d})|

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{1\leq i\leq d}(L_{i}-L_{i-1})

\displaystyle=1+L_{d}-L_{0}=1+L_{d}\;.

∎

3.2.
Thinness Lemma

The crux of our result is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4

(Thinness)
.

Consider a
(g,n_{0})
-controlled strongly monotone descending
chain
D_{0}\supsetneq D_{1}\supsetneq\cdots
of downwards-closed
subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
. If
I_{\ell}
is a proper order ideal at some
step
\ell
, then
I_{\ell}
is thin and
\ell\leq L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}}
.

The proof of
lemma

3.4
proceeds by induction over the finite
dimension
\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}=d-\dim I_{\ell}
. For the base
case where
I_{\ell}
has full dimension
\dim I_{\ell}=d
, then
I_{\ell}=(\omega,\dots,\omega)

is thin and
D_{\ell}=\mathbb{N}^{d}
is the full space, which can only occur at
step
\ell=0=L_{0}
. For the induction step, we first establish
thinness with the following claim; note that, as just argued, an order
ideal of dimension
d
is necessarily thin. We then follow with the
bound on
\ell
to complete the
proof
of
lemma

3.4
.

Claim 3.5
.

Let
0\leq d^{\prime}<d
and assume
that
lemma

3.4
holds for all proper order ideals
I^{\prime}
of
dimension
\dim I^{\prime}>d^{\prime}
. If
I
is any (not necessarily proper) order ideal of dimension
\dim I=d^{\prime}
appearing as a maximal ideal in the descending chain
D_{0}\supsetneq D_{1}\supsetneq\cdots
, then
I
is thin.

Proof of
3.5
.

Let
k
be a step where
I
appears in the descending chain

D_{0}\supsetneq D_{1}\supsetneq\cdots
, i.e.,
I\in D_{k}
, and let us
write
I_{k}\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}I
.
If
k>0
, since
D_{k}\subseteq D_{k-1}
, there exists an order ideal

I_{k-1}\in D_{k-1}
such that
I_{k}\subseteq I_{k-1}
. If
k=0
, or
by repeating this argument if
k>0
, we obtain a chain of order
ideals (with decreasing indices)

(8)

I_{k}\subseteq I_{k-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I_{0}

where
I_{m}\in D_{m}
for all
k\geq m\geq 0
. Every order ideal in
that chain must have dimension at least
\dim I_{k}=d^{\prime}
since they all
contain
I_{k}
. Two cases arise.

(1)

If every order ideal in
the chain (
8
) has dimension
\dim I_{k}
,
then because the descending chain
D_{0}\supsetneq D_{1}\supsetneq\cdots
is
(g,n_{0})
-controlled, we have
\|I_{0}\|\leq n_{0}=N_{0}
and we know by
remark

3.1
that
I_{0}
is thin. Since

I_{k}\subseteq I_{0}
and
\dim I_{k}=\dim I_{0}
,
I_{k}
is also
thin.

(2)

Otherwise there exists a first index
K
along the
chain (
8
) where the dimension increases,
i.e., such that
\dim I_{k}<\dim I_{K}
and
\dim I_{m}=\dim I_{k}
for all

k\geq m>K
. Then
I_{K}
is proper, as otherwise
D_{K+1}
would
contain two distinct but comparable order ideals in its canonical
decomposition, namely
I_{K}
and
I_{K+1}
: indeed,

I_{K+1}\subseteq I_{K}
and
\dim I_{K+1}=\dim I_{k}<\dim I_{K}

imply
I_{K+1}\subsetneq I_{K}
. By
assumption,
lemma

3.4
can be applied to
I_{K}
of dimension

\dim I_{K}>\dim I_{k}=d^{\prime}
, thus
I_{K}
is thin and
K\leq L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{K}}
.

Let us now show that
I_{K+1}
is thin, which will also yield that

I_{k}
is thin since
I_{k}\subseteq I_{K+1}
and
\dim I_{k}=\dim I_{K+1}
.

Since
\dim I_{K+1}<\dim I_{K}
, we let
f\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\dim I_{K}-\dim I_{K+1}=\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{K+1}-\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{K}>0
. As furthermore

I_{K+1}\subseteq I_{K}
,
\omega(I_{K+1})\subsetneq\omega(I_{K})
and
we let

\{i_{1},\dots,i_{f}\}\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\omega(I_{K})\setminus\omega(I_{K+1})=\mathrm{fin}(I_{K+1})\setminus\mathrm{fin}(I_{K})
.

Since
I_{K}
is thin, there exists a bijection

\sigma\colon\mathrm{fin}(I_{K})\to\{1,\dots,\mathrm{fdim}\,(I_{K})\}
such that

I_{K}(i)\leq N_{\sigma(i)}
for all
i\in\mathrm{fin}(I_{K})
. We extend

\sigma
to a bijection

\sigma^{\prime}\colon\mathrm{fin}(I_{K})\uplus\{i_{1},\dots,i_{f}\}\to\{1,\dots,\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{K}+f\}
: we let
\sigma^{\prime}(i)\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\sigma(i)
for all

, and
\sigma^{\prime}(i_{j})\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{K}+j
for all

1\leq j\leq f
. Let us check that
\sigma^{\prime}
witnesses the
thinness of
I_{K+1}
.

•

Because
I_{K+1}\subseteq I_{K}
, for all those

,
I_{K+1}(i)\leq I_{K}(i)\leq N_{\sigma(i)}=N_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}
.

•

Since
K+1\leq L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{K}}+1
and since the descending
chain
D_{0}\supsetneq D_{1}\supsetneq\cdots
is

(g,n_{0})
-controlled, we have a bound of
g^{L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{K}}+1}(n_{0})=N_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{K}+1}
on all the finite components of

, and in particular
I_{K+1}(i_{j})\leq N_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{K}+1}

for all
1\leq j\leq f
. By
remark

, we conclude that

I_{K+1}(i_{j})\leq N_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{K}+j}=N_{\sigma^{\prime}(i_{j})}
for all

1\leq j\leq f
.∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.4
.

We have already argued for the base case, so let us turn to the
inductive step where
\dim I_{\ell}<d
. If
\ell>0
and since our
descending chain is strongly monotone, we can find an order ideal

I_{\ell-1}
proper at step
\ell-1
such that

\dim I_{\ell}\leq\dim I_{\ell-1}
. Both if
\ell=0

or by repeating this argument, we obtain a sequence of
order ideals (with decreasing indices)

(9)

I_{\ell},I_{\ell-1},\dots,I_{0}

where, for each
\ell>k\geq 0
,
I_{k}
is proper at step

, and
\dim I_{k+1}\leq\dim I_{k}
.

Let us decompose our sequence (

) by identifying the
first step

where
\dim I_{L+1}<\dim I_{L}
; let
L\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}-1
if
this never occurs. After this step, for all

,
\dim I_{k}>\dim I_{\ell}
. Within the initial segment, for
\ell\geq k>L
,
the dimension
\dim I_{k}
remains constant equal to
\dim I_{\ell}
,
and the induction hypothesis allows to apply
3.5
and infer
that every order ideal
I_{k}
in this initial segment, and in
particular
I_{\ell}
among them, is thin.

It remains to provide a bound on
\ell
. The
\ell-L

order ideals in the initial segment are thin, and distinct since
they are proper, hence by
remark

3.3
,

(10)

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle\leq L+\prod_{1\leq i\leq\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}}\hskip-8.99994pt(d-i+1)(N_{i}+1)\;.\hphantom{{}_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}}L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}}}\hskip 6.49994pt\hskip 16.99998pt

If
L\geq 0
:

we can apply the induction
hypothesis to the proper order ideal
I_{L}
of finite dimension
\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{L}<\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}
along with
remark

3.1
to yield
L\leq L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{L}}\leq L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}-1}
and therefore

(11)

\displaystyle{}\ell

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}-1}+\prod_{1\leq i\leq\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}}\hskip-8.99994pt(d-i+1)(N_{i}+1)=L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}}\;.

If
L=-1
:

then (
11
) also
holds since
L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{\ell}-1}\geq 0>L
in (

).∎

We deduce a general combinatorial statement on the length of
controlled strongly monotone descending chains, that generalises and
refines
[

, Thm. 4.4]
thanks to thinness.

Theorem 3.6

(Length function for strongly monotone descending chains)
.

Consider a
(g,n_{0})
-controlled strongly monotone descending
chain
D_{0}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq D_{\ell}
of downwards-closed
subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Then
\ell\leq L_{d}+1
.

Proof.

In such a descending chain, either
\ell=0\leq L_{d}+1
, or

\ell>0
and there must be an order ideal
I
proper at step
\ell-1
, and

I
has finite dimension at most
d
. By

lemmas

3.4
and
3.1
,
\ell-1\leq L_{\mathrm{fdim}\,I}\leq L_{d}
in that case.
∎

3.3.
Thin Order Ideals and Filters

Let us conclude this section with some consequences
of
lemma

3.4
and
3.5
. Whereas thinness was
posited
a priori
in the proof of

Künnemann et al. 2023
and then shown to indeed allow a
suitable decomposition of minimal covering executions and to
eventually prove their result, here in the descending chain setting
it is an inherent property of all the order ideals appearing in the
chain, thereby providing a “natural” explanation for thinness.

Corollary 3.7
.

Consider a
(g,n_{0})
-controlled strongly monotone descending
chain
D_{0}\supsetneq D_{1}\supsetneq\cdots
of downwards-closed
subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{d}

also entails a form of thinness of the minimal
configurations in the complement of the downwards-closed sets

D_{k}

B_{k}\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\min_{\sqsubseteq}\mathbb{N}^{d}\setminus D_{k}

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}\in B_{k}

(principal) order filter

{\uparrow}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}
of

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}

such that, for all

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}(i)\leq N_{\sigma(i)}+1

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}\in\bigcup_{k}B_{k}

4.1.
Coverability in Well-Structured Transition Systems

Well-structured transition systems (WSTS) form an abstract family of
computational models where the set of configurations is equipped with
a well-quasi-ordering “compatible” with the computation steps. This
wqo ensures the termination of generic algorithms checking some
important behavioural properties like coverability and termination.
While the idea can be traced back to the 1980’s
[
21
]
, this
framework has been especially popularised through two landmark
surveys
[
1
,
22
]
that emphasised its wide
applicability, and new WSTS models keep being invented in multiple
areas to this day.

4.1.1.
Well-Structured Transition Systems

A
well-structured transition system

(WSTS)
[
1
,
22
]
is a triple
(X,{\to},{\leq})

where
X
is a set of configurations,
{\to}\subseteq X\times X
is a
transition relation, and
(X,{\leq})
is a wqo with the following

compatibility
condition: if
x\leq x^{\prime}
and
x\to y
, then there
exists
y^{\prime}\geq y
with
x^{\prime}\to y^{\prime}
.

The coverability problem below corresponds to the verification of
safety properties, i.e., to checking that no bad configuration can
ever be reached from a given initial configuration
s\in X
. Here we
are given an error configuration
t\in X
, and we assume that any
configuration larger than
t
is also an error.

Problem

(Coverability in well-structured transition systems)
.

input:

a well-structured transition system
(X,{\to},{\leq})

and two configurations
s
and
t
in
X

question:

does
s

cover

t
, i.e., does
there exist
t^{\prime}\in X
such that
s\to^{\ast}t^{\prime}\geq t
?

4.1.2.
The Backward Coverability Algorithm

The first published version of this algorithm seems to date back
to
[
3
]
, where it was used to show the decidability of
coverability in vector addition systems extended with reset
capabilities, before it was rediscovered and generalised to
well-structured transition systems
[
1
]
.

The Algorithm.

Given an instance of the coverability problem, the
backward
coverability algorithm

[
3
,
1
,
22
]
computes (a finite
basis for) the upwards-closed set

(12)

U_{\ast}\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\{x\in X\mid\exists t^{\prime}\geq t\mathbin{.}x\to^{\ast}t^{\prime}\}

of all the
configurations that cover
t
, and then checks whether
s\in U_{\ast}
.

The set
U_{\ast}
itself is computed by letting

(13)

\displaystyle U_{0}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}{\uparrow}t\;,

\displaystyle U_{k+1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}U_{k}\cup\mathrm{Pre}_{\exists}(U_{k})\;,

where, for a set
S\subseteq X
,

\mathrm{Pre}_{\exists}(S)\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\{x\in X\mid\exists y\in S\mathbin{.}x\to y\}.

Then
U_{k}=\{x\in X\mid\exists t^{\prime}\geq t\mathbin{.}x\to^{\leq k}t^{\prime}\}
is the set of configurations that can
cover
t
in at most
k
steps.

Equation

13
defines a chain

of
upwards-closed subsets of
X
. Furthermore, if
U_{\ell}=U_{\ell+1}
at
some step, then we have reached stabilisation:

U_{\ell}=U_{\ell+k}=U_{\ast}
for all
k
. Thus we focus in this
algorithm on ascending chains
U_{0}\subsetneq U_{1}\subsetneq\cdots
,
which are finite thanks to the ascending chain condition of the
wqo
(X,{\leq})
. In order to turn (
13
) into an actual
algorithm, one needs to make some effectiveness assumptions on

(X,{\to},{\leq})
, typically that
\leq
is decidable and a finite
basis for
\mathrm{Pre}_{\exists}({\uparrow}x)
can be computed for all

x\in X

[
22
, Prop. 3.5]
.

A Dual View.

Lazić and Schmitz 2021
take a dual view of the algorithm
and define from (
13
) a descending chain
D_{0}\supsetneq D_{1}\supsetneq\cdots
of the same length where

(14)

D_{k}\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}X\setminus U_{k}

for each
k
; this stops with
D_{\ast}=X\setminus U_{\ast}
the
set of configurations that do
not
cover
t
. The entire computation
in (
13
) can be recast in this dual view, by setting

(15)

\displaystyle D_{0}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}X\setminus{\uparrow}t\;,

\displaystyle D_{k+1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}D_{k}\cap\mathrm{Pre}_{\forall}(D_{k})\;,

where, for a set
S\subseteq X
,

\mathrm{Pre}_{\forall}(S)\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\{x\in X\mid\forall y\in X\mathbin{.}(x\to y\implies y\in S)\}=X\setminus(\mathrm{Pre}_{\exists}(X\setminus S)).

Under some effectiveness assumptions, in particular for manipulating
ideal representations over
X
, this can be turned into an actual
algorithm
[
32
, Sec. 3.1]
.

\mathcal{N}

such that
I-\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle b$}}=A\cdot(I_{k+1}-\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}})
. For this to hold, note
that for all
i\in\mathrm{fin}(I)
, the
i
th row of
A
must be such that

A(i,j)=0
for all
j\in\omega(I_{k+1})
. As
A
is invertible,
those
(\mathrm{fdim}\,I)
-many rows must be linearly independent. As just
argued, the
j
th column for each of these rows is made of zeroes
whenever
j\in\omega(I_{k+1})
. Thus the remaining
(\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{k+1})
-many columns must make those
\mathrm{fdim}\,I
rows linearly
independent, hence necessarily
\mathrm{fdim}\,I_{k+1}\geq\mathrm{fdim}\,I
, i.e.,

\dim I_{k+1}\leq\dim I
.
∎

Observe that the proof of
proposition

4.10
does not work for the
transfer net
\mathcal{N}_{2}
of
example

4.6
:

{\footnotesize\left[\!\begin{array}[]{c}\omega\\
\omega\end{array}\!\right]}\leadsto_{\mathcal{N}_{2}}{\footnotesize\left[\!\begin{array}[]{c}\omega\\
0\end{array}\!\right]}
;
this is exactly the kind of non-monotone behaviour invertibility was
designed to prevent. Also observe that

{\footnotesize\left[\!\begin{array}[]{c}2\\
\omega\end{array}\!\right]}\leadsto_{\mathcal{N}_{3}}{\footnotesize\left[\!\begin{array}[]{c}\omega\\
4\end{array}\!\right]}

in the invertible affine net
\mathcal{N}_{3}
, which is not an

\omega
-monotone behaviour: this illustrates the usefulness of
capturing strongly monotone descending chains, as
[
32
, Thm. 4.4 and
Cor. 4.6]
do not apply.

Complexity Upper Bounds.

We are now equipped to analyse
the complexity of the backward coverability algorithm in invertible
affine nets. Regarding the length
\ell
of the chain constructed by
the algorithm, by
propositions

4.7
and
4.10
we are in the same
situation as in
theorem

4.2
and we can simply repeat the arguments
from its proof.

Theorem 4.11
.

The backward coverability algorithm terminates after at most

n^{2^{O(d)}}
iterations on
d
-dimensional invertible affine nets
encoded in unary when
d\geq 2
.

We deduce two corollaries from
theorem

4.11
: one pertaining to the
complexity of the backward coverability algorithm in
dimension
d
, which mirrors
corollary

4.5
, and one for the
coverability problem when
d
is part of the input. Let us start with the
backward coverability algorithm.

Corollary 4.12
.

The backward coverability algorithm runs in time

n^{2^{O(d)}}
on
d
-dimensional invertible affine nets encoded
in unary when
d\geq 2
.

Proof.

Theorem

4.11
shows that the length
\ell
of the ascending chain

U_{0}\subsetneq U_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq U_{\ell}=U_{\ast}
constructed by
the backward coverability algorithm is at most
L_{d}+1
, which
is in
n^{2^{O(d)}}
.

Let
B_{k}\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\min_{\sqsubseteq}U_{k}
denote the minimal basis at
step
k
. In order to compute
B_{k+1}
as per (
13
), thanks
to
4.8
, we could essentially argue as in the proof
of
corollary

4.5
, with the caveat that computing bluntly

\min_{\sqsubseteq}\mathrm{Pre}_{\exists}({\uparrow}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}})
for each

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}\in B_{k}
is dangerously similar to a linear integer
programming question and will incur an additional cost.

Alternatively, recall from
eq.

16
that

\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathsf{thin+1}}(\mathbb{N}^{d})
, the set of order filters

{\uparrow}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}
such that
\textstyle v
is nearly thin, has
at most
n^{2^{O(d)}}
elements, and that

|B_{k}|\leq|\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathsf{thin+1}}(\mathbb{N}^{d})|

by
corollaries

3.7
and
3.8
. Thus in order to compute

B_{k+1}
one can enumerate the nearly thin vectors
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathsf{thin+1}}(\mathbb{N}^{d})
and check for each

(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}},A,\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle b$}})\in\mathcal{N}
such that
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}}

whether there exists
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}\in B_{k}
such that
A\cdot(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}^{\prime}-\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}})+\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle b$}}\sqsupseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}
. Each such check can be performed in
time polynomial in
\|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle v$}}^{\prime}\|\leq N_{d}+1=n\cdot(L_{d-1}+2)+1
,

n
,
d
, and
|B_{k}|\leq|\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathsf{thin+1}}(\mathbb{N}^{d})|
.
Thus the entire computation can be carried out in
n^{2^{O(d)}}
.

The same upper bound holds for the dual version of the backward
coverability algorithm. At each step
k
, by
corollary

3.7
, in
order to compute
D_{k+1}
one can enumerate the thin order ideals

I\in\mathrm{Idl}^{\mathsf{thin}}(\mathbb{N}^{d})
and check for each such
I

whether
I\subseteq D_{k}
and
I\subseteq\mathrm{Pre}_{\forall}(D_{k})
,
before removing the non-maximal ones. Note that

I\subseteq\mathrm{Pre}_{\forall}(D_{k})
if and only if

\mathrm{Post}_{\exists}(I)\subseteq D_{k}
, if and only if

\{A\cdot(I-\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}})+\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle b$}}\mid(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}},A,\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle b$}})\in\mathcal{N},I\sqsupseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}}\}\subseteq D_{k}
, which can be checked in
time polynomial in
\|I\|\leq N_{d}=n\cdot(L_{d-1}+2)
,
n
,
d
,
and
|D_{k}|\leq|\mathrm{Idl}^{\mathsf{thin}}(\mathbb{N}^{d})|\leq L_{d}+1

by
remark

3.3
. The entire computation can be performed in
time polynomial in
L_{d}
,
n
, and
d
, and this remains in

n^{2^{O(d)}}
.∎

As VAS are a particular case of invertible affine nets, the upper
bounds in
corollary

4.12
are optimal assuming the
exponential time hypothesis by
[
30
, Thm. 4.2]
.

Our last result concerns the complexity of coverability in invertible
affine nets when
d
is part of the input. Note that the arguments
leading to an algorithm working in space
O(d\lg(n\cdot\ell))
in the
VAS case
[
30
, Thm. 3.2]
—which are essentially the
same as those used to derive a
\mathsf{2EXPSPACE}
upper bound
for invertible affine nets in
[
7
, Thm. 6]
—do not work
here, as the configurations along an execution of an affine net can
grow exponentially with
\ell
.

Corollary 4.13
.

The coverability problem for invertible affine nets is

\mathsf{EXPSPACE}
-complete.

Proof.

The hardness for
\mathsf{EXPSPACE}
follows from the hardness
of the coverability problem for VAS
[
34
]
.

Regarding the upper bound, consider the execution of the classical
backward coverability algorithm as defined in
eq.

13
on an
invertible affine net
\mathcal{N}
with target configuration
\textstyle t
: this
is an ascending chain
U_{0}\subsetneq U_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq U_{\ell}
where
U_{\ell}=U_{\ell+1}=U_{\ast}
. The following
characterisation of coverability actually holds more generally in
WSTS.

Claim 4.14
.

In an affine net
\mathcal{N}
,
\textstyle s

covers
\textstyle t
if and only if there exists
\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell
and a sequence of
configurations
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{0},\dots,\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{\ell^{\prime}}
, called
a
coverability pseudo-witness
, satisfying

(17)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{0}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}\;,

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k+1}

\displaystyle\in\min_{\sqsubseteq}\mathrm{Pre}_{\exists}({\uparrow}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k})\;,

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\sqsubseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}\;.

Proof of
4.14
.

If a coverability pseudo-witness exists, then we claim that for all

\ell^{\prime}\geq k\geq 0
there exists
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}_{k}\sqsupseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k}
such
that
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}=\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}_{\ell^{\prime}}\to_{\mathcal{N}}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}_{\ell^{\prime}-1}\to_{\mathcal{N}}\cdots\to_{\mathcal{N}}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}_{k}
, and thus in particular
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}\to_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ast}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}_{0}\geq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{0}
for
k=0
. We can check this by
induction over
k
. For the base case
k=\ell^{\prime}
, define
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{\scalebox{.5}{{def}}}}}{{=}}$}}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}
. For the induction step
k
, since

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k+1}\in\mathrm{Pre}_{\exists}({\uparrow}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k})
there exists

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}^{\prime}_{k}\sqsupseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k}
such that
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k+1}\to_{\mathcal{N}}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}^{\prime}_{k}
; by WSTS compatibility and since
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}_{k+1}\sqsupseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k+1}
, there exists
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}_{k}\sqsupseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}^{\prime}_{k}
such that

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}_{k+1}\to_{\mathcal{N}}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}_{k}
.

Conversely, assume that
\textstyle s
covers
\textstyle t
in
\mathcal{N}
. Then

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}\in U_{\ell}
, and let
\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell
be the least index such
that
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}\in U_{\ell^{\prime}}
. Then either
\ell^{\prime}=0
, i.e.,
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}\sqsupseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}=\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{0}
and we are done, or
\ell^{\prime}>0
. Because

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}\in U_{\ell^{\prime}}
there must be
some
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{\ell^{\prime}}\in\min_{\sqsubseteq}U_{\ell^{\prime}}
with
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle s$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle s$}}\sqsupseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{\ell^{\prime}}
, and
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{\ell^{\prime}}\not\in U_{\ell^{\prime}-1}

as otherwise
\textstyle s
would be in

, contradicting the
minimality of
\ell^{\prime}
. In general, if we have
found a sequence
(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{j})_{\ell^{\prime}\geq j\geq k>0}

satisfying (
17
) until rank

that
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k}\in(\min_{\sqsubseteq}U_{k})\setminus U_{k-1}
,
then either

and
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{1}\in\min_{\sqsubseteq}\mathrm{Pre}_{\exists}({\uparrow}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{0})
by
definition of

and

in (

), or

and because

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k}\not\in U_{k-1}
, there exists
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k-1}\in\min_{\sqsubseteq}U_{k-1}
such that

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k}\in\min_{\sqsubseteq}\mathrm{Pre}_{\exists}({\uparrow}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k-1})
, and
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k-1}\not\in U_{k-2}
as otherwise we would
have
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k}
in

. Repeating this process yields a coverability
pseudo-witness.
∎

By
4.14
, a non-deterministic algorithm for
coverability can guess and check the existence of a coverability
pseudo-witness. By
theorem

4.11
, such a pseudo-witness has a length

\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell
in
n^{2^{O(d)}}
. Furthermore,
by
4.8
the components in each
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}_{k}
in such a
pseudo-witness are bounded by
\|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle t$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle t$}}\|+\|\mathcal{N}\|\cdot k\leq(\ell+1)\cdot n
, which is still in
n^{2^{O(d)}}
. Thus
exponential space suffices. Note that this also holds when we
assume the invertible affine net to be encoded in binary, by
substituting
2^{n}
for
n
in the bound
n^{2^{O(d)}}
.
∎

Remark 4.15

(Strictly increasing affine nets)
.

Strictly increasing affine nets
[
43
,
23
,
12
]
are
intuitively the affine nets devoid of any form of reset or transfer;
in
example

4.6
, only
\mathcal{N}_{1}
is strictly increasing. All the
results we have proven for invertible affine nets in this
section—namely in
theorems

4.11
,
4.12
and
4.13
—also hold for
strictly increasing affine nets, because the descending chains of
downwards-closed sets they generate when running the backward
coverability algorithm are

\omega
-monotone.

Claim 4.16
.

The descending chain
D_{0}\supsetneq D_{1}\supsetneq\cdots
defined by

eqs.

13
to
15
for a
d
-dimensional strictly increasing affine net
\mathcal{N}
and a target
vector
\textstyle t
is
\omega
-monotone.

Proof of
4.16
.

Let
I_{k+1}
be proper at step
k+1
. By
4.9
,
there exists an order ideal
I
and an order ideal
I_{k}
proper at
step
k
such that
I_{k+1}\leadsto_{\mathcal{N}}I\subseteq I_{k}
. Let us show
that
\omega(I_{k+1})\subseteq\omega(I)
; as

\omega(I)\subseteq\omega(I_{k})
because
I\subseteq I_{k}
, this will
yield the result.

Since
I_{k+1}\leadsto_{\mathcal{N}}I
, there exists
(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}},A,\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle b$}})
in
\mathcal{N}

such that
I_{k+1}\sqsupseteq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}}
and

.
Because
\mathcal{N}
is strictly increasing,
A=I_{d}+A^{\prime}
for some matrix

A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{d\times d}
, hence
I=I_{k+1}-\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}}+A^{\prime}\cdot(I_{k+1}-\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}})+\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle b$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle b$}}
. Thus
I\sqsupseteq(I_{k+1}-\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle a$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle a$}})
and therefore

\omega(I)\supseteq\omega(I_{k+1})
.
∎

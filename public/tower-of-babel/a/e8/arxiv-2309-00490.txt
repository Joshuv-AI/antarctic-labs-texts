Small weight codewords of projective geometric codes II

Abstract
The p p -ary linear code 𝒞 k ​ ( n , q ) \mathcal{C}_{k}\!\left(n,q\right) is defined as the row space of the incidence matrix A A of k k -spaces and points of PG ⁡ ( n , q ) \mathrm{PG}\!\left(n,q\right) . The expander mixing lemma

We will introduce a helpful tool for counting problems in finite geometry.
This lemma is situated in algebraic graph theory, but we can use it in the context of finite geometry without having to use graph theory terminology.
As far as we are aware, the earliest occurrence of the expander mixing lemma in the form in which we will use it was in the PhD thesis of Haemers, see e.g. the summary article of his thesis
[

(including the paragraph after the proof of Theorem 5.1 for the determination of the relevant eigenvalue).
For a statement of the expander mixing lemma more closely resembling the one that we will use, the reader may consult for instance

Recall that a

\displaystyle\left|e(S,T)-\frac{\theta_{j}}{\theta_{n}}\left|S\right|\left|T\right|\right|<\sqrt{q^{j}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{j}_{q}\left|S\right|\left|T\right|}.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{q^{j}}{q^{k-1}}\frac{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}_{q}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k-1}{j-1}_{q}}\frac{1}{\frac{\theta_{j}}{\theta_{j-1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{q^{j}}{q^{k-1}}\frac{q^{k}-1}{q^{j}-1}\frac{q^{j}-1}{q^{j+1}-1}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{q^{k+j}-q^{j}}{q^{k+j}-q^{k-1}}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\frac{q^{k+j}}{q^{k+j}-q^{k+j-2}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{q^{2}}{q^{2}-1}.

Next, we prove that

\frac{q^{k-1}}{\theta_{j-1}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k-1}{j-1}_{q}\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{\theta_{j}}>(q-1)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}.

Dividing both sides by
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k-1}{j-1}_{q}
, we need to prove that

\frac{q^{k-1}(q-1)}{q^{j}-1}\frac{q^{k+2}-1}{q^{j+1}-1}>(q-1)\frac{q^{k+1}-1}{q^{j+1}-1}\frac{q^{k}-1}{q^{j}-1}.

This is equivalent to

q^{k-1}(q^{k+2}-1)>(q^{k+1}-1)(q^{k}-1),

which is easy to check.

Combining this with
Equation

7
, this yields

\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)>\underbrace{2\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\frac{q^{2}}{q^{2}-1}}\right)}_{\eqqcolon C_{q}}q\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}

The expression
C_{q}
is increasing in
q
.
Since we assumed that
q\geqslant 8
, we get that
C_{q}\geqslant C_{8}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}
, the last inequality being checked by computer.
∎

For the induction step, we will again make use of
\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}
(see
2.5
).

Lemma 5.4
.

Consider a point
R
and a hyperplane
\Pi\not\ni R
.

(1)

For
i<j
,
\textnormal{\dn l}_{i}\circ\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}=\mathrm{proj}^{(i)}_{R,\Pi}\circ\textnormal{\dn l}_{i}
.

(2)

\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}
maps
\mathcal{K}_{j,k}(n,q)
to
\mathcal{K}_{j,k}(n-1,q)
.

Proof.

(1)
Take
v\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathcal{G}_{j}(n,q)}
.
Choose an
i
-space
\iota
in
\Pi
.
Then

\displaystyle\textnormal{\dn l}_{i}\left(\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}(v)\right)(\iota)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{j}(\Pi)\\
\iota\subset\lambda^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}(v)(\lambda^{\prime})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{j}(\Pi)\\
\iota\subset\lambda^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{G}_{j}(\left\langle R,\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle)}v(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{G}_{j}(n,q)}v(\lambda)\underbrace{\left|\left\{\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{j}(\Pi)\,:\,\iota\subset\lambda^{\prime},\,\lambda\subset\left\langle R,\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\}\right|}_{\eqqcolon f_{1}(\lambda)}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathrm{proj}^{(i)}_{R,\Pi}\left(\textnormal{\dn l}_{i}(v)\right)(\iota)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\iota^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{i}(\left\langle R,\iota\right\rangle)}\textnormal{\dn l}_{i}(v)(\iota^{\prime})=\sum_{\iota^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{i}(\left\langle R,\iota\right\rangle)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathcal{G}_{j}(n,q)\\
\iota^{\prime}\subset\lambda\end{subarray}}v(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{G}_{j}(n,q)}v(\lambda)\underbrace{|\left\{\iota^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{i}(\left\langle R,\iota\right\rangle)\,:\,\iota^{\prime}\subset\lambda\right\}|}_{\eqqcolon f_{2}(\lambda)}.

Thus, we need to prove that
f_{1}(\lambda)\equiv f_{2}(\lambda)\pmod{p}
, for every
j
-space
\lambda
.
Note that

f_{2}(\lambda)=|\mathcal{G}_{i}(\left\langle R,\iota\right\rangle\cap\lambda)|\equiv\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\dim\left(\left\langle R,\iota\right\rangle\cap\lambda\right)\geqslant i,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\pmod{p}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\dim\left(\left\langle R,\iota\right\rangle\cap\lambda\right)=\begin{cases}\dim\left(\iota\cap\lambda\right)+1&\text{if }R\in\lambda,\\
\dim\left(\iota\cap\lambda\right)&\text{if }R\notin\lambda.\end{cases}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle f_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=|\left\{\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{j}(\Pi)\,:\,\iota\subset\lambda^{\prime},\left\langle R,\lambda\right\rangle\cap\Pi\subseteq\lambda^{\prime}\right\}|

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\dim\left(\left\langle\iota,\left\langle R,\lambda\right\rangle\cap\Pi\right\rangle\right)\leqslant j,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\pmod{p}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\dim\left(\left\langle\iota,\left\langle R,\lambda\right\rangle\cap\Pi\right\rangle\right)=\dim\left(\left\langle\iota,R,\lambda\right\rangle\right)-1=\begin{cases}\dim\left(\left\langle\iota,\lambda\right\rangle\right)-1&\text{if }R\in\lambda,\\
\dim\left(\left\langle\iota,\lambda\right\rangle\right)&\text{if }R\notin\lambda.\end{cases}

Thus, we need to prove that

\begin{cases}\dim\left(\iota\cap\lambda\right)+1\geqslant i\iff\dim\left(\left\langle\iota,\lambda\right\rangle\right)-1\leqslant j&\text{if }R\in\lambda,\\
\dim\left(\iota\cap\lambda\right)\geqslant i\iff\dim\left(\left\langle\iota,\lambda\right\rangle\right)\leqslant j&\text{if }R\notin\lambda.\end{cases}

This follows in both cases from Grassmann’s identity:

\dim\left(\iota\cap\lambda\right)+\dim\left(\left\langle\iota,\lambda\right\rangle\right)=i+j.

(2) It follows directly from (1) that
\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}
maps
\ker(\textnormal{\dn l}_{j-1})
to
\ker(\textnormal{\dn l}_{j-1})
.
By
2.6
(1),
\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}
also maps
\mathcal{C}_{j,k}\!\left(n,q\right)
to
\mathcal{C}_{j,k}\!\left(n-1,q\right)
.
The statement follows.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose that
q\geqslant 8
and
j>0
.
If
c\in\mathcal{K}_{j,k}(n,q)\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
, then

\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)>\frac{1}{2}q\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}.

Proof.

We prove this proposition by induction on
n
.
The base case
n=k+1
was dealt with in
5.3
.
So assume that
n\geqslant k+2
and suppose that the proposition holds for
\mathcal{K}_{j,k}(n-1,q)
.
Take a non-zero codeword
c\in\mathcal{K}_{j,k}(n,q)
and a
j
-space
\lambda\in\mathrm{supp}\!\left(c\right)
.
We again denote
\mathrm{supp}_{0}\!\left(c\right)
by
S
.

First, suppose that there exists a
(j+1)
-space
\sigma
through
\lambda
that contains no other
j
-space of
\mathrm{supp}\!\left(c\right)
and contains a point
R\notin S
.
Choose a hyperplane
\Pi
intersecting
\sigma
in
\lambda
.
By
5.4
(2),
\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}(c)
is a codeword of
\mathcal{K}_{j,k}(n-1,q)
.
Moreover, since
\lambda
is the only
j
-space of
\mathrm{supp}\!\left(c\right)
in
\sigma
,
\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}(c)(\lambda)=c(\lambda)\neq 0
.
In particular, this means that
\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}(c)\neq\bm{0}
.
Using
2.6
(2) and the induction hypothesis, this implies that

\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)\geqslant\mathrm{wt}\!\left(\mathrm{proj}^{(j)}_{R,\Pi}(c)\right)>\frac{1}{2}q\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}.

Now suppose that every
(j+1)
-space through
\lambda
contains either another
j
-space of
\mathrm{supp}\!\left(c\right)
or contains no points outside of
S
.
Then every
(j+1)
-space through
\lambda
contains at least
q^{j}
points of
S\setminus\lambda
.
Therefore,

|S|\geqslant\theta_{j}+\theta_{n-j-1}q^{j}>\theta_{n-1}\geqslant\theta_{k+1}.

As in the proof of
5.3
, we have that

\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)\geqslant 2\frac{q^{k-1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k-1}{j-1}_{q}}{\theta_{j-1}\theta_{j}}|S|\geqslant 2\frac{q^{k-1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k-1}{j-1}_{q}}{\theta_{j-1}\theta_{j}}\theta_{k+1}.

It suffices to check that the right-hand side of the above inequality is greater than
\frac{1}{2}q\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}
.
This is equivalent to

2q^{k-1}\left(q^{k+2}-1\right)(q-1)>\frac{1}{2}q\left(q^{k+1}-1\right)\left(q^{k}-1\right).

Since
q^{k+2}-1>q\left(q^{k+1}-1\right)
, this follows from

2q^{k-1}(q-1)>\frac{1}{2}\left(q^{k}-1\right),

which can be easily checked to hold for every
q\geqslant 2
.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Suppose that
q\geqslant 8
and suppose that
C_{q}\leqslant\frac{1}{4}q
is a constant such that every codeword
c\in\mathcal{C}_{j-1,k}(n,q)
with
\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)\leqslant C_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j}_{q}
is a linear combination of exactly
\left\lceil\frac{\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j}_{q}}\right\rceil

k
-spaces.
Then every codeword
c\in\mathcal{C}_{j,k}(n,q)
with
\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)\leqslant C_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}
is a linear combination of exactly
\left\lceil\frac{\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}}\right\rceil

k
-spaces.

Proof.

Suppose that the condition of the proposition holds for
\mathcal{C}_{j-1,k}(n,q)
.
Take a codeword
c\in\mathcal{C}_{j,k}(n,q)
with
\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)\leqslant C_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}
.
Consider
c^{\prime}\coloneqq\textnormal{\dn l}_{j-1}(c)
.
Perform a double count on

\left\{(\iota,\lambda)\in\mathrm{supp}\!\left(c^{\prime}\right)\times\mathrm{supp}\!\left(c\right)\,:\,\iota\subset\lambda\right\}.

If
\iota\in\mathrm{supp}\!\left(c^{\prime}\right)
, then
\iota
is incident with at least
\theta_{k-j}

j
-spaces of
\mathrm{supp}\!\left(c\right)
by
5.2
(5).
Hence,

\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c^{\prime}\right)\leqslant\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)\frac{\theta_{j}}{\theta_{k-j}}\leqslant C_{q}\frac{q^{j+1}-1}{q^{k-j+1}-1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}=C_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j}_{q}.

By our hypothesis,
c^{\prime}
is a linear combination of at most
C_{q}

k
-spaces, i.e.

c^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{C_{q}}\alpha_{i}\kappa_{i}^{(j-1)},

for some scalars
\alpha_{i}
and
k
-spaces
\kappa_{i}
.
Then

c^{\prime}=\textnormal{\dn l}_{j-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{C_{q}}\alpha_{i}\kappa_{i}^{(j)}\right)

by
5.2
(1,2).

This implies that
c^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq c-\sum_{i=1}^{C_{q}}\alpha_{i}\kappa_{i}^{(j)}
is contained in
\mathcal{K}_{j,k}(n,q)
.
Since
c^{\prime\prime}
is equal to the difference of two functions, each of weight at most
C_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}
, we find that
\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c^{\prime\prime}\right)\leqslant 2C_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}q\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}
.
By
5.5
, this means that
c^{\prime\prime}=\bm{0}
, hence
c
is a linear combination of at most
C_{q}
characteristic functions of
k
-spaces.
By
2.3
(1),
c
is a linear combination of exactly
\left\lceil\mathrm{wt}\!\left(c\right)/\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}_{q}\right\rceil

k
-spaces.
∎

Stable recovery of a metric tensor from the partial hyperbolic Dirichlet to Neumann map
Mourad Bellassoued

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the inverse problem of determining on a compact Riemannian manifold the metric tensor in the wave equation with Dirichlet data from measured Neumann boundary observations. This information is enclosed in the dynamical Dirichlet-to-Neumann map associated to the wave equation. We prove in dimension n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 that the knowledge of the partial Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for the wave equation uniquely determines the metric tensor and we establish logarithm-type stability.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Statement of the problem

Let
\Omega
be a bounded and connected domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, with smooth boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
. Assume that
\mathrm{g}=(\mathrm{g}_{jk})
is given Riemannian metric in
\Omega
, with symmetric and smooth coefficients
\mathrm{g}_{jk}\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(\Omega)
,
k\geq 2
, and

(1.1)

\mathrm{g}(x)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\mathrm{g}_{jk}(x)dx_{j}\otimes dx_{k}.

We let
\Delta_{\mathrm{g}}
denote the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Omega
, given by

(1.2)

\Delta_{\mathrm{g}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathrm{g}|}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\sqrt{|\mathrm{g}|}\,\mathrm{g}^{jk}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\right).

Here
(\mathrm{g}^{jk})
is the inverse of the metric
\mathrm{g}
and
|\mathrm{g}|=\det(\mathrm{g}_{jk})
. A summary of the main Riemannian geometric notions needed in this paper is provided in Section 2. Given
T>0
, we denote
Q=(0,T)\times\Omega
and
\Sigma=(0,T)\times\Gamma
. We consider the
following initial boundary value problem for the wave equation,

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\left(\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta_{\mathrm{g}}\right)u=0&\textrm{in }\;Q,\cr u(0,\cdot)=\partial_{t}u(0,\cdot)=0&\textrm{in }\;\Omega,\cr u=f&\textrm{on }\;\Sigma.\end{array}\right.

The purpose of this paper is to study the inverse problem of determining the metric tensor
\mathrm{g}
appearing in the wave equation (
1.3
).
Before going to the main context of the paper, let us briefly mention about the well-posedness of the forward problem (
1.3
). Following
[
10
]
, we define the space
H^{1,1}_{0}(\Sigma)
by

(1.4)

H^{1,1}_{0}(\Sigma)=\left\{f\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Gamma))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma)),\,\textrm{and}\,\,f(0,x)=0,\,\textrm{for}\,x\in\Gamma\right\}.

As shown in
[
10
]
that for
f\in H^{1,1}_{0}(\Sigma)
, system (
1.3
) admits a unique solution

(1.5)

u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))\cap\mathcal{C}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega)).

Furthermore, there is a constant
C>0
such that

(1.6)

\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1,1}(\Sigma)}.

The normal derivative is given by

(1.7)

\partial_{\nu}u:=\left<\nabla u,\nu\right>=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\mathrm{g}^{jk}\nu_{j}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{k}}

where
\nu
is the unit outward vector field to
\Gamma
. In particular the following operator, usually called the Dirichlet to Neumann map

(1.8)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}:H_{0}^{1,1}(\Sigma)&\longrightarrow&L^{2}(\Sigma)\\
f&\longmapsto&\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}(f):=\partial_{\nu}u,\end{array}

is bounded from
H_{0}^{1,1}(\Sigma)
into
L^{2}(\Sigma)
.

It’s known that the problem of finding a Riemannian metric is interesting for theory and applications. For example, the problem of finding a metric from the corresponding Dirichlet to Neumann map is the basic question of the investigation of inverse problems for partial differential equations and there are many applications of this problem, particularly, in geophysics in connection with the study of distribution of the velocities of propagation of elastic waves inside the terrestrial globe. ln the study of the problem of restoring a Riemannian metric a new type problem of integral geometry appears (geodesic ray transform). It is known that the integral geometry problem is the mathematical base of tomography. This problem has many applications in various fields: the problem of the forecasting of earthquakes, diagnostics of plasma, problem of photometry, fiber optics and etc. The connection of the problem of integral geometry for differential forms with the inverse problems for equations of the hyperbolic type and kinetic equations are described in the works
[
1
]
,
[
31
]
.

An inverse problem in this setting is whether one can reconstruct the Riemannian metric
\mathrm{g}
in
\Omega
by the knowledge of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}
. There is a well known non-uniqueness for this inverse problem. For it we let
\psi:\bar{\Omega}\to\bar{\Omega}
be a diffeomorphism with
\psi=\textrm{id}
on
\Gamma
, then
\Lambda_{\psi^{*}\mathrm{g}}=\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}
, where
\psi^{*}\mathrm{g}
denote the pull back of the metric
\mathrm{g}
by the diffeomorphism
\psi
.

\psi^{*}\mathrm{g}={}^{t}\psi^{\prime}\cdot(\mathrm{g}\circ\psi)\cdot\psi^{\prime}

here
\psi^{\prime}
denotes the (matrix) differential of the smooth function
\psi
,
{}^{t}\psi^{\prime}
its transpose, and the
\cdot
represents multiplication of matrices. An interesting problem is to see if this is the only obstruction for uniqueness, i.e., if
\mathrm{g}_{1}
and
\mathrm{g}_{2}
be two Riemannian metrics on
\Omega
such that
\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}_{1}}=\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}_{2}}
, then there exists a diffeomorphism
\psi:\bar{\Omega}\to\bar{\Omega}
with
\psi=\textrm{id}
on
\Gamma
such that
\Lambda_{\psi^{*}\mathrm{g}}=\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}
. A Riemannian manifold
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
is said to be uniquely determined up to isometry, if this is the only obstruction to unique identifiability of the metric. The Dirichlet to Neumann map can be related to the geodesic distance
d_{\mathrm{g}}
which measures the travel times of geodesics joining points of the
boundary. In the case that both
\mathrm{g}_{1}
and
\mathrm{g}_{2}
are conformal to a
simple
metric
\mathrm{g}
(i.e.,
\mathrm{g}_{k}=c_{k}\mathrm{g}
), then this problem is known in seismology as the inverse kinematic problem. In this case, it has been proven, by Bellassoued and Dos Santos Ferriera
[
10
]
, under further restrictions on the metrics that if
\Lambda_{c\mathrm{g}}=\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}
, then
c=1
. In this case the diffeomorphism
\psi
must be the identity.
The questions naturally arise:

(a)

are there other types of the non-uniqueness of solution of this inverse problem?

(b)

when is a metric tensor determined by its Dirichlet to Neumann map up to isometry, identical on?

(c)

for what classes of metrics the Dirichlet to Neumann map determines a metric uniquely (
\varphi
must be the identity)?

Inverse problems with partial boundary data are encountered in mathematical physics and in various applications. For example in medical imaging and in the geophysical imaging of the Earth, measurements can usually be done only for a part of the boundary. In this paper, we will analyze the problem of finding in the wave equation (
1.3
) a Riemannian metric
\mathrm{g}
in the case when some of the Dirichlet- to Neumann map is known only in arbitrary relatively open part
\Gamma^{\natural}\subset\Gamma
. So that we consider non-empty relatively open subset
\Gamma^{\natural}
of
\Gamma
. Then we set
\Sigma^{\natural}=(0,T)\times\Gamma^{\natural}
and we introduce the
partial
Dirichlet to Neumann map

(1.9)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}^{\natural}:H_{0}^{1,1}(\Sigma)&\longrightarrow&L^{2}(\Sigma^{\natural})\\
f&\longmapsto&\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}^{\natural}(f):=\partial_{\nu}u_{|\Sigma^{\natural}},\end{array}

we observe that since
\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}
is bounded,
\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}^{\natural}
is also bounded from
H_{0}^{1,1}(\Sigma)
into
L^{2}(\Sigma^{\natural})
.

The main achievement of this paper is that the Neumann data used in this stability estimate can be measured on the sub-part
\Gamma^{\natural}
of the whole boundary, where we recall that
\Gamma^{\natural}
an arbitrary non-empty relative open subset of
\Gamma
. Here questions connected with the uniqueness and stability estimates of a solution to the problem in question will be discussed.

1.2.
Previous literature

The recovery of coefficients found in hyperbolic equations is a topic that has drawn significant interest. Several authors have treated the determination of coefficients from the Dirichlet to Neumann map analogous to
\Lambda_{\mathrm{g}}
above. Unique determination of the metric goes back to Belishev and Kurylev
[
5
]
using the boundary control method and involves works of Katchlov, Kurylev and Lassas
[
23
]
, Kurylev and Lassas
[
24
]
, Lassas and Oksanen
[
26

and Anderson, Katchalov, Kurylev, Lassas and Taylor
[
3

[
25
]
. The importance of control theory for inverse problems was first understood by Belishev
[
4
]
. He used control theory to develop the first variant of the control (BC) method. This method gives an efficient way to reconstruct a Riemannian manifold via its response operator (dynamical Dirichlet-to-Neumann map) or spectral data (a spectrum of the Beltrami-Laplace operator and traces of normal derivatives of the eigenfunctions), themselves, whereas the coefficients on these manifolds are recovered automatically.

The problem of establishing stability estimates in determining the metric was studied by Stefanov and Uhlmann in
[
35
,
32
]
for metrics close to Euclidean and generic simple metrics. Hölder type of conditional stability estimate was proven in
[

for metrics close enough to the Euclidean one in three dimensions. In
[
35

is simple, if

is strictly convex and any two point in

can be connected by a single minimizing geodesic depending smoothly on them, see Definition

Montalto consider the stability of the inverse problem of determining a simple Riemannian metric together with the lower order coefficients of the second order hyperbolic initial-boundary value problem, from the information that is encoded in the global hyperbolic Dirichlet-to-Neumann map and prove Hölder type stability estimates near generic simple Riemannian metrics for the inverse problem of recovering simultaneously the metric and the electro-magnetic potentials. In
[
10

, the author and Dos Santos Ferriera proved stability estimates for the wave equation in determining a conformal factor close to
1
and time independent potentials in simple geometries. We refer to this paper for a longer bibliography in the case of the wave equation. In
[
27

Liu and Oksanen consider the problem to reconstruct a wave speed

from acoustic boundary measurements modelled by the hyperbolic Dirichlet to Neumann map. They introduced a reconstruction formula for

The cited results in above on this problem require that the corresponding Dirichlet to Neumann map be at least measured on a sufficiently large part of the boundary of the spatial domain under consideration, if not on the whole boundary itself. However the stability by a local Dirichlet-to-Neumann map is not discussed comprehensively. In
[
26

For the Dirichlet to Neumann map for an elliptic equation, the paper

Admissible manifolds

We start by defining the Riemannian metrics in which we will give the stability results of this paper.
Let
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
be a Riemannian manifold with boundary

is clearly geodesically convex with respect to

and

which lies entirely in

. We denote by

S\Omega=\left\{(x,\xi)\in T\Omega;\,|\xi|_{\mathrm{g}}=1\right\},

the sphere bundle of
\Omega
. Let

, there exist a unique geodesic
\gamma_{x,\xi}
associated to

which is maxmimally defined on a finite interval

, with
\gamma_{x,\xi}(\tau_{\pm}(x,\xi))\in\Gamma
. We define the geodesic flow
\Phi_{t}
as following

\Phi_{t}:S\Omega\to S\Omega,\quad\Phi_{t}(x,\xi)=(\gamma_{x,\xi}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{x,\xi}(t)),\quad t\in[\tau_{-}(x,\xi),\tau_{+}(x,\xi)],

and

is a flow, that is,
\Phi_{t}\circ\Phi_{s}=\Phi_{t+s}

\partial_{\pm}S\Omega=\left\{(x,\xi)\in S\Omega,\,x\in\Gamma,\,\pm\left<\xi,\nu(x)\right>>0\right\}.

For

Now we can define the length and the scattering relation

(x,\xi)&\longmapsto&\Phi_{\tau_{\mathrm{g}}^{+}}(x,\xi),\end{array}

(x,\xi)

\Omega

For

Let

\nabla f
such that, in local coordinates, we have

(2.1)

\nabla_{\mathrm{g}}f=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathrm{g}^{ij}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{i}}\partial_{j}.

For a smooth manifold
\Omega^{*}
let
\psi:\Omega^{*}\to\Omega
be a smooth map from
\Omega^{*}
into
\Omega
. The pull back of the metric
\mathrm{g}
is defined by

(\psi^{*}\mathrm{g})(X,Y)=\mathrm{g}(d\psi(X),d\psi(Y)),

for any vector field
X,Y
on
\Omega
.

The metric tensor
\mathrm{g}
induces the Riemannian volume
\,\mathrm{dv}^{n}_{\mathrm{g}}=\sqrt{|\mathrm{g}|}\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{n}
. We denote by
L^{2}(\Omega)
the completion
of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the usual inner product

\left<u,v\right>=\int_{\Omega}u(x)\overline{v(x)}\,\mathrm{dv}^{n}_{\mathrm{g}},\qquad u,v\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\Omega).

The Sobolev space
H^{1}(\Omega)
is the completion of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
,

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Moreover, using covariant derivatives, it is possible to define coordinate invariant norms in
H^{k}(\Omega)
,
k\geq 0
.

Denote by
\textrm{div}X
the divergence of a vector field
X\in H^{1}(\Omega,T\Omega)
on
\Omega
, i.e. in local coordinates,

(2.2)

\textrm{div}X=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathrm{g}|}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\left(\sqrt{|\mathrm{g}|}\,X^{i}\right),\quad X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X^{i}\partial_{i}.

If
X\in H^{1}(\Omega,T\Omega)
and
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, the Green’s formula reads

(2.3)

\int_{\Omega}\textrm{div}X\,f\,\mathrm{dv}^{n}=-\int_{\Omega}\left<X,\nabla f\right>\,\mathrm{dv}^{n}+\int_{\Gamma}\left<X,\nu\right>f\,\mathrm{d\sigma}^{n-1}.

where
\,\mathrm{d\sigma}^{n-1}
is the volume form of
\Gamma
. Then if
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
w\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, the following identity holds

(2.4)

\int_{\Omega}\Delta wf\,\mathrm{dv}^{n}=-\int_{\Omega}\left<\nabla w,\nabla f\right>\,\mathrm{dv}^{n}+\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{\nu}wf\,\mathrm{d\sigma}^{n-1}.

The Riemannian scalar product on
T_{x}\Omega
induces the volume form on
S_{x}\Omega
, denoted by
\mathrm{d}\omega_{x}(\theta)
and given by

(2.5)

\mathrm{d}\omega_{x}(\theta)=\sqrt{|\mathrm{g}|}\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\theta^{k}\mathrm{d}\theta^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\mathrm{d}\theta^{k}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}\theta^{n}.

As usual, the notation
\,\widehat{\cdot}\,
means that the corresponding factor has been dropped.
We introduce the volume form
\,\mathrm{dv}^{2n-1}
on the manifold
S\Omega
by

\,\mathrm{dv}^{2n-1}(x,\theta)=\mathrm{d}\omega_{x}(\theta)\wedge\,\mathrm{dv}^{n}.

By Liouville’s theorem, the form
\,\mathrm{dv}^{2n-1}
is preserved by the geodesic flow.
Now, we recall the submanifolds of inner and outer vectors of
S\Omega
given by

(2.6)

\partial_{\pm}S\Omega=\left\{(x,\theta)\in S\Omega,\,x\in\Gamma,\,\pm\left<\theta,\nu(x)\right>>0\right\}.

Note that
\partial_{+}S\Omega
and
\partial_{-}S\Omega
are compact manifolds with the same boundary
S\Gamma
, and
\partial S\Omega=\partial_{+}S\Omega\cup\partial_{-}S\Omega
. We denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\Omega)
be the space of smooth functions on the manifold
\partial_{+}S\Omega
. The corresponding volume form on the boundary
\partial S\Omega=\left\{(x,\theta)\in S\Omega,\,x\in\Gamma\right\}
is given by

\,\mathrm{d\sigma}^{2n-2}=\mathrm{d}\omega_{x}(\theta)\wedge\,\mathrm{d\sigma}^{n-1}.

Let
L^{2}_{\mu}(\partial_{+}S\Omega)
be the space of square integrable functions with respect to the measure
\mu(x,\theta)\,\mathrm{d\sigma}^{2n-2}
with

\mu(x,\theta)=|\left<\theta,\nu(x)\right>|
. This Hilbert space is endowed with the scalar product

(2.7)

\left(u,v\right)_{\mu}=\int_{\partial_{+}S\Omega}u(x,\theta)\overline{v}(x,\theta)\mu(x,\theta)\,\mathrm{d\sigma}^{2n-2}.

2.2.
Geodesic ray transform of 2-symmetric tensor

We denote by
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection on
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
. For a point
x\in\Gamma
, we define the second quadratic form of the boundary on the space
T_{x}\Gamma
by

\Pi(\xi,\xi)=\left<\nabla_{\xi}\nu,\xi\right>,\quad\xi\in T_{x}\Gamma.

We say that the boundary is strictly convex if the form is positive-definite for all
x\in\Gamma
.

For
x\in\Omega
and
\xi\in T_{x}\Omega
we denote by
\gamma_{x,\xi}
the unique geodesic starting at the point
x
in the direction
\xi
. The exponential map
\exp_{x}:T_{x}\Omega\longrightarrow\Omega
is given by

(2.8)

\exp_{x}(v)=\gamma_{x,\xi}(|v|),\quad\xi=\frac{v\,\,}{|v|}.

The Riemannian manifold
(\Omega,\,\mathrm{g})
is called a non-trapping manifold, if for each
(x,\theta)\in S\Omega
, the maximal geodesic
\gamma_{x,\theta}(t)
satisfying the initial conditions
\gamma_{x,\theta}(0)=x
and
\dot{\gamma}_{x,\theta}(0)=\theta
is defined on a finite segment
[\tau_{-}(x,\theta),\tau_{+}(x,\theta)]
. An important subclass of convex non-trapping manifolds are simple manifolds.

Definition 2.1
.

(Simple manifold)
We say that the Riemannian manifold
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
(or more shortly that the metric
\mathrm{g}
) is simple, if
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
is
strictly convex with respect to
\mathrm{g}
, and for any
x\in\Omega
, the exponential map

\exp_{x}:\exp_{x}^{-1}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\Omega
is a diffeomorphism.

Any metric
\mathrm{g}
on
\Omega
so that
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
is simple will be called a simple metric on
\Omega
. The second condition above hides the requirement that any two points
x,y
in
\Omega
are connected by a unique geodesic in
\Omega
that depends smoothly on
x
,
y
. In particular, there are no conjugate points on any geodesic in
\Omega
. Note that if
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
is simple, one can extend
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
into another simple manifold
\Omega_{1}
such that
\Omega\Subset\Omega_{1}
.

Let
T^{m}_{x}\Omega
be the space of tensors fields of type
m
on
T_{x}\Omega
. We denote by
T^{m}\Omega
the tensor bundle of type
m
. In the local coordinate system a
m
-tensor field
\mathfrak{t}
can be written as

\mathfrak{t}=t_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}dx^{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes dx^{j_{m}}.

For each
x\in\Omega
,
T^{m}_{x}\Omega
is endowed with an inner product as follows

\left<\mathfrak{t}_{1},\mathfrak{t}_{2}\right>=\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=1}^{n}\mathfrak{t}_{1}(\partial_{j_{1}},\dots,\partial_{j_{m}})\mathfrak{t}_{2}(\partial_{j_{1}},\dots,\partial_{j_{m}}).

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\Omega,T^{m}\Omega)
the space of the smooth
m
-tensor fields on
\Omega
. We denote by
L^{2}(\Omega,T^{m}\Omega)
the space of square integrable
m
-tensors fields on
\Omega
as the completion of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\Omega,T^{m}\Omega)
endowed with the following inner product

\left(\mathfrak{t}_{1},\mathfrak{t}_{2}\right)=\int_{\Omega}\left<\mathfrak{t}_{1},\overline{\mathfrak{t}_{2}}\right>\,\mathrm{dv}^{n},\quad\mathfrak{t}_{1},\mathfrak{t}_{2}\in T^{m}\Omega.

We denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\textrm{sym}}^{\infty}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)
(resp.
L^{2}_{\textrm{sym}}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)
) the space of the smooth (resp. the space of square integrable) symmetric
2
-tensor fields on
\Omega
. For
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathcal{C}_{\textrm{sym}}^{\infty}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)
, we have

(2.9)

\mathfrak{s}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\mathrm{s}_{jk}dx^{j}\otimes dx^{k},\quad\mathrm{s}_{jk}=\mathrm{s}_{kj},\quad j,k=1,\ldots,n.

It is well known that for a
\mathrm{g}
smooth enough metric, each symmetric tensor
\mathfrak{s}\in L^{2}_{\textrm{sym}}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)
admits unique orthogonal decomposition

(2.10)

\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{s}^{\textrm{sol}}+\nabla_{\textrm{sym}}\mathrm{v},

into a solnoidal tensor
\mathfrak{s}^{\textrm{sol}}\in L^{2}_{\textrm{sym}}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)
, i.e.,
\delta\mathfrak{s}^{\textrm{sol}}=0
, and a potential tensor
\nabla_{\textrm{sym}}\mathrm{v}
, where
\mathrm{v}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, with

(2.11)

\nabla_{\textrm{sym}}\mathrm{v}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{j}\mathrm{v}_{k}+\nabla_{k}\mathrm{v}_{j}\right),\quad 1\leq j,k,\leq n,

and
\nabla_{j}
is the covariant derivative in metric
\mathrm{g}
.

The ray transform of symmetric
2
-tensor on a simple Riemannian manifold
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
is the linear operator:

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}:\mathcal{C}_{\textrm{sym}}^{\infty}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\partial_{+}S\Omega)

defined by

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}(\mathfrak{s})(x,\theta)=\int_{\gamma_{x,\theta}}f=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\tau_{+}(x,\theta)}\mathrm{s}_{jk}(\gamma_{x,\theta}(t))\dot{\gamma}^{j}_{x,\theta}(t)\dot{\gamma}^{k}_{x,\theta}(t)dt,

where
\gamma_{x,\theta}:[0,\tau_{+}(x,\theta)]\to\Omega
is a maximal geodesic satisfying the initial conditions
\gamma_{x,\theta}(0)=x
and
\dot{\gamma}_{x,\theta}(0)=\theta
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}(\nabla_{\textrm{sym}}\mathrm{v})=0
for any smooth vector field
\mathrm{v}
in
\Omega
with
\mathrm{v}=0
on
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.2
.

(
s
-injective) Let
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
be a simple Riemannian manifold. We say that
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}
is
s
-injective in
\Omega
, if
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}\mathfrak{s}=0
and
\mathfrak{s}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
imply
\mathfrak{s}=\nabla_{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{v}
with some vector field
\mathrm{v}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
\mathrm{v}=0
on
\Gamma
.

In other words, if
\mathfrak{s}\in H^{1}_{\textrm{sym}}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)
and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}(\mathfrak{s})=0
implies
\mathfrak{s}^{\textrm{sol}}=0
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{s}=\nabla_{\textrm{sym}}\mathrm{v}
with some vector field
\mathrm{v}
vanishing on
\Gamma
. So we have

(2.12)

|\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}(\mathfrak{s})(x,\theta)|=|\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}(\mathfrak{s}^{\textrm{sol}})(x,\theta)|\leq C\|\mathfrak{s}^{\textrm{sol}}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}},\quad\mathfrak{s}\in\mathcal{C}_{\textrm{sym}}^{0}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega).

The ray transform
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}
of symmetric
2
-tensor on a simple Riemannian manifold is a bounded operator from
L^{2}_{\textrm{sym}}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)
into
L^{2}_{\mu}(\partial_{+}S\Omega)
and can be extend to the bounded operator

(2.13)

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}:H_{\textrm{sym}}^{k}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)\longrightarrow H^{k}(\partial_{+}S\Omega).

Now, we recall some properties of the ray transform of symmetric
2
-tensors on a simple Riemannian manifold proved in
[
32
]
. Let
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
be a simple metric, we assume that
\mathrm{g}
extends smoothly as a simple metric on
\Omega_{1}\Supset\Omega
and let
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}^{*}:L^{2}_{\mu}(\partial_{+}S\Omega)\to L^{2}_{\textrm{sym}}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)
the adjoint of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}
. We denote
N_{\mathrm{g}}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}
. It is well known, see Stefanov Uhlamnn
[
32
]
, that there exists
k_{0}
such that for each
k\geq k_{0}
, the set
\mathcal{G}^{k}(\Omega)
of simple
\mathcal{C}^{k}(\Omega)
metrics in
\Omega
for which
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}
is s-injective is open and dense in the
\mathcal{C}^{k}(\Omega)
topology. Moreover, for any
\mathrm{g}\in\mathcal{G}^{k}(\Omega)
,

(2.14)

\|\mathfrak{s}^{\textrm{sol}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|N_{\mathrm{g}}(\mathfrak{s})\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{1})},

for any
\mathfrak{s}\in L^{2}_{\textrm{sym}}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)
, with a constant
C>0
that can be chosen locally uniform in
\mathcal{G}^{k}
in the
\mathcal{C}^{k}(\Omega)
topology.

If
\Omega^{\prime}
is an open set of the simple Riemannian manifold
(\Omega_{1},\mathrm{g})
, the normal operator
N_{\mathrm{g}}
is an elliptic pseudo-differential operator of order
-1
on
\Omega^{\prime}
. Therefore for each
k\geq 0
there exists a constant
C_{k}>0
such that for all
\mathfrak{s}\in H^{k}_{\textrm{sym}}(\Omega,T^{2}\Omega)
compactly supported in
\Omega^{\prime}

(2.15)

\|N_{\mathrm{g}}(\mathfrak{s})\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C_{k}\|\mathfrak{s}^{\textrm{sol}}\|_{H^{k}(\Omega^{\prime})}.

Since for
\mathrm{g}=e
,
\mathcal{I}_{e}
is
s
-injective, then for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and any metric tensor
\mathrm{g}
satisfy (
1.10
), we have
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
is simple and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{g}}
is
s
-injective.

2.3.
Change of coordinates

In this section we will construct a diffeomorphism
\psi
that fixes the boundary
\Gamma
. Let
(\Omega,\mathrm{g})
be an
n
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, compact Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary
\Gamma
, with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is convex, and bounded domain. We first assume throughout this section that
\mathrm{g}
satisfies (
1.10
). In view of (
1.10
), we can extend
\mathrm{g}
outside of
\Omega
, still denoted by
\mathrm{g}
, by setting
\mathrm{g}=e
on

\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\Omega
so that
\mathrm{g}\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(2.16)

\|\mathrm{g}-e\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\varepsilon.

The Hamiltonian related to
\mathrm{g}
, is

(2.17)

H(x,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\xi|^{2}_{\mathrm{g}}-1\right)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathrm{g}^{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}-1\big).

Consider the following Hamiltonian system:

(2.18)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\dot{x}_{k}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{g}^{kj}\xi_{j},\quad\dot{\xi}_{k}(s)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\mathrm{g}^{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}\xi_{i}\xi_{j},\quad k=1,\ldots,n,\cr x(-\varrho)=(z,-\varrho)\quad\xi(-\varrho)=e_{n}.\end{array}\right.

Here
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
,
\varrho>0
is such that
\mathrm{g}=e
for
|x|>\varrho
and the solution
\left(x(s),\xi(s)\right)=\left(x_{\mathrm{g}}(z,s),\xi_{\mathrm{g}}(z,s)\right)
depends on the parameter
z
. We remark that if
\mathrm{g}=e
, then
x_{e}(s)=(z,s-\varrho)
and
\xi_{e}(s)=e_{n}
. As in
[
32
]
, it follows from the estimate (
2.16
) that, for any
a>0
fixed, there exist
C>0
such that

(2.19)

\|x_{\mathrm{g}}-x_{e}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[0,a])}+\|\xi_{\mathrm{g}}-e_{n}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[0,a])}\leq C\varepsilon.

In particular, (
2.19
) implies that under the smallness assumption (
2.16
) the Hamiltonian flow is non-trapping for small
\varepsilon
, more precisely,
x_{\mathrm{g}}(z,s)\notin B_{\varrho}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|<\varrho\right\}
, for
s>a
with some
a>0
. Moreover, the mapping
(z,s)\to x_{\mathrm{g}}(z,s)
is a
\mathcal{C}^{k-1}
-diffeomorphism on
\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\,\,|z|\leq 2\varrho\right\}\times[0,a]
and its range covers
B_{\varrho}
provided that
\varepsilon
is small enough.

Introduce new coordinates
y=(z,s)
. Then the map
\psi:\Omega^{*}=\psi^{-1}(\Omega)\to\Omega
,
y\to x_{\mathrm{g}}(z,s)
is close to
id
in the
\mathcal{C}^{k-1}
-topology for small
\varepsilon
and therefore is a diffeomorphism. This in particular implies that for
\varepsilon
small enough

(2.20)

\|\psi-\textrm{id}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k-1}}\leq C\varepsilon.

In the new coordinates
\mathrm{g}
satisfies the following lemma

(2.21)

(\psi^{*}\mathrm{g})_{in}=\delta_{in},\quad i=1,\ldots,n.

Now let
(\mathrm{g}_{1},\mathrm{g}_{2})
a couple of
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-admissible metrics on
\Omega
. Let us denote
\psi_{\ell}:\Omega^{*}_{\ell}\to\Omega
,
y\to x_{\mathrm{g}_{\ell}}(z,s)
the diffeomorphism related to
\mathrm{g}_{\ell}
as above, where
\Omega^{*}_{\ell}=\psi_{\ell}^{-1}(\Omega)
,
\ell=1,2
. We state the following

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(\mathrm{g}_{1},\mathrm{g}_{2})
a couple of
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-admissible metrics on
\Omega
. Then we have

(2.22)

\psi_{1}^{-1}(\Gamma)=\psi_{2}^{-1}(\Gamma).

So
\psi_{1}^{-1}
and
\psi_{2}^{-1}
map
\Omega
onto a new domain
\Omega^{*}=\Omega^{*}_{1}=\Omega^{*}_{2}
with smooth boundary
\Gamma^{*}=\partial\Omega^{*}
and

(2.23)

\Gamma_{-}^{*}:=\left\{x\in\Gamma^{*},\,\,\left<\nu(x),e_{n}\right><0\right\}=\left\{x\in\Gamma,\,\,\left<\nu(x),e_{n}\right><0\right\}=\Gamma_{-}.

Moreover, we have

(2.24)

\psi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{1}=\psi_{2}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{2}\quad\textrm{in}\,\,\mathcal{O}^{*}\subset\Omega^{*},\quad\|\psi_{\ell}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{\ell}-e\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k-2}(\Omega^{*})}\leq C\varepsilon,\quad\ell=1,2,

for some positive constant
C
and a neighborhood
\mathcal{O}^{*}
of
\Gamma^{*}=\partial\Omega^{*}
.

Proof.

We denote

(2.25)

\Gamma_{\pm}^{(\ell)}=\left\{y\in\partial\Omega^{*}_{\ell},\,\,\pm\left<\nu^{*}_{\ell}(y),e_{n}\right>>0\right\},\quad\ell=1,2,

where
\nu^{*}_{\ell}
is the outer normal to
\partial\Omega^{*}_{\ell}
at
y
relative to
\psi_{\ell}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{\ell}
.

First, it is clear that
\psi_{1}^{-1}(x)=\psi_{2}^{-1}(x)=x
, for any
x\in\Gamma_{-}=\left\{x\in\Gamma,\,\left<\nu(x),e_{n}\right><0\right\}
, since
\mathrm{g}_{\ell}=e
before
s\to x_{\mathrm{g}_{\ell}}(s)
enter
\Omega_{0}:=\Omega\backslash\mathcal{O}

(2.26)

\Gamma_{-}^{(1)}=\Gamma_{-}^{(2)}=\Gamma_{-}.

Let now
y_{1}^{+}=(z,s_{1}^{+})\in\Gamma^{(1)}_{+}
with
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. We denote by
\varsigma
the geodesics in
\Omega^{*}_{\ell}
connecting

and
y_{1}^{+}=(z,s_{1}^{+})
for some point
y_{1}^{-}=(z,s_{1}^{-})\in\Gamma^{(1)}_{\!-}
. Note that
\varsigma
is straight line in
\Omega^{*}_{\ell}
with
s
is being the arclength in both metrics
\psi_{\ell}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,2
. Since we have also by (
2.26
) that
y_{1}^{-}=(z,s_{1}^{-})\in\Gamma^{(2)}_{\!-}
, then
\varsigma
relies
y_{1}^{-}=(z,s_{1}^{-})
to
y_{2}^{+}=(z,s_{2}^{+})\in\Gamma^{(2)}_{\!+}
for some point
y_{2}^{+}
. We denote

(2.27)

x=\psi_{1}(y_{1}^{-})=\psi_{2}(y_{1}^{-})\in\Gamma_{-},\quad\xi=\psi_{1*}e_{n}=\psi_{2*}e_{n}=e_{n},

where
\psi_{\ell*}:T_{y_{1}}\Omega^{*}_{\ell}\to T_{\psi_{\ell}(y_{1})}\Omega
,
\ell=1,2
, is the differential of
\psi_{\ell}
at
y_{1}
. Then we have

(2.28)

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{g}_{\ell}}(x,e_{n})=(\gamma_{x,\xi}^{(\ell)}(\tau_{+}^{(\ell)}),\dot{\gamma}_{x,\xi}^{(\ell)}(\tau_{+}^{(\ell)})):=(x_{\ell}^{+},\eta_{\ell}),\quad\tau_{+}^{(\ell)}=\tau^{+}_{\mathrm{g}_{\ell}}(x,e_{n})=d_{\mathrm{g}_{\ell}}(x,x_{\ell}^{+}),

where
\gamma_{x,\xi}^{(\ell)}=\psi_{\ell}(\varsigma)
and for some
x_{\ell}^{+}\in\Gamma
.

Since
x\in\Gamma_{-}
. By the assumption (
1.18
), we obtain

(2.29)

\tau_{+}^{(1)}(x,e_{n})=\tau_{+}^{(2)}(x,e_{n}),\quad\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{g}_{1}}(x,e_{n})=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{g}_{2}}(x,e_{n}).

In particular,
x_{1}^{+}=x_{2}^{+}
and
d_{\mathrm{g}_{1}}(x,x_{1}^{+})=d_{\mathrm{g}_{2}}(x,x_{2}^{+})
. Note that the pull-back preserves the arclength. Therefore

(2.30)

d_{\psi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{1}}(x,y_{1}^{+})=d_{\mathrm{g}_{1}}(x,x_{1}^{+})=d_{\mathrm{g}_{2}}(x,x_{2}^{+})=d_{\psi_{2}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{2}}(x,y_{2}^{+}).

Since

\Gamma^{(1)}_{+}=\Gamma^{(2)}_{+},\quad\textrm{and}\quad\psi_{1}^{-1}=\psi_{2}^{-1}\,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\Gamma.

In view of this fact and the estimate (
2.20
), we can derive the following estimate:

(2.31)

\|\psi_{\ell}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{\ell}-e\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k-2}(\Omega^{*})}\leq C\varepsilon.

Note that the

-smoothness is due to the fact the pull-back

contains the Jacobian of
\psi_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,2
.
Using the fact used above for the couple of
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-admissible metrics
(\mathrm{g}_{1},\mathrm{g}_{2})
, it is readily seen that

(2.32)

\psi_{1}^{-1}=\psi_{2}^{-1},\quad\textrm{on}\,\,\mathcal{O}.

Therefore
\psi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{1}
and
\psi_{2}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{2}
will satisfy

(2.33)

\psi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{g}_{1}=\psi^{*}_{2}\mathrm{g}_{2},\quad\textrm{on}\,\,\mathcal{O}^{*},

form some neighborhood
\mathcal{O}^{*}
of
\Gamma^{*}=\partial\Omega^{*}
.

This completes the proof of the Lemma.

Lemma 2.4

Let

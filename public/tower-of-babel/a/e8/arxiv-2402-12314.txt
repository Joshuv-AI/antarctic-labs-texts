Prescribed L p L_{p} quotient curvature problem and related eigenvalue problem
Xinqun Mei, Guofang Wang, Liangjun Weng

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the existence of admissible (and strictly convex) smooth solutions to the prescribed L p L_{p} quotient type curvature problem with p > 1 p>1 . For cases where p = k − l + 1 p=k-l+1 and p > k − l + 1 p>k-l+1 , we obtain an admissible solution without any additional conditions, which is strictly spherically convex under a convexity condition. Under the same convexity condition, we establish the existence of a strictly spherically convex solution for the case p < k − l + 1 p<k-l+1 , provided that the prescribed function is even, a condition known to be necessary.

Introduction

In this paper we are interested in the following prescribed
L_{p}
quotient type curvature problem in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, namely

Given any positive smooth function
f
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, can one find a closed, strictly convex hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{H_{k}(\kappa(X))}{H_{l}(\kappa(X))}=f(\nu(X))\langle X,\nu(X)\rangle^{1-p}\penalty\ \penalty\ ?

Here
p>1
,
0\leq l<k\leq n
,
\kappa(X)=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
and
\nu(X)
are the principal curvatures and the outward unit normal of
\Sigma
at
X
respectively, and
H_{k}(\kappa(X))
is the normalized
k
th mean curvature (cf. Section
2
). It is equivalent to finding a strictly spherical convex solution (or admissible solution)

\mathbb{S}^{n}
satisfying

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{H_{n-l}(A)}{H_{n-k}(A)}=\varphi u^{p-1},

where

and the matrix

,

is the Hessian of

\sigma

\mathbb{S}^{n}

is strictly spherical convex if

as matrix. For the admissible solution see Definition

) is a self-similar solution of the following anisotropic curvature flow

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}X=\left(f(\nu)\frac{H_{l}(\kappa(X))}{H_{k}(\kappa(X))}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\nu

and serves as singularity models of this flow.

For

In particular, if further

\displaystyle\frac{H_{n-l}(A)}{H_{n-k}(A)}=\tau u^{k-l}f^{-1}.

Finally, we investigate the case for

, with

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(A)\leq C\frac{R^{2}}{r}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma},

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some well-known properties of elementary symmetric functions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be an

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(A)=\sigma_{k}(\lambda(A))=\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},

We use the convention that
\sigma_{0}=1
and
\sigma_{k}=0
for
k>n
. Let
H_{k}(\lambda)
be the normalization of
\sigma_{k}(\lambda)
given by

\displaystyle H_{k}(\lambda):=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\sigma_{k}(\lambda).

Denote
\sigma_{k}(\lambda\left|i\right.)
the symmetric
function with
\lambda_{i}=0
and
\sigma_{k}(\lambda\left|ij\right.)
the symmetric function with
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j}=0
. Recall that the Gårding cone is defined as

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{i}(\lambda)>0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall 1\leq i\leq k\}.

Definition 2.2
.

A function

of Equation (
1.2
) is called

-admissible solution if

A=\nabla^{2}u(x)+u(x)\sigma\in\Gamma_{k}

for all point

In particular, if

, we call

strictly spherically convex.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
k=1,\ldots,n
. Then

(1)

\sigma_{k}(\lambda)=\sigma_{k}(\lambda|i)+\lambda_{i}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|i),\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq n.

(2)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\sigma_{k}(\lambda|i)}=(n-k)\sigma_{k}(\lambda).

(3)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\lambda_{i}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|i)}=k\sigma_{k}(\lambda)
.

(4)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\lambda_{i}^{2}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|i)}=\sigma_{1}(\lambda)\sigma_{k}(\lambda)-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}(\lambda)
.

We denote
\sigma_{k}(A\left|i\right.)
the symmetric function with
A
deleting the

-row and

i
-column and
\sigma_{k}(A\left|ij\right.)
the symmetric
function with
A
deleting the

-rows and
i,j
-columns.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
A=\{A_{ij}\}
is diagonal, and

is a positive integer,
then

\displaystyle\sigma_{k-1}^{ij}(A)=\begin{cases}\sigma_{k-1}(A\left|i\right.),&\text{if }i=j,\\
0,&\text{if }i\neq j,\end{cases}

where
\sigma_{k-1}^{ij}(A)=\frac{{\partial\sigma_{k}(A)}}{{\partial A_{ij}}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The following three properties hold.

(1)

For
\lambda\in\Gamma_{k}
and
k>l\geq 0
,
r>s\geq 0
,
k\geq r
,
l\geq s
, there holds

\displaystyle\left(\frac{H_{k}(\lambda)}{H_{l}(\lambda)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}\leq\left(\frac{H_{r}(\lambda)}{H_{s}(\lambda)}\right)^{\frac{1}{r-s}},

with equality holds if and only if
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}>0
.

(2)

For

,
\left(\frac{H_{k}(\lambda)}{H_{l}(\lambda)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}

(3)

. If

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda_{1}}\leq\frac{\partial F(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda_{2}}\leq\cdots\leq\frac{\partial F(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda_{n}}.

\displaystyle F(A)=\left(\frac{H_{n-l}(A)}{H_{n-k}(A)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}},\penalty\ \penalty\ F^{ij}=\frac{\partial F(A)}{\partial A_{ij}},\penalty\ \penalty\ F^{ij,pq}=\frac{\partial^{2}F(A)}{\partial A_{ij}\partial A_{pq}},

Lemma 3.1

Let

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\varphi^{-1}\leq u^{p-(k-l+1)}\leq\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\varphi^{-1}.

Assume

\displaystyle\nabla u=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \nabla^{2}u\geq 0,

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{i,j,p,q\in\mathcal{G}}^{n}\widetilde{F}^{ij,pq}A_{ij\alpha}A_{pq\alpha}-(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}})_{\alpha\alpha}-\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}

\displaystyle+O\left(\eta+\sum\limits_{i,j\in\mathcal{B}}|\nabla A_{ij}|\right).

From the inverse concavity of
\widetilde{F}(A)
, cf.
[
44
, formula (3.49)]
, we obtain

(3.37)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{i,j,p,q\in\mathcal{G}}\widetilde{F}^{ij,pq}A_{ij\alpha}A_{pq\alpha}-2\sum\limits_{i,j\in\mathcal{G}}\widetilde{F}^{ii}\frac{A_{ij\alpha}^{2}}{A_{jj}}\leq 0.

Substituting (
3.37
) and (
3.36
) into (
3.35
), we get

(3.38)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}\widetilde{F}^{ij}\eta_{ij}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{B}}\left[\sigma_{m}(\mathcal{G})+\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{B}|\alpha)-\sigma_{2}(\mathcal{B}|\alpha)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{B})}\right]\left[(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}})_{\alpha\alpha}+\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}\right]

\displaystyle+O\left(\eta+\sum\limits_{i,j\in\mathcal{B}}|\nabla A_{ij}|\right).

Direct calculations yield

\displaystyle-(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}})_{\alpha\alpha}-\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left[\left(u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}\right)_{\alpha\alpha}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}+2\left(u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}\right)_{\alpha}\left(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}\right)_{\alpha}+u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}\left(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}\right)_{\alpha\alpha}+u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left[-\frac{p-1}{k-l}u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}-1}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}(A_{\alpha\alpha}-u)+u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}\left(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}\right)_{\alpha\alpha}+u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}\right]

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{p-1}{k-l}\left(\frac{p-1}{k-l}+1\right)u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}-2}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}u_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{2(p-1)}{k-l}u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}-1}u_{\alpha}\left(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}\right)_{\alpha}\right].

Combining (
3.34
),
p\geq 1
and the fact that
\varphi^{-\frac{1}{p+k-1-l}}
is spherical convex in (
3.33
), we get

(3.39)

\displaystyle-(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}})_{\alpha\alpha}-\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}\left[\left(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}\right)_{\alpha\alpha}+\frac{p-1+k-l}{k-l}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}+O(\eta)-\left(1-\frac{k-l}{p-1+k-l}\right)\frac{\left(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}\right)_{\alpha}^{2}}{\varphi^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-u^{-\frac{p-1}{k-l}}\frac{p-1+k-l}{k-l}\varphi^{\frac{1}{p-1+k-l}-\frac{1}{k-l}}\left[\left(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{p-1+k-l}}\right)_{\alpha\alpha}+\varphi^{-\frac{1}{p-1+k-l}}\right]+O(\eta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(\eta).

From
[
2
, Lemma 2.5]
,

(3.40)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j\in\mathcal{B}}|\nabla A_{ij}|\leq C\eta.

Using (
3.39
), (
3.38
) and (
3.40
), we derive

(3.41)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}\widetilde{F}^{ij}\eta_{ij}\leq C(\eta+|\nabla\eta|),\quad\text{in}\penalty\ \mathcal{O}.

From the strong maximum principle, we have
\eta=0
in
\mathcal{O}
, and since
\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:\eta(x)=0\}
is an open and closed set, then
\eta\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. This implies that
A
has the constant rank
m
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Now by the Minkowski formula (cf.
[
39
, Formula (5.60)]
)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}uH_{m}(A)d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}H_{m+1}(A)d\mu,

we conclude that
u\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, a contradiction. Hence
A=\nabla^{2}u+u\sigma>0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
∎

As an application of Theorem
3.5
, we now complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

When
p>k-l+1
, we consider a one-parameter family of Equation (
3.31
) as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. It is easy to check that
\varphi_{t}
satisfies condition (
3.33
) for any
t\in[0,1]
. If there exists some
t_{0}\in(0,1]
such that
u_{t}
is strictly spherical convex for
0\leq t<t_{0}
, and
u_{t_{0}}
is not strictly spherical convex, then by Theorem
3.5
,
u_{t_{0}}
is strictly spherical convex, which is a contradiction. So we conclude that the solution
u
is strictly spherical convex. When
p=k-l+1
, from the approximation process we know that
u
is at least spherical convex. Then using Theorem
3.5
again, we have that
u
is strictly spherical convex.

∎

4.
Case
1<p<k-l+1

In this section, we focus on the case
1<p<k-l+1
and complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
. The crucial part of this section is to show uniform positive lower and upper bounds for even, strictly convex solutions to Equation (
1.2
) when
1<p<k-l-1
.

4.1.
A priori estimates

Following the idea in
[
24
, Lemma 2.2]
(see also
[
15
, Proposition 2.1]
,
[
21
, Proposition 3.1]
and
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
), we first establish a weighted gradient estimate for a solution to Equation (
1.2
), which plays an important role in deriving the uniformly
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates. Denote
m_{u}:=\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u
and
M_{u}:=\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1<p<k-l+1
and
1\leq l<k<n
. Suppose
u
is an even, strictly spherical convex solution to Equation (
1.2
). Then there exist positive constants
\beta\in\left(0,{2(p-1)}/{(k-l)}\right)
and
N
depending on
n,k,l,p,\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\varphi
, and
\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}
, such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}}{u^{\beta}}\leq N\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u^{2-\beta}.

Proof.

We define the function

\displaystyle\Phi:=\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}}{u^{\beta}},

for some constant
\beta\in(0,2)
.
Suppose
\Phi
attains its maximum value at some point, say
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then at
x_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\nabla\Phi=u^{-\beta}\left[(2\nabla^{2}u+2u\sigma)\nabla u-\beta u^{-1}(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2})\nabla u\right],

then

(4.2)

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}u+u\sigma)\nabla u=\frac{\beta(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2})}{2u}\nabla u,

which implies that
\nabla u
is an eigenvalue of
A
at
x_{0}
. Hence we can choose an orthonormal frame at
x_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle u_{1}=|\nabla u|,\quad\text{and}\penalty\ \nabla^{2}u\penalty\ \text{is\penalty\ diagonal}.

Denote
\rho:=\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}}
. Direct calculations yield

\displaystyle\Phi_{ii}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(u_{ii}^{2}+u_{s}u_{sii}+u_{i}^{2}+uu_{ii})}{u^{\beta}}-\frac{4\beta(u_{i}^{2}u_{ii}+uu_{i}^{2})}{u^{\beta+1}}-\beta\frac{\rho^{2}u_{ii}}{u^{\beta+1}}+(\beta+1)\beta\frac{\rho^{2}u_{i}^{2}}{u^{\beta+2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\left[\frac{2(A_{ii}^{2}+u_{s}A_{sii}-uA_{ii})}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{4\beta u_{i}^{2}A_{ii}}{u\rho^{2}}-\frac{\beta(A_{ii}-u)}{u}+\beta(\beta+1)\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}\right].

By the maximal condition at
x_{0}
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Phi}\overline{F}^{ij}\Phi_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\overline{F}^{ii}(A_{ii}^{2}+u_{s}A_{sii})-\frac{2(k-l)\varphi u^{p}}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{4\beta\overline{F}^{11}u_{1}^{2}A_{11}}{u\rho^{2}}-\beta(k-l)\varphi u^{p-2}

\displaystyle+\beta\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\overline{F}^{ii}+\beta(\beta+1)\frac{\overline{F}^{11}u_{1}^{2}}{u^{2}}.

From (
3.11
), we see

(4.4)

\displaystyle A_{iis}=u_{iis}+u_{s}=u_{sii}+u_{i}\delta_{si}=A_{sii},

combining (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we obtain

(4.5)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2u_{s}}{\rho^{2}}\left(\varphi_{s}u^{p-1}+(p-1)\varphi u^{p-2}u_{s}\right)+\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\overline{F}^{ii}A_{ii}^{2}-\frac{2(k-l)\varphi u^{p}}{\rho^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\beta^{2}\overline{F}^{11}u_{1}^{2}}{u^{2}}-\beta(k-l)\varphi u^{p-2}+\beta\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\overline{F}^{ii}+\beta(\beta+1)\frac{\overline{F}^{11}u_{1}^{2}}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(p-1)\varphi u^{p-2}u_{1}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{2u_{1}\varphi_{1}u^{p-1}}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{2(k-l)\varphi u^{p}}{\rho^{2}}-\beta(k-l)\varphi u^{p-2}+\beta\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\overline{F}^{ii}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\sum\limits_{i>1}\overline{F}^{ii}A_{ii}^{2}+\left(\frac{\beta^{2}\rho^{2}}{2u_{1}^{2}}-\beta(\beta-1)\right)\frac{\overline{F}^{11}u_{1}^{2}}{u^{2}}.

For
\beta\in(0,2)
, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}\rho^{2}}{2u_{1}^{2}}-\beta(\beta-1)\geq\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}-\beta(\beta-1)=\frac{\beta}{2}(2-\beta)>0.

To proceed, we assume for some sufficiently large
N>2
, satisfying

(4.7)

\displaystyle u_{1}^{2}>NM_{u}^{2-\beta}u^{\beta}-u^{2}\geq\frac{N}{2}M_{u}^{2-\beta}u^{\beta},

otherwise, we have completed the proof of (
4.1
).
Substituting (
4.6
), (
4.7
) into (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2(p-1)\varphi u^{p-2}u_{1}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{2u_{1}\varphi_{1}u^{p-1}}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{2(k-l)\varphi u^{p}}{\rho^{2}}-\beta(k-l)\varphi u^{p-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2u^{p-2}\varphi}{\rho^{2}}\left[\left((p-1)-\frac{\beta(k-l)}{2}\right)u_{1}^{2}-uu_{1}|(\log\varphi)_{1}|-\left((k-l)+\frac{\beta(k-l)}{2}\right)u^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2u^{p-2}\varphi}{\rho^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left((p-1)-\frac{\beta(k-l)}{2}\right)u_{1}^{2}-c_{0}u^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2u^{p-2}\varphi}{\rho^{2}}\left[\left((p-1)-\frac{\beta(k-l)}{2}\right)\frac{NM_{u}^{2-\beta}u^{\beta}}{4}-c_{0}u^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi u^{p+\beta-2}NM_{u}^{2-\beta}}{2\rho^{2}}\left[\left((p-1)-\frac{\beta(k-l)}{2}\right)-4c_{0}N^{-1}\left(\frac{u}{M_{u}}\right)^{2-\beta}\right],

where the constant
c_{0}
depends on
k,l,p
, and
\|\log\varphi\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}
.
If we choose
\beta\in\left(0,\frac{2(p-1)}{k-l}\right)
and
N
is sufficiently large such that

(p-1)-\frac{\beta(k-l)}{2}-4c_{0}N^{-1}>0,

therefore we reach a contradiction. Hence we obtain (
4.1
) and complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

If
u
is an even, strictly spherical convex function satisfying

(4.8)

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{\beta}}\leq N\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}{u}^{2-\beta},\quad\text{on}\penalty\ \mathbb{S}^{n},

for some positive constants
\beta
and
N
. Then the following non-collapsing estimate holds

\displaystyle\frac{\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u}{\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u}\leq C,

where the constant
C
depends on
n,\beta
and
N
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the result of Guan
[
15
, Lemma 3.1 and Corollary 3.1]
.
For the reader’s convenience, we include the proof here.

Let
\Omega
be the strictly convex body with the support function
u
. Since
u
is an even, strictly spherical convex function, by John’s Lemma, there exists an ellipsoid
E
centered at the origin, such that

(4.9)

\displaystyle E\subset\Omega\subset nE.

We write
E
as

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{a_{1}^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{x_{n+1}^{2}}{a_{n+1}^{2}}=1,

with
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\cdots\geq a_{n+1}>0
. Then we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle a_{1}\leq M_{u}\leq na_{1},\quad m_{u}\leq a_{n+1}\leq nm_{u}.

The support function
u_{E}
of the ellipsoid
E
is given by

\displaystyle u_{E}(x)=\left(a_{1}^{2}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n+1}^{2}x_{n+1}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Hence by (
4.9
), we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle u_{E}(x)\leq u(x)\leq nu_{E}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Restrict
u_{E}
and
u
on the slice
S:=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:x=(x_{1},0,\cdots,0,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}
. First, we have

\displaystyle q(t):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{E}(t,0,\cdots,0,\sqrt{1-t^{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1}^{2}t^{2}+(1-t^{2})a_{n+1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq a_{1}t,\quad\text{for}\penalty\ t\in[0,1].

Then for any
t\in\left[0,\left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{n+1}}\right)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}\right]
,

(4.12)

\displaystyle ta_{1}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}a_{n+1}^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}\leq q\left(t\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{1}}\right)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}\right).

Denote

\displaystyle p(t):=\left[u\left(t,0,\cdots,0,\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\right)\right]^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}.

From the weighted gradient estimate (
4.8
) and (
4.10
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}p(t)\right|\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{u}^{1-\frac{\beta}{2}}\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}(na_{1})^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}.

Using (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle p\left(t\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{1}}\right)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p(0)+N^{\frac{1}{2}}(na_{1})^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}t\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{1}}\right)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(na_{n+1})^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}+tN^{\frac{1}{2}}(na_{n+1})^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}.

Therefore

(4.13)

\displaystyle u\left(t\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{1}}\right)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}},0,\cdots,0,\left[1-t^{2}\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{1}}\right)^{2-\beta}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq n\left(1+tN^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\beta}}a_{n+1}.

Since
u(x)\geq u_{E}
, combining (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) we conclude that

\displaystyle ta_{1}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}a_{n+1}^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}\leq n\left(1+tN^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\beta}}a_{n+1}.

Choosing
t=N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, it is clear that the above inequality implies

\displaystyle\frac{a_{1}}{a_{n+1}}\leq n^{\frac{2}{\beta}}N^{\frac{1}{\beta}}2^{\frac{4}{\beta(2-\beta)}},

together with (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u}{\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u}\leq n^{1+\frac{2}{\beta}}N^{\frac{1}{\beta}}2^{\frac{4}{\beta(2-\beta)}}.

Hence we complete the proof.
∎

As a direct corollary of Lemma
4.2
, we obtain uniform
C^{1}
estimates for the solution
u
to Equation (
1.2
).

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
1<p<k-l+1
and
1\leq l<k<n
. Suppose
u
is an even, positive and strictly spherical convex solution to Equation (
1.2
). Then

\displaystyle 0<c<u<C,\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla u|\leq C,

where the constants
c,C
depend on
n,k,l,p,\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\varphi,
and
\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}
.

Proof.

By the maximum principle, we have

\displaystyle(\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u)^{p+l-(k+1)}\leq\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\varphi^{-1},\quad\text{and}\quad(\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u)^{p+l-(k+1)}\geq\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\varphi^{-1}.

Since
p\in(1,k-l+1)
,
p+l-(k+1)<0
. Hence we have a uniform upper bound for
\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u

and a lower uniform positive bound for
\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u

. Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
imply
that
0<c<u<C
for some constants
c
and
C
. The gradient bound follows from Lemma
4.1
.
∎

4.1

, we have the following theorem.

1<p<k+1-l
and
1\leq l<k<n
. Let

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C,

where the constant
C

.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

When
1<p<k+1-l
and
1\leq l<k<n
, it becomes challenging to prove that the kernel of the corresponding linearization operator of Equation (
1.2
) is trivial. Therefore, we resort to the topological degree method, as introduced in
[
30
]
. Following a similar argument as presented in
[
20
, Theorem 1.5]
or
[
14
, Section 3]
, we establish the existence of an even, strictly spherical convex solution to Equation (
1.2
). Hence, we just provide an outline for the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let us consider a one-parameter family of Equation (
3.31
) as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, and define the Banach space

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\{u\in C^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{n}):u(x)=u(-x),\quad\text{for\penalty\ all}\penalty\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}.

It is obvious that
\varphi_{t}
satisfies the assumption in Theorem
3.5
. This implies for any
0\leq t\leq 1
, the solution
u^{t}\in\mathcal{A}
to Equation (
3.31
) is strictly spherical convex. From Theorem
4.2
, we have the
C^{2}
estimates, and the high-order estimates follow from the Evans-Krylov theorem. Hence we can use the degree theory. In order to use the degree theory in
[
30
]
to establish the existence of the solution, we only need to compute the degree at
t=0
. By
[
41
, Theorem 6.4]
(or
[
29
, Theorem 1.3]
), we have that
u=1
is the unique solution for
\varphi^{0}=1
, and the same argument in
[
14
]
yields that the degree is nonzero. Hence we have the existence of theorem
1.4
. The convexity of the solution follows from Theorem
3.5
under the assumption (
3.33
).
∎

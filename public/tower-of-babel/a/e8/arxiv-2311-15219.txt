1.
Introduction

Let
1<q<\infty
and
-1+N/q\leq s<1/q
, where
N\geq 2
is the space dimension.
In this paper, we use the
L_{1}
–
B^{s+1}_{q,1}\times B^{s}_{q,1}
maximal regularity framework to show
the local well-posedness of the Navier-Stokes
equations describing the isotropic motion of the compressible viscous fluid flows
in the half-space. Let

{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\mid x_{N}>0\},\quad\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\mid x_{N}=0\}.

The equations considered in this paper read as

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho_{t}+{\rm div}\,(\rho{\mathbf{v}})=0&&\quad&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}\times(0,T)$},\\
\rho({\mathbf{v}}_{t}+{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{v}})-\alpha\Delta{\mathbf{v}}-\beta\nabla{\rm div}\,{\mathbf{v}}+\nabla P(\rho)=0&&\quad&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}\times(0,T)$},\\
{\mathbf{v}}|_{\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}=0,\quad(\rho,{\mathbf{v}})=(\rho_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})&&\quad&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here,

\alpha
and
\beta
denote respective
the viscosity coefficients and the second viscosity
coefficients satisfying the conditions

(1.2)

\alpha>0,\quad\alpha+\beta>0,

and

P(\rho)
is a smooth function defined on
(0,\infty)
satisfying
P^{\prime}(\rho)>0
,
that is, the barotropic fluid is considered.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
N-1<q<\infty
and
-1+N/q\leq s<1/q
.
Let
\rho_{*}
be a positive constant describing the mass density of the reference body,
and let
\tilde{\eta}_{0}\in B^{s+1}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
. Set
\eta(x)=\rho_{*}+\tilde{\eta}_{0}(x)
.
Assume that there exist two positive constants
\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
such that

(1.3)

\rho_{1}<\rho_{*}<\rho_{2},\quad\rho_{1}<P^{\prime}(\rho_{*})<\rho_{2},\quad\rho_{1}<\eta_{0}(x)<\rho_{2},\quad\rho_{1}<P^{\prime}(\eta_{0}(x))<\rho_{2}\quad(x\in\overline{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}).

Then,
there exist small numbers

and
\sigma>0
such that
for any initial data

\rho_{0}=\rho_{*}+\tilde{\rho}_{0}
with
\tilde{\rho}_{0}\in B^{s+1}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
and
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})
,
problem (
1.1
) admits unique solutions
\rho
and
{\mathbf{v}}

satisfying the regularity conditions:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\rho-\rho_{0}

\displaystyle\in L_{1}((0,T),B^{s+1}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}))\cap W^{1}_{1}((0,T),B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{v}}

\displaystyle\in L_{1}((0,T),B^{s+2}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})^{N})\cap W^{1}_{1}((0,T),B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})^{N})

Remark 2
.

The condition
-1+N/q\leq s<1/q
requires that
-1+N/q<1/q
. Thus, the condition

N-1<q
is necessary for our argument. On the other hand, the requirement of
s<1/q

comes from our linear theory. To use the Abidi-Paicu-Haspot theory for the Besov
space estimate of the products of functions (cf. Lemma 8 in Sect. 2 below), we have to assume that

-N/q^{\prime}<s<N/q
. Since we assume that
-1+N/q\leq s
, this condition is
satisfied, because
-N/q^{\prime}<-1+N/q
, that is
N>1
.

R. Danchin and R. Tolksdorf
[
9
]
proved the local and global well-posedness
of equations (
1.1
) in the
L_{1}
in time and
B^{N/q}_{q,1}\times B^{N/q-1}_{q,1}
in space
maximal regularity framework for some

,
and the main assumption is that the fluid domain is bounded.
In particular, they consider only the case where

in our notation for thier local well-posedness
theory.
To obtain the
L_{1}
in time maximal regularity of solutions to the linearized equations,
so called Stokes equations in the compressible fluid flow case, in
[
9
]

they used their extended version of
Da Prato and Grisvard theory
[
6
]
, which was a first result concerning
L_{1}
maximal regularity
for continuous analytic semigroups. In
[
9
]
, they assumed that the fluid domain is bounded,
which seems to be necessary to obtain the linear theory for Lamé equations cf.
[
9
, Sect. 3]

in their argument

The final goal of our study is
to solve equations (
1.1
) if the fluid domain is a general
C^{2}
class domain.
If the fluid domain is the whole space, a number of results have been estabilished

[
5
,
8
,
1
,
12
]
and references given therein.
Thus, our interest is in the initial boundary value problem case.
As a first step of our study, in this paper we consider equations in the half-space,
namely the model problem for the initial boundary value problem.

1.1.

To prove Theorem

Let

We shall drive equations in Lagrange coordinates. Let

\Bigr\{\int^{\infty}_{0}(e^{-\gamma t}\|\partial_{t}T(t)f\|_{Y})^{p}\,\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr\}^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{(Y,X)_{1-1/p,p}}.

(cf.
[
28
]
). But, this idea does not apply to the
p=1
case. Our idea is to find two spaces

Y_{\sigma}
and
Y_{-\sigma}
corresponding to a small positive constant
\sigma
such that

Y_{\sigma}\subset Y\subset Y_{-\sigma}
and there hold

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}T(t)f\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-(1-\sigma)}\|f\|_{Y_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\text{for $f\in Y_{\sigma}$},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}T(t)f\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-(1+\sigma)}\|f\|_{Y_{-\sigma}}

\displaystyle\text{for $f\in Y_{-\sigma}$}.

Then, by real interpolation method, we have

\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\|\partial_{t}T(t)\|_{Y}\,\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\|f\|_{(Y_{\sigma},Y_{-\sigma})_{1/2,1}}

If we may choose
Y
in such a way that
Y=(Y_{\sigma},Y_{-\sigma})_{1/2,1}
, then we have

\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\|\partial_{t}T(t)\|_{Y}\,\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\|f\|_{Y}.

This is
L_{1}
in time maximal regularity for
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
.

In view of the spectral analysis point of view, we assume that there exists a
\gamma>0
such that
for any
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mu}+\gamma
, there hold

\displaystyle\|\lambda(\lambda{\mathbf{I}}-A)f\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\text{for any $f\in Y$},

\displaystyle\|\lambda(\lambda{\mathbf{I}}-A)f\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq C|\lambda|^{-\sigma}\|f\|_{Y_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\text{for any $f\in Y_{\sigma}$},

\displaystyle\|\lambda\partial_{\lambda}(\lambda{\mathbf{I}}-A)f\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq C|\lambda|^{-(1-\sigma)}\|f\|_{Y_{-\sigma}}

\displaystyle\text{for any $f\in Y_{-\sigma}$}.

Then, the first estimate implies the generation of
C_{0}
analytic semigroup

\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, and the second and third estimates imply the corresponding
estimates of
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. Of couse, in the compressible Stokes equations case,
the situation is much comlicated, but the essential idea is the same as above.
Detailed proof will be given in the proof of Theorem
15
in Sect. 3 below.

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{*}+\|\tilde{\eta}_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})})\|(f,{\mathbb{g}})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})},

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\|\partial_{t}T_{2}(t)(f,{\mathbb{g}})\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})}\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{*},\|\tilde{\eta}_{0}\|_{B^{N/q}_{q,1}})\Bigl\{\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\nabla^{2}T_{2}(t)(f,{\mathbb{g}})\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+})}

Corollary 16

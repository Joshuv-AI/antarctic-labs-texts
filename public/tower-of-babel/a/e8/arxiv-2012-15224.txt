On the Moyal Star Product of Resurgent Series
Y. Li, D. Sauzin, S. Sun

Abstract
We analyze the Moyal star product in deformation quantization from the resurgence theory perspective. By putting algebraic conditions on Borel transforms, one can define the space of “algebro-resurgent series” (a subspace of 1 1 -Gevrey formal series in i ​ ℏ / 2 i\hbar/2 with coefficients in ℂ ​ { q , p } \mathbb{C}\{q,p\} ), which we show is stable under Moyal star product.

1.2
We first give some background and motivation.

Ever since quantum mechanics started in the 1920s,
quantization and semiclassical limit have become
a central theme among a variety of areas in mathematics such as
functional analysis, geometry and topology, representation theory,
pseudo-differential operators and microlocal analysis and symplectic
geometry, to name a few.

Conventional quantum mechanics are formulated in terms of linear
operators on Hilbert space that realize the fundamental Canonical
Commutation Relations (CCR), or of Feynman’s path integrals as
conceived by Dirac and developed by Feynman to make the quantum picture
more compatible with the classical one.
Built upon Wigner, Weyl and Groenewold’s insights and pioneered by
Moyal, deformation quantization is a third formulation, in full phase space, which evolved gradually into an autonomous
theory, with its own complete internal logic, that is conceptually
very appealing.

The idea of deformation quantization is to achieve the Heisenberg CCR
by deforming the commutative algebra of functions on the phase space
(classical observables) to a non-commutative associative algebra.

In
[
Moy49
]
, Moyal defined the so-called Moyal star
product
\star_{M}
in relation with statistical properties of quantum mechanics.
For one degree of freedom,
the standard Poisson structure for functions
f(q,p)
and
g(q,p)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
being

\{f,g\}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial p}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial q}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p}=\mu\circ P(f\otimes g),\quad\text{with}\kern 5.0ptP\coloneqq\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial q}-\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial p},

where
\mu
is the usual pointwise product of functions,
the Moyal star product of two classical observables is the formal series in
t
obtained as

(1.4)

f\star_{M}g=\mu\circ\exp\Big(\frac{tP}{2}\Big)(f\otimes g)=\mu\circ\Big(\mathop{\hbox{{\rm Id}}}\nolimits+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{t^{k}}{2^{k}k!}P^{k}\Big)(f\otimes g)

with

P^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-n}\mbox{$\begin{pmatrix}k\\
n\end{pmatrix}$}(\partial_{p}\,{\otimes}\,\partial_{q})^{n}(\partial_{q}\,{\otimes}\,\partial_{p})^{k-n}=\sum_{n=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-n}\mbox{$\begin{pmatrix}k\\
n\end{pmatrix}$}\partial_{p}^{n}\,\partial_{q}^{k-n}\,{\otimes}\,\partial_{p}^{k-n}\,\partial_{q}^{n}.

Here,
t
is the deformation parameter, taken to be
{i\hbar}
in quantum mechanics.
When extended to
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})[[t]]
, the Moyal star product is a non-commutative associative product.
We have
f\star_{M}g=fg+\frac{t}{2}\{f,g\}+O(t^{2})
, hence

(1.5)

[f,g]_{M}\coloneqq\frac{1}{t}(f\star_{M}g-g\star_{M}f)=\{f,g\}+O(t),

we thus recover the
Poisson algebra structure of the classical observables in the limit

\hbar=t/i\to 0
. Moreover

p\star_{M}q-q\star_{M}p=t,\quad\text{{\emph{i.e.}}\ }\kern 5.0pt[p,q]_{M}=1.

The extension to
N\geq 1

replacing

(1.6)

in (
1.4

so that (

the CCR are realized:
[p_{i},q_{i}]_{M}=1
and
[p_{i},q_{j}]_{M}=[p_{i},p_{j}]_{M}=[q_{i},q_{j}]_{M}=0
for
i\neq j
.

The Moyal star product can be viewed as a non-commutative
associative deformation of the usual product of functions. The idea to
view Quantum Mechanics as a deformation of Classical Mechanics was
promoted by Bayen-Flato-Fronsdal-Lichnerowicz-Sternheimer

[

In particular, Fedosov recursively constructed a star product through a canonical flat

Lemma 2.3

There is an integral representation of the Borel-star product in

as follows:

(2.11)

\hat{f}\ast\hat{g}(\xi,q,p)=\frac{d^{3}}{d\xi^{3}}\int_{0}^{\xi}d\xi_{1}\int_{0}^{\xi-\xi_{1}}d\xi_{2}\int_{0}^{\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}d\xi_{3}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\hat{f}(\xi_{1},q,p+\sqrt{\xi_{3}}e^{-i\theta})\hat{g}(\xi_{2},q+\sqrt{\xi_{3}}e^{i\theta},p),

where the integrand is considered as element of
\mathbb{C}[e^{\pm i\theta}][[q,p,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\sqrt{\xi_{3}}]]
and integration in
\theta
is performed termwise.

Moreover, if both factors are convergent,
i.e.

\hat{f},\hat{g}\in\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{3}

\hat{f}*\hat{g}\in\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{3}

Proof.

We expand
\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}

\begin{split}\hat{f}\ast\hat{g}(\xi,q,p)=&\sum\limits_{m,n,s\geq 0}\frac{n!m!}{(n+m+s)!s!}(\partial_{p}^{s}f_{m})(\partial_{q}^{s}g_{n})\xi^{m+n+s}\\

Using the fact that the Hadamard product of two formal series

and

\begin{split}\hat{f}\ast\hat{g}(\xi,q,p)=&\frac{d^{3}}{d\xi^{3}}\int_{0}^{\xi}d\xi_{1}\int_{0}^{\xi-\xi_{1}}d\xi_{2}\int_{0}^{\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}d\xi_{3}\\

4

In this section, we shall work in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with variables
z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}
and give the definition of
z_{1}
-simple polynomial. The proposition
4.2
is very useful in the following sections and we will prove it carefully. The reason we use the definition ‘
z_{1}
-simple polynomial’ is that we want the set (
4.2
) is non-trivial. We start with

P(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{M}b_{i}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})z_{1}^{i}\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}]=\mathbb{C}[z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}][z_{1}],

where
b_{i}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})
’s are polynomials of variables
z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}
and
b_{M}\neq 0
.
We denote by
\mathbb{F}
the fraction field of
\mathbb{C}[z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}]
and
\overline{\mathbb{F}}
the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}
. Thus,
P(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
can be written as

(4.1)

b_{M}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}\big(z_{1}-\omega_{\alpha}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\big)

with
\omega_{\alpha}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a non-zero polynomial
F(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}]
and the representation of it (formula (
4.1
)),
F
is called
z_{1}
-simple polynomial if for any
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
,
1\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}\leq M
, we have
\omega_{\alpha_{1}}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\neq\omega_{\alpha_{2}}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})
. Specially,
F
is
z_{1}
-simple polynomial if the order of
z_{1}
in
F
is zero.

Proposition 4.2
.

Any proper algebraic subvariety
V
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
can be written as
V=\bigcap\limits_{J=1}^{K}P_{J}^{-1}(0)
, where
K
is a positive integer and
P_{1},\cdots,P_{k}
are
z_{1}
-simple polynomials.

Proof.

Hilbert’s basis theorem states that every algebraic variety can be described as a common zero locus of finitely many polynomials. Thus we assume

V=\bigcap\limits_{J=1}^{K}V^{J},\quad V^{J}:=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid Q^{J}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})=0\},\text{ for }J=1,\cdots,K,

where
Q^{J},J=1,\cdots,K
, are non-zero polynomials over
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. What we want to prove is, for each
Q^{J}
, there exists a non-zero
z_{1}
-simple polynomial
P^{J}
s.t.

Q^{-1}(0)=P^{-1}(0).

We will use the abridge notations
Q
or
P
later.
Suppose
Q=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{M}b_{i}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})z_{1}^{i}
with
b_{i}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})
’s are polynomials of variables
z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}
and
b_{M}
non-zero polynomial,
then it has the following factorization in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}[z_{1}]
:

b_{M}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{N}\big(z_{1}-\omega_{\alpha}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\big)^{s_{\alpha}},

where
\omega_{\alpha}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}
,
\omega_{\alpha_{1}}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\neq\omega_{\alpha_{2}}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})
for
1\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}\leq N
, integer multiplicities
s_{\alpha}\geq 1
and
\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}s_{\alpha}=M
.
Let us suppose that for some
\alpha
,
s_{\alpha}>1
(if not, the proof is trivial).
We shall use the following notation:

\begin{split}R(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})&\coloneqq\frac{Q(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})}{b_{M}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})}=\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{N}\big(z_{1}-\omega_{\alpha}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\big)^{s_{\alpha}},\\
\widetilde{R}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})&\coloneqq\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{N}\big(z_{1}-\omega_{\alpha}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\big).\end{split}

First, we shall prove
\widetilde{R}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{F}[z_{1}]
. In fact,
R(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
is reducible in
\mathbb{F}[z_{1}]
(irreducible polynomials are separable polynomials). If we consider the minimal polynomial of each root
\omega_{i}(y)
, with Abel’s irreducibility theorem, then we get:

\widetilde{R}=R_{1}\cdots R_{m},\qquad R=(R_{1})^{{\sigma}_{1}}\cdots{(R_{m})}^{{\sigma}_{m}}

with
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}\in\mathbb{F}[z_{1}]
and
{\sigma}_{i}
’s are chosen from

\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}\}
.
The idea would be to construct inductively
R_{1}
as the minimal
polynomial in
\mathbb{F}[z_{1}]
of
\omega_{1}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}
, then
{\sigma}_{1}=s_{1}
and
R_{1}

is a product of some of the factors
z_{1}-\omega_{i}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})
including
i=1
,
and we go on with
R_{2}
minimal polynomial of one of the
\omega_{i}^{J}
’s
which has not been included in
R_{1}^{J}
, etc.

Up to now, we have
\widetilde{R}\in\mathbb{F}[z_{1}]
as announced, and we have
decompositions for

in
\mathbb{F}[z_{1}]
:

Q(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})=b_{M}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})R_{1}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})^{{\sigma}_{1}}\cdots R_{m}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})^{{\sigma}_{m}}.

And each factor
R_{j}\ (j=1,\cdots,m)
can be written as

R_{j}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})=\frac{1}{L_{j}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})}\widehat{R}_{j}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})

taking for

the l.c.m. of the denominators of the coefficients
of

in
\mathbb{F}
, and
\widehat{R}_{j}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
is a primitive polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}][z_{1}]
.
Guass’s lemma implies that the coefficients of
\widehat{R}_{1}^{{\sigma}_{1}}\cdots\widehat{R}_{m}^{{\sigma}_{m}}
are relatively prime in
\mathbb{C}[z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}]
.
Hence the coefficients of
Q
are also in
\mathbb{C}[z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}]
, which implies that
\frac{b_{M}}{(L_{1})^{\sigma_{1}}\cdots(L_{m})^{\sigma_{m}}}\in\mathbb{C}[z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}]
. We define

P=\frac{b_{M}}{L_{1}^{{\sigma}_{1}-1}\cdots L_{m}^{{\sigma}_{m}-1}}R_{1}\cdots R_{m}=\frac{b_{M}}{L_{1}^{{\sigma}_{1}}\cdots L_{m}^{{\sigma}_{m}}}\widehat{R}_{1}\cdots\widehat{R}_{m}

which is the desired
z_{1}
-simple polynomial since
R_{1}\cdots R_{m}
have distinct root in
z_{1}
by the construction. Finally,
P^{-1}(0)=Q^{-1}(0)
is obviously because both
P
and
Q
have common factors
\frac{b_{M}}{L_{1}^{{\sigma}_{1}}\cdots L_{m}^{{\sigma}_{m}}},\widehat{R}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{R}_{m}
which are all polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}][z_{1}]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Given a

-simple polynomial

,

\diamond

-simple polynomial.

\diamond

which is contained in

is both
\xi
-simple polynomial and
z
-simple polynomial.

Proof.

The proof is standard and left to the reader.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

If

is a

-simple polynomial, which means that

In this section, our goal is to prove lemma

From the definition of

-simple polynomials over

correspondingly, s.t. if

With a slight abuse of the notation, let

Definition 5.1
.

We define

Case 1:

(5.3)

&{(P_{F}^{J})}_{(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})}(\overline{P})\neq 0\end{split}\right.\right\},

where
{(P_{F}^{J})}_{(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})}(z_{1}):=P_{F}^{J}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
is treated as a polynomial of one variable

with the coefficients in

.

Case 2:
If

V_{f}^{J}:=\mathbb{C}^{n}-\left\{(z,z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\ \left|\ (P_{F}^{J})_{(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})}(z_{1})\text{ has $N$ distinct non-zero roots }\right.\right\}.

(
5.3

(5.6)

V_{f}^{J}=\left\{(z,z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\left|\begin{split}&b_{M}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})=0&\text{ or }\\

V_{f}^{J}=\left\{(z,z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\left|\begin{split}&b_{M}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})=0&\text{ or }\\

In both case, one may observe that, by the assumption (

holomorphic at origin and it admits analytic continuation along any

The proof of claim

For all

One may prove that, if there exists

\begin{split}\left(cont_{\gamma|_{t}}f\right)(z,z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})&=\int_{H_{t}}\left(cont_{(H|_{t}(s),\gamma_{z_{2}}|_{t},\cdots,\gamma_{z_{n}}|_{t})}F\right)(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})dz_{1}\\

In the set (

\omega_{i}(t)\neq\omega_{0}(t),\ \omega_{i}(t)\neq 0\ \text{and}\ \omega_{i}(t)\neq\omega_{j}(t)\quad\forall i,j=1,\cdots,M,\ i\neq j\text{ and }\forall t\in[0,1].

6.1
Introduction to Hadamard product on
\mathbb{C}

In this section, we study the analytic continuation of the Hadamard product.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
f(\xi),g(\xi)\in\mathbb{C}[[\xi]]
,
f(\xi)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\xi^{n}
,
g(\xi)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}\xi^{m}
, we define Hadamard product:

f\odot g(\xi)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{n}\xi^{n}.

A fact is that if
f,g\in\mathbb{C}\{\xi\}

Theorem E

and it admits analytic continuation along any path which starts near origin and avoids

We may give a sketch of our proof. Choose
\xi

\mathbb{D}(R_{f}R_{g}):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|<R_{f}R_{g}\}

Combinatorial sums, series and integrals involving odd harmonic numbers


Abstract
We present several types of ordinary generating functions involving central binomial coefficients, harmonic numbers, and odd harmonic numbers. Our results complement those of Boyadzhiev from 2012 and Chen from 2016. Based on these generating functions we evaluate several infinite series in closed form. In addition, we offer some combinatorial sum identities involving Catalan numbers, harmonic numbers and odd harmonic numbers. Finally, we analyze a special log-integral with Fibonacci numbers and odd harmonic numbers. Keywords: Harmonic number, odd harmonic number, central binomial coefficient, generating function, Fibonacci number, Catalan number. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : Primary 33B15; Secondary 11B39.

Introduction and motivation

Let
\Gamma(z)
be the familiar gamma function given by

\Gamma(z)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{z-1}dt
,
\Re(z)>0
.
The digamma function
\psi(z)
is defined for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\ldots\}
by

\psi(z)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(z)}{\Gamma(z)}=-\gamma-\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+z}\Big),

with
\gamma
being the Euler–Mascheroni constant

\gamma=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}-\ln n\Big)=0,577215\ldots.

We use the notation
O_{n}
for the
n
th odd harmonic number,

O_{n}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2j-1}
,
O_{0}=0
, by analogy with the usual notation
H_{n}
for the classical harmonic numbers

H_{n}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}
,
H_{0}=0
.
Since

H_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\frac{1}{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}{\frac{1}{{2j}}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lceil{n/2}\right\rceil}{\frac{1}{{2j-1}}}=\frac{1}{2}H_{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}+O_{\left\lceil{n/2}\right\rceil},

odd harmonic numbers and harmonic numbers are related by

H_{2n}=\frac{1}{2}H_{n}+O_{n}
and

H_{2n-1}=\frac{1}{2}H_{n-1}+O_{n}.

(1)

Let
F_{n}
denote the
n
-th Fibonacci and
L_{n}
the
n
-th Lucas number, both satisfying the recurrence

u_{n}=u_{n-1}+u_{n-2}
,
n\geq 2
, but with respective initial conditions

F_{0}=0
,
F_{1}=1
and

L_{0}=2
,
L_{1}=1
. For negative subscripts we have

F_{-n}=(-1)^{n-1}F_{n}
and
L_{-n}=(-1)^{n}L_{n}
.

The Binet formulas for these numbers state that

F_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta},\quad L_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

(2)

where
\alpha=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
is the golden ratio and
\beta=-1/\alpha
.

For further information
on Fibonacci and Lucas numbers, we refer the reader to entries A000045 and A000032, respectively, in the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences
[
15
]
.

Let
(G_{j}(a,b))_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the
gibonacci sequence
having the same recurrence relation as the Fibonacci and Lucas sequences but starting with arbitrary initial values; that is, let
G_{0}=a
,
G_{1}=b
,
G_{j}(a,b)=G_{j-1}(a,b)+G_{j-2}(a,b)
,
j\geq 2
, where
a
and
b
are arbitrary numbers (usually integers) not both zero. Thus,
F_{j}=G_{j}(0,1)
and
L_{j}=G_{j}(2,1)
. For brevity, we will write
G_{j}
for
G_{j}(a,b)
. Extension to negative subscripts is provided by writing the recurrence as
G_{-j}=G_{-(j-2)}-G_{-(j-1)}
; so that the gibonacci sequence is defined for all integers. The gibonacci numbers can be accessed directly through the Binet-like formula

G_{j}=\frac{{(b-a\beta)\alpha^{j}+(a\alpha-b)\beta^{j}}}{{\alpha-\beta}}.

(3)

Finally, central binomial coefficients and Catalan numbers
C_{n}
are defined for all
n\geq 0
by
\binom{2n}{n}=\frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^{2}}
and
C_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}
.

This paper is motivated by the papers by Boyadzhiev from 2012
[
4
]
and Chen from 2016
[
8
]
. Boyadzhiev studied generating function involving central binomial coefficients and harmonic numbers
H_{n}
. His main results are
[
4
, Theorem 1]

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}H_{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{1-4x}}\ln\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4x}}{2\sqrt{1-4x}}\Big),\quad x\in\Big[\!-\frac{1}{4};\frac{1}{4}\Big),

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}H_{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{1+4x}}\ln\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{1+4x}}{1+\sqrt{1+4x}}\Big),\quad x\in\Big(\!-\frac{1}{4};\frac{1}{4}\Big].

Chen added results for the sequences
\binom{2n}{n}(H_{2n}-H_{n})
,
C_{n}(H_{2n}-H_{n})
and
\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}
,
and proved among others
[
8
, Theorem 8]

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}x^{n}=-\frac{\ln\sqrt{1-4x}}{\sqrt{1-4x}},\quad x\in\Big[\!-\frac{1}{4};\frac{1}{4}\Big).

(5)

Here we derive generating functions for several related sequences, starting with a different proof of Chen’s identity (
5
) using a special family of log-integrals. As a consequence of (
5
) we state and prove interesting combinatorial sum identities involving Catalan numbers, harmonic numbers, and odd harmonic numbers. Proceeding further we offer generating functions for

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}H_{2n+1}x^{n}
,
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{O_{n}}{n+m+1}x^{n}
with
m\geq 0
, and others. Our results are also closely related to other articles, among others we mention Lehmer
[
13
]
,
Chu and Zheng
[
9
]
, Chen
[
7
]
, Furdui and Sîntămărian
[
11
]
, and Stewart
[
17
]
, who found various ordinary generating functions for sequences involving products between harmonic numbers and Fibonacci (Lucas) numbers.
In the final section, we provide an analysis of a special log-integral with Fibonacci numbers and odd harmonic numbers.

An integral and some consequences

The next lemma will be crucial for several parts of this paper.

Lemma 1
.

For all real
a>0
and integers
n\geq 0
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln x}{(a^{2}+x^{2})^{n+1}}\,dx=\pi\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\ln a-O_{n}}{(2a)^{2n+1}}.

(6)

Proof.

Entry 4.231 (7) in Gradshteyn and Ryzhik
[
12
]
states that for
a>0

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln x}{(a^{2}+x^{2})^{n}}\,dx=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\big(n-\frac{1}{2}\big)}{4(n-1)!\,a^{2n-1}}\left(2\ln\Big(\frac{a}{2}\Big)-\gamma-\psi\Big(n-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\right).

Replace
n
by
n+1
and use

\Gamma\big(n+\frac{1}{2}\big)=\frac{(2n)!\sqrt{\pi}}{4^{n}n!}
and
\psi\big(n+\frac{1}{2}\big)=-\gamma-2\ln 2+2\,O_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

For all
a\geq 1
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\ln a-O_{n}}{(2a)^{2n}}=\frac{a\ln(a^{2}+1)}{2\sqrt{a^{2}+1}},

(7)

and, for all
a>1
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\ln a-O_{n}}{(2a)^{2n}}=\frac{a\ln(a^{2}-1)}{2\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}.

(8)

Proof.

Use (
6
) and sum from
n=0
to
\infty
using the geometric series. Note that

\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln x}{a^{2}+x^{2}}dx=\frac{\pi}{2a}\ln a
.
∎

Special cases of (
7
) and (
8
) are

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{O_{n}}{4^{n}}=-\frac{\ln 2}{2\sqrt{2}},\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\ln 2-O_{n}}{16^{n}}=\frac{\ln 5}{\sqrt{5}},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\ln\alpha-O_{n}}{(2\alpha)^{2n}}=\frac{\alpha\ln(\sqrt{5}\alpha)}{2\sqrt{2+\alpha}},\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\ln\alpha-2O_{n}}{(4\alpha)^{n}}=\frac{2\ln\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha}},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\ln\alpha-O_{n}}{(2\alpha)^{2n}}=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\ln\alpha,\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\ln 2-O_{n}}{16^{n}}=\frac{\ln 3}{\sqrt{3}},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\ln(2\alpha)-2O_{n}}{(8\alpha)^{n}}=\frac{\sqrt{2\alpha}\ln 5}{2\sqrt[4]{5}}.

Our first theorem rediscovers Chen’s generating function (
5
)
[
8
, Theorem 8]
.

Theorem 1
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ln(1-4x)}{2\sqrt{1-4x}},\quad x\in\Big[\!-\frac{1}{4};\frac{1}{4}\Big),

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ln(1+4x)}{2\sqrt{1+4x}},\quad x\in\Big(\!-\frac{1}{4};\frac{1}{4}\Big].

Proof.

From Lehmer’s paper
[
13
]
(or by the generalized binomial theorem) we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}x^{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4x}},\quad x\in\Big(\!-\frac{1}{4};\frac{1}{4}\Big],

which shows that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{1}{(2a)^{2n}}=\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}.

Hence, from Lemma
2

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{O_{n}}{(2a)^{2n}}=\frac{a\ln\big(\frac{a^{2}}{a^{2}-1}\big)}{2\sqrt{a^{2}-1}},\quad a>1.

Set
x=(2a)^{-2}
and simplify.
∎

We continue with several combinatorial identities involving harmonic numbers
H_{n}
, odd harmonic numbers
O_{n}
, and Catalan numbers
C_{n}
.

Corollary 2
.

For integers
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n}4^{n-j}C_{j}H_{n-j}=2^{2n+1}H_{n+1}-\binom{2(n+1)}{n+1}O_{n+1}.

(10)

Proof.

Write the right-hand side of (
9
) as

\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4x}\cdot\left(-\frac{\ln(1-4x)}{1-4x}\right)

and use the generating functions

\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4x}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}4^{n}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\Big)_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}

where
(\lambda)_{n}=\lambda(\lambda+1)\cdots(\lambda+n-1)
,
(\lambda)_{0}=1,
is the Pochhammer symbol and

-\frac{\ln(1-4x)}{1-4x}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}4^{n}H_{n}x^{n}.

Apply Cauchy’s product rule, then extract and compare the coefficients of
x^{n}
.
∎

Remark 1
.

We can write (
10
) equivalently as follows

\sum_{j=0}^{n}4^{j}C_{n-j}H_{j}=2^{2n+1}H_{n+1}-(n+2)C_{n+1}O_{n+1}.

Also, it follows from the known formula
C_{n}=\frac{n+2}{4n+2}\,C_{n+1}
that
C_{-1}=-\frac{1}{2}
.
Hence, we have a still other form of (
10
) given by

\sum_{j=0}^{n}4^{j}C_{n-j-1}H_{j+1}=-\frac{n+2}{4}C_{n+1}O_{n+1}.

Corollary 3
.

For integers
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}C_{j}O_{n-j}=\frac{1}{2}\binom{2(n+1)}{n+1}O_{n+1}-\frac{4^{n}}{n+1}.

Proof.

Work with the relation

2\sqrt{1-4x}\cdot\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}x^{n}=-\ln(1-4x)

and apply Cauchy’s product rule.
∎

Corollary 4
.

For integers
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{2j}{j}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}O_{j}O_{n-j}=4^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{H_{n-j}}{j}.

Proof.

Work with the relation

\Big(\frac{\ln(1-4x)}{\sqrt{1-4x}}\Big)^{2}=-\ln(1-4x)\cdot\left(-\frac{\ln(1-4x)}{1-4x}\right)

in conjunction with the series

-\ln(1-x)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n},

and Cauchy’s product rule.
∎

Remark 2
.

We note that due to a result of Carlitz
[
6
]
we also have for integers
r\geq 1

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{2j}{j}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}O_{j}O_{n-j}F_{rj}L_{r(n-j)}=4^{n-1}F_{rn}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{H_{n-j}}{j}.

Corollary 5
.

For integers
n\geq 0
,

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{2j}{j}\frac{O_{j}-H_{n-j}}{4^{j}(n+1-j)}=0.

Proof.

Work with the generating function
[
14
, Entry (7.1)]

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}}{n}x^{n}=\Li_{2}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\ln^{2}(1-x),\quad x\in[-1,1),

where
\Li_{2}(x)
is the dilogarithm function defined, for
|x|\leq 1
, by
\Li_{2}(x)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n^{2}}
. Note that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}}{n}x^{n}-\Li_{2}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n-1}}{n}x^{n}.

Multiply through both sides by
-1/\sqrt{1-4x}
, apply Cauchy’s product rule, and extract the coefficient of
x
. The result is

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{2j}{j}\frac{O_{j}-H_{n-j}}{4^{j}(n+1-j)}=\frac{H_{n}}{n+1},

which is the stated identity.
∎

Theorem 6
.

For integers
n
,
p\geq 0
, we have the following identity:

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{p+j}{j}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}4^{j}O_{n-j}&=2^{2n-1}\binom{n+p+1}{n}\left(H_{n+p+1}-H_{p+1}\right)\\
&\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{p+j}{j-1}4^{j-1}C_{n-j}\left(H_{p+j}-H_{p+1}\right).\end{split}

(11)

In particular,

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{2j}{j}\frac{O_{j}}{4^{j}}=\frac{n+1}{2}(H_{n+1}-1)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{j\,C_{n-j}}{4^{n+1-j}}\left(H_{j}-1\right).

Proof.

We start with the generating function

(1-x)^{-\lambda}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda)_{n}}{n!}x^{n},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\,\,|x|<1.

As

\frac{d}{d\lambda}(\lambda)_{n}=\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+n)}{\Gamma(\lambda)}=(\lambda)_{n}\big(\psi(\lambda+n)-\psi(\lambda)\big)

we get

\frac{d}{d\lambda}(1-x)^{-\lambda}=-\frac{\ln(1-x)}{(1-x)^{\lambda}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda)_{n}}{n!}\big(\psi(\lambda+n)-\psi(\lambda)\big)x^{n}.

Thus, upon replacing
\lambda
by
p+1
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+p}{n}

\displaystyle 4^{n}x^{n}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}x^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4x}\cdot\left(-\frac{\ln(1-4x)}{(1-4x)^{p+2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4x}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+p+1}{n}4^{n}\big(\psi(p+2+n)-\psi(p+2)\big)x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4x}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+p+1}{n}4^{n}\left(H_{p+1+n}-H_{p+1}\right)x^{n},

where in the last step it was used that
\psi(n)=-\gamma+H_{n-1}
. Now, proceed as before, work with Cauchy’s product rule, extract and compare the coefficients of
x^{n}
.
∎

Remark 3
.

An equivalent version of (
11
) is given by

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{p+j}{j}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}4^{j}O_{n-j}=-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{p+j+1}{j}4^{j}C_{n-j-1}(H_{p+j+1}-H_{p+1}),

where
C_{-1}=-1/2
was used.

The next theorem offers a combinatorial expression for the same types of summands as in Theorem
6

but with an additional factor
1/j
.

Theorem 7
.

For all integers
n\geq 1
and
p\geq 0
, we have the following identity:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{p+j}{j}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}\frac{4^{j}}{j}O_{n-j}=-\binom{2n}{n}H_{p}O_{n}+2^{2n-1}S_{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}4^{n-j-1}C_{j}S_{n-j-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{1}{k}\left(4^{n}\binom{n+k}{n}(H_{n+k}-H_{k})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{j+k-1}{j-1}4^{j}(H_{k+j-1}-H_{k})C_{n-j}\right)\!,

where, for all
n\geq 1
,
S_{n}
is defined by

S_{n}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\frac{H_{n-m}}{m},\quad S_{0}=0.

In particular,

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}\frac{4^{j}}{j}O_{n-j}=2^{2n-1}S_{n}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}4^{j-1}C_{n-j}S_{j-1}.

Proof.

From
[
5
, Theorem 3]
it is known that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{n+p}{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n}=-H_{p}-\ln(1-x)+\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{1}{k(1-x)^{k}},\quad|x|<1.

Thus, applying Cauchy’s product rule once more time and keeping in mind that
O_{0}=0
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\binom{n+p}{n}\frac{4^{n}}{n}x^{n}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}x^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{j+p}{j}\frac{4^{j}}{j}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}O_{n-j}\right)x^{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{H_{p}\sqrt{1-4x}}{2}\left(-\frac{\ln(1-4x)}{1-4x}\right)-\frac{\sqrt{1-4x}}{2}\left(-\frac{\ln^{2}(1-4x)}{1-4x}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{\sqrt{1-4x}}{2k}\left(-\frac{\ln(1-4x)}{(1-4x)^{k+1}}\right)\!.

Extract the coefficient of
x^{n}
while making use of

-\frac{\ln^{2}(1-4x)}{1-4x}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}4^{n}S_{n}x^{n}.

(12)

∎

Corollary 8
.

For
n\geq 1
,

S_{n}=2\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\frac{H_{m-1}}{m}.

Proof.

This follows from combining the power series of
\ln^{2}(1-4x)
with the geometric series
(1-4x)^{-1}
, extracting the coefficient of
x^{n}
,
and comparing with (
12
). A second proof can be given by induction on
n
using the elementary identity

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{j(n-j)}=\frac{2H_{n-1}}{n}
.
∎

Theorem 9
.

For integers
n
,
p\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{j+p}{j}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}4^{j}H_{j}O_{n-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{j+p}{j}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}4^{j}O_{n-j}H_{j+p}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{j+p-k}{j}\binom{2(n-j)}{n-j}4^{j}O_{n-j}.

In particular,

H_{n+p}=H_{n}+\frac{1}{\binom{n+p}{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{1}{k}\binom{n+p-k}{n}.

Proof.

Work with the generating function
[
5
, Theorem 3]

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+p}{n}H_{n}x^{n}=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{p+1}}\left(H_{p}-\ln(1-x)-\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{(1-x)^{k}}{k}\right),\quad|x|<1.

(13)

∎

Corollary 10
.

For integers
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{2j}{j}4^{n-j}O_{j}H_{n-j}

\displaystyle=2^{2n-1}(n+1)\Big((1-H_{n+1})^{2}+1-H_{n+1}^{(2)}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_{n-j}4^{j-1}j\Big((1-H_{j})^{2}+1-H_{j}^{(2)}\Big).

Proof.

Work with the relation

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}4^{n}H_{n}x^{n}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}x^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4x}\cdot\Big(-\frac{\ln(1-4x)}{1-4x}\Big)^{2}

and use the convolution identity
[
16
]

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}H_{j}H_{n-j}=(n+1)\left((1-H_{n+1})^{2}+1-H_{n+1}^{(2)}\right).

∎

3
Two additional combinatorial identities derived from the integral

The goal of this section is to derive two additional combinatorial identities using the integral in Lemma
1
.
These identities are stated in Theorem
11
. To prove this theorem, two lemmas will be needed.

Lemma 3
.

For integers
n\geq 0
,

\frac{d^{n}}{da^{n}}\frac{\ln a}{a}=(-1)^{n}n!\,\frac{\ln a-H_{n}}{a^{n+1}}.

Proof.

This follows easily upon applying Leibniz rule for derivatives in conjunction with

\frac{d^{n}}{da^{n}}\ln a=(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!\,a^{-n}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{d^{n}}{da^{n}}a^{-1}=(-1)^{n}n!\,a^{-(n+1)}.

∎

Lemma 4
.

For integers
n\geq 0
,

\frac{d^{n}}{da^{n}}\frac{1}{x^{2}+a^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor}(-1)^{n-k}(2a)^{n-2k}\frac{(n-k)!}{k!}\frac{(n+1-2k)_{2k}}{(x^{2}+a^{2})^{n+1-k}},

where
(\lambda)_{n}
is the Pochhammer symbol.

Proof.

The first two expressions

\frac{d}{da}\frac{1}{x^{2}+a^{2}}=\frac{-2a}{(x^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{d^{2}}{da^{2}}\frac{1}{x^{2}+a^{2}}=\frac{6a^{2}-2x^{2}}{(x^{2}+a^{2})^{3}}

indicate that we can assume that the
n
th derivative can be written in the form

\frac{d^{n}}{da^{n}}\frac{1}{x^{2}+a^{2}}=\frac{Q_{n}(a,x)}{(x^{2}+a^{2})^{n+1}},

where
Q_{n}(a,x)
is a polynomial in
a
of degree
n
. Then

\frac{d^{n+1}}{da^{n+1}}\frac{1}{x^{2}+a^{2}}=\frac{Q_{n+1}(a,x)}{(x^{2}+a^{2})^{n+2}}

with

Q_{n+1}(a,x)=\frac{d}{da}Q_{n}(a,x)(a^{2}+x^{2})-2(n+1)aQ_{n}(a,x)

and
Q_{0}(a,x)=1
.
One checks with little effort that the polynomial

Q_{n}(a,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}(2a)^{n-2k}\frac{(n-k)!}{k!}(n+1-2k)_{2k}(a^{2}+x^{2})^{k}

satisfies these conditions. Finally, as

(n+1-2k)_{2k}=0
for
\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil\leq k\leq n,

the result follows.
∎

Theorem 11
.

Let
(\lambda)_{n}
be the Pochhammer symbol. Then for integers

\displaystyle=2^{n},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{2(n-k)}{n-k}}{\binom{n}{k}}\frac{(n+1-2k)_{2k}}{(k!)^{2}}O_{n-k}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{4n}{2n}\frac{L_{2n+t}}{64^{n}}O_{2n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\left(2L_{t+1}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sqrt{5}}}\,\sqrt{\sqrt{5}F_{2t-1}-2}\right)\ln\alpha\\

Proof.

Insert
x=\alpha/8

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\pi}{2^{2n+1}}\Big(\frac{2m}{\sqrt{5}}L_{2m(2n+1)}\ln\alpha+O_{n}F_{2m(2n+1)}\Big).

and

) and combine according to the Binet formulas (

7
Concluding comments

This work was inspired by the papers by Boyadzhiev
[
4
]
and Chen
[
8
]
.
Our first significant complement is a different proof of Chen’s identity (
5
) using a special family of log-integrals.
Based on this identity we derived several interesting combinatorial sum identities involving Catalan numbers, harmonic numbers,
and odd harmonic numbers. Proceeding further we offered generating functions for some related series and evaluated
them for specific Fibonacci-coefficients in closed form. Finally, we have discussed a family of related integrals
involving Fibonacci numbers and odd harmonic numbers.

We close this paper mentioning that more corollaries and further results can be obtained by writing (
9
) as

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{O_{n}}{4^{n}}x^{n}=-\frac{\ln\sqrt{1-x}}{\sqrt{1-x}},\quad|x|<1,

or

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{O_{n}}{4^{n}}(1-x^{2})^{n}=-\frac{\ln x}{x},\quad|1-x^{2}|<1.

We immediately have the trigonometric versions

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}\frac{{\cos^{n}x}}{4^{n}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ln\big(\sqrt{2}\,|{\sin(x/2)}|\big)}{\sqrt{2}\left|{\sin(x/2)}\right|},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}\frac{\cos^{n}x}{4^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln\big(\sqrt{2}\,|\cos(x/2)|\big)}{\sqrt{2}|\cos(x/2)|},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}\frac{{\cos^{2n}x}}{{4^{n}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ln\left|{\sin x}\right|}{{\left|{\sin x}\right|}},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}O_{n}\frac{{\sin^{2n}x}}{{4^{n}}}=-\frac{\ln\left|{\cos x}\right|}{{\left|{\cos x}\right|}}.

By utilizing suitable values of
x
from these formulas, we can derive several series that involve odd harmonic numbers. Here are some examples:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{O_{n}}{8^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\ln 2,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\Big(\frac{3}{16}\Big)^{n}O_{n}

\displaystyle=2\ln 2,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{5}}{16\alpha}\Big)^{n}O_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\alpha}\ln\Big(\frac{\alpha}{2}\Big),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{5}\alpha}{16}\Big)^{n}O_{n}

\displaystyle={2}{\alpha}\ln(2{\alpha}).

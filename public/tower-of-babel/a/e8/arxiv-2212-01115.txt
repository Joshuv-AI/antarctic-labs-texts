Some properties of the solution of the vertical tensor complementarity problem Thanks: This research was supported by National Natural Science Foundations of China (No. 11961082).
Li-Ming Li, Shi-Liang Wu

Abstract
In this paper, we mainly focus on the existence and uniqueness of the vertical tensor complementarity problem. Firstly, combining the generalized-order linear complementarity problem with the tensor complementarity problem, the vertical tensor complementarity problem is introduced. Secondly, we define some sets of special tensors, and illustrate the inclusion relationships. Finally, we show that the solution set of the vertical tensor complementarity problem is bounded under certain conditions, and some sufficient conditions for the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the vertical tensor complementarity problem are obtained from the view of the degree theory and the equal form of the minimum function. Keywords: The vertical tensor complementarity problem; special tensor sets; degree theory; the minimum function Mathematics Subject Classification: 90C33, 90C30, 65H10

Introduction

Over the past few decades, many mathematical researchers have conducted
extensive research on the nonlinear complementarity problem. Today,
the nonlinear complementarity problem has become a classical problem,

In recent years, with the rise of tensor research, a special case of

in
[

is
defined as

\left(\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}\right)_{i}=\sum_{i_{2},\cdots,i_{n}}^{n}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{n}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{n}},i\in[n],

which is denoted by TCP
\left(\mathcal{A},q\right)
. They studied
the existence of the solution of the TCP
\left(\mathcal{A},q\right)
through the structure of the tensor, and obtained a
sufficient and necessary condition for the non-negative symmetric
tensor
\mathcal{A}
. Bai et al.
[

,

,
9
,
8
,

,

,

,

Goeleven
[

introduced the

In this paper, combining the generalized-order linear

It is a brief description for the rest of paper. In Section 2, we go

3
Sets of tensors with special structures

In this section, we introduce some sets of tensors with special structures.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\right\}
with
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in R^{\left[m,n\right]}
. We say that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is of

1.

type
VR_{0}
if
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\wedge\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x=\theta\Rightarrow x=\theta
;

2.

type
VE
if
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\wedge\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x\leq\theta\Rightarrow x=\theta
;

3.

type
VP
if
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\wedge\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x\leq\theta\leq\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\vee\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x\Rightarrow x=\theta
;

4.

type
VP
-
I
if
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\wedge\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x\leq\theta\leq\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\vee\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x
have no solution in
\left(R_{+}^{n}/\left\{\theta\right\}\right)\cup\left(R_{-}^{n}/\left\{\theta\right\}\right)
;

5.

type
VP
-
II
if there is no tensor
\mathcal{Z}\in S^{\left[m-1,n\right]}
, whose principal diagonal elements are not all zeros such that
\mathcal{A}_{1}\mathcal{Z}\wedge\mathcal{A}_{2}\mathcal{Z}\leq\theta\leq\mathcal{A}_{1}\mathcal{Z}\vee\mathcal{A}_{2}\mathcal{Z}
;

6.

type semi-positive if there exists a vector
x>\theta
such that
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x>\theta
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x>\theta
;

7.

type
VQ
if the VTCP
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{q})
has a solution for all
q_{1},q_{2}\in R^{n}
.

Remark 3.1
.

For Definition
3.1
, the condition of item 1 is equivalent to that the following system is
inconsistent:

\begin{cases}x\neq\theta,\\
\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x\geq\theta,\\
\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\right)_{i}=\theta,\text{ if }\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x\right)_{i}>\theta,\\
\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\right)_{j}\geq\theta,\text{ if }\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x\right)_{j}=\theta;\par\end{cases}

(3.1)

the condition of item 2 is equivalent to that
\forall x\neq\theta
,
\exists i\in\left[n\right]
satisfies
\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\right)_{i}>\theta
,
\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x\right)_{i}>\theta
; the condition of
item 3 is equivalent to that
\forall x\neq\theta
,
\exists i\in\left[n\right]
satisfies
\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\right)_{i}\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x\right)_{i}>\theta
; the
condition of item 4 is equivalent to that
\forall x\in\left(R_{+}^{n}/\left\{\theta\right\}\right)\cup\left(R_{-}^{n}/\left\{\theta\right\}\right)
,
\exists i\in\left[n\right]
satisfies
\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\right)_{i}\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x\right)_{i}>\theta
; the
condition of item 5 is equivalent to that
\forall\mathcal{Z}\in S^{\left[m-1,n\right]}
with its principal diagonal elements
being not all zeros,
\exists i\in\left[n\right]
satisfies
\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\mathcal{Z}\right)_{i}\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\mathcal{Z}\right)_{i}>\theta
.

4
Solution of the VTCP

In this section, we show that the solution set of the
VTCP
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{q})
is bounded under certain
conditions, and some sufficient conditions for the existence and
uniqueness of the solution of the VTCP
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{q})
are obtained from the view of the degree theory and the equal
form of the minimum function.

To show that the solution set of the VTCP
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{q})
is bounded under certain conditions, we give the
following result, see Theorem 4.1.

Theorem 4.1
.

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is of type
VR_{0}
if and only if the solution
set of the VTCP
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{q})
for all
q_{1},q_{2}\in R^{n}
is bounded.

Proof.

It is now proved that the following three conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is of type
VR_{0}
;

(ii)

\forall q_{1},q_{2}\in R^{n}
,
t>0
,
s\in R
, the

\displaystyle\tau\left(q_{1},q_{2},s,t\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\{x:q_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{1}x^{m-1}\geq\theta,q_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{2}x^{m-1}\geq\theta,\left(q_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{1}x^{m-1}\right)^{T}\left(q_{2}+t\mathcal{A}_{2}x^{m-1}\right)\leq s\right\}

is bounded;

(iii)

The solution set of the VTCP
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{q})
for all
q_{1},q_{2}\in R^{n}
is bounded.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Assume that there exist
q_{1}^{*},q_{2}^{*}\in R^{n}
,
t^{*}>0
and
s^{*}\in R
such that the set
\tau\left(q_{1}^{*},q_{2}^{*},s^{*},t^{*}\right)
is not bounded.
Then there is an unbounded sequence
\left\{x^{k}\right\}\in\tau\left(q_{1}^{*},q_{2}^{*},s^{*},t^{*}\right)
.
Since the sequence
\{\frac{x^{k}}{\|x^{k}\|}\}
is bounded, there is a convergence subsequence
\{\frac{x^{k_{j}}}{\|x^{k_{j}}\|}\}
such that when
j\rightarrow\infty
,

\frac{x^{k_{j}}}{\left\|x^{k_{j}}\right\|}\rightarrow x_{1}\neq\theta
and
\left\|x^{k_{j}}\right\|\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}\!\left(\!\frac{x^{k^{j}}}{\left\|x^{k^{j}}\right\|}\!\right)^{m-1}\!+\!\frac{q_{1}^{*}}{\left(\!\left\|x^{k^{j}}\right\|\!\right)^{m-1}}\geq\theta,\mathcal{A}_{2}\left(\!\frac{x^{k^{j}}}{\left\|x^{k^{j}}\right\|}\!\right)^{m-1}\!+\!\frac{q_{2}^{*}}{\left(\!\left\|x^{k^{j}}\right\|\!\right)^{m-1}}\geq\theta,

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{A}_{1}\left(\!\frac{x^{k^{j}}}{\left\|x^{k^{j}}\right\|}\!\right)^{m-1}\!+\!\frac{q_{1}^{*}}{\left(\!\left\|x^{k^{j}}\right\|\!\right)^{m-1}}\right]^{T}\left[t^{*}\mathcal{A}_{2}\left(\!\frac{x^{k^{j}}}{\left\|x^{k^{j}}\right\|}\!\right)^{m-1}\!+\!\frac{q_{2}^{*}}{\left(\left\|x^{k^{j}}\right\|\right)^{m-1}}\right]\leq\frac{s^{*}}{\left(\left\|x^{k^{j}}\right\|\right)^{2m-2}}.

Let
j\rightarrow+\infty
. Then

\mathcal{A}_{1}\left(x_{1}\right)^{m-1}\geq\theta,\mathcal{A}_{2}\left(x_{1}\right)^{m-1}\geq\theta,\left[\mathcal{A}_{1}\left(x_{1}\right)^{m-1}\right]^{T}\left[t^{*}\mathcal{A}_{2}\left(x_{1}\right)^{m-1}\right]\leq\theta.

Thus,
\left[\mathcal{A}_{1}\left(x_{1}\right)^{m-1}\right]^{T}\left[\mathcal{A}_{2}\left(x_{1}\right)^{m-1}\right]=\theta
. This contradicts that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is of type
VR_{0}
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: Let
s=0
,
t=1
. Then the set

\displaystyle\tau\left(q_{1},q_{2},0,1\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\{x:q_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{1}x^{m-1}\geq 0,q_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{2}x^{m-1}\geq 0,\left(q_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{1}x^{m-1}\right)^{T}\left(q_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{2}x^{m-1}\right)\leq 0\right\}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\{x:q_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{1}x^{m-1}\geq 0,q_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{2}x^{m-1}\geq 0,\left(q_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{1}x^{m-1}\right)^{T}\left(q_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{2}x^{m-1}\right)=0\right\}

is bounded, which implies that the solution set of the VTCP
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{q})
is bounded.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Assume that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is not of type
VR_{0}
. There is
x\neq\theta
such that
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cdot x\wedge\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot x=\theta
. Hence,
kx\in\tau\left(\theta,\theta,0,1\right)
for all
k\geq\theta
. This contradicts the condition
(iii)
.

By the above discussion, the conclusion in Theorem
4.1

holds.
∎

Next, from the view of the degree theory, we discuss the existence
of the solution of the VTCP
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{q})

Here is a brief review of degree theory.
Suppose that
\Omega
is a bounded open set in
R^{n}
.
\partial\Omega
and
\overline{\Omega}
represent the boundary of

\Omega
and the set
\partial\Omega\cup\Omega

continuous function
f:\overline{\Omega}\longrightarrow R^{n}
be
given and
p\notin f\left(\partial\Omega\right)
. Then the degree
of
f=p
on
\Omega
can be well defined and denoted as
deg\left(f,\Omega,p\right)
, which is an integer and implies the number of solutions for
f=p
,
see
[
10
]
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
be of type
VR_{0}
,

F_{\left(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\right)}=\mathcal{A}_{1}x^{m-1}\wedge\mathcal{A}_{2}x^{m-1}.

Then
F_{\left(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\right)}=\theta\Rightarrow x=\theta
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set that contains
\theta
. Then
deg\left(F_{\left(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\right)},\Omega,\theta\right)
is defined. Furthermore,
deg\left(F_{\left(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\right)},\Omega,\theta\right)
is independent of the bounded open set
\Omega
. We denote it by VTCP-
deg\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
.

According to the degree theory, the following results can be obtained.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}

4.1
, the set

X=\left\{x:F\left(x,t\right)=\theta,t\in\left[0,1\right]\right\}

is bounded. Hence, there is a bounded open set
\Omega
containing the set

X\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
.
Obviously,
F\left(x,t\right)
is a homotopy connecting the mappings

By the homotopy invariance property of the degree
[

, we obtain

is nonzero, together with the

[

]

and
g(0)=c+b_{2}<0
, both
f(x_{2})
and

g(x_{2})
have only two zero points with different signs. It is
clear that (
4.13
) has a unique positive solution

x_{2}=x_{*}
. Thus, the VTCP
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{q})
has
a unique positive solution
x=\left(\sqrt{-c},x_{*}\right)^{T}
.

]
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Assume that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\right\}
is of type
VR_{0}
, where

\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in R^{\left[m,n\right]}

and
m
is an even number. If
\mathcal{A}_{1}
has an even order
right inverse
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\in R^{\left[k,n\right]}
such that
\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}
is a

R
-tensor, then
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is of type
VQ
.

Proof.

Let

G\left(x,t\right)=x\wedge\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot x^{\left[\frac{1}{\left(m-1\right)\left(k-1\right)}\right]}+td\right)

and

H\left(x,t\right)=x\wedge\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot\left(x-tq_{1}\right)^{\left[\frac{1}{\left(m-1\right)\left(k-1\right)}\right]}+tq_{2}\right),

where
t\in[0,1]
,
d>0
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in R^{n}
.
Obviously, for a given constant
t^{*}\in[0,1]
, if vector
y
is a solution of
G\left(x,t^{*}\right)=\theta
, then there is a solution

x=\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot y^{\left[\frac{1}{\left(m-1\right)\left(k-1\right)}\right]}

of
\mathcal{A}_{1}x^{m-1}\wedge\left(t^{*}d+\mathcal{A}_{2}x^{m-1}\right)=\theta
corresponding to it. If
y
is a solution of
H\left(x,t^{*}\right)
, then
x=\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot\left(y-t^{*}q_{1}\right)^{\left[\frac{1}{\left(m-1\right)\left(k-1\right)}\right]}
is a solution of

\left(t^{*}q_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{1}x^{m-1}\right)\wedge\left(t^{*}q_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{2}x^{m-1}\right)=\theta.

By Theorem
4.1
, the solution sets of
G\left(x,t\right)=\theta
and
H\left(x,t\right)=\theta
are bounded for any
t\in[0,1]
.
So, there exists a bounded open set
\Omega
that contains the sets

X_{1}=\left\{x:G\left(x,t\right)=\theta,t\in[0,1]\right\}~and~X_{2}=\left\{x:H\left(x,t\right)=\theta,t\in[0,1]\right\},

and
\left(X_{1}\cup X_{2}\right)\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
.
Since
\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}
is a
R
-tensor, there is no solution to the following system

\begin{cases}\theta\neq x\geq 0,t\geq 0,\\
\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot x\right)_{i}+t=\theta,if~x_{i}>\theta,\\
\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot x\right)_{j}+t\geq\theta,if~x_{j}=\theta,\end{cases}

(4.14)

which implies that there is no solution to the following system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\theta\neq x\geq 0,t\geq 0,\\
\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot x^{\left[\frac{1}{\left(m-1\right)\left(k-1\right)}\right]}\right)_{i}+t=\theta,if~x_{i}>\theta,\\
\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot x^{\left[\frac{1}{\left(m-1\right)\left(k-1\right)}\right]}\right)_{j}+t\geq\theta,if~x_{j}=\theta.\end{cases}

(4.15)

In fact, assume that there is an
x
that satisfies (
4.15
).
Let
y=x^{\left[\frac{1}{\left(m-1\right)\left(k-1\right)}\right]}
. Then

x_{i}>\theta\Leftrightarrow y_{i}>\theta,x_{i}=\theta\Leftrightarrow y_{i}=\theta.

Thus,
y
is a solution of (
4.14
), which contradicts that
\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}
is a
R
-tensor.
From (
4.15
),

G\left(x,t\right)=x\wedge\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot x^{\left[\frac{1}{\left(m-1\right)\left(k-1\right)}\right]}+td\right)=\theta

has only the zero solution for any
t\in[0,1]
and
d>0
.
Since
d>0
,

\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot x^{\left[\frac{1}{\left(m-1\right)\left(k-1\right)}\right]}+d>\theta~and~G\left(x,1\right)=x\wedge\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}^{R_{k}}\cdot x^{\left[\frac{1}{\left(m-1\right)\left(k-1\right)}\right]}+d\right)=x

for
x
near
\theta
.
Thus,
degree\left(G\left(x,1\right),\Omega,\theta\right)=1
.
By the homotopy invariance property of the degree
[
21
]
,

degree\left(G\left(x,1\right),\Omega,\theta\right)=degree\left(G\left(x\right),\Omega,\theta\right)=1,

where
G\left(x\right)=G\left(x,0\right)
.
Obviously,
H\left(x\right)=G\left(x\right)
, where
H\left(x\right)=H\left(x,0\right)
. By the homotopy invariance theorem
[
21
]
,

degree\left(H\left(x,1\right),\Omega,\theta\right)=degree\left(H\left(x\right),\Omega,\theta\right)=degree\left(G\left(x\right),\Omega,\theta\right)=1.

Thus,
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is of type
VQ
.
∎

4.5
and Lemma
4.7
,

.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we have introduced the vertical tensor
complementarity problem (VTCP), and defined some tensor sets with
special structures. We obtained an equivalence condition, i.e., the
tenor set
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is of type
VR_{0}
if and only if
the solution set of the VTCP
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{q})
is
bounded. Meanwhile, we obtained some sufficient conditions for the
existence and uniqueness of the solution of the VTCP from two
aspects: the degree theory and the equal form of minimum function.

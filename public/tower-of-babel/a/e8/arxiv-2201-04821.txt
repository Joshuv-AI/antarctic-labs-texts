Admissible metrics on compact Kähler varieties
Wenhao Ou

Abstract
. Let X X be a normal compact Kähler variety, and ℱ {\mathcal{F}} a coherent reflexive sheaf on X X . We investigate the existence of admissible Hermitian metrics on ℱ {\mathcal{F}} . If moreover ℱ {\mathcal{F}} is slope stable, we also study the existence of admissible Hermitian-Yang-Mills metrics on it. The existence will hold if one can prove a uniform Sobolev inequality on singular spaces.



1.
Introduction

The theory of holomorphic vector bundles is a central object in algebraic geometry and analytic geometry.
The notion of stable vector bundles on complete curves was introduced by Mumford in
[
Mum63
]
.
Such notion of stability was then extended to torsion-free sheaves on any projective manifolds (see
[
Tak72
]
,
[
Gie77
]
), and is now known as slope stability, or Gieseker stability.
An important property of stable bundles is the following Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality, involving the Chern classes of the bundle.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a projective manifold of dimension
n
, and let
H
be an ample divisor.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a
H
-stable vector bundle of rank
r
on
X
.
Then

\Big(c_{2}({\mathcal{F}})-\frac{r-1}{2r}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})^{2}\Big)\cdot H^{n-2}\geqslant 0.

When
X
is a surface, the inequality was proved in
[
Bog78
]
.
In higher dimension, one may apply Mehta-Ramanathan theorem in
[
MR82
]
to reduce to the case of surfaces, by taking hyperplane sections.
Later in
[
Kaw92
]
, as a part of the proof for the three-dimensional abundance theorem, Kawamata extended the inequality to reflexive sheaves on projective surfaces with quotient singularities.
The same technique then allows us to deduce the Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality for reflexive sheaves on projective varieties which has quotient singularities in codimension two.

On the analytic side, let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold, and
({\mathcal{F}},H)
a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle on
X
.
Lübke proved that if
H
satisfies the Einstein condition, then the following inequality holds (see
[
Lüb82
]
),

\int_{X}\Big(c_{2}({\mathcal{F}},H)-\frac{r-1}{2r}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}},H)^{2}\Big)\wedge\omega^{n-2}\geqslant 0.

We recall that
H
satisfies the Einstein condition if its Chern curvature
F
satisfies

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F=\lambda\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}},

where
\Lambda
is the contraction with
\omega
and
\lambda
is a constant.
In this case,
H
is called a Hermitian-Einstein metric, or a Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric.
It is now well understood that if
{\mathcal{F}}
is
\omega
-stable, then it admits a Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric.
The case when
X
is a complete curve was proved by Narasimhan-Seshadri in
[
NS65
]
, the case of projective surfaces was proved by Donaldson in
[
Don85
]
, and the case of arbitrary compact Kähler manifolds was proved by Uhlenbeck-Yau in
[
UY86
]
.
As a consequence, one deduces a metric version for the proof of the previous Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality.
An advantage of the analytic method is to give an insight to the equality condition.
Simpson extended the existence of Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric to stable Higgs bundles, on compact and certain non compact Kähler manifolds, in
[
Sim88
]
.
Furthermore, in
[
BS94
]
, Bando-Siu introduced the notion of admissible metrics and prove the existence of admissible Hermitian-Yang-Mills metrics on stable reflexive sheaves.

In
[
Don85
]
, Donaldson considered the following heat equation in metrics
H_{t}
on a vector bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
.

(1.1)

H_{t}^{-1}(\frac{\mathrm{\partial}}{\mathrm{\partial}t}H_{t})=-(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{t}-\lambda({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}})

where
\mu({\mathcal{F}})
is the slope of
{\mathcal{F}}
,
\lambda({\mathcal{F}})=\mu({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Vol}(X)^{-1}
and
F_{t}
is the Chern curvature of
H_{t}
.
He proved this equation admits a solution until infinite time, when
X
is a smooth projective surface.
Then by using Yang-Mills theory, it was shown that, up to gauge transforms, there is a sequence

\{F_{t_{i}}\}
which converges to a Hermitian-Yang-Mills connection
F
.
Thanks to the theorem of removable singularities, the connection
F
defines a holomorphic structure on the entire smooth vector bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
.
The stability condition then imposes that the new holomorphic structure coincides with the initial one, and hence
F
is a Hermitian-Yang-Mills connection for the holomorphic bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
.

The method of Uhlenbeck-Yau in
[
UY86
]
is different.
For a fixed initial metric
H_{0}
on the bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
, they considered the following perturbed equations in metrics
H=H_{0}h
,

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H}-\lambda({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}-\epsilon\log\,h=0.

Such an equation always admits a solution
h_{\epsilon}
for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
.
Then they argued by contradiction that, if
h_{\epsilon}
are not uniformly
L^{1}
-bounded, then
there is a
L_{1}^{2}
-subbundle (or weakly holomorphic subbundle) of
{\mathcal{F}}
, which induces a destabilizer for
{\mathcal{F}}
.
Once the uniform boundedness is obtained, one can extract a sequence
\{H_{0}h_{\epsilon_{i}}\}
which converges to a Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric.

The method of Simpson in
[
Sim88
]
is a combination of both methods above.
He proved the heat solution (
1.1
) admits a solution
H_{t}
until infinite time.
Then arguing as in
[
UY86
]
, he deduced a sequence
\{H_{t_{i}}\}
which converges to a Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric.

The main objective of this note is to study this problem for singular compact Kähler varieties
X
, with the methods of Bando-Siu and of Simpson.
The idea is as follows.
We take a desingularization
r\colon\widehat{X}\to X

such that
\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}=(r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*}))^{*}
becomes locally free.
Then we are able to solve the heat equations in
\widehat{X}
for
\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}
, with respect to small perturbations
\omega_{\epsilon}
of
r^{*}\omega
.
When
X
is smooth, Bando-Siu showed that these solutions
H_{\epsilon}
have a limit point
H
when
\epsilon
tends to zero, and such
H
is a solution for the initial heat equation on
(X,\omega)
.
This relies on the next two ingredients.
First, the solutions
H_{\epsilon}
admit estimates involving the heat kernels
K_{\epsilon}
of
(X,\omega_{\epsilon})
.
Furthermore, Bando-Siu proved a uniform Sobolev inequality for
(X,\omega_{\epsilon})
, which implies a uniform estimate on the heat kernels
K_{\epsilon}
.

There would be some difficulties when one tries to adapt this method on singular varieties.
Actually, the arguments involving local elliptic/parabolic estimates always remain valid.
But one needs to be careful while getting global estimates.
The first problem is the uniform Sobolev inequalities for singular varieties.
We conjecture that it holds.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler variety of dimension
n
.
Let
p\colon Y\to X
be a blowup at smooth center, and
\eta
a Kähler form on
Y
.
For each
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, we consider the following Kähler form on
Y
.

\omega_{\epsilon}=r^{*}\omega+\epsilon\eta.

Then
(Y,\omega_{\epsilon})
has a uniform Sobolev constant for all
0<\epsilon\leqslant 1
.
That is, there exists a constant
C_{S}
, independent of
\epsilon
, such that for any
\mathcal{C}^{1}
function
f\geqslant 0
, compactly supported in the smooth locus of
Y
, the following inequality holds,

(\int_{Y}|f|^{\frac{2n}{n-1}}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leqslant C_{S}\int_{Y}(||\nabla_{\omega_{\epsilon}}f||^{2}+|f|^{2})\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}.

Another difficulty is that, when
X
has singularities, the heat kernel and the maximum principal are less trivial.
To deal with this issue, we will investigate heat kernels on singular compact Kähler varieties in Section
4
.
Particularly, we prove the following assertion (see Corollary
4.6
), extending a result of Li-Tian in
[
LT95
]
.
It also implies
[
DNGG20
, Conjecture 3.1]
(see Corollary
4.8
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
be a smooth Zariski open dense subset of a compact Kähler variety
(X,\omega)
, and
K
the heat kernel of
(X^{\circ},\omega)
.
Then the following properties hold.

(1)

K
is bounded on
X^{\circ}\times X^{\circ}\times[t_{1},t_{2}]
for any fixed
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
.

(2)

K(x,\cdot,t)
belongs to
L_{1}^{2}
.

(3)

\int_{y\in X^{\circ}}|\Delta_{y}K(x,y,t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y<\infty.

(4)

\int_{y\in X^{\circ}}K(x,y,t)\mathrm{d}y=1.

Furthermore, instead of getting estimates of the limit solution
H
directly from
(X,\omega)
, we will converge the estimates of
H_{\epsilon}
.
For this reason, additional uniform estimates on
H_{\epsilon}
are needed in our arguments.

After we obtain the solution of the heat equation, Simpson’s method does not apply immediately yet.
Three assumptions were made for the non compact manifolds in
[
Sim88
]
, and it was proved that any Zariski open dense subset of a compact Kähler manifold satisfies the assumptions.
It is not trivial to us that this still holds while singularities are present.
We will handle this problem in Section
6
.

After all, if Conjecture
1.2
holds, we can deduce the existence of admissible metrics on normal compact Kähler varieties.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume Conjecture
1.2
.
Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact normal Kähler variety. Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a reflexive sheaf on
X
.
Then there is some initial Hermitian metric
H_{0}
on
{\mathcal{F}}
, such that the heat equation (
1.1
) admits a solution until infinite time.
Moreover,
H_{t}
are admissible for all
t>0
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume Conjecture
1.2
.
Let
(X,\omega)
be a normal compact Kähler variety.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a reflexive stable sheaf on
X
.
Then there exists an admissible Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric on
{\mathcal{F}}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume Conjecture
1.2
.
Let
(X,\omega)
be a normal compact Kähler variety of dimension
n
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a coherent reflexive stable sheaf of rank
r
on
X
.
It admits an admissible Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric
H
,
and we have the following inequality

\int_{X}\Big(c_{2}({\mathcal{F}},H)-\frac{r-1}{2r}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}},H)^{2}\Big)\wedge\omega^{n-2}\geqslant 0.

The equality holds if and only if the Chern connection is projectively flat.

This note is organized as follows. We will first recall some basic notion in Section
2
.
In Section
3
, we will construct an initial metric for the heat equation. Such a metric also enable us to define the slope of a reflexive sheaf.
In Section
4
, we will study heat kernels on compact Kähler varieties.
We will prove Theorem
1.4
in Section
5
.
Finally in Section
6
, we will complete the proofs of Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.6
.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we will fix some conventions for the paper, and recall some elementary results.
Throughout this paper, we use the symbol
\mathcal{C}^{p}
(respectively
\mathcal{C}^{p,\alpha}
) for functions whose
p
-th derivatives exist and are continuous (respectively
\alpha
-Hölder continuous).
We use the symbol
L^{p}
for functions whose
p
-th power is integrable,
L^{\infty}
for bounded functions, and

L_{i}^{p}
for functions whose derivatives, up to order
i
, are
L^{p}
.
We denote the
L^{p}
-norm by
||\cdot||_{p}
for any
0<p\leqslant\infty
.

2.A.
Possibly singular varieties.

For a complex space
X
, we denote its smooth locus by
X_{reg}
.
After
[
Gra62
]
, a Kähler variety
(X,\omega)
is an integral complex space
X
, together with a real closed
(1,1)
-form
\omega
,
such that for any point
x\in X
, there is an open analytic neighbourhood
U
of
x
such that
U
is isomorphic to a analytic subvariety of some open domain
V
of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, and
\omega|_{U}
is the restriction of some Kähler form on
V
.
By a
L_{i}^{p}
function
f
on
X
, we mean a function
f
belonging to
L_{i}^{p}(X_{reg})
.

When
X
is smooth, the we can define the gradient operator
\nabla_{\omega}
and the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\omega}
, with respect to the Riemannian metric induced by
\omega
on
X
.
More precisely, we use the convention

\Delta_{\omega}=2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial{\overline{\partial}},

where
\Lambda_{\omega}
is the contraction with
\omega
.
In particular, the Laplace-Beltrami operator has its sign as the one of
\frac{\partial^{2}}{(\partial x)^{2}}
.

The following lemma was established in the proof of
[
LT95
, Theorem 4.1 ]
.
We will use it to replace
[
Sim88
, Assumption 2]
in our demonstration on the existence of Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be a compact real variety without boundary, and
M^{\circ}\subseteq M
a smooth open subset.
Assume that the real codimension of
M\backslash M^{\circ}
is at least two.
Then there exists a sequence of smooth cut-off functions
\{\varphi_{i}\}
on
M^{\circ}
, with limit equal to the constant function
1
, such that
||\nabla{\varphi_{i}}||_{2}\to 0
when
i\to\infty
.

As a consequence, we have the following two formulas of integration by parts.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
M
be a compact real variety without boundary, and
M^{\circ}\subseteq M
a smooth open subset.
Assume that the real codimension of
M\backslash M^{\circ}
is at least two.
Then for bounded functions
u,v\in L^{2}_{1}(M^{\circ})
such that
\Delta v
exists and is integrable, we have

\int_{M}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=-\int_{M}u\Delta v.

Proof.

We choose a sequence of smooth cut-off functions
\{\varphi_{i}\}
as in Lemma
2.1
.
Then we have

\int_{M}\varphi_{i}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\int_{M}u\nabla\varphi_{i}\cdot\nabla v=-\int_{M}\varphi_{i}u\Delta v.

Since
u,v\in L^{2}_{1}(M)
are assumed to be bounded, and since
\Delta v
is integrable, we can take the limit for
i\to\infty
in the previous equality,
and deduce the corollary.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
M
be a compact real variety without boundary, and
M^{\circ}\subseteq M
a smooth open subset.
Assume that the real codimension of
M\backslash M^{\circ}
is at least two.
Then for a smooth 1-form
\xi
on
M^{\circ}
with
||\xi||_{\omega}^{2}
and
\mathrm{d}\xi
integrable, we have

\int_{M}\mathrm{d}\xi\wedge\omega^{n-1}=0.

Proof.

We choose a sequence of smooth cut-off functions
\{\varphi_{i}\}
as in Lemma
2.1
.
Then we have

0=\int_{M}\mathrm{d}(\varphi_{i}\xi)\wedge\omega^{n-1}=\int_{M}\mathrm{d}\varphi_{i}\wedge\xi\wedge\omega^{n-1}+\int_{M}\varphi_{i}\mathrm{d}\xi\wedge\omega^{n-1}

Since
||\xi||_{\omega}^{2}
is integrable and since
||\nabla{\varphi_{i}}||_{2}\to 0
when
i\to\infty
, we see that

\int_{M}\mathrm{d}\varphi_{i}\wedge\xi\wedge\omega^{n-1}\to 0.

Moreover, since
\mathrm{d}\xi
is integrable, we have

\int_{M}\varphi_{i}\mathrm{d}\xi\wedge\omega^{n-1}\to\int_{M}\mathrm{d}\xi\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

The corollary then follows.
∎

2.B.
Hermitian sheaves

Let
({\mathcal{F}},H)
be a Hermitian vector bundle on a complex manifold
X
.
We regard
H
as a selfadjoint conjugate-linear morphism from
{\mathcal{F}}
to its dual bundle
{\mathcal{F}}^{*}={\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
.
We note that the adjoint morphism is the
conjugate
of the dual morphism.
In particular, for any smooth section
h
of
{\mathcal{E}}nd{({\mathcal{E}})}
, for any complex number
\lambda
, we have
(\lambda h)^{*}=\overline{\lambda}h^{*}
for the adjoint operation
*
.
We note that the trace

\mathrm{tr}\,h
is a well-defined smooth function on
X
.
We can hence define a Hermitian pairing
<\cdot,\cdot>_{H}
on
{\mathcal{E}}nd{({\mathcal{F}})}
with respect to
H
as follows.
For any smooth sections
h
and
g
of
{\mathcal{E}}nd{({\mathcal{F}})}
, we set

<h,g>_{H}=\mathrm{tr}\,(h\circ H^{-1}\circ g^{*}\circ H)=\mathrm{tr}\,(hH^{-1}g^{*}H).

We denote the corresponding norm by
||h||_{H}=\sqrt{<h,h>_{H}}
.
We say that
h
is bounded or integrable if
||h||_{H}
is.

If
H^{\prime}
is another Hermitian metric on
H
, and if we write
h=H^{-1}H^{\prime}
, then it follows that
h
is selfadjoint with respect to both
H
and
H^{\prime}
.
That is

h^{*}H=Hh\mbox{ and }h^{*}H^{\prime}=H^{\prime}h.

In particular, we have

||h||_{H}=||h||_{H^{\prime}}=\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}\,h^{2}}.

We will denote by
\partial_{H}
the
(1,0)
-part of the Chern connection, on
{\mathcal{F}}
or on
{\mathcal{E}}nd({\mathcal{F}})
.
We define

\Delta_{H}=-2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda{\overline{\partial}}\partial_{H}.

The following computations can be found in
[
Sim88
, Lemma 3.1]

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}},H)
be a Hermitian vector bundle on a Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
.
Assume that
s
and
h
are smooth sections of
{\mathcal{E}}nd{({\mathcal{F}})}
, selfadjoint with respect to
H
. Assume that
h
is definite positive. We denote by
F_{H}
and
F_{Hh}
the corresponding Chern curvatures.
The following properties hold.

(1)

||({\overline{\partial}}s)h||_{H,\omega}^{2}=-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\mathrm{tr}\,({\overline{\partial}}s)h^{2}\partial_{H}s.

(2)

\Delta_{H}h=2\sqrt{-1}h(\Lambda F_{H}-\Lambda F_{Hh})-2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda({\overline{\partial}}h)h^{-1}\partial_{H}h.

(3)

\Delta\log\,(\mathrm{tr}\,h)\geqslant-2(||\Lambda F_{H}||_{H}+||\Lambda F_{Hh}||_{Hh}).

Thanks to item (1) of the previous lemma, we say that
s
is
L_{1}^{2}
if
||({\overline{\partial}}s)||_{H,\omega}^{2}
is integrable.
The following estimate was essentially obtained in
[
UY86
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}},H)
be a Hermitian vector bundle on a Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
.
Let
s
be a smooth section of
{\mathcal{E}}nd({\mathcal{F}})
, selfadjoint with respect to
H
.
We write
h=e^{s}
. Assume that
||s||_{H}\leqslant C
for some positive constant
C
.
Then there are two positive constants
0<C_{1}\leqslant C_{2}
, depending only on
C
, such that

C_{1}||{\overline{\partial}}s||_{H,\omega}\leqslant||{\overline{\partial}}h||_{H,\omega}\leqslant C_{2}||{\overline{\partial}}s||_{H,\omega}.

Proof.

Let
(\mathbf{e}_{\alpha})
be a smooth
H
-orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{F}}
, which diagonalizes
s
, and hence
h
.
Let
(\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}^{*})
be the dual basis of
{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
.
Then
h=e^{\lambda_{\alpha}}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}
, and
s=\lambda_{\alpha}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}
, for real-valued functions
\lambda_{\alpha}
.
We write
{\overline{\partial}}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}=A_{\alpha}^{\beta}\mathbf{e}_{\beta}
.
It follows that
{\overline{\partial}}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}^{*}=-A^{\alpha}_{\beta}\mathbf{e}_{\beta}^{*}
.
Then we have

{\overline{\partial}}h=e^{\lambda_{\alpha}}{\overline{\partial}}\lambda_{\alpha}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}+(e^{\lambda_{\alpha}}-e^{\lambda_{\beta}})A_{\alpha}^{\beta}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\beta},

and

{\overline{\partial}}s={\overline{\partial}}\lambda_{\alpha}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}+({\lambda_{\alpha}}-{\lambda_{\beta}})A_{\alpha}^{\beta}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\beta}.

Hence

||{\overline{\partial}}h||_{H,\omega}^{2}=e^{2\lambda_{\alpha}}||{\overline{\partial}}\lambda_{\alpha}||^{2}_{\omega}+(e^{\lambda_{\alpha}}-e^{\lambda_{\beta}})^{2}||A_{\alpha}^{\beta}||^{2}_{\omega},

and

||{\overline{\partial}}s||_{H,\omega}^{2}=||{\overline{\partial}}\lambda_{\alpha}||^{2}_{\omega}+({\lambda_{\alpha}}-{\lambda_{\beta}})^{2}||A_{\alpha}^{\beta}||^{2}_{\omega},

Since
||s||_{H}
is bounded by
C
, the
|\lambda_{\alpha}|
are bounded by some constant
C^{\prime}
, depending only on
C
. We also remark that
z\mapsto z^{-1}(e^{z}-1)
can be extended to a smooth positive function on
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
Hence there are two positive constants
C_{1}\leqslant C_{2}
, depending only on
C
, such that

C_{1}||{\overline{\partial}}s||_{H,\omega}\leqslant||{\overline{\partial}}h||_{H,\omega}\leqslant C_{2}||{\overline{\partial}}s||_{H,\omega}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

(X,\omega)

In the remainder of the paper, by a smooth section of
{\mathcal{F}}

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
X
be a normal complex variety and
{\mathcal{F}}
a reflexive coherent sheaf.
Let
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
be Zariski open subset whose complement has codimension at least two, and
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}|_{X^{\circ}}
a saturated coherent subsheaf.
Then
{\mathcal{E}}
extends to a coherent saturated subsheaf of
{\mathcal{F}}
, globally on
X
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the extension locally on
X
.
Hence by embedding
{\mathcal{F}}
in a free coherent sheaf as a saturated subsheaf, we may assume that
{\mathcal{F}}
is free.
Furthermore, by shrinking
X^{\circ}
, we may assume that
{\mathcal{E}}
is a subbundle of
{\mathcal{F}}|_{X^{\circ}}
.
Then we have a morphism
f\colon X^{\circ}\to G
, where
G=G(n,m)
is the Grassmannian variety, with
n=\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{F}}
and
m=\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{E}}
.
Let
\Gamma\subseteq X\times G
be the closure of the graph of
f
.
By applying
[
Siu75
, Main Theorem]
to normal varieties, as explained in
[
Siu75
, page 441]
, we obtain that
\Gamma
is a closed analytic subvariety of
X\times G
.
We denote by
p_{1}\colon\Gamma\to X
and
p_{2}\colon\Gamma\to G
.

Let
U
be the universal vector bundle on
G
. By pulling back to
\Gamma

via

p_{2}
, we get a subbundle
{\mathcal{G}}
of
p_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{F}}
on
\Gamma
.
Since
p_{1}
is proper, the direct image
(p_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{G}}
is a coherent sheaf on
X
.
Furthermore, it is a subsheaf of
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
, extending
{\mathcal{E}}
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

2.C.
Slope stability

We will first introduce two different ways to define the degree of a reflexive sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on a compact normal Kähler variety
(X,\omega)
. We note that the definitions rely on the Hermitian metric constructed in Section
3
below.

The first one was considered in
[
Sim88
, page 877]
, which
a prior
depends on the Hermitian metric.
Let
(M,\omega)
be a Kähler manifold of dimension
n
,
and
({\mathcal{F}},H)
a Hermitian vector bundle on
M
, with Chern curvature
F
.
Assume that
\mathrm{tr}\,(\Lambda_{\omega}F)
is integrable on
(M,\omega)
.
Then we define the degree of
({\mathcal{F}},H)
as

\mathrm{deg}_{\omega}\,({\mathcal{F}},H)=\sqrt{-1}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\,(\Lambda_{\omega}F)\omega^{n}.

If
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
is a non zero saturated coherent analytic subsheaf, then
H
induces a Hermitian metric on
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
by restriction.
By using Chern-Weil formula, we can define the degree
\mathrm{deg}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},H|_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}})
, which belongs to
[-\infty,\infty)
.

We consider the following situation. Let
(X,\omega)
be a normal compact Kähler variety, and
{\mathcal{F}}
a reflexive coherent sheaf on
X
. Let
H
be a metric constructed by Lemma
3.3
.
Then
\mathrm{tr}\,(\Lambda_{\omega}F)
is integrable on
X
by construction (see Remark
3.5
).
Thus we can define
\mathrm{deg}_{\omega}\,({\mathcal{F}},H)
as in the previous paragraph, by taking
M=X_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Now we introduce a second definition of
\mathrm{deg}_{\omega}\,{\mathcal{F}}
.
Let
r\colon\widehat{X}\to X
be any desingularization such that the torsion-free quotient
r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*})/{\mathrm{(torsion)}}
is locally free.
We then define

\mathrm{deg}_{\omega}\,{\mathcal{F}}=-2\pi nc_{1}\Big(r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*})/{\mathrm{(torsion)}}\Big)\wedge[r^{*}\omega]^{n-1},

where
[r^{*}\omega]
is the corresponding cohomology class.
This definition does not involve metrics.
It is also independent of the choice of resolution, and appears more intrinsic. To see this, we only need to observe the following fact. If
p\colon W\to\widehat{X}
is a modification with
W
smooth, then

p^{*}\Big(r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*})/{\mathrm{(torsion)}}\Big)\cong\Big((r\circ p)^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*})\Big)/{\mathrm{(torsion)}}.

We remark that if
X
is projective and
\omega
is induced by an ample divisor, then this definition of degree coincides with the usual one in algebraic geometry.

We claim that
\deg_{\omega}{\mathcal{F}}=\mathrm{deg}_{\omega}\,({\mathcal{F}},H)
.
That is, the previous two definitions coincide.
Indeed, By Lemma
3.3
, there is a desingulariztion
r\colon\widehat{X}\to X
such that
r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*})/{\mathrm{(torsion)}}
is locally free and
H
extends to a smooth Hermitian metric on
(r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*})/{\mathrm{(torsion)}})^{*}
.
This implies that
\deg_{\omega}{\mathcal{F}}=\mathrm{deg}\,({\mathcal{F}},H)
.
We notice that, for any saturated coherent subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
, we have

\mathrm{deg}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},H|_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}})=\mathrm{deg}_{\omega}\,{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
.

We then define the slope of
{\mathcal{F}}
, with respect to
\omega
, as

\mu_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})=\frac{\deg_{\omega}\,{\mathcal{F}}}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{F}}}.

We say that
{\mathcal{F}}
is (slope) stable if for any proper non zero saturated coherent analytic subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
, we have

\mu_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})<\mu_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}}).

2.D.
Hermitian-Yang-Mills metrics

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact normal Kähler variety of dimension
n
,
and
{\mathcal{F}}
a reflexive sheaf on
X
.
A metric
H
on
{\mathcal{F}}
is called Hermitian-Yang-Mills, or Hermitian-Einstein, if its Chern curvature
F
satisfies

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F=\lambda_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}},

where
\lambda_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})=\mu_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Vol}_{\omega}(X)^{-1}
.

We fix a Hermitian metric
H_{0}
on
{\mathcal{F}}
, with Chern curvature
F_{0}
.
For any Hermitian metric
H
on
{\mathcal{F}}
, we write
h=H_{0}^{-1}H
.
Then
h
is smooth section of
{\mathcal{E}}nd{({\mathcal{F}})}
, which is self-adjoint with respect to
H_{0}
and
H
.
The curvatures satisfy

F_{H}=F_{0}+{\overline{\partial}}((h^{-1}\partial_{H_{0}}h)).

To obtain a Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric on
{\mathcal{F}}
, we will consider a family of metrics

H_{t}=H_{0}h(t)=H_{0}h_{t}=H_{0}h

subject to the following heat equation, with
h_{0}=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}
,

(2.1)

\begin{cases}h_{t}^{-1}(\frac{\mathrm{\partial}}{\mathrm{\partial}t}h_{t})=-(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{t}-\lambda_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}})\\
\mathrm{det}\,h_{t}\equiv 1\end{cases}

where
F_{t}
is the Chern curvature of
H_{t}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume that the heat equation above admits a solution around some time
t>0
.
Then

(\frac{1}{2}\Delta-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})||\Lambda F_{t}||_{H_{t}}\geqslant 0

whenever
||\Lambda F_{t}||_{H_{t}}\neq 0.

Proof.

For simplicity, we write
u=\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{t}
.
The following computation is local and we may hence assume that
u\neq 0
.
As in the proof of
[
Sim88
, Lemma 6.1]
, we get

(\frac{1}{2}\Delta-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})||u||_{H_{t}}^{2}=2||{\overline{\partial}}u||_{H_{t}}^{2}.

We set
m=||u||_{H_{t}}^{2}
, then

\Delta||u||_{H_{t}}=\frac{\Delta m}{2||u||_{H_{t}}}-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2||u||_{H_{t}}^{3}}\Lambda\partial m{\overline{\partial}}m,

and

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}||u||_{H_{t}}=\frac{1}{2||u||_{H_{t}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}m.

We thus deduce that

(\frac{1}{2}\Delta-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})||u||_{H_{t}}=\frac{||{\overline{\partial}}u||_{H_{t}}^{2}}{||u||_{H_{t}}}-\frac{1}{4||u||_{H_{t}}^{3}}||{\overline{\partial}}m||^{2}.

We note that

||{\overline{\partial}}m||^{2}=4\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\mathrm{tr}\,(u{\overline{\partial}}u)\mathrm{tr}\,(u\partial_{H_{t}}u).

Since
u
is selfadjoint with respect to
H_{t}
, by taking a local
H_{t}
-orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{F}}
which diagonalizes
u
, we see that
4||{\overline{\partial}}u||_{H_{t}}^{2}||u||_{H_{t}}^{2}\geqslant||{\overline{\partial}}m||^{2}
.
Hence we get

(\frac{1}{2}\Delta-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})||u||_{H_{t}}\geqslant 0
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Construction of an initial metric

The goal of this section is to construct an initial metric
H_{0}
for the heat equation (
2.1
), see Lemma
3.3
below.
Such a metric will guarantee that
\Lambda F
is integrable, where
F
is the Chern curvature.
We will follow the lines of
[
BS94
, Section 3]
.
The idea is to construct a metric which is locally the restriction of a smooth metric on a vector bundle.
The first step is to construct an appropriate partition of the unity.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be an analytic variety. Let
\{U_{i}\}
be a finite open covering of
X
. We assume that each
U_{i}
can be identified as a closed analytic subvariety of a domain
V_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{N_{i}}
.
Then there is a partition of the unity, subordinate to
\{U_{i}\}
, which satisfies the following property.
For
x\in X
, there is an open neighbourhood
U
of
x
, such that

(1)

U
is contained in
U_{i}
whenever
x\in U_{i}
,

(2)

if
U\subseteq U_{i}
, then there is an open subset
W_{i}\subseteq V_{i}
containing
U
such that
\rho_{i}|_{U}
is the restriction of some smooth function
\eta_{i}\colon W_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since a partition of the unity can be constructed by taking linear combinations of products of bump functions, we will construct bump functions on
U_{i}
which satisfy the property in the proposition.

Without loss of generality, we take a bump function
\gamma_{1}
on
V_{1}
, which restricts to a bump function
\rho_{1}
on
U_{1}
. Let
x\in U_{1}
be any point.
Assume that
x\in U_{i}
.
Then there exists open subsets
x\in W_{1}\subseteq V_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{N_{1}}
and
x\in W_{i}\subseteq V_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{N_{i}}
, such that the identity map of
U_{i}\cap U_{1}
induces a transition function

\varphi_{i,1}\colon W_{i}\to W_{1},

which is holomorphic.
We set
\eta_{i}=\gamma_{1}\circ\varphi_{i,1}
.
Let
U
be an open neighbourhood
x
which is contained both in
W_{1}
and
W_{i}
.
Then
\rho_{1}|_{U}=\eta_{i}|_{U}
.
By taking account of all
U_{i}
, we complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

We will also need the following local estimate.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a Kähler subvariety of a Kähler manifold
(V,\omega)
.
Let

{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{a}

be a saturated coherent subsheaf.
Let
g
be a Hermitian metric on
{\mathcal{O}}_{V}^{a}
and
h
its restriction on
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{a}
.
We denote by
h_{\mathcal{F}}
the restriction of
h
on
{\mathcal{F}}
.
Let
F_{h_{\mathcal{F}}}
be the Chern curvature.
Then for any precompact open subset
U
of
X
, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\sqrt{-1}F\leqslant C\omega\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}

Proof.

We denote by
F_{h}
and
F_{g}
the corresponding Chern curvatures.
Then we have

F_{h}=F_{g}|_{X}.

Furthermore, we denote by
\nabla_{h}
and
\nabla_{h_{{\mathcal{F}}}}
the corresponding Chern connections.
Then there is a
(1,0)
-form
A
on
X
, with values in
{\mathcal{H}}om({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\perp_{h}})
, such that, for any smooth section
s
of
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
, we have

\nabla_{h}s=\nabla_{h_{\mathcal{F}}}s+As.

Then

\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{\mathcal{F}}}=\sqrt{-1}F_{h}|_{{\mathcal{F}}}-\sqrt{-1}A\wedge A^{*}\leqslant\sqrt{-1}F_{h}|_{{\mathcal{F}}}.

For a precompact open set
U
, then there is a precompact open subset
W\subseteq V
, which contains
U
.
Then there is some constant
C
, such that, on
W
, we have

\sqrt{-1}F_{g}\leqslant C\omega\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{V}^{a}}.

Thus
\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{\mathcal{F}}}\leqslant C\omega\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}
on
U
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Now we are ready to construct the desire metric.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a normal compact Kähler variety and
{\mathcal{F}}
a coherent reflexive sheaf on
X
.
Then there exists a Hermitian metric
H
on
{\mathcal{F}}
with Chern curvature
F
such that

\sqrt{-1}F\leqslant C\omega\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}

for some positive constant
C
.

Moreover, let
r\colon\widehat{X}\to X
be a desingularization by successively blowing up smooth centers.
Assume that the torsion-free quotient
r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*})/({\mathrm{torsion}})
is locally free, then
H
extends to a smooth Hermitian metric
\widehat{H}
on the locally free sheaf
(r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*}))^{*}
.

Proof.

There is a finite open covering
\{U_{i}\}
of
X
such that the following properties hold.

(1)

Every
U_{i}
is precompact and can be identified with an analytic subvariety of a domain
V_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{N_{i}}
.

(2)

There is a surjective morphism

{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\to{\mathcal{F}}^{*}|_{U_{i}}\to 0

such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
is a free coherent sheaf on
U_{i}
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}^{V_{i}}
be a free coherent sheaf on
V_{i}
such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}={\mathcal{E}}_{i}^{V_{i}}|_{U_{i}}
.
Let
g_{i}
be a Hermitian metric on
({\mathcal{E}}^{V_{i}})^{*}
.
By restriction, it induces a Hermitian metric on
h_{i}
on
{\mathcal{F}}|_{U_{i}}
.
Let
\{\rho_{i}\}
be a partition of the unity subordinate to
\{U_{i}\}
, as in Proposition
3.1
.
We define the Hermitian metric
H=\sum\rho_{i}h_{i}
on
{\mathcal{F}}
.
We denote by
F
its Chern curvature.
We will show that

has the properties of the lemma.

Let
x\in X
be a point. Then there is an open neighbourhood
x\in U
such that

(1)

U
is contained in
U_{i}
whenever
x\in U_{i}
.

(2)

if
x\in U\subseteq U_{i}
, then there is an open subset
W_{i}\subseteq V_{i}
containing
U
such that
\rho_{i}|_{U}
is the restriction of some smooth function
\eta_{i}\colon W_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Now we consider the diagonal embedding, followed by the product inclusion,

U{\hookrightarrow}\prod_{x\in U_{i}}U_{i}\hookrightarrow\prod_{x\in U_{i}}W_{i}.

Let
\iota\colon U\to\prod_{x\in U_{i}}W_{i}
be the composition of this sequence.
Then it realizes

as a Kähler subvariety of the manifold
\prod_{x\in U_{i}}W_{i},
up to scaling the Kähler form by the cardinality of

.

We denote by
p_{i}\colon\prod_{x\in U_{i}}W_{i}\to W_{i}
the natural projections.
By abuse of notation, we still denote by

the projection from

to

.
For the reflexive sheaf

, we have the following sequence of sheaves supported in

\iota_{*}({\mathcal{F}}|_{U})\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{i}p_{i}^{*}({\mathcal{F}}|_{U})\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{i}p_{i}^{*}({\mathcal{E}}_{i}^{*}|_{U}).

We remark that

\bigoplus_{i}p_{i}^{*}({\mathcal{E}}_{i}^{*}|_{U})=\Big(\bigoplus_{i}p_{i}^{*}(({\mathcal{E}}^{V_{i}})^{*}|_{W_{i}})\Big)\Big|_{\iota(U)}.

The vector bundle
\bigoplus_{i}p_{i}^{*}(({\mathcal{E}}^{V_{i}})^{*}|_{W_{i}})

Then the metric

For the second part of the lemma, we will first work locally.

\widehat{U}_{i}=r^{-1}(U_{i})

Since

in

By taking the dual morphism, we see that

For such a metric, we have the following statement on integrability.

Corollary 3.4
.

With the notation above, let
n=\dim X
.
Let
Z\subseteq X
be the locus where
r^{-1}
is not an isomorphism.
For any Kähler form
\eta
on
\widehat{X}
, we still denote by
\eta
its restriction on
X\backslash Z
.
Then on
X\backslash Z
, we have

||\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\eta}F||_{H}\eta^{n}\leqslant\mathrm{tr}\,\Big(2nC\omega\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}-n\mathrm{tr}\,(\sqrt{-1}F)\Big)\wedge\eta^{n-1}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\int_{{X\backslash Z}}||\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\eta}F||_{H}\eta^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2nC\cdot\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{F}}\cdot[r^{*}\omega]\wedge[\eta^{n-1}]

\displaystyle-2n\pi\cdot c_{1}\Big((r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*}))^{*}\Big)\wedge[\eta^{n-1}]

is bounded, where
[\eta]
and
[r^{*}\omega]
are the corresponding cohomology classes on
\widehat{X}
.

Proof.

We have

||\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\eta}F||_{H}\leqslant||\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\eta}(C\omega\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathcal{F}}}-F)||_{H}+||C\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\eta}\omega\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathcal{F}}}||_{H}.

For simplicity, we set
g=\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\eta}(C\omega\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathcal{F}}}-F).

Since
F
is the Chern curvature of

, we see that
g
is selfadjoint with respect to

.
Thus
||g||_{H}=\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}\,(g^{2})}

Since

||\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\eta}F||_{H}\eta^{n}\leqslant n\mathrm{tr}\,(2C\omega\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathcal{F}}}-F))\eta^{n-1}.

This proves the first inequality.

For the estimate on the integral, we note that

can be viewed as the restriction of

on

is the Chern connection of

It follows that

\int_{X\backslash Z}\mathrm{tr}\,(\sqrt{-1}F)\wedge\eta^{n-1}=\int_{\widehat{X}}\mathrm{tr}\,(\sqrt{-1}\widehat{F})\wedge\eta^{n-1}=2\pi c_{1}\Big((r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*}))^{*}\Big)\wedge[\eta^{n-1}].

This completes the proof.

Remark 3.5
.

With the notation above, we assume that

, we have

4.
Heat kernels of Kähler varieties

In this section, we will investigate the heat kernels of compact Kähler varieties.
We will start by recalling some basic properties on heat kernels.

4.A.
Heat kernesl of Riemannian manifolds

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold, which is not necessarily compact.
We denote by
\Delta
the Laplace-Beltrami operator induced by
g
.
Let
\Omega\subseteq M
be any non empty precompact open subset, with possibly empty smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
.
We consider the following heat equation on
\Omega
, with Dirichlet boundary condition, on
u(x,t)
, with variables
x\in\Omega
and
t\in[0,\infty)
,

(4.1)

\begin{cases}(\Delta-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})u(x,t)=0&\mbox{for }t>0\\
u(x,0)=f(x)&\\
u(x,t)|_{\partial\Omega}=0&\mbox{for }t>0\end{cases}

The initial condition
f
is assume to be bounded continuous.
This equation admits a smooth fundamental solution
K_{\Omega}(x,y,t)
, which is called the heat kernel of
(\Omega,g)
, in the following sense.
One solution of the heat equation (
4.1
) is the convolution

u(x,t)=\int_{y\in\Omega}K_{\Omega}(x,y,t)f(y)\mathrm{d}y.

We now pass to the following heat equation on
M
, with
f
continuous and bounded,

(4.2)

\begin{cases}(\Delta-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})u(x,t)=0&\mbox{for }t>0\\
u(x,0)=f(x)&\\
\end{cases}

Such an equation also admits a smooth fundamental solution in the previous sense.
One can be obtained as follows (see
[
Dod83
, Theorem 3.6]
).
Let
\{\Omega_{i}\}
be an exhaustion of
X
by precompact open subsets, and we set

K(x,y,t)=\limsup_{i}K_{\Omega_{i}}(x,y,t).

Then it is the smallest positive fundamental solution, and we call it the heat kernel of
(X,g)
.

We have the following properties on the heat kernels
K
and
K_{\Omega}
.
For simplicity, we omit the lower index
\Omega
for
K_{\Omega}
in the following list.

(1)

K(x,y,t)\geqslant 0
.

(2)

K(x,y,t)=K(y,x,t)
.

(3)

K(x,y,t+s)=\int K(x,z,t)K(z,y,s)\mathrm{d}z
.

(4)

K(x,y,t)\leqslant\sqrt{K(x,x,t)K(y,y,t)}.

(5)

K(x,z,t)\mathrm{d}z\leqslant 1
.

We can derive the following estimate after the maximum principal.
For the reader’s convenience, we recall the proof here.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary, and
K
its heat kernel.
Let
v(x,t)\geqslant 0
be a continuous function on
M\times[0,\infty)
.
Assume that, whenever
v(x,t)\neq 0
, it is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
in
x
,
\mathcal{C}^{1}
in
t
, and

(\frac{1}{2}\Delta-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})v\geqslant 0.

Then for any
t>0
, we have

v(x,t)\leqslant\int_{y\in M}K(x,y,\frac{1}{2}t)v(y,0)\mathrm{d}y.

Proof.

We set

u(x,t)=\int_{y\in M}K(x,y,\frac{1}{2}t)v(y,0)\mathrm{d}y\geqslant 0.

Then
u
is a smooth solution of the equation
(\frac{1}{2}\Delta-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})u=0
, with initial data
u(x,0)=v(x,0)
.
Let
w=v-u
, then

(\frac{1}{2}\Delta-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})w\geqslant 0.

We will show that
w\leqslant 0
over
M\times[0,\infty)
.
Assume by contradiction that
w
admits strictly positive values at some time
T>0
.
For some
0<\delta\ll 1
, we set

w_{\delta}(x,t)=w(x,t)-\delta t.

Then
w_{\delta}
also admits strictly positive values at
T
.
By continuity of
w_{\delta}
, it admits a maximum at some point
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times[0,T]
.
Since
w_{\delta}(x_{0},t_{0})>0
, we get
t_{0}>0
.
Moreover, both
\Delta w_{\delta}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}w_{\delta}
are well defined.

Since
w_{\delta}
has maximum at
(x_{0},t_{0})
, we get

\Delta w_{\delta}(x_{0},t_{0})\leqslant 0.

Therefore,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}w_{\delta}(x_{0},t_{0})\leqslant-\delta+\frac{1}{2}\Delta w_{\delta}(x_{0},t_{0})\leqslant-\delta.

It follows that
w_{\delta}(x_{0},\cdot)
is strictly decreasing around
t_{0}
.
Since
t_{0}\neq 0
, this contradicts that
w_{\delta}
has maximum at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
∎

We will show the next two results on upper bounds for the heat kernels.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
CKS87
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
K
be the heat kernel of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of real dimension
m
.
Then for any
t\geqslant t^{\prime}>0
, we have
K(x,x,2t)\leqslant K(x,x,2t^{\prime})
.
Assume further that we have a Sobolev type inequality as follows.
For any compactly supported
\mathcal{C}^{1}
function
f\geqslant 0
,

(\int_{M}|f|^{\frac{2m}{m-2}})^{\frac{m-2}{m}}\leqslant C_{S}\int_{M}(|\nabla f|^{2}+|f|^{2}).

Then
K(x,x,2t)\leqslant\Big(\frac{mC_{S}}{4}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{2t}t^{-\frac{m}{2}}
.

Proof.

We note that
K=\limsup K_{\Omega_{i}}
, where
\{\Omega_{i}\}
is an exhaustion of
M
consisting of precompact open subsets, with smooth boundary.
In order to prove the proposition, it is enough to prove the same statement for each
K_{\Omega_{i}}
.
For simplicity, we omit the index
\Omega_{i}
in the following argument, and assume that
K
is one of the
K_{\Omega_{i}}
. In particular, it has compact support in
M\times M
.

Let
J(x,t)=\int_{y\in M}K(x,y,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}y
. Then

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}J(x,t)=2\int_{y\in M}K(x,y,t)\Delta_{y}K(x,y,t)\mathrm{d}y=-2\int_{y\in M}|\nabla_{y}K(x,y,t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y.

In particular, we see that
J(x,t)\leqslant J(x,t^{\prime})
if
t\geqslant t^{\prime}
.

The Sobolev inequality implies that

\int_{y\in M}|\nabla_{y}K(x,y,t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y\geqslant\frac{1}{C_{S}}\Big(\int_{y\in M}K(x,y,t)^{\frac{2m}{m-2}}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{m-2}{m}}-\int_{y\in M}K(x,y,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}y.

The Hölder inequality implies that

\int_{y\in M}K(x,y,t)^{\frac{2m}{m-2}}\mathrm{d}y\geqslant\Big(\int_{y\in M}K(x,y,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{m+2}{m-2}}\Big(\int_{y\in M}K(x,y,t)\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{-\frac{4}{m-2}}.

We note that

\int_{y\in M}K(x,y,t)\mathrm{d}y\leqslant 1,

thus we have

\int_{y\in M}|\nabla_{y}K(x,y,t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y\geqslant\frac{1}{C_{S}}J(x,t)^{\frac{m+2}{m}}-J(x,t).

Therefore

-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}J(x,t)\geqslant\frac{2}{C_{S}}J(x,t)^{\frac{m+2}{m}}-2J(x,t).

Let
I(x,t)=J(x,t)e^{-2t}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}I(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2t}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}J(x,t)-2J(x,t))

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{2e^{-2t}}{C_{S}}J(x,t)^{\frac{m+2}{m}}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{2}{C_{S}}I(x,t)^{\frac{m+2}{m}}.

We remark that
I(x,t)
tends to infinity when
t
tends to zero.
Hence we have
I(x,t)\leqslant\Big(\frac{mC_{S}}{4}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}t^{-\frac{m}{2}}
, and thus
J(x,t)\leqslant\Big(\frac{mC_{S}}{4}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{2t}t^{-\frac{m}{2}}
.
∎

Following the method of
[
Gri97
]
, we obtain a Gaussian upper bound for the heat kernel.

Corollary 4.3
.

With the assumptions in Proposition

, we have the following estimate for

is the distance between

\sigma(t)=\begin{cases}\Big(\frac{2}{mC_{S}}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-t}t^{\frac{m}{2}}&\mbox{for }0<t<t_{0}\\
\Big(\frac{2}{mC_{S}}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-t_{0}}t_{0}^{\frac{m}{2}}&\mbox{for }\mbox{for }t\geqslant t_{0}\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
, we see that
K(x,x,t)\leqslant\Big(\frac{mC_{S}}{2}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{t}t^{-\frac{m}{2}}
for all

.

4.B.
Heat kernels of compact Kähler varieties

We will study the heat kernel of a Zariski dense open subset of a compact Kähler variety.
We first prove a Sobolev type inequality.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler variety of dimension
n
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{S}
, such that for any
\mathcal{C}^{1}
function

, compactly supported in the smooth locus of

(\int_{X}|f|^{\frac{2n}{n-1}}\omega^{n})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leqslant C_{S}\int_{X}(||\nabla f||^{2}+|f|^{2})\omega^{n}.

Proof.

Since
X
is compact, by using a partition of the unity, it is enough to prove the problem locally on
X
.
Hence we may assume that

is a subvariety of
\mathbb{B}^{p}
, where
\mathbb{B}^{p}
is the unit balls of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
\mathbb{C}^{q}

We note that, for two Kähler forms

is a Sobolev constant for
\omega_{a}

\omega_{b}
.
Therefore, we may assume that the Kähler form

Then

[

[
MS73

.
∎

.

.

The last property in the previous corollary is also called the conservation property. It implies that the heat kernels are the same for Zariski open dense subsets.

Corollary 4.7
.

With the assumption of Corollary
4.6
, if

is a Zariski open dense subset. Then the heat kernel

To apply the method of Bando-Siu in the setting of singular varieties, one needs a uniform Sobolev inequality as Conjecture

As in the proof of Lemma
4.5
, thanks to the compactness assumption, we can reduce it to the following local version.

Conjecture 4.9
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{k}=N
be an
m
-dimensional integral complex analytic subvariety defined around the origin

Let
(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})
be a coordinates system of
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{k}

We consider the Kähler forms for

\omega_{\delta}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\partial{\overline{\partial}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2}+\delta^{2}\log\,(|\mathbf{z}|^{2})+|\mathbf{w}|^{2}).

U
of

C

\int_{X}h\omega_{\delta}^{m}\leqslant C\int_{X}|\nabla_{X_{\delta}}h|\omega_{\delta}^{m}.

\omega_{\delta}

\omega_{\delta}

Furthermore, we observe that there is a constant

\omega_{0}\leqslant\omega_{1}\leqslant\frac{A}{|\mathbf{z}|^{2}}\omega_{0}.

the closed unit ball and the closed unit sphere in

We will prove Conjecture

Proposition 4.10
.

Conjecture
4.9
holds if
k=0
.

Corollary 4.11
.

Conjecture
4.9
holds if
X=\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{k}
.

Proof.

Let
Y=\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then there is a uniform Sobolev constant
C_{1}
for

(Y,\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\partial{\overline{\partial}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2}+\delta^{2}\log\,(|\mathbf{z}|^{2})))

4.10
.
Hence there is a uniform Sobolev constant
C
for
(X,\omega_{\delta})

and

To see this, we only need to apply the following argument several times.
Let
(M,g)
is a Riemannian manifold of real dimension
d
, with a Sobolev constant
D
,
then

admit Sobolev inequalities with Sobolev constant

\mathcal{C}^{1}

\displaystyle\int_{M^{\prime}}h^{\frac{d+1}{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big(\int_{M_{s}}h^{\frac{d+1}{d}}\Big)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big(\Big(\int_{M_{s}}h\Big)^{\frac{1}{d}}\Big(\int_{M_{s}}h^{\frac{d}{d-1}}\Big)^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\Big)\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle D\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big(\int_{M_{s}}h\Big)^{\frac{1}{d}}\Big(\int_{M_{s}}|\nabla_{M^{\prime}}h|\Big)\mathrm{d}s.

\displaystyle\int_{M}h^{\frac{d+1}{d}}\leqslant D\Big(\int_{M^{\prime}}|\nabla_{M^{\prime}}h|\Big)^{\frac{d+1}{d}}.

Lemma 4.12
.

Lemma 4.16

Proof.

5.
Existence of admissible metrics

In this section, we will assume Conjecture
1.2
and then prove Theorem
1.4
.
That is, the existence of admissible solutions until infinite time of the heat equation (
2.1
),

\begin{cases}h_{t}^{-1}(\frac{\mathrm{\partial}}{\mathrm{\partial}t}h_{t})=-(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{t}-\lambda_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}})\\
\mathrm{det}\,h_{t}\equiv 1\end{cases}

with an initial metric
H_{0}
constructed as in Section
3
.
The method goes back to
[
BS94
]
.
We will first solve the heat equation on a desingularization

, with respect to small perturbation of

.
Then we will try to converge the solutions and get the solution on
(X,\omega)
.
In order to guarantee the convergence, we will prove some uniform estimates on the solutions with respect to perturbed metrics, in Section
5.A
.
Once we get the uniform estimates, we will use induction to conclude Theorem
1.4
in Section
5.B
.
In the last subsection, we will deduce some estimate on the shifted solution
g(t)=h(1)^{-1}h(t)
.
It will serve the proof on existence of Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric in Section
6
.

5.A.
Uniform estimates

Let

Assume that we have the following Sobolev inequality

\mathcal{C}^{1}

Let

Lemma 5.1
.

We have the following two estimate for
||\Lambda_{\omega}F(x,t)||_{H_{t}}

(1)

If
||\Lambda_{\omega}F_{0}(x)||_{H_{0}}\leqslant B
for some positive constant

||\Lambda_{\omega}F(x,t)||_{H_{t}}\leqslant B

(2)

Let
U\subseteq V
be two precompcat open subset of

Assume that there are strictly positive numbers

and
\delta=\delta(U,V)
such that

\mathrm{dist}\,(U,X\backslash V)\geqslant\delta,

\sup_{x\in V}||\Lambda F_{0}(x)||_{H_{0}}\leqslant S.

Then there exists a positive constant

, depending on

||\Lambda_{\omega}F(x,t)||_{H_{t}}\leqslant M

for all

Proof.

For (1), we use the fact that

to deduce that

||\Lambda_{\omega}F(x,t)||_{H_{t}}\leqslant\int_{z\in X^{\circ}}K(x,z,\frac{1}{2}t)||\Lambda_{\omega}F_{0}(z)||_{H_{0}}\omega^{n}\leqslant B.

For (2), we follow the idea of
[

, Lemma 2.2]
.
We fix

and split the integral

For

Thus by Corollary

\Big(\frac{1}{nC_{S}}\Big)^{n}e^{-t_{0}}t_{0}^{n}&\mbox{for }\mbox{for }t\geqslant t_{0}\\
\end{cases}

and

Hence there is a positive constant

depending only on

Lemma 5.2
.

With the notation above, assume that

(2)

The following local estimate is crucial for the proof Theorem

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a Kähler manifold of dimension
n
and let

.
We write
B_{R}(x)
as the ball of radius

We assume that

Assume that the sectional curvature of

on

is contained in

Assume that

and

are smooth functions on

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda{\overline{\partial}}(h^{-1}(\partial h))=f.

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leqslant a^{ij}(y)\xi_{i}\overline{\xi_{j}}\leqslant\mu|\xi|^{2}

for any

and

Then

is
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
in a neighbourhood

of

. Moreover, there is a number

, depending on

As mentioned earlier, the proof of Theorem
1.4

We will first prove a single induction step.
We know that a desingularization can be obtained by successively blowing up smooth centers.

Let

be a blowup at smooth center, where
(X,\omega)
is a compact Kähler variety of dimension

.
Let

be a Kähler form on

We assume Conjecture
1.2
.
Then there is a uniform Sobolev constant

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume Conjecture
1.2
holds.
Let

be a Zariski open dense subset, disjoint from the center of the blowup.
Let
Y^{\circ}=p^{-1}(X^{\circ})\cong X^{\circ}
,
and let
K_{\epsilon}
be the heat kernel of

.
Then we may extract a sequence
\{\epsilon_{i}\}
converging to zero, such that

converge, smoothly over any compact subset of

to the heat kernel

of
(X^{\circ},\omega)
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{\epsilon}
and
\Delta
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator of
\omega_{\epsilon}
and
\omega
respectively.
Then
\Delta_{\epsilon}
converges smoothly to

We remark that

is a solution of the heat equation on

and Corollary

.
Hence by applying Lemma

, we may extract a sequence

converging to zero, such that

converge, smoothly over any compact subset of

.
We note that

is bounded on

It remains to show that

is the heat kernel of
(X^{\circ},\omega)

We first show that it is a fundamental solution for the heat equation.

As a consequence,

Hence we can deduce that

. Hence the last equality holds.

We will now show that

We note that

.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
H_{0}

, and we consider the heat equation (
2.1
). We will prove that it admits a solution until infinite time.

Let
\widehat{X}=X_{k}\to\cdots\to X_{0}=X
be a sequence of blowups, at smooth centers disjoint from

is a desingularization and
r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*})/{(\mathrm{torsion})}
is locally free.
In particular,
H_{0}
extends to a smooth Hermitian metric
\widehat{H}_{0}
on the locally free sheaf
\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}=(r^{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{*}))^{*}
by Lemma
3.3
.

Let
\eta_{i}
be a Kähler form on
X_{i}

\widehat{X}

For

\omega_{\epsilon_{1},...,\epsilon_{k}}=\omega+\epsilon_{1}\eta_{1}+\cdots+\epsilon_{k}\eta_{k}.

Then we can solve the heat equation (

) with respect to the compact Kähler manifold
(\widehat{X},\omega_{\epsilon_{1},...,\epsilon_{k}})

for the locally free sheaf
\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}
, with initial metric
\widehat{H}_{0}

By Lemma
2.7
and Proposition
4.1
, we have an estimate for the Chern curvatures of the solutions as follows (we omit the lower index

||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{\epsilon}(x,t)||_{H_{\epsilon}}\leqslant\int_{z\in\widehat{X}}K_{\epsilon}(x,z,\frac{1}{2}t)||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{0}(z)||_{H_{0}}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n},

where
K_{\epsilon}
is the corresponding heat kernel, and
F_{0}
is the Chern curvature of
\widehat{H}_{0}

Moreover, we have

\int_{\widehat{X}}||F_{\epsilon}||^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}=\int_{\widehat{X}}||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{\epsilon}||^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}+4\pi^{2}n(n-1)\Big(2c_{2}(\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}})-c_{1}(\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}})^{2}\Big)\wedge[\omega_{\epsilon}]^{n-2}.

L=\sup_{0<\epsilon_{1},...,\epsilon_{k}\leqslant 1}\Big|4\pi^{2}n(n-1)\Big(2c_{2}(\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}})-c_{1}(\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}})^{2}\Big)\wedge[\omega_{\epsilon_{1},...,\epsilon_{k}}]^{n-2}\Big|,

\int_{\widehat{X}}||F_{\epsilon}||^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}\leqslant\int_{\widehat{X}}||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{\epsilon}||^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}+L.

Now by Lemma
5.8
, for any
\epsilon_{1},...,\epsilon_{k-1}

\omega+\epsilon_{1}\eta_{1}+\cdots+\epsilon_{k-1}\eta_{k-1}.

.

on

||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{\epsilon}(x,t)||_{H_{\epsilon}}\leqslant\int_{z\in X_{k-1}}K_{\epsilon}(x,z,\frac{1}{2}t)||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{0}(z)||_{H_{0}}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}.

Moreover, after Lemma
5.9
, we get

\int_{X_{k-1}}||F_{\epsilon}||^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}\leqslant\int_{X_{k-1}}||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{\epsilon}||^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}+L.

We remark that the Assumption
5.6
is always guaranteed, as explained in Remark

.
Therefore, we can continue carrying out the induction steps of Lemma

on
(X,\omega)
.
Furthermore, Lemma
5.9
shows that, for any

∎

5.C.
Shifted solutions of the heat equations

In Simpson’s method in
[

]
, the initial metric for the heat equation is assumed to have bounded
\Lambda F
.
This might not be the case for a metric constructed in Lemma
3.3
, which we used in Theorem

Thus, in order to prove the existence of admissible Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric,
we will consider following shifted solution

g(x,t)=h(x,1)^{-1}h(x,t+1)\mbox{ and }G_{0}=H(x,1)=H_{0}h_{1}

on
(X,\omega)
.
In another word,
g
is the solution of the heat equation (
2.1
) with initial metric
G_{0}
. Now
G_{0}
is admissible by Theorem
1.4
.
Let
G=G_{0}g
and let
F_{G}
be the corresponding Chern curvature.
We have the following estimates on

.

Proposition 5.10
.

With the notation above, the following properties hold.

(1)

The metrics
G(t)
are admissible for all

, with uniform upper bounds on
||\Lambda F_{G(t)}||_{G(t)}
and on
\int_{X}||F_{G_{t}}||_{G(t)}^{2}
.

(2)

The norm
||g||_{G_{0}}=||g||_{G}
is bounded over

for any

.

(3)

For

, we have

\int_{X}||({\overline{\partial}}g(t))g(t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}||_{{G_{0},\omega}}^{2}\omega^{n}\leqslant\int_{X}|\mathrm{tr}\,(g\Lambda F_{G}-g\Lambda F_{G_{0}})|\omega^{n}.

(4)

\Delta_{G_{0}}g(t)
is integrable for any fixed
t\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

Since
G(t)=H(t+1)
, it is admissible from Theorem
1.4
.
The uniform bounds for item (1) can be deduced from Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.9
.
For item (2), since
G_{0}
is admissible, by item (1) of Lemma
5.2
,
we see that
\mathrm{tr}\,g
is bounded over
X^{\circ}\times[0,T]
for any

.
Since
g
is selfadjoint and definite positive with respect to

, this implies that
||g||_{G_{0}}=||g||_{G}
is bounded.
We observe that, after Lemma
2.4
, item (4) follows from item (1) and (3).

Hence it remains to prove item (3). First, we notice that, if
X
is smooth and
{\mathcal{F}}
is locally free, then by taking the trace of item (2) of Lemma

\int_{X}||({\overline{\partial}}g)g^{-\frac{1}{2}}||_{{G_{0},\omega}}^{2}\omega^{n}\leqslant\int_{X}|\mathrm{tr}\,(g\Lambda F_{G}-g\Lambda F_{G_{0}})|\omega^{n},

as in this case, the integral of

For the general case, since the solution

is obtained by converging successively the solutions on blowups of

∎

Lemma 5.11
.

With the notation of Lemma
5.8
,
we set

g_{\epsilon}(x,t)=h_{\epsilon}(x,1)^{-1}h_{\epsilon}(x,t+1),\ G_{\epsilon,0}=H_{\epsilon}(1)\ \mathrm{and}\ G_{\epsilon}=G_{\epsilon,0}g_{\epsilon}.

Assume that

\int_{Y^{\circ}}||({\overline{\partial}}g_{\epsilon})g_{\epsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}||_{G_{\epsilon,0},\omega_{\epsilon}}^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}\leqslant\int_{Y^{\circ}}|\mathrm{tr}\,(g_{\epsilon}\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{G_{\epsilon}}-g_{\epsilon}\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{G_{\epsilon,0}})|\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}.

Then for the shifted solution
g(t)=h(1)^{-1}h(t+1)

\int_{X^{\circ}}||({\overline{\partial}}g)g^{-\frac{1}{2}}||_{{G_{0},\omega}}^{2}\omega^{n}\leqslant\int_{X^{\circ}}|\mathrm{tr}\,(g\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G}-g\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{0}})|\omega^{n}.

Proof.

We note that
g_{\epsilon_{i}}
converge to

smoothly on any compact subset of

Thus it is sufficient to show that

\int_{Y^{\circ}}|\mathrm{tr}\,(g_{\epsilon}\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{G_{\epsilon}}-g_{\epsilon}\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{G_{\epsilon,0}})|\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}.

are uniformly integrable.
We fix some

.

By Proposition

It follows by assumption that

||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{G_{\epsilon,0}}(z)||_{G_{\epsilon,0}}=||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{H_{\epsilon}}(z,1)||_{H_{\epsilon}(1)}\leqslant\int_{z\in Y^{\circ}}K_{\epsilon}(x,z,\frac{1}{2})||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{0}(z)||_{H_{0}}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}

Hence by item (1) of Lemma

||\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}F_{G_{\epsilon}}||_{G_{\epsilon}}

By item (1) of Lemma

||g_{\epsilon}||_{G_{\epsilon}}=||g_{\epsilon}||_{G_{\epsilon,0}}

Hence we get the desire uniform integrability of the previous paragraph.

∎

The following lemma says that if
{\mathcal{F}}
is stable, then the stability persists during the heat flow.

Lemma 5.12
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}
is stable with respect to
\omega
.
Let
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
be a proper non zero saturated coherent subsheaf, and let
\pi
be the
G_{0}
-orthogonal projection from
{\mathcal{F}}
to
{\mathcal{E}}
.
Assume that
||{\overline{\partial}}\pi||_{G_{0}}^{2}
is integrable.
Then

\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},G_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{E}}}<\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}},G_{0})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{F}}}.

Proof.

We write
G_{t}=H_{t+1}
for
t\geqslant-1
.
In particular, the initial metric
H_{0}
is just
G_{-1}.

We first note that
\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}},G_{t})
is constant since
\det\,g_{t}\equiv 1
for all
t
.
In the following argument, we will let
t
tend to
-1
.

For any
t\geqslant-1
, we denote by
\pi_{t}
the
G_{t}
-orthogonal projection from
{\mathcal{F}}|_{X_{\mathcal{F}}}
to
{\mathcal{E}}
.
Then from the Chern-Weil formula,

(5.1)

\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},G_{t}|{{}_{\mathcal{E}}})=\sqrt{-1}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\,\pi_{t}\Lambda F_{G_{t}}-\int_{X}||{\overline{\partial}}\pi_{t}||_{G_{t}}^{2}.

Let
p_{t}=\pi g_{t}\pi
.
Then by taking local
G_{0}
-orthonormal basis, which diagonalizes
\pi
, we see that

G_{t}|_{\mathcal{E}}=(G_{0}g_{t}\pi)|_{\mathcal{E}}=G_{0}|_{{\mathcal{E}}}p_{t}|_{{\mathcal{E}}}.

The corresponding Chern curvatures satisfy

F_{G_{t}|_{\mathcal{E}}}=F_{G_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}}}+{\overline{\partial}}\Big((p_{t}|_{\mathcal{E}})^{-1}(\partial_{G_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}}}p_{t}|_{{\mathcal{E}}})\Big).

By taking the trace, we get

\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},G_{t}|_{\mathcal{E}})=\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},G_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}})+n\sqrt{-1}\int_{X}{\overline{\partial}}\mathrm{tr}\,\Big((p_{t}|_{\mathcal{E}})^{-1}(\partial_{G_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}}}p_{t}|_{{\mathcal{E}}})\Big)\omega^{n-1}.

For any
t>-1
, we see that
||{\overline{\partial}}(p_{t}|_{\mathcal{E}})||_{G_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}}}^{2}=||{\overline{\partial}}p_{t}||_{G_{0}}^{2}
, which is integrable, since
\pi
and
g_{t}
are bounded and
L_{2}^{1}
, as discussed in Proposition
5.10
.
Furthermore
||(p_{t}|_{{\mathcal{E}}})^{-1}||_{G_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}}}
is bounded, as
g_{t}
is bounded with determinant equal to
1
.
Thus

\mathrm{tr}\,\Big((p_{t}|_{\mathcal{E}})^{-1}(\partial_{G_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}}}p_{t}|_{{\mathcal{E}}})\Big)

is square integrable.
By Corollary
2.3
, we deduce that, for
t>-1
,

\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},G_{t}|_{\mathcal{E}})=\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},G_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}}).

Now we assume by contradiction that

\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},G_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{E}}}\geqslant\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}},G_{0})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{F}}}=\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}},H_{0})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{F}}}.

Then from the discussion above, for all
t>-1
,

\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},G_{t}|_{\mathcal{E}})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{E}}}\geqslant\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}},H_{0})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{F}}}.

We let
t
tend to
-1
in the equation (
5.1
) above.
We note that
||\pi_{t}||^{2}_{G_{t}}
is always equal to the rank of
{\mathcal{E}}
.
By Lemma
5.3
, and by Fatou’s lemma, we deduce that

\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},G_{-1}|{{}_{\mathcal{E}}})\geqslant\lim_{t\to-1}\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},G_{t}|{{}_{\mathcal{E}}}).

It follows that

\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},H_{0}|_{\mathcal{E}})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{E}}}\geqslant\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}},H_{0})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{F}}}.

This contradicts the stability condition.
∎

6.
Existence of admissible Hermitian-Yang-Mills metrics

In this section, we will assume Conjecture
1.2
and prove the existence of admissible Hermitian-Yang-Mills metrics for stable reflexive
sheaves on a compact normal Kähler variety.
We will follow the method of
[
Sim88
]
.

We first recall the following Donaldson’s functional
M(\cdot,\cdot)
, as defined in
[
Sim88
, Section 4]
.
Let
({\mathcal{F}},H)
be a Hermitian reflexive sheaf on a compact normal Kähler variety
(X,\omega)
.
Let
s
be a smooth section of
{\mathcal{E}}nd({\mathcal{F}})
, selfadjoint with respect to
H
.
Locally on
X_{\mathcal{F}}
, let
(\mathbf{e}_{\alpha})
be an
H
-orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{F}}
, which diagonalizes
s
.
In particular, we have
s(\mathbf{e}_{\alpha})=\lambda_{\alpha}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}
for real-valued functions
\lambda_{\alpha}
.
Let
(\mathbf{e}^{*}_{\alpha})
the dual basis of
{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
.
Let

\Psi(a,b)=\frac{e^{a-b}-1-(a-b)}{(a-b)^{2}}

be a smooth positive function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
For any section
A
of
{\mathcal{E}}nd({\mathcal{F}})|_{X_{\mathcal{F}}}
with local expression

A=A_{\alpha,\beta}\mathbf{e}^{*}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\beta},

we define

\Psi(s)(A)=\Psi(\lambda_{\alpha},\lambda_{\beta})A_{\alpha,\beta}\mathbf{e}^{*}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\beta}.

The following lemma follows directly from the definition of
\Psi
.

Lemma 6.1
.

With the notation above, assume that
||s||_{H}\leqslant C
for some constant
C
, then there exists some constant
C^{\prime}>0
, depending only on
C
, such that for any smooth
(0,1)
-form
A
with values in
{\mathcal{E}}nd({\mathcal{F}})
, we have

0\leqslant-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda<\Psi(s)A,A>_{H}\leqslant-C^{\prime}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda<A,A>_{H}=C^{\prime}||A||^{2}_{H,\omega}.

Now we assume that
\Lambda F_{H}
is integrable, where
F_{H}
is the Chern curvature of
H
.
Let
s
be a bounded
L_{1}^{2}
smooth section of
{\mathcal{E}}nd({\mathcal{F}})
.
Then the Donaldson’s functional
M(H,He^{s})
is defined as

M(H,He^{s})=\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\,(s\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H})+\int_{X}-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda<\Psi(s)({\overline{\partial}}s),{\overline{\partial}}s>_{H}.

We will write
M(H,He^{s})=\int_{X}N_{1}(H,He^{s})+\int_{X}N_{2}(H,He^{s})
according to this expression.

Lemma 6.2
.

With the assumption above, assume that
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are bounded
L_{1}^{2}
smooth section of
{\mathcal{E}}nd({\mathcal{F}})
, with
\Lambda F_{He^{s_{1}}}
integrable.
Let
\{\varphi_{i}\}
be a sequence of cut-off functions as in Lemma
2.1
, and we set
u_{1,i}=\varphi_{i}s_{1}
and
u_{2,j}=\varphi_{j}s_{2}
.
Then we have the following statements.

(1)

If
s_{2}
has compact support, then

\lim_{i\to\infty}M(He^{u_{1,i}},He^{u_{1,i}}e^{s_{2}})=M(He^{s_{1}},He^{s_{1}}e^{s_{2}}).

(2)

\lim_{i\to\infty}M(H,He^{u_{1,i}})=M(H,He^{s_{1}}).

(3)

\lim_{i\to\infty}M(H,He^{u_{1,i}}e^{s_{2}})=M(H,He^{s_{1}}e^{s_{2}}).

(4)

\lim_{j\to\infty}M(H,He^{s_{1}}e^{u_{2,j}})=M(H,He^{s_{1}}e^{s_{2}}).

(5)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}M(He^{u_{1,i}},He^{u_{1,i}}e^{u_{2,j}})=M(He^{s_{1}},He^{s_{1}}e^{s_{2}}).

Proof.

We will only give detailed proofs for item (1) and item (3).
The proof for item (4) is analogue to the one for item (3).
Item (2) follows from item (4) by taking
s_{2}=0
.
Item (5) follows by applying item (1) and then item (2).

For item (1), since
s_{2}
has compact support, there is some
i_{0}
such that, if
i\geqslant i_{0}
, then
s_{1}=u_{1,i}
on the support of
s_{2}
.
Hence (2) holds.

In the remainder of the proof, we focus on item (3).
Let
T_{i}=\{\varphi_{i}\neq 1\}
. Then the volumes of
T_{i}
tend to zero when
i\to\infty
. We use the notation that
s_{1}=u_{1,\infty}
and
\varphi_{\infty}\equiv 1
.
We write
e^{v_{i}}=h_{i}=e^{u_{1},i}e^{s_{2}}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Then
||v_{i}||_{\infty}
are bounded, and
v_{i}
converge to
v_{\infty}
, smoothly on any compact subset of
X_{\mathcal{F}}
.
We pick a constant
C
such that
||v_{i}||_{\infty},||s_{1}||_{\infty},||s_{2}||_{\infty},||{\overline{\partial}}s_{1}||_{2}
and
||{\overline{\partial}}s_{2}||_{2}
are bounded by
C
.

First, since
\Lambda F_{H}
is integrable, and since
||v_{i}||_{\infty}\leqslant C
for all
i
, we get

\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}N_{1}(H,He^{v_{i}})=\int_{X}N_{1}(H,He^{v_{\infty}}).

For the other integral in
M(\cdot,\cdot)
, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{X}N_{2}(H,He^{v_{\infty}})-\int_{X}N_{2}(H,He^{v_{i}})=\int_{T_{i}}N_{2}(H,He^{v_{\infty}})-\int_{T_{i}}N_{2}(H,He^{v_{i}})

By Lemma
6.1
, we see that there is a constant
C_{1}
, depending only on
C
, such that

N_{2}(H,He^{v_{i}})\leqslant C_{1}||{\overline{\partial}}v_{i}||_{H}^{2}

for
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.

We have the following computation, for
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,

{\overline{\partial}}h_{i}={\overline{\partial}}(e^{u_{1,i}}e^{s_{2}})={\overline{\partial}}e^{u_{1,i}}e^{s_{2}}+e^{u_{1,i}}{\overline{\partial}}e^{s_{2}}.

By Lemma
2.5
, there is a constant
C_{2}
, depending only on
C
, such that

||{\overline{\partial}}e^{u_{1,i}}||_{{H,\omega}}^{2}\leqslant C_{2}||{\overline{\partial}}u_{1,i}||_{{H,\omega}}^{2}\mbox{ and }||{\overline{\partial}}e^{s_{2}}||_{H,\omega}^{2}\leqslant C_{2}||{\overline{\partial}}s_{2}||^{2}_{H,\omega}.

We note that

||{\overline{\partial}}u_{1,i}||_{H,\omega}^{2}=||{\overline{\partial}}\varphi_{i}s_{1}+\varphi_{i}{\overline{\partial}}s_{1}||_{H,\omega}^{2}\leqslant 2||\nabla\varphi_{i}||^{2}||s_{1}||_{H,\omega}^{2}+2\varphi_{i}^{2}||{\overline{\partial}}s_{1}||_{H,\omega}^{2}.

Thus there is a constant
C_{3}
, depending only on
C
, such that

||{\overline{\partial}}h_{i}||_{H,\omega}^{2}\leqslant C_{3}(||{\overline{\partial}}s_{1}||^{2}_{H,\omega}+||{\overline{\partial}}s_{2}||^{2}_{H,\omega}+||\nabla\varphi_{i}||_{\omega}^{2}).

By Lemma
2.5
again, this implies that there is a constant
C_{4}
, depending only on
C
, such that
||{\overline{\partial}}v_{i}||_{{H,\omega}}^{2}\leqslant C_{4}(||{\overline{\partial}}s_{1}||^{2}_{H,\omega}+||{\overline{\partial}}s_{2}||^{2}_{H,\omega}+||\nabla\varphi_{i}||_{\omega}^{2})
.
In conclusion,

N_{2}(H,He^{v_{i}})\leqslant C_{1}C_{4}(||{\overline{\partial}}s_{1}||^{2}_{H,\omega}+||{\overline{\partial}}s_{2}||^{2}_{H,\omega}+||\nabla\varphi_{i}||_{\omega}^{2}).

Since
||\nabla\varphi_{i}||_{2}\to 0
when
i\to\infty
, and since
s_{1},s_{2}
are
L_{1}^{2}
, we obtain that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\Big(\int_{T_{i}}N_{2}(H,He^{v_{\infty}})-\int_{T_{i}}N_{2}(H,He^{v_{i}})\Big)=0.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

We have

M(H,He^{s_{1}})+M(He^{s_{1}},He^{s_{1}}e^{s_{2}})=M(H,He^{s_{1}}e^{s_{2}}).

Proof.

We first assume that
\Delta_{H}s_{1}
and
\Delta_{H}s_{2}
are integrable.
Then we can apply the argument of
[
Sim88
, Proposition 5.1]
, by just replacing the use of
[
Sim88
, Lemma 5.2]
by Corollary
2.3
.
For the general case, we can apply Lemma
6.2
.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

We note that, for the proof of the previous lemma, in
[
Sim88
, Proposition 5.1]
, the integrability of
\Delta_{H}s_{1}
is mandatory. However, for the solution
h=e^{s}
of our heat equation, we can only prove the integrability for
\Delta_{H}h
. It is not obvious to us that this implies the integrability of
\Delta_{H}s
.
For this reason, we prove Lemma
6.2
.

We replace
[
Sim88
, Assumption 3]
by the following property on normal compact Kähler varieties.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a normal compact Kähler variety of complex dimension
n
, and
B
a positive number.
Then there exist constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that the following property holds.
Let
f
be a smooth bounded positive function, defined over some smooth Zariski open dense subset of
X
, such that

\Delta f\geqslant-B,

and that

\int_{X}||\nabla f||^{2}<\infty,\ \int_{X}|\Delta f|<\infty.

Then we have

||f||_{\infty}\leqslant C_{1}||f||_{1}+C_{2}.

Proof.

We follow the idea of
[
LZZ17
, Lemma 5.2]
.
By Lemma
4.5
, we have a Sobolev inequality as follows.
There is a positive constant
C_{S}
such that for any
\mathcal{C}^{1}
function
g\geqslant 0
, compactly supported in the smooth locus of
X
,

(\int_{X}|g|^{\frac{2n}{n-1}})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leqslant C_{S}\int_{X}(||\nabla g||^{2}+|g|^{2}).

We claim that the same inequality holds for any bounded smooth
L_{1}^{2}
function
g\geqslant 0
, defined over some smooth Zariski open dense subset
X^{\circ}
of
X
.
Indeed, we can take a sequence of cut-off functions
\{\varphi_{i}\}
on
X^{\circ}
as in Lemma
2.1
, such that
||\nabla\varphi_{i}||_{2}\to 0
.
By considering the previous inequality for
\varphi_{i}g
, and by letting
i
to
\infty
, we deduce the claim.

On the one hand, by taking
g=(f+1)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}
, where
q\geqslant 1
, we obtain that

(\int_{X}|f+1|^{\frac{n(q+1)}{n-1}})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leqslant C_{S}\int_{X}\Big(\frac{(q+1)^{2}}{4}|f+1|^{q-1}||\nabla f||^{2}+|f+1|^{q+1}\Big).

On the other and, by assumption, we have

(f+1)^{q}\Delta f\geqslant-B(f+1)^{q}.

By integrating both sides, we get

\int_{X}(f+1)^{q}\Delta f\leqslant B\int_{X}(f+1)^{q}.

By the assumption of integrability, we can apply Corollary
2.2
and obtain that

\int_{X}(f+1)^{q}\Delta f=\int_{X}\nabla((f+1)^{q})\cdot(\nabla f)=\int_{X}q(f+1)^{q-1}||\nabla f||^{2}.

Since
f
is positive, we get
\int_{X}(f+1)^{q}\leqslant\int_{X}(f+1)^{q+1}.

Therefore we have

(\int_{X}|f+1|^{\frac{n(q+1)}{n-1}})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leqslant C_{S}\Big(\frac{(q+1)^{2}B}{4q}+1\Big)\int_{X}|f+1|^{q+1}.

By taking the
(q+1)
-root, we obtain that

||f+1||_{\frac{n(q+1)}{n-1}}\leqslant\Big(\frac{(q+1)^{2}BC_{S}}{4q}+C_{S}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q+1}}||f+1||_{q+1}.

By using Moser’s iteration procedure, we can conclude the lemma.
∎

The following statement is a variant of
[
Sim88
, Proposition 5.3]
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a reflexive sheaf on a normal compact Kähler variety
(X,\omega)
.
We fix a positive number
B
.
Assume that
H
is a Hermitian metric on
{\mathcal{F}}
with Chern curvature
F_{H}
such that

||\Lambda F_{H}||_{H}\leqslant B.

Assume furthermore that, for any proper non zero saturated coherent subsheaf
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
with orthogonal projection
\pi
, which is
L_{1}^{2}
, we have

\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},H|_{{\mathcal{E}}})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{E}}}<\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}},H)}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{F}}}.

Then there exist two positive constant
D_{1}
,
D_{2}
such that

||s||_{H}\leqslant D_{1}+D_{2}M(H,He^{s}),

for any smooth section
s
of
{\mathcal{E}}nd({{\mathcal{F}}})|_{X_{\mathcal{F}}}
which satisfies the following properties,

(1)

s
is selfadjoint with respect to
H
,

(2)

\mathrm{tr}\,s=0
,

(3)

||s||_{H}
is bounded,

(4)

s
is
L_{1}^{2}
,

(5)

|\Delta\log\,(\mathrm{tr}\,e^{s})|
is integrable,

(6)

||\Lambda F_{s}||_{He^{s}}\leqslant B
, where
F_{s}
is the Chern curvature of the metric
He^{s}
.

Proof.

Let
h=e^{s}
. By Lemma
2.4
, we deduce that

\Delta\log\,(\mathrm{tr}\,h)\geqslant-2(||\Lambda F_{H}||_{H}+||\Lambda F_{s}||_{Hh})\geqslant-4B.

Moreover, since
s
is bounded and
L_{1}^{2}
, by Lemma
2.5
,
we see that
||\nabla\log\,(\mathrm{tr}\,h)||^{2}
is integrable.
Hence by Lemma
6.5
, there are positive constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
such that

||\log\,(\mathrm{tr}\,h)||_{\infty}\leqslant C_{1}||\log\,(\mathrm{tr}\,h)||_{1}+C_{2}.

By taking a local
H
-orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{F}}
which diagonalizes
s
, we see that there are positive constants
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},A_{4}
, depending only on the rank of
{\mathcal{F}}
, such that

A_{1}||s||_{H}-A_{2}\leqslant\log\,(\mathrm{tr}\,h)\leqslant A_{3}||s||_{H}+A_{4}.

Hence there are positive constants
C^{\prime}_{1}
,
C^{\prime}_{2}
such that

\sup_{X}||s||_{H}\leqslant C^{\prime}_{1}\int_{X}||s||_{H}\omega^{n}+C^{\prime}_{2}.

Now we can argue as in
[
Sim88
, Proposition 5.3]
.
Assume by contradiction that lemma does not hold.
Then using the previous inequality, we can show that there is a

-subbundle
\pi\in L^{2}_{1}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}})
on

.
More precisely,

\pi^{2}=\pi

As in
[
UY86

(or
[
Pop05

defines a saturated coherent analytic subsheaf

.
Moreover, by construction, we have
{\mathcal{E}}\subsetneq{\mathcal{F}}|_{X_{\mathcal{F}}}
, and

\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{E}},H|_{{\mathcal{E}}})}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{E}}}\geqslant\frac{\deg_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}},H)}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{F}}}.

By Lemma
2.6
,

extends to a coherent subsheaf of
{\mathcal{F}}
, globally on

We thus obtain a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
H_{0}
be a metric constructed as in Section
3
.
Then by Theorem
1.4
, the heat equation (
2.1
) admits a solution with initial metric
H_{0}
.
We consider the shifted solution
G=G_{t}=G_{0}g_{t}
as in Proposition
5.10
. We recall the following properties.

(1)

The metrics
G_{t}
are admissible for all
t\geqslant 0
, with uniform upper bounds on
||\Lambda F_{G_{t}}||_{G_{t}}
and on
\int_{X}||F_{G_{t}}||_{G_{t}}^{2}
.

(2)

The norm
||g||_{G_{0}}
is bounded over

for any

.

(3)

For

, we have

\int_{X}||({\overline{\partial}}g(t))g(t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}||_{{G_{0},\omega}}^{2}\omega^{n}\leqslant\int_{X}|\mathrm{tr}\,(g\Lambda F_{G}-g\Lambda F_{G_{0}})|\omega^{n}.

(4)

\Delta_{G_{0}}g_{t}
is integrable for any fixed
t\geqslant 0
.

We set
g_{t}=e^{s_{t}}=e^{s}
. Then the Donaldson’s functional
M(G_{t},G_{t^{\prime}})
is well defined for any
t,t^{\prime}\geqslant 0
.
We claim that

(6.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}M(G_{0},G_{t})=-\int_{X}||\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{t}}-\lambda_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}||_{G_{t}}^{2}.

Admitting this equality at the moment.
With Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
5.12
, we verify that the conditions of Lemma
6.6
are satisfied for
s_{t}
.
Thus the lemma shows that
||s||_{G_{0}}
is bounded on
X_{\mathcal{F}}\times[0,\infty)
.
Hence
||g||_{G_{0}}
is bounded as well.
Thanks to Lemma
5.4
and local elliptic estimate, we may extract a sequence
\{t_{i}\}
diverging to
\infty
such that
g_{t_{i}}
converge to a section
g_{\infty}
, smoothly on any compact subset of
X_{\mathcal{F}}
.
It follows that
\mathrm{det}\,g_{\infty}\equiv 1
and the metric
G_{\infty}=G_{0}g_{\infty}
is admissible.

By Lemma
2.5
, and the properties (1) and (3) of
g
, we see that the integrals

\int_{X^{\circ}}||({\overline{\partial}}s_{t})||_{{G_{0},\omega}}^{2}\omega^{n}

are uniformly bounded.
From Lemma
6.1
, we obtain that
M(G_{0},G)
is a bounded function on
[0,\infty)
.
Therefore, the equation (
6.1
) implies that the integrals

\int_{X}||\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{t}}-\lambda_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}||_{G_{t}}^{2}

converge to zero when

diverges to
\infty
.
Thus
\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{\infty}}=\lambda_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}
, and
G_{\infty}
is a Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric.

It remains to prove the equality (
6.1
) above.
We will adapt the method of
[
Sim88
, Lemma 7.1]
.
By Lemma
6.3
, we only need to prove the equality at

.
We recall that

M(G_{0},G_{0}e^{s})=\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\,(s\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{G_{0}})+\int_{X}-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda<\Psi(s)({\overline{\partial}}s),{\overline{\partial}}s>_{G_{0}}.

The heat equation implies that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big|_{t=0}s_{t}=-(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{0}}-\lambda_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}).

Item (1) of Lemma
5.2
implies that there is a constant
C

||\frac{1}{t}s_{t}||_{G_{0}}\leqslant C

for all
0<t\leqslant 1.

Hence we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big|_{t=0}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\,(s_{t}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{0}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\,\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big|_{t=0}s_{t}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{0}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\,\Big((\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{0}}-\lambda_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}})\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{0}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}||\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{0}}-\lambda_{\omega}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}||_{G_{0}}^{2}.

Thus we only need to show that

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}\int_{X}-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}<\Psi(s)({\overline{\partial}}s),{\overline{\partial}}s>_{G_{0}}=0.

We note that
s
is bounded on
X\times[0,1]
after property (2) of
g
.
Thus by Lemma
6.1
, it suffices to show that

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}\int_{X}||{\overline{\partial}}s||_{G_{0}}^{2}=0.

By Lemma
2.5
, it is enough to show that

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}\int_{X}||({\overline{\partial}}g(t))g(t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}||_{G_{0}}^{2}=0.

Thanks to property (3) of

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}\int_{X}|\mathrm{tr}\,(g\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G}-g\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{0}})|\omega^{n}=0.

From the derivative estimate of item (1) of Lemma

such that, for

||g-\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}||_{G_{0}}\leqslant C_{1}t.

We also remark that, since

Therefore, there is a constant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\int_{X}|\mathrm{tr}\,(g\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G}-g\Lambda F_{G_{0}})|

Since

\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{X}||\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G}-\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{0}}||_{G_{0}}\omega^{n}=0.

Thus
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}\int_{X}|\mathrm{tr}\,(g\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G}-g\Lambda_{\omega}F_{G_{0}})|\omega^{n}=0.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

By Theorem
1.5
, there exists an admissible Hermitian-Yang-Mills metric
H
on
{\mathcal{F}}
. Let
F
be its Chern curvature and

T=F-\frac{1}{nr}\omega(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F).

Then on
X_{\mathcal{F}}
, we have the following inequality (see for example
[

\Big(c_{2}({\mathcal{F}},H)-\frac{r-1}{2r}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}},H)^{2}\Big)\wedge\omega^{n-1}=\frac{||T||_{H}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\omega^{n}\geqslant 0.

Since

Furthermore, the equality holds if and only if

which means that the Chern connection is projectively flat.

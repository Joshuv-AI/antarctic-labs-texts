Towards the Automorphism Conjecture I: Combinatorial Control
Bernd S. W. Schröder

Abstract
This paper exploits adjacencies between the orbits of an ordered set P P and a consequence of the classification of finite simple groups to, in many cases, bound the number of automorphisms by 2 0.99 ​ | P | 2^{0.99|P|} . Results clearly identify the structures which currently prevent the proof of such an exponential bound, or which indeed inflate the number of automorphisms beyond such a bound. This is a first step towards a possible resolution of the Automorphism Conjecture for ordered sets.

1
Introduction

An
ordered set

consists of an underlying set
P
equipped with a
reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive relation
\leq
, the order
relation.
An
order-preserving self-map
,
or, an
endomorphism
,
of an ordered set
P
is a
self map

f:P\to P
such that
p\leq q
implies

f(p)\leq f(q)
.
Consistent with standard terminology,
endomorphisms with an inverse that is
an endomorphism, too, are called
automorphisms
.
The set of endomorphisms is denoted
{\rm End}(P)

and the set of automorphisms is denoted
{\rm Aut}(P)
.
Rival and Rutkowski’s
Automorphism Problem (see
[
13
]
, Problem 3)
asks the following.

Open Question 1.1

(Automorphism Problem.)

Is it true that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{|P|=n}{|{\rm Aut}(P)|\over|{\rm End}(P)|}=0?

The
Automorphism Conjecture
states that
the
Automorphism Problem
has an affirmative answer.
In light of the facts that, for
almost every ordered set, the identity is the only
automorphism
(see
[
12
]
, Corollary 2.3a), that
every ordered set
with
n
elements
has at least
2^{{2\over 3}n}
endomorphisms
(see
[
7
]
, Theorem 1),
and that this lower bound has been improved to
at least
2^{n}
endomorphisms
in the unpublished manuscript

[
10
]
,
this conjecture
is quite natural.
However, the Automorphism Conjecture has been remarkably resilient
against attempts to prove it in general.

With lower bounds on the number of endomorphisms available,
it is natural to also consider upper bounds on the number of
automorphisms.
Section
2

shows that,
for the Automorphism Conjecture,
we need not be concerned with
order-autonomous antichains.
Section
3

presents
key results from the
theory of permutation groups
and
Section
4

shows
how to use them to
recursively generate an upper bound for the number of
automorphisms.
Recall that a
block
for a permutation group is a set
B
that,
under each permutation, is either invariant or it is mapped to
a set disjoint from
B
.
Section
5

presents the key notion of
combinatorial control between
blocks
of groups of automorphisms.
Section
6

shows that
block sets on which the automorphism group induces an
alternating or a symmetric group are
indeed the only obstacles to
obtaining
a bound of the form

2^{0.99|P|}
.
Section
7

presents a key notion in which such block sets can be
interrelated.
Section
8
shows how to
compensate for factorials when the
connected components with respect
to this interrelation become too large.
Section
9
provides the main result
with explicit references to all necessary definitions
and Section
10

provides a quick application
to ordered sets of width at most
9
.

2
Exempting Order-Autonomous Antichains
from Further Analysis

Let
P
be an ordered set.
For sets
B,T\subseteq P
, we will write

B<T
iff every
b\in B
is strictly below every
t\in T
.
For singleton sets, we will omit the set braces.
Recall that a nonempty subset
A\subseteq P
is called

order-autonomous

iff, for all
z\in P\setminus A
, we have that
existence of an
a\in A
with
z<a
implies
z<A
, and,
existence of an
a\in A
with
z>a
implies
z>A
.
An
order-autonomous subset
A\subseteq P
will be called
nontrivial

iff
|A|\not\in\{1,|P|\}
.
Further recall that an
antichain
is a subset in which no two distinct elements
are comparable.

Every permutation of a
nontrivial order-autonomous antichain can be
extended to an automorphism of the surrounding ordered set
by mapping all other points to themselves.
Therefore,
nontrivial order-autonomous antichains
inflate the
number of automorphisms and they
tend to clutter
any estimates of their number.
Proposition
2.1
below shows that,
if the Automorphism Conjecture holds
in the absence of nontrivial order-autonomous antichains,
then the Automorphism Conjecture also holds in the presence of
nontrivial order-autonomous antichains.
The underlying idea is a
technique used, for example, in
[
8
,
9
]
:
When there are “many” automorphisms,
we show that
there are
“enough corresponding endomorphisms” to guarantee the
automorphism to endomorphism
ratio’s convergence to zero.

Proposition 2.1

Let
{\cal T}
be a class of finite
ordered sets such that no set in

{\cal T}
has a nontrivial order-autonomous antichain
and for which the Automorphism
Conjecture holds.
Let

{\cal A}
be the
class of
ordered sets that can be obtained from
ordered sets
T\in{\cal T}
by replacing every
element of
T
with a nonempty, but possibly trivial,
order-autonomous antichain.
Then
the Automorphism
Conjecture holds for

{\cal A}
.

Proof.

Let

\varepsilon>0
.
Fix

K\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

{K!\over K^{K}}<\varepsilon

and

2^{-K}<\varepsilon
.
For every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let

h(n):=\sup_{T\in{\cal T},|T|\geq n}{|{\rm Aut}(T)|\over|{\rm End}(T)|}.

Choose
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that, for all

n\geq N
, we have

h\left(n-K^{2}\right)\leq{\varepsilon\over\left(K^{2}\right)!}
.
Now let

P\in{\cal A}

have
n\geq N
elements.

Let
M\subseteq{\rm Aut}(P)
be the
subgroup of automorphisms that
map every maximal
(with respect to inclusion)
order-autonomous antichain
to itself.
Let
\Phi\in M
.
Because every
\Psi\in{\rm Aut}(P)
maps
maximal order-autonomous antichains
to maximal
order-autonomous antichains,
every

\Psi^{-1}\Phi\Psi

maps every
maximal
order-autonomous antichain
to itself.
Therefore
M
is a normal subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(P)

and we have

|{\rm Aut}(P)|=|{\rm Aut}(P)/M|\cdot|M|
.

Let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell}
be the individual sizes of the
nontrivial
maximal order-autonomous antichains
in
P
.
Then
|M|=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}m_{j}!
.

Case 1:

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}m_{j}>K^{2}
.

Every order-autonomous antichain
with
m_{j}
elements has a total of

m_{j}^{m_{j}}
self-maps.
Hence, in every
[\Psi]\in{\rm Aut}(P)/M
,
there are exactly

\prod_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}!

automorphisms of
P
and,
for every
[\Psi]\in{\rm Aut}(P)/M
,
there are at least

\prod_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}^{m_{j}}

corresponding endomorphisms.
Hence

{|{\rm Aut}(P)|\over|{\rm End}(P)|}\leq{|{\rm Aut}(P)/M|\prod_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}!\over|{\rm Aut}(P)/M|\prod_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}^{m_{j}}}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}{m_{j}!\over m_{j}^{m_{j}}}
.
Now,
if there is an

m_{j}\geq K
,
we obtain

{|{\rm Aut}(P)|\over|{\rm End}(P)|}\leq{m_{j}!\over m_{j}^{m_{j}}}\leq{K!\over K^{K}}<\varepsilon.

Otherwise, for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, we
have

m_{j}<K
.
In this case,

\ell\geq K

and
hence

{|{\rm Aut}(P)|\over|{\rm End}(P)|}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}{m_{j}!\over m_{j}^{m_{j}}}\leq\left({2\over 4}\right)^{\ell}\leq\left({1\over 2}\right)^{K}<\varepsilon.

Case 2:

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}m_{j}\leq K^{2}
.

Let
T\subseteq P
be obtained by,
from every nontrivial maximal
order-autonomous antichain,
deleting all but one
element.
For every
t\in T
, let
A_{t}
be the unique
maximal order-autonomous antichain that contains
t
.
Then,
for every
equivalence class
[\Psi]\in{\rm Aut}(P)/M
,
and all

\Phi\in[\Psi]
, we have that

\Phi[A_{t}]\cap T=\Psi[A_{t}]\cap T
.
Hence if, for every
[\Psi]\in{\rm Aut}(P)/M
and
every
t\in T
, we set

\Psi_{T}[t]:=\Psi[A_{t}]\cap T
, we obtain a well-defined function, and
it is easy to check that
\Psi_{T}\in{\rm Aut}(T)
.
Via the map
[\Psi]\mapsto\Psi_{T}
,
we see that

{\rm Aut}(T)

contains a subgroup that is isomorphic to
the factor group

{\rm Aut}(P)/M
.
Moreover, because every endomorphism
f
of
T

can be extended to an endomorphism of
P

by mapping every
A_{t}
to
f(t)
, we have

|{\rm End}(P)|\geq|{\rm End}(T)|
.
Therefore

{|{\rm Aut}(P)|\over|{\rm End}(P)|}\leq{|{\rm Aut}(T)|\cdot\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}m_{j}!\right)\over|{\rm End}(T)|}\leq h\left(|T|\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}m_{j}\right)!\leq h\left(n-K^{2}\right)\left(K^{2}\right)!<\varepsilon.

Primitive Nestings

Because the automorphism group is a special type of permutation
group, we need
key concepts and
deep results from the theory of permutation groups.
In the case of Corollary
3.5
below,
a
simple computation leads to a simple improvement
of a result by A. Maróti.
As indicated in the Mathematical Review MR1943938
of
[
11
]
,
Theorem
3.3

below rests on the shoulders of the giant which is the
Classification of Finite Simple Groups.
Consequently, the subsequent
results on the Automorphism Problem
are rather deep by default.
Thanks to the accessible writing in
[
5
]

and
[
11
]
, this depth is
within reach of the author’s humble combinatorial means.

We should note that
the results from
[
11

are also used to compute
upper bounds for the order of
automorphism groups of graphs, see,
for example,
[
2
]
.
However, our goal here
is an exponential bound of
2^{0.99n}

plus a clear understanding
of the structure of the sets for which this bound does not hold,
see Theorem
9.1
below.
Meanwhile, for graphs,
the goal
is a subexponential bound, which necessitates a focus on
very special classes of graphs.
Consequently, the approach here is complementary to
corresponding work in graph theory, with the intersection

[

Let
G

X

The

of

|X|
of the underlying set

For every

(
G

of

We call
G

Without further knowledge about interactions between orbits,
it is natural to focus on transitive permutation groups.

explicitly stated or part of the definition.
Starting in Section

Proposition 3.7

(Folklore.)
Let
G
be a
transitive
permutation group on the set
X

and let
B
be a
G
-block.
Then
the
block system

G\cdot B
is a partition of
X
,
for every
\sigma\in G
and every block
B^{\prime}\in G\cdot B
,
we have

\sigma[B^{\prime}]\in G\cdot B
, and,
if
A
is a block that contains
B
, then
every block in
G\cdot B
that intersects
A

is contained in
A
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma,\tau\in G
such that

\sigma[B]\cap\tau[B]\not=\emptyset
.
Then
\tau^{-1}\sigma[B]\cap B\not=\emptyset
.
Hence, because
B
is a block, we infer

\tau^{-1}\sigma[B]\cap B=B
, and then
\sigma[B]=\tau[B]
.
This proves that

G\cdot B
is a partition of
X
. By definition,
every
\sigma\in G
maps every block in
G\cdot B

to another block in
G\cdot B
.

Finally, let
A
be a block that contains
B

and let
C:=\sigma[B]\in G\cdot B
intersect

.
Then
\sigma[A]\cap A\not=\emptyset
,
and therefore
\sigma[A]=A
.
Consequently,
C=\sigma[B]\subseteq\sigma[A]=A
.

4
Reverse Depth
Enumerations for Primitive Nesting Collections

Absence of nontrivial order-autonomous antichains is
crucial for obtaining exponential upper bounds, and,
for the Automorphism Conjecture,
Proposition
2.1

allows us
to exclude nontrivial order-autonomous antichains
from further consideration.
Moreover, we will later need to
apply some results to subgroups
G^{*}
of the
automorphism group,
and we will mostly work with the actions of
G^{*}
on orbits.
Therefore, we introduce
the following terminology.

Definition 4.1

Let
U

be an ordered set without nontrivial
order-autonomous antichains.
Let
G^{*}
be a
subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(U)
.
The set of all
G^{*}
-orbits will be denoted
{\cal D}
.
We will call
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})

a
standard configuration
.

For every
G^{*}
-orbit

D\in{\cal D}
,
we define

\Lambda^{*}(D):=\left\{\Phi|_{D}:\Phi\in G^{*}\right\}
,
where the superscript shall be our indication that we work with
the subgroup
G^{*}
.

Clearly, the group
G^{*}
, viewed as a
permutation group,
will not be transitive.
Rather, the groups
\Lambda^{*}(D)

can be viewed as a collection of
“interacting transitive permutation groups.”
It will prove useful to
associate a primitive
nesting with every
D\in{\cal D}
.

Definition 4.2

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration.
For every
D\in{\cal D}
, we can choose a primitive nesting

\{x^{D}\}=B_{0}^{D}\subsetneq B_{1}^{D}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq B_{m_{D}}^{D}=D

for

\Lambda^{*}(D)
.
Upon making such a choice for every
D\in{\cal D}
,
we call the set

{\cal B}:=\left\{B_{j}^{D}:D\in{\cal D},j\in\{0,\ldots,m_{D}\}\right\}

a
primitive nesting collection

for
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
.
We let
{\cal F}\subseteq(G^{*}\cdot{\cal B})^{2}
be
the set of all factorial pairs
(S,B)\in(G^{*}\cdot{\cal B})^{2}

of width
w\geq 5
.

We call

(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B})
, as well as,
when factorial pairs are specifically addressed,

(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B},{\cal F})
, a

nested standard configuration
.

Within a primitive nesting collection
{\cal B}
,
ordered by inclusion,
for every non-maximal block, we have unique successor.
Because the pair consisting of a
block and its successor is a primitive pair,
these unique successors
are useful for enumeration,
and they
will be useful for all
blocks in
G^{*}\cdot{\cal B}
, too.

Definition 4.3

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B})
be a
nested standard configuration.
For every

C\in G^{*}\cdot{\cal B}
,
we define
D_{C}\in{\cal D}
to be the unique orbit
that
contains
C
.
For every

C\in{\cal B}
,
we define the

nesting depth
of
C
to be the unique index
j
such that

C=B_{j}^{D_{C}}
.
Moreover, when the nesting depth of

C\in{\cal B}
is

j<m_{D_{C}}
, we define

C^{\dagger}
to be the unique block in

{\cal B}

such that
\left(C,C^{\dagger}\right)
is a primitive pair, that is,

\left(C,C^{\dagger}\right)=\left(B_{j}^{D_{C}},B_{j+1}^{D_{C}}\right)
.
For every conjugate
\widetilde{C}\in\Lambda^{*}(D_{C})\cdot C

of
C
, we define the
successor

\widetilde{C}^{\dagger}\in\Lambda^{*}(D_{C})\cdot C^{\dagger}

to be the unique conjugate of
C^{\dagger}

such that
\left(\widetilde{C},\widetilde{C}^{\dagger}\right)

is a primitive pair.

Some of the following
lemmas will be needed later for general sets
{\cal K}
of

G^{*}
-blocks that need not be
associated with a primitive nesting
{\cal B}
.
Hence, although in this section these sets will
satisfy
{\cal K}\subseteq{\cal B}
,
when possible, results will be proved in the absence of
any
primitive nesting collections.

Definition 4.4

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a
standard configuration,
let
G^{\prime}
be a subgroup of
G^{*}
,
and let

{\cal K}
be a set of blocks for
G^{*}
.
We define

G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}:=\left\{\Phi\in G^{\prime}:(\forall K\in{\cal K})\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}={\rm id}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}\right\}.

In cases in which
{\cal K}
can be easily enumerated
as
{\cal K}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}
, we will
write

G^{\prime}_{\searrow B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}}

instead of

G^{\prime}_{\searrow\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}}
.

Lemma 4.5

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a
standard configuration,
let
G^{\prime}
be a subgroup of
G^{*}
,
and let

{\cal K}
be a set of blocks for
G^{*}
.
Then
G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}

is a normal subgroup of
G^{\prime}
.
Moreover,
for every

B\not\in{\cal K}
, the factor
group

G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}/G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}\cup\{B\}}

is isomorphic to

\left\{\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B}:\Phi\in G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}\right\}
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi\in G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}
,
let
\Psi\in G^{\prime}
and let

K\in{\cal K}
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\Psi^{-1}\circ\Phi\circ\Psi\right)\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Psi^{-1}\right)\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}\circ\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}\circ\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Psi^{-1}\right)\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}\circ{\rm id}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}\circ\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Psi^{-1}\right)\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}\circ\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Psi^{-1}\Psi\right)\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm id}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{K})\cdot K},

and hence, because
K\in{\cal K}
was arbitrary,

G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}

is a normal subgroup of

G^{\prime}
.

Now let

B\not\in{\cal K}

and let

[\Phi],[\Psi]\in G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}/G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}\cup\{B\}}
.
Then
[\Phi]=[\Psi]

iff there is a
\Delta\in G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}\cup\{B\}}

such that

\Phi\circ\Delta=\Psi
,
which is the case iff

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B}
.
Thus the function

[\Phi]\mapsto\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B}

is well-defined and injective
from

G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}/G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}\cup\{B\}}

to

\left\{\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B}:\Phi\in G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}\right\}
.
Because the function
clearly is surjective and
a homomorphism, we obtain that

G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}/G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}\cup\{B\}}

is isomorphic to

\left\{\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B}:\Phi\in G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}\right\}
.

Definition 4.6

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B})
be a nested
standard configuration,
let

{\cal K}\subseteq{\cal B}

and let
B\in{\cal B}\setminus{\cal K}
.
We define

\displaystyle\Lambda^{*}\left({\cal K}\not\searrow B\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B}:\Phi\in G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal K}}\right\}.

Remark 4.7

Because

\left|\Lambda^{*}\left({\cal K}\not\searrow B\right)\right|

can be cumbersome to compute,
via Proposition
3.9
,
we
note the following upper bounds when
B^{\dagger}\in{\cal K}
.

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda^{*}\left({\cal K}\not\searrow B\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\left\{\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B}:\Phi\in G^{*},\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B^{\dagger}}={\rm id}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B^{\dagger}}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D_{B})\cdot B}_{B^{\dagger}}\right|^{\left|D_{B}\right|\over\left|B^{\dagger}\right|}

Note that, when

\left(B,B^{\dagger}\right)

is not a factorial pair of width
\geq 5
, then the above is
bounded by

\left(2^{d_{\left|B^{\dagger}\right|/|B|}{\left|B^{\dagger}\right|\over\left|B\right|}}\right)^{\left|D_{B}\right|\over\left|B^{\dagger}\right|}=2^{d_{\left|B^{\dagger}\right|/|B|}{\left|D_{B}\right|\over\left|B\right|}}
.
Products of such powers will be crucial for our estimates and they
lead us to Lemma
4.8
below.

Lemma 4.8

Let
1\leq c_{0}<c_{1}<\cdots<c_{m}=x
be natural numbers such that,
for all
j\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
, the quotient

{c_{j+1}\over c_{j}}

is an integer greater than or equal to
2
.
Then

\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}2^{d_{c_{j+1}/c_{j}}{x\over c_{j}}}\leq 2^{1.54{x\over c_{0}}}.

Proof.

Note that, for
b_{j}:={c_{j}\over c_{0}}
, we have that

{b_{j+1}\over b_{j}}={c_{j+1}\over c_{j}}\geq 2
.
Because
b_{0}=1
, we obtain
b_{j}\geq 2^{j}
.
Now we have the following.

\displaystyle\lg\left(\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}2^{d_{c_{j+1}/c_{j}}{x\over c_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{c_{j+1}/c_{j}}{x\over c_{j}}={x\over c_{0}}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{b_{j+1}/b_{j}}{1\over b_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{x\over c_{0}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s}d_{b_{j+1}/b_{j}}{1\over b_{j}}+\sum_{j=s+1}^{m-1}{d_{b_{j+1}/b_{j}}\over b_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{x\over c_{0}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s}d_{b_{j+1}/b_{j}}{1\over b_{j}}+\sum_{j=s+1}^{m-1}{1.38\over 2^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{x\over c_{0}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s}d_{b_{j+1}/b_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}{b_{i-1}\over b_{i}}+{1.38\over 2^{s}}\right)

It is simple to write the nested loops that compute
an upper bound for

\sum_{j=0}^{s}d_{b_{j+1}/b_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}{b_{i-1}\over b_{i}}

for
s=7
:
The factor

d_{b_{j+1}/b_{j}}

from Definition
3.4

only depends on
the number

b_{j+1}/b_{j}
,
and, for

b_{j+1}/b_{j}\not\in I
, we have

d_{b_{j+1}/b_{j}}=1
.
For any reciprocal

{b_{i}\over b_{i-1}}\in I
,
we use

{b_{i-1}\over b_{i}}
as a factor in the product.
For any reciprocal

{b_{i}\over b_{i-1}}\not\in I
,
we use

{1\over 14}
as an upper bound
for the factor

{b_{i-1}\over b_{i}}

in the product.
Thus, for each factor,
if the
reciprocal is not in
I
,
we can use a single number,
leading to
finitely many combinations that need to be considered.

Computationally, we obtain

\sum_{j=0}^{7}d_{b_{j+1}/b_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}{b_{i-1}\over b_{i}}\leq 1.5284
.
Together with

{1.38\over 2^{7}}\leq 0.0108
,
we obtain

{x\over c_{0}}(1.5284+0.0108)\leq 1.54{x\over c_{0}}

as an upper bound for the logarithm.

Lemma

4.5

suggests the successive expansion of
a set
{\cal K}\subset{\cal B}
by adding controlled blocks.
To apply Lemma
4.8
, we must have primitive
pairs, which is the motivation behind
part
2
of Definition
4.9

below.

Definition 4.9

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B})
be a nested
standard configuration.
A vector
\overrightarrow{{\cal K}}=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{k})\in{\cal B}^{k}

is a
reverse depth enumeration

iff
the following hold.

1.

For all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
we have
B_{i}\not=B_{j}
.

2.

For all

j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, the set

{\cal K}_{j}:=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{j}\}

is
upper block closed
, that is,
for all
K\in{\cal K}_{j}
and
B\in{\cal B}
, we have that

K\subseteq B
implies
B\in{\cal K}_{j}
.

We set

{\cal K}:=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\}
, and,
for every

B\in{\cal K}
,
we define

\overleftarrow{\cal K}(B):={\cal K}_{j-1}
, where

j
is the index of
B
in the reverse depth enumeration.
For simpler notation, we will write

\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal K}\not\searrow B\right)

instead of

\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal K}(B)\not\searrow B\right)
.

We call

\left(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B},\overrightarrow{{\cal K}}\right)

a
partially enumerated nested
standard configuration
.
In case
{\cal K}={\cal B}
, we will
call

\left(U,{\cal D},G^{*};\overrightarrow{\cal B}\right)
,
as well as

\left(U,{\cal D},G^{*};\overrightarrow{\cal B},{\cal F}\right)
,
an

enumerated nested
standard configuration
.

Components of a reverse depth enumeration
\overrightarrow{K}
will
typically be denoted
C
, as they are
considered to be
controlled
.

Proposition 4.10

Let

\left(U,{\cal D},G^{*};\overrightarrow{{\cal B}}\right)

be an enumerated nested
standard configuration.
Then

\displaystyle{\left|G^{*}\right|=\prod_{C\in{\cal B}}\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal B}\not\searrow C\right)\right|.}

Proof.

Let

\overrightarrow{{\cal B}}=:(B_{1},\ldots,B_{m})

and let
B_{0}:=\emptyset
.
By Lemma
4.5
,
for every

k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, the factor
group

G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal B}_{k-1}}/G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal B}_{k}}=G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal B}_{k-1}}/G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal B}_{k-1}\cup\{B_{k}\}}

is isomorphic to

\Lambda^{*}\left({\cal B}_{k-1}\not\searrow B_{k}\right)=\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal B}\not\searrow B_{k}\right)
.

Because
\{{\rm id}\}=G^{*}_{\searrow{\cal B}_{m}}\triangleleft G^{*}_{\searrow{\cal B}_{m-1}}\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft G^{*}_{\searrow{\cal B}_{1}}\triangleleft G^{*}_{\searrow{\cal B}_{0}}=G^{*}

is a normal series, we obtain

\left|G^{*}\right|=\prod_{k=1}^{m}\left|G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal B}_{k-1}}/G^{\prime}_{\searrow{\cal B}_{k}}\right|=\prod_{k=1}^{m}\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal B}\not\searrow B_{k}\right)\right|
.

If none of the primitive groups

\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal B}\not\searrow C\right)

contains an alternating group, then
Remark
4.7
and
Lemma
4.8

provide an exponential bound
for the product in
Proposition

4.10
, albeit with an exponent that is
slightly too high.
To gain better control over the product, we
introduce a bounding product in which
every factor

\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C)

corresponds to a

\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal B}\not\searrow C\right)\right|
, but in which we
will have the freedom
to
“trade factors
between sets of blocks.”
There will be three types of
problematic factors
\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C)
,
and they give rise to the set
{\cal K}_{u}^{\gamma}
below:
Those that do not satisfy a
bound like a primitive group of the same degree would satisfy
and
those for which the block
C
is of level zero, but
insufficient to
drop the factor in the exponent from

1.54
to
0.99
, in which case we distinguish
whether

\left(C,C^{\dagger}\right)
is factorial of
width
\geq 5
or not.

Definition 4.11

Let

\left(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B},\overrightarrow{{\cal K}}\right)

be a partially enumerated nested
standard configuration.
We call the function

\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}:{\cal K}\to[1,\infty)

a
control function

iff
the following hold.

1.

\displaystyle{\prod_{C\in{\cal K}}\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal K}\not\searrow C\right)\right|\leq\prod_{C\in{\cal K}}\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C).}

2.

For all

C\in{\cal K}
of
depth at least
1

such that

\left(C,C^{\dagger}\right)
is
of width
w
, but
not factorial
of width
w\geq 5
,
we have

\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C)\leq\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal K}\not\searrow C\right)\right|\leq 2^{d_{w}|\Lambda^{*}(D_{C})\cdot C|}
.

We also define

{\cal K}_{f}

to be the set of all

S\in{\cal K}
such that

\left(S,S^{\dagger}\right)
is factorial
of width
w\geq 5
,
we define

{\cal K}_{0}

to be the set of all

S\in{\cal K}
that
have depth
0
,
and we define the set of

(
\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}
-)uncontrolled factors in
{\cal K}
to be

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{u}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{C\in{\cal K}_{f}\setminus{\cal K}_{0}:\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C)>2^{d_{\left|C^{\dagger}\right|/|C|}|\Lambda^{*}(D_{C})\cdot C|}\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{C\in{\cal K}_{f}\cap{\cal K}_{0}:\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C)\prod_{T\subset D_{C},T\in{\cal K}\setminus{\cal K}_{f}}\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(T)>2^{0.99|D_{C}|}\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{C\in{\cal K}_{0}\setminus{\cal K}_{f}:\prod_{T\subset D_{C},T\in{\cal K}\setminus{\cal K}_{f}}\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(T)>2^{0.99|D_{C}|}\right\}.

Because our combinatorial analysis will focus on
individual orbits and their interactions, the
following reindexing will be helpful.

Definition 4.12

Let

\left(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B},\overrightarrow{{\cal K}}\right)

be a partially enumerated nested
standard configuration,
and let
\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}
be a control function.
For every
D\in{\cal D}
, we define

{\cal K}^{D}:={\cal K}\cap\left\{B_{0}^{D},B_{1}^{D},\ldots,B_{m_{D}-1}^{D}\right\}
,

{\cal K}^{\gamma,D}_{u}:={\cal K}_{u}^{\gamma}\cap\left\{B_{0}^{D},B_{1}^{D},\ldots,B_{m_{D}-1}^{D}\right\}
,

\displaystyle\Omega_{{\cal K}}\left(D\right):=\prod_{C\in{\cal K}^{D}}\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C)

\displaystyle{\rm and}

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{{\cal K}}\left(D\right):=\prod_{C\in{\cal K}^{\gamma,D}_{u}}\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C).

Proposition 4.13

Let

\left(U,{\cal D},G^{*};\overrightarrow{{\cal B}}\right)

be an enumerated nested
standard configuration,
and let
\gamma_{\cal B}^{*}
be a control function.
Then

\displaystyle{\left|G^{*}\right|\leq\prod_{D\in{\cal D}}\Omega_{{\cal B}}\left(D\right)\leq 2^{0.99|U|}\prod_{D\in{\cal D}}\Upsilon_{{\cal B}}\left(D\right).}

Proof.

By Proposition
4.10

and by part
1

of Definition
4.11
,
we have

\left|G^{*}\right|=\prod_{C\in{\cal B}}\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal B}\not\searrow C\right)\right|\leq\prod_{C\in{\cal K}}\gamma_{\cal B}^{*}(C)
.
The inequalities follow because
\prod_{D\in{\cal D}}\Omega_{{\cal B}}\left(D\right)

is a
simple reindexing of

\prod_{C\in{\cal B}}\gamma_{\cal B}^{*}(C)

and from the fact that

\Omega_{\cal B}(D)\leq 2^{0.99|D|}\Upsilon_{\cal B}(D)
.

Finally, we note that the
net contribution of the factors that
are not in
{\cal K}_{u}^{\gamma,D}

is exponential, albeit with a factor that is slightly
too large.

Proposition 4.14

Let

\left(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B},\overrightarrow{{\cal K}}\right)

be a partially enumerated nested
standard configuration,
let
\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}
be a control function,
and let
D\in{\cal D}
.

1.

If

B_{0}^{D}\not\in{\cal K}^{D}
,
then

\Omega_{{\cal K}}(D)\leq 2^{1.54{|D|\over\left|B_{1}^{D}\right|}}\Upsilon_{{\cal K}}\left(D\right)
.

2.

If

B_{0}^{D}\not\in{\cal K}^{D}
and

\left|\Lambda^{*}\left({\cal K}\not\searrow B_{0}^{D}\right)\right|\leq 2^{d_{\left|B_{1}^{D}\right|/\left|B_{0}^{D}\right|}|D|}
,
then

\Omega_{{\cal K}}(D)\left|\Lambda^{*}\left({\cal K}\not\searrow B_{0}^{D}\right)\right|\leq 2^{1.54{|D|}}\Upsilon_{{\cal K}}\left(D\right)
.

Proof.

Let
{\cal K}^{D}=:\{C_{0},\ldots,C_{m-1}\}
, enumerated
so that
C_{0}\subset C_{1}\subset\cdots\subset C_{m-1}\subset C_{m}:=D
.

To prove part
1
,
let
C_{0}=B_{0}^{D}\not\in{\cal K}^{D}
.
By Definition

4.12

and by Lemma
4.8
,
we have

\displaystyle{\Omega_{{\cal K}}\left(D\right)\over\Upsilon_{{\cal K}}\left(D\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{C\in{\cal K}^{D}\setminus{\cal K}^{\gamma,D}_{u}}\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C)\leq\prod_{C\in\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{m-1}\}}2^{d_{\left|C^{\dagger}\right|/|C|}|\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot C|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}2^{d_{\left|C_{j+1}\right|/|C_{j}|}{|D|\over|C_{j}|}}\leq 2^{1.54{|D|\over|C_{1}|}}

To prove part
2
,
let

B_{0}^{D}\not\in{\cal K}^{D}
and

\left|\Lambda^{*}\left({\cal K}\not\searrow B_{0}^{D}\right)\right|\leq 2^{d_{\left|B_{1}^{D}\right|/\left|B_{0}^{D}\right|}|D|}
.
The same computation as for
part
1
,
with estimates starting with

C_{0}
instead of
C_{1}
yields

\Omega_{{\cal K}}(D)\left|\Lambda^{*}\left({\cal K}\not\searrow B_{0}^{D}\right)\right|\leq 2^{1.54{|D|}}\Upsilon_{{\cal K}}\left(D\right)
.

For a
fully enumerated nested
standard configuration

\left(U,{\cal D},G^{*};\overrightarrow{{\cal B}}\right)
,
Propositions
4.13

and
4.14

show that a control function

\gamma_{\cal B}^{*}

such that

\Upsilon_{{\cal B}}\left(D\right)=1

for all
D\in{\cal D}

will give the desired bound

\left|G^{*}\right|\leq\prod_{D\in{\cal D}}\Omega_{{\cal B}}\left(D\right)\leq 2^{0.99|U|}
.
Note that, for
any
reverse depth enumeration
\overrightarrow{\cal K}
that
does not contain any blocks of depth
0
,

\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C):=\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal K}\not\searrow C\right)\right|
defines a
control function.
Therefore,
to achieve a bound

|{\rm Aut}(U)|\leq 2^{0.99|U|}
, we must compensate for
the factorial factors

\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal B}\not\searrow C\right)\right|

that are collected in the product

\prod_{D\in{\cal D}}\Upsilon_{{\cal B}}\left(D\right)
,
and
we must expand the function to blocks at depth
0

so that any new large factors can be similarly compensated.
Before we turn to the combinatorial side of this issue, we
record a useful fact about the dichotomous heredity of
factorial pairs.
Because the ideas of
Lemma

4.15

below
will need to be iterated, instead of
G^{*}
,
we use
a subgroup
G^{\prime}
of
G^{*}
.

Lemma 4.15

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a
standard configuration,
let
G^{\prime}
be a subgroup of
G^{*}
,
let

{\cal K}
be a set of blocks for
G^{*}
,
and let
(S,B)

be a factorial
pair of width
w\geq 5
for
G^{\prime}
.
If

G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}

has more than one element, then

\left(S,B\right)
is a factorial
pair of width
w\geq 5
for

G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{\prime}
,
too.

Proof.

Because

\left(S,B\right)
is a factorial
pair
of width
w\geq 5

for
G^{\prime}
, the group

G^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}

is the symmetric or the alternating group on
the set

B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]
, which has

w\geq 5
elements.
By Proposition
3.9
,
because

G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}

has more than one element,

G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}

must have more than one element, too.

We now prove that

G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}

is a normal subgroup of

G^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}
.
Let
\sigma\in G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}

and let

\mu\in G^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}
.
Then there are

\Phi\in G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{\prime}

such that

\sigma=\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}

and

\Psi\in G^{\prime}
such that

\mu=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}
.
Now

\displaystyle\mu^{-1}\circ\sigma\circ\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}\right)^{-1}\circ\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}\circ\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Psi^{-1}\right)\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}\circ\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}\circ\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Psi^{-1}\circ\Phi\circ\Psi\right)\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{*}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B},

where the final step is guaranteed by Lemma
4.5
.
Hence

G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}

is a normal subgroup of

G^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}
.

The only normal subgroups of a symmetric group

S_{w}
with
w\geq 5
are
S_{w}
itself,
the alternating group
A_{w}
, and the
singleton group
\{{\rm id}\}
.
The only normal subgroups of an alternating group

A_{w}
with
w\geq 5
are
A_{w}
itself and the
singleton group
\{{\rm id}\}
.
Because

G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{\prime}\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}

is not the singleton group
\{{\rm id}\}
,
it must be the alternating group or the symmetric group
on
B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]
.
Hence

\left(S,B\right)
is a factorial
pair for

G_{\searrow{\cal K}}^{\prime}
.

5
Inter-Orbit Combinatorial Control

For two orbits
D,E\in{\cal D}
,
the action of

G^{*}

on
E
can partially or completely
determine its action on
D
.
Lemma
5.10
and
Definition
5.11

below
show
how this
“direct inter-orbit combinatorial control”
actually
manifests between block
systems
of the groups

\Lambda^{*}(E)

and

\Lambda^{*}(D)
.
This “control”
will be our primary tool to avoid, or
compensate for,
large factors

\gamma_{\cal K}^{*}(C)

that are collected
in
\prod_{D\in{\cal D}}\Upsilon_{\cal K}(D)
.
Because the interactions investigated in this section are local, we
will not fix a global primitive nesting collection.
Rather, a (controlling) block
C\subseteq E
will
be used to control the
action on (sub)blocks
S\subset D
, which are themselves
contained in a block
B\subseteq D
.
As in the previous section,
blocks will routinely be used as representatives
of their corresponding block systems.
We start by indicating how blocks can be related to each other.

Definition 5.1

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let
D,E\in{\cal D}
,
let
S
be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(D)
,
and
let

C
be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(E)
.

1.

We say that
C
is

(nontrivially) woven
with

S

and write
C\curlyvee S
iff there
are elements
c\in C
and
s\in S
that are comparable, as well as
elements
c^{\prime}\in C
and
s^{\prime}\in S
that are incomparable.

2.

We say that

S
is
made
by
C

and write
C\diamondsuit S

iff
C>S
or
C<S
.

3.

We say that
C
is
incomparable to (every element in)

S

and write
C\|S
iff
no element
c\in C
is comparable to any element
s\in S
.

In case

S\diamondsuit C
or
S\|C
, we will also say that

C
is
(trivially) woven
with
S
.

Some
weavings
can be considered similar/equivalent.

Definition 5.2

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let
D,E\in{\cal D}
, let
S_{1},S_{2}
be blocks of

\Lambda^{*}(D)
,
and
let

C_{1},C_{2}
be blocks of

\Lambda^{*}(E)
.
Then we say that
(C_{1},S_{1})
and
(C_{2},S_{2})
are

similarly
woven

and write
(C_{1},S_{1})\bowtie(C_{2},S_{2})

iff there is a

\Phi\in G^{*}
such that

\Phi[C_{1}]=C_{2}
and
\Phi[S_{1}]=S_{2}
.

Lemma 5.3

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let

D,E\in{\cal D}
, let

S
be a
block of

\Lambda^{*}(D)
,
and
let

C
be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(E)
.
Then the relation

\bowtie
is an equivalence relation on
the product

\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S\times\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C
.
Moreover,
in case
S\not=D
or
C\not=E
,
no
\bowtie
-equivalence class is a singleton.

Proof.

Because
the identity is an automorphism,

\bowtie
is reflexive.
Because
every
automorphisms has an
inverse,

\bowtie
is symmetric.
Because
the composition of two automorphisms is again an automorphism,

\bowtie
is transitive.

Finally, in case
S\not=D
or
C\not=E
,
one of

\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S

or

\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C

has more than one element.
Because
G^{*}
acts transitively on

\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S

and on
\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C
,
we infer that
no
\bowtie
-equivalence class is a singleton.

Definition 5.4

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let
D,E\in{\cal D}
, let

S
be a
block of

\Lambda^{*}(D)
,
and
let

C
be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(E)
.
Let
{\cal S}_{1},\ldots,{\cal S}_{p}
be the
equivalence classes of

\bowtie

on
\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S\times\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C

that contain
nontrivially
woven
pairs

C^{\prime}\curlyvee S^{\prime}
.
Then,
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
and all

(C^{\prime},S^{\prime})\in{\cal S}_{j}
,
we will write
C^{\prime}\curlyvee_{j}S^{\prime}
.
These relations technically are
not symmetric, and they, as well as their number
p
,
depend on the
containing orbits, as well as the
block systems involved.
Because the direction, the containing
orbits and the block systems
will be clear from the context, similar to

\diamondsuit
and
\|
, they
will not be indicated in the
relation symbol

\curlyvee_{j}

or in the number
p
.

Notation 5.5

From now on,
p
will denote the
number of
\bowtie
-equivalence classes that contain
similarly
nontrivially woven
pairs

C^{\prime}\curlyvee S^{\prime}
, and

\curlyvee_{1},\ldots,\curlyvee_{p}

will denote the corresponding weaving relations.

Now note that
the
blocks in one orbit, which have the same
weavings
with
certain blocks in
other
orbits,
can be combined
to form a “block of blocks.”

Definition 5.6

Let
w,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{w}
be
pairwise disjoint nonempty sets.
We define
(P_{1},\ldots,P_{m})
to be a

sorted partition

of
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{w}\}
iff
every

P_{j}
is a (not necessarily nonempty)
subset of
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{w}\}

and the set
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}\}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
is a partition
of
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{w}\}
.

Definition 5.7

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let
D,E\in{\cal D}
, let
C\subsetneq E
be a

\Lambda^{*}(E)
-block, let

,
and
let

S
be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(D)
.
The

S
-partition of

\left(S_{\diamondsuit}(Z),S_{\|}(Z),S_{\curlyvee_{1}}(Z),\ldots,S_{\curlyvee_{p}}(Z)\right)

of

The set

of blocks in

.

2.

The set

of blocks in

.

3.

The sets

-woven
with

.

Definition 5.8

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let

D,E\in{\cal D}
,
let

be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(E)

let

,

let

be a

\Lambda^{*}(D)

-home block of

,

Lemma 5.9

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let

D,E\in{\cal D}
, let
S
be a
block of

\Lambda^{*}(D)
,
and
let

C
be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(E)
.
Then, for every
S^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S
, the

\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C
-home block

H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})
of
S^{\prime}
is a block of
the action of

G^{*}

on
\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S
.

Proof.

Let
S^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S
and let

\Phi\in G^{*}

such that

\Phi\left[H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})\right]\cap H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})\not=\emptyset
.
Because, for every
S^{\prime\prime}\in H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})
,
we have

H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime\prime})=H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})
,
we can assume without
loss of generality that

\Phi(S^{\prime})\in H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})
.

Let

\bigstar\in\{\diamondsuit,\|,\curlyvee_{1},\ldots,\curlyvee_{p}\}

and let
C^{\prime}\in{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}
.
Then

S^{\prime}\bigstar C^{\prime}

iff

\Phi[S^{\prime}]\bigstar\Phi[C^{\prime}]
,
and, because

\Phi(S^{\prime})\in H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})
,
this is the case iff

S^{\prime}\bigstar\Phi[C^{\prime}]
.
Hence

\displaystyle\Phi\left[S^{\prime}_{\bigstar}({\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\left[\left\{C^{\prime}\in{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}:S^{\prime}\bigstar C^{\prime}\right\}\right]=\left\{\Phi\left[C^{\prime}\right]\in{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}:S^{\prime}\bigstar C^{\prime}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\Phi\left[C^{\prime}\right]\in{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}:S^{\prime}\bigstar\Phi\left[C^{\prime}\right]\right\}=\left\{C^{\prime\prime}\in{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}:S^{\prime}\bigstar C^{\prime\prime}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{\prime}_{\bigstar}\left({\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}\right).

Consequently,

\Phi
maps each set in the canonical

S^{\prime}
-partition of

{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}
to itself.
Now let

\widetilde{S}\in H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})
.
Then,
for every

\bigstar\in\{\diamondsuit,\|,\curlyvee_{1},\ldots,\curlyvee_{p}\}
,
we have

\displaystyle\Phi\left[\widetilde{S}\right]_{\bigstar}\left({\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\widehat{C}\in{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}:\Phi\left[\widetilde{S}\right]\bigstar\widehat{C}\right\}=\left\{\Phi\left[\widetilde{C}\right]\in{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}:\Phi\left[\widetilde{S}\right]\bigstar\Phi\left[\widetilde{C}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\Phi\left[\widetilde{C}\right]\in{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}:\widetilde{S}\bigstar\widetilde{C}\right\}=\Phi\left[\left\{\widetilde{C}\in{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}:\widetilde{S}\bigstar\widetilde{C}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\left[\widetilde{S}_{\bigstar}\left({\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}\right)\right]=\Phi\left[S^{\prime}_{\bigstar}\left({\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}\right)\right]=S^{\prime}_{\bigstar}\left({\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}\right),

that is,

\Phi\left[\widetilde{S}\right]\in H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})
.
Hence

\Phi\left[H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})\right]=H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})

and
H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})
is a block of
the action of

G^{*}

on
\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S
.

Lemma 5.10

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let

D,E\in{\cal D}
, let
(S,B)
be a
primitive pair for

\Lambda^{*}(D)
,
and
let

C
be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(E)

such that
the
C
-canonical partition of

B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]

has more than one nonempty set.
Then, for any
\Phi,\Psi\in G^{*}
,
we have that

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}

and

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot B}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot B}

implies that

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}
.

Proof.

For any
S^{\prime}\in B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]
,
we define
K_{C}(S^{\prime}):=H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})\cap B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]
.
By Lemma
5.9
,

H_{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}(S^{\prime})

is a block of the action of
G^{*}

on
\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S
.
Because the intersection of two blocks is again a block, we
have that

K_{C}(S^{\prime})
is a block of the
action of
G^{*}

on

B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]
, too.

Let
S^{\prime}\in B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]
and
suppose, for a contradiction, that

K_{C}(S^{\prime})

has more than one element.
Because

K_{C}(S^{\prime})
is a block and
(S,B)
is a primitive pair, this means that

K_{C}(S^{\prime})=B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]
.
That is, for any two

\widehat{S},\widetilde{S}\in B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]

the
canonical

\widehat{S}
-partition of

\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C
equals the
canonical

\widetilde{S}
-partition of

\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C
.
However, then
the
C
-canonical partition of

B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]

has exactly one nonempty set, a contradiction.
Thus,
for every
S^{\prime}\in B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]
,
we have that
K_{C}(S^{\prime})

has exactly one element.

Now let

\Phi,\Psi\in G^{*}
,
such that

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}

and

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot B}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot B}
.
Because
\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot B}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot B}
,
we obtain

\Psi^{-1}\Phi[B]=B
.
Because

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}
,
we obtain
that

\left(\Psi^{-1}\Phi\right)\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}

is the identity on
\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C
.
Hence,
for any
S^{\prime}\in B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]
, we have

K_{C}\left(\Psi^{-1}\Phi[S^{\prime}]\right)=K_{C}(S^{\prime})
, which implies

\Psi^{-1}\Phi[S^{\prime}]=S^{\prime}
and then
\Phi[S^{\prime}]=\Psi[S^{\prime}]
.

We have thus proved that the hypotheses imply

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B}
.
Let
B^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot B
.
Because
\Lambda^{*}(D)
is transitive, there is
a
\Delta\in G^{*}
such that

\Delta[B]=B^{\prime}
,
and hence

B^{\prime}
fulfills the hypotheses with
C^{\prime}:=\Delta[C]
.
Via the same argument, we obtain that

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B^{\prime}}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}_{B^{\prime}}
.
Because

B^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot B
was arbitrary, we conclude

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}=\Psi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S}
.

In light of Lemma
5.10
,
we define the following.

Definition 5.11

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let

D,E\in{\cal D}
, let
(S,B)
be a
primitive pair for

\Lambda^{*}(D)
,
and
let

C
be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(E)
.
Then we define

C\blacktriangleright^{B}S
and say that

C
(together with
B
)

controls

S
iff
the
C
-canonical partition of

B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]

has more than one nonempty set.

It turns out that
control will be exerted from
a larger block, too, or
control will be mutual,
as the
following lemma shows.

Lemma 5.12

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let

D,E\in{\cal D}
, let
(S,B)
be a
primitive pair for

\Lambda^{*}(D)
,
let

C
be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(E)

such that

C\blacktriangleright^{B}S
,
and
let

T
be a block of

\Lambda^{*}(E)

such that

(C,T)
is a primitive pair.
Then

T\blacktriangleright^{B}S

or

S\blacktriangleright^{T}C
.

Proof.

Consider the case that

S\not\blacktriangleright^{T}C
.
Then the
S
-canonical partition of

T[\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C]

has exactly one nonempty set.
Let

\bigstar\in\{\diamondsuit,\|,\curlyvee_{1},\ldots,\curlyvee_{p}\}

such that, for all

C^{\prime}\in T[\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C]
,
we have
S\bigstar C^{\prime}
.
Because

C\blacktriangleright^{B}S
,
there are
\widehat{S}\in B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]

and

\blacktriangledown\in\{\diamondsuit,\|,\curlyvee_{1},\ldots,\curlyvee_{p}\}\setminus\{\bigstar\}

such that

\widehat{S}\blacktriangledown C
.
We conclude that

(S,T)\not\bowtie\left(\widehat{S},T\right)
,
which means

T\blacktriangleright^{B}S
.

Lemma
5.12
shows that control can be mutual,
and this will be an important notion in the future.
Hence, we define the following.

Definition 5.13

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let

D,E\in{\cal D}
, let
(S,B)
be a
primitive pair for

\Lambda^{*}(D)
,
and
let

(C,T)
be a primitive pair for

\Lambda^{*}(E)
.
We
say that

(S,T)
and
(C,T)

,
and we write

(S,T)\matrix{\blacktriangleright\vskip-7.22743pt\cr\blacktriangleleft\cr}(C,T)
,
iff

C\blacktriangleright^{B}S

and
S\blacktriangleright^{T}C
.

As Definition
4.2
indicates,
we will usually work with a
fixed primitive nesting collection
{\cal B}
.
When we have
a fixed primitive nesting collection
{\cal B}
,
the natural

to investigate

.
Moreover, although

may not control

\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C
does.

Definition 5.14

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B})
be a
nested standard configuration

.
We will write

.

of

such that
C^{\prime}\blacktriangleright S
.
We define

C\matrix{\vartriangleright\vskip-7.22743pt\cr\vartriangleleft\cr}S

iff
there is a conjugate
C^{\prime}
of

C^{\prime}\matrix{\blacktriangleright\vskip-7.22743pt\cr\blacktriangleleft\cr}S
.

For blocks at depth zero, a controlling block always exists.
We also provide an insight into the mutual control of

.

Lemma 5.15

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B})
be a
nested standard configuration.
Then, for every
B_{0}^{D}\in{\cal B}
, there is
a
B_{0}^{E}\in{\cal B}
such that

B_{0}^{E}\vartriangleright B_{0}^{D}
.

Proof.

Because
B_{1}^{D}
cannot be an order-autonomous antichain,
there are
x,y\in B_{1}^{D}

and a

such that

z\sim x
and
z\not\sim y
.
Let
E\in{\cal D}
be the orbit that
contains

. Then

B_{0}^{E}\vartriangleright B_{0}^{D}
.

Lemma 5.16

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*};{\cal B})
be a nested standard configuration,
let

C,S\in G^{*}\cdot{\cal B}
be depth
0
blocks
such that

\left(\left|C^{\dagger}\right|{\rm,\ respectively}\right)

\left(C^{\dagger}{\rm,\ respectively}\right)

Proof.

Let

The max-lock relation

Definition 7.1

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let
D,E\in{\cal D}
,
let
w\geq 5
,
let

(S,B)
be a
primitive

w

for

\Lambda^{*}(D)
,
and let
(C,T)
be a primitive pair of width
w

for

\Lambda^{*}(E)

We say that

(C,T)

and

(S,B)

are

and write

(S,B)\blacksquare(C,T)
iff

T[\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C]=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{w}\}
,

B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{w}\}

and
there are

\bigstar,\blacktriangledown\in\{\diamondsuit,\|,\curlyvee_{1},\ldots,\curlyvee_{p}\}

such that, for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,w\}
, we have

S_{j}\bigstar C_{j}
, and,
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,w\}\setminus\{j\}
,
we have

S_{i}\blacktriangledown C_{j}
.
Moreover, we define

\Theta_{B,T}:B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]\to T[\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C]

by

\Theta_{B,T}(S_{j}):=C_{j}

and
we define

\Theta_{T,B}:T[\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C]\to B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]

by
\Theta_{T,B}(C_{j}):=S_{j}
.

Lemma 7.2

Let
(U,{\cal D},G^{*})
be a standard configuration,
let
w\geq 5
,
let
D,E\in{\cal D}
,
let
(C,T)
be a primitive pair
of width
w

for

\Lambda^{*}(E)
,
and
let

(S,B)
be a
primitive pair
of width
w

for

\Lambda^{*}(D)

such that

(S,B)\blacksquare(C,T)
.
Then,

\Phi\in G^{*}

\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(E)\cdot C}\circ\Theta_{B,T}=\left.\Theta_{\Phi[B],\Phi[T]}\circ\Phi\!\Rsh^{\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot\Phi[S]}\right|_{B[\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot S]}
.

Proof.

First note that,
because
\Phi\in G^{*}
is an automorphism, for any

\blacktriangle\in\{\bigstar,\blacktriangledown\}
and any

\Phi[S_{i}]\blacktriangle\Phi[C_{j}]

.
Hence the “image block sets”

and
\Phi[B][\Lambda^{*}(D)\cdot\Phi[S]]

and, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,w\}
, we have

\Theta_{\Phi[B],\Phi[T]}(\Phi[S_{i}])=\Phi[C_{i}]=\Phi[\Theta_{B,T}(S_{i})]
, which
establishes the equation.

\ell<k
we have
B^{\ell}<B^{k}
, or,
for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,w\}
,

x_{j}^{\ell}
is
the unique element of

B^{\ell}
that is below

x_{j}^{k}

or it is
the unique element of

B^{\ell}
that is not below

x_{j}^{k}
.
In particular, we
obtain that
{\cal P}
is synchronized.

Theorem 10.3

The Automorphism Conjecture is true for
the class
{\cal W}_{9}
of ordered sets of width at most
9
.

Proof.

Let
P
be an ordered set of width at most
9

with
n
elements and no nontrivial order-autonomous
antichains.
Let

\left(P,{\cal D},{\rm Aut}(P);{\cal B},{\cal F}\right)

be a nested
standard configuration.

By Lemma
10.2
,
every component

{\cal P}

of

({\cal F},\blacksquare)

is thin and synchronized.
Therefore, if
{\cal P}

is undersized, then

U({\cal P})\leq\lfloor\lg(9)\rfloor=3
.
Moreover, for every
w\in\{5,6,7,8,9\}
, we have that

w!<2^{0.99\cdot 3w}
.
Therefore,

|U({\cal P}|=3
implies

\prod_{S\in{\cal B}\cap G^{*}\cdot L({\cal P})}\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal B}\not\searrow S\right)\right|\leq 2^{0.99|L({\cal P})|}
,
and hence, in this case,
we can
incorporate factors

\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal B}\not\searrow S\right)\right|

from
S\in L({\cal P})
into the exponential
in
Theorem
9.1
.
Consequently,
because a component
{\cal P}

of

({\cal F},\blacksquare)

with
|U({\cal P})|=1
would be a nontrivial order-autonomous antichain,

\prod_{D\in{\cal B}}\Upsilon_{{\cal B}}(D)

is reduced to factors

\left|\Lambda^{*}\left(\overleftarrow{\cal B}\not\searrow S\right)\right|

from components
{\cal Q}

of

({\cal F},\blacksquare)

such that
|U({\cal Q})|=2
.
Let
f_{P}
be the number of elements
x\in P

that are contained in
the corresponding sets
L({\cal Q})
.

If

f_{P}\leq\lg(n)
,
by Theorem
9.1
,
we obtain

|{\rm Aut}(P)|\leq 2^{0.99|P|}f_{P}!\leq 2^{0.99n}\lg(n)!\leq 2^{0.99n}\lg(n)^{\lg(n)}=2^{0.99n+\lg(n)\lg(\lg(n))}
.

Otherwise, let

{\cal V}
be the set of components

{\cal Q}

of

({\cal F},\blacksquare)

that are thin, synchronized, and satisfy

|U({\cal Q})|=2
,
and let

V:=|{\cal V}|\geq{\lg(w)\over 18}.

For each
{\cal Q}\in{\cal V}
,
let
w_{\cal Q}
be the width of
the underlying set

Q:=\bigcup L({\cal Q})
, and note that

Q
is either the
disjoint union of
w_{\cal Q}
chains with two elements each,
or
it is a set

\left\{x_{1}^{0},\ldots,x_{w_{\cal Q}}^{0}\right\}\cup\left\{x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{w_{\cal Q}}^{1}\right\}

with
x_{j}^{0}<x_{i}^{1}
iff
j\not=i
and no
further comparabilities.
In either case, it is easy to see that
there are at least
\left(w_{\cal Q}-1\right)^{w_{\cal Q}}

endomorphisms of

Q
that map maximal elements to maximal elements and minimal elements to
minimal elements.

Similar to the proof of Proposition
2.1
,
the automorphisms of
P
can be split into
the cosets of the normal subgroup
M
that, for every

{\cal Q}\in{\cal V}

maps each block in
U({\cal Q})
to itself.
Every
[\Psi]\in{\rm Aut}(P)/M
contains exactly

\prod_{Q\in{\cal V}}w_{\cal Q}!

automorphisms.
By Lemma
10.2
,
for every
{\cal Q}\in{\cal V}
,
no
B\in U({\cal Q})
is nontrivially woven with
an orbit
D\not\in U({\cal Q})
.
Hence,
again similar to the proof of Proposition
2.1
,
for every
[\Psi]\in{\rm Aut}(P)/M
,
there are at least

\prod_{Q\in{\cal V}}\left(w_{\cal Q}-1\right)^{w_{\cal Q}}

corresponding endomorphisms.
Thus

{|{\rm Aut}(P)|\over|{\rm End}(P)|}\leq{|{\rm Aut}(P)/M|\prod_{Q\in{\cal V}}w_{\cal Q}!\over|{\rm Aut}(P)/M|\prod_{Q\in{\cal V}}\left(w_{\cal Q}-1\right)^{w_{\cal Q}}}=\prod_{Q\in{\cal V}}{w_{\cal Q}!\over\left(w_{\cal Q}-1\right)^{w_{\cal Q}}}\leq\left({5!\over 4^{5}}\right)^{|{\cal V}|}\leq 0.12^{\lg(n)\over 18}
.

Therefore the
Automorphism Conjecture is true for
the class of ordered sets of width at most
9

that do not contain nontrivial order-autonomous antichains.
The claim now follows from
Proposition
2.1
.

Example
5.17

and

In this case,

through non-controlling weavings with other

Example 10.5

Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1,2\}
and consider the
ordered set

U=\bigcup{\cal D}
with
{\cal D}=\{D_{1},D_{2},D_{3},D_{4}\}
,

D_{i}=\{x_{1}^{i},\ldots,x_{M}^{i}\}
such that

x_{j}^{1}<x_{k}^{2}
iff

j\not=k
,

x_{j}^{3}<x_{k}^{2}
iff

j=k
,

x_{j}^{3}<x_{k}^{4}
iff

j=k
,

x_{j}^{1}<x_{k}^{4}
iff

j=k
, and no further comparabilities.
See Figure
3
for a visualization.
Then every
f\in{\rm End}(U)

that maps every
D_{i}\in{\cal D}
to itself
is an automorphism.

Let

f\in{\rm End}(U)

map every
D_{i}\in{\cal D}
to itself.
Because we are free to work with a power of
f
,
without loss of generality, we can assume that

f^{2}=f

(see Proposition 4.4 in
[
14
]
).
Hence
f|_{f[U]}={\rm id}|_{f[U]}
.

Let
x_{j}^{1}\in f[D_{1}]
.
Then

f\left(x_{j}^{1}\right)=x_{j}^{1}
, which implies

f\left(x_{j}^{4}\right)=x_{j}^{4}\in D_{4}
, which implies

f\left(x_{j}^{3}\right)=x_{j}^{3}\in D_{3}
, which implies

f\left(x_{j}^{2}\right)=x_{j}^{2}\in D_{2}
.
Because
f\left(x_{j}^{2}\right)=x_{j}^{2}\in D_{2}
,
no element of

D_{1}\setminus\{x_{j}^{1}\}
is mapped to

x_{j}^{1}
.

Because
x_{j}^{1}\in f[D_{1}]
was arbitrary,
no element in
D_{1}\setminus f[D_{1}]
can be mapped to any
element of
f[D_{1}]
.
Hence
f[D_{1}]=D_{1}
and, subsequently, for
j=2,3,4
,

f[D_{j}]=D_{j}
, which means

f
must be an automorphism.

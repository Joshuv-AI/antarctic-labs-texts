Joint Schmidt-type decomposition for two bipartite pure quantum states
Christopher Eltschka 1 and Jens Siewert 2,3

Abstract
It is well known that the Schmidt decomposition exists for all pure states of a two-party quantum system. We demonstrate that there are two ways to obtain an analogous decomposition for arbitrary rank-1 operators acting on states of a bipartite finite-dimensional Hilbert space. These methods amount to joint Schmidt-type decompositions of two pure states where the two sets of coefficients and local bases depend on the properties of either state, however, at the expense of the local bases not all being orthonormal and in one case the complex-valuedness of the coefficients. With these results we derive several generally valid purity-type formulae for one-party reductions of rank-1 operators, and we point out relevant relations between the Schmidt decomposition and the Bloch representation of bipartite pure states.



I
Introduction

The Schmidt decomposition theorem states that any pure state of
a bipartite quantum system of finite dimension
can be written as the superposition of a minimum
number of states, where the coefficients are real and the superposed states
are tensor products of the elements of two preferred local orthonormal bases.
There are only few tools in quantum information theory comparable in
the power of the method and the ubiquity of their applicability with the
Schmidt decomposition.
While Schmidt’s original work
Schmidt1906
investigates kernels of
integral equations,
the decomposition for finite-dimensional systems—as it is mostly applied
in quantum physics nowadays—was given by
H. Everett, III
Everett1957
;
Ekert1995
.

There are several routes toward generalization of the method.
There is a mixed-state
analog
Zyczkowski2006
;
MMWolf
,
which is not as frequently used as the pure-state
decomposition
footnote0
, but has
important applications, e.g., in decomposition of quantum
gates and entanglement theory
Nielsen2003
;
Cariello2014
.
Further, it would be desirable to have a similar method for
multipartite states, e.g.,
Tucker1966
;
Carteret2000
;
deMoor2000
;
Alber2014
.
While for three qubits this question has lead to
important results
Acin2000
,
to date there is no generally accepted
counterpart for the Schmidt decomposition in multipartite systems.

A third option is to ask whether there exists a simultaneous
Schmidt-type decomposition for several pure bipartite states.
In Ref.
Hiroshima2004
, the conditions for simultaneous
applicability of a (slightly generalized) standard Schmidt decomposition
were studied;
mixtures of such jointly Schmidt-decomposable states
(that is, Schmidt-decomposable
in one and the same pair of local bases) were then called
maximally correlated.
However, beyond this restrictive concept nothing
seems to be known regarding simultaneous Schmidt decomposability of two or
more bipartite states.
The reason for this is that in general the reduced states have
nonvanishing overlap and therefore it is not obvious which basis one has to
choose in the subspace of their joint support.
Our present work fills this gap by
analyzing the question of how two pairs of correlated
local bases can be found that allow
for Schmidt-like decompositions of two arbitrary finite-dimensional
bipartite states. We show that in general there are two options for
such decomposability, and that those generalized Schmidt bases
differ from the standard—“single-state”—Schmidt bases whenever
the reduced states have non-vanishing overlap. As a direct consequence of
these results we derive several interesting relations
for the reductions of rank-1 operators. As the Schmidt decomposition,
the existence of relations for one-party reductions, and the Bloch
representation
Fano1957
;
Mahler1995
;
Mahler2004
;
Badziag2008
;
Kloeckl2015
;
Tran2015
;
Huber2017
;
ES2018

of bipartite quantum states are intimately related
concepts
Kloeckl2015
;
Huber2017
;
Appel2017
,
we conclude our discussion by analyzing the most salient of these
mathematical connections.

II
The usual Schmidt decomposition

We start with a brief reminder of how the Schmidt decomposition is
obtained following Preskill
Preskill1998
. Consider the
generic normalized state
\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle

of a bipartite Hilbert space,
\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
,

\langle\psi_{AB}|\psi_{AB}\rangle=1
.
We write it
with respect to some orthonormal product bases as

\displaystyle\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}a_{jk}\left|{j}\right\rangle_{A}\otimes\left|{k}\right\rangle_{B}\equiv\sum_{j}\left|{j}\right\rangle_{A}\otimes\left|{\tilde{j}}\right\rangle_{B}\ \ ,

(1)

where the states
\left|{\tilde{j}}\right\rangle_{B}
in general are neither normalized nor
orthogonal. If we choose, however, for
\{\left|{j}\right\rangle_{A}\}
the basis in which the
reduced state

\rho_{A}\ =\ \Tr_{B}\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\psi_{AB}}\right|\ =\ \sum_{jk}\langle\tilde{k}|\tilde{j}\rangle\left|{j}\right\rangle_{A}\!\left\langle{k}\right|

is diagonal, the states
\{\left|{\tilde{j}}\right\rangle_{B}\}
do become orthogonal,
and by introducing
\left|{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle_{B}=\lambda_{j}^{-1/2}\left|{\tilde{j}}\right\rangle_{B}

for
\lambda_{j}=\langle\tilde{j}|\tilde{j}\rangle>0
we get the
Schmidt decomposition of
\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle\ =\ \sum_{j,k=1}^{d}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\left|{j}\right\rangle_{A}\otimes\left|{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle_{B}\ \ ,\ \ \sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\ =\ 1\ \ .

(2)

It is an immediate consequence that with this choice of local bases
also
\rho_{B}=\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\psi_{AB}}\right|
is diagonal and has the
same set of nonzero eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}\}
as
\rho_{A}
, so that one finds
for the purities of the local states the well-known relation

\displaystyle\Tr\rho_{A}^{2}\ =\ \Tr\rho_{B}^{2}\ =\ \sum_{j}\ \lambda_{j}^{2}\ \ .

(3)

III
Decomposition of rank-1 operators

III.1
Decomposition based on singular value decomposition

The Schmidt decomposition Eq. (
2
) for a projector

\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\psi_{AB}}\right|
reads

\displaystyle\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\psi_{AB}}\right|\ =\ \sum_{jk}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}}\left|{a_{j}}\right\rangle_{A}\!\left\langle{a_{k}}\right|\otimes\left|{b_{j}}\right\rangle_{B}\!\left\langle{b_{k}}\right|

(4)

with the Schmidt basis
\{\left|{a_{j}}\right\rangle_{A}\otimes\left|{b_{k}}\right\rangle_{B}\}
. This way of writing
the decomposition imposes the following question: What happens if, in the
expression
\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\psi_{AB}}\right|
,
we do not choose both sides equal,
that is, is there an analogous expansion for
the—non-Hermitian—rank-1 operator

\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi_{AB}}\right|
with
\left|{\phi_{AB}}\right\rangle\neq\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle
?
An obvious idea would be to resort to the operator-level Schmidt
decompositon, however, this way one simply retrieves the usual Schmidt
decompsition of the individual states (cf. Ref.
footnote0
).

In order to find a new answer we assume that

\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle,\left|{\phi_{AB}}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
are
normalized and make an ansatz similar to what we had
in Eq. (
1
)

\displaystyle\left|{\psi_{AB}}\right\rangle\ =\

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left|{u_{j}}\right\rangle_{A}|\tilde{j}^{\psi}\rangle_{B}

(5a)

\displaystyle\left|{\phi_{AB}}\right\rangle\ =\

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\left|{v_{k}}\right\rangle_{A}|\tilde{k}^{\phi}\rangle_{B}\ \ ,

(5b)

where
\{\left|{u_{j}}\right\rangle_{A}\}
,
\{\left|{v_{k}}\right\rangle_{A}\}
are orthonormal bases
(from now on we will drop the tensor product signs and the lower
indices for the partitions
A
and
B
).
If we choose the bases

\{\left|{u_{j}}\right\rangle\}
,
\{\left|{v_{k}}\right\rangle\}
such that they belong to the singular
value decomposition (SVD) of
\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
with singular
values
q_{j}\geqq 0
, the marginal operator for party
A
reads

\displaystyle\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|=\sum_{jk}\left|{u_{j}}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{v_{k}}\right|\langle\tilde{k}^{\phi}|\tilde{j}^{\psi}\rangle=\sum_{j}q_{j}\left|{u_{j}}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{v_{j}}\right|

(6)

with
\langle\tilde{k}^{\phi}|\tilde{j}^{\psi}\rangle=q_{j}\delta_{jk}
.
That is, we find that
\{\left|{\tilde{j}^{\psi}}\right\rangle\}

and
\{|\tilde{k}^{\phi}\rangle\}
are dual bases.
If we normalize as before

\left|{\mathsf{d}_{j}^{\psi}}\right\rangle\equiv\frac{\left|{\tilde{j}^{\psi}}\right\rangle}{\sqrt{\mu_{j}^{\psi}}}\ \ \ ,\ \ \ \left|{\mathsf{d}_{k}^{\phi}}\right\rangle\equiv\frac{|\tilde{k}^{\phi}\rangle}{\sqrt{\nu_{k}^{\phi}}}

we can finally write the following for the decomposition
of
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
and
\left|{\phi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\ =\

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\sqrt{\mu_{j}^{\psi}}\left|{u_{j}}\right\rangle|\mathsf{d}_{j}^{\psi}\rangle\ \ ,

(7a)

\displaystyle\left|{\phi}\right\rangle\ =\

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sqrt{\nu_{k}^{\phi}}\left|{v_{k}}\right\rangle|\mathsf{d}_{k}^{\phi}\rangle\ \ .

(7b)

One can term this the SVD-based simultaneous Schmidt-like decomposition
of
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
and
\left|{\phi}\right\rangle
.
We recognize the analogy of Eqs. (
7a
) and (
7b
)
with the usual
Schmidt decomposition Eq. (
2
). Note that, while the
generalized “Schmidt coefficients”
\mu^{\psi}_{j}
,
\nu^{\phi}_{k}
are still
real, only the bases
\{\left|{u_{j}}\right\rangle\}
,
\{\left|{v_{k}}\right\rangle\}
are orthonormal.
The normalized bases

\{|\mathsf{d}_{j}^{\psi}\rangle\}
,
\{|\mathsf{d}_{k}^{\phi}\rangle\}

are dual with

\sqrt{\mu_{j}^{\psi}\nu_{k}^{\phi}}\langle\mathsf{d}^{\psi}_{j}|\mathsf{d}^{\phi}_{k}\rangle=q_{j}\delta_{jk}
,
but not orthogonal. It is worthwhile noting that in general
the orthonormal bases
\{\left|{u_{j}}\right\rangle\}
,
\{\left|{v_{k}}\right\rangle\}
bear no special relation
with one another. Again, Eqs. (
7
) represent superpositions
with the minimum number of components, which equals the rank of the
reduced operator
\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
.
As the “Schmidt vectors” in Eqs. (
7
) are still orthogonal
we have
\sum_{j}\mu_{j}^{\psi}=\sum_{k}\nu_{k}^{\phi}=1
.
Moreover, there is the condition

\sum_{j}q_{j}\langle v_{j}|u_{j}\rangle=\langle\phi|\psi\rangle
.
Clearly, one finds an analogous decomposition with exchanged roles
of parties
A
and
B
by considering the reduced operator

\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
and modifying Eqs. (
5
)
correspondingly.

The decompositions Eqs. (
7
) may be viewed as the result
of selecting preferred local bases for
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
and
\left|{\phi}\right\rangle
depending
on the overlap of these bipartite states on party
B
only.
This dependence does not exist if
\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|=0
. We will discuss this case below.

III.2
Decomposition based on diagonalization

Interestingly, Eqs. (
7
) is not the only way to obtain
a joint Schmidt-like decomposition of two pure states.
To see this, we start with an ansatz similar to Eqs. (
5
)
where we drop the assumption that the bases on party
A
be orthonormal

\displaystyle\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\ =\

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left|{x_{j}}\right\rangle\left|{\tilde{j}^{\psi}}\right\rangle\ \ ,

(8a)

\displaystyle\left|{\phi}\right\rangle\ =\

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\left|{y_{k}}\right\rangle|\tilde{k}^{\phi}\rangle\ \ .

(8b)

The trick that led to the decomposition was to find a diagonal form of the
reduced operator
\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
, which was achieved
via the singular value decomposition. There is an alternative
to this approach, namely to diagonalize
\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
.
Note that diagonalizability of non-Hermitian matrices is not guaranteed.
A sufficient condition is that
\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
has
the maximum number of nonzero eigenvalues, which all have
to be different
HornJohnson
.

Hence we assume that a matrix representation
\mathsf{M}
of

\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
is similar
to a diagonal matrix
\mathsf{D}

\mathsf{M}\ =\ \mathsf{S}\cdot\mathsf{D}\cdot\mathsf{S}^{-1}\ \ ,

where
\mathsf{S}
is an invertible matrix. The columns of
\mathsf{S}

are the right eigenvectors of
\mathsf{M}
, whereas the
rows of
\mathsf{S}^{-1}
are the left eigenvectors. Correspondingly we
can write the reduced operator

\displaystyle\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|\ =\ \sum_{j}\Delta_{j}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{j}}\left|{\mathsf{s}_{j}}\right\rangle\!\langle\mathsf{s}^{-1}_{j}|\ \ ,

(9)

where
\{\left|{\mathsf{s}_{j}}\right\rangle\}
,
|\mathsf{s}^{-1}_{k}\rangle\}
are
(non-orthogonal) dual bases of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
, i.e.,

\langle\mathsf{s}_{j}|\mathsf{s}^{-1}_{k}\rangle=\delta_{jk}
. Note
that
\left(\langle\mathsf{s}^{-1}_{j}|\right)^{\dagger}\neq\left|{\mathsf{s}_{j}}\right\rangle
.
Moreover, we have explicitly written the phases of the eigenvalues
implying that
\Delta_{j}\geqq 0
.

In analogy with the discussion following Eq. (
6
)
we then find that
\left|{\tilde{j}^{\psi}}\right\rangle
,

|\tilde{k}^{\phi}\rangle
are proportional to the vectors of two
dual bases
\{\left|{\mathsf{t}_{j}}\right\rangle\}
,
\{|\mathsf{t}_{k}^{-1}\rangle\}
with

\langle\mathsf{t}_{j}|\mathsf{t}^{-1}_{k}\rangle=\delta_{jk}
,
so that we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\ =\

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\sqrt{\xi_{j}}\left|{\mathsf{s}_{j}}\right\rangle\left|{\mathsf{t}_{j}}\right\rangle

(10a)

\displaystyle\left|{\phi}\right\rangle\ =\

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sqrt{\eta_{k}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi_{k}}\left|{\mathsf{s}^{-1}_{k}}\right\rangle\left|{\mathsf{t}^{-1}_{k}}\right\rangle

(10b)

with
\xi_{j},\eta_{k}\geqq 0
and
\sqrt{\xi_{j}\eta_{j}}=\Delta_{j}
.
Equations (
10
) represent the
second simultaneous Schmidt-like decomposition of
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle

and
\left|{\phi}\right\rangle
, which now is diagonalization-based.
Here, the “Schmidt coefficients” have complex phases, but
there is a freedom to distribute each of the phases at will
among the two states. Interestingly, both reduced operators

\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
and
\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
have
the same nonzero (now complex)
eigenvalues
\{\Delta_{j}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}\}
,
analogously to the usual Schmidt decomposition of a single state.
Since the “Schmidt vectors” are not
orthogonal, there is no normalization condition for the
\xi_{j},\eta_{k}
.
In contrast to the decomposition Eqs. (
7
), both of the local
bases of
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
are strongly related with the corresponding
basis in
\left|{\phi}\right\rangle
, however, none of them are necessarily orthogonal
or normalized. We stress again that the decomposition Eqs. (
10
)
may not exist, while the decomposition in Eqs. (
7
)
can always be found,
hence the latter is the stronger statement.

III.3
Remarks and special cases

The obvious special case for the decompositions in Eqs. (
7
) and
(
10
) is equality
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle=\left|{\phi}\right\rangle
.
Here we get back the
known result Eq. (
2
) because the
dual bases become self-dual and therefore also orthonormal. For Hermitian
matrices the singular value decomposition coincides with diagonalization,
this ensures the usual Schmidt decomposition also for the first generalization
option.

One might expect that also orthogonality

\langle\phi|\psi\rangle=0

represents a special case, but as long as the reduced operators

\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
,
\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
do not
vanish, the resulting bases do not display special properties.

However, there is a case related to orthogonality

\langle\phi|\psi\rangle=0
that needs to be discussed:
the possibility that
\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|=0
or/and

\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|=0
. Any of these conditions imply
global orthogonality, because, e.g.,

\langle\phi|\psi\rangle=\Tr\big(\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|\big)=0
.
With the latter condition, e.g., our approach
to derive Eqs. (
7
) or Eqs. (
10
), respectively,
does not lead to the selection of preferred bases on party
A
.
For
\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|=0

the states
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
and
\left|{\phi}\right\rangle
have disjoint support
in
\mathcal{H}_{B}
,
implying also that none of them has full (usual) Schmidt rank.
Another consequence is orthogonality of the local states

\Tr\big[\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\psi}\right|\Tr_{A}\left|{\phi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|\big]=0
.

In the case of disjoint support on
\mathcal{H}_{B}
one can try to check

\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
; if it is nonzero
the simultaneous Schmidt decomposition can be found as shown before, that is,
with dual bases in
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and singular value decomposition
(or diagonalization) in
\mathcal{H}_{B}
.
If, however, also
\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|=\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|=0
,
the states have disjoint support on the entire composite Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
, and it is
not possible (but also not necessary) to select preferred local bases
whose properties depend on both
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
and
\left|{\phi}\right\rangle
. It suffices then
to diagonalize the local states of
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
and
\left|{\phi}\right\rangle
separately and
to use their standard Schmidt decomposition.

IV
Generalized purity relations

As an immediate application of the decomposition
Eqs. (
7
), (
10
) we derive several formulae
that may be regarded as the generalizations of the
purity relation Eq. (
3
).

Consider first the squares of the reduced rank-1 operators.
By using Eq. (
6
)
we find
\Tr\big(\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|\big)^{2}=\sum_{jk}q_{j}q_{k}\langle v_{j}|u_{k}\rangle\langle v_{k}|u_{j}\rangle
.
On the other hand,
\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|=\sum_{jk}(\mu^{\psi}_{j}\nu^{\phi}_{k})^{1/2}\langle v_{k}|u_{j}\rangle|\mathsf{d}_{j}^{\psi}\rangle\!\langle\mathsf{d}_{k}^{\phi}|
.
Because of

(\mu^{\psi}_{j}\nu^{\phi}_{k})^{1/2}\langle\mathsf{d}_{k}^{\phi}|\mathsf{d}_{j}^{\psi}\rangle=q_{j}\delta_{jk}
it follows that

\displaystyle\Tr\big(\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|\big)^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{jklm}\sqrt{\mu^{\psi}_{j}\nu^{\phi}_{k}}\sqrt{\mu^{\psi}_{l}\nu^{\phi}_{m}}\ \ \times

\displaystyle\times\ \langle v_{k}|u_{j}\rangle\langle v_{m}|u_{l}\rangle\langle\mathsf{d}_{k}^{\phi}|\mathsf{d}_{l}^{\psi}\rangle\langle\mathsf{d}_{m}^{\phi}|\mathsf{d}_{j}^{\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{jk}q_{j}q_{k}\langle v_{j}|u_{k}\rangle\langle v_{k}|u_{j}\rangle\ \ ,

so that we conclude

\displaystyle\Tr\big(\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|\big)^{2}\ =\ \Tr\big(\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|\big)^{2}\ \ .

(11)

If the reduced operators are diagonalizable Eq. (
11
)
follows practically without calculation, because
\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
and
\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
have the
same (in general complex) eigenvalues.

Alternatively, one can read Eq. (
3
) as Hilbert-Schmidt
scalar products. Then, we derive in a completely analogous manner
the second generalized purity relation

\displaystyle\Tr\bigg[\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|

\displaystyle\Tr_{B}\left|{\phi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\psi}\right|\bigg]\ =\ \sum_{j}q_{j}^{2}\ =

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\Tr\bigg[\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\psi}\right|\Tr_{A}\left|{\phi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|\bigg]\ .

(12)

We can go one step further and turn
Eqs. (
11
), (
12
) into a single equality
linking the reductions of four bipartite states
\psi,\phi,\chi,\zeta\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}

\displaystyle\Tr\bigg[\Tr_{A}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\chi}\right|\Tr_{A}\left|{\phi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\zeta}\right|\bigg]=\Tr\bigg[\Tr_{B}\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\zeta}\right|\Tr_{B}\left|{\phi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\chi}\right|\bigg]\ .

(13)

The relations Eq. (
11
)–(
13
)
constitute the second central result of our article.
They are directly connected with the Bloch representation
of quantum states; therefore they are highly useful
in calculations within that formalism, as will be demonstrated
in forthcoming work.
We will highlight some of the links to the Bloch representation
in the last part of our discussion.

V
Schmidt decomposition and Bloch representation

Our main point in generalizing the Schmidt decomposition was to
consider rank-1 operators such as
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
and

\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\psi}\right|
rather than state vectors like
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
.
Moreover, we have seen in the preceding sections that a discussion of
the Schmidt decomposition (both the usual and the joint version) is closely
related to analyzing the properties of the marginals. Thus one is led
to associate the entire discussion with yet another topic where operators
and their marginals are intrinsically tied together, namely the Bloch
representation of quantum states. This link is rarely emphasized,
therefore we use this section to work out some of its details.
We will restrict this analysis mainly to properties of the usual
(single-state) Schmidt decomposition, cf. Sec. II, however, some of
the results we derived earlier will turn out to be useful.
In our considerations we will assume Hilbert spaces of
equal dimension,
\dim\mathcal{H}_{A}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{B}=d
(this can always
be done by extending the Hilbert space of lower dimension), because it
makes the expressions more transparent.

The Bloch representation of bipartite states
is defined as follows (cf., e.g.,
Fano1957
;
Mahler1995
;
Mahler2004
;
Badziag2008
;
Kloeckl2015
;
Tran2015
;
Huber2017
;
ES2018
):
Given an orthonormal basis of trace-free Hermitian matrices
\{\mathsf{h}_{j}\}

(with normalization
\Tr\left(\mathsf{h}_{j}\mathsf{h}_{k}\right)=d\,\delta_{jk}
and

\mathsf{h}_{0}\equiv\mathds{1}
)
we can expand any density operator

\rho
acting on
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}

\displaystyle\rho\ =\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}}\bigg[\left(\Tr\rho\right)\mathds{1}\otimes\mathds{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{d^{2}-1}r_{j0}\mathsf{h}_{j}\otimes\mathds{1}+\bigg.

\displaystyle\bigg.+\sum_{k=1}^{d^{2}-1}r_{0k}\mathds{1}\otimes\mathsf{h}_{k}+\sum_{l,m=1}^{d^{2}-1}r_{lm}\mathsf{h}_{l}\otimes\mathsf{h}_{m}\bigg]\ \ .

(14)

With the simplifying choice of Hermitian matrices
\mathsf{h}_{j}

the coefficients
r_{j0}
,
r_{0k}
and
r_{lm}
are real.
We call the sum of terms with one summation index “1-sector”,
and the one with two indices “2-sector”. The first term
on the right-hand side of Eq. (
14
) can
be denoted the “0-sector”, accordingly.
The purity
condition for the state
\rho
translates into
the Bloch vector length as the sum
of sector lengths
Kloeckl2015
;
Tran2015
;
Huber2017
;
ES2018

\displaystyle d^{2}\Tr\rho^{2}=\left(\Tr\rho\right)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d^{2}-1}r_{j0}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{d^{2}-1}r_{0k}^{2}+\sum_{l,m=1}^{d^{2}-1}r_{lm}^{2}\ .

(15)

Each of the sums in Eq. (
15
) is
invariant under local unitary transformations
footnote1
.
The purity condition (
3
) for the reduced states
corresponds to the
equality of the normalized 1-sector lengths

\sum_{j}r_{j0}^{2}=\sum_{k}r_{0k}^{2}
.
Moreover, we note that for
normalized states
\sum_{j,k=0}^{d^{2}-1}r_{jk}^{2}\leqq d^{2}
.

Consider now the Bloch representation of the rank-1 operator

\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|
,

\displaystyle\left|{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle{\phi}\right|\ =\

VI
Conclusions

We have devised two ways to obtain a simultaneous
Schmidt-type decomposition of two arbitrary bipartite pure states in
finite dimensions, Eqs. (
7
) and Eqs. (
10
),
based on singular value decomposition
of the reduced rank-1 operator on the one hand,
and on diagonalization thereof, on the other.
The corresponding “Schmidt bases” depend on the overlap of the reduced
states in the local Hilbert spaces; consequently, if there is no overlap
one can simply use the standard Schmidt decomposition.
It is surprising that the simultaneous Schmidt decompositions
maintain the simplicity and many of the important properties of
the single-state Schmidt
decomposition, such as a minimum number of (real) coefficients that equals
the rank of the reduced operator.
As an immediate consequence from these decompositions we have derived
several interesting purity-type relations for the reductions of
bipartite pure states, Eq. (
11
)-(
13
).
Moreover we used these results to analyze the mathematical relations
between the Bloch representation and the Schmidt decomposition.
To this end, we introduced the diagonal and offdiagonal operator
parts
\mathsf{diag}
,
\mathsf{offdiag}

of a projector which make explicit the extreme properties of
the Schmidt bases regarding their contribution to the sector
lengths of the Bloch vector.

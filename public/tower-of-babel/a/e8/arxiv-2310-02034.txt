Solubilizers in profinite groups
Andrea Lucchini

Abstract
. The solubilizer of an element x x of a profinite group G G is the set of the elements y y of G G such that the subgroup of G G generated by x x and y y is prosoluble. We propose the following conjecture: the solubilizer of x x in G G has positive Haar measure if and only if g g centralizes ‘almost all’ the non-abelian chief factors of G G . We reduce the proof of this conjecture to another conjecture concerning finite almost simple groups: there exists a positive c c such that, for every finite simple group S S and every ( a , b ) ∈ ( Aut ⁡ ( S ) ∖ { 1 } ) × Aut ⁡ ( S ) (a,b)\in(\aut(S)\setminus\{1\})\times\aut(S) , the number of s s is S S such that ⟨ a , b ​ s ⟩ \langle a,bs\rangle is insoluble is at least c ​ | S | c|S| . Work in progress by Fulman, Garzoni and Guralnick is leading to prove the conjecture when S S is a simple group of Lie type. In this paper we prove the conjecture for alternating groups.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a profinite group. For every
x\in G
, we denote by

{\mathcal{S}}_{G}(x)
the ‘solubilizer’ of
x
in
G
, i.e. the subset of
G
consisting of elements
g\in G
with the property that
\langle x,g\rangle
is a prosoluble group. In general
{\mathcal{S}}_{G}(x)
is not a subgroup of
G
, but it
can be easily seen that
{\mathcal{S}}_{G}(x)
is a closed subset of
G.

Indeed if
\mathcal{N}
is the family of all open normal subgroups of
G,
then
{\mathcal{S}}_{G}(x)=\cap_{N\in\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{S}}_{G,N}(x),
where
{\mathcal{S}}_{G,N}(x)
is the open subset of
G
consisting of the elements
y
with the property that
\langle x,y\rangle N/N
is a soluble subgroup of
G/N.

Let
\mu
be the normalized
Haar measure on
G
. Then
\mu({\mathcal{S}}_{G}(x))
can be viewed as the probability that a randomly chosen element of
G
generates a prosoluble subgroup together with
x
.

In this paper we want to address the question whether there exists a characterization of the elements
g\in G
such that
\mu(\mathcal{S}_{G}(g))>0.
Before to propose our conjecture we need to introduce some notations. Let
\Sigma_{\rm{pos}}(G)
be the set of the elements
g\in G
such that
\mu(\mathcal{S}_{G}(g))>0.
Given a non-negative integer
t,
we say that
g
is a
t
-centralizer in
G
if, for every open normal subgroup
N
of
G
, every chief series of
G/N
contains at most
t
non-abelian factors that are not centralized by
g.
Let
\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G,t)
be the set of the
t
-centralizers in
G
and
\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G)=\cup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G,t).

It is not difficult to prove that
\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G)\subseteq\Sigma_{\rm{pos}}(G)
for every profinite group
G
(see
Lemma

20
). We conjecture that the two subsets
\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G)
and
\Sigma_{\rm{pos}}(G)
coincide.

Coniecture 1
.

\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G)=\Sigma_{\rm{pos}}(G)
for every profinite group
G.

Notice that if
g_{1}\in\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G,t_{1})
and
g_{2}\in\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G,t_{2})
, then
g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}\in\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G,t_{1}+t_{2})
. So
\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G)
is a subgroup of
G.

Hence
1
implies that
\Sigma_{\rm{pos}}(G)
is a subgroup of
G.
Before to discuss our conjecture, we need to introduce other two definitions.

Definition 2
.

Let
N
be a finite non-abelian characteristically simple group
and let
a,b\in\aut(N).
We identify
N
with
\inn(N)
and we denote by
{\rm{P_{ins}}}(N,a,b)
the probability that a randomly chosen element

is such that
\langle a,bn\rangle
insoluble.

Definition 3
.

Let
N
be a finite non-abelian characteristically simple group. Given a positive real number
\eta

is
\eta
-insoluble if
{\rm{P_{ins}}}(N,a,b)>\eta
for every

with

If
G
is a profinite group, then with the term ‘composition factor’ of
G
we mean a composition factor of
G/N
for some open normal subgroup

of
G
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a profinite group. If there exists
\eta>0
such that all the non-abelian composition factors of

are
\eta
-insoluble, then
\Gamma_{\rm{cent}}(G)=\Sigma_{\rm{pos}}(G).

We propose the following conjecture:

Coniecture 5
.

There exists a positive real number
\eta
such that all the finite non-abelian simple groups are
\eta
-insoluble.

By
4
, if
5
is true, then
1
is also true.

For a finite non-abelian simple group

and
a,b\in\aut(S)
with

let

be the probability that a randomly chosen element

is such that

. Clearly
{\rm{P_{ins}}}(S,a,b)\geq Q(S,a,b).
Fulman and Guralnick
[
5
]
have a paper in
preparation showing that there exists a universal positive constant

such
that

for every finite simple group

of Lie type
and for every pair
(a,b)\in S^{2}
with

. Moreover Fulman, Garzoni and Guralnick
[
6
]
have an article in preparation
extending the Fulman-Guralnick result to cosets of almost simple groups. This would imply that there exists
\eta>0
such that

\eta
-insoluble. By
[

, if

is cyclic, then

Thus there exists

such that every sporadic simple group is
\eta
-insoluble. In the case of the alternating group, the analogue of the result of Fulman, Garzoni and Guralnick does not hold. It follows from a result of Babai and Hayes

Revising the arguments used by Babai and Hayes we prove that if

However

when

and

Theorem 6
.

There exists a positive constant
\eta>0
such that
\alt(n)
is
\eta
-insoluble for every

\eta
-insoluble characteristically simple groups

Theorem 7
.

[

Let

For every

Proposition 8
.

Let

with

and

is
\eta
-insoluble, then

We identify
\aut(N)
with the wreath product
\aut(S)\wr\perm(n).
In this identification, any element of
\aut(N)
can be written in the form
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\rho,

a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\aut(S)
and
\rho\in\perm(n).
For

let

The map
\phi_{i}:X_{i}\to S

to

We want to prove that the set

Since

Let

First assume that there exists

\tau

So it is not restrictive to assume

3.
Alternating groups

The aim of this section is to prove that there exists a constant
c>0
such that
\alt(n)
is

-insoluble for every

The first part of the section, culminating with

when

Lemma 9
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and let
a
be a non-negative integer with
a<n
. Then

\sum_{x=0}^{a}\frac{(n-x-1)!}{(a-x)!}=\frac{n!}{a!(n-a)}.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
n
. Observe that, when
a=0
, the lemma is readily seen to be true, for every value of
n
. Suppose now that the result is true for
n
and
a
, we show that it is also true for
n+1
and
a
. Indeed if
a<n-1
, arguing inductively, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{x=0}^{a}\frac{(n+1-x-1)!}{(a-x)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{a!}+\sum_{x=1}^{a}\frac{(n-x)!}{(a-x)!}=\frac{n!}{a!}+\sum_{x=0}^{a-1}\frac{(n-x-1)!}{(a-1-x)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{a!}+\frac{n!}{(a-1)!(n-a+1)}=\frac{(n+1)!}{a!(n+1-a)}.

The only case that remains to be discussed is when
a=n-1
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{x=0}^{a}\frac{(n-x-1)!}{(a-x)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}1=n-1=\frac{n!}{a!(n-a)}.\qed

Lemma 10
.

Let
\Omega
be a set and let
A
be a subset of
\Omega
. Then

|\{\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)\mid\textrm{there exists }B\subseteq A\hbox{ with }B\neq\varnothing\hbox{ and }B^{\tau}=B\}|=|A|(|\Omega|-1)!.

Proof.

For symplicity, given a set
\Delta
and a subset
C
of
\Delta
, we let

\iota_{\Delta}(C)=\{\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta)\mid\textrm{there exists }B\subseteq C\hbox{ with }B\neq\varnothing\hbox{ and }B^{\tau}=B\}.

In particular, we need to show that
|\iota_{\Omega}(A)|=|A|(|\Omega|-1)!
.

We argue by induction on
|A|
. When
A=\varnothing
,
A
has no proper subset and
|A|=0
; thus the result holds true.

Suppose now that
|A|>0
and let
a\in A
. Clearly, we may partition the set
\iota_{\Omega}(A)
as

\iota_{\Omega}(A)=\iota_{\Omega}(A\setminus\{a\})\cup(\iota_{\Omega}(A)\setminus\iota_{\Omega}(A\setminus\{a\})).

By induction, we have
|\iota_{\Omega}(A\setminus\{a\})|=(|A|-1)(|\Omega|-1)!
. Let
\tau\in\iota_{\Omega}(A)\setminus\iota_{\Omega}(A\setminus\{a\})
. Since
\tau\in\iota_{\Omega}(A)
,
\tau
fixes setwise some non-empty subset of
A
; however, as
\tau\notin\iota_{\Omega}(A\setminus\{a\})
,
\tau
fixes setwise no non-empty subset of
A\setminus\{a\}
. This implies that the only non-empty
\tau
-invariant subset of
A
is the support of the cycle of
\tau
containg
a
. Let
X\cup\{a\}\subseteq\Omega
be the support of the cycle of
\tau
containing the point
a
, with
a\notin X
. Summing up,
\tau
fixes setwise
X\cup\{a\}
and
\tau
fixes setwise no non-empty subset of
A\setminus(X\cup\{a\})
. Let
\tau_{1}
be the permutation induced by
\tau
on
X\cup\{a\}
and let
\tau_{2}
be the permutation induced by
\tau
on
\Omega\setminus(X\cup\{a\})
. Therefore, we have
|X|!
choices for the cycle
\tau_{1}
.
Moreover, as

\tau_{2}\in\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega\setminus(X\cup\{a\}))\setminus\iota_{\Omega\setminus(X\cup\{a\})}(A\setminus(X\cup\{a\})),

if we apply our inductive hypothesis with
\Omega
replaced by
\Omega\setminus(X\cup\{a\})
and with
A
replaced by
A\setminus(X\cup\{a\})
, we deduce that we have

\displaystyle(|\Omega|-|X|-1)!

\displaystyle-|\iota_{\Omega\setminus(X\cup\{a\})}(A\setminus(X\cup\{a\}))|

\displaystyle=(|\Omega|-|X|-1)!-(|A|-|X|-1)(|\Omega|-|X|-2)!

\displaystyle=(|\Omega|-|A|)(|\Omega|-|X|-2)!

choices for the permutation
\tau_{2}
.

Summing up,

\displaystyle|\iota_{\Omega}(A)|

\displaystyle=(|A|-1)(|\Omega|-1)!+\sum_{X\subseteq A\setminus\{a\}}|X|!(|\Omega|-|A|)(|\Omega|-|X|-2)!

\displaystyle=(|A|-1)(|\Omega|-1)!+(|\Omega|-|A|)\sum_{x=0}^{|A|-1}{|A|-1\choose x}x!(|\Omega|-x-2)!

\displaystyle=(|A|-1)(|\Omega|-1)!+(|A|-1)!(|\Omega|-|A|)\sum_{x=0}^{|A|-1}\frac{(|\Omega|-x-2)!}{(|A|-x-1)!}.

From Lemma

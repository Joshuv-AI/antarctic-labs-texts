1.
Introduction

Given a global field
F
, let
F^{\operatorname{sep}}
be a choice of separable closure of
F
, and set
\operatorname{G}_{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(F^{\operatorname{sep}}/F)
. Although class-field theory provides us with a complete description of the maximal abelian quotient of
\operatorname{G}_{F}
, the structure of
\operatorname{G}_{F}
is shrouded in mystery. A systematic approach to study questions at the heart of number theory is to classify representations of
\operatorname{G}_{F}
. When
F
is a number field, such representations are known to arise from geometry, for instance, from abelian varieties and certain automorphic representations. On the other hand, when
F
is a global function field over a finite field, such representations are known to arise from Drinfeld modules and Drinfeld modular forms. In this article, the field
F
is taken to be rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, where
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite field with
q
-elements. This is the function field of the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, and is the function field analogue of the field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
. Set
A
to denote the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
.

The inverse Galois problem for
F
can be interpreted as follows: given a finite group
G
, does there exist a surjective homomorphism
\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{F}\rightarrow G
? A stronger variant of this question is to ask for the minimal number
n
, such that there exists a surjection
\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{F}\rightarrow G
that is unramified away from
n
primes. Abhyankar
[
Abh57
]
studied related questions for finite Galois covers of the projective line (over a finite field), with specified ramification. In this context, celebrated
Abhyankar’s conjecture
gives a criterion for when a finite Galois cover of the projective line exists ramified at a prescribed number of points. This conjecture was resolved by Raynaud
[
Ray94
]
and Harbater
[
Har94
]
.

In this article, I study a variant of this question, where instead of a finite group
G
, I consider the groups of the form
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})
, where
\mathcal{O}
is the completion of
A
at a non-zero prime ideal. Since such homorphisms arise as Galois representations from structures in function field arithmetic, this setting is of natural interest. More precisely, I consider the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Let
q
be a power of a prime
p
, and
F
be the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
. Given
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
and a non-zero prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
, does there exist a representation

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})

which is unramified away from
\{\mathfrak{p}\}
, and such that the image of
\rho
is a finite-index subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})
?

Over
\mathbb{Q}
, the analogue of the above question was considered by Greenberg
[
Gre16
]
. Let
p
be an odd
regular prime number
(i.e.,
p
does not divide the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})
) and
n
be a positive integer. Greenberg shows that if
p\geq 4\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1
, then there is a Galois representation

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
that is unramified at all primes
\ell\notin\{p,\infty\}
. Refinements and generalizations of Greenberg’s result has been obtained in various contexts, cf.
[
CR18
,
Kat20
,
Ray21
,
Tan23
,
Mal23
,
Mai23
,
Ray23
]
.

In this article, I obtain an affirmative answer to the Question
1.1
. Moreover, one has an explicit description of how the inertia group at
\mathfrak{p}
acts. Before stating the main result, I remind the reader that the degree of
\mathfrak{p}
is defined to be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-dimension of
A/\mathfrak{p}
, or equivalently, the degree of an irreducible monic polynomial that generates
\mathfrak{p}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a non-zero prime of
A
and
\mathcal{O}
the completion
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
with uniformizer
\varpi
. Let
r\geq 2
be an integer. There is a Galois representation

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O}),

such that

•

\rho
is unramified at all non-zero primes
\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{p}
of
A
.

•

If
\operatorname{deg}\mathfrak{p}=1
, then
\rho
is also constructed to be unramified at
\infty\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(1/T)
.

•

The image of
\rho
has finite index in
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})
.

•

The restriction of
\rho
to the inertia group
\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is of the form

\rho_{|\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi&\ast\\
0&\operatorname{Id}_{r-1}\\
\end{array}}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}\chi&\ast&\ast&\cdots&\ast&\ast\\
&1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
&&1&0&\cdots&0\\
&&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
&&&&1&0\\
&&&&&1&\\
\end{pmatrix},

where
\chi
is a totally ramified character. Thus there is an
(r-1)
-dimensional
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/F_{\mathfrak{p}})
-quotient on which
\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}
acts trivially.

The Galois representation constructed does also have the property of being geometric in a suitable sense. In fact, it arises from a Drinfeld module
\phi
of rank
r
over
F
. It is shown that the endomorphism ring of
\phi
is naturally isomorphic to
A
. The open image result is then deduced from a theorem of Pink and Rütsche
[
PR09
]
. This Drinfeld module has good reduction at all other primes
\mathfrak{l}
of
A
and has stable reduction at
\mathfrak{p}
. The Galois representation has very little ramification at
\mathfrak{p}
and is also unramified at
\infty
if the degree of
\mathfrak{p}
is
1
. These properties are quite remarkable. The argument showing that the Galois representation is unramified at
\infty
is delicate and does not readily generalize to higher degrees.

For

of

Denote by

is fraction field of

. Denote by

. Let
\bar{F}
be the separable closure of

, and
\operatorname{G}_{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}/F)

.

Let
K
be a field equipped with a homomorphism of rings
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow K
. Such a field is referred to as an
A
-field
. In practice,
K
will be
F
,

or the residue field

A
-field. The

\operatorname{char}_{A}(K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0

Let
K\{\tau\}
be the non-commutative
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra of twisted polynomials over
K
. Elements of
K\{\tau\}
are polynomials of the form

f(\tau)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}\tau^{i},

where
a_{i}\in K
. Addition is defined
term-wise
as follows

\begin{split}&\sum_{i}a_{i}\tau^{i}+\sum_{i}b_{i}\tau^{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i}(a_{i}+b_{i})\tau^{i},\\
&(a\tau^{i})(b\tau^{j})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=ab^{q^{i}}\tau^{i+j}.\end{split}

The above relations uniquely determine the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra structure on
K\{\tau\}
. Let
K[x]
be the polynomial ring over
K
. A polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
is
additive
if the following relation holds in
K[x,y]

f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y).

The polynomial
f
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear if it is additive and for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, one has that

f(\alpha x)=\alpha f(x).

Let
K\langle x\rangle
consist of polynomials in
K[x]
that are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear. This set of polynomials is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace, however it is not closed under multiplication. On the other hand, if
f,g\in K\langle x\rangle
, then, their composite
f\circ g
is also
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear. With respect to addition and composition
K\langle x\rangle
forms an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra.

Given a twisted polynomial
f(\tau)=\sum_{i}a_{i}\tau^{i}
, set
f(x)
to be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear polynomial defined by
f(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i}a_{i}x^{q^{i}}
. This defines a map

\Pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\{\tau\}\rightarrow K\langle x\rangle,

sending
f(\tau)
to
f(x)
, and
\Pi
is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras.

A twisted polynomial
f(\tau)
can be written as

f(\tau)=a_{h}\tau^{h}+a_{h+1}\tau^{h+1}+\dots+a_{d}\tau^{d},

where
a_{h},a_{d}\neq 0
. The numbers
h,d
satisfy
h\leq d
, and are the
height
and
degree
of
f(\tau)
respectively. I set

\operatorname{ht}_{\tau}(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=h\text{ and }\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=d.

Note that the degree of
f(x)
has degree
q^{\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}(f)}
as a polynomial in
x
. Let
\partial\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\{\tau\}\rightarrow K
be the
derivative map
sending

\sum_{n}a_{n}\tau\mapsto a_{0}.

Thus,
\operatorname{ht}(f)=0
if and only if
\partial(f)\neq 0
. Observe that
\partial(f)
is equal to the derivative
\frac{df}{dx}
(as a function of
x
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be an integer and

.

The second condition is equivalent to requiring that

Let

\bar{K}

3.
Galois representations ramified at one prime

In this section, I prove my main result. Fix an integer
r\geq 2
, a non-zero prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
, and set
\mathcal{O}
to denote the completion
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Let
f(T)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f_{\mathfrak{p}}(T)
be the monic irreducible polynomial that generates
\mathfrak{p}
, and let
\phi
be the Drinfeld module of rank
r
with coefficients in
F
defined by

\phi_{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T+\tau+f(T)\tau^{r},

and set
d\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{deg}_{T}(f(T))
.

3.1.
Good reduction away from
\{\mathfrak{p},\infty\}

In this brief subsection, I describe the reduction type of
\phi
at all primes
\mathfrak{l}\notin\{\mathfrak{p},\infty\}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The Drinfeld module
\phi
has good reduction at all primes
\mathfrak{l}\notin\{\mathfrak{p},\infty\}
.

Proof.

Let
v\in\Omega_{F}
be the valuation associated with
\mathfrak{l}
and
\phi_{v}
the completion of
\phi
at
v
. I find that
\phi_{v}
has all of its coeffcients in
R_{v}
and the leading coefficient
f(T)
is a unit in
R_{v}
. Therefore the reduction
\bar{\phi}_{v}
is a Drinfeld module of rank
r
. This shows that
\phi
has good reduction at
v
.
∎

Let
\rho
be the Galois representation

\rho=\widehat{\rho}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O}).

Proposition 3.2
.

The Galois representation
\rho
is unramified at all primes
\mathfrak{l}\notin\{\mathfrak{p},\infty\}
.

Proof.

Let
a\in A
be a monic generator of
\mathfrak{p}
. I show that
\phi[a]
is unramified at all primes
\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{p}
. Let
\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{p}
, and
v=v_{\mathfrak{l}}\in\Omega_{F}
be the associated valuation. By Lemma
3.1
,
\phi
has good reduction at
\mathfrak{l}
. Therefore
\phi_{v}
is isomorphic to a Drinfeld module
\psi
over
R_{v}
, such that
\bar{\psi}
is a Drinfeld module of rank
r
. Since
\bar{\psi}_{a}^{\prime}(x)=a\neq 0
, it follows that
\bar{\psi}_{a}^{\prime}(x)
has distinct roots. Hensel’s lemma implies that all roots of
\psi_{a}(x)
lie in
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\cdot F_{v}
, the maximal unramified extension of
F_{v}
. Therefore,
\psi[a]
is unramified. Since
\phi_{v}[a]\simeq\psi[a]
as
\operatorname{G}_{F_{v}}
-modules, it follows that
\phi_{v}[a]
is unramified. In other words,
\phi[a]
is unramified at
v
.
∎

3.2.
The endomorphism ring

Let
\kappa
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is a quotient of
A
by some non-zero prime ideal
\mathfrak{l}
. Let
C
be the Carlitz module over
\kappa
. This is the Drinfeld module
C\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow\kappa\{\tau\}
defined by
C_{T}=t+\tau
, where
t
is the image of
T
of the quotient map
A\rightarrow A/\mathfrak{l}=\kappa
.

Proposition 3.3
.

With respect to notation above, the structure map

\operatorname{str}_{C}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\bar{\kappa}}(C)

is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras.

Proof.

First, I show that
\operatorname{str}_{C}
is injective. Let
a\in A
be in the kernel of
\operatorname{str}_{C}
. Then, since
\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}(C_{a})=\operatorname{deg}_{T}(a)
, it follows that
\operatorname{deg}_{T}(a)=0
, i.e.,
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. On the other hand,
C\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow\kappa\{\tau\}
is a map of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras, hence,
a=0
. Next, I show that
\operatorname{str}_{C}
is surjective. Let
u=u_{0}+u_{1}\tau+\dots+u_{m}\tau^{m}\in\bar{\kappa}\{\tau\}
be an element in
\operatorname{End}_{\bar{\kappa}}(\phi)
. I show that for some
b\in A
, one has that
u=C_{b}
. Assume that
u_{m}\neq 0
, i.e.,
m=\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}(u)
. I prove the result by induction on
m
. First, when
m=0
,
u=u_{0}
, i.e.,
u\in\kappa
. Then, the relation

u_{0}C_{T}=C_{T}u_{0},

implies

\begin{split}&u_{0}(T+\tau)=(T+\tau)u_{0},\\
\text{i.e.,}&u_{0}T+u_{0}\tau=u_{0}T+u_{0}^{q}\tau.\end{split}

This implies that
u_{0}=u_{0}^{q}
, i.e.
u=u_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
C
is a homomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras, I find that
u=C_{u_{0}}
. Thus, one may assume that the assertion holds for all
v\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(C)
such that
\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}(v)<m
. Comparing the highest degree terms for the relation

uC_{T}=C_{T}u

I have that

u_{m}\tau^{m+1}+\text{ lower degree terms }=\tau u_{m}\tau^{m}+\text{ lower degree terms }.

Thus, I find that

u_{m}\tau^{m+1}=\tau u_{m}\tau^{m}=u_{m}^{q}\tau^{m+1}.

Therefore, one finds that
u_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. I deduce that

v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=u-C_{u_{m}T^{m}}=u-u_{m}(T+\tau)^{m}

satisfies the property that
\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}(v)<m
. By inductive hypothesis,
v
is in the image of
\operatorname{str}_{C}
, and hence,
u
is in the image of
\operatorname{str}_{C}
.
∎

Let
K
be the completion of
F
at a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
. Let
\mathbb{C}_{K}
be the completion of the algebraic closure of
K
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\phi
be a Drinfeld module over
K
, an
A
-lattice of rank
d
is a free
A
-module of rank
d
contained in
{}^{\phi}K^{\operatorname{sep}}
which is
\operatorname{G}_{K}
-stable and discrete in
\mathbb{C}_{K}
.

Theorem 3.5

(Drinfeld-Tate Uniformization)
.

Let
r_{1}>0,r_{2}>0
be integers and
r\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=r_{1}+r_{2}
. There is a natural bijection

\begin{split}\{(\phi,\Lambda)\mid&\phi\text{ is a Drinfeld module over }R\text{ with good reduction and of rank }r_{1},\\
&\text{ and }\Lambda\text{ is a }\phi\text{ lattice of rank }r_{2}\}\\
\xrightarrow{\sim}\{\psi\mid&\psi\text{ is a Drinfeld module of rank }r\text{ over }R\text{ with stable reduction}\\
&\text{ and reduction rank }r_{1}\}\end{split}

and,
\bar{\phi}
is equal to
\bar{\psi}
. The pair
(\phi,\Lambda)
is referred to as the uniformization of
\psi
.

Proof.

For a proof of the above result, see
[
Dri74
, section 7]
or
[
Pap23
, Theorem 6.2.11]
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
be Drinfeld modules of rank
r
over
R
with stable reduction. Assume that
\operatorname{rank}(\bar{\psi}_{1})=\operatorname{rank}(\bar{\psi}_{2})
. Let
(\phi_{i},\Lambda_{i})
be the Drinfeld-Tate uniformization of
\psi_{i}
. Then, there is an injection of
A
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{K}}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{K}}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}).

Proof.

The result follows from
[
Pap23
, Theorem 6.2.12]
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer,
\mathfrak{p}
be a non-zero prime ideal of
A
and
\mathcal{O}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Let
f(T)
be the monic polynomial generator of
\mathfrak{p}
and
\phi
be the Drinfeld module of rank
r
with coefficients in
F
defined by

\phi_{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T+\tau+f(T)\tau^{r}.

Then, the structure map

\operatorname{str}_{\phi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\bar{F}}(\phi)

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

At the prime
\mathfrak{p}
, the Drinfeld module has stable reduction with reduction rank
1
. Let
\kappa
denote the residue field of
A
at
\mathfrak{p}
. Take
\psi
to be the completion of
\phi
at
\mathfrak{p}
. I find that
\psi
is defined over
\mathcal{O}
and that its reduction mod-
\mathfrak{p}
is equal to
C
(the Carlitz module over
\kappa
). Set
K
to denote the fraction field of
\mathcal{O}
. Let
(\psi^{\prime},\Lambda)
be the Drinfeld-Tate uniformization of
\psi
. It follows from the Theorem
3.5
that
\bar{\psi}^{\prime}=\bar{\psi}
, and hence,
\bar{\psi}^{\prime}=C
. It follows from Proposition
3.3
that the structure map

\operatorname{str}_{C}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\bar{\kappa}}(C)

is an isomorphism of
A
-modules. This structure map factors as a composite of natural maps

A\xrightarrow{\alpha}\operatorname{End}_{\bar{K}}(\psi)\xrightarrow{\beta}\operatorname{End}_{\bar{K}}(\psi^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\gamma}\operatorname{End}_{\bar{\kappa}}(C).

The map
\alpha
is the structure map
\operatorname{str}_{\psi,K}
. The map
\beta
is injective by Proposition
3.6
. The map
\gamma
is the reduction map and is injective by
[
Pap23
, Corollary 6.1.12]
. Since
\gamma\beta\alpha=\operatorname{str}_{C}
is surjective and
\gamma\beta
is injective, it follows that
\alpha
is surjective. On the other hand, since
\gamma\beta\alpha=\operatorname{str}_{C}
is injective, it follows that
\alpha
is injective. Hence the map
\operatorname{str}_{\psi}=\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\bar{K}}(\psi)
is an isomorphism. This map factors as a composite

A\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\bar{F}}(\phi)\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\bar{K}}(\phi),

the latter map is injective. It follows that
\operatorname{str}_{\phi}
is an isomorphism of
A
-modules.
∎

3.3.
Ramification at
\mathfrak{p}

In this subsection, I analyze the reduction type of
\phi
at
\mathfrak{p}
and the image of the local Galois representation at
\mathfrak{p}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer,
\mathfrak{p}
be a non-zero prime ideal of
A
and
\mathcal{O}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Let
f(T)
be the monic polynomial generator of
\mathfrak{p}
and
\phi
be the Drinfeld module of rank
r
with coefficients in
F
defined by

\phi_{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T+\tau+f(T)\tau^{r}.

The following assertions hold

(1)

\operatorname{End}_{\bar{F}}(\phi)=A
,

(2)

the image of
\rho
has finite index in
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})
,

(3)

the restriction of
\rho
to the inertia group
\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is of the form

\rho_{|\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\chi&\ast&\ast&\cdots&\ast&\ast\\
&1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
&&1&0&\cdots&0\\
&&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
&&&&1&0\\
&&&&&1&\\
\end{pmatrix},

where
\chi
is a totally ramified character.

Proof.

Corollary
3.7
asserts that

\operatorname{str}_{\phi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\bar{F}}(\phi)

is an isomorphism. That the image of
\rho
has finite index in
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})
follows from Theorem
2.3
. The reduction
\bar{\phi}
of
\phi
at
\mathfrak{p}
is equal to the Carlitz module, and hence
\bar{\phi}
is of height
1
. It then follows that there is an exact sequence of
A[\operatorname{G}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}]
-modules

0\rightarrow\phi^{0}[\mathfrak{p}]\rightarrow\phi[\mathfrak{p}]\rightarrow\bar{\phi}[\mathfrak{p}]\rightarrow 0,

where
\phi^{0}[\mathfrak{p}]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\alpha\in\phi[\mathfrak{p}](\bar{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid|\alpha|<1\}
. As
A
-modules,

\phi^{0}[\mathfrak{p}]\simeq(A/\mathfrak{p})^{\operatorname{H}(\bar{\phi})}=(A/\mathfrak{p}),

and

\bar{\phi}[\mathfrak{p}]\simeq(A/\mathfrak{p})^{r-\operatorname{H}(\bar{\phi})}=(A/\mathfrak{p})^{r-1}.

Moreover, the module
\phi^{0}[\mathfrak{p}]
(resp.
\bar{\phi}[\mathfrak{p}]
) is totally ramified (resp. unramified). For a proof of these statements, the reader may refer to
[
Pap23
, section 6.3]
. Taking
T_{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi)^{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\phi[\mathfrak{p}]^{0}
, I have a short exact sequence of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}[\operatorname{G}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}]
-modules

0\rightarrow T_{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi)^{0}\rightarrow T_{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi)\rightarrow T_{\mathfrak{p}}(\bar{\phi})\rightarrow 0,

where

T_{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi)^{0}\simeq A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ and }T_{\mathfrak{p}}(\bar{\phi})\simeq A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{r-1}.

The Galois action on
T_{\mathfrak{p}}(\bar{\phi})
is unramified. Thus, I find that the restriction
\rho_{|\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is of the desired form.
∎

3.4.
Ramification at
\infty

In this subsection, I show that
\rho
is indeed unramified at
\infty
. This part of the argument does require that I assume that
\deg\mathfrak{p}=1
. Recall that
F_{\infty}
is the completion of
F
at
\infty
. Fix an algebraic closure of
\bar{F}_{\infty}/F_{\infty}
and think of every algebraic extension of
F_{\infty}
as a subfield of
\bar{F}_{\infty}
. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let
L_{k}/F_{\infty}
be the splitting field of the polynomial
g_{k}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\phi_{f(T)^{k}}(x)
, with the understanding that
L_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=F_{\infty}
. Let
v=v_{\infty}
be the valuation at
\infty
normalized by
v(T)=-1
, and note that that
v(\cdot)=-\operatorname{deg}(\cdot)
. Since it is assumed that
\operatorname{deg}f=1
, one may write
f(T)=(T-c)
, where
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

With respect to notation above, one has that for all
k\geq 1
, the extension
L_{k}/F_{\infty}
is unramified.

Proof.

I prove the result by induction on
k
, starting with
k=1
. Let
v_{1}
be the valuation on
L_{1}
which extends
v
. Let

G(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(T-c)^{-1}\phi_{(T-c)}(x)=x+(T-c)^{-1}x^{q}+x^{q^{r}}

and
\bar{G}(x)
be the reduction of
G(x)
modulo the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\llbracket T^{-1}\rrbracket
. It is clear that for all
k\geq 1
,
L_{k}
is the splitting field of
G(x)
over
L_{k-1}
. It is easy to see that
\bar{G}(x)=x+x^{q^{r}}
and
\bar{G}^{\prime}(x)=1
. Thus,
\bar{G}(x)
has the same number of roots as
G(x)
and its roots are all distinct. By Hensel’s lemma, the roots of
G(x)
lie in
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\cdot F_{\infty}
, the maximal unramified extension of
F_{\infty}
. Therefore, in particular,
L_{1}/F_{\infty}
is unramified. I argue by induction and assume that
L_{k-1}/F_{\infty}
is unramified. Then, the valuation
v
is unchanged when extended to
L_{k-1}
. Note that
L_{k}
is the splitting field of
G(x)
over
L_{k-1}
. Thus, the same argument gives us that
L_{k}/L_{k-1}
is unramified. Thus,
L_{k}
is an unramified extension of
F_{\infty}
, and I conclude that
\rho
is unramified at
\infty
when
\operatorname{deg}\mathfrak{p}=1
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Alternatively, Lemma
3.9
can be proven via analyzing the Newton polygon of
G(x)
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Assume that
\deg\mathfrak{p}=1
. Then,
\rho
is unramified at
\infty
.

Proof.

Choose an embedding of
\bar{F}
into
\bar{F}_{\infty}
. This is equivalent to the choice of a prime of
\bar{F}
that lies above
\infty
. It is clear that the field
F_{\infty}(\phi[\mathfrak{p}^{k}])
coincides with the splitting field of
g_{k}(x)
. From Lemma
3.9
, I find that
F_{\infty}(\phi[\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}])\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcup_{k}F_{\infty}(\phi[\mathfrak{p}^{k}])=\bigcup_{k}L_{k}
is an unramified extension of
F_{\infty}
. Consider the local Galois representation

\rho_{|\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}_{\infty}/F_{\infty})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})

and observe that
F_{\infty}(\phi[\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}])
is simply the field fixed by the kernel of
\rho_{|\infty}
. Thus, the inertia group at
\infty
is contained in the kernel of
\rho_{|\infty}
, or in other words,
\rho
is unramified at
\infty
.
∎

3.5.
Proof of the main theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

It follows from Proposition
3.2
that
\rho
is unramified at all primes
\mathfrak{l}\notin\{\mathfrak{p},\infty\}
, and from Proposition
3.8
that
\operatorname{im}\rho
has finite index in
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})
and that the restriction
\rho_{|\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is of the desired form. When
\deg\mathfrak{p}=1
, Proposition
3.11
asserts that
\rho
is also unramified at
\infty
.
∎

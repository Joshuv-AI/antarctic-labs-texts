Introduction

Nature’s three iterations of fermionic states would seem to indicate that its particle content is not merely random. With this said, the three generation problem in particle physics is as old as the Standard Model itself,

One known threefold structure, however, goes by the name of

models,

With this said, it is an underappreciated fact that the phenomenon of triality occurs not only for the octonions, but for all four of the finite dimensional normed division algebras over the reals. In this article, we pursue a proposal first introduced in
[
20

\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})

\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})=\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})

This article begins by explaining the common meanings imparted on the term

\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)_{\textup{Y}}

\mathbb{C}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+}

How might one then unfurl a third generation?

We introduce a technique known as

that allows us to recast
\mathbb{C}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}
as a product of spinor and conjugate spinor representations. Together, these spinor and conjugate spinor representations yield the states of a third generation, sterile neutrino included.

This technique allows us to address some challenges familiar to the problem of three generations,
[
23
]
. Namely, it allows us to set up a three-generation triality model with the correct Standard Model charges across all three generations, whereby the three generations are seen to be linearly independent.

We then introduce
Cartan factorization diagrams
, and subsequently provide a complete set of diagrams demonstrating
\mathbb{C}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}

This article compiles a list of possible Yukawa terms, some of which are familiar from the Standard Model, while others not. We observe that those Yukawa terms familiar from the Standard Model comprise a special class that fulfill the requirements of a
non-degenerate trilinear form
. This leads us to propose the
NDTF constraint

One interesting feature of
\mathbb{C}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}

Finally, we mention that this model is compatible with a Freudenthal-Tits triality construction of

\mathfrak{e}_{7}\simeq\left(\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})\right)+3\hskip 1.42262pt\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O},

\mathfrak{e}_{7},

II.1
\mathbb{R},

\mathbb{C},

\mathbb{H},

\mathbb{O}

A theorem by Hurwitz,
[

[

, states that, up to isomorphism, there exist exactly four unital finite-dimensional normed division algebras over
\mathbb{R}
. They are the real numbers,
\mathbb{R}
, the complex numbers,
\mathbb{C}
, the quaternions,
\mathbb{H},
and the octonions,
\mathbb{O}
.

The real numbers (
\mathbb{R}
) are ubiquitous in physics; the complex numbers (
\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{R}\otimes\mathbb{C}

\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}\otimes\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}

[
15
]

\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O}=\mathbb{R}\otimes\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O}
.

In order to maintain notational consistency with previous work,
[
19
]
-
[
24
]
, we will describe a generic complex number as
r_{0}+r_{1}i
, where
r_{0},r_{1}\in\mathbb{R},

i^{2}=-1
, and
(r_{0}+ir_{1})^{*}=r_{0}-ir_{1}
.

Similarly, we will describe a generic quaternion as
r_{0}+r_{m}\epsilon_{m}
, for
m\in\{1,2,3\},
where
r_{0},r_{m}\in\mathbb{R}.
Multiplicatively,
\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon_{3}\epsilon_{3}=-1
. When
m\neq n,
we have
\epsilon_{m}\epsilon_{n}=\varepsilon_{mnp}\epsilon_{p}
where
\varepsilon_{mnp}
is the usual totally anti-symmetric tensor with
\varepsilon_{123}=1
. Furthermore,
\left(\widetilde{r_{0}+r_{m}\epsilon_{m}}\right)=r_{0}-r_{m}\epsilon_{m}
with
\widetilde{ab}=\widetilde{b}\hskip 1.42262pt\widetilde{a}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{H}
.

Finally, we will describe a generic octonion as
r_{0}+r_{j}e_{j}
, for
j\in\{1,2,\dots 7\},
where
r_{0},r_{j}\in\mathbb{R}.
Multiplicatively,
e_{1}e_{1}=e_{2}e_{2}=\dots=e_{7}e_{7}=-1
. When

we have
e_{i}e_{j}=f_{ijk}e_{k}
, where
f_{ijk}
is again a totally antisymmetric tensor with
f_{ijk}=1
when
ijk\in\{124,235,346,457,561,672,713\}
. Those remaining values of
f_{ijk}
not determined by anti-symmetry are otherwise zero. Furthermore,
\left(\widetilde{r_{0}+r_{j}e_{j}}\right)=r_{0}-r_{j}e_{j}
with
\widetilde{ab}=\widetilde{b}\hskip 1.42262pt\widetilde{a}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{O}
. With this particular choice of indices, octonionic multiplication enjoys an index cycling symmetry and index doubling symmetry. Explicitly,
e_{i}e_{j}=e_{k}\Rightarrow e_{i+1}e_{j+1}=e_{k+1},

e_{i}e_{j}=e_{k}\Rightarrow e_{2i}e_{2j}=e_{2k},
respectively, where indices are understood to be elements of
\{1,2,\dots 7\}

\mathbb{Z}_{7}
.

Throughout this paper, tensor products will be understood to be over
\mathbb{R}
, unless otherwise stated. We will be particularly interested in algebras
\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O}

b_{0}+b_{m}\epsilon_{m}+b_{j}^{\prime}e_{j}+b_{mj}^{\prime\prime}\hskip 1.42262pt\epsilon_{m}e_{j}

b_{0}+b_{m}\epsilon_{m}+b_{j}^{\prime}e_{j}+b_{mj}^{\prime\prime}\hskip 1.42262pt\epsilon_{m}e_{j}

b_{0},b_{m},b_{j}^{\prime},b_{mj}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{C}

\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O}
in the canonical way for tensor products of algebras. As a concrete example, consider
\left(b_{0}+b_{1}\epsilon_{1}+b_{24}^{\prime\prime}\hskip 1.42262pt\epsilon_{2}e_{4}\right)\left(c_{0}+c_{35}^{\prime\prime}\hskip 1.42262pt\epsilon_{3}e_{5}\right)=b_{0}c_{0}+b_{0}c_{35}^{\prime\prime}\hskip 1.42262pt\epsilon_{3}e_{5}+b_{1}c_{0}\hskip 1.42262pt\epsilon_{1}-b_{1}c_{35}^{\prime\prime}\hskip 1.42262pt\epsilon_{2}e_{5}+b_{24}^{\prime\prime}c_{0}\hskip 1.42262pt\epsilon_{2}e_{4}+b_{24}^{\prime\prime}c_{35}^{\prime\prime}\hskip 1.42262pt\epsilon_{1}e_{7}

II.2
Triality, as it relates to duality

We begin by introducing triality via a certain type of scalar that is formed from spinor and vector inputs familiar to physicists,

t(\Psi_{+},V,\Psi_{-})=\langle\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{-}\rangle.

(2)

Given two vector spaces
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
over
\mathbb{R}
, a
duality
is a non-degenerate bilinear map

f:W_{1}\times W_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}.

(3)

The non-degeneracy property of
f
means that there exists no non-zero vector in one vector space such that
f
maps to zero for all vectors in the other vector space.

Now as a natural extension, a
triality
is a non-degenerate trilinear map

t:W_{1}\times W_{2}\times W_{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}.

(4)

The non-degeneracy property of
t
means that there exists no two non-zero vectors in two of the vector spaces such that
t
maps to zero for all vectors in the third vector space,
[
46
]
.

Importantly, this trilinear map may be reformulated as a bilinear map

m:W_{1}\times W_{2}\rightarrow W_{3}^{\star},

(5)

where
W_{3}^{\star}
is the dual of
W_{3}
. As a result of the non-degeneracy of
t
, each of
W_{1},

W_{2}
,
W_{3},
and their duals, may be identified with the same vector space
W
. As a consequence, we see that
m
describes multiplication,

m:W\times W\rightarrow W.

(6)

From here, it can be shown,
[
46
]
, that
W
together with
m
must be isomorphic to either
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{H}
, or
\mathbb{O}
. We write
\mathbb{W}:=\left(W,m\right)\simeq\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O}.
In other words, under these conditions,
triality occurs exactly four times
, one for each of the finite dimensional normed division algebras over the reals:
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{H}
, and
\mathbb{O}
.

In practice, one may write down this trilinear form as

\begin{array}[]{ll}t(\Psi_{+},V,\Psi_{-})&=\langle\left(\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV\right)\Psi_{-}\rangle=\langle\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\left(V\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{-}\right)\rangle\\
&:=\langle\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{-}\rangle,\end{array}

(7)

for
\Psi_{+}\in W_{1},

V\in W_{2},
and
\Psi_{-}\in W_{3}
. Here, the angled brackets
\langle\cdots\rangle
mean to take the real part. Furthermore,
\dagger
may be interpreted as
*
when
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{C}
, and as
\hskip 2.84526pt\widetilde{}\hskip 2.84526pt
when
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{H}
or
\mathbb{O}.

One then finds that for each of
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O},
the scalar
\langle\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{-}\rangle
is invariant under a given symmetry. At the Lie algebra level, these symmetries are known as
triality algebras
,
[
46
]
,
[
47
]
, and are given by

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\emptyset,\\
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1),\\
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})\simeq\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2),\\
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})\simeq\mathfrak{so}(8).\end{array}

(8)

In the next subsection, we describe the specific transformation properties of
\Psi_{+},

V,
and
\Psi_{-}
under these symmetries.

It may be noted that
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{W})
contains the norm-preserving symmetries
\mathfrak{so}(1)\simeq\emptyset,

\mathfrak{so}(2)\simeq\mathfrak{u}(1),

\mathfrak{so}(4)\simeq\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2),
and
\mathfrak{so}(8)
for
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O},
respectively.

II.2.1
Hughes’ Higgs

Recently, a novel physical interpretation was found by Hughes,
[
20
]
, for this triality scalar in the case of
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{H}
. Namely, she identified

\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})
with
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{spin}}
, left-handed fermions with
\Psi_{-}=\Psi_{\textup{L}}\simeq\left(\bf{2},\bf{1},\bf{2}\right)
, right-handed fermions as
\Psi_{+}=\Psi_{\textup{R}}\simeq\left(\bf{1},\bf{2},\bf{2}\right)
, and a left-right symmetric Higgs as
V=\Phi\simeq\left(\bf{2},\bf{2},\bf{1}\right)
. The triality scalar,
\langle\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{-}\rangle
then embodies a Yukawa coupling. Upon breaking
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{R}}
, Hughes’ left-right symmetric Higgs reduces to the familiar Standard Model Higgs.

II.3
Decomposition via derivations

Although we have listed the triality Lie algebras under which
\langle\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{-}\rangle
is invariant, we still have yet to specify exactly how
\Psi_{+},

\Psi_{-},
and
V
transform under these symmetries in the general case. In order to write down these transformation rules, it will be helpful to make use of a certain decomposition of
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{W})
.

The triality algebras listed in equations (
8
) follow a certain pattern. Namely, for
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{H}
, or
\mathbb{O}
, one finds that

\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{W})=\mathfrak{der}(\mathbb{W})+\mathfrak{Im}(\mathbb{W})+\mathfrak{Im}(\mathbb{W}).

(9)

The term
\mathfrak{Im}(\mathbb{W})
refers to the imaginary part of
\mathbb{W}

of

We define the

\widehat{d}(a_{1}a_{2})=\widehat{d}(a_{1})\hskip 1.42262pta_{2}+a_{1}\hskip 1.42262pt\widehat{d}(a_{2})\hskip 28.45274pt\forall a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{A}.

In the cases of

we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathfrak{der}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\emptyset,\\
\mathfrak{der}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\emptyset,\\
\mathfrak{der}(\mathbb{H})\simeq\mathfrak{su}(2),\\
\mathfrak{der}(\mathbb{O})\simeq\mathfrak{g}_{2}.\end{array}

(11)

Explicit descriptions of
\mathfrak{der}(\mathbb{H})
and
\mathfrak{der}(\mathbb{O})

Finally,
[

]

\begin{array}[]{lll}\delta_{V}V&=&\widehat{d}\hskip 2.56073ptV+L_{a}\hskip 1.42262ptV+R_{b}\hskip 1.42262ptV,\\

in which case, the invariance of

a,b\in\mathfrak{Im}(\mathbb{W})

, while

II.4
Cartan’s Triality Principle

Thus far, we have discussed those transformations on
\{\Psi_{+},V,\Psi_{-}\}
that hold
t(\Psi_{+},V,\Psi_{-})
fixed. However, as mentioned earlier in equation (
5
), this trilinear map,
t
, may be reformulated as a bilinear map,
m
. It is in this new context that
Cartan’s Triality Principle
applies.

Let us suppose that
V=\Psi_{+}\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}
, where
\Psi_{+}
and
\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}
are multiplied via division algebraic multiplication,
m.
Suppose that
\Psi_{+}
and
\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}
transform according to equations (
12
).
Cartan’s Triality Principle
(CTP) then states that

\delta_{V}V=\left(\delta_{+}\Psi_{+}\right)\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}+\Psi_{+}\left(\delta_{-}\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}\right).

(13)

In other words, the vector representation results from
m
-multiplying the spinor and conjugate spinor representations,
[
45
]
.

Alternatively, suppose that
\Psi_{+}=V\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{-}
, with
V
and
\Psi_{-}
transforming according to equations (
12
). Then CTP states that

\delta_{+}\Psi_{+}=\left(\delta_{V}V\right)\Psi_{-}+V\left(\delta_{-}\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}\right)^{\dagger}.

(14)

In other words, the spinor representation results from
m
-multiplying the vector and conjugate spinor representations.

Finally, suppose that
\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}=\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV
with
\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}
and
V
transforming according to equations (
12
). Then CTP states that

\delta_{-}\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}=\left(\delta_{+}\Psi_{+}\right)^{\dagger}V+\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\left(\delta_{V}V\right).

(15)

In other words, the conjugate spinor representation results from
m
-multiplying the spinor and vector representations.

These division algebraic factorizations of triality representations

\begin{array}[]{lcl}V=\Psi_{+}\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}&\hskip 5.69054pt\Leftrightarrow&V^{\dagger}=\Psi_{-}\Psi_{+}^{\dagger},\\
\Psi_{+}=V\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{-}&\Leftrightarrow&\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}=\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV^{\dagger},\\
\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}=\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV&\Leftrightarrow&\Psi_{-}=V^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{+}\end{array}

(16)

will be of special importance; we will refer to them as
Cartan factorizations
. In particular, the first of these three factorizations, the
Cartan vector factorization
, will allow us to identify a third generation of Standard Model spinors from within a set of bosons that includes Higgs representations.

III
Multiple Simultaneous Trialities

In this section we will first consider the tensor product
\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O}
. This set up will lead us towards Standard Model fermions in a Majorana representation, as opposed to the usual Weyl representation. The fact that such a Majorana description is possible (not only for sterile neutrinos) has been demonstrated extensively in
[
50
]
. Readers may also find reference
[
51
]
and
[
15
]
helpful.

Let us identify
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}
-active Majorana fermions with
\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt\mathbb{O}_{-}
and
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}
-inactive Majorana fermions with
\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt\mathbb{O}_{+}
. We will then show three generations of Standard Model fermion irreps as ultimately originating from

(\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+})\oplus(\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-})\oplus(\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}).

III.1
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})
actions

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}_{+}\in\mathbb{H}_{+},

\mathcal{H}_{-}\in\mathbb{H}_{-},

\mathcal{H}_{V}\in\mathbb{H}_{V},

\mathcal{O}_{+}\in\mathbb{O}_{+},

\mathcal{O}_{-}\in\mathbb{O}_{-},

\mathcal{O}_{V}\in\mathbb{O}_{V},
where the subscripts allow us to distinguish spinor, conjugate spinor, and vector representations.
Then a generic element of
\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+}
may be written a sum of objects of the form
\mathcal{H}_{+}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{+}
. Analogous statements hold true for
\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-}
and
\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}
.
Omitting
\otimes
symbols between
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{O}
, for notational simplicity, these objects transform as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\delta_{V}\left(\mathcal{H}_{V}\mathcal{O}_{V}\right)&=&\delta^{V}_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\mathcal{H}_{V}\right)\mathcal{O}_{V}+\mathcal{H}_{V}\hskip 1.42262pt\delta^{V}_{\mathbb{O}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{V}\right)\\
\delta_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}_{+}\mathcal{O}_{+}\right)&=&\delta^{+}_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\mathcal{H}_{+}\right)\mathcal{O}_{+}+\mathcal{H}_{+}\hskip 1.42262pt\delta^{+}_{\mathbb{O}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{+}\right)\\
\delta_{-}\left(\mathcal{H}_{-}\mathcal{O}_{-}\right)^{\dagger}&=&\delta^{-}_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\mathcal{H}_{-}^{\dagger}\right)\mathcal{O}_{-}^{\dagger}+\mathcal{H}_{-}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262pt\delta^{-}_{\mathbb{O}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{-}^{\dagger}\right),\end{array}

(20)

where

\begin{array}[]{lll}\delta^{V}_{\mathbb{H}}&=&\widehat{d}_{\mathbb{H}}+L_{a}+R_{b}\ ,\\
\delta^{+}_{\mathbb{H}}&=&\widehat{d}_{\mathbb{H}}+L_{a}+R_{a-b},\\
\delta^{-}_{\mathbb{H}}&=&\widehat{d}_{\mathbb{H}}+L_{b-a}+R_{b}\end{array}

(21)

for
a,b\in\mathfrak{Im}(\mathbb{H})
, and

\begin{array}[]{lll}\delta^{V}_{\mathbb{O}}&=&\widehat{d}_{\mathbb{O}}+L_{\alpha}+R_{\beta}\ ,\\
\delta^{+}_{\mathbb{O}}&=&\widehat{d}_{\mathbb{O}}+L_{\alpha}+R_{\alpha-\beta},\\
\delta^{-}_{\mathbb{O}}&=&\widehat{d}_{\mathbb{O}}+L_{\beta-\alpha}+R_{\beta}\end{array}

(22)

for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{Im}(\mathbb{O}).

The quaternionic derivation algebra
\mathfrak{der}(\mathbb{H})=\mathfrak{su}(2)
acts on
\mathbb{H}
as

\widehat{d}_{\mathbb{H}}:=L_{r}-R_{r}

(23)

for
r\in\mathfrak{Im}(\mathbb{H})
, while the octonionic derivation algebra
\mathfrak{der}(\mathbb{O})=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
acts on
\mathbb{O}
as

\begin{array}[]{lll}\widehat{d}_{\mathbb{O}}&:=&\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}\left(L_{34}-L_{15}\right)+\frac{\rho_{2}}{2}\left(L_{14}+L_{35}\right)\\
&+&\frac{\rho_{3}}{2}\left(L_{13}-L_{45}\right)-\frac{\rho_{4}}{2}\left(L_{25}+L_{46}\right)\\
&+&\frac{\rho_{5}}{2}\left(L_{24}-L_{56}\right)-\frac{\rho_{6}}{2}\left(L_{16}+L_{23}\right)\\
&-&\frac{\rho_{7}}{2}\left(L_{12}+L_{36}\right)-\frac{\rho_{8}}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(L_{13}+L_{45}-2L_{26}\right)\\
&+&\frac{\rho_{9}}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(L_{15}+L_{34}+2L_{27}\right)+\frac{\rho_{10}}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(-L_{14}+L_{35}-2L_{67}\right)\\
&+&\frac{\rho_{11}}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(L_{46}-L_{25}+2L_{17}\right)+\frac{\rho_{12}}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(L_{24}+L_{56}-2L_{37}\right)\\
&+&\frac{\rho_{13}}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(-L_{16}+L_{23}+2L_{47}\right)+\frac{\rho_{14}}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(L_{12}-L_{36}-2L_{57}\right),\end{array}

(24)

where
\rho_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and
L_{ij}
is shorthand for
L_{e_{i}}L_{e_{j}}
.

III.2
Standard Model symmetries inside
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})

With the actions of
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})
and
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})
defined, we may now identify a copy of
\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)_{\textup{Y}}
within them.

It has been known since at least the 1970s that
\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}
may be identified within
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
as the Lie subalgebra fixing an octonionic imaginary unit,
[
52
]
. Readers may confirm that setting
\rho_{9}
to
\rho_{14}
of equation (
24
) to zero defines an
\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}
subalgebra that fixes the octonionic imaginary unit
e_{7}
. We are then left with

\begin{array}[]{lll}\delta_{\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}}^{+}&=&\delta_{\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}}^{-}\hskip 2.84526pt=\hskip 2.84526pt\delta_{\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}}^{V}\\
&=&\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}\left(L_{34}-L_{15}\right)+\frac{\rho_{2}}{2}\left(L_{14}+L_{35}\right)\\
&+&\frac{\rho_{3}}{2}\left(L_{13}-L_{45}\right)-\frac{\rho_{4}}{2}\left(L_{25}+L_{46}\right)\\
&+&\frac{\rho_{5}}{2}\left(L_{24}-L_{56}\right)-\frac{\rho_{6}}{2}\left(L_{16}+L_{23}\right)\\
&-&\frac{\rho_{7}}{2}\left(L_{12}+L_{36}\right)-\frac{\rho_{8}}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(L_{13}+L_{45}-2L_{26}\right).\end{array}

(25)

Given that
\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}\subset\mathfrak{der}({\mathbb{O}})
, one finds that
\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}
acts identically on the spinor, conjugate spinor, and vector representations.

On the other hand, we will identify
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}
with the
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})
Lie subalgebra for which
a=-r
and
b=-2r
. For spinor, conjugate spinor, and vector representations,
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}
acts as

\delta_{\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}}^{+}=0,\hskip 14.22636pt\delta_{\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}}^{-}=-R_{3r},\hskip 14.22636pt\delta_{\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}}^{V}=-R_{3r},

(26)

with two of the three representations transforming identically.

Using the weak hypercharge conventions of
[
50
]
, we may define
\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Y}
within
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})
by setting
\rho_{i}=0

\forall i
,
\alpha=\frac{y}{6}e_{7}
,
\beta=-\frac{y}{6}e_{7}
,
r=b=-\frac{y}{6}\epsilon_{3}
, and
a=-\frac{y}{3}\epsilon_{3}
for
y\in\mathbb{R}
. This results in the weak hypercharge actions

\begin{array}[]{lll}\delta^{+}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Y}}&=&L_{\frac{y}{6}e_{7}-\frac{y}{2}\epsilon_{3}}+R_{\frac{y}{3}e_{7}},\\
\delta^{-}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Y}}&=&L_{-\frac{y}{3}e_{7}}+R_{-\frac{y}{6}e_{7}},\\
\delta^{V}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Y}}&=&L_{\frac{y}{6}e_{7}-\frac{y}{2}\epsilon_{3}}+R_{-\frac{y}{6}e_{7}},\end{array}

(27)

which behave differently on each representation. It is also worth noting that weak hypercharge here is described using a single octonionic, and a single quaternionic imaginary unit.

III.3
B-L
and electric charge
Q

Likewise,
\mathfrak{u}(1)_{B-L}
and
\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Q}
may be found within
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})
, and act as

\begin{array}[]{lll}\delta^{+}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{B-L}}&=&L_{\frac{\ell}{3}e_{7}}+R_{\frac{2\ell}{3}e_{7}},\\
\delta^{-}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{B-L}}&=&L_{-\frac{2\ell}{3}e_{7}}+R_{-\frac{\ell}{3}e_{7}},\\
\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\delta^{V}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{B-L}}&\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=&\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L_{\frac{\ell}{3}e_{7}}+R_{-\frac{\ell}{3}e_{7}},\end{array}

(28)

and

\begin{array}[]{lll}\delta^{+}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Q}}&=&L_{\frac{q}{6}e_{7}-\frac{q}{2}\epsilon_{3}}+R_{\frac{q}{3}e_{7}},\\
\delta^{-}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Q}}&=&L_{-\frac{q}{3}e_{7}}+R_{-\frac{q}{6}e_{7}+\frac{q}{2}\epsilon_{3}},\\
\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\delta^{V}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Q}}&\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=&\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L_{\frac{q}{6}e_{7}-\frac{q}{2}\epsilon_{3}}+R_{-\frac{q}{6}e_{7}+\frac{q}{2}\epsilon_{3}},\end{array}

(29)

for
\ell,q\in\mathbb{R}
. The vector representations for these symmetries are worth pointing out. That is,
\delta^{V}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{B-L}}
is described simply by a commutator with
e_{7}
, whereas
\delta^{V}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Q}}
is described by a commutator with a certain linear combination of
e_{7}
and
\epsilon_{3}
.

First two generations

With spinor, conjugate spinor, and vector representations of
\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)_{\textup{Y}}
defined, we may now begin identifying fermion states within

(\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+})\oplus(\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-})\oplus(\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}).

In keeping with previous articles,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
22
]
, we will define
\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{O}
basis vectors as

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\ell}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+ie_{7}\right),&{\ell}^{*}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-ie_{7}\right),\\
\alpha_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(-e_{5}+ie_{4}\right),&\alpha_{1}^{\dagger}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(e_{5}+ie_{4}\right),\\

drops out in the result.

Two generations of
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}
-inactive states may be identified within
\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+}
as

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+}&=&\mathcal{V}^{1}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow}+\mathcal{V}^{1*}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}\\
&+&\mathcal{V}^{2}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}-\mathcal{V}^{2*}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}\\
&+&\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}-\mathcal{E}^{1*}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}\\
&+&\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}+\mathcal{E}^{2*}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow}\\
&+&\mathcal{U}^{i1}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\alpha_{i}}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow}-\mathcal{U}^{i1*}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt\alpha_{i}^{\dagger}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}\\
&+&\mathcal{U}^{i2}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\alpha_{i}}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}+\mathcal{U}^{i2*}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt\alpha_{i}^{\dagger}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}\\
&+&\mathcal{D}^{i1}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\alpha_{i}}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}+\mathcal{D}^{i1*}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt\alpha_{i}^{\dagger}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}\\
&+&\mathcal{D}^{i2}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\alpha_{i}}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}-\mathcal{D}^{i2*}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt\alpha_{i}^{\dagger}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow},\\
\end{array}

(32)

where
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{\textup{R}},\mathcal{V}^{2}_{\textup{R}},\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\textup{R}},\dots\mathcal{D}^{i2}_{\textup{R}}
are complex coefficients. At this stage, readers may already confirm that all factors of the complex
i
drop out. In the labeling of fermions throughout this text, we have opted against identifying whether particles come from the first, second, or third generation. This will only be appropriate once masses are assigned.

In an analogous fashion, we find that two generations of
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}
-active states may be identified within
\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-}
as

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-}&=&\mathcal{V}^{1}_{\textup{L}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow}+\mathcal{V}^{1*}_{\textup{L}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}\\
&+&\mathcal{V}^{2}_{\textup{L}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}-\mathcal{V}^{2*}_{\textup{L}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}\\

\end{array}

where
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{\textup{L}},\mathcal{V}^{2}_{\textup{L}},\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\textup{L}},\dots\mathcal{D}^{i2}_{\textup{L}}\in\mathbb{C}.

III.5
An obscured third generation

Between
\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+}
and
\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-}
, we have now accounted for two generations. Clearly, we would like
\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}
to account for a third generation. However, calculation of electroweak transformations,
\delta_{\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}}^{V}
and
\delta^{V}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)_{Y}}
results in what would look, naïvely, to be incorrect charges. That is, we obtain instead the real representations corresponding to the familiar
(\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}},\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}},\mathfrak{u}(1)_{\textup{Y}})
complex representations

\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},\frac{1}{2}\right),\hskip 5.69054pt\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right),\hskip 5.69054pt\left(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},\frac{1}{6}\right),\hskip 5.69054pt\left(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},-\frac{5}{6}\right),

(34)

and their conjugates. Again, for a discussion on real representations, please see
[
50
]
,
[
51
]
.

It should be noted that the first two representation spaces align with the transformation properties of the familiar Standard Model Higgs. They are also closely related to Hughes’ quaternionic Higgs, first introduced in
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
.

We label these four irreducible representations (
34
) as
h,

H,

V_{q},
and
V_{r},
respectively. Accordingly,
\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}
may be labeled as

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}&=&h^{\uparrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow}+h^{\uparrow*}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}\\
&+&h^{\downarrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}-h^{\downarrow*}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}\\
&+&H^{\uparrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}-H^{\uparrow*}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}\\
&+&H^{\downarrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}+H^{\downarrow*}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow}\\
&+&V_{q}^{i\uparrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\alpha_{i}}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow}-V_{q}^{i\uparrow*}\hskip 1.99168pt\alpha_{i}^{\dagger}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}\\
&+&V_{q}^{i\downarrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\alpha_{i}}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}+V_{q}^{i\downarrow*}\hskip 1.99168pt\alpha_{i}^{\dagger}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}\\
&+&V_{r}^{i\uparrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\alpha_{i}}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}+V_{r}^{i\uparrow*}\hskip 1.99168pt\alpha_{i}^{\dagger}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}\\
&+&V_{r}^{i\downarrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\alpha_{i}}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}-V_{r}^{i\downarrow*}\hskip 1.99168pt\alpha_{i}^{\dagger}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow},\\
\end{array}

(35)

for
h^{\uparrow},h^{\downarrow},H^{\uparrow},H^{\downarrow},\dots V^{i\downarrow}_{r}\in\mathbb{C}
. As with
\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+}
and
\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-}
, it may be confirmed that all factors of the complex
i
drop out.

On one hand, it is of interest to find the familiar Standard Model Higgs representation within
\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}
. On the other hand,
\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}
has not decomposed into the set of irreducible representations corresponding to a third generation. So has the model then failed? Or could there be a method with which one may extract a third generation from
\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}
?

(\mathbb{C}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+})\oplus(\mathbb{C}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-})\oplus(\mathbb{C}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}).

In this case, equations (

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathbb{C}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+}&=&\mathcal{V}^{1}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow}+\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{1}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}\\
&+&\mathcal{V}^{2}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}-\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{2}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}\\
&+&\dots,\end{array}

(40)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathbb{C}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-}&=&\mathcal{V}^{1}_{\textup{L}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow}+\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{1}_{\textup{L}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}\\
&+&\mathcal{V}^{2}_{\textup{L}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}-\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{2}_{\textup{L}}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}\\
&+&\dots,\par\end{array}

(41)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathbb{C}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}&=&h^{\uparrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\uparrow}+\bar{h}^{\uparrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}\\
&+&h^{\downarrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}-\bar{h}^{\downarrow}\hskip 1.99168pt{\ell}^{*}\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}\\
&+&\dots,\end{array}

where the complex coefficients written with bars overtop are now independent from the unbarred complex coefficients. It is worth noting that the Cartan factorizations of the previous subsection carry over in an obvious manner to the complexified case.

Upon complexification, it is natural to consider
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O}),
as first proposed in

. Pushing the idea one step further, in future work we will explore a possible extension of the triality construction of the Freudenthal-Tits magic square. The vector space associated with this new Lie algebra is written as

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})\\
\oplus\\
(\mathbb{C}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+})\oplus(\mathbb{C}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-})\oplus(\mathbb{C}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}),\end{array}

(43)

whose action generalizes that of
\mathfrak{e}_{7}\simeq\left(\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})\right)+3\left(\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O}\right)
in the obvious way,

. Alternatively, one may consider

III.8
Yukawa terms and multiple Higgs

It is straightforward to confirm that the representations of Subsection
III.7
may be assembled into Yukawa scalars of the following types:

\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.42262pt{h}\hskip 0.56905pt{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{L}}\rangle,&\langle\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.42262pt{H}\hskip 1.42262pt{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{L}}\rangle,\\
\langle\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.42262pt{h}\hskip 0.56905pt{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textup{L}}\rangle,&\langle\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.42262pt{H}\hskip 1.42262pt{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textup{L}}\rangle,\\
\langle\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.42262pt{V_{q}^{*}}\hskip 0.56905pt{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textup{L}}\rangle,&\langle\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.42262pt{V_{r}}\hskip 1.42262pt{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textup{L}}^{*}\rangle,\\
\langle\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.42262pt{V_{q}}\hskip 0.56905pt{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{L}}^{*}\rangle,&\langle\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.42262pt{V_{r}}\hskip 1.42262pt{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{L}}^{*}\rangle,\\
\langle\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.42262pt{V_{q}^{*}}\hskip 0.56905pt{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textup{L}}^{*}\rangle,&\langle\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}_{\textup{R}}\hskip 1.42262ptV_{r}^{*}\hskip 1.42262pt{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textup{L}}^{*}\rangle,\\
\end{array}

(44)

and their conjugates. Here
\mathcal{V}_{\textup{R}}
transforms as
\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1},0\right)
,
h
transforms as
\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},\frac{1}{2}\right),

\mathcal{L}_{\textup{L}}
transforms as
\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right),
etc.

A few comments are in order. First of all, we point out that in the complexified case, there are effectively four
\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right)
Higgs representations,
h^{*},

H,

\bar{h},

\bar{H}^{*},
in contrast to the Standard Model’s single Higgs. (Incidentally, there are four masses per generation.)

Secondly, notice that the preservation of
h^{*}
and
H
as separate representations breaks
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{R}}
symmetry, thereby ensuring
chirality.

Finally, we point out that while the
4\hskip 1.42262pt\mathbb{R}
dimensional
h^{*}
and
H
bosons align relatively closely with the Standard Model’s phenomenology, the
12\hskip 1.42262pt\mathbb{R}
dimensional
V_{q}
and
V_{r}
bosons do not. Could there be a constraint that suppresses or eliminates the effect of
V_{q}
and
V_{r}
while preserving the effect of the Higgs representations?

III.9
The Non-Degenerate Trilinear Form (NDTF) Constraint

Suppose we require that all Yukawa terms originate from a
non-degenerate
trilinear form

t(\Psi_{+},V,\Psi_{-})=\langle\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{-}\rangle

(45)

of Section
II.2
. Recall that this constraint requires that
\Psi_{+},V,\Psi_{-}
each live in algebras isomorphic to
\mathbb{R},

\mathbb{C},

\mathbb{H},
or
\mathbb{O}
.

Then it is straightforward to see that certain linear combinations of
h,

h^{*},

\bar{h},

\bar{h}^{*},

H,

H^{*},

\bar{H},
and
\bar{H}^{*}
accommodate quaternionic versions of this trilinear form. For a detailed example of one such quaternionic triality Higgs system, please see
[
20
]
.

In contrast, with their
12\hskip 1.42262pt\mathbb{R}
dimensions, no linear combination of
V_{q},

V_{q}^{*},

\bar{V}_{q},

\bar{V}_{q}^{*},

V_{r},

V_{r}^{*},

\bar{V}_{r},

\bar{V}_{r}^{*}
acts on fermions as
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{H}
, or
\mathbb{O}
. Hence, we would expect these Yukawa terms to be suppressed or excluded at energy levels where the Standard Model’s
\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)_{\textup{Y}}
symmetries are relevant. It is of interest to note that the
\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},\pm\frac{1}{2}\right),
Higgs representations constitute the minimal set within
\mathbb{C}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}
necessary to produce all of the third-generation fermions states.

In continuing work, we explore how this
non-degenerate trilinear form (NDTF) constraint
may be used to build a Lagrangian in the context of a field theory.

IV
Summary

In this article, we started out by identifying the Standard Model’s
\mathfrak{su}(3)_{\textup{C}}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\textup{L}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)_{\textup{Y}}
symmetries within the triality symmetries
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O})\subset\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb{O}).
We applied these Standard Model symmetries onto
(\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+})\oplus(\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-})\oplus(\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V})
, and subsequently its complexification
(\mathbb{C}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{+})\oplus(\mathbb{C}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{-}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{-})\oplus(\mathbb{C}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V})
. We found that together, the spinor and conjugate spinor representations gave two generations of Standard Model irreps, including sterile neutrinos. However, the vector representations did not immediately produce the irreps of a third generation. Instead, one finds

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},\frac{1}{2}\right),&\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right),&\left(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},\frac{1}{6}\right),&\left(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},-\frac{5}{6}\right),\\
\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right),&\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},\frac{1}{2}\right),&\left(\mathbf{3}^{*},\mathbf{2},-\frac{1}{6}\right),&\left(\mathbf{3}^{*},\mathbf{2},\frac{5}{6}\right)\end{array}

(46)

in the complexified case. Notably, this set includes four copies of the familiar Standard Model Higgs representations.

It turns out that a third generation may be identified within
\mathbb{C}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}
by making use of
Cartan factorization,
which allows vector representations to be factorized into spinor and conjugate spinor representations. We demonstrate that this method yields a third generation.

Within the set of
\mathbb{C}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{H}_{V}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{V}
irreps, the Standard Model Higgs are found to constitute the smallest representations necessary to produce a full generation of fermions. With this said, our model, at least naïvely, seems to make a significant departure from the Standard Model. Instead of one Higgs with four Yukawa coupling constants across a generation, one finds four Higgs representations.

Future work will explore the other Cartan factorizations,
\Psi_{+}=V\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{-}
and
\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}=\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\hskip 1.42262ptV,
in the context of the first two generations.

Finally, we point out that Yukawa terms involving the above
\left(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},\frac{1}{6}\right)
and
\left(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},-\frac{5}{6}\right)
representations may be excluded, while Yukawa terms involving
\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},\pm\frac{1}{2}\right)
may be preserved, if one requires that our Lagrangian’s Yukawa terms are built only from non-degenerate trilinear forms. This requirement is introduced as the
non-degenerate trilinear form (NDTF) constraint
. Dynamics and symmetry breaking in this model are subjects of current investigation.

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we consider a coupled system, known as
Kobayashi–Warren–Carter system,
abbreviated as the
KWC system
. KWC system consists of an Allen–Cahn type equation and a singular diffusion equation, and it was proposed by [Kobayashi et al, Phys. D, 140, 141–150 (2000)] as a possible mathematical model of grain boundary motion. The focus of this work is on the dynamic boundary condition imposed in our KWC system, and the mathematical interest is in a conflicting situation between: the continuity of the transmission condition included in the dynamic boundary condition; and the discontinuity encouraged by the singular diffusion equation. On this basis, we will prove the Main Theorem concerned with the existence of solution to our KWC system with energy-dissipation. Additionally, as a sub-result, we will prove a key-lemma that is to give a certain mathematical interpretation for the conflicting situation.

\displaystyle|\nabla\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell}(t)|\,dx-\left.\int_{\overline{\Omega}}d[|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}\right|\,dt

(3.89)

\displaystyle\leq m_{\ell}\cdot\frac{2^{-\ell}}{m_{\ell}}+\bigl|\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}-\overline{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}\bigr|_{L^{1}(I;L^{1}(\Gamma))}+(3+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\cdot 2^{-\ell}

(3.90)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(4+(1+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\Gamma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\cdot 2^{-\ell},\mbox{ for $\ell=1,2,3,\dots$.}

(3.91)

These estimates lead to the convergence (
3.61
).

Furthermore, by taking a subsequence if necessary, we can include the pointwise convergence (
3.63
) as a property of the sequence
\bigl\{[\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell},\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}]\bigr\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\subset L^{\infty}(I;V_{\delta_{\Gamma}})
.
∎

Key-Lemma 3
.

Let
I\subset(0,T)
be an open interval. Let us assume:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta\in L^{\infty}(I;H^{1}(\Omega)),~\beta(t)\in Y_{0}(\Omega)\mbox{ a.e. $t\in I$,}\\[2.15277pt]
\bm{\vartheta}=[\vartheta,\vartheta_{\Gamma}]\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)),~\bigl|D\vartheta(\cdot)\bigr|_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})\in L^{1}(I).\end{cases}

(3.92)

Then, the function of time:

\displaystyle t\in I\mapsto[\beta(t)|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})\in[0,\infty]

is measurable. In particular, if:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta\in C(\overline{I};L^{2}(\Omega)),~\beta(t)\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Y_{\mathrm{c}}}}(\Omega)\mbox{ a.e. $t\in I$,}\\[2.15277pt]
\bm{\vartheta}=[\vartheta,\vartheta_{\Gamma}]\in C(\overline{I};L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)),\end{cases}

(3.93)

then this function is l.s.c. on
[0,T]
.

Proof.

By the assumption (
3.92
), we can apply Key-Lemma
2
, and we can take a sequence of functions
\{\bm{\widetilde{\vartheta}}_{\ell}=[\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell},\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}]\}\subset L^{\infty}(I;V_{\varepsilon})
, that achieve the convergences (
3.61
) and (
3.63
).

On this basis, let us fix
t\in I
satisfying (
3.63
). Besides, with Fact
1
in mind, let us put:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle A_{1}:=[({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+\beta(t))|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}(\overline{\Omega}),\\[4.30554pt]
A_{2}:=[({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+|\beta(t)|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\beta(t))|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}(\overline{\Omega}),\end{cases}

(3.94)

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle a_{\ell}^{1}:=[({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+\beta(t))|D\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell}(t)|]_{\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}(t)}(\overline{\Omega}),\\[4.30554pt]
a_{\ell}^{2}:=[({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+|\beta(t)|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\beta(t))|D\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell}(t)|]_{\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}(t)}(\overline{\Omega}),\end{cases}\mbox{for $\ell=1,2,3,\dots$.}

(3.95)

Let
{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a sufficiently large open ball such that
{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}\supset\Omega
. Then with the properties of extension
[{}\cdot{}]^{\mathrm{ex}}
(see Section 7.2 in Appendix, for details), one can see that:

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-12.91663pt\parbox{219.08612pt}{\vskip-8.61108pt\begin{itemize}\itemize@item@$[\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}\to[\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}$ in $H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$, and in $W^{1,1}({\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega})$,
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@$[\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell}(t)]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma,\ell}(t)}^{\mathrm{ex}}\to[\vartheta(t)]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}^{\mathrm{ex}}$ in $L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$, and weakly-$*$ in $BV({\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega})$,
\end{itemize}\vskip-8.61108pt}\right.~\mbox{ as $\ell\to\infty$,}

(3.96)

\displaystyle A_{1}~

\displaystyle=[({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+\beta(t))|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})

(3.97)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}}[\vartheta(t)]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}^{\mathrm{ex}}\,\mathrm{div}\,\bigl(({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+\beta(t))\varphi\bigr)\,dx&\parbox{99.58464pt}{$\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}({\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})$, $|\varphi|\leq 1$ on ${\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}$}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\bigl({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+[\beta(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}\bigr)|\nabla[\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}|\,d\Gamma

(3.99)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}[\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell}(t)]_{\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}(t)}\,\mathrm{div}\,\bigl(({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+\beta(t))\varphi\bigr)\,dx&\parbox{99.58464pt}{$\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}({\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})$, $|\varphi|\leq 1$ on ${\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}$}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad-\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\bigl({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+[\beta(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}\bigr)|\nabla[\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}|\,d\Gamma

(3.101)

\displaystyle\leq\varliminf_{\ell\to\infty}\left(\sup\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}}[\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell}(t)]_{\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}(t)}\,\mathrm{div}\,\bigl(({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+\beta(t))\varphi\bigr)\,dx&\parbox{99.58464pt}{$\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}({\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})$, $|\varphi|\leq 1$ on ${\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}$}\end{array}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\bigl({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+[\beta(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}\bigr)|\nabla[\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}|\,d\Gamma

(3.103)

\displaystyle=\varliminf_{\ell\to\infty}[({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+\beta(t))|D\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell}(t)|]_{\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})=\varliminf_{\ell\to\infty}a_{\ell}^{1},

(3.104)

as well as,

\displaystyle A_{2}~

\displaystyle=[({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+|\beta(t)|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\beta(t))|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})

(3.105)

\displaystyle\leq\varliminf_{\ell\to\infty}[({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+|\beta(t)|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\beta(t))|D\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell}(t)|]_{\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\Gamma,\ell}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})=\varliminf_{\ell\to\infty}a_{\ell}^{2},

(3.106)

and furthermore,

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\,

(3.108)

So, we can apply Fact
1
to the case when
m=2
, and we can observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\,d[\beta(t)|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\ell}(t)|\,dx,\mbox{ for a.e. $t\in I$.}

(3.109)

It implies the measurability of

3.93
) to verify the lower semi-continuity of

on
\overline{I}
. Under (

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-12.91663pt\parbox{367.04037pt}{\vskip-8.61108pt\begin{itemize}\itemize@item@$[\beta]^{\mathrm{ex}}:t\in\overline{I}\mapsto[\beta(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ is weakly continuous, and $[\beta]^{\mathrm{ex}}\in C(\overline{I};L^{2}({\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}))$

\vskip-4.30554pt\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@$[\vartheta]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}}^{\mathrm{ex}}:t\in\overline{I}\mapsto[\vartheta(t)]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}^{\mathrm{ex}}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega})$ is continuous.
\end{itemize}\vskip-8.61108pt}\right.

(3.110)

In view of this, the lower semi-continuity of

can be verified, as follows:

\displaystyle[\beta|D\vartheta|(t)]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})=\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}}d[\beta(t)|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}-\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}[\beta]^{\mathrm{ex}}(t)|\nabla[\vartheta_{\Gamma}]^{\mathrm{ex}}(t)|\,dx

}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad-\lim_{\varsigma\to t}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}[\beta]^{\mathrm{ex}}(\varsigma)|\nabla[\vartheta_{\Gamma}]^{\mathrm{ex}}(\varsigma)|\,dx

(3.113)

\displaystyle\leq\varliminf_{\varsigma\to t}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}}d[\beta(\varsigma)|D\vartheta(\varsigma)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(\varsigma)}-\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}[\beta]^{\mathrm{ex}}(\varsigma)|\nabla[\vartheta_{\Gamma}]^{\mathrm{ex}}(\varsigma)|\,dx\right)

(3.114)

\displaystyle=\varliminf_{\varsigma\to t}\,[\beta|D\vartheta|(\varsigma)]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(\varsigma)}(\overline{\Omega}),\mbox{ for any $t\in\overline{I}$.}

(3.115)

Thus, we conclude this key-lemma.
∎

Key-Lemma 4
.

Let

be a fixed open interval. Let

and

be as in (

\bigl\{\beta_{n}(t)\,\bigl|\,n=1,2,3,\dots\bigr\}\subset Y_{0}(\Omega),~\mbox{ a.e. $t\in I$.}\end{cases}

(3.116)

Let
\{[\vartheta_{n},\vartheta_{\Gamma,n}]\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))
be a sequence of functional pairs such that:

\left\{~\parbox{227.62204pt}{$\bigl\{[|D\vartheta_{n}({}\cdot{})|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}({}\cdot{})}(\overline{\Omega})\bigr\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset L^{1}(I)$,

}\right.

(3.117)

and

\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{I}[\beta_{n}(t)|D\vartheta_{n}(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma,n}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})\,dt\geq\int_{I}[\beta(t)|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})\,dt.

(3.119)

Proof.

For the proof of (
3.119
), it is sufficient to consider only the case when:

\displaystyle\Phi_{*}~

\displaystyle=\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{I}[\beta_{n}(t)|D\vartheta_{n}(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma,n}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})\,dt<\infty,

because the other case is trivial.
In this case, we can find a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\{n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, such that:

(3.121)

Then, by taking more one subsequence if necessary, the assumptions (
3.116
)–(
3.118
), and Remark
8
in Appendix enable us to suppose:

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-12.91663pt\parbox{270.30118pt}{\vskip-8.61108pt\begin{itemize}\itemize@item@$0\leq[\beta]^{\mathrm{ex}}(t)\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$, for a.e. $t\in I$,
\vskip-15.0694pt\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@$\displaystyle L_{*}^{\mathrm{ex}}:=\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\,\bigl||D[\vartheta_{n}]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma,n}}^{\mathrm{ex}}|({\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega})\bigr|_{L^{1}(I)}<\infty$.
\end{itemize}\vskip-8.61108pt}\right.

(3.122)

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-12.91663pt\parbox{219.08612pt}{\vskip-8.61108pt\begin{itemize}\itemize@item@$[\beta_{n_{k}}]^{\mathrm{ex}}\to[\beta]^{\mathrm{ex}}$ in $L^{2}(I;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))$, and weakly-$*$ in $L^{\infty}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))$,
\vskip-2.15277pt\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@$[\vartheta_{\Gamma,n_{k}}]^{\mathrm{ex}}\to[\vartheta_{\Gamma}]^{\mathrm{ex}}$ in $L^{2}(I;H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))$, and in $L^{1}(I;W^{1,1}({\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}))$,
\vskip-2.15277pt\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@$[\vartheta_{n_{k}}]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma,n_{k}}}^{\mathrm{ex}}\to[\vartheta]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}}^{\mathrm{ex}}$ in $L^{2}(I;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))$,,
\end{itemize}\vskip-8.61108pt}\right.\mbox{as $k\to\infty$,}

(3.123)

and

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-12.91663pt\parbox{227.62204pt}{\vskip-8.61108pt\begin{itemize}\itemize@item@$[\beta_{n_{k}}]^{\mathrm{ex}}(t)\to[\beta]^{\mathrm{ex}}(t)$ in $L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$, and weakly in $H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$,
\vskip-2.15277pt\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@$[\vartheta_{\Gamma,n_{k}}]^{\mathrm{ex}}(t)\to[\vartheta_{\Gamma}]^{\mathrm{ex}}(t)$ in $H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$, and in $W^{1,1}({\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega})$,
\vskip-2.15277pt\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@$[\vartheta_{n_{k}}(t)]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma,n_{k}}(t)}^{\mathrm{ex}}\to[\vartheta(t)]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}^{\mathrm{ex}}$ in $L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$,
\end{itemize}\vskip-8.61108pt}\right.~\mbox{ a.e. $t\in I$, as $k\to\infty$.}

(3.124)

Now, let us take any

. Then, on account of (
1.11
), (
3.121
)–(
3.124
), and Fatou’s lemma, we can compute that:

\displaystyle\int_{I}[\beta(t)|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})\,dt\leq\int_{I}[(\beta(t)+r)|D\vartheta(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})\,dt

(3.125)

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\sup\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}}[\vartheta(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}\,\mathrm{div}\bigl([\beta(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}+r)\varphi\bigr)\,dx&\parbox{85.35826pt}{$\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{N})$, $|\varphi|\leq 1$ on ${\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}$
}\end{array}\right\}\,dt

(3.127)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\varliminf_{k\to\infty}\left(\sup\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}}[\vartheta_{n_{k}}(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}\,\mathrm{div}\bigl([\beta_{n_{k}}(t)]^{\mathrm{ex}}+r)\varphi\bigr)\,dx&\parbox{85.35826pt}{$\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{N})$, $|\varphi|\leq 1$ on ${\mathbb{B}}_{\Omega}$
}\end{array}\right\}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\varliminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{I}[(\beta_{n_{k}}(t)+r)|D\vartheta_{n_{k}}(t)|]_{\vartheta_{\Gamma,n_{k}}(t)}(\overline{\Omega})\,dt\leq\Phi_{*}+L_{*}r,\mbox{ for any $r>0$.}

(3.130)

\Gamma

6.1
Notations in BV-theory

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
be a fixed dimension, and let
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be an open set. We denote by
\mathscr{M}(U)
(resp.
\mathscr{M}_{\rm loc}(U)
) the space of all finite Radon measures (resp. the space of all Radon measures) on
U
. Recall that the space
\mathscr{M}(U)
(resp.
\mathscr{M}_{\rm loc}(U)
) is the dual of the Banach space
C_{0}(U)
(resp. dual of the locally convex space
C_{\rm c}(U)
), for any open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For a function
u\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(U)
and a point
x_{0}\in U
, we denote by
[\,\widetilde{u}\,](x_{0})
the
approximate limit
of
u
at
x_{0}
, and as well as, we denote by
[\widetilde{u_{+}}](x_{0})
(resp.
[\widetilde{u_{-}}](x_{0})
) the
approximate limit-sup (resp. approximate limit-inf)
of
u
at
x_{0}
(cf.
[
3
, Section 3.6]
).
Besides, we set

S_{u}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}x\in U&[\widetilde{u_{+}}](x)\neq[\widetilde{u_{-}}](x),~\mbox{i.e. $[\widetilde{u_{+}}](x)>[\widetilde{u_{-}}](x)$}\end{array}\right\}.

A function
u\in L^{1}(U)
(resp.
u\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(U)
) is called a function of bounded variation, or a BV-function, (resp. a function of locally bounded variation or a BV
loc
-function) on
U
, iff. its distributional differential
Du
is a finite Radon measure on
U
(resp. a Radon measure on
U
), namely
Du\in\mathscr{[}{M}(U)]^{d}
(resp.
Du\in[\mathscr{M}_{\rm loc}(U)]^{d}
).

We denote by
BV(U)
(resp.
BV_{\rm loc}(U)
) the space of all BV-functions (resp. all BV
loc
-functions) on
U
. For any
u\in BV(U)
, the Radon measure
Du
is called the variation measure of
u
, and its total variation
|Du|
is called the total variation measure of
u
. Additionally, the value
|Du|(U)
, for any
u\in BV(U)
, can be calculated as follows:

|Du|(U)=\sup\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\displaystyle\int_{U}u\ {\rm div}\,\varphi\,dy&\varphi\in[C_{\rm c}^{1}(U)]^{d}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ |\varphi|\leq 1\ \mbox{on}\ U\end{array}\right\}.

The space
BV(U)
is a Banach space, endowed with the following norm:

|u|_{BV(U)}:=|u|_{L^{1}(U)}+|Du|(U),\ \ \mbox{for any}\ u\in BV(U).

Also,
BV(U)
is a complete metric space, endowed with the following distance:

[u,v]\in BV(U)^{2}\mapsto|u-v|_{L^{1}(U)}+\left|\int_{U}|Du|-\int_{U}|Dv|\right|.

The topology provided by this distance is called the
strict topology of
BV(U)
.

Now, by Radon-Nikodým’s theorem
[
3
, Theorem 1.28]
, the measure
Du
for
u\in BV(U)
is decomposed in the absolutely continuous part
D^{a}u
for
\mathcal{L}^{N}
and the singular part
D^{s}u
, i.e.

Du=D^{a}u+D^{s}u\mbox{ \ in $\mathscr{M}(U)$.}

Furthermore, (cf.
[
5
,
3
]
), this decomposition is precisely expressed as follows:

D^{a}u=\nabla u\,\mathcal{L}^{d}\,\lfloor\,(U\setminus S_{u})\mbox{ and }D^{s}u=([\widetilde{u_{+}}]-[\widetilde{u_{-}}])\nu_{u}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\,\lfloor\,S_{u}+D^{s}u\,\lfloor\,(U\setminus S_{u}).

In this context,
\nabla u\in L^{1}(U)
denotes the Radon-Nikodým density of
D^{a}u
for
\mathcal{L}^{d}
. The part
([\widetilde{u_{+}}]-[\widetilde{u_{-}}])\nu_{u}\mathcal{H}^{{d}-1}\,\lfloor\,S_{u}
is called the jump part of
Du
, and it provides an exact expression of the variation at the discontinuities of
u
. The part
D^{s}u\,\lfloor\,(U\setminus S_{u})
is called the Cantor part of
Du
and it is denoted by
D^{c}u
. We denote by SBV the space of special functions of bounded variation; i.e. those BV functions such that
D^{c}u=0
. We note that a function defined as

x\in U\mapsto[u]^{*}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[\,\widetilde{u}\,](x),&\mbox{if $x\in U\setminus S_{u}$,}\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\frac{[\widetilde{u_{+}}](x)+[\widetilde{u_{-}}](x)}{2},&\mbox{if $x\in S_{u}$, and $[\widetilde{u_{+}}](x)$ and $[\widetilde{u_{-}}](x)$ exist,}\\[8.61108pt]
0,&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

provides a precise representative of
u\in BV(U)
,
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e. on
U
.

In particular, if
U
is bounded and the boundary
\partial U
is Lipschitz, then the space
BV(U)
is continuously embedded into
L^{d/(d-1)}(U)
and compactly embedded into
L^{q}(U)
for any
1\leq q<N/(N-1)
(cf.
[
3
, Corollary 3.49]
or
[
5
, Theorems 10.1.3 and 10.1.4]
). Also, there exists a bounded linear operator
\mathfrak{tr}_{{\partial U}}:BV(U)\mapsto L^{1}(\partial U)
, called the trace operator, such that
\mathfrak{tr}_{{\partial U}}\varphi=\varphi|_{\partial U}
on
\partial U
for any
\varphi\in C^{1}(\overline{U})
. The value of trace
\mathfrak{tr}_{{\partial U}}u\in L^{1}(\partial U)
is often abbreviated by
u{{}_{|_{\partial U}}}
. The trace operator
\mathfrak{tr}_{{\partial U}}:BV(U)\mapsto L^{1}(\partial U)
is continuous with respect to the strict topology of
BV(U)
. Namely,
\mathfrak{tr}_{{\partial U}}u_{n}\to\mathfrak{tr}_{{\partial U}}u
in
L^{1}(\partial U)
as
n\to\infty
, if

,
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset BV(U)
and

in the strict topology of

as

. Additionally, if

, then the space

is dense in

for the intermediate convergence (cf.
[
5

such that

in

Generalization of the Apollonius theorem for simplices and related problems
Michael N. Vrahatis

Abstract
. The Apollonius theorem gives the length of a median of a triangle in terms of the lengths of its sides. The straightforward generalization of this theorem obtained for m -simplices in the n -dimensional Euclidean space for n greater than or equal to m is given. Based on this, generalizations of properties related to the medians of a triangle are presented. In addition, applications of the generalized Apollonius’s theorem and the related to the medians results, are given for obtaining: (a) the minimal spherical surface that encloses a given simplex or a given bounded set, (b) the thickness of a simplex that it provides a measure for the quality or how well shaped a simplex is, and (c) the convergence and error estimates of the root-finding bisection method applied on simplices.

2.2.
Generalization of the Apollonius theorem

Apollonius’ theorem
(
cf.
Theorem
1.1
) can be formulated for 2-simplices as follows:

Theorem 2.15

(Apollonius’ theorem for 2-simplices)
.

Let
\sigma^{2}=[{\upsilon}^{0},{\upsilon}^{1},{\upsilon}^{2}]
be a
2
-simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geqslant 2)
.
If
\mu_{i}^{2}
is the
i
-th median of
\sigma^{2}
that corresponds to the vertex
{\upsilon}^{i}
,
i=0,1,2
, it holds that:

(2.4)

2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{2}\|\upsilon^{i}-\upsilon^{j}\|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=0\\
p\neq i\end{subarray}}^{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=p+1\\
q\neq i\end{subarray}}^{2}\|\upsilon^{p}-\upsilon^{q}\|^{2}+2^{2}\,\|\mu_{i}^{2}\|^{2},\quad i=0,1,2.

Example 2.16
.

By setting in Eq. (
2.4
)
i=0
it follows that:

2\bigl(\|{\upsilon}^{0}-{\upsilon}^{1}\|^{2}+\|{\upsilon}^{0}-{\upsilon}^{2}\|^{2}\bigr)=\|{\upsilon}^{1}-{\upsilon}^{2}\|^{2}+2^{2}\,\|\mu_{0}^{2}\|^{2},

(
cf.
Theorem
1.1

Theorem 2.17

(Generalization of the Apollonius theorem for simplices)
.

Assume that
\sigma^{m}=[{\upsilon}^{0},{\upsilon}^{1},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{m}]

is an
m
-simplex in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(n\geqslant m)

with barycenter
\kappa^{m}
.
Assume further that
\kappa_{i}^{m}
is the barycenter of the

i

(m-1)
-face
\sigma^{m}_{\neg i}

\sigma^{m}

\mu_{i}^{m}=[\kappa_{i},\,{\upsilon}^{i}]
be the

{\upsilon}^{i}

Then it holds that:

(2.5)

m\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\|\upsilon^{i}-\upsilon^{j}\|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=0\\
p\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=p+1\\
q\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\|\upsilon^{p}-\upsilon^{q}\|^{2}+m^{2}\|\mu_{i}^{m}\|^{2},\quad i=0,1,\ldots,m.

Proof.

Assume that
{\upsilon}^{i}=({\upsilon}^{i}_{1},{\upsilon}^{i}_{2},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{i}_{n})
is a vertex
of
\sigma^{m}
, then we have

\|\mu_{i}^{m}\|^{2}=\|\kappa_{i}-{\upsilon}^{i}\|^{2}
.
Equivalently, we can obtain

(2.6)

\|\mu_{i}^{m}\|^{2}={\frac{1}{m^{2}}}\,\mathit{\Psi}_{i},

where

\mathit{\Psi}_{i}=\sum^{n}_{t=1}\,\Biggl\{\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}-m{\upsilon}^{i}_{t}\Biggr\}^{2}.

The term
\mathit{\Psi}_{i}
can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\mathit{\Psi}_{i}=\sum^{n}_{t=1}\Biggl\{\biggl[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}\biggr]^{2}+m^{2}\bigl[{\upsilon}^{i}_{t}\bigr]^{2}-2m{\upsilon}^{i}_{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}\Biggr\}\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathit{\Psi}_{i}=\sum^{n}_{t=1}\Biggl\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\bigl[{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}\bigr]^{2}+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=0\\
p\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=p+1\\
q\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}{\upsilon}^{p}_{t}{\upsilon}^{q}_{t}+m^{2}\bigl[{\upsilon}^{i}_{t}\bigr]^{2}-2m{\upsilon}^{i}_{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}\Biggr\},

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\mathit{\Psi}_{i}=\sum^{n}_{t=1}\Biggl\{m\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\bigl[{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}\bigr]^{2}+m^{2}\bigl[{\upsilon}^{i}_{t}\bigr]^{2}-2m{\upsilon}^{i}_{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}-(m-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\bigl[{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}\bigr]^{2}

\displaystyle\kern 52.63777pt+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=0\\
p\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=p+1\\
q\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}{\upsilon}^{p}_{t}{\upsilon}^{q}_{t}\Biggr\}\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathit{\Psi}_{i}=m\sum^{n}_{t=1}\Biggl\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\bigl[{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}\bigr]^{2}+m\bigl[{\upsilon}^{i}_{t}\bigr]^{2}-2{\upsilon}^{i}_{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\kern 52.63777pt-\sum^{n}_{t=1}\Biggl\{(m-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\bigl[{\upsilon}^{l}_{t}\bigr]^{2}-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=0\\
p\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=p+1\\
q\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}{\upsilon}^{p}_{t}{\upsilon}^{q}_{t}\Biggr\},

or equivalently, after some algebraic manipulations, we obtain

(2.7)

\mathit{\Psi}_{i}=m\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\|\upsilon^{i}-\upsilon^{j}\|^{2}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=0\\
p\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=p+1\\
q\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\|\upsilon^{p}-\upsilon^{q}\|^{2}.

Companying Eq. (
2.6
) and Eq. (
2.7
) we have

(2.8)

\|\mu_{i}^{m}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\Biggl(m\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\|\upsilon^{i}-\upsilon^{j}\|^{2}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=0\\
p\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=p+1\\
q\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\|\upsilon^{p}-\upsilon^{q}\|^{2}\Biggr).

Thus the theorem is proved.

Remark 2.21

It is well known the property of a triangle that its three medians concur in the centroid

In 1565 Commandino

2

Theorem 2.23

(Generalization of Commandino’s theorem for simplices)

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem

\displaystyle\kern-56.9055pt\kappa^{m}-{\upsilon}^{i}=\Bigl\{

\displaystyle\Bigl[\frac{1}{m+1}\,\Bigl({\upsilon}^{0}_{1}+{\upsilon}^{1}_{1}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{m}_{1}\Bigr)-{\upsilon}^{i}_{1}\Bigr],\ldots,

\displaystyle\Bigl[\frac{1}{m+1}\,\Bigl({\upsilon}^{0}_{n}+{\upsilon}^{1}_{n}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{m}_{n}\Bigr)-{\upsilon}^{i}_{n}\Bigr]\Bigr\}\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\kappa^{m}-{\upsilon}^{i}=\frac{1}{m+1}\,\Bigl\{

\displaystyle\Bigl({\upsilon}^{0}_{1}+{\upsilon}^{1}_{1}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{i-1}_{1}-m{\upsilon}^{i}_{1}+{\upsilon}^{i+1}_{1}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{m}_{1}\Bigr),\ldots,

\displaystyle\,\Bigl({\upsilon}^{0}_{n}+{\upsilon}^{1}_{n}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{i-1}_{n}-m{\upsilon}^{i}_{n}+{\upsilon}^{i+1}_{n}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{m}_{n}\Bigr)\Bigr\}.

Similarly for the barycenter

of the oppositive to the vertex

(m-1)
-face
\sigma^{m}_{\neg i}
of
\sigma^{m}
we have

\displaystyle\kern 14.22636pt\kappa^{m}_{i}-{\upsilon}^{i}=\Bigl\{

\displaystyle\Bigl[\frac{1}{m}\,\Bigl({\upsilon}^{0}_{1}+{\upsilon}^{1}_{1}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{i-1}_{1}+{\upsilon}^{i+1}_{1}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{m}_{1}\Bigr)-{\upsilon}^{i}_{1}\Bigr],\ldots,

\displaystyle\Bigl[\frac{1}{m}\,\Bigl({\upsilon}^{0}_{n}+{\upsilon}^{1}_{n}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{i-1}_{n}+{\upsilon}^{i+1}_{n}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{m}_{n}\Bigr)-{\upsilon}^{i}_{n}\Bigr]\Bigr\}\Longleftrightarrow

(2.11)

\displaystyle\kappa^{m}_{i}-{\upsilon}^{i}=\frac{1}{m}\,\Bigl\{

\displaystyle\Bigl({\upsilon}^{0}_{1}+{\upsilon}^{1}_{1}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{i-1}_{1}-m{\upsilon}^{i}_{1}+{\upsilon}^{i+1}_{1}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{m}_{1}\Bigr),\ldots,

\displaystyle\,\Bigl({\upsilon}^{0}_{n}+{\upsilon}^{1}_{n}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{i-1}_{n}-m{\upsilon}^{i}_{n}+{\upsilon}^{i+1}_{n}+\cdots+{\upsilon}^{m}_{n}\Bigr)\Bigr\}.

Companying Eq. (
2.10

Thus, the points

of
\sigma^{m}

Next, since

(2.13)

\|\kappa^{m}-{\upsilon}^{i}\|=m\|\kappa^{m}_{i}-\kappa^{m}\|,\quad i=0,1,\ldots,m.

Thus the theorem is proved.

Theorem 2.25

(Median properties for simplices)
.

Let
\sigma^{m}=[{\upsilon}^{0},{\upsilon}^{1},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{m}]

be an
m
-simplex in

\kappa^{m}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}\|\mu_{i}^{m}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{m+1}{m^{2}}\sum_{p=0}^{m-1}\sum_{q=p+1}^{m}\|{\upsilon}^{p}-{\upsilon}^{q}\|^{2},

\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\

-\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=0\\

Theorem 2.28

(Medians of regular simplices)
.

Let
\sigma^{m}=[{\upsilon}^{0},{\upsilon}^{1},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{m}]

be a regular
m
-simplex in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(n\geqslant m)

with edge length
{\rm diam}(\sigma^{m})
and barycenter
\kappa^{m}
.
Let
\kappa_{i}^{m}
be the barycenter of the

i
-th
(m-1)
-face
\sigma^{m}_{\neg i}
of
\sigma^{m}
and

\mu_{i}^{m}=[{\upsilon}^{i},\,\kappa_{i}^{m}]

i
-th median that corresponds to the vertex
{\upsilon}^{i}
for

Then, a) the
i
-th median of
\sigma^{m}
coincides with its corresponding
i
-th altitude,
b)
\sigma^{m}
is orthocentric and c) the orthocenter

of
\sigma^{m}
coincides with its barycenter
\kappa^{m}
.

Proof.

By Eq. (
2.8
) for the
i
-th median we obtain

\|{\upsilon}^{i}-\kappa_{i}^{m}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\left(m^{2}-\binom{m}{2}\right){\rm diam}(\sigma^{m})^{2},\quad i=0,1,\ldots,m,

or equivalently we have

(2.21)

\|{\upsilon}^{i}-\kappa_{i}^{m}\|^{2}=\frac{m+1}{2m}\,{\rm diam}(\sigma^{m})^{2},\quad i=0,1,\ldots,m.

Let
\mu_{ij}^{m}=[{\upsilon}^{j},\,\kappa_{ij}^{m}]
be the
j
-th median,
j=0,1,\ldots,m
,

{\upsilon}^{j}

of the
i
-th
(m-1)
-face
\sigma^{m}_{\neg i}
.
Then, similarly to the above we have

(2.22)

\|{\upsilon}^{j}-\kappa_{ij}^{m}\|^{2}=\frac{m}{2(m-1)}\,{\rm diam}(\sigma^{m})^{2}.

Using Eq. (
2.12
) we obtain

(2.23)

\|{\upsilon}^{j}-\kappa_{i}^{m}\|^{2}=\frac{(m-1)^{2}}{m^{2}}\|{\upsilon}^{j}-\kappa_{ij}^{m}\|^{2}.

Companying Eqs. (
2.22

(2.24)

\|{\upsilon}^{j}-\kappa_{i}^{m}\|^{2}=\frac{m-1}{2m}\,{\rm diam}(\sigma^{m})^{2},\quad j=0,1,\ldots,m,\kern 8.5359ptj\neq i.

\kappa^{m}

3.2.
Simplex thickness

Remark 3.23
.

The thickness of a simplex is the dimensionless quantity that it is determined by
the ratio of the minimal distance from its barycenter to its boundary, to that of the diameter of the simplex.
In general, it provides a measure for the quality or how well shaped a simplex is.

Lemma 3.24

Let
\sigma^{m}

be an
m
-simplex in

\beta_{\rm inr}^{m}=\min_{0\leqslant i\leqslant m}\Bigl\{\min_{x\in{\sigma_{\neg i}^{m}}}\|\kappa^{m}-x\|\Bigr\},

and the thickness of
\sigma^{m}
is as folows:

(3.6)

{\theta}({\sigma}^{m})=\min_{0\leqslant i\leqslant m}\Bigl\{\min_{x\in{\sigma_{\neg i}^{m}}}\|\kappa^{m}-x\|\Bigr\}/{\rm diam}(\sigma^{m}).

Proof.

Since it holds that
{\vartheta}\kern 0.56917pt
{\sigma}^{m}=\cup_{i=0}^{m}\sigma^{m}_{\neg i}
,
the proof can be easily obtained by companying Eq. (
2.2
) and Eq. (

).
∎

Lemma 3.25

In 1958 Eggleston

The twelve possible inequalities are categorized as follows. Specifically, the first four trivial inequalities are as follows:

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm inr}^{n}

\displaystyle{\rm diam}(P)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\rho_{\rm cir}^{n},

\displaystyle{\rm wid}(P)

\displaystyle\leqslant{\rm diam}(P).

The above inequalities imply the following two ones:

(3.12)

\kern 11.38092pt\rho_{\rm inr}^{n}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}{\rm diam}(P),\kern 45.52458pt{\rm wid}(P)\leqslant 2\rho_{\rm cir}^{n}.

(3.13a)

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm cir}^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda\,\rho_{\rm inr}^{n},

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm cir}^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda\,{\rm wid}(P),

(3.13b)

\displaystyle{\rm diam}(P)

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda\,\rho_{\rm inr}^{n},

\displaystyle{\rm diam}(P)

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda\,{\rm wid}(P),

and the remaining two inequalities are quite interesting and have the forms:

(3.14)

\kern 11.38092pt\rho_{\rm cir}^{n}\leqslant\lambda\,{\rm diam}(P),\kern 45.52458pt{\rm wid}(P)\leqslant\lambda\,\rho_{\rm inr}^{n}.

Remark 3.43
.

An
m
-simplex of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is bisected by choosing its longest edge, dividing it in two equal parts and defining two new
m
-simplices.
Each of them will, in turn, be bisected and this process continues for a finite number of iterations.

Definition 3.44

(Simplex bisection
[
45
,
67
,
72
]
)
.

Let

{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}=[{\upsilon}^{0},{\upsilon}^{1},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{m}]

be an
m
-simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geqslant m)
.
Assume that
[{\upsilon}^{i},{\upsilon}^{j}]

is the longest edge of
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}
and
let
M=({\upsilon}^{i}+{\upsilon}^{j})/2

be its midpoint.
Then the
bisection
of
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}
is the order
pair of
m
-simplices

\bigl\{{{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{10}\rangle},\,{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{11}\rangle}}\bigr\}

where:

\displaystyle{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{10}\rangle}=[{\upsilon}^{0},{\upsilon}^{1},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{i-1},M,{\upsilon}^{i+1},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{j},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{m}],

\displaystyle{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{11}\rangle}=[{\upsilon}^{0},{\upsilon}^{1},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{i},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{j-1},M,{\upsilon}^{j+1},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{m}].

The
m
-simplices
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{10}\rangle}
and
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{11}\rangle}

are called
lower simplex
and

upper simplex
respectively corresponding to

{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}
, while both
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{10}\rangle}
and
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{11}\rangle}

are called
elements of the bisection
of
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}
.
Next, each of the elements of the bisection will, in turn, be bisected and the process will be continued to get a sequence of sets of simplices.
In case where one of the elements is selected then this
element is called
selected
m
-simplex produced after one
bisection of

{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}
and it is denoted by

{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{1}\rangle}
.
Assume now that the bisection is applied
with
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{1}\rangle}
replacing
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}

giving thus the
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{2}\rangle}
.
Suppose further that this process continues for
p

iterations. Then the
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}
is called
the
selected
m
-simplex produced after
p

iterations of the bisection of
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}
.

Theorem 3.45

(Kearfott’s theorem (1978)
[
45
]
)
.

Let

{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}=[{\upsilon}^{0},{\upsilon}^{1},\ldots,{\upsilon}^{m}]

be an
m
-simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geqslant m)

and let
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}
be any

-simplex produced after
p

bisections of
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}

(3.18)

{\rm diam}\bigl({\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}\bigr)\leqslant\bigl(\sqrt{3}/2\bigr)^{\lfloor p/m\rfloor}{\rm diam}\bigl({\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}\bigr),

where
{\rm diam}\bigl({\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}\bigr)
and
{\rm diam}\bigl({\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}\bigr)
are the diameters
of
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}
and
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}
respectively and

.

Lemma 3.46
.

Assume that
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}
,
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}
,

{\rm diam}\bigl({\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}\bigr)
and
{\rm diam}\bigl({\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}\bigr)
are
as in Theorem
3.45

\kappa^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}

and

{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}
respectively. Then for any point

in
{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}

(3.19)

\|x-\kappa^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}\|\leqslant\frac{m}{m+1}\bigl(\sqrt{3}/2\bigr)^{\lfloor p/m\rfloor}{\rm diam}\bigl({\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}\bigr).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.3
we obtain that

\|x-\kappa^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}\|\leqslant\beta_{\rm cir}^{m}\bigl({\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}\bigr)\leqslant\frac{1}{m+1}\Bigl(m^{2}\,{\rm diam}\bigl({\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{p}\rangle}\bigr)^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2},

{\sigma}^{m}_{\langle{0}\rangle}

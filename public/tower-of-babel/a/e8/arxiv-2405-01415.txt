Abstract
. We study the L ∞ ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) boundedness for Riesz transforms of the form V a ​ ( − 1 2 ​ Δ + V ) − a , {V^{a}(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+V)^{-a}}, where a > 0 a>0 and V V is a non-negative potential with power growth acting independently on each coordinate. We factorize the semigroup e − t ​ L e^{-tL} into one-dimensional factors, estimate them separately and combine the results to estimate the original semigroup. Similar results with additional assumption a ⩽ 1 a\leqslant 1 are obtained on L 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) .

1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the Schrödinger operator
L
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

L=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+V,

with
V
being a non-negative potential, and the associated Riesz transform

(1.1)

R_{V}^{a}f(x)=V(x)^{a}L^{-a}f(x)=\frac{V(x)^{a}}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tL}f(x)\,t^{a-1}\,dt,\quad a>0.

Such Riesz transforms related to Schrödinger operators have been studied by numerous authors, see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
13
,
14
]
. For general
V\in L^{2}_{\textrm{loc}}
Sikora proved in
[
13
, Theorem 1]
that
R_{V}^{1/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for
1<p\leqslant 2
(in fact the result applies not only to Riesz transforms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
but also on more general doubling spaces), it is also well known that
R_{V}^{1}
is bounded on
L^{1}
with norm estimated by 1, see for example
[
5
]
,
[
8
, Lemma 6]
and
[
2
, Theorem 4.3]
. When the potential
V
belongs to the reverse Hölder class
B_{q}
for some
q\geqslant\frac{d}{2}
, then it is known, see
[
12
, Theorem 5.10]
, that
R_{V}^{1}
is bounded on
L^{1}
. There are also two results regarding polynomial potentials, namely Dziubański
[
3
, Theorem 4.5]
proved that
R_{V}^{a}
,
a>0
, is bounded on
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
if
V
is a polynomial and then Urban and Zienkiewicz proved in
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
that
R_{V}^{1}
is bounded on
L^{\infty}
independently of the dimension for
V
being a polynomial satisfying a certain condition of C. Fefferman. Recently it has been proved in
[
10
]
that
R_{V}^{a}
is bounded on
L^{p}
with
0\leqslant a\leqslant 1/p
and
1<p\leqslant 2
for general
V\in L^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}
and that
R_{V}^{a}
,
a>0
, is bounded on
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
if the potential
V
has polynomial or exponential growth.

Obtaining dimension-free bounds for the Riesz transforms related to Schrödinger operators seems to be a significantly harder task. The only available results are the aforementioned paper by Urban and Zienkiewicz
[
14
]
, the well-known bound for
R_{V}^{1}
for general potentials and a result regarding a particular case of
R_{V}^{1/2}
with
V(x)=\absolutevalue{x}^{2}
, see
[
6
,
11
,
9
]
. Our goal is to extend these dimension-free results and get
L^{\infty}
bounds for
R_{V}^{a}
with
a>0
and
L^{1}
bounds for
R_{V}^{a}
with
a\leqslant 1
when the potential
V
is of the form

(1.2)

V(x)=V_{1}(x)+\dots+V_{d}(x),

where each
V_{i}
acts only on the
i
-th coordinate of the argument
x
and has polynomial growth with the exponent not greater than 2, i.e. there are absolute constants
m
and
M
such that

(1.3)

m\absolutevalue{x_i}^{\alpha}\leqslant V_{i}(x)\leqslant M\absolutevalue{x_i}^{\alpha}

for some
0<\alpha\leqslant 2
. This holds for example if
V_{i}(x)=x_{i}^{2}
and
V(x)=\absolutevalue{x}^{2}
, which results in the operator
L=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\absolutevalue{x}^{2}
called the harmonic oscillator. The reason why we can only handle
\alpha\leqslant 2
is related to the distribution of the Brownian motion, which arises in the Feynman–Kac formula (
2.3
), and is visible in (
3.10
).

By the definition (
1.1
) of
R_{V}^{a}
and the positivity-preserving property of the semigroup
e^{-tL}
obtaining the
L^{\infty}
bounds for
R_{V}^{a}
amounts to estimating the value of
R_{V}^{a}(\mathbbm{1})(x)
independently of
x
and
d
, which in turn hints that the main part of the proof is estimating the semigroup applied to the constant function 1, i.e.
e^{-tL}(\mathbbm{1})
. The particular structure of
V
(
1.2
) lets us write

(1.4)

L=\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{i},\quad\text{where }L_{i}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+V_{i},

and, as a consequence, factorize the semigroup
e^{-tL}
in the following way

(1.5)

e^{-tL}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}e^{-tL_{i}}\quad\text{and hence}\quad e^{-tL}(\mathbbm{1})(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x).

This is the key property allowing us to get estimates that does not depend on the dimension
d
.

The main result of the paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
0<\alpha\leqslant 2
and let
V
given by (
1.2
) satisfy (
1.3
). For
a>0
let the Riesz transform
R_{V}^{a}
be defined as in (
1.1
). Then there is a constant
C>0
depending on
m
,
M
, and
\alpha
and independent of the dimension
d
such that

\norm{R_V^a f}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant C\norm{f}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

As a by-product of our considerations we also obtain
L^{1}
estimates for
R_{V}^{a}
, but only for a limited range of
a
. The reason for this is that we need to use concavity of the function
x^{a}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
0<\alpha\leqslant 2
and let
V
given by (
1.2
) satisfy (
1.3
). For
0<a\leqslant 1
let the Riesz transform
R_{V}^{a}
be defined as in (
1.1
). Then there is a constant
C>0
depending on
m
,
M
, and
\alpha
and independent of the dimension
d
such that

\norm{R_V^a f}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant C\norm{f}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Remark
.

For technical reasons we will assume that
d\geqslant 3
. The case of
d=1,2
follows from previous results, e.g.
[
10
]
.

1.1.
Structure and methods

The main part of the proof is contained in Section
3
where we prove that the one-dimensional semigroups
e^{-tL_{i}}
decay exponentially in
t
and
V(x)
for small values of
t
, i.e. we have

e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant e^{-c_{N}tV_{i}(x)}\quad\textrm{for }t\leqslant N.

It is noteworthy that the constant in front of the exponential in the above estimate is 1, which means that we can multiply one-dimensional bounds to estimate the full semigroup
e^{-tL}
without constants growing with the dimension. The proof is divided into three cases depending on the value of
\absolutevalue{x_i}
and
tV_{i}(x)
but in all of them the main ingredient is the Feynman–Kac formula (
2.3
).

In Section
4
we use results from Section
3
and a similar result
[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
giving an exponential decay of the semigroup for large values of
t
, namely

e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant e^{-ct}\quad\textrm{for }t\geqslant N,

to estimate the
L^{\infty}
norm of
R_{V}^{a}
.

Finally in Section
5
we estimate the
L^{1}
norm of the Riesz transform. We use duality between
L^{\infty}
and
L^{1}
which reduces estimating the
L^{1}
norm of the operator
R_{V}^{a}=V^{a}L^{-a}
to estimating the
L^{\infty}
norm of the adjoint operator

(L^{-a}V^{a})f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tL}(V^{a}f)(x)\,t^{a-1}\,dt.

Again, using the positivity-preserving property of the semigroup
e^{-tL}
reduces the task to estimating
e^{-tL}(V^{a})
. In this case, although the factorization (
1.5
) of the semigroup as an operator still applies, it does not behave well when the semigroup is applied to
V^{a}
instead of the constant function, so we use the following formula

e^{-tL}(V)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{-tL}(V_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{-tL^{i}}(\mathbbm{1})\,e^{-tL_{i}}(V_{i}),\quad\text{where }L^{i}=L-L_{i}.

2.
Definitions

We begin by defining the semigroup
e^{-tL}
and then we proceed to defining the Riesz transform
R_{V}^{a}
. By the result of Kato
[
7
, p. 137]
the operator
L=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+V
is essentially self-adjoint on
C_{c}^{\infty}
and hence it has a non-negative self-adjoint extension. This in turn means that
L
generates a strongly continuous semigroup of contractions on
L^{2}
which can be expressed using the Feynman–Kac formula

(2.1)

e^{-tL}f(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V(X_{s})\,ds}f(X_{t})\right],\quad f\in L^{2},

where the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{x}
is taken with respect to the Wiener measure of the standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion
\{X_{s}\}_{s>0}
starting at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};
here
X_{s}=(X_{s}^{1},\ldots,X_{s}^{d}).
Since the right-hand makes sense also for
f\in L^{\infty}
, we use the Feynman–Kac formula to define
e^{-tL}
acting on
L^{\infty}
as

(2.2)

e^{-tL}f(x)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V(X_{s})\,ds}f(X_{t})\right],\quad f\in L^{\infty}.

Similarly, using the fact that
V
, and hence
L
, act on each coordinate separately, see (
1.2
) and (
1.4
), we define one-dimensional semigroups
e^{-tL_{i}}
,
i=1,\dots,d
, as follows

(2.3)

e^{-tL_{i}}f(x)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds}f_{x^{i}}(X_{t}^{i})\right],\quad f\in L^{\infty},

where

f_{x^{i}}(y)=f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},y,x_{i+1},\dots,x_{d}).

Here the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}
is taken with regards to the Wiener measure of the standard one-dimensional Brownian motion
\{X_{s}^{i}\}_{s>0}
starting at
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.

As the next lemma shows, this is the definition that suits best our purpose of factorizing the semigroup
e^{-tL}
into one-dimensional factors
e^{-tL_{i}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix
d
and let the
d
-dimensional semigroup
e^{-tL}
be given by (
2.2
) and the one-dimensional semigroup
e^{-tL_{i}}
by (
2.3
). Then for
f\in L^{\infty}
we have

(2.4)

e^{-tL}f(x)=\left(\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}e^{-tL_{i}}\right)f\right)(x)\quad\text{and}\quad e^{-tL}(\mathbbm{1})(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\right).

Proof.

We will prove by induction that for
k=1,\dots,d
we have

(2.5)

\left(\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}e^{-tL_{i}}\right)f\right)(x)=\mathbb{E}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds}f(X_{t}^{1},\dots,X_{t}^{k},x_{k+1},\dots,x_{d})\right],

which justifies the first formula in (
2.4
) if we take
k=d
.

The case
k=1
is clear from the definition (
2.3
) of
e^{-tL_{1}}
. Now suppose that (
2.5
) holds. Then

\displaystyle\left(\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}e^{-tL_{i}}\right)f\right)(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x_{k+1}}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{k+1}(X_{s})\,ds}\left(\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}e^{-tL_{i}}\right)f\right)_{x^{k+1}}(X_{t}^{k+1})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x_{k+1}}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{k+1}(X_{s})\,ds}\,\mathbb{E}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds}f(X_{t}^{1},\dots,X_{t}^{k},X_{t}^{k+1},x_{k+2},\dots,x_{d})\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k+1})}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds}f(X_{t}^{1},\dots,X_{t}^{k+1},x_{k+2},\dots,x_{d})\right].

Note that we can use the same Brownian motion in the inner and in the outer expected value since its coordinates are independent of each other and
V_{i}(X_{s})
depends only on
X_{s}^{i}
.

The second formula in (
2.4
) follows from the definitions of
e^{-tL}
and
e^{-tL_{i}}
and the fact that the coordinates of
d
-dimensional Brownian motion are independent.
∎

Now we take
a>0
and a non-negative function
f\in L^{\infty}
and define the Riesz transform

(2.6)

R_{V}^{a}f(x)=\frac{V(x)^{a}}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tL}f(x)\,t^{a-1}\,dt,

where
e^{-tL}f(x)
is defined as in (
2.2
). Lastly, we use the positivity-preserving property of the semigroup
e^{-tL}
, which means that
e^{-tL}f\geqslant 0
whenever
f\geqslant 0
, to rewrite the main theorem in a simpler form. Namely, we have

\absolutevalue{e^{-tL}f(x)}\leqslant e^{-tL}\left(\norm{f}_{\infty}\mathbbm{1}\right)(x)=\norm{f}_{\infty}\,e^{-tL}(\mathbbm{1})(x),\quad f\in L^{\infty},

which means that the Riesz transform
R_{V}^{a}
is bounded on
L^{\infty}
if

\norm{R_V^a(\ind{})}<\infty

with its norm being

\norm{R_V^a}_{\infty\to\infty}=\norm{R_V^a(\ind{})}_{\infty}.

Thus, ?THM?
can be rewritten as

Theorem 2.2
.

Fix
0<\alpha\leqslant 2
and let
V
given by (
1.2
) satisfy (
1.3
). For
a>0
let the Riesz transform
R_{V}^{a}
be defined as in (
2.6
). Then there is a constant
C>0
depending on
m
,
M
, and
\alpha
and independent of the dimension
d
such that

\norm{R_V^a (\ind{})}_{\infty}\leqslant C.

3.
One-dimensional estimates

In this section we prove the aforementioned exponential decay of the one-dimensional semigroup which we will then combine to estimate the semigroup
e^{-tL}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
N>0
there is a constant
c_{N}>0
such that

(3.1)

e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant e^{-c_{N}tV_{i}(x)}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0\leqslant t\leqslant N
. Moreover, if
\absolutevalue{x_i}\leqslant 4
, then

(3.2)

e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant e^{-c_{N}\left(t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}+tV_{i}(x)\right)},\quad t\leqslant N.

Proof.

First we will show that (
3.1
) is satisfied for
0\leqslant t\leqslant t_{0}
for some
t_{0}
and then we will extend the estimate to all
0\leqslant t\leqslant N
.

We begin with the case
\absolutevalue{x_i}\leqslant 4
. We will make use of the inequality

(3.3)

e^{-x}\leqslant 1-x+\frac{x^{2}}{2},\qquad x\geqslant 0.

The Feynman–Kac formula (
2.3
) together with (
3.3
) give

(3.4)

e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant 1-\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds\right]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds\right)^{2}\right].

We need to estimate the first and the second expected value in the expression above. In order to do this we will use the fact that for any
a,b\geqslant 0
and
\alpha>0
we have

(3.5)

(a+b)^{\alpha}\approx a^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha},

and an estimate for the moments of normal distribution

(3.6)

\mathbb{E}\absolutevalue{X_s^i}^{\alpha}\approx s^{\alpha/2}.

Let us begin by estimating
\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}V_{i}(X_{s})
from below and assume without loss of generality that
x_{i}\geqslant 0
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\,V_{i}(X_{s})\gtrsim\mathbb{E}_{0}\absolutevalue{X_s^i + x_i}^{\alpha}\geqslant\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{s}^{i}\geqslant 0\}}(X_{s}^{i}+x_{i})^{\alpha}\right]\approx s^{\alpha/2}+x_{i}^{\alpha}

Integrating this gives

\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds\right]\gtrsim t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}+tx_{i}^{\alpha}.

Now we estimate the last term in (
3.4
) using Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\absolutevalue{X_s^i + x_i}^{2\alpha}\right]ds\lesssim t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\absolutevalue{X_s^i}^{2\alpha}+x_{i}^{2\alpha}\right]ds

\displaystyle\approx t\int_{0}^{t}s^{\alpha}+x_{i}^{2\alpha}ds\approx t^{\alpha+2}+t^{2}x_{i}^{2\alpha}\lesssim\left(t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}+tx_{i}^{\alpha}\right)^{2}.

Plugging this into (
3.4
), recalling that
\absolutevalue{x_i}\leqslant 4
, and choosing
t_{0}
sufficiently small yields

\displaystyle e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant 1-c_{1}\left(t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}+tx_{i}^{\alpha}\right)+c_{2}\left(t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}+tx_{i}^{\alpha}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 1-c\left(t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}+tx_{i}^{\alpha}\right)\leqslant e^{-c\left(t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}+tx_{i}^{\alpha}\right)}

which implies (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) for
t\leqslant t_{0}
.

The second case is when
\absolutevalue{x_i}>4
and
tV_{i}(x)\leqslant 2A\log 5
, where
A=\frac{2^{\alpha}M}{m}
with
m
and
M
as in (
1.3
). We will roughly show that then we have

(3.7)

\frac{d}{dt}e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)=-e^{-tL_{i}}(V_{i})(x)\leqslant-cV_{i}(x).

However since the equality may not hold, we replace
V_{i}
with
V_{i}^{n}(x)=\min(V_{i}(x),n)
for any
n>0
, establish (
3.7
) for
V_{i}^{n}
, then we prove (
3.1
) for
V_{i}^{n}
and finally we deduce (
3.1
) for
V_{i}
.

Recall that
V_{i}
satisfies
m\absolutevalue{x_i}^{\alpha}\leqslant V_{i}(x)\leqslant M\absolutevalue{x_i}^{\alpha}
and take
x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\absolutevalue{x_i-y_i}\leqslant\frac{\absolutevalue{x_i}}{2}
. Then
\frac{\absolutevalue{x_i}}{2}\leqslant\absolutevalue{y_i}\leqslant 2\absolutevalue{x_i}
so that we have

V_{i}(y)\leqslant M\absolutevalue{y_i}^{\alpha}\leqslant 2^{\alpha}M\absolutevalue{x_i}^{\alpha}\leqslant\frac{2^{\alpha}M}{m}V_{i}(x)=AV_{i}(x)

and

V_{i}(y)\geqslant m\absolutevalue{y_i}^{\alpha}\geqslant m\frac{\absolutevalue{x_i}^{\alpha}}{2^{\alpha}}\geqslant\frac{m}{2^{\alpha}M}V_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{A}V_{i}(x)

We also calculate the probability that
\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\absolutevalue{X_s^i-x_i}\geqslant\frac{\absolutevalue{x_i}}{2}
using the reflection principle to get

(3.8)

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\absolutevalue{X_s^i-x_i}\geqslant\frac{\absolutevalue{x_i}}{2}\right)\leqslant 4e^{-\frac{\absolutevalue{x_i}^{2}}{8t}}.

Now, for
n>0
, we define
V_{i}^{n}(x)=\min(V_{i}(x),n)
and
L_{i}^{n}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+V_{i}^{n}
and use the Feynman–Kac formula and (
3.8
) to get

\displaystyle e^{-tL_{i}^{n}}(V_{i}^{n})(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}^{n}(X_{s})\,ds}V_{i}^{n}(X_{t})\right]\geqslant\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds}V_{i}^{n}(X_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\forall_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\ \tfrac{V_{i}^{n}(x)}{A}\leqslant V_{i}^{n}(X_{s})\text{ and }V_{i}(X_{s})\leqslant AV_{i}(x)\right)\frac{V_{i}^{n}(x)}{A}e^{-AtV_{i}(x)}

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\forall_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\ \tfrac{V_{i}(x)}{A}\leqslant V_{i}(X_{s})\leqslant AV_{i}(x)\right)\frac{V_{i}^{n}(x)}{A}e^{-AtV_{i}(x)}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{V_{i}^{n}(x)}{A}\left(1-8e^{-\frac{\absolutevalue{x_i}^{2}}{8t}}\right)e^{-2A^{2}\log 5}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{V_{i}^{n}(x)}{A}\left(1-8e^{-\frac{4^{2}}{8t_{0}}}\right)e^{-2A^{2}\log 5}\geqslant cV_{i}^{n}(x)

if
t_{0}
is sufficiently small, which proves that

(3.9)

\frac{d}{dt}e^{-tL_{i}^{n}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)=-e^{-tL_{i}^{n}}(V_{i}^{n})(x)\leqslant-cV_{i}^{n}(x).

Differentiation is allowed here by the Leibniz integral rule. Now we show that this implies a version of (
3.1
) with
V_{i}^{n}
. Consider the function

f(t)=e^{-tL_{i}^{n}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\,e^{ctV_{i}^{n}(x)}.

If we differentiate it and use (
3.9
), we get

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}e^{-tL_{i}^{n}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\,e^{ctV_{i}^{n}(x)}+cV_{i}^{n}(x)\,e^{-tL_{i}^{n}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\,e^{ctV_{i}^{n}(x)}

\displaystyle\leqslant-cV_{i}^{n}(x)\,e^{ctV_{i}^{n}(x)}+cV_{i}^{n}(x)\,e^{ctV_{i}^{n}(x)}=0.

Since
f(0)=1
, we conclude that

e^{-tL_{i}^{n}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant e^{-ctV_{i}^{n}(x)}.

Now we take the limit as
n
goes to infinity on both sides of the inequality. The left-hand side becomes

\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{-tL_{i}^{n}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}^{n}(X_{s})\,ds}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds}\right]=e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x).

Passing with the limit under the integral sign is allowed since the integrand is dominated by the constant function
\mathbbm{1}
which is integrable.
On the right-hand side we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{-ctV_{i}^{n}(x)}=e^{-ctV_{i}(x)},

so altogether we get (
3.1
).

The last case to consider is
\absolutevalue{x_i}>4
and
tV_{i}(x)>2A\log 5
. We choose sufficiently small
t_{0}
and use (
3.8
) to obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-\frac{tV_{i}(x)}{A}}\mathbb{P}\left(\forall_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\ \tfrac{V_{i}(x)}{A}\leqslant V_{i}(X_{s})\right)+1\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\exists_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\ \tfrac{V_{i}(x)}{A}>V_{i}(X_{s})\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-\frac{tV_{i}(x)}{A}}+4e^{-\frac{\absolutevalue{x_i}^{2}}{8t}}\leqslant 5e^{-\frac{tV_{i}(x)}{A}}\leqslant e^{-\frac{tV_{i}(x)}{2A}},

which is (
3.1
). In the second-to-last inequality we used the assumption
\alpha\leqslant 2
.

Recall that we have just proved that

e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant e^{-ctV_{i}(x)}

is satisfied for
t\leqslant t_{0}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
N\leqslant t_{0}
, then the proof is finished, so suppose that
N>t_{0}
and take
t\in[t_{0},N]
. Then we have

e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant e^{-t_{0}L_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant e^{-ct_{0}V_{i}(x)}=e^{-c\frac{t_{0}}{t}tV_{i}(x)}\leqslant e^{-c\frac{t_{0}}{N}tV_{i}(x)}=e^{-c_{N}tV_{i}(x)}.

The inequality (
3.2
) can be extended to
t\in[0,N]
in a very similar way. Suppose that
N>t
and take
t\in[t_{0},N]
. Then

e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant e^{-c\left(t_{0}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}+t_{0}V_{i}(x)\right)}\leqslant e^{-c\left(\left(\frac{t_{0}}{N}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}+\frac{t_{0}}{N}tV_{i}(x)\right)}=e^{-c_{N}\left(t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}+tV_{i}(x)\right)}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

4.
L^{\infty}
dimension-free estimates — proof of ?THM?

In this section we prove ?THM?
using one-dimensional estimates from ?THM?
. The other relevant result is
[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
, which guarantees that there exist universal constants
C>0
and
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that

for all

and

. This in turn means, thanks to (
2.4
), that we have

(4.1)

e^{-tL}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant e^{-d\delta t}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\geqslant N
, where
N>0
and
\delta>0
are universal constants.

First we estimate the upper part of the integral in (
2.6
), i.e. the integral from
N
to
\infty
, dividing the calculations into two cases depending on the value of
a
. If

\displaystyle V(x)^{a}\int_{N}^{\infty}

\displaystyle e^{-tL}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\,t^{a-1}\,dt\leqslant V(x)^{a}\,e^{-\frac{N}{2}L}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\int_{N}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t}{2}\delta d}\,t^{a-1}\,dt

for
a\leqslant 1
and
x_{i}\geqslant 0
.

If, on the other hand,

\displaystyle V(x)^{a}\int_{N}^{\infty}

\displaystyle e^{-tL}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\,t^{a-1}\,dt\leqslant V(x)^{a}\,e^{-\frac{N}{2}L}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\int_{N}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t}{2}\delta d}\,t^{a-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(\delta d)^{a}}V(x)^{a}\,e^{-\frac{N}{2}L}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\lesssim\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}(x)^{a}\,e^{-\frac{N}{2}L_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x).

Here in the last inequality we used that fact that

\left(x_{1}+\dots+x_{d}\right)^{a}\leqslant d^{a-1}\left(x_{1}^{a}+\dots+x_{d}^{a}\right)

for
a\geqslant 1
and
x_{i}\geqslant 0
, which follows from Jensen’s inequality or Hölder’s inequality.
Thus, we have reduced our problem to the one-dimensional case of estimating

which can be done by invoking (

(4.2)

V_{i}(x)^{a}\,e^{-\frac{N}{2}L_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\leqslant V_{i}(x)^{a}\,e^{-\frac{N}{2}c_{N}V_{i}(x)}\leqslant\left(\frac{2a}{Nc_{N}e}\right)^{a}

and then integrate

This completes the proof of ?THM?

5.
L^{1}
dimension-free estimates

In this section we will again use the one-dimensional estimates for the semigroups
e^{-tL_{i}}
to prove dimension-free estimates of the
L^{1}
norm of
R_{V}^{a}
for
0<a\leqslant 1
. The idea is to estimate the
L^{\infty}
norm of the adjoint operator formally given by

(L^{-a}V^{a})f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tL}(V^{a}f)(x)\,t^{a-1}\,dt.

As before, the positivity-preserving property of
e^{-tL}
lets us reduce the task to estimating the
L^{\infty}
norm of

(5.1)

L^{-a}(V^{a})(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tL}(V^{a})(x)\,t^{a-1}\,dt.

However, since
V^{a}
may be unbounded, it is not clear if the integral above is a measurable function of
x
. The issue was addressed in detail in
[
10
, Section 5]
. Briefly, we define

(5.2)

L^{-a}(V^{a})(x)\coloneqq\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tL}(V^{a}\mathbbm{1}_{\absolutevalue{V}<N})(x)\,t^{a-1}\,e^{-t/N}\,dt

and we see that each integral is finite and measurable by
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
, hence the limit is also measurable. Later it will turn out that the integral in (
5.1
) is finite, which lets us handle (
5.1
) instead of using (
5.2
). As in the
L^{\infty}
case this lets us reformulate ?THM?
in the following way

Theorem 5.1
.

Fix
0<\alpha\leqslant 2
and let
V
given by (
1.2
) satisfy (
1.3
). For
0<a\leqslant 1
let the Riesz transform
R_{V}^{a}
be defined as in (
2.6
). Then there is a constant
C>0
depending on
m
,
M
, and
\alpha
and independent of the dimension
d
such that

\norm{L^{-a} (V^a)}_{\infty}\leqslant C.

Before we move to the proof, we need two general results regarding the semigroup
e^{-tL}
. The first one is a factorization property for
e^{-tL}(V)

(5.3)

e^{-tL}(V)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{-tL}(V_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{-tL^{i}}(\mathbbm{1})\,e^{-tL_{i}}(V_{i}),\quad\text{where }L^{i}=L-L_{i}.

The second one is an estimate for

\displaystyle e^{-tL_{i}}(V_{i}^{a})(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds}\,V_{i}(X_{t})^{a}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s}+x)\,ds}\,V_{i}(X_{t})^{a}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s}+x)\,ds}\,V_{i}(x)^{a}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[V_{i}(X_{t})^{a}\right]+V_{i}(x)^{a}\,\mathbb{E}_{x_{i}}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(X_{s})\,ds}\right]

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{a\alpha}{2}}+V_{i}(x)^{a}\,e^{-tL_{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x),

valid for
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Here we used estimate (
1.3
) for
V
and (
3.5
).
Now we are in position to prove ?THM?

Proof of ?THM?
.

We begin with the upper part of the integral in (

), i.e. the integral from
N
to
\infty
. Using subadditivity of the function

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{N}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{-tL^{i}}(\mathbbm{1})(x)\,e^{-tL_{i}}(V_{i}^{a})(x)\,t^{a-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{N}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{-t\delta(d-1)}e^{-tL_{i}}(V_{i}^{a})(x)\,t^{a-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant N^{a-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{N}^{\infty}\norm{e^{-tL_i}(V_i^a)}_{\infty}e^{-t\delta(d-1)}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{d}\norm{e^{-NL_i}(V_i^a)}_{\infty}\int_{N}^{\infty}e^{-t\delta(d-1)}\,dt

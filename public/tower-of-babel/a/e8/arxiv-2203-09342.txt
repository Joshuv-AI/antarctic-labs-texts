A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of non-trivial S n S_{n} -invariants in the splitting algebra
Kevin Schlegel

Abstract
For a monic polynomial f f over a commutative, unitary ring A A the splitting algebra A f A_{f} is the universal A A -algebra such that f f splits in A f A_{f} . The symmetric group acts on the splitting algebra by permuting the roots of f f . It is known that if the intersection of the annihilators of the elements 2 2 and D f D_{f} (where D f D_{f} depends on f f ) in A A is zero, then the invariants under the group action are exactly equal to A A . We show that the converse holds.



Introduction

Given a commutative, unitary ring
A
and a monic polynomial
f
of degree
n
with coefficients in
A
, we can construct the
splitting algebra

A_{f}=A[\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{n}]
such that
f
splits in
A_{f}
with the
universal roots

\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{n}
. The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
A_{f}
by permuting the universal roots.

One can show that the polynomial ring
A[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]
of degree
n
over
A
can be viewed as a splitting algebra with the universal roots
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}
. Further, if
A
is a field one can show that the splitting algebra
A_{f}
modulo an ideal is isomorphic to the splitting field of
f
. This yields an approach to symmetric functions and Galois theory using splitting algebras (see
[
EL
]
).

In both cases the elements in
A_{f}
that are invariant under the action of
S_{n}
are of particular interest. It is always true that the elements in
A\subseteq A_{f}
are invariants.

Question.
When exactly are there no elements in
A_{f}\backslash A
that are invariant under the action of
S_{n}
?

As it turns out, this solely depends on the annihilators of the elements
2
and
D_{f}:=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\tau_{i}+\tau_{j})
in
A
. As shown in
[
Th
, Theorem 7, p. 4]
if their intersection is zero, then the only invariants are
A
. We show that the converse holds as well:

Main theorem.

The elements in
A_{f}
that are invariant under the action of
S_{n}
are exactly the elements in
A
if and only if

\displaystyle\text{Ann}_{A}\,2\cap\text{Ann}_{A}\,D_{f}=0.

(
\ast
)

1.2 Construction.
Let

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{i-1}&:=A_{i}[\tau_{i}]\\

In the following we fix the ring

1.3 Remark.
Let

1.4 Remark.
By the construction,

is a free

\displaystyle B:=\{\tau_{1}^{m_{1}}\tau_{2}^{m_{2}}\dots\tau_{n}^{m_{n}}\mid 0\leq m_{i}<i\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n\}.

1.5 The universal property.

For every
A
-algebra
\varphi:A\rightarrow B
such that
\varphi(f)
splits in
B
with some roots
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{n}

A
-algebra homomorphism
\psi:A_{f}\rightarrow B

\tau_{i}\mapsto\nu_{i}
for

.

Proof.
We prove the existence of

by induction on

. For

and chose

.

For

consider the
A
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi^{\prime}:A[\tau_{n}]\rightarrow B

\tau_{n}\mapsto\nu_{n}
. Since
\varphi(f)(\nu_{n})=0
,
\varphi^{\prime}
is well-defined. Hence, we can view

as an

-algebra and
\varphi^{\prime}(f_{n-1})
splits in

\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{n-1}
. By induction, there exists an

-algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\psi:A[\tau_{n}]_{f_{n-1}}\longrightarrow B

\tau_{i}\mapsto\nu_{i}
for

. Since

, it follows that
\psi
is the desired

-algebra homomorphism. Because
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{n}
generate

as an

-algebra,
\psi
is unique. ∎

Let

be a subgroup of

. For

2
The generic splitting algebra

In this section the polynomial ring
A[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]
of degree
n
is constructed as a splitting algebra as in
[
EL
]
. By that, we are able to introduce a new concept of
trivial
G
-invariants
for an arbitrary subgroup
G
of
S_{n}
that will be important for the proof of the main theorem.

2.1 Definition.
Let
g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n}
be independent variables over
A
and consider the polynomial
g:=t^{n}+g_{1}t^{n-1}+\dots+g_{n}
. The
generic splitting algebra (of degree
n
over
A
)
is the splitting algebra of
g
over
A[g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n}]
and it is denoted by
\Omega_{n}(A)
.

Further, we denote the universal roots of
\Omega_{n}(A)
by
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{n}
and for a subgroup
G
of
S_{n}
we write
\Omega_{n}^{G}(A)
for the
G
-invariants in
\Omega_{n}(A)
.

2.2 Lemma.

Let
B
and
C
be
A
-algebras,
\varphi:B\rightarrow C
an
A
-algebra homomorphism and

a polynomial of degree
n
with coefficients in
B
. If
\varphi(h)
splits with some roots
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{n}
, then there is a commutative diagram of
A
-algebra homomorphisms

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A[g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{n}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

that is uniquely determined by
\alpha(g)=h
and
\psi(\omega_{i})=\nu_{i}
for all

.

Proof.
Because
g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n}
are independent variables over

, we may chose

\alpha(g)=h
. Now consider

as an

. Since
\varphi\alpha(g)=\varphi(h)

we obtain the desired

2.3 Proposition.

Let
A[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]
be the polynomial ring of degree
n
over

.Then there is an
A
-algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle\beta:A[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]\longrightarrow\Omega_{n}(A),t_{i}\mapsto\omega_{i}.

Proof.
We chose
B:=C:=A[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]
and
h:=(t-t_{1})(t-t_{2})\dots(t-t_{n})

in Lemma 2.2. This yields an
A
-algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\psi:\Omega_{n}(A)\longrightarrow A[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}],\omega_{i}\mapsto t_{i}.

Clearly,
\beta
and
\psi
are mutually inverse.∎

2.4 Definition.
We choose
B:=A,C:=A_{f}
and
h:=f
in Lemma 2.2 to obtain an
A
-algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\psi:\Omega_{n}(A)\longrightarrow A_{f},\omega_{i}\mapsto\tau_{i}.

For a subgroup

of

we define the
A
-algebra
A_{f}^{G,\text{tr}}:=\psi\Omega_{n}^{G}
and call the elements in

-invariants
. Clearly,
\psi
and
\sigma
commute for all
\sigma
in
S_{n}
and it follows that
A_{f}^{G,\text{tr}}
is contained in
A_{f}^{G}
. We call the elements in
A_{f}^{G}\backslash A_{f}^{G,\text{tr}}

non-trivial
G
-invariants.

In the following we fix
\psi:\Omega_{n}(A)\longrightarrow A_{f}
as in the above definition.

2.5 Remark.
Let

be subgroups of

and let

. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma A_{f}^{G,\text{tr}}&=A_{f}^{\sigma G\sigma^{-1},\text{tr}}\\

\Sigma_{G}^{H}A_{f}^{G,\text{tr}}&\subseteq A_{f}^{H,\text{tr}}.\end{split}

Proof.
Because

and

commute for all
\sigma

, the claim follows. ∎

2.6 Remark.
Let

and

. Then

\displaystyle A_{f}^{G,\text{tr}}=A[\tau_{m+1},\tau_{m+2},\dots,\tau_{n}]_{f_{m}}^{G,\text{tr}}.

Proof.
Throughout we make use of Proposition 2.3. We have a commutative diagram of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Omega_{n}(A)}}

with

3.1 Lemma.

Let
n=2
. Then
\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}\in A
and
A_{f}^{S_{2}}
decomposes as an
A
-module via

\displaystyle A_{f}^{S_{2}}=A\oplus\left(\text{Ann}_{A}\,2\cap\text{Ann}_{A}\,(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\right)\tau_{2}.

In particular

\text{Ann}_{A_{f}}\,2\cap\text{Ann}_{A_{f}}\,(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\subseteq A_{f}^{S_{2}}
.

Proof.
We write
f=t^{2}+pt+q
for
p
and
q
in
A
. Since
f
factors in
A_{f}
via
f=(t-\tau_{1})(t-\tau_{2})
, we obtain
\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}=-p\in A
. Using the standard basis of
A_{f}
,an arbitrary element
x
in
A_{f}
can be written as
x=a+b\tau_{2}
with
a
and
b
in
A
. Then,
x
is an
S_{2}
-invariants if and only if

\displaystyle 0=x-(1\,2)x=b(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})=bp+2b\tau_{2}.

Now the right hand side equals zero if and only if
b\in\text{Ann}_{A}\,p\cap\text{Ann}_{A}\,2
. This proves the claim. ∎

3.2 Lemma.

Let
n>2
. Then

\displaystyle A_{f}^{S_{n}}\cap A[\tau_{n}]=A.

Proof.
Let
p
be the coefficient of
t^{n-1}
in
f
and let
q
be the coefficient of
t^{n-2}
in
f_{n-1}
. Since
f=(t-\tau_{n})f_{n-1}
we obtain
q=p+\tau_{n}
. Further,
\tau_{n-1}
is a root of
f_{n-1}
which yields

\displaystyle 0=f_{n-1}(\tau_{n-1})=\tau_{n-1}^{n-1}+(p+\tau_{n}){\tau_{n-1}^{n-2}}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-3}q_{i}\tau_{n-1}^{i}

with
q_{i}
in
A[\tau_{n}]
. Hence, we can replace
\tau_{n}\tau_{n-1}^{n-2}
with
\tau_{n-1}^{n-1}
in the standard basis of
A_{f}
and obtain a new basis
B^{\prime}
. Notice, we have
\tau_{n}\tau_{n-1}^{n-2}\neq\tau_{n}^{j}
for all
j\leq n-1
(because
n>2
). Now let
x=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}\tau_{n}^{i}
be an
S_{n}
-invariant with
a_{i}
in
A
and
\sigma:=(n-1\,n)
. Then

\displaystyle 0=x-\sigma x=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}\tau_{n}^{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}\tau_{n-1}^{i}.

Since the right hand side is expressed in the basis
B^{\prime}
, we obtain
a_{i}=0
for all
i>0
.∎

3.3 Lemma.

Let
n\geq i\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle A_{f}^{S_{n}}\cap A[\tau_{i+1},\tau_{i+2}\dots,\tau_{n}]=A.

Proof.
We will show the claim by induction on
n
. For
n=2
it is trivial. Let
n>2
. Then
A_{f}^{S_{n}}\subseteq A[\tau_{n}]_{f_{n-1}}^{S_{n-1}}
and by induction

\displaystyle A_{f}^{S_{n}}\cap A[\tau_{i+1},\tau_{i+2},\dots,\tau_{n}]\subseteq A[\tau_{n}]_{f_{n-1}}^{S_{n-1}}\cap A[\tau_{n}][\tau_{i+1},\tau_{i+2},\dots,\tau_{n-1}]=A[\tau_{n}].

Now the claim follows by Lemma 3.2.∎

3.4 Proposition.

Let
n\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle A_{f}^{S_{n}}\cap A_{f}^{S_{2},\textnormal{tr}}=A.

Proof.
First, we proof the claim for

: It is equivalent to
A_{f}^{S_{2},\text{tr}}=A
. Consider the generic splitting algebra
\Omega_{2}(A)=A[g_{1},g_{2}]_{g}
. As seen before, we have
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}=-g_{1}
and thus
\text{Ann}_{A[g_{1},g_{2}]}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})=0
. By Lemma 3.1 we obtain
\Omega_{2}^{S_{2}}(A)=A[g_{1},g_{2}]
and the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A[g_{1},g_{2}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{n}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}

yields the desired result. For

, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{f}^{S_{2},\text{tr}}=A[\tau_{3},\tau_{4},\dots,\tau_{n}]_{f_{2}}^{S_{2},\text{tr}}=A[\tau_{3},\tau_{4},\dots,\tau_{n}].

By Lemma 3.3 this yields the desired result. ∎

The trivial

-invariants are exactly equal to

3.6 Definition.
We define
D_{f}:=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\tau_{i}+\tau_{j})

is the image of

The following proposition proves the sufficiency of

3.7 Proposition.

Let

be an
S_{n}
-invariant. Then, both

and

are contained in

. In particular if
\textnormal{Ann}_{A}\,2\cap\textnormal{Ann}_{A}\,D_{f}=0
, then

.

Proof.
We have
2x\in A_{f}^{S_{n}}
and
2x=\Sigma_{1}^{(1\,2)}x\in A_{f}^{(1\,2),\text{tr}}
. By Proposition 3.4 it follows that

.

Now we define

\displaystyle E_{f}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
(i,j)\neq(1,2)\end{subarray}}(\tau_{i}+\tau_{j}).

Then
D_{f}=E_{f}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})
and
E_{f}\in A_{f}^{(1\,2),\text{tr}}
. Further, we obtain

\displaystyle(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})x=\Sigma_{1}^{(1\,2)}(\tau_{1}x)\in A_{f}^{(1\,2),\text{tr}}.

It follows that
D_{f}x=E_{f}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})x\in A_{f}^{(1\,2),\text{tr}}
but also
D_{f}x\in A_{f}^{S_{n}}
. Again by Proposition 3.4 we obtain

. Now if
x\in A_{f}^{S_{n}}\backslash A
, then we find a non-zero element in
\text{Ann}_{A}\,2\cap\text{Ann}_{A}\,D_{f}
using the standard basis of
A_{f}
. ∎

4

4.1 Lemma.

Let

be in
A_{f}
,

a subset of
S_{n}
and

an ideal of
A_{f}
with
\sigma I=I
for all
\sigma
in
X
. We set

. Then
I\cap\textnormal{Ann}_{A_{f}}x=0
if and only if
I\cap\textnormal{Ann}_{A_{f}}y=0.

Proof.
Because
A_{f}\rightarrow A_{f},y\mapsto\sigma y
is an
A
-algebra homomorphism for all
\sigma
in
S_{n}
, we may assume
1\in X
. Then
x
divides
y
and
\Leftarrow
is trivial. We prove the implication
\Rightarrow
by induction on
|X|
. For
|X|=1
we have
x=y
. For
|X|>1
let
z\in I
with
zy=0
. Then

\displaystyle 0=zy=(zx)\prod_{\sigma\in X\backslash{1}}\sigma x

with
zx\in I
. By induction it follows that
zx=0
and thus
z=0
. ∎

4.2 Lemma.

Let
1\leq i<j\leq n
and set
\sigma:=(i\,j)
.

(a)

Let
x\neq 0
be a trivial
\sigma
-invariant. Then
x\tau_{j}\notin A_{f}^{\sigma,\textnormal{tr}}
.

(b)

There is an inclusion
\textnormal{Ann}_{A_{f}}2\cap\textnormal{Ann}_{A_{f}}(\tau_{i}+\tau_{j})\subseteq A_{f}^{\sigma}
.

Proof.
First, let
i=1
and
j=2
. We have
A_{f}=A[\tau_{3},\tau_{4},\dots,\tau_{n}]_{f_{2}}
. Thus, (b) follows by Lemma 3.1. Further, by Corollary 3.5

\displaystyle A_{f}^{\sigma,\text{tr}}=A_{f}^{S_{2},\text{tr}}=A[\tau_{3},\tau_{4}\dots,\tau_{n}].

Hence, (a) follows with the standard basis of
A_{f}
.

For arbitrary
i
and
j
we set
\pi:=(1\,i)(2\,j)
. Then
\pi(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})=\tau_{i}+\tau_{j}
and
\sigma=\pi(1\,2)\pi^{-1}
. Thus, (a) and (b) follow by the case
i=1
and
j=1
together with Remark 1.7 and Remark 2.5. ∎

In the following we fix
\sigma:=(n-1\,n)
and
G:=\langle S_{n-2},\sigma\rangle
.

4.3 Proposition.

If
\textnormal{Ann}_{A}2\cap\textnormal{Ann}_{A}D_{f}\neq 0
, then there exists a non-trivial
\sigma
-invariant that is also a trivial
S_{n-2}
-invariant.

Proof.
By Lemma 4.1 it follows that
\text{Ann}_{A_{f}}2\cap\text{Ann}_{A_{f}}(\tau_{n-1}+\tau_{n})\neq 0
. Using the standard basis of
A[\tau_{n},\tau_{n-1}]_{f_{n-2}}=A_{f}
we obtain

\displaystyle I:=\text{Ann}_{A[\tau_{n},\tau_{n-1}]}2\cap\text{Ann}_{A[\tau_{n},\tau_{n-1}]}(\tau_{n-1}+\tau_{n})\neq 0.

By Corollary 3.5 we have
A_{f}^{S_{n-2},\text{tr}}=A[\tau_{n},\tau_{n-1}]_{f_{n-2}}^{S_{n-2},\text{tr}}=A[\tau_{n},\tau_{n-1}]
and thus
I\subseteq A_{f}^{S_{n-2},\text{tr}}
. By Lemma 4.2 (b)
I\subseteq A_{f}^{\sigma}
. Now let
0\neq x\in I
. If
x\notin A_{f}^{\sigma,\textnormal{tr}}
we are done. Otherwise
\tau_{n}x\in I
and
\tau_{n}x\notin A_{f}^{\sigma,\text{tr}}
by Lemma 4.2 (a).∎

4.4 Lemma.

Let
x\in A_{f}^{S_{n-2},\textnormal{tr}}\cap A_{f}^{\sigma}
. Then
\Sigma_{G}^{S_{n}}x=x+y
for
y\in A_{f}^{\sigma,\textnormal{tr}}
.

Proof.
Consider the (left) group action of
\sigma
on
S_{n}/G
and assume that the stabilizer of an element
\pi G
is non-trivial. Then
\sigma\pi G=\pi G
and so
\pi^{-1}\sigma\pi\in G
. Now write
\pi^{-1}\sigma\pi=(i\,j)
with
\pi(i)=n-1
and
\pi(j)=n
. It follows that
(i\,j)\in S_{n-2}
or
(i\,j)=\sigma
. In the case
(i\,j)=\sigma
we obtain
\pi\in G
. In the case
(i\,j)\in S_{n-2}
we obtain
\pi x\in A_{f}^{\pi(i\,j)\pi^{-1},\text{tr}}=A_{f}^{\sigma,\text{tr}}
. In total this yields

\displaystyle\Sigma_{G}^{S_{n}}x=x+\sum_{\pi}\pi x+\Sigma_{1}^{\sigma}z

for
z\in A_{f}
where the sum runs over some
\pi\in S_{n}
with
\pi x\in A_{f}^{\sigma,\text{tr}}
. Since also
\Sigma_{1}^{\sigma}z\in A_{f}^{\sigma,\text{tr}}
, we are done.∎

The following corollary proves the necessity of
(\ast)
in the main theorem.

4.5 Corollary.

If
\textnormal{Ann}_{A}2\cap\textnormal{Ann}_{A}D_{f}\neq 0
, then there exists a non-trivial
S_{n}
-invariant.

Proof.
By Proposition 4.3 there exists a non-trivial
\sigma
-invariant
x
that is also an
S_{n-2}
-invariant. Assume that
\Sigma_{G}^{S_{n}}x
is a trivial
S_{n}
-invariant. By Lemma 4.4
\Sigma_{G}^{S_{n}}x=x+y
for a trivial
\sigma
-invariant
y
. This yields

\displaystyle x=\Sigma_{G}^{S_{n}}x-y\in A_{f}^{\sigma,\text{tr}}

which is a contradiction. Hence,
\Sigma_{G}^{S_{n}}x
is a non-trivial
S_{n}
-invariant. ∎

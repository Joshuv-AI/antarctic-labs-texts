1.
Introduction

Given a compact metric space
M
and a continuous map
f:M\to M
, it is a trivial task to verify that for any
f
-invariant probability
\mu
we have the integral
\int(\alpha-\alpha\circ f)d\mu=0
for any continuous function
\alpha:M\to\mathbb{R}
. Also, if
x\in M
is a periodic point with period
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then the sum
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\alpha-\alpha\circ f)(f^{i}(x))=0
. It is readily obtained from the previous remark, by considering the following
f
-invariant probability:

\mu=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_{f^{i}(x)}.

The question about the converse of this fact leads to the well known Livsic Theorem (see
[
13
]
, Theorem 19.2.1), which states:

Theorem 1.1

(Livsic Theorem)
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold,
U\subset M
open,
f:U\to M
a smooth embedding,
\Lambda\subset U
a compact topologically transitive hyperbolic set, and
\varphi:\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}
Hölder continuous. Suppose that for every
x\in\Lambda
such that
f^{n}(x)=x
we have
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\varphi(f^{i}(x))=0
. Then there exists a continuous function
\Phi:\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi=\Phi-\Phi\circ f
. Moreover,
\Phi
is unique up to an additive constant and Hölder with the same exponent as
\varphi
.

There are variations of this classical result, as can be seen, for example, in the Introduction of
[
9
]
. Livsic Theorem is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Livsic Theorem)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
and
T:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
be a
C^{\omega}
expanding map. Let
f:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
be
C^{k}
for some
k=1,2,\dots,\infty
(resp.
\beta
-Hölder for some
0<\beta\leq 1
), and suppose that
\int fd\mu=0
for all
T
-invariant probability
\mu
. Then there exists a
C^{k}
(resp.
\beta
-Hölder) continuous function
\varphi
such that
f=\varphi-\varphi\circ T
.

Also in
[
9
]
, Theorem A, the following somehow related result appears:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
T:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
be a
C^{1}
expanding map. Let
f:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous
\beta
-Hölder function for some
0<\beta\leq 1
, and suppose that
\int fd\mu\geq 0
for all
T
-invariant probability
\mu
. Then there exists a
\beta
-Hölder function
\varphi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f\geq\varphi-\varphi\circ T
.

This theorem has been stated and proved independently by several authors. It first appears in an unpublished manuscript by Conze-Guivarc’h in
[
11
]
, where it is proved using thermodynamic formalism; the same approach is used by Savchenko in
[
17
]
. More direct proofs, which do no use the Ruelle transfer operator, can be found in
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
10
]
.

In this work, we extend this result to the context of topologically exact zooming maps with a mild condition, which includes the topologically exact non-uniformly expanding maps (since the contractions are exponential and the summability with exponent
\alpha
holds), by using techniques that can be seen in
[
10
]
, Proposition 11. Our approach is using Zorn’s Lemma to obtain for every neighbourhood a compact subset of the neighbourhood and a homeomorphism between this compact subset and the whole space. After that, for every zooming pre-ball we construct a continuous function by composing the given Hölder function with the local homeomorphism. By taking the infimum of Birkhoff sums in these local compositions over all zooming pre-balls we obtain a continuous coboundary on the whole space. By denseness of the Hölder functions in the space of continuous functions with respect to the supremum norm, we obtain the desired Hölder coboundary.

Our work is organized as follows. In section 2, we begin by giving some preliminaries and notations that will be useful for the remainder of the work and giving our main result. In section 3, we proceed with the proof of our main result. In section 4, we finish our paper by giving some applications. We stress that our result extends to several important classes of examples beyond expanding maps on the circle.

From now and on, we proceed with definitions and statements. We begin by defining zooming maps as can be seen in
[
15
]
.

2.
Preliminaries and Main Result

2.1.
Zooming Maps

The zooming times generalize hyperbolic times beyond the exponential context. Details can be seen in
[
15
]
. Let
f:M\to M
be a Borel measurable map defined on a connected, compact, separable metric space
M

Definition 1
.

(Zooming contractions). A
zooming contraction
is a sequence of functions
\alpha_{n}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that

•

\alpha_{n}(r)<r,\text{for all}\,\,n\in\mathbb{N},\text{for all}\,\,r>0.

•

\alpha_{n}(r)<\alpha_{n}(s),\,\,if\,\,0<r<s,\text{for all}\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

\alpha_{m}\circ\alpha_{n}(r)\leq\alpha_{m+n}(r),\text{for all}\,\,r>0,\text{for all}\,\,m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in(0,1)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}(r)<\infty
.

Definition 2
.

As defined in
[
16
]
, we call the contraction
(\alpha_{n})_{n}

exponential
if
\alpha_{n}(r)=e^{-\lambda n}r
for some
\lambda>0
and
Lipschitz
if
\alpha_{n}(r)=a_{n}r
with
0\leq a_{n}<1,a_{m}a_{n}\leq a_{m+n}
and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}<\infty

In particular, every exponential contraction is Lipschitz. We can also have the example with

does not need to be monotone.

Definition 3
.

(Zooming times). Let

. We say that

-zooming time

of

•

sends

•

for every
x,y\in V_{n}(p)
and every

.

We call

a
(\alpha_{0},\delta)
-
zooming ball
and

a
(\alpha_{0},\delta)
-
zooming pre-ball
.

We denote by

the set of points in

for which

is an
(\alpha_{0},\delta)
- zooming time.

(Zooming measure) A

(1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\{1\leq j\leq n\mid x\in Z_{j}(\alpha_{0},\delta,f)\}>0,

Definition 5

(Zooming set) A forward invariant set

Definition 6
.

(Bounded distortion) Given an
f
-non-singular finite measure

bounded distortion
if there exists
\rho>0
such that

\bigg|\log\frac{J_{\mu}f(y)}{J_{\mu}f(z)}\bigg|\leq\rho d(f^{n}(y),f^{n}(z)),

for

,
\mu

(\alpha,\delta)
-zooming time

The map
f

-zooming measure with bounded distortion is called a
zooming map
. Every non-uniformly expanding map as considered in
[
4

[

[
15

to guarantee existence of invariant measures, which are needed to use in our Theorem A. We observe that a general zooming measure does not need to have bounded distortion. With respect to our main result one may prove it for general measurable zooming map, but our proof needs topological exactness.

We say that a map
f:M\to M
is
topologically exact
if for every open set
V\subset M

k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

.
We denote the set of all

We say that

\int\phi d\mu=\displaystyle S(\phi,f)=:\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal{M}_{f}^{1}(M)}\bigg\{\int\phi d\eta\bigg\}.

Remark 1
.

We observe that the set
\mathcal{M}_{f}^{1}(M)
since it always contains absolutely continuous invariant probabilities with respect to the reference zooming measure
\mu
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
denote the space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
. We define the following norm on
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}

\parallel\varphi\parallel_{\alpha}:=\sup_{x,y\in M,x\neq y}\bigg\{\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}\bigg\}<\infty.

By denoting as
\parallel\cdot\parallel_{\infty}
the supremum norm on the space of continuous functions

(the norm of uniform convergence), we have that the space

is Banach with respect to the followin norm:

\parallel\cdot\parallel:=\parallel\cdot\parallel_{\infty}+\parallel\cdot\parallel_{\alpha}.

Now, we state our main result.

Theorem A
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a zooming map with Lipschitz contractions which is locally injective and topologically exact.

(1)

We have that the set of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous potentials admitting a unique maximizing measure is generic with respect to the norm
\parallel\cdot\parallel
. For such functions, the map
f
is strictly ergodic on the support of its unique maximizing measure.

(2)

Given an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous potential
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}^{\alpha}<\infty
(
a_{i}
as defined in Definition
2
) and

\int\phi d\eta\geq 0,\forall\eta\in\mathcal{M}_{f}^{1}(M),

then there exists a Hölder continuous function
\lambda_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\phi\geq\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{0}\circ f.

We readily obtain the following corollary of the item (2) as a weak version of Livsic Theorem.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a zooming map with Lipschitz contractions which is locally injective and topologically exact. Given an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous potential
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}^{\alpha}<\infty
(
a_{i}
as defined in Definition
2
) and

\int\phi d\eta=0,\forall\eta\in\mathcal{M}_{f}^{1}(M),

then there exist Hölder continuous functions
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{1}\circ f\leq\phi\leq\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}\circ f.

Proof.

In fact, we have both
\int\phi d\eta\geq 0
and
\int-\phi d\eta\geq 0
for all
\eta\in\mathcal{M}_{f}^{1}(M)
. By applying Theorem A for both
\phi
and
-\phi
, we obtain the result.
∎

3.
Proof of the Main Result

The item (1) is analogous to Theorem 6 in
[
10
]
and the proof follows Proposition 10 in
[
10
]
along the same lines, and we omit the proof. For item (2), we will divide the proof into two Lemmas.

Firstly, by using the exactness of the map
f:M\to M
we obtain the following Lemma. We observe that a
compact neighbourhood
is a connected compact set with nonempty interior.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given any compact neighbourhood
U\subset M
, there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a compact neighbourhood
K\subset U
such that the map
S:=(f^{k})_{|K}:K\to M
is a homeomorphism.

Proof.

As
f
is a topologically exact map, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{k}(U)=M
. In order to find a compact neighbourhood
K\subset U
such that the map
S:=(f^{k})_{|K}:K\to M
is a homeomorphism, we will use Zorn’s Lemma.

Since
f
is a locally injective map, we can take a compact neighbourhood
W_{0}\subset U
such that the map
f^{k}_{|W_{0}}:W_{0}\to M
is injective. It implies that the following collection is nonempty:

\Gamma:=\{W\subset U\mid W\,\,\text{is compact neighbourhood and}\,\,f^{k}_{|W}:W\to M\,\,\text{is injective}\}.

Let
\Omega:=\{W_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in J}
be a totally ordered chain of subsets
W_{\lambda}\in\Gamma
for every
\lambda\in J
. We claim that
\Omega
has an upper bound. In fact, let us consider the set

\Omega_{0}:=\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda}}\subset U.

As
\Omega_{0}
is compact, it remains to show that
\Omega_{0}=\overline{A}
for some open subset
A\subset U
and the map
f^{k}_{|\Omega_{0}}:\Omega_{0}\to M
is injective. Let
U_{\lambda}
be the interior of
W_{\lambda}
. We will show that
\Omega_{0}=\overline{\cup_{\lambda\in J}U_{\lambda}}
and we take
A:=\cup_{\lambda\in J}U_{\lambda}\subset\Omega_{0}
.

In fact, if
x_{0}\in\Omega_{0}
, given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
x_{n}\in\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda}
such that
d(x_{n},x_{0})<\epsilon/2
with
n\geq n_{0}
for some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. We have that
x_{n}\in W_{\lambda_{n}}
for some
\lambda_{n}\in J
and there exists
y_{n}\in U_{\lambda_{n}}
such that
d(y_{n},x_{n})<\epsilon/2
, because
W_{\lambda_{n}}=\overline{U_{\lambda_{n}}}
. It implies that
d(y_{n},x_{0})<\epsilon
and, as a consequence,
x_{0}\in\overline{\cup_{\lambda\in J}U_{\lambda}}
. Then,
\Omega_{0}=\overline{\cup_{\lambda\in J}U_{\lambda}}
because
x_{0}
and
\epsilon
are taken arbitrary. In order to show that the map
f^{k}_{|\Omega_{0}}:\Omega_{0}\to M
is injective, we observe that the following map is injetive:

f^{k}_{|\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda}}:\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda}\to M.

In fact, given
x,y\in\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda}
, there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in J
such that
x\in W_{\lambda_{1}},y\in W_{\lambda_{2}}
, which implies that either
x,y\in W_{\lambda_{1}}
or
x,y\in W_{\lambda_{2}}
. Since
f^{k}
is injective on both
W_{\lambda_{1}}
and
W_{\lambda_{2}}
, we have that
T^{k}_{|\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda}}
is also injective. Let us suppose that there exists
a,b\in\partial\Omega_{0}
such that
f^{k}(a)=f^{k}(b):=c
. There exist open sets
a\in U_{a},b\in U_{b}\subset U
such that
f_{|U_{a}},f_{|U_{b}}
are injective and
d(U_{a},U_{b})>0
. We also have
d(U_{a}\cap\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda},U_{b}\cap\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda})>0
and by injectivity of
f^{k}_{|\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda}}:\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda}\to M
, we can suppose that
U_{a},U_{b}
are taken such that
d(f^{k}(U_{a}\cap\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda}),f^{k}(U_{b}\cap\cup_{\lambda\in J}W_{\lambda}))>\delta>0
. By continuity, we have
d(f^{k}(a),f^{k}(b))\geq\delta
, which is a contradiction. It guarantees that
(f^{k})_{|\Omega_{0}}
is injective and
\Omega_{0}
is an upper bound for
\Omega
. Zorn’s Lemma gives a maximal element
K\in\Gamma
. Now, we show that the map
f^{k}_{|K}:K\to M
is surjective. In fact, if we had some
z\not\in M\backslash f^{k}(K)
, we could take
z_{0}\in U\backslash K
such that
f^{k}(z_{0})=z
because
(f^{k})_{|U}
is surjective and some compact neighbourhood
W_{z_{0}}\subset U\backslash K
where
(f^{k})_{|W_{z_{0}}}
is injective, because
f
is locally injective. So, the following map is injective:

(f^{k})_{|(K\uplus W_{z_{0}})}:(K\uplus W_{z_{0}})\to M,

which contradicts the maximality of
K
. So, the map
g:=f^{k}_{|K}:K\to M
is bijective. As it is continuous, the compactness of
M
guarantees that it is a homeomorphism.
∎

Now, by using Lemma
3.1
we will construct a continuous function
\overline{\lambda}:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi\geq\overline{\lambda}-\overline{\lambda}\circ f
and, by using the denseness of the Hölder function in the space of continuous functions with respect to the norm
\parallel\cdot\parallel_{\infty}
, we obtain a Hölder function
\lambda_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi\geq\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{0}\circ f
, proving the item (2). Recall that

\displaystyle S(\phi,f)=\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal{M}_{f}^{1}(M)}\bigg\{\int\phi d\eta\bigg\}>0.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given an
\alpha
-Hölder function
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{Z}
the collection of all closures of zooming pre-balls
V:=V_{n}(x)
(the closures are compact neighbourhoods), with zooming balls
B:=f^{n}(V)=B_{\delta}(f^{n}(x))
, there exists a continuous function
\lambda:M\to\mathbb{R}
(and setting
\overline{\lambda}:=-\lambda
) such that

\phi\geq\lambda\circ f-\lambda+S(\phi,f)\,\,\text{i.e.}\,\,\phi\geq\overline{\lambda}-\overline{\lambda}\circ f.

As a consequence, there exists a Hölder function
\lambda_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi\geq\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{0}\circ f
.

Proof.

Let
V\in\mathcal{Z}
and
K\subset V
and
g=f_{|K}^{k}:K\to M
the homoeomorphism given by Lemma
3.1
. We take
\varphi:=\phi-S(\phi,f)
and recall that
S_{n}\varphi=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\varphi\circ f^{i},n\geq 1
and
S_{0}\varphi=0
. Define
\lambda:M\to\mathbb{R}
as

\lambda(x):=\inf_{\mathcal{Z}}\{S_{n}\varphi(g^{-1}(x))\}.

We recall that

\parallel\varphi\parallel_{\alpha}:=\sup_{x,y\in M,x\neq y}\bigg\{\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}\bigg\}<\infty.

Given
x,y\in V_{n}(z)
, we have

d(f^{i}(x),f^{i}(y))\leq a_{n-i}d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y)),0\leq i\leq n.

So,

|S_{n}\varphi(x)-S_{n}\varphi(y)|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}|\varphi(f^{i}(x))-\varphi(f^{i}(y))|

\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{|\varphi(f^{i}(x))-\varphi(f^{i}(y))|}{d(f^{i}(x),f^{i}(y))^{\alpha}}d(f^{i}(x),f^{i}(y))^{\alpha}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\parallel\varphi\parallel_{\alpha}a_{i}^{\alpha}d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y))^{\alpha}=

=\parallel\varphi\parallel_{\alpha}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}^{\alpha}\bigg)d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y))^{\alpha}\leq\parallel\varphi\parallel_{\alpha}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}^{\alpha}\bigg)d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y))^{\alpha}.

We obtained that

|S_{n}\varphi(x)-S_{n}\varphi(y)|\leq\parallel\varphi\parallel_{\alpha}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}^{\alpha}\bigg)d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y))^{\alpha},

that is,

|S_{n}\varphi(g^{-1}(x))-S_{n}\varphi(g^{-1}(y))|\leq\parallel\varphi\parallel_{\alpha}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}^{\alpha}\bigg)d(f^{n}(g^{-1}(x)),f^{n}(g^{-1}(y)))^{\alpha},

and

|\lambda(x)-\lambda(y)|\leq\parallel\varphi\parallel_{\alpha}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}^{\alpha}\bigg)d(f^{n}(g^{-1}(x)),f^{n}(g^{-1}(y)))^{\alpha},

which implies that
\lambda
is continuous because the composition
f^{n}\circ g^{-1}
is continuous.
The property of infimum implies that
\lambda\circ f\leq\varphi+\lambda
, which implies that

\varphi=\phi-S(\phi,f)\geq\lambda\circ f-\lambda\implies\phi>\phi-S(\phi,f)\geq(-\lambda)-(-\lambda)\circ f,

and we obtain
\phi>\overline{\lambda}-\overline{\lambda}\circ f
. Moreover, by denseness of the Hölder functions, there exists a Hölder function
\lambda_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that for
\epsilon>0
small enough we have
\parallel\overline{\lambda}-\lambda_{0}\parallel_{\infty}<\epsilon
and
\phi>\overline{\lambda}-\overline{\lambda}\circ f>\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{0}\circ f+2\epsilon
. For
\epsilon>0
small enough we have
\phi\geq\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{0}\circ f
.
∎

4.2.
Uniformly Expanding Maps

As can be seen in
[
14
]
Chapter 11, we have the so-called
uniformly expanding maps
which is defined on a compact differentiable manifold
M
as a
C^{1}
map
f:M\to M
(with no critical set) for which there exists
\sigma>1
such that

\|Df(x)v\|\geq\sigma\|v\|,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,x\in M,v\in T_{x}M.

For compact metric spaces
(M,d)
we define it as a continuous map
f:M\to M
, for which there exists
\sigma>1,\delta>0
such that for every
x\in M
we have that the image of the ball
B(x,\delta)
contains a neighbourhood of the ball
B(f(x),\delta)
and

d(f(a),f(b))\geq\sigma d(a,b),\,\,\text{for every}\,\,a,b\in B(x,\delta).

We observe that the uniformly expanding maps on differentiable manifolds satisfy the conditions for the definition on compact metric spaces, when they are seen as Riemannian manifolds. If the metric space
M
is connected, uniform expansion implies topological exactness.

4.3.
Local Diffeomorphisms

As can be seen in details in

Here we present a robust (

In general, these maps are not uniformly expanding: deformation can be made in such way that the new map has periodic saddles.

Let

•

•

4.6.1.
Contracting Lorenz Attractor

The geometric Lorenz attractor is the first example of a robust attractor for a flow containing a hyperbolic singularity. The attractor is a transitive maximal invariant
set for a flow in three-dimensional space induced by a vector field having a singularity at the origin for which the derivative of the vector field at the singularity has
real eigenvalues
\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{3}<0<\lambda_{1}
with
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}>0

attractor from being hyperbolic.

The geometric construction of the contracting Lorenz attractor (Rovella attractor) is the same as the geometric Lorenz attractor. The only difference is the condition
(A1)(i) below that gives in particular
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}<0

(A1)

(A2)

The top of the cube is a Poincaré section foliated by stable lines

In the following, we give definitions for a map on a metric space to have similar behaviour to maps with hyperbolic times and which can be found in
[

Given
M

f:M\to M
, we define for
p\in M

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{-}(p)=\liminf\frac{d(f(x),f(p)}{d(x,p)}

Define also,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{+}(p)=\limsup\frac{d(f(x),f(p)}{d(x,p)}

We will consider points
x\in M
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\log\mathbb{D}^{-}\circ f^{i}(x)>0.

The critical set is the set of points
x\in M
such that
\mathbb{D}^{-}(x)=0
or
\mathbb{D}^{+}(x)=\infty
. For the non-degenerateness we ask that there exist
B,\beta>0
such that

•

\frac{1}{B}d(x,\mathcal{C})^{\beta}\leq\mathbb{D}^{-}(x)\leq\mathbb{D}^{+}(x)\leq Bd(x,\mathcal{C})^{-\beta}
.

For every
x,y\in M\backslash\mathcal{C}
with
d(x,y)<d(x,\mathcal{C})/2
we have

•

\mid\log\mathbb{D}^{-}(x)-\log\mathbb{D}^{-}(y)\mid\leq\frac{B}{d(x,\mathcal{C})^{\beta}}d(x,y)
.

With these conditions we can see that all the consequences for hyperbolic times are valid here and the expanding sets and measures are zooming sets and measures.

Definition 10
.

We say that a map is
conformal at p
if
\mathbb{D}^{-}(p)=\mathbb{D}^{+}(p)
. So, we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(p)=\lim\frac{d(f(x),f(p)}{d(x,p)}.

4.7.

Let

4.8.
Zooming sets on a metric space (not expanding)

Let
\sigma:\Sigma_{2}^{+}\to\Sigma_{2}^{+}
be the one-sided shift, with the following metric for
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}<\infty

\displaystyle d(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\mid x_{n}-y_{n}\mid,

where
x=\{x_{n}\},y=\{y_{n}\}
and
b_{n+k}\leq b_{n}b_{k}
for all

b_{n}\leq b_{1}^{n}

b_{n}\leq a_{n}:=(n+b)^{-a},a>1,b>0

We claim that

defines a Lipschitz contraction for the shift map. We require that there exists
n_{0}>1
such that
b_{n}>a_{1}^{n}\geq b_{1}^{n}
for

. So, the contraction is not exponential. In fact, if

belongs to the cylinder

we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle d(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\mid x_{n}-y_{n}\mid=\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}b_{n}\mid x_{n}-y_{n}\mid=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n+k}\mid x_{n+k}-y_{n+k}\mid

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle b_{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\mid x_{n+k}-y_{n+k}\mid=b_{k}d(\sigma^{k}(x),\sigma^{k}(y))\leq a_{k}d(\sigma^{k}(x),\sigma^{k}(y)).

It implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle d(\sigma^{i}(x),\sigma^{i}(y))\leq a_{k-i}d(\sigma^{k-i}(\sigma^{i}(x)),\sigma^{k-i}(\sigma^{i}(y)))=a_{k-i}d(\sigma^{k}(x),\sigma^{k}(y)),i\leq k.

It means that the sequence

defines a Lipschitz contraction, as we claimed.

Every positively invariant set (in particular the whole
\Sigma_{2}^{+}
) and all invariant measure
for the shift
\sigma
are zooming.

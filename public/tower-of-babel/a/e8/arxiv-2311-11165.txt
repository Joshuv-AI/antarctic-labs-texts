Character groups of metacyclic groups
Justin A. Stevenson

Abstract
. Pontryagin duality provides a powerful tool for analyzing the structure and properties of locally compact abelian groups, in particular finite abelian groups. Now much can be done with non-abelian groups via the construction of character groups. In this paper we provide a set of conditions a group must satisfy to be realized as a balanced character group for a metacyclic group. Furthermore we provide a classification for the balanced character groups of a particular class of metacyclic groups, the dicyclic groups.



1.
Introduction

The dual group of a locally compact abelian topological group
A
is defined as the set of continuous homomorphisms from
A
to the circle group, the multiplicative group of complex numbers of unit modulus. In other words it is the collection
\widehat{A}
of all complex-valued continuous characters on
A
. The group structure is given by pointwise multiplication of characters, with the identity being the trivial character sending everything to
1
.
Pontryagin duality
states that the double dual group is naturally isomorphic to the original group.

Now we are able to construct an analogue of the character group for not necessarily abelian finite groups. A construction was given in
[
9
]
to endow the collection of irreducible characters of a finite group with the structure of a weighted quasigroup, which, as shown by Hilton in
[
5
]
, can be lifted to a quasigroup, a
character quasigroup
of the finite group. Under this definition every finite abelian group has a unique lift, namely itself. This observation underlies our view that character quasigroups (especially when they are associative
character groups
) provide a generalization of the dual group to the non-abelian case.

In a previous paper, the character groups of the dihedral and generalized quaternion groups were studied
[
10
]
. The goal of the current paper is to develop the theory within a more general class of groups, the metacylic groups. A group is said to be
metacyclic
if it has a cyclic normal subgroup such that the quotient group is also a cyclic group. Equivalently, a group
G
is metacyclic if and only if there exist two cyclic subgroups
N
and
K
, with
N
normal in
G
, such that
G\cong NK
.

Let
x\in G
be such that
N=\braket{x}\cong\mathbb{Z}/_{n}
with isomorphism given by
x^{a}\mapsto n\mathbb{Z}+a
, and let
G/N=\braket{yN}
for some
y\in G
. Assuming that the order of
G/N
is
m
, we have
y^{m}\in N
, so
y^{m}=x^{l}
for some integer
l
. Additionally, since
N
is normal, we must have
x^{y}=x^{k}
for some integer
k
such that
\gcd(n,k)=1
and the congruences
l(k-1)\equiv 0\mod n
and
k^{m}\equiv 1\mod n
hold. The second condition results from the following argument:

x^{l(k-1)}=x^{lk}x^{-l}=(x^{y})^{l}x^{-l}=(x^{l})^{y}x^{-l}=(y^{m})^{y}x^{-l}=y^{m}x^{-l}=1\,,

and the computation

x=x^{-l}xx^{l}=y^{-m}xy^{m}=y^{-m}y^{m}x^{k^{m}}=x^{k^{m}}

justisfies the last condition.

It was shown by Hölder
[
11
]
that a group
G
is metacyclic if and only if it has a presentation

(1.1)

G=\braket{x,y\ |\ y^{m}=x^{l},x^{n}=1,x^{y}=x^{k}}

where
\gcd(n,k)=1
and the congruences
k^{m}\equiv 1\mod n
and
l(k-1)\equiv 0\mod n
hold. In this case, we will denote the metacyclic group with parameters
k,l,m,n
by
M(k,l,m,n)
. One must be careful though, as these parameters are not invariants for the group: It is possible for two metacyclic groups with different parameters to be isomorphic. Additionally, it is worth noting that
m
need not be the smallest integer such that
k^{m}\equiv 1\mod n
. The metacyclic group

\braket{x,y|x^{3}=1,y^{4}=1,x^{y}=x^{2}}

provides an example. It is not isomorphic to any metacyclic group with parameter
m=2
.

2.
Quasigroups, weighted quasigroups and character quasigroups

2.1.
Quasigroups

A
quasigroup
is a pair
(Q,\cdot)
where
Q
is a set and
\cdot
is a binary operation such that for all
a,b\in Q
, there exist unique solutions
x,y\in Q
to the equations

(2.1)

a\cdot x=b\quad\mbox{ and }\quad y\cdot a=b\,.

Small finite quasigroups are conveniently displayed by their Cayley or multiplication tables. The above condition requires every element of
Q
to occur in each row and column exactly one time. The body of the Cayley table of a finite quasigroup defines a Latin square, and conversely each Latin square is the body of the Cayley table of some finite quasigroup.

This definition of a quasigroup is combinatorial. Algebraically speaking, we may define a quasigroup as a tuple
(Q,\cdot,/,\backslash)
where
Q
is a set and
\cdot,/,\backslash
are all binary operations, called the
multiplication
,
left division
and
right division
respectively, such that:

\displaystyle a\cdot(a\backslash b)

\displaystyle=b\,;\quad(a/b)\cdot b=a\,;

\displaystyle a\backslash(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle=b\,;\quad(a\cdot b)/b=a\,.

Using this notation, we may write the solutions to equations (
2.1
) as
x=a\backslash b
and
y=a/b
. For further reading and background on the theory of quasigroups, see
[
8
]
.

2.2.
Weighted quasigroups

The definition of a quasigroup can be generalized by allowing the base set
Q
to be a multiset. Concretely, we say that a
weighted quasgroup
is a triple
(X,w,\alpha)
where
X
is a set, equipped with a
weight function

w:X\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and a
multiplication function

\alpha\colon X\times X\times X\to\mathbb{N};(x,y,z)\mapsto\alpha_{z}(x,y)

such that

(2.2)

\sum_{z\in X}\alpha_{z}(x,y)=\sum_{z\in X}\alpha_{x}(z,y)=\sum_{z\in X}\alpha_{y}(x,z)=w(x)w(y)

for all
x,y\in X
.
This definition appears in
[
4
,
5
]
. The multiplication function may also be displayed in a Cayley table format:

(2.3)

*

…

y

…

\vdots

\vdots

x

…

\sum_{z\in X}\alpha_{z}(x,y)z

…

\vdots

\vdots

We define the
gross weight
of a weighted quasigroup
(X,w,\alpha)
to be
\sum_{z\in X}w(z)
. A quasigroup
(Q,\cdot)
of finite order
n
may be regarded as a weighted quasigroup of gross weight
n
, with
w(x)=1
and
\alpha(x,y,z)=\delta_{x\cdot y,z}
for
x,y,z\in Q
. In this case, the conditions of (
2.2
) amount to the Latin square conditions that each symbol from
Q
appears exactly once in each row and column of the body of the multiplication table of
Q
.

2.3.
Covering weighted quasigroups with quasigroups

A quasigroup
Q
of finite order
n
is said to
cover
a weighted quasigroup
(X,w,\alpha)
of gross weight
n
if there is a surjective function
f\colon Q\to X
such that

w(x)=\big|f^{-1}\Set{x}\big|

and

\alpha(x,y,z)=\big|\Set{(a,b)\in f^{-1}\Set{x}\times f^{-1}\Set{y}}{f(a\cdot b)=z}\big|

for all
x,y,z\in X
. We will also say that
(X,w,\alpha)

lifts
to
Q
if such a covering exists. The function
f
is called a
covering
or
lifting
.

The following theorem was presented in the given form as
[
9
, Th. 5.3]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Each weighted quasigroup
of gross weight
n
lifts to a quasigroup
of order
n
.

The original formulation appeared in
[
3
]
, and then again in different language as
[
4
, Th. 1]
,
[
5
, Th. 1]
. The proofs were purely combinatorial, relying on regularity results from graph theory to build up a Latin square stepwise by row, column, and symbol.

2.4.
Weighted character quasigroups

Suppose that
G
is a group of finite order
n
, with set
\widetilde{\Gamma}(G)
or
\widetilde{\Gamma}=\Set{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{t}}
of irreducible characters.
1
1

1

The notation
\widetilde{\Gamma}
refers to the dual of the conjugacy class association scheme
\Gamma
of
G
, as discussed in
[
1
, Exs. 2.2, 3.1]

[
2
, Ex. 2.1(2),§2.5]
,
[
9
, §2.2.3]
.
Then a weighted quasigroup structure is defined on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
by

(2.4)

w\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{N};\theta_{i}\mapsto\theta_{i}(1)^{2}

and

(2.5)

\alpha\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}^{3}\to\mathbb{N};(\theta_{i},\theta_{j},\theta_{k})\mapsto\theta_{i}(1)\theta_{j}(1)\theta_{k}(1)\braket{\theta_{i}\cdot\theta_{j}|\theta_{k}}

with scalar product

(2.6)

\braket{\theta|\varphi}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{g\in G}\theta(g)\overline{\varphi(g)}

for functions
\theta,\varphi\colon G\to\mathbb{C}

[
9
, Cor. 5.10]
. The structure
(\widetilde{\Gamma},w,\alpha)
is called the
weighted character quasigroup
of
G
. Its gross weight
is
\sum_{\theta_{i}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}}\theta_{i}(1)^{2}=n
.

2.5.
Character quasigroups

Specializing the conditions of §
2.3
, the weighted character quasigroup lifts to a quasigroup
Q
if there exists a surjection
f\colon Q\to\widetilde{\Gamma}
such that
\theta_{i}(1)^{2}=|f^{-1}\{\theta_{i}\}|
for
\theta_{i}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}
and

(2.7)

\alpha(\theta_{i},\theta_{j},\theta_{l})=\big|\Set{(a,b)\in f^{-1}\{\theta_{i}\}\times f^{-1}\{\theta_{j}\}}{f(a\cdot b)=\theta_{l}}\big|

for all
\theta_{i},\theta_{j},\theta_{l}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}
. A quasigroup cover
Q
of
\big(\widetilde{\Gamma}(G),w,\alpha\big)
is called a
character quasigroup
of
G
, and a
character group
of
G
if the cover is itself a group.

By
[
9
, Th. 5.11]
, character quasigroups are guaranteed to exist for any finite group
G
. However, it still unknown if a character group always exists. The current paper seeks to answer this question in the affirmative for the metacyclic groups.

3.
Characters of metacyclic groups

Recall that a metacyclic group is a group with the following presentation

M(k,l,m,n)=\braket{x,y\ |\ y^{m}=x^{l},x^{n}=1,x^{y}=x^{k}}\,,

where where
\gcd(n,k)=1
and the congruences
k^{m}\equiv 1\mod n
and
l(k-1)\equiv 0\mod n
hold. The character theory of the metacyclic groups is well known: see
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
, for example. The group
K:=\braket{y}
acts on
\mathbb{Z}/_{n}
via
y\cdot(n\mathbb{Z}+1)=n\mathbb{Z}+k
, under which
y^{m}
acts trivially. The orbit and stabilizer of an element
n\mathbb{Z}+a
will be denoted as
Ka
and
K_{a}
respectively. Let
I
be a set of distinct representatives for the orbits of this action. Note that the size of the orbit
Ka
is equivalently the smallest integer such that
ak^{|Ka|}\equiv a\mod n
, which we will often denote by
r_{a}
. Similarly we will denote
|K_{a}|
by
s_{a}
.

For each
a\in I
we let
\Set{q_{a,1},\dots,q_{a,s_{a}}}
be a full set of integers, pairwise incongruent modulo
|K_{a}|
with
-\frac{al}{n}\leq q_{a,\alpha}<m-\frac{al}{n}
for integers
\alpha
in the range
1\leq\alpha\leq s_{a}
, and set

e_{a,\alpha}=\frac{al+nq_{a,\alpha}}{\gcd(n,l)}\,.

For concreteness, we make the canonical choice of

q_{a,\alpha}=\alpha-\lfloor{al}/{n}\rfloor\,,

and thus obtain the integers

(3.1)

e_{a,\alpha}=\frac{n\alpha+al-n\lfloor\frac{al}{n}\rfloor}{\gcd(n,l)}=\frac{n}{\gcd(n,l)}(\alpha+\{al/n\})

for
a\in I
and
1\leq\alpha\leq s_{a}
, where
\{x\}
denotes the fractional part of
x
. In particular, the relation

(3.2)

\frac{n}{\gcd(n,l)}\bigg\{\frac{al}{n}\bigg\}\in\mathbb{Z}

holds.

Next, let
\omega
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity, and let
\eta
be a primitive
\bigg(\dfrac{nm}{\gcd(n,l)}\bigg)
-th root of unity, such that
\omega^{\gcd(n,l)}=\eta^{m}
. The characters
\theta_{a,\alpha}
are all induced from the linear characters
\chi_{a,\alpha}\colon G_{a}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with
G_{a}=\braket{y^{r_{a}},x}
, where

\chi_{a,\alpha}(y^{r_{a}})=\eta^{r_{a}e_{a,\alpha}}

and

\chi_{a,\alpha}(x)=\omega^{a}

[
6
]
.
For each
a\in I
and
0\leq\alpha<r_{a}
, the specification

\theta_{a,\alpha}(y^{j}x^{i})=\begin{cases}\eta^{je_{a,\alpha}}\sum_{s\in Ka}\omega^{si}&\mbox{if }j=r_{a}j^{\prime}\mbox{ for some }0\leq j^{\prime}<s_{a}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\\
\end{cases}

defines the character of an irreducible representation of
G
of dimension
r_{a}
.

3.1.
Products of characters

For
a,b,c\in I
, define

S(a,b,c):=|\Set{(r,s)\in Ka\times Kb:r+s\equiv c\mod n}|\,.

The motivation for this definition of
S(a,b,c)
is the following identity, which will appear in our calculation of the product of two characters:

(3.3)

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{s\in Ka}\sum_{r\in Kb}\sum_{t\in Kc}\omega^{(s+r-t)i}=nr_{c}S(a,b,c)\,.

The identity follows from the easily verified fact that
S(a,b,c)=S(a,b,ck^{t})
for any choice of
1\leq t\leq r_{c}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a,b,c
in
I
be such that
S(a,b,c)\neq 0
, and let
0\leq\alpha<r_{c},0\leq\beta<r_{b}
and
0\leq\gamma<r_{c}
. Then the congruence

e_{a,\alpha}+e_{b,\beta}-e_{c,\gamma}\equiv 0\mod\frac{n}{\gcd(n,l)}

holds, using the notation of (
3.1
).

Proof.

Since
a,b,c\in I
are chosen such that
S(a,b,c)\neq 0
, by definition we have that
c=ak^{r}+bk^{s}
for some
0\leq r<r_{a}
and
0\leq s<r_{b}
, which means that the congruence

\displaystyle l\cdot(a+b-c)

\displaystyle=l\cdot(a(1-k^{r})+b(1-k^{s}))

\displaystyle=a\cdot l(1-k^{r})+b\cdot l(1-k^{s})\equiv 0\mod n

holds.
Furthermore we have

\displaystyle e_{a,\alpha}+e_{b,\beta}-e_{c,\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{al+nq_{a,\alpha}}{\gcd(n,l)}+\frac{bl+nq_{b,\beta}}{\gcd(n,l)}-\frac{cl+nq_{c,\gamma}}{\gcd(n,l)}

\displaystyle=\frac{l\cdot(a+b-c)+n\cdot(q_{a,\alpha}+q_{b,\beta}-q_{c,\gamma})}{\gcd(n,l)}\equiv 0\mod\frac{n}{\gcd(n,l)}\,,

finishing our claim.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
a,b,c
in
I
be such that
S(a,b,c)\neq 0
. Then

\bigg\{\frac{al}{n}\bigg\}+\bigg\{\frac{bl}{n}\bigg\}-\bigg\{\frac{cl}{n}\bigg\}

is an integer.

Proof.

By (
3.2
),

\frac{n}{\gcd(n,l)}\bigg[\bigg\{\frac{al}{n}\bigg\}+\bigg\{\frac{bl}{n}\bigg\}-\bigg\{\frac{cl}{n}\bigg\}\bigg]

is an integer. By (
3.1
) and Lemma
3.1
, we have

\frac{n}{\gcd(n,l)}\bigg[\bigg\{\frac{al}{n}\bigg\}+\bigg\{\frac{bl}{n}\bigg\}-\bigg\{\frac{cl}{n}\bigg\}\bigg]\equiv 0\mod\frac{n}{\gcd(n,l)}\,.

The claim follows.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
a,b,c\in I
and
0\leq\alpha<s_{a},0\leq\beta<s_{b},0\leq\gamma<r_{c}
. the specification

(3.4)

\begin{cases}\dfrac{r_{c}S(a,b,c)}{\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})}&\text{if }\alpha+\beta-\gamma\equiv\{al/n\}+\{bl/n\}-\{cl/n\}\mod\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})\,,\\
0&otherwise\end{cases}

gives the value of
\braket{\theta_{a,\alpha}\cdot\theta_{b,\beta},\theta_{c,\gamma}}
.

Proof.

First notice that
\theta_{a,\alpha}\theta_{b,\beta}(y^{j}x^{i})\neq 0
if and only if
j=\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})j^{\prime}
for some
0\leq j^{\prime}<\dfrac{m}{\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})}=\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})
. Using this we have
\langle\theta_{a,\alpha}\cdot\theta_{b,\beta},\theta_{c,\gamma}\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\theta_{a,\alpha}\cdot\theta_{b,\beta})(y^{j}x^{i})\overline{\theta_{c,\gamma}(y^{j}x^{i})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{j=1}^{\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\theta_{a,\alpha}\cdot\theta_{b,\beta})(y^{\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})j}x^{i})\overline{\theta_{c,\gamma}(y^{\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})j}x^{i})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{j=1}^{\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})}\eta^{\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})(e_{a,\alpha}+e_{b,\beta}-e_{c,\gamma})j}\underbrace{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{s\in Ka}\sum_{r\in Kb}\sum_{t\in Kc}\omega^{(r+s-t)i}}_{nr_{c}S(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{c}S(a,b,c)}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})}\eta^{\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})(e_{a,\alpha}+e_{b,\beta}-e_{c,\gamma})j}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\dfrac{r_{c}S(a,b,c)\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})}{m}&\text{if }\tfrac{\gcd(n,l)}{n}\cdot(e_{a,\alpha}+e_{b,\beta}-e_{c,\gamma})\equiv 0\mod\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})\,,\\
0&otherwise\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\dfrac{r_{c}S(a,b,c)}{\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})}&\text{if }\alpha+\beta-\gamma\equiv\{al/n\}+\{bl/n\}-\{cl/n\}\mod\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})\,,\\
0&otherwise\,,\end{cases}

where the penultimate equality holds because
\eta^{\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})(e_{a,\alpha}+e_{b,\beta}-e_{c,\gamma})}
is a
\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})
-th root of unity by Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Equation
3.4
tells us that the product of two characters may be written explicitly as

\theta_{a,\alpha}\cdot\theta_{b,\beta}=\sum_{c\in I}\sum_{\gamma}\frac{r_{c}S(a,b,c)}{\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})}\theta_{c,\gamma}

for each
a,b\in I,0\leq\alpha<s_{a}
and
0\leq\beta<s_{b}
, where the second summation is over all
0\leq\gamma<s_{c}
such that the congruence

(3.5)

\alpha+\beta-\gamma\equiv\{al/n\}+\{bl/n\}-\{cl/n\}\mod\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})

holds.

Lemma 3.3
.

For each
a,b\in I
and
0\leq\alpha<s_{a}
and
0\leq\beta<s_{b}

(3.6)

\theta_{a,\alpha}\cdot\theta_{b,\beta}=\frac{r_{a}}{\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})}\sum_{s\in Kb}\sum_{\gamma}\theta_{a+s,\gamma}\,,

where the second summation is over all
\gamma
which satisfy (
3.5
).

Proof.

Since
lk\equiv l\mod n
, it immediately follows that
lk^{i}\equiv l\mod n
for any choice of
i
. Therefore, the value of
\{ak^{i}l/n\}
is independent of the choice of
i
. In other words (
3.5
) is unchanged by different choices of representatives for
a,b
and
c
. Hence it suffices to show that for fixed
\gamma
, we have

\sum_{c\in I}r_{c}S(a,b,c)\theta_{c,\gamma}=\sum_{s\in Kb}\tfrac{r_{a}}{r_{a+s}}\theta_{a+s,\gamma}\,.

To see why this is the case, recall that
S(a,b,c)\neq 0
if and only if
c=a+bk^{j}
for some
0\leq j<r_{b}
. Additionally, we have the following identity:

\sum_{i=1}^{r_{a}}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{b}}(ak^{i}+bk^{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{r_{b}}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{a}}(a+bk^{j-i})k^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{r_{b}}\frac{r_{a}}{r_{a+bk^{j}}}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{a+bk^{j}}}(a+bk^{j})k^{i}\,.

For fixed
0\leq r\leq r_{b}
, the number of times that any element of the form
(a+bk^{r})k^{s}
with
0\leq s\leq r_{a+bk^{r}}
appears in the left hand side is exactly
r_{a+bk^{r}}S(a,b,c)
, while on the right hand side they appear
\sum_{j=1}^{r_{b}}{r_{a}}/{r_{a+bk^{j}}}
many times.
∎

4.
The character groups

4.1.
Quasigroup semialgebras

Let
Q
be a quasigroup, say a character quasigroup for a finite group
G
. Let
\mathbb{N}Q
be the free
\mathbb{N}
-semimodule over
Q
, with the product
\cdot
defined by semilinear extension of the quasigroup multiplication on
Q
. In particular, if
Q
is a group, then
\mathbb{N}Q
forms the fragment of the integral group algebra where all the coefficients are nonnegative.

A multisubset
M
of
Q
, where each element
q
of
Q
appears in
M
with muliplicity
m_{q}
, is represented in
\mathbb{N}Q
as

M=\sum_{q\in Q}m_{q}q\,.

Additionally if
S
and
T
are multisubsets of
Q
, then
m_{q}(S,T)
will denote the multiplicity of
q
in the product
S\cdot T
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(X,w,\alpha)
be a weighted quasigroup. A quasigroup
Q
covers
(X,w,\alpha)
if and only if there are
|X|
many disjoint subsets
\{S_{x}\}_{x\in X}
such that
|S_{x}|=w(x)
for each
x\in X
and

(4.1)

\sum_{q\in S_{z}}m_{q}(S_{x},S_{y})=\alpha(x,y,z)

for each
x,y,z
,

Proof.

Given the sets
S_{x}
, define
f:Q\rightarrow X
by
S_{x}\mapsto x
, and conversely given the covering
f
define
S_{x}:=f^{-1}(x)
.
∎

Given a weighted quasigroup
(X,w,\alpha)
, the covering quasigroups can be difficult to work with in general, so we would like to restrict ourselves to studying a subset of the covering quasigroups which are more well-behaved. Let
Q
be a covering quasigroup
Q
, together with the sets
\{S_{x}\}_{x\in X}
guaranteed by Lemma
4.1
. We say
Q
is a
balanced covering
of
(X,w,\alpha)
if
m_{q}(S_{x},S_{y})
does not depend on the choice of
q\in S_{z}
for all
x,y,z\in X
.

Remark 4.1
.

If one does not want to refer explicitly to the notation of Lemma
4.1
in the definition of a balanced covering, they may equivalently take it as a covering such that

\forall\ x,y\in X\,,\ \alpha(x,y,z)=\alpha(x,y,z^{\prime})

whenever
f^{-1}\{z\}=f^{-1}\{z^{\prime}\}
.

Note that in a balanced covering

(4.2)

m_{q}(S_{x},S_{y})=\frac{\alpha(x,y,z)}{w(z)}

for all
x,y,z\in X
and
q\in S_{z}
. In particular, when the covering is balanced, (
4.1
) becomes

\displaystyle S_{x}\cdot S_{y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in Q}m_{q}(S_{x},S_{y})q=\sum_{z\in X}\sum_{q\in S_{z}}m_{q}(S_{x},S_{y})q

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in X}\sum_{q\in S_{z}}\frac{\alpha(x,y,z)}{w(z)}q=\sum_{z\in X}\frac{\alpha(x,y,z)}{w(z)}S_{z}\,.

In other words, the sets
\set{S_{x}}_{x\in X}
carry a nice algebraic structure when the covering is balanced. This property can be used to compute character groups, by building a presentation for them.

Let
G
be a finite group with set
\tilde{\Gamma}(G)=\Set{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{t}}
of irreducible characters, satisfying

\varphi_{i}\cdot\varphi_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\langle\varphi_{i}\cdot\varphi_{j},\varphi_{k}\rangle\varphi_{k}\,.

Summarizing the properties of balanced coverings of weighted quasigroups to the case where
Q
is a character quasigroup, we have the following result.

Corollary 4.1
.

A quasigroup
Q
is a balanced cover of
G
if and only if there exist subsets
\Set{S_{i}}_{1\leq i\leq t}
of
Q
such that
|S_{i}|=\varphi_{i}(1)^{2}
and

(4.3)

S_{i}\cdot S_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\left(\frac{\varphi_{i}(1)\varphi_{j}(1)\langle\varphi_{i}\cdot\varphi_{j},\varphi_{k}\rangle}{\varphi_{k}(1)}\right)S_{k}

for each
1\leq i,j\leq t
.

Specializing to the case of metacyclic groups, we obtain the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

A quasigroup
Q
is a balanced cover of

G=\braket{x,y\ |\ y^{m}=x^{l},x^{n}=1,x^{y}=x^{k}}

if and only if there exist disjoint subsets
S_{a,\alpha}
of size
r_{a}^{2}
for each
a\in I
and
0\leq\alpha<s_{a}
such that

(4.4)

S_{a,\alpha}\cdot S_{b,\beta}=gcd(r_{a},r_{b})\sum_{a\in I}\sum_{\gamma}S(a,b,c)S_{c,\gamma}\,,

where
\gamma
satisfies (
3.5
).

Proof.

Corollary
4.1
yields

S_{a,\alpha}\cdot S_{b,\beta}=\sum_{c\in I}\sum_{\gamma}\frac{r_{a}r_{b}r_{c}}{r_{c}\text{lcm}(r_{a},r_{b})}S_{c,\gamma}=\gcd(r_{a},r_{b})\sum_{c\in I}\sum_{\gamma}S(a,b,c)S_{c,\gamma}\,,

where
\gamma
satisfies (
3.5
).
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

A quasigroup
Q
is a balanced cover of
M(k.l,m,n)

if and only if there exist disjoint subsets
S_{a,\alpha}
of size
r_{a}^{2}
for each
a\in I
and
0\leq\alpha<s_{a}
such that

(4.5)

S_{a,\alpha}\cdot S_{b,\beta}=r_{a}\gcd(r_{a},r_{b})\sum_{i=0}^{r_{b}-1}\sum_{\gamma}\frac{S_{a+bk^{i},\gamma}}{r_{a+bk^{i}}}\,,

where
\gamma
satisfies (
3.5
).

Proof.

Corollary
4.1
yields

S_{a,\alpha}\cdot S_{b,\beta}=\sum_{i=0}^{r_{b}-1}\sum_{\gamma}\frac{r_{a}r_{b}r_{a}}{r_{a+bk^{i}}\lcm(r_{a},r_{b})}S_{a+bk^{i},\gamma}=r_{a}\gcd(r_{a},r_{b})\sum_{i=0}^{r_{b}-1}\sum_{\gamma}\frac{S_{a+bk^{i},\gamma}}{r_{a+bk^{i}}}\,,

where
\gamma
satisfies (
3.5
).
∎

Note that for each
0\leq j<s_{b}
,

S_{0,s_{b}j}\cdot S_{b,\beta}=\sum_{i=0}^{r_{b}-1}\sum_{\gamma}\tfrac{S_{bk^{i},\gamma}}{r_{bk^{i}}}=\sum_{\gamma}S_{b,\gamma}=S_{b,\beta}\,,

and so multiplication by
S_{0,s_{b}j}
fixes
S_{b,\beta}
setwise, allowing us to write

S_{b,\beta}=\sum_{j=0}^{r_{b}-1}S_{0,s_{b}j}T_{b,\beta}

for each
b\in I
, where
T_{b,\beta}
is a set of size
r_{b}
for each
0\leq\beta<s_{b}
.

4.2.
The dihedral groups

The dihedral group
D_{d}
of degree
d
and order
2d
is the metacyclic group
M(-1,0,2,d)
. Balanced character groups of dihedral groups were studied in
[
10
]
. We now reproduce one of the results from that work
[
10
, Th. 4.1]
within the present context. For simplicity, we omit the references to generalized quaternion groups that also appeared in
[
10
, Th. 4.1]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
Q
be a finite quasigroup of order
2d
, where
d=2k
is even. Then
Q
functions as a character quasigroup of
D_{d}

if and only if there exist elements
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4}\in Q
and subsets
S_{i}\subseteq Q
for
0<i<k
, each of size
4
, along with
S_{0}=\Set{q_{1},q_{2}}
and
S_{k}=\Set{q_{3},q_{4}}
, such that the conditions

(0)

Q
is the disjoint union of the sets
S_{0},\dots,S_{k};

(1)

S_{0}\cup S_{k}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
as a subgroup of
Q\,;

(2)

\begin{cases}(\mathrm{a})\quad q_{j}S_{i}=S_{i}=S_{i}q_{j}&\mbox{for }0<i<k\mbox{ and }j=1,2\,,\\
(\mathrm{b})\quad q_{j}S_{i}=S_{k-i}=S_{i}q_{j}&\mbox{for }0<i<k\mbox{ and }j=3,4\,;\end{cases}

(3)

S_{j}\cdot S_{i}=S_{i}\cdot S_{j}=S_{k-i}\cdot S_{k-j}\mbox{ for }0<i,j,i+j<k\,;

(4)

for
0<i,j<k
,

S_{i}\cdot S_{j}=\begin{cases}4S_{0}+4S_{k}&\mbox{if }\ i=j=k/2,\\
2S_{i+j}+4S_{0}&\mbox{if }\ i=j\neq k/2,\\
4S_{k}+2S_{i-j}&\mbox{if }\ i+j=k\mbox{ and }i\neq j,\\
2S_{i+j}+2S_{i-j}&\mbox{otherwise}\\
\end{cases}

are satisfied. In condition
(4)
, the indices
h
on the sets
S_{h}
are interpreted as residues modulo
d
, with the convention that
S_{h}=S_{-h}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.2
, a quasigroup
Q
is a balanced covering of
D_{d}
if and only if there exist disjoint subsets
S_{a,\alpha}
of size
r_{a}^{2}
for each
a\in I
and
0\leq\alpha<s_{a}
such that (
4.2
) is satisfied. When
r_{a}=r_{b}=1
, Equation
3.5
becomes
\alpha+\beta\equiv\gamma\mod 2
, and so in this case

S_{a,\alpha}\cdot S_{b.\beta}=S_{a+b,\alpha+\beta}\,.

With
\set{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4}}=\set{S_{0,0},S_{0,1},S_{k,0},S_{k,1}}
, condition
(1)
is clearly satisfied.
Conditions
(2)
and
(3)
are trivially verified, and when
r_{a}=r_{b}=2
, Equation
3.5
becomes
\alpha+\beta\equiv\gamma\mod 1
for any
c\in I
, and in particular is satisfied for all
0\leq\gamma<s_{c}
. Therefore in this case equation
3.5
becomes

S_{a,0}\cdot S_{b,\beta}=2\sum_{c=0}^{k}\sum_{\gamma}S(a,b,c)S_{c,\gamma}\,.

Easy case analyses reveal that
S(a,b,0)=4
when
a=b
, and
S(a,b,k)=4
when
a+b=k
, while
S(a,b,a+b)=S(a,b,a-b)=2
in all other cases, yielding condition
(4)
.
∎

Using Corollary
4.3
, we can get an upgraded theorem for the balanced covering groups of the dihedral groups.

Theorem 4.3
.

A quasigroup
Q
is a balanced cover of

if and only if there exist disjoint subsets

of size

for each

and

).

4.3.
The dicyclic groups

The dicyclic group Dic
l
is defined to be the metacyclic group
M(-1,l,2,2l)

[
7

. We specialize our general results to this case.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
S(a,b,c)\neq 0
, then
\{a/2\}+\{b/2\}-\{c/2\}
is an integer.

Proof.

This is an instance of Corollary
3.1
, noting that
n=2l
here.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

A quasigroup
Q
is a balanced cover of

G=\braket{x,y\ |\ y^{2}=x^{l},x^{2l}=1,y^{-1}xy=x^{-1}}

if and only if there exist disjoint subsets
S_{a}\subseteq Q
for
0\leq a\leq l
such that
S_{a}
is of size
r_{a}
and:

(a)

\langle S_{0},S_{l}\rangle\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/_{2}&\text{if $l$ is even;}\\
\mathbb{Z}/_{4}&\text{if $l$ is odd;}\\
\end{cases}

(b)

For all
0\leq a,b\leq l
with
(r_{a},r_{b})\neq(1,1)
,

(4.6)

S_{a}\cdot S_{b}=\tfrac{4\gcd(r_{a},r_{b})}{r_{b}}\sum_{i=0}^{r_{b}-1}\tfrac{S_{a+b(-1)^{i}}}{r_{a+b(-1)^{i}}}\,,

where we impose
S_{a}=S_{ak^{i}}
for all
i\geq 0
and the subscript is taken modulo
n
.

Proof.

First suppose that
Q
covers

. By Corollary
4.2
there exist disjoint subsets
S_{a,\alpha}
of size
r_{a}^{2}
for each
a\in I
and
0\leq\alpha<s_{a}
which satisfy the required equations. If we take
S_{0}=S_{0,0}+S_{0,1}
and
S_{l}=S_{l,0}+S_{l,1}
, these will clearly satisfy
(a)
, and upon dividing the right hand side of (
4.4
) by

, we obtain condition
(b)
.

Conversely, suppose we are given disjoint subsets
S_{a}
of size
r_{a}
satisfying conditions
(a)
and
(b)
. Define each of the following:

S_{0,0}=e,\qquad S_{0,1}=S_{0},\qquad S_{l,0}=S_{l},\qquad S_{l,1}=S_{0}S_{l},\qquad

and
S_{a}=S_{a,0}
for each
0<a<l
. By Lemma
4.2
, equation (
3.5
) is always satisfied when
(r_{a},r_{b})\neq(1,1)
, and so (
4.4
) must hold.
∎

For the duration of this section we will fix
S_{0}=x_{0}+y_{0}
and
S_{l}=x_{l}+y_{l}
, which by previous observations means we may write
S_{a}=(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{a}+y_{a})
for some
x_{a},y_{a}\in Q
for each
0<a<l
. Without loss of generality we are able to chose
x_{0}
such that
x_{0}y_{0}=y_{0}
. In particular, if
Q
is a group, then
x_{0}=1
.

By Theorem
4.4
,
\set{x_{0},y_{0},x_{l},y_{l}}
must form a copy of either
\mathbb{Z}/_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/_{2}
if
l
is even or
\mathbb{Z}/_{4}
if
l
is odd. We already have a partial Cayley table

\cdot

x_{0}

y_{0}

x_{l}

y_{l}

x_{0}

x_{0}

y_{0}

x_{l}

y_{l}

y_{0}

y_{0}

x_{0}

x_{l}

x_{l}

y_{l}

y_{l}

filled out, which immediately allows us to say
y_{0}\cdot x_{l}=y_{l}
and
y_{0}\cdot y_{l}=x_{l}
. Moreover, if
x_{l}^{2}=x_{0}
, then the collection is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/_{2}
, and if
x_{l}^{2}=y_{0}
, then it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/_{4}
. Thus, we require
x_{l}^{2}=y_{0}^{l}
, for

to be a character quasigroup for Dic

.

Lemma 4.3
.

For each
1\leq i\leq l-1
, the relation

(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{i}+y_{i})(x_{0}+y_{0})=2(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{i}+y_{i})

holds

Proof.

By equation
4.6
the quartets must satisfy
S_{i}\cdot S_{0}=2S_{i}
, in particular

(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{i}+y_{i})(x_{0}+y_{0})=2(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{i}+y_{i})

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
Q
be a character quasigroup for Dic
l
. Then exactly one of the following holds:

(a)

x_{1}^{2},y_{1}^{2}\in\set{x_{0},y_{0}}
and
S_{2}=x_{1}y_{1}+y_{1}x_{1}
;

(b)

x_{1}^{2},x_{1}y_{1}\in\set{x_{0},y_{0}}
and
S_{2}=y_{1}x_{1}+y_{1}^{2}
;

(c)

x_{1}^{2},y_{1}x_{1}\in\set{x_{0},y_{0}}
and
S_{2}=x_{1}y_{1}+y_{1}^{2}
;

(d)

x_{1}y_{1},y_{1}x_{1}\in\set{x_{0}.y_{0}}
and
S_{2}=x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}
.

Proof.

We will compute
S_{1}^{2}
in two ways. On the one hand, using the previous lemma, we have

S_{1}^{2}=(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{1}+y_{1})(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{1}+y_{1})=2(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}y_{1}+y_{1}x_{1}+y_{1}^{2})\,,

which by Theorem
4.4
is equal to
4e+4q+2S_{2}
. Thus exactly two of
x_{1}^{2},x_{1}y_{1},y_{1}x_{1}
and
y_{1}^{2}
belong to the set
\set{x_{0},y_{0}}
and the remaining two elements belong to the set
S_{2}
.

If both
x_{1}^{2}
and
y_{1}^{2}
belong to
\{x_{0},y_{0}\}
then we are in case (a), and if exactly one of them is in the set then without loss of generality we may assume that it is
x_{1}^{2}
, leading to cases (b) and (c). Finally if
x_{1}^{2}
and
y_{1}^{2}
both do not belong to the set
\set{x_{0},y_{0}}
then we get case (d).
∎

Remark 4.5
.

If
Q
is a group then case (c) of Lemma
4.4
is no longer possible, since if
y_{1}x_{1}=y_{0}^{j}
for some
j\in\set{0,1}
, then
y_{1}=y_{0}^{i}x^{-1}=y_{0}^{i+j}x_{1}
, which contradicts the size of
S_{1}
. In case (b) we can further say that
x_{1}y_{1}=y_{0}
and
x_{1}^{2}=x_{0}
, since they clearly cannot be equal, and if
x_{1}y_{1}=x_{0}
and
x_{1}^{2}=y_{0}
, then
y_{1}=x_{1}^{-1}x_{0}=y_{0}x_{0}
, again contradicting the size of
S_{1}
. Finally, in case (d) we can say
x_{1}y_{1}=y_{1}x_{1}
, since
x_{1}y_{1}=x_{0}
if and only if
y_{1}x_{1}=x_{0}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
Q
be a character quasigroup for Dic
n
. Then exactly one of the following holds:

(0)

x_{1}y_{0}=x_{1}
;

(1)

x_{1}y_{0}=y_{1}
;

(2)

x_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}x_{1}
and
y_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}y_{1}
;

(3)

x_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}y_{1}
.

Proof.

Since
S_{1}y_{0}=S_{1}
we have

(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{1}+y_{1})q=(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{1}+y_{1})\,,

from which it follows that
x_{1}y_{0}\in\{x_{1},y_{1},y_{0}x_{1},y_{0}y_{1}\}
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

If
Q
is a group, case
(0)
in Lemma
4.5
is impossible, since it would imply that
x_{0}=y_{0}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
G
be a character group for Dic
l
, such that
x_{1}^{2},y_{1}^{2}\not\in\{x_{0},y_{0}\}
. Then
G\cong\mathbb{Z}/_{2l}\times\mathbb{Z}/_{2}
or
D_{2l}
if
l
is even, and
\mathbb{Z}/_{4l}
if
l
is odd.

Proof.

First note that since
S_{2}=(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2})
, we can prove via induction that
S_{i}=(x_{0}+y_{0})(x_{1}^{i}+y_{1}^{i})
for each
1\leq i<l
and
S_{l}=x_{1}^{l}+y_{1}^{l}
. Indeed, by (
4.6
), we have
S_{1}\cdot S_{i}=S_{i-1}+S_{i+1}
and

\displaystyle S_{1}\cdot S_{i}

\displaystyle=(e+q)(x_{1}+y_{1})(e+q)(x_{1}^{i}+y_{1}^{i})

\displaystyle=2(e+q)(x_{1}+y_{1})(x_{1}^{i}+y_{1}^{i})

\displaystyle=2(e+q)(x_{1}^{i+1}+x_{1}y_{1}^{i}+y_{1}x_{1}^{i}+y_{1}^{i+1})

\displaystyle=2(e+q)(x_{1}^{i-1}+y_{1}^{i-1}+x_{1}^{i+1}+y_{1}^{i+1})\,.

This immediately implies that
x_{1}^{2l}=x_{l}^{2}=y_{0}^{l}
, meaning if
l
is odd then
G
is necessarily cyclic, so from this point on we will assume that
l
is even.

By Lemma
4.5
we have three possibilities to check.

•

If
y_{1}=x_{1}y_{0}
, then
y_{1}^{2}=y_{1}x_{1}y_{0}\in\set{x_{0},y_{0}}
, which contradicts our assumption.

•

If
x_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}x_{1}
and
y_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}y_{1}
, then
G
has presentation

\braket{x_{1},y_{1},y_{0}\ |\ x_{1}^{2l}=y_{1}^{2l}=y_{0}^{2}=1,x_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}x_{1},y_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}y_{1},x_{1}y_{1}=y_{1}x_{1}=y_{0}^{i}}

where

. In either case this group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/_{2l}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/_{2}
.

•

Finally if
x_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}y_{1}
then
G
has presentation

\displaystyle\braket{x_{1},y_{1},y_{0}\ |\ x_{1}^{2l}=y_{1}^{2l}=y_{0}^{2}=1,x_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}y_{1},x_{1}y_{1}=y_{1}x_{1}=y_{0}^{i}}

\displaystyle\rule{113.81102pt}{0.0pt}=\braket{x_{1},y_{0}\ |\ x_{1}^{2l}=y_{0}^{2}=1,y_{0}^{-1}x_{1}y_{0}=x_{1}^{-1}y_{0}^{i}}

where

. Clearly if

, and if

then
x_{1}^{2}=y_{0}
, leading to the contradiction

.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

If
l
is odd, then no character group of Dic
l
satisfies condition
(b)
of Lemma
4.4
. and if
l
is even with character group
G
such that
x_{1}^{2}=e,x_{1}y_{1}=y_{0}
and
y_{1}^{2}\not\in\set{x_{0},y_{0}}
, then

.

Proof.

Note that in this case
S_{2}=(x_{0}+y_{0})(y_{1}x_{1}+y_{1}^{2})
. Using an induction argument similar to that of the previous theorem, we can show that
S_{i}=(x_{0}+y_{0})(y_{1}^{i-1}x_{1}+y_{1}^{i})
for
1\leq i<l
and
S_{l}=y_{1}^{l-1}x_{1}+y_{1}^{l}
. So we have
y_{1}^{2l}=y_{0}^{l}
and
(y_{1}^{l-1}x_{1})^{2}=y_{0}^{l}
, and

y_{0}^{l}=y_{1}^{l-1}x_{1}y_{1}^{l-1}x_{1}=(x_{1}y_{0})^{l-1}x_{1}(x_{1}y_{0})^{l-1}x_{1}=x_{1}(y_{0}x_{1})^{l-1}(x_{1}y_{0})^{l-1}x_{1}=1\,,

which shows that

must be even. In this case

has presentation

\braket{x_{1},y_{0}\ |\ x_{1}^{2}=y_{0}^{2}=1,(x_{1}y_{0})^{2l}=1}

.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
G
be a character group for Dic
l
, such that
x_{1}^{2},y_{1}^{2}\in\set{e,q}
. Then
l
is either
1
or
2
modulo

, and
G\cong\braket{x,y\ |\ x^{4}=y^{2}=(xy)^{l}=1,x^{2}y=yx^{2}}
.

Proof.

Using a similar inductive procedure as before, one can show that:

x_{i}=\begin{cases}(x_{1}y_{1})^{\tfrac{i}{2}}&\text{ if $i$ is even};\\
(x_{1}y_{1})^{\tfrac{i-1}{2}}x_{1}&\text{ if $i$ is odd};\end{cases}\quad\mbox{and}\quad y_{i}=\begin{cases}(y_{1}x_{1})^{\tfrac{i}{2}}&\text{ if $i$ is even};\\
(y_{1}x_{1})^{\tfrac{i-1}{2}}y_{1}&\text{ if $i$ is odd}.\\
\end{cases}

By Lemma
4.5
, we have three cases to consider.

•

First if
x_{1}y_{0}=y_{1}
then

x_{1}y_{1}=x_{1}^{2}y_{0}\in\set{x_{0},y_{0}}\,,

which cannot happen.

•

If
x_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}y_{1}
, then
y_{1}^{2}=y_{0}x_{1}^{2}y_{0}=x_{1}^{2}
, and moreover both of
x_{1}^{2}
and

must be

, since otherwise

y_{1}=y_{0}x_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}y_{0}^{2}y_{0}=1\,.

If

is even, then

y_{0}=x_{l}y_{l}=(x_{1}y_{1})^{\frac{l}{2}}(y_{1}x_{1})^{\frac{l}{2}}=1\,,

a clear contradiction; and if

is odd then

y_{0}=x_{l}^{2}=(x_{1}y_{1})^{\frac{l-1}{2}}x_{1}(x_{1}y_{1})^{\frac{l-1}{2}}x_{1}=1\,,

again a clear contradiction.

•

Finally assume that
x_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}x_{1}
and
y_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}y_{1}
, and moreover first suppose that

is even, so that

x_{l}=(x_{1}y_{1})^{\tfrac{l}{2}}

and

y_{l}=(y_{1}x_{1})^{\tfrac{l}{2}}
.
This implies that

y_{0}=x_{l}y_{l}=(x_{1}y_{1})^{\frac{l}{2}}(y_{1}x_{1})^{\frac{l}{2}}=q^{\frac{l}{2}(i+j)}\,,

which means that

\tfrac{l}{2}(i+j)

must be
1
modulo
2
. This is impossible if
l
is
0
modulo
4
, so we conclude that
l
must be
2
modulo
4
. We can also conclude that
i\neq j
, thus without loss of generality we may assume that
i=1
and
j=0
. Therefore, the presentation for
G
becomes

\displaystyle\braket{x_{1},y_{1},y_{0}\ |\ x_{1}^{2}=y_{0},y_{1}^{2}=y_{0}^{2}=1,\ x_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}x_{1},\ y_{1}y_{0}=y_{0}y_{1},\ (x_{1}y_{1})^{l}=1}

\displaystyle\rule{142.26378pt}{0.0pt}=\braket{x_{1},y_{1}\ |\ x_{1}^{4}=y_{1}^{2}=(x_{1}y_{1})^{l}=1,x_{1}^{2}y_{1}=y_{1}x_{1}^{2}}\,.

On the other hand, if
l
is odd, then

x_{l}=(x_{1}y_{1})^{\tfrac{l-1}{2}}x_{1}

and

y_{1}=(y_{1}x_{1})^{\tfrac{l-1}{2}}y_{1}
.
Therefore

y_{0}=x_{l}^{2}=((x_{1}y_{1})^{\frac{l-1}{2}}x_{1})^{2}=y_{0}^{\frac{l+1}{2}(i+j)}\,,

which is impossible if
l
is
3
modulo
4
or

, and so the same conclusion follows.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.4.
Split metacyclic groups

A metacyclic group is split when
l=0
, in this case being nothing more than a semidirect product of two cyclic groups. We simply have

e_{a,\alpha}=q_{a,\alpha}=\alpha

for all
a\in I
. Therefore (
3.5
) reduces to
\alpha+\beta\equiv\gamma\mod\gcd(s_{a},s_{b})
. We will set

G=:\mathbb{Z}/_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/_{m}=\braket{x,y\ |\ y^{m}=1,b^{n}=1,x^{y}=x^{k}}

throughout this section.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
Q
be a quasigroup of order
nm
. If
Q
has an element
q
of order
m
, and disjoint subsets
T_{a}
of size
r_{a}
for each
a\in I
such that

qT_{a}=T_{a}q

and:

(1)

\bigcup_{\gamma=1}^{m}\bigcup_{a\in I}q^{\gamma}T_{a}=Q
;

(2)

T_{a}\cdot T_{b}=\sum_{c\in I}S(a,b,c)T_{c}

for all
a,b\in I
,

then
Q
is a balanced cover of
G
.

Proof.

Suppose that we are given elements
\Set{q_{0},\dots,q_{m-1}}
and disjoint subsets
T_{a}
of size
r_{a}
for each
a\in I
which satisfy conditions
(1)
and
(2)
. We define

S_{a,\alpha}=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r_{a}-1}q_{s_{a}i+\alpha}\right)T_{a}

for each
a\in I
and
0\leq\alpha<r_{a}-1
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

The group

\mathbb{Z}/_{n}\rtimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{Z}/_{m}

acts as a balanced cover of

\mathbb{Z}/_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/_{m}

for any group homomorphism

\sigma\colon\mathbb{Z}/_{m}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/_{n})

Proof.

Suppose
\sigma\colon 1\mapsto[1\mapsto k^{b}]
for some
b
. Define

T_{a}=\Set{(ak^{i},0):0\leq i<r_{a}}

for each
a\in I
, and
q_{j}=(0,j)
for each
0\leq j<m-1
. Then the conditions
(1)
and
(2)
of Theorem

are clearly satisfied.
∎

Total positivity and two inequalities by Athanasiadis and Tzanaki
Lili Mu, Volkmar Welker

Abstract
. Let Δ \Delta be a ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -dimensional simplicial complex and h Δ = ( h 0 Δ , … , h d Δ ) h^{\Delta}=(h_{0}^{\Delta},\ldots,h_{d}^{\Delta}) its h h -vector. For a face uniform subdivision operation ℱ \mathcal{F} we write Δ ℱ \Delta_{\mathcal{F}} for the subdivided complex and H ℱ H_{\mathcal{F}} for the matrix such that h Δ ℱ = H ℱ ​ h Δ h^{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}}=H_{\mathcal{F}}h^{\Delta} . In connection with the real rootedness of symmetric decompositions Athanasiadis and Tzanaki studied for strictly positive h h -vectors the inequalities h 0 Δ h 1 Δ ≤ h 1 Δ h d − 1 Δ ≤ ⋯ ≤ h d Δ h 0 Δ \frac{h_{0}^{\Delta}}{h_{1}^{\Delta}}\leq\frac{h_{1}^{\Delta}}{h_{d-1}^{\Delta}}\leq\cdots\leq\frac{h_{d}^{\Delta}}{h_{0}^{\Delta}} and h 1 Δ h d − 1 Δ ≥ ⋯ ≥ h d − 2 Δ h 2 Δ ≥ h d − 1 Δ h 1 Δ \frac{h_{1}^{\Delta}}{h_{d-1}^{\Delta}}\geq\cdots\geq\frac{h_{d-2}^{\Delta}}{h_{2}^{\Delta}}\geq\frac{h_{d-1}^{\Delta}}{h_{1}^{\Delta}} . In this paper we show that if the inequalities holds for a simplicial complex Δ \Delta and H ℱ H_{\mathcal{F}} is TP 2 (all entries and two minors are non-negative) then the inequalities hold for Δ ℱ \Delta_{\mathcal{F}} . We prove that if ℱ \mathcal{F} is the barycentric subdivision then H ℱ H_{\mathcal{F}} is TP 2 . If ℱ \mathcal{F} is the r r th -edgewise subdivision then work of Diaconis and Fulman shows H ℱ H_{\mathcal{F}} is TP 2 . Introduction

We study when a subdivision operation of simplicial complexes preserves a set of inequalities on the
h
-vector of the complex, first considered by Athanasiadis and Tzanaki in
[
4
]
in the context of
real rooted symmetric decompositions of
h
-polynomials.

For a (finite) simplicial complex
\Delta
of dimension

d-1
the
h
-vector
h^{\Delta}=(h_{0}^{\Delta},\ldots,h_{d}^{\Delta})

is an encoding of the face numbers
of the simplicial complex (see for example
[
5
]
for definitions and
background). Athanasiadis and Tzanaki
[
4
]

study the following two conditions and inequalities

(1)

\displaystyle h_{0}^{\Delta},\ldots,h_{d}^{\Delta}>0\text{ and }\frac{h_{0}^{\Delta}}{h_{d}^{\Delta}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{h_{1}^{\Delta}}{h_{d-1}^{\Delta}}\leq\cdots\leq\frac{h_{d-1}^{\Delta}}{h_{1}^{\Delta}}\leq\frac{h_{d}^{\Delta}}{h_{0}^{\Delta}},

(2)

\displaystyle h_{1}^{\Delta},\ldots,h_{d-1}^{\Delta}>0\,,\,h_{d}^{\Delta}=0\text{ and }\frac{h_{1}^{\Delta}}{h_{d-1}^{\Delta}}\geq\cdots\geq\frac{h_{d-2}^{\Delta}}{h_{2}^{\Delta}}\geq\frac{h_{d-1}^{\Delta}}{h_{1}^{\Delta}}.

By
h_{0}^{\Delta}=1
and the Dehn-Summerville equations

h_{i}^{\Delta}=h_{d-i}^{\Delta}
it follows that (
1
) and (
2
)
(except for
h_{d}^{\Delta}=0
) hold
with equality for any Gorenstein
*
complex.
In
[
4
, Question 7.2]
the authors ask if (
1
)
holds for all
2
-Cohen-Macaulay simplicial complexes and in

[
4
, Question 7.3]
they ask which triangulations of balls satisfy
(
2
).
Since
2
-Cohen-Macaulayness
is a topological property a positive answer to
[
4
, Question 7.2]

would imply that the inequalities should be preserved under
any subdivision of a
2
-Cohen-Macaulay complexes. We do not know how to
leverage the fact that the complex is
2
-Cohen-Macaulay when trying to
show that (
1
) is preserved under subdivisions. Instead we show
in
Proposition 2.0
that if we subdivide by a face uniform subdivision

\mathcal{F}
(see
[
2
]
) then the
preservation of (
1
) or (
2
) follows from the TP
2
property of the

h
-vector transformation matrix
H_{\mathcal{F}}
of the subdivision. Recall that a real matrix is totally positive or TP if all its minors are non-negative, it
has the
TP
r
property if all its
(k\times k)
-minors
are non-negative for
k=1,\ldots,r

TP-theory arises from unimodality and log-concavity questions in a quite
natural way. Since Brenti
[
8
]
applied
this theory to prove and generate unimodal, log-concave sequences, this theory
has proven to be a very useful tool in combinatorics and has been applied
frequently and in a wide range of contexts

[
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]

We show for two prominent face uniform subdivisions that they satisfy the assumption of
Proposition 2.0

In

we prove that

is TP
2
if
\mathcal{F}
is the barycentric
subdivision. The latter is defined via a refined descent statistics on the symmetric group.
As a byproduct of our studies we obtain in

We recall results by Diaconis & Fulman
[
10
]
and Mao & Wang
[

]
which imply the

is TP
2

[

]
and even TP
[
11
]
if
\mathcal{F}
is the

th
-edgewise subdivision.

In particular, (
1
) or (
2

)
and

).

We also provide examples which show that there are face uniform

\mathcal{F}
for which

Triangulations,
f

For a finite set
\Omega
a simplicial complex over ground set
\Omega

a set of subsets of
\Omega

\sigma\subseteq\tau\in\Delta

\sigma\in\Delta
. We call a

\Delta

The dimension of

\max_{\sigma\in\Delta}\dim(\sigma)
.
If
\Delta

(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex then the

-vector of
\Delta

is called the
f
-polynomial of

\Delta
. Expanding
f^{\Delta}(x-1)=h^{\Delta}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}h_{i}^{\Delta}x^{d-i}
yields the
h
-polynomial
with coefficient sequence
h^{\Delta}=(h_{0}^{\Delta},\ldots,h_{d}^{\Delta})
the
h
-vector of
\Delta
.
To each simplicial complex
\Delta
there is a geometric realization
|\Delta|
in some
real vectorspace in which
each face
\sigma
of
\Delta
is represented by a geometric simplex
|\sigma|
of dimension
\dim(\sigma)

such that
|\sigma|\cap|\tau|=|\sigma\cap\tau|
for all
\sigma,\tau\in\Delta
.
A simplicial complex

is called a face uniform triangulation or subdivision of

if there are geometric realizations

such that

•

each
|\sigma|
for
\sigma\in\Delta
is a union of
|\sigma^{\prime}|
for

\sigma^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}
and

•

there are numbers

such that

\sigma\in\Delta
we have
f_{ij}=\#\{\tau\in\Delta^{\prime}~:~|\tau|\subseteq|\sigma|,\dim(\tau)=i\}
.

We write
\mathcal{F}
for the triangular array

. Since we are only interested in the
enumerative aspects of the triangulation we
write
\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}
for
\Delta^{\prime}
in this case and speak of

as a face uniform triangulation (in dimension

).

B_{d+1}=[i!S(j,i)]_{0\leq i,j\leq d},

where
S(j,i)
is the Stirling number of the second kind.
Using (
10
), to show

(1,x,\dots,x^{d})H_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}=(P_{0}(x),P_{1}(x),\dots,P_{d}(x)),

it suffices to prove that

(11)

\displaystyle(1,x,\dots,x^{d})\,A_{d+1}=\big(\,P_{0}(x),P_{1}(x),\dots,P_{d}(x)\,\big)\,A_{d+1}\,B_{d+1}.

By the definition of
A_{d+1}
, it is easy to check that

(1,x,\dots,x^{d})\,A_{d+1}=\big(\,(1-x)^{d},x(1-x)^{d-1},\dots,x^{i}(1-x)^{d-i},\dots,x^{d}\,\big).

We claim the following for the right hand side of (
11
):

Claim 3.0
.

\big(\,P_{0}(x),P_{1}(x),\dots,P_{d}(x)\,\big)A_{d+1}=\big(\,f_{0}(x),\dots,f_{d}(x)\,\big),

where

f_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{i!}(1-x)^{d-i}x(2x-1)\cdots(ix-i+1).

Proof of
Claim 3.0
.

By the definition of
A_{d+1}
, we have

f_{i}(x)=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=i}^{d}P_{j}(x)(-1)^{d-j}\binom{d-i}{j-i}}
.
Then all we need to prove is the following identity:

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{d}P_{j}(x)(-1)^{d-j}\binom{d-i}{j-i}=\frac{1}{i!}(1-x)^{d-i}x(2x-1)\cdots(ix-i+1).

Take
x=\frac{a}{a+1}
for
0\leq a\leq d-1
.
Then

Left hand side of
(
12
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=i}^{d}P_{j}\big(\,\frac{a}{a+1}\,\big)(-1)^{d-j}\binom{d-i}{j-i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d!(a+1)^{d}}\sum_{j=0}^{a-i}(a+d-i-j)_{d}(-1)^{j}\binom{d-i}{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(a+1)^{d}}\sum_{j=0}^{a-i}\binom{a+d-i-j}{d}(-1)^{j}\binom{d-i}{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(a+1)^{d}}\binom{a}{i},

where
(x)_{k}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)
is the lower factorial.
The last equality holds since

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m+d-j}{m-j}(-1)^{j}\binom{d-i}{j}=\binom{m+i}{m}.

Right hand side of
(
12
)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i!}\frac{1}{(a+1)^{d-i}}\frac{(a)_{i}}{(a+1)^{i}}=\frac{1}{(a+1)^{d}}\binom{a}{i}=\text{Left hand side of }\eqref{fi}.

It follows that the polynomials on both sides of (
12
) evaluate to
the same number for

d+1
different arguments. By the fact that both polynomials have degree
d
this completes the proof of the claim.
∎

Claim 3.0
.

\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}f_{i}(x)i!S(j,i)=x^{j}(1-x)^{d-j}}
.

Proof.

By
Claim 3.0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}f_{i}(x)i!S(j,i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{1}{i!}(1-x)^{d-i}x(2x-1)\cdots(ix-i+1)i!S(j,i)

\displaystyle=(1-x)^{d}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}x(2x-1)\cdots(ix-i+1)(1-x)^{-i}S(j,i)

\displaystyle=(1-x)^{d}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(\frac{1}{1-x}-1)\cdots(\frac{1}{1-x}-i)S(j,i).

Note that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k)x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)=x^{n}.

We have

\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(\frac{1}{1-x}-1)\cdots(\frac{1}{1-x}-i)S(j,i)=(\frac{1}{1-x}-1)^{j},

which completes the proof.
∎

Combining the two claims, we get that (
11
) holds.
∎

Appendix: Some well known facts about TP
2
matrices

For a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, row indices
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq n
and
column indices
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq m
we denote by

A^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}
the submatrix of
A
obtained by selecting
rows
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
and columns
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A=(\,a_{ij}\,)_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \leq i \leq n}{1 \leq j \leq m}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
such that
a_{ij}>0
for
1<i<n
and
1<j<m
.
Then
A
is TP
2
if and only if
all
2\times 2
-minors of consecutive rows and columns are non-negative.

Proof.

If
A
is TP
2
then all
2\times 2
-minors corresponding to consecutive rows and
columns must be non-negative.

Now assume that the
2\times 2
-minors of consecutive rows and columns are non-negative.
If there exists an entry in the first row (or column) such that
a_{1j_{1}}=0
(
a_{i_{1}1}=0
) then by the assumption for all
j_{2}>j_{1}
,
a_{1j_{2}}=0
(
i_{2}>i_{1}
,
a_{i_{2}1}=0
).

Let
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{2}\leq n
and
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}\leq m
be row and
column indices.
By elementary calculations we have

a_{i_{2},j_{2}}\cdot\det(A^{i_{1},i_{3}}_{j_{1},j_{2}})=a_{i_{3},j_{2}}\cdot\det(A^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{2}})+a_{i_{1},j_{2}}\cdot\det(A^{i_{2},i_{3}}_{j_{1},j_{2}})\text{ and }

a_{i_{2},j_{2}}\cdot\det(A^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{3}})=a_{i_{2},j_{3}}\cdot\det(A^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{2}})+a_{i_{2},j_{1}}\cdot\det(A^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{2},j_{3}}).

By our assumptions
a_{i_{2},j_{2}}>0
and thus
induction on
i_{3}-i_{1}
and
j_{3}-j_{1}
yields that

A^{i_{1},i_{3}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}
and
\det(A^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{3}})

are non-negative.

It follows that all
2
-minors are non-negative.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times m}
.
If
A
and
B
are TP
2
then so is
AB
.

Proof.

Let
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq n
be row indices and
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq m
be
column indices. Then by the Cauchy-Binet formula we obtain

\det\big(\,(AB)^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}\,\big)=\sum_{1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}\leq r}\det(\,A^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}\,)\cdot\det(\,B^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}\,).

By assumption all terms on the right hand side are non-negative. It follows
that
AB
is TP
2
.
∎

Local large deviations for periodic infinite horizon Lorentz gases
Ian Melbourne, Françoise Pène, Dalia Terhesiu

Abstract
We prove local large deviations for the periodic infinite horizon Lorentz gas viewed as a ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} -cover ( d = 1 , 2 d=1,2 ) of a dispersing billiard. In addition to this specific example, we prove a general result for a class of nonuniformly hyperbolic dynamical systems and observables associated with central limit theorems with nonstandard normalisation.

Introduction

Local large deviations (LLD) for one dimensional i.i.d. random variables that do not satisfy the classical central limit theorem (with the standard normalisation) but are in the domain of a stable law were recently obtained by Caravenna & Doney
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
and refined by Berger
[
6
, Theorem 2.3]
. Such results have been extended to multivariate i.i.d. random variables in the domain of the stable laws by Berger in
[
7
]
. Roughly speaking, an LLD measures the probability that the sum of the random variables assumes precise, but asymptotically large values. In the absence of second and even first moments, the proofs are considerably harder.

For dynamical systems, the first LLD results in the absence of the classical central limit theorem were obtained in
[
17
]
; they are as optimal as
[
6
, Theorem 2.3]
. The main shift in that paper is an analytic proof which overcomes the restriction of having independence.
Although promising, the results in
[
17
]
are limited to the Gibbs Markov maps.
The aim of this paper is to prove an LLD estimate for

infinite
horizon periodic Lorentz maps, which were shown to satisfy a central limit theorem
with nonstandard normalisation by Szász & Varjú
[
21
]
. A crucial new ingredient of the proofs of the present LLD results consists of a new operator renewal technique on the Young tower for the billiard map.

Periodic dispersing billiards and Lorentz gases were introduced into ergodic theory and studied by
[
20
]
. For a general reference, see
[
11
]
.
We recall that the classical central limit theorem was proved in the finite horizon case by
[
8
]
and local, moderate and large deviations were recently
obtained in Dolgopyat & Nándori
[
13
]
. In the same work
[
13
]
the authors designed a strategy to prove the local limit theorem and mixing properties for group extensions (such as
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) of
probability preserving flows by free flight functions with finite second moments. For a similar strategy but weaker results we refer to
[
3
]
.
The strategy in
[
13
]
consists of the systematic use of local large and moderate deviations
for the underlying probability preserving Poincaré map.
Their result applies to the
finite
horizon Lorentz flow.
In that case, both the free flight and the roof function are bounded. The LLD obtained in this paper (see Theorem
1.1
below) is close to optimal (see Remark
1.2
below)
and we believe that it can be used to prove the local limit theorem and mixing properties for
the
infinite
horizon Lorentz flow.

We say that

•

There are constants

.

Let
F:Y\to Y
be a Gibbs-Markov map and let

\sigma:Y\to\mathbb{Z}^{+}

\mu_{Y}(\sigma>n)=O(e^{-an})

\bar{\Delta}=Y^{\sigma}

\bar{\Delta}=\{(y,\ell)\in Y\times\mathbb{Z}:0\leq\ell\leq\sigma(y)-1\},\quad\bar{f}(y,\ell)=\begin{cases}(y,\ell+1)&\ell\leq\sigma(y)-2\\
(Fy,0)&\ell=\sigma(y)-1\end{cases}.

.

\bar{\Delta}

The range
n\ll\log|N|

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
in the range
n\ll\log|N|
. This estimate holds at the level of
T:M\to M
and
\kappa:M\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(without requiring consideration of Young towers).
Recall that
d\in\{1,2\}

Lemma 3.1

Let
\omega>0
. There exists
C>0
such that

\mu(\kappa_{n}=N)\leq C\frac{n}{a_{n}^{d}}\frac{(\log|N|\log\log|N|)^{d/2}}{|N|^{2}}\quad\text{for all $n\geq 1$, $N\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}$ with $n\leq\omega\log|N|$.}

Proof.

Let

S_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|\kappa|\circ T^{j},\qquad M_{n}=\max_{j=0,\dots,n-1}|\kappa|\circ T^{j}.

Note that for
q\geq 1
,

\mu(M_{n}>q)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\mu(|\kappa|\circ T^{j}>q)=n\mu(|\kappa|>q)\ll n/q^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mu(\kappa_{n}=N)\leq\mu(S_{n}\geq|N|)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\big(M_{n}>\tfrac{|N|}{2}\big)+\mu\big(S_{n}\geq|N|,\,M_{n}\leq\tfrac{|N|}{2}\big)

\displaystyle\ll n/|N|^{2}+\mu\big(S_{n}\geq|N|,\,M_{n}\leq\tfrac{|N|}{2}\big).

(3.1)

Suppose that
S_{n}\geq|N|
and
M_{n}\leq|N|/2
.
There exists
i\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
such that

|\kappa|\circ T^{i}\geq|N|/n
.
Then
\sum_{0\leq j\leq n-1,\,j\neq i}|\kappa|\circ T^{j}\geq|N|/2

so there exists
j\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}\setminus\{i\}
such that
|\kappa|\circ T^{j}\geq|N|/(2(n-1))
.
We can choose
c>0
so that
|N|/(2(n-1))\geq cN^{4/5}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\mu\big(S_{n}\geq|N|,\,M_{n}\leq\tfrac{|N|}{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=0,\dots,n-1,\,i\neq j}\mu\big(|N|\geq|\kappa|\circ T^{i}\geq\tfrac{|N|}{n},\,|\kappa|\circ T^{j}\geq c|N|^{4/5}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0,\dots,n-1,\,i\neq j}\mu\big(|N|\geq|\kappa|\geq\tfrac{|N|}{n},\,|\kappa|\circ T^{j-i}\geq c|N|^{4/5}\big)

\displaystyle\leq n\sum_{|r|=1,\dots,n-1}\mu\big(|N|\geq|\kappa|\geq\tfrac{|N|}{n},\,|\kappa|\circ T^{r}\geq c|N|^{4/5}\big)

\displaystyle=n\sum_{|N|\geq p\geq|N|/n}\sum_{1\leq|r|<n}\mu\big(|\kappa|=p,\,|\kappa|\circ T^{r}\geq c|N|^{4/5}\big)

\displaystyle\leq n\sum_{p\geq|N|/n}\sum_{1\leq|r|<n}\mu\big(|\kappa|=p,\,|\kappa|\circ T^{r}\geq cp^{4/5}\big).

Note that the constraints
p\geq|N|/n
,
n\leq\omega\log|N|
imply that

n\ll\log|N|\leq\log(np)=\log n+\log p\ll n/2+\log p,

so there is a constant
\omega^{\prime}>0
such that
n\leq\omega^{\prime}\log p
.
Hence

\mu\big(S_{n}\geq|N|,\,M_{n}\leq\tfrac{|N|}{2}\big)\leq n\sum_{p\geq|N|/n}\sum_{1\leq|r|<\omega^{\prime}\log p}\mu\big(|\kappa|=p,\,|\kappa|\circ T^{r}\geq cp^{4/5}\big).

By
[
21
, Lemma 16]
(see also
[
12
, Lemma 5.1]
), there is a constant
C>0
such that

\mu\big(|\kappa|=p,\,|\kappa|\circ T^{r}\geq cp^{4/5}\big)\leq Cp^{-2/45}\mu(|\kappa|=p)\ll p^{-(3+2/45)}

for
1\leq|r|<\omega^{\prime}\log p
.
Hence taking
\eta=1/45
,

\displaystyle\mu\big(S_{n}\geq|N|,\,M_{n}\leq\tfrac{|N|}{2}\big)

\displaystyle\ll n\sum_{p\geq|N|/n}(\log p)\,p^{-(3+2/45)}\ll n\sum_{p\geq|N|/n}p^{-(3+\eta)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{n^{3+\eta}}{|N|^{2+\eta}}=\frac{n}{|N|^{2}}\frac{n^{2+\eta}}{|N|^{\eta}}\ll\frac{n}{|N|^{2}}.

Combining this with (
3.1
),

\mu\big(\kappa_{n}=N)\ll\frac{n}{|N|^{2}}=\frac{n}{a_{n}^{d}|N|^{2}}a_{n}^{d}.

Finally,

a_{n}=(n\log n)^{1/2}\ll(\log|N|\log\log|N|)^{1/2},

completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.4

Let
\beta\geq 0
,

,

There exist
C>0

\int_{B_{2\delta}(0)}|t|^{\beta}L(t)^{r}|\lambda_{k,t}|^{n}\,dt\leq C\frac{(\log n)^{r}}{a_{n}^{d+\beta}}\quad\text{for all $n\geq 1$}.

|\lambda_{k,t}|-1\in(-1,0],\quad\log|\lambda_{k,t}|\leq|\lambda_{k,t}|-1\leq|\lambda_{k,t}-\lambda_{k}|\leq-b|t|^{2}L(t)

The result follows from

5.1

Let

For

denote the unique preimage

Recall that

and that there is a constant

0<\xi(y_{a})\leq C\mu_{Y}(a),\qquad|\xi(y_{a})-\xi(y^{\prime}_{a})|\leq C\mu_{Y}(a)d_{\theta}(y,y^{\prime}),

(5.1)

for all

,

.
(Standard references for properties of the transfer operator

for a Gibbs-Markov map include
[

2
]

Define the Banach space

of observables

with

Proposition 5.1

There exists
C>0
such that

\|R(uv)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}(Y)}\leq C\|u\|_{1}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}(Y)}

for all

constant on partition elements and all
v\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(Y)
.

Proof.

Since
u
is constant on partition elements, we write
u(a)=u|_{a}
.
By (
5.1
),

\|R(uv)\|_{\infty}\ll{\textstyle{\sum_{a}}}\mu_{Y}(a)|u(a)|\,d\mu_{Y}\,\|v\|_{\infty}=\|u\|_{1}\|v\|_{\infty}.

Next,
let
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
. Then

(R(uv))(y)-(R(uv))(y^{\prime})=I_{1}+I_{2}
where

I_{1}={\textstyle{\sum_{a}}}(\xi(y_{a})-\xi(y^{\prime}_{a}))u(a)v(y_{a}),\qquad I_{2}={\textstyle{\sum_{a}}}\xi(y^{\prime}_{a})u(a)(v(y_{a})-v(y^{\prime}_{a})).

By (
5.1
),

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\ll{\textstyle{\sum_{a}}}\mu_{Y}(a)d_{\theta}(y,y^{\prime})|u(a)|\|v\|_{\infty}=\|u\|_{1}\|v\|_{\infty}\,d_{\theta}(y,y^{\prime}),

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\ll{\textstyle{\sum_{a}}}\mu_{Y}(a)|u(a)|\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}(Y)}\,d_{\theta}(y_{a},y^{\prime}_{a})\leq\|u\|_{1}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}(Y)}\,d_{\theta}(y,y^{\prime}).

Hence
|(R(uv))(y)-(R(uv))(y^{\prime})|\ll\|u\|_{1}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}(Y)}\,d_{\theta}(y,y^{\prime})
, and the result follows.
∎

5.2
Further estimates

In this subsection, we exploit the fact (Proposition
2.1
) that

\mu_{Y}(\psi>n)=O(n^{-2})
.

Proposition 5.6

There exist
C>0
,
\delta>0
,
b>0
such that

\|\partial_{j}\partial_{z}{\widehat{R}}(z,t+h)-\partial_{j}\partial_{z}{\widehat{R}}(z,t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}(Y)}\leq C|h|L(h)^{2}\big\{1+|h|^{-b\log|z|}L(h)(|z|-1)\big\},

for all

t,h\in B_{\delta}(0)
, all
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
1\leq|z|\leq 1+\delta
,
and all
j=1,\dots,d
.

Proof.

In this argument, we take
|x|=\max_{j=1,\dots,d}|x_{j}|
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
so that
\psi
is integer-valued.
Now,

\partial_{j}\partial_{z}{\widehat{R}}(z,t)v=iR((\kappa_{\sigma})_{j}e^{it\cdot\kappa_{\sigma}}\sigma z^{\sigma-1}v)
.
By Proposition
5.1
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{j}\partial_{z}{\widehat{R}}(z,t+h)-\partial_{j}\partial_{z}{\widehat{R}}(z,t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}(Y)}

\displaystyle\ll\int_{Y}|\kappa_{\sigma}||e^{ih\cdot\kappa_{\sigma}}-1|\sigma|z|^{\sigma}\,d\mu_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{Y}\psi\min\{|h|\psi,1\}\sigma|z|^{\sigma}\,d\mu_{Y}=2\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}r_{m,n}

where

r_{m,n}=\mu_{Y}\big(\psi=m,\sigma=n\big)mn\min\{|h|m,1\}|z|^{n}.

Recall that
\mu_{Y}(\sigma=n)=O(e^{-an})
for some
a>0
.
Fix
a_{1}\in(0,a)
and
\delta>0

so that
e^{-a}(1+\delta)<e^{-a_{1}}
.
Then

r_{m,n}\ll|h|m^{2}ne^{-an}|z|^{n}\ll|h|m^{2}e^{-a_{1}n}.

Fixing
b>0
sufficiently large,

\sum_{n>b\log m}r_{m,n}\ll|h|m^{2}e^{-a_{1}b\log m}(1-e^{-a_{1}})^{-1}\ll|h|m^{2}m^{-a_{1}b}\leq|h|m^{-2}.

Hence

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n>b\log m}r_{m,n}\ll|h|.

It remains to consider the terms with
n\leq b\log m
.
Now

|z|^{n}=1+(|z|^{n}-1)\leq 1+n|z|^{n-1}(|z|-1)\ll 1+(\log m)m^{b\log|z|}(|z|-1).

Hence

r_{m,n}\ll\mu_{Y}(\psi=m,\sigma=n)m(\log m)\min\big\{|h|m,1\}\{1+(\log m)m^{b\log|z|}(|z|-1)\big\}.

and so

\sum_{n\leq b\log m}r_{m,n}\ll\mu_{Y}(\psi=m)m\min\big\{|h|m,1\}\{\log m+(\log m)^{2}m^{b\log|z|}(|z|-1)\big\}.

Let
K=[1/|h|]\geq 1
. Then for
m\leq K
,

\sum_{n\leq b\log m}r_{m,n}\ll\mu_{Y}(\psi=m)|h|m^{2}\{\log K+(\log K)^{2}K^{b\log|z|}(|z|-1)\big\},

and so by resummation,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{K}\sum_{n\leq b\log m}r_{m,n}

\displaystyle\ll|h|\sum_{m=1}^{K}\mu_{Y}(\psi=m)m^{2}\{\log K+(\log K)^{2}K^{b\log|z|}(|z|-1)\big\}

\displaystyle\ll|h|(\log K)^{2}+|h|(\log K)^{3}K^{b\log|z|}(|z|-1)

\displaystyle\ll|h|L(h)^{2}\big\{1+|h|^{-b\log|z|}L(h)(|z|-1)\big\}.

(5.2)

Next,

\displaystyle\sum_{m>K}\sum_{n\leq b\log m}r_{m,n}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{m>K}\mu_{Y}(\psi=m)m(\log m)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(|z|-1)\sum_{m>K}\mu_{Y}(\psi=m)m^{1+b\log|z|}(\log m)^{2}.

Now,

\displaystyle\sum_{m>K}

\displaystyle\mu_{Y}(\psi=m)m^{1+b\log|z|}(\log m)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m>K}\mu_{Y}(\psi\geq m)m^{1+b\log|z|}(\log m)^{2}-\sum_{m>K}\mu_{Y}(\psi>m)m^{1+b\log|z|}(\log m)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{Y}(\psi>K)K^{1+b\log|z|}(\log K)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{m>K}\mu_{Y}(\psi\geq m)(m^{1+b\log|z|}(\log m)^{2}-(m-1)^{1+b\log|z|}(\log(m-1))^{2}

\displaystyle\ll K^{b\log|z|-1}(\log K)^{2}+(1+b\log|z|)\sum_{m>K}\mu_{Y}(\psi\geq m)m^{b\log|z|}(\log m)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll|h|^{1-b\log|z|}L(h)^{2}+\sum_{m>K}m^{b\log|z|-2}(\log m)^{2}.

By Karamata,

\displaystyle\sum_{m>K}m^{b\log|z|-2}(\log m)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll(1-b\log|z|)^{-1}K^{b\log|z|-1}(\log K)^{2}\ll|h|^{1-b\log|z|}L(h)^{2}.

Hence

\sum_{m>K}\sum_{n\leq b\log m}r_{m,n}\ll|h|L(h)\big\{1+|h|^{-b\log|z|}L(h)(|z|-1)\big\}.

This combined with (
5.2
) gives the desired estimate for

\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{n\leq b\log m}r_{m,n}
, completing the proof.
∎

\displaystyle J_{k}(z,t)

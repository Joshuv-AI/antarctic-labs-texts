Abstract
In this work, we prove rigorous error estimates for a hybrid method introduced in [ 15 ] for solving the time-dependent radiation transport equation (RTE). The method relies on a splitting of the kinetic distribution function for the radiation into uncollided and collided components. A high-resolution method (in angle) is used to approximate the uncollided components and a low-resolution method is used to approximate the the collided component. After each time step, the kinetic distribution is reinitialized to be entirely uncollided. For this analysis, we consider a mono-energetic problem on a periodic domains, with constant material cross-sections of arbitrary size. To focus the analysis, we assume the uncollided equation is solved exactly and the collided part is approximated in angle via a spherical harmonic expansion ( P N \text{P}_{N} method). Using a non-standard set of semi-norms, we obtain estimates of the form C ⁡ ( ε , σ , Δ ​ t ) ​ N − s C(\varepsilon,\sigma,\Delta t)N^{-s} where s ≥ 1 s\geq 1 denotes the regularity of the solution in angle, ε \varepsilon and σ \sigma are scattering parameters, Δ ​ t \Delta t is the time-step before reinitialization, and C C is a complicated function of ε \varepsilon , σ \sigma , and Δ ​ t \Delta t . These estimates involve analysis of the multiscale RTE that includes, but necessarily goes beyond, usual spectral analysis. We also compute error estimates for the monolithic P N \text{P}_{N} method with the same resolution as the collided part in the hybrid. Our results highlight the benefits of the hybrid approach over the monolithic discretization in both highly scattering and streaming regimes.



1
Introduction

The radiation transport equation (RTE)
[
20
,
19
,
5
]
describes the movement of particles through a material medium by means of a kinetic distribution function that gives the density of particles with respect to the local phase space measure. In a general setting, the phase space is six dimensional: three dimensions for particle position and three for particle momentum, the latter of which is typically decomposed into energy and direction (or angle) of flight. Thus in the time-dependent setting, the RTE is defined over a seven-dimensional domain.

The RTE describes two basic processes: particle advection and interactions with the material medium. These interactions can be of various types and include scattering and emission/absorption processes. The rate at which these processes occur is determined by the properties of the material, expressed via cross-sections. Material cross-sections may vary in space and depend on the particle energy and, in situations that the material evolves, the cross-sections may evolve as well. When cross-sections vary significantly, the RTE may exhibit multiscale behavior. It is the combination of this multiscale behavior with the high-dimensional phase space that makes simulating the RTE a challenging task.

A well-known multi-scale feature of the RTE is the diffusion limit. In regions where the scattering cross-section is large, the solution of the RTE can be accurately approximated by its angular average
[
17
,
4
]
. Moreover this average is well-approximated by the solution of a diffusion equation. This solution to diffusion equation has long been used as a cheap approximation the solution to the RTE in scattering dominated regimes.

Another common limit is the absorption limit, which is characterized by a complete lack of scattering. In this case, the RTE does not have a simple asymptotic approximation. However, due the abscence of scattering, there is no coupling between the angles and energies of the kinetic distribution. Thus, with a proper discretiation, the RTE solution can be easily parallelized.

In problems for which the scattering cross-section varies dramatically, both of the limits above can exist simultaneously, along with a range of transition regimes in between. A consequence of this fact is that a monolithic numerical treat of the RTE will require many degrees of freedom that are strongly coupled. In practice, the time-dependent RTE is often updated in time with an implicit scheme. In such cases, designing the linear solvers can be a challenge.

A variety of approaches have been proposed for addressing the multiscale challenges posed of the RTE. These include micro-macro decompositions
[
18
]
, high order-low order (HOLO) methods
[
6
]
, diffusion-based acceleration
[
19
,
1
]
, and preconditioned Krylov approaches
[
21
]
. In the current paper, we consider a hybrid formulation
[
15
]
that is based on the notion of first-collision source
[
2
]
. In this hybrid formulation, the RTE is split into two components: an uncollided component that tracks the particles up to point of their first material interaction and a collided component that track the particles that remain. The resulting system is then approximated with two different angular discretizations: a high-resolution discretization for the uncollided equation and a low-resolution for the collided equation. The intuition that drives this strategy is that scattering produces a smoother solution; hence the collided equation should require less resolution to recover an accurate solution. The uncollided equation, on the other, requires higher resolution; however it takes the form of a purely absorbing RTE and can therefore be solved much more efficiently the original RTE using the same number of degrees of freedom. The efficiency of the hybrid approach for the RTE has been demonstrated in several papers
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, including generalizations to hybrid energy discretizations
[
22
]
and hybrid spatial discretizations
[
16
]
.

A key component of the hybrid implementation for the time-dependent RTE is a relabeling procedure that, after a given time step, maps the collided numerical solution into the space of the uncollided numerical solution and then uses the sum to re-initialize the uncollided equation. Meanwhile, the collided equation is re-initialized to zero. This relabeling step is critical, since otherwise the hybrid numerical solution would eventually convergence to a low-resolution numerical solution of the collided equation.

Despite the intuitive motivation of the hybrid and the success of the hybrid approach in numerical simulations, the method still lacks rigorous justification. This is due in part to complications introduced by the multiscale behavior of the RTE. For example, spectral approximations of the RTE in angle are fairly straightforward to analyze
[
13
]
, but a multiscale analysis that takes into account the degree of scattering is significantly more complicated
[
7
]
. The relabeling step of the hybrid formulation complicates the situation even further.

In the current paper, we take a first step in analyzing the hybrid method for the time-dependent mono-energetic version of the RTE with isotropic scattering. We focus only on the angular discretization of the RTE, comparing the standard spectral approximation (
\text{P}_{N}
) for the full system with a discretization of the hybrid that features a spectral approximation of the same resolution for the collided equation but assumes an exact solution for the uncollided equation. Clearly, the hybrid formulated in this way is more expensive than the monolithic approach. Thus the goal of the analysis is determine what is gained from the extra work involves in a high-resolution simulation of the uncollided equation, which in practice is computed with a high-fidelity collocation method or with a Monte-Carlo method.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the RTE, reduce it to the purely scattering problem, recall the
\text{P}_{N}
method, and then describe the setup of the hybrid. Having established the setting of of the problem, we then summarize the main results of the paper. In Section 3, we derive error estimates for the
\text{P}_{N}
equations. In Section 4, we analyze the hybrid problem. In Section 5, we generalize results back to the original RTE with non-zero absorption. In Section 6, we provide a short conclusion. The appendix contains some generic results used for the estimates of the main paper.

2.3
The hybrid method

The hybrid method is based on a separation of
\psi
into a
collided component

\psi_{\mathrm{c}}
and an
uncollided component

\psi_{\mathrm{u}}

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{t}\psi_{\mathrm{u}}+\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{\mathrm{u}}+\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\psi_{\mathrm{u}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon q,

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{t}\psi_{\mathrm{c}}+\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{\mathrm{c}}+\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\psi_{\mathrm{c}}

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{t}\psi_{{\mathrm{u}},m}+\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{{\mathrm{u}},m}+\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\psi_{{\mathrm{u}},m}=\varepsilon q,

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{t}\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}+\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}+\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}=\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}(\overline{\psi_{\mathrm{u},m}}+\overline{\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}}),

\displaystyle\left.\psi_{\mathrm{u},m}\right|_{t=t_{m-1}}=\begin{cases}g,&\quad m=1,\\

. This result is expected since in that case the uncollided solution and the transport solution agree. As expected, the error is monotonic in
\Delta t
; however, small time steps require more evaluations of the uncollided equation and more reintializions. In practice, this additional cost must be taken into account.

3
\text{P}_{N}
Analysis

3.1
Spherical harmonics preliminaries

A natural space to analyze the transport equation and the
\text{P}_{N}
approximation is the Sobolev space
H_{\circ}^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. To describe this space, we recall some elementary facts about spherical harmonics which can be found, for example, in
[
3
,
11
]
. For
u,v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, let

(u,v)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{\ell}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2},

(20)

where

\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{\ell}=\sum_{k=-\ell}^{\ell}u_{\ell,k}v_{\ell,k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2}=\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}=\sum_{k=-\ell}^{\ell}|u_{\ell,k}|^{2}.

(21)

A standard way to define Sobolev spaces on the sphere is via the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\circ}
, which is the spherical component of the Laplacian and for which the spherical harmonics are eigenfunctions:

-\Delta_{\circ}Y_{\ell,j}=\ell(\ell+1)Y_{\ell,j}.
For even integers
s
, the usual norm is

\|u\|_{H^{s}_{\circ}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}:=\left\|\left(\frac{1}{4}+\Delta_{\circ}\right)^{s/2}u\right\|=\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}b_{\ell}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2},\qquad b_{\ell}=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\ell\right)^{2s}.

(22)

The definition of this inner product extends naturally to all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and the space
H^{s}_{\circ}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
is then the completion of smooth functions under the
H^{s}_{\circ}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
norm
[
3
]
.

Rather than working directly with the
H^{s}_{\circ}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
norm in (
22
), it is convenient in the analysis below to use an equivalent norm. For
s\geq 0
, define the
H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
semi-norm and norm by

\displaystyle|u|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}:=\left(\sum_{\ell=s}^{\infty}b_{\ell}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\left(s\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}+|u|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)^{1/2},

(23)

respectively, where the sum in the semi-norm definition in (
23
) begins at
s
for technical arguments that are used in the proof of Lemma (
5
) below.
When
s=0
, the norms coincide:
\|u\|_{H^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\|u\|_{H^{0}_{\circ}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}
. More generally, the following equivalence holds.

Lemma 1

(Norm equivalence)
.

For any
s\geq 0
,

c_{1}(s)\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\|u\|_{H^{s}_{\circ}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq c_{2}(s)\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(24)

where

c_{1}(s)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad s=0,\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{3s}}&\text{if}\quad s\geq 1\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{2}(s)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad s=0,\\
\sqrt{\frac{5}{s}}\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s}&\text{if}\quad s\geq 1\end{cases}.

(25)

Proof.

When
s=0
, the norms are equal, so (
24
) holds trivially. Thus assume that
s\geq 1
. The first inequality in (
24
) follows from the fact that

\begin{split}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}&=s\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2}+\sum_{\ell=s}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\ell\right)^{2s}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2}\leq 3s\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\ell\right)^{2s}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{[c_{1}(s)]^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{\circ}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}.\end{split}

(26)

To prove the second inequality in (
24
), we use the elementary inequality

\frac{s}{4}\leq\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s},\quad s\geq 1,

(27)

to conclude that

\begin{split}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{\circ}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}&=\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\ell\right)^{2s}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2}+|u|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{s}\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}s\|{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}+|u|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{s}\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}s\|{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}+\frac{4}{s}\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}|u|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{5}{s}\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}\left(s\|{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}+|u|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\right)=[c_{2}(s)]^{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}.\qquad\qquad\qquad\end{split}

(28)

∎

Lemma 2

(Approximation property)
.

For
s\geq 0
and
N\geq\max\{0,s-1\}
,

\|(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}_{N})u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{(N+1)^{s}}|(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}_{N})u|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{(N+1)^{s}}|u|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(29)

Proof.

From the definition of the projection
\mathcal{P}_{N}
in (
5
),

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2}}_{=\|(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}_{N})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}}\leq\frac{1}{(N+1)^{2s}}\underbrace{\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\ell\right)^{2s}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2}}_{=|(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}_{N})u|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}}\leq\frac{1}{(N+1)^{2s}}\underbrace{\sum_{\ell=s}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\ell\right)^{2s}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|^{2}}_{=|u|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}}

(30)

Taking square roots of each term above yields the desired result.
∎

For vector-valued functions of space, we define the usual
L^{2}(X)
inner-product and norm by

(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})_{L^{2}(X)}=\int_{X}\mathbf{v}(x)^{\top}\mathbf{w}(x)\,dx\quad\text{and}\quad\|{\mathbf{v}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}=(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})_{L^{2}(X)}=\int_{X}\|\mathbf{v}(x)\|^{2}\,dx.

(31)

The space
H^{0,s}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})=L^{2}(X;H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))
is the space of measurable functions
u\colon X\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with the semi-inner product

\displaystyle(u,v)_{H^{0,s}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\int_{X}\sum_{\ell=s}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\ell\right)^{2s}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}(x)^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{\ell}(x)\,dx=\sum_{\ell=s}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\ell\right)^{2s}(\mathbf{u}_{\ell},\mathbf{v}_{\ell})_{L^{2}(X)}

(32)

such that the semi-norm

\displaystyle|u|^{2}_{H^{0,s}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\int_{X}\sum_{\ell=s}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\ell\right)^{2s}|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}(x)|^{2}dx=\sum_{\ell=s}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\ell\right)^{2s}\|\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}

(33)

is bounded.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
and
s\geq 0
we define
H^{r,s}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
to be the space of functions
u\colon X\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
such that the semi-norms.

\displaystyle|u|_{H^{\varsigma,s}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}:=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{\varsigma}=1}^{3}|\partial_{x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\dots x_{i_{\varsigma}}}u|_{H^{0,s}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(34)

are bounded for all positive integers
\varsigma\leq r
. These semi-norms above are equivalent to the standard semi-norms, but are more convenient in the context of the RTE since

|\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}u|_{H^{r,s}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\partial_{x_{i}}u|_{H^{r,s}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=|u|_{H^{r+1,s}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})},

(35)

which will be used in the analysis below. Henceforth, the domain of integration for
H^{s}
and
H^{r,s}
will be left off when
there is no ambiguity, i.e.,

|u|_{H^{s}}:=|u|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\quad\text{and}\quad|u|_{H^{r,s}}:=|u|_{H^{r,s}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}

(36)

Finally, for
p\geq 1
and measurable functions
u\colon X\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[\alpha,\beta]\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote the space-time semi-norms by

|u|_{L^{p}([\alpha,\beta];H^{r,s})}=\left(\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}|u|_{H^{r,s}}^{p}\,d\tau\right)^{1/p}\quad\text{and}\quad|u|_{L^{\infty}([\alpha,\beta]);H^{r,s})}=\operatorname*{ess~sup}_{t\in[\alpha,\beta]}{|u|_{H^{r,s}}}.

(37)

3.2
Stability of the
\text{P}_{N}
system

In this section, we derive estimates on high-order semi-norms that arise in the subsequent error analysis. The analysis requires iterated inequalities of Grönwall type (see Lemma
21
in the Appendix), so we define the following notation to simplify integrals that arise.
Let
0\leq\alpha\leq t\leq\beta<\infty
, and for any function
f\in L^{1}([\alpha,\beta])
, define the bounded linear operator
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\colon L^{1}([\alpha,\beta])\rightarrow C^{0}([\alpha,\beta])
and its powers
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\alpha},k\in\mathbb{N}^{\geq 0}
, by

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}[f](t):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\alpha}^{t}e^{-\sigma(t-\tau)/\varepsilon^{2}}f(\tau)d\tau\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\alpha}[f](t)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{I},&k=0,\\
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}[\mathcal{A}^{k-1}_{\alpha}[f]](t),&k\geq 1.\end{cases}

(38)

In addition, let
\mathbbm{1}\in L^{1}([\alpha,\beta])
be the function that is identically one, and let

F_{\alpha}(t)=e^{-\sigma(t-\alpha)/\varepsilon^{2}},\quad t\in[\alpha,\beta].

(39)

It is clear from the definition in (
38
)
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is monotonic; that is, if
0\leq f(t)\leq g(t)
for a.e.
t\in[\alpha,\beta]
, then
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}[f](t)\leq\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}[g](t)
for all
t\in[\alpha,\beta]
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
F_{\alpha}
be given as in (
39
). Then for all
t\geq\alpha
and every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
,

\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\alpha}[\mathbbm{1}](t)\leq\min\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{k}}{\sigma^{k}},\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{t-\alpha}{\varepsilon}\right)^{k}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\alpha}[F_{\alpha}](t)=\frac{(t-\alpha)^{k}e^{-\sigma(t-\alpha)/\varepsilon^{2}}}{k!\varepsilon^{k}}

(40)

Proof.

We first prove the bound in (
40
). Since
e^{-\sigma(t-\tau)/\varepsilon^{2}}\leq 1
, a direct calculation gives

\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\alpha}[\mathbbm{1}](t)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k}}\int_{\alpha}^{t}\int_{\alpha}^{\tau_{k-1}}\cdots\int_{\alpha}^{\tau_{1}}\mathbbm{1}d\tau_{0}\cdots d\tau_{k-1}=\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{t-\alpha}{\varepsilon}\right)^{k}.

(41)

On the other hand, it follows directly from the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
that

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}[\mathbbm{1}](t)=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}\left(1-F_{\alpha}(t)\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}\quad\implies\quad\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\alpha}[\mathbbm{1}](t)\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{k}}{\sigma^{k}}

(42)

Together (
41
) and (
42
) yield (
40
).

We prove the second statement in (
40
) by induction on
k
. When
k=0
, the statement follows trivially,

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{0}[F_{\alpha}](t)=F_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{(t-\alpha)^{0}e^{-\sigma(t-\alpha)/\varepsilon^{2}}}{0!e^{0}},

(43)

Now let us assume the statement is true for arbitrary
k
, and

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{k+1}[F_{\alpha}](t)=\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\left[\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{k}[F_{\alpha}]\right](t)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\alpha}^{t}e^{-\sigma(t-\tau)/\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{(\tau-\alpha)^{k}e^{-\sigma(\tau-\alpha)/\varepsilon^{2}}}{k!\varepsilon^{k}}\,d\tau=\frac{(t-\alpha)^{k+1}e^{-\sigma(t-\alpha)/\varepsilon^{2}}}{(k+1)!\varepsilon^{k+1}}

(44)

∎

3.2.1
Estimates for the
\text{P}_{N}
system

We derive evolution equations for the
H^{r,s}
semi-norms, defined in (
34
). For
r=0
, we test each equation in (
8
) by
b_{\ell}{\bm{\uppsi}}^{N}_{{\ell}}
, integrate by parts, and then sum over
\ell\in\{s,s+1,...,N\}
. This gives

\begin{split}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\partial_{t}|\psi^{N}|_{H^{0,s}}^{2}&+\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}|\psi^{N}|_{H^{0,s}}^{2}=\varepsilon(\mathcal{P}_{N}q,\psi^{N})_{H^{0,s}}\\
&\quad-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{\ell=s}^{N}\left(\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}-\gamma_{s,\ell}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}\right)\left({\bm{\uppsi}}^{N}_{{\ell}},\left(a_{\ell}^{(i)}\right)^{T}\partial_{x_{i}}{\bm{\uppsi}}^{N}_{{\ell-1}}\right)_{L^{2}(X)},\end{split}

(45)

where
\gamma_{s,\ell}=(1-\delta_{s,\ell})
is used to handle the first non-zero term in the sum over
\ell
.
For the special case
s=0
, (
45
) recovers the usual
L^{2}
energy equation:

\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|{\psi^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\|{\psi^{N}-\overline{\psi^{N}}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}=\varepsilon(\mathcal{P}_{N}q,\psi^{N})_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}.

(46)

To find a closed estimate with respect to the
H^{0,s}
semi-norms, we focus on the summation in (
45
).

Lemma 4
.

Let
s\geq 1
and
\ell\geq s
.
Then

\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}-\gamma_{s,\ell}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}\leq 2es\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}.

(47)

Proof.

We first establish an elementary inequality. Since
\ell\geq s
,

\ell+\frac{1}{2}=\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{s-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)e^{1/(s-1/2)}

(48)

Therefore

\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}\leq e^{\frac{s-1}{s-1/2}}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}\leq e\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}.

(49)

We use (
49
) to show (
47
). There are two cases:

Case 1 (
\ell=s
):

In this case,
\gamma_{s,\ell}=0
. Since
2s>s+1/2
and by (
49
),

\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}=\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s}\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}\leq 2es\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}

(50)

Case 2 (
\ell>s
):

In this case,
\gamma_{s,\ell}=1
.
Applying a binomial expansion and then (
49
) gives

\begin{split}&\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}-\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}=\sum_{k=0}^{2s}\binom{2s}{k}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}-\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}=\sum_{k=0}^{2s-1}\binom{2s}{k}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}\\
&\quad=\sum_{k=0}^{2s-1}\frac{2s}{2s-k}\binom{2s-1}{k}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}\leq 2s\sum_{k=0}^{2s-1}\binom{2s-1}{k}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}\\
&\quad=2s\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s-1}=2s\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s}\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}\leq 2es\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}\end{split}

(51)

∎

Lemma 5

(Semi-norm recurrence)
.

Let
s\geq 1
,
q\in L^{1}([0,T];H^{r,s})
and
g\in H^{r,s}

\displaystyle|\psi^{N}|_{H^{r,s}}(t)\leq Cs\mathcal{A}_{0}[|\psi^{N}|_{H^{r+1,s-1}}](t)+|\mathcal{P}_{N}g|_{H^{r,s}}F_{0}(t)+\varepsilon\mathcal{A}_{0}[|\mathcal{P}_{N}q|_{H^{r,s}}](t),

(52)

where
F_{0}
is defined in (
39
) and
C
is a constant independent of the data.

Proof.

We assume first that
r=0
and focus on the last term in (
45
). It follows from (i) the induced norm bound
\|a_{\ell}^{(i)}\|_{2}\leq 4

[
13
]
, (ii) the bounds in Lemma
4
, (iii) the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and (iv) the
H^{r,s}
semi-norm definitions in (
33
) and (
34
) that

\begin{split}&\sum_{\ell=s}^{N}\left(\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}-\gamma_{s,\ell}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}\right)\left({\bm{\uppsi}}^{N}_{{\ell}},\left(a_{\ell}^{(i)}\right)^{T}\partial_{x_{i}}{\bm{\uppsi}}^{N}_{{\ell-1}}\right)_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\quad\leq 2es\sum_{\ell=s}^{N}\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s-1}\|a_{\ell}^{(i)}\|_{2}\kern 5.0pt\|{\bm{\uppsi}}^{N}_{{\ell}}\|_{L^{2}(X)}\kern 5.0pt\|\partial_{x_{i}}{\bm{\uppsi}}^{N}_{{\ell-1}}\|_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\quad\leq Cs\left(\sum_{\ell=s}^{N}\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2s}|{\bm{\uppsi}}^{N}_{{\ell}}|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\ell=s}^{N}\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2(s-1)}|\partial_{x_{i}}{\bm{\uppsi}}^{N}_{{\ell-1}}|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\quad\leq Cs|\psi^{N}|_{H^{0,s}}|\partial_{x_{i}}\psi^{N}|_{H^{0,s-1}}.\end{split}

(53)

Applying the bound above to the right-hand side of (

The case

∎

For a general function

\xi_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}
becomes

\begin{split}\|{\xi_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t)&\leq\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{t_{m-1}}^{t}\|{e_{\mathrm{u},m}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(\tau)\,d\tau+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{t_{m-1}}^{t}\|{\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\eta_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(\tau)\,d\tau\\
&\leq\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|{e_{{m-1}}^{N}}\|(t_{m-1}^{-})\int_{t_{m-1}}^{t}e^{-\sigma(\tau-t_{m-1})/\varepsilon^{2}}\,d\tau+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{t_{m-1}}^{t}\|{\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\eta_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(\tau)\,d\tau\\
&=\left(1-e^{-\sigma(t-t_{m-1)}/\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\|{e_{{m-1}}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t_{m-1}^{-})+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{t_{m-1}}^{t}\|{\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\eta_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(\tau)\,d\tau.\end{split}

(92)

Adding
\|{\eta_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t)
to the both sides recovers (
89b
).∎

Now we can state an error for all time that only depends on the approximation properties of the spherical harmonic discretization on the solution.

Theorem 16
.

The hybrid error
e_{M}^{N}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|{e_{M}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t_{M}^{-})\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(\|{\eta_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t_{m}^{-})+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|{\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\eta_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}}\|_{L^{1}([t_{m-1},t_{m}];L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\right).

(93)

Proof.

Adding the inequalities in Lemma
15

and taking the limit
t\to t_{m+1}^{-}
gives

\begin{split}\|{e_{M}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t^{-}_{M})\leq\|{e_{{M-1}}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t^{-}_{M-1})&+\|{\eta_{\mathrm{c},{M}}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t^{-}_{M})\\
&+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|{\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\eta_{\mathrm{c},{M}}^{N}}\|_{L^{1}([t_{M-1},t_{M}];L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\end{split}

(94)

Exhausting this recursion until
e_{0}^{N}(t^{-}_{0})=0
yields the result.
∎

Lemma 17
.

Let
s\geq 0
,

. Then the
m
-th projection error

\|{\eta_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t^{-}_{m})\leq\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon(N+1)^{s}}\sup_{\tau\in[t_{m-1},t_{m}]}\left|\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}\right|_{H^{1,s}}(\tau).

(95)

Proof.

An application of Lemma
10
with
\alpha=t_{m-1}
,
\beta=t_{m}
,
\eta_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}(t_{m-1})=0
, an isotropic source

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}[f](t)\leq\varepsilon^{-1}(t-\alpha)\sup_{\tau\in[\alpha,t]}f(\tau)

(cf. (
40

2
, gives

\begin{split}&\|{\eta_{\mathrm{c},m}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t_{m}^{-})\leq\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\sup_{\tau\in[t_{m-1},t_{m}]}\|{\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}(\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}))}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(\tau)\,d\tau\\

(96)

∎

4.3
Estimating hybrid error in terms of the data

Finally, we will apply the approximation properties and stability estimates to the estimate in Theorem
16
to obtain an estimate that depends only on the regularity of the data.

Theorem 18
.

Let
s\geq 1
and
N\geq s-1
. If
q\in L^{1}([0,T];H^{s+1,0})
and
g\in H^{s+1,0}
, then

\displaystyle\|{e_{M}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(T^{-})\leq\frac{2C_{s}}{(N+1)^{s}}\left(\left|g\right|_{H^{s+1,0}}+T\left|q\right|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s+1,0})}\right)\min\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{s-1}s!T}{\sigma^{s}},\frac{\Delta t^{s}T}{\varepsilon^{s+1}}\min\left(1,\frac{\Delta t\sigma}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right).

(97)

Proof.

Using
\|\Omega\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\|\nabla_{x}\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}
, and applying Lemma
2
and Lemma
17
to Theorem
16
yields, for
N\geq s-1
,

\displaystyle\|{e_{M}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t^{-}_{M})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(N+1)^{s}}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\sup_{\tau\in[t_{m-1},t_{m}]}\left|\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}\right|_{H^{1,s}}(\tau)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}|_{L^{1}([t_{m-1},t_{m}];H^{1,s})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\Delta t}{\varepsilon(N+1)^{s}}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sup_{\tau\in[t_{m-1},t_{m}]}|\psi_{\mathrm{c},m}|_{H^{1,s}}(\tau)

Applying Corollary
1
with
q=\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon^{2}}\overline{\psi_{\mathrm{u},m}}
and
r=1
,

\|{e_{M}^{N}(t^{-}_{M})}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq 2C_{s}s!\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon^{3}}\frac{\Delta t}{(N+1)^{s}}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sup_{\tau\in[t_{m-1},t_{m}]}\mathcal{A}_{t_{m-1}}^{s}\left[|\psi_{\mathrm{u},m}|_{L^{1}([t_{m-1},\bullet];H^{s+1,0})}\right](\tau).

(98)

For the summand above, it follows from (
83b
), the monotonicity of
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
, and Lemma
3
that

\begin{split}\sup_{\tau\in[t_{m-1},t_{m}]}\mathcal{A}_{t_{m-1}}^{s}\left[|\psi_{\mathrm{u},m}|_{L^{1}([t_{m-1},\bullet];H^{s+1,0})}\right](\tau)&\leq\sup_{\tau\in[t_{m-1},t_{m}]}|\psi_{\mathrm{u},m}|_{L^{1}([t_{m-1},t_{m}];H^{s+1,0})}\mathcal{A}_{t_{m-1}}^{s}\left[\mathbbm{1}\right](\tau)\\
&\hskip-50.0pt\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\sigma}\left(\left|g\right|_{H^{s+1,0}}+\left|q\right|_{L^{1}([0,T];H^{s+1,0})}\right)\mathcal{A}_{t_{m-1}}^{s}\left[\mathbbm{1}\right](t_{m})\\

(99)

Pluggin the above bound into (
98

)

\begin{split}\|{e_{M}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t^{-}_{M})&\leq 2C_{s}s!\frac{\Delta tM}{\varepsilon(N+1)^{s}}\left(\left|g\right|_{H^{s+1,0}}+\left|q\right|_{L^{1}([0,T];H^{s+1,0})}\right)\min\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{s}}{\sigma^{s}},\frac{\Delta t^{s}}{s!\varepsilon^{s}}\right)\\

(100)

On the other hand, it follows from (

\begin{split}&\sup_{\tau\in[t_{m-1},t_{m}]}\mathcal{A}_{t_{m-1}}^{s}\left[|\psi_{\mathrm{u},m}|_{L^{1}([t_{m-1},\bullet];H^{s+1,0})}\right](\tau)\\

\displaystyle\|{e_{M}^{N}}\|_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}(t^{-}_{M})\leq\frac{2C_{s}}{(N+1)^{s}}\left(\left|g\right|_{H^{s+1,0}}+T\left|q\right|_{L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{s+1,0})}\right)\min\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{s-1}s!T}{\sigma^{s}},\frac{\Delta t^{s+1}\sigma T}{\varepsilon^{s+3}}\right).

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have derived multiscale error estimates for the
\text{P}_{N}
approximation of the RTE and for a hybrid approximation for the RTE that is built using the
\text{P}_{N}
approximation. By construction, the hybrid is is more expensive; we use these error estimates to understand the benefits of the additional expense for different parameter regimes. At each time step in the hybrid approximation, the collided equation is equipped with isotropic initial conditions and zero initial condition. In scattering dominating regimes, this property is key to improved estimates over the monolithic
\text{P}_{N}
approach. Meanwhile, in purely absorbing regimes, the hybrid captures the RTE solution exactly.

In the future work, we intend to revisit the current analysis for more general problems on non-periodic domains, with non-constant cross-sections and inflow boundary conditions. In addition, we intend to explicitly examine the effects of angular discretization errors in the treatment of the uncollided equation, which for the purposes of the current paper was assumed to be solved exactly.

Other estimates

Lemma 21
.

Assume
\chi
is a non-negative continuous function on
[\alpha,\beta]
. Assume
\phi\in C^{1}([\alpha,\beta])
,
\phi\geq 0
, and satisfies the following differential inequality

\frac{1}{2}(\phi(t)^{2})^{\prime}+\kappa\phi(t)^{2}\leq\chi(t)\phi(t),\quad\phi(\alpha)=\phi_{\alpha}\geq 0.

(108)

Then for all
t\in[\alpha,\beta]
,

\phi(t)+\kappa\int_{\alpha}^{t}\phi(\tau)\,d\tau\leq\phi_{\alpha}+\int_{\alpha}^{t}\chi(\tau)\,d\tau.

(109)

Furthermore

\phi(t)\leq e^{-\kappa(t-\alpha)}\phi_{\alpha}+\int_{\alpha}^{t}e^{-\kappa(t-\tau)}\chi(\tau)\,d\tau.

(110)

Proof.

We prove first (
109
). Since
\phi
and
\chi
are non-negative functions, it follows that for any arbitrary
\delta>0
, the following differential inequality holds

\phi(t)\phi^{\prime}(t)+\kappa\phi(t)^{2}\leq\chi(t)(\phi(t)+\delta),

(111)

dividing both sides of the inequality by
\phi+\delta
, and integrating in time, we arrive at

\displaystyle\phi(t)+\kappa\int_{\alpha}^{t}\phi(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{\alpha}+\int_{\alpha}^{t}\chi(\tau)\,d\tau+\delta\ln\left|\frac{\phi(t)+\delta}{\phi_{\alpha}+\delta}\right|+\kappa\delta\int_{\alpha}^{t}\frac{\phi(\tau)}{\phi(\tau)+\delta}\,d\tau,

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{\alpha}+\int_{\alpha}^{t}\chi(\tau)\,d\tau+\delta\ln\left|\frac{\phi(t)+\delta}{\phi_{\alpha}+\delta}\right|+\kappa\delta(t-\alpha),

the conclusion follows taking
\delta\to 0^{+}
.

We next prove (
110
). When
\kappa=0
the result follows immediately from (
109
):

\phi(t)\leq\phi_{\alpha}+\int_{\alpha}^{t}\chi(\tau)\,d\tau.

(112)

For the general case we multiply (
108
) by
e^{2\kappa t}
, obtaining

\frac{1}{2}\left[\Phi(t)^{2}\right]^{\prime}\leq e^{\kappa t}\chi(t)\Phi(t),

(113)

where
\Phi(t)=e^{\kappa t}\phi(t)
. Applying (
112
) to
\Phi
and undoing the transformation yields (
110
).
∎

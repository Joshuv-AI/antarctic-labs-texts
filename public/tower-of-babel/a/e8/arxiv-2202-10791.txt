Aparajita Dasgupta, Anirudha Poria

Abstract
. In this paper, we study a class of pseudo-differential operators known as time-frequency localization operators on ℤ n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n} , which depend on a symbol ς \varsigma and two windows functions g 1 g_{1} and g 2 g_{2} . We define the short-time Fourier transform on ℤ n × 𝕋 n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n} and modulation spaces on ℤ n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n} , and present some basic properties. Then, we use modulation spaces on ℤ n × 𝕋 n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n} as appropriate classes for symbols, and study the boundedness and compactness of the localization operators on modulation spaces on ℤ n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n} . Then, we show that these operators are in the Schatten–von Neumann class. Also, we obtain the relation between the Landau–Pollak–Slepian type operator and the localization operator on ℤ n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n} . Finally, under suitable conditions on the symbols, we prove that the localization operators are paracommutators, paraproducts and Fourier multipliers.

1.
Introduction

Time-frequency localization operators are a mathematical tool to analyze functions in different regions in the time-frequency plane. They can be viewed as transformations that modify the content of a function simultaneously in time and frequency and reconstruct a filtered signal. The localization operators were introduced and studied by Daubechies
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
, Ramanathan and Topiwala
[
18
]
, and extensively investigated in
[
12
,
20
,
22
]
. This class of operators occurs in various branches of applied and pure mathematics and has been studied by many authors. Localization operators are recognized as an important new mathematical tool and have found many applications to the theory of differential equations, quantum mechanics, time-frequency analysis, and signal processing (see
[
5
,
16
,
17
,
14
,
18
,
22
]
). They are also known as Toeplitz operators, wave packets, anti-Wick operators, or Gabor multipliers (see
[
17
,
2
,
6
,
12
]
). For a detailed study of the theory of localization operators, we refer to the series of papers of Wong
[
3
,
15
,
21
,
23
,
24
]
, and also the book of Wong
[
22
]
. In this paper, we attempt to study the localization operators on modulation spaces on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Also, we show that localization operators with separable symbols are paracommutators. Paracommutators are bilinear pseudo-differential operators that contain Hankel operator, Toeplitz operator, and many other operators as special cases. The motivation to study the localization operators as paracommutators is to generalize many well-known operators. In particular, if the symbol is dependent on one variable, we show that localization operators can be viewed as paraproducts and Fourier multipliers.

Time-frequency localization operators were defined using the Schrödinger representation and the short-time Fourier transform, which suggests studying these operators as a part of time-frequency analysis. Modulation spaces were used as the appropriate function spaces for understanding these operators, as these spaces are associated to the short-time Fourier transform. Since localization operators are known as a class of pseudo-differential operators, recent works in pseudo-differential operators on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
(see
[
4
]
) motivated us to study the localization operator on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Here, we define the modulation space on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
using the short-time Fourier transform on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. Our main aim in this paper is to expose and study the boundedness and compactness of the localization operators on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
under suitable conditions on symbols and windows and show that these operators are in the Schatten–von Neumann class. Then, we provide a connection between the Landau–Pollak–Slepian type operator and the localization operator on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Finally, we show that under suitable conditions on the symbols the localization operators are paracommutators, paraproducts and Fourier multipliers.

For the study of the localization operator on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, what kind of spaces should be considered for the symbol? In the case of the localization operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the symbol is a function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recent works in pseudo-differential operators on topological groups
G
suggest that the correct phase space to work in is
G\times\widehat{G}
, where
\widehat{G}
is the dual group of
G
. Since the dual group of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the same as
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the phase space on which symbols are defined is
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For the group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the dual group is
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and the phase space
G\times\widehat{G}
is
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. In this paper, we consider the symbol as a function on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and study the localization operator on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the set of all measurable functions
F
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that

\|F\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{p}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|F(k)|^{p}<\infty

is denoted by
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. We define
L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})
to be the set of all measurable functions
f
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
for which

\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{p}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|f(w)|^{p}\;dw<\infty.

Next, we define the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}F
of
F\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
to be the function on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
by

\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}F\right)(w)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{-2\pi ik\cdot w}F(k),\quad w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}.

Let
f
be a function on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
, then we define the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}f
of
f
to be the function on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by

\left(\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}f\right)(k)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}e^{2\pi ik\cdot w}f(w)\;dw,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Note that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})
is a surjective isomorphism.
Also,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}=\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}^{-1}=\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}^{*}
and
\left\|\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}F\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}=\|F\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})},

F\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we define
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)
to be the space of all measurable functions
H
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
such that

\|H\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{p}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|H(k,w)|^{p}\;dw<\infty.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
,
we define the short-time Fourier transform on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and study some basic properties such as orthogonality relation, Plancherel’s formula, and inversion formula. In Section
3
, we define the modulation spaces on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and present some basic properties. In Section
4
, we define and study the localization operator on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. We use modulation spaces on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
as appropriate classes for symbols, and study the boundedness and compactness of the localization operators on modulation spaces on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Also, we show that these operators are in the Schatten–von Neumann class. In Section
5
, we define the Landau–Pollak–Slepian type operator on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and show that this operator is a localization operator on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. In Section
6

6.1
, we show that the localization operators with separable symbols are paracommutators. Then, in Subsection
6.2

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
only, then the localization operator can be expressed in terms of a paraproduct. Finally, in Subsection
6.3

{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
only, then the localization operator is a Fourier multiplier.

2.
Short-time Fourier transform on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}

The Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the space of rapidly decreasing functions
g:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, i.e.
g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
if for any
M<\infty
there exists a constant
C_{g,M}
such that

|g(k)|\leq C_{g,M}(1+|k|)^{-M},\quad\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

The topology on
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
is given by the seminorms
p_{j}
, where
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
and
p_{j}(g):=\sup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|k|)^{j}|g(k)|
. The space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
of all continuous linear functionals on
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
is called the space of tempered distributions.

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,
w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. For
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, the translation operator
T_{k}
is defined by
T_{k}f(m)=f(m-k)
and the modulation operator
M_{w}
is defined by
M_{w}f(m)=e^{2\pi iw\cdot m}f(m)
. Let
g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
be a fixed window function. Then, the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) of a function
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
with respect to
g
is defined to be the function on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
given by

V_{g}f(m,w)=\left\langle f,M_{w}T_{m}g\right\rangle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}f(k)\overline{M_{w}T_{m}g(k)}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}f(k)\overline{g(k-m)}e^{-2\pi iw\cdot k}.

For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, we define
\tilde{g}(k)=g(-k)
. Then, we can write
V_{g}f
as a convolution on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

V_{g}f(m,w)=e^{-2\pi iw\cdot m}\left(f*M_{w}\overline{\tilde{g}}\right)(m).

Next, we present some basic properties of the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

(1)

(
Orthogonality relation
)
For every
f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

(2.1)

\left\langle V_{g_{1}}f_{1},V_{g_{2}}f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\langle g_{2},g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

(2)

(
Plancherel’s formula
)
Let
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function. Then for every
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

(2.2)

\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

(3)

(
Inversion formula
)
Let
g,\gamma\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
\langle g,\gamma\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\neq 0
. Then for every
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

f=\frac{1}{\langle\gamma,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}V_{g}f(m,w)\;M_{w}T_{m}\gamma\;dw.

Proof.

(1) Note that we can write
V_{g}f(m,w)=\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(f\cdot T_{m}\overline{g})(w)
. Using Parseval’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle V_{g_{1}}f_{1},V_{g_{2}}f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}V_{g_{1}}f_{1}(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}f_{2}(m,w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(f_{1}\cdot T_{m}\overline{g}_{1})(w)\;\overline{\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(f_{2}\cdot T_{m}\overline{g}_{2})(w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(f_{1}\cdot T_{m}\overline{g}_{1})(k)\;\overline{(f_{2}\cdot T_{m}\overline{g}_{2})(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}f_{1}(k)\;\overline{g_{1}(k-m)}\;\overline{f_{2}(k)}\;g_{2}(k-m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}f_{1}(k)\;\overline{f_{2}(k)}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}g_{2}(m)\;\overline{g_{1}(m)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\langle g_{2},g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

(2) By taking
g_{1}=g_{2}=g
and
f_{1}=f_{2}=f
in the relation (
2.1
), we obtain the Plancherel formula (
2.2
).

(3) Since
V_{g}f\in L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)
, the integral

\tilde{f}=\frac{1}{\langle\gamma,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}V_{g}f(m,w)\;M_{w}T_{m}\gamma\;dw

is a well-defined function in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Also, for any
h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using the orthogonality relations, we obtain that

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{f},h\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\gamma,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}V_{g}f(m,w)\;\overline{\left\langle h,M_{w}T_{m}\gamma\right\rangle}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\gamma,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\left\langle V_{g}f,V_{\gamma}h\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}=\langle f,h\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Thus
\tilde{f}=f
, and we obtain the inversion formula.
∎

3.
Modulation spaces on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

The modulation spaces were introduced by Feichtinger
[
10
,
11
]
, by imposing integrability conditions on the STFT of tempered distributions. Here we define the modulation spaces on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
using the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Fix a non-zero window
g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then the modulation space
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
consists of all tempered distributions
f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
such that
V_{g}f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. The norm on
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\|V_{g}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\bigg(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|V_{g}f(m,w)|^{p}\;dw\bigg)^{1/p}<\infty,

with the usual adjustments if
p
is infinite.

The definition of
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is independent of the choice of
g
in the sense that each different choice of
g
defines an equivalent norm on
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Each modulation space is a Banach space. For
p=2
, we have that
M^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}).
For other
p
, the space
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is not
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. In fact for
p>2
, the space
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is a superset of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Here we collect some basic properties and inclusion relations of modulation spaces on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. These results can be obtained using simple modifications of the corresponding proofs for the case of modulation spaces defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define the space of special windows
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\right)=\left\{f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}):\;f=V_{g}^{*}F=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}F(m,w)\;M_{w}T_{m}g\;dw,\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{where}\;F\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\;\text{and}\;\hbox{supp\,}F\;\text{is compact}\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{S}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\right)
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\right)
is dense in
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
.
Let
B
be a Banach space of tempered distributions with the following properties: (1)
B
is invariant under time-frequency shifts, and
\left\|T_{m}M_{w}f\right\|_{B}\leq C\|f\|_{B}
for all
f\in B
, (2)
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\cap B\neq\{0\}
. Then
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is embedded in
B
(see
[
14
]
, Theorem 12.1.9). Also,
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is invariant under time-frequency shifts and
\left\|T_{m}M_{w}f\right\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}
.
Since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\right)\subseteq M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\cap M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using a similar method as in Corollary 12.1.10 in
[
14
]
, we obtain the following inclusions

\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\subset M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\subset M^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\subset M^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\subset\mathcal{S^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}).

In particular, we have
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for
1\leq p\leq 2
, and
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\hookrightarrow M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for
2\leq p\leq\infty
. Furthermore, the dual of a modulation space is also a modulation space, if
p<\infty
,
(M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))^{{}^{\prime}}=M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
. Next, we present convolution relations which we will use in the proof of the main results.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
F\in L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
G\in L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. Then

(3.1)

\|F*G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq\|F\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
H\in L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. Using Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle|\langle F*G,H\rangle|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}F*G(m,w)\;\overline{H(m,w)}\;dw\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left|G(m-l,w-x)\right|\;|F(l,x)|\;dx\right)|H(m,w)|\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left|T_{(l,x)}G(m,w)\right|\;|H(m,w)|\;dw\right)|F(l,x)|\;dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|F(l,x)|\;\|T_{(l,x)}G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|F(l,x)|\;dx\;\|G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

By duality, we have

\|F*G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\sup\left\{|\langle F*G,H\rangle|:\|H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq 1\right\}\leq\|F\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

∎

Next, we define the modulation spaces on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
using the STFT on
({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\times({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})^{\wedge}
. For
g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\right)\backslash\{0\}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the modulation space
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
consists of all tempered distributions
F\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\right)
such that
V_{g}F\in L^{p}(({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\times({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})^{\wedge})
. The norm on
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
is defined by

\|F\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\|V_{g}F\|_{L^{p}(({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\times({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})^{\wedge})}
. Let
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\omega,\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. The STFT
V_{g}F
on
({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\times({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})^{\wedge}
is defined as

V_{g}F(m,\omega,\xi,k)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}e^{-2\pi ij\xi}e^{-2\pi i\eta k}F(j,\eta)\overline{g(j-m,\eta-\omega)}d\eta.

Here
(m,\omega)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
(\xi,k)\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\right)^{\wedge}
. For
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
, we define
\tilde{g}(j,\eta)=g(-j,-\eta)
. Then, we can write the STFT
V_{g}F
as

\displaystyle V_{g}F(m,\omega,\xi,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}e^{-2\pi im\xi}e^{-2\pi i\omega k}F(j,\eta)e^{2\pi i(m-j)\xi}e^{2\pi i(\omega-\eta)k}\overline{\tilde{g}(m-j,\omega-\eta)}d\eta

\displaystyle=e^{-2\pi im\xi}e^{-2\pi i\omega k}\left(F*M_{(\xi,k)}\overline{\tilde{g}}\right)(m,\omega),

where
M_{(\xi,k)}
is the modulation operator, that is given by

\left(M_{(\xi,k)}h\right)(j,\eta)=e^{2\pi ij\xi}e^{2\pi ik\eta}h(j,\eta),\quad j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\;\;\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

Similarly, the inclusion relations of modulation spaces on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
can be obtained using the same techniques as in modulation spaces on locally compact abelian groups (see
[
1
,
10
]
). We refer to Gröchenig’s book
[
14
]
for further properties and uses of modulation spaces.

4.
Localization operators on discrete modulation spaces

In this section, we define the localization operators on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and we show that these operators are bounded. Also, we prove that localization operators are compact and in the Schatten–von Neumann class.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\varsigma\in L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\cup L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. The localization operator associated with the symbol
\varsigma
and two window functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, is denoted by
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
, and defined on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, by

(4.1)

\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f(k)=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\;V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\;M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}(k)\;dw,\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

Also, it is useful to rewrite the definition of
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
in a weak sense as, for every
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})

(4.2)

\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\;V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)}\;dw.

We denote by
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the space of all bounded linear operators from
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
into itself. In particular,
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))
denotes the C
∗
-algebra of bounded linear operator
\mathcal{A}
from
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
into itself, equipped with the norm

\|\mathcal{A}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}=\sup_{\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq 1}\|\mathcal{A}(f)\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Next, we define the Schatten–von Neumann class
S_{p}
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. For a compact operator
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))
, the eigenvalues of the positive self-adjoint operator
|\mathcal{A}|=\sqrt{\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A}}
are called the singular values of
\mathcal{A}
and denoted by
\{s_{n}(\mathcal{A})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the Schatten–von Neumann class
S_{p}
is defined to be the space of all compact operators whose singular values lie in
\ell^{p}
.
S_{p}
is equipped with the norm

\|\mathcal{A}\|_{S_{p}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(s_{n}(\mathcal{A}))^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

For
p=\infty
, the Schatten–von Neumann class
S_{\infty}
is the class of all compact operators with the norm
\|\mathcal{A}\|_{S_{\infty}}:=\|\mathcal{A}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}
. In particular, for
p=1
, we define the trace of an operator
\mathcal{A}
in
S_{1}
by

tr(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{A}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},

where
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
is any orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{A}
is positive, then

tr(\mathcal{A})=\|\mathcal{A}\|_{S_{1}}.

A compact operator
\mathcal{A}
on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is called Hilbert–Schmidt, if the positive operator
\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A}
is in the trace class
S_{1}
. Then for any orthonormal basis
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

\|\mathcal{A}\|_{HS}^{2}:=\|\mathcal{A}\|_{S_{2}}^{2}=\|\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A}\|_{S_{1}}=tr(\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{A}v_{n}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

4.1.
Boundedness and compactness of localization operators

In this subsection, we consider window functions
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and establish the following boundedness and compactness results of localization operators.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\varsigma\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
is in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))
and we have

\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Proof.

For every
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using
Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\varsigma(m,w)|\;\left|V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\right|\;\left|V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)\right|\;dw

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\varsigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\left\|V_{g_{1}}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\left\|V_{g_{2}}h\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Using Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|\leq\left\|\varsigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|h\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Since
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\subset\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|g_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Hence,

\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
is in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))
and we have

\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Since
M^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
is the dual space of
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\varsigma(m,w)|\;\left|V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)}\right|\;dw

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\left\|V_{g_{1}}f\cdot\overline{V_{g_{2}}h}\right\|_{M^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Since
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\subset M^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\subset\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
), we obtain

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left\|V_{g_{1}}f\cdot\overline{V_{g_{2}}h}\right\|_{M^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|V_{g_{1}}f\cdot\overline{V_{g_{2}}h}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|V_{g_{1}}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\left\|V_{g_{2}}h\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|h\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|h\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Thus from (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we get

\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
is in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))
and we have

\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Proof.

For every
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using Hölder’s inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\varsigma(m,w)|\;\left|V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)}\right|\;dw

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\varsigma\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\left\|V_{g_{1}}f\cdot\overline{V_{g_{2}}h}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Since
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})=M^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, using the estimate obtained in (
4.4
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|\leq\left\|\varsigma\right\|_{M^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|h\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Hence,

\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
,
1<p<2
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then, for fixed
\varsigma\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
the operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}
can be uniquely extended to a bounded linear operator on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, such that

\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
1<p<2
. For every
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\cap M^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, using Proposition
4.3
, Proposition
4.4
, the fact that the modulation spaces
M^{p}
interpolate exactly like the corresponding mixed-norm spaces
L^{p}
and the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem (see
[
19
]
), we obtain

\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
\{\varsigma_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of functions in
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\cap M^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
such that
\varsigma_{n}\to\varsigma
in
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
as
n\to\infty
. Hence for every
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma_{n}}-\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma_{k}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma_{n}-\varsigma_{k}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Therefore,
\{\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))
. Let
\{\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
. Then the limit
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
is independent of the choice of
\{\varsigma_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma_{n}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\varsigma_{n}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\varsigma\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
,
1\leq p\leq 2
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is compact.

Proof.

Assume that
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
be an orthonormal basis for
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Since
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\subset L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, using Parseval’s identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}v_{n},v_{n}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\;\langle v_{n},M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\langle M_{w}T_{m}g_{2},v_{n}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;dw

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle v_{n},M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\langle M_{w}T_{m}g_{2},v_{n}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right)dw

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle v_{n},M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle M_{w}T_{m}g_{2},v_{n}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}|^{2}\right)\;dw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\varsigma\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}).\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

Therefore, the operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
is in
S_{1}
. Next, assume that
\varsigma\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. We consider a sequence of functions
\{\varsigma_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})\cap M^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
such that
\varsigma_{n}\to\varsigma
in
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
as
n\to\infty
. Then, using Theorem
4.5
, we get

\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma_{n}}-\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma_{n}-\varsigma\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
. Hence,
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma_{n}}\to\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))
as
n\to\infty
. From the above, we obtain that
\{\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence of linear operators in
S_{1}
and hence compact, so
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
is compact.
∎

Next, we calculate the adjoint
(\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma})^{*}
of the operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
determined by the relation

\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\left\langle f,(\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma})^{*}h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

We have

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\;V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\;\langle f,M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\;\langle f,V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)\;M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle f,\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\overline{\varsigma(m,w)}\;V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)\;M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\;dw\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle f,\mathfrak{L}^{g_{2},g_{1}}_{\overline{\varsigma}}h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Therefore, we have

(\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma})^{*}=\mathfrak{L}^{g_{2},g_{1}}_{\overline{\varsigma}}.

Hence, the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
is a self-adjoint operator if
g_{1}=g_{2}
and the symbol
\varsigma
is a real-valued function.

4.2.
Localization operators in
S_{p}

In this subsection, we prove that the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}
is in
S_{p}
and provide an upper bound of the norm
\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{p}}
. We begin with the following proposition.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
g\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is in
S_{1}
and

\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{1}}\leq 4\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Proof.

If
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, then from the first part of the proof of Theorem
4.6
, the operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}
is in
S_{1}
. Now, to prove the estimate, assume that
\varsigma
is non-negative real-valued and in
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. Then
(({\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}})^{*}\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma})^{1/2}=\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}
. Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
be an orthonormal basis for
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
consisting of eigenvalues of
(({\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}})^{*}\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma})^{1/2}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
.
Then by using the estimate obtained in the first part of the proof of Theorem
4.6
, we get

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle(({\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}})^{*}\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma})^{1/2}v_{n},v_{n}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}v_{n},v_{n}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Next, assume that
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
is an arbitrary real-valued function. We can write
\varsigma=\varsigma_{+}-\varsigma_{-}
, where
\varsigma_{+}=\max(\varsigma,0)
and
\varsigma_{-}=-\min(\varsigma,0)
. Then, using relation (
4.5
), we obtain

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{1}}=\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma_{+}}-\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma_{-}}\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma_{+}}\|_{S_{1}}+\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma_{-}}\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}(\|\varsigma_{+}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}+\|\varsigma_{-}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Finally, assume that
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
is a complex-valued function. Then, we can write
\varsigma=\varsigma_{1}+i\varsigma_{2}
, where
\varsigma_{1},\varsigma_{2}
are the real and imaginary parts of
\varsigma
respectively. Then, using relation (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{1}}=\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma_{1}}+i\;\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma_{2}}\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma_{1}}\|_{S_{1}}+\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma_{2}}\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}(\|\varsigma_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}+\|\varsigma_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
g\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is in
S_{p}
, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and

\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{p}}\leq 2^{2/p}\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Proof.

The proof follows from Proposition
4.4
, Proposition
4.7
and by interpolation theorems (see
[
22
]
, Theorems 2.10 and 2.11).
∎

Next, we improve the constant given in the previous proposition and also we provide a lower bound of the norm
\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{1}}
, more precisely we have the following.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
g\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}
is in
S_{1}
and we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\;\|\tilde{\varsigma}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{1}}\leq\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})},

where
\tilde{\varsigma}
is given by
\tilde{\varsigma}(m,w)=\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}(M_{w}T_{m}g),M_{w}T_{m}g\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}
.

Proof.

Since
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, by Proposition
4.7
,
\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}
is in
S_{1}
. Using the canonical form of compact operators (see
[
22
]
, Theorem 2.2), we obtain

(4.7)

\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma})\langle f,v_{n}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}u_{n},

where
\{s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma})\}_{n}
are the positive singular values of
\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}
,
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
is an orthonormal basis for the orthogonal complement of the null space of
\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}
consisting of eigenvectors of
|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}|
and
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is an orthonormal set in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}v_{n},u_{n}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma})=\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{1}}.

Now, using Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality and Bessel’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}v_{n},u_{n}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\;V_{g}v_{n}(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g}u_{n}(m,w)}\;dw

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\varsigma(m,w)|\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|V_{g}v_{n}(m,w)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|V_{g}u_{n}(m,w)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}dw

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\varsigma\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Next, we show that
\tilde{\varsigma}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. Using formula (
4.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\tilde{\varsigma}(m,w)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}(M_{w}T_{m}g),M_{w}T_{m}g\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma})\left\langle M_{w}T_{m}g,v_{n}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\left\langle u_{n},M_{w}T_{m}g\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma})\left(\left|\left\langle M_{w}T_{m}g,v_{n}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|^{2}+\left|\left\langle M_{w}T_{m}g,u_{n}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|^{2}\right).

Now, using Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\varsigma}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\tilde{\varsigma}(m,w)|\;dw

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma})\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\left|\left\langle M_{w}T_{m}g,v_{n}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|^{2}+\left|\left\langle M_{w}T_{m}g,u_{n}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|^{2}\right)dw

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma})

\displaystyle=\|g\|^{2}_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|\mathfrak{L}^{g,g}_{\varsigma}\|_{S_{1}}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.3.
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
Boundedness

In this section, we prove that the localization operators
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
are bounded. We begin with the following propositions.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
,
g_{1}\in M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
g_{2}\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is a bounded linear operator, and we have

\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
g\in M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then from Hölder’s inequality, we have

(4.8)

\left|V_{g}f(m,w)\right|\leq\|f\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

For every
f\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
h\in M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using the relations (
4.2
) and (
4.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\varsigma(m,w)|\;\left|V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\right|\left|V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)\right|\;dw

\displaystyle\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|f\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|h\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Hence,

\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

∎

Next, we obtain an
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
-boundedness result using the Schur technique. The estimate obtained for the norm
\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}
is different from the previous Proposition.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\cap\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then there exists a bounded linear operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\max(\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g_{2}\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})})\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the function defined on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
by

(4.9)

\mathcal{K}(k,l)=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\;\overline{M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}(l)}\;M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}(k)\;dw.

Then we define

\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f(k)=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\mathcal{K}(k,l)\;f(l).

Now, for any
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}|\mathcal{K}(k,l)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\varsigma(m,w)|\left|\overline{M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}(l)}\right|\;\left|M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}(k)\right|\;dw

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})},

and for any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, we obtain

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}|\mathcal{K}(k,l)|\leq\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Thus using Schur’s lemma (see
[
13
]
), we conclude that, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is a bounded linear operator, and we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\max(\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g_{2}\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})})\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

∎

Remark 4.12
.

From the Proposition
4.11
, we conclude that the bounded linear operator on
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, obtained in Proposition
4.10
is actually the discrete integral operator on
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
with the kernel
\mathcal{K}
given by (
4.9
).

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
\varsigma\in L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
,
g_{1}\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
g_{2}\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, for
1\leq p\leq 2
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
is in
\mathcal{B}(M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))
, and we have

\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
h\in M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
. Using the duality between the modulation spaces
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\varsigma(m,w)|\;\left|V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\right|\;\left|V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)\right|\;dw

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\varsigma\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\|f\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g_{1}\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|h\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g_{2}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Since
1\leq p\leq 2
, we have
p^{\prime}\geq 2
and
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\subset M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Using
\|g_{1}\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}
, we obtain

\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.4.
Compactness of
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
for symbols in
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})

In this section, we prove that the localization operators
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}),\;1<p<\infty
are compact for symbols
\varsigma
in
M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. Let us start with the following proposition.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then, for fixed
2\leq p<\infty
, the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is compact.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of functions in
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
such that
f_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
as
n\to\infty
. It is sufficient to prove that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f_{n}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=0
. From the relation (
4.1
), we obtain

(4.10)

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f_{n}(k)\right|\leq\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\varsigma(m,w)|\left|\left\langle f_{n},M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|\;\left|M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}(k)\right|\;dw.

Since
f_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we deduce that

(4.11)

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\varsigma(m,w)|\left|\left\langle f_{n},M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|\;\left|M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}(k)\right|=0,\quad\text{for all}\;\;m,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\text{\;and\;}w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}.

Moreover, as
f_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
as
n\to\infty
, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\|f_{n}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq C
. Hence, for all
m,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\text{\;and\;}w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
, we obtain

(4.12)

|\varsigma(m,w)|\left|\left\langle f_{n},M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|\left|M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}(k)\right|\leq C|\varsigma(m,w)|\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\left|M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}(k)\right|.

Since
p\geq 2
, using
\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\subset\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\subset M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we get

(4.13)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f_{n}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f_{n}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\varsigma(m,w)|\left|\left\langle f_{n},M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|\left|M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}(k)\right|\;dw

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|\varsigma(m,w)|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\left|M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}(k)\right|\;dw

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}<\infty.

Thus, using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem and the relations (
4.10
) – (
4.13
), we obtain that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f_{n}\|_{M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
,
g_{1}\in M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
g_{2}\in M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, for
1<p<\infty
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is compact.

Proof.

Let
p^{\prime}
be the conjugate exponent of
p
. We first show that the conclusion of the previous proposition holds for
p^{\prime}
. The operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is the adjoint of the operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{2},g_{1}}_{\overline{\varsigma}}:M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, which is compact by the previous proposition for fixed
2\leq p<\infty
. Hence, by the duality properties of modulation spaces,
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is compact for fixed
1<p^{\prime}\leq 2
. Finally, using the interpolation of the compactness on
M^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and on
M^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, the proof is complete.
∎

5.
The Landau–Pollak–Slepian Operator

In this section, we show that the Landau–Pollak–Slepian type operator is actually a localization operator. We first need to introduce the following operators to define the Landau–Pollak–Slepian operator on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

Let
\Omega
and
T
be two positive numbers. Then we define the operators
P_{\Omega}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
Q_{T}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
by

(5.1)

(P_{\Omega}f)^{\wedge}(w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{f}(w),&|w|\leq\Omega,\\
0,&|w|>\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
,
|w|=|w_{1}|+|w_{2}|+\cdots+|w_{n}|
and

(5.2)

\left(Q_{T}f\right)(k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}f(k),&|k|\leq T,\\
0,&|k|>T,\end{array}\right.

where
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,
|k|=|k_{1}|+|k_{2}|+\cdots+|k_{n}|
and
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The operators
P_{\Omega}
and
Q_{T}
are self-adjoint projections.

Proof.

For
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using Plancherel’s formula, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle P_{\Omega}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(P_{\Omega}f)^{\wedge},\hat{h}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(P_{\Omega}f)^{\wedge}(w)\;\overline{\hat{h}(w)}\;dw=\int_{B_{\Omega}}\hat{f}(w)\;\overline{\hat{h}(w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\Omega}}\hat{f}(w)\;\overline{(P_{\Omega}h)^{\wedge}(w)}\;dw=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(w)\;\overline{(P_{\Omega}h)^{\wedge}(w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{f},(P_{\Omega}h)^{\wedge}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\left\langle f,P_{\Omega}h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},

where
B_{\Omega}:=\left\{w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}:\;|w|\leq\Omega\right\}
. Hence the operator
P_{\Omega}
is self-adjoint. Also, for
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle Q_{T}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(Q_{T}f)(k)\;\overline{h(k)}=\sum_{k\in B_{T}}f(k)\;\overline{h(k)}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}f(k)\;\overline{(Q_{T}h)(k)}=\left\langle f,Q_{T}h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},

where
B_{T}:=\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:\;|k|\leq T\right\}
. Hence the operator
Q_{T}
is self-adjoint. Since the operator
P_{\Omega}
is self-adjoint, using Plancherel’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle P^{2}_{\Omega}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle P_{\Omega}f,P_{\Omega}h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\left\langle(P_{\Omega}f)^{\wedge},(P_{\Omega}h)^{\wedge}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(P_{\Omega}f)^{\wedge}(w)\;\overline{(P_{\Omega}h)^{\wedge}(w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\Omega}}\hat{f}(w)\;\overline{\hat{h}(w)}\;dw=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(P_{\Omega}f)^{\wedge}(w)\;\overline{\hat{h}(w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(P_{\Omega}f)^{\wedge},\hat{h}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\left\langle P_{\Omega}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},

for all
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Thus,
P^{2}_{\Omega}=P_{\Omega}
and therefore
P_{\Omega}
is a projection. Finally, using the fact that the operator
Q_{T}
is self-adjoint, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle Q^{2}_{T}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle Q_{T}f,Q_{T}h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(Q_{T}f)(k)\;\overline{(Q_{T}h)(k)}=\sum_{k\in B_{T}}f(k)\;\overline{h(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(Q_{T}f)(k)\;\overline{h(k)}=\left\langle Q_{T}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},

for all
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Thus,
Q^{2}_{T}=Q_{T}
and therefore
Q_{T}
is a projection.
∎

The linear operator
P_{\Omega}Q_{T}P_{\Omega}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is called the Landau–Pollak–Slepian operator on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Next, we show that the operator
Q_{2T}P_{\Omega}Q_{2T}
is actually a localization operator on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be two functions on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
defined by

g_{1}(k)=g_{2}(k)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}}\chi_{B_{T}}(k),\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},

where
\mathrm{card}(B_{T})
is the cardinality of
B_{T}
and

\chi_{B_{T}}
is the characteristic function on
B_{T}
defined by

\chi_{B_{T}}(k)=\begin{cases}1,&|k|\leq T,\\
0,&|k|>T.\end{cases}

Let
\varsigma
be the characteristic function on
B_{T}\times B_{\Omega}
, given by

\varsigma(m,w)=\begin{cases}1,&m\in B_{T}\;\mathrm{and}\;w\in B_{\Omega},\\
0,&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then the operator
Q_{2T}P_{\Omega}Q_{2T}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is equal to the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. In fact,
Q_{2T}P_{\Omega}Q_{2T}=\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}.

Proof.

We have

\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\left|\chi_{B_{T}}(k)\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}\sum_{k\in B_{T}}1=1.

Also, for all
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\;V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in B_{T}}\int_{B_{\Omega}}V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)}\;dw.

Now, we compute

\displaystyle V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f,M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\langle\hat{f},\widehat{M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\langle\hat{f},T_{w}M_{-m}\hat{g_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{T_{w}M_{-m}\hat{g_{1}}(\xi)}\;d\xi=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{M_{-m}\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)\;e^{2\pi im\cdot(\xi-w)}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot w}\;e^{2\pi im\cdot\xi}\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}}\sum_{k\in B_{T}}e^{2\pi ik\cdot(\xi-w)}\;d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}}\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot w}\sum_{k\in B_{T}}e^{-2\pi ik\cdot w}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)\;e^{2\pi i(m+k)\cdot\xi}\;d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}}\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot w}\sum_{k\in B_{T}}e^{-2\pi ik\cdot w}\;f(m+k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}}\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot w}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}e^{-2\pi ik\cdot w}\;(T_{-m}Q_{2T}f)(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}}\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot w}\;(T_{-m}Q_{2T}f)^{\wedge}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}}\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot w}\;M_{m}(Q_{2T}f)^{\wedge}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}}\;(Q_{2T}f)^{\wedge}(w).

Similarly, we get

V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}}\;(Q_{2T}h)^{\wedge}(w).

Hence, using Plancherel’s formula and the fact that the operators
P_{\Omega}
and
Q_{2T}
are self-adjoint projections, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{card}(B_{T})}\sum_{m\in B_{T}}\int_{B_{\Omega}}(Q_{2T}f)^{\wedge}(w)\;\overline{(Q_{2T}h)^{\wedge}(w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\Omega}}(Q_{2T}f)^{\wedge}(w)\;\overline{(Q_{2T}h)^{\wedge}(w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(P_{\Omega}Q_{2T}f)^{\wedge}(w)\;\overline{(P_{\Omega}Q_{2T}h)^{\wedge}(w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(P_{\Omega}Q_{2T}f)^{\wedge},(P_{\Omega}Q_{2T}h)^{\wedge}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\left\langle P_{\Omega}Q_{2T}f,P_{\Omega}Q_{2T}h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle P^{2}_{\Omega}Q_{2T}f,Q_{2T}h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\left\langle Q_{2T}P_{\Omega}Q_{2T}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},

for all
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, and therefore we have the desired result.
∎

6.
Localization operators for different types of symbols

6.1.
Paracommutators

Let
\varsigma
be a function on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
given by

\varsigma(m,w)=\alpha(m)\beta(w),\quad(m,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n},

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are suitable functions on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
respectively. Then for all
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using Plancherel’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varsigma(m,w)\;V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\alpha(m)\beta(w)\;\langle f,M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\overline{\langle h,M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}\rangle}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;dw

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\alpha(m)\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\;\langle\hat{f},\widehat{M_{w}T_{m}g_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\overline{\langle\hat{h},\widehat{M_{w}T_{m}g_{2}}\rangle}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;dw

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\alpha(m)\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\;\langle\hat{f},T_{w}M_{-m}\hat{g_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\overline{\langle\hat{h},T_{w}M_{-m}\hat{g_{2}}\rangle}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;dw

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\alpha(m)\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{T_{w}M_{-m}\hat{g_{1}}(\xi)}\;d\xi\right)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;T_{w}M_{-m}\hat{g_{2}}(\eta)\;d\eta\right)dw

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\alpha(m)\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{M_{-m}\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;d\xi\right)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;M_{-m}\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\eta\right)dw

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\alpha(m)\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)\;e^{2\pi im\cdot(\xi-w)}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;d\xi\right)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot(\eta-w)}\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\eta\right)dw

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\alpha(m)\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)\;e^{2\pi im\cdot\xi}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;d\xi\right)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot\eta}\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\eta\right)dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\alpha(m)\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot(\eta-\xi)}\right)\beta(w)\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\xi\;d\eta\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{\alpha}(\eta-\xi)\;\beta(w)\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\xi\;d\eta\;dw

(6.1)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}A(\xi,\eta)\;\hat{\alpha}(\eta-\xi)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;d\xi\;d\eta,

where

A(\xi,\eta)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\;\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;dw,

for all
\xi,\eta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. Thus, the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}
is a paracommutator with Fourier kernel
A
and symbol
\alpha
.

6.2.
A Paraproduct Connection

Here, we consider the case when the symbol
\varsigma
is independent of the second variable, i.e.,

\varsigma(m,w)=\alpha(m),\quad(m,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n},

where
\alpha
is a function on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Let
\tilde{g_{1}}
be the function on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
defined by
\tilde{g_{1}}(k)=\overline{g_{1}(-k)}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Now, for all
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using Plancherel’s formula as in the preceding subsection, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{\alpha}(\eta-\xi)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\xi\;d\eta\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{\alpha}(\eta-\xi)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;T_{w}\hat{\tilde{g_{1}}}(\xi)\;T_{w}\hat{g_{2}}(\eta)\;d\xi\;dw\right)\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{\alpha}(\eta-\xi)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}})^{\wedge}(\xi)\;(M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}(\eta)\;d\xi\;dw\right)\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{\alpha}(\eta-\xi)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}})^{\wedge}(\xi)\;(M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}(\eta)\;d\xi\right)dw\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{\alpha}(\eta-\xi)\;(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)^{\wedge}(\xi)\;(M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}(\eta)\;d\xi\right)dw\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(\hat{\alpha}*(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)^{\wedge})(\eta)\;(M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}(\eta)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;d\eta\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(\alpha(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f))^{\wedge}(\eta)\;(M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}(\eta)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;d\eta\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(\alpha(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)*M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}(\eta)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;d\eta\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left\langle(\alpha(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)*M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge},\hat{h}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left\langle(\alpha(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)*M_{w}g_{2}),h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;dw

\displaystyle=\left\langle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(\alpha(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)*M_{w}g_{2})\;dw,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Hence

\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f(k)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(\alpha(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)*M_{w}g_{2})(k)\;dw,\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

Further,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\left\langle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(\alpha(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)*M_{w}g_{2})\;dw,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(\alpha(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)*M_{w}g_{2})(k)\;dw\;\overline{h(k)}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\alpha(k)\;p_{g_{1},g_{2}}(f,h)(k),

for all
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, where
p_{g_{1},g_{2}}(f,h)
is the paraproduct of
f
and
h
with respect
to the window functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
given by

p_{g_{1},g_{2}}(f,h)(k)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)(k)\;\overline{(\overline{M_{w}g_{2}(-\;\cdot)}*h)(k)}\;dw,\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

Next, we give an
\ell^{1}
-estimate on the paraproduct
p_{g_{1},g_{2}}(f,h)
, where
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, and the window functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are also in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}p_{g_{1},g_{2}}(f,h)(k)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)(k)\;\overline{(\overline{M_{w}g_{2}(-\;\cdot)}*h)(k)}\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left\langle(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f),(\overline{M_{w}g_{2}(-\;\cdot)}*h)\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left\langle(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)^{\wedge},(\overline{M_{w}g_{2}(-\;\cdot)}*h)^{\wedge}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}}*f)^{\wedge}(\xi)\;\overline{(\overline{M_{w}g_{2}(-\;\cdot)}*h)^{\wedge}(\xi)}\;d\xi\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}})^{\wedge}(\xi)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{(\overline{M_{w}g_{2}(-\;\cdot)})^{\wedge}(\xi)}\;\overline{\hat{h}(\xi)}\;d\xi\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}(M_{w}\tilde{g_{1}})^{\wedge}(\xi)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;(M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}(\xi)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\xi)}\;d\xi\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}T_{w}\hat{\tilde{g_{1}}}(\xi)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;T_{w}\hat{g_{2}}(\xi)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\xi)}\;d\xi\;dw

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;\hat{g_{2}}(\xi-w)\;dw\right)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\xi)}\;d\xi.

In the following, we obtain an
\ell^{1}
-estimate for the paraproduct
p_{g_{1},g_{2}}(f,h)
in terms of the
\ell^{2}
-norms of
f
,
h
,
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
. From Proposition
4.2
, we know that localization operators
\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
associated to symbols
\varsigma\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
are bounded linear operators such that

\left\|\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Hence

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\alpha(k)\;p_{g_{1},g_{2}}(f,h)(k)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|h\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Since
p_{g_{1},g_{2}}(f,h)\in\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, it follows from the Hahn–Banach theorem that
p_{g_{1},g_{2}}(f,h)
is in the dual
(\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}))^{*}
of
\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and

\|p_{g_{1},g_{2}}(f,h)\|_{\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq\|g_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|h\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

6.3.
Fourier Multipliers

Let
\varsigma
be a function on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
given by

\varsigma(m,w)=\beta(w),\quad(m,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}.

Then, by (
6.1
), we obtain for all
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)\;e^{2\pi im\cdot\xi}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;d\xi\right)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot\eta}\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\eta\right)dw

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;e^{2\pi im\cdot(\xi-\eta)}\;\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\xi\;d\eta\;dw.

Let
\phi(m)=e^{-|m|^{2}/2}
,
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. For all positive numbers
\varepsilon
, let
\phi_{\varepsilon}(m)=e^{-\varepsilon^{2}|m|^{2}/2}
and
I_{\varepsilon}
be the number defined by

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;e^{-\varepsilon^{2}|m|^{2}/2}\;e^{2\pi im\cdot(\xi-\eta)}\;\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\eta\;dw\;d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}e^{-\varepsilon^{2}|m|^{2}/2}\;e^{-2\pi im\cdot(\xi-\eta)}\right)\beta(w)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\eta\;dw\;d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\eta)\;\beta(w)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{\hat{g_{1}}(\xi-w)}\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;\hat{g_{2}}(\eta-w)\;d\eta\;dw\;d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\hat{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\eta)\;(M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}(\eta)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\eta)}\;d\eta\right)\beta(w)\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;\overline{(M_{w}g_{1})^{\wedge}(\xi)}\;dw\;d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\;\overline{(M_{w}g_{1})^{\wedge}(\xi)}\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;(((M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}\;\overline{\hat{h}})*\hat{\phi_{\varepsilon}})(\xi)\;dw\;d\xi.

Now, there exists a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of positive numbers such that
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
, as
j\to\infty
and

((M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}\;\overline{\hat{h}})*\hat{\phi_{\varepsilon_{j}}}\to(M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}\;\overline{\hat{h}}

in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and almost everywhere on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
as
j\to\infty
. Thus,

I_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\;\overline{(M_{w}g_{1})^{\wedge}(\xi)}\;\hat{f}(\xi)\;(M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}(\xi)\;\overline{\hat{h}(\xi)}\;dw\;d\xi.

Moreover, we can write

I_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\;e^{-\varepsilon^{2}|m|^{2}/2}\;V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)}\;dw.

Thus, using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we obtain that

I_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\;V_{g_{1}}f(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}h(m,w)}\;dw=\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

as
j\to\infty
. Hence for all
f,h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
,

\left\langle\mathfrak{L}^{g_{1},g_{2}}_{\varsigma}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\left\langle T_{\mu}f,h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},

where
T_{\mu}
is the Fourier multiplier with symbol
\mu
given by

\mu(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\beta(w)\;\overline{(M_{w}g_{1})^{\wedge}(\xi)}\;(M_{w}g_{2})^{\wedge}(\xi)\;dw,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}.

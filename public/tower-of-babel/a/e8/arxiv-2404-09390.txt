Skyrmion-mechanical hybrid quantum systems: Manipulation of skyrmion qubits via phonons
Xue-Feng Pan, Xin-Lei Hei, Xiao-Yu Yao, Jia-Qiang Chen, Yu-Meng Ren, Xing-Liang Dong, Yi-Fan Qiao, Peng-Bo Li

Abstract
Skyrmion qubits are a new highly promising logic element for quantum information processing. However, their scalability to multiple interacting qubits remains challenging. We propose a hybrid quantum setup with skyrmion qubits strongly coupled to nanomechanical cantilevers via magnetic coupling, which harnesses phonons as quantum interfaces for the manipulation of distant skyrmion qubits. A linear drive is utilized to achieve the modulation of the stiffness coefficient of the cantilever, resulting in an exponential enhancement of the coupling strength between the skyrmion qubit and the mechanical mode. We also consider the case of a topological resonator array, which allows us to study interactions between skyrmion qubits and topological phonon band structure, as well as chiral skyrmion-skyrmion interactions. The scheme suggested here offers a fascinating platform for investigating quantum information processing and quantum simulation with magnetic microstructures.

II.3
The phonon-skyrmion hybrid quantum system

The total Hamiltonian of the hybrid quantum system is represented as

\hat{H}_{\rm{TTS}}=\hat{H}_{\rm{Sky}}+\hat{H}_{m}+\hat{H}_{\rm{TS}}+\hat{H}_{\rm{mw}}.

(11)

In this case, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm{Sky}}
is not diagonalized. The eigenstates of the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm{Sky}}
are
|\psi_{+}\rangle=\cos\theta|1\rangle-\sin\theta|0\rangle
and
|\psi_{-}\rangle=\sin\theta|1\rangle+\cos\theta|0\rangle
, and the corresponding eigenenergies are
\mathcal{E}_{\pm}=\pm\sqrt{\mathcal{A}_{0}^{2}+\mathcal{B}_{0}^{2}}/2
with
\tan(2\theta)\equiv\mathcal{B}_{0}/\mathcal{A}_{0}
. In terms of
|\psi_{+}\rangle
and
|\psi_{-}\rangle
, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm{Sky}}
can be expressed as
\hat{H}_{\rm{Sky}}=\omega_{q}\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{D}/2
, where the resonance frequency of the skyrmion qubit is denoted by
\omega_{q}=\mathcal{E}_{+}-\mathcal{E}_{-}
. In the subspace
\left\{|\psi_{+}\rangle,|\psi_{-}\rangle\right\}
, the interaction Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm{TS}}
and the microwave-driven Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm{mw}}
reduce to

\hat{H}_{\rm{TS}}=-\frac{\lambda_{\rm{TS}}}{2}\left(\hat{a}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)\left[\cos(2\theta)\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{D}+\sin(2\theta)\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{D}\right],

(12a)

\hat{H}_{\rm{mw}}=\Omega_{\rm{mw}}\cos\left(\omega_{\rm{mw}}t\right)\left[\cos(2\theta)\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{D}+\sin(2\theta)\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{D}\right]

(12b)

with Pauli operators
\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{D}=|\psi_{+}\rangle\langle\psi_{+}|-|\psi_{-}\rangle\langle\psi_{-}|
and
\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{D}=|\psi_{+}\rangle\langle\psi_{-}|+|\psi_{-}\rangle\langle\psi_{+}|
. If the
\mathfrak{S}_{z}
qubit is supposed to work near the degeneracy point, subsequently

and
\sin(2\theta)\sim 1
can be obtained. The term containing
\cos(2\theta)
can therefore be removed, reducing the Hamiltonian (
12a
) and (

) to

\hat{H}_{\rm{TS}}=-\frac{\lambda_{\rm{TS}}}{2}\left(\hat{a}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{D},

(13a)

\hat{H}_{\rm{mw}}=\Omega_{\rm{mw}}\cos\left(\omega_{\rm{mw}}t\right)\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{D}.

(13b)

The Hamiltonian of the
\mathfrak{S}_{z}
qubit

\hat{H}_{\rm{dress}}=\frac{\omega_{q}}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{D}+\Omega_{\rm{mw}}\cos\left(\omega_{\rm{mw}}t\right)\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{D}

(14)

can be diagonalized by the eigenstates
|\widetilde{\psi}_{+}\rangle=\cos\beta|\psi_{+}\rangle+\sin\beta|\psi_{-}\rangle
and
|\widetilde{\psi}_{-}\rangle=\sin\beta|\psi_{+}\rangle-\cos\beta|\psi_{-}\rangle

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\pm}=\pm 1/2\sqrt{\Delta_{\rm qmw}^{2}+\Omega_{\rm{mw}}^{2}}
. Here
\Delta_{\rm qmw}=\omega_{q}-\omega_{\rm{mw}}
and
\tan(2\beta)=\Omega_{\rm{mw}}/\Delta_{\rm qmw}
. In this case, the total Hamiltonian of the hybrid system is

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{\rm{TTS}}&=\frac{\widetilde{\omega}_{q}}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{z}+\omega_{m}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\\
&-\frac{\lambda_{\rm{TS}}}{2}\left(\hat{a}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)\left[\sin(2\beta)\hat{\sigma}_{z}-\cos(2\beta)\hat{\sigma}_{x}\right],\end{split}

(15)

where
\widetilde{\omega}_{q}\equiv\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{+}-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{-}
,
\hat{\sigma}_{z}\equiv|\widetilde{\psi}_{+}\rangle\langle\widetilde{\psi}_{+}|-|\widetilde{\psi}_{-}\rangle\langle\widetilde{\psi}_{-}|

\hat{\sigma}_{x}\equiv|\widetilde{\psi}_{+}\rangle\langle\widetilde{\psi}_{-}|+|\widetilde{\psi}_{-}\rangle\langle\widetilde{\psi}_{+}|

\hat{H}_{\rm{TTS}}=\frac{\widetilde{\omega}_{q}}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{z}+\omega_{m}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+\bar{\lambda}_{\rm{TS}}\left(\hat{a}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)\hat{\sigma}_{x},

where the coupling strength is defined as

VI
Conclusion

We propose a hybrid quantum system composed of skyrmion qubits and nanomechanical cantilevers, and show that the coherent coupling between them can reach the strong coupling regime. It is feasible to modulate the skyrmion qubit by employing phonons as quantum interfaces. Firstly, we show the enhancement and modulation of the skyrmion-phonon coupling strength by modulating the two phonon drive. Even ultrastrong coupling of the system can be obtained by increasing the squeezed parameter
r
. Furthermore, by adiabatically eliminating the phonon modes, we establish strong skyrmion-skyrmion interactions. When considering a coupled resonator array, the long-range skyrmion-skyrmion interactions by exchanging virtual phonons can be realized
[
128
]
. More interestingly, when a skyrmion qubit is coupled to a topological oscillator array, we can obtain a chiral skyrmion-phonon bound state, and we can alter the chirality by changing the squeezed parameter
r
. Utilizing the chiral skyrmion-phonon bound state, chiral skyrmion-skyrmion interactions can be obtained. It can be modulated by altering the coupling position of the skyrmion qubit or the hopping rate between phonons. The highly controllable hybrid quantum system suggested here offers a feasible platform for quantum technologies and quantum information.

Appendix A
The classical skyrmion

A skyrmion is a centrosymmetric vortex-structured spin texture. The skyrmion in the frustrated material, in particular, possesses an internal degree of freedom: helicity
\varphi_{0}
. According to the Ginzburg-Landau theory
[
63
]
, the Hamiltonian of the skyrmion in frustrated material can be represented as

\mathcal{H}=\int d\widetilde{\bm{r}}\left[-\frac{\mathcal{J}_{1}^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla_{\widetilde{\bm{r}}}\bm{s}\right)^{2}+\frac{\mathcal{J}_{2}a^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla^{2}_{\widetilde{\bm{r}}}\bm{s}\right)^{2}-\frac{H}{a^{2}}s_{z}+\frac{K}{a^{2}}s_{z}^{2}\right].

(35)

The vector
\bm{s}
is the normalized spin vector, defined as
\bm{s}=\bm{S}/|\bm{S}|=(s_{x},s_{y},s_{z})
, employed to describe the spin direction at location
\widetilde{\bm{r}}=(\widetilde{\rho},\phi)
, where
\widetilde{\rho}
and
\phi
are the polar coordinate variables. We have assumed that the skyrmion is in the
xy
plane, with its center at the coordinate origin. For a classical skyrmion,
\bm{s}=[\sin\Theta(\widetilde{\rho})\cos\Phi,\sin\Theta(\widetilde{\rho})\sin\Phi,\cos\Theta(\widetilde{\rho})]
with
\Phi=\phi+\varphi_{0}
. Minimizing the energy
\mathcal{H}
yields the classical stationary solutions
\Theta_{0}
and
\Phi_{0}
. Here we take the approximate solution
\Theta_{0}=\pi/\sqrt{\rho^{2}+1}\exp(-\mathcal{Y}_{\rm{Re}}\rho)\cos(-\mathcal{Y}_{\rm{Im}}\rho)
with
\mathcal{Y}_{\rm{Re}}=\rm{Re}(\mathcal{Y})
,
\mathcal{Y}_{\rm{Im}}=\rm{Im}(\mathcal{Y})

\mathcal{Y}=\sqrt{-1+\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}}/\sqrt{2}

\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}=\sqrt{1-4(h+\kappa_{z})}

Appendix B
The skyrmion qubit

The collective coordinate quantization approach has been thoroughly discussed in Ref.
[
112
,
113
,
60
,
64
]
, and the main process of the method will be described here. The partition function of the skyrmion studied here is

\mathcal{Z}=\int\mathcal{D}\bm{s}\exp\left[i\mathcal{S}\left(\bm{s},\dot{\bm{s}}\right)\right]

(36)

with action
\mathcal{S}=\int dt\mathcal{L}
, where
\mathcal{L}
denotes Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\bar{S}\int d\bm{r}\left[\mathfrak{A}\left(\bm{s}\right)\cdot\dot{\bm{s}}-\mathcal{F}\right]

(37)

with gauge potential
\mathfrak{A}=\left\{[1-\widetilde{e}_{\Phi}\cdot(e_{\Phi}\times\bm{s})]/(\widetilde{e}_{\Phi})\cdot\bm{s}\right\}e_{\Phi}
.
\mathfrak{A}(\bm{s})\cdot\bm{s}=(1-\cos\Theta)\dot{\Phi}
can be obtained by using
\widetilde{e}_{\Phi}=(\cos\Phi,\sin\Phi,0)
and
e_{\Phi}=(-\sin\Phi,\cos\Phi,0)
.
\mathcal{F}
is the energy density functional, which is defined as

\mathcal{F}=-\frac{\mathcal{J}_{1}^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla_{\widetilde{\bm{r}}}\bm{s}\right)^{2}+\frac{\mathcal{J}_{2}a^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla^{2}_{\widetilde{\bm{r}}}\bm{s}\right)^{2}-\frac{H}{a^{2}}s_{z}+\frac{K}{a^{2}}s_{z}^{2}.

(38)

Based on the global symmetry of the model, we can get
\bm{s}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}(\varphi_{0})\bm{s}
, where
\mathcal{M}(\varphi_{0})
is given by

\mathcal{M}(\varphi_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\varphi_{0}&-\sin\varphi_{0}&0\\
\sin\varphi_{0}&\cos\varphi_{0}&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(39)

By introducing the vector
\bm{n}=\sqrt{1-\cos\Theta}\bm{s}/\sin\Theta
, the gauge potential can be redefined as
\bm{\mathfrak{A}}(\bm{n})=\partial_{\Phi}\bm{n}
. Based on the collective coordinate quantization method, we introduce
\delta
constraints
[
113
,
141
]

\int\mathcal{D}\varphi_{0}\mathcal{D}\mathfrak{S}_{z}J_{\varphi_{0}}J_{\mathfrak{S}_{z}}\delta(F_{1})\delta(F_{2})=1

(40)

with

\begin{split}F_{1}&=\int d\bm{r}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}(\bm{n}_{0})\cdot\left(\widetilde{\bm{n}}-\bm{n}_{0}\right),\\
F_{2}&=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int d\bm{r}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}(\bm{n}_{0})\cdot\left[\widetilde{\bm{\mathfrak{A}}}(\bm{n})-\bm{\mathfrak{A}}(\bm{n}_{0})\right],\\
J_{\varphi_{0}}&=\frac{\delta F_{1}}{\delta\varphi_{0}},~J_{\mathfrak{S}_{z}}=\frac{\delta F_{2}}{\delta\mathfrak{S}_{z}},\end{split}

(41)

which eliminate zero modes. The Jacobians of the transformation are
J_{\varphi_{0}}
and
J_{\mathfrak{S}_{z}}
. We express the field as its classical solution plus its corresponding quantum fluctuations, introducing
\bm{n}=\widetilde{\bm{n}}_{0}+\widetilde{\bm{\gamma}}
and
\bm{\mathfrak{A}}(\bm{n})=c\widetilde{\bm{\mathfrak{A}}}(\bm{n}_{0})+\widetilde{\bm{\zeta}}
. The parameter
c
ensures that the transformations described above are canonical. And applying the constraint
P-\mathfrak{S}_{z}=F_{2}
, we can get

c=\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{z}-\int d\bm{r}\widetilde{\bm{\zeta}}\cdot\partial_{\phi}\bm{n}}{\int d\bm{r}\widetilde{\bm{\mathfrak{A}}}(\bm{n}_{0})\cdot\partial_{\phi}\bm{n}}.

(42)

In other words, we can derive the path integral in phase space retaining the canonical form
\int dtd\bm{r}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}(\bm{n})\cdot\dot{\bm{n}}=\int dt\mathfrak{S}_{z}\dot{\varphi}_{0}+\int dtd\bm{r}\bm{\zeta}\cdot\dot{\bm{\gamma}}
. In order to simplify the analysis of the transformation of the energy functional
\mathcal{F}
, the transformation

\begin{split}\widetilde{\Pi}(\bm{r},t)&=\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{z}-\int d\bm{r}\eta(\bm{r,t})\partial_{\phi}\Phi(\bm{r},t)}{\Lambda}\widetilde{\Pi}_{0}(\bm{r},t)+\eta(\bm{r},t),\\
\Phi(\bm{r},t)&=\Phi_{0}\left[\bm{r},\varphi_{0}(t)\right]+\xi(\bm{r},t)\end{split}

(43)

is introduced, ensuring that the canonical form of the Wess-Zumino (WZ) term
\int d\bm{r}\widetilde{\Pi}\dot{\Phi}=\mathfrak{S}_{z}\dot{\varphi}_{0}+\int d\bm{r}\eta\dot{\xi}
is maintained. Based on
\mathcal{S}=\int dt\mathcal{L}
and Eq. (
37
), we can derive the real-time action as
[
141
]

\mathcal{S}=\frac{\bar{S}}{a^{2}}\int d\widetilde{t}d\widetilde{\bm{r}}\dot{\Phi}\left(1-\Pi\right)-\int d\widetilde{t}\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{S}_{z}},

(44)

where
\dot{\Phi}\equiv\partial_{t}\Phi
and
\Pi\equiv\cos\Theta
. Please keep in mind that we only discuss the construction of
\mathfrak{S}_{z}
qubits, which can be described by the Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{S}_{z}}=\bar{S}\int d\widetilde{\bm{r}}\left[-\frac{H}{a^{2}}s_{z}+\frac{K}{a^{2}}s_{z}^{2}-EP_{E}a\hat{\bm{e}}_{z}\cdot\bm{P}\right],

(45)

(46)

Appendix F
Tuning the hopping rate to implement a SSH chain

The hopping rates
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
between phonons depend on the voltage
U
. One can realize the SSH model by adjusting the voltage
U
. At the beginning, it is assumed that the hopping rates between phonons are all equal, i.e.,
G_{1}=G_{2}=G
, corresponding to a voltage of
U_{0}
. By adjusting the voltage
U
we get
G_{1}=(1+\delta)G
and
G_{2}=(1-\delta)G
, corresponding to the voltages
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, respectively. Using
\textcolor
[rgb]0,0,1
g_{n}=z_{0}^{2}U_{n}^{2}C^{2}C_{W}^{2}/[h^{2}(2C+C_{W})^{3}]
and
\textcolor
[rgb]0,0,1
G_{n}=g_{n}\exp(2r_{n})/2
, we get

\begin{split}G_{1}=\frac{g_{1}}{2}\exp(2r_{1})&=\frac{z_{0}^{2}U_{1}^{2}C^{2}C_{W}^{2}}{h^{2}(2C+C_{W})^{3}}\frac{\exp(2r_{1})}{2}\\
&=(1+\delta)\frac{z_{0}^{2}U_{0}^{2}C^{2}C_{W}^{2}}{h^{2}(2C+C_{W})^{3}}\frac{\exp(2r_{0})}{2},\\
G_{2}=\frac{g_{2}}{2}\exp(2r_{2})&=\frac{z_{0}^{2}U_{2}^{2}C^{2}C_{W}^{2}}{h^{2}(2C+C_{W})^{3}}\frac{\exp(2r_{2})}{2}\\
&=(1-\delta)\frac{z_{0}^{2}U_{0}^{2}C^{2}C_{W}^{2}}{h^{2}(2C+C_{W})^{3}}\frac{\exp(2r_{0})}{2}.\end{split}

(65)

Then we can obtain the dependence of
\delta
on squeezing parameter
r
and voltage
U
as

\delta=1-\frac{U_{2}^{2}}{U_{0}^{2}}e^{2(r_{2}-r_{0})}=\frac{U_{1}^{2}}{U_{0}^{2}}e^{2(r_{1}-r_{0})}-1.

(66)

That is, we can realize the modulation of
\delta
by adjusting the voltage
U
or the squeezing parameter
r
, and it satisfies the relation

U_{1}^{2}e^{2(r_{1}-r_{0})}+U_{2}^{2}e^{2(r_{2}-r_{0})}=2U_{0}^{2}.

(67)

In particular, when
r_{1}=r_{2}=r_{0}
, one can obtain
U_{1}^{2}+U_{2}^{2}=2U_{0}^{2}
. That is, we need to adjust the voltages to
U_{1}>U_{0}
and
U_{2}<U_{0}
.

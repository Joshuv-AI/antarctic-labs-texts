Abstract
. The main goal of this article is to study the effect of small, highly nonlinear, unbounded drifts (small time large deviation principle (LDP) based on exponential equivalence arguments) for a class of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) with fully monotone coefficients driven by multiplicative Gaussian noise. The small time LDP obtained in this paper is applicable for various quasi-linear and semilinear SPDEs such as porous medium equations, Cahn-Hilliard equation, 2D Navier-Stokes equations, convection-diffusion equation, 2D liquid crystal model, power law fluids, Ladyzhenskaya model, p p -Laplacian equations, etc., perturbed by multiplicative Gaussian noise.



1.
Introduction

In this work, we analyze the small time asymptotics of the following class of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) with fully local monotone coefficients in a Gelfand triplet
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}^{*}
driven by a multiplicative Gaussian noise:

(1.1)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}(t)&=\mathrm{A}(t,\mathbf{Y}(t))\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{B}(t,\mathbf{Y}(t))\mathrm{d}\mathrm{W}(t),\ \ t\in[0,T],\\
\mathbf{Y}(0)&=\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{H},\end{aligned}}}\right.

\mathbb{H}
and

denote the dual of the spaces

and
\mathbb{H}

and

. Also, we have

\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{V}
. Let
\mathbb{U}
be an another Hilbert space, and
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}^{*}\ \text{ and }\ \mathrm{B}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{H}),

1.2.
Literature survey

In the past few decades, the large deviation theory received the required attention due to its applications in the areas like risk management, mathematical finance, fluid mechanics, quantum physics and statistical mechanics, etc. (cf.
[
6
,
7
,
11
,
16
,
36
,
46
,
47
]
, etc. and references therein). The small time large deviation principle (LDP) basically focus on the asymptotic behaviors of the tails of a family of probability measures at a given point in the space when the time is very small. More specifically, it observes the limiting behavior of the solution in a time interval
[0,t]
as
t
tends to 0. An inspiration for considering such kind of problems arises from the following Varadhan-type small time asymptotics for the diffusion process
\mathbf{X}
:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}2t\log\mathbb{P}\big\{\mathbf{X}(0)\in\mathrm{E},\;\mathbf{X}(t)\in\mathrm{F}\big\}=-\vartheta^{2}(\mathrm{E},\mathrm{F}),

where
\vartheta
is an appropriate Riemann distance associated with the diffusion.

The celebrated theory of small time LDP by Varadhan
[
48
]
considered the small time asymptotics for the finite dimensional diffusion processes. The small time LDP for infinite dimensional diffusion process has been considered in the works
[
12
,
20
,
49
,
53
]
, etc. Many authors contributed in the direction of small time LDP for different types of SPDEs. The most significant results in this direction are the small time LDP for stochastic 2D Navier-Stokes equations in
[
50
]
, stochastic 3D tamed Navier-Stokes equations in
[
44
]
, stochastic 2D non-Newtonian fluids in
[
29
]
, stochastic quasi-geostrophic equations in the sub-critical case in
[
33
]
, 3D stochastic primitive equations in
[
17
]
, scalar stochastic conservation laws in
[
24
]
, stochastic convective Brinkman-Forchheimer equations in
[
36
]
, a class of SPDEs with locally monotone coefficients, in
[
24
]
, stochastic Ladyzhenskaya-Smagorinsky equation in
[
35
]
, etc. Most of the works discussed above deal with the small time asymptotics for semilinear type SPDEs except
[
24
]
.

1.4.
Main result

Our main aim in this work is to analyze the small time LDP for the solutions of the system (
1.1
) with fully local monotone coefficients. We need to find an estimate for the maximal inequality for the stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{B}(s,\cdot(s))\mathrm{d}\mathrm{W}(s)
in the Banach space
\mathbb{V}
in an appropriate way. Therefore, we assume that the space
\mathbb{V}
is a 2-smooth Banach space. Let
\gamma(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{V})
be the space of all
\gamma
-radonifying operators from
\mathbb{U}
to
\mathbb{V}
. Let us recall that if
\mathrm{T}\in\gamma(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{V})
, then the series

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma_{j}\mathrm{T}(\mathbf{e}_{j})\text{ converges in }\mathrm{L}^{2}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{V}),

for any sequence
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of independent Gaussian real-valued random variables defined on a probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathscr{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
and any orthonormal family
\{\mathbf{e}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of the Hilbert space
\mathbb{U}
. Then, the space
\gamma(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{V})
is endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{T}\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{V})}:=\bigg\{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma_{j}\mathrm{T}(\mathbf{e}_{j})\bigg\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

which does not depend on the choice of
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}},\{\mathbf{e}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, and form a Banach space. For convenience we denote the norm of the space
\gamma(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{V})
by
\|\cdot\|_{\gamma}
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{V})}
.

1.4.1.
Large deviation principle

Let
(\mathcal{E},\nu)
be a Polish space. We are given a family of probability measures
\{{\varrho_{\epsilon}}\}_{\epsilon>0}
on
\mathcal{E}
and a lower semicontinuous function
\mathrm{I}:\mathcal{E}\to[0,+\infty]
, not identically equals to infinity and such that its level sets,

K_{M}:=\{\ell\in\mathcal{E}:\mathrm{I}(\ell)\leq M\},\ \ M>0,

are compact for any
M\in[0,+\infty]
. The family
\{\varrho_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
is said to satisfy the LDP or to have a large deviation property with respect to the rate function
\mathrm{I}(\cdot)
if

(1)

for any closed set
\mathrm{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon\log\varrho_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{F})\leq-\inf_{\ell\in\mathrm{F}}\mathrm{I}(\ell),

(2)

for any open set
\mathrm{G}\subset\mathcal{E}
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon\log\varrho_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{G})\geq-\inf_{\ell\in\mathrm{G}}\mathrm{I}(\ell).

Define a functional
\mathrm{I}(\ell)
on
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
by

(1.17)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}(\ell):=\inf_{\ell\in\Lambda_{k}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\dot{\ell}(t)\|_{\mathbb{U}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\},

where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}=\bigg\{

\displaystyle\ell\in\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{H}):\ell(\cdot)\text{ is absolutely continuous and such that }

\displaystyle\quad k(t)=x+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{B}(s,k(s))\dot{\ell}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in[0,T]\bigg\}.

For
\epsilon>0
, we aim to study the probabilistic asymptotic behavior for small time process
\{\mathbf{Y}^{\epsilon}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}=\{\mathbf{Y}(\epsilon t)\}_{t\geq 0}
as
\epsilon\to 0
. Let us now state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\varrho_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\epsilon}
be the law of
\mathbf{Y}^{\epsilon}(\cdot)
in
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
. Then under Hypotheses
1.2
and
1.6
,
\varrho_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\epsilon}
satisfies an LDP with the rate function
\mathrm{I}(\cdot)
, that is,

(1)

for any closed set
\mathrm{F}\subset\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon\log\varrho_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\epsilon}(\mathrm{F})\leq-\inf_{\ell\in\mathrm{F}}\mathrm{I}(\ell),

(2)

for any open set
\mathrm{O}\subset\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon\log\varrho_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\epsilon}(\mathrm{O})\geq-\inf_{\ell\in\mathrm{O}}\mathrm{I}(\ell).

We prove the above theorem in the subsequent section.

\displaystyle\quad+2\sqrt{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m}}\big((\mathrm{B}(\epsilon s,\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s))-\mathrm{B}(\epsilon s,\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)))\mathrm{d}\mathrm{W}(s),\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\big).

, and estimate it using the Cauchy-Shwarz inequality, Hypothesis
1.2
(H.4), Young’s inequality and the fact that
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}
as

\displaystyle\big|2\langle\mathrm{A}(\epsilon\cdot,\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}),\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\mathrm{A}(\epsilon\cdot,\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon})\|_{\mathbb{V}^{*}}\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathrm{A}(\epsilon\cdot,\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon})\|_{\mathbb{V}^{*}}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}+\theta\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(f(\epsilon\cdot)+C\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta}\big)\big(1+\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\alpha}\big)+\theta\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq Cf(\epsilon\cdot)\big(1+\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\alpha}\big)+C\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta}+C\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta+\alpha}+\theta\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta},

for
\theta\in(0,\xi)
. Substituting (
2
) in (
2
), and then using Hypothesis

(H.2)
∗

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m})-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m}}\big[Cf(\epsilon s)\big(1+\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\alpha}\big)+C\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta}+C\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta+\alpha}\big]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m}}\bigg(\big(f(\epsilon s)+\rho(\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s))+\eta(\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s))\big)\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-(\xi-\theta)\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta}\bigg)\mathrm{d}s

(2.51)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sqrt{\epsilon}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m}}\big((\mathrm{B}(\epsilon s,\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s))-\mathrm{B}(\epsilon s,\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)))\mathrm{d}\mathrm{W}(s),\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\big)\bigg|.

Applying Gronwall’s inequality in the inequality (
2
), we deduce

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m})-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\{C\epsilon\bigg[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m}}\big[f(\epsilon s)\big(1+\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\alpha}\big)+\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta}+\|\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta+\alpha}\big]\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sqrt{\epsilon}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m}}\big((\mathrm{B}(\epsilon s,\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s))-\mathrm{B}(\epsilon s,\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)))\mathrm{d}\mathrm{W}(s),\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s)\big)\bigg|\bigg\}

(2.52)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\bigg\{\epsilon\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m}}\big(f(\epsilon s)+\rho(\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s))+\eta(\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(s))\big)\mathrm{d}s\bigg\}.

Then, for any

\displaystyle\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau_{R}^{\epsilon}]}\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(t)-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2q}\bigg]\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{C\epsilon^{2}\bigg[\big(R^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+1\big)\int_{0}^{T}f(\epsilon s)\mathrm{d}s+R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}T+R^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}T\bigg]^{2}}}\right.

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\qquad+16Cq\epsilon\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}L_{\mathrm{B}}(s)\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}L_{\mathrm{B}}(\epsilon s)\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{r\in[0,s\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m}]}\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(r)-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(r)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2q}\bigg]\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{q}}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)}}\right\}

(2.53)

\displaystyle\qquad\times e^{\big[\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s+C(1+R^{\frac{\zeta}{2}})(\epsilon T+R)+\epsilon C_{R,T}^{1}\big]},

where we have used a calculation similar to (

\displaystyle\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau_{R}^{\epsilon}]}\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(t)-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2q}\bigg]\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\big[\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s+C(1+R^{\frac{\zeta}{2}})(\epsilon T+R)+\epsilon C_{R,T}^{1}\big]}C\bigg[\big(R^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+1\big)\int_{0}^{\epsilon T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s+\epsilon R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}T+\epsilon R^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}T\bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\bigg\{16Cq\epsilon T^{2}\bigg(\esssup_{s\in[0,T]}L_{\mathrm{B}}(s)\bigg)^{2}\bigg\}.

Fixing

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\log\bigg\{\varpi^{-q}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau_{R,\epsilon}^{m}]}\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\epsilon}(t)-\mathbf{X}_{m}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2q}\bigg]\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq-2\log\varpi+\log\bigg\{C\bigg[\big(R^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+1\big)\int_{0}^{\epsilon T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s+\epsilon R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}T+\epsilon R^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}T\bigg]^{2}\bigg\}+\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\to-\infty\ \text{ as }\ \epsilon\to 0,

where we have used the fact that

3.1.
Cahn-Hilliard equation

The well-known Chan-Hilliard equations were framed in
[
9
]
, which describe a phase separation in a binary alloy. This model is one of the fundamental equations in material science. This model can be read as

(3.1)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u(t)&=-\Delta^{2}u(t)+\Delta\varphi(u(t)),\ \ \text{ in }\ \ (0,T)\times\mathcal{O},\\
\nabla u(t)\cdot\nu&=\nabla(\Delta u(t))\cdot\nu=0,\ \ \text{ on }\ \ [0,T)\times\partial\mathcal{O},\\
u(0)&=u_{0},\ \text{ in }\ \mathcal{O},\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
u:[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the scaled concentration,
\mathcal{O}
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n=1,2,3,
with smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
and
\nu
is the outward drawn unit normal vector on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
.
Let us assume that the nonlinear term
\varphi
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
,

(2)

there exists a positive constant
C
and
p\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[2,\frac{n+4}{n}}}\right]
such that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(x)\geq-C\ \text{ and }\ |\varphi(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{p}),

and

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq C(1+|x|^{p-1}+|y|^{p-1})|x-y|.

Let
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mathbb{V}=\{u\in\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{O}):\nabla u\cdot\nu=\nabla(\Delta u)\cdot\nu=0\ \text{ on }\ \partial\mathcal{O}\}
. Then, we have the Gelfand triplet
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}^{*}
and the embedding
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}
is compact. Set

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}(u)=-\Delta^{2}u+\Delta\varphi(u).

Then from
[
43
]
(see Example 4.4 or Example 5.2.27,
[
31
]
), we infer that Hypothesis
1.2
(H.1)-(H.4) hold. More precisely, the local monotonicity property (H.2) can be stated as

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{A}(u)-\mathrm{A}(v),u-v\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}+C\big(1+\rho(u)+\eta(v)\big)\|u-v\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},

where
\rho(\cdot)
and
\eta(\cdot)
are such that

\displaystyle|\rho(u)|+|\eta(u)|\leq C\|u\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\frac{n(p-1)}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\frac{(4-n)(p-1)}{2}}.

One should note that
\frac{n(p-1)}{2}\leq 2\Leftrightarrow p\leq\frac{n+4}{n}
. For
d=1,2
, the function
\varphi(\cdot)
can be chosen as
\varphi(x)=x^{3}-x
, which is the derivative of the double well potential
F(x)=\frac{1}{4}(x^{2}-1)^{2}
.

Under the above assumption on
\varphi(\cdot)
, the stochastic counterpart to the system (
3.1
) can be stated as follows:

(3.2)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\mathrm{Y}(t)&=\big[\mathrm{A}(\mathrm{Y}(t))+\Delta\varphi(\mathrm{Y}(t))\big]\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{B}(t,\mathrm{Y}(t))\mathrm{d}\mathrm{W}(t),\ \ t\in(0,T),\\
\mathrm{Y}(0)&=u_{0}\in\mathbb{H},\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
\mathrm{W}(\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{U}
-cylindrical Wiener process on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
and
\mathrm{B}(\cdot)
is Lipschitz from
\mathbb{H}
to
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{H})
. The well-posedness of the system (
3.2
) is established in
[
43
]
(see Theorem 2.6).

Now, consider the small time process
\mathrm{Y}(\epsilon\cdot)
and
\varrho_{u_{0}}^{\epsilon}
be the law of
\mathrm{Y}(\epsilon\cdot)
on
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
. Then using Theorem
1.8
, we obtain the small time LDP for the problem (
3.2
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that Hypotheses
1.2
and
1.6
hold, and the embedding
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}
is compact. Then, (
3.2
) has a unique solution
\mathrm{Y}(\cdot)
for the initial data
u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
and
\varrho_{u_{0}}^{\epsilon}
satisfies the LDP with the rate function
\mathrm{I}(\cdot)
given by (
1.17
).

3.2.
Stochastic 2D Navier-Stokes equations

The classical Navier-Stokes equations is a very important model in fluid mechanics which describe the time evolution of fluids. Here we discuss only for two dimensional case and it can be expresses as follows: Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be any bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
. Consider

(3.3)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}(t)&=\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}(t)-(\boldsymbol{u}(t)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}(t)-\nabla p+\boldsymbol{f}(t),\ \text{ in }\ (0,T)\times\mathcal{O},\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}(t)&=0,\ \text{ in }\ (0,T)\times\mathcal{O},\\
\boldsymbol{u}&=0,\ \text{ on }\ [0,T)\times\partial\mathcal{O},\\
\boldsymbol{u}(0)&=\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\ \text{ in }\ \mathcal{O},\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
\boldsymbol{u}(t)=\big(u_{1}(t,x),u_{2}(t,x)\big)
denotes the velocity of the fluid,
p
is the pressure,
\nu
is the kinematic viscosity and
f
is the external forcing of the fluid, and
\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}u_{i}\partial_{i}
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}:=\bigg\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{W}_{0}^{1,2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}=0,\ \|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\bigg\},

and
\mathbb{H}
is the closure of the above space
\mathbb{V}
in the norm
\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}:=\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\boldsymbol{v}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x
.

Let us define the Stokes operator
\mathrm{A}:\mathbb{W}^{2,2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{H}
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{u})=-\mathrm{P}_{\mathbb{H}}\Delta\boldsymbol{u},\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{W}^{2,2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathbb{V},

where
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbb{H}}
is the Helmholtz-Leray projection from
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
\mathbb{H}
, and the nonlinear operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})=\mathrm{P}_{\mathbb{H}}\big[(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}\big],\ \mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{u}):=\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}).

Then, we can write the stochastic counterpart to the system (
3.3
) as

(3.4)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}(t)&=-\big[\nu\mathrm{A}(\mathbf{Y}(t))+\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{Y}(t))-\mathrm{P}_{\mathbb{H}}\boldsymbol{f}(t)\big]\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{B}(t,\mathbf{Y}(t))\mathrm{d}\mathrm{W}(t),\ t\in(0,T),\\
\mathbf{Y}(0)&=\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{H},\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
\mathrm{W}(\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{U}
-cylindrical Wiener process on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
. From
[
30
]
(see Example 3.3), we know that the coefficients of stochastic 2D Navier-Stokes equations satisfy Hypothesis
1.2
(H.1), (H.4) and
1.6
(H.2)
∗
. The well-posedness of the system (
3.4
) has been obtained in Theorem 2.6,
[
46
]
. Let
\varrho^{\epsilon}
be the law of
\mathbf{Y}(\epsilon\cdot)
on
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
. Then using Theorem
1.8
, we obtain the small time LDP for the stochastic 2D Navier-Stokes equations (
3.4
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that Hypotheses
1.2
and
1.6
hold. Then, (
3.4
) has a unique solution
\mathbf{Y}(\cdot)
for the initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
and
\varrho_{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}^{\epsilon}
satisfies the LDP with the rate function
\mathrm{I}(\cdot)
given in (
1.17
).

Remark 3.3
.

The small time LDP for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations have been obtained in
[
50
]
. We provide it here since our formulation also covers the system (
3.4
).

3.3.
Quasilinear SPDEs

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
. Let us consider the following quasilinear partial differential equations:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(t,x,u(t,x),\nabla u(t,x))-\boldsymbol{a}_{0}(t,x,u(t,x),\nabla u(t,x)),\ \text{ in }\ (0,T)\times\mathcal{O},

with the zero Dirichlet boundary conditions (the case of other boundary conditions can be handled in a similar way), where
u:[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
, the vector
\nabla u(t,x)=\big(\partial_{j}u(t,x)\big)_{j=1}^{n}
is the gradient of
u(\cdot)
with respect to the spatial variable
x
and
\boldsymbol{a}=(\boldsymbol{a}_{1},\boldsymbol{a}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{a}_{n})
is a vector with
\boldsymbol{a}_{j}:[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
for each
j=0,1,\ldots,n
.

Hypothesis 3.4
.

Let us assume that
\boldsymbol{a}_{j},\ j=0,1,\ldots,n
satisfies the following conditions: There exists a constant
\beta>1
if
n=1,2
and
\beta\geq\frac{2n}{n+2}
if
n\geq 3
, such that the following assumptions hold:

(A.1)

\boldsymbol{a}_{j}
satisfies the Carathéodory conditions:
for a.e. fixed
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}
,
\boldsymbol{a}_{j}(t,x,u,z)
is continuous in
(u,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for each fixed
(u,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\boldsymbol{a}_{j}(t,x,u,z)
is measurable with respect to
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}
.

(A.2)

There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}\geq 0
and a function
f_{1}\in\mathrm{L}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}((0,T)\times\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that for a.e.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}
and all
(u,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{a}_{j}(t,x,u,z)|\leq c_{1}|z|^{\beta-1}+c_{2}|u|^{\frac{(\beta-1)(n+2)}{n}}+f_{1}(t,x).

(A.3)

There exist constants
c_{3},c_{4}\geq 0
and a function
f_{2}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}((0,T)\times\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that for a.e.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}
and all
(u,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{a}_{j}(t,x,u,z)z_{j}+\boldsymbol{a}_{0}(t,x,u,z)u\geq c_{3}|z|^{\beta}-c_{4}|u|^{2}-f_{2}(t,x).

(A.4)

For a.e.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}
and all
u\in\mathbb{R}
and
z^{1},z^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big[\boldsymbol{a}_{j}(t,x,u,z^{1})-\boldsymbol{a}_{j}(t,x,u,z^{2})\big](z^{1}_{j}-z^{2}_{j})>0.

For a.e.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for any
R>0
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{|z|\to\infty}\frac{\sup\limits_{|\boldsymbol{u}|\leq R}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{a}_{j}(t,x,u,z)z_{j}}{|z|+|z|^{\beta-1}}=\infty.

Let
\mathbb{H}:=\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mathbb{V}:=\mathbb{W}_{0}^{1,\beta}(\mathcal{O})
. Using the Sobolev embedding we have the Gelfand triplet
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}^{*}
, with the compact embedding
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}
.

For
u,v\in\mathbb{V}
, the operator
\mathrm{A}(\cdot)
is defined as follows:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{A}(t,u),v\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{O}}\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{a}_{j}(t,x,u(x),\nabla u(x))\partial_{j}u(x)+\boldsymbol{a}_{0}(t,x,u(x),\nabla u(x))v(x)\bigg\}\mathrm{d}x.

Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\beta}}^{\delta}\|u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1-\delta},\text{ where }\delta\in[0,1]\text{ and }\frac{1}{p}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{n}}}\right)\delta+\frac{1-\delta}{2}.

Then, form (A.2) and (
3.11
), we obtain that
\mathrm{A}(\cdot)
is a measurable mapping from
[0,T]\times\mathbb{V}
to
\mathbb{V}^{*}
. Also, we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{A}(t,u)\|_{\mathbb{V}^{*}}\leq c_{1}\|u\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta}+c\|u\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta}\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\frac{2\beta}{n}}+F(t),

where the function
F(t)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}f_{1}(t,x)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\mathrm{d}x
is in the space
\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{+})
, which implies Hypothesis
1.2
(H.4).

Using Hypothesis
3.4
(A.1) and (A.2), we obtain the hemicontinuity property (H.1). Combining (A.3) and (
3.11
), we obtain the coercivity property (H.3). Again, by (A.1), (A.2) and (A.4), one can show that the operator
\mathrm{A}(\cdot)
is pseudo-monotone for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
(see Theorems 10.65 and 10.63,
[
42
]
or Theorem 2.8,
[
25
]
). By Corollary 2.7,
[
43
]
, we obtain the existence of a
probabilistically weak solution
to the corresponding stochastic partial differential equations.

A typical example of the formulation (
3.5
) is the
p
-Laplacian for
p\geq 2
,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\nabla\cdot\big(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\big)-c|u|^{p-2}u,

where
c>0
. Fix
\beta=p
, and one can see that Hypothesis
3.4
remains valid.

Since our aim is to establish LDP, therefore we need the existence of
probabilistically strong solutions
. For that we have to modify Hypothesis
3.4
(A.4), by the following condition:

Hypothesis 3.5
.

(A.4)
∗

For
\beta\geq n
,
\gamma\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[0,\beta\big(1+\frac{2}{n}\big)-2}}\right]
and
f_{3}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{+})
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that for a.e.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}
and all
u^{1},u^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
z^{1},z^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big[\boldsymbol{a}_{j}(t,x,u^{1},z^{1})-\boldsymbol{a}_{j}(t,x,u^{2},z^{2})\big](z^{1}_{j}-z^{2}_{j})

(3.14)

\displaystyle+\big[\boldsymbol{a}_{0}(t,x,u^{1},z^{1})-\boldsymbol{a}_{0}(t,x,u^{2},z^{2})\big](u^{1}-u^{2})>-c\big(f_{3}(t)+|u^{1}|^{\gamma}+|u^{2}|^{\gamma}\big)|u^{1}-u^{2}|^{2}.

Under the above assumption, we find

(3.15)

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{A}(t,u)-\mathrm{A}(t,v),u-v\rangle\leq\big(f_{3}(t)+\rho(u)+\eta(v)\big)\|u-v\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},

where
\rho(\cdot)
and
\eta(\cdot)
are such that

\displaystyle|\rho(u)|+|\eta(u)|\leq C\|u\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\gamma\delta}\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma(1-\delta)},

with
\delta=\frac{\beta n}{\beta n+2\beta-2n}
. Thus, the local monotonicity property (H.2) is verified. Then, there exists a unique
probabilistically strong solution
(see Theorem 2.6,
[
43
]
).

Let
\varrho^{\epsilon}
be the law of small time process
\mathrm{Y}(\epsilon\cdot)
on
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
. Then, using Theorem
1.8
, we establish the small time LDP for the stochastic counterpart to the system (
3.5
) with Hypothesis
1.6
(H.6) for the noise coefficients
\mathrm{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Thus, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let Hypotheses
1.2
and
1.6
be satisfied. Then there exists a unique solution
\mathrm{Y}(\cdot)
of the stochastic counterpart of the system (
3.5
) for the initial data
u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
and
\varrho_{u_{0}}^{\epsilon}
satisfies the LDP with the rate function
\mathrm{I}(\cdot)
given in (
1.17
).

3.4.
Convection-diffusion equation

The convection-diffusion equation describes the physical phenomena where particles, energy or any other physical quantities injected into a physical system due to two processes:
diffusion
and
convection
. It has a variety of applications in fluid dynamics, mass and heat transfer, etc. We consider the following system on the
n
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
:

(3.16)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u(t)&=\nabla\cdot\big(\boldsymbol{a}(u)\nabla u+\boldsymbol{b}(u)\big),\ \text{ on }\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{n},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in\mathbb{H},\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is the flux function,
\boldsymbol{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the diffusion matrix
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{M}_{n\times n}
here
\mathbb{M}_{n\times n}
is the set of all real
n\times n
matrices. The coefficients
\boldsymbol{a}(\cdot)
and
\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot)
satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

Both
\boldsymbol{a}(\cdot)
and
\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot)
are continuous;

(2)

\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot)
has linear growth;

(3)

\boldsymbol{a}(\cdot)
is bounded and uniformly positive definite, that is, there exist positive constants
\delta
and
C
such that for any
u\in\mathbb{R}
and
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\delta|\boldsymbol{z}|^{2}\leq\langle\boldsymbol{a}(u)\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{z}\rangle\leq C|\boldsymbol{z}|^{2}.

Let
\mathbb{H}:=\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
\mathbb{V}:=\mathbb{W}^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then, we have the Gelfand triplet
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}^{*}
with the compact embedding
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}
. For any
u,v\in\mathbb{V}
, define the operator
\mathrm{A}(\cdot)
as

(3.18)

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{A}(u),v\rangle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(\boldsymbol{a}(u(x))\nabla u(x)+\boldsymbol{b}(u(x)),\nabla v(x))\mathrm{d}x.

Under the above conditions, the coefficients in the system (
3.16
) satisfies Hypothesis
3.4
, which implies Hypothesis
1.2
holds except local monotonicity condition (H.2). Also, the operator
\mathrm{A}(\cdot)
defined in (
3.18
) is a pseudo-monotone operator (cf. Example 4.2,
[
43
]
).

For the stochastic counterpart of (
3.16
), we assume that the diffusion coefficient is globally Lipschitz in
\mathbb{H}
. By Corollary 2.7,
[
43
]
, we deduce the existence of
probabilistically weak solutions
, for the corresponding stochastic equation of the problem (
3.16
). With an additional assumption, that is, the coefficients
\boldsymbol{a}(\cdot)
and
\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot)
are Lipschitz, we obtain the existence and uniquenss of
probabilistically strong solution
for the corresponding stochastic equation to the problem (
3.16
) (cf. Example 4.2,
[
43
]
and Theorem 3.1,
[
21
]
).

Let
\varrho^{\epsilon}
be the law of small time process
\mathrm{Y}(\epsilon\cdot)
on
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
. Then, using Theorem
1.8
, we deduce the small time LDP for the corresponding stochastic equation to (
3.16
) with Hypothesis
1.6
(H.6) for the noise coefficient
\mathrm{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Thus, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3.7
.

Under assumptions
1.2
and
1.6
, there exists a unique solution
\mathrm{Y}(\cdot)
of the corresponding stochastic equation to (
3.13
) for the initial data
u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
and
\varrho_{u_{0}}^{\epsilon}
satisfies the LDP with the rate function
\mathrm{I}(\cdot)
given in (
1.17
).

3.5.
2D Liquid crystal model

The elementary form of the hydrodynamics of liquid crystals is a simplified version of Ericksen-Leslie system with Ginzburg-Landau approximation, which is discussed in
[
26
]
. We consider the following model in the bounded domain
\mathcal{O}
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
,

(3.19)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}(t)&=\Delta\boldsymbol{u}(t)-(\boldsymbol{u}(t)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}(t)-\nabla p-\nabla\cdot(\nabla\boldsymbol{n}(t)\otimes\nabla\boldsymbol{n}(t)),\text{ in }(0,T)\times\mathcal{O},\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}(t)&=0,\ \text{ on }\ (0,T)\times\partial\mathcal{O},\\
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{n}(t)&=\Delta\boldsymbol{n}(t)-(\boldsymbol{u}(t)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{n}(t)-\Phi(\boldsymbol{n}(t)),\ \text{ in }\ (0,T)\times\mathcal{O},\\
\boldsymbol{u}&=0,\ \text{ on }[0,T)\times\partial\mathcal{O},\\
\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}{\partial\nu}&=0,\text{ on }[0,T)\times\partial\mathcal{O},\\
\big(\boldsymbol{u}(0),&\boldsymbol{n}(0)\big)=\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{n}_{0}\big),\ \text{ in }\ \mathcal{O},\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
\boldsymbol{u}:[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the velocity,
p:[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
is the pressure,
\boldsymbol{n}:[0,T]\times\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the director field of the liquid crystal molecules and
\nu
is the outward unit normal vector on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
. The symbol
\nabla\boldsymbol{n}\otimes\nabla\boldsymbol{n}
denotes a
2\times 2
matrix whose entries are given by

\displaystyle(\nabla\boldsymbol{n}\otimes\nabla\boldsymbol{n})_{i,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}(\partial_{i}n_{k})(\partial_{j}n_{k}).

Furthermore, we assume that
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfies the following conditions: there exists a
k
degree polynomial
\psi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\displaystyle\Phi(\boldsymbol{n})=\psi(|\boldsymbol{n}|^{2})\boldsymbol{n}=\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}|\boldsymbol{n}|^{2i}\bigg)\boldsymbol{n},

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,\ldots,k-1
and
a_{k}>0
.

Let
\mathbb{V}=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O})^{2}:\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=0,\ \boldsymbol{u}\big|_{\partial\mathcal{O}}=0\}
and
\mathbb{H}
be closure of
\mathbb{V}
under the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-norm
\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}:=\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\boldsymbol{u}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x
. Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}:=\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O})^{3}\ \text{ and }\ \mathcal{V}:=\mathbb{V}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{O})^{3}:\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}{\partial\nu}=0}}\right\},

with the norms in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
denoted by

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}:=\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{n}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2},\ \text{ and }\ \|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}:=\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{n}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2},

for
\mathbf{Y}=(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{n})
, respectively and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*}\times\mathcal{V}}
denotes the duality pairing between
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}^{*}
. Thus, we have a Gelfand triplet
\mathcal{V}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{*}
with the compact embedding
\mathcal{V}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}
.

One should note that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\nabla\boldsymbol{n}\otimes\nabla\boldsymbol{n})=\frac{1}{2}\nabla(|\nabla\boldsymbol{n}|^{2})+\nabla\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\Delta\boldsymbol{n}.

Let us define the Helmholtz-Leray projection
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbb{H}}
from
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})^{2}
to
\mathbb{H}
, and define

(3.21)

\mathrm{A}(\mathbf{Y}):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{aligned} \mathrm{P}_{\mathbb{H}}\big[&\Delta\boldsymbol{u}-(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}-\nabla\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\Delta\boldsymbol{n}\big]\\
&\Delta\boldsymbol{n}-(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}-\psi(\boldsymbol{n})\end{aligned}}}\right).

The operator
\mathrm{A}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{*}
satisfies Hypothesis
1.2
(H.1)-(H.4), since (cf. Example 4.5,
[
43
]
)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{A}(\mathbf{Y})\|_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}^{2}\leq C\big(1+\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{4k+2}\big)\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{*}}^{2},\ \text{ and }\ \langle\mathrm{A}(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{Y}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*}\times\mathcal{V}}\leq-\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Also, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{A}(\mathbf{Y}_{1})-\mathrm{A}(\mathbf{Y}_{2}),\mathbf{Y}_{1}-\mathbf{Y}_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*}\times\mathcal{V}}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{Y}_{1}-\mathbf{Y}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C\big(1+\rho(\mathbf{Y}_{1})+\eta(\mathbf{Y}_{2})\big)\|\mathbf{Y}_{1}-\mathbf{Y}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

where
\rho(\cdot)
and
\eta(\cdot)
are such that

\displaystyle|\rho(\mathbf{Y}_{1})|\leq\|\mathbf{Y}_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{4k},\ \ \text{ and }\ \ |\eta(\mathbf{Y}_{2})|\leq\|\mathbf{Y}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{4k}+\|\mathbf{Y}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\|\mathbf{Y}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}.

Thus, the stochastic counterpart to the system (
3.19
) has a unique
probabilistically strong solution
under Hypothesis
1.2
(for more details see Example 4.5,
[
43
]
).

Remark 3.8

(Remark 4.6,
[
43
]
)
.

(1)

The authors in the work
[
5
]
considered the stochastic counterpart to the system (
3.19
) with the noise in the equation for
\boldsymbol{u}
depending only on
\boldsymbol{u}
in the Itô sense, with linear multiplicative noise only depending on
\boldsymbol{n}
in the Stratonovich sense in the equation for
\boldsymbol{n}
. Using Theorem 2.6,
[
43
]
, we obtain the existence and uniqueness of a
probabilistically strong solution
to stochastic 2D liquid crystal equations perturbed by multiplicative noise which can depend on both
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{n}
.

(2)

If we assume
n:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are scalar functions appearing in the system (
3.19
), then the corresponding system is called
Allen-Cahn-Navier-Stokes equations (ACNSEs)
, which describe the motion of mixture of two incompressible viscous fluids. For more details one can see
[
27
,
34
]
, etc. and references therein. ACNSEs is also related to the magneto-hydrodynamics equations (MHDEs), that is the Navier-Stokes equations coupled with the Maxwell equations. In particular, for two dimensions and the nonlinear term
\Phi(n)=0
, the resultant model is equivalent to MHDEs (see
[
51
]
). The well-posedness of both models, that is, ACNSEs and MHDEs are covered by the formulation in
[
43
]
, and hence it falls into the framework discussed in this paper.

Let
\varrho^{\epsilon}
be the law of the small time process
\mathbf{Y}(\epsilon\cdot)
on
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathcal{H})
. Then, using our main theorem (Theorem
1.8
), we obtain the small time LDP for the corresponding stochastic equation to (
3.19
) with Hypothesis
1.6
(H.6) for the noise coefficients
\mathrm{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Thus, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3.9
.

Assume that Hypotheses
1.1
and
1.6
hold and the embedding
\mathcal{V}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is compact. Then, there exists a unique solution
\mathbf{Y}(\cdot)
of the corresponding stochastic equation to (
3.19
) for the initial data
(\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{n}_{0})\in\mathcal{H}
and
\varrho_{\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{n}_{0}}^{\epsilon}
satisfy the LDP with the rate function
\mathrm{I}(\cdot)
given in (
1.17
).

Singular equivalences induced by ring extensions
Yongyun Qin

Abstract
Let B ⊆ A B\subseteq A be an extension of finite dimensional algebras. We provide a sufficient condition for the existence of triangle equivalences of singularity categories (resp. Gorenstein defect categories) between A A and B B . This result is applied to trivial extensions, Morita rings and triangular matrix algebras to give several reduction methods on singularity categories and Gorenstein defect categories of algebras.

Introduction

Throughout all algebras are finite dimensional associative
algebras over a field
k
, and all modules are finitely generated left modules unless stated
otherwise. The
singularity category

D_{sg}(A)
of an algebra

A
is defined as the Verdier quotient of the bounded derived category of finitely generated modules over

by the
full subcategory of perfect complexes
[
4
]
.
It measures the “regularity” of

in the sense

if and only if

has finite global
dimension. According to
[
4
]
, there is an embedding functor

from the stable category
\underline{\mathrm{Gproj}}A
of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
modules to
D_{sg}(A)
, and the
Gorenstein
defect category
of
A
is defined to be the Verdier quotient

D_{def}(A):=D_{sg}(A)/\mathrm{Im}F
, see
[
3
]
. This category
measures how far the algebra
A
is from
being Gorenstein in the sense that

D_{def}(A)=0
if and only if
A
is Gorenstein
[
3
]
.
In general, it is difficult to describe the singularity categories
and Gorenstein defect categories. Many people described these categories for
some special kinds of algebras
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
12
,
21
,
34
]
,
and some experts compared these categories

,

,

,

An
extension
of algebras is simply a finite dimensional algebra
A
with an unital
subalgebra
B
, denoted
B\subseteq A
, and it provide us a useful framework
to reduce
the homological properties of algebras. Indeed, there are numerous works
comparing the
homological properties of
A
and
B
under certain conditions
[
15
,
20
,
26
,
37
,
38
]
.
From
[
15
]
, an extension
B\subseteq A
is called

left (resp. right) bounded
if
A/B
has finite projective
dimension as a
B
-bimodule,
A/B
is projective as a left (resp. right)
B
-module and

is 0. Using the Jacobi-Zariski long exact sequence, Cibils et al. proved that
for a bounded extension

Moreover, Iusenkoa and MacQuarrie showed the finitistic

On the other hand, both the Hochschild homology and the finitistic

[

]
.
So it is nature to compared the singularity categories
between two algebras linked by a bounded extension.
Our main result is the following, which is listed in Theorem
3.4

.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
and
B

be two algebras and
B\subseteq A
be an extension. Assume that

\mathrm{pd}_{B^{e}}(A/B)<\infty
,
(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}p}=0
for some integer
p
and

\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(A/B,(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j})=0
for each
i,j\geq 1
.
Then there is a
singular equivalence of Morita type with level between
A
and
B
. Moreover,
this singular equivalence induces an equivalence on the Gorenstein defect categories
if the extension is split.

Here, an extension
B\subseteq A
is split if the algebra inclusion
B\hookrightarrow A

splits. From
[
10
]
, the Tor-vanishing condition in Theorem
1.1

is equivalent to
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}((A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j},A/B)=0
for each
i,j\geq 1
.
Therefore, the assumption of Theorem
1.1
is weaker than the definition
of bounded extension,
where the Tor-vanishing condition is replaced
by
A/B
is projective as a left or a right
B
-module.
As a result, we generalize the main result of
Cibils et al.
[
15
]
by showing that Han’s conjecture is preserved
under certain extensions which are strictly bigger than
bounded extensions, see Remark
3.5
and Corollary
3.6
.
Moreover, we apply Theorem
1.1
to trivial extensions, Morita rings
and triangular matrix algebras, and we get several reduction methods on
singularity categories and Gorenstein defect categories,
see Corollary
4.1
, Corollary
4.2
,

Definitions and conventions

In this section we will fix our notations and recall some basic definitions.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of objects of
a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
. We denote by
\mathrm{thick}\mathcal{S}

the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}

containing
\mathcal{S}
and closed under taking direct summands.

Let

Denote by

Let

of complexes over
\mathrm{Mod}A
, and

over
\mathrm{proj}A

(resp.
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}A)
)
the derived category (resp. bounded derived category) of complexes over
\mathrm{Mod}A
(resp.
\mathrm{mod}A
),
and denote by
[-]
the shift functor on complexes.
Usually, we
just write
\mathcal{D}A
(resp.
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
) instead of
\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}A)
(resp.
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}A)
).
Up to isomorphism, the objects in
K^{b}(\mathrm{proj}A)
are
precisely all the compact objects in
\mathcal{D}A
. For
convenience, we do not distinguish

from the
perfect derived category

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{per}}(A)
of

, i.e., the
full triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{D}A

.

Following

,
28
]

singularity category
of

is the
Verdier quotient

.
A finitely
generated

\textstyle{P^{-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d^{-1}}
\textstyle{P^{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d^{0}}
\textstyle{P^{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots}

of

It is well known that

Moreover, there is a canonical triangle functor

sending a Gorenstein projective module to the corresponding stalk complex concentrated in degree zero

.

.

(See

)

3
Singularity categories and ring extensions

In this section, we will compare the singularity categories and the Gorenstein
defect categories between two algebras linked by an extension.
The following lemma is well-known. Here, we give a proof for readers’ convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
,

and

-

\mathcal{D}(A\otimes C^{op})

Let

A

and let

be the deleted complex

\cdots\longrightarrow P^{-2}\longrightarrow P^{-1}\longrightarrow P^{0}\longrightarrow 0.

Then

M\otimes_{B}^{L}N\cong P^{\bullet}\otimes_{B}N
in
\mathcal{D}(A\otimes C^{op})
. Since
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(M,N)=0
for any
i>0
, the complex
of

C

\cdots\longrightarrow P^{-2}\otimes_{B}N\longrightarrow P^{-1}\otimes_{B}N\longrightarrow P^{0}\otimes_{B}N\longrightarrow M\otimes_{B}N\longrightarrow 0

is exact, and thus
M\otimes_{B}N\cong P^{\bullet}\otimes_{B}N
in
\mathcal{D}(A\otimes C^{op})
.
Therefore,
M\otimes_{B}^{L}N\cong M\otimes_{B}N\cong
in
\mathcal{D}(A\otimes C^{op})
.
∎

Lemma 3.2

Let

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
B\subseteq A
be a ring extension.
If
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(A/B,(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j})=0

, then the following statements hold:

(1)

\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(A,A)=0

(2)

\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}((A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j},A)=0
for each

(3)

\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(A,(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j}\otimes_{B}A)=0

Proof.

(1) For any

to the exact sequence of left

and using the fact that
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(A/B,A/B)=0
,
we have that

\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}(A/B,A)=0
. Similarly, applying

we get

(2) It follows from

\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}((A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j},A/B)=0

to the exact sequence of left

we infer that
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B}((A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j},A)=0
for each

(3) For each

.

4

In this section, we will apply our main result to trivial extensions, Morita rings
and triangular matrix algebras, and we get several reduction methods
on singularity categories and Gorenstein defect categories.

Corollary 4.1

. Therefore, the extension

and Theorem

.

Corollary 4.2
.

Keep the above notations.
If
\mathrm{pd}_{C\otimes B^{op}}M<\infty
,
then there are triangle equivalences

D_{sg}(A)\cong D_{sg}(B)\coprod D_{sg}(C)\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ D_{def}(A)\cong D_{def}(B)\coprod D_{def}(C).

Proof.

It is clear that
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B&0\\
M&C\end{array}\right)\cong(B\times C)\ltimes M
,
M\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{B\times C}M=0

and
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{B\times C}(M,M)=0
for
i\geq 1
. Therefore,
the statement follows from Corollary
4.1
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let

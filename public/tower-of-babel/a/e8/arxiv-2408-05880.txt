Constructions of Frenet Curves with respect to Semi-Symmetric Metric Connection
Şaban Güvenç

Abstract
. Using a semi-symmetric metric connection, we construct Frenet frame and curvatures of curves in 3-dimensional manifolds and give examples of semi-symmetric Frenet curves in Euclidean, Sasakian and Kenmotsu manifolds.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53C07; Secondary 53C15, 53C25.

1.
Introduction

In a Riemannian manifold
\left(M^{n},g\right)
, a linear connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
is called a semi-symmetric metric connection if its
torsion tensor field
T
satisfies

T\left(X,Y\right)=\omega(Y)X-\omega(X)Y

and

\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{Z}g\right)\left(X,Y\right)=0,

where
\omega
is a
1
-form and
X,Y,Z\in\chi(M)
. Let
\nabla
denote
the Levi-Civita connection on
M
. Then, the semi-symmetric metric
connection has the form

(1.1)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+\omega(Y)X-g\left(X,Y\right)U,

where
U
is a vector field associated to
\omega
, i.e.
\omega(X)=g\left(X,U\right)
,
\forall X\in\chi(M)

[
7
]
.

This study consists of six sections. The first section is Introduction. In
Section
2
, we introduce semi-symmetric Frenet
curves in 3-dimensional manifolds and give necessary definitions. Then we
find the necessary and sufficient conditions for semi-symmetric Frenet
curves to be semi-symmetric geodesics of
\mathbb{E}^{3},

\mathbb{R}^{3}(-3)
and
\mathbb{H}^{3}(-1)
in Sections
3
,
4
and
5
, respectively. The
final section is reserved for the examples of semi-symmetric Frenet curves
in these manifolds.

2.
Semi-Symmetric Frenet Curves in
3
-Dimensions

In this section, we will define Frenet curves in
3
-dimensional Riemannian
manifolds endowed with a semi-symmetric metric connection.

Let
\gamma:I\rightarrow M
be a unit-speed smooth curve, where
M=\left(M,g\right)
is a
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold endowed with a
semi-symmetric metric connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
. Let
T=\gamma^{\prime}
denote the unit tangent vector field along the curve
\gamma
.
Firstly, if we differentiate
g(T,T)=1
with respect to
\widetilde{\nabla}
, since it is a metric connection, we find
g(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T,T)=0
. So
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T\perp T.

Definition 1
.

Let

\widetilde{\kappa}=\left\|\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T\right\|=\sqrt{g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T\right)}.

We call
\widetilde{\kappa}
as
semi-symmetric curvature of

\gamma
. If
\widetilde{\kappa}=0
(i.e.
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T=0
),
then
\gamma
is called a
semi-symmetric geodesic
. Semi-symmetric
geodesics are called
semi-symmetric Frenet curves of order

1
.

Definition 2
.

If
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T\neq 0
, then

N=\frac{\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T}{\widetilde{\kappa}}

is called
semi-symmetric normal of

\gamma
.

Notice that
g(N,N)=1
. Then

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}g(N,N)=0,

that is
g(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N,N)=0.
Thus
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N\perp N.

Definition 3
.

If
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N
is parallel to
T
, we call
\gamma
a

semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
2
.

Let
\gamma
be a semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
2
.
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N=\lambda T
for some function
\lambda.
Since
g(T,N)=0
, we
find

g(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T,N)+g\left(T,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N\right)=0,

g\left(\widetilde{\kappa}N,N\right)+g\left(T,\lambda T\right)=0,

g\left(T,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N\right)=\lambda=-\widetilde{\kappa}.

As a result

Proposition 1
.

If
\gamma
is a semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
2
, then
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N=-\widetilde{\kappa}T.

Definition 4
.

If
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N
is not parallel to
T
, we call
\gamma
a

semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
3
.

Let
\gamma
be a semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
3
. Since
g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N,N\right)=0,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N
can be
written as

(2.1)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N=g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N,T\right)T+g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N,B\right)B

for some unit vector field
B,
which is g-orthogonal to
T
and
N
.
B
is
called the
semi-symmetric binormal of

\gamma
. From
g(T,N)=0
,
we have

g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N,T\right)=-\widetilde{\kappa},

so we can write

g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N,B\right)B=\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N+\widetilde{\kappa}T.

Definition 5
.

For a semi-symmetric metric Frenet curve of order
3
, we define

\widetilde{\tau}=\left\|\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N+\widetilde{\kappa}T\right\|>0,

which we call
semi-symmetric torsion of
\gamma
.

As a result, from (
2.1
), for a semi-symmetric metric Frenet curve
of order
3,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N=-\widetilde{\kappa}T+\widetilde{\tau}B.

Proposition 2
.

For a semi-symmetric metric Frenet curve of order
3
,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B=-\widetilde{\tau}N.

Proof.

Since
M
is
3
-dimensional, we
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B\in span\left\{T,N,B\right\}
, which is a
g
-orthonormal vector basis along the curve
\gamma
by construction. So we can write

(2.2)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B=g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B,T\right)T+g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B,N\right)N+g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B,B\right)B.

Differentiating
g\left(T,B\right)=0,

g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T,B\right)+g\left(T,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B\right)=0,

g\left(\widetilde{\kappa}N,B\right)+g\left(T,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B\right)=0,

(2.3)

g\left(T,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B\right)=0.

Differentiating
g\left(N,B\right)=0,

g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N,B\right)+g\left(N,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B\right)=0,

g\left(-\widetilde{\kappa}T+\widetilde{\tau}B,B\right)+g\left(N,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B\right)=0,

(2.4)

g\left(N,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B\right)=-\widetilde{\tau}.

Differentiating
g\left(B,B\right)=1,

(2.5)

g\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B,B\right)=0.

If we write (
2.3
),(
2.4
) and (
2.5
) in (
2.2
), we get
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B=-\widetilde{\tau}N.

∎

Now,

Definition 6
.

We call
\left\{T,N,B\right\}
the
semi-symmetric Frenet frame
field of

\gamma
if its order is
3
. For order
2
,
\left\{T,N\right\}
will be called the s
emi-symmetric Frenet frame field of

\gamma.

Semi-symmetric Frenet curves of order
1
(semi-symmetric geodesics) have
the s
emi-symmetric metric Frenet frame field
\left\{T\right\}.

Definition 7
.

A semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
1
is a
semi-symmetric
geodesic
. A semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
2
with constant
\widetilde{\kappa}>0
is a
semi-symmetric circle
. A semi-symmetric
Frenet curve of order
3
with constant
\widetilde{\kappa}>0
and constant

\widetilde{\tau}>0
is a
semi-symmetric helix
.

To sum up our construction, we call the next formulas as
semi-symmetric Frenet formulas
:

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T=-\widetilde{\kappa}N,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N=-\widetilde{\kappa}T+\widetilde{\tau}B,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}B=-\widetilde{\tau}N,

where
\widetilde{\kappa}=\left\|\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T\right\|

is the semi-symmetric curvature and
\widetilde{\tau}=\left\|\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}N+\widetilde{\kappa}T\right\|
is the semi-symmetric
torsion of
\gamma.

Remark 1
.

Notice that semi-symmetric normal
N
and semi-symmetric binormal
B
do not
have to coincide with the ones of the Levi-Civita connection. But
T=\gamma^{\prime}
is the same. Also notice that for order
3
,
\left\{T,N,B\right\}
is fundamentally constructed using Gram-Schmidt process of

\left\{T,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T\right\},

which can also be calculated after construction as follows:

Proposition 3
.

For a semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
3
,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T=-\widetilde{\kappa}^{2}T+\widetilde{\kappa}^{\prime}N+\widetilde{\kappa}\widetilde{\tau}B.

For a semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
2
,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T=-\widetilde{\kappa}^{2}T+\widetilde{\kappa}^{\prime}N.

For a semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
1
,
\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T=0.

3.
Semi-Symmetric Geodesics of
\mathbb{E}^{3}

Let us consider the Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}^{3}
endowed with the
semi-symmetric connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
, where we choose

U=\frac{\partial}{\partial z},\text{ }\omega\left(X\right)=g\left(X,U\right),

where
\left\{x,y,z\right\}
are coordinate functions,
g=dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}
is the Euclidean metric. It is well-known that

e_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\text{ }e_{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\text{ }e_{3}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

form a g-orthonormal basis of
\chi\left(\mathbb{E}^{3}\right)
.

Let
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{3},

\gamma(s)=\left(\gamma_{1}(s),\gamma_{2}(s),\gamma_{3}(s)\right)
be a semi-symmetric geodesic
of
\mathbb{E}^{3}
, where
s
denotes the arc-length parameter. Then we
find

(3.1)

0=\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T=\nabla_{T}T+g(T,U)T-g(T,T)U,

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection and
T=\frac{d}{ds}=\gamma^{\prime}=\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime},\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)
. In
\mathbb{E}^{3},
it is well-known that

\nabla_{T}T=\gamma^{\prime\prime}=\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime\prime},\gamma_{3}^{\prime\prime}\right).

We also get

g(T,U)=\gamma_{3}^{\prime}

and

(3.2)

g(T,T)=\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)^{2}=1.

From (
3.1
), we can write

\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime\prime},\gamma_{3}^{\prime\prime}\right)+\gamma_{3}^{\prime}.\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime},\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)-\left(0,0,1\right)=0,

which gives us the following system of differential equations:

\gamma_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{3}^{\prime}=0,

\gamma_{2}^{\prime\prime}+\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\gamma_{3}^{\prime}=0,

\gamma_{3}^{\prime\prime}+\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)^{2}-1=0.

If we solve this system, we can state the following result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{3},

\gamma(s)=\left(\gamma_{1}(s),\gamma_{2}(s),\gamma_{3}(s)\right)
be a semi-symmetric Frenet
curve of
\mathbb{E}^{3}
endowed with the semi-symmetric connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
with unit vector field
U=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
, where
s
denotes the arc-length parameter. Then
\gamma
is a
semi-symmetric geodesic if and only if

\gamma_{1}(s)=e^{-c_{1}}.c_{2}.\arctan\left(e^{s-c_{1}}\right)+c_{3},

\gamma_{2}(s)=e^{-c_{1}}.c_{4}.\arctan\left(e^{s-c_{1}}\right)+c_{5},

\gamma_{3}(s)=-s+\ln\left(e^{2s}+e^{2c_{1}}\right)+c_{6},

where
c_{i}
,
i=\overline{1,6}
are arbitrary constants satisfying

4e^{2c_{1}}=c_{2}^{2}+c_{4}^{2}.

4.
Semi-symmetric Geodesics of
\mathbb{R}^{3}(-3)

Let
M=\mathbb{R}^{3}
with coordinate functions
\left\{x,y,z\right\}
.
Let us define

\xi=2\frac{\partial}{\partial z},\text{ }\eta=\frac{1}{2}\left(dz-ydx\right),

X=2\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\text{ }Y=2\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+y\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right),

\varphi X=Y,\text{ }\varphi\xi=0,

g=\frac{1}{4}\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}\right)+\eta\otimes\eta.

\left(M,\varphi,\xi,\eta,g\right)
is a Sasakian manifold known as
\mathbb{R}^{3}(-3)
.
\left\{X,Y,\xi\right\}
is a
g
-orthonormal basis
of
\chi\left(M\right)
and the Levi-Civita connection is calculated as

\nabla_{X}X=0,\text{ }\nabla_{X}Y=\xi,\nabla_{X}\xi=-Y,

\nabla_{Y}X=-\xi,\text{ }\nabla_{Y}Y=0,\nabla_{Y}\xi=X,

\nabla_{\xi}X=-Y,\text{ }\nabla_{\xi}Y=X,\nabla_{\xi}\xi=0.

If we choose
U=\xi
and denote the semi-symmetric metric connection by
\widetilde{\nabla}
, we find

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}X=-\xi,\text{ }\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=\xi,\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\xi=X-Y,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}X=-\xi,\text{ }\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}Y=-\xi,\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}\xi=X+Y,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\xi}X=-Y,\text{ }\widetilde{\nabla}_{\xi}Y=X,\widetilde{\nabla}_{\xi}\xi=0.

Let
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}(-3),

\gamma=\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\right)
be a semi-symmetric geodesic. Then

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime},\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma_{2}^{\prime}X+\gamma_{1}^{\prime}Y+\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}\right)\xi\right].

Since
\gamma
is unit-speed, we have

(4.1)

\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}\right)^{2}=4.

Using
\widetilde{\nabla}
, we obtain

(4.2)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}X=\frac{1}{2}\left[-\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}+\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\right)\xi-\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}\right)Y\right],

(4.3)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}Y=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)\xi+\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}\right)X\right],

(4.4)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\xi=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}+\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\right)X+\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)Y\right].

As a result, we get

(4.5)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T=\frac{1}{2}\left[\lambda X+\mu Y+\nu\xi\right]=0,

where

\lambda=\gamma_{2}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}^{\prime}-\gamma_{2}\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{3}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\gamma_{3}^{\prime},

\mu=\gamma_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{2}\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{3}^{\prime}-\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\gamma_{3}^{\prime},

\nu=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{\prime\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}^{\prime}.

Now we can state the following theorem:

Theorem 2
.

Let
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}(-3),

\gamma(s)=\left(\gamma_{1}(s),\gamma_{2}(s),\gamma_{3}(s)\right)
be a semi-symmetric Frenet
curve of
\mathbb{R}^{3}(-3)
endowed with the semi-symmetric connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
with unit vector field
U=\xi
, where
s
denotes the
arc-length parameter. Then
\gamma
is a semi-symmetric geodesic if and
only if it satisfies the system of non-linear differential equations
\lambda=0,

\mu=0,\nu=0,
and

\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}\right)^{2}=4.

Corollary 1
.

If
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}(-3)
is a semi-symmetric
geodesic, then

\gamma_{3}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}=\frac{2\left(e^{2s}-e^{4c_{1}}\right)}{e^{2s}+e^{4c_{1}}},

\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\right)^{2}=\frac{16\left(e^{2s+4c_{1}}\right)}{e^{2s}+e^{4c_{1}}},

where
s
is the arc-length parameter and
c_{1}
is a constant.

Proof.

From (
4.1
) and
\nu=0
, we find

2\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}\right)^{\prime}=\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\right)^{2}=4-\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}\right)^{2}.

If we denote
f(s)=\gamma_{3}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2},

we have Riccati’s differential equation

2f^{\prime}=4-f^{2},

which gives the result.
∎

5.
Semi-symmetric Geodesics of
\mathbb{H}^{3}(-1)

Let us recall the Poincare model of the hyperbolic space. Let

M=\left\{\left(x,y,z\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:z>0\right\},

g=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}\right).

Then

e_{1}=z\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\text{ }e_{2}=z\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\text{ }e_{3}=-z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

form a
g
-orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{H}^{3}(-1)
. The corresponding
Levi-Civita connection is

\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{1}=-e_{3},\text{ }\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}=0,\text{ }\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{3}=e_{1},

\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{1}=0,\text{ }\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{2}=-e_{3},\text{ }\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{3}=e_{2},

\nabla_{e_{3}}e_{1}=0,\text{ }\nabla_{e_{3}}e_{2}=0,\text{ }\nabla_{e_{3}}e_{3}=0.

If we choose
U=e_{3}
, we calculate the semi-symmetric metric connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
as

\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{1}}e_{1}=-2e_{3},\text{ }\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{1}}e_{2}=0,\text{ }\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{1}}e_{3}=2e_{1},

\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{2}}e_{1}=0,\text{ }\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{2}}e_{2}=-2e_{3},\text{ }\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{2}}e_{3}=2e_{2},

\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{3}}e_{1}=0,\text{ }\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{3}}e_{2}=0,\text{ }\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{3}}e_{3}=0.

Let
\gamma:I\rightarrow H^{3}\left(-1\right)
be a semi-symmetric
geodesic. Then

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}=\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime},\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{3}}e_{1}+\frac{\gamma_{2}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{3}}e_{2}-\frac{\gamma_{3}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{3}}e_{3}.

Since
g\left(T,T\right)=1
, we find

(5.1)

\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)^{2}=\gamma_{3}^{2}.

Using
\widetilde{\nabla}
, we obtain

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}e_{1}=-\frac{2\gamma_{1}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{3}}e_{3},

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}e_{2}=-\frac{2\gamma_{2}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{3}}e_{3},

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}e_{3}=\frac{2\gamma_{1}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{3}}e_{1}+\frac{2\gamma_{2}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{3}}e_{2}.

As a result, we find

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma_{3}^{2}}\left[\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{3}-3\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)e_{1}\right.

\displaystyle+\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{3}-3\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)e_{2}

\displaystyle\left.-\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{3}-\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+2\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+2\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\right)e_{3}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Now we can state the following theorem:

Theorem 3
.

Let
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}(-1),

\gamma(s)=\left(\gamma_{1}(s),\gamma_{2}(s),\gamma_{3}(s)\right)
be a semi-symmetric Frenet
curve of
\mathbb{H}^{3}(-1)
endowed with the semi-symmetric connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
with unit vector field
U=e_{3}
, where
s
denotes the
arc-length parameter. Then
\gamma
is a semi-symmetric geodesic if and
only if

\gamma_{1}(s)=k_{1}+\frac{2c_{2}e^{3s+\frac{c_{3}}{2}}}{3\sqrt{1+c_{1}^{2}}}\text{ }_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{2},\frac{7}{4};-e^{4s+c_{3}}\right),

\gamma_{2}(s)=l_{1}+\frac{2c_{2}e^{3s+\frac{c_{3}}{2}}}{3\sqrt{1+c_{1}^{2}}}\text{ }_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{2},\frac{7}{4};-e^{4s+c_{3}}\right),

\gamma_{3}(s)=\frac{c_{2}e^{s}}{\sqrt{e^{c_{3}+4s}+1}},

where
c_{1,2,3}
and
k_{1},l_{1}
are constants
\left(c_{2}>0\right)

and
{}_{2}F_{1}
is the hypergeometric function.

6.
Examples of Semi-Symmetric Frenet Curves

Next examples are based on the choice of
U
as previous sections. Firstly,
we give examples in
\mathbb{E}^{3}
:

Example 1
.

\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{3},

\gamma\left(s\right)=\left(s,0,1\right)
is a semi-symmetric circle with
\widetilde{\kappa}=1
and
the semi-symmetric Frenet frame

\left\{T=\left(1,0,0\right),\text{ }N=(0,0,-1)\right\}.

It is obvious that it is a line (i.e. a geosedic) in the usual sense with no
curvature and torsion. It only has a tangential vector field
T=\left(1,0,0\right).

Example 2
.

\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{3},

\gamma\left(s\right)=\left(0,s,1\right)
is also a semi-symmetric circle with
\widetilde{\kappa}=1.

It is a line as well in the usual sense.

Example 3
.

\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{3},

\gamma\left(s\right)=\left(\cos s,\sin s,0\right)
is a semi-symmetric circle with
\widetilde{\kappa}=\sqrt{2}
and the semi-symmetric Frenet frame field

\left\{T=\left(-\sin s,\cos s,0\right),\text{ }N=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(-\cos s,-\sin s,1\right)\right\}.

It is a circle with curvature
1
in the usual sense.

Example 4
.

\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{3},

\gamma\left(s\right)=\left(0,\cos s,\sin s\right)
is a semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
2
with
\widetilde{\kappa}=1+\sin s
and the Frenet frame field

\left\{T=\left(0,-\sin s,\cos s\right),\text{ }N=\left(0,-\cos s,-\sin s\right)\right\}.

It is a circle with curvature
1
in the usual sense. Notice that it is

not
a semi-symmetric circle.

Example 5
.

\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{3},

\gamma\left(s\right)=\left(\gamma_{1}(s),\gamma_{2}(s),\gamma_{3}(s)\right)
be a unit-speed
semi-symmetric Frenet curve of order
3
. Then

\left(\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\gamma_{3}^{\prime}\right)^{2}=1

and one can calculate

On the equivalence of Lurie’s ∞ \infty -operads and dendroidal ∞ \infty -operads
Vladimir Hinich, Ieke Moerdijk

Abstract
. In this paper we prove the equivalence of two symmetric monoidal ∞ \infty -categories of ∞ \infty -operads, the one defined in Lurie [ L.HA ] and the one based on dendroidal spaces.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

In this paper we return to the question of the comparison of various
notions of
\infty
-operad occurring in the literature. One such notion
is the one defined by Lurie in terms of simplicial sets over the nerve
of the category of finite pointed sets, see
[
L.HA
]
, Sect. 2;
another is the one defined in terms of dendroidal sets, or dendroidal
spaces
[
CM
]
. Lurie’s
\infty
-category and the dendroidal one are
in fact symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories where the monoidal structures resemble the Boardman-Vogt tensor product of operads. The
\infty
-categories themselves are underlying
Quillen model structures. The dendroidal model category has been shown in

[
CM
]
to be Quillen equivalent to the model category of classical
simplicial or topological operads.

A first such comparison was made in
[
HHM
]
, where it was shown that
if one restricts oneself to operads without constants, the Lurie model
and the dendroidal one are (Quillen) equivalent at the level of model
categories. Moreover, this equivalence respects the monoidal structure
of the associated homotopy categories, which is a shadow of the much
richer structure of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category.

In a long paper
[
B
]
, Barwick constructs another
\infty
-category based on his notion of operator category, and proves this
\infty
-category to be equivalent to Lurie’s
version mentioned above. A next comparison was studied in
[
CHH
]
, where the
dendroidal model was shown to be equivalent to Barwick’s version at the
level of
\infty
-categories. Combined with Barwick’s equivalence, this
gives a composed equivalence between Lurie’s
\infty
-category and the
dendroidal one, now avoiding the condition on the absence of constants
of
[
HHM
]
. However, the comparison of
[
CHH
]
does not address
the question of equivalence as symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories.

The goal of this paper is to prove a relatively direct and explicit
equivalence between two symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories. One is
the
\infty
-category
\mathtt{LOp}
(short for “Lurie operads”) underlying
Lurie’s model category, the other is the
\infty
-category
\mathtt{DOp}

(for “dendroidal operads”) underlying the dendroidal model category.
To give the reader a rough idea already at this stage, we remark that
our proof is based on a functor from level forests to forests,
denoted

\omega:\mathbb{F}\to\Phi,

see
2.2.2
and
3.1
for the notation.

\mathtt{LOp}
is an
\infty
-category of presheaves on
\mathbb{F}
and
\mathtt{DOp}
is one
on
\Phi
, and the equivalence is simply realized by the functors

2.
Preliminary definitions

2.1.
\infty
-categorical conventions

We present here some basic notation and discuss a few standard recipes
for working with
\infty
-categories.

In what follows the word “category” means
\infty
-category, operad means
\infty
-operad, functor means
\infty
-functor and so on.
If we wish to emphasize that an
\infty
-category (or an

\infty
-operad) can be modelled by a
strict
inner Kan complex,
we sometimes refer to it as a
conventional
category (or operad).

The most basic category is the category of spaces
\mathcal{S}

underlying the Quillen model category of simplicial sets. Given two categories
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{D}
, there is a category of functors
\operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})

satisfying the standard equivalence

\operatorname{Map}(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}))=\operatorname{Map}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}).

The category of categories
\mathtt{Cat}
can be realized as a full subcategory
of the category of simplicial spaces
P(\Delta)=\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{S})
,
spanned by the simplicial presheaves
X:\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{S}

satisfying
the
\infty
-categorical variant of the Segal and completeness
properties, see
[
R
]
:

1.

For any
n
the natural map

X_{n}\to X_{1}\times_{X_{0}}\cdots_{X_{0}}\times X_{1}

is an equivalence.

2.

The map
\operatorname{Map}(J,X)\to\operatorname{Map}(*,X)

induced by a map
*\to J
, is an equivalence. (
J\in P(\Delta)
is the presheaf corresponding to the category having two objects and a unique isomorphism between them.)

This approach allows one to define the opposite of a category
defined by a simplicial space
\mathcal{C}:\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{S}
as the composition
of
\mathcal{C}
with the functor
\mathrm{op}:\Delta\to\Delta
reversing the ordering of a finite totally ordered set.

2.1.1.

Another endofunctor of
\Delta
, carrying
[n]
to the join

[n]^{\mathrm{op}}\star[n]=[2n+1]
, gives the construction of
\mathtt{Tw}(\mathcal{C})
, the category of twisted arrows in
\mathcal{C}
. The canonical projection

\mathtt{Tw}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}

is a left fibration; that is, it is classified by a functor

\widetilde{Y}:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{S}.

This functor can be rewritten as the Yoneda embedding
Y:\mathcal{C}\to P(\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{S})
.

Dealing with
\infty
-categories requires extra care when writing formulas. It is in general not allowed to define functors by
describing them on objects and arrows as there is no way to describe all
required compatibilities. However, some standard formulas
do define functors. For instance, given a functor
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
,
one has a functor
\widetilde{f}:\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{S}
defined by the
formula
\widetilde{f}(d,c)=\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{D}}(d,f(c))
. This formula just means that

\widetilde{f}
is defined as the composition

\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widetilde{Y}}}{{\to}}\mathcal{S}.

2.1.2.

A map of spaces
f:X\to Y
(for example, modelled by Kan simplicial sets) exhibits
X
as
a subspace of
Y
if
f
induces an equivalence of
X
with a union of a subset of connected components of
Y
. This
notion generalizes to any category
\mathcal{C}
: an arrow
f:c\to d
is mono,
if for any
x\in\mathcal{C}
the induced map of spaces
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,c)\to\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,d)

is an inclusion of a subspace. In the case when
\mathcal{C}=\mathtt{Cat}
, we get the notion of
subcategory: it is defined by a subspace of objects, and a subspace
of morphisms for each pair of objects.

2.1.3.
Subfunctor

The notion of subspace mentioned above allows one to construct
a subfunctor of a given functor.
In this context the following elementary result is useful
(see
[
H.Lec
]
, 9.2.3).

Proposition
.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a functor. Let, for each
x\in\mathcal{C}
, a subobject
G_{x}
of
F(x)
be given, so that for each
a:x\to y
the composition
G_{x}\to F(x)\to F(y)
factors through
G_{y}
. Then the collection of subobjects
G_{y}
uniquely glues into a subfunctor

G:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
.

2.1.4.

A marked category is a pair

\mathcal{C}^{\natural}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\circ})
where
\mathcal{C}
is a category
and
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
containing
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{eq}}
, the maximal subspace of
\mathcal{C}
.
The category
\mathtt{Cat}^{+}
of marked categories is defined
as the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Fun}([1],\mathtt{Cat})
spanned by the embeddings

\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{C}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\flat}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{eq}})
the category
\mathcal{C}
endowed with the minimal marking. The embedding

\mathtt{Cat}\to\mathtt{Cat}^{+}

carrying
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{C}^{\flat}
has a left adjoint called localization
and denoted

(1)

L:\mathtt{Cat}^{+}\to\mathtt{Cat}.

For marked categories represented by a pair of simplicial categories,
the localization is represented by the Dwyer-Kan construction.

2.1.5.

Many important
\infty
-categories appear as the ones

. Another one is the complete Segal model
for the

\infty

The

Fortunately, one can often present the

3

A localization

2.2.
Dendroidal
\infty
-operads

2.2.1.

We begin by recalling the definition of the category
\Omega
introduced in
[
MW
]
and discussed in detail in
[
HM
]
, Section 3.2,
see also the Appendix below.
The objects of
\Omega
are finite trees, allowed to have “external”
edges attached to just one vertex. One of these external edges is
specified as the
root
of the tree, the other external edges are called leaves. The edges of a tree connected to two vertices are called
internal
or
inner
edges. The category
\Omega
includes the object
\eta
consisting of just one edge that is at the same time the root and the leaf. The choice of a root defines an orientation of each edge in the tree, towards the root. This specifies for each vertex
v
an outgoing edge
\mathit{out}(v)
and
a set
\mathit{in}(v)
of incoming edges. The cardinality of
\mathit{in}(v)

is called the
valence
of the vertex
v
, and it is allowed to be zero. Vertices of valence zero are called
stumps
.
To define the morphisms of the category
\Omega
, we observe that each such tree
T
defines a (symmetric) colored operad

o(T)
. The colors of
o(T)
are the edges of
T
and its operations are generated by the vertices, each vertex
v
defining an operation from the set
\mathit{in}(v)
to
\mathit{out}(v)
. The morphisms
S\to T
in
\Omega
are now defined to be the operad maps
o(S)\to o(T)
. In particular, this makes
o
into a full embedding of
\Omega
into the category
\mathtt{Op}(\mathtt{Set})
of operads in sets.

Alternatively, one may define the morphisms
S\to T
in
\Omega
as generated by “elementary morphisms”: these are

(a)

isomorphisms
S\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}T
;

(b)

degeneracies
S\twoheadrightarrow T
where
S
is obtained from
T
by putting a new vertex in the middle of an edge of
T
;

(c)

inner face maps
S\rightarrowtail T
where
S
is obtained from
T
by contracting an inner edge of
T
; and

(d)

outer face maps where
S
is obtained by chopping off
an external vertex from
T
(i.e., a vertex attached to just one inner edge). In addition, if
T
is a corolla, i.e. a tree
with just one vertex, each edge of
T
defines an elementary morphism
\eta\to T
.

We observe now that there is a full embedding
\iota:\Delta\hookrightarrow\Omega
of the simplex category
\Delta
into
\Omega
,
which assigns to each object
[n]
of
\Delta
the linear tree

\iota[n]
with
n
vertices (all of valence
1
) and
n+1
edges. Under this identification of
\Delta
with a subcategory of
\Omega
, degeneracies, inner and outer faces have their usual meaning.

2.2.2.

It is convenient to extend the category
\Omega
to include disjoint unions of trees. To this end we define the
category of forests
\Phi
similar to
\Omega
, as the full subcategory
of
\mathtt{Op}(\mathtt{Set})
, spanned by
o(F)
where
F
is a disjoint union of trees and
o(F)
is defined as the coproduct of the operads
o(T_{i})
where
T_{i}
are the components of
F
. We will denote by

the full embedding described above, see diagram
(
28
). The category
\Phi
can be alternatively defined as the category
obtained from
\Omega
by formally adjoining finite coproducts.
(Note that our definition of

is different from that of

[

]
. First, our category
\Phi
has an empty forest; and, secondly,
the version of
[
HHM
]
has fewer arrows (independence property) than
our version.)

2.2.3.

The (
\infty
-) category of dendroidal operads
\mathtt{DOp}
is defined as the full subcategory
of the category
P(\Omega)
of presheaves (of spaces) spanned by the presheaves
satisfying Segal and completeness properties:

(D1)

For an inner edge

T

T_{b}
and

be the upper and the lower part
of

X(T)\to X(T_{b})\times_{X(b)}X(T^{b})

(D2)

Completeness:
\iota^{*}(X)\in P(\Delta)
is a complete
Segal space.

It will be convenient for us to realize

as a full subcategory in

spanned by the presheaves
satisfying the above two properties, as well as the extra (also Segal-type) property.

(D3)

The natural map

In particular,

It is a standard fact that the category

is equivalent to the one underlying
the model category of dendroidal complete Segal spaces, or the equivalent one of dendroidal sets,
see
[
CM
]
or
[
HM
]
.

2.3.
Lurie

-operads

In what follows
\mathit{Fin}_{*}
denotes the conventional category of finite pointed sets. We denote by

An arrow

A functor

(Fib2)

(Fib3)

3.1.1.

Consider an object

The set of edges of the forest

immediately above

\mathit{in}(v_{a})=\alpha^{-1}_{i}(a)

In particular, the set of leaves in the forest can be identified with
A_{0}

consists of the elements of
A_{n}

together with the elements of
A_{i}
sent to the basepoint
*
under
\alpha_{i+1}:A_{i}\to A_{i+1}
for

Here is an example
of the forest corresponding to the map

with

\langle 4\rangle
\langle 3\rangle

Note the following property of

.

3.1.2 Lemma
.

Any forest
F\in\Phi
is a retract of some

for some

In order to present a forest

\operatorname{Fun}^{\natural}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})

-algebras in

4.1.1 Theorem
.

Let

be a morphism of operads which is essentially surjective on colors. Assume that the functor

f^{*}:\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathcal{S})\to\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})

is an equivalence. Then
f
is an equivalence of operads.

Note that the essential surjectivity condition cannot be dropped: the
embedding of a category into its Karoubian envelope induces an equivalence
of the categories of presheaves!
The proof of the theorem is given in
4.1.6
.

Note the following easy result.

4.1.2 Lemma
.

Let
\alpha:X\to Y
be an operadic equivalence in

\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}^{\natural}_{*}}
. Then the map

\operatorname{Fun}^{\natural}(Y,\mathcal{S})\to\operatorname{Fun}^{\natural}(X,\mathcal{S}),

where
\mathcal{S}
is considered as a Lurie operad, is an equivalence.

Proof.

Given
K\in\mathtt{Cat}
, the category
\mathcal{S}^{K}=\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathcal{S})
has a cartesian symmetric monoidal structure, so it can be considered as an object of

\mathtt{LOp}
. The operadic equivalence
\alpha:X\to Y
induces
an equivalence

\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}^{\natural}_{*}}}(Y,\mathcal{S}^{K})\to\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}^{\natural}_{*}}}(X,\mathcal{S}^{K}).

Now the equivalence

\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}^{\natural}_{*}}}(X,\mathcal{S}^{K})=\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}^{\natural}_{*}}}(X\times K^{\flat},\mathcal{S})

yields an equivalence
\operatorname{Map}(K,\operatorname{Fun}^{\natural}(Y,\mathcal{S}))=\operatorname{Map}(K,\operatorname{Fun}^{\natural}(X,\mathcal{S}))
.
∎

In particular, for trees
S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
the tensor product

S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}
can be reconstructed from the tensor product of their interiors
S^{\circ}_{1},\ldots,S^{\circ}_{n}
: precisely, if

S^{\circ}_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{\circ}_{n}=\bigcup_{j}A_{j}=\operatorname{colim}A_{\alpha}
,
then

S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}=\bigcup_{j}A_{j}[\bar{E}]=\operatorname{colim}A_{\alpha}[\bar{E}]
,
where

E=\{(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})|\textrm{ each }e_{j}\textrm{ is a leaf of }S_{j}^{\circ},\textrm{ at least one}\ e_{i}\textrm{ is a stump of }S_{i}\}.

From this observation one easily deduces the following lemma.

A.2.1 Lemma
.

For trees
S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
and the set
E
as above, the map

(S_{1}^{\circ}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}^{\circ})\sqcup^{E}\bar{E}\to S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}

is a weak equivalence.

Proof.

Write
S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}
as the colimit
\operatorname{colim}A_{\alpha}

of the diagram of finite intersections of shuffles. This is a Reedy
cofibrant diagram, so
S_{1}^{\circ}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}^{\circ}
is
also the homotopy colimit. Then

(S_{1}^{\circ}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}^{\circ})\sqcup^{E}\bar{E}

is the colimit of the diagram of the
A_{\alpha}
together with the inclusions

\bar{\eta}\leftarrow\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{\to}}A_{\alpha}

for each
e
in
E
and each
\alpha
. This colimit is the same as the colimit of the pushouts
A_{\alpha}\sqcup^{E}\bar{E}
. But
A_{\alpha}\sqcup^{E}\bar{E}\to A_{\alpha}[\bar{E}]
is a weak equivalence for each
\alpha
, see (
29
), and
S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}=\operatorname{colim}_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}[\bar{E}]
is again the homotopy colimit of the corresponding Reedy cofibrant diagram. Therefore, the weak equivalences

A_{\alpha}\sqcup^{E}\bar{E}\to A_{\alpha}[\bar{E}]
for different
\alpha
yield the one in the lemma.
∎

A.3.
Segal condition

Consider trees
S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
and a further tree
T
. Let
d
be an
inner edge in
T
. Cutting the tree
T
at
d
results in two trees

T^{d}
and
T_{d}
of which
T=T^{d}\circ_{d}T_{d}
is a grafting.

A.3.1 Proposition
.

The map

S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}\otimes(T^{d}\cup T_{d})\to S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}\otimes T

is a weak equivalence in the operadic model structure on
\mathtt{dSet}
.

Proof.

The claim is known to hold if all the trees are open, see
[
HHM
]
, Lemma 6.3.5.
The general case immediately follows from the lemma above, at least if
d
itself is not a stump in
T
. If it is,

T_{d}=\bar{\eta}
and
T=T^{d}[\bar{\eta}]
, and the map
in the proposition is

S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}\otimes(T^{d}\sqcup^{d}\bar{d})\to S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}\otimes T.

But in this case

S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}\otimes(T^{d}\sqcup^{d}\bar{d})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{=}}(S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}\otimes T^{d})\sqcup^{D}\bar{D}

where
D=\{(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},d)|e_{i}\textrm{ is a leaf in }S_{i}\}
,
and the proposition becomes a special case of the lemma again.
∎

A.4.
Associativity

The tensor product of dendroidal sets is not associative. For example, cosider trees
R,S
and
T
. If
S\otimes T
is a union of shuffles, say,
S\otimes T=\bigcup A_{j}
, then
R\otimes(S\otimes T)=\bigcup R\otimes A_{j}
is a union of only a subset of shuffles making up
R\otimes S\otimes T
, see
[
HM
]
, Section 4.4. More generally, if
S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}=\bigcup_{j\in J}A_{j}
as above, then the map

(31)

S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes(S_{i}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{j})\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}\longrightarrow S_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S_{n}

is an inclusion of the form
\bigcup_{k\in K}A_{k}\to\bigcup_{j\in J}A_{j}
where
K\subseteq J
.

A.4.1 Proposition
.

Let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
be trees. Then for any
1\leq i<j\leq n
the map
(
31
) is a weak equivalence, and similarly for more
nested bracketings.

Proof.

This map is a weak equivalence (in fact, inner anodyne) if all the
S_{i}

are open, and the same holds for more nested bracketings, see
[
HHM
]
, Lemma 6.3.6. So for general trees
S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
the map

S^{\circ}_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes(S^{\circ}_{i}\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{\circ}_{j})\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{\circ}_{n}\longrightarrow S^{\circ}_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{\circ}_{n}

is a weak equivalence. As before, we can write this map as

\operatorname{colim}A_{\beta}\to\operatorname{colim}A_{\alpha}
where
\beta\subseteq K
and
\alpha\subseteq J
are nonempty subsets, and the colimits are the colimits of Reedy cofibrant diagrams. Taking the pushout along

\sqcup_{E}\eta\to\sqcup_{E}\bar{\eta}
for
E
as before yields the top map in the diagram

(32)

\textstyle{\operatorname{colim}A_{\beta}\sqcup^{E}\bar{E}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{\operatorname{colim}A_{\alpha}\sqcup^{E}\bar{E}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{colim}A_{\beta}[\bar{E}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{colim}A_{\alpha}[\bar{E}]}

The vertical maps are colimits of grafting weak equivalences of the form (
29
),
so the bottom map is a weak equivalence as well. But this is precisely the map in the proposition.
∎

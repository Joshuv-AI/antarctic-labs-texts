A randomised trapezoidal quadrature
Yue Wu

Abstract
A randomised trapezoidal quadrature rule is proposed for continuous functions which enjoys less regularity than commonly required. Indeed, we consider functions in some fractional Sobolev space. Various error bounds for this randomised rule are established while an error bound for classical trapezoidal quadrature is obtained for comparison. The randomised trapezoidal quadrature rule is shown to improve the order of convergence by half. Keywords. Randomised trapezoidal quadrature, Fractional sobolev space, Almost sure convergence, L p L^{p} convergence. MSC2020. 65C05, 65D30.



1
Introduction

It is well known that the trapezoidal quadrature in classical numerical analysis is a technique for approximating
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued definite integral when the integrand is at least twice differentiable. Without loss of generality we consider the time interval is
[0,T]
and
g\in C^{2}([0,T])
is the integrand of interest, where
C^{2}([0,T]):=C^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-continuous functions, endowed with the uniform norm topology, that have continuous first two derivatives. The trapezoidal quadrature is proven to achieve order of convergence
2
for evaluating the integral
I[g]:=\int_{0}^{T}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
with finite many point evaluations
[
4
]
. To implement this, partition the interval
[0,T]
into
N
equidistant intervals with stepsize
h_{N}=\frac{T}{N}
, i.e.,

\Pi_{h}:=\{t_{j}:=jh\}_{j=0}^{N}\subset[0,T],

(1)

where the subscription
N
is suppressed in h for the
sake of notational simplicity but
assumed implicitly in all of the quantities introduced involving
h
. Define

Q_{h}[g]:=\frac{h}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\big(g(t_{i})+g(t_{i+1})\big).

(2)

When
g
has less regularity, trapezoidal quadrature will show a slower convergence and a sharp bound
[
3
]
. To accelerate its convergence when
g
is ’rougher’, we consider a
randomised trapezoidal quadrature
, which is inspired by the randomised version of mid-point Runge-Kunta quadrature rule
[
7
]
and stochastic version of trapezoidal quadrature for Itô integral
[
5
]
. In this paper, the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued target function
g
is assumed to be in fractional Sobolev space
W^{\sigma,p}(0,T)
under Sobolev-Slobodeckij norm:

\|g\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(0,T)}=\big(\int_{0}^{T}|g(t)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}|\dot{g}(t)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|\dot{g}(t)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+(\sigma-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}s\big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(3)

for
\sigma\in(1,2)
and
p\in[2,\infty)
. We may write
\|g\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(0,T)}
as
\|g\|_{W^{\sigma,p}}
for short.
Let us define a randomised trapezoidal quadrature,

RQ^{\tau,n}_{h}[g]:=\frac{h}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\big(g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h)+g(t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h)\big)\mbox{ for }n\in[N],

(4)

where
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N-1}
is a sequence of independent and identically (i.i.d.) uniformly distributed random variables on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
,
\bar{\tau}_{i}:=1-\tau_{i}
and
[N]:=\{1,\ldots,N\}
. The main result, Theorem
3.2
, shows that the convergence rate can be improved to
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\sigma-\frac{1}{2}})
compared to
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\sigma})
achieved by classical trapezoidal quadrature (Theorem
3.1
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we present
some prerequisites from probability theory. In Section 3 we state and prove error estimates
for both classical trapezoidal quadrature and randomised trapezoidal quadrature. In addition, we also investigate the error estimate in almost sure sense for randomised trapezoidal quadrature in Theorem
3.3
, which is proven still superior to classical one. In the last section, we verify the results through several numerical experiments.

2
Preliminaries

This section is devoted to a briefly review on essential probability results for audience who are not familiar with probability theory. Most of the contents are repeated material from Section 2 in
[
7
]
. One may refer to
[
2
]
for a more detailed introduction.

Recall that a
probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}})

consists of a measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
endowed with a finite
measure
{\mathbb{P}}
satisfying
{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)=1
. A random variable
X\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is
called
integrable
if
\int_{\Omega}|X(\omega)|\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}(\omega)<\infty
.
Then, the
expectation
of
X
is defined as

{\mathbb{E}}[X]:=\int_{\Omega}X(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}(\omega)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}x\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{X}(x),

where
\mu_{X}
is distribution of X on its image space. We write
X\in L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
p\in[1,\infty)
if

\int_{\Omega}|X(\omega)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}(\omega)<\infty
, where

L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a Banach space endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|X\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[|X|^{p}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|X(\omega)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}(\omega)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We will write
\|X\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}})}
as
\|X\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
for short.

We say that a
family of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued random variables
(X_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a discrete time

stochastic process
if we interpret the index
m
as a time parameter. An crucial concept in our main proof is
martingales
, which is a special case of discrete time stochastic process with many nice properies. If
(X_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is an independent family of integrable random variables
satisfying
{\mathbb{E}}[X_{m}]=0
for each
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then the stochastic process
defined by the partial sums

\displaystyle S_{n}:=\sum_{m=1}^{n}X_{m},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

is a discrete time martingale. One of the most important inequalities for martingale Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality. In this paper we need its discrete time version.

Theorem 2.1

(Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality)
.

For each
p\in(1,\infty)
there exist positive constants
c_{p}
and
C_{p}

such that for every discrete time martingale
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

c_{p}\|[X]_{n}^{{1}/{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\big\|\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}|X_{j}|\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\big\|[X]_{n}^{{1}/{2}}\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

where
[X]_{n}=|X_{1}|^{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{n}|X_{k}-X_{k-1}|^{2}
denotes the

quadratic variation
of
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
up to
n
.

3
Trapezoidal quadratures for a rougher integrand

This section investigate the errors from trapezoidal rules for approximating integral of
g\in W^{\sigma,p}
. The error bound from classical trapezoidal rule is obtained in Section
3.1
and the ones from randomised trapezoidal rule is in Section
3.2
.

3.1
Classical trapezoidal quadrature for
g\in W^{\sigma,p}

Theorem 3.1
.

If
g\in W^{\sigma,p}(0,T)
for
\sigma\geq 1
, then we have

|I[g]-Q_{h}[g]|\leq CT^{1-\frac{1}{p}}h^{\sigma}\|g\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(0,T)},

(5)

where
C
is a constant that only depends on
p
.

Proof.

To show Eqn. (
5
), we follow
[
5
]
to rewrite

g(t_{i})+g(t_{i+1})=2g(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}})+\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i+1}}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(6)

where
t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=\frac{1}{2}(t_{i}+t_{i+1})
. Then the LHS of Eqn. (
5
) can be rewritten as

I[g]-Q_{h}[g]=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}E_{1}^{i,i+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}E_{2}^{i,i+1},

where

E^{i,i+1}_{1}:=\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}(g(t)-g(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}))\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}\dot{g}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}t,

(7)

and

E^{i,i+1}_{2}:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i+1}}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(8)

Regarding
E_{1}^{i,i+1}
, first note that

\displaystyle V_{i}:=\frac{1}{h}(g(t_{i+1})-g(t_{i}))\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}(t-t_{i+\frac{1}{2}})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

Then we can rewrite
E_{1}^{i,i+1}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{1}^{i,i+1}=E_{1}^{i,i+1}-V_{i}=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}\dot{g}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t-V_{i}\\
&=\frac{1}{h}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}(\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s))\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(9)

Thus evaluating
\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}E_{1}^{i,i+1}
under
L^{p}
norm gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big|\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}E_{1}^{i,i+1}\Big|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\end{split}

(10)

where the second line is deduced by applying Hölder’s inequality twice, and
\frac{1}{q}:=1-\frac{1}{p}
.
For the case
\sigma=1
and any
p\geq 2
, we may directly apply the discrete Hölder’s inequality to the last term above:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(r)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(r)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}=ChT^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}}.

For the case
\sigma>1
and any
p\geq 2
, we may first make use of the definition of
W^{\sigma,p}
and then apply the discrete Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{p}+\sigma-1}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\frac{|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}}{|r-s|^{1+(\sigma-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=h^{\sigma}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\frac{|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}}{|r-s|^{1+(\sigma-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq h^{\sigma}T^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}}.

For term
E_{2}^{i,i+1}
, we can follow a similar argument in
[
5
]
to show that

E_{2}^{i,i+1}=\frac{1}{2h}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\Big(\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}}(\dot{g}(s)-\dot{g}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i+1}}(\dot{g}(s)-\dot{g}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t.

(11)

Indeed, note that

(t_{i}-t_{i+\frac{1}{2}})+(t_{i+1}-t_{i+\frac{1}{2}})=0,

If defining a new process

\displaystyle P_{i}:=\frac{t_{i}-t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}{2h}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\dot{g}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{t_{i+1}-t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}{2h}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\dot{g}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}t,

then Eqn. (
11
) can be obtained through the fact that

\displaystyle E_{2}^{i,i+1}=E_{2}^{i,i+1}-P_{i}.

Thus applying a similar argument as for
\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}E_{1}^{i,i+1}
, we can show that

\Big|\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}E_{2}^{i,i+1}\Big|\leq CT^{1-\frac{1}{p}}h^{\sigma}\|g\|_{W^{\sigma,p}[0,T]}.

(12)

Finally, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\big|I[g]-Q_{h}[g]\big|\leq\Big|\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}E_{1}^{i,i+1}\Big|+\Big|\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}E_{2}^{i,i+1}\Big|\leq CT^{1-\frac{1}{p}}h^{\sigma}\|g\|_{W^{\sigma,p}[0,T]}.

∎

For classical trapezoidal quadrature (CTQ), Theorem
3.1
states that its order of convergence would be the same as the regularity of the integrand. The boundary case is when
g\in W^{1,p}
, then the order is
1
.

3.2
Randomised trapezoidal rules for
g\in W^{\sigma,p}

For the randomised trapezoidal quadrature (
4
), the proof follows the idea of randomised quadrature given by
[
7
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Define
I^{n}:=\int_{0}^{t_{n}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
for
n\in[N]
for
g\in W^{\sigma,p}
with
\sigma\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
. Then
RQ^{\tau,n}_{h}[g]\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and is an unbiased estimator of
I^{n}[g]
, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[RQ_{h}^{\tau,n}[g]]=I^{n}[g]
. Moreover, it holds true that

\big\|I[g]-RQ^{\tau,N}_{h}[g]\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{p}|T|^{\frac{p-2}{2p}}h^{\frac{1}{2}+\sigma}\|g\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(0,T)},

(13)

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends only on
p
.

Proof.

First due to
g\in W^{\sigma,p}
we have
\|g\|_{L^{p}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
. Recall that
\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{U}(0,1)
for each
i\in[N-1]\cup\{0\}
. Then it follows that

\frac{h}{2}\big(\|g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g(t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big)=\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|g(t)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Hence
RQ_{h}^{\tau,n}[g]\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for
n\in[N]
. To show
RQ_{h}^{\tau,n}[g]
is unbiased, we need to examine each term in RHS of Eqn.(
4
) through spelling out the expectation and changing variable, i.e.,

\frac{h}{2}\mathbb{E}[g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h)]=\frac{h}{2}\int_{0}^{1}g(t_{i}+rh)\,\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

and

\frac{h}{2}\mathbb{E}[g(t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h)]=\frac{h}{2}\int_{0}^{1}g(t_{i}+(1-r)h)\,\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Summing these terms up gives that
RQ^{\tau,n}_{h}[g]
is unbiased for
I^{n}[g]
. Furthermore, if define the error term like

E^{n}:=I^{n}[g]-RQ^{\tau,n}_{h}[g]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(2g(t)-g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h)-g(t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t,

(14)

then each summand is a mean-zero random variable, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(2g(t)-g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h)-g(t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\big]=0.

Note that the summands are mutually independent due to the independence of
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N-1}
. In addition, it is easy to show
E^{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore,
E^{n}
is a
L^{p}
-martingale. Then applying the discrete version of the Burkholder
-
Davis
-
Gundy inequality leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big\|\max_{n}|E^{n}|\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\big\|\mathbf{[}E^{n}\mathbf{]}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&=\frac{C_{p}}{2}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(2g(t)-g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h)-g(t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq C_{p}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(g(t)-g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h))\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&+C_{p}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(g(t)-g(t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\end{split}

(15)

where in the second line we substitute the quadratic variation
[E^{n}]_{N}
. Due to symmetric property, it is easy to see we only need to handle the first term on the RHS of Eqn.(
15
). Note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&C_{p}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(g(t)-g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h))\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&=C_{p}\Big\|\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(g(t)-g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h))\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C_{p}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big\|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(g(t)-g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h))\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(16)

Then we have that

\displaystyle C_{p}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big\|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(g(t)-g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h))\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=C_{p}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big\|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}+\tau_{i}h}^{t}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Big\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big\|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}+\tau_{i}h}^{t}|\dot{g}(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Big\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}h\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{p}h\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(s)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

When
p=2
, the term on the right hand side above can be directly bounded by

C_{p}h\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(s)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{p}h^{\frac{3}{2}}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}}.

(17)

where
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
. For
p>2
, we may apply discrete Hölder inequality and get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&C_{p}h\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(s)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C_{p}h\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}\frac{p}{p-2}}\Big)^{\frac{p-2}{2p}}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|\dot{g}(s)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq C_{p}h^{1+\big(\frac{2p}{q(p-2)}-1\big)\frac{p-2}{2p}}|T|^{\frac{p-2}{2p}}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}}=C_{p}h^{\frac{3}{2}}|T|^{\frac{p-2}{2p}}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}}.\end{split}

(18)

Now we have shown Bound (
13
) when
\sigma=1
. For Bound (
13
) under
\sigma>1
, we first note that Eqn.(
6
) remains true if replacing
t_{i}
by
t_{i}+\tau_{i}h
and
t_{i+1}
by
t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h
, i.e.,

g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h)+g(t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h)=2g(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}})+\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}+\tau_{i}h}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(19)

Thus the second line of Eqn.(
15
) can be further splitted as the follows:

\displaystyle\frac{C_{p}}{2}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(2g(t)-g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h)-g(t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(g(t)-g(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}})\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+C_{p}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}+\tau_{i}h}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Similar as in the proof of Theorem

defined in Eqn. (

E^{i,i+1}_{2}(\tau):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}+\tau h}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}+\bar{\tau}h}\dot{g}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(20)

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
,
E^{i,i+1}_{1}
can be handled through the equivalent form Eqn.(
9
) and
E_{2}^{i,i+1}(\tau)
can be treated in a similar way as Eqn.(
11
) by replacing
t_{i}
by

and

by

in the inner integral of Eqn.(

), i.e.,

E_{2}^{i,i+1}=\frac{1}{2h}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\Big(\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}+\tau_{i}h}(\dot{g}(s)-\dot{g}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h}(\dot{g}(s)-\dot{g}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t.

(21)

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{C_{p}}{2}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(2g(t)-g(t_{i}+\tau_{i}h)-g(t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i}^{N-1}|E_{1}^{i,i+1}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+C_{p}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i}^{N-1}|E_{2}^{i,i+1}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{p}}{h}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}(\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s))\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{p}}{2h}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\Big(\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}+\tau_{i}h}(\dot{g}(s)-\dot{g}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{i}+\bar{\tau}_{i}h}(\dot{g}(s)-\dot{g}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

where the first term on the right hand side from Eqn.(

) and the second term is due to Eqn. (

). Let us now deal with the first term, the second term can be handled in the same way. Following a similar argument in (

), we have that

\displaystyle\frac{C_{p}}{h}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}(\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s))\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{p}}{h}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big\|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}(\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s))\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Big\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|\,\mathrm{d}r\big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{4}{q}}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{4}{q}+\frac{2}{p}+2(\sigma-1)}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}\frac{|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}}{|r-s|^{1+(\sigma-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=C_{p}h^{\sigma}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}\frac{|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}}{|r-s|^{1+(\sigma-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we apply Hölder’s inequality in Line 4 and 5. Similarly as in (
17
), for
p=2

C_{p}h^{\sigma}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}\frac{|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}}{|r-s|^{1+(\sigma-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{p}h^{\sigma+\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{W^{\sigma,p}}.

Applying discrete Hölder inequality for

\displaystyle C_{p}h^{\sigma}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{\frac{2}{q}}\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}\frac{|\dot{g}(r)-\dot{g}(s)|^{p}}{|r-s|^{1+(\sigma-1)p}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}h^{\sigma+\frac{1}{2}}T^{\frac{p-2}{2p}}\|g\|_{W^{\sigma,p}}.

3.3.2
Example 2

Consider the function:

g_{B}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}B(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\ \mbox{for }t\in[0,T],

(24)

where
B(s)
is a realisation of standard Brownian motion (BM) (c.f. Section 3.1 in
[
6
]
). It is well known that
B\in C^{\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon}
for arbitrary small
\epsilon>0
, therefore
g_{B}\in W^{\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon,p}
for
p>1
. Figure
3
illustrates how one BM path looks like and the curve of its
g_{B}
.

Figure 3:
One realisation of standard Brownian motion and function values for the corresponding
g_{B}
.

We are interested in approximating
I[g_{B}]
.

Due to the nature of BM, it is not easy to obtain the exact value of
g_{B}
. To approximating terms
g_{B}(t_{n})
, we simply apply Euler method, i.e.,

\displaystyle g_{B}(t_{n})=\int_{0}^{t_{n}}B(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}B(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\approx h\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}B(t_{i}).

For CTQ, for a fixed stepsize
h\in[0,1],
we have that

\displaystyle Q_{h}[g_{B}]=\frac{h}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(g_{B}(t_{n})+g_{B}(t_{n+1}))=h\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_{B}(t_{n})+\frac{h}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(g_{B}(t_{n+1})-g_{B}(t_{n}))

\displaystyle=h\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_{B}(t_{n})+\frac{h}{2}g_{B}(t_{N})=h\sum_{n=1}^{N}g_{B}(t_{n})-\frac{h}{2}g_{B}(t_{N})\approx h^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}B(t_{i})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}B(t_{i}).

For RTQ, define the corresponding i.i.d. uniform distributed sequence is
\{\tau^{h}_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, we have a similar expression:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&RQ_{h}^{\tau,N}[g_{B}]=\frac{h}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(g_{B}(t_{n}+\tau^{h}_{n}h)+g_{B}(t_{n}+\bar{\tau}^{h}_{n}h))\\
&=h\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_{B}(t_{n})+\frac{h}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\big(\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+\tau^{h}_{n}h}B(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+\bar{\tau}^{h}_{n}h}B(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\big)\\
&\approx h\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_{B}(t_{n})+\frac{h}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\Big(\frac{\tau^{h}_{n}h}{2}\big(B(t_{n})+B(t_{n}+\tau^{h}_{n}h)\big)+\frac{\bar{\tau}^{h}_{n}h}{2}\big(B(t_{n})+B(t_{n}+\bar{\tau}^{h}_{n}h)\big)\Big)\\
&=h\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_{B}(t_{n})+\frac{h^{2}}{4}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}B(t_{n})+\frac{h^{2}}{4}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\big(\tau^{h}_{n}B(t_{n}+\tau^{h}_{n}h)+\bar{\tau}^{h}_{n}B(t_{n}+\bar{\tau}^{h}_{n}h)\big).\end{split}

(25)

Note that to deduce the third line, we make use of CTQ rather than Euler method. The reason for this is that using Euler method will result in the same expression as
Q_{h}[g_{B}]
. It is easy to see the difference between expressions for CTQ and RTQ lies in the last two terms of the equation above.

To compute the reference solution, we first sampled a BM path with a small stepsize
h_{\mbox{ref}}=2^{-14}
. Then we generated an i.i.d. standard uniformly distributed sequence
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, and sampled
B((j+\tau_{j})h_{\mbox{ref}})
, which is determined by property of Brownian bridge (c.f. Section 3.1 in
[
6
]
), i.e.,

\displaystyle B\big((j+\tau_{j})h_{\mbox{ref}}\big)\sim\mathcal{N}\Big(\bar{\tau}_{j}B\big((j+1)h_{\mbox{ref}}\big)+\tau_{j}B(jh_{\mbox{ref}}),\tau_{j}\bar{\tau}_{j}h_{\mbox{ref}}\Big),

where
\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
is normal distribution with mean
\mu
and variance
\sigma^{2}
, and
\bar{\tau}_{j}:=1-\tau_{j}
for all
j
. The reference solution was thus computed via CTQ on grid points consisting of
\{jh_{\mbox{ref}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
as well as these intermediate
\{(j+\tau_{j})h_{\mbox{ref}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.

The reason for including randomness at this early stage is that this allows an easier sampling procedure for
\{\tau^{h}_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
on coarser grids of stepsize
h
. For instance, if
h=2h_{\mbox{ref}}
and consider interval
[t_{0},t_{0}+h]
, then
t_{0}+\tau_{0}h_{\mbox{ref}}
and
t_{0}+h_{\mbox{ref}}+\tau_{1}h_{\mbox{ref}}
are in the same interval. Thus
\tau^{h}_{0}
can be determined from

\displaystyle t_{0}+\tau^{h}_{0}h=\mathbbm{1}_{U_{0}}(0)(t_{0}+\tau_{0}h_{\mbox{ref}})+\mathbbm{1}_{U_{0}}(1)(t_{0}+h_{\mbox{ref}}+\tau_{1}h_{\mbox{ref}}),

where
\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot}(\cdot)
is the indicator function,
U_{0}\sim\mathcal{U}\{0,1\}
, i.e., a discrete uniform distribution on the integers 0 and 1 (shown in Figure
4
).

\tau^{h}_{0}

The
numerical approximations were calculated for both trapezoidal quadratures with larger step sizes
h\in\{2^{-i}:i=5,\ldots,10\}

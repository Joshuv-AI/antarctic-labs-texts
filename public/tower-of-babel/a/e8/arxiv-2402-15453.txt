Sandpile groups for cones over trees Thanks: Authors partially supported by NSF grant DMS-2053288.
Victor Reiner, Dorian Smith

Abstract
. Sandpile groups are a subtle graph isomorphism invariant, in the form of a finite abelian group, whose cardinality is the number of spanning trees in the graph. We study their group structure for graphs obtained by attaching a cone vertex to a tree. For example, it is shown that the number of generators of the sandpile group is at most one less than the number of leaves in the tree. For trees on a fixed number of vertices, the paths and stars are shown to provide extreme behavior, not only for the number of generators, but also for the number of spanning trees, and for Tutte polynomial evaluations that count the recurrent sandpile configurations by their numbers of chips.

1.
Introduction

This paper studies sandpile groups for graphs which are cones over trees. We first explain some background on
sandpile groups, some motivation
for examining cones over trees, and then our
main results.

1.1.
Laplacians and sandpile groups

Let
G
be a finite, undirected, connected graph
G=(V,E)
on vertices
V
and edges
E
, with parallel edges allowed, but no self-loops.
Its
sandpile group

K(G)
is an interesting isomorphism invariant, a finite abelian group whose cardinality is the number
\tau(G)
of spanning trees in
G
. It can be defined starting with
the
Laplacian matrix
in
\mathbb{Z}^{V\times V}
, whose rows and columns are both indexed by
the vertices
v
in
V
, with
(v,v^{\prime})
-entry given by

(1)

(L_{G})_{v,v^{\prime}}:=\begin{cases}\deg_{G}(v)&\text{ if }v=v^{\prime},\\
-\#\{\text{edges from }v\text{ to }v^{\prime}\}&\text{ if }v\neq v^{\prime}.\end{cases}

This Laplacian matrix may also be viewed as
L_{G}=\partial\circ\partial^{T}
where
\partial:\mathbb{Z}^{E}\longrightarrow Z^{V}
is the cellular boundary map for any orientation of the edges of
G
.
From here, one picks any choice of root vertex
v_{0}
in
V
,
to define a reduced Laplacian matrix
\overline{L}_{G}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{(V-\{v_{0}\})\times(V-\{v_{0}\})}
,
and then the
sandpile group
is its integer cokernel:

(2)

K(G):=\coker(\overline{L}_{G}:\mathbb{Z}^{V-\{v_{0}\}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{V-\{v_{0}\}})=\mathbb{Z}^{V-\{v_{0}\}}/\im\overline{L}_{G}.

The name
sandpile group
comes from the dynamics of the

abelian sandpile model
from physics, which leads to certain distinguished representatives
\mathbf{x}=(x_{v})_{v\in V-\{v_{0}\}}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{V-\{v_{0}\}}
for the cosets
\mathbf{x}+\im\overline{L}_{G}
, called
recurrent chip configurations
; see Corry and Perkinson
[
CP18
, Chap. 6]
and Klivans
[
Kli19
, Chap. 4]
for more
background. Letting
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
, one thinks of vectors
\mathbf{x}
in
\mathbb{N}^{V-\{v_{0}\}}
as
valid chip configurations
, with
x_{v}
denoting the number chips piled at vertex
v
; one does not keep track of the
number of chips at the root vertex
v_{0}
, which one can think of as
+\infty
.
Say that vertex
v
is
ready to fire
in a valid chip configuration
\mathbf{x}
if
x_{v}\geq\deg_{G}(v)
. Then
firing

\mathbf{x}
at
v

subtracts from
\mathbf{x}
the
v^{th}
column of
\overline{L}_{G}
; equivalently, one thinks of vertex
v
as passing one chip along each of its incident edges to its neighbors in
G
. Say
\mathbf{x}
is a
stable
chip configuration if
0\leq x_{v}\leq\deg_{G}(v)-1

for all
v
in
V-\{v_{0}\}
, so that no vertex in
V-\{v_{0}\}
is ready to fire. One calls

\mathbf{x}
a
recurrent
(or
critical
) chip configuration if it is both stable and has the property that
after firing the root vertex
v_{0}
(passing one chip along each edge incident to
v_{0}
to its neighbors), there exists some sequence of firings at vertices in
V-\{v_{0}\}
that leads back to
\mathbf{x}
. These recurrent configurations then give a system of coset representatives for
K(G)
, that is,

(3)

K(G)=\{\mathbf{x}+\im\overline{L}_{G}:\text{ recurrent }\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{N}^{V-\{v_{0}\}}\}

allowing one way to compute in the group.

Recurrent chip configurations also relate to an interesting isomorphism invariant of
G
, its
Tutte polynomial

T_{G}(x,y)
, reviewed in Section
3
below. Recall that
T_{G}(x,y)
is a bivariate polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
with nonnegative coefficients, which can
be defined either via a deletion-contraction recurrence, or as a bivariate generating function for the spanning trees
T\subset E
according to two statistics.
Thus
T_{G}(1,1)=|K(G)|=\tau(G)
,
the spanning trees number.
More generally, one has the following result of C. Merino
[
ML97
]
, conjectured by N. Biggs, showing
T_{G}(1,y)
is (up to a power of
y
)
the generating function
\sum_{\mathbf{x}}y^{\wt(\mathbf{x})}
for recurrent configurations
\mathbf{x}
counted by
their number of chips or
weight

\wt(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{v\in V-\{v_{0}\}}x_{v}
;
see
[
Kli19
, §3.3]
,
[
CP18
, §14.4]
.

Theorem.

[
ML97
, Thm. 3.6]

For a graph
G
with
m
edges, and any root vertex
v_{0}
, one has

(4)

T_{G}(1,y)=y^{\deg_{G}(v_{0})-m}\cdot\sum_{\mathbf{x}}y^{\wt(\mathbf{x})}.

where in the sum here,
\mathbf{x}
runs over all recurrent chip configurations with respect to the root
v_{0}
.

3.
The Tutte polynomial and deletion-contraction lemmas

We recall two definitions of the
Tutte polynomial
T_{G}(x,y)
, and derive recurrences for cones over trees.

3.1.
Two definitions of the Tutte polynomial

The first definition of
T_{G}(x,y)
is recursive.

Definition 3.1
.

For a graph
G=(V,E)
an edge
e
is
a
loop
if its endvertices are equal,
and a
coloop
(or
isthmus
or
bridge
) if its deletion
G\setminus e

has one more connected component
than
G
.

One can then define the
Tutte polynomial

T_{G}(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
recursively on
|E|
, via these rules:

(13)

T_{G}(x,y)=\begin{cases}T_{G\setminus e}(x,y)+T_{G/e}(x,y)&\text{if }e\text{ is neither a loop nor a coloop},\\
y\cdot T_{G\setminus e}(x,y)&\text{if e is a loop.}\\
x\cdot T_{G/e}(x,y)&\text{if e is a bridge.}\\
1&\text{if }G\text{ has no edges, that is, }E=\varnothing.\end{cases}

It can be shown that
T_{G}(x,y)
has the following
property: when
v
is a cut-vertex in
G
, meaning that
its deletion

Definition 3.2

of the edges

\displaystyle\IntAct_{\prec}(T)

\displaystyle:=\{e\in T:e\prec f\text{ whenever }(T-e)\cup f\text{ is a spanning tree}\},

\displaystyle\ExtAct_{\prec}(T)

\displaystyle:=\{f\in E-T:f\prec e\text{ whenever }(T-e)\cup f\text{ is a spanning tree}\}.

The cardinalities of these sets are called the

\displaystyle\intact_{\prec}(T)

\displaystyle T_{\Cone(T)}(1,y)

\displaystyle=T_{\Cone(T-\ell)}(1,y)+T_{\mathrm{Cone}^{(+v)}(T-\ell)}(1,y),

or

. Our last step is to analyze the cokernel of

Case 1.

s=1

is even.

When

, as desired:

\left(\begin{matrix}m_{p,s}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&2&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&2&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

Case 2.

Here adding
\frac{F_{2p-1}-1}{2}

to the first column results in this

\left(\begin{matrix}m_{p,s}&0&0&\cdots&0\\

-1&0&2&\cdots&0\\

-2&2&0&\cdots&0\\
-2&0&2&\cdots&0\\

Similar to Case 1, this last matrix has cokernel

∎

Proof of Theorem

This section proves Theorem

Recall the statement,

Proof of inequality (B)

Lemma 6.2

where the last equality used the recursive definition of
F_{n}(y)
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

For
n,m\geq 1
not both even, one has a coefficientwise inequality

F_{n}(y)F_{m}(y)\leq F_{n+m-1}(y).

Proof.

Induct on
n+m
, in two cases. If
n,m
are both odd then

\displaystyle F_{n}(y)F_{m}(y)=F_{n}(y)[F_{m-1}(y)+yF_{m-2}(y)]

\displaystyle=F_{n}(y)F_{m-1}(y)+yF_{n}(y)F_{m-2}(y)

\displaystyle\leq F_{n+m-2}(y)+yF_{n+m-3}(y)\quad\text{ by induction}

\displaystyle=F_{n+m-1}(y).

If
n
is even and
m
is odd then

\displaystyle F_{n}(y)F_{m}(y)=F_{m}(y)[F_{n-1}(y)+F_{n-2}(y)]

\displaystyle=F_{m}(y)F_{n-1}(y)+F_{m}(y)F_{n-2}(y)

\displaystyle\leq F_{n+m-2}(y)+F_{n+m-3}(y)\quad\text{ by induction}

\displaystyle=F_{n+m-1}(y).\qed

As mentioned in the Introduction, our goal was to use the graphs

as test cases for the Main Question on how the “shape”

G

K(G)
, including its size
\tau(G)=|K(G)|
, its minimal number of generators
\mu(G)
, and the coefficients of
T_{G}(1,y)
. We discuss this further here.

7.1.
Csikvári’s poset on trees

Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
suggest that the nunber of leaves
\ell(T)
in the tree
T
has some
positive correlation
with
\mu(\Cone(T))
, while Theorem
1.4
suggests
\ell(T)
has some
negative correlation
with both
\tau(\Cone(T))
and the coefficients of
T_{\Cone(T)}(1,y)
.

Seeing this, it is natural to consider work of
P. Csikvári
[
Csi10
,
Csi13
]
which introduced an interesting ranked poset structure on the set of all trees with
n
vertices, having the path
P_{n}
as its
unique minimal element, the star
S_{n}
as its unique maximal, with rank function
\mathrm{rank}(T)=\ell(T)-2
; see
[
Csi10
, Thm. 2.4, Cor. 2.5]
.
Many interesting graph invariants for trees behave in a monotone
fashion with respect to this poset, so that they
are bounded by the values on
P_{n},S_{n}
; see
[
Csi13
, §11]
.
His poset is the transitive closure of
covering relations
T\lessdot T^{\prime}
coming from
this operation called a
generalized tree shift

[
Csi10
, Defn. 2.1]
:

Let
(x,y)
be an ordered pair of distinct vertices in a tree
T
such that
all interior vertices on the path from
x
to
y

have degree two in
T
. Let
x^{\prime}
be the unique neighbor of
y
on this path to
x
; possibly
x^{\prime}=x
. Then the
generalized tree shift

T^{\prime}
(of
T
at
x,y
) is obtained from
T
by deleting all edges between
y
and its
T
-neighbors, except for the edge
\{y,x^{\prime}\}
, and adding in edges between
x
and all of the former
T
-neighbors of
y
except
x^{\prime}
.

For example, one can check that for
p\geq 3
, one has
CT(p,s)\lessdot CT(p-1,s+1)
in Csikvári’s poset on trees with

7.2.
Degree sequences and line graph edge density

As one goes upward from the path
P_{n}
to the star
S_{n}
in Csikvári’s order on trees
T

with
n
vertices, certain measures of the “shape” of the graph
G=\Cone(T)
move upward.
For example, the
line graph

\Line(G)=(V_{\Line(G)},E_{\Line(G)})

for
G=\Cone(T)

has its edge density
\frac{|E_{\Line(G)}|}{|V_{\Line(G)}|}
increasing as one goes up.
Also, the weakly decreasing degree sequence

d(G)=(d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n+1})
moves upward in the
dominance
or
majorization
order on
partitions.

Question 7.2
.

For
any
graph
G=(V,E)
, not necessarily a cone on a tree, consider these two parameters:

•

line graph edge density
\frac{|E_{\Line(G)}|}{|V_{\Line(G)}|}
, or

•

height of the degree sequence

d(G)=(d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots)
in the dominance order on partitions of
2|E|
.

Do either of these parameters of the graph
G
exhibit

•

positive correlation with
\mu(G)
, or

•

negative correlation with
\tau(G)

and with the coefficients of

Introduction

The width of an image along various directions is basic information in the
Image Processing Technique. We are interested in the deviation of the
widths of compact convex sets in the plane along a random direction.
Although it is an important and useful quantity both practically and theoretically,
as long as we know, there is no serious study on it.

Theorem 1
.

For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\eta=\arg(\beta/\alpha)\in(-\pi,\pi]
,
the following statements hold:

(i)

\mathbb{E}(X_{\alpha})=|\alpha|/\pi

(ii)

\mathbb{E}(X_{\alpha}X_{\beta})=\frac{1}{4\pi}|\alpha||\beta|V(\eta)

V(x)=(\pi-|x|)\cos x+\sin|x|~~(-\pi<x\leq\pi).

Figure 4:
Function

Proof
(i)
Let
\theta=\arg(\alpha)
. Then, we have

\mathbb{E}(X_{\alpha})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\alpha|\sin(\theta-\xi)_{+}d\xi=\frac{|\alpha|}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin\xi d\xi=\frac{|\alpha|}{\pi}.

(ii)
We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X_{\alpha}X_{\beta})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\alpha||\beta|\sin(\theta-\xi)_{+}\sin(\theta+\eta-\xi)_{+}d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}|\alpha||\beta|\int_{[0,\pi]\cap[-\eta,\pi-\eta]}\sin\xi\sin(\xi+\eta)d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}|\alpha||\beta|\int_{[0,\pi]\cap[-\eta,\pi-\eta]}(\cos\eta-\cos(2\xi+\eta))d\xi.

Therefore, if
\eta<0
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X_{\alpha}X_{\beta})=\frac{1}{4\pi}|\alpha||\beta|\int_{-\eta}^{\pi}(\cos\eta-\cos(2\xi+\eta))d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}|\alpha||\beta|((\pi+\eta)\cos\eta-\sin\eta)=\frac{1}{4\pi}|\alpha||\beta|V(\eta).

If
\eta\geq 0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X_{\alpha}X_{\beta})=\frac{1}{4\pi}|\alpha||\beta|\int_{0}^{\pi-\eta}(\cos\eta-\cos(2\xi+\eta))d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}|\alpha||\beta|((\pi-\eta)\cos\eta+\sin\eta)=\frac{1}{4\pi}|\alpha||\beta|V(\eta).

.

The maximum of

Proof

Truncation of the regular triangle

Let
X,Y
be general
\mathbb{R}
-valued, square integrable random variables on the
probability space
\Omega
. Assume further that
X\geq 0
everywhere and

\mathbb{E}(X)>0
. Recall that

\kappa(X)=\frac{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(X)^{2}}=\delta(X)^{2}+1.

It holds for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
with sufficiently small modulus that

\displaystyle\kappa(X+tY)=\frac{\mathbb{E}((X+tY)^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(X+tY)^{2}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(X)^{2}}\frac{1+2t\frac{\mathbb{E}(XY)}{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}+t^{2}\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}}{1+2t\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y)}{\mathbb{E}(X)}+t^{2}\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}(X)^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(X)^{2}}\left(1+2t\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}(XY)}{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}-\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y)}{\mathbb{E}(X)}\right)+O(t^{2})\right).

If
\frac{\mathbb{E}(XY)}{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}-\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y)}{\mathbb{E}(X)}=0
, then we have

\kappa(X+tY)=\frac{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(X)^{2}}\left(1+t^{2}\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}-\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}(X)^{2}}\right)+O(t^{3})\right).

Hence, the following Lemma holds.

Lemma 3
.

(i)

\frac{d}{dt}\delta(X+tY)|_{t=0}>,=,<0
if and only if

\frac{\mathbb{E}(XY)}{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}>,=,<\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y)}{\mathbb{E}(X)}
, respectively.

(ii)
Assume that “

(i)
. Then, there exists

for any

if

\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(X^{2})}>,<\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}(X)^{2}}

Proof
(i)
follows since

\textstyle\frac{d}{dt}\delta(X+tY)=(1/2)(\kappa(X+tY)-1)^{-1/2}\frac{d}{dt}\kappa(X+tY).

(ii)

Lemma 4
.

Let
T
and
S

\mathbb{C}

X_{T}=X_{S}
(a.s.)
holds if and only if there exists

S=-T+z

Proof
X_{-T}=X_{T}
holds since they have the same asymmetrization. Hence, the “if” part holds.

Let us prove the “only if” part. Let

Consider

\omega\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}

Then, it is locally minimal if and only if

\pm(\arg(\beta)-\arg(\alpha)),~\pm(\arg(\gamma)-\arg(\beta)),~\pm(\arg(\alpha)-\arg(\gamma))

X_{T}=X_{S}
(a.s.), then they should have the same set of
\omega

as this. Also, at any of these

that either

or
S=-T+z
for some

.

\Box

Lemma 5
.

Let
T
and
S
be triangles in
\mathbb{C}
. Then,

\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{S})\leq\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})^{1/2}\mathbb{E}(X_{S}^{2})^{1/2}.

The equality holds if and only if there exist
\lambda\geq 0
and

such that
either
S=\lambda T+z

S=-\lambda T+z

Proof
This is the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality for

\langle X,Y\rangle=E(XY)
.
The equality holds if and only if there exists

\lambda>0

Hence, by Lemma

S=\lambda T+z

S=-\lambda T+z

\Box

Lemma 6
.

Let
T=T(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
be a regular triangle.
Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Then,

\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\sigma})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{\sigma})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T})}.

The equality holds if and only if
\vec{0\sigma}
is parallel to
one of the edges of
T
, that is,
\arg(\sigma)
is equal to one of

\pm(\arg(\beta)-\arg(\alpha)),~\pm(\arg(\gamma)-\arg(\beta)),~\pm(\arg(\alpha)-\arg(\gamma))

modulo
2\pi
.

Proof
Without loss of generality, we assume that the length of the edges of
T
is 1.
It holds that
\mathbb{E}(X_{\mu z_{1}}X_{\mu z_{2}})=\mathbb{E}(X_{z_{1}}X_{z_{2}})
for any

\mu,z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\mu|=1
.
Moreover, since
e^{2\pi\mathbf{i}/3}T=T
, we have

\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\sigma})=\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{e^{2\pi\mathbf{i}/3}\sigma})=\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{e^{4\pi\mathbf{i}/3}\sigma}).

Hence,
\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\sigma})=(1/3)\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{S})

with the regular triangle
S=T(0,\sigma,e^{\pi\mathbf{i}/3}\sigma)
.
Therefore by Lemma
5
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\sigma})=(1/3)\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{S})\leq(1/3)\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})^{1/2}\mathbb{E}(X_{S}^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle=(1/3)\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})^{1/2}\mathbb{E}((|\sigma|X_{T})^{2})^{1/2}=(|\sigma|/3)\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2}).

The equality holds if and only if
\vec{0\sigma}
is parallel to
one of the edges of
T
. Thus, we have

\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\sigma})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}\leq\frac{|\sigma|}{3}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{\sigma})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T})}

with the equality if and only if
\vec{0\sigma}
is parallel to one of the edges of

.

Theorem 8
.

A parallel truncation of the regular triangle increases the deviation rate, while
a non-parallel truncation decreases it.

Parallel truncation:

Let
T=T(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
be a regular triangle of the edge length 1.
We also assume that it is of the counter clockwise order.
Let
t>0
be sufficiently small. Let

\beta_{t}=(1-t)\alpha+t\beta,~\gamma_{t}=(1-t)\alpha+t\gamma.

Let

V_{t}=T(\beta_{t},\beta,\gamma,\gamma_{t})

be a parallel truncation of
T
at
\alpha
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle X_{V_{t}}=X_{\beta-\beta_{t}}+X_{\gamma-\beta}+X_{\gamma_{t}-\gamma}+X_{\beta_{t}-\gamma_{t}}

\displaystyle=X_{T}-tX_{\beta-\alpha}-tX_{\alpha-\gamma}+tX_{\beta-\gamma}

\displaystyle=X_{T}-tX_{Y}

with
Y=X_{\vec{c}}+X_{\vec{b}}-X_{-\vec{a}}
,
where we denote
\vec{a}=\gamma-\beta,~\vec{b}=\alpha-\gamma,~\vec{c}=\beta-\alpha
.
Since
\mathbb{E}(X_{\vec{c}})=\mathbb{E}(X_{\vec{b}})=\mathbb{E}(X_{-\vec{a}})=1/\pi
, we have by Lemma
6
,

\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\vec{c}})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\vec{b}})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{-\vec{a}})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}=\frac{1/\pi}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T})},

and hence,

\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}Y)}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\vec{c}})+\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\vec{b}})-\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{-\vec{a}})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})+\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})-\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}=\frac{1/\pi}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T})}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y)}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T})}.

Now, we prove that

\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}>\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T})^{2}}

so that
\delta(V_{t})>\delta(T)
for sufficiently small
|t|\neq 0
by Lemma
3
.
We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y)=\mathbb{E}(X_{\vec{b}}+X_{\vec{c}}-X_{-\vec{a}})=\frac{1}{\pi}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X_{T})=\frac{3}{\pi}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y^{2})=\mathbb{E}((X_{\vec{b}}+X_{\vec{c}}-X_{-\vec{a}})^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}+(2/4\pi)(V(2\pi/3)-2V(\pi/3))=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})=\frac{3}{4}+(6/4\pi)V(2\pi/3)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}

\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}=\frac{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}}{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}}=0.214\cdots>\frac{1}{9}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(Y)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T})^{2}},

and complete the proof that
\delta(V_{t})>\delta(T)
for sufficiently small
|t|\neq 0

in the case of parallel truncation.

Non-parallel truncation:

Let
T=T(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
be a regular triangle of the edge length 1.
We also assume that it is of the counter clockwise order.
Let
0<\lambda\neq 1
and
t>0
be sufficiently small. Let

\beta_{t}=(1-t)\alpha+t\beta,~\gamma_{t}=(1-\lambda t)\alpha+\lambda t\gamma.

Let

V_{t}=T(\beta_{t},\beta,\gamma,\gamma_{t})

be a non-parallel truncation of
T
at
\alpha
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle X_{V_{t}}=X_{\beta-\beta_{t}}+X_{\gamma-\beta}+X_{\gamma_{t}-\gamma}+X_{\beta_{t}-\gamma_{t}}

\displaystyle=X_{T}-tX_{\vec{c}}-tX_{\lambda\vec{b}}+tX_{\vec{e}}

\displaystyle=X_{T}-tY,

where we denote
\vec{b}=\alpha-\gamma,~\vec{c}=\beta-\alpha,~\vec{e}=\vec{c}+\lambda\vec{b}

and
Y=X_{\vec{c}}+X_{\lambda\vec{b}}-X_{\vec{e}}
.

By Lemma
6
, we have

\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\vec{c}})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\vec{b}})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}=\frac{1/\pi}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T})}~\mbox{and}~\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\vec{e}})}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}<\frac{|\vec{e}|/\pi}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T})}.

\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\vec{c}})+\lambda\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\vec{b}})-\mathbb{E}(X_{T}X_{\vec{e}}))}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})+\lambda\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})-|\vec{e}|\mathbb{E}(X_{T}^{2})}>\frac{1/\pi}{\mathbb{E}(X_{T})},

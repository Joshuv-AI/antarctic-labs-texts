Abstract
. In this article, we mainly study the products of commutator ideals of Lie-admissible algebras such as Novikov algebras, bicommutative algebras, and assosymmetric algebras. More precisely, we first study the properties of the lower central chains for Novikov algebras and bicommutative algebras. Then we show that for every Lie nilpotent Novikov algebra or Lie nilpotent bicommutative algebra 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , the ideal of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} generated by the set { a b − b a ∣ a , b ∈ 𝒜 } \{ab-ba\mid a,b\in\mathcal{A}\} is nilpotent. Finally, we study properties of the lower central chains for assosymmetric algebras, study the products of commutator ideals of assosymmetric algebras and show that the products of commutator ideals have a similar property as that for associative algebras.

2.
Central chains of ideals for Novikov and bicommutative algebras

The aim of this section is to show that some properties of lower central chain of associative algebras also hold for Novikov algebras and bicommutative algebras. These properties will be very useful for the study of Lie nilpotent Novikov algebras and Lie nilpotent bicommutative algebras in the next section. We begin with some basic facts on Lie-admissible algebras.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an arbitrary Lie-admissible algebra over a given field
F
. We define

[a,b]=ab-ba

for all
a
and
b\in\mathcal{A}
.
For all subspaces
A
,
B
,
C
of
\mathcal{A}
, we define

[A,B]=\mathsf{span}\{[a,b]\mid a\in A,b\in B\},\ \ AB=\mathsf{span}\{ab\mid a\in A,b\in B\}

and

(A,B,C)=\mathsf{span}\{(a,b,c)\mid a\in A,b\in B,c\in C\},

where the associator
(a,b,c)
means
(ab)c-a(bc)
.
We call a space
V\subseteq\mathcal{A}
a
Lie ideal
of
\mathcal{A}
if we have
[V,\mathcal{A}]\subseteq V
.
Finally, for all subspaces
A
and
B
of
\mathcal{A}
, we define

A\circ B=\mathsf{Id}([A,B]),

that is, the ideal of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
[A,B]
. Following the idea of Jennings
[
14
]
, we call
A\circ B
the
commutator ideal
of
A
and
B
. We clearly have
A\circ B=B\circ A
.

Equipped with the notion of commutator ideals, we are now able to recall the notion of central chains of ideals of a Lie-admissible algebra
\mathcal{A}
.

Let

(2.1)

\mathcal{A}=A_{1}\supseteq A_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq A_{m}\supseteq A_{m+1}=(0)

be a chain of ideals of
\mathcal{A}
. Such a chain is called a
central chain of ideals
if we have

(2.2)

\mathcal{A}\circ A_{i}\subseteq A_{i+1}\ \ \ (i=1,2,...\hbox{}\hskip 0.56905pt,m).

We shall soon see that Novikov algebras, bicommutative algebras and assosymmetric algebras which possess central chains of ideals have special properties; we investigate some of them by considering a particular central chain:

Definition 2.1
.

For every Lie-admissible algebra
\mathcal{A}
we form a series of ideals

(2.3)

H_{1}:=\mathcal{A},\ H_{i+1}:=H_{i}\circ\mathcal{A}\mbox{ for }i\geq 1.

We say that
\mathcal{A}
is of
finite class
if
H_{n}=(0)
for some positive integer
n
. For the minimal integer
n
such that
H_{n}=(0)
, we call
n-1
the
class
of
\mathcal{A}
, and call

(2.4)

\mathcal{A}=H_{1}\supseteq H_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq H_{n-1}\supseteq H_{n}=(0)

the
lower central chain
of
\mathcal{A}
. To avoid too many repetitions, we shall fix the notation of
H_{i}
for all
i\geq 1
.

With the notations of (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), it is straightforward to show that
H_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
by induction on
i
.

The following lemma provides us with a description of commutator ideals of Novikov algebras or bicommutative algebras.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be either a Novikov algebra or a bicommutative algebra, and let
A
and
B
be two Lie ideals of
\mathcal{A}
. Then we have
A\circ B=[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B]
.

Proof.

Clearly, it suffices to show that
[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B]
is an ideal of
\mathcal{A}
. In either case,
\mathcal{A}
is a Lie-admissible algebra. So we have

[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]=0

for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{A}
. In particular, for all
a\in A
,
b\in B
and
x\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

[a,b]x-x[a,b]=[[a,b],x]=[[a,x],b]+[a,[b,x]].

Since
[a,x]\in A
and
[b,x]\in B
, we obtain that

[a,b]x=x[a,b]+[[a,x],b]+[a,[b,x]]\in[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B].

For all
y\in\mathcal{A}
, by the right commutativity, we obtain that

(x[a,b])y=(xy)[a,b]\in[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B].

Moreover, since
\mathcal{A}
is Lie-admissible algebra, we have

y(x[a,b])=y(-[[a,x],b]-[a,[b,x]]+[a,b]x)=-y[[a,x],b]-y[a,[b,x]]+y([a,b]x).

If
\mathcal{A}
is a Novikov algebra, then by left symmetry and by the above reasoning, we deduce

\displaystyle y(x[a,b])=

\displaystyle-y[[a,x],b]-y[a,[b,x]]+y([a,b]x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-y[[a,x],b]-y[a,[b,x]]+(y[a,b])x+[a,b](yx)-([a,b]y)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-y[[a,x],b]-y[a,[b,x]]+(yx)[a,b]+[a,b](yx)-([a,b]y)x

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\,[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B].

It follows that
[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B]
is an ideal, and so we have
A\circ B=[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B]
if
\mathcal{A}
is a Novikov algebra.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a bicommutative algebra, then by left commutativity and by the above reasoning, we have

\displaystyle y(x[a,b])

\displaystyle=-y[[a,x],b]-y[a,[b,x]]+y([a,b]x)

\displaystyle=-y[[a,x],b]-y[a,[b,x]]+[a,b](yx)

\displaystyle\in\,[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B].

It follows that
[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B]
is an ideal, and so we have
A\circ B=[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B]
if
\mathcal{A}
is a bicommutative algebra, thus finishing the proof.
∎

It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
) that an associative algebra is of finite class if and only if it has a central chain of ideals, the proof of which is quite easy and holds for every Lie-admissible algebra. In light of this note, we shall focus on the study of properties of the lower central chain of Novikov algebras and bicommutative algebras that are of finite class.

For associative algebras that are of finite class, Jennings
[
14
]
showed nice connections between the central chains (
4.9
) and (
2.4
). In particular, in
[
14
, Theorems 3.3 and 3.4]
Jennings proved that
H_{p}A_{q}\subseteq A_{p+q-1}
and
H_{p}\circ A_{q}\subseteq A_{p+q}
for all integers
p,q\geq 1
, and the proof was based essentially on the associativity. In the following theorem (Theorem
2.5
), we provide an analogue of this result for Novikov algebras and bicommutative algebras with new techniques.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be either a Novikov algebra or a bicommutative algebra, and let
\mathcal{A}=A_{1}\supseteq A_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq A_{m}\supseteq\cdots
be a chain of ideals of
\mathcal{A}
satisfying
\mathcal{A}\circ A_{i}\subseteq A_{i+1}
for all
i\geq 1
.
Define a series of ideals inductively by the rule

H_{1}=\mathcal{A}
,
H_{i+1}=\mathcal{A}\circ H_{i}\mbox{ for }i\geq 1
.
Then we have
H_{p}\circ A_{q}\subseteq A_{p+q}
. In particular, we have
H_{p}\circ H_{q}\subseteq H_{p+q}
.

Proof.

Obviously, for all
p,q\geq 1
, all
H_{p}
and
A_{q}
are Lie ideals of
\mathcal{A}
.
We use induction on
p
to prove the claim. For
p=1
, the claim immediately follows from the definitions of
H_{p}
and
A_{q}
. Assume that
p>1
. By Lemma
2.2
, we have

H_{p}\circ A_{q}=[H_{p},A_{q}]+\mathcal{A}[H_{p},A_{q}]

and

H_{p}=[H_{p-1},\mathcal{A}]+\mathcal{A}[H_{p-1},\mathcal{A}].

Since
A_{p+q}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{A}
, it suffices to show
[h,a]\in A_{p+q}
for all
h\in H_{p}
,
a\in A_{q}
.
We need to consider the following cases.
Case 1: If
h=[h_{p-1},x]
for some elements
h_{p-1}\in H_{p-1}
and
x\in\mathcal{A}
, then by induction hypothesis, we have

[h,a]=[[h_{p-1},x],a]=[[h_{p-1},a],x]+[h_{p-1},[x,a]]\\
\in[A_{p+q-1},\mathcal{A}]+[H_{p-1},A_{q+1}]\subseteq A_{p+q-1}\circ\mathcal{A}+H_{p-1}\circ A_{q+1}\subseteq A_{p+q}.

Case 2: If
h=y[h_{p-1},x]
for some elements
h_{p-1}\in H_{p-1}
and
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
, then we denote
[h_{p-1},x]
by
h^{\prime}
. Thus we have
h^{\prime}\in H_{p}
and
h=yh^{\prime}
. By Case 1, for all
a\in A_{q}
, we have
[h^{\prime},a]\in A_{p+q}
. In particular, we have

[h^{\prime},a]y,\ y[h^{\prime},a],\ [h^{\prime},ya]\in A_{p+q}
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a Novikov algebra, then by induction hypothesis, we obtain

\displaystyle[h,a]=

\displaystyle(yh^{\prime})a-a(yh^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ya)h^{\prime}-(ay)h^{\prime}-y(ah^{\prime})+(ya)h^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ya)h^{\prime}-h^{\prime}(ay)+[h^{\prime},ay]-y(h^{\prime}a)+y[h^{\prime},a]+h^{\prime}(ya)-[h^{\prime},ya]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ya)h^{\prime}-h^{\prime}(ay)-y(h^{\prime}a)+(h^{\prime}y)a+y(h^{\prime}a)-(yh^{\prime})a+[h^{\prime},ay]+y[h^{\prime},a]-[h^{\prime},ya]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-h^{\prime}(ay)+(h^{\prime}y)a+[h^{\prime},ay]+y[h^{\prime},a]-[h^{\prime},ya]\ (\mbox{by right commutativity})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-h^{\prime}(ay)+(h^{\prime}a)y+[h^{\prime},ay]+y[h^{\prime},a]-[h^{\prime},ya]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-h^{\prime}(ay)+(ah^{\prime})y+[h^{\prime},a]y+[h^{\prime},ay]+y[h^{\prime},a]-[h^{\prime},ya]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-h^{\prime}(ay)+(ay)h^{\prime}+[h^{\prime},a]y+[h^{\prime},ay]+y[h^{\prime},a]-[h^{\prime},ya]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[h^{\prime},a]y+y[h^{\prime},a]-[h^{\prime},ya]\in A_{p+q}.

Therefore, we have
H_{p}\circ A_{q}\subseteq A_{p+q}
, thus finishing the proof for the case when
\mathcal{A}
is a Novikov algebra.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a bicommutative algebra, then by induction hypothesis and by the above reasoning, we obtain

\displaystyle[h,a]=

\displaystyle(yh^{\prime})a-a(yh^{\prime})=(yh^{\prime})a-h^{\prime}(ya)+h^{\prime}(ya)-a(yh^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ya)h^{\prime}-h^{\prime}(ya)+y(h^{\prime}a)-y(ah^{\prime})=[ya,h^{\prime}]+y[h^{\prime},a]\in A_{p+q}.

Therefore, we have
H_{p}\circ A_{q}\subseteq A_{p+q}
, thus finishing the proof.
∎

Before going further, we shall show that both Novikov algebras and bicommutative algebras are two-sided Alia
[
7
]
. Note that both (left) Novikov algebras and bicommutative algebras are right commutative and Lie-admissible, the following lemma can be viewed as a corollary of
[
7
, Proposition 6.1]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
7
, Proposition 6.1]

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a right commutative Lie-admissible algebra. Then
\mathcal{A}
is a two-sided Alia algebra, more precisely, we have
[x,y]z+[y,z]x+[z,x]y=0
and
x[y,z]+y[z,x]+z[x,y]=0
for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{A}
.
In particular, Novikov algebras and bicommutative algebras are two-sided Alia.

Proof.

By right commutativity, we have

\displaystyle[x,y]z+[y,z]x+[z,x]y=

\displaystyle(xy)z-(yx)z+(yz)x-(zy)x+(zx)y-(xz)y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(xy)z-(xz)y+(yz)x-(yx)z+(zx)y-(zy)x=0.

Moreover, since
\mathcal{A}
is Lie-admissible, we have

[x,y]z-z[x,y]+[y,z]x-x[y,z]+[z,x]y-y[z,x]=[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]=0.

Therefore, we obtain that

x[y,z]+y[z,x]+z[x,y]=[x,y]z+[y,z]x+[z,x]y=0,

thus finishing the proof.
∎

Recall that for an arbitrary subspace
V
of a Lie-admissible algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we define
V^{1}=V
and
V^{n}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n-1}V^{i}V^{n-i}
for all
n\geq 2
. Then
V
is called
nilpotent
if
V^{n}=(0)
for some positive integer
n
.

We are now in position to provide the main result of this subsection, which shows that if
\mathcal{A}
is a Novikov algebra (or bicommutative algebra) that is of finite class, then the commutator ideal
\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}
is nilpotent.

Theorem 2.5
.

Retain the hypotheses and notations as Lemma
2.3
, we have
H_{p}H_{q}\subseteq H_{p+q-1}
for all positive integers
p
and
q
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{A}
is of finite class, then
\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}
is nilpotent of nilpotent index less or equal to the class of
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

We first use induction on
\min\{p,q\}
to prove
H_{p}H_{q}\subseteq H_{p+q-1}
. If
\min\{p,q\}=1
, then it is clear because
H_{p}
and
H_{q}
are ideals of
\mathcal{A}
.
Assume that
p,q\geq 2
. By Lemma
2.3
, for all
h_{p}\in H_{p}
and
h_{q}\in H_{q}
, we have

h_{p}h_{q}-h_{q}h_{p}=[h_{p},h_{q}]\subseteq H_{p}\circ H_{q}\subseteq H_{p+q}\subseteq H_{p+q-1}.

In other words,
h_{p}h_{q}
lies in
H_{p+q-1}
if and only if so does
h_{q}h_{p}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
p\leq q
.
If
h_{p}=x_{2}[h_{p-1},x_{1}]
for some
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
and
h_{p-1}\in H_{p-1}
, then we have

h_{p}h_{q}=(x_{2}[h_{p-1},x_{1}])h_{q}=(x_{2}h_{q})[h_{p-1},x_{1}]=h_{q}^{\prime}[h_{p-1},x_{1}]

for
h_{q}^{\prime}=x_{2}h_{q}\in H_{q}
. So
h_{p}h_{q}
lies in
H_{p+q-1}
if and only if so does
h_{q}^{\prime}[h_{p-1},x_{1}]
. By the above reasoning, it suffices to show
[h_{p-1},x_{1}]h_{q}^{\prime}\in H_{p+q-1}
.
So we may assume
h_{p}=[h_{p-1},x]
for some
x\in\mathcal{A}
and
h_{p-1}\in H_{p-1}
. By Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
and by induction hypothesis, we deduce

h_{p}h_{q}=[h_{p-1},x]h_{q}=-[x,h_{q}]h_{p-1}-[h_{q},h_{p-1}]x\in H_{q+1}H_{p-1}+H_{p-1+q}x\subseteq H_{p+q-1}.

For the second claim, since
\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}=H_{2}
, we shall use induction on
m
to show
(H_{2})^{m}\subseteq H_{m+1}
. For
m=1
, there is nothing to prove. Assume that
m\geq 2
. Then by induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle(H_{2})^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq m-1}(H_{2})^{i}(H_{2})^{m-i}\subseteq\sum_{1\leq i\leq m-1}H_{i+1}H_{m-i+1}

\displaystyle\subseteq\sum_{1\leq i\leq m-1}H_{i+1+m-i+1-1}=H_{m+1}.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is of finite class, we may assume
H_{m+1}=(0)
for some integer
m
, and so
H_{2}
is nilpotent of index not greater than
m
, thus finishing the proof.
∎

3.
Lie nilpotent Novikov and bicommutative algebras

In this section, based on Theorem
2.5
, we show that a Novikov algebra or bicommutative algebra
\mathcal{A}
is Lie nilpotent if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is of finite class (Theorem
3.4
). We also find a property of Novikov algebras or bicommutative algebras on the products of commutator ideals that does not hold in general for associative algebras (Theorem
3.3
).

We begin this section by recalling useful notions. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Lie-admissible algebra. We define

\mathcal{A}_{[1]}=\mathcal{A}\ \mbox{ and }\ \mathcal{A}_{[i+1]}=[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}_{[i]}]\text{ for all }i\geq 1.

We call
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})
the
i
th commutator ideal
of
\mathcal{A}
. And the algebra
\mathcal{A}
is called
Lie nilpotent
if
\mathcal{A}_{[i]}=(0)
for some integer
i
.

For every integer
i\geq 1
, the linear space
\mathcal{A}_{[i]}
is obviously a Lie ideal of
\mathcal{A}
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.2
, we immediately obtain the construction of
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})
when
\mathcal{A}
is a Novikov algebra or bicommutative algebra.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be either a Novikov algebra or a bicommutative algebra. Then we have
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})=\mathcal{A}_{[i]}+\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}_{[i]}
for all integer
i\geq 1
.

Lots of interests have been attracted by the subject of commutator ideals of associative algebras. Etingof, Kim and Ma
[
11
]
studied the quotient of a free algebra by its
i
-th commutator ideal, and studied the products of such commutator ideals. Kerchev
[
18
]
studied the filtration of a free algebra by its associative lower
central series. We refer to
[
3
,
6
]
and the references therein for a detailed history and overview of this direction. It is worth mentioning that there is also a series of interesting results on the solvability and nilpotency of Poisson algebras with respect to their Lie structures
[
20
,
24
,
25
]
. In light of these notes, it is natural and interesting to study the commutator ideals of Lie-admissible algebras. Let us begin with the following easy observation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a right commutative Lie-admissible algebra. Then we have
\mathcal{A}_{[2]}\mathcal{A}_{[i]}\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i+1]})
for all integer
i\geq 1
. In particular, for every Novikov algebra or bicommutative algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we have
\mathcal{A}_{[2]}\mathcal{A}_{[i]}\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i+1]})
for all integer
i\geq 1
.

Proof.

For all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
, for all
c_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{[i]}
, by Lemma
2.4
, we have

[a,b]c_{i}=-[b,c_{i}]a-[c_{i},a]b\in\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i+1]}),

thus finishing the proof.
∎

Recall that for an arbitrary Lie-admissible algebra, we have
H_{1}=\mathcal{A}
and
H_{i+1}=\mathcal{A}\circ H_{i}
for all
i\geq 1
. In the following theorem we show that
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})=H_{i}
if
\mathcal{A}
is either a Novikov algebra or a bicommutative algebra. In particular, Theorem
2.5
then describes the products of the commutator ideals of
\mathcal{A}
, which is in general not true for Lie nilpotent associative algebras.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be either a Novikov algebra or a bicommutative algebra. Define a series of ideals by the rule

H_{1}=\mathcal{A}
,
H_{i+1}=\mathcal{A}\circ H_{i}\mbox{ for all }i\geq 1
. Then we have
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})=H_{i}
for all integer
i\geq 1
. In particular, we have
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[p]})\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[q]})\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[p+q-1]})
for all integers
p,q\geq 1
.

Proof.

By induction on
i
, it is straightforward to show
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})\subseteq H_{i}
. So it suffices to prove that
H_{i}\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})
for all integer
i\geq 1
, and we shall use induction on
i
to prove this claim. For
i\leq 2
, it is clear. Assume that
i\geq 3
. By induction hypothesis and by Lemma
3.1
,
H_{i}
is generated by

[\mathcal{A},H_{i-1}]=[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}_{[i-1]}]+[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}_{[i-1]}]=\mathcal{A}_{[i]}+[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}_{[i-1]}].

Therefore, it suffices to show
[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}_{[i-1]}]\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a Novikov algebra, then for all
a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}
and
d_{i-2}\in\mathcal{A}_{[i-2]}
, by Lemma
3.2
, we deduce

\displaystyle[a,b[c,d_{i-2}]]=

\displaystyle a(b[c,d_{i-2}])-(b[c,d_{i-2}])a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a([b,[c,d_{i-2}]]+[c,d_{i-2}]b)-(ba)[c,d_{i-2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a[b,[c,d_{i-2}]]+a([c,d_{i-2}]b)-(ba)[c,d_{i-2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a[b,[c,d_{i-2}]]+(a[c,d_{i-2}])b+[c,d_{i-2}](ab)-([c,d_{i-2}]a)b-(ba)[c,d_{i-2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a[b,[c,d_{i-2}]]+(ab)[c,d_{i-2}]-(ba)[c,d_{i-2}]+[c,d_{i-2}](ab)-([c,d_{i-2}]a)b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a[b,[c,d_{i-2}]]+[a,b][c,d_{i-2}]+[c,d_{i-2}](ab)-(a[c,d_{i-2}])b+[a,[c,d_{i-2}]]b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a[b,[c,d_{i-2}]]+[a,b][c,d_{i-2}]+[c,d_{i-2}](ab)-(ab)[c,d_{i-2}]+[a,[c,d_{i-2}]]b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a[b,[c,d_{i-2}]]+[a,b][c,d_{i-2}]-[ab,[c,d_{i-2}]]+[a,[c,d_{i-2}]]b

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})+\mathcal{A}_{[2]}\mathcal{A}_{[i-1]}\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]}).

If
\mathcal{A}
is a bicommutative algebra, then for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
d_{i-1}\in\mathcal{A}_{[i-1]}
, we deduce

\displaystyle[a,bd_{i-1}]=

\displaystyle a(bd_{i-1})-(bd_{i-1})a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b(ad_{i-1})-(ba)d_{i-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b(ad_{i-1})-b(d_{i-1}a)+b(d_{i-1}a)-(ba)d_{i-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b[a,d_{i-1}]+d_{i-1}(ba)-(ba)d_{i-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b[a,d_{i-1}]-[ba,d_{i-1}]

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]}).

Therefore, we have
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})=H_{i}
for all integer
i\geq 1
.

Finally, by Theorem
2.5
, we have

\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[p]})\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[q]})=H_{p}H_{q}\subseteq H_{p+q-1}=\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[p+q-1]}),

thus finishing the proof.
∎

Consequently, we obtain the main theorem of this section, which shows that the notions of “finite class” and “Lie nilpotency” for Novikov algebras and bicommutative algebras are the same.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be either a Novikov algebra or a bicommutative algebra. Then
\mathcal{A}
is Lie nilpotent if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is of finite class. Consequently, the ideal of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
\{ab-ba\mid a,b\in\mathcal{A}\}
is nilpotent if
\mathcal{A}
is Lie nilpotent.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.3
, we have
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})=H_{i}
for all integer
i\geq 1
. It follows that
\mathcal{A}
is Lie nilpotent if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is of finite class. By Theorem
2.5
, the second claim follows, thus finishing the proof.
∎

Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
indicates that bicommutative algebras and Novikov algebras possess lots of close properties. We also note that over a field of characteristic
\neq 2
, a Novikov algebra
\mathcal{A}
is Lie-solvable if and only if the ideal of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
\{ab-ba\mid a,b\in\mathcal{A}\}
is right nilpotent
[
27
]
. But an analog does not hold for Lie-solvable bicommutative algebras in general. It is well-known that for every bicommutative algebra
\mathcal{B}
,
the subalgebra
\mathcal{B}^{2}=\{ab\mid a,b\in\mathcal{B}\}
is commutative and associative
[
8
]
. So
\mathcal{B}
is Lie-metabelian, namely, for all
a,b,c,d\in\mathcal{B}
, we have
[[a,b],[c,d]]=0
. However, it is easy to see that for the free bicommutative algebra
\mathcal{B}
generated by more than two elements, the ideal of
\mathcal{B}
generated by
\{ab-ba\mid a,b\in\mathcal{B}\}
is not right/left nilpotent.

We conclude this section with an example constructed by S. Pchelintsev, which shows that some conditions are missing in
[
14
, Theorem 6.6]
. More precisely,
it is claimed
[
14
, Theorem 6.6]
that an associative algebra is Lie nilpotent if and only if it is of finite class, but the following example shows that this theorem does not hold in general.

Example 3.5
.

(S. Pchelintsev)
Let
\mathcal{A}
be the Grassmann algebra
generated by the infinite set
\{e_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
over a field of characteristic not 2. Then
\mathcal{A}
is Lie nilpotent of index 3, namely,
[[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}],\mathcal{A}]=0
, but
\mathcal{A}
is not of finite class and
\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}
is not nilpotent.

Proof.

Note that the set
X:=\{e_{i_{1}}\dots e_{i_{n}}\mid i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n},n\geq 1\}
forms a linear basis of
\mathcal{A}
(if we assume that
\mathcal{A}
is non-unital). Moreover, elements in
X
of the form
e_{i_{1}}\dots e_{i_{2n}}
lies in the center of
\mathcal{A}
. It follows immediately that
[[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}],\mathcal{A}]=0
.

We claim that
e_{1}e_{2}...e_{2n}
lies in
H_{n+1}
, where
H_{n+1}
is defined in Definition
2.1
. For
n=1
, we have
[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{1}e_{2}-e_{2}e_{1}=2e_{1}e_{2}\in H_{2}
. Since the characteristic of the underlying field is not 2, it follows that
e_{1}e_{2}\in H_{2}
. By induction hypothesis,
e_{1}e_{2}...e_{2n}\in H_{n+1}
and thus
e_{1}e_{2}...e_{2n}e_{2n+1}\in H_{n+1}
. It follows that
[e_{1}e_{2}...e_{2n}e_{2n+1},e_{2n+2}]\in H_{n+2}
and thus
e_{1}e_{2}...e_{2n}e_{2n+1}e_{2n+2}\in H_{n+2}
. Finally, note that
e_{2i-1}e_{2i}=(1/2)[e_{2i-1},e_{2i}]\in\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}
and
e_{1}e_{2}...e_{2n}\neq 0
. The result follows.
∎

4.
Commutator ideals of assosymmetric algebras

In this section we mainly study assosymmetric algebras of finite class and study commutator ideals of assosymmetric algebras. We show that some of the properties for associative algebras also hold for assosymmetric algebras, namely, for such properties the associativity is not necessary and can be replaced by left symmetry and right symmetry.

4.1.
Assosymmetric algebras of finite class

The aim of this subsection is to study assosymmetric algbras of finite class. We shall show that assosymmetric algbras of finite class have properties similar to that of associative algebras. Since the associativity does not hold for assosymmetric algebras, new techniques are needed. Recall that for all
x,y,z
in an algebra
\mathcal{A}
, the associator
(x,y,z)
means
(xy)z-x(yz)
. So in every assosymmetric algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we have
(y,x,z)=(x,y,z)=(x,z,y)
for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{A}
.

From now on,
\mathcal{A}
always means an assosymmetric algebra over a field
F
.
It is proved in
[
19
]
that for all
x,y,z,w
in an assosymmetric algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we have

(4.1)

([x,y],z,w)=0\mbox{ if }\mathsf{char}(F)\neq 2,3.

By the same technique developed in
[
19
]
, we obtain some more identities as follows when
\mathsf{char}(F)=2
or
3
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
x,y,z,w
in an assosymmetric algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we have

(4.2)

(x,y,z)=-[x,y]z+x[y,z]+[xz,y];

(4.3)

([w,x],y,z)=[w,(x,y,z)]+[x,(w,y,z)]\mbox{ if }\mathsf{char}(F)=2;

(4.4)

[xy,z]=-[yz,x]-[zx,y]\mbox{ if }\mathsf{char}(F)=3;

Proof.

(i)
Proof of identity (
4.2
). Note that

\displaystyle(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=-(x,y,z)+(y,x,z)+(x,z,y)

\displaystyle=-(xy)z+x(yz)+(yx)z-y(xz)+(xz)y-x(zy)

\displaystyle=-(xy)z+(yx)z+x(yz)-x(zy)+(xz)y-y(xz)

\displaystyle=-[x,y]z+x[y,z]+[xz,y].

The proof of identity (
4.2
) is completed.

(ii)
Proof of identity (
4.3
). Following
[
19
]
, we define

f(w,x,y,z)=(wx,y,z)-x(w,y,z)-(x,y,z)w.

Then it is obvious that

(4.5)

f(w,x,y,z)=f(w,x,z,y).

We also note that
[
19
]
in any algebra we have

(4.6)

(wx,y,z)-(w,xy,z)+(w,x,yz)=w(x,y,z)+(w,x,y)z.

By identity (
4.6
), we deduce

\displaystyle f(w,x,y,z)+f(z,w,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(wx,y,z)-x(w,y,z)-(x,y,z)w+(zw,x,y)-w(z,x,y)-(w,x,y)z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(wx,y,z)-x(w,y,z)-(x,y,z)w+(zw,x,y)-(wx,y,z)+(w,xy,z)-(w,x,yz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-x(w,y,z)-(x,y,z)w+(zw,x,y)+(w,xy,z)-(w,x,yz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-x(w,y,z)-(x,y,z)w+(xy,z,w)-(x,yz,w)+(x,y,zw)=0.

Combining this with identity (
4.5
), we obtain

(4.7)

f(w,x,y,z)=-f(z,w,x,y)=f(y,z,w,x),

and thus

(4.8)

f(w,x,y,z)=f(y,z,w,x)=f(y,z,x,w)=f(x,w,y,z).

Therefore, if
\mathsf{char}(F)=2
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=2f(w,x,y,z)

\displaystyle=f(w,x,y,z)+f(x,w,y,z)

\displaystyle=(wx,y,z)+x(w,y,z)+(x,y,z)w+(xw,y,z)+w(x,y,z)+(w,y,z)x

\displaystyle=([w,x],y,z)+[w,(x,y,z)]+[x,(w,y,z)].

The proof of identity (
4.3
) is completed.

(iii)
Proof of identity (
4.4
). If
\mathsf{char}(F)=3
, then we have

\displaystyle[xy,z]+[yz,x]+[zx,y]

\displaystyle=(xy)z-z(xy)+(yz)x-x(yz)+(zx)y-y(zx)

\displaystyle=(x,y,z)+(y,z,x)+(z,x,y)=3(x,y,z)=0.

Identity (
4.4
) follows immediately.
∎

Now we begin to study associators involving Lie ideals of an assosymmetric algebra
\mathcal{A}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be Lie ideals of
\mathcal{A}
. Then the following statements are true:

(i)
For all
x\in B
,
y,z\in\mathcal{A}
,

(y,x,z)\in\mathcal{A}[B,\mathcal{A}]+[B,\mathcal{A}]
;
In particular,
(y,x,z)
is contained in the ideal of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
[B,\mathcal{A}]
;

(ii)

A\circ B=[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B]=[A,B]+[A,B]\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

(i)
By identity (
4.2
), we deduce

\displaystyle(y,x,z)

\displaystyle=-[y,x]z+y[x,z]+[yz,x]=-[[y,x],z]-z[y,x]+y[x,z]+[yz,x]

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{A}[B,\mathcal{A}]+[B,\mathcal{A}].

The proof is completed.

(ii)
Clearly,
[A,B]
is an Lie ideal of
\mathcal{A}
. It follows that

[A,B]\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{A}[A,B]+[[A,B],\mathcal{A}]\subseteq\mathcal{A}[A,B]+[A,B].

In particular,
[A,B]+\mathcal{A}[A,B]
is an ideal of
\mathcal{A}
if and only if so does
[A,B]+[A,B]\mathcal{A}
.

By
(i)
, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
,
a\in A
,
b\in B
, we have

(x,[a,b],y)\in[[A,B],\mathcal{A}]+\mathcal{A}[[A,B],\mathcal{A}]\subseteq\mathcal{A}[A,B]+[A,B].

It follows that

x(y[a,b])=(xy)[a,b]-(x,y,[a,b])=(xy)[a,b]-(x,[a,b],y)\in\mathcal{A}[A,B]+[A,B].

Therefore, we deduce

(x[a,b])y=(x,[a,b],y)+x([a,b]y)=(x,[a,b],y)+x(y[a,b])+x[[a,b],y]\in\mathcal{A}[A,B]+[A,B].

The proof is completed.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\{A_{p}\mid p\geq 1\}
be a family of ideals of
\mathcal{A}
such that
\mathcal{A}\circ A_{p}\subseteq A_{p+1}
for every
p\geq 1
. Then for all
a\in A_{p}
, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
(x,a,y)\in A_{p+1}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, we have

(x,a,y)\in[A_{p},\mathcal{A}]+\mathcal{A}[A_{p},\mathcal{A}]=\mathcal{A}\circ A_{p}\subseteq A_{p+1}.

The proof is completed.
∎

Let

(4.9)

\mathcal{A}=A_{1}\supseteq A_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq A_{m}\supseteq A_{m+1}=(0)

be a central chain of ideals of
\mathcal{A}
. And let
H_{i}

(i\geq 1)
be as in Definition
2.1
. When
\mathcal{A}
is assosymmetric, we have the following analogs as those for associative algebras. Again, as the associativity does not hold, new techniques are necessary.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an assosymmetric algebra. Then we have
H_{p}A_{q}\subseteq A_{p+q-1}
,
A_{q}H_{p}\subseteq A_{p+q-1}
,
[H_{p},A_{q}]\subseteq A_{p+q}
and
(H_{p},A_{q},\mathcal{A})\subseteq A_{p+q}
. In particular, we have
H_{p}H_{q}\subseteq H_{p+q-1}
.

Proof.

Since
H_{p}\subseteq A_{p}
,
A_{p+q}\subseteq A_{p+q-1}
and
A_{q}H_{p}\subseteq[A_{q},H_{p}]+H_{p}A_{q}
, it suffices to prove
H_{p}A_{q}\subseteq A_{p+q-1}
,
[H_{p},A_{q}]\subseteq A_{p+q}
and
(H_{p},A_{q},\mathcal{A})\subseteq A_{p+q}
.

We use induction on
p
to prove these claims. For
p=1
, we
have
H_{1}A_{q}\subseteq A_{q}
,
[H_{1},A_{q}]\subseteq A_{q+1}
and by Corollary
4.3
, we obtain

(H_{1},A_{q},\mathcal{A})\subseteq(\mathcal{A},A_{q},\mathcal{A})\subseteq\mathcal{A}\circ A_{q}\subseteq A_{q+1}.

Now we assume
p\geq 2
. If
h_{p}=[h_{p-1},x]
for some
h_{p-1}\in H_{p-1}
and
x\in\mathcal{A}
, then for every
a\in A_{q}
, by induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle h_{p}a

\displaystyle=[h_{p-1},x]a

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq3.1.1}}{=}-(h_{p-1},x,a)+h_{p-1}[x,a]+[h_{p-1}a,x]

\displaystyle\in(H_{p-1},A_{q},\mathcal{A})+H_{p-1}A_{q+1}+[H_{p-1}A_{q},\mathcal{A}]

\displaystyle\subseteq A_{p+q-1}.

By the Jacobi identity and induction hypothesis, we obtain

[h_{p},a]=[[h_{p-1},x],a]=[[h_{p-1},a],x]+[h_{p-1},[x,a]]\in[A_{p+q-1},\mathcal{A}]+[H_{p-1},A_{q+1}]\subseteq A_{p+q}.

We continue to show
([h_{p-1},x],a,w)\in A_{p+q}
for every
w\in\mathcal{A}
. There are several cases to discuss depending on the characteristic of the field. If
\mathsf{char}(F)\neq 2,3
, then by identity (
4.1
), we have
([h_{p-1},x],a,w)=0\in A_{p+q}
.
If
\mathsf{char}(F)=2
, then by identity (
4.3
) and by Corollary
4.3
, we have

([h_{p-1},x],a,w)=[h_{p-1},(x,a,w)]+[x,(h_{p-1},a,w)]\in[H_{p-1},A_{q+1}]+[\mathcal{A},A_{p+q-1}]\subseteq A_{p+q}.

If
\mathsf{char}(F)=3
, then by identities (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) and by the above reasoning, we have

\displaystyle([h_{p-1},x],a,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[[h_{p-1},x],a]w+[h_{p-1},x][a,w]+[[h_{p-1},x]w,a]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[[h_{p-1},x],a]w+[h_{p-1},x][a,w]-[wa,[h_{p-1},x]]-[a[h_{p-1},x],w]

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle A_{p+q}+[h_{p-1},x]A_{q+1}+[A_{p+q-1},\mathcal{A}]\subseteq A_{p+q}.

Now we prove for the case when
p\geq 2
and
h_{p}=[h_{p-1},x]y
for some elements
h_{p-1}\in H_{p-1}
and
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
. By the above reasoning and by the right-symmetric identity, we have

h_{p}a=([h_{p-1},x],y,a)+[h_{p-1},x](ya)=([h_{p-1},x],a,y)+[h_{p-1},x](ya)\in A_{p+q-1}.

By identity (
4.2
) and by the above reasoning, we obtain

\displaystyle[h_{p},a]=[[h_{p-1},x]y,a]

\displaystyle=([h_{p-1},x],a,y)+[[h_{p-1},x],a]y-[h_{p-1},x][a,y]

\displaystyle\in A_{p+q}+[h_{p-1},x]A_{q+1}\subseteq A_{p+q}.

Finally, by the above reasoning and by induction hypothesis, for every
w\in\mathcal{A}
, we deduce

\displaystyle([h_{p-1},x]y,a,w)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq3.1.1}}{=}-[[h_{p-1},x]y,a]w+([h_{p-1},x]y)[a,w]+[([h_{p-1},x]y)w,a]

\displaystyle\in[H_{p},A_{q}]\mathcal{A}+H_{p}A_{q+1}+[H_{p},A_{q}]\subseteq A_{p+q}.

The proof is completed.
∎

By Lemma
4.4
and by a similar reasoning as the proof for Theorem
2.5
, we immediately obtain the following description for assosymmetric algebras that generalizes the corresponding result of associative algebras.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an assosymmetric algebra of finite class. Then
\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}
is nilpotent of nilpotent index less or equal to the class of
\mathcal{A}
.

4.2.
Products of commutator ideals of assosymmetric algebras

The aim of this subsection is to study products of commutator ideals of an arbitrary assosymmetric algebra
\mathcal{A}
over a field
F
such that
\mathsf{char}(F)\neq 2,3
. We shall prove that
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[j]})\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i+j-1]})
if
i
is odd or
j
is odd, which generalizes the corresponding result
[
3
, Corollary 1.4]
for associative algebras.
The main idea of this subsection comes from
[
3
]
. But since the associativity does not hold, we need new techniques.

For all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
, we define

x\ast y=xy+yx\ \mbox{ and }\ [x,y]=xy-yx.

The main difference for the above mentioned result between associative algebras and assosymmetric algebras is the proof of the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an assosymmetric algebra over a field
F
such that
\mathsf{char}(F)\neq 2,3
. For every positive odd integer
j
, we have
[\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[j]}),\mathcal{A}]\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]}
. Moreover, we have
[\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[j]}),\mathcal{A}_{[i]}]\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{[i+j]}
.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]-[[x,z],y]
. So the second claim follows immediately from the first one.
We use induction on
j
to prove the lemma. For
j=1
, the claim follows immediately by the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{[2]}
and by the above reasoning if
i\geq 2
. Now we assume that
j
is an odd integer such that
j\geq 3
. For all
x,y,z,u,v\in\mathcal{A}
, it suffices to show
[x[y,[z,u]],v]\in\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]}
if
u\in\mathcal{A}_{[j-2]}
.
By assumption, we have
\mathsf{char}(F)\neq 2
, so we have

x[y,[z,u]]=(1/2)([x,[y,[z,u]]]+x\ast[y,[z,u]]).

So in order to show
[x[y,[z,u]],v]\in A_{[j+1]}
, it suffices to prove
[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]\in A_{[j+1]}
. The idea of the proof is to show that
[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]
is sort of skew symmetric. More precisely, we shall prove that, if one of
x,y,z,u,v
lies in
\mathcal{A}_{[j-2]}
then

[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]\equiv[x\ast[z,[u,y]],v]\equiv[x\ast[u,[y,z]],v]\mod A_{[j+1]}.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is an assosymmetric algebra, by identity (
4.1
), we have

(x,y,[z,u])=(x,[z,u],y)=([z,u],x,y)=0=(xy)[z,u]-x(y[z,u])=(x[z,u])y-x([z,u]y),

and thus

\displaystyle x\ast[y,[z,u]]+y\ast[x,[z,u]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(y[z,u]-[z,u]y)+(y[z,u]-[z,u]y)x+y(x[z,u]-[z,u]x)+(x[z,u]-[z,u]x)y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(xy)[z,u]-x([z,u]y)+y([z,u]x)-[z,u](yx)

\displaystyle+(yx)[z,u]-y([z,u]x)+x([z,u]y)-[z,u](xy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(xy+yx)[z,u]-[z,u](xy+yx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x\ast y,[z,u]].

Now we assume that one of
x,y,z,u,v
lies in
\mathcal{A}_{[j-2]}
.
Since
[\mathcal{A}_{[i]},\mathcal{A}_{[t]}]\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{[i+t]}
,
by induction hypothesis, we obtain that

[[x\ast y,[z,u]],v]
lies in
A_{[j+1]}
,
and thus we deduce

(4.10)

[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]\equiv-[y\ast[x,[z,u]],v]\mod A_{[j+1]}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]+[v\ast[y,[z,u]],x]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x[y,[z,u]]+[y,[z,u]]x)v-v(x[y,[z,u]]+[y,[z,u]]x)

\displaystyle+(v[y,[z,u]]+[y,[z,u]]v)x-x(v[y,[z,u]]+[y,[z,u]]v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x([y,[z,u]]v)+[y,[z,u]](xv)-(vx)[y,[z,u]]-v([y,[z,u]]x)

\displaystyle+v([y,[z,u]]x)+[y,[z,u]](vx)-(xv)[y,[z,u]]-x([y,[z,u]]v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[y,[z,u]](x\ast v)-(x\ast v)[y,[z,u]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[x\ast v,[y,[z,u]]].

Again, since one of
x,y,z,u,v
lies in
\mathcal{A}_{[j-2]}
, by induction hypothesis,
we easily obtain that
[x\ast v,[y,[z,u]]]
lies in
A_{[j+1]}
, and thus we deduce

(4.11)

[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]\equiv-[v\ast[y,[z,u]],x]\mod A_{[j+1]}.

On the other hand, by the Jacobi identity and by identity (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]

\displaystyle=-[x\ast[z,[u,y]],v]-[x\ast[u,[y,z]],v]

\displaystyle\equiv[z\ast[x,[u,y]],v]+[u\ast[x,[y,z]],v]\ \mod\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]};

Interchanging
x
and
y
in the above equation, we obtain

[y\ast[x,[z,u]],v]\equiv[z\ast[y,[u,x]],v]+[u\ast[y,[x,z]],v]\ \mod\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]}.

So by the above two Equations and by the Jacobi identity, we deduce

\displaystyle 2[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]-[y\ast[x,[z,u]],v]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle[z\ast[x,[u,y]],v]+[u\ast[x,[y,z]],v]-[z\ast[y,[u,x]],v]-[u\ast[y,[x,z]],v]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle[z\ast[u,[x,y]],v]-[u\ast[z,[x,y]],v]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2[z\ast[u,[x,y]],v]\ \mod\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]}.

Since
\mathsf{char}(F)\neq 2
, we obtain

(4.12)

[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]\equiv[z\ast[u,[x,y]],v]\mod\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]}.

Therefore, in the vector space
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]}
, we have

(4.13)

[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]\overset{\eqref{chan-12}}{\equiv}-[y\ast[x,[z,u]],v]\overset{\eqref{chan-15}}{\equiv}[v\ast[x,[z,u]],y]\overset{\eqref{chan-12}}{\equiv}-[x\ast[v,[z,u]],y],

(4.14)

[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]\overset{\eqref{chan-1234}}{\equiv}[z\ast[u,[x,y]],v]\overset{\eqref{chan-15}}{\equiv}-[v\ast[u,[x,y]],z]\overset{\eqref{chan-1234}}{\equiv}[x\ast[y,[v,u]],z],

and thus

(4.15)

[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]=-[x\ast[y,[u,z]],v]\overset{\eqref{chan-35}}{\equiv}[x\ast[y,[v,z]],u]\equiv-[x\ast[y,[z,v]],u].

Therefore, we deduce

\displaystyle[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{chan-25}}{\equiv}-[x\ast[v,[z,u]],y]\overset{\eqref{chan-45}}{\equiv}[x\ast[v,[z,y]],u]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{chan-25}}{\equiv}-[x\ast[u,[z,y]],v]\equiv[x\ast[u,[y,z]],v]\mod\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]}.

It follows that

[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]\equiv[x\ast[u,[y,z]],v]\equiv[x\ast[z,[u,y]],v]\mod\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]}.

Finally, since
\mathcal{A}
is Lie-admissible, we obtain

3[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]\equiv[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]+[x\ast[u,[y,z]],v]+[x\ast[z,[u,y]],v]\equiv 0\mod\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]}.

Since
\mathsf{char}(F)\neq 3
, we have
[x\ast[y,[z,u]],v]\in\mathcal{A}_{[j+1]}
. The proof is completed.
∎

We conclude the article with the main result of this subsection, which generalizes the corresponding property of associative algebras.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an assosymmetric algebra. Then we have
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[j]})\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i+j-1]})
if
i
or
j
is odd.

Proof.

If
i=1
or
j=1
, then clearly we have
\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[j]})\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i+j-1]})
.
Now we assume
i\geq 2
and
j\geq 2
. Then by Lemma
4.2
(ii)
and by identity (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]})\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[j]})

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{A}_{[i]}+\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}_{[i]})(\mathcal{A}_{[j]}+\mathcal{A}_{[j]}\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{[i]}\mathcal{A}_{[j]}+\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]}\mathcal{A}_{[j]})+(\mathcal{A}_{[i]}\mathcal{A}_{[j]})\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{A}_{[i]}\mathcal{A}_{[j]})\mathcal{A}.

So it suffices to show
\mathcal{A}_{[i]}\mathcal{A}_{[j]}\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i+j-1]})
if one of
i
and
j
is odd. Since

\mathcal{A}_{[i]}\mathcal{A}_{[j]}\subseteq[\mathcal{A}_{[i]},\mathcal{A}_{[j]}]+\mathcal{A}_{[j]}\mathcal{A}_{[i]}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{[i+j]}+\mathcal{A}_{[j]}\mathcal{A}_{[i]},

we may assume that
j
is odd and thus we may assume
j\geq 3
and
i\geq 2
. For all
x\in\mathcal{A}
,
y\in\mathcal{A}_{[i-1]}
and
z\in\mathcal{A}_{[j]}
, by identity (
4.1
) and by Lemma
4.6
, we have

\displaystyle[x,y]z

\displaystyle=(xy)z-(yx)z=x(yz)-y(xz)=x(yz)-x(zy)+x(zy)-y(xz)

\displaystyle=x(yz)-x(zy)+(xz)y-y(xz)=x[y,z]+[xz,y]

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}_{[i+j-1]}+[\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[j]}),\mathcal{A}_{[i-1]}]\subseteq\mathsf{Id}(\mathcal{A}_{[i+j-1]}).

The proof is completed.
∎

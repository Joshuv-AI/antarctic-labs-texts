Temperature effects for e − + e + → μ − + μ + \displaystyle e^{-}+e^{+}\rightarrow\mu^{-}+\mu^{+} scattering in very special relativity
A. F. Santos, Faqir C. Khanna

Abstract
The electron-positron scattering process is investigated in the context of very special relativity (VSR). This theory assumes that the true symmetry of nature is not the full Lorentz group, but some of its subgroups, such as the subgroups S ​ I ​ M ​ ( 2 ) \displaystyle SIM(2) and H ​ O ​ M ​ ( 2 ) \displaystyle HOM(2) . In this context, the cross-section for electron-positron scattering at finite temperature is calculated. The effects of temperature are introduced using the Thermo Field Dynamics (TFD) formalism. Our result shows that the cross-section is changed due to both effects, the VSR contributions and temperature effects. An estimated value for the VSR parameter using experimental data available in the literature is discussed.

Introduction

The structure of the standard model, as well as all fundamental physics, is based on Lorentz symmetry. However, the search for a new physics beyond the standard model leads to the possibility that Lorentz symmetry is not an absolute symmetry of nature, but only approximate, valid up to a certain limit of energy. Thus, in recent years, Lorentz invariance and its breakdown have been intensively explored. One motivation, among many others, for such an investigation is the quantization of gravity, the deepest puzzle of contemporary physics. Numerous attempts to construct a consistent theory of quantum gravity lead to the Lorentz violation, such as string theory, loop quantum gravity, non-commutative geometry, among others

As a consequence, a new physics with different degrees of freedom can be explored. In order to investigate these new possibilities, theories that can accommodate deviations from these symmetries, in the context of standard model particle physics or gravitational phenomena, have been constructed. An example is the standard model extension (SME)

QED in Very Special Relativity

In this section, the main characteristics of QED in VSR are presented. The
\displaystyle SIM(2)
VSR-invariant Lagrangian that describes the interaction between fermions and photons is given as

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}+\bar{\psi}\left(i\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}-m_{e}\right)\psi-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left(\tilde{\partial}_{\mu}A^{\mu}\right)^{2},

(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}=\tilde{\partial}_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\tilde{\partial}_{\nu}A_{\mu}

(2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+\frac{m^{2}}{2}\frac{n_{\mu}}{n\cdot\partial}.

(3)

Here,
\displaystyle n_{\mu}=(1,0,0,1)
is a light-like four-vector that represents the preferred null direction and the
\displaystyle m
parameter sets the scale for the VSR effects. It is to be noted that, using the wiggle derivative in the field strength the VSR-Maxwell equation leads to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\partial^{2}+m^{2}\right)A^{\nu}=0.

(4)

This implies that
\displaystyle A^{\nu}
is a massive field. Furthermore, it has been shown that the gauge symmetry associated with the VSR provides massive modes
as in the standard case, i.e., without changing the number of physical polarization states of the photon
Cheon
;
Rivelles
. It is important to note that, in this context, the photon mass coming from a term that is gauge invariant, unlike the case where a term of the type
\displaystyle m^{2}A^{\mu}A_{\mu}
is not gauge invariant.

In the VSR framework, the minimal coupling between fermions and photons is determined by a new gauge invariant covariant derivative that is given as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\psi=D_{\mu}\psi+\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{n\cdot D}n_{\mu}\psi,

(5)

where
\displaystyle D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-ieA_{\mu}
is the usual covariant derivative. The term
\displaystyle 1/(n\cdot D)
in the covariant derivative has a non-local character that leads to an infinite number of interactions in the coupling
\displaystyle e

Dunn

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal L}_{int}=\bar{\psi}{\cal V}^{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2})\psi A_{\mu},

(6)

where
\displaystyle{\cal V}^{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal V}^{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2})=-ie\left[\gamma^{\mu}+\frac{m^{2}}{2}\frac{\not{n}\,n^{\mu}}{(n\cdot p_{1})(n\cdot p_{2})}\right].

\displaystyle\displaystyle i\Delta^{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{\eta^{\mu\nu}}{\tilde{k}^{2}}

(8)

with
\displaystyle\tilde{k}^{2}=k^{2}-m^{2}

III
TFD formalism

Here a brief introduction to TFD formalism is presented. The main motivation for constructing the TFD formalism, which is a real time formalism of quantum field theory at finite temperature, is to obtain that
\displaystyle\langle A\rangle=\langle 0(\beta)|A|0(\beta)\rangle
, i.e., the thermal average of any operator A is equal to its temperature dependent vacuum expectation value, with
\displaystyle|0(\beta)\rangle
being a thermal vacuum. Such a development requires two essential elements: (i) the doubling the degrees of freedom in a Hilbert space and (ii) the Bogoliubov transformation.

The doubling is defined by the tilde (
∼
) conjugation rules, that associate each operator in
\displaystyle S
to two operators in
\displaystyle S_{T}
, where the expanded space is
\displaystyle S_{T}=S\otimes\tilde{S}
, with
\displaystyle S
being the standard Hilbert space and
\displaystyle\tilde{S}
the tilde (dual) space. The map between the tilde
\displaystyle\tilde{A_{i}}
and non-tilde
\displaystyle A_{i}
operators is defined by the following tilde (or dual) conjugation rules:

\displaystyle\displaystyle(A_{i}A_{j})^{\thicksim}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{A_{i}}\tilde{A_{j}},\quad(\tilde{A_{i}})^{\thicksim}=-\xi A_{i},

(9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle(A_{i}^{\dagger})^{\thicksim}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{A_{i}}^{\dagger},\quad(cA_{i}+A_{j})^{\thicksim}=c^{*}\tilde{A_{i}}+\tilde{A_{j}},

with
\displaystyle\xi=-1
for bosons and
\displaystyle\xi=+1
for fermions.

The other fundamental ingredient of the TFD formalism is the Bogoliubov transformation that introduces a rotation in the tilde and non-tilde variables, thus the effects of temperature are introduced. The Bogoliubov transformations for fermions, with
\displaystyle c_{p}^{\dagger}
and
\displaystyle c_{p}
being creation and annihilation operators respectively, are

\displaystyle\displaystyle c_{p}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{u}(\beta)c_{p}(\beta)+\mathsf{v}(\beta)\tilde{c}_{p}^{\dagger}(\beta),

(10)

\displaystyle\displaystyle c_{p}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{u}(\beta)c_{p}^{\dagger}(\beta)+\mathsf{v}(\beta)\tilde{c}_{p}(\beta),

(11)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{p}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{u}(\beta)\tilde{c}_{p}(\beta)-\mathsf{v}(\beta)c_{p}^{\dagger}(\beta),

(12)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{p}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{u}(\beta)\tilde{c}_{p}^{\dagger}(\beta)-\mathsf{v}(\beta)c_{p}(\beta),

(13)

with
\displaystyle\mathsf{u}(\beta)=\cos\theta(\beta)
and
\displaystyle\mathsf{v}(\beta)=\sin\theta(\beta)
. The anti-commutation relations for creation and annihilation operators are similar to those at zero temperature

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{c(k,\beta),c^{\dagger}(p,\beta)\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\delta^{3}(k-p),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{\tilde{c}(k,\beta),\tilde{c}^{\dagger}(p,\beta)\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\delta^{3}(k-p),

(14)

and other anti-commutation relations are null.

The Bogoliubov transformations for bosons are given as

\displaystyle\displaystyle a_{p}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{u}^{\prime}(\beta)a_{p}(\beta)+\mathsf{v}^{\prime}(\beta)\tilde{a}_{p}^{\dagger}(\beta),

(15)

\displaystyle\displaystyle a_{p}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{u}^{\prime}(\beta)a_{p}^{\dagger}(\beta)+\mathsf{v}^{\prime}(\beta)\tilde{a}_{p}(\beta),

(16)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{p}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{u}^{\prime}(\beta)\tilde{a}_{p}(\beta)+\mathsf{v}^{\prime}(\beta)a_{p}^{\dagger}(\beta),

(17)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{p}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{u}^{\prime}(\beta)\tilde{a}_{p}^{\dagger}(\beta)+\mathsf{v}^{\prime}(\beta)a_{p}(\beta),

(18)

where
\displaystyle a_{p}^{\dagger}
and
\displaystyle a_{p}
are creation and annihilation operators, respectively. Here,
\displaystyle\mathsf{u}^{\prime}(\beta)=\cosh\theta(\beta)
,
\displaystyle\mathsf{v}^{\prime}(\beta)=\sinh\theta(\beta)
and algebraic rules satisfy the relations

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left[a(k,\beta),a^{\dagger}(p,\beta)\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\delta^{3}(k-p),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left[\tilde{a}(k,\beta),\tilde{a}^{\dagger}(p,\beta)\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\delta^{3}(k-p),

(19)

and other commutation relations are null.

V
Conclusion

Studies with sufficiently high energies lead to the possibility that a small violation of the Lorentz symmetry may arise. Then, the search for theoretical and phenomenological aspects of the Lorentz violation has increased in recent years. Here, quantum electrodynamics in the VSR Lorentz violating framework is considered. The most interesting feature of the VSR is that the laws of physics are invariant under the (kinematical) subgroups of the Poincaré group, preserving the basic elements of special relativity. In this context, the cross-section for the electron-positron scattering at finite temperature is calculated. The effects of temperature are introduced using the TFD formalism. TFD is a thermal quantum field theory that is composed by two fundamental ingredients: the doubling the degrees of freedom in a Hilbert space and the Bogoliubov transformation. Our result shows that the VSR effects change the usual QED cross-section for this scattering process. However, it is important to note that the VSR effects have the same energy profile as the QED. In addition, the cross-section is affected by the finite temperature. Using experimental data at zero temperature, an estimate for the VSR parameter is obtained. But this binding limit is not significant, since this parameter is compared with the mass of the photon. On the other hand, this limit can be improved if the electron-positron scattering is carried out at high temperatures. Although the deviation predicted in this paper due to the VSR is of theoretical interest, any significant constraints on the parameters of the model will be improved from the experiments performed at non-zero temperature.

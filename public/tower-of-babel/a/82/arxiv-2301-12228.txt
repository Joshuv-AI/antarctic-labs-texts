Entanglement Coupled Cluster Theory: Exact Spin-Adaptation Preprint: APS/123-QED
Sarai Dery Folkestad, Bendik Støa Sannes, Henrik Koch

Abstract
We present a novel framework for spin-adapted coupled cluster theory. The approach exploits the entanglement of an open-shell molecule with electrons in a non-interacting bath. Together, the molecule and the bath form a closed-shell system, and electron correlation can be included using the standard spin-adapted closed-shell coupled cluster formalism. A projection operator, which enforces conditions on the electrons in the bath, is used to obtain the desired state of the molecule. This entanglement coupled cluster theory is outlined , and proof-of-concept calculations for doublet states are reported. The approach is further extendable to open-shell systems with other values of the total spin.



I
Introduction

The interpretation of numerous spectroscopic experiments requires an exact treatment of electron spin in order to achieve satisfactory accuracy.
As the non-relativistic molecular electronic Hamiltonian commutes with the square of the total spin (
\vec{S}^{2}
) and the projected spin (
{S}_{z}
), a common set of eigenfunctions exist for these operators.
Electronic structure methods that enforce the proper spin symmetries are called spin-adapted models.
Unfortunately, the formulation of accurate spin-adapted models for open-shell systems is complicated.
Consequently, the requirement that the wave function is an eigenfunction of
\vec{S}^{2}
is often relaxed. Examples are the unrestricted Hartree-Fock (UHF) and spin-unrestricted (spin-orbital) coupled cluster theory.

The complication with spin-unrestricted methods is the appearance of spin contamination.
For UHF, spin contamination appears in two different situations.
(
Krylov 2000
)
For open-shell systems, it arises from differences in the mean field experienced by the
\alpha
– and
\beta
–electrons. For closed-shell systems, it appears when static correlation becomes important, for instance, when bonds are stretched
or broken
.
(
Andrews et al. 1991
;
Krylov 2000
;
Helgaker, Jorgensen, and Olsen 2014
)

While spin contamination can be significant at low levels of theory, it is
significantly
reduced as the description of the electronic structure improves.
(
Stanton 1994
;
Krylov 2000
)

In the limit of full configuration interaction (FCI), there is no spin contamination.

Some molecular properties
are highly sensitive to the appearance of spin contamination and where
a wave function with the correct spin properties is desirable. For instance, this was demonstrated for the X-ray spectroscopy of the benzene cation
(
Vidal et al. 2020
)
, and in general
in the description of magnetic properties.
(
Jost and van
Wüllen 2013
)
Aside from the use of spin-adapted approaches,
the problem of spin contamination has motivated the development of methods where contamination is reduced or where the spin properties are imposed in an average way.
(
Rittby and Bartlett 1988
;
Knowles, Hampel, and Werner 1993
;
Knowles, Hampel, and Werner 2000
;
Neogrády, Urban, and Hubac̆ 1994
;
Szalay and Gauss 1997
;
Tsuchimochi and Scuseria 2011
;
Biktagirov, Schmidt, and Gerstmann 2020
)

Spin adaptation is trivial for closed-shell species. In Hartree-Fock theory, a single determinant with doubly occupied molecular orbitals is an eigenfunction of both
\vec{S}^{2}
and
{S}_{z}
. Post-Hartree-Fock methods can be parameterized in terms of singlet excitations of the closed-shell Hartree-Fock reference, thereby producing pure singlet approximate wave functions.
(
Helgaker, Jorgensen, and Olsen 2014
)
All singlet excitation operators with respect to the closed-shell reference determinant commute. In coupled cluster theory, this ensures that the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff (BCH) expansion of the similarity-transformed Hamiltonian truncates after
(
at most
)
four nested commutators with the cluster operator.

For open-shell systems, spin adaptation becomes more complicated. At the Hartree-Fock level, the restricted open-shell (ROHF) variant provides
a spin-adapted wave function
.
However, including dynamical correlation with coupled cluster theory in a spin-adapted manner has
turned out
to be a significant challenge.
As for closed-shell states,
a spin-adapted formulation of coupled cluster theory relies on
expressing
the cluster operator in terms of singlet excitation operators (also called unitary group generators).
For open-shell systems, this implies the inclusion of singlet excitation operators where electrons are both created and annihilated in the singly occupied (active) orbitals. Such excitation operators do not commute and the BCH expansion for the similarity-transformed Hamiltonian no longer truncates after four nested commutators.
Furthermore, the formulation of the cluster operator is not unique.
Li and Paldus 1994
;
Herrmann and Hanrath 2020

One of the first implementations of spin-adapted coupled cluster theory for high-spin open-shell systems was presented by Janssen and Schaefer.
(
Janssen and Schaefer 1991
)
Their choice of the cluster operator lacks terms necessary to span the full spin space for some spatial configurations.
(
Li and Paldus 1994
;
Herrmann and Hanrath 2020
)

A similar approach is the unitary group approach developed by Li, Paldus, Jeziorski, and Jankowski.
(
Li and Paldus 1994
;
Li and Paldus 1995a
;
Li and Paldus 1995b
;
Jeziorski, Paldus, and Jankowski 1995
;
Jankowski and Jeziorski 1999
)

They criticized the cluster operator used by Janssen and Schaefer, which generates a linearly dependent basis for the coupled cluster equations.
This leads to an ambiguity in the formulation of the theory and convergence problems.
Li and Paldus 1994
In the unitary group approach, the cluster operator is defined such that the corresponding basis for the cluster amplitude equations is linearly independent and orthonormal.

Recently, Herrmann and Hanrath
(
Herrmann and Hanrath 2020
;
Herrmann and Hanrath 2022
)
present the automatic generation of a set of excitation operators for spin-adapted open-shell coupled cluster theory. The resulting basis for the coupled cluster equations is linearly independent but non-orthogonal. They arrive at a cluster operator (see Ref.
21
for CCSD) that can be used for arbitrary high-spin open-shell systems. However, the operator (and its construction) is significantly more complicated than in the closed-shell theory, as is the case in the unitary group approach.

The spin-adapted open-shell coupled cluster methods described so far are complicated by non-commuting contributions to the cluster operator, and by the complexity of the operator itself.

Several authors
(
Nooijen 1996
;
Nooijen and Bartlett 1996
;
Nooijen and Lotrich 2001
;
Datta and Mukherjee 2008
;
Datta and Gauss 2013
;
Datta and Gauss 2015
)
have advocated the use of normal ordering of the exponential operator to circumvent the problems arising from non-commuting terms.
Nevertheless, the complexity of spin-adapted open-shell coupled cluster theory makes manual derivation and implementation impractical and automated generation of equations and code is seemingly inevitable.
(
Janssen and Schaefer 1991
;
Li and Paldus 1994
;
Nooijen and Lotrich 2001
;
Datta and Gauss 2013
;
Herrmann and Hanrath 2020
;
Herrmann and Hanrath 2022
)

In this paper, we explore a novel strategy to obtain a spin-adapted description
for open-shell systems
.
In entanglement coupled cluster theory,
orbitals of the molecular system are mixed with orbitals from a non-interacting electron bath. A closed-shell determinant is constructed in this mixed orbital basis and used as a reference for spin-adapted closed-shell coupled cluster theory.
The cluster operator is defined as in standard spin-adapted closed-shell theory. That is, in terms of singlet excitation operators which all commute with each other.
Hence, the BCH expansion of the similarity transformed Hamiltonian truncates after four nested commutators.

The desired state of the molecular system is obtained by applying a projection operator that enforces the spin properties of the non-interacting bath. Due to the coupling of the system and the bath—into a
singlet
state—this projection
also
imposes restrictions on the molecular system.

Compared to other approaches, the entanglement coupled cluster approach is simple in its formulation.

Still
, the projection operator introduces significant complexity
in
the working equations. The projection operator commutes with the Hamiltonian and the equations can be recast as a change of the projection manifold for the standard
closed-shell
coupled cluster equations. The entanglement coupled cluster equations have a non-unit metric,
i.e.,
the matrix elements of the projection operator in the basis defined by the cluster operator. This metric is rank-deficient, and linear dependencies in the basis must be removed.
However, this is straightforward.

In the following, we present the entanglement coupled cluster theory and its application to doublet states. We describe the ground state equations and the extension to equation-of-motion for excited states. We also outline how triplet systems can be described within the same framework. Finally, we present proof-of-concept calculations for the CCS and CCSD variants of the theory.

II.4
Spin-adapted closed-shell coupled cluster theory

In
coupled cluster theory, the wave function

\displaystyle|\mathrm{CC}\rangle=\exp(T)|\mathrm{R}\rangle,

(25)

where

In the spin-adapted closed-shell theory,

\displaystyle A_{pq}=a^{\dagger}_{p\alpha}a_{q\alpha}+a^{\dagger}_{p\beta}a_{q\beta}=A_{pq}^{\alpha}+A_{pq}^{\beta}.

Entanglement coupled cluster theory

\displaystyle|\mathrm{ECC}\rangle=\mathcal{P}\exp(T)|\mathrm{R}\rangle.

(42)

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
6
) and the projection operators
\mathcal{P}
in Eqs. (
20
)–(
23
) are defined in terms of the original creation and annihilation operators
c^{\dagger}_{p\sigma}
and
c_{p\sigma}
.
Before solving the coupled cluster equations, we must transform
H
and
\mathcal{P}
to the mixed orbital basis. For the Hamiltonian, this amounts to a transformation of the one- and two-electron integrals,

\displaystyle H=\sum_{pq}\tilde{h}_{pq}A_{pq}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{pqrs}\tilde{g}_{pqrs}(A_{pq}A_{rs}-\delta_{qr}A_{ps}),

(43)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{pq}

\displaystyle=\sum_{tu}U_{pt}{h}_{tu}U_{qu}

(44)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{pqrs}

\displaystyle=\sum_{tuvw}U_{pt}U_{qu}{g}_{tuvw}U_{rv}U_{sw}.

(45)

For the projection operators
P^{\sigma}
in Eq. (
18
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}P^{\sigma}=&\phantom{-}\sin^{2}\theta A_{II}^{\sigma}+\cos^{2}\theta A_{AA}^{\sigma}\\
&-\cos\theta\sin\theta(A^{\sigma}_{IA}+A^{\sigma}_{AI}).\end{split}

(46)

By pre-multiplying the Schrödinger equation with
\exp(-T)\mathcal{P}
and projecting onto the vectors
\{\langle\mathrm{R}|,\langle\mu|\}
, we obtain the equations for the ground state energy and amplitudes
:

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{R}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}\bar{H}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{R}\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{0}\langle\mathrm{R}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{R}\rangle

(47)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu}

\displaystyle={\Omega}^{S}_{\mu}E_{0},

(48)

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathrm{\mu}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}\bar{H}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{R}\rangle

(49)

\displaystyle\Omega^{S}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\langle\mu\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{R}\rangle.

(50)

Here, we have
used
the resolution of the identity
\exp(-T)\exp(T)=1
,
introduced

\bar{\mathcal{P}}=\exp(-T)\mathcal{P}\exp(T)
, and
used

[H,\mathcal{P}]=0
and
\mathcal{P}^{2}=\mathcal{P}
.
Compared to standard coupled cluster theory, a non-unit metric enters the right-hand sides of Eqs. (
47
) and (
48
).
The equations can be viewed as a change of the projection manifold in the standard theory to

\{\langle\mathrm{R}|\bar{\mathcal{P}},\langle\mu|\bar{\mathcal{P}}\}
.

To derive the working equations, we evaluate
\bar{H}|\mathrm{R}\rangle
and the projection onto
\{\langle\mathrm{R}|\bar{\mathcal{P}},\langle\mu|\bar{\mathcal{P}}\}
. Due to the definition of
T
, the BCH expansion of
\bar{H}
truncates after four nested commutators in general. With a
T_{1}
-transformed
H
, maximally three nested commutators with
T_{2}
enter the ECCSD equations, since
\langle\mu_{2}|\bar{\mathcal{P}}
contains quadruply excited determinant
s

\langle\mu_{2}|E_{IA}^{\beta}E_{IA}^{\alpha}
.

The basis
\{\langle\mu|\bar{\mathcal{P}}\}
can have redundancies that must be removed to solve the ECC equations uniquely. We have found these redundancies by diagonalizing the matrix

\displaystyle{J}^{S}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\langle\mu\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\nu\rangle

(51)

and analyzing the null space. There are two cases:

1.

\langle\mu|\bar{\mathcal{P}}=0
, in which case
\langle\mu|
is removed from the projection space and
\tau_{\mu}
is removed from
T
.

2.

The v
ectors
\{\langle\mu|\bar{\mathcal{P}}\}
are linearly dependent, in which case we remove the appropriate number of vectors and corresponding excitation operators, preferring to remove those of higher excitation order.

The overall scaling of solving the ECCSD ground state equations, Eq. (
47
), is
\mathcal{O}(N^{6})
, because any contributions to the vectors
\{\langle\mathrm{R}|\bar{\mathcal{P}},\langle\mu|\bar{\mathcal{P}}\}
from excited determinants of
excitation
order three and four have restricted indices (see Appendix
C
). These higher order determinants result in additional costs compared to CCSD, but not higher scaling. All contributions from singly and doubly excited determinants can be implemented at the same cost as standard CCSD (with some extra
\mathcal{O}(N^{4})
operations).

The ECC excited states are obtained within the EOM framework. We obtain the generalized eigenvalue equations

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{H}R}^{k}=E_{k}\bm{\bar{{S}}R}^{k}

(52)

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{L}^{k}=E_{k}\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{T}\bm{L}^{k},

(53)

where

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{H}}=\begin{pmatrix}\langle\mathrm{R}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}\bar{H}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{R}\rangle&\bm{{\eta}}^{\mathrm{T}}\\
\bm{{\Omega}}&\bm{J}\end{pmatrix},

(54)

and

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{{S}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\langle\mathrm{R}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{R}\rangle&{\bm{\eta^{S}}}^{\mathrm{T}}\\
\bm{\Omega^{S}}&\bm{{J^{S}}}\end{pmatrix},

(55)

and where we have introduced

\displaystyle\begin{split}\eta_{\nu}&=\langle\mathrm{R}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}\bar{H}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\nu\rangle\\
J_{\mu\nu}&=\langle\mu\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}\bar{H}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\nu\rangle\\
{\eta}^{S}_{\nu}&=\langle\mathrm{R}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{\nu}\rangle.\end{split}

(56)

The lowest generalized eigenvalue is the ground state energy and the remaining eigenvalues are excited state energies. The corresponding right and left eigenvectors have the same form as in standard closed-shell EOM theory, see Eqs. (
39
) and (
40
). However, the
\bm{\bar{t}}
-equation is now given by

\displaystyle(\bm{J}-E_{0}{\bm{J^{S}}})^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\bar{t}}=E_{0}{\bm{\eta^{S}}}-\bm{\eta}.

(57)

II.6
Size-extensivity of excited states

In this section, we will analyze the scaling properties of the EOM entanglement coupled cluster energies.
We consider two non-interacting systems,
A
and
B
.
System
A
is open-shell and system
B
is a singlet.

Since the systems
A
and
B
do not interact, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}T&=T_{A}+T_{B}\\
H&=H_{A}+H_{B},\end{split}

(58)

and since only system
A
is open-shell, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{A}.

(59)

We have the following commutator relations between the operators of systems
A
and
B
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&[T_{A},T_{B}]=0\\
&[H_{A},T_{B}]=[H_{B},T_{A}]=0\\
&[\mathcal{P}_{A},T_{B}]=0.\end{split}

(60)

The size-extensivity of the ground state follows directly from these commutator relations, as in the standard theory (see Ref.
3
).

Using the properties of the operators (Eqs. (
58
) and (
59
)) and the commutator relations (Eq. (
60
)), we obtain the block structure

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\bar{H}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\bar{H}}_{1}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{2}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{3}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{H}_{0,0}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{0,A}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{0,B}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{0,AB}\\
\bm{\bar{H}}_{A,0}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{A,A}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{A,B}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{A,AB}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{B,B}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{B,AB}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{AB,B}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{AB,AB}\\
\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(61)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\bar{{S}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{1}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{3}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{{S}}_{0,0}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{0,A}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{A,0}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{A,A}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{B,B}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{B,AB}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{AB,B}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{AB,AB}\\
\end{pmatrix},\\
\end{split}

(62)

in the basis

|\mu_{A},\mu_{B}\rangle
=
|\mu_{A}\rangle\otimes|\mu_{B}\rangle
, for
\mu_{X}\geq 0
.

The
subscript
0
denotes the reference determinant for both systems
A
and
B
. Subscripts
A
,
B
, or
AB
denote excited determinants in system
A
,
B
, or both.
The characteristic equation for the generalized eigenvalue equation becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\det\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\bar{H}}_{1}-E\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{1}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{2}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{3}-E\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{3}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\det(\bm{\bar{H}}_{1}-E\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{1})\det(\bm{\bar{H}}_{3}-E\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{3})=0.\end{split}

(63)

Thus, the generalized eigenvalues of
\{\bm{\bar{H}},\bm{\bar{{S}}}\}
are the collected generalized eigenvalues of
\{\bm{\bar{H}}_{1},\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{1}\}
and
\{\bm{\bar{H}}_{3},\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{3}\}
.
We start by considering
\bm{\bar{H}}_{1}
and
\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{1}
. We have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\bar{H}}_{1}&=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{H}_{0,0}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{0,A}\\
\bm{\bar{H}}_{A,0}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{A,A}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{{S}}^{A}_{0}E_{A}&\bm{\bar{H}}^{A}_{0,A}\\
\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,0}E_{A}&\bm{\bar{H}}^{A}_{A,A}\end{pmatrix}+E_{B}\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{1},\end{split}

(64)

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{{S}}^{A}_{0,0}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{0,A}\\
\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,0}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,A}\end{pmatrix}=\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{1},

(65)

where we have used the relations

\displaystyle\bar{H}_{0,0}=

\displaystyle\;\bar{{S}}^{A}_{0,0}E_{A}+\bar{{S}}^{A}_{0,0}E_{B},

(66)

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{H}}_{A,0}=

\displaystyle\;\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,0}E_{A}+\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,0}E_{B},

(67)

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{H}}_{0,A}=

\displaystyle\;\bm{\bar{H}}^{A}_{0,A}+\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{0,A}E_{B},

(68)

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{H}}_{A,A}=

\displaystyle\;\bm{\bar{H}}^{A}_{A,A}+\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,A}E_{B}.

(69)

derived in Appendix
A
. The superscript
A
denotes that the matrix element only refers to quantities of system
A
; e.g.,
\bar{{S}}^{A}_{0,0}=\langle\mathrm{R}_{A}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{A}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{R}_{A}\rangle
.
We may recast the generalized eigenvalue equation of
\{\bm{\bar{H}}_{1},\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{1}\}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\det\Bigg(\begin{pmatrix}\bar{{S}}^{A}_{0}E_{A}&\bm{\bar{H}}^{A}_{0,A}\\
\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,0}E_{A}&\bm{\bar{H}}^{A}_{A,A}\end{pmatrix}-(E-E_{B})\begin{pmatrix}\bar{{S}}^{A}_{0,0}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{0,A}\\
\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,0}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,A}\end{pmatrix}\Bigg)=0.\end{split}

(70)

This is equivalent to the EOM-ECC equations for system
A
, and hence, the eigenvalues
E_{A}+\omega_{A}=E-E_{B}
are the energies of system
A
. The total energy is
E=E_{A}+E_{B}+\omega_{A}
. Therefore, we can conclude that the spectrum of
\bm{\bar{H}}_{1}
contains ground state energy and the excited state energies corresponding to an excitation in system
A
.

We now proceed with
\bm{\bar{H}}_{3}
and
\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{3}
, and we will show that the excited state energies of system
B
can be found in this generalized eigenvalue problem.
In a separate calculation on system
B
, we identify
\bm{r}^{B}
as a right eigenvector of the Jacobian matrix with eigenvalue
\omega_{B}
:

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{B,B}\bm{r}^{B}=\omega_{B}\bm{r}^{B}.

(71)

We now want to demonstrate that the vector

\displaystyle\bm{X}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{r}^{B}\\
\bm{0}\end{pmatrix}

(72)

is a generalized eigenvector of
\{\bm{\bar{H}}_{3},\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{3}\}
. We have

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{H}}_{3}\bm{X}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\bar{H}}_{B,B}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{B,AB}\\
\bm{\bar{H}}_{AB,B}&\bm{\bar{H}}_{AB,AB}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{r}^{B}\\
\bm{0}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\bar{H}}_{B,B}\bm{r}^{B}\\
\bm{\bar{H}}_{AB,B}\bm{r}^{B}\end{pmatrix}=(E_{A}+E_{B}+\omega_{B})\begin{pmatrix}\bar{{S}}^{A}_{0,0}\bm{r}^{B}\\
\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,0}\otimes\bm{r}^{B}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(73)

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{3}\bm{X}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{B,B}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{B,AB}\\
\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{AB,B}&\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{AB,AB}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{r}^{B}\\
\bm{0}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{B,B}\bm{r}^{B}\\
\bm{\bar{{S}}}_{AB,B}\bm{r}^{B}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{{S}}^{A}_{0,0}\bm{r}^{B}\\
\bm{\bar{{S}}}^{A}_{A,0}\otimes\bm{r}^{B}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(74)

where a detailed derivation is given in Appendix
A
.
Hence, we have
\bm{\bar{H}_{3}}\bm{X}=(E_{A}+E_{B}+\omega_{B})\bm{\bar{{S}}_{3}}\bm{X}
, and the generalized eigenvalues correspond to the energy of an excited state in system
B
(with excitation energy
\omega_{B}
) and the ground state of system
A
.
With this, we conclude that the EOM-ECC energies are size-extensive.

III
Entanglement coupled cluster theory for doublet and singlet systems

III.1
Doublet states

To determine doublet systems in ECC, we can use either
\mathcal{P}=P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}
or
\mathcal{P}=P^{\alpha}Q^{\beta}
in the procedure outlined in Section
II.5
to obtain
S_{\mathrm{m}z}=\pm\frac{1}{2}
for the molecule.
We choose
S_{\mathrm{m}z}=\frac{1}{2}
, and therefore use the projection
\mathcal{P}=P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}
.
Upon application of the projector to the coupled cluster state, some parameters are redundant and must be eliminated. In Table
1
, we list the parameters that are explicitly removed.

In an ECC calculation for a doublet system of
N_{\mathrm{m}}
electrons, we can use the ROHF orbitals of the target
N_{\mathrm{m}}
-electron system or the RHF orbitals from an
(N_{\mathrm{m}}+1)
-electron calculation.
If we use ROHF orbitals, the singly occupied orbital is taken to be active and is mixed with the bath orbital. If, on the other hand, we use RHF orbitals, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) is chosen to be active.

For the projected spin of the bath, we can show that

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{b}z}P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2}P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha},

(75)

and it follows that

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{b}z}P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}|\Psi\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}|\Psi\rangle,

(76)

as long as
P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}|\Psi\rangle\neq 0
. For the spin projection of the molecule, we have (see Appendix
B
for a detailed derivation)

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{m}z}P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}|\Psi\rangle=-S_{\mathrm{b}z}P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}|\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{2}P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}|\Psi\rangle.

(77)

For the total spin, we use the relation

\displaystyle\vec{S}^{2}_{\mathrm{b}}=S_{\mathrm{b}z}(S_{\mathrm{b}z}-1)+S_{\mathrm{b}+}S_{\mathrm{b}-}

(78)

and one may show that

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{b}+}S_{\mathrm{b}-}P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}=0,

(79)

which implies for the squared spin

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vec{S}^{2}_{\mathrm{b}}P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}|\Psi\rangle&=S_{\mathrm{b}z}(S_{\mathrm{b}z}-1)P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}|\Psi\rangle\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2}-1\Bigr)P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}|\Psi\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}+1\Bigr)P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}|\Psi\rangle.\end{split}

(80)

With this, we have established that
S_{\mathrm{b}}=\frac{1}{2}
. From the addition theorem (the Clebsch-Gordan series), we conclude that
S_{\mathrm{m}}=\frac{1}{2}
, such that
|S_{\mathrm{m}}-S_{\mathrm{b}}|=0
.
The state
\mathcal{P}\exp(T)|\mathrm{R}\rangle=P^{\beta}Q^{\alpha}\exp(T)|\mathrm{R}\rangle
is, therefore, a spin-pure state for the molecular doublet system.

IV
Extension to triplet states

An extension to higher values of
S_{\mathrm{m}}
with the ECC approach results in a significant increase in complexity of the working equations. An electron bath with more than a single orbital necessitates the use of automated generation of equations and code. In this section, we outline the procedure to obtain triplet states within the ECC framework.

Considering
two active orbitals and two bath orbitals, we can choose to mix the active and bath orbitals in the following way:

\displaystyle\psi_{I}

\displaystyle=\phi_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\cos\theta-\phi_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\sin\theta,

(81)

\displaystyle\psi_{A}

\displaystyle=\phi_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\sin\theta+\phi_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\cos\theta,

(82)

\displaystyle\psi_{J}

\displaystyle=\phi_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\cos\theta-\phi_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}\sin\theta,

(83)

\displaystyle\psi_{B}

\displaystyle=\phi_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\sin\theta+\phi_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}\cos\theta,

(84)

where we have chosen to use a single mixing angle
\theta
.

A minimal example of a triplet system has two orbitals. In this case, the corresponding mixed-orbital reference becomes

|\mathrm{R}\rangle={a}^{\dagger}_{I\alpha}{a}^{\dagger}_{I\beta}{a}^{\dagger}_{J\alpha}{a}^{\dagger}_{J\beta}|\text{vac}\rangle.

(85)

This state can be expressed
in the original MO basis. There are 16 resulting terms
. However,
if we only consider those with two electrons in the
molecule
(and consequently two electrons in the bath)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\mathrm{R}\rangle=C&\Big(c^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{B}_{2}\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{B}_{2}\beta}+c^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{B}_{1}\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{B}_{1}\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{A}_{2}\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{A}_{2}\beta}\\

VI
Summary and concluding remarks

In this paper, we have introduced the entanglement coupled cluster
(ECC)
approach for a spin-adapted treatment of open-shell systems.
We have demonstrated how this approach can be used to obtain ground and excited state energies and that the energies are size-extensive.

In the ECC approach, the orbitals of the system of interest are mixed with the orbitals of a non-interacting bath. A closed-shell reference is constructed in the mixed orbital basis and is used in the exponential parametrization of coupled cluster theory. Since the reference determinant is closed-shell, the closed-shell spin-adapted formulation of coupled cluster theory can be used. The complications of defining a spin-free operator for an open-shell reference are avoided and the BCH expansion of the similarity-transformed Hamiltonian truncates after only four nested commutators with the cluster operator.
In order to obtain the actual system of interest, a projection operator is applied to the coupled cluster state.

A pilot implementation is presented for the ECCSD doublet ground state, in addition to the ground and excited states with ECCS. Proof-of-concept calculations demonstrate that the error of the ECCSD energy is comparable to that of spin-adapted closed-shell CCSD. For OH and OH
-
, we have demonstrated that this holds at all bond lengths. ECCS energies equal the ROHF energies, when ROHF orbitals are used, and for the excited states, ECCS yields Tamm-Dancoff TD-ROHF energies, analogous to the relation between CCS and RHF.

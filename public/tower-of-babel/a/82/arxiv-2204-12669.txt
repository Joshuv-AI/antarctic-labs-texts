On Local Energy decay for solution of the Benjamin-Ono equation
Ricardo Freire, Felipe Linares, Claudio Muñoz, Gustavo Ponce

Abstract
. We consider the long time dynamics of large solutions to the Benjamin-Ono equation. Using virial techniques, we describe regions of space where every solution in a suitable Sobolev space must decay to zero along sequences of times. Moreover, in the case of exterior regions, we prove complete decay for any sequence of times. The remaining regions not treated here are essentially the strong dispersion and soliton regions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 37K15, 35Q53. Secondary: 35Q51, 37K10

Resumé
. On considère la dynamique en temps longs de l’équation de Benjamin-Ono. En utilisant des techniques du viriel, on décrit les régions du space où toute solution doît décroire vers zéro dans une espace de Sobolev bien choisi, au moins sûr une suite de temps. Puis, on montre la décroissance complète dans le cas de régions extérieures. Les régions pas traitées ici sont celles avec de la dispersion forte et la region des solitons.

1.
Introduction

We consider the initial value problem (IVP) associated to the Benjamin-Ono (BO) equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{H}\partial^{2}_{x}u+u\partial_{x}u=0,\quad x,t\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
u=u(x,t)
is a real-valued function and
\mathcal{H}
is the Hilbert transform, defined on the line as

\mathcal{H}f(x)={\rm p.v.}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}dy.

(1.2)

The BO equation was first deduced in the context of long internal gravity waves in a stratified fluid
[
4
,
35
]
. Later, the BO equation was shown to be completely integrable (see
[
1
]
and references therein).

In particular, it possesses an infinite number of conservation laws, being the first three the following :

\begin{split}I_{1}(u)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\,dx,\\
I_{2}(u)&=M(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\,dx,\\
I_{3}(u)&=E(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|D^{1/2}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{6}u^{3}\right)\,dx,\end{split}

(1.3)

where
\widehat{D^{s}f}(\xi)=|\xi|^{s}\,\widehat{f}(\xi)

The

provides a global in time

estimate of the norm
\,\|D^{(k-2)/2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

u=u(x,t)
of the (
1.1

The IVP (
1.1
) has been extensively studied, especially, the local well-posedness (LWP) and global well-posedness (GWP) measured in the Sobolev scale
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})=(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-s/2}L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

. In this regard, one has the following list of works: Iorio
[

[

[

[

We recall that the BO equation possesses traveling wave solutions (solitons)

Our main results in this work are the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
u=u(x,t)
be the global in time solution of the IVP (
1.1
) such that

u\in C(\mathbb{R}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})).

Then

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\int_{B_{t^{b}}(0)}u^{2}(x,t)\,dx=0,

(1.5)

where

denotes the ball centered in the origin with radius

B_{t^{b}}(0):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|x|<t^{b}\}\quad\mbox{with}\quad 0<b<\frac{2}{3}.

\int_{B_{t_{n}^{b}}(0)}u^{2}(x,t_{n})\,dx\leq\frac{C}{\log^{\frac{(1-b)}{b}}(t_{n})}.

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\int_{B_{t^{b}}(t^{m})}u^{2}(x,t)\,dx=0,

whenever

. We remark that this term is bounded in
\{t\gg 1\}
.

To estimate
A(t)
, we first differentiate in time to write

\begin{split}A(t)&=\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{t}(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&\quad-\frac{\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x,t)\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&\quad-\frac{q\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}^{\prime}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&=A_{1}(t)+A_{2}(t)+A_{3}(t).\end{split}

(3.11)

Using the equation in (
1.1
) and integrating by parts yield

\begin{split}A_{1}(t)&=\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}u(x,t)\,\partial_{x}^{2}\left(\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\right)\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+\frac{1}{2\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+\frac{1}{2\mu(t)\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}^{\prime}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&=:A_{1,1}(t)+A_{1,2}(t)+A_{1,3}(t).\end{split}

(3.12)

We remark that
\,A_{1,2}(t)
is the term we want to estimate in (
3.12
). Then we need to show that the reminder terms
are in
L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
.

Differentiating with respect to
x
, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Hilbert’s transform properties, the conservation of mass,
and the definition of
\mu(t)
and
\mu_{1}(t)
we deduce that

\begin{split}|A_{1,1}(t)|&\leq\frac{1}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}^{3/2}(t)}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\|\phi_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+\frac{1}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}^{(1+2q)/2}(t)}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\|\phi_{\delta}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+\frac{1}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}^{3q/2}(t)}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{\sigma,\delta}\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}}{t^{(2+b)/2}\log^{1/2}t}+\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}}{t^{(2-b+2bq)/2}\log^{(\frac{3}{2}-q)}(t)}\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}}{t^{(2-2b+3bq)/2}\log^{(4-3q)/2}t}.\end{split}

(3.13)

Since
q>1
it follows that
A_{1,1}(t)\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
.

The term
A_{1,3}
can be bounded by employing the conservation of mass, and the definition of

\mu(t)
and
\mu_{1}(t)
.

\begin{split}|A_{1,3}|&\leq\frac{\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{2|\mu(t)\mu_{1}^{q}(t)|}\left\|\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\phi_{\delta}^{\prime}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\lesssim_{\sigma,\delta}\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{t^{1-b+bq}\log^{(2-q)}t},\end{split}

(3.14)

because of
q>1
one has that
A_{1,3}(t)\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
.

Next we turn our attention to the other terms of (
3.11
). First, by means of Young’s inequality, we have for
\epsilon>0

\begin{split}|A_{2}(t)|&\leq\Big|\frac{\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\Big(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)\phi_{\delta}\Big(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\Big)\Big|\Big[\frac{u^{2}}{4\epsilon}+4\epsilon\Big|\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big|^{2}\Big]\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\Big|\frac{\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\Big(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)\phi_{\delta}\Big(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\Big)\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+4\epsilon\Big|\frac{\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\Big|\,\|\phi_{\delta}\Big(\frac{\cdot}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\Big)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Big(\frac{\cdot}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\Big)^{2}\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\Big(\frac{\cdot}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\Big)\|_{L^{1}}.\end{split}

Then, taking
\epsilon=\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)
, which is positive in
\{t\gg 1\}
, we get

\begin{split}|A_{2}(t)|&\leq\Big|\frac{1}{4\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+C_{\delta,\sigma}\frac{(b\log t-1)^{2}}{t^{3-3b}\log^{6}t}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}A_{1,2}(t)+C_{\delta,\sigma}\frac{(b\log t-1)^{2}}{t^{3-3b}\log^{6}t},\end{split}

(3.15)

where
C_{\sigma,\delta}
is a constant depending on
\sigma
and
\delta
.

Notice that the last term in the last inequality of (
3.15
) is integrable in
{t\gg 1}
since
b<\frac{2}{3}
.

Finally, we consider the term
A_{3}(t)
. Young’s inequality and the conservation of mass tell us that

\begin{split}|A_{3}(t)|&\leq\left|\frac{q\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\right|\|\psi_{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{1-b}u^{2}(x,t)\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+\left|\frac{q\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\right|\|\psi_{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{t^{1-b}}\left[\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}^{\prime}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\right]^{2}\,dx\\
&\lesssim_{\sigma,\delta}\left|\frac{qt^{1-b}\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\right|+\left|\frac{q\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}{t^{1-b}\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\right|.\end{split}

(3.16)

Hence, the conditions on (
3.5
) imply

|A_{3}(t)|\lesssim_{\sigma,\delta}\frac{1}{t\log^{2}t}+\frac{1}{t^{3-b(2+q)}\log^{2+q}t}.

(3.17)

Since
b\leq\frac{2}{2+q}
,
A_{3}(t)\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
.

Gathering the information in (
3.9
), (
3.11
), (
3.13
), (
3.14
), (
3.15
), (
3.16
)
and (
3.17
) together we conclude that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx\leq&\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}(t)+h(t)\end{split}

(3.18)

where
h(t)\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
, as desired.
∎

The next lemma will give us a key bound in our analysis.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
be the solution of the IVP (
1.1
). Then, there exists a constant
0<C<\infty
, such that

\int_{\{t\gg 1\}}\frac{1}{t\log t}\int_{B_{t^{b}}}u^{2}(x,t)\,dxdt\leq C.

(3.19)

Proof.

From the definition,
\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)=t\log t
and a straightforward computation involving the properties of the function
\phi
, it follows that

\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{B_{t^{b}}}u^{2}(x,t)\,dx\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}\left(\frac{{x}}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx,

for suitable
\sigma
and
\delta
, whenever
q>1
is chosen sufficiently close to 1 and
b
slightly smaller if necessary. Lemma
3.2
implies that

\int_{\{t\gg 1\}}\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{B_{t^{b}}}u^{2}(x,t)\,dxdt\leq-\mathcal{I}(t)+\int_{\{t\gg 1\}}|h(t)|\,dt.

(3.20)

The first term on the right hand side of inequality (
3.20
) is bounded because of
b\leq\frac{2}{2+q}<\frac{2}{3}
and the last one is bounded by the proof of Lemma
3.2
. This
completes the proof of the lemma.

∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Since the function
\frac{1}{t\log t}\notin L^{1}(B^{c}_{r}(1))
, from the previous lemma, we can ensure that there exists a sequence
(t_{n})\to\infty
, such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B_{(t_{n})}^{b}}u^{2}(x,t_{n})\,dx=0.

Therefore,
0
is an accumulation point and using that
u^{2}\geq 0
we can conclude the result.

To end this section we will give a sketch of the proof of Corollary
1.2
.

3.2.
Proof of Corollary
1.2

The proof of this result follows the same argument as the proof given to prove Theorem
1.1
. Hence
we will present the new details introduced in the proof. We consider
the functional

\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(t)=\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\int u(x,t)\,\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx

where
\rho(t)=\pm t^{m}
,
m
as in the statement of the corollary,
\mu(t)
and
\mu_{1}(t)
defined as in (
3.3
), and

\psi_{\sigma}
and
\phi_{\delta}
defined as above.

As in Lemma
3.1
we have that

\sup_{t\gg 1}|\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(t)|<\infty.

We also obtain a similar inequality as (
3.8
) in Lemma
3.2
, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int u^{2}(x,t)\,\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 4\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(t)+h_{\rho}(t),

where
h_{\rho}(t)\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
. Besides the terms previously handle in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, here we need to estimate two additional

-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx=A(t)

and

-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\delta}^{\prime}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx=B(t).

\begin{split}|A(t)+B(t)|&\leq\left|\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}^{1/2}(t)\mu(t)}\right|\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\|\phi_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\quad+\left|\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)\mu(t)}\right|\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi_{\delta}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{\sigma,\delta,m}\frac{1}{t^{(4-2m-b)/2}\log^{3/2}t}+\frac{1}{t^{(4-2b-2m+bq)/2}\log^{(4-q)/2}t}.\end{split}

We observe that the first term in the last inequality is in
L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
since
m\leq 1-\frac{b}{2}
. Similarly, the last term in last inequality is also in

L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
since
m\leq 1-\frac{b}{2}<1-b\frac{1-q}{2}
.

From this point on the argument of proof to establish Theorem

can be applied to end the proof of Corollary

.

4.
Proof of Theorem

In this section contains the proof of Theorem

4.1.
Asymptotic Behavior of
\left\|D^{1/2}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let u
\in C(\mathbb{R}:H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}:H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}))
the solution
of the IVP (
1.1
). Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\int_{\{t\gg 1\}}\frac{1}{t\log t}\int_{B_{t^{b}}(0)}\left|D^{1/2}u(x,t)\right|^{2}\,dxdt\leq C.

(4.1)

Proof.

Consider the functional

{\mathcal{J}}(t):=\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\,dx.

(4.2)

where
\mu(t)
and
\mu_{1}(t)
were defined in (
3.3
).

Differentiating (
4.2
) yields

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{J}}(t)&=-\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu^{2}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)\,\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&\quad+\frac{2}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x,t)\partial_{t}u(x,t)\,\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&\quad-\frac{\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)\,\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&=A(t)+B(t)+C(t).\end{split}

(4.3)

Combining the properties
\mu(t)
and
\mu_{1}(t)
, the conservation of mass, and using (
3.3
), it follows that

|A(t)|+|C(t)|\lesssim_{\sigma}\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{t^{2-b}\log^{2}t}.

(4.4)

Thus, the terms
A(t)
,
C(t)
are integrable in
\{t\gg 1\}
.

Regarding
B(t)
, we use the equation in (
1.1
) and integrate by parts to write

\begin{split}B(t)&=-\frac{2}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}u\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}u\,\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt-\frac{2}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}u\,\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+\frac{2}{3\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{3}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)dx\\
&=B_{1}(t)+B_{2}(t)+B_{3}(t).\end{split}

(4.5)

From Hilbert’s transform properties, integrating by parts and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we obtain

\begin{split}|B_{1}(t)|=&\left|-\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\partial_{x}\left[\mathcal{H},\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\right]\partial_{x}u\,dx\right|\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\partial_{x}\Big[\mathcal{H},\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\Big]\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(4.6)

Lemma
2.1
gives us

\begin{split}|B_{1}(t)|&\leq\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{\sigma}\frac{1}{t^{1+b}},\end{split}

(4.7)

which belongs to
L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
.

To estimate
B_{2}(t)
, we apply Plancherel’s identity to obtain

\begin{split}B_{2}(t)&=-\frac{2}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}uD^{1/2}\left[D^{1/2},\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\right]u\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt-\frac{2}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(D^{1/2}u\right)^{2}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)dx\\
&=B_{2,1}(t)+B_{2,2}.\end{split}

(4.8)

Notice that
B_{2,2}(t)
is the term we want to estimate.

To bound the term
B_{2,1}(t)
we use Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, the conservation of mass, (
2.5
), and properties of the Fourier transform
to deduce that

\begin{split}|B_{2,2}(t)|&\leq\left|\frac{2}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\right|\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\widehat{\left(\partial_{x}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\right)}\right\|_{L^{1}}\\
&\lesssim_{\sigma}\frac{1}{t^{1+b}}\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\}).\end{split}

(4.9)

Finally, notice that by Lemma
2.2

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{3}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)dx

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|u|\,\zeta_{n})^{3}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|u\,\zeta_{n}\|^{3}_{L^{3}}\left(\sup_{x\in[n,n+1]}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|u\,\zeta_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|D^{1/2}(u\,\zeta_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\left(\sup_{x\in[n,n+1]}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\right).

Moreover, by Lemma
2.3
and hypothesis,

\displaystyle\|D^{1/2}(u\zeta_{n})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|D^{1/2}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{1/2}\zeta_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1/2}}.

Combining these estimates we deduce that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x,t)|^{3}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)dx\\
\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|u\,\zeta_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\left(\sup_{x\in[n,n+1]}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\right).

A similar analysis to that given in Lemma 4.1 in
[
28
]
(see also
[
20
]
) yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x,t)|^{3}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\,dx.

(4.12)

Using the properties of the function
\phi
in (
3.1
) for suitable
\delta
and
\sigma
we can apply Lemma
3.3
to deduce
that
B_{3}(t)\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
.

Collection the information in (
4.3
), (
4.4
), (
4.7
), (
4.9
) and (
4.12
)
we deduce that

\frac{1}{t\log t}\int_{B_{t^{b}}}|D^{1/2}u(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt\leq\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{J}(t)+g(t),

where
\mathcal{J}(t)
is bounded and
g(t)\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
.

A similar analysis as the one implemented in the proof of Theorem
1.1
yields the desired result.

∎

5.
Proof of Theorems
1.5
and Theorem
1.7

The proofs of Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
are based on the following virial identity.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
be the global real solution of IVP (
1.1
). Then for any weighted function
\varphi=\varphi(x,t)
with

\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R}:L^{\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{4}(\mathbb{R}))

the following identity holds

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int u^{2}(x,t)\varphi(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int u\partial_{x}\big[\mathcal{H};\varphi\big]\partial_{x}u\,dx

(5.1)

\displaystyle-\int(D^{1/2}_{x}u)^{1/2}\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle-\int uD^{1/2}_{x}\big[D^{1/2}_{x};\partial_{x}\varphi\big]u\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{3}\int u^{3}\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx+\int u^{2}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\equiv A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}+A_{4}+A_{5}.

Remark 5.2
.

The terms
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
derive from the (linear) dispersive part of the equation.
A_{2}
corresponds to the local smoothing effect of Kato type, first deduced in solutions of the KdV equation
[
17
]
. As it was proved in Section 2 the terms
A_{1},A_{3}
are of order zero on
u
and of order two, in the homogeneous sense, on the weighted function
\varphi
.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

Using the equation we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int

\displaystyle u^{2}(x,t)\varphi(x,t)\,dx=2\int u\partial_{t}u\varphi\,dx+\int u^{2}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx

(5.2)

\displaystyle=2\int u(\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u-u\partial_{x}u)\varphi\,dx+\int u^{2}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=2\int u\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u\varphi\,dx+\frac{2}{3}\int u^{3}\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx+\int u^{2}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=2\int u\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u\varphi\,dx+A_{4}+A_{5}.

By integration by parts it follows that

2\int u\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u\varphi\,dx=-2\int\partial_{x}u\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}u\varphi\,dx-2\int u\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}u\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx.

(5.3)

Since

\displaystyle-\int\partial_{x}u\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}u\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int\partial_{x}u\mathcal{H}\left(\partial_{x}u\varphi\right)\,dx

(5.4)

\displaystyle=\int\partial_{x}u\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}u\varphi\,dx+\int\partial_{x}u\big[\mathcal{H};\varphi\big]\partial_{x}u\,dx,

one has that

\displaystyle-2\int\partial_{x}u\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}u\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int\partial_{x}u\big[\mathcal{H};\varphi\big]\partial_{x}u\,dx

(5.5)

\displaystyle=-\int u\partial_{x}\big[\mathcal{H};\varphi\big]\partial_{x}u\,dx=A_{1}.

Also

\begin{split}-2\int u&\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}u\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx=-2\int uD_{x}u\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx\\
&=-2\int D_{x}^{1/2}uD^{1/2}_{x}(u\partial_{x}\varphi)\,dx\\
&=-2\int D_{x}^{1/2}uD^{1/2}_{x}u\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx-2\int\!D_{x}^{1/2}u\big[D^{1/2}_{x};\partial_{x}\varphi\big]u\,dx\\

&=A_{2}+A_{3}.\end{split}

(5.6)

Inserting (

) in (

), and this in (

) we obtain (

).

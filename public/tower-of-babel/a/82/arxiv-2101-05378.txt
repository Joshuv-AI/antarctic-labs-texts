On the Schwartz correspondence for Gelfand pairs of polynomial growth
Francesca Astengo, Bianca Di Blasio, Fulvio Ricci

Abstract
. Let ( G , K ) (G,K) be a Gelfand pair, with G G a Lie group of polynomial growth, and let Σ ⊂ ℝ ℓ \Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell} be a homeomorphic image of the Gelfand spectrum, obtained by choosing a generating system D 1 , … , D ℓ D_{1},\dots,D_{\ell} of G G -invariant differential operators on G / K G/K and associating to a bounded spherical function φ \varphi the ℓ \ell -tuple of its eigenvalues under the action of the D j D_{j} ’s. We say that property (S) holds for ( G , K ) (G,K) if the spherical transform maps the bi- K K -invariant Schwartz space 𝒮 ⁡ ( K \ G / K ) \mathcal{S}(K\backslash G/K) isomorphically onto 𝒮 ⁡ ( Σ ) \mathcal{S}(\Sigma) , the space of restrictions to Σ \Sigma of the Schwartz functions on ℝ ℓ \mathbb{R}^{\ell} . This property is known to hold for many nilpotent pairs, i.e., Gelfand pairs where G = K ⋉ N G=K\ltimes N , with N N nilpotent. In this paper we enlarge the scope of this analysis outside the range of nilpotent pairs, stating the basic setting for general pairs of polynomial growth and then focussing on strong Gelfand pairs.

1.
Introduction

Within harmonic analysis on general locally compact groups, the theory of Gelfand pairs is the branch that exploits forms of commutativity in a noncommutative context.

If a group
G
is not abelian, convolution on it is not commutative, but it may happen that it becomes commutative when restricted to functions satisfying appropriate invariance properties.

Most of the relevant examples of this kind can be reconducted to the situation where commutativity holds for
L^{1}
-functions that are bi-invariant with respect to a compact subgroup
K
of
G
, i.e.,

(1.1)

f(k_{1}xk_{2})=f(x)\ ,\qquad\forall\,x\in G\ ,\ k_{1},k_{2}\in K\ .

In this case one says that
(G,K)
is a
Gelfand pair
.

The importance of this type of condition is that bi-
K
-invariant functions represent the kernels of integral operators on the
G
-homogeneous space
M=G/K
which commute with the action of
G
.

The theory becomes particularly rich when
G
is assumed to be a Lie group and
M
is connected. Then condition (
1.1
) is equivalent to commutativity of the algebra of
G
-invariant differential operators on
M
. In this case the homogeneous space
M
is also called a
commutative space
. The simplest examples of commutative spaces are spheres, Euclidean and hyperbolic spaces, with the action of their isometry groups.

The notion of Gelfand pair appeared first in the study of (infinite dimensional) irreducible unitary representations of semisimple Lie groups. Since then it has been widely applied to analysis on semisimple groups and symmetric spaces
[
17
,
33
]
. More recent is the interest in pairs where the group
G
is not semisimple, with motivations that are both algebraic
[
8
,
32
,
35
]
and analytic
[
6
,
7
,
10
,
19
]
.

In this paper we are only interested in Gelfand pairs
(G,K)
with
G
a Lie group and this condition must be assumed even if not made explicit. The group
K
is a compact subgroup of
G
and we will almost always require that
M=G/K
is connected.

With a standard language abuse, we use the same notation
L^{p}(G/K)
,
C^{\infty}(G/K)
etc., to denote both spaces of functions on the homogeneous space
M
and spaces of right-
K
-invariant functions on
G
. In the same way,
L^{p}(K\backslash G/K)
etc. will stand both for spaces of
K
-invariant functions on
M
and for spaces of bi-
K
-invariant functions on
G
.

Spherical analysis on a Gelfand pair
(G,K)
is the analogue of Fourier analysis on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or on the torus
\mathbb{T}
.

In fact, Fourier analysis on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is spherical analysis on the pair
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\{0\})
and similarly on
\mathbb{T}
.

Precisely, it comes from application of Gelfand theory to the commutative convolution algebra
L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)
.

Our interest is in the mapping properties of the spherical transform when
G
has polynomial growth. More precisely we want to know if the transform establishes a correspondence between Schwartz spaces (in the sense that will be explained soon), as the classical Fourier transform does on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

This problem has been investigated, always with positive answers, on a large number of “nilpotent pairs”
[
3
,
4
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
and it can be conjectured that the same should hold for general nilpotent pairs. In absence of a general approach able to cover the totality of pairs, progress on this topic goes by gradual bootstrapping from simpler cases to less simple ones.

Here we move in a different direction, opening the problem of validity of the Schwartz correspondence when
G
has polynomial growth but the pair is not nilpotent. In this perspective, the first case to analyze is that of
strong Gelfand pairs
of polynomial growth, which present a particularly neat structure. As it will be explained in the first paragraph of Section 4, the problem does not make sense in the same terms when
G
has not polynomial growth.

The scope of this paper is threefold. It wants to be an introduction to the problem, recalling the basic notions and properties of Gelfand pairs of general Lie groups, emphasizing the special features of pairs with polynomial growth and of strong Gelfand pairs. It proposes a paradigm for concrete attack of the Schwartz correspondence problem for strong pairs via reduction to
K
-types.

The method presented here finds application in
[

]
to the strong Gelfand pair
(U_{2}\ltimes\mathbb{C}^{2},U_{2})
. Finally, it gives a first application of this paradigm for strong pairs with
K
abelian.

Along the way, we give a proof of Schwartz correspondence for general compact Gelfand pairs.

We explain now in what sense we talk of Schwartz correspondence in the context of Gelfand pairs.

The spherical transform
\mathcal{G}f
of a function
f\in L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)
is defined on the Gelfand spectrum
\Sigma
, the set of bounded
spherical functions
, as

\mathcal{G}f(\varphi)=\int_{G}f(x)\varphi(x^{-1})\,dx\ ,\qquad\varphi\in\Sigma\ .

The operator
\mathcal{G}
has several basic properties in common with the Fourier transform, in particular, it is injective and

\mathcal{G}(f*g)=(\mathcal{G}f)(\mathcal{G}g)\ ,\qquad\mathcal{G}:L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)\longrightarrow C_{0}(\Sigma)\ .

Despite the abstract definition of
\Sigma
and of its topology, i.e., the weak* topology inherited from its inclusion in
L^{\infty}(K\backslash G/K)
, it is possible to give concrete realizations of
\Sigma
.

In fact, when realized as a function on
M
, a spherical functions
\varphi
is an eigenfunction of all
G
-invariant differential operators. In the algebra of such operators we can then fix a finite set of generators
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{\ell}\}
and associate to
\varphi
the
\ell
-tuple
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\ell})\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
of its eigenvalues under
D_{1},\dots,D_{\ell}
. This
\ell
-tuple identifies
\varphi
uniquely and the map
\varphi\longmapsto\xi
is a homeomorphism with closed image
[
12
]
, denoted by
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
.

If
G
has polynomial growth, choosing symmetric generators
D_{j}
, the eigenvalues are real, so that
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.
Setting

(1.2)

\mathcal{S}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}})=\big\{g_{|_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}}:g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})\big\}\cong\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})/\{g:g_{|_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}}=0\}\ ,

endowed with the quotient topology,
the Schwartz correspondence problem concernes the validity of the following property:

(S)

\mathcal{G}
maps
\mathcal{S}(K\backslash G/K)
onto
\mathcal{S}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}})
isomorphically.

2.
Gelfand pairs

Let
G
be a Lie group and
K
a compact subgroup. We introduce the following notation.

•

If
X(G)
is a space of functions on
G
, we denote by
X(K\backslash G/K)
the subspace of bi-
K
-invariant functions.

•

If
K^{\prime}
is a group of automorphisms of
G
,
X(G)^{K^{\prime}}
denotes the subspace of
K^{\prime}
-invariant elements of
X(G)
.

•

By
{\rm Int}(K)
we denote the group of conjugations by elements of
K
. It will be clear from the context whether conjugations are meant on
K
itself or on
G
. An
{\rm Int}(K)
-invariant function on
G
is called
K
-central
. Obviously,

(2.1)

X(K\backslash G/K)\subset X(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}\ .

•

By
\mathbb{D}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}\cong\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{{\rm Ad}(K)}
we denote the algebra of left-invariant differential operators on
G
which are also invariant under
{\rm Int}(K)
.

•

By
\mathbb{D}(G/K)\cong\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{{\rm Ad}(K)}/\big(\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{{\rm Ad}(K)}\cap\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{k}\big)
we denote the algebra of
G
-invariant differential operators on
G/K
.

We refer to
[
11
,
29
,
34
]
for undefined terms, unproven statements and further details.

Definition
.

The pair
(G,K)
is called a
Gelfand pair
if either of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(i)

the convolution algebra
L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)
is commutative;

(ii)

if
\pi\in\widehat{G}
contains the trivial representation of
K
, it does so without multiplicity.

Assuming
G/K
connected, the above conditions are also equivalent to

(iii)

\mathbb{D}(G/K)
is commutative.

Notice that it is not necessary to specify which Haar measure is being used in (i) to define
L^{1}
, because in either case commutativity of
L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)
implies unimodularity of
G
.

2.1.
K
-invariant functions on a group
H

A large class of Gelfand pairs arise in the following context.

Let
K
is a compact group of automorphisms of another Lie group
H
, and set
G=K\ltimes H
, with product

(2.2)

(k,h)(k^{\prime},h^{\prime})=\big(kk^{\prime},h(k\cdot h^{\prime})\big)\ ,

where
(k,h)\longmapsto k\cdot h
denotes the action of
k\in K
on
H
.

We identify the quotient space
G/K
with
H
, via the correspondence
h\longmapsto hK=\{(k,h):k\in K\}
.
For
f
defined on
H
, we call
\widetilde{f}
its right-
K
-invariant lifting to
G
,
\widetilde{f}(k,h)=f(h)
. Then
\widetilde{f}
is bi-
K
-invariant if and only if
f
is
K
-invariant. In particular, under this correspondence,

(2.3)

L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)\cong L^{1}(H)^{K}

as convolution algebras.
In a similar way one establishes the isomorphism

(2.4)

\mathbb{D}(G/K)\cong\mathbb{D}(H)^{K}

as operator algebras. This implies that the equivalences (i)-(iii) given above can be reformulated only in terms of
H
and
K
. In order to deal with (ii), we need the following notation.

Given an irreducible unitary representation
\pi
of
H
, let
K_{\pi}
be the stabilizer of
[\pi]\in\widehat{H}
under the action
k:\pi\longmapsto\pi\circ k^{-1}
of
K
. Let also
\sigma_{\pi}
be the associated projective representation of
K_{\pi}
, i.e., such that
\pi(k^{-1}\cdot h)=\sigma_{\pi}(k)\pi(h)\sigma_{\pi}(k)^{-1}
for all
k\in K
,
h\in H
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
H
,
K
,
G=K\ltimes H
be as above.
Each of the following is equivalent to the condition that
(G,K)
is a Gelfand pair:

(i’)

L^{1}(H)^{K}
is commutative;

(ii’)

for every
\pi\in\widehat{H}
,
\sigma_{\pi}
decomposes without multiplicities.

If
H
is connected, these conditions are also equivalent to

(iii’)

\mathbb{D}(H)^{K}
is commutative.

2.2.
Strong Gelfand pairs and
K
-types

The pair
(G,K)
is called a
strong Gelfand pair
if the
K
-central convolution algebra
L^{1}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}
is commutative. By (
2.1
), a strong Gelfand pair is a Gelfand pair.

The structure of strong Gelfand pairs is made more precise by introducing the notion of
K
-type for functions on
G
.

For
\tau\in\widehat{K}
, a function
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(G)
is called
of
K
-type
\tau
if the following identity holds for every
x\in G
:

(2.5)

f*_{K}(d_{\tau}\overline{\chi_{\tau}})(x)=d_{\tau}\int_{K}f(xk^{-1})\overline{\chi_{\tau}(k)}\,dk=f(x)\ ,

where
\chi_{\tau}
and
d_{\tau}
are character and dimension of
\tau
, respectively. Every
f\in L^{1}(G)
decomposes into
K
-types,

(2.6)

f\sim\sum_{\tau\in\widehat{K}}f_{\tau}\ ,\qquad\big(f_{\tau}=f*_{K}(d_{\tau}\overline{\chi_{\tau}})\ \big)\ .

If
f
is
K
-central, also its
K
-types
f_{\tau}
are
K
-central, and the space
L^{1}_{\tau}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}
of
K
-central functions of type
\tau
is a convolution algebra. Moreover, the convolution of two
K
-central functions of different
K
-types is zero. In particular, for
f,g\in L^{1}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}
,

f*g\sim\sum_{\tau\in\widehat{K}}f_{\tau}*g_{\tau}\ .

Let now
V_{\tau}
be the representation space of (some representative of)
\tau\in\widehat{K}
and
{\rm End}(V_{\tau})
the algebra of endomorphisms of
V_{\tau}
.
There is another realization of the
\tau
-type
K
-central algebra that we now describe
[
9
,
25
,
27
,
33
]
.

For
f\in L^{1}_{\tau}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}
, we set

A_{\tau}f(x)=\int_{K}f(xk)\tau(k)\,dk\in L^{1}\big(G,{\rm End}(V_{\tau})\big)\overset{\rm def}{=}L^{1}(G)\otimes{\rm End}(V_{\tau})\ .

Then

(2.7)

A_{\tau}f(k_{1}xk_{2})=\tau(k_{2}^{-1})A_{\tau}f(x)\tau(k_{1}^{-1})\ ,

and we express this equivariance condition by writing
A_{\tau}f\in L^{1}_{\tau,\tau}\big(G,{\rm End}(V_{\tau})\big)
.

Conversely, if
F\in L^{1}_{\tau,\tau}\big(G,{\rm End}(V_{\tau})\big)
, the function

S_{\tau}F(x)=d_{\tau}\text{\rm tr\,}F(x)

is in
L^{1}_{\tau}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}
and the two maps
A_{\tau}
,
S_{\tau}
are inverse of each other. Moreover they establish algebra isomorphisms, if convolution on
L^{1}_{\tau,\tau}\big(G,{\rm End}(V_{\tau})\big)
is defined as

F_{1}*F_{2}(x)=\int_{G}F_{1}(y^{-1}x)F_{2}(y)\,dy\ .

Proposition 2.2
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

(G,K)
is a strong Gelfand pair;

(b)

the pair
(K\times G,{\rm diag}K)\cong\big({\rm Int}(K)\ltimes G,{\rm Int}(K)\big)
is a Gelfand pair;

(c)

for every
\pi\in\widehat{G}
,
\pi_{|_{K}}
decomposes into irriducibles without multiplicities;

(d)

for every
\tau\in\widehat{K}
,
L^{1}_{\tau}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}
is commutative;

(e)

for every
\tau\in\widehat{K}
,
L^{1}_{\tau,\tau}\big(G,{\rm End}(V_{\tau})\big)
is commutative.

If
G
is connected, these conditions are also equivalent to

(f)

the algebra
\mathbb{D}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}
is commutative.

Notice that, denoting by
\tau_{0}
the trivial representation of
K

L^{1}_{\tau_{0}}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}=L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)
.

3.
Spherical functions and spectra

From now on, we only consider Gelfand pairs
(G,K)
with
G/K
connected and, consequently, only strong Gelfand pairs
(G,K)
with
G
connected.

The
spherical functions
on
G
are the
K
-invariant functions on
G/K
that are
joint eigenfunctions
\varphi
of all elements of
\mathbb{D}(G/K)
and have
\varphi(eK)=1
. We use the same term, and the same symbol
\varphi
, for the lifted bi-
K
-invariant function on
G
.

The set
\Sigma
of
bounded
spherical functions, endowed with the compact-open topology, represents the Gelfand spectrum of
L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)
, also called the spectrum of
(G,K)
. Then every nontrivial multiplicative functional on
L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)
can be expressed as

(3.1)

f\longmapsto\int_{G}f(x)\varphi(x^{-1})\,dx\overset{\rm def}{=}\mathcal{G}f(\varphi)\ .

The operator
\mathcal{G}:L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)\longrightarrow C_{0}(\Sigma)
is the
spherical transform
of
(G,K)
.

Calling, for
\varphi
spherical,
\lambda_{\varphi}(D)
the eigenvalue of
\varphi
under the action of
D\in\mathbb{D}(G/K)
, the algebra homomorphism
\xi_{\varphi}:\mathbb{D}(G/K)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
identifies
\varphi
uniquely. Since
\mathbb{D}(G/K)
is finitely generated
[
17
, Cor. 2.8, Thm. 5.6]
, we can identify spherical functions with finite tuples of eigenvalues.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{\ell}\}
be a finite generating system in
\mathbb{D}(G/K)
.
The map

(3.2)

\iota_{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi)=\big(\xi_{\varphi}(D_{1}),\dots,\xi_{\varphi}(D_{\ell})\big)\ .

establishes a homeomorphism between
\Sigma
and its image
\iota_{\mathcal{D}}(\Sigma)=\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
. Moreover,
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
is closed in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
.

If
G=K\ltimes H
, we can appeal to (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) and regard spherical functions as
K
-invariant functions on
H
, which are eigenfunctions of the operators in
\mathbb{D}(H)^{K}
.

Something more must be added in the case of a strong Gelfand pair
(G,K)
, recalling from Proposition
2.2
that this means that
\big({\rm Int}(K)\ltimes G,{\rm Int}(K)\big)
is a Gelfand pair. Coherently with the previous remark, we regard spherical functions as
K
-central functions on
G
, which are eigenfunctions of the operators in
\mathbb{D}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}
. Each spherical function has a unique
K
-type
[
25
, Prop. 7.3]
, and this determines a partition

(3.3)

\Sigma=\bigcup_{\tau\in\widehat{K}}\Sigma^{\tau}

of the Gelfand spectrum according to
K
-types, where each
\Sigma^{\tau}
is the Gelfand spectrum of the
K
-type subalgebra
L^{1}_{\tau}(G)^{{\rm Int}(K)}
.

Notice that, for the trivial representation
\tau_{0}
, the component
\Sigma^{0}
is the spectrum of the underlying (non-strong) Gelfand pair
(G,K)
.

4.
Pairs with polynomial growth

It is not possible, in general, to find generators such that the embedding (
3.2
) is in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
. This is the case, for instance, of symmetric pairs of the noncompact type, where the bounded spherical functions, as well as their eigenvalues, depend holomorphically on complex parameters
[
16
, Ch. 4]
.

On the contrary, embeddings in real Euclidean spaces are possible when the group
G
has
polynomial (volume) growth
, i.e., if there is
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that, given a compact neighborhood
U
of the identity, the Haar measures
m(U^{n})
of powers of
U
are
O(n^{k})
as
n\to\infty
.
We say that the pair
(G,K)
has polynomial growth if
G
has polynomial growth.

The following result extends Theorem G in
[
6
]
to general Gelfand pairs of polynomial growth.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(G,K)
be a Gelfand pair with polynomial growth. All bounded spherical functions are positive definite. Let
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{\ell}\}
be a generating system of
\mathbb{D}(G/K)
, with all the
D_{j}
symmetric operators. Then
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a bounded spherical function and

\Lambda_{\varphi}(f)=\int_{G}f(x)\varphi(x)\,dx

the associated multiplicative functional on
L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)
.
Since
L^{1}(G)
is symmetric
[
18
]
,
applying
[
23
, Thm. 1, p. 305]
, we can extend
\Lambda_{\varphi}
to an irreducible

-representation
\pi
of the whole algebra
L^{1}(G)
. In formulae, there is a one dimensional subspace
V
of the representation space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
such that

\pi(f)v=\Lambda_{\varphi}(f)v\qquad\forall\ v\in V\ ,\ f\in L^{1}(K\backslash G/K).

Denoting by
\pi_{0}
the irreducible unitary representation of the group
G
on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
such that

\pi(f)=\int_{G}f(x)\,\pi_{0}(x)\,dx\ ,

it follows that, if

is a unit vector in

\int_{G}f(x)\,\varphi(x^{-1})\,dx=\int_{G}f(x)\,\langle\pi_{0}(x)v,v\rangle\,dx\ ,\ \forall f\in L^{1}(K\backslash G/K)\ ,

therefore
\varphi
can be expressed as
\varphi(x)=\langle v,\pi_{0}(x)v\rangle
and
\varphi
is positive definite.

The bi-
K
-invariance of

implies that
v\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{0}}^{K}
, the subspace of

is a Gelfand pair. Then
\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{0}}^{K}
consists of

preserves

. Il follows that

D_{j}\varphi(x)=\langle v,\pi_{0}(x)d\pi_{0}(D_{j})v\rangle=\xi_{j}\varphi(x)\ .\qquad\qed

We call

an
embedded copy of

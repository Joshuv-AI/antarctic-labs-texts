Solutions to the Dirac equation in the background of 1 monopole with 2 singularities
Thomas Harris

Abstract
We use the Nahm transform to construct explicit L 2 L^{2} solutions to the Dirac equation in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} in the background of one nonabelian U ⁡ ( 2 ) U(2) monopole with one positive and one negative Dirac singularity.



1
Introduction

In 1931, Dirac argued
Dirac
that the existence of a magnetic monopole would imply charge quantization of electrons.
U(1)
electromagnetism prohibits monopoles via the Bianchi identity:

\rho_{\textrm{mag}}=\nabla\cdot B=0.

(1)

But by working in a
U(2)
Yang-Mills-Higgs theory, solitons which carry magnetic charge such as the ’t Hooft-Polyakov monopole
tHooft
can arise. BPS monopoles are then considered solutions to the Bogomolny equations

F_{A}=*D_{A}\Phi,

(2)

where
A
is the connection of some
U(2)
bundle over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\Phi\in\mathfrak{u}(2)
,
D_{A}
is the covariant derivative and
F_{A}
is the curvature. The magnetic charge is

\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S^{2}_{R}}\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi F_{A}).

(3)

In general, these are non-linear partial differential equations. Nahm
Nahm1
adapted the ADHM construction of instantons to obtain static self dual solutions to the self-dual Yang-Mills equations:

F_{A}=*F_{A}.

(4)

By considering the time component of this static connection as the Higgs field, we can recover the Bogomolny equations. Following Nahm, we consider a family of solutions to the Bogomolny equations given by modifying
\Phi
to
\Phi-t\mathbbm{1}
where
t
is some constant. The zero modes of the Dirac equation induce a vector bundle over some interval
t\in\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{R}
with induced connection
\nabla
and endomorphisms
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
solving the Nahm equations (
15
). An analogous process, the Nahm transform, can be applied to the data
(\nabla,T_{1},T_{2},T_{3})
over
\mathcal{I}
to recover the original connection.

In this paper, we use the Nahm transform to construct solution to Dirac equation. We refer the reader
Jardim
for a survey of the Nahm transform.

1.1
Bogomolny Equations

Given compact lie group
G
and a principal
G
-bundle over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with connection
A
and Higgs field
\Phi
, we can define a Lagrangian density via

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}Tr(F_{A}\wedge*F_{A})+Tr(D_{A}\Phi\wedge*D_{A}\Phi).

(5)

Here
F_{A}
denotes the curvature and
D_{A}
is the covariant derivative. The resulting energy from this Lagrangian is

E=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}Tr(F_{A}\wedge*F_{A})+Tr(D_{A}\Phi\wedge*D_{A}\Phi).

(6)

We are looking for pairs
(\Phi,A)
which minimize
E
. Under gauge transformation
g
,
\Phi,A,F_{A}
transform as follows:

\Phi\rightarrow g^{-1}\Phi g

(7)

A\rightarrow g^{-1}Ag-g^{-1}dg

(8)

F_{A}\rightarrow g^{-1}F_{A}g.

(9)

Note that while
\mathcal{L}
is invariant under gauge transformation,
\Phi
need not be. Denote the stabilizer of
\Phi
by
H\leq G
. We embed a 2-sphere of radius
R
,
S^{2}_{R}
, into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. By looking at very large
R
,
\Phi
induces a map
S^{2}_{R}\rightarrow G/H
. In the case that
G/H
is a
2-
sphere, this map divides our monopoles into topological classes determined by the degree of said map, which we will call
Q
. You could ask, given such a
Q
, how could we find a pair
(\Phi,A)
of the topological class? The lower bound of the energy
E
is proportional to
Q
. By extremizing the energy, we get the Bogomolny equations:

F_{A}=*D_{A}\Phi.

(10)

1.2
Monopoles

Two of the Maxwell’s equations are

\rho_{\textrm{mag}}=\nabla\cdot B=0

(11)

\nabla\cdot E=4\pi\rho_{e},

(12)

where
\rho_{\textrm{mag}}
represents magnetic charge density,
B
the magnetic field and
E
the electric field. Dirac considered the magnetic field that would arise from a point-like magnetic particle

B=m\frac{\overrightarrow{r}}{r^{3}},

(13)

which led him to the Dirac monopole. A property of this solution is that it is defined on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
minus a line, the Dirac string. We refer to the reader to
solitons
for the connection between Dirac’s monopole and the Bogomolny equations.

The Dirac monopole is now interpreted as a
U(1)
monopole and the Dirac string a gauge artifact. Kronheimer
Kron
also considered monopoles where
G
is
U(1)
. In this case, the Bogomolny equations imply:

d*d\Phi=0.

(14)

Thus
\Phi
is harmonic. If
\Phi
is bounded and positive, and defined on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then
\Phi
is constant. Kronheimer then allows the possibility that the monopole has singular points. Singular monopoles on compact manifolds were analyzed in
Pauly
. The ’t Hooft-Polyakov monopole
tHooft
,
Polyakov
is non-singular and contributes a
U(2)
gauge field. In
Durcan
, Durcan explicitly computes the Higgs field, connection, and solutions to the Dirac equation used in this paper. Solutions to Dirac equation for
N
abelian BPS monopoles all located at the same place were found in
Cheng
. Solutions for dipoles were found in
Baal
and solutions for
N
abelian BPS monopoles at generic positions were found in
Poirer
.

We can construct solutions to Bogomolny equations indirectly. If
E
is a Hermitian vector bundle associated to our principal
G
bundle, we can tensor it with the spinor bundle over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and define a Dirac operator on the resulting bundle. The Nahm Transform can be used to construct BPS monopoles, as well as solutions to the Dirac equation coupled to a BPS monopole
Schenk
. In this note we construct explicit normalizable solutions to the Dirac equation over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
coupled to gauge fields arising from one BPS monopole with two Dirac singularities with opposite charge. There are three specified locations on our manifold, and if we choose two of them to move to infinity, we can recover a known solution to the Dirac equation.

This work is an extension of Brian Durcan’s Master Thesis
Durcan
. Durcan constructed solutions to the Bogomolny equations by using the Nahm Transform. Durcan did this by solving the Nahm equations over
\mathcal{I}
, then finding normalized zero modes of the resulting Dirac equation and finally using the zero modes to construct the Higgs field and connection over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
which solved Bogomolny equations.

2
The Nahm Transform

We consider a Hermitian vector bundle
E
with structure group
G
over
\mathcal{I}:=(\infty,-\lambda)\cup(-\lambda,\lambda)\cup(\lambda,\infty)
parametrized by
s
. Solutions to the Nahm equations
Nahm1
are
T_{0},T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
, where
\frac{d}{ds}+iT_{0}
is a connection on
E
and
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
are endomorphisms of
E
living in the adjoint representation of the structure group of
E
, satisfying the Nahm equations:

\frac{dT_{i}}{ds}-\mathrm{i}[T_{0},T_{i}]=-\mathrm{i}[T_{j},T_{k}],

(15)

with
(i,j,k)
being any cyclic permutation of
(1,2,3)
.

By choice of gauge, we can set
T_{0}=0
resulting in

\frac{dT_{i}}{ds}=-\mathrm{i}[T_{j},T_{k}].

(16)

Let us briefly summarize the Nahm transform
Nahm1
. Since there are two Dirac operators appearing in this paper, we will refer to the first one as the Weyl operator. We use the following notation

\not{x}=\sigma_{1}x_{1}+\sigma_{2}x_{2}+\sigma_{3}x_{3},

(17)

where the Pauli matrices
\sigma_{i}
are given by:

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(18)

Given a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
the twisted Weyl operator is defined as

{\not{D}_{x}}=\frac{d}{{ds}}-(\not{T}(s)-\not{x}),

(19)

and its Hermitian conjugate

{\not{D}^{\dagger}_{x}}=-\frac{d}{{ds}}-(\not{T}(s)-\not{x}).

(20)

The Weyl operator acts of sections of
L^{2}(\mathcal{I},\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes E)
. The Dirac Laplacian
{\not{D}^{\dagger}_{x}}{\not{D}_{x}}
is:

{\not{D}^{\dagger}_{x}}{\not{D}_{x}}=\mathbbm{1}(-\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}++\sum_{j=1}^{3}T_{j}^{2})-\mathrm{i}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sigma_{i}(\frac{dT_{i}}{ds}+\mathrm{i}[T_{j},T_{k}]).

(21)

By (
16
), the imaginary part vanishes, thus the Dirac Laplacian is real.

Let
\rho_{x}
be a matrix whose columns form an orthonormal basis for Ker
\not{D}^{\dagger}_{x}
and
G_{x}(t,s)
the Green’s function of
{\not{D}^{\dagger}_{x}}{\not{D}_{x}}
.
\rho_{x}
induces a frame of a Hermitian vector bundle over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with Higgs field
\Phi
and connection
A_{j}
defined as

\Phi=\int s\rho_{x}^{\dagger}\rho_{x}\,ds

(22)

A_{j}=i\int\rho_{x}^{\dagger}\frac{d}{{dx^{j}}}\rho_{x}\,ds.

(23)

This choice of
\Phi
,
A_{j}
is a solution the Bogomolny equations.
Nahm1

By tensoring the resulting Hermitian vector bundle with the spin bundle over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, using
\Phi
and
A_{j}
, we define the twisted Dirac operator over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as

D_{t}=-(\Phi-t)+\overset{\rightharpoonup}{\sigma}\otimes(\frac{d}{d\overset{\rightharpoonup}{x}}-i\overset{\rightharpoonup}{A}).

(24)

This is the monopole Dirac operator. The zero modes of the Dirac equation,
D_{t}\chi_{t}=0
, are given by
Schenk

\widetilde{\chi}_{t}(x)=\int\rho^{\dagger}_{x}(s)G_{x}(t,s)\,ds

(25)

\widetilde{\chi}_{t}=\chi^{T}_{t}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(26)

Here we are identifying the

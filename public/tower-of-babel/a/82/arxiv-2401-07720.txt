Coarse cohomology of configuration space and coarse embedding
Arka Banerjee

Abstract
. We introduce a notion of equivariant coarse cohomology of the complement of a subspace in a metric space. We use this cohomology to define a notion of coarse cohomology of the two-points configuration space of a metric space and develop tools to compute this cohomology under various conditions. As an application of this theory, we show that certain classes in the coarse cohomology of two-points configuration space obstruct coarse embedding between two metric spaces.

Introduction

Van Kampen
[
14
]
developed an obstruction theory for embeddings of
n
-dimensional simplicial complexes into
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.
A modern approach to his theory uses (co)homology of the two-points configuration space. In this article, we develop an analogous obstruction theory for coarse embedding by introducing a notion of coarse cohomology of two-points configuration space.
For simplicity, throughout this article, we will say configuration space to mean two-points configuration space.

Let us first briefly describe the classical van Kampen obstruction for a topological space
X
.
Let
\delta(X)
be the diagonal set
\{(x,x)\mid x\overset{}{\in}X\}\overset{}{\subset}X\times X
.
Consider the following deleted product

\tilde{X}:=X\times X-\delta(X)=\{(x,y)\overset{}{\in}X\times X\mid x\neq y\}

\tilde{X}
has a natural free action by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by switching the coordinates.
Consider the corresponding
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
covering map
q:\tilde{X}\rightarrow\tilde{X}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
The space
\tilde{X}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
which we denote by
\Conf(X)
, is the unordered configuration space of two points in
X
.
There exists a classifying map from the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-bundle
q:\tilde{X}\rightarrow\tilde{X}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
to the universal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-bundle
S^{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}
as follows.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\infty}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Conf(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\phi}}

If there is an embedding
g:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then we can choose
\tilde{\phi}
so that it factors through
S^{n-1}
. More precisely, we can choose
\tilde{\phi}
to be the following map
\tilde{X}\rightarrow S^{n-1}\overset{}{\subset}S^{\infty}

(x,y)\mapsto\frac{g(x)-g(y)}{|g(x)-g(y)|}

In this case,
\phi

\Conf(X)
to
\mathbb{R}P^{n-1}\overset{}{\subset}\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}
.

So the induced map
\phi^{*}:\myh^{n}(\mathbb{R}P^{\infty})\rightarrow\myh^{n}(\Conf(X))
is trivial as it factors through
\myh^{n}(\mathbb{R}P^{n-1})
.
In particular, if
\eta^{n}\overset{}{\in}\myh^{n}(\mathbb{R}P^{\infty};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
denotes
the nonzero class, then
\phi^{*}(\eta^{n})
would be trivial.
In other words, the cohomology class
\phi^{*}(\eta^{n})
gives an obstruction for the embedding of
X
into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We will call the class
\phi^{*}(\eta^{n})
the van Kampen obstruction class of degree
n
and denote it by
vk^{n}(X)
.

Let us now turn our attention to the coarse world.
A map
f:X\rightarrow Y
between two metric spaces is said to be a
coarse embedding
if there exist two proper non-decreasing maps

\rho_{-},\rho_{+}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that

\rho_{-}(d(x,y))\leq d(f(x),f(y))\leq\rho_{+}(d(x,y))\quad\text{for all}\quad x,y\overset{}{\in}X

Roe
[
12
]
defined the notion of coarse cohomology of a metric space which can be thought of as a coarse analog of Alexander–Spanier
cohomology in the topological setting.
The main motivation of this paper is to define a coarse version of
vk^{*}
that obstructs coarse embedding between metric spaces.

The study of obstruction to the coarse embedding of finitely generated groups into proper contractible
n
-manifold was initiated by Bestvina, Kapovich, and Kleiner
[
3
]
.
For several interesting classes of metric spaces (for example CAT(0) space, Gromov-hyperbolic space) one can attach a boundary at infinity to the space.
Suppose
\partial_{\infty}X
denotes such boundary of a space
X
.
A popular theme in the study of large scale geometry is to find topological properties of
\partial_{\infty}X
that provide information about the coarse geometry of
X
.
A special case of the work of Bestvina–Kapovich–Kleiner
[
3
]
roughly says the following: if
\partial_{\infty}X
does not embed into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
due to the van Kampen embedding obstruction, then
X
does not coarsely embed into
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with the Euclidean metric.
One of the motivations behind the present work was to understand their obstruction in the language of Roe’s coarse cohomology.

To this end, recall that the van Kampen obstruction class
vk^{*}(X)
lives in the cohomology of the configuration space of
X
, which is the same as the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariant cohomology of
X\times X-\delta(X)
where the coefficients
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with the trivial
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
action.
This suggests that a coarse version of van Kampen obstruction class should live in some coarse version of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariant cohomology of complement of
\delta(X)
in
X\times X
.
This motivates us to define a notion of equivariant coarse cohomology of the complement of subspace in a metric space.

Building on Roe’s theory, authors in
[
1
]
defined a notion of coarse cohomology of complement of a subspace in a metric space.
In this paper, we extend the work of
[
1
]
to the equivariant setting.
We then define the coarse cohomology of the configuration space of
X
simply to be the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariant coarse cohomology of the complement of
\delta(X)
in
X\times X
, where the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
on
X\times X
is by switching coordinate.

Once we have a proper notion of the coarse cohomology of the configuration space, we can search for a coarse
vk^{*}
in that cohomology that obstructs coarse embeddings.
Indeed, when
X
is a separable metric space, we find a class in the
n^{th}
degree of the coarse cohomology of the configuration space of
X
, which we denote by
cvk^{n}(X)
, that obstructs coarse embedding of
X
into
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

If

.

So one way to determine when a given space does not admit coarse embedding into another space is to compare their

However, the equivariant coarse cohomology of the complement where

A big part of this paper is devoted to the computation of equivariant coarse cohomology of the complement for certain spaces which may be of independent interest.
We use these computations to estimate

Preliminaries

Alexander–Spanier complexes

We will refer to points in
X^{n+1}
as
n
-simplices
.
Let
R
be an abelian group.
We will think of functions
X^{n+1}\to R
as
n
-cochains or
n
-chains on
X
, depending on the context, and in the latter case will use additive notation
c=\sum_{\sigma\overset{}{\in}X^{*+1}}c_{\sigma}\sigma
.

The basic complex is the complex of finitely supported chains

\myc_{*}(X;R):=\{c=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}\sigma_{i}\mid c_{i}\overset{}{\in}R\text{ and }\sigma_{i}\overset{}{\in}X^{*+1}\}

equipped with the usual boundary map, defined on the basis by

\partial(x_{0},\cdots,x_{n}):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(x_{0},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n}).

Note that the boundary map
\partial
is well-defined on a larger complex of
locally finite
chains
\myc_{*}^{lf}(X;R)
which consists of chains
c
satisfying the following property: for any bounded
B\overset{}{\subset}X
only finitely many simplices in
c
have vertices in
B
.

The algebraic dual of
\myc_{*}(X)
is the complex of all Alexander–Spanier cochains

\myc^{*}(X;R)=\{\phi:X^{*+1}\to R\}

with the coboundary operator

d(\phi)(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\phi(x_{0},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n}).

Lemma 2.1
.

\myc^{*}(X;R)
is an acyclic complex i.e the homology of the complex is trivial in each degree except at degree zero where it is isomorphic to
R
.

Proof.

In degree zero, the cohomology consists of all the constant functions
X\rightarrow R
which is isomorphic to
R
.

To prove that the cohomology is trivial in degree
\geq 1
, choose
a\overset{}{\in}X
.
Consider the following cone operator
D_{*}:\myc_{*}(X)\rightarrow\myc_{*+1}(X)

D_{n}:(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(a,x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}).

For any
n
-simplex
\sigma
with
n\geq 1
, we observe that
D_{*}\partial(\sigma)+\partial D_{*}(\sigma)=\sigma
.
By considering the dual, we have that for any cochain
\phi\overset{}{\in}\myc^{*}(X;R)
in degree
\geq 1
,

dD^{*}(\phi)+D^{*}d(\phi)=\phi.

In particular,

dD^{*}(\phi)=\phi
when
\phi
is a cocycle.
The claim follows.
∎

All the cochain complexes that we will consider in this paper would be subcomplexes
of the complex
(\myc^{*},d)
.

For a cochain
\phi\overset{}{\in}\myc^{*}(X)
, let
||\phi||
be the intersection of the diagonal
\Delta=\{(x,x,\ldots,x)\mid x\overset{}{\in}X\}\overset{}{\subset}X^{*+1}
and the closure of the support of the function
\phi:X^{*+1}\rightarrow R
.
Let
\myc^{*}_{0}(X;R)
be the complex of locally zero cochains:

\myc^{*}_{0}(X;R)=\{\phi\overset{}{\in}\myc^{*}(X;R)\mid\lVert\phi\rVert=\emptyset\}.

Note that the restriction of
d
gives a well defined map
\myc^{*}_{0}(X)\rightarrow\myc^{*+1}_{0}(X)
.
Consequently,
d
induces a well defined map
\myc^{*}_{as}(X;R)\rightarrow\myc^{*+1}_{as}(X;R)
, where

\myc^{*}_{as}(X;R)=\myc^{*}(X;R)/\myc^{*}_{0}(X;R).

Alexander–Spanier cohomology, denoted by
\myh^{*}(X;R)
, is the cohomology of the complex
(\myc^{*}_{as}(X;R),d)
.
We will denote by
\tilde{\myh}^{*}(X;R)

Coarse intersection

Now we recall from
[

Equivariant coarse cohomology of the complement

We start by briefly reviewing the notion of coarse cohomology of the complement from
[
1
]
.
Roughly the idea is that the coarse complement of a subset
A
in
X
is determined by the collection of subsets
\mathcal{S}
of
X
which are
coarsely disjoint
from
A
:

\mathcal{S}:=\{B\overset{}{\subset}X\mid N_{r}(B)\cap N_{r}(A)\text{ is bounded for any $r\geq 0$}\}

Coarse cohomology of the complement of
A
, denoted by
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A)
, is defined to be the cohomology of the following complex with the usual coboundary operator
d
mentioned in the previous section.

\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{n}(X-A;R)=\{\phi\overset{}{\in}\myc^{n}(X;R)\mid\phi|_{B}\overset{}{\in}\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{n}(B;R)\text{ for all }B\overset{}{\in}\mathcal{S}\}

Recall that
i:X\to X^{\infty}
denote the map
x\mapsto(x,\dots,x,\dots)
and
\Delta=i(X)
.
For a subset
A\overset{}{\subset}X
, we denote the set
i(A)\overset{}{\subset}\Delta
by
\Delta_{A}
.
For our purpose, we will work with the following equivalent description of
\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A)
(see
[
1
]
, Lemma 3.2)

\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{n}(X-A;R)=\{\phi\overset{}{\in}\myc^{n}(X;R)\mid\lvert\phi\rvert\mathbin{\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\cap}}\Delta\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\subset}\Delta_{A}\}

Note that this description of coarse cochains of the complement is closer to the spirit of Roe’s original definition of coarse cochains discussed in the previous section.

Example 3.1
.

When
X\mathbin{\overset{\mathrm{c}}{=}}A
, we can see that
\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A;R)
coincides with
\myc^{*}(X;R)
because in this case
\Delta\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\subset}\Delta_{A}
and therefore the support condition
\lvert\phi\rvert\mathbin{\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\cap}}\Delta\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\subset}\Delta_{A}
holds for any
\phi\overset{}{\in}\myc^{*}(X;R)
.
Since
\myc^{*}(X;R)
is an acyclic complex,
in this case
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A;R)
is trivial in all degrees except at degree
0
where it is isomorphic to
R
.

Example 3.2
.

If
A
is bounded, then
\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A;R)
coincides with
\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X;R)
.
Hence,
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A,R)=\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X;R)

Definition 3.4

Example 3.5

Any locally finite simplicial complex is locally acyclic.
If a space is locally acyclic, then it is locally acyclic away from any of its subset.
However, the converse is not true.
For instance, any bounded metric space is locally acyclic away from any subset.

Definition 3.6

Example 3.7

Universal covers of compact aspherical complexes are uniformly acyclic. Any uniformly acyclic space is uniformly acyclic away from any of its subset.
Moreover, if we remove a bounded set from a uniformly acyclic space, then the resulting space is uniformly acyclic away from any point.

Coarse van Kampen obstruction class
.

Let
X
be a separable metric space and
g:X\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}
be a coarse expanding map. Such a map exists because any separable metric space admits an isometric embedding into
\ell^{\infty}
by the work of Fréchet (
[
7
]
).
We consider the induced map
g^{*}:\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}((\ell^{\infty})^{2}-\delta(\ell^{\infty});\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X);\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
Recall from Example
5.3
that
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}((\ell^{\infty})^{2}-\delta(\ell^{\infty});\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
n\geq 2
.
Let
e^{n}
be the nontrivial element in
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}((\ell^{\infty})^{2}-\delta(\ell^{\infty}))
for
n\geq 2
.
Proposition
6.1
implies that
g^{*}(e^{n})
depends only on the space
X
, not on
g
.
We call the class
g^{*}(e^{n})
to be the
n^{th}
degree coarse van Kampen obstruction class
of
X
and denote it by
cvk^{n}(X)
where
n\geq 2
.

Assumption
.

From now on all our metric spaces will be separable. In the non-equivariant setting, our coefficient group will be
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
in the equivariant setting, the coefficient group will be
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with the trivial
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action unless stated otherwise.
In those cases, we will omit the coefficient from the notation.

Definition 6.3

(Coarse obstruction dimension)
.

The coarse obstruction dimension of a space
X
, denoted by
\cobdim(X)
, is
0
if
X
is bounded, is
1
if
cvk^{n}(X)=0
for all
n
, and otherwise, it is the largest
n
such that
cvk^{n}(X)\neq 0
.

Now we prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 6.4
.

If
X
admits a coarse expanding map into
Y
, then
\cobdim(X)\leq\cobdim(Y)
.

Proof.

If
\cobdim(X)=0
, then there is nothing to prove.

If
\cobdim(X)=1
, then
X
is unbounded by definition. This means
Y
is also unbounded and hence
\cobdim(Y)\geq 1
by definition.

Suppose
\cobdim(X)=n\geq 2
.
Let
g:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
be a coarse expanding map.
Consider the following composition.

X\times X\xrightarrow{f}Y\times Y\xrightarrow{g}\ell^{\infty}\times\ell^{\infty}

By Proposition
5.8
, the above maps induce the following maps between coarse cohomology of the configuration spaces

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}((\ell^{\infty})^{2}-\delta(\ell^{\infty}))\xrightarrow{g^{*}}\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(Y^{2}-\delta(Y))\xrightarrow{f^{*}}\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))

Let
e^{n}\overset{}{\in}\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}((\ell^{\infty})^{2}-\delta(\ell^{\infty}))
be the generator.
Then
cvk^{n}(Y)=g^{*}(e^{n})
and
cvk^{n}(X)=f^{*}g^{*}(e^{n}))=f^{*}(cvk^{n}(Y))
.
By assumption
cvk^{n}(X)\neq 0
and hence
cvk^{n}(Y)\neq 0
.
So, we get
\cobdim(Y)\geq n
.
∎

Recall that
X
and
Y
are said to be
coarsely equivalent
if there exists a coarse embedding map
f:X\rightarrow Y
such that
Y\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\subset}f(X)
.
One can observe that two coarsely equivalent spaces coarsely embed into each other.
As a consequence, the above theorem immediately yields the following.

Corollary 6.5
.

If
X
and
Y
are coarsely equivalent, then
\cobdim(X)=\cobdim(Y)
.

In Example
5.2
, we saw that
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{2}-\delta(\mathbb{R}^{n}))=0
for all
*>n
.
Hence
\cobdim(\mathbb{R}^{n})\leq n
. Using Theorem
6.4
, we obtain

Corollary 6.6
.

If
\cobdim(X)\geq n
, then
X
does not admit a coarse expanding map into
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

Relation to the classical van Kampen obstruction

Let us recall the classical van Kampen obstruction class.
Let
X
be any topological space.
Any continuous embedding

induces a map

This map depends only on

because the quotient map

q:(\mathbb{R}^{\infty})^{2}-\delta(\mathbb{R}^{\infty})\rightarrow((\mathbb{R}^{\infty})^{2}-\delta(\mathbb{R}^{\infty}))/{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-bundle.

be the generator.
The cohomology class

is called the van Kampen obstruction class in degree

and will be denoted by

.
Note that the quotient map

-bundle because

Hence we can use
\ell^{\infty}
instead of
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
to define

.
We use this viewpoint in the next proposition.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let

be a metric space. Suppose
i:\myh^{*}((X^{2}-\delta(X))/\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow\varinjlim\myh^{*}((X^{2}-N_{r}(\delta(X)))/\mathbb{Z}_{2})

for each

. If
i(vk^{n-1}(X))
is nontrivial for some

, then
cvk^{n}(X)
is nontrivial.

Proof.

Let
f:X\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}
be an isometry.
Fix
n\geq 2
.
Suppose
i(vk^{n-1}(X))\neq 0
.
We consider the following commutative diagram where all the horizontal maps are canonically induced by
f
.
We want to show that the top horizontal map is nontrivial.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varinjlim\myh^{n-1}(((\ell^{\infty})^{2}-N_{r}(\delta(\ell^{\infty})))/\mathbb{Z}_{2})}

.

8.
A Coarse Gysin sequence

Recall that coarse van Kampen obstruction class lives in
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))
.
In this section, we relate
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
to
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}
and apply that to compute
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))
for certain
X
.

Suppose
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is acting on some metric space
X
by isometries and
A
is the subset of
X
that is fixed by the action.
We consider the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\xrightarrow{i}\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A)\xrightarrow{p}\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\xrightarrow{r}\myc^{*}(A)\rightarrow 0

where
i
is the inclusion map and
p(\phi):\sigma\mapsto\phi(\sigma)+\phi(g\sigma)
where
g
is the generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
r
is the restriction map.
The image of
p
consists of those cochains in
\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)
that send any simplex supported on
A
to zero.
It is easy to see that collection of such cochains gives a subcomplex of
\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)
.
We denote this complex by
\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)
, and the corresponding cohomology by
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)
.
Hence, the above four-term short exact sequence splits into the following two short exact sequences.

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\xrightarrow{i}\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A)\xrightarrow{p}\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)\xrightarrow{i}\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\xrightarrow{r}\myc^{*}(A)\rightarrow 0

These two short exact sequences give us two long exact sequences in the cohomology which we record as our next lemma.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
X
be a metric space and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\curvearrowright(X,A)
such that
A
is the fixed point set of the action.
Then we have the following two long exact sequences

(8.1)

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*+1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\rightarrow\cdots

(8.2)

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\rightarrow\myh^{*}(\myc^{*}(A))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*+1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)\rightarrow\cdots

Lemma 8.2
.

Suppose
A\overset{}{\subset}X
and
X
is not coarsely contained in
A
.
If
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\curvearrowright(X,A)
where
A
is the fixed point set of the action, then

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $*=0$}\\
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}&\text{if $*=1$}\\
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)&\text{if $*\geq 2$}\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
X
is not coarsely contained in
A
, we have

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)=\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)=0.

By Lemma
2.1
, the homology of the complex
\myc^{*}(A)
vanishes everywhere except in the degree

where it is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Combining this with the second long exact sequence of the Lemma

, we obtain

(8.3)

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\\
\cdots 0\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\rightarrow 0\cdots

where

.

Since the coefficient group is

, the first five terms give us a split short exact sequence.
Hence
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A,A)=\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
It also follows from (

) that
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A,A)=\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A)
for all
*\geq 2
.

∎

Hence under the hypothesis of Lemma
8.2
, we can rewrite the first long exact sequence of Lemma
8.1
as follows.

Lemma 8.3

(Coarse Gysin sequence)
.

Suppose
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\curvearrowright(X,A)
where
A\overset{}{\subset}X
is the fixed point set of the action.
Moreover, assume that
A
is unbounded and
X
is not coarsely contained in
A
. Then there is a long exact sequence of the following form:

0\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{0}(X-A)\rightarrow 0\\
\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}(X-A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\\
\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A)\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X-A)\rightarrow\cdots

where
*\geq 2
.

We are now ready to state our main result of this section.

Proposition 8.4
.

Suppose
X
is unbounded and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is acting on
X^{2}
by permuting the coordinates. Then there is a long exact sequence of the following form:

(8.4)

0\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{0}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow 0\\
\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\\
\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\cdots

where
*\geq 2
.

Proof.

By hypothesis,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\curvearrowright(X^{2},\delta(X))
and
\delta(X)
is the set of fixed points of the action. Since
X
is unbounded
X^{2}
is not coarsely contained in
\delta(X)
.
So, we can apply Lemma
8.3
on
(X^{2},\delta(X))
, and the claim follows.
∎

Remark 8.5
.

If
X
is unbounded and
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
, then it follows from Proposition
8.4
that
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
. As a consequence, we can write the beginning part of the coarse Gysin sequence (
8.4
) as follows

(8.5)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\cdots

This observation will be useful for us in our next theorem where we compute
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))
when
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X^{2}-\delta(X))
is concentrated in some degree.

Theorem 8.6
.

Suppose
X
is a metric space such that for some
n\geq 1

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}&*=n,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Moreover, suppose that
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{i}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
for some
i\geq n+1
.
Then

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{Z}_{2}&\quad\text{if $n\geq 2$ and }2\leq*\leq n,\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Elements of
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{0}(X^{2}-\delta(X))
and
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))
are constant functions on
X^{2}
with support contained in a neighborhood of
\delta(X)
.
Since
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{0}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
, we have

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
.

Next, we will show that
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
if
*\geq n+1
. Consider the following part of the coarse Gysin sequence where
*\geq 2
.

(8.6)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*+1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*+1}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow

Since
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
for
*\geq n+1
, the middle map is an isomorphism for
*\geq n+1
.
Hence,

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\quad\text{ for all }*\geq n+1.

That means, if
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\neq 0
then
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\neq 0
for all
*\geq n+1
.
By hypothesis,
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{i}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
for some
i\geq n+1
.
Therefore,
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
and consequently,

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0\quad\text{ for all }*\geq n+1

We divide the rest of the calculations into three cases.

Case 1 (
n=1
):

We only have to show that
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
.
By the hypothesis,
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
.
Hence, we get the following from the coarse Gysin sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow 0

Since the third map is surjective,
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))
cannot have more than one element and hence it is trivial.

Case 2 (
n=2
):
By hypothesis,
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
, Therefore,
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
(see remark
8.5
).
Furthermore, we have
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by hypothesis and we already showed
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
.
So a part of the sequence (
8.5
) takes the following form.

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow 0

So there is an injective map and a surjective map from
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
into

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))
.
It follows that
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Case 3 (
n>2
):
In this case,
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
.
The remark
8.5
gives us
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
.

Since
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
, the beginning part of the sequence (
8.5
) takes the following form.

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\cdots

Hence
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Since
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
for
*\leq n-1
,
it follows from
(\ref{mid})
that

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*+1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\quad\text{ when }\quad 2\leq*\leq n-2.

That implies

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\text{ when }2\leq*\leq n-1.

Finally to compute
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))
, consider the following part of the coarse Gysin sequence (Lemma
8.3
).

\dots\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\rightarrow\cdots

Since
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\neq 0
by assumption, it follows from the above sequence that
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))\neq 0
.
We already know that the fourth term is trivial in the above sequence. That means the third map is surjective. Since
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we can conclude that
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
∎

Remark 8.7
.

It follows from the first part of the proof of the above theorem that if
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
for all
*\geq n+1
and
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
for some
*\geq n+1
, then
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
for all
*\geq n+1
.
In particular, for such
X
,
cvk^{*}(X)
is trivial for
*\geq n+1
and hence
\cobdim(X)\leq n
.

9.
Upper bound of cobdim

In this section, our goal is to apply Theorem
8.6
to estimate
\cobdim
for proper, uniformly acyclic
n
-manifolds.
The key tool that we are going to exploit here is a coarse Alexander duality theorem that holds for such spaces.
In fact, such duality holds for a more general class of metric spaces called coarse PD(
n
) spaces, first introduced by
Kapovich and Kleiner in
[
10
]
where they proved a coarse Alexander duality theorem for these spaces.
A different treatment of coarse PD(
n
) space and coarse Alexander duality using coarse cohomology is given in
[
1
]
.
Let us now recall the definition of a coarse PD(
n
) space from
[
1
]
.

Definition 9.1
.

A metric space
X
is a
coarse PD(
n
) space
, if there exist chain maps
p:\myc^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to\myc{\mathrm{X}}_{n-*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
and
q:\myc{\mathrm{X}}_{n-*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to\myc^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, so that
pq
and
qp
are chain homotopic to identities via chain homotopies
G:\myc{\mathrm{X}}_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to\myc{\mathrm{X}}_{*+1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
and
F:\myc^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to\myc^{*-1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
which are controlled:

\displaystyle\forall\phi\overset{}{\in}\myc^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\qquad\lvert p(\phi)\rvert\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\subset}\lvert\phi\rvert,

\displaystyle\forall\phi\overset{}{\in}\myc^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\quad\lvert F(\phi)\rvert\mathbin{\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\cap}}\Delta\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\subset}\lvert\phi\rvert,

\displaystyle\forall c\overset{}{\in}\myc{\mathrm{X}}_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\quad\lvert q(c)\rvert\mathbin{\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\cap}}\Delta\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\subset}\lvert c\rvert,

\displaystyle\forall c\overset{}{\in}\myc{\mathrm{X}}_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\qquad\lvert G(c)\rvert\overset{\mathrm{c}}{\subset}\lvert c\rvert.

Example 9.2
.

Any proper, uniformly acyclic
n
-manifold is a coarse PD(
n
) space
[
1
]
*Corollary 8.3. In particular, the universal cover of a closed, aspherical
n
-manifold is a coarse PD(
n
) space.

We now recall the coarse Alexander duality theorem from
[
1
]
.

Theorem 9.3

(Coarse Alexander duality
[
1
]
)
.

If
X
is a coarse PD(
n
) space, then for any
A\overset{}{\subset}X
and finitely generated abelian group
G

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-A;G)\cong\myh{\mathrm{X}}_{n-*}(A;G).

As a consequence we have the following.

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
X
be a coarse PD(
n
) space. Then

\myh{\mathrm{X}}_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}&*=n,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Theorem
9.3
, we have
\myh{\mathrm{X}}_{n-*}(X;G)=\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-X;G)
.
Observe that
\myc{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X-X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\myc^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Since
\myc^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is acyclic by Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\myh{\mathrm{X}}_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{n-*}(X-X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}&*=n,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

∎

For the rest of the paper, the omitted coefficient will mean
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Lemma 9.5
.

If
X
is a coarse PD(
n
) space, then

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}&*=n,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
X
is a coarse PD(
n
) space,
then
X^{2}
is a coarse PD(
2n
) space.
We obtain

\displaystyle\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}(X^{2}-\delta(X))

\displaystyle=\myh{\mathrm{X}}_{2n-*}(\delta(X))

by Theorem
9.3

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}&*=n,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

by Lemma
9.4

∎

Lemma 9.6
.

If
X
is a proper, uniformly acyclic
n
-manifold where

, then
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{i}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0

Proof.

If

is bounded, then

and the claim follows from Theorem
3.8
(

).
So, we assume that

is unbounded.
This implies that

is not coarsely contained in

By hypothesis,

Hence we can apply Corollary

to the pair

and obtain

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=\varinjlim\tilde{\myh}^{*-1}((X^{2}-N_{r}(\delta(X))/\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

Since
(X^{2}-N_{r}(\delta(X)))/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is a

\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
for all

.
∎

Theorem 9.7
.

If
X
is a proper, uniformly acyclic

\cobdim(X)\leq n
.

Proof.

For

, the claim is trivial.
For

and Lemma

implies that

, we have

.

∎

Remark 9.8
.

Note that to prove Theorem
9.7
, we did not need the full strength of the Theorem
8.6
.
We needed to show
\myh{\mathrm{X}}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X^{2}-\delta(X))=0
for

which only requires vanishing of

for

Our next goal is to improve Theorem

For that, we need to impose a condition on the metric of the boundary.
This is the purpose of the following definition which is inspired by the uniformly locally
k
-connected space defined in

Definition 9.9
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is uniformly locally acyclic, if
for every
\epsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
such that any ball of radius
\delta
is acyclic inside a ball of radius
\epsilon
.

Example 9.10
.

Any compact, locally acyclic space is uniformly locally acyclic. Similarly, any locally acyclic space that admits a cocompact group action by homeomorphisms, is uniformly locally acyclic.
In particular, universal cover of a compact manifold is uniformly locally acyclic.

Any uniformly locally acyclic space is also locally acyclic. However, the converse is not true. For example, the set
\{\frac{1}{n}\}
with the subspace metric from
\mathbb{R}
is locally acyclic, but it is not uniformly locally acyclic.

Lemma 9.11
.

Let
(X,d)
be a uniformly locally
acyclic metric space. Then the space
X\times[1,\infty)
has a metric that makes the space uniformly acyclic away from
X\times\{1\}
and the map
x\mapsto(x,1)
is an isometric embedding of
X
into
X\times[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

The construction of the metric follows the one appearing in Lemma 2.2 of
[
6
]
.
Choose a continuous strictly increasing function
\phi:[1,\infty)\rightarrow[1,\infty)
with

\phi(1)=1
. Let
d
be the original metric on
X
and define a function
\rho^{\prime}
by

(1)

\rho^{\prime}((x,t),(x^{\prime},t))=\phi(t)d(x,x^{\prime})
.

(2)

\rho^{\prime}((x,t),(x,t^{\prime}))=|t-t^{\prime}|
.

We then define
\rho:(X\times[1,\infty))^{2}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
to be

\rho((x,t),(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}))=\inf\sum_{i=1}^{l}\rho^{\prime}((x_{i},t_{i}),(x_{i-1},t_{i-1}))

where the sum is over all chains

(x,t)=(x_{0},t_{0}),(x_{1},t_{1}),\ldots,(x_{l},t_{l})=(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})

and each segment is either horizontal or vertical.
Also
\phi(1)=1
implies that
X\times\{1\}
with the subspace metric is isometric to
X
via the map
(x,1)\mapsto x
.
Now we will describe a
\phi
, so that the corresponding metric
\rho
makes
X\times[1,\infty)
uniformly acyclic away from
X\times\{1\}
.
Since
X
is uniformly locally acyclic, we have an infinite positive decreasing sequence
\{r_{i}\}
with
r_{1}=1
such that for every
x\overset{}{\in}X
, the inclusions
\dots\overset{}{\subset}B_{r_{i}}^{d}(x)\overset{}{\subset}B_{r_{i-1}}^{d}(x)\overset{}{\subset}
are nullhomotopic maps.
Set
\phi(t)=\frac{1}{r_{t}}
for
t\overset{}{\in}\mathbb{N}
.
For nonintegral values of
t
, we set

\phi(t)=\phi([t])+(t-[t])\phi([t]+1)

Suppose,
N_{i}=\frac{\phi(i)}{\phi(i-1)}
.
Now we consider the ball
B^{\rho}_{k}(x,i)\overset{}{\subset}X\times[1,\infty)
.
Note that
B^{\rho}_{k}(x,i)\overset{}{\subset}B^{d}_{\frac{k}{N_{i-k}}}(x)\times[i-k,i+k]
and that
B^{\rho}_{k}(x,i)
contracts in itself to
B^{\rho}_{k}(x,i)\cap(X\times[i-k,i])\overset{}{\subset}B^{d}_{\frac{k}{N_{i-k}}}(x)\times[i-k,i]
.
Also,
B^{d}_{\frac{k}{N_{i-k-1}}}(x)\times\{i-k-1\}\overset{}{\subset}B_{k+2}^{\rho}(x,i)
.
So,
B^{\rho}_{k}(x,i)
can be contracted inside
B_{k+2}^{\rho}(x,i)
by pushing it down to
(i-k-1)
-level and contracting it there.
∎

The following gluing lemma in a slightly different form can be found in

Lemma 9.12

(
[

Let

be an isometry. Let

Proposition 9.13
.

Any uniformly acyclic, proper
n
-manifold with uniformly locally acyclic boundary admits an isometric embedding into a uniformly acyclic, proper
n
-manifold.

Proof.

Since
\partial M
is uniformly locally acyclic, Lemma
9.11
allows us to equip
\partial M\times[1,\infty)
with a metric so that it is uniformly acyclic away from
\partial M\times\{1\}
.
Let
\rho,\mu:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be two functions such that any ball
B(x,r)
in
\partial M\times[1,\infty)
is acyclic inside
B(x,\rho(r))
whenever
d(x,\partial M\times\{1\})\geq\mu(r)
.
We glue
\partial M\times[1,\infty)
to
M
along
\partial M
by the attaching map
(x,1)\mapsto x
.
Let
Y
be the resulting space.
By Lemma
9.12
, there is a proper metric on

such that the canonical maps
M\hookrightarrow Y
and
\partial M\times[1,\infty)\hookrightarrow Y

For the rest of the proof we will regard
M

\partial M\times[1,\infty)

We claim that
Y
is uniformly acyclic.
Since
M
is uniformly acyclic, there exists a function
\tau:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that, for any

in

is acyclic inside a concentric ball of radius
\tau(r)
.
Take a ball

of radius

Y
.
If
x\overset{}{\in}M
and

, then
B_{r}(x)

\partial M\times[1,\infty)

\partial M
by the construction of the metric on

. Hence,
B_{r}(x)
is acyclic inside
B_{\tau(r)}(x)
.
If
x\overset{}{\in}\partial M\times[1,\infty)
and
d(x,\partial M)\geq\mu(r)+r

B(x,r)\overset{}{\subset}\partial M\times[1,\infty)
and hence is acyclic in

.
If

\partial M\times[1,\infty)

until it lands on
\partial M\times\{1\}
.
Note that this deformation takes place inside
\partial M\times[1,\mu(r)+2r]

\partial M\times\{1\}
.
Hence the deformation takes place in a set of diameter at most
\mu(r)+2r+2r
.
Also, the deformed ball is now contained in
M
and has diameter at most
2r
and hence it is acyclic inside a set of diameter at most
\tau(2r)
by uniform acyclicity of
M
.
Hence, we conclude that
B_{r}(x)
is acyclic inside a set of diameter at most
\mu(r)+4r+\tau(2r)
.
Hence
Y
is uniformly acyclic.
∎

As a consequence of the above proposition, we get the following.

Theorem 9.14
.

If
X
is a proper, uniformly acyclic
n
-manifold with uniformly locally acyclic boundary, then
\cobdim(X)\leq n
.

Proof.

By Proposition
9.13
, we have a uniformly acyclic proper
n
-manifold
Y
such that
X
embeds isometrically in
Y
.
By Theorem
6.4
, it follows that
\cobdim(X)\leq\cobdim(Y)
.
By Corollary
9.7
, we know
\cobdim(Y)\leq n
and hence
\cobdim(X)\leq n
.
∎

Corollary 9.15
.

If

Definition 9.16

(Cocompact action dimension)
.

The
cocompact action dimension

\cadim(G)
of a group
G
is the least dimension of a contractible manifold (possibly with boundary) that admits a proper cocompact
G

Corollary 9.17
.

\cadim(G)\geq\cobdim(G)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\cadim(G)=n
.
Then
G
admits a proper, cocompact action on an acyclic
n
-manifold
M
.
Choose a point

By Milnor–Schwarz Lemma, the map
g\mapsto g.x_{0}

f:G\rightarrow M
.
Since

is acyclic and it admits a cocompact action,

is uniformly acyclic.
Similarly, since
\partial M
is locally acyclic and it admits a cocompact action, it is uniformly locally acyclic.
Since
M
is proper,
by Corollary
9.15
, we get
\cobdim(G)\leq n=\cadim(G)
.

∎

Remark 9.18
.

The action dimension
\actdim(G)

G

G
-action.
Note that

Nonetheless, we believe that

.
A naive approach to prove this might be as follows:
Suppose,

n

, sending

Furthermore, the image of

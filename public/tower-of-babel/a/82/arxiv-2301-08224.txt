On mean curvature flow translators with prescribed ends
Ao Sun, Zhihan Wang

Abstract
. Given a smooth closed embedded self-shrinker S S with index I I in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , we construct an I I -dimensional family of complete translators polynomially asymptotic to S × ℝ S\times\mathbb{R} at infinity. We further prove that ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} can be decomposed in many ways into a one-parameter family of closed sets ∐ a ∈ ℝ T a \coprod_{a\in\mathbb{R}}T_{a} , and each closed set T a T_{a} contains a complete translator asymptotic to S × ℝ S\times\mathbb{R} at infinity. If the closed set T a T_{a} fattens, namely it has nonempty interior, then there are at least two translators asymptotic to each other at an exponential rate, which can be viewed as a kind of nonuniqueness. We show that this fattening phenomenon is non-generic but indeed happens.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study translators of mean curvature flow. Let
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
be parametrized by
(x,z)
, and
z
is the last coordinate function, with
\partial_{z}
being the coordinate vector. A
translator
is a hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that

\vec{H}_{\Sigma}=\vec{v}^{\perp},

for some unit vector
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, where
\vec{H}_{\Sigma}
is the mean curvature vector of
\Sigma
and
\perp
denotes the projection onto the normal bundle of
\Sigma
. By composing with a rotation in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\vec{v}=\partial_{z}
throughout this article.

Every translator
\Sigma
is naturally associated with an eternal mean curvature flow
\{\Sigma+t\partial_{z}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. In this way, translators are natural type II singularity models of mean curvature flow, see
[
AV97
,
Ham95
]
. Various examples of mean curvature flow translators have been constructed in the literature, especially in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, see
[
Mul56
,
AW94
,
CSS07
,
Wan11
,
Ngu09
,
Ngu13
,
Ngu15
,
DDPN17
,
Smi15
,
BLT20
,
HIMW19
,
HMW19
,
HMW22
]
.
The analogs of translators also play essential roles in the study of other geometric flows. For example, there are various researches in Ricci flow that focus on the steady Ricci solitons, which is known to be the analog of mean curvature flow translators. Such solutions are important to the study of Ricci flow singularities, see the pioneering work of Hamilton
[
Ham88
]
.

Start with a translator
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of
finite entropy
(see the definition in Section
2.1
), an argument of Ilmanen
[
Ilm94
]
shows that any tangent flow at
-\infty
of the ancient mean curvature flow
\{\Sigma+t\partial_{z}\}_{t<0}
is generated by a
self-shrinker
splitting in
z
-direction (see Corollary
2.4
for a precise statement).
Here, a
self-shrinker
is a (weak) submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfying
\vec{H}=-X^{\perp}/2
. Self-shrinkers are known to be the type I singularity model for mean curvature flows and have been widely studied in recent years. Besides the simplest example of generalized round cylinders
\mathbb{S}_{\sqrt{2k}}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, more examples of self-shrinkers have been constructed, see
[
Ang92
,
Møl11
,
Ngu14
,
KKM18
,
Ket16
,
DLN18
,
SWZ20
,
KM20
,
BNS21
,
Rie22
]
. We include a brief discussion of self-shrinkers in Section

.

From this asymptotic point of view, all the examples above of translators turn out to be either of infinite entropy, or have tangent flow at infinity in the above sense to be the generalized round cylinders
\mathbb{S}^{k}_{\sqrt{2k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
, possibly with multiplicities. This brings to us the natural question of whether other self-shrinkers can be the asymptotic of some translator in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

In this paper, we construct new families of translators with prescribed ends. Throughout this paper, we will be focused on hypersurfaces, namely submanifolds with codimension
1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a closed embedded smooth self-shrinker with
index
I
. Then there exists an
I
-parameter family of complete embedded translators in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, possibly with singularities of dimension
\leq n-7
, each of which has the tangent flow at
-\infty
to be
S\times\mathbb{R}
with multiplicity one.

Recall that self-shrinkers are critical points of the Gaussian area functional
\int_{S}e^{-|x|^{2}/4}
, and the
index
of a self-shrinker
S
is defined as the dimension of the negative eigenspace of the Jacobi operator
-L_{S}:=-(\Delta_{S}-\nabla_{S}+|A_{S}|^{2}+1/2)
of the Gaussian area functional on
S
. It was proved by Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM12
]
that any closed self-shrinker in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
other than the round sphere has index
I\geq n+2
. Because the space of translations in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
acting on translators has dimension
n+1
, our construction gives at least one dimensional family of pairwise non-congruent translators when
S
is not a round sphere. On the other hand, if
S
is the round sphere, then by the work of Choi-Haslhofer-Hershkovits-White
[
CHHW22
]
, any such translator with the tangent flow at
-\infty
to be
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{\sqrt{2n-2}}\times\mathbb{R}
is a translation of the well-known bowl soliton
[
AW94
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, which is recovered from our construction.

The possible existence of low dimensional singularities in the Theorem comes from the regularity theory of minimizing hypersurface. In particular, Theorem
1.1
produces smooth translators with prescribed end in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1\leq 7}
. Hereafter in this paper, when we refer to a hypersurface, we allow for a singular set of codimension
\geq 7
unless otherwise specified.

Our main theorem also answers a question of Ilmanen. As the parabolic blow-down limit of a translator gives a corresponding self-shrinker, in
[
Ilm94
, Appendix J]
, Ilmanen asked for what class of translators and self-shrinkers, this correspondence is one-to-one. Theorem
1.1
implies that for any closed self-shrinker other than the sphere, this correspondence is not one-to-one, but multi-family-to-one.

1.1.
Translating ends

Our construction is highly motivated by the work of Chan
[
Cha97
]
to construct minimizing hypersurfaces that are asymptotic to a given strictly minimizing hypercone. In Chan’s work, she first constructed a continuous family of exterior minimal graphs over the given cone; As an analogy, we derive,

(c.f. Theorem

)

Suppose

polynomially tends to

as

\mathrm{ES}[u_{\varphi,z_{0}}]:=\{\left(\sqrt{z}(x+u(x,z)\nu_{x}),z\right):x\in S,z>z_{0}\},

is the unit ball in

Moreover, for different

A precise description of this family

As opposed to the minimal surface case where the given minimal cone itself provides a canonical exterior graph, there’s no canonical translating end asymptotic to

One technical difficulty for our construction is to study the dominated linear operator of translator-mean-curvature,

We obtain the desired asymptotic estimate by using both elliptic estimates and parabolic estimates for different transformations of this operator. Based on this, Theorem

We also find a natural

on the space of pl-simple translating end over

\Sigma_{0}
to some pl-simple end
\Sigma_{a}
lying on one side of
\Sigma_{0}
. See Lemma

. A similar one-sided deformation action for minimal hypersurfaces is mentioned in
[
Cha97
, Section 4, (2)]
. In the context of mean curvature flow, the one-sided perturbation also plays a significant role, see
[
CM12
,
CIMW13
,
CCMS20
,
SX21b
,
SX21a
]
.

1.2.
Complete translators

To go from translating ends to complete translators, we use the following variational characterization of translators. Recall that translators are critical points of the weighted area functional

\mathcal{I}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}e^{z}\ d\mathscr{H}^{n}(x,z).

Hence the translators are minimal hypersurfaces with respect to a certain noncomplete metric of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

[
Ilm94
]

Now we state our theorem on the construction of complete translators.

(c.f. Theorem

-minimizing translator exponentially asymptotic to
\Sigma_{0}

(ii)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=\coprod_{a\in\mathbb{R}}T[\varpi(a)[\Sigma_{e}]].

1.3.
Fattening phenomena

Another novel discovery in this paper is the fattening phenomenon. As in Theorem
1.3
,
T[\Sigma_{0}]
either is the support of a translator, or it has nonempty interior. We call
T[\Sigma_{0}]
in case (ii)
fattening
. We show that non-fattening is a generic phenomenon, while fattening can also happen.

Theorem 1.4

(c.f. Corollary
4.5
& Thereom
5.1
)
.

For every closed self-shrinker
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for generic pl-simple ends
\Sigma_{0}
,
T[\Sigma_{0}]
does not fattens;
On the other hand, suppose
S
is the Angenent torus
[
Ang92
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then there exists a pl-simple translating end
\Sigma_{0}
, such that
T[\Sigma_{0}]
fattens.

In the minimal surface analogy, Chan
[
Cha97
, Section 4, (1)]
asked whether the minimizing hypersurface fast asymptotic to every exterior minimal graph is unique or not. Our Theorem
1.4
provides a negative answer to the analogous of this question for translators.

In our construction, the fattening shows up because of the topology gap. More precisely, we find explicit examples in the one-parameter family of translators in Theorem
1.3
with different topology types. This implies that there must be a jump in this one parameter family which corresponds to fattening. Therefore, the topology of self-shrinkers seems to be an important factor in yielding fattening.

We adapt the terminology “fattening” from the study of level set flow. Level set flow is a weak formulation of mean curvature flow. Each time slice of the mean curvature flow is viewed as the nodal set of the level set flow function, and the evolution of mean curvature flow is non-unique if the nodal set fattens, namely it has nonempty interior.

There are several similarities between the fattening of level set flow and the fattening phenomenon in this paper. First, both fattening phenomena lead to the nonuniqueness of solutions. Second, both fattening phenomena seem to be the consequences of topological factors. For the level set flow, there are a few plausible examples of fattenings
[
AIC95
,
Whi02
]
, and all the examples rely on some topological arguments. In the proof of Theorem
1.4
, the topology of the Angenent torus also plays an essential role. Third, the fattening phenomena rarely happen. For the level set flow, it is known that fattening can only happen for a countably many level sets, hence it is not generic, see
[
Ilm94
, 11.4]
. We prove that the fattening can only happen for a meager set of ends, c.f. Corollary
4.5
.

Theorem
1.3
and
1.4
provide a new perspective to understanding the fattening of mean curvature flow.

2.1.
Mean curvature flow and entropy

Let
N\geq n>0
be integers,
J\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval. A family of
n
-dimensional submanifolds
\{M_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\}_{t\in J}
is
flowing by mean curvature
if they satisfy

\left(\partial_{t}X(t)\right)^{\perp}=\vec{H}_{M_{t}}.

Here
X(t)
is the position vector of
M_{t}
,
\perp
means projection onto the normal bundle of
M_{t}
.

In this paper, we will be focused on the asymptotics of a mean curvature flow at
-\infty
time. In order to study the asymptotics of mean curvature flow, Huisken
[
Hui90
]
introduced the

. Suppose
\{M_{t}\}
is defined for

, then we can define a new flow
\tilde{M}_{\tau}:=e^{\tau/2}\cdot M_{-e^{-\tau}}
for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, satisfying the equation

(2.1)

(\partial_{\tau}\tilde{X})^{\perp}=\vec{H}_{\tilde{M}_{\tau}}+\frac{\tilde{X}(\tau)^{\perp}}{2}\,.

Recall that
Gaussian area
of a
n
-dimensional submanifold

is defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}[S]:=\sqrt{4\pi}^{-n}\int_{S}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}}\ d\mathscr{H}^{n}.

Huisken showed that any RMCF

is a gradient flow of

is non-increasing in

\vec{H}_{S}+\frac{X^{\perp}}{2}=0\ .

Let

[

]

\delta^{2}\mathcal{F}[S](u,u)=C_{N}\cdot\int_{S}\left(|\nabla_{S}u|^{2}-\left(|A_{S}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)u^{2}\right)e^{-|x|^{2}/4}\ d\mathscr{H}^{N-1}(x),

for some constant

with respect to the Gaussian

norm is the

of

(2.3)

\displaystyle L_{S}:=\Delta_{S}-\frac{X}{2}\cdot\nabla_{S}+|A_{S}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,.

For a general submanifold

is called the

of

Colding-Minicozzi

By Huisken’s monotonicity formula, if

\phi\geq 0
,

\limsup_{s\to t}\frac{\mu_{s}(\phi)-\mu_{t}(\phi)}{s-t}\leq\int(-\phi H^{2}+\nabla^{\bot}\phi\cdot\vec{H})d\mu_{t},

where
\vec{H}
is the mean curvature vector of
\mu_{t}
whenever
\mu_{t}
is rectifiable and has
L^{2}
-mean curvature in the varifold sense, otherwise the right-hand side is defined to be
-\infty
. In this paper, we only focus on
integral Brakke motion
, i.e.
\mu_{t}
are associated Radon measure of integral varifolds in
\mathcal{I}\mathcal{V}_{n}(\Omega)
for a.e.
t
. The
support
of a Brakke motion is a closed subset of spacetime given by,

\text{spt}(\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}):=\text{Clos}\Big(\bigcup_{t\geq 0}\text{spt}(\mu_{t})\Big)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}.

We call a sequence of flow
\{\mu_{t}^{j}\}_{t\geq 0}
converges to
\{\mu_{t}^{\infty}\}_{t\geq 0}

in the Brakke sense
, if for all but countably many
t
,
\mu^{j}_{t}
measure-converges to
\mu^{\infty}_{t}
. Brakke
[
Bra78
]
proved that any sequence of integral Brakke motion with uniformly bounded area has a converging subsequence in the Brakke sense. And if the flow converges in the Brakke sense, then by Huisken’s Monotonicity and avoidance principle, the support of the flow also converges locally in the Hausdorff distant sense.

n
-varifolds are generalizations of submanifolds, so it is natural to define translation and dilation of a varifold
V
. For any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)
, we denote for simplicity
(V-x_{0})/t_{0}
to be the push forward of
V\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{V}_{n}(\Omega)
by
\eta_{x_{0},t_{0}}
, in other words, for any measurable set
E\subset((\Omega-x_{0})/t_{0})\times\mathbf{Gr}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\left((V-x_{0})/t_{0})\right)(E):=(\eta_{x_{0},t_{0}}{}_{\sharp}V)(E)=t_{0}^{-n}\cdot V((\eta_{x_{0},t_{0}}^{-1}\times id_{\mathbf{Gr}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})(E)).

The definition of the Gaussian area
\mathcal{F}
and the entropy
\lambda
are also naturally extended to varifolds. Moreover, the entropy is lower semi-continuous under varifold convergence.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of integral
n
-varifolds in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
V_{j}

\mathbf{F}
-converges to a integral
n
-varifold
V_{\infty}
as
j\to\infty
. Then

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\lambda[V_{j}]\geq\lambda[V_{\infty}].

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}[(V_{j}-x_{0})/t_{0}]\geq\mathcal{F}[(V_{\infty}-x_{0})/t_{0}]
. Up to a translation and dilation, we only need to show
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}[V_{j}]\geq\mathcal{F}[V_{\infty}]
. This is straightforward from the lower semi-continuity of the integrals over varifolds.
∎

In general, the varifolds may not be smooth, and
\mathbf{F}
-convergence can be complicated. Thanks to the following Brakke-White’s regularity theorem, we have a better characterization of convergence if the flows are regular.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
Whi05
]
)
.

Suppose
\epsilon>0
,
\{\Sigma_{t}^{j}\}_{t\in(0,\epsilon)}
is a sequence of smooth mean curvature flow in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
converging to a smooth mean curvature flow
\{\Sigma_{t}^{\infty}\}_{t\in(0,\epsilon)}
with multiplicity
1
in the Brakke sense. Then the convergence is in
C_{loc}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\epsilon))
.

2.3.
Translators

Now we restrict our attention to translators. Let
N\geq n>0
be integers; parametrize
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
by
(x,z)
. We may view
z
to be the last coordinate function, with
\partial_{z}
being the coordinate vector. We may also abuse the notation to view
\partial_{z}=(\mathbf{0},1)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
. Throughout this paper, we only consider translators moving in the
\partial_{z}
direction.

A
translator
, or a translating soliton, is a submanifold
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
satisfying

(2.5)

\displaystyle\vec{H}_{\Sigma}-\partial_{z}^{\perp}=0\,.

Motivated by this equation,
\vec{H}_{\Sigma}-\partial_{z}^{\perp}
is also called the
translator-mean-curvature vector
for a general submanifold
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. Translators are named due to the following fact:
\Sigma
is a translator if and only if
\{\Sigma+t\partial_{z}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a mean curvature flow in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
.

There is a variational characterization of translators introduced by Ilamnen
[
Ilm94
]
. For
\epsilon>0
and an
n
-dimensional submanifold (possibly with boundary)
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
, define

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\epsilon}[\Sigma]:=\int_{\Sigma}e^{z/\epsilon}\ d\mathscr{H}^{n}(x,z)\,.

For simplicity, we write
\mathcal{I}:=\mathcal{I}^{1}
. Clearly, this functional is invariant under translations of submanifolds in
x
-directions. Under rescalings, we have
\mathcal{I}[\Sigma]=\epsilon^{-n}\mathcal{I}^{\epsilon}[\epsilon\cdot\Sigma]
. By
[
Ilm94
]
, critical points of
\mathcal{I}
are translators. (Note that our functional
\mathcal{I}
differs from the one of Ilmanen by a sign in
z
, which corresponds to the opposite translating direction.)
It’s also clear from (
2.6
) that translators are minimal submanifolds under the conformal metric
g=e^{2z/n}g_{\text{Euc}}
, and the translator-mean-curvature vector is also proportional to the mean curvature vector under
g
. In particular, the strong maximum principle of Solomon-White
[
SW89
]
and Ilmanen
[
Ilm96
]
also applies to translators.

It is not hard to see that a complete translator can not be compact. In fact, given a complete translator
\Sigma
, for sufficiently large
z_{0}>>0
,
\Sigma\cap\{z=z_{0}\}\not=\emptyset
. To understand the asymptotic behavior of
\Sigma
as
z\to+\infty
, we need a blow-down analysis. For
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we let

(2.7)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau):=e^{\tau/2}\cdot(\Sigma-e^{-\tau}\partial_{z})\,,

Recall that by
[
CIMW13
]
,
\{\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)\}_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}
solves the RMCF equation,

(2.8)

\displaystyle(\partial_{\tau}\tilde{X})^{\perp}=\vec{H}_{\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)}+\frac{\tilde{X}(\tau)^{\perp}}{2}\,.

The following lemma is proved in
[
Ilm94
]
, which shows that the blow-down limit of translators splits in
\mathbb{R}_{z}
-direction.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open subset;
\epsilon_{j}\searrow 0
,
R_{j}\epsilon_{j}\nearrow+\infty
be sequences of constants; Let
V_{j}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{V}_{n}(\Omega\times(-R_{j},0))
be stationary with respect to
\mathcal{I}^{\epsilon_{j}}
,
1\leq j<+\infty
. Suppose when
j\to\infty
,
\{V_{j}(t):=V_{j}+(t/\epsilon_{j})\partial_{z}\}_{-R_{j}\epsilon_{j}<t<0}
converges to
\{V_{\infty}(t)\}_{t<0}
in Brakke sense.
Then
V_{\infty}(t)
splits in
\mathbb{R}_{z}
-direction for all but countably many
t<0
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially presented in
[
Ilm94
, 8.8]
.
∎

By Huisken’s monotonicity formula, the Gaussian area is monotone non-increasing along the RMCF
\{\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)\}_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}
, and if

\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\mathcal{F}[\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)]<+\infty\,,

then any subsequent
\mathbf{F}
-limit of
\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)
when
\tau\to-\infty
will be a self-shrinker in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
, known as a
tangent flow
of
\{\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)\}
(or simply, of
\Sigma
if there’s no confusion) at
-\infty
. As a corollary of Lemma
2.3
, we have,

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
is an
n
-dimensional translator with finite entropy. Then any tangent flow
\Gamma\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{V}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
of
\Sigma
at
-\infty
is a self-shrinker splitting in
z
-direction, in other words, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Gamma+\alpha\partial_{z}=\Gamma
. Moreover,
\lambda[\Sigma]=\mathcal{F}[\Gamma]
.

Proof.

The tangent flow being splitting follows directly from Lemma
2.3
. To compute the entropy of
\Sigma
, first by Proposition
2.1
and translation dilation invariance of the entropy,
\lambda[\Sigma]\geq\lambda[\Gamma]=\mathcal{F}[\Gamma]
. On the other hand, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
s>0
, by Huisken’s monotonicity formula,

\mathcal{F}[t_{0}^{-1}(\Sigma-x_{0})]\leq\mathcal{F}[(t_{0}^{2}+s)^{-1/2}(\Sigma-s\partial_{z}-x_{0})]

Now suppose
\Gamma
is the limit of
s_{i}^{-1/2}\cdot(\Sigma-s_{i}\partial_{z})
for a sequence
s_{i}\nearrow+\infty
. Then taking
s=s_{i}-t_{0}^{2}
and notice that
s_{i}^{-1/2}x_{0}\searrow 0
,
s_{i}^{-1/2}t_{0}^{2}\searrow 0
and the following non-concentration of
\mathcal{F}
near infinity,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\nearrow+\infty}\ \limsup_{\tau\to-\infty}\ \mathcal{F}[\tilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}]=0,

we see that
\mathcal{F}[t_{0}^{-1}(\Sigma-x_{0})]\leq\mathcal{F}[\Gamma]
. This implies that
\lambda[\Sigma]\leq\mathcal{F}[\Gamma]
. Hence
\lambda[\Sigma]=\mathcal{F}[\Gamma]
.

To prove (
2.9
), notice that for every
R>1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\tilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}]

\displaystyle=R^{n}\int_{(\tilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}/R)\setminus\mathbb{B}_{1}}(4\pi)^{-n/2}e^{-|y|^{2}(R^{2}-1)/4}\cdot e^{-|y|^{2}/4}\ d\mathscr{H}^{n}(y)

\displaystyle\leq R^{n}e^{-(R^{2}-1)/4}\cdot\mathcal{F}[\tilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}/R]\ \ \leq R^{n}e^{-(R^{2}-1)/4}\cdot\lambda[\Sigma],

where the RHS does not depend on
\tau
and tends to
0
as
R\nearrow+\infty
.
∎

In application, we shall also compute the entropy of translators with simple ends. The following Lemma guarantees that Corollary
2.4
applies in this case.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
is a translator with unique tangent flow at
-\infty
to be
S\times\mathbb{R}
, where
S
is a smooth closed self-shrinker. Then
\lambda[\Sigma]<+\infty
.

Proof.

Because
S\times\mathbb{R}
is the unique tangent flow at
-\infty
, by Brakke-White regularity Theorem
2.2
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\tau_{0}>0
such that for
\tau>\tau_{0}
,
\tau^{-1/2}\Sigma\cap\{z=\tau^{1/2}\}
is a graph of function
v_{\tau}
over
S\times\{\tau^{1/2}\}
inside
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\{\tau^{1/2}\}
, with
\|v_{\tau}\|_{C^{2}}\leq\epsilon
. We denote by
\widetilde{\Sigma}:=\Sigma\cap\{z\geq 2\tau_{0}\}
, and
\Sigma_{\tau}:=\Sigma\cap\{z=\tau\}
.

By the definition of
\lambda
, it is straightforward to check that for two hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
,
\lambda[\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}]\leq\lambda[\Sigma_{1}]+\lambda[\Sigma_{2}]
. So it suffices to show
\lambda\left[\ \overline{\Sigma\backslash\widetilde{\Sigma}}\ \right]<+\infty
and
\lambda[\widetilde{\Sigma}]<+\infty
. Because
\overline{\Sigma\backslash\widetilde{\Sigma}}
is compact, by
[
CM12
, Lemma 7.2]
,
\lambda\left[\ \overline{\Sigma\backslash\widetilde{\Sigma}}\ \right]<+\infty
. To prove the claim, it suffices to show
\lambda[\widetilde{\Sigma}]<+\infty
.

From
[
BS18
, Section 2]
, if
\epsilon
is chosen sufficiently small, when
\tau\geq 2\tau_{0}
,
\lambda[\Sigma_{\tau}]<\lambda[S]+1
, where
\Sigma_{\tau}
is viewed as a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
t_{0}>0
,

\begin{split}&\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}(4\pi t_{0})^{-{N-1}/2}e^{-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}+(z-z_{0})^{2}}{4t_{0}}}d\mathcal{H}^{N}(x,z)\\
&=\int_{\tau=2\tau_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}(4\pi t_{0})^{-{N-1}/2}e^{\frac{-|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{4t_{0}}}\frac{1}{|\nabla_{\Sigma}z|}d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)e^{-\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}}{4t_{0}}}d\mathcal{H}^{1}(\tau)\\
&\leq 2(\lambda[S]+1)\int_{\tau=2\tau_{0}}^{\infty}(4\pi t_{0})^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}}{4t_{0}}}d\mathcal{H}^{1}(\tau)\leq 2(\lambda[S]+1).\end{split}

Here we use the coarea formula, and we notice that
|\nabla_{\Sigma}z|^{2}=|(e_{z})^{\top}|^{2}=1-|(e_{z})^{\bot}|^{2}=1-H^{2}
, and when
\tau_{0}
is chosen sufficiently large,
\widetilde{\Sigma}
has
H<\sqrt{4/3}
, which yields
|\nabla_{\Sigma}z|^{-1}\leq 2
. This concludes that
\lambda[\widetilde{\Sigma}]<+\infty
. ∎

In view of Corollary
2.4
, an interesting question is whether tangent flows at
-\infty
of a general translator is unique or not. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the uniqueness of cylindrical tangent flow for mean curvature flow is still widely open. Only few special cases are proved, see
[
CM15
,
Zhu20
]
. On the other hand, in view of the work of Simon
[
Sim89
]
on minimal graphs, it’s plausible to conjecture that tangent flows at
-\infty
of a general translator is unique, provided one of the tangent flow is a smooth closed or asymptotic conic self-shrinker
\times\mathbb{R}
with multiplicity
1
.

3.
Moduli Space of pl-Simple Translating End

Hereafter in this paper, we only work in codimension
1
case, though many of the discussions also work in higher codimensions.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a self-shrinker,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
;
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{>z_{0}}
be a properly embedded hypersurface. We call
\Sigma
a
translating end
over
m|S\times\mathbb{R}_{+}|
if
\Sigma
satisfies (
2.5
) and when
R\to\infty
,
|R^{-1/2}(\Sigma-R\partial_{z})|

\mathbf{F}
-converges to
m|S\times\mathbb{R}|
.

We call
\Sigma
a
simple end
over
S\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
if
m=1
.

In the present paper, we shall only deal with smooth closed self-shrinkers
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The abundance of such self-shrinkers has been established by
[
Ang92
,
Møl11
,
DLN18
,
KM20
,
Rie22
]
. We may discuss the case of asymptotic conic self-shrinkers in future works.

Given a self-shrinker
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with unit normal
\nu=\nu_{S}
, let
\mathrm{ES}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be the
auxiliary end
given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ES}:=\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:z>0,x/\sqrt{z}\in S\}

We shall parametrize
\mathrm{ES}
by
\Phi:S\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathrm{ES}
,
(x,z)\mapsto(\sqrt{z}x,z)
. More generally, for
z_{0}>0
and
u\in C^{1}_{loc}(S\times\mathbb{R}_{>z_{0}})
, define

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{u}:S\times\mathbb{R}_{>z_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\ \ (x,z)\mapsto(\sqrt{z}(x+u(x,z)\nu_{x}),z).

And denote for simplicity
\mathrm{ES}[u]:=\Phi_{u}(S\times\mathbb{R}_{>z_{0}})
. Clearly,
\mathrm{ES}=\mathrm{ES}[\mathbf{0}]
and
\Phi=\Phi_{\mathbf{0}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth closed self-shrinker. Then a translating end
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{>z_{0}}
is a simple end over
S\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
if and only if there exists
z_{0}^{\prime}>1
and
u\in C^{1}(S\times\mathbb{R}_{>z_{0}^{\prime}})
such that
\Sigma\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{>z_{0}^{\prime}}=\mathrm{ES}[u]
, and that when
R\to+\infty
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{S\times[R,2R]}\left(|u|+|\nabla u|+R|\partial_{z}u|\right)\to 0\,,

where
\nabla
denote the gradient in
S
direction.

Proof.

The proof is basically by changing coordinates and applying White’s
\epsilon
-regularity
[
Whi05
]
.
First recall that since
\Sigma
satisfies the translator equation (
2.5
), we have
\{\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau):=e^{\tau/2}(\Sigma-e^{-\tau}\partial_{z})\}
is a RMCF.

Suppose that
\Sigma
is a simple end over
S\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, then by White’s
\epsilon
-regularity
[
Whi05
]
, when
\tau\to-\infty
,
\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)

C^{\infty}_{loc}
-converges to
S\times\mathbb{R}
. Hence for
\tau\leq\tau_{0}<<-1
, we can write

\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[-1,1]=\mathrm{graph}_{S\times\mathbb{R}}(\tilde{u}(\cdot,\tau)),

for some
\tilde{u}\in C^{2}(S\times[-1,1]\times(-\infty,\tau_{0}))
and
\tilde{u}(\cdot,\cdot+\tau)

C^{2}
-converges to
\mathbf{0}
as
\tau\to-\infty
. Therefore when
\tau<<-1
, we have
\Sigma\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq e^{-\tau}}=\mathrm{ES}[u]
, where

u(x,e^{-\tau}):=\tilde{u}(x,0;\tau).

The desired estimates (
3.3
) then follows from the
C^{1}
-estimates on
\tilde{u}
.

Conversely, suppose
\Sigma=\mathrm{ES}[u]
for some
u\in C^{2}(S\times\mathbb{R}_{>z_{0}^{\prime}})
with estimate (
3.3
), then for each
\tau\leq-\log z_{0}^{\prime}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)=\left\{\left(\sqrt{1+e^{\tau/2}\hat{z}}\cdot(x+u(x,e^{-\tau}+e^{-\tau/2}\hat{z})\nu_{x}),\hat{z}\right):\hat{z}\in[e^{\tau/2}z_{0}^{\prime}-e^{-\tau/2},+\infty)\right\}.

To rewrite such
\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)
as a graph over
S\times\mathbb{R}
, we introduce the following notation. By Lemma
B.1
, there exists some
0<\vartheta_{S}<<1
such that for every
u\in C^{1}(S\times[0,1])
with
\|u\|_{C^{1}}\leq\vartheta_{S}
, there’s a unique
\check{u}\in C^{1}(S\times[0,1]\times(1-\vartheta_{S},1+\vartheta_{S}))
such that for every
z\in[0,1]
and every
|a-1|<\vartheta_{S}
, we have

\mathrm{graph}_{S}(\check{u}(\cdot,z,a))=a\cdot\mathrm{graph}_{S}(u(\cdot,z)),

and estimates,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\partial_{a}\check{u}\|_{C^{0},S\times[0,1]}&\leq C_{S},\\
\|\check{u}(\cdot,\cdot,a)\|_{C^{1},S\times[0,1]}&\leq C_{S}(\|u\|_{C^{1},S\times[0,1]}+|a-1|),\ \ \ \ \ \forall|a-1|<\vartheta_{S}.\end{split}

With this notation, for each
\tau<<-\log z_{0}
and each
0<L<e^{-\tau/2}/4
,

\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[-L,L]=\mathrm{graph}_{S\times\mathbb{R}}(\tilde{u}(\cdot,\tau)),

for some

\tilde{u}(x,\hat{z},\tau)=\check{v}(x,\hat{z},\sqrt{1+e^{\tau/2}\hat{z}}),

where
v(x,\hat{z}):=u(x,e^{-\tau/2}\hat{z}+e^{-\tau})
. Then (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) together shows that
\tilde{u}(\cdot,\cdot,\tau)

C^{1}_{loc}
-converges to
0
as
\tau\to-\infty
.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

We call
\mathrm{ES}[u]\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
a
polynomially asymptotic simple translating end
(or simply a
pl-simple end
) over
S\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
if it is a simple end over
S\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

\limsup_{R\to+\infty}\|u(\cdot,R)\|_{C^{0}(S)}\cdot R^{\epsilon}<+\infty.

Remark 3.4
.

Although we only require
C^{0}
-polynomial decay in the definition above, by the proof of Lemma
3.2
, classical parabolic regularity theory could upgrade this to a polynomial decay in the
C^{2}_{\star}
-norm, where

\|u\|_{C^{2}_{\star},S,R}:=\sup_{S\times[R,2R]}\left(|u|+|\nabla u|+R|\partial_{z}u|+|\nabla^{2}u|+\sqrt{R}|\partial_{z}\nabla u|+R|\partial^{2}_{z}u|\right).

Remark 3.5
.

It’s interesting to see whether every simple translating end over a smooth closed self-shrinker
S
is pl-simple. It is expected so if
S
is
integrable
, i.e. every element in
\mathrm{Ker}L_{S}
is induced by a continuous family of nearby self-shrinker.

Example 3.6
.

Recall by
[
AW94
,
CSS07
]
, the rotationally symmetric bowl soliton in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1\geq 3}
with a tip at the origin is given by
\Sigma:=\{(x,F(x)):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
, where

F(x)=\frac{|x|^{2}}{2(n-1)}-\log|x|+O(|x|^{-1}),

as
x\to\infty
.
Let
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, then
\Sigma_{a,b}:=\Sigma+(a,b)
is also a translator in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and when
z>>1
,
\Sigma_{a,b}\cap\mathbb{R}_{>z}=\mathrm{ES}[U_{a,b}]\cap\mathbb{R}_{>z}
, where

\displaystyle U_{a,b}(x,z)=\begin{cases}O(z^{-1}\log z),&\ \text{ if }a=\mathbf{0},\\
O(z^{-1/2}),&\ \text{ if }a\neq\mathbf{0}.\end{cases}

In particular, the translations of bowl solitons in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
are all pl-simple ends over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{\sqrt{2(n-1)}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

The goal for this section is to study the space of pl-simple translating ends over
S\times\mathbb{R}
, where
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a fixed closed self-shrinker. First recall (
2.3
),

L_{S}:=\Delta_{S}-\frac{X}{2}\cdot\nabla_{S}+|A_{S}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}

is the Jacobi operator of Gaussian energy on
S
, where
X
is the position vector. Let
\Gamma(S):=\{\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\dots\nearrow+\infty\}
be the set of eigenvalues of
-L_{S}
;
E_{j}
be the corresponding eigensubspace of
\mu_{j}
. Let

\mu^{-}=\mu^{-}(S):=\sup\left(\Gamma(S)\cap(-\infty,0)\right).

Note that by
[
CM12
]
,
-1,-1/2\in\Gamma(S)
, hence
\mu_{1}\leq-1
and
\mu^{-}\in[-1/2,0)
.

By Appendix
A
, the EL operator of
\mathcal{I}
-functional at
\mathrm{ES}[u]
is given by

(3.6)

Theorem 3.7
.

Given a closed self-shrinker

, let

Then there exists a constant

such that for every

, there’s a continuous family
\{u_{\varphi,z_{0}}\}_{\varphi\in\mathbb{B}^{I}}
of smooth functions on

such that for every
\varphi\in\mathbb{B}^{I}
,

(i)

\mathscr{T}(u_{\varphi,z_{0}})=0
on
S\times\mathbb{R}_{>z_{0}}
, and for every

,

\|u_{\varphi,z_{0}}\|_{C^{2}_{\star},S,R}\leq R^{\mu^{-}/2};

In particular,
\mathrm{ES}[u_{\varphi,z_{0}}]
is a pl-simple translating end over
S\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
;

(ii)

If

, then,

\displaystyle C(S,z_{0})^{-1}\|\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{I}}\leq\sup_{R\geq z_{0}}\|u_{\varphi,z_{0}}-u_{\varphi^{\prime},z_{0}}\|_{C^{2}_{\star},S,R}\cdot R^{-\mu^{-}/2}\leq C(S,z_{0})\|\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{I}}

\limsup_{z\to+\infty}z^{-\mu_{1}}\cdot\|(u_{\varphi,z_{0}}-u_{\varphi^{\prime},z_{0}})(\cdot,z)\|_{C^{0}(S)}>0.

(iii)

If

is another pl-simple translating end over

, then for every

, there exists

Moreover, if there’s a Lie Group

.

Let

where

, and by (

),

satisfies the estimates,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\xi\cdot\tilde{B}_{1,R}+R^{-1}\hat{z}^{-1}\eta\cdot\tilde{B}_{2,R}&\geq\frac{\lambda}{4}(|\xi|^{2}+R^{-1}\eta^{2})-\Lambda p^{2},\\
|\tilde{B}_{1,R}|+\sqrt{R}^{-1}|\tilde{B}_{2,R}|+|\tilde{B}_{3,R}|&\leq 4\Lambda(|\xi|+\sqrt{R}^{-1}|\eta|+|p|);\end{split}

Let

,
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(1,4)
be a non-negative cut-off function;
k:=\|f\|_{L^{\infty},S\times(R,4R)}
,
\bar{u}:=\sup\{u,0\}+k
. Multiplying the equation by

and taking integration by part provides,

\displaystyle\int_{S\times(1,4)}|\nabla_{S}(\bar{u}^{(p+1)/2}\phi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S\times(1,4)}C(\lambda,\Lambda)(1+\beta^{2})(\phi^{2}+\dot{\phi}^{2})\bar{u}^{p}(\bar{u}+|f_{R}|)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S\times(1,4)}C(\lambda,\Lambda)(1+\beta^{2})(\phi^{2}+\dot{\phi}^{2})\bar{u}^{p+1}.

Then apply the Sobolev inequality on

and run the Moser iteration
[
GT01
]
to get

Then scale back gives the upper bound for
u
.
∎

3.3.
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate

We first derive a long term
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate, which is governed by almost parabolicity of
T_{S}
when

is large. For

and

norm for functions on

by,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}[f]_{\alpha;S,R}^{\star}:=&\ \sup\left\{\frac{|f(x,z)-f(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}+R^{-\alpha/2}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}:(x,z),(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in S\times[R,2R]\right\};\\
\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\star};S,R}:=&\ \sup_{S\times[R,2R]}|f|+[f]_{\alpha;S,R}^{\star};\\
\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\star},S,R}:=&\ \Big(\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\star};S,R}+\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\star};S,R}+R\|\partial_{z}u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\star};S,R}\\
&\ +\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\star};S,R}+\sqrt{R}\|\partial_{z}\nabla u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\star};S,R}+R\|\partial_{z}^{2}u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\star};S,R}\Big).\end{split}

Since
S
is a fixed self-shrinker, we may omit the subscript
S
for sake of notations.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
u
be solving
(\partial_{z}^{2}+\partial_{z}+z^{-1}L_{S})u=f
on

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\star},S,3R}\leq C(S,\alpha)\left(\|u\|_{C^{0},S\times(R,8R)}+\sup_{R<\rho<4R}\rho\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\star},S,\rho}\right).

Proof.

Let
u_{R}(\tau,x,\hat{z}):=u(x,R\tau+\sqrt{R}\hat{z})
be defined on
\Omega:=(1,7)\times S\times(0,4)
. Then
u_{R}
solves

\partial_{\tau}u_{R}+\partial_{\hat{z}}^{2}u_{R}+\frac{1}{\tau+\hat{z}/\sqrt{R}}L_{S}u_{R}=Rf_{R},

on
\Omega
. Let
\Omega^{\prime}:=(1,6)\times S\times(1,3)
. By interior parabolic Schauder estimate
[
Kne80
]
we have,

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|u_{R}\|_{2+\alpha,\Omega^{\prime}}^{*}\leq C(S)(\|u_{R}\|_{C^{0},\Omega}+\|Rf_{R}\|^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega}),

where the Hölder norm
\|\cdot\|^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega^{\prime}}
is taking difference only in space, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|v\|^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\|v\|_{C^{0},\Omega^{\prime}}+\sup_{(\tau,x,\hat{z})\neq(\tau,x^{\prime},\hat{z}^{\prime})\in\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{|v(\tau,x,\hat{z})-v(\tau,x^{\prime},\hat{z}^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}+|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}};

\displaystyle\|v\|^{*}_{2+\alpha,\Omega^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\|v\|^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega^{\prime}}+\|\nabla_{S\times\mathbb{R}}v\|^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega^{\prime}}+\|\nabla^{2}_{S\times\mathbb{R}}v\|^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega^{\prime}}+\|\partial_{\tau}v\|^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega^{\prime}}.

It’s easy to check that,

\displaystyle\|u_{R}\|^{*}_{2+\alpha,\Omega^{\prime}}\geq\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\star},S,3R}\,,

\displaystyle\|f_{R}\|^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega}\leq\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\star},S\times[R,8R]}.

Therefore, the Lemma follows immediately from (
3.21
).
∎

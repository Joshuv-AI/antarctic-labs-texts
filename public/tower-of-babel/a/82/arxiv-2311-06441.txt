Global dynamics of epidemic network models via construction of Lyapunov functions
Rachidi B. Salako, and, Yixiang Wu

Abstract
In this paper, we study the global dynamics of epidemic network models with standard incidence or mass-action transmission mechanism, when the dispersal of either the susceptible or the infected people is controlled. The connectivity matrix of the model is not assumed to be symmetric. Our main technique to study the global dynamics is to construct novel Lyapunov type functions.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we study the global dynamics of the following Susceptible-Infected-Susceptible (SIS) epidemic network (patch) model

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle S_{i}^{\prime}&=d_{S}\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Omega,j\neq i}(L_{ij}S_{j}-L_{ji}S_{i})-f_{i}(S_{i},I_{i})+\gamma_{i}I_{i},&i\in\Omega,\ t>0,\cr\displaystyle I_{i}^{\prime}&=d_{I}\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Omega,j\neq i}(L_{ij}I_{j}-L_{ji}I_{i})+f_{i}(S_{i},I_{i})-\gamma_{i}I_{i},&i\in\Omega,\ t>0,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\bm{S}=(S_{1},\dots,S_{n})
and
\bm{I}=(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})
denote the number of susceptible and infected people living in
n-
patches (e.g., cities, countries, etc.) respectively;
n\geq 2
is the number of patches;
\Omega=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
is the collection of patch labels;
\gamma_{i}
is the disease recovery rate; and
f_{i}(S_{i},I_{i})
describes the interaction of susceptible and infected people.
The coefficient
L_{ij}
,
j\neq i
, is the degree of movement of individuals from patch
j
to
i
, and
d_{S}
and
d_{I}
are the dispersal rates of susceptible and infected people, respectively. For simplicity, let
L_{ii}=-\sum_{j\in\Omega,j\neq i}L_{ji}
, which is the total degree of movement out from patch
i
. The matrix
\mathcal{L}=(L_{ij})
is called the connectivity matrix, which will
not
be assumed to be symmetric. We can rewrite model (
1.1
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle S_{i}^{\prime}&=d_{S}\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Omega}L_{ij}S_{j}-f_{i}(S_{i},I_{i})+\gamma_{i}I_{i},&i\in\Omega,\ t>0,\cr\displaystyle I_{i}^{\prime}&=d_{I}\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Omega}L_{ij}I_{j}+f_{i}(S_{i},I_{i})-\gamma_{i}I_{i},&i\in\Omega,\ t>0.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

Adding up all the equations in (
1.1
), we obtain
\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i\in\Omega}(S_{i}+I_{i})=0
. This means that the total population
N:=\sum_{i\in\Omega}(S_{i}+I_{i})
remains a constant for all
t\geq 0
.

Model (
1.1
) when
d_{S}>0
and
d_{I}>0
has been studied recently in order to understand the joint impact of environmental heterogeneity and population movement on disease transmission. In
[
1
]
, Allen
et al.
studied (
1.1
) with standard incidence transmission mechanism
f_{i}=\beta_{i}S_{i}I_{i}/(S_{i}+I_{i})
(
\beta_{i}
is called the disease transmission rate) and symmetric connectivity matrix
\mathcal{L}
. In particular, the authors defined a basic reproduction number
\mathcal{R}_{0}
and showed that
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is a threshold parameter: the solution converges to the disease free equilibrium if
\mathcal{R}_{0}<1
and the model has a unique endemic equilibrium (EE) (i.e. positive equilibrium) if
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1
. Moreover, they showed that the disease component of the EE converges to zero if
d_{S}\to 0
when
\beta_{i}-\gamma_{i}
changes sign for
i\in\Omega
. Biologically, this result indicates that one may control the disease by limiting the movement of susceptible people. In contrast, Li and Peng
[
11
]
show that one may not eliminate the disease by limiting the movement rate of infected people
d_{I}
. In
[
4
]
, Chen
et al.
show that the above results remain true if the connectivity matrix
\mathcal{L}=(L_{ij})
is not symmetric. Model (
1.1
) with the mass-action incidence transmission mechanism
f_{i}=\beta_{i}S_{i}I_{i}
has also received some attention recently (see
[
12
]
for symmetric
\mathcal{L}
and
[
16
]
for asymmetric
\mathcal{L}
). These results show that whether the disease can be eliminated by limiting
d_{S}
is depending on the total population size
N
and the infected people may concentrate on the patches of the highest risk if
d_{I}\to 0
. Moreover, it has been shown in
[
16
]
that the model with mass-action incidence transmission mechanism may have multiple EE solutions when
\mathcal{R}_{0}<1
. Therefore, the predictions on the consequences of disease control by limiting population movement are depending on the chosen transmission mechanism, the population size, and the control strategy. We remark that Gao
et al.

[
6
,
7
]
studied the impact of population movement on the number of infected cases using model (
1.1
) with the standard incidence transmission mechanism, and
the related reaction-diffusion models have attracted many research interests
(see, e.g.,
[
2
,
13
,
19
,
3
]
).

The global dynamics of epidemic network models have attracted tremendous research interests recently. In particular, a graph theoretical method based on Kirkland’s matrix tree theorem to construct Lyapunov functions for network models has been proposed in
[
8
,
14
]
. The idea of this method is to take a weighted combination of the Lyapunov functions for the decoupled local models (i.e., the system without dispersal), and the weights are determined by a Laplacian matrix that is related to the network structure.
This method has been applied to obtain the global stability of positive equilibrium for many biological network models (see, e.g.,
[
18
,
20
,
17
,
9
]
).

The main objective of the current paper is to investigate the global dynamics of model (
1.1
) with
d_{S}=0
or
d_{I}=0
. As pointed out in the recent work
[
15
]
on the reaction-diffusion counterpart of model (
1.1
), the asymptotic profiles of the EE can reflect the consequences of disease control by limiting population movement if the solutions of (
1.1
) always establish at some EE and the disease dynamics happens in a faster time scale than the disease control. However, if the disease control happens in a faster time scale, then the global dynamics of (
1.1
) with
d_{S}=0
or
d_{I}=0
may better tell the impact of the disease control strategies. As in
[
15
]
, we will construct Lyapunov type functions to study the global dynamics of the solutions. The Lyapunov functions constructed by us do not use the graph-theoretical method, and we will conquer the difficulties arising from asymmetric
\mathcal{L}
by proving a useful result, i.e., Theorem
2.4
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we present some preliminary results. In section 3, we study the global dynamics of (
1.1
) with the mass-action incidence mechanism. In section 4, we study the global dynamics of the model with the standard incidence mechanism.

2.2
Some useful results

We present an important result, Theorem
2.4
, which will help us to construct delicate Lyapunov functions later on. An
n\times n
real matrix
A=(a_{ij})
is called
line-sum symmetric
if
\sum_{j}a_{ij}=\sum_{j}a_{ji}
, i.e. the sum of the
i
-th row equals the sum of the
i
-th column for all
i=1,\dots,n
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
(A1)
holds and
\mathcal{L}
is line-sum symmetric. Then
\sum_{i,j\in\Omega}L_{ij}X_{i}X_{j}\leq 0
for any
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the equality holds if and only if
\bm{X}
is a multiple of
\bm{1}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}
is line-sum symmetric,
-L_{ii}=\sum_{j\neq i,j\in\Omega}L_{ij}=\sum_{j\neq i,j\in\Omega}L_{ji}
for any
i=1,\dots,n
. For any
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\Omega}L_{ij}X_{i}X_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in\Omega,j\neq i}L_{ij}X_{i}X_{j}+\sum_{i\in\Omega}L_{ii}X_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in\Omega,j\neq i}L_{ij}(X_{i}-X_{j})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in\Omega,j\neq i}L_{ij}X_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in\Omega,j\neq i}L_{ij}X_{j}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\Omega}L_{ii}X_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in\Omega,j\neq i}L_{ij}(X_{i}-X_{j})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in\Omega,j\neq i}L_{ij}X_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in\Omega,j\neq i}L_{ji}X_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\Omega}L_{ii}X_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in\Omega,j\neq i}L_{ij}(X_{i}-X_{j})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\Omega}L_{ii}X_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\Omega}L_{ii}X_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\Omega}L_{ii}X_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in\Omega,j\neq i}L_{ij}(X_{i}-X_{j})^{2}\leq 0.

Since
L
is irreducible, the equality holds if and only if
X_{1}=\dots=X_{n}
, i.e.,
\bm{X}
is a multiple of
\bm{1}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

By Lemma
2.2
, if
\mathcal{L}
satisfies
(A1)
and is line-sum symmetric, then
(\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{L}^{T})/2

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose that
(A1)
holds. Let
\bm{\alpha}
be defined as above and
\bm{\theta}:=1/\bm{\alpha}
. Then
\sum_{i,j}\theta_{i}L_{ij}X_{i}X_{j}\leq 0
for any
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the equality holds if and only if
\bm{X}
is a multiple of
\bm{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
D=\text{diag}(\bm{\alpha})
. Then, we have
\mathcal{L}D=(L_{ij}\alpha_{j})_{i,j=1}^{n}
. The sum of the
j
-th column of
\mathcal{L}D
is
\sum_{i}L_{ij}\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{j}\sum_{i}L_{ij}=0
for any
1\leq j\leq n
, since the sum of each column of
\mathcal{L}
is zero. The sum of the
i
-th row of
\mathcal{L}D
is
\sum_{j}L_{ij}\alpha_{j}=0
for any
1\leq i\leq n
, since
\alpha
is an eigenvector of
\mathcal{L}
corresponding with eigenvalue zero. Therefore,
\mathcal{L}D
is line-sum symmetric, where the sum of each column and row is zero. By Lemma
2.2
, we have
\sum_{i,j}L_{ij}\alpha_{j}Y_{i}Y_{j}\leq 0
for any
\bm{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the equality holds if and only if
\bm{Y}
is a multiple of
\bm{1}
.

We notice that
\sum_{i,j}L_{ij}\alpha_{j}Y_{i}Y_{j}=\sum_{i,j}\theta_{i}L_{ij}(\alpha_{i}Y_{i})(\alpha_{j}Y_{j})
. Since
\bm{\alpha}\gg\bm{0}
and
\bm{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
were arbitrary, we have
\sum_{i,j}\theta_{i}L_{ij}X_{i}X_{j}\leq 0
for any
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the equality holds if and only if
\bm{X}
is a multiple of
\bm{\alpha}
.
∎

3
Model with the mass-action incidence mechanism

In this section, we focus on the model with the mass-action incidence mechanism, that is

\begin{cases}\bm{S}^{\prime}=d_{S}\mathcal{L}\bm{S}-\bm{\beta}\circ\bm{S}\circ\bm{I}+\bm{\gamma}\circ\bm{I},\quad&t>0,\cr\bm{I}^{\prime}=d_{I}\mathcal{L}\bm{I}+\bm{\beta}\circ\bm{S}\circ\bm{I}-\bm{\gamma}\circ\bm{I},\quad&t>0.\end{cases}

(3.1)

First, we will discuss the scenario of a complete limitation of the movement of infected population, i.e
d_{I}=0
, and obtain Theorem
3.1
. Then, we will present our result on the scenario of a complete limitation of the movement of susceptible population, i.e
d_{S}=0
, and establish Theorem
3.3
.

We introduce the low-risk, moderate-risk, and high-risk
\tilde{H}^{-}
,
\tilde{H}^{0}
, and
\tilde{H}^{+}
patches as follows:

\tilde{H}^{-}=\{i\in\Omega:1<r_{i}/(N\alpha_{i})\},\quad\tilde{H}^{0}:=\{i\in\Omega:1=r_{i}/(N\alpha_{i})\},\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{H}^{+}:=\{i\in\Omega:1>r_{i}/(N\alpha_{i})\}.

The sets
\tilde{H}^{-}
,
\tilde{H}^{0}
, and
\tilde{H}^{+}
partition
\Omega
into disjoint subsets, and either of these sets can be empty. Note that if the system is decoupled,
R_{i}=N\alpha_{i}/r_{i}=N\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}/\gamma_{i}
is the basic reproduction number of patch
i
.

Given an initial data
(\bm{S}^{0},\bm{I}^{0})
, depending on the initial distribution of the infected population, we introduce the set

\Omega^{0}_{\bm{I}^{0}}:=\{i\in\Omega:\ I^{0}_{i}=0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}:=\{i\in\Omega:\ I_{i}^{0}>0\}

and the quantity

\tilde{r}_{m}=\min_{i\in\Omega_{\bm{I}^{0}}^{+}}\frac{r_{i}}{N\alpha_{i}}.

The patches realizing the above minimum will be called the patches of the highest-risk.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
(A1)-(A3)
holds,
d_{S}>0
and
d_{I}=0
. Then every solution
(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))
of (
3.1
) satisfies

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\bm{S}(t)-(\sum_{i\in\Omega}S_{i}(t)){\bm{\alpha}}\|_{\infty}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{j\in\tilde{H}^{-}{\cup\tilde{H}^{0}}\cup\Omega^{0}_{\bm{I}^{0}}}I_{j}(t)=0.

(3.2)

Furthermore, the following conclusions hold.

(i)

If
\tilde{H}^{+}\cap\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}=\emptyset
, then
\|\bm{S}(t)-{N{\bm{\alpha}}}\|_{\infty}\to 0
and
\|\bm{I}(t)\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
;

(ii)

If
\tilde{H}^{+}\cap\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}\neq\emptyset
, then
\|\bm{S}(t)-N\tilde{r}_{m}{\bm{\alpha}}\|_{\infty}\to 0
,
\sum_{j\in\Omega}I_{j}(t)dx\to N-|\Omega|\tilde{r}_{m}
, and
I_{j}(t)\to 0
on
\{j\in\Omega:\ r_{j}/(N\alpha_{j})\neq\tilde{r}_{m}\}
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

Define

V(\bm{S},\bm{I})=\sum_{i\in\Omega}\theta_{i}\left(\frac{S_{i}^{2}}{2}+r_{i}I_{i}\right),

where
\theta_{i}=1/\alpha_{i}
for
i\in\Omega
. It is easy to check that

\dot{V}(\bm{S},\bm{I})=d_{S}\sum_{i,j\in\Omega}\theta_{i}L_{ij}S_{i}S_{j}-\sum_{i\in\Omega}\theta_{i}\beta_{i}\left(S_{i}-r_{i}\right)^{2}I_{i}.

By Theorem
2.4
, we have
\sum_{i,j\in\Omega}\theta_{i}L_{ij}S_{i}S_{j}\leq 0
for all
\bm{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and the equality holds if and only if
\bm{S}
is a multiple of
\bm{\alpha}
.

Denote
\mathcal{M}:=\{(\bm{S},\bm{I})\in\mathcal{E}:\ \dot{V}(\bm{S},\bm{I})=0\}
. Then,

\mathcal{M}=\left\{(\bm{S},\bm{I})\in\mathcal{E}:\ S_{i}=r_{i}\ \text{or}\ I_{i}=0\ \ \forall i\in\Omega\ \ \text{and}\ \bm{S}=a\bm{\alpha}\text{ for some }a\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

The maximal connected invariant subsets of
\mathcal{M}
are

\mathcal{M}_{i}:=\left\{(\bm{S},\bm{I})\in\mathcal{E}:\ \bm{S}=\frac{r_{i}}{\alpha_{i}}\bm{\alpha}\ \ \text{and}\ \ I_{j}=0\ \ \text{if}\ \frac{r_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\neq\frac{r_{i}}{\alpha_{i}}\ \forall j\in\Omega\right\},\ \ i\in\Omega,

and

\mathcal{M}_{0}:=\left\{(\bm{S},\bm{I})\in\mathcal{E}:\ \bm{S}=\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\bm{\alpha}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \bm{I}=\bm{0}\right\}.

Hence by the invariance principle
[
10
, Theorem 4.3.4]
,
(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))\to\mathcal{M}_{i}
for some
i=0,1,...,n
as
t\to\infty
.
It follows from the equation of
\bm{S}
in (
3.1
) and Lemma
2.6
that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\Big\|\bm{S}(t)-{\Big(\sum_{j\in\Omega}S_{j}(t)\Big)}{\bm{\alpha}}\Big\|_{\infty}=0.

(3.3)

If
\tilde{H}^{-}\neq\emptyset
, by its definition, we can choose
\varepsilon>0
such that

\eta_{\varepsilon}:=\max_{i\in\tilde{H}^{-}}\left\{\beta_{i}\left((N+\varepsilon)\alpha_{i}-r_{i}\right)\right\}<0.

By (
3.3
) and
\sum_{i\in\Omega}(S_{i}+I_{i})=N
for all
t\geq 0
, there is
t_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\bm{S}(t)\leq\Big(N-\sum_{i\in\Omega}I_{i}+\varepsilon\Big)\bm{\alpha},\ \ \forall\;t\geq t_{\varepsilon}.

(3.4)

It follows that

I_{i}^{\prime}=\beta_{i}\left(S_{i}-r_{i}\right)I_{i}\leq\beta_{i}\left((N+\epsilon)\alpha_{i}-r_{i}\right)I_{i}\leq\eta_{\varepsilon}I_{i},\ \ \forall\ t\geq t_{\varepsilon},\ i\in\tilde{H}^{-}.

Noticing
\eta_{\varepsilon}<0

I_{i}(t)\leq I_{i}(t_{\varepsilon})e^{\eta_{\varepsilon}t}\to 0\ \ \text{as}\ t\to\infty,\quad\forall\;i\in\tilde{H}^{-}.

Next, if
\tilde{H}^{0}\neq\emptyset

3.4
),

I_{i}^{\prime}=\beta_{i}(S_{i}-r_{i})I_{i}\leq\beta_{i}((N-I_{i}+\varepsilon)\alpha_{i}-r_{i})I_{i}=\beta_{j}\alpha_{i}(\varepsilon-I_{i})I_{i},\quad r\geq t_{\varepsilon},\ i\in\tilde{H}^{0}.

Hence

\limsup_{t\to\infty}I_{i}(t)\leq\varepsilon\quad\forall\ i\in\tilde{H}^{0}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
in the last inequality, we obtain that
I_{i}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
for each
i\in\tilde{H}^{0}.

Since
I_{i}(t)=0
for all
t\geq 0
for all
i\in\Omega^{0}_{\bm{I}^{0}}
, (
3.2
) holds.

It remains to prove (i)-(ii). We first show that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i\in\Omega}I_{i}(t)\leq N(1-\tilde{r}_{m})_{+}.

(3.5)

Since
I_{i}(t)=0
for all
t\geq 0
for all
i\in\Omega^{0}_{\bm{I}^{0}}
, it suffices to prove (
3.5
) with
\Omega
replaced by
\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}
. To see this, by (
3.4
),

\displaystyle I_{i}^{\prime}=\beta_{i}\left(S_{i}-r_{i}\right)I_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{i}\Big(\Big(N-\sum_{i\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}}I_{i}+\epsilon\Big)\alpha_{i}-r_{i}\Big)I_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\Big(N(1-\tilde{r}_{m})_{+}+\varepsilon-\sum_{i\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}}I_{i}\Big)I_{i},\ \ \forall\ t\geq t_{\varepsilon},\ i\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}.

Define

F(t)=\frac{\sum_{i\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}}\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}I_{i}(t)}{\sum_{i\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}}I_{i}(t)},\ \ t\geq 0.

It then follows that

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}}I_{i}\leq F(t)\Big(N(1-\tilde{r}_{m})_{+}+\varepsilon-\sum_{i\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}}I_{i}\Big)\sum_{i\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}}I_{i},\ \ \forall\ t\geq t_{\varepsilon}

Noticing
0<\min\{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}:\ x\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}\}\leq F(t)\leq\max\{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}:\ x\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}\}
, we have

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i\in\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}}I_{i}(t)\leq N(1-\tilde{r}_{m})_{+}+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, (
3.5
) holds.

(i) If
H^{+}\cap\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}=\emptyset
, then
(1-\tilde{r}_{m})_{+}=0
. By (
3.5
), we have
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\bm{I}(t)\|_{\infty}=0
. Since
\sum_{i\in\Omega}(S_{i}+I_{i})=N
for all

,
\sum_{i\in\Omega}S_{i}(t)\to N
as
t\to\infty
. Then it follows from (

as
t\to\infty
.

(ii) If

, then
1>\tilde{r}_{m}
.
By (
3.5
) and
\sum_{i\in\Omega}(S_{i}(t)+I_{i}(t))=N
for all

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i\in\Omega}S_{i}(t)\geq N{\tilde{r}_{m}}.

By (
3.3
), we have

\liminf_{t\to\infty}S_{i}(t)\geq N{\tilde{r}_{m}}\alpha_{i},\ \ \forall\ i\in\Omega.

Now we claim that there exists

such that

\liminf_{t\to\infty}S_{i_{0}}(t)=N{\tilde{r}_{m}}\alpha_{i_{0}}.

(3.6)

Assume to the contrary that the claim is false. Then there exist

Let

By (

(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))\to\mathcal{M}_{i_{0}}

Remark 3.2
.

By Theorem
3.1
, if
\tilde{H}^{+}\cap\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}=\emptyset
, the disease can be eliminated by limiting the movement of infected people when the model has mass-action mechanism. However if
\tilde{H}^{+}\cap\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}\neq\emptyset
,
the infected people will concentrate on the patches of the highest risk,
\{j\in\Omega:\ r_{j}/(N\alpha_{j})\neq\tilde{r}_{m}\}
, when limiting the movement of them. These predictions largely agree with those in
[
12
,
16
]
for the model with mass-action mechanism, which are based on the asymptotic profiles of the EE as
d_{I}\to 0
. The main difference is that our results are depending on the distribution of initial data
\bm{I}^{0}
.

Next, we examine the scenario of a total restriction of the movement of the susceptible population. In this direction, our result reads as follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
(A1)-(A3)
holds,
d_{S}=0
and
d_{I}>0
. Then any solution
(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))

\bm{S}^{*}\gg\bm{0}
. Furthermore, exactly one of the following statements holds:

(i)

\bm{S}^{*}=\bm{\lambda}^{*}\circ\bm{S}^{0}+({\bm{1}}-\bm{\lambda}^{*})\circ\bm{r}
for some
\bm{\lambda}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
{\bm{0}}\ll\bm{\lambda}^{*}\ll{\bm{1}}
,
\sum_{j\in\Omega}S^{*}_{j}=N
and
s(d_{I}\mathcal{L}+{\rm diag}(\bm{\beta}\circ\bm{\lambda}^{*}\circ(\bm{S}^{0}-\bm{r})))\leq 0
, and
\bm{I}(t)\to\bm{0}
as

.

(ii)

\bm{S}^{*}=\bm{r}
and
\|\bm{I}(t)-\bm{I}^{*}\|_{\infty}\to 0
as

, where
\bm{I}^{*}=(N-\sum_{j\in\Omega}r_{j})\bm{\alpha}\gg\bm{0}
.

Moreover,
(i)
holds if

while

holds if

(3.7)

Proof.

Let

for

and
t\geq 0
and define

\tilde{J}_{i}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{i}(\tau)d\tau=\lim_{t\to\infty}J_{i}(t)\in(0,\infty].

We consider two cases.

Case 1.

\tilde{J}_{i_{0}}<\infty
for some
i_{0}\in\Omega
.
By Lemma
2.7
, there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\bm{I}_{M}(t)\leq C_{1}I_{i}(t),\quad\forall\;t\geq 1,i\in\Omega.

(3.8)

Therefore,

\tilde{J}_{i}=\tilde{J}_{i}(1)+\int_{1}^{\infty}I_{i}(\tau)d\tau\leq\tilde{J}_{i}(1)+C_{1}\int_{1}^{\infty}I_{i_{0}}(\tau)d\tau\leq{J}_{i}(1)+C_{1}\tilde{J}_{i_{0}},\quad\forall\;i\in\Omega.

Hence,
|\tilde{J}_{i}|<\infty
for all
i\in\Omega
and
\|\bm{\tilde{J}}\|_{\infty}<\infty
.
Since
|I^{\prime}_{i}|
is uniformly bounded for
t\in(0,\infty)
and
\tilde{J}_{i}<\infty
, it follow from Lemma
2.8
that
I_{i}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
for each
i\in\Omega
.

By (

) again, we have

\|\tilde{\bm{J}}-\bm{J}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\int_{t}^{\infty}I_{i_{0}}(\tau)d\tau\to 0\ \ \text{as}\ \ t\to\infty.

By the first equation of (
3.1
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}(S_{i}-r_{i})=-\beta_{i}(S_{i}-r_{i})I_{i},\quad i\in\Omega,t>0.

It follows that

(3.9)

This implies that

Define

,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{I}\sum_{i,j\in\Omega}L_{ij}\psi_{i}I_{j}+\sum_{i\in\Omega}(\beta_{i}S_{i}-\gamma_{i})\psi_{i}I_{i}

Remark 3.4
.

By Theorem
3.3
, whether limiting the movement of susceptible people can eliminate the disease is depending on the total population
N
: if
N\leq\sum_{j\in\Omega}r_{j}
, then disease can be controlled; if
N
is large, then it cannot be controlled by limiting the movement of susceptible people. These predictions agree with those in
[
12
,
16
]
where the predictions are based on the asymptotic profiles of the EE as

.

Remark 3.5
.

Alternatively, we may use a Lyapunov function to prove Theorem
3.3
. Define

V(\bm{S},\bm{I})=\sum_{i\in\Omega}\left(\frac{\beta_{i}S_{i}^{2}}{2\gamma_{i}}+I_{i}\right).

Then it is easy to check that

\dot{V}(\bm{S},\bm{I})=-\sum_{i\in\Omega}\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}}\left(\beta_{i}S_{i}-\gamma_{i}\right)^{2}I_{i}\leq 0.

4
Model with the standard incidence mechanism

In the current section, we focus on the model with standard incidence mechanism, that is

\begin{cases}\bm{S}^{\prime}=d_{S}\mathcal{L}\bm{S}-(\bm{\beta}\circ\bm{S}\circ\bm{I})/(\bm{S}+\bm{I})+\bm{\gamma}\circ\bm{I}&t>0,\cr\bm{I}^{\prime}=d_{I}\mathcal{L}\bm{I}+(\bm{\beta}\circ\bm{S}\circ\bm{I})/(\bm{S}+\bm{I})-\bm{\gamma}\circ\bm{I}&t>0.\end{cases}

(4.1)

We will study the global dynamics of the model when
d_{S}=0
or
d_{I}=0
as in the previous section.

Here, we define the low-risk, moderate-risk, and high-risk patches
{H}^{+}
,
H^{0}
and
{H}^{-}
as

H^{-}:=\{i\in\Omega:1<r_{i}\},\quad H^{0}:=\{i\in\Omega:1=r_{i}\},\quad\text{and}\quad H^{+}:=\{i\in\Omega:1>r_{i}\}.

The sets
H^{-}
,
H^{0}
, and
H^{+}
partition
\Omega
into disjoint subsets, and either of these sets can be empty. Note that
1/r_{i}=\beta_{i}/\gamma_{i}
is the basic reproduction number
of patch
i
when the system is decoupled.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
(A1)-(A3)
holds,
d_{I}=0
and
d_{S}>0
. Let
(\bm{S},\bm{I})
be the solution of (
4.1
). Then,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\bm{S}(t)=k\bm{\alpha},\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}I_{i}(t)=0,\quad\forall\;i\in H_{1},\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}I_{i}(t)=\frac{\beta_{i}-\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}}k\alpha_{i},\quad\forall\;i\in H_{2},

where
H_{1}:=H^{0}\cup H^{-}\cup\Omega_{\bm{I}^{0}}^{0}
,
H_{2}:=H^{+}\cap\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}
and

k=N/({1+\sum_{i\in H_{2}}\frac{\beta_{i}-\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}}\alpha_{i}}).

Proof.

First, we claim that there exists
\underline{S}>0
such that
\liminf_{t\to\infty}S_{i}(t)\geq\underline{S}
for all
i\in\Omega
. To see this, by the equation of
\bm{S}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i\in\Omega}S_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\Omega}\gamma_{i}I_{i}-\sum_{i\in\Omega}\beta_{i}\frac{I_{i}}{I_{i}+S_{i}}S_{i}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\displaystyle\geq\bm{\gamma}_{m}\sum_{i\in\Omega}I_{i}-\bm{\beta}_{M}\sum_{i\in\Omega}S_{i}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

So we can choose

such that

\sum_{i\in\Omega}S_{i}(t)\geq\frac{\bm{\gamma}_{m}N}{2(\bm{\beta}_{M}+\bm{\gamma}_{m})},\quad\forall\;t\geq t_{0}.

(4.2)

By Lemma
2.7
, there exists

such that

(4.3)

By the equation
\bm{S}

2.4

\sum_{i,j\in\Omega}\theta_{i}L_{ij}S_{i}S_{j}\leq 0

\bm{\alpha}

\bm{0}<\underline{S}\bm{1}\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\bm{S}(t)\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\bm{S}(t)\leq N\bm{1}

. By (
4.9
), we have that
I_{i}(t)\to\frac{k^{*}(\beta_{i}-\gamma_{i})}{\gamma_{i}}
as
t\to\infty
, for each
i\in H_{2}
. Recalling that
\|\bm{S}(t)+\bm{I}(t)\|_{1}=N
for all
t\geq 0
, then
N=k^{*}(1+\sum_{j\in H_{2}}(\beta_{j}-\gamma_{j})\alpha_{j}/\gamma_{j})
, which yields that
k^{*}=N/((1+\sum_{j\in H_{2}}(\beta_{j}-\gamma_{j})\alpha_{j}/\gamma_{j}))
. Since
k^{*}
is independent of the chosen subsequence, the desired result follows.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

By Theorem
4.1
, the infected people will live exactly on
H^{+}\cap\Omega^{+}_{\bm{I}^{0}}
(i.e. the high-risk patches with positive initial infected cases) for the model with standard incidence mechanism when the dispersal of infected people is limited.
As before, these predictions agree with previous studies for the model with standard incidence mechanism in
[
11
,
4
]
, which are based on the asymptotic profiles of the EE as
d_{I}\to 0
. The main difference is that our results are depending on the distribution of initial data
\bm{I}^{0}
.

Next, we examine the scenario of a total restriction of the movement of the susceptible population. In this direction, our result reads as follows.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that
(A1)-(A3)
holds,
d_{S}=0
and
d_{I}>0
. Let
(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))
be the solution of (
1.1
). Then the following conclusions hold:

(i)

If
H^{-}\cup H^{0}
is nonempty, then
\bm{I}(t)\to\bm{0}
as
t\to\infty
and
\liminf_{t\to\infty}S_{j}(t)>0
for
j\in H^{-}\cup H^{0}
.

(ii)

If
H^{+}=\Omega
, then
(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))\to(I^{*}(\bm{r}\circ\bm{\alpha}/(\bm{1}-\bm{r})),I^{*}\bm{\alpha})
as
t\to\infty
, where
I^{*}=N/(1+\|\bm{1}/(\bm{1}-\bm{r})\|_{1})
.

Proof.

Define

V(\bm{S},\bm{I})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\Omega}\theta_{i}\frac{(1-r_{i})}{r_{i}}S_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\Omega}\theta_{i}I_{i}^{2},

where
\bm{\theta}=\bm{1}/\bm{\alpha}
.
Then
-\frac{N^{2}}{2}\sum_{i\in\Omega}\theta_{i}\leq V(\bm{S},\bm{I})\leq N^{2}(1+\|\bm{1}/\bm{r}\|_{\infty})\sum_{i\in\Omega}\theta_{i}
and

\dot{V}(\bm{S},\bm{I})=\sum_{i,j\in\Omega}\theta_{i}L_{ij}I_{i}I_{j}-\sum_{i\in\Omega}\frac{\theta_{i}\beta_{i}^{2}}{\gamma_{i}}\big((1-r_{i})S_{i}-r_{i}I_{i}\big)^{2}\frac{I_{i}}{S_{i}+I_{i}}.

By Theorem
2.4
,
\sum_{i,j\in\Omega}\theta_{i}L_{ij}I_{i}I_{j}\leq 0
, where the equality holds if and only if
\bm{I}
is a multiple of
\bm{\alpha}
. Denote
\mathcal{N}:=\{(\bm{S},\bm{I})\in\mathcal{E}:\ \dot{V}(\bm{S},\bm{I})=0\}
. Then,

\mathcal{N}=\left\{(\bm{S},\bm{I})\in\mathcal{E}:\ \big((1-r_{i})S_{i}-r_{i}I_{i}\big)^{2}I_{i}=0\ \ \forall i\in\Omega\ \ \text{and}\ \bm{I}=a\bm{\alpha}\text{ for some }a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\right\}.

From this point, we distinguish two cases.

(i) Suppose that
H^{-}\cup H^{0}\neq\emptyset
. In this case, the maximal connected invariant subset of
\mathcal{N}
is

\mathcal{N}_{1}:=\left\{(\bm{S},\bm{I})\in\mathcal{E}:\ \bm{I}=\bm{0}\right\}.

Hence by the invariance principle
[
10
, Theorem 4.3.4]
,
(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))\to\mathcal{N}_{1}
as
t\to\infty
. Thus
\bm{I}(t)\to\bm{0}
as
t\to\infty
.

Next, fix
i\in H^{-}\cup H^{0}
. Then

S_{i}^{\prime}=(\gamma_{i}I_{i}-(\beta_{i}-\gamma_{i})S_{i})\frac{I_{i}}{S_{i}+I_{i}}\geq\frac{\gamma_{i}I_{i}^{2}}{S_{i}+I_{i}}\geq\frac{\bm{\gamma}_{m}}{N}I_{i}^{2}.

Integrating both sides and taking
\liminf
as
t\to\infty
, we obtain

\liminf_{t\to\infty}S_{i}(t)\geq S_{i}^{0}+\frac{\bm{\gamma}_{m}}{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{i}^{2}dt>0.

(ii) Suppose that
H^{-}\cup H^{0}=\emptyset
, that is
H^{+}=\Omega
. Then, in addition to the set
\mathcal{N}_{1}
, the set
\mathcal{N}_{2}:=\{(I^{*}(\bm{r}\circ\bm{\alpha}/(\bm{1}-\bm{r})),I^{*}\bm{\alpha})\}

is also a maximal connected invariant subset of
\mathcal{N}
, where
I^{*}=N/(1+\|\bm{1}/(\bm{1}-\bm{r})\|_{1})
. Hence by the invariance principle
[
10
, Theorem 4.3.4]
,
(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))\to\mathcal{N}_{i}
for some
i=1,2
as
t\to\infty
. Observe that
\mathcal{N}_{2}
contains a single point. To complete the proof of the result, it remains to show that
\bm{I}(t)\nrightarrow\bm{0}
as
t\to\infty
. To this end, setting
F_{i}(t)=I_{i}/(S_{i}+I_{i})
for
t>0
and
i\in\Omega
, then
(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))
solves the cooperative system

\begin{cases}\bm{S}^{\prime}=(\bm{\gamma}\circ\bm{I}-(\bm{\beta}-\bm{\gamma})\circ\bm{S})\circ\bm{F},&t>0,\cr\bm{I}^{\prime}=d_{I}\mathcal{L}\bm{I}+((\bm{\beta}-\bm{\gamma})\circ\bm{S}-\bm{\gamma}\circ\bm{I})\circ\bm{F},&t>0.\end{cases}

(4.10)

Taking

(\underline{\bm{S}},\underline{\bm{I}})=(l(\bm{\gamma}/(\bm{\beta}-\bm{\gamma}))\circ\bm{\alpha},l\bm{\alpha})

where
l:=\min\{(\bm{S}(1)\circ(\bm{\beta}-\bm{\gamma})/\bm{\gamma}\circ\bm{\alpha})_{m},(\bm{I}(1)/\bm{\alpha})_{m}\}>0
, we have that
(\underline{\bm{S}},\underline{\bm{I}})
solves (
4.10
) and
(\underline{\bm{S}},\underline{\bm{I}})\leq(\bm{S}(1),\bm{I}(1))
. Therefore, by the comparison principle for cooperative systems, we have that
(\underline{\bm{S}},\underline{\bm{I}})\leq(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))
for all
t\geq 1
. This shows that the omega limit set of
(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))
doesn’t intersect with
\mathcal{N}_{1}
. Therefore,
(\bm{S}(t),\bm{I}(t))\to\mathcal{N}_{2}
as
t\to\infty
.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

By Theorem
4.3
, if
H^{-}\cup H^{0}
is nonempty, the disease can be controlled by limiting the movement of susceptible people. This agrees with previous studies for the model with standard incidence mechanism in
[
1
,
4
]
, which are based on the asymptotic profiles of the EE as
d_{I}\to 0
. If
H^{-}\cup H^{0}
is empty, then the disease cannot be controlled by limiting the movement of susceptible people.

Bipartite entanglement of noisy stabilizer states through the lens of stabilizer codes
Kenneth Goodenough, Aqil Sajjad, Eneet Kaur, Saikat Guha, Don Towsley

Abstract
Stabilizer states are a prime resource for a number of applications in quantum information science, such as secret-sharing and measurement-based quantum computation. This motivates us to study the entanglement of stabilizer states across a bipartition, in the presence of Pauli noise. We show that the spectra of the corresponding reduced states can be expressed in terms of properties of an associated stabilizer code. In particular, this allows us to show that the coherent information is related to the so-called syndrome entropy of the underlying code. We use this viewpoint to find stabilizer states that are resilient against noise, allowing for more robust entanglement distribution in near-term quantum networks. We specialize our results to the case of graph states, where the found connections with stabilizer codes reduces back to classical linear codes for dephasing noise. On our way we provide an alternative proof of the fact that every qubit stabilizer code is equivalent up to single-qubit Clifford gates to a graph code.

II-B

Conjugate codes/subspaces

Any stabilizer code
H
has several closely associated codes, which differ only by phases
\pm 1
in front of the

. We call these the conjugate codes/subspaces of
H

\langle\pm XX,\pm ZZ\rangle

\pm 1

Second, any two different such subspaces are orthogonal. This is because they differ in the phase of at least one stabilizer, meaning that any two states in two different conjugate subspaces can be deterministically distinguished by measuring that stabilizer. Note that this argument still holds for mixed states that are convex combinations of basis states of the respective subspaces.

Since the

conjugate subspaces (corresponding to the

in a different conjugate subspace

2

2

On a deeper level this follows from the orthogonality of irreducible characters, such that the above result also generalizes to prime qudit stabilizers.

\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muH\mkern-3.5mu}\mkern 3.5mu

In the preceding discussion, we interpreted the syndrome entropy of the code
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muH\mkern-3.5mu}\mkern 3.5mu\backslash A

\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muH\mkern-3.5mu}\mkern 3.5mu\backslash A

\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muH\mkern-3.5mu}\mkern 3.5mu\backslash A

In the above we assumed the setting where all qubits underwent uniform depolarizing noise. Let us now focus on the setting where only Bob’s qubits undergo uniform depolarizing noise; this setting corresponds exactly to the

, i.e. Alice prepares

Theorem 6
.

The coherent information of a stabilizer state across a bipartition

\displaystyle\hat{S}(\rho_{B})-\hat{S}(\rho_{AB})=k+S(\mathbf{s}_{\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muH\mkern-3.5mu}\mkern 3.5mu\backslash A})-S(\mathbf{s}_{\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muH\mkern-3.5mu}\mkern 3.5mu/A})\ ,

\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muH\mkern-3.5mu}\mkern 3.5mu
is the stabilizer group of the state shared between Alice and Bob.

Proof.

Clearly the state
\rho_{B}
is the same as in the setting of Theorem
5

\hat{S}(\rho_{AB})
term.

As in Theorem
5
, the state
\rho_{AB}
before noise is the code projector
\rho_{\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muH\mkern-3.5mu}\mkern 3.5mu}

P\rho_{H}P^{\dagger}
, but where the

now vanish on
A
. Let us denote the set of such Pauli strings by
\mathcal{P}^{B}_{n+k}
, which has a natural bijection to
\mathcal{P}_{n+k}/A\cong\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

As before, an equivalence relation
\sim
on

indicates the subsets of Pauli strings that affect the state

. Note that since

Algorithm

We now sketch our algorithm for calculating the coherent information of a stabilizer state that underwent uniform depolarizing noise. The goal is to construct an error basis
E
.
E
is then used to reconstruct the cosets
C_{e}
, from which one can calculate the syndrome entropy through Eq. (

Let us first focus on the
\rho_{AB}
term. Assume that a stabilizer tableau
M
(represented as an element of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{(n+k)\times 2(n+k)}
) is given for the initial state
\ket{\psi}
. This matrix has rank
n+k
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Our goal is to find an error basis
E
, from which the cosets
C_{e}
can be reconstructed. The error basis

is of size
n+k
and each of its elements is linearly independent from

We construct such a basis

as follows. Initialize
E
as the empty set. Start with
M
and check whether

is in the row span of

, where

i

with the added row
e_{1}
have the same rank over
\mathbb{F}_{2}

e_{1}
to

and add
e_{1}
as a row vector to
M
. Now repeat the procedure but with
e_{2}
, and repeat until the matrix has maximum rank.

III-B

Reduction for dephasing on graph states

Here we limit our discussion to graph states and dephasing on them, and show how the above connections reduce to classical linear codes.

Let us define dephasing with the following parameterization,
D_{Z}(\cdot)=p(\cdot)+\left(1-p\right)Z(\cdot)
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\ket{G}
be a graph state whose biadjacency matrix across a bipartition
A,B
is given by
G_{AB}
. The coherent information of
\ket{G}
after uniform dephasing with parameter
p

A,B

\displaystyle S(\rho_{B})-S(\rho_{AB})=k+S(\mathbf{s}_{G_{AB}})-\left(n+k\right)\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muS\mkern-3.5mu}\mkern 3.5mu(p)\ ,

where

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{CZ}

\rho_{AB}
is given by
\left(n+k\right)\cdot S(\{p,1-p\})\equiv\left(n+k\right)\cdot\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muS\mkern-3.5mu}\mkern 3.5mu(p)

To continue, we need to construct the stabilizer tableau after deletion. We find that

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c|c|c}&&&\\

&&&\\
\hline\cr&&&\\
\>0&I_{n}&G_{AB}^{T}&\>0\\
&&&\end{array}\right]\xrightarrow{\textrm{deletion}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&G_{AB}\\
\hline\cr&\\

The coherent information for dephasing noise can be calculated using a similar technique as for depolarizing noise.

We close with two comments. First, assume the graph state in question has been created by first preparing

V
Discussion

In this paper we interpreted noisy stabilizer states in terms of certain associated stabilizer codes. This suggests that stabilizer states robust against noise/distribution in networks have good codes associated to them; we confirmed this intuition for a number of codes.
However, there seems no structural way to find the state with the largest coherent information for fixed
n
,
k
and noise parameter
\lambda
. This should come as no surprise, since finding optimal codes for any metric is a difficult task.

On a higher level
, we have shown that the spectra of noisy stabilizer states can be expressed in terms of weight enumerators, quantities that are known to be hard to compute (even in the classical case) but have been well-studied
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
.
Fortunately bounds on the syndrome entropy have been investigated for the classical case; it would be of interest to see if those results can be exploited for the case considered here. This would however require a good enough control on the error terms, since the coherent information involves the difference of two syndrome entropies.

The techniques developed here apply straightforwardly to the multipartite setting. One possible application would be to express upper bounds
[
30
,
31
]
on the multipartite squashed entanglement
[
32
]
to find upper bounds on the multipartite distillable key.

Flat Delaunay Complexes for Homeomorphic Manifold Reconstruction
Dominique Attali, André Lieutier

Abstract
Given a smooth submanifold of the Euclidean space, a finite point cloud and a scale parameter, we introduce a construction which we call the flat Delaunay complex (FDC). This is a variant of the tangential Delaunay complex (TDC) introduced by Boissonnat et al. [ 5 , 7 ] . Building on their work, we provide a short and direct proof that when the point cloud samples sufficiently nicely the submanifold and is sufficiently safe (a notion which we define in the paper), our construction is homeomorphic to the submanifold. Because the proof works even when data points are noisy, this allows us to propose a perturbation scheme that takes as input a point cloud sufficiently nice and returns a point cloud which in addition is sufficiently safe. Equally importantly, our construction provides the framework underlying a variational formulation of the reconstruction problem which we present in a companion paper [ 4 ] .

Manifold reconstruction and learning.

In many practical situations, the shape of interest is only known
through a finite set of data points. Given these data points as input,
it is then natural to try to construct a triangulation of the shape,
that is, a set of simplices whose union is homeomorphic to the
shape. This problem has given rise to many research works in the
computational geometry community, motivated by applications to 3D
model reconstruction and manifold learning; see for instance

[

In manifold learning, data sets typically live in high dimensional
spaces but are assumed to be distributed near unknown relatively low
dimensional smooth manifolds. In this context, reconstruction
algorithms have to deal efficiently with manifolds having an arbitrary
codimension and, most importantly, should have a complexity which is
only polynomial in the ambient dimension.
The tangential Delaunay complex of Boissonnat et al.
[

]
and

enjoys this polynomial complexity with respect to
the ambient dimension.

Tangential Delaunay complex (TDC).

Consider a set of data points

locally around a point

and collecting Delaunay simplices incident to

. The tangential Delaunay complex (TDC)
is defined as the union of the stars of all points in

The stars in the TDC are said to be consistent if any simplex in the TDC
belongs to the star of each of its vertices. The authors prove in
particular that (1) when the data set is sufficiently dense with
respect to the reach of
\mathcal{M}
, a weight assignment – through Moser
Tardos Algorithm
[
17
]

consistent and (2) that when the stars are consistent, the TDC is a
triangulation of the manifold, more precisely, the TDC is embedded and
the projection onto
\mathcal{M}
restricted to the TDC is an homeomorphism.

Our contributions.

We propose a construction called the

flat Delaunay complex
(FDC) that exhibits the same behavior as
the TDC described above. First, it has a geometric characterization
of simplices analog to that of the TDC, the only difference being that
around each point
p
, we replace the computation of the weighted
Delaunay complex by that of an unweighted one and, as a counterpart,
restrict computations inside a ball of radius
\rho
around

p
. While, from an application perspective, our FDC would lead to
similar practical algorithms than the TDC, we claim that it brings
significant theoretical contributions.

First, while the criterion of star consistency in TDC is simple and
elegant, the proof of homeomorphism for TDC, once this consistency is
assumed, prove to be rather involved, requiring in particular the use
of a lemma by Whitney about the projection of oriented PL
pseudo-manifolds; see
[
5
, Lemma 5.14]
,

[
9
]
and
[
19
, Lemma 15a, Appendix
II]
. Our construction defines instead what we
call
prestars
everywhere in space, not merely at the points of the
data set and, for each
d
-simplex
\sigma
in the FDC, requires these
prestars to agree at every pair of points in
\operatorname{conv}\sigma
and not
merely at the vertices of
\sigma
. This allows us to give a
more direct and, in our opinion, more insightful proof for the
homeomorphism.

Second, as in the proof of correctness for TDC, a crucial ingredient
consists in quantifying some metric distorsion between projections on
various affine
d
-spaces. By considering metric distorsions in the
context of relations instead of maps (as in Gromov-Hausdorff distance
definition
[
11
, Section 5.30]
), we are able to
generalize stability results to the case of noisy data points. By
assuming
P\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\delta}
instead of
P\subseteq\mathcal{M}
,
this gives us the flexibility to perturb the data points and ensure
correctness of the FDC after some particular perturbation.

Third, the framework of the FDC is particularly convenient for supporting
the proof of correctness of a linear variational formulation, which
we present in a companion paper
[
4
]
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we review the necessary background and explain some of our terms.

2.1
Subsets and submanifolds

Given a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the affine space spanned by

A
is denoted by
\operatorname{aff}A
and the convex hull of
A
by
\operatorname{conv}A
.
The

of
A
, denoted as
\operatorname{axis}(A)
, is the set of points
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
that have at least two closest points in
A
. The

projection map

\pi_{A}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\operatorname{axis}(A)\to A

associates to each point
x
its unique closest point in
A
. The
reach
of

is the infimum of distances between
A
and its medial
axis and is denoted as
\operatorname{reach}A
. By definition, the projection map

\pi_{A}
is well-defined on every subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
that does not intersect the
medial axis of
A
. In particular, letting the
r
-tubular
neighborhood
of
A
be the set of points
A^{\oplus r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid d(x,A)\leq r\}
, the projection map
\pi_{A}
is
well-defined on every
r
-tubular neighborhood of
A
with
r<\operatorname{reach}A
. For short, we say that a subset
\sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is

\rho
-small if it can be enclosed in a ball of radius
\rho
.

Throughout the paper,
\mathcal{M}
designates a compact
C^{2}

d
-dimensional
submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
for
d<N
. For any point
m\in\mathcal{M}
, the tangent plane to
m
at
\mathcal{M}
is denoted as
\mathbf{T}_{m}\mathcal{M}
. Because
\mathcal{M}
is

and therefore
C^{1,1}
, the reach of
\mathcal{M}
is
positive
[
14
]
. We let
\mathcal{R}
be a fixed finite constant such that
0<\mathcal{R}\leq\operatorname{reach}\mathcal{M}
.

2.2
Simplicial complexes

In this section, we review some background notation on simplicial
complexes. For more details, the reader is referred to

[
18
]
. We also introduce the concept of faithful
reconstruction which encapsulates what we mean by a “desirable”
approximation of a manifold.

All simplicial complexes that we consider are abstract. An

abstract simplicial complex
is a collection
K
of finite
non-empty sets, such that if
\sigma
is an element of
K
, so is
every non-empty subset of
\sigma
. The element
\sigma
of
K
is
called an
abstract simplex
and its
dimension
is one less
than its cardinality. The
vertex set
of
K
is the union of its
elements,
\operatorname{Vert}{K}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in K}\sigma
. We are
interested in the situation where the vertex set of
K
is a subset of

\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In that situation, each abstract simplex
\sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is naturally associated to a geometric simplex
defined as
\operatorname{conv}\sigma
. The dimension of
\operatorname{conv}\sigma
is the
dimension of the affine space
\operatorname{aff}\sigma
and cannot be larger than
the dimension of the abstract simplex
\sigma
. When
\dim(\sigma)=\dim(\operatorname{aff}\sigma)
, we say that
\sigma
is

non-degenerate
. Equivalently, the vertices of
\sigma
form an affinely
independent set of points.

Given a set of simplices
\Sigma
with vertices in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(not necessarily forming a
simplicial complex), let us define the
shadow
of
\Sigma
as
the subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
covered by the relative interior of
the geometric simplices associated to abstract simplices in
\Sigma
,

\boldsymbol{\lvert}\Sigma\boldsymbol{\rvert}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\operatorname{relint}(\operatorname{conv}\sigma)
.
We shall
say that
\Sigma
is
geometrically realized
(or

embedded
) if (1)

\dim(\sigma)=\dim(\operatorname{aff}\sigma)
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
and (2)

\operatorname{conv}(\alpha\cap\beta)=\operatorname{conv}\alpha\cap\operatorname{conv}\beta
for all

\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma
.

Definition 1

(Faithful reconstruction)
.

Consider a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
whose reach is positive,
and a simplicial complex
K
with a vertex set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We
say that
K

reconstructs
A
faithfully
(or is a

faithful reconstruction
of
A
) if the following three
conditions hold:

Embedding:

K
is geometrically realized;

Closeness:

\boldsymbol{\lvert}K\boldsymbol{\rvert}
is contained in the
r
-tubular neighborhood of
A
for some
0\leq r<\operatorname{reach}A
;

Homeomorphism:

The restriction of
\pi_{A}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\operatorname{axis}(A)\to A
to
\boldsymbol{\lvert}K\boldsymbol{\rvert}
is a homeomorphism.

2.3
Height, circumsphere and smallest enclosing ball

All simplices we consider in the paper are abstract, unless
explicitely stated otherwise. The

of a simplex
\sigma

is
0pt{\sigma}=\min_{v\in\sigma}d(v,\operatorname{aff}(\sigma\setminus\{v\}))
. The height of
\sigma
vanishes if and only if

is

-sphere
that circumscribes
\sigma

-sphere that

\sigma
. Let

denote the

, respectively. Let

and

denote the center and radius of the smallest

-ball

\sigma
, respectively. Clearly,

and both

belong to

. The

is a
(d-1)
-sphere which is

-sphere circumscribing

in

.

2.4
Delaunay complexes

Consider a finite point set
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We say
that an
(N-1)
-sphere is
Q
-empty if it is the boundary of a ball
that contains no points of
Q

\sigma\subseteq Q
is a

-sphere that circumscribes

and is

-empty. The

Definition 2

(General position)
.

Let

. We say that

is in

if no

points of

lie on a common
(d-1)
-dimensional sphere.

Lemma 3
.

When

is in general position,
\operatorname{Del}(Q)
is geometrically realized.

Flat Delaunay complex

For simplicity, whenever

, we

.
Afterwards, we assume once and for all that

with respect to

at scale
\rho

(Section

) and

Definition 4

(Stars and Prestars)

Given a point

\begin{split}\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)=\{&\sigma\subseteq P\cap B(x^{*},\rho)\mid\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{x^{*}}\mathcal{M}}(\sigma)\in\operatorname{Star}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x^{*},\rho)\}.\end{split}

construction of the prestar of a point

.

Remark 5
.

By definition, if two points
x
and
y
share the same projection
onto
\mathcal{M}
, that is, if
x^{*}=y^{*}
, then they also share the
same prestar at scale
\rho
, that is,
\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)=\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(y,\rho)
. In particular,
\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)=\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x^{*},\rho)

Definition 6

(Flat Delaunay complex)
.

The

P
with respect to
\mathcal{M}
at scale

\rho
is the set of simplices

\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)=\bigcup_{p\in P}\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(p,\rho).

Note that the flat Delaunay complex is not necessarily a
simplicial complex but becomes one under the assumptions of our two main theorems
(Theorems
12
and
17
).

3.2
Preliminary remarks

Remark 7
.

By definition, if a simplex
\sigma
belongs to the prestar of some
point
x
at scale
\rho
, then
\sigma
fits in a ball of radius

\rho
and therefore is
\rho
-small and so are simplices
in
\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)
.

Remark 8
.

For all points
x
at which
d(x,\mathcal{M})<\operatorname{reach}M
and all
m\in\mathcal{M}
, we have that
m=\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{m}\mathcal{M}}(x)\iff m=\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(x)
.

We now provide two alternate characterizations of simplices in
the prestar. The first one is a direct consequence of the above
remark and will be useful in the proof of
Theorem
17
and the
second one will facilitate the proof of
Lemma
37
.

Remark 9

(First characterization of prestars)
.

If
x
is a point at which
d(x,\mathcal{M})<\operatorname{reach}\mathcal{M}
and
\rho<\operatorname{reach}\mathcal{M}
, then:

\sigma\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)~\iff~\begin{cases}\sigma\subseteq P\cap B(x^{*},\rho)\\
\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{x^{*}}\mathcal{M}}(\sigma)\in\operatorname{Del}(\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{x^{*}}\mathcal{M}}(P\cap B(x^{*},\rho)))\\
x^{*}\in\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\operatorname{conv}\sigma)\end{cases}

Remark 10

(Second characterization of prestars)
.

For all simplices
\sigma
such that
\operatorname{conv}\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\rho}
with
\rho<\operatorname{reach}\mathcal{M}

and all
m\in\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\operatorname{conv}\sigma)
,

\sigma\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)~\iff~\begin{cases}\sigma\subseteq P\cap B(m,\rho)\\
\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{m}\mathcal{M}}(\sigma)\in\operatorname{Del}(\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{m}\mathcal{M}}(P\cap B({m},\rho)))\end{cases}

Indeed, applying Remark
9
with
x=m
,
we observe that the last condition on the right side of the
equivalence is redundant because
m^{*}=m\in\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\operatorname{conv}\sigma)
.

4
Faithful reconstruction from structural conditions

In this section, we exhibit a set of structural conditions
under which
\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)
is a faithful reconstruction of

\mathcal{M}
. These conditions are encapsulated in our first reconstruction
theorem (Theorem

12
below). Among
the conditions, we find that every
\rho
-small
d
-simplex
\sigma\subseteq P
must have its prestars in agreement at scale
\rho
:

Definition 11

(Prestars in agreement)
.

We say that the
prestars of
\sigma
are in agreement at scale

\rho
if for all
x,y\in\operatorname{conv}\sigma
, the following
equivalence holds:
\sigma\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)\iff\sigma\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(y,\rho)
.

Compare to the work in

[
7
,
5
]
, we define the
prestars everywhere in space, not merely at the data points
P
and we
enforce the prestars to agree at every pair of points in
\operatorname{conv}\sigma

and not merely at the vertices of
\sigma
. This trick allows us to
provide a short proof that agreement of prestars, among other
lighter conditions, imply that the flat Delaunay complex is a faithful
reconstruction of
\mathcal{M}
.

Theorem 12

(Faithful reconstruction from structural conditions)
.

Suppose that
P\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\rho}
with
\rho<\frac{\mathcal{R}}{2}
and assume that the following structural conditions
are satisfied:

(1)

For every
\rho
-small
d
-simplex
\sigma\subseteq P
, the
map
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\pi_{\mathcal{M}}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\operatorname{conv}\sigma}}
is injective;

(2)

For all
m\in\mathcal{M}
, the map
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{m}\mathcal{M}}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{P\cap B(m,\rho)}}
is injective;

(3)

For all
m\in\mathcal{M}
,
\boldsymbol{\lvert}\operatorname{Star}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)\boldsymbol{\rvert}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in a neighborhood of
m
;

(4)

For all
m\in\mathcal{M}
,
\operatorname{Star}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)
is geometrically realized;

(5)

Every
\rho
-small
d
-simplex

\sigma\subseteq P
has its prestars in agreement at scale
\rho
.

Then,

•

\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)
is a simplicial complex;

•

For all
m\in\mathcal{M}
,
\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)\mid m\in\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\operatorname{conv}\sigma)\}
;

•

\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)
is a faithful reconstruction of
\mathcal{M}
.

Before giving the proof, we start with a remark.

Remark 13
.

For any simplex
\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\rho}
that can be
enclosed in a ball of radius
\rho<\frac{\mathcal{R}}{2}
, then
\operatorname{conv}\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\oplus 2\rho}
. Indeed, for all
x\in\operatorname{conv}\sigma
,
d(x,\mathcal{M})\leq d(x,\sigma)+\rho\leq 2\rho
. Hence, the
map
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\pi_{\mathcal{M}}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\operatorname{conv}\sigma}}
is well-defined.

Proof of Theorem
12
.

We prove the lemma in seven (short) stages:

(a)
First, we prove the following implication:

\begin{cases}\sigma\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)\\
\text{for all $x\in\operatorname{conv}\sigma$}\end{cases}\implies\quad\begin{cases}\tau\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)\\
\text{for all $\tau\subseteq\sigma$ and all $x\in\operatorname{conv}\tau$}\end{cases}

Indeed, suppose that
\sigma\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)
for all

x\in\operatorname{conv}\sigma
. Using Remark
9
,
this is equivalent to saying that for all
x\in\operatorname{conv}\sigma
:

\begin{cases}\sigma\subseteq P\cap B(x^{*},\rho)\\
\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{x^{*}}\mathcal{M}}(\sigma)\in\operatorname{Del}(\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{x^{*}}\mathcal{M}}(P\cap B(x^{*},\rho)))\\
x^{*}\in\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\operatorname{conv}\sigma)\end{cases}

Letting
x
be any point of
\operatorname{conv}\tau
and using
\tau\subseteq\sigma
, we
obtain that:

\begin{cases}\tau\subseteq P\cap B(x^{*},\rho)\\
\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{x^{*}}\mathcal{M}}(\tau)\in\operatorname{Del}(\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{x^{*}}\mathcal{M}}(P\cap B(x^{*},\rho)))\\
x^{*}\in\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\operatorname{conv}\tau)\end{cases}

But, using again Remark
9
, this
translates into saying that
\tau\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)
for all
x\in\operatorname{conv}\tau
as desired.

(b)
Second, we establish the following implication:

\text{$\tau\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)$ for some $m\in\mathcal{M}$}\implies\text{$\tau\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)$ for all $x\in\operatorname{conv}\tau$}.

Consider a simplex
\tau\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)
for some
m\in\mathcal{M}
and
let us show that
\tau\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(x,\rho)
for all
x\in\operatorname{conv}\tau
.
Since
\tau\in\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)
, this implies that
\tau^{\prime}=\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{m}\mathcal{M}}(\tau)\in\operatorname{Star}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)
. Because of
hypothesis
(3)
,
\boldsymbol{\lvert}\operatorname{Star}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)\boldsymbol{\rvert}

is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and therefore contains at least
one
d
-simplex
\sigma^{\prime}\supseteq\tau^{\prime}
. Since
\sigma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Star}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)
, it follows from the definition of the star that
\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{m}\mathcal{M}}(P\cap B(m,\rho))
. Because of hypothesis

(2)
, the projection

\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{m}\mathcal{M}}
restricted to
P\cap B(m,\rho)
is injective
and therefore there exists a unique
\sigma\subseteq P\cap B(m,\rho)
such that
\sigma^{\prime}=\pi_{\mathbf{T}_{m}\mathcal{M}}(\sigma)
and
furthermore
\sigma^{\prime}\supseteq\tau^{\prime}
implies that
\sigma\supseteq\tau
. Since
\sigma\subseteq P\cap B(m,\rho)
and
\sigma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Star}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)

5
Faithful reconstruction from sampling and safety conditions

In this section, we state our second reconstruction theorem
(Theorem
17

below). The theorem describes geometric conditions on
P
under
which (1)
\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)
is a faithful reconstruction of
\mathcal{M}
and
(2)
\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)
satisfies certain properties that are needed
in our companion paper
[
4
]
. In particular, the theorem provides a
characterization of
d
-simplices in
\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)
as the

d
-simplices that are delloc in
P
at scale
\rho
:

Definition 14

(Delloc simplex)
.

We say that a simplex
\sigma
is
delloc
in
P
at
scale
\rho
if
\sigma\in\operatorname{Del}(\pi_{\operatorname{aff}\sigma}(P\cap B(c_{\sigma},\rho))
.

Note that deciding whether a simplex is delloc does not require the
knowledge of the manifold
\mathcal{M}
. We emphasize the fact that the
characterization of
d
-simplices in the flat Delaunay complex as the
one being delloc turns out to be crucial in our companion
paper
[
4
]
.

In this section, we first introduce the necessary notations and
definitions to describe the geometric conditions on
P
that we
need. We then state our second reconstruction theorem and sketch the proof.

Definition 15

(Dense, accurate, and separated)
.

We say that
P
is an
\varepsilon
-dense
sample of
\mathcal{M}
if for
every point
m\in\mathcal{M}
, there is a point
p\in P
with
\|p-m\|\leq\varepsilon
or, equivalently, if
\mathcal{M}\subseteq P^{\oplus\varepsilon}
. We say that
P
is a
\delta
-accurate
sample of

\mathcal{M}
if for every point
p\in P
, there is a point
m\in\mathcal{M}
with

\|p-m\|\leq\delta
or, equivalently, if
P\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\delta}
. Let
\operatorname{separation}(P)=\min_{p,q\in P}\|p-q\|
.

We stress that our definition of a protected simplex differs slightly
from the one in
[
8
,
7
]
.

Definition 16

(Protection)
.

We say that a non-degenerate simplex
\sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}

is
\zeta
-protected
with respect to

if for all
q\in Q\setminus\sigma
, we
have
d(q,S(\sigma))>\zeta
. We shall simply say that
\sigma
is

protected
with respect to
Q
when it is
0
-protected with respect
to
Q
.

Let
\mathcal{H}(\sigma)=\{\mathbf{T}_{m}\mathcal{M}\mid m\in\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\operatorname{conv}\sigma)\}\cup\{\operatorname{aff}\sigma\}
, and
\Theta(\sigma)=\max_{H_{0},H_{1}\in\mathcal{H}(\sigma)}\angle(H_{0},H_{1})
. To the pair

we now
associate three quantities that describe the

P
at scale
\rho

\operatorname{height}(P,\rho)=\min_{\sigma}0pt{\sigma}
, where the
minimum is over all
\rho

d
-simplices

•

, where
\sigma
ranges over all

\rho
-small
d
-simplices of

;

•

\operatorname{protection}(P,\rho)=\min_{\sigma}\min_{q}d(q,S(\sigma))
, where
the minima are over all
\rho
-small

-simplices

Theorem 17

(Faithful reconstruction from sampling and safety conditions)
.

Let
\varepsilon
,
\delta
,
\rho
,
\theta
be non-negative real
numbers and set
A=4\delta\theta+4\rho\theta^{2}
. Assume that

\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{6}
,
\delta\leq\varepsilon
, and

16\varepsilon\leq\rho<\frac{\mathcal{R}}{4}
. Suppose that
P
satisfies the following sampling conditions:
P
is a

\delta
-accurate
\varepsilon
-dense sample of
\mathcal{M}
. Suppose furthermore that
P
satisfies
the following safety conditions:

(1)

\Theta(P,\rho)\leq\theta-2\arcsin\left(\frac{\rho+\delta}{\mathcal{R}}\right)
.

(2)

\operatorname{separation}(P)>2A+6\delta+\frac{2\rho^{2}}{\mathcal{R}}
;

(3)

\operatorname{height}(P,\rho)>0
and
\operatorname{protection}(P,3\rho)>2A\left(1+\frac{4d\varepsilon}{\operatorname{height}(P,\rho)}\right)
.

Then we have the following properties:

Faithful reconstruction:

\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)
is a faithful reconstruction of
\mathcal{M}
;

Prestar formula:

\operatorname{Prestar}_{P,\mathcal{M}}(m,\rho)=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)\mid m\in\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\operatorname{conv}\sigma)\},\;\forall m\in\mathcal{M}
;

Circumradii:

For all
d
-simplices
\sigma\in\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)
, we have that
R(\sigma)\leq\varepsilon
;

Characterization:

For all
d
-simplices
\sigma

\sigma\in{\operatorname{FlatDel}_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\rho)}\iff\sigma
delloc in
P

at scale
\rho
.

The geometric conditions that we need for our result can be
divided in two groups: the sampling conditions and the safety
conditions. Roughly speaking, the sampling conditions say that

must be “sufficiently” dense and “sufficiently” accurate. The
safety conditions say that (1) the angle that
\rho
-small

-simplices make with “nearby” tangent space to
\mathcal{M}
must be
sufficiently small; (2) points in
P
must be “sufficiently” well

at scale

\rho
must be “sufficiently” lower bounded. Whereas it seems reasonable to assume
that
P
satisfies the samping conditions, it is less clear that, in
practice,

can satisfy both the sampling and safety
conditions. We show in Section
9

that starting from a situation where

satisfies some “strong”

in such a
way that after perturbation,

satisfies both the sampling and

Theorem
17
.

It is easy to see that a delloc simplex

at scale

Our Delaunay stability hypothesis is equivalent to saying that for all

, we have

(a)

\iff

(b
x
)
and for all

)

\iff

(b

)

\|Z(\sigma)-x\|\leq\frac{d\xi^{2}}{20pt\sigma}.

Notice that the above bound becomes meaningless as the simplex
\sigma

becomes degenerate because then the right side of the inequality tends
to
+\infty
. Applying the above lemma in our context, we get the
following lemma:

Lemma 32

(Stability of Delaunay simplices through distortion)
.

Let
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
be two
d
-dimensional affine spaces in

\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Consider a binary relation
\mathscr{R}\subseteq H_{0}\times H_{1}
and suppose that
\mathscr{R}
is an additive
A
-distortion for some

A\geq 0
. Let
\mathscr{Q}\subseteq\mathscr{R}
be a finite one-to-one
relation. Let
Q_{0}=\operatorname{Domain}(\mathscr{Q})
and
Q_{1}=\operatorname{Range}(\mathscr{Q})
. Consider
\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{Q}

such that both
\sigma_{0}=\operatorname{Domain}(\mathscr{S})
and
\sigma_{1}=\operatorname{Range}(\mathscr{S})
are non-degenerate abstract
d
-simplices. Suppose

\sigma_{0}
is
\zeta
-protected with respect to
Q_{0}
. Suppose there
exists
\varepsilon\geq 0
such that for
i\in\{0,1\}

\displaystyle 2A\left(1+\frac{2d\varepsilon}{0pt{\sigma_{i}}}\right)<\zeta.

Then, we have the following two implications:

•

R(\sigma_{0})\leq\varepsilon
,
Z(\sigma_{0})\in\operatorname{Domain}(\mathscr{R})
and
\sigma_{0}\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{0})

\implies

\sigma_{1}\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{1})
and is protected with respect to
Q_{1}
;

•

R(\sigma_{1})\leq\varepsilon
,
Z(\sigma_{1})\in\operatorname{Range}(\mathscr{R})
and
\sigma_{1}\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{1})

\implies

\sigma_{0}\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{0})
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
R(\sigma_{0})\leq\varepsilon
,
Z(\sigma_{0})\in\operatorname{Domain}(\mathscr{R})
and
\sigma_{0}\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{0})
and let us prove that

\sigma_{1}\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{1})
and is protected with respect to
Q_{1}
. In other words, we need to prove that for
all
(a_{0},a_{1})\in\mathscr{S}
and all
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathscr{Q}\setminus\mathscr{S}
, we have
\|a_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|<\|q_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|
. Let
z_{1}\in\operatorname{Range}(\mathscr{R})
such that
(Z(\sigma_{0}),z_{1})\in\mathscr{R}
. On one hand, for all
(a_{0},a_{1})\in\mathscr{S}
, we have:

\displaystyle\|a_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|a_{1}-z_{1}\|+\|z_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|

\displaystyle\leq A+\|a_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|+\|z_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|

\displaystyle\leq A+R(\sigma_{0})+\|z_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|.

(4)

On the other hand, for all
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathscr{Q}\setminus\mathscr{S}
, we have:

\displaystyle\|q_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|

\displaystyle\geq\|q_{1}-z_{1}\|-\|z_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|

\displaystyle\geq\|q_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|-A-\|z_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|

\displaystyle\geq R(\sigma_{0})+\zeta-A-\|z_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|.

(5)

Thus, we obtain that
\|a_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|<\|q_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|
as soon
as the right side of (
4
) is smaller
than the right side of
(
5
), that is, as soon as:

2\|z_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|+2A<\zeta.

(6)

Because for all
a_{1}\in\sigma_{1}
we have
R(\sigma_{0})-A\leq\|z_{1}-a_{1}\|\leq R(\sigma_{0})+A
, we get that for all

a_{1},a_{1}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{1}
:

\left\lvert\|z_{1}-a_{1}\|^{2}-\|z_{1}-a_{1}^{\prime}\|^{2}\right\rvert\leq(R(\sigma_{0})+A)^{2}-(R(\sigma_{0})-A)^{2}=4AR(\sigma_{0}).

Applying Lemma
31
, we obtain that

\|z_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|\leq\frac{2AdR(\sigma_{0})}{0pt{\sigma_{1}}}.

Using this inequality, we get that Inequality
(
6
) holds as soon as

2A\left(1+\frac{2dR(\sigma_{0})}{0pt{\sigma_{1}}}\right)<\zeta

which follows directly from our assumptions. Thus,
\sigma_{1}\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{1})
. Suppose now that
R(\sigma_{1})\leq\varepsilon
,

Z(\sigma_{1})\in\operatorname{Range}(\mathscr{R})
and
\sigma_{0}\not\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{0})

and let us prove that
\sigma_{1}\not\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{1})
. Because

\sigma_{0}\not\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{0})
, there exists
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathscr{Q}

such that
\|q_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|<R(\sigma_{0})
and because
\sigma_{0}

is
\zeta
-protected with respect to
Q_{0}
, we have

\|q_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|<\|a_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|-\zeta
for all pairs

(a_{0},a_{1})\in\mathscr{S}
. Let us prove that
\|q_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|<\|a_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|
for any
(a_{0},a_{1})\in\mathscr{S}
. Let
z_{0}\in\operatorname{Domain}(\mathscr{R})
such that

(z_{0},Z(\sigma_{1}))\in\mathscr{R}
. On one hand, we have:

\displaystyle\|q_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|q_{0}-z_{0}\|+A

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|+\|Z(\sigma_{0})-q_{0}\|+A

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|+R(\sigma_{0})-\zeta+A

(7)

On the other hand, for any
(a_{0},a_{1})\in\mathscr{S}
, we have:

\displaystyle\|a_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|

\displaystyle\geq\|a_{0}-z_{0}\|-A

\displaystyle\geq\|a_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|-\|z_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|-A

\displaystyle\geq R(\sigma_{0})-\|z_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|-A

(8)

Thus, we obtain that
\|q_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|<\|a_{1}-Z(\sigma_{1})\|
as soon as the
right side of (
7
) is smaller than the right side of
(
8
), that is, as soon as:

2\|z_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|+2A<\zeta.

(9)

Because for all
(a_{0},a_{1})\in\mathscr{S}
, we have

R(\sigma_{1})-A\leq\|z_{0}-a_{0}\|\leq R(\sigma_{1})+A
, we get that for all
a_{0},a_{0}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{0}
:

\left\lvert\|z_{0}-a_{0}\|^{2}-\|z_{0}-a_{0}^{\prime}\|^{2}\right\rvert\leq(R(\sigma_{1})+A)^{2}-(R(\sigma_{1})-A)^{2}=4AR(\sigma_{1}).

Applying Lemma
31
, we obtain that

\|z_{0}-Z(\sigma_{0})\|\leq\frac{2AdR(\sigma_{1})}{0pt{\sigma_{0}}}

Using this inequality, we get that Inequality
(
9
) holds as soon as

2A\left(1+\frac{2dR(\sigma_{1})}{0pt{\sigma_{0}}}\right)<\zeta

which follows directly from our assumptions. Thus,
\sigma_{1}\not\in\operatorname{Del}(Q_{1})
.
∎

7.2
Specific distortions

We now consider relations of the form
\mathscr{R}=\{(\pi_{H_{0}}(x),\pi_{H_{1}}(x))\mid x\in X\}
and find values of
A

for which
\mathscr{R}
is an additive
A
-distortion. We consider first
the case of a set
X
contained in
\mathcal{M}
in Lemma

33
(non-noisy
case) before handling the case of a set
X
contained in
M^{\oplus\delta}
in Lemma
34

(noisy case).

Lemma 33

(Distortion in the non-noisy case)
.

Consider a subset
U\subseteq\mathcal{M}
and two
d
-dimensional spaces

H_{0}
and
H_{1}
. Suppose that there is
\theta\leq 1
such that for

i\in\{0,1\}

\sup_{u,u^{\prime}\in U}\angle(H_{i},uu^{\prime})\leq\theta.

Then,

\mathscr{R}=\{(\pi_{H_{0}}(u),\pi_{H_{1}}(u))\mid u\in U\}

is an additive
(\operatorname{Diam}(U)\times\theta^{2})
-distortion.

Proof.

Note that for all

u,u^{\prime}\in U
:

\displaystyle\cos\theta\times\|u^{\prime}-u\|

\displaystyle~~\leq~~\|\pi_{H_{0}}(u^{\prime})-\pi_{H_{0}}(u)\|

\displaystyle~~\leq~~\|u^{\prime}-u\|,

\displaystyle\cos\theta\times\|u^{\prime}-u\|

\displaystyle~~\leq~~\|\pi_{H_{1}}(u^{\prime})-\pi_{H_{1}}(u)\|

\displaystyle~~\leq~~\|u^{\prime}-u\|.

Hence, for all
u,u^{\prime}\in U
,

\cos\theta\times\|\pi_{H_{0}}(u^{\prime})-\pi_{H_{0}}(u)\|\leq\|\pi_{H_{1}}(u^{\prime})-\pi_{H_{1}}(u)\|\leq\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\times\|\pi_{H_{0}}(u^{\prime})-\pi_{H_{0}}(u)\|

Thus,
\mathscr{R}
is a multiplicative
\left(\frac{1-\cos\theta}{\cos\theta}\right)
-distortion. Noting that for all
t
, we
have
1-\cos(t)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}
and using
\theta\leq 1
, we
obtain that
\frac{1-\cos\theta}{\cos\theta}\leq\frac{\theta^{2}}{2-\theta^{2}}\leq\theta^{2}
and therefore
\mathscr{R}

is a multiplicative
\theta^{2}
-distortion. Applying Lemma

30
, it follows that
\mathscr{R}

is an additive
(\operatorname{Diam}(U)\times\theta^{2})
-distortion.
∎

Lemma 34

(Distortion in the noisy case)
.

Suppose
P\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\delta}
for some
\delta\leq\frac{\mathcal{R}}{2}
. Consider a point
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and two
d
-dimensional spaces
H_{0}
and

H_{1}
. Suppose that there is
\theta\leq 1
such that for
i\in\{0,1\}

\sup_{m,m^{\prime}\in\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\delta}\cap B(z,\rho))}\angle(H_{i},mm^{\prime})\leq\theta.

Then, the binary relation
\mathscr{R}=\{(\pi_{H_{0}}(a),\pi_{H_{1}}(a))\mid a\in\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\delta}\cap B(z,\rho)\}
is an additive

A
-distortion for
A=4\delta\theta+4\rho\theta^{2}
. If
furthermore
\operatorname{separation}(P)>2A+6\delta
, the
restricted relation
\mathscr{Q}=\{(\pi_{H_{0}}(p),\pi_{H_{1}}(p))\mid p\in P\cap B(z,\rho)\}
is one-to-one.

Proof.

Whenever the projection of a point
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
onto
\mathcal{M}
is well-defined,
let us write
a^{*}=\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(a)
for short. Observe that for all
a\in\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\delta}\cap B(z,\rho)
and all
i\in\{0,1\}
,

\angle(H_{i},\mathbf{T}_{a^{*}}\mathcal{M})\leq\theta
and consequently,

\|\pi_{H_{i}}(a)-\pi_{H_{i}}(a^{*})\|\leq\delta\sin\theta.

Let us bound from above the diameter of the set
U=\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\delta}\cap B(z,\rho))
. We know from
[
14
, page 435]

that for
0\leq\delta<\mathcal{R}
the projection map
\pi_{\mathcal{M}}

onto
\mathcal{M}
is
\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{R}-\delta}\right)
-Lipschitz for points at distance less than
\delta
from

\mathcal{M}
. For any two points
a,b\in\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\delta}\cap B(z,\rho)
,
we thus have

\|a^{*}-b^{*}\|\leq\frac{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{R}-\delta}\times\|a-b\|\leq 4\rho

and therefore
\operatorname{Diam}(U)\leq 4\rho
. Applying Lemma

33
, we get that for all

a,b\in\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\delta}\cap B(z,\rho)
:

\left\lvert\|\pi_{H_{1}}(a^{*})-\pi_{H_{1}}(b^{*})\|-\|\pi_{H_{0}}(a^{*})-\pi_{H_{0}}(b^{*})\|\right\rvert\leq 4\rho\theta^{2}.

Let us introduce
\Delta_{i}=\|\pi_{H_{i}}(a)-\pi_{H_{i}}(b)\|
and

\Delta_{i}^{*}=\|\pi_{H_{i}}(a^{*})-\pi_{H_{i}}(b^{*})\|
. We have

|\Delta_{i}-\Delta_{i}^{*}|\leq\|\pi_{H_{i}}(a)-\pi_{H_{i}}(a^{*})\|+\|\pi_{H_{i}}(b)-\pi_{H_{i}}(b^{*})\|\leq 2\delta\theta

and therefore
|\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{0}|\leq|\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{1}^{*}|+|\Delta_{1}^{*}-\Delta_{0}^{*}|+|\Delta_{0}^{*}-\Delta_{0}|\leq 4\delta\theta+4\rho\theta^{2}
. It follows that
\mathscr{R}
is an
additive
A
-distortion for
A=4\delta\theta+4\rho\theta^{2}

and so is its restricted relation
\mathscr{Q}
. Writing
Q_{0}=\operatorname{Domain}(\mathscr{Q})
, we now suppose in addition that
\operatorname{separation}(P)>2A+6\delta
and deduce that
\operatorname{separation}(Q_{0})>A
. Using

\cos\theta\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we get that for all
a,b\in P\cap B(z,\rho)

\displaystyle\|\pi_{H_{0}}(a)-\pi_{H_{0}}(b)\|

\displaystyle\geq\|\pi_{H_{0}}(a^{*})-\pi_{H_{0}}(b^{*})\|-\|\pi_{H_{0}}(a)-\pi_{H_{0}}(a^{*})\|-\|\pi_{H_{0}}(b^{*})-\pi_{H_{0}}(b)\|

\displaystyle\geq\|a^{*}-b^{*}\|\cos\theta-2\delta\theta

\displaystyle\geq\left(\|a-b\|-\|a-a^{*}\|-\|b-b^{*}\|\right)\cos\theta-2\delta\theta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|a-b\|-2\delta\right)-2\delta\theta.

Thus,
\operatorname{separation}(Q_{0})\geq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{separation}(P)-3\delta>A
. Applying Lemma
30
, we
get that
\mathscr{Q}
is a multiplicative
\Psi
-distortion for

\Psi=\frac{A}{\operatorname{separation}(Q_{0})-A}
and using Remark
28
, we conclude that

\mathscr{Q}
is one-to-one.
∎

7.3
Technical lemma

The next lemma provides conditions under which
\sigma
is Delaunay
stable for
P
at scale
\rho
with respect to
\{(h_{i},H_{i})\}_{i\in\{0,1\}}
. Roughly speaking, our conditions say that for each pair

(h_{i},H_{i})
, we need
h_{i}
to be “close” to
\mathcal{M}
,
h_{0}
and
h_{1}

to be “close” to one another and

to make a
“small” angle with
\mathcal{M}
“near”
\operatorname{conv}\sigma
. Precisely:

Lemma 35

(Technical lemma)
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
,
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{\rho}{16}
,
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{6}
and
A=4\delta\theta+4\rho\theta^{2}
and assume that

P\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\oplus\delta}
,
\mathcal{M}\subseteq P^{\oplus\varepsilon}
and
\operatorname{separation}(P)>2A+6\delta
. Consider a
d
-simplex
\sigma\subseteq P
, a

d
-dimensional space
H_{0}
passing through a point
h_{0}
and a

d
-dimensional space
H_{1}
passing through a point
h_{1}
. For

, write
\sigma_{i}=\pi_{H_{i}}(\sigma)
. Suppose that

\sigma_{0}
is
\zeta
-protected with respect to
\pi_{H_{0}}(P\cap B(h_{0},2\rho))

(1)

For

\sigma_{i}
has dimension
d

(2)

For

(3)

For

\|h_{0}-h_{1}\|\leq 4\varepsilon

or

(6)

For

\sigma

at scale

•

\sigma\subseteq P\cap B(h_{1},\rho)
and

.

is protected with respect to

,

and

.

(b)

Let us prove

\implies

. Suppose

While assuming the sample to be

for a standard procedure

\delta
-accurate
\varepsilon
-dense sample of
\mathcal{M}
and
perturbe it to obtain a point set

that satisfies the assumptions
of our main theorem. For this, we use the Moser Tardos Algorithm

[
17
]
as a perturbation scheme in the spirit of
what is done in
[

, Section 5.3.4]
.

The perturbation scheme is parametrized with real numbers
\rho\geq 0
,
r_{\operatorname{pert.}}\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Heigh}_{\min}>0
, and

\mathrm{Prot}_{\min}>0
. To describe it, we need some notations and
terminology. Let
\tilde{T}_{p}=T_{p}(P,3\rho)
be the
d
-dimensional

and parallel to the
d
-dimensional

To each point

associated to the point

, the

of

is the

q
uniformely at random in

q
to

p^{\prime}
. Finally, we call any of the two situations below a

:

Violation of the height condition by
\sigma^{\prime}
:

A
\rho
-small
d
-simplex

Violation of the protection condition by

A pair
(p^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})

d
-simplex

----- END WHILE

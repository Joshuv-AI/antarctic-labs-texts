Shift-Invariance of the Colored TASEP and Finishing Times of the Oriented Swap Process
Lingfu Zhang

Abstract
We prove a new shift-invariance property of the colored TASEP. From the shift-invariance of the colored stochastic six-vertex model (proved in Borodin-Gorin-Wheeler or Galashin), one can get a shift-invariance property of the colored TASEP at one time, and our result generalizes this to multiple times. Our proof takes the single-time shift-invariance as an input, and uses analyticity of the probability functions and induction arguments. We apply our shift-invariance to prove a distributional identity between the finishing times of the oriented swap process and the point-to-line passage times in exponential last-passage percolation, which is conjectured by Bisi-Cunden-Gibbons-Romik and Bufetov-Gorin-Romik, and is also equivalent to a purely combinatorial identity related to the Edelman-Greene correspondence. With known results from last-passage percolation, we also get new asymptotic results on the colored TASEP and the finishing times of the oriented swap process.



1
Introduction

The Totally Asymmetric Simple Exclusion Process (TASEP) is a classical interacting particle system, where one considers a collection of particles in
\mathbb{Z}
, such that each site contains at most one particle. There is an independent Poisson clock on each edge
(x,x+1)
, such that when it rings, if there is a particle at site
x
, and the site
x+1
is empty (in other words, there is a hole at site
x+1
), then the particle jumps to site
x+1
.
See e.g. the book of Liggett
[
Lig12
]
and references therein.

{sec:intro}

The colored TASEP is a variant of this model: there is a particle at each site in
\mathbb{Z}
, and each particle has an additional property called
color
, which is usually integer-valued. A particle with a smaller color is considered ‘stronger’ than a particle with a larger color: when the Poisson clock on edge
(x,x+1)
rings, if the particles on sites
x
and
x+1
have colors
i
and
j
respectively, then they will swap if and only if
i<j
.
The uncolored TASEP can be viewed as a colored TASEP with two colors: particles with the larger color correspond to holes, and particles with the smaller color correspond to particles.
Another degeneration of the colored TASEP is TASEP with second-class particles.
In these models, there are normal particles, second-class particles, and holes on
\mathbb{Z}
.
The rule is that a normal or second-class particle can jump to a hole right next to it, and a normal particle can swap with a second-class particle right next to it.
These models involving second-class particles have been proven powerful in understanding the evolution of the TASEP and related exactly solvable models

[
FKS91
,
Fer92
,
DJLS93
,
Spe94
,
BCS06
,
BS10
,
MSZ21
]
.

In this paper, we consider the following colored TASEP, studied in e.g.
[
AHR09
,
AAV11
,
Buf20
,
BF22
,
BB21
]
. The particles have mutually different colors, and initially, the particle at each site
x
is colored
x
.
The configuration of this process at any time
t\geq 0
can be viewed as a bijection
\zeta_{t}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
, where
\zeta_{t}(x)
is the color of the particle at site
x
at time
t
. Then
\zeta_{0}
is the identity map, and
t\mapsto\zeta_{t}(x)
is a cadlag function for each
x\in\mathbb{Z}
.
There are several distributional identities for this process, due to natural symmetries.
For example, take any
y\in\mathbb{Z}
, the function
x\mapsto\zeta_{t}(x-y)+y
has the same distribution as
\zeta_{t}
.
There is also a reflection symmetry, which says that
x\mapsto-\zeta_{t}(-x)
has the same distribution as
\zeta_{t}
.
There are also some less obvious equalities in distribution or symmetries that have been proved previously. For example, in
[
AAV11
]
, it is proved that
\zeta_{t}
and its inverse (as a permutation of
\mathbb{Z}
) have the same distribution.
A version of this symmetry in a finite interval is given in
[
AHR09
]
.
A more general color-to-position symmetry is also proved in
[
BB21
]
(and also
[
Buf20
]
).

We present a new shift-invariance property, concerning the multi-time distribution of this colored TASEP.
It is stated for the distribution of ‘passage times’, a notion coming from the connection between TASEP and the directed last-passage percolation (LPP) with i.i.d. exponential weights, and the distribution of related objects such as the geodesics.
A main question on the colored TASEP is about the scaling limit of the passage times.
Our shift-invariance implies convergence of the colored TASEP passage times on some ‘ordered sets’ to the Airy sheet, a universal 2D random process constructed in
[
DOV18
]
and proven to be the scaling limit of LPP and its variants in
[
DV21
]
.

Another major application of our result is to prove a conjectured identity between LPP and the oriented swap process (OSP), a type of random sorting network that can be viewed as the colored TASEP in a finite interval.
Using this identity, we can use results from LPP to deduce asymptotic results of OSP.
For example, we show that the vector of the finishing times of OSP convergences to the Airy
2
process, under the KPZ scaling; and the site where the last swap happens has fluctuation in the order of
N^{2/3}
(for OSP with
N
numbers). On a smaller scale, the finishing times converge to random walks in total variation distance.

Our shift-invariance is related to various other hidden invariance in exactly solvable models, from
[
BGW22
,
Dau22a
,
Gal21
]
, as will be discussed in Section
1.2.2
; however, the approach we take is quite different from these previous works. In particular, we avoid working with formulas from algebraic combinatorics or representation theory.
Our starting point is a crucial input of a shift-invariance property of the colored stochastic six-vertex model, proved in
[
Gal21
]
.
Our central idea is to use the analyticity of the distribution functions, combined with the independence properties of the colored TASEP in different space-time areas.
See Section
2
for a more detailed explanation of our strategy, with a simple example.

1.1
Passage times and last-passage percolation

To state our results we start with the following setup.
For the colored TASEP starting from the identity map, it is also equivalent to the coupling of a family of (uncolored) TASEPs, starting with step initial conditions.
More precisely, for each
A\in\mathbb{Z}
, we consider a TASEP such that initially, there is a particle on each site
\leq A
, and each site
>A
is empty.
For time
t\geq 0
, we denote the configuration as
\mu^{A}_{t}:\mathbb{Z}\to\{0,\infty\}
, where
0
denotes a particle and
\infty
denotes a hole.
We couple
\mu^{A}=(\mu^{A}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
for all
A\in\mathbb{Z}
, such that the jump of particles follow the same Poisson clock.
Such
\mu^{A}
can be obtained from
\zeta=(\zeta_{t})_{t\geq 0}
: we just let
\mu^{A}_{t}(x)=0
if
\zeta_{t}(x)\leq A
, and
\mu^{A}_{t}(x)=\infty
if
\zeta_{t}(x)>A
.
We can also recover
\zeta
from such
\mu^{A}
for all
A\in\mathbb{Z}
,
by letting
\zeta_{t}(x)=\min\{A\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu^{A}_{t}(x)=0\}
.

The evolution of the colored TASEP
\zeta
can also be described by the following family of random variables, which we call
passage times
.
For each
A\in\mathbb{Z}
and
B,C\in\mathbb{N}
, denote

\displaystyle T_{B,C}^{A}

\displaystyle=\inf\{t\geq 0:|\{x\geq A+B+1-C:\zeta_{t}(x)\leq A\}|\geq C\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{t\geq 0:|\{x\geq A+B+1-C:\mu^{A}_{t}(x)=0\}|\geq C\},

i.e.,
T_{B,C}^{A}
is the first time, when in
\mu^{A}
there are at least
C
particles on or to the right of site
A+B+1-C
.
In other words,
T_{B,C}^{A}
is the time when the
B
-th leftmost hole swaps with the
C
-th rightmost particle in
\mu^{A}
.
From
\{T_{B,C}^{A}\}_{B,C\in\mathbb{N}}
we can recover the evolution of
\mu^{A}
.
We take such random variables and notations partially due to the connection between (uncolored) TASEP and LPP, dating back to Rost
[
Ros81
]
.
We now state this connection, and we start by formally defining the model of 2D LPP (with i.i.d. exponential weights).

To each vertex
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we associate an independent weight
\omega(v)
with
\Exp(1)
distribution.
For two vertices
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we say
u\leq v
if
u
is coordinate-wise less than or equal to
v
.
For such
u,v
and any up-right path
\gamma
from
u
to
v
, we define the
passage time of the path
to be

L(\gamma):=\sum_{w\in\gamma}\omega(w).

Then almost surely there is a unique up-right path from
u
to
v
that has the largest passage time.
We will always assume such uniqueness in this paper, and we call this path the
geodesic

\Gamma_{u,v}
, and call
L_{u,v}:=L(\Gamma_{u,v})
the
passage time from
u
to
v
.

For any
A\in\mathbb{Z}
, we can couple
\mu^{A}
with a family of i.i.d.
\Exp(1)
random variables
\{\omega^{A}(v)\}_{v\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}
, where for any
B,C\in\mathbb{N}
,
\omega^{A}((B,C))
is the waiting time for the
B
-th leftmost hole to swap with the
C
-th rightmost particle.
We note that at any time
t
, the
B
-th leftmost hole is to the right of the
C
-th rightmost particle, if and only if there are at least
C
particles on or to the right of site
A+B+1-C
.
Thus for the swap (between the
B
-th leftmost hole and the
C
-th rightmost particle), one starts the ‘waiting’ when there are
C-1
particles on or to the right of site
A+B+2-C
, and another particle arrives at site
A+B-C
.
Thus we can formally write

\omega^{A}((B,C))=T^{A}_{B,C}-T^{A}_{B-1,C}\vee T^{A}_{B,C-1},

where we assume that
T^{A}_{B-1,C}=0
if
B=1
, and
T^{A}_{B,C-1}=0
if
C=1
.
Such
\omega^{A}((B,C))
are i.i.d.
\Exp(1)
for
B,C\in\mathbb{N}
, since in
\mu^{A}
, the jumps happen with rate
1
independently.

Let
L^{A}_{u,v}
denote the passage time from
u
to
v
under the random field
\omega^{A}
.
Then we have
L^{A}_{(1,1),(B,C)}=T^{A}_{B,C}
for any
B,C\in\mathbb{N}
, which explains the name of ‘passage times’ for
T^{A}_{B,C}
.

From this connection between LPP and TASEP, we get a coupling of random fields
\omega^{A}
for different
A
.
We now give a more direct but slightly more involved description of this coupling.
From
\omega^{A}
one can get
\omega^{A+1}
in the following way.
Take a family of i.i.d.
\Exp(1)
random variables
\{E_{i,j}\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
, independent of
\omega^{A}
.
We then recursively define a function
\pi:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{N}
and a sequence of times
\{J_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
, as follows.
Let
\pi(0)=1
and
J_{0}=0
.
Given any
\pi(i)
and
J_{i}
, we let

\pi(i+1)=\inf\{j\geq\pi(i):L^{A}_{(1,1),(i+1,j)}-L^{A}_{(1,1),(i+2,j-1)}\vee J_{i}\geq E_{i+1,j}\},

and
J_{i+1}=L^{A}_{(1,1),(i+2,\pi(i+1)-1)}\vee J_{i}+E_{i+1,\pi(i+1)}
.
Then for
B,C\in\mathbb{N}
, we let

L^{A+1}_{(1,1),(B,C)}=\begin{cases}L^{A}_{(1,1),(B,C-1)},\quad&C>\pi(B),\\
L^{A}_{(1,1),(B+1,C)},\quad&C<\pi(B),\\
J_{B},\quad&C=\pi(B).\end{cases}

From this we can get
\omega^{A+1}
via
\omega^{A+1}_{B,C}=L^{A+1}_{(1,1),(B,C)}-L^{A+1}_{(1,1),(B-1,C)}\vee L^{A+1}_{(1,1),(B,C-1)}
.
We note that the above random variables are defined from the colored TASEP: each
E_{i,j}
corresponds to the waiting time for the
i
-th right jump of the particle colored
A+1
, when there are
j-1
particles with smaller colors to its right; each
J_{i}
corresponds to the time when the particle colored
A+1
makes the
i
-th jump to the right, and
\pi(i)-1
corresponds to the number of particles with smaller colors to its right at time
J_{i}
.

In other words, the function
\pi
splits
\omega^{A}
into two parts; and by shifting the upper left part by
(0,1)
and shifting the lower right part by
(-1,0)
, we get
\omega^{A+1}
, except for some vertices around the boundary.
By repeating this procedure, we can get
\omega^{A^{\prime}}
for any
A^{\prime}>A
; and by the reflection symmetry, we can do a similar procedure to get
\omega^{A^{\prime}}
for any
A^{\prime}<A
.
More precisely, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find non-decreasing function
\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, with
\pi_{1}<\cdots<\pi_{k}
, such that they split
\omega^{A}
into
k+1
parts.
For the
i
-th part from the top, we shift it by
(1-i,k+1-i)
, and we get
\omega^{A+k}
, except for vertices around the boundaries.

1.2
Colored TASEP identities

{ssec:colortasep-iden}

We now state our shift-invariance of
\zeta
, using a graphical representation.
For each
A\in\mathbb{Z}
and
B,C\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{R}^{A}_{B,C}
be the rectangle of lattice points
[1+A,B+A]\times[1-A,C-A]\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. From the connection with LPP,
T^{A}_{B,C}
can be thought of as the passage time from the bottom-left corner to the up-right corner of
\mathcal{R}^{A}_{B,C}
, where the random field is
\omega^{A}
shifted by
(A,-A)
.

Ordering of rectangles.

We make the collection of all such rectangles a partially ordered set, as follows.
For two rectangles
\mathcal{R},\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we say
\mathcal{R}\leq\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
if the projection of
\mathcal{R}
onto the first coordinate contains the projection of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
onto the first coordinate, and the projection of
\mathcal{R}
onto the second coordinate is contained in the projection of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
onto the second coordinate.
In other words, suppose
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{A}_{B,C}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}^{A^{\prime}}_{B^{\prime},C^{\prime}}
, then
\mathcal{R}\leq\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
if and only if
A\leq A^{\prime}
and
A+B\geq A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}
,
A-C\geq A^{\prime}-C^{\prime}
.
We say that
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
are
ordered
if
\mathcal{R}\leq\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
or
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\leq\mathcal{R}
.
This ordering might look artificial, but it is crucial for our shift-invariance. See the discussions in Section
6.1
.

Our main result states that, if certain ordering relations are preserved, one can shift these rectangles while the joint distribution of the passage times is invariant.

Theorem 1.1
.

{thm:main-de}

Let
g\in\mathbb{N}
,
k_{1},\ldots,k_{g}\in\mathbb{N}
, and take
A_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
B_{i,j},C_{i,j}\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
1\leq i\leq g
and
1\leq j\leq k_{i}
.
Let
1\leq\iota<g
, and for any
1\leq i\leq g
and
1\leq j\leq k_{i}
we let
A_{i,j}^{+}=A_{i,j}+\mathds{1}[i>\iota]
. Suppose that for any
1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq g
, and
1\leq j\leq k_{i},1\leq j^{\prime}\leq k_{i^{\prime}}
we have
\mathcal{R}^{A_{i,j}}_{B_{i,j},C_{i,j}}\leq\mathcal{R}^{A_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}}_{B_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}},C_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}}
and
\mathcal{R}^{A_{i,j}^{+}}_{B_{i,j},C_{i,j}}\leq\mathcal{R}^{A_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{+}}_{B_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}},C_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}}
.
Then the vectors

\{\max_{1\leq j\leq k_{i}}T_{B_{i,j},C_{i,j}}^{A_{i,j}}\}_{i=1}^{g}
and
\{\max_{1\leq j\leq k_{i}}T_{B_{i,j},C_{i,j}}^{A_{i,j}^{+}}\}_{i=1}^{g}
are equal in distribution.

In words, we can take a collection of passage times and divide them into groups, and shift the color parameters (i.e.,
A_{i,j}
) for some groups.
As long as some ordering across groups always holds, the joint distribution of the maximums in different groups remains unchanged.

We also state the shift-invariance in another setting,
with some slightly different constraints on the parameters.

Theorem 1.3
.

{thm:main-sa}

Let
g\in\mathbb{N}
, and
A_{i},A_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
B_{i},C_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
for each
1\leq i\leq g
.
Let
V_{i}
be the rectangle consisting of all
(B,C)\in[1,B_{i}]\times[1,C_{i}]\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, such that
\mathcal{R}^{A_{j}}_{B_{j},C_{j}}
and
\mathcal{R}^{A_{i}}_{B,C}
are ordered,
\mathcal{R}^{A_{j}^{\prime}}_{B_{j},C_{j}}
and
\mathcal{R}^{A_{i}^{\prime}}_{B,C}
are ordered, for each
1\leq j\leq g
.
Then
\{\{T^{A_{i}}_{B,C}\}_{(B,C)\in V_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{g}
has the same distribution as
\{\{T^{A_{i}^{\prime}}_{B,C}\}_{(B,C)\in V_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{g}
.

From these identities, we can get invariance for some related objects. For example, in LPP one can locally construct geodesics using passage times, thus we immediately get the following equality in distribution for geodesics.

For each
A\in\mathbb{Z}
and
B,C\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
\Gamma^{A}_{B,C}
be the LPP geodesic from
(1,1)
to
(B,C)
, with the random field
\omega^{A}
given by
\mu^{A}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

{cor:main-geo}

In the setting of Theorem
1.3
, let
W_{i}\subset V_{i}
consist of all
(B,C)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, such that
\{(B-1,C),(B,C-1)\}\cap\mathbb{N}^{2}\subset V_{i}
.
Then
\{\Gamma^{A_{i}}_{B_{i},C_{i}}\cap W_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
has the same distribution as

1.2.1
Convergence to the Airy sheet

A natural question to ask is about the scaling limit of the colored TASEP.
Let’s first consider the following scaling limit of LPP, the Airy sheet
\mathcal{S}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, constructed in
[
DOV18
]
.
For each
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
y\mapsto\mathcal{S}(x,x+y)+y^{2}
is the so-called Airy
2
process on
\mathbb{R}
from
[
PS02
]
, which is stationary and ergodic, and has the GUE Tracy-Widom one-point distributions.
The convergence from LPP to
\mathcal{S}
can be stated as follows. Take
\mathcal{S}_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
where

\mathcal{S}_{n}(x,y)=2^{-4/3}n^{-1/3}(L_{(1+\lfloor 2^{2/3}n^{2/3}x\rfloor,1-\lfloor 2^{2/3}n^{2/3}x\rfloor),(n+\lfloor 2^{2/3}n^{2/3}y\rfloor,n-\lfloor 2^{2/3}n^{2/3}y\rfloor)}-4n).

In words,
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is a rescaled version of all the LPP passage times, for two endpoints varying in lines
\{(1+i,1-i):i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and
\{(n+i,n-i):i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.
In
[
DV21
]
it is proved that
\mathcal{S}_{n}\to\mathcal{S}
weakly, in the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.

We note that for each fixed
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\{L_{(1+i,1-i),v}\}_{(1+i,1-i)\leq v}
is equivalent to the evolution of an (uncolored) TASEP with step initial condition, via the coupling between LPP and TASEP.
Thus LPP with starting point varying in
\{(1+i,1-i):i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
gives a coupling of a family of (uncolored) step initial TASEPs, indexed by
\mathbb{Z}
. Its law is different from that of
\{\mu^{A}\}_{A\in\mathbb{Z}}
, given by the colored TASEP.
For the scaling limit of the colored TASEP, it is also believed to be the Airy sheet, i.e., these two families of (uncolored) step initial TASEP have the same scaling limit.
We state it using the passage times of the colored TASEP.

Question 1.5
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the random function, such that

\mathcal{S}_{n}^{*}(x,y)=2^{-4/3}n^{-1/3}(T_{n+\lfloor 2^{2/3}n^{2/3}y\rfloor-\lfloor 2^{2/3}n^{2/3}x\rfloor,n-\lfloor 2^{2/3}n^{2/3}y\rfloor+\lfloor 2^{2/3}n^{2/3}x\rfloor}^{\lfloor 2^{2/3}n^{2/3}x\rfloor}-4n),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Find the limit of
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{*}
, as
n\to\infty
. In particular, does the limit have the same distribution as
\mathcal{S}
?

Combined with the LPP shift-invariance proved in
[
Dau22a
]
, and the convergence of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
, our Theorem
1.1
or
1.3
implies the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

{thm:conv-colored}

Take any
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that for any
u=(u_{1},u_{2}),v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\Theta
, there is either
u_{1}\leq v_{1}
and
u_{2}\geq v_{2}
, or
u_{1}\geq v_{1}
and
u_{2}\leq v_{2}
.
Then
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{*}\to\mathcal{S}
in
\Theta
, weakly in the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.

1.2.2
Discussions on related hidden invariances

{ssec:dis}

We now explain the relation between our shift-invariance, and various other hidden invariance in exactly solvable models, from
[
BGW22
,
Dau22a
,
Gal21
]
.

With the connection to LPP, our results take a similar form as those in
[
Dau22a
]
. In particular, for the ordered relation between two lattice rectangles
\mathcal{R},\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
, it is equivalent to the following notion widely used in
[
Dau22a
]
: for any up-right paths from the bottom-left corner to the up-right corner in
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
respectively, they must intersect.
However, as noted above, for
\mu^{A}
with different
A\in\mathbb{Z}
, the corresponding LPP random fields are different and coupled in a non-trivial way, thus the above identities do not follow from the results in
[
Dau22a
]
. We also do not see how our results can be proved using the framework or similar arguments as those in
[
Dau22a
]
, via certain conditional independence obtained from the RSK correspondence.

In
[
BGW22
,
Gal21
]
, certain shift- and flip-invariance statements are proved for the colored stochastic six-vertex model.
Our colored TASEP can be obtained from the colored stochastic six-vertex model, by passing the parameters there to a certain limit.
As pointed out in
[
BGR22
, Remark 2.3]
, when passing the shift- or flip-invariance statements from the colored stochastic six-vertex model to the colored TASEP, one gets equalities in distribution at a single time (see e.g.
[
BGR22
, Theorem 2.2]
).
More precisely, we define the height function as

\mathcal{H}^{A}(x,t)=\text{number of particles on or to the right of $x$ with color $\leq A$ at time $t$,}

for any
A,x\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\geq 0
. This is degenerated from the height function of the colored stochastic six-vertex model, as appeared in
[
BGW22
,
Gal21
]
and also below.
The single-time equality in distribution is the following result.

Theorem 1.7
.

{thm:eqgal}

For any
t\geq 0
,
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
,
\{A_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,

\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell},\{C_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
,
\{x_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{C_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, such that

A_{1},\ldots,A_{\ell}<A_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,A_{n}^{\prime},\quad x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\geq x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n}^{\prime},

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{H}^{A_{i}}(x_{i},t)\geq C_{i},\mathcal{H}^{A_{j}^{\prime}}(x_{j}^{\prime},t)\geq C_{j}^{\prime},\forall 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq\ell\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{H}^{A_{i}+1}(x_{i}+1,t)\geq C_{i},\mathcal{H}^{A_{j}^{\prime}}(x_{j}^{\prime},t)\geq C_{j}^{\prime},\forall 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq\ell\right]

In
[
BGR22
]
, a slightly weaker result is stated (
[
BGR22
, Theorem 2.2]
).
Our Theorem
1.1
generalizes Theorem
1.7
to the multiple time setting. For comparison, we restate Theorem
1.1
using the height function, as follows.

Theorem 1.1 (Height Function Version)
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{N}
,
k_{1},\ldots,k_{g}\in\mathbb{N}
, and take
A_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x_{i,j},C_{i,j}\in\mathbb{N}
,
t_{i}\geq 0
, for all
1\leq i\leq g
and
1\leq j\leq k_{i}
.
Let
1\leq\iota<g
, and for any
1\leq i\leq g
and
1\leq j\leq k_{i}
we let
A_{i,j}^{+}=A_{i,j}+\mathds{1}[i>\iota]
and
x_{i,j}^{+}=x_{i,j}+\mathds{1}[i>\iota]
. Suppose that for any
1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq g
, and
1\leq j\leq k_{i},1\leq j^{\prime}\leq k_{i^{\prime}}
we have
A_{i,j}^{+}\leq A_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
,
A_{i,j}-C_{i,j}\geq A_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{+}-C_{i,j}
, and
x_{i,j}+C_{i,j}\geq x_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{+}+C_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{H}^{A_{i,j}}(x_{i,j},t_{i})\geq C_{i,j},\forall 1\leq i\leq g,1\leq j\leq k_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{H}^{A_{i,j}^{+}}(x_{i,j}^{+},t_{i})\geq C_{i,j},\forall 1\leq i\leq g,1\leq j\leq k_{i}\right].

We note that to allow for multiple times, we have to impose more restrictions on the spatial parameters.
It seems that there are no corresponding arguments (that can prove this multiple-time version) as those in the proofs in
[
BGW22
,
Gal21
]
in the degenerated setting of the colored TASEP model.
This is because the full power of the colored stochastic six-vertex model is necessary for their proofs.

We also note that our Theorem
1.1
is in a spirit close to the main results in
[
BGW22
]
, for we require that all pairs of rectangles (of different
i
’s) are ordered.
It is asked as
[
BGW22
, Conjecture 1.5]
whether such shift-invariance holds in more generality, with ordering imposed only for endpoints where relative shift happens.
This has been answered in
[
Gal21
]
and
[
Dau22a
]
in different settings.
It is natural to ask if a similar extension holds for our results, i.e., are some of the ordering inequalities in Theorem
1.1
not necessary for the shift-invariance to hold?
Our Theorem
1.3
can be viewed as one step toward this, for there we allow rectangles with the same
A_{i}
to be not ordered.
See Section
6.1
for some further discussions on this.

1.3
The oriented swap process and the conjectured identity

Consider the Cayley graph of the symmetric group
S_{N}
, generated by swaps of numbers at adjacent sites
k
and
k+1
, for each
1\leq k\leq N-1
.
A
sorting network
, first studied by Stanley
[
Sta84
]
, is one of the shortest paths between the identity permutation
(1,\ldots,N)
and the reverse permutation
(N,\ldots,1)
. Equivalently, it is a sequence of
N(N-1)/2
adjacent swaps, from the identity permutation to the reverse permutation.
We note that in such a sequence, each swap must increase the total number of inversions (i.e., switch adjacent
i,j
to
j,i
for some
i<j
); otherwise more than
N(N-1)/2
swaps are needed to obtain the reverse permutation from the identity permutation.

One natural way of defining a random sorting network is to assign equal probability to each sorting network.
This model has been extensively studied in
[
AHRV07
,
AGH12
,
Roz16
,
GR19
,
DV20
,
Dau22b
]
.
Another natural way of defining a random sorting network is the so-called oriented swap process (OSP), a Markovian construction from
[
AHR09
]
.
One lets the permutation evolve in a continuous way, such that at any time, for every two adjacent sites, if the number at the left site is smaller than the number at the right site, with rate
1
these two numbers swap.
More formally, one can think of OSP as a finite interval version of the colored TASEP.
Consider
N
particles on the finite lattice
\{1,\ldots,N\}
, such that initially (at time
0
) the particle at site each
k
is colored
k
.
For each edge
(k,k+1)
(for
1\leq k\leq N-1
), there is an independent Poisson clock that rings at rate
1
; and whenever it rings, the particles occupying the sites
k
and
k+1
will attempt to swap.
Suppose that these two particles have colors
i
and
j
respectively, then the swap succeeds only if
i<j
, i.e., only if the swap increases the number of inversions in the sequence of particle colors.

We consider the vector of
finishing times

\mathbf{U}_{N}=(U_{N}(1),\ldots,U_{N}(N-1))
, where
U_{N}(k)
is the last time such that a swap happens between the sites
k
and
k+1
.
As has been observed in
[
AHR09
]
, for each individual
k
, the time
U_{N}(k)
has the same distribution as
L_{(1,1),(k,N-k)}
. Using this, it is proved that if we let
N\to\infty
and assume
k/N\to y
for some
0<y<1
, then
U_{N}(k)
grows linearly in
N
with a fluctuation in the order of
N^{1/3}
, and (after appropriate rescaling) the fluctuation converges to GUE Tracy-Widom distribution
(
[
AHR09
, Theorem 1.6]
).

It is asked in
[
AHR09
]
about the asymptotic behavior of the
absorbing time
of OSP, defined as
\max_{1\leq k\leq N-1}U_{N}(k)
.
It is conjectured by Bisi, Cunden, Bibbson, and Romik that the vector
\mathbf{U}_{N}
and
\{L_{(1,1),(k,N-k)}\}_{k=1}^{N-1}
are equal in distribution (
[
BCGR22
, Conjecture 1.2]
and
[
BCGR20
, Conjecture 1.2]
); and assuming such equality in distribution they show that the absorbing time of OSP (after appropriate rescaling) converges to the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution.

Starting from this, in
[
BGR22
]
it is proved that
\max_{1\leq k\leq N-1}U_{n}(k)
has the same distribution as
\max_{1\leq k\leq N-1}L_{(1,1),(k,N-k)}
, using distributional identities from the colored stochastic six-vertex model, obtained in
[
BGW22
]
.
The latter corresponds to the passage time of TASEP with the flat initial condition, and it is known to converge to the Tracy-Widom GOE distribution (see e.g.
[
BFPS07
,
Sas05
]
).
Thus the OSP absorbing time convergence problem is settled in
[
BGR22
]
.
However, the joint equality in distribution between
\mathbf{U}_{N}
and
\{L_{(1,1),(k,N-k)}\}_{k=1}^{N-1}
remains open.
As pointed out in
[
BGR22
]
, such joint equality in distribution does not follow directly from any known bijections and also escapes the generality of the method taken there, therefore some new ideas are needed.

In
[
BCGR22
]
, several other special cases of joint equality in distribution have been verified.
For example, it is shown that the equality in distribution holds for
2\leq N\leq 6
, by a computer-assisted proof using the equivalent combinatorial formulation (see Section
1.3.2
); and it is also shown that
(U_{N}(1),U_{N}(N-1))
has the same distribution as
(L_{(1,1),(1,N-1)},L_{(1,1),(N-1,1)})
.

Using Theorem
1.1
, we prove the desired joint equality in distribution, thus resolve
[
BCGR22
,
BCGR20
, Conjecture 1.2]
(also stated as
[
BGR22
, Conjecture 1.3]
).

Theorem 1.8
.

{thm:udv}

The vectors
\mathbf{U}_{N}
and
\{L_{(1,1),(k,N-k)}\}_{k=1}^{N-1}
are equal in distribution.

1.3.1
Asymptotic results

With the equality in distribution of Theorem
1.8
, we can deduce results on the OSP finishing times from corresponding LPP results, strengthening results in
[
AHR09
]
.

{ssec:asyr}

Let
\mathcal{A}_{2}
denote the stationary Airy
2
process on
\mathbb{R}
, then
\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)
has the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution.
The fluctuation of the vector
\mathbf{U}_{N}
converges to
\mathcal{A}_{2}
minus a parabola.

Theorem 1.9
.

{cor:j-airy}

For any
y\in(0,1)
, as
N\to\infty
, the function

x\mapsto\frac{(y(1-y))^{1/6}}{(1+2\sqrt{y(1-y)})^{2/3}N^{1/3}}(U_{N}(\lfloor yN+xN^{2/3}\rfloor)-(1+2\sqrt{y(1-y)})N-\frac{1-2y}{\sqrt{y(1-y)}}xN^{2/3})

weakly converges to
\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)-x^{2}
, in the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.

We consider the
last swap location

k_{*}\in\{1,\cdots,N-1\}
, such that the last swap is between sites
k_{*}
and
k_{*}+1
.
We then get the following convergence about
k_{*}
.

Theorem 1.10
.

{cor:max-loc}

As
N\to\infty
,
\frac{k_{*}-N/2}{N^{2/3}}
converges in distribution, to
\argmax_{x}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)-x^{2}
.

The limiting distribution of
\argmax_{x}\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)-x^{2}
has been studied in
[
FQR13
,
Sch12
]
, with explicit formulas given.

In a scale smaller than
N^{2/3}
, the local fluctuations are simple random walks.

Theorem 1.11
.

{cor:loc-srw}

Take any
y\in(0,1)
, and positive integers
K_{N}
such that
N^{-2/3}K_{N}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
.
Consider two random functions
f_{N},g_{N}:[-K_{N},K_{N}]\cap\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
f_{N}(x)=U_{N}(\lfloor yN\rfloor+x)-U_{N}(\lfloor yN\rfloor)
, and
g_{N}
is a two-sided random walk, such that
g_{N}(x+1)-g_{N}(x)
are i.i.d. for each
-K_{N}\leq x<K_{N}
, with

\mathbb{P}[g_{N}(x+1)-g_{N}(x)=t]=\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{y(1-y)}}{1+2\sqrt{y(1-y)}}e^{-\frac{\sqrt{y}t}{\sqrt{y}+\sqrt{1-y}}}\;\;&t\geq 0,\\
\frac{\sqrt{y(1-y)}}{1+2\sqrt{y(1-y)}}e^{\frac{\sqrt{1-y}t}{\sqrt{y}+\sqrt{1-y}}}\;\;&t<0.\end{cases}

Then the total variation distance between
f_{N}
and
g_{N}
decays to zero, as
N\to\infty
.

Using the shift-invariance, we can also get the distribution of the OSP local dynamics, near the finishing times. As an example, the following result can be directly deduced from Theorem
1.11
.

For any
1\leq k\leq N
, we consider the last jump of the number
k
in OSP (of size
N
), and we denote
\LAL(N,k)
as the event where the last jump of
k
is to the left.
Noting that this event
\LAL(N,k)
is equivalent to that
U_{N}(N+1-k)>U_{N}(N-k)
(assuming that
U_{N}(0)=U_{N}(N)=0
), we immediately get the following result.

Corollary 1.12
.

{cor:left}

Take any
y\in(0,1)
, and a sequence of integers
k_{N}
such that
N^{-1}k_{N}\to y
as
N\to\infty
.
Then we have that
\mathbb{P}[\LAL(N,k_{N})]\to\frac{\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}+\sqrt{1-y}}
.

1.3.2
An equivalent formulation: Young tableaux and sorting networks

{ssec:combf}

In
[
BCGR22
]
, it is proved that there is an equivalent formulation of Theorem
1.8
in combinatorics, which gives an identity between rational functions raised from Young tableaux and sorting networks. It can be seen as an extension of the remarkable Edelman-Greene correspondence between Young tableaux and sorting networks
[
EG87
]
.
We record this result here.

We will mostly follow the notations in
[
BCGR22
]
.
Denote
\delta_{N}
as the Young diagram of
N-1
rows, where the
k
-th row has
N-k
boxes, and let
\SYT(\delta_{N})
denote all Young tableaux with shape
\delta_{N}
, i.e., all
\lambda=\{\lambda_{i,j}\}_{i,j\geq 1,i+j\leq N}
, such that these numbers are precisely
1,\ldots,N(N-1)/2
, and
\lambda_{i,j}<\lambda_{i+1,j}\wedge\lambda_{i,j+1}
, for any
i,j\geq 1
,
i+j\leq N-1
.
For each
\lambda\in\SYT(\delta_{N})
, we denote
\co_{\lambda}=(\lambda_{N-1,1},\ldots,\lambda_{1,N-1})
as the vector of the last entries of each row.
Let
\sigma_{\lambda}\in S_{N-1}
be a permutation, such that for any
1\leq i<j\leq N-1
, we have
\sigma_{\lambda}(i)<\sigma_{\lambda}(j)
if and only if
\co_{\lambda}(i)<\co_{\lambda}(j)
.
We let
\overline{\co}_{\lambda}
be the increasing rearrangement of
\co_{\lambda}
, i.e.,
\overline{\co}_{\lambda}=\co_{\lambda}\circ\sigma_{\lambda}^{-1}
.
We also define a
N(N-1)/2
dimensional vector
\de_{\lambda}
as follows: we consider the sequence of Young diagrams
\emptyset=\delta_{\lambda}^{(0)},\delta_{\lambda}^{(1)},\ldots,\delta_{\lambda}^{(N(N-1)/2)}=\delta_{N}
, such that each
\delta_{\lambda}^{(k)}
contains all boxes
(i,j)
with
\lambda_{i,j}\leq k
.
Then for each
0\leq k\leq N(N-1)/2-1
, we let
\de_{\lambda}(k)
be the number of boxes
(i,j)\subset\delta_{N}\setminus\delta_{\lambda}^{(k)}
, such that
\delta_{\lambda}^{(k)}\cup\{(i,j)\}
is a Young subdiagram of
\delta_{N}
.
For each permutation
\sigma\in S_{N-1}
, we take the rational function
F_{\sigma}\in\mathbb{C}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N-1})
as

F_{\sigma}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N-1})=\sum_{\lambda\in\SYT(\delta_{N}),\sigma_{\lambda}=\sigma}\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\prod_{i=\overline{\co}_{\lambda}(k-1)+1}^{\overline{\co}_{\lambda}(k)}\frac{1}{x_{k}+\de_{\lambda}(i)}.

There is a similar set of quantities in the sorting network.
Let
\SN_{N}
be the collection of all sorting networks from
(1,\ldots,N)
to
(N,\ldots,1)
, where each
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N(N-1)/2})\in\SN_{N}
is a sequence of numbers, each in
\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, such that the numbers at sites
s_{k}
and
s_{k}+1
are swapped at step
k
.
We let
\la_{s}=(\la_{s}(1),\ldots,\la_{s}(N-1))
such that
\la_{s}(k)=\max\{1\leq i\leq N(N-1)/2:s_{i}=k\}
for each
1\leq k\leq N-1
.
Let
\sigma_{s}\in S_{N-1}
be a permutation, such that for any
1\leq i<j\leq N-1
, we have
\sigma_{s}(i)<\sigma_{s}(j)
if and only if
\la_{s}(i)<\la_{s}(j)
, and let
\overline{\la}_{s}=\la_{s}\circ\sigma_{s}
.
For each
0\leq k\leq N(N-1)/2-1
, we let
\de_{s}(k)=|\{1\leq i\leq N-1:v^{(k)}(i)<v^{(k)}(i+1)\}|
, where
v^{(k)}\in S_{N-1}
is the configuration of the sorting network
s
after the
k
-th step.
For each permutation
\sigma\in S_{N-1}
, we take the rational function
G_{\sigma}\in\mathbb{C}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N-1})
as

G_{\sigma}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N-1})=\sum_{s\in\SN_{N},\sigma_{s}=\sigma}\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\prod_{i=\overline{\la}_{s}(k-1)+1}^{\overline{\la}_{s}(k)}\frac{1}{x_{k}+\de_{s}(i)}.

As proved in
[
BCGR22
]
, from the density functions and taking certain Fourier transforms, Theorem
1.8
is equivalent to the following.

Theorem 1.13
.

{thm:EG}

For
N\geq 2
and any
\sigma\in S_{N-1}
, we have that

F_{\sigma}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N-1})=G_{\sigma}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N-1}).

In
[
Sta84
]
Stanley computed
|\SN_{N}|
and noticed that it equals
|\SYT(\delta_{N})|
, later Edelman and Greene
[
EG87
]
gave a bijection
\EG_{N}:\SYT_{N}\to\SN_{N}
, which played crucial roles in the study of uniform random sorting networks in e.g.
[
AHRV07
,
AGH12
]
.
The bijection
\EG_{N}
also gives that
\la_{\EG_{N}(\lambda)}=\co_{\lambda}
and

\sigma_{\EG_{N}(\lambda)}=\sigma_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda\in\SYT_{N}
; and also

|\{\lambda\in\SYT(\delta_{N}),\sigma_{\lambda}=\sigma\}|=|\{s\in\SN_{N},\sigma_{s}=\sigma\}|
.
However, it is difficult to use the Edelman-Greene correspondence to study the OSP, or to deduce Theorem
1.13
, because (as pointed out in
[
BCGR22
]
) the identity

\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\prod_{i=\overline{\co}_{\lambda}(k-1)+1}^{\overline{\co}_{\lambda}(k)}\frac{1}{x_{k}+\de_{\lambda}(i)}=\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\prod_{i=\overline{\la}_{\EG(\lambda)}(k-1)+1}^{\overline{\la}_{\EG(\lambda)}(k)}\frac{1}{x_{k}+\de_{\EG(\lambda)}(i)}

is not generally true for any
\lambda\in\SYT_{N}
.
Thus one cannot find a direct proof of Theorem
1.13
via a bijection (that may be different from
\EG_{N}
) between
\SYT_{N}
and
\SN_{N}
.

2
Proof strategy

{sec:stra}

In this section, we explain the general approach to prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
.

As discussed in Section
1.2.2
, using the colored stochastic six-vertex model distributional identities from
[
BGW22
,
Gal21
]
, we can prove a single-time equality in distribution for the colored TASEP model (Theorem
1.7
).
Our main work is to upgrade Theorem
1.7
to Theorem
1.1
, which is the multi-time distributional identity.

For better expository, we shall avoid the notion of the height function, and write the arguments using the passage times.
Recall that for each
A\in\mathbb{Z}
and
B,C\in\mathbb{N}
, the passage time
T_{B,C}^{A}
is the first time when there are at least
C
particles and on or to the right of site
A+B+1-C
with color
\leq A
.
For any
t\geq 0
, we denote
I^{t}[A,B,C]
as the event where
T_{B,C}^{A}\leq t
.
Then
I^{t}[A,B,C]
is also equivalent to the event
\mathcal{H}^{A}(A+B+1-C,t)\geq C
.
We can then write Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.1
as follows.

Theorem 1.7*
.

{thm:eqgals}

For any
t>0
, and

\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell},\;\{A_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\;\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell},\;\{C_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell},\;\{B_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{C_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{n},

such that

A_{1},\ldots,A_{\ell}<A_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,A_{n}^{\prime},

and

A_{1}+B_{1}-C_{1},\ldots,A_{\ell}+B_{\ell}-C_{\ell}\geq A_{1}^{\prime}+B_{1}^{\prime}-C_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,A_{n}^{\prime}+B_{n}^{\prime}-C_{n}^{\prime},

we have

\mathbb{P}\left[\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}I^{t}[A_{i},B_{i},C_{i}]\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}I^{t}[A_{i}^{\prime},B_{i}^{\prime},C_{i}^{\prime}]\right)\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}I^{t}[A_{i}+1,B_{i},C_{i}]\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}I^{t}[A_{i}^{\prime},B_{i}^{\prime},C_{i}^{\prime}]\right)\right].

Theorem 1.1*
.

{thm:main-des}

Let
g\in\mathbb{N}
,
k_{1},\ldots,k_{g}\in\mathbb{N}
, and take
A_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}

and
1\leq j\leq k_{i}
.
Let
1\leq\iota<g

1\leq i\leq g
and
1\leq j\leq k_{i}

A_{i,j}^{+}=A_{i,j}+\mathds{1}[i>\iota]

1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq g
, and
1\leq j\leq k_{i},1\leq j^{\prime}\leq k_{i^{\prime}}

A_{i,j}\leq A_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}},\quad A_{i,j}^{+}+B_{i,j}\geq A_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{+}+B_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}},\quad A_{i,j}^{+}-C_{i,j}\geq A_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{+}-C_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}.

(2.1)

Then for any

, we have

\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{g}\bigcap_{j=1}^{k_{i}}I^{t_{i}}[A_{i,j},B_{i,j},C_{i,j}]\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{g}\bigcap_{j=1}^{k_{i}}I^{t_{i}}[A_{i,j}^{+},B_{i,j},C_{i,j}]\right].

This theorem is equivalent to Theorem
1.1
because for any

and

, the condition
\mathcal{R}_{B_{1},C_{1}}^{A_{1}}\leq\mathcal{R}_{B_{2},C_{2}}^{A_{2}}
is equivalent to that

, and
A_{1}+B_{1}\geq A_{2}+B_{2}

A_{1}-C_{1}\geq A_{2}-C_{2}
.

Our proof for Theorem
1.1*
follows the same strategy.

1.

We will find certain stopping times, which we call ‘cutting times’, like the time

2.

The next step will be to show that the cutting time distribution is invariant under shift. We will get formulas similar to (
2.2
).
In some settings, at this step, we use the induction hypothesis (instead of Theorem

).

3.

We then prove that the probability density function of cutting times is analytic.

4.

B^{\prime}

Thus we conclude that the cutting time distributions are the same.

We remark that in the setting of multiple times and multiple particles, the independence property in Step 1 is delicate.
Thus one main difficulty in our proof is designing the appropriate setup,
mainly including the induction setup and the choice of the cutting times, to get the independence property.

For Theorem

1.1
, plus some arguments using the independence of the Poisson field in different areas.

The oriented swap process identity (Theorem

The asymptotic results in Section

and various existing results on LPP.

3.4
Sum of exponential random variables

We need the following basic results, on the analyticity of density functions of sums of independent exponential random variables.

{ssec:density}

Lemma 3.7
.

{lem:analy-single}

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
E_{1},\cdots,E_{m}
be i.i.d.
\Exp(1)
random variables, and
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}>0
. The function

r\mapsto\mathbb{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}E_{i}=r\right],

is analytic in
[0,\infty)
, and can be extended to an analytic function on
\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

{lem:analy-multi}

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
E_{1},\cdots,E_{m}
be i.i.d.
\Exp(1)
random variables, and
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}>0
.
Take integers
1\leq m_{1}<\cdots<m_{k}\leq m
, and
0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}
.
The function

r\mapsto\mathbb{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j}}a_{i}E_{i}<t_{j}-r,\;\forall 1\leq j\leq k\right],

is analytic in
[0,t_{1}]
, and can be extended to an analytic function on
\mathbb{C}
.

To prove these two lemmas, we consider the following space
\Gamma_{n}
of analytic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which consists of all (finite) linear combinations of

(r_{1},\cdots,r_{n})\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}^{a_{i}}e^{b_{i}r_{i}}

where
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
.
We have the following properties.

Lemma 3.9
.

{lem:gam-prop-1}

For any
f_{1},f_{2}\in\Gamma_{1}
, there is
f_{3}\in\Gamma_{1}
, such that for any
r>0
, we have

f_{3}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}f_{1}(s)f_{2}(r-s)ds.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
f_{1}(r)=r^{a_{1}}e^{b_{1}r}
and
f_{2}(r)=r^{a_{2}}e^{b_{2}r}
, for some
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
When
b_{1}=b_{2}
, we have

\int_{0}^{r}f_{1}(s)f_{2}(r-s)ds=e^{b_{1}r}\int_{0}^{r}s^{a_{1}}(r-s)^{a_{2}}ds,

and the integral is a polynomial of
r
.
When
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
, we have

\int_{0}^{r}f_{1}(s)f_{2}(r-s)ds=e^{b_{2}r}\int_{0}^{r}s^{a_{1}}(r-s)^{a_{2}}e^{(b_{1}-b_{2})s}ds,

and the integral is
F_{1}(r)+F_{2}(r)e^{(b_{1}-b_{2})r}
, for some polynomials
F_{1},F_{2}
.
Thus the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

{lem:gam-prop-2}

For any
f_{1}\in\Gamma_{n}
, and
0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}
, there is
f_{2}\in\Gamma_{1}
, such that

f_{2}(r)=\int_{\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{i}s_{i^{\prime}}<t_{i}-r,\forall 1\leq i\leq n}f_{1}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}ds_{i},

for any
r\in[0,t_{1}]
.

To prove this we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.11
.

{lem:gam-prop-3}

For any
f_{1}\in\Gamma_{n+1}
, and
t>0
, there is
f_{2}\in\Gamma_{n}
, such that

f_{2}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n})=\int_{0}^{t-\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}}f_{1}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n+1})ds_{n+1},

for any
s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}\geq 0
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}<t
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume that
f_{1}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n+1})=\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}s_{i}^{a_{i}}e^{b_{i}s_{i}}
,
where each
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}s_{i}^{a_{i}}e^{b_{i}s_{i}}ds_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t-\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}}s_{n+1}^{a_{n+1}}e^{b_{n+1}s_{n+1}}ds_{n+1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}^{a_{i}}e^{b_{i}s_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left(F\left(t-\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\right)e^{b_{n+1}(t-\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i})}+\frac{a_{n+1}!}{(-b_{n+1})^{a_{n+1}+1}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}^{a_{i}}e^{b_{i}s_{i}},

where
F
is a polynomial.
Then the conclusion follows.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.10
.

For each
0\leq m\leq n
, we can inductively prove that there is some
g_{m}\in\Gamma_{n-m+1}
, such that

g_{m}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n-m},r)=\int_{\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{i}s_{i^{\prime}}<t_{i}-r,\forall n-m+1\leq i\leq n}f_{1}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n})\prod_{i=n-m+1}^{n}ds_{i},

(3.1)

for any
s_{1},\cdots,s_{n-m},r\geq 0
with
r+\sum_{i=1}^{n-m}s_{i}<t_{n-m+1}
.
Indeed, for the base case we just take
g_{0}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n},r)=f_{1}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n})
.
Then given
g_{m}
for some
0\leq m<n
, by Lemma
3.11
we let
g_{m+1}\in\Gamma_{n-m}
be the function with

g_{m+1}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n-m-1},r)=\int_{0}^{t_{n-m}-r-\sum_{i=1}^{n-m-1}s_{i}}g_{m}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n-m},r)ds_{n-m},

for any
s_{1},\cdots,s_{n-m-1},r\geq 0
with
r+\sum_{i=1}^{n-m-1}s_{i}<t_{n-m}
. Then this
g_{m+1}
satisfies (
3.1
).
Finally, we just take
f_{2}=g_{n}
, and the conclusion follows.
∎

We can now prove the lemmas on the probabilities about sums of exponential random variables.

Proof of Lemma
3.7

By induction in
m
, the conclusion follows from Lemma

, using that for each

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

Denote
m_{0}=0
.
By Lemma
3.7
, for each
1\leq j\leq k
we take
f_{j}\in\Gamma_{1}
such that

f_{j}(s)=\mathbb{P}\left[\sum_{i=m_{j-1}+1}^{m_{j}}a_{i}E_{i}=s\right].

Then by Lemma
3.10
, we can find
f\in\Gamma_{1}

Thus the conclusion follows.

Recall the setup of Theorem

\hat{g}=g-1

The base case where

(actually the equivalent form of Theorem
1.7*
). Now we assume that
\hat{g}\geq 2
.

Take
1\leq\tau\leq g
such that
t_{\tau}\leq t_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq g
.
Without loss of generality (and by reflection symmetry), we assume that
\tau\leq\iota
.
In the case where
t_{\iota}=t_{\iota+1}
, we also assume that
\iota\geq 2
, since otherwise, we must have
\tau=\iota=1
, so we can always set
\tau=2
instead and do a reflection of particles and colors (note that
\hat{g}\geq 2
and
g\geq 3
).

For the events involved in Theorem
1.1*
, for simplicity of notation we denote

I=\bigcap_{i=\tau+1}^{g}\bigcap_{j=1}^{k_{i}}I^{t_{i}}[A_{i,j},B_{i,j},C_{i,j}],\quad I^{+}=\bigcap_{i=\tau+1}^{g}\bigcap_{j=1}^{k_{i}}I^{t_{i}}[A_{i,j}^{+},B_{i,j},C_{i,j}],

and

J=\bigcap_{i=1}^{\tau}\bigcap_{j=1}^{k_{i}}I^{t_{i}}[A_{i,j},B_{i,j},C_{i,j}].

Namely, we split the events into two groups:
J
contains those with ‘group index’
i\leq\tau
, and
I
or
I^{+}
contains those with ‘group index’
i>\tau
.
The reason behind this splitting will be clear later.
Then the goal is to prove that
\mathbb{P}[J\cap I]=\mathbb{P}[J\cap I^{+}]
.
Note that we already have

\mathbb{P}[I]=\mathbb{P}[I^{+}].

(4.1)

When
\tau<\iota
this is by the induction hypothesis of Theorem
1.1*
. When
\tau=\iota
this is by a simple symmetry (of the colored TASEP).

A projection.
Our next step is to simplify the problem by considering a projection of the colored TASEP
\zeta
, which we describe now.
Let

A_{*}=\min_{\tau<i\leq g,1\leq j\leq k_{i}}A_{i,j},

(4.2)

and take
B_{*},C_{*}
such that

A_{*}+B_{*}=\max_{\tau<i\leq g,1\leq j\leq k_{i}}A_{i,j}+B_{i,j},

(4.3)

A_{*}-C_{*}=\min_{1\leq i\leq\tau,1\leq j\leq k_{i}}A_{i,j}-C_{i,j}.

(4.4)

Then by (
2.1
), we have

A_{*}\geq\max_{1\leq i\leq\tau,1\leq j\leq k_{i}}A_{i,j},

(4.5)

Cutting information.

For any

Proof of Theorem

For any

, which can be thought of as
\hat{\mu}^{A_{0},C_{0}}
, while any particle disappears once arrives at site
A_{0}+B_{0}+1
.
To be more precise, for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\geq 0
, we let
\xi_{t}(x)=\infty
if
x>A_{0}+B_{0}
, and
\xi_{t}(x)=\hat{\mu}_{t}^{A_{0},C_{0}}(x)
otherwise.
As
A_{0}+B_{0}=A_{*}+B_{*}\geq A_{i,j}+B_{i,j}
for any
\tau<i\leq g
and
1\leq j\leq k_{i}
, we have that
\eta
on
\mathbb{Z}\cap(-\infty,A_{*}+B_{*}]
is determined by
\xi
, and
T_{B_{i,j},C_{i,j}}^{A_{i,j}}
is a certain swap time in
\xi
.

Suppose the total number of swaps (of two particles, or of a particle jumping to an empty site) in the process
\xi
is
M
.
Then we always have that
M\leq B_{0}C_{0}+C_{0}^{2}
.
This bound is from the following reasoning: for the particle colored
i
, it moves from
A_{0}-C_{0}+i
to
A_{0}+B_{0}+1
, and it can be swapped with a smaller colored particle (i.e., make a left jump)
i-1
times;
thus it can jump to the right at most
(A_{0}+B_{0}+1)-(A_{0}-C_{0}+i)+(i-1)=B_{0}+C_{0}
times.

For
1\leq i\leq M
, we take
X_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that the
i
-th swap (in
\xi
) is between sites
X_{i}
and
X_{i}+1
, and it happens at time
w_{i}
. Take
w_{0}=0
, and for each
1\leq i\leq M
we let
E_{i}=w_{i}-w_{i-1}
.
Then conditional on
M
and
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
, we have that

are independent, and the distribution for
E_{i}
is
\Exp(1/a_{i})
, where
a_{i}
is the number of possible swaps at time
w_{i-1}
; i.e.,

a_{i}=|\{x\in\mathbb{Z}:\xi_{w_{i-1}}(x)<\xi_{w_{i-1}}(x+1)\}|.

Note that for each
i
, such
a_{i}
is determined by
\{X_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
, since
\xi_{w_{i-1}}
is determined by
\{X_{j}\}_{j=1}^{i-1}
.

Below we write
\mathbf{X}=\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
. From the bound of
M
, we have that there are finitely many
\mathbf{x}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
, such that
\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{x}]>0
.
It remains to show that for any such
\mathbf{x}
, the function

\mathbf{r}\mapsto\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{R}^{k}=\mathbf{r},\mathbf{L}^{k}=\boldsymbol{\ell},I\mid\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{x}]

is analytic in
\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k,t_{\tau}\}
,
and it can be analytically extended to
\mathbb{C}^{k}
.

We note that
\mathbf{L}^{k}
is determined by
\mathbf{X}
, so it remains to consider the function

\mathbf{r}\mapsto\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{R}^{k}=\mathbf{r},I\mid\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{x}].

(5.1)

For each
1\leq i\leq k
, we have
R_{i}\in\{w_{i^{\prime}}\}_{i^{\prime}=1}^{M}
; and we let
\kappa[i]
be the number such that

R_{i}=w_{\kappa[i]}
.
Then we have
\kappa[1]<\kappa[2]<\cdots<\kappa[k]
.
For each
\tau<i\leq g
and
1\leq j\leq k_{i}
, we have
T^{A_{i,j}}_{B_{i,j},C_{i,j}}\in\{w_{i^{\prime}}\}_{i^{\prime}=1}^{M}
; and we let
\overline{\kappa}[i]
be the number such that

\max_{1\leq j\leq k_{i}}T^{A_{i,j}}_{B_{i,j},C_{i,j}}=w_{\overline{\kappa}[i]}
.
We note that the indices
\{\kappa[i]\}_{i=1}^{k}
and
\{\overline{\kappa}[i]\}_{i=\tau+1}^{g}
are determined by
\mathbf{X}
.

Since

are independent exponential random variables,
we can then write the function (
5.1
) as (supposing that
\mathbf{r}=\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
,
r_{0}=0
, and
\kappa[0]=0

\mathbb{P}\left[\sum_{i^{\prime}=\kappa[k]+1}^{\overline{\kappa}[i]}E_{i^{\prime}}<t_{i}-r_{k},\;\forall\tau<i\leq g,\overline{\kappa}[i]>\kappa[k]\mid\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{x}\right]\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\left[\sum_{i^{\prime}=\kappa[i-1]+1}^{\kappa[i]}E_{i^{\prime}}=r_{i}-r_{i-1}\mid\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{x}\right].

By applying Lemma
3.7
or Lemma
3.8
to each factor, we get that this is analytic in
\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k,t_{\tau}\}
, and can be analytically extended to
\mathbb{C}^{k}
.
Thus the conclusion follows.
∎

5.3
Partial order and induction of cutting colors

Let
\Lambda=\bigcup_{k=1}^{C_{*}}\Lambda\{k\}
.
We define a partial order
\prec
on the
\Lambda
: for any
\boldsymbol{\ell}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
, we let
\boldsymbol{\ell}\prec\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
, if one of the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\Lambda\{k\}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\in\Lambda\{k^{\prime}\}
, for some
1\leq k<k^{\prime}\leq C_{*}
, and the first
k^{\prime}
coordinates of
\boldsymbol{\ell}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
are the same.

2.

Both
\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\in\Lambda\{k\}
for some
1\leq k\leq C_{*}
, and one can apply a ‘swap operation’ to
\boldsymbol{\ell}
to obtain
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
; i.e., if we write
\boldsymbol{\ell}=\{\ell_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}=\{\ell_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{k}
, then there exists some
1\leq i<k
with
\ell_{i}>\ell_{i+1}
, such that
\ell_{i}^{\prime}=\ell_{i+1}
and
\ell_{i+1}=\ell_{i}^{\prime}
, and
\ell_{j}=\ell_{j}^{\prime}
for any
1\leq j\leq k
,
j\not\in\{i,i+1\}
.

Also, suppose
\boldsymbol{\ell}\prec\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\prec\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
, we let
\boldsymbol{\ell}\prec\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
.
In other words, for any
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\Lambda
, the sequences that are greater than it under
\prec
are those that can be obtained in the following way: first, apply a sequence of swap operations; then take the first a few coordinates.
Under this ordering,
\{C_{*},\cdots,1\}
is the minimum element in
\Lambda
, and
\emptyset
is the maximum element in
\Lambda
.

.

{ex:order}

On the other hand, we must have

•

From the rough inequality between the above two integrals, we can extract the equality between
G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\cdot,I^{+})
and
G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\cdot,I)
(i.e., prove (
5.3
)), by using (1) the fact that they are both analytic, and (2) refined
\lambda\to\infty
asymptotic behavior of
F(\boldsymbol{\ell},\cdot,I)
(Lemma
5.6
below).

As already indicated, the main steps in the above strategy are given by various estimates on
F
, i.e., the probability of
\mathcal{E}
conditioned on the cutting information (up to time
t_{\tau}
) and
\hat{I}
.
Below we give these estimates.

Recall that
\mathcal{E}
is the event that in
\eta_{t_{*}}
, the particle colored
\ell_{i}
is at site
m_{i}=2^{\lambda^{k+1-i}}
(for each
1\leq i\leq k
).
When
\lambda
is large, this requires that the particle colored
\ell_{i}
move roughly
m_{i}
steps within
t_{*}
time.
Thus
F
should be of order at most
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}{m_{i}!}
.
Our first estimate states that conditioned on certain cutting information,
F
is smaller.

Lemma 5.4
.

{lem:npr}

For any
1\leq k^{\prime}\leq C_{*}
,
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\in\Lambda\{k^{\prime}\}
,
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\not\preceq\boldsymbol{\ell}
, and
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
\hat{I}=I
or
\hat{I}=I^{+}
, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\hat{I})d\mathbf{r}\to 0,

as we send
\lambda\to\infty
.

We now explain this lemma heuristically.
Suppose that
\mathbf{L}^{k^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}
holds. If each number in
\boldsymbol{\ell}
is also contained in
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
,
we must have that
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\preceq\boldsymbol{\ell}
, by the definition of the ordering
\prec
.
Therefore, for any
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\not\preceq\boldsymbol{\ell}
, the events
\mathbf{L}^{k^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}
imply that there is a number
\ell_{j}
in
\boldsymbol{\ell}
that is not contained in
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
.
If in addition
R_{k^{\prime}+1}>t_{\tau}
is assumed, in
\eta_{t_{\tau}}
the particle colored
\ell_{j}
is still on or to the left of site
A_{*}+B_{*}-k^{\prime}
.
Thus this particle needs to move roughly
m_{j}
steps between time
t_{\tau}
and time
t_{*}
, and that happens with probability of order at most
\frac{(t_{*}-t_{\tau})^{m_{j}}}{m_{j}!}
.
In summary, for
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\not\preceq\boldsymbol{\ell}
the function
F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\hat{I})
is of order at most
((t_{*}-t_{\tau})/t_{*})^{m_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}{m_{i}!}
, which is much smaller than
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}
for large
\lambda
.

Our second estimate compares the probabilities of
\mathcal{E}
conditioned on
I
or
I^{+}
(i.e.,
F(\cdot,\cdot,I)
versus
F(\cdot,\cdot,I^{+})
).

Lemma 5.5
.

{lem:pr}

For any
k\leq k^{\prime}\leq C_{*}
,
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\in\Lambda\{k^{\prime}\}
,
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\preceq\boldsymbol{\ell}
, and
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}}\big|F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)-F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I^{+})\big|d\mathbf{r}\to 0,

as we send
\lambda\to\infty
.

The heuristics behind this lemma is as follows.
In
F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)
and
F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I^{+})
, the cutting information up to time
t_{\tau}
are conditioned to be the same.
The difference is on
I
versus
I^{+}
, and that can only affect the cutting information after time
t_{\tau}
.
For the cutting information after time
t_{\tau}
to affect the event
\mathcal{E}
, there must exist a particle that is on or to the left of
A_{*}+B_{*}-k^{\prime}
in
\eta_{t_{\tau}}
, and interacts with the particles with colors in
\boldsymbol{\ell}
before time
t_{*}
.
Then this particular particle should move at least order
m_{k}
steps before time
t_{*}
, because under
\mathcal{E}
, at time
t_{\tau}
each particle with color in
\boldsymbol{\ell}
is likely to have already moved order
m_{k}
steps.
Note that the probability for a particle to move
cm_{k}
steps in
t_{*}-t_{\tau}
time is roughly
\frac{(t_{*}-t_{\tau})^{cm_{k}}}{(cm_{k})!}
(here
c>0
is a constant).
Then the difference
|F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)-F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I^{+})|
is expected to be of order at most
\frac{(t_{*}-t_{\tau})^{cm_{k}}}{(cm_{k})!}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}{m_{i}!}
, which is much smaller than
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}
for large
\lambda
.

The next result is a more refined asymptotic result of
F
, with a scaling of times.

Lemma 5.6
.

{lem:asy-m-t}

Take any
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}>0
.
As we send
\lambda\to\infty
, there is

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(m_{i}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-i))!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-i)}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell},\{\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{k},I)\to e^{-kt_{*}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}e^{-s_{i}/t_{*}}.

(5.6)

In addition, when
\lambda
is large enough (depending on
A_{*},B_{*},C_{*},t_{*},t_{\tau}
), for any
0<\frac{s_{1}}{m_{1}}<\cdots<\frac{s_{k}}{m_{k}}<t_{\tau}
, the left-hand side is bounded by
\prod_{i=1}^{k}2e^{-s_{i}/t_{*}}
.

This lemma can be explained by the following more careful analysis of the event
\mathcal{E}
.
For each
1\leq i\leq k
, the events
R_{i}=\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}}
and
L_{i}=\ell_{i}
imply that at time
\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}}
, the particle colored
\ell_{i}
is at site
A_{*}+B_{*}+1-i
.
For
\mathcal{E}
to happen, this particle needs to move
m_{i}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-i)
steps between time
\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}}
and time
t_{*}
.
As
m_{i}=2^{\lambda^{k+1-i}}
, when
\lambda
is large, these
k
particles travel at very different and high speeds, and one can regard their moves as being independent of each other.
Thus
F(\boldsymbol{\ell},\{\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{k},I)
should be roughly
\prod_{i=1}^{k}e^{-t_{*}}\frac{(t_{*}-s_{i}/m_{i})^{m_{i}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-i)}}{(m_{i}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-i))!}
, and (
5.6
) follows.

We leave the proofs of the above lemmas to the next subsections, and now finish the proof of Proposition
4.2
assuming them.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

For
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}\mid I]=\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}\mid I^{+}]
from Lemma
5.2
, we apply (
5.5
) to both hand sides.
Take any
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we multiply both sides by
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}
, and get

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\in\Lambda\{k^{\prime}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)d\mathbf{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\in\Lambda\{k^{\prime}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I^{+})G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I^{+})d\mathbf{r}.

We will then send
\lambda\to\infty
, and analyze the behavior of each term in the summations on both sides.

We claim that
G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\hat{I})
is bounded, uniformly in
\hat{I}=I
or
\hat{I}=I^{+}
, and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\in\Lambda
,
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}
.
Indeed, we have

\displaystyle G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\hat{I})=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{R}^{k^{\prime}}=\mathbf{r},R_{k^{\prime}+1}>t_{\tau},\mathbf{L}^{k^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\mid\hat{I}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\hat{I}]^{-1}\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{R}^{k^{\prime}}=\mathbf{r},\mathbf{L}^{k^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\hat{I}].

By Lemma
5.1
, for each
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
and
\hat{I}
this is analytic as a function of
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}
with an extension to
\mathbb{C}^{k^{\prime}}
, thus must be bounded uniformly in
\mathbf{r}
.
As there are only finitely many choices of
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
and
\hat{I}
, the above claim then follows.

With this claim, by Lemma
5.4
,
for any
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\not\preceq\boldsymbol{\ell}
and
\hat{I}=I
or
I^{+}
, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\hat{I})G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\hat{I})d\mathbf{r}\to 0,

as
\lambda\to\infty
.
For any
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\prec\boldsymbol{\ell}
, by the induction hypothesis (of this proposition), we have that
G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)=G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I^{+})
.
Thus the difference

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)d\mathbf{r}-\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I^{+})G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I^{+})d\mathbf{r}

equals

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}}\big(F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)-F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I^{+})\big)G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)d\mathbf{r}.

As
G(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},I)
is uniformly bounded, by Lemma
5.5
we have that this expression
\to 0
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Now only the term where
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{\ell}
remains, i.e., as
\lambda\to\infty
we have

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k,t_{\tau}\}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I)G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I)d\mathbf{r}-\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k,t_{\tau}\}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I^{+})G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I^{+})d\mathbf{r}\to 0.

By Lemma
5.5
we have that as
\lambda\to\infty
,

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k,t_{\tau}\}}(F(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I)-F(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I^{+}))G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I^{+})d\mathbf{r}\to 0,

since
G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I^{+})
is uniformly bounded.
Thus we have that

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot m_{i}!}{t_{*}^{m_{i}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k,t_{\tau}\}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I)(G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I)-G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I^{+}))d\mathbf{r}\to 0,

(5.7)

as we send
\lambda\to\infty
.

By Lemma
5.1
, the function

\mathbf{r}\mapsto G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I)-G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{r},I^{+})

(5.8)

is analytic for
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k,t_{\tau}\}
, and can be analytically extended to
\mathbb{C}^{k}
.
Thus if it were not identically
0
, by taking the first (in the dictionary order of the exponents) nonzero term in its Taylor expansion, there would exist
\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, such that for any
s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}>0
, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}^{\beta_{i}}(G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\{\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{k},I)-G(\boldsymbol{\ell},\{\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{k},I^{+}))\to D\prod_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}^{\beta_{i}},

where
D\neq 0
is a constant; and that the absolute value of the left-hand side is bounded by some constant, uniformly in all
m_{i}
and
s_{i}
.
Using this and Lemma
5.6
, by dominated convergence theorem we get a contradiction with (
5.7
) (since
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are arbitrarily taken).
Thus we have that (
5.8
) must be
0
, i.e.,
(
5.3
) holds for any
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k,t_{\tau}\}
, which is precisely the conclusion we want.
∎

In the next two subsections, we prove Lemmas
5.4
,
5.5
,
5.6
, via making rigorous the heuristic explanations following each lemma.

5.4
Bounds on transition probabilities from cutting information

In this subsection, we prove Lemma
5.4
and
5.5
. We start with the pre-limit bounds.
The first one is a straightforward approximation by independent jumps to the right.

Recall that
\mathcal{E}
is the ‘high-speed event’ defined in Section
5.2
, using some fixed
\boldsymbol{\ell}=\{\ell_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\in\Lambda\{k\}
.
Also recall the vectors
\mathbf{R}^{k^{\prime}}=\{R_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}
,
\mathbf{L}^{k}=\{L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}
for each
1\leq k^{\prime}\leq C_{*}
, and that the cutting information is given by
\{\mathds{1}[R_{i}\leq t_{\tau}]R_{i}\}_{i=1}^{C_{*}},\{\mathds{1}[R_{i}\leq t_{\tau}]L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{C_{*}}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

{lem:bd-tfc}

Take any
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}=\{\ell_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{C_{*}}\in\Lambda\{C_{*}\}
(which is a permutation of
1,\cdots,C_{*}
), and let
\kappa[j]
be the number such that
\ell_{\kappa[j]}^{\prime}=\ell_{j}
for any
1\leq j\leq k
.
Take any
\mathbf{r}=\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{C_{*}}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{C_{*},\infty\}
.
If each
r_{\kappa[j]}<t_{*}
, and
\lambda
is large enough (depending on
t_{*}
and
A_{*}+B_{*}
), we have

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}\mid\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}}=\mathbf{r},\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}]<\prod_{j=1}^{k}2e^{-(t_{*}-r_{\kappa[j]})}\frac{(t_{*}-r_{\kappa[j]})^{m_{j}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[j])}}{(m_{j}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[j]))!}.

If
r_{\kappa[j]}\geq t_{*}
for some
1\leq j\leq k
then the left-hand side equals zero.

Proof.

The second part (where
r_{\kappa[j]}\geq t_{*}
) is obvious from the definition of
\mathcal{E}
, so we focus on the first part; i.e., we assume that
r_{\kappa[j]}<t_{*}
for the rest of this proof.
In discussion below we also assume the event that
\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}}=\mathbf{r},\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
.

For each
1\leq j\leq k
, and
i^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
w_{j,i^{\prime}}
be the time when the particle colored
\ell_{\kappa[j]}^{\prime}=\ell_{j}
makes the
i^{\prime}
-th jump to the right since time
r_{\kappa[j]}
.
Note that here we simply ignore jumps to the left.
We also denote
w_{j,0}=r_{\kappa[j]}
.
Denote
E_{j,i^{\prime}}
as the total amount of time, between times
w_{j,i^{\prime}-1}
and
w_{j,i^{\prime}}
, such that the particle right next to the particle colored
\ell_{\kappa[j]}^{\prime}=\ell_{j}
has a larger color.
In other words,
E_{j,i^{\prime}}
is the amount of time when the particle colored
\ell_{j}
is able to jump to the right, between times
w_{j,i^{\prime}-1}
and
w_{j,i^{\prime}}
.
It can also be understood as the ‘waiting time’ for the particle colored
\ell_{j}
to make the
i^{\prime}
-th jump.
Then we have that
E_{j,i^{\prime}}
for all
1\leq j\leq k
and
i^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
are i.i.d.
\Exp(1)
.

Under the event
\mathcal{E}
we must have that

\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{m_{j}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[j])}E_{j,i^{\prime}}\leq t_{*}-r_{\kappa[j]},

(5.9)

for each
1\leq j\leq k
.
We note that the probability of (
5.9
) is bounded by

2e^{-(t_{*}-r_{\kappa[j]})}\frac{(t_{*}-r_{\kappa[j]})^{m_{j}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[j])}}{(m_{j}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[j]))!},

when
m_{j}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[j])
is large enough.
Therefore the conclusion follows by taking the product over
j
.
∎

The next pre-limit bound is on the difference between the transition probabilities, for different sets of cutting information.

Lemma 5.8
.

{lem:bd-dtfc}

Take any
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}=\{\ell_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{C_{*}},\overline{\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\prime}=\{\overline{\ell}_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{C_{*}}\in\Lambda\{C_{*}\}
, and any
\mathbf{r}=\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{C_{*}},\overline{\mathbf{r}}=\{\overline{r}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{C_{*}}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{C_{*},\infty\}
.
Let
k\leq k^{\prime}\leq C_{*}
, and suppose that
\ell_{i}^{\prime}=\overline{\ell}_{i}^{\prime}
,
r_{i}=\overline{r}_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq k^{\prime}
, and
r_{k^{\prime}}<t_{\tau}
, and
r_{k^{\prime}+1},\overline{r}_{k^{\prime}+1}>t_{\tau}
if
k^{\prime}<C_{*}
.
Let
\kappa[j]
be the number such that
\ell_{\kappa[j]}^{\prime}=\ell_{j}
for any
1\leq j\leq k
, and we also assume that each
\kappa[j]\leq k^{\prime}
(i.e., the colors in
\boldsymbol{\ell}
are contained in
\{\ell_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}
).
Then we have

\left|\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}\mid\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}}=\mathbf{r},\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}]-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}\mid\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}}=\overline{\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}=\overline{\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\prime}]\right|\\
<H\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}\left(\frac{t_{*}-t_{\tau}}{t_{*}-r_{\kappa[j]}}\right)^{m_{j}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(t_{*}-r_{\kappa[j]})^{m_{j}}}{(m_{j}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[j]))!},

if
\lambda>H
.
Here
H
is a large constant depending on
A_{*},B_{*},C_{*},t_{*},t_{\tau}
.

The general idea of this lemma is that, for two sets of cutting information that differ only after time
t_{\tau}
, the transition probabilities are close. This is because the particles jump fast, and it is unlikely for a particle joining after time
t_{\tau}
to catch up with and affect the evolution of those particles to its right.

Proof of Lemma
5.8
.

We take
\overline{\eta}
as a copy of
\eta
, and we consider the evolution of
\eta
on

\Omega=\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq C_{*}}\{(x,t):x\geq A_{*}+B_{*}+1-i,t\geq r_{i}\},

conditional on
\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}}=\mathbf{r},\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
;
and the evolution of
\overline{\eta}
on

\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq C_{*}}\{(x,t):x\geq A_{*}+B_{*}+1-i,t\geq\overline{r}_{i}\},

conditional on
\overline{\mathbf{R}}^{C_{*}}=\overline{\mathbf{r}},\overline{\mathbf{L}}^{C_{*}}=\overline{\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\prime}
.
Here
\overline{\mathbf{R}}^{C_{*}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{L}}^{C_{*}}
are the
\overline{\eta}
version of
\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}}
and
\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}
.
We couple
\eta
and
\overline{\eta}
by using the same Poisson clocks on
\Omega\cap\overline{\Omega}
.

We consider an additional particle
p_{*}:[t_{\tau},\infty)\to\mathbb{Z}
, such that
p_{*}(t_{\tau})=A_{*}+B_{*}-k^{\prime}
, and it jumps to the right according to the same Poisson clocks (note that
(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-k^{\prime},t_{\tau})\in\Omega\cap\overline{\Omega}
), until
T_{*}:=\inf\{t\geq t_{\tau}:\eta_{t}(p_{*}(t))\in\boldsymbol{\ell}\}
, the time when
p_{*}
catches up with any particle with color in
\boldsymbol{\ell}
.
After time
T_{*}
, we let
p_{*}
continue to jump to the right with rate
1
, but use independent Poisson clocks.

The purpose of introducing
p_{*}
is that, from the coupling using the same Poisson clocks, at any time
t\in[t_{\tau},T_{*}]
in either
\eta_{t}
or
\overline{\eta}_{t}
there is no particle to the right of
p_{*}(t)
with color in
\{\ell_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=k^{\prime}+1}^{C_{*}}
or
\{\overline{\ell}_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=k^{\prime}+1}^{C_{*}}
.
Then we have that
\eta_{t}
and
\overline{\eta}_{t}
are the same on
[p_{*}(t)+1,\infty)\cap\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus if
T_{*}\geq t_{*}
, the event
\mathcal{E}
holds for
\eta
if and only if it holds for
\overline{\eta}
.
With symmetry between
\eta
and
\overline{\eta}
, it now suffices to upper bound

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E},T_{*}<t_{*}\mid\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}}=\mathbf{r},\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}].

For each
i^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
E_{i^{\prime}}
be the waiting time between the
i^{\prime}-1
-th jump and the
i^{\prime}
-th jump of the particle
p_{*}
.
For each
1\leq j\leq k
,
we again take
E_{j,i^{\prime}}
as in the proof of Lemma
5.7
; i.e., it is the waiting time for the
i^{\prime}
-th jump to the right since time
r_{\kappa[j]}
, for the particle colored
\ell_{\kappa[j]}^{\prime}=\ell_{j}
.
Then we have that they are all independent
\Exp(1)
random variables, since before
T_{*}
the particle
p_{*}
is always to the left of the particles with colors in
\boldsymbol{\ell}
.

The events
\mathcal{E}
and
T_{*}<t_{*}
imply the following:

1.

Particle with color
\ell_{j}
reaches
m_{j}
:
for each
1\leq j\leq k
,

\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{m_{j}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[j])}E_{j,i^{\prime}}\leq t_{*}-r_{\kappa[j]};

2.

Some particle is caught up by
p_{*}
:
for some
1\leq\overline{j}\leq k
and some
1\leq\overline{i}\leq m_{\overline{j}}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[\overline{j}])
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
,
0\leq\gamma<C_{*}
, there is

\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{\overline{i}-\kappa[\overline{j}]+k^{\prime}}E_{i^{\prime}}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=\overline{i}+\gamma}^{m_{\overline{j}}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[\overline{j}])+\gamma}E_{\overline{j},i^{\prime}}\leq t_{*}-t_{\tau}.

Here
\gamma
corresponds to the number of left steps the particle colored
\ell_{\kappa[\overline{j}]}^{\prime}=\ell_{\overline{j}}
has taken before
T_{*}
, and
A_{*}+B_{*}+\overline{i}-\kappa[\overline{j}]
corresponds to
p_{*}(T_{*})
.

When
\lambda
is large enough, the probability of the first event (for each
1\leq j\leq k
) is bounded by

2e^{-(t_{*}-r_{\kappa[j]})}\frac{(t_{*}-r_{\kappa[j]})^{m_{j}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[j])}}{(m_{j}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[j]))!}.

(5.10)

For the second event, for fixed
\overline{j},\overline{i},\gamma
, when
\lambda
is large enough the probability is bounded by

2e^{-(t_{*}-t_{\tau})}\frac{(t_{*}-t_{\tau})^{m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}+k^{\prime}}}{(m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}+k^{\prime})!}.

(5.11)

Below we use
H
to denote a large constant depending on
A_{*},B_{*},C_{*},t_{*},t_{\tau}
, and the value can change from line to line.
For (
5.11
), we first sum over
1\leq\gamma<C_{*}
, and get an upper bound of
H\frac{(t_{*}-t_{\tau})^{m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}+k^{\prime}}}{(m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}+k^{\prime})!}
; then by summing over
\overline{i}
we get an upper bound of

Hm_{\overline{j}}\frac{(t_{*}-t_{\tau})^{m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}+k^{\prime}}}{(m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}+k^{\prime})!}<Hm_{\overline{j}}\frac{(t_{*}-t_{\tau})^{m_{\overline{j}}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[\overline{j}])}}{(m_{\overline{j}}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[\overline{j}]))!}.

Then with the bound (
5.10
) for the probabilities of the first event for each
j\neq\overline{j}
, by summing over
\overline{j}
the conclusion follows.
∎

To deduce Lemma
5.4
and

,
\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}},\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}

Lemma 5.9
.

{lem:cut-e-i}

Take any
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\in\Lambda\{C_{*}\}
,
\hat{I}=I
or
I^{+}
, and any
\mathbf{r}=\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{C_{*}}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{C_{*},\infty\}
.
If
\lambda
is large enough (depending on
t_{*}
and
A_{*}+B_{*}
), we have

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}\mid\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}}=\mathbf{r},\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\hat{I}]=\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}\mid\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}}=\mathbf{r},\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}].

Proof.

This proof follows essentially the same ideas as the (feasible cutting information case of) the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

We denote
\mathcal{E}_{-}
as the event where
\mathbf{R}^{C_{*}}=\mathbf{r},\mathbf{L}^{C_{*}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
.
We will show that conditional on
\mathcal{E}_{-}
, the events
\hat{I}
and
\mathcal{E}
are independent.

We recall that
\Pi
is the Poisson field on
\mathbb{Z}\times[0,\infty)
, where for each
x\in\mathbb{Z}
and any
0\leq a<b
,
\Pi(\{x\}\times[a,b])
is the number of times that the clock on the edge
(x-1,x)
rings, in the time interval
[a,b]
.
Also recall that for any
A\in\mathbb{Z}
and
B,C\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote
\mathcal{P}[A,B,C]=(A+B+1-C,T^{A}_{B,C})
.

Denote

U^{-}:=\{(x,t):x\leq A_{*}+B_{*}-C_{*}\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{C_{*}}\{(x,t):0\leq t<r_{i},x\leq A_{*}+B_{*}+1-i\},

and
U^{+}=\mathbb{Z}\times[0,\infty)\setminus U^{-}
.
By Lemma
3.5
, we have that the event
\mathcal{E}_{-}
is measurable with respect to
\Pi
on
U_{-}
, so independent of
\Pi
on
U_{+}
.

We then claim that conditional on
\mathcal{E}_{-}
, the event
\hat{I}
is determined by
\Pi
on
U^{-}
.
Indeed, given
\mathcal{E}_{-}
, for any
\tau<i\leq g
and
1\leq j\leq k_{i}
, we have that
\mathcal{P}[A_{i,j},B_{i,j},i^{\prime}]\in U^{-}
and
\mathcal{P}[A_{i,j}^{+},B_{i,j},i^{\prime}]\in U^{-}
for any
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq C_{i,j}
.
Thus by Lemma
3.5
, conditional on
\mathcal{E}_{-}
,
T^{A_{i,j}}_{B_{i,j},C_{i,j}}
and
T^{A_{i,j}^{+}}_{B_{i,j},C_{i,j}}
are determined by
\Pi
on
U^{-}
.

We next show that conditional on
\mathcal{E}_{-}
, the event
\mathcal{E}
is measurable with respect to
\Pi
on
U^{+}
, when
\lambda
is large enough.
Recall from the proof of Lemma
5.2
, that the event
\mathcal{E}
is determined by
I^{t_{*}}[A,B,C]
, for all
A\in\mathbb{Z}
and
B,C\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

A\leq A_{*}
,
A-C\geq A_{*}-C_{*}
, and
A+B+1-C\geq m_{k}
.
By taking
\lambda
large enough we have that
A+B+1-C\geq A_{*}+B_{*}
.
We next show that for such
A,B,C
, the event
I^{t_{*}}[A,B,C]
is determined by
\Pi
on
U^{+}
, conditional on
\mathcal{E}_{-}
.

Indeed, let
\kappa[1]<\cdots<\kappa[C]
be the first
C
numbers in the set
\{i:1\leq i\leq C_{*},\ell^{\prime}_{i}\leq C_{*}+A-A_{*}\}
.
Such numbers exist because
|\{i:1\leq i\leq C_{*},\ell^{\prime}_{i}\leq C_{*}+A-A_{*}\}|=C_{*}+A-A_{*}\geq C
.
For each
1\leq i\leq C
, we take
B^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
A+B^{\prime}_{i}+1-i=A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[i]
.
Then
T^{A}_{B^{\prime}_{i},i}
is precisely

\inf\{t>0:|\{x\in\mathbb{Z}:x\geq A_{*}+B_{*}+1-\kappa[i],\eta_{t}(x)\leq C_{*}+A-A_{*}\}|\geq i^{\prime}\},

according to Lemma
3.4
.
Under
\mathcal{E}_{-}
this equals
r_{\kappa[i]}
, so
T^{A}_{B^{\prime}_{i},i}=r_{\kappa[i]}
.
Then by Lemma
3.6
, conditional on
\mathcal{E}_{-}
, the event
I^{t_{*}}[A,B,C]
is determined by
\Pi
on
U^{+}
.

By considering all such
A,B,C
, we have that
\mathcal{E}
is determined by
\Pi
on
U^{+}
, conditional on
\mathcal{E}_{-}
.
Thus we conclude that the events
\hat{I}
and
\mathcal{E}
are independent conditional on
\mathcal{E}_{-}
, and that the conclusion follows.
∎

We can now prove Lemma
5.4
using Lemmas
5.7
and

.

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.

We write
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}=\{\ell_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}
.
For each
1\leq j\leq k
, let
\kappa[j]
be the number such that
\ell_{\kappa[j]}^{\prime}=\ell_{j}
, if
\ell_{j}\in\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
, and
\kappa[j]=0
otherwise.
By Lemmas
5.7
and
5.9
, for any
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}
we have

F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\hat{I})<H\prod_{j:1\leq j\leq k,\kappa[j]>0}\frac{t_{*}^{m_{j}}}{(m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+\kappa[j])!}\prod_{j:1\leq j\leq k,\kappa[j]=0}\frac{(t_{*}-t_{\tau})^{m_{j}}}{(m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}+k^{\prime})!}.

where
H
is a constant depending on
t_{*},t_{\tau},A_{*},B_{*},C_{*}
.

We next integrate over all
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}
, and multiply
\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{m_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\cdot m_{j}!}{t_{*}^{m_{j}}}
; then we get that

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{m_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\cdot m_{j}!}{t_{*}^{m_{j}}}\int_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{<}\{k^{\prime},t_{\tau}\}}F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\hat{I})d\mathbf{r}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle Ht_{\tau}^{k^{\prime}}\prod_{j:1\leq j\leq k,\kappa[j]>0}\frac{m_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\cdot m_{j}!}{(m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+\kappa[j])!}\prod_{j:1\leq j\leq k,\kappa[j]=0}\frac{m_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\cdot m_{j}!(1-t_{\tau}/t_{*})^{m_{j}}}{(m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}+k^{\prime})!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ht_{\tau}^{k^{\prime}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}^{\alpha_{j}+|A_{*}+B_{*}|}\prod_{j:1\leq j\leq k,\kappa[j]=0}(1-t_{\tau}/t_{*})^{m_{j}}.

Assuming that there exists some
j
such that
\kappa[j]=0
, we must have the last line
\to 0
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Finally, we consider the case where
\kappa[j]>0
for each
1\leq j\leq k
.
In this case, we must have that
k^{\prime}\geq k
.
We claim that we always have
F(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\hat{I})=0
:
otherwise, we can take a sequence of swaps starting from
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
, ending with
\boldsymbol{\ell}
being the first
k
coordinates.
Thus we have
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\preceq\boldsymbol{\ell}
from the definition of the partial ordering
\prec
, which contradicts with a condition in the statement.
Thus the conclusion holds.
∎

Proof of Lemma

.

We write

5.5
Asymptote of transition probability from cutting information

This subsection is devoted to the proof of Lemma
5.6
.

Proof of Lemma
5.6
.

For the second statement, by Lemmas
5.7
and
5.9
, when
\lambda
is large, we can bound the left-hand side of (
5.6
) by

\prod_{i=1}^{k}2e^{-(t_{*}-s_{i}/m_{i})}\left(1-\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}t_{*}}\right)^{m_{i}-(A_{*}+B_{*}+1-i)}<\prod_{i=1}^{k}2e^{-s_{i}/t_{*}},

where the inequality is by
\left(1-\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}t_{*}}\right)^{m_{i}}\leq e^{-s_{i}/t_{*}}
and
e^{-(t_{*}-s_{i}/m_{i})}\left(1-\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}t_{*}}\right)^{-|A_{*}+B_{*}|}<1
(for
\lambda
large enough).

Below we prove the first statement.
We denote
\mathcal{D}
as the intersection of the events
\mathbf{R}^{k}=\{\frac{s_{i}}{m_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{k}
,
R_{k+1}>t_{\tau}
,
\mathbf{L}^{k}=\boldsymbol{\ell}
, and
I
, for simplicity of notation.

Conditional on
\mathcal{D}
,
we recall the following setup from proofs in the previous subsection.
For each
1\leq j\leq k
, and
i^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
w_{j,i^{\prime}}
be the time when the particle colored
\ell_{j}
makes the
i^{\prime}
-th jump to the right since
\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}}
(note that we ignore jumps to the left).
We also denote
w_{j,0}=\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}}
.
Define
E_{j,i^{\prime}}
as follows: it is the time between
w_{j,i^{\prime}-1}
and
w_{j,i^{\prime}}
such that the particle right next to the particle colored
\ell_{j}
has a larger color.
Then
E_{j,i^{\prime}}
is also the ‘waiting time’ for the particle colored
\ell_{j}
to make the
i^{\prime}
-th jump.

We consider an additional particle
p_{*}:[t_{\tau},\infty)\to\mathbb{Z}
, such that
p_{*}(t_{\tau})=A_{*}+B_{*}-k
, and it jumps to the right according to the same Poisson clocks, until time
T_{*}:=\inf\{t\geq t_{\tau}:\eta(p_{*}(t))\in\boldsymbol{\ell}\}
.
We let
p_{*}
continue to jump to the right with rate
1
after time
T_{*}
, but use some independent Poisson clocks.
Thus before
T_{*}
, in
\eta
only the particles with colors in
\boldsymbol{\ell}
are to the right of
p_{*}
.

For each
i^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
E_{k+1,i^{\prime}}
be the waiting time between the
i^{\prime}-1
-th jump and the
i^{\prime}
-th jump of
p_{*}
.
Then all these
E_{j,i^{\prime}}
, for
1\leq j\leq k+1
and
i^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, are independent
\Exp(1)
random variables.

Proxy events.

We next consider the event
\mathcal{E}_{1}
, defined as follows.
For each
1\leq j\leq k
and
1\leq\gamma\leq m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j
, we have

\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{\gamma}E_{j,i^{\prime}}<\frac{s_{j+1}}{m_{j+1}}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{\gamma}E_{j+1,i^{\prime}}.

Here and below we take
m_{k+1}=1
and
s_{k+1}=t_{\tau}
.
We can then think of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
as the event where the particle colored
\ell_{j+1}
does not catch up with the particle colored
\ell_{j}
, for each
1\leq j<k
; and the particle
p_{*}
does not catch up with the particle colored
\ell_{k}
.

We let
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be the event where for each
1\leq j\leq k
,

\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j}E_{j,i^{\prime}}\leq t_{*}-\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}}\leq\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}+j}E_{j,i^{\prime}}.

This event is the proxy of
\mathcal{E}
, assuming
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
.
We also let
\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\prime}
be the event where for each
1\leq j\leq k
,

\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j}E_{j,i^{\prime}}\leq t_{*}-\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}}.

(5.12)

Then conditional on
\mathcal{D}
, we have that
\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}_{2}\cap\mathcal{E}_{1}
, and
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{2}\subset\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\prime}
.
We also note that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{2}\mid\mathcal{D}]=\prod_{j=1}^{k}e^{-(t_{*}-\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}})}\frac{(t_{*}-\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}})^{m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j}}{(m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j)!},

since
\mathcal{E}_{2}
precisely means that for each
1\leq j\leq k
, for a rate
1
Poisson point process on
[0,t_{*}-\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}}]
there are
m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j
particles.
This implies that

\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j)!}{t_{*}^{m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j}}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{2}\mid\mathcal{D}]\to e^{-kt_{*}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}e^{-s_{j}/t_{*}},

(5.13)

as we send
\lambda\to\infty
.
It now suffices to bound
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{1}\mid\mathcal{D}]+\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{1}\mid\mathcal{D}]
, thus suffices to bound
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{1}\mid\mathcal{D}]
.

Bound the difference
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{1}\mid\mathcal{D}]
.
For each
1\leq\overline{j}\leq k
, we let
\mathcal{E}^{(\overline{j})}
denote the event where there exists
1\leq\gamma\leq m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+\overline{j}
, such that

\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{\gamma}E_{\overline{j}+1,i^{\prime}}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=\gamma+1}^{m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+\overline{j}}E_{\overline{j},i^{\prime}}\leq t_{*}-\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}}.

(5.14)

We then have that
\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\bigcup_{\overline{j}=1}^{k}\mathcal{E}^{(\overline{j})}
.

Below we let
H
denote a large number depending on
t_{*},t_{\tau},A_{*},B_{*},C_{*}
, and its value can change from line to line.
When
\lambda>H
, for given
\gamma
the probability of (
5.14
) is bounded by

2e^{-(t_{*}-\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}})}\frac{(t_{*}-\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}})^{m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+\overline{j}}}{(m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+\overline{j})!}.

By summing over
\gamma
we get

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}^{(\overline{j})}\mid\mathcal{D}]<\frac{Hm_{\overline{j}}(t_{*}-\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}})^{m_{\overline{j}}}}{(m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+\overline{j})!}.

We also note that (
5.12
) for
j<\overline{j}
is independent of
\mathcal{E}^{(\overline{j})}
.
These imply that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{1}\mid\mathcal{D}]<H\sum_{\overline{j}=1}^{k}\frac{m_{\overline{j}}(t_{*}-\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}})^{m_{\overline{j}}}}{(m_{\overline{j}}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+\overline{j})!}\prod_{j=1}^{\overline{j}-1}\frac{(t_{*}-\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}})^{m_{j}}}{(m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j)!},

so

\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j)!}{t_{*}^{m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j}}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{1}\mid\mathcal{D}]\\
<H\sum_{\overline{j}=1}^{k}\frac{m_{\overline{j}}(t_{*}-\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}})^{m_{\overline{j}}}}{t_{*}^{m_{\overline{j}}}}\prod_{j=1}^{\overline{j}-1}\frac{(t_{*}-\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}})^{m_{j}}}{t_{*}^{m_{j}}}\prod_{j=\overline{j}+1}^{k}\frac{(m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j)!}{t_{*}^{m_{j}}}.

(5.15)

For the
\overline{j}
-th summand in the right-hand side, as we send
\lambda\to\infty
, we get

\frac{m_{\overline{j}}(t_{*}-\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}})^{m_{\overline{j}}}}{t_{*}^{m_{\overline{j}}}}e^{\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{t_{*}}\frac{m_{\overline{j}}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}}}\to 0,

(5.16)

since by taking the logarithm, we get

\log(m_{\overline{j}})+\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{t_{*}}\frac{m_{\overline{j}}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}}\left(1+\frac{t_{*}m_{\overline{j}+1}}{s_{\overline{j}+1}}\log\left(1-\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{t_{*}m_{\overline{j}+1}}\right)\right)<\log(m_{\overline{j}})-\frac{1}{2}\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{t_{*}}\frac{m_{\overline{j}}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}}\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{t_{*}m_{\overline{j}+1}},

where the inequality is by the elementary inequality of
1+x^{-1}\log(1-x)<-x/2
(for any
0<x<1
).
As
\lambda\to\infty
this
\to-\infty
.
For each
1\leq j<\overline{j}
, we have

\frac{(t_{*}-\frac{s_{j}}{m_{j}})^{m_{j}}}{t_{*}^{m_{j}}}\to e^{-s_{j}/t_{*}},

(5.17)

and for each
\overline{j}<j\leq k
, we have

e^{-\frac{1}{k}\frac{s_{\overline{j}+1}}{t_{*}}\frac{m_{\overline{j}}}{m_{\overline{j}+1}}}\frac{(m_{j}-A_{*}-B_{*}-1+j)!}{t_{*}^{m_{j}}}\to 0

(5.18)

Thus by (
5.15

6.1
Some further questions

We now discuss some other possible extensions to Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
.

{ssec:shift-cons}

A major question is whether some shift constraints in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
can be lifted.
Let’s consider just two passage times.
Using arguments that slightly generalize those in Example
2.1
, or some simplification of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we can get the following conditional equality in distribution.

Example 6.1
.

{ex:condi}

Take
A_{1},A_{2},A_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
B_{1},B_{2},C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfying the following conditions:
A_{1}\leq A_{2},A_{2}^{\prime}
, and
A_{1}+B_{1}-C_{1}\geq A_{2}+B_{2}-C_{2},A_{2}^{\prime}+B_{2}-C_{2}
, and
A_{1}+B_{1}\geq A_{2}+B_{2},A_{2}^{\prime}+B_{2}
. For any
0<t_{1}\leq t_{2}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[T^{A_{1}}_{B_{1},C_{1}}<t_{1},T^{A_{2}}_{B_{2},C_{2}}<t_{2}]=\mathbb{P}[T^{A_{1}}_{B_{1},C_{1}}<t_{1},T^{A_{2}^{\prime}}_{B_{2},C_{2}}<t_{2}].

We note that this statement is non-trivial only if
C_{1}<C_{2}
, since otherwise we have that
T^{A_{1}}_{B_{1},C_{1}}\leq T^{A_{2}}_{B_{2},C_{2}}
and
T^{A_{1}}_{B_{1},C_{1}}\leq T^{A_{2}^{\prime}}_{B_{2},C_{2}}
.
The main difference between this example and Theorem
1.3
is that here the condition
A_{1}-C_{1}\geq A_{2}-C_{2},A_{2}^{\prime}-C_{2}
is replaced by the weaker ones
A_{1}+B_{1}-C_{1}\geq A_{2}+B_{2}-C_{2},A_{2}^{\prime}+B_{2}-C_{2}
; whereas the equality in distribution only holds in half of the space
[0,\infty)^{2}
.

To see that the condition
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
is necessary, we can compute the following example.

Example 6.2
.

For any
t_{1},t_{2}>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}[T^{0}_{3,1}=t_{1},T^{0}_{1,2}=t_{2}]=\mathbb{P}[T^{0}_{3,1}=t_{1},T^{1}_{1,2}=t_{2}]=\begin{cases}e^{-t_{2}}-e^{-t_{1}-t_{2}}(1+t_{1}),\quad&\text{ if }t_{1}\leq t_{2}\\
e^{-t_{1}}(t_{1}-t_{2}+1)-e^{-t_{1}-t_{2}}(1+t_{1}),\quad&\text{ if }t_{1}>t_{2}\end{cases},

and

\mathbb{P}[T^{0}_{3,1}=t_{1},T^{2}_{1,2}=t_{2}]=\begin{cases}e^{-t_{2}}-e^{-t_{1}-t_{2}}(1+t_{1}),\quad&\text{ if }t_{1}\leq t_{2}\\
2e^{-t_{1}}+e^{-t_{1}-t_{2}}(t_{2}(t_{1}-t_{2})^{2}/2-2(t_{2}+1)),\quad&\text{ if }t_{1}>t_{2}\end{cases}.

From this, it seems that the constraint of rectangle ordering is needed to get equalities in distribution.
However, we expect that some conditional equalities in distribution hold with weaker constraints, and it will be interesting to find such an identity generalizing Example
6.1
to more than two passage times.

In another direction (of lifting some shift constraints), one can ask whether the ordering condition can be imposed only on pairs of passage times where relative shift happens.
To be more precise, one can consider the following question, which is in a similar spirit as
[
BGW22
, Conjecture 1.5]
.

Question 6.3
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{N}
, and take
A_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
B_{i},C_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
1\leq i\leq g
.
Let
1\leq\iota<g
, and for any
1\leq i\leq g
we let
A_{i}^{+}=A_{i}+\mathds{1}[i>\iota]
. Suppose that for any
1\leq i\leq\iota<i^{\prime}\leq g
, we have
\mathcal{R}^{A_{i}}_{B_{i},C_{i}}\leq\mathcal{R}^{A_{i^{\prime}}}_{B_{i^{\prime}},C_{i^{\prime}}}
and
\mathcal{R}^{A_{i}^{+}}_{B_{i},C_{i}}\leq\mathcal{R}^{A_{i^{\prime}}^{+}}_{B_{i^{\prime}},C_{i^{\prime}}}
.
Are the vectors

\{T_{B_{i},C_{i}}^{A_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{g}
and
\{T_{B_{i},C_{i}}^{A_{i}^{+}}\}_{i=1}^{g}
equal in distribution?

This equality in distribution (if true) implies both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
.
The main difference of this with Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
is that, the rectangles
\mathcal{R}^{A_{i}}_{B_{i},C_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq\iota
or for
\iota<i\leq g
are not ordered, and these
A_{i}
can be different. We hope that an answer to this question will give a better understanding of the limitation of and the mechanism behind these distributional equalities in the colored TASEP.

It is also natural to ask if some equalities in distribution can be obtained for the height function of the colored stochastic six-vertex model.
For example, can we prove some shift-invariance of the height function for more general points (than those in Theorem
3.1
), thus extending the results of
[
BGW22
]
and
[
Gal21
]
?
We note that the colored stochastic six-vertex model can be viewed as a discrete-time version of the colored ASEP (Asymmetric Simple Exclusion Process), which is a generalization of the colored TASEP, such that when the Poisson clock on edge
(x,x+1)
rings, and the color at site
x
is larger than the color at site
x+1
, we swap the particles with some probability
p\in[0,1]
.
The colored TASEP then corresponds to the colored stochastic six-vertex model with the parameter
b_{2}=0
.
By adapting our arguments to the discrete-time setting, and using the invertibility of the transition matrices instead of the analyticity of the transition distribution functions, we can get extensions of the height function shift-invariance of the colored stochastic six-vertex model, when
b_{2}=0
.
However, for the general case where
b_{2}>0
,
one might need to prove some shift-invariance for the multi-time distribution of the colored ASEP with
p>0
.
Our arguments for the colored TASEP rely on the following observation: for the trajectories of the
i
-th rightmost particle in some
\mu^{A}
, and of the
j
-th leftmost hole in some
\mu^{A^{\prime}}
, they will be independent once they cross each other; but this is not true for the colored ASEP.
Some new ideas are needed to prove (or disprove) possible extensions to the ASEP setting.

{sec:osp}

In this section, we prove Theorem

Recall that for the colored TASEP, we encoded it with a family of TASEPs

We similarly encode OSP on

\{1,\ldots,N\}

\nu^{N,A}_{0}(x)=0

is the time for the last jump of

We use the following input from
[

]

To state it, we consider two kinds of operators on particle-hole configurations on

introduced there.
For each

For each

be the

, which pushes all particles onto
(-\infty,n]\cap\mathbb{Z}
: for the
j
-th rightmost particle, it is moved to site
x\wedge n+1-j
, if it is originally at site
x
.

We couple the colored TASEP with OSP on
\{1,\ldots,N\}
, such that the Poisson clocks on
(k,k+1)
for each
1\leq k\leq N-1
are the same.

Lemma 7.1

(
[
AHR09
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

{lem:cbopera}

Under the above coupling, we have that
B_{N}R_{A}\mu^{A}
on
\{1,\cdots,N\}
is the same as
\nu^{N,A}
, for each
1\leq A\leq N-1
.

We can now prove the joint equality in distribution between
\mathbf{U}_{N}
and passage times in LPP, from Theorem
1.1
and using this connection.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

By Lemma
7.1
,
for each
1\leq A\leq N-1
, if and only if
t\leq T^{A}_{N-A,A}
, there are at least
A
particles on or to the right of site
N-A+1
in
\mu^{A}_{t}
, thus in
\nu^{N,A}_{t}
; and this is equivalent to that

\nu^{N,A}_{t}(x)=0,\forall N-A+1\leq x\leq N
and
\nu^{N,A}_{t}(x)=\infty,\forall 1\leq x\leq N-A
.
Thus we must have that
T^{A}_{N-A,A}=U_{N}(A)
.

Via the connection between LPP and TASEP, as stated in Section
1
, and symmetry between the two coordinates in LPP, we have that
\{T^{1}_{N-A,A}\}_{A=1}^{N-1}
has the same distribution as
\{L_{(1,1),(A,N-A)}\}_{A=1}^{N-1}
.
It remains to show that it also has the same distribution as
\{T^{A}_{N-A,A}\}_{A=1}^{N-1}
.
For this we apply Theorem
1.1
: for each
1\leq i<N-1
, we have that
\{T^{A\wedge i}_{N-A,A}\}_{A=1}^{N-1}
has the same distribution as
\{T^{A\wedge(i+1)}_{N-A,A}\}_{A=1}^{N-1}
.
Thus
\{T^{A\wedge i}_{N-A,A}\}_{A=1}^{N-1}
has the same distribution for each
1\leq i\leq N-1
.
By taking
i=1
and
i=N-1
we get the conclusion.
∎

We now deduce the asymptotic results from Theorem
1.8
, using existing results of LPP.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
and
1.10
.

By Theorem
1.8
, it suffices to consider the function

x\mapsto\frac{(y(1-y))^{1/6}}{(1+2\sqrt{y(1-y)})^{2/3}N^{1/3}}\\
\times(L_{(1,1),(\lfloor yN+xN^{2/3}\rfloor,N-\lfloor yN+xN^{2/3}\rfloor)}-(1+2\sqrt{y(1-y)})N-\frac{1-2y}{\sqrt{y(1-y)}}xN^{2/3}).

We have that it converges to
\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)-x^{2}
weakly, in the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.
The finite-dimensional convergence follows from e.g.
[
BF08
,
BP08
]
, and it can be upgraded to uniform convergence on compact sets using a tightness result from
[
Pim18
]
.
See
[
BGZ21
, Theorem 3.8]
for a proof in the
y=1/2
case, which also goes through essentially verbatim for any fixed
y\in(0,1)
.

For Theorem
1.10
, we note that
k_{*}=\argmax_{1\leq k\leq N-1}L_{(1,1),(k,N-k)}
.
Then we just need the following two additional facts: the process
\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)-x^{2}
attains its maximum at a unique point, and that
\frac{k_{*}-N/2}{N^{2/3}}
is tight as
N\to\infty
.
The first fact is proved in
[
CH14
]
.
The tightness follows from a transversal estimate in LPP, e.g.
[
BGZ21
, Proposition 4.2]
, which implies the following: there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, such that for any
\phi
large enough and any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{1\leq k\leq N-1,|k-N/2|>\phi N^{2/3}}L_{(1,1),(k,N-k)}>2N-c_{1}\phi^{2}N^{1/3}\right]<e^{-c_{2}\phi^{3}}.

We also have that
\mathbb{P}[L_{(1,1),(\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\lceil N/2\rceil)}-2N<-\phi N^{1/3}]<c^{-1}e^{-c\phi^{3}}
for some constant
c>0
.
This is due to that
L_{(1,1),(\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\lceil N/2\rceil)}
has the same distribution as the largest eigenvalue of

X^{*}X
, where
X
is an
\lfloor N/2\rfloor\times\lceil N/2\rceil
matrix with i.i.d. standard complex Gaussian entries (see
[
Joh00
, Proposition 1.4]
); and the estimate is from
[
LR10
, Theorem 2]
.
With these bounds on the passage times, we get tightness of
\frac{k_{*}-N/2}{N^{2/3}}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

It is proved in
[
BBS21
, Theorem 2.1]
that for the function

x\mapsto L_{(1,1),(\lfloor yN\rfloor+x,N-\lfloor yN\rfloor-x)}-L_{(1,1),(\lfloor yN\rfloor,N-\lfloor yN\rfloor)},

its total variation distance to
g_{N}
decays to zero (actually it is stated for the total variation distance between the difference of the passage times, and the so-called Busemann function, which has the distribution of a two-sided random walk).
Then the conclusion immediately follows from Theorem
1.8
.
∎

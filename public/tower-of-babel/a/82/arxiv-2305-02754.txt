Abstract.

We present a new lower bound for Euler’s beta function,
B(x,y)
, which states that the inequality

B(x,y)>\frac{x+y}{xy}\left(1-\frac{2xy}{x+y+1}\right)

holds on
(0,1]\times(0,1]
, which improves a lower bound obtained by P. Ivády
[
12
, Theorem, (3.2)]
in the case of
0<x+y<1
.

Keywords.
beta function, gamma function, psi function, inequalities

Introduction

The classical gamma function and its logarithmic derivative (or psi function) are defined as

\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{x-1}dt\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\psi(x)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(x)}{\Gamma(x)},\hskip 20.00003ptx>0.

The derivatives of
\psi
, i.e.
\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime},\cdots
, are called polygamma functions. The beta function, as a well-known Euler’s integral of the first kind, is defined by

B(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}dt,\hskip 10.00002ptx,y>0,

which is closely related to the gamma function by a elegant identity

B(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(x+y)}.

The utility of the beta function is often overshadowed by that of the gamma function, partly perhaps because it can be evaluated in terms of the gamma function. In fact, it has various applications not only in the theory of special functions, but it also plays a role in other fields, for instance, statistics, mathematical physics, and graph theory; see
[
1
,
10
,
17
]
.

In the recent past years, numerous papers on remarkable inequalities related to the gamma and polygamma functions were published (see
[
25
,
5
,
7
,
15
,
18
,
14
,
16
,
9
]
and and the references therein), only few inequalities concerning the beta function can be found in the literature
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
11
]
.

In 2000, S. Dragomir at al.
[
8
]
proved that the inequality

0\leq\frac{1}{xy}-B(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{4}

holds for
x,y\geq 1
. Later, Alzer
[
2
]
improved
1/4
to the best possible constant
\max\limits_{x\geq 1}\{\Delta(x)\}=0.08731\cdots
, where
\Delta(x)=1/x^{2}-\Gamma^{2}(x)/\Gamma(2x)
.

For all
x,y\in(0,1]
, sharp rational bounds for the difference
1/(xy)-B(x,y)
were obtained in
[
4
]
, which are given by

\frac{1}{xy}\left[1-\alpha\frac{(1-x)(1-y)}{(1+x)(1+y)}\right]\leq B(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{xy}\left[1-\beta\frac{(1-x)(1-y)}{(1+x)(1+y)}\right]

(1.1)

with the best possible constants
\alpha=\frac{2}{3}\pi^{2}-4=2.57973\cdots
and
\beta=1
.

In 2012, Ivády
[
12
]
strengthened the right side of (
1.1
), although there were some errors in the proof and had been corrected in
[
13
]
, in which a double inequality for
B(x,y)
was presented, explicitly, for all
x,y\in(0,1]
, the inequalities

\frac{x+y-xy}{xy}\leq B(x,y)\leq\frac{x+y}{xy(1+xy)}

(1.2)

hold with equality only for
x=y=1
. We remark that not only the upper bound of (
1.1
) has been improved, but the lower bound of (
1.2
) is also better than that of (
1.1
) for
x,y\in(0,1]
with
x+y\leq 1
.
Indeed, due to
\alpha>5/2
, for
x+y\leq 1
,

\displaystyle x+y-xy-\left[1-\alpha\frac{(1-x)(1-y)}{(1+x)(1+y)}\right]>x+y-xy-\left[1-\frac{5}{2}\frac{(1-x)(1-y)}{(1+x)(1+y)}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-x)(1-y)(3-2x-2y-2xy)}{2(1+x)(1+y)}\geq\frac{(1-x)(1-y)\left[3-2x-2y-(x+y)^{2}\right]}{2(1+x)(1+y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-x)(1-y)(1-x-y)(3+x+y)}{2(1+x)(1+y)}\geq 0.

The aim of this article is to present a new lower bound of
B(x,y)
for all
x,y\in(0,1]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
x,y\in(0,1]
. Then the inequality

B(x,y)>\frac{x+y}{xy}\left(1-\frac{2xy}{x+y+1}\right)

holds.

Remark 1.1
.

It was observed that

1-\frac{xy}{x+y}\geq 1-\frac{2xy}{x+y+1}

holds for
x+y\geq 1
and
xy>0
, which together with (
1.2
) implies

B(x,y)\geq\frac{x+y}{xy}\left(1-\frac{xy}{x+y}\right)\geq\frac{x+y}{xy}\left(1-\frac{2xy}{x+y+1}\right),

(1.3)

where the equality cannot hold simultaneously. This enables us to prove the Theorem
1.1
only in the case of
0<x+y<1
. Moreover, if Theorem
1.1
is true, then

B(x,y)>\frac{x+y}{xy}\left(1-\frac{2xy}{x+y+1}\right)\geq\frac{x+y}{xy}\left(1-\frac{xy}{x+y}\right)

holds for
x,y\in(0,1]
with
x+y\leq 1
, of which in the case, that is to say, our lower bound is better than that of (
1.2
).

The following lemma offers a simple criterion to determine the sign of a class of special polynomial, which is called a Positive-Negative type (PN type for short) polynomial (see

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
24
, Lemma 4]
)
Let
P_{n}(x)
be a Positive-Negative type polynomial of degree
n
, which is given by

P_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{k}x^{k}-\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}a_{k}x^{k},

where
a_{k}\geq 0
for all
k\geq 0
and there exist at least two integers
0\leq k_{1}\leq m
and
m+1\leq k_{2}\leq n
such that
a_{k_{1}},a_{k_{2}}\neq 0
.
Then there exist
x_{0}\in(0,\infty)
such that

P_{n}(x_{0})=0
and
P_{n}(x)>0
for
x\in(0,x_{0})
and
P_{n}(x)<0
for
x\in(x_{0},\infty).

Remark 2.1
.

For a PN type polynomial, it can be easily seen that
P_{n}(x)>0
for
x\in(0,x_{1})
if

and
P_{n}(x)<0
for

if

. Correspondingly, for a NP type polynomial
P_{n}(x)
, the signs of
P_{n}(x)
on
(0,x_{1})
and
(x_{2},\infty)
are reversed.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
in the case of
0<x+y\leq 1
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
x\leq y
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
0<x\leq y\leq 1-x
(namely,
0<x\leq 1/2
) and

\displaystyle F(x,y)

\displaystyle=\log\left[\frac{xyB(x,y)}{x+y}\right]-\log\left(1-\frac{2xy}{x+y+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left[\frac{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(y+1)}{\Gamma(x+y+1)}\right]-\log\left(1-\frac{2xy}{x+y+1}\right).

(3.1)

Differentiating
F
with respect to
x
(res.
y
) gives

\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}=\psi(x+1)-\psi(x+y+1)+\frac{2y(1+y)}{(1+x+y)(1+x+y-2xy)}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=\psi(y+1)-\psi(x+y+1)+\frac{2x(1+x)}{(1+x+y)(1+x+y-2xy)}

\displaystyle=\psi(y+1)-\psi(y+x)-\frac{1}{x+y}+\frac{2x(1+x)}{(1+x+y)(1+x+y-2xy)}.

(3.3)

We divide into two cases to complete the proof.

\displaystyle=\frac{xQ(x,y)}{(1+x+y-2xy)\prod_{j=1}^{3}\big[(y+j)(x+y+j)\big]},

(

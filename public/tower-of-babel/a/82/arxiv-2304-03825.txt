Abstract
For integers r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 and g ≥ 3 g\geq 3 , an ( r , g ) (r,g) -graph is an r r -regular graph with girth g g , and an ( r , g ) (r,g) -cage is an ( r , g ) (r,g) -graph of minimum order. It is conjectured that all ( r , g ) (r,g) -cages with even g g are bipartite, that is, have chromatic number 2. Here we introduce the idea of an ( r , g , χ ) (r,g,\chi) -graph, an r r -regular graph with girth g g and chromatic number χ \chi . We investigate the existence of such graphs and study in detail the ( r , 3 , 3 ) (r,3,3) -graphs of minimum order. We also consider ( r , g , χ ) (r,g,\chi) -graphs for which there is a χ \chi -coloring where the color classes differ by at most 1.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we work with simple and finite graphs. We study a generalization of the
Cage Problem
, which has been widely studied since cages were introduced by Tutte
[
9
]
in 1947 and after Erdös and Sachs
[
4
]
proved their existence in 1963. An
(r,g)
-graph
is an
r
-regular graph in which the shortest cycle has length equal to
g
; that is, it is an
r
-regular graph with girth
g
. An
(r,g)
-cage
is an
(r,g)
-graph with the smallest possible number of vertices among all
(r,g)
-graphs; the order of an
(r,g)
-cage is denoted by
n(r,g)
. The Cage Problem consists of finding
(r,g)
-cages; it is well-known that
(r,g)
-cages have been determined only for very limited sets of parameter pairs
(r,g)
. There exists a simple lower bound for
n(r,g)
, given by Moore (see
[
5
]
), and denoted by
n_{0}(r,g)
. The cages that attain this bound are called
Moore cages
.

The existence of cages for all such integers
r,g
has been proved, but very few of these cages
have been determined. However, based on these known cages, an interesting conjecture has been made (this conjecture appears in
[
8
]
, but we can say that it is a
folklore Conjecture
, because it is very well-known among people interested on the Cage Problem).

Conjecture 1.1
.

For even values of girth
g
, all
(r,g)
-cages are bipartite.

This conjecture naturally leads to a relationship between
(r,g)
-cages and chromatic number since another way of stating this conjecture is the following:

Conjecture 1.2
.

(Conjecture
1.1
)

For even values of girth
g
, all
(r,g)
-cages have chromatic number equal to 2.

Clearly, every
(r,g)
-cage with odd
g
has chromatic number at least
3
. Furthermore, for any
g\geq 3
, any
(r,g)
-graph, independent of whether it is a cage or not, may have chromatic number at least
3
.

For

Proof.

We first determine a lower bound on

. Let
G
be an

-regular graph with

Color

Since

|A|+|C|\geq r.
Consequently

|V(G)|\geq\frac{3r}{2}
and so

In the case
r

In the case

r

The graph

-regular. Removing any 1-factor from

Finally, if
r

r+\lceil r/2\rceil

|A|=|B|=\lceil r/2\rceil
and
|C|=\lfloor r/2\rfloor.
Notice that every vertex in
A
is adjacent to every vertex in
B\cup C
and every vertex in
B
is adjacent to every vertex in

But then each vertex in
C
has degree
r+1
, which contradicts the assumption that
G
is
r
-regular. Thus,
|V(G)\leq r+\lceil r/2\rceil+1
, which is odd. Since

is

-regular with odd

as above, so that every vertex of

has degree

in

has degree

in

Select

4
Existence of
(r,g,3)
-graphs

In this section, we will prove, assuming Conjecture
1.1
, that
(r,g,3)
-graphs exist for all
r\geq 2
and any girth
g\geq 3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The
(r,g,3)
-graphs exist for any
r\geq 2
and
g\geq 3
.

Proof.

We will divide the proof into two cases depending on the parity of the girth.

•

Case 1:
Suppose that girth
g\geq 3
is odd. Clearly a
g
-cycle is a
(2,g,3)
-graph, in fact, it is a
(2,g,3)
-cage and Brooks’ Theorem, together with the existence of
(3,g)
-cages, implies the existence of
(3,g,3)
-graphs, or
(3,g,3)
-cages, for odd
g\geq 3
. We now prove the existence of
(r,g,3)
-graphs for
r\geq 4
and
g\geq 3
odd. Let
G
be an
(r,g+1)
-cage and, as
g
is odd, by Conjecture
1.2
,
G
is a
(r,g+1,2)
-cage.

Now we divide the proof in other two sub-cases, depending of the parity of
r
:

–

Case (1a):
Suppose that
r\geq 4
is even. Let
x
be a vertex in a
(g+1)
- cycle and let
N(x)=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r}\}
, ordering these neighbours with the property that, at least,
\{x_{1},x,x_{2}\}
is in a
(g+1)
-cycle. Let
H=G-x
; we construct a new graph
H^{\prime}
with
V(H^{\prime})=V(H)
and
E(H^{\prime})=E(H)\cup\{x_{i},x_{i+1}\}
for odd
1\leq i\leq r-1
. As
d_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})=2
, for any
1\leq\{i,j\}\leq r
and
i\not=j
, and
\{x_{1},x,x_{2}\}
is in a
(g+1)
-cycle in
G
, the edge
\{x_{1}x_{2}\}
is in a
g
-cycle in
H^{\prime}
, and we obtain an
(r,g)
-graph.
As
g
is odd, the chromatic number of
H^{\prime}
is at least
3
. To finish, we will give a
3
-coloring of
H^{\prime}
.
As
G
is bipartite, if
f:V(G)\longrightarrow\{0,1\}
is a
2
-coloring of
G
, it follows that if
f(x)=0
then
f(N(x))=1
. We give a
3
-coloring of
H^{\prime}
as
f^{\prime}(V(H^{\prime}))\longrightarrow\{0,1,2\}
with
f(x_{j})=2
for all even
2\leq j\leq r
and
f^{\prime}(z)=f(z)
in any other case.

–

Case (1b):
Suppose that
r\geq 4
is odd. Take an
xy
-edge, let
N(x)-\{y\}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r-1}\}
and
N(y)-\{x\}=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{r-1}\}
, with the property that also here
\{x_{1},x,x_{2}\}
is in a
(g+1)
-cycle. As
r
is odd,
r-1
is even; let
H=G-\{x,y\}
. Then
H^{\prime}
a graph such that
V(H^{\prime})=V(H)
and
E(H^{\prime})=E(H)\cup\{x_{i}x_{i+1},y_{i}y_{i+1}\}
, for odd
1\leq i\leq r-2
. As in the previous case, the edge
\{x_{1}x_{2}\}
is in a
g
-cycle in
H^{\prime}
, and we obtain an
(r,g)
-graph.
Also, as before, the chromatic number of
H^{\prime\prime}
is at least
3
and we give a
3
-coloring
f^{\prime}
of
H^{\prime}
taking into account the
2
-coloring of
G
as before. Here
f^{\prime}(V(H^{\prime}))\longrightarrow\{0,1,2\}
with
f(\{x_{j},y_{j}\})=2
for all even
2\leq j\leq r-1
and
f^{\prime}(z)=f(z)
in any other case.

In both cases, we obtain an
(r,g,3)
-graph for odd
g\geq 3
.

•

Case 2:
Suppose the girth
g\geq 4
is even and
let
G
be an
(r,g)
-cage. By Conjecture
1.2
,
G
is an
(r,g,2)
-cage. First, we will note that in
G
, there is no edge
e
that is in all the cycles of length
g
. To prove this statement we will use the constructions given in
Case 1
. Suppose that the statement is false and that all the
g
-cycles in
G
contain
xy=e
.
Here, divide the proof into two cases as before, depending on the parity of
r
. As we will use the previous constructions, we will give the proof without many details. Suppose that
r
is even. Delete the vertex
x
and, as a consequence, we also delete the edge
e=xy
. By hypothesis, the new graph has girth at least
g+1
, all the vertices have degree
r
except a set of them (all of degree
r-1
and mutually at distance
2
). We make the same construction as in
Case 1a
and obtain a
r
-regular graph of girth

of order

, which is a contradiction. In the other case, if
r
is odd, we delete the edge
e=xy
and repeat the construction given in
Case 1b
. Since we delete the edge
e
, none of the cycles in the new graph has length smaller than
g+1
. Also, in this construction, we only add edges between vertices that, in the original graph, were at distance two and that were not incident with the edge
e
, that is all of the new cycles have length at least
g+1
. Then, we obtain an
r
-regular graph of girth
g
and order
n(r,g)-2
, which also is a contradiction.

Consequently, given an edge
xy
in
G
contained in a cycle of length
g
there exists at least one other cycle of length
g
that does not contain
xy
. Subdividing the edge
xy
with a vertex

of degree two, we obtain a graph
H
, such that
V(H)=V(G)\cup\{z\}
and
E(H)=E(G)\cup\{xz,zy\}
, where

is a
2
-path in
H
. As

was in a
g
-cycle in
G
, now the

-path is in a
(g+1)
-cycle of odd length (because

. Therefore, the chromatic number of

3
and if

is colored as before, the coloring of

preserves all the colors of

and adds a new color in

. Notice also that, as there exists a

-cycle that does not contain

the girth of the graph is still

.
Here we also give two different constructions depending of the parity of
r
.

–

Case (2a):
If
r
is even, we take
\frac{r}{2}
copies of
H
and we identify all the vertices
z
in each copy. All the vertices have degree
r
and the graph has girth
g
because all of these graphs have
g
-cycles that do not include the edge
xy
.

–

Case (2b):
If
r
is odd, first of all take a copy of
H
, called
H^{\prime}
. Clearly, the chromatic number of
H
is equal to three, and
H
inherited the coloring of
G
except for the new vertex
z
that has color
2
. Recoloring
H^{\prime}
exchanging the chromatic classes of colors
0
and
2
of

in
H^{\prime}
, that is,
H^{\prime}
is coloring with colors
1
and
2
in all the vertices, except one vertex, called

, that receives the color

. Join these two graphs adding the edge

. Here all the vertices have degree

except

and

that have degree

. Take
\frac{r-3}{2}
copies of

and
H^{\prime}
(preserving their colorings) and identify them in

respectively. In this new graph called

, all the vertices have degree

is clearly

) is now

.

In both cases, we obtain

-graphs, and we prove their existence.

∎

Definition 5.1
.

Let
G

\chi
a vertex coloring of
G
. We say that
\chi
is an
equitable coloring
of

Definition 5.2
.

An
(r,g,\chi)
- graph

(r,g,\chi)
-
equitable graph
if there exits an
\chi
-equitable coloring of

Remark 5.1
.

All the

-cages constructed in the proof of Theorem

We next show, assuming Theorem 4.1, that

Theorem 5.1
.

The
(r,g,3)
-equitable graphs exist for all
r\geq 2
and
g\geq 3
.

Proof.

Let
G
be an
(r,g,3)
-graph and consider a 3-coloring of

Assume further that

Construct a new graph

consisting of three disjoint copies

of

Then

is
r

and

. Let

in

We give an equitable 3-coloring of

Define

Then

Thus

is an equitable 3-coloring of

is an equitable

and this implies that no vertex of
\mathcal{C}_{5}^{4}
other than
x_{13}
can be red and no vertices in

A similar analysis must be done if we have two red vertices in

Future work.

Obviously, natural future research is working with regular graphs of odd girth and a chromatic number greater or equal to
4
, because in this paper we only worked with graphs of chromatic number equal to
3
.

For example, for girth
5
the first cage that has chromatic number equal to
4
is the
(5,5)
-cage which has order
30
. Obviously, it is a
(5,5,4)
-cage, but we do not know if it is equitable or not.
Moreover, there exist a lot of nice questions about the chromatic number of the cages, that is, about the chromatic number of the minimum
(k;g)
-graphs known until this moment (for any
k\geq 3
and odd
g
).

On the other hand, when
g
is even and
\chi\not=2
, the problem of finding
(r,g,\chi)
-graphs, or
(r,g,\chi)
-cages, is naturally related to the problem of finding cages with given girth pair. These graphs were introduced in
[
6
]
and studied, for example in
[
1
,
3
]
. The
(r;g,h)
-
cages
are
r
-regular graphs of minimum order and fixed girth
g
, where
h
is the length of the smallest cycle of different parity of
g
and
g<h
. Obviously, if
g
is even, an
(r;g,h)
-graph
G
(in this context) could be an
(r,g,3)
-graph (in our context), if the chromatic number of
G
is
3
. For us, a natural future research question is to study the relationship between these two “kinds” of cages.

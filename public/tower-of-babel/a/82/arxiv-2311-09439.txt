Appendix A
Robot dynamics

Here we present the complete robot dynamics
as a linear-time invariant system of equations representing the discrete-time Hill-Clohessy-Wiltshire equations for relative orbital motion as defined in
[
1
]
.
This system describes the robot’s state as a function of applied controls and state at the previous time step.

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{i}_{t+1}=A\mathbf{x}^{i}_{t}+B\mathbf{u}_{t}^{i}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}4-3\cos(n\Delta t)&0&0\\
6(\sin(n\Delta t)-n\Delta t)&1&0\\
0&0&\cos(n\Delta t)\\
3n\sin(n\Delta t)&0&0\\
-6n(1-\cos(n\Delta t))&0&0\\
0&0&-n\sin(n\Delta t)\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right.\ \cdots

\displaystyle\;\cdots\ \left.\begin{smallmatrix}\frac{1}{n}\sin(1-n\Delta t)&\frac{2}{n}(1-\cos(n\Delta t))&0\\
-\frac{2}{n}(1-\cos(n\Delta t))&\frac{1}{n}(4\sin(n\Delta t)-3n\Delta t)&0\\
0&0&\frac{1}{n}\sin(n\Delta t)\\
\cos(n\Delta t)&2\sin(n\Delta t)&0\\
-2\sin(n\Delta t)&4\cos(n\Delta t)-3&0\\
0&0&\cos(n\Delta t)\end{smallmatrix}\right]

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\frac{1}{n}\sin(n\Delta t)&\frac{2}{n}(1-\cos(n\Delta t))\\
-\frac{2}{n^{2}}(n\Delta t-\sin(n\Delta t))&\frac{4}{n^{2}}(1-\cos(n\Delta t))-\frac{3}{2}\Delta t^{2}\\
0&0\\
\frac{1}{n}\sin(n\Delta t)&\frac{2}{n}(1-\cos(n\Delta t)\\
-\frac{2}{n}(1-\cos(n\Delta t))&\frac{4}{n}\sin(n\Delta t)-3\Delta t\\
0&0\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right.\ \cdots

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\,\cdots\left.\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
0\\
\frac{1}{n^{2}}(1-\cos(n\Delta t))\\
0\\
0\\
\frac{1}{n}\sin(n\Delta t)\end{smallmatrix}\right]

where
\Delta t
is the discretization interval and
m
is the satellite mass.
n\approx\sqrt{G/a}
is the angular speed required for a body to complete one orbit (mean motion) and is defined in terms of the universal constant of gravitation
G
and the orbit semi-major axis
a
. We assume a circular orbit around Earth, in this case,
a=(\mathrm{orbital~altitude}+\mathrm{~Earth^{\prime}s~radius})
.

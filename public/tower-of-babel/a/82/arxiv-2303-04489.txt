KEK-TH-2503 New non-supersymmetric heterotic string theory with reduced rank and exponential suppression of the cosmological constant
Sota Nakajima

Abstract
We study the heterotic asymmetric orbifold model in which supersymmetry is broken by the stringy Schark-Schwarz mechanism. This model is a natural non-supersymmetric extension of CHL strings and can also be interpreted as the interpolating model between the E 8 × E 8 ′ E_{8}\times E^{\prime}_{8} theory and the non-supersymmetric E 8 E_{8} theory. The enhancement of gauge groups, of which the rank is reduced to 8 + d 8+d , is explored. In particular, the enhancement to non-simply-laced groups is possible with d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , as well as in the CHL model. We also give the conditions that the massless matter spectrum must satisfy. Moreover, the one-loop cosmological constant is evaluated in the regime where supersymmetry is asymptotically restored, and we show that the exponential suppression can occur unless d = 1 d=1 .

3
Non-supersymmetric CHL strings

In the following sections, we construct the asymmetric orbifold with
\bm{\mathrm{R}}\bm{\mathrm{T}}(-1)^{F}
and explore the non-supersymmetric CHL model. A point that we have to note is that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\bm{\mathrm{R}}\bm{\mathrm{T}}
as well in the CHL model, while fermionic states take the combinations such that the eigenvalue under
\bm{\mathrm{R}}\bm{\mathrm{T}}
is
-1

The twisted sector in this case turns out to contain the scalar and conjugate spinor (co-spinor) conjugacy class of the spacetime
SO(8)
(see appendix
A
).

We provide the partition function which is not only useful to explore the spectrum but also necessary to evaluate the cosmological constant in section
4
. Then we study the massless spectrum of the non-supersymmetric CHL string. The examples for
d=1,2
are given, and we will explicitly see that the gauge symmetries are enhanced to non-ADE algebras in the case
d=2
.

3.1

The partition function of the non-supersymmetric CHL string is expressed in terms of the building blocks

Performing the transformation

\displaystyle\left(\bar{O}_{8}-\bar{C}_{8}\right)Z(1,g)\xrightarrow{\tau\to\tau+1}-\left(\bar{O}_{8}+\bar{C}_{8}\right)Z(g,g).

3.2
Spectrum

The mass formula is given in the same form as (
2.35

3.2.1
Untwisted sector

A surviving state in the untwisted sector takes the form

\displaystyle\ket{\varphi}_{\text{untw}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\alpha\ket{w,n,\pi,\pi^{\prime}}+(-1)^{n_{1}+F}g(\alpha)\ket{w,n,\pi^{\prime},\pi}\right).

The bosonic spectrum is the same as in the untwisted sector of the CHL string, while the fermionic one is made of the combination of states with the opposite sign under the projection.

Let us first consider the case

In the vector conjugacy class with

In the spinor conjugacy class with

and

are projected out, while there are eight massless spinors which come from

With

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{w,n,\pi,\pi^{\prime}}+(-)^{n_{1}+F}\ket{w,n,\pi^{\prime},\pi}\right),

where

is a solution of (
2.37
) and (
2.43

is always the same since

, i.e, if one find a solution of (
2.37
) and (
2.43

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{w,n,\pi,\pi^{\prime}}+(-1)^{n_{1}}\ket{w,n,\pi^{\prime},\pi}\right),~~~\text{for bosonic states},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{w,n,\pi,\pi^{\prime}}-(-1)^{n_{1}}\ket{w,n,\pi^{\prime},\pi}\right),~~~\text{for fermionic states}.

(3.14)

On the other hand, for

even give gauge bosons with long roots, while those with

To summarize, the procedure to identify the massless spectrum in the untwisted sector is as follows:

(1)

There are a gravitational multiplet, gauge bosons of
U(1)_{r}^{d}\times U(1)_{l}^{d}\times U(1)_{L}^{8}
and eight neutral spinors in the massless spectrum at any point in the moduli space.

(2)

For a given moduli
\left(E,a\right)
, find a set of solutions
\left(w,n,\pi_{+}\right)
for (
2.37
) and (
2.43
).

(3)

For
P_{L}^{2}=1

3.13
).

(4)

For
P_{L}^{2}=2
, the solution is a long root (weight) element in the vector (spinor) conjugacy class if
n_{1}
is even (odd).

As well as in the CHL model, if
d=1
, there is no solution of

with

even and thus the symmetry enhancement to non-ADE algebras can not be realized. On the other hand, we can obtain solutions of

5
Summary and discussion

In this paper, we have studied the reduced rank non-supersymmetric heterotic string model constructed by the asymmetric orbifold with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-twist
\bm{\mathrm{R}}\bm{\mathrm{T}}(-1)^{F}
. We first reviewed the CHL string in which all sixteen supercharges are preserved and the rank of gauge groups is reduced to
8+d
. We then considered a natural extension of the CHL model to a non-supersymmetric one by combining the orbifold twist with a
2\pi
spatial rotation. The conditions on the internal momenta of massless states have been figured out.
We gave concrete examples of the symmetry enhancements and the matter spectra in the cases
d=1,2
. In
d=2
, in particular, the enhanced groups are non-simply-laced and the fermions (and scalars) that non-trivially transform under the gauge group are found. We also evaluated the cosmological constant in the regime with
R
large and argued that its exponential suppression is possible if
d\geq 2
. It turned out that in order to realize the suppression, the existence of massless fermions with long weight elements of gauge groups is crucial.

Although the conditions that internal momenta of massless states should satisfy are given in this paper, it is not shown how to solve them in an efficient way. In refs.
[
57
,
51
]
, the algorithms to systematically explore the maximal enhancement of gauge groups have been proposed in heterotic strings with sixteen supercharges. By using the relations (
3.26
) and (
3.27
), it is expected that the methods in refs.
[
57
,
51
]
can be applied to the non-supersymmetric case, and patterns of the maximal enhancements and the corresponding representations of co-spinors can be figured out. However, it is unclear whether it can be also done in the spinor and scalar conjugacy classes because the orbifold projection in these classes is opposite to the CHL strings and the momentum lattices do not have simple relevance, such as (
3.26
) and (
3.27
).

5.1
Moduli stabilization

In this paper, we did not take the moduli stabilization into consideration. In general, some moduli in non-supersymmetric string theory are frozen because they acquire potentials at the quantum level. For instance, we have already obtained the one-loop effective potential of the Wilson line moduli by eq.(
4.18
), and we can perform the stability analysis of the Wilson lines.
Let us again assume that the Wilson line
a_{1}
satisfy
\rho\cdot a_{1}=n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all solutions of
p^{\prime}_{R}=0
and
P^{\prime 2}_{R}=2
. Note that such points are critical points for the
a_{1}
-directions:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Lambda^{(10-d)}}{\partial a_{1}^{I}}\sim\sum_{\rho}\rho^{I}\sin\left[\pi(2n-1)\rho\cdot a_{1}\right]=0.

(5.1)

Let
\Delta_{+}
and
\Delta_{-}
be sets of
\rho
satisfying
p^{\prime}_{R}=0
and
P^{\prime 2}_{R}=2
with
\rho\cdot a_{1}
even and odd respectively:

\displaystyle\Delta_{+}=\left\{\rho\left|~p^{\prime}_{R}=0,~P^{\prime 2}_{R}=2,~\rho\cdot a_{1}\in 2\mathbb{Z}\right.\right\},~~~~\Delta_{-}=\left\{\rho\left|~p^{\prime}_{R}=0,~P^{\prime 2}_{R}=2,~\rho\cdot a_{1}\in 2\mathbb{Z}+1\right.\right\}.

(5.2)

In order for the cosmological constant (
4.18
) to vanish, the difference between the numbers of elements in
\Delta_{+}
and
\Delta_{-}
must be
-8
:

\displaystyle|\Delta_{+}|-|\Delta_{-}|=-8.

(5.3)

The Hessian
H_{IJ}
for

is

\displaystyle H_{IJ}:=\frac{\partial^{2}\Lambda^{(10-d)}}{\partial a_{1}^{I}\partial a_{1}^{J}}\sim\sum_{\rho}\rho^{I}\rho^{J}\cos\left[\pi(2n-1)\rho\cdot a_{1}\right]=\sum_{\rho}\rho^{I}\rho^{J}(-1)^{\rho\cdot a_{1}}.

(5.4)

The necessary condition for the critical point to be a minimum requires that all diagonal components of the Hessian be positive, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho\in\Delta_{+}}\left(\rho^{I}\right)^{2}-\sum_{\rho\in\Delta_{-}}\left(\rho^{I}\right)^{2}>0~~~\text{for all $I=1,\cdots,8$}.

(5.5)

The question is whether a point
(E^{\prime},a^{\prime},a_{1})
satisfying both eq.(
5.3
) and eq.(
5.5
) exists or not. Actually, one can check that the examples given in the previous section that satisfy eq.(
5.3
) do not satisfy eq.(
5.5

d=2

and

\displaystyle H_{11}>0,~~~H_{II}<0~~\text{for $I\neq 1$},

(5.6)

Also, in this paper, we just assume that the radion

It is an open question whether (meta)stable de Sitter vacua exist or not. In order to realize that, some extra ingredients (e.g. fluxes) are expected to be needed such as in refs.
[
58
,
59
,
60
]
.

E_{8}
model is expressed as the sum of the building blocks:

\displaystyle Z_{\mathcal{N}=0}^{(10)}=\frac{1}{2}Z_{B}^{(8)}\sum_{a,b=0,1}Z^{(10)}(g^{a},g^{b}),

(B.2)

where

\displaystyle Z^{(10)}(1,1)

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{V}_{8}-\bar{S}_{8}\right)\frac{1}{\eta^{16}}\sum_{\pi\in\Gamma_{E_{8}}}\sum_{\pi^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{E^{\prime}_{8}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\pi^{2}+\pi^{\prime 2}\right)},

(B.3)

\displaystyle Z^{(10)}(1,g)

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{V}_{8}+\bar{S}_{8}\right)\frac{1}{\eta^{16}}\left(\frac{2\eta^{3}}{\vartheta_{2}}\right)^{4}\sum_{\pi\in\sqrt{2}\Gamma_{E_{8}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}},

(B.4)

\displaystyle Z^{(10)}(g,1)

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{O}_{8}-\bar{C}_{8}\right)\frac{1}{\eta^{16}}\left(\frac{\eta^{3}}{\vartheta_{4}}\right)^{4}\sum_{\pi\in\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Gamma_{E_{8}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}},

(B.5)

\displaystyle Z^{(10)}(g,g)

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{O}_{8}+\bar{C}_{8}\right)\frac{1}{\eta^{16}}\left(\frac{\eta^{3}}{\vartheta_{3}}\right)^{4}\sum_{\pi\in\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Gamma_{E_{8}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}}e^{\pi i\pi^{2}}.

(B.6)

The total partition function is then written as

\displaystyle Z_{\mathcal{N}=0}^{(10)}=\frac{1}{2}Z_{B}^{(8)}\frac{1}{\eta^{16}}

\displaystyle\left\{\bar{V}_{8}\left(\sum_{\pi\in\Gamma_{E_{8}}}\sum_{\pi^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{E^{\prime}_{8}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\pi^{2}+\pi^{\prime 2}\right)}+\left(\frac{2\eta^{3}}{\vartheta_{2}}\right)^{4}\sum_{\pi\in\sqrt{2}\Gamma_{E_{8}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\bar{S}_{8}\left(\sum_{\pi\in\Gamma_{E_{8}}}\sum_{\pi^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{E^{\prime}_{8}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\pi^{2}+\pi^{\prime 2}\right)}-\left(\frac{2\eta^{3}}{\vartheta_{2}}\right)^{4}\sum_{\pi\in\sqrt{2}\Gamma_{E_{8}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\bar{O}_{8}\sum_{\pi\in\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Gamma_{E_{8}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}}\left(\left(\frac{\eta^{3}}{\vartheta_{4}}\right)^{4}+\left(\frac{\eta^{3}}{\vartheta_{3}}\right)^{4}e^{\pi i\pi^{2}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\bar{C}_{8}\sum_{\pi\in\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Gamma_{E_{8}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}}\left(\left(\frac{\eta^{3}}{\vartheta_{4}}\right)^{4}-\left(\frac{\eta^{3}}{\vartheta_{3}}\right)^{4}e^{\pi i\pi^{2}}\right)\right\}.

(B.7)

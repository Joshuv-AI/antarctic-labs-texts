Precise determination of the pole position of the exotic Z c ​ ( 3900 ) Z_{c}(3900)
Yun-Hua Chen, Meng-Lin Du, Feng-Kun Guo

Abstract
Abstract: We perform a unified description of the experimental data of the π + ​ π − \pi^{+}\pi^{-} and J / ψ ​ π ± J/\psi\pi^{\pm} invariant mass spectra for e + ​ e − → J / ψ ​ π + ​ π − e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow J/\psi\pi^{+}\pi^{-} and the D 0 ​ D ∗ ⁣ − D^{0}D^{\ast-} mass spectrum for e + ​ e − → D 0 ​ D ∗ ⁣ − ​ π + e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow D^{0}D^{\ast-}\pi^{+} at e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} center-of-mass energies 4.23 and 4.26 GeV. The analysis takes into account open-charm meson loops that contain triangle singularities, the J / ψ ​ π J/\psi\pi - D ​ D ¯ ∗ D\bar{D}^{*} coupled-channel interaction respecting unitarity, and the strong π ​ π \pi\pi - K ​ K ¯ K\bar{K} final state interaction using dispersion relations. The analysis leads to a precise determination of the Z c ​ ( 3900 ) Z_{c}(3900) pole with the pole mass and width ( 3880.7 ± 1.7 stat ± 22.4 syst ) (3880.7\pm 1.7_{\text{stat}}\pm 22.4_{\text{syst}}) MeV and ( 35.9 ± 1.4 stat ± 15.3 syst ) (35.9\pm 1.4_{\text{stat}}\pm 15.3_{\text{syst}}) MeV, respectively, and hints at that the D ​ D ¯ ∗ D\bar{D}^{*} molecular and non-molecular components are of similar importance in the Z c ​ ( 3900 ) Z_{c}(3900) formation. Keywords: exotic hadrons, dispersion relations, final state interaction, triangle singularity PACS codes: 13.66.Bc, 36.10.-k, 13.30.Eg, 11.55.Fv

4. Summary

In this Letter, we provide a simultaneous description of
the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and

J/\psi\pi^{\pm}
invariant mass distributions for
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow J/\psi\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and the
D^{0}D^{\ast-}
mass spectrum for
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow D^{0}D^{\ast-}\pi^{+}
at two energy points
E=4.23
GeV and
4.26
GeV.
The open-charm triangle diagrams that contain TS contributions near the physical region, the
J/\psi\pi
-
D\bar{D}^{*}

S
-wave interactions that are flexible enough to have a
Z_{c}
resonance pole, and the strong
\pi\pi
-
K\bar{K}
FSI in
e^{+}e^{-}\to J/\psi\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
are all taken into account, with the latter treated using a dispersive approach.
Our best fit leads to a robust and precise extraction of the
Z_{c}(3900)
pole position. The pole mass and width are
(3880.7\pm 1.7_{\text{stat}}\pm 22.4_{\text{syst}})
MeV, and
(35.9\pm 1.4_{\text{stat}}\pm 15.3_{\text{syst}})
MeV, respectively. The compositeness defined in
Matuschek et al. 2021
for
Z_{c}(3900)
implies that the
D\bar{D}^{\ast}
molecular and additional components are of similar importance in the formation of the
Z_{c}(3900)
state.
Exploration of possible origins of the short-distance component would shed light on understanding this and other exotic hadrons.

\end{array}\right),\quad\epsilon_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0,0,1\end{array}\right).

Given in the order of
(+,-,0)
, the rhc and lhc helicity amplitudes in matrix form read

\displaystyle H^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}H_{A}^{++}+H_{B}^{++}&H_{B}^{+-}&H_{B}^{+0}\\
H_{B}^{-+}&H_{A}^{--}+H_{B}^{--}&H_{B}^{-0}\\
H_{B}^{0+}&H_{B}^{0-}&H_{A}^{00}+H_{B}^{00}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\hat{H}_{A}^{++}+\hat{H}_{B}^{++}&\hat{H}_{B}^{+-}&\hat{H}_{B}^{+0}\\
\hat{H}_{B}^{-+}&\hat{H}_{A}^{--}+\hat{H}_{B}^{--}&\hat{H}_{B}^{-0}\\
\hat{H}_{B}^{0+}&\hat{H}_{B}^{0-}&\hat{H}_{A}^{00}+\hat{H}_{B}^{00}\end{array}\right),

respectively, with

\displaystyle H_{A}^{++}=

\displaystyle\,H_{A}^{--}=H_{A}^{00}=M_{A},

(39)

\displaystyle H_{B}^{++}=

\displaystyle\,H_{B}^{--}=-\frac{1}{2}H_{B}^{00}=-\frac{1}{8|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}}\left(M_{B}^{t}\left[2\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}+|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2}-|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+M_{B}^{u}\left[2\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}-|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2}+|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)\right]\right),

(40)

\displaystyle H_{B}^{+-}=

\displaystyle\,H_{B}^{-+}=-\frac{3(M_{B}^{t}+M_{B}^{u})}{8|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}}\lambda\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right),

(41)

\displaystyle H_{B}^{+0}=

\displaystyle-H_{B}^{0+}=H_{B}^{-0}=-H_{B}^{0-}=-\frac{3i}{4\sqrt{2}|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}}\sqrt{-\lambda\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)}\left(M_{B}^{t}\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}+|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2}-|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-M_{B}^{u}\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}-|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2}+|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)\right),

(42)

and

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{A}^{++}=

\displaystyle\,\hat{H}_{A}^{--}=\hat{H}_{A}^{00}=\hat{M}_{A},

(43)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{B}^{++}=

\displaystyle\,\hat{H}_{B}^{--}=-\frac{1}{2}\hat{H}_{B}^{00}=-\frac{1}{8|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}}\left(\hat{M}_{B}^{t}\left[2\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}+|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2}-|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\hat{M}_{B}^{u}\left[2\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}-|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2}+|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)\right]\right),

(44)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{B}^{+-}=

\displaystyle\,\hat{H}_{B}^{-+}=-\frac{3(\hat{M}_{B}^{t}+\hat{M}_{B}^{u})}{8|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}}\lambda\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right),

(45)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{B}^{+0}=

\displaystyle-\hat{H}_{B}^{0+}=\hat{H}_{B}^{-0}=-\hat{H}_{B}^{0-}=-\frac{3i}{4\sqrt{2}|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}}\sqrt{-\lambda\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2},|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)}\left(\hat{M}_{B}^{t}\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}+|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2}-|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\hat{M}_{B}^{u}\left(|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}-|\mathbf{p}|_{c}^{2}+|\mathbf{p}|_{d}^{2}\right)\right).

(46)

One observes that there are eight independent terms:
H_{A}^{++},H_{B}^{++},H_{B}^{+-}
,
H_{B}^{+0}
,
\hat{H}_{A}^{++},\hat{H}_{B}^{++},\hat{H}_{B}^{+-}
, and
\hat{H}_{B}^{+0}
.
In the
H^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
and
\hat{H}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
matrices given in Eq. (
I.2
), the
H_{A}
and
\hat{H}_{A}
terms only appear in the diagonal elements. Using the relations
H_{A}^{++}=H_{A}^{--}=H_{A}^{00}
,
H_{B}^{00}=-2H_{B}^{++}=-2H_{B}^{--}
,
\hat{H}_{A}^{++}=\hat{H}_{A}^{--}=\hat{H}_{A}^{00}
, and
\hat{H}_{B}^{00}=-2\hat{H}_{B}^{++}=-2\hat{H}_{B}^{--}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\left|H_{A}^{++}+H_{B}^{++}+\hat{H}_{A}^{++}+\hat{H}_{B}^{++}\right|^{2}+\left|H_{A}^{--}+H_{B}^{--}+\hat{H}_{A}^{--}+\hat{H}_{B}^{--}\right|^{2}+\left|H_{A}^{00}+H_{B}^{00}+\hat{H}_{A}^{00}+\hat{H}_{B}^{00}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2\left|H_{A}^{++}+H_{B}^{++}+\hat{H}_{A}^{++}+\hat{H}_{B}^{++}\right|^{2}+\left|H_{A}^{++}-2H_{B}^{++}+\hat{H}_{A}^{++}-2\hat{H}_{B}^{++}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,3\left|H_{A}^{++}+\hat{H}_{A}^{++}\right|^{2}+6\left|H_{B}^{++}+\hat{H}_{B}^{++}\right|^{2}\,,

(47)

where no interference between
(H_{A}+\hat{H}_{A})
and
(H_{B}+\hat{H}_{B})
survives in the squared amplitudes.

Now we briefly discuss the
(H_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+\hat{H}_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}})
and
(H_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+\hat{H}_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}})
terms which can be treated independently
in the dispersive approach. Typically, the unitary conditions read as follows

\displaystyle\textrm{Im}\,(H_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+H_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}})=T^{\ast}\sigma\left[H_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+H_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+\hat{H}_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+\hat{H}_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\right]\,,

(48)

where
T
denotes the scattering amplitude, and
\sigma
is the phase space factor.
Specifically,

\displaystyle\textrm{Im}\,(H_{A}^{++}+H_{B}^{++})

\displaystyle=T^{\ast}\sigma\left[H_{A}^{++}+H_{B}^{++}+\hat{H}_{A}^{++}+\hat{H}_{B}^{++}\right],

(49)

\displaystyle\textrm{Im}\,(H_{A}^{00}+H_{B}^{00})

\displaystyle=T^{\ast}\sigma\left[H_{A}^{00}+H_{B}^{00}+\hat{H}_{A}^{00}+\hat{H}_{B}^{00}\right].

(50)

Using the relations
H_{A}^{++}=H_{A}^{00}
,
H_{B}^{00}=-2H_{B}^{++}
,
\hat{H}_{A}^{++}=\hat{H}_{A}^{00}
, and
\hat{H}_{B}^{00}=-2\hat{H}_{B}^{++}
, Eq. (
50
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\textrm{Im}\,(H_{A}^{++}-2H_{B}^{++})=T^{\ast}\sigma\left[H_{A}^{++}-2H_{B}^{++}+\hat{H}_{A}^{++}-2\hat{H}_{B}^{++}\right].

(51)

Combining Eqs. (
49
) and (
51
), we obtain

\displaystyle\textrm{Im}\,H_{A}^{++}

\displaystyle=T^{\ast}\sigma\left[H_{A}^{++}+\hat{H}_{A}^{++}\right],

(52)

\displaystyle\textrm{Im}\,H_{B}^{++}

\displaystyle=T^{\ast}\sigma\left[H_{B}^{++}+\hat{H}_{B}^{++}\right].

(53)

Note that the
(H_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+\hat{H}_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}})
term does not interfere with the
(H_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+\hat{H}_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}})
term in the unitary conditions, and therefore they can be treated separately in the dispersive approach. This is a direct consequence of

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{pol.}}\left(\epsilon_{Y}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{*}\right)^{*}\left(3\mathbf{p}_{c/d}\cdot\epsilon_{Y}\mathbf{p}_{c/d}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{*}-\mathbf{p}_{c/d}^{2}\epsilon_{Y}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{*}\right)=0.

(54)

The partial-wave projection of the
Y\to J/\psi PP
helicity amplitudes is given as follows

\hat{H}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},l}(s)=\frac{2l+1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}\cos\theta\,d_{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime},0}^{l}(\theta)\hat{H}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(s,\theta)\,.

(55)

I.3
Subtraction terms in the dispersion relations

In order to determine the necessary number of subtractions to
guarantee the dispersive integrals in
Eqs. (
6
) and (
8
) convergent, we need to analyze the high-energy behavior of the integrands.
We have checked that in the intermediate energy region of
1\,\text{GeV}^{2}\lesssim s\lesssim 100\,\text{GeV}^{2}
, for the
\pi\pi

S
-wave, the integrand
\Omega^{-1}(x)T_{0}^{0}(x)\Sigma(x)\mathbf{\hat{H}}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},0}(s)
in Eq. (
8
)
grows at most linearly in
s
, and thus two subtractions are sufficient
to make the dispersive integrals convergent. For the
\pi\pi

D
-wave, the integrand
\hat{H}_{\pi}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},2}(s)\sin\delta_{2}^{0}(x)/|\Omega_{2}^{0}(x)|
in Eq. (
6
)
\sim s^{0}
at large
s
, and therefore one subtraction is sufficient to guarantee the convergence of the dispersive integral.
On the other hand, as mentioned above the terms with different tensor structures, namely the
(H_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+\hat{H}_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}})
term and the
(H_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+\hat{H}_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}})
term, do not interfere with each other in the unitary conditions. Therefore, they can be treated separately in the dispersive approach.

First we consider the
(H_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+\hat{H}_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}})
term which is purely proportional to
\epsilon_{Y}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{\ast}
.
Since at low energies, the amplitude should agree with the leading chiral results, the subtraction terms can be determined by matching the chiral contact terms.
As the internal structure of the initial state
Y
is not yet clear, we follow Ref.
Chen et al. 2019
to split it into a flavor SU(3) singlet part and an octet part, whose matrix form in the SU(3) flavor space may be written as
Chen et al. 2019

|Y\rangle=a|\mathbbm{V}_{1}\rangle+\sqrt{1-a^{2}}|\mathbbm{V}_{8}\rangle=\frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}V_{1}\cdot\mathbbm{1}+\frac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{6}}V_{8}\cdot\text{diag}\left(1,1,-2\right),

(56)

where the indices
1
and
8
denote the singlet and octet, respectively.
The effective Lagrangian for the contact
Y\to J/\psi\pi\pi
and

Y\to J/\psi K\bar{K}
couplings, at the lowest order in the chiral expansion
and respecting the heavy-quark spin symmetry,
reads
Chen et al. 2019
;
Mannel and Urech 1997
;
Chen et al. 2016b
;
Chen et al. 2017
;
Chen and Guo 2019

\mathscr{L}_{Y\psi\Phi\Phi}=g_{1}\langle\mathbbm{V}_{1}^{\alpha}J^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\rangle\langle u_{\mu}u^{\mu}\rangle+h_{1}\langle\mathbbm{V}_{1}^{\alpha}J^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\rangle\langle u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\rangle v^{\mu}v^{\nu}+g_{8}\langle J^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\mathbbm{V}_{8}^{\alpha}u_{\mu}u^{\mu}\rangle+h_{8}\langle J^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\mathbbm{V}_{8}^{\alpha}u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\rangle v^{\mu}v^{\nu}+\mathrm{H.c.}\,,

(57)

where
\langle\ldots\rangle
represents the trace in the SU(3) flavor space,
J=(\psi/\sqrt{3})\cdot\mathbbm{1}
, and

v^{\mu}=(1,\mathbf{0})
denotes the velocity of the heavy quark.
By making use of the Lagrangian
Eq. (
57
), one obtains the amplitudes as

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\chi,\pi\pi}(s,\theta)

\displaystyle=M^{\chi,\pi\pi}(s,\theta)\epsilon_{Y}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{\ast}=-\frac{4}{F_{0}^{2}}\sqrt{M_{Y}M_{\psi}}\bigg[\Big(g_{1}+\frac{g_{8}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)p_{c}\cdot p_{d}+\Big(h_{1}+\frac{h_{8}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)p_{c}^{0}p_{d}^{0}\bigg]\epsilon_{Y}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{\ast}\,,

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\chi,K\bar{K}}(s,\theta)

\displaystyle=M^{\chi,K\bar{K}}(s,\theta)\epsilon_{Y}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{\ast}=-\frac{4}{F_{0}^{2}}\sqrt{M_{Y}M_{\psi}}\bigg[\Big(g_{1}-\frac{g_{8}}{2\sqrt{2}}\Big)p_{c}\cdot p_{d}+\Big(h_{1}-\frac{h_{8}}{2\sqrt{2}}\Big)p_{c}^{0}p_{d}^{0}\bigg]\epsilon_{Y}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{\ast}\,.

(58)

Note that the chiral contact terms in
Eq. (
58
) is purely proportional to
\epsilon_{Y}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{\ast}
, and at low energies the amplitudes

\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},0}(s)
and
H_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},2}(s)
should match to those from chiral amplitudes.
Namely, in the chiral limit,
the subtraction terms should agree with the partial-wave projection of the low-energy chiral amplitudes
given in Eq. (
58
).
Therefore, for the
\pi\pi

S
-wave, the integral equations take the form

\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},0}(s)=\Omega(s)\bigg\{\mathbf{M}^{\chi,0}(s)+\frac{s^{3}}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{{\text{d}}x}{x^{3}}\frac{\Omega^{-1}(x)T_{0}^{0}(x)\Sigma(x)\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{A}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},0}(s)}{x-s}\bigg\}\,,

(59)

where
\mathbf{M}^{\chi}_{0}(s)=\big(M^{\chi,\pi\pi,0}(s),2/\sqrt{3}\,M^{\chi,K\bar{K},0}(s)\big)^{T}
, while
for the
D
-wave, it can be written as follows

H_{A}^{\pi,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},2}(s)=\Omega_{2}^{0}(s)\bigg\{M^{\chi,\pi\pi,2}(s)+\frac{s^{3}}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{{\text{d}}x}{x^{3}}\frac{\hat{H}_{A}^{\pi,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},2}(s)\sin\delta_{2}^{0}(x)}{|\Omega_{2}^{0}(x)|(x-s)}\bigg\}\,.

(60)

Note that in Eqs. (
59
) and (
60
), oversubtracted dispersion relations have been implemented since
M^{\chi,\pi\pi(K\bar{K}),0}(s)
and
M^{\chi,\pi\pi,2}(s)
all grow
as
s^{2}
.

Next we discuss the
\hat{H}_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
term that is produced by the triangle diagrams with
D
-wave
D_{1}D^{\ast}\pi
coupling.
Since there is no corresponding term with the same tensor structure in the chiral contact Lagrangian, which is constructed at the leading order of the heavy quark expansion, here we introduce new subtraction terms by hand. Namely, for the
\pi\pi

S
-wave, the integral equations are written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},0}(s)=\Omega(s)\bigg\{\mathbf{P}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(s)+\frac{s^{2}}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{{\text{d}}x}{x^{2}}\frac{\Omega^{-1}(x)T_{0}^{0}(x)\Sigma(x)\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},0}(s)}{x-s}\bigg\}\,,

(61)

where
\mathbf{P}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(s)=\big(j_{1}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+j_{2}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}s,2(j_{3}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+j_{4}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}s)/\sqrt{3}\big)
.
For the
\pi\pi

D
-wave, the integral equation can be written as

H_{B}^{\pi,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},2}(s)=\Omega_{2}^{0}(s)\bigg\{j_{5}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}+\frac{s}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{{\text{d}}x}{x}\frac{\hat{H}_{B}^{\pi,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},2}(s)\sin\delta_{2}^{0}(x)}{|\Omega_{2}^{0}(x)|(x-s)}\bigg\}\,.

(62)

As stated above, there are three independent
\hat{H}_{B}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
terms:
\hat{H}_{B}^{++},\hat{H}_{B}^{+-}
, and
\hat{H}_{B}^{+0}
. To reduce the number of free parameters in the fits, here we only take into account the subtraction term corresponding to
\hat{H}_{B}^{++}
, but set the subtraction terms corresponding to
\hat{H}_{B}^{+-}
and
\hat{H}_{B}^{+0}
to be zero. Namely we choose
j_{i}=j_{i}^{++}(i=1,...,5)
.
In the actual fits, we find that strong correlations exist between
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
,
j_{3}
and
j_{4}
, respectively. Therefore we can set
j_{2}=j_{4}=0
in the fits, and we have checked that this setting has negligible effect on the value of
\chi^{2}
of the obtained best fits.

On the L ∞ structure of Poisson gauge theory
O. Abla, V. G. Kupriyanov, M. Kurkov

Abstract
The Poisson gauge theory is a semi-classical limit of full non-commutative gauge theory. In this work we construct an L f ​ u ​ l ​ l ∞ {}_{\infty}^{full} algebra which governs both the action of gauge symmetries and the dynamics of the Poisson gauge theory. We derive the minimal set of non-vanishing ℓ \ell -brackets and prove that they satisfy the corresponding homotopy relations. On the one hand, it provides new explicit non-trivial examples of L ∞ algebras. On the other hand, it can be used as a starting point for bootstrapping the full non-commutative gauge theory. The first few brackets of such a theory are constructed explicitly in the text. In addition we show that the derivation properties of ℓ \ell -brackets on L f ​ u ​ l ​ l ∞ {}_{\infty}^{full} with respect to the truncated product on the exterior algebra are satisfied only for the canonical non-commutativity. In general, L f ​ u ​ l ​ l ∞ {}_{\infty}^{full} does not have a structure of P ∞ algebra.

2
Poisson gauge theory

The aim of this section is to review the construction of the field theoretical model which has a Poisson gauge algebra (
8
) as a corresponding algebra of gauge symmetries. We start with the definition of the Poisson gauge transformations
\delta_{f}A
which should satisfy the following two conditions: the closure of the algebra (
8
)
and the correct commutative limit to the standard
U(1)
gauge transformations,
\lim_{\Theta\to 0}\delta_{f}A=\partial f
.
If
\Theta^{ij}
is constant, one may easily see that the straightforward expression,
\delta^{can}_{f}A=\partial f+\{A,f\}
, satisfies (
8
). However, for non-constant
\Theta^{ij}(x)
the standard Leibniz rule with respect to the partial derivative is violated,
\partial\{f,g\}\neq\{\partial f,g\}+\{f,\partial g\}
, therefore the same expression will not close the algebra (
8
) anymore. To overcome this problem one has to modify the expression for the gauge transformations introducing the corrections proportional to the derivatives of the non-commutativity
\partial\Theta^{ij}(x)
which would compensate the violation of the Leibniz rule.

The problem with the violation of the Leibniz rule can be solved in an extended space
[
11
]
. To each coordinate
x^{i}
we introduce a conjugate variable
p_{i}
, in such a way that the corresponding Poisson brackets,

\{x^{i},x^{j}\}=\Theta^{ij}(x)\,,\qquad\{x^{i},p_{j}\}=\gamma^{i}_{j}(x,p)\,,\qquad\{p_{i},p_{j}\}=0\,,

(9)

should satisfy the Jacobi identity. For constant
\Theta^{ij}
one finds,
\gamma^{i}_{j}(x,p)=\delta^{i}_{j}
, so the Poisson bracket,
\{f(x),p_{i}\}=\partial_{i}f(x)
, is just a partial derivative of this function. In case if
\Theta^{ij}(x)
is not constant the expression for
\gamma^{i}_{j}(x,p)
is more complicated. The Jacobi identity for the algebra (
9
) implies the partial differential equation on the matrix
\gamma^{i}_{j}(x,p)
,

\gamma^{l}_{b}\,\partial^{b}_{p}\gamma_{a}^{k}-\gamma^{k}_{b}\,\partial^{b}_{p}\gamma^{l}_{a}+\Theta^{lm}\,\partial_{m}\gamma_{a}^{k}-\Theta^{km}\,\partial_{m}\gamma_{a}^{l}-\gamma^{m}_{a}\,\partial_{m}\Theta^{lk}=0\,,

(10)

with
\partial_{m}=\partial/\partial x^{m}
and
\partial^{b}_{p}=\partial/\partial p_{b}
. Perturbative solution is given by,

\displaystyle\gamma^{k}_{a}(x,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{a}^{k(n)}

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{k}_{a}-\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{a}\Theta^{kb}p_{b}-\frac{1}{12}\left(2\,\Theta^{cm}\partial_{a}\partial_{m}\Theta^{bk}+\partial_{a}\Theta^{bm}\partial_{m}\Theta^{kc}\right)p_{b}p_{c}+{\cal O}(\Theta^{3})\,.

The recursive formula for the construction of the functions,
\gamma_{a}^{k(n)}=\gamma_{a}^{k|j_{1}\dots j_{n}}(x)\,p_{j_{1}}\dots p_{j_{n}}
, can be found in
[
13
]
.

Instead of working with standard partial derivatives
\partial_{i}
in the definition of the gauge transformation one may introduce the ‘twisted’ derivative of function
f(x)
defined in terms of Poisson bracket with
p

\partial^{\tau}_{i}f(x):=\{f(x),p_{i}\}=\gamma^{l}_{i}(x,p)\partial_{l}f(x)

\partial^{\tau}_{a}\{f,g\}=\{\partial^{\tau}_{a}f,g\}+\{f,\partial^{\tau}_{a}g\}

The price to pay however is that the expression

depends also on the auxiliary non-physical

\delta_{f}A_{a}=\gamma^{l}_{a}(A)\,\partial_{l}f(x)+\{A_{a}(x),f(x)\}\,,

where,
\gamma^{l}_{a}(A):=\gamma^{l}_{a}(x,p)|_{p_{a}=A_{a}(x)}

To be consistent with the semi-classical limit the gauge variation of the matter field

is defined as,
\delta_{f}\psi=\{\psi,f\}
. The gauge covariant derivative of matter field is the object which should satisfy the following two conditions: it should transform covariantly under the gauge transformation,

\displaystyle\delta_{f}{\cal D}_{a}(\psi)=\{{\cal D}_{a}(\psi),f\}\,,

(13)

and reproduce the standard derivative in the commutative limit,
\lim_{\Theta\to 0}{\cal D}_{a}(\psi)=\partial_{a}\psi\,.
Such an object can be constructed following

{\cal D}_{a}(\psi)=\rho_{a}^{i}(A)\left(\gamma_{i}^{l}(A)\,\partial_{l}\psi+\{A_{a},\psi\}\right)\,,

where the matrix,

\displaystyle\gamma^{j}_{b}\,\partial^{b}_{p}\,\rho_{a}^{i}+\rho_{a}^{b}\,\partial^{i}_{p}\gamma^{j}_{b}+\Theta^{jb}\,\partial_{b}\rho_{a}^{i}=0\,.

(15)

Perturbative solution of this equation reads,

\displaystyle\rho^{i}_{a}(x,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{a}^{i(n)}

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{i}_{a}-\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{a}\Theta^{ib}p_{b}+\frac{1}{6}\left(2\,\Theta^{cm}\partial_{a}\partial_{m}\Theta^{ib}-\partial_{a}\Theta^{bm}\partial_{m}\Theta^{ic}\right)p_{b}p_{c}+{\cal O}(\Theta^{3})\,.

For constant
\Theta^{ij}

\displaystyle\left[{\cal D}_{a},{\cal D}_{b}\right]=\{{\cal F}_{ab},\,\cdot\,\}+\left({\cal F}_{ad}\,{\Lambda}_{b}{}^{de}-{\cal F}_{bd}\,{\Lambda}_{a}{}^{de}\right){\cal D}_{e}\,,

3.1
Basic definitions and identities

The definition of the L
∞
algebra can be resumed as follows. An L
∞
-algebra is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\,V_{k}
together with a sequence of graded antisymmetric multilinear maps,

\displaystyle\ell_{n}:V^{\otimes n}\to V\ ,\quad v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{n}\longmapsto\ell_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\,,

called
\ell
-brackets. The property of graded antisymmetry menas

\ell_{n}(\dots,v,v^{\prime},\dots)=-(-1)^{|v|\,|v^{\prime}|}\,\ell_{n}(\dots,v^{\prime},v,\dots)\ ,

(29)

where we denote the degree of a homogeneous element
v\in V
by
|v|
.
The
\ell
-bracket is a map of degree
|\ell_{n}|=2-n
, implying that

\big|\ell_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\big|=2-n+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\,|v_{j}|\ .

The brackets
\ell_{n}
should satisfy an (infinite) tower of
identities
{\cal J}_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})=0
for each
n\geq 1
, called homotopy relations, with

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=1}\,(-1)^{i\,(n-i)}\ \sum_{\sigma\in{\rm Sh}_{i,n-i}}\,\chi(\sigma;v_{1},\dots,v_{n})

(30)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\ell_{n+1-i}\big(\ell_{i}(v_{\sigma(1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(i)}),v_{\sigma(i+1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(n)}\big)\ ,

where, for each
i=1,\dots,n
, the second sum runs over
(i,n-i)
-shuffled permutations

\sigma\in S_{n}
of degree
n
which are restricted as

\sigma(1)<\dots<\sigma(i)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sigma(i+1)<\dots<\sigma(n)\ .

The Koszul sign
\chi(\sigma;v_{1},\dots,v_{n})=\pm\,1
is determined from the grading by

\displaystyle v_{\sigma(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{\sigma(n)}=\chi(\sigma;v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\ v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}\ .

In particular, the first relation
{\cal J}_{1}(v):=\ell_{1}(\ell_{1}(v))=0
signifies that
\ell_{1}
is a
differential making
(V,\ell_{1})
into a cochain complex, while the second identity,

{\cal J}_{2}(v_{1},v_{2}):=\ell_{1}\big(\ell_{2}(v_{1},v_{2})\big)-\ell_{2}\big(\ell_{1}(v_{1}),v_{2}\big)-(-1)^{|v_{1}|}\,\ell_{2}\big(v_{1},\ell_{1}(v_{2})\big)\,,

implies that
\ell_{1}
is a (graded)
derivation of the bracket
\ell_{2}
, i.e.
\ell_{2}
is a cochain
map. The identity with three entries schematically written as
{\cal J}_{3}:=\ell_{1}\ell_{3}+\ell_{2}\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}\ell_{1}=0
means that the bracket
\ell_{2}
obeys the Jacobi identity
up to exact in
\ell_{1}
terms, i.e.
\ell_{2}
induces a (graded) Lie bracket on the
cohomology of
\ell_{1}
.

In this work we are dealing with the structure of a

∞
-algebra describing
the cochain complex,

\displaystyle V_{0}\xrightarrow{\ \ \ell_{1}\ \ }V_{1}\xrightarrow{\ \ \ell_{1}\ \ }V_{2}

(31)

corresponding to the underlying graded vector space

V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\ .

Physically,

corresponds to the space of gauge parameters or the zero-forms

contains the gauge fields represented by the

Comments on P
∞

The Poisson infinity or P
∞
structures appeared in the context of quantization of co-isotropic sub-manifolds of Poisson manifolds
[
23
,
24
]
. In fact P
∞
algebra is an L
∞
algebra
(\ell_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
on
V
in which, in addition, it is introduced an operation of the associative multiplication between the elements. To each pare of elements,
\forall u,v\in V
, it is assigned the third element
u\otimes v\to u\cdot v\in V
in such a way that
(u\cdot v)\cdot w=u\cdot(v\cdot w)
. And the corresponding
\ell
-brackets should satisfy the Leibniz rule with respect to this product,

\ell_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},u\cdot v)=\ell_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},u)\cdot v+u\cdot\ell_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},v)\,.

(112)

Following
[
11
, Proposition 5.23]
we define the multiplication by truncating the
product on the exterior algebra at degree
2
,
that is,

\displaystyle f\cdot g

\displaystyle=f\,g\ ,\quad f\cdot A=f\,A\,,\qquad f\cdot E=f\,E\,,

(113)

\displaystyle A\cdot B

\displaystyle=A\wedge B\,,\qquad A\cdot E=0\,,\qquad E\cdot F=0\,.

for all zero-forms
f
and
g
, one-forms
A
and
B
and two-forms
E
and
F
. We remind that the spaces of higher forms are empty by construction.

The same
[
11
, Proposition 5.23]
shows that the subalgebra of L
{}_{\infty}^{full}
called L
{}_{\infty}^{gauge}
which is concentrated in the degrees
0
and
1
and describes the action of the gauge symmetries on the gauge fields defines the P
∞
algebra, i.e., the derivation properties of the corresponding brackets are satisfied,

\displaystyle\ell_{2}(f,g\cdot h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell_{2}(f,g)\cdot h+g\cdot\ell_{2}(f,h)\,,

(114)

\displaystyle\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n-1},f\cdot g\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n-1},f\right)\cdot g+f\cdot\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n-1},g\right)\,,

\displaystyle\ell_{n}\left(f,A^{\otimes n-2},g\cdot A\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell_{n}\left(f,A^{\otimes n-2},g\right)\cdot A+g\cdot\ell_{n}\left(f,A^{\otimes n-1}\right)\,.

To check whether L
{}_{\infty}^{full}
algebra can be made into the P
∞
structure we have to check the derivation properties (
112
) on the
\ell
-brackets which belongs to the degree two, i.e.,
\ell_{2}(f,E)
and
\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n}\right)
. The check involving
\ell_{2}(f,E)
is straightforward. The Leibniz rule for the bracket
\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n}\right)
reads,

\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n-1},f\cdot A\right)=f\cdot\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n}\right)+\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n-1},f\right)\cdot A\,,\qquad n>1\,.

(115)

Using the explicit form of the bracket
\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n}\right)
given by (
58
) the left hand side of (
115
) can be represented as,

\displaystyle\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n-1},f\cdot A\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\cdot\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n}\right)+

(116)

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{2}\,(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\left(P_{ab}{}^{cd(n-1)}\,A_{d}\,\partial_{c}f+R_{ab}{}^{cd(n-2)}\,A_{d}\left\{A_{c},f\right\}\right){\mathsf{d}}x^{a}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}x^{b}\,.

According to the same formulas (
58
) and (

\displaystyle\ell_{2}(A,f)\cdot A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\gamma^{c(1)}_{a}\,\partial_{c}f+\{A_{a},f\}\right)A_{b}\,{\mathsf{d}}x^{a}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}x^{b}\,

(117)

\displaystyle\ell_{n}\left(A^{\otimes n-1},f\right)\cdot A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)!\,(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\,\gamma_{a}^{c(n-1)}\partial_{c}f\,A_{b}\,{\mathsf{d}}x^{a}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}x^{b}\,,\qquad n>2\,.

In order (

6

Towards full non-commutative gauge theory

As it has been already mentioned in the introduction the L
∞
-bootstrap approach is a powerful tool for the construction of order by order in
\Theta
and
\hbar
corrections of given undeformed gauge theory. Suppose we have a full non-commutative gauge theory with a corresponding L
∞
algebra defined by the brackets
\ell^{NC}_{n}
satisfying the relations (
30
) schematically denoted by,

{\cal J}_{n}:=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\,(-1)^{i\,(n-i)}\ell^{NC}_{n-i+1}\ell^{NC}_{i}=0\,.

(119)

According to the prescription of the L
∞
bootstrap (
1
) and (
2
) the initial setup reads,

\ell^{NC}_{1}(f)={\mathsf{d}}f\,,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\ell^{NC}_{2}(f,g)=i[f,g]_{\star}\,.

(120)

Let us set,
\ell_{1}(f)=\ell^{NC}_{1}(f)
, and

\ell_{n}=\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\,\frac{1}{\hbar^{n-1}}\,\ell^{NC}_{n}\,,\qquad n>1\,.

(121)

In particular,

\ell_{2}(f,g)=\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\,\frac{1}{\hbar}\,[f,g]_{\star}=-\{f,g\}\,.

(122)

If the brackets
\ell^{NC}_{n}
satisfy the L
∞
relations (
119
), then the brackets
\ell_{n}
also do, and the brackets
\ell_{1}(f)
and
\ell_{2}(f,g)
are exactly the starting point for the L
∞
algebra of Poisson gauge theory. In this sense we may call
\{\ell_{n}\}
as a quasi-classical limit of
\{\ell^{NC}_{n}\}
and the Poisson gauge theory is a quasi-classical limit of the full non-commutative gauge theory as it was mentioned in the introduction.
The brackets
\ell_{n}
given by the Proposition
57
provide the leading in
\hbar
order of the brackets
\ell_{n}^{NC}
,

\ell^{NC}_{n}=\hbar^{n-1}\ell_{n}+{\cal O}(\hbar^{n})\,.

(123)

The question is how to construct the higher order in
\hbar
contributions to the brackets
\ell^{NC}_{n}
. The aim of this section is to outline the solution to this problem within the L
∞
-bootstrap approach.

For the future convenience we start introducing the condensed indices,

(i)^{p}=(i_{1}\dots i_{p})\,,\qquad\left(\partial_{i}\right)^{p}=\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{p}}\,,

(124)

where the round bracket means symmetrization. In particular,

K^{(i)^{p}j}\left(\partial_{i}\right)^{p}A_{j}=K^{i_{1}\dots i_{p}j}\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{p}}A_{j}\,.

The non-commutative deformation of the space-time is introduced through the given Hermitean,
(f\star g)^{\ast}=g^{\ast}\star f^{\ast}
, unital,
f\star 1=1\star f=f
, star product defined by the expression,

f\star g=f\,g+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{i\hbar}{2}\right)^{n}B_{n}(f,g)\,,\qquad B_{1}=\{f,g\}\,.

(125)

The bi-differential operators

B_{n}(f,g)=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}B_{n}^{(i)^{k}|(j)^{l}}(\partial_{i})^{k}f\,(\partial_{j})^{l}g\,,

(126)

are determined to satisfy the associativity condition,
f\star(g\star h)=(f\star g)\star h
. The coefficient functions
B_{n}^{(i)^{k}|(j)^{l}}
can be constructed following the prescription of the Formality Theorem
[
25
]
, or also using the poly-differential approach proposed in
[
26
]
. The Hermiticity of the star product implies that,
B_{2n}(f,g)=B_{2n}(g,f)
, and
B_{2n+1}(f,g)=-B_{2n+1}(g,f)
.
Thus the expression for the bracket
\ell_{2}^{NC}(f,g)
becomes,

\ell^{NC}_{2}(f,g)=2i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{i\hbar}{2}\right)^{2n+1}B_{2n+1}(f,g)=-\hbar\,\{f,g\}+{\cal O}(\hbar^{3})\,.

(127)

To determine the bracket
\ell_{2}^{NC}(f,A)
one has to solve the homotopy relation,
{\cal J}_{2}(f,g)=0
, written as,

\ell^{NC}_{2}(\ell^{NC}_{1}(f),g)+\ell_{2}^{NC}(f,\ell^{NC}_{1}(g))=\ell^{NC}_{1}(\ell_{2}^{NC}(f,g))\,.

(128)

Remind that the element
\ell^{NC}_{1}(f)
belongs to the space of the gauge fields, that is, we have a combination of yet undetermined brackets of the form
\ell_{2}(f,A)
on the left expressed in terms of the combination of given brackets on the right. One may check that setting,

\displaystyle\ell_{2}^{NC}(f,A)=\left\{i[f,A_{a}]_{\star}+\sum_{k,l=1}^{\infty}\tilde{B}_{a}^{(i)^{k}|(j)^{l}}(\partial_{i})^{k}f\,(\partial_{j})^{l-1}A_{j_{l}}\right\}{\mathsf{d}}x_{a}\,,

(129)

where,

\tilde{B}_{a}^{(i)^{k}|(j)^{l}}=i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{i\hbar}{2}\right)^{2n+1}\partial_{a}B_{2n+1}^{(i)^{k}|(j)^{l}}\,,

(130)

and defining according to the graded symmetry,
\ell_{2}^{NC}(A,f):=-\ell_{2}^{NC}(f,A)

128

The non-trivial technical issue appears on the following steps of the bootstrap procedure. Let us discuss the definition of the bracket
\ell_{2}^{NC}(A,B)
entering the decomposition of the non-commutative field strength (
6

{\cal J}(f,A)=0
, written in the form,

\displaystyle\ell^{NC}_{2}(\ell^{NC}_{1}(f),A)+\ell^{NC}_{2}(f,\ell^{NC}_{1}(A))=\ell^{NC}_{1}(\ell^{NC}_{2}(f,A))\,.

(131)

The bracket,
\ell_{2}^{NC}(f,A)
on the right is given by (
129

\ell_{2}^{NC}(A,B)
and
\ell^{NC}_{2}(f,\ell^{NC}_{1}(A))
reminding that the element
\ell^{NC}_{1}(A)
belongs to the space space of gauge covariant objects
V_{2}
. Following the reasoning of the Proposition
57
for the element
{\cal F}\in V_{2}
we just set,

\displaystyle\ell^{NC}_{2}(f,{\cal F})=i[f,{\cal F}]_{\star}\,.

(132)

In this case, the associativity of the star product implies the Jacobi identity for the bracket (

{\cal J}_{3}(f,g,{\cal F})=0
, will be automatically satisfied and there is no need for the higher brackets
\ell_{n+2}^{NC}(f,{\cal F},A^{n})
. The gauge covariance condition thus becomes,
\delta_{f}^{NC}{\cal F}=i[f,{\cal F}]_{\star}
.
Now we rewrite the eq. (

\displaystyle\ell^{NC}_{2}(\ell^{NC}_{1}(f),A)={\cal J}_{2}^{R}(f,A)\,,

(133)

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{2}^{R}(f,A):=\ell^{NC}_{1}\left(\ell_{2}^{NC}(f,A)\right)-i[f,\ell^{NC}_{1}(A)]_{\star}\,.

(134)

Again, yet undetermined bracket on the left is expressed as a combination of the previously determined brackets on the right. One may schematically represent the r.h.s. as,

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{2}^{R}(f,A)=\sum_{p,q=1}^{\infty}Q_{ab}{}^{(i)^{p}|(j)^{q-1}j_{q}}(\partial_{i})^{p}f\,(\partial_{j})^{q-1}A_{j_{q}}\,{\mathsf{d}}x^{a}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}x^{b}

where the coefficient functions

are constructed from the Poisson bi-vector

and its derivatives according to the prescription (

\ell_{2}^{NC}(A,B)
can be also schematically represented as a sum,

The graded symmetry of the bracket,
\ell_{2}^{NC}(A,B)=\ell_{2}^{NC}(B,A)
, implies the symmetry relation of the corresponding coefficients,

R_{ab}{}^{(i)^{p-1}i_{p}|(j)^{q-1}j_{q}}=R_{ab}{}^{(j)^{q-1}j_{q}|(i)^{p-1}i_{p}}\,.

To sum up, the coefficient functions

are given while

should be determined from the equation (

The bracket (

[
10
]
. First we observe that the graded symmetry of the bracket,

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{2}^{R}(f,\ell^{NC}_{1}(g))-{\cal J}_{2}^{R}(g,\ell^{NC}_{1}(f))=\,0\,.

The later however is automatically satisfied due to the previously solved homotopy relation,

\displaystyle\ell^{NC}_{1}\left(\ell_{2}^{NC}(f,\ell^{NC}_{1}(g))\right)-\ell^{NC}_{1}\left(\ell_{2}^{NC}(g,\ell^{NC}_{1}(f))\right)=\ell^{NC}_{1}\left(\left[\ell^{NC}_{1}\left(\ell_{2}^{NC}(f,g)\right)-{\cal J}_{2}(f,g)\right]\right)\equiv\,0\,.

Model Equivalences
Michael Benedikt and Ehud Hrushovski

Abstract
. We look at equivalence relations on the set of models of a theory such that the class of equivalent pairs is itself an elementary class, in a language appropriate for pairs of models. We call these 𝖬𝖤𝖱 {\mathsf{MER}} s, for short. We provide many examples of definable ​ 𝖬𝖤𝖱 {\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}} s, along with the first steps of a classification theory for them. We characterize the special classes of definable ​ 𝖬𝖤𝖱 {\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}} s associated with preservation of formulas, either in classical first order logic or in continuous logic, and uncover an intrinsic role for the latter. We bring out a nontrivial relationship with interpretations (imaginary sorts), leading to a wider hierarchy of classes related to the preservation of reducts. We give results about the relationship between these classes, both for general theories and for theories satisfying additional model-theoretic properties, such as stability.



1.
Introduction

We will study equivalence relations defined on the set of models of a given theory defined via a set of logical formulas in a vocabulary appropriate
for pairs of models,
{\mathsf{MER}}
s for short.

A sentence in the language of pairs of models will usually not define an equivalence relation. There are some simple classes of sentences that do give
{\mathsf{MER}}
s, and we pay particular attention to these. One family of
{\mathsf{MER}}
s is associated with preservation of open formulas (a.k.a. definable relations) – either formulas of first-order logic or (“ydlept
{\mathsf{MER}}
s”) or open formulas
of continuous logic (“yclept
{\mathsf{MER}}
s”). We also extend the ydlept
{\mathsf{MER}}
to a hierarchy of families (“
n
-ydlept”, for
n
a natural number). These can be described via the preservation of definable families of sets, or by considering ydlepts over imaginaries.
We also consider the subclass of
{\mathsf{MER}}
s defined by restricting the quantifier complexity of the formula in the two-model language defining
the
{\mathsf{MER}}
.

One motivation for the study is a step towards model-theoretic analysis of families of mappings between structures. We have examples of
{\mathsf{MER}}
s that arise
from considering natural function classes.
A second motivation is to generalize classification theory to the study of models “up to an equivalence relation”.

Naturally, we are far from achieving any of these goals.
We will show that continuous logic plays a natural role in the study of
{\mathsf{MER}}
s, in that every
{\mathsf{MER}}
can be associated
with a continuous logic lower approximation.
We are able to identify some connections between families mentioned above: for example,

{\mathsf{MER}}
s that are definable with restricted quantifier alternation must be given by an open formula.
We point the reader to Theorem
11.4
, which gives a classification of the
{\mathsf{MER}}
s that are given by reducts
in continuous logic.
The reader may wish to look ahead to Figure
1
, which shows the classes we study, illustrating some connections.

We also establish some connections between traditional
model-theoretic criteria on the underlying theory and properties of the corresponding
{\mathsf{MER}}
s. In stable theories one family of
{\mathsf{MER}}
s collapses to an a priori
smaller class. We show that theories defining a nontrivial partial ordering on their universe always admit non-yclept
{\mathsf{MER}}
s: see Proposition
14.1
.
The hypothesis is natural in view of Shelah’s basic stability classification, where one of the classes is theories with a partial ordering on tuples (SOP).

Finally, we provide a large class of examples of
{\mathsf{MER}}
s, in the process showing non-connections between families.

Preliminaries

We often refer to two-valued first order logic as DL (discrete, or disconnected logic), in the context of formulas
or theories, contrasting
it with Continuous Logic (CL). In this work, unless otherwise specified,
T
will denote a DL theory in language
L
.
The word
“definable” will always mean, by default, definable with a formula without parameters
.

Most of our results require only basic logic. Occasionally we will also discuss statements specific to certain tame classes in Shelah’s classification, and for these we will assume some basic familiarity:

•

Stable theories,
[
Poi00
]
.

•

NIP theories and the strict order property
[
Sim15
]

•

o-minimal theories (which are NIP)
[
Dri98
]

In fact we use little more than their definitions; NIP occurs only in examples. Stability enters in §
9
via the more general notion of
stable embeddedness
, see e.g.
[
Sim15
, 3.1]
.

We will make use of continuous logic (CL) as in

[
YBHU08
]
, but “unmetrized”
(as in
[
CK66
]
). Equivalently
we can use the foundations of
[
YBHU08
]
but assume a trivial metric. This drastically reduces the complexity of the metatheory. Continuous logic works over models in which predicates take
truth values in a bounded subset of the reals.

Formulas are built up from real-valued predicates
using composition with continuous functions from products of reals to the reals, along with the quantifiers
sup and inf. Each such formula defines a bounded real-valued function on a model. We also close under
uniform limits: if
F_{i}(x)
are real-valued predicates that converge for all
x
, their limit is a new real-valued predicate.

In much of the paper, we will be interested in CL over classical models – the truth values of the predicates are just
\{0,1\}
, but the formulas can take more general real values. The reader might look ahead to Example
15.8
and
Example
15.4
for typical uses of CL.
While in most of the paper we will need little more than the semantics of CL formulas,
in Section
11
and Section
7
we will make use of the notion of a
CL theory
: this is a collection of assertions
of the form
\phi=0
, where
\phi
is a CL sentence. The notion of a model satisfying such a theory is the obvious one. We crucially use type spaces as well; they are compact Hausdorff but not necessarily totally disconnected, in fact they may be connected.

Compactness forms an essential feature of the theory of
{\mathsf{MER}}
s. We will use it both
directly and via the usual device of ’sufficiently saturated models’. In our setting, since we work with expansions throughout, the notion of
resplendency
provides a well-suited precise measure of the degree of saturation required. We use it for countable models. We define a countable

-structure

to be

if for any finite

, for any expansion

of

by finitely many symbols, any recursive

-theory

such that

.

Note that for a countable model

,
resplendence is equivalent to recursive saturation: any recursive partial type over a finite subset of

.
See
[
BS76
]
. We leave the transposition to continuous logic to the reader.
For any recursively saturated

, there exists a Borel map
b
from pairs
(M,T)
, with

a recursively

a finite

, such that
b(M,T)\models T
.
See the proof of Proposition
11.6
for one way to see this.

3.
Definition of model equivalences and basic properties

Let
L
be a vocabulary, possibly with multiple sorts. We let
S_{0}
be a subset of the sorts of
L
: the
coupled sorts
. The other
sorts are the
decoupled sorts
of
L
.

We define a new language
(L,=_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})
.
It will have two copies of the decoupled sorts, unprimed
and primed, and only one copy of the coupled sorts. It will also have
two copies of each non-logical symbol of
L
.
Suppose we have a relation
R(x_{1},\ldots x_{n})
of
L
, where
x_{i}
has sort
S_{i}
. Then
in
(L,=,L^{\prime})
we have a relation
R(x_{1}\ldots x_{n})
, where each
x_{i}
has the unprimed sort corresponding to
S_{i}
,
along with a relation
R^{\prime}(x_{1}\ldots x_{n})
where
x_{i}
has sort
S_{i}
if
S_{i}
has a coupled sort, while
x_{i}
has sort
S^{\prime}_{i}
if
S_{i}

is a decoupled sort.
The intuition for
(L,=_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})
is that it describes a pair of
L
-structures, where on the coupled sorts they are forced to have the
same universe, while on the decoupled sorts they are disjoint.

We now formalize this intuition.
Let
T
be a first order
L
theory
Let
\Omega
be a universe for each coupled sort of
L
. By
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}_{\Omega}(T)
we denote the models of theory
T
where the universes of the coupled
sorts match
\Omega
. A pair of models in
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}_{\Omega}(T)
can be made into a model for

(L,=_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})
.
in the obvious way, just by priming
the relations and the decoupled sorts in the second model.
Thus we can talk about a pair of models satisfying a sentence in
(L,=_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})
.

We consider the situation where a theory
\tau
in
(L,=_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})

defines an equivalence relation on
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}_{\Omega}(T)
, for any set
\Omega
.
We say that
\Xi
is a
{\mathsf{MER}}
. If
\tau
is
finitely axiomatizable, we talk of a

bidefinable Model Equivalence Relation
or just
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
.
Thus in case
T
has no finite models, it suffices to check the definition of a
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
on a single set
\Omega
of size at least
|L|+\aleph_{0}
; and similarly for
{{\mathsf{MER}}}
s. Only
{{\mathsf{MER}}}
’s (and among them,
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
’s) will be considered from here on.

For most of the result in this work, it suffices to deal with the case where all sorts of
L
are coupled: we refer to
these as
completely-coupled

{\mathsf{MER}}
s, and this will be our default.
We refer to the language for pairs in this case as
(L,=,L^{\prime})
.
While when we want to highlight the more general case with some decoupled sorts, we refer to
decoupled
{\mathsf{MER}}
s
.
In the completely-coupled case we will sometimes assume a single sort for simplicity.

We note that when the language is finite, we can basically assume the theory is finite as well:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T
be a theory in a finite language
L
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
for
T
. Then
there exists a finite
T_{0}\subset T
and a
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}

\mathcal{E}_{0}
on models of
T_{0}
, such that
\mathcal{E}_{0}\upharpoonright{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}(T)=\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

We can assume that
L
consists of a single relation
R
.

So
(L,=,L^{\prime})
is generated by
R,R^{\prime}
and the equality across models. We write
T(R^{\prime})
for the theory
T
, with
R
replaced everywhere by
R^{\prime}
.
We abuse notation to identify
\mathcal{E}
with its defining formula.
We have:
\mathcal{E}(R,R^{\prime})
is symmetric, reflexive and transitive on
(L,=,L^{\prime})
-models of
T(R)+T(R^{\prime})
. By compactness, for some finite

T_{0}\subset T
,
\mathcal{E}(R,R^{\prime})
is symmetric, reflexive and transitive on
(L,=,L^{\prime})
-models of
T_{0}(R)+T_{0}(R^{\prime})
.
∎

Actually the finite language assumption is unnecessary in Lemma
3.1
; we leave this to the reader.

3.1.
Correspondence with definable groupoids

A
groupoid
is a category where every morphism is invertible.
All groupoids considered here will have the same class of objects, namely
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}(T)
;
so they will be distinguished by their morphisms.
We have the groupoid
Iso_{T}
of models of
T
with isomorphisms. We also have the larger groupoid
Bij_{T}
whose objects are models of
T
, and whose morphisms
Bij_{T}(M,N)
are all bijections
M\to N
.
By a
T
-groupoid we mean an intermediate groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, with objects
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}(T)
and
morphisms
\mathcal{G}(M,N)
with
Iso_{T}(M,N)\subset\mathcal{G}(M,N)\subset Bij_{T}(M,N)
.

We consider a language describing two
L
-structures, along with bijections between
their coupled sorts
S_{0}
. A groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is
\bigwedge
-definable
if there exists
a set
\Theta
of sentences of
(L,+_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})
whose models are precisely the

\mathcal{G}
morphisms. If
\Theta
is finite, we say
\mathcal{G}
is definable.

In more detail, let
S_{0}
again represent the coupled sorts.

By
(L,+_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})
we denote the language with two copies of each sort of
L
,
referred to as primed and unprimed. For each relation or function in

L
,
(L,+_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})
has two copies,
one taking the unprimed sorts and the other taking the primed sorts.
We have additional function symbols,
f
, going from the unprimed copy of a sort in
S_{0}
to its primed copy.
A
bidefinable groupoid

{\mathcal{G}}
over coupled sorts
S_{0}

is given
by a formula
\Theta
in
(L,+_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})
, where

\mathcal{G}(M,N)=\{f:(M,N,f)\models\Theta\}
. If
\Theta

is allowed to be a possible infinite set of formulas, we refer
to an
\bigwedge
-definable groupoid. We write
Th(\mathcal{G})
to denote the
(L,+_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})
-partial type defining
groupoid
\mathcal{G}
.
Thus
Th(\mathcal{G})
is the
(L,+_{S_{0}},L^{\prime})
theory of
\{(M,N,f):M,N\models T,f\in\mathcal{G}(M,N)\}
.
When all sorts are coupled we refer simply to
(L,+,L^{\prime})
.

Warning 3.2
.

The definition of bidefinability and
\bigwedge
-bidefinability used in groupoids
applies even if it happens that
M=N
. It is not the same as definability in the structure
(M,f)
, since
in our definition the sorts of the two components are distinct, so we cannot compare
(e.g.)
x
and
f(x)
directly. Put another way, equality in the
(L,=,L^{\prime})
setting is being replaced by
identification via
f
in
(L,+,L^{\prime})
, so we do not have an additional equality.

In the rest of this section, we will assume for simplicity a single-sorted completely-coupled setting. That is,

L
has a single sort, this is the coupled sort.
In this case there is a single function symbol in
(L,+,L^{\prime})
.

Proposition 3.3
.

There is a natural 1-1 correspondence between
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
s for
T
,
and bidefinable groupoids for
T
,
and similarly for
{{\mathsf{MER}}}
s and
\bigwedge
-definable groupoids.

Proof.

Let
\Psi
be a
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
for
T
.
Define a
T
-groupoid
Gr(\Psi)
by letting the morphisms
Gr(\Psi)(M,M^{\prime})
be the set of bijections
f:M\to M^{\prime}
such that
M+f^{*}M^{\prime}\models\Psi
,
where
M+f^{*}M^{\prime}
is the
L=L
structure with the same universe as

such that the interpretation of the first copy of a given

is
R^{M}
,
and of the second copy,
f^{-1}(R^{M})
.

Conversely, given a
T
-groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, let
\Psi_{\mathcal{G}}
be the set of double models
(M^{\prime},M^{\prime\prime})
of
T
with the same universe

, such that

.
It is easy to see that these operations are inverse to each other, and that

∎

The following proposition indicates that the behavior of the groupoid is in some sense determined by its restriction to a single model.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume
T
is complete. Let
M
be any resplendent model of
T
, countable or not.
Then there is a canonical
1
to
1
correspondence between
definable\,groupoid
s for
T
and
bidefinable subgroups of
Sym(M)
containing
Aut(M)
. Likewise for
\bigwedge
-definable groupoid and
\bigwedge
-bidefinable subgroups.

See Warning
3.2
regarding the meaning of “bidefinability” even when the two parts are the same model.

Proof.

In one direction the correspondence is obvious: a
\bigwedge
-definable groupoid
\mathcal{G}
maps to
\mathcal{G}(M,M)\leq Sym(M)
. Let us show that this is one to one.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be a
\bigwedge
-definable groupoid,
and assume
\mathcal{G}(M,M)=H
is known. We show that
\mathcal{G}
is the unique
\bigwedge
-definable groupoid groupoid inducing

on

. Given any

and bijection

, one can find
(M_{1},M_{2},b^{\prime})
elementarily equivalent to
(N_{1},N_{2},b)
, as
(L,+,L^{\prime})
structures,
and with
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
saturated of the same cardinality as
M
. Then
M_{i}\cong M
and we
have
(M_{1},M_{2},b^{\prime})\cong(M,M,f)
for some bijection

.
We have
b\in\mathcal{G}(N_{1},N_{2})
iff
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(M_{1},M_{2})

iff
f\in\mathcal{G}(M,M)=H
. Thus the values of the groupoid on an arbitrary pair of models

are determined
by
H
.

It remains to prove surjectivity. Let
H
be a
\bigwedge
-definable subgroup of
Sym(M)
containing
Aut(M)
.
Then
H
is defined by some collection
\Psi
in
(L,+,L^{\prime})
. Define
\mathcal{G}
by

\mathcal{G}(N_{1},N_{2})=\{f:N_{1}\to N_{2}:(N_{1},N_{2},f)\models\Psi\}

So

is a
\bigwedge
-definable set of functions

, but we have to check it is a groupoid: if

Suppose otherwise, and take a saturated
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},f^{\prime},g^{\prime})

. Then

It is clear that going in this way from a subgroup

The other direction follows from the fact, shown in the first paragraph, that

Remark 3.5
.

It is remarkable that in the setting of

Similarly, a definable partial ordering on models automatically gives a definable category.

Assume for simplicity that

is complete and that

4.
Special classes of equivalence relations

We will
look at equivalence relations defined over models of
T
using preservation of a set of CL or DL formulas.

A
DL reduct
of a theory
T
is given by a collection of formulas of discrete logic and the associated theory.
Given a set of relation symbols, we consider the DL reduct given by these relations as atomic formulae. Thus
a language
L
can be considered a vacuous reduct of itself.
A CL reduct is defined analogously.

Now suppose we are given a reduct
R
on the coupled sorts within
L
, given by formulas of the corresponding logic. We let
\equiv_{R}
be
the equivalence relation saying that two structures agree on these formulas, and
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Aut}}}(R)
the
corresponding groupoid, which consists of mappings
g
taking a model
M
to a model
M^{\prime}
.
Thus, for a CL reduct given by a collection of formulas
F
, we are saying that each the function defined by
f\in F
on model
M
is the same as the function
defined on
M^{\prime}
.

When the theory is incomplete, the reduct may include
0
-place relations, corresponding to sentences the theory does not decide. To say that these relations agree on two
L
-structures just means that they have the same truth value.

Definition 4.1
.

We will say that a model equivalence relation is
yclept

if it has the form
\equiv_{R}
for some continuous logic reduct
R
. And we say that it is
ydlept

if
R
consists of standard first-order two-valued
formulas: that is, it is a “disconnected” or “discrete” or ’‘classical” first-order logic reduct.

Note that
a reduct only defines a
{\mathsf{MER}}
when its vocabulary is on the coupled sorts, since on the decoupled sorts we cannot
talk about two formulas having the same tuples
. Often in the sequel, we will specify an yclept or ydlept by simply listing
the formulas or relations that are preserved. The default convention is that the coupled sorts are all the sorts.
Note also that a ydlept
{\mathsf{MER}}
is not necessarily definable, since it can involve preservation of infinitely many first order formulas.

A yclept
{\mathsf{MER}}
can also involve either finitely many or infinitely many CL formula. But we observe that
any yclept equivalence relation, even one involving infinitely many formulas, is
\bigwedge
-definable:

Lemma 4.2
.

The equivalence relation corresponding to preserving a CL reduct is a
{{\mathsf{MER}}}
.

Proof.

The reduct includes certain uniform limits
f(x)
of
T
-definable functions
f_{n}(x)
with finite range in
[0,1]
. We
may assume
|f-f_{n}|\leq 1/n
uniformly.
Thus the groupoid consists of triples
(M,M^{\prime},g)
such that
M\models T,M^{\prime}\models T

and

The maximal CL reduct

We study here the maximal definable structure preserved by a
\bigwedge
-definable groupoid. Our proofs,
as well as the main statements, require

is a DL theory; see Example
15.8
.
In case

is small, any CL reduct is essentially DL so that the statements are true for
the maximal DL reduct; however our proofs still go through
continuous logic.

For a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
and theory

is

\mathcal{G}
-resplendent
if
M
is
\aleph_{0}
-homogeneous, and whenever

and the

partial type

with

. Note that if

is countable, any countable

has a
\mathcal{G}
-resplendent countable

is countable and recursively-in-

-resplendent. If

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

be a first order theory in a language

, and
\mathcal{G}
be a
\bigwedge
-definable groupoid for

. Then:

(1)

There exists a unique richest continuous logic reduct

of

, such that,

Iso_{T}={Iso}_{L}\leq\mathcal{G}\leq{Iso}_{(L^{\mathcal{G}})}
.

(2)

\mathcal{G}
is dense in each automorphism group of the groupoid corresponding to

M

\mathcal{G}
-resplendent,
the orbits of
\mathcal{G}
and of

coincide for each

(3)

Suppose

is small: only countably many
n
-types over
\emptyset

for each

. Then
{Iso}_{L^{\mathcal{G}}}
is ydlept: that is, is given by a DL reduct.

Proof.

We start by proving (1). Let

consist of all continuous logic relations, that are
\mathcal{G}
-invariant.
Here continuous logic relations are viewed as
0

is a finite product of sorts of

, and

-definable means that

is
\bigwedge
-definable for any closed

\mathcal{G}

.
It is clear that

is the richest continuous logic reduct

of

\mathcal{G}\leq{Iso}_{L^{\prime}}
.

We turn to (2), density. Let
S_{n}
be the space of types of
T
on

.
Define an equivalence relation on

:
p\sim q
iff there exist

and

in

and

. This is a closed subset of

by an ultraproduct argument: if

for

, this is witnessed by

.
Let
U
be any ultrafilter on

be the ultraproduct of
M_{i}

let

be the limit of the

along

, and similarly

. Let

be the ultraproduct of

is

\bigwedge
-bidefinable,

.

Note that
p\sim q
iff for some/any
\mathcal{G}
-resplendent

and

with

. The second passage from “some” to “any” uses the fact that

, composing with an automorphism taking one realization of

to another. It follows that

is an equivalence relation. In the case

,

are completions of

, and

iff for some resplendent pair

iff this holds for all resplendent pairs of models of

.

Let

be the quotient space

,

the quotient map. From the fact that

is closed in

is a Hausdorff space,
and since it is compact it is thus a normal space, and by Urysohn’s Lemma points can be separated

Let

, and suppose there is no

Let

Finally, we argue for (3). For small

∎

6.
DL and CL reducts

With the definitions behind us, we now begin the second part of the paper, where we address some basic questions about yclept
{\mathsf{MER}}
s versus
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
s.
For example, we give properties of
{\mathsf{MER}}
s that are both yclept and a
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
, but show
that this does not imply being ydlept.

In small theories the distinction between CL and DL reducts is not critical. Let us show a converse
to this.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
T
be a theory in a countable language. Then

T
is small iff every CL reduct of an expansion of

T
by finitely many constants can be presented as a DL reduct.

Therefore a small
T
as well as expansions by finitely many constants have
the property that ydlept = yclept:
if an equivalence relation comes from a CL reduct
then it comes from a DL reduct.

Proof.

If
T
is small, then every expansion by constants and every CL reduct of such an expansion is small. Hence every CL reduct is
generated by the discretely valued formulas in it: the type spaces are totally disconnected.

If
T
is not small, some expansion by constants has uncountably many
1
-types. So we may assume this holds for
T
. So

S_{x}
is uncountable. Then
S_{x}
contains a perfect subset
P
.

P
is homeomorphic to the Cantor set, and admits a continuous map onto the interval
[0,1]
. By the Tietze extension theorem, there exists a continuous, surjective
F:S_{x}\to[0,1]
. Now
F
can be viewed as a CL relation in the variables
x
. Since
x
is a single variable, the reduct generated by
F
admits quantifier elimination, and actually

F(x)=\alpha
generates a complete type, so that the space of
1
-types is homeomorphic to
[0,1]
.
Hence every unary relation of the reduct to
F
is a continuous function
C(F(x))
of
F(x)
, and as
[0,1]
is connected, if

C\circ F
takes more than one value then it takes infinitely many. So
F
cannot be approximated by
definable relations with finitely many values. ∎

7.
An yclept, non-ydlept

We exhibit here an yclept

There is a tension between ensuring that a yclept is not ydlept

To assert that a CL formula has the same interpretation in two models, one must express that
at any input they take the same real value; this seems to require infinitely many formulas,
asserting equality digit-by-digit as it were. We overcome this using a phenomenon of chaotic dynamics: two orbits cannot permanently stay near each other. Thus knowing the approximate location of a point over all time determines the point precisely.

11.
Smoothness and ycleptness

We give a more robust characterization of yclept
{\mathsf{MER}}
s. relating it to the notion of smoothness
on Borel equivalence relations. We then provide two applications, one to definable equivalence relations in the usual sense (Subsection
11.1
) and the other to consequences of cardinality restrictions on
{\mathsf{MER}}
s
(Section
12
).

In this section we
consider a consistent theory
T
in a countable language with no finite models
. We will only consider countable models,
indeed models with universe
\Omega={\mathbb{N}}
. This suffices of course for determining a
{\mathsf{MER}}
if the language is countable.

Fix a universe
\Omega
and consider a
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}

\mathcal{E}
on
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}_{\Omega}(T)
.
By Morley-izing
T
, noting that the set of models of
T_{\forall}
on
\Omega
is naturally a Polish space.
In the Morleyization, the axioms are
\forall\exists
, thus the set of models of
T
forms a
G_{\delta}

subset, we can also view
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}_{\Omega}(T)
as a Polish space.
\mathcal{E}
induces a Borel equivalence relation on
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}_{\Omega}(T)
. So one can ask if it is smooth in the sense of descriptive set theory,
i.e. admits complete invariants in a standard Borel space. Recall that
\mathcal{E}
is smooth if there exists a Borel map
\phi
on
Mod_{\Omega}(T)
into a Polish space, such that
xEy
iff
\phi(x)=\phi(y)
. This depends only on the Borel structure of
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}_{\Omega}(T)
, not on the Polish space structure.
See
[
DH23
]
for another use of this notion in model theory.

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a
{{\mathsf{MER}}}
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is yclept iff
\mathcal{E}
is smooth.

Proof.

We will show that these are also equivalent to:
\mathcal{G}(M,M)
is closed in
Sym(\Omega)
, for any resplendent countable
M
.

Here we take the usual group topology on
Sym(\Omega)
; it is induced from the product topology on
(^{\Omega}\Omega)^{2}
via the map
g\mapsto(g,g^{-1})
.
\Omega
itself is taken to have the discrete topology. We note that for the functions in the groupoid, this is the same as the pointwise convergence topology.
In general, a sequence of invertible functions may converge to a non-invertible one. But if
g_{n},g
lie in the groupoid and
if
g_{n}
pointwise converges to
g
, for each
a
, we must have
g_{n}(a)=g(a)
for large
n
, since the topology on
\Omega
is by definition discrete. Then letting
b=g^{-1}(a)
we have
g_{n}(b)=g(b)=a
for large
n
, so
g_{n}^{-1}(a)=b=g^{-1}(a)
for large
n
. This equivalence allows us to apply the characterizations in
Proposition
5.1
and its corollaries.

Assume
\mathcal{E}
is yclept.
We note that ycleptness implies that the group
G(\Omega,\Omega)
is closed in
Sym(\Omega)
: this follows from
Corollary
5.6
.
Thus
\mathcal{E}
is smooth: the invariant function is just the “reduct” function
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}_{\Omega}(T)\to{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}_{\Omega}(T^{\prime})
.
We leave to the reader the choice
of Borel codes for
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}(T)
and
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Mod\,}}}(T^{\prime})
and the verification that the restriction map is Borel.

Now we prove the direction from closed to yclept. Assume
\mathcal{G}(M,M)
is closed for all
M
, or just for
\mathcal{G}
-resplendent
M
.
Then
\mathcal{E}
is yclept by Corollary
5.6
.

Finally, we prove the direction from smooth to closed. Assume
\mathcal{E}
is smooth. Let
M
be a homogeneous and resplendent model of
T
, meaning that for any tuple

a\in M^{n}
there exist
b\neq b^{\prime}\in M
with
tp(b/a)=tp(b^{\prime}/a)
. Therefore
\mathop{\rm dcl}\nolimits(a)\neq M
.
We will show that
\mathcal{G}(M,M)
is closed in
Sym(M)
.

The group
H:=\mathcal{G}(M,M)
is a Borel subgroup of
Sym(\Omega)
. In fact, it is at a finite level on the Borel hierarchy, since
\mathcal{E}
, or the associated groupoid, is definable.
Let
{\mathbf{H}}
be the closure of
H
in
Sym(\Omega)
.
Note that
{\mathbf{H}}
is a perfect Polish space: if
{\mathbf{H}}
has an isolated point, then by translating this point to the identity element
1
we see
that
1
is isolated. Thus there exists some tuple
a\in M^{n}
such that the only element
h
of
{\mathbf{H}}
with

is the identity.
In particular this is true for
Aut(M)
since

. But that contradicts the assumption that

M
is homogeneous and resplendent.

If
H
is non-meager in
{\mathbf{H}}
, then by
[
Pet50
]

H=HH^{-1}
contains an open neighborhood of the identity of
{\mathbf{H}}
. Hence
H
is open. Since
{\mathbf{H}}
is a topological group, this implies that it is closed in
{\mathbf{H}}
. Thus
H
must equal
{\mathbf{H}}
, and thus
H
is closed in
Sym(\Omega)
, and we are done in this case.

So consider the case where
H
is meager in
{\mathbf{H}}
. Then by
[
BK96
, Theorems 3.4.3 and 3.4.5]
,
the right coset equivalence relation of

, namely

, is not smooth.
However, note that for

, we have

M^{\prime}\mathcal{E}M^{\prime\prime}
then

, since

is an isomorphism from the
(L,+,L^{\prime})
-structure

is bipartite

-structures. Thus for

are right

for some

iff

is a Borel function giving a complete invariant for

.
This shows that the right

and in particular on

∎

15.1.
First-order reducts

15.2.
Definable topologies

Let

[
FZ80
]

The invariants are the set of open sets, and

For

For the specific model

The fact that the

(2)

plus Proposition

Note that Proposition

15.3.
Uniform continuity

Let
T=Th({\mathbb{Q}},+,<)
. Consider the category of uniformly continuous maps:

(\forall u>0)(\exists v>0)(\forall x)(\forall x^{\prime})(|x-x^{\prime}|<v\to(f(x)-f(x^{\prime})|<u)

For maps from
M
to

but

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the groupoid of invertible morphisms in this category. The same can be done on a definable set

take

to be the plane (the set of pairs). Then even looking at just the single model

,
it is clear that the maximal CL reduct is trivial.

It is clear that no definable family of definable subsets of the plane

can be preserved by all uniformly continuous maps, unless it is definable in pure equality on
\Pi

For if

is neither finite nor co-finite, it will have a 1-dimensional boundary which is a finite Boolean combination of lines and points, but with at least one line or interval among them. Then a uniformly continuous

It should not be difficult (though possibly a little tedious) to extend this and

15.10.
The Lipschitz groupoid

We give an
example of an equivalence relation which is definable by a

is non-empty, there will be witnesses to disjuncts in

in sufficiently large models.
And since disjuncts are disjoint, no disjuncts in
E
will be satisfied by these tuples.

Lemma 15.7
.

For each
G
in
C_{\phi}
, for each
\vec{x}_{0}
in
G
satisfying a disjunct
of
A
in
\phi
, there
is
G^{\prime}
containing
\vec{x}_{0}
with the same adjacency sets as
G^{\prime}
,
with
\vec{x}_{0}
not satisfying
\phi
in
G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For each
A_{i}
in
A
satisfied by
\vec{x}_{0}
in
G
we choose
B_{i}
a relational atom or the negation
of such in
A_{i}
such that
\vec{x}_{0}
satisfies
B_{i}
, and let
B^{\prime}_{i}
be the corresponding ground atom with

\vec{x}_{0}
substituted.
We will construct
G^{\prime}
so that
\vec{x}_{0}
does not satisfy
B_{i}
in
G^{\prime}
.
Since the disjuncts of
E
are not satisfied in
G
, they will not be satisfied in
G^{\prime}
.

We let
c_{1}\ldots c_{k}
be the elements of
P
occurring as the first elements of such an atom.
Let
S_{i}
be the adjacency set of
c_{i}
.
For each
c_{i}
, we let
D_{i}
be the set of elements
d
such that
G(c_{i},d)
occurs
as an atom in
B_{i}
and
E_{i}
be the elements
e
such that
\neg G(c_{i},e)
occurs as an atom of

B_{i}
. Thus
S_{i}
contains
D_{i}
and is disjoint from
E_{i}
.

We claim that there is
c^{\prime}_{i}
whose adjacency set contains each
E_{i}
and is disjoint from
D_{i}
.
This follows easily from the fact that
G
is sufficiently random.
Let
G^{\prime}
be formed from
G
by swapping the adjacency sets of each
c_{i}
and each
c^{\prime}_{i}
.
By construction, the adjacency sets represented in
G^{\prime}
are the same as those in
G
.
∎

Putting the two lemmas together proves Proposition
15.5
.

While we have examined examples of definable equivalence relations that are not ydlept, and not even yclept or

-ydlept. But we know little about
which theories admit such examples.
Even with very strong hypotheses on the theories we cannot say anything about all
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}

Question 16.1
.

Are totally categorical theories
\forall
-ydlept? Or
\forall
-yclept? Or even “
\forall
-
\omega
-ydlept: every
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
is an
n
-ydlept for some
n
.

We do not know the answer for a single non-disintegrated strictly minimal set. Nor do we know it for the Morley rank two theory, interpretable over pure equality, of pairs of elements from an infinite set.
In the opposite direction, we do not know a counterexample even for superstable
T
. For stable theories, we know from Proposition
9.5
that higher ydlepts are ydlept. And we know it is possible to have yclept equivalence relations that are not ydlept:
see Example
15.9
, for
{{\mathsf{MER}}}
s, and the example from Section
7
. But at this level too we do not know if non-yclept definable equivalence relations are possible.

We do not have example with
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
s that are not
n
-ydlept for any
n
even weakening the hypotheses on the theories considerably.

For example, moving out to NSOP theories, for all we know every
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
could be
n
-ydlept for some
n
.

Question 16.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a nontrivial
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
on the models of an NSOP,
\aleph_{0}
-categorical theory
T
.
Must
\mathcal{E}
preserve a definable family of definable sets,
not definable in pure equality?

We do not know the answer for the random graph. Without
\aleph_{0}
-categoricity,
15.9
is a counterexample.

We also know almost nothing about what happens over finite structures. In particular, our examples of
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
s that are not
n
-ydlept for any
n
are all on
infinite structures.

We can show that for every
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
there is an ydlept
{\mathsf{MER}}
that agrees with it on finite structures “modulo some small distortion”.

Let
Mod_{fin}(T)
denote the category of finite models of
T
, with morphisms being isomorphisms.

Proposition 16.3
.

Let
L
be a finite language, let
T
be an
L
-theory and let
\mathcal{E}
be a
{\rm definable~{\mathsf{MER}}}
. Then there is an ydlept
{\mathsf{MER}}

(T_{1},\mathcal{E}_{1})
and an interpretation
\psi
taking models of
T_{1}
to models of
T
such that for every pair of models
M_{1},M^{\prime}_{1}
of
T_{1}
,

\psi(M_{1})\mathcal{E}\psi(M^{\prime}_{1})
iff
M_{1}\mathcal{E}_{1}M^{\prime}_{1}
, and

\psi
induces an isomorphism between
Mod_{fin}(T_{1})
and
Mod_{fin}(T)
.

Proof.

We know that
T
is bi-interpretable with a theory of graphs; so we may assume
L
consists of a single sort and a single binary relation
R
.

L_{1}
will have a new predicate
A
, the relation symbol
R
on
A^{2}
, and defining
B
to be the complement of
A
, also a ternary relation
\epsilon\subset A^{2}\times B
.
The theory
T_{1}
says
\epsilon
is a “membership” relation on
A^{2}\times B
, in the sense that extensionality holds, any singleton set exists, and the union of two sets exists.
Let
ext(b)
denote the set
\{a\in A^{2}:a~\epsilon~b\}
. Then an example of extensionality would be
(\forall a\in A^{2})(\exists b\in B)(ext(b)=\{a\})
, etc. If
N\models T
, we write

A(N)
for the set defined by
A
, but also for the
L
-structure
(A^{N},R^{N})
.

We now give the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
. Let

D=\{b\in B:(A,ext(b))~\mathcal{E}~(A,R)\}

This is a definable subset of
B
in
N
. Given two models
N,N^{\prime}
of
T^{\prime}
and a bijection
g:N\to N_{1}
the pair
(N,gN^{\prime})
is in the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
corresponding to
\mathcal{E}_{1}

iff
g
preserves the
0
-definable sets
A,B,\epsilon,D
.

The interpretation
\psi
is just the forgetful functor, forgetting about
B
and
\epsilon
.
On finite models, it is clear that this functor is
an equivalence, i.e. any finite
M\models T
is obtained in this way, and if
N_{1},N_{2}\in Mod_{fin}(T_{1})
then any isomorphism between
\psi N_{1}
and
\psi N_{2}
lifts to an isomorphism
N_{1}\to N_{2}
.

For finite
N_{1},N_{2}\models T_{1}
, a bijection
g:N_{1}\to N_{2}
lies in the groupoid corresponding to
\mathcal{E}_{1}
iff it preserves
A
,
B
,
\epsilon
and the restriction to

A(N_{1})\to A(N_{2})
is in the groupoid of
\mathcal{E}
.

∎

Note that the size increase in applying
\psi^{-1}
is simply exponential in a polynomial of
n
. More precisely, for some
k
, for a finite model of
T
with
n
elements,
\psi^{-1}(M)
has size
\leq 2^{n^{k}}
; even in case the
{\mathsf{MER}}
is
4
-ydlept, say.

Remark 16.6

Let

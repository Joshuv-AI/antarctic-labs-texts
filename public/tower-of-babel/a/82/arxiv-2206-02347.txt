Total closure for permutation actions of finite nonabelian simple groups
Saul D. Freedman, Michael Giudici, Cheryl E. Praeger

Abstract
For a positive integer k k , a group G G is said to be totally k k -closed if for each set Ω \Omega upon which G G acts faithfully, G G is the largest subgroup of Sym ⁡ ( Ω ) {\rm Sym}(\Omega) that leaves invariant each of the G G -orbits in the induced action on Ω × ⋯ × Ω = Ω k \Omega\times\cdots\times\Omega=\Omega^{k} . Each finite group G G is totally | G | |G| -closed, and k ⁡ ( G ) k(G) denotes the least integer k k such that G G is totally k k -closed. We address the question of determining the closure number k ⁡ ( G ) k(G) for finite simple groups G G . Prior to our work it was known that k ⁡ ( G ) = 2 k(G)=2 for cyclic groups of prime order and for precisely six of the sporadic simple groups, and that k ⁡ ( G ) ⩾ 3 k(G)\geqslant 3 for all other finite simple groups. We determine the value for the alternating groups, namely k ⁡ ( A n ) = n − 1 k(A_{n})=n-1 . In addition, for all simple groups G G , other than alternating groups and classical groups, we show that k ⁡ ( G ) ⩽ 7 k(G)\leqslant 7 . Finally, if G G is a finite simple classical group with natural module of dimension n n , we show that k ⁡ ( G ) ⩽ n + 2 k(G)\leqslant n+2 if n ⩾ 14 n\geqslant 14 , and k ⁡ ( G ) ⩽ ⌊ n / 3 + 12 ⌋ k(G)\leqslant\lfloor n/3+12\rfloor otherwise, with smaller bounds achieved by certain families of groups. This is achieved by determining a uniform upper bound (depending on n n and the type of G G ) on the base sizes of the primitive actions of G G , based on known bounds for specific actions. We pose several open problems aimed at completing the determination of the closure numbers for finite simple groups.

Next we prove a result about the
k
-closure of an intransitive action of a nonabelian simple group.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a nonabelian simple group acting on a set
\Omega
with orbits
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{t}
with

is not transitive on
\Delta_{j}

G^{(k),\Delta_{i}}=G^{\Delta_{i}}\cong G

Proof.

For each

2.2

G^{(k),\Omega}\leqslant\prod_{i=1}^{t}(G^{(k),\Omega})^{\Delta_{i}}\leqslant\prod_{i=1}^{t}G^{(k),\Delta_{i}}\cong G^{t}

[

\{\mathcal{O}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{\ell}\}

such that, for each

(H_{j})^{\Delta}=1

Suppose that

contains the diagonal subgroup

, which induces

on

. By assumption, there exists

such that

is not transitive on

. Choose
\alpha\in\Delta_{j}
and consider the

-tuple

of points. By definition of the

, there exists

such that

and

. Since

3
Links between bases and closures

For a finite group
G
acting on a set
\Omega
, let
b(G^{\Omega})
denote the minimum size of a base for the permutation group
G^{\Omega}\leqslant{\rm Sym}(\Omega)
induced by
G
on
\Omega
;
b(G^{\Omega})
is called the
base size
of
G^{\Omega}
. When
G
is transitive, a
maximal block system
for
G
on
\Omega
is a
G
-invariant partition
\Sigma
of
\Omega
such that
|\Sigma|>1
and the induced action
G^{\Sigma}
is primitive. In other words, the only
G
-invariant partition strictly coarser than
\Sigma
(that is, with each part a union of more than one part of
\Sigma
) is the universal partition
\{\Omega\}
with just one part. Note that
\Sigma
is allowed to be the partition into singletons.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite nonabelian simple group, and suppose that
G
has a faithful permutation representation on a set
\Omega
. Let
\Omega_{0}
be a
G
-orbit on
\Omega
with
|\Omega_{0}|>1
, and let
\Sigma
be a maximal block system for
G
on
\Omega_{0}
. Then the base size
b(G^{\Omega})
satisfies
b(G^{\Omega})\leqslant b(G^{\Sigma})
.

Proof.

Since
|\Sigma|>1
and
G
is simple it follows that
G
acts faithfully on
\Sigma
. Let
b=b(G^{\Sigma})
. Then
G
has a base
\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{b}\}
in
\Sigma
, that is
G_{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{b}}=1
. Let
\alpha_{i}\in\sigma_{i}
for each
i
. Then
G_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{b}}
fixes each of the
\sigma_{i}
setwise, and hence

G_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{b}}\leqslant G_{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{b}}=1
. It follows that
b(G^{\Omega})\leqslant b
.
∎

We may restate Wielandt’s result
[
29
, Theorem 5.12]
as follows.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
G
is a finite group, and
G
has a permutation representation on a set
\Omega
, such that
b(G^{\Omega})=b
, then
G^{(b+1),\Omega}=G
.

This has the following useful consequence.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite nonabelian simple group, and let
b_{maxprim}(G)
denote the maximum of
b(G^{\Omega})
over each faithful primitive permutation representation of
G
on a set
\Omega
. Then the closure number
k(G)
is at most
b_{maxprim}(G)+1
.

Proof.

Let
G
be faithfully represented on a set
\Omega
. Then, by Lemma
3.1
,
b(G^{\Omega})
is at most the base size of some primitive representation of
G
, and hence
b(G^{\Omega})\leqslant b_{maxprim}(G)=b
, say. It follows from Lemma
3.2
that
G^{(b+1),\Omega}=G
. Hence, by definition of the closure number,
k(G)\leqslant b+1
.
∎

{\rm P}\Omega^{+}(6,q)
or of
{\rm PSL}(4,q)
has a base size of at most
5
, while the remaining actions on subspaces here all have a base size of at most
3
. When
q
is even, the action of
{\rm PSp}(2,q)
corresponding to case (b)(ii) of Definition
3.4
also has base size at most
3

[
17
, p. 30]
. Thus
b_{maxstand}(G)\leqslant b_{upper}(G)
in each case.

It remains to consider the cases where our upper bound
b_{upper}(G)
for

may be strictly less than
b_{maxprim}(G)
. By Lemma
3.5
, this can occur only if
n+1-\delta_{2q}<4
, i.e., only if
n=2
or
G={\rm PSL}(3,2)
. This same lemma now shows that the base size of a non-standard action of
G
is at most
3=n+1-\delta_{2q}
. Therefore, in each linear case,
b_{maxprim}(G)\leqslant b_{upper}(G)
.

Case (b)
:
G\cong{\rm PSU}(n,q)
, with
n\geqslant 3
. As
\lfloor n/3+11\rfloor\leqslant n
for all
n\geqslant 16
, we see from Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
that
b_{maxsub}(G)\leqslant b_{upper}(G)
.

The remaining standard actions correspond to the isomorphisms
{\rm PSU}(4,q)\cong{\rm P}\Omega^{-}(6,q)
and
{\rm PSU}(4,2)\cong{\rm PSp}(4,3)\cong{\rm P}\Omega(5,3)
. Lemma
4.3
shows that

(since

, the group
{\rm P}\Omega^{-}(6,q)
has no primitive subspace action on three-dimensional subspaces of its natural module). Moreover, if
b_{upper}(G)<4
, then
b_{maxsub}(G)\leqslant 3=n
. Hence Lemma
3.5
yields
b_{maxprim}(G)\leqslant b_{upper}(G)
in each case.

Case (c)
:
G\cong{\rm PSp}(n,q)
, with
n\geqslant 4
. When
q
is even, the subspace action of
G
from case (b)(ii) of Definition
3.4
has base size
n
if
n\geqslant 6

[
24
, Proposition 1]
, or base size at most
5
if
n=4

[
17
, p. 30]
. Since
\lfloor n/3+10\rfloor
and
\lfloor n/4+11\rfloor
are less than or equal to
n
for all
n\geqslant 14
, we obtain
b_{maxsub}(G)\leqslant b_{upper}(G)
from Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
.

We now consider the isomorphisms
{\rm PSp}(4,3)\cong{\rm PSU}(4,2)\cong{\rm P}\Omega^{-}(6,2)
and
{\rm PSp}(4,q)\cong{\rm P}\Omega(5,q)
. In each case, Lemma
4.3
yields
b_{maxstand}(G)\leqslant b_{upper}(G)
. As no symplectic

satisfies

, it follows from Lemma
3.5
that
b_{maxprim}(G)\leqslant b_{upper}(G)
.

Case (d)
:
G\cong{\rm P}\Omega^{\varepsilon}(n,q)
, with

. Noting that
\lfloor n/3+11\rfloor\leqslant n-1
for all

and
\lfloor n/4+11\rfloor\leqslant\lfloor n/3+10\rfloor
for all

, we see from Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
that
b_{maxsub}(G)\leqslant b_{upper}(G)
.

As

, there are no nontrivial isomorphisms of classical groups to consider. Moreover,
b_{upper}(G)>3
, and so
b_{maxprim}(G)\leqslant b_{upper}(G)
by Lemma
3.5
. ∎

We can now prove Theorem
1.2
for classical groups.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
(c).

Let

be a classical group that is not isomorphic to an alternating group. Then Theorem
1.2
(c) follows from Corollary
3.3
and the upper bound for

given in Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Of course, if

is a low-dimensional unitary, symplectic or orthogonal group that is not explicitly mentioned in Theorem

for

from this theorem is often higher than the actual upper bound that follows from Theorem

. Notice also that the lower and upper bounds for

from Table

again denotes the Kronecker delta, and

is as in (

).

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
G
be a finite simple classical group that is not isomorphic to an alternating group.

(i)

If

, then

.

(ii)

If

(iii)

If

(iv)

, and with

.

Remark 4.5
.

Observe that if

is one of the groups in Corollary

for which

is known exactly, then the upper bound for the closure number

from Theorem

is equal to

.
Hence this theorem gives the best upper bound for

that can be obtained solely using Corollary

. On the other hand, for many low-dimensional unitary, symplectic and orthogonal groups

on the base sizes of primitive actions of

and hence for

.

5
k
-closures of sporadic groups

We start with the following lemma on certain simple groups.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a finite nonabelian simple group acting primitively on a set
\Omega
such that
\Out(G)=1
and
G
is maximal in
{\rm Alt}(\Omega)
. If
G
is
k
-transitive on
\Omega
but not
(k+1)
-transitive for some integer
k
such that
1\leqslant k<|\Omega|-2
, then
G^{(k+1),\Omega}=G
but
G^{(k),\Omega}\neq G
.

Proof.

Note that since
G
is
k
-transitive we have that
G^{(k),\Omega}={\rm Sym}(\Omega)\neq G
.
Since
G
is maximal in
{\rm Alt}(\Omega)
it follows that
G^{(k+1),\Omega}\cap{\rm Alt}(\Omega)=G
or
{\rm Alt}(\Omega)
. However,
{\rm Alt}(\Omega)
is
(k+1)
-transitive on
\Omega
while
G
is not, so
{\rm Alt}(\Omega)\not\leqslant G^{(k+1),\Omega}
. Hence
G^{(k+1),\Omega}\cap{\rm Alt}(\Omega)=G
. Thus
|G^{(k+1),\Omega}:G|\leqslant 2
. Since
G
acts primitively on
\Omega
(and
|G|
is not prime) we have that
C_{{\rm Sym}(\Omega)}(G)=1
. Then as
\Out(G)=1
it follows that
G^{(k+1),\Omega}=G
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

(a)

Let
G=M_{23}\leqslant{\rm Sym}(\Omega)
with
|\Omega|=23
. Then
G^{(5),\Omega}=G
.

(b)

Let
G=Co_{3}\leqslant{\rm Sym}(\Omega)
. with
|\Omega|=276
. Then
G^{(3),\Omega}=G
.

(c)

Let
G=Co_{2}\leqslant{\rm Sym}(\Omega)
with
|\Omega|=2300
. Then
G^{(2),\Omega}=G
.

Proof.

By
[
21
]
,
G
is maximal in
{\rm Alt}(\Omega)
. Additionally,
\Out(G)=1
, and so the result follows from Lemma
5.1
.
∎

When
G=M_{23}
,
Co_{2}
or
Co_{3}
we have from
[
10
, Table 1]
that
b_{maxprim}(G)=6
so our next result is a slight improvement on what can be deduced from Corollary
3.3
. We suspect that it is not best possible.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G=M_{23}
,
Co_{2}
or
Co_{3}
. Then
k(G)\leqslant 6
.

Proof.

Let
n=23
if
G=M_{23}
,
n=276
if
G=Co_{3}
and
n=2300
if
G=Co_{2}
, and let
G
act faithfully on a set
\Omega
. We will prove that
G^{(6),\Omega}=G^{\Omega}\cong G
, and from this it will follow that
k(G)\leqslant 6
. If
G
has an orbit with a maximal block system
\mathcal{B}
such that
|\mathcal{B}|\neq n
, then
b(G^{\mathcal{B}})\leqslant 5
, by
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, and so
b(G^{\Omega})\leqslant 5
, by Lemma
3.1
. Hence by Lemma
3.2
,
G^{(6),\Omega}=G^{\Omega}\cong G
. Thus it remains to consider the case where on each orbit
\Delta
, each maximal block system has size
n
. Let
\Delta
be an orbit of
G
and let
\mathcal{B}
be a maximal block system on
\Delta
.
By Lemma
2.5
and Corollary
5.2
we have that
G^{(6),\Delta}
preserves
\mathcal{B}
and
(G^{(6),\Delta})^{\mathcal{B}}=G^{(6),\mathcal{B}}=G^{\mathcal{B}}\cong G
. Moreover, by
[
10
, Theorem 1]
there exist
B_{1},\ldots,B_{6}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
G_{B_{1},\ldots,B_{6}}=1
(since
G
is faithful on
\mathcal{B}
), and it follows from Lemma
2.5
that
G^{(6),\Delta}
acts faithfully on
\mathcal{B}
. Thus
G^{(6),\Delta}\cong G
so
G^{(6),\Delta}=G^{\Delta}
.

If
\Omega=\Delta
then we are done so we may assume that
G
has more than one orbit. Recall that, on each
G
-orbit, each maximal block system has size
n
. Let
\Delta_{1}
be a
G
-orbit and let
\mathcal{B}_{1}
be a maximal block system for
G
on
\Delta_{1}
. Let
\beta\in B\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
. Then
G_{\beta}\leqslant G_{B}
. When
G=M_{23}
we have that
G_{B}=M_{22}
, when
G=Co_{3}
we have
G_{B}=McL.2
and when
G=Co_{3}
we have
G_{B}={\rm PSU}_{6}(2).2
. In all cases
|G_{B}|
is not divisible by
23
but
n
is, and so
G_{B}
does not have a transitive action of degree
n
. Thus
G_{B}
, and hence also
G_{\beta}
is not transitive on any orbit of
G
. Since
G^{(6),\Delta}=G^{\Delta}
for each
G
-orbit
\Delta
, it follows from Lemma
2.3
that
G^{(6),\Omega}=G^{\Omega}\cong G
and the result follows.
∎

We use Lemma
5.3
for
M_{23}
, as well as the fundamental Lemma
2.1
, to determine the closure number of
M_{24}.

Lemma 5.4
.

k(M_{24})=6
.

Proof.

Let
G=M_{24}
act faithfully on a set
\Omega
. We divide the orbits
\Delta
of
G
into three types:

(a)

|\Delta|=24
.

(b)

G
has a maximal block system on
\Delta
of size other than 24.

(c)

Each maximal block system of
G
on
\Delta
has size 24 and at least one of these maximal block systems is not the partition into singletons.

Recall that we allow a maximal block system to be the partition into singletons.

Let
\Delta
be an orbit of
G
and suppose first that
\Delta
is of type (a).
Then
G
acts 5-transitively but not 6-transitively on
\Delta
. By
[
21
]

G
is a maximal subgroup of
A_{24}
and so by Lemma
5.1
,
k(G)\geqslant 6
and
G^{(6),\Delta}=G^{\Delta}\cong G
.

Next suppose that
\Delta
has type (b) and let
\mathcal{B}
be a maximal block system on
\Delta
with
|\mathcal{B}|\neq 24
. Then by
[
10
, Theorem 1]
the base size
b(G^{\mathcal{B}})\leqslant 4
. Hence by Lemma
3.1
, also
b(G^{\Delta})\leqslant 4
and so by Lemma
3.2
,
G^{(5),\Delta}=G^{\Delta}\cong G
. This implies that
G^{(6),\Delta}=G^{\Delta}\cong G
.

Finally suppose that
\Delta
is of type (c) and let
\mathcal{B}
be a maximal block system of size 24 that is not the partition into singletons. Let
B\in\mathcal{B}
. By Lemma
2.5
and our result on orbits of type (a), we have
M_{24}\cong G^{\mathcal{B}}\leqslant(G^{(6),\Delta})^{\mathcal{B}}\leqslant G^{(6),\mathcal{B}}\cong M_{24}
, and so
(G^{(6),\Delta})^{\mathcal{B}}\cong G
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.5
,
M_{23}\cong(G^{\Delta})_{B}^{B}\leqslant(G^{(6),\Delta})_{B}^{B}\leqslant(G_{B})^{(6),B}=M_{23}^{(6),B}
, which is isomorphic to
M_{23}
by Lemma
5.3
. Thus
G^{(6),\Delta}\leqslant M_{23}\wr M_{24}
acting imprimitively. Furthermore, since the minimal degree of a transitive representation of
M_{23}
is 23 (see for example
[
13
, p71]
) we have
|B|\geqslant 23
. Let
K
be the kernel of the action of
G^{(6),\Delta}
on
\mathcal{B}
and suppose that
K\neq 1
. Then by a lemma of Scott (
[
28
, p. 328]
or see
[
27
, Theorem 4.16]
) the group
K
is a direct product of
\ell
diagonal subgroups
H_{j}\cong M_{23}
for
j=1,\ldots,\ell
(where
\ell\geqslant 1
), with pairwise disjoint supports, that is to say, there is a partition
\{\mathcal{O}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{\ell}\}
of
\mathcal{B}
such that, for each
j
,
(H_{j})^{B}\cong M_{23}
for all
B\in\mathcal{O}_{j}
and
(H_{j})^{B}=1
for all
B\notin\mathcal{O}_{j}
. Suppose that
\ell\geqslant 2
. Then there exist distinct
B_{j},B_{k}\in\mathcal{B}
such that for all
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}\in B_{j}
and all
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in B_{k}
, there exists
h\in K
such that
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5})^{h}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5})
and
\beta_{1}^{h}=\beta_{2}
.
Since
K\leqslant G^{(6),\Delta}
, there exists
g\in G
such that
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5},\beta_{1})^{g}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5},\beta_{1})^{h}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5},\beta_{2})
. As
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
were arbitrary points of
B_{k}
, it follows that
G_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}}
acts transitively on
B_{k}
. Note that
G_{B_{j}}=M_{23}
, so
G_{B_{j}}
acts faithfully on
B_{j}
. Now
G_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}}=(G_{B_{j}})_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}}=(M_{23})_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}}
while
|B_{k}|\geqslant 23
. Suppose first that
G_{B_{j}}
has a maximal block system
of size greater than 23 on
B_{j}
. Then by
[
10
, Theorem 1]
and Lemma
3.1
, we have
b(G_{B_{j}}^{B_{j}})\leqslant 4
. Hence we could have chosen
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}
such that
(M_{23})_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}}=1
, contradicting
G_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}}
acting transitively on
B_{k}
. Hence all maximal block systems
\Sigma
of

on

have size 23. However, in this case
we can choose
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}\in B_{j}
from pairwise distinct blocks of

such that
|(M_{23})_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}}|<23
, again contradicting
G_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5}}
acting transitively on
B_{k}
.

Thus
\ell=1
so
K\cong M_{23}
, and
G^{(6),\Delta}=M_{23}\times M_{24}
. Hence
G^{(6),\Delta}
has an intransitive normal subgroup
N
isomorphic to
M_{24}
such that the normal subgroup
L\cong M_{23}
acts transitively on the set
\Sigma
of
N
-orbits. Now
\Sigma
is a
G
-invariant partition of
\Delta
, and a maximal block system for the
G
-action on
\Sigma
corresponds to a maximal block system, say
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, for
G^{\Delta}
. By assumption all maximal block systems for
G
on
\Delta
have size 24 and so

. Hence the
G
-action on
\Sigma
has a block system of size
24
. Thus the subgroup

of

is transitive on

preserving a block system of size

. This implies that

has a subgroup of index

, which is a contradiction (see for example
[

, p71]
).
Therefore

and
G^{(6),\Delta}=M_{24}
.

Thus we have shown that
G^{(6),\Delta}=G^{\Delta}\cong G
for each orbit
\Delta
.
In particular, if
G
is transitive on
\Omega
then we have shown that

. Suppose now that

is intransitive on

. If there is some orbit

of type (b) then we saw above that

acts faithfully on

. If all orbits are of type (a) then all actions of

It remains to consider the case where there is at least one orbit

preserves a block system

on

. Let

. Since

, and the fact that

. Thus we have shown that

. Moreover, equality holds since if

∎

We can now complete the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Let

, so suppose further that

Wave-front sets for p p -adic Lie algebras Thanks: The author is supported by NSTC grant 112-2115-M-001-009.
Cheng-Chiang Tsai

Abstract
. We study the wave-front set of an element in a p p -adic reductive Lie algebra (for p ≫ rank p\gg\operatorname{rank} ), namely the set of maximal nilpotent orbits appearing in its Shalika germ expansion. By adapting an algorithm of Waldspurger that computes orbital integrals, we obtain an inductive algorithm to compute an invariant that determines the wave-front set. This gives an algorithm to compute the wave-front sets for regular supercuspidal representations and reveals examples whose wave-front sets are not contained in a single geometric orbit, for arbitrarily large p p within a fixed rank.



1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field with residue field
k
and
p:=\operatorname{char}(k)
,
G
a connected reductive group over
F
and
\mathfrak{g}:=\operatorname{Lie}G
. Throughout this paper we enforce some restrictions on
p:=\mathrm{char}(k)
for which we refer to Appendix
A
. In the study of representations and characters of
G(F)
, it has been long observed that they demonstrate an inductive structure. For the more recent landmarks, Jiu-Kang Yu
[
Yu01
]
constructed supercuspidal representations inductively from smaller twisted Levi subgroups. In
[
KM06
]
, Ju-Lee Kim and Fiona Murnaghan gave an inductive proof for a formula of the local characters of these and more representations. After that, Jeff Adler and Loren Spice
[
AS09
]
and eventually Spice
[
Spi18
,
Spi21
]
obtained an inductive description for the full character of Yu’s supercuspidal representations, which are known to be all supercuspidal representations
[
Kim07
,
Fin21
]
under restrictions on
p
. Meanwhile, Jean-Loup Waldspurger
[
Wal06
]
proved that the fundamental lemma in mixed and equal characteristic are equivalent, by developing an inductive algorithm to compute the orbital integrals needed and showing that the algorithm depends only on some data defined in terms of the residue field. (This was already done for
GL_{n}
in
[
Wal91
]
.) In
[
Tsa15
]
the author mentioned how a pretty similar algorithm can be used to compute Shalika germs. All aforementioned works largely employ the same inductive process which we discuss in §
3
.

Let us recall the theory of Shalika germs as enhanced by the homogeneity theorems of Waldspurger
[
Wal95
]
and DeBacker
[
DeB02a
]
. Denote by
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim
the set of nilpotent
\operatorname{Ad}(G(F))
-orbits in
\mathfrak{g}(F)
. We have

Theorem 1.1
.

[
DeB02a
, Thm. 2.1.5]
For any
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}(F)
, there exists
c_{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma)\in\mathbb{Q}
indexed by
\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim
and a lattice
\Lambda_{\gamma}\subset\mathfrak{g}(F)
such that

I_{\gamma}(f)=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\sim}c_{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma)I_{\mathcal{O}}(f),\;\forall f\in C_{c}(\mathfrak{g}(F)/\Lambda_{\gamma}).

Here we denote by
C_{c}(\mathfrak{g}(F)/\Lambda_{\gamma})\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}(F))
the space of
\mathbb{C}
-valued compactly supported functions satisfying
f(X+Y)=f(X)
for any
Y\in\Lambda_{\gamma}
. The coefficients
c_{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma)
are called
Shalika germs
, generalizing the theorem of Shalika
[
Sha72
]
for
\gamma\in G(F)
strongly regular semisimple and sufficiently close to
1
. Consider the partial order on
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim
for which
\mathcal{O}_{1}\leq\mathcal{O}_{2}
iff the former is contained in the closure of the latter in the
p
-adic topology. With Theorem
1.1
, we may define the wave-front set

\operatorname{WF}(\gamma):=\max\{\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim\;|\;c_{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma)\not=0\}.

This can be viewed as the linearized version of wave-front sets for representations of
G(F)
. For example, the wave-front set for a regular supercuspidal representation
[
Kal19
]
is equal to the aforementioned wave-front set for specific elliptic
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}(F)
, see Remark
7.1
. Given that wave-front sets of representations of
G(F)
are important for degenerate Whittaker models
[
MW87
]
and related to the Gan-Gross-Prasad program (see e.g.
[
JLZ22
, §4]
),
\operatorname{WF}(\gamma)
appears to be a basic object for the representation theory of
G(F)
, and a natural question is whether we can compute
\operatorname{WF}(\gamma)
inductively.

It came as a surprise to us that if we take the question in the literal and strict sense, then the answer is no. Namely in the inductive process when we write
\gamma=\gamma_{r}+\gamma_{>r}
and
H:=Z_{G}(\gamma_{r})
, the set
\operatorname{WF}^{G}(\gamma)

cannot
be determined by
\gamma_{r}
and
\operatorname{WF}^{H}(\gamma_{>r})
; see Example
6.6
. This is the subtlety discussed in
[
Nev23
, Rmk. 3.6]
. Nevertheless, we define for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
another set (see §
2
for the notation):

c_{G,s}(\gamma):=\{(\varsigma,c)\;|\;\varsigma\text{ an }s\text{-facet, }c\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma=s},\;\operatorname{Ad}(G(F))\varsigma\cap\left(c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>s}\right)\not=\emptyset\}.

The set implicitly appeared in
[
Wal06
]
and also resembles the residue field version of
\operatorname{Nil}(\gamma)
in
[
Nev23
]
. The set
c_{G,s}(\gamma)
determines
\operatorname{WF}(\gamma)
, and we manage to adapt the algorithm of Waldspurger in
[
Wal06
]
to inductively compute
c_{G,r}(\gamma)
. Consider

(1)

C_{G,s}:=\{(\varsigma,c)\;|\;\varsigma\text{ an }s\text{-facet, }c\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma=s}\}

so that every
c_{G,s}(\gamma)
is a subset of
C_{G,s}
. There will be a set
C_{G}
which is an appropriate union
C_{G}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}C_{G,s}
and we will define a directed acyclic graph with vertex set
C_{G}
and some edges, so that
c_{G,s}(\gamma)
is equal the subset of those vertices
C_{G}
who have a path to any vertex in
c_{G,r}(\gamma)
. On the other hand, when
\gamma=\gamma_{r}+\gamma_{>r}
,
c_{G,r}(\gamma)
is explicitly determined by
c_{H,r}(\gamma_{>r})
where
H=Z_{G}(\gamma_{r})
. Together this gives an inductive algorithm to determine any
c_{G,s}(\gamma)
.

There are two kinds of complications for this algorithm. For a vertex
v
in the graph
C_{G}
, the set of its out-neighbors correspond to the set of rational orbits in specific graded Lie algebra over
k
that intersect specific affine subspace (e.g. Slodowy slices). A good theory about such orbits - and therefore the edges of the graph - seems very difficult to achieve. We call this the geometric problem (Problem
5.8
). On the other hand, the graph
C_{G}
is defined in terms of the Bruhat-Tits building for
G
, or effectively an apartment of it, which is still sort of complicated. Furthermore, for a given directed acylic graph it needs not be easy to determine if there is a path from a specific vertex to another set of vertices (“
does this maze have a solution?
”). We call these the combinatorial problem.

We prove some necessary conditions and sufficient conditions for edges in the graph to exist, mostly to address the geometric problem. Our main existence result, Corollary
5.21
, is based on Theorem
B.4
which generalizes the result established by Dan Ciubotaru and Emile Okada in
[
CO23
, §4]
. When the semisimple part of
\gamma
is contained in
(\operatorname{Lie}T)(F)
for
T\subset G
an unramified subtorus, it seems possible that necessary and sufficient conditions are already strong enough to give the correct unramified wave-front set
\operatorname{WF}^{ur}(\gamma)
; see Conjecture
6.4
. In some cases we analyze our algorithm and confirm that. This includes the case corresponding to unramified toral supercuspidal representations already worked out in
[
CO23
]
, as well as an interesting example (Example
6.2
) with
G=U_{6}
and that
\operatorname{WF}(\gamma)
contains orbits of type
[4,1,1]
and
[3,3]
, for
p
arbitrarily large.

Let us now explain the organization of the paper. In §
2
we explain how to determine
\operatorname{WF}(\gamma)
from
c_{G,s}(\gamma)
. In §
3
we explain a result of Kim-Murnaghan that allows us to reduce the computation of
c_{G,s}(\gamma)
to twisted Levi subgroups. Then in §
4
we present the main construction (see Theorem
4.7
) of the graph
C_{G}
, hence our algorithm for computing
c_{G,s}(\gamma)
, citing a construction of Waldspurger to be explained in §
5
. The longest section §
5
analyzes the algorithm, or mostly analyzes a number of questions arising from
[
DeB02b
]
relating Moy-Prasad quotients and nilpotent orbits. Several results in §
5
will refer to Appendix
B
regarding (graded) Lie algebras over the residue field. In §
6
we present demonstrations and examples for the algorithm, and lastly in §
7
we discuss the relation between wave-front sets for elements in the Lie algebra and wave-front sets for representations of
G(F)
.

2.
DeBacker’s parametrization

We begin with some notations. Let
\mathcal{B}(G,F)
be the Bruhat-Tits building (hereafter always the extended building) for
G/_{F}
. For any
x\in\mathcal{B}(G,F)
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
, denote by
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x\geq r}
the Moy-Prasad lattice
[
MP94
, §3]
of depth
r
, by
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x>r}
that of depth
>r
, and by
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x=r}:=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x\geq r}/\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x>r}
the Moy-Prasad quotient at depth
r
. We also denote by
G(F)_{x\geq 0}
the parahoric subgroup and
\mathsf{G}_{x}/_{k}
the (algebraic) reductive quotient. In particular
\mathsf{G}_{x}(k)
is a quotient of
G(F)_{x\geq 0}
with pro-
p
kernel, and
\operatorname{Lie}\mathsf{G}_{x}=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x=0}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Our notations for the Moy-Prasad filtration are different from the usual notations. The new notations are extensively used in
[
Spi18
]
and
[
Spi21
]
. They will also prove convenient for our purpose here.

We also denote by
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\geq r}:=\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{B}(G,F))}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x\geq r}
and likewise
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{>r}:=\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{B}(G,F))}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x>r}
. We define

(2)

\operatorname{dep}(\gamma):=\max\{r\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\geq r}\}

Following
[
DeB02b
]
we say
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(G,F)
are in the same
r
-facet iff
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x_{1}\geq r}=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x_{2}\geq r}
and
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x_{1}>r}=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x_{2}>r}
.
Denote by
\Phi^{G}(r)
the set of
r
-facets, which form a partition of
\mathcal{B}(G,F)
. Recall that
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)\subset\mathfrak{g}(F)
is the nilpotent cone. Let us begin with
[
DeB02b
, Def. 4.1.1]
:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\varsigma\in\Phi^{G}(r)
for some
r\in\mathbb{R}
. We say a coset
c\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma=r}
is
nilpotent
iff
(c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>r})\cap\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)\not=\emptyset
.

In
loc. cit.
DeBacker (following Moy-Prasad) call such cosets degenerate. We choose the term “nilpotent” here, since it relates very well with other usage of nilpotency (but degeneracy can mean several different things). We also remark that we tend to think of
c
as if it was an element in
\mathfrak{g}(F)
. This indeed can be realized if
F
is a local function field, and that the coset is nilpotent iff it is nilpotent in
\mathfrak{g}(F)
. In any case we will usually write
c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>r}
(instead of just
c
) to emphasize the coset as a subset of
\mathfrak{g}(F)
. Recall the notation
C_{G,r}=\{(\varsigma,c)\;|\;\varsigma\in\Phi^{G}(r),c\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma=r}\}
. Let us also write

C_{G,r}^{nil}=\{(\varsigma,c)\in C_{G,r}\;|\;c\text{ is nilpotent}\}.

For any
(\varsigma,c)\in C_{G,r}\in C_{H,r}^{nil}
,
[
DeB02b
, Cor. 5.2.5, Lemma 5.3.3]
asserts the existence of a unique minimal nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}(\varsigma,c)
such that
c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>r}\cap\mathcal{O}(\varsigma,c)\not=\emptyset
.
Whenever
C\subset C_{G,r}^{nil}
is a subset, we denote by

\operatorname{DB}_{r}(C)=\{\mathcal{O}(\varsigma,c)\;|\;(\varsigma,c)\in C\}.

For two subsets
S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim
we say
S_{1}\equiv S_{2}
iff, for every
\mathcal{O}_{i}\in S_{i}
there exists
\mathcal{O}_{j}\in S_{j}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{i}\leq\mathcal{O}_{j}
, for any
i,j=1,2
. We have

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}(F)
with
\operatorname{dep}(\gamma)=r
. For any
s<r
we have
\operatorname{DB}(c_{G,s}(\gamma))\equiv\operatorname{WF}(\gamma)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{O}_{0}\in\operatorname{DB}_{s}(c_{G,s}(\gamma))
. By definition this says there exists
\varsigma\in\mathcal{F}(\gamma)
and
c\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma=s}
such that
\gamma\in c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>s}
and
\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\varsigma,c)
. Denote by
[c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>s}]
the characteristic function of the coset. Since
\operatorname{dep}(\gamma)>s
, by Theorem
[
DeB02a
, Thm. 2.1.5]
we have

(3)

I_{\gamma}([c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>s}])=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\sim}c_{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma)I_{\mathcal{O}}([c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>s}])

We have
I_{\gamma}([c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>s}])>0
. By definition of
\mathcal{O}(\varsigma,c)
, we also have
I_{\mathcal{O}}([c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>s}])>0
iff
\mathcal{O}\geq\mathcal{O}(\varsigma,c)
. Hence we have
c_{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma)\not=0
for some
\mathcal{O}\geq\mathcal{O}(\varsigma,c)
.

To conclude that
\operatorname{DB}_{s}(c_{G,s}(\gamma))\equiv\operatorname{WF}(\gamma)
, suppose on the contrary we have
\mathcal{O}_{0}\in\operatorname{WF}(\gamma)
such that
\mathcal{O}_{0}\not\leq\mathcal{O}(\varsigma,c)
for any
(\varsigma,c)\in c_{G,s}(\gamma)
. By
[
DeB02b
, Thm. 5.6.1]
we can write
\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\varsigma^{\prime},c^{\prime})
for some
(\varsigma^{\prime},c^{\prime})\in C_{G,s}
. Applying the last equation to
[c^{\prime}+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma^{\prime}>s}]
, we see that the LHS of
[
DeB02a
]
vanishes while the RHS does not. This gives the desired contradiction and proves the proposition.
∎

3.
Reduction to twisted Levi

In this section we fix

(1)

A semisimple element
\gamma_{r}
that is
good
of depth
r
in the sense of
[
AR00
, Def. 5.2]
. That is, for any torus
T\subset G
such that
\gamma_{r}\in\operatorname{Lie}T
, and for every
\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)
, not necessarily defined over
F
, we have that
d\alpha(\gamma_{r})
is either
0
or has normalized valuation
r
, where a uniformizer of
F
has valuation
1
. We write
H:=Z_{G}(\gamma_{r})
and
\mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{Lie}H
.

(2)

An element
\gamma_{>r}\in\mathfrak{h}(F)_{>r}
.

We write
\gamma:=\gamma_{r}+\gamma_{>r}
. Such a decomposition is always possible for any
\gamma
and
r=\operatorname{dep}(\gamma)
by applying
[
AR00
, Prop. 5.4]
to the semisimple part of
\gamma
, and such that
H\subsetneq G
is a proper twisted Levi subgroup.
There is an embedding
\mathcal{B}(H,F)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(G,F)
that is canonical up to the action of
X_{*}(Z(H)/_{F})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
on
\mathcal{B}(H,F)
. Here
X_{*}(Z(H)/_{F})
is the group of cocharacters
\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow Z(H)
defined over
F
. We fix any such embedding
\mathcal{B}(H,F)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(G,F)
.

The key step is to apply
[
KM03
, Lemma 2.3.3]
with their
\Gamma
being our
\gamma_{r}
, their
X^{\prime}
being our
\gamma_{>r}
, their
G^{\prime}
being our
H
, and their
d(\Gamma)
being our
r
. The reference asserts that for any
x\in\mathcal{B}(G,F)
:

(4)

\gamma_{r}\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x\geq r}\Leftrightarrow x\in\mathcal{B}(H,F).

(5)

\gamma_{r}+\gamma_{>r}\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x\geq r}\Rightarrow x\in\mathcal{B}(H,F).

Note that (
4
) has the following nice application:

Corollary 3.1
.

For an
r
-facet
\varsigma\subset\mathcal{B}(G,F)
for
G
which intersects
\mathcal{B}(H,F)
non-trivially, we have
\varsigma\subset\mathcal{B}(H,F)
. In other words,
\mathcal{B}(H,F)
is the union of some
r
-facets in
\mathcal{B}(G,F)
.

Namely every
\varsigma\in\Phi^{G}(r)
is contained in an
r
-facet in
\mathcal{B}(H,r)
, which we denote by
\varsigma^{H}\in\Phi^{H}(r)
. Let
\gamma_{=r}
be the image of
\gamma_{r}
in
Z(\mathfrak{h})(F)_{=r}
. We will derive from (
4
) and (
5
) that

Proposition 3.2
.

The set
c_{G,r}(\gamma)
is determined by
c_{H,r}(\gamma_{>r})
is the following identity

(6)

c_{G,r}(\gamma)=G(F).\{(\varsigma,\,c_{H}+\gamma_{=r})\;|\;\varsigma\in\Phi^{G}(r),(\varsigma^{H},c_{H})\in c_{H,r}(\gamma_{>r})\}.

Proof.

For the “
\subset
” direction, say we have
(\varsigma,c)\in c_{G,r}(r)
. Modulo
G(F)
-action we may WLOG assume
\gamma\in c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>r}\subset\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma\geq r}
. By (
5
), this gives
\varsigma\subset\mathcal{B}(H,F)
. Since
\gamma\in\mathfrak{h}(F)
, this shows that
c=c_{H}
for some
c_{H}\in\mathfrak{h}(F)_{\varsigma^{H}=r}
, and
\gamma_{>r}\in c_{H}-\gamma_{=r}+\mathfrak{h}(F)_{\varsigma^{H}=r}
. The latter implies
(\varsigma^{H},c_{H}-\gamma_{=r})\in\underline{c}_{\mathfrak{h},r}(\gamma_{>r})
, which by definition says
(\varsigma,c)
is contained in the RHS of (
6
).

For the “
\supset
” direction, let
(\varsigma,c)\in C_{G,r}
be such that there exists
g\in G(F)
such that
g^{-1}.\varsigma\subset\mathcal{B}(H,F)
and there exists
c_{H}
such that

(1)

(\varsigma^{H},c_{H})\in c_{H,r}(\gamma_{>r})
, and

(2)

g^{-1}.c=\gamma_{=r}+c_{H}\in\mathfrak{h}(F)_{\varsigma^{H}=r}
.

That
c_{H}\in c_{H,r}(\gamma_{>r})
is to say that there exists
h\in H(F)
such that
\operatorname{Ad}(h)\gamma_{>r}\in c_{H}+\mathfrak{h}(F)_{\varsigma^{H}>r}
. Since
\gamma_{r}\in\gamma_{=r}+Z(\mathfrak{h})(F)_{>r}\subset Z(\mathfrak{h})_{\geq r}
, this implies

\operatorname{Ad}(g^{-1}h)(\gamma)=\operatorname{Ad}(g^{-1})\operatorname{Ad}(h)\gamma_{>r}+\operatorname{Ad}(g^{-1})\gamma_{r}\in c_{H}+\gamma_{=r}+\mathfrak{h}(F)_{\varsigma^{H}>r}\subset c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma>r}

which gives
(\varsigma,c)\in c_{G,r}(\gamma)
.
∎

4.
The graph
C_{G}

The goal of this section is to define the set
C_{G}
(
C
for cosets) as a glued union
C_{G}=\bigcup_{r\in\mathbb{R}}C_{G,r}
and a graph with vertex set
C_{G}
which we will use to compute
c_{G,r}(\gamma)
. In fact, we will allow some choices in defining the graph, yet any set of choices produce a graph that satisfies Theorem
4.7
below. A large part of this section comes from
[
Wal06
]
, especially §2.4 and §7
op. cit.
. Recall that
\Phi^{G}(r)
is the set of
r
-facets and
C_{G,r}:=\{(\varsigma,c)\;|\;\varsigma\in\Phi^{G}(r),\;c\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\varsigma=r}\}
.
Note that
C_{G,r}
is essentially the sets
I_{r}
in the introduction of
[
DeB02b
]
. We warn the reader that we are
not
taking the
r
-associativity relation in
[
DeB02b
, §3.3]
.

We would like to revisit the above notations by considering all
r\in\mathbb{R}
altogether, following the framework in
[
Wal06
, §2.4]
. Consider the (underlying) set
\mathcal{B}^{a}(G,F):=\mathcal{B}(G,F)\times\mathbb{R}
which we will call the
augmented building
for
G(F)
. An
augmented apartment
is
\mathcal{A}^{a}:=\mathcal{A}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathcal{B}^{a}(G,F)
where
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{B}(G,F)
is an apartment in the usual sense. We decompose
\mathcal{B}^{a}(G,F)
into
augmented facets
in the following way: two elements
(x_{1},r_{1}),(x_{2},r_{2})\in\mathcal{B}^{a}(G,F)
are in the same augmented facets iff
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x_{1}\geq r_{1}}=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x_{2}\geq r_{2}}
and
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x_{1}>r_{1}}=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x_{2}>r_{2}}
. Denote by
\Phi^{G}
the set of augmented facets. The group
G(F)
acts on
\mathcal{B}^{a}(G,F)
by
g.(x,r):=(g.x,r)
. This action obviously acts on
\Phi^{G}
and acts transitively on the set of augmented apartments. Observe that the set of
r
-facets can be identified
\Phi^{G}(r)=\{\sigma\in\Phi^{G}\;|\;\sigma\cap\left(\mathcal{B}(G,F)\times\{r\}\right)\not=\emptyset\}
as a subset of
\Phi^{G}
. We will use the two viewpoints on
\Phi^{G}(r)
interchangeably.

For any augmented apartment
\mathcal{A}^{a}\subset\mathcal{B}^{a}(G,F)
, the collection

\Phi^{G}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\{\sigma\cap(\mathcal{A}^{a})\;|\;\sigma\subset\mathcal{B}^{a}(G,F)\text{ an augmented facet, }\sigma\cap(\mathcal{A}^{a})\not=\emptyset\}

is cut out by two types of hyperplanes: the first type is “affine root hyperplanes” which in the language of
[
Tit79
, §1.6, §1.7]
are of the form
\{(x,r)\in\mathcal{A}^{a}\;|\;\alpha(x)=r\}
for some affine root
\alpha
. The second type is
\mathcal{A}\times\{s\}
for
s\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
\mathfrak{z}_{\geq s}\supsetneq\mathfrak{z}_{>s}
where
\mathfrak{z}
is the Lie algebra of the torus
Z
associated to
\mathcal{A}
as in
[
MP94
, §2.2, §3.2]
. Note that the projection map
\mathcal{A}^{a}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{A}
sends any of the above hyperplane (of either type) isomorphically onto
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 4.1
.

We call both types of the aforementioned hyperplanes on
\mathcal{A}^{a}

critical
, and call the open half-spaces cut out by a critical hyperplane a

\mathcal{O}(x,r,c)\leq\mathcal{O}(x+s\tilde{\lambda},r+\ell s,c)
.

Proposition
5.2
asserts that
\mathcal{O}(x,r,c)
and
\mathcal{O}(x+s\tilde{\lambda},r+\ell s,c)
have the same weighted Dynkin diagram as that of
c
in the corresponding
\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\clubsuit}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{y}^{\clubsuit}
where
y=x+s\tilde{\lambda}
. Suppose
s=\frac{b}{n}
with
b,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n
coprime to
p
. Then
\tilde{\lambda}(\varpi^{b/n})
sends
\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\clubsuit}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{y}^{\clubsuit}
and maps
c
to
c
. This shows that the weighted Dynkin diagram of
c\in\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\clubsuit}
and
c\in\mathfrak{g}_{y}^{\clubsuit}
can be identified by an element in
G(F^{s})
, and consequently
\mathcal{O}(x,r,c)
and
\mathcal{O}(x+s\tilde{\lambda},r+\ell s,c)
have the same weighted Dynkin diagram, i.e. they are in the same
\operatorname{Ad}(G(F^{s}))
-orbit. This together with
\mathcal{O}(x,r,c)\leq\mathcal{O}(x+s\tilde{\lambda},r+\ell s,c)
shows that they are the same
\operatorname{Ad}(G(F))
-orbit.
∎

Suppose we have another nilpotent element
c^{\prime}\in c+{}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit})
. We end this subsection with another lemma:

Lemma 5.5
.

We have that
\mathcal{O}(x,r,c)\leq\mathcal{O}(x,r,c^{\prime})
. If there is an equality, then
c^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ad}({}^{\rho}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}(k))c
.

Proof.

By definition of Moy-Prasad filtration, for
s\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
small enough we have

\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x+s\tilde{\lambda}>r+\ell s}=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x=r}\times_{{}^{\rho}_{a}\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit}}{}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit})

and thus

c^{\prime}+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x>r}\subset c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x+s\tilde{\lambda}>r+\ell s}.

By definition of
\mathcal{O}(x,r,c^{\prime})
, this gives
\mathcal{O}(x,r,c^{\prime})\geq\mathcal{O}(x+s\tilde{\lambda},r+\ell s,c)=\mathcal{O}(x,r,c)
where the last equality follows from Lemma
5.4
. Now suppose
\mathcal{O}(x,r,c^{\prime})=\mathcal{O}(x,r,c)
. By Proposition
5.2
, this shows that
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\clubsuit}
have the same weighted Dynkin diagram, i.e.
\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\clubsuit}(\bar{k}))c=\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\clubsuit}(\bar{k}))c^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
B.2
, this implies
c^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ad}({}^{\rho}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}(k))c
as desired.
∎

5.2.
Edges as in (

We continue the setup in the previous subsection and study

. Recall that one of the two constructions of the edges, namely (

\sigma
. Hence we may pick

with

is the reduced expression as in the previous subsection. We had (after some choices) tori

,

and its corresponding apartment

Let

for which

(i)

\sigma
is contained in the boundary of
\sigma^{\dagger}

(ii)

(iii)

There exists
\delta>0
such that for any

,

Apparently, if

\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\geq\sigma^{\dagger}}=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\geq\sigma}\times_{\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma}}{}^{\lambda}_{\geq 2}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma},\;\;\mathfrak{g}(F)_{>\sigma^{\dagger}}=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\geq\sigma}\times_{\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma}}{}^{\lambda}_{>2}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma},\;\;\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma^{\dagger}}={}^{\lambda}_{2}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma}

We have

Lemma 5.6
.

Every element in
c+{}^{\lambda}_{>2}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma}+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{>\sigma}=c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{>\sigma^{\dagger}}
is conjugate under
G(F)
to an element in
c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{>\sigma}

Proof.

We sketch essentially the same proof in
[

, Lemma 7.2.2 & 7.3.2]
. Note that
c\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma}={}^{\rho}_{a}\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit}
and
{}^{\lambda}_{>2}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma}={}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{>2}\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit})
. By the property of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple as in (
13

{}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{>2}\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit})=[c,{}^{\rho}_{0}({}^{\lambda}_{>0}\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit})]
. From this one deduces that every element in

is conjugate under
\exp({}^{\lambda}_{>0}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x=0})\subset\mathsf{G}_{x}(k)
to

. Consequently, every element in

is conjugate under the parahoric

to an element in

∎

The lemma is exactly what we claimed and used for Theorem
4.7
. That is, in (
11
) we put

to be the element identified with

. We highlight that Lemma
5.4
gives

\mathcal{O}(\sigma,c)=\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{\dagger},c^{\dagger}).

Remark 5.7
.

One can show that once

and

are fixed,

does not depend on the choice of

up to the action of the stabilizer of

in

. As in Remark

to all such

5.3.
Edges as in (
10

We turn to the edges defined in (
10

\sigma
and another horizontal augmented facet
\sigma^{\prime}
below it, following the choice made after Definition
4.8
.
From now on, let
(x,r)\in\sigma^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}^{a}_{(p)}
be any choice. Here
\mathcal{A}^{a}_{(p)}:=\mathcal{A}_{(p)}\times\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is any augmented apartment intersecting
\sigma
and thus
\sigma^{\prime}
. Say
S\subset G
is the corresponding maximal
F
-split torus. There exists
\tilde{\lambda}\in X_{*}(S)
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that there exists
\delta>0
such that
0<s<\delta\implies(x,r)+(s\tilde{\lambda},-\ell s)\in\sigma
. We have
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{G}_{x}
the reductive quotient of
S
, with
X_{*}(S)=X_{*}(\mathsf{S})
so that we denote by
\lambda\in X_{*}(\mathsf{S})
the image of
\tilde{\lambda}
. Unfolding the definition of Moy-Prasad filtration we have

(18)

\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\geq\sigma}=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\geq\sigma^{\prime}}\times_{\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{}^{\lambda}_{\leq\ell}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma^{\prime}},\;\;\mathfrak{g}(F)_{>\sigma}=\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\geq\sigma^{\prime}}\times_{\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma^{\prime}},\;\;\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma}={}^{\lambda}_{\ell}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma^{\prime}}.

By (
10
), we draw an edge from
(\sigma,c)
to
(\sigma^{\prime},c^{\prime})
for any
c^{\prime}\in c+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{>\sigma}/\mathfrak{g}(F)_{>\sigma^{\prime}}
, i.e. for any
c^{\prime}\in c+{}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma^{\prime}}
. Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be the denominator of

as in §
5.1
, we have that
c\in{}^{\lambda}_{\ell}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma^{\prime}}={}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{\ell}\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit})
and
{}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma^{\prime}}={}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit})
. Recall also that
{}^{\rho}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}=\mathsf{G}_{x}
. As in Remark
5.7
we are happy to conjugate our elements by
\mathsf{G}_{x}
whenever applicable. This brings us to the geometric problem, which we highlight below:

Problem 5.8
.

Given

(1)

A connected reductive group
\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}
over our finite field
k
with
\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit}:=\operatorname{Lie}\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}
,

(2)

An integer
m>0
prime to
p
and an algebraic group homomorphism
\rho:\mu_{m}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit})
,

(3)

A cocharcter
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow{}^{\rho}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}
, a residue class
a\in\mathbb{Z}/m
, an integer
\ell>0
, and an element
c\in{}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{\ell}\mathfrak{g}^{\heartsuit}_{x})
.

What are the
\operatorname{Ad}({}^{\rho}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}(k))
-orbits in
{}^{\rho}_{a}\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit}
that intersects
c+{}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}^{\heartsuit}_{x})
?

Consider any
c^{\prime}\in c+{}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}^{\heartsuit}_{x})
. We begin by the simpler situation when
c^{\prime}
is nilpotent. Recall that
\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{\prime},c^{\prime})
is the minimal
\operatorname{Ad}(G(F))
-orbit that intersects
c^{\prime}+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{>\sigma^{\prime}}=c^{\prime}+\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x>r}
. We have

Corollary 5.9
.

Suppose
c^{\prime}\in c+{}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}^{\heartsuit}_{x})
is nilpotent. Then
\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{\prime},c^{\prime})\geq\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{\prime},c)=\mathcal{O}(\sigma,c)
. When the equality holds, we have
c^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ad}({}^{\rho}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}(k))c=\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}(k))c
.

Proof.

The equality
\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{\prime},c)=\mathcal{O}(\sigma,c)
comes from Lemma
5.4
while the rest of the corollary come from Lemma
5.5
.
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

Under the identification
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma}={}^{\lambda}_{\ell}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{\sigma^{\prime}}
there is an edge from
(\sigma,c)
to
(\sigma^{\prime},c)
, with
\mathcal{O}(\sigma,c)=\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{\prime},c)
. Any edge from
(\sigma,c)
to
(\sigma^{\prime},c^{\prime})
such that
c^{\prime}
is nilpotent and
\mathcal{O}(\sigma,c)=\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{\prime},c^{\prime})
is
G(F)
-conjugate to the edge in the first sentence.

Proof.

That there is an edge from
(\sigma,c)
to
(\sigma^{\prime},c)
follows immediately from definition. The rest of the statements follows from Corollary
5.9
.
∎

Now we turn the case of non-nilpotent
c^{\prime}\in c+{}^{\rho}_{a}({}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}^{\heartsuit}_{x})
, for which we can get weaker results. Firstly we have a minor generalization of
[
CO23
, Prop. 3.6]
(in
loc. cit.
it is assumed that
\lambda
is associated to an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple completing
c
), which is just a rephrasement of Lemma
B.2
:

Lemma 5.11
.

The element
c
is contained in the Zariski closure of
\bar{k}\cdot\operatorname{Ad}(({}^{\rho}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit})(\bar{k}))c^{\prime}
.

Because the dimension of centralizer for an element in a Lie algebra is lower semi-continuous (it is the rank of the adjoint action), the lemma implies

Corollary 5.12
.

We have
\dim\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}(\bar{k}))c^{\prime}\geq\dim\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}(\bar{k}))c
.

Let
c^{\prime}=c_{s}^{\prime}+c_{n}^{\prime}
be the Jordan decomposition. Consider the nilpotent geometric
\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit})(\bar{k})
-orbit
N(c^{\prime})\subset\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit}
given by the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction of the orbit
\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}(\bar{k}))c_{n}^{\prime}
from
Z_{\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}}(c_{s}^{\prime})
to
\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}
; see Definition
B.3
. Ciubotaru and Okada observed
[
CO23
, Lemma 4.2]
that the closure of

is the intersection of
\bar{k}\cdot\operatorname{Ad}(({}^{\rho}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit})(\bar{k}))c^{\prime}
with the nilpotent cone of
\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit}
. With Lemma
5.11
we have

Corollary 5.13
.

(
[
CO23
, Lemma 4.2]
) The element
c
is contained in the Zariski closure of
N(c^{\prime})
.

Note that
N(c^{\prime})
is only a
\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}(\bar{k})
-orbit, not a
({}^{\rho}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit})(\bar{k})
-orbit. In general
N(c^{\prime})\cap{}^{\rho}_{a}(\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\heartsuit})(\bar{k})
is the closure of the union of a number of
\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}(\bar{k})
-orbits, say
\{N^{\rho}_{1}(c^{\prime}),...,N^{\rho}_{n}(c^{\prime})\}
. Recall that
(x,r)\in\sigma^{\prime}
. Similar to before we denote by
\mathcal{O}^{ur}(\sigma^{\prime},N^{\rho}_{i}(c^{\prime}))
the minimal nilpotent
\operatorname{Ad}(G(F^{ur}))
-orbit that meets
N^{\rho}_{i}(c^{\prime})+\mathfrak{g}(F^{ur})_{>\sigma^{\prime}}
. We have the following corollary to Corollary
5.13
, which is implicit in
[
CO23
, §5]
:

Corollary 5.14
.

We have
\mathcal{O}(\sigma,c)=\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{\prime},c)
is contained in the
p
-adic closure of one of
\mathcal{O}^{ur}(\sigma^{\prime},N^{\rho}_{i}(c^{\prime}))
(
1\leq i\leq n
) above.

Proof.

By definition
c
is contained in the Zariski closure of one of
N^{\rho}_{i}(c^{\prime})
. Thanks to Corollary
5.3
, we have
\operatorname{Ad}(G(F^{ur}))\mathcal{O}(\sigma,c)=\mathcal{O}^{ur}(\sigma,c)
. The latter is contained in the
p
-adic closure of
\mathcal{O}^{ur}(\sigma^{\prime},N^{\rho}_{i}(c^{\prime}))
thanks to Lemma
5.5
applied with
F
replaced by
F^{ur}
.
∎

Corollary
5.9
to Corollary
5.14
all give necessary conditions for the existence of an edge in
C_{G}
from
(\sigma,c)
to
(\sigma^{\prime},c^{\prime})
, with the exception of Corollary
5.10
which also contains a rather naive existence result. When

, we have

so that
\rho
is trivial. In this case we will prove a stronger existence result. To accurately state what we can achieve we need more notions. Define an equivalence relation
\sim_{semi}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim
as follows:

Definition 5.15
.

We denote by
\sim_{semi}
the equivalence relation on
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim
generated by the following relation: For
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim
we declare
\mathcal{O}_{1}\sim_{semi}\mathcal{O}_{2}
if there exist
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x\in\mathcal{B}(G,F)
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x=r}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{i}=\mathcal{O}(x,r,c_{i})
and
\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}(\bar{k}))c_{1}=\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}(\bar{k}))c_{2}
.

The following lemma will not be used in this article; it is just there so that Definition
5.15
looks slightly simpler.

Lemma 5.16
.

In Definition
5.15
, it suffices to take
r=0
, i.e. those relations given by
r=0
generate the same equivalence relation.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim
,
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x\in\mathcal{B}(G,F)
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x=r}
are such that
\mathcal{O}_{i}=\mathcal{O}(x,r,c_{i})
and
\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}(\bar{k}))c_{1}=\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{x}(\bar{k}))c_{2}
. Then
c_{1},c_{2}
has the same weighted Dynkin diagram in
\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x=r}\cong\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x=0}\cong\operatorname{Lie}\mathsf{G}_{x}
. Hence their
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples have the same associated cocharacter. In particular there exists
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}/_{k}\rightarrow\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{G}_{x}
such that
c_{1},c_{2}\in{}^{\lambda}_{2}\mathfrak{g}(F)_{x=r}
, and that
\operatorname{Ad}({}^{\lambda}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}(\bar{k}))c_{1}=\operatorname{Ad}({}^{\lambda}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}(\bar{k}))c_{2}
. Identify
\lambda
as
\tilde{\lambda}\in X_{*}(S)
where
S\subset G
is a maximal
F
-split torus with reductive quotient
\mathsf{S}
and such that the corresponding apartment contains
x
. Then Lemma
5.4
gives
\mathcal{O}(x,r,c_{i})=\mathcal{O}(x-\frac{r}{2}\tilde{\lambda},0,c_{i})
. Write
y=x-\frac{r}{2}\tilde{\lambda}
. We have
{}^{\lambda}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{y}={}^{\lambda}_{0}\mathsf{G}_{x}
. Thus
\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{y}(\bar{k}))c_{1}=\operatorname{Ad}(\mathsf{G}_{y}(\bar{k}))c_{2}
and by replacing
x
with
y
we have reduced to the case
r=0
.
∎

Definition 5.17
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim_{semi}
the quotient set of
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim
by
\sim_{semi}
. We give
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F)/\!\!\sim_{semi}
the partial order generated by that in
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}
, i.e. for any
\mathcal{O}_{1}\leq\mathcal{O}_{2}

For
(\sigma,c)\in C_{G}
or
(x,r)\in\mathcal{B}^{a}(G,F)
,

we denote by
\mathcal{O}^{semi}(\sigma,c)
(resp.
\mathcal{O}^{semi}(x,r,c)
) the image of
\mathcal{O}(\sigma,c)
(resp.
\mathcal{O}(x,r,c)
) under the quotient map. For

among the image of

under the quotient map. We call

the

.

Denote by
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(F^{ur})/\!\!\sim

]
shows that an element in

needs not match with an element in

.

In the example when

is an unramified unitary group,

We have the following variant of Corollary
5.14
whose proof is identical.

Corollary 5.19
.

Suppose

. Then we have
\mathcal{O}(\sigma,c)=\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{\prime},c)
is contained in the closure of one of

.

(Corollary

) Suppose

.

With the notations in Theorem

, up to conjugating by an element in
\mathsf{G}_{x}^{\heartsuit}(k)=\mathsf{G}_{x}(k)

-triple completing

and

\bar{k}\cdot\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0}(\bar{k}))c^{\prime}
. If furthermore
c^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ad}(G(\bar{k}))c
, then
c^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ad}({}^{\lambda}_{<0}G_{0}(k))c
.

Proof.

We claim that any element in
c+{}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}_{a}
is in the same
\operatorname{Ad}({}^{\lambda}_{<0}G_{0}(k))
-orbit as some element in
c+V
. The claim together with Lemma
B.1
finishes the proof. We use induction for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
on the statement that any element in
c+{}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}_{a}
is in the same
\operatorname{Ad}({}^{\lambda}_{<0}G_{0}(k))
-orbit as some element in
c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq\ell-j}\mathfrak{g}_{a}+V
. For
j
large enough this proves the lemma. The statement is trivially true for
j=1
. For a general element in
c+{}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g}_{a}
for which we already conjugate it by
{}^{\lambda}_{<0}G_{0}(k)
to
c_{j}\in c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq\ell-j}\mathfrak{g}_{a}+V
, we would like to conjugate it again so that it lives in
c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq\ell-j-1}\mathfrak{g}_{a}+V
. Denote by
{}^{\lambda}_{\ell-j}(c_{j})
the projection of
c_{j}
from
\mathfrak{g}_{a}=\bigoplus{}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathfrak{g}_{a}
to
{}^{\lambda}_{\ell-j}\mathfrak{g}_{a}
.
By the construction of
V_{\ell-j}
, there exists an
y\in{}^{\lambda}_{-j}\mathfrak{g}_{0}(k)
such that
{}^{\lambda}_{\ell-j}(c_{j})+[y,c]\in V_{\ell-j}\subset{}^{\lambda}_{\ell-j}\mathfrak{g}_{a}
. This implies that
\exp(y)c_{j}\in c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq\ell-j-1}\mathfrak{g}_{a}+V
, as desired.
∎

B.1.
Existence of rational points

From now on until Proposition
B.13
, we will not make use of the action
\rho:\mu_{m}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(G)
. Consider
x\in\mathfrak{g}(k)
with the Jordan decomposition
x=x_{s}+x_{n}
. We say
x

splits
over a field extension
k^{\prime}/k
if
L:=Z_{G}(x_{s})
is a Levi subgroup over
k^{\prime}
, i.e.
L
is contained in a parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
defined over
k^{\prime}
, and just say
x
splits if it does so over
k
. Apparently
x
splits over
\bar{k}
. Let
P
be as above (defined over
\bar{k}
) and
\mathfrak{u}_{P}
be the Lie algebra of its unipotent radical.

Definition B.3
.

[
LS79
]
We denote by
N(x)
unique nilpotent
\operatorname{Ad}(G(\bar{k}))
-orbit whose intersection with
x_{n}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}(\bar{k})
is a dense open subset of the latter.

The orbit
N(x)
is the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction of
x_{n}
from
L
to
G
. Following
[
CO23
, §4]
, consider the variety
\Theta(x)
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
(c,h,d)\in\mathfrak{g}(\bar{k})^{3}
such that

(1)

c,d\in N(x)
, and

(2)

x\in c+Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(d)
is in the so-called Slodowy slice.

The first goal of this section is to show the following generalization of
[
CO23
, Lemma 4.5]
.

Theorem B.4
.

The connected centralizer
Z_{G}(x)^{o}
acts transitively on
\Theta(x)
. Hence
\Theta(x)(k)
is non-empty.

Remark B.5
.

The second sentence of the theorem follows from the first thanks to Lang’s theorem. The result is essentially that every element is good in the sense of
[
CO23
, §4]
. It also gives a different proof of
[
Kat82
, Thm. 0.3]
(also used in
[
CO23
, §4]
), which is equivalent to that
\Theta(x)
is a
Z_{G}(x)
-orbit.

Let
C(\mathfrak{g}(k))
be the space of
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on
\mathfrak{g}(k)
. We fix a
G(k)
-invariant non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot):\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}
and a non-trivial additive character
\psi:k\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. For
f,f^{\prime}\in C(\mathfrak{g}(k))
, write

\langle f,f^{\prime}\rangle:=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{g}(k)}f(x)f^{\prime}(x),\;\;\hat{f}(y):=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{g}(k)}\psi(B(x,y))f(x).

Let
J(\mathfrak{g}(k))\subset C(\mathfrak{g}(k))
be the subspace of
\operatorname{Ad}(G(k))
-invariant functions, and
J(\mathfrak{g}(k))^{nil}\subset J(\mathfrak{g}(k))
be the subspace of those supported on
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(k)
, the nilpotent cone. The Fourier transform
f\mapsto\hat{f}
maps
J(\mathfrak{g}(k))
isomorphically to itself, and let
J(\mathfrak{g}(k))^{conil}
be the image of
J(\mathfrak{g}(k))^{nil}
under the Fourier transform. For any sub-
k
-variety
X\subset\mathfrak{g}
, let
I_{X}
be the function that takes the value
1
on
X(k)
and zero elsewhere. The idea of the following proposition comes from Springer theory, particularly
[
Spr76
, Thm. 4.4]
. See also the MathOverflow question
[
Tsa
]
.

Proposition B.6
.

Let
x
be split with
L=Z_{G}(x_{s})
and
P
a parabolic subgroup containing
L
and defined over
k
. We have

(34)

|U_{P}(k)|\cdot\langle I_{x},\xi\rangle=\langle I_{x_{n}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}},\xi\rangle,\;\forall\xi\in J(\mathfrak{g}(k))^{conil}.

Proof.

We have
I_{x+\mathfrak{u}_{P}}(y)=\sum_{u\in U_{P}(k)}I_{x}(\operatorname{Ad}(u)y)
and thus

(35)

|\mathfrak{u}_{P}(k)|\cdot\langle I_{x},\xi\rangle=\langle I_{x+\mathfrak{u}_{P}},\xi\rangle,\;\forall\xi\in J(\mathfrak{g}(k)).

We compare
I_{x+\mathfrak{u}_{P}}
and
I_{x_{n}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}}
by looking at Fourier transforms. The two functions are apparently invariant under translation by
\mathfrak{u}_{P}(k)
. Thus their Fourier transforms are supported on
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{P}
where
\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{Lie}P
and
\mathfrak{l}=\operatorname{Lie}L
. Note that
x-x_{n}=x_{s}\in Z(\mathfrak{l})
. Since
Z(\mathfrak{l})
is necessarily perpendicular to
\mathfrak{l}^{der}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}
under our bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
, we have that
\hat{I}_{x+\mathfrak{u}_{P}}
and
\hat{I}_{x_{n}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}}
have the same restriction on
\mathfrak{l}^{der}(k)+\mathfrak{u}_{P}(k)
. Since all nilpotent elements in
\mathfrak{p}
lies in
\mathfrak{l}^{der}(k)+\mathfrak{u}_{P}(k)
, this shows that
\hat{I}_{x+\mathfrak{u}_{P}}
and
\hat{I}_{x_{n}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}}
have the same restriction to the nilpotent cone
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(k)
. This implies that

\langle\hat{I}_{x+\mathfrak{u}_{P}},\xi\rangle=\langle\hat{I}_{x_{n}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}},\xi\rangle,\;\forall\xi\in J(\mathfrak{g}(k))^{nil}.

which by Fourier inversion is equivalent to

\langle I_{x+\mathfrak{u}_{P}},\xi\rangle=\langle I_{x_{n}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}},\xi\rangle,\;\forall\xi\in J(\mathfrak{g}(k))^{conil}

which together with (
35
) implies (
34
).
∎

By our assumption on
\operatorname{char}(k)
, for any
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
(c,h,d)
there is a cocharacter
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow G
associated to
(c,h,d)
such that
c\in{}^{\lambda}_{2}\mathfrak{g}
(the weight
2
subspace),
h\in{}^{\lambda}_{0}\mathfrak{g}
and
d\in{}^{\lambda}_{-2}\mathfrak{g}
, and

(1)

\operatorname{ad}(c)
maps
{}^{\lambda}_{\leq-1}\mathfrak{g}
injectively into
{}^{\lambda}_{\leq 1}\mathfrak{g}
.

(2)

{}^{\lambda}_{\leq 1}\mathfrak{g}=Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(d)\oplus[c,{}^{\lambda}_{\leq-1}\mathfrak{g}]
.

Let
{}^{\lambda}_{\leq-1}G
be the unipotent subgroup of
G
whose Lie algebra is
{}^{\lambda}_{\leq-1}\mathfrak{g}
; we can also characterize
{}^{\lambda}_{\leq-1}G
as the unipotent subgroup of the parabolic subgroup determined by
\lambda
. The following lemma is somewhat standard and can be proved analogous to Lemma
B.2
(with extra care to prove the uniqueness part).

Lemma B.7
.

For every element
y\in c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq 1}\mathfrak{g}(k)
, there is a unique element
u\in({}^{\lambda}_{\leq-1}G)(k)
such that
\operatorname{Ad}(u)y\in c+Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(d)
.

For any
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
(c,h,d)
denote by
f_{c,h,d}\in C(\mathfrak{g}(k))
the function with
f_{c,h,d}(x)=\sum_{g\in G(k)}I_{c+Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(d)}(\operatorname{Ad}(g)x)
.

Lemma B.8
.

We have
f_{c,h,d}\in J(\mathfrak{g}(k))^{conil}
.

Proof.

As before let
I_{c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq 1}\mathfrak{g}}\in C(\mathfrak{g}(k))
be the function that takes the value
1
on
c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq 1}\mathfrak{g}(k)
and
0
elsewhere. Since
I_{c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq 1}\mathfrak{g}}
is invariant under translation by
{}^{\lambda}_{\leq 1}\mathfrak{g}(k)
, its Fourier transform
\hat{I}_{c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq 1}\mathfrak{g}}
is supported on
{}^{\lambda}_{\leq-2}\mathfrak{g}(k)
, in particular in
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(k)
. Lemma
B.7
implies that

f_{c,h,d}(x)=\sum_{g\in G(k)}I_{c+Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(d)}(\operatorname{Ad}(g)x)=\frac{1}{|({}^{\lambda}_{\leq-1}G)(k)|}\sum_{g\in G(k)}I_{c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq 1}\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ad}(g)x).

The Fourier transform
\hat{\xi}
is thus a sum of conjugates of
\hat{I}_{c+{}^{\lambda}_{\leq 1}\mathfrak{g}}
, all supported in
\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(k)
, i.e.
\hat{f}_{c,h,d}\in J(\mathfrak{g}(k))^{nil}
and thus
f_{c,h,d}\in J(\mathfrak{g}(k))^{conil}
.
∎

We also need more lemmas regarding
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples:

Lemma B.9
.

The centralizer
Z_{G}(c)
acts transitively on the set of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
(c,h^{\prime},d^{\prime})
completing

, and for any such we have
\pi_{0}(Z_{G}(c,h^{\prime},d^{\prime}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_{0}(Z_{G}(c))
.

Lastly, let us recall the following which relies on a little bit of Weil Conjectures.

Lemma B.10
.

A variety
X
over
k
has dimension
\delta
and only one geometric component of dimension
\delta
if and only if there exists a constant
C
and a finite extension
k^{\dagger}/k
such that
|X(k^{\prime})-|k^{\prime}|^{\delta}|<C\cdot|k^{\prime}|^{\delta-1}
for any finite extension
k^{\prime}/k^{\dagger}
.

For our convenience, let us denote the second condition in the lemma by
|X(k^{\prime})|\sim|k^{\prime}|^{\delta}
. To prove Theorem
B.4
, we first prove a weaker version of it:

Proposition B.11
.

Let
c\in N(x)
. The variety
\Theta(x)
has dimension
\delta_{c}:=\dim Z_{G}(c)/Z_{G}(c,h,d)
and has only one geometric component of this dimension.

Proof.

Consider the case
x
is nilpotent, i.e.
x=c
. Then
N(c)=\operatorname{Ad}(G(\bar{k}))c
. By Lemma
B.1
applied to our case without
\rho
(i.e.
\rho
is trivial),
\Theta(c)
is equal to the variety of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
(c,h^{\prime},d^{\prime})
completing
c
, on which
Z_{G}(c)
acts transitively by Lemma
B.9
. Moreover, we have
\pi_{0}(Z_{G}(c,h^{\prime},d^{\prime}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_{0}(Z_{G}(c))
and that
Z_{G}(c)
acts transitively on
\Theta(c)
with stabilizer at
(c,h,d)\in\Theta(c)
equal to
Z_{G}(c,h,d)
, i.e. we have
Z_{G}(c)/Z_{G}(c,h,d)\cong Z_{G}(c)^{o}/Z_{G}(c,h,d)^{o}\cong\Theta(c)
. Thanks to Lemma
B.10
this proves the proposition for
x=c
, i.e.
|\Theta(c)(k^{\prime})|\sim|k^{\prime}|^{\delta_{c}}
.

For
y\in\mathfrak{g}(k)
, denote by
\Xi_{x}(y)
the set of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
(c,h,d)
with
c\in N(x)
and such that
y\in c+Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(d)
. Hence
\Theta(x)=\Xi_{x}(x)
. The
k
-points in
N(x)
is a finite number of
\operatorname{Ad}(G(k))
-orbits, say
c_{1},c_{2},..,c_{m}
. We can complete each of them to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
(c_{i},h_{i},d_{i})
. By Lemma
B.9
,
\Xi_{x}(y)(k)
is the disjoint union of the set of
\operatorname{Ad}(G(k))
-conjugates
(c,h,d)
of
(c_{i},h_{i},d_{i})
such that
x\in c+Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(d)
. Hence for any
y\in\mathfrak{g}(k)
we have

(36)

|\Xi_{x}(y)(k)|=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|Z_{G}(c_{i},h_{i},d_{i})(k)|}\cdot\langle I_{y},f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}}\rangle.

For general
x
, the statement to prove is geometric and there is no harm to perform a base change on the ground field
k
. In particular, we may and shall assume that
x
is split. Plug
y=x
into (
36
) and apply Proposition
B.6
and Lemma
B.8
to the RHS, we have

(37)

|\mathfrak{u}_{P}(k)|\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|Z_{G}(c_{i},h_{i},d_{i})(k)|}\cdot\langle I_{x},f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}}\rangle=\sum_{u\in\mathfrak{u}_{P}(k)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|Z_{G}(c_{i},h_{i},d_{i})(k)|}\cdot\langle I_{x_{n}+u},f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}}\rangle.

Using (
36
) for both
y=x
and
y=x_{n}+u
, we have

|\mathfrak{u}_{P}(k)|\cdot|\Xi_{x}(x)(k)|=\sum_{u\in\mathfrak{u}_{P}(k)}|\Xi_{x}(x_{n}+u)(k)|

By definition of the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction
N(x)
, the element
x_{n}+u
is either in
N(x)
(for
u
in a dense subset of
\mathfrak{u}_{P}
) or is contained in an orbit of a smaller dimension. In the latter case, the orbit of
x_{n}+u
must not meet
c_{i}+Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(d_{i})
for any nilpotent orbit meeting
c_{i}+Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(d_{i})
contains
c_{i}
in its closure, i.e.
\Xi_{x}(x_{n}+u)=\emptyset
when
x_{n}+u
is in an orbit of a smaller dimension. For the other “generic”

we have
\Xi_{x}(x_{n}+u)=\Theta(x_{n}+u)
with
|\Theta(x_{n}+u)(k^{\prime})|\sim|k^{\prime}|^{\delta_{c}}
. Hence we have

|\mathfrak{u}_{P}(k^{\prime})|\cdot|\Theta(x)(k^{\prime})|\sim|k^{\prime}|^{\delta_{c}+\dim\mathfrak{u}_{P}}\implies|\Theta(x)(k^{\prime})|\sim|k^{\prime}|^{\delta_{c}},

which proves the proposition.
∎

Proof of Theorem
B.4
.

The centralizer
Z_{G}(x)
acts on
\Theta(x)
. Since
\dim Z_{G}(x)=\dim Z_{G}(c)
for
c\in N(x)

[
LS79
, Thm. 1.3(a)]
, any
Z_{G}(x)
-orbit on
\Theta(x)
is a subvariety of dimension at least
\dim Z_{G}(x)-\dim Z_{G}(c,h,d)=\delta_{c}
(for
(c,h,d)\in\Theta(x)
). By Proposition
B.11
there can be at most one such orbit, and the orbit has to be geometrically connected, i.e. it has to be a
Z_{G}(x)^{o}
-orbit. Hence we are done.
∎

We view Theorem
B.4
as saying that any
x\in\mathfrak{g}(k)
is “supported” by a nilpotent element in the following sense:

Definition B.12
.

For
x\in\mathfrak{g}(k)
,
c\in\mathfrak{g}^{nil}(k)
, let us say
c

supports

x
if we can complete it to an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(c,h,d)\in\Theta(x)(k)
.

Let us consider the homomorphism
\rho:\mu_{m}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(G)
again and fix
a\in\mathbb{Z}/m
. The
G
-invariant form
B(\cdot,\cdot):\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}
necessarily restricts to a perfect pairing
\mathfrak{g}_{a}\times\mathfrak{g}_{-a}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}
. (Here
\mathbb{G}_{a}
is the standard additive group scheme and has a priori nothing to do with
\mathfrak{g}_{a}
; we apologize for this funny notational clash.) Let
C(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))
be the set of
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on
\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k)
. For
f\in C(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))
we put

\hat{f}(y)=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k)}\psi(B(x,y))f(x),\;\forall y\in\mathfrak{g}_{-a}(k)

so that
f\mapsto\hat{f}
defines a
G_{0}(k)
-equivariant isomorphism
C(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))\xrightarrow{\sim}C(\mathfrak{g}_{-a}(k))
. Let
J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))\subset C(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))
be the subspace of
G_{0}(k)
-invariant functions so that Fourier transform again gives
J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))\xrightarrow{\sim}J(\mathfrak{g}_{-a}(k))
. Denote again by
J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))^{nil}\subset J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))
the subspace of those supported on nilpotent elements and
J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))^{conil}
the image of
J(\mathfrak{g}_{-a}(k))^{nil}
under Fourier transform (note the subscript
-a
).

Proposition B.13
.

The map
f\mapsto f|_{\mathfrak{g}_{a}^{nil}(k)}
induces an isomorphism
J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))^{conil}\xrightarrow{\sim}J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))^{nil}
.

Proof.

Let
c_{1},c_{2},...
be a set (apparently finite) of representatives of
G_{0}(k)
-orbits in
\mathfrak{g}_{a}^{nil}(k)
, ordered so that
\dim\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0})c_{i}\leq\dim\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0})c_{j}
for
i\leq j
. We complete each
c_{i}
into an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(c_{i},h_{i},d_{i})
with
h_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,
d_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-a}
. Consider
I_{c_{i}+Z_{\mathfrak{g}_{a}}(d_{i})}
the function with value
1
on
c_{i}+Z_{\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k)}d_{i}
and
0
elsewhere, and
f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}}\in J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))
with
f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}}(x)=\sum_{g\in G_{0}(k)}I_{c_{i}+Z_{\mathfrak{g}_{a}}}(\operatorname{Ad}(g)x)
. By the same proof as in Lemma
B.8
we have
f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}}\in J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))^{conil}
.

By Lemma
B.1
,

\operatorname{supp}(f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}})\cap\mathfrak{g}_{a}^{nil}
is the union of
\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0}(k))c_{i}
and some
\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0}(k))c_{j}
lying in larger
\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0})
-orbits. In particular
\operatorname{supp}(f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}})
must be disjoint from
\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0}(k))c_{i}
for
j<i
. This proves that
f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{a}^{nil}(k)}
form a basis of
J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))^{nil}
. In particular, the map
J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))^{conil}\rightarrow J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))^{nil}
given by restriction is surjective. Hence it is an isomorphism as both sides have the same dimension over the coefficient field
\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Corollary B.14
.

Suppose a function
f\in J(\mathfrak{g}_{a}(k))^{conil}
takes values in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Suppose we have some
c\in\operatorname{supp}(f)\cap\mathfrak{g}_{a}^{nil}
, and that
c^{\prime}\in\operatorname{supp}(f)\cap\mathfrak{g}_{a}^{nil}
only if
c\in\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0}(k))c^{\prime}
or
c
is contained in the Zariski boundary of
\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0}(\bar{k}))c^{\prime}
. Then there exist

(1)

Nilpotent elements
c_{1},...,c_{r}
such that
c
is contained in the Zariski boundary of
\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0}(\bar{k}))c_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq r
. Each is completed into an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(c_{i},h_{i},d_{i})
. We also complete

to
(c,h,d)
. All these
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples are defined over
k
.

(2)

Constants
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{r}\in\mathbb{R}

such that

f=\alpha\cdot f_{c,h,d}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\cdot f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}}.

Proof.

This follows from the property established in the proof of Proposition
B.13
that
f_{c_{i},h_{i},d_{i}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{a}^{nil}(k)}
is supported at
\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0}(k))c_{i}
and a number of
\operatorname{Ad}(G_{0}(k))
-orbits with strictly larger geometric orbits.
∎

The point of the previous and the next corollary is that they give our template for test functions:

Corollary B.15
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

The Fourier transform of

and thus

. The rest follows from Lemma

.
∎

In the next corollary we go back to the case when

Corollary B.16
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}/_{k}\rightarrow G_{0}
be any cocharacter, and

. Suppose

(Definition

\operatorname{Ad}(G(k))x\cap(c+{}^{\lambda}_{<\ell}\mathfrak{g})\not=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Apply Corollary
B.14
to
\langle I_{x},f_{\lambda,c}\rangle
where
f_{\lambda,c}
is as in Corollary

The Far-/Near-Field Beam Squint and Solutions for THz Intelligent Reflecting Surface Communications
Wanming Hao, Member, IEEE, Xiaobei You, Fuhui Zhou, Senior Member, IEEE, Zheng Chu, Member, IEEE, Gangcan Sun, Pei Xiao, Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract
Terahertz (THz) and intelligent reflecting surface (IRS) have been regarded as two promising technologies to improve the capacity and coverage for future 6G networks. Generally, IRS is usually equipped with large-scale elements when implemented at THz frequency. In this case, the near-field model and beam squint should be considered. Therefore, in this paper, we investigate the far-field and near-field beam squint problems in THz IRS communications for the first time. The far-field and near-field channel models are constructed based on the different electromagnetic radiation characteristics. Next, we first analyze the far-field beam squint and its effect for the beam gain based on the cascaded base station (BS)-IRS-user channel model, and then the near-field case is studied. To overcome the far-field and near-field beam squint effects, we propose to apply delay adjustable metasurface (DAM) to IRS, and develop a scheme of optimizing the reflecting phase shifts and time delays of IRS elements, which effectively eliminates the beam gain loss caused by beam squint. Finally, simulations are conducted to demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed schemes in combating the near and far field beam squint.

To our best knowledge, this has not yet been investigated to date.

To fill this gap, in this paper, we first analyze the far-field and near-field channel models and beam squints in THz IRS communications, respectively. Specifically, by theoretical derivation, the far-field beam squint makes that the beams reflected by IRS at different subcarriers squint toward different directions, while the near-field beam squint makes that the beams reflected by IRS at different subcarriers aim at different locations and most of beams fail to focus on the user. Next, to deal with the far-field and near-field beam squint effects, we propose an effective scheme based on the delay adjustable metasurface (DAM)

On this basis, we propose a scheme of jointly optimizing the reflection phase shifts of IRS elements and the time delay of DAMs in far-field and near-field, respectively, which makes the beams at all subcarriers focusing on the desired direction or location.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. The far-field and near-field channel models of IRS-aided wideband THz communications are introduced, respectively, in Section II. The far-field and near-field beam squint effects are revealed, respectively, in Section III. In Section IV, we describe our proposed schemes for overcoming the far-field and near-field beam squint effects, respectively. Section V presents the simulation results to verify our proposed schemes, and final conclusions are drawn in Section VI.

II-A

The Far-Field Channel Model

In the far-field, it is usually assumed that the IRS array size is much smaller than the distance between BS and IRS as well as the distance between IRS and user. Based on this, the time delay
\tau_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}
and
\tau_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}}
can be written as

\displaystyle\tau_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}=\tau_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}+(r-1)\frac{d\sin\chi_{l_{1}}}{c}=\tau_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}+(r-1)\frac{\varphi_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}}{f_{c}},

(9)

\displaystyle\tau_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}}=\tau_{l2}^{\rm{RU}}-(r-1)\frac{d\sin\psi_{l_{2}}}{c}=\tau_{l2}^{\rm{RU}}-(r-1)\frac{\varphi_{l2}^{\rm{RU}}}{f_{c}},

(10)

where

and

are the speed of light and the IRS element spacing, respectively. We set

as half wavelength, i.e.,

, where

is the carrier wavelength. For notational simplicity, we define
\tau_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}=\tau_{l1,1}^{\rm{BR}}
and
\tau_{l2}^{\rm{RU}}=\tau_{l2,1}^{\rm{RU}}

Moreover,

and

represent the angle of arrival (AOA) of the
l_{1}
-th path between the BS and IRS as well as the angle of departure (AOD) of the
l_{2}
-th path between the IRS and user, respectively. Denote
\varphi_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}
and
\varphi_{l2}^{\rm{RU}}
as the normalized AOA and AOD with the range
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
, respectively.

According to (9) and (10), the CIR of the
r
-th cascaded BS-IRS-user channel in (8) can be obtained as

\displaystyle h_{r}^{\rm{far}}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{l_{1}=1}^{L_{1}}\sum\nolimits_{l_{2}=1}^{L_{2}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{1}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}\tau_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{2}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}\tau_{l2}^{\rm{RU}}}

\displaystyle\cdot{e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}(r-1)\frac{\varphi_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}}{f_{c}}}e^{j2\pi{f_{c}}(r-1)\frac{\varphi_{l2}^{\rm{RU}}}{f_{c}}}\delta(t-\tau_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}-\tau_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}})}.

(11)

Let
\alpha_{l_{1}}={\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{1}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}\tau_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}}

\displaystyle h_{r}^{\rm{far}}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{l_{1}=1}^{L_{1}}\sum\nolimits_{l_{2}=1}^{L_{2}}\alpha_{l_{1}}\alpha_{l_{2}}e^{-j2\pi(r-1)\varphi_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}h_{r}^{\rm{far}}(t)e^{-j2\pi{f}t}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{L_{1}}\sum_{l_{2}=1}^{L_{2}}\alpha_{l_{1}}\alpha_{l_{2}}e^{-j2\pi(r-1)(\varphi_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}-\varphi_{l2}^{\rm{RU}})}e^{-j2\pi{f}(\tau_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}+\tau_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{3}=1}^{L_{1}L_{2}}\alpha_{l_{3}}^{C}e^{-j2\pi(r-1)\varphi_{l3}^{\rm{C}}(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})}e^{-j2\pi{f}\tau_{l3}^{\rm{C}}},

where
\alpha_{l_{3}}^{C}

\alpha_{l_{3}}^{C}=\alpha_{l_{1}}\alpha_{l_{2}}

\varphi_{l3}^{\rm{C}}=\varphi_{l1}^{\rm{BR}}-\varphi_{l2}^{\rm{RU}}

\tau_{l3}^{\rm{C}}=\tau_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}+\tau_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}}

II-B

The Near-Field Channel Model

It is well known that as IRS array size increases, so does the Fraunhofer distance. Based on this, the CIR of the
r
-th cascaded BS-IRS-user channel can be written as

\displaystyle h_{r}^{\rm{near}}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{l_{1}=1}^{L_{1}}\sum\nolimits_{l_{2}=1}^{L_{2}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{1}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{2}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}\tau_{l1,r}^{\rm{near,BR}}}

\displaystyle e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}\tau_{l2,r}^{\rm{near,RU}}}\delta(t-\tau_{l1,r}^{\rm{near,BR}}-\tau_{l2,r}^{\rm{near,RU}}),

(14)

where
\tau_{l1,r}^{\rm{near,BR}}
denotes the time delay of the
l_{1}
-th path between BS and the
r
-th IRS element, and
\tau_{l2,r}^{\rm{near,RU}}
denotes the time delay of the
l_{2}
-th path between the
r
-th IRS element and the user, which can be defined as
\tau_{l1,r}^{\rm{near,BR}}=\frac{d_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}}{c}
and
\tau_{l2,r}^{\rm{near,RU}}=\frac{d_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c}
,
where

is the distance of the
l_{1}
-th path from the BS to the
r
-th IRS element, and

is the distance of the

-th path from the
r
-th IRS element to the user.

Substituting
\tau_{l1,r}^{\rm{near,BR}}
and
\tau_{l2,r}^{\rm{near,RU}}
into (14), we have

\displaystyle h_{r}^{\rm{near}}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{l_{1}=1}^{L_{1}}\sum\nolimits_{l_{2}=1}^{L_{2}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{1}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}\frac{d_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}}{c}}

\displaystyle{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{2}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}\frac{d_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c}}\delta(t-\frac{d_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}}{c}-\frac{d_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c}).

(15)

Similar to (13), we take Fourier transform to (15), the frequency response of (15) can be expressed as

\displaystyle h_{r}^{\rm{near}}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}h_{r}^{\rm{near}}(t)e^{-j2\pi{f}t}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{L_{1}}\sum_{l_{2}=1}^{L_{2}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{1}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{2}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}\frac{d_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}}{c}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}\frac{d_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c}}e^{-j2\pi{f}(\frac{d_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}}{c}+\frac{d_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{L_{1}}\sum_{l_{2}=1}^{L_{2}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{1}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{2}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{c}}(\frac{d_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}}{c}+\frac{d_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c})}e^{-j2\pi{f}(\frac{d_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}}{c}+\frac{d_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{L_{1}}\sum_{l_{2}=1}^{L_{2}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{1}}{\bar{\alpha}}_{l_{2}}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})(d_{l1,r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{l2,r}^{\rm{RU}})}.

It is clear that the phase

.

III
The Far-field and Near-Field Beam Squint Effects

In this section, the far-field and near-field beam squint effects are analyzed, respectively. Specially, the far-field beam squint makes that the beams at different subcarriers squint toward different directions, which is distinct from that in the near-field, where the beam squint generates a new effect as the beams at different subcarriers spread to different locations.

III-A

The Far-Field Beam squint Effect

For analyze the far-field beam squint effect, we consider a single path and set as
L_{1}=1
,
L_{2}=1
for sake of simplicity. Thus, the far-field channel can be rewritten as

\displaystyle h_{r}^{\rm{far}}(f)=\alpha{e^{-j2\pi(r-1)\varphi(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})}}e^{-j2\pi{f}\tau_{0}},

(17)

where
\varphi=\frac{d}{\lambda_{c}}(\sin\chi-\sin\psi)
denotes the equivalent angle of the cascaded BS-IRS-user channel and
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}^{\rm{BR}}+\tau_{0}^{\rm{RU}}
is the equivalent time delay. Let
\upsilon=\sin\chi-\sin\psi
, we have

\displaystyle h_{r}^{\rm{far}}(f)=\alpha{e^{-j2\pi(r-1)\frac{d}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})\upsilon}}e^{-j2\pi{f}\tau_{0}}.

(18)

Based on (18), the cascaded channel vector
{\bf{h}}(f)=[h_{1}(f),h_{2}(f),\cdots,h_{R}(f)]^{T}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle{\bf{h}}^{\rm{far}}(f)=\alpha{{\bf{a}}((1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})\varphi)}e^{-j2\pi{f}\tau_{0}},

(19)

where

\displaystyle{\bf{a}}((1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})\varphi)=[1,e^{-j2\pi\frac{d}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})\upsilon},\cdots,e^{-j2\pi(R-1)\frac{d}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})\upsilon}]^{T},

is the spatial steering vector. We define
g(f_{m},\nu,\phi_{r})=\left|{\bf{a}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\varphi)\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\rm{far}}\right|
as the gain at frequency
f_{m}
and it can be expressed as

\displaystyle g(f_{m},\nu,\phi_{r})=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{a}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\varphi)\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\rm{far}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j2\pi(r-1)\frac{d}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu}e^{j\phi_{r}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j[\phi_{r}-\pi(r-1)(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu]}\right|.

(20)

By setting
f_{m}=f_{c}
and
\varphi=\varphi_{0}
with
\varphi_{0}=\frac{d}{\lambda_{c}}(\sin\chi_{0}-\sin\psi_{0})
, one can observe when
\phi_{r}-\pi(r-1)(1+\frac{f_{c}}{f_{c}})\nu_{0}=0
, (20) can reach its maximum value, and thus we have

\displaystyle\phi_{r,c}=2\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}.

(21)

According to (21), the reflection phase shift vector
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{\rm{far}}
can be written as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{\rm{far}}=[1,e^{j2\pi\nu_{0}},\cdots,e^{j2\pi(R-1)\nu_{0}}]^{T}.

(22)

Therefore, the beam gain achieved by the user under an arbitrary direction
\varphi
at frequency
f_{m}
can be denoted as

\displaystyle g(f_{m},\nu,\phi_{r,c})=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{a}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\varphi)\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{\rm{far}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j2\pi(r-1)\frac{d}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu}{e^{j2\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j\pi(r-1)[2\nu_{0}-(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu]}\right|.

(23)

To maximize the beam gain, it is observed that
2\nu_{0}-(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu=0
should be satisfied. In this case, the beam direction can be expressed as

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{2}{1+{f_{m}}/{f_{c}}}\nu_{0}.

(24)

From (24), one can observe
\nu\thickapprox\nu_{0}
for any subcarrier in low frequency narrowband system due to
\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}\thickapprox 1
. This means that the beams at all subcarriers will focus on the same direction and the maximum beam gain of each subcarrier can be obtained at this direction. However, for the ultra-wide band THz system,
{f_{m}}/{f_{c}}\thickapprox 1
no longer holds, specifically for edge subcarriers, it indicates that angle
\nu
changes with the frequency
f_{m}
. This means that the beams at different subcarriers will squint toward different directions. Moreover, if we set
{\bf{a}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\varphi)={\bf{a}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\varphi_{0})
, for subcarrier frequency
f_{m}
, (20) should be

\displaystyle g(f_{m},\nu_{0},\phi_{r,c})=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{a}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\varphi_{0})\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j\pi(r-1)[(1-\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu_{0}]}\right|.

(25)

Similarly, it is clear that the beam gain
g(f_{m},\nu_{0},\phi_{r,c})
reaches its maximum value since
f_{m}\thickapprox{f_{c}}
in narrowband systems, while (25) can only obtain its maximum value around the central subcarriers in wideband THz IRS systems. This means that the beams at different subcarriers will be steered towards different directions. We will show the above phenomenon in simulation results section.

III-B

The Near-Field Beam squint Effect

In the previous section, we have analyzed the characteristic of the beam squint in THz IRS communications based on far-field, and next we study the near-far beam squint.

We assume that the location axis of BS and user are, respectively,
(0,0)
and
(x,y)
, and
(x_{R},y_{R})
denotes the axis of the first IRS element. Thus, the axis of the
r
-th IRS element is
(x_{R}+(r-1)d,y_{R})
, and we assume that IRS is an (
1\times{R}
) ULA for the convenience.
Here, we still consider a single path and set
L_{1}=1
,
L_{2}=1
to ease explanation of the beam squint. Thus, the near-field channel can be expressed as

\displaystyle h_{r}^{\rm{near}}(f)={\alpha}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})}.

(26)

According to (26), the cascaded channel vector
{\bf{h}}^{\rm{near}}(f)=[h_{1}^{\rm{near}}(f),h_{2}^{\rm{near}}(f),\cdots,h_{R}^{\rm{near}}(f)]^{T}
can be written as

\displaystyle{\bf{h}}^{\rm{near}}(f)=\alpha{{\bf{b}}((1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}))}e^{-j2\pi{f}\tau_{0}},

(27)

where

\displaystyle{\bf{b}}((1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}))=

\displaystyle[1,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})(d_{1}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{1}^{\rm{RU}})},\cdots,

\displaystyle e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})(d_{R}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{R}^{\rm{RU}})}]^{T},

is the spatial steering vector. Based on the definition of the beam gain in the previous section, for subcarrier frequency
f_{m}
at location
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle g(f_{m},d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}},\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}})=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{b}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}))\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\rm{near}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}})}e^{j\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j[\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}}-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}})]}\right|,

(28)

where
d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}
is the distance between the
r
-th IRS element and the location
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, while the distance from BS to the
r
-th IRS element
d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}
is fixed. Moreover, we set
f_{m}=f_{c}
and
d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}=d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}
, (28) can be re-expressed as

\displaystyle g(f_{c},d_{r}^{\rm{RU}},\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}})=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{b}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{c}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}))\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\rm{near}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{c}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})}e^{j\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j[\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}}-\frac{4\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})]}\right|.

(29)

When
g(f_{c},d_{r}^{\rm{RU}},\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}})
reaches its maximum value, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}}-\frac{4\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})=0,

(30)

and

\displaystyle\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}}=\frac{4\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}).

(31)

Therefore, the optimal reflecting phase shift vector
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{\rm{near}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{near}=[e^{j\frac{4\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{1}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{1}^{\rm{RU}})},e^{j\frac{4\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{2}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{2}^{\rm{RU}})},\cdots,e^{j\frac{4\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{R}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{R}^{\rm{RU}})}]^{T}.

(32)

Similar to (20), the beam gain obtained with the reflecting phase shift vector
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{near}
at location
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
and subcarrier frequency
f_{m}
can be denoted as

\displaystyle g(f_{m},d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}},\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}})=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{b}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}})){\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{near}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}})}e^{j\frac{4\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}[2(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})-(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}})]}\right|.

(33)

It is obvious that the maximum value of (33) can be obtained when
2(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})-(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}})=0
, and then the distance
d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}
between the
r
-th IRS element and location
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
can be written as

\displaystyle d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}=\frac{2(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})}{1+{f_{m}}/{f_{c}}}-d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}.

(34)

From (34), one can observe that the distance
d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}
can be approximated as
d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}\thickapprox{d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}}
over a given frequency band in narrowband systems due to
{f_{m}}/{f_{c}}\thickapprox 1
. This means that the beams at all subcarriers will focus on the same location
(x,y)
so that the high beam gain of all subcarriers can be obtained at location
(x,y)
. On the contrary, one can observe that the beams at different subcarriers will be spread over different locations in wideband THz IRS systems, which is a new phenomenon referred to as the beam squint in the near-field. We will show the above phenomenon in simulation results section.

When
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(x,y)
, we have
{\bf{b}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}))={\bf{b}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}))
. In this case, (35) can be expressed as

\displaystyle g(f_{m},d_{r}^{\rm{RU}},\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}})=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{b}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})){\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{near}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}[(1-\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})]}\right|.

(35)

Based on the above analysis, when
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(x,y)
, the phase
e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}[(1-\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})]}
will change with the IRS element
r
, which suffers the beam gain loss for several subcarriers. We will show the above phenomenon in simulation results section.

IV
Proposed Schemes for Overcoming Far-field and Near-Field Beam Squint Effects

In Section III, we have analyzed the far-field and near-field beam squint effects, respectively. The results have shown exist serious beam gain loss due to the fact that the reflecting beams by IRS can not focus on the desired direction or location at all subcarriers. To address the above issue, inspired by the frequency-dependent characteristic of time delay line, we propose an effective scheme based on the DAM technique. Specifically, we apply the DAM to IRS so that the delay of the reflected signals by IRS can be adjusted, which can effectively mitigate the far-field and near-field beam squints effects by optimizing the reflection coefficients and delays of the IRS elements.

IV-A

The Proposed Scheme for Overcoming Far-Field Beam squint Effect

Based on the DAM-based IRS structure, the frequency response of the
r
-th cascaded BS-IRS-user channel with DAM can be expressed as

\displaystyle h_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}(f)=\alpha{e^{-j2\pi(r-1)\frac{d}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})\nu}}e^{-j2\pi{f}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}}e^{-j2\pi{f}\tau_{0}},

(36)

where
{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\rm{D,far}}}=[\tau_{1}^{\rm{D,far}},\tau_{2}^{\rm{D,far}},\cdots,\tau_{R}^{\rm{D,far}}]^{T}
denotes time delays at IRS. We denote
g(f_{m},\nu,\phi_{r},\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}})=\left|{\bf{a}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\varphi)\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\rm{D,far}}}\right|
as the beam gain at subcarrier frequency
f_{m}
, which can be represented as

\displaystyle g(f_{m},\nu_{0},\phi_{r},\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{a}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\varphi_{0})\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{\rm{far}}{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\rm{D,far}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j2\pi(r-1)\frac{d}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu_{0}}{e^{j2\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j[2\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}-\pi(r-1)(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu_{0}-2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}]}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j[\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}-\pi(r-1)\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}\nu_{0}-2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D}}]}\right|.

(37)

where
\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}
characterizes the time delay imposed by the
r
-th DAM. To maximize the beam gain, we have

\displaystyle-2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}=

\displaystyle\pi(r-1)\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}\nu_{0}-\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}(1-\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}).

(38)

From (38), one can observe that
\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}
is decided by angle
\nu_{0}
and subcarrier frequencies
f_{m}
. Next, we divide
\pi(r-1)\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}\nu_{0}-\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}
into
\pi(r-1)\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}\nu_{0}
and
-\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}
. Considering
\pi(r-1)\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}\nu_{0}
is frequency-dependent, we set
\tau_{r}^{\rm{D}}
as

\displaystyle-2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}=\pi(r-1)\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}\nu_{0},

(39)

then,
\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}
can be written as

\displaystyle\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}=-(r-1){\nu_{0}}/{2f_{c}}.

(40)

From (40), one can see that
\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}
is a function of
r
,
f_{c}
and
\nu_{0}
, which can be applied to all subcarriers due to its frequency independent nature.

Next, the second part
-\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}
can be realized by adding an extra phase shift at IRS. Therefore, the optimal reflecting coefficient
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\rm{far}}_{c}
can be written as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\rm{far}}_{c}=[1,e^{j2\pi\nu_{0}}e^{-j\pi\nu_{0}},\cdots,e^{j2\pi(R-1)\nu_{0}}e^{-j\pi(R-1)\nu_{0}}]^{T}.

(41)

Consequently, the phase shift of the
r
-th IRS element is

\displaystyle\phi^{\rm{far}}_{r,c}=\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}.

(42)

Considering
\tau_{r}^{\rm{D}}
is larger than
0
, the time delay of the
r
-th IRS DAM can be rewritten as

\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} -(r-1)\frac{\nu_{0}}{2f_{c}}&&{\nu_{0}\leqslant 0},\\
(R-1)\frac{\nu_{0}}{2f_{c}}-(r-1)\frac{\nu_{0}}{2f_{c}}&&{\nu_{0}>0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(43)

Based on the obtained IRS reflecting coefficients and time delays, the beam gain of the
m
-th subcarrier can be expressed as

\displaystyle g(f_{m},\nu,\phi_{r}^{\rm{far}},\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{a}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\varphi)\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\rm{far}}_{c}{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\rm{D,far}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j2\pi(r-1)\frac{d}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu}e^{j\pi(r-1)\nu_{0}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,far}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j\pi(r-1)(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu}e^{j\pi(r-1)(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})\nu_{0}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j\pi(r-1)[(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(\nu_{0}-\nu)]}\right|.

(44)

It is obvious that (44) can always reach its maximum value for any subcarrier as long as
\nu_{0}=\nu
. This means that the beam squint effects can be eliminated by optimizing the reflecting coefficient and time delays to satisfy
\nu_{0}=\nu
.

IV-B

The Proposed Scheme for Overcoming Near-Field Beam squint Effect

Similarly, the near-field frequency response of the
r
-th cascaded BS-IRS-user channel at location
(x,y)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle h_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}(f)={\alpha}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})}e^{-j2\pi{f}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}},

(45)

where
{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\rm{D,near}}}=[\tau_{1}^{\rm{D,near}},\tau_{2}^{\rm{D,near}},\cdots,\tau_{R}^{\rm{D,near}}]^{T}
denotes the time delays at IRS, and
\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}
characterizes the time delay imposed by the
r
-th IRS element. Additionally, the beam gain
g(f_{m},d_{r}^{\rm{RU}},\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}})=\left|{\bf{b}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}))\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{\rm{near}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}}\right|
at location
(x,y)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle g(f_{m},d_{r}^{\rm{RU}},\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{b}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}))\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{\rm{near}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})}e^{j\phi_{r,c}^{\rm{near}}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}[(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})(1-\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})]-j2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}}\right|.

(46)

Similarly, to obtain the maximum beam gain, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}[(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})(1-\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})]-2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}=0.

(47)

According to (47), the phase adjusted by DAM can be written as

\displaystyle-2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}).

(48)

Similar to the previous analysis, we divide
-2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}
into
\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}
and
-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})
. It is obvious that the former is frequency-dependent and can be achieved by setting
\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}
as

\displaystyle-2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}}.

(49)

Fig. 2:
(a) The normalized beam gain versus the angle in narrowband IRS systems. (b) The beam model in narrowband IRS systems. (c) The normalized beam gain versus the angle in wideband systems. (d) The far-field beam squint model in THz IRS systems.

Thus,
\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}=-\frac{d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c}.

(50)

From (50), one can observe that the
r
-th IRS time delay in the near-field is only relevant to the distance from BS to the
r
-th IRS and from the
r
-th IRS to the location
(x,y)
and the speed of light
c
, which is straightforward to implement for all subcarriers.

Next, the second part
-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})
can be obtained by DAM, namely setting an extra phase shift to the IRS element. Thus, the phase shifts provided by IRS can be written as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{near}=

\displaystyle[e^{j\frac{4\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{1}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{1}^{\rm{RU}})}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{1}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{1}^{\rm{RU}})},e^{j\frac{4\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{2}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{2}^{\rm{RU}})}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{2}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{2}^{\rm{RU}})},

\displaystyle\cdots,e^{j\frac{4\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{R}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{R}^{\rm{RU}})}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{R}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{R}^{\rm{RU}})}]^{T}.

(51)

The phase shift of the
r
-th IRS element is

\displaystyle\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}}=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}).

(52)

However, one can observe that the time delay
-\frac{d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c}
is less than 0, we can add a common delay
T
to satisfy
min\{\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near}}\}\geqslant 0
. Finally, the optimal time delay of the
r
-th IRS can be represented as

\displaystyle\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near*}}=T-\frac{d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c}.

(53)

Based on the above analysis, we investigate the beam gain at location
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
based on the near-field. By substituting (52) and (53) into
g(f_{m},d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}},\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}},\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near*}})=\left|{\bf{b}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}))\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{\rm{near}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near*}}}\right|
over a given frequency band, the beam gain can be expressed as

\displaystyle g(f_{m},d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}},\phi_{r}^{\rm{near}},\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|{\bf{b}}^{T}((1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}))\boldsymbol{\theta}_{c}^{\rm{near}}e^{-j2\pi{f_{m}}\tau_{r}^{\rm{D,near*}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}})}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})}e^{-j2\pi{f_{m}}(T-\frac{d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}}{c})}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}})}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})[(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU}})-(d_{r}^{\rm{BR}}+d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}})]}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{R}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{c}}(1+\frac{f_{m}}{f_{c}})(d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}-d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}})}\right|.

(54)

From (54), it is apparent that the maximum beam gain can be obtained for any subcarrier at location
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, namely
d_{r}^{\rm{RU}}=d_{r}^{\rm{RU^{\prime}}}
. This means that the beam squint effect can also be elimated by optimizing the reflecting coefficients and time delays of the IRS. We will present the performance analysis in Section V.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we first pointed out the far-field and near-field beam squint effects in an IRS-aided wideband THz communications. Specially, the far-field beam squint makes that beams at different subcarriers squint toward different directions, while the near-field beam squint spreads beams at different subcarriers point over different locations. Next, we proposed a scheme that applying DAM to IRS for overcoming the far-field and near-field beam squint effects, through joint optimization of the time delays and reflection phase shifts of IRS elements. Simulations results showed that our proposed schemes are effective to deal with beam squint effects in THz IRS communications. The far-field and near-field beam squint issues in multi-user MIMO systems will be considered in our future work.

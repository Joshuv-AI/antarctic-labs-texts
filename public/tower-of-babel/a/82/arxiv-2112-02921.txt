Introduction

Throughout this paper, we assume that
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
with the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
and suppose
\operatorname{Ass}(I)
is the set of associated prime ideals of
R/I
. Brodmann
[
2
]
showed that there exists an integer
t_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{t})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{t_{0}})
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. The smallest such integer
t_{0}
is called the
index of Ass-stability
of
I
, and denoted by
\operatorname{astab}(I)
. Brodmann
[
3
]
also showed that there exists an integer
t_{0}
such that
\operatorname{depth}R/I^{t}=\operatorname{depth}R/I^{t_{0}}
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. The smallest such integer
t_{0}
is called the
index of depth stability
of
I
and denoted by
\operatorname{dstab}(I)
.

Let
d,a_{1},...,a_{n}
be positive integers. We put
I_{(d;a_{1},...,a_{n})}\subset R
be the monomial ideal generated by the monomials
u\in R
of degree
d
satisfying
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)\leq a_{i}
for all
i=1,...,n
. Monomial ideals of this type are called ideals of Veronese type.
Monomial ideals of Veronese type are polymatroidal. In addition, if
a_{i}=1
for all
i
, then we use
I_{d;n}
instead of
I_{(d;1,\ldots,1)}
.
Note that the product of polymatroidal ideals is again polymatroidal (see
[
4
, Theorem 5.3]
). In particular each power of a polymatroidal ideal is polymatroidal. In addition, every polymatroidal ideal is a normal ideal (see
[
9
, Theorem 3.4]
).

Katzman
[
13
]
gave an explicit formula for the Hilbert series of the Veronese type ideals ( see also
[
5
]
).
Jarrah
[
12
]
presented
M_{n,t}=(x^{e_{1}},\ldots,x^{e_{n}})
a monomial ideal of
R
, where

and he proved that these ideals are unmixed monomial ideals of
R
such that their integral closures have embedded primes.
In
[
8
]
the equigenerated monomial ideal
I
of
R
is called a Freiman ideal, if
\mu(I^{2})=\ell(I)\mu(I)-{\ell(I)\choose 2}
, where
\ell(I)
is the analytic spread of
I
, that is, the dimension of
\mathcal{R}(I)/{{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{R}(I)}
.

The purpose of this paper is to give a simply proof for the main theorem of
[
12
]
and also we compute the Hilbert series of

.
Furthermore, we show that
M_{n,t}
is a Freiman ideal.
For any unexplained notion or terminology, we refer the reader to

1.
Integral closure of a special monomial ideal

We start this section by the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
R
. Then an element
\alpha\in R
is integral over
I
, if there is an equation

{\alpha}^{k}+a_{1}{\alpha}^{k-1}+\ldots+a_{k-1}\alpha+a_{k}=0,

with
a_{i}\in I^{i}
. The set of elements
\overline{I}
in
R
which are integral over
I
is the integral closure of
I
. The ideal
I
is integrally closed, if
I=\overline{I}
, and
I
is normal if all powers of
I
are integrally closed. If
u\in\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}
or if
u\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus\mathfrak{m}^{2}
, then the ideal
(u)+\mathfrak{m}^{k}
is integrally closed (see
[
11
, Exercises 1.18, 1.19]
).
Moreover, if
I
is monomial ideal, then
\overline{I}
is a monomial ideal generated by all monomials
u\in R
for which there exists an integer
k
such that
u^{k}\in I^{k}
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
I=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
be a monomial ideal of
R
and
J=(u_{1}^{t},\ldots,u_{k}^{t})
, where
t
is a natural number. Then
\overline{J}=\overline{I^{t}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
a\in I^{t}
. Then
a=u_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots u_{k}^{e_{k}}
, where
\Sigma_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}=t
. It therefore follows
a^{t}=(u_{1}^{t})^{e_{1}}\ldots(u_{k}^{t})^{e_{k}}\in J^{t}
. Consider
x^{t}-a^{t}
as an equation of integral dependence and so
a\in\overline{J}
. Thus
I^{t}\subseteq\overline{J}
and hence
\overline{I^{t}}\subseteq\overline{J}
. For the converse it is clear
J\subseteq I^{t}
and so
\overline{J}\subseteq\overline{I^{t}}
. Therefore
\overline{J}=\overline{I^{t}}
, as required.

∎

Example 1.3
.

Let
n=2
and
I=(x_{1}^{k},x_{2}^{k+1})
be an ideal of
R
. Then
\overline{I}=((x_{1},x_{2})^{k+1},x_{1}^{k})
.

Proof.

It is clear
(x_{1}^{k+1},x_{2}^{k+1})\subseteq I
and so, by Lemma,
1.2

(x_{1},x_{2})^{k+1}=\overline{(x_{1},x_{2})^{k+1}}\subseteq\overline{I}
. It therefore follows
((x_{1},x_{2})^{k+1},x_{1}^{k})\subseteq\overline{I}
. Also,
I\subseteq((x_{1},x_{2})^{k+1},x_{1}^{k})
and hance
\overline{I}\subseteq\overline{((x_{1},x_{2})^{k+1},x_{1}^{k})}
.
Since
x_{1}^{k}\in(x_{1},x_{2})^{k}
, it follows that
((x_{1},x_{2})^{k+1},x_{1}^{k})
is integrally closed and so
\overline{I}\subseteq((x_{1},x_{2})^{k+1},x_{1}^{k})
. Therefore
\overline{I}=((x_{1},x_{2})^{k+1},x_{1}^{k})
.

∎

Example 1.4
.

Let
n=2
and
I=(x_{1},x_{2}^{k})
. Then
I
is normal.

Proof.

Since
x_{1}\in(x_{1},x_{2})\setminus(x_{1},x_{2})^{2}
, it follows
\overline{I}=\overline{((x_{1},x_{2})^{k},x_{1})}=((x_{1},x_{2})^{k},x_{1})=I
. Now, by using
[
11
, Theorem 1.4.10]

I
is normal, as required.

∎

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
such that
\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. Then
\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{I})=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
.

Proof.

Since
\sqrt{I}=\sqrt{\overline{I}}
, it follows that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{I})
. If
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{I})
, then
\mathfrak{q}=(\overline{I}:u)

for some monomial element
u
of
R
. Let
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{q}\setminus\mathfrak{p}
. Then
x_{i}u\in\overline{I}
and so
(x_{i}u)^{k}\in I^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
. Since
I
is a
\mathfrak{p}-
primary ideal, it follows
u^{t}\in I^{t}
for some positive integers
t
and so
u\in\overline{I}
and this is a contradiction. Therefore
\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{I})=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
, as required.

∎

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
M_{n,t}
and
I_{n-1;n}
be as before. Then
\overline{M_{n,t}}=I_{n-1;n}^{t}
.

Proof.

As before
M_{n,t}=(x^{e_{1}},\ldots,x^{e_{n}})
, where
x^{e_{i}}=x_{1}^{t}\ldots x_{i-1}^{t}x_{i+1}^{t}\ldots x_{n}^{t}
. Thus, by Lemma
1.2
,
\overline{M_{n,t}}=\overline{I^{t}_{n-1,n}}
. Since Veronese type ideals are normal, it follows that
\overline{M_{n,t}}=I_{n-1;n}^{t}
.

∎

If you raise the
x_{i}
uniformly to some power, say
x_{i}
is replaced by
x_{i}^{t}
everywhere in the generated of square-free Veronese type ideal
I_{n-1;n}
, then the resulting ideal
M_{n,t}
is the image of the flat map
R\rightarrow R
with
x_{i}\mapsto x_{i}^{t}
for all
i
. Thus in this case
M_{n,t}
will be Cohen-Macaulay, if
I_{n-1;n}
is so ( see
[
10
]
).
By
[
7
, Theorem 4.2]
the square-free Veronese type ideal
I_{n-1;n}
is Cohen-Macaulay and so
M_{n,t}
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
M_{n,t}
be as before. Then
M_{n,t}
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\overline{M_{n,t}}
has embedded primes.

Proof.

By the above arguments, since
I_{n-1;n}
is Cohen-Macaulay by
[
7
, Theorem 4.2]
, it follows that
M_{n,t}
is Cohen-Macaulay. By using
[
9
, Lemma 5.1]
,
[
15
, Theorem 3.4]
and Theorem
1.6
,
\overline{M_{n,t}}=I_{n-1;n}^{t}
has embedded primes, as required.

∎

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
M_{n,t}
be as before. Then
\operatorname{astab}(M_{n,t})=\operatorname{dstab}(M_{n,t})=n-1
.

Proof.

Consider the flat map
R\rightarrow R
with
x_{i}\mapsto x_{i}^{t}
for all
i
. Therefore
I_{n-1;n}R=M_{n,t}
. Thus
\operatorname{Ass}(M_{n,t}^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k}_{n-1;n}R)

and
\operatorname{depth}(R/{M_{n,t}^{k}})=\operatorname{depth}(R/{I^{k}_{n-1;n}R})
for all positive integer

. Since by using
[
9
, Corollariy 5.7]

\operatorname{astab}(I_{n-1;n})=\operatorname{dstab}(I_{n-1;n})=n-1
, it follows that
\operatorname{astab}(M_{n,t})=\operatorname{dstab}(M_{n,t})=n-1
, as required.

∎

2.
Hilbert series of a special monomial ideal

We start this section by the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

The Hilbert function of
M_{n,t}
is given by

H(i)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}{{{(-1)}^{j}}}\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}n\\
j\end{array}}\right)\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{i(n-1)-ji-j+n-1}\\
{n-1}\end{array}}\right).

Proof.

By
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]

H(i)
is the number of sequences
(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n})
satisfying

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{{\beta_{j}}=i(n-1)t}
,
0\leq\beta_{j}\leq it
and
\beta_{j}=t\alpha_{j}
such that
0\leq\alpha_{j}\leq i
. So
H(i)
is the number of sequences
(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
satisfying
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{{\alpha_{j}}=i(n-1)}
and
0\leq\alpha_{j}\leq i
. In fact
H(i)
is the number of integer solution of the equation

\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=i(n-1),~~~~0\leq\alpha_{j}\leq i,~~~~1\leq j\leq n.

For
\alpha_{j}\geq 0
,
1\leq j\leq n
, the number of integer solution of the equation

\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=i(n-1)
is

\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{i(n-1)+n-1}\\
{n-1}\end{array}}\right).

For
\alpha_{1}>i
and
\alpha_{j}\geq 0
,
2\leq j\leq n
, the number of integer solution of the equation

\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=i(n-1)
is the same as the number of integer solution of the equation
\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{n}=i(n-1)-i-1
, where
\gamma_{1}=\alpha_{1}-i-1
and
\gamma_{j}=\alpha_{j}
for
2\leq j\leq n
and equal to

\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{i(n-1)-i-1+n-1}\\
{n-1}\end{array}}\right).

For
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>i
and
\alpha_{j}\geq 0
,
3\leq j\leq n
, the number of integer solution of the equation

\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=i(n-1)
is the same as the number of integer solution of the equation
\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{n}=i(n-1)-2i-2
, where
\gamma_{1}=\alpha_{1}-i-1
,
\gamma_{2}=\alpha_{2}-i-1
and
\gamma_{j}=\alpha_{j}
for
3\leq j\leq n
and equal to

\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{i(n-1)-2i-2+n-1}\\
{n-1}\end{array}}\right).

By similar argument the number of integer solution of the equation

\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=i(n-1)
such that
\alpha_{j_{1}},\alpha_{j_{2}},\cdots,\alpha_{j_{r}}>i
for some
\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{r}\}\subseteq\{1,\cdots,n\}
and
\alpha_{j}\geq 0
for
\{1,\cdots,n\}\setminus\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{r}\}
is equal to

\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{i(n-1)-ji-j+n-1}\\
{n-1}\end{array}}\right).

Now, by rule of sum we have

H(i)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}{{{(-1)}^{j}}}\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}n\\
j\end{array}}\right)\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{i(n-1)-ji-j+n-1}\\
{n-1}\end{array}}\right).

In combinatorial it is known that for positive integer
n
and
d
, the coefficient of
x_{1}^{n_{1}}\ldots x_{d}^{n_{d}}
in the expansion
(x_{1}+x_{2}+\ldots+x_{d})^{n}
is

\frac{n!}{n_{1}!n_{2}!n_{3}!\ldots n_{d}!}

where each
n_{i}
is an integer with
0\leq n_{i}\leq n
, for all
1\leq i\leq d
, and
n_{1}+n_{2}+\ldots+n_{d}=n
. So the coefficient of
t^{i}
in the expansion
(1+t+t^{2}+t^{3}+\ldots+t^{d-1})^{n}
is

\frac{n!}{n_{1}!n_{2}!n_{3}!\ldots n_{d}!}

where each
n_{i}
is an integer with
0\leq n_{i}\leq n
, for all
1\leq i\leq d
,
n_{1}+n_{2}+\ldots+n_{d}=n
and
n_{2}+2n_{3}+3n_{4}+\ldots+(d-1)n_{d}=i
. Katzman
[
13
]
denoted this coefficient by
A_{i}^{n,d}
. Actually

(1+t+t^{2}+t^{3}+\ldots+t^{d-1})^{n}=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}{A_{i}^{n,d}}t^{i}.

Katzman
[
13
]
proved the following lemmas in general
[
13
, Theorem 2.5, Lemma 2.7 and Lemma 2.10]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(1-t)^{n-r}\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}{{i(n-j-1)+n-r-1\choose n-r-1}t^{i}}=\sum\limits_{l\geq 0}A_{l(n-j-1)}^{n-r,n-j-1}t^{l}.

Lemma 2.3
.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}{i(n-j-1)-j+n-1\choose n-1}t^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r=0}^{j}(-1)^{r}{j\choose r}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}{i(n-j-1)+n-r-1\choose n-r-1}t^{i}.

Lemma 2.4
.

\sum\limits_{k\geqslant 0}{A_{k(n-j-1)}^{n,n-j-1}}=(n-j-1)^{n-1}.

Now, we are ready to prove the following result.

Theorem 2.5
.

The Hilbert polynomial of
M_{n,t}
is

Q(n)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}{{(-1)}^{j}}{n\choose j}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{j}{{(-1)}^{r}}{j\choose r}{(1-t)^{r}}\sum\limits_{k\geqslant 0}{A_{k(n-j-1)}^{n-1,n-j-1}{t^{k}}}.

Proof.

By definition of Hilbert polynomial we have

Q(n)={(1-t)^{n}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}{{{(-1)}^{j}}{n\choose j}}}{i(n-1)-ji-j+n-1\choose n-1}{t^{i}}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle{(1-t)^{n}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}{{{(-1)}^{j}}{n\choose j}}{i(n-1)-ji-j+n-1\choose n-1}{t^{i}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}{(1-t)^{n}}{{{{(-1)}^{j}}{n\choose j}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}{i(n-1)-ji-j+n-1\choose n-1}{t^{i}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}(1-t)^{n}(-1)^{j}{n\choose j}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{j}{{(-1)}^{r}}{j\choose r}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}{i(n-j-1)+n-r-1\choose n-r-1}{t^{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{j}{n\choose j}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{j}{{(-1)}^{r}}{j\choose r}(1-t)^{r}(1-t)^{n-r}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}{i(n-j-1)+n-r-1\choose n-r-1}{t^{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{j}{n\choose j}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{j}{{(-1)}^{r}}{j\choose r}(1-t)^{r}\sum\limits_{k\geqslant 0}{A_{k(n-j-1)}^{n-r,n-j-1}{t^{k}}}.

The second equality follows from Lemma
2.3
and the last equality follows from Lemma
2.2
.

∎

Corollary 2.6
.

The Hilbert series of
M_{n,t}
is

P(n,t)=\frac{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}{{(-1)}^{j}}{n\choose j}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{j}{{(-1)}^{r}}{j\choose r}{(1-t)^{r}}\sum\limits_{k\geqslant 0}{A_{k(n-j-1)}^{n-1,n-j-1}{t^{k}}}}{(1-t)^{n}}.

Corollary 2.7
.

The multiplicity of
M_{n,t}
is

e(M_{n,t})=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-2}{(-1)^{j}{n\choose j}(n-j-1)^{n-1}}.

If
(S,\mathfrak{m})
is a Noetherian
local ring and
J\subseteq I
be a pair of ideals of
S
, then
J
is a reduction of
I
if
and only if
\bar{J}=\bar{I}
. In particular,
\ell(I)=\ell(J)
( see
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Now, by Theorem
1.6
we have
\bar{M}_{n,t}
is equal to the Veronese type ideal
I_{(t(n-1);t,\ldots,t)}
. Therefore
\ell(M_{n,t})=n
.

Theorem 2.8
.

M_{n,t}
is a Freiman ideal for all
n
and
t
.

Proof.

Set
I=M_{n,t}
. Then
I
is a Freiman ideal if and only if

\mu(I^{2})=\ell(I)\mu(I)-{\ell(I)\choose 2}.

\mu(I^{2})
is the number of sequence
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{n})
satisfies
\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\ldots+\beta_{n}=2t(n-1)
such that
\beta_{i}=0,t
or
2t
.
So we can say,
\mu(I^{2})
is the number of sequence
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
satisfies
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}=2(n-1)
and
0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq 2
.

The number of integer solution of equation
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}=2(n-1)
such that
\alpha_{1}=0
is one.
Therefore the number of integer solution of equation
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}=2(n-1)
such that
\alpha_{1}=0
or
\alpha_{2}=0
or
\ldots
or
\alpha_{n}=0
is
n={n\choose 1}
.

Also the number of integer solution of equation
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}=2(n-1)
such that
\alpha_{1}=1
is
n-1
.
Therefore the number of integer solution of equation
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}=2(n-1)
such that
\alpha_{1}=1
or
\alpha_{2}=1
or
\ldots
or
\alpha_{n}=1
is

{n-1\choose 1}+{n-2\choose 1}+\ldots+{1\choose 1}.

So
\mu(I^{2})={n\choose 1}+{n-1\choose 1}+\ldots+{1\choose 1}={n+1\choose 2}
.
Hence
\mu(I^{2})=n\mu(I)-{n\choose 2}
and

is Freiman ideal for any

and

.

∎

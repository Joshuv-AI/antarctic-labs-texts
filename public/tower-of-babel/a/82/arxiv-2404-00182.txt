Abstract
. We analyse the sharpness of the Sobolev order for vector fields on compact Riemannian manifolds. As an application, we then prove ill-posedness for a certain class of Cauchy problems.

1.
Introduction

The study of global properties concerning vector fields defined on compact manifolds has attracted the attention of the scientific community for over half a century. Notable research on this topic can be found in
[
1
,
2
,
9
]
. Upon further exploration of these and other references in the field, it becomes clear that the focus is on the study of global solvability and global hypoellipticity when the smooth manifold under consideration is a torus. This emphasis is due to two main reasons: the first being the conjecture of S. Greenfield and N. Wallach, which suggests the nonexistence of globally hypoelliptic vector fields on compact manifolds that are non-diffeomorphic to tori (refer to
[
3
,
4
]
); the second reason is the development of a solid set of techniques over the past three decades, specifically tailored for studying vector fields on tori. These techniques rely mainly on the good properties of the Fourier series, the analysis of the asymptotic behavior of eigenvalues of such vector fields, a priori estimates, and Diophantine conditions that naturally arise in this context, as seen in
[
2
,
4
,
9
]
.

In recent times, there have been significant advancements in the quantization framework initially established by Ruzhansky and Turunen in
[
11
]
. As a result, some of these results have been extended from the tori to general compact Lie groups. This progress was made possible through the development of new techniques, which are outlined in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
13
]
. During their exploration of this new context, the authors came across a relevant question that had not yet been addressed in the literature. This question posed a challenge to the further development of the theory and can be stated as follows.

Let
(G,g)
denote a compact Riemannian manifold with metric
g
. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the Sobolev space
L^{2}_{s}(G)
, obtained by completion of
C^{\infty}(G)
with respect to the norm:

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{s}}:=\|(-\Delta_{G})^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(G)},

where
-\Delta_{G}
is the (positive) Laplacian on
G
associated to the Riemannian metric
g
.

The question is the following:

“Can a left-invariant nonzero vector field
X
on
G
be bounded from
L^{2}_{s}(G)
to
L^{2}_{s-\varepsilon}(G)
, for some
0<\varepsilon<1
?”

It is well-known that any vector field
X:L^{2}_{s}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}_{s-1}(G)
is bounded. Therefore, our inquiry concerns the sharpness of this continuity property.

This paper provides a negative answer to this question by establishing the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(G,g)
denote a compact Riemannian manifold.
For
0\leq\varepsilon<1
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, any smooth vector field
X\in TG\setminus\{0\}
does not represent a bounded operator from
L^{2}_{s}(G)
to
L^{2}_{s-\varepsilon}(G)
.

The proof of this result, detailed in Section
2
, relies on results and techniques from pseudo-differential theory on compact manifolds. One consequence of this result is that the sequence of eigenvalues associated with a left-invariant vector field in a compact Lie group has a subsequence that grows at a polynomial rate as the index tends to infinity. In Section
3
, we use this fact to prove that a class of Cauchy problems on a compact Lie group is ill-posed in
[0,T]\times G
.

Notation.
Here the notation
A\sim B,
indicates that we have the inequality
C_{1}B\leq A\leq C_{2}B,
for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
independent of fundamental parameters, while the notation
A\lesssim B
indicates that we have the inequality
A\leq C_{2}B
with
C_{2}
independent of fundamental parameters.

2.
Sobolev boundedness of vector fields

In this section we are going to prove our main Theorem
1.1
. To illustrate our approach let us discuss the case where
\varepsilon=0.
Indeed, note that
X
can be realised as an unbounded linear operator on
L^{2}(G).
That
X
is not bounded on
L^{2}(G)
can be proved as follows. One can find a local coordinate system
(\omega,\phi)
where
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},

\phi:\omega\rightarrow\phi(\omega)\subset G
is a diffeomorphism, and by a translation and a possible rotation of the chart
\omega,
we can assume that
0\in\omega,
and also that the vector field
X
can be identified with one of the canonical vector fields
\partial_{x_{j}}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}.
By making the identification
\omega\cong\phi(\omega)
we have that
X\cong\partial_{x_{j}}.

Define the sequence of compactly supported functions on
\omega,

f_{n}(x)=e^{in\cdot x}\phi_{\omega},\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}^{d},

where
\phi_{\omega}
is a positive, smooth, and compactly supported function on
\omega.

Note that

\|f_{n}\circ\phi^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\phi(\omega))}\asymp\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\|\phi_{\omega}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\lesssim 1.

However,

\|X(f_{n}\circ\phi^{-1})\|_{L^{2}(\phi(\omega))}\asymp\|X(f_{n}\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\asymp\|\partial_{x_{j}}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

=\|e^{in\cdot x}\partial_{x_{j}}\phi_{\omega}+in_{j}e^{in\cdot x}\phi_{\omega}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Since

|e^{in\cdot x}\partial_{x_{j}}\phi_{\omega}+in_{j}e^{in\cdot x}\phi_{\omega}|\geq n_{j}|\phi_{\omega}|-|\partial_{x_{j}}\phi_{\omega}|

we have for
n_{j}\rightarrow\infty,
that
\|X(f_{n}\circ\phi^{-1})\|_{L^{2}(\phi(\omega))}\rightarrow\infty,
showing that there is subsequence
\{f_{n_{k}}\circ\phi^{-1}\}
such that the
L^{2}
-norm of the functions
X(f_{n_{k}}\circ\phi^{-1})
tends to infinity when
k\rightarrow\infty,
with the subsequence
\{f_{n_{k}}\circ\phi^{-1}\}
being bounded on
L^{2}(G).
In consequence, we have proved that
X
is not bounded on
L^{2}(G).
This idea will be generalised to prove Theorem
1.1
when
0\leq\varepsilon<1,
by using the pseudo-differential calculus on the manifold
G
as well as the localisation technique illustrated above. For the basic aspects of the theory of pseudo-differential operators, we refer the reader e.g. to Ruzhansky and Turunen
[
11
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Assume that
0\neq X:L^{2}_{s}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}_{s-\varepsilon}(G)
is bounded where
\varepsilon\in[0,1).

In particular,
X:L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}_{-\varepsilon}(G)
is bounded, that is, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that:

\|(-\Delta_{G})^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}Xf\|_{L^{2}}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Observe that

R=X(-\Delta_{G})^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}:L^{2}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}(G)

is also bounded. Indeed, note that

R=X\Delta^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}_{G}-\Delta^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}_{G}X+\Delta^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}_{G}X=[X,\Delta^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}_{G}]+\Delta^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}_{G}X

(2.1)

where the commutator
[X,\Delta^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}_{G}]\in\Psi^{-\varepsilon}_{1,0}(G).
Note that
\Delta^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}_{G}
is a pseudo-differential operator of order
-\varepsilon\leq 0,
and then it is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(G)
in view of the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem.
Since
[X,\Delta^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}_{G}]:L^{2}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}(G)
is bounded we have the boundedness of
R
from
L^{2}(G)
into
L^{2}(G),
that is, there exists
C>0
such that:

\|Rf\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Microlocally, we can represent the operator
R
in a coordinate chart
U
of the manifold
G
as follows

Ru(x)=\smallint e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)d\xi,

(2.2)

where
a\in S^{m}_{1,0}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a symbol of order
m
(see e.g. Ruzhansky and Turunen
[
11
, Chapter 2]
) and
\widehat{u}:=\widehat{u}(\xi)
denotes the Euclidean Fourier transform of
u.
Here,
m=-\varepsilon+1=1-\varepsilon
.

Without loss of generality assume that
y=0
is an interior point of
U,
and by using a rotation if necessary, assume that
X\cong\partial_{x_{j}}
corresponds to one of the partial derivatives with respect to one of the canonical coordinates.
If
K
is the kernel of
R
, we have that

\displaystyle\|x\mapsto\smallint K(x,y){f}(y)dy\|_{L^{2}(U)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(U)},

(2.3)

for any distribution
f\in L^{2}(U)
.
Let
\delta_{0}
be the Delta-Kronecker distribution at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Let us consider an approximation of
\delta_{0}
in the sense of distributions. To do so, let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be supported in the ball
B(0,1)=\{x:|x|<1\},
and such that

\smallint\Phi(x)dx=1.

(2.4)

Define the family of (compactly supported) functions

x\mapsto\Phi_{t}(x)=t^{-n}\Phi(t^{-1}x),\,t>0.

(2.5)

As
y=0
is an interior point of
U,
there is
t_{0}>0
such that for any
0<t<t_{0},

\textnormal{supp}(\Phi_{t})\subset U.

In the sense of distributions we have that

\delta_{0}=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\Phi_{t}.

(2.6)

Consider
(\Delta^{-\frac{\delta}{2}})^{\prime}
the localisation of
(\Delta_{G})^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}
by the local coordinate systems associated to
U
.
Define
f_{t}=(-\Delta^{-\frac{\delta}{2}})^{\prime}\Phi_{t}.
Since
\Phi_{t}\in C^{\infty}_{0},
in view of the continuity of property

(-\Delta^{-\frac{\delta}{2}})^{\prime}:C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(2.7)

we have that
f_{t}=(\Delta^{-\frac{\delta}{2}})^{\prime}\Phi_{t}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Now, consider
\delta>n/2,
which will be specified below. Using that
(-\Delta_{G})^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}:L^{2}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}(G)
is bounded, we have that

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}f_{t}=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}(\Delta^{-\frac{\delta}{2}})^{\prime}\Phi_{t}=(\Delta^{-\frac{\delta}{2}})^{\prime}\delta_{0}\in L^{2}(U),

(2.8)

where the limit is taken with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm.

Using the inequality in (
2.3
)
applied to
f=(\Delta^{-\frac{\delta}{2}})^{\prime}\delta_{0}
,
we have that

\displaystyle\|x\mapsto\smallint K(x,y)(-\Delta^{-\frac{\delta}{2}})^{\prime}\delta_{0}(y)dy\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(-\Delta^{-\frac{\delta}{2}})^{\prime}\delta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<+\infty.

(2.9)

Observe that
R(-\Delta_{G})^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}
is a pseudo-differential operator on
G
of order
m^{\prime}=1-\varepsilon-\delta.
Even, since
0\leq\varepsilon<1,
and
\delta>n/2,
we can take

\delta=\frac{n}{2}+\lambda,

(2.10)

where
\lambda>0
is such that

0<\lambda\leq 1-\varepsilon.

(2.11)

In view of the factorisation

R(-\Delta_{G})^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}=X(-\Delta_{G})^{-\frac{\varepsilon+\delta}{2}},

(2.12)

and using that
(-\Delta^{-\frac{\varepsilon+\delta}{2}})^{\prime}\delta_{0}(x)\sim|x|^{-(n-\varepsilon-\delta)},
(see e.g. Ruzhansky
[
10
, Page 359]
) the action of
X\cong\partial_{x_{j}}
on
(-\Delta^{-\frac{\varepsilon+\delta}{2}})^{\prime}\delta_{0}(x)
implies that

|K(x,y)(-\Delta^{-\frac{\delta}{2}})^{\prime}\delta_{0}(y)|\sim|y|^{-(n+m^{\prime})}\in L^{2}(U).

(2.13)

In consequence we have that

2(n+m^{\prime})<n.

(2.14)

However observe that

\displaystyle 2(n+m^{\prime})

\displaystyle=2n+2m^{\prime}=2n+2(1-\varepsilon)-2\delta

\displaystyle=2n+2(1-\varepsilon)-n-2\lambda

\displaystyle=n+2(1-\varepsilon-\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq n,

where we have used that
1-\varepsilon-\lambda\geq 0
in view of (
2.11
). This fact contradicts (
2.14
). So, we conclude there does not exist
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
such that
X:L^{2}_{s}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}_{s-\varepsilon}(G)
is bounded.
∎

3.
Application to the ill-posed of certain Cauchy problems associated to left-invariant vector fields

In this section we give an application of our main Theorem
1.1
. The notations used in this section are taken from Ruzhansky and Turunen
[
11
, Chapter 10]
.
Let
G
be a compact Lie group, let
dx
be its normalised Haar measure, let us consider its unitary dual
\widehat{G}
(that is the set of all equivalence classes of irreducible continuous unitary representations of
G
) and let
X\neq 0
be a left-invariant vector field in
G
. We will use Theorem
1.1
to show the following fact.

Theorem 3.1
.

The Cauchy problem:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)+iXu(t,x)=0,\qquad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times G,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\qquad x\in G.\end{cases}

(3.1)

for any
T>0
and every
0\neq u_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
is not well-posed on
L_{1}^{2}(G)
.

Proof.

Indeed, suppose there exists
u\in C([0,T];L_{1}^{2}(G))
solution to (
3.1
). Note that
iX
is a symmetric operator on
L^{2}(G).
For any function
f\in C^{\infty}(G)
we can associate to
iX
a matrix symbol according to the quantisation formula

iXf(x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}\textnormal{dim}(\xi)\textnormal{Tr}[\xi(x)\sigma_{iX}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)],

(3.2)

where
\textnormal{dim}(\xi)
denotes the dimension of each representation
\xi,
and
\xi\mapsto\widehat{f}(\xi)
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
at
\xi
which is given by

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\smallint_{G}f(x)\xi(x)^{-1}dx.

(3.3)

The matrix symbol of
iX
is diagonalisable by unitary matrices, therefore for each
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
there exists a representative of
[\xi]
, which we will denote also by
\xi
, such that
iX
has matrix symbol
\sigma_{iX}
satisfying

\sigma_{iX}(\xi)_{\alpha\beta}=\mu_{\alpha}(\xi)\delta_{\alpha\beta}

for
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq\dim(\xi)
, where
\delta_{\alpha\beta}
is the Kroenecker-Delta and
\mu_{\alpha}(\xi)
are real numbers.

Now, as
iX
is not bounded from
L_{s}^{2}(G)
to
L_{s-\frac{1}{2}}^{2}(G)
, one can verify that there must exist a sequence of distinct terms
([\xi_{n}])_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\widehat{G}
and
C>0
such that

|\mu_{\alpha(n)}(\xi_{n})|\geq C\langle\xi_{n}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}},

for some
1\leq\alpha(n)\leq\dim(\xi_{n})
and every
n\in\N
.

Indeed, suppose this was not true. Then, there would exist
C>0
sufficiently big such that

|\mu_{\alpha}(\xi)|\leq C\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}},

for every
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
,
1\leq\alpha\leq\dim(\xi)
. Now let
v\in L^{2}_{s}(G)
. Then

\displaystyle\|iXv\|_{L_{s-\frac{1}{2}}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}\dim(\xi)\sum_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq\dim(\xi)}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s-1}|\mu_{\alpha}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{v}(\xi)_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}\dim(\xi)\sum_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq\dim(\xi)}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{v}(\xi)_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|v\|_{L_{s}^{2}}^{2},

so that
iX:L^{2}_{s}\rightarrow L^{2}_{s-\frac{1}{2}}
is bounded, a contradiction.

By composing
\xi_{n}
with a change of variables, we may further assume that
\alpha(n)=1,\,\forall n\in\N
. As
\mu_{\alpha}(\overline{\xi})=-\mu_{\alpha}(\xi)
, we may also assume that

\mu_{1}(\xi_{n})\geq C\langle\xi_{n}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}

(3.4)

for all
n\in\N
.

Back to the Cauchy problem, taking the group Fourier transform in
G
on (
3.1
) yields

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)_{\alpha\beta}+\mu_{\alpha}(\xi)\widehat{u}(t,\xi)_{\alpha\beta}=0,\qquad(t,\xi)\in[0,T]\times\widehat{G},1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq\dim(\xi),\\
\widehat{u}(0,\xi)_{\alpha\beta}=\widehat{u}(\xi)_{\alpha\beta},\qquad\xi\in\widehat{G},1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq\dim(\xi).\end{cases}

(3.5)

From which it follows that

\widehat{u}(t,\xi)_{\alpha\beta}=e^{\mu_{\alpha}(\xi)t}\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)_{\alpha\beta},

(3.6)

for every
\xi\in\widehat{G},1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq\dim(\xi),t\in[0,T].
Now, consider
u_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
given by

\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi_{n})_{11}=e^{-\frac{CT}{2}\langle\xi_{n}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}},

(3.7)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)_{\alpha\beta}=0
otherwise. In fact,
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(G)\subset L^{2}(G)
. Now equation
(
3.6
)
and inequality (
3.4
) imply that

|\widehat{u}(t,\xi_{n})_{11}|\geq e^{C(t-\frac{T}{2})\langle\xi_{n}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}},

for every
n\in\N
, and so
u(t,\cdot)\not\in L^{2}(G)
for
t\geq\frac{T}{2}
by Parseval’s identity, a contradiction. Therefore, the problem is ill-posed.
∎

Mixed local and nonlocal Sobolev inequalities with extremal and associated quasilinear singular elliptic problems
Prashanta Garain and Alexander Ukhlov

Abstract
. In this article, we consider mixed local and nonlocal Sobolev ( q , p ) (q,p) -inequalities with extremal in the case 0 < q < 1 < p < ∞ 0<q<1<p<\infty . We prove that the extremal of such inequalities is unique up to a multiplicative constant that is associated with a singular elliptic problem involving the mixed local and nonlocal p p -Laplace operator. Moreover, it is proved that the mixed Sobolev inequalities are necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of weak solutions of such singular problems. As a consequence, a relation between the singular p p -Laplace and mixed local and nonlocal p p -Laplace equation is established. Finally, we investigate the existence, uniqueness, regularity and symmetry properties of weak solutions for such problems.

Sharp Sobolev inequalities in both the local and nonlocal cases have been extensively studied in the literature to date and have a wide range of applications in the theory of partial differential equations. Sharp constants and the extremal functions of such inequalities plays a crucial role (see for instance, Aubin

(q,p)

\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{N}

(1.1)

S\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\,\,1<q<p^{*}:={Np}/{(N-p)},

for any function
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
1\leq p<N

is the Sobolev constant. The best constant

(1.2)

\lambda_{q}(\Omega):=\inf_{0\neq u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx}{\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}

of the inequality (

of the following Dirichlet boundary value problem (see Ôtani
[

(1.3)

-\Delta_{p}u:=-\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=\lambda_{q}(\Omega)\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{p-q}|u|^{q-2}u\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

In the case
q=p

\lambda_{p}(\Omega)
is equal to the first eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplace operator with the Dirichlet boundary condition, which is widely studied in the literature, see Lindqvist
[

In the case

For

is achieved at a solution

In the nonlocal context, for any

is achieved at a solution

-Laplace equation,

(1.7)

where

Our contribution:

We start with the existence results (Theorem
2.13
-
2.15
) for the problem
\mathcal{(S)}
, where the idea stem from the approximation approach in Boccardo-Orsina
[
17
]
. To this end, employing the idea from Boccardo-Murat
[
16
]
, a gradient convergence theorem (Theorem
7.1
) is established in the mixed case that is very crucial to pass the limit in obtaining our existence results. It turns out that for
0<\delta\leq 1
, a solution
u
lies in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Although it is worth mentioning that the situation is different for
\delta>1
, since here we only find solutions
u
in
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
such that
u^{\frac{\delta+p-1}{p}}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Such phenomena is referred to as the boundary condition of our solutions. Indeed, a more general boundary condition is considered in Definition
2.9
. Under this definition, a uniqueness result (Theorem
2.17
) is proved. We emphasis that to deal with our general boundary condition, the solutions may not belong to
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and therefore, we cannot apply the density arguments as in Lemma
5.1
that is an important ingredient to prove the uniqueness result in Corollary
5.2
. We overcome this difficulty by establishing a comparison principle (see Lemma
4.5
) for subsolutions and supersolutions of the problem
\mathcal{(S)}
following the idea from Canino-Sciunzi
[
24
]
. As a consequence, we deduce a symmetry result, that in particular gives radial solution, provided the given data is radial (Theorem
2.18
). Further, we obtain a regularity result in Theorem
2.16
.

Next, we establish a mixed Sobolev inequality with an extremal (Theorem
2.19
). It is shown that such extremals are simple in the sense that they are equal upto a multiplicative constant. Moreover, extremals are associated with the obtained solutions of
\mathcal{(S)}
. We also observe that the mixed Sobolev inequality is a necessary and sufficient condition (Theorem
2.21
) for the existence of solutions for the problem
\mathcal{(S)}
and an analogous result holds in the separate local and nonlocal cases (Theorem
2.22
-
2.23
). As a byproduct, we prove that the singular mixed local and nonlocal
p
-Laplace equation
\mathcal{(S)}
admits a weak solution if and only if the singular
p
-Laplace equation
\mathcal{(P)}
does so (Corollary
2.25
). As far as we are aware, our main results are new even in the semilinear case
p=2
.

Preliminaries and main results

In this section, we present some known results for the fractional Sobolev spaces, see
[
34
]
for more details.
Let

E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

be a measurable set. Recall that the Lebesgue space

L^{p}(E),1\leq p<\infty
,
is defined as the space of
p
-integrable functions
u:E\to\mathbb{R}
with the finite norm

\|u\|_{L^{p}(E)}=\left(\int\limits_{E}|u(x)|^{p}\penalty\ dx\right)^{1/p}.

By

L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(E)

we denote the space of locally
p
-integrable functions, means that,

u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(E)

if and only if

u\in L^{p}(F)

for every compact subset

F\subset E
. In the case
0<p<1
, we denote by
L^{p}(E)
a set of measurable functions such that

\int_{E}|u(x)|^{p}\penalty\ dx<\infty
.

If
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, is defined
as a Banach space of locally integrable weakly differentiable functions

u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
equipped with the following norm:

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

The space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is defined as a closure of a space
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
of smooth functions with compact support in the norm of the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
be any open set. The fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
,
0<s<1<p<\infty
, is defined by

W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p}+s}}\in L^{p}(\Omega\times\Omega)\Big\}

and endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The fractional Sobolev space with zero boundary values is defined by

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)={\big\{u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\big\}}.

Both
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
and
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
are reflexive Banach spaces, see
[
34
]
. The space
W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is defined analogously.

For the rest of the article, we shall assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
is a Lipschitz bounded domain,
0<s<1<p<\infty
,
\delta>0
and denote by
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
to mean the classical Sobolev space with first weak derivatives, unless otherwise mentioned. The next result asserts that the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in the fractional Sobolev space, see
[
34
, Proposition
2.2
]

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s)>0
such that

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)},\quad\forall\,u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

Next, we have the following result from
[
20

2.1
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists a constant

such that

(2.1)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx,\quad\forall\,u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

Remark 2.4

Using Lemma

Theorem 2.17

Let
\delta>0
and
f\in L^{t}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be nonnegative such that

if

,

if

and

. Then the problem
(\mathcal{S})
admits at most one weak solution in

.

As a consequence of Theorem

, we obtain the following symmetry result:

Theorem 2.18
.

Let

,

and

be nonnegative such that

if

,

if

and

. Assume that

and

Then every weak solution

. In particular, if

Our main result associated with
\mu(\Omega)
stated as follows:

Theorem 2.19
.

Let

and

\mu(\Omega):=\inf_{v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy:\int_{\Omega}|v|^{1-\delta}f\,dx=1\right\}\\
=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{p}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{\delta}(x)-u_{\delta}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1-\delta-p}{1-\delta}}.

(b)

(Mixed Sobolev Inequality) Moreover, for every

, the following mixed Sobolev inequality

(2.9)

3.
Preliminaries for the existence and regularity results

Throughout this section, we assume the space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
under the norm
\|\cdot\|
given by (
2.3
). For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a nonnegative
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
, let
f_{n}(x):=\min\{f(x),n\}
and consider the following approximated problem

[formula]

-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=\frac{f_{n}(x)}{\big(u^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

First, we obtain the following useful result for a general mixed nonsingular problem.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be nonnegative. Then, there exists a unique solution
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
of the problem

(3.1)

\begin{split}-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=g\text{ in }\Omega,\,u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{split}

Moreover, for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C(\omega)
such that
u\geq C(\omega)>0
in
\omega
.

Proof.

Existence:
We define the energy functional
J:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
by

J(v):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx+\frac{1}{p}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy-\int_{\Omega}gv\,dx.

•

Coercive:
We observe that
J
is coercive. Indeed, by Lemma
2.5
and using the fact that
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}J(v)&\geq\frac{\|v\|^{p}}{p}-C|\Omega|^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|v\|,\end{split}

where
C>0
is the Sobolev constant. Since
p>1
, from (
3.2
), we get
J
is coercive.

•

Weak lower semicontinuity:
Firstly, we observe that
J
is convex. To this end, we write
J=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}
, where for
i=1,2,3
we define
J_{i}:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

J_{1}(v):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx,

J_{2}(v):=\frac{1}{p}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy,

and

J_{3}(v):=-\int_{\Omega}gv\,dx.

Since
p>1
, using the convexity of
|\cdot|^{p}
, we obtain that both the functional
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
are convex. It directly follows that
J_{3}
is the linear functional. As a consequence, the functional
J
become convex and moreover
J
is a
C^{1}
functional. Thus
J
is weakly lower semicontinuous.

Thus the coercive, weak lower semicontinuty and
C^{1}
property of
J
asserts that
J
has a minimizer, say
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
which solves the following problem

(3.3)

-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=g\text{ in }\Omega.

Uniqueness:
Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
solves the problem (
3.3
). Thus, for every
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we have

(3.4)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{1}\nabla\phi\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{1}(x,y)\big)\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}g\phi\,dx,\end{split}

and

(3.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{2}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{2}\nabla\phi\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{2}(x,y)\big)\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}g\phi\,dx.\end{split}

Choosing
\phi=u_{1}-u_{2}
and then subtracting (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we arrive at

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{1}-|\nabla u_{2}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{2}\right)\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2})\,dx\\
&+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\mathcal{A}\big(u_{1}(x,y)\big)-\mathcal{A}\big(u_{2}(x,y)\big)\right)\big((u_{1}-u_{2})(x)-(u_{1}-u_{2})(y)\big)\,d\mu=0.\end{split}

Applying Lemma
2.6
, both the terms in the above estimate become nonnegative and hence we obtain from (
3.6
),

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{1}-|\nabla u_{2}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{2}\right)\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2})\,dx=0.\end{split}

Again, applying Lemma
2.6
in (
3.7
), we obtain
u_{1}=u_{2}
in
\Omega
. Therefore the solution of (
3.3
) is unique.

Boundedness:
In order to prove the boundedness, let

A(k):=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)\geq k\}\,\,\text{for any}\,\,k\geq 1.

Choosing
\phi_{k}:=(u-k)^{+}=\max\{u-k,0\}
as a test function in (
3.3
), we have

(3.8)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{k}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)\big(\phi_{k}(x)-\phi_{k}(y)\big)\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}g\phi_{k}\,dx.\end{split}

First we estimate the nonlocal integral in (
3.8
). To this end, we observe that

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)\big(\phi_{k}(x)-\phi_{k}(y)\big)\\
&=|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\big((u(x)-k)^{+}-(u(y)-k)^{+}\big)\\
&=\begin{cases}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p},\text{ if }u(x)>k,\,u(y)>k,\\
(u(x)-u(y))^{p-1}\big(u(x)-k\big),\text{ if }u(x)>k\geq u(y),\\
\big(u(y)-u(x)\big)^{p-1}\big(u(y)-k\big),\text{ if }u(y)>k\geq u(x),\\
0,\text{ if }u(x)\leq k,\,u(y)\leq k.\end{cases}\\
&\geq 0.\end{split}

Therefore, using (
3.9
) in (
3.8
) along with the continuity of the mapping
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{l}(\Omega)
for some
l>p
, from Lemma
2.5

(3.10)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{k}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}g\phi_{k}\,dx\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{A(k)}(u-k)\,dx\\
\leq C_{0}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|A(k)|^{\frac{l-1}{l}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
C_{0}
is the Sobolev constant. Hence, we have

(3.11)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{p}\,dx\leq C|A(k)|^{\frac{p(l-1)}{l(p-1)}},

for some positive constant
C=C(C_{0},\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})
. Now choose
h
such that
1\leq k<h
. Then
u(x)-k\geq(h-k)
on
A(h)
and
A(h)\subset A(k)
. Noting this fact along with (
3.11
), we get

(h-k)^{p}|A(h)|^{\frac{p}{l}}\leq\left(\int_{A(h)}\big(u(x)-k\big)^{l}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{l}}\leq\left(\int_{A(k)}\big(u(x)-k\big)^{l}\penalty\ dx\right)^{\frac{p}{l}}\\
\leq C_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{p}\penalty\ dx\leq C|A(k)|^{\frac{p(l-1)}{l(p-1)}},

for some positive constant
C=C(C_{0},\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})
. Therefore, we have

|A(h)|\leq\frac{C}{(h-k)^{l}}|A(k)|^{\frac{l-1}{p-1}}.

We observe that
\frac{l-1}{p-1}>1
. Thus using
[
52
, Lemma B.1]
we obtain

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C,

for some positive constant
C=C(C_{0},\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})
. Hence,
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Positivity:
Choosing
u_{-}:=\min\{u,0\}
as a test function in (
3.3
) and using
g\geq 0
, we have

(3.12)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{-}|^{p}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)\big(u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y)\big)\,d\mu&=\int_{\Omega}gu_{-}\,dx\leq 0,\end{split}

where
\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)=|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)
.
We write,

\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}=\cup_{i=1}^{4}S_{i},

where

\begin{split}S_{1}&=\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:u(x)\geq 0,u(y)\geq 0\big\},\\
S_{2}&=\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:u(x)\geq 0,u(y)<0\big\},\\
S_{3}&=\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:u(x)<0,u(y)\geq 0\big\},\\
S_{4}&=\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:u(x)<0,u(y)<0\big\}.\end{split}

For
(x,y)\in S_{1}
, we have
u_{-}(x)=u_{-}(y)=0
. If
(x,y)\in S_{2}
, then
u_{-}(x)=0
,
u_{-}(y)=u(y)
, which gives
u(x)-u(y)\geq u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y)>0
. When
(x,y)\in S_{3}
, we have
u_{-}(x)=u(x),u_{-}(y)=0
. Hence,
u(x)-u(y)\leq u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y)<0
. In case of
(x,y)\in S_{4}
, we have
u_{-}(x)=u(x)
and
u_{-}(y)=u(y)
.

Therefore, for any
S_{i}
with
i=1,\ldots,4
, if
(x,y)\in S_{i}
, then we have

(3.13)

|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\big(u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y)\big)\geq 0.

Using (
3.13
) in (
3.12
) we obtain

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{-}|^{p}\,dx=0.

Hence,
u\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Since
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, the function
u
is nonnegative in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and from the above step,
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. As
g\not\equiv 0
, we have
u\not\equiv 0
in
\Omega
. Thus, using
[
46
, Theorem
8.2
]
for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C(\omega)>0
such that
u\geq C(\omega)>0
in
\Omega
. Hence
u>0
in
\Omega
.
∎

Next, we obtain the following existence and further qualitative properties of solutions for the problem
\mathcal{(A)}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique positive solution
u_{n}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
of
(\mathcal{A})
. Moreover,
u_{n+1}\geq u_{n}
in
\Omega
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C(\omega)>0
(independent of
n
) such that
u_{n}\geq C(\omega)>0
in
\omega
.

Proof.

Existence:
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. Then, by Lemma
3.1
, for every
h\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

(3.14)

\begin{split}-\Delta_{p}v+(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}v=\frac{f_{n}}{\big(h^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}\text{ in }\Omega,\,v>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,v=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{split}

Therefore we can define the operator
T:L^{p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Omega)
by
T(h)=v
, where
v
solves (
3.14
). Choosing
v
as a test function in (
3.14
), using Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}n^{\delta+1}v\,dx\leq Cn^{\delta+1}|\Omega|^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
C>0
is the Sobolev constant. This gives

\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}n^{\frac{\delta+1}{p-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Keeping in mind Lemma
2.5
and the above estimate, arguing exactly as in the proof of
[
23
, Proposition
2.3
]
, it follows that the mapping
T
is continuous and compact. Hence, by the Schauder fixed point theorem, there exists a fixed point of
T,
say
u_{n}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Thus,
u_{n}
solves the problem
\mathcal{(A)}
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.1
, we have
u_{n}>0
in
\Omega
such that, for every
\omega\Subset
there exists a positive constant
C(\omega)
satisfying
u_{n}\geq C(\omega)>0
in
\omega
.

Monotonicity and uniqueness:
Since
u_{n}
and
u_{n+1}
are positive solutions of the problem
(\mathcal{A})
, for every
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we have

(3.15)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}(x)}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}\phi\,dx,

(3.16)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n+1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n+1}\nabla\phi\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n+1}(x,y)\big)\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n+1}(x)}{\big(u_{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}\big)^{\delta}}\phi\,dx.

Choosing
\phi=(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}
as a test function in (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we have

(3.17)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\,dx\\
\quad+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)\Big(\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(x)-\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(y)\Big)\,d\mu\\
\quad\quad=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}(x)}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(x)\,dx,

and

(3.18)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n+1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n+1}\nabla(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\,dx\\
\quad+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n+1}(x,y)\big)\Big(\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(x)-\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(y)\Big)\,d\mu\\
\quad\quad=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n+1}(x)}{\big(u_{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}\big)^{\delta}}\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(x)\,dx,

respectively. Noting the fact
f_{n}(x)\leq f_{n+1}(x)
for
x\in\Omega
, we have

(3.19)

\int_{\Omega}\left\{\frac{f_{n}(x)}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}-\frac{f_{n+1}(x)}{\big(u_{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}\big)^{\delta}}\right\}\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(x)\,dx\leq 0.

Subtracting (
3.17
) with (
3.18
) and using the fact (
3.19
), we get

(3.20)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u_{n+1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n+1}\big)\nabla(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\,dx\\
&\quad+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)-\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n+1}(x,y)\big)\Big)\Big(\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(x)-\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(y)\Big)\,d\mu\leq 0.\end{split}

Following the same arguments from the proof of
[
54
, Lemma
9
]
, we obtain

(3.21)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)-\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n+1}(x,y)\big)\Big)\Big(\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(x)-\big(u_{n}-u_{n+1}\big)^{+}(y)\Big)\,d\mu\geq 0.

Hence, applying (
3.21
) in (
3.20
) we obtain

(3.22)

\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u_{n+1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n+1}\big)\nabla(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\,dx\leq 0.

Then, using Lemma
2.6
we obtain
u_{n+1}\geq u_{n}
in
\Omega.
Uniqueness follows similarly.

Uniform Positivity:
By Lemma
3.1
for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C(\omega)>0
such that
u_{1}\geq C(\omega)>0
in
\omega
. Again, by the monotonicity we have
u_{n}\geq u_{1}
in
\Omega
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

u_{n}(x)\geq C(\omega)>0,\text{ for }x\in\omega,

where
C(\omega)>0
is a constant independent of
n
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

By Lemma
3.2
, since
\{u_{n}\}
is monotone, we can define the pointwise limit of
u_{n}
in
\Omega
, say by
u
. Hence,
u\geq u_{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Below, we prove that
u
is our required solution for the problem
(\mathcal{S})
.

Next, we obtain some boundedness estimates (Lemma
3.4
-
3.8
) for the sequence of positive solutions
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
of the problem
\mathcal{(A)}
given by Lemma
3.2
. These estimates are important to deduce the existence and regularity results.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
0<\delta<1
and
f\in L^{m}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be nonnegative such that
m=\big(\frac{p^{*}}{1-\delta}\big)^{\prime}
if
1<p<N
,
m>1
if
p=N
and
m=1
if
p>N
. Then, the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
, where
u_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
are solutions of the approximate problem
(\mathcal{A})
, is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Because
u_{n}
are solutions of
(\mathcal{A})
, choosing
u_{n}
as a test function in
\mathcal{(A)}
, we get

\|u_{n}\|^{p}\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}(x)u_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}\,dx.

Let
1<p<N
, then noting that
f\in L^{m}(\Omega)
and
(1-\delta)m^{\prime}=p^{*}
, along with Lemma
2.5

\|u_{n}\|^{p}\leq\int_{\Omega}fu_{n}^{1-\delta}\,dx\leq\|f\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{(1-\delta)m^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}}\\
=\|f\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1-\delta}{p^{*}}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\|u_{n}\|^{1-\delta},

for some constant
C>0
, independent of
n
. Therefore, we have

\|u_{n}\|\leq C.

Hence, the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. For
p\geq N
, the result follows similarly.
∎

In fact, the uniform boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}
follows for any integrable function if the mixed Sobolev inequality (
2.9
) holds. More precisely, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
0<\delta<1
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be nonnegative. Then for any
1<p<\infty
, the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
, where
u_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
are solutions of the approximate problem
(\mathcal{A})
, is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, provided (
2.9
) holds.

Proof.

Choosing
u_{n}
as a test function in
(\mathcal{A})
and using the inequality (
2.9
) and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\|u_{n}\|^{p}\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}(x)u_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{1-\delta}f\,dx\leq C\|u_{n}\|^{1-\delta},

for some constant
C>0
(independent of
n
). Hence, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose
\delta=1
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
is nonnegative in
\Omega
. Then, for any
1<p<\infty
, the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
, where
u_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
are solutions of the approximate problem
(\mathcal{A})
, is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Choosing
\phi=u_{n}
as a test function in
(\mathcal{A})
we obtain

(3.23)

\begin{split}\|u_{n}\|^{p}&\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)}u_{n}\,dx\\
&\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Thus,
\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
\delta>1
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
is nonnegative in
\Omega
. Then, for any
1<p<\infty
, the sequences
\left\{u_{n}^{\frac{\delta+p-1}{p}}\right\}
and
\{u_{n}\}
, where
u_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
are solutions of the approximate problem
(\mathcal{A})
, is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega)
respectively.

Proof.

Uniform boundedness in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
:
By Lemma
3.2
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
u_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Therefore, since
\delta>1
, we choose
u_{n}^{\delta}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
as a test function in
(\mathcal{A})
to obtain

(3.24)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla u_{n}^{\delta}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)\big((u_{n}(x)^{\delta}-u_{n}(y)^{\delta}\big)\,d\mu&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}u_{n}^{\delta}\,dx.\end{split}

Using Lemma
2.7
, for almost every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

(3.25)

\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)\big(u_{n}(x)^{\delta}-u_{n}(y)^{\delta}\big)=|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)|^{p-2}\big(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)\big)\big(u_{n}(x)^{\delta}-u_{n}(y)^{\delta}\big)\geq 0.

Therefore, from (
3.25
) and (
3.24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta\Big(\frac{p}{\delta+p-1}\Big)^{p}\left|\nabla u_{n}^{\frac{\delta+p-1}{p}}\right|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla u_{n}^{\delta}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}u_{n}^{\delta}\,dx\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

which gives

(3.26)

\left\|u_{n}^{\frac{\delta+p-1}{p}}\right\|\leq C,

for some constant
C>0
independent of
n
. Thus, the sequence
\left\{u_{n}^{\frac{\delta+p-1}{p}}\right\}
is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Uniform boundedness in
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
:
Let
\omega\Subset\Omega
, then by Lemma
3.2
,
u_{n}\geq C(\omega)>0
in
\omega
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
C(\omega)>0
is a constant independent of
n
. Since,
\delta>1
, then

(3.27)

\int_{\omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx=\Big(\frac{p}{\delta+p-1}\Big)^{p}\int_{\omega}u_{n}^{1-\delta}\left|\nabla u_{n}^{\frac{\delta+p-1}{p}}\right|^{p}\,dx\leq\Big(\frac{p}{\delta+p-1}\Big)^{p}C(\omega)^{1-\delta}C^{p},

where the constant
C
is given by (
3.26
). Therefore, the sequence
\left\{\nabla u_{n}\right\}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. Again using (
3.26
) and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

(3.28)

\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{p}\,dx\leq|\Omega|^{\frac{\delta-1}{\delta+p-1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{\delta+p-1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{\delta+p-1}}\leq C,

for some constant
C>0
, independent of
n
. Hence, from (
3.27
) and (
3.28
), it turns out that
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
∎

The following a priori estimate is crucial to prove our regularity result.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\delta>0
and
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be nonnegative such that
q>\frac{p^{*}}{p^{*}-p}
if
1<p<N
,
q>\frac{l}{l-p}
for some
l>p
if
p=N
and
q=1
if
p>N
. Then
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C
, for some positive constant
C
independent of
n
.

Proof.

We prove the result only for the case
1<p<N
, since the proof for
p\geq N
is analogous. Let
k\geq 1
and define
A(k)=\{x\in\Omega:u_{n}(x)\geq k\}
. Choosing
\phi_{k}(x)=(u_{n}-k)^{+}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
as a test function in
(\mathcal{A})
, using Hölder’s inequality with the exponents
p^{*^{\prime}},p^{*}
and then by Young’s inequality with exponents
p
and
p^{\prime}
along with (
3.9
) and Lemma
2.5
we obtain

(3.29)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}\phi_{k}\,dx\leq\int_{A(k)}f(x)\phi_{k}\,dx\\
\leq\left(\int_{A(k)}f^{p^{*^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi_{k}^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\leq C\left(\int_{A(k)}f^{p^{*^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\leq\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{p}\,dx+C(\epsilon)\left(\int_{A(k)}f^{p^{*^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*^{\prime}}}}.

Here,
C
is the Sobolev constant and
C(\epsilon)>0
is some constant depending on
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. Note that
q>\frac{p^{*}}{p^{*}-p}
gives
q>p^{*^{\prime}}
. Therefore, fixing
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and again using Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{q}{p^{*^{\prime}}}
and
\big(\frac{q}{p^{*^{\prime}}}\big)^{\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{A(k)}f^{p^{*^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*^{\prime}}}}\leq C\left(\int_{A(k)}f^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}}|A(k)|^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\big(\frac{q}{p^{*^{\prime}}}\big)^{\prime}}}.

Let
h>0
be such that
1\leq k<h
. Then,
A(h)\subset A(k)
and for any
x\in A(h)
, we have
u_{n}(x)\geq h
. So,
u_{n}(x)-k\geq h-k
in
A(h)
.
Noting these facts, for some constant
C>0
(independent of
n
), we have

(h-k)^{p}|A(h)|^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}\leq\left(\int_{A(h)}(u_{n}-k)^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}\leq\left(\int_{A(k)}(u_{n}-k)^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}\\
\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{p}\,dx\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{p^{\prime}}|A(k)|^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\big(\frac{q}{p^{*^{\prime}}}\big)^{\prime}}}.

Thus, for some constant
C>0
(independent of
n
), we have

|A(h)|\leq C\frac{\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p^{*}}{p-1}}}{(h-k)^{p^{*}}}|A(k)|^{\alpha},\,\,\text{where}\,\,\alpha={\frac{p^{*}p^{\prime}}{pp^{*^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\big(\frac{q}{p^{*^{\prime}}}\big)^{\prime}}}.

Due to the assumption,
q>\frac{p^{*}}{p^{*}-p}
, we have
\alpha>1
. Hence, by
[
52
, Lemma B.
1
]
, we have

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C,

for some positive constant
C>0
, independent of
n
.
∎

4.
Preliminaries for the uniqueness results

Throughout this section, we assume that
\delta>0
and
f\in L^{t}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
is nonnegative such that
t=(p^{*})^{\prime}
if
1<p<N
,
t>1
if
p=N
and
t=1
if
p>N
. Here, we obtain a comparison principle (Lemma
4.5
), that is crucial to obtain the uniqueness result. Firstly, we define the notion of weak subsolution and weak supersolution for the problem
(\mathcal{S})
.

Definition 4.1
.

(Weak supersolution)
We say that
u\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}(\Omega)
is a weak supersolution of the problem
(\mathcal{S})
, if

u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\text{ and }\frac{f}{u^{\delta}}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),

such that for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

(4.1)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big){\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)}\,d\mu\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{u(x)^{\delta}}\phi(x)\,dx.

Definition 4.2
.

(Weak subsolution)
We say that
u\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}(\Omega)
is a weak subsolution of the problem
(\mathcal{S})
, if

u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\text{ and }\frac{f}{u^{\delta}}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),

such that for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

(4.2)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big((u(x,y)\big){\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)}\,d\mu\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{u(x)^{\delta}}\phi(x)\,dx.

Remark 4.3
.

Note that Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
ensures that Definition
4.1
-
4.2
makes sense.

Let us fix a weak supersolution
v
of the problem
(\mathcal{S})
and define the following nonempty, closed and convex subset of
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
given by

\mathcal{K}:=\{\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega):0\leq\phi\leq v\text{ in }\Omega\}.

For
k>0
, we define the truncated function

g_{k}(l):=\begin{cases}\text{min}\{l^{-\delta},k\},&\text{ if }l>0,\\
k,&\text{ if }l\leq 0.\end{cases}

Let
\mathcal{G}_{k}
be the primitive of
g_{k}
and we define the functional
J_{k}:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to[-\infty,+\infty]
by

J_{k}(\phi):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\,dx+\frac{1}{p}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}(\phi)\,dx.

Note that
J_{k}
is coercive. Indeed, for
1<p<N
with
f\in L^{t}(\Omega)
where
t=(p^{*})^{\prime}
, we have

\left|\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}(\phi)\,dx\right|\leq k\int_{\Omega}f|\phi|\,dx\leq k\|f\|_{L^{t}(\Omega)}\|\phi\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}\leq kC_{0}\|f\|_{L^{t}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

for every
\phi\in\mathcal{K}
,
C_{0}
is the Sobolev constant obtained by Lemma
2.5
. Similar estimate holds for
p\geq N
. Hence for somce constant
C>0
, we have

J_{k}(\phi)\geq\frac{1}{p}||\phi||^{p}-C\|\phi\|,

where
\|\cdot\|
is given by the gradient norm (
2.3
). Therefore,
J_{k}
is coercive over
\mathcal{K}
. Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we get
J_{k}
is weakly lower semicontinuous over
\mathcal{K}
. Therefore there exists a minimum of
J_{k}
, say
z\in\mathcal{K}
such that for every
\psi\in z+\big(W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\big)
with
\psi\in\mathcal{K}
, we have

(4.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{p-2}\nabla z\nabla(\psi-z)\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big((z(x,y)\big)((\psi-z)(x)-(\psi-z)(y))\,d\mu\\
&\quad\geq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(z)(\psi-z)\,dx,\end{split}

where
L^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
denotes the set of bounded functions with compact support in
\Omega
. Now we establish the following variational inequality.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every nonnegative
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

(4.4)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{p-2}\nabla z\nabla\phi\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big((z(x,y)\big)(\phi(x)-\phi(y))\,d\mu\geq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(z)\phi\,dx,

where
z\in\mathcal{K}
is given by (
4.3
).

Proof.

Let us consider a real valued function
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
0\leq\xi\leq 1
in
\mathbb{R}
,
\xi\equiv 1
in
[-1,1]
and
\xi\equiv 0
in
(-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty)
. Define the function
\phi_{h}:=\xi(\frac{z}{h})\phi
and
\phi_{h,t}:=\text{min}\{z+t\phi_{h},v\}
with
h\geq 1
and
t>0
for a given nonnegative
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
.
Then by the inequality (
4.3
), we have

(4.5)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{p-2}\nabla z\nabla(\phi_{h,t}-z)\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big((z(x,y)\big))((\phi_{h,t}-z)(x)-(\phi_{h,t}-z)(y))\,d\mu\\
&\quad\geq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(z)(\phi_{h,t}-z)\,dx.\end{split}

We define,

\displaystyle I:

\displaystyle=C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\phi_{h,t}-z)|^{2}(|\nabla\phi_{h,t}|+|\nabla z|)^{p-2}\,dx

\displaystyle+C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\big(|\phi_{h,t}(x)-\phi_{h,t}(y)|+|z(x)-z(y)|\big)^{p-2}\big((\phi_{h,t}-z)(x)-(\phi_{h,t}-z)(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy,

where
C
is given by Lemma
2.6
. First applying Lemma
2.6
and then by using (
4.5
), we have

I\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\phi_{h,t}|^{p-2}\nabla\phi_{h,t}-|\nabla z|^{p-2}\nabla z\right)\nabla(\phi_{h,t}-z)\,dx\\
\quad+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(\mathcal{A}(\phi_{h,t}(x,y))-\mathcal{A}(z(x,y))\big)\big((\phi_{h,t}-z)(x)-(\phi_{h,t}-z)(y)\big)\,d\mu\\
\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{h,t}|^{p-2}\nabla\phi_{h,t}\nabla(\phi_{h,t}-z)\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big((\phi_{h,t}(x,y)\big)\big((\phi_{h,t}-z)(x)-(\phi_{h,t}-z)(y)\big)\,d\mu\\
\quad\quad-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(z)(\phi_{h,t}-z)\,dx.

Therefore,

(4.6)

I-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\big(\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(\phi_{h,t})-\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(z)\big)(\phi_{h,t}-z)\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{h,t}|^{p-2}\nabla\phi_{h,t}\nabla(\phi_{h,t}-z)\,dx\\
+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(\phi_{h,t}(x,y)\big)\big((\phi_{h,t}-z)(x)-(\phi_{h,t}-z)(y)\big)\,d\mu-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(\phi_{h,t})(\phi_{h,t}-z)\,dx\\
=\Big\{\int_{\Omega}g(x)\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x,y)\,dxdy-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}{{}^{\prime}}(\phi_{h,t})(\phi_{h,t}-z-t\phi_{h})\,dx\Big\}\\
+t\Big\{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{h,t}|^{p-2}\nabla\phi_{h}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(\phi_{h,t}(x,y)\big)\big(\phi_{h}(x)-\phi_{h}(y)\big)\,d\mu-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(\phi_{h,t})\phi_{h}\,dx\Big\},

where

g(x)=|\nabla\phi_{h,t}|^{p-2}\nabla\phi_{h,t}\nabla(\phi_{h,t}-z-t\phi_{h})

and

h(x,y)=\frac{\mathcal{A}\big(\phi_{h,t}(x,y)\big)\big((\phi_{h,t}-z-t\phi_{h})(x)-(\phi_{h,t}-z-t\phi_{h})(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}.

Let us denote by

g_{v}(x)=|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\nabla(\phi_{h,t}-z-t\phi_{h})

and

h_{v}(x,y)=\frac{\mathcal{A}\big((v(x,y)\big)\big((\phi_{h,t}-z-t\phi_{h})(x)-(\phi_{h,t}-z-t\phi_{h})(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}.

Now following the exact arguments as in the proof of
[
45
, Lemma
4.6
]
and
[
23
, Lemma
4.1
]
we obtain

\int_{\Omega}g(x,y)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}g_{v}(x)\,dx\text{ and }\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x,y)\,dxdy=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h_{v}(x,y)\,dxdy.

Noting the above property along with the fact that
v
is a weak supersolution of
(\mathcal{S})
, we choose
(z+t\phi_{h}-\phi_{h,t})
as a test function in (
4.1
) and using

\displaystyle\phi_{h,t}=\begin{cases}v\text{ on }S_{v}:=\{x\in\Omega:z(x)+t\phi_{h}(x)\geq v(x)\},\\
z+t\phi_{h}\text{ on }S_{v}^{c},\end{cases}

we obtain

\int_{\Omega}g(x)\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x,y)\,dxdy-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(\phi_{h,t})(\phi_{h,t}-z-t\phi_{h})\,dx\\
\quad=\int_{\Omega}g_{v}(x)\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h_{v}(x,y)\,dxdy-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(\phi_{h,t})(\phi_{h,t}-z-t\phi_{h})\,dx\leq 0.

Using the above fact in (
4.6
) with the observation that
I\geq 0
and
\phi_{h,t}-z\leq t\phi_{h}
, we have

(4.7)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla&\phi_{h,t}|^{p-2}\nabla\phi_{h,t}\nabla\phi_{h}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big((\phi_{h,t}(x,y)\big)\big((\phi_{h}(x)-\phi_{h}(y)\big)\,d\mu\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(\phi_{h,t})\phi_{h}\,dx\geq-\int_{\Omega}f|\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(\phi_{h,t})-\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(z)|\phi_{h}\,dx.\end{split}

Noting
z\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and recalling the definition of
\phi_{h,t}
, by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, letting
t\to 0
in (
4.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{p-2}\nabla z\nabla\phi_{h}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big((z(x,y)\big)\big((\phi_{h}(x)-\phi_{h}(y)\big)\,d\mu\geq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(z)\phi_{h}\,dx.

Now, passing the limit as
h\to\infty
in the above inequality, the result follows.
∎

Now we have the following comparison principle.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
u
is a weak subsolution of
(\mathcal{S})
such that
u\leq 0
on
\partial\Omega
and
v
be a weak supersolution of
(\mathcal{S})
. Then
u\leq v
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
k>0
and
\epsilon=2k^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}
. Since
u\leq 0
on
\partial\Omega
, for
z\in\mathcal{K}
as given by Lemma
4.4
, we have
(u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. For
\eta>0
, let us define

T_{\eta}(s):=\min\{s,\eta\}
, if
s\geq 0
and
T_{\eta}(-s)=-T_{\eta}(s)
, if
s<0
. Applying Lemma
4.4
, for any
\eta>0
, by density arguments, it follows that

(4.8)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{p-2}\nabla z\nabla T_{\eta}\big((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\big)\,dx\\
&\quad+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(z(x,y)\big)\big(T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(x))-T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(y))\big)\,d\mu\\
&\quad\quad\geq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(z)T_{\eta}\big((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\big)\,dx.\end{split}

Since
(u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, there exists a sequence
\phi_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\phi_{n}\to(u-z-\epsilon)^{+}
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. We define

\psi_{n,\eta}:=T_{\eta}\big(\text{min}\{(u-z-\epsilon)^{+},\phi_{n}^{+}\}\big)\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Since
u
is a weak subsolution of
(\mathcal{S})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\psi_{n,\eta}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)\big(\psi_{n,\eta}(x)-\psi_{n,\eta}(y))\big)\,d\mu\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f}{u^{\delta}}\psi_{n,\eta}\,dx.

As
|\nabla u|^{p}
is integrable in the support of
(u-z-\epsilon)^{+}
, passing the limit as
n\to\infty
,

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla T_{\eta}\big((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\big)\,dx\\
&\quad+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)\big(T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(x))-T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(x))\big)\,d\mu\\
&\quad\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f}{u^{\delta}}T_{\eta}(u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\,dx.\end{split}

Following the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 4.2]
we have

(4.10)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{A}(u(x,y))\big(T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(x))-T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(x))\big)\\
&=\mathcal{A}(u(x,y))\big((u-z)(x)-(u-z)(y)\big)H(x,y),\end{split}

and

(4.11)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{A}(z(x,y))\big(T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(x))-T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(x))\big)\\
&=\mathcal{A}(z(x,y))\big((u-z)(x)-(u-z)(y)\big)H(x,y),\end{split}

with

H(x,y):=\frac{T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(x))-T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(y))}{(u-z)(x)-(u-z)(y)},

where
(u-z)(x)-(u-z)(y)\neq 0
. Subtracting (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) and then, using (
4.10
), (
4.11
) along with Lemma
2.6
, we have

C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla T_{\eta}\big((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\big)|^{2}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla z|)^{p-2}\,dx\\
+C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\big(|u(x)-u(y)|+|z(x)-z(y)|\big)^{p-2}\big((u-z)(x)-(u-z)(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}H(x,y)\,dxdy\\
\leq\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla z|^{p-2}\nabla z)\nabla T_{\eta}\big((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\big)\,dx\\
+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(\mathcal{A}(u(x,y))-\mathcal{A}(z(x,y))\big)\big(T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(x))-T_{\eta}((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}(y))\big)\,d\mu\\
\leq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\left(\frac{1}{u^{\delta}}-\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(z)\right)T_{\eta}\big((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\big)\,dx\\
\leq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\big(\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(u)-\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}(z)\big)T_{\eta}\big((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\big)\,dx\leq 0.

We note that in the final estimate above, we have used
\epsilon>k^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}
, the definition of
g_{k}
and the fact that
u\geq\epsilon
in the support of
(u-z-\epsilon)^{+}
. Therefore, using the nonnegativity of
H
, letting
\eta\to\infty
, the above estimate yields

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\big((u-z-\epsilon)^{+}\big)|^{2}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla z|)^{p-2}\,dx=0.

As a consequence,
u\leq z+2k^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq v+2k^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}.
Letting
k\to\infty
, we get
u\leq v
in
\Omega
.
∎

5.
Preliminaries for the mixed Sobolev inequalities

Throughout this section, we assume
0<\delta<1
,
f\in L^{m}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
, where
m
is given by (
2.8
) unless otherwise mentioned. For
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
by
\|v\|
, we mean the following norm as defined by (
2.2
)

(5.1)

\|v\|:=\|v\|_{W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Let
u_{n}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be the solution of the problem
(\mathcal{A})
as given by Lemma
3.2
and denote by
u_{\delta}
to be the pointwise limit of
u_{n}
in
\Omega
. Note that, by Theorem
2.13
,
u_{\delta}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is the weak solution of
(\mathcal{S})
. Moreover, by Remark
3.3
, we have
u_{n}\leq u_{\delta}
in
\Omega
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Next, we obtain some auxiliary results, which are very useful to prove Theorem
2.19
.

First, we prove the following result, which allows us to choose test functions from the space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
in the equation
\mathcal{(W)}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\delta>0
,
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be nonnegative and
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of the problem
(\mathcal{S})
, then
(\mathcal{W})
holds for every
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
solves the problem
(\mathcal{S})
. Therefore, for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

(5.2)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big){\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)}\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{u(x)^{\delta}}\phi(x)\,dx.

By density, for every
\psi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, there exists a sequence of functions
0\leq\psi_{n}\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\psi_{n}\to|\psi|
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
and pointwise almost everywhere in
\Omega
. We observe that

(5.3)

\Big|\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{u(x)^{\delta}}\psi\,dx\Big|\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{u(x)^{\delta}}|\psi|\,dx\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{u(x)^{\delta}}\psi_{n}\,dx=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u,\psi_{n}\rangle\\
=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\psi_{n}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big){\big(\psi_{n}(x)-\psi_{n}(y)\big)}\,d\mu\\
\leq C\|u\|^{p-1}\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\psi_{n}\|\leq C\|u\|^{p-1}\||\psi|\|\leq C\|u\|^{p-1}\|\psi\|,

for some positive constant
C
(independent of
n
). Let
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, then there exists
\phi_{n}\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
converges to
\phi
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Now, using
\psi=\phi_{n}-\phi
in (
5.3
), we obtain

(5.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big|\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{u(x)^{\delta}}(\phi_{n}-\phi)\,dx\Big|\leq C\|u\|^{p-1}\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\phi_{n}-\phi\|=0.

Again, since
\phi_{n}\to\phi
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
, we have

(5.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(\phi_{n}-\phi)\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big){\big((\phi_{n}-\phi)(x)-(\phi_{n}-\phi)(y)\big)}\,d\mu\right\}=0.

Hence, using (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) in (
5.2
) the result follows.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
5.1
, we have the following simple proof of the uniqueness result.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\delta>0
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be nonnegavite. Then, the problem
\mathcal{(S)}
admits at most one weak solution in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By contradiction, suppose
u_{1},u_{2}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
are two weak solutions of the problem
\mathcal{(S)}
. Then, by Lemma
5.1
, for every
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we have

(5.6)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{1}(x,y)\big){\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)}\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{u_{1}(x)^{\delta}}\phi(x)\,dx,

(5.7)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{2}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{2}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{2}(x,y)\big){\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)}\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x)}{u_{2}(x)^{\delta}}\phi(x)\,dx.

Therefore, first choosing
\phi=(u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
in (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) and then subtracting them, we have

(5.8)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left\{|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{1}-|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{1}\right\}\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}\,dx\\
&\quad+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left\{\mathcal{A}\big(u_{1}(x,y)\big)-\mathcal{A}\big(u_{2}(x,y)\big)\right\}{\big((u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}(x)-(u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}(y)\big)}\,d\mu\\
&=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\left\{\frac{1}{u_{1}(x)^{\delta}}-\frac{1}{u_{2}(x)^{\delta}}\right\}(u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}(x)\,dx\leq 0.\end{split}

Again, following the same arguments from the proof of
[
54
, Lemma
9
]
, we obtain

(5.9)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left\{\mathcal{A}\big(u_{1}(x,y)\big)-\mathcal{A}\big(u_{2}(x,y)\big)\right\}{\big((u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}(x)-(u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}(y)\big)}\,d\mu\geq 0.

Hence using (
5.9
) in (
5.8
) and then applying Lemma
2.6
, we have
u_{2}\geq u_{1}
in
\Omega
. Similarly, we obtain
u_{1}\geq u_{2}
in
\Omega
. Hence, the result follows.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Corollary
5.2
extends Theorem
2.17
to the case of
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
to obtain uniqueness in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for every
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we have

(5.10)

\|u_{n}\|^{p}\leq\|\phi\|^{p}+p\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{n}-\phi)}{(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}}f_{n}\,dx.

Moreover, we have

(5.11)

\|u_{n}\|\leq\|u_{n+1}\|\text{ for every }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Let
h\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, then by Lemma
3.1
, there exists a unique solution
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
to the problem

-\Delta_{p}v+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}v=\frac{f_{n}(x)}{(h^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}},\,v>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,v=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

Moreover, we observe that
v
is a minimizer of the functional
J:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

J(\phi):=\frac{1}{p}\|\phi\|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{(h^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}}\phi\,dx.

Therefore, for every
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we have
J(v)\leq J(\phi)
. Hence, we have

(5.12)

\frac{1}{p}\|v\|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{(h^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}}v\,dx\leq\frac{1}{p}\|\phi\|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{(h^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}}\phi\,dx.

Then the inequality (
5.10
) follows by choosing
v=h=u_{n}
in the inequality (
5.12
). Now, choosing
\phi=u_{n+1}
in (
5.10
) and using the monotone property
u_{n}\leq u_{n+1}
from Lemma
3.2
, we obtain
\|u_{n}\|\leq\|u_{n+1}\|
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Upto a subsequence
\{u_{n}\}
converges to
u_{\delta}
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

Proof.

Note that
u_{n}\leq u_{\delta}
. Thus, choosing
\phi=u_{\delta}
in (
5.10
), we obtain

\|u_{n}\|\leq\|u_{\delta}\|,

which gives from the monotone property (
5.11
) of Lemma
5.4
,

(5.13)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|\leq\|u_{\delta}\|.

By Lemma
3.4
, the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and thus, upto a subsequence,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{\delta}
weakly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Therefore

(5.14)

\|u_{\delta}\|\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|.

Hence from (
5.13
), (
5.14
) and the uniform convexity of
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, the result follows.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

As a consequence of Lemma
5.5
, upto a subsequence
\nabla u_{n}\to\nabla u_{\delta}
pointwise almost everywhere in
\Omega
, which coincides with Theorem
7.1
, provided
0<\delta<1
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
I_{\delta}:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be a functional defined by

I_{\delta}(v):=\frac{1}{p}\|v\|^{p}-\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{1-\delta}f\,dx.

Then
u_{\delta}
is a minimizer of the functional
I_{\delta}.

Proof.

We define the auxiliary functional
I_{n}:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
by

I_{n}(v):=\frac{1}{p}\|v\|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}G_{n}(v)f_{n}\,dx,

where

G_{n}(t):=\frac{1}{1-\delta}\Big(t^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{1-\delta}-\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{-\delta}t^{-}.

We observe that
I_{n}
is bounded below, coercive and a
C^{1}
functional. Hence,
I_{n}
consists a minimizer
v_{n}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\langle I_{n}{{}^{\prime}}(v_{n}),\phi\rangle=0,\text{ for all }\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

Now the fact
I_{n}(v_{n})\leq I_{n}(v_{n}^{+})
gives
v_{n}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Therefore,
v_{n}
solves the approximated problem
\mathcal{(A)}
. By the uniqueness result in Lemma
3.2
, we get
u_{n}=v_{n}
and so
u_{n}
is a minimizer of
I_{n}
. Therefore, for every
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we have

(5.15)

I_{n}(u_{n})\leq I_{n}(v^{+}).

Since
u_{n}\leq u_{\delta}
, by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}G_{n}(u_{n})f_{n}\,dx=\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}u_{\delta}^{1-\delta}f\,dx.

Moreover, by Lemma
5.5
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|=\|u_{\delta}\|.

Hence, we have

(5.16)

\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{n}(u_{n})=I_{\delta}(u_{\delta}).

Furthermore, for every
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we get

(5.17)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}G_{n}(v^{+})f_{n}\,dx=\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{1-\delta}f\,dx.

Noting
\|v^{+}\|\leq\|v\|
and using (
5.16
) and (
5.17
) in (
5.15
), we arrive at
I_{\delta}(u_{\delta})\leq I_{\delta}(v)
, for all
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Hence, the result follows.
∎

Using (

6.2
) and (

) in
(\mathcal{A})

2.10

u

(\mathcal{S})
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
2.14

3.6

Proof of Theorem

By Lemma
3.7

. Now following the lines of the proof of Theorem
2.13
, for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
, we obtain

(6.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi\,dx\text{ and }

(6.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f}{u^{\delta}}\phi\,dx.

For the nonlocal part, following the same arguments as in the proof of
[
23
, Theorem

]

Hence, from (
6.4

6.4.
Proof of the mixed Sobolev inequalities

Proof of Theorem
2.19
:

Here we consider the norm of a function
v
in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
defined by (
2.2
) as follows:

\|v\|=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(a)

To obtain the result, it is enough to prove that

\mu(\Omega)=\inf_{v\in S_{\delta}}\|v\|^{p}=\|u_{\delta}\|^{\frac{p(1-\delta-p)}{1-\delta}},

where

S_{\delta}:=\left\{v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|v|^{1-\delta}f\,dx=1\right\}.

To this end, we consider
V_{\delta}=\tau_{\delta}u_{\delta}\in S_{\delta}
, where

\tau_{\delta}=\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{\delta}^{1-\delta}f\,dx\right)^{-\frac{1}{1-\delta}}.

By Lemma
5.1
, choosing
u_{\delta}
as a test function in
(\mathcal{W})
, we get

(6.7)

\|u_{\delta}\|^{p}=\int_{\Omega}u_{\delta}^{1-\delta}f\,dx.

Now using (
6.7
) we have

(6.8)

\|V_{\delta}\|^{p}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla V_{\delta}|^{p}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|V_{\delta}(x)-V_{\delta}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy\\
=(\tau_{\delta})^{p}\|u_{\delta}\|^{p}=\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}u_{\delta}^{1-\delta}f\,dx\Bigg)^{-\frac{p}{1-\delta}}\|u_{\delta}\|^{p}=\|u_{\delta}\|^{\frac{p(1-\delta-p)}{1-\delta}}.

Let
v\in S_{\delta}
and
\lambda=\|v\|^{-\frac{p}{p+\delta-1}}
. By Lemma
5.7
,
u_{\delta}
minimizes the functional
I_{\delta}
, which gives
I_{\delta}(u_{\delta})\leq I_{\delta}(\lambda|v|)
. Therefore, using (
6.7
), we have

\Big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{1-\delta}\Big)\|u_{\delta}\|^{p}=I_{\delta}(u_{\delta})\leq I_{\delta}(\lambda|v|)=\frac{\lambda^{p}}{p}\||v|\|^{p}-\frac{\lambda^{1-\delta}}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{1-\delta}f\,dx\\
=\frac{\lambda^{p}}{p}\||v|\|^{p}-\frac{\lambda^{1-\delta}}{1-\delta}\leq\frac{\lambda^{p}}{p}\|v\|^{p}-\frac{\lambda^{1-\delta}}{1-\delta}=\Big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{1-\delta}\Big)\|v\|^{\frac{p(\delta-1)}{\delta+p-1}}.

Since
v\in S_{\delta}
is arbitrary, the above estimate gives

(6.9)

\|u_{\delta}\|^{\frac{p(1-\delta-p)}{1-\delta}}\leq\inf_{v\in S_{\delta}}\|v\|^{p}.

As
V_{\delta}\in S_{\delta}
, from (
6.8
) and (
6.9
), the result follows. ∎

(b)

Suppose the mixed Sobolev inequality (
2.9
) holds. If
C>\mu(\Omega)
, by (a) and (
6.8
), we have

C\Big(\int_{\Omega}V_{\delta}^{1-\delta}f\,dx\Big)^{\frac{p}{1-\delta}}>\|V_{\delta}\|^{p},

which contradicts our assumption (
2.9
). Conversely, assume that

C\leq\mu(\Omega)=\inf_{v\in S_{\delta}}\|v\|^{p}\leq\|w\|^{p},

for all
w\in S_{\delta}
. Observe that the claim directly follows if
v\equiv 0
. Thus we only deal with the case of
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
for which we have

w=\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{1-\delta}f\,dx\Bigg)^{-\frac{1}{1-\delta}}v\in S_{\delta}.

Therefore, we have

C\leq\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{1-\delta}f\,dx\Bigg)^{-\frac{p}{1-\delta}}\|v\|^{p},

which proves the result.

(c)

From (
6.8
) in
(a)
, we know that
\mu(\Omega)=\|V_{\delta}\|^{p}
. Let
v\in S_{\delta}
be such that
\mu(\Omega)=\|v\|^{p}
. First, we claim that
v
has a constant sign in
\Omega
. Indeed if
v
changes sign in
\Omega
, then using the fact that

\big||v(x)|-|v(y)|\big|<|v(x)-v(y)|,

we have

(6.10)

\||v|\|^{p}<\|v\|^{p}.

Moreover, since
|v|\in S_{\delta}
, we have

\|v\|^{p}=\mu(\Omega)\leq\||v|\|^{p},

which contradicts (
6.10
). Hence, the claim follows. Without loss of generality, we assume that
v\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Using the fact that
V_{\delta},v\geq 0
and
0<1-\delta<1
, we have

g=\left(\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{v}{2}+\frac{V_{\delta}}{2}\Big)^{1-\delta}f\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{v}{2}f^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}}+\frac{V_{\delta}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}}\Big)^{1-\delta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}}\\
\geq\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{v}{2}\right)^{1-\delta}f\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{V_{\delta}}{2}\right)^{1-\delta}f\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}}\\
\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}v^{1-\delta}f\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}V_{\delta}^{1-\delta}f\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1.

Then, since
h=\frac{v+V_{\delta}}{2g}\in S_{\delta}
, we obtain

\mu(\Omega)\leq\|h\|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{g^{p}}\left\|\frac{v+V_{\delta}}{2}\right\|^{p}\leq\frac{\mu(\Omega)}{g^{p}}\leq\mu(\Omega).

Hence, we get
g=1
and therefore,
\frac{v+V_{\delta}}{2}\in S_{\delta}
and

\mu(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left\|\frac{v+V_{\delta}}{2}\right\|=\frac{\|v\|}{2}+\frac{\|V_{\delta}\|}{2}.

Since the norm

v\to\|v\|

is strictly convex, we have
v=V_{\delta}
. So,
\mu(\Omega)=\|v\|^{p}
for some
v\in S_{\delta}
, if and only if
v=V_{\delta}
or
-V_{\delta}
. Thus, if (
2.10
) holds for some
w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
, we have
\gamma w\in S_{\delta}
, where

\gamma=\left(\int_{\Omega}|w|^{1-\delta}f\,dx\right)^{-\frac{1}{1-\delta}}.

Thus
w=\gamma^{-1}V_{\delta}
or
-\gamma^{-1}V_{\delta}
. Since
V_{\delta}=\tau_{\delta}u_{\delta}
, the result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem

7.
Appendix

In this section, we obtain the pointwise convergence of the gradient of the approximate solutions
\{u_{n}\}
found in Lemma
3.2
.

Theorem 7.1
.

(Gradient convergence theorem in the mixed case)

Let
1<p<\infty
. Suppose
\{u_{n}\}
is the sequence of approximate solutions for the problem
(\mathcal{A})
given by Lemma
3.2
and
u
is the pointwise limit of
\{u_{n}\}
. For
0<\delta<1
, let
f\in L^{m}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be nonnegative, where
m
is given by (
2.8
) and for
\delta\geq 1
, let
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be nonnegative respectively. Then, upto a subsequence,
\nabla u_{n}\to\nabla u
pointwise almost everywhere in
\Omega
.

Proof.

By the given hypothesis, for
0<\delta\leq 1
, by Lemma
3.4
and
3.6
,

(7.1)

\text{the sequence }\{u_{n}\}\text{ is uniformly bounded in }W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

If
\delta>1
, by Lemma
3.7
, the sequences

(7.2)

\begin{split}\{u_{n}\}\text{ and }\left\{u_{n}^{\frac{\delta+p-1}{p}}\right\}\text{ are uniformly bounded in }W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\text{ and }W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\text{ respectively.}\end{split}

Then, we have

(7.3)

\begin{split}u_{n}&\rightharpoonup u\text{ weakly in }W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\text{ and }\end{split}

(7.4)

\begin{split}u_{n}&\to u\text{ strongly in }L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega).\end{split}

By Remark
3.3
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

(7.5)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\geq u_{n}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Also, we note that the function

(7.6)

|t|^{p-2}t\text{ is monotone increasing over }\mathbb{R}.

Next, we prove the result in the following two steps.

Step 1.
Let
K\subset\Omega
be a compact set and consider a function
\phi_{K}\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\mathrm{supp}\,\phi_{K}=\omega
,
0\leq\phi_{K}\leq 1
in
\Omega
and
\phi_{K}\equiv 1
in
K
. For
\mu>0
, we define the truncated function
T_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(7.7)

T_{\mu}(s)=\begin{cases}s,\text{ if }|s|\leq\mu,\\
\mu\frac{s}{|s|},\text{ if }|s|>\mu.\end{cases}

Choosing
v_{n}=\phi_{K}T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)\big)\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
as a test function in
(\mathcal{A})
, we obtain

(7.8)

\begin{split}I+J=R,\end{split}

where

I=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla v_{n}\,dx,

J=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)\big(v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)\big)\,d\mu\text{ and }R=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}v_{n}\,dx.

Estimate of
I
:

We have

(7.9)

\begin{split}I&=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla v_{n}\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{K}\big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\big)\nabla T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)\big)\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}\phi_{K}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)\big)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)\big)|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi_{K}\,dx\\
&=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{split}

Estimate of
I_{2}
:
Using (
7.3
) we get
T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)\big)\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. As a consequence,

(7.10)

\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{K}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)\big)\,dx=0.

Estimate of
I_{3}
:
For
\omega=\mathrm{supp}\,\phi_{K}
, by Hölder’s inequality, using the uniformly boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}
in
W^{1,p}(\omega)
, we have

(7.11)

|I_{3}|=\left|\int_{\Omega}T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)\big)|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi_{K}\,dx\right|\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{K}\|u_{n}-u\|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-1}\,dx\\
\leq\|\nabla\phi_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\left(\int_{\omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\leq C\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{p}(\omega)},

for some constant
C>0
, independent of
n
. Thus using (
7.4
) in (
7.11
), we obtain

(7.12)

\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{3}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)\big)|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi_{K}\,dx=0.

Therefore, using the estimates (
7.10
) and (
7.12
) in (
7.9
) we obtain

(7.13)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}I=\limsup_{n\to\infty}I_{1}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{K}\big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\big)\nabla T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)\big)\,dx.

Estimate of
J
:
We have

(7.14)

\begin{split}J&=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)\big(v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)\big)\,d\mu\\
&=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{K}(x)\Big(\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)-\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)\Big)\Big(T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(x)\big)-T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)\Big)\,d\mu\\
&\quad+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)\big(\phi_{K}(x)-\phi_{K}(y)\big)\,d\mu\\
&\quad\quad+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)\big(\phi_{K}(y)-\phi_{K}(x)\big)\,d\mu\\
&\quad\quad\quad+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)\Big(\phi_{K}(x)T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(x)\big)-\phi_{K}(y)T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)\Big)\,d\mu\\
&=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}.\end{split}

Estimate of
J_{1}
:
We claim that

J_{1}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{K}(x)\Big(\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)-\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)\Big)\Big(T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(x)\big)-T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)\Big)\,d\mu\geq 0.

To this end, it is enough to prove that, for almost every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{1}

\displaystyle=\phi_{K}(x)\Big(\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)-\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y)\big)\Big)\Big(T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(x)\big)-T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)\Big)\geq 0.

We observe that

\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}=\cup_{i=1}^{4}S_{i},

where

S_{1}=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:|(u_{n}-u)(x)|\leq\mu,\,|(u_{n}-u)(y)|\leq\mu\Big\},

S_{2}=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:|(u_{n}-u)(x)|\leq\mu<|(u_{n}-u)(y)|\Big\},

S_{3}=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:|(u_{n}-u)(y)|\leq\mu<|(u_{n}-u)(x)|\Big\}

and

S_{4}=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:|(u_{n}-u)(x)|>\mu,\,|(u_{n}-u)(y)|>\mu\Big\}.

Case
1
.

Let
x,y\in S_{1}
. Then,
|(u_{n}-u)(x)|\leq\mu
and
|(u_{n}-u)(y)|\leq\mu
, which gives

T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(x)\big)=(u_{n}-u)(x)\text{ and }T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)=(u_{n}-u)(y).

Therefore, we have

\hat{J}_{1}=\phi_{K}(x)\big(\mathcal{A}(u_{n}(x,y))-\mathcal{A}(u(x,y))\big)((u_{n}-u)(x)-(u_{n}-u)(y)),

which is nonnegative, by Lemma
2.6
.

Case
2
.

Let
x,y\in S_{2}
. Then,
|(u_{n}-u)(x)|\leq\mu<|(u_{n}-u)(y)|
, which gives from (
7.5
) that
u(x)-u_{n}(x)\leq\mu<u(y)-u_{n}(y)
. Therefore, we have

(7.15)

T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(x)\big)-T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)=(u_{n}-u)(x)+\mu\geq 0.

Moreover, in this case,
u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)>u(x)-u(y)
.
Thus, by (
7.6
), we obtain

(7.16)

\mathcal{A}(u_{n}(x,y))-\mathcal{A}(u(x,y))\geq 0.

From (
7.15
) and (
7.16
), it follows that
\hat{J}_{1}\geq 0
.

Case
3
.

Let
x,y\in S_{3}
. Then,
|(u_{n}-u)(y)|\leq\mu<|(u_{n}-u)(x)|
, which gives from (
7.5
) that
u(y)-u_{n}(y)\leq\mu<u(x)-u_{n}(x)
. Therefore, we have

(7.17)

T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(x)\big)-T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)=-\mu-(u_{n}-u)(y)\leq 0.

Moreover, in this case, we get
u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)<u(x)-u(y)
. Thus, by (
7.6
), we deduce

(7.18)

\mathcal{A}(u_{n}(x,y))-\mathcal{A}(u(x,y))\leq 0.

From (
7.17
) and (
7.18
), it follows that
\hat{J}_{1}\geq 0
.

Case
4
.

Let
x,y\in S_{4}
. Then,
|(u_{n}-u)(x)|>\mu
and
|(u_{n}-u)(y)|>\mu
, which gives

(7.19)

T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(x)\big)-T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)=-\mu+\mu=0.

Hence,
\hat{J}_{1}=0
.

Therefore, we have

(7.20)

J_{1}\geq 0.

Hence, the claim follows.

Estimate of
J_{2}
:

We have

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)\big(\phi_{K}(x)-\phi_{K}(y)\big)\,d\mu.

First, we claim that for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2N}
, such that

(7.21)

\begin{split}J_{2}^{1}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus\mathcal{K}}T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)\big(\phi_{K}(x)-\phi_{K}(y)\big)\,d\mu\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2},\end{split}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. To this end, let us set

\omega=\mathrm{supp}\,\phi_{K},\quad\quad\omega^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus(\omega^{c}\times\omega^{c}),

and as
\phi_{K}\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a compact set
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(\epsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2N}
such that

(7.22)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus\mathcal{L}}\frac{|\phi_{K}(x)-\phi_{K}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Then we choose
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{L}
and applying Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

(7.23)

J_{2}^{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus\mathcal{K}}T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)\mathcal{A}\big(u_{n}(x,y)\big)\big(\phi_{K}(x)-\phi_{K}(y)\big)\,d\mu\\
\leq\left(\int_{\omega^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{K}}\frac{|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\omega^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{K}}\frac{|T_{\mu}\big((u_{n}-u)(y)\big)|^{p}|\phi_{K}(x)-\phi_{K}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\leq\mu\left(\int_{\omega^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{K}}\frac{|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\omega^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{K}}\frac{|\phi_{K}(x)-\phi_{K}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

If
0<\delta\leq 1
, then using the estimate (
7.22
) and from (
7.1
) the uniform boundedness of

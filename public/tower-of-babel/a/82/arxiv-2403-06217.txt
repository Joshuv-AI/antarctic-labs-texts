Non-existence of Shimura curves of Mumford type generically in the non-hyperelliptic locus Thanks: This work is supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China, Fundamental Research Funds for central Universities, and Science and Technology Commission of Shanghai Municipality (No. 22DZ2229014)
Xin Lu, Shengli Tan, Kang Zuo

Abstract
. We show that there does not exist any Shimura curve with strictly maximal Higgs field generically in the Torelli locus of non-hyperelliptic curves of genus g ≥ 4 g\geq 4 . In particular, Shimura curves of Mumford type are not generically in the Torelli locus of non-hyperelliptic curves of genus g ≥ 4 g\geq 4 .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14J10,14E30

1.
Introduction

Shimura varieties are classically constructed as quotients of bounded symmetric
domains by arithmetic groups.
A large class of Shimura varieties, called ‘of Hodge type’, parametrizes families of abelian
varieties with certain properties.
A characterization of Shimura varieties to be of Hodge type can be found in
[
Mum69
]
.
In the same paper, Mumford also constructs an interesting
and surprising example of Shimura curves
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{4}
of Hodge type which are
not of PEL type (i.e. not characterized by the existence of algebraic endomorphisms
on the abelian varieties),
where
\mathcal{A}_{4}
is the moduli space of principally polarized abelian 4-folds.
Such Shimura curves
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{4}
will be referred as Shimura curves of Mumford type in
\mathcal{A}_{4}
.
A general fiber over such a Shimura curve
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{4}
is an abelian
4
-fold admitting an endomorphism ring equal to
\mathbb{Z}
.
The properties of such Shimura curves of Mumford type have been extensively studied by many mathematicians, cf.
[
Noo00
,
Noo01
,
VZ04
]
.
In this paper, we are mainly interested in the intersection of the Shimura curves of Mumford type with the Torelli locus of algebraic curves.

Fixing
n\geq 3
an integer, we have
\mathcal{M}_{g,[n]}
the moduli space of smooth projective curves over
complex number
\mathbb{C}
of genus
g\geq 2
with a full level
n
-structure,
and
\mathcal{A}_{g,[n]}
the moduli space of
g
-dimensional
principally polarized abelian varieties over
\mathbb{C}
with a full level-
n
structure.
No specific choice of the level
n(\geq 3)
is made because it is only imposed to assure the representability,
which plays no essential role in our study.
For simplicity, the subscript ‘
[n]
’ is omitted without causing much confusion.
The Torelli morphism

j^{\circ}:~\mathcal{M}_{g}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g}

sends a curve to
its Jacobian with its canonical principal polarization and level structure.
The image of
j^{\circ}
, denoted as
\mathcal{T}_{g}^{\circ}
, is a locally closed subvariety in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
and called
the open Torelli locus.
Its closure will be denoted as
\mathcal{T}_{g}
, and called the Torelli locus.
Denote by
\mathcal{TH}_{g}^{\circ}=j^{\circ}(\mathcal{H}_{g})
the image of the hyperelliptic locus,
and
\mathcal{TH}_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
its closure which we call the Torelli locus of hyperelliptic curves,
where
\mathcal{H}_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{g}
is the locus corresponding to the hyperelliptic curves.

A closed subvariety
Z\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
(of positive dimension) is said to be
contained generically

in the Torelli locus
(resp. the Torelli locus of hyperelliptic curves), written as
Z\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}
(resp.
Z\Subset\mathcal{TH}_{g}
),
if
Z\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{g}
and
Z\cap\mathcal{T}_{g}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset

(resp.
Z\subseteq\mathcal{TH}_{g}
and
Z\cap\mathcal{T}_{g}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
).
Meanwhile, we will say a subvariety
Z\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
is contained generically in the Torelli locus of non-hyperelliptic curves, if
Z\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}
but

Z\not\Subset\mathcal{TH}_{g}
.
It is easy to see that
Z\cap j^{\circ}(\mathcal{NH}_{g})
is Zariski dense in
Z
for any closed subvariety
Z
contained generically in the Torelli locus of non-hyperelliptic curves, where
\mathcal{NH}_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{g}
is the locus corresponding to the non-hyperelliptic curves.

Note that the Shimura curve
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{4}
of Mumford type constructed in
[
Mum69
]
is complete.
Recall also that the Torelli locus
\mathcal{T}_{4}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{4}
is a subvariety of codimension one,
which is in fact ample as a divisor by
[
Igu81
]
.
It follows immediately that
C_{0}\cap\mathcal{T}_{4}\neq\emptyset
for any Shimura curve
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{4}
of Mumford type.
Oort
[
Oor19
, § 7]
(see also
[
MO13
, Question 6.5]
) has asked the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Is there any Shimura curve of Mumford type contained generically in
\mathcal{T}_{4}
?

Recently, Baldi-Klingler-Ullmo
[
BKU24
, Theorem 3.17]
made a progress on the above question
by showing that there exists a Shimura curve
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{4}
of Mumford type such that
C_{0}\cap\mathcal{T}_{4}^{\circ}
is a non-CM point.
Here we recall that
p\in\mathcal{A}_{g}
is a CM point if the corresponding abelian variety
A_{p}
admits a ’large’ endomorphism ring, i.e.,
\mathrm{End}(A_{p})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
contains a commutative semi-simple
algebra of
\text{{\rm rank\,}}=2g
.
Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

There does not exist any Shimura curve of Mumford type contained generically in the Torelli locus of non-hyperelliptic curves of genus
g=4
.

The proof relies on the characterization of Shimura curves by Viehweg-Zuo
[
VZ04
]
.
Recall that the special subvarieties in
\mathcal{A}_{g}

are just totally geodesic subvarieties containing CM points, cf.
[
Moo98
]
.
They are of particular interest because on one hand they are locally symmetric in the sense of differential geometry,
and on the other hand they parameterize abelian varieties with prescribed Hodge classes.
Special subvarieties of dimension zero are exactly CM points,
and special curves will be also called as Shimura curves in this paper.
The Coleman-Oort conjecture
[
Col87
,
Oor97
]
predicts the non-existence of special subvarieties of positive dimension contained generically in the Torelli locus.

Conjecture 1.3

(Coleman-Oort conjecture)
.

For any sufficiently large
g
, there does not exist any positive-dimensional special subvariety
Z\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}
;
or equivalently, there exist at most finitely many CM points contained in
\mathcal{T}_{g}^{\circ}
if
g
is sufficiently large.

We refer to
[
MO13
]
and the references therein for an introduction to the Coleman-Oort conjecture.
We remark also that for small genera (
g\leq 7
), there indeed exist positive-dimensional special subvarieties
Z\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}
.

In
[
VZ04
]
, it is proved that an algebraic curve
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
is Shimura if and only if
C_{0}
contains at least one CM point and the Higgs bundle associated to
C_{0}
admits maximal Higgs field;
see also
Section 2.2
for more details.
Based on such a characterization,
we proved the non-exsistence of Shimura curves generically in the Torelli locus of hyperelliptic curves if
g\geq 8
in
[
LZ17
]
.
Furthermore, we
[
CLZ21
]
showed that there are at most finitely many CM points contained in
j^{\circ}(\mathcal{S}_{g})
if
g\geq 8
, where
\mathcal{S}_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{g}
is the locus corresponding to the superelliptic curves;
in particular,
\mathcal{TH}_{g}^{\circ}
contains at most finitely many CM points if
g\geq 8
.

Our main interest here is the Shimura curves of Mumford type in
\mathcal{A}_{4}
, as well as their generalizations in
[
VZ04
]
,
which will be also referred as Shimura curves of Mumford type (cf.
[
LZ19
, § 2.2]
).
The Higgs bundle associated to a Shimura curve
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
of Mumford type admits strictly maximal Higgs field
[
VZ04
]
.
Thus
Theorem 1.2
follows immediately from the next theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

There does not exist any Shimura curve with strictly maximal Higgs field generically in the Torelli locus of non-hyperelliptic curves of genus
g\geq 4
.

When
C_{0}\Subset\mathcal{TH}_{g}
is a Shimura curve with strictly maximal Higgs field, then the family of semi-stable curves representing
C_{0}
admits also strictly strictly maximal Higgs field, cf.
Section 2.3
.
Combining the above theorem together with
[
LZ19
, Theorem D]
, we obtain

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
be a Shimura curve with strictly maximal Higgs field.
If
g>4
, then
C_{0}\not\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In
Section 2
, we recall some known facts about the Shimura curves of Mumford type, Higgs bundles and semi-stable families representing Shimura curves generically in the TOrelli locus.
In
Section 3
we prove a slope inequality related to the family of semi-stable curves representing a Shimura curve of Mumford type,
and the main result will be proved in
Section 4
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The Shimura curves of Mumford type

In this subsection, we briefly recall the construction of the Shimura curves of Mumford type in
\mathcal{A}_{4}
by Mumford
[
Mum69
]
,
and refer to
[
VZ04
]
(see also
[
LZ19
, § 2.2]
) for the generalizations in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
with
g
being large.

Let
F
be a totally real cubic field over
\mathbb{Q}
with
3
distinct real embeddings

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}
.
Let
R
be a quaternion algebra over
F
such that

\rho_{1}:R\otimes_{\sigma_{1}}\mathbb{R}\simeq\mathrm{Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad\rho_{i}:R\otimes_{\sigma_{i}}\mathbb{R}\simeq\mathbb{H},\ (i=2\text{~or~}3),

where
\mathbb{H}
is Hamilton’s quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{R}
.
The corestriction
D:=\mathrm{Cor}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}R
is a central simple algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Suppose
R
is chosen such that the corestriction
D
splits, i.e.,

D\cong\mathrm{Mat}_{8}(\mathbb{Q}).

Denote by
K
the kernel of the reduced norm

{\rm Nm}:~R^{\times}\longrightarrow F^{\times}.

The
\mathbb{Q}
-group
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
associated to
K
is connected and semi-simple
with

\mathbf{G}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{R}).

The homomorphism

R^{\times}\longrightarrow D\cong\mathrm{Mat}_{8}(\mathbb{Q})

defines a representation of
K
which preserves a symplectic form on
V=\mathbb{Q}^{8}
.
Enlarge
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
to a connected reductive
\mathbb{Q}
-group
\mathbf{G}
which only differs from
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
by the split center
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},\mathbb{Q}}
. It is the
\mathbb{Q}
-group associated to

R^{*}:=\{a\in R:{\rm Nm}(a)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\subset F^{\times}\}.

We thus obtain the Shimura datum
(\mathbf{G},X)
,
where
X
is the
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})
-conjugacy class of the following homomorphism

h:\mathbb{S}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
given by

z=\rho\exp(\sqrt{-1}\theta)\mapsto\bigg(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\rho\cos\theta&\rho\sin\theta\\
-\rho\sin\theta&\rho\cos\theta\end{array}\right],\,I_{2},I_{2}\bigg).

It is clear that
X
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\pm}
, and
(\mathbf{G},X)
is a subdatum of

(\mathrm{GSp}_{8},\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{4})
by the representation of
\mathbf{G}
induced by
R^{\times}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mat}_{8}(\mathbb{Q})
,
where
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{g}
is the Siegel double half spaces of complex symmetric
g\times g
matrices with definite imaginary part.
It follows that the Shimura datum
(\mathbf{G},X)
defines a Shimura curve
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{4}
.

By construction, the representation of
G^{\prime}
on the space
V=\mathbb{Q}^{8}
is absolutely irreducible.
Hence
A_{p}
admits no non-trivial endomorphism for any general point
p\in C_{0}
,
where
A_{p}
is the abelian
4
-fold corresponding to
p\in\mathcal{A}_{4}
.

2.2.
The Higgs bundle associated to a Shimura curve

Let
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
be any closed curve.
We assume that
C_{0}
is smooth by normalization if necessary.
Denote by
\mathfrak{h}_{0}:\,\mathfrak{X}_{0}\to C_{0}
the universe family of
g
-dimensional abelian varieties over
C_{0}
,
which is assumed to be extended to a semi-stable family

\mathfrak{h}:\,\mathfrak{X}\to C
over the compactification
C
by arising the level structure if necessary.
The local system
\mathbb{V}_{C_{0}}:=R^{1}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{X}_{0}}
admits
unipotent local monodromy around the boundary
\Delta_{C}:=\,C\setminus C_{0}
.
By taking the grading of the extended Hodge filtration, one obtains a logarithmic system of Hodge bundles

\left(E_{C},\,\theta_{C}\right)=\big(E^{1,0}_{C}\oplus E^{0,1}_{C},\,\theta_{C}\big)=\Big(\mathfrak{h}_{*}\big(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}/C}(\log\Upsilon_{C})\big)\oplus R^{1}\mathfrak{h}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},\,\theta_{C}\Big),

where
\Upsilon_{C}=\mathfrak{h}^{-1}(\Delta_{C})
, and the Higgs field

\theta_{C}:~E^{1,0}_{C}\longrightarrow E^{0,1}_{C}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{C}(\log\Delta_{C})

(2.1)

is just the extension of the following Kodaira-Spencer map on the Hodge bundles

\theta_{0}:~(\mathfrak{h}_{0})_{*}\big(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}/C_{0}}\big)\longrightarrow R^{1}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{C_{0}}.

We will call
\left(E_{C},\,\theta_{C}\right)
the logarithmic Higgs bundle associated to
C_{0}
(or
C
).
The logarithmic Higgs bundle admits the following Fujita decomposition (cf.
[
Fuj78
]
):

\left(E_{C}^{1,0}\oplus E_{C}^{0,1},~\theta_{C}\right)=\left(A_{C}^{1,0}\oplus A_{C}^{0,1},~\theta_{C}\big|_{A_{C}^{1,0}}\right)\oplus\left(F_{C}^{1,0}\oplus F_{C}^{0,1},~0\right),

(2.2)

where
A_{C}^{1,0}
is an ample vector bundle, while
F_{C}^{1,0}

and
F_{C}^{0,1}
are flat vector bundles associated to unitary
local subsystems
\mathbb{F}_{C}^{1,0}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{C}^{0,1}\subseteq\mathbb{V}_{C_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{C}.

Definition 2.1

(
[
VZ03
]
)
.

The Higgs bundle
(E_{C}^{1,0}\oplus E_{C}^{0,1},~\theta_{C})

is said to admit
maximal
Higgs field if the map

\theta_{C}\big|_{A_{C}^{1,0}}:\,A_{C}^{1,0}\longrightarrow A_{C}^{0,1}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{C}(\log\Delta_{C})

is an isomorphism;
and to admit
strictly maximal
Higgs field if furthermore
F_{C}^{1,0}\oplus F_{C}^{0,1}=0
, namely the map
\theta_{C}
in (
2.1
) is an isomorphism.

By
[
VZ03
]
the Higgs field
\theta_{C}
is strictly maximal (resp. maximal) if and only if
the following
Arakelov equality
holds

\displaystyle\deg E^{1,0}_{C}

\displaystyle={g\over 2}\cdot\deg\Omega^{1}_{C}(\log\Delta_{C}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Big(\text{resp.}\quad\deg E^{1,0}_{C}

\displaystyle={\text{{\rm rank\,}}A_{C}^{1,0}\over 2}\cdot\deg\Omega^{1}_{C}(\log\Delta_{C})~\Big).

Recall also that if the Higgs bundle
\theta_{C}
is maximal,
then the ample part
A_{C}^{1,0}
must be semi-stable.
In particular, if
\theta_{C}
is strictly maximal, then
E^{1,0}_{C}
is semi-stable.

The following theorem gives numerical characterizations of Shimura curves,
and more generally of totally geodesic curves.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
Möl11
,
VZ04
]
)
.

Let
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
be a closed curve,
and
(E_{C}^{1,0}\oplus E_{C}^{0,1},~\theta_{C})
the associated logarithmic Higgs bundle as above. Then

(1)

The Higgs field
\theta_{C}
is maximal if and only if
C_{0}
is a totally geodesic curve.

(2)

The Higgs field
\theta_{C}
is maximal if and only if either
C_{0}
is a Shimura curve of Mumford type or
C_{0}
parametrizes abelian varieties isogeneous to the self-product of a modular family of elliptic curves.

In particular, if
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
is a Shimura curve of Mumford type
with
(E_{C}^{1,0}\oplus E_{C}^{0,1},~\theta_{C})
the associated logarithmic Higgs bundle as above,
then the Higgs field
\theta_{C}
is strictly maximal.
Note also that Shimura curves of Mumford type is itself compact, from which it follows that
C=C_{0}
is this case.
Therefore, the Arakelov equality (
2.3
) on a Shimura curve
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
of Mumford type becomes

\deg E^{1,0}_{C}={g\over 2}\cdot\deg\Omega^{1}_{C}.

(2.4)

2.3.
The family of semi-stable curves representing a Shimura curve generically in the Torelli locus

Given a smooth closed curve
C_{0}\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}
with a suitable smooth compactification
C

by joining a finite set of cusps
\Delta_{C}=C\setminus C_{0}
,
we have constructed a family
f:\,S\to B
of semi-stable curves representing
C_{0}
in
[
LZ19
, § 3]
.
For convenience, we briefly recall here the construction.

Fix an integer
n
,
let
\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}=\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g,[n]}\supseteq\mathcal{M}_{g}=\mathcal{M}_{g,[n]}
be the partial compactification of
the moduli space of smooth projective genus-
g
curves with level-
n
structure by adding
stable curves with compact Jacobians.
When
n\geq 3
, it carries a universal family of stable curves with compact Jacobians

\mathfrak{f}:~\mathcal{S}_{g}^{ct}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}^{ct}_{g}.

(2.5)

The Torelli morphism
j^{\rm o}
can be naturally extended to
\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}
:

j:~\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g},\qquad\text{with~}\mathcal{T}_{g}=j\big(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}\big).

The morphism
j^{\rm o}
is 2:1 and ramified exactly over the locus of hyperelliptic curves.
However, the relative dimension of
j
is positive along the boundary
\mathcal{T}_{g}\setminus\mathcal{T}^{\rm o}_{g}
.

Given a smooth closed curve
C_{0}\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}
,
let
B_{0}
be the normalization of the strict inverse image
j^{-1}(C)
of
C
,
and denote by
\pi_{0}:\,B_{0}\to C_{0}
the induced morphism.
If
B_{0}
is reducible, then we replace
B_{0}
by any one of the two irreducible components.
By pulling back the universal family over
\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}

to
B_{0}
and resolving the singularities,
one gets a family
f_{0}:S_{0}\to B_{0}
of semi-stable curves that extends uniquely to a family

f:S\to B
of semi-stable curves
over the smooth completion
B\supseteq B_{0}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The family
f:\,S\to B
is called the family of semi-stable curves representing
C_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{g}

via the Torelli morphism.

By construction, any singular fiber over the boundary
\Delta_{nc}:=\,B\setminus B_{0}
admits non-compact Jacobian.
Moreover, the local system
\mathbb{V}_{B_{0}}:=R^{1}(f_{0})_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{S_{0}}=\pi_{0}^{*}\mathbb{V}_{C_{0}}
.
It is well-known that the logarithmic Higgs bundle associated to
\mathbb{V}_{B_{0}}
has the form

\left(E_{B}^{1,0}\oplus E_{B}^{0,1},~\theta_{B}\right)=\left(f_{*}\omega_{S/B}\oplus R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S},~\theta_{B}\right),

(2.6)

and it admits a decomposition of Higgs bundles similar to (
2.2
):

\left(E_{B}^{1,0}\oplus E_{B}^{0,1},\theta_{B}\right)=\left(A_{B}^{1,0}\oplus A_{B}^{0,1},~\theta_{B}\big|_{A_{B}^{1,0}}\right)\oplus\left(F_{B}^{1,0}\oplus F_{B}^{0,1},~0\right).

(2.7)

Let
\pi:\,B\to C
be the induced map.
Then
\Delta_{nc}=\pi^{-1}(\Delta_{C})
, and

\pi^{*}\left(E^{1,0}_{C}\oplus E^{0,1}_{C},~\theta_{C}\right)=\left(E^{1,0}_{B}\oplus E^{0,1}_{B},~\theta_{B}\right),

which is compatible with the Fujita decompositions (
2.2
) on
C
and (
2.7
) on
B
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
f:\,S\to B
be any family of semi-stable curves of genus
g\geq 2

and
\Upsilon_{nc}\to\Delta_{nc}
the singular fibres with non-compact Jacobians.
Then
f
is said to admit
maximal
Higgs field if

\theta_{B}\big|_{A_{B}^{1,0}}:\,A_{B}^{1,0}\longrightarrow A_{B}^{0,1}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{B}(\log\Delta_{nc})

is an isomorphism; and to be with
strictly maximal
Higgs field if furthermore
F_{B}^{1,0}\oplus F_{B}^{0,1}=0
.
Similar to (
2.3
),
f
has strictly maximal (resp. maximal) Higgs field if and only if

\displaystyle\deg f_{*}\omega_{S/B}

\displaystyle={g\over 2}\cdot\deg\Omega^{1}_{B}(\log\Delta_{nc}),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Big(\text{resp.}\quad\deg f_{*}\omega_{S/B}

\displaystyle={\text{{\rm rank\,}}A^{1,0}_{B}\over 2}\cdot\deg\Omega^{1}_{B}(\log\Delta_{nc})~\Big).

Proposition 2.5

(
[
LZ19
, Proposition 3.4]
)
.

Let
f:\,S\to B
be the family of semi-stable curves representing a closed curve
C_{0}\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}

as above. Then

(i).
if
C\Subset\mathcal{TH}_{g}
, then

\deg f_{*}\omega_{S/B}=\deg E^{1,0}_{C},\quad\deg\Omega_{B}^{1}(\log\Delta_{nc})=\deg\Omega_{C}^{1}(\log\Delta_{C});

(2.9)

(ii).
if
C\not\Subset\mathcal{TH}_{g}
, then either
\pi:\,B\to C
is an isomorphism, in which case (
2.9
) holds,
or
\pi:\,B\to C
is double cover, in which case we have

\deg f_{*}\omega_{S/B}=2\deg E^{1,0}_{C},\quad\deg\Omega_{B}^{1}(\log\Delta_{nc})=2\deg\Omega_{C}^{1}(\log\Delta_{C})+|\Lambda|,

where
\Lambda
is the ramification locus of the morphism
\pi_{0}:\,B_{0}\to C_{0}
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
f:\,S\to B
be the family of semi-stable curves representing a Shimura curve
C_{0}\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}
with strictly maximal Higgs field.
Suppose that
C_{0}\not\Subset\mathcal{TH}_{g}
.
Then either
\pi:\,B\to C
is an isomorphism, in which case
f
has strictly maximal Higgs field, i.e.,

\deg f_{*}\omega_{S/B}=\frac{g}{2}\deg\Omega_{B}^{1}(\log\Delta_{nc});

(2.10)

or
\pi:\,B\to C
is a
double cover, in which case
f_{*}\omega_{S/B}
is semi-stable with

\deg f_{*}\omega_{S/B}=\frac{g}{2}\deg\Omega_{B}^{1}(\log\Delta_{nc})-{g\over 2}\cdot|\Lambda|,

(2.11)

where
\Lambda
is the ramification locus of the morphism
\pi_{0}:\,B_{0}\to C_{0}
.

3.
Slope inequality

In this section, we would like to consider the slope inequality for a
family
f:\,S\to B
of semi-stable curves representing a closed curve
C_{0}\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}
as in
Section 2.3
.
We will suppose in this section that
C_{0}
is not contained generically in the Torelli locus
\mathcal{TH}_{g}
of hyperelliptic curves,
and that the induced map
\pi_{0}:\,B_{0}\to C_{0}
is a double cover with ramification locus
\Lambda\subseteq B_{0}
.

Let
\tau_{0}:\,B_{0}\to B_{0}
be the involution on
B_{0}
induced by the double cover

f:\,S|_{U_{p}}\to U_{p}
,

(3.11)

where
p=\psi(\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(q))\in B
, and
U_{p}\subseteq B
and
U_{q}\subseteq\widetilde{C}
are local neighborhoods of
p
and
q
respectively (Here we remark that
\psi(\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(q))
consists of only one point). It follows that

3l_{h}(\widetilde{F}_{q})+2l_{1}(\widetilde{F}_{q})-3=3l_{h}(F_{p})+2l_{1}(F_{p})-3,\quad\forall\,q\in\tilde{\pi}(\psi^{-1}(\Delta_{ct}\setminus\Lambda_{1}))\text{~and~}p=\psi(\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(q)).

Therefore, similar to the proof of
[
LZ19
, Theorem 5.2]
,
it suffices to prove that

\mathrm{length\,}\mathcal{S}_{q}\geq\left\{\begin{aligned} &3l_{h}(\widetilde{F}_{q})+2l_{1}(\widetilde{F}_{q})-3,&&\forall\,q\in\tilde{\pi}(\psi^{-1}(\Delta_{ct}\setminus\Lambda_{1}));\\
&g-2,&&\forall\,q\in\tilde{\pi}(\psi^{-1}(\Lambda)).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.12)

Applying (
3.11
), we see that the first inequality in (
3.12
) is in fact already proved in
[
LZ19
, Lemmas 5.3]
.
It remains to prove the last inequality of (
3.12
).
But this is clear, since the fiber
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{q}
over
q\in\tilde{\pi}(\psi^{-1}(\Lambda))
is hyperelliptic, and hence

\mathrm{length\,}\mathcal{S}_{q}\geq\dim\mathrm{coker}(\nu_{q})\geq g-2,

where

\nu_{q}:\,S^{2}H^{0}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{q},\omega_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{q}})\longrightarrow H^{0}\big(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{q},\omega_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{q}}^{\otimes 2}\big),

is the natural multiplication map.
This proves (
3.12
).
∎

We now come back to the proof of
Theorem 3.1
.
Since
\psi
is unramified,

\omega_{\widetilde{S}/\widetilde{B}}^{2}=d\,\omega_{S/B}^{2},\qquad\deg\tilde{f}_{*}\omega_{\widetilde{S}/\widetilde{B}}=d\deg f_{*}\omega_{S/B},\qquad\text{where $d=\deg\psi$}.

Combining this with (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), one proves (
3.1
).
∎

4.
The proof of
Theorem 1.4

In this section, we prove out main result.

Proof of
Theorem 1.4
.

We prove by contradiction.
Suppose that there exists a Shimura curve
C_{0}\Subset\mathcal{T}_{g}
with strictly maximal Higgs field generically in the Torelli locus of non-hyperelliptic curves of genus
g\geq 4
.

Let
B_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{g}^{ct}
be the inverse image curve,
and let
f:\,S\to B
be the family of semi-stable curves representing
C_{0}
as in
Section 2.3
.
Since
C_{0}
is contained generically in the Torelli locus of non-hyperelliptic curves,
the family
f
is non-hyperelliptic.
If
B_{0}
is reducible, then the induced cover
\pi:\,B\to C
is an isomorphism and the family
f
has strictly maximal Higgs field by
Section 2.3
.
This contradicts
[
LZ19
, Theorem 7.3]
.
Thus we may assume that
C_{0}
is irreducible,
in which case,
\pi:\,B\to C
is a double cover and
f_{*}\omega_{S/B}
is semi-stable satisfying (
2.11
) by
Section 2.3
.
Thus by
Theorem 3.1
, one has

\displaystyle\omega_{S/B}^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{5g-6}{g}\deg f_{*}\omega_{S/B}+3(g-2)|\Lambda|

(4.1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{1}}4\big(l_{h}(F_{p})+l_{1}(F_{p})-1\big)+\sum_{p\in\Delta_{ct}\setminus\Lambda_{1}}\big(3l_{h}(F_{p})+2l_{1}(F_{p})-3\big),

Here we recall that
\Lambda\subseteq B_{0}
is the ramification divisor of the double cover
\pi_{0}:\,B_{0}\to C_{0}
,

\Delta_{ct}\subseteq B_{0}
is the subset over which the fiber of
f_{0}:\,S_{0}\to B_{0}
is singular,

\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda\cap\Delta_{ct}
.
On the other hand, according to
[
LZ19
, Theorem 4.3]
, one has

\displaystyle\omega_{S/B}^{2}~\leq

\displaystyle(2g-2)\cdot\deg\left(\Omega^{1}_{B}(\log\Delta_{nc})\right)+\sum_{p\in\Delta_{ct}\cap\Lambda^{\prime}}\frac{3}{2}\cdot\big(l_{h}(F_{p})+l_{1}(F_{p})-1\big)

(4.2)

\displaystyle+\left(2g-2-\frac{g-1}{6}\right)\cdot|\Delta_{ct,b}|+\sum_{p\in\Delta_{ct,ub}}\big(3l_{h}(F_{p})+2l_{1}(F_{p})-3\big),

where

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}\,=

\displaystyle\left\{p\in B~\Bigg|\begin{aligned} &F_{p}\text{~is a hyperelliptic curve with compact Jacobian, and any}\\
&\text{two irreducible irrational components of $F_{p}$ do not intersect}\end{aligned}~\right\};

\displaystyle\Delta_{ct,b}\,=

\displaystyle\left\{p\in\Delta_{ct}\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}~\big|~\text{each irreducible component of $F_{p}$ is of genus $0$ or $1$}\right\};

\displaystyle\Delta_{ct,ub}\,=

\displaystyle\Delta_{ct}\setminus\left\{\Lambda^{\prime}\cup\Delta_{ct,b}\right\}.

According to the proof of
[
LZ19
, Theorem 4.2]
in
[
LZ19
, § 4.3]
,
it follows that
\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}
,
and hence
\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{ct}\cap\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Combining this with (
2.11
) and (
4.2
), one obtains that

\displaystyle\omega_{S/B}^{2}~\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4g-4}{g}\deg f_{*}\omega_{S/B}+(2g-2)|\Lambda|+\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{1}}\frac{3}{2}\cdot\big(l_{h}(F_{p})+l_{1}(F_{p})-1\big)

\displaystyle+\left(2g-2-\frac{g-1}{6}\right)\cdot|\Delta_{ct,b}|+\sum_{p\in\Delta_{ct}\setminus(\Lambda_{1}\cup\Delta_{ct,b})}\big(3l_{h}(F_{p})+2l_{1}(F_{p})-3\big),

Together with (

), we get

\displaystyle\frac{g-2}{g}\deg f_{*}\omega_{S/B}\leq

\displaystyle(4-g)|\Lambda|-\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{1}}\frac{5}{2}\cdot\big(l_{h}(F_{p})+l_{1}(F_{p})-1\big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{p\in\Delta_{ct,b}}\left(2g-2-\frac{g-1}{6}-\big(3l_{h}(F_{p})+2l_{1}(F_{p})-3\big)\right)

\displaystyle\frac{7-g}{6}\,\big|\Delta_{ct,b}\big|.

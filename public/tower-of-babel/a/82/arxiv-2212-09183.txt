Schrödinger equation for two quasi-exactly solvable
potentials

Abstract
We apply solutions of Heun’s general equation to the stationary Schrödinger equation with two quasi-exactly solvable elliptic potentials which depend on a real parameter 𝒍 \bm{l} . We get finite-series solutions from power series expansions for Heun’s equation if 𝒍 \bm{l} is an integer, except if 𝒍 = − 1 , − 2 , − 3 , − 4 \bm{l}=-1,-2,-3,-4 . If 𝒍 ≠ − 5 / 2 \bm{l}\neq-5/2 is half an odd integer, we obtain finite series in terms of hypergeometric functions. The quasi-exact solvability is expressed by the finite series solutions. However, for any value of 𝒍 \bm{l} , we find infinite-series eigenfunctions which are convergent and bounded for all values of the independent variable.

\displaystyle\mbox{converges absolutely for }|z|<1,\mbox{ diverges for }|z|>1,

\displaystyle\mbox{converges also on }|z|=1\mbox{ if Re(c-a-b)}>0.

5
Conclusions

By using solutions of the Heun general equation (
2
), we have seen that the
potentials (
1
) and (
1
) are quasi-exactly solvable (QES)
when
\bm{l}
is
integer, excepting
\bm{l}=-1,-2,-3,-4
; however they are QES
even when

\bm{l}\neq-5/2
is half an odd integer. Furthermore, this procedure also gives two convergent infinite
series for each value of the parameter
\bm{l}
, a fact which
does not follow from the previous approaches.

In order to obtain the infinite series we have used four
of the eight solutions
\mathring{H}^{(i)}(x)
(resulting from homotopic transformations) in powers of
x
for the Heun equation; the remaining
solutions contain unbounded multiplicative factors. Beside this, in the present cases, the range of the independent
variable
x=\operatorname{sn}^{2}u
lies inside the region of convergence
of
\mathring{H}^{(i)}(x)
which includes only two singular points,
x=0
and
x=1
.

On the other side, we have already found
[
1
]
that expansions in powers series and in series
of hypergeometric functions for Heun’s general equation lead to solutions for the confluent Heun equation (CHE) (also called generalized spheroidal equation
[
20
,
21
]
). Then,
we can ask if there are QES potentials which, in addition to finite-series solutions, also
admit infinite series as the ones aforementioned.

In fact, for two Ushveridge’s trigonometric potentials
[
12
]
,
the Schrödinger equation (
1
) obeys a CHE
[
22
]
. Thence,
to treat the above problem, first we have to generate new solutions by using the appropriate transformations of the CHE
[
23
,
24
]
. Indeed,
it seems that these transformations will generate finite-series and infinite-series
solutions for such QES potentials.

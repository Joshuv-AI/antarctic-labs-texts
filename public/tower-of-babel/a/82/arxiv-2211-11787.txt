Counting atypical black hole microstates
from entanglement wedges

Abstract
Disentangled black hole microstates are atypical states in holographic CFTs whose gravity duals do not have smooth horizons. If there exist sufficiently many disentangled microstates to account for the entire black hole entropy, then any black hole microstate can be written as a superposition of states without smooth horizons. We show that there exist sufficiently many disentangled microstates to account for almost the entire black hole entropy of a large AdS black hole at the semiclassical limit G N → 0 G_{N}\rightarrow 0 . In addition, we also argue that in generic quantum many-body systems with short-ranged interactions, there exist sufficiently many area law states in the microcanonical subspace to account for almost the entire thermodynamic entropy in the standard thermodynamic limit. Area law states are atypical since a typical state should contain volume law entanglement. Furthermore, we also present an explicit way to construct such a set of area law states, and argue that the same construction may also be used to construct disentangled states.

2.1
Entanglement of formation and other correlation measures

Consider a bipartite system composited by
A
and
B
, and denote the Hilbert space associated with it as
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
. For a (possibly mixed) state
\rho\in{\cal D}({\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B})
, there are many quantities to measure the entanglement and correlation between
A
and
B
.

Since entanglement is an important resource which classical systems does not have, one may want to construct a function which counts only the amount of entanglement but not any classical correlations between
A
and
B
. Such a function is called an entanglement measure. More precisely, an entanglement measure must satisfy some axioms
[
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
, which guarantees that it can quantitatively measure the amount of entanglement and does not contain any classical correlations,
which can be generated via LOCC operations.
See appendix
A
for a set of axioms required for an entanglement measure. On the other hand, functions which do not fully satisfy all the axioms are often called correlation measures. In the following, let us list some entanglement measures and correlation measures which will be used in this paper.

Entanglement entropy

When
\rho
is a pure state, the entanglement entropy is defined as

\displaystyle E_{vN}(\rho)=S(\rho_{A})=S(\rho_{B}),

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{A(B)}\equiv{\rm Tr}_{B(A)}(\rho),

(2.2)

is the reduced density matrix on
{\cal H}_{A(B)}
and

\displaystyle S(\sigma)=-{\rm Tr}\left(\sigma\log{\sigma}\right)

(2.3)

is the von Neumann entropy. The reduced von Neumann entropy on pure states is the only function which satisfy all the axioms for entanglement measures
[
35
]
. Therefore, the entanglement entropy is an entanglement measure when restricted to pure states.

On the other hand, constructing a computable entanglement measure for a mixed state which has a good operational meaning is much more challenging.

Entanglement of formation

One way to define a correlation measure for a mixed state
\rho
is to regard
\rho
as an ensemble of pure states

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|,

(2.4)

and then consider the average entanglement entropy over this ensemble:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}p_{i}E_{vN}(|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|).

(2.5)

The entanglement of formation
[
29
]
of
\rho
is defined as the minimal one of such average entanglement entropies over all variations of possible ensemble decomposition:

\displaystyle E_{F}(\rho)\equiv\inf\left\{\sum_{i}p_{i}E_{vN}(|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|):\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|\right\}.

(2.6)

The entanglement of formation is not an entanglement measure, and it is usually not computable since the definition includes a minimization. However, it has a good operational meaning related to quantum measurements
[
29
]
which motivates people to consider it.

In this paper, the scaling behavior of
E_{F}
in holographic CFTs and quantum many-body systems will play a central role. Therefore, we will review two correlation measures which give rigorous upper bounds to
E_{F}(\rho)
.

Entanglement of purification

Another idea to measure the correlation between
A
and
B
for a mixed state
\rho
is to first uplift it into a pure state and then consider entanglement entropy on that pure state. Such an uplifting procedure is called purification. More precisely, for an arbitrary ancilla
C
and a pure state
|\psi_{ABC}\rangle
in
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}\otimes{\cal H}_{C}
, if it reduces to
\rho\in{\cal D}({\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B})
after tracing out
C
, i.e.

\displaystyle\rho={\rm Tr}_{C}|\psi_{ABC}\rangle\langle\psi_{ABC}|,

(2.7)

then
|\psi_{ABC}\rangle
is called a purification of
\rho
. By artificially dividing
{\cal H}_{C}
into two parts

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{C}={\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}},

(2.8)

and assigning

to

, we can get
A\cup A^{\prime}~(B\cup B^{\prime})
as an extension of
A(B)
. It is natural to expect that the entanglement entropy between

Obviously, different choices of

\displaystyle E_{P}(\rho)\equiv\inf\Bigg\{S\left({\rm Tr}_{BB^{\prime}}|\psi_{ABC}\rangle\langle\psi_{ABC}|\right):\rho={\rm Tr}_{C}|\psi_{ABC}\rangle\langle\psi_{ABC}|~{\rm and}~C=A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}\Bigg\}.

(2.9)

E_{P}(\rho)
gives a rigorous upper bound of entanglement of formation

as

\displaystyle E_{F}(\rho)\leq E_{P}(\rho).

(2.10)

On the other hand, while

Reflected entropy

Since the entanglement of purification is not computable in general, one may want to consider a specific way of purification with which one can get a computable quantity. For example, let us consider a mixed state
\rho\in{\cal D}({\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B})
and diagonalize it as

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{\nu}p_{\nu}|\varphi_{\nu}\rangle\langle\varphi_{\nu}|.

(2.11)

For each
|\varphi_{\nu}\rangle\in{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
, we can consider its CPT conjugate and denote it as
|\varphi_{\nu}^{*}\rangle\in{\cal H}_{A^{*}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{*}}
. Then, obviously, the state

\displaystyle|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle=\sum_{\nu}\sqrt{p_{\nu}}|\varphi_{\nu}\rangle|\varphi_{\nu}^{*}\rangle

(2.12)

defined on
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}\otimes{\cal H}_{A^{*}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{*}}
serves as a purification of
\rho
. Such a purification is called the canonical purification
[
34
]
.

Associated with the canonical purification, the reflected entropy
[
34
]
is defined as the entanglement entropy between

and

\displaystyle E_{RE}(\rho)=S({\rm Tr}_{BB^{*}}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle\sqrt{\rho}|).

(2.13)

By definition, it satisfies

\displaystyle E_{P}(\rho)\leq E_{RE}(\rho).

(2.14)

Therefore, combining with (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle E_{F}(\rho)\leq E_{P}(\rho)\leq E_{RE}(\rho).

(2.15)

The best advantage of

is that it is usually easy to compute. We will apply these features to discuss generic quantum many-body systems in the thermodynamic limit.

2.3
Holographic dual of the microcanonical ensemble

As we have explained above, not all the states in a holographic CFT are geometric states. Since the standard AdS/CFT dictionary

46

Geometric states constructed in this way include the ground state, canonical ensembles

In this paper, we would like to focus on microcanonical ensembles

(2.23)

which are more complicated to prepare with a standard path integral. Fortunately, the gravity dual of the canonical purifications of (

\displaystyle|{\rm MCTFD}(E)\rangle\propto\sum_{i{\rm~s.t.}~E_{i}\in[E-\Delta E,E]}|E_{i}\rangle|E_{i}^{*}\rangle

(2.24)

has been already discussed in
[
48
]
11
11

11

In fact, the exact setup analyzed in
[
48
]
is
|\psi\rangle\propto\sum_{E^{\prime}}e^{-\beta E^{\prime}}f(E^{\prime}-E)|E^{\prime}\rangle|E^{\prime*}\rangle
where

. However, as commented in
[

, the results hold for any

concentrated around

.
. This is again given by a two-sided black hole in the semiclassical limit

The difference between the gravity duals of a canonical ensemble and a microcanonical ensemble is that, while we need to find the classical geometry which minimize the gravitational action
I_{\rm grav}
on the AdS side when using a standard path integral to prepare a canonical ensemble or a thermofield double state, we need to find the one which maximize another functional, which coincides the black hole entropy for black hole geometries, when considering the microcanonical counterparts
[
48
]
.

The results explained above can be naturally understood from the view point of the relation between statistical mechanics and thermodynamics. In statistical mechanics, different ensembles including the canonical ensemble and the microcanonical ensemble are equivalent to each other in reproducing the macroscopic quantities at the limit of large degrees of freedom (i.e. the thermodynamic limit). In the current case, the semiclassical limit
1/G_{N}\rightarrow\infty
, where
G_{N}
is the Newton constant, plays a similar role of the thermodynamic limit. At the thermodynamic limit, the natural thermodynamic potential associated with the canonical ensemble is the Helmholtz free energy, which holds temperature
1/\beta
as a natural variable
12
12

12

Here, “a thermodynamic potential
A

as a function of
x,y,\cdots
. For example, when expressing the Helmholtz free energy

. However, when expressing

as a function of the energy
E
and the volume

, some information is lost in

. On the other hand, the natural thermodynamic potential associated with the microcanonical ensemble is the thermodynamic entropy, which holds energy
E
as the natural variable. In the current case, the gravitational action

parameterized by
\beta
corresponds to the Helmholtz free energy, and the functional which coincides the black hole entropy in black hole geometries
[
48
]
parameterized by
E
corresponds to the thermodynamic entropy. The thermodynamic equilibrium is realized when the Helmholtz free energy is minimized (or equivalently, when the thermodynamic entropy is maximized).

Moreover, it is also shown in
[

]
that the Ryu-Takayanagi formula
[

and its covariant extension, the Hubeny-Rangamani-Takayanagi formula

) for microcanonical ensembles.

Then we can take another disentangled state

5.1
Constructing an ensemble of states with low entanglement

In this subsection, we construct an ensemble of states

that generates

show that the average entanglement entropy associated with this ensemble is bounded by the reflected entropy and hence follows an area law under conjecture
5
.

To be concrete, let us consider a discrete system defined on a lattice with
L^{d}
sites. For a continuous quantum field theory, we can consider its lattice regularization. Then we have
O(e^{L^{d}})
orthogonal product states which span the Hilbert space of the whole system. Let us denote the set of these product states as
\{|P_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1,2,\cdots}
. Since the microcanonical ensemble
\rho^{\rm(micro)}
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\rho^{\rm(micro)}

\displaystyle=e^{-S_{\rm micro}}\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}

\displaystyle=e^{-S_{\rm micro}}\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}\left(\sum_{i}|P_{i}\rangle\langle P_{i}|\right)\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left(e^{-S_{\rm micro}/2}\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}|P_{i}\rangle\right)\left(\langle P_{i}|\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}e^{-S_{\rm micro}/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}e^{-S_{\rm micro}}\langle P_{i}|\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}|P_{i}\rangle\left(\frac{\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}|P_{i}\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle P_{i}|\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}|P_{i}\rangle}}\right)\left(\frac{\langle P_{i}|\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}}{\sqrt{\langle P_{i}|\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}|P_{i}\rangle}}\right),

(5.1)

it seems plausible to expect that

\displaystyle p_{i}

\displaystyle=e^{-S_{\rm micro}}\langle P_{i}|\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}|P_{i}\rangle,

(5.2)

\displaystyle|\psi_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}|P_{i}\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle P_{i}|\Pi_{[E-\Delta E,E]}|P_{i},\rangle}},

(5.3)

gives a small average entanglement. This is indeed the case that the average entanglement entropy in this case can be bounded by the reflected entropy of
\rho^{\rm(micro)}
. In fact, we can show a more general theorem presented below.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\rho
be a density matrix defined on a bipartite system
{\cal H}={\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
.
Take any set of orthogonal states
\{|P_{i}\rangle_{A(B)}\}

for which
\sum_{i}|P_{i}\rangle\langle P_{i}|_{A(B)}
gives the unity matrix
I_{A(B)}
on
{\cal H}_{A(B)}
.
Then, using product states
|P_{ij}\rangle=|P_{i}\rangle_{A}\otimes|P_{j}\rangle_{B}
,
one can decompose
\rho
as

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{i,j}\sqrt{\rho}|P_{ij}\rangle\langle P_{ij}|\sqrt{\rho}=\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}|\psi_{ij}\rangle\langle\psi_{ij}|,

(5.4)

where

\displaystyle p_{ij}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{ij}|\rho|P_{ij}\rangle,

(5.5)

\displaystyle|\psi_{ij}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\rho}|P_{ij}\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle P_{ij}|\rho|P_{ij}\rangle}}.

(5.6)

For this ensemble of states, the average entanglement entropy is upper bounded by the reflected entropy of
\rho
:

\displaystyle\overline{E_{vN}}=\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}E_{vN}\left(|\psi_{ij}\rangle\langle\psi_{ij}|\right)\leq E_{RE}(\rho),

(5.7)

if
|P_{ij}^{*}\rangle=|P_{i}^{*}\rangle_{A}\otimes|P_{j}^{*}\rangle_{B}
.
14
14

14

Note that this assumption holds for any theory whose CPT transformation respects the locality. This is the type of theory we are interested in.

Proof.

A key observation in our proof is that

\displaystyle I_{A}\otimes I_{B}\otimes|P_{i}^{*}\rangle\langle P_{i}^{*}|_{A^{*}}\otimes|P_{j}^{*}\rangle\langle P_{j}^{*}|_{B^{*}}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{p_{ij}}|\psi_{ij}\rangle|P_{ij}^{*}\rangle,

(5.8)

where
|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle
is the canonical purification of
\rho
.
Indeed, by definition of the canonical purification (
2.12
), we have

\displaystyle|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho}\otimes I\sum_{\nu}|\varphi_{\nu}\rangle|\varphi_{\nu}^{*}\rangle=\sqrt{\rho}\otimes I\sum_{\nu}\left(\sum_{i,j}|P_{ij}\rangle\langle P_{ij}|\right)|\varphi_{\nu}\rangle|\varphi_{\nu}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho}\otimes I\sum_{\nu}\sum_{i,j}\langle P_{ij}|\varphi_{\nu}\rangle|P_{ij}\rangle|\varphi_{\nu}^{*}\rangle=\sqrt{\rho}\otimes I\sum_{\nu}\sum_{i,j}\langle\varphi_{\nu}^{*}|P_{ij}^{*}\rangle|P_{ij}\rangle|\varphi_{\nu}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho}\otimes I\sum_{i,j}|P_{ij}\rangle\left(\sum_{\nu}|\varphi_{\nu}^{*}\rangle\langle\varphi_{\nu}^{*}|\right)|P_{ij}^{*}\rangle=\sqrt{\rho}\otimes I\sum_{i,j}|P_{ij}\rangle|P_{ij}^{*}\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\sqrt{p_{ij}}|\psi_{ij}\rangle|P_{ij}^{*}\rangle,

(5.9)

where in the second line, we have used the antiunitarity of the CPT operator.
Eq. (
5.8
) motivates us to evaluate the changes in the entanglement entropy
due to the local projective measurements on
A^{*}
and
B^{*}
.

Consider the projection

\displaystyle K_{j}

\displaystyle=I_{A}\otimes I_{B}\otimes I_{A^{*}}\otimes|P_{j}^{*}\rangle\langle P_{j}^{*}|_{B^{*}}.

(5.10)

From the completeness of
K_{j}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{BB^{*}}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle\sqrt{\rho}|=\sum_{j}\mathrm{Tr}_{BB^{*}}\left[K_{j}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle\sqrt{\rho}|{K_{j}}^{\dagger}\right].

(5.11)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle E_{RE}(\rho)=S(\mathrm{Tr}_{BB^{*}}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle\sqrt{\rho}|)=S\left(\sum_{j}s_{j}\mathrm{Tr}_{BB^{*}}|K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}|\right),

(5.12)

where

\displaystyle|K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{j}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\sqrt{\rho}|K_{j}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle}},

(5.13)

\displaystyle s_{j}

\displaystyle=\langle\sqrt{\rho}|K_{j}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle.

(5.14)

Further, using the matrix concavity of the von Neumann entropy,
we obtain

\displaystyle E_{RE}(\rho)\geq\sum_{i}s_{j}S(\mathrm{Tr}_{BB^{*}}|K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}|).

(5.15)

In the same way, we also obtain

\displaystyle S(\mathrm{Tr}_{BB^{*}}|K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\mathrm{Tr}_{AA^{*}}|K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}|)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{j}r_{ij}S(\mathrm{Tr}_{AA^{*}}|J_{i}K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle J_{i}K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}r_{ij}S(\mathrm{Tr}_{BB^{*}}|J_{i}K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle J_{i}K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}|),

(5.16)

where

\displaystyle J_{i}

\displaystyle=I_{A}\otimes I_{B}\otimes|P_{i}^{*}\rangle\langle P_{i}^{*}|_{A^{*}}\otimes I_{B^{*}},

(5.17)

\displaystyle|J_{i}K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{i}K_{j}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\sqrt{\rho}|J_{i}K_{j}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle}},

(5.18)

\displaystyle r_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\sqrt{\rho}|J_{i}K_{j}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle}{\langle\sqrt{\rho}|K_{j}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle}.

(5.19)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle E_{RE}(\rho)\geq\sum_{i,j}r_{ij}s_{j}S(\mathrm{Tr}_{BB^{*}}|J_{i}K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\langle J_{i}K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}|).

(5.20)

Using Eq. (

\displaystyle r_{ij}s_{j}

(5.21)

\displaystyle|J_{i}K_{j}\sqrt{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\psi_{ij}\rangle|P_{ij}^{*}\rangle.

\displaystyle E_{RE}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}\left\{S(\mathrm{Tr}_{B}|\psi_{ij}\rangle\langle\psi_{ij}|)+S(\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{*}}|P_{ij}^{*}\rangle\langle P_{ij}^{*}|)\right\}\geq\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}E_{vN}(|\psi_{ij}\rangle\langle\psi_{ij}|)=\overline{E_{vN}}.

(5.23)

Hence Eq. (
5.7

6
Summary and Discussions

In this paper, we have studied two types of low-entanglement atypical states in holographic CFTs and generic quantum many-body systems. Our results can be summarized as follows:

•

There exist at least
\exp\left[S_{\rm micro}-f(S_{\rm micro})\right]
(
\forall f(x)\in\omega(1)
) orthogonal disentangled black hole microstates in the microcanonical subspace
{\cal H}_{[E-\Delta E,E]}
in holographic CFTs at the semiclassical limit
G_{N}\rightarrow 0
.

•

There exist at least
\exp\left[S_{\rm micro}-O(\log S_{\rm micro})\right]
orthogonal area law states in the microcanonical subspace in generic quantum many-body systems, under the assumption that the microcanonical TFD obeys an area law of entanglement. Holographic CFTs and generic one-dimensional quantum many-body systems with short-ranged interactions satisfy this assumption.

•

We have presented an explicit algorithm to construct
\exp\left[S_{\rm micro}-f(S_{\rm micro})\right]
orthogonal area law states in generic quantum many-body systems and discussed its potential to be used to construct disentangled states in holographic CFTs.

Let us discuss possible improvements of our arguments, relations with other topics including additivity conjectures and black hole microstate counting, and future directions below.

6.1
Connections to additivity conjectures

In
[
13
]
, the authors argue that the nonexistence of extensive violations of additivity conjectures leads to the existence
\exp\left[S_{\rm can}-O(1)\right]
orthogonal disentangled microstates. Our results, the existence
\exp\left[S_{\rm micro}-f(S_{\rm micro})\right]
(
\forall f(x)\in\omega(1)
) orthogonal disentangled microstates, is not directly comparable with this since the entropies used are computated from different ensembles.
However, we would like to note that since
S_{\rm micro}
and
S_{\rm can}
matches at the leading order, the associated physics is essentially the same. Moreover, we can even argue that the existence
\exp\left[S_{\rm micro}-f(S_{\rm micro})\right]
(
\forall f(x)\in\omega(1)
) orthogonal disentangled microstates implies the existence
\exp\left[S_{\rm can}-O(1)\right]
orthogonal disentangled microstates, under some assumptions from thermodynamics. This comparison will be discussed in appendix
C
.
Therefore, it seems that trying to find extensive violations of additivity conjectures from the black hole states might be a less fruitful direction than people expected
[
13
,
25
]
.

However, our arguments have the potential to give crucial insights into additivity conjectures from an alternative point of view. The reason is that bounding the entanglement of formation
E_{F}
plays a significant role in our argument, and there exists a version of additivity conjecture which is profoundly related to
E_{F}

[
22
]
. The entanglement of formation
E_{F}
is lower bounded by the entanglement cost
E_{C}

[
29
]
, i.e.
E_{F}\geq E_{C}
, in any bipartite systems. Here, the entanglement cost of a bipartite state is defined as the minimal number of Bell pairs one needs to prepare the state with LOCC. It is known that additivity conjectures lead to
E_{F}=E_{C}
. Also in this case, while small violations are known
[
23
,
52
]
, extensive violations have not been found yet
[
53
]
. Therefore, it would be interesting to find possible relations between our arguments and this version of additivity conjectures.

6.2
Atypical microstate counting for black holes

Although it has long been known that one can find sufficiently many black hole microstates from string theory to match the leading order of the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy since
[
54
]
, less knowledge is known about the atypical state counting. One of the most successful atypical microstate counting from string theory is that of superstrata. Superstrata
[
55
,
56
]
are horizonless geometries which are solutions of supergravity and describe microstates of the D1-D5-P black hole. These geometries deviate from the black hole and cap off smoothly. In this sense, the Ryu-Takayanagi surface in it would look very similar to the blue surface shown in the right of figure
1
. This suggests superstrata are likely to be explicit examples of disentangled states. However, so far, only about
O\left(\exp\left[S_{BH}^{3/4}\right]\right)
superstrata have been found. This is parametrically smaller than our lower bound of disentangled states which is sufficient to recover the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy. Although not every disentangled state is guaranteed to have a geometrical dual, this comparison is expected to give hints to atypical microstate counting in string theory.

6.3
Why atypicality is important in studying quantum gravity?

Let us get some hints from statistical mechanics and argue why studying atypicality is important in understanding quantum gravity. In statistical mechanics, although one knows almost every state in a microscopic theory is typical, it is usually hard to explicitly construct a typical state by experience
[
57
]
, otherwise, we would just pick up such a typical state to get macroscopic information rather than using canonical ensembles or microcanonical ensembles.

An intuitive reason is as follows. In order to pick up a microstate from the energy shell in such a complicated many-body system, one usually needs to design a sophisticated algorithm. As a result, the state will be special in the sense that it is picked up by a sophisticated algorithm, and hence tend to be atypical. From this point of view, it is natural why solving supergravity induced from string theory can give so many atypical geometries. This might also be able to help us understand why the chaotic nature of gravity seems to disappear when supersymmetry is involved in AdS
2
holography
[
58
,
59
]
.

On the other hand, this also suggests that studying generic and collective features of atypical states should be important in understanding the microscopic structure of quantum gravity since the microstates which are easier to get access to are expected to be atypical.

Axioms for entanglement measures

In this appendix, we summarize the axioms (or postulates)

\dim{\cal H}_{B}\leq\dim{\cal H}_{A}
without loss of generality. The axioms are as follows.

(i)

If
\rho
is a separable state, then
E(\rho)=0
.

(ii)

Normalization -
If
\rho
is a maximally entangled state, then
E(\rho)=\log\dim{\cal H}_{B}
.

(iii)

Monotonicity under LOCC -

E(\rho)
is monotonically decreasing under LOCC, i.e., for any LOCC operation
\Gamma_{\rm LOCC}(\cdot)
,

\displaystyle E(\Gamma_{\rm LOCC}(\rho))\leq E(\rho).

(A.1)

(iv)

Asymptotic Continuity -
For any sequence of Hilbert space
\{\mathcal{H}_{n}\}
and any sequence of states
\{\rho_{n}\}
and
\{\sigma_{n}\}
with
\rho_{n},\sigma_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
,

\displaystyle\|\rho_{n}-\sigma_{n}\|_{1}\rightarrow 0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{E\left(\rho_{n}\right)-E\left(\sigma_{n}\right)}{1+\log{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}_{n}}\rightarrow 0.

(A.2)

(v)

Additivity -
For any state
\rho\in{\cal D}\left({\cal H}\right)={\cal D}\left({\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}\right)

(A.3)

(vi)

Convexity -
For any

\displaystyle E(\lambda\rho+(1-\lambda)\sigma)\leq\lambda E(\rho)+(1-\lambda)E(\sigma).

(A.4)

(vii)

For any

into

\displaystyle E(\rho\otimes\sigma)\leq E(\rho)+E(\sigma).

(A.5)

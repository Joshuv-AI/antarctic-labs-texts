A note on generalized tensor CUR approximation for tensor pairs and tensor triplets based on the tubal product Journal: Elsevier
Salman Ahmadi-Asl, Naeim Rezaeian

Abstract
In this note, we briefly present a generalized tensor CUR (GTCUR) approximation for tensor pairs ( 𝐗 ¯ , 𝐘 ¯ ) (\underline{\bf X},\underline{\bf Y}) and tensor triplets ( 𝐗 ¯ , 𝐘 ¯ , 𝐙 ¯ ) (\underline{\bf X},\underline{\bf Y},\underline{\bf Z}) based on the tubal product (t-product). We use the tensor Discrete Empirical Interpolation Method (TDEIM) to do these extensions. We show how the TDEIM can be utilized to generalize the classical tensor CUR (TCUR) approximation, which acts only on a single tensor, to jointly compute the TCUR of two and three tensors. This approach can be used to sample relevant lateral/horizontal slices of one data tensor relative to one or two other data tensors. For some special cases, the Generalized TCUR (GTCUR) method is reduced to the classical TCUR for both tensor pairs and tensor triplets in a similar fashion as shown for the matrices.

Introduction

The matrix CUR (MCUR) approximation is a well-known technique for fast low-rank approximation of matrices
[
1
]
. It offers an interpretable approximation and a more compact data representation as it uses the actual columns/rows of the underlying data matrix to build the factor matrices.
The generalized MCUR (GMCUR) approximation for matrix pairs and matrix triplets were presented in
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, respectively. Similar to the SVD, the MCUR is applied to a single matrix while the GMCUR considers a pair and a triplet of matrices and provides low-rank approximations of two/three matrices simultaneously. The key idea behind this generalization is using the same column indices for two given data matrices. To be more precise, let two data matrices
{\bf X}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}},\,(I_{1}\geq I_{2}),\,{\bf Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{3}\times I_{2}},\,(I_{3}\geq I_{2})
that have the same number of columns be given. The generalized SVD (GSVD)
[
4
,
5
]
, guarantees the following decompositions

\displaystyle{\bf X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf U}\,{\rm diag}(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{I_{2}})\,{\bf Z},\quad\alpha_{i}\in[0,1]

(1)

\displaystyle{\bf Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf V}\,{\rm diag}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{I_{2}})\,{\bf Z},\quad\beta_{i}\in[0,1]

(2)

where
\alpha_{i}^{2}+\beta_{i}^{2}=1
with the ratios
\beta_{i}/\alpha_{i}
of nondecreasing order for
i=1,2,\ldots,I_{2}
. Here,
{\bf U}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{1}},\,{\bf V}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{3}\times I_{3}}
are orthogonal, while
{\bf Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{2}\times I_{2}}
is a nonsingular matrix. As can be seen, the GSVD shares the common factor matrix
{\bf Z}
between the given data matrices
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
. On the other hand, from the theory of the MCUR approximation, we know that by applying the Discrete Empirical Interpolation Method (DEIM)
[
6
]
to the basis
{\bf Z}
(to be discussed in Section
3
), we can sample appropriate column indices from the data matrices
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
. Since the same basis matrix
{\bf Z}
is used within the DEIM, the same indices will be produced for sampling the columns of the matrix
{\bf X}
and the matrix
{\bf Y}
. Besides, the matrices
{\bf U}
and
{\bf V}
can be used to sample row indices of the matrices
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
, respectively. This means that the selected row indices for these matrices can be different. As a result, for two matrices
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
with the same number of columns, we can use the identical column indices for their MCUR approximations. The authors in
[
2
]
show that this idea can be potentially used in applications where one is interested in extracting the
most discriminative features from one data set relative to another data set. It is also motivated for recovering data matrices perturbed with colored noise and subgroup discovery application. Following this idea, in
[
3
]
, the authors consider triplet tensors
(\underline{\bf X},\underline{\bf Y},\underline{\bf Z})
and use the restricted SVD
[
7
]
to build their common factor matrices. Then, the DEIM is applied to the common factor matrices to sample columns and rows. It is shown that this approach can be used to extract the
most discriminative features from one data set relative to another data set. The GMCUR for tensor triplets is also motivated by the canonical correlation analysis and was successfully used for recovering datasets perturbed with colored noise, See
[
2
,
2
]
for details.

In this note, due to the applications of tensors
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, we focus on the tensor SVD (t-SVD)
[
11
]
that is defined based on the tubal product (t-product). The t-SVD has similar properties as the classical SVD. In particular, in contrast to the Tucker decomposition
[
12
,
13
]
or Canonical polyadic decomposition
[
14
,
15
]
, its truncation provides the best low tubal rank approximation in the least-squares sense. The GSVD was generalized to tensors based on the t-SVD in
[
16
,
17
]
.
Motivated by the works
[
2
,
3
]
, we extend the tensor CUR (TCUR) approximation
[
18
]
to a pair and a triplet of tensors based on the t-product and we refer to as the generalized TCUR (GTCUR). As real-world datasets often have multidimensional structure, it is more favorable to generalize the ideas proposed in
[
2
,
19
]
to tensors so that the structure of the datatensors are preserved. Indeed, we aim at achieving this target and present several generalizations of GMCUR to tensors. To this end, we use the tensor DEIM (TDEIM) index selection algorithm
[
20
]
, which we recently developed to sample important lateral and horizontal slices, and this plays a key role in the development of the GTCUR method. Similar to the matrix case, the TDEIM helps to compute the same lateral/horizontal slices for a given pair or triplet of third order tensors. We remark that alternative sampling procedures, such as top tubal leverage scores
[
21
,
18
]
or its sampling version, may be employed in place of the TDEIM, but because the TDEIM has been shown to give the optimal sample method
[
6
]
, we adopt it in our research.

The remainder of this note is organized as follows. We first present some basic tensor concepts in Section

Definition 2
.

(Transpose)

Definition 3

(Identity tensor)

.

(Orthogonal tensor)

(f-diagonal tensor)

Matrix CUR (MCUR) approximation of a single matrix and matrix pairs

The Matrix CUR (MCUR), or cross approximation, is a well-known method for fast low-rank matrix approximation with interpretable factor matrices and linear computational complexity
[
1
]
. This technique proposes to choose the individual columns and rows of a matrix in order to preserve the original data matrix’s structure, such as negativity or sparsity. Let us describe it formally. Given
{\bf X}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}}
be a give data matrix. The MCUR approximation seeks the approximation of the form
{\bf X}\approx{\bf C}{\bf U}{\bf R}
where
{\bf C}={\bf X}(:,\mathcal{J}),\,{\bf R}={\bf X}(\mathcal{I},:)
and
\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,2,\ldots,I_{1}\}
and
\mathcal{J}\subset\{1,2,\ldots,I_{2}\}
. It is obvious that the best middle matrix is

\displaystyle{\bf U}={\bf C}^{{\dagger}}{\bf X}{{\bf R}}^{{\dagger}},

(4)

as it minimizes the error term or

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf U}}\|{\bf X}-{\bf C}{\bf U}{\bf R}\|^{2}_{F}.

(5)

A deterministic or random selection of the columns and rows is possible, with the option of obtaining additive or relative approximation errors. It is generally known that the columns or rows with the highest volume might provide virtually optimal solutions in a deterministic scenario
[
29
]
. The DEIM is another deterministic way to select a matrix’s columns or rows and relies on the top singular vectors
[
6
]
. The three probability distributions that are most typically used to choose columns and rows are uniform, length-squared, and leverage-score distributions. For additional details on these sampling techniques, see
[
21
]
. It has been shown that approximations with relative error accuracy that are more practical in practice can be produced by sampling columns with the leverage-score probability distribution
[
30
]
.

The MCUR approximation of matrix pairs (matrices with the same number of columns) as the extension of the classical MCUR approximations were presented in
[
2
]
.
The key idea of this generalization is to select columns from two matrices that share the same column indices in order to extract the features of one dataset that are most pertinent to the other. Here, the common factor matrix produced by the GSVD of the two matrices is used to sample the columns and rows using the Discrete Empirical Interpolation Method (DEIM) technique. The MCUR approximation of triplet matrices was also presented in
[
3
]
, with the goal of identifying pertinent characteristics of one data matrix in relation to two other matrices. The authors in this instance apply the RSVD to produce two common factor matrices, to which the DEIM is then applied to sample the column and row indices. We first discuss the DEIM extension to tensors based on the t-product in the next section before using it to extend the TCUR technique to tensor pairs and tensor triplets in Sections
5
and
6
, respectively.

Tensor Discrete Empirical Interpolation Method (TDEIM) for lateral/horizontal slice sampling

We have recently generalized the DEIM method to the tensor case based on the t-product
[
20
]
that we call tensor DEIM (TDEIM). It has been experimentally shown that the TDEIM achieved the best sampling accuracy compared to the existing sampling techniques, such as top tubal leverage score sampling and uniform sampling.
The TDEIM to select horizontal slices is presented in Algorithm

Conclusion

In this note, we showed how the tensor CUR (TCUR) approximation can be extended to tensor pairs and tensor triplets. We use the tensor Discrete Interpolatory Empirical Method (TDEIM) to generalize the TCUR to tensor pairs and tensor triplets. This extension is called the generalized TCUR (GTCUR) method. We established connections between some special cases of the GTCUR and the classical TCUR approximation. Efficient algorithms are presented to compute the GTCUR approximation for both tensor pairs and tensor triplets. We are investigating the theoretical and numerical aspects of the presented GTCUR approaches for tensor pairs and tensor triplets in a practical setting.

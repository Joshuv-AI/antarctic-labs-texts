On the role of gradient terms in coercive quasilinear differential inequalities on Carnot groups
Guglielmo Albanese, Luciano Mari, Marco Rigoli

Abstract
In the sub-Riemannian setting of Carnot groups, this work investigates a priori estimates and Liouville type theorems for solutions of coercive, quasilinear differential inequalities of the type Δ 𝔾 φ ​ u ≥ b ⁡ ( x ) ​ f ​ ( u ) ​ l ​ ( | ∇ u | ) . \Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}u\geq b(x)f(u)l(|\nabla u|). Prototype examples of Δ 𝔾 φ \Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi} are the (subelliptic) p p -Laplacian and the mean curvature operator. The main novelty of the present paper is that we allow a dependence on the gradient l ⁡ ( t ) l(t) that can vanish both as t → 0 + t\rightarrow 0^{+} and as t → + ∞ t\rightarrow+\infty . Our results improve on the recent literature and, by means of suitable counterexamples, we show that the range of parameters in the main theorems are sharp.

Introduction

The search for
a-priori
estimates and Liouville type properties for solutions of coercive quasilinear differential inequalities of the type

\Delta^{\varphi}u\geq b(x)f(u)l(|\nabla u|)

(1.1)

has captured the interest of researchers and stimulated a great amount of work in recent years, especially in the Euclidean space. In this respect, the purpose of the present paper is to investigate the role played by the gradient term
l(|\nabla u|)
in the qualitative behaviour of solutions of (
1.1
). Our setting is that of Carnot groups, although the investigation could be carried over general Riemannian manifolds.
To begin with, we need to recall some basic facts about Carnot groups, referring to
[
3
]
for a thorough exposition. A Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
(of step
r
) is a connected, simply-connected nilpotent Lie group with a graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{G}=\oplus_{i=1}^{r}V^{m_{i}}_{i}
,
m_{i}=\dim V_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,r
generated by
V_{1}
, that is,
[V_{1},V_{i}]=V_{i+1}
,
[V_{1},V_{r}]=0
. Each Carnot group is isomorphic to a homogeneous Carnot group, that is, a triple
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\circ,\{\delta_{R}\})
where
\circ
is a Lie group structure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=\sum_{1}^{r}m_{i}
, and
\{\delta_{R}\}
is a distinguished family of Lie group automorphisms (called dilations) acting as follows: if we write
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(r)})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{r}}
, then

\delta_{R}(x)\doteq(Rx^{(1)},R^{2}x^{(2)},\ldots,R^{r}x^{(r)}).

In what follows, we will identify
\mathbb{G}
with the associated homogeneous Carnot group
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\circ)
via the procedure described in
[
3
]
(Theorem 2.2.18). The collection

\{x^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}\doteq\{x^{(1)}_{1},\ldots,x^{(1)}_{m_{1}},x^{(2)}_{1},\ldots,x^{(2)}_{m_{2}},\ldots,x^{(r)}_{1},\ldots,x^{(r)}_{m_{r}}\}

will then denote the induced coordinate system on
\mathbb{G}
, and integrations will always be performed with respect to the standard Lebesgue measure
\mathrm{d}x
, which is left-invariant on
\mathbb{G}
. The integer
Q\doteq\sum_{j=1}^{r}jm_{j}
is called the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}
.

Remark 1.1
.

The easiest example of homogeneous Carnot group is the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{Q}
,
Q\geq 1
, with the Lie group structure
+
. We remark that each Carnot group which is different from
(\mathbb{R}^{Q},+)
has homogeneous dimension
Q\geq 4
, being at least of step
2
.

For each
1\leq j\leq m_{1}
, let
X_{j}
be the left-invariant vector field generated by the coordinate vector field
\partial_{j}
at the unit element (the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). We can endow the span of
\{X_{j}\}
, called the first layer, with a Riemannian metric
\langle\,,\,\rangle
given by declaring
\{X_{j}\}
to be orthonormal. Consequently, one has a natural notion of horizontal gradient and divergence: for
u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{G})
and
Y=y^{j}X_{j}
horizontal (i.e. in the first layer), we set

\nabla_{0}u\doteq\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}X_{j}(u)X_{j},\qquad\mathrm{div}_{0}Y=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}X_{j}(y^{j}).

Thus, we can define the canonical sub-Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
, that is, the hypoelliptic operator

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}X_{j}X_{j}(u).

(1.2)

A key fact is that
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
possesses a fundamental solution
\Gamma(x)
with pole at the origin. Hence, if
Q\geq 3
(that is, if
\mathbb{G}\neq\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}
), we can consider

r(x)\doteq\Gamma(x)^{\frac{1}{2-Q}},

which is continuous on
\mathbb{G}
, smooth out of the origin and gives rise to a symmetric, homogeneous norm, that is,
r(x)>0
iff
x\neq 0
,
r(\delta_{R}(x))=Rr(x)
and
r(x^{-1})=r(x)
. Being
r
homogeneous of degree
1
with respect to dilations,
|\nabla_{0}r|
is homogeneous of degree
0
, whence in particular
|\nabla_{0}r|
is bounded. It can be proved that each pair of symmetric homogeneous norms
r_{1},r_{2}
on
\mathbb{G}
are equivalent, that is, they satisfy
C^{-1}r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq Cr_{1}
on
\mathbb{G}
for some constant
C>0
; thus, for the purpose of our paper, it is irrelevant which norm we will use, and, for this reason, hereafter we will fix one such
r
and rescale it in order to satisfy
|\nabla_{0}r|\leq 1
. As for the cases
Q=1
and
Q=2
, that is, when
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, in what follows we will consider the Euclidean homogeneous norm
r(x)=|x|
. Let
B_{R}\doteq\{x:r(x)<R\}
be a sub-level set for
r
, hereafter called a ball of radius
R
. The homogeneity of
r
with respect to
\delta_{R}
imply that the volume of
B_{R}
grows polynomially in
R
: indeed, changing variables according to
x=\delta_{R}(y)
, we have
\det((\delta_{R})_{*})=R^{Q}
and thus

\mathrm{vol}\big(B_{R}\big)=\int_{\{r(x)<R\}}\mathrm{d}x=R^{Q}\int_{\{r(y)<1\}}\mathrm{d}y=CR^{Q}.

(1.3)

Example
.

The simplest, non-trivial example of Carnot group is the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{m}
of real dimension
2m+1
, that is, the manifold
\mathbb{H}^{m}=\mathbb{C}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}
with group structure given by

(z,t)\circ(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(z+z^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+2\mathrm{Im}\langle z,z^{\prime}\rangle),\qquad\forall\,(z,t),(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}^{m},

where
\langle\,,\,\rangle
denotes the usual hermitian product in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. A basis for the left-invariant vector fields of
\mathbb{H}^{m}
is given by

X_{k}=2\mathrm{Re}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}}+2\mathrm{Im}z_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ ,\quad Y_{k}=2\mathrm{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}}-2\mathrm{Re}z_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ ,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

(1.4)

for
k=1,\ldots,m
, and they satisfy Heisenberg’s canonical commutation relations

\qquad[X_{j},Y_{k}]=-4\delta_{jk}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},

(1.5)

all the other commutators being zero. The fields
\{X_{j},Y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
generate the first layer, and dilations are given by
\delta_{R}(z,t)=(Rz,R^{2}t)
. The standard homogeneous norm is

r(x)=r(z,t)=\left(\left|z\right|^{4}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},

(1.6)

where
|\cdot|
is the norm in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, and a simple computation shows that
|\nabla_{0}r|^{2}=|z|^{2}/r^{2}\leq 1
.

Given a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
, the sub-Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
can always be written in divergence form with respect to the underlying Euclidean structure: more precisely, If
X_{j}=c_{j\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}
is the expression of
X_{j}
in the canonical basis
\{\partial_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}
, then

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=c_{j\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\big(c_{j\beta}\partial_{\beta}u\big)=\partial_{\alpha}\big(c_{j\alpha}c_{j\beta}\partial_{\beta}u\big),

(1.7)

where the last identity follows from an important property of Carnot groups, namely, the fact that the coefficient
c_{j\alpha}
does not depend on the
\alpha
-th coordinate (
[
3
]
, p. 64). Consequently, we can consider weak solutions of differential inequalities with
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
in regularity classes less demanding than
C^{2}(\mathbb{G})
. More generally, given
\varphi
satisfying

\varphi\in C^{0}\big([0,+\infty)\big),\qquad\varphi\geq 0\ \text{ on }\,[0,+\infty),

(1.8)

one can define a
\varphi
-Laplacian by setting

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}u=\mathrm{div}_{0}\left(\frac{\varphi(|\nabla_{0}u|)}{|\nabla_{0}u|}\nabla_{0}u\right)

in the weak sense. Typical examples are:

-

the
p
-Laplacian, where
\varphi(t)=t^{p-1}
and
p\geq 1
;

-

the (generalized) mean curvature operator, where
\varphi(t)=t^{k-1}(1+t^{k})^{-1/2}
and
k\geq 1
. The usual mean curvature operator is recovered for
k=2
.

In what follows, for convenience we will just consider
\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-solutions. We recall that a function
u:U\subset\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be Lipschitz if there exists
C>0
such that

u(x)-u(y)\leq Cr(x^{-1}y)\qquad\text{for each $x,y\in U$,}

(1.9)

where
r
is a fixed homogeneous norm. By (
1.7
) and a standard argument (see for instance
[
10
]
, Thm.5 p. 137), it is not hard to show that (
1.9
) implies the existence and the local boundedness of the weak derivatives
X_{j}(u)
.

Notation
.

Hereafter, given a function
u
on
\mathbb{G}
, we denote with
u^{*}\doteq\sup_{\mathbb{G}}u
,
u_{*}\doteq\inf_{\mathbb{G}}u
. We set
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\doteq[0,+\infty)
,
\mathbb{R}^{+}\doteq(0,+\infty)

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
,
l\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+})
and
b(x)\in C^{0}(\mathbb{G})
. A function
u\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{G})
is said to be a weak solution of

\Delta^{\varphi}_{\mathbb{G}}u\geq b(x)f(u)l(|\nabla_{0}u|)\qquad\text{(resp. $\leq$)}

(1.10)

if, for each
0\leq\phi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
,

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{\varphi(|\nabla_{0}u|)}{|\nabla_{0}u|}\langle\nabla_{0}u,\nabla_{0}\phi\rangle\mathrm{d}x\leq-\int_{\mathbb{G}}b(x)f(u)l(|\nabla_{0}u|)\phi\mathrm{d}x\qquad\text{(resp. $\geq$).}

(1.11)

In the present paper, we investigate solutions
u\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{G})
of

\Delta^{\varphi}_{\mathbb{G}}u\geq b(x)f(u)l(|\nabla_{0}u|),

(1.12)

possibly changing sign, under the following basic assumptions:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{(WpC)}&:\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}),\quad\varphi(0)=0,\quad\varphi(t)>0\ \text{ on }\,\mathbb{R}^{+};\\[2.84544pt]
\text{there exists $p\geq 1$, $C>0$ such that }\,\varphi(t)\leq Ct^{p-1}\ \text{ on }\,\mathbb{R}^{+};\end{array}\right.\\[14.22636pt]
&\quad b\in C^{0}(\mathbb{G}),\qquad b>0\ \text{ on }\,\mathbb{G};\\[5.69046pt]
&\quad f\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad l\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}),\qquad l(t)>0\ \text{if }\,t>0.\end{array}\right.

(1.13)

Agreeing with the notation in
[
8
,
9
]
, the tag (WpC) above denotes the
weak
p
-coercivity
of
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}
. Note that the
p
-Laplacian is (WpC), and the generalized mean curvature operator is (WpC) for each
k/2\leq p\leq k
.

Remark 1.2

S_{*}\doteq\inf_{\mathbb{G}}S>0
. Inequality (
2.3
) can be rewritten as

\Delta^{\varphi}_{\mathbb{G}}u\geq K(1+r)^{-\mu}\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{\chi}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}.

(2.7)

Moreover, we can suppose
K>0
, otherwise the estimate is trivial. Note that (
2.7
) is invariant with respect to translations
u\mapsto u_{s}\doteq u+s
. Fix
b>\hat{u}
. We claim that a suitable translated
u_{s}
satisfies

u_{s}\leq b(1+r)^{\sigma}\qquad\text{on }\,M,\qquad u_{s}>0\ \text{ somewhere.}

(2.8)

Indeed, if
\sigma>0
, (
2.2
) implies that
u<b(1+r)^{\sigma}
outside a large compact set
\Omega
, and translating
u
downwards we can achieve the same inequality also in
\Omega
, still keeping
u_{s}>0
somewhere. On the other hand, the claim is obvious if
\sigma=0
. In this last case, note that here we do
not
claim that
\hat{u}
is not attained: this would follow from a strong maximum principle, which seems to be not known under the sole assumption (WpC). Using that the resulting
u_{s}
is positive somewhere, we can also assume
\gamma\geq 0
. Hereafter, computations will be performed with
u=u_{s}
. Set for convenience

\eta\doteq\mu+(\sigma-1)(p-\chi),

(2.9)

and note that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\sigma\leq\frac{p-\chi-\mu}{p-\chi-1}&\Longleftrightarrow&\sigma\geq\eta.\end{array}

(2.10)

Choose
\alpha>b
and define

v(x)=\alpha\big(1+r(x)\big)^{\sigma}-u(x),

so that

(\alpha-b)(1+r)^{\sigma}\leq v\leq\alpha(1+r)^{\sigma}\qquad\text{on }\,\Omega_{\gamma}.

(2.11)

Fix a function
\lambda\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

0\leq\lambda\leq 1,\quad\lambda\equiv 0\ \ \text{ on }\,(-\infty,\gamma],\qquad\lambda>0\ \ \text{on }\,(\gamma,\infty),\quad\lambda^{\prime}\geq 0,

and a cut-off function
\zeta\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(M)
to be specified later, whose support has nontrivial intersection with
\Omega_{\gamma}
. Next, consider
F\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
F=F(r,v)
, satisfying

F(r,v)>0,\qquad F_{v}\doteq\frac{\partial F}{\partial v}(r,v)<0.

(2.12)

We insert the test function

\zeta^{p}\lambda(u)F(v,r)\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma})

in the weak definition of (
2.7
). Using
\lambda^{\prime}\geq 0
together with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
|\nabla_{0}r|\leq 1
and taking into account (
2.6
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle K\int\zeta^{p}\lambda F(1+r)^{-\mu}\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{\chi}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\leq\displaystyle p\int\zeta^{p-1}\lambda F\varphi(|\nabla_{0}u|)|\nabla_{0}\zeta|+\int\zeta^{p}\lambda F_{v}\varphi(|\nabla_{0}u|)|\nabla_{0}u|\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int\zeta^{p}\lambda\varphi(|\nabla_{0}u|)\left|\alpha\sigma(1+r)^{\sigma-1}F_{v}+F_{r}\right|\\[14.22636pt]
\qquad\qquad\leq\displaystyle p\int\zeta^{p-1}\lambda F\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-1}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}|\nabla_{0}\zeta|+\int\zeta^{p}\lambda F_{v}\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int\zeta^{p}\lambda\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-1}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\left|\alpha\sigma(1+r)^{\sigma-1}F_{v}+F_{r}\right|.\end{array}

(2.13)

Rearranging,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int\zeta^{p}\lambda\left|F_{v}\right|B(x,u)\leq p\int\zeta^{p-1}\lambda F\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-1}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}|\nabla_{0}\zeta|,\end{array}

(2.14)

with

\begin{array}[]{lcl}B(x,u)&=&\displaystyle K(1+r)^{-\mu}\frac{F}{|F_{v}|}\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{\chi}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}+\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\\[14.22636pt]
&&\displaystyle-\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-1}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\left|-\alpha\sigma(1+r)^{\sigma-1}+\frac{F_{r}}{|F_{v}|}\right|\\[14.22636pt]
&=&\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{\chi}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\left[K(1+r)^{-\mu}\frac{F}{|F_{v}|}+|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-\chi}\right.\\[14.22636pt]
&&\displaystyle\left.-|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-1-\chi}\left|-\alpha\sigma(1+r)^{\sigma-1}+\frac{F_{r}}{|F_{v}|}\right|\right].\end{array}

(2.15)

Let assume the validity of the following

\emph{claim:}\quad B(x,u)\geq\Lambda\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\qquad\text{for some $\Lambda>0$ independent of $r$.}

(2.16)

Plugging into (
2.14
) gives

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda}{p}\int\zeta^{p}\lambda\left|F_{v}\right|\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\leq\int\zeta^{p-1}\lambda F\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-1}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}|\nabla_{0}\zeta|,\end{array}

(2.17)

and thus, by Hölder inequality,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{p}}{p^{p}}\int\zeta^{p}\lambda\left|F_{v}\right|\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}&\leq&\displaystyle\int\frac{\lambda F}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\left(\frac{F}{|F_{v}|}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla_{0}\zeta|^{p}\\[14.22636pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle S_{*}^{-1}\int\lambda F\left(\frac{F}{|F_{v}|}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla_{0}\zeta|^{p}.\end{array}

(2.18)

Fix
R_{0}
large enough that
u
is not constant on
\Omega_{\gamma}\cap B_{R_{0}}\neq\emptyset
. We claim that the horizontal gradient
\nabla_{0}u
is not identically zero on
\Omega_{\gamma}\cap B_{R_{0}}
. Otherwise, since the first layer generates the whole Lie Algebra of
\mathbb{G}
, the whole Euclidean gradient of
u
would be zero, hence
u
would be constant on connected components of
\Omega_{\gamma}
. By the very definition of
\Omega_{\gamma}
, this would imply that
\Omega_{\gamma}\equiv\mathbb{G}
and
u
be constant, contradiction. For
R>2R_{0}
, we choose
\zeta
in such a way that

0\leq\zeta\leq 1,\quad\zeta\equiv 1\ \text{ on }B_{R},\quad\mathrm{supp}(\zeta)\subset B_{2R},\quad|\nabla_{0}\zeta|\leq\frac{C}{R},

(2.19)

for some absolute constant
C
.
Inserting into (
2.18
) and recalling that
\lambda\leq 1
we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{p}}{p^{p}}\int_{\Omega_{\gamma}\cap B_{R_{0}}}\lambda\left|F_{v}\right|\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{C^{p}}{S_{*}R^{p}}\int_{(B_{2R}\backslash B_{R})\cap\Omega_{\gamma}}F\left(\frac{F}{|F_{v}|}\right)^{p-1}.\end{array}

(2.20)

We now need to check the validity of the claim in (
2.16
), for a suitable choice of
F
. Observe that the expression in square brackets in (
2.15
) is a function of the type

g(s)=\rho+s^{p-\chi}-\beta s^{p-\chi-1},

for
s=|\nabla_{0}u|
and positive parameters

\rho\doteq K(1+r)^{-\mu}\frac{F}{|F_{v}|},\qquad\beta\doteq\left|-\alpha\sigma(1+r)^{\sigma-1}+\frac{F_{r}}{|F_{v}|}\right|

depending on
r
. It is a calculus exercise to check that
g(s)\geq\Lambda s^{p-\chi}
on
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
when

\Lambda\leq 1-\frac{p-\chi-1}{(p-\chi)^{\frac{p-\chi}{p-\chi-1}}}\left(\frac{\beta^{p-\chi}}{\rho}\right)^{1/(p-\chi-1)}.

(2.21)

Inequality (
2.21
) yields (
2.16
) provided that
\beta^{p-\chi}/\rho
is bounded from above by a (small enough) quantity independent of
r
. This suggests our choice of
F
, that will be different from case to case.

First case:

\sigma>\eta
(that is,
\sigma<\sigma^{*}
).

We choose

F(v,r)=\exp\left\{-\tau v(1+r)^{-\eta}\right\},

(2.22)

for a real number
\tau>0
to be specified later. Then, on
\Omega_{\gamma}

\frac{F}{|F_{v}|}=\frac{(1+r)^{\eta}}{\tau},\qquad\frac{F_{r}}{|F_{v}|}=\frac{v\eta}{(1+r)},

and hence, by (
2.11
) and using
\sigma>\eta
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\alpha(\sigma-\eta)(1+r)^{\sigma-1}\leq-\alpha\sigma(1+r)^{\sigma-1}+\frac{F_{r}}{|F_{v}|}\leq 0&\quad\text{if }\,\eta<0.\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\left[-\alpha(\sigma-\eta)-\eta b\right](1+r)^{\sigma-1}\leq-\alpha\sigma(1+r)^{\sigma-1}+\frac{F_{r}}{|F_{v}|}\leq 0&\quad\text{if }\,\eta\geq 0\end{array}

Plugging into (
2.15
) we get

B(x,u)\geq\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{\chi}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\left[\frac{K}{\tau}(1+r)^{\eta-\mu}+|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-\chi}-|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-1-\chi}\beta^{*}(1+r)^{\sigma-1}\right],

(2.23)

with

0<\beta^{*}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha(\sigma-\eta)&\quad\text{if }\,\eta<0\\[8.5359pt]
\alpha(\sigma-\eta)+\eta b&\quad\text{if }\,\eta\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

In view of identity (
2.9
), inequality (
2.21
) applied with

\rho\doteq\frac{K}{\tau}(1+r)^{\eta-\mu},\qquad\beta\doteq\beta^{*}(1+r)^{\sigma-1}

gives

\Lambda\leq 1-(p-\chi-1)\left(\frac{\beta^{*}}{p-\chi}\right)^{\frac{p-\chi}{p-\chi-1}}\left(\frac{\tau}{K}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-\chi-1}}.

(2.24)

For
\theta\in(0,1)
, choosing
\Lambda,\tau
in such a way that

\Lambda=1-\theta,\qquad\tau=\theta^{p-\chi-1}\frac{K}{(\beta^{*})^{p-\chi}}\frac{(p-\chi)^{p-\chi}}{(p-\chi-1)^{p-\chi-1}},

then (
2.24
) is met with the equality sign and the claim in (
2.16
) is proved. By our choice of
F
, (
2.20
) becomes

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\left(\frac{\tau\Lambda}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{B_{R_{0}}\cap\Omega_{\gamma}}\lambda F(1+r)^{-\eta}\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{C^{p}}{S_{*}R^{p}}\int_{(B_{2R}\backslash B_{R})\cap\Omega_{\gamma}}F(1+r)^{|\eta|(p-1)}.\end{array}

(2.25)

However, on
(B_{2R}\backslash B_{R})\cap\Omega_{\gamma}
, since
\sigma-\eta>0
, (
2.11
) gives

F(v,r)\leq\exp\left(-\tau(\alpha-b)\big(1+R\big)^{\sigma-\eta}\right),\qquad(1+r)^{|\eta|(p-1)}\leq(1+2R)^{|\eta|(p-1)}.

Inserting into (
2.25
) and using (
1.3
), we eventually get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&<&\displaystyle\left(\frac{\tau\Lambda}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\Omega_{\gamma}\cap B_{R_{0}}}\lambda F(1+r)^{-\eta}\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\\[14.22636pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{C^{p}}{S_{*}R^{p}}\exp\left(-\tau(\alpha-b)\big(1+R\big)^{\sigma-\eta}\right)(1+2R)^{|\eta|(p-1)}\mathrm{vol}(B_{2R})\\[14.22636pt]
&&\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }\,R\rightarrow+\infty.\end{array}

(2.26)

Therefore, letting
R\rightarrow+\infty
we deduce

\int_{\Omega_{\gamma}\cap B_{R_{0}}}\lambda F(1+r)^{-\eta}\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}=0.

However, since
\nabla_{0}u\not\equiv 0
on
\Omega_{\gamma}\cap B_{R_{0}}
and
F>0
, the above integral cannot be zero. Concluding, when
\sigma<\sigma^{*}
our assumption
K>0
leads to a contradiction, hence
K\leq 0
, as was to be proved.

Second case:

\sigma=\eta
(that is,
\sigma=\sigma^{*}
).

In this case, by (
2.9
) it holds
\sigma-\mu=(\sigma-1)(p-\chi)
. We choose

F(v,r)=v^{-\tau},

(2.27)

\tau>0
to be determined. Then, using (
2.11
),

\begin{array}[]{lcl}B(x,u)&\geq&\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{\chi}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\left[\frac{K}{\tau}(1+r)^{-\mu}v+|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-\chi}\right.\\[14.22636pt]
&&\displaystyle\left.-|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-1-\chi}\alpha\sigma(1+r)^{\sigma-1}\right]\\[14.22636pt]
&\geq&\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{\chi}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\left[\frac{K(\alpha-b)}{\tau}(1+r)^{\sigma-\mu}+|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-\chi}\right.\\[14.22636pt]
&&\displaystyle\left.-|\nabla_{0}u|^{p-1-\chi}\alpha\sigma(1+r)^{\sigma-1}\right],\end{array}

(2.28)

and, by (
2.21
), (
2.16
) holds whenever

\Lambda\leq 1-(p-\chi-1)\left(\frac{\alpha\sigma}{p-\chi}\right)^{\frac{p-\chi}{p-\chi-1}}\left(\frac{\tau}{K(\alpha-b)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-\chi-1}}.

(2.29)

For
\theta\in(0,1)
, set

\Lambda=1-\theta,\qquad\tau=\theta^{p-\chi-1}\frac{(p-\chi)^{p-\chi}}{(p-\chi-1)^{p-\chi-1}}\frac{K(\alpha-b)}{(\alpha\sigma)^{p-\chi}}

(2.30)

in order to satisfy (
2.29
) with the equality sign. Inequality (
2.20
) now reads

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{p}}{p^{p}}\int_{\Omega_{\gamma}\cap B_{R_{0}}}\lambda\left|F_{v}\right|\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{C^{p}}{S_{*}R^{p}}\int_{(B_{2R}\backslash B_{R})\cap\Omega_{\gamma}}v^{-\tau}\left(\frac{v}{\tau}\right)^{p-1}\\[14.22636pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{C^{p}}{\tau^{p-1}R^{p}}\int_{(B_{2R}\backslash B_{R})\cap\Omega_{\gamma}}v^{p-1-\tau}.\\[14.22636pt]
\end{array}

(2.31)

Estimating
v
with the aid of (
2.11
) and according to the sign of
p-1-\tau
, and using (
1.3
), the right-hand side is bounded from above by

C_{1}R^{Q-p+\sigma(p-1-\tau)},

for a suitable constant
C_{1}>0
. Letting
R\rightarrow+\infty
, for (
2.31
) to be compatible it is necessary that
Q-p+\sigma(p-1-\tau)\geq 0
, that is,

\tau\sigma\leq Q-1+(p-1)(\sigma-1).

(2.32)

(i)

If
Q-1+(p-1)(\sigma-1)<0
or
Q-1+(p-1)(\sigma-1)=0
and
\sigma>0
, then there exists no
\tau>0
satisfying the inequality. Therefore,
K>0
leads to a contradiction, as required. This is the third case for
H=0
in (
2.5
).

(ii)

If
Q-1+(p-1)(\sigma-1)\geq 0
then, setting
\alpha=tb
for
t>1
in expression (
2.30
) for
\tau
, inserting (
2.30
) into (
2.32
) and solving (
2.32
) with respect to
K
, letting
\theta\uparrow 1
and
b\downarrow\hat{u}
we deduce

K\leq\left[Q-1+(\sigma-1)(p-1)\right]\frac{(p-\chi-1)^{p-\chi-1}}{(p-\chi)^{p-\chi}}\hat{u}^{p-\chi-1}\sigma^{p-\chi-1}\frac{t^{p-\chi}}{t-1},

and minimizing over
t\in(1,+\infty)
we get

K\leq\left[Q-1+(\sigma-1)(p-1)\right]\hat{u}^{p-\chi-1}\sigma^{p-\chi-1}.

(2.33)

This concludes the case
Q-1+(p-1)(\sigma-1)>0
and
\sigma=\sigma^{*}>0
. To deal with the remaining case
Q-1+(p-1)(\sigma-1)\geq 0
and
\sigma=\sigma^{*}=0
, we can consider a downward translation
u_{s}
of
u
in place of
u
, and
\gamma=0
. Then,
u_{s}
satisfies (
2.7
) with the same constant
K
, hence (
2.33
) holds for each
\hat{u}_{s}
. However,
\hat{u}_{s}
can be made as small as we wish, and since we have assumed
K>0
this would contradict (
2.33
). Concluding, necessarily
K\leq 0
, as required.

Sharpness of Proposition
2.1
and Theorem
1.4

We consider the mean curvature operator in
(\mathbb{R}^{Q},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
with
Q\geq 2
, for which
p=2
and
S(t)=\sqrt{1+t^{2}}
in (
2.6
). Our aim is to produce solutions of

\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)\geq\bar{C}(1+r)^{-\mu}\frac{|\nabla u|^{\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{R}^{Q}

satisfying
u(x)=\mathcal{O}\big(r(x)^{\sigma}\big)
, outside of the range where
H=0
in (
2.5
). More precisely, we produce such solutions when:

\displaystyle 0\leq\chi<1,\qquad\mu<2-\chi,\qquad\sigma\geq\sigma^{*}\doteq\frac{2-\chi-\mu}{1-\chi}.

(2.34)

We underline that, in our setting,
(p-1)(\sigma-1)=\sigma-1>1-Q
. Hence, in the borderline case
\sigma=\sigma^{*}
we are in the range complementary to the one that imply
H=0
.

Fix a smooth, non-decreasing function
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0})
, and consider
u(x)=h(r^{2}(x))
with
r(x)=|x|
. Then, since
\mathrm{Hess}\,(r^{2})=2\langle\,,\,\rangle
,

\nabla u=2rh^{\prime}\nabla r,\quad|\nabla u|^{2}=4r^{2}(h^{\prime})^{2},\qquad\nabla\mathrm{d}u=4r^{2}h^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{d}r\otimes\mathrm{d}r+2h^{\prime}\langle\,,\,\rangle,

hence, using that
h^{\prime}\geq 0
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)=\displaystyle\frac{\Delta u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}-\frac{\mathrm{Hess}u(\nabla u,\nabla u)}{(1+|\nabla u|^{2})^{3/2}}\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\left[2(Q-1)h^{\prime}+\frac{4r^{2}h^{\prime\prime}+2h^{\prime}}{1+4r^{2}(h^{\prime})^{2}}\right]\end{array}

(2.35)

Choosing
h(t)=(1+t)^{\sigma/2}
, for some
\sigma>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)\geq\frac{\sigma(1+r^{2})^{\frac{\sigma-4}{2}}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\left[(Q-1)(1+r^{2})+\frac{1+r^{2}(\sigma-1)}{1+\sigma^{2}r^{2}(1+r^{2})^{\sigma-2}}\right].

(2.36)

If
\sigma\geq 1
, we can get rid of the second term in square brackets. On the other hand, if
\sigma\in(0,1)
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)&\geq&\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(1+r^{2})^{\frac{\sigma-4}{2}}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\left[(Q-1)(1+r^{2})+(1+r^{2})(\sigma-1)\right]\\[14.22636pt]
&=&\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(1+r^{2})^{\frac{\sigma-4}{2}}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}(Q+\sigma-2)(1+r^{2}).\end{array}

Summarizing, for each
\sigma>0
there exists a constant
C(\sigma,Q)
such that

\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)\geq\frac{C(1+r)^{\sigma-2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{R}^{Q}.

(2.37)

Now, consider parameters
\chi,\mu,\sigma
in the range prescribed in (
2.34
). Since

|\nabla u|=\sigma r(1+r^{2})^{\frac{\sigma-2}{2}}\leq\sigma(1+r^{2})^{\frac{\sigma-1}{2}}\leq C_{1}(1+r)^{\sigma-1}

we deduce, by the third in (
2.34
),

|\nabla u|^{\chi}(1+r)^{-\mu}\leq C_{2}(1+r)^{\chi(\sigma-1)-\mu}\leq C_{3}(1+r)^{\sigma-2},

(2.38)

and inserting into (
2.37
) we infer

\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)\geq C_{4}(1+r)^{-\mu}\frac{|\nabla u|^{\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{R}^{Q},

(2.39)

for some constant
C_{4}>0
. Therefore, if
Q\geq 2
the range where
H=0
in (
2.5
) cannot be improved.

Proposition 3.1

\displaystyle\displaystyle K\int\psi^{\eta}\lambda\frac{u^{\alpha+\omega}}{(1+r)^{\mu}}\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{\chi}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}

\displaystyle\leq-\displaystyle\int\frac{\varphi(|\nabla_{0}u|)}{|\nabla_{0}u|}\langle\nabla_{0}u,\nabla_{0}(\psi^{\eta}\lambda u^{\alpha})\rangle

\displaystyle-\alpha\int\psi^{\eta}\lambda u^{\alpha-1}\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{p}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}.

By (

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{J}_{1}&=&\displaystyle\frac{K}{2\eta}\psi^{\eta}\lambda\frac{u^{\alpha+\omega}}{(1+r)^{\mu}}\frac{|\nabla_{0}u|^{\chi}}{S(|\nabla_{0}u|)}\\[14.22636pt]

\begin{array}[]{rllrll}i)&\text{powers of $u$:}&\ \displaystyle\alpha=\frac{\alpha+\omega}{z_{1}}+\frac{\alpha-1}{z_{2}}&\quad ii)&\text{powers of $|\nabla_{0}u|$:}&\ \displaystyle p-1=\frac{\chi}{z_{1}}+\frac{p}{z_{2}}\\[14.22636pt]

Proof.

\nabla_{0}u_{2}&\hbox{on $\Omega_{2}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:u_{1}<u_{2}\right\}$}\,,\end{cases}

(4.7)

from which it follows

B(x,u,\nabla_{0}u)=B(x,u_{1},\nabla_{0}u_{1})\chi_{\Omega\backslash\Omega_{2}}+B(x,u_{2},\nabla_{0}u_{2})\chi_{\Omega_{2}}\,.

(4.8)

To prove that

]
: consider

\begin{cases}\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]\,,\quad\gamma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\\
\gamma^{\prime}\geq 0\quad\hbox{on $\mathbb{R}$}\\
\gamma(t)=0\,\,\hbox{if $t\leq 0$},\quad\gamma(t)=1\,\,\hbox{if $t\geq 1$}\,\end{cases}

(4.9)

and, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define
\gamma_{n}(t)\doteq\gamma(nt)
. Note that
\gamma_{n}(t)\nearrow\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(t)
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Consider a test function
0\leq\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, and define
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(U)
as being
\phi_{1}=\left[1-\gamma_{n}(u_{2}-u_{1})\right]\phi
and
\phi_{2}=\left[\gamma_{n}(u_{2}-u_{1})\right]\phi
. By approximation,
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are admissible test functions, and clearly
\gamma_{n}(u_{2}-u_{1})\rightarrow\chi_{\Omega_{2}}
pointwise.
Since both the
u_{i}
are solutions of (
4.5
), from (
4.4
) we deduce

\int_{\Omega}\left\{\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{i}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{i}\right|}\langle\nabla_{0}u_{i},\nabla_{0}\phi_{i}\rangle+B(x,u_{i},\nabla_{0}u_{i})\phi_{i}\right\}\leq 0\,,

(4.10)

thus, adding the two inequalities, computing
\nabla_{0}\phi_{i}
and rearranging, we get

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\phi\langle\left[\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|}\nabla_{0}u_{2}-\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|}\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right],\nabla_{0}(u_{2}-u_{1})\rangle

(4.11)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{n}\langle\left[\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|}\nabla_{0}u_{2}-\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|}\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right],\nabla_{0}\phi\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left[B(x,u_{2},\nabla_{0}u_{2})-B(x,u_{1},\nabla_{0}u_{1})\right]\gamma_{n}\phi

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left\{\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|}\langle\nabla_{0}u_{1},\nabla_{0}\phi\rangle+B(x,u_{1},\nabla_{0}u_{1})\phi\right\}\,.

Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\geq 0
, the first integral satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\phi\langle\left[\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|}\nabla_{0}u_{2}-\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|}\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right],\nabla_{0}\left(u_{2}-u_{1}\right)\rangle

(4.12)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\phi\left[\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|)-\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|)\right]\left(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|-\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|\right).

The product

\left[\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|)-\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|)\right]\left(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|-\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|\right)

is non-negative because of (
4.6
) (if
u_{1},u_{2}
are both non-constant) or (
4.2
) (if one of them is constant). Hence, the first term in the right-hand side of (
4.11
) is non-negative and can be thrown away. Letting then
n\rightarrow+\infty
in (
4.11
) and using Lebesgue convergence theorem, we deduce

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega_{2}}\langle\left[\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|}\nabla_{0}u_{2}-\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|}\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right],\nabla_{0}\phi\rangle

(4.13)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left[B(x,u_{2},\nabla_{0}u_{2})-B(x,u_{1},\nabla_{0}u_{1})\right]\phi

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left\{\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|}\langle\nabla_{0}u_{1},\nabla_{0}\phi\rangle+B(x,u_{1},\nabla_{0}u_{1})\phi\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left\{\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{2}\right|}\langle\nabla_{0}u_{2},\nabla_{0}\phi\rangle+B(x,u_{2},\nabla_{0}u_{2})\phi\right\}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{2}}\left\{\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u_{1}\right|}\langle\nabla_{0}u_{1},\nabla_{0}\phi\rangle+B(x,u_{1},\nabla_{0}u_{1})\phi\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left\{\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u\right|}\langle\nabla_{0}u,\nabla_{0}\phi\rangle+B(x,u,\nabla_{0}u)\phi\right\}\,.

Since
\phi
is an arbitrary test function, this concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The pasting lemma below is a useful refinement of the above result.

Proposition 4.1

(The pasting lemma)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{G}
be open domains, and suppose that
\varphi
satisfies (
1.8
). Let
u\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
u^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega^{\prime})
be such that

\begin{cases}\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}u\geq B(x,u,\nabla_{0}u)&\hbox{on $\Omega$}\\
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}u^{\prime}\geq B(x,u^{\prime},\nabla_{0}u^{\prime})&\hbox{on $\Omega^{\prime}$}\\
u^{\prime}(x)\geq u(x)&\hbox{on $\Omega^{\prime}\cap\partial\Omega$}\,.\end{cases}

(4.14)

Then, the function
v\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega^{\prime})
defined as

v=\begin{cases}\max\left\{u,u^{\prime}\right\}&\hbox{on $\Omega$}\\
u^{\prime}&\hbox{on $\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\Omega$}\,,\end{cases}

(4.15)

satisfies

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}v\geq B(x,v,\nabla_{0}v)\quad\quad\hbox{on $\Omega^{\prime}$}\,.

(4.16)

Proof.

We first suppose that
u^{\prime}(x)>u(x)
on
\Omega^{\prime}\cap\partial\Omega
. By continuity of
u
and
u^{\prime}
,
u^{\prime}(x)>u(x)
still holds on some open set
V\subset\Omega^{\prime}
containing
\Omega^{\prime}\cap\partial\Omega
. This implies that
v=u^{\prime}
on
U\doteq(\Omega^{\prime}\backslash\Omega)\cup V
and thus

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}v\geq B(x,v,\nabla_{0}v)\quad\quad\hbox{on $U$}\,.

(4.17)

From Lemma
4.1
it follows that

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}v\geq B(x,v,\nabla_{0}v)\quad\quad\hbox{on $\Omega$}\,.

(4.18)

Since
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega\cup U
the proposition is proved.

To deal with the general case, for
\varepsilon>0
we set
u_{\varepsilon}(x)\doteq u(x)-\varepsilon
, and we define

B_{\varepsilon}(x,t,X)=\min\left\{B(x,t,X),B(x,t-\varepsilon,X)\right\}.

(4.19)

Then, clearly

\begin{cases}\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}u_{\varepsilon}\geq B_{\varepsilon}(x,u_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{0}u_{\varepsilon})&\hbox{on $\Omega$}\\
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}u^{\prime}\geq B_{\varepsilon}(x,u^{\prime},\nabla_{0}u^{\prime})&\hbox{on $\Omega^{\prime}$}\\
u^{\prime}(x)>u_{\varepsilon}(x)&\hbox{on $\Omega^{\prime}\cap\partial\Omega$}\,.\end{cases}

(4.20)

By the first part of the proof, the function

v_{\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}\max\left\{u_{\varepsilon},u^{\prime}\right\}&\hbox{on $\Omega$}\\
u^{\prime}&\hbox{on $\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\Omega$}\,,\end{cases}

(4.21)

solves

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\varphi}v_{\varepsilon}\geq B_{\varepsilon}(x,v_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon})\quad\quad\hbox{on $\Omega^{\prime}$}\,.

(4.22)

Thus, for any test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})
,
\phi\geq 0
we have that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left\{\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon}\right|}\langle\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{0}\phi\rangle+B_{\varepsilon}(x,v_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon})\phi\right\}

(4.23)

To conclude, we need to check that the terms in (
4.23
) appropriately converge as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. By the definition of
B_{\varepsilon}
,
v_{\varepsilon},v
, the continuity of
B(x,\cdot,\cdot)
and Lebesgue theorem,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int\phi\left|B_{\varepsilon}(x,v_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon})-B(x,v,\nabla_{0}v)\right|\\[14.22636pt]
\quad\displaystyle\leq\int\phi\Big[\left|B_{\varepsilon}(x,v_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon})-B(x,v_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon})\right|+\left|B(x,v_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon})-B(x,v,\nabla_{0}v)\right|\Big]\\[14.22636pt]
\quad\displaystyle\leq\int\phi\left|B(x,v_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon,\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon})-B(x,v_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon})\right|\\[14.22636pt]
\qquad+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap\{u\in[u^{\prime},u^{\prime}+\varepsilon]\}}\phi\left|B(x,u^{\prime},\nabla_{0}u^{\prime})-B(x,u,\nabla_{0}u)\right|\\[14.22636pt]
\quad\displaystyle\rightarrow\int_{\Omega\cap\{u^{\prime}=u\}}\phi\left|B(x,u^{\prime},\nabla_{0}u^{\prime})-B(x,u,\nabla_{0}u)\right|=0,\end{array}

(4.24)

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, where the last equality follows from Stampacchia’s theorem. In a similar way,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int\left|\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon}\right|}\nabla_{0}v_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}v\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}v\right|}\nabla_{0}v\right||\nabla_{0}\phi|\\[14.22636pt]
\qquad\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\cap\{u\in[u^{\prime},u^{\prime}+\varepsilon]\}}\left|\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u^{\prime}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u^{\prime}\right|}\nabla_{0}u^{\prime}-\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u\right|}\nabla_{0}u\right||\nabla_{0}\phi|\\[14.22636pt]
\qquad\rightarrow\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap\{u^{\prime}=u\}}\left|\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u^{\prime}\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u^{\prime}\right|}\nabla_{0}u^{\prime}-\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}u\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}u\right|}\nabla_{0}u\right||\nabla_{0}\phi|=0.\end{array}

(4.25)

Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
in (
4.23
) we thus get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left\{\frac{\varphi(\left|\nabla_{0}v\right|)}{\left|\nabla_{0}v\right|}\langle\nabla_{0}v,\nabla_{0}\phi\rangle+B(x,v,\nabla_{0}v)\phi\right\},

(4.26)

which was to be proved.
∎

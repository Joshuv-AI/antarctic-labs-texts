Function spaces on formal manifolds
Fulin Chen, Binyong Sun, Chuyun Wang

Abstract
. This is a paper in a series that studies smooth relative Lie algebra homologies and cohomologies based on the theory of formal manifolds and formal Lie groups. In a previous paper, we introduce the notion of formal manifolds and develop the foundational framework of formal manifolds. In this paper, we study various function spaces on formal manifolds, including generalizations of vector-valued generalized functions and vector-valued distributions on smooth manifolds to the setting of formal manifolds.

Introduction and the main results

This is a sequel to

in a series to study smooth (co)homogogy theory for representations of general Lie pairs.

1.1.
Motivation

For the construction of Harish-Chandra modules and unitary representations,
authors are often interested in (co)homologies of representations of certain Lie pairs
(\mathfrak{q},L)
. Here
\mathfrak{q}
is a finite-dimensional complex Lie
algebra and
L
is a compact Lie group, with additional structures that satisfy specific compatibility conditions (see
[
KV
, (1.64)]
for details).
Motivated by the recent development in Lie group representations and automorphic forms, it is essential to establish a smooth
(co)homology theory for representations of
general Lie pairs
(\mathfrak{q},L)
, with
L
not necessarily compact. We refer to this theory as smooth relative Lie algebra (co)homology theory, which can be viewed as a generalization of the smooth (co)homology theory for Lie groups (see
[
HM
]
and
[
BW
]
) in a relative setting.

In
[
CSW
]
, we formulate and study a notion of what we call formal manifolds, inspired by the notion of formal schemes in algebraic geometry.
As one of the main results therein, we prove that finite products exist in the category of formal manifolds. Then we have a notion of formal Lie gorups, which are the group objects in the category of formal manifolds. We will prove in a subsequent paper that there is an equivalence of categories between the category of formal Lie groups and that of Lie pairs. Using this equivalence, we will establish the smooth relative Lie algebra (co)homology theory by studying (co)homologies of representations of formal Lie groups.

It is well-known that smooth representations of Lie groups are often
realized as various function spaces.
For example, the maximal globalization of a Harish-Chandra module for a real reductive group
G

can be realized by using the space
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(G)
or
{\mathrm{C}}^{-\infty}(G)
,
and the minimal globalization can be realized by using the space
{\mathrm{D}}^{\infty}_{c}(G)
or
{\mathrm{D}}^{-\infty}_{c}(G)
(see
[
KS
,
Schm
]
). Here and below, for a smooth manifold
N
, we use the notations

(1.1)

{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N),\quad{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(N),\quad{\mathrm{C}}^{-\infty}(N),\quad{\mathrm{D}}_{c}^{\infty}(N),\quad{\mathrm{D}}_{c}^{-\infty}(N)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathrm{D}}^{-\infty}(N)

to denote the spaces of (complex-valued) smooth functions, compactly supported smooth functions, generalized functions, compactly supported smooth densities, compactly supported distributions and distributions on
N
, respectively.
On the other hand, in the smooth (co)homology theory of Lie groups, the standard (projective or injective) resolutions of a smooth representation
V

can be constructed by using the spaces of suitable
V
-valued smooth functions, distributions or generalized functions (see
[
HM
,
BW
,
KS
]
).

Thus, to establish the smooth (co)homology theory for general Lie pairs (or equivalently, formal Lie groups), it is desirable
to generalize Schwartz’s (vector-valued) distribution theory (see
[
Sc2
,
Sc3
,
Sc4
]
) to the formal manifold setting.

1.2.
Notations and conventions

Before presenting the main results of this paper, we provide some notations and conventions used throughout this paper.
For every smooth manifold
N
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, denote by
N^{(k)}
the locally ringed space
(N,{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)})
over
\mathrm{Spec}({\mathbb{C}})
, where

{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)}(U):={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]\quad(U\ \text{is an open subset of}\ N).

Recall from
[
CSW
]
that a formal manifold is a locally ringed space
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
over
\mathrm{Spec}({\mathbb{C}})
such that

•

the topological space
M
is paracompact and Hausdorff; and

•

for every
a\in M
, there is an open neighborhood
U
of
a
in
M
and
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
(U,{\mathcal{O}}|_{U})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{(k)}
as locally ringed spaces over
\mathrm{Spec}({\mathbb{C}})
.

For every
a\in M
, the numbers
n
and
k
are respectively called the dimension and the degree of
M
at
a
, denoted by
\dim_{a}(M)
and
\deg_{a}(M)
. We denote by
\pi_{0}(M)
the set of all connected components in
M
, which may or may not be countable. An element in
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is called a formal function on
M
.

In this paper, by an LCS,
we mean a
locally convex topological vector
space over
\mathbb{C}
, which may or may not be Hausdorff.
However, a complete (or quasi-complete) LCS is always assumed to be Hausdorff.
All function spaces in (
1.1
) are equipped with the usual topologies so that they are naturally complete LCS. Additionally, we equip the spaces of formal power series and polynomials with the
term-wise convergence topology and the usual inductive limit topology, respectively.

For two LCS
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, let
{\mathcal{L}}(E_{1},E_{2})
denote the space of all continuous linear maps from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
. When
E_{2}=\mathbb{C}
, we also set
E_{1}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{L}}(E_{1},E_{2})
.
Unless otherwise mentioned,
{\mathcal{L}}(E_{1},E_{2})
is equipped with the strong topology.

As stated in
[
G
]
, there are three useful topological tensor products on LCS:
the inductive tensor product
\otimes_{\mathrm{i}}
, the projective tensor product
\otimes_{\pi}
, and the epsilon tensor product
\otimes_{\varepsilon}
.
We denote the quasi-completions and completions of these topological tensor products by

\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}},\,\,\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi},\,\,\widetilde{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}},\,\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi},\,\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon},

respectively.
Some basics on these topological tensor products are reviewed in Appendixes

and
B
.

Throughout this paper, let
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
be a formal manifold,
and let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules. Additionally, except in the appendix, let
E
be a quasi-complete LCS.
For every open subset
U
of
M
, we equip
{\mathcal{F}}(U)
with the smooth topology (see
[
CSW
, Definition 4.1]
). When
M=N^{(k)}
with
N
a smooth manifold and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have the LCS identifications (see Example

)

{\mathcal{O}}(M)={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathbb{C}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathbb{C}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]].

2.1.
The cosheaf

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be open subsets of

. Then
the transpose

{}^{t}\mathrm{res}_{V,U}:({\mathcal{F}}(V))^{\prime}\rightarrow({\mathcal{F}}(U))^{\prime}

\mathrm{ext}_{U,V}:={}^{t}\mathrm{res}_{V,U}|_{\mathrm{D}^{\infty}_{c}(V;{\mathcal{F}})}:\quad\mathrm{D}^{\infty}_{c}(V;{\mathcal{F}})\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{\infty}_{c}(U;{\mathcal{F}}).

Proof.

Let
D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{c}({\mathcal{F}}|_{V},\underline{\mathcal{D}}|_{V})
. Write

\mathrm{ext}_{U,V}:\mathrm{Diff}_{c}({\mathcal{F}}|_{V},\underline{\mathcal{D}}|_{V})\rightarrow\mathrm{Diff}_{c}({\mathcal{F}}|_{U},\underline{\mathcal{D}}|_{U})

for the extension map in
\mathcal{D}\mathrm{iff}_{c}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{\mathcal{D}})

Then

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{U}(\mathrm{ext}_{U,V}(D)),u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U}\mathrm{ext}_{U,V}(D)(u)=\int_{V}D(u|_{V})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{V}(D),u|_{V}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{}^{t}\mathrm{res}_{V,U}(\rho_{V}(D)),u\rangle

for every
u\in{\mathcal{F}}(U)
. It follows that
the map (

) is well-defined and satisfies the relation

(2.5)

\rho_{U}(\mathrm{ext}_{U,V}(D))=\mathrm{ext}_{U,V}(\rho_{V}(D)).

To see the injectivity of (

Take an open neighborhood

\displaystyle=

Since

Let

be a

This yields an element

Then we have that

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{1},u_{1}\rangle=\langle\rho_{U_{1}}(D_{1}),u_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U_{1}}D_{1}(u_{1})=\int_{U^{\prime}}(D_{1}(u_{1}))|_{U^{\prime}}\,\,\,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad(\text{as $\mathrm{supp}\,D_{1}(u_{1})\subset K_{1}\subset U^{\prime}$})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U}(\mathrm{ext}_{U,U_{1}}(D_{1}))(fu_{1})\,\,\,\,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad(\text{as $(fu_{1})|_{U^{\prime}}=u_{1}|_{U^{\prime}}$})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{U}(\mathrm{ext}_{U,U_{1}}(D_{1})),fu_{1}\rangle\quad

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{U}(\mathrm{ext}_{U,U_{1}}(D_{1})+\mathrm{ext}_{U,U_{2}}(D_{2})),fu_{1}\rangle\quad\,\quad(\text{as $\mathrm{supp}\,(fu_{1})\cap\mathrm{supp}\,D_{2}=\emptyset$})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\phi((\rho_{U}(D_{1}),\rho_{U}(D_{2}))),fu_{1}\rangle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\,(\text{by \eqref{eq:cosheafhom}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\phi(\eta),fu_{1}\rangle=0,

which proves the lemma.
∎

Set

. For each

, take an

of

Lemma 2.5
.

We have that
\eta^{\prime}_{1}+\eta^{\prime}_{2}=0
, and
\eta_{i}=\mathrm{ext}_{U_{i},V}(\eta^{\prime}_{i})
for

Proof.

For every

Together with the fact that

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{i},u_{i}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{i},(g_{i}+g_{i}^{\prime})u_{i}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\eta_{i},g_{i}u_{i}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U_{i}}D_{i}(g_{i}u_{i}^{\prime})=\int_{V}D_{i}^{\prime}(u_{i}^{\prime}|_{V})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{\prime}_{i},u_{i}^{\prime}|_{V}\rangle=\langle\mathrm{ext}_{U_{i},V}(\eta^{\prime}_{i}),u_{i}^{\prime}\rangle.

2.2.
The topology on

In this subsection, we define an

[

Let
X\in\mathrm{Diff}_{c}(\underline{\mathcal{D}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})

For every

\lvert D\rvert_{X,u}:=\sup_{a\in M}|((X\circ D)(u))(a)|.

\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{X,u}

\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{fin}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{\mathcal{D}})

\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{fin}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{\mathcal{D}})

\underline{\mathcal{D}}(M)

Lemma 2.8
.

A net
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}

\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{fin}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{\mathcal{D}})

converges to
0
if and only if

0
in
\underline{\mathcal{D}}(M)
for all

on
\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{fin}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{\mathcal{D}})
is Hausdorff.

There is a directed set
(\mathcal{C}(M),\preceq)
, where

(2.10)

\mathcal{C}(M):=\{(K,r)\,:\,\text{$K$ is a compact subset of $M$ and $r\in{\mathbb{N}}$}\},

and for two pairs

(K,r)\preceq(K^{\prime},r^{\prime})
if and only if
K\subset K^{\prime}
and

For

Write

\mathrm{Diff}_{K,r}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{\mathcal{D}})

Then
\mathrm{D}^{\infty}_{K,r}(M;{\mathcal{F}})
becomes an LCS.
Equip

\mathrm{D}^{\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})=\varinjlim_{(K,r)\in\mathcal{C}(M)}\mathrm{D}^{\infty}_{K,r}(M;{\mathcal{F}})

For every

Proof.

is closed in

for every

\mathrm{D}^{\infty}_{K,r}(M;{\mathcal{F}})

By definition, we have that

(2.12)

\mathrm{D}^{\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})=\varinjlim_{(K,r)\in\mathcal{C}(M)}\mathrm{D}^{\infty}_{K,r}(M;{\mathcal{F}})=\bigoplus_{Z\in\pi_{0}(M)}\varinjlim_{(K,r)\in\mathcal{C}(Z)}\mathrm{D}^{\infty}_{K,r}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})

Proposition 2.17
.

Suppose that

as above. Then

Proof.

).

3.2.

Let
N
be a smooth manifold.
As usual, we write

Recall from

We have the following generalization in the setting of formal manifolds.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{F}}
is locally free of finite rank.
Then

•

\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}})
is a complete nuclear reflexive LCS, and
\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)

•

•

\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E=\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)=\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E

as LCS.

Proof.

When

is secondly countable, the proposition is implied by Corollaries
B.9
,
2.22
, Proposition
2.20
, and Lemma
B.8
.
In general, the first assertion follows from (
3.2
) and the fact that a product of nuclear (resp. reflexive; complete; quasi-complete) LCS is still a nuclear (resp. reflexive; complete; quasi-complete) LCS.
For the second one, by (
3.2
) and

[
CSW
, Lemma 6.6]
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E

\displaystyle\prod_{Z\in\pi_{0}(M)}\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E

\displaystyle\prod_{Z\in\pi_{0}(M)}\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(Z;{\mathcal{F}};E)

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E).

Similarly, when

is complete, we also obtain from (

) and

[

, Lemma 6.6]
that

\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)=\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E.

∎

When

From (

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{O}})

\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K\cap\mathrm{supp}\,f}(U;{\mathcal{F}};E),\quad\eta\mapsto(\eta\circ f)|_{U}

is well-defined and continuous. Here

is defined as in (

).

Proof.

Let
B
be a bounded subset of
{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(U)
, and let
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu}
be a continuous seminorm on

.
Set

.

|((\eta\circ f)|_{U})|_{B,\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu}}=|\eta|_{B^{\prime},\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu}},

Endow

with the inductive limit topology. Here compact subsets of

The following result is straightforward (

Lemma 5.3
.

For every open subset

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
M
,
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
a finite cover of

, and

\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}

\mathrm{supp}\,f_{i}\subset U_{i}\,\,\text{ for $1\leq i\leq s$}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{s}f_{i}|_{K}=1.

Write

Then the linear map

(5.2)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K_{i}}(U_{i};{\mathcal{F}};E)&\rightarrow&\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E),\\

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)&\rightarrow&\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K_{i}}(U_{i};{\mathcal{F}};E),\\

Proof.

5.2.
Structure of
{\mathrm{D}}_{c}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)

For a smooth manifold
N
, write

\mathrm{D}_{c}^{-\infty}(N;E)
for the LCS of compactly supported
E
-valued distributions
on
N
, and write

\mathrm{D}_{c}^{-\infty}(N):=\mathrm{D}_{c}^{-\infty}(N;\mathbb{C}).

It is known that if
E
is a DF space (see
[
K1
, § 29.3]
), then for every

,

(5.5)

\mathrm{D}_{c}^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\widetilde{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}_{c}^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\widehat{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}_{c}^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};E)={\mathcal{L}}_{b}({\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),E)

The main goal of this subsection is to generalize

We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 5.7
.

The space
{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M)
is dense in
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
.

Proof.

For every
Z\in\pi_{0}(M)
, choose a sequence
\{U_{r,Z}\}_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of relatively compact open subsets in
Z
such that

U_{0,Z}\subset\overline{U_{0,Z}}\subset U_{1,Z}\subset\overline{U_{1,Z}}\subset U_{2,Z}\subset\overline{U_{2,Z}}\subset\cdots

and

\cup_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}U_{r,Z}=Z
.
Let
\Gamma
be the directed set of all open subsets in
M
that have the form

(5.6)

U_{j_{1},Z_{1}}\cup U_{j_{2},Z_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup U_{j_{r},Z_{r}}\quad(j_{1},\dots,j_{r}>0,\,Z_{1},\dots,Z_{r}\in\pi_{0}(M)),

which are directed by inclusions.
For every
U\in\Gamma
as in (
5.6
), take a formal function
f_{U}
on
M
such that

\mathrm{supp}\,f_{U}\subset U\quad\text{and}\quad f_{U}|_{\overline{U_{j_{1}-1,Z_{1}}}\cup\overline{U_{j_{2}-1,Z_{2}}}\cup\cdots\cup\overline{U_{j_{r}-1,Z_{r}}}}=1.

The assertion then follows by the fact that for every
u\in{\mathcal{F}}(M)
,
the net
\{f_{U}u\}_{U\in\Gamma}
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M)
converges to
u
.
∎

By Lemma
5.7
, the transpose

(5.7)

{\mathcal{L}}_{b}({\mathcal{F}}(M),E)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)={\mathcal{L}}_{b}({\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M),E)

of the inclusion
{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is injective.

Lemma 5.8
.

The space
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
is contained in the image of (
5.7
).

Proof.

Let
\eta
be an element in
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
, where
K
is a compact subset of
M
.
Extend
\eta
to a linear map
\eta^{\prime}:{\mathcal{F}}(M)\rightarrow E
by setting

\langle\eta^{\prime},u\rangle:=\langle\eta,fu\rangle\quad\text{for all}\ u\in{\mathcal{F}}(M),

where
f
is a compactly supported formal function on
M
such that
f|_{K}=1
. We only need to show that
\eta^{\prime}
is continuous.

Let
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu}
be a continuous seminorm on
E
.
Since

\eta|_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{supp}\,f}(M)}:{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{supp}\,f}(M)\rightarrow E

is continuous,
there is a
D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{c}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})
such that

|\langle\eta,v\rangle|_{\nu}\leq|v|_{D}\quad\text{for all}\ v\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{supp}\,f}(M).

Using this, we find that

|\langle\eta^{\prime},u\rangle|_{\nu}=|\langle\eta,fu\rangle|_{\nu}\leq|fu|_{D}=|u|_{D\circ f}\quad\text{for all}\ u\in{\mathcal{F}}(M).

Here
D\circ f\in\mathrm{Diff}_{c}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})
is as in (
2.1
).
Thus
\eta^{\prime}
is contained in
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}(M),E)
, as required.
∎

In general, the space
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
may not be the image of (
5.7
) (even when
M
is reduced).
However, we still have the following partial result.
Recall from
[
K2
, § 40.2]
that a linear map
\phi:E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}
of LCS is called bounded if
there is a neighborhood
U
of
0
in
E_{1}
such that
\phi(U)
is bounded in
E_{2}
.

Lemma 5.9
.

If every element in
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}(M),E)
is bounded, then
the image of (
5.7
) is exactly
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be an element in
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}(M),E)
. Since
\phi
is bounded,
there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
\{D_{i}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{c}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that the set

B:=\left\{\langle\phi,v\rangle\,:\,v\in{\mathcal{F}}(M)\ \text{with}\ \sum_{1\leq i\leq n}|v|_{D_{i}}<\varepsilon\right\}

is bounded in
E
.
Let
u
be an element in
{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M)
supported in
M\setminus(\cup_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathrm{supp}\,D_{i})
.
Then
\langle\phi,\lambda u\rangle
lies in
B
for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
This forces that
\langle\phi,u\rangle=0
, and so
\phi
is supported in
\cup_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathrm{supp}\,D_{i}
, as required.
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

When
E=\mathbb{C}
, the image of (
5.7
) is exactly
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})
.

It is known that every continuous linear map from a Fréchet (resp. DF) space to a DF (resp. Fréchet) space is bounded (see
[
Sc3
, Page 62, footnote (2)]
and
[

, § 40.2 (9)]
).

Corollary 5.11
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is a Fréchet (resp. DF) space and

is a DF (resp. Fréchet) space. Then the image of (

\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)

implies that there is a natural injective linear map

The following result implies that (

is
Hausdorff.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of

.
If

is

then the injective linear map

\iota_{E}|_{\mathrm{D}_{K}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)}:\ \mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}_{b}({\mathcal{F}}(M),E)

induced by (

Proof.

Let
B
be a bounded subset of
{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M)
and let
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu}
be a continuous seminorm on
E
.
Since
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is Hausdorff, it follows from Lemma
4.3
that

B
is also bounded in
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
. Equip

\iota_{E}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E))\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{b}({\mathcal{F}}(M),E)

with the subspace topology.
Then viewing as a seminorm on

(see (

))
is still continuous.
This shows that the linear map

\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)\rightarrow\iota_{E}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E))

is open.

On the other hand, let
B^{\prime}
be a bounded subset of
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
and let
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu}
be a continuous seminorm on

.
Take a compactly supported formal function

on

such that

.
Then we have a bounded subset

fB^{\prime}:=\{fu\,:\,u\in B^{\prime}\}

of
{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M)

When

says that the map

Lemma 5.13
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is barreled
and
{\mathcal{F}}(M)_{b}^{\prime}
is bornological.
Then the continuous map

\iota_{\mathbb{C}}:\quad\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}(M)_{b}^{\prime}

is a topological linear isomorphism.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.10
and Lemma
5.12
, it suffices to prove that

{\mathcal{F}}(M)^{\prime}_{b}=\varinjlim_{K}\iota_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}}))

as LCS, where
\iota_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}}))
is equipped with the subspace topology.
This is equivalent to prove that for every seminorm
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert
on
{\mathcal{F}}(M)^{\prime}_{b}
, if its
restriction on
\iota_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}}))
is continuous for all compact subsets
K
of
M
, then
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert
is continuous on
{\mathcal{F}}(M)^{\prime}_{b}
.

Let
B
be a bounded subset in
{\mathcal{F}}(M)^{\prime}_{b}
.
Since
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is barreled,
B
is an equicontinuous set in
{\mathcal{F}}(M)^{\prime}_{b}
(see
[
T
, Theorem 33.2]
).
Thus there exist
D_{1},D_{2},\dots,D_{n}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{c}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
v\in{\mathcal{F}}(M)
, if
|v|_{D_{i}}<\varepsilon
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, then

|\langle\phi,v\rangle|\leq 1\quad\text{for all}\ \phi\in B.

Let
\phi\in B
, and let
u
be an element in
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
supported in
M\setminus\cup_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathrm{supp}\,D_{i}
.
Then we have that
|\langle\phi,\lambda u\rangle|\leq 1
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.
This implies that
\langle\phi,u\rangle=0
, and hence

\phi\in\iota_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{\cup_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathrm{supp}\,D_{i}}(M;{\mathcal{F}})).

Due to the assumption that
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert
is continuous on
\iota_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{\cup_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathrm{supp}\,D_{i}}(M;{\mathcal{F}}))
,
we see that
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert
sends
B
into a bounded set.
This, together with the fact that
{\mathcal{F}}(M)^{\prime}_{b}
is bornological, implies that
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert
is continuous on
{\mathcal{F}}(M)^{\prime}_{b}
, as required.
∎

Proposition 5.17
.

with

Let

. It is known that

Proposition 5.18
.

Assume that
M
is secondly countable, and
{\mathcal{F}}
is locally free of finite rank. Then

{\mathcal{F}}(M)_{b}^{\prime}\widetilde{\otimes}E={\mathcal{L}}_{b}({\mathcal{F}}(M),E)

as
LCS. And, if
E
is complete, then

{\mathcal{F}}(M)_{b}^{\prime}\widetilde{\otimes}E={\mathcal{L}}_{b}({\mathcal{F}}(M),E)={\mathcal{F}}(M)_{b}^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}E

as LCS.

Proof.

Note that in this case,
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is a nuclear Fréchet space (see
[
CSW
, Corollary 4.7]
).
By Corollary
B.9
, it suffices to prove that
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
has the strict approximation property.

Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a cover of
M
by relatively compact open subsets of
M
such that for every
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\overline{U_{i}}\subset U_{i+1}
.
Let
f_{i}
be a formal function supported in
U_{i+1}
with
f_{i}|_{\overline{U_{i}}}=1
.
Set

m_{f_{i}}:\ {\mathcal{F}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M),\quad u\mapsto f_{i}u,

which is a continuous linear map from
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
to
{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M)
.
Identify
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}(M),{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M))
as a subspace of
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}(M),{\mathcal{F}}(M))
through the inclusion
{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M)\subset{\mathcal{F}}(M)
.
Then it is obvious that the sequence

\{m_{f_{i}}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}

converges to the identity operator in
{\mathcal{L}}_{b}({\mathcal{F}}(M),{\mathcal{F}}(M))
.
This, together with the fact that
{\mathcal{F}}_{c}(M)
has the strict approximation property (see Lemma

), implies that
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
also has
the strict approximation property (see
[
Sc3
, Préliminaires, Proposition 2]
).
∎

Also, we have the following result, which generalizes the identification (
5.5
).

Proposition 5.19
.

Assume that
M
is secondly countable,
{\mathcal{F}}
is locally free of finite rank, and

E
is a DF space.
Then
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
is a complete DF space, and the following LCS identifications hold:

(5.11)

\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)={\mathcal{L}}_{b}({\mathcal{F}}(M),E).

Proof.

Recall that for every compact subset
K
of
M
,
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
is closed in
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
(see Lemma
5.1
).
It then follows from Proposition
4.14
that
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
is a complete LCS.
By Lemma
A.3
(d),

\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
is also a complete LCS.

Note that

\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E

by Proposition
4.14
.
This implies that

\varinjlim_{K}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E)=\varinjlim_{K}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E)=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)

as LCS. Consequently, the inductive limit

\varinjlim_{K}(\mathrm{D}_{K}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E)
is quasi-complete, and the inductive limit

\varinjlim_{K}(\mathrm{D}_{K}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E)

is complete.
Then it follows from Lemma
A.6

that the canonical maps

\varinjlim_{K}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E)\rightarrow(\varinjlim_{K}\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}}))\widetilde{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E

and

\varinjlim_{K}(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E)\rightarrow(\varinjlim_{K}\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{K}(M;{\mathcal{F}}))\widehat{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E

are both topological linear isomorphisms. Thus

\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)

as LCS.
Since the completed projective tensor product of two DF spaces is still a DF space (see
[
K2
, § 41.4 (7)]
),

\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
is a complete DF space by Corollary
5.15
.

By using
Propositions
5.14
and
5.18
, we conclude that

\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E={\mathcal{F}}(M)_{b}^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}E={\mathcal{L}}_{b}({\mathcal{F}}(M),E)

as LCS. This completes the proof.
∎

Without assuming second countability in Proposition

,
we still have the following result by replacing the
projective tensor products by inductive tensor products.

Proposition 5.20
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}
is locally free of finite rank, and
E
is a barreled DF space.
Then
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)
is a direct sum of complete barreled DF spaces, and

\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E

as LCS.

Proof.

Recall that for two complete barreled DF spaces, their completed projective tensor product is a complete barreled DF space, and coincides with their completed inductive tensor product (see
[
K2
, § 40.2 (11) and § 41.4 (7)]
). Then
for every
Z\in\pi_{0}(M)
, it follows from Proposition

5.19
and Lemma
A.10
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}};E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E,

which are all complete barreled DF spaces.
This, together with Corollary
5.6

and (
A.5
), shows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E=\left(\bigoplus_{Z\in\pi_{0}(M)}\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})\right)\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E=\bigoplus_{Z\in\pi_{0}(M)}\left(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{Z\in\pi_{0}(M)}\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}};E)=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}};E)=\bigoplus_{Z\in\pi_{0}(M)}\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}};E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{Z\in\pi_{0}(M)}\left(\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E\right)=\left(\bigoplus_{Z\in\pi_{0}(M)}\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{c}(M;{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E,

as required.
∎

5.3.
Formal distributions supported at a point

Let
a\in M
. Write

\mathrm{Dist}_{a}(M):=\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}_{\{a\}}(M;{\mathcal{O}}),

which is equipped with
the subspace topology of
\mathrm{D}^{-\infty}(M;{\mathcal{O}})
.
On the other hand, we equip the space of polynomials with the usual
inductive limit topology (see Example
B.2
).

The main goal of this subsection is to prove the following description of the LCS
\mathrm{Dist}_{a}(M)
(
cf
.
[

, Theorem 26.4]
).

Proposition 5.21
.

For every
a\in M
, we have that

\mathrm{Dist}_{a}(M)\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*},\dots,x_{n}^{*},y_{1}^{*},y_{2}^{*},\dots,y_{k}^{*}]

as LCS, where

and
k:=\deg_{a}(M)
.

In view of Proposition
5.14
,
we identify
\mathrm{Dist}_{a}(M)

as a subspace of
({\mathcal{O}}(M))_{b}^{\prime}
.
Recall the continuous character
\mathrm{Ev}_{a}\in({\mathcal{O}}(M))_{b}^{\prime}
defined in
[
CSW
, (5.1)]
.

Example 5.22
.

Let
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}

suppose that
M=N^{(k)}
for some open submanifold

of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

\{\mathrm{Ev}_{a}\circ(\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{y}^{J})\}_{I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n},J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}

forms a basis of the complex-vector space
\mathrm{Dist}_{a}(M)
.

The rest of this subsection is devoted to a proof of
Proposition
5.21
.
We endow the stalk

(5.12)

{\mathcal{O}}_{a}:=\varinjlim_{U}{\mathcal{O}}(U)

with the inductive limit topology, where
U
runs over all open neighborhoods of

in
M
.
Then
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}
becomes an LCS that may not be Hausdorff.

Note that every

gives rise to a seminorm

\displaystyle|\cdot|_{D,a}:\ {\mathcal{O}}_{a}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\quad g\mapsto|D_{a}(g)(a)|

on

, (3.18)]

Lemma 5.23
.

Let
a\in M
. The topology on
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}
is defined by the family

of seminorms. Moreover, the linear map

{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{a}

Proof.

The family (

) of seminorms defines another locally convex topology on the underlying vector space of

.

.

The intersection

in

In view of Lemma

.

Appendix A
Preliminaries on topological tensor products

This appendix provides an overview of topological tensor products of LCS, along with related results necessary for the paper.

A.1.
Topological tensor products of LCS

Let
E
and
F
be two LCS. As discussed in
[
G
]
, the algebraic tensor product
E\otimes F
admits at least three valuable locally convex topologies: the inductive tensor product
E\otimes_{\mathrm{i}}F
, the projective tensor product
E\otimes_{\pi}F
, and the epsilon (injective) tensor product
E\otimes_{\varepsilon}F
. Explicit definitions can be found in
[
G
]
.
In this context, as mentioned in the Introduction, we denote the quasi-completions and completions of these topological tensor product spaces as follows:

\text{$E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F$,
$E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F$, $E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F$\quad and\quad$E\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F$,
$E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F$, $E\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F$}.

When
E
or
F
is nuclear, it is a classical result of Grothendieck that their projective tensor product coincides with the epsilon tensor product (see
[
T
, Theorem 50.1]
).
In this case, we
will simply write

E\widetilde{\otimes}F:=E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F=E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F\quad\text{and}\quad E\widehat{\otimes}F:=E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F=E\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F.

Given two continuous linear maps
\phi_{1}:E_{1}\rightarrow F_{1}
and
\phi_{2}:E_{2}\rightarrow F_{2}
between LCS, we use
\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}
to denote the continuous linear map on various topological tensor products (or their quasi-completions or completions) obtained by the tensor product of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
. For example, we have the maps

\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}:\ E_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{2}\rightarrow F_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}:\ E_{1}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E_{2}\rightarrow F_{1}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F_{2}.

A.2.
Inductive limits of LCS

For a directed set
(I,\leq)
and
a directed system

\{E_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of LCS, the inductive limit
\varinjlim_{i\in I}E_{i}
exists and is unique in the category of LCS. It is topologically linearly isomorphic to the LCS

\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}E_{i}\right)/\left(\sum_{i,j\in I;i\leq j}\{v_{i}-f_{ij}(v_{i}):v_{i}\in E_{i}\}\right),

which is equipped with the quotient topology. See
[
J
, Page 110]
for details.

Definition A.1
.

An inductive limit

\varinjlim_{i\in I}E_{i}
is said to be strict if
for every
i\leq j
, the canonical map
E_{i}\rightarrow E_{j}
is a linear topological embedding.
Furthermore, an inductive limit

\varinjlim_{i\in I}E_{i}
is said to be regular if it is strict, and
every bounded subset of it is contained and bounded in
some
E_{i}
.

We remark that when an inductive limit
\varinjlim_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}E_{i}
is strict, the map

E_{j}\rightarrow\varinjlim_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}E_{i}

is a linear topological embedding for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
(see
[
Bo
, § 4.6 Proposition 9 (i)]
).

Definition A.2
.

An LCS is said to be an LF space if it is isomorphic to a strict inductive limit of the form

\varinjlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}E_{r}
,
where
E_{r}
is a Fréchet space for every
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

The inductive limits of LCS considered in this paper often have the following form:

(A.1)

E=\bigoplus_{i\in I}E_{i}\quad(E_{i}=\varinjlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}E_{i,r}\ \text{is a strict inductive limit of LCS}).

Here
E
is regarded as the inductive limit of the family
\{\bigoplus_{j\in J}E_{j,r}\}_{J\in\mathfrak{F},r\in{\mathbb{N}}}
consisting of finite partial sums, where
\mathfrak{F}
is the set of all finite subsets of
I
.
In the subsequent lemma, we collect some basic properties about these inductive limits.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
E
be an LCS as in (
A.1
).

(a) Let
J
be a subset of
I
, and
r_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
for all
j\in J
. Then the subspace topology on the partial sum
\bigoplus_{j\in J}E_{j,r_{j}}
induced by
E
coincides with its direct sum topology.

(b) If every
E_{i,r}
is Hausdorff, then
E
is Hausdorff.

(c) If every
E_{i,r}
is Hausdorff and closed in
E_{i,r+1}
, then the inductive
limit

E=\varinjlim_{J\in\mathfrak{F},r\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left(\bigoplus_{j\in J}E_{j,r}\right)

is regular, and

E_{i,r}
is closed in
E
.

(d) If every
E_{i,r}
is complete, then
E
is complete.

(e) If every
E_{i,r}
is quasi-complete and closed in
E_{i,r+1}
, then

E
is quasi-complete.

Proof.

The assertion (a) follows from
[
Bo
, Chapitre II, § 4.5 Proposition 8 (i) and § 4.6 Proposition 9 (i)]
.
The assertion (b) is a direct consequence of
[
Bo
, Chapitre II, § 4.5 Corolliare 2 and § 4.6 Proposition 9 (i)]
.
The assertion (c) is a consequence of
[
Bo
, Chapitre II, § 4.5 Corollaire 2 and § 4.6 Proposition 9 (ii)]
and
[
Bo
, Chapitre III, § 1.4 Propositions 5 and 6]
.
The assertion (d) follows from (b),
[
Bo
, Chapitre III, § 3.6 Corolliare 2 and Chapitre II, § 4.6 Proposition 9 (iii)]
.
The assertion (e) is a consequence of (b),
[
Bo
, Chapitre III, § 1.6 Proposition 9 (iii) and (iv)]
.
∎

For two LCS
E=\varinjlim_{i\in I}E_{i}
and
F=\varinjlim_{j\in J}F_{j}
, it is easy to check that

(A.2)

E\otimes_{\mathrm{i}}F=\varinjlim_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\otimes_{\mathrm{i}}F_{j})

as
LCS by using the universal property. Consequently, we have the following result (
cf
.
[
G
, Chapitre I, Proposition 14]
).

Lemma A.4
.

Let
E=\varinjlim_{i\in I}E_{i}
and
F=\varinjlim_{j\in J}F_{j}
be inductive limits of LCS. If the
inductive limit
\varinjlim_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F_{j})

is
quasi-complete, then

(A.3)

E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F=\varinjlim_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F_{j})

as LCS.
Similarly, if the inductive limit
\varinjlim_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F_{j})
is complete, then

(A.4)

E\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F=\varinjlim_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F_{j})

as LCS.

The following lemma is a special case of Lemma
A.4
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let

E=\bigoplus_{i\in I}E_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad F=\bigoplus_{j\in J}F_{j}

be both direct sums of Hausdorff LCS. Then

(A.5)

E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F=\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F_{j})\quad\text{and}\quad E\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F=\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F_{j})

as LCS.

Recall the notion of DF spaces introduced by Grothendieck (see
[
K1
, § 29.3]
).
Note that a DF space is quasi-complete if and only if it is complete (see
[
K1
, §29.5 (3) a]
).
Let
F
be a DF space, and let
E=\varinjlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}E_{r}
be an inductive limit of Hausdorff LCS,
it is proved in
[
G
, Chapitre I, Proposition 6 (2)]
that if
E
and
\varinjlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}(E_{r}\otimes_{\pi}F)
are Hausdorff, then

(A.6)

E\otimes_{\pi}F=\varinjlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}(E_{r}\otimes_{\pi}F)

as LCS. Consequently, we have the following result.

Lemma A.6
.

Let the notation be as above. If the inductive limit

\varinjlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}(E_{r}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F)
is quasi-complete, then

(A.7)

E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F=\varinjlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}(E_{r}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F)

as LCS. Similarly, if the inductive limit
\varinjlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}(E_{r}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F)
is complete, then

(A.8)

E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F=\varinjlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}(E_{r}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F)

as LCS.

A.3.
Strong dual of LCS

Let
E
and
F
be two LCS. As in the Introduction, we use the notation
{\mathcal{L}}(E,F)
to denote the space of all continuous linear maps from
E
to
F
.
For a continuous linear map
f:E\rightarrow G
,
let

(A.9)

{}^{t}f:\ {\mathcal{L}}(G,F)\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(E,F),\quad u^{\prime}\mapsto(v\mapsto\langle u^{\prime},f(v)\rangle)

denote the transpose of
f
, where
G
is another LCS.

Unless otherwise mentioned,
{\mathcal{L}}(E,F)
is endowed with the strong topology, and the resulting LCS is denoted
by
{\mathcal{L}}_{b}(E,F)
.
Let
B
be a bounded set of
E
, and let
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu}
be a continuous seminorm on
F
. Then

(A.10)

\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{B,\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu}}:\ u^{\prime}\mapsto\sup_{v\in B}|\langle u^{\prime},v\rangle|_{\nu}\qquad(u^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}(E,F))

is a continuous seminorm on
{\mathcal{L}}_{b}(E,F)
, and the topology on
{\mathcal{L}}_{b}(E,F)
is defined by all such seminorms.
When
F=\mathbb{C}
, we also write

E^{\prime}:={\mathcal{L}}(E,\mathbb{C}),\quad E^{\prime}_{b}:={\mathcal{L}}_{b}(E,\mathbb{C})\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{B}:=\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{B,\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert}.

Lemma A.7
.

The strong dual of a metrizable LCS is a complete DF space (see
[
K1
, § 29.3]
), and the strong dual of a DF space is a
Fréchet space (see
[
K1
, § 29.3 (1)]
).

The following result is about the strong dual of a product and a direct sum of LCS,
whose proof can be found in
[
Bo
, Chapitre IV, § 1.5]
for example.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of Hausdorff LCS. Then

(A.11)

\left(\prod_{i\in I}E_{i}\right)_{b}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}(E_{i})_{b}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}E_{i}\right)_{b}^{\prime}=\prod_{i\in I}(E_{i})_{b}^{\prime}

as LCS.

As an abstract vector space, the continuous dual of an inductive limit
F=\varinjlim_{j\in J}F_{j}
of Hausdorff LCS is canonically
isomorphic to the projective limit
\varprojlim_{j\in J}F_{j}^{\prime}
(see
[
J
, § 8.8, Corollary 11]
). This isomorphism may not be a topological linear isomorphism in general
(under the strong topologies). However,
when the inductive limit
F=\varinjlim_{j\in J}F_{j}
is regular, one immediately has the following result.

Lemma A.9
.

If
F=\varinjlim_{j\in J}F_{j}
is a regular inductive limit of Hausdorff LCS, then

(A.12)

\left(\varinjlim_{j\in J}F_{j}\right)_{b}^{\prime}=\varprojlim_{j\in J}(F_{j})_{b}^{\prime}

as LCS.

A.4.
Complete reflexive LCS

Recall that an LCS
E
is called reflexive if the canonical map

E\rightarrow(E_{b}^{\prime})_{b}^{\prime}
is an isomorphism of LCS.
The strong dual of a reflexive LCS is reflexive, a product of reflexive LCS is reflexive, and a direct sum of reflexive LCS is reflexive (see
[
K1
, § 23.5 (9)]
).
It is known that every nuclear Fréchet space, or more generally, every nuclear LF space, is reflexive
(see
[
T
, Corollary of Proposition 36.9, Corollary 3 of Proposition 50.2 and
Corollaries 1, 3 of Proposition 33.2]
).
Thus, we have the following examples of complete reflexive LCS:

•

the products of nuclear Fréchet spaces as well as their strong dual; and

•

the direct sums of nuclear LF spaces as well as their strong dual.

We would like to point out that various function spaces on formal manifolds
studied in this paper all have the above forms.

Lemma A.10
.

Let
E
be a product of nuclear Fréchet spaces, and let
F
be a direct sum of nuclear LF spaces. Then
E,E_{b}^{\prime},F,F_{b}^{\prime}
are all barreled, and
E_{b}^{\prime},F
are bornological.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from the fact that every reflexive LCS is barreled (see
[
K1
, § 23.3 (4)]
), and the second one follows from the following facts:

•

all Fréchet spaces are bornological (
[
K1
, § 28.1 (4)]
);

•

the strong dual of every reflexive Fréchet space is bornological (see
[
K1
, § 29.4 (4)]
); and

•

if an inductive limit of bornological LCS is Hausdorff, then it is still bornological (see
[
K1
, § 28.4 (1)]
).

∎

Together with Lemma
A.10
, the following lemma
A.11
is a direct consequence of the following facts.

•

For arbitrary two Fréchet (resp. barreled DF) spaces, their projective tensor product coincides with their inductive tensor product (see
[
K2
, § 40.2 (1),(11)]
).

•

The strong dual of all reflexive Fréchet spaces are reflexive DF spaces, and vice versa (by Lemma
A.7
).

•

Every complete nuclear DF space is barreled (see
[
J
, §21.5.4]
and
[
K1
, § 27.1 (2)]

•

•

Every reflexive LCS is barreled (see

Lemma A.11
.

A.5.
Strong dual of topological tensor products

Let

and

We finish Appendix

Lemma A.12
.

Let
E=\prod_{i\in I}E_{i}
be a product of nuclear Fréchet spaces, and let

F=\prod_{j\in J}F_{j}
be a product of reflexive Fréchet spaces. Then
E\widehat{\otimes}F=\prod_{(i,j)\in I\times J}E_{i}\widehat{\otimes}F_{j}
is a product of Fréchet spaces, and

(A.15)

(E\widehat{\otimes}F)_{b}^{\prime}=E_{b}^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F_{b}^{\prime}

as LCS.

Proof.

Since the completed projective tensor product of arbitrary two Fréchet spaces is also a Fréchet space, the first assertion follows by
[
CSW
, Lemma 6.6]
.

For the second one, we have that

\displaystyle(E\widehat{\otimes}F)_{b}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(\prod_{i\in I}E_{i}\right)\widehat{\otimes}\left(\prod_{j\in J}F_{j}\right)\right)_{b}^{\prime}=\left(\prod_{(i,j)\in I\times J}E_{i}\widehat{\otimes}F_{j}\right)_{b}^{\prime}\quad(\text{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{CSW}{}{}, Lemma 6.6]}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\widehat{\otimes}F_{j})_{b}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i})_{b}^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}(F_{j})_{b}^{\prime}\qquad\qquad\quad\,\,\,(\text{by}\ \eqref{eq:sdualofprodandsum}\ \text{and}\ \eqref{eq:dualprincipal})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i})_{b}^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}(F_{j})_{b}^{\prime}=\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}(E_{i})_{b}^{\prime}\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}\left(\bigoplus_{j\in J}(F_{j})_{b}^{\prime}\right)\ (\text{by\ \eqref{eq:threetopontenprod=}\ and\ \eqref{eq:sumcomiten}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i\in I}E_{i}\right)_{b}^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}\left(\prod_{j\in J}F_{j}\right)_{b}^{\prime}=E_{b}^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F_{b}^{\prime}\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\,\,\,(\text{by}\ \eqref{eq:sdualofprodandsum}).

∎

Vector-valued smooth functions

In this appendix, we review some basics of vector-valued smooth functions, and present some examples that are necessary in Sections
2
-
5
.
We also review the notion of
strict approximation property on LCS introduced in
[
Sc3
]
.

B.1.
Vector-valued smooth functions

In Appendixes
B.1
and
B.2
, let

N
be a smooth manifold, and let
E
be a quasi-complete LCS.

Write
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)
for the space of all
E
-valued smooth functions on
N
.
Endow
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)
with
the topology defined by the seminorms

\lvert f\rvert_{D,\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\mu}}:=\sup_{a\in N}\lvert(Df)(a)\rvert_{\mu}\qquad(f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)),

where
D
is a compactly supported differential operator on
N
, and
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\mu}
is a continuous seminorm on
E
.
Then
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)
is a quasi-complete LCS.

Let
\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E)
denote the space of all compactly supported
E
-valued smooth functions on
N
,
and let
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{K}(N;E)
denote the space of those
E
-valued smooth functions supported in
K
, where

K
is a compact subset of
N
.
Equip
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{K}(N;E)
with the subspace topology of
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)
, and equip

\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E)
with the inductive limit topology. We also write

{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{K}(N):={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{K}(N;\mathbb{C})\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(N):={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(N;\mathbb{C}).

Identify
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\otimes E
as a linear subspace of
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)
, which consists of the smooth functions
whose image is contained in a finite-dimensional subspace of
E
.
By
[
Sc1
, Thémrème 1]
, we have that

(B.1)

{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widetilde{\otimes}E={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)

as LCS,
and if
E
is complete, then

(B.2)

{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widetilde{\otimes}E={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widehat{\otimes}E

as LCS.

The identification (
B.1
) induces an LCS identification

(B.3)

{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{K}(N)\widetilde{\otimes}E={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{K}(N;E)

for every compact subset
K
of
N
.
By taking the inductive limit, the identifications (
B.3
) for various
K
yield a continuous linear map

(B.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E)\rightarrow\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(N)\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}E.

Note that the map (
B.4
) is injective, but it may not be a linear topological embedding, and may not be a linear isomorphism (as abstract vector spaces). See
[
Sc1
, Pages 107-108]
for details.

Remark B.1
.

Assume that
N
is secondly countable. In
[
Sc1
]
, Schwartz introduced the following variant of
{\mathrm{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E)
:

\widetilde{\mathrm{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E):=\{f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)\,:\,(a\mapsto\langle f(a),u^{\prime}\rangle)\in{\mathrm{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(N)\ \text{for all}\ u^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}\}.

The topology on
\widetilde{\mathrm{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E)
is given as follows: a net
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in it converges to zero if and only if for
every equicontinuous subset
H^{\prime}
of
E^{\prime}
, the nets
\{\langle f_{i},u^{\prime}\rangle\}_{i\in I}
for all
u^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
converge uniformly to zero in

{\mathrm{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(N)
.
There is an LCS identification (see
[
Sc1
, Thémrème 1]
)

{\mathrm{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(N)\widetilde{\otimes}E=\widetilde{{\mathrm{C}}}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E),

and if
E
is complete, we also have that

{\mathrm{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(N)\widehat{\otimes}E=\widetilde{{\mathrm{C}}}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E)

as LCS.
Note that,
if there exists a continuous norm on
E
, then
\widetilde{{\mathrm{C}}}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E)={\mathrm{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E)
as abstract vector spaces, and their bounded subsets coincide.
Furthermore, if
E
is a DF space, then
\widetilde{{\mathrm{C}}}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E)={\mathrm{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(N;E)
as LCS. See

[
G
, Chapitre II, Pages 83-84]
for details.

Here we present some examples of vector-valued smooth functions, which arise naturally in the study of vector-valued formal functions.

Example B.2
.

Let

E[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]:=\left\{\sum_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}v_{J}y^{J}\,:\,v_{J}\in E\textrm{ for all }J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}\right\}

Equip

\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu,J}:\quad\sum_{I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}f_{I}y^{I}\mapsto\lvert f_{J}\rvert_{\nu},

.

Let

.

B.3.
The strict approximation property

Let
E
and
F
be two LCS.
In the study of vector-valued distributions (see
[
Sc3
]
),
it is more natural to equip
{\mathcal{L}}(E,F)

with the topology of convex compact convergence.
As in
[
Sc3
]
, we denote the resulting LCS by
{\mathcal{L}}_{c}(E,F)
, and also denote
E_{c}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{L}}_{c}(E,\mathbb{C})
.

In what follows, we will often identify
E^{\prime}\otimes F
with the space of all continuous linear maps of finite rank from
E
to
F
.
Following
[
G
,
Sc3
]
, we make the following definition.

Definition B.6
.

An LCS
E
is said to have the approximation (resp. strict approximation) property if

E^{\prime}\otimes E
is dense (resp. strictly dense) in
{\mathcal{L}}_{c}(E,E)
.

Note that
E
has the approximation (resp. strict approximation) property if and only if the identity operator in
{\mathcal{L}}(E,E)
is contained in the closure (resp. quasi-closure) of

E^{\prime}\otimes E
in
{\mathcal{L}}_{c}(E,E)
(see
[
Sc3
, Préliminaires, Page 5, Remarques]
).
Furthermore, if
E=(E_{c}^{\prime})_{c}^{\prime}
as LCS,
then the canonical map

{\mathcal{L}}_{c}(E,E)\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}_{c}(E_{c}^{\prime},E_{c}^{\prime}),\qquad f\mapsto{}^{t}f

is a topological linear isomorphism (see
[
Sc3
, Préliminaires, Page 11, Remarques 2]
).
Thus, in this case,
E
has the approximation (resp. strict approximation) property if and only if
E_{c}^{\prime}
does.

According to
[
Sc3
]
, we have the following result, which is useful in the studying of vector-valued distributions.

Lemma B.7
.

Assume that

•

E_{c}^{\prime}
and
F
are quasi-complete (resp. complete);

•

(E_{c}^{\prime})_{c}^{\prime}=E
as LCS; and

•

E
has the strict approximation (resp. approximation) property.

Then the canonical injective linear map
E^{\prime}\otimes F\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(E,F)

induces an LCS identification

E^{\prime}_{c}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F={\mathcal{L}}_{c}(E,F)\quad(\text{resp.}\ E^{\prime}_{c}\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F={\mathcal{L}}_{c}(E,F)).

Proof.

The first condition in the lemma implies that

{\mathcal{L}}_{c}(E,F)
is topologically isomorphic to the
\varepsilon
-product
E_{c}^{\prime}\varepsilon F
of

E_{c}^{\prime}
and
F
(see
[
Sc3
, Chapitre I, Corolliare of Poposition 5]
).
On the other hand, the second and the third conditions imply that
the
\varepsilon
-product
E_{c}^{\prime}\varepsilon F
is topologically isomorphic to

E^{\prime}_{c}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F
(resp.
E^{\prime}_{c}\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F
) (see
[
Sc3
, Chapitre I, Corolliare 1 of Poposition 11]
).
This proves the lemma.
∎

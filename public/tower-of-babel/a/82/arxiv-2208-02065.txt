A Mikhlin–Hörmander multiplier theorem for the partial harmonic oscillator
Xiaoyan Su, Ying Wang, Guixiang Xu

Abstract
. We prove a Mikhlin–Hörmander multiplier theorem for the partial harmonic oscillator H par = − ∂ ρ 2 − Δ x + | x | 2 H_{\textup{par}}=-\partial_{\rho}^{2}-\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2} for ( ρ , x ) ∈ ℝ × ℝ d (\rho,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d} by using the Littlewood–Paley g g and g ∗ g^{\ast} functions and the associated heat kernel estimate. The multiplier we have investigated is defined on ℝ × ℕ \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N} .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
42B15, 42B25.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we prove a Mikhlin–Hörmander multiplier theorem for the partial harmonic oscillator in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
:

H_{\textup{par}}=-\partial_{\rho}^{2}-\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}-\dots-\partial_{x_{d}}^{2}+|x|^{2}.

The Schrödinger flows for the
operator
H_{\textup{par}}
arises in various branches of physics, such
as the Bose–Einstein condensates, and the propagation of mutually incoherent wave
packets in nonlinear optics (see
[
6
]
).

The classical Mikhlin–Hörmander multiplier theorem states that for
1<p<\infty
,

\|(m\widehat{f})^{\vee}\|_{p}\leq C_{p,d}\|f\|_{p}

provided that the Fourier multiplier
m\in C^{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
and satisfies

|\partial^{\alpha}m(\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha}|\xi|^{-\alpha}
for all multi-indices
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1
.
This result can be proved either by the Calderón–Zygmund singular integral operator theory in
[
5
,
11
]
, or
by the Littlewood–Paley
g
-functions in
[
12
]
. The use of the Fourier transform stems from the fact that the Laplacian operator only has the continuous spectrum in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For the operators with discrete spectrum, such as the spherical Laplacian operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}
, or the Hermite operator
-\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2}
, a sufficient condition to guarantee the
L^{p}
-boundedness of multipliers is the proper decay in the finite differences. More precisely, the multiplier operator for Hermite expansions is defined by

\displaystyle T_{m}f(x)=\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}m(2|\mu|+d)(f(x),\Phi_{\mu})\Phi_{\mu}(x),

where
\Phi_{\mu}
is a Hermite function, see Section
2
below.
By Theorem 1 in
[
14
]
,
T_{m}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
1<p<\infty
provided that

\displaystyle|\triangle_{k}^{j}m(k)|\leq C_{N}k^{-j}\ \text{for}\ j=0,1,\dots,N,

whenever
N>\frac{d}{2}
, where
\triangle_{k}^{j}
is the
j
-th forward finite difference.
This above result is shown by use of the Littlewood–Paley
g
-functions, see
[
1
,
14
]
.

In this paper, our goal is to show a Mikhlin–Hörmander multiplier theorem for the Schrödinger operator
H_{\textup{par}}
, which serves as an example for which the multiplier is defined in both continuous and discrete variables.
We remark that the operator
H_{\textup{par}}
is a polynomial perturbation of the Laplacian operator. Some multiplier results and Littlewood–Paley square function estimates for operators with polynomial perturbations have been established in
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
by using nilpotent Lie algebras. Recently, Killip, Miao, etc, make use of by the Calderón–Zygmund singular integral operator theory in
[
5
,
11
]
to show the Mikhlin-Hörmander multiplier theorem for the Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}:=-\Delta+\tfrac{a}{|x|^{2}}
,
a\geq-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
in
[
7
]
. This result was crucially used in
[
8
,
10
]
to obtain the scattering result of the solution for nonlinear Schrödinger and wave equations with the inverse-square potential.

Our method closely relies on the structure of the operator
H_{\textup{par}}
and the Mehler formula, and offers a different view towards understanding the operator
H_{\textup{par}}
. We can refer to a companion paper
[
13
]
for the Riesz transform and Sobolev spaces associated to the operator
H_{\textup{par}}
.

1.1.
Main result

For smooth function
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
,
H_{\textup{par}}f
can be reformulated by Fourier analysis as follows:

\displaystyle H_{\textup{par}}f(\rho,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\tau\rho}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f(\tau,\cdot),\Phi_{\mu}(\cdot))\Phi_{\mu}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\tau\rho}(\tau^{2}+2k+d)P_{k}\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f(\tau,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau,

(1.1)

where
\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f
is the Fourier transform with respect to
\rho
, and
P_{k}
is the projection to the
k
th eigenspace of the operator
H_{\textup{par}}
in
x
, which is spanned by the eigenfunctions
\Phi_{\mu}
’s for
|\mu|=k
: see Section
2.2
below.

Let
m=m(\tau,k)
be defined on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
. We define the operator
T_{m}
for
H_{\textup{par}}
by

\displaystyle T_{m}f(\rho,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\tau\rho}m(\tau,k)P_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f)(\tau,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad\text{for any }\;f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}).

(1.2)

In particular, if
m(\tau,k)=m(\tau^{2}+2k+d)
, the multiplier operator
T_{m}
coincides with
m(H_{\textup{par}})
defined by the functional calculus (see
[
13
]
), so the multipliers defined in (
1.2
) are more general than those defined by the spectral measure.

The main result in this paper is as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Mikhlin–Hörmander multiplier)
.

Suppose that a function
m(\tau,k)
defined on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfies the estimates

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial^{N}}{\partial\tau^{N}}m(\tau,k)\Big|\leq C(\tau^{2}+2k+d)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\ \text{and}\ \big|\triangle_{k}^{N}m(\tau,k)\big|\leq C(\tau^{2}+2k+d)^{-N}

(1.3)

for all
0\leq N\leq\lfloor\frac{d+1}{2}\rfloor+1
. Then, we have for any
1<p<\infty

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.

Remark 1.2
.

The similar result for the generalized
partial harmonic oscillator
-\Delta_{y}-\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2}
with
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
holds by the same argument.

Let
m(r)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
with
\operatorname{supp}m\subseteq[\frac{3}{8},\frac{3}{4}]
. Denote
m_{j}(r)=m(2^{-j}r)
, and the operator
\Delta_{j}f(\rho,x)=T_{m_{j}(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+2k+d})}f(\rho,x).
As a direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
and Khintchine’s inequality, we have the following Littlewood–Paley square function estimates for the operator
H_{\textup{par}}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

For
1<p<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\simeq\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\Delta_{j}f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.

Furthermore, for
\alpha\geq 0
,
1<p<\infty
, the Sobolev spaces
{W}_{H_{\textup{par}}}^{\alpha,p}
associated to the operator
H_{\textup{par}}
(see
[
13
]
) can be characterized by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{W}_{H_{\textup{par}}}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\simeq\Big\|\big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|2^{j\alpha}\Delta_{j}f|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.

We omit the proof and the readers can refer to
[
5
,
11
]
.

Lastly, this paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
, we introduce some preliminary results about Hermite functions, the Mehler formula and the heat kernel of the operator
H_{\textup{par}}
. In Section
3
, we show the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hermite functions

We first recall the Hermite functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as in
[
15
]
.
The Hermite functions
h_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}
are defined by

\displaystyle h_{k}(x)=(2^{k}k!\sqrt{\pi})^{-1/2}(-1)^{k}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{k}}(e^{-x^{2}})e^{-x^{2}/2}.

Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{d})
be a multi-index and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The Hermite functions
\Phi_{\mu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is defined by taking the product of the 1-dimensional Hermite functions
h_{\mu_{j}}(x_{j})
:

\Phi_{\mu}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}h_{\mu_{j}}(x_{j}).

The functions
\Phi_{\mu}
form a complete orthonormal system for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
If we define the operators
A_{j}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq d
, then

\displaystyle A_{j}\Phi_{\mu}=\sqrt{2(\mu_{j}+1)}\Phi_{\mu+e_{j}},

(2.1)

where
e_{j}
is the
j
th coordinate vector in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
.

Denote by
P_{k}
the spectral projection to the
k
th eigenspace of
-\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2}
,

\displaystyle P_{k}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{|\mu|=k}\Phi_{\mu}(x)\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})f(x^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

(2.2)

These projections are the integral operators with kernels

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x,x^{\prime})=\sum_{|\mu|=k}\Phi_{\mu}(x)\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime}).

The Mehler formula for
\Phi_{k}(x,x^{\prime})
is

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r^{k}\Phi_{k}(x,x^{\prime})=\pi^{-d/2}(1-r^{2})^{-d/2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}(|x|^{2}+|x^{\prime}|^{2})+\frac{2rx\cdot x^{\prime}}{1-r^{2}}},

(2.3)

for
0<r<1
, see
[
15
, p. 6]
.

The following lemmas are the direct consequences of the Mehler formula, which will be frequently used in the next section.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
15
]
, P. 92)
.

For all
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}e^{-t|\mu|}\Phi_{\mu}(x)^{2}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{2}},\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}e^{-t(2|\mu|+d)}\Phi_{\mu}(x)^{2}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=C(\sinh t)^{-d}.

(2.5)

2.2.
Heat kernel for the operator
H_{\textup{par}}

We write
z=(\rho,x)
or
z^{\prime}=(\rho^{\prime},x^{\prime})
to denote variables in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

From (
1.1
), we can define the heat semigroup with
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
as follows:

\displaystyle e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f(\rho,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\tau\rho}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f(\tau,\cdot),\Phi_{\mu}(\cdot))\Phi_{\mu}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\tau\rho}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2k+d)}{P_{k}}(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f)(\tau,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}K(t,z,z^{\prime})f(z^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime},

where we use the Mehler formula (
2.3
) in the last step and

\displaystyle K(t,z,z^{\prime})=2^{-\frac{d+2}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}t^{-1/2}(\sinh 2t)^{-d/2}e^{-B(t,z,z^{\prime})},

(2.6)

and

\displaystyle B(t,z,z^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4}(2\coth 2t-\tanh t)|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{\tanh t}{4}|x+x^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{(\rho-\rho^{\prime})^{2}}{4t}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We follow the arguments as in
[
5
,
12
]
. It suffices to show the following estimates

\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|g_{N+1}(T_{m}f)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|g_{N}^{\ast}(f)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}}

(3.1)

for some integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

First, given
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the Littlewood–Paley
g_{N}
-function by

g_{N}(f)(z)=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\partial_{t}^{N}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f(z)\big|^{2}t^{2N-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each
N\geq 1
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, there holds

\|g_{N}(f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}^{2}=2^{-2N}\Gamma(2N)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}^{2}.

(3.2)

Proof.

By the orthogonality of Hermite functions, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{t}^{N}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f(\rho,\cdot)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\tau\rho}(\tau^{2}+2k+d)^{N}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2k+d)}P_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f)(\tau,\cdot)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\Big\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

It follows from the Plancherel theorem in
\rho
that

\displaystyle\|g_{N}(f)(z)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\partial_{t}^{N}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f(z)\big|^{2}t^{2N-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|(\tau^{2}+2k+d)^{N}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2k+d)}P_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f)(\tau,\cdot)\Big|^{2}t^{2N-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}|P_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f)(\tau,\cdot)|^{2}\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}(\tau^{2}+2k+d)^{2N}e^{-2t(\tau^{2}+2k+d)}t^{2N-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Big]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=2^{-2N}\Gamma(2N)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}|P_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f)(\tau,\cdot)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=2^{-2N}\Gamma(2N)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}^{2},

which completes the proof. ∎

Lemma 3.2

(Equivalence of
L^{p}
norms)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for all
f\in L^{p}
, we have

C_{1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\leq\|g_{N}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.

Proof.

The fact that
\|g_{N}(f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}=C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}
is given by Lemma
3.1
. For general case
p\in(1,\infty)
, we will view

g_{N}
as a singular integral with a kernel taking values in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};t^{2N-1}dt)
by the auxillary function

\tilde{g}_{N}(f)(t,z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\frac{\partial^{N}K(t,z,z^{\prime})}{\partial t^{N}}f(z^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime},

where
K(t,z,z^{\prime})
is the kernel (
2.6
).
By definition, we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{g}_{N}(f)(\cdot,z)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=g_{N}(f)(z)^{2},

\displaystyle\big\|\|\tilde{g}_{N}(f)(\cdot,z)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle=\|g_{N}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.

The kernel of
\tilde{g}_{N}(f)
is

G_{N}(t,z,z^{\prime})=\frac{\partial^{N}K(t,z,z^{\prime})}{\partial t^{N}}.

We claim the following facts hold:

\displaystyle|G_{N}(t,z,z^{\prime})|\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-N}e^{-\frac{1}{16t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle|\partial_{z}G_{N}(t,z,z^{\prime})|+|\partial_{z^{\prime}}G_{N}(t,z,z^{\prime})|\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+2}{2}-N}e^{-\frac{1}{16t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}}.

(3.4)

Now we show the estimates (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). For
N=0
, the basic estimates that

\displaystyle 2\coth 2t-\tanh t>\coth 2t>

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2t},

\displaystyle\tanh t>t,\;\sinh 2t\geq

\displaystyle t

imply the upper bound

\displaystyle|K(t,z,z^{\prime})|\leq Ct^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{8t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}}e^{-\frac{t}{4}|x+x^{\prime}|^{2}}.

Let
N\geq 1
.
By using the high order derivative formula

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\sinh t}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N}}

\displaystyle=-i^{N+1}\sin\Big(it+\frac{\pi N}{2}\Big)

and the Faà di Bruno formula, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}(\sinh t)^{-d/2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N}}=\sum C_{N,m_{1},\dots m_{N}}(\sinh t)^{-d/2-(m_{1}+\dots+m_{N})}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{j}\sinh t}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{j}}\Big)^{m_{j}},

where the sum is over all
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
m_{1}+2m_{2}+\dots+Nm_{N}=N
. As

\displaystyle\Big|\sin\Big(it+\frac{\pi N}{2}\Big)\Big|\lesssim\begin{cases}1,&0<t<1,\\
e^{t},&t>1,\end{cases}

it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}(\sinh 2t)^{-d/2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N}}\right|\lesssim t^{-d/2-N}.

(3.5)

To estimate the derivatives for
B(t,z,z^{\prime})
, we use the following formulas:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\coth t}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N}2^{N+1}\operatorname{Li}_{-N}(e^{-2t}),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\tanh t}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N}}

\displaystyle=-2^{N+1}\operatorname{Li}_{-N}(-e^{2t}),

where
\operatorname{Li}_{-N}
is the polylogarithm in
[
9
]
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{N}B(t,z,z^{\prime})}{\partial t^{N}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Big[2^{N+1}(-1)^{N}2^{N+1}\operatorname{Li}_{-N}(e^{-4t})-2^{N+1}\operatorname{Li}_{-N}(-e^{2t})\Big]|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}

\displaystyle-2^{N-1}\operatorname{Li}_{-N}(-e^{2t})|x+x^{\prime}|^{2}+C_{N}\frac{(\rho-\rho^{\prime})^{2}}{t^{N+1}}.

(3.6)

Since

\lvert\operatorname{Li}_{-N}(s)|\lesssim 1
for
0<s<1/2,

we have

\lvert\operatorname{Li}_{-N}(e^{-t})|\lesssim 1\ \text{when }t>1.

By
(7.187)
and
(7.191)
in
[
9
]
, we also have

\lvert\operatorname{Li}_{-N}(-e^{t})|\lesssim 1\ \text{when }t>1.

When
0<t<1
, by the Laurent expansions of
\tanh t
and
\coth t
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\coth t}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N}}\Big|\lesssim t^{-(N+1)},\quad\Big|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\tanh t}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N}}\Big|\lesssim 1.

From this and (
3
), we have for
N\geq 1
that

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial^{N}B(t,z,z^{\prime})}{\partial t^{N}}\Big|\lesssim t^{-(N+1)}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}+|x+x^{\prime}|^{2}.

(3.7)

Direct computation gives the following upper bound for the derivatives in
z
and
z^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial^{N+1}B(t,z,z^{\prime})}{\partial t^{N}\partial z}\Big|+\Big|\frac{\partial^{N+1}B(t,z,z^{\prime})}{\partial t^{N}\partial z^{\prime}}\Big|\lesssim t^{-(N+1)}|z-z^{\prime}|+|x+x^{\prime}|.

(3.8)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\partial^{N}K(t,z,z^{\prime})}{\partial t^{N}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N_{1}+N_{2}=N}C_{N,N_{1}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N_{1}}(t^{-1/2})}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N_{1}}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N_{2}}(\sinh 2t)^{-d/2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N_{2}}}e^{-B(t,z,z^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}+N_{2}+N_{3}=N\\
N_{3}\geq 1\end{subarray}}C_{N,N_{1},N_{2}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N_{1}}(t^{-1/2})}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N_{1}}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N_{2}}(\sinh 2t)^{-d/2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{N_{2}}}e^{-B(t,z,z^{\prime})}\frac{\partial^{N_{3}}B(t,z,z^{\prime})}{\partial t^{N_{3}}}.

Using the upper bound of (
3.5
), (
3.7
), and
\lambda^{N}e^{-\lambda}\lesssim 1
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\Big|\frac{\partial^{N}}{\partial t^{N}}K(t,z,z^{\prime})\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}+N_{2}=N}t^{-1/2-N_{1}-\frac{d}{2}-N_{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{8t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}-\frac{t}{4}|x+x^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}+N_{2}+N_{3}=N\\
N_{3}\geq 1\end{subarray}}t^{-1/2-N_{1}-\frac{d}{2}-N_{2}}\big(t^{-N_{3}-1}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}+|x+x^{\prime}|^{2}\big)e^{-\frac{1}{8t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}-\frac{t}{4}|x+x^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-N}e^{-\frac{1}{16t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}}+\sum_{N_{3}\geq 1}t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}+N_{3}-N-1}e^{-\frac{1}{16t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-N}e^{-\frac{1}{16t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}},

which is (
3.3
). By (
3.8
), the similar argument gives (
3.4
).

The estimates (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) would imply that
G_{N}
is a Calderón–Zygmundkernel with value in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, and hence we have
\|g_{N}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}
. The reverse inequality follows from the boundedness of
g_{N}
, duality argument and Lemma
3.1
. In fact, by integrating (
3.3
) in
t
,

\displaystyle\|G_{N}(\cdot,z,z^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}t^{-(d+1)-2N}e^{-\frac{1}{8t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-(d+1)-2N}e^{-\frac{1}{8t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim|z^{\prime}-z|^{-2(d+1)}+e^{-|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|z^{\prime}-z|^{-2(d+1)}.

Similarly, by (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}G_{N}(\cdot,z,z^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}},\|\partial_{z^{\prime}}G_{N}(\cdot,z,z^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\lesssim|z^{\prime}-z|^{-(d+2)}.

That is,
G_{N}(t,z,z^{\prime})
is a Calderón–Zygmundkernel with value in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, and hence for all
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\|g_{N}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}=\big\|\|\tilde{g}_{N}(f)(\cdot,z)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.

As mentioned above, by the duality argument, we can obtain the reverse inequality, and complete the proof.
∎

Next, we define the
g_{N}^{\ast}
-function by

\displaystyle g_{N}^{\ast}(f)(z)^{2}={\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\!\!\!\!t^{1-\frac{d+1}{2}}(1+t^{-1}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2})^{-N}|\partial_{t}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f(z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}.

(3.9)

By (
1.1
), the Schwartz kernel of the operator
e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}T_{m}
is

\displaystyle M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\tau(\rho-\rho^{\prime})}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}m(\tau,|\mu|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})\Phi_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\tau(\rho-\rho^{\prime})}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2k+d)}m(\tau,k)\Phi_{k}(x,x^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.10)

The following result is the key estimate to show the second inequality in (
3.1
), that is where we use the decay assumption on
m
.

Lemma 3.3

(Pointwise estimate)
.

Under the assumption (
1.3
), for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, the following pointwise estimate

g_{N+1}(T_{m}f)(z)\leq Cg_{N}^{\ast}(f)(z)

(3.11)

holds for all
0\leq N\leq\lfloor\frac{d+1}{2}\rfloor+1
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{N}\partial_{s}e^{-(t+s)H_{\textup{par}}}(T_{m}f)(z)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}^{N}(e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}T_{m})(\partial_{s}e^{-sH_{\textup{par}}}f)(z).

(3.12)

In particular, by choosing
s=t
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{N+1}e^{-2tH_{\textup{par}}}(T_{m}f)(z)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}^{N}(e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}T_{m})(\partial_{t}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f)(z).

In order to show (
3.11
), it suffices to show for
t>0
and each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
that

\big|\partial_{t}^{N+1}{e^{-2tH_{\textup{par}}}}(T_{m}f)(z)\big|^{2}\\
\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-2N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(1+t^{-1}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2})^{-N}|\partial_{t}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f(z)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}.

(3.13)

This estimate (
3.13
) follows from the following claim whose proof we postpone in next lemma:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(1+t^{-1}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2})^{N}|\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-2N}.

(3.14)

By (
3.12
), (
3.14
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{N+1}e^{-2tH_{\textup{par}}}(T_{m}f)(z)|^{2}=\big|\partial_{t}^{N}(e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}T_{m})(\partial_{t}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f)(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})(\partial_{t}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f)(z^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(1+t^{-1}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2})^{N}|\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(1+t^{-1}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2})^{-N}|\partial_{t}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f(z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-2N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(1+t^{-1}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2})^{-N}|\partial_{t}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f(z)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime},

which gives (
3.13
) and hence completes the proof.
∎

Now we turn to show the claim (
3.14
), which follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4

(Estimates for the kernel
M_{t}
)
.

Under the assumption (
1.3
), for all
0\leq N\leq\lfloor\frac{d+1}{2}\rfloor+1
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})|\lesssim_{N}t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-N},

(3.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2N}|\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\lesssim_{N}t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-N}.

(3.16)

Proof.

We firstly prove the pointwise estimate (
3.15
). By (
2.4
), (
3
), the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the
L^{\infty}
bound of
m
, and the fact that
\lambda^{N}e^{-\lambda}\lesssim_{N}1
for any
\lambda>0
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\tau(\rho-\rho^{\prime})}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)^{N}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}m(\tau,|\mu|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})\Phi_{\mu}(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-N}\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{t}{2}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau|\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})\Phi_{\mu}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-N}e^{-\frac{t}{2}d}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{t}{2}\tau^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\Big(\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}e^{-t|\mu|}\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}e^{-t|\mu|}\Phi_{\mu}(x)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-N},

which gives (
3.15
).

Next, we show (
3.16
). We firstly consider the case
N=0
, that is

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}|M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}.

(3.17)

From (
3
), we know that

\displaystyle M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\tau\rho^{\prime}}\Big\{e^{i\tau\rho}\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}m(\tau,|\mu|)\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})\Phi_{\mu}(x)\Big\}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

Combining this with the Plancherel theorem in
\rho^{\prime}
, the
L^{\infty}
bound of
m
, (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}|M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\Big|\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}m(\tau,|\mu|)\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})\Phi_{\mu}(x)\Big|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2t\tau^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}e^{-t(2|\mu|+d)}\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}e^{-t(2|\mu|+d)}\Phi_{\mu}(x)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sinh t)^{-d}e^{-td}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}.

(3.18)

This implies (
3.16
) when
N=0
.

For
N\geq 1
, by the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2N}|\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}|(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)^{N}\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}+\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:\;|\beta|=N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}|(x-x^{\prime})^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\mathrm{I}_{N}+\mathrm{II}_{N}.

(3.19)

For the first term, by integration by parts, we have

(\rho-\rho^{\prime})^{N}\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\tau\rho^{\prime}}\Big\{(-i)^{N}e^{i\tau\rho}\\
\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{\partial^{N}}{\partial\tau^{N}}\Big[(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)^{N}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}m(\tau,|\mu|)\Big]\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})\Phi_{\mu}(x)\Big\}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

In the following, we write
\mathbf{f}(\tau,k)=(\tau^{2}+2k+d)^{N}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2k+d)}m(\tau,k)
for brevity.
By the Plancherel theorem in
\rho^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{N}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\big|(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)^{N}\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})\big|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\!\Big|\!\!\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\!\frac{\partial^{N}}{\partial\tau^{N}}\mathbf{f}(\tau,k)\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})\Phi_{\mu}(x)\Big|^{2}\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

(3.20)

We claim the following estimate holds under the first assumption in (
1.3
),

\Big|\frac{\partial^{N}}{\partial\tau^{N}}\mathbf{f}(\tau,|\mu|)\Big|\lesssim t^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{-\frac{t}{2}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}.

(3.21)

In fact, notice that for
j\geq 3
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)=2\tau,\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\tau^{2}}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)=2,\ \text{and}\ \frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\tau^{j}}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)=0.

Then the
Faà di Bruno formulagives

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial^{N_{1}}}{\partial\tau^{N_{1}}}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)^{N}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}=N_{1}}C_{N_{1},m_{1},m_{2}}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)^{N-m_{1}-m_{2}}(2\tau)^{m_{1}}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)^{N-\frac{N_{1}}{2}}.

By the Faà di Bruno formulaagain for
e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial^{N_{2}}}{\partial\tau^{N_{2}}}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}=N_{2}}C_{N_{2},n_{1},n_{2}}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}(-2t\tau)^{n_{1}}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{N_{2}}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)^{\frac{N_{2}}{2}}.

By the Leibniz rule and the assumption that

\Big|\frac{\partial^{N_{3}}}{\partial\tau^{N_{3}}}m(\tau,|\mu|)\Big|\lesssim(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)^{-N_{3}/2},

we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\Big|\frac{\partial^{N}}{\partial\tau^{N}}\mathbf{f}(\tau,|\mu|)\Big|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{N_{1}+N_{2}+N_{3}=N}C_{N,N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}\frac{\partial^{N_{1}}}{\partial\tau^{N_{1}}}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)^{N}\frac{\partial^{N_{2}}}{\partial\tau^{N_{2}}}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}\frac{\partial^{N_{3}}}{\partial\tau^{N_{3}}}m(\tau,|\mu|)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}+N_{2}+N_{3}=N}t^{N_{2}}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)^{N-\frac{N_{1}}{2}+\frac{N_{1}}{2}-\frac{N_{3}}{2}}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}.

Again by using that
\lambda^{N}e^{-\lambda}\lesssim_{N}1
for any
\lambda>0
, we can obtain (
3.21
).

Inserting (
3.21
) into (
3.20
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\Big|\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}e^{-\frac{t}{2}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})\Phi_{\mu}(x)\Big|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-N}.

(3.22)

Next, we estimate
\mathrm{II}_{N}
.
It suffices to show

\mathrm{II}_{N}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\big|(x-x^{\prime})^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})\big|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-N},\quad\text{for all }|\beta|=N.

(3.23)

We rewrite
M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})
as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})=(-1)^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Psi_{k}^{N}(\rho-\rho^{\prime})\Phi_{k}(x,x^{\prime}),

where

\Psi_{k}^{N}(\rho-\rho^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\tau(\rho-\rho^{\prime})}\mathbf{f}(\tau,k)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

Recall that
A_{j}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}
. Define also
A^{\prime}_{j}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}^{\prime}}+x_{j}^{\prime}
.
From Lemma
3.2.3
in
[
15
]
, we have

\displaystyle(x-x^{\prime})^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma,\delta}C_{\gamma,\delta}\triangle_{k}^{|\delta|}\Psi_{k}^{N}(\rho-\rho^{\prime})(A^{\prime}-A)^{\gamma}\Phi_{k}(x,x^{\prime}),

where
(A^{\prime}-A)^{\gamma}\coloneqq\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}(A^{\prime}_{j}-A_{j})^{\gamma_{j}}
, and
\sum_{\gamma,\delta}
denotes the sum over all multi-indices
\gamma
and
\delta
satisfying

2\delta_{j}-\gamma_{j}=\beta_{j}
and
\delta_{j}\leq\beta_{j}.

Hence,

\displaystyle(x-x^{\prime})^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\tau\rho^{\prime}}\Big\{e^{i\tau\rho}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma,\delta}C_{\gamma,\delta}\big[\triangle_{k}^{|\delta|}\mathbf{f}(\tau,k)\big](A^{\prime}-A)^{\gamma}\Phi_{k}(x,x^{\prime})\Big\}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using the Plancherel theorem in
\rho^{\prime}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\big|(x-x^{\prime})^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})\big|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

(3.24)

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigg|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma,\delta}C_{\gamma,\delta}\big[\triangle_{k}^{|\delta|}\mathbf{f}(\tau,k)\big](A^{\prime}-A)^{\gamma}\Phi_{k}(x,x^{\prime})\bigg|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

On one hand, we claim that the second assumption in (
1.3
) implies that

\displaystyle\big|\triangle_{k}^{|\delta|}\mathbf{f}(\tau,k)\big|\lesssim t^{-(N-|\delta|)}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2k+d)}.

(3.25)

Indeed, this follows from the following Leibniz rule for finite differences,

\displaystyle\triangle_{k}^{N}(f(k)g(k)h(k))

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=N}C_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}}\triangle_{k}^{m_{1}}f(k)\triangle_{k}^{m_{2}}g(k+m_{1})\triangle_{k}^{m_{3}}h(k+m_{1}+m_{2}),

the second assumption in (
1.3
), and the bounds

\displaystyle\big|\triangle_{k}^{N_{1}}(\tau^{2}+2k+d)^{N}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim(\tau^{2}+2k+N_{1}+d)^{N-N_{1}},

\displaystyle\big|\triangle_{k}^{N_{2}}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2k+d)}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{N_{2}}\ e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2k+d)}.

On the other hand, by using (
2.1
) to expand
(A^{\prime}-A)^{\gamma}\Phi_{k}(x,x^{\prime})
, we obtain

\displaystyle(A^{\prime}-A)^{\gamma}\Phi_{k}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\mu|=k}\sum_{\tau+\sigma=\gamma}A^{\tau}\Phi_{\mu}(x)(A^{\prime})^{\sigma}\Phi_{\mu}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\big(2(k+1)\big)^{\frac{|\gamma|}{2}}\sum_{|\mu|=k}\sum_{\tau+\sigma=\gamma}\Phi_{\mu+\tau}(x)\Phi_{\mu+\sigma}(x^{\prime}).

(3.26)

Inserting (
3.25
) and (
3
) into (
3.24
), we obtain (
3.23
) as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\big|(x-x^{\prime})^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{N}M_{t}(z,z^{\prime})\big|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\Big|\sum_{k\geq 0,\gamma,\delta}t^{-N+|\delta|}e^{-t(\tau^{2}+2k+d)}\big(2(k+1)\big)^{\frac{|\gamma|}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\mu|=k\\
\tau+\sigma=\gamma\end{subarray}}\Phi_{\mu+\tau}(x)\Phi_{\mu+\sigma}(x^{\prime})\Big|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\Big|\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}e^{-\frac{t}{2}(\tau^{2}+2|\mu|+d)}\sum_{\gamma,\delta}\sum_{\tau+\sigma=\gamma}\Phi_{\mu+\tau}(x)\Phi_{\mu+\sigma}(x^{\prime})\Big|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}-N}.

Finally, by combining (
3.17
), (
3.19
), (
3.22
) and (
3.23
), we obtain (
3.16
), which concludes the proof of Lemma
3.4
.∎

At last, we are ready to show the boundedness of the operator
g^{*}_{N}
by the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function estimate and the boundedness of
g_{N}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
2<p<\infty
and
N>\frac{d+1}{2}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|g_{N}^{\ast}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.

Proof.

Let
q
be the Hölder conjugate exponent of
p/2
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(1+t^{-1}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2})^{-N}|h(z)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\lesssim Mh(z^{\prime}),\quad\text{for }\;N>\frac{d+1}{2},

where
M
is the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\quad\big\|g_{N}^{\ast}(f)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}^{2}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|h\|_{L^{q}}=1\end{subarray}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(g_{N}^{\ast}f)^{2}h(z)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|h\|_{L^{q}}=1\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!t|\partial_{t}e^{-tH_{\textup{par}}}f(z^{\prime})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\!\!\!t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}(1+t^{-1}|z^{\prime}-z|^{2})^{-N}|h(z)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|h\|_{L^{q}}=1\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}|(g_{1}f)(z^{\prime})|^{2}M\big(\big|h(z^{\prime})\big|\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|h\|_{L^{q}}=1\end{subarray}}\|g_{1}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}}\|M(|h|)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}^{2},

where we have used Lemma
3.2
in the last inequality. This complets the proof.
∎

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We are now ready to show

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})},\ \text{for}\ 1<p<\infty,

under the assumption (
1.3
).

For the case
p=2
, the Plancherel theorem in
\rho
gives

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}}=\big\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m(\tau,k)P_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f)(\cdot,x)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}}.

By the orthogonality of Hermite functions, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big\|m(\tau,k)P_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f)(\cdot,x)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|m(\tau,k)\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big\|P_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\rho}f)(\cdot,x)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}^{2}.

Hence, the result in Theorem
1.1
holds for
p=2
.

For the case
2<p<\infty
. Let
N_{0}=\lfloor\frac{d+1}{2}\rfloor+1
, by Lemmas
3.2
,
3.3
and
3.5
, we have

\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|g_{N_{0}+1}(T_{m}f)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|g_{N_{0}}^{\ast}(f)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

Finally, the boundedness of the operator
T_{m}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
with
1<p<2
follows from the duality argument. ∎

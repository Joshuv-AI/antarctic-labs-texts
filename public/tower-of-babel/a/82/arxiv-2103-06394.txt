Quasi-isolated blocks and the Alperin-McKay conjecture
Lucas Ruhstorfer

Abstract
. The Alperin–McKay conjecture is a longstanding open conjecture in the representation theory of finite groups. Späth showed that the Alperin–McKay conjecture holds if the so-called inductive Alperin–McKay (iAM) condition holds for all finite simple groups. In a previous paper, the author has proved that it is enough to verify the inductive condition for quasi-isolated blocks of groups of Lie type. In this paper we show that the verification of the iAM-condition can be further reduced in many cases to isolated blocks. As a consequence of this we obtain a proof of the Alperin–McKay conjecture for 2 2 -blocks of finite groups with abelian defect.

Introduction

Alperin–McKay conjecture

In the representation theory of finite groups some of the most important conjectures predict a very strong relationship between the representations of a finite group
G
and certain representations of its
\ell
-local subgroups, where
\ell
is a prime dividing the order of
G
. One of these conjectures is the Alperin–McKay conjecture.
For an
\ell
-block
b
of
G
we denote by
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(G,b)
the set of height zero characters of
b
. Then the Alperin–McKay predicts the following:

Conjecture

(Alperin–McKay)
.

Let
b
be an
\ell
-block of
G
with defect group
D
and
B
its Brauer correspondent in
\mathrm{N}_{G}(D)
. Then

|\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(G,b)|=|\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\mathrm{N}_{G}(D),B)|.

Späth
[
39
, Theorem C]
showed that the Alperin–McKay conjecture holds if the so-called inductive Alperin–McKay condition holds for all finite groups. In a previous article, the author has reduced the verification of the inductive Alperin–McKay condition to so-called quasi-isolated blocks of groups of Lie type
[
36
]
. The overall aim of this article is to further reduce the verification of this condition to isolated blocks of groups of Lie type. Using this, we are then able to verify the inductive Alperin–McKay condition for many important classes of blocks.

Equivariant Bonnafé–Dat–Rouquier equivalence

One of our main ingredients towards such a reduction is the recent result by Bonnafé–Dat–Rouquier
[
4
]
.
They have constructed a Morita equivalence which can be seen as a modular analogue of Lusztig’s Jordan decomposition for characters. Let
\mathbf{G}
be a simple, simply connected algebraic group with Frobenius endomorphism
F:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
defining an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-structure where

is a power of a prime
p
. Fix a prime
\ell
different from

and let
(\mathcal{O},K,k)
be an
\ell
-modular system as in
1.1
below. In the following
\Lambda
denotes either the discrete valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
or its residue field
k
. Let
(\mathbf{G}^{\ast},F^{\ast})
be a group in duality with
(\mathbf{G},F)
and
s\in(\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}
a semisimple element of
\ell^{\prime}
-order. Let
e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}\in\mathrm{Z}(\Lambda\mathbf{G}^{F})
be the central idempotent associated to
s
as in
[
11
, Theorem 9.12]
. We assume that
\mathbf{L}^{\ast}
is the minimal Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
containing
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}^{\circ}(s)
. Let
N
be the common stabilizer in
\mathbf{G}^{F}
of the idempotent

and
\mathbf{L}
and suppose that

[
4

Theorem A

(see Theorem

\Lambda\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{F}\langle F_{0}\rangle e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}
.

Quasi-isolated blocks of type
A

In the next part of our paper we consider the case of groups of type
A
. According to the main result of
[
36
]
it is prove the inductive Alperin–McKay condition it suffices to consider strictly quasi-isolated block, i.e. blocks of
\Lambda\mathbf{G}^{F}e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}
such that
\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)\operatorname{C}_{(\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}}(s)

\mathbf{G}^{\ast}

\mathbf{G}
is of type

and

\mathbf{G}^{F}
is more complicated and the quotient group

can become arbitrary large with the rank of

Using the explicit description of quasi-isolated elements in groups of type

in
\mathbf{G}^{F}

[
4
]
is still applicable in this slightly more general situation and we obtain a Morita equivalence between

(see Corollary

)

Assume that
\mathbf{G}
is of type A and let

\ell^{\prime}
-order. If

-Blocks with abelian defect group

.

-module.

Proof.

Recall that the projection map

H

\mathbf{G}^{F}

of

3.
Construction for twisted groups

We generalize the construction of the previous section. This is essentially necessary for working with automorphisms of twisted groups. We suppose now that
F_{0}
is a Frobenius endomorphism with
F_{0}^{r}\rho=F
for some integer
r
and
\rho:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
a graph automorphism of order
l
which commutes with
F_{0}
. We denote by
\mathcal{A}
the subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{F})
generated by
F_{0}
.

The construction in this section will be done in two seperate steps. We first consider the connected reductive group

\mathbf{G}_{\rho}:=\{(g,\rho(g),\dots,\rho^{l-1}(g))\mid g\in\mathbf{G}\}.

We note that the projection onto the first coordinate defines an isomorphism

\mathrm{pr}_{1}:\mathbf{G}_{\rho}\to\mathbf{G}
. Denote by
\tau:\mathbf{G}_{\rho}\to\mathbf{G}_{\rho}
the automorphism given by

\tau(g,\rho(g),\dots,\rho^{l-1}(g)):=(\rho(g),\rho^{2}(g),\dots,\rho^{l-1}(g),g).

With this notation the projection onto the first coordinate defines a
(\tau F_{0}^{r},F)
-equivariant isomorphim
\mathbf{G}_{\rho}\to\mathbf{G}
.

Define now the group

\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}:=\mathbf{G}_{\rho}\times\dots\times\mathbf{G}_{\rho}

as the
r
-fold product of the group
\mathbf{G}_{\rho}
.
We define the permutation
\sigma_{0}
on the set
\{1,\dots,rl\}
as follows:

\sigma_{0}(i)=\begin{cases}i-l&\text{if }i>l,\\
rl-l+i&\text{if }1\leq i\leq l.\end{cases}

Consider the automorphism
\tau_{0}:\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}\to\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}
which for an element
(g_{1},\dots,g_{lr})\in\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}
is given by
\tau_{0}((g_{1},\dots,g_{lr})):=(g_{\sigma_{0}(1)},\dots,g_{\sigma_{0}(rl)})
.
Observe that

\sigma^{r}_{0}(i)=\begin{cases}i-1&\text{if }l\nmid(i-1),\\
i+l-1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It follows that
(\tau_{0})^{r}=\tau
(here
\tau
is understood as the permutation
\tau\times\dots\times\tau
on
\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}
). In particular, the morphism
\tau_{0}F_{0}
satisfies
(\tau_{0}F_{0})^{r}=\tau F_{0}^{r}
. Moreover,
\tau_{0}F_{0}
cyclically permutes the
r
copies
\mathbf{G}_{\rho}
of the group
\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}
. We deduce that the projection map
\mathrm{pr}_{2}:\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}\to\mathbf{G}_{\rho}
onto the first factor
\mathbf{G}_{\rho}
induces an isomorphism
\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}^{\tau F_{0}}\cong\mathbf{G}_{\rho}^{\tau_{0}F_{0}^{r}}
.

Notation 3.1
.

Assume that
\mathbf{H}
is a closed subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
. Then we define
\mathbf{H}_{\rho}:=\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{H})
. We then define

\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\rho}:=\mathbf{H}_{\rho}\times(\tau F_{0})^{r-1}(\mathbf{H}_{\rho})\times\dots\times(\tau F_{0})(\mathbf{H}_{\rho})

considered as a subgroup of
\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}
.
Furthermore, we denote
\underline{\mathbf{H}}^{\prime}_{\rho}:=(\mathbf{H}_{\rho})^{r}
.

Remark 3.2
.

If the subgroup
\mathbf{H}
is
\rho F_{0}
-stable, then
\mathbf{H}_{\rho}
is
\tau F_{0}
-stable. Thus,
\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\rho}=\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\rho}^{\prime}
in this case.
We remark that the automorphism
F_{0}
of the finite group
\mathbf{G}^{F}
corresponds to the automorphism
\tau_{0}^{-1}
of
\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}^{\tau_{0}F_{0}}
.

Let
\mathbf{P}
be a parabolic subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
with Levi decomposition
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{L}\ltimes\mathbf{U}
. Then we observe that
\underline{\mathbf{P}}_{\rho}
is a parabolic subgroup of
\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}
with Levi decomposition

\underline{\mathbf{P}}_{\rho}=\underline{\mathbf{L}}_{\rho}\ltimes\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{\rho}.

Assume now that
\mathbf{L}
is
F
-stable. It follows that
\mathbf{L}_{\rho}
is
\tau F_{0}^{r}
-stable and consequently the Levi subgroup
\underline{\mathbf{L}}_{\rho}
is
\tau_{0}F_{0}
-stable. We can therefore consider the Deligne–Lusztig variety
\mathbf{Y}_{\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{\rho}}^{\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho},F_{0}\tau_{0}}
which is a
\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}^{F_{0}\tau_{0}}\times(\underline{\mathbf{L}}_{\rho}^{F_{0}\tau_{0}})^{\mathrm{opp}}
-variety. Under the isomorphism
\mathbf{G}^{F}\cong\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}^{F_{0}\tau_{0}}
induced by the projection map

\mathrm{pr}:=\mathrm{pr}_{1}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{2}:\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}\to\mathbf{G}

we will in the following regard it as a
\mathbf{G}^{F}\times(\mathbf{L}^{F})^{\mathrm{opp}}
-variety.

Proposition 3.3
.

With the assumptions as above, the projection
\mathrm{pr}:\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho}\to\mathbf{G}
onto the first coordinate defines an isomorphism

\mathbf{Y}_{\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{\rho}}^{\underline{\mathbf{G}}_{\rho},\tau_{0}F_{0}}\cong\mathbf{Y}_{\mathbf{U}}^{\mathbf{G},F}

of varieties which is
\mathbf{G}^{F}\times(\mathbf{L}^{F})^{\mathrm{opp}}
-equivariant.

Proof.

The projection onto the first coordinate defines a
(\tau F_{0}^{r},F)
-equivariant isomorphim
\mathbf{G}_{\rho}\to\mathbf{G}
. Thus, we obtain a
\mathbf{G}^{F}\times(\mathbf{L}^{F})^{\mathrm{opp}}

\mathbf{Y}_{\mathbf{U}_{\rho}}^{\mathbf{G}_{\rho},\tau F_{0}^{r}}\to\mathbf{Y}_{\mathbf{U}}^{\mathbf{G},F}.

The morphism

and cyclically permutes the
r

\mathbf{G}_{\rho}
of the group

For this consider the connected reductive group

The group

4.
An equivariant Bonnafé–Dat–Rouquier equivalence

In this section we give a partial answer to the question whether the Morita equivalence constructed by Bonnafé–Dat–Rouquier is automorphism-equivariant. We often use the following well known fact.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group and
\phi:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
a Frobenius endomorphism.
The norm map
N_{\phi^{r}/\phi}:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G},\,x\mapsto\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\phi^{i}(x),
is surjective and maps
\mathbf{G}^{\phi^{r}}
to
\mathbf{G}^{\phi}
.

Proof.

By Lang’s theorem we can write
y\in\mathbf{G}
as
y=a^{-1}\phi^{r}(a)
. Then for
x:=a^{-1}\phi(a)
we have
N_{\phi^{r}/\phi}(x)=y
. The second statement is a straightforward computation.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a simple, simply connected algebraic group of type
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
or
E_{7}
such that either
n>2
or
q
is odd. Then there exists a Frobenius morphism
F_{0}:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
stabilizing
\mathbf{L}
such that the image of
\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{F}\rtimes\langle F_{0}\rangle
in the outer automorphism group of
\mathbf{G}^{F}
is
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{G}^{F})_{e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}}
. There exists a Morita equivalence between
\Lambda\mathbf{N}^{F}e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}}
and
\Lambda\mathbf{G}^{F}e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}
which lifts to a Morita equivalence between
\Lambda\mathrm{N}_{\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{F}\langle F_{0}\rangle}(\mathbf{L},e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}})e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}}
and
\Lambda\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{F}\langle F_{0}\rangle e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of
[
35
, Corollary 4.2]
we see that there exists a field endomorphism
\phi:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
such that
\phi^{r}=F
for some integer
r
and such that
\phi
together with
\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{F}
generates
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{G}^{F})_{e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}}
.
The Levi subgroup
\phi(\mathbf{L})
is
\mathbf{G}^{F}
-conjugate to
\mathbf{L}
. Thus, there exists some
y\in\mathbf{G}^{F}
such that
\mathbf{L}
is
y\phi
-stable. Since
e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}
is
\phi
-stable there exists some
x\in\mathrm{N}_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{L})
such that
e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}}
is
xy\varphi
-stable. Denoting
z:=xy
we have
(z\phi)^{r}=N_{F/\phi}(z)F
and therefore
N_{F/\phi}(z)\in\mathrm{N}_{\mathbf{G}^{\phi}}(\mathbf{L},e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}})
.

Suppose first that
z_{0}:=N_{F/\phi}(z)\in\mathbf{L}^{F}
. The Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}
is
z\phi
-stable and we have
z_{0}\in\mathbf{L}^{z\phi}
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.1
there exists
l\in\mathbf{L}^{z_{0}F}
such that
z_{0}^{-1}=N_{z_{0}F/z\phi}(l)
. We define
F_{0}:=lz_{0}\phi
. Then we have

F_{0}^{r}=(lz_{0}\phi)^{r}=(l(z_{0}\phi))^{r}=N_{z_{0}F/z\phi}(l)z_{0}F=F.

Since
F_{0}=lz_{0}\phi
commutes with
F
we have
F(lz_{0})(lz_{0})^{-1}=F(l)l^{-1}\in\mathrm{Z}(\mathbf{G})
. Thus,
\phi
and
F_{0}
do not necessarily have the same image in
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{G}^{F})
but differ by the diagonal automorphism induced by
\mathrm{ad}(l)
. Since
e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}}
is
z_{0}\phi
-stable and stable under diagonal automorphisms we observe that
e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}}
is
F_{0}
-stable. From this we conclude that
F_{0}
together with
\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{F}
generates the stabilizer of
e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}
in
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{G}^{F})
.
Note that
(\mathbf{N}/\mathbf{L})^{F_{0}}=\mathbf{N}/\mathbf{L}
since by the remarks following
[
34
, Lemma 3]
the group
\mathbf{N}/\mathbf{L}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\mathrm{Z}(\mathbf{G})
which has at most order
2
.
We can now apply Lemma
2.1
and conclude that there exists a
\Lambda[\mathbf{G}^{F}\times(\mathbf{N}^{F})^{\operatorname{opp}}\Delta(\langle F_{0}\rangle)]
-module
M
extending
H_{c}^{\mathrm{dim}}(\mathbf{Y}_{\mathbf{U}},\Lambda)e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}}
which induces a Morita equivalence between
\Lambda\mathbf{N}^{F}e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}}
and
\Lambda\mathbf{G}^{F}e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}
. In particular, the bimodule
\tilde{M}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbf{G}^{F}\times(\mathbf{N}^{F})^{\operatorname{opp}}\Delta\tilde{\mathbf{N}}^{F}\langle F_{0}\rangle}^{\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{F}\langle F_{0}\rangle\times(\tilde{\mathbf{N}}^{F}\langle F_{0}\rangle)^{\operatorname{opp}}}(M)
induces a Morita equivalence between
\Lambda\tilde{\mathbf{N}}^{F}\langle F_{0}\rangle e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}}
and
\Lambda\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{F}\langle F_{0}\rangle e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}
.

Assume now that that
N_{F/\phi}(z)\notin\mathbf{L}^{F}
. Since
\mathbf{N}/\mathbf{L}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\mathrm{Z}(\mathbf{G})
we conclude that
N_{F/\phi}(z)
generates the quotient group
\mathbf{N}/\mathbf{L}
. Thus, in this case the bijective morphism
F_{0}:=\phi
has the property that the element
zF_{0}\in G\langle F_{0}\rangle
generates the quotient group
\mathrm{N}_{G\langle F_{0}\rangle}(\mathbf{L},e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}})/\mathbf{L}^{F}
which is in particular cyclic. We conclude that in this case the
\Lambda(\mathbf{G}^{F}\times(\mathbf{L}^{F})^{\operatorname{opp}}\Delta(\tilde{\mathbf{L}}^{F}))
-module
H_{c}^{\mathrm{dim}}(\mathbf{Y}_{\mathbf{U}},\Lambda)e_{s}^{\mathbf{L}^{F}}
extends to a
\mathbf{G}^{F}\times(\mathbf{L}^{F})^{\operatorname{opp}}\Delta(\operatorname{N}_{\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{F}\langle F_{0}\rangle}(\mathbf{L},e_{s}^{L}))
-module. The claim is now a consequence of
[
27
, Theorem 3.4]
(see also
[
35
, Theorem 1.7]
).
∎

5.
Reduction to isolated blocks: type
A

5.1.
Strictly quasi-isolated blocks

The following notion will become important when dealing with actual blocks of groups of Lie type.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group. We say that a semisimple element
s\in(\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}
is
strictly quasi-isolated in
(\mathbf{G}^{\ast},F^{\ast})
if
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)^{F^{\ast}}\mathrm{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}^{\circ}(s)
is not contained in a proper Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
.

We denote by
A(s)
the component group
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)/\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)
. The proof of the following lemma is similar to the proof of
[
2
, Corollary 2.9]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
\mathbf{G}
is simple, simply connected of type
A
. If
s\in(\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}
is a semisimple element which is strictly quasi-isolated, then we have
A(s)^{F^{\ast}}=A(s)
.

Proof.

Let
\iota^{\ast}:\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{\ast}\to\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
be the map dual to the map
\iota:\mathbf{G}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{\tilde{G}}
and let
\tilde{s}\in(\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}
such that
\iota^{\ast}(\tilde{s})=s
.
Consider the injective morphism
\omega_{s}:A(s)\to\mathrm{Z}(\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{\ast})
as in
[
2
, Corollary 2.8]
. Let
e
denote the exponent of the subgroup
A(s)^{F^{\ast}}
of
A(s)
. Let
g\in\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)\mathrm{C}_{(\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}}(s)
. Then
1=\omega_{s}(g)^{e}=\omega_{s^{e}}(g)
and therefore
g\in\mathrm{Ker}(\omega_{s}^{e})=\iota^{\ast}(\mathrm{C}_{\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{\ast}}(\tilde{s}^{e}))
. Consequently,
\iota^{\ast}(\mathrm{C}_{\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{\ast}}(\tilde{s}^{e}))=\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s^{e})
is a Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
containing
\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)\mathrm{C}_{(\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}}(s)
. This is a proper Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
unless
s^{e}=1
. By the classification of quasi-isolated elements of
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}=\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})
in
[
2
, Proposition 5.2]
, we have
\mathrm{o}(s)=|A(s)|
which implies that
e=|A(s)|
. Since
e
is the exponent of
A(s)^{F^{\ast}}
we must necessarily have
A(s)=A(s)^{F^{\ast}}
.
∎

5.2.
Duality for groups of type
A

From now on until Section
12
we assume that
\mathbf{G}=\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})
is of type
A_{n}
. For an integer
q=p^{f}
we let
F_{q}:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
be the Frobenius endomorphism which raises every matrix entry to its
q
th power. For
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
we define
F^{\prime}=F_{q}(\gamma^{\prime})^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}}
. Here
\gamma^{\prime}:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G},\,g\mapsto g^{-\mathrm{tr}}
is the graph automorphism given by transpose inversion. We denote by
\mathbf{T}_{0}
the torus of diagonal matrices and by
\mathbf{B}_{0}
the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices. We denote by
W:=\operatorname{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T}_{0})/\mathbf{T}_{0}
the Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}
and identify
W
with the symmetric group
S_{n+1}
.

Note that with our choices of
F^{\prime}
the torus
\mathbf{T}_{0}
is not maximally split in the twisted case. This is because the graph automorphism
\gamma^{\prime}
doesn’t stabilize the Borel subgroup
\mathbf{B}_{0}
. Therefore, we define
\gamma:=\operatorname{ad}(n_{0})\gamma^{\prime}
, where
n_{0}\in\operatorname{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T}_{0})
is the matrix with entry
(-1)^{l+1}
at position
(l,n+1-l)
with
1\leq l\leq n
and
0
elsewhere. Observe that its image
w_{0}\in W
is the longest element of
W
. For
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and fixed
q
we consider the Frobenius endomorphism
F:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
given by
F:=F_{q}\gamma^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}}
.

To efficiently compute with dual groups we will use some simplifications specific to the situation in type
A
. Since
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
is adjoint of type
A
there exists a surjective morphism
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
. Suppose that
f:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
is a bijective morphism such that
f(\mathbf{T}_{0})=\mathbf{T}_{0}
. Then we denote by
f^{\ast}:\mathbf{G}^{\ast}\to\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
the unique morphism satisfying
\pi\circ f=f^{\ast}\circ\pi
.

If
f
is a Frobenius endomorphism then the pair
(\mathbf{G}^{\ast},f^{\ast})
is dual to
(\mathbf{G},f)
. For a Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}
of
\mathbf{G}
we define
\mathbf{L}^{\ast}:=\pi(\mathbf{L})
. We observe that
W^{\ast}:=\operatorname{N}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(\mathbf{T}_{0}^{\ast})/\mathbf{T}_{0}^{\ast}
is the Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
and we obtain a natural isomorphism
\pi:W\to W^{\ast}
.

If
\mathbf{L}
is
\mathrm{ad}(g)f
-stable, for some
g\in\mathbf{G}^{F}
, then one can check the pair
(\mathbf{L},\mathrm{ad}(g)f)
is again in duality to
(\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathrm{ad}(\pi(g))f^{\ast})
.

Remark 5.3
.

We consider
\mathbf{G}
with the Frobenius
F^{\prime}
as defined above and we let
s\in(\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F^{\prime\ast}}
be a strictly quasi-isolated element. The aim of this remark is to compute the
F^{\prime}
-type of the Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}^{\ast}:=\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)
. For an integer
m
dividing
n+1
we fix a primitive
m
th root of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
and define

t_{m}:=t:=(1,\zeta,\dots,\zeta^{m-1})\otimes I_{e},

where
me=n+1
. According to
[
2
, Proposition 5.2]
there exists
g\in\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
and
m
dividing
n+1
such that

. Since

is
(F^{\prime})^{\ast}
-stable we have
F^{\prime\ast}(t)={}^{F^{\prime\ast}(g)}s={}^{F^{\prime\ast}(g)g^{-1}}t
. Hence,

is
w^{\ast}F^{\prime\ast}
-stable, where
w^{\ast}
is the image of
g^{-1}F^{\prime\ast}(g)
in
W_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(\mathbf{T}_{0}^{\ast})
. Since

is assumed to be strictly quasi-isolated Lemma
5.2
yields

. This implies that
A(t)^{w^{\ast}F^{\prime\ast}}=A(t)
. By
[
2

the component group

has order

. On the other hand
A(s)
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\mathrm{Z}(\mathbf{G}^{F})
which has order
(n,q-\varepsilon)
. Therefore
m
divides
(n,q-\varepsilon)
. From this we deduce that the element
t
is
(F^{\prime})^{\ast}
-stable. From this it follows that
w^{\ast}\in W(t):=\{w\in W^{\ast}\mid{}^{w}t=t\}
. Let
v_{m}\in W^{\ast}
be the permutation defined by

v_{m}(i)\equiv i+e\,\mathrm{mod}\,(n+1)

such that
A(t)
is generated by

and has order

.
Denote by

the Weyl group of
\mathbf{L}_{m}^{\ast}:=\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(t)
relative to the maximal torus
\mathbf{T}_{0}^{\ast}
such that we have

, see
[
2

. To determine the type of

and we therefore conclude that
\mathbf{L}

F^{\prime}
-type

, where

.

If we consider
\mathbf{G}
with the twisted Frobenius endomorphism

instead of

is of
F
-type

with

.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
s
be a strictly quasi-isolated element. Then the Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}^{\ast}=\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)
is
d
-split for some integer

dividing
2|A(s)|
.

Proof.

We keep the notations of Remark
5.3
. Assume first that
\varepsilon=1
.
Let
w\in\langle v_{m}\rangle
as in Remark
5.3
such that
\mathbf{L}^{\ast}
is of type
W^{\circ}(t)w
. Then
w
has order

with
d

and we can decompose

into disjoint cycles each

of order

The subgroup

Lemma 6.4
.

There exists a subgroup
V\subseteq\mathrm{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T}_{0})
and a surjective group homorphism
\rho:V\to W
such that the following holds.
Every
\gamma
-stable element

has a
\langle F_{p},\gamma\rangle
-stable preimage in

Lemma 6.5
.

Every
\langle F,\gamma\rangle
-stable element of
\mathrm{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{L}_{m})/\mathbf{L}_{m}

\langle F,\gamma\rangle

Let
W_{\mathbf{L}_{m}}

\mathbf{L}_{m}

of

Using

we thus obtain

. We first show that every
\gamma
-stable element
\sigma
of
\mathrm{N}_{W}(W_{\mathbf{L}_{m}})/W_{\mathbf{L}_{m}}

\gamma
-stable preimage in

. For this we write
\sigma\in S_{m}
as a product
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{r}

corresponds to a permutation of the

Denote by

with

. Note that the element

corresponds to an

\langle\overline{v}\rangle\to\langle\overline{v}\rangle,\overline{v}\mapsto\gamma(\overline{v})\overline{v}^{-1}=\overline{v}^{-2}

. In other words,
\overline{xw}
is
\gamma
-stable.

Assume now that
\overline{v}
has even order. We denote by

the sign map on the symmetric group

and by

its kernel. Since

∎

6.2.
Stabilizers of blocks

Recall from the remarks before Remark
5.3
that we defined
f^{\ast}:\mathbf{G}^{\ast}\to\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
as the unique morphism satisfying
f^{\ast}\circ\pi=\pi\circ f
, where
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
is the natural surjective map.
The following observation will be used throughout this section:

Lemma 6.7
.

The surjective map
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
induces a natural isomorphism between the quotient groups
\mathrm{N}_{G\langle f\rangle}(\mathbf{L}^{\prime},e_{s}^{L^{\prime}})/L^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{C}_{(\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}\langle f^{\ast}\rangle}(s)((\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}/((\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}
given by sending the class of
gf
to
\pi(g)f^{\ast}
.

Proof.

By definition of
f
and
f^{\ast}
it follows that for every element
g\in\mathbf{G}^{F}
the morphisms
\mathrm{ad}(g)f
and
\mathrm{ad}(\pi(g))f^{\ast}
are in duality. Now assume that
g\in\mathbf{G}^{F}
is such that
gf
stabilizes the pair
(\mathbf{L}^{\prime},e_{s}^{L^{\prime}})
. It follows that
\pi(g)f^{\ast}
stabilizes
(\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{\ast}
. Since
(\mathrm{ad}(\pi(g))f^{\ast})|_{(\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{\ast}}
is in duality with
(\mathrm{ad}(g)f)|_{\mathbf{L}^{\prime}}
we deduce that
\pi(g)f^{\ast}
stabilizes the
((\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}
-conjugacy class of
s
. Since
\pi
maps
(\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{F}
to
((\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}
it follows that the map in the statement of the lemma is well-defined and injective. On the other hand, assume that
xf^{\ast}
stabilizes the
((\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}
-conjugacy class of
s
. Let
g^{\prime}\in\mathbf{G}
such that
\pi(g^{\prime})=x
. Hence,
F(g^{\prime})(g^{\prime})^{-1}\in\mathrm{Z}(\mathbf{G})\subseteq\mathbf{L}^{\prime}
and by applying Lang’s theorem to
F:\mathbf{L}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{L}^{\prime}
we deduce that there exists some
l^{\prime}\in(\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{\ast}
such that
g:=g^{\prime}l^{\prime}
is
F
-stable. We have
\pi(g)=x\pi(l^{\prime})
with
\pi(l^{\prime})\in((\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}
and so indeed
gf
is a preimage of
xf^{\ast}
.
∎

The most striking property of strictly quasi-isolated blocks (concerning the action of group automorphisms) is the following:

Lemma 6.8
.

Assume that
e_{s}^{\mathbf{G}^{F}}
is
\gamma
-stable. Then there exists some

and a parabolic subgroup
\mathbf{P}
with Levi complement
\mathbf{L}

y\gamma
stabilizes

and the idempotent

Proof.

Let
\gamma^{\ast}:\mathbf{G}^{\ast}\to\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
be the dual of the graph automorphism
\gamma
. There exists some

such that
{}^{g}s=t
. Consider the element

\tilde{t}=\tilde{t}_{m}:=(1,\zeta,\dots,\zeta^{m-1})\otimes I_{e}\in\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{\ast}=\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}),

a preimage of

under the natural map
\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})\to\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})

and we observe that
\gamma^{\ast}(\tilde{t})=tz
, where

. So,

is
\gamma^{\ast}
-stable and thus
\gamma^{\ast}(s)={}^{\gamma^{\ast}(g^{-1})g}s
.

Since

we conclude that
{}^{g^{-1}\gamma^{\ast}(g)\gamma^{\ast}}s={}^{g^{-1}\gamma^{\ast}g}s=s
. Consider the element

. By assumption there exists some
y\in(\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F^{\ast}}
such that
s
is
y\gamma^{\ast}
-stable. Therefore

by Lemma

we deduce that

F^{\ast}(xy^{-1})(xy^{-1})^{-1}=F^{\ast}(x)x^{-1}\in\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)=\mathbf{L}^{\ast}.

Thus, by Lang’s theorem there exists some
l\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}^{\circ}(s)
such that

is
F^{\ast}
-stable. We have

(g^{-1}\gamma^{\ast}g)^{2}=1
which implies that
(lx\gamma^{\ast})^{2}\in\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)
.
Recall that
\mathbf{L}^{\ast}={}^{g}\mathbf{L}_{m}^{\ast}
and that

denotes the Weyl group of
\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(t_{m})
relative to the maximal torus
\mathbf{T}_{0}^{\ast}

\mathbf{P}_{m}^{\ast}
be the standard parabolic subgroup associated with the parabolic subsystem

of

with Levi complement
\mathbf{L}_{m}^{\ast}
. We define

and we observe that the pair
(\mathbf{L}_{m}^{\ast},\mathbf{P}_{m}^{\ast})

\gamma^{\ast}
-stable. Consequently,

stabilizes the pair

and since

we observe that

also stabilizes this pair. The statement follows now by using duality, see Lemma

.
∎

We observe that the conclusion of Lemma

remains valid for the Levi subgroup

constructed in Lemma

.

Corollary 6.9

∎

7.

7.1.
Extending modules

Let
X
be a normal subgroup of a finite group
Y
. If
M
is a
\Lambda X
-module then in practice it is often quite hard to decide whether
M
extends to a
\Lambda Y
-module if
\ell\mid|Y:X|
unless
M
is simple or projective. We will therefore in the following often consider the following weaker notion:

Definition 7.1
.

Let
X
be a normal subgroup of a finite group
Y
such that the quotient group
Y/X
is solvable. If
M
is a
\Lambda X
-module then we say that it
almost extends to
Y
if
M
extends to a
\Lambda H
-module
M^{\prime}
, where
H/X
is a Hall
\ell^{\prime}
-subgroup of
Y/X
, such that the extension
M^{\prime}
is
Y
-stable.

We will check almost extendibility with the following remark.

Remark 7.2
.

In the situation of the previous definition assume that
M
is
Y
-stable and
E:=\mathrm{End}_{\Lambda N}(M)/J(\mathrm{End}_{\Lambda N}(M))
is abelian. If the Hall
\ell^{\prime}
-subgroup
H/X
is normal in
Y/X
and
M
extends to
H
then by
[
42
, Corollary 2.6]
the module
M
automatically almost extends to
Y
.

We will later use the following result about almost extendibility.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
X
be a normal subgroup of a finite group
Y
such that
Y/X
is abelian. Assume that
M
is a
\Lambda X
-module such that
E:=\mathrm{End}_{\Lambda X}(M)/J(\mathrm{End}_{\Lambda X}(M))
is abelian and
M
almost extends to
Y
. If
M^{\prime}
is an extension of
M
to a subgroup
X^{\prime}
of
Y
such that
\ell\nmid|X^{\prime}:X|
then
M^{\prime}
almost extends to
Y
as well.

Proof.

Let
H/X
be the Hall
\ell^{\prime}
-subgroup of
Y/X
. By assumption,
H/X^{\prime}
is the Hall
\ell^{\prime}
-subgroup of
Y/X^{\prime}
. By Remark
7.2
it is therefore enough to show that
M^{\prime}
extends to
H
.

Assume that
U
is maximal among the subgroups of
H
containing
X^{\prime}
with the property that
M^{\prime}
extends to
U
. Denote by
M^{\prime\prime}
an extension of
M^{\prime}
to
U
. If
M^{\prime\prime}
is stable under some element
x\in H\setminus U
then by
[
33
, Lemma 10.2.13]
the module
M^{\prime\prime}
extends to
\langle U,x\rangle
. Therefore, the stabilizer of
M^{\prime\prime}
in
H
is
U
.

Denote by
\bar{\,}:Y\to Y/X
the projection map.
By the remark following
[
42
, Theorem 2.5]
the set of
H
-invariant extension of
M
to
U
is in bijection with
H^{1}(\overline{U},E^{\times})^{\overline{H}/\overline{U}}
. Since
Y/X
is abelian, the action of
{\overline{H}/\overline{U}}
on this cohomology group is trivial. On the other hand, by
[
42
, Proposition 2.4]
the set of extensions of
M
to
U
is in bijection with
H^{1}(\overline{U},E^{\times})
. From this it follows that every extension of
M
to
U
is
H
-stable. In particular,
M^{\prime}
is
H
-stable and we conclude that
U=H
.
∎

7.2.

Our aim in this section is to show that the cohomology module
H_{c}^{\mathrm{dim}}(\mathbf{Y}_{\mathbf{U}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{G}},\Lambda)e_{s}^{L^{\prime}}

13.
A variant of Späth’s reduction theorem

In her paper
[
39
]
Späth shows that the Alperin–McKay conjecture holds for every finite group if and only if the inductive Alperin–McKay condition holds for every simple group. Our aim here is to modify her proof in order to get a similar statement involving preferably only blocks with abelian defect. Unfortunately in her proof, it is necessary to consider central extensions of groups and a block might have abelian defect group but a block of a central extension dominating it might not. Therefore, we need to consider blocks whose defect group lies in a slightly larger class of groups.

For the following definition recall that if
G
is a finite group its upper central series is defined recursively as
Z_{0}(G):=1
and
\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)
is the unique subgroup of
G
containing
\operatorname{Z}_{i-1}(G)
such that
\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)/\operatorname{Z}_{i-1}(G)=\operatorname{Z}(G/\operatorname{Z}_{i-1}(G))
.

Definition 13.1
.

We say that a subgroup
D
of a finite group
G
is
almost abelian in

G
if there exists an
i
such that
D\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)/\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)
is abelian.

Observe that the property of
D
being almost abelian can depend on the ambient group
G
. Moreover, if one considers the hypercenter
\operatorname{Z}_{\infty}(G)
of
G
(i.e. the union of all
\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)
for
i\geq 0
) then
D
is almost abelian in
G
if and only
D\operatorname{Z}_{\infty}(G)/\operatorname{Z}_{\infty}(G)
is abelian.

Lemma 13.2
.

Suppose that
D
is almost abelian in
G
.

(a)

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
and
E\leq H\cap D
. Then
E
is almost abelian in
H
.

(b)

For
j=1,2
the groups
D_{j}
are almost abelian in
G_{j}
if and only if
D_{1}\times D_{2}
is almost abelian in
G_{1}\times G_{2}
.

(c)

A subgroup
E
of
G
is almost abelian in
G
if and only if
E\operatorname{Z}(G)/\operatorname{Z}(G)
is almost abelian in
G/\operatorname{Z}(G)
.

Proof.

Let us first prove part (a). By assumption, there is an
i
such that
D\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)/\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)
is abelian. By induction one easily shows that
\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)\cap H\lhd\operatorname{Z}_{i}(H)
. From this we deduce that
\frac{(D\cap H)\operatorname{Z}_{i}(H)}{\operatorname{Z}_{i}(H)}
is abelian. Therefore,
D\cap H
is almost abelian in
H
. Consequently,
E\leq D\cap H
is almost abelian as well.

For part (b) we observe that
\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G_{1}\times G_{2})=\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G_{1})\times\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G_{2})
. Thus,

\frac{(D_{1}\times D_{2})\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G_{1}\times G_{2})}{\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G_{1}\times G_{2})}\cong\frac{D_{1}\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)}{\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)}\times\frac{D_{2}\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)}{\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)}

and the claim follows from this.

For part (c) one first shows by induction that
\operatorname{Z}_{i-1}(G/\operatorname{Z}(G))=\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G)/Z(G)
for all
i
. Hence,
D\operatorname{Z}(G)/\operatorname{Z}(G)
is almost abelian in

if and only if there exists an
i
such that

\frac{\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G/\operatorname{Z}(G))D\operatorname{Z}(G)/\operatorname{Z}(G)}{\operatorname{Z}_{i}(G/\operatorname{Z}(G))}\cong\frac{\operatorname{Z}_{i+1}(G)D}{\operatorname{Z}_{i+1}(G)}

is abelian. From this is follows that

is almost abelian in

if and only if

is almost abelian in
G
.
∎

.

Assume therefore that

Proposition 14.6

Let

\mathbf{G}
. There exist a Frobenius endomorphism

14.3.
The inductive Alperin–McKay condition for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

Proposition 14.7
.

The iAM-condition holds for the principal
2
-block of
\mathbf{G}^{F}=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
relative to its defect group.

Proof.

By
[
6
]
we can assume that
\mathbf{G}^{F}
has non-exceptional Schur multiplier.
We follow the proof (and notation) of Proposition
14.6
. Using
[
11
, Theorem 21.14]
it’s easy to see that the principal block
b
is the unique
2
-block of maximal defect. Hence,
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(b)=\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B)=\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(D))
, where
B
is the Brauer correspondent of

.

We place ourselves in the situation of
[
18
, Section 15]
, where it was shown that
S=G/\mathrm{Z}(G)
is McKay-good. More precisely, it was shown that there exists an intermediate subgroup
\operatorname{N}_{G}(D)\subset H
and an
\mathrm{Aut}(G)_{H}
-equivariant bijection
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\to\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(H)
. Modifying their bijection we obtain an
\mathrm{Aut}(G)_{D}
-equivariant bijection
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(G,b)\to\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(D),B)

2^{\prime}
-character of
G
respectively
\operatorname{N}_{G}(D)
has the
2

\operatorname{Z}(G)
in its kernel. One then checks that all characters of
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(S,\overline{b})
extend to their inertia group in

(the stabilizer is either cyclic or the characters are unipotent). Similarly, one also checks that the characters of
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\operatorname{N}_{S}(\overline{D}),\overline{B})
extend to their inertia group: If

is self-normalizing then every character in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\operatorname{N}_{S}(\overline{D}),\overline{B})
is linear and one can check the claim by explicit computations. Otherwise, both characters of
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\mathrm{N}_{G}(D),B)
with non-cyclic inertia group in

are unipotent characters of
\operatorname{N}_{G}(D)\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(3)
and their extension to the inertia group follows again from explicit computations. Now using similar arguments as in Proposition
14.6
shows that the block

∎

14.4.
Groups with exceptional Schur multiplier

After having checked the iAM-condition for all quasi-isolated
2
-blocks of groups of Lie type with almost abelian defect group in non-defining characteristic we are left to show the iAM-condition for the remaining simple groups and blocks under consideration.

Proposition 14.8
.

Suppose that
S
is a simple group not of Lie type in odd characteristic with exceptional Schur multiplier. Then the inductive Alperin–McKay condition holds for all
2
-blocks with almost abelian defect group of the universal covering group
X
of
S
.

Proof.

Observe first that the iAM-condition holds for all simple sporadic groups by
[
6
]
. Assume that
S
is a simple group of Lie type in characteristic different from
2
with exceptional Schur multiplier and let
X
be its universal covering group. Then
S
is one of the following groups:
A_{1}(9)
,
{}^{2}A_{3}(3)
,
B_{3}(3)
or
G_{2}(3)
. According to
[
6
]
both
A_{1}(9)\cong A_{6}
and
G_{2}(3)
are AM-good. Observe that the blocks of the universal covering groups of
{}^{2}A_{3}(3)
and
B_{3}(3)
with maximal defect have a defect group which is not almost abelian. Recall that every defect group of
X
contains
\mathrm{Z}(X)_{\ell}
. An inspection of the blocks of the universal covering group of
{}^{2}A_{3}(3)
using
[
7
]
now shows that all
2
-blocks with almost abelian defect groups have central defect. Therefore, there is nothing to check in this case. For the universal covering group of
B_{3}(3)
the same arguments easily rule out all blocks except the blocks denoted by
2
,
3
,
8
and
9
in
[
7
]
.

We claim that the blocks
2
,
8
and
9
of
X
have a defect group which is not almost abelian. For this let
b
be any of these blocks. Consider a central extension
X\to X^{\prime}
whose kernel is of order
2
. According to
[
31
, Theorem 9.10]
we obtain a bijection
b\to\overline{b}
between blocks of
X
and
X^{\prime}
. The block of
X^{\prime}
corresponding to
b
under this bijection has according to
[
31
, Theorem 9.10]
a defect group of order
8
and still two Brauer characters. The groups

and

admit no automorphisms of odd order. By the remarks following

, Theorem 1.30]
we therefore deduce that these groups arise as defect groups of nilpotent blocks only. Moreover, any block with defect group

can’t have exactly two Brauer characters, see

, Theorem 13.1]
. Since the block

is not nilpotent (it has

Brauer characters) its defect group is therefore not abelian. Since

is a
3
-cover of

, we deduce that the block

cannot have an almost abelian defect group.

It therefore remains to consider the block labeled

in
[
7
]
. This block can however be consider as a block of the

-cover of

, i.e. as a block of
\mathbf{G}^{F}
, and can therefore be treated as a block with non-exceptional covering group. Using the reduction theorem in
[

we can thus conclude that this block is AM-good.

For alternating groups the inductive AM condition is known to hold by the main result of
[
13
]
and
[
39
, Corollary 8.3]
. By
[
25
]
the iAM-condition holds for Suzuki and Ree groups.

Let us finally assume that

is a simple group of Lie type defined over a field of characteristic

. Let
\mathbf{G}^{F}
be such that

be the universal covering group of

whose kernel is the Sylow

-group

of

. According to
[
31
, Theorem 9.10]
we obtain a bijection

between blocks of
\hat{G}
and
\mathbf{G}^{F}
which maps blocks with almost abelian defect to each other.

It is known (see
[

, Theorem 6.18]
) that the only

-blocks of
\mathbf{G}^{F}
are blocks of maximal defect and blocks of height zero. We must therefore consider the cases where a Sylow

-subgroup of

is abelian. This is however precisely the case when

6

we know that

is AM-good whenver

has an exceptional Schur multiplier. Using the properties of the bijection constructed in
[

in conjunction with

shows that the principal block of

∎

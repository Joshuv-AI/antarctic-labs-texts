Tensorization of p p -weak Differentiable structures
Sylvester Eriksson-Bique, Tapio Rajala, Elefterios Soultanis

Abstract
. We consider p p -weak differentiable structures that were recently introduced in [ 9 ] , and prove that the product of p p -weak charts is a p p -weak chart. This implies that the product of two spaces with a p p -weak differentiable structure also admits a p p -weak differentiable structure. We make partial progress on the tensorization problem of Sobolev spaces by showing an isometric embedding result. Further, we establish tensorization when one of the factors is PI.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

A weak notion of differentiable charts in singular metric spaces first arose in Cheeger’s seminal paper
[
7
]
.
Lipschitz differentiability charts
, or Cheeger charts, have since become ubiquitous in analysis in metric spaces, with connections to rectifiability, (non-)embedding results and other topics in geometric measure theory. A Cheeger chart
(U,\varphi)
consists of a Borel set
U\subset X
and a Lipschitz map
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that every
f\in\mathrm{LIP}(X)
admits a differential
x\mapsto\mathrm{d}_{x}f\colon U\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
, uniquely determined for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in U
, satisfying

(1.1)

f(y)-f(x)=\mathrm{d}_{x}f((\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))+o(d(x,y)).

Cheeger charts describe the infinitesimal behaviour of Lipschitz functions but are not well suited for studying Sobolev functions in the absence of additional assumptions. In
[
9
]
the first and third named authors developed
p
-weak charts
, a further weakening of Cheeger charts. We refer to Section 2 for their definition and mention here that
p
-weak charts control the behaviour of Sobolev functions curvewise and exist under very mild assumptions, e.g. when the underlying space has finite Hausdorff dimension. While weaker than the notion of Cheeger charts, the existence of non-trivial
p
-weak charts guarantees the existence of non-negligible families of curves, and induces a pointwise norm given as the essential supremum of directional derivatives along these curves. Indeed, Sobolev functions admit a
p
-weak differential with respect to
p
-weak charts, and the minimal upper gradient is recovered as the pointwise norm of the differential.

In this paper we consider products of spaces admitting a
p
-weak differentiable structure and prove that they likewise admit a
p
-weak differentiable structure.

1.2.
Statement of main results

Let
X=(X,d_{X},\mu)
and
Y=(Y,d_{Y},\nu)
be two metric measure spaces, i.e. complete separable metric spaces equipped with Radon measures that are finite on balls. Throughout this paper we equip the product space
X\times Y
with the product measure
\mu\times\nu
and metric

(1.2)

d((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\|(d_{X}(x,x^{\prime}),d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime}))\|,\quad(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in X\times Y,

where
\|\cdot\|
is a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Note that the behaviour of the norm on the first quadrant determines the metric. In the theorem below we denote by

\|(x,y)\|^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max\{|ax+by|:\|(a,b)\|=1,\ a,b\geq 0\},\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

the
partial dual norm
of a given planar norm
\|\cdot\|
. While
\|\cdot\|^{\prime}\leq\|\cdot\|^{*}
in general, the equality
\|\cdot\|^{\prime}=\|\cdot\|^{*}
holds for

-norms (and more generally norms satisfying
\|(a,b)\|=\|(|a|,|b|)\|
for
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
). Roughly speaking the need for the partial (rather than the “full”) dual norm comes from the fact that the metric speed of product curves does not distinguish between the direction in which each of the component curves is traversed, see estimate (
4.3
) in the proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
(U,\varphi)
and
(V,\psi)
are
p
-weak charts of dimension
N
and
M
in
X
and
Y
, respectively. Then
(U\times V,\varphi\times\psi)
is an
(N+M)
-dimensional
p
-weak chart for
X\times Y
. The local norm on
(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}\times(\mathbb{R}^{M})^{*}\equiv(\mathbb{R}^{N+M})^{*}
is given by

\displaystyle|(\xi_{X},\xi_{Y})|_{(x,y)}=\|(|\xi_{X}|_{x},|\xi_{Y}|_{y})\|^{\prime},\quad(\xi_{X},\xi_{Y})\in(\mathbb{R}^{N+M})^{*},

for
\mu\times\nu
-almost every
(x,y)\in X\times Y
.

Theorem
1.1
yields the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two metric measure spaces which admit a
p
-weak differentiable structure. Then
X\times Y
with the product metric (
1.2
) admits a
p
-weak differentiable structure.

In proving Theorem
1.1
we will use a characterization of
p
-weak charts in terms of existence and uniqueness of differentials in the spirit of (
1.1
). In the following definition, let
U\subset X
be a Borel set with
\mu(U)>0
and
\varphi\in N^{1,p}_{loc}(X;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Definition 1.3
.

A Borel map
\bm{\xi}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a
p
-weak differential of a function
f\in N^{1,p}(X)
with respect to
(U,\varphi)
, if

(1.3)

\displaystyle(f\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}=\bm{\xi}_{\gamma_{t}}((\varphi\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}),\quad a.e.\ t\in\gamma^{-1}(U)

for
p
-a.e. curve
\gamma
in
X
.

Note that there is no uniqueness condition imposed in the definition above. We say that
f\in N^{1,p}(X)
admits a
unique

p
-weak differential with respect to
(U,\varphi)
, if the map
\bm{\xi}
in (
1.3
) is unique in the following sense: if
\bm{\xi}^{\prime}\colon U\to(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
is another Borel map satisfying (
1.3
) for
p
-a.e.
\gamma
, then
\bm{\xi}=\bm{\xi}^{\prime}

\mu
-a.e. on
U
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
U\subset X
be a Borel set with
\mu(U)>0
and
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
a Lipschitz map. The following are equivalent.

(1)

(U,\varphi)
is a
p
-weak chart;

(2)

every
f\in N^{1,p}(X)
admits a unique differential
\mathrm{d}f\colon U\to(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
with respect to
(U,\varphi)
;

1.3.
Application: Tensorization problem of Sobolev spaces

We use Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
to make partial progress on the “tensorization problem” for Sobolev spaces. The tensorization problem first appeared in
[
4
]
and was formulated using Sobolev spaces defined via test plans, which we denote here by
W^{1,p}
, see Definition
2.4
. It has later been investigated e.g. in
[
5
]
. Given
p\geq 1
, the
Beppo–Levi
space
J^{1,p}(X,Y)
consists of Borel functions
f\in L^{p}(X\times Y)
, which satisfy the following:

(a1)

For
\mu_{X}
-a.e.
x\in X
,
f_{x}:=f(x,\cdot)\in W^{1,p}(Y)
;

(a2)

For
\mu_{Y}
-a.e.
y\in Y
,
f^{y}:=f(\cdot,y)\in W^{1,p}(X)
, and

(a3)

\displaystyle\int_{X\times Y}(|Df^{y}|_{p}^{p}(x)+|Df_{x}|_{p}^{p}(y))\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\nu(y)<\infty.

We refer to the Appendix for the measurability of the integrand in (a3). Observe that, although the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}
and
N^{1,p}
are isometrically isomorphic, it is not trivial that one can replace
W^{1,p}
by
N^{1,p}
in (a1) and (a2), a point we address in Section 5. The space
J^{1,p}(X,Y)
equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{J^{1,p}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(\|f\|_{L^{p}}+[f]_{J^{1,p}}^{p})^{1/p}
is a Banach space, where
[f]_{J^{1,p}}
is the seminorm

(1.4)

[f]_{J^{1,p}}=\left(\int_{X\times Y}(\|(|Df^{y}|_{p}(x),|Df_{x}|_{p}(y))\|^{\prime})^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\right)^{1/p}.

The “tensorization problem” of Sobolev spaces asks whether or not the equality

(1.5)

W^{1,p}(X\times Y)=J^{1,p}(X,Y)

holds. Roughly speaking this amounts to asking whether knowledge of the directional derivatives in the
X
and
Y
directions is enough to ensure Sobolev regularity in
X\times Y
. While the inclusion
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)\subset J^{1,p}(X,Y)
is elementary not much else is known without additional assumptions.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
X
and
Y
admit a
p
-weak differentiable structure and let
f\in N^{1,p}(X\times Y)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}f_{(x,y)}=(\mathrm{d}_{x}f^{y},\mathrm{d}_{y}f_{x})

and

\displaystyle|Df|_{p}(x,y)=\|(|Df^{y}|_{p}(x),|Df_{x}|_{p}(y))\|^{\prime}

for
\mu\times\nu
-a.e.
(x,y)
. In particular, the embedding
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)\subset J^{1,p}(X,Y)
is isometric.

Theorem
1.5
leaves open the question of equality in (
1.5
) but shows that the elementary inclusion
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)\subset J^{1,p}(X,Y)
is isometric (e.g. when the spaces are finite dimensional), providing partial evidence in favour of the tensorization property.

While a full solution to the tensorization problem remains open, we are able to establish (
1.5
) under the additional assumption that one of the factors is a PI-space. Recall that a
p
-PI space is a complete doubling metric measure space supporting a weak
p
-Poincaré inequality, cf.
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
X
is a
p
-PI-space and
Y
admits a
p
-weak differential structure. Then
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)=J^{1,p}(X,Y)
and the norms coincide.

Previously the same conclusion was (essentially) known to hold if
both
factors are PI-spaces, see
[
5
, Theorem 3.4]
for a proof in the case
p=2
.

2.1.
Product spaces

Let
X
and
Y
be complete separable metric spaces, and
\|\cdot\|
a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Throughout this paper we will use the product metric on
X\times Y
defined by

\displaystyle d((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\|(d_{X}(x,x^{\prime}),d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime}))\|,\quad(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in X\times Y.

Given an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)
in

\displaystyle|(\alpha,\beta)_{t}^{\prime}|=\|(|\alpha_{t}^{\prime}|,|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|)\|\quad a.e.\ t,

where

and
|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|

\alpha
and
\beta

and

Horizontal
curves are curves whose

H([0,1];X\times Y)
. Similarly,
vertical
curves have constant
X
-component, and their collection is denoted
V([0,1];X\times Y)
. In Sections 5 and 6 we will need the notion of
horizontal-vertical
curves which are obtained as concatenations of horizontal and vertical curves. The formal definition is given below.

Definition 2.1
.

A curve
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)\in AC([0,1];X\times Y)
is called a
hv-curve
if there exists a partition of
[0,1]
given as
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}=1
so that for every
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}

either
\alpha
or
\beta
is constant in
[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
. We denote the set of hv-curves by
HV([0,1];X\times Y)

Given a HV-curve
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)
its constant speed parametrization
\bar{\gamma}=(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta})
is also a HV-curve with the additional property that
\bar{\gamma}|_{I}
is non-constant on any open interval
I\subset[0,1]
unless
\gamma
is a constant curve. For (non-constant)
\bar{\gamma}
we may find a decomposition
0<t_{1}\ldots<t_{n}<1
, with either
\alpha
or
\beta
constant on each
[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
, which is maximal in the following sense: for any

and open

, both
\bar{\alpha}|_{I}
and
\bar{\beta}|_{I}
are non-constant (i.e. none of the intervals
[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
can be enlarged while keeping one of the component curves constant). By convention constant curves have an empty decomposition.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a curve
\gamma
, we call the unique decomposition
\bm{t}(\gamma)=\{t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}\}
as above the collection of turning times of
\gamma
. For

, we denote by

the subset of

consisting of curves
\gamma
such that the constant speed parametrization
\bar{\gamma}
has exactly
n

2.2.
Partial upper gradients

We refer the reader to
[
11
,
6
]
for a good account of modulus, line integrals along curves, weak upper gradients, and Newton-Sobolev spaces, and omit their definitions here.

Let

be a family of curves in a metric measure space

. We say that a Borel function

is an upper gradient of a Borel function

along

(2.1)

|f(\gamma_{t})-f(\gamma_{s})|\leq\int_{\gamma|_{[s,t]}}g\mathrm{d}s,\quad 0\leq s<t\leq 1

\gamma\in\Gamma
. We moreover say that

is a

-weak upper gradient along

if (

p
-a.e. curve

. If

we say that

is an upper gradient (resp.

-weak upper gradient) of

We record the following result which establishes the existence of minimal weak partial upper gradients.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma\subset AC([0,1];X)
be a family of curves and

a locally integrable function which admits a
p
-weak upper gradient

along
\Gamma
. Then there exists a

p
-weak upper gradient

of

along
\Gamma
.

Minimality in the claim above is intended in the sense that (a)
|Df|_{p,\Gamma}
is a
p
-weak upper gradient of
f
along
\Gamma
, and (b)
|Df|_{p,\Gamma}\leq g
for any locally
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradient

of
f
.

Proof.

By Fuglede’s Lemma, the family of partial weak upper gradients of
f
is closed under (local)
L^{p}
-convergence. The collection of partial weak upper gradients of

has the lattice property by argument in the proof of
[
11

. The existence of a minimal element in the lattice follows as in
[
11
, Theorem 6.3.20]
.

2.4.

\bm{\bar{\pi}}:=|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\bar{\eta}}
satisfies

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{(f\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}}{|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\bm{\pi}_{x})}=\left\|\frac{(f\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}}{|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\bm{\bar{\pi}}_{x})}\quad\bm{\eta}^{\#}-a.e.\ x\in X,\quad f\in N^{1,p}(X).

Proof.

First we obtain a plan with parametric barycenter in
L^{\infty}
by a suitable parametrization of
\bm{\eta}
-a.e. curve (see also
[
1
, Theorem 8.5]
). Note that if
q=\infty
the
q
-plan
\bm{\eta}
already has parametric barycenter in
L^{\infty}
and thus we assume that
q<\infty
.

Let
\bm{\eta}_{1}:=\frac{\ell}{1+\ell}\bm{\eta}_{l}
, where
\bm{\eta}_{l}=l_{*}\bm{\eta}
(with

the constant speed parametrization map). Observe that
\bm{\eta}^{\#}\ll(\bm{\eta}_{1})^{\#}\leq\bm{\eta}^{\#}
. The
parametric barycenter

\nu_{1}:=e_{\ast}(\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{1})
satisfies
\bm{\eta}^{\#}\ll\nu_{1}\leq\bm{\eta}^{\#}
so that
\bm{\eta}_{1}
has parametric barycenter in
L^{q}
. Denote by
\rho_{1}
the density of
\nu_{1}
with respect to
\mu
and set

h_{1}=\frac{1}{\max\{1,\rho_{1}\}}.

For
\gamma\in AC([0,1];X)
, define

\displaystyle b_{\gamma}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}h_{1}(\gamma_{t})\mathrm{d}t,\quad\tau_{\gamma}(s)=\frac{b_{\gamma}(s)}{b_{\gamma}(1)},\quad s\in[0,1].

For
\bm{\eta}_{1}
-a.e.
\gamma
we have that
0<b_{\gamma}(1)\leq 1
and
(\tau_{\gamma})^{\prime}(s)=\frac{h_{1}(\gamma_{s})}{b_{\gamma}(1)}>0
a.e.
s\in[0,1]
. For such
\gamma
it follows that the inverse
\sigma_{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
of
\tau_{\gamma}
is absolutely continuous. Moreover, the maps
\gamma\mapsto b_{\gamma}
and
H:=\gamma\mapsto\gamma\circ\sigma_{\gamma}
are Borel, cf.
[
1
, Lemma 8.4]
.

Define
\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2}:=\frac{b_{\gamma}(1)}{(1+\ell(\gamma))^{q}}H_{\ast}(\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{1})
. By Lemma
A.2

\bm{\eta}_{2}^{\#}
and
\bm{\eta}_{1}^{\#}
are mutually absolutely continuous since
\frac{b_{\gamma}(1)}{(1+\ell(\gamma))^{q}}>0

\bm{\eta}_{1}
-a.e.. For
\bm{\eta}_{1}
-a.e.
\gamma
and any Borel map

we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g((\gamma\circ\sigma_{\gamma})_{t})\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}g(\gamma_{s})(\tau_{\gamma})^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{b_{\gamma}(1)}\int_{0}^{1}g(\gamma_{s})h_{1}(\gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}s.

Thus,
\nu_{2}:=e_{\ast}(\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2})
satisfies

\displaystyle\int g\mathrm{d}\nu_{2}\leq\int\int_{0}^{1}g((\gamma\circ\sigma_{\gamma})_{t})\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{1}=\int\int_{0}^{1}g(\gamma_{s})h_{1}(\gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{1}=\int gh_{1}\rho_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu.

It follows that
\mathrm{d}\nu_{2}\leq h_{1}\rho_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu
. In particular
\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
\nu_{1}\ll\nu_{2}\ll\nu_{1}
. Similarly, for
\bm{\eta}_{1}
-a.e.

we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|(\gamma\circ\sigma_{\gamma})^{\prime}_{t}|^{q}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sigma_{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)^{q}|\gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma_{\gamma}(t)}|^{q}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{1}\sigma_{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)^{q-1}|\gamma_{s}^{\prime}|^{q}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=b_{\gamma}(1)^{q-1}\int 1\vee\rho_{1}^{q-1}(\gamma_{s})|\gamma_{s}^{\prime}|^{q}\mathrm{d}s=[b_{\gamma}(1)\ell(\gamma)]^{q}\int_{0}^{1}1\vee\rho_{1}^{q-1}(\gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}s.

Thus,

\displaystyle E_{q}(\bm{\eta}_{2})=

\displaystyle\int\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|^{q}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2}=\int\left(\frac{b_{\gamma}(1)\ell(\gamma)}{1+\ell(\gamma)}\right)^{q}\int_{0}^{1}(1\vee\rho_{1})^{q-1}(\gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{1}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{1}+\int\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{1}^{q-1}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{1}=\bm{\eta}_{1}(C([0,1];X))+\int\rho_{1}^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu<\infty.

We conclude that
\bm{\eta}_{2}
is a plan with finite
q
-energy and its parametric barycenter
\mathrm{d}\nu_{2}=:\rho_{2}\mathrm{d}\mu
satisfies
\rho_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
\bm{\eta}^{\#}\ll\nu_{2}\ll\bm{\eta}^{\#}
. A repeated application of Lemma
A.2
implies that

(2.5)

\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((\bm{\pi}_{2})_{x})}=\|G\|_{L^{\infty}(\bm{\pi}_{x})}\quad\bm{\eta}^{\#}-a.e.\ x\in X

for every
G
satisfying (
A.2
).

We now modify
\bm{\eta}_{2}
to obtain a test plan with the desired properties (cf.
[
1
, Theorem 9.4]
). Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and given

, let

\displaystyle\bm{\eta}_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}r_{\tau\ast}\bm{\eta}_{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.

We claim that
\bm{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
is a test plan. Indeed,

\displaystyle E_{q}(\bm{\eta}_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int\int_{0}^{1}|(\gamma\circ r_{\tau})^{\prime}_{t}|^{q}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2}\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int\int_{\tau}^{1+\tau-\varepsilon}(1-\varepsilon)^{q-1}|\gamma_{s}^{\prime}|^{q}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\leq E_{q}(\bm{\eta}_{2}),

proving that

has finite

and

we may calculate

\displaystyle\int_{X}ge_{t\ast}(\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int g((\gamma\circ r_{\tau})_{t})\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2}\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}g(\gamma(\tau+(1-\varepsilon)t))\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\int_{(1-\varepsilon)t}^{\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)t}g(\gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{X}g\rho_{2}\mathrm{d}\mu.

It remains to show that

2.3
) and (

) (for any choice of

). Denote

. For any Borel

\displaystyle\int G\mathrm{d}\bm{\pi}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int\int_{0}^{1}G(\gamma\circ r_{\tau},t)|(\gamma\circ r_{\tau})_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int\int_{\tau}^{1+\tau-\varepsilon}G(\gamma\circ r_{\tau},r_{\tau}^{-1}(s))|\gamma_{s}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int R_{\varepsilon}G\mathrm{d}\bm{\pi}_{2},

where

\displaystyle R_{\varepsilon}G(\gamma,s):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\chi_{[\tau,1-\varepsilon+\tau]}(s)G(\gamma\circ r_{\tau},r_{\tau}^{-1}(s))\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\chi_{[s-(1-\varepsilon),s]}(\tau)G(\gamma\circ r_{\tau},r_{\tau}^{-1}(s))\mathrm{d}\tau.

If

satisfies (
A.2
) for all

,

, we obtain

\displaystyle R_{\varepsilon}G(\gamma,s)=m_{\varepsilon}(s)G(\gamma,s),\quad m_{\varepsilon}(s)=\frac{|[0,\varepsilon]\cap[s-(1-\varepsilon),s]|}{\varepsilon}.

In particular, choosing

for arbitrary Borel

, we obtain
\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{\#}=\lambda_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{2}^{\#}
where
\displaystyle\lambda_{\varepsilon}(x):=\int m_{\varepsilon}(s)\mathrm{d}\bm{(}\pi_{2})_{x}>0
for
\bm{\eta}_{2}^{\#}
-a.e.

. This proves (
2.3
).

The disintegration

of
\bm{\pi}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int G\mathrm{d}(\bm{\pi}_{\varepsilon})_{x}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(x)}\int R_{\varepsilon}G\mathrm{d}(\bm{\pi}_{2})_{x}\quad\bm{\eta}^{\#}-a.e.\ x\in X.

Thus, for
G
satisfying (
A.2
) for all
\{r_{\tau}\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]\}_{\tau\in[0,\varepsilon]}
we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int G^{m}\mathrm{d}(\bm{\pi}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\right)^{1/m}=\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(x)}\int m_{\varepsilon}(s)G^{m}\mathrm{d}(\bm{\pi}_{2})_{x}\right)^{1/m}\quad\textrm{for all }m\in\mathbb{N}\quad\bm{\eta}^{\#}-a.e.\ x\in X

and letting

we obtain

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((\bm{\pi}_{\varepsilon})_{x})}=\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((\bm{\pi}_{2})_{x})}\quad\bm{\eta}^{\#}-a.e.\ x\in X.

Finally, given

consider
\displaystyle G(\gamma,s)=\frac{(f\circ\gamma)_{s}^{\prime}}{|\gamma_{s}^{\prime}|}
and observe that

satisfies (

) for every absolutely continuous injection

. Now combining (

. ∎

3.
Existence of
p
-weak differentials

We lay some groundwork for the proof of Theorem
1.4
. Fix a pair
(U,\varphi)
, and let
\Phi\colon X\times(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}\to[0,\infty]
canonically represent the gradient of
\varphi
. For
\mu
-a.e.
x\in U
we denote by

|\xi|_{x}=\Phi(x,\xi),\quad\xi\in(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}

the pointwise seminorm, and write
W_{x}:=(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}/\{\xi:|\xi|_{x}=0\}
. We also let
L_{x}\colon W_{x}\to(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
denote the right inverse of the canonical projection map
[\cdot]\colon(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}\to W_{x}
, given by sending each
[\xi]\in W_{x}
(which is an affine subspace of
(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
) to the unique vector
\zeta\in[\xi]
with smallest Euclidean norm. Note that
|\cdot|_{x}
is a norm on
{\rm Im}(L_{x})
.

Define the vector space
\Gamma_{p}(T^{*}U)
as the set of Borel maps
\bm{\xi}\colon U\to(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
with
\bm{\xi}(x)\in{\rm Im}(L_{x})

\mu
-a.e. such that

\displaystyle\|\bm{\xi}\|_{\Gamma_{p}(U)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left(\int_{U}|\bm{\xi}|\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/p}

is finite, with the usual identification of elements that agree
\mu
-a.e. It is a standard exercise to show that
\|\cdot\|_{\Gamma_{p}}
is a norm making
\Gamma_{p}(T^{*}U)
a Banach space. Moreover we let
\Gamma_{p,loc}(T^{*}U)
the collection of all Borel maps
\bm{\xi}:U\to(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
so that
\bm{\xi}|_{K}\in\Gamma_{p}(T^{*}K)
for all compact
K\subset U
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
\bm{\xi}\colon U\to(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
is a
p
-weak differential of
f\in N^{1,p}(X)
with respect to
(U,\varphi)
. Then

(1)

\bm{\xi}^{\prime}:=L[\bm{\xi}]
is a
p
-weak differential of
f
with respect to
(U,\varphi)
;

(2)

We have that
|\bm{\xi}|=|Df|_{p}

\mu
-a.e. on
U
.

Proof.

Let
g_{f}
be a Borel representative of
|Df|_{p}
and define the Borel function
g=\chi_{U}|\bm{\xi}|+\chi_{X\setminus U}g_{f}
. The identity (
1.3
) implies that

|\bm{\xi}((\varphi\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime})|\leq g_{f}(\gamma_{t})|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|\quad a.e.\ t\in\gamma^{-1}(U)

for
p
-a.e. curve. By the definition of
\Phi
this yields
|\bm{\xi}(x)|_{x}\leq g_{f}(x)

\mu
-a.e.
x\in U
. Thus
g\leq g_{f}
. The identity (
1.3
) also yields that for
p
-a.e. curve
\gamma
we have

|(f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}_{t}|=|\bm{\xi}_{\gamma(t)}((\varphi\circ\gamma)^{\prime}_{t})|\leq g(\gamma_{t})|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|

for a.e.
t\in\gamma^{-1}(U)
. Thus
g
is a
p
-weak upper gradient, and
g\geq g_{f}
and (2) follows. To prove (1) note that, since
|\bm{\xi}^{\prime}-\bm{\xi}|=0

\mu
-a.e. on
U
by definition, it follows that
\bm{\xi}((\varphi\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}))=\bm{\xi}^{\prime}((\varphi\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}))
a.e.
t\in\gamma^{-1}(U)
for
p
-a.e.
\gamma
, cf.
[
9
, Lemma 4.3(2)]
. The claim in (1) follows directly from this.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\in N^{1,p}(X)
. Assume that there exist
f_{i}\in N^{1,p}(X)
,
C_{i}\subset X
, for each
i\in I
in a countable index set
I
such that (1)
f_{i}
admits a
p
-weak differential with respect to
(U,\varphi)
and
f|_{C_{i}}=f_{i}|_{C_{i}}
for each
i\in I
, and (2)
\displaystyle\mu\left(U\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}C_{i}\right)=0
. Then
f
admits a
p
-weak differential with respect to
(U,\varphi)
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{\xi}_{i}
be a
p
-weak differential of
f_{i}
for each
i\in I
. Identify
I
with a subset of
\mathbb{N}
and define the sets

W_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=U\cap C_{j}\setminus\bigcup_{i<j}C_{i}.

Let
J
be the set of
j\in I
for which
\mu(W_{j})>0
. Then
\{W_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is a partition of
U
up to a null-set and
f|_{W_{j}}=f_{j}|_{W_{j}}
for all
j\in J
. We claim that

\bm{\xi}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{j\in J}\chi_{W_{j}}\bm{\xi}_{j}\colon U\to(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}

is a
p
-weak differential of
f
with respect to
(U,\varphi)
. Indeed, note that
p
-a.e. curve
\gamma
in
X
has the following properties:

(i)

f\circ\gamma,\ \varphi\circ\gamma
and
f_{j}\circ\gamma
are absolutely continuous for all
j\in J
;

(ii)

almost every
t\in\gamma^{-1}(U)
is a density point of
\gamma^{-1}(W_{j})
for some
j
;

(iii)

(f_{j}\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}=(\bm{\xi}_{j})_{\gamma(t)}((\varphi\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime})
a.e.
t\in\gamma^{-1}(U)
.

For any such
\gamma
we have that

\displaystyle(f\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}=(f_{j}\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}=(\bm{\xi}_{j})_{\gamma(t)}((\varphi\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime})=\bm{\xi}_{\gamma(t)}((\varphi\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime})

for some
j
, for almost every
t\in\gamma^{-1}(U)
. This proves the claim.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Lemma

shows in particular the local nature of
p
-weak differentials: if
V\subset U
and
f_{1}=f_{2}

\mu
-a.e. on

, then a

-weak differential

of

with respect to

is a
p
-weak differential of
f_{2}
with respect to
(V,\varphi)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
(f_{j})\subset N^{1,p}_{loc}(X)
is a sequence such that
f_{j}\to f
in

and
(|Df_{j}|_{p})_{j}
is equi-integrable. If

has a

has a

-weak differential with respect to

.

For each

, let

be a

-weak differential of

. By Lemma

we may assume that

the sequence

and

in

, respectively, in norm. Now we may argue as in the proof of

(using Fuglede’s lemma) to conclude that the identities

for

-a.e.

. This proves that

is a

-weak differential of

with respect to
(U,\varphi)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The proof above shows that any weak limit in

of a sequence of

-weak differentials of the

’s is a

-weak differential of

.

We close this section by proving Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

If
(U,\varphi)
is a
p
-weak chart, the existence of
p
-weak differentials of Newton–Sobolev functions is proved in
[
9
, Theorem 1.7]
, and their uniqueness follows from
p
-independence and Theorem
2.9
(c). The implication (2)
\implies
(3) is trivial. Thus it suffices to prove (3)

(1).

Assume that every

admits a unique

-weak differential with respect to
(U,\varphi)
. It follows that
(U,\varphi)
is
p
-independent. Indeed, let
\Phi
represent the gradient of
\varphi
canonically, cf. Theorem
2.9
. Since any Borel map
\bm{\xi}\colon U\to(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
with
\bm{\xi}_{x}\in\ker\Phi^{x}
is a

-weak differential of the zero function, the uniqueness of

-weak differentials with respect to

implies that
\ker\Phi^{x}=\{0\}

\mu
-a.e.

. Theorem

(d) implies that
(U,\varphi)
is
p
-independent.

It remains to show that
(U,\varphi)
is
p
-maximal. Suppose that
V\subset U
has positive measure and that
\psi\in{\rm LIP}(X;\mathbb{R}^{M})
is

-independent on

. We will show that

. Let
\mathrm{d}\psi_{i}\in(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
be the unique

-weak differentials of the components

of

for

. To reach a contradiction assume

. Then
\mathrm{d}\psi_{i}
are linearly dependent, and there are Borel functions

so that

, with
\mathbf{a}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{M})\neq 0

\mu
-a.e. on

. But, then for

-a.e. absolutely continuous

and a.e.

By Theorem

(c) this implies that

for a.e.

, where

canonically represents the gradient of

. By Theorem

(d) this is a contradiction to

-independence.
∎

4.
Products of charts and tensorization

4.1.

Throughout this section we fix

-weak charts

and

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

,

,

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

and

\psi

Proof.

Lemma 5.4

Let

(5.2)

\displaystyle\bm{\eta}^{\#}=(\bm{\eta}|_{[0,s]})^{\#}+(\bm{\eta}|_{[s,1]})^{\#}.

Proof.

For any bounded Borel function

\displaystyle\int_{X}g\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}^{\#}

\displaystyle=\int\int_{0}^{1}g(\gamma_{t})|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}

\displaystyle=\int\left[\int_{0}^{s}g(\gamma_{t})|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t+\int_{s}^{1}g(\gamma_{t})|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t\right]\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}

∎

6.
Tensorization when one factor is a PI-space

In this section we prove Theorem
1.6
. Our proof uses a characterization of the tensorization property in terms of (weak) approximation properties. Specifically, we use the doubling property and Poincaré inequality to construct Lipschitz approximants of a Beppo–Levi function satisfying (3) in Proposition
6.1
below.

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume
p\in(1,\infty)
. Suppose that
X
and
Y
admit
p
-weak differentiable structures. If any of the following (equivalent) conditions is satisfied, then
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)=J^{1,p}(X,Y)
.

(1)

Density:

W^{1,p}(X\times Y)
is dense in
J^{1,p}
in norm.

(2)

(Weak) Approximation by Sobolev functions:
For every bounded
f\in J^{1,p}(X,Y)
with bounded support, there exists a constant
C>0
and a sequence of functions
f_{i}\in N^{1,p}(X\times Y)
with
\|f_{i}\|_{N^{1,p}}\leq C
so that
f_{i}
converges to
f
in
L^{p}(X\times Y)
.

(3)

(Weak) Approximation by Lipschitz functions:
For every bounded
f\in J^{1,p}(X,Y)
with bounded support, there is a constant
C>0
and a sequence of Lipschitz functions
f_{i}\in J^{1,p}(X,Y)
with bounded support, so that
\|f_{i}\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C
, and so that
f_{i}
converges to
f
in
L^{p}(X\times Y)
.

Proof.

Since
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)\subset J^{1,p}(X,Y)
is an isometric closed subset, then equality
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)=J^{1,p}(X,Y)
is equivalent to
(1)
.

Clearly
(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
. Further,
(2)\Longleftrightarrow(3)
by density in Energy of Lipschitz functions, for
p\in(1,\infty)
see
[
3
]
(alternative proof in
[
8
]
).

Next, we show that
(3)
implies
(1)
. Suppose
f\in J^{1,p}(X,Y)
.

By Corollary
1.2
,
X\times Y
admits a
p
-weak differential structure. By
[
9
, Corollary 6.7]
, the space
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)
is reflexive. Thus bounded functions with bounded support are dense in
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)
.

Let
f\in J^{1,p}(X,Y)
be bounded and with bounded support. Let
(f_{i})
be a sequence of Lipschitz functions satisfying the conclusion in (3). By reflexivity and Mazur’s Lemma, a convex combination
\tilde{f}_{i}
of a suitable subsequence of
(f_{i})
converges in the norm of
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)
and in
L^{p}(X\times Y)
. Since the inclusion
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(X\times Y)
is injective, and since the sequence also converges in
L^{p}(X\times Y)
to
f
, then the limit in
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)
equals
f
. Therefore,
f\in W^{1,p}(X\times Y)
. The density of
W^{1,p}(X\times Y)
in
J^{1,p}(X,Y)
follows.
∎

To construct Lipschitz approximants in the proof of Theorem

we use a so-called discrete convolution in the

fix an

-net

. That is, fix a set

so that for each

there is a

with

, and for each

we have

For a function

T_{n}f(x)=\sum_{a\in N_{n}}\psi^{n}_{a}(x)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(a,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(a,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(a,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(a,2^{-n})}}fd\mu.

Remark 6.3
.

Indeed, our proof of Theorem

PI-spaces are the only context where such a discrete convolution operators are known to exist. One plausible approach to tensorization of Sobolev spaces would involve constructing such convolutions more generally.

A.1.
Elementary properties of disintegration

We record some elementary properties of disintegrations of plans. In the following statement, the space
ACB([0,1])
consists of absolutely continuous bijections
\sigma\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
with absolutely continuous inverse, and
\{\bm{\pi}_{x}\}
is the disintegration of the measure
\bm{\pi}
given by
\mathrm{d}\bm{\pi}:=|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}
with respect to
e
. The following properties are easy to verify from the definition by direct calculation using the uniqueness of disintegration. Thus we omit the proofs.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
F\colon AC([0,1];X)\to[0,\infty]
be bounded and Borel and denote

f(x):=\int_{e^{-1}(x)}F\mathrm{d}\bm{\pi}_{x}

for
\bm{\eta}^{\#}
-a.e.
x\in X
. Let
\sigma\colon C([0,1];X)\to ACB([0,1])
be Borel and denote

H\colon C([0,1];X)\to C([0,1];X),\quad\gamma\mapsto\gamma\circ\sigma_{\gamma}.

(a)

The plan
\bm{\eta}_{F}:=F\bm{\eta}
and the disintegration
\{(\bm{\pi}_{F})_{x}\}
of
\mathrm{d}\bm{\pi}_{F}:=|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{F}
satisfy

\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{F}^{\#}(x)=f(x)\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}^{\#}(x),\quad(\bm{\pi}_{F})_{x}=\frac{1}{f(x)}F\bm{\pi}_{x}\quad\bm{\eta}^{\#}-a.e.\ x\in\{f>0\}.

(b)

The plan
\bm{\eta}_{H}:=H_{\ast}\bm{\eta}
and the disintegration
\{(\bm{\pi}_{H})_{x}\}
of
\mathrm{d}\bm{\pi}_{H}:=|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}_{H}
satisfy

\bm{\eta}_{H}^{\#}=\bm{\eta}^{\#},\quad(\bm{\pi}_{H})_{x}=(H_{\sigma^{-1}})_{\ast}\bm{\pi}_{x}\quad\bm{\eta}^{\#}-a.e.\ x\in X.

Here
H_{\sigma^{-1}}(\gamma,t):=(H\gamma,\sigma_{\gamma}^{-1}(t))
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
F
and
H
be as in Lemma
A.1
and suppose that
F>0

\bm{\eta}
-a.e. Consider
\bm{\eta}_{F,H}:=FH_{\ast}\bm{\eta}
and the disintegration

of

and

(A.1)

\displaystyle\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((\bm{\pi}_{F,H})_{x})}=\|G\|_{L^{\infty}(\bm{\pi}_{x})}\quad\bm{\eta}^{\#}-a.e.\ x\in X

(A.2)

G(\gamma\circ\sigma_{\gamma},t)=G(\gamma,\sigma_{\gamma}(t))\quad\bm{\pi}-a.e.\ (\gamma,t).

(since

\bm{\eta}^{\#}
-a.e.) and moreover

For any Borel function

\bm{\eta}^{\#}

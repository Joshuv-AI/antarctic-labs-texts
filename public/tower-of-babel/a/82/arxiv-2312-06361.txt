The Picard Group of a Reductive Group over a Number Field
Dylon Chow

Abstract
. We give a description of the Picard group of a reductive group over a number field as an abelianized Galois cohomology group. It gives another approach of a result due to Labesse.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a connected reductive group defined over a number field
F
. This note is concerned with the Picard group
\text{Pic}(G)
of
G
. It is known to be finite. Its Pontryagin dual is also a finite group with the same order. It was identified in a paper by Labesse
[
LBC99
]
as a certain abelian Galois cohomology group. For the notion of abelianized Galois cohomology, we refer the reader to
[
Bor98
]
. The purpose of this note is to give an alternate, more functorial approach to this bijection.

To state the main result of this paper, we introduce some notation. Let
G^{sc}
be the simply connected cover of the derived group of
G
. We consider the natural homomorphism
\rho:G^{sc}\rightarrow G
. Let
T
be a maximal torus in
G
and let
T^{sc}=\rho^{-1}(T)
.

Let
\overline{F}
be an algebraic closure of
F
. For each number field
K
, we let
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}
denote the ring of adeles of
K
. Let
\overline{{\mathbb{A}}}=\varinjlim{\mathbb{A}}_{K}
, the direct limit of the rings
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}
, taken over all the finite Galois extensions of
F
contained in
\overline{F}
. We define the adelic abelian cohomology groups

{\mathbb{H}}^{i}_{ab}({\mathbb{A}}_{F}/F,G):={\mathbb{H}}^{i}(F,T^{sc}(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})/T^{sc}({\overline{F}})\rightarrow T(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})/T({\overline{F}})).

Labesse proved the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Labesse,
[
LBC99
, Prop. 1.7.3]
)
Let
G
be a connected reductive group over a global field
F
. There is a canonical bijection

H^{1}_{ab}({\mathbb{A}}_{F}/F,G)\to\text{Hom}(\text{Pic}(G),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}).

Labesse proved the result in a quite complicated way. He first used the isomorphism constructed by Kottwitz that described the Picard group of
G
in terms of the center
Z(G^{\vee})
of the connected component of the Langlands dual group
G^{\vee}

[
Kot84
]
. Then he used an extension of the Tate-Nakayama theorem (due to Nyssen) that extends an isomorphism due to Langlands on the Langlands correspondence for tori, also described in terms of
Z(G^{\vee})
.

Our approach avoids using the center of the dual group and formulates our results in terms of the algebraic fundamental group of
G
. This construction is functorial for all morphisms. We use a flasque resolution of reductive groups, constructed by Colliot-Thelene. Similar results on the dual of the Picard group for reductive groups over non-archimedean local fields are known. Our main ingredient is an extension of the Tate-Nakayama theorem to complexes of tori, due to Kottwitz and Shelstad
[
KS99
]
.

The proof in this short note may be well-known but I don’t know of any reference. It was written in 2021 when I was trying to learn about Galois cohomology by reading some articles of Borovoi and Kottwitz from the 1980’s. Before getting into the details, I would like to thank Mikhail Borovoi for answering many of my questions via e-mail and for showing me some papers on flasque resolutions.

2.
Tate-Nakayama Duality for Complexes of Tori

In this section we review some class field theory. Let
F
be a number field,
{\overline{F}}
an algebraic closure, and
\Gamma=\text{Gal}({\overline{F}}/F)
the Galois group of
{\overline{F}}
over
F
. For every finite extension
K
of
F
in
{\overline{F}}
, let
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}
be the ring of adeles of
K
,
J_{K}
the group of ideles of
K
, and
C_{K}=J_{K}/K^{\times}
the idele class group.

Let
\overline{{\mathbb{A}}}_{F}=\varinjlim{\mathbb{A}}_{K}
, the direct limit of the rings
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}
(the limit being taken over the directed set of finite extensions
K
of
F
in
{\overline{F}}
. Let
J_{{\overline{F}}}=\overline{{\mathbb{A}}}_{F}^{\times}=\varinjlim{\mathbb{A}}_{K}/K^{\times}=\varinjlim J_{K}
, and let
C_{{\overline{F}}}=\varinjlim C_{K}=\overline{{\mathbb{A}}}_{F}^{\times}/{\overline{F}}^{\times}
.

The group
\overline{{\mathbb{A}}}_{F}
is a smooth
\Gamma
-module, and
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}
can be viewed as the fixed points of
\text{Gal}(K/F)
in
\overline{{\mathbb{A}}}
. It is possible to define the set
T({\mathbb{A}}_{K})
of points of
T
with values in
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}
, and we let
T(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})=\varinjlim T({\mathbb{A}}_{K})
.

For a linear algebraic group
G
over a field
F
, we write
G_{*}
for the group of cocharacters and
G^{*}
for the group of characters.

We have
T(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})=T_{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}J_{F}
, and
T({\overline{F}})=T_{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\overline{F}}^{\times}
. A character
\chi\in X^{*}(T)
defines compatible maps

T({\overline{F}})\to{\overline{F}}^{\times}\ \text{and}\ T(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})\to J_{F},

and hence a map

T(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})/T({\overline{F}})\to J_{F}/{\overline{F}}^{\times}=\varinjlim C_{K},

which induces cup product pairings

H^{r}(G_{F},X^{*}(T))\times H^{2-r}(G_{F},T(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})/T({\overline{F}}))\to H^{2}(G_{F},\varinjlim C_{K})={\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}.

For
r=1
, the cup product pairing induces an isomorphism

H^{1}(F,T(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})/T({\overline{F}}))\to\text{Hom}(H^{1}(F,X^{*}(T)),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}).

(see
[
KS99
, Lemma D.2.A]
).

Let
T
and
U
be two
F
-tori, and
f\colon T\to U
a morphism. There is a long exact sequence

\dots\to H^{i}(F,T({\overline{F}})\to U({\overline{F}}))\to H^{i}(F,T(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})\to U(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}}))\to H^{i}(F,T(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})/T({\overline{F}}))\to U(\overline{A})/U({\overline{F}}))\to\dots

For complexes of length two, the two spectral sequences of hypercohomology reduce to long exact sequences. One of the long exact sequences is

\dots\to H^{r}(G,A\to B)\to H^{r}(G,A)\to H^{r}(G,B)\to H^{r+1}(G,A\to B)\to\dots

where the map
i
is given by

(a,b)\mapsto a

for any hypercocycle
(a,b)\in C^{r}(G,A)\oplus C^{r-1}(G,B)
, the map
f
is induced by
f\colon A\to B
, and the map
j
is given by

b\to(0,b)

for any cocycle
b
in
C^{r}(G,B)
(see
[
KS99
, A.2]
).

We define

H^{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{F}/F,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})=H^{2}(F,C_{{\overline{F}}})=H^{2}(\Gamma_{F},\overline{{\mathbb{A}}}_{F}^{\times}/{\overline{F}}^{\times}).

Recall that if
K/F
is a finite Galois extension with group
\Gamma(K/F)
, then for any place
v
of
F
, we have the decomposition subgroup
\Gamma_{v}=\Gamma(K_{v}/F_{v})
associated with
v
. We define

{\rm inv}_{K/F}\colon H^{2}(\Gamma,I_{K})\to\frac{1}{[K:F]}{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}

by the formula

{\rm inv}_{K/F}(c)=\sum_{v\in\Omega_{F}}{\rm inv}_{K_{v}/F_{v}}(c_{v}),

where
c_{v}
is the component at
v
of
c\in H^{2}(\Gamma(K/F),I_{K})=\bigoplus_{v\in\Omega_{F}}H^{2}(\Gamma_{v},K_{v}^{\times}).

Passing to the limit over the finite Galois extensions
K
of
F
, we obtain a map

{\rm inv}_{F}\colon H^{2}(F,I)\to{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}},

which induces an isomorphism

{\rm inv}_{F}\colon H^{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{F}/F,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})=H^{2}(F,C)\to{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}.

We define groups

{\mathbb{H}}^{i}({\mathbb{A}}/F,T\to U):={\mathbb{H}}^{i}(F,T(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})/T({\overline{F}})\to U(\overline{{\mathbb{A}}})/U({\overline{F}})).

There is a Tate-Nakayama pairing (
[
KS99
, C.2.1]
)

{\mathbb{H}}^{r}({\mathbb{A}}_{F}/F,T\to U)\otimes{\mathbb{H}}^{2-r}(F,U^{*}\to T^{*})\to{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}},

the
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
coming from the canonical isomorphism

{\rm inv}_{F}\colon H^{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{F}/F,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})=H^{2}(F,C)\to{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}.

Apply the exact sequence for
T\to U
and
U^{*}\to T^{*}
. The 5-lemma implies that the Tate-Nakayama pairing induces an isomorphism

H^{1}({\mathbb{A}}/F,T\to U)\to{\rm Hom}(H^{1}(F,U^{*}\to T^{*}),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})

(
[
KS99
, Lemma C.2.A]
).

3.
Picard Group as an Abelian Cohomology Group

In this section we explain how the Picard group of
G
may be viewed as a hypercohomology group. Such an isomorphism was constructed by Borovoi and van Hamel in a general setting (
[
BvH09
]
), but there is an easier construction when the variety is an algebraic group. This may be well-known but I cannot find this result in the literature. I am grateful to Mikhail Borovoi for indicating a proof.

By
[
CT08
, Proposition-Definition 3.1]
, there exists a resolution

1\to S\to H\to G\to 1,

where
S
is an
F
-torus and
H
is a quasi-trivial reductive group, that is,
[H,H]
is simply connected and
P:=H/[H,H]
is a quasi-trivial
F
-torus.

By
[
CT08
, Proposition 3.3]
, we have an exact sequence

(P^{*})^{\Gamma}\to(S^{*})^{\Gamma}\to\text{Pic}\ G\to 0.

The hypercohomology group
{\mathbb{H}}^{1}(F,P^{*}\to S^{*})
fits into the exact sequence

(P^{*})^{\Gamma}\to(S^{*})^{\Gamma}\to{\mathbb{H}}^{1}(F,P^{*}\to S^{*})\to H^{1}(F,P^{*})=0.

Thus we obtain an isomorphism

\text{Pic}\ G\cong{\mathbb{H}}^{1}(F,P^{*}\to S^{*}).

There is a canonical isomorphism

{\mathbb{H}}^{1}(F,T^{*}\to T_{sc}^{*})\cong{\mathbb{H}}^{1}(F,P^{*}\to S^{*}),

which gives an isomorphism

\text{Pic}(G)\cong{\mathbb{H}}^{1}(F,T^{*}\to T_{sc}^{*}).

Applying this we get

{\rm Hom}(\text{Pic}(G),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})={\rm Hom}({\mathbb{H}}^{1}(F,T^{*}\to T_{sc}^{*}),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{1}({\mathbb{A}}/F,T^{sc}\to T)=:H^{1}_{ab}({\mathbb{A}}_{F}/F,G).

This completes the proof.

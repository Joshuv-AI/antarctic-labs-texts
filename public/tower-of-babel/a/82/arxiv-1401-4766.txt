The radial defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation in three space dimensions
Jason Murphy

Abstract
. We study the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation in three space dimensions. We prove that any radial solution that remains bounded in the critical Sobolev space must be global and scatter. In the energy-supercritical setting, we employ a space-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality of Bourgain. In the inter-critical regime, we prove long-time Strichartz estimates and frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequalities.



1.
Introduction

We consider the initial-value problem for the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) on
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u=|u|^{p}u,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

This equation enjoys a scaling symmetry, namely

u(t,x)\mapsto\lambda^{2/p}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),

(1.2)

which defines a notion of criticality for (
1.1
). In particular, the only homogeneous
L_{x}^{2}
-based Sobolev space that is left invariant by this rescaling is
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, where the
critical regularity
is defined by
s_{c}:=3/2-2/p
. We study (
1.1
) in both the
inter-critical
(
0<s_{c}<1
) and
energy-supercritical
(
s_{c}>1
) settings. We show that any radial solution to (
1.1
) that remains bounded in
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}
must be global and scatter.

We begin with a few definitions.

Definition 1.1

(Solution)
.

A function
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
is a
(strong) solution
to (
1.1
) if it belongs to
C_{t}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(K\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L_{t,x}^{5p/2}(K\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
for any compact
K\subset I
and obeys the Duhamel formula

u(t)=e^{it\Delta}u_{0}-i\textstyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}(|u|^{p}u)(s)\,ds

for any
t\in I
. We call
I
the
lifespan
of
u
; we say that
u
is a
maximal-lifespan solution
if it cannot be extended to any strictly larger interval. If
I=\mathbb{R}
we call
u

global
.

Definition 1.2

(Scattering size, blowup)
.

We define the
scattering size
of a solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
1.1
) by

S_{I}(u):=\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u(t,x)|^{5p/2}\,dx\,dt.

If there exists
t_{0}\in I
so that
S_{[t_{0},\sup I)}(u)=\infty
, then we say
u

blows up (forward in time)
. If there exists
t_{0}\in I
so that
S_{(\inf I,t_{0}]}(u)=\infty
, then we say
u

blows up (backward in time)
.

If
u
is a global solution to (
1.1
) that obeys
S_{\mathbb{R}}(u)<\infty
, then standard arguments show that
u

scatters
, that is, there exist
u_{\pm}\in\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
so that

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\|u(t)-e^{it\Delta}u_{\pm}\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=0.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s_{c}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})\cup(\tfrac{3}{4},1)\cup(1,\tfrac{3}{2}).

Suppose
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
is a radial maximal-lifespan solution to (
1.1
) such that
\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\infty
. Then
u
is global and scatters, with

S_{\mathbb{R}}(u)\leq C(\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}})

for some function
C:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty).

Equivalently, we see that any radial solution that fails to scatter must blow up its
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}
-norm. We note here that the cases covered in Theorem
1.3
correspond to choosing
p\in(4/3,2)\cup(8/3,4)\cup(4,\infty)
. The range
p\in(2,8/3]
was already addressed in the non-radial setting
[
27
]
.

The motivation for Theorem
1.3
comes from the celebrated global well-posedness and scattering results for the defocusing mass- and energy-critical NLS. In space dimension
d
the equation (
1.1
) is
mass-critical
for
p=4/d
and
energy-critical
for
p=4/(d-2)
(and
d\geq 3
). These equations are distinguished by the presence of a conserved quantity at the level of the critical regularity. For the mass-critical NLS, the critical regularity is
s_{c}=0
and the conserved quantity is the
mass
, defined by

M[u(t)]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx.

For the energy-critical NLS, the critical regularity is
s_{c}=1
and the conserved quantity is the
energy
, defined by

E[u(t)]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{p+2}|u(t,x)|^{p+2}\,dx.

For the defocusing mass- and energy-critical NLS, it is now known that arbitrary initial data in the critical Sobolev space lead to global solutions that scatter
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
19
,
24
,
29
,
32
,
33
,
35
,
36
]
. (See also
[
9
,
14
,
19
,
21
,
24
]
for results in the focusing case.) One of the key difficulties in establishing these results was the fact that none of the known monotonicity formulae (that is, Morawetz estimates) scale like the mass or energy. It was Bourgain’s
induction on energy
technique that showed how one can move beyond this difficulty: by finding solutions that concentrate on a characteristic length scale, one can ‘break’ the scaling symmetry of the problem and hence bring the available Morawetz estimates back into play, despite their non-critical scaling.

In the mass- and energy-critical settings one has some
a priori
control over solutions due to the conservation laws. Specifically, in the defocusing setting one has uniform (in time)
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}
-bounds. For
s_{c}\notin\{0,1\}
, one has no such
a priori
control. However, the success of the techniques developed to treat the mass- and energy-critical problems inspires the following conjecture: for any
s_{c}>0
, any solution to defocusing NLS that remains bounded in
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}
will be global and scatter.

Progress has been made on this conjecture in both the inter-critical and energy-supercritical settings in dimensions
d\geq 3

[
15
,
22
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
. In three dimensions, the case
s_{c}=1/2
was treated by Kenig–Merle
[
15
]
, while the range
1/2<s_{c}\leq 3/4
was addressed by the author in
[
27
]
. Restricting to the case of radial solutions, we consider all remaining cases for which
s_{c}>0
in three dimensions. In particular, we address the energy-supercritical regime for the first time in three dimensions, as well as the range
0<s_{c}<1/2
for the first time in any dimension. The origin of the restriction to the radial setting and the possibility of extending Theorem
1.3
to the non-radial case will be discussed below.

1.1.
Outline of the proof of Theorem
1.3

We begin by recording a local well-posedness result for (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.4

(Local well-posedness)
.

Let
s_{c}\geq 0
. For any
u_{0}\in\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique maximal-lifespan solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
1.1
) with
u(t_{0})=u_{0}.
Furthermore

•

(Local existence)
I
is an open neighborhood of
t_{0}
.

•

(Blowup criterion) If
\sup I
is finite, then
u
blows up forward in time in the sense of Definition
1.2
. If
\inf I
is finite, then
u
blows up backward in time.

•

(Scattering and wave operators) If
\sup I=\infty
and
u
does not blow up forward in time, then
u
scatters forward in time. That is, there exists
u_{+}\in\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
so that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)-e^{it\Delta}u_{+}\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=0.

(1.3)

Conversely, for any
u_{+}\in\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, there exists a unique solution to (
1.1
) defined in a neighborhood of
t=\infty
such that (
1.3
) holds. The analogous statements hold backward in time.

•

(Small data scattering) If
\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
is sufficiently small, then
u
is global and scatters, with
S_{\mathbb{R}}(u)\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{5p/2}.

This theorem follows from well-known arguments. Specifically, arguments from
[
2
]
suffice to establish the result first for data in the inhomogeneous space
H_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see
[
20
,
22
]
, for example). To remove the assumption that
u_{0}\in L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, one can use a stability result such as the following.

Theorem 1.5

(Stability)
.

Let
s_{c}\geq 0
. Let
I
be a compact time interval and
v:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
a solution to
(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)v=|v|^{p}v+e
for some function
e
. Assume that

\|v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq E\quad\text{and}\quad S_{I}(v)\leq L.

Let
t_{0}\in I
and
u_{0}\in\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(E,L)
such that if

\|u_{0}-v(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}e\|_{L_{t,x}^{10/7}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\varepsilon

for some
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, then there exists a solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
1.1
) with
u(t_{0})=u_{0}
and
S_{I}(u-v)\lesssim_{E,L}\varepsilon.

This result also follows from well-known arguments, similar to those used to prove local well-posedness. For small-power nonlinearities (that is,
|u|^{p}u
for
p<1
), the stability theory can become quite delicate; see
[
20
, Section 3.4]
for a detailed discussion with references. In our setting, however, the stability theory is fairly straightforward; one can find the necessary arguments in
[
22
,
27
]
.

We can now outline the proof of Theorem
1.3
. We argue by contradiction and suppose that Theorem
1.3
were false. As scattering holds for sufficiently small initial data (cf. Theorem
1.4
), we deduce the existence of a threshold below which solutions scatter, but above which we can find solutions with arbitrarily large scattering size. Using a limiting argument, we can then prove the existence of nonscattering solutions living exactly at the threshold, that is,
minimal counterexamples
. To complete the proof, we need only to rule out the existence of such minimal counterexamples.

To accomplish this, we make use of an important property of minimal counterexamples, namely
almost periodicity
modulo the symmetries of the equation. Let us now briefly discuss this property and some of its consequences. For a more extensive treatment, one can refer to
[
20
]
.

Definition 1.6

(Almost periodic solutions)
.

Let
s_{c}>0
. A solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
1.1
) is
almost periodic (modulo symmetries)
if

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})

(1.4)

and there exist functions
x:I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
N:I\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and
C:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
so that for all
t\in I
and
\eta>0
,

\int_{|x-x(t)|>\frac{C(\eta)}{N(t)}}\big||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u(t,x)\big|^{2}\,dx+\int_{|\xi|>C(\eta)N(t)}|\xi|^{2s_{c}}|\widehat{u}(t,x)|^{2}\,d\xi<\eta.

We call
N(t)
the
frequency scale function
,
x(t)
the
spatial center
, and
C(\eta)
the
compactness modulus function
.

Remark 1.7
.

As a consequence of radiality, the solutions we consider can only concentrate near the spatial origin. In particular, we may take
x(t)\equiv 0
.

The frequency scale function of an almost periodic solution obeys the following local constancy property (see
[
20
, Lemma 5.18]
).

Lemma 1.8

(Local constancy)
.

If
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
is a maximal-lifespan almost periodic solution to (
1.1
), then there exists
\delta=\delta(u)>0
so that for all
t_{0}\in I

[t_{0}-\delta N(t_{0})^{-2},t_{0}+\delta N(t_{0})^{-2}]\subset I.

Moreover,
N(t)\sim_{u}N(t_{0})
for
|t-t_{0}|\leq\delta N(t_{0})^{-2}.

Thus, for an almost periodic solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
, we may divide the lifespan
I
into
characteristic subintervals

J_{k}
on which we can set
N(t)\equiv N_{k}
for some
N_{k}
, with
|J_{k}|\sim_{u}N_{k}^{-2}
. This requires us to modify the compactness modulus function by a time-independent multiplicative factor.

Lemma
1.8
also provides information about the behavior of the frequency scale function at the blowup time. In particular, we have the following (see
[
20
, Corollary 5.19]
).

Corollary 1.9

(
N(t)
at blowup)
.

Let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be a maximal-lifespan almost periodic solution to (
1.1
). If
T
is a finite endpoint of
I
, then
N(t)\gtrsim_{u}|T-t|^{-1/2}
.

The next lemma relates the frequency scale function of an almost periodic solution to its Strichartz norms.

Lemma 1.10

(Spacetime bounds)
.

Let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution to (
1.1
). Then

\textstyle\int_{I}N(t)^{2}\,dt\lesssim_{u}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim_{u}1+\textstyle\int_{I}N(t)^{2}\,dt.

To prove this lemma, one can adapt the proof of
[
20
, Lemma 5.21]
. The key is to notice that
\int_{I}N(t)^{2}\,dt
counts the number of characteristic subintervals
J_{k}
inside
I
, and that for each such subinterval we have

\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\sim_{u}1.

We record here some final consequences of almost periodicity (see the proofs of (7.2) and (7.4) in
[
26
]
for very similar arguments).

Lemma 1.11
.

Let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero almost periodic solution to (
1.1
) such that
x(t)\equiv 0
. Then there exists
C(u)>0
so that

\inf_{t\in I}\big(N(t)^{2s_{c}}\textstyle\int_{|x|\leq\frac{C(u)}{N(t)}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\big)\gtrsim_{u}1.

(1.5)

If
\inf_{t\in I}N(t)\geq 1
, then there exists
C(u)>0
and
N_{0}>0
so that for
N<N_{0}
,

\inf_{t\in I}\big(N(t)^{2s_{c}}\textstyle\int_{|x|\leq\frac{C(u)}{N(t)}}|u_{>N}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\big)\gtrsim_{u}1.

(1.6)

If
\sup_{t\in I}N(t)\leq 1
, then there exists
C(u)>0
and
N_{1}>0
so that for
N>N_{1}
,

\inf_{t\in I}\big(N(t)^{2s_{c}}\textstyle\int_{|x|\leq\frac{C(u)}{N(t)}}|u_{\leq N}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\big)\gtrsim_{u}1.

(1.7)

We can now state the first major step in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 1.12

(Reduction to almost periodic solutions)
.

If Theorem
1.3
fails, then there exists a radial maximal-lifespan solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
1.1
) that is almost periodic and blows up forward and backward in time.

The reduction to almost periodic solutions is now fairly standard in the field of nonlinear dispersive PDE, especially in the setting of NLS. Keraani
[
17
]
originally established the existence of minimal blowup solutions to NLS, while Kenig–Merle
[
14
]
were the first to use them as a tool to prove global well-posedness. This technique has since been used in a variety of settings and has proven to be extremely effective. One can refer to
[
12
,
15
,
19
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
34
,
37
]
for some examples in the case of NLS; see also
[
20
]
for a good introduction to the method.

The proof of Theorem
1.12
requires three main ingredients. The first is a linear profile decomposition for the free propagator
e^{it\Delta}
; the particular result we need can be found in
[
30
]
. The second ingredient is a stability result, such as Theorem
1.5
above. Finally, one needs to prove a decoupling statement for nonlinear profiles. Arguments of Keraani
[
16
]
suffice to establish such a decoupling in the mass- and energy-critical settings; however, these arguments rely on pointwise estimates and hence fail to be directly applicable in the presence of fractional derivatives (for example, when
s_{c}\notin\{0,1\}
).

Some strategies are now available for dealing with this technical issue. In
[
22
]
, Killip–Visan devised a strategy that works in the energy-supercritical regime, while some alternate approaches are available in the inter-critical setting
[
12
,
15
,
27
,
28
]
. As for the cases we consider, the arguments of
[
22
]
apply in the cases for which
s_{c}>1
, while the arguments of
[
27
]
apply in the cases for which
0<s_{c}<1
.

Next, we make some refinements to the solutions given in Theorem
1.12
.

If
s_{c}>1/2
, we proceed as follows. First, a rescaling argument as in
[
19
,
21
,
33
]
allows us to restrict attention to almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.12
that do not escape to arbitrarily low frequencies on at least half of their lifespan, say
[0,T_{max})
. Next, as described above, we divide
[0,T_{max})
into characteristic subintervals
J_{k}
so that
N(t)\equiv N_{k}
on
J_{k}
, with
|J_{k}|\sim_{u}N_{k}^{-2}
. Finally, we recall from Remark
1.7
that we may take
x(t)\equiv 0
. Altogether, we get the following.

Theorem 1.13
.

Fix
s_{c}\in(\tfrac{3}{4},1)\cup(1,\tfrac{3}{2})
and suppose Theorem
1.3
fails. Then there exists a radial almost periodic solution
u:[0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
S_{[0,T_{max})}=\infty
and

N(t)\equiv N_{k}\geq 1\quad\text{for }t\in J_{k},\ \text{with}\ [0,T_{max})=\cup J_{k}\ \text{and}\ |J_{k}|\sim_{u}N_{k}^{-2}.

If
s_{c}<1/2
, we proceed similarly; however, we instead restrict attention to almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.12
that do not escape to arbitrarily
high
frequencies on
[0,T_{max})
. In light of Corollary
1.9
, this implies
T_{max}=\infty
. We arrive at the following.

Theorem 1.14
.

Fix
s_{c}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
and suppose Theorem
1.3
fails. Then there exists a radial almost periodic solution
u:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
S_{[0,\infty)}(u)=\infty
and

N(t)\equiv N_{k}\leq 1\quad\text{for }t\in J_{k},\ \text{with }[0,\infty)=\cup J_{k}\ \text{and}\ |J_{k}|\sim_{u}N_{k}^{-2}.

To prove Theorem
1.3
, it therefore suffices to rule out the existence of almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.13
and Theorem
1.14
. As alluded to above, the best tools available are monotonicity formulae that hold for defocusing NLS, which are known as
Morawetz estimates
. In this paper, we will use versions of the
Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality
of
[
25
]
, which is given by

\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt\lesssim\||\nabla|^{1/2}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

(1.8)

Because of the weight
\tfrac{1}{|x|}
, the Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality is well suited for preventing concentration near the origin, and hence it is most effective in the radial setting. In fact, it is the use of Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality that leads to the restriction to the radial setting in Theorem
1.3
. We cannot use this estimate directly, however, as the solutions we consider need only belong to
L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}
(and hence the right-hand side of (
1.8
) need not be finite).

For
s_{c}>1/2
, one needs to suppress the low frequencies of solutions in order to access the estimate (
1.8
). In his work on the 3D radial energy-critical NLS
[
1
]
, Bourgain accomplished this by proving a space-localized version of (
1.8
) (see also
[
11
,
32
]
). In Section
3
, we adopt the same approach to treat the range
1<s_{c}<3/2
. As one of the error terms resulting from space-localization requires control of the solution at the level of
\dot{H}_{x}^{1}
, a different approach is needed to handle the range
3/4<s_{c}<1
. In particular, in Section
4
we prove a version of (
1.8
) localized to high frequencies. For
s_{c}<1/2
, one instead needs to suppress the high frequencies of solutions in order to access (
1.8
). In Section
5
we prove a version of (
1.8
) localized to low frequencies.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows:

In Section
2
we record some notation and background results.

In Section
3
we rule out almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.13
with
1<s_{c}<3/2
. We consider separately the cases
T_{max}<\infty
and
T_{max}=\infty
. In Section
3.1
we deal with the case
T_{max}<\infty
. We show that the existence of such solutions is inconsistent with the conservation of energy. We rely on the following ‘reduced’ Duhamel formula for almost periodic solutions to (
1.1
) (see
[
20
, Proposition 5.2]
).

Proposition 1.15

(Reduced Duhamel formula)
.

Let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be a maximal-lifespan almost periodic solution to (
1.1
). Then for all
t\in I
, we have

u(t)=\lim_{T\nearrow\sup I}i\textstyle\int_{t}^{T}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}(|u|^{p}u)(s)\,ds

as a weak limit in
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

In Section
3.2
we treat the case
T_{max}=\infty
. As mentioned above, we use a space-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality as in the work of Bourgain
[
1
]
. Combining this estimate with Lemma
1.11
, we derive the bound

\textstyle\int_{I}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt\lesssim_{u}|I|^{s_{c}-1/2}

for any compact interval
I\subset[0,\infty)
. Recalling that
\inf N(t)\geq 1
and
s_{c}<3/2
, we reach a contradiction by taking
I
sufficiently large inside
[0,\infty)
.

Sections
4
and
5
are devoted to ruling out solutions as in Theorem
1.13
and Theorem
1.14
for which
s_{c}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})\cup(\tfrac{3}{4},1)
. In both regimes we break into two cases depending on the control given by the Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality. Specifically, we consider separately the cases

\textstyle\int_{0}^{T_{max}}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\textstyle\int_{0}^{T_{max}}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt=\infty,

where
T_{max}=\infty
for
s_{c}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
. We refer to the first case as the
rapid frequency-cascade
scenario and to the second as the
quasi-soliton
scenario.

In Section
4
we rule out solutions as in Theorem
1.13
with
3/4<s_{c}<1
. (In fact, the arguments are equally valid in the range
1/2<s_{c}\leq 3/4
; however, these cases have already been treated in the non-radial setting
[
27
]
.) The main technical tool we use is a
long-time Strichartz estimate
, which appears as Proposition
4.1
. This type of estimate was originally developed by Dodson
[
6
]
for the mass-critical NLS; similar estimates have since appeared in the energy-critical, inter-critical, and energy-supercritical settings
[
23
,
26
,
27
,
37
]
. In all previous cases, the estimates have been adapted to the interaction Morawetz inequality. We instead prove long-time Strichartz estimates adapted to the Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality.

In Section
4.2
we rule out the rapid frequency-cascade scenario. We use Proposition
4.1
to show that such a solution would possess additional decay. We then use the conservation of mass to derive a contradiction.

In Section
4.3
we establish a frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality (Proposition
4.6
). We begin as in the proof of the standard Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality, truncate the solution to high frequencies, and use Proposition
4.1
to control the resulting error terms.

In Section
4.4
we use Proposition
4.6
to rule out the quasi-soliton scenario. In particular, we combine Proposition
4.6
with Lemma
1.11
to deduce a uniform bound on
\int_{I}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt
for any compact interval
I\subset[0,T_{max})
. We reach a contradiction by taking
I
to be sufficiently large inside
[0,T_{max})
.

In Section
5
we rule out the existence of solutions as in Theorem
1.14
, in which case
0<s_{c}<1/2
. The structure of Section
5
is quite similar to that of Section
4
. In particular, we prove a long-time Strichartz estimate (Proposition
5.1
) and a frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality (Proposition
5.6
). However, as mentioned above, in the range
0<s_{c}<1/2
we need to work with the low frequency component of solutions rather than the high frequencies, and hence the details of the analysis change. We find that rapid frequency-cascades now possess additional regularity, which can be combined with the conservation of energy to derive a contradiction. For the quasi-soliton scenario, we again use a frequency-localized Morawetz inequality to derive a uniform bound on
\int_{I}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt
, which yields the same contradiction as before.

To conclude, we discuss the possibility of extending Theorem
1.3
to the non-radial setting. The most natural approach would be to work with the interaction Morawetz inequality (introduced in
[
4
]
) instead of the Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality. In particular, for solutions
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
to defocusing NLS, one has the following estimate:

\displaystyle-\int_{I}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle|u(t,x)|^{2}\Delta\big(\tfrac{1}{|x-y|}\big)|u(t,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{1/2}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

(1.9)

The estimate (
1.9
) controls the degree to which mass can interact with itself throughout all of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and hence it is useful even in the non-radial setting. As above, however, the right-hand side of (
1.9
) may not be finite, and hence the estimate may not be directly applicable. In their pioneering work on the energy-critical NLS
[
5
]
, Colliander–Keel–Staffilani–Takaoka–Tao addressed this issue by proving a frequency-localized version of (
1.9
). This approach has since been adopted in many different settings
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
23
,
26
,
27
,
29
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
. To prove such an estimate, one must control the error terms that arise in the standard interaction Morawetz inequality when one applies a frequency cutoff to a solution to NLS. The long-time Strichartz estimates in the work of Dodson and others are designed to handle such error terms.

In
[
27
]
, the author proved long-time Strichartz estimates adapted to the interaction Morawetz inequality in three dimensions for
1/2<s_{c}<1
. In this range, one works with the high-frequency component of solutions to guarantee the finiteness of the right-hand side of (
1.9
). However, for
s_{c}>3/4
, one of the error terms that arises from the frequency cutoff cannot be controlled unless one also imposes a spatial truncation; this is the approach taken in the energy-critical setting, for example
[
5
,
23
]
. This spatial truncation results in additional error terms that require control over the solution at the level of
\dot{H}_{x}^{1}
. Thus, in the energy-critical case, one can use the conservation of energy and ultimately push the arguments through, while in the case
3/4<s_{c}<1
, one would need significant additional input to control the additional error terms. For a further discussion, see
[
27
]
.

For
s_{c}\geq 1
in three dimensions, it becomes difficult to prove long-time Strichartz estimates adapted to the interaction Morawetz inequality. Killip–Visan
[
23
]
were able to prove such an estimate at the level
s_{c}=1
, but they also show that their estimate is sharp in the sense that it is saturated by the ground state solution to the focusing equation. Thus in this case no stronger estimate could be proven without explicitly incorporating the defocusing nature of the equation. For
s_{c}>1
there is no ground state to contend with, and hence it may still be possible to establish a long-time Strichartz estimate adapted to the interaction Morawetz in this setting; however, this seems to be a difficult problem and we have been unable to establish such an estimate.

Finally, for
0<s_{c}<1/2
there is also some difficulty associated to proving a frequency-localized interaction Morawetz inequality. In particular, as one needs to control both the
L_{x}^{2}
- and
\dot{H}_{x}^{1/2}
-norms of the solution in (
1.9
), one would need to truncate both the low and high frequencies of the solution. While this approach may ultimately prove to be tractable, we did not pursue this direction here. Instead, we chose to work with the Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality, in which case it suffices to truncate the high frequencies only. As mentioned above, the use of the Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality necessitates the restriction of our final result to the radial setting.

2.
Notation and useful lemmas

2.1.
Some notation

For nonnegative
X,Y
we write
X\lesssim Y
to denote
X\leq CY
for some
C>0
. If
X\lesssim Y\lesssim X
, we write
X\sim Y.
The dependence of implicit constants on parameters will be indicated by subscripts, e.g.
X\lesssim_{u}Y
denotes
X\leq CY
for some
C=C(u)
. Dependence of constants on the ambient dimension or the power
p
will be not be explicitly indicated.

We use the expression
\text{\O}(X)
to denote a finite linear combination of terms that resemble
X
up to Littlewood–Paley projections, maximal functions, and complex conjugation.

We denote the nonlinearity
|u|^{p}u
by
F(u)
. At various points throughout the paper, we will use the following basic pointwise estimates:

\displaystyle\big||u+v|^{p}(u+v)-|u|^{p}u\big|\lesssim|v|^{p+1}+|v|\,|u|^{p},

\displaystyle\big||u+v|^{p+2}-|u|^{p+2}-|v|^{p+2}\big|\lesssim|u|\,|v|^{p+1}+|u|^{p+1}|v|.

For a time interval
I
, we write
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
for the Banach space of functions
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}:=\big(\textstyle\int_{I}\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{q}\,dt\big)^{1/q},

with the usual adjustments when
q
or
r

q=r
, we write
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{q}=L_{t,x}^{q}
. We will often abbreviate
\|f\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|f\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}
and
\|f\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|f\|_{L_{x}^{r}}.

We define the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

\widehat{f}(\xi):=(2\pi)^{-3/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx.

The fractional differentiation operator
|\nabla|^{s}
is then defined by
\widehat{|\nabla|^{s}f}(\xi):=|\xi|^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)
, and the corresponding homogeneous Sobolev norm is given by

\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}:=\||\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

2.2.
Basic harmonic analysis

We recall the standard Littlewood–Paley projection operators. Let
\varphi
be a radial bump function supported on
\{|\xi|\leq 11/10\}
and equal to one on
\{|\xi|\leq 1\}.
For
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{\leq N}f}(\xi):=\widehat{f_{\leq N}}(\xi):=\varphi(\xi/N)\widehat{f}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{>N}f}(\xi):=\widehat{f_{>N}}(\xi):=(1-\varphi(\xi/N))\widehat{f}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{N}f}(\xi):=\widehat{f_{N}}(\xi):=(\varphi(\xi/N)-\varphi(2\xi/N))\widehat{f}(\xi).

We also define

P_{M<\cdot\leq N}:=P_{\leq N}-P_{\leq M}.

The Littlewood–Paley projection operators commute with derivative operators and the free propagator. They are self-adjoint, bounded on every
L_{x}^{p}
and
\dot{H}_{x}^{s}
space for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\geq 0
, and bounded pointwise by the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function. They also obey the following standard Bernstein estimates.

Lemma 2.1

(Bernstein)
.

For
1\leq r\leq q\leq\infty
and
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s}f_{N}\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\sim N^{s}\|f_{N}\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s}f_{\leq N}\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s}\|f_{\leq N}\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle\|f_{>N}\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-s}\||\nabla|^{s}f_{>N}\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle\|f_{\leq N}\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{3}{r}-\frac{3}{q}}\|f_{\leq N}\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

We will use the following fractional calculus estimates from
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.2

(Fractional product rule
[
3
]
)
.

Let
s>0
and
1<r,r_{j},q_{j}<\infty
satisfy
\tfrac{1}{r}=\tfrac{1}{r_{j}}+\tfrac{1}{q_{j}}
for
j=1,2
. Then

\||\nabla|^{s}(fg)\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{r_{1}}}\||\nabla|^{s}g\|_{L_{x}^{q_{1}}}+\||\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L_{x}^{r_{2}}}\|g\|_{L_{x}^{q_{2}}}.

Lemma 2.3

(Fractional chain rule
[
3
]
)
.

Suppose
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C})
and
s\in(0,1]
Let
1<r,r_{2}<\infty
and
1<r_{1}\leq\infty
be such that
\tfrac{1}{r}=\tfrac{1}{r_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{r_{2}}.
Then

\||\nabla|^{s}G(u)\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\|G^{\prime}(u)\|_{L_{x}^{r_{1}}}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L_{x}^{r_{2}}}.

We next record a paraproduct estimate in the spirit of
[
37
, Lemma 2.3]
(see also
[
26
,
27
,
29
,
35
,
36
]
). We will need this estimate in Section
4.2
.

Lemma 2.4

(Paraproduct estimate)
.

Let
1<r<r_{1}<\infty
,
1<r_{2}<\infty
, and
0<s<1

\tfrac{1}{r_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{r_{2}}=\tfrac{1}{r}+\tfrac{s}{3}<1.
Then

\||\nabla|^{-s}(fg)\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{-s}f\|_{L_{x}^{r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\||\nabla|^{s}g\|_{L_{x}^{r_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

We also record Hardy’s inequality, which appears in Sections
4.3
and
5.3
.

Lemma 2.5

(Hardy)
.

For
0<s<d
and

\|\tfrac{1}{|x|^{s}}f\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Using Hardy’s inequality and interpolation, we can also derive the following estimate for

\||\nabla|^{s}(\tfrac{x}{|x|}u)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

(2.1)

Finally, we record a few facts about the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, which we denote by

2.3.
Strichartz estimates

We record here the standard Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger equation, as well as a bilinear variant that will be used in Section
5.1
.

We begin with a definition.

Definition 2.6

(Admissible pairs)
.

We call a pair of exponents
(q,r)

admissible
if

\tfrac{2}{q}+\tfrac{3}{r}=\tfrac{3}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad 2\leq q\leq\infty.

For a time interval
I
, we define

\|u\|_{S(I)}:=\sup\big\{\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}:(q,r)\ \text{admissible}\big\}.

We define
S(I)
to be the closure of the test functions under this norm.

We can now state the standard Strichartz estimates in the form that we need.

Proposition 2.7

(Strichartz
[
10
,
13
,
31
]
)
.

Let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be a solution to

(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u=F

and let
s\geq 0
. Then

\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{S(I)}\lesssim\|u(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s}}+\min\big\{\||\nabla|^{s}F\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})},\||\nabla|^{s}F\|_{L_{t,x}^{10/7}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big\}.

for any
t_{0}\in I
.

We next record a bilinear Strichartz estimate. The particular version we need can be deduced from
[
20
, Corollary 4.19]
.

Proposition 2.8

(Bilinear Strichartz)
.

Let
0<s_{c}<1
. For a time interval
I
and frequencies
M,N>0
, we have

\|u_{\leq M}v_{>N}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim M^{1-s_{c}}N^{-1/2}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{S^{*}(I)}\|v_{>N}\|_{S^{*}(I)},

where

\|u\|_{S^{*}(I)}:=\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u\|_{L_{t,x}^{10/7}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

We use Proposition
2.8
in Section
5.1
. In that setting, we take
u=v
an almost periodic solution to (
1.1
) and
I=J_{k}
a characteristic subinterval. In particular, we use the following corollary.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
0<s_{c}<1
and
s>0
. Suppose
u
is an almost periodic solution to (
1.1
) such that
u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}
and let
J_{k}
be a characteristic subinterval. Then

\|u_{\leq M}u_{>N}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim_{u}M^{1-s_{c}}N^{-1/2-s}.

Proof.

This result will follow from Proposition
2.8
, provided we can show

\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{S^{*}(J_{k})}\lesssim_{u}1

(2.2)

and

\|u_{>N}\|_{S^{*}(J_{k})}\lesssim_{u}N^{-s}.

(2.3)

For (
2.2
), we first note that interpolating between (
1.4
) and Lemma
1.10
gives
\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{S(J_{k})}\lesssim_{u}1
. Thus, using the fractional chain rule and Sobolev embedding, we find

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{S^{*}(J_{k})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|u\|_{L_{t,x}^{5p/2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{10/3}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}1+\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{S(J_{k})}^{p+1}\lesssim_{u}1.

For (
2.3
), we first apply Strichartz and the fractional chain rule to estimate

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{S(J_{k})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}}+\|u\|_{L_{t,x}^{5p/2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{S(J_{k})}.

As
\|u\|_{L_{t,x}^{5p/2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim_{u}1
, a standard bootstrap argument gives

\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{S(J_{k})}\lesssim_{u}1.

Thus, using Bernstein we find

\displaystyle\|u_{>N}\|_{S^{*}(J_{k})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-s}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}+N^{-s}\||\nabla|^{s}F(u)\|_{L_{t,x}^{10/7}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}N^{-s}+N^{-s}\|u\|_{L_{t,x}^{5p/2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{S(J_{k})}\lesssim_{u}N^{-s}.

This completes the proof of Corollary
2.9
.
∎

3.
The case
1<s_{c}<3/2

In this section, we rule out the existence of solutions as in Theorem
1.13
with
1<s_{c}<3/2
. We treat separately the cases
T_{max}<\infty
and
T_{max}=\infty
. We show that the existence of solutions with

is inconsistent with the conservation of energy. For the case

, we employ a space-localized Morawetz inequality as in the work of Bourgain on the radial energy-critical NLS

3.1.
Finite time blowup

In this section, we preclude the existence of almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.13
for which
1<s_{c}<3/2
and
T_{max}<\infty
.

Theorem 3.1
.

There are no almost periodic solutions solutions to (
1.1
) as in Theorem
1.13
with
1<s_{c}<3/2
and
T_{max}<\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose
u
were such a solution.
For
N>0
and
t\in[0,T_{max})
, we use Proposition
1.15
, Strichartz, Hölder, and Bernstein to estimate

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}P_{N}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}P_{N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}([t,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(T_{max}-t)^{1/2}N^{1-\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}F(u)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\frac{12p}{11p+4}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(T_{max}-t)^{1/2}N^{1-\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{{3p}/{2}}}^{p}\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\frac{12p}{3p+4}}}.

Using Sobolev embedding and (
1.4
), we deduce

\|P_{\leq N}|\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{u}(T_{max}-t)^{1/2}N^{1-\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}

(3.1)

for
t\in[0,T_{max})
and
N>0
.

For the high frequencies, Bernstein and (
1.4
) give

\|P_{>N}|\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}\lesssim_{u}N^{-\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}.

(3.2)

We now let
\eta>0
. We first choose
N
sufficiently large so that
N^{-\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}<\eta
, and subsequently choose
t
close enough to
T_{max}
that
(T_{max}-t)^{1/2}N^{1-\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}<\eta
. Adding (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) then gives

\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{u}\eta.

As
\eta
was arbitrary, we deduce

\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}s_{c}}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to T_{max}.

As
\tfrac{1}{2}s_{c}<1<s_{c}
, we may interpolate this with (
1.4
) to deduce that

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to T_{max}.

(3.3)

We can also use interpolation and Sobolev embedding to estimate

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{p+2}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{6}}^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{{3p}/{2}}}^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\lesssim\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{p}{p+2}},

so that (
3.3
) and (
1.4
) imply

\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{p+2}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to T_{max}.

(3.4)

Adding (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we deduce that
E[u(t)]\to 0
as
t\to T_{max}
. By the conservation of energy, we conclude
E[u(t)]\equiv 0
. Thus we must have
u\equiv 0
, which contradicts the fact that

blows up. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

3.2.
Morawetz à la Bourgain

In this section, we preclude the existence of almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.13
for which
1<s_{c}<3/2
and
T_{max}=\infty
. We employ a space-localized version of the Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality as in the work of Bourgain
[
1
]
on the radial energy-critical NLS. See also
[
11
,
32
]
.

Proposition 3.2

(Space-localized Morawetz)
.

Let
1<s_{c}<3/2
and
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be a solution to (
1.1
). Then for
C\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{I}\int_{|x|\leq C|I|^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\frac{|u(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim(C|I|^{1/2})^{2s_{c}-1}\bigg\{\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p+2}\bigg\}.

(3.5)

Proof.

We follow the presentation in
[
32
]
. Using the scaling symmetry (
1.2
), we may assume
C|I|^{1/2}=1
. We define the Morawetz action

\text{Mor}(t):=2\,\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla a(x)\cdot\nabla u(t,x)\bar{u}(t,x)\,dx,

where
a(x)=(\varepsilon^{2}+|x|^{2})^{1/2}\chi(x),

0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, and
\chi
is a bump function supported on
\{|x|\leq 2\}
and equal to one on
\{|x|\leq 1\}
.

A standard computation shows that for
u
solving (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\text{Mor}(t)

\displaystyle\geq c\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta a(x)|u(t,x)|^{p+2}\,dx

(3.6)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}-\Delta\Delta a(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx

(3.7)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u(t,x)\cdot\nabla^{2}a(x)\nabla\bar{u}(t,x)\,dx\bigg].

(3.8)

For
|x|\leq 1
, we have
\Delta a(x)\gtrsim(\varepsilon^{2}+|x|^{2})^{-1/2}
, while the contributions of
(
3.7
)
and
(
3.8
)
are nonnegative. For
1<|x|\leq 2
, the weight
a
and its derivatives are bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
. Thus, applying the fundamental of calculus, we are led to

\displaystyle\int_{I}\int_{|x|\leq 1}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{p+2}}{(\varepsilon^{2}+|x|^{2})^{1/2}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\text{Mor}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I)}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{I}\int_{1<|x|\leq 2}|u(t,x)|^{p+2}\,dx\,dt

(3.10)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{I}\int_{1<|x|\leq 2}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt

(3.11)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{I}\int_{1<|x|\leq 2}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt

(3.12)

We now show that (
3.9
) through (
3.12
) can be controlled by
\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}^{2}
or
\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}^{p+2},
so that sending
\varepsilon\to 0
we can recover (
3.5
). Using Hölder, Sobolev embedding, and the fact that
|I|=C^{-2}\leq 1
, we estimate

\displaystyle\eqref{bound M}\lesssim\|\nabla a\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{2(p-2)}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{p+2}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{{3p}/{2}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},

\displaystyle\eqref{bound potential}\lesssim|I|\,\|1\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{p-4}}(|x|\sim 1)}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p+2}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p+2},

\displaystyle\eqref{bound mass}\lesssim|I|\,\|1\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{3p-4}}(|x|\sim 1)}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},

\displaystyle\eqref{bound kinetic}\lesssim|I|\,\|1\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{p-4}}(|x|\sim 1)}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{p+2}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

We now use Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
1.11
to prove the following.

Theorem 3.3
.

There are no almost periodic solutions
u
to (
1.1
) as in Theorem
1.13
with
1<s_{c}<3/2
and
T_{max}=\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose that
u
were such a solution. In particular,
u
is nonzero and
x(t)\equiv 0
, so that Lemma
1.11
applies.

We let
I\subset[0,\infty)
be a compact time interval, which is a contiguous union of characteristic subintervals
J_{k}
. Choosing
C(u)
sufficiently large, we use (
1.4
), Proposition
3.2
, Hölder, and (
1.5
) to estimate

\displaystyle|I|^{s_{c}-1/2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\sum_{J_{k}\subset I}\int_{J_{k}}\int_{|x|\leq C(u)|J_{k}|^{1/2}}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\sum_{J_{k}\subset I}\int_{J_{k}}N_{k}\int_{|x|\leq\frac{C(u)}{N_{k}}}|u(t,x)|^{p+2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\sum_{J_{k}\subset I}\int_{J_{k}}N_{k}^{1+\frac{3p}{2}}\bigg(\int_{|x|\leq\frac{C(u)}{N(t)}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\sum_{J_{k}\subset I}\int_{J_{k}}N(t)^{3+ps_{c}}(N(t)^{-2s_{c}})^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\int_{I}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt.

Recalling that
s_{c}<3/2
and
\inf N(t)\geq 1
, we now reach a contradiction by taking
I
sufficiently large inside of
[0,\infty)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.3
.∎

4.
The case
3/4<s_{c}<1

In this section, we rule out the existence of solutions as in Theorem
1.13
with
3/4<s_{c}<1
. The main technical tool we use is a long-time Strichartz estimate, Proposition
4.1
, which we prove in the next subsection. In Section
4.2
we rule out the rapid frequency-cascade scenario by showing that the existence such solutions is inconsistent with the conservation of mass. In Section
4.3
we prove a frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality, which we use in Section
4.4
to rule out the quasi-soliton scenario.

The arguments in this section are equally valid in the range
1/2<s_{c}\leq 3/4
; however, these cases have already been addressed in the non-radial setting
[
27
]
.

4.1.
Long-time Strichartz estimates

In this section, we prove a long-time Strichartz estimate adapted to the Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality. We will work under the assumption

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}([0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3})

(4.1)

for some
s\leq s_{c}
. We have from (
1.4
) that (
4.1
) holds for
s=s_{c}
. In Section
4.2
we will show that rapid frequency-cascade solutions actually satisfy (
4.1
) for
s<s_{c}
.

Throughout Section
4
, we use the following notation:

A_{I}:=\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})},

(4.2)

K_{I}:=\int_{I}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt\sim_{u}\sum_{J_{k}\subset I}N_{k}^{1-2s_{c}}.

(4.3)

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 4.1

(Long-time Strichartz estimate)
.

Let
u:[0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.13
with
3/4<s_{c}<1
. Let
I\subset[0,T_{max})
be a compact time interval, which is a contiguous union of characteristic subintervals
J_{k}
. Suppose (
4.1
) holds for some
s_{c}-1/2<s\leq s_{c}
. Then for any
N>0
, we have

A_{I}(N)\lesssim_{u}1+N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2},

(4.4)

where
\sigma(s):=2s_{c}-s-1/2.

In particular, using (
1.4
), we have

A_{I}(N)\lesssim_{u}1+N^{s_{c}-1/2}K_{I}^{1/2}.

(4.5)

Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(\varepsilon)>0
so that for any
N\leq N_{0}
,

A_{I}(N)\lesssim_{u}\varepsilon(1+N^{s_{c}-1/2}K_{I}^{1/2}).

(4.6)

We prove Proposition
4.1
by induction. The inductive step relies on the following.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\eta>0
and
u,

I,

s
,
\sigma
be as above. For any
N>0
, we have

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}C_{\eta}\sup_{J_{k}\subset I}\|u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{M\geq N/\eta}\big(\tfrac{M}{N}\big)^{s_{c}}A_{I}(M).

Proof.

Throughout the proof, all spacetime norms are taken over
I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
unless indicated otherwise.

We fix
0<\eta<1
. Using almost periodicity, we may choose
c(\eta)
sufficiently small so that

\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq c(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\eta.

(4.7)

We decompose the nonlinearity as follows:

F(u)=F(u_{\leq N/\eta})+[F(u)-F(u_{\leq N/\eta})].

We first restrict our attention to an individual characteristic subinterval
J_{k}
. Using the fractional chain rule, Hölder, the triangle inequality, and Sobolev embedding, we estimate

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}

\displaystyle P_{\leq N}F(u_{\leq N/\eta})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{\leq c(\eta)N_{k}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\quad+\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{>c(\eta)N_{k}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

For the first term, we use (
4.7
) to get

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}

\displaystyle P_{\leq c(\eta)N_{k}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta^{s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(4.8)

For the next term, we note that we only need to consider the case
c(\eta)N_{k}<N/\eta
, in which case we have
1\lesssim C_{\eta}(\tfrac{N}{N_{k}})^{s_{c}-1/2}.
Using Bernstein, Lemma
1.10
, and (
1.4
), we estimate

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}

\displaystyle P_{>c(\eta)N_{k}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}C_{\eta}(\tfrac{N}{N_{k}})^{s_{c}-1/2}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}C_{\eta}(\tfrac{N}{N_{k}})^{s_{c}-1/2}N^{s_{c}-s}\|u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(4.9)

Summing (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) over
J_{k}\subset I
and using (
4.3
), we find

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}

\displaystyle P_{\leq N}F(u_{\leq N/\eta})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\eta^{s_{c}}A_{I}(N/\eta)+C_{\eta}\sup_{J_{k}\subset I}\|u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}.

Next we use Bernstein, Hölder, Sobolev embedding, and (
1.4
) to estimate

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq N/\eta})\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s_{c}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p}\sum_{M>N/\eta}\|u_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\sum_{M>N/\eta}\big(\tfrac{N}{M}\big)^{s_{c}}A_{I}(M).

Collecting the estimates, we complete the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

We turn to the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

We proceed by induction. For the base case, we take
N>\sup_{t\in I}N(t)\geq 1
, so that
N^{2(s_{c}-s)}\big(\tfrac{N}{N(t)}\big)^{2s_{c}-1}\geq 1
for
t\in I
. Lemma
1.10
gives

\displaystyle A_{I}(N)^{2}\lesssim_{u}1+\textstyle\int_{I}N(t)^{2}\,dt\lesssim_{u}N^{2(s_{c}-s)}N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I}.

Thus for
N>\sup_{t\in I}N(t)
, we have

A_{I}(N)\leq C_{u}\big[1+N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}\big].

(4.10)

This inequality clearly remains true if we replace
C_{u}
by any larger constant.

We now suppose that (
4.10
) holds at frequencies
\geq 2N
; we will use Lemma
4.2
to show that it holds at frequency
N
.

Applying Strichartz, Lemma
4.2
, (
1.4
), and (
4.14
) gives

\displaystyle A_{I}(N)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{u}\big[\inf_{t\in I}\|u_{\leq N}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}+C_{\eta}\sup_{J_{k}\subset I}\|u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{M\geq N/\eta}\big(\tfrac{N}{M}\big)^{s_{c}}A_{I}(M)\big]

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{u}\big[1+C_{\eta}N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}+\sum_{M\geq N/\eta}\big(\tfrac{N}{M}\big)^{s_{c}}A_{I}(M)\big].

We let
\eta<1/2
and notice that
s>s_{c}-1/2
guarantees
\sigma(s)<s_{c}
. Thus, using the inductive hypothesis, we find

\displaystyle A_{I}(N)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{u}\big[1+C_{\eta}N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}+\sum_{M\geq N/\eta}\big(\tfrac{N}{M})^{s_{c}}(C_{u}+C_{u}M^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2})\big]

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{u}\big[1+C_{\eta}N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}\big]+C_{u}\tilde{C}_{u}\big[\eta^{s_{c}}+\eta^{s_{c}-\sigma(s)}N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}\big]

Choosing
\eta
sufficiently small depending on
\tilde{C}_{u}
, we find

A_{I}(N)\leq\tilde{C}_{u}(1+C_{\eta}N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2})+\tfrac{1}{2}C_{u}(1+N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}).

Finally, choosing
C_{u}
possibly even larger to guarantee
C_{u}\geq 2(1+C_{\eta})\tilde{C}_{u}
, we deduce from the above inequality that

A_{I}(N)\leq C_{u}(1+N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}),

as was needed to show. This completes the proof of (
4.4
).

The estimate (
4.5
) follows directly from (
4.4
) with
s=s_{c}
. With (
4.5
) in place, we can prove (
4.6
) by continuing from (
4.11
), choosing
\eta
sufficiently small, and noting that
\inf_{t\in I}N(t)\geq 1
implies

\lim_{N\to 0}\big[\inf_{t\in I}\|u_{\leq N}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}+\sup_{J_{k}\subset I}\|u_{\leq N/\eta}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big]=0

for any
\eta>0
. This completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

4.2.
The rapid frequency-cascade scenario

In this section we rule out solutions as in Theorem
1.13
for which
3/4<s_{c}<1
and

K_{[0,T_{max})}=\int_{0}^{T_{max}}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt<\infty.

(4.12)

We show that (
4.12
) and Proposition
4.1
imply that such solutions would possess additional decay. We then use the conservation of mass to derive a contradiction.

Note that we have

\lim_{t\to T_{max}}N(t)=\infty,

(4.13)

whether
T_{max}
is finite or infinite. Indeed, in the case
T_{max}<\infty
this follows from Corollary
1.9
, while in the case
T_{max}=\infty
this follows from (
4.12
) and (
4.3
).

We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3

(Improved decay)
.

Let
u:[0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.13
with
3/4<s_{c}<1
. Suppose

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}([0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\text{for some}\quad s_{c}-1/2<s\leq s_{c}.

(4.14)

If (
4.12
) holds, then

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{\sigma}([0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\text{for all}\quad s-\sigma(s)<\sigma\leq s_{c},

(4.15)

where as above
\sigma(s):=2s_{c}-s-1/2
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we take all spacetime norms over
[0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

We first use Proposition
4.1
and (
4.12
) to show

A_{[0,T_{max})}(N)\lesssim_{u}N^{\sigma(s)}.

(4.16)

Let
I_{n}\subset[0,T_{max})
be a nested sequence of compact time intervals, each of which is a contiguous union of chracteristic subintervals. We let
\eta>0
and apply Strichartz, Lemma
4.2
, and (
4.14
) to estimate

A_{I_{n}}(N)\lesssim_{u}\inf_{t\in I_{n}}\|u_{\leq N}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}+C_{\eta}N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I_{n}}^{1/2}+\sum_{M\geq N/\eta}\big(\tfrac{N}{M}\big)^{s_{c}}A_{I_{n}}(M).

As (
4.4
) gives
A_{I_{n}}(N)\lesssim_{u}1+N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I_{n}}^{1/2}
, we may choose
\eta
sufficiently small and continue from above to get

A_{I_{n}}(N)\lesssim_{u}\inf_{t\in I_{n}}\|u_{\leq N}\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}+N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I_{n}}^{1/2}.

(4.17)

Using (
4.13
), we get that for any
N>0
we have
\lim_{t\to T_{max}}\|u_{\leq N}\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}=0.
Thus sending
n\to\infty
, continuing from (
4.17
), and using (
4.12
), we deduce that (
4.16
) holds.

We next show that (
4.16
) and (
4.14
) imply

\||\nabla|^{s}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{u}N^{\sigma(s)}.

(4.18)

We first use Proposition
1.15
and Strichartz to estimate

\||\nabla|^{s}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{u}\||\nabla|^{s}P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}.

We decompose the nonlinearity as
F(u)=F(u_{\leq N})+[F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})].
Noting that
s_{c}-1/2<s\leq s_{c}
implies
6\leq\tfrac{3p}{2+ps-p}<\infty
, we can first use Hölder, the fractional chain rule, Sobolev embedding, (
1.4
), and (
4.14
) to estimate

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s}F(u_{\leq N})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p-1}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\frac{6}{3-2s}}}\||\nabla|^{s}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{2+ps-p}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p-1}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}N^{\sigma(s)}.

Next, we note that
F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})=\text{\O}(u_{>N}u^{p})
and that
s>s_{c}-1/2
implies
\sigma(s)<s_{c}
. Thus we can use Bernstein, Lemma
2.4
, the fractional chain rule, Sobolev embedding, (
1.4
), (
4.14
), and (
4.16
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle|\nabla|^{s}P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{-(s_{c}-s)}\big(u^{p}u_{>N}\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}-s}(u^{p})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{5p-4-2ps}}}\sum_{M>N}\||\nabla|^{-(s_{c}-s)}u_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{2+ps-p}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p-2}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\frac{6}{3-2s}}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}-s}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\frac{6}{3-2s}}}\sum_{M>N}\big(\tfrac{N}{M}\big)^{s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s}u_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{2+ps-p}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{p-1}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\sum_{M>N}\big(\tfrac{N}{M}\big)^{s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\sum_{M>N}\big(\tfrac{N}{M}\big)^{s_{c}}M^{\sigma(s)}.

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}N^{\sigma(s)}.

Note that in the case
s=s_{c}
, we would simply use Hölder instead of Lemma
2.4
and the fractional chain rule.

The last two estimates together imply (
4.18
).

Finally, we use (
4.18
) to prove (
4.15
). We fix
s-\sigma(s)<\sigma\leq s_{c}
and use Bernstein, (
1.4
), and (
4.18
) to estimate

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{\sigma}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\geq 1}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\sum_{M\leq 1}M^{\sigma-s}\||\nabla|^{s}u_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}1+\sum_{M\leq 1}M^{\sigma-s+\sigma(s)}\lesssim_{u}1.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.3
. ∎

We now iterate Lemma
4.3
to establish additional decay.

Proposition 4.4

(Additional decay)
.

Let
u:[0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.13
with
3/4<s_{c}<1
. If (
4.12
) holds, then
u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{-\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

Let
0<\delta<\min\{1/4,s_{c}-1/2\}
and for each
n\geq 0
define
s_{n}:=s_{c}-n\delta
. We have from Lemma
4.3
that

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{n}}\implies u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{\sigma}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0\leq n<\tfrac{1}{2\delta}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{n}-\sigma(s_{n})<\sigma\leq s_{c}.

The restriction
n<\tfrac{1}{2\delta}
guarantees
s_{n}>s_{c}-1/2
. As above,
\sigma(s):=2s_{c}-s-1/2
.

As (
1.4
) gives
u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{0}}
and the constraint
0<\delta<s_{c}-1/2
guarantees
s_{n}-\sigma(s_{n})<s_{n+1}\leq s_{c}
for all
n\geq 0
, we get by induction that

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{\sigma}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0\leq n<\tfrac{1}{2\delta}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{n}-\sigma(s_{n})<\sigma\leq s_{c}.

(4.19)

As
\delta<1/4
, we may find
n^{*}
so that
\tfrac{1}{4\delta}<n^{*}<\tfrac{1}{2\delta}.
As the constraint
n^{*}>\tfrac{1}{4\delta}
implies
s_{n^{*}}-\sigma(s_{n^{*}})<0
, we deduce from (
4.19
) that
u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{-\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
. This completes the proof of Proposition
4.4
. ∎

Finally, we turn to the following.

Theorem 4.5

(No frequency-cascades)
.

There are no almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.13
with
3/4<s_{c}<1
such that (
4.12
) holds.

Proof.

Suppose
u
were such a solution and let
\eta>0
. By almost periodicity, we may find
c(\eta)
small enough that
\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq c(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}<\eta.
Thus, by interpolation and Proposition
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{\leq c(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq c(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{s_{c}+\varepsilon}}\||\nabla|^{-\varepsilon}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{s_{c}}{s_{c}+\varepsilon}}\lesssim_{u}\eta^{\frac{\varepsilon}{s_{c}+\varepsilon}}

for some
\varepsilon>0
.

On the other hand, using Bernstein and (
1.4
) we get

\|u_{>c(\eta)N(t)}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{u}[c(\eta)N(t)]^{-s_{c}}\quad\text{for any}\quad t\in[0,T_{max}).

Thus

\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{u}\eta^{\frac{\varepsilon}{s_{c}+\varepsilon}}+[c(\eta)N(t)]^{-s_{c}}\quad\text{for any}\quad t\in[0,T_{max}).

Using (
4.13
) and the fact that
\eta>0
was arbitrary, we deduce

\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to T_{max}.

By the conservation of mass, we conclude that
M[u(t)]\equiv 0
. Thus we must have that
u\equiv 0
, which contradicts that
u
blows up. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.5
. ∎

4.3.
A frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality

In this section, we use Proposition
4.1
to prove a frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality, which we use in Section
4.4
to rule out the quasi-soliton scenario. As
s_{c}>1/2
, we prove an estimate that is localized to high frequencies.

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 4.6

(Frequency-localized Morawetz)
.

Let
u:[0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.13
with
3/4<s_{c}<1
. Let
I\subset[0,T_{max})
be a compact time interval, which is a contiguous union of characteristic subintervals
J_{k}
. Then for any
\eta>0
, there exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(\eta)>0
such that for
N<N_{0}
, we have

\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u_{>N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt\lesssim_{u}\eta(N^{1-2s_{c}}+K_{I}),

(4.20)

where
K_{I}
is as in (
4.3
).

To prove Proposition
4.6
, we begin as in the proof of the standard Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality (
1.8
). We truncate the low frequencies of the solution and work with
u_{>N}
for some
N>0
. As
u_{>N}
is not a true solution to (
1.1
), we need to control error terms arising from this frequency projection. To do this, we choose
N
small enough to capture ‘most’ of the solution and use the estimates proved in Section
4.1
. We make these notions precise in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7

(High and low frequency control)
.

Let
u,

I,

K_{I}
be as above. With all spacetime norms over
I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have the following.

For any
N>0
, we have

\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\lesssim_{u}N^{-s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}.

(4.21)

For any
\eta>0
, there exists
N_{1}=N_{1}(\eta)
so that for
N<N_{1}
, we have

\||\nabla|^{1/2}u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1/2-s_{c}}.

(4.22)

For any
\eta>0
, there exists
N_{2}=N_{2}(\eta)
so that for
N<N_{2}
, we have

\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\lesssim_{u}\eta(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}.

(4.23)

Proof.

For (
4.21
), we use Bernstein and (
4.5
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{M>N}M^{-s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\sum_{M>N}M^{-s_{c}}(1+M^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}N^{-s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}.

For (
4.22
), we let
\eta>0
. Using almost periodicity and the fact that
\inf N(t)\geq 1
, we may find
c(\eta)>0
so that
\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq c(\eta)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}<\eta.
Thus Bernstein gives

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{1/2}

\displaystyle u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim c(\eta)^{1/2-s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{>c(\eta)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+N^{1/2-s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{N<\cdot\leq c(\eta)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}c(\eta)^{1/2-s_{c}}+\eta N^{1/2-s_{c}}.

Choosing
N_{1}\ll\eta^{1/(s_{c}-1/2)}c(\eta)
, we recover (
4.22
).

Finally, we note that (
4.23
) is just a restatement of (
4.6
).
∎

We turn to the proof of Proposition
4.6
.

Proof of Proposition
4.6
.

Throughout the proof, we take all spacetime norms over
I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

We let
0<\eta\ll 1
and choose

N<\min\{N_{1}(\eta),\eta^{2}N_{2}(\eta^{2s_{c}})\},

where
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are as in Lemma
4.7
. In particular, we note that (
4.21
) gives

\|u_{>N/\eta^{2}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{-s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}.

(4.24)

Moreover, as
N/\eta^{2}<N_{2}(\eta^{2s_{c}})
, we can apply (
4.23
) to get

\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\eta^{2}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\lesssim_{u}\eta(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}.

(4.25)

We define the Morawetz action

\text{Mor}(t)=2\,\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nabla u_{>N}(t,x)\bar{u}_{>N}(t,x)\,dx.

A standard computation using
(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{>N}=P_{>N}\big(F(u)\big)
gives

\partial_{t}\text{Mor}(t)\gtrsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\{P_{>N}\big(F(u)\big),u_{>N}\}_{P}\,dx,

where the
momentum bracket

\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{P}
is defined by
\{f,g\}_{P}:=\text{Re}(f\nabla\bar{g}-g\nabla\bar{f}).

Thus, by the fundamental theorem of calculus, we get

\displaystyle\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\{P_{>N}\big(F(u)\big),u_{>N}\}_{P}\,dx\lesssim\|\text{Mor}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I)}.

(4.26)

Noting that
\{F(u),u\}_{P}=-\tfrac{p}{p+2}\nabla(|u|^{p+2}),
we may write

\displaystyle\{P_{>N}

\displaystyle\big(F(u)\big),u_{>N}\}_{P}

\displaystyle=\{F(u),u\}_{P}-\{F(u_{\leq N}),u_{\leq N}\}_{P}

\displaystyle\quad-\{F(u)-F(u_{\leq N}),u_{\leq N}\}_{P}-\{P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big),u_{>N}\}_{P}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{p}{p+2}\nabla(|u|^{p+2}-|u_{\leq N}|^{p+2})-\{F(u)-F(u_{\leq N}),u_{\leq N}\}_{P}

\displaystyle\quad-\{P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big),u_{>N}\}_{P}

\displaystyle=:I+II+III.

Integrating by parts, we see that
I
contributes to the left-hand side of (
4.26
) a multiple of

\displaystyle\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle\frac{|u_{>N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt

and to the right-hand side of (
4.26
) a multiple of

\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}(|u|^{p+2}-|u_{>N}|^{p+2}-|u_{\leq N}|^{p+2})\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}.

(4.27)

For term
II
, we use
\{f,g\}_{P}=\nabla\text{\O}(fg)+\text{\O}(f\nabla g)
. When the derivative hits the product, we integrate by parts. We find that
II
contributes to the right-hand side of (
4.26
) a multiple of

\displaystyle\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}[F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})]\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

(4.28)

\displaystyle+\|\nabla u_{\leq N}[F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})]\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}.

(4.29)

Finally, for
III
, we integrate by parts when the derivative hits
u_{>N}.
We find that
III
contributes to the right-hand side of (
4.26
) a multiple of

\displaystyle\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{>N}P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

(4.30)

\displaystyle+\|u_{>N}\nabla P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}.

(4.31)

Thus, continuing from (
4.26
), we see that to complete the proof of Proposition
4.6
it will suffice to show that

\|\text{Mor}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I)}\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1-2s_{c}}

(4.32)

and that the error terms (

) through (

) are acceptable, in the sense that they can be controlled by
\eta(N^{1-2s_{c}}+K_{I}).

To prove (

), we use Bernstein, (
2.1
), (
4.22
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|\text{Mor}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{-1/2}\nabla u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\||\nabla|^{1/2}(\tfrac{x}{|x|}u_{>N})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{1/2}u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1-2s_{c}}.

We next turn to the estimation of the error terms (
4.27
) through (
4.31
).

For (
4.27
), we first write

(
4.27
)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}(u_{\leq N})^{p+1}u_{>N}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

(4.33)

\displaystyle\quad+\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}(u_{>N})^{p+1}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}.

(4.34)

For (

), we use Hölder, Hardy, the chain rule, Bernstein, (
1.4
), (
4.21
), and (
4.23
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}(u_{\leq N})^{p+1}u_{>N}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}(u_{\leq N})^{p+1}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla(u_{\leq N})^{p+1}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p}\|\nabla u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

which is acceptable.

For (

), we consider two cases. If

, then we can absorb this term into the left-hand side of (
4.26

\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}|u_{>N}|^{p+2}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}<\infty.

(4.35)

Otherwise, we are back in the situation of (

\displaystyle\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}|u_{>N}|^{p+2}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

We next turn to (

we recognize the error terms that we just estimated, namely (

) and (

For (

) (

), and (

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta N^{1-2s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I}),

which is acceptable.

Finally, for (

) and (

), we first use Hardy to estimate

(
4.30
)

\displaystyle+\eqref{v}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}+\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|\nabla P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

Thus, in light of (

), and (

\displaystyle\|\nabla

\displaystyle P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N\|F(u)-F(u_{\leq N/\eta^{2}})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}+N^{1-s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}F(u_{\leq N/\eta^{2}})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}.

\displaystyle\lesssim N\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p}\|u_{>N/\eta^{2}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}+N^{1-s_{c}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\eta^{2}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

.
∎

4.4.
The quasi-soliton scenario

In this section, we preclude the existence of solutions as in Theorem
1.13
with
3/4<s_{c}<1
and

K_{[0,T_{max})}=\int_{0}^{T_{max}}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt=\infty.

(4.36)

We rely on the frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality established in Section
4.3
. We also need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.8

(Lower bound)
.

Let
u:[0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.13
with
3/4<s_{c}<1
. Let
I\subset[0,T_{max}).
Then there exists
N_{0}>0
such that for any
N<N_{0},
we have

K_{I}\lesssim_{u}\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u_{>N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt,

(4.37)

where
K_{I}
is as (
4.3
).

Proof.

We choose
C(u)
and
N_{0}
as in Lemma
1.11
. Then for
N<N_{0}
, we use Hölder and (
1.6
) to estimate

\displaystyle\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u_{>N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\int_{I}N(t)\int_{|x|\leq\frac{C(u)}{N(t)}}|u_{>N}(t,x)|^{p+2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\int_{I}N(t)^{1+\frac{3p}{2}}\bigg(\int_{|x|\leq\frac{C(u)}{N(t)}}|u_{>N}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\int_{I}N(t)^{3+ps_{c}}\big(N(t)^{-2s_{c}}\big)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\,dt\gtrsim_{u}K_{I}.

∎

Finally, we prove the following.

Theorem 4.9

(No quasi-solitons)
.

There are no almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.13
with
3/4<s_{c}<1
such that (
4.36
) holds.

Proof.

Suppose
u
were such a solution. Let
\eta>0
and let
I\subset[0,\infty)
be a compact time interval, which is a contiguous union of characteristic subintervals.

Combining (
4.20
) and (

), we find that for

sufficiently small, we deduce

. We now contradict (

) by taking

sufficiently large inside of

4.9
.
∎

5.
The case
0<s_{c}<1/2

In this section, we rule out the existence of solutions as in Theorem
1.14
, in which case
0<s_{c}<1/2
. As in Section
4
, the main technical tool we use is a long-time Strichartz estimate, Proposition
5.1
, which we prove in the next subsection. In Section
5.2
, we rule out the rapid frequency-cascade scenario; we show that such solutions are inconsistent with the conservation of energy. In Section

, we prove a frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality, which we use in Section

to rule out the quasi-soliton scenario.

5.1.
Long-time Strichartz estimates

In this section, we establish a long-time Strichartz estimate adapted to the Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality for almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.14
. A key ingredient in the proof is the bilinear Strichartz estimate, Proposition
2.8
.

We work under the assumption

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})

(5.1)

for some
s\geq s_{c}
. We know from (
1.4
) that (
5.1
) holds for
s=s_{c}
. In Section
5.2
we will show that rapid frequency-cascade solutions actually satisfy (
5.1
) for
s>s_{c}
.

Throughout Section
5
, we make use of the following notation:

A_{I}(N):=\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})},

(5.2)

K_{I}:=\int_{I}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt\sim_{u}\sum_{J_{k}\subset I}N_{k}^{1-2s_{c}}.

(5.3)

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 5.1

(Long-time Strichartz estimate)
.

Let
u:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.14
. Let
I\subset[0,\infty)
be a compact time interval, which is a contiguous union of characteristic subintervals
J_{k}
. Suppose (
5.1
) holds for some
s_{c}\leq s<3/2+s_{c}
. Then for any
N>0
, we have

A_{I}(N)\lesssim_{u}N^{-s_{c}}+N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2},

(5.4)

where
\sigma(s):=1/2+s-s_{c}
.

In particular, using (
1.4
), we have

A_{I}(N)\lesssim_{u}N^{-s_{c}}+N^{-1/2}K_{I}^{1/2}.

(5.5)

Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N_{0}(\varepsilon)>0
so that for
N\geq N_{0}
,

A_{I}(N)\lesssim_{u}\varepsilon\big(N^{-s_{c}}+N^{-1/2}K_{I}^{1/2}\big).

(5.6)

We will prove Proposition
5.1
by induction. The inductive step will rely on the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\eta>0
and
u,

I,

s
,
\sigma
be as above. For any
N>0
, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{>N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}C_{\eta}\sup_{J_{k}\subset I}\|u_{>\eta N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2s_{c}}N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{M\leq\eta N}(\tfrac{M}{N})^{2}A_{I}(M),

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Throughout the proof, all spacetime norms will be taken over
I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
unless stated otherwise.

We begin by writing
F(u)=F(u_{\leq\eta N})+F(u)-F(u_{\leq\eta N}).
We use Bernstein, the chain rule, Sobolev embedding, and (
1.4
) estimate

\displaystyle\|P_{>N}F(u_{\leq\eta N})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-2}\|\Delta F(u_{\leq\eta N})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-2}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{p}\sum_{M\leq\eta N}\|\Delta u_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\sum_{M\leq\eta N}(\tfrac{M}{N})^{2}A_{I}(M).

(5.7)

Next, we use almost periodicity to choose
C(\eta)
large enough that

\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{>C(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\eta^{2}.

(5.8)

By almost periodicity and the embedding
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}\hookrightarrow L_{x}^{3p/2}
we may choose
C(\eta)
possibly even larger to guarantee

\|(1-\chi_{\frac{C(\eta)}{N(t)}})u_{\leq C(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\eta^{2},

(5.9)

where
\chi_{R}
denotes the characteristic function of
\{|x|\leq R\}
.

We now write

F(u)-F(u_{\leq\eta N})\lesssim u_{>\eta N}\text{\O}\big\{(u_{\leq C(\eta)N(t)})^{p}+(u_{>C(\eta)N(t)})^{p}\big\},

so that

\displaystyle\|P_{>N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq\eta N})\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{>\eta N}(u_{>C(\eta)N(t)})^{p}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

(5.10)

\displaystyle+\|(1-\chi_{\frac{C(\eta)}{N(t)}})u_{>\eta N}(u_{\leq C(\eta)N(t)})^{p}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

(5.11)

\displaystyle+\|\chi_{\frac{C(\eta)}{N(t)}}u_{>\eta N}(u_{\leq C(\eta)N(t)})^{p}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}.

(5.12)

Using Hölder, (
1.4
), and (
5.8
), we estimate the contribution of (
5.10
) as follows:

\displaystyle\|u_{>\eta N}(u_{>C(\eta)N(t)})^{p}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{>\eta N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|u_{>C(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p}\lesssim\eta^{2}A_{I}(\eta N).

(5.13)

Similarly, we estimate the contribution of (
5.11
) as follows:

\displaystyle\|(1

\displaystyle-\chi_{\frac{C(\eta)}{N(t)}})u_{>\eta N}(u_{\leq C(\eta)N(t)})^{p}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(1-\chi_{\frac{C(\eta)}{N(t)}})u_{\leq C(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p-1}\|u_{>\eta N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\eta^{2}A_{I}(\eta N).

(5.14)

Finally, we estimate the contribution of (
5.12
). We first restrict our attention to a single characteristic subinterval
J_{k}
. We define the following exponents:

q=\tfrac{2(p^{2}+2p-4)}{3p-4},\quad r_{0}=\tfrac{3p(p^{2}+2p-4)}{8p-p^{2}-8},\quad r=\tfrac{6(p^{2}+2p-4)}{3p^{2}-4}.

Note that as
4/3<p<2
, we have
4<q<\infty
,
2<r_{0}<6
, and
2<r<3
. We also note that we have the embedding
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c},r}\hookrightarrow L_{x}^{r_{0}}
and that
(q,r)
is an admissible pair.

With all spacetime norms over
J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we use Hölder, the bilinear Strichartz estimate (Corollary
2.9
), Sobolev embedding, Lemma
1.10
, (
1.4
), and (
5.1
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\frac{C(\eta)}{N_{k}}}u_{>\eta N}(u_{\leq C(\eta)N_{k}})^{p}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\chi_{\frac{C(\eta)}{N_{k}}}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{6}{(1-2s_{c})^{2}}}}\|u_{>\eta N}u_{\leq C(\eta)N_{k}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}^{1-2s_{c}}\|u_{>\eta N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{2s_{c}}\|u_{\leq C(\eta)N_{k}}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r_{0}}}^{p-1+2s_{c}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}C_{\eta}N_{k}^{-(1-2s_{c})^{2}/2}\big[N_{k}^{1-s_{c}}N^{-1/2-s}\big]^{1-2s_{c}}N^{-2s\cdot s_{c}}\|u_{>\eta N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}}^{2s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}^{p-1+2s_{c}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}C_{\eta}N_{k}^{1/2-s_{c}}N^{-(1/2+s-s_{c})}\|u_{>\eta N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}}^{2s_{c}}.

Summing over
J_{k}\subset I
and using (
5.3
), we find

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\frac{C(\eta)}{N(t)}}u_{>\eta N}(u_{\leq C(\eta)N(t)})^{p}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}\lesssim_{u}C_{\eta}\sup_{J_{k}\subset I}\|u_{>\eta N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2s_{c}}N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}.

(5.15)

Adding the estimates (
5.7
), (
5.13
), (
5.14
), and (
5.15
), we complete the proof of Lemma
5.2
.
∎

We turn to the proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

We proceed by induction. For the base case, we let
N\leq\inf_{t\in I}N(t)\leq 1,
so that
N^{-2(s-s_{c})}\big(\tfrac{N(t)}{N}\big)^{1-2s_{c}}\geq 1
for
t\in I
. We use Bernstein and Lemma
1.10
to estimate

\displaystyle A_{I}(N)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-2s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim_{u}N^{-2s_{c}}+N^{-2s_{c}}\textstyle\int_{I}N(t)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}N^{-2s_{c}}+N^{-1-2(s-s_{c})}K_{I}.

Thus for
N\leq\inf_{t\in I}N(t),
we have

A_{I}(N)\leq C_{u}\big[N^{-s_{c}}+N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}\big].

(5.16)

This inequality remains true if we replace
C_{u}
by any larger constant.

We now suppose that (
5.16
) holds at frequencies
\leq N/2
; we will use Lemma
5.2
to show that it holds at frequency
N
.

Applying Strichartz, Bernstein, Lemma
5.2
, (
1.4
), and (
5.1
), we find

\displaystyle A_{I}(N)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{u}\big[N^{-s_{c}}\inf_{t\in I}\|u_{>N}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}+C_{\eta}\sup_{J_{k}\subset I}\|u_{>\eta N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2s_{c}}N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{M\leq\eta N}(\tfrac{M}{N})^{2}A_{I}(M)\big]

(5.17)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{u}\big[N^{-s_{c}}+C_{\eta}N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}+\sum_{M\leq\eta N}(\tfrac{M}{N})^{2}A_{I}(M)\big].

We now let
\eta<1/2
and note that
s<3/2+s_{c}
gives
\sigma(s)<2
. Thus, using the inductive hypothesis, we find

\displaystyle A_{I}(N)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{u}\big[N^{-s_{c}}+C_{\eta}N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}+\sum_{M\leq\eta N}(\tfrac{M}{N})^{2}(C_{u}M^{-s_{c}}+C_{u}M^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2})\big]

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{u}\big[N^{-s_{c}}+C_{\eta}N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}\big]+C_{u}\tilde{C}_{u}\big[\eta^{2-s_{c}}N^{-s_{c}}+\eta^{2-\sigma(s)}N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}\big].

If we now choose
\eta
sufficiently small depending on
\tilde{C}_{u}
, we get

A_{I}(N)\leq\tilde{C}_{u}(N^{-s_{c}}+C_{\eta}N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2})+\tfrac{1}{2}C_{u}(N^{-s_{c}}+N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}).

Finally, if we choose
C_{u}
possibly larger so that
C_{u}\geq 2(1+C_{\eta})\tilde{C}_{u}
, then the above inequality implies

A_{I}(N)\leq C_{u}(N^{-s_{c}}+N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}),

as was needed to show. This completes the proof of (
5.4
).

The estimate (
5.5
) follows directly from (
5.4
) with

. With (
5.5
) in place, we can prove (
5.6
) by continuing from (
5.17
), choosing
\eta
sufficiently small, and noting that
\sup_{t\in I}N(t)\leq 1
implies

\lim_{N\to\infty}\big[\inf_{t\in I}\|u_{>N}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}+\sup_{J_{k}\subset I}\|u_{>\eta N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2s_{c}}\big]=0.

for any

. This completes the proof of Proposition
5.1
.
∎

5.2.
The rapid frequency-cascade scenario

In this section, we preclude the existence of rapid frequency-cascade solutions, that is, almost periodic solutions
u
as in Theorem
1.14
for which

K_{[0,\infty)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt<\infty.

(5.18)

We show that (
5.18
) and Proposition
5.1
imply that such a solution would possess additional regularity. We then use the additional regularity and the conservation of energy to derive a contradiction.

We note here that (
5.18
) implies

\lim_{t\to\infty}N(t)=0.

(5.19)

We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3

(Improved regularity)
.

Let
u:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.14
. Suppose

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\text{for some}\quad s_{c}\leq s<3/2+s_{c}.

(5.20)

If (
5.18
) holds, then

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{\sigma}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\text{for all}\quad s_{c}\leq\sigma<\sigma(s),

(5.21)

where as above
\sigma(s):=1/2+s-s_{c}
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we take all spacetime norms over
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

We will first use Proposition
5.1
and (
5.18
) to establish

A_{[0,\infty)}(N)\lesssim_{u}N^{-\sigma(s)}.

(5.22)

Let
I_{n}\subset[0,\infty)
be a nested sequence of compact subintervals, each of which is a contiguous union of characteristic subintervals
J_{k}
. We let
\eta>0
and apply Bernstein, Strichartz, Lemma
5.2
, and (
5.20
) to estimate

\displaystyle A_{I_{n}}(N)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}N^{-s_{c}}\inf_{t\in I_{n}}\|u_{>N}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}+C_{\eta}N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I_{n}}^{1/2}+\sum_{M\leq\eta N}(\tfrac{M}{N})^{2}A_{I_{n}}(M).

As (
5.1
) gives
A_{I_{n}}(N)\lesssim_{u}N^{-s_{c}}+N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I_{n}}^{1/2},
we may choose
\eta
sufficiently small and continue from above to get

A_{I_{n}}(N)\lesssim_{u}N^{-s_{c}}\inf_{t\in I_{n}}\|u_{>N}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}+N^{-\sigma(s)}K_{I_{n}}^{1/2}.

(5.23)

Using (
5.19
), we see that for any
N>0
we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u_{>N}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}}=0.

Hence sending
n\to\infty
, continuing from (
5.23
), and using (
5.18
), we get

A_{[0,\infty)}(N)\lesssim_{u}N^{-\sigma(s)}.

We now show that (
5.22
) implies

\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim_{u}N^{-\sigma(s)}.

(5.24)

We first use Proposition
1.15
and Strichartz to estimate

\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\|P_{>N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}.

We write
F(u)=F(u_{\leq N})+F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})
. Noting that
s<3/2+s_{c}
implies
\sigma(s)<2
, we use Bernstein, the chain rule, (
1.4
), and (
5.22
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|P_{>N}\big(F(u_{\leq N})\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-2}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{p}\sum_{M\leq N}\|\Delta u_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\sum_{M\leq N}(\tfrac{M}{N})^{2}M^{-\sigma(s)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}N^{-\sigma(s)}.

We next use Hölder, Sobolev embedding, (
1.4
), and (
5.22
) to estimate

\|P_{>N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\lesssim_{u}N^{-\sigma(s)}.

Adding the last two estimates gives (
5.24
).

Finally, we use (
5.24
) to prove (
5.21
). We fix
s_{c}\leq\sigma<\sigma(s)
and use Bernstein, (
1.4
), and (
5.24
) to estimate

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{\sigma}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq 1}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\sum_{M>1}M^{\sigma}\|u_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}1+\sum_{M>1}M^{\sigma-\sigma(s)}\lesssim_{u}1.

This completes the proof of Lemma
5.3
. ∎

We now iterate Lemma
5.3
to establish additional regularity.

Proposition 5.4

(Additional regularity)
.

Let
u:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.14
. If (
5.18
) holds, then
u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{1+\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

As
0<s_{c}<1/2
, we may choose
c
such that

2s_{c}<\tfrac{2}{3-2s_{c}}<c<\tfrac{3+2s_{c}}{4}<1.

We define
s_{0}=s_{c}
, and for
n\geq 0
we define
s_{n+1}=c\cdot\sigma(s_{n}),
where as above
\sigma(s):=1/2+s-s_{c}
. The constraint
c>2s_{c}
guarantees that the sequence
s_{n}
is increasing and bounded above by
\ell:=\tfrac{c(1-2s_{c})}{2(1-c)}
. In fact, elementary arguments show that the sequence
s_{n}
converges to
\ell
, and the constraint
c>\tfrac{2}{3-2s_{c}}
guarantees that
\ell>1
.

We have that
s_{n}\geq s_{c}
for all
n\geq 0
, while the constraint
c<\tfrac{3+2s_{c}}{4}
guarantees
s_{n}<3/2+s_{c}
for all
n\geq 0
. Thus, noting that
s_{n}\leq s_{n+1}<\sigma(s_{n})
for each
n\geq 0
, we deduce from Lemma
5.3
that

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{n}}\implies u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{n+1}}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\geq 0.

As (
1.4
) gives
u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{0}}
, we get by induction that
u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{n}}
for all
n\geq 0
. As
s_{n}\to\ell>1
, we conclude that
u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{1+\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
.
∎

Combining Proposition
5.4
with almost periodicity and the conservation of energy, we preclude the existence of rapid frequency cascades.

Theorem 5.5

(No frequency-cascades)
.

There are no almost periodic solutions
u
as in Theorem
1.14
such that (
5.18
) holds.

Proof.

Suppose
u
were such a solution and let
\eta>0
. By almost periodicity, we may find
C(\eta)
large enough that
\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{>C(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}<\eta.

Thus, by interpolation and Proposition
5.4
, we have

\|\nabla u_{>C(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{>C(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon-s_{c}}}\||\nabla|^{1+\varepsilon}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{1+\varepsilon-s_{c}}}\lesssim_{u}\eta^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon-s_{c}}}

for some
\varepsilon>0
.

On the other hand, by Bernstein and (
1.4
) we have

\|\nabla u_{\leq C(\eta)N(t)}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{u}[C(\eta)N(t)]^{1-s_{c}}\quad\text{for any}\quad t\in[0,\infty).

Thus we find

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{u}\eta^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon-s_{c}}}+[C(\eta)N(t)]^{1-s_{c}}\quad\text{for any}\quad t\in[0,\infty).

Using (
5.19
) and the fact that
\eta>0
was arbitrary, we deduce that

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

(5.25)

We next use Hölder and Sobolev embedding to estimate

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{p+2}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{6}}^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p+2}},

so that (
1.4
) and (
5.25
) imply

\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{p+2}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

(5.26)

Adding (
5.25
) and (
5.26
) implies that
E[u(t)]\to 0
as
t\to\infty.
By the conservation of energy, we conclude
E[u(t)]\equiv 0
. Thus we must have
u\equiv 0
, which contradicts the fact that
u
blows up. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.5
.
∎

5.3.
A frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality

In this section, we use Proposition
5.1
to prove a frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality, which we will use in Section
5.4
to rule out the quasi-soliton scenario. As
s_{c}<1/2
, we prove an estimate that is localized to low frequencies.

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 5.6

(Frequency-localized Morawetz)
.

Let
u:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.14
. Let
I\subset[0,\infty)
be a compact time interval, which is a contiguous union of characteristic subintervals
J_{k}
. Then for any
\eta>0
, there exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(\eta)
such that for
N>N_{0}
, we have

\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u_{\leq N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt\lesssim_{u}\eta(N^{1-2s_{c}}+K_{I}),

(5.27)

where
K_{I}
is as in (
5.3
).

To prove Proposition
5.6
, we begin as in the proof of the standard Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality (
1.8
). We truncate the high frequencies of the solution and work with
u_{\leq N}
for some
N>0
. As
u_{\leq N}
is not a true solution to (
1.1
), we need to control error terms arising from this frequency projection. To do this, we choose
N
large enough to capture ‘most’ of the solution and use the estimates proved in Section
4.1
. We make these notions precise in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.7

(Low and high frequency control)
.

Let
u,

I
,
K_{I}
be as above. With all spacetime norms over
I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have the following.

For any
N>0
and
s>1/2
,

\||\nabla|^{s}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\lesssim_{u}N^{s-s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}.

(5.28)

For any
\eta>0
and
s>s_{c}
, there exists
N_{1}=N_{1}(s,\eta)
such that for
N>N_{1}
,

\||\nabla|^{s}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{s-s_{c}}.

(5.29)

For any
\eta>0
, there exists
N_{2}=N_{2}(\eta)>0
such that for
N>N_{2}
, we have

\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{-s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}.

(5.30)

Proof.

For (
5.28
), we let
s>1/2
and use (
5.5
) to estimate

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{M\leq N}M^{s}\|u_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\sum_{M\leq N}M^{s-s_{c}}(1+M^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}N^{s-s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}.

For (
5.29
), we first let
\eta>0
. Using almost periodicity and the fact that
\sup N(t)\leq 1
, we may find
C(\eta)>0
so that
\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{>C(\eta)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}<\eta.
Thus we can use Bernstein to see

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s}

\displaystyle u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\eta)^{s-s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq C(\eta)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+N^{s-s_{c}}\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{C(\eta)<\cdot\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}C(\eta)^{s-s_{c}}+\eta N^{s-s_{c}}.

Choosing
N_{1}\gg\eta^{-1/(s-s_{c})}C(\eta)
, we recover (
5.29
).

Finally, we note that (
5.30
) is just a restatement of (
5.6
). ∎

We turn to the proof of Proposition
5.6
.

Proof of Proposition
5.6
.

We take all spacetime norms over
I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

We let
0<\eta\ll 1
and choose

N>\max\{N_{1}(\tfrac{1}{2},\eta),N_{1}(\tfrac{1+s_{c}}{2},\eta^{2}),N_{1}(1,\eta),\tfrac{1}{\eta^{2}}N_{2}(\eta^{2})\},

where
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are as in Lemma
5.7
. In particular, interpolating (
5.28
) and (
5.29
) with
s=(1+s_{c})/2
, we get

\||\nabla|^{(1+s_{c})/2}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{3}}\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{(1-s_{c})/2}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/4}.

(5.31)

Moreover, as
\eta^{2}N>N_{2}
, we can apply (
5.30
) to
u_{>\eta^{2}N}
to get

\|u_{>\eta^{2}N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\lesssim\eta N^{-s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}.

(5.32)

We define the Morawetz action

\text{Mor}(t):=2\,\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nabla u_{\leq N}(t,x)\,\bar{u}_{\leq N}(t,x)\,dx.

A standard computation using
(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{\leq N}=P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)
gives

\partial_{t}\text{Mor}(t)\gtrsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\{P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big),u_{\leq N}\}_{P}\,dx,

(5.33)

where the
momentum bracket

\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{P}
is defined by
\{f,g\}_{P}:=\text{Re}(f\nabla\bar{g}-g\nabla\bar{f}).

Noting that
\{F(u),u\}_{P}=-\tfrac{p}{p+2}\nabla(|u|^{p+2}),
we integrate by parts in (
5.33
) to get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\text{Mor}(t)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int\frac{|u_{\leq N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx+\int\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\{P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)-F(u_{\leq N}),u_{\leq N}\}_{P}\,dx.

Thus, by the fundamental theorem of calculus we have

\displaystyle\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle\frac{|u_{\leq N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\text{Mor}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I)}+\bigg|\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\{P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)-F(u_{\leq N}),u_{\leq N}\}_{P}\,dx\,dt\bigg|.

To complete the proof of Proposition
5.6
, it therefore suffices to show

\|\text{Mor}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I)}\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1-2s_{c}},

(5.34)

\bigg|\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\{P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)\big)-F(u_{\leq N}),u_{\leq N}\}_{P}\,dx\,dt\bigg|\lesssim_{u}\eta(N^{1-2s_{c}}+K_{I}).

(5.35)

To prove (
5.34
), we use Bernstein, (
2.1
), and (
5.29
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|\text{Mor}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{-1/2}\nabla u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\||\nabla|^{1/2}(\tfrac{x}{|x|}u_{\leq N})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{1/2}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1-2s_{c}}.

We now turn to (
5.35
). We begin by rewriting

\displaystyle\{P_{\leq N}

\displaystyle\big(F(u)\big)-F(u_{\leq N}),u_{\leq N}\}_{P}

\displaystyle=\{F(u)-F(u_{\leq N}),u_{\leq N}\}_{P}-\{P_{>N}\big(F(u)\big),u_{\leq N}\}_{P}

\displaystyle=:I+II.

Writing

I=\text{\O}\big\{[F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})]\nabla u_{\leq N}+u_{\leq N}\nabla[F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})]\big\}

and integrating by parts in the second term, we find that the contribution of
I
to (
5.35
) is controlled by

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\leq N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

(5.36)

\displaystyle\quad+\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}.

(5.37)

Similarly, writing

II=\text{\O}\big\{P_{>N}\big(F(u)\big)\nabla u_{\leq N}+\nabla P_{>N}\big(F(u)\big)u_{\leq N}\big\}

and integrating by parts in the second term, we find that the contribution of
II
to (
5.35
) is controlled by

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\leq N}P_{>N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

(5.38)

\displaystyle\quad+\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}P_{>N}\big(F(u)\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

(5.39)

To complete the proof of (
5.35
), it therefore suffices to show that the error terms (
5.36
) through (
5.39
) are acceptable, in the sense that they can be controlled by
\eta(N^{1-2s_{c}}+K_{I}).

We first turn to (
5.36
). Using Hölder, (
1.4
), (
5.28
), and (
5.30
), we estimate

(
5.36
)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|u_{>N}\text{\O}(u^{p})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1-2s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I}),

which is acceptable.

We next turn to (
5.37
). We first write

(
5.37
)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}(u_{>N})^{p+1}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}+\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}(u_{\leq N})^{p+1}u_{>N}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

For the first piece, we use Hölder, Hardy, Bernstein, (
1.4
), (
5.29
), and (
5.30
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}(u_{>N})^{p+1}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}^{2}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\|\nabla u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}N^{s_{c}}\|\nabla u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1-2s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I}),

which is acceptable.

For the second piece, we use Hölder, Hardy, the chain rule, (
1.4
), (
5.28
), and (
5.30
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}(u_{\leq N})^{p+1}u_{>N}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}(u_{\leq N})^{p+1}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla(u_{\leq N})^{p+1}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p}\|\nabla u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|u_{>N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1-2s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I}),

which is acceptable. This completes the estimation of (
5.37
).

We next turn to (
5.38
). We first write

\eqref{IIa}\lesssim\|\nabla u_{\leq N}P_{>N}\big(F(u_{\leq\eta^{2}N})\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}+\|\nabla u_{\leq N}P_{>N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq\eta^{2}N})\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}.

For the first piece, we use Hölder, Bernstein, the chain rule, (
1.4
), and (
5.28
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\leq N}P_{>N}\big(F(u_{\leq\eta^{2}N})\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1}\|\nabla u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|\nabla F(u_{\leq\eta^{2}N})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1}\|\nabla u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p}\|\nabla u_{\leq\eta^{2}N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1-2s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I}),

which is acceptable.

For the second piece, we use Hölder, (
1.4
), (
5.28
), and (
5.32
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\leq N}P_{>N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq\eta^{2}N})\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|u_{>\eta^{2}N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1-2s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I}),

which is acceptable. This completes the estimation of (
5.38
).

Finally, we turn to (
5.39
). We first write

\eqref{IIb}\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}P_{>N}\big(F(u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}})\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}+\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}P_{>N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}})\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}.

For the first piece, we begin by noting that

P_{>N}\big(F(u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}})\big)=P_{>N}\big(P_{>\frac{N}{2}}(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{p})u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}\big).

Thus, using Cauchy–Schwarz, Hölder, Hardy, maximal function estimates, Bernstein, Sobolev embedding, (
1.4
), (
5.28
), and (
5.31
), we can estimate

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}P_{>N}\big(F(u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}})\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}\mathcal{M}\big(P_{>\frac{N}{2}}(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{p})u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}\big[\mathcal{M}\big(\big|P_{>\frac{N}{2}}(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{p})\big|^{2}\big)\big]^{1/2}\big[\mathcal{M}\big(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{2}\big)\big]^{1/2}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|^{1/2}}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\frac{12p}{4+p}}}\|\mathcal{M}\big(\big|P_{>\frac{N}{2}}(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{p})\big|^{2}\big)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{5p-4}}}^{1/2}\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}\mathcal{M}\big(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{2}\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{6p}{4+p}}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{1/2}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\frac{12p}{4+p}}}\|P_{>\frac{N}{2}}(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{p})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{6p}{5p-4}}}\|\nabla\mathcal{M}(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{2})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{6p}{4+p}}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1}\||\nabla|^{(1+s_{c})/2}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{3}}\|\nabla(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{p})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{6p}{5p-4}}}\|\nabla(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{2})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{6p}{4+p}}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1}\||\nabla|^{(1+s_{c})/2}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{3}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{p-1}\|\nabla u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{3p/2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}N^{-1}\||\nabla|^{(1+s_{c})/2}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{3}}\|\nabla u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}\|\nabla u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{1-2s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I}),

which is acceptable.

For the second piece, we write

\displaystyle\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}P_{>N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}})\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}\mathcal{M}\big(u_{>\frac{N}{4}}(u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}})^{p}\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

(5.40)

\displaystyle\quad+\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}\mathcal{M}\big((u_{>\frac{N}{4}})^{p+1}\big)\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}.

(5.41)

For (
5.40
), we use Cauchy–Schwarz, Hölder, Hardy, the maximal function estimate, Sobolev embedding, (
1.4
), (
5.28
), (
5.30
), and (
5.31
) to estimate

(
5.40
)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}u_{\leq N}\big[\mathcal{M}\big(|u_{>{\frac{N}{4}}}|^{2}|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{2(p-1)}\big)\big]^{1/2}\big[\mathcal{M}\big(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{2}\big)\big]^{1/2}\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tfrac{1}{|x|^{1/2}}u_{\leq N}\|_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\frac{12p}{4+p}}}\|\mathcal{M}\big(|u_{>\frac{N}{4}}|^{2}|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{2(p-1)}\big)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{\frac{3p}{5p-4}}}^{1/2}\|\tfrac{1}{|x|}\mathcal{M}\big(|u_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}|^{2})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{6p}{4+p}}}^{1/2}

5.4.
The quasi-soliton scenario

In this section, we preclude the existence of solutions as in Theorem
1.14
for which

K_{[0,\infty)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt=\infty.

(5.42)

We rely primarily on the frequency-localized Lin–Strauss Morawetz established in Section
5.3
. We also need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.8

(Lower bound)
.

Let
u:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.14
. Let
I\subset[0,\infty)
. Then there exists
N_{1}>0
such that for any
N>N_{1}
, we have

K_{I}\lesssim_{u}\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u_{\leq N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt,

(5.43)

where
K_{I}
is as in (
5.3
).

Proof.

We choose
C(u)
and
N_{1}
as in Lemma
1.11
. Then for
N>N_{1}
, we use Hölder and (
1.7
) to estimate

\displaystyle\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u_{\leq N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\int_{I}N(t)\int_{|x|\leq\frac{C(u)}{N(t)}}|u_{\leq N}(t,x)|^{p+2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\int_{I}N(t)^{1+\frac{3p}{2}}\bigg(\int_{|x|\leq\frac{C(u)}{N(t)}}|u_{\leq N}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{u}\int_{I}N(t)^{3+ps_{c}}\big(N(t)^{-2s_{c}}\big)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\,dt\gtrsim_{u}K_{I}.

∎

Finally, we prove the following.

Theorem 5.9

(No quasi-solitons)
.

There are no almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.14
such that (
5.42
) holds.

Proof.

Suppose
u
were such a solution. Let
\eta>0
and let
I\subset[0,\infty)
be a compact time interval, which is a contiguous union of characteristic subintervals.

Combining (
5.27
) and (
5.43
), we find that for
N
sufficiently large, we have

K_{I}\lesssim_{u}\eta(N^{1-2s_{c}}+K_{I}).

Choosing
\eta
sufficiently small, we deduce
K_{I}\lesssim_{u}N^{1-2s_{c}}
uniformly in
I
. We now contradict (
5.42
) by taking
I
sufficiently large inside of
[0,\infty)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.9
.
∎

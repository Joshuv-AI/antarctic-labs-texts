1.
Introduction

Generalized Orlicz spaces have been studied since the 1940s. They are also known as Musielak–Orlicz spaces due to more comprehensive presentation of J. Musielak in his monograph published in 1983
[
24
]
. Intuitively generalized Orlicz space
L^{\varphi}
consists of all measurable functions
f
such that

\varrho_{\varphi}(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x,|f(x)|)\,dx<\infty.

We call the function
\varrho_{\varphi}(f)
a
modular
. If the function
\varphi
does not depend directly on
x
,
\varphi(x,t)=\varphi(t)
,
then we obtain
Orlicz spaces
. Function
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}
gives us the
Lebesgue space

L^{p}
and
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
, the
variable exponent Lebesgue space

L^{p(\cdot)}
. Other important space is given by the double phase functional
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}
, where
q>p
. Apart from being a natural generalization for many well-researched function spaces, the study of generalized Orlicz spaces has applications to image processing, fluid dynamics, and differential equations; see, e.g.,
[
27
,
1
,
10
,
2
,
16
]
.

The Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
M
is a central tool in harmonic analysis.
It was proved by D. Gallardo in 1988
[
11
]
that
M:L^{\varphi}\to L^{\varphi}
for an Orlicz function

\varphi(x,t)=\varphi(t)
if and only if
\varphi
satisfies
(aInc)
p
with
p>1
. For an Orlicz function, condition
(aInc)
p
implies the existence of an equivalent convex function
\psi^{1/p}
(see Lemma 2.2.1,
[
15
]
), for which a Jensen-type inequality (Lemma
2.5
) can be obtained. Jensen’s inequality is a helpful tool in obtaining boundedness results, but the inequality does not hold as such in the generalized Orlicz space.

In 2004, L. Diening proved the boundedness of
M
locally in the variable exponent, with the exponent
p
being log-Hölder continuous
[
9
]
. The boundedness was a result of a trick that
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
satisfies Jensen’s inequality up to an error term independent of
t
. The technique was soon generalized to the global case by D. Cruz-Uribe, A. Fiorenza, and C.J. Neugebauer
[
5
]
, and independently by A. Nekvinda
[
25
]
. In the book
[
7
]
, Diening’s trick was named as
Key estimate
and formulated into Theorem 4.2.4.

In 2013, F-Y. Maeda, Y. Mizuta, T. Ohno and T. Shimomura

[
22
]
provided the first sufficient conditions for boundedness of
M
in
generalized Orlicz space using rather heavy machinery.
In 2015, P. Hästö
[
17
]
proved a sharper version with
\varphi
satisfying conditions
(A0)
-
(A2)
and
(aInc)
and simplified the proof. The proof was based on generalized version of the key estimate that was obtained by using
(A1)
to estimate the generalized
\varphi
function with a regular Orlicz function locally and then using Jensen-type inequality
2.5
. Finally, we note that the condition
(A0)
excludes weighted norm
inequalities, both in the classical case
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}\omega(x)
and in
the variable exponent case
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}\omega(x)
. In either case,
the condition
(A0)
requires the weight to be essentially constant.

While generalized Orlicz spaces have been actively studied during the last twenty years, weighted generalized Orlicz spaces have got only a little attention. The weighted space is defined with a weighted modular

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x,|f(x)|)\omega(x)\,dx,

where
\varphi
satisfies
(A0)
and
\omega
is a weight function. The crucial question is to give a property for the weight
\omega
so that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function is bounded. In 1972, B. Muckenhoupt
[
23
]
defined a class of weight functions, denoted by
A_{p}
, for which the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function is bounded in the weighted Lebesgue space
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}},\omega)
. The functions in this class bear the name
Muckenhoupt weights
. In variable exponent spaces, power-type weights have been studied by V. Kokilashvili N. Samko and S. Samko; see, e.g.
[
20
,
21
,
19
,
18
]
. Muckenhoupt-type weights have been studied by D. Cruz-Uribe, L. Diening, and P. Hästö
[
4
]
. The latter two authors have presented an alternative approach in preprint
[
8
]
, where the weight
\omega
is treated as a measure instead of a multiplier.

It was proved by A. Gogatishvili and V. Kokilashvili
[
13
]
in 1994 that the maximal operator
M
is bounded in weighted Orlicz space if and only if
\varphi
satisfies
(aInc)
p
and
\omega\in A_{p}
. This condition for weight
\omega
is also known to be sufficient in the variable exponent space
[
8
]
.
In this paper we prove the sufficiency of this condition in generalized Orlicz space. We use a similar approach as in
[
8
]
by treating the weight
\omega
as a measure. We obtain a weighted version of the
key estimate
(Theorem 4.3.2 from
[
15
]
), which plays a major role in the proof of unweighted case. This requires generalizing conditions
(A1)
and
(A2)
to
(A1)
ω
and
(A2)
ω
, which are in line with the weighted space. At the end of this paper, we consider these new conditions with functions
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
and
\varphi(x,t):=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
be an open set equipped with the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. By
B
we always denote an open ball in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. If there exists a constant
C
such that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
for almost every
x
, then we write
f\lesssim g
. If
g\lesssim f\lesssim g
, then we write
f\approx g
. We say that functions
\varphi,\psi:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
are
equivalent
if there exists
L\geq 1
such that
\psi(x,\frac{1}{L}t)\leq\varphi(x,t)\leq\psi(x,Lt)
. A function
f:(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called
almost increasing
if there exists a constant
a\geq 1
such that
f(s)\leq af(t)
for all
0<s<t
.

For a locally integrable function
f\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
, we define the (Hardy-Littlewood)
maximal operator

M
by

Mf(x)=\sup_{B\ni x}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B\cap\Omega}|f(y)|\,dy,

where the supremum is taken over all open balls
B
containing
x
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
p>0
. We say that a function
\varphi:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
satisfies (aInc)
p
if
t\to\frac{\varphi(x,t)}{t^{p}}
is almost increasing, i.e there exists
a\geq 1
such that

\frac{\varphi(x,s)}{s^{p}}\leq a\frac{\varphi(x,t)}{t^{p}}

for all
0<s<t
.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
\varphi:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is said to be a (generalized)
\Phi
-
prefunction
if
x\to\varphi(x,|f(x)|)
is measurable for every
f\in L^{0}(\Omega)
and
t\to\varphi(x,t)
is increasing with
\varphi(x,0)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\varphi(x,t)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\varphi(x,t)=\infty
for almost every
x\in\Omega
.

We say that the
\Phi
-
prefunction
is a
weak

\Phi
-
function
, and write
\varphi\in\Phi_{\textnormal{w}}(\Omega)
, if it satisfies
(aInc)
1
on
(0,\infty)
for a.e
x\in\Omega
. If, in addition,
\varphi\in\Phi_{\textnormal{w}}(\Omega)
is convex and left-continuous with respect to
t
for almost every
x
, then we write
\varphi\in\Phi_{\textnormal{c}}(\Omega)
.

For
\varphi\in\Phi_{\textnormal{w}}(\Omega)
and
B\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, we define

\varphi_{B}^{+}(t):=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{x\in B\cap\Omega}\varphi(x,t)\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{B}^{-}(t):=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,inf}_{x\in B\cap\Omega}\varphi(x,t).

We say that
\varphi\in\Phi(\Omega)
is
degenerate
if
\varphi|_{(0,\infty)}\equiv 0
or
\varphi|_{(0,\infty)}\equiv\infty
. If
\varphi^{+}_{B}
or
\varphi^{-}_{B}
is non-degenerate, then it is a weak
\Phi
-function (
[
15
]
, Lemma 2.5.16).

By
\omega
we always denote a
weight
, that is a non-negative and locally integrable function. We often denote
\omega(\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)dx
, and in this sense we treat
\omega
as a measure.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{\textnormal{w}}(\Omega)
and
p\geq 0
. We say that
\varphi
satisfies

(A0):

if there exists
\beta_{0}\in(0,1]
such that
\varphi(x,\beta_{0})\leq 1\leq\varphi(x,\frac{1}{\beta_{0}})
for almost every
x\in\Omega
.

(A1)
ω
:

if there exists
\beta_{1}\in(0,1]
such that
\varphi(x,\beta_{1}t)\leq\varphi(y,t)
for every
\varphi(y,t)\in[1,\frac{1}{\omega(B)}]
, almost every
x,y\in B\cap\Omega
and every ball
B
with
\omega(B)\leq 1
.

(A2)
ω
:

if for every
s>0
there exist
\beta_{2}\in(0,1]
and
h\in L^{1}(\Omega,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
h\geq 0
, such that
\varphi(x,\beta_{2}t)\leq\varphi(y,t)+h(x)+h(y)
for almost every
x,y\in\Omega
when
\varphi(y,t)\in[0,s]
.

If
\omega(x)=1
, then condition
(A1)
ω
is equivalent to the standard condition (A1
′
) presented in the book
[
15
]
. According to book’s Lemma 4.2.5,
(A2)
ω
is equivalent to condition (A2), but the book formulation of (A2) contains a flaw that was recently corrected in
[
14
]
.

The following formulation of
(A2)
ω
will be useful when considering the condition in variable exponent spaces.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{\textnormal{w}}(\Omega)
. Then
\varphi
satisfies
(A2)
ω
if and only if there exist
\varphi_{\infty}\in\Phi_{\textnormal{w}}
,
h\in L^{1}(\Omega,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\beta\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(x,\beta t)\leq\varphi_{\infty}(t)+h(x)\quad\text{when}\quad\varphi_{\infty}(t)\in[0,1],

and

\displaystyle\varphi_{\infty}(\beta t)\leq\varphi(x,t)+h(x)\quad\text{when}\quad\varphi(x,t)\in[0,1]

for almost every
x\in\Omega
.

Proof.

The proof does not differ from the proofs of Lemmas 4.2.7 and 4.2.10 from
[
15
]
, which cover the case
\omega(x)=1
.
∎

The next Jensen-type inequality is an important tool in obtaining the
key estimate
, Theorem
4.1
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
15
]
, Lemma 4.3.1)
.

Let
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
be a prefunction that satisfies
(aInc)
p
,
p>0
. Then there exists
\beta\in(0,1]
such that the following inequality holds for every measurable set
U
,
|U|\in(0,\infty)
, and every
f\in L^{1}(U)
:

\varphi\left(\beta\fint_{U}|f|\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\fint_{U}\varphi(f)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dx.

Next we give an exact definition of
weighted generalized Orlicz space
; starting with the definition of modular
\varrho_{\varphi}^{\omega}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{\textnormal{w}}(\Omega)
. We define the
weighted modular

\varrho_{\varphi}^{\omega}(\cdot)
for
f\in L^{0}(\Omega)
by

\varrho_{\varphi}^{\omega}(f):=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|f(x)|)\omega(x)\,dx.

The
weighted generalized Orlicz space
is defined as the set

L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega):=\{f\in L^{0}(\Omega):\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}<\infty\},

where

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}:=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\varrho^{\omega}_{\varphi}\bigg(\frac{f}{\lambda}\bigg)\leq 1\bigg\}.

An useful relation between the modular and the norm is the so-called
unit ball property:

Lemma 2.7

(
[
15
]
, Lemma 3.2.3)
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{\textnormal{w}}(\Omega).
Then

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}<1\ \implies\ \varrho^{\omega}_{\varphi}(f)\leq 1\ \implies\ \left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}\leq 1.

If
\varphi
is left-continuous, then
\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{\omega}_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}\leq 1\ \iff\ \varrho^{\omega}_{\varphi}(f)\leq 1
.

The unit ball property holds for any weight
\omega
and more general measures as well.

For more information about generalized Orlicz spaces see the monograph
[
15
]
.

3.
Properties of
A_{p}
-weights

The classical Muckenhoupt condition can be stated as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
1<q<\infty
and
q^{\prime}
satisfy
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
. If a non-negative and locally integrable weight function
\omega
satisfies

[\omega]_{A_{q}}:=\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}|B|^{-q}\omega(B)\left(\int_{B}\omega(x)^{-q^{\prime}/q}\,dx\right)^{q-1}<\infty,

where
\mathcal{B}
denotes the family of all open balls
B\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, then we say that
\omega\in A_{q}
.

We define the class
A_{\infty}
as the union of all classes
A_{q}
,
q\in[1,\infty)
, and the class
A_{1}
to consist of all weights
\omega
satisfying
M\omega\lesssim\omega
.

In a paper
[
23
]
, published in 1972, B. Muckenhoupt showed the following famous result for weights satisfying the above condition.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
23
]
, Theorem 9)
.

Let
1<q<\infty
. Then
\omega\in A_{q}
if and only if

M:L^{q}(\Omega,\omega)\to L^{q}(\Omega,\omega).

The next lemma gives us an alternative definition for the class
A_{q}
, which is more closely related to boundedness of
M
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
12
]
, Theorem 1.12)
.

Let
1<q<\infty
and
[\omega]_{A_{q}}
be the constant from
A_{q}
condition. Then
\omega\in A_{q}
if and only if

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}f(x)\,dx\right)^{q}\leq\frac{[\omega]_{A_{q}}}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}f(x)^{q}\omega(x)\,dx

for all measurable
f\geq 0
and for all open balls
B\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.

The following property of weights in
A_{q}
classes is often useful in controlling
\omega(B)
with various operations; see, e.g., Lemma
5.4
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1<q<\infty
. If
\omega\in A_{q},
then

\omega(B(x,r))\gtrsim\omega(B(y,R))\left(\frac{r}{|x-y|+r+R}\right)^{qn},

for every
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
R,r>0
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
R,r>0
. Since
\omega\in A_{q}
, we have by Lemma
3.2
that
M:L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{n}},\omega)\to L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{n}},\omega)
. Then

\displaystyle\omega(B(x,r))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\chi_{B(x,r)}(z))^{q}\omega(z)dz\gtrsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(M\chi_{B(x,r)}(z))^{q}\omega(z)dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sup_{B\ni z}\frac{|B\cap B(x,r)|}{|B|}\right)^{q}\omega(z)dz.

Let us choose
B=B(y,|x-y|+r+R)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sup_{B\ni z}\frac{|B\cap B(x,r)|}{|B|}\right)^{q}\omega(z)dz

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(y,R)}\left(\frac{|B(x,r)|}{|B(y,|x-y|+r+R)|}\right)^{q}\omega(z)dz

\displaystyle=\omega(B(y,R))\left(\frac{r}{|x-y|+r+R}\right)^{qn}.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

We can observe that the weight
\omega\in A_{q}
is doubling as a measure since by Lemma
3.4
it follows that
\omega(B(x,r))\gtrsim\omega(B(x,2r))3^{-qn}.

Remark 3.6
.

We can also notice that by Lemma
3.4
the weight
\omega\in A_{\infty}
has as at most polynomial growth i.e
\omega(0,r)\lesssim\omega(B(0,1))(1+r)^{qn}\lesssim r^{qn}
for some
q\in[1,\infty)
when
r>1
.

4.
Boundedness

Next we prove the key estimate in weighted generalized Orlicz space. We extend the techniques from
[
15
]
to the weighted case.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfy
(A0)
,
(A1)
ω
,
(A2)
ω
and
(aInc)
p
, with
p\in(0,\infty)
. Let
\omega\in A_{p}
. Then there exists
\beta>0
and
h\in L^{1}(\Omega,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\varphi\left(x,\frac{\beta}{|B|}\int_{B\cap\Omega}|f|\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B\cap\Omega}\varphi(y,|f|)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy+h(x)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B\cap\Omega}h(y)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy

for every ball
B
,
x\in B\cap\Omega
and
f\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)
with
\varrho_{\varphi}^{\omega}(f)\leq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\beta_{0}
be the constant from
(A0)
,
\beta_{1}
the constant from
(A1)
ω
, and
\beta_{2}
from
(A2)
ω
. Let
\beta_{J}
be the constant from the Jensen-type inequality Lemma
2.5
.
We may assume without loss of generality that
f\geq 0
. Fix a ball
B
, denote
\hat{B}:=B\cap\Omega
and choose
x\in\hat{B}
. Denote
f_{1}:=f\chi_{\{f>1/\beta_{0}\}}
,
f_{2}:=f-f_{1}
, and
D_{i}:=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}f_{i}\,dy
.

We first estimate
D_{1}
. Let
c_{\omega}:=\max\{1,[\omega]_{A_{p}}\}
. Now
\varphi
satisfies
(aInc)
1
and thus
\varphi_{B}^{-}(\frac{1}{a}c_{\omega}^{-1}f_{1})\leq c_{\omega}^{-1}\varphi_{B}^{-}(f_{1})
. Then by Lemmas
2.5
and
3.3
, assuming
\varrho_{\varphi}^{\omega}(f_{1})\leq\varrho_{\varphi}^{\omega}(f)\leq 1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\varphi_{B}^{-}(\tfrac{1}{2a}c_{\omega}^{-1}\beta_{J}D_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}\varphi^{-}_{B}(\tfrac{1}{2a}c_{\omega}^{-1}f_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{[\omega]_{A_{p}}}{\omega(B)}\int_{\hat{B}}\varphi^{-}_{B}(\tfrac{1}{2a}c_{\omega}^{-1}f_{1})\omega(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{\hat{B}}\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{-}_{B}(f_{1})\omega(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\omega(B)}\int_{\hat{B}}\varphi(y,f_{1})\omega(y)\,dy<\frac{1}{\omega(B)},

where we used
(aInc)
1
for the third inequality.
Denote
\beta^{\prime}_{J}:=\frac{1}{2a}c_{\omega}^{-1}\beta_{J}
, and we have
\varphi_{B}^{-}(\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})<\frac{1}{\omega(B)}
. We may also note that
\beta^{\prime}_{J}\leq 1
.

Suppose first that
\varphi^{-}_{B}(\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})\geq 1
. This implies
\omega(B)<1
. Then there exists
y\in\hat{B}
with
\varphi(y,\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})\in[1,\frac{1}{\omega(B)}]
and
\varphi(y,\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})\leq 2\varphi_{B}^{-}(\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})
. Then by
(A1)
ω
and Lemma
2.5
for the third inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi(x,\beta_{1}\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\varphi(y,\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varphi_{B}^{-}(\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}\varphi_{B}^{-}(f_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy\leq\frac{2}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}\varphi(y,f_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy.

Next we consider the case
\varphi^{-}_{B}(\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})<1
. By
(A0)
, this implies that
\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1}\leq\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}
. By
(aInc)
p
and
(A0)
, we conclude that

\varphi(x,\beta_{0}^{2}\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq a^{\frac{1}{p}}\beta_{0}\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1}\varphi(x,\beta_{0})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq a^{\frac{1}{p}}\beta_{0}\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1}\leq a^{\frac{1}{p}}D_{1}.

By
(A0)
,
1\leq\varphi(y,1/\beta_{0})
. Since
f_{1}>1/\beta_{0}
, when it is non-zero, we find by
(aInc)
p
for the second inequality, that

D_{1}=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}f_{1}\,dy\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}f_{1}\varphi(y,\frac{1}{\beta_{0}})^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy\leq\frac{a^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\beta_{0}|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}\varphi(y,f_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy.

In view of this and the conclusion of previous paragraph, we obtain final estimate for
f_{1}

\varphi(x,\frac{\beta_{0}}{2a^{\frac{3}{p}}}\beta_{0}^{2}\beta_{1}\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{\beta_{0}}{2a^{\frac{2}{p}}}\varphi(x,\beta_{0}^{2}\beta_{1}\beta^{\prime}_{J}D_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}\varphi(y,f_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy,

where we also used
(aInc)
p
for the first inequality.

Let us move to
f_{2}
. Since
f_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}
, by
(A0)
we obtain
\varphi(y,\beta_{0}^{2}f_{2})\leq\varphi(y,\beta_{0})\leq 1
. This allows us to use
(A2)
ω
, and with Lemma
2.5
for
\varphi(x,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{p}}
(with
x
fixed) we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi(x,\beta_{J}\beta_{0}^{2}\beta_{2}D_{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}\varphi(x,\beta_{0}^{2}\beta_{2}f_{2}(y))^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}\varphi(y,\beta_{0}^{2}f_{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}+h(x)^{\frac{1}{p}}+h(y)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}\varphi(y,f_{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}+h(x)^{\frac{1}{p}}+h(y)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy.

Since
\varphi^{\frac{1}{p}}
is increasing,

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x,\frac{\beta}{|B|}\int_{\hat{B}}f\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(x,2\beta\max\{D_{1},D_{2}\})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(x,2\beta D_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}+\varphi(x,2\beta D_{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
\beta>0
.
Adding the estimates for
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, we conclude the proof by choosing
\beta:=\frac{1}{2}\min\{2^{-1}a^{-3/p}\beta_{0}^{3}\beta_{1}\beta^{\prime}_{J},\ \beta_{J}\beta_{0}^{2}\beta_{2}\}
.
∎

This key estimate gives us the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{\textnormal{w}}(\Omega)
satisfy
(A0)
,
(A1)
ω
,
(A2)
ω
and
(aInc)
p
with
p\in(0,\infty)
. Let
\omega\in A_{p}
. Then there exists
\beta>0
and
h\in L^{1}(\Omega,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\varphi(x,\beta Mf(x))^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim M(\varphi(\cdot,f)^{\frac{1}{p}})(x)+M(h^{\frac{1}{p}})(x)

for every
f\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)
with
\varrho_{\varphi}^{\omega}(f)\leq 1
.

Now we are ready to prove the main result.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{\textnormal{w}}(\Omega)
satisfy
(A0)
,
(A1)
ω
,
(A2)
ω
and
(aInc)
p
, with
p\in(1,\infty)
. If
\omega\in A_{p}
, then for all
f\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)
,

\|Mf\|_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}<1
.
From the unit ball property (Lemma
2.7
) we have that
\varrho_{\varphi}^{\omega}(f)\leq 1
.
Then by Corollary
4.2
we obtain

\varphi(x,\beta Mf(x))^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim M(\varphi(\cdot,f)^{\frac{1}{p}})(x)+M(h^{\frac{1}{p}})(x).

Multiplying both sides with
\omega^{1/p}
, raising to the power
p
and integrating, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,\beta Mf(x))\omega(x)\,dx\lesssim\int_{\Omega}[M(\varphi(\cdot,f)^{\frac{1}{p}})

\displaystyle(x)]^{p}\omega(x)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}[M(h^{\frac{1}{p}})(x)]^{p}\omega(x)\,dx

Since
\omega\in A_{p}
we have by Lemma
3.2
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,\beta Mf(x))\omega(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,f)\omega(x)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}h(x)\omega(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\varrho^{\omega}_{\varphi}(f)+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega,\omega)}.

Thus
\varrho^{\omega}_{\varphi}(\beta Mf)\leq c(1+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega,\omega)})=:c_{1}
and by (aInc)
1
,
\varrho^{\omega}_{\varphi}(\frac{\beta}{ac_{1}}Mf)\leq 1
. Again by the unit ball property we have that
\left\lVert Mf\right\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}\leq\frac{ac_{1}}{\beta}\lesssim 1
and the case
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}<1
is complete.

If
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}\geq 1
, then we can reduce the claim to the previous case by considering the function
g:=\frac{f}{2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\omega)}}
with norm less than one.
∎

5.
Special cases

Variable exponent Lebesgue spaces

We obtain variable exponent

Lebesgue spaces when
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
for some measurable function
p:\Omega\to[1,\infty]
. Here we interpret
t^{\infty}:=\infty\chi_{(1,\infty)}(t)
and
t^{\frac{1}{\infty}}:=\chi_{(0,\infty)}(t)
. Next, we present the standard assumptions for the exponent
p
: local
\log
-Hölder continuity and
\log
-Hölder decay. If we assume any weaker modulus of continuity than
\log
-Hölder continuity, then the maximal operator
M
need not be bounded
[
26
]
. In this sense the
\log
-Hölder continuity is necessary for boundedness of
M
. For more information on variable exponent spaces, see
[
7
,
6
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

If there exists
c_{1}>0
such that the measurable function
p:\Omega\to[1,\infty]
satisfies

\left|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p(y)}\right|\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\log(\mathrm{e}+1/|x-y|)}

for every
x,y\in\Omega
, then we say that
\frac{1}{p}
is
locally

\log
-Hölder
continuous
on
\Omega
,
\frac{1}{p}\in C^{\log}
.

If there exist
p_{\infty}\in[1,\infty]
and
c_{2}>0
such that

\left|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}\right|\leq\frac{c_{2}}{\log(\mathrm{e}+|x|)}

for every
x\in\Omega
, then we say that
\frac{1}{p}
satisfies the
\log
-Hölder
decay condition
.

If
\frac{1}{p}
satisfies both of the conditions above, we denote
\frac{1}{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\Omega)
. We also denote
p^{+}_{B}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{x\in B\cap\Omega}p(x)
and
p^{-}_{B}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,inf}_{x\in B\cap\Omega}p(x)
.

Remark 5.2
.

The
\log
-Hölder decay condition can be weakened by a condition introduced by A. Nekvinda in
[
25
]
. The decay condition implies Nekvinda’s condition which states that there exists
\beta>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}\beta^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}\,dx<\infty.

This remains true also in the weighted case, as will be seen in Lemma
5.6
.

Next we are going to link these conditions to the ones used in weighted generalized Orlicz space.

Lemma 5.3

(Lemma 7.1.1,
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\frac{1}{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\Omega)
. Then the
\Phi
-function
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
satisfies
(A0)
and
(Inc)
1
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\frac{1}{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\Omega)
and
\omega\in A_{\infty}
. Then for all
B\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
we have

\omega(B)^{\frac{1}{p_{B}^{+}}-\frac{1}{p_{B}^{-}}}\lesssim 1.

Proof.

Let
B:=B(x,r)
. Since
\omega\in A_{\infty}
, there exists
q>1
such that
\omega\in A_{q}
.
By Lemma
3.4

\omega(B(x,r))\gtrsim\omega(B(0,1))\left(\frac{r}{|x|+r+1}\right)^{qn}.

With estimate
\frac{r}{|x|+r+1}\geq\frac{r}{|x|+2\max\{r,1\}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{r}{\max\{r,1\}}(|x|+1)^{-1}
we have that

\displaystyle\omega(B(x,r))^{\frac{1}{p_{B}^{+}}-\frac{1}{p_{B}^{-}}}\lesssim\omega(B(0,1))^{\frac{1}{p_{B}^{+}}-\frac{1}{p_{B}^{-}}}(\tfrac{r}{\max\{r,1\}})^{\left(\frac{1}{p_{B}^{+}}-\frac{1}{p_{B}^{-}}\right)qn}(|x|+1)^{\left(\frac{1}{p_{B}^{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{B}^{+}}\right)qn}.

Now the first term is a constant, second is either
1
or bounded by
\log
-Hölder continuity, and the last term is bounded by
\log
-Hölder decay condition.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\omega\in A_{\infty}
and
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
, where
\frac{1}{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\Omega)
. Then
\varphi
satisfies
(A1)
ω
.

Proof.

Assume that
B
is a ball with
\omega(B)\leq 1
and
x,y\in B\cap\Omega
. We need to show that there exists
\beta_{1}
such that
\varphi(x,\beta_{1}t)\leq\varphi(y,t)
when
\varphi(y,t)\in[1,\frac{1}{\omega(B)}]
. This is equivalent to showing that
t^{\frac{p(x)-p(y)}{p(x)}}\lesssim 1
when
t^{p(y)}\in[1,\frac{1}{\omega(B)}]
. We may assume that
p(x)>p(y)
, since the other case follows directly from
t>1
. Then by Lemma
5.4
,

t^{\frac{p(x)-p(y)}{p(x)}}\leq\omega(B)^{\frac{p(y)-p(x)}{p(x)p(y)}}\leq\omega(B)^{p_{B}^{-}-p_{B}^{+}}\lesssim 1,

and the claim follows.
∎

The next lemma shows that the weighted Nekvinda decay condition follows.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\omega\in A_{\infty}
and
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
, where
\frac{1}{p}
satisfies the
\log
-Hölder decay condition. Then there exists
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that

\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}\omega(x)\,dx<\infty.

Proof.

By the
\log
-Hölder decay condition,
|\tfrac{1}{p(x)}-\tfrac{1}{p_{\infty}}|\leq\frac{c}{\log(e+|x|)}
. Since
\omega\in A_{\infty}
, there exists
q>1
such that
\omega\in A_{q}.
Then by Lemma
3.4
,
\omega(B(0,r))\lesssim r^{qn}
when
r>1
. It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}\omega(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{B(0,j)\setminus B(0,j-1)}\lambda^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}\omega(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{c\log(e+j-1)}\int_{B(0,j)\setminus B(0,j-1)}\omega(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{c\log\lambda}\omega(B(0,j))

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{c\log\lambda+qn}<\infty,

by choosing
\lambda<e^{-(\frac{qn+1}{c})}
.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\omega\in A_{\infty}
and
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
, where
\frac{1}{p}
satisfies the
\log
-Hölder decay condition.. Then
\varphi
satisfies
(A2)
ω
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.6
, there exist
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
p_{\infty}\in[1,\infty]
such that

\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}\omega(x)\,dx<\infty.

Let us assume that
\varphi_{\infty}(t):=t^{p_{\infty}}\leq 1
. If
p(x)\geq p_{\infty}
, then
(\lambda t)^{p(x)}\leq t^{p_{\infty}}
. When
p(x)<p_{\infty}<\infty
, it follows from Young’s inequality that

(\lambda t)^{p(x)}\leq t^{p_{\infty}}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}.

If
p_{\infty}=\infty
, then
t^{p_{\infty}}=0
and
\lambda^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}}=\lambda^{p(x)}
. The other case, where
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}\leq 1
, is analogous. By Lemma
2.4
with

h(x):=\lambda^{\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}|}},

\varphi
satisfies
(A2)
ω
.∎

Double phase spaces

In the double phase case
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}
, with
1\leq p<q<\infty
, the central issue is the behavior
of
a
around the zero set
\{x:a(x)=0\}
. M. Colombo and C. Mingione
[
3
]

found that the critical Hölder exponent with which
a
must
approach zero is
\frac{n}{p}(q-p)
, which also gives a sufficient
condition for (A1) to hold
[
17
]
. A similar observation applies in the weighted double phase case.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}
with
1\leq p<q<\infty
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
non-negative. Then
\varphi
satisfies
(A0)
,
(A2)
ω
, and
(Inc)
p
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as that of Proposition 7.2.1 from
[
15
]
.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}
with
1\leq p<q<\infty
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be non-negative. Then
\varphi
satisfies
(A1)
ω
if and only if

a(x)\lesssim a(y)+\omega(B)^{\frac{q-p}{p}}

for every
x,y\in B\cap\Omega.

Proof.

We may notice that
\varphi(x,t)\approx\max\{t^{p},a(x)t^{q}\}
. Then
(A1)
ω
becomes

\max\{\beta_{1}^{p}t^{p},a(x)\beta_{1}^{q}t^{q}\}\leq\max\{t^{p},a(y)t^{q}\}

for
x,y\in B\cap\Omega
,
\omega(B)\leq 1
and
\varphi(y,t)\in[1,\frac{1}{\omega(B)}]
.
Dividing the inequality by
t^{q}
we get

\max\{\beta_{1}^{p}t^{p-q},a(x)\beta_{1}^{q}\}\leq\max\{t^{p-q},a(y)\}.

This holds trivially if
a(x)\beta_{1}^{q}\leq\beta_{1}^{p}t^{p-q}
, and thus the condition is equivalent to

a(x)\beta_{1}^{q}\leq\max\{t^{p-q},a(y)\}

for
a(x)\beta_{1}^{q}>\beta_{1}^{p}t^{p-q}
.

From
\varphi(y,t)\in[1,\frac{1}{\omega(B)}]
it follows that
t\leq\frac{1}{\omega(B)^{1/p}}
. Since
p-q<0
it suffices to use only the upper bound for
t
. Thus, the inequality above becomes

a(x)\lesssim\max\{a(y),\omega(B)^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\}\approx a(y)+\omega(B)^{\frac{q-p}{p}}.\qed

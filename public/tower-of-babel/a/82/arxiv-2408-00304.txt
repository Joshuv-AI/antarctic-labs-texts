Constraint Energy Minimizing Generalized Multiscale Finite Element Method for Convection Diffusion Equations with Inhomogeneous Boundary Conditions
Po Chai WONG, Eric T. CHUNG, Changqing YE, Lina ZHAO

Abstract
\emph{Keywords} constraint energy minimization, multiscale finite element methods, inhomogeneous boundary value problem, convection-diffusion equation



1
Introduction

Convection diffusion equation is involved in many physical applications of partial differential equations. Computational difficulty may arise in two-fold: (1) coefficients in high contrast and multiple scales and (2) demanding discretization for a high Péclet number. A lot of multiscale effort has contributed to the problems such as multiscale finite element method
[
1
]
,
variational multiscale method
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
, multiscale discontinuous Galerkin method
[
6
,
7
]
, multiscale stablization
[
8
,
9
]
.
In particular, the Generalized Multiscale Finite Element Method (GMsFEM) aims to create multiscale basis to apply Galerkin approximation and it has been applied to an array of partial differential equations
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.
Similar to the finite element method, it consists of two stages: first the offline stage where basis functions are generated and used to span the approximation solution manifold; and second the online stage where the actual approximation is found in the generated space. However, due to the complexity of the problem, it is necessary to further reduce the computational cost of the offline stage.

A spectral decomposition method, Constraint Energy Minimization Generalized Multiscale Finite Element Method (CEM-GMsFEM), is then applied to the scheme to generate a sufficiently large solution manifold by using a few basis functions
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.
Each basis function is formed by the eigenfunction in a local spectral problem and captures some information about the medium and velocity, and thereby dependent on the Péclet number. One main important property of such eigenfunction is exponential decay. The span of such basis therefore can capture the cell decaying part of the solution while the non-decaying part is left to be dealt with in the online stage. However, it is only common to see the above results conducted in a homogeneous setting whilst, in practice, inhomogeneous boundary conditions are necessary. We propose a numerical scheme to apply CEM-GMsFEM to solve the convection-diffusion equation with inhomogeneous boundary conditions. There are two versions of CEM-GMsFEM: constrained and relaxed. The relaxed-CEM-GMsFEM is used to develop the error analysis, which takes advantage of the elliptic projection of the solution as a bridge between our multiscale space and the continuous space. An account of relaxed-CEM-GMsFEM applied to some inhomogeneous Boundary Value Problems was given already in
[
25
]
. Our goal is to extend this idea to the convection-diffusion equation. We will study three main cases, Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin conditions. We also give a theoretical account of the convergence analysis and prove the scheme has first-order convergence in energy norm and second-order in
L^{2}-
norm, as verified by numerical examples.

For non-time independent problems, some work of applying CEM-GMsFEM has been done for parabolic equations
[
23
]
on homogeneous conditions. Our goal is to extend our method to the convection-diffusion equation with inhomogeneous boundary conditions. Since we assume the medium and velocity are independent of time, we can reuse the multiscale space in the time-independent case. For time-invariant boundary conditions, the corrector can be pre-computed once. However, for time-variant boundary conditions, the time derivative of the corrector and the boundary conditions need to be taken into account. Therefore, we give a new formulation to update the corrector at each time step in a relaxed CEM-GMsFEM fashion. The error of such approximation is also proved to be exponentially decaying in space. In addition, there are two versions of Backward Euler schemes to compute the next steps of the solution: the diffusion approach (D-approach) and the convection-diffusion approach (CD-approach). The former is less expensive but can be shown to bear a lower accuracy. We compare them via convection diffusion IBVPs and verify that our proposal gives a more accurate result at a higher computational expense.

Moreover, we introduce a nonlinearity into the IBVPs, which greatly increases the difficulty. A classical approach is to apply the Strang splitting method which considers convection, diffusion, and the nonlinearity terms in separate intermediate steps. This method utilizes the symmetric property of the algorithm to split operators in intermediate timesteps. This has been applied to a variety of problems including parabolic equations
[
26
]
,
diffusion-reaction equations
[
27
,
28
]

and diffusion-reaction-advection equations with a homogeneous boundary conditions
[
29
]
.
However, it was numerically tested that the inhomogeneous boundary conditions would drag the overall accuracy of the algorithm
[
30
,
29
,
31
]
.
To resolve this, some classical approaches can be found in
[
31
,
32
]
.
More recent approaches involve designing a time-dependent boundary corrector in the intermediate steps
[
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
,
which is aligned with our previous sections. In particular, in the classical Strang splitting method applied to convection diffusion equations, the convection and diffusion operators are split and considered separately with their corresponding nonlinear terms
[
37
]
.
Now with our scheme, we can consider them in the same step. We tested our choice of boundary corrector, the same as the previous part, to attain both spatial and temporal convergence.

The paper is organized as follows: we first give the problem setting and some preliminaries in section 2. The convergence analysis of its application to the time-independent convection diffusion equation is given in section 3, along with numerical results on the Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin conditions. In section 4, both time-variant and time-invariant IBVPs are presented with analysis and numerical results, along with the comparison of different finite difference schemes for temporal discretization. A demonstration of applying this to nonlinear problems with Strang splitting is presented in this section as well.

Preliminaries

2.1
Problem setting

We consider the following convection diffusion initial boundary value problem:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\bm{\beta}(x)\cdot\nabla u=\nabla\cdot(\bm{A}(x)\nabla u)+f,&\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u=g(x,t),&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}\times(0,T],\\
b(x)u+\bm{\nu}\cdot(\bm{A}(x)\nabla u-\bm{\beta}(x)u)=q(x,t),&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N}\times(0,T],\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{\operatorfont init},&\text{ in }\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the computational domain,
\bm{\beta}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{2}
, and
0<T<\infty
.
The medium
\bm{A}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and the velocity
\bm{\beta}
are heterogeneous coefficients with multiple scales and potentially high contrast.
\bm{A}
is a positive definite matrix. There exist
\kappa_{0}
and
\kappa_{1}
such that

and
\kappa_{1}/\kappa_{0}

We denote
\bm{\nu}
the outward unit normal vectors to
\partial\Omega
and
\Gamma_{D}
and
\Gamma_{N}
two nonempty disjoint part of
\partial\Omega
.
We assume the velocity flows inward on the Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{N}
and
\bm{\beta}
is incompressible, i.e.,

\nabla\cdot\bm{\beta}=0
. i.e.,
\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu}\leq 0
on
\Gamma_{N}
. Denote
\beta_{0}\geq 1
and
\beta_{1}
as the infimum and supremum of
|\bm{\beta}|
respectively.
The function
b(x)\geq 0
a.e.

and there exists a positive constant
b_{0}>0
and a subset
\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{N}
with positive measure such that
b(x)\geq b_{0}
for a.e.
x\in\Gamma
.
The Dirichlet boundary value term
g\in\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D}\times[0,T])
and the Neumann boundary value term
q\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{N}\times[0,T])
.

From now on, we denote

\displaystyle a(w,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{A}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Gamma_{N}}(b-\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu})wv\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(w,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{A}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Gamma_{N}}(b-\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu})wv\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma+\int_{\Omega}(\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla w)v.

So the variational form of the problem is: for
t\in(0,T]
, find
u_{0}(\cdot,t)\in V\coloneqq\{v\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)\colon v=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}\}
such that at

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}u_{0},v)+\mathcal{A}(u_{0},v)

\displaystyle=(f,v)-\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{g},v)+(q,v)_{\Gamma_{N}}

\displaystyle\text{ for }v\in V

\displaystyle(u_{0}(\cdot,0),v)

\displaystyle=(u_{\operatorfont init}-\widetilde{g}(\cdot,0),v)

\displaystyle\text{ for }v\in V

where
\widetilde{g}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega\times[0,T])
with
\widetilde{g}=g
on
\Gamma_{D}
. The solution
u
to equation (
6
) is then
u=u_{0}+\widetilde{g}
.

Denote the energy norms on
V

\|v\|_{a(\omega)}=\left(\int_{\omega}\bm{A}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Gamma_{N}\cap\partial\omega}(b-\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu})v^{2}\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma\right)^{1/2}\text{ and }\|v\|_{a}=\|v\|_{a(\Omega)}.

Also, notice that under the assumptions on
\bm{\beta}
and

v\in V
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(v,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{A}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Omega}(\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla v)v+\int_{\Gamma_{N}}(b-\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu})v^{2}\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{A}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\left(b-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu}\right)v^{2}\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma\geq 0.

So, the following quasi-norms are also well defined on

\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}(\omega)}=\int_{\omega}\bm{A}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\partial\omega\cap\Gamma_{N}}(b-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu})v^{2}\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma,

for

and
\|v\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\|v\|_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega)}

It is also easy to show that

3.2
Analysis

We will use the following notation:
for

Before we give an account of the analysis, we will summarize all the quasi-norms in the following lemma,

Lemma 3.1
.

For any

\|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq Hc_{\#}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}},

\mathcal{A}(w,v)\leq\overline{C}\|w\|_{\mathcal{A}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}},

where

,

Proof.

First,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq H\beta_{0}^{-1}\kappa_{1}^{-1/2}\|v\|_{s}\leq H\beta_{0}^{-1}\kappa_{1}^{-1/2}(1+\Lambda^{-1})^{1/2}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(w,v)

\displaystyle=\int\bm{A}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v+\int\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla wv+\int_{\partial\Omega}(b-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu})wv\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{a}\|v\|_{a}+H\sqrt{\int\bm{A}\nabla w\cdot\nabla w}\sqrt{\int\widetilde{\kappa}v^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{a}\|v\|_{a}+H\|w\|_{a}(\|\pi v\|_{s}+\|v-\pi v\|_{s})

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{a}\|v\|_{a}+H\|w\|_{a}(\|\pi v\|_{s}+\Lambda^{1/2}\|v\|_{a})

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}\|w\|_{\mathcal{A}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(w,v)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(w,v)+s(\pi w,\pi v)

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}\|w\|_{\mathcal{A}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}}+\|\pi w\|_{s}\|\pi v\|_{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\overline{C}\|w\|_{\mathcal{B}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

∎

\mathcal{P}_{i}:V^{\prime}\rightarrow V
such that for all
v\in V
,

\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}},v)=\left<t_{i},v\right>

(12)

and
\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}:V^{\prime}\rightarrow V^{m}_{i}
with

\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}},v)=\left<t_{i},v\right>.

(13)

We aim to estimate

\left\|\sum^{N}_{i=1}{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}-{\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}=\left\|\sum^{N}_{i=1}{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}-{\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}}\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}+\left\|\sum^{N}_{i=1}{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}-{\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}}\right\|_{s}^{2}.

To solve this problem, we prepare ourselves with the following lemmas
3.3
,
3.4
and
3.5
.
To prove them, we need to define cutoff functions
\{\chi_{i}^{n,m}\}
.
Let
V^{H}
be the Lagrange basis function space of
\mathcal{T}^{H}
. For
K_{i}\in\mathcal{T}^{H}
,
a cutoff function
\chi^{n,m}_{i}\in V^{H}
with
n<m
satisfies that:

\chi^{n,m}_{i}(x)=1\text{ in }K^{n}_{i};

\chi^{n,m}_{i}(x)=0\text{ in }\Omega\backslash K^{m}_{i};

0\leq\chi^{n,m}_{i}\leq 1\text{ in }K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{n}_{i}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
m\geq 1
. Then there exists
0<\theta<1
such that

\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\Omega\backslash K^{m}_{i})}^{2}\leq\theta^{m}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}

where

\theta=\frac{c_{*}}{c_{*}+1}

and

c_{*}(\Lambda,\beta_{0})=\max_{x\in[0,\pi/2]}((\beta_{0}^{-1}+\kappa_{0}^{-1/2}\kappa_{1}^{-1/2}H)\cos(x)+\sin(x))\left(\Lambda^{-1/2}\cos(x)+\sin(x)\right).

Proof.

Note that
1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\equiv 0
in
K^{m-1}_{i}

and
1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\equiv 1
in
\Omega\backslash K_{i}
,
implying that
supp(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i})\subset\Omega\backslash K_{i}^{m-1}
.
Put
v\coloneqq\left(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\right){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}
in equation (
12
), namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}({\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}},(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}})+s(\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}},\pi(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}})

\displaystyle=0.

Then,

\displaystyle\quad\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega\backslash K^{m}_{i})}^{2}+\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(\Omega\backslash K^{m}_{i})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}\left(\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}-1\right)\bm{A}\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\cdot\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}x+\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\bm{A}\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\cdot\nabla\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}H^{-2}\kappa_{1}|\bm{\beta}|^{2}\pi({\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}})\cdot\pi\left(\left(\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}-1\right){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right)\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{N}\backslash\partial K^{m}_{i}}(-b+\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu}){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}(\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}-1){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}.

Since
\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}-1\leq 0
in
K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}
, then
I_{1}\leq 0
.
Also, since
\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu}<b
on
\Gamma_{N}
, we have
I_{4}\leq 0
.

For
I_{2}
, by using the Cauchy Schwartz inequality,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\bm{A}\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\cdot\nabla\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}\bm{A}\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\cdot\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}}\,\sqrt{\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}\left({\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right)^{2}\bm{A}\nabla\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\cdot\nabla\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\,\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}x}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{0}^{-1}\left\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right\|_{a(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}.

The last inequality is due to the assumptions on
\bm{A}
and
\bm{\beta}
,

\displaystyle\beta_{0}^{2}\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}^{2}\bm{A}\nabla\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\cdot\nabla\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\leq\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}^{2}|\bm{\beta}|^{2}CH^{-2}\kappa_{1}=\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}^{2}.

For
I_{3}
,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}H^{-2}\kappa_{1}|\bm{\beta}|^{2}\pi({\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}})\cdot\pi\left(\left(\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}-1\right){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right)\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\|\pi((\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}-1){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}})\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\|(\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}-1){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}.

For
I_{5}
,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}(\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}-1){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{0}^{-1/2}\kappa_{1}^{-1/2}H\sqrt{\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}\bm{A}\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\cdot\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}}\sqrt{\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}|\bm{\beta}|^{2}H^{-2}\kappa_{1}|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{0}^{-1/2}\kappa_{1}^{-1/2}H\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{a(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}.

By inequality (
2
),

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}-\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda^{-1/2}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{a(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}.

So, in summary,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\Omega\backslash K^{m}_{i})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left((\beta_{0}^{-1}+\kappa_{0}^{-1/2}\kappa_{1}^{-1/2}H)\left\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right\|_{a(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\right)\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{*}(\Lambda,\beta_{0})\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}{(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}}.

The last inequality comes from considering

\cos(\theta)=\frac{\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{A}(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}}{\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}}\text{ and }\sin(\theta)=\frac{\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}}{\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}}

for some
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
. Now,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}{(K^{m-1}_{i})}}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}(\Omega\backslash K^{m}_{i})}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1+\frac{1}{c_{*}}\right)\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}^{2}.

Then, iteratively we can obtain

\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\Omega\backslash K^{m}_{i})}^{2}\leq\theta^{m}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

With the notations in lemma
3.3
,
then

\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}-{\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\leq\overline{C}^{2}c_{\star}\theta^{m-1}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2},

where

\displaystyle c_{\star}(\Lambda,\beta_{0})\coloneqq\max_{x\in[0,\pi/2]}\left[(1+\beta^{-1}_{0}\Lambda^{-1/2})\cos(x)+\beta^{-1}_{0}\sin(x)\right]^{2}+\left[\Lambda^{-1/2}\cos(x)+\sin(x)\right]^{2}.

Proof.

Let
z_{i}\coloneqq{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}-{\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}}
and decompose it as

z_{i}=\left\{\left(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\right){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right\}+\left\{\left(\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}-1\right){\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}}+\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}z_{i}\right\}\eqqcolon z^{\prime}_{i}+z^{\prime\prime}_{i}.

By definition,
z^{\prime\prime}_{i}\in V^{m}_{i}
, so by equations (
12
) and (
13
),

\mathcal{B}(z_{i},z_{i}^{\prime\prime})=0
.
Then,

\displaystyle\|z_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}=\mathcal{B}(z_{i},z_{i}^{\prime})\leq\overline{C}\|z_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}}\|z_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\overline{C}^{2}\|z_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}.

To compute
\|z^{\prime}_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}}
, we investigate
\|z^{\prime}_{i}\|_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\|z_{i}^{\prime}\|_{s}
.

\displaystyle\|z_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\left(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\right){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}\lesssim\|\left(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\right){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i})^{2}\bm{A}\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\cdot\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}-2\int_{\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i})\bm{A}\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\cdot\nabla\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i}}{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}^{2}\bm{A}\nabla\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\cdot\nabla\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}+\int_{\Gamma_{N}\cap\partial K^{m}_{i}}(b-\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\nu}){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}^{2}\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{-2}_{0}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}^{2}+2\beta^{-1}_{0}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\beta^{-1}_{0}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{a(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\beta^{-1}_{0}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\right)^{2}.

Again, by inequality (
2
),

\displaystyle\|z_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{-1}_{0}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(K^{m}_{i}\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}

\displaystyle=\left(\beta^{-1}_{0}\Lambda^{-1/2}+1\right)\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\beta^{-1}_{0}\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}.

Also,

\displaystyle\|\pi z_{i}^{\prime}\|_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\pi\left(\left(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\right){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right)\right\|_{s}\leq\left\|\left(1-\chi^{m-1,m}_{i}\right){\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right\|_{s(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\leq\Lambda^{-1/2}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}.

Therefore, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|z_{i}\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}+\|\pi z_{i}\|_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}^{2}\|z_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}^{2}\left\{\left[\left(\beta^{-1}_{0}\Lambda^{-1/2}+1\right)\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\beta^{-1}_{0}\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}\right]^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\left.(\Lambda^{-1/2}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}+\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})})^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}^{2}c_{\star}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\Omega\backslash K^{m-1}_{i})}^{2}.

Thus by lemma
3.3
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}-{\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\leq\overline{C}^{2}c_{\star}\theta^{m-1}\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}.

∎

Assumption 1
.

There exists a constant
C_{\operatorfont ol}>0
such that for all
K_{i}\in\mathcal{T}^{H}
and
m>0
,

\#\{K\in\mathcal{T}^{H}\colon K\subset K^{m}_{i}\}\leq C_{\operatorfont ol}m^{d}.

Lemma 3.5
.

With the notations in lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\sum^{N}_{i=1}{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}-{\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\leq\overline{C}^{4}C_{\operatorfont ol}c_{\star}^{3}(m+1)^{d}\theta^{m-1}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\left<t_{i},{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right>.

Proof.

Let
z_{i}\coloneqq{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}-{\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}}
and
z\coloneqq\sum^{N}_{i=1}z_{i}
.
Decompose
z
as

z=\left\{\left(1-\chi^{m,m+1}_{i}\right)z\right\}+\left\{\chi^{m,m+1}_{i}z\right\}=:z^{\prime}+z^{\prime\prime}.

Notice that
supp(z^{\prime})\subset\Omega\backslash K^{m}_{i}
and thus
supp(\pi z^{\prime})\subset\Omega\backslash K^{m}_{i}
.
Also,
supp({\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}})\subset cl(K^{m}_{i})
and thus
supp(\pi{\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}})\subset cl(K^{m}_{i})
.
Then, by equations (
12
) and (
13
),

\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{P}^{m}_{i}t_{i}},z^{\prime})=0\text{ and }\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}},z^{\prime})=0,

granting us

\mathcal{B}(z_{i},z^{\prime})=0.

Now, similar to the proof of lemma
3.4
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(z_{i},z)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(z_{i},z^{\prime\prime})\leq\overline{C}\|z_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}}\|z_{i}^{\prime\prime}\|_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}\left\{\left((1+\beta_{0}^{-1}\Lambda^{-1/2})\|z\|_{\mathcal{A}(K^{m+1}_{i})}+\beta_{0}^{-1}\|\pi z\|_{s(K^{m+1}_{i})}\right)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\left(\Lambda^{-1/2}\|z\|_{\mathcal{A}(K^{m+1}_{i})}+\|\pi z\|_{s(K^{m+1}_{i})}\right)^{2}\right\}^{1/2}\|z_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}c_{\star}\|z\|_{\mathcal{B}{(K^{m+1}_{i})}}\|z_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

Also, by the definition of
C_{\operatorfont ol}
,

\sum^{N}_{i=1}\|z\|_{\mathcal{A}(K^{m+1}_{i})}^{2}+\|\pi z\|_{s(K^{m+1}_{i})}^{2}\leq C_{\operatorfont ol}(m+1)^{d}\|z\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2},

and recall by equation (

),

\|{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}+\|\pi{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\|_{s}^{2}=\left<t_{i},{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right>.

Hence, by the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality and lemma
3.4
,

\displaystyle\|z\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|z\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}+\|\pi z\|_{s}^{2}=\sum^{N}_{i=1}\mathcal{B}(z_{i},z)\leq\sum^{N}_{i=1}\overline{C}c_{\star}\|z\|_{\mathcal{B}(K^{m+1}_{i})}\|z_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}c_{\star}\left[\sum^{N}_{i=1}\|z\|_{\mathcal{B}(K^{m+1}_{i})}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\left[\sum^{N}_{i=1}\|z_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}c_{\star}\left[C_{\operatorfont ol}(m+1)^{d}\|z\|_{\mathcal{B}(K^{m+1}_{i})}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\left[\sum^{N}_{i=1}\|z_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}c_{\star}\left[C_{\operatorfont ol}(m+1)^{d}\|z\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\left[\overline{C}^{2}c_{\star}\theta^{m-1}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\left<t_{i},{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right>\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}^{4}C_{\operatorfont ol}c_{\star}^{3}(m+1)^{d}\theta^{m-1}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\left<t_{i},{\mathcal{P}_{i}t_{i}}\right>.

∎

-\mathcal{N}^{m}q
.

Corollary 3.5.1
.

With the notations in lemmas
3.3
,
3.4
and
3.5
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}^{\operatorfont glo}\widetilde{g}-\mathcal{D}^{m}\widetilde{g}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\leq\overline{C}^{5}C_{\operatorfont ol}c_{\star}^{3}(m+1)^{d}\theta^{m-1}\|\widetilde{g}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}};

(14)

\|\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}q-\mathcal{N}^{m}q\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\leq\overline{C}^{4}C_{\operatorfont ol}c_{\star}^{3}(m+1)^{d}\theta^{m-1}C^{2}_{\operatorfont tr}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}^{2},

where

Proof.

With lemma
3.5
, it suffices to estimate

\sum^{N}_{i=1}\mathcal{A}_{(K_{i})}(\widetilde{g},\mathcal{D}^{\operatorfont glo}_{i}\widetilde{g})\text{ and }\sum^{N}_{i=1}\int_{\Gamma_{N}\cap\partial K_{i}}q\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}_{i}q\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma.

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}^{\operatorfont glo}\widetilde{g}-\mathcal{D}^{m}\widetilde{g}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}^{5}C_{\operatorfont ol}c_{\star}^{3}(m+1)^{d}\theta^{m-1}\|\widetilde{g}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}}.

Similarly we start from the equation (

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}_{i}q\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}_{i}q\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}+\|\pi\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}_{i}q\|_{s}^{2}=\int_{\partial K_{i}\cap\Gamma_{N}}q\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}_{i}q\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma

.

\displaystyle\quad\sum^{N}_{i=1}\int_{\partial K_{i}\cap\Gamma_{N}}q\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}_{i}q\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma\leq\sum^{N}_{i=1}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\partial K_{i}\cap\Gamma_{N})}\left\|\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}_{i}q\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}

To further analyse function spaces

.

),

, lemma

,

\displaystyle+\overline{C}^{2}\sqrt{C_{\operatorfont ol}}c_{\star}^{3/2}(m+1)^{d/2}\theta^{(m-1)/2}\left\{\overline{C}^{2}\max(C^{2}_{\operatorfont inv},1)\|w^{\operatorfont glo}\|_{\mathcal{B}}\right.\left.+\overline{C}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{\mathcal{A}}+C_{\operatorfont tr}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}\right\}.

,

Proof.

By definition of

,
\mathcal{R}^{\operatorfont glo}
is surjective.
Then there exists

.

, we use the similar argument in corollary

.

\displaystyle\quad\|\mathcal{R}^{\operatorfont glo}_{i}\varphi_{*}-\mathcal{R}^{m}_{i}\varphi_{*}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\leq\overline{C}^{4}C_{\operatorfont ol}c_{\star}^{3}(m+1)^{d}\theta^{m-1}\sum^{N}_{i=1}s(\pi_{i}\varphi_{*},\pi\mathcal{R}^{\operatorfont glo}_{i}\varphi_{*})

3.6
,
there exists

Now, let

and
u^{\operatorfont glo}_{0}\coloneqq w^{\operatorfont glo}-\mathcal{D}^{\operatorfont glo}\widetilde{g}+\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}q
. Then,

\displaystyle\|u-u^{\operatorfont ms}\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u_{0}-u^{\operatorfont ms}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}=\mathcal{A}(u_{0}-u^{\operatorfont ms}_{0},u_{0}-v)

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}\|u_{0}-u^{\operatorfont ms}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{A}}\|u_{0}-v\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

Putting

\displaystyle\|u_{0}-v\|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{B}}+\|\widetilde{u}_{0}-v\|_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\|u-u^{\operatorfont ms}\|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\overline{C}\|u_{0}-v\|_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}\left\{\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{A}}+\|w^{\operatorfont glo}-w^{m}_{*}\|_{\mathcal{B}}+\|\mathcal{D}^{\operatorfont glo}\widetilde{g}-\mathcal{D}^{m}\widetilde{g}\|_{\mathcal{B}}+\|\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}q-\mathcal{N}^{m}q\|_{\mathcal{B}}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\overline{C}^{2}\sqrt{C_{\operatorfont ol}}c_{\star}^{3/2}(m+1)^{d/2}\theta^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\left\{\max(C_{\operatorfont inv},1)\|w^{\operatorfont glo}\|_{\mathcal{B}}\right.\left.+\overline{C}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{\mathcal{A}}+C_{\operatorfont tr}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}\right\}.

Now, first recall that we have
\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{A}}=O(1)
. Note that
\overline{C}=O(1)
.
By the equations (
14
) and (
15
), we have
\|\mathcal{D}^{\operatorfont glo}\widetilde{g}-\mathcal{D}^{m}\widetilde{g}\|_{\mathcal{A}}=O(1)
and
\|\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}q-\mathcal{N}^{m}q\|_{\mathcal{A}}

=O(1)
.
Assume
(m+1)^{d/2}\theta^{\frac{m-1}{2}}C_{\operatorfont inv}=O(H^{2})
. It suffices to estimate
\|w^{\operatorfont glo}\|_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\|\pi w^{\operatorfont glo}\|_{s}
.
By equation (
10
),

\|w^{\operatorfont glo}\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}=\mathcal{A}(u_{0},w^{\operatorfont glo})+\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D}^{\operatorfont glo}\widetilde{g},w^{\operatorfont glo})-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{N}^{\operatorfont glo}q,w^{\operatorfont glo})

4
Time Dependent Convection-Diffusion Initial Boundary Value Problems

4.1
Derivation of the Method

We present the convection diffusion initial boundary value problem as follows:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\nabla\cdot(\bm{A}(x)\nabla u(x,t))+\bm{\beta}(x)\cdot\nabla u(x,t)=f(x,t)&\text{ on }\Omega\times(0,T]\\
u(x,t)=g(x,t)&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}\times(0,T]\\
b(x)u(x,t)+\bm{\nu}\cdot(\bm{A}(x)\nabla u(x.t)-\bm{\beta}(x)u(x,t))=q(x,t)&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N}\times(0,T]\\
u(x,0)=u_{\operatorfont init}(x)&\text{ on }\Omega.\end{cases}

(20)

Notice
f
is still independent of the solution
u
.
The variational formulation becomes:
find
u_{0}(\cdot,t)\in V
such that for
v\in V
,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}u_{0},v)+\mathcal{A}(u_{0},v)

\displaystyle=(f,v)+(q,v)_{\Gamma_{N}}-\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{g},v)-(\widetilde{g}_{t},v)

(21)

\displaystyle(u_{0}(\cdot,0),v)

\displaystyle=(u_{\operatorfont init}(x)-\widetilde{g}(\cdot,0),v)

(22)

and the actual solution would be
u(\cdot,t)=u_{0}(\cdot,t)+\widetilde{g}(\cdot,t)
.
We will use the same auxiliary spaces
V^{\operatorfont aux}
and multiscale space
V^{m}_{\operatorfont ms}
as in the previous section, and thereby the same set of correctors
\mathcal{D}^{m}\widetilde{g}
and
\mathcal{N}^{m}q
.
In particular, for
t\in(0,T]
,

1.

Find
\mathcal{D}^{m}_{i}\widetilde{g}(\cdot,t)\in V^{m}_{i}
such that
v\in V^{m}_{i}
,

\begin{cases}(\mathcal{D}^{m}_{i}\widetilde{g}_{t},v)+\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}^{m}_{i}\widetilde{g},v)=(\widetilde{g}_{t},v)_{(K_{i})}+\mathcal{A}_{(K_{i})}(\widetilde{g},v)\\
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}^{m}_{i}\widetilde{g}(\cdot,0),v)=\mathcal{A}_{(K_{i})}(\widetilde{g}(\cdot,0),v).\end{cases}

(23)

Denote
\mathcal{D}^{m}\widetilde{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{D}^{m}_{i}\widetilde{g}
.

2.

Find
\mathcal{N}^{m}_{i}q(\cdot,t)\in V^{m}_{i}
such that
v\in V^{m}_{i}
,

\begin{cases}(\mathcal{N}^{m}_{i}q_{t},v)+\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N}^{m}_{i}q,v)=\int_{\Gamma_{N}\cap\partial K_{i}}qv\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma\\
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N}^{m}_{i}q(\cdot,0),v)=\int_{\Gamma_{N}\cap\partial K_{i}}q(\cdot,0)v\mathop{}\!{\operatorfont d}\sigma.\end{cases}

(24)

Denote
\mathcal{N}^{m}q=\sum^{N}_{i=1}\mathcal{N}^{m}_{i}q.

3.

find

Lemma 4.1

Let

Proof.

\pi(\widetilde{u}-u)=0

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}-{u}\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(f,\widetilde{u}-u)+((u_{0})_{t},\widetilde{u}-u)

Lemma 4.2
.

With the notations in lemma

Proof.

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(z,v)=(u-\widetilde{u},v)

\displaystyle\text{ for }v\in V

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{z},v)=(u-\widetilde{u},v)

needs to be introduced.
On the other hand, with the current scheme on convection diffusion equations, the convection and diffusion operators can be considered at once. In other words, our ultimate goal is to construct the solution at the next step via:

u^{n+1}=(\mathcal{S}^{\tau/2}_{f-\eta_{n}}\circ\mathcal{S}^{\tau}_{CD+\eta_{n}}\circ\mathcal{S}^{\tau/2}_{f-\eta_{n}})(u^{n})

where
\mathcal{S}^{\tau/2}_{f-\eta_{n}}
maps to the solution considering the adjusted nonlinearity term
f-\eta_{n}
for a stepsize
\tau/2
and
\mathcal{S}^{\tau}_{CD+\eta_{n}}
maps to the solution considering the convection diffusion equation with the source term
\eta_{n}
.
Also, this corrector term
\eta_{n}
is to be carefully selected and get cancelled out in the process; otherwise can be accumulated over time and become a stiff term. The problem becomes even more apparent with time-variant boundary conditions. In light of this, we will decompose the nonlinearity into two parts, one dependent on the boundary and one independent. We will illustrate this idea using time-invariant Dirichlet boundary conditions. The time-variant version extends naturally by following a similar fashion.
The proof of this well-known method can be found in
[
33
,
34
,
36
]
. To summarise: at each step
n
,

1.

Define
z\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that
\left.z\right|_{\partial\Omega}=g(\cdot,t_{n})
.

2.

Find
w(\frac{\tau}{2})
by

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w=f(w+z)-f(z)\\
w(0)=u^{n}-z.\end{cases}

3.

Find
v(\tau)
by

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+\mathcal{A}v=f(z)-\mathcal{A}z-\partial_{t}z\\
v(0)=w(\frac{\tau}{2}).\end{cases}

4.

Find
w(\frac{\tau}{2})
by

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w=f(w+z)-f(z)\\
w(0)=v(\tau).\end{cases}

5.

Define
z\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that
\left.z\right|_{\partial\Omega}=g(\cdot,t_{n+1})
.

6.

Define
u^{n+1}=w(\frac{\tau}{2})+z
.

Above, the introduction of the corrector
f(z)
is independent of the solution in Step 3. Therefore,
f(z)-\mathcal{A}z-\partial_{t}z
can be treated as a source term of a convection diffusion equation, which has been discussed already.
We note that for the first step, one has the flexibility to determine the interior value of
z
.
Aligned with our previous direction, we will use our choice of
\mathcal{D}^{m}\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{g}
and
\mathcal{N}^{m}q
for
z
, catering to the corresponding boundary conditions.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we propose an application of CEM-GMsFEM to solve convection diffusion equations under various types of inhomogeneous boundary conditions along with high-contrast coefficients. The method begins with constructing an auxiliary space and builds a multiscale space upon it. Boundary correctors are built upon this multiscale space by solving local oversampled cell problems. For both time independent and dependent problems, we provide convergence anaylsis and show second order convergence in
L^{2}
and first order convergence in energy norm with respect to the coarse mesh, given sufficient oversampling, as agreed with numerical results. We also compare different time discretization strategies using the Backward Euler scheme. For nonlinear problems, we apply this modified method with Strang Splitting and demonstrate using a Dirichlet initial boundary value problem.

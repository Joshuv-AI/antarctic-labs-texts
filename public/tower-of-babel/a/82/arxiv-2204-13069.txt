On subspace designs
Paolo Santonastaso, Ferdinando Zullo

Abstract
Guruswami and Xing introduced subspace designs in 2013 to give the first construction of positive rate rank metric codes list decodable beyond half the distance. In this paper we provide bounds involving the parameters of a subspace design, showing they are tight via explicit constructions. We point out a connection with sum-rank metric codes, dealing with optimal codes and minimal codes with respect to this metric. Applications to two-intersection sets with respect to hyperplanes, two-weight codes, cutting blocking sets and lossless dimension expanders are also provided.



Keywords
: Subspace design; subspace evasive subspace;
s
-design; MSRD code; scattered subspace; sum-rank metric code; cutting design; dimension expander

1
Introduction

Guruswami and Xing in
[
48
]
introduced a linear-algebraic list decoder which starts by setting the coefficients of the message into a
periodic subspace
, with some more restrictions on such coefficients are needed in order to get a
small
list of solutions.
This last restriction was rephrased in finding a sufficiently large set of subspaces with
small
intersections with all subspaces of fixed dimension. This led to the definition of a (strong)
subspace design
.

Definition 1.1
.

(see
[
48
, Definition 7]
)
An ordered set
(V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{t})
, where
V_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V=V(k,q)
, for any
i\in[t]
, is called a
strong

(s,A)
-
subspace design
in
V
if for every
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
W\subseteq V
of dimension
s
,

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{i}\cap W)\leq A.

Using the probabilistic method one can show the existence of strong subspace designs with large size and dimension, see e.g.
[
48
]
. These subspace designs were used to give a randomized construction of optimal rate list-decodable codes over constant-sized
large alphabets and sub-logarithmic (and even smaller) list size. Moreover, in
[
44
]
, the authors were able to construct a large
explicit
strong subspace design, where a strong
(s,A)
-subspace design
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{t})
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k}
is said to be explicit if there exists an algorithm which gives a basis for
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{t})
in time poly
(q,k,t)
.

Using such a subspace design, they gave an explicit construction of a subcode of a Gabidulin code which has small intersection with the output of the linear-algebraic list decoder they introduced.
Hence, the code obtained in this way turns out to be efficiently list-decodable.
This was the first explicit example of an efficiently list-decodable rank metric code.

These techniques have been further investigated to obtain more rank metric codes and subspace codes which are efficiently list-decodable (see e.g.
[
47
,
46
]
), to obtain efficiently list-decodable algebraic-geometric codes
[
49
]
and to construct explicit constant degree
dimension expanders
over large fields
[
45
]
.

In all of these constructions, the obtained subspace designs are large and with a small intersection with the family of subspaces considered.
However, explicitly constructing subspace designs satisfying these properties seems challenging.
In
[
44
]
explicit constructions of strong subspace designs with parameters close to the probabilistic construction of
[
48
]
were found, by requiring large field size, whereas in
[
50
]
, using algebraic function fields, the authors constructed strong subspace designs over any field, up to some restrictions on the total intersection dimension.

More recently, in
[
57
]
the authors introduced the notion of
almost affinely disjoint subspaces
, which is strongly related to the subspace designs we are going to introduce, and they have been also used to construct
primitive batch codes
, see
[
54
]
.

Our aim is to investigate subspace designs.

Definition 1.2
.

An ordered set
\mathcal{U}=(U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{t})
, where
U_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V=V(k,q^{m})
, for any
i\in[t]
, is called an
(s,A)_{q}
-
subspace design
in
V
if

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq s
and for every
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
W\subseteq V
of dimension
s
,

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap W)\leq A.

Moreover, if

\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=V

we say that
\mathcal{U}
is a
non-degenerate

(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design.

In the above mentioned applications, the authors did not use the strong subspace design they constructed directly, but they used the intersection of such strong subspace designs with a fixed
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace, obtaining in this way a subspace design as in Definition
1.2
.
Moreover, when
t=1
,
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace designs coincide with the notion of
(s,A)_{q}
-
evasive subspaces
, originally introduced in
[
82
]
by Pudlák and Rödl.

It is easy to see that for a given subspace design the following property holds (see Proposition
3.1
).
For an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design
(U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{t})
in
V=V(k,q^{m})
,
s\leq A
.

This paper focuses on the following two problems.

Problem 1.3
.

Investigate subspace designs attaining equality in Proposition
3.1
.

Problem 1.4
.

Given an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design
\mathcal{U}
, what is the smallest
A^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{U}
is an
(s,A^{\prime})_{q}
-subspace design?

In order to study Problem
1.3
, we give the following definition.

Definition 1.5
.

An ordered set
(U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{t})
, where
U_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V=V(k,q^{m})
, for any
i\in[t]
, is an
s
-
design
in
V
if it is an
(s,s)_{q}
-subspace design in
V
.

We start by providing some properties of subspace designs and showing some procedures to construct subspace designs from other subspace designs, such as by using direct sum of subspaces.
As a consequence of the bounds proved in
[
13
,
19
,
31
]
, we obtain a first bound regarding the dimension of the subspaces of a subspace design.

The
s
-designs are important examples of subspace designs.
Indeed, when the dimension of all the subspaces is at most
m
, then any
(k-1)
-design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
(with
m\geq k
) is an
optimal subspace design
, that is connected to
optimal sum-rank metric codes
.
Then we prove that this is also true when considering maximum
1
-designs, i.e.
1
-designs for which the dimension of the subspaces is
km/2
(when
km
is even).
We investigate the problem of determining the intersection pattern of the pointsets defined by the subspaces of an
s
-design with hyperplanes. For
s=1
we show that if such pointsets do not cover the entire space, we obtain a two-intersection set with respect to the hyperplanes.
We conclude the section by showing constructions of maximum
1
-designs.
As we will see,
s
-designs give explicit construction of lossless dimension expanders, without any restriction on the size of the field.

Subsequently, we point out a connection between sum-rank metric codes and subspace designs, already developed in
[
77
]
.
The sum-rank metric has been recently investigated especially because of the performance of multishot network coding based on sum-rank metric codes, see
[
78
]
.
This metric extends the Hamming and rank metric and it is still gaining attention.
Indeed, important and fundamental results were achieved not long ago, see e.g.
[
25
,
64
,
63
,
66
,
76
]
.

In this paper, we characterize those subspaces defining optimal sum-rank metric codes (that is the ones satisfying equality in the Singleton bound, known as maximum sum-rank metric codes) in terms of subspace designs.
Since the sum-rank metric generalizes the Hamming and rank metric, this connection extends the well-known correspondence between linear maximum distance separable codes and arcs in projective spaces for the Hamming metric (see
[
8
]
for a survey on this topic) and linear maximum rank distance codes and scattered subspaces/linear sets for the rank metric (see
[
81
]
).
Those subspace designs will be called
optimal subspace designs
(see Section
5.3
).
By surveying the known examples of linear MSRD codes, we will show the associated subspace designs.
Another important way of constructing subspace designs from a fixed one is by using two types of dualities known as ordinary duality and Delsarte duality, the latter one is defined through the connection with sum-rank metric codes.
We will then explore how to obtain subspace designs from strong subspace designs via subspace evasive subspaces, intermediate fields, high-degree places and Cameron-Liebler sets.
Finally, we will study subspace designs with the property of capturing the structure of hyperplanes, that is their elements contain a basis of every hyperplane.
We call them
cutting design
, since they represent an extension of the notion of cutting blocking sets recently introduced by Bonini and Borello in
[
22
]
. We then show how to construct cutting designs from known (linear) cutting blocking sets.
Similarly to the case of cutting blocking sets, cutting designs are in correspondence with
minimal
sum-rank metric codes. Minimal codes attracted attention for their use in secret sharing schemes (see
[
69
]
) and very recently in
[
2
]
this notion was extended to the rank metric. In this paper we introduce minimal sum-rank metric codes, that naturally extend those in the Hamming and rank metric.

Organization of the paper

In Section

2.1
Linearized polynomials

Let
\Gal({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
be the Galois group of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
\sigma
be a generator of
\Gal({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
A
\sigma
-
polynomial
(or
\sigma
-
linearized polynomial
) is a polynomial of the form

F(x)=f_{0}x+f_{1}x^{\sigma}+f_{2}x^{\sigma^{2}}+\ldots+f_{d}x^{\sigma^{d}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x].

The
\sigma
-degree of a nonzero
\sigma
-polynomial is defined naturally as
\max\{i:f_{i}\neq 0\}
and it is denoted by
\deg_{\sigma}(F(x))
.
We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{m,\sigma}
the set of
\sigma
-linearized polynomials and we equip it
with the usual addition
+
beetween polynomials of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x]
and the composition
\circ
defined as

ax^{\sigma^{i}}\circ bx^{\sigma^{j}}=a\sigma^{i}(b)x^{\sigma^{i+j}},

and then extended to
\sigma
-polynomials by associativity and distributivity.
With these two operations and with the multiplication by elements in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
,
\mathcal{L}_{m,\sigma}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-algebra and an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space. For any element
F(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}x^{\sigma^{i}}\in\mathcal{L}_{m,\sigma}
, one can consider the map

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\phi_{F}:&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\\
&\beta&\longmapsto&F(\beta):=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}\sigma^{i}(\beta).\end{array}

Then the map
F(x)\mapsto\phi_{F}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-algebra epimorpishm between
\mathcal{L}_{m,\sigma}
and
\End_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
. So we can identify
F(x)
with the map
\phi_{F}
, and we will refer to
\mathrm{rk}_{q}(F(x))
and
\ker(F(x))
to indicate the rank over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of
\phi_{F}
and its kernel, respectively.

For
\sigma
-polynomials we have the following bound on the number of roots.

Theorem 2.1
.

(see
[
42
, Lemma 3.2]
and
[
41
, Theorem 5]
)
Consider a non-zero
\sigma
-polynomial

F(x)
.
Then

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(F(x)))\leq\deg_{\sigma}(F(x)).

\mathcal{L}_{m,\sigma}
is a right-Euclidean domain with respect to
\sigma
-degree. This implies that if
F_{1}(x),F_{2}(x)
are nonzero
\sigma
-polynomials, then the notions of greatest common right divisor, which we denote by
\mathrm{gcrd}(F_{1}(x),F_{2}(x))
, and least common left multiple, denoted by
\mathrm{lclm}(F_{1}(x),F_{2}(x))
are well-defined.

Let
F(x)=f_{0}x+f_{1}x^{\sigma}+\cdots+f_{d}x^{\sigma^{d}}\in\mathcal{L}_{m,\sigma}
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. Denote by
F_{\alpha}(x)
the
\sigma
-polynomial

F_{\alpha}(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}\mathrm{N}_{\sigma}^{i}(\alpha)x^{\sigma^{i}},

where
\mathrm{N}_{\sigma}^{i}(\alpha)=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\sigma^{j}(\alpha)
.

For a non-zero
\sigma
-polynomial
F(x)\in\mathcal{L}_{m,\sigma}
, and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
, the
\lambda
-value for
F(x)
is the integer

d_{\lambda}(F(x))=\deg(\mathrm{gcrd}(F(x),x^{\sigma^{n}}-\lambda x)),

see
[
71
, Proposition 3]
and
[
76
, Definition 2.8]
. The
\lambda
-value of a
\sigma
-polynomial
F(x)
gives information on the number of roots of

with
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\alpha)=\lambda
as stated in the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

(see
[
76
, Theorem 3.10, Proposition 6.1]
)
Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. Let
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\alpha_{i})=\lambda_{i}
. Suppose that
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
, if
i\neq j
. Let
F(x)=f_{0}x+f_{1}x^{\sigma}+\ldots+f_{d}x^{\sigma^{d}}\in\mathcal{L}_{m,\sigma}
be a non-zero
\sigma
-polynomial with
\deg_{\sigma}(F(x))=d
. We have the following:

1.

for any
i\in[t]

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(F_{\alpha_{i}}(x)))=d_{\lambda_{i}}(F(x));

2.

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(F_{\alpha_{i}}(x)))\leq\deg_{\sigma}(F(x));

3.

if
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(F_{\alpha_{i}}(x)))=d,
then

2.2
Subspace evasive subspaces and scattered subspaces

Let
V
be any non-empty set.
In
[

]
, Pudlák and Rödl introduced the notion of being evasive for a subset of

the family of all subspaces of

In this paper we will mainly use the following notion of subspace evasive subspace, which corresponds to
[

Definition 2.3
.

An
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace

(s,r)_{q}

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}})\geq s

Subspace evasive subspaces have been recently investigated in

\left(k-1,\frac{km}{s+1}-m+s\right)_{q}
-evasive subspace.

2.3
Linear sets

Let
V
be a
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space and let
\Lambda=\mathrm{PG}(V,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})=\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.
Let
U\neq\{0\}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V
of dimension
n
, then the set of points

L_{U}=\{\langle{u}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}:{u}\in U\setminus\{{0}\}\}\subseteq\Lambda

is said to be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-
linear set of rank
n
.

The
weight
of a subspace
\mathcal{S}=\mathrm{PG}(W,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})\subseteq\Lambda
in
L_{U}
is defined as

w_{L_{U}}(\mathcal{S})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap W).

Denote by
N_{i}
the number of points of weight
i
in
L_{U}
.

The following relations hold:

|L_{U}|\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1},

(1)

N_{1}+N_{2}(q+1)+\ldots+N_{n}(q^{n-1}+\ldots+q+1)=q^{n-1}+\ldots+q+1.

(2)

When
|L_{U}|
satisfies equality in (
1
)
L_{U}
is called
scattered
, or equivalently, if all the points of
L_{U}
have weight one. It is easy to see that
L_{U}
is scattered if and only if
U
is
(1,1)_{q}
-evasive subspace, that is a
1
-scattered subspace without the assumption that it spans the entire space.

Remark 2.8
.

Let
L_{U_{1}},\ldots,L_{U_{t}}
be
t

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear sets in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
of rank
m
and with
t<q
.
Then there exists a point
P\in\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})\setminus(L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}})
.
This immediately follows from (
1
), indeed

|L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}}|\leq t\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}\leq q^{m}-1,

and
q^{m}-1
is less than the number of the points in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.
As a consequence, if
U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}
are
t

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
V=V(k,q^{m})
of dimension
m
and with
t<q
, then there exists
v\in V\setminus\{0\}
such that
U_{i}\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=\{0\}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.

We refer to
[
56
]
and
[
80
]
for comprehensive references on linear sets and their applications.

3
First properties of subspace designs

In this section we will provide some general properties and examples of subspace designs.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
(U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design in
V=V(k,q^{m})

then
s\leq A
.

Proof.

Let
W=\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
. Since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(W)\geq s
, there exist

\{u_{i,j}\colon i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}\mbox{ and }j\in\{1,\ldots,j_{i}\}\}\subseteq W

such that
u_{i,j}\in U_{i}
, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
,
u_{i,j}
’s are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linearly independent and
j_{1}+\ldots+j_{t}\geq s
. This implies that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap W)\geq j_{i}
for any
i
and so
A\geq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap W)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{t}j_{i}=s
.
∎

An
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design of
V=V(k,q^{m})
is also an
(i,A^{\prime})_{q}
-subspace design for any
i\leq s
and some integer
A^{\prime}\leq A
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
,
then it is also an
(s-s^{\prime},A-s^{\prime})_{q}
-subspace design in
V
, for any
s^{\prime}\in\{0,\ldots,s-1\}
.
In particular, for
s>1
, an
s
-design is also an
i
-design for any
i\leq s
.

Proof.

Let choose
s^{\prime}=1
.
By contradiction, suppose that there exists an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
H
having dimension
s-1
of
V
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\geq A
. Since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq s
,
U_{1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{t}
is not contained in
H
. Hence, there exists
u\in(U_{1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{t})\setminus H
so that

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap\langle{u},H\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{t}(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap\langle{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}))\geq A+1,

a contradiction.
The assertion for a general
s^{\prime}
follows by repeating the previous argument
s^{\prime}
times.
∎

As a direct consequence, the elements of an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design are related to
(s-s^{\prime},A^{\prime})_{q}
-evasive subspaces for any
s^{\prime}
as follows.

Corollary 3.3
.

If
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
,
then for every
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
H
having dimension
s-s^{\prime}
and for any
i\in[t]

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(H\cap U_{i})\leq A-s^{\prime}.

In particular, if
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq s-s^{\prime}
, then
U_{i}
is an
(s-s^{\prime},A-s^{\prime})_{q}
-evasive subspace.

In the next proposition, we show how to extend the subspaces of an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design and how this reflects on the parameter
A
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that there exists an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
where
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
in
V=V(k,q^{m})
. Let
j_{1},\ldots,j_{t}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0\leq j_{i}\leq mk-n_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{t}j_{i}=s^{\prime}
.
Then there exists an
(s,A+s^{\prime})_{q}
-subspace design
(U_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,U^{\prime}_{t})
in
V
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime}_{i})=n_{i}+j_{i}
, for every
i\in[t]
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose that
mk-n_{1}>0
. Then there exists
{w}\in V\setminus U_{1}
. Replacing
U_{1}
by
U^{\prime}_{1}=\langle U_{1},{w}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, we obtain that
(U^{\prime}_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(s,A+1)_{q}
-subspace design in
V
.
Indeed, suppose for the contrary that there exist
{u}_{i,1},\ldots{u}_{i,\ell_{i}}\in U_{i}
such that
\ell_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{t}=A+2
and the sets

D_{1}=\{{w}+{u}_{1,1},\ldots,{w}+{u}_{1,\ell_{1}}\}\subseteq U^{\prime}_{1}

and

D_{i}=\{{u}_{i,1},\ldots,{u}_{i,\ell_{i}}\}\subseteq U_{i},\,\,\,2\leq i\leq t

are sets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent elements contained in the same
s
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
H
of
V
, for every
i
. Note that
\ell_{1}>0
, otherwise

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)=\sum_{i=2}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\geq A+2>A,

that is a contradiction to
(U_{2},\ldots,U_{t})
being an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design.
Then
D^{\prime}_{1}=\{{u}_{1,1}-{u}_{1,\ell_{1}},\ldots,{u}_{1,\ell_{1}-1}-{u}_{1,\ell_{1}}\}\subseteq U_{1}
is a set of
\ell_{1}-1

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent elements of
U_{1}
in
H
and
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\geq A+1
, a contradiction. The result follows by repeating the previous argument
s^{\prime}
times.
∎

We can construct a subspace design by extending a subspace design lying in a hyperplane of the entire space.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be a
(k-1,A)_{q}
-subspace design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{i}
. Let
\ell
be a positive integer such that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}-A\leq\ell\leq m
, then there exists a
(k,A+\ell)_{q}
-subspace design in
V^{\prime}=V(k+1,q^{m})
with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{i}+e_{i}
for every
i
, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}e_{i}=\ell
.

Another way to construct subspace designs is via the direct sum of subspace designs.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
where
V_{i}=V(k_{i},q^{m})
and
V=V(k,q^{m})
. If
(U^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,U^{\prime}_{t})
is an
(s,A_{1})_{q}
-subspace design in
V_{1}
and
(U^{\prime\prime}_{1},\ldots,U^{\prime\prime}_{t})
is an
(s,A_{2})_{q}
-subspace design in
V_{2}
, then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
, where
U_{i}=U^{\prime}_{i}\oplus U^{\prime\prime}_{i}
for every
i\in[t]
, is an
(s,A_{1}+A_{2}-s)_{q}
-subspace design in
V
with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\prime})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\prime\prime})
for every
i
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{1},\ldots U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq s.

Now, suppose that there exists an
s
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
W
of
V
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(W\cap U_{i})\geq A_{1}+A_{2}-s+1.

(3)

Note that by Proposition
3.1
,
A_{1}+A_{2}-s+1>A_{1}
and
A_{1}+A_{2}-s+1>A_{2}
.
Clearly,
W
cannot be contained in
V_{1}
since
(U^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,U^{\prime}_{t})
is an
(s,A_{1})_{q}
-subspace design in
V_{1}
.
Let
W_{1}:=W\cap V_{1}
and
h:=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(W_{1})
. Then
h<s
and by Proposition
3.2
, the ordered set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces
(U_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,U^{\prime}_{t})
is a
(h,A_{1}-s+h)_{q}
-subspace design in
V_{1}
.
Let denote
\langle U^{\prime}_{i},W\cap U_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
by
\overline{U}_{i}
, then the Grassmann’s formula and (
3
) imply

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\overline{U}_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime}_{i})\geq A_{1}+A_{2}-s+1-(A_{1}-s+h)=A_{2}+1-h.

(4)

Consider the quotient space
V/V_{1}
(which is isomorphic to
V_{2}
) and consider the subspace
T:=W+V_{1}
of
V/V_{1}
. Then
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(T)=s-h
and
T
contains the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces

M_{i}:=\overline{U}_{i}+V_{1},

for every
i\in[t]
.
Since
M_{i}
is also contained in the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U_{i}+V_{1}=U^{\prime\prime}_{i}+V_{1}
for any
i
, the ordered set
(M_{1},\ldots,M_{t})
is an
(s,A_{2})_{q}
-subspace design in
V/V_{1}
and hence by Proposition
3.2

(M_{1},\ldots,M_{t})
is also an
(s-h,A_{2}-h)_{q}
-subspace design in
V/V_{1}
.

On the other hand,

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(M_{i}\cap T)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(M_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\overline{U}_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\overline{U}_{i}\cap V_{1})

\geq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\overline{U}_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap V_{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\overline{U}_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime}_{i}),

and hence, (
4
) implies

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(M_{i}\cap T)\geq A_{2}-h+1,

a contradiction to the fact that
(M_{1},\ldots,M_{t})
is also an
(s-h,A_{2}-h)_{q}
-subspace design in
V/V_{1}
.
∎

The above result can be generalized as follows.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\ell}
where
V_{i}=V(k_{i},q^{m})
and
V=V(k,q^{m})
. If
(U_{i,1},\ldots,U_{i,t})
is an
(s,A_{i})
-subspace design in
V_{i}
, for every
i\in[\ell]
, then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
, where
U_{i}=U_{1,i}\oplus\cdots\oplus U_{\ell,i}
, is an
(s,\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}A_{i}-(\ell-1)s)_{q}
-subspace design in
V
with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{j,i})
.

As a consequence of Theorem
2.5
, we obtain the following bounds on the dimensions of the subspaces of a subspace design.

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
Let
n_{i}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})
for every
i\in[t]
and suppose that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq s
for every
i
.

1.

If
s=k-1
and
A<(k-1)m
, then
n_{i}\leq m+A-1
for any
i
, and hence

n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}\leq t(m+A-1);

2.

If
s=k-1
and
A<k-2+m/(k-1)
, then
n_{i}\leq m+A-1-k
and hence

n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}\leq t(m+A-1-k);

3.

If
A<m
, then
n_{i}\leq mk-\frac{mks}{A+1}
and hence

n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}\leq t\left(mk-\frac{mks}{A+1}\right);

4.

More generally,
n_{i}\leq\frac{(q^{A}-1)(q^{km}-1)}{q^{sm}-1}+1
and hence

n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}\leq t\left(\frac{(q^{A}-1)(q^{km}-1)}{q^{sm}-1}+1\right).

However, some of the bounds provided in the above corollary still hold when considering a subspace design with no assumptions on the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-span of its elements.
The proof follows the same arguments of
[
31
, Theorem 2.3]
(see also
[
13
, Corollary 4.9]
) with the aid of Corollary
3.3
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
s
-design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
Then for every
i\in[t]

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})\leq\begin{cases}k,&\mbox{ if }m<s+1,\\
\frac{mk}{s+1},&\mbox{ if }m\geq s+1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
U
be in
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\}
and denote by
n
its dimension.
Since
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
s
-design, then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap W)\leq s,

for every
s
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
W
of
V
.
If
\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=V
then
U
is an
s
-scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace and the result follows from 1. and 2. of Theorem
2.5
. Suppose that
\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\neq V
, then we can consider
U
as an
s
-scattered subspace in
\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
and apply again Theorem
2.5
to get the assertion.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

The above theorem extends
[
31
, Theorem 2.3]
to any subspace which is part of an
s
-design. This condition is weaker than the one of being
s
-scattered since we do not require that the subspace spans over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
the entire space or an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of dimension

as done in
[

.

Subspaces attaining the bound of the previous theorem have the property that their span over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
coincides with the ambient space.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
s
be a positive integer with
s<k
. Let
U
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V=V(k,q^{m})
that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap W)\leq s,

for every
s
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
W
of
V
. Suppose that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=\begin{cases}k,&\mbox{ if }m<s+1,\\
\left\lfloor\frac{mk}{s+1}\right\rfloor,&\mbox{ if }m\geq s+1.\end{cases}

Then
\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=V
.

Proof.

Assume that
m<s+1
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=k
. Since
k>s
, first we note that
U
is not contained in any
s
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
. Now, suppose by contradiction that
\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=T
, where
T
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
of dimension
k^{\prime}<k
. Then by Theorem
2.5
, since
U
is an
(s,s)_{q}
-evasive subspace contained in
T
and
m<s+1
, we get

k=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)\leq k^{\prime},

a contradiction as
k^{\prime}<k
, so this implies that
\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=V
. Assume now that
m\geq s+1
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=\left\lfloor\frac{mk}{s+1}\right\rfloor
. Since
m\geq s+1
, we get
\left\lfloor\frac{mk}{s+1}\right\rfloor>s
and we get that
U
is not contained in any
s
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
.
Suppose by contradiction that
\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=T
, where
T
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
of dimension
k^{\prime}<k
. Then by Theorem
2.5
, since
U
is an
(s,s)_{q}
-evasive subspace contained in

and

, we get

\frac{mk-s}{s+1}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{mk}{s+1}\right\rfloor=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)\leq\frac{mk^{\prime}}{s+1}\Rightarrow s\geq m(k-k^{\prime})\geq m,

a contradiction to the fact that
s<m
.
∎

3.9
to the elements of an

.

Suppose that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
s
-design in

.
Let
n_{i}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})
for every

If

n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}\leq t\frac{mk}{s+1}.

(5)

Whereas, for the bound of Corollary

.

(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be an

4
Maximum
s
-designs

This section is mainly devoted to the study of Problem
1.3
under certain assumptions. Indeed,
we investigate maximum
s
-designs, i.e.
s
-designs attaining equality in Corollary
3.8
.
Their interest is also related to the fact they can be seen as an extension of
s
-scattered subspaces in the framework of subspace designs.

4.1
Constructions

We start by constructing examples of
(k-1)
-designs by partitioning an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-basis of the ambient space.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-basis of
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
Let consider
\mathcal{P}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}\}
a partition of the set
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\}
, with
t\geq 2
. Let
U_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V
generated by the elements of
X_{i}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, for any
i\in[t]
. Then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a
(k-1)
-design.

Proof.

Let
W\subseteq V
a
(k-1)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace and assume that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap W)\geq k
. Since

k\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap W)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=k,

it follows that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap W)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})
and then
U_{i}\subseteq W
for every
i
, that is
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in W
, a contradiction.
∎

A more involved construction is the following, which turns out to be also maximum.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
t<q
,
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h_{0}+h_{1}=\frac{q^{mk}-1}{q^{m}-1},\\
\gamma_{0}h_{0}+\gamma_{1}h_{1}=\left(t\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}-1}{q-1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m(k-1)}-1}{q^{m}-1}\right),\end{array}\right.

(13)

where
\gamma_{0}=t\frac{q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}}-1}{q-1}
and
\gamma_{1}=(t-1)\frac{q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}}-1}{q-1}+\frac{q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}+1}-1}{q-1}
.
Since
\gamma_{1}>\gamma_{0}
, the System (
13
) is determined and hence
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
are uniquely determined as in (
12
).
If
h_{1}
would be zero, then System (
13
) implies that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h_{0}=\frac{q^{mk}-1}{q^{m}-1},\\
\gamma_{0}h_{0}=\left(t\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}-1}{q-1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m(k-1)}-1}{q^{m}-1}\right),\end{array}\right.

that is

q^{m(k-2)/2}+q^{mk}=q^{mk/2}+q^{m(k-1)},

which cannot happen.
Now, suppose that
h_{0}=0
, then
(
12
) implies that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h_{1}=\frac{q^{mk}-1}{q^{m}-1},\\
\gamma_{1}h_{1}=\left(t\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}-1}{q-1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m(k-1)}-1}{q^{m}-1}\right),\end{array}\right.

that is

(t-1)(q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}}-1)(q^{mk}-1)+(q^{mk}-1)(q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}+1}-1)=t(q^{\frac{mk}{2}}-1)(q^{m(k-1)}-1),

which can be rewritten as follows

q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}}(q^{\frac{mk}{2}}-1)[(q-1)q^{\frac{mk}{2}}-tq^{m}+q+t-1]=0,

and it happens if and only if

t=(q-1)\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1}{q^{m}-1}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

As a corollary we obtain examples of two-intersection sets with respect to hyperplanes.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be a maximum
1
-design in

.
Consider the associated
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear sets

In particular,

•

•

•

t\leq(q-1)\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1}{q^{m}-1}
.

Proof.

Since
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a maximum
1
-design then the

’s are scattered and pairwise disjoint. This means that

\left|\mathcal{H}\cap(L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}})\right|=\left|\mathcal{H}\cap L_{U_{1}}\right|+\ldots+\left|\mathcal{H}\cap L_{U_{t}}\right|.

Theorem
4.13
implies that for any hyperplane

of

we have the following two possibilities:

•

•

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
m
and
k
be two positive integers such that
mk
is even and let

be a prime power. Then
(q-1)\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1}{q^{m}-1}
is an integer if and only if

is odd and

.

Proof.

Let

be the greatest common divisor of
q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1
and
\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}=q^{m-1}+\ldots+q+1
. Clearly,
(q-1)\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1}{q^{m}-1}
is an integer if and only if

. Since
\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}>2
, if
r\in\{1,2\}
, it results that
(q-1)\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1}{q^{m}-1}
is not an integer. So suppose that
r>2
and
r=r_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdot r_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots r_{g}^{e_{g}}
is the decomposition in prime factor of
r
with
r_{1},\ldots,r_{g}
distinct prime numbers and
e_{1},\ldots,e_{g}
positive integers. Let start by observing that, since
r
divides
q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1
, the
r_{i}
’s are different from
p
otherwise
r
divides 1. This means that
r
and
q^{j}
are coprime for every positive integer
j
. Since
r
divides
q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1
and
q^{m-1}+\ldots+q+1
, then
r
divides their difference, i.e.
q^{\frac{mk}{2}}-q^{m-1}-\ldots-q=q(q^{\frac{mk}{2}-1}-q^{m-2}-\ldots-1)
and so
r
divides
q^{\frac{mk}{2}-1}-q^{m-2}-\ldots-1
. Again since
r
divides
q^{m-1}+\ldots+q+1
, we get that
r
divides their sum, i.e.
q^{\frac{mk}{2}-1}-q^{m-1}=q^{m-1}\left(q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}}-1\right)
, which implies that
r
also divides
q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}}-1
. Repeating
i
times this argument, we get that
r
also divides

q^{\frac{m(k-2i)}{2}}-1

(14)

when
i\leq\lfloor k/2\rfloor
. If
k
is even, then for
i=k/2
, we have that
r
divides
2
, a contradiction. Therefore,
k
is odd and as a consequence
m
is even. In this case, when
i=(k-1)/2
in (
14
) we get that
r
divides
q^{\frac{m}{2}}+1
. In this way,
r\leq q^{\frac{m}{2}}+1\leq\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}
, and so
(q-1)\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1}{q^{m}-1}
is an integer if and only if
r=q^{\frac{m}{2}}+1=\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}
, which is possible if and only if

.
∎

In particular, this means that all the elements of a maximum

-design having

subspaces define canonical subgeometries. As we will see in the next section, such subspace designs always exist under the assumptions that

Proposition 4.16

We can now consider the linear codes associated with the linear sets defined by a maximum

These codes turn out to very interesting, indeed we can completely determine their weight enumerator and they are two-weight codes (except for a special case).
So, by Theorem

Corollary 4.24
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be a maximum
1
-design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
Consider the associated
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear sets

.
Let consider

the multiset where

\mathrm{m}(P)=1,\text{for every}\,\,P\in\mathcal{P}.

defines an

(that is a graph in which every vertex has the same number of neighbors) with

are its parameters. Now, we describe the connection between a two-intersection set with respect to hyperplanes and a strongly regular graph.

Let
\mathcal{P}

Embed
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)

of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)

Define

as the graph whose vertices are the points in
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)\setminus\mathcal{H}
and two vertices

are adjacent if the line

meets
\mathcal{H}
in a point of
\mathcal{P}
.
When
\mathcal{P}
is a two-intersection set with respect to hyperplanes, then

is a strongly regular graph and its parameters are described in the following result.

Theorem 4.26
.

(see
[
26
]

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of points in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)
of size

.
Let
N=t\frac{q^{\frac{km}{2}}-1}{q-1}
,
w_{0}=t\frac{q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}}-1}{q-1}
and
w_{1}=(t-1)\frac{q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}}-1}{q-1}+\frac{q^{\frac{m(k-2)}{2}+1}-1}{q-1}
. Then
\Gamma(L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}})
is a strongly regular graph with parameters
(v,K,\lambda,\mu)
, where

•

v=q^{mk}
;

•

K=N(q^{m}-1)
;

•

\lambda=K^{2}+3K-q^{m}(2N-w_{1}-w_{0})-Kq^{m}(2N-w_{1}-w_{0})+q^{2m}(N-w_{1})(N-w_{0})
;

•

\mu=\frac{q^{2m}(N-w_{1})(N-w_{0})}{q^{mk}}
.

Remark 4.28
.

By the constructions in Section
4.3
, we always have examples of maximum
1
-design when
k
is even, and hence a strongly regular graph with parameters as in Corollary
4.27
always exist for every even
k
.

5
Singleton bound for sum-rank metric codes and optimal subspace designs

In this section we will first recall some definitions and results on sum-rank metric codes and then we will identify the geometric counterparts of those which are optimal.
We will call them
optimal subspace designs
.

5.1
Sum-rank metric codes

The sum-rank metric has been recently investigated especially because of the performance of multishot network coding based on sum-rank metric codes, see
[
78
]
.
In the following we will recall some results from
[
4
,
25
,
64
,
63
,
66
,
73
,
77
]
.

Let
t
be a positive integer. Let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{t},n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}
be positive integers.
We consider the product of
t
matrix spaces

\Pi:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m_{i}\times n_{i}}.

The
sum-rank distance
is the function

d:\Pi\times\Pi\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}

defined by

d(X,Y)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathrm{rk}(X_{i}-Y_{i}),

where
X=(X_{1}\lvert\ldots\lvert X_{t})
,
Y=(Y_{1}\lvert\ldots\lvert Y_{t})
, with
X_{i},Y_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m_{i}\times n_{i}}
.

We define the
sum-rank weight
of an element
X=(X_{1}|\ldots|X_{t})\in\Pi
as

w(X):=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathrm{rk}(X_{i}).

Clearly,
d(X,Y)=w(X-Y)
, for every
X,Y\in\Pi
.

A (linear)
sum-rank metric code

\mathrm{C}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear subspace of
\Pi
endowed with the sum-rank distance.
The
minimum sum-rank distance
of a sum-rank metric code
\mathrm{C}
is defined as usual via

d(\mathrm{C})=\min\{w(X):X\in\mathrm{C},X\neq 0\}.

However, for our purposes we will need the description of sum-rank metric codes in terms of vectors.

5.2
Vector sum-rank metric codes and the associated subspace designs

In this section we set

\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{t}
an ordered tuples with
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\ldots\geq n_{t}
and we set
N\coloneqq n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
. We use the following compact notations for the direct sum of vector spaces

{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}.

Furthermore, we follow the notation used in
[
4
, Section 3.3]
.

Let start by recalling that the rank of a vector
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is the dimension of the
vector space generated over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by its entries, i.e.
\mathrm{rk}_{q}(v)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}})
. The sum-rank weight of an element
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
is

\mathrm{w}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathrm{rk}_{q}(x_{i}).

A (linear vector) sum-rank metric code
\mathrm{C}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
endowed with the sum-rank distance defined as

\mathrm{d}(x,y)=\mathrm{w}(x-y)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathrm{rk}_{q}(x_{i}-y_{i}),

where
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}),y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
.
Let
\mathrm{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
be a sum-rank metric code. We will write that
\mathrm{C}
is an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code (or
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code) if
k
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-dimension of
\mathrm{C}
and
d
is its minimum distance, that is

d=d(\mathrm{C})=\min\{\mathrm{d}(x,y)\colon x,y\in\mathrm{C},x\neq y\}.

The matrix and vector settings for the sum-rank metric described above are related in the following way.
For every
r\in[t]
, let
\Gamma_{r}=(\gamma_{1}^{(r)},\ldots,\gamma_{m}^{(r)})
be an ordered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, and let
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t})
. Given
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
, define the element
\Gamma(x)=(\Gamma_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,\Gamma_{t}(x_{t}))\in\Pi
, where

x_{r,i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma_{r}(x_{r})_{ij}\gamma_{j}^{(r)},\qquad\mbox{ for all }i\in[n_{r}].

In other words, the
r
-th block of
\Gamma(x)
is the matrix expansion of the vector
x_{r}
with respect to the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\Gamma_{r}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.
As already noted in
[
77
, Theorem 2.7]
, the map

\Gamma:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\longrightarrow\Pi

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear isometry between the metric spaces
({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}},\mathrm{d})
and
(\Pi,d)
.
For more details on this two settings, see
[
25
]
and
[
76
]
.

It is possible to define a Singleton-like bound for a sum-rank metric code. It has been stated and proved in the matrix setting (see
[
25
, Theorem 3.2]
), but in the next we adapt it for the vector setting.
Let
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{t}
with
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\ldots\geq n_{t}
and let
d
be a positive integer such that
d\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\min\{m,n_{i}\}
. Let
j
be the minimum positive integer such that
d\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j}\min\{m,n_{i}\}
. Then there exists a unique non-negative integer
\delta
such that
d=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\min\{m,n_{i}\}+\delta+1
with
0\leq\delta\leq\min\{m,n_{i}\}-1
. This way of writing
d
allows us to find the maximum possible size for an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathrm{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code.
Let
j
and
\delta
be the unique integers satisfying

d-1=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\min\{m,n_{i}\}+\delta\mbox{ and }0\leq\delta\leq\min\{m,n_{j}\}-1.

Then

\lvert\mathrm{C}\rvert\leq q^{m\sum_{i=j}^{t}n_{i}-\max\{m,n_{j}\}\delta}.

(15)

In particular,

1.

If
m\geq n_{1}
, then
d-1=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}n_{i}+\delta\mbox{ with }0\leq\delta\leq n_{j}-1
and

\lvert\mathrm{C}\rvert\leq q^{m(N-d+1)}.

2.

If
n=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}\geq m
then
d-1=m(j-1)+\delta,\,\,0\leq\delta\leq m-1
and

\lvert\mathrm{C}\rvert\leq q^{n(tm-d+1)}.

A
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code is called
Maximum Sum-Rank Distance code
(or shortly
MSRD code
) if its size attains the bound of Theorem
5.1
.

The map

(x,y)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}\cdot y_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}

is a symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
.
The dual of an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\mathrm{C}
is defined as

\mathrm{C}^{\perp}=\left\{y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}\cdot y_{i}=0\mbox{ for all }x=(x_{1}\lvert\ldots\lvert x_{t})\in\mathrm{C}\right\}.

(16)

The dual of a sum-rank code
\mathrm{C}
is also a sum-rank code and

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\mathrm{C}^{\perp})=N-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\mathrm{C}).

(17)

Also, the MSRD property is invariant under duality.

Theorem 5.2
.

(see
[
25
, Theorem 6.1]
,
[
64
, Theorem 5]
)
Let
\mathrm{C}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code.
Then,

d(\mathrm{C}^{\perp})\leq\begin{cases}N-d+2,\mbox{ if }n_{1}\leq m,\\
tm-d+2,\mbox{ if }m\leq n_{1}\mbox{ and }n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t},\end{cases}

and equality holds when
\mathrm{C}
is an MSRD code. In particular, if
\mathrm{C}
is an MSRD code then
\mathrm{C}^{\perp}
is an MSRD code as well.

The second part of the above result follows by Theorem
5.1
.

Now, we recall the definition of equivalence between sum-rank metric codes. An
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometry
\phi
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear map of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
that preserves the distance, i.e.
\mathrm{w}(x)=\mathrm{w}(\phi(x))
, for every
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, or equivalently
\mathrm{d}(x,y)=\mathrm{d}(\phi(x),\phi(y))
, for every
x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
.
The
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
equipped with sum-rank metric have been classified in
[
65
, Theorem 2]
, see also
[
4
, Theorem 3.7]
.

In order to present such a classification, we introduce the following notation.
Let
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{n}):=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}
, and let
\ell:=\lvert\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{n})\rvert
. Let
n_{i_{1}},\ldots,n_{i_{\ell}}
be the distinct elements of
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{n})
.
By
\lambda(\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
we will denote the vector whose entries are

\lambda_{j}:=\lvert\{k\colon n_{k}=n_{i_{j}}\}\rvert,\ \ \ \ \mbox{for each }j=1,\ldots,\ell.

So,
\lambda_{j}
denotes the number of blocks that have the same length
n_{i_{j}}
.
For a vector
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell})\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
, we define

S_{\mathbf{v}}=S_{v_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{v_{\ell}},

where
S_{i}
is the symmetric group of order
i
.
Similarly, we denote by
\hbox{{\rm GL}}(\mathbf{v},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
the direct product of the general linear groups of degree
v_{i}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, i.e.

\hbox{{\rm GL}}(\mathbf{n},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})=\hbox{{\rm GL}}(n_{1},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\times\ldots\times\hbox{{\rm GL}}(n_{t},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}).

Theorem 5.3
.

(see
[
65
, Theorem 2]
)
The group of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of the space
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
endowed with the sum-rank metric is

(({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\ast})^{t}\times\hbox{{\rm GL}}(\mathbf{n},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}))\rtimes S_{\lambda(\mathbf{n})},

which acts as

({a},M_{1},\ldots,M_{t},\pi)\cdot(c^{(1)}\mid\ldots\mid c^{(t)})\longmapsto(a_{1}c^{(\pi^{-1}(1))}M_{1}\mid\ldots\mid a_{t}c^{(\pi^{-1}(t))}M_{t}).

This means that the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
are the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear maps that multiply each block for a nonzero scalar in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and for invertible matrices with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and permute blocks of the same length.

We will say that two sum-rank metric codes
\mathrm{C}_{1}
and
\mathrm{C}_{2}
are equivalent if there exists an isometry
\varphi
as in Theorem
5.3
such that
\varphi(\mathrm{C}_{1})=\mathrm{C}_{2}
.
We denote the set of equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
sum-rank metric codes by
\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
.

Remark 5.4
.

It is easy to see that two sum-rank metric codes
\mathrm{C}_{1}
and
\mathrm{C}_{2}
are equivalent if and only if their dual codes
\mathrm{C}_{1}^{\perp}
and
\mathrm{C}_{2}^{\perp}
are equivalent
Indeed, if
\mathrm{C}_{1}
and
\mathrm{C}_{2}
are equivalent then there exists
({a},M_{1},\ldots,M_{t},\pi)\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\ast})^{t}\times\hbox{{\rm GL}}(\mathbf{n},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\rtimes S_{\lambda(\mathbf{n})}
such that
(a,M_{1},\ldots,M_{t},\pi)\mathrm{C}_{1}=\mathrm{C}_{2}
.
The codes
\mathrm{C}_{1}^{\perp}
and
\mathrm{C}_{2}^{\perp}
are equivalent via the isometry defined by
(a^{\prime},(M_{1}^{\top})^{-1},\ldots,(M_{t}^{\top})^{-1},\pi)
, where
{a}^{\prime}=(a_{1}^{-1},\ldots,a_{t}^{-1})
.
The converse clearly holds.

Let
\mathrm{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
be a linear sum-rank metric code. Let
G=(G_{1}\lvert\ldots\lvert G_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times N}
be a generator matrix of
\mathrm{C}
, with
G_{1},\ldots,G_{t}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times{n_{i}}}
. We say that
\C
is
non-degenerate
if the columns of
G_{i}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent for
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
; see
[
77
, Definition 2.11, Proposition 2.13]
.

We will recall now some results from
[
77
]
, on the connections between sum-rank metric codes and some sets of subspaces, which will be then rephrased in terms of subspace designs.

Theorem 5.5
.

(see
[
77
, Theorem 3.1]
)
Let
\mathrm{C}
be a non-degenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Let
G=(G_{1}\lvert\ldots\lvert G_{t})
be an its generator matrix.
Let
U_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.
The sum-rank weight of an element
xG\in\mathrm{C}
, with
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, is

{\mathrm{w}}(xG)=N-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap x^{\perp}),

where
x^{\perp}=\{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\colon x\cdot y=0\}.
In particular,

d=N-\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\colon H\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

(18)

The equivalence classes of Hamming-metric non-degenerate codes are in one-to-one correspondence with equivalence classes of projective systems. Recently, in
[
83
]
it has been shown that equivalence classes of non-degenerate rank metric codes are in one-to-one correspondence with equivalence classes of
q
-systems, where the latter constitute the
q
-analogue of projective systems.
The following definition extends the notions of projective systems and
q
-systems.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{t}
, with
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}
. An
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-
system

\mathrm{U}
is an ordered set
(U_{1},\cdots,U_{t})
, where, for any
i\in[t]
,
U_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
of dimension
n_{i}
, such that

\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and

d=N-\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\mid H\textnormal{ is an ${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}$-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

Moreover, two
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-systems
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
and
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{t})
are
equivalent
if there exists an isomorphism
\varphi\in\hbox{{\rm GL}}(k,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
and a permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
, such that for every
i\in[t]

\varphi(U_{i})=a_{i}V_{\sigma(i)}.

We denote the set of equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-systems by
\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
.

Clearly, systems naturally defines subspace designs with respect to hyperplanes as follows.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be an ordered set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-system if and only if
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
form an
(k-1,N-d)_{q}
-subspace design of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
such that

•

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{i}
,

•

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)=N-d
, for some
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplane
H
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
,

•

\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.

In
[
77
]
, it has been proved that there is a one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of sum-rank non-degenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code and equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-systems.
This correspondence can be formalized by the following two maps

\displaystyle\Psi:\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}.

Let
[\mathrm{C}]\in\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
and let
\overline{\mathrm{C}}
be a representative of
[\mathrm{C}]
. Let
\mathbf{G}=(G_{1}\lvert\ldots\lvert G_{t})
be a generator matrix for
\overline{\mathrm{C}}
. Then
\Psi([\mathrm{C}])
is the equivalence class of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-systems
[\mathrm{U}]
, where
\mathrm{U}=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
and
U_{i}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
for every
i\in[t]
. In this case
U
is also called the
system associated with

\overline{\mathrm{C}}
. Viceversa, given
[(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})]\in\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
, for every
i\in[t]
. Define
G_{i}
as the matrix whose columns are an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
U_{i}
. Then
\Phi([(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})])
is the equivalence class of the sum-rank metric codes generated by
G=(G_{1}\lvert\ldots\lvert G_{t})
.
\Psi
and
\Phi
are well-posed and they are inverse of each other.

Remark 5.8
.

As observed in
[
77
, Remark 3.6]
, when
t=1
, the definition of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-system coincides with the definition of
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-system introduced in
[
83
]
and the correspondence
(\Psi,\Phi)
gives us a one-to-one correspondence between classes of non-degenerate
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
rank metric codes and classes of
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-system, for more details see
[
83
]
and
[
2
]
.
When
n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}=1
, the definition of
[\mathbf{1},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-system does not immediately coincide with that of projective system (see
[
90
]
), but we can still identify classes of projective systems and classes of
[\mathbf{1},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-system.

5.3
Optimal subspace designs

We are now ready to give an answer to Problem
1.4
when considering subspace designs with respect to hyperplanes. The strategy regards the use of Proposition
5.7
, which points out a connection between subspace designs with respect to hyperplanes and systems, and the use of the connection shown in the previous section between systems and linear sum-rank metric codes. This allows us to use the Singleton bound to get a lower bound on the parameter
A
of a
(k-1,A)
-subspace design which is non-degenerate.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\mathrm{U}=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be a non-degenerate
(k-1,A)_{q}
-subspace design in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, with
n_{i}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})
for every
i
, and
n_{1}\geq\ldots\geq n_{t}
and let

M=\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\colon H\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}

and
d=N-M
, where
N=\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}
.
Let
j
and
\delta
be the unique integers satisfying

d-1=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\min\{m,n_{i}\}+\delta\mbox{ and }0\leq\delta\leq\min\{m,n_{j}\}-1.

Then

A\geq N-\left(\frac{m\sum_{i=j}^{t}n_{i}-mk}{\max\{m,n_{j}\}}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\min\{m,n_{j}\}+1\right).

(19)

In particular,

A\geq\begin{cases}k-1,&\mbox{ if }n_{1}\leq m,\\
N-tm+\frac{m}{n}k-1,&\mbox{ if }m\leq n=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}.\end{cases}

Moreover, a sum-rank metric code

\mathrm{C}\in\Phi([\mathrm{U}])
is an MSRD code if and only if

M\leq N-\left(\frac{m\sum_{i=j}^{t}n_{i}-mk}{\max\{m,n_{j}\}}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\min\{m,n_{j}\}+1\right).

In particular,
\mathrm{C}
is an MSRD code if and only if

M\leq\begin{cases}k-1,&\mbox{ if }n_{1}\leq m,\\
\\
N-tm+\frac{m}{n}k-1,&\mbox{ if }m\leq n=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}.\end{cases}

(20)

Proof.

First, we observe that
\mathrm{U}=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-system. Let
\mathrm{C}\in\Phi([\mathrm{U}])
, then
\mathrm{C}
is an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code and by (
15
) we have that

mk\leq m\sum_{i=j}^{t}n_{i}-\max\{m,n_{j}\}\delta=m\sum_{i=j}^{t}n_{i}-\max\{m,n_{j}\}(d-1-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\min\{m,n_{i}\}).

Since
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a
(k-1,A)_{q}
-subspace design, then
M\leq A
and
d\geq N-A
. Then (
19
) follows. The remaining part follows by the definition of MSRD code.
∎

The subspace designs as in Theorem
5.9
satisfying equality in (
19
) will be called
optimal subspace designs
, i.e. when the associated sum-rank metric code is an MSRD code.

Remark 5.10
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be a non-degenerate
(k-1,A)_{q}
-design in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})\leq m
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.
Then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an optimal subspace design if and only if
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(k-1)
-design.

We will see some examples of optimal subspace designs in the next section.

5.4
Examples of optimal subspace designs

In order to construct optimal subspace designs, we can consider a construction of MSRD codes in
[
25
]
, relying on
MRD codes
, that is rank metric codes whose parameters satisfy equality in the Singleton bound for rank metric codes, and rephrased in the vector framework.

The study of MSRD codes started with Martínez-Peñas
[
63
]
, and since then many other papers analyzed
their structure
[
67
,
68
,
79
]
from the vector point of view. In this framework, the linearized Reed-Solomon codes represented the first family of MSRD codes constructed.
Neri in
[
76
]
extended the family of generalized twisted Gabidulin codes to a new family of MSRD codes.
In Theorem
4.2
, we already constructed examples of
(k-1)
-designs in
V=V(k,q^{m})
which turned out to be optimal subspace designs.

Other examples can be provided in
[
25
, Construction 7.2 (a)]
. Let
m\geq n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}
and let embed
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{1}}
and consider an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear MRD-code in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{1}}
. It is possible to construct an MSRD code whose first block is obtained by considering the elements of the fixed MRD code.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
m\geq n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\ldots\geq n_{t}
and let
N=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
.
Let
\hat{\mathrm{C}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{1}}
be an MRD code with minimum distance
2
, i.e.
\hat{\mathrm{C}}
is an
[n_{1},n_{1}-1,2]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Let consider the map
\phi_{i}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{1}}
defined by adding
n_{1}-n_{i}
zero entries to the given vector. Define the sum-rank metric code as

\mathrm{C}=\left\{\left({v}_{1}-\sum_{i=2}^{t}\phi_{i}({v}_{i}),{v}_{2},\ldots,{v}_{t}\right):{v}_{1}\in\hat{\mathrm{C}},{v}_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}\right\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{N}

Then
\mathrm{C}
is an
[\mathbf{n},N-1,2]_{q^{m}/q}
-code, and hence an MSRD code.

Thanks to the previous construction we can define the following optimal design. This can be obtained by plugging together an
(n_{1}-1)
-scattered subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{1}}
and subgeometries of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
.

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
m,n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}
be positive integers such that
m\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}
and let
N=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
. Let
U
be an
[n_{1},n_{1}-1,2]_{q^{m}/q}
-system. Let
{g}_{1}=(g_{1,1},\ldots,

g_{1,n_{1}-1}),\ldots,

{g}_{n_{1}}=(g_{n_{1},1},\ldots,g_{n_{1},n_{1}-1})
be a basis of
U
.
Let

U_{1}=\{(\alpha_{1}g_{1,1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n_{1}}g_{n_{1},1},\ldots,\alpha_{1}g_{1,n_{1}-1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n_{1}}g_{n_{1},n_{1}-1}\lvert

-\alpha_{1},\ldots,-\alpha_{n_{2}}\lvert\ldots\lvert-\alpha_{1},\ldots,-\alpha_{n_{t}}):\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{N-1}

and

U_{i}=0{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{1}-1}\oplus_{j=2}^{t}\delta_{i,j}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{j}},

for any

if

-system with
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})

(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(N-2)
-design in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{N-1}
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{i}
.

Other examples arise from the maximum
1
-design investigated in Section

.

Since the dimensions of the

In Theorem

, the linear sets

for every hyperplane

6
Dualities

6.1
Ordinary dual

Let
\sigma:V\times V\longrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
be a non-degenerate reflexive sesquilinear form on
V=V(k,q^{m})
and define

\sigma^{\prime}:({u},{v})\in V\times V\rightarrow\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\sigma({u},{v}))\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}.

It is known that
\sigma^{\prime}
is a non-degenerate reflexive sesquilinear form on
V
, when
V
is regarded as an
mk
-dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
be the orthogonal complement maps defined by
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
on the lattices of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
V
, respectively. Recall that if
W
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
and
U
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V
then
W^{\tau}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
,
U^{\tau^{\prime}}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V
,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(W^{\tau})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(W)=k
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\tau^{\prime}})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=mk
. It is easy to see that
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
coincide when applied to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces, that is
W^{\tau}=W^{\tau^{\prime}}
for each
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
W
of
V
. Also,
U^{\tau^{\prime}}
is called the
dual
of
U
(with respect to
\tau^{\prime}
). The dual of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V
does not depend on the choice of the non-degenerate reflexive sesquilinear forms
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
on
V
. More precisely, consider two non-degenerate reflexive sesquilinear forms
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
and then consider
\tau_{1}^{\prime}
and
\tau_{2}^{\prime}
as above, then
U^{\tau_{1}^{\prime}}
and
U^{\tau_{2}^{\prime}}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}(k,q^{m})
-equivalent, for any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U
of
V
(see
[
80
, Section 2]
). If
W
is an
s
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
and
U
is a
h
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V
, then

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\tau^{\prime}}\cap W^{\tau})-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap W)=km-h-ms.

(21)

For more details see
[
89
, section 7]
.

Let consider an ordered set
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
The ordered set
(U_{1}^{\tau^{\prime}},\ldots,U_{t}^{\tau^{\prime}})
will be called the
dual subspaces
of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
.
Furthermore, note that the dual subspaces of
(U_{1}^{\tau^{\prime}},\ldots,U_{t}^{\tau^{\prime}})
coincide with the ordered set
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
.

From Equation (
21
) the next result immediately follows.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
, with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{i}
for any
i
and suppose that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{1}^{\tau^{\prime}},\ldots,U_{t}^{\tau^{\prime}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq k-s.

Then the dual subspaces
(U_{1}^{\tau^{\prime}},\ldots,U_{t}^{\tau^{\prime}})
of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(k-s,A+t(k-s)m-N)_{q}
-subspace design where
N=\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\tau^{\prime}})=mk-n_{i}
.

When the assumptions of the above proposition are satisfied, we say that
(U_{1}^{\tau^{\prime}},\ldots,U_{t}^{\tau^{\prime}})
is the
dual subspace design
of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
.

Now we show that the property of being maximum
1
-design is preserved via duality.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be a maximum
1
-design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
. Then the dual subspace design of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a maximum
1
-design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.12
, it follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\in\left\{\frac{tm(k-2)}{2},\frac{tm(k-2)}{2}+1\right\},

for every hyperplane
H
of
V=V(k,q^{m})
, i.e.
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is also an
\left(k-1,\frac{tm(k-2)}{2}+1\right)_{q}
-subspace design.
Since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{1}^{\tau^{\prime}},\ldots,U_{t}^{\tau^{\prime}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq 1
(with the above notation), by Proposition
6.1
the dual subspace design
(U_{1}^{\tau^{\prime}},\ldots,U_{t}^{\tau^{\prime}})
of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
(seen as
\left(k-1,\frac{tm(k-2)}{2}+1\right)_{q}
-subspace design) is a
(1,\frac{tm(k-2)}{2}+1+tm-t\frac{mk}{2})_{q}
-subspace design, i.e. the dual subspace design of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a
1
-design.
Moreover, since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\tau^{\prime}})=\frac{mk}{2}
,
(U_{1}^{\tau^{\prime}},\ldots,U_{t}^{\tau^{\prime}})
is a maximum
1
-design.
∎

As a corollary we get a characterization of maximum
1
-design as a
(k-1,A)_{q}
-subspace design.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be an ordered set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
V=V(k,q^{m})
having dimension
\frac{mk}{2}
.
Then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a maximum
1
-design in
V
if and only if it is a
\left(k-1,\frac{tm(k-2)}{2}+1\right)_{q}
-subspace design in
V
.

Proof.

The if part follows by Proposition
3.11
and Theorem
4.12
.
For the converse, suppose that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a
\left(k-1,\frac{tm(k-2)}{2}+1\right)_{q}
-subspace design, then its dual subspace design
(U_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,U_{t}^{\prime})
of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a maximum
1
-design because of Proposition
6.1
.
Now, Theorem
6.2
implies the assertion.
∎

Corollary
6.3
gives a one-to-one correspondence between maximum
1
-designs and MSRD codes with certain parameters, i.e. maximum
1
-designs are optimal subspace designs.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
m,k
be two integers such that
mk
is even with
k\geq 2
.
Let
\mathrm{U}=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be an ordered set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
of dimension
\frac{mk}{2}
such that

\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Then every code in
\Phi([\mathrm{U}])
is an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
MSRD code with
\mathbf{n}=(mk/2,\ldots,mk/2)
and

d=t\frac{mk}{2}-t\frac{m(k-2)}{2}-1=mt-1

if and only if
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a maximum
1
-design.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathrm{U}
defines an MSRD code, then by Theorem
5.9
it follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\leq t\frac{mk}{2}-tm+1,

for every hyperplane
H
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. This implies that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a
\left(k-1,\frac{tm(k-2)}{2}+1\right)_{q}
-subspace design and hence by Corollary
6.3
it is also a maximum
1
-subspace design.
Conversely, if
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a maximum
1
-subspace design, then again applying Corollary
6.3
and Theorem
5.9
we obtain that every code in
\Phi([\mathrm{U}])
is an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
MSRD code.
∎

We recall an upper bound on the number of blocks of an MSRD code.

Theorem 6.5
.

(see
[
25
, Theorem 6.12]
)
Let
\mathrm{C}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
MSRD code with
n=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}\geq m
and
d\geq 3
. Then

t\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d-3}{m}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{q^{m}-q^{m\left\lfloor\frac{d-3}{m}\right\rfloor+m-d+3}+(q-1)(q^{n}+1)}{q^{m}-1}\right\rfloor.

(22)

Theorem
6.4
gives a new bound on the number of blocks that an MSRD code associated with a maximum
1
-design can have. Indeed, using the connection between maximum
1
-design and MSRD code, the number of blocks corresponds to the number of subspaces in a maximum
1
-design. This latter number has been bounded in Corollary
4.14
and we showed that it is sharp for some set of parameters (see Theorem
4.21
).

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
\mathrm{C}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
MSRD code with
\mathbf{n}=(mk/2,\ldots,mk/2)
and
d=tm-1
. Then
t\leq\left\lfloor(q-1)\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1}{q^{m}-1}\right\rfloor
. Moreover, if
t\geq 3
this bound improves (
22
) of Theorem
6.5
.

Proof.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be a system associated with
\mathrm{C}
. By Theorem
6.4
,
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a maximum
1
-design and so by Corollary
4.14
, we get
t\leq(q-1)\frac{q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1}{q^{m}-1}
that proves the first part. Moreover, since
t\geq 3
we have
\left\lfloor\frac{tm-4}{m}\right\rfloor\geq 1
and so it follows that

\begin{array}[]{c l}\left\lfloor\frac{(q-1)(q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1)}{q^{m}-1}\right\rfloor&\leq\left\lfloor\frac{tm-4}{m}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{q^{m}-q^{m\left\lfloor\frac{tm-4}{m}\right\rfloor+m-tm+4}+(q-1)(q^{\frac{mk}{2}}+1)}{q^{m}-1}\right\rfloor,\end{array}

where the last quantity corresponds to the right hand side of the bound (
22
). This proves the last part.
∎

As a consequence, this bound shows that the bound in Theorem
6.5
is not sharp for these parameters.

6.2
Delsarte dual

In this section we provide another duality acting on subspace designs by using their connection with sum-rank metric codes.

Let
\mathrm{U}=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be a non-degenerate
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
Suppose that
n_{i}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})
for every
i\in[t]
and let
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})
and
N=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
, and w.l.o.g. suppose that
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}
.
Up to coordinatize
V
, we may assume that
V={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.
Let consider the class of sum-rank metric codes
[\mathrm{C}]=\Phi([\mathrm{U}])
. Let consider
\overline{\mathrm{C}}\in[\mathrm{C}]
. Let
\overline{\mathrm{C}}^{\perp}
be the dual of
\overline{\mathrm{C}}
defined as in (
16
) and suppose that
\overline{\mathrm{C}}^{\perp}
is non-degenerate. We call
[\mathrm{U}^{\prime}]=\Psi([\overline{\mathrm{C}}^{\perp}])
the
Delsarte dual class
of
[\mathrm{U}]
and any ordered set
(U_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,U^{\prime}_{t})
such that
(U_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,U^{\prime}_{t})\in\Psi([\overline{\mathrm{C}}^{\perp}])
will be called a
Delsarte dual design
of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
.
This definition is well-posed because of Remark
5.4
.

In order to give a relation on the parameters of a subspace design and its dual we need the following property.

Proposition 6.7
.

Suppose that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a non-degenerate subspace design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
, with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{i}
, for any
i
.

Let
M=\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\colon H\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-hyperplane of }V\}
.
Then the Delsarte dual subspace design
(U_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,U_{t}^{\prime})
of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
has the following properties:

•

there exists
\sigma\in S_{t}
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\prime})=n_{\sigma(i)}
for any
i
;

•

if
M^{\prime}=\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}^{\prime}\cap H)\colon H\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-hyperplane of }V\}
then

M^{\prime}\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}N-M-2&\text{if}\,\,m\geq\max\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\},\\
2N-tm-M-2&\text{if}\,\,m\leq n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
N=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}
. The assertion follows by considering a sum-rank metric code
\mathrm{C}
in
\Phi([(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})])
and then apply Theorem
5.5
, so that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
can be seen as an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-system with
d=N-M
.
Clearly,
\mathrm{C}^{\perp}\in\Phi([(U_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,U_{t}^{\prime})])
and then apply Theorem
5.5
, so that
(U_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,U_{t}^{\prime})
can be seen as an
[\mathbf{n},k,d^{\prime}]_{q}
-system with
d^{\prime}=N-M^{\prime}
.
The assertion then follows by Theorem
5.2
.
∎

As a consequence we obtain the following corollary, also taking into account Theorem
5.2
.

Corollary 6.8
.

Suppose that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a non-degenerate
(k-1,A)_{q}
-subspace design in
V=V(k,q^{m})
, with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=n_{i}
for any
i
, and suppose that

A=\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\colon H\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-hyperplane of }V\right\}.

Then the Delsarte dual subspace design
(U_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,U_{t}^{\prime})
of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(k-1,A^{\prime})
-subspace design with

A^{\prime}\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}N-A-2&\text{if}\,\,m\geq\max\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\},\\
2N-tm-A-2&\text{if}\,\,m\leq n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
N=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
. In particular, the Delsarte dual of an optimal subspace design is an optimal subspace design.

7
More bounds and constructions

In this section we will further explore bounds and constructions of subspace designs whose parameters satisfy equality in the provided bounds.

As a consequence of the definition of subspace evasive subspaces we obtain that the ordered set of subspace evasive subspaces form a subspace design with the following parameters.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be an ordered set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
V=V(k,q^{m})
such that
U_{i}
is an
(s,r_{i})_{q}
-evasive subspace for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
. Then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(s,A)_{q}
-subspace design with
A=\sum_{i=1}^{t}r_{i}
.

Under certain assumptions, the bounds in Corollary
3.8
may be improved.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be a
(k-1,A)_{q}
-subspace design of
V=V(k,q^{m})
. Let
n=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})
for any
i
. If
n\geq m
then

1.

if
A<tm(k-1)
then
n\leq m+\frac{A}{t}-1
;

2.

if
A<\frac{tm}{k-1}+k-2
then
tn\leq tm+A-k+1
.

Proof.

By definition,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq k-1
. First suppose that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=k-1
. Then
H=\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and so

tn=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\leq A\leq A+tm-t,

proving 1. of the assertion. Since

, by the assumption in 2. we have that

and so

proving also 2. of the assertion. Assume now that
\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}

is an

-system, with

and

where

.

Since
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a
(k-1,A)_{q}
-subspace design, it follows that

.

Let

. We can apply the Singleton bound (2. of Theorem
5.1
) on the parameters of
[\mathrm{C}]
.
Indeed, we have

mk\leq n(tm-d+1),

mk\leq n(tm-tn+A+1).

(23)

To prove the first part, suppose for the contrary

To prove the second part, first we observe that (

to the contrary suppose that

and so

from which we obtain

∎

8
Constructions from strong subspace designs

In most of the applications strong subspace designs have been used to construct subspace designs.
So, in this section we describe how to obtain a subspace design from a strong subspace design using different tricks.

8.1
Using subspace evasive subspaces

The first, and probably the most used, subspace design employs subspace evasive subspaces.

Theorem 8.1
.

Consider a strong
(s,A)
-subspace design
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{t})
in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
Let
k_{i}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(V_{i})
, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.
Let
S\subseteq V
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of dimension
d
with the property that
S
is a
(h,ch)_{q}
-evasive subspace for every
h\leq s
, with
c
a positive number. Let
U_{i}=V_{i}\cap S
, for every
i
. If
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq s
, then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(s,cA)_{q}
-subspace design and each
U_{i}
has dimension at least
mk_{i}-km+d
.

Proof.

By applying Grassmann’s formula to the
U_{i}
’s we have that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})\geq mk_{i}-km+d
.
Now, let
W
be a
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of dimension
s
in
V
. By the assumptions we have

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(V_{i}\cap W)\leq A.

(25)

For each
i
, we have that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(W\cap V_{i})=s_{i}\leq s
. Then by the assumptions on
S
, we have that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(W\cap U_{i})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((W\cap V_{i})\cap S)\leq cs_{i}.

So,

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(W\cap U_{i})\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(W\cap V_{i})\leq cA,

and the assertion follows by (
25
).
∎

More generally and following the above proof one gets the following result.

Theorem 8.2
.

Consider a strong
(s,A)
-subspace design
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{t})
in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
Let
k_{i}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(V_{i})
, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.
Let
S\subseteq V
be an
(s,r)_{q}
-evasive subspace. Let
U_{i}=V_{i}\cap S
, for every
i
. If
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\geq s
, then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
(s,t(r-s)+A)_{q}
-subspace design and each
U_{i}
has dimension at least
mk_{i}-km+d
.

Remark 8.3
.

When

in Theorem

is an

-scattered subspace in

.

8.2

Another way to get subspace designs from strong subspace designs regards the use of intermediate fields
[

]

To see this, first observe that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k}
is an
s
-scattered subspace in

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
c
be an integer such that
m\lvert c
. Let
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{t})
be a strong
(s,A)
-subspace design in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Then
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{t})
is an
(s,mA)_{q}
-subspace design in

Proof.

Let

be an

-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{c}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{c}}^{k}
. Since
V_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
we have that

. Let

∎

To construct subspace designs we can also use high-degree places, as done by in

for a fixed strong subspace design.
Consider a real number

8.4
Cameron-Liebler sets

We conclude this section by describing a way to obtain strong subspace design from well-known objects in finite geometry known as Cameron-Liebler sets of
n
-dimensional projective subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)=\mathrm{PG}(V,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
introduced in
[
84
]
, generalizing Cameron-Liebler sets of lines in the projective space
\mathrm{PG}(3,q)
originally introduced by Cameron and Liebler in
[
27
]
.
Indeed, they introduced specific line classes
\mathcal{L}
of size
x(q^{2}+q+1)
in
\mathrm{PG}(3,q)
with the property that every line spread in
\mathrm{PG}(3,q)
has
x
lines in common with
\mathcal{L}
; such a
x
is called the
parameter
of
\mathcal{L}
.

In
[
18
]
are proven several equivalent properties that defines Cameron-Liebler sets. We choose one that is useful for us.

Definition 8.7
.

(see
[
18
, Theorem 2.9]
)
A set of
n
-dimensional projective subspaces
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
with

is a

of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
with parameter

if and only if
for a given

and any
n
-dimensional projective subspaces
\pi
of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
, the numbers of elements of
\mathcal{L}
, meeting
\pi
in a
(n-i)
-dimensional projective subspace is given by:

\begin{cases}w_{i}=\left((x-1)\frac{q^{n+1}-1}{q^{n-i+1}-1}+q^{i}\frac{q^{k-n}-1}{q^{i}-1}\right)q^{i(i-1)}\binom{k-n-1}{i-1}_{q}\binom{n}{i}_{q}&\mbox{ if }\pi\in\mathcal{L},\\
w^{\prime}_{i}=x\binom{k-n-1}{i-1}_{q}\binom{n+1}{i}_{q}q^{i(i-1)}&\mbox{ if }\pi\notin\mathcal{L}.\end{cases}

Since there is a one-to-one correspondence between
i
-dimensional projective subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
and the
(i+1)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
V

Theorem 8.8
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{t}\}
be a Cameron-Liebler set of
n
-dimensional projective subspaces of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)=\mathrm{PG}(V,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
with parameter
x
and
k\geq 2n+1
.
Let

be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces such that
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\mathrm{PG}(V_{i},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
Then
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{t})
is a strong
(n+1,A)
-subspace design of
V
with

A=n+1+\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}w_{i}(n-i+1),

and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{i})=n+1
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.

Proof.

Let
W
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional subspace of
V
and let

. We have

\sum_{i}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{i}\cap W)=\sum_{i}^{t}(\dim(\mathcal{S}_{i}\cap\mathcal{W})+1).

In
\mathcal{L}

in a

This means that

Since

, for

∎

-subspace design if

9
Cutting designs and minimal sum-rank metric codes

In this section we study subspace designs with a special property that involves the hyperplanes of the ambient space, which we will call cutting designs. The name arises from
cutting blocking sets
, recently introduced by Bonini and Borello in
[
22
]
, with the aim of constructing minimal codes.
This structure has been also studied before the paper
[
22
]
, under other names such as strong blocking sets and generator sets, in connection with saturating sets and covering codes by Davydov, Giulietti, Marcugini and Pambianco
[
32
]
and higgledy-piggledy line arrangements by Fancsali and Sziklai
[
38
]
(see also
[
52
]
and
[
39
]
).
In this section we propose a generalization of this notion to subspace designs which turns out to be connected with minimal sum-rank metric codes, as we will see later.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be an ordered set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
If for any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplanes
H,H^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
such that

U_{i}\cap H\subseteq U_{i}\cap H^{\prime}\,\,\mbox{for every}\,\,i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}

implies
H=H^{\prime}
, then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is called a
cutting design
.

We now provide an easier to handle characterization of cutting designs, which follows the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 3.3]
(see also
[
22
, Theorem 3.5]
).

Proposition 9.2
.

Consider an ordered set
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
Then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a cutting design if and only if for every
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplane
H
of
V
we have
\langle U_{1}\cap H,\ldots,U_{t}\cap H\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=H
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a cutting design and, by contradiction, suppose that there exists an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplane
H
of
V
such that
\langle U_{1}\cap H,\ldots,U_{t}\cap H\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=X\subsetneq H
. Let
H^{\prime}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplane of
V
containing
X
different from
H
. So we have that
U_{i}\cap H\subseteq U_{i}\cap H^{\prime}
, for every
i
. Since
H^{\prime}\neq H
, this yields a contradiction.

Conversely, assume that for every
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplane
H
we have
\langle U_{1}\cap H,\ldots,U_{t}\cap H\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=H
.
Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
two hyperplanes such that

U_{i}\cap H\subseteq U_{i}\cap H^{\prime}\,\,\mbox{for every}\,\,i\in[t].

This implies that

\langle U_{1}\cap H,\ldots,U_{t}\cap H\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\subseteq\langle U_{1}\cap H^{\prime},\ldots,U_{t}\cap H^{\prime}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}},

that is
H=H^{\prime}
.
∎

Remark 9.3
.

By Proposition
9.2
, it follows that a cutting design
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
of
V(k,q^{m})
is also a non-degenerate
(k-1,A)_{q}
-design, for a certain

.
Actually, it has a more strong property:
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\geq k-1
for every
H
hyperplane of
V
.

As the previous remark suggests, cutting designs are subspace designs with special pattern of intersections with hyperplanes. In particular, examples of cutting designs can be obtained from subspace designs with the property that the sum of the dimension of intersections with hyperplanes is a non-zero constant; this will allow us to give constructions of cutting designs (cf. Construction
9.14
).

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be an order set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
V=V(k,q^{m})
. Suppose that there exists a positive integer
c
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)=c
for every hyperplane of
V
. Then
c\geq k-1
and
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a non-degenerate cutting design.

Proof.

Let
n_{i}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})
. Let start by proving that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a non-degenerate subspace design. Suppose by contradiction that
\langle U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\subseteq H
, for a hyperplane
H
. This means that
U_{i}\subseteq H
for every
i
, and hence
c=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}
. Suppose, without loss of generality that
n_{1}>0
and let
v\in U_{1}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
H^{\prime}
be a hyperplane of
V
such that
v\notin H^{\prime}
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H^{\prime})<\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}=c
, a contradiction to the fact that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H^{\prime})=c
. Now we show that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a cutting design.
Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
be two hyperplanes of
V
such that

U_{i}\cap H\subseteq U_{i}\cap H^{\prime}\,\,\mbox{for every}\,\,i\in[t].

So, we get that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H^{\prime})
for every
i
, and since

c=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H^{\prime})=c,

it follows that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H^{\prime})
and therefore
U_{i}\cap H=U_{i}\cap H^{\prime}
for every
i
. In particular,
U_{i}\cap H=U_{i}\cap H^{\prime}=U_{i}\cap H\cap H^{\prime}\subseteq H\cap H^{\prime}
for any
i
. Suppose that
H\neq H^{\prime}
, this means that
H\cap H^{\prime}
is a
(k-2)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
. Let
U_{i}^{\prime}=U_{i}\cap H=U_{i}\cap H^{\prime}
for every
i
. Therefore, there exists an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U_{i}^{\prime\prime}
of
V
such that
U_{i}=U_{i}^{\prime}\oplus U_{i}^{\prime\prime}
for every
i
and note that
U_{i}^{\prime\prime}\cap H=U_{i}^{\prime\prime}\cap H^{\prime}=\{0\}
. Now, if all the
U_{i}^{\prime\prime}
had dimension
0
for every
i
then
U_{i}\subseteq H
for every
i
, a contradiction since
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a non-degenerate subspace design. Hence, without loss of generality, suppose that
U_{1}^{\prime\prime}\neq\{0\}
and let
u\in U_{1}^{\prime\prime}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
H^{\prime\prime}
be the hyperplane
(H\cap H^{\prime})\oplus\langle u\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
. As a consequence,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}^{\prime})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}\cap H\cap H^{\prime})<\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}\cap H^{\prime\prime})
.
Hence, we have

c=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime}_{i})<\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1}\cap H^{\prime\prime})+\sum_{i=2}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime}_{i})

\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H^{\prime\prime})=c,

a contradiction, and so
H=H^{\prime}
.
∎

By Remark
9.3
, a cutting design also defines a system and clearly, if in an equivalence class of an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system there is a cutting design (that is the set of the entries of the
t
-ple is a cutting design), then all the elements of equivalence class are cutting design as well.
Therefore, an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system with the property that the set of its entries forms a cutting design will be called a
cutting
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system
. This latter notion can be very useful when we need to enlighten the dimensions of the subspaces of a cutting design.

9.1
Motivation

We now will show how the notion of cutting design extends previously known notions of cutting blocking sets.
For a survey on blocking sets we refer to
[
21
]
.

Definition 9.5
.

Let
g,r,k
be positive integers with
r<k
. A
g
-fold
r
-blocking set
in
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
is a set
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
such that for every
(k-r)
-subspace
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
we have

\lvert\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{M}\rvert\geq g.

When
r=1
, we refer to
\mathcal{M}
as
g
-fold blocking set
. When
g=1
, we will refer to it as an
r
-blocking set
. When
r=g=1
,
\mathcal{M}
is simply a
blocking set
.

A cutting
r
-blocking set is a blocking set that, roughly speaking, captures the structure of all the
(k-r)
-subspaces; see
[
22
, Definition 3.4]
.

Definition 9.6
.

Let
r,k
be positive integers with
r<k
. An
r
-blocking set
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
is
cutting
if for every pair of
(k-r)
-subspaces
\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
we have

\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}.

Equivalently,

Proposition 9.7
.

(see
[
1
, Proposition 3.3]
)
An
r
-blocking set
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
is cutting if and only if for every
(k-r)
-space
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
we have
\langle\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{S}\rangle=\mathcal{S}
.

The
q
-analogue of the notion of a cutting blocking set is the linear cutting blocking set, introduced by Alfarano, Borello, Neri and Ravagnani in
[
2
, Definition 5.3]
.

Definition 9.8
.

An
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
system
U
is called a
linear cutting blocking set
if for any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplanes
H,H^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
we have
U\cap H\subseteq U\cap H^{\prime}
implies
H=H^{\prime}
.

If we need to underline the involved parameters of
U
, we will also say that
U
is a
linear cutting
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
blocking set
.

Remark 9.9
.

If
U
is a linear cutting
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
blocking set, then
L_{U}
is a cutting
1
-blocking set of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.

9.2
Constructions and bounds

We now list examples of cutting designs which can be obtained from the known examples of cutting blocking sets. Some of them can be obtained easily, but for others more attention needs to be paid.
However, all of them are consequences of the following result where we show that the cutting design property can be required directly on the union of the linear sets associated with the design.

Proposition 9.10
.

Consider an ordered set
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
V=V(k,q^{m})
.
Then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a cutting design if and only if
L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}}
is a cutting blocking set.

Proof.

Suppose that
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a cutting design. Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{PG}(W,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
be a hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(V,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}).
By Proposition
9.2
,
U_{1}\cap W\cup\ldots\cup U_{t}\cap W
contains an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-basis of
W
and so
L_{U_{1}\cap W}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}\cap W}
contains
k-1
points of
\mathcal{H}
such that their span is
\mathcal{H}
. Therefore,

\mathcal{H}\supseteq\langle\mathcal{H}\cap(L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}})\rangle=\langle L_{U_{1}\cap W}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}\cap W}\rangle\supseteq\mathcal{H}.

The assertion follows by Proposition
9.7
.
Conversely, suppose that
L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}}
is a cutting blocking set. Let
W
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplane of
V
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{PG}(W,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
. By Proposition
9.7
, we have that
L_{U_{1}\cap W}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}\cap W}=\mathcal{H}\cap(L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}})
contains a set of points
\{P_{1}=\langle v_{1}\rangle,\ldots,P_{k-1}=\langle v_{k-1}\rangle\}
of
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{H}=\langle P_{1},\ldots,P_{k-1}\rangle
and
v_{i}\in U_{1}\cup\ldots\cup U_{t}
for every
i
. This implies that
\langle W\cap{U_{1}},\ldots,W\cap{U_{t}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=W
, and so the assertion follows by Proposition
9.2
.
∎

Construction 9.11

(From a linear cutting blocking set)
.

If the ordered set
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
V=V(k,q^{m})
is such that at least one of the
U_{i}
’s is a linear cutting blocking set, then
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a cutting design.

The above example provides a lot of constructions of cutting designs. However, interesting constructions of cutting designs are those in which the
cutting property
is not satisfied individually by the elements of the design.
The next examples provides constructions of cutting designs not containing any linear cutting blocking sets.

Construction 9.12

(From rational normal tangent set,
[

Suppose that

For each line

9.3
Connection with minimal sum-rank metric codes

In this subsection, we extend the notion of minimal codes in the Hamming metric and the in rank metric to sum-rank metric such that this definition is consistent with that in the Hamming and in the rank metric.

Blakley and Shamir in 1979
[
17
,
85
]
introduced independently
secret sharing schemes
, that are protocols for distributing a secret among a certain number of participants. Then McEliece and Sarwate presented in
[
70
]
a more general construction based on linear codes (equipped with Hamming metric), where Reed-Solomon codes were used. In
[
69
]
, Massey relates the secret sharing protocol to
minimal codewords
: the minimal access structure in his secret sharing protocol is given by the support of the minimal codewords of a linear code
\mathcal{C}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, having first coordinate equal to
1
. A codeword
x\in\mathcal{C}
is said
minimal
if, for every
y\in\mathcal{C}
, such that
\supp(y)\subseteq\supp(x)
we have that
y=\alpha x
for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. However, finding the minimal codewords of a general linear code is a difficult task. For this reason, a special class of codes has been introduced: a linear code is said to be
minimal
if all its nonzero codewords are minimal. The analogue in the rank metric has been recently introduced by Alfarano, Borello, Neri and Ravagnani in
[
2
]
.
In
[
1
,
58
,
88
]
, minimal codes in the Hamming metric are geometrically characterized as cutting blocking sets, whereas in
[
2
]
minimal codes in the rank metric are geometrically characterized as linear cutting blocking sets.
In this section we will extend these connections providing a characterization of cutting designs in terms of sum-rank metric codes, which coincides with the aforementioned links when the sum-rank metric is either the Hamming or the rank metric.

9.4
Supports in the sum-rank metric

The interested reader is referred to
[
64
]
for a more detailed description of the theory of supports in the sum-rank metric.

The support of an element of
\Pi=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m_{i}\times n_{i}}
is defined as follows.

Definition 9.18
.

Let
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{t})\in\Pi
.
The
sum-rank support
of
X
is defined as the space

\supp(X)=(\mathrm{colsp}(X_{1}),\mathrm{colsp}(X_{2}),\ldots,\mathrm{colsp}(X_{t}))\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}},

where
\mathrm{colsp}(X_{i})
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
X_{i}
and
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})
.

The notion of support does not depend on the choice of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, as shown by the following result (see e.g.
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
).

Proposition 9.19
.

Let
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t}),\Lambda=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{t})
be two tuples of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-bases of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and let
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
. Then

\supp(\Gamma(x))=\supp(\Lambda(x))
.

The above definition allows us to give the following definition of support for an element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\bf n}
.

Definition 9.20
.

The
sum-rank support
of an element
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
is the tuple

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(x)=\supp(\Gamma(x)),

for any (and hence all) choice of
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t})
, where
\Gamma_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
for each
i\in[t]
.

9.5
Minimal sum-rank metric codes

In this section we propose the notion of minimal code in the sum-rank metric, which extends the ones of minimal codes in both Hamming and rank metric. We will then characterize geometrically minimal codes as those associated with cutting designs, extending the previously known connections between minimal codes in the Hamming metric and rank.

Definition 9.21
.

Let
\mathrm{C}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. A codeword
x\in\mathrm{C}
is said
minimal
if for every
y\in\mathrm{C}
such that
\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(y)\subseteq\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(x)
then
y=\alpha x
for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. We say that
\mathrm{C}
is
minimal
if all of its codewords are minimal.

The following characterization of the inclusion of the supports has been used in
[
2
]
to describe geometrically minimal rank metric codes.

Theorem 9.22
.

(see
[
2
, Theorem 5.6]
)
Let
G
be a generator matrix of a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Let
U
be the
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
system associated with
G
and
u,v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
. Then,

\supp_{n}(uG)\subseteq\supp_{n}(vG)\mbox{ if and only if }\langle u\rangle^{\perp}\cap U\supseteq\langle v\rangle^{\perp}\cap U.

The above result naturally extends in the sum-rank metric.

Theorem 9.23
.

Let
G
be a generator matrix of a non-degenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
sum-rank code. Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be the
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system associated with
G
and
u,v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
. Then,

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(uG)\subseteq\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(vG)\mbox{ if and only if }\langle u\rangle^{\perp}\cap U_{i}\supseteq\langle v\rangle^{\perp}\cap U_{i}\mbox{ for any $i\in[t]$}.

As a consequence of Theorem
9.23
, the geometric correspondence described in Section
5.2
and Definition
9.1
give us a
1
-to-
1
correspondence between classes of minimal sum-rank metric codes and classes of cutting designs.

Corollary 9.24
.

There is a
1
-to-
1
correspondence between classes of minimal
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
codes and classes of cutting
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
systems.

When the sum-rank metric corresponds to the Hamming or to the rank metric, the above corollary coincides with
[
1
, Theorem 3.4]
(see also
[
88
]
) and
[
2
, Corollary 5.7]
, respectively.
Therefore, all the examples of cutting designs given in Section
9.2
yield constructions of minimal sum-rank metric codes.

Finally, we observe that, as in the Hamming and in the rank metrics, all the one-weight sum-rank metric codes are minimal, see e.g.
[
2
]
.

Proposition 9.25
.

Let
\mathrm{C}
be a non-degenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. If all the codewords of
\mathrm{C}
have the same sum-rank metric weight then
\mathrm{C}
is a minimal sum-rank metric code.

Proof.

Let
G
be a generator matrix of
\mathrm{C}
and let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system associated with
G
. Since all the codewords of
\mathrm{C}
have the same sum-rank metric weight, by Theorem
5.5
, we have that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)
is constant for every hyperplane
H
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Theorem
9.4
can now be used to obtain the assertion.
∎

9.6
Minimal sum-rank metric codes in the Hamming metric

We first describe the connection between sum-rank metric codes and Hamming metric code in
[

(see also
[

For a collection of multisets
({\mathcal{M}}_{1},\mathrm{m}_{1}),\ldots,({\mathcal{M}}_{t},\mathrm{m}_{t})
of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})

as

\biguplus_{i=1}^{t}({\mathcal{M}}_{i},\mathrm{m}_{i})\coloneqq({\mathcal{M}},\mathrm{m}),

where
{\mathcal{M}}={\mathcal{M}}_{1}\cup\ldots\cup{\mathcal{M}}_{t}
, and
\mathrm{m}(P)=\mathrm{m}_{1}(P)+\ldots+\mathrm{m}_{t}(P)
for every
P\in\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.
To every
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, it is possible to associate the multiset
(L_{U},\mathrm{m}_{U})
, where
L_{U}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set defined by
U
and

\mathrm{m}_{U}(\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\coloneqq\frac{q^{w_{L_{U}}(\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})}-1}{q-1}.

By (
2
), this means that the multiset
(L_{U},\mathrm{m}_{U})
of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
has cardinality (counted with multiplicity)
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
. Consider now an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
and define the multiset

\mathrm{Ext}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})\coloneqq\biguplus\limits_{i=1}^{t}(L_{U_{i}},\mathrm{m}_{U_{i}}).

Then
\mathrm{Ext}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a projective
[\frac{q^{n_{1}}+\ldots+q^{n_{t}}-t}{q-1},k]_{q^{m}}
system.

Hence, we can give the following definition.

Definition 9.26
.

Let
\mathrm{C}
be a non-degenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be a system associated with
\mathrm{C}
. Any code
C\in\Psi^{H}(\mathrm{Ext}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}))
, where
\Psi^{H}
is defined as in Section
4.4
, is called an
associated Hamming-metric code
with
\mathrm{C}
.

Minimal sum-rank metric codes also defines minimal codes in the Hamming metric via the codes in Definition
9.26
.

Corollary 9.27
.

Let
\mathrm{C}
be a non-degenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Then
\mathrm{C}
is minimal if and only if any associated Hamming-metric code is minimal.

Proof.

The proof directly follows from Proposition
9.10
.
∎

10
Dimension expanders

The notion of dimension expander was introduced by Wigderson, see
[
91
]
, where he also pointed out the problem of constructing these structures. A dimension expander is a collection of
m
linear maps
\Gamma_{j}:{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell}
, where
{\mathbb{F}}
is a field, such that for any subspace
U\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
of sufficiently small dimension, the subspace
\Gamma_{1}(U)+\ldots+\Gamma_{m}(U)
has dimension significantly larger than
\dim(U)
. Their interest is related to the fact that they can be seen as the linear-algebraic analogue of expander graph and hence they play an important role in the theory of
algebraic pseudorandomness
. See
[
45
, Section 1]
for a nice overview of the problem.
Constructions in zero characteristic were then given by Lubotzky and Zelmanov in
[
59
]
and by Harrow in
[
51
]
, but very recently some constructions were given when the characteristic is positive by Guruswami, Resch and Xing in
[
45
]
.

The definition over finite fields is the following.

Definition 10.1
.

Let
\ell,m\geq 1
be integers,
\eta>0
and
\zeta>1
. Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{m}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ell}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ell}
be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear maps. The collection
\{\Gamma_{j}\colon j\in[m]\}
forms an
(\eta,\zeta)
-
dimension expander
if for all subpace
U\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ell}
of dimension at most
\eta\ell
,

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma_{j}(U)\right)\geq\zeta\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U).

The
degree
of the dimension expander is
m
.
If
\zeta=\Omega(m)
then the dimension expander is said to be
degree-proportional
.

In
[
45
]
, the authors ably reduce the problem of constructing dimension expanders (with some constrains on the parameters) to the construction of subspace designs.
We will now describe the reduction and then we will use the
s
-designs to get interesting and new examples of dimension expanders.

Suppose
\ell=mk
. Let
U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}
, with
t\mid\ell
and
t\leq m
be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{\ell}}
with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=\ell/t
. Consider

\mathcal{D}=\left\{f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}f_{i}x^{q^{i}}\colon f_{i}\in U_{i+1},i\in\{0,\ldots,t-1\}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{m,q}.

Since
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{\ell}}\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ell}\cong\mathcal{D}
as
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector spaces and the maps of the dimension expanders are from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ell}
in itself, we can identify the domain of the maps of a dimension expander with
\mathcal{D}
and the image space with
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{\ell}}
. Let
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m})
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Define

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\Gamma_{j}:&\mathcal{D}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{\ell}}\\
&f(x)&\longmapsto&f(\beta_{j}),\end{array}

(27)

for
j\in[m]
.

In
[
45
]
the authors show that when
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is a subspace design with certain parameters, then the construction described above of the maps
\{\Gamma_{j}\colon j\in[m]\}
gives a dimension expander for which the parameters depend on the subspace design
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
.

Theorem 10.2
.

(see
[
45
, Theorem 3.4]
)
Let
\ell=mk
with
m
and
k
positive integers. Let
t
be a positive integer such that
t\leq m
.
Consider an ordered set
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap S)\leq As

for any
s
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
S
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, for all
s\leq\mu\ell
, for some
0<\mu<1/m
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=\ell/t
, for
i\in[t]
. Then the maps
\{\Gamma_{j}\colon j\in[m]\}
defined as in (
27
) form a
(\mu A,\frac{m-t+1}{A})
-dimension expander.
Moreover, if the subspaces
U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}
are explicit, then the dimension expander is explicit.

Remark 10.3
.

In the above result by explicit subspaces we mean that there is a polynomial time algorithm in
m
and
k
which gives a basis for each of those subspaces.

Using the construction of
s
-designs provided in Theorem
4.8
, we obtain the following explicit dimension expanders.

Theorem 10.4
.

Let
m\geq q-1
. Suppose that
q-1
divides
k
. Then there exists an explicit
\left(\frac{q-2}{mk},m-q+2\right)
-dimension expander in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{mk}
of degree
m
.

Proof.

Choosing
s=q-2
,
t=q-1
, Theorem
4.8
ensures the existence of an (explicit)
s
-design
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
, with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i})=\frac{mk}{q-1}
. Let
\mu=\frac{q-2}{mk}
. Note that
\mu<1/m
, since
q-1\mid k
and
k>q-2
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.2
,
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
is an
s^{\prime}
-design for each
s^{\prime}\leq\mu mk=q-2
. Hence by Theorem
10.2
the assertion follows. Since the subspace design of Theorem
4.8
is explicit, then the constructed dimension expander is explicit as well.
∎

Special instances of the above result give examples of degree-proportional dimension exapanders.

Corollary 10.5
.

Let
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{q-2}{q-1}\right)
such that
1/\delta\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
m=\frac{q-2}{\delta}
and
k=\frac{q-1}{\delta}
. Then there exists an explicit construction of a
(\eta,\zeta)
-dimension expander in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{mk}
of degree
m
, where
\eta=\Omega\left(\frac{\delta m}{m}\right)
and
\zeta=\Omega((1-\delta)m)
. In particular, it is also degree-proportional.

Proof.

By choosing
m=\frac{q-2}{\delta}
and
k=\frac{q-1}{\delta}
in Theorem
10.4
, we obtain an explicit

\left(\frac{q-2}{mk},m-q+2\right)
-dimension expander in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{mk}
of degree
m
. So

\eta=\frac{q-2}{mk}=\frac{\delta(q-2)}{(q-1)m}=\Omega\left(\frac{\delta}{m}\right)

and

\zeta=m-q+2=\frac{q-2}{\delta}-q+2=(1-\delta)m,

and hence the assertion follows.
∎

Remark 10.6
.

In
[
45
, Theorem 5.1]
the authors construct degree-proportional dimension expanders in which
q\geq(mk)^{\delta}
for some
\delta>0
, whereas the construction in Corollary
10.5
has no restrictions on
q
.
Furthermore, in
[
45
, Theorem 5.1]
further assumptions on the parameters are needed which in Corollary
10.5
are not required.

11
Conclusions and Open problems

Motivated by their applications in list decoding for rank metric codes and Hamming metric codes, and to dimension expanders, in this paper we provided bounds and constructions of subspace designs in
V(k,q^{m})
.
Moreover, we pointed out which subspace designs correspond to maximum sum-rank metric codes, i.e. the optimal subspace designs.
We then generalized the notion of
s
-scattered subspaces to the subspace designs, introducing the
s
-designs.
Subsequently, we proved that for
s=1
and
s=k-1
they correspond to the optimal subspace designs.
We paid particular attention to the case
s=1
for which we provided examples and characterizations.
Then we also investigated cutting designs and we showed their connection with minimal sum-rank metric codes.

There are some problems that still remain open. We list some of them.

•

In Corollary
4.11
, we proved that a maximum
s
-design in
V(k,q^{m})
is also a
(k-1,t(mk/(s+1)-m+s))_{q}
subspace design. Nevertheless, when
s=1
we proved that a maximum
1
-design is a
(k-1,tmk/2-tm+1))_{q}
-subspace design, improving what is proved in Corollary
4.11
. This also suggests that a maximum
s
-design in
V(k,q^{m})
is also a
(k-1,t(mk/(s+1)-m)+s)_{q}
-subspace design, but the double counting arguments become too difficult to deal with.

•

Construct maximum
1
-designs in the case where
t>1
,
k
is odd and
m\neq 2
is even.

•

Is it true that maximum
h
-designs in
V(k,q^{m})
are optimal subspace designs also when
2\leq h\leq k-2
?

•

In Corollary
4.14
we showed that maximum
1
-designs yield two intersection sets with respect to hyperplanes. In particular, this means that from a maximum
1
-design, we can define linear codes equipped with the Hamming metric with two nonzero weights (known also as
two-weight codes
) and we can consider the associated strongly regular graph; see Section
4.4
. However, we do not know whether or not the parameters of these codes and of these graphs are new, except for the union of Baer subgeometries and the case in which the design has one element, see
[
6
]
and
[
20
]
. A first check with the available databases of two weight codes/strongly regular graphs (see
[
28
,
24
]
) seems to suggest that the parameters are new.

•

Classification of optimal subspace designs using known classification results on scattered spaces. This would clearly imply a classification for MSRD codes.

•

Is it possible to give a geometric description of the Delsarte dual operation on subspace designs similarly to the Delsarte duality introduced in
[
31
, Section 3]
?

•

Obtain new (non)-existence results of Cameron-Liebler sets by making use of the connection established in Theorem
8.8
, the techniques developed in Sections
8.1
,
8.2
,
8.3
and the Singleton bound for sum-rank metric codes.

•

Show a non-trivial sum-rank metric analogue of the Ashikhmin-Barg condition
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
for the minimality of a sum-rank metric code.

•

An ordered set
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
with the property that there exists a positive integer
c
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap H)=c
for every hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, corresponds to one-weight rank metric codes, see
[
77
]
and Proposition
9.25
. As proved in Theorem
9.4
they also define a cutting design. It would be interesting to prove that if
L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}}
cover the entire space or to find examples of cutting designs
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
such that
L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}}\neq\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.

Hyperbolicity for large automorphism groups of projective surfaces
Serge Cantat, Romain Dujardin

Abstract
. We study the hyperbolicity properties of the action of a non-elementary automorphism group on a compact complex surface, with an emphasis on K3 and Enriques surfaces. A first result is that when such a group contains parabolic elements, Zariski diffuse invariant measures automatically have non-zero Lyapunov exponents. In combination with our previous work, this leads to simple criteria for a uniform expansion property on the whole surface, for groups with and without parabolic elements. This, in turn, has strong consequences on the dynamics: description of orbit closures, equidistribution, ergodicity properties, etc. Along the way, we provide a reference discussion on uniform expansion of non-linear discrete group actions on compact (real) manifolds and the construction of Margulis functions under optimal moment conditions.

1.1.
Wehler examples

To understand the motivation behind our general results,

\mathcal{W}

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

This family

Slightly abusing notation, we respectively denote by

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a dense Zariski open subset

, there exists

\Gamma
-invariant finite set

\Gamma
-invariant totally real analytic surface

(c)

In this statement both

and

may be empty, depending on

For instance,
[

says that

is empty for a dense set of Wehler surfaces

is defined over

and

.

If we restrict to real parameters in
\mathcal{W}
, we
also have a fairly complete understanding
of the asymptotic distribution of random orbits.
By this we mean the following.
Let
\nu
be the probability measure on
\Gamma
defined by

\nu=\frac{1}{3}(\delta_{\sigma_{1}}+\delta_{\sigma_{2}}+\delta_{\sigma_{3}})
. For any

, and for any sequence

(g_{n}\cdots g_{0}(x))_{n\geq 0}
.
Let

be a union of connected components of

. We say that these random trajectories, starting at

in

if for
\nu^{\mathbf{N}}
-almost every

, the empirical measures

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a dense Zariski open subset
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{exp}}(\mathbf{R})\subset\mathcal{W}_{0}(\mathbf{R})

such that for every
X\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{exp}}(\mathbf{R})
, there exists
a
\Gamma
-invariant finite set

such that for every
x\in X(\mathbf{R})

(a)

either

(b)

An interesting point in Theorems
1.1
and

is that their

. Let us explain how these theorems fall within
the progression of
[
21
,
19
,

and what the last missing ingredient was until the present paper.

First, the existence of the maximal finite invariant set

follows from
[

, that is automorphisms whose

is virtually unipotent and

Now, the scheme of proof of Theorem
1.2
is as follows.
The random walk on
\Gamma
induced by
\nu
gives rise to a random dynamical system on
X

We refer to
[

13

for general references on this topic,
and to Sections 4 and 7 of

for our holomorphic context.
In particular, we shall use the notions of stationary and invariant measures

Fix

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{g_{k}\cdots g_{0}(x)}

-stationary measure. We

that every

-stationary measure is

-invariant, and

[

]
we showed that any invariant measure is either supported on

of

. Therefore, any cluster value of

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{g_{k}\cdots g_{0}(x)}
is a convex combination of point masses on

and

{\sf{vol}}_{X^{\prime}(\mathbf{R})}
. Thus the last step is to show that if
\Gamma(x)
is infinite, the limiting empirical

.

For Theorem

the situation is similar:
most of the work was done in
[
20
, §8]

that the accumulation locus of an infinite orbit could be contained in a finite invariant set.

These difficulties were already addressed for homogeneous random dynamical systems in
[

]
and in the context of non-linear actions on real
surfaces in
[

,
23
]
.
The key is to show that if

set

of Theorem

(resp.

of Theorem

), the maximal finite invariant set

is

for the random dynamics. Since we do not know the set

, nor its cardinality (examples of Wehler surfaces with large finite invariant sets were recently constructed

[

), we make a large detour and prove a uniform

Establishing this property relies on ergodic-theoretic arguments, the first of which is an
automatic hyperbolicity property that we describe in the next paragraph.

1.2.
Hyperbolicity of invariant measures

It is a fundamental (and widely open) problem in conservative dynamics to show the typicality of non-zero Lyapunov exponents
on a set of positive Lebesgue measure. In deterministic dynamics, a recent breakthrough is the work of
Berger and Turaev
[
7
]
. Adding some randomness makes such a hyperbolicity result easier to obtain:
see
[
9
]
for random perturbation of the standard map, and

[
3
,
45
]

for random conservative diffeomorphisms on closed real surfaces. The results of Barrientos and Malicet
[
3
]
and of
Obata and Poletti
[
45
]
are perturbative in nature, so they do not give explicit examples.
In our context, the rigidity properties of holomorphic diffeomorphisms will
enable us to exhibit explicit criteria ensuring such a non-uniform hyperbolicity.

In
[
20
]
we have classified
invariant measures for non-elementary groups containing parabolic elements. We say
that a measure
\mu
on
X
is

Zariski diffuse

if it gives zero mass to proper Zariski closed subsets. If
\mu
is
\Gamma
-invariant and ergodic for some

\Gamma\subset\mathsf{Aut}(X)
, this is equivalent to its support
\supp(\mu)

being Zariski dense. Roughly speaking, our classification of invariant measures says that every Zariski diffuse, ergodic, invariant probability measure is
given by an analytic
4
-form on
X
or by an analytic
2
-form on some invariant, real analytic subset
Y\subset X
of dimension
2
.
Here we proceed to a finer study of the dynamical properties of these invariant measures. For this, we
fix a probability measure
\nu
on
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
, satisfying the moment condition

(M)

\int\left(\log{\left\|f\right\|}_{C^{1}(X)}+\log{\left\|f^{-1}\right\|}_{C^{1}(X)}\right)\,d\nu(f)<+\infty,

and view any invariant measure
\mu

as a
\nu
-stationary measure, that is,

\int f_{\varstar}\mu\,d\nu(f)=\mu
. Then by (
M
),
the Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
are well defined.
We denote by
\Gamma_{\nu}\subset\mathsf{Aut}(X)

the closed subgroup generated by
\supp(\nu)
(
1
1

1

Note that
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
is discrete unless

X
is a torus, see
[
21
, §3]

so in most cases
\Gamma_{\nu}=\langle\supp(\nu)\rangle
.
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a compact complex surface and

\Gamma
be a non-elementary subgroup of
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
containing parabolic elements.
Let
\mu
be a Zariski diffuse ergodic
\Gamma
-invariant probability measure on
X
.
Let
\nu
be any probability measure on
\mathsf{Aut}(X)

satisfying
\Gamma_{\nu}=\Gamma
and the moment condition (
M
).

Then, viewed as a
\nu
-stationary measure,
\mu
is hyperbolic and its fiber entropy
h_{\mu}(X,\nu)
is positive.

A variant of this result will also be obtained when
\Gamma_{\nu}
contains a Kummer example
instead of a parabolic element (see Theorem
7.4
).

Example 1.4
.

When
X
is a torus or a K3 surface, the canonical bundle
K_{X}
is trivial and, up to multiplication by a complex number of modulus
1
, there is
a unique section
\Omega_{X}
of
K_{X}
that satisfies
\int_{X}\Omega_{X}\wedge\overline{\Omega_{X}}=1
. The volume form
{\sf{vol}}_{X}:=\Omega_{X}\wedge\overline{\Omega_{X}}

is
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
-invariant. Likewise, every Enriques surface
S
inherits such an invariant volume form
{\sf{vol}}_{S}
from its universal cover
X
(a
2
-to-
1
cover by a K3 surface).
Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
,
{\sf{vol}}_{X}
is
\Gamma
-ergodic,
thus we conclude that
it is hyperbolic
. Other examples are provided by some rational surfaces (see the discussion on Coble surfaces in
[
18
]
).

In these situations the 2-form
\Omega_{X}
also induces a natural measure
{\sf{vol}}_{Y}
on any totally real surface
Y\subset X
(see
[
20
, Rmk 2.3]
). For instance, if
X
is projective and defined over
\mathbf{R}
,
\Gamma
is contained in
\mathsf{Aut}(X_{\mathbf{R}})
, and
Y
is a
\Gamma
-invariant connected component of
X(\mathbf{R})
, Theorem
1.3
asserts that
{\sf{vol}}_{Y}
is hyperbolic.

1.3.
Uniform expansion

Fix a riemannian metric on
X
. We say that the measure
\nu
on
\mathsf{Aut}(X)

is
uniformly expanding
if there exists
c>0
and an integer
n_{0}
such that for
every

x\in X
and
every

v\in T_{x}X\setminus\{0\}
,

(1.1)

\int_{\mathsf{Aut}(X)}\log\frac{{\left\|D_{x}f(v)\right\|}}{{\left\|v\right\|}}d\nu^{(n_{0})}(f)\geq c;

here
\nu^{(n)}
denotes the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
convolution power of
\nu
.
This notion is taken from

[
23
,
26
,
43
,
49
]
(see also
[
30
,
46
]
for the linear context) and has a number
of strong ergodic and topological consequences on the action of
\Gamma_{\nu}
.
So far, uniform expansion has been verified only in the context of homogeneous dynamics, or for certain perturbative situations, or with the help of numerical methods.
The geometric analysis of stationary measures developed in
[
21
]
together with Theorem
1.3

will be used to obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a compact complex surface which is not rational.
Let
\nu
be a probability measure on
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
. Assume that:
(i)
\nu
satisfies the moment condition (
M
) and (ii) the group

\Gamma=\Gamma_{\nu}
is non-elementary and contains parabolic elements.

Then
\nu
is uniformly expanding if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(1)

every finite
\Gamma
-orbit is uniformly expanding;

(2)

there is no
\Gamma
-invariant algebraic curve.

Here, by definition,
a finite orbit
F
of
\Gamma
is said uniformly expanding if condition (
1.1
)
holds for every
x\in F
. This is the repulsion property alluded to at the end of §
1.1
.

Checking condition (2) is not hard in practice and boils down to cohomological computations (see §
6.3
). However, in a non-linear setting,
Condition (1) is more delicate to verify. On the positive side,
if
X
is not a torus and condition (2) holds, then by
[
19
]

there are only finitely many finite
\Gamma
-orbits. Moreover, if
\nu
is symmetric and satisfies a slightly stronger moment condition (
M+
), Theorem
8.14
provides a
checkable necessary and sufficient condition for a given finite

\Gamma
-orbit to be uniformly expanding:
it is equivalent to the tangent action of
\Gamma
being proximal and strongly irreducible. It
follows that when
\nu
is symmetric (and
X
is not rational)

the uniform expansion property
depends only on
\Gamma
, and not on
\nu
(Corollary
8.15
).

On the negative side, so far there is no a priori bound on the number of finite orbits. Nevertheless, we show in Theorem
9.1
that under the assumptions of
Theorem
1.5
, if there is no invariant algebraic curve,

there is a computable number
N=N(X,\Gamma)

such that any finite orbit of length greater than
N
is uniformly expanding
(see §
9.1
for details on what we mean by computable here).
Consequently there is a simple algorithm for checking uniform expansion: verify that finite orbits of length at most
N
have non-elementary tangent action.

An immediate consequence of Theorem
1.5
is the following.

Corollary 1.6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.5
, if there is no proper algebraic
\Gamma_{\nu}
-invariant subset, then

\nu
is uniformly expanding.

Since uniform expansion is an open property in the
C^{1}
topology, and since by
[
19
, Thm A]

a dense set of Wehler examples has no proper Zariski closed invariant set, this implies
that uniform expansion is satisfied on an open and dense set of Wehler examples.
To deduce Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we must further
show that this subset is open for the Zariski topology:
for this, we use the effective result described above, and prove
that
in the Wehler family, the integer
N(X,\Gamma)
is uniformly bounded

(here
\Gamma=\left\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\right\rangle
; see Theorem
9.3
and Proposition
9.7
).
In particular the equations defining
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{exp}}
and
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{exp}}(\mathbf{R})

could in principle be written down explicitly.

1.4.
Ergodicity

Given an action of a general non-elementary group
\Gamma
on a compact complex surface
X
,
one may ask the following two basic questions:
does there exist a dense orbit? is the action ergodic with respect to Lebesgue measure?
(The latter makes sense even when there is no invariant volume form.)
If
\Gamma
contains a parabolic element, by
[
20
]
the answer to both questions is ‘yes’,
but without parabolic elements, the answer is unknown.
A natural obstruction to the existence of
a dense orbit could be the presence of a non-trivial Fatou
component for
\Gamma
. No example of such a Fatou component is known so far;
note that examples do exist for algebraic actions on
affine
surfaces
(see
[
15
,
47
]
).

As a matter of fact, the failure of ergodicity is
associated to a lack of expansion: indeed a
theorem of Dolgopyat and Krikorian
[
26
]
asserts
that a conservative uniformly expanding action on a (real) surface must be ergodic. It is not difficult
to extend their argument to the complex setting (see Theorem
10.2
).
In Theorem
8.9
we state
a general criterion (i.e. without parabolic elements) for uniform expansion
which shows that under the conditions (1) and (2) of
Theorem
1.5
,
the failure of uniform expansion, is due to the existence of a

\Gamma
-invariant measure with exceptional properties (see Theorems
8.9
and
A.1
).
We expect it to be an extremely rare phenomenon.
Incidentally,
this shows that the question of ergodicity for general non-elementary groups (i.e. without parabolic elements)
ultimately boils down to the
classification of
\Gamma
-invariant measures.

Another interesting consequence of our results, together with
[
26
]
, is that
a generic real Wehler example is
stably ergodic
among
C^{2}
volume preserving actions, that is,
if
X
belongs to the open set
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{exp}}(\mathbf{R})

of Theorem
1.2
and

\sigma^{\prime}_{1}
,
\sigma^{\prime}_{2}
,
\sigma^{\prime}_{3}
are
C^{2}

volume preserving diffeomorphisms sufficiently close to
of
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
,
\sigma_{3}
in the
C^{1}
topology, then

\Gamma^{\prime}:=\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\sigma^{\prime}_{2},\sigma^{\prime}_{3}\rangle
is ergodic for
{\sf{vol}}_{X(\mathbf{R})}
.

In an opposite direction, the examples from
[
20
, §9]
of
\mathsf{Aut}(X_{\mathbf{R}})
-invariant
domains with boundary in
X(\mathbf{R})
(which admit an invariant curve)
provide explicit counterexamples to uniform expansion.

1.5.
Organization of the paper

The first part of this paper (Sections
2
to
5
) is
devoted to a general study of the notion of uniform expansion on compact (real) manifolds. Much of this material is inspired from other sources; the novelty here is that we strive for
optimal moment conditions.
In Section
2
we give several equivalent definitions of uniform expansion:
this is inspired by Liu
[
42
]
and Chung
[
23
]
.
In Section
3
we show that uniform expansion is preserved
when restricting to a finite index
subgroup or taking a finite extension (Proposition
3.3
); this
is useful when dealing with invariant sets made of finitely many connected components. Section
4
deals with the construction of

Margulis functions
. In a nutshell, a Margulis function
near a finite uniformly expanding invariant set
F
is a function

u:M\setminus F\to\mathbf{R}_{+}
that tends to infinity at
F
and decreases on average along orbits.
The existence of such a function
guarantees that empirical measures of random orbits do not accumulate at
F
. These functions have played an
important role in random dynamics since the work of Eskin and Margulis
[
31
]
.
Here, thanks to the work by Bénard and De Saxcé
[
4
]
, we construct such Margulis functions under optimal moment conditions (Theorem
4.1
);
note that the usual average
decay property
\int u(f(x))d\nu(f)\leq au(x)+b
,
a<1
, is then replaced by
\int u(f(x))d\nu(f)\leq u(x)-\gamma
,
\gamma>0
. This repulsion property does not hold if
F
is an invariant submanifold (see Example
4.6
). However in the holomorphic context, Margulis functions
can be constructed for invariant
totally real manifolds of maximal dimension (Theorem
4.5
): a typical situation
is that of
X(\mathbf{R})\subset X
for real projective manifolds.
In Section
5
, we elaborate on an ergodic-theoretic criterion for uniform expansion borrowed
from
[
23
]
.

In the second part of the paper (Sections
6
to
10
), we
consider groups of automorphisms of projective surfaces. Theorem
1.3

is established in Section
7
.
In Section
8
we prove a general version of
Theorem
1.5
and
study uniform expansion along periodic orbits; this makes essential use of the results of the first part.
The focus in §
9.1
is on finding
algorithmically checkable conditions for uniform expansion along finite orbits (Theorem
9.1
);
this leads to a precise description of the locus of uniform expansion in the Wehler family (Theorem
9.3
).
In §
9.2
, we construct uniformly expanding actions by perturbing Kummer examples in the Wehler family;
in particular this work for “thin” subgroups of
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
containing no parabolic element.
In Section
10
we study orbit closures and equidistribution by proving
general versions of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
;
we also explain the adaptation to the complex setting of the ergodicity
theorem of Dolgopyat and Krikorian
[
26
]
.

The paper ends with an appendix on the rigidity of zero entropy measures.

Part I
Uniform expansion for discrete group actions on manifolds

2.
Generalities

In this section,
M
denotes a compact manifold and
\nu

is a probability measure on the group
{\mathsf{Diff}}^{1}(M)

of
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms of
M
. We fix a Riemannian metric on
M
. We denote by
{\left\|\cdot\right\|}

the norm induced by the metric on the tangent bundle
TM
, and by
T^{1}M
the unit tangent bundle.

2.1.
Moment conditions

If
f
is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism of
M
, we denote by
f_{\varstar}
its action on
TM
. Note that
if
v\in TM
is a tangent vector based at
x
(that is,
v\in T_{x}M
), then

f_{\varstar}v=D_{x}f(v)
is based at
f(x)
. By definition,

4.
Margulis functions

In this section we develop some tools for the proof of the equidistribution
Theorem
1.2
.
Under appropriate assumptions, we show that the measures

\nu^{n}\ast\delta_{x}
and
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{f^{k}_{\omega}(x)}

do not cluster at a
\Gamma
-periodic orbit, except when
\Gamma(x)
is itself finite.
The basic tool is the construction of a proper function, defined on the complement of such a periodic orbit,
which essentially decreases along random trajectories. After
[
31
]
it is often
referred to as a “Margulis function”, even if this strategy has a long history in the Markov chain literature (see

[
44
]
). Our presentation is greatly influenced by
[
5
]
and

[
4
]
.

4.1.
A general recurrence criterion

For concreteness, instead of general Markov chains,
we consider the setting of group actions.

Theorem 4.1

(Bénard-De Saxcé
[
4
]
)
.

Let
U

be a locally compact topological space. Let
\Gamma
be a group of homeomorphisms of
U
, and
\nu
be a probability measure on
\Gamma
.
Assume that there exists a function
u:U\to\mathbf{R}_{+}
satisfying the assumptions:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\exists A>0,\ \exists\gamma>0,\ \forall x\in U,\ u(x)\geq A\Rightarrow\int u(f(x))d\nu(f)\leq u(x)-\gamma

(4.2)

\displaystyle\exists B>0,\exists\eta>0,\forall x\in U,\ \int\left|u(f(x))-u(x)\right|^{1+\eta}d\nu(f)\leq B.

Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
R>0
such that for all
x
in
U
,

(1)

there exists
n_{x}\geq 0
, such that
(\nu^{n}\ast\delta_{x})(\left\{u\geq R\right\})\leq\varepsilon
for all
n\geq n_{x}
;

(2)

for
\nu^{\mathbf{N}}
-almost every
\omega
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\#\left\{k\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}\;;\;u\left(f^{k}_{\omega}(x)\right)\geq R\right\}\leq\varepsilon.

Furthermore the integer
n_{x}
in (1) depends only on
u(x)
.

For the proof, see Proposition 1.2 in
[
4
]
, and the comments following it. More
precisely, we refer to
[
4
, Prop. 2.5]
for the conclusion (1),
including the uniformity statement on
n_{x}
, and to
[
4
, Prop. 2.7]
for (2).
Even if this is not required in the proof, the function
u
has to be understood as a proper function on

U
, in which case the conclusions (1) and (2) correspond to a “non-escape of mass” property.

The original contraction property for the Margulis function
u
in

[
31
,
5
]

is

(4.3)

\exists 0<a<1,\ \exists b>0,\ \forall x\in U,\ \int u(f(x))d\nu(f)\leq au(x)+b

instead of (
4.1
). One easily checks that if
u
satisfies (
4.1
) and the following strong integrability property

(4.4)

\exists B>0,\forall x\in U,\ \int\exp\left(\left|u(f(x))-u(x)\right|\right)d\nu(f)\leq B,

then
e^{\delta u}

satisfies (
4.3
) for small
\delta>0
. Under this assumption, Theorem
4.1
was established in

[
5
]
.

4.2.
Finite orbits of
C^{2}
actions

Let
\nu
be a probability measure on the group of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms of a compact Riemannian
manifold
M
of dimension
d
.
As in §
2.1
we consider the moment conditions

(M
2,p
)

\displaystyle\int\left(\log{\left\|f\right\|}_{C^{2}}+\log{\left\|f^{-1}\right\|}_{C^{2}}\right)^{p}\,d\nu(f)<+\infty,

(M
2,+
)

\displaystyle\exists p>1,\ \mathrm{(M}_{2,p}\mathrm{)}\text{ holds},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad

Remark 4.2
.

For a holomorphic action on a compact
complex manifold, these conditions are equivalent to their respective

C^{1}
analogues (

) and (
M+
), because a uniform control on the first derivatives provides a uniform control of higher derivatives as well.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms of a compact Riemannian
manifold
M
, and
\nu
be a measure on

\Gamma
satisfying the moment condition (M
2,+
).
Let
F
be a finite orbit of
\Gamma
such that
\nu
is uniformly expanding on
F
.
Then for every
x\in M\setminus F
, for every
\varepsilon>0

there exists a compact set
K\Subset M\setminus F
such that:

(1)

(\nu^{n}\ast\delta_{x})(K)\geq 1-\varepsilon
for
n\geq n_{x}
, and

(2)

for
\nu^{\mathbf{N}}
-almost every
\omega
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\#\left\{k\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\},\ f^{k}_{\omega}(x)\in K\right\}\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Furthermore the integer
n_{x}
in (1) is locally uniform in
M\setminus F
.

This result seems to be new: it appears under stronger (exponential) moment assumptions
in e.g.
[
42
,
23
]
. Note that such a result is not expected to hold under the
first moment condition (M
2,1
), as explained in
Examples 1 and 2 of Section 2 in
[
4
]
.

Proof.

First, the proof of Proposition 3.3 in
[
4
]
shows that if
the conclusions (1) and (2) hold for
\nu^{(n_{0})}
, then they hold for
\nu
. So we can replace
\nu
by
\nu^{(n_{0})}
and hence assume that the uniform expansion property (
1.1
) holds (on
F
) for
n_{0}=1
.

Let
d(\cdot,\cdot)
be the Riemannian distance on
M
. According to Theorem
4.1
, we only need to show that

u\colon x\mapsto-\log d(x,F)
is a
proper function

satisfying Properties (
4.1
)
and (
4.2
).

Preliminaries
.– We set

N(f)={\left\|f\right\|}_{C^{2}}+{\left\|f^{-1}\right\|}_{C^{2}}
and note that
N(f)\geq\mathrm{Lip}(f)+\mathrm{Lip}(f^{-1})
for every

.
In particular, for every
x\in X

(4.5)

\frac{1}{N(f)}\leq\frac{d(f(x),F)}{d(x,F)}\leq N(f).

For
R>0
, set
\Gamma(R)=\{f\in\Gamma\;;\;N(f)\leq R\}
.
We choose
\eta>0
such that the moment condition (

) is satisfied with

. Then,

(4.6)

I_{\eta}:=\int_{\Gamma}\left(\log(N(f))\right)^{1+\eta}d\nu(f)

is a finite positive number. In what follows, we choose
R>1
such that

(4.7)

\frac{2I_{\eta}}{(\log(R))^{\eta}}<\frac{c}{4}

where
c
is the expansion factor in Equation (
1.1
) (along the finite orbit
F
).

Take
s>0
such that

–

s
is smaller than the injectivity radius of
M
at
y
, for every
y\in F
;

–

the balls
B(y;s)
, for
y
in
F
, are pairwise disjoint;

–

C_{0}R^{2}s<c/4
, where

is the expansion factor as above, and

is the constant appearing
below in the Taylor expansion (Equation (
4.9
)).

Then, define
V
and

by

(4.8)

V=\bigcup_{y\in F}B(y;s),\quad V^{\prime}=\bigcup_{y\in F}B(y;s/R).

By (

) we have

for every

.

If
x
belongs to
V
, we denote by

the unique point of
F
at distance
\leq s
from
x
, and we denote by

the unique vector in

such that
\exp_{\pi(x)}(w_{x})=x
and
{\left\|w_{x}\right\|}=d(x,\pi(x))
.

First estimate
.– For
f
in
\Gamma(R)
and

, Taylor’s second order formula yields

(4.9)

\left|d(f(x),f(\pi(x)))-{\left\|f_{\varstar}(w_{x})\right\|}\right|\leq C_{0}N(f)d(x,\pi(x))^{2},

for some uniform constant

, that does not depend on

. This gives

(4.10)

\left|\frac{d(f(x),F)}{d(x,F)}-\frac{{\left\|f_{\varstar}(w_{x})\right\|}}{{\left\|w_{x}\right\|}}\right|\leq C_{0}N(f)d(x,F).

Now, using the Lipschitz estimate (
4.5
) and the fact that
\left|\log(a)-\log(b)\right|\leq N\left|a-b\right|

(4.11)

\left|\log\left(\frac{d(f(x),F)}{d(x,F)}\right)-\log\left(\frac{{\left\|f_{\varstar}(w_{x})\right\|}}{{\left\|w_{x}\right\|}}\right)\right|\leq C_{0}N(f)^{2}d(x,F).

By the definition of

and the requirements on

(4.12)

\displaystyle\int_{f\in\Gamma(R)}\left|\log\left(\frac{d(f(x),F)}{d(x,F)}\right)-\log\left(\frac{{\left\|f_{\varstar}(w_{x})\right\|}}{{\left\|w_{x}\right\|}}\right)\right|d\nu(f)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}R^{2}d(x,F)\leq\frac{c}{4},

because

.

Second estimate
.– Now, for any

in
\Gamma

(4.13)

\left|\log\left(\frac{d(f(x),F)}{d(x,F)}\right)-\log\left(\frac{{\left\|f_{\varstar}(w_{x})\right\|}}{{\left\|w_{x}\right\|}}\right)\right|\leq 2\log(N(f))

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int_{f\in\Gamma(R)^{\complement}}\left|\log\left(\frac{d(f(x),F)}{d(x,F)}\right)-\log\left(\frac{{\left\|f_{\varstar}(w_{x})\right\|}}{{\left\|w_{x}\right\|}}\right)\right|d\nu(f)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\log(R)^{\eta}}I_{\eta}\leq\frac{c}{4}.

Conclusion
.– Summing the integrals over

in
\Gamma(R)
and
\Gamma(R)^{\complement}
, we obtain

(4.15)

\int_{f\in\Gamma}\left|\log\left(\frac{d(f(x),F)}{d(x,F)}\right)-\log\left(\frac{{\left\|f_{\varstar}(w_{x})\right\|}}{{\left\|w_{x}\right\|}}\right)\right|d\nu(f)\leq\frac{c}{2}.

Since

is a vector tangent to

(4.16)

\int\log\left(\frac{{\left\|f_{\varstar}(w_{x})\right\|}}{{\left\|w_{x}\right\|}}\right)d\nu(f)\geq c

and
then (

) implies that

(4.17)

\int-\log{d(f(x),F)}d\nu(f)\leq-\log(d(x,F))-c/2.

In other words,

satisfies Property (

) (with

). Property (

satisfies the assumptions of Theorem

.

Under the assumptions of Theorem

4.3.
Totally real invariant manifolds

We now consider a situation which is specific to the complex setting.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of dimension

. Let
\Gamma
be a group of holomorphic diffeomorphisms of

,
endowed with a probability measure
\nu
satisfying (

). Let
Y\subset X

be a
\Gamma
-invariant,
analytic, totally real submanifold of maximal (real) dimension

, such that

is uniformly expanding on

.
Then for any

and any

, there exists a compact subset

such that the

The result also holds if

admits finitely many singular points, under the stronger

\nu
is finitely supported.

By “uniformly expanding along

that the uniform expansion condition (

) holds in

.

Note also that this statement is specific to totally real submanifolds and holomorphic actions. In other words,
there is no analogue of Theorem

when

is replaced by an arbitrary submanifold:
see Example

below.

Proof of Theorem

when

is smooth.

We suppose

such that

defines a

) and (

))
for

. Then, as explained before,

shows that (

) and (

) are automatically satisfied with

. As in Theorem

and
the bilipschitz property; so we focus on (

For every

there exists a local chart in which

becomes

[

covering a neighborhood of

there exists an absolute constant

This argument shows that it is enough to prove uniform expansion for

Let

).
In

, write

\phi_{i}(x)=z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})

\phi_{i}(Y)=\{{\mathsf{Im}}(z)=0\}

As before let

\left|\frac{d_{U_{j}}(f(x),Y)}{d_{U_{i}}(x,Y)}-{\left\|df_{\pi(x)}(v_{x})\right\|}\right|\leq CRd_{U_{i}}(x,Y),

∎

5.
An ergodic-theoretic criterion for expansion

5.1.
Construction of stationary measures

Let
M
be a compact manifold endowed with a riemannian metric; let
T^{1}M
denote its unit tangent bundle
and
\pi\colon T^{1}M\to M
be the canonical projection. As in Section
2
,
if
f
is a diffeomorphism of
M
, we denote by
f_{\varstar}
its action on
TM
.
Let
\nu
be a probability measure on
{\mathsf{Diff}}^{1}(M)

satisfying the moment condition (
M
).
We apply a classical strategy to get the
following theorem (see e.g.
[
23
, Prop. 3.17]
, and
[
37

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that there exists an increasing sequence
(n_{k})\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathbf{N}}
and a
sequence of unit tangent vectors
(u_{k})\in(T^{1}M)^{\mathbf{N}}

such that

(5.1)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\int\log{\left\|f_{\varstar}u_{k}\right\|}d\nu^{(n_{k})}(f)=\chi_{0}.

Then, there exists a real number
\chi\geq\chi_{0}
, an ergodic
\nu
-stationary probability measure
\hat{\mu}
on
T^{1}M
,
and a
\nu
-almost surely invariant sub-bundle
V\subset TM
such that the top Lyapunov exponent of the projected measure
\mu:=\pi_{\varstar}\hat{\mu}
in restriction to
V
is equal to
\chi
. Likewise, there exists
a real number
\chi^{\prime}\leq\chi_{0}
that satisfies the same property for
some pair
(\hat{\mu}^{\prime},V^{\prime})
.

Note that if
\hat{\mu}
is a probability measure on
T^{1}M
that is

\nu
-stationary for the tangent action, then its projection
\mu
on
M
is
\nu
-stationary as well; and if
\hat{\mu}
is ergodic, so is
\mu
.
When
\chi>0
, one typically obtains

.

Proof (see
[
23
,
37
]
).

Consider the
sequence of measures
\hat{\mu}_{k}
on
T^{1}M
defined by

(5.2)

\hat{\mu}_{k}=\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}\nu^{(j)}\star\delta_{u_{k}}=\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}\int\frac{f_{\varstar}u_{k}}{{\left\|f_{\varstar}u_{k}\right\|}}d\nu^{(j)}(f),

where
\nu^{(j)}\star\delta_{u_{k}}
denotes the convolution for the action of
{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
on the unit tangent bundle. Since
T^{1}M
is compact and the

\hat{\mu}_{k}
are probability measures, we can extract a subsequence (still denoted by
\hat{\mu}_{k}
for simplicity) that converges weakly towards a
probability measure
\hat{\mu}_{\infty}
on

. By construction, this measure is
\nu
-stationary.

The function
{\mathrm{Dil}}(f,u):=\log{\left\|f_{\varstar}u\right\|}
is continuous on

{\mathsf{Diff}}^{1}(M)\times T^{1}M
. For

the chain rule gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int\log{\left\|(f^{n}_{\omega})_{\varstar}u\right\|}d\nu^{\mathbf{N}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int{\mathrm{Dil}}\left(f_{j},\frac{(f^{j}_{\omega})_{\varstar}u}{{\left\|(f^{j}_{\omega})_{\varstar}u\right\|}}\right)d\nu^{\mathbf{N}}(\omega)

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\int_{g\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int{\mathrm{Dil}}\left(g,\frac{h_{\varstar}u}{{\left\|h_{\varstar}u\right\|}}\right)d\nu^{(j)}(h)\right)d\nu(g)

If we apply this equation to

and

\int{\mathrm{Dil}}\left(g,u\right)d\hat{\mu}_{k}(u),

so, letting

(5.4)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\int\log{\left\|f_{\varstar}u_{k}\right\|}d\nu^{(n_{k})}(f)=\chi_{0}=\int_{{\mathsf{Diff}}^{1}(M)}\int_{T^{1}M}{\mathrm{Dil}}(g,u)d\hat{\mu}_{\infty}(u)d\nu(g)

Thus, there exists
\chi\geq\chi_{0}
(resp.
\chi\leq\chi_{0}
) and
an ergodic component
\hat{\mu}
of
\hat{\mu}_{\infty}

such that

(5.5)

\int_{{\mathsf{Diff}}^{1}(M)}\int_{T^{1}M}{\mathrm{Dil}}(g,u)d\hat{\mu}(u)d\nu(g)=\chi.

As observed above,

is an

\nu
-stationary probability measure. Denote by
\hat{\mu}_{x}
the conditional measures obtained
by disintegration of
\hat{\mu}
with respect to the fibers of
\pi

\hat{\mu}=\int\hat{\mu}_{x}d\mu(x)
. For
\mu

be the linear span of

\supp(\hat{\mu}(x))
. Since
\supp(\hat{\mu})
is
\nu
-almost invariant and

acts linearly along the fibers of

, we infer that

is a
\nu
-almost invariant
measurable sub-bundle. The Furstenberg formula asserts that
the top Lyapunov exponent of
\mu
in restriction
to

is equal to
\chi
. For completeness let us recall the argument:
the ergodic theorem shows that for
(\nu^{\mathbf{N}}\times\mu)
-almost every

and

-almost every

(5.6)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}{\mathrm{Dil}}\left(f_{j},(f^{j}_{\omega})_{\varstar}u\right)=\int_{{\mathsf{Diff}}^{1}(M)^{\mathbf{N}}}\int_{T^{1}M}{\mathrm{Dil}}(f^{1}_{\omega},u)\,d\hat{\mu}(u)d\nu^{\mathbf{N}}(\omega)=\chi

, and the proof is complete.

5.2.
Application: Chung’s criterion

The following theorem, taken from
[

plays an important role in this paper; a variant of this result appears in

]

It is stated in
[

Theorem 5.2

(Chung)
.

Let
M
be a compact manifold.
Let
\nu
be a probability measure on
{\mathsf{Diff}}^{1}(M)

that satisfies (
M
). If
\nu
is not uniformly expanding there exists an ergodic
\nu
-stationary measure
\mu
on
M
and a
\mu
-measurable
subbundle

such that

(a)

0<\dim(W)\leq\dim(M)
;

(b)

is
\nu
-almost surely invariant;

(c)

in restriction to

, the top Lyapunov exponent of

Conversely, if such a pair

is not uniformly expanding.

When

is a surface and

is supported by the group of diffeomorphisms preserving some fixed area form
the Lyapunov exponents of any ergodic stationary measure

6.1.

Let

(6.1)

\pi\circ h=h_{B}\circ\pi.

If

preserves every fiber of

Remark 6.1

If
\Gamma
is non-elementary and contains a Halphen twist (resp. a parabolic automorphism)

contains Halphen twists (resp. parabolic automorphisms) associated with infinitely many distinct invariant fibrations (see
[

Suppose now that

is a Halphen twist.
Then,

acts by translation on every smooth fiber of
\pi
(see
[
20
, §3]
).
To be more precise, denote by

the finite set of critical values of
\pi
and set

Fix some simply connected open subset

, endowed with a section
\sigma
of

\pi
and a continuous choice of basis for

.
Each fiber

, is an elliptic curve with zero

, and one can find a holomorphic function

on

is isomorphic to

is a translation

, for some holomorphic function

The points

(6.2)

The following lemma summarizes this discussion.

Lemma 6.2

to find sufficient conditions for as well as obstructions to

8.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.5
and related results

8.1.1.
Applying Chung’s criterion

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\nu
be a probability measure on
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
. A

\nu
-stationary measure
\mu
on
X
is said
non-expanding
if
every ergodic component
\mu^{\prime}
of
\mu
satisfies:

(i)

either both Lyapunov exponents of
\mu^{\prime}
are non-positive,

(ii)

or
\mu^{\prime}
is hyperbolic and its field of Oseledets stable directions is non-random.

Theorem
5.2
asserts that the existence
of non-expanding
\nu
-stationary measures is the obstruction to uniform expansion of
\nu
:

Corollary 8.2

(of Theorem
5.2
)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex surface and
\nu
be a probability measure on the group
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
, satisfying the moment condition (
M
).
Then
\nu
is uniformly expanding if
and only if non-expanding
\nu
-stationary measures do not exist, hence
if and only if every ergodic
\nu
-stationary measure
\mu
on
X
satisfies one of the following properties:

–

\mu
has a positive Lyapunov exponent and its stable distribution depends non-trivially on the itinerary;

–

the two Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
are strictly positive.

8.1.2.
Groups with invariant curves

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
X
be a compact complex surface. Let

\Gamma
be a subgroup of
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
that preserves a complex curve
C\subset X
. If
\nu
is a probability measure on
\Gamma
satisfying (
M
), then
\nu
is not uniformly expanding.

Remark 8.4
.

We leave the reader check that the proof adapts to the real case in the following sense: if
X
,
\Gamma
and
C
are defined over
\mathbf{R}
and
C(\mathbf{R})
is of dimension 1 (that is, neither empty nor a finite set), then
\nu
is not uniformly expanding in restriction to
C(\mathbf{R})
.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
C
be a compact Riemann surface. Then,
\mathsf{Aut}(C)
does not support any uniformly expanding probability measure.

Proof.

Let
\kappa
be a Kähler form on
C
that satisfies
\int_{C}\kappa=1
. For every
f\in\mathsf{Aut}(C)
,

\int_{C}f^{*}\kappa=1=\int_{C}{\left\|D_{x}f\right\|}^{2}\kappa=1
, so by the Jensen inequality

\int_{C}\log{\left\|D_{x}f\right\|}\kappa\leq 0
. Now, if
\nu
is any probability measure on
\mathsf{Aut}(C)
, then

\int_{C}\int_{\mathsf{Aut}(C)}\log{\left\|D_{x}f\right\|}d\nu(f)\kappa\leq 0,

hence Property (
2.6
) cannot be satisfied
by
\nu
(for any
n_{0}\geq 1
).
∎

Note that the same argument applies to conformal diffeomorphisms, in particular for

\mathsf{Diff}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Lemma
8.5
and Remark
2.1

imply Proposition
8.3
when
C
is smooth; we now prove Proposition
8.3
in full generality.

Proof of Proposition
8.3
.

Arguing by contradiction, we assume that
\nu
is uniformly expanding. Let
\Gamma_{1}\leq\Gamma

be the finite index subgroup fixing each component of
C
,
and each of its branches at each of its singular points; let
\nu_{1}
be
the hitting measure on
\Gamma_{1}
associated to

\nu^{(n_{0})}

\Gamma

supported on

such that the tangential Lyapunov exponent along

is non-positive.

By Lemma
8.5
we may assume that
the singular set

is non-empty.
If the genus of

, the invariance of

\nu
. Thus,

is a rational curve;

\hat{\Gamma}\subset\mathsf{Aut}(\hat{C})\simeq{\sf{PGL}}_{2}(\mathbf{C})

\Gamma

\nu
induces a

\hat{\nu}
on
\hat{\Gamma}
.
Fix
\hat{p}\in\hat{C}

\hat{C}
at
\hat{p}
determines one of the branches of

at

; our assumptions imply that

\hat{p}
is fixed by
\hat{\Gamma}
. There are local coordinates

for

and
(z,w)\in(\mathbf{C}^{2},(0,0))
for

in which

(8.1)

t\mapsto(\pi_{1}(t),\pi_{2}(t))=(\alpha t^{q},\beta t^{r})\ \ {\text{modulo higher order terms}}

where

\hat{p}
is

(1,0)\in\mathbf{C}^{2}
.
Let
\lambda_{(\hat{C},\hat{p})}
be the

\hat{\nu}
at
\hat{p}
, and
\lambda_{(C,p)}
be the Lyapunov exponent of
\nu

in the tangent direction of this branch.

Lemma 8.6
.

With notation as above

\lambda_{(C,p)}=q\lambda_{(\hat{C},\hat{p})}
. In particular
\lambda_{(C,p)}
and
\lambda_{(\hat{C},\hat{p})}

have the same sign.

Proof.

Pick
f\in\Gamma
, write

f(z,w)=(f_{1}(z,w),f_{2}(z,w))
in the local coordinates
(z,w)
, and expand
f_{1}
in power series:
f_{1}(z,w)=\sum_{i,j}a_{i,j}z^{i}w^{j}
.
Since the branch determined by
\lambda_{(\hat{C},\hat{p})}
is
f

D_{p}f(1,0)=(a_{1,0},0)
with
a_{1,0}\neq 0
. Thus,

(8.2)

f_{1}(\pi(t))=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}a_{i,j}\alpha^{i}\beta^{j}t^{qi+rj}=a_{1,0}\alpha t^{q}\mod(t^{q+1}).

Now,

\hat{f}
of
\hat{C}
fixing
\hat{p}
. Writing

\pi_{1}(\hat{f}(t))=\alpha\lambda^{q}t^{q}\mod(t^{q+1})
. Then,

gives

, and we are done.
∎

We resume the proof of Proposition
8.3
. We fix an affine coordinate
s
on
\hat{C}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{C})
such that
\hat{p}=\infty
. Then, every lift

can be written as an affine map

Lemma 8.7
.

The functions
\log\left|a_{g}\right|
and
\log^{+}\left|b_{g}\right|
are
\nu
-integrable and
\mathbb{E}(\log\left|a_{g}\right|)<0
.

Proof.

For the spherical metric, the derivative of
\hat{g}
at
\infty
in
\hat{C}
is
1/a_{g}
. The computations
of Lemma
8.6
show that the derivative

at

is
1/a_{g}^{q}
for some

. So (

) implies that
\mathbb{E}(\left|\log\left|a_{g}\right|\right|)<\infty
.
Since
\nu
is uniformly expanding, this direction
is repelling on average: by Lemma
8.6
, we get

\mathbb{E}(\log\left|a_{j}^{-1}\right|)>0
.
To estimate
\left|b_{g}\right|
, we note that

\dist_{X}(\pi(s),p)\asymp\left|s\right|^{-q}

approaches

. Changing the affine coordinate

we may assume that

(8.3)

\frac{1}{\left|b_{g}\right|^{q}}\asymp\dist_{X}(\pi(\hat{g}(0)),\pi(\infty))=\dist_{X}(g(\pi(0)),g(p))\leq{\left\|g\right\|}_{C^{1}}\dist_{X}(\pi(0),p).

From this and (

) it follows that
\mathbb{E}(\log^{+}\left|b_{g}\right|)<\infty
.
∎

The integrability provided by Lemma
8.7
now allows us to construct a stationary
measure with full mass in the affine chart
\mathbf{C}\subset C
with non-positive Lyapunov exponent
(relative to the affine metric).
This is classical, we briefly recall the argument for completeness (see
[
11
]
).
For
\omega=(g_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, write
g_{n}(s)=a_{n}s+b_{n}
, and
consider the sequence of right products

r_{n}(\omega)=g_{0}\cdots g_{n-1}
. One easily checks that

(8.4)

r_{n}(\omega)(s)=a_{0}\cdots a_{n-1}s+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{0}\cdots a_{j-1}b_{j}.

For
\nu^{\mathbf{N}}
-almost every
\omega
,
\frac{1}{n}\log\left|a_{0}\cdots a_{n-1}\right|
converges to
\lambda:=\mathbb{E}(\log\left|a_{g}\right|)<0
.
Fix
\varepsilon<\left|\lambda\right|
. Since
\mathbb{E}(\log^{+}\left|b_{g}\right|)<\infty
,
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nu\{\left|b_{g}\right|>e^{\varepsilon j}\}<\infty
.
By the Borel-Cantelli Lemma,
\left|b_{j}\right|\leq e^{\varepsilon j}
for
\nu^{\mathbf{N}}
-almost every
\omega
and for large

; hence, the series on
the right hand side of (
8.4
) converges. It follows that
r_{n}(\omega)(s)
converges almost surely to a limit
e_{\omega}

that does not depend on
s\in\mathbf{C}
. The distribution of
e_{\omega}
is the desired stationary measure
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}
.
If
\mu
is any stationary measure with
\mu(\mathbf{C})=1
, then
r_{n}(\omega)_{\varstar}\mu
converges to

\delta_{e_{\omega}}
almost surely: this shows that
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}
is the unique stationary measure
with
\mu(\mathbf{C})=1
; in particular,
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}
is ergodic.
Since the affine derivative of
g
is the constant
a_{g}
,
the Lyapunov exponent of
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}
, relative to the affine metric, is equal to
\lambda
.

To conclude the proof, note that
\mu:=\pi_{\varstar}(\mu_{\mathbf{C}})
is an ergodic

\nu
-stationary measure on
X
which has a well-defined Lyapunov exponent, thanks to the moment condition (
M
).
If
\mu
gives positive mass to the singular set of
C
, then it must be concentrated on a
single singular point of
C
(and likewise
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}
is a single atom in
\hat{C}
).
By Lemma
8.6
the corresponding branch is
attracting on average, which contradicts uniform expansion. Therefore
\mu
gives no mass to

{\mathrm{Sing}}(C)
, and we claim that its Lyapunov exponent
\lambda(\mu)\arrowvert_{TC}

in the direction of

equals
\lambda
(even if the ratio between the
ambient and affine metrics on
\mathbf{C}\subset C
is unbounded). Indeed, for

\mu\times\nu^{\mathbf{N}}
-almost every
(x,\omega)
and

, we can fix a subsequence

such that

is far
from the singularities of
C
(hence from
p=\pi(\infty)
). If
j
is large,

\frac{1}{n_{j}}\log{\left\|D_{x}f^{n_{j}}_{\omega}\right\|}
is both close to
\lambda
and to

\lambda(\pi_{\varstar}\mu)\arrowvert_{TC}
.
We conclude that
\lambda(\pi_{\varstar}\mu)\arrowvert_{TC}<0
, which again is contradictory. The proof is complete. ∎

8.1.3.
Zariski diffuse measures

From now on we focus on the case of a minimal Kähler surface

X
of Kodaira dimension zero, that is,
a torus, a K3 surface, or an Enriques surface. In this case

\mathsf{Aut}(X)
preserves a canonical volume form
{\sf{vol}}_{X}
(see Example
1.4
).

From Corollary
8.2
, the obstruction
to uniform expansion is the existence of a non-expanding stationary measure
\mu
. Moreover, in the first case of Definition
8.1
, both
exponents must vanish because we are in a volume preserving setting. In this situation,
Theorems
7.2

and
7.3
give a precise description of
\mu
.

Theorem 8.8
.

Let
X
be a torus, a K3 surface, or an Enriques surface. Let
\nu
be a probability measure on

\mathsf{Aut}(X)
satisfying (
M
) such that
\Gamma_{\nu}
is non-elementary.
If
\mu
is a Zariski diffuse
\nu
-stationary measure, the following properties are equivalent

(a)

\mu
is non-expanding;

(b)

the fiber entropy
h_{\mu}(X,\nu)
vanishes.

Morover under these assumptions,
\mu
is invariant and
h_{\mu}(f)=0
for every
f\in\Gamma_{\nu}
.

Proof.

As a preliminary step, observe that almost every ergodic component of
\mu
is
Zariski diffuse: this follows from the fact that there are
only finitely many invariant curves and countably many isolated periodic points. In addition, by linearity of the entropy, if
h_{\mu}(X,\nu)=0
then almost every ergodic component of
\mu
has zero fiber entropy as well.
Thus for both implications we may further assume that
\mu
is ergodic as a stationary measure.

Since there is an invariant volume form, either
both Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
vanish or
\mu
is hyperbolic. In the first case,
the invariance principle guarantees that
\mu
is
\Gamma_{\nu}
-invariant and
the fibered version of the Ruelle inequality (see e.g.
[
21
, §7]
) implies that its fiber entropy
vanishes. If
\mu
is hyperbolic, the invariance of
\mu
and the vanishing of the entropy follow from

[
21
, Thm. 9.1]
.
Thus, Property (a) implies Property (b), together with the invariance of
\mu
.

Consider the converse implication. Again, if
\mu
has zero Lyapunov exponents then it is non-expanding and
invariant. Otherwise it is hyperbolic and
by applying the whole argument of
[
13
]
in the complex case, we infer that if the stable directions of

\mu
depend on the itinerary, its conditionals along Pesin unstable manifolds admit a non-trivial
translation invariance; in particular they are non-atomic. It follows that
h_{\mu}(X,\nu)>0

(see also
[
21
, Rmk 9.2]
). So under assumption (b) the stable directions are non-random and, as
already explained,
\mu
is invariant by
[
21
, Thm. 9.1]
.

The fact that
h_{\mu}(f)=0
for all
f\in\Gamma_{\nu}
will be shown in
Theorem
A.1
.
∎

8.1.4.
Refined criterion

The discussion of the previous paragraphs leads to a version of
Theorem
1.5
that does not require
\Gamma_{\nu}
to contain parabolic elements:

Theorem 8.9
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler surface which is not rational. Let
\nu
be a probability measure on

\mathsf{Aut}(X)
satisfying (
M
) such that
\Gamma_{\nu}
is non-elementary.
Then
\nu
is uniformly expanding if and only if the three following conditions
hold:

(1)

every finite
\Gamma_{\nu}
-orbit is uniformly expanding;

(2)

there is no
\Gamma_{\nu}
-invariant algebraic curve;

(3)

there is no Zariski diffuse invariant measure
\mu
with zero fiber entropy.

Proof.

If a compact Kähler surface
X
is ruled (over a curve of positive genus) or has a positive Kodaira dimension, then
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
is elementary (in the first case, it preserves the ruling; in the second case, it preserves the Kodaira-Iitaka fibration, acting as a finite group on the base). Thus, the Kodaira dimension of
X
vanishes. If
X
is not minimal, the uniqueness of the minimal model shows that there is a
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
-invariant curve, and we know this is incompatible with uniform expansion (Proposition
8.3
). Now if
{\mathsf{kod}}(X)=0
,
X
is minimal, and
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
is non-elementary, then
X
is a torus, a K3 surface, or an Enriques surface; hence, we can assume that
X
is such a surface.

If
\nu
is uniformly expanding, Property (1) is obvious, Property (2) follows from Proposition
8.3
, and
Property (3) follows from Corollary
8.2
and Theorem
8.8
.

Conversely, if these properties hold, and if
\mu
is an ergodic
\nu
-stationary measure then by Property (2)

\mu
is either Zariski diffuse or finitely supported. Then, Theorem
8.8
and Property (1) imply that
\mu
is not non-expanding, and we conclude with Corollary
8.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

This follows directly from Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
8.9
.
∎

.

\Rightarrow
(a) in Theorem

relies on the following fact:
for a hyperbolic stationary measure, if the stable directions of

depend on the itinerary, then its unstable conditionals satisfy some non-trivial translation invariance

]

. On the other hand, it is not used in Theorem

Using Theorem

8.2.
Uniform expansion along finite orbits

Using classical results on random products of matrices, it is easy
to characterize when a fixed point under
\Gamma_{\nu}
is uniformly expanding. We say that a subgroup of
{\sf{GL}}_{2}(\mathbf{C})
is

strictly triangular
if it is reducible with exactly one invariant direction.

Proposition 8.12
.

Let
X
be a torus, a K3 surface, or an Enriques surface. Let

\nu
be a probability measure on
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
satisfying (
M
), and let
x_{0}
be a fixed point of

\Gamma_{\nu}
. Then
\nu
is uniformly expanding on
T_{x_{0}}X
if and only if one of the following holds

(a)

the
induced action of
\Gamma_{\nu}
on
T_{x_{0}}X
is non-elementary;

(b)

this action is strictly triangular and its invariant direction
is expanding.

If
\nu
is symmetric, it is uniformly expanding on
T_{x_{0}}X
if and only if (a) holds.

In case (b) there exists
u\in T_{x_{0}}X
such that
f_{\varstar}u=\lambda_{f}u
for every
f\in\Gamma_{\nu}
, and the expansion means that
\int\log\left|\lambda_{f}\right|d\nu(f)>0
.

Proof (see also
[
46
]
).

By Lemma
2.3
, to prove uniform expansion it is enough to show that for every
v\in T_{x_{0}}X
,
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\left\|(f_{\omega}^{n})_{\varstar}v\right\|}>0
.
The proof is based on the work of Furstenberg and Kifer
[
35
]
(see also
[
10
, §3.7]
). These references deal with general random products of matrices in
{\sf{GL}}_{d}(\mathbf{R})
; in our volume preserving situation the Lyapunov exponents
\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}
of the random
product in
{\sf{GL}}_{2}(\mathbf{C})

satisfy
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0
, so they can be read off directly from the action on

\mathbb{P}T_{x_{0}}X
. According to Theorems 3.5 and 3.9 of
[
35
]
, there are two possibilities:

(i)

for every
v\in T_{x_{0}}X
and
\nu^{\mathbf{N}}
-almost every
\omega
,
\frac{1}{n}\log{\left\|(f_{\omega}^{n})_{\varstar}v\right\|}\to\lambda_{1}
;

(ii)

there
exists a
non-random,
\Gamma_{\nu}
-invariant filtration
\left\{0\right\}=L_{2}<L_{1}<L_{0}=T_{x_{0}}X

and
\beta_{1}<\beta_{0}

such that for
i=0,1
for any
v\in L_{i}\setminus L_{i+1}
, for
\nu^{\mathbf{N}}
-almost every
\omega
,

\frac{1}{n}\log{\left\|(f_{\omega}^{n})_{\varstar}v\right\|}\to\beta_{i}
. Furthermore
\beta_{0}=\lambda_{1}
.

We now compare this dichotomy with the classification of subgroups of
{\sf{PGL}}_{2}(\mathbf{C})
(with a slight abuse of notation, we also denote by
\Gamma_{\nu}
the induced subgroup of
{\sf{PGL}}_{2}(\mathbf{C})
).

-
If
\Gamma_{\nu}
is strongly irreducible, we are in case (i) and there are two possibilities.
If
\Gamma_{\nu}
is proximal (hence non-elementary)
then
\lambda_{1}>0
and
\nu
is uniformly expanding. If
\Gamma_{\nu}
is not proximal, it is contained in a
compact subgroup and
\nu
is not uniformly expanding.

-
If
\Gamma_{\nu}
is irreducible but not strongly irreducible, we are in case (i) and there are two lines which are permuted by
\Gamma
. In some affine coordinate
z
on
\mathbb{P}T_{x_{0}}X
,
\Gamma_{\nu}
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\left\{z\mapsto\lambda z^{\varepsilon}\;;\ \lambda\in\mathbf{C}^{\times},\ \varepsilon=\pm 1\right\}
and
\varepsilon=-1
with positive probability. In this case
\lambda_{1}=0
(see e.g.
[
28
, Prop. 5.3]
), so
\nu
is not uniformly expanding.

-
If
\Gamma_{\nu}
is reducible it preserves one or two directions in
T_{x_{0}}X
. If
\Gamma
preserves a direction with exponent
\leq 0
, then
\nu
is not uniformly expanding. So, we can assume that
\Gamma
preserves a unique direction, and that the corresponding exponent
\beta
is positive. By (i) and (ii) we see that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\left\|(f^{n}_{\omega})_{\varstar}v\right\|}\geq\beta
for any
v\in T_{x_{0}}X
and almost every
\omega
; so

\nu
is uniformly expanding.

This covers all possible cases and the proof is complete.
∎

Let
F
be a finite set, viewed as a
0
-dimensional manifold, and
V
be a real or complex vector bundle of dimension
d
over
F
; identify

V
with
F\times\mathbf{K}^{d}
, for
{\mathbf{K}}=\mathbf{R}
or
\mathbf{C}
. Let

be the group of bijections of

where

by permuting the factors. We say that a subgroup of
{\sf{GL}}(V)
is

if it acts transitively on
F

and the stabilizer of any

acts strongly irreducibly on the fiber
\{x\}\times{\mathbf{K}}^{d}
of
V
; equivalently, if there is no invariant and finite collection of subspaces of dimension
\neq 0,d
in some fibers of
V
. Similar notations and notions are defined for
{\sf{PGL}}(V)
.

Assume now that
F
is a finite
\Gamma
-orbit on
X
,
and consider the induced action of
\Gamma
on
TX\arrowvert_{F}:=\bigcup_{x\in F}T_{x}X
.
We say that this action is
non-elementary

if its image in
{\sf{PGL}}\left(TX\arrowvert_{F}\right)
is strongly irreducible and unbounded. When

\Gamma
preserves a volume form on
X
, its image in
{\sf{GL}}\left(TX\arrowvert_{F}\right)
is unbounded if and only if it is unbounded in
{\sf{PGL}}\left(TX\right)
.
We say that it is
strictly triangular
if the only proper
\Gamma
-invariant subbundle in
TX\arrowvert_{F}
is given by a
1
-dimensional subbundle
L\subset TX\arrowvert_{F}
.

Pick a point
x
in
F
and set
\Gamma_{x}=\Stab_{\Gamma}(\left\{x\right\})
. Since
F
is an orbit,
[\Gamma:\Gamma_{x}]=\left|F\right|
and the image of
\Gamma
in
{\sf{PGL}}\left(TX\arrowvert_{F}\right)
is unbounded if and only if the image of
\Gamma_{x}
in
{\sf{PGL}}\left(T_{x}X\right)
is unbounded. Thus, one easily gets the following lemma.

Lemma 8.13
.

If
F
is a finite
\Gamma
-orbit, the action of

\Gamma
on
TX\arrowvert_{F}
is non-elementary (resp. strictly triangular) if and only if for some, hence any,
x\in F

the action of
\Stab_{\Gamma}(\left\{x\right\})
on
T_{x}X
is non-elementary (resp. strictly triangular).

Theorem 8.14
.

Let
X
be a torus, a K3 surface, or an Enriques surface. Let

\nu
be a probability measure on
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
satisfying (
M+
), and
F
be a finite
\Gamma_{\nu}
-orbit.
Then
\nu
is uniformly expanding on
F
if and only if the
induced action of
\Gamma_{\nu}
on
TF
is

(a)

either non-elementary;

(b)

or strictly triangular and the field of invariant directions
L\subset TX\arrowvert_{F}
is uniformly expanding.

If
\nu
is symmetric, it is uniformly expanding on
F
if and only if (a) holds.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{F}
be the finite index subgroup fixing every point of
F
.
Assume that
\nu
is uniformly expanding. Then by Proposition
3.2
, for some

n_{0}
, the induced measure
(\nu^{(n_{0})})_{\Gamma_{F}}
is uniformly expanding. Therefore,
by Proposition
8.12

\Gamma_{F}
satisfies Property (a) or (b) at every point of
F
, and
we conclude by Lemma
8.13
.
Conversely, assume that (a) or (b) holds. Note that by Theorem
3.1
,

\nu_{\Gamma_{F}}
satisfies (
M+
).
By Lemma
8.13
and Proposition
8.12
,

\nu_{\Gamma_{F}}
is uniformly expanding on
F
,
hence by Proposition
3.3

\nu
is uniformly expanding on
F
, as desired.
∎

This theorem shows that when
\nu
is symmetric
all conditions in Theorem
8.9
depend only on

\Gamma_{\nu}
, and not on
\nu
. Thus we obtain:

Corollary 8.15
.

Let
X
be a torus, a K3 surface, or an Enriques surface. Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary subgroup of
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
. Let
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
be symmetric probability measures
on
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
satisfying (
M+
) such that
\Gamma_{\nu}=\Gamma_{\nu^{\prime}}=\Gamma
. Then
\nu
is uniformly expanding if and only if
\nu^{\prime}
is
uniformly expanding.

In the following if
X
is a torus, K3, or Enriques surface, we will
say that the action of a non-elementary subgroup

is

if this property holds for some (hence any)

satisfying (

) and
generating

.

9.
Examples of uniformly expanding actions

9.1.
A finitary version of Theorem
1.5
and application to Wehler surfaces

In
[
23
, § §7-8]
, Chung uses computer assistance to prove the uniform expansion of
some concrete algebraic actions on real surfaces.
In our situation
Theorem
1.5
can be used to
check uniform expansion, but this requires a description of all invariant Zariski-closed subsets.
As already explained, invariant curves can be determined by cohomological computations; for instance, if
X
is a generic Wehler surface, there is no
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
-invariant curve. Thus the main problem is to study finite orbits.

If the group
\Gamma
is non-elementary, contains parabolic elements, and has no invariant curve, the main
result of
[
19
]
says that
\Gamma
admits only finitely many finite orbits, except when
(X,\Gamma)
is a Kummer example. However, the proof given in
[
19
]
does not provide any bound
on the number or the lengths of such orbits; so, there is
a priori
no hope of numerically checking
uniform expansion along all of them, nor proving that there are no finite orbits. The next result explains how to overcome this issue.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface and

\Gamma
be a non-elementary subgroup of
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
containing parabolic elements, which does not
preserve any algebraic curve. Assume that we are given:

(i)

algebraic equations for
X
, and the formulas defining a generating subset
S
of
\Gamma
;

(ii)

a basis of
{\mathrm{NS}}(X;\mathbf{R})
and the matrices of
s^{\varstar}\colon{\mathrm{NS}}(X;\mathbf{R})\to{\mathrm{NS}}(X;\mathbf{R})
, for
s
in
S
;

(iii)

a parabolic element
g\in\Gamma
, given as a word in the generators
s\in S
,
and its invariant fibration
\pi\colon X\to B
.

Then, there is an analytically computable integer
N(X,\Gamma)

such that the action of
\Gamma
on any
finite orbit of length greater than
N(X,\Gamma)
is non-elementary.

By
analytically computable
, we mean
computable by a computer able to solve real analytic equations; by
algebraically computable
, we mean
computable by a computer able to solve algebraic equations. The proof will provide an analytically computable subset containing all possible non-expanding finite orbits.

Example 9.2
.

Let
h\in\Gamma
be a conjugate of
g
with a distinct invariant fibration.
Denote by
\mathrm{Tor}_{N}(g)
the finite set of
fibers of the
g
-invariant fibration in which
g
is a periodic translation of period
\leq N
.
Then, the set of finite orbits of
\Gamma
of length
\leq N
is algebraically computable since it is contained in

(9.1)

\mathrm{Tor}_{N}(g)\cap\mathrm{Tor}_{N}(h)=\{x\in X\;;\;g^{N}(x)=h^{N}(x)=x\}.

A typical application of Theorem
9.1
is to the Wehler family.
Recall from §
1.1

that
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}
is the family of Wehler surfaces which are smooth and do not contain any
fiber of the three natural projections

(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{3}\to(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{2}
.
Under these assumptions the group
\Gamma
generated by the three basic involutions

\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
and
\sigma_{3}
is non-elementary and
has no invariant curve (see
[
19
, Prop. 2.2]
).
It turns out that in this case
N(X,\Gamma)
is constant on a Zariski
dense open subset (see Proposition
9.7
below).
This leads to:

Theorem 9.3
.

There is a dense Zariski open subset of
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}

(resp. of the family
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\mathbf{R})
of real Wehler surfaces),
in which the action of
\Gamma=\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\rangle
is
uniformly expanding on
X
.

9.1.1.
Preliminaries on Halphen twists

Let us resume the discussion from §
6.1
and add a few preliminaries on Betti foliations and the non-twisting locus. Let
h
be a Halphen twist with associated fibration
\pi\colon X\to B
.
Consider a simply connected open subset
U
of
B^{\circ}
together with a section
\sigma\colon U\to X
of
\pi

and a continuous frame for the homology of the fibers above
U
.
For
w\in U
, one can identify the fiber
X_{w}
to
\mathbf{C}/{\mathrm{Lat}}(w)
(
\sigma(w)
corresponding to the zero of
\mathbf{C}/{\mathrm{Lat}}(w)
), as in §
6.1
.
Then, above
U
, there is a unique real-analytic diffeomorphism
\Psi:\pi^{-1}(U)\to U\times\mathbf{R}^{2}/\mathbf{Z}^{2}

such that

(a)

\pi\circ\Psi=\Psi\circ\pi_{U}
, where
\pi_{U}
is the projection onto
U
;

(b)

\Psi
maps
\sigma
to the zero section
w\mapsto(w,(0,0))
of
\pi_{U}
, and maps the basis of
H_{1}(X_{w};\mathbf{Z})
to the standard basis of
H_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2}/\mathbf{Z}^{2};\mathbf{Z})=\mathbf{Z}^{2}
;

(c)

on each fiber,
\Psi
is a real analytic isomorphism of real Lie group.

Above
U
, the
Betti foliation
is the foliation by
submanifolds of the form
\Psi^{-1}(U\times\left\{(x,y)\right\})
; these leaves are local holomorphic sections of
\pi
, with
\sigma
corresponding to
\Psi^{-1}(U\times\left\{(0,0)\right\})
.
Conjugating by

\Psi
, we get

(9.2)

\Psi\circ h\circ\Psi^{-1}\colon(w,(x,y))\mapsto(w,(x,y)+T(w)),

where
T\colon U\to\mathbf{R}^{2}/\mathbf{Z}^{2}
is real analytic. By
[
20
, Lem. 3.9]
, the map
T

is an (orientation preserving)
branched covering, so it behaves topologically like
w\mapsto w^{k}
. In
U
, the

non-twisting locus

\mathrm{NT}_{h}

is the set
\{w\in U\;;\;D_{w}T=0\}
; equivalently,
\mathrm{NT}_{h}\cap U=\pi(\left\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{q}\right\})
, where

\left\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{q}\right\}
is the set of tangencies between
the Betti foliation and the section
h\circ\sigma
. These definitions do not depend on the above choices and
\mathrm{NT}_{h}
can indeed be defined globally on
B^{\circ}
.
A key fact is that
\mathrm{NT}_{h}
is a finite subset of
B^{\circ}

(see
[
20
, Prop. 3.14]
or
[
27
, Cor. 7.7.10]
).
We denote by
\left|{\mathrm{NT}}_{h}\right|
its cardinality, and by
\mult({\mathrm{NT}}_{h})

its cardinality counted with multiplicity, that is, taking into account the
degree of the local branched covering
T
.

Note that, once
h
and
\pi
are given,
the set
{\mathrm{NT}}_{h}\subset B^{\circ}
is analytically computable
: one has to compute the periods of
X_{w}

to get
{\mathrm{Lat}}(w)
, then
\Psi
is
\mathbf{R}
-linear from
\mathbf{C}/{\mathrm{Lat}}(w)
to
\mathbf{R}^{2}/\mathbf{Z}^{2}
, and
T
is then obtain from
h
by conjugacy.

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
g,h
be a pair of Halphen twists in
\Gamma
with distinct invariant fibrations, and let

be a point with a finite
\Gamma
-orbit.
If this orbit is not uniformly expanding, then
it is contained in

{\mathrm{STang}}(\pi_{g},\pi_{h})\cup{\mathrm{NT}}_{g}^{X}\cup{\mathrm{NT}}_{h}^{X}
.

Proof.

We argue by contraposition: replacing

by another point in its orbit if necessary,
we assume that

Since

is finite, there are positive integers

and

such that
g^{k}
and

are in
\mathrm{Stab}_{\Gamma}(x)

is uniformly expanding. ∎

The intersection number of

(resp.

be the maximum of these intersection numbers:

n_{0}=\max\{ [{\mathrm{NT}}_{g}^{X}\cup{\mathrm{STang}}(\pi_{g},\pi_{h})]\cdot[X_{w}^{h}]\;;\;[{\mathrm{NT}}_{h}^{X}\cup{\mathrm{STang}}(\pi_{g},\pi_{h}]\cdot[X_{w}^{g}])\}.

The set

Then, we set

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
g,h
be a pair of Halphen twists in
\Gamma
with distinct invariant fibrations. Let

where

is defined by (

Proof.

The statement of the lemma concerns the orbit

itself.
If

Otherwise by Lemma

,
x
belongs to

{\mathrm{NT}}_{g}^{X}\setminus{\mathrm{NT}}_{h}^{X}
or
{\mathrm{NT}}_{h}^{X}\setminus{\mathrm{NT}}_{g}^{X}
.
Assume that
x\in{\mathrm{NT}}_{g}^{X}\setminus{\mathrm{NT}}_{h}^{X}
.
The
h

is finite and by Lemma

again,
for every

is contained in

for

). Thus,

, where

. Set

. The fiber

The set

Conclusion of the proof of Theorem
9.1
in the general case.

By assumption,
\Gamma
is non-elementary and has no invariant curve. Let
\Gamma^{*}
be its image in
{\sf{GL}}({\mathrm{NS}}(X;\mathbf{Z}))
.

If
g
is the parabolic element given in assumption (iii) of the theorem, up to sign, there is a unique integral primitive class
c(g)\in{\mathrm{NS}}(X)
such that
g^{\varstar}c(g)=c(g)
and
c(g)\cdot c(g)=0
. By the assumptions (ii) and (iii), this class can be computed explicitly. An element
f
of
\mathsf{Aut}(X)
preserves the
g
-invariant fibration
\pi
(permuting its fibers) if and only if it fixes
c(g)
. Since
\Gamma
is non-elementary, it is not contained in the stabilizer of
c(g)
. Thus, according to Proposition 3.2 of
[
32
]
, there is a computable integer
N
, and a composition
f
of length
N
in the generators
s\in S
that does not preserve
c(g)
. Then,
h:=f\circ g\circ f^{-1}
is a parabolic element of
\Gamma
with invariant fibration
\pi\circ f\neq\pi
.

Since
g
and its invariant fibration
\pi
, as well as
f
, are explicit, we can compute the degree of
{\mathrm{STang}}(\pi_{g},\pi_{h})
(for the embedding
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{m}(\mathbf{C})
given by assumption (i)). Denote by
(C_{i})_{i\in I}
the irreducible components of
{\mathrm{STang}}(\pi_{g},\pi_{h})
; we have
|I|\leq\deg({\mathrm{STang}}(\pi_{g},\pi_{h}))
.

Suppose that for each
C_{i}
, one can exhibit some
f_{i}\in\Gamma
for which
f_{i}(C_{i})\not\subset{\mathrm{STang}}(\pi_{g},\pi_{h})
. Then the set of pairs

(9.8)

\left\{(g,h)\right\}\cup\left\{(f_{i}gf_{i}^{-1},f_{i}hf_{i}^{-1})\;;\;\ i\in I\right\}

satisfies
|\bigcap_{i}{\mathrm{STang}}(\pi_{g_{i}},\pi_{h_{i}})|<+\infty
, and we are done because the cardinality of this finite set is algebraically computable.
So, we now fix such an irreducible component
C_{i}
, and we construct such an
f_{i}
.

First, assume that
C_{i}
is an irreducible component of
{\mathrm{Tang}}^{\mathrm{tt}}(\pi_{g},\pi_{h})
. Then
C_{i}
is generically transverse to
\pi
, hence
\deg(g^{n}(C_{i}))
tends to infinity. We can thus set

for some large enough

(explicitely computable from the action on
{\mathrm{NS}}(X)
).

The second case is when
C_{i}
is an irreducible component of
{\mathrm{Sing}}(\pi_{g})\cup{\mathrm{Sing}}(\pi_{h})
;
in particular, its self-intersection

is
\leq 0
.
By
[

, there exists a loxodromic element

. Since

is loxodromic, this inequation is equivalent to

(f_{0}^{\left|I\right|!})_{\varstar}[C_{i}]\neq[C_{i}]
. Indeed, either

10.2.
Ergodicity

In
[
26
]
, the original motivation to introduce uniform
expansion was a criterion for ergodicity. The same holds in our setting, with a few caveats which will be explained below.

Theorem 10.2

(Dolgopyat-Krikorian

, Cor. 2]

[
42

23
]
)
.

Let

\Gamma

\Gamma
-ergodic.

(see
[
20

for a short discussion).
Here we content ourselves with saying that

is irreducible when
\mathrm{Reg}(Y)
is connected.
Observe also that the ergodicity of

follows directly from Theorem
6.4

Proof (sketch).

The proof in
[
26
]
is a bit sketchy, but it was already
expanded in
[
42

23
]
(see also
[
49
]
).
Here we just make a few comments on (1) the extension to the holomorphic case for the action on
X

Regarding the action on
X
, let us recall that the proof of

variation on the Hopf argument in which the asymptotic behavior of the Birkhoff

The key technical ingredients are the facts that under the uniform expansion assumption:

–

Given these facts, we can copy the proof of

Remark 10.3

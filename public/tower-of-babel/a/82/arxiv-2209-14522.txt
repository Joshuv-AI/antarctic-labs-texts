1.
Introduction

We are interested in the construction of solutions of the following generalized parabolic Cahn-Hilliard equation

(1.1)

u_{t}=-\Delta\left[\Delta u-W^{\prime}(u)\right]+W^{\prime\prime}(u)\left[\Delta u-W^{\prime}(u)\right],\qquad\forall\,(t,x)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
n=2
or
n\geq 4
,

\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
is given by

\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}(0,\infty),&\quad\mbox{if }n=2,\\[5.69054pt]
(-\infty,0),&\quad\mbox{if }n\geq 4,\end{array}\right.

and the functions
W^{\prime}(s)
and
W^{\prime\prime}(s)
denote the derivatives of first and second orders of
W
respectively.
The potential
W(s)
is a smooth function, which satisfies
the following assumptions

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}W(s)>W(-1)=W(1)\qquad\text{in}\ (-1,1),\\[2.84526pt]
W(s)=W(-s),\qquad\text{for all}\ s\in\mathbb{R},\\[2.84526pt]
W^{\prime}(-1)=W^{\prime}(1)=0,\\[2.84526pt]
W^{\prime\prime}(-1)=W^{\prime\prime}(1)>0.\end{array}\right.

The potential
W(u)
has two non-degenerate local minimum points
u=+1
and
u=-1
, which are stable equilibria of (
1.1
).
In particular, the function
W(s)=\frac{1}{4}(1-s^{2})^{2}
obviously satisfies the above conditions in (
1.2
).
Up to a scaling, the function
W(s)=\cos(s)
also satisfies (
1.2
).

1.1.
Backgrounds

Generally speaking, the Cahn-Hilliard equation means the following equation

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}=-\Delta\left[\Delta u-W^{\prime}(u)\right],\qquad\forall\,(t,x)\in(-\infty,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\hskip 56.9055pt\forall\,x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
denotes a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
), which describes phase separation processes of binary alloys in
[
7
]
.
Various kinds of problems of this class equation have been extensively studied in recent thirty years.
By applying a priori estimate and continuity argument, the existence and asymptotic behaviors of global smooth solutions of Cauchy problem (
1.3
) have been proved in
[
30
]
when the initial value
u_{0}
is close to stable equilibria
\bar{u}
(
W(\bar{u})=0
) in the
L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
space.
Using Fourier transform and estimates on the kernel of a linear parabolic operator, a uniform
L^{\infty}
bound estimate for solutions of perturbed Cauchy problem (
1.3
) with additional nonlinear terms, was established by Caffarelli and Muler in
[
12
]
.
In a bounded domain, the global well-posedness and long-time behavior of solutions to problem (
1.3
) with several types of dynamic boundary conditions were studied in
[
10
,
11
,
25
,
31
,
36
]
.

Based on the works of De Giorgi in
[
14
,
15
]
, numerous authors
[
3
,
35
,
38
]
studied the diffuse approximation of the Willmore functional:

(1.4)

\mathcal{W}(S,\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial S\cap\Omega}\left|H_{\partial S}(x)\right|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\mu^{n-1},

where
\Omega
is a given open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary
\partial S\subset\Omega
, and
H_{\partial S}(x)
is the mean curvature of surface
\partial S
at point
x\in\partial S
. The approximating functional is defined by

(1.5)

\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varepsilon\Delta u-\frac{W^{\prime}(u)}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x,\qquad\text{if}\ u\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,2}(\Omega),\\[5.69054pt]
+\infty,\hskip 110.96556pt\text{otherwise in}\ L^{1}(\Omega),\end{array}\right.

where the function
W
satisfies (
1.2
). The essential and more challenging work is to rigourously prove that the approximating functional

\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(u)

\Gamma
-converges to the Willmore functional
\mathcal{W}(S,\Omega)
as
\varepsilon
goes to
0
. Bellettini
and Paolini in
[
2
]
proved the
\Gamma
-lim sup inequality for smooth Willmore hypersurfaces. However, the
\Gamma
-lim inf inequality is
more hard to prove. Up to now, it has been proved in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n=2,3
in
[
35
]
or
n=2
in
[
34
]
. This problem is still open in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 4
.
The relation of the critical points of (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) was exemplified by M. Rizzi
[
37
]
and also A. Malchiodi, R. Mandel, M. Rizzi
[
33
]
.
On the other hand, for the parabolic Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1.3
), the gamma convergence results have obtained by Le in
[
32
]
under suitable conditions. And see
[
5
]
for the case of (
1.3
) on the one-dimensional torus.

The
L^{2}
gradient flow of the approximating energy
\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(u)
is equivalent to the evolution equation:

(1.6)

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=-\Delta\Big[\Delta u_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\Big]+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}W^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\Big[\Delta u_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\Big]\qquad\text{in}\ (-\infty,+\infty)\times\Omega,

which was introduced in
[
20
]
to describe the deformation of a vesicle membrane under
the elastic bending energy, with prescribed bulk volume and surface area. The well-posedness of the phase field model (
1.6
) with fixed
\varepsilon
has been proved in
[
8
]
providing a volume constraint for the average of
u
, or in
[
9
]
with both volume and area constraints. By applying formal method of matched asymptotic expansions, Loreti and March in
[
29
]
(or Wang in
[
39
]
) showed that if
\Gamma(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n=2
or
3
, is a family of compact closed smooth interfaces and evolves by Willmore flow, it can be approximated by nodal set of the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to the phase field (
1.6
) when
\varepsilon
goes to
0
. The Willmore flow equation is given by

(1.7)

V(t)=\Delta_{\Sigma(t)}H-\frac{1}{2}H^{3}+H\|A\|^{2},

which is the
L^{2}
gradient flow for (
1.4
) with
\partial S(t)=\Sigma(t)
, where
V(t)
denotes the outer normal velocity at
x\in\Sigma(t)
,
\Delta_{\Sigma(t)}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on surface
\Sigma(t)
,
H
and
A
are the mean curvature and the second fundamental form of
\Sigma(t)
respectively, and
\|A\|^{2}
is the sum of squared coefficients of
A
. Fei and Liu
[
22
]
proved that for given a solution
\Gamma_{0}(t)
of (
1.7
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}(n=2,3)
, there exists a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of equation (
1.6
) with Neumann boundary condition such that its level set convergence to
\Gamma_{0}(t)
as the parameter
\varepsilon
goes to zero.
Moreover, a variety of problems for the Willmore flow (
1.7
) has been investigated by Kuwert and Schätzle in several papers (for example see
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
). However, the study of connections between equation (
1.6
) and the Willmore flow (
1.7
) is a challenging work when
n\geq 4
. The present paper is one of the first attempts in this direction.

1.2.
Main results

In this paper, we want to find solutions of (
1.1
) whose values lie at all times in
[-1,1]
,
and approach either
+1
or
-1
in the most of the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
This type of solution
corresponds to a continuous realization of a material, in which the two states
(
u=-1
and
u=+1
) coexist. The main difficult point of the study of this type solution
of (
1.1
), is to derive qualitative information on the interface region(the walls
separating the two phases).
It is easy to find that
u(t,x)
is a solution of (
1.1
) if and only if
u_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=u(\varepsilon^{-4}t,\varepsilon^{-1}x)
satisfies equation (
1.6
). Basing on the results in
[
29
]
or
[
39
]
with
n=2
and
3
, we know that the nodal set of
u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
approximates to the solution of Willmore flow (
1.7
). Let us
consider the sphere
\Gamma_{n}(t)
evolving by the Willmore flow in (
1.7
).
Then we have that the radius
\gamma_{n}(t)
of
\Gamma_{n}(t)
satisfies the equation

(1.8)

\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n-1}{\gamma_{n}(t)}\Big)^{3}+\frac{\big(n-1\big)^{2}}{\big(\gamma_{n}(t)\big)^{3}},

which has a solution

(1.9)

\gamma_{n}(t):=\sqrt[4]{-2(n-3)(n-1)^{2}t},

where
t\leq 0
when
n\geq 3
and
t\geq 0
when
n=2
. Due to the self-similarity, there holds that the sphere
\left|x\right|=\gamma_{n}(t)
is also the transition layer (nodal set) for
u(t,x)
, which is a solution of (
1.1
).

Our aim is to construct solutions to equation (
1.1
) with one transition layer closes to the sphere
\left|x\right|=\gamma_{n}(t)
. In which,
t\leq 0
when
n\geq 4
, these solutions are called ancient solutions. And
t\geq 0
for
n=2
, they are long time solutions.
We shall mention that the ancient radially symmetric solutions for the parabolic Allen-Cahn equation

(1.10)

u_{t}=\Delta u+u-u^{3}\qquad\text{in}\ (-\infty,0]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

have been obtained by del Pino and Gkikas in
[
18
]
for
n=1
and
[
19
]
with
n\geq 2
.

Let us introduce a layer function by considering the following problem of semilinear elliptic equation

(1.11)

\omega^{\prime\prime}(y)-W^{\prime}\big(\omega(y)\big)=0,\quad\omega^{\prime}(y)>0,\quad y\in\mathbb{R},\quad\omega(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow\pm\infty}\omega(y)=\pm 1,

which has a unique smooth solution
\omega(y)
obtained in
[
1
]
, where
W
satisfies the conditions in (
1.2
).
In particular if we choose
W^{\prime}(s)=s^{3}-s
, then (
1.11
) is the elliptic Allen-Cahn equation, and its solution is written as

\omega(y)=\tanh(\frac{y}{\sqrt{2}}).

For general functions
W
satisfying (
1.2
), the solution
\omega
of
(
1.11
) has no explicit expression. In
[
1
]
, the inverse function of
\omega
is given by

(1.12)

\lambda(s):=\int^{s}_{0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(W(\tau)-W(1))}}\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad s\in(-1,1).

Thanks to (
1.2
), the function
\lambda(s)
is well-defined. Taking
\omega
as a basic layer
(the name layer is motivated by the fact that
\omega
approaches the limits
1
and
-1
at
\pm\infty
), we will construct a
solution of (
1.1
) with a ‘transition layer’ which is symmetric about the sphere
\left|x\right|=\gamma_{n}(t)
in (
1.9
).

More precisely, we want to find a solution of equation (
1.1
), which has the following asymptotical behavior

(1.13)

u(t,x)\approx\omega\big(\left|x\right|-\rho(t)\big),

where
\omega
is given by (
1.11
).
The function
\rho(t)
satisfies

(1.14)

\rho(t)=\gamma_{n}(t)+h(t),\qquad\text{with}\ h(t)=O\left(\frac{1}{\log\left|t\right|}\right),

where
h(t)
is an
C^{1}
function with respect of
t
and
the function
\gamma_{n}(t)
is defined by (
1.9
).
In fact,
\rho(t)
can be chosen by solving the following ODE

\rho^{\prime}(t)+\frac{(n-3)(n-1)^{2}}{2\rho^{3}(t)}=Q\big(\rho(t),\rho^{\prime}(t)\big),\qquad\text{with}\ Q\big(\rho(t),\rho^{\prime}(t)\big)=O\left(\frac{1}{\left|t\right|\big[\log\left|t\right|\big]^{2(p-1)}}\right),

for all
t\leq 0
when
n\geq 4
or
t\geq 0
when
n=2
, where
p\in(n,n+1]
, see Section
5
.

Our main results can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

When
n=2
, there exists a radial solution
u(t,\left|x\right|)
of equation
(\ref{eq1.1})
with
t\geq 0
, which has the form
u(t,x)\approx\omega\big(\left|x\right|-\rho(t)\big)+\phi(t,\left|x\right|)
, with

(1.15)

\rho(t)=\sqrt[4]{2t}+h(t).

Moreover,
h(t)
is a
C^{1}
function with the decay of order
O(\frac{1}{\log\left|t\right|})
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
and
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\phi(t,\left|x\right|)=0
uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
∎

Theorem 1.2
.

When
n\geq 4
, there exists an ancient solution
u(t,x)
of equation with
t\leq 0
,
which has the form
u(t,x)\approx\omega\big(\left|x\right|-\rho(t)\big)+\phi(t,\left|x\right|)
with

(1.16)

\rho(t)=\sqrt[4]{-2(n-3)(n-1)^{2}t}+h(t).

Moreover,
h(t)
is a
C^{1}
function with the decay of order
O(\frac{1}{\log\left|t\right|})
as
t\rightarrow-\infty
and
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\phi(t,\left|x\right|)=0
uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

We prove Theorem
1.2
by using Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method.
This method has been widely applied to solving the existence problem of solutions to various kinds of equations
[
6
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
19
]
.
Comparing to the previous works, we are facing
three difficulties during the process of dealing with problem (
1.1
) as the following:

(1)
.
Firstly, the main term
\omega\big(\left|x\right|-\rho(t)\big)
in (
1.14
)
is not a good approximate solution since it does not satisfy the boundary conditions in (

) and does not provide enough decay of the error.
To modify it, we introduce a cut-off function around the origin and a correction function,

) in Section

.
This main idea comes from
[

]
.

(2)
.
Secondly, equation (

) is a parabolic equation of fourth order, which does not satisfy the Maximum Principle since the heat kernel of the biharmonic parabolic operator is sign-changing, see

21
]
.
To overcome it, we employ the blow-up technique together with the representation of parabolic kernel in Section
3
.

(3)
.
Lastly, the correlative heat kernel of the linearization operator of the equation (
1.1
) is more intricate, which leads to hard task to get suitable a priori estimates of
linearized problem of equation (
1.1
).
To solve it, we modify the estimate of the error term

in (
2.18
) with an polynomial decay in Section
2

∎

2.1.
Some estimates of the basic layer

Before proving the main theorems, we first derive the decay estimates of the basic layer
\omega
, which is the solution to equation (
1.11
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\omega
be the solution of problem (
1.11
), then we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(y)-1+\beta e^{-\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}y}}{\beta^{2}e^{-2\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}y}}=\frac{W^{(3)}(1)}{6W^{\prime\prime}(1)};\\[8.53581pt]
\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{\omega(y)+1-\beta e^{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(-1)}y}}{\beta^{2}e^{2\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(-1)}y}}=-\frac{W^{(3)}(-1)}{6W^{\prime\prime}(-1)};\\[8.53581pt]
\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega^{\prime}(y)}{e^{-\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}y}}=\beta\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{\omega^{\prime}(y)}{e^{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(-1)}y}}=\beta\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(-1)},\end{array}\right.

where
W^{\prime\prime}(s)
and
W^{(3)}(s)
denote the derivatives of second and third orders of the function
W(s)
respectively,
and

(2.2)

\beta:=\exp\Bigg\{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}\int^{1}_{0}\Bigg[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\big(W(s)-W(1)\big)}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}\,(1-s)}\Bigg]\mathrm{d}s\Bigg\}.

Proof.

Since the function
W
satisfies the conditions in
(\ref{eq1.4})
,
especially
W^{\prime\prime}(1)=W^{\prime}(-1)>0
,
the above limits in
(\ref{eq2.0})
are well-defined and
\beta
is finite.
Recall that the inverse function of
\omega
is the function
\lambda(s)
given by (
1.12
). Using L’Hospital’s rule,
Taylor’s formula, the function
W\in C^{3,1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies (
1.2
) and (
1.12
), we find that

\lim_{s\rightarrow 1^{-}}\frac{1}{1-s}\Bigg\{\lambda(s)+\frac{\ln(1-s)}{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}}-\int^{1}_{0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(W(s)-W(1))}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}(1-s)}\mathrm{d}s\Bigg\}=-\frac{W^{(3)}(1)}{6(W^{\prime\prime}(1))^{3/2}}

and

\lim_{s\rightarrow(-1)^{+}}\frac{1}{1+s}\Bigg\{\lambda(s)-\frac{\ln(1+s)}{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(-1)}}-\int^{0}_{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(W(s)-W(1))}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}(1+s)}\mathrm{d}s\Bigg\}=\frac{W^{(3)}(-1)}{6(W^{\prime\prime}(-1))^{3/2}}.

According to the fact that if
x=\lambda(s)
, then
s=\omega(y)
, using Taylor’s formula again and the evenness of
W
in
(-1,1)
, we easily deduce that
the first two limits in (
2.12
) hold.
So we have

\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1-\omega(y)}{e^{-\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}y}}=\beta\ \ \text{and}\ \ \lim\limits_{y\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{1+\omega(y)}{e^{\sqrt{-W^{\prime\prime}(1)}y}}=\beta.

Using L’Hospital’s rule again, the condition (
1.2
) and the equation (
1.11
), we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega^{\prime}(y)}{e^{-\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}y}}=\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\Bigg\{\frac{\omega(y)-1}{-\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}e^{-\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}y}}\times\frac{W^{\prime}(\omega)-W^{\prime}(1)}{\omega(y)-1}\Bigg\}=\beta\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}

and

\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{\omega^{\prime}(y)}{e^{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(-1)}y}}=\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow-\infty}\Bigg\{\frac{\omega(y)+1}{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(-1)}e^{\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(-1)}y}}\times\frac{W^{\prime}(\omega)-W^{\prime}(-1)}{\omega(y)+1}\Bigg\}=\beta\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(-1)}.

∎

Next we consider the kernel of a fourth order linear operator. The main result is stated as the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
W(s)
be a smooth function satisfying the conditions in (
1.2
).
Then we have that any solution of the homogeneous problem

\big[\,\partial_{xx}-W^{\prime}(\omega(x))\,\big]^{2}\varphi=0,\quad\left|\varphi\right|\leq 1,\quad\left|\varphi_{xx}\right|\leq 1\qquad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R},

has the form

\varphi(x)=c\omega^{\prime}(x),

with some constant

.

For the proof of this lemma, see Lemma
3.6
which is a more general result.
∎

2.2.
The setting-up of the problem

We will prove that Theorem

holds.
Thus, we always assume that
n\geq 4
and
t<0
in the rest of the present paper.

Let
\widetilde{u}(t,\left|x\right|)
be a solution of (
1.1
), by a translation
u(t,\left|x\right|)=\widetilde{u}(t-T,\left|x\right|)
with some abuse of notation, then we have that
u(t,r)
satisfies the following problem

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}=-u_{rrrr}-\frac{2(n-1)}{r}u_{rrr}+\Big(2W^{\prime\prime}(u)-\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}\Big)u_{rr}+\Big(\frac{2(n-1)W^{\prime\prime}(u)}{r}+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{3}}\Big)u_{r}\\[8.53581pt]
\hskip 22.76228pt+W^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)u^{2}_{r}-W^{\prime}(u)W^{\prime\prime}(u),\qquad\ \ \forall\,(t,r)\in(-\infty,-T]\times(0,+\infty),\\[8.53581pt]
u_{rrr}(t,0)=u_{r}(t,0)=0,\hskip 66.86414pt\text{for all}\ t\in(-\infty,-T],\end{array}\right.

where
r=\left|x\right|
and
T
is a large positive number whose value can be adjusted at different steps.
For convenience, we denote the right hand side of the first equation
in (
2.3
) by
F(u)
, that is

(2.4)

\displaystyle F(u):=

\displaystyle-u_{rrrr}-\frac{2(n-1)}{r}u_{rrr}+\Big(2W^{\prime\prime}(u)-\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}\Big)u_{rr}

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{2(n-1)W^{\prime\prime}(u)}{r}+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{3}}\Big)u_{r}+W^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)u^{2}_{r}-W^{\prime}(u)W^{\prime\prime}(u).

Our purpose is to find a solution of (
2.3
) with the property

u(t,r)\approx\omega\big(r-\rho(t)\big),

where
\omega(y)
is the solution of problem (
1.11
).
Firstly, we notice that
\omega\big(r-\rho(t)\big)
does not satisfies the boundary conditions in (
2.3
).
A smooth cut-off function
\chi(r)
can be defined in the form

(2.5)

\chi(r)=0,\qquad\text{for}\ r\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\chi(r)=1,\qquad\text{for}\ r\geq\delta_{0},

for some small fixed positive number
\delta_{0}
.
We define the first approximate solution of (
2.3
) as the following

(2.6)

\widehat{\omega}(t,r)=\omega\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\chi(r)+\chi(r)-1.

Here we assume that the function
\rho(t)
has the from

(2.7)

\rho(t)=\gamma_{n}(t)+h(t),

where the function
h(t)=O((\log|t|)^{-1})
as
t\rightarrow-\infty

and the function
\gamma_{n}(t)
is defined in (
1.8
)-(
1.9
), i.e.

(2.8)

\gamma_{n}(t):=\sqrt[4]{-2(n-1)^{2}(n-3)t},\qquad t<0,

which is a radial solution of Willmore flow equation (
1.7
) with
n\geq 4
.
More precisely, we assume that
h(t)
satisfies the following constraint

(2.9)

\sup_{t\leq-1}\left|h(t)\right|+\sup_{t\leq-1}\left\{\frac{\left|t\right|}{\log\left|t\right|}\left|h^{\prime}(t)\right|\right\}\leq 1.

Secondly, by using (
1.11
), we have that

(2.10)

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\widehat{\omega}(t,r)+F(\widehat{\omega}(t,r))

\displaystyle=\rho^{\prime}(t)\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)-\frac{(n-3)(n-1)}{r^{2}}\omega^{\prime\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{3}}\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big),\qquad\text{for}\ r>\delta_{0},

where the operator
F(u)
is defined by (
2.4
).
By (
2.8
), we find that the second term in the right hand side of equality (
2.10
),
that is

-\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}\omega^{\prime\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big),

has a slow decay of order
O\left(\left|t\right|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)
as
t
goes to negative infinity.
However, it is not enough to solve equation
(
2.10
) since this term is much bigger than other terms in (
2.10
).
To cancel it and improve the approximate solution, inspired by
[
37
]
, we define a correction function

(2.11)

\widetilde{\omega}(y):=-\omega^{\prime}(y)\int_{0}^{y}\Bigg[(\omega^{\prime}(\hat{y}))^{-2}\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{y}}\frac{s(\omega^{\prime}(s))^{2}}{2}\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]{\mathrm{d}}\hat{y},

Then we have that

(2.12)

\displaystyle L^{*}(\widetilde{\omega}):=\big[-\partial_{yy}+W^{\prime\prime}(\omega(y))\big]\widetilde{\omega}(y)=\frac{1}{2}y\omega^{\prime}(y),\qquad(L^{*})^{2}\big[\widetilde{\omega}(y)\big]=-\omega^{\prime\prime}(y),\qquad\forall\,y\in\mathbb{R},

and
\widetilde{\omega}(y)
is an odd function with exponential decay such that

(2.13)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega^{\prime}(y)\widetilde{\omega}(y){\mathrm{d}}y=0\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\widetilde{\omega}(y)\right|\leq Ce^{-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\left|y\right|},\qquad\text{for}\ y\in\mathbb{R}.

At last, we define an approximate solution of problem (
2.3
) as the following

(2.14)

z(t,r):=\omega\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\chi(r)+\chi(r)-1+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}\widetilde{\omega}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\chi(r),

where the cut-off function
\chi(r)
and the function
\rho(t)
are given
by (
2.5
) and (
2.7
).

We will look for a solution of equation (
2.3
) of the form

(2.15)

u(t,r)=z(t,r)+\phi(t,r),

where
\phi
is a small perturbation term.
This can be done by using the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method in two steps.

(1).

The first step (see Sections
3
-
4
) is solving the following projected version of problem (
2.3
) in terms of
\phi(t,r)
:

(2.16)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}=L[\phi]+E(t,r)+N(\phi)-c(t)\partial_{r}\widehat{\omega}(t,r)\qquad\text{in}\ (-\infty,-T]\times(0,\infty),

and

(2.17)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t,r)\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)r^{n-1}{\mathrm{d}}r=0,\qquad\text{for all}\ t<-T,

where the function
\widehat{\omega}(t,r)
is defined by (
2.6
), the error term
E(t,r)
and nonlinear term

are defined respectively by

(2.18)

E(t,r):=F\big(z(t,r)\big)-\frac{\partial z(t,r)}{\partial t}

and

(2.19)

N(\phi):=F\big(z(t,r)+\phi(t,r)\big)-F\big(z(t,r)\big)-F^{\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)[\phi].

In the above,
F(u)
is defined by (
2.4
) and the linear operator
L[\phi]:=F^{\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)[\phi]
is defined as follows

(2.20)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)[\phi]:=

\displaystyle-\phi_{rrrr}-\frac{2(n-1)}{r}\phi_{rrr}+\Bigg[2W^{\prime\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)-\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}\Bigg]\phi_{rr}

\displaystyle-\big(W^{\prime\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)\big)^{2}\phi+\Bigg[\frac{2(n-1)W^{\prime\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)}{r}-\frac{(3-n)(n-1)}{r^{3}}\Bigg]\phi_{r}

\displaystyle+2W^{\prime\prime\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)\phi_{r}z_{r}-W^{\prime\prime\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)W^{\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)\phi+W^{(4)}\big(z(t,r)\big)\left|z_{r}\right|^{2}\phi

\displaystyle+2W^{\prime\prime\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)z_{rr}\phi+2\frac{n-1}{r}W^{\prime\prime\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)z_{r}\phi.

(2).

The second step is to choose the function
c(t)
in such a way that
\phi
satisfies the orthogonality condition (
2.17
), namely the following equality holds:

\displaystyle c(t)\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{r}\widehat{\omega}(t,r)\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)r^{n-1}{\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\omega^{\prime\prime\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)+\frac{n-1}{r}\omega^{\prime\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)-W^{\prime\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left(-\phi_{rr}-\frac{n-1}{r}\phi_{r}+W^{\prime\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)\phi\right)r^{n-1}{\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\partial_{rr}z(t,r)+\frac{n-1}{r}\partial_{r}z(t,r)-W^{\prime}\big(z(t,r)\big)\right]\phi\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)r^{n-1}{\mathrm{d}}r

(2.21)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t,r)\partial_{t}\big[\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\big]r^{n-1}{\mathrm{d}}r\ +\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\big(E(t,r)+N(\phi)\big)\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)r^{n-1}{\mathrm{d}}r,

for all
t<-T
. Later on, in Section
5
, we will choose

such that

.
This means that the function
u
in (
2.15
) will exactly solve (
2.3
).

2.3.
Estimates of the error terms

We will establish some estimates for the error term
E(t,r)
in (
2.18
).
By Taylor’s formula, the definitions in (
2.4
) and (
2.14
), we have that

\displaystyle E(t,r)=

\displaystyle F\big(z(t,r)\big)-\frac{\partial z(t,r)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F\big(\widehat{\omega}(t,r)+\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big)-\frac{\partial\big[\widehat{\omega}(t,r)+\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big]}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F\big(\widehat{\omega}(t,r)\big)+F^{\prime}\big(\widehat{\omega}(t,r)\big)\big[\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big]+F^{\prime\prime}\big(\widehat{\omega}(t,r)+\theta\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big)\big[\widetilde{z}(t,r),\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big]

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial\widetilde{z}(t,r)}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial\widehat{\omega}(t,r)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle E_{1}(t,r)+E_{2}(t,r),

where
\theta\in(0,1)
.
In the above, the operators
F(u)
and
F^{\prime}(u)[v]
are given by (
2.4
) and (
2.20
) respectively, and
the function
\widehat{\omega}(t,r)
is given by (
2.6
) and
\widetilde{z}(t,r)
is defined by

(2.22)

\displaystyle\widetilde{z}(t,r):=\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}\widetilde{\omega}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\chi(r),

with
\rho(t)
and
\chi(r)
given by (
2.7
) and (
2.5
) respectively.
The operator
F^{\prime\prime}(u)[v_{1},v_{2}]
is defined as the following

(2.23)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(u)[v_{1},v_{2}]:=

\displaystyle\Delta\big[W^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)v_{1}v_{2}\big]\ -\ \left\{W^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)W^{\prime\prime}(u)\ +\ W^{(4)}(u)\big[-\Delta u+W^{\prime}(u)\big]\right\}v_{1}v_{2}

\displaystyle+\ W^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)\Big\{\big[\Delta v_{1}-W^{\prime\prime}(u)v_{1}\big]v_{2}\ +\ \big[\Delta v_{2}-W^{\prime\prime}(u)v_{2}\big]v_{1}\Big\}.

The terms

and

have the following explicit forms

(2.24)

E_{1}(t,r):=F\big(\widehat{\omega}(t,r)\big)-\frac{\partial\widehat{\omega}(t,r)}{\partial t}+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}\partial_{rr}\widehat{\omega}(t,r)+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)^{2}}{2r^{3}}\partial_{r}\widehat{\omega}(t,r),

and

\displaystyle E_{2}(t,r):=

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}\partial_{rr}\widehat{\omega}(t,r)+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)^{2}}{2r^{3}}\partial_{r}\widehat{\omega}(t,r)\right]+F^{\prime}\big(\widehat{\omega}(t,r)\big)\big[\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big]

(2.25)

\displaystyle+F^{\prime\prime}\big(\widehat{\omega}(t,r)+\theta\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big)\big[\widetilde{z}(t,r),\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big]-\frac{\partial\widetilde{z}(t,r)}{\partial t}.

The main result is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha:=\sqrt{W^{\prime\prime}(1)}
,
p\in(n,n+1]
and
T>1
, we set

(2.26)

\Phi(t,r):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\log\left|t\right|}{\left|t\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{\big(1+\left|r-\gamma_{n}(t)-\frac{1}{4\alpha}\log\left|t\right|\right|\big)^{p}},\qquad\text{if}\ r\in\left[\delta_{0},+\infty\right);\\[11.38109pt]
\frac{\log\left|t\right|}{\left|t\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\overline{\chi}_{\left\{\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\leq r<\delta_{0}\right\}},\hskip 66.29497pt\text{if}\ r\in\left(0,\delta_{0}\right);\end{array}\right.

where the function
\gamma_{n}(t)
is defined by (
2.8
)
and
\delta_{0}
is a small positive number given in (
2.5
).
Here
\overline{\chi}_{A}
is the characteristic function of the set
A
.
Then there exists constant
C>0
which depends only on
\delta_{0}
,
\alpha
, and
n
, such that

|E(t,r)|\leq C\frac{\Phi(t,r)}{\log\left|t\right|},

for all
(t,r)\in(-\infty,-T]\times(0,+\infty)
.

Proof.

We first estimate the term
E_{1}(t,r)
in (
2.24
). By the definitions of
F(u)
and
\widehat{\omega}(t,r)
in (
2.4
) and (
2.6
), lemma
2.1
,
we have that

\displaystyle E_{1}(t,r)=

\displaystyle\left[\rho^{\prime}(t)+\frac{(n-1)^{2}(n-3)}{2r^{3}}\right]\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\overline{\chi}_{\left\{r\geq\delta_{0}\right\}}+O\left(\frac{1}{[\gamma_{n}(t)]^{2}}\right)\overline{\chi}_{\left\{\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}<r<\delta_{0}\right\}},

where
\overline{\chi}_{A}
is the characteristic function of the set
A
.
Furthermore, using the assumption of
\rho(t)
in (
2.7
)-(
2.9
), the definition of
\gamma_{n}(t)
in (
2.8
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have that

\displaystyle\left|E_{1}(t,r)\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\Bigg[\frac{1}{\left|t\right|^{\frac{3}{4}}}\overline{\chi}_{\left\{r\geq\delta_{0}\right\}}+\frac{1}{r^{3}}\overline{\chi}_{\left\{r\geq\frac{\gamma_{n}(t)}{2}\right\}}+\frac{1}{r^{3}}\overline{\chi}_{\left\{\frac{\gamma_{n}(t)}{2}\geq r\geq\delta_{0}\right\}}\Bigg]e^{-\alpha\left|r-\gamma_{n}(t)\right|}\,+\,\frac{C}{\left|t\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\overline{\chi}_{\left\{\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}<r<\delta_{0}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{e^{-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\left|r-\gamma_{n}(t)-\frac{1}{3\alpha}\log\left|t\right|\right|}}{\left|t\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\overline{\chi}_{\left\{r\geq\delta_{0}\right\}}+\frac{C}{r^{3}}e^{-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\left|r-\gamma_{n}(t)\right|}\overline{\chi}_{\left\{\frac{\gamma_{n}(t)}{2}\geq r\geq\delta_{0}\right\}}+\frac{C}{\left|t\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\overline{\chi}_{\left\{\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}<r<\delta_{0}\right\}}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{\Phi(t,r)}{\log\left|t\right|},\qquad\text{ for all}\ (t,r)\in(-\infty,-T]\times(0,+\infty),

where
C
is a positive constant only depending on
\delta_{0}
,
\alpha
and
n
. Here we used the fact that there exits a positive constant
C>0
only depending on
\delta_{0}
,
\alpha
and
n
such that

(2.28)

\frac{1}{r^{3}}e^{-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\left|r-\gamma_{n}(t)\right|}\overline{\chi}_{\left\{\frac{\gamma_{n}(t)}{2}\geq r\geq\delta_{0}\right\}}\leq Ce^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}\gamma_{n}(t)}\quad\text{and}\quad e^{-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\left|x\right|}\leq\frac{C}{\big(1+\left|x\right|\big)^{p}},

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in(n,n+1]
, where
C>0
does not depend on
x
and
t
.

Next we consider the term
E_{2}(t,r)
in (
2.25
). Using the definitions of linear operators
F^{\prime}(u)[v]
and

in (

) and (

\displaystyle E_{2}(t,r)=

\displaystyle\Bigg\{F^{\prime}\big(\omega\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\big)\big[\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big]-\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}\omega^{\prime\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)^{2}}{2r^{3}}\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)

\displaystyle+F^{\prime\prime}\big(\omega\big(r-\rho(t)\big)+\theta\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big)\big[\widetilde{z}(t,r),\widetilde{z}(t,r)\big]+\rho^{\prime}(t)\omega^{\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}\Bigg\}\overline{\chi}_{\left\{r\geq\delta_{0}\right\}}

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{1}{[\gamma_{n}(t)]^{2}}\right)\overline{\chi}_{\left\{\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}<r<\delta_{0}\right\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg\{\left[-\Big(\partial_{rr}-W^{\prime\prime}\big(\omega\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\big)\Big)^{2}\widetilde{\omega}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)-\omega^{\prime\prime}\big(r-\rho(t)\big)\right]\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{r^{2}}

Remark 2.4
.

According to the above proof of Lemma
2.3
, it is easy to find that the error term

has an exponentially decaying in space variable

(2.29)

\left|E(t,r)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left|t\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}\left|r-\gamma_{n}(t)-\frac{1}{4\alpha}\log\left|t\right|\right|}},

Our goal is to solve nonlinear problem (

)-(

).
Hence, according to (

-\partial_{t}\varphi+F^{\prime}(z(t,r))[\varphi]=f(t,r),\qquad(t,r)\in(-\infty,-T)\times(0,+\infty),

with the function

3.
The Linear Problem

In this section, firstly, we will obtain the solvability of a class of semilinear biharmonic parabolic equations by applying
some properties of biharmonic heat kernel and fixed-point arguments, which is given in Proposition
3.4
.
Secondly, we will prove that the linear projected problem (
3.60
) is solvable by using Proposition
3.4
and a priori estimate in Lemma
3.5
. The main result is given by Proposition
3.9
.

3.1.
A few results of linear parabolic equations with a biharmonic operator

We first collect some known results for homogeneous biharmonic parabolic equation, from
[
21
,
23
,
24
]
. Its solution can be represented by the convolution of a biharmonic heat kernel and initial function.

Proposition 3.1
.

We consider the following Cauchy problem for the biharmonic heat equation:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{2}u=0\qquad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}:=(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\[5.69054pt]
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\hskip 8.5359pt\qquad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

where
n\geq 1
and
u_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then (
3.1
) admits a unique global in time solution explicitly given by

u(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{0}(y)p_{n}(t,x-y){\mathrm{d}}y,\qquad p_{n}(t,x):=\bar{\alpha}_{n}t^{-n/4}f_{n}\Big(\frac{\left|x\right|}{t^{1/4}}\Big),\qquad\forall\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}.

Here
p_{n}(t,x)
is called the biharmonic heat kernel and
\bar{\alpha}_{n}
denotes a suitable positive normalization number which depends on
n
and satisfies

\bar{\alpha}_{n}t^{-n/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{n}\Big(\frac{\left|y\right|}{t^{1/4}}\Big){\mathrm{d}}y=1,\qquad\text{for all}\ t>0.

Moreover, the function
f_{n}
is given by

f_{n}(s):=s^{1-n}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\varrho^{4}}(s\varrho)^{\frac{n}{2}}J_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(s\varrho){\mathrm{d}}\varrho,\qquad\text{for}\ s>0,

where
J_{\nu}
denotes the
\nu
-th Bessel function of the first kind.
There exist two constants
K_{n}
and
\mu_{n}
depending on
n
such that

(3.2)

\left|f_{n}(s)\right|\leq K_{n}\exp(-\mu_{n}s^{4/3}),\qquad\text{for all}\ s\geq 0.

For the derivative of
f_{n}
, the following formula holds

(3.3)

f^{\prime}_{n}(s)=-sf_{n+2}(s).

∎

Next we will use the above results to study the following inhomogeneous problem:

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{2}u=f(t,x)\qquad\text{in}\ (t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\[5.69054pt]
u(t_{0},x)=u_{0}(x)\hskip 48.36958pt\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

where
f(t,x)\in L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 3.2
.

Assume that
t_{1}\in(t_{0},+\infty)
. We say that
u\in L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a mild solution of (
3.4
) if

(3.5)

u(t,x)=\Gamma_{t-t_{0}}[u_{0}](x)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\Gamma_{t-\tau}[f(\tau,\cdot)](x){\mathrm{d}}\tau,\qquad\text{for}\ (t,x)\in(t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Here
\Gamma_{t}
is a linear operator defined by

\Gamma_{t}[u](x):=\Big(p_{n}(t,\cdot)\ast u\Big)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p_{n}(t,x-y)u(y){\mathrm{d}}y,

where the biharmonic heat kernel
p_{n}(t,x)
is given by Proposition
3.1
.
In addition, when
t_{1}=+\infty
, a mild solution also can be defined by
(
3.5
) provided
u(t,x)\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(t_{0},+\infty)\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Notice that it is not hard to verify that
u
is a mild solution of (
3.4
) if and only if
u
solves it in the pointwise sense.
We give some regularity estimates for mild solutions.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
u
be a mild solution of problem (
3.4
). Assume that
t_{1}<+\infty
. Then the following estimates are valid:

•

If
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for every
t_{*}\in(t_{0},t_{1})
,
t_{*}>0
,
\vartheta\in(0,4)
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, it holds that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(t_{*},t_{1})}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|u(\cdot,x)\|_{C^{\theta}(t_{*},t_{1})}\leq C_{1}\Big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{*},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big),

for some positive constant
C_{1}
depending on
n,\theta,t_{1}-t_{0},t_{1}-t_{*},\vartheta
and
t_{*}
.

•

If
u_{0}\in C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
\vartheta\in(0,4)
and
t_{0}>0
, then it holds

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(t_{0},t_{1})}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|u(\cdot,x)\|_{C^{\frac{\vartheta}{4}}(t_{0},t_{1})}\leq C_{2}\Big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{0}\|_{C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big),

for some positive constant
C_{2}
depending on
t_{1}-t_{0},n
and
\vartheta
.

Proof.

According to the formula (
3.3
) and some direct computations, we can derive some estimates for the derivatives of biharmonic heat kernel
p_{n}(t,x)
as follows:

•

\left|\partial_{t}p_{n}(t,x)\right|\leq C_{n}\Big(t^{-1}\left|p_{n}(t,x)\right|+t^{-1}\left|x\right|^{2}\left|p_{n+2}(t,x)\right|\Big)
;

•

\left|\nabla_{x}p_{n}(t,x)\right|\leq C_{n}\left|x\right|\left|p_{n+2}(t,x)\right|
;

•

\left|\nabla^{2}_{x}p_{n}(t,x)\right|\leq C_{n}\Big(\left|p_{n+2}(t,x)\right|+\left|x\right|^{2}\left|p_{n+4}(t,x)\right|\Big)
;

•

\left|\nabla^{3}_{x}p_{n}(t,x)\right|\leq C_{n}\Big(\left|x\right|\left|p_{n+4}(t,x)\right|+\left|x\right|^{3}\left|p_{n+6}(t,x)\right|\Big)
;

•

\left|\nabla^{4}_{x}p_{n}(t,x)\right|\leq C_{n}\Big(\left|p_{n+4}(t,x)\right|+\left|x\right|^{2}\left|p_{n+6}(t,x)\right|+\left|x\right|^{4}\left|p_{n+8}(t,x)\right|\Big)
,

where
C_{n}>0
only depends on
n
.

We decompose the mild solution in (
3.5
) as follows

u(t,x)=U_{1}(t,x)+U_{2}(t,x),

where

U_{1}(t,x)=\Gamma_{t-t_{0}}[u_{0}](x),\quad U_{2}(t,x)=\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\Gamma_{t-\tau}[f(\tau,\cdot)](x){\mathrm{d}}\tau.

The analysis will begin with the first term
U_{1}(t,x)
.
For any
t>t_{0}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, using the above estimates and (
3.2
), we derive that

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{t}U_{1}(t,x)\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}p_{n}(t-t_{0},x-y)u_{0}(y){\mathrm{d}}y\right|\leq\frac{C_{n}}{t-t_{0}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Moreover, using (
3.2
), we have that

\left|U_{1}(t,x)\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|p_{n}(t-t_{0},x-y)u_{0}(y)\right|{\mathrm{d}}y\leq C_{n}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

For any
\vartheta\in(0,4)
and
t,t^{1}\in(t_{0},t_{1})
, using the fact
p_{n}(t,x)=t^{-n/4}p_{n}(\frac{x}{t^{1/4}},1)
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\left|U_{1}(t,x)-U_{1}(t^{1},x)\right|}{\left|t-t^{1}\right|^{\frac{\vartheta}{4}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u_{0}\big((t-t_{0})^{1/4}y\big)\,-\,u_{0}\big((t^{1}-t_{0})^{1/4}y\big)}{\left|t-t^{1}\right|^{\frac{\vartheta}{4}}}p_{n}(1,y){\mathrm{d}}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|p_{n}(1,y)\right|\left|y\right|^{\vartheta}{\mathrm{d}}y\leq C_{n}\|u_{0}\|_{C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Here we have used the inequality
a^{\vartheta/4}-b^{\vartheta/4}\leq(a-b)^{\vartheta/4}
for any
a\geq b\geq 0
and
\vartheta\in(0,4)
in the second inequality,
C_{n}>0
only depends on
n
.

Moreover, for the derivatives of
U_{1}
with respect to
x
, by (
3.2
), we have that

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}U_{1}(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla_{x}p_{n}(t-t_{0},x-y)u_{0}(y){\mathrm{d}}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|y\right|\left|p_{n+2}(y,t-t_{0})\right|{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{n}}{(t-t_{0})^{1/4}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
C_{n}
does only depend on
n
.
Similarly, we can get that

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{2}_{x}U_{1}(t,x)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{n}}{(t-t_{0})^{1/2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\qquad\left|\nabla^{3}_{x}U_{1}(t,x)\right|\leq\frac{C_{n}}{(t-t_{0})^{3/4}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

and

\left|\nabla^{4}_{x}U_{1}(t,x)\right|\leq\frac{C_{n}}{(t-t_{0})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Thus for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have

[U_{1}]_{\theta}(t):=\frac{\left|U_{1}(t,x)-U_{1}(t,y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{\theta}}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}2\|U_{1}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\ \quad\ \quad\text{if}\ \left|x-y\right|\geq 1,\\[5.69054pt]
\|\nabla_{x}U_{1}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\qquad\text{if}\ \left|x-y\right|\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

Hence, for any
t_{*}>t_{0}
, we have

\sup_{t\in(t_{*},t_{1})}[U_{1}]_{\theta}(t)\leq\frac{C_{n}}{(t_{*}-t_{0})^{1/4}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

By similar arguments, we can obtain the following estimates

\sup_{t\in(t_{*},t_{1})}[\nabla U_{1}]_{\theta}(t)\leq\frac{C_{n}}{(t_{*}-t_{0})^{1/2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\qquad\sup_{t\in(t_{*},t_{1})}[\nabla^{2}U_{1}]_{\theta}(t)\leq\frac{C_{n}}{(t_{*}-t_{0})^{3/4}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

and

\sup_{t\in(t_{*},t_{1})}[\nabla^{3}U_{1}]_{\theta}(t)\leq\frac{C_{n}}{(t_{*}-t_{0})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Combining the above estimates, we have that for any
\vartheta\in(0,4)
,
t_{*}>t_{0}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
,

(3.9)

\sup_{t\in(t_{*},t_{1})}\|U_{1}(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|U_{1}(\cdot,x)\|_{C^{\theta}(t_{*},t_{1})}\leq C_{n}\big[1+\varrho(t_{*}-t_{0})\big]\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where
\varrho(t_{*}-t_{0})=\frac{1}{(t_{*}-t_{0})}+\frac{1}{(t_{*}-t_{0})^{1/4}}+\frac{1}{(t_{*}-t_{0})^{\theta}}
.

Using the formula

U_{1}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p_{n}(t-t_{0},y)u_{0}(x-y){\mathrm{d}}y

and (
3.8
), for
\vartheta\in(0,4)
, we have

(3.10)

\sup_{t\in(t_{0},t_{1})}\|U_{1}(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|U_{1}(\cdot,x)\|_{C^{\frac{\vartheta}{4}}(t_{0},t_{1})}\leq C_{n}\|u_{0}\|_{C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
C_{n}
is a positive constant which depends on
n,\vartheta
.

Next we will estimate
U_{2}(t,x)
. First we find that

\displaystyle\left|U_{2}(t,x)\right|\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|p_{n}(t-\tau,x-y)f(\tau,y)\right|{\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}(t_{1}-t_{0})\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}((t_{1},t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}).

For any
t,t^{1}\in(t_{0},t_{1})
, by the previous estimate of
\partial_{t}p_{n}(t,x)
, we have

\left|p_{n}(t,x)-p_{n}(t^{1},x)\right|\leq C_{n}\left|\log t-\log t^{1}\right|\Big(\left|p_{n}\big((1-\theta_{1})t+\theta_{1}t^{1},x\big)\right|+\left|x\right|^{2}\left|p_{n+2}\big((1-\theta_{1})t+\theta_{1}t^{1},x\big)\right|\Big),

for some
\theta_{1}\in(0,1)
.
Using the above inequality and (
3.2
), for
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left|U_{2}(t,x)-U_{2}(t^{1},x)\right|}{\left|t-t^{1}\right|^{\theta}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[p_{n}(t-\tau,x-y)-p_{n}(t^{1}-\tau,x-y)\Big]f(y,\tau){\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}\tau\right|}{\left|t-t^{1}\right|^{\theta}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left|\int^{t}_{t^{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p_{n}(t^{1}-\tau,x-y)f(\tau,y){\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}\tau\right|}{\left|t-t^{1}\right|^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}\Bigg(1+\log\frac{t_{1}-t_{0}}{\left|t-t^{1}\right|}\Bigg)\left|t-t^{1}\right|^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{1},t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
C_{n}
is a positive constant which only depends on
n
.

Second, we study the derivatives of
U_{2}(t,x)
with respect to
x
. By some direct computations and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}U_{2}(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla_{x}p_{n}(t-\tau,x-y)f(\tau,y){\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|y\right|\left|p_{n+2}(t-\tau,y)\right|{\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-1/4}{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}(t-t_{0})^{3/4}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

By the same arguments as above, we can derive that

\left|\nabla^{2}_{x}U_{2}(t,x)\right|\leq C_{n}(t-t_{0})^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})},\qquad\left|\nabla^{3}_{x}U_{2}(t,x)\right|\leq C_{n}(t-t_{0})^{1/4}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})},

and
for any
\theta\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\nabla^{3}_{x}U_{2}(t,x_{1})-\nabla^{3}_{x}U_{2}(t,x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\theta}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}\frac{\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{n+3}{4}}\Big[\nabla^{3}_{x}f_{n}\Big(\frac{\left|x_{1}-y\right|}{(t-\tau)^{1/4}}\Big)-\nabla^{3}_{x}f_{n}\Big(\frac{\left|x_{2}-y\right|}{(t-\tau)^{1/4}}\Big)\Big]f(y,\tau){\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}\tau\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\frac{\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\Big[\nabla^{3}_{x}f_{n}\Big(\left|y-\frac{x_{1}}{(t-\tau)^{1/4}}\right|\Big)-\nabla^{3}_{x}f_{n}\Big(\left|y-\frac{x_{2}}{(t-\tau)^{1/4}}\right|\Big)\Big]f\Big((t-\tau)^{1/4}y,\tau\Big){\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}\tau\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}(t-t_{0})^{(1-\theta)/4}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Combing the above estimates of
U_{2}
, for any
\vartheta\in(0,4)
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have that

(3.11)

\sup_{t\in(t_{0},t_{1})}\|U_{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|U_{2}(\cdot,x)\|_{C^{\theta}(t_{0},t_{1})}\leq C_{n}\Big[\upsilon(t_{1}-t_{0})+(t_{1}-t_{0})^{1-\theta}\Big]\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

On the other hand, for
\vartheta\in(0,4)
, there holds

(3.12)

\sup_{t\in(t_{0},t_{1})}\|U_{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{\vartheta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|U_{2}(\cdot,x)\|_{C^{\frac{\vartheta}{4}}(t_{0},t_{1})}\leq C_{n}\upsilon(t_{1}-t_{0})\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\upsilon(t-t_{0})=(t-t_{0})+(t-t_{0})^{1-\frac{\vartheta}{4}}
and
C_{n}
is a positive constant which depends on
n
.

The Proposition
3.3
follows from (
3.9
), (
3.10
), (
3.11
) and (
3.12
).
∎

We will apply the above proposition to study the solvability of a class of semilinear parabolic equations.
Let
u_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and we consider the initial value problem

(3.13)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{2}u=G[\Delta u,\nabla u,u,t,x]\qquad\text{in}\ (t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\[5.69054pt]
u(t_{0},x)=u_{0}(x)\hskip 95.3169pt\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

where
G[p,\vec{q},s,t,x]:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times[t_{0},+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function satisfying

(1)

For every
M>0
and
T>t_{0}
, there exists
C_{T,M}>0
such that

\Big|G[p,\vec{q},s,t,x]\Big|\leq C_{T,M}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t\in[t_{0},T]
and
p,\left|\vec{q}\right|,s\in[-M,M]
.

(2)

There is a constant
\sigma>0
in such a way that

(3.14)

\Big|G[p_{1},\vec{q}_{1},s_{1},t,x]-G[p_{2},\vec{q}_{2},s_{2},t,x]\Big|\leq\sigma\Big(\left|p_{1}-p_{2}\right|+\left|\vec{q}_{1}-\vec{q}_{2}\right|+\left|s_{1}-s_{2}\right|\Big),

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t\geq t_{0}
,
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\vec{q}_{1},\vec{q}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.

In particular, we can take

(3.15)

G[\Delta u,\nabla u,u,t,x]=a(t,x)\Delta u+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b^{i}(t,x)\nabla_{x_{i}}u+c(t,x)u+g(t,x),

where those functions
a(t,x)
,
b^{1}(t,x),\cdots,b^{n}(t,x)
,
c(t,x)
are all belonging to
L^{\infty}([t_{0},+\infty),C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
g\in L^{\infty}([t_{0},+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Assume that
u(t,x)
satisfies that

\Delta u,\ \left|\nabla u\right|,\ u\in L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}),

then by (
3.5
),
u
is a mild solution of
(
3.13
) if and only if

(3.16)

u(t,x)=\Gamma_{t-t_{0}}[u_{0}](x)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\Gamma_{t-\tau}\Big[G[\Delta u,\nabla u,u,\tau,\cdot](\tau,\cdot)\Big](x){\mathrm{d}}\tau\qquad\text{for}\ (t,x)\in(t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Notice that
G[\Delta u,\nabla u,u,t,x]\in L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
by the assumptions on
u
and
G
.
Thus (
3.16
) is well defined by Proposition
3.3
.

Now we devote to the study of the solvability of problem (
3.13
).
For convenience, we define a map
\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}

from
L^{\infty}[(t_{0},t_{1});C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]
to itself as follows

\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}[u](t,x):=\Gamma_{t-t_{0}}[u_{0}](x)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\Gamma_{t-\tau}\Big[G[\Delta u,\nabla u,u](\cdot,\tau)\Big](x){\mathrm{d}}\tau\qquad\text{for}\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(t_{0},t_{1}),

for
u_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u\in L^{\infty}[(t_{0},t_{1});C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]
. The main result is the following:

Proposition 3.4
.

Given any
u_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a unique mild solution
u
to problem (
3.13
) with
t_{1}=+\infty
. Moreover, if
u_{0}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then the solution
u
has the property: for
s>t_{0}
, there exists
C
independent of
t
such that

(3.17)

\sup_{t\in(t_{0},s)}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\Big[(s-t_{0})+(s-t_{0})^{1/2}\Big]\|G(0,0,0,x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},s)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{0}\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big).

Proof.

Notice that this proposition is equivalent to that problem (
3.13
) has a unique solution
u
for any
t_{1}\in(t_{0},+\infty)
.
According to the definition in (
3.5
),
u
is a mild solution of (
3.13
) if and only if
u
is a fixed point of the map
\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}
. Thus we will study the existence and uniqueness of fixed point for the map
\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}
.

Using Proposition
3.3
and the assumptions on
G
, we have that
\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}
is an operator from the space
L^{\infty}[(t_{0},t_{1});C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]
to itself.
We claim that the map
\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}
is a contraction when
T_{1}:=t_{1}-t_{0}
is small enough.

Indeed, by Proposition
3.1
, the assumptions on
G
in (
3.14
) and some direct computations, we have that for any
u,w\in L^{\infty}[(t_{0},t_{1});C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]
,

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}[u](t,x)-\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}[w](t,x)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|p_{n}(t-\tau,x-y)\right|\cdot\Big|G[\Delta u,\nabla u,u](\tau,y)-G[\Delta w,\nabla w,w](\tau,y)\Big|{\mathrm{d}}y{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\sigma T_{1}C_{n}\Big(\|\Delta u-\Delta w\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla u-\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u-w\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big),

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t\in(t_{0},t_{1})
, where
C_{n}
is a positive number which only depends on
n
.
Hence, we can choose
T_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2\sigma C_{n}}
such that

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}[u](t,x)-\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}[w](t,x)\big\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\Delta u-\Delta w\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla u-\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u-w\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big).

Thus, the map
\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}}
is a contraction.

Next we consider the following iterative sequence

u_{m}(t,x)=(\mathcal{N}_{G,u_{0}})^{m}[0](t,x),

by Banach Fixed Theorem, then there is
u\in L^{\infty}[(t_{0},t_{1});C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]
such that
u_{m}(x,t)
converges to

Lemma 3.6

Considering the Hilbert space

Proof.

For any
\phi\in H
, since
\omega^{\prime}>0

\phi(y)=\zeta_{1}(y)\omega^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi^{\prime\prime}(y)-W^{\prime\prime}(\omega)\phi(y)\right|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}y=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\omega^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)\zeta_{1}(y)+2\zeta^{\prime}_{1}(y)\omega^{\prime\prime}(y)+\zeta^{\prime\prime}_{1}(y)\omega^{\prime}(y)-W^{\prime\prime}(\omega)\zeta_{1}(y)\omega^{\prime}(y)\right|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|2\omega^{\prime\prime}(y)\zeta_{1}^{\prime}(y)+\omega^{\prime}(y)\zeta_{1}^{\prime\prime}(y)\right|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}y\geq 0,

\omega^{\prime\prime\prime}=W^{\prime\prime}(\omega)\omega^{\prime}

Hence we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi^{\prime\prime}(y)-W^{\prime\prime}(\omega)\phi(y)\right|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}y=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\ 2\omega^{\prime\prime}(y)\zeta_{1}^{\prime}(y)+\omega^{\prime}(y)\zeta_{1}^{\prime\prime}(y)=0.

All solutions of the last equation have the following form

\zeta_{1}(x)=c_{1}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{(\omega^{\prime}(y))^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}y+c_{2},\qquad c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

Since

we have that

and

.

Now we assume that there exists a sequence

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi_{m}\right|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}y=1\ \ \text{and}\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi^{\prime\prime}_{m}-W^{\prime\prime}(\omega)\phi_{m}\right|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}y\leq\frac{1}{m}.

Thus,

in

5.2.

In the rest of this section we will study the ODE in (

It is easily to check that if

\alpha

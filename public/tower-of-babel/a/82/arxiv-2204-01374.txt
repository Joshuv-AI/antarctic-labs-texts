Silting, cosilting, and extensions of commutative ring
Simion Breaz, Michal Hrbek, George Ciprian Modoi

Abstract
. We study the transfer of (co)silting objects in derived categories of module categories via the extension functors induced by a morphism of commutative rings. It is proved that the extension functors preserve (co)silting objects of (co)finite type. In many cases the bounded silting property descends along faithfully flat ring extensions. In particular, the notion of bounded silting complex is Zariski local.

1.
Introduction

Silting theory provides useful tools in the study of various triangulated categories. We refer to
[
2
]
and
[
33
]
for details about the influence of this. In particular, it studies
t
-structures with special properties that provide good approximations. In the case of the unbounded derived category of a module category these
t
-structures are induced by some objects (called silting, respectively cosilting, objects) that can be interpreted as generalizations of tilting complexes (in particular, they also generalize the
n
-tilting modules),
[
5
]
,
[
41
]
. In
[
4
]
it is shown that if we are in the derived category of a commutative ring, the silting complexes of length
2
are related with other structures associated to that ring (Gabriel filters, torsion theories of finite type, the spectrum, cf.
[
20
]
. Moreover, it was proved in
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
that there exists a strong connection between the spectrum of the ring and the
t
-structures associated to (co)silting objects in the unbounded derived category.

Since we have such a connection, it is natural to ask if the silting property is Zariski local or, even more, if it is an ad-property (see §
2.2
for the relevant definitions). It is already known that the properties of being projective,
1
-tilting and
2
-silting are ad-properties (cf.
[
29
]
,
[
19
]
, and
[
8
]
). The Zariski locality for
n
-tilting modules was also proved in
[
19
]
. In order to provide answers for this question, we will study transfers, induced by a morphism
\lambda:R\to S
of commutative rings, of the (co)silting properties of objects from
\mathbf{D}({R})
or
\mathbf{D}({S})
. More precisely, we will consider the algebraic transfer that uses the derived functors
-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S:\mathbf{D}({R})\to\mathbf{D}({S})
and
\RHom_{R}(S,-):\mathbf{D}({R})\to\mathbf{D}({S})
of the covariant, respectively contravariant, extension functors associated to
\lambda
, and the topological transfer (for cosilting objects of cofinite type) via the canonical map
\lambda^{\star}:\mathrm{Spec}({S})\to\mathrm{Spec}({R})
. In this way we continue the investigations realized in
[
19
]
for the case of tilting modules and in
[
8
]
for silting complexes of length
2
. For other contributions in the study of the transfer of some related properties via ring extensions we refer to the already mentioned paper
[
29
]
(projective modules), but also to
[
12
]
(injective modules),
[
28
]
(compact tilting complexes) and
[
39
]
(
n
-tilting and
n
-cotilting modules).

We will prove in Theorem
4.2
that the silting property
ascends
along
\lambda
(i.e., the functor
-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
preserves silting objects), and that the same is true for pure-injective cosilting objects via the functor
\RHom_{R}(S,-)
. Moreover, these functors preserve the (co)finiteness type properties, see Theorem
4.3
. For (co)silting objects of (co)finite type we can also apply a topological transfer since
\lambda^{\star}
is continuous with respect to Hochster’s topology, Theorem
5.4
. In fact, this transfer coincides up to equivalence to that realized by using the derived extension functors. Moreover, if
\lambda
is faithfully flat then
\lambda^{\star}
is also closed, and this allows us to identify all (co)silting complexes of (co)finite type from
\mathbf{D}({S})
that can be obtained, up to equivalence, by ascending a (co)silting object from
\mathbf{D}({R})
.

On the other side, as in the case of
n
-tilting modules (cf.
[
19
]
), it is not clear if the descent of the (co)silting property is valid for all commutative rings. More precisely, the (co)silting property
descends
with respect to a faithfully flat morphism
\lambda:R\to S
of commutative rings if the functor
-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
reflects the silting objects (respectively,
\RHom_{R}(S,-)
reflects the cosilting objects). In the last part of the paper we study the property for bounded (co)silting complexes. Even though we are not able to prove the descent property for all bounded complexes over commutative rings, we can indicate many situations when it is valid. We prove in Theorem
5.12
that the cosilting property descends for complexes that are duals of complexes of projectives. Note that this result is satisfactory for noetherian rings since in this case all bounded cosilting complexes have this form, up to equivalence. For the silting case, we prove in Corollary
6.9
that the bounded silting property is Zariski local. Moreover, we provide in Remark
6.11
an extensive list of rings for which the silting property descends for bounded complexes of projectives, cf. Proposition
6.4
. This list includes all noetherian rings, all rings that are of finite injective dimension or finite pure global dimension, in particular all rings of cardinality
\aleph_{n}
for some integer
n\geq 0
. Also, the
n
-tilting property descends for these rings (Corollary
6.5
).

Unfortunately, we were not able to prove that the properties used in Lemma
3.2
to characterize (co)silting objects in
\mathbf{D}({R})
descend along faithfully flat morphisms (in
[
8
]
and
[
19
]
it is proved that these properties descend together for complexes of length
2
and for
1
-tilting modules, but the proofs cannot be extended to general bounded complexes). In order to avoid this obstruction, we use two results that can be of independent interest. More precisely, in Theorem
3.11
it is proven that for bounded silting complexes the closure of the class
T^{\perp_{>0}}
with respect to direct sums can be replaced by the weaker condition
\mathrm{Add}(T)\subseteq T^{\perp_{>0}}
. This generalizes a recent result proved for
n
-tilting modules by Positselski and Šťovíček
[
34
, §2]
. The other one is presented in Theorem
6.10
, where it is proved that if
R
is a commutative ring such that
\mathbf{D}({R})
coincides with its smallest localizing subcategory that contains all pure-injective objects then for every injective morphism of rings
\lambda:R\to S
that is pure, the smallest localizing subcategory of
\mathbf{D}({R})
that contains
S
is
\mathbf{D}({R})
.

Although in this paper we are mostly interested in commutative rings, for the descending property of silting objects we also need to consider a non-commutative setting: Marks and Vitória had notice in
[
27
]
that every bounded silting complex can be interpreted as a tilting module in the category of representations of a Dynkin quiver. Therefore, in Section
3
the results will be presented for general rings. In Section
4
we study the ascent properties for silting and cosilting for some morphisms that are induced by a ring morphism whose domain is commutative (note that for general rings the property to be silting does not ascend, e. g.
[
8
, Example 2.5]
). The next two sections are dedicated to extensions induced by morphisms of commutative rings. In Section
5
we study the transfer of (co)silting complexes of cofinite type by using both the algebraic and topological transfers mentioned above. In the last section we study the descent property for bounded silting complexes.

If
R
is a ring then
\hbox{\rm Mod-}R
will denote the category of all right
R
-modules, and
\mathbf{D}({R})
will be the unbounded derived category of
\hbox{\rm Mod-}R
. We recall that
\mathbf{D}({R})
is a triangulated category, and we will denote by
{\_}[1]
the shift. The
i
-th homology functor is denoted by
H^{i}:\mathbf{D}({R})\to Ab
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Derived functors

Let
R
and
S
be two rings and let
X\in\mathbf{D}({R^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S})
be a complex of
(R,S)
-bimodules. Then the derived tensor product

-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}X:\mathbf{D}({R})\to\mathbf{D}({S})

is the left adjoint of the derived hom functor

\RHom_{S}(X,-):\mathbf{D}({S})\to\mathbf{D}({R}).

The link between the right derived hom functor and the usual hom functor is expressed by the natural isomorphism

H^{i}\RHom_{R}(X,Y)=\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(X,Y[i]).

Moreover the adjunction isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({S})}(-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}X,-)\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(-,\RHom_{S}(X,-))

has an enriched version

\RHom_{S}(-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}X,-)\cong\RHom_{R}(-,\RHom_{S}(X,-)),

the initial isomorphism being recovered by taking
H^{0}
.
Letting
X
to run over all objects in
\mathbf{D}({R^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S})
we get two bifunctors. In particular, if
R
is commutative, the derived tensor product induces a monoidal structure on the category
\mathbf{D}({R})
, the unit being
R
itself, and the internal hom being
\RHom_{R}(-,-)
.

Using these functors one can define two dualities on
\mathbf{D}({R})
, namely

(\ )^{*}:=\RHom_{R}(-,R):\mathbf{D}({R})\to\mathbf{D}({R^{\mathrm{op}}})

and

(\ )^{+}:=\RHom_{\mathbb{Z}}(-,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}):\mathbf{D}({R})\to\mathbf{D}({R^{\mathrm{op}}}).

We denote by the same symbols the respective dualities going in the other sense, namely
\mathbf{D}({R^{\mathrm{op}}})\to\mathbf{D}({R})
.

We record some basic properties of these functors. Recall that an object
C\in\mathbf{D}({R})
is
compact
if the covariant functor
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(C,-)
commutes with respect to direct sums (this definition is valid for general triangulated categories). In the case of
\mathbf{D}({R})
the compact objects are those objects that are isomorphic to bounded complexes of finitely generated projective modules.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
3
, Lemma 21]
. Let
X,C\in\mathbf{D}({R})
and
Y\in\mathbf{D}({R^{\mathrm{op}}})
, with
C
compact. Then:

(1)

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
H^{n}(X^{+})\cong H^{-n}(X)^{+}
and
H^{n}(X^{+})=0
if and only if
H^{-n}(X)=0
.

(2)

There are natural isomorphisms

\RHom_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}(Y,X^{+})\cong(X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}Y)^{+}\cong\RHom_{R}(X,Y^{+}),

\RHom_{R}(C,X)\cong X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}C^{*},

and

\RHom_{R}(C,X)^{+}\cong C\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}X^{+}.

2.2.
Local properties of objects of
\mathbf{D}({R})

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be some property which can be satisfied by an object of the unbounded derived category of a commutative ring. Given a commutative ring
R
, let
\mathfrak{P}_{R}
denote the full subcategory of objects of
\mathbf{D}({R})
satisfying
\mathfrak{P}
.

In general, it is possible to extend the consideration of the property
\mathfrak{P}
from the affine setting of modules over commutative rings to the non-affine setting of the category
\hbox{\rm Qcoh-}X
of quasi-coherent sheaves over a scheme
X
in the following way. Given a scheme
X
, let
\mathbf{D}({X})=\mathbf{D}({\hbox{\rm Qcoh-}X})
denote the unbounded derived category of the category of quasi-coherent sheaves over
X
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{X}
denote the structure sheaf on
X
. For any open subset
U\subseteq X
we have the module of sections functor
{\_}(U):\hbox{\rm Qcoh-}X\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}
. If
U
is open affine then this functor is exact
[
43
, Lemma 30.2.2.]
, and so it extends naturally to a triangle functor
{\_}(U):\mathbf{D}({X})\to\mathbf{D}({\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)})
. Then we say that an object
\mathcal{M}\in\mathbf{D}({X})
satisfies the property
\mathfrak{P}
if the section object
\mathcal{M}(U)\in\mathbf{D}({\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)})
belongs to
\mathfrak{P}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}
for
any
open affine subset
U
of
X
.

Well-behaved properties of quasi-coherent sheaves are expected to be verifiable on any choice of open affine cover of
X
. A property
\mathfrak{P}
is therefore called
(Zariski) local
if for any scheme
X
, any open affine cover
X=\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i}
, and any
\mathcal{M}\in\mathbf{D}({X})
, we have that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies
\mathfrak{P}
whenever
\mathcal{M}(U_{i})\in\mathfrak{P}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i})}
for all
i\in I
. Thanks to the following result, called the
Affine Communication Lemma
, there is a purely module-theoretic criterion sufficient to check that a property is local.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a property of objects of
\mathbf{D}({R})
where
R
runs over commutative rings. Assume the following two conditions for any commutative ring
R
:

(1)

If
X\in\mathbf{D}({R})
satisfies
\mathfrak{P}
then
X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}R[f^{-1}]\in\mathfrak{P}_{R[f^{-1}]}
for any element
f\in R
,

(2)

Let
f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}\in R
be elements such that
R=\sum_{i<n}f_{i}R
and consider the ring homomorphism
R\to S:=\prod_{i<n}R[f_{i}^{-1}]
. Then for any
X\in\mathbf{D}({R})
, if
X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in\mathfrak{P}_{S}
then
X\in\mathfrak{P}_{R}
.

Then the property
\mathfrak{P}
is local.

Proof.

The proof is completely analogous to the non-derived version in
[
15
, Lemma 3.4]
, see also
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

It is often the case that a local property satisfies a stronger variant than the conditions of Lemma
2.2
. Following
[
15
, Definition 3.5]
, we say that a property
\mathfrak{P}
is an
ad-property (=ascent-descent property)
if the following two conditions hold for any commutative ring
R
and
X\in\mathbf{D}({R})
:

(1)

If
X\in\mathfrak{P}_{R}
then
X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in\mathfrak{P}_{S}
for any flat ring morphism
R\to S
,

(2)

If
X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in\mathfrak{P}_{S}
then
X\in\mathfrak{P}_{R}
for any faithfully flat ring morphism
R\to S
.

2.3.
Phantoms

We say that a morphism
\varphi:U\to V
in
\mathbf{D}({R})
is a
phantom
if for every compact object
C\in\mathbf{D}({R})
we have
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(C,\varphi)=0
,
[
23
]
. A triangle
X\to Y\to Z\overset{\delta}{\to}X[1]
is called
pure
if
\delta
is a phantom.

Lemma 2.5

Remark 2.6

If

3.
Silting and cosilting in
\mathbf{D}({R})

3.1.
TTF-triples in triangulated categories

We recall some standard notions and notations in triangulated categories. Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category.
For a subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and a subset
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
of the set of integers, we denote

\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{I}}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}\mid\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(C,X[i])=0,\hbox{ for all }C\in\mathcal{C}\hbox{ and all }i\in I\}

and

{{}^{\perp_{I}}\mathcal{C}}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}\mid\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(X,C[i])=0,\hbox{ for all }C\in\mathcal{C}\hbox{ and all }i\in I\}.

Often the set
I
is symbolized as
\leq n
,
<n
,
=n
or similar, where
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, with the obvious meaning.

A
torsion pair
in
\mathcal{D}
is a pair
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
of subcategories
\mathcal{D}
such that:

(1)

Both
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are closed under direct summands.

(2)

\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{0}}=\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{U}={{}^{\perp_{0}}\mathcal{V}}
.

(3)

Every objects
X\in\mathcal{D}
lies in a triangle
U\to X\to V\to U[1]
with

U\in\mathcal{U}
and
V\in\mathcal{V}
.

It is clear that if
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a torsion pair, then
\mathcal{U}
is closed under coproducts and
\mathcal{V}
is closed under products.
A
TTF-triple
in
\mathcal{D}
is a triple that
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
such that both
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
and
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
are torsion pairs. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of objects from
\mathcal{D}
. The TTF triple is
generated by
\mathcal{S}
if
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{S}^{\perp_{0}}
. An object
C\in\mathcal{D}
is called
compact
if the covariant functor
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(C,-)
commutes with respect to direct sums. If
\mathcal{S}
is a set of compact objects then we will say that the TTF-triple is
compactly generated
.

A torsion pair is called
t-structure
(
co-t-structure
) if in addition
\mathcal{U}
is closed under positive (respectively negative shifts). Note that the closure of
\mathcal{U}
under positive (negative) shifts, that is
\mathcal{U}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{U}
(respectively
\mathcal{U}[-1]\subseteq\mathcal{U}
) is equivalent to
\mathcal{V}[-1]\subseteq\mathcal{V}
(respectively
\mathcal{V}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{V}
). In these cases, we say that
\mathcal{U}
is the aisle, respectively
\mathcal{V}
is the coaisle of the (co)-t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
.

3.2.
Silting and cosilting objects in derived categories

Let
T\in\mathbf{D}({R})
. We say that
T
is a
silting object
if the pair
(T^{\perp_{>0}},T^{\perp_{\leq 0}})
is a t-structure in
\mathbf{D}({R})
. In this case, the induced t-structure is called the
silting t-structure
induced by
T
. A class of objects is a
silting class
if it is an aisle of a silting t-structure.
Dually, an object
C\in\mathbf{D}({R})
is called
cosilting
if
({{}^{\perp_{\leq 0}}C},{{}^{\perp_{>0}}C})
is a t-structure, called the
cosilting t-structure
induced by
C
. A coaisle of a cosilting t-structure is called a
cosilting class
. Two (co)silting objects are
equivalent
if they induce the same (co)silting class.

Remark 3.1
.

We know by
[
35
, Lemma 4.5]
that two silting objects
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are equivalent if and only if
\mathrm{Add}(T_{1})=\mathrm{Add}(T_{2})
. Similarly, two cosilting objects
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are equivalent if and only if
\mathrm{Prod}(C_{1})=\mathrm{Prod}(C_{2})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

An object
T\in\mathbf{D}({R})
is silting if and only if

(S1)

T\in T^{\perp_{>0}}
.

(S2)

T^{\perp_{>0}}
is closed under coproducts.

(S3)

T
generates
\mathbf{D}({R})
, that is
T^{\perp_{\mathbb{Z}}}=\{0\}
.

Dually, a pure-injective object
C\in\mathbf{D}({R})
is cosilting if and only if

(C1)

C\in{{}^{\perp_{>0}}C}
.

(C2)

{{}^{\perp_{>0}}C}
is closed under products.

(C3)

C
cogenerates
\mathbf{D}({R})
, that is
{{}^{\perp_{\mathbb{Z}}}C}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

The characterization of the silting complexes is a particular case of
[
35
, Proposition 4.13]
.
For the cosilting case, the argument is dual, the only modification occurs when we need to show that

C
induces a t-structure whose aisle is
{{}^{\perp_{\leq 0}}C}
(and consequently the coaisle is the smallest cosuspended and closed under products subcategory of
\mathbf{D}({R})
). Here we use the additional hypothesis that
C
is pure-injective and then the claim follows by
[
26
, Corollary 5.11]
(the main result of
[
32
]
is essentially the reason why no extra assumption is needed on the silting side.)
∎

.

(1) A
t
-structure

3.3.
(Co)Silting of (co)finite type

For a not necessarily commutative ring

[

Lemma 3.7
.

For an object

(a)

The equality (a) follows by the isomorphism

valid for any

For the equality (b), note that for all

3.4.
Bounded (co)silting complexes

In what follows, we will be particularly interested in silting complexes which are
bounded
in the sense that they are isomorphic in the derived category to bounded complexes of projective modules. This is a natural condition to consider since it is part of the definition of
n
-tilting modules, and also it ensures a well-behaved theory of derived equivalences
[
35
]
. It turns out that the characterization of Lemma
3.2
simplifies considerably in this situation.

For a (full) subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbf{D}({R})
, let
\mathrm{add}(C)
(resp.,
\mathrm{Add}(C)
) denote the subcategory formed by all direct summands of finite coproducts (resp., all coproducts) of objects from
\mathcal{C}
. Similarly,
\mathrm{Prod}(\mathcal{C})
is formed by all direct summands of arbitrary products of objects of
\mathcal{C}
. If
\mathcal{C}=\{X\}
for some object
X
, we drop the curly brackets in the notation.
We denote by
\mathrm{susp}\,(\mathcal{C})
(resp.
\mathrm{cosusp}\,(\mathcal{C})
) the suspended (resp. cosupended) closure of
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathbf{D}({R})
, that is, the smallest full subcategory of
\mathbf{D}({R})
containing
\mathcal{C}
and closed under direct summands, extensions and
[1]
(resp.,
[-1]
). Both
\mathrm{susp}\,(\mathcal{C})
and
\mathrm{cosusp}\,(\mathcal{C})
admit an explicit description as follows. Let
\mathcal{E}^{+}_{0}=\mathrm{add}\{C[n]\mid C\in\mathcal{C},n\geq 0\}
and
\mathcal{E}^{-}_{0}=\mathrm{add}\{C[n]\mid C\in\mathcal{C},n\leq 0\}
. For
i>0
, define inductively subcategory
\mathcal{E}^{+}_{i}
consisting of all
X\in\mathbf{D}({R})
fitting into a triangle
E_{0}\to X\to E_{i-1}\to E_{0}[1]
with
E_{0}\in\mathcal{E}^{+}_{0}
and
E_{i-1}\in\mathcal{E}^{+}_{i-1}
; subcategories
\mathcal{E}^{-}_{i}
are defined in the analogous way. Then one can easily check that
\mathrm{susp}\,(\mathcal{C})=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{E}^{+}_{i}
and
\mathrm{cosusp}\,(\mathcal{C})=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{E}^{-}_{i}
. This description has a consequence important in what follows:

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a full subcategory of
\mathbf{D}({R})
closed under products (resp. coproducts). If
X\in\mathrm{susp}\,(\mathcal{C})
then
X^{I}\in\mathrm{susp}\,(\mathcal{C})
(resp.
X^{(I)}\in\mathrm{susp}\,(\mathcal{C})
) for any set
I
, and the same holds for
\mathrm{cosusp}\,(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathcal{C}
is closed under products and let
I
be a set. Since
X\in\mathrm{susp}\,(\mathcal{C})
, there is by the above description some
i\geq 0
such that
X\in\mathcal{E}^{+}_{i}
. We prove
X^{I}\in\mathcal{E}^{+}_{i}
by induction on
i\geq 0
. If
i=0
then
X\in\mathrm{add}\{C[n]\mid C\in\mathcal{C},n\geq 0\}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is closed under products, clearly
X^{I}\in\mathrm{add}\{C[n]\mid C\in\mathcal{C},n\geq 0\}
. If
i>0
then there is a triangle
E_{0}\to X\to E_{i-1}\to E_{0}[1]
with
E_{0}\in\mathcal{E}^{+}_{0}
and
E_{i-1}\in\mathcal{E}^{+}_{i-1}
. Then the triangle
E_{0}^{I}\to X^{I}\to E_{i-1}^{I}\to E_{0}^{I}[1]
together with the induction hypothesis shows that
X^{I}\in\mathcal{E}^{+}_{i}
. The case of coproduct closure and/or the cosuspended closure is handled the same way.
∎

The following result extends
[
34
, Theorem 2.3 and Theorem 4.3]
from the case of
n
-(co)tilting modules, and the proof idea is similar to
loc. cit.
with some necessary modifications. For basics on homotopy limits and colimits in triangulated categories we refer to
[
31
, §1.6]
. We will also use the notations

\mathbf{D}({R})^{\geq n}=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({R})\mid H^{i}(X)=0\textrm{ for all }i<n\}
and
\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq n}=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({R})\mid H^{i}(X)=0\textrm{ for all }i>n\}.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
T\in\mathbf{D}({R})
be an object which is isomorphic to a bounded complex of projective
R
-modules. Then
T
is silting if and only if

(Sb1)

\mathrm{Add}(T)\subseteq T^{\perp_{>0}}
.

(Sb2)

T

\mathbf{D}({R})
, that is
T^{\perp_{\mathbb{Z}}}=\{0\}
.

Dually, let

be an object which is isomorphic to a bounded complex of injective

-modules. Then

is cosilting if and only if

(Cb1)

\mathrm{Prod}(C)\subseteq{{}^{\perp_{>0}}C}
.

(Cb2)

C

\mathbf{D}({R})
, that is
{{}^{\perp_{\mathbb{Z}}}C}=\{0\}

Proof.

We prove only the silting result, the cosilting one follows by a completely analogous argument. Put
(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})=(T^{\perp_{>0}},T^{\perp_{\leq 0}})
and our goal is to show that this constitutes a t-structure in
\mathbf{D}({R})
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
T
is a complex of projective
R
-modules concentrated in degrees
-n+1,-n+2,\ldots,-1,0
for some

.

First, we show that for any
X\in\mathbf{D}({R})
there is a triangle
Y\to X\to Z\to Y[1]
with
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
and
Z\in\mathcal{Z}
. Put
X_{0}:=X
and define inductively a sequence of triangles

T^{(H_{i})}[i]\xrightarrow{h_{i}}X_{i}\xrightarrow{f_{i}}X_{i+1}\to T^{(H_{i})}[i+1]

where
H_{i}=\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(T[i],X_{i})
and
h_{i}
is the canonical evaluation morphism. Since
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(T[i],h_{i})
is surjective for each
i\geq 0
and by (Sb1), we see that

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(T[j],X_{i})=0~\textrm{ for all }j=0,1,\ldots,i-1.

This construction defines an inductive system

X_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}X_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}X_{3}\xrightarrow{f_{3}}\cdots

and we consider its homotopy colimit
Z=\mathrm{hocolim}_{i\geq 0}(X_{i})
. Recall that for any
j\geq 0
we have
Z=\mathrm{hocolim}_{i\geq j}(X_{i})
, see
[
31
, Lemma 1.7.1]
. Considering
X_{j}
as the trivial homotopy colimit
X_{j}=\mathrm{hocolim}_{i\geq j}(X_{j})
of an inductive tower consisting of identities on
X_{j}
,
[
31
, Lemma 1.6.6]
, we obtain a commutative square

\begin{CD}\coprod_{i\geq j}X_{i}@>{(1-\text{shift}(f_{i}))}>{}>\coprod_{i\geq j}X_{i}\\
@A{\coprod_{i\geq j}f_{ji}}A{}A@A{\coprod_{i\geq j}f_{ji}}A{}A\\
\coprod_{i\geq j}X_{j}@>{(1-\text{shift}(\mathrm{Id}_{X_{j}}))}>{}>\coprod_{i\geq j}X_{i}\\
\end{CD}

where
f_{ji}=f_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{j+1}\circ f_{j}
for
j<i
and
f_{jj}=\mathrm{Id}_{X_{j}}
. For each
0\leq j\leq i
, let
S_{ji}
be the cone of
f_{ji}
. Then
S_{ji}
is an iterated extension of the objects
T[k+1]^{(H_{k})}
where
k=j,j+1,\ldots,i-1
. In particular, since
T\in\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq 0}
, we have
S_{ji}\in\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq-j-1}
. By
[
6
, 1.1.11]
, the square above extends to a diagram

\begin{CD}\coprod_{i\geq j}S_{ji}@>{}>{}>\coprod_{i\geq j}S_{ji}@>{}>{}>C\\
@A{}A{}A@A{}A{}A@A{}A{}A\\
\coprod_{i\geq j}X_{i}@>{(1-\text{shift}(f_{i}))}>{}>\coprod_{i\geq j}X_{i}@>{}>{}>Z\\
@A{\coprod_{i\geq j}f_{ji}}A{}A@A{\coprod_{i\geq j}f_{ji}}A{}A@A{}A{}A\\
\coprod_{i\geq j}X_{j}@>{(1-\text{shift}(\mathrm{Id}_{X_{j}}))}>{}>\coprod_{i\geq j}X_{i}@>{}>{}>X_{j}\\
\end{CD}

in which all squares commute and all rows and columns extend to triangles. Since
S_{ji}\in\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq-j-1}
, we have
\coprod_{i\geq j}S_{ji}\in\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq-j-1}
, and therefore
C\in\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq-j-1}
. Using that
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(T,\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq-n})=0
, we argue from the rightmost vertical triangle of the diagram that
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(T[i],Z)\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(T[i],X_{i+n+1})=0
for any
i\geq 0
. This shows that
Z\in\mathcal{Z}=T^{\perp_{\leq 0}}
.

Now extend the map
X=X_{0}\to Z
to a triangle
Y\to X\to Z\to Y[1]
and let us show that
Y\in\mathcal{Y}=T^{\perp_{>0}}
. The diagram above puts
Y
into a triangle

\textstyle Y\to\coprod_{i\geq 0}S_{0i}\to\coprod_{i\geq 0}S_{0i}\to Y[1].

Put
C_{0i}=S_{0i}[-1]
for all
i>0
, so that
Y
is the mapping cone of
\coprod_{i\geq 0}C_{0i}\to\coprod_{i\geq 0}C_{0i}
.

Recall that
C_{0i}
is an iterated extension of objects
T^{(H_{0})},\ldots,T^{(H_{i-1})}[i-1]
, and therefore
C_{0i}\in\mathrm{susp}(\mathrm{Add}(T))\subseteq\mathcal{Y}=T^{\perp_{>0}}
, the latter inclusion follows from (Sb1) and the obvious closure properties of
\mathcal{Y}
. It remains to show that also the coproduct
\coprod_{i\geq 0}C_{0i}
belongs to
\mathcal{Y}
. Note that by the assumption on
T
, we clearly have
\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq-n}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
. Put
m=n+1
. For any
i>m
, there is a triangle
C_{0m}\to C_{0i}\to C_{mi}\to C_{0m}[1]
, and we know that
C_{mi}\in\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq-m}
. Consider the coproduct triangle

\textstyle\coprod_{i>m}C_{0m}\to\coprod_{i>m}C_{0i}\to\coprod_{i>m}C_{mi}\to\coprod_{i>m}C_{0m}[1].

Then
\coprod_{i>m}C_{mi}\in\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq-m}=\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq-n-1}
. Since
\mathbf{D}({R})^{\leq-n}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
, we see that
\coprod_{i>m}C_{0i}\in\mathcal{Y}
if and only if
\coprod_{i>m}C_{0m}\cong C_{0m}^{(\omega)}\in\mathcal{Y}
. But the latter inclusion follows from Lemma
3.10
because
C_{0m}\in\mathrm{susp}\,(\mathrm{Add}(T))\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
. Therefore,
\coprod_{i>m}C_{0i}\in\mathcal{Y}
. Since we already know that
\coprod_{i\leq m}C_{0i}\in\mathcal{Y}
, we showed that
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
.

Finally we need to show that
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})=0
. Take
X\in\mathcal{Y}
, and consider the same triangle
Y\to X\to Z\to Y[1]
as constructed above. Since
Y,X\in\mathcal{Y}
, also
Z\in\mathcal{Y}
. But then
Z\in\mathcal{Y}\cap\mathcal{Z}=T^{\perp_{\mathbb{Z}}}
. It follows by (Sb2) that
Z=0
and so
X\cong Y
. By the construction,
Y
belongs to the smallest suspended and coproduct closed subcategory of
\mathbf{D}({R})
generated by
T
(since we constructed
Y
from
T
by using extensions,
[1]
, and coproducts). Since
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(T,\mathcal{Z})=0
, this shows
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})=0
, which in turn renders
(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})
a t-structure.
∎

[

]
.

.

is a module. Then

is
n
-tilting (for a positive integer

) if and only if

;

.
∎

4.
Transfer of (co)silting objects using ring morphisms

In this section we will use the following

Setting 1
.

Let
\lambda:R\to S
be a morphism of commutative rings. Suppose that
A
is an
R
-algebra such that
R
is central and that
B=A\otimes_{R}S
. Then we have a ring morphism
A\to B
. The covariant, respectively contravariant, extension functor and the restriction functor induced by this morphism (we use the terminology from
[
9
]
) and their derivates (see
[
40
]
) can be described, up to natural isomorphism, in the following ways:

•

-\otimes_{A}B\cong-\otimes_{R}S:\hbox{\rm Mod-}{A}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{B}
(the covariant extension functor);

•

-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}B:\mathbf{D}({A})\to\mathbf{D}({B})
(the derived covariant extension functor);
if we assume that
A
or
S
is flat over
R
then we have a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle(-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}B)

\displaystyle\cong(-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}(A\otimes_{R}S))\cong(-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}(A\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S))

\displaystyle\cong((-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}A)\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)\cong(-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S).

•

\Hom_{A}(B,-)\cong\Hom_{R}(S,-):\hbox{\rm Mod-}{A}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{B}
(the contravariant extension functor);

•

\RHom_{A}(B,-):\mathbf{D}({A})\to\mathbf{D}({B})
(the derived contravariant extension functor); again, the supplementary assumption that
A
or
S
is flat over
R
allows us to compute

\displaystyle\RHom_{A}(B,-)

\displaystyle\cong\RHom_{A}(S\otimes_{R}A,-)\cong\RHom_{A}(S\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}A,-)

\displaystyle\cong\RHom_{R}(S,\RHom_{A}(A,-))\cong\RHom_{R}(S,-).

•

\Hom_{B}(B,-)\cong-\otimes_{B}B\cong\Hom_{S}(S,-)\cong-\otimes_{S}S:\hbox{\rm Mod-}{B}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{A}
(the restriction functor);

•

\RHom_{B}(B,-)\cong-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{B}B\cong\RHom_{S}(S,-)\cong-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{S}S:\mathbf{D}({B})\to\mathbf{D}({A})
(the derived restriction functor).

Note that the restriction functors do not change the module/complex. Therefore, there is no danger of confusion if we write
X
instead of
\Hom_{B}(B,X)
or
\RHom_{B}(B,X)
, respectively.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{A}
is a subcategory in
\mathbf{D}({A})
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}\bigcap\mathbf{D}({B})
the subcategory of
\mathbf{D}({B})
defined by

\mathcal{A}\bigcap\mathbf{D}({B})=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({B})\mid\RHom_{B}(B,X)=X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{B}B\in\mathcal{A}\}.

4.1.
The extension of scalars functors applied on (co)silting objects

We will prove that the derived covariant (contravariant) extension functors defined above preserve the silting (respectively, pure-injective cosilting) objects from
\mathbf{D}({R})
.

We start by observing that
[
1
, Lemma 1.10]
and
[
16
, Proposition 2.3]
are still valid in our setting (with
verbatim
proofs).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R
be commutative and
A
be an
R
-algebra such that
R
is central. Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
be a t-structure in
\mathbf{D}({A})
. For every
X\in\mathbf{D}^{\leq 0}(R)
we have:

(1)

If
U\in\mathcal{U}
then
U\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}X\in\mathcal{U}
.

(2)

If

then
\RHom_{R}(X,V)\in\mathcal{V}
.

In the next theorem we will use the notation presented in Remark
3.3
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that we are in the hypotheses of Setting
1
, and
A
or
S
is flat over
R
.

(I)

Suppose that
T\in\mathbf{D}({A})
is silting.

(1)

The complex
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
is silting in
\mathbf{D}({B})
.

(2)

\mathcal{Y}_{T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S}=\mathcal{Y}_{T}\bigcap\mathbf{D}({B})
, and
\mathcal{Z}_{T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S}=\mathcal{Z}_{T}\bigcap\mathbf{D}({B})
.

(3)

\mathcal{X}_{T}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S}
, and
\mathcal{Y}_{T}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\subseteq\mathcal{Y}_{T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S}
.

(4)

If
T
is bounded then
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
has the same property.

(II)

Suppose that
C\in\mathbf{D}({A})
is pure-injective cosilting.

(1)

The complex
\RHom_{R}(S,C)
is pure-injective cosilting in
\mathbf{D}({B})
.

(2)

\mathcal{U}_{\RHom_{R}(S,C)}=\mathcal{U}_{C}\bigcap\mathbf{D}({B})
, and
\mathcal{V}_{\RHom_{R}(S,C)}=\mathcal{V}_{C}\bigcap\mathbf{D}({B})
.

(3)

\RHom_{R}(S,\mathcal{V}_{C})\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{\RHom_{R}(S,C)}
, and
\RHom_{R}(S,\mathcal{W}_{C})\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\RHom_{R}(S,C)}
.

(4)

If
C
is bounded cosilting, then
\RHom_{R}(S,C)
has the same property.

Proof.

(I) (1) The ring homomorphism
\lambda
induces a structure of
R
-module on
S
, and as a complex of
R
modules it belongs to
\mathbf{D}^{\leq 0}(R)
. Since
T
is silting, it induces a t-structure
(T^{\perp_{>0}},T^{\perp_{\leq 0}})
in
\mathbf{D}({A})
. From
T\in{T^{\perp_{>0}}}
it follows by Lemma
4.1
that
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in T^{\perp_{>0}}
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and every
X\in\mathbf{D}({B})
, we have the adjunction isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({A})}(T,X[n])\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({B})}((T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S),X[n]).

Using this isomorphism for
n>0
and
X=T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
, together with
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in T^{\perp_{>0}}
, we deduce that the
B
-complex
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
belongs to
(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{\perp_{>0}}
.

Further, the same isomorphism, applied for
n=0
and
X=\coprod_{i\in I}X_{i}
, together with the fact that (the derived functor of) the restriction of the scalars functor
\mathbf{D}({B})\to\mathbf{D}({A})
preserves all coproducts, shows that the class
(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{\perp_{>0}}\subseteq\mathbf{D}({B})
is closed under coproducts.

Finally, if
X\in\mathbf{D}({B})
and
X\in(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{\perp_{\mathbb{Z}}}
, it follows that
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({A})}(T,X[n])=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
X
is acyclic as an
A
-complex, hence it is acyclic as a
B
-complex.

(2) These equalities are consequences of the isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({B})}(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S,Y)\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({B})}(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}A\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S,Y)\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(T,\RHom_{B}(B,Y)).

(3) Let
Y\in\mathcal{Y}_{T}
. Then
\RHom_{B}(B,Y\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)=Y\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in\mathcal{Y}_{T}
. For all
i>0
we have

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({B})}(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S,Y\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S[i])\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({B})}(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}B,Y\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S[i])

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({A})}(T,\RHom_{B}(B,Y\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)[i])\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({A})}(T,Y\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S[i])=0.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}_{T}
and
Y\in\mathcal{Y}_{T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S}
. Then
\RHom_{B}(B,Y)\in\mathcal{Y}_{T}
, hence the property
X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S=X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}B\in\mathcal{X}_{T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S}
can be obtained by applying the adjunction isomorphism.

(4) If
T
is bounded, then obviously the same property holds true for
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
.

(II) The functor
\RHom_{A}(B,-)
preserves all products, hence it preserves the pure-injectivity property,
[
38
, Lemma 5.3]
. Therefore, using the natural isomorphisms

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({A})}(U\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{S}S,V)\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({B})}(U,\RHom_{A}(B,V)),

a proof can be done by following the lines of the proof for (I).
∎

4.2.
The transfer of (co)silting complexes of (co)finite type

In the following, we will study the transfer of (co)silting complexes of (co)finite type. In this case, we have more connections between the associated TTF triples.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that we are in Setting
1
, and
A
or
S
is flat over
R
.

(I)

If
C\in\mathbf{D}({A})
is cosilting of cofinite type then
\RHom_{R}(S,C)
is cosilting of cofinite type in
\mathbf{D}({B})
.

(II)

If
T\in\mathbf{D}({A})
is silting of finite type then
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
is of finite type in
\mathbf{D}({B})
.

Proof.

(I) Since all cosilting objects of cofinite type are pure-injective, it follows that
\RHom_{R}(S,C)\in\mathbf{D}({B})
is cosilting.

There exists a set of compact objects
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{C}
such that
\mathcal{V}_{C}=\mathcal{P}^{\perp_{0}}
. Using the fact the the restriction functor preserves coproducts, it follows that
\mathcal{P}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S=\{P\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\mid P\in\mathcal{P}\}
is a set of compact objects from
\mathbf{D}({B})
.

We have
V\in\mathcal{V}_{\RHom_{R}(S,C)}
if and only if
V\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{S}S=\RHom_{S}(S,V)\in\mathcal{V}_{C}
, and this is equivalent to

0=\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(\mathcal{P},\RHom_{S}(S,V))\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({S})}(\mathcal{P}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S,V).

This implies that
\mathcal{V}_{\RHom_{R}(S,C)}=(\mathcal{P}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{\perp_{0}},
hence
\RHom_{R}(S,C)
is of cofinite type.

(II) The proof is similar to the proof used for the cosilting case. ∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that we are in the hypothesis of Setting
1
. Assuming that

is faithfully flat,

, and

Using Lemma

, the equivalence is a consequence of the isomorphisms

that hold for all

.
∎

.

4.3.

T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in T^{\perp_{>0}}
. By Proposition
4.2
we know that
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{\perp_{>0}}
, and that
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
is a weak generator in
\mathbf{D}({R})
. Since
S
is flat,
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S=T\otimes_{R}S
is also a
B
-module, hence
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{\perp_{<0}}
. The conclusion follows from Corollary
3.12
.

The last part is a consequence of Lemma
4.7
since all tilting cotorsion pairs are generated by families of strongly finitely presented modules.
∎

5.
Cosilting complexes of cofinite type over commutative rings

Let
\lambda:R\to S
be a morphism of commutative rings. In this section we consider the transfers of (co)silting objects of (co)finite type provided by the functors associated to
\lambda
.

5.1.
Thomason filtrations

Since
R
is a commutative ring, any compactly generated t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
in
\mathbf{D}({R})
is parametrized by so called Thomason filtration on

\mathrm{Spec}({R})
. More precisely we call
Thomason (open)
a subset of
\mathrm{Spec}({R})

a union of the subsets of the form
V(I)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Spec}({R})\mid I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}
,
where
I
is a finitely generated ideal of
R
(such a subset is called a
basic Thomason set
). Note that the basic Thomason sets are closed in the Zariski topology, that is the Thomason topology is the Hochster dual of the Zariski one.

A
Thomason filtration
on
\mathrm{Spec}({R})
is a family
\mathbb{X}=(X_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
such that for every integer
k
we have
X_{k}\subseteq\mathrm{Spec}({R})
is a Thomason set, and
X_{k+1}\subseteq X_{k}
. This filtration is called
non-degenerate
if
\bigcap_{n}X_{n}=\varnothing
and
\bigcup_{n}X_{n}=\mathrm{Spec}({R})
. Moreover, we will say that
\mathbb{X}
is
bounded
if there exist two integers
m_{0}
and
n_{0}
such that
X_{m}=\mathrm{Spec}({R})
for all
m\leq m_{0}
and
X_{n}=\emptyset
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. It was proved in
[
17
, Proposition 5.12]
that non-degenerate Thomason filtrations parametrize cosilting complexes of cofinite type. We record, for further use, this correspondence.

Theorem 5.1
.

(I)
There are bijective correspondences between the following classes:

i)

equivalence classes of cosilting complexes of cofinite type in
\mathbf{D}({R})
;

ii)

compactly generated TTF-triples that are cosuspended and non-degenerate;

iii)

non-degenerate Thomason filtrations on
\mathrm{Spec}({R})
.

(II)
These bijections restrict to bijections between equivalence classes of bounded cosilting complexes of cofinite type, TTF-triples that are cointermediate and cosuspended, and bounded Thomason filtrations.

Proof.

The first part is proved in
[
17
, Proposition 5.12]
. These bijections extend the bijections described in
[
16
, Theorem 3.11]
.

The correspondence between bounded cosilting complexes and cointermediate, cosuspended TTF-triples is provided by
[
27
, Theorem 3.13]
, see also
[
3
, Corollary 2.14]
. For the correspondence between TTF-triples that are cointermediate and cosuspended and bounded Thomason filtrations, let
\mathbb{X}=(X_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a bounded Thomason filtration. By
[
16
, Theorem 3.11]
, the aisle of the corresponding
t
-structure is

\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{X}}=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({R})\mid\mathrm{Supp}(H^{k}(X))\subseteq X_{k}\textrm{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Since
X_{k}=\varnothing
for all
k\geq n
, it follows that for every
X\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{X}}
we have
H^{k}(X)=0
for all
k\geq n
. It follows that

\mathbf{D}({R})^{\geq n}=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({R})\mid H^{i}(X)=0\textrm{ for all }i<n\}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{X}}^{\perp_{0}}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{X}},

where
\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{X}}
is the coaisle of the
t
-structure associated to
\mathbb{X}
. Conversely, it is easy to see that if
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
is a TTF-triple that is cointermediate and cosuspended TTF-triple then the associated Thomason filtration is bounded.
∎

5.2.
A topological transfer.

If
\lambda:R\to S
is a morphism of commutative rings, we can use the topological properties of the associated map
\lambda^{\star}:\mathrm{Spec}({S})\to\mathrm{Spec}({R})
,
\lambda^{\star}(\mathfrak{q})=\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})
, together with Theorem
5.1
. Therefore, we obtain a topological method to transfer the cosilting complexes of cofinite type from
\mathbf{D}({R})
to cosilting complexes of cofinite type in
\mathbf{D}({S})
. We start by recalling some basic results.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f:\mathfrak{S}_{1}\to\mathfrak{S}_{2}
be a surjective closed map between two topological spaces. If
f^{-1}(Y)\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{1}
is an open set, then
Y
is open in
\mathfrak{S}_{2}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

(I)

[
37
, Proposition 5.9]
The map
\lambda^{\star}
is continuous with respect to Thomason’s topology.

(II)

[
19
, Lemma 3.15]
If
\lambda
is faithfully flat then
\lambda^{\star}
is a surjective closed map with respect to Thomason’s topology.

We will say that a Thomason subset
X\subseteq\mathrm{Spec}({S})
is
Thomason saturated
(with respect to
\lambda
) if

X=(\lambda^{\star})^{-1}(\lambda^{\star}(X))
. This means that for every
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathrm{Spec}({S})
we have
\mathfrak{q}\in X
if and only if
\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})\in\lambda^{\star}(X)
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that
\lambda:R\to S
is a morphism of commutative rings. If
\mathbb{X}=(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a non-degenerate Thomason filtration in
\mathrm{Spec}({R})
then
\mathbb{Y}=((\lambda^{\star})^{-1}(X_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a non-degenerate Thomason filtration in
\mathrm{Spec}({S})
.

Therefore,
\lambda
induces a map

\mathbf{\Phi}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\hbox{compactly generated }\\
\hbox{ t-structures in }\mathbf{D}({R})\end{array}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\hbox{compactly generated }\\
\hbox{ t-structures in }\mathbf{D}({S})\end{array}\right\},

that is defined in the following way:

If
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a compactly generated t-structure in
\mathbf{D}({R})
and
\mathbb{X}=(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the Thomason filtration associated to it then
\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is the
t
-structure in
\mathbf{D}({S})
that is defined by the Thomason filtrations
\mathbb{Y}=((\lambda^{\star})^{-1}(X_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. All components of
\mathbb{Y}
are Thomason saturated with respect to
\lambda
.

Moreover, if
\lambda
is faithfully flat then it induces a map
\Psi
that associates to every compactly generated
t
-structure
(\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
in
\mathbf{D}({S})
with the property that all components
Y_{n}
of the associated Thomason filtration
\mathbb{Y}=(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
are Thomason saturated with respect to
\lambda
, the t-structure of
\mathbf{D}({R})
that corresponds to
\mathbb{X}=(\lambda^{\star}(Y_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. In this case we have
\mathbf{\Psi}\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})=(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
for every compactly generated
t
-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\in\mathbf{D}({R})

Proof.

Since
\lambda^{\star}
is continuous with respect to Hochster’s topology it follows that
\mathbb{Y}
is a system of Thomason sets.

Observe that
\mathfrak{q}\in(\lambda^{\star})^{-1}(X_{n})
if and only if
\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})\in X_{n}
. Using this, it is easy to see that
\mathbb{Y}
is a Thomason filtration.

For the last statement, we apply Lemma
5.3
and Lemma
5.2
to conclude that the terms of the sequence
\mathbb{X}
are Thomason sets. As before, it is an easy exercise to prove that
\mathbb{X}
is a Thomason filtration.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

As we already mentioned in the introduction,
\mathbf{\Phi}
induces a map

\mathbf{\Phi}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\hbox{equivalence classes of }\\
\hbox{ cosilting objects }\\
\hbox{of cofinite type in }\mathbf{D}({R})\end{array}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\hbox{equivalence classes of }\\
\hbox{ cosilting objects }\\
\hbox{ of cofinite type in }\mathbf{D}({S})\end{array}\right\}.

If
\lambda
is faithfully flat, this map is injective.

5.3.
The ascend of (co)silting complexes of (co)finite type via derived functors

We already proved in Theorem
4.3
that the (co)induction functor associated to
\lambda
preserves the (co)finite type property. In the following we will see that the map induced by the coinduction functor on the equivalence classes of cosilting objects of cofinite type is in fact the map
\Phi
described in Remark
5.5
. We will need the following

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose that
\lambda:R\to S
is a morphism of commutative rings.

(I)

If
C\in\mathbf{D}({R})
is an object such that
\RHom_{R}(S,C)\in\mathbf{D}({S})
is cosilting of cofinite type and
\mathbb{Y}=(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the Thomason filtration associated to
\RHom_{R}(S,C)
then all sets
Y_{n}
are Thomason saturated with respect to
\lambda
.

(II)

If
T\in\mathbf{D}({R})
is an object such that
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in\mathbf{D}({S})
is silting of finite type and
\mathbb{Y}=(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the Thomason filtration associated to
(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}
then all sets
Y_{n}
are Thomason saturated with respect to
\lambda
.

Proof.

(I) Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. From
[
17
, Lemma 3.7]
and Theorem
4.2
(applied for
A=R
and, consequently,
B=S
) it follows that

\displaystyle Y_{n}

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{q}\in\mathrm{Spec}({S})\mid\kappa(\mathfrak{q})[-n]\in\mathcal{U}_{\RHom_{R}(S,C)}\}

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{q}\in\mathrm{Spec}({S})\mid\kappa(\mathfrak{q})[-n]\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{S}S\in\mathcal{U}_{C}\}.

Note that for every
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathrm{Spec}({S})
there exists a canonical morphism (of
R
-algebras)
\kappa(\lambda^{\star}(\mathfrak{q}))\to\kappa(\mathfrak{q})
that is induced by the morphism
R/\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})\to S/\mathfrak{q}
. This morphism is unital, and
\kappa(\lambda^{\star}(\mathfrak{q}))
and
\kappa(\mathfrak{q})
are fields. It follows that, as an
R
-module,
\kappa(\mathfrak{q})
is a direct sum of copies of
\kappa(\lambda^{\star}(\mathfrak{q}))
. Then
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(\kappa(\mathfrak{q}),V)=0
if and only if
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(\kappa(\lambda^{\star}(\mathfrak{q})),V)=0
.

Since
\kappa(\mathfrak{q})[-n]\in\mathcal{U}_{\RHom_{R}(S,C)}
if and only if for all
i\leq 0
we have

0=\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({S})}(\kappa(\mathfrak{q})[-n],\RHom_{R}(S,C)[i])\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(\kappa(\mathfrak{q})[-n],C[i]),

we conclude that
\kappa(\mathfrak{q})[-n]\in\mathcal{U}_{\RHom_{R}(S,C)}
if and only if for all
i\leq 0
we have
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(\kappa(\lambda^{\star}(\mathfrak{q}))[-n],C[i])=0
. Then
Y_{n}
is Thomason saturated with respect to
\lambda
.

(II) For this case we apply (I) and the isomorphism
(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}\cong\RHom_{R}(S,T^{+})
.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\lambda:R\to S
be a morphism of commutative rings.

(I)

If
C\in\mathbf{D}({R})
is cosilting of cofinite type then
\RHom_{R}(S,C)\in\mathbf{\Phi}(C)
, where
\mathbf{\Phi}
is the map described in Remark
5.5
.

(II)

If
T\in\mathbf{D}({R})
is silting of finite type then
(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}\in\mathbf{\Phi}(T^{+})
.

Proof.

(I) From Lemma
5.6
it follows that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
Y_{n}=(\lambda^{\star})^{-1}(X_{n})
, where
\mathbb{X}=(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the Thomason filtration associated to
C
, and the proof is complete.

(II) This is also a consequence of Lemma
5.6
.
∎

If
\lambda
is faithfully flat, we can apply Theorem
5.7
together with the second part of Theorem
5.4
, we obtain the following cosilting version of Corollary
4.6
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Suppose that
\lambda:R\to S
is faithfully flat. If
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbf{D}({R})
are cosilting complexes of cofinite type such that the cosilting complexes
\RHom_{R}(S,C_{1})
and
\RHom_{R}(S,C_{2})
are equivalent then
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are equivalent.

5.4.
Some descent properties

If
\lambda:R\to S
is faithfully flat, we can use the above results to describe all cosilting complexes of cofinite type from
\mathbf{D}({S})
that can be obtained as images of the functor
\RHom_{R}(S,-)
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
\lambda:R\to S
be a faithfully flat morphism of commutative rings. If
\overline{C}\in\mathbf{D}({S})
is a cosilting complex of cofinite type and
\mathbb{Y}=(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the associated Thomason filtration on
\mathrm{Spec}({S})
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

there exists a cosilting complex of cofinite type
C
in
\mathbf{D}({R})
such that
\overline{C}
is equivalent to
\RHom_{R}(S,C)
;

(ii)

all
Y_{n}
are Thomason saturated with respect to
\lambda
.

Proof.

i)
\Rightarrow
ii) follows from Lemma
5.6
.

ii)
\Rightarrow
i) From the proof of Theorem
5.4
it follows that
\mathbb{X}=(\lambda^{\star}(Y_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a Thomason filtration. Let
C\in\mathbf{D}({R})
be a cosilting complex that corresponds to
\mathbb{X}
. It follows from Theorem
5.7
that
\RHom_{R}(S,C)
is equivalent to
\overline{C}
.
∎

Using the equivalence described in Theorem
3.5
(2), we obtain:

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
\lambda:R\to S
be a faithfully flat morphism of commutative rings. If
\overline{T}\in\mathbf{D}({S})
is a bounded silting complex, the following are equivalent:

(i)

there exists a (bounded) silting complex
T
in
\mathbf{D}({R})
such that
\overline{T}
is equivalent to
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
;

(ii)

if
\mathbb{Y}=(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the Thomason filtration associated to
\overline{T}^{+}
then
all sets
Y_{n}
are Thomason saturated with respect to
\lambda
.

Proof.

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is proved in Lemma
5.6
. Conversely, since
\mathbb{Y}=(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a (bounded) Thomason filtration that is saturated with respect to
\lambda
, there exists a bounded cosilting complex
C\in\mathbf{D}({R})
such that
\RHom_{R}(S,C)
is equivalent to
\overline{T}^{+}
. Since
C
is bounded, it follows that
C
is equivalent to a complex of the form
T^{+}
with
T\in\mathbf{D}({R})
a bounded silting complex. Applying Theorem
3.5
(2), it follows that the bounded silting complexes
\overline{T}
and
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
are equivalent.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

The above corollary is also valid for all silting complexes of finite type
\overline{T}\in\mathbf{D}({S})
if we assume that all cosilting complexes of cofinite type from
\mathbf{D}({R})
are of the form
T^{+}
, with

a silting complex of finite type (e.g., if

is noetherian, cf.
[

, Theorem 3.8]
).

The above considerations let us prove a descend property for bounded cosilting complexes:

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
T\in\mathbf{D}({R})
be a bounded complex of projectives.
If
\RHom(S,T^{+})
is cosilting in
\mathbf{D}({S})
then
T^{+}
is cosilting in
\mathbf{D}({R})
.

Proof.

Since the Thomason filtration of
\RHom_{R}(S,T^{+})\cong(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}
is bounded and Thomason saturated with respect to
\lambda
, we can apply Proposition
5.10
to observe that there exists a bounded complex of projectives
\widetilde{T}
in
\mathbf{D}({R})
such that
\widetilde{T}
is silting and
(\widetilde{T}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}
is equivalent to
(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}
.
It follows that
(\widetilde{T}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}
and
(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}
, induce the same t-structure whose aisle and coaisle are

\mathcal{V}_{(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}}={{}^{\perp_{\leq 0}}[(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}]}={{}^{\perp_{\leq 0}}[(\widetilde{T}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}]},

respectively

\mathcal{W}_{(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}}={{}^{\perp_{>0}}[(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}]}={{}^{\perp_{>0}}[(\widetilde{T}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}]}.

Using Corollary
4.4
twice, we get

{{}^{\perp_{\leq 0}}(T^{+})}=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({R})\mid X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in\mathcal{V}_{(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}}\}={{}^{\perp_{\leq 0}}(\widetilde{T}^{+})}

and

{{}^{\perp_{>0}}(T^{+})}=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({R})\mid X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S\in\mathcal{W}_{(T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S)^{+}}\}={{}^{\perp_{>0}}(\widetilde{T}^{+})}.

Therefore the pair
({{}^{\perp_{\leq 0}}(T^{+})},{{}^{\perp_{>0}}(T^{+})})
coincides with the cosilting t-structure induced by

. Hence

is cosilting too and it is obviously equivalent to
(\widetilde{T})^{+}
.
∎

6.
The descend property for bounded silting complexes

6.1.
Bounded silting complexes as modules over Dynkin quivers

Let
T
be a bounded silting complex in
\mathbf{D}({R})
. We assume that it is concentrated in the degrees
-n+1,\dots,-1,0
, and we will use the interpretation presented in

[
27
]
. In order to do this, we consider the Dynkin quiver

\mathbb{A}_{n}:\overset{-n+1}{\bullet}\longrightarrow\overset{-n+2}{\bullet}\longrightarrow\dots\longrightarrow\overset{-1}{\bullet}\longrightarrow\overset{0}{\bullet},

and we denote by
A(R)
the
R
-algebra
R\mathbb{A}_{n}/I
, where I is the ideal generated by all paths of length
2
. Note that
A(R)\cong T_{n}(R)/J(T_{n}(R))^{2}
, where
T_{n}(R)
denotes the ring of lower triangular matrices over
R
and
J(T_{n}(R))
is the corresponding Jacobson radical.

Let
\mathrm{Rep}(A(R))
be the category of representations bounded by
I
of
\mathbb{A}_{n}
in
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{R}
. Then
\mathrm{Rep}(A(R))
is equivalent to the category
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{A}(R)
, and it can be identified to the category of complexes over
R
that are concentrated in
-n+1,\dots,-1,0
, so we have a fully faithful functor from
\mathrm{Rep}(A(R))
to the category of complexes. If
X
is a complex concentrated in
-n+1,\dots,-1,0
, we will denote by
\widehat{X}
the corresponding object from
\mathrm{Rep}(A(R))
. The above mentioned functor induces a functor
\mathbf{\Psi}_{R}:\mathrm{Rep}(A(R))\to\mathbf{D}({R})
. We have the following useful result

Lemma 6.1
.

[
27
, Lemma 2.4]
If
\mathbf{\Psi}(\widehat{T})
is a complex of projectives then for every
\widehat{X}\in\mathrm{Rep}(A(R))
and for every
i>0
we have

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}({R})}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{R}(\widehat{T}),\mathbf{\Psi}_{R}(\widehat{X})[i])=\Ext^{i}_{\mathrm{Rep}(A(R))}(\widehat{T},\widehat{X}).

This correspondence is compatible with respect to change of rings.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose that
\lambda:R\to S
is a flat morphism of commutative ring. Then
A(S)\cong A(R)\otimes S
and we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{Rep}(A(R))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{-\otimes_{R}S}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{\Psi}_{R}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Rep}(A(S))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{\Psi}_{S}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}({R})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S}
\textstyle{\mathbf{D}({S})}

Proof.

For the first isomorphism we observe that
(R\mathbb{A}_{n}/I)\otimes_{R}S\cong(R\mathbb{A}_{n}\otimes_{R}S)/(I\otimes_{R}S)\cong S\mathbb{A}_{n}/K
, where
K
is the ideal in
S\mathbb{A}_{n}
generated by all paths of length
2
(see
[
13
]
). The commutativity of the diagram can be checked by direct computations.
∎

With these preparatory considerations in hand, we are ready to prove the following

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\lambda:R\to S
be a faithfully flat morphism of commutative rings.
Suppose that
T
is a bounded complex of projective
R
-modules that is concentrated in
-n+1,\dots,-1,0
. If
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
is silting then
\mathrm{Add}(T)\subseteq T^{\perp_{>0}}
.

Proof.

Since
T\otimes_{R}S
is bounded silting, we can use Lemma
5.6
and Corollary
5.10
to observe that there exists a silting complex
P\in D(R)
such that
P\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
is equivalent to
T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S
. Moreover, since
\lambda
is faithfully flat, we can assume that
P
is concentrated in
-n+1,\dots,-1,0
. We use
[
27
, Theorem 2.10]
to observe that
\widehat{P}
is an
(n-1)
-tilting module over
A(R)
, and that
\widehat{P\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S}\cong\widehat{P}\otimes_{R}S
and
\widehat{T\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}S}
are equivalent
(n-1)
-tilting modules over
A(S)
. From Proposition
4.8
it follows that
\widehat{T}^{(I)}\in\mathcal{K}_{\widehat{P}}\bigcap\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{P}}
for all sets
I
. Then
\Ext^{i}_{A(R)}(T,\mathrm{Add}(T))=0
for all
i>0
. Since
T
is a complex of projectives, we can use Lemma
6.1
to obtain the conclusion.
∎

There, we now see that the condition (Sb1) of the characterization of bounded silting complexes from Theorem
3.11
descends along all faithfully flat morphisms. Although we are not able to show an analogous result for (Sb2) in full generality, we shall show that it holds in many situations.

Recall that a full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbf{D}({R})
is
localizing
if it is a triangulated subcategory closed under coproducts. If
\mathcal{C}
is an arbitrary subcategory of
\mathbf{D}({R})
we denote by
\mathrm{Loc}(\mathcal{C})
the smallest localizing subcategory of
\mathbf{D}({R})
containing
\mathcal{C}
, and we write just
\mathrm{Loc}(X)
if
\mathcal{C}=\{X\}
. Recall from
[
2
, Theorem 3.14]
that
(\mathrm{Loc}(X),X^{\perp_{\mathbb{Z}}})
is a t-structure. Therefore,
\mathrm{Loc}(X)=\mathbf{D}({R})
if and only if
X
is a generator of
\mathbf{D}({R})
, that is,
X^{\perp_{\mathbb{Z}}}=0
. We will freely use the fact that any localizing subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbf{D}({R})

Remark 6.6

Lemma 6.8
.

The property of
{X}\in\mathbf{D}({R})
of being isomorphic to a bounded complex of projectives is an ad-property.

Proof.

Clearly, if
X
is a bounded complex of projective
R
-modules then
X\otimes_{R}S
is a bounded complex of projective
S
-modules for any flat ring morphism
R\to S
.

Let
R\to S
be faithfully flat and assume without loss of generality that
X\otimes_{R}S
is a complex of projective
S
-modules concentrated in degrees
-n,-n+1,\ldots,0
. Since
R\to S
is faithfully flat and
X\otimes_{R}S
is bounded, we see immediately that homology of
X
vanishes outside of degrees
-n,-n+1,\ldots,0
. Therefore, there is a complex
P
of projective
R
-modules concentrated in non-positive degrees which is isomorphic to
X
in
\mathbf{D}({R})
. Since
P\otimes_{R}S
is a complex of projective
S
-modules isomorphic in
\mathbf{D}({S})
to
X\otimes_{R}S
. By the assumption on
X\otimes_{R}S
, the cokernel of the differential map
X^{-n-1}\otimes_{R}S\to X^{-n}\otimes_{R}S
, which is the same as
\Coker(X^{-n-1}\to X^{-n})\otimes_{R}S
, is a projective
S
-module. Then by the result of Raynaud and Gruson
[
43
, Theorem 95.5]
, the cokernel of the differential
X^{-n-1}\to X^{-n}
is a projective
R
-module. It follows that the soft truncation
\tau^{\geq-n}X
, which is quasi-isomorphic to
X
, is a bounded complex of projective
R
-modules.
∎

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring with the property that
\mathrm{Loc}(\mathcal{I})=\mathbf{D}({R})
. Then any faithfully flat ring homomorphism
R\to S
of commutative rings has the property that
\mathrm{Loc}(S)=\mathbf{D}({R})
.

Proof.

Recall from
[

, Lemma 35.4.8.]
that the map
\lambda:R\to S
is a pure monomorphism of
R
-modules. It follows that for any object

the morphism

is a pure monomorphism in

3

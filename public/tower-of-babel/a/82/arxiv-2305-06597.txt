Nature of Some Solitons on Almost coKähler Manifolds and Asymptotically Harmonic Manifolds
Paritosh Ghosh, Hemangi Madhusudan Shah, Arindam Bhattacharyya

Abstract
. In this research, we study the nature of η \eta -Einstein and gradient η \eta -Einstein soliton in the framework of almost coKähler manifolds and ( κ , μ ) (\kappa,\mu) -almost coKähler manifolds. We find some expressions for scalar curvature of the almost coKähler manifold admitting η \eta -Einstein soliton in various cases. We also prove that if a ( κ , μ ) (\kappa,\mu) -almost coKähler manifold admits a gradient η \eta -Einstein soliton, then either the manifold is coKähler, or N ⁡ ( κ ) N(\kappa) -almost coKähler, or the soliton is trivial. We present an example which validates our results. Finally, we investigate the asymptotically harmonic manifolds admitting non-trivial Ricci solitons and show that they exhibit rigid behavior if for example, scalar curvature attains maximum.

3.
Einstein Solitons on Almost CoKähler Manifolds

In this section, we examine the nature of an Einstein soliton on almost
coKähler manifolds. We begin this section with this theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider an almost coKähler manifold
M^{2n+1}(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
admits an Einstein soliton. If the non-zero potential vector field is pointwise collinear with the Reeb vector field, then the soliton is

(1)

expanding if
r>\|h\|^{2}
,

(2)

steady if
r=\|h\|^{2}
,

(3)

shrinking if
r<\|h\|^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
V=\alpha\xi
, where
\alpha
some non-zero smooth function. Taking the covarient derivative of
V
in the direction of a vector field
X
and using (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}V=\nabla_{X}(\alpha\xi)=X(\alpha)\xi+\alpha\nabla_{X}\xi=X(\alpha)\xi+\alpha h^{\prime}X.

(15)

After expanding (
1
), we have

g(\nabla_{X}V,Y)+g(X,\nabla_{Y}V)+2S(X,Y)=(2\lambda+r)g(X,Y).

(16)

For
V=\alpha\xi
, using (
15
) and the fact that
h^{\prime}
is symmetric, we get from the above equation

X(\alpha)\eta(Y)+Y(\alpha)\eta(X)+2\alpha g(h^{\prime}X,Y)+2S(X,Y)=(2\lambda+r)g(X,Y).

(17)

Now consider at each point
p\in M^{2n+1}
, a local
\varphi
-bases
\{e_{k};0\leq\kappa\leq 2n\}
on
T_{p}M
, the tangent space at
p
. Replacing
X=Y=e_{k}
in the last equation, summing over
\kappa
and using (
5
) (
\operatorname{tr}(h^{\prime})=0
), we acquire

\xi(\alpha)=(2n+1)\lambda+\frac{2n-1}{2}r.

(18)

Putting
Y=\xi
in (
17
) and using the symmetric property of
h^{\prime}
, we obtain

X(\alpha)+\xi(\alpha)\eta(X)+2S(X,\xi)=(2\lambda+r)\eta(X).

(19)

Using (
18
), after some calculations (
19
) gives

X(\alpha)+2S(X,\xi)=-\bigg[(2n-1)\lambda+\frac{2n-3}{2}r\bigg]\eta(X).

(20)

Now, eliminating
X
, using (
7
) we get

\xi(\alpha)=2\|h\|^{2}-\left[(2n-1)\lambda+\frac{2n-3}{2}r\right].

(21)

Compairing (
18
) and (
21
), we obtain

\lambda=\frac{1}{4n}(\|h\|^{2}-r).

(22)

Therefore the soliton is expanding for
r>\|h\|^{2}
, steady for
r=\|h\|^{2}
and shrinking for
r<\|h\|^{2}
.

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
M^{2n+1}(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
be an almost coKähler manifold admitting an Einstein soliton. If the non-zero potential vector field is pointwise collinear with the Reeb vector field and if the Ricci tensor
S=\frac{1}{4n}(\|h\|^{2}+(2n-1)r)g
, then the soliton is trivial.

Proof.

The proof follows from the above theorem. Notice that for
S=\frac{1}{4n}(\|h\|^{2}+(2n-1)r)g
and
\lambda=\frac{1}{4n}(\|h\|^{2}-r)
,
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha\xi}g=0
, that is,
V=\alpha\xi
is Killing. Therefore the soliton is trivial.

∎

Rigidity of Asymptotically Harmonic Manifolds admitting Ricci Solitons

In this section, we exhibit the rigidity of
asymptotically harmonic manifold admitting Ricci
soliton. The results here are inspired by the work
of
[
12
]
. We begin with the defintion of an
asymptoically harmonic manifold.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete manifold without conjugate points. Then the universal covering space
(\tilde{M},g)
is simply-connected, complete and without conjugate points. Therefore, by the Cartan-Hadamard theorem
\tilde{M}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Thus it is non-compact.

Busemann function:

Let
\gamma_{v}
be a geodesic line in
\tilde{M}
, then the two
Busemann functions

associated to
\gamma_{v}
are defined as
[
13
]
:

b_{v}^{+}(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}d(x,\gamma_{v}(t))-t,

b_{v}^{-}(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}d(x,\gamma_{v}(t))+t.

Asymptotically harmonic manifold:

(M,g)
is called
asymptotically harmonic
manifold if
\tilde{M}
is so, that is, the mean curvature of all the horospheres of
\tilde{M}
is a constant. Equivalently,
\Delta{b^{\pm}_{v}}\equiv h\geq 0
,
\forall v\in\ S\tilde{M}
, the unit sphere in
T_{p}{\tilde{M}}
.

Our goal is to study Ricci solitons on asymptotically harmonic manifolds. Note that as asymptotically harmonic manifolds are by definition simply connected manifolds, by Poincaré lemma, Ricci soliton
X
is a gradient soliton. Let
X
be a Ricci soliton on
asymptotically harmonic manifold

M
, then
X=\nabla f
, for some function
f:M\rightarrow R
.
Therefore the Ricci soliton equation

S+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{L}_{X}g=\lambda g

(42)

reduces to

S+\nabla^{2}f=\lambda g.

(43)

The simply connected, complete, non-compact, homogeneous and Einstein asymptotically harmonic
manifolds are completely classified in Theorem
1.1

of
[
6
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete, simply connected, non-compact, homogeneous and asymptotically harmonic Einstein manifold. Then
M
is flat,
or rank one symmetric of non-compact type or a nonsymmetric Damek-Ricci space.

The following result is well-known,
its proof can be found in
[
22
]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

If
M
is compact asymptotically harmonic manifold with negative sectional curvature, then M is a rank one locally symmetric space.

Also it is known that
asymptotically harmonic manifold of
constant
h
have volume entropy

h
(cf.
[
22
]
). Suppose that
X
is a trivial soliton. Then
S=\lambda g
. Therefore
(M,g)
is Einstein and asymptotically harmonic. Suppose
\lambda>0
. Then by Bonnet-Myer’s theorem
M
is compact with finite fundamental group and conjugate point occurs at distance
\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{(n-1)\lambda}}
. But, by definition asymptotically harmonic manifolds can’t have conjugate points. Hence shrinking solitons can’t occur.
Now suppose
\lambda=0
, that is,
S=\lambda g=0
. But simply connected and complete Ricci flat asymptotically harmonic manifolds are flat
[
14
]
.

Therefore, from the above results we can
conclude that:

Proposition 6.3
.

If there exists trivial potential vector field
X=0
corresponding to Ricci soliton on a complete, asymptotically harmonic manifold, then it is Einstein, of constant
\lambda
. If
\lambda>0
, then shrinking soliton does not exist on
M
. If
\lambda=0
, then
\tilde{M}
is flat. If

\lambda<0
, then
M
is non-flat manifold of exponential volume growth. In case,
\lambda<0
and if
\tilde{M}
is homogeneous, then
it is rank one symmetric space of non-compact type or Damek-Ricci non-symmetric space. In case,
\lambda<0
and if
K_{M}<0
, then
M
is rank one symmetric of compact type.

Now suppose that
\nabla f
is a non-trivial Killing vector field for the Ricci soliton on asymptotically harmonic manifold
M
. Then
\nabla^{2}f=0
with
f
non-constant.
Therefore,
\nabla f
is a parallel vector field with
\lVert\nabla f\rVert
constant. So,

\frac{1}{2}\Delta\lVert\nabla f\rVert^{2}=\lVert{\nabla}^{2}f\rVert^{2}-S(\nabla f,\nabla f).

This implies that
S(\nabla f,\nabla f)=0
.
As
\nabla f
is a Killing Ricci soliton, we have from Ricci soliton equation,
S=\lambda g,
which implies that
\lambda=0
. Therefore,
S=0
, so that
M
is Ricci flat, hence
\tilde{M}
is flat. And
f=b_{v}^{-}
is potential function on
\tilde{M}

[
14
]
.
Thus we have shown that:

Proposition 6.4
.

If
X
is a Killing vector field for non-trivial Ricci soliton on asymptotically harmonic manifold
M
, then
\tilde{M}
must be flat, and
f=b_{v}^{-}
is the potential function on
\tilde{M}
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Suppose that there exists a non-trivial Ricci soliton on compact asymptotically harmonic manifold
M
, then
M
is Einstein. If scalar curvature,
r=0
, then
\tilde{M}
is flat. If
r<0
and
K_{M}<0
, then
M
is rank one symmetric space of compact type. If
r>0
, Ricci solitons don’t exist.

Proof.

Clearly (
43
) implies

r+\Delta f=\lambda n
. Integrating we get,
\lambda=\frac{r}{n}
. Consequently,

\Delta f=0
, therefore
f
is constant.
Again (
43
) implies that
S=\lambda g,

therefore
M
is Einstein. Now the conclusion follows from Proposition
6.3
.
∎

Proposition 6.6
.

If Ricci soliton on an asymptotically hamronic manifold
M
is of Gaussian type, then
M
is compact quotient of flat space and
f=\frac{\lambda}{2}d(x,p)^{2}
, if
\lambda\neq 0
.

Proof.

By hypothesis,

\nabla^{2}f=(\lambda-r)g
, for
\lambda\neq 0
. The result follows from
[
14
]
.
∎

Our next results are inspired by

[
12
]
.

Proposition 6.7
.

If
(M,g)
admits a gradient Ricci soliton, then

\nabla r=2S(\nabla f).

Proof.

Tracing (
43
) and differentiating we obtain,

\nabla r=-\nabla\Delta f
. We also have
\nabla r=2\operatorname{div}S
. Therefore,
\nabla r=-2\operatorname{div}\nabla^{2}f
.
But by Bochner’s formula:

\operatorname{div}\nabla^{2}f=S(\nabla f)+\nabla\Delta f.

Consequently,
\nabla r=2S(\nabla f)
.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
(M,g)
be an asymptotically harmonic manifold.
Then Ricci curvature of
M
is non-positive.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{M}
be the universal covering space of
M
.
Let
L_{t}={\nabla}^{2}{b_{v}}^{+}
denote the second fundamental form of horospheres
b_{v}^{-1}(t)
of

\tilde{M}
.
Then
L_{t}
satisfies the Riccati equation, that is for

x_{t}\in\{\gamma^{\prime}(t)\}^{\perp}
,

{L_{t}}^{\prime}(x_{t})+{L_{t}}^{2}(x_{t})+R(x_{t},\gamma^{\prime}(t))\gamma^{\prime}(t)=0.

Tracing the above equation, we obtain that

\mbox{tr}{L_{t}}^{2}=-\mbox{Ricci}(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t)).

∎

Proposition 6.9
.

Suppose that
\nabla f(p)\neq 0
for all
p\in M
, and scalar curvature of asymptotically harmonic manifold
M
admitting a Ricci soliton is radially constant. Then
M
is flat.

Proof.

\nabla_{\nabla f}r=2S(\nabla f,\nabla f)=0
, by hypothesis.

Therefore,

S\bigg(\frac{\nabla f(p)}{\lVert\nabla f(p)\rVert},\frac{\nabla f(p)}{\lVert\nabla f(p)\rVert}\bigg)=0,\;\forall p\in M.

(44)

So
M
can’t be asymptotically harmonic manifold of constant
h>0
, as they have strictly negative Ricci curvature. Therefore,
M
is asymptotically harmonic of constant
h=0
. Thus
any Busemann function on
\tilde{M}
satisfies

\Delta b^{+}_{v}=0.

As (
44
) holds, all the radial Ricci curvatures are zero.

Consider the Bochner’s formula for
b^{+}_{v}
.

\frac{1}{2}\Delta\lVert\nabla{b_{v}^{+}}\rVert^{2}=\lVert\nabla^{2}{b_{v}^{+}}\rVert^{2}+S(\nabla{b_{v}^{+}},\nabla b^{+}_{v})

for all
v\in SM
.
The foregoing equation gives
\lVert\nabla^{2}{b_{v}}^{+}\rVert^{2}=0
, which implies
\nabla^{2}b_{v}=0
,
\forall v\in SM
. Consequently, by Ricatti equation,
R(X,\nabla b_{v})\nabla b_{v}=0
.
Hence,
\tilde{M}
is flat and consequently
M

is a compact quotient of a flat space.
∎

Corollary 6.10
.

Suppose that
M
, satisfying the hypothesis of the above proposition, has constant scalar curvature and admits a Ricci soliton with

Proof.

As
\nabla_{\nabla f}r=2S(\nabla f,\nabla f)=0
, the result follows from the above proposition.
∎

Theorem 6.11
.

Suppose that an asymptotically harmonic manifold

admits a Ricci soliton with

. Then (i) if
|X|
attains maximum,
(ii) if for

Proof.

Suppose that
(M,g)
admits a non-trivial Ricci soliton with

. Then by
[

]
, it satisfies

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|X|^{2}=|\nabla X|^{2}-S(X,X).

As
(M,g)
is asymptotically harmonic manifold,
S\leq 0
(Proposition

).
Thus
u=|X|^{2}
is a subharmonic function on
M
.
If
|X|
attains maximum or if

\int_{M}|du|<\infty
, then by Yau
[
21
]
,

is a harmonic function on
M
. Therefore,
|\nabla X|^{2}=S(X,X)\leq 0
, implies
\nabla f
is a parallel vector field. Hence,

.

M
is asymptotically harmonic manifold with minimal horospheres.
M
is flat if

is harmonic and

If a manifold admits a gradient Ricci soliton, then the scalar curvature satisfies

Theorem 6.12

If
(M,g)
is an asymptotically harmonic manifold admitting shrinking or steady soliton with

(\nabla f)(p)\neq 0
. If the

Proof.

Clearly

is an elliptic operator.
And as

-superharmonic

.
Hence, by

.

Proposition 6.13
.

If

On Sibson’s α \alpha -Mutual Information
Amedeo Roberto Esposito, Adrien Vandenbroucque, Michael Gastpar

Abstract
We explore a family of information measures that stems from Rényi’s α \alpha -Divergences with α < 0 \alpha<0 . In particular, we extend the definition of Sibson’s α \alpha -Mutual Information to negative values of α \alpha and show several properties of these objects. Moreover, we highlight how this family of information measures is related to functional inequalities that can be employed in a variety of fields, including lower-bounds on the Risk in Bayesian Estimation Procedures.



I
Introduction

Sibson’s
\alpha
-Mutual Information is a generalization of Shannon’s Mutual Information with several applications in probability, information and learning theory
[
1
]
. In particular, it has been used to provide concentration inequalities in settings where the random variables are
not
independent, with applications to learning theory
[
1
]
. The measure is also connected to Gallager’s exponent function, a central object in the channel coding problem both for rates below and above capacity
[
2
,
3
]
. Moreover, a new operational meaning has been given to the measure with
\alpha\!=\!+\infty
when a novel measure of information leakage has been proposed in
[
4
]
, under the name of Maximal leakage. Similarly to
I_{\alpha}
, Maximal Leakage has recently found applications in learning and probability theory
[
1
]
. In this work we will look at some properties of
I_{\alpha}
with a different perspective and we will extend the definition in order to include negative values of
\alpha
as well. The reason for this extension is strongly operationally driven and will be tied to a family of functional-analytic bounds that we can provide for Sibson’s
\alpha
MI for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

II
Background and definitions

Throughout the paper we will often use the notion of
L^{p}
-norms: let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and consider the measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
, let
f
be a measurable function with respect to the space, then

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mu)}=\left(\int|f|^{p}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(1)

The definition can be extended to values of
p<1
, however in those cases one does not have norms anymore e.g., if
0<p<1
one recovers a quasi-norm but not a norm.

II-A

Sibson’s
\alpha
-Mutual Information

Introduced by Rényi as a generalization of entropy and KL-divergence,
\alpha
-divergence has found many applications ranging from hypothesis testing to guessing and several other statistical inference and coding problems
[
5
]
. Indeed, it has several useful operational interpretations (e.g., hypothesis testing, and the cut-off rate in block coding
[
6
,
7
]
). It can be defined as follows
[
6
]
:

Definition 1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P}),(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{Q})
be two probability spaces. Let
\alpha>0
be a positive real number different from
1
. Consider a measure
\mu
such that
\mathcal{P}\ll\mu
and
\mathcal{Q}\ll\mu
(such a measure always exists, e.g.
\mu=(\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{Q})/2
) and denote with
p,q
the densities of
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}
with respect to
\mu
. The
\alpha
-Divergence of
\mathcal{P}
from
\mathcal{Q}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}\|\mathcal{Q})=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\int p^{\alpha}q^{1-\alpha}d\mu.

(2)

Remark 1
.

The definition is independent of the chosen measure
\mu
. It is indeed possible to show that

\int p^{\alpha}q^{1-\alpha}d\mu=\int\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{1-\alpha}d\mathcal{P}
, and that whenever
\mathcal{P}\ll\mathcal{Q}
or
0<\alpha<1,
we have
\int p^{\alpha}q^{1-\alpha}d\mu=\int\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\alpha}d\mathcal{Q}
, see
[
6
]
.

It can be shown that if
\alpha>1
and
\mathcal{P}\not\ll\mathcal{Q}
then
D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}\|\mathcal{Q})=\infty
. The behaviour of the measure for
\alpha\in\{0,1,\infty\}
can be defined by continuity. In general, one has that
D_{1}(\mathcal{P}\|\mathcal{Q})=D(\mathcal{P}\|\mathcal{Q})
but if
D(\mathcal{P}\|\mathcal{Q})=\infty
or there exists
\beta
such that
D_{\beta}(\mathcal{P}\|\mathcal{Q})<\infty
then
\lim_{\alpha\downarrow 1}D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}\|Q)=D(\mathcal{P}\|\mathcal{Q})
[
6
, Theorem 5]
. For an extensive treatment of
\alpha
-divergences and their properties we refer the reader to
[
6
]
.
Starting from Rényi’s Divergence and the geometric averaging that it involves, Sibson built the notion of Information Radius
[
8
]
:

Definition 2
.

Let
(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})
be a family of probability measures and
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
be a set of weights s.t.
w_{i}\geq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}>0
. Let
\alpha\geq 1
, the information radius of order
\alpha
is defined as:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\min_{\nu\ll\sum_{i}w_{i}\mu_{i}}\log\left(\sum_{i}w_{i}\exp((\alpha-1)D_{\alpha}(\mu_{i}\|\nu))\right).

Suppose now we have two random variables
X,Y
jointly distributed according to
\mathcal{P}_{XY}
. It is possible to generalise Def.
2
and see that the information radius is a special case of the following quantity
[
5
]
:

I_{\alpha}(X,Y)=\min_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y}}D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y}).

(3)

I_{\alpha}(X,Y)
represents a generalisation of Shannon’s Mutual Information and possesses many interesting properties
[
5
]
. Indeed,
\lim_{\alpha\to 1}I_{\alpha}(X,Y)=I(X;Y)
.
On the other hand when
\alpha\to\infty
, we get:
I_{\infty}(X,Y)=\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}_{Y}}\left[\sup_{x:\mathcal{P}_{X}(x)>0}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{XY}(\{x,Y\})}{\mathcal{P}_{X}(\{x\})\mathcal{P}_{Y}(\{Y\})}\right]=\mathcal{L}\left(X\!\!\to\!\!Y\right)
, where
\mathcal{L}\left(X\!\!\to\!\!Y\right)
denotes the Maximal Leakage from
X
to
Y
, a recently defined information measure with an operational meaning in the context of privacy and security
[
4
]
.
For more details on Sibson’s
\alpha
-MI, as well as a closed-form expression, we refer the reader to
[
5
]
, as for Maximal Leakage the reader is referred to
[
4
]
.

II-B

Rényi’s
\alpha
-Divergence - Negative Orders

Not much is known or has been explored on Rényi’s
\alpha
-Divergence with
\alpha<0
. According to Rényi himself
[
9
]
only positive orders can be regarded as information measures. One of the reasons behind this statement is probably the fact that, taking an axiomatic approach to measures of information like the one undertaken in
[
9
]
,
D_{\alpha}
with
\alpha<0
satisfies many of those properties with the wrong sign of inequality. In fact
[
6
]
:

Proposition 1
.

Let
\alpha<0
and
\mu,\nu
be two probability measures such that
\nu\ll\mu
then:

1.

D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\mu)\leq 0
(as opposed to
\geq 0
);

2.

D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\mu)
is
concave
in
\nu
(as opposed to
convex
in
\mu
);

3.

D_{\alpha}
is
upper
semi-continuous in the pair
(\nu,\mu)
in the topology of set-wise convergence (as opposed to
lower
semi-continuous) ;

4.

Let
K
be a Markov Kernel, then
D_{\alpha}(K\nu\|K\mu)\geq D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\mu)
(as opposed to
D_{\alpha}(K\nu\|K\mu)\leq D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\mu)
);

However, some of the properties are maintained, like the following:

Theorem

(​​
[
6
, Thm 39]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in[-\infty,+\infty]
and let
\nu,\mu
be two probability measures such that
\nu\ll\mu
, then
D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\mu)
is non-decreasing in
\alpha
.

And to conclude, let us present a result connecting negative and positive orders:

Lemma 1

(​​
[
6
, Lemma 10]
- Skew Symmetry)
.

For every
\alpha\in(-\infty,+\infty)\setminus\{0,1\}
, let let
\nu,\mu
be two probability measures such that
\nu\equiv\mu

D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\mu)=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}D_{(1-\alpha)}(\mu\|\nu).

(4)

Such a relationship, although very useful, is restricted to cases in which the two measures are equivalent with respect to each other,
i.e.
, absolute continuity holds in both directions:
\nu\ll\mu

and

\mu\ll\nu
otherwise one might incur in settings where one divergence is finite and the other is infinite. However,
D_{\alpha}
with
\alpha<0
is not concave in the second argument.

Counterexample 1
.

Consider a discrete setting and point mass functions. Let
\mu_{1}=(0.32,0.68),\mu_{2}=(0.5,0.5),\nu=(0.13,0.87)
and
\lambda=0.4
. One has that with
\alpha=-2
,
D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\mu_{1})=-0.2855
,
D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\mu_{2})=-0.6744
and, moreover,
-0.5287=D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\lambda\mu_{1}+(1-\lambda)\mu_{2})<\lambda D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\mu_{1})+(1-\lambda)D_{\alpha}(\nu\|\mu_{2})=-0.5188.

III
Definition

The starting point will, once again, be (
3
). From now on we will consider
\alpha
to be always strictly negative unless specified otherwise and we will restrict ourselves to discrete probability measures for simplicity. We thus bring forth the following definition:

Definition 3
.

Let
X,Y
be two random variables whose joint measure is
\mathcal{P}_{XY}
and the corresponding marginals are given by
\mathcal{P}_{X}
and
\mathcal{P}_{Y}
. Let
\alpha\in(-\infty,0)

I_{\alpha}(X,Y)=-\max_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y}}D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y}).

(5)

Remark 2
.

The minus in the definition is included in order to make sure that all the properties of an information measures are satisfied (non-negativity, DPI, etc.). A similar approach can be undertaken in order to define
D_{\alpha}
and
H_{\alpha}
with negative
\alpha
by simply changing the multiplicative constant from
\frac{1}{\alpha-1}
to
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
for Rényi’s divergence and from
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
to
\frac{1}{\alpha-1}
for Rényi’s entropy for negative orders.

This quantity has, much like Sibson’s
\alpha
-MI with
\alpha>0
, a closed-form expression given by the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X,Y
be two random variables whose joint measure is
\mathcal{P}_{XY}
and the corresponding marginals are given by
\mathcal{P}_{X}
and
\mathcal{P}_{Y}
. Let
\alpha<0

I_{\alpha}(X,Y)=-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}_{Y}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}_{X}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left[\left(\frac{d\mathcal{P}_{XY}}{d\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{P}_{Y}}\right)^{\alpha}\right]\right].

(6)

Proof.

We have that for every
\mathcal{Q}_{Y}
,

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\left[\mathcal{P}_{Y|X}^{\alpha}\mathcal{Q}_{Y}^{-\alpha}\right]\right]

(7)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\mathbb{E}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y}}\left[\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\left[\mathcal{P}_{Y|X}^{\alpha}\right]}{Q_{Y}^{\alpha}}\right]

(8)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y}}\left[\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}_{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\left[\mathcal{P}_{Y|X}^{\alpha}\right]}{Q_{Y}}\right]

(9)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\sum_{y}\mathbb{E}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}_{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\left[\mathcal{P}_{Y|X}^{\alpha}\right]

(10)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\sum_{y}\mathbb{E}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}_{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\left[\mathcal{P}_{XY}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{X}^{-\alpha}\right]

(11)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}_{Y}}\left[\mathbb{E}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}_{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal{P}_{X|Y}}{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\right)^{\alpha}\right]\right]

(12)

\displaystyle=-I_{\alpha}(X,Y).

(13)

Where (
9
) follows from the convexity of
x^{\frac{1}{k}},k<0
and Jensen’s inequality.
This implies that for every
\mathcal{Q}_{Y}
:

\displaystyle-I_{\alpha}(X,Y)\geq D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y}).

(14)

Moreover one has that, given

\mathcal{Q}_{Y}^{\star}(y)=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{Y}(y)\left(\sum_{x}\mathcal{P}_{X|Y=y}(x)^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{X}(x)^{1-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}_{Y}}\left[\left(\sum_{x}\mathcal{P}_{X|Y=y}(x)^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{X}(x)^{1-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right]}

(15)

then,

D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}^{\star}_{Y})=-I_{\alpha}(X,Y).

(16)

∎

Remark 3
.

In a setting where both
X
and
Y
are discrete random variables then denoting with
p_{XY}
,
p_{Y}
and
p_{X}
the joint point mass functions and the corresponding marginals and denoting with
p_{X|Y=y}
the conditional point mass functions one has that:

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(X,Y)\!

\displaystyle=\!\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\!\log\!\sum_{y}\!p_{Y}(y)\!\!\left(\!\sum_{x}\!\left(\frac{p_{X|Y=y}(x)}{p_{X}(x)}\!\right)^{\alpha}\!\!\!p_{X}(x)\!\!\right)^{\!\!\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\!\log\!\sum_{y}\!\!\left(\!\sum_{x}p_{Y|X=x}(y)^{\alpha}p_{X}(x)\right)^{\!\!\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(17)

Theorem 2
.

Let
X,Y
be two discrete random variables whose joint point mass function is
p_{XY}
and the corresponding marginals are given by
p_{X}
and
p_{Y}
then

I_{-\infty}(X,Y)=-\log\sum_{y}\left(\min_{x:p_{X}(x)>0}\mathcal{P}_{Y|X=x}(y)\right).

(18)

Proof.

The result follows from noticing that one can re-write
I_{\alpha}
as follows

-I_{\alpha}(X,Y)=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}_{Y}}\left[\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{P}_{X|Y}}{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{X})}\right],

(19)

where
\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{P}_{X|Y}}{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{X})}
denotes the ”
\alpha-
norm” with respect to
\mathcal{P}_{X}
. Taking the limit of
\alpha\to-\infty
one has that
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\to 1
and
\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{P}_{X|Y}}{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{X})}\to\essinf_{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{X|Y}}{\mathcal{P}_{X}}.

The conclusion then follows from simple algebraic manipulations.
∎

Remark 4
.

The quantity
I_{-\infty}
has already appeared in the literature as “Maximal-Cost Leakage”
[
4
, Thm. 15, Eq. (95)]
. Indeed, assuming equivalence between
\mathcal{P}_{XY}
and
\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y}
and using the Skew Symmetry of
D_{\alpha}
one has that
-D_{-\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y})=D_{\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y}\|\mathcal{P}_{XY})
and consequently

\displaystyle I_{-\infty}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=-\max_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y}}D_{-\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y})

(20)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y}}D_{\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y}\|\mathcal{P}_{XY})

(21)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{c}(X\!\!\to\!\!Y).

(22)

IV
Properties

For
I_{\alpha}
with
\alpha<0
we can show the following properties.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X,Y
be two random variables such that
\mathcal{P}_{XY}\ll\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{P}_{Y}
and assume that
\alpha<0
, then:

1.

I_{\alpha}(X,Y)\geq 0
with equality iff
X
and
Y
are independent;

2.

I_{\alpha}(X,Y)\neq I_{\alpha}(Y,X)
;

3.

Let
0>\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{2}
then
I_{\alpha_{1}}(X,Y)\leq I_{\alpha_{2}}(X,Y)
;

4.

I_{\alpha}(X,Y)\leq\mathcal{L}^{c}(X\!\!\to\!\!Y)
for every
-\infty<\alpha<0
;

5.

Let
X-Y-Z
be a Markov Chain,
I_{\alpha}(X,Z)\leq\min\{I_{\alpha}(X,Y),I_{\alpha}(Y,Z)\}
;

6.

\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}I_{\alpha}(X,Y)\right)
is concave in
\mathcal{P}_{Y|X}
and
I_{\alpha}(X,Y)
is convex in
\mathcal{P}_{Y|X}
.

Proof.

1
): We have that
I_{\alpha}(X,Y)=-D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y}^{\star})
and given that
D_{\alpha}\leq 0
if
\alpha\leq 0
we have the non-negativity of
I_{\alpha}
. Moreover, if
X
and
Y
are independent then
\mathcal{Q}^{\star}_{Y}(y)=\mathcal{P}_{Y}(y)
and
D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{P}_{Y})=0
. On the other hand, if
I_{\alpha}(X,Y)=0
then
D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}^{\star}_{Y})=0
which means that
\mathcal{P}_{Y|X=x}=\mathcal{Q}_{Y}^{\star}
, hence
Y
does not depend on
X
.

2
): It’s clear from the definition and shown more concretely in Example
2
.

3
): We have that, denoting with
\mathcal{Q}_{Y}^{\alpha_{2}}=\argmax_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y}}D_{\alpha_{2}}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y})

\displaystyle-I_{\alpha_{1}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\max_{Q_{Y}}D_{\alpha_{1}}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y})

(23)

\displaystyle\geq D_{\alpha_{1}}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y}^{\alpha_{2}})

(24)

\displaystyle\geq D_{\alpha_{2}}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y}^{\alpha_{2}})

(25)

\displaystyle=-I_{\alpha_{2}}(X,Y)

(26)

where (
25
) follows from the non-decreasability of
D_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in[-\infty,\infty]

[
6
, Thm. 39]
.

4
): Follows from
3
) and the fact that
\mathcal{L}^{c}(X\!\!\to\!\!Y)=

\lim_{\alpha\to-\infty}I_{\alpha}(X,Y)
.

5
): let
X-Y-Z
be a Markov chain and let
K_{Z|Y}
denote the corresponding Markov Kernel
K_{Z|Y}:(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{F})\to(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{\hat{F}})
induced by the transition probabilities
\mathcal{P}_{Z|Y}
. Consequently, given any probability measure
\mathcal{Q}_{Y}
one can construct a corresponding
\mathcal{Q}_{Z}=\mathcal{Q}_{Y}K_{Z|Y}
where substantially
\mathcal{Q}_{Z}(z)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y}}[\mathcal{P}_{Z|Y}(z)]
. By the Data-Processing Inequality for
D_{\alpha}
one has that for every
\mathcal{Q}_{Y}
:

-D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XZ}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Z})\leq-D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XY}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y})

(27)

Taking the
\inf
with respect to
\mathcal{Q}_{Y}
leads to

-D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XZ}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}^{\star}_{Z})\leq I_{\alpha}(X,Y)

(28)

and given that
I_{\alpha}(X,Z)\leq-D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XZ}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Z})
for every
\mathcal{Q}_{Z}
the statement follows.
The other inequality follows by observing that considering the Kernel determined by
\mathcal{P}_{XZ|XY}
and denoted by
K_{XZ|YZ}
one has that
\mathcal{P}_{XZ}=\mathcal{P}_{YZ}K_{XZ|YZ}
while
\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Z}=(\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Y})K_{XZ|YZ}
, consequently by the Data-Processing Inequality for
D_{\alpha}
one has that

-D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{XZ}\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{Q}_{Z})\leq-D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{YZ}\|\mathcal{P}_{Y}\mathcal{Q}_{Z}).

(29)

The statement follows from a similar argument as above.

6
): One can rewrite the expression as follows

\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}I_{\alpha}(X,Y)\right)=\sum_{y}\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{Y|X}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{X})}

(30)

and concavity follows from the Reverse-Minkowski’s inequality.
Convexity of
I_{\alpha}(X,Y)
follows from the concavity just proven and the fact that
-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log(x)
is a non-increasing convex function for a given
\alpha
and composition of a non-increasing convex function with a concave one gives rise to a convex function
[
10
, Eq (3.10), pag. 84]
.
∎

Following
[
5
]
we will now look at some specific choices of
X
and
Y
and compute the corresponding values of
I_{\alpha}
. Given that the expression for the information measure is essentially identical for
\alpha<0
and
\alpha
, the values in these examples match the one for
I_{\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
except for a minus sign that ensures the non-negativity of the measure.

Example 1
.

Let
X,Y\sim\text{Ber}(1/2)
and let
\mathbb{P}(Y\neq X)=\delta
then if
\alpha<0

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(X,Y)=I_{\alpha}(Y,X)=-d_{\alpha}(\delta\|1/2),

(31)

where
d_{\alpha}(p\|q)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log(p^{\alpha}q^{1-\alpha}+(1\!-p)^{\alpha}(1\!-q)^{1-\alpha})
denotes the binary
\alpha
-divergence.

Example 2
.

Let
X\sim\text{Ber}(1/2)
and let
Y\in\{0,1,e\}
. Assume also that

P_{Y|X=x}(y)=\begin{cases}1-\delta,\,\,x=y\\
\delta,\,\,y=e\\
0,\text{ else}.\end{cases}

(32)

Then, if
\alpha<0

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log_{2}\left(\delta+(1-\delta)2^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\right)

(33)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(Y,X)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log_{2}\left(\delta+(1-\delta)2^{\alpha-1}\right)

(34)

Example 3
.

Let
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{X})
and
N\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{N})
with
N
independent from
X
then if
-\frac{\sigma^{2}_{N}}{\sigma^{2}_{X}}<\alpha<0

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(X,X+N)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\alpha\frac{\sigma^{2}_{X}}{\sigma^{2}_{N}}\right).

(35)

This example shows a major difference in behaviour between the two families of information measures: the value of
I_{\alpha}(X,X+N)
has the familiar expression only if
|\alpha|\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}_{N}}{\sigma^{2}_{X}}
.

V
Bound

Theorem 4
.

Let
X,Y
be two random variables whose joint measure is
\mathcal{P}_{XY}
and the corresponding marginals are given by
\mathcal{P}_{X}
and
\mathcal{P}_{Y}
.
For every
f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
one has that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}_{XY}}[f(X,Y)]\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}_{\mathcal{P}_{Y}}\left[\mathbb{E}^{\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\beta}}_{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\left[f(X,Y)^{\beta}\right]\right]

(36)

\displaystyle\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}_{\mathcal{P}_{Y}}\left[\mathbb{E}^{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\alpha}}_{\mathcal{P}_{X}}\left[\left(\frac{d\mathcal{P}_{XY}}{d\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{P}_{Y}}\right)^{\alpha}\right]\right],

(37)

where
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=1=\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}
and
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}<1
.

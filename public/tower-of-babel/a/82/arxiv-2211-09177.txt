Simplicial Volume of Closed Locally Homogeneous Riemmannian Manifolds
Peng Hui How

Abstract
. We show (in Theorem 1.2 ) that every closed, locally homogeneous Riemannian manifold with positive simplicial volume must be homeomorphic to a locally symmetric space of non-compact type, giving a converse to [ LS06 ] within the scope of closed, locally homogeneous Riemannian manifolds. This characterizes all closed locally homogeneous Riemannian manifolds with nonzero simplicial volume.



1.
Introduction

In
[
Gro82
]
, Gromov introduced the following non-negative topological invariant for a connected, closed manifold:

Definition 1
.

Let
M
be a connected, closed,
n

If

is oriented, the

of

-norm of its fundamental class
[M]\in H_{n}(M;\mathbb{R})

\lVert M\rVert=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in C_{k}(M;\mathbb{R})\\
\partial{c}=0\\
{[c]}=[M]\end{subarray}}\lVert c\rVert_{1}

\lVert\sum_{i}c_{i}\sigma_{i}\rVert_{1}=\sum_{i}\lvert c_{i}\rvert,

If

Before we proceed with our main result, we recall two definitions, in their bare minimal forms.
For a more conceptual and comprehensive formulation, see

Definition 2

Let

Remark 1.1
.

Since
\lVert M\rVert
is a topological invariant of
M
, to answer whether or not
\lVert M\rVert=0
, it suffices to analyze its topological type.
In particular, it suffice to analyze its diffeomorphism type.
In Lemma
3.1
, we show that
M
is diffeomorphic to a double coset manifold
\Gamma\backslash G/K
where

•

G
is a Lie group with finitely many connected components.

•

K
is a compact subgroup of
G
.

•

\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of

that acts freely on

Conversely, for any

This allows us to treat locally homogeneous Riemannian manifolds as double coset manifolds interchangably.

Let

be a closed locally homogeneous Riemannian manifold.
A criterion for

is of natural interest.
In
[
LS06
]
, applying estimates of Connell-Farb in
[

]
on the barycenter map in higher rank, Lafont-Schmidt showed that every closed, locally symmetric space of non-compact type has positive simplicial volume.
In this paper, we prove the converse:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a closed topological manifold that admits a smooth structure that admits a locally homogeneous Riemannian metric.
Then
\lVert M\rVert>0
if and only if

is a closed, locally symmetric space of non-compact type.

A notion closely related to local homogeneity is Thurston model geometry; see
[

In this language, Theorem

immediately implies the following:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold modeled on some Thurston geometry.
Then
\lVert M\rVert>0
if and only if
M
is homeomorphic to a closed locally symmetric space of non-compact type.

Remark 1.4
.

Let

.
The special cases of

were essentially solved by Gromov.
The special case of

(resp.

]
(resp. Neofytidis-Zhang in

We are now ready to state the structure theorem for closed, locally homogeneous Riemannian manifolds.

Proof.

We use the notations in Theorem

.
The only thing to be proven is that

To do so, we recall the following:

Proposition 2.2

(
[
Mor01
, §12.1,§12.2]
)
.

(1)

The following types of groups are amenable: compact, solvable.

(2)

The amenable property is preserved under: group extension, quotients, taking closed subgroups, taking finite-index subgroups.

Continuing with the proof of Corollary
2.1
, since
G
is (solvable-by-compact-by-abelian)-by-compact, by Proposition

,

is amenable.

To prove that

Since

By Proposition

, since the amenable property is closed under group extension, it suffices to show that

is abelian.
This holds, since the long exact sequence of fibration for

yields the short exact sequence of groups

1\to\pi_{1}(K)\to\pi_{1}(G)\to\pi_{1}(G/K)\to 1,

By elementary algebraic topology, the fundamental group of any topological group is abelian.
Thus, in particular,
\pi_{1}(G)
is abelian, and thus
\pi_{1}(G/K)
is also abelian.
Since

is abelian, by Proposition

3.
The Structure Theorem for Closed, Locally Homogeneous Riemannian Manifolds

Our goal in this section is to prove Theorem
1.6
(the structure theorem for closed locally homogeneous Riemannian manifolds).
To start off, it is crucial to note that a locally homogeneous Riemannian manifold is diffeomorphic to a double coset manifold.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a locally homogeneous Riemannian manifold.
Then, there exists

•

G
a Lie group with finitely many connected-components

•

K
a compact subgroup of
G

•

\Gamma
a discrete subgroup of
G
that acts freely on
G/K

where

(1)

The universal cover
\widetilde{M}
of
M
is diffeomorphic to
G/K
, and

(2)

M
is diffeomorphic to
\Gamma\backslash G/K
.

Proof.

Let
M
is a locally homogeneous Riemannian manifold.
By Singer
[
Sin60
]
, its universal cover
\widetilde{M}
, equipped with the pullback metric, is a homogeneous Riemannian manifold.
Let
G
be the isometry group of
\widetilde{M}
, and
K
the isotropy subgroup of any arbitrary point in
\widetilde{M}
. Since
K
is the image of
\mathfrak{o(\mathfrak{g})}
under the exponential map, it is compact.

We claim that
\widetilde{M}
is diffeomorphic to
G/K
.
To see this, note that by assumption,
G
acts transitively on
\widetilde{M}
.
If
G
acts on
\widetilde{M}
on the left (resp. right), then, as Riemannian manifolds,
\widetilde{M}=G/K
(resp.
\widetilde{M}=K\backslash G
), where the latter is equipped with a Riemannian metric descended from a left-invariant (resp. right-invariant) Riemannian metric on
G
.
In particular, as smooth manifolds,
\widetilde{M}=G/K
, as desired.

We claim that
G
has finitely many connected components.
To see this, note that since
\widetilde{M}
is simply-connected, it follows from the long exact sequence of fibration of
K\to G\to\widetilde{M}
that
\pi_{0}(G)\cong\pi_{0}(K)
.
Since
K
is compact, thus
\pi_{0}(K)
is finite, and so is
\pi_{0}(G)
, as desired.

With above in mind, let
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
.
Since it is the deck transformation group of
\widetilde{M}\to M
, which is a local isometry, thus
\pi_{1}(M)
is a discrete subgroup of
G
that acts freely on
\widetilde{M}=G/K
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Now, recall that Theorem
1.6
is a statement that holds up to finite cover.
Thus, with the following (a corollary of Lemma
3.1
), we reduce our consideration to the case when
M
is a double coset manifold
\Gamma\backslash G/K
where
G
is a connected Lie group.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
M
be a closed locally homogeneous Riemannian manifold.
Then
M
has a finite-sheeted cover given by a double coset manifold
\Gamma\backslash G/K
where

•

G
is a connected Lie group

•

K
is a compact subgroup of
G

•

\Gamma
is a cocompact lattice in
G

Proof.

Let
M
be a closed locally homogeneous Riemannian manifold.
By Lemma
3.1
, there exists

•

G^{\prime}
a Lie group with finitely many connected-components

•

K^{\prime}
a compact subgroup of
G

•

\Gamma^{\prime}
a discrete subgroup of
G^{\prime}
that acts freely on
G^{\prime}/K^{\prime}

where

(1)

The universal cover
\widetilde{M}
of
M
is diffeomorphic to
G^{\prime}/K^{\prime}
, and

(2)

M
is diffeomorphic to
\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}/K^{\prime}
.

With above in mind, let

•

G
be the identity connected component of
G^{\prime}
.

•

K=K^{\prime}\cap G
(
K
is a compact subgroup in
G
).

•

\Gamma=\Gamma^{\prime}\cap G
.

Since
G
is a finite-index subgroup of
I
, thus
\Gamma
is a finite-index subgroup of
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
Thus,
\Gamma\backslash G/K=\Gamma\backslash G^{\prime}/K^{\prime}
is a finite-sheeted cover of
M
.

It remains to justify that
\Gamma
is a cocompact lattice in
G
, equivalently,
\Gamma\backslash G
is compact.
Since
K
is compact, this is equivalent to
\Gamma\backslash G/K
being compact.
This holds, since
\Gamma\backslash G/K
is a finite-sheeted cover of the compact
M
.
This completes the proof.
∎

From now on, we assume that
M=\Gamma\backslash G/K
, where
G
is a connected Lie group, and

•

K
is a compact subgroup of
G

•

\Gamma
is a cocompact lattice of
G

With this in mind, in order to prove Theorem
1.6
, the following Lemma about Lie groups will be crucial.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. Then there is a short exact sequence of connected Lie groups

1\to H\to G\xrightarrow{p}\overline{G}\to 1

where

(1)

H
is lattice hereditary (i.e. every lattice
\Gamma
in
G
yields a lattice
\Gamma\cap H
as a lattice in
H
)

(2)

H
is solvable-by-compact-by-abelian

(3)

\overline{G}
is a semisimple Lie group without compact factors and without center.

Proof.

We will define a sequence of surjective Lie group homomorphisms.

p:G=:G_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{1}}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}G_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{2}}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}G_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{3}}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}G_{3}=:\overline{G}

Let
G^{sol}
be a solvradical (maximal connected solvable normal Lie subgroup) of
G
.
By
[
Rag72
]
,
G^{sol}
exists, and is unique up to conjugation.
Fix a choice of
G^{sol}
.
A classical result (Levi decomposition) states that
G_{1}:=G/G^{sol}
is semisimple.
Let
p_{1}:G\to G_{1}
be the canonical projection map,
so that
\ker(p_{1})=G^{sol}
is solvable.

Let
G_{1}^{K}
be a compact radical (maximal connected compact normal Lie subgroup) of
G_{1}
.
Then,
G_{2}:=G_{1}/G_{1}^{K}
is a semisimple Lie group without compact factor.
Let
p_{2}:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be the canonical projection map,
so that
\ker(p_{2})=G_{1}^{K}
is compact.

Let
G_{2}^{Z}
be the center of
G_{2}
.
By
[
Kna96
, § 3, Proposition 6.30]
,
G_{3}:=G_{2}/G_{2}^{Z}
is a semisimple Lie group without center and without compact factors.
Let
p_{3}:G_{2}\to G_{3}
be the canonical projection map,
so that
\ker(p_{3})=G_{2}^{Z}
is abelian.

Now, let
p:=p_{3}\circ p_{2}\circ p_{1}
, and
H:=\ker(p)
.
Then, by construction (c.f. Remark
1.5
),
\ker(p)
is solvable-by-compact-by-abelian.
Thus, combining the above, this proves 1 and 3.

It remains to prove 2, i.e.
H=\ker(p)
is lattice hereditary.
To do so, we recall the following the following technical proposition:

Proposition 3.4

(
[
Gen15
, Theorem 2.6]
,
[
OV00
]
,
[
Rag72
, Theorem 1.13 (statement and proof)]
)
.

Let
G
be a Lie group.
Let
H<G
be a closed subgroup, and let
\Gamma<G
be lattice.
If either
\Gamma
is cocompact or
H
is normal, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Gamma\cap H
is a lattice in
H
(in this case, we say that
H
is
\Gamma
-hereditary
).

(2)

\Gamma\cap H
is a cocompact lattice in
H
(when
\Gamma
is cocompact).

(3)

The image of
\Gamma
in
G/H
is a lattice (when
H
is normal).

(4)

The image of
\Gamma
in
G/H
is a cocompact lattice (when
H
is normal and
\Gamma
is cocompact).

Note that it will be used repeatedly, even in the main proof of Theorem

.

To see this, let
\Lambda
be any lattice in
G
.
By the Bieberbach-Auslander-Wang Theorem
[
BK18
, Proposition 2.5]
,
G^{N}:=\ker(p_{2}\circ p_{1})
is lattice hereditary in
G
.
Thus,
\Lambda\cap G^{N}
is a lattice in
G^{N}
.
By Proposition
3.4
,
\Lambda_{2}:=p_{2}(p_{1}(\Lambda))=\Lambda/(\Lambda\cap G^{N})
is a lattice in
N_{2}
.
By
[
Rag72
, Proposition 5.18]
,
\ker(p_{3})=G_{2}^{Z}
is lattice hereditary in
G_{2}
.
Thus,
\Lambda_{2}\cap G_{2}^{Z}
is a lattice in
G_{2}^{Z}
.
By Proposition
3.4
,
p(\Lambda)=p_{3}(\Lambda_{2})
is a lattice in
G_{3}
.
By Proposition

is a lattice in

, as desired.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

It might be tempting to use the Levi decomposition to justify Lemma

Unfortunately, a solvradical need not be lattice hereditary, even when
\Gamma
is cocompact.
To see this, consider
G=\mathbb{R}\times SO(3)
, so that
G^{sol}=\mathbb{R}
.
Let
A
an infinite order element in

and
\Gamma:=\rho(\mathbb{Z})
where

\displaystyle\rho:\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\to G

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\mapsto(n,A^{n}).

Then
\Gamma
is a cocompact lattice in

where
\Gamma\cap S=\{0\}
, which is clearly not cocompact in

.

We are now ready to use Lemma
3.3
to prove Theorem
1.6
.

First, we would like to specify the base of the fiber bundle in Theorem
1.6
, which is supposed to be a closed locally symmetric space of non-compact type, as a double coset manifold given by the centerless semisimple
\overline{G}
.
With
\overline{K}^{\max}
being any fixed maximal compact subgroup of
\overline{G}
that contains
p(K)
, we have a symmetric space of non-compact type
\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
.
We would like to specify a cocompact lattice
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}
in
\overline{G}
that acts freely on
\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
, which would yield a closed locally symmetric space of non-compact type
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}\backslash\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
.
A natural candidate for
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}
is
\overline{\Gamma}:=p(\Gamma)=\Gamma/(\Gamma\cap H)
.
However, a priori, there are two potential problems:

(1)

\overline{\Gamma}
might not be a cocompact lattice (or even a discrete subgroup) in
\overline{G}
.

(2)

Even if we assume that
\overline{\Gamma}
is discrete,
we can only guarantee that
\overline{\Gamma}
acts on
\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
with finite point-stabilizer (since the stabilizer of the point
\overline{g}\overline{K}^{\max}\in\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
is
\overline{\Gamma}_{\overline{g}\overline{K}^{\max}}=\Gamma\cap\overline{g}\overline{K}^{\max}\overline{g}^{-1}
, which is discrete and compact, hence finite).
In particular,
\overline{\Gamma}
might not act freely on
\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
.

Fortunately, it turns out that
\overline{\Gamma}
is a virtually torsion-free, cocompact lattice in
\overline{G}
,
so that it admits a finite-index subgroup that satisfies the required conditions for
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}
.

Claim 1
.

Let
\overline{\Gamma}
and
\overline{G}
be as above.
Then
\overline{\Gamma}
admits a finite-index subgroup
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}
such that the double coset manifold
B=\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}\backslash\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
is a closed locally symmetric space of non-compact type.

Proof.

Since
\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
is a symmetric space of non-compact type, to prove the claim, it suffices to show that
\overline{\Gamma}
admits a finite-index subgroup
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}
is a torsion-free, cocompact lattice in
\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
, so that
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}\backslash\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
is a closed locally symmetric space of non-compact type.
Since any finite-index subgroup of a cocompact lattice in
\overline{G}
is still a finite-index subgroup of a cocompact lattice in
\overline{G}
, it suffices to prove that
\overline{\Gamma}
is a virtually torsion-free, cocompact lattice in
\overline{G}
.
To complete the proof, we prove the following facts about
\overline{\Gamma}
accordingly:

(1)

\overline{\Gamma}
is virtually torsion-free:
Since
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a compact manifold, it is finitely-generated.
Since
\overline{\Gamma}
is a quotient of
\Gamma
, it is also finitely-generated.
By Lemma
3.3
,
\overline{G}
is a centerless semisimple Lie group, thus its adjoint map is injective, and thus it is linear.
Thus, altogether,
\overline{\Gamma}
is a finitely-generated linear group over field of characteristic-
0
, thus by a classical result of Selberg, it is virtually torsion-free.

(2)

\overline{\Gamma}
is a cocompact lattice in
\overline{G}
:
By Lemma
3.3
,
H
is lattice hereditary, thus
\Gamma\cap H
is a lattice in
H
.
By Proposition
3.4
,
\Gamma\cap H
is a cocompact lattice in
H
.
By Proposition
3.4
again,
\overline{\Gamma}
is a cocompact lattice in
\overline{G}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

To proceed, we fix a choice of
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}
as in Claim
1
.
The resulting double coset manifold
B:=\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}\backslash\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
will be the base of the fiber bundle in Theorem
1.6
.

Next, we specify a finite-sheeted cover of
M
that covers
B
, that will serve as the total space in Theorem
1.6
.

Claim 2
.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}:=p^{-1}(\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime})\cap\Gamma
.
The double coset manifold
M^{\prime}:=\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G/K
is a finite cover of
M
.

Proof.

By construction,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a finite-index subgroup of
\Gamma
,
thus
\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G/K
is a finite cover of
\Gamma\backslash G/K=M
.
∎

We are now ready to specify the fiber bundle in the statement of Theorem
1.6
.
The choice of the projection map is the most obvious one (i.e. the canonical coset map).
The key point here is that it is indeed a fiber bundle.

Claim 3
.

Recall that
M^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G/K
and
B=\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}\backslash\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
.
Let
f:M^{\prime}\to B
be the (surjective) canonical coset (set-theoretic) map.
Then
f
is a fiber bundle with compact fiber.

Proof.

By the Ehresmann fibration theorem, it suffices to prove that
f
is a proper submersion.
Then, since the total space if compact, it follows immediately that both the fiber and the base are compact.
To complete the proof, we prove the following accordingly:

(1)

f
is a surjective submersion:
This follows from the following, in logical order:

(a)

p:G\to\overline{G}
is a surjective submersion: this holds by the fact that
H
is a closed subgroup of
G
and Cartan’s closed subgroup theorem

(b)

\overline{p}:G/K\to\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
(with
\overline{p}
being the canonical coset map) is a surjective submersion: this follows from the following facts:

(i)

\overline{p}
is smooth (as it descends from the smooth
p
) with respect to proper (right) actions by
K
(resp.
\overline{K}^{\max}
)

(ii)

\overline{p}
is equivariant with respective to the transitive left
G
-action (c.f.
[
Lee12
, Theorem 7.25 (equivariant rank theorem)]
)

(c)

f:\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G/K\to\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}\backslash\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
is a surjective submersion: this follows from the following facts:

(i)

\overline{p}
is
p
-equivariant (i.e.
l_{p(g)}\circ\overline{p}=\overline{p}\circ l_{g}
for all
g\in G
)

(ii)

\Gamma^{\prime}
(resp.
\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}=p(\Gamma^{\prime})
) acts freely and properly discontinuously on
G/K
(resp.
\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
)

(2)

\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}\backslash\overline{G}/\overline{K}^{\max}
is compact: this follows from the fact that
\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G/K
is compact and
f
is surjective.

This completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we show that the fiber of
f
is connected, and is presented as a double coset manifold as in the statement of Theorem
1.6
.

Claim 4
.

Let
F
be the fiber of
f
.
Then
F
is diffeomorphic to
(\Gamma^{\prime}\cap P)\backslash P/K
where
P
is a connected, (solvable-by-compact-by-abelian)-by-compact subgroup of
G
.
(The connectedness of
P
immediately implies the connectedness of
F
.)

Proof.

By Claim
3
,
F
is compact.

To complete the proof, note that the fiber
F
is homeomorphic to the preimage at the identity double coset point in the base
B
, i.e.

F=f^{-1}(\text{Id})=(\Gamma^{\prime}\cap P)\backslash P/K.

where
P=p^{-1}(\overline{K}^{\max})
.

Note that
P
is the total space of the fiber bundle given by restricting the base of the fiber bundle (given by the short exact sequence of the underlying Lie groups)

H\to G\to\overline{G}

to
\overline{K}^{\max}
.
Thus,

(1)

there exists a short exact sequence of Lie groups

1\to H\to P\to\overline{K}^{\max}\to 1.

By Lemma
3.3
,
H
is solvable-by-compact-by-abelian.
Since
\overline{K}^{\max}
is compact, thus
P
is (solvable-by-compact-by-abelian)-by-compact.

(2)

Since
\overline{G}
is homotopy equivalent to
\overline{K}^{\max}
, thus
P
is homotopy equivalent to
G
, and hence is connected.

This completes the proof.
∎

Since Claims 1-4 form the statement of Theorem
1.6
, this completes the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

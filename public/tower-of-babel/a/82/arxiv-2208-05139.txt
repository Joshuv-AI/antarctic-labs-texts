Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of Representations of GL n {\mathrm{GL}}_{n} over a non-archimedean local field
Kenta Suzuki

Abstract
. We calculate the asymptotic behavior of the dimension of the fixed vectors of π \pi with respect to compact open subgroups 1 + M n ​ ( 𝔭 N ) ⊂ GL n ​ ( F ) 1+M_{n}({\mathfrak{p}}^{N})\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F) for π \pi an admissible representation of GL n ​ ( F ) {\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F) , and F F a nonarchimedean local field. Such dimensions can be calculated by germs of the character of π \pi . We also make some observations on how those dimensions behave under instances of Langlands functoriality, such as the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence and cyclic base change, where relations between characters are known.



1.
Introduction

Smooth representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
, for a nonarchimedean local field
F
such as
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
or
\mathbb{F}_{p}((t))
, is of great interest in number theory, for instance due to the local Langlands correspondence. However, most such representations are infinite-dimensional, so finding a finite measure of their size is important. One such measure is the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension:

Definition
.

Let
\pi
be a (complex) admissible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
. The
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension
(henceforth, GK-dimension) of
\pi
is a real number
r\geq 0
, denoted
\dim_{GK}(\pi)
, such that

\dim(\pi^{K_{N}})\sim q^{rN},

where
K_{N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}1+M_{n}({\mathfrak{p}}_{F}^{N})
is a compact open subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
and
q
is the order of the residue field
{\mathscr{O}}_{F}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{F}
. Here, for functions
f(N)
and
g(N)
, denote
f(N)\sim g(N)
if there exists real numbers
0<a<b
such that for sufficiently large integers
N>0
, the inequality
af(N)<g(N)<bf(N)
is satisfied.

In fact, Harish-Chandra and Howe’s local character formula says this function
\dim(\pi^{K_{N}})
is a polynomial in
q^{N-1}
for
N
large (see Corollary
5.7
), which we call the
growth polynomial
of
\pi
. Now,
\dim_{GK}(\pi)
is exactly the degree of the growth polynomial (and, in particular, is an integer). A more ambitious goal would be to calculate the entire growth polynomial. The coefficients of the growth polynomial contain important information about the representation, as they behave well under instances of Langlands functoriality (see Section
6
.)

Irreducible representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
were classified by Bernstein and Zelevinsky
[
21
]
in terms of multisegments—combinatorial data—of supercuspidal representations (those corresponding to irreducible representations under the Langlands correspondence). Roughly, arbitrary representations are obtained by inducing from parabolic subgroups, and supercuspidal representations are those not obtained from such a procedure.

For supercuspidal representations
\rho
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
, it is a result of Howe
[
12
]
that
\dim_{GK}(\rho)=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
, and in fact, the leading term of the growth polynomial is known (see Proposition
5.8
):

\dim(\rho^{K_{N}})=(1+o(1))[n!]_{q}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}(N-1)},

where
[n!]_{q}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[1]_{q}\cdots[n]_{q}=\frac{q-1}{q-1}\cdots\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
is the
q
-factorial. This was extended to generic representations by Rodier
[
15
]
.

We further extend these results, and calculate the leading term of the growth polynomial for
arbitrary
admissible representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
, based on the combinatorial datum of the Bernstein-Zelevinsky classification
[
21
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\pi
be an arbitrary admissible irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
. Then, there exists a multisegment
a=\{\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{m}\}
such that
\rho=\langle a\rangle
, where
\Delta_{i}=[\rho_{i},\nu^{r_{i}-1}\rho_{i}]
for supercuspidal representations
\rho_{i}
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{i}}(F)
. Then,

\dim(\pi^{K_{N}})=(1+o(1))\frac{[n!]_{q}}{[r_{1}!]_{q^{n_{1}}}\cdots[r_{m}!]_{q^{n_{m}}}}(q^{N-1})^{\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-n_{1}r_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{m}r_{m}^{2})},

so in particular,

\dim_{GK}(\pi)=\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-n_{1}r_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{m}r_{m}^{2}).

In fact, we are able to express the growth polynomial of arbitrary admissible representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)

exactly
in terms of growth polynomials for supercuspidal representations. As a consequence, we deduce that
\dim(\pi^{K_{N}})
is an
integer-coefficient
polynomial in
q^{N-1}
(see Corollary
3.8
), generalizing a result of Howe
[
12
]
.

The difficulty is that although every irreducible representation of
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
appears as a subquotient of some induced representation
\nInd_{P}^{G}(\rho)
with
\rho
a supercuspidal representation of the parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
, such representations may have
multiple
irreducible subquotients, and we can a priori only detect the maximal GK-dimension (see Lemma
3.4
). We identify exactly the irreducible subquotient of
\nInd_{P}^{G}(\rho)
with maximal GK-dimension (see Lemma
3.9
) and for each irreducible representation
\pi
find a pair
(P,\rho)
such that
\pi
has maximal GK-dimension among the irreducible subquotients of
\nInd_{P}^{G}(\rho)
. Here,
\rho
may no longer be supercuspidal, but are products of representation of the form
\langle\Delta\rangle
for some segment
\Delta
, whose growth polynomial is calculated in Subsection
3.1
.

The paper is structured as follows: In section
2
we gather the notation used throughout the paper. In section
3
we prove the main result, the calculation of the leading term of the growth polynomial for arbitrary irreducible representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
. In section
4
we calculate some examples, corresponding to those multisegments with particularly simple combinatorial structure. In section
5
we look at Howe’s local character formula, and reproduce a proof that indeed
\dim(\pi^{K_{N}})
is a polynomial in
q^{N-1}
. Finally, in section
6
we explore how growth polynomials behave under some instances of Langlands functoriality, such as Jacquet-Langlands, base change, and automorphic induction.

We also briefly consider the growth polynomial for representations of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
in appendix
A
, which we deduce from the theory for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
. In appendix
B
we provide a more direct proof of
\dim_{GK}(\rho)=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
for
\rho
supercuspidal, based on Bushnell and Kutzko’s classification
[
7
]
. Finally, in appendix
C
we provide explicit calculations for growth polynomials of level zero representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{3}
, based on the techniques developed in appendix
B
.

2.
Terminology and Conventions

Throughout the paper,
F
is a nonarchimedean local field of residual characteristic
p
. That is,
F
is a finite extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
or
\mathbb{F}_{p}((T))
. Let
{\mathscr{O}}_{F}
denote the ring of integers of
F
, and let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{F}\subset{\mathscr{O}}_{F}
denote the unique maximal ideal. Moreover, let
q_{F}
denote the size of the residue field
k_{F}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathscr{O}}_{F}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{F}
. Subscripts are dropped whenever
F
is clear from context.

Note that
F
is assumed to have characteristic zero in Sections
5
and
6
.

Fix a nontrivial character
\psi\colon F\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
of level zero, i.e.,
\psi
is trivial on
{\mathfrak{p}}_{F}
but not on
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}
.

By a
representation
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
, we always mean a complex, admissible, smooth representations. That is, we will only consider representations
(\pi,V)
such that
\pi\colon{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)
is continuous with respect to the discrete topology on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)
, and such that for any compact open subgroup
K\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
the space of fixed vectors

V^{K}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{v\in V:\pi(k)v=v\text{ for any }k\in K\}

is finite-dimensional. The notation
\pi
is used for arbitrary admissible smooth representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
, while
\rho
is reserved for supercuspidal representations. We will denote induction by
\Ind
, compact induction by
\cInd
, and normalized induction by
\nInd
(i.e.,
\nInd_{H}^{G}(\pi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Ind_{H}^{G}(\delta_{G}^{1/2}\delta_{H}^{-1/2}\otimes\pi)
).

Let
\mathcal{R}
be the Grothendieck ring of representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}
, i.e., the free abelian group generated by representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
, modulo
\phi-\pi-\sigma
for any exact sequence

0\to\pi\to\phi\to\sigma\to 0

of representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
. It becomes a graded commutative ring by the product defined in Section
3
(see also
[
21
, Thm 1.9]
).

We define a series of compact open subgroups of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
, by
K_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathscr{O}}_{F})
, the maximal compact subgroup, and for
N>0
, by

K_{N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}1+M_{n}({\mathfrak{p}}_{F}^{N})=\ker(K_{0}\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathscr{O}}_{F}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{F}^{N})).

The subgroups
\{K_{N}\}_{N\geq 0}
form a fundamental system of neighborhoods of
1\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
.

Definition
.

Let
\pi
be a representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
. Then, the
growth polynomial
of
\pi
is the polynomial
G_{\pi}(X)\in{\mathbb{C}}[X]
such that for
N\gg 0
,

\dim(\pi^{K_{N}})=G_{\pi}(q^{N-1}).

Such a polynomial exists by Corollary
5.7
and Lemma
3.4
. Denote by
\dim_{GK}(\pi)
the degree of
G_{\pi}
.

Finally, let
\mathrm{rec}_{F}\colon\mathbf{Irr}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F))\to\mathcal{G}_{n}(F)
be the local Langlands correspondence, where
\mathbf{Irr}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F))
is the set of irreducible representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
and
\mathcal{G}_{n}(F)
is the set of semisimple Deligne representations of the Weil group
\mathcal{W}_{F}
(a close relative to the absolute Galois group
\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
of
F
).

3.
Reduction to supercuspidal representations

Notation and terminology in this section mostly follows
[
21
]
, so
\nu\colon F^{\times}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
is the character
\nu(g)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}|\det(g)|
, and a
segment
is a set of supercuspidal representations of the form
[\rho,\nu^{r-1}\rho]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\rho,\nu\rho,\dots,\nu^{r-1}\rho\}
for some supercuspidal representation
\rho
and integer
r>0
. By
[
21
, Thm 6.1]
irreducible representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
are classified by
multisegments
—multisets of segments.

A key construction in Bernstein and Zelevinsky’s classification is inducing from parabolic subgroups
P\subset G
. Conjugacy classes of
P
correspond to partitions of
n
. Indeed, a partition
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}
, denoted
\lambda
, corresponds to

P_{\lambda}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{1}}&*&&*\\
&{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{2}}&*\\
&&\ddots&*\\
0&&&{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{r}}\end{pmatrix}\subset G.

Let
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r}
be admissible representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{1}},\dots,{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{r}}
, respectively. Following the notation in
[
21
]
, we denote

\pi_{1}\times\cdots\times\pi_{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\nInd_{P_{\lambda}}^{G}(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\pi_{r}),

where
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\pi_{r}
is inflated via the surjection
P_{\lambda}\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{r}}
, which quotients out the unipotent radical
N_{\lambda}\subset P_{\lambda}
.

The following lemma on the size of the double cosets
P_{\lambda}\backslash G/K_{N}
is useful:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda
be the partition
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}
. Then,

|P_{\lambda}\backslash G/K_{N}|=\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\end{bmatrix}_{q}(q^{N-1})^{(n^{2}-n_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{r}^{2})/2}

for
N>0
, where
\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\end{bmatrix}_{q}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{[n!]_{q}}{[n_{1}!]_{q}\cdots[n_{r}!]_{q}}
, and
[n!]_{q}
is the
q
-analogue of
n!
, defined in Section
1
.

Proof.

Since
G=P_{\lambda}K_{0}
, we have

P_{\lambda}\backslash G/K_{N}\cong P_{\lambda}({\mathscr{O}})\backslash K_{0}/K_{N}\cong P_{\lambda}({\mathscr{O}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{N})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathscr{O}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}),

the partial flag variety over
{\mathscr{O}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}
corresponding to the partition
\lambda
. Now, the subgroup

\begin{pmatrix}I_{n_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
{\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}&I_{n_{2}}&&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\ddots&0\\
{\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}&\cdots&{\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}&I_{n_{r}}\end{pmatrix}\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathscr{O}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{N})

acts simply on
P_{\lambda}({\mathscr{O}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{N})\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathscr{O}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{N})
from the right, with quotient
P_{\lambda}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(k)
, so

\displaystyle|P_{\lambda}\backslash G/K_{N}|

\displaystyle=|P_{\lambda}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(k)|\cdot(q^{N-1})^{(n^{2}-n_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{r}^{2})/2}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\end{bmatrix}_{q}(q^{N-1})^{(n^{2}-n_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{r}^{2})/2}.

Here, the last equality is because the quotient
P_{\lambda}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(k)
is the partial flag variety over
k
, and the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{n-1}
has size
[n]_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
.
∎

Remark
.

Here,
\dim(N_{\lambda})=\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-n_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{r}^{2})
, where
N_{\lambda}\subset P_{\lambda}
is the nilpotent radical.

The following Mackey-type formula is also useful, and will be used repeatedly throughout the paper:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a locally profinite group,
H\subset G
a closed subgroup, and
K\subset G
a compact open subgroup. Let
S\subset G
be a set of representatives for
H\backslash G/K
. Then, for a smooth representation
\lambda
of
H
,

\displaystyle(\cInd_{H}^{G}\lambda)^{K}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigoplus_{g\in S}\lambda^{H\cap gKg^{-1}}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto(f(g))_{g\in S}.

Proof.

We will first show well-definedness. Let
f\in(\cInd_{H}^{G}\lambda)^{K}
. For each
g\in S
and
h\in H\cap gKg^{-1}
, we have:

f(g)=f(h\cdot g\cdot g^{-1}h^{-1}g)=\lambda(h)f(g),

so
f(g)\in\lambda^{H\cap gKg^{-1}}
. Moreover, there is a subset
T\subset S
such that
\supp f=\sqcup_{g\in T}HgK
, where each
HgK
is open (it is a union of
K
-cosets). Thus,

H\backslash\supp f=\bigsqcup_{g\in T}H\backslash HgK,

so since
H\backslash\supp f
is compact,
T\subset S
must be finite. That is, the image lies in the direct sum.

We will construct an inverse. Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{g})_{g\in S}\in\bigoplus_{g\in S}\lambda^{H\cap gKg^{-1}}
be arbitrary. For any
u\in G=HSK
, there is a
h\in H
,
g\in S
, and
k\in K
such that
u=hgk
. Let
f_{\mathbf{a}}(u)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lambda(h)a_{g}
. We claim
f_{\mathbf{a}}\colon G\to\lambda
is well-defined. Indeed, if
u=hgk=h^{\prime}gk^{\prime}
, then

\lambda(h)a_{g}=\lambda(h^{\prime})\lambda((h^{\prime})^{-1}h)a_{g}=\lambda(h^{\prime})a_{g},

since
(h^{\prime})^{-1}h=gk^{\prime}k^{-1}g^{-1}\in H\cap gKg^{-1}
. Now,
f_{\mathbf{a}}\in(\Ind_{H}^{G}\lambda)^{K}
is clear. Finally,
f_{\mathbf{a}}
is compactly supported modulo
H
, since

H\backslash\supp f=\bigcup_{g\in T}H\backslash HgK

where
T=\{g\in S:a_{g}\neq 0\}
is finite, and each
H\backslash HgK
is the continuous image of the compact set
K
.
∎

Now, the growth polynomials (defined in Section
2
) of induced representations of the form
\pi_{1}\times\cdots\times\pi_{r}
are readily calculated:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}
be a partition and let
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r}
be admissible representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{1}},\dots,{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{r}}
, respectively. Then,

G_{\pi_{1}\times\cdots\times\pi_{r}}(X)=\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\end{bmatrix}_{q}X^{(n^{2}-n_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{r}^{2})/2}\prod_{i=1}^{r}G_{\pi_{i}}(X),

so in particular,

\dim_{GK}(\pi_{1}\times\cdots\times\pi_{r})=\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-n_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{r}^{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\dim_{GK}(\pi_{i}).

Proof.

Let
\pi=\pi_{1}\times\cdots\times\pi_{r}
. The normalization may be ignored since the character
\delta_{G}^{1/2}\delta_{P}^{-1/2}
is trivial on a sufficiently small neighborhood of
P
. By Lemma
3.2
we have

\rho^{K_{N}}=\bigoplus_{g\in P\backslash G/K_{N}}(\rho_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\rho_{r})^{P(F)\cap gK_{N}g^{-1}}.

Now, by Iwasawa decomposition
G=BK_{0}=PK_{0}
, so the representatives
g\in P\backslash G/K_{N}
can be chosen so that
g\in K_{0}
. Then,
gK_{N}g^{-1}=K_{N}
, so

\displaystyle\pi^{K_{N}}

\displaystyle=\big((\pi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\pi_{r})^{P(F)\cap K_{N}}\big)^{\oplus|P\backslash G/K_{N}|}

\displaystyle=\big((\pi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\pi_{r})^{K_{N}\times\cdots\times K_{N}}\big)^{\oplus|P\backslash G/K_{N}|}

\displaystyle=\big(\pi_{1}^{K_{N}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\pi_{r}^{K_{N}}\big)^{\oplus|P\backslash G/K_{N}|}.

Now the result follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Moreover, growth polynomials behave well with short exact sequences:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

0\to\pi\to\phi\to\sigma\to 0

be an exact sequence of admissible representations of
G
. Then,

G_{\phi}(X)=G_{\pi}(X)+G_{\sigma}(X),

so in particular,
\dim_{GK}(\phi)=\max\{\dim_{GK}(\pi),\dim_{GK}(\sigma)\}
.

Proof.

Follows immediately from
[
6
, Section 2.3]
Corollary 1, that
(-)^{K_{N}}
is an exact functor.
∎

Remark
.

A nice formalization of Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
, together with Corollary
5.7
is the following: the map from the Grothendieck ring of representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}\colon\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle\to{\mathbb{C}}[X,X^{-1}]

\displaystyle\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n})

\displaystyle\mapsto[n!]_{q}^{-1}X^{-\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}}G_{\pi}(X)

is a well-defined ring homomorphism. That is,
\mathscr{I}
normalizes
\dim(\pi^{K_{N}})
by the size of the full flag variety over
{\mathscr{O}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}
.

3.1.
The growth polynomial of
\langle\Delta\rangle

We calculate the growth polynomial for representations of the form
\langle\Delta\rangle
for some segment
\Delta
, which is of particular importance since they generate the Grothendieck ring
\mathcal{R}

[
21
, Cor 7.5]
. Thus, growth polynomial for arbitrary representations can be deduced from that of
\langle\Delta\rangle
, via Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
, as carried out in the next subsection.

We first review the theory of Bernstein-Zelevinsky derivatives, as defined in
[
3
, Section 3.5]
:

Definition
.

Let
M_{n}\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
be the
mirabolic subgroup
, the group of matrices whose last row is
(0,\cdots,0,1)
. For an admissible representation
(\tau,U)
of
M_{n}
and
0<i\leq n
, define the
i
-th derivative

\tau^{(i)}
of
\tau
, which is a representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-i}
, as

\tau^{(k)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}U/(\theta_{i}(h)v-\pi(h)v:v\in U,h\in N_{i}),

where
N_{i}
consists of elements of the form

h=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}\begin{matrix}1\\
&\ddots&\\
&&1\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}&\\
&\\
&\end{matrix}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}*&*&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{n-i}&*&*\\
1&a_{n-i+1}&*\\
&\ddots&a_{n-1}\\
&&1\end{matrix}\end{array}\right)

and
\theta_{i}(h)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\psi(a_{n-i+1}+\cdots+a_{n-1})
, where
\psi
is the fixed character of
F
as defined in Section
2
. Here, the top-left block has size
(n-i)\times(n-i)
, and the bottom-right block has size
i\times i
. Moreover, for representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
define the
i
-th derivative as the
i
-th derivative of the restriction to
M_{n}\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
.

Definition
.

Let
\pi
be a representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
. Then, let the
total derivative
be the formal sum in the Grothendieck ring

\mathscr{D}(\pi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\pi+\pi^{(1)}+\cdots+\pi^{(n)}\in\mathcal{R}.

Extending
\mathscr{D}
to
\mathcal{R}
by linearity defines a ring homomorphism
\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}
, by
[
3
, Lem 4.5]
.

Now, the following calculations of total derivatives are of particular interest to us:

Lemma 3.5

(Bernstein-Zelevinsky
[
3
, Cor 4.14 (a)]
)
.

Let
\pi
be a representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}
, and let
\varphi
be a representation of the parabolic subgroup
P_{(n-1)+1}\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
. Then, the derivatives of

\sigma\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\nInd_{P_{m+(n-1)+1}}^{P_{(m+n-1)+1}}(\pi\boxtimes\varphi)

are given by, for
i>0
,

\sigma^{(i)}=\pi\times\varphi^{(i)}.

Lemma 3.6

(Zelevinsky
[
21
, Thm 3.5]
)
.

Let
\Delta=[\rho,\nu^{r-1}\rho]
be a segment, where
\rho
is a supercuspidal representation. Then,

\mathscr{D}(\langle\Delta\rangle)=\langle\Delta\rangle+\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle,

where
\Delta^{-}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[\rho,\nu^{r-2}\rho]
.

Now, we calculate the growth polynomial for representations of the form
\langle\Delta\rangle
for some segment
\Delta
, heavily utilizing the fact that they remain irreducible when restricted to
M_{n}

[
21
, Rmk 3.6]
:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\rho
be a supercuspidal representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{1}}(F)
, and let
\Delta=[\rho,\nu^{r-1}\rho]
, so
\langle\Delta\rangle
is a representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
, with
n=n_{1}r
. Then,

\displaystyle G_{\langle\Delta\rangle}(X)

\displaystyle=[r!]_{q^{n_{1}}}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n_{1},\dots,n_{1}\end{bmatrix}_{q}X^{\frac{n_{1}(n_{1}-1)r(r-1)}{2}}G_{\rho}(X)^{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{[n!]_{q}}{[r!]_{q^{n_{1}}}}X^{\frac{n(n-r)}{2}}+(\text{lower order terms}),

so in particular,

\dim_{GK}\langle\Delta\rangle=\frac{n(n-r)}{2}.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we abbreviate the parabolic subgroup
P_{\lambda}
as the partition
\lambda
.

There is a
M_{n}
-homomorphism

\displaystyle i\colon\langle\Delta\rangle

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle\times\nu^{r-1}\rho=\nInd_{(n-n_{1})+n_{1}}^{n}(\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle\boxtimes\nu^{r-1}\rho)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{res}\sigma\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\nInd_{(n-n_{1})+(n_{1}-1)+1}^{(n-1)+1}(\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle\boxtimes\nu^{r-1}\rho|_{(n_{1}-1)+1}),

where the inclusion
\langle\Delta\rangle\hookrightarrow\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle\times\nu^{r-1}\rho
is the
G
-homomorphism defined by the characterization of
\langle\Delta\rangle
as the unique irreducible submodule of
\rho\times\cdots\times\nu^{r-1}\rho

[
21
, Section 3.1]
, and
res
is the surjection given by restricting functions
f\colon G\to\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle\boxtimes\nu^{r-1}\rho
to
P_{(n-1)+1}
. We calculate their derivatives using Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(\langle\Delta\rangle)

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle+\langle\Delta\rangle

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle\times\nu^{r-1}\rho)

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta^{--}\rangle+\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle+\langle\Delta^{--}\rangle\times\nu^{r-1}\rho+\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle\times\nu^{r-1}\rho

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle+\sigma.

Here
i
is nonzero, since both
\langle\Delta\rangle
and
\sigma
have
n_{1}
-th derivative
\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle
, but the
n_{1}
-th derivative
\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle+\langle\Delta^{--}\rangle\times\nu^{r-1}\rho
of
\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle\times\nu^{r-1}\rho
only contains the irreducible representation
\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle
with multiplicity one. Now, since
\langle\Delta\rangle
is an irreducible
M_{n}
-representation by
[
21
, Cor 7.9]
, the map
i
must be injective.

Moreover, if
\tau
is the cokernel of
i
, then its total derivative is

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{D}(\sigma)-\mathscr{D}(\langle\Delta\rangle)

\displaystyle=\sigma-\langle\Delta\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau.

That is,
\tau
has no derivatives of degree
>0
, so by
[
21
, Section 3.5]
we have
\tau=0
. Thus,
i
is an isomorphism of
M_{n}
-representations.

The overall strategy is as follows: Use the isomorphism
i
to calculate the dimension of
\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap M_{n}}\supseteq\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}}
. In fact, we can show

\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap M_{n}}=\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}}=\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap{{}^{t}}M_{n}},

so

\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}}=\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap M_{n}}\cap\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap{{}^{t}}M_{n}}=\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap M_{n}},

which allows us to calculate
\dim(\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}})
.

First, for
N_{1}\geq N
let

J_{N_{1}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}\\
K_{N}\cap{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}\\
{}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}{\mathfrak{p}}^{N_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
{\mathfrak{p}}^{N_{1}}\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr 0\quad\cdots\quad 0&1\end{array}\right)\subset M_{n}

be compact open subgroups which approximate the subgroup
K_{N}\cap{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}
of
M_{n}
, with
J_{N}=K_{N}\cap M_{n}
. Here,

(3.1)

\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}}=\bigcup_{N_{1}\geq N}\langle\Delta\rangle^{J_{N_{1}}},

where
\supseteq
is obvious and
\subseteq
is from smoothness of the representation
\langle\Delta\rangle|_{M_{n}}
. This allows us to use Lemma
3.2
on the groups

.

For each
N_{1}\geq N
we have

(3.2)

P_{(n-n_{1})+(n_{1}-1)+1}\backslash P_{(n-1)+1}/J_{N_{1}}\cong P_{(n-n_{1})+(n_{1}-1)}\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}/K_{N}\cap{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}

has size
\begin{bmatrix}n-1\\
n_{1}-1\end{bmatrix}_{q}q^{n(n_{1}-1)(N-1)}
by Lemma
3.1
, which notably does not depend on the choice of
N_{1}
. Since
i
is a
M_{n}
-isomorphism,

\displaystyle\dim(\langle\Delta\rangle^{J_{N_{1}}})

\displaystyle=\dim(\sigma^{J_{N_{1}}})

(3.3)

\displaystyle=|P_{(n-n_{1})+(n_{1}-1)+1}\backslash P_{(n-1)+1}/J_{N_{1}}|\dim\big(\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle^{K_{N}}\otimes(\nu^{r-1}\rho)^{K_{N}}\big)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}n-1\\
n_{1}-1\end{bmatrix}_{q}q^{n(n_{1}-1)(N-1)}\dim(\langle\Delta^{-}\rangle^{K_{N}})\dim(\rho^{K_{N}}),

where the second equality is by a similar argument as in Lemma
3.3
. By the independence on the choice of
N_{1}
, the chain of inclusions

\langle\Delta\rangle^{J_{N}}\subseteq\langle\Delta\rangle^{J_{N+1}}\subseteq\langle\Delta\rangle^{J_{N+2}}\subseteq\cdots

must all be equalities, so in particular, (
3.1
) tells us that
\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap M_{N}}=\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}}
.

On the other hand, by
[
2
, Thm 7.3]
we have
\langle\Delta\rangle\circ\iota\cong\langle\Delta\rangle^{\vee}\cong\langle[\nu^{1-r}\rho^{\vee},\rho^{\vee}]\rangle
, where

is the involution on

corresponding to the nontrivial automorphism of the Dynkin diagram

. Thus, for any subgroup

we have

Symmetrically, all the same arguments, with
\rho

, tells us that
\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap{{}^{t}}M_{N}}=\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap{\mathrm{GL}}_{n-1}}
.

Since the groups

and

generate

,

\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}}=\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap M_{n}}\cap\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap{{}^{t}}M_{n}}=\langle\Delta\rangle^{K_{N}\cap M_{n}}.

But this dimension was calculated in equation

By downward induction on

\begin{bmatrix}n-1\\

n_{1}\end{bmatrix}_{q}=[r]_{q^{n_{1}}}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{1}r\\

we obtain

n_{1},\dots,n_{1}\end{bmatrix}_{q}(q^{N-1})^{\frac{n_{1}(n_{1}-1)r(r-1)}{2}}\dim(\rho^{K_{N}})^{r}.

The final asymptotic is by Proposition

.
∎

3.2.
Growth polynomial of arbitrary admissible representations

Although Proposition
3.7
allows for the calculation of growth polynomials for arbitrary representations, explicitly expressing representations in terms of
\langle\Delta\rangle
is difficult in general. Nonetheless, Theorem
3.10
calculates the leading term in terms of the Bernstein-Zelevinsky classification.

The following lemma allows us to relate the leading term of the growth polynomial of the representation
\langle a\rangle
to that of the easier-understood representation
\pi(a)
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For any multiset
a
of segments,

\dim(\langle a\rangle^{K_{N}})=(1+o(1))\dim(\pi(a)^{K_{N}})

so in particular,
\dim_{GK}(\pi(a))=\dim_{GK}(\langle a\rangle)
.

Proof.

First of all, we claim that if
a<b
(i.e.,
a
can be obtained from
b
via a chain of elementary operations) then
\dim_{GK}\pi(a)<\dim_{GK}\pi(b)
. Indeed, by Lemma
3.3
it suffices to check that for linked segments
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
,

\dim(\langle\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}\rangle\times\langle\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime}\rangle)<\dim(\langle\Delta\rangle\times\langle\Delta^{\prime}\rangle).

Let
\Delta=[\rho,\nu^{r-1}\rho]
and
\Delta^{\prime}=[\nu^{s}\rho,\nu^{s+t-1}\rho]
for some
0<s\leq r<s+t
and
\rho
a supercuspidal representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{1}}(F)
. We hope to show

\dim_{GK}(\langle\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}\rangle\times\langle\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime}\rangle)=(t+s)(r-s)n_{1}^{2}+((t+s)^{2}+(r-s)^{2})\frac{n_{1}(n_{1}-1)}{2}.

is less than

\dim_{GK}(\langle\Delta\rangle\times\langle\Delta^{\prime}\rangle)=rtn_{1}^{2}+(r^{2}+t^{2})\frac{n_{1}(n_{1}-1)}{2}.

This reduces to the fact that
(t+s)^{2}+(t-s)^{2}>r^{2}+t^{2}
, i.e., the convexity of
x^{2}
.

Now by
[
21
, Thm 7.1]
, in the Grothendieck ring
\mathcal{R}
of representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}
we have

\pi(a)=\langle a\rangle+\sum_{b<a}m(b;a)\langle b\rangle

for some nonnegative integers
m(b;a)
. For
b<a
we have
\dim_{GK}\langle b\rangle\leq\dim_{GK}\pi(b)<\dim_{GK}\pi(a)
, so

\dim(\pi(a)^{K_{N}})=\dim(\langle a\rangle^{K_{N}})+\sum_{b<a}m(b;a)\dim(\langle b\rangle^{K_{N}})=(1+o(1))\dim(\langle a\rangle^{K_{N}}).\qed

We finally arrive at our main theorem:

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\pi
be an arbitrary admissible irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
. Then, there exists a multisegment
a=\{\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{m}\}
such that
\rho=\langle a\rangle
, where
\Delta_{i}=[\rho_{i},\nu^{r_{i}-1}\rho_{i}]
for supercuspidal representations
\rho_{i}
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{i}}(F)
. Then,

G_{\pi}(X)=\frac{[n!]_{q}}{[r_{1}!]_{q^{n_{1}}}\cdots[r_{m}!]_{q^{n_{m}}}}X^{\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-n_{1}r_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{m}r_{m}^{2})}+(\text{lower order terms}),

so in particular,
\dim_{GK}(\pi)=\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-n_{1}r_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{m}r_{m}^{2})
.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
3.3
, Lemma
3.9
, and Proposition
3.7
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\dim(\pi^{K_{N}})

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\dim((\langle\Delta_{1}\rangle\times\cdots\times\langle\Delta_{m}\rangle)^{K_{N}})

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n_{1}r_{1},\dots,n_{m}r_{m}\end{bmatrix}_{q}(q^{N-1})^{\frac{n^{2}-n_{1}^{2}r_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{m}^{2}r_{m}^{2}}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\dim(\langle\Delta_{i}\rangle^{K_{N}})

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\frac{[n!]_{q}}{[r_{1}!]_{q^{n_{1}}}\cdots[r_{m}!]_{q^{n_{m}}}}(q^{N-1})^{\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-n_{1}r_{1}^{2}-\cdots-n_{m}r_{m}^{2})}.\qed

5.
General theory of local character expansions

Section
3
reduces the calculation of growth polynomials of representations to that of supercuspidal representations. To calculate the growth polynomials of supercuspidal representations, we use the
local character expansion
, which tells us the germ of a character.

Throughout this section,
assume
F
has characteristic zero
. Let
G
be the group of
F
-rational points of a connected reductive algebraic
F
-group (e.g.,
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
or
{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(D)
, for a division algebra
D
over
F
.) Also let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be its Lie algebra, so there is an exponential map

\exp_{G}\colon{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\to G_{1},

for some neighborhood
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
of
0\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and neighborhood
G_{1}
of
1\in G
. In fact,
\exp_{G}
is bijective for sufficiently small neighborhoods
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\ni 0
and
G_{1}\ni 1
.

Example 5.1
.

For
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
(resp.,
{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(D)
), we have
{\mathfrak{g}}=M_{n}(F)
(resp.,
M_{m}(D)
), and for
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}M_{n}(p{\mathscr{O}}_{F})
(resp.,
M_{m}(p{\mathscr{O}}_{D})
) and
G_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}1+{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
, there is an isomorphism of groups

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}

\displaystyle\to G_{1}

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mapsto 1+X+\frac{X^{2}}{2}+\cdots.

Unlike in the archimedean case,
\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{X^{n}}{n!}
does not converge on the entire Lie algebra.

Let
Ch_{\pi}
denote the character associated to an admissible representation
(\pi,V)
of
G
. That is, for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)
a compactly supported function on
G
,

\mathrm{tr}\,\pi(f)=\int_{G}Ch_{\pi}(g)f(g)dg,

where
\pi(f)=\int_{G}f(g)\pi(g)dg\colon V\to V
has finite rank so the trace is well-defined (restrict to a finite-dimensional subspace
\pi(f)(V)\subset W
such that
\pi(f)(W)\subset W
). Note that
\pi(g)\colon V\to V
for
g\in G
need not have finite rank so its “literal” trace is ill-defined.

For a compactly supported function
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathfrak{g}})
, define the
Fourier transform
as

\widehat{f}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}}f(Y)\cdot\psi(B(X,Y))dY,

where
B(-,-)
is the Killing form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\psi
is the fixed character of
F
as defined in Section
2
.

Definition
.

Let
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}

6.1.
Jacquet-Langlands correspondence

Recall the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence
[
9
]
:

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
D
be a division algebra of dimension
d^{2}
over
F
, and let
E^{2}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F))
(resp.,
E^{2}({\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(D))
) be the set of irreducible square-integrable representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
(resp,
{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(D)
), where
n=md
. Then, there exists a bijection

\displaystyle E^{2}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F))

\displaystyle\to E^{2}({\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(D))

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\mapsto\pi_{D}

such that for
g^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(D)
a regular element, whose conjugacy class corresponds to a regular element
g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
, then

(-1)^{m}Ch_{\pi_{D}}(g^{\prime})=(-1)^{n}Ch_{\pi}(g).

Now, we have:

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\pi\in E^{2}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F))
be an irreducible square-integrable representation, corresponding to
\pi_{D}\in E^{2}({\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(D))
under the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence. Then

(-1)^{m}c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)=(-1)^{n}c_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(\pi_{D}),

where
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}
are nilpotent orbits of
G^{\prime}
and
G
, respectively (via identifying the Lie algebras of
G^{\prime}
and
G
, tensored with
\overline{F}
).

Proof.

Let
m=m_{1}+\dots+m_{r}
be an arbitrary partition of
m
, and consider a block diagonal matrix
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{r})\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
where
g_{i}\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{dm_{i}}(F)
are elliptic elements (i.e., has an irreducible characteristic polynomial) and the minimal polynomials of
g_{i}
are coprime. Then
g
is regular semisimple, corresponding to the diagonal matrix
g^{\prime}=(g_{1}^{\prime},\dots,g_{r}^{\prime})\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(D)
where each
g_{i}^{\prime}\in D_{dm_{i}}^{\times}\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{m_{i}}(D)
is similar to
g_{i}\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{dm_{i}}(F)
, where
D_{dm_{i}}\supset D
is a division algebra of dimension
d^{2}m_{i}^{2}
over
F
.

Then, by Proposition
5.5
and Lemma
5.6
, when
g
as above is sufficiently close to
1
,

Ch_{\pi}(g)=\sum_{{\mathcal{O}}}c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)Ch_{\Ind_{P}^{G}(1)}(g).

Here by
[
18
, Thm 3]
the character
Ch_{\Ind_{P}^{G}(1)}(g)
is locally supported on the union of the conjugates of
P
, and so
Ch_{\Ind_{P}^{G}(1)}(g)=0
for
P
not corresponding to a partition of the form
a_{1}d+\cdots+a_{i}d
.

Let
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}^{d}
be the set of such nilpotent orbits, i.e., those corresponding to partitions with all parts a multiple of
d
. There is a bijection
{\mathcal{N}}_{G^{\prime}}\simeq{\mathcal{N}}_{G}^{d}
, sending
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{G^{\prime}}
to the unique nilpotent orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
of
G
such that
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\otimes\overline{F}\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}\otimes\overline{F}
(on the level of partitions,
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}\mapsto a_{1}d+\cdots+a_{i}d
).

Now, Proposition
6.1
gives the equality

(6.1)

(-1)^{m}\sum_{{\mathcal{O}}\in\mathcal{N}_{G}^{d}}c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)Ch_{\Ind_{P}^{G}(1)}(g)=(-1)^{n}\sum_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{G^{\prime}}}c_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(\pi_{D})Ch_{\Ind_{P}^{G}(1)}(g),

since by Lemma
5.6
,

\widehat{\mu}_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})=Ch_{\Ind_{P^{\prime}}^{G^{\prime}}(1_{P^{\prime}})}(g^{\prime})=Ch_{\Ind_{P}^{G}(1_{P})}(g).

where
P^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(P\otimes\overline{F})\cap G^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
. Moreover, for parabolic subgroups
P_{1}\subsetneq P_{2}
of
G
we have
\supp(Ch_{\Ind_{P_{1}}^{G}(1)})\subsetneq\supp(Ch_{\Ind_{P_{2}}^{G}(1)})
in a neighborhood of

, so the characters
\{Ch_{\Ind_{P}^{G}(1)}:P\subset G\text{ parabolic}\}
are linearly independent. Thus, equation
6.1
gives

(-1)^{m}c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)=(-1)^{n}c_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(\pi_{D}).\qed

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\pi\in E^{2}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F))
be an irreducible square-integrable representation, and let
\pi_{D}\in E^{2}(D^{\times})
be the corresponding representation under the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence, where
D
is a
n^{2}
-dimensional division algebra. Then the growth polynomial
G_{\pi}(X)
has constant term
(-1)^{n-1}\dim(\pi_{D})
.

Proof.

Since
D^{\times}
is compact modulo center, the representation
\pi_{D}
is finite-dimensional. Thus,
c_{0}(\pi_{D})=\dim(\pi_{D})
, and so
c_{0}(\pi)=(-1)^{n-1}c_{0}(\pi_{D})=(-1)^{n-1}\dim(\pi_{D})
.
∎

The combination of Proposition

and Corollary

\displaystyle|E^{\times}U_{D}^{m}\backslash D^{\times}|

Proposition 6.6

\pi=\mathrm{AI}_{E/F}(\theta)

\displaystyle\mathrm{rec}_{E}(\Pi)

\displaystyle=(\Ind_{\mathcal{W}_{E}}^{\mathcal{W}_{F}}\mathrm{rec}_{E}\theta)|_{\mathcal{W}_{E}}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\mathcal{W}_{E}/\mathcal{W}_{F}}\mathrm{rec}_{E}\theta^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{rec}_{E}\theta\oplus\mathrm{rec}_{E}\theta^{\tau}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathrm{rec}_{E}\theta^{\tau^{d-1}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(I_{\tau}|_{\Pi^{K_{N}}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{N}(E))}\int_{K_{N}(E)^{\tau-reg}}Ch_{\Pi,\tau}(g)dg

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{N}(E))}\int_{K_{N}(E)^{\tau-reg}}Ch_{\pi}(\mathcal{N}g)dg

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{O}}c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{N}(E))}\int_{K_{N}(E)^{\tau-reg}}Ch_{\Ind_{P}^{G}(1)}(\mathcal{N}g)dg,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{N}(E))}\int_{K_{N}(E)^{\tau-reg}}Ch_{\Ind_{P}^{G}(1)}(\mathcal{N}g)dg

On the other hand,

(B.2)

S\subset K_{0}\{(\varpi^{a_{1}},\cdots,\varpi^{a_{n}})\in G:a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n},\text{ and for each }i,a_{i}-a_{i+1}\leq N\}K_{0}.

We will show the converse; let

g=u(\varpi^{a_{1}},\cdots,\varpi^{a_{r}},\varpi^{a_{r+1}},\cdots,\varpi^{a_{n}})v

where
a_{r}-a_{r+1}>N
and
u,v\in K_{0}
. We hope to show
g\notin S
. For any
M>0
, we claim

(K_{0}\cap gK_{N}g^{-1})\cdot K_{m}=(K_{0}\cap hK_{N}h^{-1})\cdot K_{m}

so in particular

\lambda^{K\cap gK_{N}g^{-1}}=\lambda^{K\cap hK_{N}h^{-1}}

if

h=u(\varpi^{a_{1}+M},\cdots,\varpi^{a_{r}+M},\varpi^{a_{r+1}},\cdots,\varpi^{a_{n}})v.

Indeed,

(K_{0}\cap hK_{N}h^{-1})\cdot K_{m}=(1_{n\times n}+({\mathfrak{p}}^{a_{ij}}))\cdot K_{m},

where

a_{ij}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}\max\{N+a_{i}-a_{j},0\}&\text{if }i,j\leq r\text{ or }i,j>r,\\
\max\{N+a_{i}-a_{j}+M,0\}&\text{if }i\leq r<j,\\
\max\{N+a_{i}-a_{j}-M,0\}&\text{if }i>r\geq j.\end{cases}

We see that if
i>r\geq j
then

N+a_{i}-a_{j}-M\leq N+a_{r}-a_{r+1}\leq 0

so
a_{ij}=0
, and otherwise
a_{ij}\geq N
, so

(K_{0}\cap hK_{N}h^{-1})K_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}1_{r\times r}&0_{r\times(n-r)}\\
*&1_{(n-r)\times(n-r)}\end{pmatrix}\cdot K_{m},

which is independent of the choice of
M
.

In particular,
g\in S
if and only if
h\in S
. Thus, if
g\in S
then

ZK_{0}\backslash S/K_{N}\supset ZK_{0}\backslash\bigcup_{M\geq 0}ZK_{0}(\varpi^{a_{1}+M},\cdots,\varpi^{a_{r}+M},\varpi^{a_{r+1}},\cdots,\varpi^{a_{n}})K_{0}/K_{N},

which is clearly infinite, contradicting
ZK_{0}\backslash S/K_{N}
being finite.

Finally, putting together (
B.1
), (
B.2
), and Lemma
B.2
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\dim(\rho^{K_{N}})

\displaystyle\sim|{\mathbf{K}}\backslash S/K_{N}|

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}\\
a_{i}-a_{i+1}<N\end{subarray}}q^{\sum_{i<j}\min\{a_{i}-a_{j},N\}}

\displaystyle\sim q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}N},

concluding the proof of Proposition
B.3
that for
\rho
a supercuspidal representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
,

\dim_{GK}(\rho)=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}.\qed

Remark
.

The technique in the proof of Proposition
B.3
is quite general, once one knows a representation is induced from a compact open modulo center subgroup. For instance,
[
7
, Thm 2.1]
shows any supercuspidal representation
\rho
of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
is induced from a compact open subgroup, so again,

\dim(\rho^{K_{N}\cap{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)})\sim q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}N}.

A more precise bound is given in Appendix
A
.

Appendix C
Level zero representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{3}

An irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
is
of level zero
if
\pi^{K_{1}}\neq 0
. Such representations which are cuspidal are classified by the following:

Proposition C.1

(
[
5
, Section 1.2]
)
.

Let
\rho
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of level zero. Then, there exists a representation
\Lambda
of
K_{0}Z
such that
\Lambda|_{K_{0}}
is inflated from a cuspidal representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(k)
, such that

\rho\cong\cInd_{K_{0}Z}^{G}\Lambda.

The growth polynomial of level zero representations may be calculated using the techniques in the proof of Proposition
B.3
. A general formula for
\dim(\rho^{K_{N}})
, for
\rho
supercuspidal of level zero is an immediate consequence of Proposition
5.9
:

Corollary C.2
.

If
\rho
is a supercuspidal level zero representation, then

G_{\rho}(X)=\sum_{{\mathcal{O}}}(-1)^{n+r}n(r-1)!w_{\mathcal{O}}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\end{bmatrix}_{q}X^{\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathcal{O}}},

where the nilpotent orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
correspond to the partition
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}
, and
w_{\mathcal{O}}
is the number of permutation of
r
letters which fix the
r
-tuple
(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
.

We offer an alternate,
more direct
calculation for level zero representations for small
n
.

Let
\rho=\cInd_{K_{0}Z}^{G}\Lambda
be an irreducible supercuspidal representation of level zero.
By Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\rho^{K_{N}}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{g\in K_{0}Z\backslash G/K_{N}}\Lambda^{K_{0}Z\cap gK_{N}g^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{g\in K_{0}Z\backslash G/K_{N}}\sigma^{(K_{0}\cap gK_{N}g^{-1})/K_{1}},

where
\Lambda|_{K}
is inflated from the representation
\sigma
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(k)
. Since
K_{0}Z
normalizes
K_{N}
, only the images of
g
in
K_{0}Z\backslash G/K_{0}
matter. That is, we may suppose
g
is of the form
(\varpi^{a_{1}},\dots,\varpi^{a_{n}})
for integers
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}=0
. Thus,

(C.1)

\dim(\rho^{K_{N}})=\sum_{a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}=0}|K_{0}\backslash K_{0}(\varpi^{a_{1}},\dots,\varpi^{a_{n}})K_{0}/K_{N}|\cdot\dim(\sigma^{(K_{0}\cap gK_{N}g^{-1})/K_{1}}).

This gives a polynomial expression for the dimension
\dim(\rho^{K_{N}})
. To illustrate the techniques, we will give an explicit formula when
n=2,3
(in the case
n=2
, this is a direct confirmation of Example
6.4
):

Proposition C.3
.

When
\rho
is a supercuspidal level zero representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
,

G_{\rho}(X)=[2!]_{q}X-2

and when
\rho
is a supercuspidal level zero representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{3}
,

G_{\rho}(X)=[3!]_{q}X^{3}-3[3]_{q}X^{2}+3,

where
X=q^{N-1}
, and
[n]_{q}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
,
[n!]_{q}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[1]_{q}\cdots[n]_{q}
.

Proof.

When
n=2
and
g=(\varpi^{a},1)\in K_{0}Z\backslash G/K_{0}
with
a\geq 0
, then

(K_{0}\cap gK_{N}g^{-1})/K_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\max\{0,N-a\}}&1\end{pmatrix}/K_{1}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }N>a\\
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
{*}&1\end{pmatrix}&\text{if }N\leq a,\end{cases}

so since
\sigma
is cuspidal,

\sigma^{(K_{0}\cap gK_{N}g^{-1})/K_{1}}=\begin{cases}\sigma&\text{if }N>a\\
0&\text{if }N\leq a.\end{cases}

Thus, since
\dim(\sigma)=q-1
(see
[
6
, Thm 6.4]
), by (
C.1
) and Lemma
B.2
we have:

\displaystyle\dim(\rho^{K_{N}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq a<N}(q-1)|K_{0}\backslash K_{0}(\varpi^{a},1)K_{0}/K_{N}|

\displaystyle=(q-1)+\sum_{0<a<N}(q-1)\cdot q^{a-1}(q+1)

\displaystyle=(q+1)q^{N-1}-2.

When
n=3
and
g=(\varpi^{a},\varpi^{b},1)\in K_{0}Z\backslash G/K_{0}
with
a\geq b\geq 0
then

(K_{0}\cap gK_{N}g^{-1})/K_{1}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }N>a\\
\begin{pmatrix}1\\
&1\\
{*}&&1\end{pmatrix}&\text{if }a\geq N,a-b,b<N\\
\text{Levi subgroup}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In the last case,
\sigma^{(K_{0}\cap gK_{N}g^{-1})/K_{1}}=0
since
\sigma
is cuspidal. In the first case,
\sigma^{(K_{0}\cap gK_{N}g^{-1})/K_{1}}=\sigma
. For the second case, decompose
\sigma
as follows:

\sigma\cong\bigoplus_{\chi:N\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}}\chi^{m_{\chi}},

where
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
&1\\
{*}&{*}&1\end{pmatrix}
is a nilpotent subgroup. Since
\sigma
is cuspidal,
m_{1}=0
, and the conjugation action by
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)\times k^{\times}
on the subgroup
N
permutes the other characters transitively so
m_{\chi}
is constant for nontrivial
\chi
. Thus, in fact
\sigma\cong\bigoplus_{\chi\neq 1}\chi^{\oplus d/(q^{2}-1)}
, where
d=(q-1)(q^{2}-1)
is the dimension of
\sigma
.

Thus,
\sigma^{\begin{pmatrix}1\\
&1\\
{*}&&1\end{pmatrix}}
is of dimension
d/(q+1)
. Combining,

\displaystyle\dim(\rho^{K_{N}})=d\sum_{N>a\geq b\geq 0}

\displaystyle|K_{0}\backslash K_{0}(\varpi^{a},\varpi^{b},1)K_{0}/K_{N}|

\displaystyle+\frac{d}{q+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\geq b\geq 0\\
a-b,b<N,a\geq N\end{subarray}}|K_{0}\backslash K_{0}(\varpi^{a},\varpi^{b},1)K_{0}/K_{N}|.

For each of these sums, we will run casework on what partition
\{a,b,0\}
corresponds to and apply Lemma
B.2
:

•

3=3
:

d\sum_{a=b=0}|K_{0}\backslash K_{0}(\varpi^{a},\varpi^{b},1)K_{0}/K_{N}|=d.

•

3=2+1
:

\displaystyle d\sum_{N>a=b>0}|K_{0}\backslash K_{0}(\varpi^{a},\varpi^{b},1)K_{0}/K_{N}|

\displaystyle=d\sum_{N>a>0}q^{-2+2a}[3]_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{d[3]_{q}}{q^{2}-1}(q^{2N-2}-1).

•

3=1+2
: the formula is the same as in
3=1+2
.

•

3=1+1+1
:

\displaystyle d\sum_{N>a>b>0}|K_{0}\backslash K_{0}(\varpi^{a},\varpi^{b},1)K_{0}/K_{N}|

\displaystyle=d\sum_{N>a>0}(a-1)q^{-3+2a}[3!]_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{d[3!]_{q}}{(q^{2}-1)^{2}}((N-2)q^{2N-1}-(N-1)q^{2N-3}+q).

For the second term, only the partition
3=1+1+1
appears, and evaluates as (this is the main term):

\displaystyle\frac{d}{q+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a>b>0\\
a-b,b<N,a\geq N\end{subarray}}q^{-3+a+N}[3!]_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{q+1}\sum_{a=N}^{2N-2}(2N-a-1)q^{-3+a+N}[3!]_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{d[3!]_{q}}{(q+1)(q-1)^{2}}(q^{3N-3}-Nq^{2N-2}+(N-1)q^{2N-3}).

Combining everything, and using
d=(q-1)(q^{2}-1)
, we obtain:

\dim(\rho^{K_{N}})=[3!]_{q}\cdot q^{3N-3}-3[3]_{q}q^{2N-2}+3.\qed

Remark
.

Combining the sums corresponding to the partition
3=1+1+1
from both terms gives a polynomial in
q^{N-1}
, suggesting a clever reorganization of sums may help simplify and generalize the calculation.

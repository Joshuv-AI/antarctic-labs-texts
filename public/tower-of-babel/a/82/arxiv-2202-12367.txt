Smooth Linearization of Nonautonomous Coupled Systems
Lucas Backes, Davor Dragičević

Abstract
. In a joint work with Palmer [ 3 ] we have formulated sufficient conditions under which there exist continuous and invertible transformations of the form H n ​ ( x , y ) H_{n}(x,y) taking solutions of a coupled system x n + 1 = A n ​ x n + f n ​ ( x n , y n ) , y n + 1 = g n ​ ( y n ) , x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n}+f_{n}(x_{n},y_{n}),\quad y_{n+1}=g_{n}(y_{n}), onto the solutions of the associated partially linearized uncoupled system x n + 1 = A n ​ x n , y n + 1 = g n ​ ( y n ) . x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n},\quad y_{n+1}=g_{n}(y_{n}). In the present work we go one step further and provide conditions under which H n H_{n} and H n − 1 H_{n}^{-1} are smooth in one of the variables x x and y y . We emphasise that our conditions are of a general form and do not involve any kind of dichotomy, nonresonance or spectral gap assumptions for the linear part which are present on most of the related works.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Autonomous linearization

The classical Grobman-Hartman theorem
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
is one of the most important results in the qualitative theory of dynamical systems and differential equations. It asserts that a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is topologically equivalent (or conjugated) to the associated linear part on a neighborhood of a hyperbolic fixed point. This result was later generalized to
Banach spaces independently by Palis
[
22
]
and Pugh
[
24
]
. It is well-known that in general, the conjugacy is only locally Hölder continuous
[
29
]
and that it can fail to exhibit higher regularity even for
C^{\infty}
dynamics. On the other hand, topological conjugacy is often not sufficient since for example it can fail to distinguish a node from a focus as pointed out by
van Strien
[
32
]
.

Therefore, it was important to study the problem of formulating sufficient conditions that would ensure that the conjugacy in the Grobman-Hartman theorem exhibits higher regularity properties. The first results in this direction are due to Sternberg
[
30
,
31
]
who
proved that
C^{k}
(
k\geq 1
) diffeomorphisms can be
C^{r}
linearized
near hyperbolic fixed points, where the integer
r>k
depends on
k
and so-called nonresonant conditions. Thus, in order to obtain
C^{r}
-linearization his results required that the dynamics exhibits higher regularity. Later Belitskii
[
4
,
5
]
gave conditions for
C^{k}
linearization of
C^{k,1}
(
k\geq 1
)
diffeomorphisms under appropriate nonresonant conditions. His results was partially generalized to infinite-dimensional setting in
[
14
,
27
,
36
]
. For other relevant results in this direction, we refer to
[
13
,
28
,
34
,
35
]
and references therein.

1.2.
Nonautonomous topological linearization

So far, we have only discussed the case of autonomous dynamics. The first version of the Grobman-Hartman theorem for nonautonomous dynamics was obtained by Palmer
[
23
]
. Let us formulate the version of his result in the case of discrete time which was obtained in
[
1
]
. Assume that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of bounded and invertible linear operators acting on a Banach space
X=(X,|\cdot|)
. Furthermore, suppose that
f_{n}\colon X\to X
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is a sequence of (nonlinear) maps. We can consider the associated nonlinear and nonautonomous difference equation

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n}+f_{n}(x_{n})\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

(1)

as well as its linear part given by

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n}\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2)

Assume that the following conditions hold:

•

the sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
admits an exponential dichotomy (see
[
9
]
);

•

there exist
M,c>0
such that
|f_{n}(x)|\leq M
and

|f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y)|\leq c|x-y|,

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x,y\in X
.

Then, provided that
c
is sufficiently small, equations (
1
) and (
2
) are topologically equivalent. Moreover, under additional mild conditions the conjugacies are locally Hölder continuous
[
29
]
.

More recently, several authors obtained extensions of Palmer’s theorem by weakening the hyperbolicity requirement for the linear part (
2
), which also required changing the assumptions dealing with nonlinear terms
f_{n}
. We refer to
[
2
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
25
,
26
]
and references therein.

1.3.
Nonautonomous linearization: higher regularity of conjugacies

Motivated by previously described results dealing with autonomous dynamics, in recent years several authors dealt with the problem of formulating sufficient conditions under which the conjugacies in the nonautonomous linearization exhibit higher regularity.

To the best of our knowledge, the first results in this direction are those in
[
6
]
(see also
[
7
]
) dealing with the case when the linear part admits an exponential contraction. In
[
10
]
, the authors have established the nonautonomous versions of Sternberg’s results by formulating sufficient conditions under which the conjugacies are of order
C^{r}
,
r\geq 1
. These conditions require that the linear part (
2
) admits an exponential dichotomy and that appopriate nonresonant conditions expressed in terms of the so-called Sacker-Sell spectrum hold.
Similar in nature (although different in techniques) are the result established in
[
11
,
12
]
which give conditons for
C^{1}
-linearization of nonautonomous systems. There, it is required that (
2
) admits a nonuniform exponential dichotomy and that appropriate spectral-gap conditions hold.

Finally, we mention the recent paper
[
8
]
in which the authors formulate sufficient conditions for
C^{1}
-linearization of nonautonomous discrete systems. The main novelties when compared with the previous works are the following:

•

(
2
) is assumed to admit a nonuniform dichotomy which is not necessarily exponential;

•

no nonresonance or spectral-gap conditions are required.

The main idea of
[
8
]
is that the lack of hyperbolicity and spectral data can be compensated by appropriate assumptions related to the size of nonlinear terms
f_{n}
in (
1
).
However, it should be emphasized that when restricted to the setting of (for example)
[
11
]
, the result in
[
8
]
can fail to be applicable or could yield weaker results from that in
[
11
]
. We refer to Remark
3.10
for a detailed discussion.

1.4.
Contributions of the present paper

In the present paper (following
[
3
,
29
,
33
]
), we study the coupled nonautonomous and nonlinear system

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n}+f_{n}(x_{n},y_{n}),\quad y_{n+1}=g_{n}(y_{n}),

(3)

as well as the associate uncoupled system

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n},\quad y_{n+1}=g_{n}(y_{n}).

(4)

Here,
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of bounded and invertible linear operators on a Banach space
X
,
g_{n}\colon Y\to Y
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is a sequence of homeomorphisms of a Banach space
Y
, and
f_{n}\colon X\times Y\to X
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is a sequence of nonlinear maps.
Observe that when
Y
is a trivial Banach space, (
3
) and (
4
) reduce to (
1
) and (
2
), respectively.

In
[
3
]
, the authors have formulated very general conditions under which (
3
) and (
4
) are topologically conjugated. The goal of the present paper is to formulate conditions under which these conjugacies are smooth. Our approach is inspired by that in the previously described paper by Castañeda and Jara
[
8
]
. We stress that our arguments require a nontrivial modification of those in
[
8
]
as those need to be combined with techniques developed in
[
3
]
. In addition, we do not impose any dichotomy conditions for the linear part in (
4
).

(5)

(6)

6

For

\mathcal{A}(m,n)=\begin{cases}A_{m-1}\cdots A_{n}&\text{for $m>n$;}\\

Let
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

do not need to be projections. Then we define

\mathcal{G}(m,n)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}&\text{for $m\geq n$;}\\
-\mathcal{A}(m,n)(\Id-P_{n})&\text{for $m<n$.}\end{cases}

(7)

2.1.
Hypothesis

In order to facilitate future reference, we gather in this subsection all the hypothesis we are going to use in our results. We group them into two categories: basic and advanced conditions.

2.1.1.
Basic conditions:

(BC1)

there exist sequences
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
[0,\infty)
such that

|f_{n}(x,y)|\leq\mu_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad|f_{n}(x_{1},y)-f_{n}(x_{2},y)|\leq\gamma_{n}|x_{1}-x_{2}|,

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x,x_{1},x_{2}\in X
and
y\in Y
;

(BC2)

N:=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=-\infty}|\mathcal{G}(m,n)|\mu_{n-1}<\infty;

(BC3)

q:=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=-\infty}|\mathcal{G}(m,n)|\gamma_{n-1}<1;

(BC4)

|A_{n}^{-1}|\gamma_{n}<1
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The basic conditions
(BC1)
,
(BC2)
,
(BC3)
and
(BC4)
ensure that the systems (

) and (

(Theorem 3.1 of

]
)

Suppose that

,

,
(BC3)
and

, where
\sup_{n,x,y}|h_{n}(x,y)|<\infty
, such that if

is a solution of (

),
then

3.
Main results

3.1.
Regularity of conjugacies with respect to the first variable

We are interested in formulating sufficient conditions under which conjugacies
H_{n}
and
\bar{H}_{n}
are smooth with respect to the first variable.

We start by studying the regularity properties of the map
\xi\to x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)
.
Consider

C_{k,n}=\begin{cases}\prod_{j=n}^{k-1}(|A_{j}|+\gamma_{j})&\text{for $k>n$;}\\
1&\text{for $k=n$;}\\
\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{|A_{j}^{-1}|}{1-\gamma_{j}|A_{j}^{-1}|}&\text{for $k<n$.}\end{cases}

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
(BC1)
and
(BC4)
are satisfied. Then, for any
\xi,\zeta\in X
,
\eta\in Y
and
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have that

|x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)-x_{2}(k,n,\zeta,\eta)|\leq C_{k,n}|\xi-\zeta|.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. By (
5
) we have that

\begin{split}x_{2}(n+1,n,\xi,\eta)&=A_{n}x_{2}(n,n,\xi,\eta)+f_{n}(x_{2}(n,n,\xi,\eta),y(n,n,\eta))\\
&=A_{n}\xi+f_{n}(\xi,\eta).\\
\end{split}

(8)

Consequently, using
(BC1)
,

\begin{split}|x_{2}(n+1,n,\xi,\eta)-x_{2}(n+1,n,\zeta,\eta)|&=|A_{n}\xi+f_{n}(\xi,\eta)-A_{n}\zeta-f_{n}(\zeta,\eta)|\\
&\leq\left(|A_{n}|+\gamma_{n}\right)|\xi-\zeta|.\end{split}

Thus, using that

x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)=x_{2}(k,m,x_{2}(m,n,\xi,\eta),y(m,n,\eta))\quad k\geq m\geq n,

and proceeding inductively we conclude that

\begin{split}|x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)-x_{2}(k,n,\zeta,\eta)|&\leq\prod_{j=n}^{k-1}(|A_{j}|+\gamma_{j})|\xi-\zeta|\\
&=C_{k,n}|\xi-\zeta|,\end{split}

for every
k>n
.

We now consider the case when
k<n
. Given
(\xi,\eta)\in X\times Y
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, let us consider

F_{j}(\xi,\eta)=A_{j}\xi+f_{j}(\xi,\eta)

and

T_{j}(\xi,\eta)=A_{j}^{-1}\xi-A_{j}^{-1}f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta).

(9)

It is easy to check that
F_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)=\xi
. In addition, we we have that

\begin{split}T_{j}(F_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)&=A_{j}^{-1}F_{j}(\xi,\eta)-A_{j}^{-1}f_{j}(T_{j}(F_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta))\\
&=A_{j}^{-1}(A_{j}\xi+f_{j}(\xi,\eta))-A_{j}^{-1}f_{j}(T_{j}(F_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta))\\
&=\xi+A_{j}^{-1}f_{j}(\xi,\eta)-A_{j}^{-1}f_{j}(T_{j}(F_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)),\end{split}

which implies that

|T_{j}(F_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)-\xi|\leq|A_{j}^{-1}|\gamma_{j}|T_{j}(F_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)-\xi|.

By
(BC4)
, we conclude that
T_{j}(F_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)=\xi
. Moreover, since

\begin{split}\xi&=A_{n-1}x_{2}(n-1,n,\xi,\eta)+f_{n-1}(x_{2}(n-1,n,\xi,\eta),y(n-1,n,\eta))\\
&=F_{n-1}(x_{2}(n-1,n,\xi,\eta),y(n-1,n,\eta)),\end{split}

it follows from the previous observations that

T_{n-1}(\xi,y(n-1,n,\eta))=x_{2}(n-1,n,\xi,\eta).

(10)

Consequently,

\begin{split}&|x_{2}(n-1,n,\xi,\eta)-x_{2}(n-1,n,\zeta,\eta)|\\
&=|T_{n-1}(\xi,y(n-1,n,\eta))-T_{n-1}(\zeta,y(n-1,n,\eta))|.\end{split}

Now, using
(BC1)
, for any
y\in Y
we have that

\begin{split}&|T_{n-1}(\xi,y)-T_{n-1}(\zeta,y)|\\
&=|A_{n-1}^{-1}(\xi-\zeta)+A_{n-1}^{-1}\left(f_{n-1}(T_{n-1}(\zeta,y),y)-f_{n-1}(T_{n-1}(\xi,y),y)\right)|\\
&\leq|A_{n-1}^{-1}||\xi-\zeta|+|A_{n-1}^{-1}|\gamma_{n-1}|T_{n-1}(\zeta,y)-T_{n-1}(\xi,y)|.\end{split}

Therefore, using
(BC4)
we conclude that

\begin{split}|T_{n-1}(\xi,y)-T_{n-1}(\zeta,y)|&\leq\frac{|A_{n-1}^{-1}|}{1-\gamma_{n-1}|A_{n-1}^{-1}|}|\xi-\zeta|,\end{split}

which combined with the previous observations implies that

\begin{split}|x_{2}(n-1,n,\xi,\eta)-x_{2}(n-1,n,\zeta,\eta)|&\leq\frac{|A_{n-1}^{-1}|}{1-\gamma_{n-1}|A_{n-1}^{-1}|}|\xi-\zeta|.\end{split}

Consequently, by proceeding inductively as in the case when
k>n
, we conclude that

\begin{split}|x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)-x_{2}(k,n,\zeta,\eta)|&\leq C_{k,n}|\xi-\zeta|\end{split}

for every
k<n
. The case when
k=n
is obvious and therefore the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that
(BC1)
,
(BC4)
and
(AC1)
hold. Then, the map
\xi\to x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)
is of class
C^{r}
,
r\geq 1
, for every
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\eta\in Y
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. By (
5
), we have that

\begin{split}x_{2}(n+1,n,\xi,\eta)&=A_{n}\xi+f_{n}(\xi,\eta).\\
\end{split}

Consequently, it follows from
(AC1)
that
\xi\to x_{2}(n+1,n,\xi,\eta)
is of class
C^{r}
. Thus, proceeding inductively, we conclude that
\xi\to x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)
is of class
C^{r}
for every
k>n
.

Next, we claim that the map
\xi\to T_{j}(\xi,\eta)
is of class
C^{r}
, where
T_{j}
is given by (
9
). Indeed, by
(BC1)
we have that

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial f_{j}(u,v)}{\partial u}\right|&=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{|f_{j}(u+\delta,v)-f_{j}(u,v)|}{|\delta|}\\
&\leq\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\gamma_{j}|\delta|}{|\delta|}\\
&=\gamma_{j},\end{split}

(11)

where
\frac{\partial f_{j}(\cdot,\cdot)}{\partial u}
denotes the derivative of
f_{j}
with respect to the first variable.
Thus, by
(BC4)
, it follows that the operator
\Id+A_{j}^{-1}\frac{\partial f_{j}(\xi,\eta)}{\partial\xi}
is invertible. In particular, we can consider

L:=\left(\Id+A_{j}^{-1}\frac{\partial f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}\right)^{-1}A_{j}^{-1}=\left(A_{j}+\frac{\partial f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}\right)^{-1}.

(12)

Now, observing that

\begin{split}&T_{j}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-T_{j}(\xi,\eta)-L\delta\\
&=A_{j}^{-1}\delta-A_{j}^{-1}\left(f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi+\delta,\eta),\eta)-f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)\right)-L\delta\\
&=A_{j}^{-1}\delta-A_{j}^{-1}\left(f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi+\delta,\eta),\eta)-f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta)+L\delta,\eta)\right)\\
&\phantom{=}-A_{j}^{-1}\left(f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta)+L\delta,\eta)-f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)\right)-L\delta,\\
\end{split}

it follows from
(BC1)
that

\begin{split}&\left|T_{j}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-T_{j}(\xi,\eta)-L\delta\right|\\
&\leq\left|A_{j}^{-1}\left(f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi+\delta,\eta),\eta)-f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta)+L\delta,\eta)\right)\right|\\
&\phantom{=}+\left|A_{j}^{-1}\delta-A_{j}^{-1}\left(f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta)+L\delta,\eta)-f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)\right)-L\delta\right|\\
&\leq\gamma_{j}|A_{j}^{-1}|\left|T_{j}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-T_{j}(\xi,\eta)-L\delta\right|\\
&\phantom{=}+\left|A_{j}^{-1}\delta-A_{j}^{-1}\left(f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta)+L\delta,\eta)-f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)\right)-L\delta\right|.\\
\end{split}

Using
(BC4)
, we obtain that

\begin{split}&(1-\gamma_{j}|A_{j}^{-1}|)\left|T_{j}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-T_{j}(\xi,\eta)-L\delta\right|\\
&\leq\left|A_{j}^{-1}\delta-A_{j}^{-1}\left(f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta)+L\delta,\eta)-f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)\right)-L\delta\right|.\\
\end{split}

Therefore, since by
(AC1)

u\to f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta)+Lu,\eta)
is differentiable,

\frac{\partial f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta)+Lu,\eta)}{\partial u}\bigg|_{u=0}=\frac{\partial f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}L

and since

\begin{split}&A_{j}^{-1}\frac{\partial f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}L+L\\
&=\left(\Id+A_{j}^{-1}\frac{\partial f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}\right)L\\
&=\left(\Id+A_{j}^{-1}\frac{\partial f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}\right)\left(\Id+A_{j}^{-1}\frac{\partial f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}\right)^{-1}A_{j}^{-1}\\
&=A_{j}^{-1},\end{split}

we get that

\begin{split}\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\left|A_{j}^{-1}\delta-A_{j}^{-1}\left(f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta)+L\delta,\eta)-f_{j}(T_{j}(\xi,\eta),\eta)\right)-L\delta\right|}{|\delta|}=0.\\
\end{split}

Thus, combining the previous observations we conclude that

\begin{split}\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\left|T_{j}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-T_{j}(\xi,\eta)-L\delta\right|}{|\delta|}=0.\\
\end{split}

In particular,
T_{j}(\xi,\eta)
is differentiable with respect to
\xi
and
\frac{\partial T_{j}(\xi,\eta)}{\partial\xi}=L
. Moreover, from (
12
) and
(AC1)
, we have that
\xi\to T_{j}(\xi,\eta)
is
C^{r}
.

Consequently, since by (
10
) we have that
T_{n-1}(\xi,y(n-1,n,\eta))=x_{2}(n-1,n,\xi,\eta)
, it follows that
\xi\to x_{2}(n-1,n,\xi,\eta)
is
C^{r}
. Finally, proceeding inductively we conclude that
\xi\to x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)
is
C^{r}
for every
k<n
. Therefore, since the desired conclusion clearly holds for
k=n
, the proof of the proposition is completed.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
(BC1)
,
(BC2)
,
(BC3)
,
(BC4)
and
(AC1)
hold. Moreover, given
n\in\mathbb{Z}
suppose that hypothesis
(AC2)
is satisfied for
n
. Then, the map
\xi\to\bar{H}_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is
C^{1}
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We start by recalling (see the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
) that

\bar{H}_{n}(\xi,\eta)=(\xi+\bar{h}_{n}(\xi,\eta),\eta),

where

\bar{h}_{n}(\xi,\eta)=-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)f_{k}(x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta),y(k,n,\eta)),

(13)

for
(\xi,\eta)\in X\times Y
. Then, it remains to prove that the map
\xi\to\bar{h}_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is
C^{1}
.

By hypothesis
(AC1)
and Proposition
3.2
, we have that each of the terms involved in the series (
13
) is differentiable with respect to
\xi
. Moreover, by (
11
) we have that

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial f_{k}(u,v)}{\partial u}\right|&\leq\gamma_{k}.\end{split}

Similarly, using Lemma
3.1
we conclude that

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)}{\partial\xi}\right|&=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{|x_{2}(k,n,\xi+\delta,\eta)-x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)|}{|\delta|}\\
&\leq\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{C_{k,n}|\delta|}{|\delta|}\\
&=C_{k,n}.\end{split}

Combining these observations with
(AC2)
, it follows that

\begin{split}&\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)f_{k}(x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta),y(k,n,\eta))\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)|\left|\frac{\partial f_{k}(x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta),y(k,n,\eta))}{\partial u}\right|\left|\frac{\partial x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta)}{\partial\xi}\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)|\gamma_{k}C_{k,n}<+\infty.\\
\end{split}

(14)

In particular, the series

-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)f_{k}(x_{2}(k,n,\xi,\eta),y(k,n,\eta))

converges uniformly. Thus, it coincides with
\frac{\partial\bar{h}_{n}(\xi,\eta)}{\partial\xi}
and
\xi\to\bar{h}_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is
C^{1}
. Consequently,
\xi\to\bar{H}_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is
C^{1}
as claimed.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose hypothesis
(BC1)
,
(BC2)
,
(BC3)
,
(BC4)
and
(AC1)
are satisfied. Moreover, given
n\in\mathbb{Z}
assume that the hypothesis
(AC3)
is satisfied for
n
. Then, the map
\xi\to H_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is
C^{1}
.

Proof.

Recall that by Theorem
2.1
,
H_{n}(\xi,\eta)=(\xi+h_{n}(\xi,\eta),\eta)
. Hence, it remains to prove that the map
\xi\to h_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is
C^{1}
. We start by observing that hypothesis
(AC3)
and the estimates from the proof of Theorem
3.3
(see (
14
)) imply that
\left|\frac{\partial\bar{h}_{n}(\xi,\eta)}{\partial\xi}\right|<1
, for every
(\xi,\eta)\in X\times Y
. In particular, the operator
\Id+\frac{\partial\bar{h}_{n}(\xi,\eta)}{\partial\xi}
is invertible and we may consider

R:=-\left(\Id+\frac{\partial\bar{h}_{n}(\xi+h_{n}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\bar{h}_{n}(\xi+h_{n}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u},

(15)

where
\frac{\partial\bar{h}_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)}{\partial u}
denotes the derivative of
\bar{h}_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)
with respect to the first variable.

We now claim that
\xi\to h_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is differentiable and that
\frac{\partial h_{n}(\xi,\eta)}{\partial\xi}=R
. We first note that Theorem
2.1
implies that
\bar{H}_{n}(H_{n}(\xi,\eta))=(\xi,\eta)
, and consequently

h_{n}(\xi,\eta)=-\bar{h}_{n}(\xi+h_{n}(\xi,\eta),\eta),\quad\text{for every $(\xi,\eta)\in X\times Y$.}

(16)

Moreover, since by Theorem
3.3

\bar{h}_{n}
is differentiable, we have that

\begin{split}&h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)\\
&=-\bar{h}_{n}(\xi+\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta),\eta)+\bar{h}_{n}(\xi+h_{n}(\xi,\eta),\eta)\\
&=-\frac{\partial\bar{h}_{n}(\xi+h_{n}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}\left(\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)\right)\\
&\phantom{=}+\mathbf{o}(\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)),\\
\end{split}

where
l(x)=\mathbf{o}(x)
means that
\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{|l(x)|}{|x|}=0
. Thus,

\begin{split}&\left(\Id+\frac{\partial\bar{h}_{n}(\xi+h_{n}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}\right)\left(h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)\right)\\
&=-\frac{\partial\bar{h}_{n}(\xi+h_{n}(\xi,\eta),\eta)}{\partial u}\delta\\
&\phantom{=}+\mathbf{o}(\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)),\\
\end{split}

which implies that

\begin{split}h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)=R\delta+\mathbf{o}(\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)).\\
\end{split}

(17)

On the other hand, since
\xi\to h_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is continuous,

\mathbf{o}(\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta))\leq\frac{1}{2}|\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)|,

whenever
|\delta|
is sufficiently small. Consequently, by taking the norm on both sides of (
17
) we get that

\begin{split}|h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)|&\leq|R||\delta|+\frac{1}{2}|\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)|\\
&\leq\left(|R|+\frac{1}{2}\right)|\delta|+\frac{1}{2}|h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)|,\\
\end{split}

whenever
|\delta|
is sufficiently small. In particular, there exist
C>0
and
\rho>0
such that

\frac{|h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)|}{|\delta|}\leq C,\quad\text{for $|\delta|<\rho$.}

Thus, whenever
|\delta|<\rho
, we have that

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathbf{o}(\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta))}{|\delta|}\\
&=\frac{\mathbf{o}(\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta))}{|\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)|}\cdot\frac{|\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)|}{|\delta|}\\
&\leq(C+1)\frac{\mathbf{o}(\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta))}{|\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)|}.\end{split}

(18)

Consequently, since
\xi\to h_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is continuous, we have that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}|\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)|=0,

and thus

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\mathbf{o}(\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta))}{|\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)|}=0.

This fact combined with (
18
) implies that

\mathbf{o}(\delta+h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta))=\mathbf{o}(\delta)

which together with (
17
) yields that

\begin{split}h_{n}(\xi+\delta,\eta)-h_{n}(\xi,\eta)=R\delta+\mathbf{o}(\delta).\\
\end{split}

Hence,
\xi\to h_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is differentiable and
\frac{\partial h_{n}(\xi,\eta)}{\partial\xi}=R
. Moreover, it follows from the expression of
R
given in (
15
) and the fact that
\xi\to\bar{h}_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is
C^{1}
that
\xi\to h_{n}(\xi,\eta)
is also
C^{1}
. The proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

Example 3.5
.

It is easy to construct examples where property
(AC2)
is satisfied for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
simultaneously. For instance, suppose that

M:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\max\{|A_{k}|,|A_{k}^{-1}|,|P_{k}|\}<+\infty.

Without any loss of generality, we may assume that
M\geq 1
.
For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, take
\gamma_{k}
such that
0\leq\gamma_{k}\leq\frac{1}{(2M)^{2|k|+1}}
.
Observe that
\frac{|A_{j}^{-1}|}{1-\gamma_{j}|A_{j}^{-1}|}\leq 2M
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Consequently,

\begin{split}K_{n}&=\sum_{k<n}|\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)|\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{|A_{j}^{-1}|}{1-\gamma_{j}|A_{j}^{-1}|}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{k<n}M^{n-k}\frac{1}{(2M)^{2|k|}}(2M)^{n-k}\\
&\leq(2M)^{2|n|}\sum_{k<n}\frac{1}{2^{|k|}}<+\infty,\end{split}

for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Similarly, since
|A_{k}|+\gamma_{k}\leq 2M
, it follows that

\begin{split}J_{n}&=\sum_{k>n}|\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)|\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=n}^{k-1}(|A_{j}|+\gamma_{j})\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{k>n}M^{k+2-n}\frac{1}{(2M)^{2|k|}}(2M)^{k-n}\\
&\leq M^{2}(2M)^{2|n|}\sum_{k>n}\frac{1}{2^{|k|}}<+\infty,\end{split}

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We conclude that Theorem
3.3
is applicable under mild assumptions on the linear part in (
6
), which in particular does not have to exhibit any asymptotic behaviour. For example, we can take
A_{k}=P_{k}=\Id
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

As for
(AC3)
, fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and choose
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M^{2}(2M)^{2|n|}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{|k|+n_{0}}}<1
. Then, if
\gamma_{k}<\frac{1}{(2M)^{2|k|+n_{0}}}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, our assumptions combined with the previous estimates implies that
(AC3)
is satisfied for
n
.

We now present examples under which
(AC3)
holds for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 3.6
.

We consider the case when
X
is a product of a Banach space
\mathcal{X}=(\mathcal{X},|\cdot|_{\mathcal{X}})
with itself, i.e.
X=\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}
. Then,
X
is a Banach space with respect to the norm
|(x_{1},x_{2})|:=\max\{|x_{1}|_{\mathcal{X}},|x_{2}|_{\mathcal{X}}\}
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
.

Take an arbitrary
\lambda>0
. Let us consider sequences of bounded linear operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
acting on
X
given by

A_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\lambda}\Id&0\\
0&e^{-\lambda}\Id\\
\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\Id\\
\end{pmatrix},

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\Id
denotes the identity operator on
\mathcal{X}
.

Then,

\mathcal{G}(m,n)=\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&e^{-\lambda(m-n)}\Id\end{pmatrix}&\text{for $m\geq n$;}\\
-\begin{pmatrix}e^{-\lambda(n-m)}\Id&0\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}&\text{for $m<n$.}\end{cases}

Consequently,

|\mathcal{G}(m,n)|=e^{-\lambda|m-n|}.

Set

M:=\prod_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(1+e^{-\lambda|j|}\right)<+\infty.

Now, let us consider a sequence
(\gamma_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of nonnegative numbers such that:

\gamma_{k}\leq\frac{1}{e^{\lambda(|k|+1)}+e^{\lambda}}\quad\text{for $k\in\mathbb{Z}$},

(19)

and

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma_{k}<\frac{1}{e^{\lambda}M}.

(20)

Observe that it follows from (
19
) that

\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{1-e^{\lambda}\gamma_{k}}\leq 1+e^{-\lambda|k|},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(21)

Then, using (
21
) we have that

\begin{split}K_{n}&=\sum_{k<n}|\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)|\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{|A_{j}^{-1}|}{1-\gamma_{j}|A_{j}^{-1}|}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{k<n}e^{-\lambda(n-k-1)}\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{e^{\lambda}}{1-e^{\lambda}\gamma_{j}}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{k<n}e^{-\lambda(n-k-1)}\gamma_{k}e^{\lambda(n-k)}\left(\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-e^{\lambda}\gamma_{j}}\right)\\
&\leq e^{\lambda}\sum_{k<n}\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-e^{\lambda}\gamma_{j}}\right)\\
&\leq e^{\lambda}\sum_{k<n}\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\left(1+e^{-\lambda|j|}\right)\right)\\
&\leq e^{\lambda}M\sum_{k<n}\gamma_{k}.\\
\end{split}

Similarly, since
1+e^{-\lambda}\gamma_{j}\leq 1+\gamma_{j}\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{j}}
, it follows from (
21
) that

\begin{split}J_{n}&=\sum_{k>n}|\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)|\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=n}^{k-1}(|A_{j}|+\gamma_{j})\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{k>n}e^{-\lambda(k+1-n)}\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=n}^{k-1}(e^{\lambda}+\gamma_{j})\right)\\
&=\sum_{k>n}e^{-\lambda(k+1-n)}\gamma_{k}e^{\lambda(k-n)}\left(\prod_{j=n}^{k-1}(1+e^{-\lambda}\gamma_{j})\right)\\
&\leq e^{-\lambda}M\sum_{k>n}\gamma_{k}\\
&\leq e^{\lambda}M\sum_{k>n}\gamma_{k}.\end{split}

Finally, since
M\geq 1
we have that
|\mathcal{G}(n,n+1)|\gamma_{n}=e^{-\lambda}\gamma_{n}\leq e^{\lambda}M\gamma_{n}
. Thus, from (
20
) we conclude that

K_{n}+J_{n}+|\mathcal{G}(n,n+1)|\gamma_{n}\leq e^{\lambda}M\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma_{n}<1,

for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 3.7
.

Let
X
be as in Example
3.6
. Furthermore, take a sequence of isometries

(B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
acting
\mathcal{X}
and let
(\theta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be any sequence of numbers satisfying
\theta_{n}\geq 1
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, suppose that
\theta_{n}\leq\theta_{n+1}
and that there exists a constant
T\geq 1
such that
\frac{\theta_{n+1}}{\theta_{n}}\leq T
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We now consider sequences of bounded linear operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
acting on
X
given by

A_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\theta_{n}}{\theta_{n+1}}\Id&0\\
0&B_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\Id&0\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix},

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then,

\mathcal{G}(m,n)=\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\theta_{n}}{\theta_{m}}\Id&0\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}&\text{for $m\geq n$;}\\
-\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\mathcal{B}(m,n)\\
\end{pmatrix}&\text{for $m<n$,}\end{cases}

where

\mathcal{B}(m,n)=\begin{cases}B_{m-1}\cdots B_{n}&\text{for $m>n$;}\\
\Id&\text{for $m=n$;}\\
B_{m}^{-1}\ldots B_{n-1}^{-1}&\text{for $m<n$.}\\
\end{cases}

Consequently,

|\mathcal{G}(m,n)|=\begin{cases}\frac{\theta_{n}}{\theta_{m}}&\text{for $m\geq n$;}\\
1&\text{for $m<n$.}\end{cases}

Set

M:=\prod_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(1+\frac{1}{2^{|j|}}\right)<+\infty.

Now, let us consider a sequence
(\gamma_{k})_{k}
of nonnegative numbers such that:

\gamma_{k}<\frac{1}{T(2^{|k|+1}+1)}\quad\text{for $k\in\mathbb{Z}$,}

(22)

and

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma_{k}<\frac{1}{TM}.

(23)

Observe that it follows from (
22
) that

\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{1-T\gamma_{k}}\leq 1+\frac{1}{2^{|k|}},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(24)

Then, observing that
|A^{-1}_{j}|=\frac{\theta_{j+1}}{\theta_{j}}
and using (
24
), we get that

\begin{split}K_{n}&=\sum_{k<n}|\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)|\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{|A_{j}^{-1}|}{1-\gamma_{j}|A_{j}^{-1}|}\right)\\
&=\sum_{k<n}\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{\theta_{n}}\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{\left(\frac{\theta_{j+1}}{\theta_{j}}\right)}{1-\left(\frac{\theta_{j+1}}{\theta_{j}}\right)\gamma_{j}}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{k<n}\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{\theta_{n}}\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{\theta_{j+1}}{\theta_{j}}\left(\frac{1}{1-T\gamma_{j}}\right)\right)\\
&=\sum_{k<n}\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{\theta_{n}}\gamma_{k}\frac{\theta_{n}}{\theta_{k}}\left(\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-T\gamma_{j}}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{k<n}TM\gamma_{k}.\\
\end{split}

Similarly, observing that
|A_{j}|=1
and
1+\gamma_{j}\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{1-T\gamma_{j}}
, we have that

\begin{split}J_{n}&=\sum_{k>n}|\mathcal{G}(n,k+1)|\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=n}^{k-1}(|A_{j}|+\gamma_{j})\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{k>n}\gamma_{k}\left(\prod_{j=n}^{k-1}(1+\gamma_{j})\right)\\
&\leq M\sum_{k>n}\gamma_{k}\\
&\leq TM\sum_{k>n}\gamma_{k}.\end{split}

Finally, since
M,T\geq 1
, it follows that
|\mathcal{G}(n,n+1)|\gamma_{n}=\gamma_{n}\leq TM\gamma_{n}
. Thus, from (
23
) we conclude that

K_{n}+J_{n}+|\mathcal{G}(n,n+1)|\gamma_{n}\leq TM\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma_{k}<1,

for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

.

Corollary 3.8

Groupoid semidirect product Fell bundles I — Actions by isomorphisms
Lucas Hall, S. Kaliszewski, John Quigg, Dana P. Williams

Abstract
. Given an action of a groupoid by isomorphisms on a Fell bundle (over another groupoid), we form a semidirect-product Fell bundle, and prove that its C ∗ C^{*} -algebra is isomorphic to a crossed product.



1.
Introduction

Groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras are a natural generalization of transformation
group
C^{*}
-algebras first studied by Effros and Hahn back in the 1960s

[
5
]
. Since then the construction of
C^{*}
-algebras
from groupoids has gone through myriad generalizations including
groupoid crossed products modeled on crossed-product
C^{*}
-algebras and
many others that are all subsumed by Fell-bundle
C^{*}
-algebras. In

[
10
,
11
]
, Muhly and the last three named authors consider
actions of a group
G
on a Fell bundle
p:\mathcal{A}\to H
by automorphisms
and analyze the consequences for the group semidirect-product

\mathcal{A}\rtimes G\to H\rtimes G
. When the group action is specialized,
the authors deduce an equivalence theorem
[
10
]
*Theorem 3.1
which unifies many imprimitivity theorems under the context of Fell
bundles and their semidirect products.

As suggested in
[
10
]
, we want to extend some of the results
there to actions by groupoids. In this note, we take up this task.
We let a groupoid
G
act on another groupoid
H
by groupoid
isomorphisms. Then we can build a semidirect-product groupoid

S(H,G)
. (We have purposely avoided the usual group notation
for semidirect products,

H\rtimes G
, as this coincides with the usual notation for a
transformation groupoid.) Given a Fell bundle
p:\mathcal{A}\to H
and an
action of
G
on
\mathcal{A}
by isomorphisms covering the given action on

H
, we can form a Fell bundle
q:S(\mathcal{A},G)\to S(H,G)
. Our main
result is that the corresponding Fell-bundle
C^{*}
-algebra

C^{*}(S(H,G),S(\mathcal{A},G))
is isomorphic to a groupoid crossed product

C^{*}(H,\mathcal{A})\rtimes_{\alpha}G
for an action
\alpha
. This leaves off
the task of extending the equivalence theorem of
[
10
]
from
group to groupoid actions, which we accomplish in the forthcoming
article
[
8
]
.

We begin in Section
2
by recording our conventions for
locally compact groupoids, groupoid actions on spaces, action
groupoids, upper-semicontinuous Banach bundles, Fell bundles

p:\mathcal{A}\to G
over a groupoid
G
, and pull-back Fell bundles.

In Section
3
we record our conventions for bundles

(T,p,B)
, morphisms of bundles, and pull-backs. In Section
4
we record our conventions for an action of a groupoid
G

on a bundle
p:T\to B
, and in Section
5
we apply this to
actions on Fell bundles over groupoids.

In Section
6
we develop a notion of a semidirect product

S(H,G)
of groupoids
H
and
G
when
G
acts on
H
by
isomorphisms. We then promote this to semidirect-product Fell bundles
where
G
acts on a Fell bundle
p:\mathcal{A}\to H
by isomorphisms.

Finally, in Section
7
we prove that if
G
acts on a Fell
bundle
p:\mathcal{A}\to H
by isomorphisms, then, under a mild assumption
involving Haar systems, the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(S(H,G),S(\mathcal{A},G))
of
the semidirect-product Fell bundle is isomorphic to the crossed
product by an appropriate action of
G
on the Fell-bundle algebra

C^{*}(H,\mathcal{A})
.

A alternate version of Theorem
7.3
could be
derived from the unpublished preprint
[
3
]
of Buss and
Meyer. Their “iterated-crossed-product decomposition” result
(Theorem 6.1 in their paper) would exhibit
C^{*}(S(H,G),S(\mathcal{A},G))

as the
C^{*}
-algebra of an abstract Fell bundle over
G
. Because of
the general nature of their result,
considerable work would be required to show that their Fell
bundle
C^{*}
-algebra is isomorphic to the groupoid crossed product

C^{*}(H,\mathcal{A})\rtimes_{\alpha}G
. In particular, an extra hypotheses on
the Haar systems is required to build the isomorphisms for the
action
\alpha
in the dynamical system. Obtaining the crossed
product decomposition in Theorem
7.3
gives the
advantage of more concrete realization of
C^{*}(S(H,G),S(\mathcal{A},G))
as
a crossed product rather than a more general Fell bundle

C^{*}
-algebra. For example, crossed products can
be studied using covariant representations as
outlined in
[
13
]
*Section 7. Furthermore, our
presentation is self-contained and does not require untangling, or
reference to, the
impressive machine from
[
3
]
.

We also mention the work of Duwenig and Li involving Zappa-Szép
products for Fell bundles over étale groupoids

[
4
]
. Following Brownlowe
et al
, they allow for two
groupoids to act on each other in a way that generalizes our semidirect
product. They then consider how the Zappa-Szép product of groupoids
lifts to an associated product for Fell bundles over an étale
groupoid. They thus recover and generalize our result, as long as one
considers the significantly restrictive case of étale groupoids. In
view of the present article, there is promise that the Duwenig-Li
construction generalizes to non-étale groupoids, but we do not
pursue this here.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout,
G
and
H
will be a second countable, locally compact Hausdorff
groupoids with Haar systems
\lambda_{G}=\{\,\lambda_{G}^{u}\,\}_{u\in G^{(0)}}

and
\lambda_{H}=\{\,\lambda_{H}^{v}\,\}_{v\in H^{(0)}}
, respectively.
In fact, when not obviously contradicted by context, the term
“groupoid” means a locally compact Hausdorff groupoid with open
range and source maps.

2.1.
Open Maps

Since open maps often play a key role in
the theory, it is useful to have a criterion for establishing that a
given map is open. The following is called “Fell’s
Criterion” in

[
17
]
*Proposition 1.1 and comes from

[
6
]
*Proposition II.13.2.

Lemma 2.1

(Fell’s Criterion)
.

Suppose that
f:X\to Y
is a surjection. Then
f
is open if and
only if given a net
\{\,y_{j}\,\}_{i\in I}
converging to
f(x)
in

Y
, then there is a subnet
\{\,y_{j}\,\}_{j\in J}
and a net

\{\,x_{j}\,\}_{j\in J}
such that
x_{j}\to x
and
f(x_{j})=y_{j}
.

We often use Fell’s Criterion to “lift” nets as follows. Suppose that
f:X\to Y
is an open surjection and
y_{i}\to f(x)
. Then we can pass to a
subnet
\{\,y_{i_{j}}\,\}
and find
x_{j}\to x
with

f(x_{j})=y_{i_{j}}
. Typically, we dispense with the distracting
subnet notation and replace the original net with the subnet. We
summarize this by saying that “we can replace
\{\,y_{i}\,\}
with a
subnet, relabel, and assume that are
x_{i}\to x
with

f(x_{i})=y_{i}
”.

Nets and subnets are discussed briefly at the end of

[
16
]
*§1.1.

2.2.
Groupoid Actions

Groupoid actions are treated in detail in
[
17
, §2.1]
.
In particular, in order for
G
to act on the left of a space
T
, we
need a continuous
moment map

\rho_{T}:T\to G^{(0)}
. Then we require a a continuous
map from
G*T=\{\,(x,t)\in G\times T:s_{G}(x)=\rho_{T}(t)\,\}
to
T
such that
the usual axioms hold—see
[
17
, Definition 2.1]
. This
means that
\rho_{T}(t)\cdot t=t
for all
t\in T
,

\rho_{T}(x\cdot t)=r_{G}(x)
, and
x\cdot(y\cdot t)=(xy)\cdot t

whenever
s_{G}(y)=\rho_{T}(t)
and
(x,y)\in G^{(2)}
. In the
literature, it is often assumed that the moment map,
\rho_{T}
, is
open as well as continuous. Here we will only make that assumption
when necessary. Except when it would be
confusing otherwise, it is common to delete the subscripts from
expressions such as
s_{G}(x)=\rho_{T}(t)
and simply write

s(x)=\rho(t)
assuming that the meaning is clear from context.
Right actions are defined similarly except that the moment map is
usually denoted by
\sigma_{T}
and then
x\cdot t
is defined when

r_{G}(x)=\sigma_{T}(t)
.

Notation
.

We
employ the following standard notation for a
G
-space
T
with moment
map
\rho_{T}:T\to G^{(0)}
,
N\subset G
, and
S\subset T
:

(1)

N*S=\{\,(x,t)\in N\times S:s(x)=\rho(t)\,\}
;

(2)

N\cdot S=\{\,x\cdot t:(x,t)\in N*S\,\}
;

(3)

S*T=\{\,(t,u)\in S\times T:u\in G\cdot t\,\}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let a locally compact groupoid
G
act on the left of a locally compact Hausdorff space
T
.
Then the
action groupoid
,
G\rtimes T
,
is the space
G*T=\{\,(x,t)\in G\times T:s(x)=\rho(t)\,\}

with operations given by

(x,t)(y,s)=(xy,s)\text{\kern 5.0ptif $t=y\cdot s$}\quad\text{and}\quad(x,t)^{-1}=(x^{-1},x\cdot t).

Notice that the range and source maps are given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle r(x,t)=(r(x),x\cdot t)\quad\text{and}\quad s(x,t)=(s(x),t).

Then the unit space is

(G\rtimes T)^{(0)}=\{\,(u,t)\in G^{(0)}\times T:u=\rho(t)\,\}.

Since the coordinate projection

\pi_{2}:(G\rtimes T)^{(0)}\to T

is a homeomorphism, it is common practice to identify
(G\rtimes T)^{(0)}

with
T
. The action groupoid
G\rtimes T
has open range and source maps
when
G
does.

Remark 2.3
.

[Action Groupoids in the Literature] In the literature, the
formulation of an action groupoids—also called transformation
groupoids—is not consistent. If
T
is a left
G
-space, then in

[
17
]
*Definition 2.5 the action groupoid

is
the set
\{\,(t,x,t^{\prime})\in T\times G\times T:t=x\cdot t^{\prime}\,\}
of triples
equipped with the relative product topology and with the natural
groupoid operations:
(t,x,t^{\prime})(t^{\prime},y,t^{\prime\prime})=(t,xy,t^{\prime\prime})
and

(t,x,t^{\prime})^{-1}=(t^{\prime},x^{-1},t)
. Using triples makes the definition
both natural and transparent. It is also redundant. As a result,
it is common practice to denote elements of

more compactly
as pairs in

. Our definition of
G\rtimes T
above is pulled
back from the homeomorphism
(x,t)\mapsto(x\cdot t,x,t)
of

onto

. However, one can also consider the set

\{\,(x,t):r(x)=\rho(t)\,\}
and pull-back a groupoid structure via
the map
(x,t)\mapsto(x,t,x^{-1}\cdot t)
. This groupoid structure
has the advantage that if
G
is a group, then we arrive at the
usual formulas for transformation group algebras. The convention
here has the advantage that it is the same as used in

[
11
]
*Appendix A.1. However, it differs, for example, from
that used in
[
17
]
or
[
1
]
. Of course, there are
similar considerations for right actions.

An (upper-semicontinuous)

6.
Semidirect products

When
G
and
H
are groups and
G
acts on
H
via automorphisms,
then we can construct their semidirect-product
S(H,G)

containing
H
as a normal subgroup. In this section we develop an analogue of this
for groupoids.

Definition 6.1
.

Suppose that
G
and
H
are locally compact groupoids such that
G

acts on
H
by isomorphisms. Then the
semidirect-product
groupoid
is the set

S(H,G)=\{\,(h,x)\in H\times G:\rho(h)=r(x)\,\}
, where we declare

(h,x)
and
(k,y)
to be composable if
s(h)=x\cdot r(k)
. Then we
define

(6.1)

\displaystyle(h,x)(k,y)=(h(x\cdot k),xy)\quad\text{and}\quad(h,x)^{-1}=(x^{-1}\cdot h^{-1},x^{-1}).

If
s(h)=x\cdot r(k)
, then
s(h)=r(x)
and

s(x)=\rho(r(k))=\rho(k)=r(y)
. Hence
x\cdot k
is defined,

(h,x\cdot k)\in H^{(2)}
and
(x,y)\in G^{(2)}
. So the product in
(
6.1
) makes sense. Then routine computations—say following

[
17
]
*Definition 1.2—show that
S(H,G)
is a groupoid with

(6.2)

\displaystyle s(h,x)=(x^{-1}\cdot s_{H}(h),s_{G}(x))\quad\text{and}\quad r(h,x)=(r_{H}(h),r_{G}(x)).

In particular,

(6.3)

S(H,G)^{(0)}=\{\,(v,u)\in H^{(0)}\times G^{(0)}:\rho_{H}(v)=u\,\},

and the coordinate projection
\pi_{1}:S(H,G)^{(0)}\to H^{(0)}
is a
homeomorphism.

Example 6.2
.

Suppose that
G
acts on the left of a
space

H
. Then

G
acts on
H
by isomorphisms when
H
is viewed as a
groupoid. Then the map
(h,x)\mapsto(x,x^{-1}h)
is a groupoid
isomorphism of the semidirect product
S(H,G)
onto the
action groupoid
G\rtimes H
.

Remark 6.3
.

Although their notation is slightly different, in
[
3
]

Buss and Meyer introduce semidirect products calling them
“transformation groupoids for classical actions of
G
on
H

If
G
and

have Haar systems and
G
acts on
H
by
isomorphisms, then Buss and Meyer show in

[
3
]
*Theorem 5.1 that

always has a Haar system.
Since we want the additional structure of a dynamical system, we
have to require that, in addition,

have an invariant Haar
system. Since this makes constructing a Haar system on the
semidirect product routine, we give the
elementary construction that is required for our
purposes.

For étale groupoids, Brownlowe
et al

[
2
]

introduced Zappa-Szép products of groupoids,
which generalize our semidirect products in that context.

Lemma 6.4
.

Suppose that
G
acts on
H
by
isomorphisms and that
H
has an invariant Haar system

\lambda_{H}=\{\,\lambda_{H}^{v}\,\}_{v\in H^{(0)}}
so that

\int_{H}f(x\cdot t)\,d\lambda_{H}^{v}(t)=\int_{H}f(t)\,d\lambda_{H}^{x\cdot v}(t).

Then we get a Haar system on the semidirect product
S(H,G)
by

d\lambda^{(v,w)}(t,x)=d\lambda_{H}^{v}(t)\,d\lambda_{G}^{w}(x).

Proof.

It suffices to integrate over
S(H,G)
functions of the form

f\otimes g
for
f\in C_{c}(H)
and
g\in C_{c}(G)
:

\displaystyle\int_{S(H,G)}(f\otimes g)\bigl((t,x)(u,y)\bigr)\,d\lambda^{s(t,x)}(u,y)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{G}\int_{H}(f\otimes g)(t(x\cdot u),xy)\,d\lambda^{(x^{-1}\cdot s(t),s(x))}(u,y)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{H}f(t(x\cdot u))\,d\lambda_{H}^{x^{-1}\cdot s(t)}(u)\int_{G}g(xy)\,d\lambda^{s(x)}(y)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{H}f(tu)\,d\lambda_{H}^{s(t)}(u)\int_{G}g(y)\,d\lambda^{r(x)}(y)\quad\text{(invariance) }

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{H}f(u)\,d\lambda_{H}^{r(t)}(u)\int_{G}g(y)\,d\lambda^{r(x)}(y)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{S(H,G)}(f\otimes g)(u,y)\,d\lambda^{r(t,x)}(u,y).\qed

Suppose that
p:\mathcal{A}\to H
is a Fell bundle and that
G
acts on
\mathcal{A}

by isomorphisms. We get a Banach bundle over

via the pull-back

\pi_{1}^{*}\mathcal{A}=\{\,(a,h,x)\in\mathcal{A}\times S(H,G):p(a)=h\,\}
. As
usual, we adopt a more compact notation and write elements of this
pull-back as pairs
(a,x)
in
\mathcal{A}\times G
such that

\rho(p(a))=r(x)
. Then we define the Fell-bundle operations by

(6.4)

\displaystyle(a,x)(b,y)=\bigl(a(x\cdot b),xy\bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad(a,x)^{*}=(x^{-1}\cdot a^{*},x^{-1}).

We denote the pull-back with these operations by

and write

for the projection

onto

.
Then we can generalize the constructions in
[

as

Lemma 6.5
.

With the operations defined above,

is a Fell bundle.

Proof.

We have the candidates for multiplication and involution defined
above, so let’s proceed with checking Definition

with

and is also equivariant for the

by isomorphisms.

For the multiplication condition, see that

\displaystyle p^{\prime}\bigl((a,x)(b,y)\bigr)

\displaystyle=p^{\prime}\bigl(a(x\cdot b),xy\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(p\bigl(a(x\cdot b)\bigr),xy\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl(p(a)p(x\cdot b),xy\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(p(a)\bigl(x\cdot p(b)\bigr),xy\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl(p(a),x\bigr)\bigl(p(b),y\bigr)

\displaystyle=p^{\prime}(a,x)p^{\prime}(b,y).

For the compatibility of the projection with involution,

\displaystyle=p^{\prime}(x^{-1}\cdot a^{*},x^{-1})

\displaystyle=\bigl(x^{-1}\cdot p(a)^{*},x^{-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl(p(a),x\bigr)^{*}

\displaystyle=p^{\prime}(a,x)^{*}.

Next, we check that the involution on

\displaystyle=\Bigl(y^{-1}x^{-1}\cdot\bigl((x\cdot b)^{*}a^{*}\bigr),y^{-1}x^{-1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\bigl(y^{-1}x^{-1}\cdot(x\cdot b^{*})\bigr)\bigl(y^{-1}x^{-1}\cdot a^{*}\bigr),y^{-1}x^{-1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl((y^{-1}\cdot b^{*})(y^{-1}x^{-1}\cdot a^{*}),y^{-1}x^{-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl((y^{-1}\cdot b^{*})\bigl(y^{-1}\cdot(x^{-1}\cdot a^{*})\bigr),y^{-1}x^{-1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(y^{-1}\cdot b^{*},y^{-1})(x^{-1}\cdot a^{*},x^{-1})

\displaystyle=(b,y)^{*}(a,x)^{*}.

Finite distortion curves: Continuity, Differentiability and Lusin’s (N) property
Lauri Hitruhin, Athanasios Tsantaris

Abstract
. We define finite distortion ω \omega -curves and we show that for some forms ω \omega and when the distortion function is sufficiently exponentially integrable the map is continuous, differentiable almost everywhere and has Lusin’s (N) property. This is achieved through some higher integrability results about finite distortion ω \omega -curves. It is also shown that this result is sharp both for continuity and for Lusin’s (N) property. We also show that if we assume weak monotonicity for the coordinates of a finite distortion ω \omega -curve we obtain continuity.

1.
Introduction

The study of mappings of
finite distortion
is a central theme in geometric function theory. Their importance stems in part from the various connections with other fields like holomorphic dynamics, PDEs and non-linear elasticity to name a few. A mapping
f\in W_{loc}^{1,1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is said to be of finite distortion if
J_{f}\in L^{1}_{loc}
and there exists a measurable function
K_{f}(x)\geq 1
which is finite almost everywhere such that the following inequality is satisfied

(1.1)

|Df(x)|^{n}\leq K_{f}(x)J_{f}(x)\ \text{a.e.},

where
|Df|
denotes the sup norm of the differential
Df
and
J_{f}
the Jacobian determinant. When
K_{f}(x)\leq K<\infty
almost everywhere, where
K
constant, such a map is called
quasiregular
(also known as maps of bounded distortion).

Quasiregular maps are the correct higher dimensional generalization of holomophic maps in the complex plane. Starting with Reshetnyak, they have been studied extensively since the sixties and by now their theory is well developed. We refer to the books
[
18
,
19
]
for more details on quasiregular maps. In many applications the distortion must be allowed to blow up in a controlled manner. This has led to systematic study of the more general class of mappings with finite distortion. By now there is a quite extensive literature for these maps and we refer to the books
[
7
,
8
]
for their general theory.

One of the basic facts about maps of finite distortion concerns their continuity. In
[
21
]
Vodopyanov and Goldshtein showed that a mapping of finite distortion
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
that belongs in the Sobolev space
W_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
has a continuous representative in the same Lebesgue class. Notice that a finite distortion mapping does not necessarily belong in
W_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. However, in
[
10
, Theorem 7.1]
it was shown that if we assume
e^{\lambda K_{f}(x)}\in L^{1}_{loc}
, for some large enough
\lambda>0
then we have that
f
is in
W_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and as a result it has a continuous representative. In fact, in
[
11
]
it was shown that
e^{\lambda K_{f}(x)}\in L^{1}_{loc}
, for any
\lambda>0
is enough in order to obtain continuity. Moreover, mappings of finite distortion with
e^{\lambda K_{f}(x)}\in L^{1}_{loc}
, for some
\lambda>0
, are almost everywhere differentiable and have Lusin’s (N) property, i.e. they map sets of zero (Lebesgue) measure to sets of zero measure, see
[
7
]
and also
[
13
,
14
]
.

Recently there has been a lot of interest in generalizing quasiregular mappings in the setting where the range and the domain of definition do not have the same dimension, see
[
17
,
16
,
6
,
5
]
. Pankka in
[
17
]
called such maps
quasiregular curves
in accordance with their holomorphic counterparts which are called holomorphic curves. In this paper we study under what conditions do
curves of finite distortion
, which are a generalization of quasiregular curves, have the desirable properties we mentioned above. By
\Omega^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
we denote the space of smooth differential
n
-forms in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
n\leq m
. In the following definition
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
is a smooth, non-vanishing and closed differential
n
-form. We call such forms
n
-
volume forms
.

Definition 1

(Finite distortion
\omega
-curve)
.

A mapping
f\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, where
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
n\leq m
is called a finite distortion
\omega
-curve if
\star f^{*}\omega\in L^{1}_{loc}
and there exists a measurable function
K_{f}(x)\geq 1
which is finite almost everywhere such that the following inequality is satisfied

(1.2)

(||\omega||\circ f)|Df(x)|^{n}\leq K_{f}(x)(\star f^{*}\omega),

almost everywhere on
\Omega
.

Here
\star f^{*}\omega
is the Hodge star of the
n
-volume form
f^{*}\omega
(the pullback of
\omega
), i.e. the function that satisfies
(\star f^{*}\omega)dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}=f^{*}\omega
. The function
||\omega||\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to[0,\infty)
is the pointwise comass norm of
\omega
defined as

(1.3)

||\omega||(p)=\sup\{|\omega_{p}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})|\colon\ v_{1},\dots,v_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\ |v_{i}|\leq 1\}

for each
p\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

When
K_{f}(x)\leq K<\infty
, where
K
is some constant, we get the class of quasiregular
\omega
-curves. We note here that in
[
17
]
quasiregular curves are assumed to be continuous by definition. However in
[
16
]
Pankka and Onninen showed that the continuity assumption can be dropped from the definition when the form
\omega
has constant coefficients.

Our first result shows that, unlike the case of mappings of finite distortion,
\exp(\lambda K_{f}(x))\in L^{1}_{loc}
, for any
\lambda>0
is not enough to guarantee the existence of a continuous representative even for the most simple forms. In fact it shows that even
f\in W^{1,n}_{loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is not enough (compare this with
[
11
, Theorem 1.4]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
\lambda<2
there exists a finite distortion
\omega
-curve,
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
for
\omega=dx\wedge dy
such that
f\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\exp(\lambda K_{f}(x))\in L^{1}_{loc}
, but
f
does not have a continuous representative.

The situation is similar for Lusin’s (N) condition as well. The condition
\exp(\lambda K_{f}(x))\in L^{1}_{loc}
, for any
\lambda>0
is not enough.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\lambda<2
there exists a finite distortion
\omega
-curve,
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
for
\omega=dx\wedge dy
such that
f\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{4})
and
\exp(\lambda K_{f}(x))\in L^{1}_{loc}
, but
f
does not have Lusin’s (N) property.

To explain why finite distortion curves differ from finite distortion maps in terms of their continuity properties we need to introduce the important concept of weak monotonicity. A real valued function
u\in W_{loc}^{1,1}(\Omega)
is called weakly monotone if the following holds: For all balls
B
compactly contained in
\Omega
and all constants
m,M\in\mathbb{R}
,
m\leq M
such that

(1.4)

|M-u|-|u-m|+2u-m-M\in W^{1,1}_{0}(B)

we have that

m\leq u(x)\leq M,

for almost every
x\in B
. Here
W^{1,1}_{0}(B)
stands for the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(B)
, the compactly supported smooth functions, with respect to the Sobolev space norm. For continuous functions this is equivalent to the more well known notion of monotonicity, namely

(1.5)

\osc_{B}u\leq\osc_{\partial B}u,

where
\osc_{B}u=\sup_{x,y\in B}|u(x)-u(y)|
.

The reason that weak monotonicity is important when discussing continuity is that the coordinate functions of a finite distortion map in a suitable Orlicz-Sobolev space, are weakly monotone, see for example
[
8
, Theorem 7.3.1]
. It is also well known that weakly monotone functions in that same Orlicz-Sobolev space have continuous representatives (for more precise formulations see Lemma
2.1
in section
2
). Thus, roughly speaking, to show the existence of a continuous representative for a mapping of finite distortion it is enough to show that the map has sufficient regularity and its coordinates are weakly monotone.

However, the situation is different for curves of finite distortion. Their coordinate functions are not necessarily weakly monotone as our example in Theorem
1.1
shows. On the other hand, if we assume the weak monotonicity of the coordinate maps and that
e^{\lambda K_{f}(x)}\in L^{1}_{loc}
, for some
\lambda>0
, then by adapting the arguments in
[
7
, Theorem 2.4]
and
[
8
, Theorem 7.5.2]
we obtain the existence of a continuous representative.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a finite distortion
\omega
-curve, for a bounded
n
-volume form
\omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, such that the coordinate functions
f_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
are weakly monotone. Suppose that there exists a
\lambda>0
such that
e^{\lambda K_{f}(x)}\in L^{1}_{loc}
. Then
f
has a continuous representative in
\Omega
.

Our next result gives some sufficient conditions in order for a finite distortion
\omega
-curve to have a continuous representative which is also almost everywhere differentiable and has Lusin’s (N) property in the simple case of constant coefficient forms. We will call an
n
-volume form in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\omega=\sum_{I}\phi_{I}(x)dx_{I}
, where the sum is over all multi-indices
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
,
1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{n}\leq m
, a
constant coefficient form
when the functions
\phi_{I}
are constant. Here
dx_{I}
denotes the
n
-covector
dx_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{i_{n}}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a finite distortion
\omega
-curve, for a constant coefficient
n
-volume form
\omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. There exists a
c_{2}=c_{2}(n)>0
such that if
\exp(\lambda K_{f}(x))\in L^{1}_{loc}
, for some
\lambda>c_{2}
, then
f
has a continuous and almost everywhere differentiable representative. Moreover, this representative has Lusin’s (N) property, that is, it maps sets of zero
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure to sets of zero
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

Our results in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
essentially show that the integrability condition on the distortion cannot be relaxed.

Next we consider more general classes of forms. We will call an
n
-volume form in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\omega=\sum_{I}\phi_{I}(x)dx_{I}

bounded
if all the non-zero, real valued functions
\phi_{I}(x)
are bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and there is a constant
c>0
such that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, there is a multi index
I_{x}
such that
|\phi_{I_{x}}(x)|>c
. Notice that for a bounded form
\omega
, there is a constant
c>0
so that
||\omega||(x)>c
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We will use the symbols
|\omega|_{\ell_{1}}
,
|\omega|_{inf}
to denote

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{I}|\phi_{I}(x)|\ \text{and}\ \inf\{||\omega||(x)\colon x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}

respectively. When the functions
\phi_{I}
are constant we say that
\omega
is a constant coefficient form. Notice that for bounded forms we have that
|\omega|_{\ell_{1}}<\infty
and
|\omega|_{inf}>0
. For an
n
-volume form
\omega=\sum_{I}\phi_{I}(x)dx_{I}
we will also denote by
H_{\omega}
the set of multi-indices
I
such that
\phi_{I}(x)
is not identically zero.

We define now
E_{f}
to be the following set

(1.6)

\displaystyle E_{f}=\{x\in\Omega\colon

\displaystyle\text{there exists a sequence}\ x_{n}\to x\ \text{such that}\

\displaystyle\star f^{*}dx_{I}(x_{n})\to\infty,\ \text{for some multi-index}\ I\in H_{\omega}\}.

Notice that
\star f^{*}dx_{I}
is a real valued function for all
I
.
For a bounded
n
-form
\omega
we say that the pair
(f,\omega)
satisfies
condition (D)
if the set
E_{f}
can be covered by a countable number of open balls
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and on each ball
B_{i}
there exists a multi index
I_{i}
such that
\star f^{*}dx_{I_{i}}\geq\max_{I}\{\star f^{*}dx_{I}\}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a finite distortion
\omega
-curve, for a bounded
n
-volume form
\omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, such that the pair

satisfies condition (D). Suppose that

, where

constant. If

. Moreover, this representative has Lusin’s (N) property, that is, it maps sets of zero

n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

2.
Preliminaries on Orlicz-Sobolev spaces and finite distortion maps

Here we collect results and terminology from Orlicz-Sobolev spaces and finite distortion maps that we are going to need for the proofs of our results, we refer to
[
8
,
20
]
for more details.

First we need to introduce the
Orlicz spaces
and the
Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
. An Orlicz function is a continuous and increasing function
P:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
P(0)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}P(t)=\infty
. We will also assume that the function
P
is convex. The Orlicz space
L^{P}(\Omega)
consists of all measurable functions
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.1)

||u||_{P}:=\int_{\Omega}P(\lambda|u|)<\infty,\ \text{for some}\ \lambda=\lambda(u)>0.

The functional
||\cdot||_{P}
, which is usually called the Luxemburg functional, is a norm in
L^{P}
and in fact
L^{P}
is a Banach space with this norm.

The Orlicz space
L^{P}
for
P(t)=t^{p}\log^{a}(e+t)
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
a>1-n
will be denoted as
L^{p}\log^{a}L(\Omega)
.

For a an Orlicz function
P
now, the Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,P}(\Omega)
is simply the set of functions that belong in
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
and their weak partial derivatives are also in
L^{P}(\Omega)
. It is easy to see that the vector valued versions of the above spaces are simply the maps whose coordinate functions are in the corresponding space of real valued functions.

For the proof of Theorem
1.3
we require the following result, see
[
8
, Theorem 7.5.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u\in W^{1,P}(\Omega)
, where
P(t)=t^{n}/\log(e+t)
. If
u
is a weakly monotone function, then
u
has a continuous representative.

For the proof of Theorem
1.5
we are going to need the following lemma which is an amalgamation of Theorem B and C in
[
12
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a function in the Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,P}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, where
P(t)=t^{n}\log^{a}(e+t)
with
a>n-1
. Then
g
has a continuous and almost everywhere differentiable representative. Moreover, this representative has Lusin’s (N) property.

We note that the aforementioned theorems in
[
12
]
are stated about functions in the Lorentz space. However, there is an equivalent way of defining Lorentz spaces using Orlicz spaces which we have used in the above lemma. We refer to the discussion preceding Theorems B and C in
[
12
]
for more details.

Due to the above Lemma, to prove Theorem
1.5
it is enough to show that the coordinate functions
f_{i}
of our finite distortion
\omega
-curve
f
are in the right Orlicz-Sobolev space. To prove that, we require another important result about the higher integrability of finite distortion maps proven in
[
4
]
, compare this with Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
g\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
be a mapping of finite distortion. Assume that the distortion function
K_{g}(x)
satisfies
\exp(\beta K_{g})\in L^{1}_{loc}
, for some
\beta>0
. Then there is a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(n)
,
0<c_{1}<1
such that

(2.2)

J_{g}(x)\log^{a}(e+J_{g}(x))\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)\ \text{and}\ |Dg(x)|^{n}\log^{a-1}(e+|Dg(x)|)\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)

for all
a<c_{1}\beta
.

We are going to need the following important inequality. Its proof can be found in
[
4
, Appendix]
or
[
7
, Lemma 6.2]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
x,\ y\geq 1
then for any
a>-1
,
b>0
we have

(2.3)

xy\log^{a}(C(n)(xy)^{1/n})\leq\frac{C(n)}{b}x\log^{a+1}(x^{1/n})+C(a,b,n)\exp(by),

We also require a slight variant of the above inequality. We include a proof here for completeness.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
x,\ y\geq 1
then for any
a>-1
,
b>0
we have

(2.4)

xy\log^{a}(e+(xy)^{1/n})\leq\frac{C_{1}(n)}{b}x\log^{a+1}(e+x^{1/n})+C_{2}(a,b,n)\exp(by),

where
C_{1}(n)
,
C_{2}(a,b,n)
constants.

Proof.

If
x<e^{by/2}
then since there is a
C_{2}=C_{2}(a,b,n)
such that

y\log^{a}(e+(xy)^{1/n})\leq C_{2}e^{by/2}

we obtain that

(2.5)

xy\log^{a}(e+(xy)^{1/n})\leq C_{2}e^{by}

If on the other hand,
x\geq e^{by/2}
then
y\leq\frac{2}{b}\log x
. Hence we have that

(2.6)

\displaystyle y\log^{a}(e+(xy)^{1/n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{b}\log x\log^{a}\left(e+\left(x\frac{2}{b}\log x\right)^{1/n}\right)

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{b}\log(e+x)\log^{a}\left(e+\left(x^{2/b+1}\right)^{1/n}\right)

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{b+2}{b}\right)^{a}\frac{2n}{b}\log(e+x)^{1/n}\log^{a}\left(e+x^{1/n}\right)

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(a,b,n)\log^{a+1}(e+x^{1/n}),

where
C_{1}(a,b,n)=\left(\frac{b+2}{b}\right)^{a}\frac{2n}{b}
. Thus in that case we have

(2.10)

xy\log^{a}(e+(xy)^{1/n})\leq C_{1}(a,b,n)x\log^{a+1}(e+x^{1/n})

and we are done.

3.
Higher integrability of finite distortion curves

In this section we give the proofs of Theorems

and

which combined with Lemma

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\Omega
and
P(t)=t^{n}\log^{a}(e+t)
. We want to find the values of
a
for which
\int_{K}P(\star f^{*}\omega)dx<\infty
. To that end, write

(3.1)

\int_{K}P(\star f^{*}\omega)dx=I_{1}+I_{2},

where

Here the sets

from condition (D) such that

where

We now want to estimate the integrals

and

. We start with

,

. Moreover, since the

. By condition (D) now we know that on the set

,

any multi-index, are uniformly bounded by a constant

now notice first that

On each of the sets

V_{i}
where

. We can now rewrite (

) as

\left(||\omega||\circ f\right)(x)|Df(x)|\leq K_{f}(x)\star f^{*}dx_{I_{j}}\sum_{I}\phi_{I}(f(x))

, where

, then by (

Notice that

. Hence that above equation implies that

is a mapping of finite distortion on

with distortion function

. By assumption now we know

\exp(\lambda\frac{|\omega|_{inf}}{|\omega|_{\ell_{1}}}K_{f_{I_{j}}})\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega),\ \text{for some}\ \lambda>0.

. Hence

Next we want to show that

, for all

. By the distortion inequality we obtain that

By what we have proven so far, we have that the first term of the right hand side is integrable on

. Moreover, for suitable

Lemma 3.1

(Generalized Gehring’s lemma)

0

B\subset\subset\Omega

\fint_{\frac{1}{2}B}\star f^{*}\omega dx\leq C(n,m)\left(\fint_{B}(K_{f}(x)\star f^{*}\omega)^{n/(n+1)}dx\right)^{(n+1)/n}.

Proof.

By following the argument in the proof of
[

, where the authors prove a Cacciopoli type inequality for quasiregular
\omega
-curves, we can show that for all balls

we have

\int_{B}\phi(x)(\star f^{*}\omega)(x)dx\leq||\omega||\int_{B}|\nabla\phi(x)||f(x)-f_{B}||Df(x)|^{n-1}dx,

where
f_{B}=\fint_{B}f(x)dx
(integration is meant coordinate-wise). It is important to note that this argument uses the fact that
f\in W^{1,n}_{loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. We choose now

so that
\phi(x)=1
when
x\in\frac{1}{2}B
,
0\leq\phi\leq 1
and
|\nabla\phi|\leq 2/r
. With this choice for
\phi
and by applying Hölder’s inequality we obtain

(3.46)

\int_{\frac{1}{2}B}(\star f^{*}\omega)(x)dx\leq\frac{2||\omega||}{r}\left(\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|^{n^{2}}dx\right)^{1/n^{2}}\left(\int_{B}|Df(x)|^{n^{2}/(n+1)}\right)^{n^{2}-1/n^{2}}.

By applying now the Poincare-Sobolev inequality, see for example
[
7
, Theorem A.18]
, coordinate-wise it is easy to see that

(3.47)

\left(\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|^{n^{2}}dx\right)^{1/n^{2}}\leq C_{0}(n,m)\left(\int_{B}|Df(x)|^{n^{2}/(n+1)}\right)^{(n+1)/n^{2}}.

Hence, (
3.46
) becomes

(3.48)

\fint_{\frac{1}{2}B}(\star f^{*}\omega)(x)dx\leq||\omega||C(n,m)\left(\fint_{B}|Df(x)|^{n^{2}/(n+1)}\right)^{(n+1)/n}.

Applying the distortion inequality (

(3.49)

\fint_{\frac{1}{2}B}(\star f^{*}\omega)(x)dx\leq C(n,m)\left(\fint_{B}(K_{f}(x)\star f^{*}\omega)^{n/(n+1)}\right)^{(n+1)/n},

which is what we wanted.
∎

The proof of Theorem

now readily follows.

From Lemma

First notice that because the function

is increasing and because

is a bounded form we have that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\frac{|Df|^{n}}{\log(e+|Df|)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{0}K_{f}(x)\star f^{*}\omega}{\log(e+(c_{0}K_{f}(x)\star f^{*}\omega)^{1/n})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{0}K_{f}(x)\star f^{*}\omega}{\log(e+(c_{0}\star f^{*}\omega)^{1/n})}

\displaystyle\leq n\frac{c_{0}K_{f}(x)\star f^{*}\omega}{\log(e+c_{0}\star f^{*}\omega)},

where

is a constant that depends on
\omega

(5.4)

\int_{K}\frac{|Df|^{n}}{\log(e+|Df|)}dx\leq n\int_{K}\frac{c_{0}K_{f}(x)\star f^{*}\omega}{\log(e+c_{0}\star f^{*}\omega)}dx

By using now (

) and the inequality (see
[
7
, Lemma 2.7]
for a proof)

(5.5)

ab\leq\exp(\kappa a)+\frac{2b}{\kappa}\log(e+b/\kappa),\ \text{for all}\ a\geq 1,b\geq 0,\kappa>0

for

,

and
\kappa=\lambda
we obtain

(5.6)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\frac{|Df|^{n}}{\log(e+|Df|)}dx\leq n

\displaystyle\int_{K}\exp(\lambda K_{f}(x))dx

(5.7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2n}{\lambda}\int_{K}\frac{c_{0}\star f^{*}\omega}{\log(e+c_{0}\star f^{*}\omega)}\log\left(e+\frac{c_{0}\star f^{*}\omega}{\lambda\log(e+c_{0}\star f^{*}\omega)}\right)dx

|Df_{3}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{|z|\left(e-\log|z|\right)\log(e-\log|z|)},\hskip 5.69054pt\text{for}|z|\leq 1

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists a function

Let

[-1,1]^{2}
in dyadic squares. We set

S_{0}=\{[-1,0]\times[0,1],[-1,0]\times[-1,0],[0,1]\times[0,1],[0,1]\times[-1,0]\}

and more generally
S_{n}
will be a collection of
4^{n+1}
squares obtained by partitioning each of the squares of
S_{n-1}
into
4
. By
K_{i,n}
,
i=1,\dots 4^{n+1}
we will denote the elements of
S_{n}
and by
w_{i,n}
their centres. Also we let
w_{0,-1}=0
.

We proceed now with the definition of
\{G_{n}\}
. First we define
G_{0}=0
. Assuming that we have defined
G_{0},\dots,G_{n}
we are going to construct
G_{n+1}
. We are going to use the sequences
R_{n}
and
r_{n}
from the construction of
F
. First we can choose
R_{0}
small enough so that the two dimensional version of function
u
from Lemma
6.1
satisfies (
6.11
) when
|z|\leq R_{0}
. Moreover, since
\int_{B(0,1)}\Phi(|\nabla u(z)|)dz<\infty
we can choose
R_{n}
small enough so that

(6.31)

\int_{B(0,R_{n})}\Phi(|\nabla u(z)|)dz<\frac{1}{2^{n+1}4^{n+1}},

for all
n=0,1,\dots
. Finally, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
we first temporarily choose
r_{n}=R^{\prime}_{n}
and then take
R^{\prime}_{n}<R_{n}
small enough so that

(6.32)

\frac{1}{\log|\log|z-c_{i,n}||}\leq\varepsilon\sqrt{a_{n}},

when
|z-c_{i,n}|\leq R^{\prime}_{n}
. This is possible since
a_{n}
is constant on the annulus
A_{i,n}(c_{i,n},R_{n},r_{n})
. Next since
u(z)\to\infty
as
z\to 0
we can in fact reselect
r_{n}<R^{\prime}_{n}
so that

(6.33)

u(r_{n},r_{n})-u(R^{\prime}_{n},R^{\prime}_{n})=d_{n},

where
d_{n}
denotes the distance between the centres of a square in
S_{n}
and one of its subdividing squares in
S_{n+1}
,
K_{j,n+1}\subset K_{i,n}
. Remember there is no restriction on
r_{n}
and decreasing it makes
a_{n}
larger so (
6.32
) is not affected by that. Define now
G_{n+1}
to be
G_{n}
outside of the balls
B_{i,n}(c_{i,n},R^{\prime}_{n})
. When
r_{n}\leq|z-c_{i,n}|\leq R^{\prime}_{n}
define

(6.34)

G_{n+1}(z)=w_{j,n-1}+(u(z-c_{i,n})-u(R^{\prime}_{n},R^{\prime}_{n}))\frac{w_{i,n}-w_{j,n-1}}{|w_{i,n}-w_{j,n-1}|},

where
j
is the unique index such that
K_{i,n}\subset K_{j,n-1}
. Finally, when
|z-c_{i,n}|\leq r_{n}
we define

G_{n+1}(z)=w_{i,n}.

Next we need to show that the sequence
G_{n}
converges uniformly. To prove that, notice that
G_{n}
and
G_{n+1}
differ only inside the balls
B_{i,n}(c_{i,n},R^{\prime}_{n})
where
G_{n}
is constant while
G_{n+1}
maps the ball to a segment of length
d_{n}
. Hence, for
m>n

(6.35)

||G_{m}-G_{n}||_{\infty}\leq\sum_{k=n}^{m-1}||G_{k+1}-G_{k}||_{\infty}\leq\sum_{k=n}^{m-1}d_{k}.

Since the lengths
d_{k}
shrink at a geometric rate we have that
G_{n}
is a uniformly Cauchy sequence and we are done. Thus the limit map
G
will be continuous and it is easy to see that (
6.31
) implies

(6.36)

\sum_{i=1}^{4^{n+1}}\int_{B_{i,n}(c_{i,n},R^{\prime}_{n})}\Phi(|DG_{n+1}(z)|)dz<\frac{1}{2^{n+1}},

which in turn implies that
G\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Moreover, from (
6.11
) and (
6.32
) we obtain that

(6.37)

|DG(z)|=|\nabla u(z)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt{a_{n}}}{|z-c_{i,n}|(1-\log|z-c_{i,n}|)},

when
r_{n}\leq|z-c_{i,n}|\leq R^{\prime}_{n}
. Finally, it is easy to see that for any
w_{i,n}
there exists a sequence of points
z_{k}\in A_{i,k}(c_{i,k},R^{\prime}_{k},r_{k})
, converging to some point in the segment
[-1,1]\times\{0\}
, such that
G(z_{k})=w_{i,n}
. Since the set of all centres
\{w_{i,n}\}_{i,n\in\mathbb{N}}
is dense in the square
[-1,1]^{2}
and since
G
is continuous we have that

G([-1,1]\times\{0\})=[-1,1]^{2},

as we wanted.
∎

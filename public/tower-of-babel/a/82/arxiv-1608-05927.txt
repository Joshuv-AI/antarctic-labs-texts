Expressing the statement of the Feit-Thompson theorem with diagrams in the category of finite groups
Misha Gavrilovich mi​​​ishap​​​p@sd​​​df.org

Abstract
We reformulate the statement of the Feit-Thompson theorem in terms of diagrams in the category of finite groups, namely iterations of the Quillen lifting property with respect to particular morphisms.

Introduction. Structure of the Paper

We reformulate several notions in finite group theory
in terms of diagrams in the category of finite groups,
namely being solvable, nilpotent,
p
-group and prime-to-
p
group,
abelian, perfect, subnormal subgroup, injective and surjective homomorphism
(see Fig. 1 and Fig. 2).
These notions are enough to formulate the Feit-Thompson theorem.

These properties are obtained by iterating the same diagram chasing “trick”
in the category of groups,
often starting from a single morphism-counterexample; the “trick”
is known as the Quillen lifting property and was introduced
by Quillen [Qui] to axiomatise algebraic topology
in terms of categories.

Our motivation was to formulate part of finite group theory in a form amenable
to automated theorem proving while remaining human readable;
[G1] tried to do the same thing for the basics of general topology.

In more detail, we reformulate these notions using the following four operations

P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,l}
,
P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}
,
P_{0\rightarrow*}
,
P_{*\rightarrow 0}
on the classes (properties)
P
of morphisms in a category.

Left, resp. right, Quillen negation

P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,l}
, resp.
P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}
, of a property
P
is
the class of all morphisms
p^{l}
, resp.
p^{r}
, such that

p^{l}\,\rightthreetimes\,p
, resp.
p\,\rightthreetimes\,p^{r}
, for all
p\in P
; see Fig. 1 for the definition of the Quillen lifting property
\,\rightthreetimes\,
:

P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,l}:=\{\,p^{l}\ :\ p^{l}\,\,\rightthreetimes\,\,p\,\ \text{for each }p\in P\,\}

P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}:=\{\,p^{r}\ :\ p\,\,\rightthreetimes\,p^{r}\,\ \text{for each }p\in P\,\}

The classes
P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,l}
,
P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}
are subcategories which contain no morphisms from
P
but isomorphisms; hence the terminology.

Classes
P_{0\rightarrow*}
,
P_{*\rightarrow 0}
denote the subclass consisting of morphisms from/to the terminal object
0
,
i.e. the trivial group.

Classes
(P_{0\rightarrow*})^{\,\rightthreetimes\,l}_{0\rightarrow*}=(P_{0\rightarrow*})^{l}_{0\rightarrow*}
,
and
(P_{0\rightarrow*})^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}_{0\rightarrow*}=(P_{0\rightarrow*})^{r}_{0\rightarrow*}

can described as classes of objects admitting no non-trivial map from/to
the objects corresponding to
P_{0\rightarrow*}
.

We also say that a morphism
f
is
left, resp. right, Quillen unlike
P
or
P
-unlike

iff
f\in P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,l}
, resp.
f\in P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}
, and that
f
is

left, resp. right, exemplified by
P

iff
f\in(P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r})^{\,\rightthreetimes\,l}
, resp.
f\in(P^{\,\rightthreetimes\,l})^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}
.

In this notation our main observations are that a finite group
G
is soluble
iff
0\longrightarrow G
lies in the class

(((\{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\})^{\,\rightthreetimes\,rl})_{0\rightarrow*})^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}

or, equivalently, right exemplified by taking the commutator subgroup, i.e. lies in

\{[S,S]\longrightarrow S\,:\,S\text{ an arbitrary group}\,\}^{\,\rightthreetimes\,lr}
.

A finite group
G
is nilpotent iff the diagonal map
G\xrightarrow{\Delta}G\times G
,
g\mapsto(g,g)

is exemplified by morphisms from the trivial group, i.e. iff
the diagonal map lies in
(0\longrightarrow*)^{\,\rightthreetimes\,lr}
where
(0\longrightarrow*)
denotes the class of morphisms from the trivial group
0
.

A finite group
G
is perfect iff
0\longrightarrow G
is exemplified by
(\{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\})

or, equivalently, left unlike taking the commutator subgroup,
i.e.
0\longrightarrow G
lies in

(\{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\})^{\,\rightthreetimes\,rl}

or, equivalently, in the class
\{[S,S]\longrightarrow S\,:\,S\text{ an arbitrary group}\,\}^{\,\rightthreetimes\,l}
.

A group
G
is a prime-to-
p
-group, resp. a
p
-group, iff
0\longrightarrow G
lies in the class

(0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/\!p\mathbb{Z})^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}
, resp.
(0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/\!p\mathbb{Z})^{\,\rightthreetimes\,rr}
; in words,
it is right
(0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/\!p\mathbb{Z})
-unlike, resp. right unlike a
(0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/\!p\mathbb{Z})
-unlike morphism.
Classes
(0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z})^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}
and
(\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow 0)^{\,\rightthreetimes\,r}
are the classes of surjective and injective morphisms,
i.e. a map is surjective, resp. injective, iff it is
(0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z})
-unlike, resp.
(\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow 0)
-unlike.

Little attempt has been made to go beyond these examples.
Hence open questions remain: are there other interesting examples of lifting properties in the category of (finite) groups?
Can a complete group-theoretic argument be reformulated in terms of diagram chasing, say
the classification of CA-groups or
pq
-groups, or
elementary properties of subgroup series; can category theory notation
be used to make expositions easier to read?
Can these reformulations be used in automatic theorem proving?
Is there a decidable fragment of (finite) group theory
based on the Quillen lifting property and, more generally,
diagram chasing, cf. [GLS]?

Examples of lifting properties outside of group theory may be found in
a short note [DMG] in
The De Morgan Gazette
and [G1].

This approach was motivated in part by hopes to express some statements
of finite group theory in terms of diagrams in the category of finite groups,
and then use automated diagram chasing to construct short formal proofs.
Particularly important for our motivation was the fact that in the few examples
of lifting properties we did find, it was the lifting property with respect
to a simple counterexample in some intuitive sense, and that the lifting properties
are closely related to the usual definitions.

Expressing properties of finite groups as diagrams.

Figure 2 lists the diagrams representing the properties of groups. Below we describe the same diagrams, and make a couple of remarks about the diagram chasing and the notion of an inner automorphism and Sylow theory.

There is no non-trivial homomorphism from a group
F
to
G
, write
F\not\to G
, iff

0\longrightarrow F\,\,\rightthreetimes\,0\longrightarrow G\text{ or equivalently }F\longrightarrow 0\,\rightthreetimes\,G\longrightarrow 0.

A group
A
is
Abelian
iff

\left<a,b\right>\,\longrightarrow\,\left<a,b:ab=ba\right>\,\rightthreetimes\,\,\,A\longrightarrow 0

where
\left<a,b\right>\,\longrightarrow\,\left<a,b:ab=ba\right>
is the abelianisation morphism sending the free group into the Abelian free group on two generators;
a group
G
is
perfect
,
G=[G,G]
, iff
G\not\to A
for any Abelian group
A
, i.e.

\left<a,b\right>\,\longrightarrow\,\left<a,b:ab=ba\right>\,\rightthreetimes\,\,\,A\longrightarrow 0\ \implies\ G\longrightarrow 0\,\rightthreetimes\,A\longrightarrow 0

equivalently, for an arbitrary homomorphism
g
,

\left<a,b\right>\,\longrightarrow\,\left<a,b:ab=ba\right>\,\rightthreetimes\,\,\,g\ \ \implies\ G\longrightarrow 0\,\rightthreetimes\,\,\,g\,

Yet another reformulation is that, for each group
S
,

0\longrightarrow G\,\,\rightthreetimes\,\,[S,S]\longrightarrow S.

In the category of finite or algebraic groups,
a group
H
is
soluble
iff
G\not\to H
for each perfect group
G
,
i.e.

0\longrightarrow G\,\,\rightthreetimes\,0\longrightarrow H\text{ or equivalently }G\longrightarrow 0\,\rightthreetimes\,H\longrightarrow 0.

Alternatively, a group
H
is
soluble
iff for every homomorphism
f
it holds

f\,\,\rightthreetimes\,\,[G,G]\longrightarrow G\text{ for each group }G\ \implies\ f\,\,\rightthreetimes\,0\longrightarrow\,H.

A prime number
p
does not divide the number elements of a finite group
G

iff
G
has no element of order
p
, i.e. no element
x\in G
such that
x^{p}=1_{G}
yet
x^{1}\neq 1_{G},...,x^{p-1}\neq 1_{G}
,
equivalently
{\mathbb{Z}}/\!p{\mathbb{Z}}\not\to G
, i.e.

0\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}/\!p{\mathbb{Z}}\,\,\rightthreetimes\,0\longrightarrow G\text{ or equivalently }{\mathbb{Z}}/\!p{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow 0\,\rightthreetimes\,G\longrightarrow 0.

A finite group
G
is a
p

p
, iff in the category of finite groups

0\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}/\!p{\mathbb{Z}}\,\rightthreetimes\,0\longrightarrow H\implies 0\longrightarrow H\,\rightthreetimes\,0\longrightarrow G.

A group
H
is the normal closure of the image of
N
,
i.e. no proper normal subgroup of
H
contains the image of
N
, iff for an arbitrary group

N\longrightarrow H\,\,\rightthreetimes\,0\longrightarrow G.

A group

is a subnormal subgroup of a finite group

N\longrightarrow H\,\,\rightthreetimes\,0\longrightarrow B\text{ for each group }B\ \implies N\longrightarrow H\,\,\rightthreetimes\,\,D\longrightarrow G

D\longrightarrow G
right-lifts wrt any map
N\longrightarrow H
such that

is the normal closure of the image of

; the lifting property implies that

is injective. Recall that
D
is a subnormal subgroup of a finite group

D=G_{0}\vartriangleleft G_{1}\vartriangleleft\ldots\vartriangleleft G_{n}=G

such that

,

.

D

then

Given a square corresponding to

, the preimage of

Now assume

of

By construction no proper normal subgroup of

Finally, a finite group
G
is nilpotent iff the diagonal group

G\times G
[Nilp], i.e. iff
the diagonal map
G\xrightarrow{\Delta}G\times G
,
g\mapsto(g,g)

right-lifts wrt any

Sylow theorem implies in a finite group

It is not clear how to express this in a satisfactory manner in terms of category theory (diagram chasing).
Perhaps something along the following lines:
(in the category of finite groups)
each arrow
0\longrightarrow G
decomposes as

0\xrightarrow{(p\text{-group})}Syl_{p}(G)\xrightarrow{(\text{prime to }p)}G

uniquely up to conjugation.
Here

Ord\,C/Ord\,Im\,B
is prime to
p
.

We remark that the notion of
an inner automorphism can be reformulated in a diagram chasing manner.
An inner automorphism
g\longmapsto aga^{-1}
of a group
G
extends to an automorphism
h\longmapsto\iota(a)h\iota(a)^{-1}

of a group
H
for any embedding
\iota:G\longrightarrow H
. [Inn, Sch] show this is a characterisation:
an automorphism
\sigma:G\longrightarrow G
is inner iff it extends to an automorphism of
H
for any embedding
\iota:G\longrightarrow H
.
See [Inn] and references therein for several more similar reformulations.

The Feit-Thompson theorem can be expressed as a combination of lifting properties:
the theorem says says that each (finite) group of odd order is soluble, i.e.
for each perfect finite group
G
and each finite group
H
,

0\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\,\rightthreetimes\,0\longrightarrow H\implies 0\longrightarrow G\,\rightthreetimes\,0\longrightarrow H.

Note that all these examples but the last one have a flavour of negation—a notion
being defined by the lifting property
with respect to the simplest counterexample.

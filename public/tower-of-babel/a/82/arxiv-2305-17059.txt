Abstract
. In this paper we study the behaviour of selective separability properties in the class of Frechét-Urysohn spaces. We present two examples, the first one given in ZFC proves the existence of a countable Frechét-Urysohn (hence R R -separable and selectively separable) space which is not H H -separable; assuming 𝔭 = 𝔠 \mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{c} , we construct such an example which is also zero-dimensional and α 4 \alpha_{4} . Also, motivated by a result of Barman and Dow stating that the product of two countable Frechét-Urysohn spaces is M M -separable under PFA, we show that the MA is not sufficient here. In the last section we prove that in the Laver model, the product of any two H H -separable spaces is m ​ H mH -separable.

1.
Introduction

A space
X
is said to be
Frechét-Urysohn
, briefly FU, if for every
A\subset X
and
x\in\overline{A}\setminus A
there exists a sequence
S\subset A\setminus\{x\}
converging to
x
, i.e.,
S\in[A\setminus\{x\}]^{\omega}
and
|U\setminus A|<\omega
for every open neighbourhood
U
of
x
.
It has been known since 50 years ago that the product of two FU spaces
does not have to be a FU space, neither for countable nor for compact spaces,
see
[
8
,
17
,
18
,
21
]
and references therein.
However, it is still interesting which properties
products of two FU spaces must (at least consistently) possess.
In this paper we study various combinatorial versions of the separability of such products. Our motivation comes from the following result of
Barman and Dow:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
3
, 3.3]

(PFA) The product of finitely many countable FU spaces is M-separable.

Recall from
[
20
]
that a topological space
X
is said to be
selectively separable
(or
M-separable
) if for every sequence
\langle D_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
of dense subsets of
X
, there are finite sets
F_{n}\subset D_{n}
,
n\in\omega
, such that
\bigcup\{F_{n}:n\in\omega\}
is dense in
X
.

In contrast with Theorem
1.1
, Barman and Dow proved in
[
2
]
that every separable FU space is
M
-separable and that under CH, there are two
countable FU spaces whose product is not
M
-separable.
In
[
16
, 6.1 and 6.2]

this result has been improved by showing that under CH there are two countable FU
H
-separable topological groups whose
product is not
M
-separable. The main result of this paper
is the following

Theorem 1.2
.

It is consistent with (MA+
\neg\mathit{CH}
) that there are
two countable regular FU spaces whose product is not
M
-separable.

Among other things Theorem
1.2
shows that Theorem
1.1

cannot be proved using only the equality between some standard
cardinal characteristics of the continuum, as all these characteristics are also equal to
\mathfrak{c}
under MA.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on the application
of certain kind of separated mad families, whose existence
is consistent with MA by essentially the same argument as in

[
9
]
, see Section
4
.

The notion of
M
-separability motivated
Bella, Bonanzinga and Matveev to introduce in
[
4
]

further variations thereof of this property. In particular, a topological space
X
is said to be
H-separable
(resp.,
R-separable
), if for every sequence
\langle D_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
of dense subsets of
X
, there are finite sets
F_{n}\subset D_{n}
(resp., points
x_{n}\in D_{n}
),
n\in\omega
, such that every nonempty open set in
X
meets all but finitely many
F_{n}
(resp.,
\{x_{n}:n\in\omega\}
is dense in
X
).

Gruenhage and Sakai in
[
11
]
proved that separable FU spaces are
R
-separable (this improves the result of Barman and Dow asserting that separable FU spaces are
M
-separable). In Section
2
we prove that the
H
-separability behaves differently, i.e., in ZFC there exists a countable Hausdorff FU space which is not
H
-separable, see Theorem
4.5
. This gives new examples of a space which is
R
-separable but not
H
-separable, see
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
for more examples of this kind.

In
[
11
]
, using different terminology, the following weaker versions of combinatorial density properties were introduced: A topological space
X
is said to be

mP
-separable,
where
P\in\{M,H,R\}
, if
it satisfies the conclusion of the
P
-separability for any decreasing sequence of dense subsets of
X
. For instance,

X
is
mR-separable
if for every decreasing sequence
\langle D_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
of dense subsets of
X
, there are points
x_{n}\in D_{n}
such that
\{x_{n}:n\in\omega\}
is dense in
X
.

that the

-separability and

-separability are in fact equivalent, which motivated the authors to ask in
[

whether

-separable space which is not
H
-separable.

Since
[

combined with
[

implies that every countable FU space is

-separable, the FU non-

We do not know how to get a regular (equivalently, zero-dimensional)
example outright in ZFC.

Question 1.3
.

(1)

Is there a ZFC example of a regular countable FU (resp.
mH
-separable) space which is not
H
-separable?

(2)

Is there a (ZFC example of) a [regular] countable
mR
-separable space

Given a space
X
and
x\in X
, we denote by
\Gamma_{x}
the set of all
A\in[X\setminus\{x\}]^{\omega}
which converge to
x
. In
[
1
]
, Arhangel’skiĭ introduced the following local properties
of a space

at some point

(\alpha_{1})

For each
\langle S_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle\in\Gamma^{\omega}_{x}
, there is
S\in\Gamma_{x}
such that
S_{n}\subset^{*}S
for all
n\in\omega
;

(\alpha_{2})

For each
\langle S_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle\in\Gamma^{\omega}_{x}
, there is
S\in\Gamma_{x}
such that
S_{n}\cap S
is infinite for all
n\in\omega
;

(\alpha_{3})

For each
\langle S_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle\in\Gamma^{\omega}_{x}
, there is
S\in\Gamma_{x}
such that
S_{n}\cap S
is infinite for infinitely many
n\in\omega
;

(\alpha_{4})

For each
\langle S_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle\in\Gamma^{\omega}_{x}
, there is
S\in\Gamma_{x}
such that
S_{n}\cap S\neq\emptyset
for infinitely many
n\in\omega
.

A space

is an
\alpha_{i}
space, where
i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
,
if it is an
\alpha_{i}
space at each
x\in X.

Each of these
\alpha_{i}
-properties obviously imply the next one.
The idea behind the introduction of
\alpha_{i}
spaces was to find additional properties which together with the FU property guarantee that
the product of two spaces is again FU, see
[
1
,
17
]

for more details.

In Section
3
we prove that every separable
\alpha_{2}
FU space is
H
-separable, and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{c}
yields a countable regular FU
\alpha_{4}
which is not
H
-separable.
This leaves the following question open, which actually consists of several
subquestions.

Question 1.4
.

Is it consistent that every (regular)
\alpha_{4}
[resp.
\alpha_{3}
]
FU space is
H
-separable?

In Section
5
we show that
it is consistent that the product of two countable
H
-separable spaces
is
mH
-separable. More precisely, this is the case in the classical Laver model introduced in
[
14
]
.
This improved an earlier result from
[
19
]

stating that in the same model, the product of any two countable
H
-separable spaces is
M
-separable.
Consequently, in the Laver model the product of any two
H
-separable spaces is
mH
-separable provided that it is hereditarily separable.
This result motivates the following

Question 1.5
.

(1)

Is there a countable (regular)
mH
-separable space in the Laver model
which is not
H
-separable?

(2)

Is the class of

-separable (

-separable) spaces
consistently closed under finite products? What about the Laver model?

(3)

Is the product of 3 (resp. finitely many)

-separable spaces

-separable in the Laver model?

Finally, the proof of Theorem

gives actually the

-separability of the product, but we do not know whether it can be
pushed further.

Question 1.6
.

Does PFA imply that the product of two countable
FU spaces is
mH
- (resp.
R
-) separable?

,

It is worth noting that the proof given by the authors provides a stronger result: their argument proves that the product of finitely many countable FU space under PFA is in fact

The aim of this section is to show that MA is not sufficent in Theorem

Let
\tau_{0}
be a topology on
\omega

More precisely, the following result was proved by Dow and Shelah.

9

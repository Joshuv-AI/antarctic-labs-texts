Double scaling limit of multi-matrix models at large D D
V. Bonzom, V. Nador, A. Tanasa

Abstract
In this paper, we study a double scaling limit of two multi-matrix models: the U ​ ( N ) 2 × O ⁡ ( D ) U(N)^{2}\times O(D) -invariant model with all quartic interactions and the bipartite U ⁡ ( N ) × O ⁡ ( D ) U(N)\times O(D) -invariant model with tetrahedral interaction ( D D being here the number of matrices and N N being the size of each matrix). Those models admit a double, large N N and large D D expansion. While N N tracks the genus of the Feynman graphs, D D tracks another quantity called the grade. In both models, we rewrite the sum over Feynman graphs at fixed genus and grade as a finite sum over combinatorial objects called schemes. This is a result of combinatorial nature which remains true in the quantum mechanical setting and in quantum field theory. Then we proceed to the double scaling limit at large D D , i.e. for vanishing grade. In particular, we find that the most singular schemes, in both models, are the same as those found in Benedetti et al. for the U ​ ( N ) 2 × O ⁡ ( D ) U(N)^{2}\times O(D) -invariant model restricted to its tetrahedral interaction. This is a different universality class than in the 1-matrix model whose double scaling is not summable.

The main theorems are the following.

Theorem I.1
.

Any 2-point graph can be reconstructed from a unique scheme of the same genus and grade by extending chains and adding melons.

In other words, each scheme represents an infinite family of graphs. It is therefore possible to repackage the sum of all graphs of any given genus
g

G_{g,l}=\sum_{\text{Schemes $\mathcal{S}$}}P_{\mathcal{S}}(C(M),M)

Theorem I.2
.

The set of schemes at fixed genus and grade is finite in both models.

Characterizing all schemes of a given genus and grade is still a hard combinatorial problem which has not been solved (also for tensor models).

Just like in
Benedetti et al. 2022

Theorem I.3
.

The double scaling limit at large
D
is dominated by rooted binary plane trees.

We recall that a binary tree is a tree (i.e. a graph with no cycle), such that each vertex has exactly zero or two children. Vertices with no children are called leaves. In a plane tree, the tree is embedded so that the two children of a vertex can be labeled as left and right.

Let us recall that, with respect to

-invariant model, we will allow for all quartic interactions (instead of just the tetrahedral one). We perform the double scaling limit at large

As we have already pointed out, our strategy and results are similar to previous scheme decompositions and double scaling limits in the literature. To further emphasize the universality of the approach we have organized both Sections

The representation (ribbon graphs or edge-colored graphs) however does not affect the general strategy of the proofs in both models.

The most difficult part in both models, as was already the case in

II.3.2
Dominant schemes

There are finitely many schemes for a given genus and grade
(h,l)
, thus all singularities of their generating function come from the generating series of melons and chains. As seen in Section
II.3.1
, the leading singularity is that of broken chains, which we will show are in bounded number in a scheme of fixed genus and grade. Moreover, we restrict attention to schemes of vanishing grade as they are the ones dominating the large
N
, large
D
expansion, just as in
Benedetti et al. 2022
. The schemes of vanishing grade which have the maximal number of broken chains for their genus are said to be
dominant
.

Note that those dominant schemes cannot be found from the equivalent result for the
O(N)^{3}
model. Indeed, since we have embedded our model into the
O(N)^{3}
model and used that to prove the finiteness of the number of schemes at fixed genus and grade, it is a natural question to ask whether the latter model can also be used to identify the dominant schemes. However, dominant schemes of the
O(N)^{3}
have sub-schemes of degree
\omega=1/2
(the leaves from the tree in the representation explained below). A graph of the
U(N)^{2}\times O(D)
model which is mapped to a graph of degree 1/2 of the
O(N)^{3}
model has non-vanishing grade. It is thus not possible to deduce the dominant schemes of the
U(N)^{2}\times O(D)
model from those of the
O(N)^{3}
model.

The combinatorial analysis of dominant schemes for the
U(N)^{2}\times O(D)
-invariant model with tetrahedral interaction only has been done in
Benedetti et al. 2022
. It can be adapted to our situation with minor adjustments. We sketch the main points of the derivation.

Definition of the model and its large

III.1.1
Feynman graphs, genus and grade

The model.

The
U(N)\times O(D)
multi-matrix model is a close cousin of the
U(N)^{2}\times O(D)
tensor model. We consider a single quartic interaction and its complex conjugate,

S_{U(N)\times O(D)}(X_{\mu},X_{\mu}^{\dagger})=-ND\sum\limits_{\mu=1}^{D}\Tr(X_{\mu}^{\dagger}X_{\mu})+\frac{\lambda}{4}ND^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{\mu,\nu=1}^{D}\Bigl(\Tr(X_{\mu}X_{\nu}X_{\mu}X_{\nu})+\Tr(X^{\dagger}_{\mu}X^{\dagger}_{\nu}X^{\dagger}_{\mu}X^{\dagger}_{\nu})\Bigr)

(75)

It is invariant under

X_{\mu}\mapsto\sum_{\mu^{\prime}=1}^{D}O_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}\ UX_{\mu^{\prime}}U^{\dagger}

(76)

where
O\in O(D)
and
U\in U(N)
.

The Feynman expansion is performed using a different graphical representation than in the previous model, which is closer to matrix models.

•

We represent an interaction
\sum_{\mu,\nu}\Tr(X_{\mu}X_{\nu}X_{\mu}X_{\nu})
as a white vertex of degree 4, and each matrix as an incident half-edge. These half-edges are cyclically ordered, say counter-clockwise, around each vertex, reflecting the structure of the trace.

•

The interaction
\sum_{\mu,\nu}\Tr(X_{\mu}^{\dagger}X_{\nu}^{\dagger}X_{\mu}^{\dagger}X_{\nu}^{\dagger})
is represented as a black vertex, also with 4 incident, cyclically ordered, half-edges.

•

Propagators glue half-edges incident to black vertices to half-edges incident to white vertices.

Feynman graphs are thus
combinatorial maps
(i.e. ribbon graphs), which are bipartite and tetravalent. The faces of the map are the cycles obtained by following edges from black to white vertices and corners at vertices counter-clockwise (a corner is the portion between two consecutive edges at a vertex).

The propagator is
\frac{1}{ND}\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}\delta_{\mu\nu}
. It therefore identifies the matrix indices of half-edges and as usual in matrix models, each face receives a weight
N
. Moreover, propagators also identify the vector indices of half-edges. There is therefore another notion of cycles which receive the weight
D
. They are cycles which follow edges and cross the vertices (i.e. leaving one half-edges on each side). There are similar to the bicolored cycles of color
\{0,3\}
in the previous model, which also received the weight
D
, and we will call them
straight cycles
.

Let
\bar{\mathbb{M}}
be the set of vacuum, connected Feynman graphs. For
\bar{\mathcal{M}}\in\bar{\mathbb{M}}
we denote
V
,
E
,
F
,
\phi
the numbers of vertices, edges, faces and straight cycles.

An important result of
Carrozza et al. 2020
is the
1/N
and
1/D
expansion of this model.

Theorem III.1

(
Carrozza et al. 2020
)
.

The free energy admits the following expansion on maps

2-point maps.

Due to the symmetries of the model, the 2-point function has the form

\langle(X_{\mu})_{ab}(X^{\dagger}_{\nu})_{cd}\rangle=\frac{1}{N^{2}D}G_{N,D}(\lambda)\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc},

From a 2-point map

Note that in contrast with the model of the previous section where we had defined equivalent objects, it is here not true that the amplitudes of

are simply related by a factor

However, it is clear that the amplitude of a rooted map is the same as that of the vacuum map obtained by removing the marking on the root. Although there is a clear bijection between 2-point maps and rooted maps, the expansions are thus a bit different. In the following we will focus on the rooted function

If

Chains.

A chain is either an isolated dipole, or a sequence of dipoles glued side by side, as in Equation (

) where each dipole-vertex can now be a U-dipole or an O-dipole. The length of a chain is its number of dipoles. A chain of length

We distinguish between chains made of O-dipoles only (resp. U-dipoles) named

(resp.
U-chains
), which can have length greater than or equal to 1, and other chains which are called

and are made of at least two different dipoles. The following proposition is easily proved.

Proposition 5
.

Changing the length of a chain does not change the genus nor the grade (both in vacuum and 2-point graphs).

Let

and

be the generating functions of O-chains, U-chains and broken chains respectively, decorated with melons. They are given by

\displaystyle C_{O}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{U}{1-U}

\displaystyle C_{U}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2U}{1-2U}

(90)

\displaystyle B(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{3U}{1-3U}-\frac{U}{1-U}-\frac{2U}{1-2U}=\frac{(4-6U)U^{2}}{(1-U)(1-2U)(1-3U)}

In the following, we will further need to distinguish between O-chains of even and odd lengths. Their respective generating functions are

III.1.3
Singularity analysis

The singularity analysis is simpler than in the previous model, and is completely straightforward.

The leading singularity of

and of

occurs at

. It is such that

. The denominators of the series

Therefore the “most singular” objects are the broken chains as they are the only ones to diverge at the critical point.

Lemma 2

The skeleton graph

III.2.3
Finiteness of the number of schemes

To prove Theorem

, we will use the same 2-step strategy as in

Bonzom et al. 2022

1.

2.

This proves Theorem

We start by showing that the number of chain-vertices is bounded by the genus and the grade. We have the following Lemma, the same as in

Lemma 3

If a scheme

We sketch the proof and refer to

. The edges in the complement

correspond in
\mathcal{S}
to a set of non-separating chain-vertices. Removing each of them decreases the genus by 1 or the grade by 4. After all those removals, one obtains a scheme

of genus

and grade

As an illustration, we describe the attachment of

From this lemma, we get the following bound on the number of chain-vertices of a scheme.

Proposition 6
.

A scheme of genus
g
and grade

has at most

Proof.

By contraposition of the Lemma

Via Theorem

\displaystyle C_{O,o}=\frac{1}{24}+O(\frac{t}{t_{c}})

(107)

\displaystyle U(t)\underset{t\rightarrow t_{c}}{\sim}\frac{1}{3}-\sqrt{\frac{8}{27}}\left(1-\frac{t}{t_{c}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(108)

Therefore it follows that

\displaystyle B(t)\underset{t\rightarrow t_{c}}{\sim}\sqrt{\frac{27}{8}}\left(1-\frac{t}{t_{c}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(109)

Thus near the critical point
t_{c}
we find

G_{\text{dom}}^{g}(t)\underset{t\rightarrow t_{c}}{\sim}L^{2-2g}\frac{4}{3}\Cat_{g-1}\left(\frac{1}{8}+t_{c}\frac{1}{24}\right)^{g}\left(1+6t_{c}\right)^{g-1}\left(\frac{27}{8}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{t}{t_{c}}\right)}\right)^{g-\frac{1}{2}}

(110)

Therefore, after introducing the double scaling parameter
\kappa
defined as

\kappa^{-1}=L^{2}\frac{1}{\left(\frac{1}{8}+t_{c}\frac{1}{24}\right)\left(1+6t_{c}\right)}\frac{8}{27}\left(1-\frac{t}{t_{c}}\right)

(111)

the following equality holds

\displaystyle\left(1+3t\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{8}{27}\left(1-\frac{t}{t_{c}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{L}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{8}+t_{c}\frac{1}{24}}{1+6t_{c}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(112)

so that in the double scaling limit,
G_{dom}^{g}(t)
, for
g>0
, contributes to the 2-point function as

G_{dom}^{g}(t)=\frac{4}{3}L\Cat_{g-1}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{8}+t_{c}\frac{1}{24}}{1+6t_{c}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa^{g-\frac{1}{2}}.

(113)

For
g=0
, we simply have to account for melons thus contributing as
M(t_{c})=\frac{4}{3}
.

Summing over the genus
g
, it is found that the series is convergent for
\kappa\leq\frac{1}{4}
. This gives the expression of the 2-point function in the double scaling limit,

\displaystyle G_{2}^{DS}(t)

\displaystyle=L^{-2}\sum\limits_{g\geq 0}G_{dom}^{g}(t)

(114)

\displaystyle=4/3+\frac{4}{3}\frac{1}{L\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{8}+t_{c}\frac{1}{24}}{1+6t_{c}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum\limits_{g}\Cat_{g-1}\kappa^{g}

\displaystyle=4/3+\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{L}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{8}+t_{c}\frac{1}{24}}{1+6t_{c}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\kappa}}{\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}}

(115)

This function has a square-root singularity, just like the double scaling 2-point function of tensor graphs in
Gurau and Schaeffer 2016
, as well as the one in the multi-orientable model
Gurau et al. 2015
and the
O(N)^{3}
-invariant model
Bonzom et al. 2022
, and also similar to that of the
U(N)^{2}\times O(D)
-invariant model with tetrahedral interaction
Benedetti et al. 2022
and to the double scaling function we found in the previous model, Equation (
74
).

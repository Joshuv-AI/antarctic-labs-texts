Iwasawa Invariants for elliptic curves over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions and Kida’s Formula
Debanjana Kundu, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. This paper aims at studying the Iwasawa λ \lambda -invariant of the p p -primary Selmer group. We study the growth behaviour of p p -primary Selmer groups in p p -power degree extensions over non-cyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of a number field. We prove a generalization of Kida’s formula in such a case. Unlike the cyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension, where all primes are finitely decomposed, in the ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions we consider, primes may be infinitely decomposed. In the second part of the paper, we study the relationship of Iwasawa invariants with respect to congruences, obtaining refinements of the results of R. Greenberg–V. Vatsal and K. Kidwell. As an application, we provide an algorithm for constructing elliptic curves with large anticyclotomic λ \lambda -invariant. Our results are illustrated by explicit computation.

Introduction

The classical Riemann–Hurwitz formula describes the relationship of the Euler characteristics of two surfaces when one is a ramified covering of the other.
Suppose
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}R_{1}\rightarrow R_{2}
is an
n
-fold covering of compact, connected Riemann surfaces and
g_{1},\ g_{2}
are their respective genus.
The classical Riemann–Hurwitz formula is the statement

2g_{2}-2=(2g_{1}-2)n+\sum\left(e(P_{2})-1\right)

where the sum is over all points
P_{2}
on
R_{2}
and

denotes the ramification index of
P_{2}
for the covering
\pi
(see
[
Sil09
, Chapter II Theorem 5.9]
).
An analogue of the above formula for algebraic number fields was proven by Y. Kida in
[
Kid80
]
.
Kida’s formula describes the change of (cyclotomic) Iwasawa
\lambda
-invariants in a

-extension in terms of the degree and the ramification index.
In

which are not necessarily finite.
More precisely,

(
[

.

Let

-extension of

.

-invariant,
\mu(K^{\operatorname{cyc}})=0
.
Then

\lambda(\mathcal{L})=p\lambda(K^{\operatorname{cyc}})+\sum_{w^{\prime}}\left(e(w^{\prime}|v^{\prime})-1\right)+(p-1)(h_{2}-h_{1}).

The sum is over all primes
w^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{L}
(above
v^{\prime}
in
K^{\operatorname{cyc}}
) not above
p

is the rank of the abelian group

is the group of all units of

.

In the study of rational points of elliptic curves, the Selmer group plays a crucial role.
In

]

-extension of number fields.

Kida-type formulae for

Basic Notions

Throughout this article, let
p\geq 5
be a fixed prime number and
K
be a number field.
Let
K_{\infty}
be any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
.
Two examples of interest are as follows:

(1)

K
is a number field and
K_{\infty}=K^{\operatorname{cyc}}
is the
cyclotomic

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
.
This is the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
contained in
K(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
.

(2)

K
is an imaginary quadratic field and
K_{\infty}=K^{\operatorname{ac}}
is an
anticyclotomic

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
.
This is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
which is Galois and pro-dihedral over
\mathbb{Q}
.
More generally,
K_{\infty}
may be chosen to be an anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension over any CM field,
K
.

If
K
is any number field with signature
(r_{1},r_{2})
, the Leopoldt’s conjecture predicts that the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-rank of the maximal abelian pro-
p
extension of
K
unramified away from primes above
p
is
1+r_{2}
.
In other words, it predicts that there are
1+r_{2}

independent

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of
K
.
For an imaginary quadratic field, the cyclotomic and anticyclotomic extensions together generate a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension, containing infinitely many
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions that are unramified at all primes
v\nmid p
and not Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Set
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\Gamma_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K_{n})
.
The Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda
is the completed group ring
\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}\rrbracket
.
Choose a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
There is an isomorphism
\Lambda\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
associating the formal variable
T
with
\gamma-1
.

For a discrete
p
-primary abelian group
M
, set
M^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{cnts}}(M,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})
to denote its
Pontryagin dual
.
We say that
M
is a cofinitely generated (resp. cotorsion)
\Lambda
-module if
M^{\vee}
is finitely generated (resp. torsion) as a
\Lambda
-module.
For a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\Lambda
-module
M
, the
Structure Theorem of
\Lambda
-modules
asserts that
M^{\vee}
is pseudo-isomorphic to a finite direct sum of cyclic
\Lambda
-modules, i.e., there is a map of
\Lambda
-modules

M^{\vee}\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{m_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(h_{j}(T))\right)

with finite kernel and cokernel.
Here,
m_{i}>0
and
h_{j}(T)
is a distinguished polynomial (i.e., a monic polynomial with non-leading coefficients divisible by
p
).
The
characteristic ideal
of
M^{\vee}
is (up to a unit) generated by the characteristic element,

f_{M}^{(p)}(T)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=p^{\sum_{i}m_{i}}\prod_{j}h_{j}(T).

The
\mu
-invariant of
M
is defined as the power of
p
in
f_{M}^{(p)}(T)
.
More precisely,

\mu(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{ if }s=0\\
\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}&\textrm{ if }s>0.\end{cases}

The
\lambda
-invariant of
M
is the degree of the characteristic element, i.e.

\lambda(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\deg h_{j}(T).

.

finite, it follows that

For a
\Lambda
-module
M

M^{\iota}
for the
\Lambda

h_{G}\left(H^{1}\left(K_{S}/L_{\infty},E[p^{\infty}]\right)\right)=h_{G}\left(E(L_{\infty})[p^{\infty}]\right)=1.

(2)

The local Herbrand quotient is expressed as follows

h_{G}\left(J_{v}(E/L_{\infty})\right)=\prod_{w^{\prime}\in v^{*}(L_{\infty})}h_{G}\left(E(L_{\infty,w^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]\right).

In the above, the empty product is taken to be
1
.

Proof.

(1)

This assertion follows from
[
HM99
, Lemma 4.1]
.

(2)

By
[
HM99
, Lemmas 4.2 and 4.3]
,

h_{G}\left(J_{v}(E/L_{\infty})\right)=\prod_{w^{\prime}\in v(L_{\infty})}h_{G}\left(E(L_{\infty,w^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]\right).

If
w^{\prime}|p
, the Herbrand quotient
h_{G}\left(E(L_{\infty,w^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]\right)=1
by
[
HM99
, Lemma 4.3]
, which generalizes verbatim to our setting.
Next, assume that
v\in S\setminus S_{p}
.
To prove the claim, we show that
h_{G}\left(E(L_{\infty,w^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]\right)=1
for
w^{\prime}\notin v^{*}(L_{\infty})
.
When
w^{\prime}\notin v^{*}(L_{\infty})
, either of the following conditions is satisfied

(a)

is unramified,

(b)

w^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
.

If
w^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
, then it follows from Lemma
4.5
that

Therefore, assume that
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
.
Thus,
L_{\infty,w^{\prime}}/K_{\infty,v^{\prime}}
must be an unramified extension.
By Assumption
4.2
, since
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
, the prime
v\in\Sigma
and is finitely decomposed in
K_{\infty}
.
Since

, it follows that
K_{\infty,v^{\prime}}=K_{v}^{\operatorname{cyc}}
, the unique unramified pro-
p
extension of
K_{v}
.
Since
L_{\infty,w^{\prime}}/K_{\infty,v^{\prime}}
is unramified, it follows that

L_{\infty,w^{\prime}}=K_{\infty,v^{\prime}}=K_{v}^{\operatorname{cyc}}.

In this case, it is clear that the Herbrand quotient

h_{G}\left(E(L_{\infty,w^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]\right)=1

since the group
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\infty,w^{\prime}}/K_{\infty,v^{\prime}})
is trivial.

∎

.

With notation as above,

Proof of Theorem

.

Recall that it suffices to prove the theorem when

We recall an equivalent definition of the Selmer group due to Greenberg.

H^{1}(K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}/K_{\infty},E[p^{\infty}])\rightarrow\prod_{v\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\mathcal{H}_{v}(E/K_{\infty}).

Lemma 6.5

The cohomology of left-symmetric conformal algebra and its applications
Jun Zhao, Bo Hou

Abstract
. In this paper, we develop a cohomology theory of a left-symmetric conformal algebra and study its some applications. We define the cohomology of a left-symmetric conformal algebra, and then give an isomorphism between the cohomology spaces of the left-symmetric conformal algebra and its sub-adjacent Lie conformal algebra. As applications of the cohomology theory, we study linear deformations, formal 1 1 -parameter deformations, T ∗ T^{*} -extensions of a left-symmetric conformal algebra respectively and obtain some properties.

Similar to the relation between Lie algebras and left-symmetric algebras

Left-symmetric conformal algebras appear useful for studying Lie conformal algebras. The notion of a left-symmetric conformal algebra was introduced in

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall notions of Lie conformal algebras, conformal modules and the cohomology of Lie conformal algebras in

In Section 4, we introduce the Nijienhuis operator of a left-symmetric conformal algebra and obtain that a Nijenhuis operator can also generate a trivial linear deformation as the Theorem

3.
The cohomology of a left-symmetric conformal algebra

In this section, we recall some relative definitions about a left-symmetric conformal algebra and define the cohomology of a left-symmetric conformal algebra. Then discuss about the relation between the cohomology of a left-symmetric conformal algebra and its sub-adjacent Lie conformal algebra. The following notions can be found in

Definition 3.1
.

A

A
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module endowed with a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

\displaystyle(\partial a)_{\lambda}b=-\lambda a_{\lambda}b,\qquad a_{\lambda}(\partial b)=(\partial+\lambda)a_{\lambda}b,

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c-a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)=(b_{\mu}a)_{\lambda+\mu}c-b_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c),

for any

A left-symmetric conformal algebra

is called
finite
if it is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module. The

of a left-symmetric conformal algebra

is its rank as a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module.

A=\mathbb{C}[\partial]a

Example 3.3

CurA=\mathbb{C}[\partial]\otimes A,\qquad a_{\lambda}b=a\cdot b,\ \forall\ a,b\in A.

4.
Linear deformations of a left-symmetric conformal algebra

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra. In this section, we study the linear deformations of the left-symmetric conformal algebra
A
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra and
\omega\in C^{2}(A,A)
be a conformal bilinear map. If

a\cdot_{\lambda}^{\omega}b=a_{\lambda}b+t\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)

defines a new left-symmetric conformal algebra structure on
A
, we say that
\omega
generates
a linear deformation
of the left-symmetric conformal algebra
A
.

It is direct to check that
\omega
is a linear deformation of a left-symmetric conformal algebra
A
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(7)

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(a_{\lambda}b,c)+{\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-\omega_{\lambda}(a,b_{\mu}c)-a_{\lambda}\omega_{\mu}(b,c)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ =\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(b_{\mu}a,c)+{\omega_{\mu}(b,a)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-\omega_{\mu}(b,a_{\lambda}c)-b_{\mu}\omega_{\lambda}(a,c),

(8)

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(\omega_{\lambda}(a,b),c)-\omega_{\lambda}(a,\omega_{\mu}(b,c))=\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(\omega_{\mu}(b,a),c)-\omega_{\mu}(b,\omega_{\lambda}(a,c)).

Obviously, (
7
) implies that
\omega
is a
2
-cocycle of the left-symmetric conformal algebra
A
with coefficients in the adjoint module, i.e.

(\delta\omega)_{\lambda,\mu}(a,b,c)=0.

Furthermore, (
8
) means that
(A,\omega)
is a left-symmetric conformal algebra.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra. Two linear deformations
\omega^{1}
and
\omega^{2}
are said to be
equivalent
if there exists a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
N:A\rightarrow A
such that

T_{t}={\rm id}+tN

is a left-symmetric conformal algebra homomorphism from
(A,\cdot^{\omega^{2}}_{\lambda})
to
(A,\cdot^{\omega^{1}}_{\lambda})
. In particular, a linear deformation
\omega
is said to be
trivial
if there exists a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
N:A\rightarrow A
such that

T_{t}={\rm id}+tN

is a left-symmetric conformal algebra homomorphism from
(A,\cdot^{\omega}_{\lambda})
to
A
.

Let
T_{t}={\rm id}+tN
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra homomorphism from
(A,\cdot^{\omega^{2}}_{\lambda})
to
(A,\cdot^{\omega^{1}}_{\lambda})
.
Then by (
4
) we obtain

(9)

\displaystyle\omega^{1}_{\lambda}(N(a),N(b))=0,

(10)

\displaystyle\omega^{1}_{\lambda}(a,N(b))+\omega^{1}_{\lambda}(N(a),b)=N(\omega^{2}_{\lambda}(a,b))-{N(a)}_{\lambda}N(b),

(11)

\displaystyle\omega^{2}_{\lambda}(a,b)-\omega^{1}_{\lambda}(a,b)={N(a)}_{\lambda}b+a_{\lambda}N(b)-N(a_{\lambda}b).

Note that (
11
) implies that
\omega^{2}-\omega^{1}=\delta N.
Then we have

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra. If two linear deformations
\omega^{1}
and
\omega^{2}
are equivalent, then
\omega^{1}
and
\omega^{2}
are in the same cohomology class of
H^{2}(A,A)
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra. A
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
N:A\rightarrow A
is called a
Nijenhuis operator
on
A
if
N
satisfies

(12)

\displaystyle N(a)_{\lambda}N(b)=N(a\cdot^{N}_{\lambda}b),\qquad\forall\ a,b\in A,

where the product
\cdot^{N}
is defined by

(13)

\displaystyle a\cdot^{N}_{\lambda}b={N(a)}_{\lambda}b+a_{\lambda}N(b)-N(a_{\lambda}b).

By (
9
)-(
11
), it is obvious that a trivial linear deformation of a left-symmetric conformal algebra gives rise to a Nijenhuis operator. Conversely, a Nijenhuis operator can also generate a trivial linear deformation as the following theorem shows.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
N
be a Nijenhuis operator on a left-symmetric conformal algebra
A
. Then a linear deformation
\omega
of
A
can be obtained by putting

\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)=(\delta N)_{\lambda}(a,b)=a\cdot^{N}_{\lambda}b.

Furthermore, this linear deformation
\omega
is trivial.

Proof.

To show that
\omega
generates a linear deformation of
A
, we only to verify that (
8
) holds, that is
(A,\cdot^{\omega})
is a left-symmetric conformal algebra. By direct computations, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(\omega_{\lambda}(a,b),c)-\omega_{\lambda}(a,\omega_{\mu}(b,c))-\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(\omega_{\mu}(b,a),c)+\omega_{\mu}(b,\omega_{\lambda}(a,c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda+\mu}({N(a)}_{\lambda}b,c)+\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(a_{\lambda}N(b),c)-\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(N(a_{\lambda}b),c)-\omega_{\lambda}(a,{N(b)}_{\mu}c)

\displaystyle-\omega_{\lambda}(a,b_{\mu}N(c))+\omega_{\lambda}(a,N(b_{\mu}c))-\omega_{\lambda+\mu}({N(b)}_{\mu}a,c)-\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(b_{\mu}N(a),c)

\displaystyle+\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(N(b_{\mu}a),c)+\omega_{\mu}(b,{N(a)}_{\lambda}c)+\omega_{\mu}(b,a_{\lambda}N(c))-\omega_{\mu}(b,N(a_{\lambda}c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{N\big({N(a)}_{\lambda}b\big)}_{\lambda+\mu}c+\big({N(a)}_{\lambda}b\big)_{\lambda+\mu}N(c)-N\big(({N(a)}_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c\big)+{N\big(a_{\lambda}N(b)\big)}_{\lambda+\mu}c

\displaystyle+\big(a_{\lambda}N(b)\big)_{\lambda+\mu}N(c)-N\big((a_{\lambda}N(b))_{\lambda+\mu}c\big)-{N^{2}(a_{\lambda}b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-{N(a_{\lambda}b)}_{\lambda+\mu}N(c)

\displaystyle+N\big({N(a_{\lambda}b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c\big)-{N(a)}_{\lambda}\big({N(b)}_{\mu}c\big)-a_{\lambda}N\big({N(b)}_{\mu}c\big)+N\big(a_{\lambda}({N(b)}_{\mu}c)\big)

\displaystyle-{N(a)}_{\lambda}\big(b_{\mu}N(c)\big)-a_{\lambda}N\big(b_{\mu}N(c)\big)+N\big(a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}N(c))\big)+{N(a)}_{\lambda}N(b_{\mu}c)

\displaystyle+a_{\lambda}N^{2}(b_{\mu}c)-N\big(a_{\lambda}N(b_{\mu}c)\big)-{N\big({N(b)}_{\mu}a\big)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-\big({{N(b)}_{\mu}a\big)}_{\lambda+\mu}N(c)

\displaystyle+N\big(({N(b)}_{\mu}a)_{\lambda+\mu}c\big)-{N\big(b_{\mu}N(a)\big)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-{(b_{\mu}N(a))}_{\lambda+\mu}N(c)+N\big({(b_{\mu}N(a))}_{\lambda+\mu}c\big)

\displaystyle+{N^{2}(b_{\mu}a)}_{\lambda+\mu}c+{N(b_{\mu}a)}_{\lambda+\mu}N(c)-N\big({N(b_{\mu}a)}_{\lambda+\mu}c\big)+{N(b)}_{\mu}\big({N(a)}_{\lambda}c\big)

\displaystyle+b_{\mu}N\big({N(a)}_{\lambda}c\big)-N\big(b_{\mu}({N(a)}_{\lambda}c)\big)+{N(b)}_{\mu}\big(a_{\lambda}N(c)\big)+b_{\mu}N\big(a_{\lambda}N(c)\big)

\displaystyle-N\big(b_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}N(c))\big)-{N(b)}_{\mu}\big(N(a_{\lambda}c)\big)+b_{\mu}N^{2}(a_{\lambda}c)-N\big(b_{\mu}(N(a_{\lambda}c))\big).

By the definitions of left-symmetric conformal algebra and Nijenhuis operator on
A
, the above equation is
0
. So
\omega
generates a linear deformation of
A
.

We define
T_{t}={\rm id}+tN
. It is straightforward to deduce that
T_{t}
is a left-symmetric conformal algebra homomorphism from
(A,\cdot^{\omega})
to
(A,\cdot)
. Thus, the linear deformation generated by

is trivial.
∎

By (
8
), (
13
) and Theorem
4.5
, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
N
be a Nijenhuis operator on a left-symmetric conformal algebra
(A,\cdot)
, then
(A,\cdot^{N})
is a left-symmetric conformal algebra, and
N
is a homomorphism from
(A,\cdot^{N})
to
(A,\cdot)
.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
R
be a Lie conformal algebra. A
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
N:R\rightarrow R
is called a
Nijenhuis operator
on

if it satisfies

(14)

\displaystyle[N(a)_{\lambda}N(b)]=N([a_{\lambda}b]_{N}),\qquad\forall\ a,b\in R,

where the

-bracket

is defined by

(15)

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]_{N}=[{N(a)}_{\lambda}b]+[a_{\lambda}N(b)]-N([a_{\lambda}b]).

Definition 4.8
.

Let
R
be a Lie conformal algebra and
\omega\in C^{2}(R,R)
. If

[a_{\lambda}b]^{\omega}=[a_{\lambda}b]+t\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)

defines a new Lie conformal algebra structure on

, we say that

generates
a linear deformation
of the Lie conformal algebra

.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra. If
\omega\in C^{2}(A,A)
generates a linear deformation of

, then
\tilde{\omega}\in C^{2}(g(A),A)

defined by

{\tilde{\omega}}_{\lambda}(a,b)=\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)-\omega_{-\partial-\lambda}(b,a)

.

If
\omega
generates a linear deformation of

, then
(A,\cdot^{\omega})
is a left-symmetric conformal algebra. Considering the sub-adjacent Lie conformal algebra of

, we have

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]^{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot^{\omega}_{\lambda}b-b\cdot^{\omega}_{-\partial-\lambda}a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}b+t\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)-b_{-\partial-\lambda}a-t\omega_{-\partial-\lambda}(b,a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]+t{\tilde{\omega}}_{\lambda}(a,b).

Thus,

Proposition 4.10
.

If

is a Nijenhuis operator on a left-symmetric conformal algebra

is also a Nijenhuis operator on its sub-adjacent Lie conformal algebra

For all

\displaystyle[N(a)_{\lambda}N(b)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(a)_{\lambda}N(b)-N(b)_{-\partial-\lambda}N(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\big(N(a)_{\lambda}b+a_{\lambda}N(b)-N(a_{\lambda}b)-N(b)_{-\partial-\lambda}a+b_{-\partial-\lambda}N(a)+N(b_{-\partial-\lambda}a)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\big([N(a)_{\lambda}b]+[a_{\lambda}N(b)]-N([a_{\lambda}b])\big).

Thus,

5.
Formal deformations of a left-symmetric conformal algebra

In this section, we study the formal deformation theory of a left-symmetric conformal algebra and show that the cohomology defined in Section 3 controls formal deformations of the left-symmetric conformal algebra. Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra and
\theta\in C^{2}(A,A)
be
\theta_{\lambda}(a,b)=a_{\lambda}b
, for
a,b\in A
. Consider the space
A[t]
of formal power series in
t
with coefficients in
A
.

Definition 5.1
.

A
formal
1
-parameter deformation
of
A
is a product
\theta_{t}
on
A[t]

\theta_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\theta_{i}t^{i},\qquad\quad{\rm where}\ \theta_{i}\in C^{2}(A,A)\ {\rm with}\ \theta_{0}=\theta

such that the map
\theta_{t}
defines a left-symmetric conformal algebra structure on
A[t]
.

According the above definition, for
a,b,c\in A
,
\theta_{t}
is a formal
1
-parameter deformation of
A
if and only if

{\theta_{t}}_{\lambda}(a,{\theta_{t}}_{\mu}(b,c))-{\theta_{t}}_{\lambda+\mu}({\theta_{t}}_{\lambda}(a,b),c)={\theta_{t}}_{\mu}(b,{\theta_{t}}_{\lambda}(a,c))-{\theta_{t}}_{\lambda+\mu}({\theta_{t}}_{\mu}(b,a),c),

and if and only if

(16)

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{i+j=n}\big({\theta_{i}}_{\lambda+\mu}({\theta_{j}}_{\lambda}(a,b),c)-{\theta_{i}}_{\lambda}(a,{\theta_{j}}_{\mu}(b,c))\big)=\sum_{i+j=n}\big({\theta_{i}}_{\lambda+\mu}({\theta_{j}}_{\mu}(b,a),c)-{\theta_{i}}_{\mu}(b,{\theta_{j}}_{\lambda}(a,c))\big).

Definition 5.2
.

A
formal
1
-parameter deformation
of a Lie conformal algebra
R
is a product
\theta_{t}
on
R[t]

\theta_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\theta_{i}t^{i},\qquad\quad{\rm where}\ \theta_{i}\in C^{2}_{Lie}(R,R)\ {\rm with}\ \theta_{0}=[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]

such that the map
\theta_{t}
defines a Lie conformal algebra structure on
R[t]
.

Similarly, for
a,b,c\in R
,
\theta_{t}
is a formal
1
-parameter deformation of
R
if and only if

{\theta_{t}}_{\lambda}(a,{\theta_{t}}_{\mu}(b,c))={\theta_{t}}_{\lambda+\mu}({\theta_{t}}_{\lambda}(a,b),c)+{\theta_{t}}_{\mu}(b,{\theta_{t}}_{\lambda}(a,c)),

and if and only if

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{i+j=n}{\theta_{i}}_{\lambda}(a,{\theta_{j}}_{\mu}(b,c))=\sum_{i+j=n}\big({\theta_{i}}_{\lambda+\mu}({\theta_{j}}_{\lambda}(a,b),c)+{\theta_{i}}_{\mu}(b,{\theta_{j}}_{\lambda}(a,c))\big).

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra and
\theta_{t}
a formal
1
-parameter deformation of
A
. Then
{\Theta}_{t}
defined by

{{\Theta}_{t}}_{\lambda}(a,b)={\theta_{t}}_{\lambda}(a,b)-{\theta_{t}}_{-\partial-\lambda}(b,a),\qquad\forall\ a,b\in A,

is a formal
1
-parameter deformation of its sub-adjacent Lie conformal algebra
g(A)
.

Proof.

It can be obtained by direct computations.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

Two formal
1
-parameter deformations
\theta_{t}
and
\theta^{\prime}_{t}
of a left-symmetric conformal algebra
A
are said to be
equivalent
if there is a formal isomorphism

\phi_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\phi_{i}t^{i}:A[t]\rightarrow A[t],\qquad{\rm where}\ \phi_{i}\in{{\rm CHom}}(A,A)\ {\rm with}\ \phi_{0}=\rm id_{A}

such that
\phi_{t}
is a morphism of left-symmetric conformal algebras from
(A[t],\theta^{\prime}_{t})
to
(A[t],\theta_{t})
.

According the above definition,
\theta_{t}
and
\theta^{\prime}_{t}
are equivalent if and only if

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}{\phi_{i}}({\theta^{\prime}_{j}}_{\lambda}(a,b))=\sum_{i+j+k=n}{\theta_{i}}_{\lambda}(\phi_{j}(a),\phi_{k}(b)).

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\theta_{t}
be a formal
1
-parameter deformation of the left-symmetric conformal algebra
A
. Then
\theta_{1}
is a
2
-cocycle with coefficients in the adjoint representation. Furthermore, the corresponding cohomology class in
H^{2}(A,A)
depends on the equivalence class of the deformation
\theta_{t}
.

Proof.

Since
\theta_{t}
is a formal
1
-parameter deformation, for
a,b,c\in A
, from equation (
16
) for
n=1
, we have

\displaystyle\quad{\theta_{1}}_{\lambda+\mu}(a_{\lambda}b,c)-{\theta_{1}}_{\lambda}(a,b_{\mu}c)+{({\theta_{1}}_{\lambda}(a,b))}_{\lambda+\mu}c-a_{\lambda}({\theta_{1}}_{\mu}(b,c))

(18)

\displaystyle={\theta_{1}}_{\lambda+\mu}(b_{\mu}a,c)-{\theta_{1}}_{\mu}(b,a_{\lambda}c)+{({\theta_{1}}_{\mu}(b,a))}_{\lambda+\mu}c-b_{\mu}({\theta_{1}}_{\lambda}(a,c)),

for any
a,b,c\in A
. The equation (

) is equivalent to
\delta\theta_{1}=0
.

Suppose that
\theta_{t}
and
\theta^{\prime}_{t}
are two equivalent deformations. For

, from (

), we have

(\theta^{\prime}_{1}-\theta_{1})_{\lambda}(a,b)=\phi_{1}(a)_{\lambda}b+a_{\lambda}\phi_{1}(b)-\phi_{1}(a_{\lambda}b),\qquad\forall\ a,b\in A.

Hence,

, which implies
\theta^{\prime}_{1}
and

are in the same cohomology class in

.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra. If

then any formal

-parameter deformation of

is equivalent to the trivial deformation, i.e.
\theta^{\prime}_{t}=\theta
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\theta_{t}
is a formal
1
-parameter deformation of
A
, then
\theta_{1}
is a

-cocycle from Theorem

. Since

Let

is equivalent to
\theta_{t}
. Since

, we can get that the linear terms of

vanish. By repeat this argument, we get that
\theta_{t}
is equivalent to
\theta^{\prime}_{t}=\theta
.
∎

6.

-extensions of a left-symmetric conformal algebra

In this section, we study the

-extensions of a left-symmetric conformal algebra and then give sufficient and necessary conditions for two
T^{*}
-extensions equivalent and isometrically respectively.

Definition 6.1
.

A
conformal bilinear form
on a left-symmetric conformal algebra
A
is a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear map
B_{\lambda}:A\times A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
satisfying

B_{\lambda}(\partial a,b)=-\lambda B_{\lambda}(a,b),\qquad B_{\lambda}(a,\partial b)=\lambda B_{\lambda}(a,b),\qquad\forall a,b\in A.

A conformal bilinear form
B
is called
symmetric
if it satisfies

B_{\lambda}(a,b)=B_{-\lambda}(b,a),

invariant
if it satisfies

B_{\lambda+\mu}(a_{\lambda}b,c)-B_{\lambda}(a,b_{\mu}c)=B_{\lambda+\mu}(b_{\mu}a,c)-B_{\mu}(b,a_{\lambda}c),

non-degenerate
if the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{*c}
given by
\varphi(a)_{\lambda}(b)=B_{\lambda}(a,b)
, is an isomorphism.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra. If
A
admits a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
B
, then we call
A

a quadratic left-symmetric conformal algebra
.

Let
A,A^{\prime}
be two quadratic left-symmetric conformal algebras and
B,B^{\prime}
be the corresponding bilinear forms respectively. Two quadratic left-symmetric conformal algebras
A,A^{\prime}
are said to be
isometric
if there exists a left-symmetric conformal algebra isomorphism
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
such that
\varphi(B_{\lambda}(a,b))={B}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\varphi(a),\varphi(b)),\ \forall\ a,b\in A
.

Recall that
(A^{*c},L^{*}_{A}-R^{*}_{A},-R^{*}_{A})
is the coadjoint module of a left-symmetric conformal algebra
A
. Then we have

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra and
\omega_{\lambda}:A\times A\rightarrow A^{*c}
be a bilinear map. Define the
\lambda
-product on the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
A\oplus A^{*c}
as follows:

(19)

\displaystyle(a+f)\cdot^{\omega}_{\lambda}(b+g)=a_{\lambda}b+\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)+{(L^{*}_{A}-R^{*}_{A})(a)}_{\lambda}(g)-{R^{*}_{A}(b)}_{-\partial-\lambda}(f),

for any
a,b\in A,f,g\in A^{*c}
. Then
(A\oplus A^{*c},\cdot^{\omega})
is a left-symmetric conformal algebra with the above
\lambda
-product if and only if
\omega_{\lambda}:A\times A\rightarrow A^{*c}
is a
2
-cocycle with coefficients in the coadjoint module.

Proof.

Since
(A^{*c},L^{*}_{A}-R^{*}_{A},-R^{*}_{A})
is the coadjoint module of a left-symmetric conformal algebra
A
, then we can get the semi-direct sum
A\ltimes_{L^{*}_{A}-R^{*}_{A},-R^{*}_{A}}A^{*c}
. The
\lambda
-product on
A\ltimes_{L^{*}_{A}-R^{*}_{A},-R^{*}_{A}}A^{*c}
is defined by

(a+f)_{\lambda}(b+g)=a_{\lambda}b+{(L^{*}_{A}-R^{*}_{A})(a)}_{\lambda}(g)-{R^{*}_{A}(b)}_{-\partial-\lambda}(f),\qquad\forall\ a,b\in A,f,g\in A^{*c}.

By direct computations,
A\oplus A^{*c}
is a left-symmetric conformal algebra with
\lambda
-product (
19
) if and only if

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(a_{\lambda}b,c)-R^{*}_{A}(c)_{-\partial-\lambda-\mu}\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)-\omega_{\lambda}(a,b_{\mu}c)-{(L^{*}_{A}-R^{*}_{A})(a)}_{\lambda}\big(\omega_{\mu}(b,c)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(b_{\mu}a,c)-R^{*}_{A}(c)_{-\partial-\lambda-\mu}\omega_{\mu}(b,a)-\omega_{\mu}(b,a_{\lambda}c)-{(L^{*}_{A}-R^{*}_{A})(b)}_{\mu}\big(\omega_{\lambda}(a,c)\big)

holds, and if and only if
\omega
is a
2
-cocycle with coefficients in the coadjoint module.
∎

In particular, we also have

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra and
\omega_{\lambda}:A\times A\rightarrow A^{*c}
be a bilinear map. Define the
\lambda
-product on the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
A\oplus A^{*c}
as follows:

\displaystyle(a+f)\cdot^{\omega}_{\lambda}(b+g)=a_{\lambda}b+\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)+{L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}(g),

for any
a,b\in A,f,g\in A^{*c}
. Then
(A\oplus A^{*c},\cdot^{\omega})
is a left-symmetric conformal algebra with the above
\lambda
-product if and only if
\omega_{\lambda}:A\times A\rightarrow A^{*c}
is a
2
-cocycle with coefficients in the module
A^{*c}_{L}=(A^{*c},L^{*}_{A},0)
.

Clearly,
A^{*c}
is an ideal of left-symmetric conformal algebra
A\oplus A^{*c}
, and
A
is isomorphic to the factor left-symmetric conformal algebra
(A\oplus A^{*c})/A^{*c}
. Moreover, consider the following symmetric bilinear form
B
on
A\oplus A^{*c}
for all
a+f,b+g\in A\oplus A^{*c}
:

B_{\lambda}(a+f,b+g)=f_{\lambda}(b)+g_{-\lambda}(a).

Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
A,A^{*c},\omega
be in Proposition
6.4
and
B
be as above. Then the left-symmetric conformal algebra in
(A\oplus A^{*c},\cdot^{\omega})
defined in Proposition
6.4
is a quadratic left-symmetric conformal algebra
if and only if
\omega
is invariant.

Proof.

First, it is obvious that the bilinear form
B
on
A\oplus A^{*c}
is symmetric. Assume
a+f\in A\oplus A^{*c}
satisfying
B_{\lambda}(a+f,b+g)=0
for any
b+g\in A\oplus A^{*c}
. Then
f_{\lambda}(b)=0
and
g_{-\lambda}(a)=0
for any
b\in A,g\in A^{*c}
, that is
a=0,f=0
. So the bilinear form is non-degenerate.

Now suppose that
a+f,b+g,c+h\in A\oplus A^{*c}
, we have

\displaystyle B_{\lambda+\mu}\big((a+f)\cdot^{\omega}_{\lambda}(b+g),c+h\big)-B_{\lambda}\big(a+f,(b+g)\cdot^{\omega}_{\mu}(c+h)\big)

\displaystyle-B_{\lambda+\mu}\big((b+g)\cdot^{\omega}_{\mu}(a+f),c+h\big)+B_{\mu}\big(b+g,(a+f)\cdot^{\omega}_{\lambda}(c+h)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{\lambda+\mu}\big(a_{\lambda}b+\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)+{L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}g,c+h\big)-B_{\lambda}\big(a+f,b_{\mu}c+\omega_{\mu}(b,c)+{L^{*}_{A}(b)}_{\mu}h\big)

\displaystyle-B_{\lambda+\mu}\big(b_{\mu}a+\omega_{\mu}(b,a)+{L^{*}_{A}(b)}_{\mu}f,c+h\big)+B_{\mu}\big(b+g,a_{\lambda}c+\omega_{\lambda}(a,c)+{L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}h\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c+{({L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}g)}_{\lambda+\mu}c+h_{-\lambda-\mu}(a_{\lambda}b)-f_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)-{\omega_{\mu}(b,c)}_{-\lambda}a-({L^{*}_{A}(b)}_{\mu}h)_{-\lambda}a

\displaystyle-{\omega_{\mu}(b,a)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-({L^{*}_{A}(b)}_{\mu}f)_{\lambda+\mu}c-h_{-\lambda-\mu}(b_{\mu}a)+g_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c)+{\omega_{\lambda}(a,c)}_{-\mu}b+({L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}h)_{-\mu}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-g_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c)+h_{-\lambda-\mu}(a_{\lambda}b)-f_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)-{\omega_{\mu}(b,c)}_{-\lambda}a+h_{-\lambda-\mu}(b_{\mu}a)

\displaystyle-{\omega_{\mu}(b,a)}_{\lambda+\mu}c+f_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)-h_{-\lambda-\mu}(b_{\mu}a)+g_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c)+{\omega_{\lambda}(a,c)}_{-\mu}b-h_{-\lambda-\mu}(a_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-{\omega_{\mu}(b,c)}_{-\lambda}a-{\omega_{\mu}(b,a)}_{\lambda+\mu}c+{\omega_{\lambda}(a,c)}_{-\mu}b.

Then the bilinear form
B
on
A\oplus A^{*c}
is invariant if and only if
\omega
is invariant.
The lemma holds.
∎

Now, if
\omega_{\lambda}:A\times A\rightarrow A^{*c}
is a invariant
2
-cocycle, we can get a quadratic left-symmetric conformal algebra
(A\oplus A^{*c},\cdot^{\omega})
which is called a
T^{*}
-extension
of
A
. Denote it by
T^{*}_{\omega}A
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra and
\omega^{1}_{\lambda},\omega^{2}_{\lambda}:A\times A\rightarrow A^{*c}
be two invariant
2
-cocycles. The corresponding
T^{*}
-extensions
T^{*}_{\omega^{1}}A
and
T^{*}_{\omega^{2}}A
are said to be
equivalent
if there exists a left-symmetric conformal algebra isomorphism
\Phi:T^{*}_{\omega^{1}}A\rightarrow T^{*}_{\omega^{2}}A
which is the identity on the ideal
A^{*c}
, and which induces the identity on the factor left-symmetric conformal algebra
(T^{*}_{\omega^{1}}A)/A^{*c}\cong A\cong(T^{*}_{\omega^{2}}A)/A^{*c}
. The two
T^{*}
-extensions
T^{*}_{\omega^{1}}A
and
T^{*}_{\omega^{2}}A
of
A
are said
to be
isometrically equivalent
if they are equivalent and
\Phi
is an isometry.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
A
be a left-symmetric conformal algebra and
\omega^{1}_{\lambda},\omega^{2}_{\lambda}:A\times A\rightarrow A^{*c}
be two invariant
2
-cocycles. Then we have

(i)

T^{*}_{\omega^{1}}A
is equivalent to
T^{*}_{\omega^{2}}A
if and only if there exists
\theta\in C^{1}(A,A^{*c})
such that

(20)

\displaystyle\omega^{1}_{\lambda}(a,b)-\omega^{2}_{\lambda}(a,b)={L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}\theta(b)-\theta(a_{\lambda}b),\qquad\quad\forall\ a,b\in A.

In this case, if we define
\beta_{\lambda}(a,b):=\frac{1}{2}({\theta(a)}_{\lambda}b+{\theta(b)}_{-\lambda}a)
, then
\beta
induces a symmetric invariant bilinear form on
A
. Furthermore,
\omega^{1}=\omega^{2}
if and only if
\theta
is a
1
-cocycle with coefficients in the module
A^{*c}_{L}=(A^{*c},L^{*}_{A},0)
.

(ii)

T^{*}_{\omega^{1}}A
is isometrically equivalent to
T^{*}_{\omega^{2}}A
if and only if there exists
\theta\in C^{1}(A,A^{*c})
such that (
20
) holds and the bilinear form
\beta
induced by
\theta
on
A
vanishes.

Proof.

(i)

T^{*}_{\omega^{1}}A
is equivalent to
T^{*}_{\omega^{2}}A
if and only if there exists a left-symmetric conformal algebra isomorphism
\Phi:T^{*}_{\omega^{1}}A\rightarrow T^{*}_{\omega^{2}}A
satisfying
\Phi|_{A^{*c}}={\rm id}_{A^{*c}}
and
a-\Phi(a)\in A^{*c},\ \forall\ a\in A.

If
\Phi
satisfies the above conditions, we define
\theta:A\rightarrow A^{*c}
by
\theta(a)=a-\Phi(a).
Then
\theta\in C^{1}(A,A^{*c})
and for any
a+f,b+g\in A\oplus A^{*c}
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi((a+f)\cdot^{\omega^{1}}_{\lambda}(b+g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(a_{\lambda}b+{\omega^{1}}_{\lambda}(a,b)+{L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}b+\theta(a_{\lambda}b)+{\omega^{1}}_{\lambda}(a,b)+{L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}(g),

\displaystyle\Phi(a+f)\cdot^{\omega^{2}}_{\lambda}\Phi(b+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a+\theta(a)+f)\cdot^{\omega^{2}}_{\lambda}(b+\theta(b)+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}b+\omega^{2}_{\lambda}(a,b)+{L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}\theta(b)+{L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}(g),

then (
20
) holds. It is obvious that
\omega^{1}=\omega^{2}
if and only if
\theta
is a
1
-cocycle.

Conversely, if there exists
\theta\in C^{1}(A,A^{*c})
such that (
20
), define
\Phi(a+f)=a+\theta(a)+f,\ \forall\ a+f\in A\oplus A^{*c}
. It is easy to prove that
\Phi:T^{*}_{\omega^{1}}A\rightarrow T^{*}_{\omega^{2}}A
satisfying
\Phi|_{A^{*c}}={\rm id}_{A^{*c}}
and
a-\Phi(a)\in A^{*c},\ \forall\ a\in A
, is a left-symmetric conformal algebra isomorphism.

Consider the bilinear form
\beta
induced by
\theta
. Note that for any
a,b,c\in A,
we have

(21)

\displaystyle{\omega^{1}_{\lambda}(a,b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-{\omega^{2}_{\lambda}(a,b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{({L^{*}_{A}(a)}_{\lambda}\theta(b))}_{\lambda+\mu}c-{\theta(a_{\lambda}b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\theta(b)}_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c)-{\theta(a_{\lambda}b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c

and
\omega^{1},\omega^{2}
are invariant, i.e.

(22)

\displaystyle{\omega^{1}_{\lambda}(a,b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-{\omega^{1}_{\mu}(b,c)}_{-\lambda}a={\omega^{1}_{\mu}(b,a)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-{\omega^{1}_{\lambda}(a,c)}_{-\mu}b,

(23)

\displaystyle{\omega^{2}_{\lambda}(a,b)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-{\omega^{2}_{\mu}(b,c)}_{-\lambda}a={\omega^{2}_{\mu}(b,a)}_{\lambda+\mu}c-{\omega^{2}_{\lambda}(a,c)}_{-\mu}b.

Combine (
21
), (
22
) and (
23
), we can get

\beta_{\lambda+\mu}(a_{\lambda}b,c)-\beta_{\lambda}(a,b_{\mu}c)=\beta_{\lambda+\mu}(b_{\mu}a,c)-\beta_{\mu}(b,a_{\lambda}c).

Thus
\beta
is a symmetric invariant bilinear form on
A
.

(ii)
Let the isomorphism
\Phi
be defined as
(i)
. Then for any
a+f,b+g\in A\oplus A^{*c}
, we have

\displaystyle B_{\lambda}(\Phi(a+f),\Phi(b+g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{\lambda}(a+\theta(a)+f,b+\theta(b)+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\theta(a)}_{\lambda}(b)+f_{\lambda}(b)+{\theta(b)}_{-\lambda}(a)+g_{-\lambda}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\theta(a)}_{\lambda}(b)+{\theta(b)}_{-\lambda}(a)+B_{\lambda}(a+f,b+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\beta_{\lambda}(a,b)+B_{\lambda}(a+f,b+g).

Thus,
\Phi
is an isometry if and only if
\beta=0
.
∎

Properties of chiral nucleon-nucleon interaction at N 3 LO with high cutoffs studied by local projection
H.Y. Shang, R.Z. Hu, J.C. Pei, F.R. Xu

Abstract
The chiral nucleon-nucleon ( N ​ N \displaystyle NN ) interaction at high cutoffs has been plagued by the presence of spurious bound states. In this work, the chiral N ​ N \displaystyle NN interaction at N 3 LO is studied by the local projection method as the cutoff increases. The evolution of short-range behaviors of pion-exchange interactions and contact interactions is intuitively demonstrated. The P \displaystyle P -channel potentials toward high cutoffs appear to be erratic at short ranges to compromise with phase shifts, while such erratic behaviors can be avoided in S \displaystyle S and D \displaystyle D channels. Furthermore, a chiral N ​ N \displaystyle NN interaction at N 3 LO is studied at a cutoff of 700 MeV. The properties of deuteron and triton are testified with this interaction. Such a hard interaction is expected to provide an alternative choice for studies of short-range correlations and high density nuclear matter.

The three-nucleon forces appear at N

The applications of

and nuclear matter

;
Drischler et al. 2019

;

.

The nuclear force based on
\displaystyle\chi
EFT has been conventionally written in terms of (
\displaystyle Q/\Lambda_{\chi}
)
v
, where
\displaystyle Q
denotes the momentum
and
\displaystyle\Lambda_{\chi}

In addition, an explicit cutoff
\displaystyle\Lambda

Currently the high quality chiral nuclear interaction up to N

The chiral nuclear interactions usually adopt a finite cutoff around 500 MeV and the

;

;

;
Entem et al. 2017

In the framework of Weinberg power counting, the chiral interactions are known to have regulator dependence

;
Reinert et al. 2018
.

In Weinberg’s power counting, the
potential is obtained by summing irreducible diagrams
in orders of
\displaystyle Q/\Lambda_{\chi}
, followed by a non-perturbative resummation in the Lippmann-Schwinger equation to infinite
order to yield the amplitude
Nogga et al. 2005
. This can be a problem for renormalization of chiral interactions

;
Entem et al. 2008
.
With a high cutoff, the limit-cycle behaviors of parameters

and unphysical deep bound states can emerge in some partial waves,
which are related to the presence of attractive singular potentials.
Such behaviors have been studied at LO and N
2
LO before
Nogga et al. 2005
;
Epelbaum et al. 2004a
.
However, the emergence of spurious states at high cutoffs at N
3
LO have not been studied comprehensively yet.

The chiral interaction is naturally developed in momentum space. The local projection method
provides a direct visualization of the range-dependent features of the non-local momentum interactions.
This has been successfully applied in studying the softening behavior of nuclear interactions in similarity renormalization group evolution
Wendt et al. 2012
.
This method is expected to be useful to study the cutoff dependence of chiral interactions,
since it is too complicated to study the limit-cycle behaviors and deep bound states at N
3
LO analytically.
For nuclear interactions, not only the phase shift but also the
behaviors of nuclear interactions in coordinate space should be considered.

It is always curious to explore realistic nuclear forces at high cutoffs although soft interactions are favorable in many-body calculations.
Actually
\displaystyle NN
scattering data in the elastic channel is available up to 3 GeV
Arndt et al. 2007
.
The
\displaystyle\Delta
degree of freedom would play a role at high cutoffs but the role of full chiral interactions with
\displaystyle\Delta

is not conclusive in actual calculations

;
Nosyk et al. 2021
;
Machleidt and Entem 2011
.
The role of three-nucleon forces is different in hard
\displaystyle NN
interactions

.
The high energy electron scattering measurement indicates that

can better describe short-range correlations
Arrington et al. 2022
;
Schmidt et al. 2020
.
In addition, it would be more realistic to describe nuclear matter at higher densities using nuclear interactions with high cutoffs.

In this work, we study the behaviors of chiral nuclear interactions at N
3
LO using the local
projection method while the cutoff varies. Thus the emergence of spurious deep bound states can be intuitively
demonstrated, which is difficult to be studied analytically at N
3
LO.
The optimization of chiral
\displaystyle NN
interactions at N
3
LO is also performed with a high cutoff of 700 MeV.
The short range features of this interaction are also studied with local projection.
The properties of deuteron and triton are calculated with this hard interaction.

II
Methods

II.1
The chiral NN potential

Generally the nuclear potential is the sum of contact terms
\displaystyle V_{\rm ct}
and
pion-exchange terms
\displaystyle V_{\rm pe}

Machleidt and Entem 2011
:

V\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime},\vec{p}\right)=V_{\rm ct}\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime},\vec{p}\right)+V_{\rm pe}\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime},\vec{p}\right),

(1)

where
\displaystyle{\vec{p}}~^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle\vec{p}
denote the final and initial nucleon momenta in
the center-of-mass (CMS) system.

In Weinberg’s power counting,
the potential is expanded in terms of powers of small external
momenta over a large scale as
\displaystyle(Q/\Lambda_{\chi})^{\nu}
. The pion-exchange potential is the sum of various pion-exchange
contributions.
The
leading order is abbreviated as LO, and next-to leading order as NLO, etc.
The contact potential is the sum of even polynomials of the momentum as:

\displaystyle V_{\rm ct}=

\displaystyle C_{S}+C_{1}q^{2}+C_{2}k^{2}+D_{1}q^{4}+D_{2}k^{4}

(2)

\displaystyle+D_{3}q^{2}k^{2}+D_{4}\left(\vec{q}\times\vec{k}\right)^{2}+\cdots,

where the momentum transfer is denoted by
\displaystyle\vec{q}\equiv{\vec{p}}~^{\prime}-\vec{p}
and
the average momentum by
\displaystyle\vec{k}\equiv\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime}+\vec{p}\right)/2
.
In coordinate spaces, the contact potential is associated with delta function and derivatives of delta function.

The specific form of the pion-exchange
component is given in Ref.
Entem et al. 2015
;
Entem et al. 2017
. For parameters of the axial-vector coupling constant

\displaystyle g_{A}
, pion-decay constant
\displaystyle f_{\pi}
, pion masses, nucleon masses and

\displaystyle\pi N
low-energy constants (LECs), we adopt the values in Table I and II of
Ref.
Entem et al. 2017
. To study the contributions of
different types of diagrams, the two-pion exchange (2PE) terms within
\displaystyle\mathrm{N^{3}LO}
(
\displaystyle V_{2\pi}^{(4)}
)
are further decomposed
Entem et al. 2015
to facilitate discussions. To be specific, the
\displaystyle 2\pi
contribution is decomposed into
one-loop and two-loop (tl) contributions. The one-loop contribution includes terms of

\displaystyle c_{i}^{2}
(football diagram or fd), corrections related to
\displaystyle c_{i}/M_{N}
(cm), and leading relativistic corrections (rc), as given in Appendix A.
The contact terms under the partial wave representation are
summerized in Appendix B. The specific charge dependency
will be discussed in subsequent sections. The chiral
\displaystyle NN
interaction can be expressed as

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{LO}}

\displaystyle=V_{1\pi}+V_{\mathrm{ct}}^{(0)},

(3)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{NLO}}

\displaystyle=V_{\mathrm{LO}}+V_{2\pi}^{(2)}+V_{\mathrm{ct}}^{(2)},

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{N^{2}LO}}

\displaystyle=V_{\mathrm{NLO}}+V_{2\pi}^{(3)},

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{N}^{3}\mathrm{LO}}

\displaystyle=V_{\mathrm{N^{2}LO}}+V_{\mathrm{ct}}^{(4)}+V_{2\pi,\mathrm{tl}}

\displaystyle+V_{2\pi,\mathrm{rc}}+V_{2\pi,\mathrm{fd}}+V_{2\pi,\rm cm}.

II.2
The NN scattering

In principle, we can
substitute the potential
\displaystyle V
into the Blankenbeclar-Sugar (BbS) equation to obtain the
scattering matrix
\displaystyle T
, as given in Ref.
Blankenbecler and Sugar 1966
.

The Bbs equation can be transformed by defining

\widehat{V}\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime},\vec{p}\right)\equiv\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{M_{N}}{E_{p^{\prime}}}}V\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime},\vec{p}\right)\sqrt{\frac{M_{N}}{E_{p}}}

(4)

and

\widehat{T}\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime},\vec{p}\right)\equiv\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{M_{N}}{E_{p^{\prime}}}}T\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime},\vec{p}\right)\sqrt{\frac{M_{N}}{E_{p}}},

(5)

where
\displaystyle M_{N}
is nucleon mass and
\displaystyle E_{p^{\prime}}
is nucleon energy
\displaystyle\sqrt{M_{N}^{2}+p^{\prime 2}}
.
Actually we solve the non-relativistic Lippmann-Schwinger (LS) equation:

\displaystyle\widehat{T}\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime},\vec{p}\right)=

\displaystyle\widehat{V}\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime},\vec{p}\right)+\int d^{3}p^{\prime\prime}\widehat{V}\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime},{\vec{p}}~^{\prime\prime}\right)

(6)

\displaystyle\times\frac{M_{N}}{p^{2}-p^{\prime 2}+i\epsilon}\widehat{T}\left({\vec{p}}~^{\prime\prime},\vec{p}\right).

\displaystyle\widehat{V}
and
\displaystyle\widehat{T}
can be regarded as the non-relativistic potential and scattering matrix, respectively.
By parameterizing the on-shell (
\displaystyle p
=
\displaystyle p^{\prime}
)
\displaystyle T
-matrix elements, we can obtain the phase shifts. In this work,
\displaystyle\widehat{V}

and
\displaystyle\widehat{T}
are denoted as
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle T
.

When solving the LS equation, a cutoff on
\displaystyle V
at a high momenta has to be applied
to avoid infinities in the integral.
It is convenient to choose the regulator function as
Machleidt and Entem 2011

f\left(p^{\prime},p\right)=\exp\left[-\left(p^{\prime}/\Lambda\right)^{2n}-(p/\Lambda)^{2n}\right],

(7)

and it remains unaffected in the partial wave decomposition. For the pion-exchange component, we adopt
\displaystyle n=2
for 2PE and
\displaystyle n=4

for one-pion exchange (1PE). The choice of powers
\displaystyle n
in contact terms depends on the partial wave and the order.

The numerical methods in solving the LS equation can be found
in Ref.
Machleidt 1993
and the specific formulas for the calculation of phase
shifts are given in Ref.
Machleidt 2001
. Note that the Stapp convention is adopted in calculations of phase shifts
Machleidt 2001
.

II.3
Local projection

In partial wave basis, we denote the potential matrix elements
\displaystyle\mathrm{i}^{L-L^{\prime}}\left\langle p^{\prime}L^{\prime}SJM|V|pLSJM\right\rangle
by

\displaystyle V^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}\left(p^{\prime},p\right)
and
\displaystyle\left\langle r^{\prime}L^{\prime}SJM|V|rLSJM\right\rangle
by

\displaystyle V^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}\left(r^{\prime},r\right)
. The coordinate-space and momentum-space matrix elements are associated by the Fourier transform

V^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}\left(r^{\prime},r\right)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}p^{\prime 2}dp^{\prime}p^{2}dpj_{L^{\prime}}\left(p^{\prime}r^{\prime}\right)j_{L}(pr)V^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}(p^{\prime},p),

(8)

where
\displaystyle j_{L^{\prime}}
and
\displaystyle j_{L}
are spherical Bessel functions. If the matrix elements in coordinate space can be expressed by

V^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}\left(r^{\prime},r\right)=V^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}(r)\frac{\delta\left(r-r^{\prime}\right)}{r^{\prime 2}},

(9)

such a potential is a local potential. Otherwise the potential is nonlocal.

Up to N
2
LO, the pion-exchange terms obtained from calculations of Feynman diagrams are local. However, the relativistic corrections and the
\displaystyle c_{i}/M_{N}
terms appearing at N
3
LO, the chosen regulator,
and the
\displaystyle\vec{k}
-dependent contact terms, would introduce non-localities. These non-localities prohibit direct visualizations
and it is difficult to observe range-dependent features of the potential. In this work,
a local projection of the potential is applied,
which is described in Ref.
Wendt et al. 2012
.

The local projection can be straightforwardly obtained by integrating
out one of the coordinates
\displaystyle r^{\prime}
in the coordinate space potential,

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}\left[V\left({\vec{r}}~^{\prime},\vec{r}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\delta\left({\vec{r}}~^{\prime}-\vec{r}\right)\int d^{3}r^{\prime\prime}V\left({\vec{r}}~^{\prime\prime},\vec{r}\right)

(10)

\displaystyle\equiv\delta\left({\vec{r}}~^{\prime}-\vec{r}\right)\bar{V}(\vec{r}),

where
\displaystyle\mathrm{L}
denotes the local projection, and
\displaystyle\bar{V}
is the resulted local potential.
In the partial wave basis, the equation above is written as

\bar{V}^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\prime 2}dr^{\prime}V^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}\left(r^{\prime},r\right).

(11)

By substituting this expression into Eq. (
8
) and integrating over the free coordinates
analytically, we obtain

\bar{V}^{JS}_{L0}(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}k^{2}dkj_{L}(kr)V^{JS}_{L0}(k,0)

(12)

and (for
\displaystyle L,L^{\prime}>0
)

\bar{V}^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}(r)=N_{L^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dkdk^{\prime}\frac{k^{2}}{k^{\prime}}j_{L}(kr)V^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}(k^{\prime},k),

(13)

where
1
1

1

Compared to Ref.
Wendt et al. 2012
, a factor of
\displaystyle i^{L-L^{\prime}}

is absorbed in the definiation of matrix elements in the momentumm space.

N_{L^{\prime}}=\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{L^{\prime}+3}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{2}\right)}

(14)

and
\displaystyle\Gamma
denotes the gamma function.

This approach essentially averages the non-locality, retaining the main features of the potential.
By plotting
\displaystyle\bar{V}
as a function of
\displaystyle r
, we can provide a visual representation
of the potential. This allows us to study the variations and the counterbalance of contact terms and
pion-exchange contributions as the cutoff increases.

III.1
Potential with different cutoffs

A high cutoff in momentum space can be roughly interpreted as a small-radius cutoff in coordinate space. As elucidated in
Refs.
Beane et al. 2001
;
van Kolck 2020
, an attractive potential of the form

V_{\mathrm{L}}(r)=-\frac{\alpha}{2\mu r^{n}}

(15)

\displaystyle l(l+1)/\left(2\mu r^{2}\right)

\displaystyle n=2

\displaystyle\alpha
is
not very small and
\displaystyle n\geq 3

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{L}}(r)
dominates at small distances in low partial waves, leading to a ‘singular’ potential.
The potential in the coordinate space can be separated into a short-range part and a long-range part with a sharp cutoff
\displaystyle R
,

V(r)=V_{\mathrm{S}}(R)\theta(R-r)+V_{\mathrm{L}}(r)\theta(r-R).

(16)

One can ensure the low-energy observables by adjusting
\displaystyle V_{S}
when
\displaystyle R
is fixed.
As
\displaystyle R\to 0
(
\displaystyle\Lambda\to\infty
),
the parameter displays a limit-cycle behavior

and unphysical bound states appear.

For
\displaystyle NN
potentials, similar situation occurs in low partial waves. At LO, Ref.
Nogga et al. 2005
demonstrates that the tensor interaction of
1PE is ‘singular’ and spurious bound states occur in
{}^{3}P_{0}
,
{}^{3}S_{1}-{}^{3}D_{1}
and
{}^{3}D_{2}
channels. At N
2

{}^{3}P_{1}

Up to date, chiral nuclear forces are usually developed with a cutoff
\displaystyle\Lambda
around 500 MeV,
and the phase shifts up to 300 MeV are taken into the fitting procedure
Epelbaum et al. 2015
;
Reinert et al. 2018
;
Ekström et al. 2015
;
Ekström et al. 2013
;
Entem and Machleidt 2003
;
Entem et al. 2017
.
Recently there are increasing strong interests to probe the short range properties of realistic nuclear forces
Hen et al. 2017
.
For example, high energy electron scattering is a very useful tool to
study short range correlations and for the verification of the point-like nucleon models
Schmidt et al. 2020
.
The cutoff of conventional chiral nuclear forces is not sufficiently high to describe such measurements.
In addition,
ab initio
descriptions of dense nuclear matter suffers large
uncertainties above the saturation density
Drischler et al. 2019
. With more observations of gravitational waves
Abbott et al. 2017
;
Abbott et al. 2018
,
it is of strong interests to explore nuclear forces at a higher cutoff.
In this context, we are developing chiral
\displaystyle NN
forces at N
3
LO by adopting a cutoff at
\displaystyle\Lambda=700
MeV
and taking into account the phase shift data up to 500 MeV.

In this work, we only adjust the
\displaystyle NN
contact terms to fit the low partial wave phase shifts.
The scattering experimental phase shifts up to 500 MeV are included.
For
\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{lab}}
energy between 0 and 10 MeV, we adopt data from the Nijmegen multienergy
\displaystyle np
phase-shift analysis PWA93
Stoks et al. 1993
.
For
\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{lab}}
above 10 MeV, we adopt the phase
shift analysis of GWU SP07.
In the range where both sets of analysis are available, the data differences are very small.
We denote the cutoff in the phase-shift database as
\displaystyle T^{\mathrm{cut}}_{\mathrm{lab}}
.
The optimization is achieved by using the genetic algorithm
Charbonneau 2002
, which is changed into an OpenMP parallel scheme in this work.

Generally, in minimizing the chi-square (
\displaystyle\chi^{2}=(w\times(\delta_{\mathrm{N^{3}LO}}-\delta_{\mathrm{expt}}))^{2}

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{lab}}

the contribution of this part to the chi-square is multiplied by a small weight coefficient as

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{lab}}

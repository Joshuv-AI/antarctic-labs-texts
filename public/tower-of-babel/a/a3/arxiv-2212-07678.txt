The operator algebra of cyclic orbifolds
Benoit Estienne, Yacine Ikhlef, Andrei Rotaru

Abstract
We identify the maximal chiral algebra of conformal cyclic orbifolds. In terms of this extended algebra, the orbifold is a rational and diagonal conformal field theory, provided the mother theory itself is also rational and diagonal. The operator content and operator product expansion of the cyclic orbifolds are revisited in terms of this algebra. The fusion rules and fusion numbers are computed via the Verlinde formula. This allows one to predict which conformal blocks appear in a given four-point function of twisted or untwisted operators, which is relevant for the computation of various entanglement measures in one-dimensional critical systems.

2
Cyclic orbifolds

In this section, we provide some basic background on cyclic orbifolds. We shall first bring the reader to some intuition on how the cyclic orbifold arises in the study of entanglement in 1D systems, with a presentation of the replica trick, and show how orbifold correlators are related to physically relevant quantities such as Rényi entropies. By now, the computation of entanglement entropies in the context of conformal field theory is pretty standard, and for a more detailed exposition the reader can consult the reviews
[

,
8

30
]
. Following this, we define the basic features of the cyclic orbifold
\mathcal{M}_{N}
, such as the twist operators, the conserved currents, the symmetry algebra
{\rm OVir}_{N}
, and the induction procedure.

2.1
Motivation: Rényi entropies of quantum critical 1D systems

Let us first briefly recall how Rényi entropies are related to partition functions on replicated surfaces
[
1
,
2
]
, and in turn to correlation functions of twist operators of cyclic orbifold CFTs. While it is possible to deal with systems at finite temperature and/or finite volume, for simplicity we consider a quantum critical 1D system on the infinite line
\mathbb{R}
at zero temperature.
We introduce a bipartition of the system into two spatial subregions, namely the interval
A=[0,\ell]
and its complement
B=\mathbb{R}\backslash A
. The
N^{\textrm{th}}
Rényi entropy (relative to this bipartition) is defined through

S_{N}=\frac{1}{1-N}\log\mathrm{Tr}_{A}(\rho_{A}^{N})\,,\qquad\rho_{A}=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}_{B}(|\Psi_{0}\rangle\langle\Psi_{0}|)}{\langle\Psi_{0}|\Psi_{0}\rangle}\,,

(2.1)

where
|\Psi_{0}\rangle
denotes the ground-state of the whole system, and
\rho_{A}
is the reduced density matrix describing the state of the region
A
.

In the transfer-matrix (or Euclidean path integral) formalism, the quantum Hamiltonian for the physical 1D system is viewed as the anisotropic limit of the transfer matrix of a classical 2D statistical model, which we call
\mathcal{M}
. For any pair of states
(\alpha,\beta)
of the subsystem
A
, the matrix element
(\rho_{A})_{\alpha\beta}
is given by the ratio

(\rho_{A})_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{Z_{\alpha\beta}}{Z_{1}}\,,

(2.2)

where
Z_{1}
stands for the partition function of
\mathcal{M}
on the plane, and
Z_{\alpha\beta}
is the partition function on the plane with a cut along a segment of length
\ell
, and boundary conditions defined by
\alpha
and
\beta
on both sides of the cut: see Fig.
1
a. By construction,
\rho_{A}
has unit trace. If
N
is a positive integer, the quantity
\mathrm{Tr}_{A}(\rho_{A}^{N})
is obtained by stacking
N
copies of the plane, and connecting them cyclically along the cut, to form the closed replicated surface
\mathcal{R}_{N}
depicted in Fig.
1
.b. Thus, we have the relation

\mathrm{Tr}_{A}(\rho_{A}^{N})=\frac{Z_{N}}{Z_{1}^{N}}\,,

(2.3)

where
Z_{N}
is the partition function of the system on the replicated surface
\mathcal{R}_{N}
. This surface is a ramified covering of the plane, with branch points located at the extremities of the interval
A
. Geometrically, these branch points are conical singularities with an excess angle
2\pi(N-1)
. Conical singularities are
not
singularities as far as the smooth (and even complex) structure is concerned
1
1

1

in the sense that there exists a smooth (and in fact holomorphic) coordinate chart about any branch point.
. But they are singularities in the sense of Riemannian geometry : while the metric is flat everywhere else on
\mathcal{R}_{N}
, there is an infinite negative curvature (proportional to the excess angle) localized at each
branch points, in accordance with the Gauss-Bonnet theorem.

Instead of working with replicated surfaces, we can adopt a different perspective, and replicate the degrees of freedom. To establish this idea, one can discretize the theory
\mathcal{M}
and consider instead a 2D statistical model such as a classical spin model on the square lattice, with nearest-neighbour interactions. The partition function on the plane is

Z(\mathcal{M})=\sum_{\{s(i)\}}\prod_{\langle i,j\rangle}W[s(i),s(j)]\,,

(2.4)

where
s(i)
is the spin on site
i
,
\langle i,j\rangle
denotes a pair of neighbouring sites, and
W
is the Boltzmann weight which defines the local interaction.

For any positive integer
N
, we define the (lattice version of the) cyclic orbifold
\mathcal{M}_{N}
as follows. The model
\mathcal{M}_{N}
lives on the original surface, say the plane in the above example. At each lattice site
i
, we have
N
independent spin variables
(s_{1}(i),\dots,s_{N}(i))
, and the nearest-neighbour interaction is given by
\prod_{a=0}^{N-1}W[s_{a}(i),s_{a}(j)]
– in other words, the copies
a=0,\dots,N-1
are decoupled. The partition function thus reads

Z(\mathcal{M}_{N})=\sum_{\{s_{a}(i)\}}\prod_{\langle i,j\rangle}\prod_{a=1}^{N}W[s_{a}(i),s_{a}(j)]=Z(\mathcal{M})^{N}\,.

(2.5)

Local operators are
N
-tuples of
\mathcal{M}
-operators. Additionally, since the model has an internal
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry generated by cyclic permutations of the copies, we can have topological defects, which are oriented lines across which the local interaction is modified as
W[s_{a}(i),s_{a}(j)]\mapsto W[s_{a}(i),s_{a+1}(j)]
. We thus introduce the
twist operators

\sigma
and
\sigma^{\dagger}
, which insert the endpoints of a topological defect of this type. The construction of such defect operators is in fact pretty standard in statistical models enjoying a discrete or continuous symmetry – this includes for instance
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
disorder operators in the Ising model
[
42
]
, and “electric” defects in the six-vertex model
[
43
]
.

Via the above construction, correlation functions of twist operators on a surface
\Sigma
exactly correspond to partition functions of
\mathcal{M}
on ramified coverings of
\Sigma
with branch points at the position of the twists. In particular, in the above example, we have

\langle\sigma(0)\sigma^{\dagger}(\ell)\rangle=\frac{Z_{N}}{Z_{1}^{N}}\,,

(2.6)

where the left-hand side is a correlator of the orbifold model
\mathcal{M}_{N}
on the plane, and the numerator
Z_{N}
on the right-hand side is, as above, the partition function of the original model
\mathcal{M}
on the ramified covering
\mathcal{R}_{N}
of the plane. Thus, in this simple example, we see that the Rényi entropy for positive integer
N
is related to a correlation function of twist operators in the cyclic orbifold
\mathcal{M}
:

S_{N}=\frac{1}{1-N}\log\langle\sigma(0)\sigma^{\dagger}(\ell)\rangle\,.

(2.7)

Since the original 1D quantum model is assumed to be critical and to have local interactions, the scaling limit of the lattice model
\mathcal{M}
is a 2D CFT. The lattice twist operators then scale to primary operators in the cyclic orbifold CFT corresponding to
\mathcal{M}_{N}
, and we still denote them by
\sigma,\sigma^{\dagger}
. Their conformal dimension is given
[
7
]
by
h_{\sigma}=(N-N^{-1})c/24
, where
c
is the central charge of the mother theory: see (
2.15
). The two-point function thus reads:

\langle\sigma(0)\sigma^{\dagger}(\ell)\rangle=\ell^{-4h_{\sigma}}\,,

(2.8)

which yields the well-known result (see the seminal work

]
)

S_{N}(\ell)\sim\frac{c}{6}\frac{(N+1)}{N}\log\ell\,.

(2.9)

Several other physical situations can be treated in the framework of cyclic orbifolds.
For instance, if the subsystem
A
is the union of
m

. We should also mention the Rényi entropy of a single interval in an excited primary state (instead of the ground state), which is related to the four-point correlation function

\langle\Phi(\infty)\sigma(1)\sigma^{\dagger}(z,\bar{z})\Phi(0)\rangle\,,

(2.10)

where
\Phi=\phi\otimes\dots\otimes\phi
is the corresponding replicated local primary operator in the cyclic orbifold CFT

2.2
The cyclic orbifold CFT

With the above motivation in mind, we define cyclic orbifold CFTs as follows. Let
\mathcal{M}
be any CFT with central charge
c
and primary operator content
\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3},\dots\}
, which we call the mother CFT. By convention, we always take
\phi_{1}
to be the identity operator:
\phi_{1}={\boldsymbol{1}}
. The cyclic orbifold CFT
\mathcal{M}
for a positive integer
N
, which is usually denoted as

\mathcal{M}_{N}=\mathcal{M}^{\otimes N}/\mathbb{Z}_{N}\,,

(2.11)

is most easily defined in the path-integral formalism. If the configurations of the mother theory are described by a field
\varphi(r)
with action
\mathcal{A}[\varphi]
, then the configurations of
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes N}
are (locally) described by
N
independent copies
(\varphi_{1}(r),\dots\varphi_{N}(r))
, with action
\mathcal{A}[\varphi_{1}]+\dots+\mathcal{A}[\varphi_{N}]
. Furthermore, the modding out by
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
means that configurations with topological defects (as described in the previous section) are to be included. In particular, on the cylinder (and on the torus, see section
4.1
) this implies that one must consider all possible
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
twisted boundary conditions. Accordingly, the Hilbert space (for a closed system) splits into
N
distinct sectors labelled by the
twist charge

[k]\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, corresponding to boundary conditions twisted by the permutation of copies
a\mapsto a+k
. Via the state-operator correspondence, local fields also carry a twist charge.

In the untwisted sector
[k=0]
, the Hilbert space is simply the
N^{\textrm{th}}
tensor product of the mother theory Hilbert space. The associated operators are called
untwisted
operators and are spanned
by products of local operators acting on each copy. These are generated, under the Operator Product Expansion (OPE), by operators acting on a single copy
a

\phi_{a}={\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\underset{(a)}{\phi}\otimes{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes{\boldsymbol{1}}\,.

(2.12)

By contrast, the Hilbert space in the twisted sector
[k=1]
is in one-to-one correspondence with the mother theory Hilbert space
[
7
]
. Indeed, one can untangle the
N
copies coupled via the twist
a\to a+1
into a single copy, at the cost of making the system
N
times larger. More generally, the Hilbert space in the twisted sector
[k]
maps to the tensor product of
C_{k}
copies of the mother theory Hilbert space, where
C_{k}
is the number of cycles of the permutation
a\to a+k
(in particular
C_{k}=1
exactly when
k
and
N
are coprime).

A generic operator
\mathcal{O}^{[k]}
with twist charge
[k]
inserts a defect line as explained above,
and hence its monodromy relative to a diagonal operator
\phi_{a}
reads:

\phi_{a}(e^{2i\pi}z,e^{-2i\pi}\bar{z})\cdot\mathcal{O}^{[k]}(0)=\phi_{a-k}(z,\bar{z})\cdot\mathcal{O}^{[k]}(0)\,.

(2.13)

being understood that upon going around the twisted field
\mathcal{O}^{[k]}(0)
, the field
\phi_{a}(z,\bar{z})
does not encircle any other (twisted) field.

Note that the twist charge
[k]
is conserved under fusion, in the sense that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}^{[k_{1}]}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\dots\mathcal{O}_{n}^{[k_{n}]}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle=0\quad\textrm{if}\quad k_{1}+\dots+k_{n}\neq 0\mod N\,.

(2.14)

As previously mentioned, visualizing the twisted fields as points where defect lines are inserted, with each line carrying an associated charge
[k]
, proves to be a useful approach. Under this interpretation, the charge neutrality condition in the correlator above can be understood as a topological constraint, ensuring that all defect lines must have two ends.

Of particular interest is the bare twist operator
\sigma^{[k]}
, which is simply the most relevant operator with twist charge
[k]
. Under the state-operator correspondence, it maps to the lowest energy state in the twisted sector
[k]
. In the untwisted sector this is simply the identity
\sigma^{[0]}={\boldsymbol{1}}
. Moreover, to lighten the notation, we write
\sigma
and
\sigma^{{\dagger}}
instead of
\sigma^{[1]}
and
\sigma^{[-1]}
, respectively.
The conformal dimension of the twist operators
\sigma
and
\sigma^{{\dagger}}
is

h_{\sigma}=\frac{c}{24}\left(N-\frac{1}{N}\right)\,.

(2.15)

This fundamental result, which is at the heart of the universal behaviour (
2.9
) of entanglement entropy for 1D critical systems, is surprisingly easy to establish. Indeed, it is a simple consequence of the finite size-scaling of eigenenergies in CFT. For a periodic system of size
L
, the orbifold Hamiltonian is
\frac{2\pi}{L}(L_{0}+\overline{L}_{0}-Nc/12)
, as the orbifold central charge is
Nc
, where
c
is the central charge of the mother theory. Thus, the lowest energy in the sector
[k=1]
is

E=\frac{4\pi}{L}\left(h_{\sigma}-\frac{Nc}{24}\right)\,.

(2.16)

since
\sigma
is a scalar field (
h_{\sigma}=\bar{h}_{\sigma}
). But as stated above, the twisted sector
[1]
for a system of size
L
is nothing but the Hilbert space of the mother theory for a system of size
NL
. From this perspective the Hamiltonian is
\frac{2\pi}{NL}(L_{0}+\overline{L}_{0}-c/12)
, and the state with the lowest energy is the vacuum, therefore

E=-\frac{4\pi}{NL}\frac{c}{24}\,.

(2.17)

Equating these two expressions yields (
2.15
). The argument is easy to generalize to arbitrary
k
, and one finds

h_{\sigma^{[k]}}=\frac{c}{24}\sum_{\text{cycle }j}\left(n_{kj}-\frac{1}{n_{kj}}\right)\,.

(2.18)

where the sum is over all cycles of the permutation
a\mapsto a+k
, and
n_{kj}
is the length of the
j^{\textrm{th}}
cycle. In particular, when
N
is prime, one gets
h_{\sigma^{[k]}}=h_{\sigma}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{\times}
.

2.3
Orbifold Virasoro algebra

Now that we have discussed the splitting of the Hilbert space into twisted sectors, we can describe the action of the extended orbifold algebra.
In the orbifold CFT
\mathcal{M}_{N}
, each copy
a
of the mother CFT carries the components
T_{a}(z),\overline{T}_{a}(\bar{z})
of the stress-energy tensor, with OPEs

\displaystyle T_{a}(z)T_{b}(w)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ab}\left[\frac{c/2}{(z-w)^{4}}+\frac{2T_{b}(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\partial T_{b}(w)}{z-w}\right]+\mathrm{reg}_{z\to w}\,,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\overline{T}_{a}(\bar{z})\overline{T}_{b}(\bar{w})

\displaystyle=\delta_{ab}\left[\frac{c/2}{(\bar{z}-\bar{w})^{4}}+\frac{2\overline{T}_{b}(\bar{w})}{(\bar{z}-\bar{w})^{2}}+\frac{\bar{\partial}\overline{T}_{b}(\bar{w})}{\bar{z}-\bar{w}}\right]+\mathrm{reg}_{\bar{z}\to\bar{w}}\,,

where
c
is the central charge of the mother CFT, and
\mathrm{reg}_{z\to w}
denotes a function which is regular as
z
tends to
w
. It turns out to be convenient to work with the discrete Fourier modes of these currents, namely

T^{(r)}(z)=\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}\omega^{ar}\,T_{a}(z)\,,\qquad\overline{T}^{(r)}(\bar{z})=\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}\omega^{ar}\,\overline{T}_{a}(\bar{z})\,,

(2.20)

where
\omega=\exp(2i\pi/N)
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
. We get the OPEs

\displaystyle T^{(r)}(z)T^{(s)}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{r+s,0}\,Nc/2}{(z-w)^{4}}+\frac{2T^{(r+s)}(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\partial T^{(r+s)}(w)}{z-w}+\mathrm{reg}_{z\to w}\,,

(2.21)

\displaystyle\overline{T}^{(r)}(\bar{z})\overline{T}^{(s)}(\bar{w})

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{r+s,0}\,Nc/2}{(\bar{z}-\bar{w})^{4}}+\frac{2\overline{T}^{(r+s)}(\bar{w})}{(\bar{z}-\bar{w})^{2}}+\frac{\partial\overline{T}^{(r+s)}(\bar{w})}{\bar{z}-\bar{w}}+\mathrm{reg}_{\bar{z}\to\bar{w}}\,.

The invariant modes
T^{(0)}(z)
and
\overline{T}^{(0)}(\bar{z})
are the components of the total stress-energy tensor of
\mathcal{M}_{N}
, with central charge
Nc
, whereas the other Fourier modes
T^{(r)}(z),\overline{T}^{(r)}(\bar{z})
with
r\neq 0\mod N
can be regarded as additional conserved currents. Altogether, these Fourier modes encode an extended conformal symmetry.

The Laurent expansion of the currents
T^{(r)}(z)
depends on the twist sector in which they act. Indeed, since by definition an operator
\mathcal{O}^{[k]}
with charge
[k]
inserts a defect line connecting copies
a
and
a+k
, we have the monodromy conditions (in the absence of other twist operators in the vicinity of the origin)

T^{(r)}(e^{2i\pi}z)\cdot\mathcal{O}^{[k]}(0)=\omega^{kr}T^{(r)}(z)\cdot\mathcal{O}^{[k]}(0)\,.

(2.22)

As a result, the mode decomposition is of the form

T^{(r)}(z)\cdot\mathcal{O}^{[k]}(0)=\sum_{m\in-kr/N+\mathbb{Z}}z^{-m-2}\,(L_{m}^{(r)}\cdot\mathcal{O}^{[k]})(0)\,\,.

(2.23)

with
L_{n}^{(r){\dagger}}=L_{-n}^{(-r)}
.
Likewise, for
\overline{T}^{(r)}
we have

\overline{T}^{(r)}(\bar{z})\cdot\mathcal{O}^{[k]}(0)=\sum_{m\in+kr/N+\mathbb{Z}}\bar{z}^{-m-2}\,(\bar{L}_{m}^{(r)}\cdot\mathcal{O}^{[k]})(0)\,\,.

(2.24)

We stress that, in the twist sector
[k]
, the allowed indices for
L_{m}^{(r)}
(resp.
\bar{L}_{m}^{(r)}
) are
m\in-kr/N+\mathbb{Z}
(resp.
m\in+kr/N+\mathbb{Z}
).
The OPEs (
2.21
) yield the commutation relations

\displaystyle\left[L^{(r)}_{m},L^{(s)}_{n}\right]=(m-n)L_{m+n}^{(r+s)}+\frac{Nc}{12}m(m^{2}-1)\,\delta_{m+n,0}\,\delta_{r+s,0}\,,

(2.25)

\displaystyle\left[\bar{L}^{(r)}_{m},\bar{L}^{(s)}_{n}\right]=(m-n)\bar{L}_{m+n}^{(r+s)}+\frac{Nc}{12}m(m^{2}-1)\,\delta_{m+n,0}\,\delta_{r+s,0}\,,

\displaystyle\left[L^{(r)}_{m},\bar{L}^{(s)}_{n}\right]=0\,,

where the Kronecker symbols
\delta_{r+s,0}
are understood modulo
N
.
These relations define the two commuting orbifold Virasoro algebras
\mathrm{OVir}_{N}
and
\overline{\rm OVir}_{N}
.

The invariant modes
L_{m}^{(0)}
are the modes of the total stress-energy tensor, and their index
m
is always an integer, as it should be. Of course, they form a Virasoro algebra, with central charge
Nc
:

\left[L^{(0)}_{m},L^{(0)}_{n}\right]=(m-n)L_{m+n}^{(0)}+\frac{Nc}{12}m(m^{2}-1)\,\delta_{m+n,0}\,,\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(2.26)

and similarly for the
\bar{L}_{m}^{(0)}
’s.

Due to the conservation of Fourier indices in the commutation relations (
2.25
), the two families of algebra elements

L_{m_{1}}^{(r_{1})}\dots L_{m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{L}_{m_{1}}^{(r_{1})}\dots\bar{L}_{m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\,,\qquad\text{with }r_{1}+\dots+r_{p}=0\mod N

(2.27)

generate algebras of the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathrm{OVir}_{N}
and
\overline{\rm OVir}_{N}
, which we shall call the
neutral algebras

A_{N}
and
\bar{A}_{N}
, respectively. Recall that indices are restricted appropriately so that for the elements of the
A_{N}
algebra we have that
m_{i}\in-kr_{i}/N+\mathbb{Z}
, and for
\bar{A}_{N}

m_{i}\in kr_{i}/N+\mathbb{Z}
. These neutral algebras are of key importance for the classification of operators and the description of correlations in the cyclic orbifold CFT.

2.4
Orbifold induction procedure

As mentioned above, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{[1]}
of the twisted sector
[k=1]
is in one-to-one correspondence with the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of the mother theory
[
7
]
. More precisely, there is an isomorphism
\Theta_{1}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{[1]}
, that is a norm-preserving, invertible linear map.
This map is in fact quite simple : it encodes the identification of states between
\mathcal{H}^{[1]}
and
\mathcal{H}
that follows from unfolding the
N
copies of the cylinder with twisted boundary-conditions into a single copy of the cylinder.

In particular, we get the following identification between the stress-tensor
T
of the mother theory (on the cylinder of perimeter
NL
) and the stress-tensor
T_{a}
acting on the
a^{\textrm{th}}
copy of the orbifold on the cylinder of perimeter
L
:

T_{a}(x,t)=\Theta_{1}\,T(aL+x,t)\,\Theta_{1}^{-1}\,,

(2.28)

where
0\leq x<L
and
a=0,\cdots,N-1
. In terms of modes, this means

\left(L^{(r)}_{m}-\delta_{m,0}\frac{Nc}{24}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\Theta_{1}\left(L_{Nm}-\delta_{m,0}\frac{c}{24}\right)\Theta_{1}^{-1}\,,\qquad m\in-r/N+\mathbb{Z}\,,

(2.29)

that is to say

L^{(r)}_{m}=\frac{1}{N}\Theta_{1}L_{Nm}\Theta_{1}^{-1}+\frac{c}{24}\left(N-\frac{1}{N}\right)\delta_{m0}\,,\qquad m\in-r/N+\mathbb{Z}\,.

(2.30)

It follows that there is a one-to-one correspondence between Virasoro primary states
|\phi_{j}\rangle
in the mother theory and primary states under the orbifold algebra in the twisted sector
[k=1]
of the cyclic orbifold, which we will denote by
|\sigma^{[1]}_{j}\rangle
:

|\sigma^{[1]}_{j}\rangle=\Theta_{1}|\phi_{j}\rangle\,.

(2.31)

The same elementary argument of finite-size scaling used to derive (
2.15
)
yields

h_{\sigma_{j}}=\frac{c}{24}\left(N-\frac{1}{N}\right)+\frac{h_{j}}{N}=h_{\sigma}+\frac{h_{j}}{N}\,.

(2.32)

The identification map
\Theta_{1}
provides a comprehensive and explicit construction of the twisted sector
[k=1]
in terms of states of the mother theory, known as the
orbifold induction procedure

[
7
]
. Furthermore, since
\Theta_{1}
is norm-preserving, a state in the twisted sector is a null-state if and only if it is the image (under
\Theta_{1}
) of a null-state in the mother theory. In that sense, the induction procedure provides a full description of all null-states in the twisted sector. Such null-states are important, as they can be exploited to derive differential equations for twist correlation functions
[
51
]
.

Of course, the above discussion can be adapted to all twisted sectors
[k]
. In particular, when
k
and
N
are coprime (that is when the permutation
a\to a+k
mod
N
has a unique cycle), the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{[k]}
can still be identified with
\mathcal{H}
, via a map
\Theta_{k}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{[k]}
. The relation between modes becomes simply

L^{(r)}_{m}=\frac{1}{N}\Theta_{k}L_{Nm}\Theta_{k}^{-1}+\frac{c}{24}\left(N-\frac{1}{N}\right)\delta_{m0}\,,\qquad m\in-kr/N+\mathbb{Z}\,.

(2.33)

The state
|\sigma^{[k]}_{j}\rangle=\Theta_{k}|\phi_{j}\rangle
has the same conformal dimension as
|\sigma^{[1]}_{j}\rangle
.

3
Operator content, OPEs and conformal blocks

From now on, we restrict to the case when the number of copies is a prime integer

. We shall describe the operator content of the cyclic orbifold CFT
\mathcal{M}_{N}
, in terms of the primary operators
\{\phi_{j}\}
of the mother CFT

. In

, operators and states will be organized into representations of the neutral algebras

To simplify the discussion, we suppose that the mother CFT is diagonal, namely every primary operator

is scalar, so that its conformal dimensions obey

This is the case in particular for all diagonal minimal models. Under these assumptions, the torus partition function is a diagonal modular invariant

and the modular

-matrix is real symmetric. The fusion numbers

N_{ij}^{k}=\sum_{m}\frac{S_{im}S_{jm}\bar{S}_{mk}}{S_{{\boldsymbol{1}}m}}\,,

(3.2)

can only take the values

or

.

3.3
On fusion numbers

Before moving on to operator product expansions and conformal blocks, it is useful to discuss fusion numbers. From the above discussion, the cyclic orbifold is a diagonal theory with respect to the extended symmetry
A_{N}\oplus\bar{A}_{N}
, therefore operators
\{\Phi_{\alpha}\}
are labelled by a single symbol
\alpha
, also labelling the irreducible
A_{N}
-modules
\{V_{\alpha}\}
. The fusion rules are generically of the form

\Phi_{\alpha}\times\Phi_{\beta}\to\sum_{\gamma}\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\,\Phi_{\gamma}\,,

(3.33)

where the sum is over all primary invariant operators
\Phi_{\gamma}
, and the non-negative integers
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}
are the fusion numbers. They obey

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=\mathcal{N}_{\beta\alpha}^{\gamma}\,,\qquad\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\gamma^{\dagger}}^{\beta^{\dagger}}\,.

(3.34)

Recall that we are assuming that, in the mother theory, all fusion numbers
N_{ij}^{k}
are
0
or
1
. But in the orbifold, non-trivial multiplicities (
i.e.

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}>1
) are expected to appear. The fusion number
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}
is the dimension of the space of chiral vertex operators

]
of type
\left({}_{\alpha\beta}^{\,\,\gamma}\right)
. Chiral three point functions

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}(\lambda,\mu,\nu)=\langle\Phi_{\gamma}|\nu^{\dagger}\,(\lambda\cdot\Phi_{\alpha})(1)\,\mu|\Phi_{\beta}\rangle

(3.35)

for
\lambda,\mu,\nu\in A_{N}
are not all linearly independent. Indeed, they satisfy many linear relations following from:

1.

The commutation rules of
A_{N}
, which follow from

\left[L^{(r)}_{m},L^{(s)}_{n}\right]=(m-n)L_{m+n}^{(r+s)}+\frac{Nc}{12}m(m^{2}-1)\,\delta_{m+n,0}\,\delta_{r+s,0}\,.

(3.36)

2.

The properties of primary operators and states

\displaystyle[L_{m}^{(0)},\Phi_{\alpha}(z,\bar{z})]=z^{m}\,\Big((m+1)h_{\alpha}+z\partial_{z}\Big)\,\Phi_{\alpha}(z,\bar{z})\,,

(3.37)

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{\gamma}|L_{m<0}^{(r)}=0\,,\qquad\langle\Phi_{\gamma}|L_{0}^{(0)}=h_{\gamma}\langle\Phi_{\gamma}|\,,

(3.38)

\displaystyle L_{m>0}^{(r)}|\Phi_{\alpha}\rangle=0\,,\qquad L_{0}^{(0)}|\Phi_{\alpha}\rangle=h_{\alpha}|\Phi_{\alpha}\rangle\,.

(3.39)

3.

The Ward identities associated to the
{\rm OVir}_{N}
currents.
These can be expressed generally as closed contour identities, for any

-tuple of operators
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{n}
(primary or not) with twist charges

\displaystyle\oint dz\,(z-z_{1})^{q_{1}}\dots(z-z_{n})^{q_{n}}\,\langle T^{(r)}(z)\Phi_{1}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\dots\Phi_{n}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle=0\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{if }q_{j}\in-\frac{rk_{j}}{N}+\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }q_{1}+\dots+q_{n}\leq 2\,.

(3.40)

4.

The decoupling of null-vectors (

Consider the whole set of constraints (

By the use of orbifold Ward identities, one can show that the subset of coefficients

Let’s consider for instance the fusion process involving three non-diagonal untwisted operators

But while we cannot at this stage easily compute the fusion numbers involving twists fields, we can at least argue that they are finite, by showing that correlation functions in the orbifold always involve finitely many (extended) conformal blocks.

Holomorphic factorization property and rationality

Conformal field theories obey the holomorphic factorization property
[
57
]
. On the plane/sphere, it means that
correlation functions can be decomposed as

\langle\phi_{i_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\cdots\phi_{i_{n}}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle=\sum_{I,J}F_{I}({\bf z})\rho_{IJ}\overline{F}_{J}({\bf\bar{z}})

(3.62)

where the (extended) conformal blocks
F_{I}
are holomorphic functions of
{\bf z}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
, and
\overline{F}_{J}
are anti-holomorphic. More generally, on a Riemann surface
\Sigma
the factorization property becomes

Z_{\Sigma}\,\,\langle\phi_{i_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\cdots\phi_{i_{n}}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle_{\Sigma}=\sum_{I,J}F_{I}({\bf z},{\bf m})\rho_{IJ}\overline{F}_{J}({\bf\bar{z}},{\bf\bar{m}})

(3.63)

where
Z_{\Sigma}
stands for the partition function on
\Sigma
, and the conformal blocks
F_{I}
depend also holomorphically on the analytic coordinates
{\bf m}
of the moduli of
\Sigma
. The above holds in particular in the absence of field insertions (
n=0
), meaning that the partition function itself obeys the holomorphic factorization property. For instance in genus one, with a flat metric, this is simply

Z(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\sum_{i,j}\chi_{i}(\tau)\rho_{ij}\overline{\chi}_{j}(\bar{\tau})\,.

(3.64)

A CFT is said to be
rational
when all the sums involved above are finite.

Provided the mother theory
\mathcal{M}
obeys the holomorphic factorization property, then so does its cyclic orbifold
\mathcal{M}_{N}
. Consider for instance a generic correlation function of twists fields on a Riemann surface
\Sigma

\langle\sigma^{[k_{1}]}_{i_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\cdots\sigma^{[k_{n}]}_{i_{n}}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle_{\Sigma}

(3.65)

where twist charge neutrality is assumed (
\sum_{i}k_{i}=0
mod
N
), and
\sigma^{[k]}_{i}
stand for the composite twist field of charge
k
associated to the field
\phi_{i}
(as defined in section
3.1
).

One can reinterpret this correlation function as a correlation function of the fields
\phi_{i}
in the mother theory on the
N
-sheeted branched cover
p:\Sigma^{\prime}\to\Sigma
, with appropriate branch points at
z_{j}
:

\displaystyle Z_{\Sigma}\,\,\langle\sigma^{[k_{1}]}_{i_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\cdots\sigma^{[k_{n}]}_{i_{n}}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=Z_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{i_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\cdots\phi_{i_{n}}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\,,

(3.66)

where in the
r.h.s.
both the correlation function and the partition function involve the mother theory on the covering surface
\Sigma^{\prime}
, with the metric
g^{\prime}=p^{*}g
being the pull-back of the metric
g
on
\Sigma
. In practice, the picture above is slightly more complicated, as the pull-back metric
g^{\prime}
has conical singularities at the ramification points, but these can be regularized
[
58
]
.

Exploiting the holomorphic factorization property of the mother theory then yields

\displaystyle Z_{\Sigma}\,\,\langle\sigma^{k_{1}}_{i_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\cdots\sigma^{k_{n}}_{i_{n}}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle_{\Sigma}=\sum_{I,J}F_{I}({\bf z},{\bf m}^{\prime})\rho_{IJ}\overline{F}_{J}({\bf\bar{z}},{\bf\bar{m}^{\prime}})

(3.67)

The factorization property of the
l.h.s.
then follows from the fact that the moduli
{\bf m^{\prime}}={\bf m^{\prime}}({\bf m},{\bf z})
of the branched covering
\Sigma^{\prime}
depends holomorphically on the moduli
{\bf m}
of
\Sigma
and the positions
{\bf z}
of the branched points. For instance, the
2
-sheeted cover of the sphere with four branch points at positions
z_{i}
is a torus with moduli

\displaystyle\tau(x)=i\,\frac{{}_{2}\mathrm{F}_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},1;1-x\right)}{{}_{2}\mathrm{F}_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},1;x\right)},\qquad x=\frac{(z_{1}-z_{2})(z_{3}-z_{4})}{(z_{1}-z_{3})(z_{2}-z_{4})}

The above argument is easily adapted to the generic case involving descendants of twist fields and/or untwisted fields. The last quantity to consider is the partition function itself, but this is simply obtained by summing all twisted partition functions of
N
copies of the mother theory, each of which obeys the factorization property.

Furthermore, it is clear that if the mother theory is rational, then so is the orbifold. As a consequence, all fusion numbers in the cyclic orbifold are finite when the mother theory is rational.

4
Modular properties and Verlinde’s formula

In this section, we recall the modular properties of the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
orbifold and obtain the orbifold fusion rules from the Verlinde formula under the assumption that
N
is prime.

4.1
Torus partition function

Consider the torus of modular parameter
\tau

\mathbb{T}_{\tau}=\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z})\,,

(4.1)

with
\mathrm{Im}\,\tau>0
, and let us use the notations
q=e^{2i\pi\tau}
and
{\bar{q}}=e^{-2i\pi\bar{\tau}}
.
We denote by
Z_{m,n}(\tau)
the partition function of
\mathcal{M}_{N}
, where the copy
a
is connected to the copy
a+m
(resp.
a+n
) along the cycle
z\to z+\tau
(resp.
z\to z+1
). Since changing the orientation of both cycles yields the same torus, clearly
Z_{m,n}(\tau)=Z_{-m,-n}(\tau)
. Furthermore, the elementary modular transformations act as:

Z_{mn}(\tau+1)=Z_{m-n,n}(\tau)\,,\qquad Z_{mn}(-1/\tau)=Z_{n,-m}(\tau)\,.

(4.2)

For a general modular transformation, we have:

Z_{mn}\left(\tau\right)=Z_{am+bn,cm+dn}\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)\,,\qquad(a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}\,,\qquad ad-bc=1\,.

(4.3)

The full partition function of the orbifold CFT is defined as the sum over all possible defects:

Z_{\rm orb}(\tau)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m,n=0}^{N-1}Z_{mn}(\tau)\,,

(4.4)

and it is manifestly modular invariant.

Denoting by
Z(\tau)
the mother CFT partition function, we have the identities

Z_{m0}(\tau)=Z(N\tau)\,,\qquad Z_{0m}(\tau)=Z(\tau/N)\,,\qquad m\neq 0\mod N\,.

(4.5)

Using these, together with the relations (
4.3
), we can express
Z_{\rm orb}(\tau)
as:

\displaystyle Z_{\rm orb}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left[Z_{00}(\tau)+\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}Z_{m0}(\tau)+\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}Z_{mn}(\tau)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}Z(\tau)^{N}+\frac{N-1}{N}\left[Z(N\tau)+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}Z\left(\frac{\tau+k}{N}\right)\right]\,,

(4.6)

which is the standard result
[
10
]
for the partition function of the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
orbifold.
Recall the definition (
A.3
) for the cyclic permutation of copies, and the family of projectors

\mathcal{P}_{r}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}\omega^{-ar}\,\Pi^{a}\,,

(4.7)

on the eigenspaces of eigenvalue
\omega^{r}
of
\Pi
.
By construction, each individual twisted partition function
Z_{mn}(\tau)
reads

Z_{mn}(\tau)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}^{[n]}}\left[\Pi^{m}\,q^{L_{0}^{(0)}-Nc/24}\,{\bar{q}}^{\bar{L}_{0}^{(0)}-Nc/24}\right]\,,

(4.8)

where
\mathcal{H}^{[n]}
is the space of
\mathcal{M}_{N}
states with twist charge
n
. Therefore, the orbifold partition function (
4.4
) can be rewritten as

Z_{\rm orb}(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}^{[n]}}\left[\mathcal{P}_{0}\,q^{L_{0}^{(0)}-Nc/24}\,{\bar{q}}^{\bar{L}_{0}^{(0)}-Nc/24}\right]\,.

(4.9)

Hence we get the simple identity

Z_{\rm orb}(\tau)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\left[q^{L_{0}^{(0)}-Nc/24}\,{\bar{q}}^{\bar{L}_{0}^{(0)}-Nc/24}\right]\,,

(4.10)

where
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is the invariant Hilbert space defined in Sec.
3.2
. We recover that local fields coincide with invariant fields.

4.2
Modular characters

Let us first fix the notations for the characters of the mother CFT, in the case when it is rational,
i.e.
with a finite set of primary operators
\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{M}\}
.
We denote by
\chi_{j}
the Virasoro character of the module associated to
\phi_{j}
in the mother CFT:

\chi_{j}(\tau)=\mathrm{Tr}_{v_{j}}\left(q^{L_{0}-c/24}\right)\,.

(4.11)

The
\chi_{j}
’s transform under the elementary modular maps as

\chi_{j}(\tau+1)=t_{j}\,\chi_{j}(\tau)\,,\qquad\chi_{i}(-1/\tau)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}S_{ij}\,\chi_{j}(\tau)\,,

(4.12)

where
t_{j}=\exp[2i\pi(h_{j}-c/24)]
, and
S
is a unitary matrix. Moreover, since we assume that the mother theory
\mathcal{M}
is diagonal, the matrix
S
is real symmetric, and satisfies
(ST)^{3}={\boldsymbol{1}}
, where
T=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{M})
.

In the orbifold CFT, the characters associated to the
A_{N}
-modules
V_{J},V_{j}^{(r)},V_{j}^{[k](r)}
are, respectively:

\displaystyle\chi_{J}(\tau)=\chi_{j_{1}}(\tau)\dots\chi_{j_{N}}(\tau)\,,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\chi_{j}^{(r)}(\tau)=\frac{1}{N}\left[\chi_{j}(\tau)^{N}+(N\delta_{r0}-1)\chi_{j}(N\tau)\right]\,,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\chi_{j}^{[k](r)}(\tau)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}t_{j}^{-n/N}\,\omega^{-krn}\,\chi_{j}\left(\frac{\tau+n}{N}\right)\,.

(4.15)

The proof for these expressions is given in
[
7
]
.
and they can be considered as a special case of
[
67
,
68
]
(which are also employed in
[
69
]
and
[
11
]
)

From the decomposition of the invariant Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, we can write the partition function (
4.10
) as

Z_{\rm orb}(\tau)=\sum_{J=[j_{1}\dots j_{N}]}|\chi_{J}|^{2}+\sum_{j}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}|\chi_{j}^{(r)}|^{2}+\sum_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}|\chi_{j}^{[k](r)}|^{2}\,,

(4.16)

where the notations for the sums are the same as in (
3.32
).
The orbifold characters transform under the elementary modular maps as

\chi_{\alpha}(\tau+1)=\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}\,\chi_{\alpha}(\tau)\,,\qquad\chi_{\alpha}(-1/\tau)=\sum_{\beta}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\beta}\,\chi_{\beta}(\tau)\,,

(4.17)

with

\mathcal{T}_{J}=t_{j_{1}}\dots t_{j_{N}}\,,\qquad\mathcal{T}_{j}^{(r)}=t_{j}^{N}\,,\qquad\mathcal{T}_{j}^{[k](r)}=\omega^{kr}\,t_{j}^{1/N}\,,

(4.18)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{I,J}=\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}S_{i_{1},j_{1+a}}\dots S_{i_{N},j_{N+a}}\,,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{I,j^{(r)}}=\mathcal{S}_{j^{(r)},[i_{1}\dots i_{N}]}=S_{i_{1},j}\dots S_{i_{N},j}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i^{(r)},j^{(s)}}=\frac{S_{ij}^{N}}{N}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i^{(r)},j^{[k](s)}}=\mathcal{S}_{j^{[k](s)},i^{(r)}}=\frac{\omega^{-kr}\,S_{ij}}{N}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{[\ell](s)}}=\frac{\omega^{-ks-\ell r}\,(P_{\ell.k^{-1}})_{ij}}{N}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{I,j^{[k](r)}}=\mathcal{S}_{j^{[k](r)},I}=0\,.

The matrices
P_{n}
appearing in this expression are defined as

P_{n}=T^{-n/N}\cdot Q_{n}\cdot T^{[[-n^{-1}]]/N}\,,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{\times}\,,

(4.20)

where
[[-n^{-1}]]
denotes the inverse of
(-n)
modulo
N
, with
0<[[-n^{-1}]]<N
, and
Q_{n}
is the matrix representing the linear action of the modular map

\tau\mapsto q_{n}(\tau)=\frac{n\tau-(n[[-n^{-1}]]+1)/N}{N\tau-[[-n^{-1}]]}\,

(4.21)

on the characters
\chi_{j}
of the mother CFT.
We introduce the conjugation matrix
\mathcal{C}
, with matrix elements

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta^{\dagger}}\,,

(4.22)

and the diagonal matrix
\mathcal{T}
with matrix elements
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
.
The orbifold modular matrices
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
satisfy the properties

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{t}=\mathcal{S}\,,

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{2}=\mathcal{C}\,,

(4.24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\dagger}\mathcal{S}={\boldsymbol{1}}\,,

(4.25)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}\mathcal{T})^{3}=\mathcal{C}\,,

(4.26)

where
\mathcal{S}^{t}
denotes the transpose of
\mathcal{S}
.

The main arguments for the proofs of these properties are given in the Appendix
C
.
Note that the relations (
4.17
) are not sufficient to determine completely the matrices
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
, because some distinct
A_{N}
-modules have the same characters, namely

\displaystyle\chi_{[j_{1}\dots j_{N}]}(\tau)=\chi_{[j_{p(1)}\dots j_{p(N)}]}(\tau)\quad\text{for any permutation $p$ of $\{1,\dots,N\}$,}

(4.27)

\displaystyle\chi_{j}^{(r)}(\tau)=\chi_{j}^{(s)}(\tau)\quad\text{if }r,s\neq 0\,,

(4.28)

\displaystyle\chi_{j}^{[k](r)}(\tau)=\chi_{j}^{[\ell](s)}(\tau)\quad\text{if }rk=s\ell\mod N\,.

(4.29)

However, the expressions (
4.18
–
4.19
) define a simple and elegant solution to the constraints (
4.23
–
4.26
) in terms of the modular matrices of the mother CFT. Furthermore, it agrees with the
S
matrix given in equation (10) of
[
68
]
.

4.3
Fusion rules

Recall our notation for the fusion numbers:

\Phi_{\alpha}\times\Phi_{\beta}\to\sum_{\gamma}\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\,\Phi_{\gamma}\,,

(4.30)

where
\Phi_{\alpha},\Phi_{\beta},\Phi_{\gamma}
are invariant primary operators.
The fusion numbers can be computed through the Verlinde formula

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=\sum_{\delta}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\delta}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\,\mathcal{S}_{\beta\delta}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\,\mathcal{S}_{\gamma\delta}^{\dagger}}{\mathcal{S}_{{\boldsymbol{1}}\delta}}\,,

(4.31)

where the sum runs over all possible invariant primary operators
\Phi_{\delta}
described in Section
3.1
. This formula, together with (
4.23
–
4.26
), directly ensures that the properties are satisfied

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=\mathcal{N}_{\beta\alpha}^{\gamma}\,,\qquad\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=\mathcal{N}_{\alpha\gamma^{\dagger}}^{\beta^{\dagger}}\,,

(4.32)

are satisfied.
In the case of untwisted operators, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{I,J}^{K}=\sum_{a,b=0}^{N-1}N_{i_{1+a},j_{1+b}}^{k_{1}}\dots N_{i_{N+a},j_{N+b}}^{k_{N}}\,,

(4.33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{I,J}^{k^{(r)}}=\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}N_{i_{1+a},j_{1}}^{k}\dots N_{i_{N+a},j_{N}}^{k}\,,

(4.34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{I,j^{(r)}}^{k^{(s)}}=N_{i_{1},j}^{k}\dots N_{i_{N},j}^{k}\,,

(4.35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{i^{(r)},j^{(s)}}^{k^{(t)}}=\delta_{r+s,t}\,N_{ij}^{k}\,,

(4.36)

which confirms the hypothesis that the OPE coefficients constructed in Sec.
3.3
span the solution space of the linear system of equations which derive from the orbifold algebraic rules and Ward identities.
The fusion numbers involving twist operators are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{i^{[p](r)}j^{[q](s)}}^{K}=\delta_{p+q,0}\,\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{S_{i\ell}S_{j\ell}\cdot S_{k_{1}\ell}\dots S_{k_{N}\ell}}{S_{1\ell}^{N}}\,,

(4.37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{i^{[p](r)}j^{[q](s)}}^{k^{(t)}}=\frac{\delta_{p+q,0}}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\left[\frac{S_{i\ell}S_{j\ell}S_{k\ell}^{N}}{S_{1\ell}^{N}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\omega^{np(r+s-t)}\frac{(P_{-n})_{i\ell}(P_{n})_{j\ell}S_{k\ell}}{S_{1\ell}}\right]\,,

(4.38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{i^{[p](r)}j^{[q](s)}}^{k^{[m](t)}}=\frac{\delta_{p+q,m}}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\left[\frac{S_{i\ell}S_{j\ell}S_{k\ell}}{S_{1\ell}^{N}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\omega^{n(r+s-t)}\frac{(P_{pn^{-1}}^{\dagger})_{i\ell}(P_{qn^{-1}}^{\dagger})_{j\ell}(P_{mn^{-1}}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}})_{k\ell}}{S_{1\ell}}\right]\,.

(4.39)

The fusion numbers (
4.38
) depend on the Fourier indices
r,s,t
only through the combination
(r+s-t)
, as expected from the discussion in Sec.
3.3
.
Using the unitarity of
S
, some of the terms can be expressed using the fusion numbers of the mother CFT:

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{S_{i\ell}S_{j\ell}\cdot S_{k_{1}\ell}\dots S_{k_{N}\ell}}{S_{1\ell}^{N}}=\sum_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{N-1}=1}^{M}N_{ij}^{\ell_{1}}\times N_{\ell_{1}k_{1}}^{\ell_{2}}N_{\ell_{2}k_{2}}^{\ell_{3}}\dots N_{\ell_{N-2}k_{N-2}}^{\ell_{N-1}}\times N_{\ell_{N-1}k_{N-1}}^{k_{N}}\,,

(4.40)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{S_{i\ell}S_{j\ell}S_{k\ell}^{N}}{S_{1\ell}^{N}}=\sum_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{N-1}=1}^{M}N_{ij}^{\ell_{1}}\times N_{\ell_{1}k}^{\ell_{2}}N_{\ell_{2}k}^{\ell_{3}}\dots N_{\ell_{N-2}k}^{\ell_{N-1}}\times N_{\ell_{N-1}k}^{k}\,.

(4.41)

This is also the case for the first term in (
4.39
). For instance, for
N=3
, we have

\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{S_{i\ell}S_{j\ell}S_{k\ell}}{S_{1\ell}^{3}}=\sum_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}=1}^{M}N_{i\ell_{1}}^{\ell_{2}}N_{j\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{3}}N_{k\ell_{3}}^{\ell_{1}}\,,

(4.42)

and similar but more complicated expressions hold for
N>3
.

5
Example applications

In this section, we shall present some applications of our results on the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
orbifolds of minimal CFTs.

5.1
The
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
orbifold of the Yang-Lee CFT

In this section, we shall present a simple application of our results for the operator algebra of the cyclic orbifold CFT. We choose as mother theory the Yang-Lee minimal model
\mathcal{M}(5,2)
with central charge
c=-22/5
, and primary operator content
\{{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi\}
with conformal dimensions
h_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}=0
and
h_{\phi}=-1/5
. The modular
S
-matrix associated to this CFT is given by:

S=\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\sin(2\pi/5)&\sin(4\pi/5)\\
\sin(4\pi/5)&\sin(2\pi/5)\end{array}\right)\,.

(5.1)

The non-trivial fusion rule in the Yang-Lee CFT is

\phi\times\phi\to{\boldsymbol{1}}+\phi\,.

(5.2)

We will consider the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
cyclic orbifold of the above CFT. Its primary operator content (with respect to the neutral algebra
A_{N}\oplus\bar{A}_{N}
) consists of:

•

2 non-diagonal untwisted operators:
[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]
and
[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]
,

•

6 diagonal untwisted operators:
{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r)},\Phi^{(r)}
with
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
,

•

12 twist operators:
\sigma_{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r)},\sigma_{\boldsymbol{1}}^{{\dagger}(r)},\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(r)}
with
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

Recall the definition of non-diagonal untwisted operators, e.g.

[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}({\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\phi+{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes\phi\otimes{\boldsymbol{1}}+\phi\otimes{\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{1}})\,.

(5.3)

Using the results of Sec.
4.3
, we get the fusion rules between untwisted operators:

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]\times[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]\rightarrow[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]+2[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]+\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r)}\,,

(5.4)

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]\times[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]\rightarrow[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]+2[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]+\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\Phi^{(r)}\,,

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]\times[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]\rightarrow 2[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]+3[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]+\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r)}+2\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\Phi^{(r)}\,,

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]\times{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r)}\rightarrow[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]\,,

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]\times{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r)}\rightarrow[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]\,,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r)}\times{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(s)}\rightarrow{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r+s)}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi^{(r)}\times{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(s)}\rightarrow\Phi^{(r+s)}\,,

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]\times\Phi^{(r)}\rightarrow[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]+\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\Phi^{(s)}\,,

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]\times\Phi^{(r)}\rightarrow[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]+2[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]+\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\Phi^{(s)}\,,

\displaystyle\phi^{(r)}\times\phi^{(s)}\rightarrow[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]+[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]+2\times{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r+s)}+\Phi^{(r+s)}\,.

The fusion rules between untwisted and twisted operators read

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]\times\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sum_{r}\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}\,,

(5.5)

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]\times\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sum_{r}\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(r)}+\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}\,,

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]\times\sigma_{\phi}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sum_{r}\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(r)}+\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}\,,

\displaystyle[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]\times\sigma_{\phi}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sum_{r}\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(r)}+2\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}\,,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r)}\times\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(s+r)}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi^{(r)}\times\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(s+r)}+\sigma_{\phi}^{(s+r)}+\sigma_{\phi}^{(s+r+1)}\,,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\boldsymbol{1}}^{(r)}\times\sigma_{\phi}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sigma_{\phi}^{(s+r)}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi^{(r)}\times\sigma_{\phi}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(s+r-1)}+\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(s+r)}+\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\sigma_{\phi}^{(t)}\,.

Finally, the fusion rules between twist operators read

\displaystyle\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(r)}\times\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{{\dagger}(s)}\rightarrow[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]+{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(s+r)}+\Phi^{(s+r)}\,,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}\times\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{{\dagger}(s)}\rightarrow[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]+[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]+\Phi^{(s+r-1)}+\Phi^{(s+r)}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}\times\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(s)}\rightarrow[{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi]+2[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]+{\boldsymbol{1}}^{(s+r)}+\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\Phi^{(r)}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(r)}\times\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{{\dagger}(r+s)}+\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{{\dagger}(r+s+1)}+\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(r+s)}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}\times\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{{\dagger}(r+s)}+\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(t)}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}\times\sigma_{\phi}^{(s)}\rightarrow\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}^{{\dagger}(t)}+\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(r+s)}+\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(r+s+1)}+2\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(r+s+2)}\,.

The other fusion rules are easily obtained by symmetry under conjugation.

As pointed out in Sec.
3.3
, one may treat the OPE of twist operators
\sigma_{i}^{(r)}\times\sigma_{j}^{{\dagger}(s)}
or
\sigma_{i}^{(r)}\times\sigma_{j}^{(s)}
, by “unfolding” the three-point correlator corresponding to a particular orbifold fusion rule, as a mother CFT correlator. This is done in two steps: first, one translates the orbifold correlation function into an expectation value on the replicated surface
\Sigma_{n}
. Then, one conformally maps this quantity to a correlator defined on a Riemann surface that is more amenable to calculations (the Riemann sphere
\mathbb{C}
in our first example).
This approach is exemplified in Appendix
B
for the calculation of the orbifold structure constant

\mathcal{C}^{[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]}_{\sigma_{\phi}^{(0)},\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(0)}}=\langle[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]\cdot\sigma_{\phi}^{(0)}\cdot\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(0)}\rangle\neq 0\,.

(5.7)

Since the structure constant is non-vanishing, we can conclude that indeed the OPE
\sigma_{\phi}^{(0)}\times\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(0)}
produces the module
[{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi,\phi]
.
However, this unfolding does not allow us to extract the value of the associated multiplicity. From (
5.6
), we see that this multiplicity is two. Furthermore, using this technique to infer the fusion rules of the cyclic orbifold CFT from the mother CFT data is only feasible provided the resulting correlator can be calculated, and the unfolding map is known.

One encounters both of these difficulties in trying to find the fusion rules between twist operators in the same twist charge sector.
Let us consider the following orbifold three-point function of twist operators:

\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}}^{\sigma_{i}^{\dagger}}=\langle\sigma_{i}\cdot\sigma_{j}\cdot\sigma_{k}\rangle\,.

(5.8)

This translates into a three-point function on the three-sheeted cover of the Riemann sphere, with branch points at
(0,1,\infty)
, which we denote by
\Sigma_{3}
:

\langle\phi_{i}\cdot\phi_{j}\cdot\phi_{k}\rangle_{\Sigma_{3}}\,.

(5.9)

We can now calculate the genus of this surface
\Sigma_{3}
, using the Riemann-Hurwitz formula

2g-2=N(2h-2)+\sum_{i=1}^{p}(e_{i}-1)\,,

(5.10)

giving the genus
g
of an
N
-sheeted cover of a surface of genus
h
, with
p
branch points having ramification indices
e_{i}
. In our case, all the branch points have ramification indices
e_{i}=3
, so that we find
g=1
. Thus, there exists a conformal map
z\mapsto t(z)
between the surface
\Sigma_{3}
and a torus
\mathbb{T}_{\tau}
which allow us to relate (
5.9
) to the following three point function on the torus

\langle\phi_{i}(t_{1},\bar{t}_{1})\phi_{j}(t_{2},\bar{t}_{2})\phi_{k}(t_{3},\bar{t}_{3})\rangle_{\mathbb{T}_{\tau}}\,.

(5.11)

To complete such a calculation, one would have two non-trivial problems to solve: finding the conformal map
z\mapsto t(z)
, and calculating the three-point correlator (
5.11
). Assuming the conformal map has been found, one needs to calculate the torus correlators on a case-by-case basis. For example, when
(\phi_{i},\phi_{j},\phi_{k})=({\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}})
or
({\boldsymbol{1}},{\boldsymbol{1}},\phi)
, results are already known – they correspond respectively to the partition function of the Yang-Lee CFT, and the torus one-point function
\langle\phi\rangle_{\mathbb{T}_{\tau}}
, which have been calculated in
[
70
]
.

However, the issue of counting multiplicities still remains, and as we go beyond this relatively simple example of the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
orbifold of the Yang-Lee CFT, to higher
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
orbifolds, the formula (
5.10
) shows that unfolding would require the calculation of correlators on surfaces of genus
g\geq 2
, for which few exact results are available in the literature.

5.2
Counting conformal blocks of twist correlators

We will provide a few examples of conformal block counting for four-point correlators in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
cyclic orbifolds.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{3}

We will first illustrate this by using the minimal model
\mathcal{M}(4,3)
(Ising CFT) as mother theory, with central charge
c=1/2
, and primary operator content
\{{\boldsymbol{1}},s,\varepsilon\}
with conformal dimensions
h_{{\boldsymbol{1}}}=0
,
h_{s}=1/16
,
h_{\varepsilon}=1/2
. To avoid confusion with the twist operators, we d
The modular
S

S=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&1&\sqrt{2}\\
1&1&-\sqrt{2}\\
\sqrt{2}&-\sqrt{2}&0\end{array}\right)\,,

Conclusion

In this article, we have provided a formal analysis of the
\mathcal{M}_{N}
orbifold CFT, with prime
N
, of a diagonal and rational mother CFT
\mathcal{M}
. We have classified the operators of the theory under the neutral algebra of the full orbifold symmetry algebra. Analysing the spectrum of primary operators under
A_{N}\oplus\bar{A}_{N}
, we have found exact solutions for its modular data, as well as closed expressions for the fusion numbers of
\mathcal{M}_{N}
.
Our results are consistent with the ones obtained for
N=2
in
[
7
]
5
5

5

Note that since at
N=2
there is only one twist charge sector, there are no fusion rules involving three twist fields.
.

To showcase the use of our results, we have explicitly given all the fusion rules in the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold of the Yang-Lee CFT. We have also commented on the limitations of the unfolding approach, which requires, even in this relatively simple case, the computation of correlators on surfaces with genus
g\geq 1
. As a second example, we have shown how the fusion numbers can be used to count conformal blocks and commented on applications to the calculation of Rényi entropies. The block counting examples we have presented are consistent with previous results in the literature
[
51
]
.

Natural directions for investigations can be realized by considering more intricate mother CFTs as starting points. One could consider CFTs which are non-diagonal, non-rational or have extended symmetries as mother CFTs and generalize the results of this paper. Alternatively, one could consider orbifolding by a generic
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
group,
i.e.
extending the analysis to non-prime
N

Finally, it would be mathematically interesting to find an interpretation for the expressions of the fusion numbers (

Appendix A
Proofs of the properties of invariant operators

In this part of the Appendix, we give the proofs for the non-trivial statements made in Sec.
3.1
, about the properties of the operators of the form
\Phi_{[j_{1},\dots,j_{N}]}
,
\Phi_{j}^{(r)}
and
\sigma_{j}^{[k](r)}
under the neutral algebra
A_{N}\oplus\bar{A}_{N}
. In particular, we say that an operator
\Phi
is primary under
A_{N}\oplus\bar{A}_{N}
if and only if

L_{m_{1}}^{(r_{1})}\dots L_{m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\cdot\Phi=\bar{L}_{m_{1}}^{(r_{1})}\dots\bar{L}_{m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\cdot\Phi=0

(A.1)

for any
(m_{1},\dots,m_{p})
such that
m_{1}\leq\dots\leq m_{p}
and
m_{1}+\dots+m_{p}>0
, and
r_{1}+\dots+r_{p}=0\mod N
.

A.1
Untwisted sector

The untwisted sector contains local fields in
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes N}
that are invariant under cyclic permutations, therefore it is spanned by fields of the form

\Psi=\mathcal{P}_{0}\left[L_{m_{1}}^{(r_{1})}\dots L_{m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\cdot\bar{L}_{\bar{m}_{1}}^{(\bar{r}_{1})}\dots\bar{L}_{\bar{m}_{\bar{p}}}^{(\bar{r}_{\bar{p}})}\cdot(\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}})\right]\,,\quad\text{with}\ \begin{cases}m_{1}\leq\dots\leq m_{p}\leq 0\,,\\
\bar{m}_{1}\leq\dots\leq\bar{m}_{\bar{p}}\leq 0\,.\end{cases}

(A.2)

where
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is the projector onto the invariant subspace, all
m_{i},\bar{m}_{i}
are integers, and
\phi_{j_{i}}
are primary fields in the mother theory. Let
\Pi
be the elementary cyclic permutation of the copies, which acts on untwisted operators as

\Pi\cdot(\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N-1}}\otimes\phi_{j_{N}})\cdot\Pi^{-1}=(\phi_{j_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}}\otimes\phi_{j_{1}})\,.

(A.3)

and let
\mathcal{P}_{s}
be the projectors

\mathcal{P}_{s}[\Phi]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}\omega^{-sa}\,\Pi^{a}\cdot\Phi\cdot\Pi^{-a}\,.

(A.4)

Since
\Pi\cdot T^{(r)}(z)\cdot\Pi^{-1}=\omega^{r}\,T^{(r)}(z)
, where
\omega=\exp(2i\pi/N)
, it follows that

\Pi\cdot L_{m}^{(r)}\cdot\Pi^{-1}=\omega^{r}\,L_{m}^{(r)}\,.

(A.5)

and therefore

L_{m}^{(r)}\mathcal{P}_{s}=\mathcal{P}_{r+s}L_{m}^{(r)}

(A.6)

So the field
\Psi
from (
A.2
) can be recast as

\Psi=L_{m_{1}}^{(r_{1})}\dots L_{m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\cdot\bar{L}_{\bar{m}_{1}}^{(\bar{r}_{1})}\dots\bar{L}_{\bar{m}_{\bar{p}}}^{(\bar{r}_{\bar{p}})}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{-r-\bar{r}}[\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}}]\,,

(A.7)

where
r=r_{1}+\dots+r_{p}
and
\bar{r}=r_{1}+\dots+\bar{r}_{\bar{p}}
. We now have to distinguish two cases, depending on whether
\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}}
is invariant or not.

Non-diagonal untwisted operators.

When
\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}}
is not invariant under cyclic permutations, states of the form (
A.2
) are said to be
non-diagonal
. In particular, the most relevant field in that sector is

\Phi_{[j_{1}\dots j_{N}]}\propto\mathcal{P}_{0}(\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}})\,.

(A.8)

and therefore it is necessarily primary. We now prove that any operator in the untwisted, non-diagonal sector is an
(A_{N}\oplus\bar{A}_{N})
-descendant of some primary operator of the form
\Phi_{[j_{1}\dots j_{N}]}
.

Let’s start from a generic state of the form (
A.7
), that is

\Psi=L_{m_{1}}^{(r_{1})}\dots L_{m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\cdot\bar{L}_{\bar{m}_{1}}^{(\bar{r}_{1})}\dots\bar{L}_{\bar{m}_{\bar{p}}}^{(\bar{r}_{\bar{p}})}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{-r-\bar{r}}[\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}}]\,,

(A.9)

where
r=r_{1}+\dots+r_{p}
and
\bar{r}=r_{1}+\dots+\bar{r}_{\bar{p}}
. Now we simply observe that

\displaystyle L_{0}^{(r)}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{s}[\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}}]=\overline{L}_{0}^{(r)}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{s}[\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}}]=\widehat{h}^{(r)}_{j_{1}\dots j_{N}}\,\mathcal{P}_{s+r}[\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}}]\,,

(A.10)

where

\widehat{h}^{(r)}_{j_{1}\dots j_{N}}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\omega^{ar}\,h_{j_{a}}\,.

(A.11)

The point is that in the non-diagonal sector there exists
a,b
such that
h_{j_{a}}\neq h_{j_{b}}
, and therefore there exists an index
q\neq 0
such that
\widehat{h}^{(q)}_{j_{1}\dots j_{N}}\neq 0
. Hence, for any

(A.12)

and therefore

\mathcal{P}_{-r-\bar{r}}[\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}}]\propto\left(L_{0}^{(q)}\right)^{[[-rq^{-1}]]}\left(\overline{L}_{0}^{(q)}\right)^{[[-\bar{r}q^{-1}]]}\mathcal{P}_{0}(\phi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{j_{N}})

(A.13)

Thus, we can write
\Psi

descendant of
\Phi_{[j_{1}\dots j_{N}]}

\displaystyle\Psi\propto\underbrace{L_{m_{1}}^{(r_{1})}\dots L_{m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\left(L_{0}^{(q)}\right)^{[[-rq^{-1}]]}}_{\in A_{N}}\cdot\underbrace{\bar{L}_{\bar{m}_{1}}^{(\bar{r}_{1})}\dots\bar{L}_{\bar{m}_{\bar{p}}}^{(\bar{r}_{\bar{p}})}\left(\overline{L}_{0}^{(q)}\right)^{[[-\bar{r}q^{-1}]]}}_{\in\bar{A}_{N}}\,\Phi_{[j_{1}\dots j_{N}]}

It follows that any invariant operator in the untwisted, non-diagonal sector is an

\langle\sigma_{i}^{[k](r)}(z,\bar{z})\sigma_{j}^{[\ell](s)}(w,\bar{w})\rangle=\langle\sigma_{i}^{[k](r)}|\sigma_{j}^{[\ell](s)}\rangle\times|z-w|^{-2h_{\sigma_{i}^{[k](r)}}-2h_{\sigma_{j}^{[\ell](s)}}}\,.

(A.31)

This vanishes if
i\neq j
, because the modules of
\phi_{i}
and
\phi_{j}
are decoupled in the mother CFT.
This function also vanishes if
k+\ell\neq 0\mod N

For
i=j
and
k+\ell=0

\langle\sigma_{j}^{[k](r)}|\sigma_{j}^{[-k](s)}\rangle
is easily computed using the orbifold Virasoro commutation rules, which gives

\langle\sigma_{j}^{[k](r)}|\sigma_{j}^{[-k](s)}\rangle=\delta_{r+s,0}\,.

(A.32)

B.1
Three-point functions of twist operators

Let us first state a useful property of correlation functions, which actually derives from the orbifold Ward identity (
3.40
).

Property.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2},\mathcal{O}_{3}
be three operators of the orbifold CFT, with twist charges
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
, respectively, such that
k_{1}=k_{2}+k_{3}\mod N
. For any
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
and
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}
such that
q_{i}\in-k_{i}r/N+\mathbb{Z}
, and
q_{1}=q_{2}+q_{3}+1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[b_{n}\langle(L_{-q_{1}+n}^{(r)}\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\dagger})\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\mathcal{O}_{3}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\rangle-a_{n}\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\dagger}(L_{q_{2}+n}^{(r)}\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}})\mathcal{O}_{3}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\rangle+e^{i\pi q_{2}}b_{n}\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\dagger}\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}(L_{q_{3}+n}^{(r)}\mathcal{O}_{3}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}})\rangle\right]=0\,,

(B.1)

where the coefficients
a_{n},b_{n}
are given by

a_{n}=\frac{(q_{3}+1)q_{3}\dots(q_{3}-n+2)}{n!}\,,\qquad b_{n}=(-1)^{n}\,\frac{(q_{2}+1)q_{2}\dots(q_{2}-n+2)}{n!}\,.

(B.2)

Note that, if
q_{3}=-1
(resp.
q_{2}=-1
) then
a_{n}=\delta_{n0}
(resp.
a_{n}=\delta_{n0}
). For any value of
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}
, the sum in (
B.1
) is finite, because, for any
\mathcal{O}_{i}
, there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that
L_{\pm q_{i}+n}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{i}=0
when
n\geq n_{0}
.

Proof.

Consider the integral,

\oint dz\,(z-1)^{q_{2}+1}\,z^{q_{3}+1}\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\dagger}(\infty)T^{(r)}(z)\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}(1)\mathcal{O}_{3}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}(0)\rangle\,,

(B.3)

where the contour encloses the points
z=0
and
z=1
. Since the prefactor
(z-1)^{q_{2}+1}\,z^{q_{3}+1}
exactly compensates the monodromy of
T^{(r)}(z)
around each of the
\mathcal{O}_{i}
’s, the integrand is single-valued, and hence the contour integral is closed. Using the Cauchy theorem, we can split the latter into a contour enclosing only
z=0
, and a contour enclosing only
z=1
. The Taylor expansions

(1+u)^{q_{3}+1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}u^{n}\,,\qquad(1-u)^{q_{2}+1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}u^{n}\,,\qquad\text{for }|u|<1\,,

(B.4)

allow us to write the integrand for each contour as a power series in
z
or
(z-1)
, which in turn yields (
B.1
).

\blacksquare

The goal of this section is to describe a recursive algorithm for the computation of OPE coefficients of the form

\langle\sigma_{k}^{{\dagger}(-t)}\,\sigma_{i}^{(r)}\,(L_{-m_{1}}^{(r_{1})}\dots L_{-m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\cdot\Phi_{j}^{(s)})\rangle\,,

(B.5)

where
p\geq 1
, and
m_{1},\dots,m_{p}
are integers, with
m_{1}\geq\dots\geq m_{p}\geq 1
, and
r_{1}+\dots+r_{p}=0\mod N
,
in terms of the quantities

\langle\sigma_{k}\,\sigma_{i}\,(L_{-1}^{(u_{1})}\dots L_{-1}^{(u_{q})}\cdot\bar{L}_{-1}^{(\bar{u}_{1})}\dots\bar{L}_{-1}^{(\bar{u}_{\bar{q}})}\cdot\Phi_{j})\rangle\,,

(B.6)

where
u_{1}+\dots+u_{q}=-(\bar{u}_{1}+\dots+\bar{u}_{\bar{q}})=r+s-t\mod N

We start with the case

1.

If

B.7

B.6

2.

If

are primary, and
\mathcal{O}_{3}
is any scaling operator. Here,

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{k}^{\dagger}\sigma_{i}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}(L_{-m_{1}}^{(0)}\cdot L_{-m_{2}}^{(r_{2})}\dots L_{-m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\cdot\Phi_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=(m_{1}h_{\sigma_{i}}-h_{\sigma_{k}}+h_{\Phi_{j}}+m^{\prime})\,\langle\sigma_{k}^{\dagger}\sigma_{i}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}(L_{-m_{2}}^{(r_{2})}\dots L_{-m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\cdot\Phi_{j})\rangle\,,

(p-1)

3.

B.1

First, we introduce a useful definition: for any operator
\mathcal{O}

By convention, we take

b_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}
such that

Nb_{n}-na_{n}=1\,,\qquad Nb^{\prime}_{n}-(1+a_{n})a^{\prime}_{n}=1\,.

(C.20)

A simple calculation gives

q_{n}[q_{1+a_{n}}(\tau)]=\frac{(n-1)\tau-b_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{N\tau-a^{\prime\prime}_{n}}\,,

(C.21)

where
a^{\prime\prime}_{n}=Nb^{\prime}_{n}-a_{n}a^{\prime}_{n}
and
b^{\prime\prime}_{n}=nb^{\prime}_{n}-b_{n}a^{\prime}_{n}
, and hence
Nb^{\prime\prime}_{n}-(n-1)a^{\prime\prime}_{n}=1
. Hence, if
0<a^{\prime\prime}_{n}<N
we have
Q_{n}Q_{1+a_{n}}=Q_{n-1}
(otherwise we can always shift
a^{\prime\prime}_{n}
by a multiple of
N
). This yields the identity (
C.6
).

For
n=1
, we have
[[-1]]=N-1
, and

q_{1}(\tau)=\frac{\tau-1}{N(\tau-1)+1}=(s\circ t^{-N}\circ s\circ t^{-1})(\tau)\,,

(C.22)

which yields

P_{1}=T^{-1/N}ST^{-N}ST^{-1/N}\,,\qquad P_{-1}=T^{1/N}ST^{N}ST^{1/N}\,.

(C.23)

A recursion relation for the matrices
P_{n}

In this section, we shall reproduce and prove a recursion relation for the
P_{n}
matrices found in
[
68
]
, which provides their decomposition into
S
and
T
matrices after a relatively small number of recursive steps.

The key idea is to relate
P_{n}
matrices defined at
different N
. We will first make the dependence on
N
of the
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
matrices explicit through the notation:

P_{n}\rightarrow P_{n|N}\quad Q_{n}\rightarrow Q_{n|N}

(C.24)

We now consider the product of matrices

T^{N/n}ST^{n/N}P_{n|N}T^{1/(nN)}

(C.25)

We substitute in the above the definition of (
4.20
) in the above to find:

T^{N/n}S\,T^{n/N}P_{n|N}T^{1/(nN)}=T^{N/n}S\,Q_{n|N}T^{b_{n}/n}

(C.26)

Now the
S
matrix is given by:

S=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)

(C.27)

while the
Q_{n|N}
matrix is given by:

Q_{n|N}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}n&-b_{n}\\
N&-a_{n}\end{array}\right)

(C.28)

with
Nb_{n}-na_{n}=1
. Acting from the left on the above with the
S
matrix, one finds:

S\,Q_{n|N}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}N&-a_{n}\\
-n&b_{n}\end{array}\right)=Q_{N|-n}

(C.29)

since

Nb_{n}-na_{n}=1
is equivalent to
(-n)a_{n}-b_{n}N=1
. Next, we have by the definition in (
4.20
) that:

P_{N|-n}=T^{N/n}Q_{n|N}T^{a_{n}/n}

(C.30)

so that we have effectively established a recursive relation:

\boxed{P_{n|N}=T^{-n/N}ST^{-N/n}P_{N|-n}T^{-1/(nN)}}

(C.31)

for which the recursion ends with a term
P_{0|N}=P_{0|1}\equiv S
.

Example for
N=5

Suppose we want to calculate the
P_{2|5}
matrix. We use the recursion relation to find:

P_{2|5}=T^{-2/5}ST^{-5/2}P_{5|-2}T^{-1/10}

(C.32)

Noting that
P_{5|-2}=P_{1|-2}
, and using once more the recursion relation, we find:

P_{1|-2}=T^{1/2}ST^{2}P_{-2|1}T^{1/2}

(C.33)

But
P_{-2|1}=P_{0|1}=S
, so, one can put everything together to find:

P_{2|5}=T^{-2/5}ST^{-2}ST^{2}ST^{2/5}

(C.34)

C.2
The orbifold modular
\mathcal{S}
-matrix

In this section, we will show that the solution for the orbifold
\mathcal{S}
-matrix we have found satisfies the properties (
4.23
)-(
4.26
). In our calculations, the
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
matrices (and any products made with them), are evaluated “block-by-block”, in the sense that for each proof we provide here, the external indices are restricted to correspond to only one type of operator – non-diagonal (ND), diagonal(D), twisted (T). For example, checking unitarity in the D-D block means proving that:

(\mathcal{S}\mathcal{S}^{\dagger})_{i^{(r)},j^{(s)}}=\delta_{i^{(r)},j^{(s)}}\,,

(C.35)

for generic diagonal operator labels
i^{(r)},j^{(s)}
.

We will only present in this section some of the more technical demonstrations, since the rest can be quickly reproduced by the interested reader through similar or simpler arguments.

C.2.1
Symmetry of
\mathcal{S}

As a warm-up, let’s prove the symmetry of the
\mathcal{S}
matrix. The check is straightforward everywhere but in the TT block, where we need to employ the property (
C.3
) to find:

\mathcal{S}^{t}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{[\ell](s)}}=\frac{\omega^{-ks-\ell r}\,(P_{k.\ell^{-1}})_{ji}}{N}=\frac{\omega^{-ks-\ell r}\,(P_{\ell.k^{-1}})_{ij}}{N}=\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{[\ell](s)}}\,.

(C.36)

C.2.2
Unitarity of
\mathcal{S}

We present in this section the more involved proofs for the unitarity of
\mathcal{S}
in the DD and TT blocks.

In the DD block.

We want to check the property:

\sum_{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{i^{(r)},\alpha}\mathcal{S}^{*}_{i^{\prime(r^{\prime})},\alpha}=\delta_{(i,r),(i^{\prime},r^{\prime})}\,,

(C.37)

where the sum in
\alpha
runs over all the invariant primary operators in the orbifold.
We first consider the sum over ND operators :

\sum_{J=[j_{1},...,j_{N}]}\mathcal{S}_{(i,r),J}\mathcal{S}^{*}_{J,(i^{\prime},r^{\prime})}\,,

(C.38)

which, using the known form of the S-matrix elements in this sector, is:

\sum_{J}S_{i,j_{1}}\dots S_{i,j_{N}}\cdot S_{i^{\prime},j_{1}}\dots S_{i^{\prime},j_{N}}\,,

(C.39)

where the sum is over equivalence classes
J
of the
N
-tuples
(j_{1},\cdots,j_{N})
under
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, with the exception of
N
-tuples for which the indices are equal. This can be expressed as:

\sum_{J=[j_{1},...,j_{N}]}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{(j_{1},\cdots,j_{N})}-\sum_{(j,\cdots,j)}\right)\,,

(C.40)

where the first sum on the RHS is over all
N
-tuples
(j_{1},\dots j_{N})
. This means each
j_{i}
runs over the whole mother CFT spectrum so that the sum over the ND indices can be conveniently expressed as:

\sum_{J}\mathcal{S}_{i^{(r)},J}\mathcal{S}^{*}_{J,i^{\prime(r^{\prime})}}=\frac{\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}}{N}-\frac{\sum_{(j,r)}S_{i,j}^{N}S_{i^{\prime},j}^{N}}{N^{2}}

(C.41)

On the other hand, the sum over D operators is given by:

\sum_{j,s}\mathcal{S}_{i^{(r)},j^{(s)}}\mathcal{S}^{*}_{j^{(s)},i^{\prime(r^{\prime})}}=\frac{\sum_{j,r}S_{ij}^{N}S_{i^{\prime}j}^{N}}{N^{2}}

(C.42)

so that we are just left with checking that the sum over T operators satisfies

\sum_{j,s,k}\mathcal{S}_{i^{(r)},j^{[k](s)}}\mathcal{S}^{*}_{j^{[k](s)},i^{\prime(r^{\prime})}}=\frac{N\delta_{r,r^{\prime}}-1}{N}\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}

(C.43)

which is indeed the case.

In the TT block.

We want to prove in this section that:

\sum_{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},\alpha}\mathcal{S}^{*}_{\alpha,i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\delta_{r,r^{\prime}}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}

(C.44)

where
\alpha
runs once again over all invariant primary operators of the orbifold.

To start off, the sum over non-diagonal entries vanishes, since the
\mathcal{S}
-matrix entries indexed by a twisted operator and a non-diagonal operator vanish. On the other hand, the sum over diagonal field entries is easily calculated to be:

\sum_{(j,s)}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},(j,s)}\mathcal{S}^{*}_{(j,s),i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{(j,s)}w^{s(k-k^{\prime})}S_{ij}S_{ji^{\prime}}=\frac{\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}}{N}

(C.45)

Finally, we now consider the sum over T entries:

\sum_{j^{[t](s)}}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{[t](s)}}\mathcal{S}^{*}_{j^{[t](s)},i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}

(C.46)

We substitute our results and find:

\sum_{j,s,t}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{[t]s}}\,\mathcal{S}^{*}_{i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})},j^{[t](s)}}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,s,t}\omega^{-s(k-k^{\prime})-t(r-r^{\prime})}(\mathbf{P}_{tk^{-1}})_{ij}(\mathbf{P}^{\dagger}_{tk^{\prime-1}})_{ji^{\prime}}\,.

(C.47)

We now sum over
s
, to obtain a factor
\delta_{k^{-1},k^{\prime-1}}=\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}
.
Then, we perform the sum over
j
, and use the unitarity of the
P_{n}
matrices (
C.5
) to arrive at:

\sum_{j,s,t}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{[t]s}}\,\mathcal{S}^{*}_{i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})},j^{[t](s)}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t}\omega^{-t(r-r^{\prime})}\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}

(C.48)

Finally, we sum over
t
and find:

\sum_{j,s,t}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{[t]s}}\,\mathcal{S}^{*}_{i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})},j^{[t](s)}}=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}\delta_{r,r^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{N}\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}\,,

(C.49)

which, added to (
C.45
) gives the sought after result.

C.2.3
The relation
(\mathcal{ST})^{3}=\mathcal{C}

The constraint (
4.26
) on the modular data of the CFT is equivalent to:

\mathcal{STS}=\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}\mathcal{S}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}

(C.50)

As in the previous section, we will show that the relation above holds in the DD and TT blocks.

In the DD block.

We want to check the constraint:

\sum_{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{i^{(r)},\alpha}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,i^{\prime(r^{\prime})}}=\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}_{i^{(r)}}\mathcal{S}_{i^{(r)},i^{\prime(r^{\prime})}}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}_{i^{\prime(r^{\prime})}}

(C.51)

where the sum
\alpha
runs over all the invariant primary operator labels.

Substituting our expressions for the
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
matrices, we find that the sum over entries labelled by untwisted operators gives:

\sum_{\text{untwisted }\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{i^{(r)},\alpha}\,\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}\,\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,i^{\prime(r^{\prime})}}=\frac{1}{N}\left(STS\right)^{N}_{ii^{\prime}}

(C.52)

while the sum over twisted operators vanishes.
The RHS of (
C.51
) can also be conveniently written as:

\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}_{i^{(r)}}\mathcal{S}_{i^{(r)},i^{\prime(r^{\prime})}}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}_{i^{\prime(r^{\prime})}}=\frac{1}{N}\left(TST\right)_{i,i^{\prime}}^{N}

(C.53)

Since in the mother CFT the modular matrices are constrained by

STS=T^{\dagger}ST^{\dagger}

(C.54)

we find that the relation (
4.26
) is indeed satisfied in this block.

In the TT block.

We want to check the constraint:

\sum_{\alpha\in\{D,T\}}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},\alpha}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}=\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}_{i^{[k](r)}}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}_{i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}

(C.55)

where we only need to consider the sum
\alpha
running over the diagonal and twisted primary labels, since
\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},[j_{1}\dots j_{N}]}=0
.
The sum over diagonal operator labels on the LHS is swiftly calculated to be:

\sum_{j,s}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{(s)}}\mathcal{T}_{j^{(s)}}\mathcal{S}_{j^{(s)},i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}=\frac{1}{N}\left(ST^{N}S\right)_{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{k+k^{\prime},0}

(C.56)

and we use (
C.23
) to rewrite the above as:

\sum_{j,s}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{(s)}}\mathcal{T}_{j^{(s)}}\mathcal{S}_{j^{(s)},i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}=\frac{1}{N}\left(T^{-1/N}P_{-1}T^{-1/N}\right)_{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{k+k^{\prime},0}

(C.57)

Next, we want to evaluate the sum over twisted operator labels:

\sum_{j,s,t}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{[t](s)}}\mathcal{T}_{j^{[t](s)}}\mathcal{S}_{j^{[t](s)},i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{s,t}\omega^{-t(r+r^{\prime})-s(k+k^{\prime}-t)}(P_{tk^{-1}}T^{1/N}P_{k^{\prime}t^{-1}})_{ii^{\prime}}

(C.58)

We sum over
s
, to obtain a
\delta_{k+k^{\prime},t}
factor in the sum. If
k+k^{\prime}=0
, the expression vanishes since
t
runs from 1 to
N-1
. So, we obtain:

\sum_{j,s,t}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{[t](s)}}\mathcal{T}_{j^{[t](s)}}\mathcal{S}_{j^{[t](s)},i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}=\frac{(1-\delta_{k+k^{\prime},0})}{N}\omega^{-(k+k^{\prime})(r+r^{\prime})}(P_{(k+k^{\prime})k^{-1}}T^{1/N}P_{1-k(k+k^{\prime})^{-1}})_{ii^{\prime}}

(C.59)

Using the relation (
C.6
), the sum over twist fields becomes:

\sum_{j,s,t}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},j^{[t](s)}}\mathcal{T}_{j^{[t](s)}}\mathcal{S}_{j^{[t](s)},i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}=\frac{(1-\delta_{k+k^{\prime},0})}{N}\omega^{-(k+k^{\prime})(r+r^{\prime})}(T^{-1/N}P_{k^{\prime}k^{-1}}T^{-1/N})_{ii^{\prime}}

(C.60)

so that the LHS of (
C.55
) is just:

\sum_{\alpha\in\{D,T\}}\mathcal{S}_{i^{[k](r)},\alpha}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,i^{\prime[k^{\prime}](r^{\prime})}}=\frac{1}{N}\omega^{-(k+k^{\prime})(r+r^{\prime})}(T^{-1/N}P_{k^{\prime}k^{-1}}T^{-1/N})_{ii^{\prime}}

(C.61)

Substitution of our expressions for the modular data on the RHS of (
C.55
) completes the proof.

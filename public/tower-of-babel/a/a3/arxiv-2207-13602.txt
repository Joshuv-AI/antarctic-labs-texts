Abstract
. In the present paper we consider elliptic operators L = − div ( A ∇ ) L=-\divg(A\nabla) in a domain bounded by a chord-arc surface Γ \Gamma with small enough constant, and whose coefficients A A satisfy a weak form of the Dahlberg-Kenig-Pipher condition of approximation by constant coefficient matrices, with a small enough Carleson norm, and show that the elliptic measure with pole at infinity associated to L L is A ∞ A_{\infty} -absolutely continuous with respect to the surface measure on Γ \Gamma , with a small A ∞ A_{\infty} constant. In other words, we show that for relatively flat uniformly rectifiable sets and for operators with slowly oscillating coefficients the elliptic measure satisfies the A ∞ A_{\infty} condition with a small constant and the logarithm of the Poisson kernel has small oscillations.



Key words: elliptic measure,
A_{\infty}
, weak Dahlberg-Kenig-Pipher condition,
uniform rectiability, small constants.

1.
Introduction and main results

Recent developments have showed that for the class of Dahlberg-Kenig-Pipher operators the elliptic measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the Hausdorff measure on all uniformly rectifiable sets with some mild topological conditions (we will provide the relevant list of references below). The conditions on the operator and on the geometry are both essentially necessary and in fact, one can establish the equivalence of the aforementioned geometric features of the domain to the property of absolute continuity of harmonic measure (
[
AHM+20
]
).
The present paper focuses on the “small constant” version of these results, showing, roughly speaking, that for relatively flat domains and for slowly oscillating coefficients, the elliptic measure is
A_{\infty}
with respect to the Hausdorff measure with a small norm and that the oscillations of the logarithm of the Poissin kernel are small. This turned out to be surprisingly non-trivial primarily because the standard methods of work with Carleson measures, uniform rectifiability and related notions “bootstrap” and hence, enlarge the constants.

To be precise, in this paper we prove small
A_{\infty}
estimates for the elliptic measure, with pole at infinity,
of a divergence form elliptic operators
L=-\divg(A\nabla)
, with a matrix of coefficients
A

that satisfies a weak Dahlberg-Kenig-Pipher condition (see the definitions below)
with a small corresponding Carleson norm.
We will do this both in the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
and in any of the two
connected components
\Omega
bounded by a Chord-arc surface with small constant

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

Concerning the case of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}

[

]

That is, they assume in addition that the Carleson measure associated to

to say that if our result were not true on

Definition 1.10

(Weak DKP condition, vanishing weak DKP condition)
.

Let
A
be a
\mu_{0}
-elliptic matrix-valued function on
\Omega
.

•

We say that
A
satisfies the weak DKP condition with
constant

, when the measure
\mu
defined by

d\mu(x,r)=\alpha_{A}(x,r)^{2}\frac{d\sigma(x)dr}{r}

is a Carleson measure on
\Gamma\times(0,+\infty)
, with norm

\mathfrak{N}(A):=\left\|\alpha_{A}(x,r)^{2}\frac{d\sigma(x)dr}{r}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leq M,

(1.11)

and where we called
\sigma=\mathcal{H}^{d}_{|\Gamma}
the surface measure on
\Gamma
.

•

We say that
A
satisfies the vanishing weak DKP condition if
\mu
defined by

d\mu(x,r)=\alpha_{A}(x,r)^{2}\frac{d\sigma(x)dr}{r}

is a Carleson measure on
\Gamma\times(0,+\infty)
with norm vanishing trace.

The weak DKP condition is a weaker version of the celebrated Carleson condition popularized
by Dahlberg, Kenig, and Pipher (DKP), which
instead demands that
\widetilde{\alpha}_{A}(x,r)^{2}\frac{dxdr}{r}
satisfies a Carleson measure estimate, where

\widetilde{\alpha}_{A}(x,r)=r\sup_{(y,s)\in W(x,r)}|\nabla A(y,s)|.

In 1984, Dahlberg first introduced this condition, and conjectured that such a Carleson condition guarantees
the absolute continuity of the elliptic measure
with respect to the Lebesgue measure in the upper half-space. In 2001, Kenig and Pipher
[
KP01
]
proved Dahlberg’s conjecture. More precisely, they show that the DKP condition guarantees that the corresponding elliptic measure is
A_{\infty}
with respect to the surface measure in Lipschitz domains. Let
\mu
and
\nu
be two non-negative Borel measures on
\Gamma
. We say that
\mu\in A_{\infty}(\nu)
if there exists
C,\eta,\theta>0
such that for any surface ball
\Delta\subset\Gamma
and any
E\subset\Delta

\frac{\mu(E)}{\mu(\Delta)}\leq C\left(\frac{\nu(E)}{\nu(\Delta)}\right)^{\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\nu(E)}{\nu(\Delta)}\leq C\left(\frac{\mu(E)}{\mu(\Delta)}\right)^{\eta}.

(1.12)

We refer the readers to
[
CF74
]
for equivalent definitions of
A_{\infty}
.

The weak DKP condition was first introduced in
[
DLM21
]
,
in connection with the regularity of the Green function.
In the aforementioned recent work of Bortz, Toro and Zhao
[
BTZ22
]
,
the authors show that the weak DKP condition
is sufficient for

of the elliptic measure in the upper half-space (see Lemma
3.2
).

corresponds to the operator
L_{j}*=-\divg{A_{j}^{*}\nabla}
.

We could also have allowed modifications of
A_{j}
for
|x|
very large, but since this is not necessary for our construction, we will not do that.

Proof.

Notice that we allow
\rho_{j}
and

to depend wildly on

.
We prove the lemma by contradiction. Fix

and suppose that the statement is false; then
there exists a sequence of
\mu_{0}
-elliptic matrices
\left\{A_{j,k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, such that
A_{j,k}(x,t)=A_{j}(x,t)
for

,
\mathfrak{N}(A_{j,k})\leq C_{0}
, and

\left|\frac{\omega^{\infty}_{L_{j,k}}(E_{j})}{\omega_{L_{j,k}}^{\infty}(\Delta_{0})}-\frac{\sigma(E_{j})}{\sigma(\Delta_{0})}\right|<\frac{\delta_{0}}{2},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(3.18)

Let
X=(0,t_{0})
, with

to be chosen soon. Then by Lemma

(applied with

\left|\frac{\omega_{L_{j}}^{X}(E_{j})}{\omega_{L_{j}}^{X}(\Delta_{0})}-\frac{\omega_{L_{j}}^{\infty}(E_{j})}{\omega_{L_{j}}^{\infty}(\Delta_{0})}\right|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{t_{0}}\right)^{\gamma}=C{t_{0}}^{-\gamma},

where

and

.
Choose

so large (depending on

and

; then

\left|\frac{\omega_{L_{j}}^{X}(E_{j})}{\omega_{L_{j}}^{X}(\Delta_{0})}-\frac{\omega_{L_{j}}^{\infty}(E_{j})}{\omega_{L_{j}}^{\infty}(\Delta_{0})}\right|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{8}.

(3.19)

By the triangle inequality, (

) (recall that
\Delta_{j}=\Delta_{0}
)
and (

) yield

\left|\frac{\omega^{X}_{L_{j}}(E_{j})}{\omega_{L_{j}}^{X}(\Delta_{0})}-\frac{\sigma(E_{j})}{\sigma(\Delta_{0})}\right|\geq\frac{7}{8}\delta_{0},

(3.20)

while (

(3.21)

Now we want to let
k
tend to

. We claim that for any bounded Borel set

\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega^{X}_{L_{j,k}}(E)=\omega^{X}_{L_{j}}(E).

(3.22)

Once we prove this, we let

)
and get a contradiction with (

So Lemma

follows from the following lemma (proved later).
∎

Lemma 3.23
.

Let

be a
\mu_{0}
-elliptic operator, let

be

-elliptic operator that satisfies

The weak convergence of the elliptic measures, on the other hand, does not require anything for the operator apart from ellipticity. In fact, it is well known that assuming

A_{k}
and
A
elliptic, then for any fixed

[

3.3.
We neutralize the coefficients of
A_{j}
away from a band

Now we continue with a given

and construct a matrix

that coincides with
A_{j}
on the large band
\big\{(x,t)\,;\,\frac{\rho_{j}}{2}<t\leq 2M_{j}\big\}

and with the identity matrix outside of a much larger band (so that further gluing will be easier). The main point of the construction is to do it without increasing the norm
\mathfrak{N}(A_{j})
too much.

Definition 3.25

(The matrix
A_{j}^{*}
)
.

Let
A_{j}
be the matrix that we fixed earlier. Let
\rho_{j}
and
M_{j}
be as in
Lemma
3.16
. Set
N_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}=2^{j}
and denote by
I
is the identity
matrix of size
d+1
. We define
A_{j}^{*}
by

A_{j}^{*}(x,t):=\begin{cases}I,\qquad t>2^{N_{j}}M_{j}\,\text{ or }t\leq 2^{-N_{j}}\rho_{j},\\
\frac{l}{N_{j}}I+\left(1-\frac{l}{N_{j}}\right)A_{j}(x,t),\qquad 2^{l}M_{j}<t\leq 2^{l+1}M_{j},\text{ for }1\leq l\leq N_{j}-1,\\
A_{j}(x,t),\qquad\frac{\rho_{j}}{2}<t\leq 2M_{j},\\
\frac{l}{N_{j}}I+\left(1-\frac{l}{N_{j}}\right)A_{j}(x,t),\qquad 2^{-l-1}\rho_{j}<t\leq 2^{-l}\rho_{j},\text{ for }1\leq l\leq N_{j}-1.\end{cases}

Notice that we only care about the values of
t
here, and we do not modify
A_{j}(x,t)

for
|x|
large, because this is not needed for the construction.
We need a very large interval around
[\frac{\rho_{j}}{2},2M_{j}]
because we want the coefficients
to vary slowly, so that some
\ell^{2}
-norm is small. We now evaluate the
\alpha
-numbers for
A_{j}^{*}
.

Lemma 3.26
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\alpha_{A_{j}^{*}}(x,r)\begin{cases}=0,\qquad r\leq 2^{-N_{j}}\rho_{j}\text{ or }r\geq 2^{N_{j}+1}M_{j},\\
\leq C\varepsilon_{j},\qquad 2^{-N_{j}}\rho_{j}\leq r\leq\rho_{j}\text{ or }2M_{j}\leq r\leq 2^{N_{j}+1}M_{j},\\
=\alpha_{A_{j}}(x,r),\qquad\rho_{j}<r\leq 2M_{j}.\end{cases}

Here, the constant
C
depends only on
d
and
\mu_{0}
.

Proof.

Let us discuss four cases.

Case 1.

r\leq 2^{-N_{j}}\rho_{j}\text{ or }r\geq 2^{N_{j}+1}M_{j}
.

In this case, observe that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

W(x,r)=\Delta(x,r)\times(r/2,r]\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\left\{(0,2^{-N_{j}\rho_{j}}]\cup(2^{N_{j}}M_{j},\infty)\right\}.

By the definition of
A_{j}^{*}
,
A_{j}^{*}(x,t)=I
for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\left\{(0,2^{-N_{j}\rho_{j}}]\cup(2^{N_{j}}M_{j},\infty)\right\}
. So we can just take
A_{0}=I
in the definition of
\alpha_{A_{j}^{*}}
and get that

\alpha_{A_{j}^{*}}(x,r)=0\qquad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,r\leq 2^{-N_{j}}\rho_{j}\text{ or }r\geq 2^{N_{j}+1}M_{j}.

Case 2.

2^{-N_{j}}\rho_{j}\leq r\leq\rho_{j}
.

Let
k
be such that
2^{-k-1}\rho_{j}<r\leq 2^{-k}\rho_{j}
; then
0\leq k\leq N_{j}-1
and for

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\alpha_{A_{j}^{*}}(x,r)^{2}\\
\leq\frac{1}{\left|W(x,r)\right|}\int_{\Delta(x,r)}\int_{r/2}^{2^{-k-1}\rho_{j}}\left|\frac{k+1}{N_{j}}I+\left(1-\frac{k+1}{N_{j}}\right)A_{j}(y,t)-A_{0}\right|^{2}dtdy\\
+\frac{1}{\left|W(x,r)\right|}\int_{\Delta(x,r)}\int_{2^{-k-1}\rho_{j}}^{r}\left|\frac{k}{N_{j}}I+\left(1-\frac{k}{N_{j}}\right)A_{j}(y,t)-A_{0}\right|^{2}dtdy\\
=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where we use the constant matrix

A_{0}=\frac{k}{N_{j}}I+\left(1-\frac{k}{N_{j}}\right)\fiint_{T(x,2r)}A_{j}(Z)dZ.

(3.27)

Inserting the definition of
A_{0}
into the integrals, we obtain that

I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{\left|W(x,r)\right|}\int_{\Delta(x,r)}\int_{r/2}^{2^{-k-1}\rho_{j}}\left(\frac{1}{N_{j}}\right)^{2}\left(\left|I\right|+\left|A_{j}(y,t)\right|^{2}\right)dtdy\\
+\frac{1}{\left|W(x,r)\right|}\int_{\Delta(x,r)}\int_{r/2}^{2^{-k-1}\rho_{j}}\left(1-\frac{k}{N_{j}}\right)^{2}\Big|A_{j}(y,t)-\fiint_{T(x,2r)}A_{j}(Z)dZ\Big|^{2}dtdy\\
\leq CN_{j}^{-2}+\frac{1}{\left|W(x,r)\right|}\int_{\Delta(x,r)}\int_{r/2}^{2^{-k-1}\rho_{j}}\Big|A_{j}(y,t)-\fiint_{T(x,2r)}A_{j}(Z)dZ\Big|^{2}dtdy\\
\leq C\varepsilon_{j}^{2}+C\fiint_{Y\in T(x,2r)}\Big|A_{j}(Y)-\fiint_{T(x,2r)}A_{j}(Z)dZ\Big|^{2}dtdy.

At this point we find it convenient to use an observation of
[
DLM21
]
.
Set, for any elliptic matrix-valued function
A
and
(x,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
,

\gamma_{A}(x,r)=\inf_{A_{0}\in\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\mu_{0})}\bigg\{\fiint_{(y,s)\in T(x,r)}|A(y,s)-A_{0}|^{2}\bigg\}^{1/2}.

(3.28)

Notice that we integrate on
T(x,r)
, while in the definition (
1.9
)
of
\alpha_{A}(x,r)
, we only integrated on the Whitney box
W(x,r)
, so it seems that

\gamma_{A}(x,r)
should be harder to control. Yet we have the following.

Lemma 3.29

(
[
DLM21
]
, Remark 4.22)
.

Suppose that
A
is a
\mu_{0}
-elliptic matrix-valued function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Then for every surface ball
\Delta_{0}
,

\left\|\gamma_{A}(x,r)^{2}\,\frac{dx\,dr}{r}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{0})}\leq C\left\|\alpha_{A}(x,r)^{2}\,\frac{dx\,dr}{r}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}(3\Delta_{0})},

and

\gamma_{A}(x,r)^{2}\leq C\left\|\alpha_{A}(x,r)^{2}\,\frac{dx\,dr}{r}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}(3\Delta_{0})}\text{for all }(x,r)\in T_{\Delta_{0}},

(3.30)

where
C
only depends on dimension.

Return to
I_{1}
; the estimate above yields

I_{1}\leq C\varepsilon_{j}^{2}+C\gamma_{A_{j}}(x,2r)^{2}
, and now
(
3.30
) implies that
I_{1}\leq C\varepsilon_{j}^{2}+C\mathfrak{N}(A_{j})\leq C\varepsilon_{j}^{2}

(by (
3.4
)).

The estimate for
I_{2}
is easier, as most of the terms in the integral are cancelled. Again by (
3.30
),

I_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\left|W(x,r)\right|}\int_{\Delta(x,r)}\int_{2^{-k-1}\rho_{j}}^{r}\bigg|A_{j}(y,t)-\fiint_{T(x,2r)}A_{j}(Z)dZ\bigg|^{2}dtdy\\
\leq C\gamma_{A_{j}}(x,2r)^{2}\leq C\varepsilon_{j}^{2}.

So we have proved that
\alpha_{A_{j}^{*}}(x,r)^{2}\leq C\varepsilon_{j}^{2}
for any

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
when

2^{-N_{j}}\rho_{j}\leq r\leq\rho_{j}
.

Case 3.

2M_{j}\leq r\leq 2^{N_{j}+1}M_{j}
.

In this case,
\alpha_{A_{j}^{*}}(x,r)
can be estimated as in Case 2.
More precisely, write
2^{k}M_{j}<r\leq 2^{k+1}M_{j}
for some
1\leq k\leq N_{j}
. Then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\alpha_{A_{j}^{*}}(x,r)^{2}\\
\leq\frac{1}{\left|W(x,r)\right|}\int_{\Delta(x,r)}\int_{r/2}^{2^{k}M_{j}}\left|\frac{k-1}{N_{j}}I+\left(1-\frac{k-1}{N_{j}}\right)A_{j}(y,t)-A_{0}\right|^{2}dtdy\\
+\frac{1}{\left|W(x,r)\right|}\int_{\Delta(x,r)}\int_{2^{k}M_{j}}^{r}\left|\frac{k}{N_{j}}I+\left(1-\frac{k}{N_{j}}\right)A_{j}(y,t)-A_{0}\right|^{2}dtdy,

where
A_{0}
is the same constant matrix as in (
3.27
).
A straightforward computation shows that the first term on the right-hand side is bounded by

\frac{C}{N_{j}^{2}}+C\gamma_{A_{j}}(x,2r)^{2}\leq C\varepsilon_{j}^{2}.

The second term is bounded by
C\gamma_{A_{j}}(x,2r)^{2}
, and thus also bounded by
C\varepsilon_{j}^{2}
.

Case 4.

\rho_{j}<r\leq 2M_{j}
.

One only needs to observe that in this case,

W(x,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Big[\frac{\rho_{j}}{2},2M_{j}\Big]\qquad\text{for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

But
A_{j}^{*}(x,t)=A_{j}(x,t)
for

. So
\alpha_{A_{j}^{*}}(x,r)=\alpha_{A_{j}}(x,r)
.
∎

We glue the

Now we are ready to a function

Definition 3.31

(The matrix

)
.

Let

and

.
Let

Remark 3.34

The different regions

(3.35)

Also, by tracking the region where

\alpha_{A^{*}}(x,r)=\alpha_{I}(x,r)=0\\

(3.36)

Lemma 3.37
.

The matrix
A^{*}
satisfies the vanishing weak DKP condition.

Proof.

Set

\widehat{\alpha}(r_{0})=\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{\left|\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})\right|}\iint_{T(x_{0},r_{0})}\alpha_{A^{*}}(x,r)^{2}\frac{dxdr}{r}

for

.

(3.39)

Fix any

and

\lambda_{k}2^{N_{k}+1}M_{k}\leq r_{0}\leq\lambda_{k-1}2^{-N_{k-1}}\rho_{k-1}\qquad\text{for some }k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\,k\geq 2.

\alpha_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}(x,r)=\alpha_{A_{j}^{*}}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda_{j}},\frac{r}{\lambda_{j}}\right)\qquad\text{for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,r>0.

\lambda_{j}2^{-N_{j}}\rho_{j}\leq r\leq\lambda_{j}2^{N_{j}+1}M_{j}

3.43
) and (
3.44
) imply that

\alpha_{A^{*}}(x,r)=\alpha_{A_{j}^{*}}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda_{j}},\frac{r}{\lambda_{j}}\right)\qquad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\lambda_{j}2^{-N_{j}}\rho_{j}\leq r\leq\lambda_{j}2^{N_{j}+1}M_{j},\quad j\geq 1.

By Lemma
3.26
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\alpha_{A^{*}}(x,r)\begin{cases}\leq C\varepsilon_{j},\qquad\lambda_{j}2^{-N_{j}}\rho_{j}\leq r\leq\lambda_{j}\rho_{j}\text{ or }2\lambda_{j}M_{j}\leq r\leq\lambda_{j}2^{N_{j}+1}M_{j},\\

J_{1}\leq C\int_{\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})}\varepsilon_{j}^{2}N_{j}dx\leq C\varepsilon_{j}\left|\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})\right|,

J_{2}=\int_{\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})}\int_{\lambda_{j}\rho_{j}}^{2\lambda_{j}M_{j}}\alpha_{A_{j}}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda_{j}},\frac{r}{\lambda_{j}}\right)^{2}\frac{drdx}{r}=\lambda_{j}^{d}\int_{\Delta\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\lambda_{j}},\frac{r_{0}}{\lambda_{j}}\right)}\int_{\rho_{j}}^{2M_{j}}\alpha_{A_{j}}(y,s)^{2}\frac{dsdy}{s}.

\iint_{T(x_{0},r_{0})}\alpha_{A^{*}}(x,r)^{2}\frac{dxdr}{r}\leq C\sum_{j\geq k}\left(\varepsilon_{j}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\right)\left|\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})\right|\leq C\left|\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})\right|\sum_{j\geq k}2^{-j}\\

Next consider the case when

\lambda_{k}2^{-N_{k}}\rho_{k}\leq r_{0}\leq\lambda_{k}2^{N_{k}+1}M_{k}\qquad\text{for some }k>0.

(3.46)

+\int_{\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})}\int_{\lambda_{k}2^{-N_{k}}\rho_{k}}^{r_{0}}\alpha_{A^{*}}(x,r)^{2}\frac{drdx}{r}=:\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}J_{1}^{\prime}\right)+J_{2}^{\prime}.

Observe that

\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}J_{1}^{\prime}=\int_{\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{k+1}2^{N_{k+1}+1}M_{k+1}}\alpha_{A^{*}}(x,r)^{2}\frac{drdx}{r}.

So the previous case applies, and

\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}J_{1}^{\prime}\leq C2^{-k-1}\left|\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})\right|.

Turning to
J_{2}^{\prime}
, one can discuss 3 situations:

(1)

\lambda_{k}2^{-N_{k}}\rho_{k}\leq r_{0}\leq\lambda_{k}\rho_{k}
;

(2)

\lambda_{k}\rho_{k}<r_{0}\leq 2\lambda_{k}M_{k}
;

(3)

2\lambda_{k}M_{k}<r_{0}\leq\lambda_{k}2^{N_{k}+1}M_{k}
.

Using our estimates (
3.45
) for
\alpha_{A^{*}}(x,r)
, one can show as in the previous case that in all situations, we have that
J_{2}\leq C2^{-k}\left|\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})\right|
. Therefore, (
3.41
) holds for any
r_{0}
that satisfies (
3.40
) or (
3.46
). Since
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is arbitrary, this implies that
\mathfrak{N}(A^{*})\leq C
. Moreover, since

\lambda_{k-1}2^{-N_{k-1}}\rho_{k-1}\to 0
and
\lambda_{k}2^{N_{k}+1}M_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, (
3.41
) implies (
3.39
), as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.47
.

Let
A_{j}
,
L_{j}=-\divg{A_{j}\nabla}
, and
E_{j}\subset Q_{0}
be as fixed before, such that (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) hold.
Let the
\lambda_{j}
,
A^{*}
, and
L_{*}
be
as in Definition

. Then

\left|\frac{\omega^{\infty}_{L_{*}}(\lambda_{j}E_{j})}{\omega_{L_{*}}^{\infty}(\lambda_{j}\Delta_{0})}-\frac{\sigma(\lambda_{j}E_{j})}{\sigma(\lambda_{j}\Delta_{0})}\right|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2},\qquad j\geq 1.

(3.48)

Proof.

Recall that
\sigma
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This lemma will be an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.16
and the
rescaling property of the elliptic measure with pole at infinity.
In fact, for any fixed
j\geq 1
, set

\widetilde{A}(x,t):=A^{*}(\lambda_{j}x,\lambda_{j}t)=A^{*}\big(T_{\lambda_{j}}^{0}(X)\big)

where we use the notation
3.6
for the linear transformation, and
X=(x,t)
.
Then for this fixed
j
, the definitions of
A^{*}
and
A_{j}^{*}
assert that

\widetilde{A}(x,t)=A_{j}(x,t)\qquad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\rho_{j}\leq t\leq M_{j}.

Recall that our first move was to take
\Delta_{j}=\Delta_{0}
.
Applying Lemma
3.16
to
\widetilde{A}^{*}
,
one gets that

\left|\frac{\omega^{\infty}_{\widetilde{L}}(E_{j})}{\omega_{\widetilde{L}}^{\infty}(\Delta_{0})}-\frac{\sigma(E_{j})}{\sigma(\Delta_{0})}\right|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2},

where
\widetilde{L}=-\divg\widetilde{A}\nabla
. By Lemma
3.11
,

\frac{\omega_{\widetilde{L}}^{\infty}(E_{j})}{\omega_{\widetilde{L}}^{\infty}(\Delta_{0})}=\frac{\omega_{L_{*}}^{\infty}(\lambda_{j}E_{j})}{\omega_{L_{*}}^{\infty}(\lambda_{j}\Delta_{0})}.

So (
3.48
) follows.
∎

To summarize, we showed that if Theorem
1.17

is not true in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
, then we can construct an elliptic operator

that satisfies the vanishing weak DKP condition,
a sequence of numbers
\left\{\lambda_{j}\right\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}

contained in the unit ball

such that (

) holds.

On the other hand, Lemma
3.1
says that
\omega_{\widetilde{L}_{*}}^{\infty}

is absolutely continuous with respect to
\sigma

such that

.
The desired contradiction follows at once from the following lemma,
which will be proved in Section

.

Lemma 3.49
.

Let
\omega
be a positive locally finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, suppose that it is absolutely
continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\sigma
, and denote by

its Radon-Nikodym density.
Assume

. Then for any

, there exists

such that for any ball

with

,

\left|\frac{\omega(E)}{\omega(\Delta)}-\frac{\sigma(E)}{\sigma(\Delta)}\right|<\delta.

(3.50)

This completes our proof of Theorem
1.17
in

, modulo
Lemmas
3.23
and
3.49
.

4.
Small Lipschitz graphs

In this section we use a change of variable to prove Theorem
1.17
in when

\Gamma
is the graph of a Lipschitz function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, with small Lipschitz constant.
More precisely, we assume that

||\nabla\varphi||_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon,

(4.1)

set

\Gamma=\big\{(x,\varphi(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\,;\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\big\},

(4.2)

and denote by
\Omega
the connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus\Gamma

that lies above
\Gamma
. We then let
A
and
L
be as in Theorem
1.17
,
and we need to show that if
\varepsilon
is small enough, depending on
\delta
,
d
,
and the ellipticity constant
\mu_{0}
, the associated elliptic measure
\omega_{L}^{\infty}
lies in

A_{\infty}(\sigma,\delta)
.

We now define a mapping from
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
to
\Omega
which is the same as the one that is used in
[
KP01
]
, originally due to Dahlberg, Kenig, and Stein.
For
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
, let
\eta_{t}(x)=t^{-d}\eta(x/t)
, where
\eta
is a nonnegative radial

C^{\infty}
function supported in
\left\{\left|x\right|\leq 1/2\right\}
and such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta=1
.
Define a mapping

\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}\to\Omega,\quad\rho(x,t)=(x,c_{0}t+F(x,t))

(4.3)

with
F(x,t)=\eta_{t}*\varphi(x)=\int\eta(z)\varphi(x-tz)dz
,
and
c_{0}=1+c_{d}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{\infty}
, where

c_{d}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\eta(y)\right|\left|y\right|dy.

(4.4)

This choice of
c_{0}
guarantees that
\rho
is a one-to-one
bi-Lipschitz mapping of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
onto
\Omega
. In fact, we have

\nabla\rho(x,t)=\begin{pmatrix}I&\nabla_{x}F(x,t)\\
0&c_{0}+\partial_{t}F(x,t)\end{pmatrix},

(4.5)

and

\det\nabla\rho(x,t)=c_{0}+\partial_{t}F(x,t)
.
Since

\left|\partial_{t}F(x,t)\right|=\left|-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(z)z\cdot(\nabla\varphi)(x-tz)dz\right|\leq c_{d}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(4.6)

we get that

1\leq\det\nabla\rho(x,t)\leq 1+2c_{d}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{\infty}.

(4.7)

Incidentally, we have that

\left|\nabla_{x}F(x,t)\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(z)(\nabla\varphi)(x-zt)dz\right|\leq c_{d}^{\prime}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(4.8)

where
c_{d}^{\prime}=\int\left|\eta(y)\right|dy
.

Another important property of the mapping
\rho
is that
\left|\nabla^{2}\rho(x,t)\right|^{2}t\,dxdt

is a Carleson measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. This property has been mentioned in
[
KP01
]
, but no proof
nor an estimate for the Carleson norm has been given.
We give the control of the Carleson norm and sketch the easy proof.

Lemma 4.9
.

For
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the measure
\mu
defined by the density

d\mu(x,t)=\left|\nabla_{x,t}^{2}F(x,t)\right|^{2}t\,dxdt

is a Carleson measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
, and

\left\|\mu\right\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{BMO(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

where the constant
C
depends only
\eta
and
d
.

Sketch of Proof.

The lemma follows from a Carleson measure characterization of
BMO
functions
(see e.g.
[
Gra14
]
Theorem 3.3.8). In fact, all the second derivatives of
F(x,t)
can be expressed
in the form of
t^{-1}\theta_{t}*\nabla\varphi(x)
, where
\theta_{t}(x)=t^{-d}\theta(x/t)
satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\theta(x)dx=0
,

\left|\theta(x)\right|\leq C(1+\left|x\right|)^{-n-\delta}
for some
C
,
\delta\in(0,\infty)
, and

\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\hat{\theta}(s\xi)\frac{ds}{s}\right|<\infty.

Therefore,

\left\|\left|\theta_{t}*\nabla\varphi(x)\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{BMO(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

which gives the desired estimate.
∎

One can check
(see for instance, in a slightly different context, (1.42) and its proof in Lemma 6.17 in
[
DFM19
]
)
that if
u
is a solution of
Lu=-\divg{A\nabla u}=0
in
\Omega
, then
v=u\circ\rho
is a solution of
\widetilde{L}v=-\divg{\widetilde{A}\nabla v}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
, where

\widetilde{A}(x,t)=\det(\nabla\rho(x,t))(\nabla\rho(x,t)^{-1})^{T}A(\rho(x,t))\nabla\rho(x,t)^{-1}.

(4.10)

It will be useful to write out the matrix
\widetilde{A}(x,t)
. A simple computation shows that

\widetilde{A}(x,t)=\begin{pmatrix}I&\mathbf{0}\\
-\nabla_{x}F(x,t)&1\end{pmatrix}A(\rho(x,t))\begin{pmatrix}I&-\frac{\nabla_{x}F(x,t)}{c_{0}+\partial_{t}F(x,t)}\\
0&(c_{0}+\partial_{t}F(x,t))^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\\
=:P(x,t)A(\rho(x,t))Q(x,t).

(4.11)

Lemma 4.12
.

If
\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{100(1+c_{d})}
, where

is as in (
4.4
), then for
\widetilde{A}
defined in (

\mathfrak{N}(\widetilde{A})\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{\infty}^{2}+C\mathfrak{N}(A),

where
C
depends on
d
and the ellipticity constant of

Proof.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, let us use the notation

W(x,r)=\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x,r)\times(5r/6,r)
for Whitney regions in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
, where
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x,r)=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\left|y-x\right|<r\right\}
. Notice that they are slightly smaller than the Whitney regions defined in (
3.3
),
but as we said earlier, this change is insignificant in terms of Theorems
1.17
and
1.19
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
.
We denote by
W_{\Omega}(x_{\rho},r)
the Whitney
region
\left\{Y\in\Omega\cap B(x_{\rho},r):\dist(Y,\Gamma)>3r/10\right\}
in
\Omega
, where
x_{\rho}=(x,\varphi(x))\in\Gamma
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Notice that we replace the constant
1/2
in (
1.2
) with
3/10
, but we know that this does not matter.
It will be convenient to work with the following different Whitney regions in
\Omega
: for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, set

W_{\rho}(x,r):=\rho(W(x,r)).

Since
1\leq\partial_{t}(c_{0}t+F(y,t))\leq 1+2c_{d}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{\infty}
for all
(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
,

t\leq c_{0}t+F(y,t)-\varphi(x)\leq(1+2c_{d}\varepsilon)t\quad\text{for }(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}.

(4.13)

Let
(y,s)=W_{\rho}(x,r)
be given, and write
(y,s)=\rho(y,t)=(y,c_{0}t+F(y,t))
for some

(y,t)\in W(x,r)
. Thus
|y-x|<r
and
5r/6<t<r
. By (
4.13
),
5r/6<s-\varphi(y)\leq(1+2c_{d}\varepsilon)r
. This means that
\left|(y,s)-y_{\rho}\right|>5r/6
, and hence
\dist((y,s),\Gamma)\geq 3r/4
if we choose

sufficiently small.
In addition,

|(y,s)-x_{\rho}|\leq|y-x|+|s-\varphi(y)|+\left|\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)\right|\leq\left(2+(2c_{d}+1)\varepsilon\right)r\leq 5r/2,

if we choose

(4.14)

Now we fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, and estimate
\alpha_{\widetilde{A}}(x,r)
.
Let
A_{0}
be a constant coefficient matrix which achieves the infimum for
\alpha_{A}(x_{\rho},\frac{3r}{2})
,
set

a=a(x,r)=\fiint_{W(x,r)}\nabla_{y}F(y,s)dyds

and

b=b(x,r)=\fiint_{W(x,r)}\partial_{s}F(y,s)dyds,

and define

\widetilde{A}_{0}
to be the constant coefficient matrix

\widetilde{A}_{0}:=\begin{pmatrix}I&\mathbf{0}\\
-a&1\end{pmatrix}A_{0}\begin{pmatrix}I&-a\left(c_{0}+b\right)^{-1}\\
0&\left(c_{0}+b\right)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}=:P_{0}A_{0}Q_{0},

which should be compared to
the expression of
\widetilde{A}(x,t)
in
(
4.11
). Then

\alpha_{\widetilde{A}}(x,r)^{2}\leq\fiint_{W(x,r)}\left|\widetilde{A}(y,s)-\widetilde{A}_{0}\right|^{2}dyds\\
=\fiint_{W(x,r)}\left|P(y,s)A(\rho(y,s))Q(y,s)-P_{0}A_{0}Q_{0}\right|^{2}dyds.

By the triangle inequality,

\left|P(y,s)A(\rho(y,s))Q(y,s)-P_{0}A_{0}Q_{0}\right|\leq\left|(P(y,s)-P_{0})A(\rho(y,s))Q(y,s)\right|\\
+\left|P_{0}A(\rho(y,s))(Q(y,s)-Q_{0})\right|+\left|P_{0}\left(A(\rho(y,s))-A_{0}\right)Q_{0}\right|.

Using
the definitions of
P
,
Q
,
P_{0}
,
Q_{0}
, the ellipticity of
A
and
A_{0}
, and
(
4.7
) (
4.8
), we obtain that

\left|P(y,s)A(\rho(y,s))Q(y,s)-P_{0}A_{0}Q_{0}\right|\lesssim\big|\nabla_{y}F(y,s)-a\big|\\
+\big|\partial_{s}F(y,s)-b\big|+\left|A(\rho(y,s))-A_{0}\right|.

Then

\alpha_{\widetilde{A}}(x,r)^{2}\\
\lesssim\fiint_{W(x,r)}\bigg|\nabla F(y,s)-\fiint_{W(x,r)}\nabla F(z,\tau)dzd\tau\bigg|^{2}dyds+\fiint_{W(x,r)}\left|A(\rho(y,s))-A_{0}\right|^{2}dyds\\
\lesssim r^{2}\fiint_{W(x,r)}\left|\nabla^{2}F(y,s)\right|^{2}dyds+\fiint_{\rho(W(x,r))}\left|A(Y)-A_{0}\right|^{2}dY.

(4.15)

We fix any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r_{0}>0
, and estimate

\iint_{T(x_{0},r_{0})}\alpha_{\widetilde{A}}(x,r)^{2}dxdr/r
.
By Fubini’s theorem
(recall also that

\iint_{T(x_{0},r_{0})}r^{2}\fint_{W(x,r)}\left|\nabla^{2}F(y,s)\right|^{2}dyds\frac{dxdr}{r}\leq C\int_{\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x_{0},2r_{0})}\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\left|\nabla^{2}F(y,s)\right|^{2}s\,dyds,

4.9
.
By (

A_{0}

\iint_{(x,r)\in T(x_{0},r_{0})}\fiint_{Y\in\rho(W(x,r))}\left|A(Y)-A_{0}\right|^{2}dY\frac{dxdr}{r}\\
\leq C\int_{x\in\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x_{0},r_{0})}\int_{r=0}^{r_{0}}\fint_{Y\in W_{\Omega}(x_{\rho},5r/2)}\left|A(Y)-A_{0}\right|^{2}dY\frac{dxdr}{r}\\

where in fact
\alpha_{A}(x,r)

1.9
), but in terms of the larger

\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\to\partial\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d}

Proof of Theorem

5.
Chord-arc surfaces with small constants

5.1.
Geometric properties of the
CASSC

In Definition
1.13
, we
defined the
CASSC
by the fact that they have very big pieces of
\varepsilon
-Lipschitz
graphs. This is convenient for us (and also extends well to higher co-dimensions),
but we could have used different definitions,
a popular one being that the unit
normal to
\Gamma
(say, with values in
\mathbb{S}/\{\pm 1\}
) has a small
BMO norm. We refer to the early papers of S. Semmes
[
Sem89
,
Sem90b
,
Sem90a
]
for details.

Let us first give some geometric definitions.

Definition 5.1

(Two-sided Corkscrew condition
[
JK82
]
)
.

We say a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
satisfies the two-sided corkscrew condition if there exists a uniform constant
M\geq 2
such that for all
x\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,\diam\partial\Omega)
there exists
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
such that

B(X_{1},r/M)\subset B(x,r)\cap\Omega,\quad B(X_{2},r/M)\subset B(x,r)\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

We write
X_{x,r}:=X_{1}
, for the interior corkscrew point for
x
at scale
r
. We also use the notation
X_{\Delta}:=X_{x,r}
when
\Delta=\Delta(x,r)=B(x,r)\cap\partial\Omega
.

Definition 5.2

(Harnack chain condition
[
JK82
]
)
.

We say a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
satisfies the Harnack chain condition if there exists a uniform constant
M\geq 2
such that if
X_{1},X_{2}\in\Omega
with
\dist(X_{i},\partial\Omega)>\varepsilon>0
and
|X_{1}-X_{2}|<2^{k}\varepsilon
then there exists a ‘chain’ of open balls
B_{1},\dots,B_{N}
with
N<Mk
such that
X_{1}\in B_{1}
,
X_{2}\in B_{N}
,
B_{j}\cap B_{j+1}\neq\o
for
j=1,\dots,N-1
and
M^{-1}\diam B_{j}\leq\dist(B_{j},\partial\Omega)\leq M\diam B_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,N
.

Definition 5.3

(NTA domains
[
JK82
]
)
.

We say a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is an NTA domain if it satisfies the two-sided corkscrew condition and the
Harnack chain condition.

Let
\Gamma\in CASSC(\varepsilon)
be given. We systematically assume that
\varepsilon

is small enough, depending on
d
when needed. We start with simple geometric consequences of the definition the
CASSC

Lemma 5.4

5.2.
Small

In this section we deduce Theorem

Throughout the section, we let
\Gamma\in CASSC(\varepsilon)
with

small enough, and
\Omega

. We shall approximate

from outside by a Lipschitz graph as in Corollary

Theorem 5.23
.

For every choice of

, there exist

\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\delta,d,\mu_{0})>0

with the following properties. Let

Suppose

, and

and and every Borel subset

,

\left|\frac{\omega_{\Omega}^{X_{0}}(E)}{\omega_{\Omega}^{X_{0}}(\Delta)}-\frac{\sigma(E)}{\sigma(\Delta)}\right|<\delta,

whenever

X_{0}=X_{x,\varepsilon^{-\beta}r}\in\Omega
is a corkscrew point for
x
at scale
\varepsilon^{-\beta}r
, with

for some
\beta_{0}
that depends only on

and
\mu_{0}
.

Remark 5.24
.

Although Theorem
5.23
is stated differently from Theorem
1.19
, one can check that the latter can be deduced from the former. In fact, let
\delta>0
,
\kappa>1
and
X\in\Omega
be fixed. Take

with

and
\tau>0
to be determined, where
\delta(X)=\dist(X,\Gamma)
, and let
E\subset\Delta
. By Theorem
5.23
, there exists an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

\left|\frac{\omega^{X_{0}}(E)}{\omega^{X_{0}}(\Delta)}-\frac{\sigma(E)}{\sigma(\Delta)}\right|<\delta/2

(5.25)

whenever

X_{0}\in\Omega
is a corkscrew point for
x
at scale
\varepsilon_{0}^{-\beta_{0}}r
, for some
\beta_{0}=\beta_{0}(d,\mu_{0})>0
. By a change-of-pole argument similar to Lemma

\left|\frac{\omega^{X_{0}}(E)}{\omega^{X_{0}}(\Delta)}-\frac{\omega^{X}(E)}{\omega^{X}(\Delta)}\right|\leq C\left(\frac{r}{d}\right)^{\gamma}\quad\text{for }r<\frac{d}{10}

(5.26)

for some

, where

by taking

), and the triangle inequality.

1.17
can be deduced from Theorem
1.19

.

We shall need the following two lemmata that enable us

Lemma 5.27
.

There exists a constant

\widetilde{\omega}|_{\Delta(x,r)}

Lemma 5.30

There exists a constant

By Lemma

\widetilde{\omega}|_{\Delta(x,r)}

Since we want

where we have chosen

and

(5.60)

in the last inequality. Returning to (

I_{1}\geq\frac{1-\frac{C}{K^{\alpha}}}{1+\frac{C}{K^{\alpha}}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d}(E)}{\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Delta_{0})}-\frac{\delta}{4}\right)\geq\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d}(E)}{\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Delta_{0})}-\frac{\delta}{4}-C\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}\geq\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d}(E)}{\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Delta_{0})}-\frac{\delta}{2}.,

(

-elliptic matrices.

When

(5.68)

we say that

. Notice that

So we have a function

Indeed for any Whitney cube
Q_{i}
,

,

If

Let

We shall distinguish between cases, and the most interesting is probably when

\dist(y,\Gamma\cap 2B)\leq 2r\text{ and }r\leq 6Kr_{0}.

(5.76)

Let

,

(5.80)

\frac{6}{5}Q_{i}
meets
\overline{W}(y,r)
)
hence within
2\diam(Q_{i})+r
from
y
. So
|Z-y^{\ast}|\leq|Z-y|+d(y)\leq 60\diam(Q_{i})+3d(y)/2
.

Now
\frac{6}{5}Q_{i}
meets
\overline{W}(y,r)
, and if
X\in\overline{W}(y,r)\cap\frac{6}{5}Q_{i}

we both have that
\dist(X,\Gamma\cap 2B)\leq d(y)+r\leq 3d(y)/2
by (
5.84
),
and
\dist(X,\Gamma\cap 2B)\geq\dist(\frac{6}{5}Q_{i},\Gamma\cap 2B)\geq\dist(Q_{i},\Gamma\cap 2B)-\frac{1}{5}\diam(Q_{i})\geq 9\diam(Q_{i})
, so
60\diam(Q_{i})\leq 10d(y)
and (
5.88
) follows.
Therefore, choosing
A_{0}=\fint_{\Omega\cap B(y^{\ast},12d(y))}A(Y)dY
(which does not depend on
i\in I(X)
,
and not even on
X
),

|A_{i}-A_{0}|\leq|W_{i}|^{-1}\int_{W_{i}}|A(Y)-A_{0}|dY\leq|W_{i}|^{-1}\int_{B(y^{\ast},12d(y))}|A(Y)-A_{0}|dY\\
\leq C|W_{i}|^{-1}|B(y^{\ast},12d(y))|\fint_{B(y^{\ast},12d(y))}|A(Y)-A_{0}|dY.

Observe that
|W_{i}|^{-1}|B(y^{\ast},12d(y))|\leq C
. For
i\in I_{1}\cup I_{2}
, this follows from
\diam(Q_{i})\geq d(y)/24
. For
i\in I_{3}
, this is because
d(y)\leq 6Kr_{0}
. Hence,

|A_{i}-A_{0}|\leq Cd(y)^{-1}\gamma_{A}(y^{\ast},12d(y))

(5.89)

by Cauchy-Schwarz.
We notice that all the
W_{i}
,
i\in I(Z)
, stay at distance at least
C^{-1}\diam(Q_{i})\geq C^{-1}d(y)
from
\Gamma
, so we can replace
\gamma(y^{\ast},12d(y))
with

\widehat{\alpha}(y^{\ast},d(y))
,
the larger variant
of
\alpha(x,r)
, defined by

\widehat{\alpha}(x,r)=\Big\{\inf_{A_{0}}|\widehat{W}(x,r)|^{-1}\int_{\widehat{W}(x,r)}|A-A_{0}|^{2}\Big\}^{1/2},

with
\widehat{W}(x,r)=\big\{X\in\Omega\cap B(x,20r)\,;\,\dist(X,\Gamma)\geq C^{-1}r\big\}
.
Altogether,

|\nabla\widetilde{A}(X)|\leq Cd(y)^{-1}\sum_{i\in I(X)}|A_{i}-A_{0}|\leq Cd(y)^{-1}\widehat{\alpha}(y^{\ast},d(y)).

(5.90)

Because of this, the oscillation of
\widetilde{A}(X)
on
\overline{W}(y,r)
is at most

Cd(y)^{-1}r\widehat{\alpha}(y^{\ast},d(y))
, and hence

\overline{\alpha}(y,r)\leq Cd(y)^{-1}r\widehat{\alpha}(y^{\ast},d(y))
. By this and (
5.74
),

J(\Xi^{\prime}):=\int_{y\in G\cap B(y_{0},s_{0})}\int_{0<r\leq s_{0}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\Xi^{\prime}}(y,r)\,\mathbbm{1}_{d(y)\leq 6Kr_{0}}(y)\,\,\overline{\alpha}(y,r)^{2}\frac{dr}{r}d\mathcal{H}^{d}(y)\\
\leq C\int_{y\in G\cap B(y_{0},s_{0})}\int_{0<r\leq d(y)/2}\widehat{\alpha}(y^{\ast},d(y))\,d(y)^{-2}rdr\,d\mathcal{H}^{d}(y)\\
\leq C\int_{y\in G\cap B(y_{0},s_{0})}\widehat{\alpha}(y^{\ast},d(y))^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{d}(y)\\
\leq Cs_{0}^{d}\sup_{y\in G\cap B(y_{0},s_{0})}\widehat{\alpha}(y^{\ast},d(y))^{2}.

(5.91)

Now the weak DKP condition implies that
\widehat{\alpha}(z,r)^{2}\leq C\mathfrak{N}(A)\leq C\varepsilon

for all
z\in\Gamma
and
r>0
, where the Carleson norm
\mathfrak{N}(A)
is as in (
1.11
).
The verification is also easy (and is done in
[
DLM21
]
).
Thus we also get that
J(\Xi^{\prime})\leq Cs_{0}^{d}\varepsilon
, and since (
5.83
) provides the
estimate for the other piece
J(\Xi^{\prime})
, we finally get that the extension
\widetilde{A}
satisfies
the weak DKP condition on
G\times(0,\infty)
, with a norm at most
C\varepsilon
.

6.
Small and vanishing
A_{\infty}
and BMO

In this section we check some of the relations between
A_{\infty}
weights and

BMO
estimates for their logarithm. We want to do this in
(\Gamma,d\sigma)
,
where either
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is a chord-arc surface with small constant and

\sigma=\mathcal{H}^{d}_{|\Gamma}
, or
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma
is the Lebesgue measure on
\Gamma
. Because of the first case, we define BMO with balls.

We first define the mean oscillation on a set
E
such that
0<\sigma(E)<+\infty
, of the function
f\in L^{1}(E,d\sigma)
by

mo(f,E)=\sigma(E)^{-1}\int_{E}\bigg|f(y)-\Big(\fint_{E}fd\sigma\Big)\bigg|d\sigma(y).

(6.1)

We say that
f\in BMO(\Gamma)
when
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Gamma,d\sigma)
and

\|f\|_{BMO}:=\sup_{r>0}\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\,mo(f,\Delta(x,r))<+\infty,

(6.2)

and that
f\in VMO(\Gamma)
when
f\in BMO(\Gamma)
and in addition

\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\,mo(f,\Delta(x,r))=0.

(6.3)

It is easy to see that in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can replace the collection of balls
\Delta(x,r)

with the collection of cubes
Q
with faces parallel to the axes, and get the same space
BMO, with a slightly different but equivalent semi-norm

\|f\|_{\ast}:=\sup_{Q}\,mo(f,Q);

(6.4)

also
VMO(\Gamma)
could be defined with cubes. Finally, we can localize: if

\Delta=\Delta(x_{0},r_{0})
is a surface ball in
\Gamma
, we say that

f\in BMO(\Delta)
when
f\in L^{1}(\Delta,d\sigma)
and

\|f\|_{BMO(\Delta)}:=\sup_{(x,r)\in\Delta\times(0,r_{0}),\Delta(x,r)\subset\Delta}\,mo(f,\Delta(x,r))<+\infty.

(6.5)

This may be a little ugly near the boundary, but often we only care about the restriction
of
f
to
\Delta(x_{0},r_{0}/2)
anyway. In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the version with cubes is a little better.
If
Q_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a cube (with faces parallel to the axes, we won’t repeat),
we say that
f\in BMO_{\ast}(Q_{0})
when
f\in L^{1}(Q_{0},d\sigma)
and

\|f\|_{BMO_{\ast}(Q_{0})}:=\sup_{Q\subset Q_{0}}\,mo(f,Q)<+\infty,

(6.6)

where the supremum is over the cubes
Q\subset Q_{0}
.

With all these definitions at hand, we are ready to prove Lemma
3.49
.
We start with the simplest version of the John and Nirenberg theorem, with cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Lemma 6.7
.

There exist constants
c=c_{d}>0
and
C=C_{d}\geq 1
, that depend only on
d
,
such that if
Q_{0}
is a cube and
f\in BMO_{\ast}(Q_{0})
, then

\int_{Q_{0}}\exp\bigg(c\|f\|_{BMO_{\ast}(Q_{0})}^{-1}\bigg|f(x)-\Big(\fint_{Q_{0}}fd\sigma\Big)\bigg|\bigg)d\sigma(x)\leq C\sigma(Q_{0}).

(6.8)

In fact, the proof only requires a control on
mo(f,Q)
when
Q
is a dyadic subcube of

Q_{0}
(i.e., obtained from
Q_{0}
by the usual dyadic partitions).

Proof of Lemma
3.49
.

Let
\omega
and
k=\frac{d\omega}{d\sigma}
be as in the statement,
and let
\delta>0
be given. Let
\varepsilon>0
be given, and choose

r_{0}>0
so small that
mo(\log k,\Delta(x,r))<\varepsilon
for every surface ball

\Delta(x,r)
such that
0<r\leq r_{0}
. By standard manipulations of covering
cubes with balls of roughly the same sizes, we also see that

\|\log k\|_{BMO_{\ast}(Q_{0})}\leq C\varepsilon
for every cube
Q_{0}
such that

{\rm diam}(Q_{0})\leq r_{0}
.

We choose
\gamma=d^{-1/2}r_{0}
, consider a surface ball
\Delta=\Delta(x,r)

such that
r\leq\gamma
, and try to prove (
3.50
) for any Borel set

E\subset\Delta
. Call
Q_{0}
the smallest cube that contains
\Delta
; we chose

\gamma
so that
{\rm diam}(Q_{0})\leq r_{0}
, and so

\|\log(k)\|_{BMO_{\ast}(Q_{0})}\leq C\varepsilon
. This allows us to apply
Lemma
6.7
and get

\int_{Q_{0}}\exp\bigg(a\varepsilon^{-1}\bigg|\log(k(x))-\Big(\fint_{Q_{0}}\log(k)d\sigma\Big)\bigg|\bigg)d\sigma(x)\leq C,

(6.9)

with a new constant
a=C^{-1}c>0
that depends only on
d
.
Since (
3.50
) does not change when we multiply
\omega
by a positive constant,
we may assume that
\fint_{Q_{0}}\log(k)d\sigma=0
, which will simplify our computations slightly. We write

\omega(Q)=\int_{Q}k(x)d\sigma(x)\ \text{ and }\omega(E)=\int_{E}k(x)d\sigma(x)

(6.10)

and decide to cut each of these integrals in three, corresponding to the three regions

Q_{1}=\big\{x\in Q_{0}\,;\,\log(k(x))\leq-A\varepsilon\big\},\\
Q_{2}=\big\{x\in Q_{0}\,;\,|\log(k(x))|\leq A\varepsilon\big\},\\
Q_{3}=\big\{x\in Q_{0}\,;\,\log(k(x))\geq A\varepsilon\big\},

(6.11)

where the large constant
A
will be chosen soon. Notice that

a\varepsilon^{-1}|\log(k)|\geq aA
on
Q_{1}\cup Q_{3}
, so by Chebyshev

\omega(Q_{1})\leq\sigma(Q_{1})\leq\sigma(Q_{1})+\sigma(Q_{3})\leq Ce^{-aA}\sigma(Q_{0}).

(6.12)

Similarly, we cut
Q_{3}
into the regions

D_{j}=\big\{x\in Q_{3}\,;\,j+A\varepsilon\leq\log(k(x))<j+1+A\varepsilon\big\}

and get that

\omega(Q_{3})\leq\int_{Q_{3}}k(x)d\sigma(x)\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{D_{j}}k(x)d\sigma(x)\\
\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}e^{j+1+A\varepsilon}\exp\big(-a\varepsilon^{-1}(j+A\varepsilon)\big)\int_{D_{j}}\exp\big(a\varepsilon^{-1}\log(k(x))\big)d\sigma(x)\\
\leq C\sigma(Q_{0})e^{1+A\varepsilon}e^{-aA}\sum_{j\geq 0}e^{j}e^{-a\varepsilon^{-1}j}\\
\leq C\sigma(Q_{0})e^{1+A\varepsilon}e^{-aA}\leq Ce^{-aA/2}

(6.13)

if we choose
A\geq 2a^{-1}
and
\varepsilon\leq A^{-1}
.
Now let
F
be a Borel subset of
Q_{0}
; we think about
E
or
\Delta
. Then

|\omega(F)-\sigma(F)|\leq\omega(F\cap(Q_{1}\cup Q_{3}))+\sigma(F\cap(Q_{1}\cup Q_{3}))+\int_{F\cap Q_{2}}|k(x)-1|d\sigma\\
\leq\omega(Q_{1}\cup Q_{3})+\sigma(Q_{1}\cup Q_{3})+\int_{Q_{2}}\big|e^{A\varepsilon}-1\big|d\sigma\leq(Ce^{-aA/2}+2A\varepsilon)\sigma(Q_{0})

(6.14)

by (
6.11
)-(
6.13
). By definition of
Q_{0}
, we also have that

\sigma(\Delta)\geq\alpha\sigma(Q_{0})
for some constant
\alpha>0
that depends only
on
d
. Then we choose
A
, and then
\varepsilon
, so that

Ce^{-aA/2}+2A\varepsilon\leq 10^{-2}\alpha\delta
in (
6.14
).
Then

|\omega(\Delta)-\sigma(\Delta)|\leq 10^{-2}\alpha\delta\sigma(Q_{0})\leq 10^{-3}\delta\sigma(\Delta)

(6.15)

and similarly

|\omega(E)-\sigma(E|\leq 10^{-2}\delta\sigma(\Delta),

(6.16)

from which it is easy to see that

\left|\frac{\omega(E)}{\omega(\Delta)}-\frac{\sigma(E)}{\sigma(\Delta)}\right|<\delta

as in (
3.50
).
∎

Remark 6.17
.

We shall not need the fact that Lemma
3.49
is still valid when
\Gamma
is a chord-arc surface with small enough constant, but this is the case, with essentially the same proof. The point is that we can cut
\Gamma
into
collections of “dyadic cubes” that have roughly the same properties as the dyadic cubes
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then the analogue of Lemma
6.7
holds when
Q_{0}
is one of these cubes
(because we only need to estimate
mo(f,Q)
for dyadic subcubes), and then we can proceed as above (but cover the surface ball
\Delta
by a finite collection of “dyadic cubes”).
This would even work when
\Gamma
is simply Ahlfors regular
(in fact the doubling property of
\sigma
is the important feature).

Also, here we stated the lemma in terms of
VMO
and vanishing
A_{\infty}
condition,
but the same proof shows that for each
\delta>0
, we can find

\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\delta,d)>0
such that if
||\log k||_{BMO}\leq\varepsilon
,
then
\omega\in A_{\infty}(\sigma,\delta)
.

There is a converse to
3.49
and its small constant variant,
which we discuss now.

Lemma 6.18
.

There is a constant
\varepsilon>0
, that depends only on
d
, and for each

\delta>0
, a constant
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}(\delta,d)
with the following
property. Let
\Gamma\in CASSC(\varepsilon)
be a chord-arc surface
with small enough constant in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
(as in Definition
1.13
),
and let
\omega
be another measure on

\Gamma
such that
\omega\in A_{\infty}(\sigma,\varepsilon_{1})
(see Definition
1.15
).
Then
\omega
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\sigma
, and
its density
k=\frac{d\omega}{d\sigma}
satisfies
||\log(k)||_{BMO}\leq\delta
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma\in CASSC(\varepsilon)
and
\omega\in A_{\infty}(\sigma,\varepsilon_{1})

satisfy the assumption. It is a general fact about
A_{\infty}
that then

\omega
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\sigma
, so
k
is well defined
and locally integrable. Let
a>0
be small, to be chosen later (depending on
d
).
We want to show that (if
\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon_{1}
are small enough),
for every surface ball
\Delta
, there is a constant
C_{\Delta}
such that

\sigma\big(\big\{x\in\Delta\,;\,|\log(k(x))-c_{\Delta}|\geq\delta\big\}\big)\leq a\sigma(\Delta).

(6.19)

In fact, the simplest is to take
c_{\Delta}=\log\Big(\frac{\omega(\Delta)}{\sigma(\Delta)}\Big)
.
Write
\Delta=\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}\cup\Delta_{3}
, with

\Delta_{1}=\big\{x\in\Delta\,;\,k(x)<e^{c_{\Delta}-\delta}\big\},\\
\Delta_{2}=\big\{x\in\Delta\,;\,e^{c_{\Delta}-\delta}\leq k(x)\leq e^{c_{\Delta}+\delta}\big\},\\
\Delta_{3}=\big\{x\in\Delta\,;\,k(x)>e^{c_{\Delta}+\delta}\big\};

(6.20)

we want to show that
\sigma(\Delta_{1})+\sigma(\Delta_{3})\leq\sigma(\Delta)/100
.
We take
E=\Delta_{1}
in the definition (
1.16
); we get that

\left|\frac{\omega(\Delta_{1})}{\omega(\Delta)}-\frac{\sigma(\Delta_{1})}{\sigma(\Delta)}\right|<\varepsilon_{1}
or, since
\omega(\Delta)=e^{c_{\Delta}}\sigma(\Delta)
,

|e^{-c_{\Delta}}\omega(\Delta_{1})-\sigma(\Delta_{1})|\leq\varepsilon_{1}\sigma(\Delta).

(6.21)

But
\omega(\Delta_{1})=\int_{\Delta_{1}}kd\sigma\leq e^{c_{\Delta}-\delta}\sigma(\Delta)

so
\sigma(\Delta_{1})-e^{-c_{\Delta}}\omega(\Delta_{1})\geq(1-e^{-\delta})\sigma(\Delta_{1})
, so the comparison with (
6.21
) yields

\sigma(\Delta_{1})\leq\varepsilon_{1}(1-e^{-\delta})^{-1}\sigma(\Delta)<a\sigma(\Delta)/2
if
\varepsilon_{1}
is chosen small enough.
Similarly,

|e^{-c_{\Delta}}\omega(\Delta_{3})-\sigma(\Delta_{3})|\leq\varepsilon_{1}\sigma(\Delta)

(6.22)

by the proof of (
6.21
), and the definition yields

\omega(\Delta_{3})=\int_{\Delta_{3}}kd\sigma\geq e^{c_{\Delta}+\delta}\sigma(\Delta)
,
so that
e^{-c_{\Delta}}\omega(\Delta_{3})-\sigma(\Delta_{3})\geq(e^{-\delta}-1)\sigma(\Delta_{3})
and the comparison yields

\sigma(\Delta_{3})\leq\varepsilon_{1}(e^{\delta}-1)^{-1}\sigma(\Delta)<a\sigma(\Delta)/2
. This proves (
6.19
).

Now we claim that a well known result proved by F. John
[
Joh65
]
,
improved by J. O. Strömberg
[
Str79
]
, allows one to
deduce from (
6.19
) that
\log(k)\in BMO
, with a norm less than
C\delta
.
Here
C
depends on
d
, and of course the difference between
C\delta
and
\delta

could easily compensated by taking
\varepsilon_{1}
smaller.
The advantage of the result of John and Strömberg is that it is directly valid for a doubling measure.
For the sake of the reader that would know the usual proof of the John-Nirenberg theorem
(with simple stopping times), let us say two words about how the underlying ideas
could be applied.
In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if you have (
6.19
) for balls, you also get it for cubes
Q
, by the usual
trick of using (
6.19
) for the smallest ball containing
Q
, and
at the price of starting with the smaller
\widetilde{a}=a\sigma(Q)/\sigma(\Delta)
.
Then, if
a
is small enough, we deduce from (
6.19
) that if
Q
and
R

are cubes such that
Q\subset R
and
\rm{diam}(Q)\geq\rm{diam}(R)/2
,
then
|c_{Q}-c_{R}|\leq 2\delta
. Then the proof of the John-Nirenberg theorem can
then be applied, and this yields
||\log(k)||_{BMO}\leq C\delta
.
So the result is valid when
\Gamma\in CASSC(\varepsilon)
,

\varepsilon
small enough (so that
\sigma
is doubling), with the usual fake dyadic cubes
on
\Gamma
. We need to take
a
even smaller, so that in particular we can control

|c_{Q}-c_{R}|
for fake cubes, when
Q
is a child of
R
, but the proof goes through.
∎

Corollary 6.23
.

In Theorem
1.17
, we can replace the conclusion that

\omega^{\infty}\in A_{\infty}(\sigma,\delta)
with the conclusion that

\omega^{\infty}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\sigma
, with a
density
k=\frac{d\omega}{d\sigma}
such that
||\log(k)||_{BMO}\leq\delta
.

In Theorem
1.19
, we can replace the conclusion by the fact that
for each surface ball
\Delta=B(x,r)\cap\Gamma
such that

\Delta\subset B(X,\kappa\dist(X,\Gamma))
and
0<r\leq\tau\dist(X,\Gamma)
,

\omega^{X}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\sigma
on
\Delta
,
and the density
k=\frac{d\omega^{X}}{d\sigma}
is such that

.

Proof.

The first part follows at once from Theorem
1.17
and Lemma
6.18
.
The second part follows Theorem
1.19
(applied with a larger
\kappa
for security)
and the proof of Lemma

.
∎

7.
Proof of Lemma
3.23
on the elliptic measure for a limit

In this section, we prove Lemma
3.23
.

We first show that (
3.24
) holds when
E=U
is a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Let
\left\{F_{i}\right\}_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of closed sets with
F_{i}\subset U
and
F_{i}\uparrow U
as
i\to\infty
.
For each
i\geq 1
, let
g_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
, with
0\leq g_{i}\leq 1
and
g_{i}\equiv 1
on
F_{i}
.
For
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
, define

v_{i}^{(k)}(X):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{i}(y)d\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(y),

and

v_{i}(X):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{i}(y)d\omega_{L}^{X}(y).

By definition of the elliptic measures,
v_{i}^{(k)}\in W_{\loc}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
is the unique weak solution to

\begin{cases}L_{k}v_{i}^{(k)}=0\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+},\\
v_{i}^{(k)}(x,0)=g_{i}(x)\qquad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

(7.1)

that satisfies
v_{i}^{(k)}\in C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}})
and
\lim_{\left|X\right|\to\infty,\,X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}v_{i}^{(k)}(X)=0
. Similarly,
v_{i}\in W_{\loc}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
is the unique weak solution to

\begin{cases}Lv_{i}=0\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+},\\
v_{i}(x,0)=g_{i}(x)\qquad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

(7.2)

that satisfies
v_{i}\in C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}})
and
\lim_{\left|X\right|\to\infty,\,X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}v_{i}(X)=0
.
Here, and in the sequel,

\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)
.
Let us check that
for any fixed
i
,

\lim_{\left|X\right|\to\infty,\,X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}v_{i}^{(k)}(X)=0\quad\text{uniformly in }k.

(7.3)

In fact, since
\supp g_{i}\subset U
and
0\leq g_{i}\leq 1
,

0\leq v_{i}^{(k)}(X)=\int_{U}g_{i}(y)d\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(y)\leq\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(\Delta_{r}),

where
\Delta_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a ball with radius
r
that contains
U
. By Lemma
2.5
and the estimate (
2.2
) for the Green function,

\left|v_{i}^{(k)}(X)\right|\leq C\,r^{d-1}\left|X-X_{\Delta_{r}}\right|^{1-d},

where
C
depends only on
\mu_{0}
and
d
.
So (
7.3
) holds.

By our choice of
F_{i}
and
g_{i}
,

\left|v_{i}^{(k)}(X)-\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U)\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(g_{i}(y)-\mathbbm{1}_{U}(y))d\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(y)\right|\leq\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U\setminus F_{i}).

(7.4)

Since
\mathfrak{N}(A_{k})<C_{0}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, Lemma
3.2
asserts that

\omega_{L_{k}}\in A_{\infty}(d\sigma)
, and the
A_{\infty}
constants depend only on
d
and
C_{0}
.
Therefore
we can find constants
\theta
,
C
, that may depend on
X
and
U
, but not on
k

or the
F_{i}
, such that

\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U\setminus F_{i})\leq C\sigma(U\setminus F_{i})^{\theta}.

The regularity of the
Lebesgue measure now implies that
the right-hand side of (
7.4
)
tends to 0 as
i\to\infty
. So for any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
,
the limit
\lim_{i\to\infty}v_{i}^{(k)}(X)
exists, and

\lim_{i\to\infty}v_{i}^{(k)}(X)=\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U)\qquad\text{uniformly in }k.

(7.5)

Similarly,

\left|v_{i}(X)-\omega_{L}^{X}(U)\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(g_{i}(y)-\mathbbm{1}_{U}(y))d\omega_{L}^{X}(y)\right|\leq\omega_{L}^{X}(U\setminus F_{i})\to 0

as
i\to\infty
, by the regularity of
\omega_{L}^{X}
. So

\lim_{i\to\infty}v_{i}(X)=\omega_{L}^{X}(U).

(7.6)

We claim that for any fixed
i
and

X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}v_{i}^{(k)}(X)=v_{i}(X).

(7.7)

We shall justify the claim by first showing that as
k\to\infty
,
v_{i}^{(k)}
converges to a weak solution
v_{i}^{\infty}
of
Lv_{i}^{\infty}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
with boundary data
g_{i}
,
v_{i}^{\infty}\in C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}})
,
and
v_{i}^{\infty}
converges to 0 uniformly at infinity in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
.
Then by the maximum principle (or by the definition of elliptic measures),

v_{i}^{\infty}
must be the unique weak solution to the Dirichlet problem (
7.2
).
Thus,
v_{i}^{\infty}=v_{i}
, and the claim (
7.7
) will follow.

Let
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
be fixed
for the moment. Since the

v_{i}^{(k)}
are solutions with the same (smooth) boundary data
g_{i}
and the
L_{k}

have the same ellipticity constant for all
k
, the maximum principle, the Hölder regularity of solutions,
and the Arzela-Ascoli theorem imply that
there is a subsequence of
\big\{v_{i}^{(k)}\big\}_{k\geq 1}
,
which we still denote the same way, that converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}
.
That is, there is a continuous function
v_{i}^{\infty}
on
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{(k)}_{i}(X)=v_{i}^{\infty}(X),\ \text{ uniformly on compact subsets of }\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}

(7.8)

Since
v_{i}^{(k)}
is continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}
and equal to
g_{i}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we get that

v_{i}^{\infty}\in C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}),\quad\text{and }v_{i}^{\infty}(x,0)=g_{i}(x)\ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(7.9)

By (
7.8
) and (
7.3
),

\lim_{\left|X\right|\to\infty,\,X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}v_{i}^{\infty}(X)=0.

(7.10)

Next let us show that
(still for
i
fixed)

\nabla v_{i}^{(k)}\to\nabla v_{i}^{\infty}\text{ in }L^{2}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})\text{ as }k\to\infty.

(7.11)

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
, and pick a smooth cut-off function

\eta
, with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
everywhere,
\eta=1
on
K
,
and compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. Let
k,\ell\geq 0
be given; we want to estimate

I=\mu_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\big|\nabla(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\big|^{2}\eta^{2}.

(7.12)

The ellipticity of
L_{k}
gives that

I\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}A_{k}\nabla(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\cdot\nabla(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\eta^{2}\\
\hskip-56.9055pt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}A_{k}\nabla(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\cdot\nabla\Big((v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\eta^{2}\Big)\\
-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}A_{k}\nabla(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\cdot\nabla\eta\Big(\eta(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\Big)=:J_{1}-2J_{2}.

(7.13)

Set
\varphi=(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\eta^{2}
, and use it as a test function the equations

L_{k}v_{i}^{(k)}=0
and
L_{\ell}v_{i}^{(\ell)}=0
; we get that

J_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}A_{k}\nabla(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\cdot\nabla\varphi=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}A_{k}\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}\cdot\nabla\varphi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}(A_{\ell}-A_{k})\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}\cdot\nabla\varphi\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}(A_{\ell}-A_{k})\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}\cdot\nabla(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\eta^{2}+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}(A_{\ell}-A_{k})\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}\cdot\nabla\eta\Big(\eta(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\Big)\\
=:J_{3}+2J_{4}.

Then by (
7.13
),
I\leq J_{1}-2J_{2}\leq J_{3}-2J_{2}+2J_{4}
.
Now we use Cauchy Schwarz and Young’s inequality for each term. That is,

J_{3}\leq\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\big|\nabla(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\big|^{2}\eta^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\Big|(A_{k}-A_{\ell})\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}\Big|^{2}\eta^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\\
\leq\frac{1}{4}I+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\Big|(A_{k}-A_{\ell})\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}\Big|^{2}\eta^{2},

(7.14)

where
C
depends also on
\mu_{0}
, then

|2J_{2}|\leq C\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\big|\nabla(v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)})\big|^{2}\eta^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\Big|v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)}\Big|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\\
\leq\frac{1}{4}I+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\Big|v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)}\Big|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2},

(7.15)

|2J_{4}|\leq C\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}|A_{\ell}-A_{k}|^{2}|\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\eta^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\Big|v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)}\Big|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\\
\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}|A_{\ell}-A_{k}|^{2}|\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\eta^{2}+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\Big|v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)}\Big|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2},

(7.16)

Altogether,

I\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}|A_{\ell}-A_{k}|^{2}|\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\eta^{2}+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\Big|v_{i}^{(k)}-v_{i}^{(\ell)}\Big|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}=:CI_{1}+CI_{2},

(7.17)

where
C
depends only on
d
and
\mu_{0}
but not on
k
,
\ell
, or
i
.

By (
7.8
), the sequence
\big\{v_{i}^{(k)}\big\}_{k}
converges in
L^{2}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
so
I_{2}
tends to
0
when
k
and
\ell
tend to
+\infty
.
For
I_{1}
, we use Hölder’s
inequality and then the reverse Hölder inequality
for solutions (see e.g.
[
Ken94
]
Lemma 1.1.12) to get that

\left|I_{1}\right|\leq\left(\iint\left|A_{k}-A_{\ell}\right|^{2p^{\prime}}\eta^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\iint\left|\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}\right|^{2p}\eta^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\leq C\left(\iint_{\supp\eta}\left|A_{k}-A_{\ell}\right|^{2p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\iint_{K^{\prime}}\left|\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
p>1
is sufficiently close to
1
so that the reverse Hölder inequality for solutions holds,

p^{\prime}
is the Hölder exponent of
p
, and
K^{\prime}
is a slightly larger compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
than
\supp\eta
.
Here, the constant
C
depends on
p
,
\supp\eta
, and
K^{\prime}
, but is independent of
k
and
\ell
. Notice that by
Caccioppoli’s inequality, the
regularity of solutions, and the maximum principle,

\iint_{K^{\prime}}\left|\nabla v_{i}^{(\ell)}\right|^{2}\leq C\iint_{V}\left|v_{i}^{(\ell)}\right|^{2}\leq C^{\prime}\left\|v_{i}^{(\ell)}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})}\leq C^{\prime}\left\|g_{i}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C^{\prime},

where
V\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
is an enlargement of the set
K^{\prime}
, and
C
,
C^{\prime}
are constants independent of
\ell
. Therefore,

\left|I_{1}\right|\leq C^{\prime}\left(\iint_{\supp\eta}\left|A_{k}-A_{\ell}\right|^{2p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Since
A_{k}(x,t)=A(x,t)
for
t\in(2^{-k},2^{k})
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}A_{k}(x,t)=A(x,t)\qquad\text{for }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}\text{ a.e.}

(7.18)

So
I_{1}
tends to
0
when
k
and
\ell
tend to
+\infty
,
by the dominated convergence theorem,
hence
I
tends to
0
for each given
K
and
\eta
, and
(
7.11
) follows. Because of (
7.11
), one can
let
k
tend to
+\infty

in the equation

0=\iint A_{k}\nabla v_{i}^{(k)}(Y)\nabla\varphi(Y)dY

where
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
is any test function,
and get that
v_{i}^{\infty}
is a weak solution of
Lv_{i}^{\infty}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
.
It satisfies (
7.9
) and (
7.10
), and we checked earlier
that the claim (
7.7
) follows.

We claim that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega^{X}_{L_{k}}(U)=\omega_{L}^{X}(U)

(7.19)

for every bounded open subset
U
of
\Gamma
and every
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
.
This will follow from (
7.5
), (
7.6
) and (
7.7
); to see this, let
U
and
X
be fixed, and write

\left|\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U)-\omega_{L}^{X}(U)\right|\leq\left|\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U)-v_{i}^{(k)}(X)\right|+\left|v_{i}(X)-\omega_{L}^{X}(U)\right|+\left|v_{i}^{(k)}(X)-v_{i}(X)\right|.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. By (
7.5
) and (
7.6
), one can choose
i_{0}
sufficiently large so that

\left|\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U)-v_{i_{0}}^{(k)}(X)\right|<\varepsilon/3\quad\text{for all }k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}

and
\left|v_{i_{0}}(X)-\omega_{L}^{X}(U)\right|<\varepsilon/3
. For this fixed
i_{0}
, (
7.7
) allows us to take
k
sufficiently large so that

\left|v_{i_{0}}^{(k)}(X)-v_{i_{0}}(X)\right|<\varepsilon/3.

Altogether, we obtain that for
k
sufficiently large,

\left|\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U)-\omega_{L}^{X}(U)\right|<\varepsilon
.
This proves (
7.19
) (for
U
open and bounded), but apparently only for some subsequence
that we constructed. However, if (
7.19
) were to fail (for
X
and the open set
U
),
we could first replace
\{L_{k}\}
with a subsequence for which
\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U)
stays
far from
\omega_{L}^{X}(U)
, and then proceed as above with
\{L_{k}\}
,
find a subsequence with (
7.19
), a contradiction. So (
7.19
) holds for the whole sequence.

It remains to show that (
3.24
) (or (
3.24
))
also holds for any Borel (and not just open) bounded set.
So let the bounded Borel set
E
and the pole
X
be fixed.
Since
\omega_{L}^{X}
is regular, we can find
a nonincreasing sequence
\{U_{i}\}
of open sets that contain
E
and a nondecreasing sequence

\{F_{i}\}
of closed sets contained in
E
, such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\omega_{L}^{X}(U_{i})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\omega_{L}^{X}(E_{i})=\omega_{L}^{X}(E).

(7.20)

For each
i
, notice that
U_{i}
and
U_{i}\setminus F_{i}
are bounded and open,
so (
7.19
) holds for them and yields

\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega^{X}_{L_{k}}(U_{i})=\omega_{L}^{X}(U_{i})\ \text{ and }\ \lim_{k\to\infty}\omega^{X}_{L_{k}}(U_{i}\setminus F_{i})=\omega_{L}^{X}(U_{i}\setminus F_{i}).

(7.21)

Now let
\varepsilon>0
be given; by (
7.20
) we can find
i
such that

\omega_{L}^{X}(U_{i}\setminus F_{i})<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
.
Then (
7.21
) says that for
k
large,

|\omega^{X}_{L_{k}}(U_{i})-\omega_{L}^{X}(U_{i})|+|\omega^{X}_{L_{k}}(U_{i}\setminus F_{i})-\omega_{L}^{X}(U_{i}\setminus F_{i})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}

(7.22)

and since
F_{i}\subset E\subset U_{i}
,

|\omega^{X}_{L_{k}}(E)-\omega_{L}^{X}(E)|\leq|\omega^{X}_{L_{k}}(E)-\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U_{i})|+|\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U_{i})-\omega_{L}^{X}(U_{i})|+|\omega_{L}^{X}(U_{i})-\omega_{L}^{X}(E)|\\
=\omega^{X}_{L_{k}}(U_{i}\setminus E)+|\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U_{i})-\omega_{L}^{X}(U_{i})|+\omega^{X}_{L}(U_{i}\setminus E)\\
\leq\omega^{X}_{L_{k}}(U_{i}\setminus F_{i})+|\omega_{L_{k}}^{X}(U_{i})-\omega_{L}^{X}(U_{i})|+\omega^{X}_{L}(U_{i}\setminus F_{i})<\varepsilon

(7.23)

by (
7.21
) and (
7.22
). So
\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega^{X}_{L_{k}}(E)=\omega^{X}_{L}(E)
,
and completes the proof of Lemma
3.23
.

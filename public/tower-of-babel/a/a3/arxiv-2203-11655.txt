Supercharacters for parabolic contractions of finite groups of A , B , C , D A,B,C,D Lie types
A.N. Panov

Abstract




We construct supercharacter theories for the finite groups constructed by parabolic constraction from simple groups of
A,B,C,D
Lie types. In terms of rook placements in the root systems
we classify supecharacters and superclasses.

Key Words:
group representations, supercharacter theory, group contraction, irreducible character, superclasses

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification:
20C15, 17B22

1
Introduction

The notion of a supercharacter theory was introduced by P. Diaconis and I.M. Isaacs in the paper
[
1
]
in 2008.
By definition, a supercharacter theory for a given finite group
G
is a pair
({\mathfrak{S}},{\cal K})
, where
{\mathfrak{S}}=\{\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{M}\}
is a system of pairwise disjoint characters (representations) of
G
and
{\cal K}=\{K_{1},\ldots,K_{M}\}
is a partition of
G
such that
the characters from
{\mathfrak{S}}
are constant on subsets from
{\cal K}
and
\{1\}\in{\cal K}
.
The characters from
{\mathfrak{S}}
are called
supercharacters
, and subsets from
{\cal K}
–
superclasses
(see
[
1
]
). Observe the number of supercharacters equals to the number of superclasses.
An example of supercharacter theory is the pair that consists of the system of irreducible characters and the decomposition of group into conjugacy classes.
If a classification of irreducible representations is considered an extremely difficult, ”wild”  problem, we aim to construct a constructive supercharacter theory.
The classical example is the theory of basic characters for the unitriangular group
\mathrm{UT}(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
developed by C. André in
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. This example was generalized to algebra groups in the same paper
[
1
]
(by definition, an algebra group is a group of the form
{\mathfrak{G}}=1+J
, where
J
is a finite dimensional associative nilpotent algebra over a finite field).

In the present paper, we construct a supercharacter theory for parabolic contractions of the groups
{\mathrm{GL}}(n)
,
{\mathrm{O}}(n)
and
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
defined over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. For each group we classify supercharacters and superclasses.

To construct a supercharacter theory for the parabolic contraction
{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)
, we use the method of
[
10
]
(see Theorem
3.2
). The case of Borel contraction is studed in
[
11
]
, where we apply the other method from
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
that gives rise a supercharacter theory that is slightly different from the one constructed in the present paper. Also, we classify supercharacters and superclasses for the unipotent radical
U^{a}
(see Theorems
3.1
and
3.1
). For the parabolic radicals of
{\mathrm{GL}}(n)
, the similar classification is verified in
[
16
]
.

A supercharacter theory for the Sylow subgroups in the orthogonal and symplectic groups is constructed in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
17
]
.
The author proposed a supercharacter theory for parabolic subgroups in
{\mathrm{O}}(n)
and
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
in the paper
[
15
]
.
This approach is developing in the present paper for parabolic contractions of orthogonal and symplectic groups. We obtain a description of supercharacters and superclasses in the exact form (see Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
).

2
Parabolic contractions

Let
G
be a split reductive groups defined over a field
K
and
{\mathfrak{g}}
be its Lie algebra.
Fix the Cartan subgroup
H
contained in the Borel subgroup
B
. The group
B
has the unipotent radical
N_{+}
with the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}
. Applying the Killing form
(\cdot,\cdot)
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, we identify
{\mathfrak{n}}^{*}
with the opposite subalgebra
{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}
.

Let
P
be a parabolic subgroup containing
B
. The subgroup
P
is a semidirect product
P=LU_{+}
, where
L
is the Levi subgroup containing
H
, and
U_{+}
is the unipotent radical contained in
N_{+}
. The Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{p}}
of the group
P
is a semidirect sum
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{l}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}_{+}
of the Lie subalgebra
{\mathfrak{l}}=\mathrm{Lie}(L)
and the ideal
{\mathfrak{u}}_{+}=\mathrm{Lie}(U_{+})
.

As above we identify
{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}
with
{\mathfrak{u}}_{+}^{*}
.
Consider the commutative Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}^{a}
that coincides with
{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}
as a linear space and the abelian group
U_{-}^{a}=1+{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}^{a}
with multiplication
(1+{\lambda}_{1})(1+{\lambda}_{2})=1+{\lambda}_{1}+{\lambda}_{2}
.

A parabolic contraction
of the group
G
is a semidirect product
G^{a}=P\ltimes U_{-}^{a}
;
the subgroup
P
acts on
U^{a}_{-}
by the formula
p(1+{\lambda})p^{-1}=1+{\mathrm{Ad}}_{p}^{*}({\lambda})
, where
p\in P
and
{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}^{a}={\mathfrak{u}}^{*}
.
Respectively, its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}^{a}={\mathfrak{p}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}^{a}
(
a parabolic contraction
of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
) is a semidirect sum of the Lie subalgebra
{\mathfrak{p}}
and the ideal
{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}^{a}
.
These definitions are matched with the general definition of Lie algebra contraction from
[
8
,
9
]
.

3
Supercharacters and superclasses of the group

{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)

Let
{\mathrm{GL}}(n)
be the general linear group over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of
q
elements and of characteristic
\neq 2
. Fix a parabolic subgroup
P
of
{\mathrm{GL}}(n)
and consider its parabolic contraction
{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)
.
The parabolic subgroup
P
is defined by a decomposition of
[1,n]
into the system of consecutive segments
\mathcal{I}=I_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup I_{\ell}
. Let
n_{i}=|I_{i}|
. Then

L={\mathrm{GL}}(n_{1})\times\ldots\times{\mathrm{GL}}(n_{\ell})
.

Emphasize the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}^{a}(n)
of the group
{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)
is an associative algebra with respect to the multiplication for which
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a subalgebra with matrix multiplication,
{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}^{a}
is a subalgebra with zero multiplication and

t{\lambda}(x)={\lambda}(xt)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\lambda}t(x)={\lambda}(tx),

(1)

where
t\in{\mathfrak{p}}
,
x\in{\mathfrak{u}}_{+}
, and
{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}^{a}
.
The group
{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)
is realized as the subgroup of invertible elements of the associative algebra
\mathfrak{gl}^{a}(n)
.

The group
{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)
is a semidirect product
{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)=L\ltimes U^{a}
, where
U^{a}=U_{+}\ltimes U_{-}^{a}
.
The subgroup
U^{a}
is an algebra subgroup
U^{a}=1+{\mathfrak{u}}^{a}
, where

{\mathfrak{u}}^{a}={\mathfrak{u}}_{+}+{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}^{a}
is the nilradical of
\mathfrak{gl}^{a}(n)
.

Our goal is to construct a supercharacter theory for the group
{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)
following the general method from the paper
[
10
]
(where it is refered to as the
GG
-supercharacter theory).

3.1
Supercharacter theory for
U^{a}

Because
U^{a}
is an algbera subgroup, it has the supercharacter theory of P. Diaconis and I.M. Isaacs from
[
1
]
. The superclasses in algebra group
{\mathfrak{G}}=1+J
is the subsets of the form
1+{\mathfrak{G}}x{\mathfrak{G}}
, where
x\in J
.
The supercharacters
\chi_{\lambda}
of
{\mathfrak{G}}
are constructed by the following procedure. Fix the nontrivial character
t\mapsto{\varepsilon}^{t}
of the additive group of field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with values in
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Consider the system of representatives
\{{\lambda}\}
of the left-right orbits of the group
{\mathfrak{G}}\times{\mathfrak{G}}
in
J^{*}
. By each
{\lambda}
, we construct the linear character

\xi_{\lambda}(1+x)={\varepsilon}^{{\lambda}(x)}

of the stabilizer
{\mathfrak{G}}_{{\lambda},\mathrm{right}}
of the linear form
{\lambda}
with respect to the right action of
{\mathfrak{G}}
on
J^{*}
(see formula (
1
)). The supercharacter
\chi_{\lambda}
of the algebra group
{\mathfrak{G}}
is induced from
\xi_{\lambda}
to
{\mathfrak{G}}
. There is a formula for supercharacter

\chi_{\lambda}(1+x)=\frac{|{\mathfrak{G}}{\lambda}|}{|{\mathfrak{G}}{\lambda}{\mathfrak{G}}|}\sum_{\mu\in{\mathfrak{G}}{\lambda}{\mathfrak{G}}}{\varepsilon}^{\mu(x)}.

So, the superclasses and supercharacters of an algebra group
{\mathfrak{G}}
are uniquely determined by
({\mathfrak{G}}\times{\mathfrak{G}})
-orbits in
J
and
J^{*}
respectively.

In our case,
{\mathfrak{G}}=U^{a}
and a classification of
(U^{a}\times U^{a})
-orbits in
{\mathfrak{u}}^{a}
and
({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})^{*}
is considered an extremely difficult problem (see
[
10
,
14
,
16
]
).
Therefore, we consider the more ”coarse”  supercharacter theory (see Theorem
3.1
) in which superclasses are attached to the
({\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)\times{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n))
-orbits in
{\mathfrak{u}}^{a}
,
and supercharacters a up to a constant multiple equal to sums of functions

{\varepsilon}^{\mu(x)}
, where
\mu
runs through a
({\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)\times{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n))
-orbit in
({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})^{*}
.

Let
\Delta
denote the root system of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
. We attach to each root
{\alpha}=\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}
,
1\leqslant i,~j\leqslant n
,
i\neq j
, the pair
(i,j)
. To simplify notations, we write
{\alpha}=(i,j)
.
We refer to
i
as a row number of the root
{\alpha}
and to
j
as a column number of root
{\alpha}
. Notations:
i={\mathrm{row}}({\alpha})
and
j={\mathrm{col}}({\alpha})
.

A positive root
{\alpha}\in\Delta_{+}
is attached to a pair with
i<j
. Respectively, for an negative root
{\alpha}\in\Delta_{-}
, we have
i>j
.

We introduce the definition of
sum of roots
, which if different from usual one: a root
\gamma
is a sum
\gamma={\alpha}+\beta
if
{\alpha}=(i,j)
,
\beta=(j,k)
and
\gamma=(i,k)
.

To each root
{\alpha}\in\Delta_{+}
, we attach the matrix unit
E_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}
and to each root

{\alpha}\in\Delta_{-}
– the matrix unit
F_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{a}
.

Consider the subset
\Delta_{+}({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})
which consists of
{\alpha}\in\Delta_{+}
such that
E_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{u}}_{+}
. Respectively,
\Delta_{-}({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})=\{{\alpha}\in\Delta_{-}:~F_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{u}}_{-}^{a}\}
and
\Delta({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})=\Delta_{+}({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})\cup\Delta_{-}({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})
.

We realize
{\mathfrak{u}}^{a}
as the subspace of the linear space of
(n\times n)
-matrices spanned by the union of two systems of matrix units

\{E_{\alpha}:~{\alpha}\in\Delta_{+}({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})\}\cup\{F_{\alpha}:~{\alpha}\in\Delta_{-}({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})\}.

The structure relations have the form

•

E_{\alpha}E_{\beta}=E_{{\alpha}+\beta}
if
{\alpha}+\beta
is defined, otherwise
E_{\alpha}E_{\beta}=0
.

•

F_{\alpha}F_{\beta}=0
.

•

E_{\alpha}F_{\beta}=F_{{\alpha}+\beta}
if
{\alpha}+\beta
is defined and belongs to
\Delta_{-}({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})
, otherwise
E_{\alpha}F_{\beta}=0
.

•

F_{\alpha}E_{\beta}=F_{{\alpha}+\beta}
if
{\alpha}+\beta
is defined and belongs to
\Delta_{-}({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})
, otherwise
F_{\alpha}E_{\beta}=0
.

Observe that
{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}={\mathfrak{u}}_{+}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}(L)
, where
{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}(L)={\mathfrak{n}}_{+}\cap{\mathfrak{l}}
. Respectively,
{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{a}={\mathfrak{u}}_{-}^{a}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{a}_{-}(L)
, where
{\mathfrak{n}}^{a}_{-}(L)=\{{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{a}:~{\lambda}({\mathfrak{u}}_{+})=0\}
.

Lemma 3.1
. 1)
{\mathfrak{n}}^{a}_{-}(L)
is a two sided ideal in the associative algebra
\mathfrak{gl}^{a}(n)
.

2)
\mu_{L}x=x\nu_{L}=0
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{u}}_{+}
and
\mu_{L},~\nu_{L}\in{\mathfrak{n}}^{a}_{-}(L)
.

Proof
. The both statements follows from the fact that
{\mathfrak{u}}_{+}
is a two sided ideal in
{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}
.
\Box

For an arbitrary element
u=1+X\in U^{a}
, where
X\in{\mathfrak{u}}^{a}
, we consider the class

K(u)=\{1+AXB:~A,B\in{\mathrm{GL}}^{a}(n)\}.

The following Theorem
3.1
gives a classification of classes
K(u)
. We need new definitions.

A rook spacement
in
\Delta
is a subset
D\subset\Delta

that has at most one root in any row and colunm.
The rook spacement
D
is a union
D=D_{+}\cup D_{-}
, where
D_{\pm}=D\cap\Delta_{\pm}
.
In his papers, C.  André used the other term

\Delta_{+}

To each rook placement
D\subset\Delta({\mathfrak{u}}^{a})

\phi:D\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}

X_{D,\phi}\in{\mathfrak{u}}^{a}
of the form

X_{D,\phi}=\sum_{\gamma_{+}\in D_{+}}\phi(\gamma_{+})E_{\gamma_{+}}+\sum_{\gamma_{-}\in D_{-}}\phi(\gamma_{-})F_{\gamma_{-}}.

(2)

If
\phi
is identically equals to one on
D
, we take
X_{D,\phi}=X_{D}
.
Denote
u_{D}=1+X_{D}
.

For any rook placement
D\subset\Delta
and pairs of segments
I_{k},I_{m}
, where
k\neq m

r_{ij}=r_{ij}(D)=\mathrm{rank}\,X_{D}(I_{k}\times I_{m})=|D\cap(I_{k}\times I_{m})|,

n^{\prime}_{km}=\sum_{s<k}n_{s}+\sum_{t<m}r_{kt},

We say that a rook placement
D_{c}

1\leqslant k,m\leqslant\ell
,
k\neq m

(n_{km}^{\prime}+1,n_{km}^{\prime\prime}+1),\ldots,(n_{km}^{\prime}+r_{km},n_{km}^{\prime\prime}+r_{km}).

Let
W_{L}
be a Weyl group in
L
. For each
w\in W_{L}
, the action of
w
on the row numbers (respectively, the action of

\Delta
.

Lemma 3.2
. For any rook placement
D
there exist elements
w_{1},w_{2}\in W_{L}
such that
w_{1}Dw_{2}
is a rook placement

of canonical type. The rook placement

.

Proof
. For any rook placement
D
, there exists
w_{1}\in W_{L}
such that the system

has the required ordering. Multiplying by a suitable

we obtain the rook placement
D_{c}=w_{1}Dw_{2}
of canonical type. Uniqueness follows from the fact that the numbers
\{r_{km}\}
don’t change if we replace
D
by
w_{1}Dw_{2}
.
\Box

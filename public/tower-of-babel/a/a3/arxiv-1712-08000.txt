Abstract




ABSTRACT

BiHom-Novikov agebra is a generalized Hom-Novikov algebra endowed with two commuting multiplicative linear maps. The main purpose of this paper is to show that two classes of BiHom-Novikov algebras can be
constructed from BiHom-commutative algebras together with derivations and
BiHom-Novikov algebras with Rota-Baxter operators, respectively. We show
that quadratic BiHom-Novikov algebras are associative algebras and
the sub-adjacent BiHom-Lie algebras of BiHom-Novikov algebras are 2-step
nilpotent. Moreover, we develop the 1-parameter formal deformation theory of
BiHom-Novikov algebras

Key words
: BiHom-Novikov agebra, BiHom-Lie algebra, BiHom-associative algebra, derivation, Rota-Baxter operator

3
Quadratic BiHom-Novikov algebras

In this section, we extend the notions of quadratic Novikov algebras and quadratic Hom-Novikov algebras to quadratic BiHom-Novikov
algebras and provide some properties. we always assume that the structure maps
\alpha
and
\beta
are bijective.

Definition 3.1.
Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-Lie algebra and
B:A\times A\rightarrow k
be a bilinear form on
A
.

(1)
B
is said nondegenerate if

\displaystyle A^{\perp}=\{x\in A|B(x,y)=0,\forall y\in A\}=0.

(2)
B
is said symmetric if

\displaystyle B(x,y)=B(y,x),~~\mbox{for any $x,y\in A$}.

(3)
B
is said
\alpha\beta
-invariant if

\displaystyle B([\beta(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha(z))=B(\alpha(x),[\beta(y),\alpha(z)]),~~\mbox{for any $x,y,z\in A$}.

Definition 3.2.

A quadratic BiHom-Lie algebra is a quintuple
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha,\beta,B)
such that
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha,\beta)

is a BiHom-Lie algebra with a symmetric invariant nondegenerate bilinear form
B
satisfying

\displaystyle B(\alpha(x),y)=B(x,\alpha(y)),B(\beta(x),y)=B(x,\beta(y))~~~\mbox{for any $x,y\in A$}.

(3. 1)

We can define quadratic BiHom-Novikov algebras as follows.

Definition 3.2.
A quadratic BiHom-Novikov algebra is a quintuple
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta,B)
is a BiHom-Novikov algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)

with a symmetric invariant nondegenerate bilinear form
B
satisfying

\displaystyle B(\alpha(x),\beta(y)\alpha(z))=B(\beta(x)\alpha(y),\alpha(z)),~~~\mbox{for any $x,y,z\in A$}.

(3. 2)

Proposition 3.3.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta,B)
be a quadratic BiHom-Novikov algebra and
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha,\beta)

be the sub-adjacent BiHom-Lie algebra of
A
, if

\displaystyle B(\alpha(x),y)=B(x,\alpha(y)),B(\beta(x),y)=B(x,\beta(y)),~~~\mbox{for any $x,y\in A$}.

(3. 3)

Then
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha,\beta,B_{\alpha,\beta})
is a quadratic BiHom-Lie algebra, where
B_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)=B(\alpha(x),y)
.

Proof.
Since
B
is a nondegenerate bilinear form,
B_{\alpha}
is a nondegenerate
bilinear form on
A
. For all
x,y,z\in A
, using the properties of
B
, we have

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}([\beta(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\alpha[\beta(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B([\beta(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha^{2}(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\beta(x)\alpha(y),\alpha^{2}(z))-B(\beta(y)\alpha(x),\alpha^{2}(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\alpha^{2}(x),\beta(y)\alpha(z))-B(\alpha^{2}(x),\beta(z)\alpha(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\alpha^{2}(x),[\beta(y),\alpha(z)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}(\alpha(x),[\beta(y),\alpha(z)]).

Hence,
B_{\alpha}
is invariant. Using symmetry of
B
and equation (3.3), we have

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}(x,y)=B(\alpha(x),y)=B(y,\alpha(x))=B(\alpha(y),x)=B_{\alpha}(y,x).

which proves
B_{\alpha}
is symmetric. Using equation (3.3) again, we have

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}(\alpha(x),y)=B(\alpha(\alpha(x)),y)=B(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))=B_{\alpha}(x,\alpha(y)),

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}(\beta(x),y)=B(\alpha(\beta(x)),y)=B(\alpha(x),\beta(y))=B_{\alpha}(x,\beta(y)).

\square

Corollary 3.4.
Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta,B)
be a quadratic BiHom-Novikov algebra and
\alpha,\beta
satisfying
equation (3.4) and
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])

be the sub-adjacent Lie algebra, define a new multiplication on
A
by
[x,y]^{\prime}=[\alpha(x),\beta(y)]
. Then
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,B_{\alpha})

is a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra, where
B_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)=B(\alpha(x),y)
.

Proof.
Using the similar
arguments as those in the proof of Proposition 3.3, we get
B_{\alpha}
is a symmetric nondegenerate bilinear
form with equation (3.1) satisfied. It remains to show that
B_{\alpha}
is invariant. For all
x,y,z\in A
,
using invariance and symmetry of
B
, we have

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}([\beta(x),\alpha(y)]^{\prime},\alpha(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\alpha[\alpha\beta(x),\alpha\beta(y)],\alpha(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B([\alpha\beta(x),\alpha\beta(y)],\alpha^{2}(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\alpha\beta(x)\alpha\beta(y),\alpha^{2}(z))-B(\beta^{2}(y)\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha\beta(y)\alpha\beta(z))-B(\alpha^{2}(x),\beta(z)\alpha(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\alpha^{2}(x),[\alpha\beta(y),\alpha\beta(z)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}(\alpha(x),[\beta(y),\alpha(z)]^{\prime}).

\square

Proposition 3.5.
Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta,B)
be a quadratic BiHom-Novikov algebra, where
\alpha,\beta
are involution
satisfying equation (3.3), define a new multiplication on
A
by
x\star y=\alpha(x)\star\beta(y)
Then
(A,\star,\alpha,\beta,B)
is a quadratic Novikov algebra.

Proof.

(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta,B)
is a BiHom-Novikov algebra by Proposition 2.5. It suffices to show that
B

is invariant under the operation
\star
. For all
x,y,z\in A
, we have

\displaystyle B(\alpha(x),\beta(y)\star\alpha(z))=B(\alpha(x),\alpha\beta(y)\alpha\beta(z))

\displaystyle=B(\alpha\beta(x)\alpha\beta(y),\alpha(z))=B(\beta(x)\star\alpha(y),\alpha(z)),

which completes the proof.

\square

Theorem 3.6.
Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta,B)
be a quadratic BiHom-Novikov algebra, and
HLie(A)
be the sub-adjacent BiHom-Lie algebra. Then

[\beta(x),\alpha(y)]\in Z(A)
, for any
x,y\in HLie(A)
. As a consequence,
HLie(A)
is 2-step nilpotent.

Proof.
For any
x,y,z\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha(x)[y^{\prime},z^{\prime}]=\alpha(x)[\beta(y),\alpha(z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(x)(\beta(y)\alpha(z))-\alpha(x)(\beta(y)\alpha(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\beta(y))\alpha\beta(z)-(x\beta(y)\alpha\beta(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\beta(y))\alpha\beta(z)-(x\beta(y)\alpha\beta(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Using Equation (3.2), we have

\displaystyle B([y^{\prime},z^{\prime}]\alpha(d),\alpha^{2}(x))=B(\alpha(x)\alpha\beta^{-1}[y^{\prime},z^{\prime}],\alpha^{2}(d))=0

which implies
[x,y]\in Z(A)
since
\alpha,\beta
are automorphism and
B
is nondegenerate. Hence,
we have
[HLie(A),HLie(A)]\in Z(A)
. Obviously,
Z(A)\in C(HLie(A))
. Then it follows
that
HLie(A)
is 2-step nilpotent.

Let

n

Definition 4.2.
For

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\beta(x_{1})\ast f(x_{2},x_{3}-f(x_{1},x_{2})\ast\beta(x_{3})

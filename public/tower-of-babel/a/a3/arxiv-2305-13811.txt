Abstract
. For function germs g : ( ℂ n , 0 ) → ( ℂ , 0 ) g:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0) it is well known that 1 ≤ μ ⁡ ( g ) τ ⁡ ( g ) 1\leq\frac{\mu(g)}{\tau(g)} and it has recently been proved by Liu that μ ⁡ ( g ) τ ⁡ ( g ) ≤ n \frac{\mu(g)}{\tau(g)}\leq n . We give an upper bound for the codimension of map-germs f : ( ℂ n , 0 ) → ( ℂ p , 0 ) f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0) given as augmentations of other map-germs with which we prove the analog to the first inequality (known as Mond’s conjecture) for augmentations h : ( ℂ n , 0 ) → ( ℂ n + 1 , 0 ) h:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0) . Furthermore, we show that the quotient given by the image Milnor number and the codimension of any augmentation in the pair of dimensions ( n , n + 1 ) (n,n+1) is less than 1 4 ​ ( n + 1 ) 2 \frac{1}{4}(n+1)^{2} and prove the analog to the second inequality for map-germs with n = 1 n=1 and augmentations with n = 2 , 3 n=2,3 . We then prove a characterization of when a map-germ is an augmentation, finding a counterexample for the characterization given by Houston. Next, we give sufficient conditions for when the augmentation is independent of the choice of stable unfolding by studying different notions of equivalence of unfoldings. Moreover, these results allow us to give sufficient conditions for the simplicity of an augmentation, providing context to locate the moduli for non-simple augmentations.

1.
Introduction

Classification of singularities of map-germs
f:(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
with
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{R}
has been an active field of research ever since Whitney classified stable maps from surfaces to the plane. Since then many landmark classifications have taken place, such as Arnold’s classification of simple, uni-modal and bi-modal functions (
[
3
]
) or Mond’s classification under the group
\mathscr{A}
(diffeomorphisms in source and target) of simple singularities of surfaces in 3-space (
[
24
]
). It is also worth mentioning Goryunov’s method for corank 1 maps germs with
n\geq p
(
[
13
]
), and the classifications of corank 1 simple germs for
n=p=2
(Rieger in
[
29
]
),
n=p=3
(Marar and Tari in
[
23
]
) and
n=3,p=4
(Houston and Kirk in
[
18
]
). Simplicity seems to have been a reasonable criteria to stop classifying singularities when
p>1
. While modality (i.e. lack of simplicity) has been thoroughly studied for functions (see
[
34
,
11
]
, for example) with striking connections to the topology (
\mu
-constant stratum) and algebraic invariants of the singularities, for the more complicated case of
p>1
not so much can be said.

In the mentioned classifications for
p>1
, it is interesting to see that most simple germs are augmentations of other germs in lower dimensions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
be a smooth map-germ with a 1-parameter stable unfolding
F(x,\lambda)=(f_{\lambda}(x),\lambda)
.
Given any smooth function
g\colon(\mathbb{K}^{d},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
, the
augmentation of
f
by
F
via
g
will be the
\mathscr{A}
-class of the map-germ in
(\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{d},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p}\times\mathbb{K}^{d},0)
given by:

A_{F,g}(f)(x,z)=\left(f_{g(z)}(x),z\right)

We will say that
f
is the base or the augmented map-germ, and that
g
is the augmenting function.

Understanding the simplicity (or lack of it) of these augmentations is the first step towards understanding modality in the general case.
In a previous paper (
[
4
]
) we managed to characterize the simplicity of augmentations in certain cases: if
f
has codimension 1, then
A_{F,g}(f)(x,z)
is simple if and only if
g
is simple, and if
g
is a Morse function, then
A_{F,g}(f)(x,z)
is simple if and only if
f
is simple.
However, what happens when we augment a germ
f
of codimension 2 by a non-Morse function is much more subtle.
For example the augmentation of the codimension 2 germ
(y^{2},y^{5})
by the function
g(x)=x^{3}
yields the germ
F_{4}=(x,y^{2},y^{5}+x^{3}y)
from Mond’s list, which is simple, but the augmentation of the codimension 2 germ
11_{5}=(x,y^{4}+x^{2}y+xy^{2})
from Rieger’s list by
g(z)=z^{3}
is not simple.

It seems that a crucial part of understanding this phenomenon is analysing the independence of the augmentation from the choice of 1-parameter stable unfolding. While this follows from versality in the codimension 1 case (see
[
6
]
for
g
Morse and
[
4
]
for any
g
with isolated singularity), it is not trivial for higher codimension.

On the other hand, some of the biggest open problems in singularities of mappings have to do with the codimension of a germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
(
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)
) and its image Milnor number
\mu_{I}(f)
(the number of spheres in a stable perturbation). For functions
g:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
it is well-known that
\tau\leq\mu
with equality if
g
is quasihomogeneous. The analog result in mappings is only known for
n=1,2
(
[
26
,
25
,
19
]
) and is still open in general (see also
[
10
]
).

Conjecture 1 (Mond’s conjecture):
Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a smooth map germ, then

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\leq\mu_{I}(f)

with equality if
f
is quasihomogeneous.

Related to this problem, Liu has recently proved that for functions
g:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
we always have
\frac{\mu}{\tau}\leq n
. Obviously this bound is not sharp. Sharp bounds for

\frac{\mu}{\tau}
have been found when
n=2,3
(see
[
1
,
2
]
), although it is not known whether these bounds tend to infinity or not when
n
tends to infinity. We can think of the analog problem for mappings, which was communicated to the authors by Peñafort Sanchis:

Conjecture 2:
Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a smooth map germ, then:

\frac{\mu_{I}(f)}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)}\leq n+1.

In this paper we analyze all of these problems for augmentations. Section 2 gives the necessary notation. In
[
17
]
Houston gave a lower bound for the codimension of an augmentation. In Section 3 we give an upper bound and characterize the equality with the lower bound. Using these bounds and results by Houston (
[
16
]
) and Giménez Conejero and Nuño-Ballesteros (
[
12
]
) we prove Mond’s conjecture (Conjecture 1) for augmentations (without the hypotheses used by the previous authors). We also give some partial converses for the equality. We then prove Conjecture 2 for any map germ with
n=1
, for any augmentations with
n=2
and, under some hypothesis, for any augmentation with
n=3
. In general we prove that for augmentations the quotient in Conjecture 2 is less than or equal to
\frac{1}{4}(n+1)^{2}
.

In Theorem 4.4 of
[
17
]
, Houston gave a characterization for when a map germ is an augmentation. In Section 4 we construct a counterexample to his theorem and then prove a characterization that needs some extra conditions. We also give a geometric interpretation of this characterization.

Sections 5 to 8 introduce several notions of equivalence between unfoldings and relate them to the simplicity of the augmentations. In Section 5 we prove that the
\phi
-equivalence of 1-parameter stable unfoldings defined by Mancini and Ruas in
[
22
]
implies the
\mathscr{A}
-equivalence of the augmentations and give sufficient conditions for an augmentation to be simple in terms of the number of
\phi
-equivalence classes of the unfoldings. For the case of non-simple augmentations we manage to locate the modality in certain strata of the versal unfolding. In Section 6 we define the notion of weak equivalence between unfoldings and show that it preserves substantiality (see Section 2 for definitions). We also give a sufficient condition for all the unfoldings to be weak equivalent. Then, in Section 7 we show that if all 1-parameter stable unfoldings are weak-equivalent and one of them is substantial, then they are all
\phi
-equivalent.

The equivalences of unfoldings and the sufficient conditions for them to happen, which depend on tangent vector fields to the discriminant of the unfolding, are interesting by themselves and may have applications in other contexts such as deformation theory.

We give plenty of examples throughout the paper which help understand the definitions and scope of the results presented. Moreover, in Section 8 we analyze the simplicity of certain augmentations when
n=p
and thus give a proof of a conjecture we stated in
[
4
]
by giving a classification of all corank 1 simple augmentations from
\mathbb{C}^{4}
to
\mathbb{C}^{4}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Through most of this text, we will be working indistinctly over
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{R}
, denoting any of them by
\mathbb{K}
.
In the real case,
smooth
will mean infinitely differentiable; in the complex case, it will mean holomorphic.
For
s\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by
\mathcal{O}_{s}
the local ring of germs of smooth functions
(\mathbb{K}^{s},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{s}
and
\theta_{s}=\mathcal{O}_{s}\times\overset{s}{\cdots}\times\mathcal{O}_{s}
the
\mathcal{O}_{s}
-module of vector fields in
\mathbb{K}^{s}
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
be a smooth map-germ: we denote by
\theta(f)
the
\mathcal{O}_{n}
-module of vector fields along
f
, which can be identified with
\mathcal{O}_{n}\times\overset{p}{\cdots}\times\mathcal{O}_{n}
.
Recall that
\mathscr{A}=\text{Diff}_{0}(n)\times\text{Diff}_{0}(p)
acts over the set of map-germs from
\mathbb{K}^{n}\to\mathbb{K}^{p}
with composition in the source and target, being
\text{Diff}_{0}(s)
the group of germs of diffeomorphisms
(\mathbb{K}^{s},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{s},0)
.
Similarly,
\mathscr{R}=\text{Diff}_{0}(n)
acts on map-germs via composition on the source.
Lastly,
\mathscr{K}
is the subgroup of
\text{Diff}_{0}(n+p)
given by diffeomorphisms of the form
\Phi(x,X)=(\phi(x),\psi(x,X))
where
\phi\in\text{Diff}_{0}(n)
and
\psi(x,0)=0
.
They act on each
{f}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
by assigning the map-germ
\psi\left(\phi^{-1}(x),f(\phi^{-1}(x)\right)
.

Associated to
f
we can define the mappings
tf\colon\theta_{n}\to\theta(f)
,
\omega f\colon\theta_{p}\to\theta(f)
, given by
tf(\xi)=df(\xi)
and
\omega f(\eta)=\eta\circ f
for each
\xi\in\theta_{n}
,
\eta\in\theta_{p}
.
Then, we define the extended
\mathscr{A}
-tangent space and the extended
\mathscr{A}
-normal space as:

\displaystyle T\mathscr{A}_{e}f

\displaystyle=tf(\theta_{n})+\omega f(\theta_{p})

\displaystyle N\mathscr{A}_{e}f

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta(f)}{tf(\theta_{n})+\omega f(\theta_{p})}

and the
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension as
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}N\mathscr{A}_{e}f
.
We say that
f
is
\mathscr{A}
-finite if
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)<\infty
.
Similarly, for
\mathscr{K}
-equivalence we have:

N\mathscr{K}_{e}f=\frac{\theta(f)}{T\mathscr{K}_{e}f}=\frac{\theta(f)}{tf(\theta_{n})+f^{*}\mathfrak{m}_{p}\theta(f)}

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{f}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
be a smooth map-germ, a 1-parameter stable unfolding (OPSU)
F
of
f
is a map-germ
F\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p}\times\mathbb{K},0)
of the form
F(x,\lambda)=(f_{\lambda}(x),\lambda)
with
f_{0}(x)=f(x)
which is stable as a map-germ.

We will often denote
\bar{f}(x,\lambda)=f_{\lambda}(x)
.
Then
d_{x}\bar{f}
will denote the Jacobian matrix of
\bar{f}
with respect to the first
n

d_{\lambda}\bar{f}

Note specifically that
d_{\lambda}\bar{f}\in\theta(\bar{f})
.
We will denote the target variables of
F
as
(X,\Lambda)=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p},\Lambda)
, and the constant vector fields in
\mathbb{K}^{p}\times\mathbb{K}

\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{p}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}
.
For more information on 1-parameter stable unfoldings and their relevance, see
[
5

.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
{f}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
be a smooth map-germ.
The
isosingular locus
of
f
is the germ at 0 of the set of points
y\in\mathbb{K}^{p}
such that
f\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},f^{-1}(y))\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},y)
is
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent to
f
.
This is a well defined germ of a submanifold in
\mathbb{K}^{p}
, often called the
analytic stratum of
f
, and its tangent space at
0
is denoted by
\tilde{\tau}(f)
.

See Chapter 7, section 7.2 in
[
27
]
for more information on the isosingular locus.
For the purposes of this paper, it is sufficient to know that, given
F
a stable map-germ, the dimension of
\tilde{\tau}(f)
is equal to the maximum
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
F\sim_{\mathscr{A}}H\times Id_{d}
, for
H
a stable map-germ.

Recall that the Tjurina number of a function
g\in\mathcal{O}_{d}
is defined as:

\tau(g)=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{d}}{Jg+g}

where
Jg+g
is the ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{d}
generated by the partial derivatives of
g
and
g
.
We say that a function
g\in\mathcal{O}_{d}
is quasi-homogeneous if there exist weights
w,w_{1},\ldots,w_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
:

g(\lambda^{w_{1}}z_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{w_{d}}z_{d})=\lambda^{w}g(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})

Similarly, a map-germ
{f}\colon({\mathbb{K}}^{n},0)\to({\mathbb{K}}^{p},0)
will be quasi-homogeneous if there exist weights such that each component of
f
is quasi-homogeneous with respect to those same weights.

Definition 2.5
.

Given
{f}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
, a vector field
\eta\in\theta_{p}
is said to be liftable if there exists
\xi\in\theta_{n}
such that
tf(\xi)=\omega f(\eta)
.
The set of liftable vector fields of
f
is denoted by
\text{Lift}(f)
. The evaluation at 0 of vector fields in
\text{Lift}(f)
gives precisely
\tilde{\tau}(f)
(see pages 281–283 in
[
27
]
).
In many cases,
\operatorname{Lift}(f)=\operatorname{Derlog}(\Delta f)
; in particular, this holds when
f
is stable.

If
\pi\colon(\mathbb{K}^{p}\times\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
is the canonical projection given by
\pi(X,\Lambda)=\Lambda
, then the differential
d\pi
projects vector fields in
\theta_{p+1}
to their last component.
In particular, if
M\subseteq\theta_{p+1}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
-module,
d\pi(M)
is the ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
generated by all the functions in the last component of the vector fields in
M
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
F
be an OPSU of
{f}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
, we define the
degree of substantiality
of
F
as the minimum
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Lambda^{m}\in d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))
.
We denote it by
\delta(F)=m
.
If there is no such minimum, we write
\delta(F)=\infty

The last definition aims to generalize the concept of
substantial unfolding
defined by Houston in
[
17
]
: we say in particular that an OPSU is substantial if it has degree of substantiality equal to
1
.
Notice that any quasi-homogeneous OPSU is substantial, since in this case the Euler vector field
\sum_{i=1}^{p}w_{i}X_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}+w_{p+1}\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}
for some weights
w_{1},\ldots,w_{p},w_{p+1}\in\mathbb{N}
is liftable.

In particular, if
f
is quasi-homogeneous and admits an OPSU, we can find a vector field
\gamma\in\theta(f)
so that
(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)
is stable and quasi-homogeneous.
This is due to the fact that this
\gamma
will be the only non-constant
\mathbb{K}
-generator of the extended
\mathscr{K}
-normal space,
and so
\gamma
in this space will always lie on the class of a vector field with all components equal to 0 except for one with a single monomial in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
Given a representative
\gamma
in this form, if
f
is quasi-homogeneous,
(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)
will also be quasi-homogeneous.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
F(x,\lambda)=(f_{\lambda}(x),\lambda)
be a substantial unfolding of
f
(i.e.,
\delta(F)=1
), then there exist
\eta(X,\Lambda)=(\eta_{1}(X,\Lambda),\ldots,\eta_{p}(X,\Lambda),\Lambda)\in\text{Lift}(F)
and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n+1})\in\theta_{n+1}
such that
dF(\xi)=\eta\circ F
.
Computing
dF
, we see that this implies
\xi_{n+1}(x,\lambda)=\lambda
.

Then, by the Thom-Levine lemma (see Lemma 2.6 from
[
27
]
or Proposition 4.12 in
[
30
]
), we can integrate these vector fields to find families of diffeomorphisms
\psi_{t},\phi_{t}
such that
\psi_{t}\circ F=F\circ\phi_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{K}
small enough,
\phi_{0}=\text{id}_{n+1},\psi_{0}=\text{id}_{p+1}
and
\frac{\partial\psi_{t}}{\partial t}=\eta\circ\psi_{t},\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial t}=\xi\circ\phi_{t}
.

In particular, if
\psi_{t}=(\psi_{t}^{1},\ldots,\psi_{t}^{p+1})
, we can deduce from the last condition that
\frac{\partial\psi_{t}^{p+1}}{\partial t}=\eta^{p+1}\circ\psi_{t}=\psi_{t}^{p+1}
, and so solving the differential equation we can consider
\psi_{t}^{p+1}(X,\Lambda)=\Lambda e^{t}
.
Similarly, we can take
\phi_{t}(x,\lambda)=(\phi_{t}^{1}(x,\lambda),\ldots,\phi_{t}^{n}(x,\lambda),\lambda e^{t})
.
Moreover, for each
t\in\mathbb{K}
small enough, the inverse of
\phi_{t}
is also a diffeomorphism of the form
\phi_{t}^{-1}=(\phi_{t}^{-1,1},\ldots,\phi_{t}^{-1,n},\lambda e^{-t})
.

These diffeomorphisms, which maintain the parameter variable on the last component, will be of importance in later sections. In particular, in Proposition
6.6
we show that they preserve the property of an unfolding being substantial.
Diffeomorphisms
(\Phi,\Psi)\in\mathscr{A}
such that
\Psi\circ F=F\circ\Phi
form a subgroup called the
isotropy group
or

, and is often denoted by
\mathscr{A}_{F}
.
If
(\Phi,\Psi)\in\mathscr{A}
are diffeomorphisms such that
G\circ\Phi=\Psi\circ F
, then
\mathscr{A}_{G}=(\Phi,\Psi)\cdot\mathscr{A}_{F}\cdot(\Phi^{-1},\Psi^{-1})
.

3.
Codimension and Image Milnor number

For the purposes of this section, we will consider only the complex analytic case,
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
.
In
[
17
]
, Houston proved the following inequality:

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))\geq\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\tau(g)

with equality if
g
is quasi-homogeneous or
F
is substantial.
In fact he introduced there the concept of
substantiality
and related it to this result.

The goal of this section is to find an upper bound for the codimension of an augmentation, which will help us tackle Conjectures 1 and 2 for the case of augmentations.

Definition 3.1
.

The Briançon-Skoda exponent of a smooth function
g\colon(\mathbb{K}^{d},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
is defined as the minimum positive integer
r
such that
g^{r}\in Jg
.
We denote it by
BS(g)=r
.

The fact that the Briançon-Skoda number is well defined comes from
[
32
]
, where it is proven that
BS(g)\leq d
for every
g\in\mathcal{O}_{d}
.

We now want to provide an upper bound to the codimension of an augmentation, and extend the conditions of Houston’s theorem to provide an if and only if condition on the equality of the lower bound.
Our setting is the following: let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
be a complex analytic map-germ that admits an OPSU
F(x,\lambda)=(f_{\lambda}(x),\lambda)
.
If
\pi\colon(\mathbb{C}^{p+1},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
is the projection
\pi(X,\Lambda)=\Lambda
, then
d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))
is the ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
generated by the last components of the liftable vector fields of
F
.
By Damon’s Theorem
2.2
we have that:

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{p+1}}{d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda\rangle}

where
\langle\Lambda\rangle
is the ideal generated by
\Lambda
in
\mathcal{O}_{p}+1
.
Hence, if
k=\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)
, for
i=1,\ldots,k
, we can find
h_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
such that:

\frac{\mathcal{O}_{p+1}}{d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda\rangle}=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{[h_{1}(X,\Lambda)],\ldots,[h_{k}(X,\Lambda)]\right\}

where
[h_{i}]
are the classes in the quotient of each
h_{i}
.
Moreover by Hadamard’s Lemma we can find for each
i
some
\tilde{h_{i}}\in\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
such that
h_{i}(X,\Lambda)=h_{i}(X,0)+\Lambda\tilde{h_{i}}(X,\Lambda)
.
Since
\Lambda\tilde{h_{i}}\in\langle\Lambda\rangle
, we have that
[h_{i}(X,\Lambda)]=[h_{i}(X,0)]
.
That is, we can take a basis of this quotient with representatives that do not depend on
\Lambda
.
We will write this basis from now on as
h_{1}(X),\ldots,h_{k}(X)
.

Consider
g\colon(\mathbb{K}^{d},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
a smooth function such that:

\mu(g)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{d}}{Jg}<\infty

where
Jg
is the ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{d}
generated by the partial derivatives of
g
.
The number
\mu(g)
is called the Milnor number of
g
, and it is finite precisely when
g
is finitely
\mathscr{R}
-determined.
There exist some
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\in\mathcal{O}_{d}
with
r=\mu(g)
such that:

\frac{\mathcal{O}_{d}}{Jg}=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{m_{1}(Z),\ldots,m_{r}(Z)\right\}

Then:

\frac{\mathcal{O}_{p+1}}{d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda\rangle}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{d}}{Jg}=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{h_{i}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}m_{j}(Z)\right\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,k\\
j=1,\ldots,r\end{subarray}}

In the analytic case:

\frac{\mathcal{O}_{p+1+d}}{d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda\rangle+Jg}\cong\frac{\mathcal{O}_{p+1}}{d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda\rangle}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{d}}{Jg}

Hence, via the isomorphism that multiplies monomials:

(1)

\frac{\mathcal{O}_{p+1+d}}{d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda\rangle+Jg}=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)\right\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,k\\
j=1,\ldots,r\end{subarray}}

Now, Houston proved in
[
17
]
that the
\mathscr{A}_{e}
codimension of the augmentation of
f
by
F
via
g
is given by the dimension as a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of the quotient:

(2)

B=\frac{\mathcal{O}_{p+1+d}}{d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda-g\rangle+Jg}

where
\langle\Lambda-g\rangle
is the ideal generated by the function
\Lambda-g(Z)
.
We will now see that this dimension is leser than or equal to
kr=\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\mu(g)
.
For
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{p+d+1}
, denote by
[\alpha]_{B}
the class of
\alpha
in
B
.
We will proceed with all this setting and notation for the following two results.

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{p+d+1}
, there exist unique
\alpha_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
and some
b\in\mathcal{O}_{p+d+1}
such that:

[\alpha]_{B}=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)\alpha_{ij}+g(Z)b(X,Z,\Lambda)\right]_{B}

Proof.

By Equation
1
, we can find unique
\alpha_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
and some
\eta\in d\pi(\text{Lift}(f)),b\in\mathcal{O}_{p+d+1}
and
c\in Jg
such that we can decompose
\alpha
as:

\alpha(X,Z,\Lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)\alpha_{ij}+\eta(X,Z,\Lambda)+\Lambda b(X,Z,\Lambda)+c(X,Z,\Lambda)

Now, add and substract
g(Z)b(X,Z,\Lambda)
on the right-hand side to get:

\alpha(X,Z,\Lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)\alpha_{ij}+g(Z)b(X,Z,\Lambda)\\
+\eta(X,Z,\Lambda)+(\Lambda-g(Z))b(X,Z,\Lambda)+c(X,Z,\Lambda)

Taking classes in
B
we obtain the desired result.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
be a smooth map-germ with an OPSU
F(x,\lambda)=(f_{\lambda}(x),\lambda)
and
g\colon(\mathbb{C}^{d},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
a smooth function with isolated singularity.
Then:

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\tau(g)\leq\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))\leq\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\mu(g)

Moreover, the lower bound is an equality if and only if
g
is quasi-homogeneous or
F
is substantial. If
g
is quasi-homogeneous, the upper bound is also an equality.

Proof.

The lower bound is given in
[
17
]
, along with the
if
condition of the equality.
For the
only if
condition, in the cited proof it is seen that:

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{p+1+d}}{d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda-g(Z)\rangle+Jg}

Notice that the vector fields in
\text{Lift}(F)
only depend on
X,\Lambda
, and the functions in
Jg
only depend on
Z
.
The following inclusion holds:

d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda-g(Z)\rangle+Jg\subseteq d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda,g(Z)\rangle+Jg

The ideal in the right-hand side of the inclusion has codimension over
\mathcal{O}_{p+1+d}
as a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space equal to
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\tau(g)
.

If we assume
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))=\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\tau(g)
then both ideals have the same codimension, and since one is contained in the other they must be equal.
Since
\Lambda
cannot be found in the ideal generated by
Jg
and
g
, either
g
is in
Jg
, or
\Lambda\in d\Lambda(\text{Lift}(F))
.
In the former case, Saito proved in
[
31
]
that this means
g
is (
\mathscr{R}
-equivalent to a) quasi-homogeneous function; in the latter, it means
F
is substantial.

To obtain the upper bound, we will see that the classes of
h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)
also span
B
over
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{p+d+1}
.
By Lemma
3.2
there exist
b_{1}\in\mathcal{O}_{p+d+1}
and some
\alpha_{ij}^{0}\in\mathbb{C}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
and
j=1,\ldots,k
such that:

[\alpha]_{B}=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)\alpha^{0}_{ij}+g(Z)b_{1}(X,Z,\Lambda)\right]_{B}

We can apply Lemma
3.2
again to find
\alpha_{ij}^{1}\in\mathbb{C}
and
b_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{p+d+1}
such that:

[b_{1}]_{B}=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)\alpha^{1}_{ij}+g(Z)b_{2}(X,Z,\Lambda)\right]_{B}

Denote by
q=\min\{BS(g),\delta(F)\}
.
Proceeding by finite induction, we can repeatedly apply the same lemma to obtain some
b_{1},\ldots,b_{q}\in\mathcal{O}_{p+d+1}
and some
\alpha_{ij}^{l}\in\mathbb{C}
such that for each
l=1,\ldots,q-1
:

[b_{l}]_{B}=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)\alpha^{l}_{ij}+g(Z)b_{l+1}(X,Z,\Lambda)\right]_{B}

Hence:

\displaystyle[\alpha]_{B}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)\sum_{l=0}^{q-1}g(Z)^{l}\alpha_{ij}^{l}+g(Z)^{q}b_{q}(X,Z,\Lambda)\right]_{B}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)\sum_{l=0}^{q-1}g(Z)^{l}\alpha_{ij}^{l}\right]_{B}

Where the last equality comes from the fact that
g(Z)^{q}
belongs to the ideal
d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda-g\rangle+Jg
.
This can be seen like this: if
q=BS(g)
, then
g(Z)^{q}\in Jg
.
If
q=\delta(F)
, this implies
\Lambda^{q}\in d\pi(\text{Lift}(f))
, and we have:

g(Z)^{q}=\Lambda^{q}-\sum_{i=1}^{q}\Lambda^{q-i}g(Z)^{i-1}(\Lambda-g(Z))\in d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda-g\rangle

Now, for each
j=1,\ldots,r
and
l=0,\ldots,q-1
we have
m_{j}(Z)g(Z)^{l}\in\mathcal{O}_{d}
and so we can find some
\beta_{s}^{jl}\in\mathbb{C}
and a function
C_{jl}\in Jg
such that:

m_{j}(Z)g(Z)^{l}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}m_{s}(Z)\beta_{s}^{jl}+C_{jl}(Z)

That is:

[\alpha]_{B}=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{l=0}^{q-1}h_{i}(X)m_{j}(Z)g(Z)^{l}\alpha_{ij}^{l}\right]_{B}=\\
\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{l=0}^{q-1}h_{i}(X)\alpha_{ij}^{l}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{r}m_{s}(Z)\beta_{s}^{jl}+C_{jl}(Z)\right)\right]_{B}

Now, using that
C_{jl}\in Jg
for all
j,l
we have that:

\displaystyle[\alpha]_{B}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{l=0}^{q-1}h_{i}(X)\alpha_{ij}^{l}\sum_{s=1}^{r}m_{s}(Z)\beta_{s}^{jl}\right]_{B}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{r}h_{i}(X)m_{s}(Z)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{l=0}^{q-1}\alpha_{ij}^{l}\beta_{s}^{jl}\right)\right]_{B}

Therefore, the class of every
\alpha
lies in the class of some
\mathbb{C}
-linear combination of the
h_{i}(X),m_{s}(Z)
.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

The
only if
part for equality with the lower bound is important in the sense that if we find a germ
f
which admits an OPSU
F
which is substantial and another OPSU
F^{\prime}
which is not and we augment by a non-quasi-homogeneous function
g
, then
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))<\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F^{\prime},g}(f))
and so the
\mathscr{A}

Although the use of
\delta(F)
seems redundant in the proof (since the Briançon-Skoda number is always finite) we have chosen to use it since it seems like both numbers play a role in determining the
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension of the augmentation.

Remark 3.6
.

The previous example and further extensive study of examples suggests that a smaller upper bound is given by the last column in Table
1
, i.e.
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))\leq\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\tau(g)+\mu(g)-\tau(g)
, with equality if and only if
\delta(F)\geq BS(g)
. This however is not true for the last two examples in the table. The reason seems to be that these examples are augmentations of augmentations and for that upper bound to hold one must consider the Milnor and Tjurina numbers of the join of all the augmenting functions that lead to this germ. This makes things much more complicated and we have not been able to prove this. However, the Milnor and Tjurina numbers of the join of
DG_{3}
and
DG_{3}
are
900
and
738
respectively, and of the join of
M
with
M
are
46225
and
33267
respectively. Here
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\tau(g)+\mu(g)-\tau(g)
is equal to
1638
for the first case and
79492
for the second, so the conjecture still holds.

We now comment on the Mond conjecture for augmentations. The Mond conjecture for a map-germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
states that
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\leq\mu_{I}(f)
with equality if
f
is quasi-homogeneous.
Here
\mu_{I}(f)
is the image Milnor number, an analytic invariant which counts the number of spheres in the image of a stable perturbation of
f
. In Theorem 6.7 in
[
16
]
Houston proves that if a corank 1 map-germ
f
satisfies Mond’s conjecture and
g
defines an isolated hypersurface singularity then, given that
f
or
g
are quasi-homogeneous,
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\tau(g)=\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))\leq\mu_{I}(A_{F,g}(f))=\mu_{I}(f)\mu(g)
with equality if both
f
and
g
are quasi-homogeneous (where
\mu(g)
is the Milnor number of
g
). In the proof he uses his Theorem 3.3 in
[
15
]
, which is a previous version of Theorem 4.4 in
[
17
]
, so, using this second theorem in the proof instead of the first one, the condition for the inequality of
f
being quasi-homogeneous can be substituted for
F
being substantial.

On the other hand, in Corollary 2.17 of
[
12
]
, Giménez Conejero and Nuño-Ballesteros note that Houston’s equality
\mu_{I}(A_{F,g}(f))=\mu_{I}(f)\mu(g)
is true for any corank. Using their Corollary 2.16 which states that any
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension 1 germ of any corank satisfies the Mond conjecture, they conclude that any augmentation of any
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension 1 germ satisfies the Mond conjecture.

Using these results and our Theorem
3.3
we can give a much more general result.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
(of any corank) admit an OPSU
F
and suppose that
f
satisfies Mond’s conjecture. Let
g
define an isolated hypersurface singularity. Then

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))\leq\mu_{I}(A_{F,g}(f)),

with equality if
f
and
g
are quasi-homogeneous.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))

\displaystyle\leq\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\mu(g)\text{, by Theorem \ref{thm_ineq_iff},}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{I}(f)\mu(g)\text{, by Mond's conjecture for $f$,}

\displaystyle=\mu_{I}(A_{F,g}(f))\text{, by Corollary 6.4 in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{houstoninventiones}{}{}]} and Corollary 2.17 in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{gimeneznunoweak}{}{}]}.}

Now, if
g
is quasi-homogeneous, the first inequality turns to an equality, and if
f
is quasi-homogeneous, the second inequality turns to an equality.
∎

Notice also that by construction, if
f
is quasi-homogeneous it admits a quasi-homogeneous OPSU
F(x,\lambda)=(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)
, and if
g
is also quasi-homogeneous then
A_{F,g}(f)
is quasi-homogeneous. We can also prove some partial converses for the equality in Theorem
3.7
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
f
admit an OPSU
F(x,\lambda)=(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)
, suppose it satisfies the Mond conjecture and
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))=\mu_{I}(A_{F,g}(f)).
Then

i)

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)=\mu_{I}(f)
.

ii)

If
f
is quasi-homogeneous, then
g
is
\mathscr{R}
-equivalent to a quasi-homogeneous function.

Proof.

For i) by Theorem
3.3

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))\leq\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\mu(g)
, so we get
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\mu(g)=\mu_{I}(f)\mu(g)
, so we get
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)=\mu_{I}(f)
.

For ii)
f
quasi-homogeneous implies that
F
is substantial, so again by Theorem
3.3

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\tau(g)=\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))
, and since the Mond conjecture with
f
quasi-homogeneous implies
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)=\mu_{I}(f)
, we get
\tau(g)=\mu(g)
, which by Saito implies that
g
is
\mathscr{R}
-equivalent to a quasi-homogeneous function.
∎

Next we turn our attention to Conjecture 2.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
f:(\mathbb{C},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, then

\frac{\mu_{I}(f)}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)}\leq 2,

i.e. all parametrized plane curves satisfy Conjecture 2.

Proof.

All
f:(\mathbb{C},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
admits
g:(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
such that
g\circ f=0
, and vice-versa, for all plane curve
g
there is a parametrisation
f
. Now, by
[
26
]
,
\mu_{I}(f)=\delta-r+1
where
\delta
is the delta invariant of
g
and
r
is the number of branches. Since
\mu(g)=2\delta-r+1
we get
\mu_{I}(f)=\mu(g)-\delta
. On the other hand in Proposition II.2.30(5) of
[
14
]
, it is proved that
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)=\tau(g)-\delta
. Now, since in our case
r=0
, we have
\delta=\mu/2
, so we get

\frac{\mu_{I}(f)}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)}=\frac{\mu(g)-\delta}{\tau(g)-\delta}=\frac{\frac{\mu(g)}{2}}{\tau(g)-\frac{\mu(g)}{2}}=\frac{\frac{\mu(g)}{\tau(g)}}{2-\frac{\mu(g)}{\tau(g)}}.

By
[
1
]

\frac{\mu(g)}{\tau(g)}<\frac{4}{3}
, so we get

\frac{\mu_{I}(f)}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)}<2.

∎

Next, notice that Conjecture 2 is trivially satisfied for all augmentations from
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
to
(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
. In fact, the only parametrized plane curves which admit an OPSU are those
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent to
f_{k}=(y^{2},y^{2k+1})
for some
k\geq 1
. The OPSU is given by
F_{k}=(y^{2},y^{2k+1}+\lambda y,\lambda)
. All of the
f_{k}
are quasi-homogeneous so
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f_{k})=\mu_{I}(f_{k})
. Now, in order to have an augmentation with
n=2
, the augmenting function

must have one variable only, and hence
\mathscr{R}
-equivalent to a quasi-homogeneous function (an

-singularity), which implies that

. Therefore

\frac{\mu_{I}(A_{F_{k},g}(f_{k}))}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F_{k},g}(f_{k}))}=\frac{\mu_{I}(f_{k})\mu(g)}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f_{k})\tau(g)}=1.

In conclusion

Proposition 3.10
.

Let

be an augmentation. Then

is
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent to

and so

\frac{\mu_{I}(h)}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(h)}=1\leq 3,

i.e.

satisfies Conjecture 2.

In general we can say the following

Theorem 3.11
.

Suppose that Conjecture 2 is satisfied for all map germs
f:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{m+1},0)
and for all
m<n
. Let

be an augmentation. Then

\frac{\mu_{I}(h)}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(h)}\leq\frac{1}{4}(n+1)^{2}.

Proof.

If

is an augmentation, then

for a germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{k},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{k+1},0)
with OPSU F and an augmenting function

. By Theorem
3.3

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\tau(g)\leq\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))
. By Conjecture 2 for

\frac{\mu_{I}(f)}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)}\leq k+1
, and by
[

\frac{\mu(g)}{\tau(g)}\leq n-k

\frac{\mu_{I}(A_{F,g}(f))}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(A_{F,g}(f))}=\frac{\mu_{I}(f_{k})\mu(g)}{\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f_{k})\tau(g)}\leq(k+1)(n-k).

Since

, the maximum of the function

is achieved when

and substituting we get
\frac{1}{4}(n+1)^{2}

∎

Corollary 3.12
.

If Conjecture 2 is true for all map germs with

Proof.

By Proposition

, Conjecture 2 is true for

4.
Characterization of agumentations

In
[
17
]
, Theorem 4.6, Houston claimed the following: if
f
admits an OPSU
F
, then:

f\text{ is an augmentation }\iff\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\tilde{\tau}(F)\geq 1

His proof relies on the following statement, which appears as Lemma 2.6 in
[
17
]
:
suppose that
f\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{q},0)
with
n\geq q
is a smooth mono-germ of the form
f=\pi\circ i
, with
i\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{n+r},0)
,

an immersion and
\pi\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n+r},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{q},0)
a submersion. Then,
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\tilde{\tau}(f)\geq q-r
.

This statement is proven false by the following counterexample: let
i\colon(\mathbb{K}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{4},0)
,
\pi\colon(\mathbb{K}^{4},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{3},0)
and
\psi\colon(\mathbb{K}^{4},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{4},0)
be given by:

\displaystyle i(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=(X,Y,Z,0)

\displaystyle\psi(X,Y,Z,\Lambda)

\displaystyle=(\Lambda+X^{3}+XY,Y,Z,X)

\displaystyle\pi(X,Y,Z,\Lambda)

\displaystyle=(X,Y,Z)

Then,
\pi\circ\psi
is a submersion and:

\pi\circ\psi\circ i(X,Y,Z)=(X^{3}+XY,Y,Z)

This composition, which plays the role of
f
in the statement, is a cuspidal edge, that is:

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\tilde{\tau}(\pi\circ\psi\circ i)=1<3-1

and so it contradicts Houston’s lemma.

This counterexample appears when trying to reproduce the proof of Theorem 4.6 in
[
17
]
to determine if the map-germ
C_{3}(x,y)=(x,y^{2},xy^{3}+x^{3}y)
, which appears in Mond’s classification of simple map-germs of
\mathbb{K}^{2}\to\mathbb{K}^{3}
(
[
24
]
), is an augmentation.
It admits the OPSU:

\tilde{C}_{3}(x,y,\lambda)=(x,y^{2},xy^{3}+x^{3}y+\lambda y,\lambda)

which has
\tilde{\tau}(F)=1
.
We will see that, in fact,
C_{3}
is not an augmentation and is thus a counterexample to Houston’s theorem.

First we need a lemma which allows us to calculate what Tjurina number the augmenting function must have. Notice that given an augmentation
A_{F,g}(f)(x,z)=(f_{g(z)}(x),z)
there is a natural OPSU given by
(f_{g(z)+\lambda}(x),z,\lambda)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
a\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
is an augmentation and let
A\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n+1},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p+1},0)
be the natural OPSU.
Then, the order of tangency at the origin of the natural immersion
i\colon(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p+1},0)
with the isosingular locus of
A
, is equal to the Tjurina number of the augmenting function.

Proof.

Since
a
is an augmentation, there must exist a smooth function
g\colon(\mathbb{K}^{d},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
and a smooth map-germ
f\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n-d},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p-d},0)
that admits an OPSU
F(x,\lambda)=(f_{\lambda}(x),\lambda)
such that:

a\sim_{\mathscr{A}}A_{F,g}(f)(x,z)=(f_{g(z)}(x),z)

Then the following is an OPSU for the augmentation:

\tilde{F}(x,z,\lambda)=(f_{g(z)+\lambda}(x),z,\lambda)

which is equivalent after a change in the source to:

(f_{\lambda}(x),z,\lambda-g(z))

and therefore is trivial along the germ at 0 of the manifold
M=\{(0,z,-g(z))\in\mathbb{K}^{p+1}:z\in\mathbb{K}^{d}\}
.
The image of the inclusion
i
is the plane
\{(s,t,0)\in\mathbb{K}^{p+1}:(s,t)\in\mathbb{K}^{p-d}\times\mathbb{K}^{d}\}
, which has order of tangency
\tau(g)
with
M
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

The map-germ
C_{3}(x,y)=(x,y^{2},xy^{3}+x^{3}y)
is not an augmentation.

Proof.

Assume
C_{3}
is an augmentation, that is, assume there exist some base map-germ
f\colon(\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{2},0)
, an OPSU
F(x,\lambda)=(f_{\lambda}(x),\lambda)
of
f
, and a smooth function

By Theorem

3=\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}({C_{3}})=\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}\left({A_{F,g}(f)}\right)\geq\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\tau(g)

This implies

Since every simple function is

\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)=3
.
Assume first that

is equivalent to
(y^{2},y^{3})

Also,

, which means that by Proposition

we only need to check the augmentation via

By Proposition
2.8
, the class of the augmentation does not depend on the chosen OPSU.
Take for instance

which lies in the equivalence class of

as seen in

Therefore, the only possiblity that remains is that

and

, which implies that

must be the augmentation of

via a quadratic function,

Notice that augmenting by the OPSU

In any case,

admitted the OPSU:

Yet according to Lemma

This is a contradiction, and therefore,

5.
Unfoldings and Augmentations

Our aim in this section will be understanding how the choice of OPSU affects de
\mathscr{A}
-class of the resulting augmentation.
By Proposition
2.8

\mathscr{A}_{e}

But the proof of this result relies heavily on the fact that, in codimension 1, every OPSU is also a versal unfolding.
Hence, in this case all OPSUs are equivalent as unfoldings after a pull-back with a diffeomorphism.

Consider now two OPSUs,

Given the function

and

\phi
-equivalent.
In this case, the diffeomorphisms of

are pairs

with

Notice that this case of
\phi
-equivalence generalizes equivalence as unfoldings after a pull-back: instead of using unfoldings of the identity, here one is allowed to use unfoldings of any given diffeomorphism.
In particular, this equivalence relation allows us to compare the different OPSUs of two
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent map-germs.

It is extended in

Proposition 5.2

(

If

Remark 5.3
.

From now on we will be working over

Remark 5.6
.

From this point on, we will only be concerned about

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
{f}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)

and

If:

is simple

(2)

is simple

(3)

\phi
-equivalence classes in which all OPSUs of

(4)

via some deformation of

Since all singularities that appear when deforming

∎

6.
Weak equivalence and cross-substantial unfoldings

In the next section we will see that a key property to determine when all the OPSUs of a given map-germ lie on the same
\phi
-equivalence class is substantiality.
Particularly, we will see that this will hold if all its OPSUs are substantial.
To ensure this condition, along this section we will be working with a new, much coarser equivalence relation which preserves substantiality.
In most cases, all OPSUs will lie on the same orbit in this
weak
equivalence.

First, we need to construct a “normal” form for the OPSUs of a given map-germ.
Let
{f}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
be a smooth map-germ that admits an OPSU.
In particular, there must exist a
\gamma\in\theta(f)
such that
(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)
is stable.
Denote
k=\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)
.
Then:

Proposition 6.1
.

Every OPSU of
f
is
\phi
-equivalent to one of the form:

(5)

\left(f(x)+\left(1+\sum^{k-1}_{i=1}q_{i}(\lambda)s_{i}(f(x))\right)\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda\right)

for some
q_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{1},s_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}_{p}
.

Proof.

In
[
5
]
, it is seen that when a map-germ admits an OPSU, one can find a
\mathbb{K}
-basis for
N\mathscr{A}_{e}f
of the form:

N\mathscr{A}_{e}f=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\{\gamma(x),s_{1}(f(x))\gamma_{(}x),\ldots,s_{k-1}(f(x))\gamma(x)\right\}

for some polynomials
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k-1}\in\mathfrak{m}_{p}
.
In particular, this implies that every 1-parameter unfolding is equivalent as an unfolding (and, in particular,
\phi
-equivalent) to one of the form:

(6)

\left(f(x)+\left(\rho_{0}(\lambda)+\sum^{k-1}_{i=1}\rho_{i}(\lambda)s_{i}(f(x))\right)\gamma(x),\lambda\right)

for some
\rho_{0},\ldots,\rho_{k-1}\in\mathfrak{m}_{1}
.

Notice that for one of these unfoldings to be stable, it is mandatory that
\rho_{0}^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
.
In this case,
\rho_{0}
must admit an inverse function germ
\rho_{0}^{-1}\colon(\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
.
For each
i=0,\ldots,k-1
, define
\tilde{q}_{i}\colon(\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
as
\tilde{q}_{i}(\lambda)=\rho_{i}\circ\rho_{0}^{-1}(\lambda)
.
Consider the diffeomorphisms given by
h(x)=(x,\rho_{0}^{-1}(\lambda))
and
k(X,\Lambda)=(X,\rho_{0}(\Lambda))
in
\mathbb{K}^{n+1}
and
\mathbb{K}^{p+1}
respectively.
Notice that
(h,k)\in G_{e}^{\phi}
.
Composing with
h
and
k
in the source and target respectively, we see that the expression in Equation
6
is
\phi
-equivalent to:

\left(f(x)+\left(\lambda+\sum^{k-1}_{i=1}\tilde{q}_{i}(\lambda)s_{i}(f(x))\right)\gamma(x),\lambda\right)

Using the fact that
\tilde{q}_{i}(0)=0
, we apply Hadamard’s Lemma to see that there exist some
{q_{i}}\in\mathcal{O}_{1}
such that
\tilde{q}_{i}(\lambda)=\lambda q_{i}(\lambda)
for each

.
Factoring

∎

By Theorem
5.4
, any augmentation of

will be
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent to the augmentation via an unfolding of the form in Equation

.
All the OPSUs we consider from now on will be of this form.

Denote by
(x,\lambda)
the variables for
\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}
, we write
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}
the ideal of functions in
\mathcal{O}_{n+1}
multiplied by
\lambda
.
Similarly, denote the variables in
\mathbb{K}^{p}\times\mathbb{K}

Definition 6.2
.

We say that two map-germs
F,G\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p}\times\mathbb{K},0)
are
weak equivalent
if they are
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent via some germs of diffeomorphism
\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{n+1})\in\text{Diff}_{0}(n+1)
and
\Psi=(\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{p+1})\in\text{Diff}_{0}(p+1)
such that
\Phi_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\mathcal{O}_{n+1}
and
\Psi_{p+1}\in\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
.

These diffeomorphisms, whose last component is just the last variable times a unit in the ring of functions, will be called
weak
diffeomorphisms.
In particular, if a diffeomorphism is weak, its inverse will also be weak.

The normal form obtained in Proposition
6.1
does not give any particular hint on what the
s_{i}
might be, but as we will see in the next result, having some information on them would be of great use.
Since it is a representative of a non-zero class over the

-normal space to

, we can consider
\gamma(x)\in\theta(f)
as a vector field whose components are all zero except for one.
Moreover, we can assume that this component consists of a single monomial.
Without loss of generality, we will assume that this non-zero component is the last one, that is:
\gamma(x)=(0,\ldots,0,x^{\alpha})
, with
x^{\alpha}
a monomial in
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Assume that we can choose the
s_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}_{p}
in equation
5
so that they do not depend on the last component (i.e., they only depend on
f_{1},\ldots,f_{p-1}
).
Then, all OPSUs of
f
are weak equivalent.

Proof.

Notice that
\phi
-equivalence entails weak equivalence.
Using our hypothesis and Proposition
6.1
, we can choose some
s_{i}(X)=s_{i}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p})\in\mathfrak{m}_{p}
not depending on
X_{p}
so that every OPSU of
f
will be weak equivalent to one of the form:

F(x,\lambda)=\left(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{p-1}(x),f_{p}(x)+\left(1+\sum^{k-1}_{i=1}q_{i}(\lambda)s_{i}(f(x))\right)\lambda x^{\alpha},\lambda\right)

Now, consider the function
H\colon(\mathbb{K}^{k-1}\times\mathbb{K}\times\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
:

H(Y,\Lambda,\lambda)=\Lambda-\left(1+\sum^{k-1}_{i=1}q_{i}(\lambda)Y_{i}\right)\lambda

Then,
\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda}(0)\neq 0
and so by the Implicit Function Theorem there exists a smooth function
g\colon(\mathbb{K}^{k-1}\times\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
such that
H(Y,\Lambda,g(Y,\Lambda))=0
and:

\frac{\partial g}{\partial\Lambda}(Y,\Lambda)=\frac{1}{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda}(Y,\Lambda,g(Y,\Lambda))}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\Lambda}(Y,\Lambda,g(Y,\Lambda))

In particular,
\frac{\partial g}{\partial\Lambda}(0)\neq 0
. Moreover, since
H(Y,0,g(Y,0))=0
:

0=\left(1+\sum^{k-1}_{i=1}q_{i}(g(Y,0))Y_{i}\right)g(Y,0)

For this to be true in an open neighbourhood of 0,
g(Y,0)
must be 0 in a small enough neighbourhood. Hence, the germ of
g
can be expressed as
g(Y,\Lambda)=\Lambda\tilde{g}(Y,\Lambda)
for some
\tilde{g}\in\mathcal{O}_{k}
with
\tilde{g}(0)\neq 0
.

Define
S(X)=(s_{1}(X),\ldots,s_{k-1}(X))
and
\Phi\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K},0)
as
\Phi(x,\Lambda)=(x,g(S(f(x)),\Lambda))
, which is a weak diffeomorphism. Then:

F\circ\Phi(x,\Lambda)=\left(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{p-1}(x),f_{p}(x)+\Lambda x^{\alpha},\Lambda\tilde{g}(S(f(x)),\Lambda)\right)

Write
\tilde{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p-1})
. Now
S
only depends on
\tilde{X}
, hence the map-germ
\psi\colon(\mathbb{K}^{p-1}\times\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p-1}\times\mathbb{K},0)
given by:

\psi(\tilde{X},\Lambda)=\left(\tilde{X},\Lambda\tilde{g}(S(\tilde{X}),\Lambda)\right)

admits an inverse
\psi^{-1}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{p-1}\times\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p-1}\times\mathbb{K},0)
of the form:

\psi^{-1}(\tilde{X},\Lambda)=\left(\psi_{1}^{-1}(\tilde{X},\Lambda),\Lambda\psi_{2}^{-1}(\tilde{X},\Lambda)\right)

Write
\Psi(X,\Lambda)=\left(\psi_{1}^{-1}(\tilde{X},\Lambda),X_{p},\Lambda\psi_{2}^{-1}(\tilde{X},\Lambda)\right)
, which is a weak diffeomorphism, and we have:

\Psi\circ F\circ\Phi=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{p-1}(x),f_{p}(x)+\Lambda x^{\alpha},\Lambda)

∎

admits the normal form:

g(x,y)=(x,y^{4}+x^{2}y+xy^{2})

In this case,
N(g)=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\{(0,y)\right\}
and
N\mathscr{A}_{e}g=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\{(0,y),(0,xy)\right\}

Once again, Proposition
6.3
ensures all OPSUs of
g
are weak equivalent.

Weak equivalence behaves well with substantiality (the case
\delta(F)=1
of Definition
2.6
) in the following sense:

Proposition 6.6
.

If two OPSUs are weak-equivalent, and one of them is substantial, the other will also be substantial.

Proof.

From Remark
2.7
, a substantial OPSU
F
admits families of diffeomorphisms
(\Phi_{t},\Psi_{t})\in\mathscr{A}_{F}
with
\Phi_{t}(x,\lambda)=(\Phi_{t}^{1}(x,\lambda),\ldots,\Phi_{t}^{n}(x,\lambda),\lambda e^{t})

, and
\Psi(X,\Lambda)=(\Psi_{1}^{t}(X,\Lambda),\ldots,\Psi_{p}^{t}(X,\Lambda),\Lambda e^{t})

such that for each
t\in\mathbb{K}
small enough
(\Phi_{t},\Psi_{t})\in\mathscr{A}_{F}
.

If
\tilde{F}
is another OPSU and
\tilde{\Phi}\in\theta_{n+1},\tilde{\Psi}\in\theta_{p+1}
are weak diffeomorphisms such that
\tilde{F}\circ\tilde{\Phi}=\tilde{\Psi}\circ F
, then for each
t\in\mathbb{K}
small enough:

(\tilde{\Phi}\circ\Phi_{t}\circ\tilde{\Phi}^{-1},\tilde{\Psi}\circ\Psi_{t}\circ\tilde{\Psi}^{-1})\in\mathscr{A}_{\tilde{F}}

This implies, by the Thom-Levine lemma, that the vector field
\eta=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\tilde{\Psi}\circ\Psi_{t}\circ\tilde{\Psi}^{-1}\right)\right|_{t=0}
is liftable.
Applying the chain rule, this is equal to:

\eta=d\tilde{\Psi}\circ\left.\frac{\partial\Psi_{t}}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}\circ\tilde{\Psi}^{-1}

Since
\tilde{\Psi}
is weak, its last component can be expressed as
\tilde{\Psi}_{p+1}(X,\Lambda)=\Lambda\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}_{p+1}(X,\Lambda)
, with
\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}_{p+1}
a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
.
Similarly, the last component of
\tilde{\Psi}^{-1}
can be expressed as
\tilde{\Psi}^{-1}_{p+1}(X,\Lambda)=\Lambda\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime-1}_{p+1}(X,\lambda)
with
\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime-1}_{p+1}
a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
.
Then, the last component of
\eta
is:

\sum_{i=1}^{p}\Lambda\frac{\partial\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}_{p+1}}{\partial X_{i}}(X,\Lambda)\eta_{i}(\tilde{\Psi}^{-1}(X,\Lambda))+\left(\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}_{p+1}(X,\Lambda)+\Lambda\frac{\partial\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}_{p+1}(X,\Lambda)}{\partial\Lambda}\right)\Lambda\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime-1}_{p+1}(X,\Lambda)

which is just
\Lambda
times a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
.
Since
\text{Lift}(\tilde{F})
is an
\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
-module, we can multiply
d\Psi\circ\eta\circ\Psi^{-1}
by the inverse of this unit and obtain a liftable vector field of
\tilde{F}
with only
\Lambda
in the last component, and so
\tilde{F}
is substantial.

∎

Remark 6.7
.

The first part of the proof actually establishes a bijection between liftable vector fields of
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent map-germs via the diffeomorphism in the target.
This was already done in Lemma 6.1 from
[
28
]
.
Our argument here provides a different proof of the same fact.

Corollary 6.8
.

If two OPSUs are
\phi
-equivalent and one of them is substantial, then both are substantial.

Example 6.9
.

Coming back to our examples
f(y)=(y^{2},y^{5})
and
g(x,y)=(x,y^{4}+x^{2}y+xy^{2})
, all their respective OPSUs are weak equivalent.
On one hand,
f
admits the OPSU
(y^{2},y^{5}+\lambda y,\lambda)
, which is quasihomogeneous and therefore substantial.
Hence, by Proposition
6.6
all OPSUs of
f
are substantial.

On the other hand, using SINGULAR one can check that the OPSU
(x,y^{4}+x^{2}y+xy^{2}+\lambda y,\lambda)
of
g
is not substantial: hence, by Proposition
6.6
none of the OPSUs of
g
are substantial.
Notice that
g
is not quasihomogeneous, and so one cannot construct a quasihomogeneous OPSU.

One condition to guarantee that the hypothesis of Proposition
6.3
is verified would be the fact that
f_{p}(x)\gamma(x)\in T\mathscr{A}_{e}f
: then, any
s(f(x))\gamma(x)
lies in the same class over
N\mathscr{A}_{e}f
as
s(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{p-1}(x),0)\gamma(x)
.

Note that this condition is not necessary: it can be checked that the vector field
(y^{4}+xy^{2}+x^{2}y)\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
y\end{matrix}\right)
is not in the
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-tangent space of
11_{5}
, and yet we found a basis of the
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-normal space that satisfied our hypothesis.

Definition 6.10
.

Consider the OPSU
F(x,\lambda)=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{p-1}(x),f_{p}(x)+\lambda x^{\alpha},\lambda)
(which can always be considered due to the previous discussion).
We say
F
is
cross-substantial
if it admits a liftable vector field
\eta\in\theta_{p+1}
of the form
\eta(X,\lambda)=(\eta_{1}(X,\lambda),\ldots,\eta_{p}(X,\lambda),X_{p})
.

Lemma 6.11
.

If
F(x,\lambda)=(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)
is cross-substantial, then
f_{p}(x)\gamma(x)\in T\mathscr{A}_{e}f
.

Proof.

Assuming
\gamma(x)=(0,\ldots,0,x^{\alpha})
,
let
\eta(X,\lambda)=(\eta_{1}(X,\Lambda),\ldots,\eta_{p}(X,\Lambda),X_{p})
be a liftable vector field and
\xi\in\theta_{n+1}
so that
tF(\xi)+\omega F(\eta)=0
.
Looking at the
p+1
component of this equation, we get:
\xi_{n+1}(x,\lambda)=-\eta_{p+1}\circ F(x,\lambda)=-(f_{p}(x)+\lambda x^{\alpha})
.
Inspecting now the
p
component allows us to conclude that:

\left(d_{x}f_{p}+\lambda d_{x}(x^{\alpha})\right)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})(x,\lambda)+\eta_{p}(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)=x^{\alpha}(f_{p}(x)+\lambda x^{\alpha})

Any other component is of the form
d_{x}f_{i}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})(x,\lambda)+\eta_{i}\circ F(x,\lambda)=0
.
Take
\tilde{\xi}(x)=\xi(x,0)\in\theta_{n}
and
\tilde{\eta}(X)=\eta(X,0)
and we have
tf(\tilde{\xi})+\omega f(\tilde{\eta})=f_{p}(x)\gamma(x)
.
∎

Putting all this together we get that cross-substantiality is a sufficient condition for weak equivalence of all OPSUs:

Theorem 6.12
.

If
F(x,\lambda)=(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)
is cross-substantial, then all OPSUs of
f
are weak equivalent.

Proof.

Since
f
admits an OPSU, we can pick a basis for
N\mathscr{A}_{e}f
of the form:

N\mathscr{A}_{e}f=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\{\gamma(x),s_{1}(f(x))\gamma(x),\ldots,s_{k-1}(f(x))\gamma(x)\right\}

By Lemma
6.11
,
f_{p}(x)\gamma(x)\in T\mathscr{A}_{e}f
, and so each
s_{i}(f(x))\gamma(x)
lies on the same class over
N\mathscr{A}_{e}f
as
s_{i}(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{p-1}(f(x)),0)
, providing another basis that satisfies the conditions in
6.3

∎

Proposition 6.13
.

All corank 1 map-germs of
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension
1
are cross-substantial.

Proof.

In
[
6
]
it is proven that all corank 1 map-germs of
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension 1 are
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent to a quasi-homogeneous map-germ.
All quasi-homogeneous map-germs admit a substantial OPSU (see discussion after Definition
2.6
).
All OPSUs of an
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension 1 map-germ are versal unfoldings, hence all of them are equivalent as unfolding after a pull-back, and therefore all of them are weak equivalent.
By Proposition
6.6
, this implies all of them are substantial.

Apply now Damon’s Theorem
2.2
to see that for any corank 1
{f}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
of
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension 1, and any OPSU
F
:

1=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{p+1}}{d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))+\langle\Lambda\rangle}=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{p+1}}{d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))}

where the second equality comes from the fact that
F
is substantial.
Now, to have codimension 1 this means that the only non-zero class is that of the constant functions, implying
X_{p}\in d\pi(\text{Lift}(F))
.
∎

Finally, cross-substantiality can be ensured by checking a condition over the defining function of the image of
F
:

Proposition 6.14
.

Let
H\colon(\mathbb{K}^{p}\times\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
be a defining function for the image of the OPSU
F(x,\lambda)=(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)
.
If
X_{p}\in J_{X}H=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathcal{O}_{p+1}}\left\{\frac{\partial H}{\partial X_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial H}{\partial X_{p}}\right\}
, then
F
is cross-substantial.

Proof.

If
X_{p}\in J_{X}H
, there exist some
s_{1},\ldots,s_{p}\in\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
such that:

X_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}s_{i}(X,\Lambda)\frac{\partial H}{\partial X_{i}}(X,\Lambda)

Write
\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{p}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}
for the constant vector fields of
\theta_{p+1}
.
Then, the vector field

\eta=\frac{\partial H}{\partial\Lambda}(X,\Lambda)\sum_{i=1}^{p}s_{i}(X,\Lambda)\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}-X_{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}

satisfies
\eta(H)=0
and so belongs to
\operatorname{Derlog}(\Delta F)=\operatorname{Lift}(F)
(see page 283 in
[
27
]
for details).
∎

Example 6.15
.

The curve
(y^{2},y^{5})
admits the substantial OPSU
F(y,\lambda)=(y^{2},y^{5}+\lambda y,\lambda)
, which has as a defining equation the polynomial
H(X,Y,\Lambda)=Y^{2}+X(X^{2}+\Lambda)^{2}
.
Computing the Jacobian of

with respect to

, we see that
Y
belongs to it.

On the other hand, it can be checked via SINGULAR that the OPSU of

given by

is not cross-substantial, even though all of its OPSUs are weak equivalent.

7.
Relative spaces and
\phi
-equivalence

Recall our setting:
f\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
is a smooth map-germ that admits an OPSU
F(x,\lambda)=(f_{\lambda}(x),\lambda)
.
Denote
\bar{f}(x,\lambda)=f_{\lambda}(x)
.
We will be concerned with
\phi
-equivalence with
\phi(x,\lambda)=\lambda
.

The space of vector fields in
\theta_{m+1}
with last component equal to 0 will be denoted by
\theta_{m+1/1}
.
With this notation, the
\phi
-extended tangent space of
\bar{f}
can be described like this:

TG^{\phi}_{e}\bar{f}=\left\{d_{x}\bar{f}(\xi(x,\lambda))+d_{\lambda}\bar{f}(\tilde{\xi}(\lambda))+\eta(\bar{f}(x,\lambda),\lambda):\xi\in\theta_{n+1/1},\tilde{\xi}\in\mathcal{O}_{1},\eta\in\theta_{p+1/1}\right\}

This space contains what is called the
relative
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-tangent space to
F
.
To describe it, denote by
t_{\text{rel}}F
the restriction of
tF
to vector fields in
\theta_{n+1/1}
, and by
\omega_{\text{rel}}F
the restriction of
\omega F
to
\theta_{p+1/1}
.
The images of these restrictions both lie on the set of vector fields in
\theta(F)
with last component equal to zero, which can be naturally identified with the space
\theta(\bar{f})
.
Hence we can consider both mappings as being defined in the following way:

\begin{split}t_{\text{rel}}F&\colon\theta_{n+1/1}\to\theta(\bar{f})\\
\omega_{\text{rel}}F&\colon\theta_{p+1/1}\to\theta(\bar{f})\end{split}

Then, the relative
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-tangent and relative
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-normal spaces to
F
are defined as:

\begin{split}T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)&=t_{\text{rel}}F(\theta_{n+1/1})+\omega_{\text{rel}}F(\theta_{p+1/1})\\
N\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)&=\frac{\theta(\bar{f})}{t_{\text{rel}}F(\theta_{n+1/1})+\omega_{\text{rel}}F(\theta_{p+1/1})}\end{split}

We can also define a mapping
t_{1}\bar{f}\colon\mathcal{O}_{1}\to\theta(\bar{f})
given by
t_{1}\bar{f}(\tilde{\xi})=d_{\lambda}\bar{f}(\tilde{\xi})=\tilde{\xi}\cdot d_{\lambda}\bar{f}
.
With this notation,
TG_{e}^{\phi}\bar{f}
can be rewritten as:

TG_{e}^{\phi}\bar{f}=t_{\text{rel}}F(\theta_{n+1/1})+t_{1}\bar{f}(\mathcal{O}_{1})+\omega_{\text{rel}}F(\theta_{p+1/1})

Hence
T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)\subseteq TG_{e}^{\phi}\bar{f}
.

The following result allows one to compare the relative version of the tangent space of an unfolding with the normal space of the map-germ it unfolds.
The general version can be found as Lemma 3.5 in
[
27
]
: we only write the case for 1-parameter unfoldings.
Note that every vector field in
\theta(f)
can be naturally considered as a vector field in
\theta(\bar{f})
:

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
{f}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
be a smooth map-germ with
F\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p}\times\mathbb{K},0)
a 1-parameter unfolding.
Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}\in\theta(f)
.
The following are equivalent:

•

N\mathscr{A}_{e}f=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{K}}\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}\}
.

•

N\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}\}
, through the natural inclusion in
\theta(\bar{f})
.

We see that the relative normal space of an unfolding is essentially the normal space of the base map-germ multiplied by functions depending only on the parameter.
This hints at the elements that may be missing in the
\phi
-tangent space of a given OPSU: our next goal will be trying to compute them.
Recall that we denoted by
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}
the space of all functions in
\mathcal{O}_{n+1}
multiplied by
\lambda
.
Then,
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})
is the space of all vector fields in
\theta(\bar{f})
multiplied by
\lambda
, which is an
\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
-module via
F
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume
\theta(f)
is finitely generated as an
\mathcal{O}_{p}
-module via
f
.
For any OPSU
F
in the form of Equation
5
,
F
is substantial if and only if
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})\subseteq T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)
.

Proof.

Assume
F
is substantial.
We will prove the result by applying Nakayama’s lemma.
Having an OPSU in the form of Equation
5
means that we have a stable
F(x,\lambda)=(\bar{f}(x,\lambda),\lambda)
, with
\bar{f}(x,\lambda)=f(x)+\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}q_{i}(\lambda)s_{i}(f(x))\right)\lambda\gamma(x)
, so that
\gamma\in\theta(f)
,
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k-1}\in\mathfrak{m}_{p}
and:

(7)

N\mathscr{A}_{e}f=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\{\gamma(x),s_{1}(f(x))\gamma(x),\ldots,s_{k-1}(f(x))\gamma(x)\right\}

Recall that
T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)
is an
\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
-module via
F
.
Since
F
is an unfolding of
f
and
\theta(f)
is finitely generated as an
\mathcal{O}_{p}
-module via
f
,
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})
is finitely generated as an
\mathcal{O}_{p+1}
-module via F.
To apply Nakayama’s lemma we need to check:

\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})\subseteq T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)+F^{*}\mathfrak{m}_{p+1}\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})

Now, by Lemma
7.1
and Equation
7
, it suffices to check that:

\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}}\left\{\gamma(x),s_{1}(f(x))\gamma(x),\ldots,s_{k-1}(f(x))\gamma(x)\right\}\subseteq T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)+F^{*}\mathfrak{m}_{p+1}\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})

Using the fact that
\bar{f}
is equal to
f
plus an element in
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})
, we see that:

\displaystyle F^{*}\mathfrak{m}_{p+1}\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathcal{O}_{n+1}}\left\{\lambda\bar{f}_{1}(x,\lambda),\ldots,\lambda\bar{f}_{p}(x,\lambda),\lambda^{2}\right\}\theta(\bar{f})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathcal{O}_{n+1}}\left\{\lambda{f}_{1}(x),\ldots,\lambda{f}_{p}(x),\lambda^{2}\right\}\theta(\bar{f})

Hence, we only need to check that
\lambda\gamma(x)\in T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)+F^{*}\mathfrak{m}_{p+1}\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})
.
But since
F
is substantial, there exists
\eta\in\theta_{p+1}
of the form
\eta(X,\Lambda)=(\tilde{\eta}(X,\Lambda),\Lambda)
and a
\xi\in\theta_{n+1}
of the form
\xi(x,\lambda)=(\tilde{\xi}(x,\lambda),\lambda)
such that
\eta\circ F=dF\circ\xi
, that is:

\tilde{\eta}\circ F=d_{x}\bar{f}(\tilde{\xi})+d_{\lambda}\bar{f}(\lambda)=d_{x}\bar{f}(\tilde{\xi})+\lambda d_{\lambda}\bar{f}

This means that
\lambda d_{\lambda}\bar{f}\in T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)
.
Derive
\bar{f}
with respect to
\lambda
to check that:

\lambda d_{\lambda}\bar{f}=\lambda\gamma(x)+\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(q_{i}(\lambda)+\lambda q_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)s_{i}(f(x))\right)\gamma(x)

Observe now that since
q_{i}(0)=0
for each
i=1,\ldots,k-1
:

\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(q_{i}(\lambda)+\lambda q_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)s_{i}(f(x))\right)\gamma(x)\in\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathcal{O}_{n+1}}\left\{\lambda^{2}\right\}\theta(\bar{f})\subseteq F^{*}\mathfrak{m}_{p+1}\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f}).

Hence,
\lambda\gamma(x)\in T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)+F^{*}\mathfrak{m}_{p+1}\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})
.

Now, assuming
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}\theta(\bar{f})\subseteq T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)
, this implies
d_{\lambda}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)\in T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)
, which means there exist
\tilde{\xi}\in\theta_{n+1/1}
and
\tilde{\eta}\in\theta_{p+1/1}
such that:

d_{x}\bar{f}(\tilde{\xi})+\tilde{\eta}\circ F=d_{\lambda}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)

Then, if
\xi(x,\lambda)=(-\tilde{\xi}(x,\lambda),\lambda)
and
\eta(X,\Lambda)=(\tilde{\eta}(X,\Lambda),\Lambda)
, we have
dF\circ\xi=\eta\circ F
.

∎

Recall Mather’s lemma (see Theorem 6.3 from
[
27
]
):

Theorem 7.3
.

If a Lie group
G
acts smoothly on a manifold
M
, and
W\subseteq M
is a connected submanifold, a necessary and sufficient condition for
W
to be contained in a single
G
-orbit is that:

(1)

for all
x\in W
,
T_{x}W\subseteq T_{x}(Gx)
, and

(2)

the dimension of
T_{x}(Gx)
is the same for all
x\in W
.

We are now in the position to prove:

Theorem 7.4
.

If
\theta(f)
is finitely generated as an
\mathcal{O}_{p}
-module via
f
and
all OPSUs of
f
are substantial, then all of them are
\phi
-equivalent.

Proof.

First, we check that all OPSUs in the form of Equation
5
have the same
\phi_{e}
-codimension: since
TG_{\phi}^{e}\bar{f}=T\mathscr{A}_{e}(F/1)+t_{1}\bar{f}(\mathcal{O}_{1})
, we can use Lemmas
7.1
and
7.2
to see that:

\frac{\theta(\bar{f})}{TG_{\phi}^{e}\bar{f}}=\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\{s_{1}(f(x))\gamma(x),\ldots,s_{k-1}(f(x))\gamma(x)\right\}

Hence, they are all
d
-
\phi
-determined for some degree
d
big enough.
For each combination of
q_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{1}
, we can define the following family of map-germs:

F_{a}(x,\lambda)=(\bar{f}_{a}(x,\lambda),\lambda)=\left(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x)+a\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}q_{i}(\lambda)s_{i}(f(x))\gamma(x),\lambda\right)

The family
\bar{f}_{a}
defines a connected submanifold
M
of the space of
d
-jets,
J^{d}(\mathbb{K}^{n+1},\mathbb{K}^{p})
.
For each
a
, we have
T_{\bar{f}_{a}}M\subseteq TG_{e}^{\phi,(d)}\bar{f}_{a}
, where
TG_{e}^{\phi,(d)}\bar{f}_{a}
is the
\phi
-tangent space to the
\phi
-orbit of
\bar{f}_{a}
in
J^{d}(\mathbb{K}^{n+1},\mathbb{K}^{p})
, consisting of
d
-jets of vector fields in
TG_{\phi}^{e}\bar{f}
.
Since the codimension of the
\phi
-tangent space is constant along
\bar{f}_{a}
, so will be the dimension of
TG_{e}^{\phi,(d)}\bar{f}_{a}
.
Hence, applying Mather’s lemma
7.3
, all the
j^{d}\bar{f}_{a}
lie on the same
\phi
-orbit on
J^{d}(\mathbb{K}^{n+1},\mathbb{K}^{p})
.
Since all
\bar{f_{a}}
are
d
-determined, this implies they all lie on the same
\phi
-orbit as
\bar{f}_{0}(x,\lambda)=f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x)
.
This can be done for any combination of
q_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{1}
, and so every OPSU in the form of Equation
5
is
\phi
-equivalent to
F_{0}
.
In particular, by Proposition
6.1
every OPSU is
\phi
-equivalent to
F_{0}
.
∎

We can join all the results from this section to give a condition for which all OPSUs of a given map-germ are
\phi
-equivalent:

Theorem 7.5
.

If
f
admits an OPSU of the form
(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)
which is both substantial and cross-substantial, then all OPSUs of
f
are
\phi
-equivalent, and hence the choice of OPSU for augmenting

does not affect the
\mathscr{A}
-class of the augmentation.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.3
, as this OPSU is cross-substantial, all OPSUs will be weak-equivalent.
Since one of them is substantial, by Proposition
6.6
all of them will be substantial.
Apply now Theorem
7.4
.
∎

Example 7.6
.

All the map-germs in the family
f_{k}(y)=(y^{2},y^{2k+1})
for
k\geq 1
admit the cross-substantial OPSU
F_{k}(y,\lambda)=(y^{2},y^{2k+1}+\lambda y,\lambda)
, which is also substantial since it is quasi-homogeneous.
Hence, applying Theorem
7.5
, augmentations of
f_{k}
do not depend on the chosen OPSU.

Consequences and examples

We have seen in Theorem
5.4
that if two OPSUs are
\phi
-equivalent with
\phi(x,\lambda)=\lambda
, they will produce the same augmentation.
Hence, the number of
\phi
-equivalence classes of a given map-germ determines the possible different augmentations that it can produce.

Definition 8.1
.

A map-germ
f
is
good for augmentation
if all its OPSUs lie on the same
\phi
-equivalence class.

Proposition 8.2
.

If
f
is good for augmentation and
g\sim_{\mathscr{A}}f
, then
g
is good for augmentation.

Proof.

Let
h\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)
and
k\colon(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
be two germs of diffeomorphism such that
f=k\circ g\circ h
.
Define
H(x,\lambda)=(h(x),\lambda)
and
K(X,\Lambda)=(k(X),\Lambda)
, which are
\phi
-equivalence diffeomorphisms.
Given
G,G^{\prime}
any two OPSUs of
g
, we have that both
K\circ G\circ H
and
K\circ G^{\prime}\circ H
are OPSUs of
f
: hence, they are
\phi
-equivalent.
∎

Proposition 8.3
.

If
f\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
is good for augmentation, then the
\mathscr{A}
-class of the augmentation only depends on the
\mathscr{R}
-class of the augmenting function and the
\mathscr{A}
-class of
f
.

Proof.

The fact that all augmentations of a given
f
do not depend on the choice of the OPSU is given by Theorem
5.4
, and the fact that the choice of augmenting function only affects up to
\mathscr{R}
-equivalence comes from Proposition
2.9
.

Given any
g\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
in the
\mathscr{A}
-equivalence class of
f
, let
h,k
be diffeomorphisms such that
f=k\circ g\circ h
, and define
H(x,\lambda)=(h(x),\lambda)
and
K(X,\Lambda)=(k(X),\Lambda)
.
By Proposition
8.2
,
g
is also good for augmentation.
Denote by
A_{s}g
a representative in the
\mathscr{A}
-class of the augmentation of
g
via a function
s\in\mathcal{O}_{d}
.
Then,
K\circ A_{s}g\circ H
is the augmentation of
f
via
s
.
∎

Corollary 8.4
.

If
{f}\colon(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{p},0)
is simple,
F(x,\lambda)=(f(x)+\lambda\gamma(x),\lambda)
is a substantial and cross-substantial OPSU and
g\in\mathcal{O}_{d}
is simple, and all deformations of
A_{F,g}(f)
are augmentations of
f
via deformations of
g
, then
A_{F,g}(f)
is simple.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.5
,
f
is good for augmentation, and so we can apply Theorem
5.7
to ensure that the resulting augmentation is simple.
∎

Lastly, we give a list of examples with different properties in which we can apply the results developed throughout the paper.

From
[
4
]
, we know that if the augmenting function is simple and the augmented map-germ has
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension 1, then the augmentation will be simple.
Similarly, if the map-germ is simple and the augmenting function is a Morse function, the augmentation will be simple.
Moreover, if either the map-germ or the augmenting function are not simple, then the augmentation will also fail to be simple.

Example 8.5
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{K},0)\to(\mathbb{K}^{2},0)
be the curve
f(y)=(y^{2},y^{5})
.
As we saw in Examples
6.9
and
6.15
above, the OPSU
F(y,\lambda)=(y^{2},y^{5}+\lambda y)
is substantial and cross-substantial: hence by Propositions
6.3
,
6.6
and Theorem
7.4
,
f
is good for augmentation and we do not need to consider any other OPSU.

This is a simple map-germ with
\text{codim}_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)=2
, and when augmented via the function
g(z)=z^{3}
with
\tau(g)=2
, we obtain the map-germ
F_{4}
from
[
24
]
: it is a simple map-germ, yet the codimensions lie outside of the scope of the theorems mentioned from
[
4
]
.
In the versal unfolding of
F_{4}
we can find singularities of type
C_{3}\equiv(x,y^{2},xy^{3}+x^{3}y)
, which we have seen are not augmentations, so we cannot use Corollary
8.4
either. However, by Remark
5.8
, since the
C_{3}
singularities are simple,
F_{4}
is simple.

Augmenting
f
via the function
z^{4}
, which is simple and has Tjurina number equal to 3, yields a map-germ which we will call
F_{6}\equiv\left(x,y^{2},y^{5}+x^{4}y\right)
.
In its versal unfolding, besides simple, non-augmentation singularities of type
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
, we can find the family:

F_{6}^{a}(x,y)=\left(x,y^{2},y(y^{4}+ax^{2}y^{2}+x^{4})\right)

We can use Theorem
4.3
to check that these are not augmentations and the classification in
[
24
]
to see that
F_{6}^{a}
is non-simple for each
a\in\mathbb{K}
, which implies that
F_{6}
is not simple, i.e. it is not equivalent to any germ in Mond’s classification.

Example 8.6
.

We saw in Example
6.9
that the map-germ
11_{5}\equiv(x,y^{4}+x^{2}y+xy^{2})
from
[
29

does not admit any substantial OPSU, since all of them are weak equivalent and it admits a non-substantial OPSU.

This map-germ of
A_{e}
-codimension
2

to the non-simple map-germ

(see
[
23
]
).
It has
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension 4, and all the singularities that appear in its bifurcation diagram are augmentations of Beaks, Lips, Swallowtails and

(all of which appear in the bifurcation diagram of

5.10
,

Example 8.7
.

The map-germ

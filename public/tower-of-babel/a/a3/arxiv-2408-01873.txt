Inverse problem for the L L -operator in the Lax Pair of the Boussinesq equation on the circle
Andrey Badanin, Evgeny L. Korotyaev

Abstract
. We consider a third-order non-self-adjoint operator, which is an L L -operator in the Lax pair for the Boussinesq equation on the circle. We construct a mapping from the set of operator coefficients to the set of spectral data, similar to the corresponding mapping for the Hill operator constructed by E. Korotyaev. We prove that in a neighborhood of zero our mapping is analytic and one-to-one.

1.
Introduction

Consider the third-order non-self-adjoint operator

Hy=y^{\prime\prime\prime}+(py)^{\prime}+py^{\prime}+qy

in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, with real periodic coefficients
p,q
.
We assume that

\mathfrak{u}=(p,q)\in\mathfrak{H}:={\mathcal{H}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{H}},

where
{\mathcal{H}}=\{f\in L_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}({\mathbb{T}}):\int_{0}^{1}f(x)dx=0\}
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=\{f:f,f^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}}\}
,

{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The operator
H
is an
L
-operator in the Lax pair for the Boussinesq equation
on the circle

p_{tt}=-{1\over 3}(p_{xxxx}+4(p^{2})_{xx}),\hskip 10.00002ptq_{x}=p_{t},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in{\mathbb{T}}.

Kalantarov and Ladyzhenskaya
[
2
]
proved that
solutions of the Boussinesq equation may have a blow up in a finite time.
McKean
[
6
]
considered the operator
H
with small coefficients

p,q\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
. McKean does not provide a solution
to the inverse problem in fixed smoothness classes, however, the paper
[
6
]

contains some ideas that we use in our solution of the inverse problem.

We study the inverse spectral problem in the class
\mathfrak{H}
of the
coefficients. We construct a mapping from the space
\mathfrak{H}
to the
space of spectral data and prove that this mapping is an analytic
bijection of some small ball in
\mathfrak{H}
onto its image. We construct
our mapping as a composition of the Korotyaev’s mapping for the Hill
operator from
[
4
]
, and the McKean’s transformation from

[
6
]
. This transformation reduces the spectral problem for
the operator
H
to the spectral problem for the Hill operator with
an energy-dependent potential.

2.
Mapping for the Hill operator

There are two ways to solve the inverse problem for Hill operators.
First, we can apply the Marchenko-Ostrovsky results via the
conformal mapping
[
5
]
. This method requires analysis of the
properties of the quasi-momentum. Second, we can construct the
Korotyaev map
[
4
]
using the lengths of the gaps. This mapping
is constructed only in spectral terms. The second method is more
convenient for the Boussinesq equation.

Let us recall how in
[
4
]
the mapping
v\to\{\text{\it spectral data}\}
is constructed for the Hill operator
-y^{\prime\prime}+vy,v\in{\mathcal{H}}
, acting on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. The spectrum consists of the
intervals
[\lambda_{n-1}^{+},\lambda_{n}^{-}],n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, separated by the gaps

(\lambda_{n}^{-},\lambda_{n}^{+})
, here
\lambda_{0}^{+}<\lambda_{1}^{-}\leqslant\lambda_{1}^{+}<\lambda_{2}^{-}\leqslant...
are
the eigenvalues of the 2-periodic problem
y(0)=y(2),y^{\prime}(0)=y^{\prime}(2)
.
The eigenvalues
\mathfrak{m}_{n},n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, of the Dirichlet problem

y(0)=y(1)=0
are simple and satisfy
\mathfrak{m}_{n}\in[\lambda_{n}^{-},\lambda_{n}^{+}]
for
all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
The numbers
\mathfrak{m}_{n},n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are zeros of the entire
function
\varphi(1,\cdot)
, where
\varphi(x,\lambda)
is the solution of the
equation
-y^{\prime\prime}+vy=\lambda y
under the initial conditions
\varphi(0,\lambda)=0,\varphi^{\prime}(0,\lambda)=1
. Introduce the mapping
\psi:{\mathcal{H}}\to\ell^{2}\oplus\ell^{2}
from
[
4
]
by

\displaystyle\psi(v)=(\psi_{n}(v))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},\hskip 20.00003pt\psi_{n}=(\psi_{cn},\psi_{sn})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

(1)

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle\psi_{cn}={1\over 2}(\lambda_{n}^{+}+\lambda_{n}^{-})-\mathfrak{m}_{n},\hskip 10.00002pt\psi_{sn}=\big|{1\over 4}(\lambda_{n}^{+}-\lambda_{n}^{-})^{2}-\psi_{cn}^{2}\big|^{1\over 2}\mathop{\mathrm{sign}}\nolimits\mathfrak{h}_{sn},\ \ \mathfrak{h}_{sn}=\log|\varphi^{\prime}(1,\mathfrak{m}_{n})|,

where the branch of the logarithm satisfies
\log 1=0
. Recall the
result of Korotyaev
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1
.

The mapping
\psi
is a real analytic isomorphism
between
{\mathcal{H}}
and
\ell^{2}\oplus\ell^{2}
.

3.
Floquet multipliers and the 3-point problem

Here we describe our spectral data.
Consider the equation

y^{\prime\prime\prime}+(py)^{\prime}+py^{\prime}+qy=\lambda y,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathfrak{u}=(p,q)\in\mathfrak{H},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}.

(2)

Introduce the
3\times 3

monodromy matrix

M(\lambda)=(\varphi_{j}^{(k-1)}(1,\lambda))_{j,k=1}^{3},
where
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}

are fundamental solutions to Eq. (
2
), satisfying the conditions

\varphi_{j}^{(k-1)}(0,\lambda)=\delta_{jk},j,k=1,2,3
.
The matrix function
M
is entire and real for real
\lambda
.
The matrix
M
has
3
eigenvalues (
Floquet multiplier
)

\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
, and
\tau_{3}
, which satisfy the equality
\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\tau_{3}=1.

These three functions of the variable
\lambda
form three different branches
of a function analytic on some 3-sheet Riemann surface
{\mathcal{R}}
,
having only algebraic singularities in
{\mathbb{C}}
.
The branch points

of the surface
{\mathcal{R}}
coincide with the zeros of the entire function

\rho=(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})^{2}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{3})^{2}(\tau_{2}-\tau_{3})^{2}.

Introduce the operator

H_{dir}y=y^{\prime\prime\prime}+(py)^{\prime}+py^{\prime}+qy
on the interval
[0,2]
under the
3-point Dirichlet conditions

y(0)=y(1)=y(2)=0.

The spectrum of
H_{dir}
is pure discrete.

Introduce the domains
{\mathcal{D}}_{n},n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, by

\textstyle{\mathcal{D}}_{n}=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\lambda^{1\over 3}-{2\pi n\over\sqrt{3}}|<{2\over\sqrt{3}}\},\hskip 10.00002pt{\mathcal{D}}_{-n}=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:-\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}_{n}\},\hskip 10.00002ptn\geqslant 0,

here and below
\mathop{\mathrm{arg}}\nolimits\lambda^{1\over 3}\in(-{\pi\over 3},{\pi\over 3}]
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
small enough and introduce the ball

\textstyle{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}=\{\mathfrak{u}\in\mathfrak{H}:\int_{0}^{1}(|p^{\prime}|^{2}+|q|^{2})dx<\varepsilon\}.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathfrak{u}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
. Then there are exactly two (counting with multiplicity)
zeros
r_{n}^{\pm}
of the function
\rho
and exactly one simple eigenvalue

\mu_{n}
of the operator
H_{dir}
in each domain

{\mathcal{D}}_{n},n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}
. There are no other eigenvalues of the operator

H_{dir}
and zeros of the function
\rho
in
{\mathbb{C}}
, with only exception
two zeros
r_{0}^{\pm}
of the function
\rho
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{0}
.
For all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}
the numbers
r_{n}^{\pm}

and
\mu_{n}
are real,
\mu_{n}\in[r_{n}^{-},r_{n}^{+}]
, and the following
identities hold true:

r_{n}^{\pm}(\mathfrak{u})=r_{n}^{\pm}(\mathfrak{u}^{-})=-r_{-n}^{\mp}(\mathfrak{u}_{*})=-r_{-n}^{\mp}(\mathfrak{u}_{*}^{-}),\hskip 10.00002pt\mu_{-n}(\mathfrak{u})=-\mu_{n}(\mathfrak{u}_{*}^{-}),

where
\mathfrak{u}_{*}=(p,-q),\mathfrak{u}^{-}(x)=\mathfrak{u}(1-x),\mathfrak{u}_{*}^{-}(x)=\mathfrak{u}_{*}(1-x).

The eigenvalues
\mu_{n}
of the 3-point problem and the branch points

r_{n}^{\pm}
of the surface
{\mathcal{R}}
are our spectral data.
Let
y_{n}
be the eigenfunctions of the 3-point problem corresponding
to the eigenvalues
\mu_{n}
and normalized by the condition
y_{n}^{\prime}(0)=1
.

4.
The McKean transformation

Our construction of the map
\mathfrak{u}\to\{spectral\ data\}

is based on the corresponding Korotyaev map for the Hill operator
and uses the McKean transformation
[
6
]
of our third-order
operator into the Hill operator with an energy-dependent potential.
We describe this transformation briefly.

Let
\mathfrak{u}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
. Then the multiplier
\tau
, satisfying the
asymptotics
\tau(\lambda)=e^{\lambda^{1\over 3}}(1+o(1))
as
\lambda\to+\infty
, is an
analytic function on

{\mathscr{D}}=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\lambda|>1,\mathop{\mathrm{arg}}\nolimits\lambda\in(-{3\pi\over 4},{3\pi\over 4})\}
and
\tau

does not vanish in
{\mathscr{D}}
. Moreover,
\tau(\lambda)>0
for all
\lambda>1
. Let

\lambda\in{\mathscr{D}}
. Then there exists the unique solution
\mathfrak{f}
to
Eq. (
2
) such that
\mathfrak{f}(x+1,\lambda)=\tau(\lambda)\mathfrak{f}(x,\lambda),\ \mathfrak{f}(0,\lambda)=1.

Moreover,
\mathfrak{f}>0
for all
\lambda>1
. Define the function
\mathfrak{f}^{1\over 2}

on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathscr{D}}
by the condition
\mathfrak{f}^{1\over 2}>0
at
\lambda>1
. If
y
is a
solution to Eq. (
2
), then the function
u=\mathfrak{f}^{3\over 2}({y\over\mathfrak{f}})^{\prime}
satisfies the equation

\textstyle-u^{\prime\prime}+V(x,E)u=Eu,\hskip 10.00002ptV=E-2p-{3\over 2}{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime\prime}\over\mathfrak{f}}+{3\over 4}({\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\over\mathfrak{f}})^{2},

(3)

where
\textstyle E={3\over 4}\lambda^{2\over 3}\in\Omega=\{E\in{\mathbb{C}}:|E|>1,\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits E>0\}.

The energy-dependent 1-periodic potential
V(x,E),x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
is a function analytic with respect to
E
on the domain
\Omega

and real at
E\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Equations of the form (
3
) were studied in our paper
[
1
]
.
If
\mathfrak{u}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
, then there are exactly two (counting with multiplicities)
eigenvalues
E_{n}^{\pm}
of the 2-periodic problem
u(2)=u(0),u^{\prime}(2)=u^{\prime}(0)

for Eq. (
3
) and exactly one simple eigenvalue
\mathfrak{m}_{n}

of the Dirichlet problem
u(0)=u(1)=0
in each domain

\Omega_{n}=\{E\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\sqrt{E}-\pi n|<1\},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in{\mathbb{N}}.

There are no other eigenvalues in the domain
\Omega
.
All these eigenvalues are real and
\mathfrak{m}_{n}\in[E_{n}^{-},E_{n}^{+}]
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Introduce the solution
\varphi(x,E)
to Eq. (
3
),
satisfying the initial conditions
\varphi(0,E)=0,\varphi^{\prime}(0,E)=1
.
Each of the functions
\varphi(x,\cdot),x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, is analytic on
\Omega
.
The spectrum of the Dirichlet problem coincides with the set
of zeros of the function
\varphi(1,\cdot)
in
\Omega
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathfrak{u}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then
\varphi(\cdot,\mathfrak{m}_{n})

is the eigenfunction of the Dirichlet problem, corresponding
to the eigenvalue
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
, and the following identities hold true:

\displaystyle E_{n}^{\pm}(\mathfrak{u})={3\over 4}(r_{n}^{\pm}(\mathfrak{u}))^{2\over 3},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathfrak{m}_{n}(\mathfrak{u})={3\over 4}(-\mu_{-n}(\mathfrak{u}_{*}))^{2\over 3},

(4)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(1,\mathfrak{m}_{n}(\mathfrak{u}))=y_{-n}^{\prime}(1,\mathfrak{u}_{*})\tau^{-{1\over 2}}(-\mu_{-n}(\mathfrak{u}_{*}),\mathfrak{u}),\hskip 10.00002pt\tau^{-{1\over 2}}(-\mu_{-n}(\mathfrak{u}_{*}),\mathfrak{u})>0.

Theorem
3
shows that the McKean transformation maps
our spectral data for the 3rd order operator to spectral data
for the Hill operator. Namely, for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the branch points

r_{n}^{\pm}
of the Riemann surface
{\mathcal{R}}
become the eigenvalues
E_{n}^{\pm}

of the 2-periodic problem, and the eigenvalue
\mu_{n}
of the 3-point problem
becomes the eigenvalue
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
of the Dirichlet problem.

5.
Mapping.

McKeen
[
6
]
proved the following result.

Let
(p_{1},q_{1}),(p_{2},q_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})\times{\mathbb{C}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
,
be small enough and let the branch points of the surface
{\mathcal{R}}

and the eigenvalues of the 3-point problem on the surface

{\mathcal{R}}
for
(p_{1},q_{1})
and for
(p_{2},q_{2})
coincide with each other.
Then
(p_{1},q_{1})=(p_{2},q_{2})
.

In this section we formulate Theorem
4
that strengthens
and refines McKean’s result. We will construct our map

\mathfrak{u}\to\{spectral\ data\}
as a composition of
the Korotyaev map and the McKean transformation.
More precisely, substituting the identities (
4
)
into (
1
), we obtain our map (
5
).

Introduce the mapping
g:{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}\to\mathfrak{h}=\ell_{1}^{2}\oplus\ell_{1}^{2}

by the identities
g(\mathfrak{u})=(g_{n}(\mathfrak{u}))_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}}
, where

g_{n}=(g_{cn},g_{sn})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and the functions
g_{cn}(\mathfrak{u}),g_{sn}(\mathfrak{u}),\mathfrak{u}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
have the form

\displaystyle g_{n}=(g_{cn}(\mathfrak{u}),g_{sn}(\mathfrak{u})),\hskip 10.00002ptg_{-n}(\mathfrak{u})=g_{n}(\mathfrak{u}_{*}^{-}),

(5)

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle g_{cn}={3\over 4}\big({1\over 2}\big((r_{n}^{+}(\mathfrak{u}))^{2\over 3}+(r_{n}^{-}(\mathfrak{u}))^{2\over 3}\big)-(-\mu_{-n}(\mathfrak{u}_{*}))^{2\over 3}\big),\hskip 10.00002ptg_{sn}=\big|{1\over 4}\gamma_{n}^{2}(\mathfrak{u})-g_{cn}^{2}(\mathfrak{u})\big|^{1\over 2}\mathop{\mathrm{sign}}\nolimits h_{sn}(\mathfrak{u}),

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle h_{sn}(\mathfrak{u})=\log|y_{-n}^{\prime}(1,\mathfrak{u}_{*})\tau^{-{1\over 2}}(-\mu_{-n}(\mathfrak{u}_{*}),\mathfrak{u})|,\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma_{n}={3\over 4}\big((r_{n}^{+}(\mathfrak{u}))^{2\over 3}-(r_{n}^{-}(\mathfrak{u}))^{2\over 3}\big).

Here

\ell_{1}^{2}=\ell_{1}^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{0})=\{(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}}:\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}}|na_{n}|^{2}<\infty\}
.

Theorem 4
.

The mapping
g:{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}\to\mathfrak{h}
is a local analytic bijection
between
{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
and
g({\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon})
.

For the Boussinesq equation, this result means that solutions with sufficiently
small initial data maintain their smoothness class over time.

McKean
[
6
]
found a one-band periodic solution
of the Boussinesq equation in terms of the Weierstrass function.
There are no results on the existence of N-band periodic solutions
for
N\geqslant 2
in
[
6
]
. Our Theorem
4

implies the existence
of N-band solutions of the Boussinesq equations for any
N\geqslant 1
.

Corollary 5
.

The set of finite-band coefficients is dense in
{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
.

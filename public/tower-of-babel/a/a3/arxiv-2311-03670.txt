Abstract
. We prove that for ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} ( d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 ), the vertex-removal stability of harmonic measures (i.e. it is feasible to remove some vertex while changing the harmonic measure by a bounded factor) holds if and only if d = 2 d=2 . The proof mainly relies on geometric arguments, with a surprising use of the discrete Klein bottle. Moreover, a direct application of this stability verifies a conjecture of Calvert, Ganguly and Hammond [ 9 ] for the exponential decay of the least positive value of harmonic measures on ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} . Furthermore, the analogue of this conjecture for ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} with d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 is also proved in this paper, despite vertex-removal stability no longer holding.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the (discrete) harmonic measure (from infinity) on graphs, with a particular focus on the
d
-dimensional integer lattices
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(we assume
d\geq 2
throughout this paper). Roughly speaking, the harmonic measure of a finite set is the hitting distribution of this set by a random walk on the graph starting from infinity. To facilitate understanding, we first review the definition of harmonic measure on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(a more general definition will be provided later in Section
1.2
). A random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
starting from
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a discrete-time Markov process
\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
(we denote its law by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
) such that
\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(S_{0}=x\right)=1
and

(1.1)

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(S_{n+1}=z\mid S_{n}=y\right)=(2d)^{-1}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{y\sim z},\ \forall y,z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\ \text{and}\ n\geq 0,

where “
y\sim z
” means that
y
and
z
are adjacent (i.e.
|y-z|=1
, where
|\cdot|
is the Euclidean norm). For any non-empty, finite
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
y\in A
, the harmonic measure of
A
at
y
is defined as

(1.2)

\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim\limits_{|x|\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{\tau_{A}}=y\mid\tau_{A}<\infty),

where
\tau_{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\{n\geq 0\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S_{n}\in A\}
(with
\inf\emptyset=\infty
for completeness) is the first time when
\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
hits
A
. Refer to
[
20
, Theorem 2.1.3]
for the existence of the limit in (
1.2
). It follows from the definition that
\mathbb{H}_{A}(\cdot)
is a probability measure on
A
(i.e.
\sum_{y\in A}\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)=1
). Notably, the continuous analogue of
\mathbb{H}_{A}(\cdot)
can be obtained by replacing
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and random walk with
\mathrm{R}^{d}
and Brownian motion respectively, and is highly related to the Dirichlet problem in the partial differential equation (PDE) field (see
[
17
]
and
[
26
, Sections 3 and 8]
). It is also worth mentioning that there are numerous widely-studied models in statistical physics based on harmonic measures, such as Diffusion Limited Aggregation (DLA)
[
15
,
16
,
34
,
35
]
, Stationary DLA
[
27
,
31
]
, Dielectric Breakdown Model (DBM)
[
23
,
28
]
and Harmonic Activation and Transport (HAT)
[
8
,
9
]
for the discrete harmonic measure, as well as Hastings-Levitov
[
4
,
29
,
32
]
, Anisotropic Hastings–Levitov
[
11
]
and Aggregate Loewner Evolution (ALE)
[

]
for the continuous one. Readers may refer to
[

]
for excellent accounts.

This study mainly focuses on the following two fundamental questions.

Q1
:

How does removing a vertex from a set affect the harmonic measure of another vertex?

Q2
:

What is the least positive value of the harmonic measure that a vertex can have in a set of fixed cardinality?

In fact, as we will show later, these two questions are highly related to one another, and correspond to the two main components of this paper respectively.

1.1.
Vertex-removal stability of the harmonic measure

Here we formulate
Q1
. In fact, for any
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
A^{\prime}\subset A
and
y\in A^{\prime}
, we have
\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)\leq\mathbb{H}_{A^{\prime}}(y)
since
\{S_{\tau_{A}}=y\}\subset\{S_{\tau_{A^{\prime}}}=y\}
and
\{\tau_{A}<\infty\}\supset\{\tau_{A^{\prime}}<\infty\}
. Especially, removing a vertex
z\in A\setminus\{y\}
from
A
leads to an increase of the harmonic measure
\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)
. In light of this, for any
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
y\in A
with
\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)>0
, and any
z\in A\setminus\{y\}
, we define the price of removing
z
with respect to
\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)
as

(1.3)

\rho_{A,y}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\mathbb{H}_{A\setminus\{z\}}(y)}{\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)}\in\left[1,\infty\right),

where we require that
\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)>0
to avoid the case of
0
being the divisor. However,
\rho_{A,y}(z)
can be arbitrarily large. To see this, readers may refer to the following “tube” example in Figure
1
, where removing the point
z
enlarges the harmonic measure at
y
from exponentially small (with respect to
|A|
) to polynomially small (since after removing
z
, the random walk may directly reach
y
from the right side; in addition, by adapting the proof of
[
20
, Equation (2.41)]
, it can be shown that
\mathbb{H}_{A}(z)
decays polynomially with respect to
|A|
).

When discussing the vertex-removal stability, we aim to study whether for all
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
y\in A
with
\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)>0
and
|A|\geq 2
(where
|A|
is the cardinality of the set
A
), there exists a vertex
z\in A\setminus\{y\}
such that the price of removing
z
with respect to
\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)
is uniformly upper-bounded, which motivates us to present the following definition:

Definition 1
.

We define the vertex-removal stability constant for harmonic measure on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as

(1.4)

\psi(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup_{A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d},y\in A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)>0,|A|\geq 2}\min_{z\in A\setminus\{y\}}\rho_{A,y}(z).

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1
.

\psi(\mathbb{Z}^{d})<\infty
if and only if
d=2
.

Among the proof of Theorem
1
, in addition to the probabilistic properties of random walks, geometric arguments play a pivotal role. Specifically, to prove
\psi(\mathbb{Z}^{2})<\infty
, we introduce a special type of vertices named “marginal vertices” (which are candidates for removal with a bounded price) and then establish its existence in the case of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Moreover, the proof of
\psi(\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\infty
for
d\geq 3
surprisingly involves the discrete version of a classic geometric object, the Klein bottle. Furthermore, we observe that both the existence of marginal vertices and the construction of discrete Klein bottles can be naturally extended to a large class of graphs, which inspires us to consider a generalization of Theorem
1
in Remarks
3.8
and
4.4
.

1.2.
Least positive value of harmonic measures

Before turning to
Q2
, let us review a more general definition of harmonic measure called “wired harmonic measure (from infinity)”, which is introduced in
[
24
, Exercise 2.50 (c)]
. For any simple (i.e. there is no more than one edge between each two vertices, and no edge that connects a vertex to itself), locally finite (i.e. every vertex has a finite degree) and connected graph
\mathfs{G}=(\mathfs{V},\mathfs{E})
, the random walk on
\mathfs{G}
starting from
x\in\mathfs{V}
is a discrete-time Markov process
\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
(we also denote its law by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
) such that
\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{0}=x)=1
and

(1.5)

\mathbb{P}_{x}(S_{n+1}=z\mid S_{n}=y)=[\mathrm{deg}(y)]^{-1}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{y\sim z},\ \forall y,z\in\mathfs{V}\ \text{and}\ n\geq 0,

where “
y\sim z
” is equivalent to “
\{y,z\}\in\mathfs{E}
”, and
\mathrm{deg}(y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|\{v\in\mathfs{V}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v\sim y\}|
. Assume that in
\mathfs{V}
there is a prefixed vertex, which we denote by
v_{*}
. For each
v\in\mathfs{V}
, let
\|v\|
be the graph distance between
v_{*}
and
v
(i.e. the minimum length of a path on
\mathfs{G}
from
v_{*}
to
v
). For any
r\geq 0
, we denote
\mathbf{B}(r)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{v\in\mathfs{V}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\|v\|\leq r\}
. Let
\mathfs{G}_{r}
be the graph obtained from
\mathfs{G}
by identifying all vertices in
\mathfs{V}\setminus\mathbf{B}(r)
to a single vertex
\zeta_{r}
and then removing all edges connecting
\zeta_{r}
to itself. For any non-empty, finite
A\subset\mathfs{V}
and
y\in A
, the wired harmonic measure of
A
at
y
is defined as

(1.6)

\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\zeta_{r}}(S_{\tau_{A}}=y\mid\tau_{A}<\tau^{+}_{\{\zeta_{r}\}}),

where the law on the RHS is the probability distribution for the random walk on
\mathfs{G}_{r}
starting from
\zeta_{r}
, and
\tau^{+}_{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\{n\geq 1\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S_{n}\in F\}
. However, the existence of the limit in (
1.6
) is not valid for all graphs (only confirmed for transient graphs; see
[
24
, Exercise 2.50 (c)]
), and is an interesting topic on its own right (although it is not the focus of this study). Readers may refer to
[
5
]
and
[
24
, Section 10.7]
for related results. Here we employ the definition in (
1.6
) since it is consistent with the one presented in (
1.2
), and is valid for all graphs mentioned in this paper.

Extremal values of harmonic measures have been extensively studied in mathematics and physics, mostly in the context of interacting particle systems driven by diffusion (such as DLA, DBM, Hastings-Levitov and ALE mentioned above). Particularly, in the case of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, Kesten
[
14
]
proved the following polynomial upper bounds (this type of estimate is commonly named “discrete Beurling’s estimate”) for harmonic measures: for any
d\geq 2
, there exists a constant
C(d)>0
such that for every non-empty, finite, connected set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

(1.7)

\max_{y\in A}\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C(2)[\mathrm{rad}(A)]^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{for }d=2\\
&\\
C(d)|A|^{\frac{2-d}{d}}&\text{for }d\geq 3\end{array}\right.,

where
\mathrm{rad}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max_{y\in A}|y|
. Note that for
d=2
, while the endpoints of line segments achieve the RHS of (
1.7
) up to a multiplicative constant, it remains uncertain whether these points constitute the maximum in (
1.7
). With the help of (
1.7
), Kesten
[
14
]
derived an upper bound for the growth rate of DLA. Readers may refer to
[
20
, Sections 2.5 and 2.6]
for other versions of discrete Beurling’s estimates and more applications on DLA. Subsequently, Lawler and Limic
[
21
]
, and Benjamini and Yadin
[
3
]
extended the discrete Beurling’s estimates to more general random walks. Furthermore, Makarov
[
25
]
established that for any domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose boundary is a Jordan curve, the Hausdorff dimension of the support of its continuous harmonic measure is
1
. Later, Lawler
[
18
]
proved its discrete analogue as follows: on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, for any
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<1
and
\beta<\alpha-\frac{1}{2}
, there exists a constant
C(\alpha,\beta)>0
such that for every connected
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
\mathrm{rad}(A)=R
,

(1.8)

\mathbb{H}_{A}\Big(\big\{y\in A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}R^{-1}e^{-(\log R)^{\alpha}}\leq\mathbb{H}_{A}(y)\leq R^{-1}e^{(\log R)^{\alpha}}\big\}\Big)\geq 1-\frac{C}{(\log R)^{\beta}}.

Moreover, Benjamini
[
2
]
established that the cardinality of the support of the harmonic measure of any set contained in a box on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with side length
R
is at most
o(R^{d})
, and afterward, a quantitative version was given by Bolthausen and Münch-Berndl
[
6
]
. As shown above, these results concentrated on either the upper bounds, or the typical values, or the supports of harmonic measures, and only considered the sets with connectivity or bounded radius. Our understanding of the minimal value of harmonic measures of general, possibly disconnected or spread-out sets, remains limited. Recently, in the study of HAT
[
8
,
9
]
, the least positive value of harmonic measures of general sets (which is the focus of
Q2
) played an important role in bounding the time of collapse in HAT.

Another interesting followup to this paper, is to study how the least positive value of harmonic measures changes when we impose a minimum separation distance among the vertices. 3
. For Item (1), intuitively, for any
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the vertices faraway from
y
have less influence on the harmonic measure at
y
. Therefore, we expect that
\mathfs{M}_{n}^{m}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
might be realized by arranging
A
into a ball centering at some
y
as densely as possible, for which the harmonic measure at
y
is roughly
e^{-O(n^{1/d})}
. For Item (2), suppose that the
n
vertices in
A
are sufficiently spaced apart, then the harmonic measure of
A
should approximate the uniform distribution on
A
, resulting in
\frac{1}{n}
for the value at each vertex by symmetry. The interesting question is to identify an optimal estimate on the order of the function
f(n)
in Item (2).

We conclude this section by providing an overview of the organization of this paper. In Section
2
, we present necessary notations, and review useful results on graph theory and random walks. In Section
3
, we demonstrate that
\psi(\mathbb{Z}^{2})<\infty
. Subsequently, Section
4
contains the proof of
\psi(\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\infty
for
d\geq 3
. Lastly, we establish Theorem
3
in Section
5
.

We cite the following lemma concerning the connectivity of different types of boundaries.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any finite,

(1)

(
[

(2)

(
[

In the case of

, the next lemma provides slightly stronger connectivity for

(compared to Item (1) in Lemma
2.1
) under certain additional conditions.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any finite, connected
D\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, denote
\overline{D}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=D\cup\partial^{\mathrm{o}}_{\infty}D
. If
\partial_{\infty}^{\mathrm{i}}\overline{D}=\partial^{\mathrm{o}}_{\infty}D
, then
(\partial^{\mathrm{o}}_{\infty}D)\setminus\{z\}
is
*
-connected for all
z\in\partial^{\mathrm{o}}_{\infty}D
.

Proof.

By
[
13
, Corollary 2.2]
, there exists a
*
-circuit
\eta_{*}
with
\eta_{*}(0)=z
and
\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta_{*})\subset\partial^{\mathrm{o}}_{\infty}D
such that

is contained in the interior of
\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta_{*})
(i.e. the union of all finite clusters of
[\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta_{*})]^{c}
). In what follows, we prove
\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta_{*})=\partial^{\mathrm{o}}_{\infty}D
by contradiction. Assume that
w\in(\partial^{\mathrm{o}}_{\infty}D)\setminus\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta_{*})
. On the one hand, when
w
is in the interior of
\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta_{*})
, any path from

to infinity must intersect
\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta_{*})\subset(\partial^{\mathrm{o}}_{\infty}D)\setminus\{w\}
, which implies

and in turn causes contradiction with the assumption

is contained in the interior of

and therefore has a length of at least

2.4.
Random walk

Recall that
\mathbb{P}_{x}
for
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the law of the random walk
\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
starting from
x
. We denote the expectation under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
by
\mathbb{E}_{x}
. Also recall that for any non-empty
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we denote
\tau_{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\{n\geq 0\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S_{n}\in A\}
and
\tau_{A}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\{n\geq 1\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S_{n}\in A\}
. Especially, when
A=\{y\}
for some
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we may omit the braces.

Green’s function:
For
d\geq 3
, the Green’s function on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is defined as

(2.3)

G(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{x}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{S_{i}=y}\Big),\ \forall x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Note that for
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, this definition is not valid since the expectation on the RHS of (
2.3
) is infinity. However, for all
d\geq 2
, we may always define Green’s function for a non-empty set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by

(2.4)

G_{A}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{x}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{\tau_{A}}\mathbbm{1}_{S_{i}=y}\Big),\ \forall x,y\in A^{c}.

Next, we cite some basic formulas for
G_{A}(x,y)
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
22
, Lemma 4.6.1 and Proposition 4.6.2]
)
.

For any non-empty
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
x,y\in A^{c}
, we have

(2.5)

G_{A}(x,y)=G_{A}(y,x),

(2.6)

G_{A}(x,x)=\left[\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{A}<\tau^{+}_{x}\right)\right]^{-1},

(2.7)

G_{A}(x,y)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{y}<\tau_{A}\right)G_{A}(y,y).

In addition, for any non-empty
A^{\prime}\subset A
, we have

(2.8)

G_{A^{\prime}}(x,y)=G_{A}(x,y)+\sum_{w\in A\setminus A^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{A}=\tau_{w}<\infty\right)G_{A^{\prime}}(w,y).

We also need the following lemma, which is commonly referred to as “the last-exit decomposition”. Its proof follows that of
[
22
, Proposition 4.6.4]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
A_{1}\subset A_{2}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
y\in A_{1}
and
z\in A_{2}\setminus A_{1}
, we have

(2.9)

\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(\tau_{A_{1}}=\tau_{y}<\infty\right)=\sum_{v\in A_{2}\setminus A_{1}}G_{A_{1}}(z,v)\mathbb{P}_{v}\left(\tau_{A_{2}}^{+}=\tau_{y}<\infty\right).

Proof.

For the random walk starting from
z\in A_{2}\setminus A_{1}
, let
L
the be largest integer in
\left[0,\tau_{A_{1}}\right)
such that
S_{L}\in A_{2}
. Then we have that
\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(\tau_{A_{1}}=\tau_{y}<\infty\right)
equals to

\begin{split}&\sum_{v\in A_{2}\setminus A_{1}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(\tau_{A_{1}}=\tau_{y}<\infty,L=k,S_{L}=v\right)\\
&=\sum_{v\in A_{2}\setminus A_{1}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(k<\tau_{A_{1}}<\infty,S_{k}=v,S_{\tau_{A_{1}}}=y,\forall k+1\leq j<\tau_{A_{1}},S_{j}\notin A_{2}\right).\end{split}

By Markov property, the probability on the RHS can be written as

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(k<\tau_{A_{1}},S_{k}=v\right)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{v}\left(\tau_{A_{1}}<\infty,S_{\tau_{A_{1}}}=y,\forall 1\leq j<\tau_{A_{1}},S_{j}\notin A_{2}\right)\\
&=\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(k<\tau_{A_{1}},S_{k}=v\right)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{v}\left(\tau_{A_{2}}^{+}=\tau_{y}<\infty\right).\end{split}

Combining these two equations, we conclude this lemma.
∎

We then prove the subsequent lemma, which will facilitate the comparison between
Green’s functions for random walks with different starting points.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
d\geq 2
and positive numbers
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{3}
, there exist constants
C_{3}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},d),c_{3}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},d)>0
such that for any
N>C_{3}
,
A\subset\mathbf{B}(\lambda_{1}N)\cup[\mathbf{B}(\lambda_{3}N)]^{c}
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\partial^{\mathrm{i}}\mathbf{B}(\lambda_{2}N)
and
x_{3}\in\partial^{\mathrm{i}}\mathbf{B}(\lambda_{3}N)
,

(2.10)

G_{A}(x_{1},x_{3})\geq c_{3}\cdot G_{A}(x_{2},x_{3}).

Proof.

Let
\epsilon=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{d}}\cdot\min\{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}\}
. Note that for any
x_{2}\in\partial^{\mathrm{i}}\mathbf{B}(\lambda_{2}N)
, the
\ell^{2}
ball
B_{x_{2}}(\epsilon N)
is disjoint from
\mathbf{B}(\lambda_{1}N)\cup[\mathbf{B}(\lambda_{3}N)]^{c}
. By strong Markov property,

(2.11)

\begin{split}G_{A}(x_{1},x_{3})\geq&\mathbb{P}_{x_{1}}\left(\tau_{\partial^{\mathrm{i}}B_{x_{2}}(\epsilon N)}<\tau_{\mathbf{B}(\lambda_{1}N)\cup[\mathbf{B}(\lambda_{3}N)]^{c}}\right)\cdot\min_{w\in\partial^{\mathrm{i}}B_{x_{2}}(\epsilon N)}G_{A}(w,x_{3})\\
\geq&c\cdot\min_{w\in\partial^{\mathrm{i}}B_{x_{2}}(\epsilon N)}G_{A}(w,x_{3}),\end{split}

where we use the invariance principle in the second inequality. For each
w\in\partial^{\mathrm{i}}B_{x_{2}}(\epsilon N)
, since
A\cup\{x_{3}\}\subset[B_{x_{2}}(2\epsilon N)]^{c}
, one has:

(2.12)

G_{A}(w,x_{3})=\sum_{w^{\prime}\in\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B_{x_{2}}(2\epsilon N)}\mathbb{P}_{w}\left(\tau_{\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B_{x_{2}}(2\epsilon N)}=\tau_{w^{\prime}}\right)G_{A}(w^{\prime},x_{3}),

(2.13)

G_{A}(x_{2},x_{3})=\sum_{w^{\prime}\in\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B_{x_{2}}(2\epsilon N)}\mathbb{P}_{x_{2}}\left(\tau_{\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B_{x_{2}}(2\epsilon N)}=\tau_{w^{\prime}}\right)G_{A}(w^{\prime},x_{3}).

Hitting two distant vertices on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
:
The next lemma shows that for the random walk on

Lemma 2.6
.

In the case of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

and

By strong Markov property, we have

v_{1}\in\widehat{F}_{1}
and
v_{2}\in\widecheck{F}_{2}
, we have

(3.5)

G_{\widetilde{A}}(v_{1},v_{2})\leq\left[1-\mathbb{P}_{\mathring{v}_{1}}\left(\tau_{D}<\tau_{\widetilde{A}}\right)\right]^{-1}G_{A}(\mathring{v}_{1},v_{2}).

Moreover,

(3.6)

G_{\widetilde{A}}(v_{1},v_{2})\leq\left[1-\mathbb{P}_{\mathring{v}_{1}}\left(\tau_{D}<\tau_{\widetilde{A}}\right)\right]^{-1}\left[\mathbb{P}_{v_{1}}\left(\tau_{v_{2}}<\tau_{A}\right)\right]^{-1}G_{A}(v_{1},v_{2}).

Proof.

By applying (
2.8
) with
A^{\prime}=\widetilde{A}
,
x=\mathring{v}_{1}
and
y=v_{2}
, we have

(3.7)

\begin{split}G_{\widetilde{A}}(\mathring{v}_{1},v_{2})=G_{A}(\mathring{v}_{1},v_{2})+\sum_{w\in D}\mathbb{P}_{\mathring{v}_{1}}\left(\tau_{D}=\tau_{w}<\tau_{\widetilde{A}}\right)G_{\widetilde{A}}(w,v_{2}).\end{split}

We claim that any path
\eta
from
w\in D
to
v_{2}\in\widecheck{F}_{2}
without hitting
\widetilde{A}
must intersect
\widecheck{F}_{1}
. In what follows, we prove this claim separately in two cases:

(1)

When
v_{2}\in F_{1}
, since
F_{1}\subset F_{2}
, one has
(A\cup F_{2})^{c}_{\bm{0}}\subset(A\cup F_{1})^{c}_{\bm{0}}
and therefore,
v_{2}\in\widecheck{F}_{2}\cap F_{1}\subset\widecheck{F}_{1}
, which indicates that
\eta
intersects
\widecheck{F}_{1}
.

(2)

When
v_{2}\in F_{2}\setminus F_{1}
, we decompose
\eta
into two sub-paths

and
\eta_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\eta(\ell+1),...,\eta(\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta)))
, where
\ell
is the last time when
\eta
is in

. Since
\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta_{2})\subset(A\cup F_{1})^{c}
and
\eta_{2}(-1)=v_{2}\in\widecheck{F}_{2}\subset(A\cup F_{1})^{c}_{\bm{0}}
, we have
\eta(\ell+1)\in(A\cup F_{1})^{c}_{\bm{0}}
and therefore,
\eta(\ell)\in\widecheck{F}_{1}
. Thus,
\eta
intersects
\widecheck{F}_{1}
.

With the claim above confirmed and by strong Markov property, we have

G_{\widetilde{A}}(w,v_{2})=\sum_{w^{\prime}\in\widecheck{F}_{1}}\mathbb{P}_{w}\left(\tau_{\widecheck{F}_{1}}=\tau_{w^{\prime}}<\infty\right)G_{\widetilde{A}}(w^{\prime},v_{2}).

Therefore, by the maximality of

, we get

G_{\widetilde{A}}(w,v_{2})\leq\max_{w^{\prime}\in\widecheck{F}_{1}}G_{\widetilde{A}}(w^{\prime},v_{2})\leq G_{\widetilde{A}}(\mathring{v}_{1},v_{2}),\ \forall w\in D.

Combined with (
3.7
), it yields that

(3.8)

G_{\widetilde{A}}(\mathring{v}_{1},v_{2})\leq\left[1-\mathbb{P}_{\mathring{v}_{1}}\left(\tau_{D}<\tau_{\widetilde{A}}\right)\right]^{-1}G_{A}(\mathring{v}_{1},v_{2}).

By (
3.8
) and the fact that
G_{\widetilde{A}}(v_{1},v_{2})\leq G_{\widetilde{A}}(\mathring{v}_{1},v_{2})
, we conclude (
3.5
).

It follows from (
2.7
) that

G_{A}(\mathring{v}_{1},v_{2})\leq G_{A}(v_{2},v_{2})=\left[\mathbb{P}_{v_{1}}\left(\tau_{v_{2}}<\tau_{A}\right)\right]^{-1}G_{A}(v_{1},v_{2}).

Combined with (
3.5
), it implies (
3.6
) and completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.8

(Generalization of Theorem
1
for
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
)
.

In the proof of
\psi(\mathbb{Z}^{2})<\infty
presented in this section, the existence of marginal vertices (Lemma
3.1
) only relies on the planality of the graph. Moreover, for the estimates for the price of removing a vertex
z_{\dagger}
of Type
\mathrm{(i)}
and Type
\mathrm{(ii)}
, what essentially matters is that the probability for the random walk to surround each face is bounded away from
0
, which can be easily ensured when
\sup_{v}\mathrm{deg}(v)
and
\sup_{\mathcal{S}}|\bm{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{S})|
are both finite. Additionally, to bound the price of removing a vertex
z_{\dagger}
of Type
\mathrm{(iii)}
, the essntial properties we rely on are summarized in Lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
, whose proofs are based on the invariance principle. For some sufficient conditions of these properties, readers may refer to
[
1
, Sections 4.3 and 7.2]
. To summarize, we expect that the vertex-removal stability holds for all planar graphs of bounded degree and bounded number of edges surrounding every face, where the random walk from each vertex converges to a Brownian motion under a uniform rate.

4.

This section includes the proof of

Proposition 4.1
.

In the case of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for

c_{6}(d)>0
and a sequence of sets
\{K_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
with

and

(where

is an increasing sequence of integers) such that for any large enough

and any

,

(4.1)

\frac{\mathrm{Es}_{K_{n}\setminus\{z\}}(\bm{0})}{\mathrm{Es}_{K_{n}}(\bm{0})}\geq e^{c_{6}n}.

In fact, Theorem
1
for

follows immediately once Proposition
4.1

Proof of Theorem
1
for
d\geq 3
assuming Proposition
4.1
.

Let

be the sequence presented by Proposition
4.1
. For any large enough

and

2.17

Thus, it follows from Definition
1
that

(4.2)

\psi(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\geq\min_{z\in K_{n}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\frac{\mathbb{H}_{K_{n}\setminus\{z\}}(\bm{0})}{\mathbb{H}_{K_{n}}(\bm{0})}\geq e^{c_{6}n}.

Since

can be arbitrarily large, we conclude
\psi(\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\infty

.
∎

Subsequently, we present the construction of the required sequence of discrete Klein Bottles

(refer to Figure

Before proving Proposition

Lemma 4.3
.

For any self-avoiding path
\eta
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, let
\gamma(\eta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{P}_{\eta(0)}\left[\tau_{\eta(-1)}<\tau_{\partial^{\mathrm{o}}\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta)}\right]

(4.3)

\gamma(\eta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{2\sqrt{d^{2}-1}}{(d+\sqrt{d^{2}-1})^{\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta)+1}-(d-\sqrt{d^{2}-1})^{\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta)+1}}.

Proof.

For any
0\leq i\leq\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta)
, let
q_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{P}_{\eta(i)}\left[\tau_{\eta(-1)}<\tau_{\partial^{\mathrm{o}}\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta)}\right]
. Then the number sequence
\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta)}
satisfies the following:

•

q_{0}=\frac{1}{2d}q_{1}
(for the random walk from
\eta(0)
not hitting
\partial^{\mathrm{o}}\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\eta)
, the only possible move for the first step is to
\eta(1)
);

•

q_{i}=\frac{1}{2d}(q_{i-1}+q_{i+1})
for all
1\leq i\leq\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta)-1
(when the aforementioned random walk is at
\eta(i)
, it can only go forward to
\eta(i+1)
or go backward to
\eta(i-1)
);

•

q_{\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta)}=1
(
\tau_{\eta(-1)}=0
a.s. for the random walk from
\eta(-1)
).

By the basic theory for recursive sequences, the only solution is given by

q_{i}=\frac{(d+\sqrt{d^{2}-1})^{i+1}-(d-\sqrt{d^{2}-1})^{i+1}}{(d+\sqrt{d^{2}-1})^{\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta)+1}-(d-\sqrt{d^{2}-1})^{\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta)+1}},\ \forall 0\leq i\leq\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta).

By taking
i=0
, we obtain the formula (
4.3
).

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

We first prove an upper bound for
\mathrm{Es}_{K_{n}}(\bm{0})
. Arbitrarily take a large enough

. For any
y\in\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B(M)
, by Markov property and Lemma
2.4
, the probability
\mathbb{P}_{\bm{0}}\big(\tau_{K_{n}\cup\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B(M)}^{+}=\tau_{y}\big)
can be written as

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{v_{1}\in N(\bm{0})}\mathbb{P}_{v_{1}}\left(\tau_{K_{n}\cup\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B(M)}=\tau_{y}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{v_{1}\in N(\bm{0})}\sum_{v_{2}\in N(x_{n}^{1})\cap\Lambda(n)}G_{K_{n}}(v_{1},v_{2})\mathbb{P}_{v_{2}}\left(\tau^{+}_{K_{n}\cup[\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B(M)]\cup[N(x_{n}^{1})\cap\Lambda(n)]}=\tau_{y}\right).\end{split}

Note that
G_{K_{n}}(v_{1},v_{2})\leq G(\bm{0},\bm{0})
. Moreover, by Obsevation (1) in Remark
4.2
, the event
\{\tau^{+}_{K_{n}\cup[\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B(M)]\cup[N(x_{n}^{1})\cap\Lambda(n)]}=\tau_{y}\}
implies that the random walk must go to
x_{n}^{1}
in the first step, and then move along
\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{o}}\circ\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{i}}
. Thus, by Lemma
4.3
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{\bm{0}}\left(\tau^{+}_{K_{n}\cup\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B(M)}=\tau_{y}\right)\leq 2d\cdot G(\bm{0},\bm{0})\cdot\gamma(\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{o}}\circ\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{i}})\leq C\left(d+\sqrt{d^{2}-1}\right)^{-\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{o}})-\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{i}})}.\end{split}

By summing over
y\in\partial^{\mathrm{o}}B(M)
and letting

, we obtain

(4.4)

\mathrm{Es}_{K_{n}}(\bm{0})\leq C\left(d+\sqrt{d^{2}-1}\right)^{-\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{o}})-\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{i}})}.

Next, we prove a lower bound of
\mathrm{Es}_{K_{n}\setminus\{z\}}(\bm{0})
for all
z\in K_{n}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
. When
z\in K_{n}^{2}
, by Observation (4a) in Remark
4.2
,
\mathrm{Es}_{K_{n}\setminus\{z\}}(\bm{0})
is bounded from below by

(4.5)

\begin{split}&\gamma(\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}})\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}}(-1)}\left(\tau_{\hat{\eta}^{\mathrm{i}}_{z}(0)}<\tau_{K_{n}}\right)\gamma(\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{i}}\circ\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{o}})\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{o}}(-1)}\left(\tau_{K_{n}}=\infty\right)\\
&\geq c\left(d+\sqrt{d^{2}-1}\right)^{-0.3n}\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}}(-1)}\left(\tau_{\hat{\eta}^{\mathrm{i}}_{z}(0)}<\tau_{K_{n}}\right)\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{o}}(-1)}\left(\tau_{K_{n}}=\infty\right),\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used Lemma
4.3
and the fact that (recalling Observation (1) in Remark
4.2
)

\max\left\{\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}}),\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{i}}),\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{o}})\right\}\leq 0.1n.

Recall that
\min\{\bm{\mathrm{D}}(\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}}(-1),K_{n}),\bm{\mathrm{D}}(\hat{\eta}^{\mathrm{i}}_{z}(0),K_{n})\}\geq\frac{n}{100}
. Moreover,
[

shows that for a random walk starting from

, the probability that hits

before

\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}}(-1)}\left(\tau_{\hat{\eta}^{\mathrm{i}}_{z}(0)}<\tau_{K_{n}}\right)

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}}(-1)}\left(\tau_{B_{\hat{\eta}^{\mathrm{i}}_{z}(0)}(\frac{n}{200\sqrt{d}})}<\tau_{K_{n}}\right)\cdot\min_{w\in\partial^{\mathrm{i}}B_{\hat{\eta}^{\mathrm{i}}_{z}(0)}(\frac{n}{200\sqrt{d}})}\mathbb{P}_{w}\left(\tau_{\hat{\eta}^{\mathrm{i}}_{z}(0)}<\tau_{\partial^{\mathrm{i}}B_{\hat{\eta}^{\mathrm{i}}_{z}(0)}(\frac{n}{100\sqrt{d}})}\right)\geq cn^{2-d}.\end{split}

2.8

\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{o}}(-1)}\left(\tau_{K_{n}}=\infty\right)

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{o}}(-1)}\left(\tau_{\partial^{\mathrm{i}}B(4n)}<\tau_{K_{n}}\right)\cdot\min_{w\in\partial^{\mathrm{i}}B(4n)}\mathbb{P}_{w}\left(\tau_{K_{n}}=\emptyset\right)\geq c.\end{split}

Combining these two estimates and (

\mathrm{Es}_{K_{n}\setminus\{z\}}(\bm{0})\geq cn^{2-d}\left(d+\sqrt{d^{2}-1}\right)^{-0.3n}.

\begin{split}\mathrm{Es}_{K_{n}\setminus\{z\}}(\bm{0})\geq\gamma(\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}})\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}}(-1)}\big(\tau_{\hat{\eta}^{\mathrm{i}}_{x_{n}^{1}}(0)}<\tau_{K_{n}}\big)\gamma(\hat{\eta}_{x_{n}^{1}}^{\mathrm{i}}\circ\bar{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{o}}\circ\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{o}})\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{o}}(-1)}\left(\tau_{K_{n}}=\infty\right).\end{split}

\begin{split}\mathrm{Es}_{K_{n}\setminus\{z\}}(\bm{0})\geq&\gamma(\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}})\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{\bm{0}}(-1)}\big(\tau_{\hat{\eta}^{\mathrm{i}}_{z}(0)}<\tau_{K_{n}}\big)\gamma(\hat{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{i}}\circ\bar{\eta}_{z}^{\mathrm{i}}\circ\hat{\eta}_{x_{n}^{3}}^{\mathrm{o}})\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}_{x_{n}^{3}}^{\mathrm{o}}(-1)}\left(\tau_{K_{n}}=\infty\right)\\

By (

) and (
4.8

\mathrm{Es}_{K_{n}\setminus\{z\}}(\bm{0})\geq cn^{2-d}\left(d+\sqrt{d^{2}-1}\right)^{-0.3n-\max\{\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{o}}),\bm{\mathrm{L}}(\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{i}})\}}.

Combined with (

(4.9)

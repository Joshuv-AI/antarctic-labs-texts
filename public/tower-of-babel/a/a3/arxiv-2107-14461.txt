Affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties associated with generic Newton points
Xuhua He

Abstract
. This paper gives an explicit formula of the dimension of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties associated with generic Newton point in terms of Demazure product of Iwahori-Weyl groups.

To George Lusztig with admiration

Introduction

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field, and
\breve{F}
be the completion of its maximal unramified extension. Let
\sigma
be the Frobenius automorphism of
\breve{F}
over
F
. Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group over
F
. Let
\breve{}\mathcal{I}
be the standard Iwahori subgroup of
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. Let
w
be an element in the Iwahori-Weyl group
\tilde{W}
and
b\in\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. The affine Deligne-Lusztig variety associated to
(w,b)
is defined to be

X_{w}(b)=\{g\breve{}\mathcal{I}\in\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})/\breve{}\mathcal{I};g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}\}.

This is a subscheme locally of finite type, of the affine flag variety. It is introduced by Rapoport in
[
Ra05
]
and plays an important role when studying the special fiber of both Shimura varieties and moduli spaces of Shtukas.

Let
B(\mathbf{G})
be the set of
\sigma
-conjugacy classes of
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. The set
B(\mathbf{G})
is classified by the Kottwitz map and the Newton map. The set
B(\mathbf{G})
is equipped with a natural partial order by requiring the equality under the Kottwitz map and the dominance order on the associated Newton points. It is easy to see that if
b
and
b^{\prime}
are in the same
\sigma
-conjugacy class, then
X_{w}(b)
and
X_{w}(b^{\prime})
are isomorphic. The set
B(G)_{w}=\{[b]\in B(G);X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset\}
contains a unique maximal element, which we denote by
[b_{w}]
. The variety
X_{w}(b_{w})
is called the affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties associated with generic Newton points. It has been studied in
[
Mi16+
]
and
[
MV20
]
.

The main purpose of this note is as follows.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
and
[b_{w}]
be the maximal element in
B(G)_{w}
. Then

\dim X_{w}(b_{w})=\ell(w)-\lim_{n\infty}\frac{\ell(w^{\ast_{\sigma},n})}{n}.

Here
\ast
is the Demazure product on
\tilde{W}
and

w^{\ast_{\sigma},n}=w\ast\sigma(w)\ast\cdots\ast\sigma^{n-1}(w)

is the
n
-th
\sigma
-twisted Demazure power of
w
.

Now we discuss the motivation for this formula and the outline of the proof.

For simplicity, we only discuss the split group case here. In this case,
\sigma
acts trivially on
\tilde{W}
and we may simply write
w^{\ast,n}
for
w^{\ast_{\sigma},n}
.

There is a natural bijection between the poset
B(G)
and the poset of straight conjugacy classes in
\tilde{W}
, established in
[
He14
]
&
[
He16a
]
. Here by definition, an element
x\in\tilde{W}
is straight if
\ell(x^{n})=n\ell(x)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a conjugacy class of
\tilde{W}
is straight if it contains a straight element.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{w}
be the straight conjugacy class associated to
[b_{w}]
and
w^{\prime}
be a minimal length element in
C_{w}
. Then we have
\dim X_{w}(b_{w})=\ell(w)-\ell(w^{\prime})
. In particular, if
w
is a straight element, then we may take
w^{\prime}=w
. In this case,
\dim X_{w}(b_{w})=0
. By definition, for straight element
w
we have
\ell(w^{\ast,n})=\ell(w^{n})=n\ell(w)
. So the statement is obvious in this case. Theorem
0.1
gives an estimate on the “non-straightness” of the element
w
.

By our assumption, any generic element in the double coset
\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}
is
\sigma
-conjugate to an element in
\breve{}\mathcal{I}w^{\prime}\breve{}\mathcal{I}
. Let
g
be a generic element in
\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}
and
g^{\prime}\in\breve{}\mathcal{I}w^{\prime}\breve{}\mathcal{I}
such that
g
and
g^{\prime}
are
\sigma
-conjugate by an element
h\in\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. We consider the
\sigma
-twisted power defined by
g^{\sigma,n}=g\sigma(g)\cdots\sigma^{n-1}(g)
. Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
g^{\sigma,n}
and
(g^{\prime})^{\sigma,n}
are
\sigma^{n}
-conjugate by the same element
h
. By the straightness assumption on
\mathcal{O}_{w}
, we have
(g^{\prime})^{\sigma,n}\in\breve{}\mathcal{I}(w^{\prime})^{n}\breve{}\mathcal{I}
. On the other hand, we have

g^{\sigma,n}\in(\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I})(\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I})\cdots(\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I})\subset\overline{\breve{}\mathcal{I}w^{\ast,n}\breve{}\mathcal{I}}.

However, it is not clear if
g^{\sigma,n}\in\breve{}\mathcal{I}w^{\ast,n}\breve{}\mathcal{I}
.

The trick we will use to bypass this difficulty is to apply the technique in
[
HN20
]
and to translate the question on the
\sigma
-conjugation action on
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
to the question on the ordinary conjugation action on a reductive
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{C}((\epsilon))
. Let
\breve{}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
be an Iwahori subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{C}((\epsilon)))
. Using Lusztig’s theory of total positivity
[
Lu94
]
and
[
Lu19
]
, we show that for generic element
g\in\breve{}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
,
g^{n}\in\breve{}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(w^{\ast,n})\breve{}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
. The condition that there exists
h\in\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{C}((\epsilon))
such that
\breve{}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(w^{\ast,n})\breve{}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\cap h(\breve{}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(w^{\prime})^{n}\breve{}\mathcal{I}^{\prime})h^{-1}\neq\emptyset
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
implies that
\ell(w^{\prime})=\lim_{n\infty}\frac{\ell(w^{\ast,n})}{n}
. This finishes the proof.

1.
Preliminary

1.1.
Notations

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group over a non-archimedean local field
F
. Let
\breve{F}
be the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
and
\sigma
be the Frobenius morphism of
\breve{F}/F
. The residue field of
F
is a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the residue field of
\breve{F}
is the algebraically closed field
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. We write
\breve{G}
for
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. We use the same symbol
\sigma
for the induced Frobenius morphism on
\breve{G}
.

Let
S
be a maximal
\breve{F}
-split torus of
\mathbf{G}
defined over
F
, which contains a maximal
F
-split torus. Let
\mathcal{A}
be the apartment of
\mathbf{G}_{\breve{F}}
corresponding to
S_{\breve{F}}
. We fix a
\sigma
-stable alcove
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathcal{A}
, and let
\breve{}\mathcal{I}\subset\breve{G}
be the Iwahori subgroup corresponding to
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
\breve{}\mathcal{I}
is
\sigma
-stable.

Let
T
be the centralizer of
S
in
\mathbf{G}
. Then
T
is a maximal torus. We denote by
N
the normalizer of
T
in
\mathbf{G}
. The
Iwahori–Weyl group
(associated to
S
) is defined as

\tilde{W}=N(\breve{F})/T(\breve{F})\cap\breve{}\mathcal{I}.

For any
w\in\tilde{W}
, we choose a representative
\dot{w}
in
N(\breve{F})
. The action
\sigma
on
\breve{G}
induces a natural action of
\sigma
on
\tilde{W}
, which we still denote by
\sigma
.

We denote by
\ell
the length function on
\tilde{W}
determined by the base alcove
\mathfrak{a}
and denote by
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
the set of simple reflections in
\tilde{W}
. Let
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
be the subgroup of
\tilde{W}
generated by
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
. Then
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
is an affine Weyl group. Let
\Omega\subset\tilde{W}
be the subgroup of length-zero elements in
\tilde{W}
. Then

\tilde{W}=W_{\mathrm{aff}}\rtimes\Omega.

Since the length function is compatible with the
\sigma
-action, the semi-direct product decomposition
\tilde{W}=W_{\mathrm{aff}}\rtimes\Omega
is also stable under the action of
\sigma
.

For any
w\in\tilde{W}
, we choose a representative in
N(\breve{F})
, which we still denote by
w
.

1.2.
The
\sigma
-conjugacy classes of
\breve{G}

The
\sigma
-conjugation action on
\breve{G}
is defined by
g\cdot_{\sigma}g^{\prime}=gg^{\prime}\sigma(g)^{-1}
for
g,g^{\prime}\in\breve{G}
. Let
B(\mathbf{G})
be the set of
\sigma
-conjugacy classes on
\breve{G}
. The classification of the
\sigma
-conjugacy classes is due to Kottwitz
[
Ko85
]
and
[
Ko97
]
. Any
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
is determined by two invariants:

•

The element
\kappa([b])\in\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G})_{\sigma}
;

•

The Newton point
\nu_{b}\in\big((X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0},\mathbb{Q}})^{+}\big)^{\langle\sigma\rangle}
.

Here
-_{\sigma}
denotes the
\sigma
-coinvariants,

(X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0},\mathbb{Q}})^{+}
denotes the intersection of
X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=X_{*}(T)^{\Gamma_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
with the set
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
of dominant elements in
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We denote by
\leqslant
the dominance order on
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
, i.e., for
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
,
\nu\leqslant\nu^{\prime}
if and only if
\nu^{\prime}-\nu
is a non-negative (rational) linear combination of positive roots over
\breve{F}
. The dominance order on
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
extends to a partial order on
B(\mathbf{G})
. Namely, for
[b],[b^{\prime}]\in B(\mathbf{G})
,
[b]\leqslant[b^{\prime}]
if and only if
\kappa([b])=\kappa([b^{\prime}])
and
\nu_{b}\leqslant\nu_{b^{\prime}}
.

1.3.
The straight
\sigma
-conjugacy classes of
\tilde{W}

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The
n
-th
\sigma
-twisted power
of
w
is defined by

w^{\sigma,n}=w\sigma(w)\cdots\sigma^{n-1}(w).

In the case where
\sigma
acts trivally on
\tilde{W}
, we have
w^{\sigma,n}=w^{n}
is the ordinary
n
-th power of
w
.

By definition, an element
w\in\tilde{W}
is called
\sigma
-straight
if for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\ell(w^{\sigma,n})=n\ell(w)
. A
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\tilde{W}
is
straight
if it contains a
\sigma
-straight element. Let
B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}}
be the set of straight
\sigma
-conjugacy classes of
\tilde{W}
. The following result is proved in
[
He14
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The map
w[w]
induces a natural bijection

\Psi:B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}}B(\mathbf{G}).

1.4.
Affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

Following
[
Ra05
]
, we define the affine Deligne-Lusztig variety
X_{w}(b)
. Let
\mathrm{Fl}=\mathbf{G}/\breve{}\mathcal{I}
be the affine flag variety. For any
w\in\tilde{W}
and
b\in\mathbf{G}
, we set

X_{w}(b)=\{g\breve{}\mathcal{I}\in\mathrm{Fl};g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}\}.

In the equal characteristic,
X_{w}(b)
is the set of
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-points of a scheme. In the equal characteristic,
X_{w}(b)
is the set of
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-points of a perfect scheme (see
[
BS17
]
and
[
Zh17
]
).

It is easy to see that
X_{w}(b)
only depends on
w
and the
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
of
b
. For any
w\in\tilde{W}
, we set

B(\mathbf{G})_{w}=\{[b]\in B(G);X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset\}.

Let
[b_{w}]
be the
\sigma
-conjugacy class in the (unique) generic point of
\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}
. Then
[b_{w}]
is the unique maximal element in
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}
with respect to the partial ordering
\leqslant
on
B(\mathbf{G})
(see
[
MV20
, Definition 3.1]
). We choose a representative
b_{w}\in[b_{w}]
and call
X_{w}(b_{w})
the
affine Delinge-Lusztig variety associated with the generic Newton point
of
w
.

1.5.
Demazure product

Now we recall the Demazure product
\ast
on
\tilde{W}
. By definition,
(W,\ast)
is a monoid such that
w\ast w^{\prime}=ww^{\prime}
for any
w,w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
if
\ell(ww^{\prime})=\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime})
and
s\ast w=w
for
s\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
and
w\in\tilde{W}
if
sw<w
. In other words,
\tau\ast w=\tau w
and
s\ast w=\max\{w,sw\}
for
\tau\in\Omega
,
s\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
and
w\in\tilde{W}
.

The geometric interpretation of the Demazure product is as follows. For any
w\in\tilde{W}
,
\overline{\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}}=\cup_{w^{\prime}\leqslant w}\breve{}\mathcal{I}w^{\prime}\breve{}\mathcal{I}
is a closed admissible subset of
\mathbf{G}
in the sense of
[
He16a
, A.2]
. Then for any
w,w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
, we have

\overline{\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}}\,\overline{\breve{}\mathcal{I}w^{\prime}\breve{}\mathcal{I}}=\overline{\breve{}\mathcal{I}(w\ast w^{\prime})\breve{}\mathcal{I}}.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The
n
-th
\sigma
-twisted Demazure power
of
w
is defined by

w^{\ast_{\sigma},n}=w\ast\sigma(w)\ast\cdots\ast\sigma^{n-1}(w).

1.6.
Minimal length elements

For
w,w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
and
s\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
, we write
w\xrightarrow{s}_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
if
w^{\prime}=sw\sigma(s)
and
\ell(w^{\prime})\leqslant\ell(w)
. We write
w\rightarrow_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
if there is a sequence
w=w_{1},w_{2},\dotsc,w_{n}=w^{\prime}
in
\tilde{W}
such that for each
2\leq k\leq n
we have
w_{k-1}\xrightarrow{s_{k}}_{\sigma}w_{k}
for some
s_{k}\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
. We write
w\approx_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
if
w_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}_{\sigma}w
. We write
w\tilde{\approx}_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
if
w\approx_{\sigma}\tau w^{\prime}\sigma(\tau)^{-1}
for some
\tau\in\Omega
. For any
\sigma
-conjugacy class
\mathcal{O}
, we write
\ell(\mathcal{O})=\ell(x)
for any minimal length element
x
of
\mathcal{O}
.

The following result is proved in
[
HN14
, Theorem A]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
. Then there exists a minimal length element
w^{\prime}
in the same
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
w
such that
w_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
.

2.
The generic
\sigma
-conjugacy class

In this section, we study the
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b_{w}]
in more detail.

2.1.
Via the Bruhat order

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
. The generic
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b_{w}]
in
\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}
is first studied by Viehmann in
[
Vi14
]
. The following result is proved in
[
Vi14
, Corollary 5.6]

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
. Then the set

\{[w^{\prime}];w^{\prime}\leqslant w\}\subset B(\mathbf{G})

contains a unique maximal element and this maximal element equals
[b_{w}]
.

A more explicit description of the generic Newton point
\nu_{b_{w}}
is obtained by Milićević
[
Mi16+
, Theorem 3.2]
for split group
\mathbf{G}
and sufficiently large
w
.

2.2.
Via the partial order on
B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}}

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
and
\mathcal{O}\in B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}}
. We write
\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}w
if there exists a minimal length element
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}
such that
w^{\prime}\leqslant w
with respect to the Bruhat order
\leqslant
of
\tilde{W}
. Let
\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\in B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}}
. We write
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\preceq_{\sigma}\mathcal{O}
if
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\preceq_{\sigma}w
for some minimal length element
w
of
\mathcal{O}
. By
[
He16a
, §3.2]
, if
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\preceq_{\sigma}\mathcal{O}
, then
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\preceq_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
for any minimal length element
w^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{O}
. Hence
\preceq_{\sigma}
is a partial order on
B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}}
.

It is proved in
[
He16a
, Theorem B]
that

Theorem 2.2
.

The partial order
\preceq_{\sigma}
on
B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}}
coincides with the partial order
\leqslant
on
B(\mathbf{G})
via the bijection map
\Psi:B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}}B(\mathbf{G})
.

Now we show that

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
. Then the set
\{\mathcal{O}\in B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}};\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}w\}
contains a unique element
\mathcal{O}_{w}
with respect to the partial order
\preceq_{\sigma}
. Moreover,
\Psi(\mathcal{O}_{w})=[b_{w}]
.

Proof.

By
[
He16a
, §2.7]
,
\cup_{w^{\prime}\leqslant w}[w^{\prime}]=\cup_{\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}w}\Psi(\mathcal{O})
. By Proposition
2.1
, the set

\{[w^{\prime}];w^{\prime}\leqslant w\}=\{\Psi(\mathcal{O})\in B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}};\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}w\}

contains a unique maximal element, which is
[b_{w}]
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{w}=\Psi^{-1}([b_{w}])
. By Theorem
2.2
,
\mathcal{O}_{w}
is the unique maximal element of
\{\mathcal{O}\in B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}};\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}w\}
.
∎

2.3.
An algorithm

We provide an algorithm to compute
\mathcal{O}_{w}
. We argue by induction on
\ell(w)
. By
[
He16a
, §2.7]
, if
w
is a minimal length element in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class, then
\mathcal{O}_{w}
is the straight
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\tilde{W}
that corresponds to
[w]\in B(\mathbf{G})
.

If
w
is not a minimal length element in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class, then by Theorem
1.2
, there exists
w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
and a simple reflection
s
such that
w^{\prime}\approx_{\sigma}w
and
sw^{\prime}\sigma(s)<w^{\prime}
. By
[
He16a
, Proposition 2.4]
,
\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}w
if and only if
\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be a straight
\sigma
-conjugacy class with
\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
. Then there exists a minimal length element
w_{1}
of
\mathcal{O}
with
w_{1}\leqslant w^{\prime}
. If
sw_{1}>w_{1}
, then
w_{1}\leqslant\min\{w^{\prime},sw^{\prime}\}=sw^{\prime}
. If
sw_{1}<w_{1}
, then
\ell(sw_{1}\sigma(s))\leqslant\ell(sw_{1})+1=\ell(w_{1})
and
sw_{1}\sigma(s)
is also a minimal length element in
\mathcal{O}
. Moreover, we have
sw_{1}\leqslant sw^{\prime}
and
sw_{1}\sigma(s)\leqslant\max\{sw^{\prime},sw^{\prime}\sigma(s)\}=sw^{\prime}
. In either case,
\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}{sw^{\prime}}
. By inductive hypothesis on
sw^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{O}_{w}=\mathcal{O}_{w^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{sw^{\prime}}
is the unique maximal element in
\{\mathcal{O};\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}w\}=\{\mathcal{O};\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}sw^{\prime}\}
.

2.4.
Via the
0
-Hecke algebras

Let
H_{0}
be the
0
-Hecke algebra
of
\tilde{W}
. It is a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra generated by
\{t_{w};w\in\tilde{W}\}
subject to the relations

•

t_{w}t_{w^{\prime}}=t_{ww^{\prime}}
for any
w,w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
with
\ell(ww^{\prime})=\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime})
.

•

t_{s}^{2}=-t_{s}
for any
s\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
.

The automorphism
\sigma
on
\tilde{W}
induces a natural algebra homomorphism on
H_{0}
, which we still denote by
\sigma
. For any
h,h^{\prime}\in H_{0}
, the
\sigma
-commutator of
h
and
h^{\prime}
is defined by
[h,h^{\prime}]_{\sigma}=hh^{\prime}-h^{\prime}\sigma(h)
. The
\sigma
-commutator

[H_{0},H_{0}]_{\sigma}
of
H_{0}
is by definition the subspace of
H_{0}
spanned by
[h,h^{\prime}]_{\sigma}
for all
h,h^{\prime}\in H_{0}
. The
\sigma
-cocenter of
H_{0}
is defined to be
\bar{H}_{0,\sigma}=H_{0}/[H_{0},H_{0}]_{\sigma}
.

Let
\tilde{W}_{\sigma,\min}
be the set of elements in
\tilde{W}
which are of minimal length in their
\sigma
-conjugacy classes. It is easy to see that if
w\in\tilde{W}_{\sigma,\min}
and
w^{\prime}\tilde{\approx}_{\sigma}w
, then
w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}_{\sigma,\min}
. Let
\tilde{W}_{\sigma,\min}/\tilde{\approx}_{\sigma}
be the set of
\tilde{\approx}_{\sigma}
-equivalence classes in
\tilde{W}_{\sigma,\min}
.

By
[
He15
, Proposition 2.1]
, if
w\tilde{\approx}_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
, then

3.
Passing from non-archimedean local fields to
\mathbb{C}((\epsilon))

3.1.
Dimension formula

The following result follows from
[
He16b
, Theorem 2.23]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
. Then

\dim X_{w}(b_{w})=\ell(w)-\ell(\mathcal{O}_{w}).

Note that the definition of
\mathcal{O}_{w}
only depends on the triple
(\tilde{W},\sigma,w)
and is independent of the reductive
\mathbf{G}
over
F
. This allows us to reduce the calculation of the dimension of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties to the calculation of the length of certain elements in the Iwahori-Weyl group. And the latter problem will be translated to a problem on reductive groups over
\mathbb{C}((\epsilon))
. This is what we will do in this section.

3.2.
Reduction to
W_{\mathrm{aff}}

Let
w=x\tau
for
x\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}
and
\tau\in\Omega
. We write
\theta={\mathrm{Ad}}(\tau)\circ\sigma\in\Aut(W_{\mathrm{aff}})
. Define the map

\iota:W_{\mathrm{aff}}\tilde{W},\quad x^{\prime}x^{\prime}\tau.

For any
x^{\prime}\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}
and

, we have
\ell(\iota(x^{\prime})^{\sigma,n})=\ell((x^{\prime})^{\theta,n})
. Thus
x^{\prime}
is
\theta
-straight if and only if
\iota(x^{\prime})
is
\sigma
-straight. It is also easy to see that if

are in the same

-conjugacy class of

-conjugacy class of

By Proposition
2.3
, there exists a unique maximal element

in

\{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\in B(W_{\mathrm{aff}},\theta)_{\text{str}};\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\preceq_{\theta}x\}.

By definition,
\iota(\mathcal{O}_{x})\preceq_{\sigma}\iota(x)=w
and it is a maximal element in

\{\mathcal{O}\in B(\tilde{W},\sigma)_{\text{str}};\mathcal{O}\preceq_{\sigma}w\}.

Thus
\iota(\mathcal{O}_{x})=\mathcal{O}_{w}
. We have
\ell(w)-\ell(\mathcal{O}_{w})=\ell(x)-\ell(\mathcal{O}_{x})
.

Thus to prove Theorem
0.1
, it remains to show that for any diagram automorphism
\theta
of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
, we have

\ell(\mathcal{O}_{x})=\lim_{nx}\frac{\ell(x^{\theta,n})}{n}.

Abstract
Zhegalkin zebra motives are tilings of the plane by black and white polygons representing certain 𝔽 2 {\mathbb{F}}_{2} -valued functions on ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} . They exhibit a rich geometric structure and provide easy to draw insightful visualizations of many topics in the physics and mathematics literature. The present paper gives some pieces of a general theory and a few explicit examples. Many more examples will be shown in the forthcoming article “Zhegalkin zebra motives: algebra and geometry in black and white”.

Introduction

1.1
Zhegalkin zebra functions

The constructions of motives in algebraic geometry heavily depend on the intersection theory of algebraic cycles and, hence, on the fairly delicate choice of an adequate equivalence relation on algebraic cycles.
Chow motives
, for instance, are based on
rational equivalence
, which is the finest equivalence relation on algebraic cycles yielding a good intersection theory
[
15
]
.

On the contrary, the “motives” in the present paper are built with the usual set theoretical operations from simple subsets of the plane, which we call zebras. In 1927 Zhegalkin pointed out that functions with values in the field
\mathbb{F}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
with the usual addition and multiplication can replace the standard Boolean formalism. The
zebra with frequency

\mathfrak{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\mathfrak{v}\neq\mathsf{0}
, is the function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{Z}^{\mathfrak{v}}(\mathbf{x})=\lfloor 2\mathbf{x}\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\centerdot$}\mathfrak{v}\rfloor\bmod 2\qquad\textrm{for}\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.1)

Here
\centerdot
is the dot product on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and for a real number
r
the integer
\lfloor r\rfloor
is such that
0\leq r-\lfloor r\rfloor<1
. It is sometimes convenient to identify the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. In this paper we only use zebras for which the frequencies are positive integer multiples of the complex numbers

\displaystyle\mathfrak{v}_{1}=\sqrt{3}\varepsilon^{5},\qquad\mathfrak{v}_{2}=\varepsilon^{4},\qquad\mathfrak{v}_{3}=\sqrt{3}\mathbf{i},\qquad\mathfrak{v}_{4}=\varepsilon^{2},\qquad\mathfrak{v}_{5}=\sqrt{3}\varepsilon,\qquad\mathfrak{v}_{6}=1,

(1.2)

with
\mathbf{i}=\sqrt{-1}
and
\varepsilon=e^{\pi\mathbf{i}/6}
; see Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
Left: (
1.2
). Right: Zebra
\mathsf{Z}^{\mathfrak{v}}
. All bands are
\perp\mathfrak{v}
and have width
\frac{1}{2|\mathfrak{v}|}
.

Definition 1.1
.

We denote the zebra with frequency
k\mathfrak{v}_{j}
by
\mathsf{Z}^{jk}
. The elements of the ring of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
generated by the zebras
\mathsf{Z}^{jk}
are called
Zhegalkin zebra functions
. Such a function
\mathcal{F}
gives a tiling of the plane by white (
\mathcal{F}=0
) and black (
\mathcal{F}=1
) polygons. A Zhegalkin zebra function is
convex
(
czzf
) if all polygons in the tiling are bounded and convex. These tilings are the
Zhegalkin zebra motives
in the title.

Goal
.

Understand the deformation theory of these tilings.

Every Zhegalkin zebra function can be written as a
Zhegalkin zebra polynomial
, i.e., a polynomial in the variables
\mathsf{Z}^{jk}
in which all monomials have coefficients
1
and the variables in each monomial have exponent
1
. Section
5.1
describes an efficient way for evaluating a Zhegalkin zebra polynomial and drawing the black-white picture. Convexity can easily be checked by visual inspection. See Figs.
2
,
3
,
6
and
12
for examples.

A Zhegalkin zebra function
\mathcal{F}
has an automorphism group consisting of translations leaving the tiling invariant:

\displaystyle\mathsf{Aut}(\mathcal{F})=\big\{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}+\tau)=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}),\,\forall\,\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\big\}.

This is a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
if
\mathcal{F}
is convex. For every sublattice
\Lambda\subset\mathsf{Aut}(\mathcal{F})
the function
\mathcal{F}
descends to a function on the torus
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Lambda
and gives a tiling of this torus by black and white polygons.

This brings us to the setting of dimer models (a.k.a. brane tilings), quivers with superpotential and discrete differential geometry. There is an extensive literature on these topics written from very different view-points, with very different terminologies, for very different applications. Our view-point will be that the pictures are
realizations
of an underlying combinatorial structure. Our goal is:

Understand the deformations of these realizations
.

1.2
The superpotential and weight functions

The combinatorial structure consists of the set
\mathcal{E}
of edges in the picture, two permutations
\sigma_{0}
,
\sigma_{1}
of
\mathcal{E}
and an injective homomorphism
\mathfrak{p}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathsf{Perm}(\mathcal{E})
into the permutation group of
\mathcal{E}
. The cycles (= orbits) of
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{1}
correspond to the
oriented boundaries
of the white and black polygons, while the cycles of the permutation
\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{1}^{-1}\sigma_{0}
correspond to the
vertices
in the tiling. The orientation of the edges is such that the boundaries of the black (resp. white) polygons are oriented clockwise (resp. counter-clockwise). The homomorphism
\mathfrak{p}
comprises the action of
\mathsf{Aut}(\mathcal{F})
and an isomorphism
\mathsf{Aut}(\mathcal{F})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The permutations
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{1}
commute with this action. Associated with a sublattice
\Lambda
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is then the
finite set

{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}=\mathcal{E}/\Lambda
equipped with the permutations
\sigma_{0}
,
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
and an action of the finite group
\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Lambda
. Since the torus
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Lambda
has genus
1
the numbers of cycles of the permutations satisfy
|\sigma_{0}|+|\sigma_{1}|+|\sigma_{2}|=|{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}|
.

Definition 1.2
.

We call
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}=({\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}},\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})
the
superpotential
of the Zhegalkin zebra function
\mathcal{F}
and the lattice
\Lambda
.

.

[

]

|\sigma_{0}|+|\sigma_{1}|
monomials in

\big\{\widetilde{X}_{e}\big\}_{e\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}}

(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
of
\sigma_{c}
,

, contributes the monomial
(-1)^{c}\widetilde{X}_{e_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{e_{r}}
. In this formulation the cyclic structure is implicit.

The superpotential
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}=({\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}},\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})
provides three abstract graphs

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\!\!\Lambda}=\big(s,t\colon{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}\rightrightarrows\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\star}\big),\qquad\Gamma_{\!\!\Lambda}^{\vee}=\big(b,w\colon{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}\rightrightarrows\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\bullet}\cup\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\circ}\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F},\Lambda}=\textsf{vertices}\colon\ \mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\star}\cup\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\bullet}\cup\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\circ},

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F},\Lambda} =}{}\ \textsf{edges}\colon\ \{(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v})\in\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\circ}\times\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\star}\,|\,\exists\,e\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}\colon\mathbf{w}=w(e),\,\mathbf{v}=t(e)\textrm{ or }s(e)\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F},\Lambda} = {edges}\colon}\ \cup\{(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{v})\in\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\bullet}\times\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\star}\,|\,\exists\,e\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}\colon\mathbf{b}=b(e),\,\mathbf{v}=t(e)\textrm{ or }s(e)\}.

Here
\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\star}
,
\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\bullet}
,
\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\circ}
denote the respective sets of vertices, black and white polygons in the tiling of the torus
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Lambda
and
s
,
t
,
b
,
w
are the respective maps which assign to an edge its source, target, adjacent black and white polygons.

In addition to the superpotential the actual pictures also contain a map
\omega\colon\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{\mathsf{0}\}
which specifies for every edge the corresponding vector in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For a sublattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we want this specification to be
\Lambda
-invariant; i.e., it should be a map
\omega\colon{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{\mathsf{0}\}
. We call such a map
\omega
a
realization
of
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
. It also gives a realization
\Lambda_{\omega}
of the lattice
\Lambda
by translations in the plane which leave the tiling specified by
\omega
invariant. We denote the corresponding torus by

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\omega}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Lambda_{\omega}.

The Zhegalkin zebra function
\mathcal{F}
provides a tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with automorphism group
\mathsf{Aut}(\mathcal{F})
and hence a realization
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
of
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
for every sublattice
\Lambda
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. It identifies
\Lambda
with a sublattice
\Lambda_{\omega_{\mathcal{F}}}
of
\mathsf{Aut}(\mathcal{F})
.

A realization
\omega
of the superpotential gives a tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which modulo
\Lambda_{\omega}
gives an embedding of the quiver (= graph with oriented edges)
\Gamma_{\!\!\Lambda}
into the torus
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}
as the 0-cells and 1-cells in the tiling. One can subsequently embed the graphs
\Gamma_{\!\!\Lambda}^{\vee}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F},\Lambda}
into this torus by means of a function
\theta\colon{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
for which the sum over each cycle of
\sigma_{0}
and each cycle of
\sigma_{1}
is equal to
1
. This function is used to mark in each black/white polygon a point by taking a convex combination of the midpoints of its edges. This will be discussed in detail in Section
5.3
. In
[
10
]
such a function
\theta
is called a (
positive
)
fractional matching
. The existence of a fractional matching for
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
implies
|\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\circ}|=|\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\bullet}|
.

Definition 1.4
.

An
integer weight function for the superpotential
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
is a map
\nu\colon{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for which the sum over each cycle of
\sigma_{0}
and each cycle of
\sigma_{1}
is equal to an integer
\deg\nu
(the
degree of
\nu
). The integer weight functions with the operation
+
form a graded semi-group
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
. An integer weight function of degree
1
is called a
perfect matching
,
dimer covering
or
dimer configuration
. The set of perfect matchings is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}
.

An integer weight function
\nu
is said to be
positive
if
\nu(e)>0
for all
e\in{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}
.

Perfect matchings play a crucial role all over the literature on
dimer models
.
From the permutations
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{1}
one can easily check whether perfect matchings exist and determine them all.
Subsequently one can check whether the sum of all perfect matchings is a positive weight function, which then divided by its degree
|\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}|
yields a positive fractional matching.

Definition 1.5
.

We say that the superpotential
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
is
dimer complete
if the sum of all perfect matchings is a positive weight function.

If
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
is dimer complete, the semi-group
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
is generated by the perfect matchings (see Proposition
2.2
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}.

(1.3)

Rescaling the axes in the picture of the realization
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
by means of the diagonal matrix
\mathsf{diag}\bigl(\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\bigr)
yields a realization of the superpotential
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
with edge vectors
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}(e)\mathsf{diag}\bigl(\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\bigr)
. It follows from (

) that the vertices in the rescaled tiling have coordinates in

. By further rescaling with a positive integer factor one can clear the denominators and obtain a realization
\omega
of
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
with edge vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, say
\omega(e)=(\omega_{1}(e),\omega_{2}(e))
with

Now assume that the superpotential
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
is dimer complete and let

be a positive integer weight function. Then, for a sufficiently large integer

These quadrangles (for

Definition 1.6
.

Assume that the superpotential
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
is dimer complete. A
weight realization
of
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
is a triple of positive weight functions
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})
such that
\omega=(\nu_{1}-\nu_{3},\nu_{2}-\nu_{3})
is a realization of
[\mathcal{F}]_{\Lambda}
in which all black and white polygons and all quadrangles determined by
\big(\omega,\frac{1}{\deg\nu_{3}}\nu_{3}\big)
are strictly convex.

Since it is easy to draw pictures (see Section
5.3
) the conditions in Definition
1.6

\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\colon\ \mathbb{T}_{\omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},\qquad\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\circ})=0,\qquad\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\bullet})=1,\qquad\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\star})=\infty.

This is where
Zhegalkin zebra motives
meet

1

The map

induces unramified coverings of

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\setminus\bigl(\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\circ},\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\bullet},\mathsf{P}_{\!\Lambda}^{\star}\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\longleftarrow}}\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\bigl({\mathsf{P}}^{\circ},{\mathsf{P}}^{\bullet},{\mathsf{P}}^{\star}\bigr).

One can normalize the formulas describing
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
such that the

-diagonals of the quadrangles are mapped to the line
\Re z=\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\displaystyle
in
\mathbb{C}
while the midpoints of these diagonals are mapped to the point

Covert Online Decision Making: From Sequential Hypothesis Testing to Stochastic Bandits
Meng-Che Chang and Matthieu R. Bloch

Abstract
We study the problem of covert online decision making in which an agent attempts to identify a parameter governing a system by probing the system while escaping detection from an adversary. The system is modeled as Markov kernel whose input is controlled by the agent and whose two outputs are observed by the agent and the adversary, respectively. This problem is motivated by applications such as covert sensing or covert radar, in which one tries to perform a sensing task without arousing suspicion by an adversary monitoring the environment for the presence of sensing signals. Specifically, we consider two situations corresponding to different amounts of knowledge of the system. If the kernel is known but governed by an unknown fixed parameter, we formulate the problem as a sequential hypothesis testing problem. If the kernel determining the observations of the agent is unknown but the kernel determining those of the adversary is known, we formulate the problem as a best arm identification problem in a bandit setting. In both situations, we characterize the exponent of the probability of identification error. As expected because of the covertness requirement, the probability of identification error decays exponentially with the square-root of the blocklength.

Introduction

Decision making encompasses many fundamental problems in the area of communication, control, sensing, and machine learning.
A common objective in decision making problems consists in identifying the unknown but fixed parameters governing the distribution of observations. This type of decision making problem falls under the framework of

[

,
2

[

When the number of observations varies, the problem is called

[
5
,
6
,
7

The extension of sequential hypothesis to situations in which the decision maker can causally choose one of several available actions to influence the distribution of its observations, has been analyzed in

or

Another objective of a decision maker can be to identify the action that results in the largest average rewards from its observations without knowledge of output distributions. This problem falls in the framework of

(BAI) in the multi-arm bandit (MAB) literature
[

The problem formulation of BAI can be traced back to
[

, where the authors analyze the bandit problem in the

setting, i.e., the designed algorithm should identify the best arm (or action) with high probability as fast as possible. The problems of controlled sensing and BAI have very similar mathematical models. In controlled sensing, we are given a known set of distribution

\Theta
is the set of parameters and
\mathcal{X}
is the set of actions. For any parameter

\{\nu_{\theta}^{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
can also be viewed as a bandit machine, where
\mathcal{X}
is the set of arms. Because of their similarity, we analyze these two problems in a unified framework.

Concurrently to advances in decision making,
security
has become an increasingly important concern when designing an algorithm or a system because of the growing amount of sensitive data involved. A common security requirement is

[
14
,
15

, by which the objective is to ensure that no information is leaked about the transmitted data through the observations of an adversary. While secrecy is relevant for many communication problems, the concept is not always directly applicable in the context of decision making because the actions carried out do not necessarily carry information by themselves but rather act as probing signals to identify an underlying parameter. As an example,
[
16
]
analyzes secrecy in the context of controlled sensing by defining the performance metric as the ratio of error exponents between the legitimate receiver and an eavesdropper, hence capturing the ability of decision making policies to slow-down the decision making of an adversary. In addition to secrecy,

19

but our focus is on sensing rather than control. There exist conceptual similarities between our approach and recent work investigating covert communication from the perspective of change point detection

The first works studying covertness in decision making can be found in

As an attempt to study the problem in presence of uncertainty regarding the hypotheses tested, we formulate a related best-arm identification problem, which we show admits to a similar characterization. There exist related works that study the performance of decision making algorithm under the existence of adversary.
The authors in
[

Our main contributions are as follows.

•

We formally introduce the problem of covert sequential decision making, which includes sequential hypothesis testing and best-arm identification, and relate the stopping time and covertness constraints to the adversary’s ability to identify the presence of decision making protocol.

•

We characterize both lower bounds and upper bounds for the detection error exponent. Our results show that the upper bounds and the lower bounds match for certain general classes of protocols.

•

Our proofs differ from standard proofs by requiring a careful analysis of the decision making process in the presence of covertness constraints. In particular, we rely extensively on Freedman’s inequality throughout our analysis.

While the results presented here a largely of theoretical nature, they shed light on how to design decision making process that achieve their goals without arousing suspicion. Such results could already find applications in the context of quantum sensing and ranging
[
37
]

III
Problem Formulation

III-A

General Model Description

We consider the following general model of an online decision making problem with an adversary. Let
\{\nu_{\theta}^{x}\}_{x\in[0;K],\theta\in\Theta}
and
\{q_{\theta}^{x}\}_{x\in[0;K],\theta\in\Theta}
be the collections of distributions of observations of the agent (Alice) and the adversary (Willie), respectively, where
\Theta
is the known and finite set of hypotheses and
\mathcal{X}\triangleq[0;K]
is the set of actions.
We denote by
X_{t}\in\mathcal{X}
the action chosen by the agent at each time
t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. The action
X_{t}
determines the index of the distributions from which the observations of Alice and Willie are generated. Specifically, for any
t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, the observation
Y_{t}
of Alice and the observation
Z_{t}
of Willie are generated from the distribution
\nu^{X_{t}}_{\theta}
and
q^{X_{t}}_{\theta}
, respectively, when the hypothesis is
\theta
. Among the set of actions
\mathcal{X}
, we denote by
0
the null action, which corresponds to the situation in which no effective action is chosen. We assume that the distribution
\nu_{\theta}^{0}
has a zero mean for all
\theta\in\Theta
and
\mathbb{D}(\nu_{\theta}^{0}\|\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{0})=0
for all
\theta\neq\theta^{\prime}
, implying that taking the null action is useless in distinguishing different hypotheses. The fact that the null action has zero mean is also known by the agent before taking any actions. Moreover, for all
x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}
, we also assume that
0<\mathbb{D}(\nu_{\theta}^{x}\|\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x})<\infty
for all
\theta\neq\theta^{\prime}
so that different hypotheses cannot be distinguished perfectly by any single action
x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}
.
The action
X_{t}
is chosen according to some causal distribution
P_{X_{t}|X^{t-1},Y^{t-1}}
, which depends on past actions and observations. Note that if we fix the hypothesis
\theta
, the set of distributions
\mathcal{V}_{\theta}=\{\nu_{\theta}^{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}=\{q_{\theta}^{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
can be viewed as stochastic bandit machines in which the set of actions
\mathcal{X}
is the set of arms in the context of multi-arm bandits. For any
\theta\in\Theta
, we also denote by
x^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{\theta})
the arm with which the distribution
\nu_{\theta}^{x^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{\theta})}
has the largest mean among
\{\nu_{\theta}^{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
.

Different problem formulations are possible when varying the assumptions in our general model. We list the general descriptions of the problems that we would like to analyze.

(P1)

When the agent knows
\{\mathcal{V}_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
and
\{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
but not the hypothesis
\theta
, we formulate a covert active hypothesis testing problem, in which the objective is to identify
\theta

(P2)

When the agent knows
\{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
and

\mathcal{V}_{\theta}
subjecting to a covertness constraint. In this problem, we make the assumption that
\nu_{\theta}^{x}
and
q_{\theta}^{x}
are Gaussian distributions with variance
1
for all
\theta\in\Theta
and

. We denote by

Similarly, for any
\theta\in\Theta
,
\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta
and
t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we define the ordinary log likelihood ratio as

\displaystyle A_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)=\log\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\pi}(X^{t},Y^{t})}{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta^{\prime}},\pi}(X^{t},Y^{t})}.

(51)

We then specify the policy
\pi
in the achievability proof as follows.

Stopping rule
\phi

The policy stops at
t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
if there exists some
\theta\in\Theta
such that the log liklihood ratio
A_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)
is greater than a threshold
\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}
for all
\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta
. The stopping time is therefore

\displaystyle\tau=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}:\exists\>\theta\in\Theta\textnormal{ s.t. }A_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)\geqslant\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}\>\forall\>\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta\right\},

(52)

where for all
\theta\in\Theta
and
\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta
, the threshold
\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}=n\alpha_{\theta}\left(\sum_{x\neq 0}\bar{P}_{X;\theta}(x)\mathbb{D}(\nu_{\theta}^{x}\|\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x})-\zeta\right),

(53)

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{X;\theta}=\mathop{\text{argmax}}_{\bar{P}_{X}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}}\min_{\theta^{\prime\prime}\neq\theta}\frac{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}\bar{P}_{X}(x)\mathbb{D}(\nu_{\theta}^{x}\|\nu_{\theta^{\prime\prime}}^{x})}{\sqrt{\chi_{2}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}\bar{P}_{X}(x)q_{\theta}^{x}\middle\|q_{\theta}^{0}\right)}}

(54)

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{\theta}=\frac{\sqrt{2\eta}}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\chi_{2}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}\bar{P}_{X;\theta}(x)q_{\theta}^{x}\middle\|q_{\theta}^{0}\right)}},

(55)

and
\zeta>0
is some small value.

Control policy
\varphi

tFor any
t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we define
\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(t)
as the maximum likelihood estimate of the hypothesis
\theta
, i.e.,
\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(t)=\mathop{\text{argmax}}_{\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{X_{i}}(Y_{i})
. Moreover, for all
\theta\in\Theta
, we define the distribution
P_{X;\theta}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
as

\displaystyle P_{X;\theta}(x)=\begin{cases}1-\alpha_{\theta}\quad\textnormal{if }x=0\\
\alpha_{\theta}\bar{P}_{X;\theta}(x)\quad\textnormal{if }x\neq 0\end{cases}

(56)

for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, where
\bar{P}_{X;\theta}
and
\alpha_{\theta}
are defined in (
54
) and (
55
), respectively.
Then, the control policy
P_{X_{t}|X^{t-1},Y^{t-1}}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{X_{t}|Y^{t-1},X^{t-1}}(x)=\begin{cases}P_{X;\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(t-1)}(x)\quad\textnormal{if }S_{t-1}\neq\textnormal{stop}\\
\mathbf{1}(x=0)\quad\textnormal{if }S_{t-1}=\textnormal{stop}\end{cases}

(57)

for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Final decision rule
\psi

When the decision making process stops, the estimated hypothesis is given by
\psi(X^{\tau},Y^{\tau})=\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(\tau)
.

For convenience of analysis,
for any policy
\pi
satisfying (
35
),
we also define a corresponding dummy policy
\tilde{\pi}
that never stops and has the control policy

\displaystyle P_{X_{t}|Y^{t-1},X^{t-1}}(x)=P_{X;\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(t-1)}(x)

(58)

for all
x
and
t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

VI-B

Analysis of Covert Policy

Stopping time
\tau

For any
\theta\in\Theta
,
\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta
and
i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we define

\displaystyle L_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(i)=\log\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\pi}(Y_{i}|X_{i})}{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta^{\prime}},\pi}(Y_{i}|X_{i})}=\log\frac{\nu_{\theta}^{X_{i}}(Y_{i})}{\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{X_{i}}(Y_{i})}

(59)

so that

\displaystyle A_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}L_{\theta,\theta}(i)

for any
t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.
We also define the random variable
N_{\theta}
as the earliest time such that the ML estimation about the hypothesis is correct for all
t>N_{\theta}
when the true hypothesis is
\theta\in\Theta
, i.e.,

\displaystyle N_{\theta}=\sup\{t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}:\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(t)\neq\theta\}.

(60)

The standard analysis of the stopping time
\tau
in the sequential hypothesis testing given in the literature
[
8
,
30
]
relies on the fact that the estimate of the true hypothesis is incorrect for only finitely many time steps.
However, this is not true in our setting because effective actions are selected with a probability shrinking with
n
, i.e.,
\alpha_{\theta}=\Theta_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{-1/2})
for all
\theta\in\Theta
.
Nevertheless, we can still show that
N_{\theta}
grows much slower than
n
through the following lemma, proved in Appendix
-A
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\pi
be some policy that satisfies (
35
), and
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}P_{X;\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\tilde{\Theta}_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{-\alpha})
for all
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left(N_{\theta}\geqslant n^{\alpha+\epsilon}\right)=O_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{-\beta})

(61)

for arbitrarily large
\beta>0
,
\theta\in\Theta
and
\epsilon>0
, where
\tilde{\pi}
is the corresponding dummy policy of
\pi
with control policy defined in (
58
).

By inspecting (
55
) and (
57
), the policy constructed in this section satisfies the assumptions in Lemma
9
with
\alpha=1/2
.
The proof of Lemma
9
requires Lemma
8
, proved by Freedman in
[
42
]
, which is a Bernstein-style concentration bound on martingales.
Lemma
9
states that the ML estimate
\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(t)
of the hypothesis is correct for any
t\geqslant n^{1/2+\epsilon}
with high probability under the dummy policy
\tilde{\pi}
of
\pi
, where
\pi
is the policy defined in Section
VI-A
.

We are now ready to upper bound the probability that
\tau
is greater than
n
as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\pi}(\tau>n)

(62)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left(\forall t\in[1;n]\textnormal{ and }\>\forall\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta\>\exists\>\theta^{\prime\prime}\neq\theta^{\prime}\textnormal{ s.t }A_{\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime}}(t)<\Gamma_{\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime}}\right)

(63)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left(\>A_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(n)<\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}\right)

(64)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left(\>N_{\theta}>n^{1/2+\epsilon}\right)+\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}}\mathbf{1}(X_{i}\neq 0)>n^{2\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left(\>A_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(n)<\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}},N_{\theta}\leqslant n^{1/2+\epsilon},\sum_{i=1}^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}}\mathbf{1}(X_{i}\neq 0)\leqslant n^{2\epsilon}\right),

(65)

where in (
63
) we use the definition of the stopping time and the fact that the control policies of
\pi
and
\tilde{\pi}
are the same before the policy stops, (
64
) comes from the fact that

\displaystyle\left\{\forall t\in[1;n]\textnormal{ and }\>\forall\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta\>\exists\>\theta^{\prime\prime}\neq\theta^{\prime}\textnormal{ s.t }A_{\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime}}(t)<\Gamma_{\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime}}\right\}\subset\left\{\exists\>\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta\textnormal{ s.t }A_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(n)<\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}\right\}

and the union bound, and (
65
) comes from the law of total probability.
The first term on the right hand side of (
65
) goes to zero when
n\rightarrow\infty
by Lemma
9
, and the second term on the right hand side of (
65
) can be upper bounded by
e^{-\Omega_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{2\epsilon})}
by applying Freedman’s inequality for martingales as follows. By defining the martingale sequence
\{V_{t}\}_{t=1}^{\infty}
with

\displaystyle V_{t}\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathbf{1}(X_{i}\neq 0)-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}P_{X_{i}|Y^{i-1},X^{i-1}}(x)

(66)

and
U_{t}=V_{t}-V_{t-1}
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}}\mathbf{1}(X_{i}\neq 0)>n^{2\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}}\mathbf{1}(X_{i}\neq 0)-\sum_{i=1}^{n^{1/2}+\epsilon}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}P_{X_{i}|Y^{i-1},X^{i-1}}(x)>\Omega_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{2\epsilon})\right)

(67)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\exp\left(-\frac{\Omega_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{4\epsilon})}{O_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{\epsilon})+O_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{2\epsilon})}\right),

(68)

where we use the fact that

\displaystyle n^{2\epsilon}-\sum_{i=1}^{n^{1/2}+\epsilon}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}P_{X_{i}|Y^{i-1},X^{i-1}}(x)=\Omega_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{2\epsilon}),

(69)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}[U_{i}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}[\mathbf{1}(X_{i}\neq 0)|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\leqslant O_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{\epsilon}).

(70)

To analyze the third term on the right hand side of (
65
), we first define the following events for any
\theta\in\Theta
and
\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{(x^{n},y^{n})\in\mathcal{X}^{n}\times\mathcal{Y}^{n}:A_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(n)<\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}},N_{\theta}\leqslant n^{1/2+\epsilon},\sum_{i=1}^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}}\mathbf{1}(X_{i}\neq 0)\leqslant n^{2\epsilon}\right\}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{(x^{n}_{n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1},y^{n}_{n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}):\sum_{t=n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}^{n}L_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)<\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}-n^{2\epsilon}\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\},y\in\mathcal{Y}}\log\frac{{\nu}^{x}_{\theta}(y)}{{\nu}^{x}_{\theta^{\prime}}(y)}\right\}.

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left(\>A_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(n)<\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}},N_{\theta}\leqslant n^{1/2+\epsilon},\sum_{i=1}^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}}\mathbf{1}(X_{i}\neq 0)\leqslant n^{2\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in\mathcal{D}_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}(x^{n},y^{n})

(71)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in\mathcal{D}_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}(x^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}},y^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}})\prod_{t=n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}^{n}P_{X;\theta}(x_{t})\nu_{\theta}^{x_{t}}(y_{t})

(72)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\left(x^{n}_{n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1},y^{n}_{n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}\right)\in\mathcal{D}_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}^{\prime}}\prod_{t=n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}^{n}P_{X;\theta}(x_{t})\nu_{\theta}^{x_{t}}(y_{t})

(73)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{(P_{X;\theta}\circ\nu_{\theta}^{x})^{\otimes(n-n^{1/2+\epsilon})}}\left(\sum_{t=n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}^{n}L_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)<\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}-n^{2\epsilon}\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\},y\in\mathcal{Y}}\log\frac{\nu_{\theta}^{x}(y)}{\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x}(y)}\right),

(74)

where (
71
) follows from the definition of
\mathcal{D}_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}
, (
72
) follows from the definition of the control policy and the fact that
N_{\theta}\leqslant n^{1/2+\epsilon}
when the event
\mathcal{D}_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}
holds, (
73
) follows by lower bounding
L_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)
by
\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\},y\in\mathcal{Y}}\log\frac{\nu^{x}_{\theta}(y)}{\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x}(y)}
for all
t\leqslant n^{1/2+\epsilon}
satisfying
X_{t}\neq 0
and marginalizing over the sequences
(x^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}},y^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}})\in\mathcal{X}^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}}\times\mathcal{Y}^{n^{1/2+\epsilon}}
, and in (
74
) we use the notation

\mathbb{P}_{(P_{X;\theta}\circ\nu_{\theta}^{x})^{\otimes(n-n^{1/2+\epsilon})}}
to emphasize that the tuples
\{(X_{t},Y_{t})\}_{t=n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}^{n}
in (
74
) are generated i.i.d from the distribution
P_{X;\theta}\circ\nu_{\theta}^{x}
, where
P_{X;\theta}\circ\nu_{\theta}^{x}(x,y)\triangleq P_{X;\theta}(x)\nu_{\theta}^{x}(y)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
y\in\mathcal{Y}
. Therefore,
\{L_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)\}_{t=n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}^{n}
are generated independently in (
74
).
The event in the right hand side of (
74
) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{t=n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}^{n}L_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)-(n-n^{1/2+\epsilon})\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{X;\theta}(x)\mathbb{D}(\nu_{\theta}^{x}\|\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x})\right)

\displaystyle<\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}-(n-n^{1/2+\epsilon})\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{X;\theta}(x)\mathbb{D}(\nu_{\theta}^{x}\|\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x})\right)-n^{2\epsilon}\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\},y\in\mathcal{Y}}\log\frac{\nu_{\theta}^{x}(y)}{\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x}(y)},

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}-(n-n^{1/2+\epsilon})\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{X;\theta}(x)\mathbb{D}(\nu_{\theta}^{x}\|\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x})\right)-n^{2\epsilon}\min_{x\in\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}}\log\frac{\nu_{\theta}^{x}(y)}{\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x}(y)}

\displaystyle=-n\alpha_{\theta}\zeta+n^{1/2+\epsilon}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{X;\theta}(x)\mathbb{D}(\nu_{\theta}^{x}\|p_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x})\right)-n^{2\epsilon}\min_{x\in\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}}\log\frac{\nu_{\theta}^{x}(y)}{\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x}(y)}

\displaystyle\leqslant-C_{1}\zeta n^{1/2}

for some
C_{1}>0
for all
n
sufficiently large by plugging in the definition of
\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}
and choosing
\epsilon
arbitrarily small so that the term
-n\alpha_{\theta}\zeta
in the definition of
\Gamma_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}
dominates in the above expression.
Then, we apply Bernstein’s inequality to upper bound the right hand side of (
74
) by

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{C_{1}^{2}\zeta^{2}n}{\sum_{t=n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{P_{X;\theta}\circ\nu_{\theta}^{x}}[L_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)^{2}]+\frac{1}{3}MC_{1}\zeta n^{1/2}}\right),

(75)

where
0<M<\infty
is some constant such that
|L_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)|<M
almost surely for all
t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and for all

, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{X;\theta}\circ\nu_{\theta}^{x}}[L_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}(t)^{2}]=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}\alpha_{\theta}\bar{P}_{X;\theta}(x)\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\nu_{\theta}^{x}(y)\left(\log\frac{\nu_{\theta}^{x}(y)}{\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{x}(y)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant O_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{-1/2}),

(76)

where we use the fact that
\nu_{\theta}^{0}(y)\left(\log\frac{\nu_{\theta}^{0}(y)}{\nu_{\theta^{\prime}}^{0}(y)}\right)^{2}
is
0
for all

. Therefore, (

) becomes
e^{-\Omega_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{1/2})}
, which decrease to zero when
n\rightarrow 0
. Finally, combining (
65
) and (
75
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\pi}(\tau>n)=0

(77)

for any
\theta\in\Theta
and
\zeta>0
when the stopping time is defined in (
52
) with the threshold given by (
53
).

Relative Entropy

We denote the output distribution of Willie under the dummy policy
\tilde{\pi}
of
\pi
by
\tilde{P}_{Z^{n};\theta}(z^{n})\triangleq\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}(z^{n})
for any
z^{n}\in\mathcal{Z}^{n}
, and
\tilde{P}_{Z^{n};\theta}(z^{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{P}_{Z^{i}|Z^{i-1};\theta}(z_{i})
is the factorization
\tilde{P}_{Z^{n};\theta}
by the product of conditional probabilities under the dummy policy
\tilde{\pi}
. Since
\tilde{P}_{Z^{n};\theta}
is the output distribution of
Z^{n}
when the policy does not stop, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(P_{Z^{n};\theta}\|(q_{\theta}^{0})^{\otimes n})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\tilde{P}_{Z^{n};\theta}\|(q_{\theta}^{0})^{\otimes n}).

(78)

Given any
\theta\in\Theta

\mathbb{D}(\tilde{P}_{Z^{n};\theta}\|(q_{\theta}^{0})^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\tilde{P}_{Z^{n};\theta}\|(q_{\theta}^{0})^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{Z^{i-1};\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left[\mathbb{D}(\tilde{P}_{Z_{i}|Z^{i-1};\theta}\|q_{\theta}^{0})\right]

(79)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{Z^{i-1};\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1)|Z^{i-1};\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left[\mathbb{D}\left(\tilde{P}_{Z_{i}|Z^{i-1},\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1);\theta}\middle\|q_{\theta}^{0}\right)\right]

(80)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1);\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\mathbb{D}\left(\tilde{P}_{Z_{i}|\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1);\theta}\middle\|q_{\theta}^{0}\right)

(81)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}(\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1)\neq\theta)\max_{\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}(\tilde{P}_{Z_{i}|\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1)=\theta^{\prime};\theta}\|q_{\theta}^{0})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}(\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1)=\theta)\mathbb{D}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}{P}_{X;\theta}(x)q^{x}_{\theta}\middle\|q_{\theta}^{0}\right)

(82)

\displaystyle\leqslant O_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{-1})\left(n^{1/2+\epsilon}+\sum_{i=n^{1/2+\epsilon}+1}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}(N_{\theta}\geqslant i)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+n\mathbb{D}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}{P}_{X;\theta}(x)q^{x}_{\theta}\middle\|q_{\theta}^{0}\right)

(83)

\displaystyle\leqslant o_{n\rightarrow\infty}(1)+n\left(\frac{(\alpha_{\theta})^{2}}{2}\chi_{2}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}\bar{P}_{X;\theta}(x)q_{\theta}^{x}\middle\|q_{\theta}^{0}\right)+o_{n\rightarrow\infty}((\alpha_{\theta})^{2})\right)

(84)

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta+o_{n\rightarrow\infty}(1),

(85)

where (
80
) follows from the convexity of the relative entropy, Jensen’s inequality and the fact that

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{Z_{i}|Z^{i-1};\theta}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}(z|Z^{i-1})

(86)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}(\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1)=\theta^{\prime}|Z^{i-1})\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}(z|\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1)=\theta^{\prime},Z^{i-1})

(87)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1)|Z^{i-1};\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}\left[\tilde{P}_{Z_{i}|\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1),Z^{i-1};\theta}(z)\right]

(88)

for any
z\in\mathcal{Z}
,
(
81
) follows because
\tilde{P}_{Z_{i}|Z^{i-1},\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}}(i-1);\theta}=\tilde{P}_{Z_{i}|\hat{\theta}_{\textnormal{ML}(i-1)};\theta}
by our construction of the policy and the fact that
Z_{i}
is independent of
Z^{i-1}
conditioned on
X_{i}
, (

) follows by upper bounding
\max_{\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}(\tilde{P}_{Z_{i}|\hat{\theta}(i-1)=\theta^{\prime};\theta}\|q_{\theta}^{0})
by
O_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{-1})
because

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{Z_{i}|\hat{\theta}(i-1)=\theta^{\prime};\theta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{X;\theta^{\prime}}(x)q_{\theta}^{x}

(89)

for any
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
,

\sum_{x\neq 0}P_{X;\theta^{\prime}}(x)=O_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{-1/2})
for any
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
, and we apply the result from
[
18
, Lemma 1]
, and in (

) we use the result from Lemma

so that
\sum_{i=n^{1/2+\epsilon}}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\tilde{\pi}}(N_{\theta}\geqslant i)\leqslant o_{n\rightarrow\infty}(1)

.
Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{D}(P_{Z^{n};\theta}\|(q_{\theta}^{0})^{\otimes n})\leqslant\eta

(90)

for all

Estimation error

For any
\theta\in\Theta
, the decision error probability can be upper bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\pi}(\psi(X^{\tau},Y^{\tau})\neq\theta)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\pi}\left(\psi(X^{\tau},Y^{\tau})=\theta^{\prime}\right)

(91)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\pi}\left[\mathbf{1}(A_{\theta^{\prime},\theta}(\tau)\geqslant\Gamma_{\theta^{\prime},\theta})\right]

(92)

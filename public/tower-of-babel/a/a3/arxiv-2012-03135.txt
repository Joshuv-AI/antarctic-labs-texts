An infinite family of higher-order difference operators that commute with Ruijsenaars operators of type A A
Masatoshi NOUMI and Ayako SANO

Abstract




Abstract:
We introduce a new infinite family of higher-order difference
operators that commute with the elliptic Ruijsenaars difference
operators of type
A
.
These operators are related with Ruijsenaars’ operators through
a formula of Wronski type.

Keywords
: Ruijsenaars operators, Wronski formula

2010 Mathematical Subject Classification
: 81R12; 33D67

1
Introduction

In his study of relativistic quantum integrable systems
[
10
]
,
Ruijsenaars introduced a commuting family of linear
difference operators in
n
variables, denoted below by
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
,
involving sigma functions as coefficients.
In this paper
we construct an explicit infinite family of difference operators

H_{0},H_{1},H_{2},\ldots

in the commutative algebra
\mathbb{C}[D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}]

which are related with
D_{r}

(
r=1,\ldots,n
) through a formula of Wronski type.
This construction is applicable also to difference operators with
trigonometric and rational coefficients.
In order to deal with the elliptic, trigonometric and rational cases simultaneously,
as in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]

we formulate our results in terms of an entire function
[z]
satisfying
the three term relation (
1.1
) below.

We fix a nonzero entire function
[z]
in one complex variable
z\in\mathbb{C}

satisfying the three term relation of Hirota type

[z\pm\alpha][\beta\pm\gamma]+[z\pm\beta][\gamma\pm\alpha]+[z\pm\gamma][\alpha\pm\beta]=0

(1.1)

for any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, where

[\alpha\pm\beta]=[\alpha+\beta][\alpha-\beta]
.
We remark
that a generic solution of this functional equation is given by

[z]=\mbox{const.}\,e^{cz^{2}}\sigma(z;\Omega)\qquad(c\in\mathbb{C}),

(1.2)

where
\sigma(z;\Omega)
denotes the Weierstrass sigma function
associated with a period lattice
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}\omega_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\omega_{2}
.
It is also satisfied by the functions

[z]=\mbox{const.}\,e^{cz^{2}}\sin(\pi z/\omega),\quad[z]=\mbox{const.}\,e^{cz^{2}}\,z,

(1.3)

which are trigonometric and rational degenerations of the generic solution above.
It is known that any solution of (
1.1
) belongs to one of these
three categories.
Throughout this paper,
we denote by
D_{r}=D_{r}^{x}
(
r=1,\ldots,n
)
the Ruijsenaars operators in
n
variables
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

with parameters
(\delta,\kappa)
, associated with
[z]
.
They are defined by

D_{r}^{x}=\sum_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\};\,|I|=r}\ {\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;\ j\notin I}}\dfrac{[x_{i}-x_{j}+\kappa]}{[x_{i}-x_{j}]}\,{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}}\,T_{x_{i}}^{\delta},

(1.4)

where
I
runs over all subsets of indices of cardinality
r
,
and
T_{x_{i}}^{\delta}
stands for the
\delta
-shift operator

x_{i}\to x_{i}+\delta
in
x_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
.
It is proved by Ruijsenaars
[
10
]
that these operators
D_{r}

commute with each other,
namely,

D_{r}D_{s}=D_{s}D_{r}\qquad\mbox{for all $r,s=1,\ldots,n$,}

(1.5)

on the basis of a certain functional identity for the sigma function.
We denote by

\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{C}[D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}]

the commutative algebra generated by
D_{r}
(
r=1,\ldots,n
),
and refer to it as the commutative algebra of

Ruijsenaars operators
(of type
A_{n-1}
).
We also define
D_{0}=1
, and
D_{r}=0
for
r>n
.
Note also that these operators

D_{r}
(
r=1,\ldots,n
) in the trigonometric case
are the Macdonald
q
-difference operators expressed additively.

We define an infinite family of difference operators

H_{l}=H_{l}^{x}

(l=0,1,2\ldots)
by

H_{l}^{x}={\displaystyle\sum_{\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n}=l}}\ {\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}}\!\dfrac{[x_{i}\!-\!x_{j}\!+\!(\mu_{i}\!-\!\mu_{j})\delta]}{[x_{i}\!-\!x_{j}]}\,{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}{\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{\mu_{i}\!-\!1}}\dfrac{[x_{i}\!-\!x_{j}\!+\!\kappa\!+\!k\delta]}{[x_{i}\!-\!x_{j}\!+\!\delta\!+\!k\delta]}\,{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}}\,T_{x_{i}}^{\mu_{i}\delta}.

(1.6)

These operators are expressed briefly as

H_{l}^{x}={\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n};\,|\mu|=l}}\dfrac{\Delta(x+\mu\delta)}{\Delta(x)}\,{\displaystyle\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}}\dfrac{[x_{i}-x_{j}+\kappa]_{\mu_{i}}}{[x_{i}-x_{j}+\delta]_{\mu_{i}}}\,T_{x}^{\mu\delta},

(1.7)

in terms of the difference product

\Delta(x)={\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}}[x_{i}-x_{j}]
,
and the
\delta
-shifted factorials

[z]_{k}=[z][z+\delta]\cdots[z+(k-1)\delta]\quad(k=0,1,2,\ldots)

(1.8)

for
[z]
.
Also, for each multi-index

\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
,
we define
|\mu|=\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n}
and

T_{x}^{\mu\delta}=T_{x_{1}}^{\mu_{1}\delta}\cdots T_{x_{n}}^{\mu_{n}\delta}
.

Theorem 1.1

The linear difference operators
H_{l}=H_{l}^{x}

(l=0,1,2,\ldots)

defined as above belong to the commutative algebra

\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{C}[D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}]

of Ruijsenaars operators. In particular, one has

D_{r}H_{s}=H_{s}D_{r},\qquad H_{r}H_{s}=H_{s}H_{r}\qquad(r,s=0,1,2,\ldots).

(1.9)

We first remark that the family of difference operators
H_{l}
(
l=0,1,2,\ldots
)
originates from the kernel identities for Ruijsenaars operators
(
[
5
]
) and the
duality transformation formulas for multiple elliptic hypergeometric
series
(
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
).
Let
G(z)
be a nonzero meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}

such that

G(z+\delta)=[z]\,G(z)
,
and define the kernel function
\Phi(x;y)
of Cauchy type by

\displaystyle\Phi(x;y)={\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}}{\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n}}\dfrac{G(x_{i}+y_{k})}{G(x_{i}+y_{k}+\kappa)}

(1.10)

for
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
.
Then from
[
4
]
, Theorem 1.3 we have the kernel identity

\displaystyle D_{r}^{x}\,\Phi(x;y)=D_{r}^{y}\,\Phi(x;y)\qquad(r=0,1,\ldots,n)

(1.11)

for the Ruijsenaars operators
D_{r}
(
r=0,1,\ldots,r
).
On the other hand,
the duality transformation formula
for multiple elliptic hypergeometric series
implies

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{m};\,|\mu|=r}}\dfrac{\Delta(x+\mu\delta)}{\Delta(x)}{\displaystyle\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}}\dfrac{[x_{i}-x_{j}+\kappa]_{\mu_{i}}}{[x_{i}-x_{j}+\delta]_{\mu_{i}}}{\displaystyle\prod_{i,k=1}^{n}}\dfrac{[x_{i}+y_{k}]_{\mu_{i}}}{[x_{i}+y_{k}+\kappa]_{\mu_{i}}}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{m};\,|\nu|=r}}\dfrac{\Delta(y+\nu\delta)}{\Delta(y)}{\displaystyle\prod_{k,l=1}^{n}}\dfrac{[y_{k}-y_{l}+\kappa]_{\nu_{k}}}{[y_{k}-y_{l}+\delta]_{\nu_{k}}}{\displaystyle\prod_{k,i=1}^{n}}\dfrac{[y_{k}+x_{i}]_{\nu_{k}}}{[y_{k}+x_{i}+\kappa]_{\nu_{k}}}\quad(r=0,1,2,\ldots),

(1.12)

as the special case where
m=n
and
a_{i}=b_{i}=\kappa
(
i=1,\ldots,n
)
in the notation of
[
4
]
, Theorem 2.2.
This means that

\displaystyle H_{r}^{x}\,\Phi(x;y)=H_{r}^{y}\,\Phi(x;y)\quad(r=0,1,2,\ldots).

(1.13)

Namely, the kernel function for the Ruijsenaars
operators
D_{r}
(
r=0,1,\ldots,n
) simultaneously
intertwines the operators
H_{r}
(
r=0,1,2\ldots
).
In view of this fact, it would be reasonable to expect
that the operators
H_{r}
should already belong
to the commutative algebra
\mathbb{C}[D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}]

of Ruijsenaars operators.
Theorem
1.1
mentioned above ensures that
it is actually the case.

In this paper we prove
Theorem
1.1
as a consequence of the following
recurrence formula
of Wronski type
for
H_{l}

(
l=0,1,2,\ldots
).

Theorem 1.2

The difference operators
H_{l}

(l=0,1,2,\ldots)
satisfy the following
recurrence formula in relation to
D_{r}

(r=1,\ldots,n)

:

{\displaystyle\sum_{r+s=l}}\,(-1)^{r}[r\kappa+s\delta]\,D_{r}\,H_{s}=0\qquad(l=1,2,\ldots).

(1.14)

Recall that the elementary symmetric functions

e_{r}=e_{r}(\xi)={\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq m}}\,\xi_{i_{1}}\cdots\xi_{i_{r}}\qquad(r=0,1,2,\ldots)

(1.15)

in
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
, and
the complete homogeneous symmetric functions

h_{l}=h_{l}(\xi)={\displaystyle\sum_{\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n}=l}}\xi_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots\xi_{n}^{\mu_{n}}\qquad(l=0,1,2,\ldots)

(1.16)

are related to each other through the
Wronski formula

{\displaystyle\sum_{r+s=l}}(-1)^{r}e_{r}\,h_{s}=0\qquad(l=1,2,\ldots).

(1.17)

(See
[
6
]
.)
Theorem
1.2
can be thought of as an operator version
of this Wronski formula for symmetric functions.
A proof of Theorem
1.2
will be given
in Section 2 by using a functional identity for
[z]

(Lemma
2.3
).

From the recurrence formulas

\begin{array}[]{ll}[\delta]H_{1}-[\kappa]D_{1}=0,\\[4.0pt]
[2\delta]H_{2}-[\kappa+\delta]D_{1}H_{1}+[2\kappa]D_{2}=0,\\[4.0pt]
[3\delta]H_{3}-[\kappa+2\delta]D_{1}H_{2}+[2\kappa+\delta]D_{2}H_{1}+[3\kappa]D_{3}=0,\\[4.0pt]
\qquad\ldots,\end{array}

(1.18)

we see inductively that
H_{l}
belongs to the commutative algebra

\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{C}[D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}]

of Ruijsenaars operators for all
l=0,1,2,\ldots
.
In fact we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}H_{1}=\dfrac{[\kappa]}{[\delta]}D_{1},\\[10.0pt]
H_{2}=\dfrac{[\kappa][\kappa+\delta]}{[\delta][2\delta]}D_{1}^{2}-\dfrac{[2\kappa]}{[2\delta]}D_{2},\\[10.0pt]
H_{3}=\dfrac{[\kappa][\kappa+\delta][\kappa+2\delta]}{[\delta][2\delta][3\delta]}D_{1}^{3}-\dfrac{[2\kappa][\kappa+\delta]}{[2\delta][3\delta]}D_{2}D_{1}-\dfrac{[\kappa][2\kappa+\delta]}{[\delta][3\delta]}D_{1}D_{2}+\dfrac{[3\kappa]}{[3\delta]}D_{3},\\[10.0pt]
\qquad\ldots.\end{array}

(1.19)

The relationship between the two families of difference operators

D_{r}
(
r=01,2,\ldots
) and
H_{l}
(
l=0,1,2,\ldots
) is
described as follows.

Proposition 1.3

For each
l=0,1,2,\ldots
,
the difference operator
H_{l}
is expressed in terms
of
D_{r}

(r=0,1,\ldots)
by the determinant formula

H_{l}=\det\left(\dfrac{[(i\!-\!j\!+\!1)\kappa\!+\!(j\!-\!1)\delta]}{[i\delta]}D_{i-j+1}\right)_{i,j=1}^{l}\qquad(l=0,1,2,\ldots).

(1.20)

Conversely,

D_{l}=\det\left(\dfrac{[(i\!-\!j\!+\!1)\delta\!+\!(j\!-\!1)\kappa]}{[i\kappa]}H_{i-j+1}\right)_{i,j=1}^{l}\qquad(l=0,1,2,\ldots).

(1.21)

Proposition 1.4

For each
l=1,2,\ldots
,
the difference operator
H_{l}
is expressed explicitly as

H_{l}={\displaystyle\sum_{d=1}^{l}}\ (-1)^{l-d}{\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{d}=l;r_{i}\geq 1}}\,\left({\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{d}}\dfrac{[(r_{1}\!+\!\cdots\!+\!r_{i-1})\delta+r_{i}\kappa]}{[(r_{1}+\cdots+r_{i})\delta]}\right)\,D_{r_{1}}\cdots D_{r_{d}}

(1.22)

in terms of
D_{r}

(r=0,1,\ldots)
.

We also summarize the kernel identities relevant to
the difference operators
D_{r}
and
H_{r}
for the sake of reference.

Theorem 1.5

(1)
For two sets of variables
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
,
the kernel function
\Phi(x,y)
of Cauchy type in (
1.10
) satisfies
the following two types of kernel identities
:

\displaystyle(DD)\qquad

\displaystyle\quad D^{x}_{r}\Phi(x;y)=D^{y}_{r}\Phi(x;y)\qquad(r=0,1,\ldots,n),

(1.23)

\displaystyle(HH)\qquad

\displaystyle\quad H^{x}_{r}\Phi(x;y)=H^{y}_{r}\Phi(x;y)\qquad(r=0,1,2,\ldots).

(1.24)

(2)
For two sets of variables
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
,
let

\Psi(x;y)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{n}[x_{i}-y_{k}]

(1.25)

be the kernel function of dual Cauchy type.
Under the balancing condition
m\kappa+n\delta=0
,

\Psi(x;y)
satisfies the kernel identity

(HD)\qquad\quad H^{x}_{r}\Psi(x;y)=(-1)^{r}\widehat{D}^{y}_{r}\Psi(x;y)\qquad(r=0,1,2,\ldots),

(1.26)

where
\widehat{D}^{y}_{r}
denotes the difference operator obtained
from
D^{y}_{r}
by exchanging the parameters
\delta
and
\kappa
.

Propositions
1.3
and
1.4

are consequences of the recurrence
formula of Wronski type (see Section 3).
After a complement on kernel identities
for the Ruijsenaars operators (Section 4),
we finally give some remarks on the trigonometric case in Section 5.

2
Recurrence formula of Wronski type

In this section we give a proof of Theorem
1.2
.
Our goal is to establish the recurrence formula
of Wronski type between the two sequences of difference
operators
D_{r}
(
r=0,1,2,\ldots
) and

H_{s}
(
s=0,1,2,\ldots
).

Theorem 2.1

The difference operators
H_{l}

(l=0,1,2\ldots)

defined by (
1.6
) satisfy the
recurrence formulas

{\displaystyle\sum_{r+s=l}}(-1)^{r}[r\kappa+s\delta]\,D_{r}H_{s}=0\qquad(l=1,2,\ldots).

(2.1)

Since
D_{0}=1
, by this theorem
we see inductively that
H_{l}
belong to

\mathbb{C}[D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}]
for all
l=0,1,2,\ldots
.

Theorem 2.2

The difference operators
H_{l}

(l=0,1,2\ldots)
belong
to the commutative algebra
\mathbb{C}[D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}]

of Ruijsenaars operators. In particular one has

D_{r}H_{s}=H_{s}D_{r},\qquad H_{r}H_{s}=H_{s}H_{r}\qquad(r,s=0,1,2,\ldots).

(2.2)

Proof of Theorem
2.1
:
We express the difference operators
D_{r}
as

D_{r}={\displaystyle\sum_{|I|=r}}A_{I}(x)T_{x}^{\,\epsilon_{I}\delta},\qquad A_{I}(x)={\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;\,j\notin I}}\dfrac{[x_{i}-x_{j}+\kappa]}{[x_{i}-x_{j}]}=\dfrac{\Delta(x+\epsilon_{I}\kappa)}{\Delta(x)}

(2.3)

where we define
\epsilon_{I}={\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}}{\epsilon_{i}}
by using the unit vectors

\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{n}
of
\mathbb{N}^{n}
.
Similarly we express
H_{l}
as

H_{=}{\displaystyle\sum_{|\mu|=l}}H_{\mu}(x)T_{x}^{\mu\delta},\quad H_{\mu}=\dfrac{\Delta(x+\mu\delta)}{\Delta(x)}\,{\displaystyle\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}}\dfrac{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\kappa]_{\mu_{j}}}{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\delta]_{\mu_{j}}}.

(2.4)

When
r+s=l
, we compute

\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{r}H_{s}&={\displaystyle\sum_{|I|=r}}\ {\displaystyle\sum_{|\mu|=s}}A_{I}(x)H_{\mu}(x+\epsilon_{I}\delta)T_{x}^{(\epsilon_{I}+\mu)\delta}\\
&={\displaystyle\sum_{|\lambda|=l}}\ {\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq\mbox{\scriptsize supp}(\lambda);\,|I|=r}}\,A_{I}(x)H_{\lambda-\epsilon_{I}}(x+\epsilon_{I}\delta)T_{x}^{\lambda\delta},\end{array}

(2.5)

where
\mbox{supp}(\lambda)=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\ |\ \lambda_{i}>0\}
.
Hence, the recurrence formula (
2.1
) is equivalent to saying that

{\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq\mbox{\scriptsize supp}(\lambda)}}(-1)^{|I|}\big[\,|I|\kappa+(|\lambda|-|I|)\delta\,\big]\,A_{I}(x)H_{\lambda-\epsilon_{I}}(x+\epsilon_{I}\delta)=0

(2.6)

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with
|\lambda|>0
.
We now make the expression
A_{I}(x)H_{\lambda-\epsilon_{I}}(x+\epsilon_{I})
explicit.
Setting
L=\mbox{supp}(\lambda)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{I}(x)H_{\lambda-\epsilon_{I}}(x+\epsilon_{I})&=\dfrac{\Delta(x+\epsilon_{I}\kappa)}{\Delta(x)}\dfrac{\Delta(x+\lambda\delta)}{\Delta(x+\epsilon_{I}\delta)}\\[10.0pt]
&\quad\cdot{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\kappa]_{\lambda_{j}-1}}{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\delta]_{\lambda_{j}-1}}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in L\backslash I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\kappa-\delta]_{\lambda_{j}}}{[x_{j}-x_{i}]_{\lambda_{j}}}\\
&\quad\cdot{\displaystyle\prod_{i\notin I;j\in I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\kappa+\delta]_{\lambda_{j}-1}}{[x_{j}-x_{i}+2\delta]_{\lambda_{j}-1}}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\notin I;j\in L\backslash I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\kappa]_{\lambda_{j}}}{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\delta]_{\lambda_{j}}}.\end{array}

(2.7)

Noting that

\dfrac{\Delta(x+\epsilon_{I}\kappa)}{\Delta(x)}\dfrac{\Delta(x+\lambda\delta)}{\Delta(x+\epsilon_{I}\delta)}=\dfrac{\Delta(x+\lambda\delta)}{\Delta(x)}\dfrac{\Delta(x+\epsilon_{I}\kappa)}{\Delta(x+\epsilon_{I}\delta)}=\dfrac{\Delta(x+\lambda\delta)}{\Delta(x)}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\notin I;j\in I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\kappa]}{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\delta]},

(2.8)

we can compute
A_{I}(x)H_{\lambda-\epsilon_{I}}(x+\epsilon_{I})
as follows:

\displaystyle A_{I}(x)H_{\lambda-\epsilon_{I}}(x+\epsilon_{I})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Delta(x+\lambda\delta)}{\Delta(x)}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\notin I;j\in L}}\dfrac{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\kappa]_{\lambda_{j}}}{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\delta]_{\lambda_{j}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\kappa]_{\lambda_{j}-1}}{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\delta]_{\lambda_{j}-1}}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in L\backslash I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\kappa-\delta]_{\lambda_{j}}}{[x_{j}-x_{i}]_{\lambda_{j}}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Delta(x+\lambda\delta)}{\Delta(x)}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\};j\in L}}\dfrac{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\kappa]_{\lambda_{j}}}{[x_{j}-x_{i}+\delta]_{\lambda_{j}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in L\backslash I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\kappa\!-\!\delta]}{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}]}.{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in L}}\dfrac{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\lambda_{i}\delta]}{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\kappa\!+\!(\lambda_{j}\!-\!1)\delta]}.

(2.9)

Hence (
2.6
) is equivalent to

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq L}}(-1)^{|I|}\big[\,|I|\kappa\!+\!(|\lambda|\!-\!|I|)\delta\,\big]\,{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in L\backslash I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\kappa\!-\!\delta]}{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}]}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in L}}\dfrac{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\lambda_{j}\delta]}{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\kappa\!+\!(\lambda_{j}\!-\!1)\delta]}=0\end{array}

(2.10)

for any
L\neq\phi
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with
\mbox{supp}(\lambda)=L
.
Setting
\lambda=\epsilon_{L}+\nu
,
we rewrite this in the form

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq L}}(-1)^{|I|}\big[\,|I|\kappa\!+\!(|\nu|\!+\!|L|\!-\!|I|)\delta\,\big]\,{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in L\backslash I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\kappa\!-\!\delta]}{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}]}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in L}}\dfrac{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\delta\!+\!\nu_{j}\delta]}{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\kappa\!+\nu_{j}\delta]}=0\end{array}

(2.11)

for any
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with
\mbox{supp}(\nu)\subseteq L
.
Since this formula contains only those variables
x_{i}
with
i\in L
, we
have only to consider the case where
L=\{1,\ldots,n\}
(
n\geq 1
):

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}}(-1)^{|I|}\big[\,|I|\kappa\!+\!(|\nu|\!+\!m\!-\!|I|)\delta\,\big]\\
\qquad\cdot{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\notin I}}\dfrac{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\kappa\!-\!\delta]}{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}]}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}}\dfrac{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\delta\!+\!\nu_{j}\delta]}{[x_{j}\!-\!x_{i}\!+\!\kappa\!+\nu_{j}\delta]}=0\end{array}

(2.12)

for any
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
.
This identity follows from the following functional identity
by the change of variables

z_{i}=x_{i},\quad w_{i}=x_{i}+\delta+\nu_{i}\delta\quad(i=1,\ldots,n);\quad a=\kappa-\delta.

(2.13)

Lemma 2.3

Given two sets of variables
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
,
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})

and a parameter
a
, the following identity holds as a meromorphic function
in
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
for
n\geq 1
:

{\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}}(-1)^{|I|}\dfrac{[|w|-|z|+|I|a]}{[\,|w|-|z|\,]}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;j\notin I}}\dfrac{[z_{j}\!-\!z_{i}\!+a]}{[z_{j}\!-\!z_{i}]}{\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I;\,k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}}\dfrac{[w_{k}-z_{i}]}{[w_{k}-z_{i}+a]}=0,

(2.14)

where
|z|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}
,
|w|=\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}
.

Proof
:
We give a proof of the functional identity (
2.14
) for
the case where
[z]=\sigma(z;\Omega)
is
the Weierstrass sigma function associated with a period lattice
\Omega
.
By the classification of

,
it is not difficult to derive
(
2.14
)
for any
[z]
in this class from the
case of
\sigma(z;\Omega)
,
by the limiting procedures from
\sigma(z;\Omega)
to

\sin(\pi z/\omega)
and
z
, and by the
invariance of (
2.14
) under the transformation

[z]\to e^{cz^{2}}[z]
.

Identity (
2.14
) for
[z]=\sigma(z;\Omega)
can be
proved by the induction on

Since it holds trivially for
n=1
, we assume
n\geq 2

We regard the left-hand side of (
2.14
)
as a meromorphic function of
w_{n}
, and
denote it by
F(w_{n})
assuming that the other variables are generic.
Note first that

F(w_{n})
is an elliptic function possibly with simple poles
at
w_{n}\equiv z_{1}-a,\ldots,z_{n}-a
and
w_{n}\equiv|z|-|w^{\prime}|
modulo
the period lattice
\Omega
,
where

We first compute the residue of

at
w_{n}=z_{n}-a

Nontrivial residues possibly arise from the terms corresponding

I
containing

Then we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{Res}_{w_{n}=z_{n}-a}(F(w_{n})dw_{n})\\[8.0pt]

(2.15)

by the induction hypothesis (
z^{\prime}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1})

is symmetric with respect to

(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
, we see that

w_{n}\equiv z_{1}-a,\ldots,z_{n}-a

.
Hence

F(w_{n})

We next look at the value of

It is computed as

\begin{array}[]{ll}F(z_{n})&={\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n-1\}}}(-1)^{|I|}\dfrac{[|w^{\prime}|-|z^{\prime}|+|I|a]}{[|w^{\prime}|-|z^{\prime}|]}\\

&=0\end{array}

(2.16)

again by the induction hypothesis. This implies that
F(w_{n})

identically zero as a meromorphic function of
w_{n}

\square

5

In this section we consider the trigonometric cases
where
[x]=e(x/2)-e(-x/2)
in the notation

of the exponential function.
In stead of the parameter
\delta

We denote by

the multiplicative variables defined by

(

) corresponding to

For these

q
-shift operator
with respect to

(

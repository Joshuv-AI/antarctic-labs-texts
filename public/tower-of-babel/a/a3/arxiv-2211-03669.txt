A Dixmier type averaging property of automorphisms on a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra
Mikael Rørdam

Abstract
In his study of the relative Dixmier property for inclusions of von Neumann algebras and of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras, Popa considered a certain property of automorphisms on C ∗ C^{*} -algebras, that we here call the strong averaging property . In this note we characterize when an automorphism on a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra has the strong averaging property. In particular, automorphisms on commutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebras possess this property precisely when they are free. An automorphism on a unital separable simple C ∗ C^{*} -algebra with at least one tracial state has the strong averaging property precisely when its extension to the finite part of the bi-dual of the C ∗ C^{*} -algebra is properly outer, and in the simple, non-tracial case the strong averaging property is equivalent to being outer. To illustrate the usefulness of the strong averaging property we give three examples where we can provide simpler proofs of existing results on crossed product C ∗ C^{*} -algebras, and we are also able to extend these results in different directions.

Introduction

There are several notions of “non-innerness” of an automorphism on a
C^{*}
-algebra (or a von Neumann algebra), most notably outerness, proper outerness and freeness (which on commutative
C^{*}
-algebras is equivalent to proper outerness). These properties of an automorphism or a group action, in turn, facilitate properties of the associated dynamical system and of the crossed product, such as for example simplicity (for
C^{*}
-algebras) and factoriality (for von Neumann algebras).

We shall investigate a property of automorphisms considered by Popa in his paper
[
17
]
where he studies the relative Dixmier property for an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras (or von Neumann algebras), see Definition
2.1
. We call this property the (strong) averaging property of the automorphism as it resembles the Diximier averaging property. Popa proves, via establishing this property, that the inclusion of a simple
C^{*}
-algebra with the Dixmier property inside its crossed product by an arbitrary discrete group equipped with a suitable outer action on the
C^{*}
-algebra has the relative Dixmier property,
[
17
, Corollary 4.1]
.

The goal of this paper is to characterize which automorphisms enjoy the (strong) averaging property, and to demonstrate its usefulness via some examples, in particular in the direction of simplifying existing proofs.

The characterization of the strong averaging property, here abbreviated (SAveP), is given in terms of residual outerness of the automorphism (where “residual” means that the outerness property must hold in all invariant quotients), and in terms of suitable outerness of the extension of the automorphism to the bi-dual of the
C^{*}
-algebra. More specifically, we must require that the automorphism is properly outer on the finite part of the bi-dual. We remind the reader that proper outerness of an automorphism on a
C^{*}
-algebra does not imply that its extension to the bi-dual also is properly outer. However, by a result of Kishimoto,
[
11
]
, if the
C^{*}
-algebra is simple, and the automorphism extends to an
inner
automorphism on its bi-dual, then it is already inner on the
C^{*}
-algebra itself.

Part (i) of Theorem
2.18
gives a
necessary
condition on an automorphism on a
C^{*}
-algebra to
A
have (SAveP), and part (iii) gives a
sufficient
condition for (SAveP). It is shown in Example
2.23
that the sufficient condition is not necessary in general. In the other direction, we do not know if the necessary condition of (i) is also sufficient in general (it probably isn’t), but we do show that it is sufficient in three important cases: when the
C^{*}
-algebra
A
has stable rank one, Corollary
2.20
, when
A
is commutative, in which case (SAveP) is equivalent to freeness, Corollary
2.21
, and when
A
is simple, Corollary
2.19
. In the latter case (SAveP) is equivalent to outerness when
A
has no tracial state, and it is equivalent to proper outerness of the extension of the automorphism to the finite part of the bi-dual, when
A
does admit a tracial state.

Popa’s averaging property for automorphisms

We begin by formally defining the averaging properties, implicitly defined in Popa’s paper
[
17
]
, that is the topic of this paper.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra, let
{\cal U}({A})
denote the group of unitary elements in
{A}
, and let
\alpha
be an automorphism on
{A}
.

Let
b\in{A}
. Denote by
C_{A}^{\,\alpha}(b)
,
C_{A}(b)=C_{A}^{\,\mathrm{id}_{A}}(b)
, and
C_{A}^{\,\alpha}=C_{A}^{\,\alpha}(1_{A})
the norm closed convex hull of the sets

\{vb\alpha(v)^{*}:v\in{\cal U}({A})\},\qquad\{vbv^{*}:v\in{\cal U}({A})\},\qquad\{v\alpha(v)^{*}:v\in{\cal U}({A})\},

respectively. We use the same notation as above in the case where
b
belongs to a
C^{*}
-algebra
{B}
that contains
{A}
.

We say that
\alpha
has the
averaging property
(AveP) if
0\in C_{A}^{\,\alpha}
, and that
\alpha
has the
strong averaging property
(SAveP) if
0\in C_{A}^{\,\alpha}(b)
, for all
b\in{A}
.

It is easy to see that if
b^{\prime}\in C_{A}^{\,\alpha}(b)
, then
C_{A}^{\,\alpha}(b^{\prime})\subseteq C_{A}^{\,\alpha}(b)
. Recall also that the usual Dixmier property of a
C^{*}
-algebra

holds when
C_{A}(b)\cap{\mathbb{C}}\cdot 1_{A}\neq\emptyset
, for all
b\in{A}
.

Lemma 2.2

If

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha
be an automorphism on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
{A}
and let

. Then:

(i)

\mathrm{Ad}\,_{u}\circ\alpha
has
(AveP)
if and only if
0\in C_{A}^{\,\alpha}(u)

(ii)

\mathrm{Ad}\,_{u}
has
(AveP)
if and only if
0\in C_{A}(u)
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\alpha
be an automorphism on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
{A}
. If
\alpha
has
(SAveP)
, then
\mathrm{Ad}_{u}\circ\alpha
has
(AveP)
, for all
u\in{\cal U}(A)
. Conversely, if the stable rank of

is one, and if
\mathrm{Ad}_{u}\circ\alpha
has
(AveP)
, for all
u\in{\cal U}(A)
, then
\alpha
has
(SAveP)
.

Proof.

The first part follows immediately from Lemma
2.3
(i). Suppose that
A
has stable rank one and that
\mathrm{Ad}_{u}\circ\alpha
has (AveP), for all
u\in{\cal U}(A)
. Let
b\in A
. It follows from
[
18
]
that there exist unitaries
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in A
such that

b=\frac{\|b\|}{3}\big(u_{1}+u_{2}+u_{3}\big)=\frac{\|b\|}{3}u_{1}+b_{0},\qquad b_{0}=\frac{\|b\|}{3}\big(u_{2}+u_{3}\big).

Since

by Lemma
2.3
(i), we conclude that
C_{A}^{\alpha}(b)
contains elements arbitrarily close to
C_{A}^{\alpha}(b_{0})
, and in particular, that
\inf\{\|x\|:x\in C_{A}^{\alpha}(b)\}\leq\|b_{0}\|=\frac{2}{3}\|b\|
. Repeating this process yields the result.
∎

Let
{A}

Definition 2.8
.

Following Elliott,
[
6
]
, an automorphism
\alpha
on a
C^{*}
-algebra

properly outer
if its restriction to any non-zero
\alpha
-invariant ideal

For each
\alpha

It is a consequence of Sakai’s theorem (that all bounded derivations on a simple

Connes proved in

that an automorphism

Proper outerness for an automorphism on a separable

-algebra

-algebra of

[

Although a priori not obvious, the definition of proper outerness for
C^{*}
-algebras, when applied to an automorphism on a von Neumann algebra, agrees with the definition of proper outerness for von Neumann algebras. (One can use Connes’ characterization of proper outerness to see this.)

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra and let
\alpha
be a properly outer automorphism on
M
.

(i)

For each
b\in M
, for each
{\varepsilon}>0
, and for non-zero projection
p
in
M
there exists a non-zero subprojection
q
of

such that
\|qb\alpha(q)\|<{\varepsilon}
.

(ii)

For each
b\in M
and for each
{\varepsilon}>0
there exists a family
(p_{i})_{i\in I}
of pairwise orthogonal projections in

such that
\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}=1

\|p_{i}b\alpha(p_{i})\|<{\varepsilon}
, for all

.

Proof.

(i). We first note that Connes’ characterization of properly outer automorphisms implies that
(i) holds when

is a unitary. Indeed, if

is a unitary, then
\beta=\mathrm{Ad}_{u}\circ\alpha
is still properly outer, so given

there exists a non-zero projection

such that
\|qu\alpha(q)\|=\|q\beta(q)u\|=\|q\beta(q)\|<{\varepsilon}
.

We can write
b=C\cdot(u_{1}+u_{2}+u_{3})
, for some constant

(which can taken to be
C=\frac{2}{3}\|b\|
), and for unitaries

. By the argument above there exist non-zero projections

such that
\|q_{j}u_{j}\alpha(q_{j})\|<{\varepsilon}/3C
, for all

\|qu_{j}\alpha(q)\|<{\varepsilon}/3C
, for all

(ii) follows from (i) via a standard maximality argument.

It follows easily from Lemma

Proposition 2.10
.

Any automorphism on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with

Let
\alpha
be an automorphism on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra

which is not residually outer. Let

be a proper closed two-sided invariant ideal in
{A}
for which
\overset{\centerdot}{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Aut}({A}/I)

\pi(vb^{*}\alpha(v)^{*})=\pi(v)u^{*}\overset{\centerdot}{\alpha}(\pi(v))^{*}=\pi(v)\pi(v)^{*}u^{*}=u^{*}

p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{n+1}=1-p
in
A
such that
p_{1}\sim p_{2}\sim\cdots\sim p_{n+1}
. By the observation above we can find
b\in C_{A}(a)
with
p_{i}bp_{j}=0
, when
i\neq j
, and
p_{i}bp_{i}=p_{i}ap_{i}
. Note that
\|p_{i}ap_{i}\|\leq\|pap\|\leq{\varepsilon}
, when
0\leq i\leq n
. For each permutation
\sigma
of
\{0,1,2,\dots,n+1\}
fixing
0
, let
u_{\sigma}
be a unitary in
A
satisfying
u_{\sigma}p_{i}u_{\sigma}^{*}=p_{\sigma(i)}
, for all
i
.
Set

c=\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\sum_{\sigma}u_{\sigma}bu_{\sigma}^{*}\in C_{A}(a).

Then
p_{i}cp_{j}=0
, when
i\neq j
; and since
p_{i}u_{\sigma}bu_{\sigma}^{*}p_{i}=u_{\sigma}p_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}bp_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}u_{\sigma}^{*}
, we get

\|c\|=\max\{p_{i}cp_{i}:0\leq i\leq n+1\}\leq\frac{n{\varepsilon}+\|a\|}{n+1}.

Since
n\geq 1
was arbitrary this proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.17
.

If
\alpha
is a residually outer automorphism on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
{A}
, then:

(i)

\|\alpha-\mathrm{id}_{A}\|=2
,

(ii)

for each
{\varepsilon}>0
, there exists a positive element
h\in{A}
with
\|h\|=1
and
\|h\alpha(h)\|\leq{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

(i). If
\|\alpha-\mathrm{id}_{A}\|<2
, then
\alpha
is the exponential of a bounded derivation,
[
10
, Theorem 7]
, see also
[
15
, 8.7.7]
, and approximately inner, which again implies that
\alpha
is ideal preserving. Take a maximal proper closed two-sided ideal
I
in
{A}
, and consider the automorphism
\overset{\centerdot}{\alpha}
on the simple unital
C^{*}
-algebra
{A}/I
. Then
\overset{\centerdot}{\alpha}
is still the exponential of a bounded derivation, so by Sakai’s theorem,
[
21
]
, it is inner. Hence
\alpha
is not residually outer.

(ii) follows from (i) and the Olesen–Pedersen “sine-cosine” theorem,
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
.
∎

We are now ready to characterize automorphisms with the strong averaging property.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
\alpha
be an automorphism on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. In parts (ii) and (iii) assume further that
A
is separable. If
A
has no tracial state, then
A^{**}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is zero in which case we declare the condition below on
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
to be vacuously satisfied.

(i)

If
\alpha
has
(SAveP)
, then
\alpha
is residually outer and
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is properly outer.

(ii)

Conversely, if
\alpha
is residually outer and
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is properly outer, then
\mathrm{Ad}\,_{u}\circ\alpha
has
(AveP)
, for all unitaries
u\in A
.

(iii)

If
\alpha
is residually properly outer and
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is properly outer, then
\alpha
has
(SAveP)
.

Proof.

(i) has already been established in Propositions
2.10
and
2.12
.

If
\alpha
is residually outer and
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is properly outer, then so is
\mathrm{Ad}\,_{u}\circ\alpha
, for all unitaries
u\in A
. Hence, to prove (ii), it suffices to show that
\alpha
has (AveP), under the assumptions in (ii).

We combine the proofs of (ii) and (iii), since they are similar. For each
\bar{\alpha}
-invariant central projection
q
in
A^{**}
, consider the restriction
\bar{\alpha}_{q}
of
\bar{\alpha}
to
qA^{**}
, and let
\pi_{q}\colon A\to qA^{**}
be the natural
∗
-homomorphism arising as the inclusion
A\subseteq A^{**}
composed with the quotient mapping
A^{**}\to qA^{**}
.

The strategy is to prove that
\bar{\alpha}
has the SOT-(AveP), respectively, the SOT-(SAveP), by which we mean that
0
belongs to the strong operator closure of
C_{A^{**}}^{\,\bar{\alpha}}
, respectively, of
C_{A^{**}}^{\,\bar{\alpha}}(b)
, for all
b\in A
, and then apply Lemma
2.14
.

We assume that
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is properly outer, which is common for (ii) and (iii). Accordingly, we can write
{A}^{**}=q{A}^{**}+(1-q){A}^{**}
, with
q
an
\bar{\alpha}
-invariant central projection in
{A}^{**}
such that
\bar{\alpha}_{1-q}
is properly outer and
\bar{\alpha}_{q}=\mathrm{Ad}\,_{w}
for some unitary
w\in q{A}^{**}
. By the assumption that
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is properly outer, either
q=0
or
q{A}^{**}
is properly infinite. If
q=0
, then
\bar{\alpha}
is SOT-(SAveP) by Corollary
2.19
, and we are done.

We know from Lemma
2.13
that
\bar{\alpha}_{1-q}
is SOT-(SAveP), and hence SOT-(AveP). The problem is therefore reduced to the properly infinite central summand
qA^{**}
, i.e., to show that
\bar{\alpha}_{q}=\mathrm{Ad}\,_{w}
is SOT-(AveP), respectively, SOT-(SAveP) as an automorphism on
qA^{**}
. Fix
{\varepsilon}>0
. By a standard maximality argument it suffices to show that for each non-zero central subprojection
q_{0}
of
q
there exists a non-zero central subprojection
q_{1}
of
q_{0}
such that

\inf\{\|x\|:x\in C_{q_{1}A^{**}}^{\,\bar{\alpha}_{q_{1}}}\}\leq{\varepsilon},\quad\text{respectively,}\;\;\inf\{\|x\|:x\in C_{q_{1}A^{**}}^{\,\bar{\alpha}_{q_{1}}}(\bar{b}q_{1})\}\leq{\varepsilon},\;b\in A,

where
\bar{b}
is the image of
b
in
A^{**}
. Note that

C_{q_{1}A^{**}}^{\,\bar{\alpha}_{q_{1}}}=C_{q_{1}A^{**}}(w_{1})w_{1}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad C_{q_{1}A^{**}}^{\,\bar{\alpha}_{q_{1}}}(\bar{b}q_{1})=C_{q_{1}A^{**}}(w_{1}\bar{b}q_{1})w_{1}^{*},

where
w_{1}=wq_{1}
.

The assumption in (ii) and (iii) that
A
is separable implies that
\kappa_{A^{**}}\leq\aleph_{0}
, see, eg.,
[
14
, Definition 5.5 and Lemma 5.6]
. Recall that
\kappa_{M}
is the largest cardinality of a set of pairwise equivalent and pairwise orthogonal non-zero projections in a von Neumann algebra
M
. If
\kappa_{M}\leq\aleph_{0}
, then any two properly infinite projections in
M
are Murray von Neumann equivalent if and only if they have the same central support.

Assume now that
\alpha
is residually outer. Let
{\varepsilon}>0
be as given above. By Lemma
2.17
, applied to the quotient
\pi_{q_{0}}(A)
of
A
, there exists
h\in\pi_{q_{0}}(A)^{+}
such that
\|h\|=1
and
\|h\bar{\alpha}_{q_{0}}(h)\|\leq{\varepsilon}/3
. Choose continuous functions
g_{1},g_{2}
on
[0,1]
such that
0\leq g_{2}\leq g_{1}\leq 1
,
g_{2}(1)=1
,
g_{1}g_{2}=g_{2}
and
\mathrm{supp}(g_{1})\subseteq[1-{\varepsilon}/3,1]
. Choose a non-zero projection
p
in the closed hereditary sub-
C^{*}
-algebra of
q_{0}A^{**}
generated by
g_{2}(h)
. Then
pg_{1}(h)=p
and
\|g_{1}(h)(1-h)\|\leq{\varepsilon}/3
, which implies that
\|p(1-h)\|\leq{\varepsilon}/3
. It follows that

\|pw_{0}p\|=\|p\bar{\alpha}_{q_{0}}(p)w_{0}\|=\|p\bar{\alpha}_{q_{0}}(p)\|\leq\|p\bar{\alpha}_{q_{0}}(p)-ph\bar{\alpha}_{q_{0}}(hp)\|+{\varepsilon}/3\leq{\varepsilon}.

Let
q_{1}
be the central support of
p
. Then
q_{1}-p\precsim p
(since
p
is a properly infinite projection whose central support dominates the central support of
q_{1}-p
). Moreover,
\|pw_{1}p\|\leq{\varepsilon}
(with
w_{1}=wq_{1}
), so it follows from Lemma
2.16
, applied to the unital
C^{*}
-algebra
q_{1}A^{**}
, that

\inf\{\|x\|:x\in C_{q_{1}A^{**}}^{\,\bar{\alpha}_{q_{1}}}\}=\inf\{\|x\|:x\in C_{q_{1}A^{**}}(w_{1})\}\leq{\varepsilon},

as desired. This proves (ii).

To prove (iii) we assume that
\alpha
is residually properly outer. Given
b\in A
, which we may assume to be a contraction, and with
{\varepsilon}
as given above, we can find
h\in\pi_{q_{0}}(A)^{+}
such that
\|h\|=1
and
\|h\bar{b}q_{0}\bar{\alpha}_{q_{0}}(h)\|\leq{\varepsilon}/3
, cf. the comments below Definition
2.8
, where
\bar{b}
as above is the image of
b
in
A^{**}
. Choose the projection
p
as in the previous paragraph (with respect to the same functions
g_{1},g_{2}
, now applied to the present
h
). Then, arguing as above,

\|p\bar{b}q_{0}w_{0}p\|=\|p\bar{b}q_{0}\bar{\alpha}_{q_{0}}(p)\|\leq\|p\bar{b}q_{0}\bar{\alpha}_{q_{0}}(p)-phb_{0}\bar{\alpha}_{q_{0}}(hp)\|+{\varepsilon}/3\leq{\varepsilon}.

Take again
q_{1}
to be the central support of
p
. Then
\|p\bar{b}q_{1}w_{1}p\|\leq{\varepsilon}
and
q_{1}-p\precsim p
, so Lemma
2.16
yields that

\inf\{\|x\|:x\in C_{q_{1}A^{**}}^{\,\bar{\alpha}_{q_{1}}}(\bar{b}q_{1})\}=\inf\{\|x\|:x\in C_{q_{1}A^{**}}(\bar{b}q_{1}w_{1})\}\leq{\varepsilon},

as desired. This proves (iii).
∎

In the cases where the
C^{*}
-algebra
A
in Theorem
2.18
above is either simple, of stable rank one, or commutative, one can improve the statement of the theorem as in the corollaries below. Our first corollary addresses the case where
A
is simple:

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
A
be a simple separable unital
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
\alpha
be an automorphism on
A
.

(i)

If
A
admits a tracial state, then
\alpha
has
(SAveP)
if and only if
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is properly outer on
A^{**}_{\mathrm{fin}}
.

(ii)

If
A
does not have a tracial state, then
\alpha
is
(SAveP)
if and only if
\alpha
is outer on
A
.

Proof.

The “only if” parts of (i) and (ii) both follow from Theorem
2.18
(i). If
A
has a tracial state and
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is properly outer, then
\alpha
is outer (hence properly outer, hence residually properly outer, because
A
is simple), so it has (SAveP) by Theorem
2.18
(iii). If
A
has no trace, then the condition on
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
becomes vacuous, and by the same argument as above we conclude that that every outer automorphism on
A
has (SAveP).
∎

Corollary 2.20
.

For an automorphism
\alpha
on a unital separable
C^{*}
-algebra
A
of stable rank one, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\alpha
has
(SAveP)
,

(ii)

\mathrm{Ad}_{u}\circ\alpha
has
(AveP)
, for all unitaries
u\in A
,

(iii)

\alpha
is residually outer and
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is properly outer.

Proof.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is Theorem
2.18
(ii); (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(i) is Proposition
2.4
, and (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is Theorem
2.18
(i).
∎

The (strong) averaging property is equivalent to freeness on commutative
C^{*}
-algebras:

Corollary 2.21
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and consider the unital
C^{*}
-algebra
{A}=C(X)
. The following conditions are equivalent for each automophism
\alpha
on
{A}
.

(i)

\alpha
has
(AveP)
,

(ii)

\alpha
has
(SAveP)
,

(iii)

the induced homeomorphism
\hat{\alpha}
of
\alpha
on
X
is free,

(iv)

the extension
\bar{\alpha}
of
\alpha
to
A^{**}
is properly outer.

Since
A^{**}
is abelian, (iv) just says that
\bar{\alpha}
is not the identity on any central summand of
A^{**}
. The proof below does not use the full force of Theorem
2.18
.

Proof.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) holds trivially (and (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) holds when
{A}
is abelian). (iv)
\Rightarrow
(i) follows from Lemmas
2.13
and
2.14
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii). Suppose that
\hat{\alpha}
is not free. Then there is a pure state
\rho
on
{A}
which is invariant under
\alpha
. Since pure states on abelian
C^{*}
-algebras are multiplicative, we get
\rho(v\alpha(v)^{*})=1
, for all unitaries
v
in
{A}
. Hence
\alpha
cannot have (AveP).

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv). Assume that
\bar{\alpha}
is not properly outer. Then there is an invariant non-zero central projection
q\in{A}^{**}
such that
\bar{\alpha}
is inner on
{A}^{**}q
. Since
{A}^{**}
is abelian, this implies that
\bar{\alpha}
is the identity on
A^{**}q
. Consider the (canonical)
∗
-homomorphism
\pi_{q}\colon{A}\to{A}^{**}q
. Then
\pi_{q}=\bar{\alpha}\circ\pi_{q}=\pi_{q}\circ\alpha
. Take a pure state
\sigma
on
\pi_{q}({A})
and set
\rho=\sigma\circ\pi_{q}
, which is a pure state on
{A}
. Then
\rho\circ\alpha=\sigma\circ\pi_{q}\circ\alpha=\rho
. Hence
\hat{\alpha}
is not free.
∎

Example 2.22
.

Let
A=\bigotimes_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{k_{n}}({\mathbb{C}})
be a UHF-algebra with unique tracial state
\tau
, where
k_{n}\geq 2
, for all
n
. Consider the unitary elements
u_{n}=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,\cdots,1,-1)\in M_{k_{n}}({\mathbb{C}})
, and the automorphism

\alpha=\bigotimes_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ad}\,_{u_{n}}

on
A
. Then
\alpha
is outer. But if
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|1_{k_{n}}-u_{n}\|_{2}<\infty
, then

\alpha
extends to an inner automorphism
\mathrm{Ad}\,_{u}
on
\pi_{\tau}(A)^{\prime\prime}
, with
u=\bigotimes_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}\in\pi_{\tau}(A)^{\prime\prime}
. Hence
\alpha
is not (SAveP), and if
\tau(u)\neq 0
, which is the case if
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1/k_{n}<\infty
, then
\alpha
does not have (AveP).

In this example,
\alpha
is an outer automorphism on
A
(even residually properly outer), but
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is inner on
A^{**}_{\mathrm{fin}}=\pi_{\tau}(A)^{\prime\prime}
. In particular,
\alpha
does not have (SAveP).

Example 2.23
.

Let
2\leq n<\infty
and let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be the Cuntz-Toeplitz algebra generated by isometries
s_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq n
, with orthogonal range projections, described by the extension

0\to{\cal K}\to\mathcal{T}_{n}\to{\cal O}_{n}\to 0,

where
{\cal K}
denotes the compact operators on a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space.
Let us first note that no automorphism on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
is properly outer, since its restriction to
{\cal K}
always is multiplier inner. Secondly, there do exist residually outer automorphisms on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
. Take, for example, any automorphism
\alpha
on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
that permutes the generators
s_{j}
according to some permutation
\sigma\neq\mathrm{id}
on
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Then
\alpha
is outer on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and on
{\cal O}_{n}
, hence residually outer.

We finally observe that an automorphism
\alpha
on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
has (SAveP) if and only if it is residually outer. This fact, that we will prove below, does not follow from Theorem
2.18
above, and moreover shows that the sufficient condition in Theorem
2.18
(iii) is not necessary.

Take a residually outer automorphism
\alpha
on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
. Then
\alpha
descends to an outer automorphism
\overset{\centerdot}{\alpha}
on
{\cal O}_{n}
, which has (SAveP) by Corollary
2.19
above. Let
b\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
be given, and let
\pi
denote the quotient mapping
\mathcal{T}_{n}\to{\cal O}_{n}
. Let
{\varepsilon}>0
. Then there exist unitaries
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in{\cal O}_{n}
such that

\Big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}\pi(b)\overset{\centerdot}{\alpha}(v_{j})^{*}\Big\|<{\varepsilon}.

The unitaries
v_{j}
lift to unitaries
u_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
(because the unitary group of
{\cal O}_{n}
is connected). It follows that there exists
x\in{\cal K}
such that

\Big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}b\alpha(u_{j})^{*}-x\Big\|<{\varepsilon}.

The restriction of
\alpha
to
{\cal K}
is multiplier inner, say equal to
\mathrm{Ad}\,_{w}
, for some
w\in{\cal M}({\cal K})=B(H)
. It follows from
[
12
, Theorem 4.7]
, see also
[
8
, Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 2.2]
, that
0\in C_{\widetilde{{\cal K}}}(y)
, for each
y\in{\cal K}
, and in particular for
y=xw
. We can therefore find unitaries
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{n}
with

\Big\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}x\alpha(z_{i})^{*}\Big\|=\Big\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}xwz_{i}^{*}\Big\|<{\varepsilon}.

In summary, we have

\Big\|\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{i,j}z_{i}u_{j}b\alpha(z_{i}u_{j})^{*}\Big\|<2{\varepsilon}.

It was shown in Example
2.23
that the sufficient condition Theorem
2.18
(iii), to ensure the strong averaging property of an automorphism, is not necessary. We do not know if the necessary condition in Theorem
2.18
(i) is sufficient:

Question 2.24
.

Is it the case that an automorphism
\alpha
on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
has (SAveP) if and only if it is residually outer and
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is properly outer?

The “only if” part is established in Theorem
2.18
, and the example above shows that the condition “residually properly outer” is too strong. The situation in Example
2.23
is special in that every unitary in any quotient of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
lifts to a unitary in
\mathcal{T}_{n}
. This could indicate that the answer to the question above could be negative and that a complete characterization of automorphisms with (SAveP) is strictly between the necessary condition of Theorem
2.18
(ii) and the sufficient condition of Theorem
2.18
(iii).

An affirmative answer to Question
2.5
implies an affirmative answer to Question
2.24
by Theorem
2.18
(ii).

3
Applications

We give in this section three applications of the strong averaging property for an automorphism and for actions with this property. The applications provide simpler proof of existing results, which is a main purpose of advocating this property of automorphisms, but we are also able to improve some of these existing results in different directions.

Given an action
\alpha
of a discrete group
\Gamma
on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. We say that
\alpha
has (SAveP) if
\alpha_{t}
has (SAveP), for all
t\in\Gamma
,
t\neq e
, where
e\in\Gamma
denotes the neutral element. The associated reduced crossed product is denoted
A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
. Denote by
t\mapsto u_{t}
,
t\in\Gamma
, the unitary representation of
\Gamma
inside the crossed product, so that
A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
A\cup\{u_{t}:t\in\Gamma\}
.

Let
E\colon A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma\to A
denote the canonical conditional expectation, and for
t\in\Gamma
, let
E_{t}\colon A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma\to A
be given by
E_{t}(x)=E(xu_{t}^{*})
, which is the “Fourier coefficient” of
x
at
t
. Define the support,
\mathrm{supp}(x)
, of an element
x\in A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
to be the set
\{t\in\Gamma:E_{t}(x)\neq 0\}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\colon\Gamma\curvearrowright A
be a
(SAveP)
action of a discrete group
\Gamma
on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. Let
x\in A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
be given.

(i)

If
E(x)
is central in
A
, then
E(x)\in C_{A}(x)
.

(ii)

If
E_{t}(x)=1_{A}
, for some
t\in\Gamma
, then
u_{t}\in C_{A}^{\,\alpha_{t^{-1}}}(x)
.

(iii)

\mathrm{dist}(A,C_{A}(x))=0
.

Note that
C_{A}^{\,\alpha_{s}}(x)
is contained in
C^{*}(A,x)
, the smallest sub-
C^{*}
-algebra of
A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
that contains
A
and
x
, for all
x\in A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
and all
s\in\Gamma
.

Proof.

(i) and (ii). Let
t\in\Gamma
and assume that
E_{t}(x)
is central in
A
. (In part (i) we have
t=e
,
u_{t}=1_{A}
, and
E_{e}(x)=E(x)
, while
E_{t}(x)=1_{A}
in part (ii).) Let
{\varepsilon}>0
and choose
x_{0}\in A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
of finite support
F\subseteq\Gamma
such that
\|x-x_{0}\|\leq{\varepsilon}
and
E_{t}(x_{0})=E_{t}(x)
. By Lemma
2.2
we can find unitaries
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in A
such that

\big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}E_{s}(x_{0})u_{s}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(v_{j})^{*}\big\|=\big\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}E_{s}(x_{0})\alpha_{st^{-1}}(v_{j})^{*}\big\|<{\varepsilon}/|F|,

for
s\in F\setminus\{t\}
. For each
j
,
v_{j}E_{t}(x_{0})u_{t}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(v_{j})^{*}=v_{j}E_{t}(x_{0})v_{j}^{*}u_{t}=E_{t}(x)u_{t}
. It follows that

\Big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}x\alpha_{t^{-1}}(v_{j})^{*}-E_{t}(x)u_{t}\Big\|\leq\Big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}x_{0}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(v_{j})^{*}-E_{t}(x)u_{t}\Big\|+\|x-x_{0}\|<2{\varepsilon}.

This shows that (i) and (ii) hold.

(iii). Proceeding as above with
t=e
and with
E(x_{0})=E(x)
(not necessarily assuming this to be central), we arrive at

\Big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}xv_{j}^{*}-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}E(x)v_{j}^{*}\Big\|<2{\varepsilon},

from which (iii) follows. ∎

It is well-known that for an action
\alpha\colon\Gamma\curvearrowright A
on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, any tracial state
\tau
on
A
extends to a tracial state on
A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
if and only if it is invariant under the action
\alpha
, and that an extension in this case is given by
\bar{\tau}:=\tau\circ E
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\alpha\colon\Gamma\curvearrowright A
be an action of a discrete group
\Gamma
on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. Then
A\subseteq A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
has the relative Dixmier property if and only if
A
has the Dixmier property and the action
\alpha
has
(SAveP)
.

Proof.

It is clear that
A
has the Dixmier property if
A\subseteq A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
has the relative Dixmier property. To see that this also implies that
\alpha
has (SAveP), take
t\neq e
in
\Gamma
and
b\in A
. Since
C_{A}^{\,\alpha_{t}}(b)=C_{A}(bu_{t})u_{t}^{*}
, cf. (the proof of) Proposition
2.6
, it suffices to show that
0\in C_{A}(bu_{t})
. We know that
C_{A}(bu_{t})\cap{\mathbb{C}}1_{A}\neq\emptyset
, because
A\subseteq A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
has the relative Dixmier property. We easily see that
C_{A}(bu_{t})\subseteq Au_{t}
, which implies
C_{A}(bu_{t})\cap{\mathbb{C}}1_{A}\subseteq Au_{t}\cap{\mathbb{C}}1_{A}=\{0\}
.

Suppose now that the action has (SAveP). Let
x\in A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
. We show that if
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\lambda\cdot 1_{A}\in C_{A}(E(x))
, then
\lambda\cdot 1_{A}\in C_{A}(x)
. This will show that
A\subseteq A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
has the relative Dixmier property if
A
has the Dixmier property.

Let
{\varepsilon}>0
and find unitaries
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in A
such that

\big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}E(x)v_{j}^{*}-\lambda\cdot 1_{A}\big\|<{\varepsilon}
. Put
y=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}xv_{j}^{*}
. Then
y\in C_{A}(x)
and
\|E(y)-\lambda\cdot 1_{A}\|<{\varepsilon}
. Put
z=y+(\lambda\cdot 1_{A}-E(y))
, so that
E(z)=\lambda\cdot 1_{A}
. Then
\lambda\cdot 1_{A}\in C_{A}(z)
, by Lemma
3.1
(i), which implies that

\mathrm{dist}(\lambda\cdot 1_{A},C_{A}(x))\leq\mathrm{dist}(\lambda\cdot 1_{A},C_{A}(y))\leq\|y-z\|<{\varepsilon},

thus proving the claim.
∎

Theorem 3.4

Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright A
be an

(ii)

For the proof of both parts of the theorem we use the following set-up: For each intermediate

of

For each

aE_{t}(y)=E_{t}(ay),\qquad E_{t}(y)a=E_{t}(y\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a)),

\alpha_{s}(E_{t}(y))=u_{s}E(yu_{t}^{*})u_{s}^{*}=E(u_{s}yu_{t}^{*}u_{s}^{*})=E_{t}(\alpha_{s}(y)u_{st^{-1}s^{-1}t}).

(3.2)

It is clear that

is a closed linear subspace of

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\alpha\colon\Gamma\curvearrowright A
be an action of a discrete group
\Gamma
on a

-algebra

\Upsilon
be a subgroup of
\Gamma
, and let

\mathrm{supp}(x)\subseteq\Upsilon

Proof.

The “only if” part is clear. Assume that

. We may assume that

is represented on the Hilbert space
\tilde{H}=\ell^{2}(\Gamma,H)=\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes H
via the covariant representation

Write

as a disjoint union of right cosets, and let

be the projection from

onto

(viewed as a subspace of
\tilde{H}
). Let
\Psi
be the ucp map on

given by
\Psi(T)=\sum_{i\in I}E_{i}TE_{i}
. We then have a commuting diagram:

\textstyle{A\rtimes_{r}\Gamma\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathcal{E}}
\scriptstyle{\pi\times\lambda}
\textstyle{B(\tilde{H})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Psi}
\textstyle{A\rtimes_{r}\Upsilon\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi\times\lambda}
\textstyle{B(\tilde{H})}

with

, or, equivalently, that

Question 3.6
.

, and

I_{t}\,\alpha_{t}(I_{s})\subseteq I_{ts}

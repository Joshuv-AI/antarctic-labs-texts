Femtoscopy analysis in small systems at NA61/SHINE
\firstname Barnabás \lastname Pórfy \fnsep for the NA61/SHINE Collaboration

Abstract
Recent measurements of femtoscopic correlations at NA61/SHINE unravel that the shape of the particle emitting source is not Gaussian. The measurements are based on Lévy-stable symmetric sources, and we discuss the average pair transverse mass dependence of the source parameters. One of the parameters, the Lévy exponent α \alpha , is of particular importance. It describes the shape of the source, which, in the vicinity of the critical point of the QCD phase diagram, may be related to the critical exponent η \eta . Its measurement hence may contribute to the search for and characterization of the critical point of the phase diagram.

In our analysis we investigate the phase diagram of QCD using two-pion femtoscopic correlations with spherically symmetric Lévy distributions, defined as:

\mathcal{L}(\alpha,R,\textbf{r})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\textnormal{d}^{3}\vec{\zeta}e^{i\vec{\zeta}\textbf{r}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\vec{\zeta}R|^{\alpha}},

(1)

where
R
is the Lévy scale parameter,
\alpha
is the Lévy stability index,
r
is the vector of spatial coordinates and the vector
\zeta
represents the integration variable. The fundamental phenomenon utilized in femtoscopy is the connection between the spatial momentum correlations,
C(q)
(
q

S(x)
. The latter describes the probability density of particle creation for a relative coordinate
x

C(q)\cong 1+|\tilde{S}(q)|^{2}
, where
\tilde{S}(q)
is the Fourier transform of
S(x)

[
2

The Lévy assumption allows us to describe the shape of the source with a more general approach. In the case of
\alpha=2

\alpha=1

\eta

[
3

(-1-\eta)

\sim r^{-1-\alpha}

\alpha<2

r\equiv|\textbf{r}|
. It has been suggested, in Refs.
[
4

\eta

0.5

[
6

7
]

\alpha

Then, the Lévy exponent of the source distribution can be measured using two-particle femtoscopic correlation functions as
C^{0}_{2}(q)=1+\lambda\cdot e^{-(qR)^{\alpha}},

where
C^{0}_{2}(q)
is the correlation function without final state phenomena.
The equation introduces an additional physical parameter, the correlation strength
\lambda
, interpreted in the core-halo model in Refs.
[
13
,
14
]
. In the limit as
q\rightarrow 0

\lambda=\left(N_{\rm{core}}/(N_{\rm{core}}+N_{\rm{halo}})\right)^{2}

S=S_{\textnormal{core}}+S_{\textnormal{halo}}

The selection of like-charged pion pairs introduces a significant effect from the Coulomb repulsion. To account for this final state effect, the correlation function has to be modified to include the Coulomb correction (denoted by
K_{\textnormal{Coulomb}}
). To this end, the interpolation of Coulomb correction’s functional form is used. The Coulomb correction, along with further final state corrections are described in greater detail in Refs.
[
15
,
16
,
17

18
,
2

Subsequently, the correlation function can be modified to incorporate both the Coulomb correction and the effect of the halo,with the source radii of the core part remaining unaffected, as discussed in Ref.
[
19

[
20

21

is utilized, modifying the fit ansatz as follows:

C_{2}(q)=N\cdot\left(1-\lambda+(1+e^{-|qR|^{\alpha}})\cdot\lambda\cdot K_{\textnormal{Coulomb}}(q)\right),

(2)

where
N
is introduced as normalization parameter. For the case of Ar+Sc, an additional parameter,
\varepsilon

(1+\varepsilon\cdot q)

Intermittency
: Further analyses for the search of the critical point include the measurement of intermittent particle multiplicity fluctuations
[
30
,
31
]
. More precisely, intermittency measurement aims to view the dependence of the second-order scaled factorial moments of proton multiplicity distributions on the number of subdivisions in transverse momentum space. The intermittency analysis uses statistically independent data sets for every subdivision in transverse and cumulative-transverse momentum variables
[
32
,
33
]
.

The scaled factorial moments
F_{r}(M)

F_{r}(M)=\frac{\left\langle\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{M^{2}}n_{m}(n_{m}-1)...(n_{m}-r+1)\right\rangle}{\left\langle\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{M^{2}}n_{m}\right\rangle^{r}}

F_{r}(M)=F_{r}(\Delta)\cdot(M^{D})^{\phi_{q}}

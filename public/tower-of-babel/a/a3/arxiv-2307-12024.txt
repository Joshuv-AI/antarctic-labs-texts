Efficient quantum compression for identically prepared states with arbitrary dimentional
Zeyu Chen, Chunhe Xiong, Kamil Khadiev, Junde Wu

Abstract
In this paper, we present an efficient quantum compression method for identically prepared states with arbitrary dimentional.



I
The Schur transform and related representation theory background

The Schur transform relates to the decomposition of
n

d
-dimensional system
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
, which is fundamental to our compression algorithm. We first recall the basics of representation theory before introducing the decomposition.

A representation of group
\mathcal{G}
is a vector space
V
together with homomorphism
\pi
from
\mathcal{G}
to GL
(V)
, the general linear group on
V
. We say a representation
(\pi,V)
is finite-dimensional if
V
is finite-dimensional. In this paper, we will always consider complex finite dimensional representations and use the term representation to denote them, and we will denote a representation
(\pi,V)
simply by the representation space
V
. For two vector spaces
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, define Hom
(V_{1},V_{2})
be the set of homomorphisms from
V_{1}
to
V_{2}
. If
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are both representations of
\mathcal{G}
with homomorphisms
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
, then the canonical action
\pi
of
\mathcal{G}
on Hom
(V_{1},V_{2})
is given with the following rules

\displaystyle\pi(g)(\varphi)=\pi_{1}(g)\circ\varphi\circ\pi_{2}(g)^{-1}

(1)

where
g\in\mathcal{G}
and with
\varphi\in
Hom
(V_{1},V_{2})
. If
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{C}[G]
-modules, then we say that the two representations
(\pi_{1},V_{1})
and
(\pi_{2},V_{2})
are equivalent and write
V_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{G}}}{{\cong}}V_{2}
. We say a representation
(\pi,V)
is an irreducible representation (irrep) if
V
has exactly
2

\pi
-invariant subspace
\{0\}
and
V
. Let
\Lambda
be the complete set of inequivalent irreps of
\mathcal{G}
, then for any reducible representation
(\pi,V)
we have the following decomposition

\displaystyle\pi(g)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}r_{\lambda}(g)\otimes I_{n_{\lambda}}

(2)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{G}}}{{\cong}}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}V_{\lambda}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n_{\lambda}}

(3)

where
(r_{\lambda},V_{\lambda})
are irreps and
n_{\lambda}
is the multiplicity of the irrep
V_{\lambda}
in
V
.

Consider the two natural representations of the permutation group on the space
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(s)\left|i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|i_{s^{-1}(1)}\ldots i_{s^{-1}(n)}\right\rangle

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}(U)\left|i_{1}\ldots i_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=U^{\otimes n}\left|i_{1}\ldots i_{n}\right\rangle

(5)

where
s\in S_{n}
is a permutation of
n
objects,
s(i)
is the label describing the action of
s
on label
i
, and
U(d)
denote the group of
d\times d
unitary matrices.

Schur Weyl duality provides a simple but wonderful characterization of the decomposition of the tensor space under the joint action of the group
U(d)
and
S(n)
. Formally, it asserts that
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{Q}
commute and the representation space
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
decomposes into sums of products of irreps, with multiplicity 0 or 1, of
S_{n}
and
U(d)
, which are uniquely determined by a partition
[\lambda]=[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d}]
such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{d}\geq 0
and
\Sigma_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}=n
. That are

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}(U)\mathbf{P}(s)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{U}(d)\times\mathcal{S}_{n}}}{{\cong}}\bigoplus_{[\lambda]\in\mathcal{I}_{d,n}}\mathbf{q}_{[\lambda]}(U)\otimes\mathbf{p}_{[\lambda]}(s)

(6)

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{U}(d)\times\mathcal{S}_{n}}}{{\cong}}\bigoplus_{[\lambda]\in\mathcal{I}_{d,n}}\mathcal{Q}_{[\lambda]}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{[\lambda]}

(7)

where
\mathcal{I}_{d,n}
denotes a set of partitions of the integer
n
into
d
summands
[\lambda]
with the corresponding property
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{d}\geq 0
. Equivalently, Schur-Weyl duality states that there exists a basis
|{[\lambda]}\rangle|q_{[\lambda]}\rangle|p_{[\lambda]}\rangle
(which we call the Schur basis) in which the n-fold tensor action of the unitary group
U(d)
and the action of the permutation group
S_{n}
are both block diagonal

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}(U)|[\lambda]\rangle\left|q_{\lambda}\right\rangle\left|p_{[\lambda]}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=|[\lambda]\rangle\left(\mathbf{q}_{[\lambda]}^{d}(U)\left|q_{[\lambda]}\right\rangle\right)\left|p_{[\lambda]}\right\rangle

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(s)|[\lambda]\rangle\left|q_{[\lambda]}\right\rangle\left|p_{[\lambda]}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=|[\lambda]\rangle\left|q_{[\lambda]}\right\rangle\left(\mathbf{p}_{[\lambda]}(s)\left|p_{[\lambda]}\right\rangle\right)

(9)

We call the corresponding transform that maps the standard computational basis states
|i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{n}\rangle
to the Schur basis
Schur transform
. Actually, the Schur transform is a quantum circuit which performs a change of basis from a local qudit-level description of a system to a global symmetry-based representation. The reader can find more information about quantum circuit in
nc2010
.

I.1
The Construction of the Irreps
\mathcal{Q}_{[\lambda]}^{d}
and
\mathcal{P}_{[\lambda]}

In this section we describe the construction of the irreps
\mathcal{Q}_{[\lambda]}^{d}
and
\mathcal{P}_{[\lambda]}
. A general methodology for the construction of these irreps relies on the usage of
Gelfand-Zetlin basis
or
adapted basis
.

A basis of
\mathcal{Q}_{[\lambda]}^{d}
is called the
Gelfand-Zetlin basis
, while that of
\mathcal{P}_{[\lambda]}
is called the
Young-Yamanouchi basis
.

For the irreps of the
d
-dimensional unitary group
U(d)
, every irrep is characterized by a partition
[\lambda]\equiv[\lambda_{i,d}]\equiv[\lambda_{1,d},\lambda_{2,d},\ldots,\lambda_{d,d}]
of
n
non-negative integers obeying the relation
\lambda_{i,d}\geq\lambda_{i+1,d}
; conversely every partition with d integers corresponds to a unique irrep of
U(d)
. To characterize an orthonormal basis (physically, state vectors) of an irrep
[\lambda_{i,d}]
one uses the
Weyl branching law
for
U(d)
. This law asserts that under restriction of
U(d)
to
U(d-1)
the irrep
[\lambda_{i,d}]
is isomorphic to direct sum of distinct irreps
[\lambda_{i,d-1}]
of
U(d-1)
, where the non-negative integers
[\lambda_{i,d-1}]
satisfy the
betweenness conditions
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{id}\geq\lambda_{i,d-1}\geq\lambda_{i+1,d}

(10)

If two partition s
[\lambda]
and
[\mu]
satisfy the above condition, then we say that
[\mu]

interlaces

[\lambda]
and write
[\mu]\preceq[\lambda]
. It follows that a subgroup adapted basis of irrep
[\lambda_{i{\color[rgb]{1,0,0},}d}]
can be determined uniquely by
\{\lambda_{ij}\}
of the chain of subgroups
U(1)\subset U(2)\subset\cdots\subset U(d)
, where the irreps of
U(1)
are trivially one-dimentional.

An elegant notation corresponding to such a subgroup adapted basis is the
Gelfand pattern

(\lambda)
, a triangular array of
\frac{d(d+1)}{2}
non-negative integers
(\lambda)=

\{\lambda_{ij}\}
arranged as

\displaystyle\small\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1{\color[rgb]{1,0,0},}d}&&\lambda_{2{\color[rgb]{1,0,0},}d}&&\cdots&&\lambda_{d-1,d}&&\lambda_{d,d}\\
&\lambda_{1,d-1}&&\lambda_{2,d-1}&&\cdots&&\lambda_{d-1,d-1}&\\
&&&&\cdots&&&&\\
&&&\ \lambda_{1{\color[rgb]{1,0,0},}2}&&\ \lambda_{2{\color[rgb]{1,0,0},}2}&&&\\
&&&&\lambda_{1{\color[rgb]{1,0,0},}1}&&&&\\
\end{pmatrix}

(11)

The Gelfand pattern
(\lambda)
is also denoted by
\left(\begin{aligned} {}_{d}\\
(&\lambda)_{d-1}\\
\end{aligned}\right)
, where
[\lambda]_{d}
is the partition denoting an irrep in
U(d)
.

For the irreps of the permutation group
S_{n}
, every irrep is characterized by a partition
[\lambda]=[\lambda_{i}]
too, with
\sum\limits_{i{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}=1}}^{d}\lambda_{i}=n
. The way to characterize a basis is somewhat similar. We use the branching rule again, asserting that under restriction of
S_{n}
to
S_{n-1}
the irrep
[\lambda]
is isomorphic to direct sum of distinct irreps
[\mu]
with
\sum\limits_{i}\mu_{i}=n-1
and
\lambda_{i}-1\leq\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
. For convenience we denote such relationship between
[\lambda]
and
[\mu]
as
[\mu]\in[\lambda]-\square
. It follows that a subgroup adapted basis of irrep
[\lambda]
can be determined by
n
partitions
\{[\lambda]_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
satisfying
[\lambda]_{n}=[\lambda]
.

II
Construction of the compression algorithm

Since
|\phi\rangle^{\otimes n}
is obviously a symmetric state(invariant under the action of
P_{n}
), according to Schur-Weyl duality, it has the following property:

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle^{\otimes n}=\sum C^{[\lambda]}_{q_{[\lambda]},p_{[\lambda]}}|[\lambda\rangle]|q_{[\lambda]}\rangle|p_{[\lambda]}\rangle

(12)

where the sum is over all
\lambda
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{[\lambda]}(s)|p_{[\lambda]}\rangle=|p_{[\lambda]}\rangle

(13)

holds for every
s\in P_{n}
. From the construction of the irreps of
S_{n}
[4,6], we know the corresponding irrep is the trivial representation removed with labeled by
[n]
. Thus, in order to give an efficient scheme of compression for identically prepared states, we can simply implement the Schur transform.

The direct implementation of Schur transform is difficult. By introducing the CG transform, we can give a scheme to perform the recursion step in the construction of the Schur transform.

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{[\mu]}^{d}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{[\nu]}^{d}([\mu]\in\mathcal{I}_{d,n}
and
[\nu]\in\mathcal{I}_{d,m}
) be two irreps of the unitary group
U(d)
acting on qudits. Note that the tensor product
\mathcal{Q}_{[\mu]}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{[\nu]}^{d}
is also a representation of
U(d)
, then it can decompose into direct sum of irreps:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{[\mu]}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{[\nu]}^{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{U}(d)}}{{\cong}}\bigoplus_{[\lambda]\in\mathcal{I}_{d,n+m}}\mathcal{Q}_{[\lambda]}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{[\lambda]}_{[\mu],[\nu]}

(14)

where
\mathcal{M}^{[\lambda]}_{[\mu],[\nu]}
is the multiplicity space associated to
[\mu],[\nu]
and
[\lambda]
. Then
,
we call the transform implementing this isomorphism the
Clebsch-Gorden (CG) transform
.

For our purpose, we specialize in the case of
[\nu]\equiv[1]
, for which the CG transform is much simpler than the general case:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{[\mu]}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{[1]}^{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{U}(d)}}{{\cong}}\bigoplus_{[\lambda]\in\mu+\square}\mathcal{Q}_{[\lambda]}^{d}

(15)

where
[\mu]+\square
denotes, similar to the above definition, the set of
[\lambda]
such that
\sum\limits_{i}\lambda_{i}=n+1
and
\lambda_{i}-1\leq\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
. We also denote
[\lambda]=[\mu]+e_{j}
where
\lambda_{j}=\mu_{j}+1
, and
e_{j}
is.

Explicitly, a CG transform
\mathbf{U}_{CG}^{d}
is given as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{d}_{CG}|(\mu)\rangle|i\rangle=\sum_{[\mu]+e_{j}\in[\mu]+\square\atop(\lambda)\in\mathcal{Q}_{[\mu+e_{j}]}^{d}}C_{[\mu],i}^{[\lambda]}|(\lambda)\rangle

(16)

for some coefficients
C_{[\mu],i}^{[\lambda]}\in\mathbb{C}
, where
(\mu)
is a vector in
\mathcal{Q}^{d}_{[\mu]}
and
|i\rangle
is a state in
\mathcal{Q}^{d}_{[1]}
.

We can construct the Schur transform by recursive implementation of CG transforms. Suppose we start with input
|i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}=(\mathcal{Q}_{[1]}^{d})^{\otimes n}
, it suffices to decompose
(\mathcal{Q}_{[1]}^{d})^{\otimes n}
into
U(d)-
irreps since Schur-Weyl duality tells us that the multiplicity space of
U(d)-
irreps must be isomorphic to irreps of
S_{n}
. To perform the decomposition, we simply apply
n-1
uses of CG transform, one at a time. We start by inputing
|i_{1}\rangle\in\mathcal{Q}_{[\mu]}^{d}=\mathcal{Q}_{[1]}^{d}
and
|i_{2}\rangle\in\mathcal{Q}_{[1]}^{d}
to obtain a superposition of
|[1]\rangle|(\lambda)_{2}\rangle
(see eq.(
16
)). Then, we apply an another CG transform to
|(\lambda)_{2}\rangle|i_{3}\rangle
respectively, remaining
|[\lambda]_{1}\rangle=|[1]\rangle
as part of output. After that, we obtain a superposition of
|[\lambda]_{2}\rangle|(\lambda)_{3}\rangle
and we continue to apply the next CG transform. The procedure is repeated for
k=1,\ldots,n-1
and the result is a superposition of sequences of partitions
|[\lambda]_{k}\rangle
, and
|(\lambda)_{n}\rangle
.

Let us inspect our output

C_{(\lambda)_{n}}^{[\lambda]_{1},\ldots,[\lambda]_{n-1}}|[\lambda]_{1},\ldots,[\lambda]_{n-1}\rangle|(\lambda)_{n}\rangle

for some coefficients
C_{(\lambda)_{n}}^{[\lambda]_{1},\ldots,[\lambda]_{n-1}}\in\mathbb{C}
. Recall the Schur basis
|{[\lambda]}\rangle|q_{[\lambda]}\rangle|p_{[\lambda]}\rangle
, we find that
|[\lambda]\rangle=|[\lambda]_{n}\rangle
, and
|q_{[\lambda]}\rangle=|(\lambda)_{n}\rangle
. Moreover, it is natural that
|[\lambda]_{k+1}\rangle=|[\lambda]_{k}\rangle+e_{j_{k}}
for some
j_{k}\in[d]
. Then
|[\lambda]_{1},\ldots,[\lambda]_{n-1}\rangle
, together with
|[\lambda]_{n}\rangle
, forms a basis vector in some
S_{n}
-irrep
\mathcal{P}_{[\lambda]}
. Thus we obtain our desired result.

At
d=1
, the CG transform is trivial, consisting of the map
i\rightarrow i+1
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. For the case of
d=2
, CG transform can be viewed as a map from the basis of tensor products of the basis vectors of the subsystems to the basis composed of eigenvectors of the composite spin operators, which can be explicitly and efficiently calculated using classical algorithm [7]. However, the general implementation of CG transform is difficult. To solve this problem, we give a recursive construction of CG transform. Our construction follows the work of [8], where the CG transform
\mathbf{U}^{d}_{CG}
is expressed as a composite of CG transforms
\mathbf{U}^{d-1}_{CG}
and the so-called
reduced Wigner operators
.

A CG transform
\mathbf{U}^{d}_{CG}
have the following decomposition:

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{d}_{CG}|(\mu)_{n}\rangle|i\rangle=\sum_{j^{\prime}=0,1,...,n}\Phi\circ\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}^{d-1}_{CG}|(\mu)_{n}\rangle|i\rangle

(17)

where
\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}^{d-1}_{CG}
is an extension of the operator
\mathbf{U}^{d-1}_{CG}
that maps
|(\mu)_{n}\rangle|d\rangle
to
|[\mu]_{n}\rangle|(\mu)_{n-1}\rangle
, and
\Phi
is so-called reduced Wigner transform that maps
|[\mu]_{n}\rangle|[\mu]_{m-1}\rangle
to
|(\lambda)_{n}\rangle=|[\mu]_{n}+e_{j}\rangle|[\mu]_{n-1}+e_{j^{\prime}}\rangle
(
|[\mu]_{n-1}+e_{j^{\prime}}\rangle
is obtained from
\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}^{d-1}_{CG}
), And the coefficients of the operator can be calculated by the following formula:

\begin{split}&\langle[\mu]_{n}+e_{j}|\langle[\mu]_{n-1}+e_{j^{\prime}}|\Phi|[\mu]_{n}\rangle|[\mu]_{n-1}\rangle\\
&=\begin{cases}S(j^{\prime}-j)\left[\frac{\prod\limits_{s\in[d-1]\backslash j^{\prime}}(\tilde{\mu}_{j,n}-\tilde{\mu}_{s,n-1})\prod\limits_{t\in[d]\backslash j}(\tilde{\mu}_{j^{\prime},n-1}-\tilde{\mu}_{t,n}+1)}{\prod\limits_{s\in[d-1]\backslash j^{\prime}}(\tilde{\mu}_{j^{\prime},n-1}-\tilde{\mu}_{s,n-1}+1)\prod\limits_{t\in[d]\backslash j}(\tilde{\mu}_{j,n}-\tilde{\mu}_{s,n})}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{ if }j^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\},\\
S(d-j)\left[\frac{\prod_{s\in[d-1]}(\tilde{\mu}_{j,n}-\tilde{\mu}_{s,n-1})}{\prod_{t\in[d]\backslash j}(\tilde{\mu}_{j,n}-\tilde{\mu}_{s,n})}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{ if }j^{\prime}=0.\end{cases}\end{split}

(18)

where
\tilde{\mu}_{ks}=\mu_{ks}+s-k
,
S(x)=1
if
x>0
and
S(x)=-1
otherwise.

III
Analysis and modification

Let
{\cal C}_{d,n}=\{(c_{1},\dots,c_{d}):c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots+c_{d}=n,0\leq c_{1},\dots,c_{d}\leq n\}
. Direct calculation yields

\begin{split}|\phi\rangle^{\otimes n}=\sum\limits_{(c_{1},\dots,c_{d})\in{\cal C}}\ \prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}^{c_{i}}\\
\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}P(s)\left(\bigotimes\limits_{i=1,2,\dots,d}|i-1\rangle^{\otimes c_{i}}\right)\end{split}

(19)

where

|\phi\rangle=\alpha_{1}|0\rangle+\alpha_{2}|1\rangle+\cdots+\alpha_{d}|d-1\rangle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}.}

(20)

Then,

|c_{1};c_{2};\cdots;c_{d}\rangle=\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}P(s)\left(\bigotimes\limits_{i=1,2,\dots,d}|i-1\rangle^{\otimes c_{i}}\right)

is a basis of the subspace of all states
|\phi^{\otimes n}\rangle
with
c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots+c_{d}=n
(these states are obviously independent). The total number of
|c_{1};c_{2};\cdots;c_{d}\rangle
is equal to the number of different Gelfand patterns of partition
[n]
, that is, the dimension of
\mathcal{Q}_{[n]}^{d}
. Actually, our algorithm transforms
|c_{1};c_{2};\cdots;c_{d}\rangle
to
|0\rangle,|1\rangle,\dots,|dim(\mathcal{Q}_{[n]}^{d})-1\rangle
. Thus the algorithm is optimal with respect to space. The dimension of this space can be obtained with techniques of combinatorial mathematics, with result

dim(\mathcal{Q}_{[n]}^{d})=\begin{pmatrix}n+d-1\\
d-1\end{pmatrix}

(21)

Then, if
d
is not too large (that is,
d
can be regarded constant), then the space to memory the information of

is polynomial of
n
.

be the time complexity (in terms of numbers of local operations and C-NOT gates) of

with
n

. By its recursive construction, we have

is the time complexity of performing

on dimension d and partition of
\leq n
. Then, we turn our attention to

It is easy to verify that the implementation of
Algorithm 2
requires exponential complexity since
\Phi
is an

|\phi\rangle^{\otimes n}
to a state in
\mathcal{Q}_{[n]}^{d}
, and by above discussion states in
\mathcal{Q}_{[k]}^{d}
occur after CG transform only if the input state belongs to
\mathcal{Q}_{[\mu]}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{[1]}^{d}
where

, that is,

. Thus, for the purpose of compression, we take no care about the action on states that is not in
\mathcal{Q}_{[k]}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{[1]}^{d}
. Thus the implementation of

.

.

For a basis

, If
d=1

2.
Output
|(\lambda)\rangle
with
|(\lambda)\rangle=|(\mu)\rangle+e_{1}
is a partition.

3.
Else

4.
If
i<d

5.
Perform the
(d-1)
-dim CG transform on

and output the superposition of

. Record the coefficient if

.

)

A little details of the algorithm should be clarified. The implementation of the algorithm uses classical computation as a subroutine. It is clear that the calculation of
\Phi^{\prime}
as a unitary matrix is classical. The process of such calculation is as follows. Firstly, we calculate the elements with

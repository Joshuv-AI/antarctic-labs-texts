Mean square of the error term in the asymmetric many dimensional divisor problem
Xiaodong Cao, Yoshio Tanigawa, Wenguang Zhai

Abstract
Let 𝒂 = ( a 1 , a 2 , … , a k ) \boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}) , where a j ​ ( j = 1 , … , k ) a_{j}\ (j=1,\ldots,k) are positive integers such that a 1 ≤ a 2 ≤ ⋯ ≤ a k a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{k} . Let d ( 𝒂 ; n ) = ∑ n 1 a 1 ⋯ n k a k = n 1 d(\boldsymbol{a};n)=\sum_{n_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{a_{k}}=n}1 and Δ ⁡ ( 𝒂 , x ) \Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x) be the error term of the summatory function of d ⁡ ( 𝒂 , n ) d(\boldsymbol{a};n) . In this paper we show an asymptotic formula of the mean square of Δ ⁡ ( 𝒂 , x ) \Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x) under a certain condition. Furthermore, in the cases k = 2 k=2 and 3, we give unconditional asymptotic formulas for these mean squares.

1
Introduction and the statement of results

Let
k
be a fixed positive integer and
x\geq 1
. Let
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
, where
a_{j}\ (j=1,\ldots,k)

are positive integers such that
a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{k}
. Let
d(\boldsymbol{a};n)
denote the number of representations of
an integer
n
in the form
n=n_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{a_{k}}
;

d(\boldsymbol{a};n)=\sum_{n_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{a_{k}}=n}1.

(1.1)

We define the error term

\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x):=\mathop{{\;\,{\sum}^{\prime}}}_{n\leq x}d(\boldsymbol{a};n)-H(\boldsymbol{a};x),

where
H(\boldsymbol{a};x)
is the main term of the summatory function of
d(\boldsymbol{a};n)
, which is given by the sum of residues of
\prod_{j=1}^{k}\zeta(a_{j}s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}
,
and
′
in the summation symbol means that the last term
d(\boldsymbol{a};x)
should be halved when
x
is an integer.
Asymmetric many dimensional divisor problem (or the general divisor problem) is to study the behaviour of
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x)
.
See also Ivić
[
7
]
and Krätzel
[
10
]
, or the survey paper
[
9
]
.

When
a_{1}=a_{2}=1
,
d(1,1;n)=\sum_{d|n}1,\ \Delta(1,1;x)=\sum_{n\leq x}d(1,1,;n)-x(\log x+2\gamma-1)
,
(
\gamma
is the Euler constant), we have the classical Dirichlet divisor problem.
Dirichlet proved
\Delta(1,1;x)=O(x^{1/2})
by his famous hyperbola method. The exponent
1/2
was improved
by many researchers. The latest result is

\Delta(x)=O(x^{131/416}(\log x)^{26947/8320})

due to Huxley
[
6
]
. For the lower bounds, it is known that

\Delta(1,1;x)=\Omega_{+}\left(x^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log x)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log\log x)^{\frac{3+\log 4}{4}}\exp(-c\sqrt{\log\log\log x})\right)\ (c>0)

and

\Delta(1,1;x)=\Omega_{-}\left(x^{\frac{1}{4}}\exp(c^{\prime}(\log\log x)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log\log\log x)^{-\frac{3}{4}})\right)\ (c^{\prime}>0),

which are due to Hafner
[
5
]
and Corrádi and Kátai
[
3
]
, respectively. Many corresponding results for asymmetric many dimensional divisor
problem can be found in
[
7
]
and
[
10
]
.

The mean square estimate is one of the main problems in the theory of divisor problem.
Let
R(T)
be the error term defined by

\int_{1}^{T}\Delta^{2}(1,1;x)dx=cT^{3/2}+R(T),

where
c
is a constant given by
c=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d(1,1;n)^{2}}{n^{3/2}}
. Cramér
[
4
]
first proved that

R(T)=O(T^{5/4+\varepsilon}).

The above estimate of
R(T)
was improved to

R(T)=O(T\log^{5}T)

(1.2)

by Tong
[
12
]
and recently to
R(T)=O(T\log^{3}T\log\log T)
by Lau and Tsang
[
11
]
. Tong’s method which derives (
1.2
)
is the initial motivation of our previous paper
[
2
]
.

Ivić
[
8
]
studied the upper bound and
\Omega
-result of the mean square of
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x)
for general
k
.
As for the upper bound, he proved that if

\int_{1}^{T}\Delta^{2}(\boldsymbol{a};x)dx\ll T^{1+2\beta_{k}}\ \ (\beta_{k}\geq 0)

then
\beta_{k}\geq g_{k}
, where

g_{k}=\frac{r-1}{2(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r})}

and
r
is the largest integer such that

(r-2)a_{r}\leq a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r-1}\quad(2\leq r\leq k)

[
8
, (1.5)]
. Furthermore, he showed that the estimate
\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta(1/2+it)|^{2k-2}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}
implies
\beta_{k}=g_{k}
.
In particular,
\beta_{k}=g_{k}
holds for
k=2
and
3
. For the lower bound, he showed that

\int_{1}^{T}\Delta^{2}(\boldsymbol{a};x)dx=\Omega(T^{1+2g_{k}}\log^{A}T)

with some constant
A\geq 0
. From these evidence, he conjectured that

\int_{1}^{T}\Delta^{2}(\boldsymbol{a};x)dx=(E_{k}+o(1))T^{1+2g_{k}}\log^{A}T

(1.3)

for general
k
with some constants
E_{k}>0
and
A\geq 0

[
8
, (5.7)]
.

Ivić’s conjecture (
1.3
) was solved by Cao and Zhai
[
13
]
in the case
k=2
.
More precisely they proved that

\int_{1}^{T}\Delta^{2}(\boldsymbol{a};x)dx=c(\boldsymbol{a})T^{\frac{1+a_{1}+a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}}+O\left(T^{\frac{1+a_{1}+a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}-\frac{a_{1}}{2a_{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})(a_{1}+a_{2}-1)}}\log^{\frac{7}{2}}T\right),

(1.4)

where
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are integers such that
1\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}
,
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},a_{2})
and
c(\boldsymbol{a})
is some constant.
Their method is based on the transformation formula of the exponential sum applied to Chowla and Walum type representation
of
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x)
(see also
[
1
]
).
When
a_{1}=a_{2}=1
, the error term in (
1.4
) becomes
O(T^{\frac{5}{4}}\log^{\frac{7}{2}}T)
. Hence it can be said that
(
1.4
) corresponds to the result of Cramér.

In this paper we shall study the mean square estimate of the error term
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x)
more closely by the Tong’s method
[
2
,
12
]
.
For this purpose, we need an auxiliary divisor function defined by

\hat{d}(\boldsymbol{a};n)=\sum_{n_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{a_{k}}=n}n_{1}^{a_{1}-1}\cdots n_{k}^{a_{k}-1}.

(1.5)

This function can be regarded as a dual function of
d(\boldsymbol{a};n)
.
For notational convenience we put

b(n)=\pi^{2\alpha-k/2}\hat{d}(\boldsymbol{a};n)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu_{n}=\pi^{2\alpha}n,

where

\alpha=(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k})/2.

By (
1.1
) and (
1.5
), it is easy to see that

\varphi(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d(\boldsymbol{a};n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\zeta(a_{j}s)\qquad{\rm Re\,}s>1/a_{1}

and

\displaystyle\psi(s):=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b(n)}{\mu_{n}^{s}}=\pi^{2\alpha-k/2-2\alpha s}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\hat{d}(\boldsymbol{a};n)}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{2\alpha-k/2-2\alpha s}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\zeta(a_{j}s-a_{j}+1)\qquad{\rm Re\,}s>1.

(1.6)

Let
1/2\leq\sigma^{\ast}<1
be a real number defined by

\sigma^{\ast}:=\inf\left\{\sigma\ \Big|\ \int_{0}^{T}|\psi(\sigma+it)|^{2}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}\right\}

(1.7)

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Clearly we have

\sigma^{\ast}\geq 1-\frac{1}{2a_{k}}.

(1.8)

In this paper we suppose that
\sigma^{\ast}
satisfies the condition

\sigma^{\ast}<1-\frac{k-1}{4\alpha}.

(1.9)

This condition plays an important role in Tong’s theory. From (
1.8
), we note that (
1.9
) implies,
as a necessary condition, that

(k-2)a_{k}<a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k-1}.

(1.10)

We first show an asymptotic formula of the mean square of
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a},x)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(k-2)a_{k}<a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k-1}
and suppose that
\sigma^{\ast}
satisfies (
1.9
). Then we have

\int_{1}^{T}\Delta^{2}(\boldsymbol{a};x)dx=c(\boldsymbol{a})T^{1+\frac{k-1}{2\alpha}}+O\left(T^{1+\frac{k-1}{2\alpha}-\eta(\boldsymbol{a})+\varepsilon}\right),

(1.11)

where
c(\boldsymbol{a})
is a certain positive constant and

\eta(\boldsymbol{a})=\frac{2(1-\sigma^{\ast})-\frac{k-1}{2\alpha}}{2\alpha(3-2\sigma^{\ast}-\frac{1}{a_{k}})-1}.

(1.12)

It is an important problem to determine the exact value of
\sigma^{\ast}
. Generally it is a very difficult problem,
but if we assume the Lindelöf hypothesis we can see easily that
\sigma^{\ast}=1-1/2a_{k}
. Hence from Theorem 1 we have

Corollary 1
.

Suppose that the Lindelöf hypothesis is true. If
(k-2)a_{k}<a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k-1}
, we have

\int_{1}^{T}\Delta^{2}(\boldsymbol{a};x)dx=c(\boldsymbol{a})T^{1+\frac{k-1}{2\alpha}}+O\left(T^{1+\frac{k-1}{2\alpha}-\frac{2\alpha-(k-1)a_{k}}{2\alpha(2\alpha-1)a_{k}}+\varepsilon}\right).

where
c(\boldsymbol{a})
is a certain positive constant.

When
k=2
, we find that
\sigma^{\ast}=1-1/2a_{2}
unconditionally.
This is the consequence from the theorem on the fourth power moment of the Riemann zeta function. Hence (
1.11
) gives

Theorem 2
.

In the case
k=2
, let
a_{1}\leq a_{2}
. Then we have

\int_{1}^{T}\Delta^{2}(a_{1},a_{2};x)dx=c_{2}T^{1+\frac{1}{a_{1}+a_{2}}}+O\left(T^{1+\frac{1}{a_{1}+a_{2}}-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})(a_{1}+a_{2}-1)}+\varepsilon}\right),

(1.13)

where
c_{2}
is a certain positive constant.

This theorem improves the error term of (
1.4
).
We note that if we let
a_{1}=a_{2}=1

the error term in (
1.13
) is
O(T^{1+\varepsilon})
, so it can be said that
(
1.13
) corresponds to the result of Tong (
1.2
) except
T^{\varepsilon}
factor.

Another interesting case is
k=3
. We can show that

Theorem 3
.

Let
k=3
. If
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq a_{3}
and
a_{3}<a_{1}+a_{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}\Delta^{2}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3};x)dx=c_{3}T^{1+\frac{2}{a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}}}+O(T^{1+\frac{2}{a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}}-\eta_{3}+\varepsilon}),

where

\eta_{3}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})(3+2(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})(1-1/a_{3}))}\qquad\mbox{if \ $3(a_{2}+a_{3})\leq 7a_{1}$,}\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{4a_{1}a_{3}}{(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})\bigl((a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})(a_{1}+3a_{2}+3a_{3})(a_{3}-1)+a_{3}(5a_{1}+3a_{2}+3a_{3})\bigr)}\\[10.00002pt]
\hskip 85.35826pt\mbox{if \ $3(a_{2}+a_{3})>7a_{1},3a_{3}+a_{1}\leq 5a_{2}$ and $3a_{3}<a_{1}+3a_{2}$,}\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}-a_{3}}{a_{3}(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}-1)}\qquad\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and
c_{3}
is a certain positive constant.

We prove Theorem 3 in Section 4.

2
The Tong-type identity of
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x)

In
[
12
]
, Tong studied the mean square of
\Delta(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{k};x)
. By using the functional equation of
\zeta^{k}(s)

he derived a very useful identity of
\Delta(1,\ldots,1;x)
, which we call the Tong-type identity,
where the first finite sum is the same as that of the truncated Voronoï formula, while its error term is represented by the integrals
like (
2.6
) below.

In our case, by the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function

\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s)=\pi^{-(1-s)/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)\zeta(1-s),

we find easily that the functional equation of
\varphi(s)
and
\psi(s)
has a form

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(s)\varphi(s)=\Delta_{2}(1-s)\psi(1-s),

(2.1)

where

\Delta_{1}(s):=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma\left(\frac{a_{j}s}{2}\right)

(2.2)

and

\Delta_{2}(s):=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma\left(\frac{a_{j}s-a_{j}+1}{2}\right).

(2.3)

Note that
\hat{d}(\boldsymbol{a};n)
does not satisfy the Ramanujan conjecture and also the gamma factors on the left and right hand side of (
2.1
)
are not the same for general
\boldsymbol{a}
, so the pair of Dirichlet series
\varphi(s)
and
\psi(s)
is not contained in the so-called Selberg class.
In our previous paper
[
2
]
, we developed the theory of the Tong-type identity of the error term for such a pair of Dirichlet series.
In fact, we assumed that the two Dirchlet series
\varphi(s)
and
\psi(s)
satisfy the functional equation of the form

\Delta_{1}(s)\varphi(s)=\Delta_{2}(r-s)\psi(r-s)
, where
\Delta_{j}(s)
is the product of Gamma functions, and derived the Tong-type identity
of the error term of the
\varrho
-th Riesz mean of the coefficients of
\varphi(s)
.

In order to write up the Tong-type identity for
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x)
in the present case,
we follow the same notation of
[
2
]
. From (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we have (we define
\alpha
again for its importance.)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}}{2},\ \ \ r=1,

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1-k}{2},\ \ \ \mu^{\prime}=\sum_{j}\left(-\frac{a_{j}}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}=-\alpha+\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\log a_{j},\ \ \ \nu^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}a_{j}\log a_{j},

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}a_{j}\log a_{j}=\lambda^{\prime},

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=2\alpha e^{-\frac{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}{2\alpha}}=2\alpha\prod_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{-a_{j}/\alpha}

and

\theta_{\varrho}=\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}+\varrho\left(1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha}.

In this paper we only consider the case
\varrho=0
, hence

\displaystyle\theta_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha}=\frac{k-1}{4\alpha}.

(2.4)

We also need

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}-r-1=\frac{k+1}{4\alpha}-2.

(2.5)

In Tong’s theory, it is important to approximate
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x)
by the
K
-th averaging integral

\int_{\mathbf{E}_{K}}\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};\tilde{y})d\mathrm{Y}_{K},

where we use the notation

\int_{\mathbf{E}_{K}}g(\tilde{y})d\mathrm{Y}_{K}=\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}g(\tilde{y})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{K},

with

\tilde{y}=y+\frac{1}{x}(y_{1}+\cdots+y_{K})

for an integrable function
g(y)
. Let
\hat{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{a};x)
be the
error term of the summatory function of
\hat{d}(\boldsymbol{a};n)
, which is defined by the same way as
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};x)
.
Then the averaging integral can be expressed by the function defined by

I(\lambda,M,N,y)=2\pi i\int_{M}^{N}u^{\lambda}\hat{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{a};u)\exp\left(-ih(uy)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\right)du.

(2.6)

The next lemma gives the Tong-type identity of
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};y)
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
1\leq x\leq y\leq(1+\delta)x
,
N=[x^{4\alpha-1-\varepsilon}]
and
J=[(4\alpha^{2}r+4\alpha)\varepsilon^{-1}]
,
where
\delta
is a small positive constant.
In every subinterval
[t,t+Bt^{1-1/2\alpha}]\subset[1,\sqrt{N}]
, there exists
M\neq\mu_{n}
such that the following Tong-type
identity holds:

\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};y)=\sum_{j=1}^{7}R_{j}(y),

where

\displaystyle R_{1}(y)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{0}y^{\theta_{0}}\sum_{\mu_{n}\leq M}\frac{b(n)}{\mu_{n}^{1-\theta_{0}}}\cos(h(y\mu_{n})^{1/2\alpha}+c_{0}\pi)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{0}\pi^{2\alpha(\theta_{0}-1)}y^{\theta_{0}}\sum_{n\leq M^{\prime}}\frac{b(n)}{n^{1-\theta_{0}}}\cos(h\pi(yn)^{1/2\alpha}+c_{0}\pi)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{0}\pi^{2\alpha\theta_{0}-k/2}y^{\theta_{0}}\sum_{n\leq M^{\prime}}\frac{\hat{d}(\boldsymbol{a};n)}{n^{1-\theta_{0}}}\cos(h\pi(yn)^{1/2\alpha}+c_{0}\pi),

\displaystyle R_{2}(y)

\displaystyle=y^{\theta_{0}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}{\rm Re\,}\{c_{00}I(\lambda_{0},M,N,y)\},

\displaystyle R_{3}(y)

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\sum_{l=0}^{J}\sum_{m=0}^{J}}}_{l+m>0}{\rm Re\,}\left\{c_{lm}I\left(\lambda_{0}+\frac{l-m}{2\alpha},M,N,y\right)\right\}x^{-l}y^{-l+\theta_{0}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}+\frac{l-m}{2\alpha}},

\displaystyle R_{4}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{K}\sum_{m=0}^{K}{\rm Re\,}\left\{c_{jm}^{\prime}I\left(\lambda_{0}-\frac{K+m}{2\alpha},N,\infty,y+\frac{j}{x}\right)\right\}x^{K}\left(y+\frac{j}{x}\right)^{K+\theta_{0}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\frac{K+m}{2\alpha}},

\displaystyle R_{5}(y)

\displaystyle=x^{\frac{k-3}{4\alpha}}M^{\max(\frac{k-3}{4\alpha},0)+\varepsilon}+x^{\frac{k+1}{4\alpha-2}}M^{\frac{k+1}{4\alpha}+\varepsilon}+x^{\frac{k-1}{4\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}M^{\omega_{1}-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{k-1}{4\alpha}}

\displaystyle\quad+x^{(4\alpha-1)(1+\omega_{1})-2K+\frac{k}{2\alpha}+\frac{2K}{\alpha}-6\alpha},

\displaystyle R_{6}(y)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle R_{7}(y)

\displaystyle=\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};y)-\int_{{\mathbf{E}_{K}}}\Delta(\boldsymbol{a};\tilde{y})d\mathrm{Y}_{K},

where
M^{\prime}=M/\pi^{2\alpha}
and
\kappa_{0}\neq 0,c_{00},c_{lm},c_{jm}^{\prime}
are certain constants,
K
is a suitably large integer and
\omega_{1}<1
is
a certain constant.

This is Theorem 7 of
[
2
]
. We need one remark on
R_{6}(y)
. In fact
R_{6}(y)
is given as

\displaystyle R_{6}(y)\ll\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if $b(n)\geq 0$},\\
x^{\theta_{0}}M^{\omega_{0}-1+\frac{k-1}{4\alpha}},&\mbox{if $b(n)\ll n^{\omega_{0}}$}.\end{cases}

In our case we can take
R_{6}(y)=0
since
b(n)=\pi^{2\alpha-k/2}\hat{d}(\boldsymbol{a},n)
is always positive.

We recall important evaluations of the integral of
I(\lambda,M,N,y)
which we need in the next section.

Lemma 2
.

Let
M<N<x^{A}
(
A
is a fixed positive number),
w
be a real number and
0<\mu<\frac{M}{2}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{(1+\delta)x}I(\lambda,M,N,y)y^{w}\cos(h(\mu y)^{1/2\alpha}+c_{0}\pi)dy

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\ll x^{w+1-3/4\alpha+\varepsilon}\max_{M\leq P\leq N}P^{\lambda+\sigma^{\ast}+1-3/4\alpha}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
2(\lambda+\sigma^{\ast})\neq-1,\ M<N<x^{A}
(
A
is a fixed positive number) and
\delta>0
with

(1+\delta)^{1/\alpha}-1<1/4
. Then we have

\int_{x}^{(1+\delta)x}|I(\lambda,M,N,y)|^{2}dy\ll x^{1-1/\alpha+\varepsilon}\max_{M\leq P\leq N}P^{2(\lambda+\sigma^{\ast}+1)-1/\alpha}.

Lemma 4
.

Let
2(\lambda+\sigma^{\ast})\neq-1,2(\lambda+\sigma^{\ast}+1)<1/\alpha,M\geq 1
and
\delta>0
with

(1+\delta)^{1/\alpha}-1<1/4
. Then we have

\int_{x}^{(1+\delta)x}|I(\lambda,M,\infty,y)|^{2}dy\ll x^{1-1/\alpha+\varepsilon}M^{2(\lambda+\sigma^{\ast}+1)-1/\alpha}.

These lemmas are Lemmas 8, 9 and 10 of
[

]
, respectively.
See

]
for details.

3
Mean square of
\Delta(\boldsymbol{a},x)

In the asymmetric many dimensional divisor problem, the number
(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)/2=-\alpha+k/2
plays an important role.
Though the proof of Theorem 1 goes in a similar way to
[
2
, Theorem 8]
, we shall give a detailed proof for the sake of completeness.

Let

K_{1}(y)=R_{1}(y)+R_{2}(y)

and

K_{2}(y)=\sum_{j=3}^{7}R_{j}(y).

It is sufficient to evaluate the integral
\int_{x}^{(1+\delta)x}(K_{1}(y)+K_{2}(y))^{2}dy
for
1\leq x<T
,
where
\delta
is some fixed positive number.

We need the upper bound of the sum of
\hat{d}\,^{2}(\boldsymbol{a},n)
. Moreover we can prove

Lemma 5
.

Let
x>1
. Then we have

x^{2-1/a_{k}}\ll\sum_{n\leq x}\hat{d}\,^{2}(\boldsymbol{a};n)\ll x^{2-1/a_{k}+\varepsilon}.

(3.1)

Proof.

By Cauchy’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\hat{d}\,^{2}(\boldsymbol{a};n)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{a_{k}}=n}n_{1}^{a_{1}-1}\cdots n_{k}^{a_{k}-1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{a_{k}}=n}1\times\sum_{n_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{a_{k}}=n}n_{1}^{2(a_{1}-1)}\cdots n_{k}^{2(a_{k}-1)}

\displaystyle\ll n^{\varepsilon}c(\boldsymbol{a};n),

where

This completes the proof of Theorem 1. ∎

4
Proof of Theorem 3

In order to prove Theorem 3 we need some preparation. Define
m(\sigma)
(
1/2\leq\sigma<1
) as the supremum of all numbers
m
such that

\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^{m}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}.

It is known that
m(\sigma)\geq 4
for
\sigma\geq 1/2
,
m(7/12)\geq 6
and
m(5/8)\geq 8
.
Ivić studied
m(\sigma)
in great detail. One can find a lower bound in
[
7
, Theorem 8.4]
.
Here we can suppose that
m(\sigma)
is continuous.
Especially we have the following simpler but a little weaker form:

m(\sigma)\geq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{4}{3-4\sigma}&\mbox{if \ $\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq\frac{5}{8}$}\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{3}{1-\sigma}&\mbox{if \ $\displaystyle\frac{5}{8}\leq\sigma<1$}.\end{cases}

(4.1)

The following lemma is used essentially in Ivić’s argument
[
8
]
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
a_{j}\ (1\leq j\leq k)
be positive integers such that
a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{k}
and let
\psi(s)
and
\sigma^{\ast}
be
defined by (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), respectively. Define the function
H(\sigma)
by

H(\sigma)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m(a_{j}\sigma-a_{j}+1)}.

If

H(\sigma)\leq 1/2

for some
\sigma
, we have
\sigma^{\ast}\leq\sigma
.

Proof.

We write
\sigma_{j}=a_{j}\sigma-a_{j}+1
for simplicity. Suppose that

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m(\sigma_{j})}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Then by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}|\psi(s)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{T}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\zeta(\sigma_{j}+ia_{j}t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta(\sigma_{j}+ia_{j}t)|^{m(\sigma_{j})}dt\right)^{\frac{2}{m(\sigma_{j})}}\left(\int_{1}^{T}1dt\right)^{1-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{2}{m(\sigma_{j})}}

\displaystyle\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}.

Hence from the definition of
\sigma^{\ast}
, we have
\sigma^{\ast}\leq\sigma
. ∎

We remark that since
H(\sigma)
is decreasing, if we have

H\left(1-\frac{k-1}{4\alpha}\right)<\frac{1}{2},

then Theorem 1 holds.

Lemma 7
.

Let
k=3
,
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq a_{3}
and
a_{3}<a_{1}+a_{2}
. Let
\sigma^{\ast}
be defined by
(
1.7
). Then we have

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}\begin{cases}\leq\displaystyle 1-\frac{5}{4(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})}&\mbox{if $3(a_{2}+a_{3})\leq 7a_{1}$, }\\[10.00002pt]
\leq\displaystyle 1-\frac{3}{a_{1}+3a_{2}+3a_{3}}&\mbox{if $3(a_{2}+a_{3})>7a_{1}$, $3a_{3}+a_{1}\leq 5a_{2}$ and $3a_{3}<a_{1}+3a_{2}$,}\\[10.00002pt]
=\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{2a_{3}}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(4.2)

Proof.

We shall find
1-\frac{1}{2a_{3}}\leq\sigma<1-\frac{1}{a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}}
such that
H(\sigma)\leq 1/2
.
For simplicity we put
\sigma_{j}=a_{j}\sigma-a_{j}+1\ (j=1,2,3)
for
\sigma\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
as before.
It is easy to see that
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma_{3}\leq\sigma_{2}\leq\sigma_{1}<1
.

Now we use a weak version (
4.1
) of Theorem 8.4 in Ivić.

(Case 1) We first consider the case
3(a_{2}+a_{3})\leq 7a_{1}
. Put

\sigma:=1-\frac{5}{4(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})}.

Clearly
\sigma<1-1/(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})
. Since
3a_{3}\leq 7a_{1}-3a_{2}\leq(2a_{1}+5a_{2})-3a_{2}=2(a_{1}+a_{2})
, we have
\sigma\geq 1-\frac{1}{2a_{3}}

and
\sigma_{1}\leq\frac{5}{8}
. By (
4.1
) we have

\displaystyle H(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{m(\sigma_{j})}\leq\frac{3-4\sigma_{1}}{4}+\frac{3-4\sigma_{2}}{4}+\frac{3-4\sigma_{3}}{4}=\frac{1}{2}.

Hence we get
\sigma^{\ast}\leq\sigma
.

(Case 2) When
3(a_{2}+a_{3})>7a_{1}
,
3a_{3}+a_{1}\leq 5a_{2}
and
3a_{3}<a_{1}+3a_{2}
, we put

\sigma:=1-\frac{3}{a_{1}+3a_{2}+3a_{3}}.

It is clear that
\sigma<1-1/(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})
and
\sigma>1-\frac{1}{2a_{3}}
by the last condition.
One can check that by the first two conditions that
\frac{5}{8}<\sigma_{1}<1
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma_{3}\leq\sigma_{2}\leq\frac{5}{8}
. Hence

\displaystyle H(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{m(\sigma_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\sigma_{1}}{3}+\frac{3-4\sigma_{2}}{4}+\frac{3-4\sigma_{3}}{4}=\frac{1}{2}.

Hence we get
\sigma^{\ast}\leq\sigma
.

(Case 3) We consider the case
3(a_{2}+a_{3})>7a_{1}
,
3a_{3}+a_{1}\leq 5a_{2}
and
3a_{3}\geq a_{1}+3a_{2}
. In this case we put

\sigma:=1-\frac{1}{2a_{3}}.

Note that this is the best possible choice. We easily check by the last condition that

\displaystyle 3a_{3}\geq a_{1}+3a_{2}\geq 4a_{1},

hence

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=a_{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2a_{3}}\right)-a_{1}+1=1-\frac{a_{1}}{2a_{3}}\geq\frac{5}{8}.

Now we consider two cases.

(i) If
3a_{3}\leq 4a_{2}
, then
\sigma_{2}\leq\frac{5}{8}
. We use the third condition to get

\displaystyle H(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{m(\sigma_{j})}\leq\frac{1-\sigma_{1}}{3}+\frac{3-4\sigma_{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{1}+3a_{2}}{6a_{3}}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(ii) If
3a_{3}>4a_{2}
, then
\sigma_{2}>\frac{5}{8}
.
By the third condition we have
3a_{3}\geq a_{1}+3a_{2}\geq 2(a_{1}+a_{2})
. Hence

\displaystyle H(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{m(\sigma_{j})}\leq\frac{1-\sigma_{1}}{3}+\frac{1-\sigma_{2}}{3}+\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{6a_{3}}\leq\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{3}{2}=\frac{1}{2}.

Combining the two cases (i) and (ii), we have
\sigma^{\ast}=\sigma=1-1/(2a_{3})
.

(Case 4) Finally we consider the case
3(a_{2}+a_{3})>7a_{1}
,
3a_{3}+a_{1}>5a_{2}
. In this case we put

\sigma:=1-\frac{1}{2a_{3}}.

In this case we easily check by the second condition that

\displaystyle 3a_{3}>5a_{2}-a_{1}\geq 4a_{2},

hence

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}=a_{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2a_{3}}\right)-a_{2}+1=1-\frac{a_{2}}{2a_{3}}>\frac{5}{8}.

We have
1>\sigma_{2}>\frac{5}{8},1>\sigma_{1}>\frac{5}{8}
, and
\sigma_{3}=\frac{1}{2}
.
Hence

\displaystyle H(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{m(\sigma_{j})}\leq\frac{1-\sigma_{1}}{3}+\frac{1-\sigma_{2}}{3}+\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore we have
\sigma^{\ast}=\sigma=1-1/(2a_{3}).

∎

Proof of Theorem 3.

to (
1.12

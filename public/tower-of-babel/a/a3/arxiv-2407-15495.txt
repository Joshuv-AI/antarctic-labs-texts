Ground states of a coupled pseudo-relativistic Hartree system: existence and concentration behavior


Abstract
This paper is concerned with the ground states of a coupled pseudo-relativistic Hartree system in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} with trapping potentials, where the intraspecies and the interspecies interaction are both attractive. By investigating an associated constraint minimization problem, the existence and non-existence of ground states are classified completely. Under certain conditions on the trapping potentials, we present a precise analysis on the concentration behavior of the minimizers as the coupling coefficient goes to a critical value, where the minimizers blow up and the maximum point sequence concentrates at a global minima of the associated trapping potentials. We also identify an optimal blowing up rate under polynomial potentials by establishing some delicate estimates of energy functionals.

Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the following a coupled pseudo-relativistic Hartree system with trapping potentials in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

\begin{cases}\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}u_{1}+V_{1}(x)u_{1}=\mu u_{1}+a_{1}(\left|x\right|^{-1}\ast u_{1}^{2})u_{1}+\beta(\left|x\right|^{-1}\ast u_{2}^{2})u_{1},\\
\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}u_{2}+V_{2}(x)u_{2}=\mu u_{2}+a_{2}(\left|x\right|^{-1}\ast u_{2}^{2})u_{2}+\beta(\left|x\right|^{-1}\ast u_{1}^{2})u_{2},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is the suitable Lagrange multiplier, the convolution kernel
\left|x\right|^{-1}
denotes the Newtonian gravitational potential,
a_{i}>0~(i=1,2)
,
\beta>0
represent that both the intraspecies interaction inside each component and the interspecies interaction between two components are attractive.

As shown by Lieb and Yau in their seminal work
[
20
]
, system (
1.1
) arises from a quantum mechanical model describing the stellar collapse of the neutron stars. Moreover, in celebrated paper
[
4

N
-body bosons with relativistic dispersion interaction in the mean-field limit. Another physical application of system (
1.1

[
5

i\partial_{t}\psi=\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}\psi+V(x)\psi-a\left(\left|x\right|^{-1}\ast\psi^{2}\right)\psi\quad{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.2)

Here,
\psi(t,x)
is conceived to be a complex-valued wave field and the travelling solitary waves of equation (
1.2
) are given by
\psi(t,x)=e^{-i\mu t}u(x)
, where
u(x)
is a ground state of the associated nonlinear Hartree equation

\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}u+V(x)u=\mu u+a(\left|x\right|^{-1}\ast u^{2})u\quad{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.3)

For more physical details, we refer the readers to references
[

,

,
8
,

. Also, one can see
[

V(x)=0

1.3

When the non-local operator
\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}
is replaced by the Laplacian
-\triangle

1.3

In particular, it is worth mentioning the very relevant contributions for one-component equations by Guo and his collaborators in their series work
[

, where the authors identified the existence intervals of the ground states and presented a detailed description of the collapse behavior under different types of external potentials.

Note that, in the muti-component framework, one has to take into account a certain interspecies interaction between each components, which may display more complicated physical phenomena and thus makes the analysis more challenging. Stimulated by the recent work
[

[

For the main topic in the present paper, that is, a non-local system with both pseudo-relativistic operator and nonlinear Hartree terms, some interesting results arise comparing to the classical local case. As recently shown by Yang and Yang in
[
27

, where a one-component non-local system was considered, they established a delicate energy estimate on the pseudo-relativistic term by using the Fourier transform. Another similar result is proposed by Nguyen in
[

Also, one can see the recent study on the concentration behavior of the minimizers as the stellar mass tends to a certain critical value in

However, to the authors’ knowledge, the concentration behavior of the minimizers has rarely been discussed concerning the coupled pseudo-relativistic Hartree system (
1.1
). Inspired by the above work, main results of this paper are focused on not only the existence range of solutions but also more importantly the concentration behavior of the minimizers of
a coupled

V_{i}(x)
satisfying for

,

(
\Omega

To begin with, we now introduce some notations and recall some known results. Let the Sobolev space

H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}):\left\|u\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}:=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(1+2\pi\left|s\right|\right)\left|\mathcal{F}u\left(s\right)\right|^{2}ds<\infty\right\},

(1.4)

where
\mathcal{F}u
stands for the Fourier transform of
u
.
The space
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
endowed with the inner product

\left(u,v\right)_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(1+2\pi\left|{s}\right|\right)\overline{\mathcal{F}u({s})}\mathcal{F}v\left({s}\right)d{s}

(1.5)

is easily seen to be a Hilbert space. The pseudo-relativistic operator
\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}
is defined in Fourier space as multiplication by
\sqrt{\left|2\pi{s}\right|^{2}+m^{2}}
, i.e.,

\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\left|2\pi{s}\right|^{2}+m^{2}}\mathcal{F}u({s})\right),\quad u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

(1.6)

Moreover, the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
can also be given by

\left\|u\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[u\left(-\triangle\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}u+u^{2}\right]dx=\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\left|\left(-\triangle\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}u\right|^{2}+u^{2}\right]dx,

(1.7)

one can refer
[
21
]
for more details. For ground states of system (
1.1
), it is known that we can discuss equivalently the minimizers of the following constrained
variational minimization problem:

e(a_{1},a_{2},\beta):=\inf_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{M}}E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2}),

(1.8)

where the corresponding energy function
E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2})
is given by

\begin{split}E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2})=&\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[u_{i}\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}u_{i}+V_{i}(x)u_{i}^{2}\right]dx-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{a_{i}}{2}\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u_{i}^{2}(x)u_{i}^{2}(y)}{\left|x-y\right|}dxdy\\
&-\beta\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u_{1}^{2}(x)u_{2}^{2}(y)}{\left|x-y\right|}dxdy,\quad({u}_{1},{u}_{2})\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2}.\end{split}

(1.9)

Here, the mass constraint
\mathcal{M}
is defined by

\mathcal{M}:=\left\{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2}:\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\right)dx=1\right\}

(1.10)

with

\mathcal{H}_{i}=\left\{u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V_{i}(x)u^{2}dx<\infty\right\},\quad i=1,2,

(1.11)

and

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}^{2}=\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\left|(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{4}}u\right|^{2}+V_{i}(x)u^{2}\right]dx.

(1.12)

The classical equation associated to problem (
1.1
) is known as

\sqrt{-\triangle}u(x)+u(x)=\left(\left|x\right|^{-1}\ast u^{2}(x)\right)u(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.13)

Let
Q>0
be a radially symmetric ground state of (
1.13
), it is shown in Lemma 2.2 of
[
9
]
that every ground state
Q
has the decay rate

Q(x)=O(\left|x\right|^{-4})\quad{\rm as}\ \left|x\right|\to\infty.

(1.14)

Denote
a^{\ast}:=\left\|Q\right\|_{2}^{2}
, it is proved in Lemma
5.1
in Appendix that all positive ground states of (
1.13
) have the same
L^{2}
-norm, that is,
a^{\ast}
is well-defined. Besides, from (
5.2
) in Appendix,
Q
also satisfies

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{4}}Q\right|^{2}dx=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q^{2}(x)dx=\frac{1}{2}\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{Q^{2}(x)Q^{2}(y)}{\left|x-y\right|}dxdy.

(1.15)

Moreover, it is known as a classical type of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\left|x\right|^{-1}\ast u^{2}\right)u^{2}dx=\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)}{\left|x-y\right|}dxdy\leq\frac{2}{a^{\ast}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{4}}u\right|^{2}dx\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u^{2}dx

(1.16)

holds for any
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Finally, for simplicity, we shall denote the inessential value by
C
, a general positive constant which can change from line to line in the sequel.

Now, let us turn to introduce the main results. The first result is concerning the existence and non-existence of the minimizers of problem (
1.8
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\left(\Omega\right)
holds and denote

\beta^{\ast}:=a^{\ast}+\sqrt{\left(a^{\ast}-a_{1}\right)\left(a^{\ast}-a_{2}\right)},\quad 0<a_{1},a_{2}\leq a^{\ast}.

(1.17)

Then we have:

(i)

If
0<a_{1},a_{2}<a^{\ast}
and
0<\beta<\beta^{\ast}
, then problem (
1.8
) has at least one minimizer. Moreover, it holds

\lim_{\beta\to\beta^{\ast}}e\left(a_{1},a_{2},\beta\right)=e\left(a_{1},a_{2},\beta^{\ast}\right)=0,\quad\ 0<a_{1},a_{2}<a^{\ast}.

(ii)

If
a_{1}\geq a^{\ast}
or
a_{2}\geq a^{\ast}
or
\beta\geq\beta^{\ast}
, then problem (
1.8
) has no minimizer.

Remark 1.1
.

It is worth noticing that the existence result also holds true for the system with repulsive interspecies interaction, i.e.,
\beta<0
, and the system with
\beta=0
, which can be easily seen in the proof of Theorem
1.1

(i)
.

Note that, with a mass constraint different from (
1.10
) , the authors in
[
26
]
have investigated problem (
1.8
) by constructing an auxiliary functional. Comparing to the pioneer work
[
26
]
, we address a complete classification involving the three parameters
a_{1},a_{2},\beta

with the mass constraint (
1.10
), which leads to the same coefficient
\mu
of the terms

in system (
1.1
).
Indeed, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is related well to (
1.10
) and the following refined Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, i.e., for any
({u}_{1},{u}_{2})\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
it holds

\begin{split}&\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\left(u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\right)(x)\left(u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\right)(y)}{\left|x-y\right|}dxdy\\
\leq&\frac{2}{a^{\ast}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\left|(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{2}\right|^{2}\right)dx\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\right)dx,\end{split}

(1.18)

which will be described in Lemma
5.2
in Appendix. We know that
E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2})\geq E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(\left|u_{1}\right|,\left|u_{2}\right|)
due to the fact that (
[
27
]
, Lemma 4.2)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}udx\geq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|u\right|\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}\left|u\right|dx,\quad u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

(1.19)

therefore, without loss of generality, we next restrict the minimizers of problem (
1.8
) to nonnegative ones.

Moreover, let us define a positive constant
\gamma
, i.e.,

\gamma=\frac{\sqrt{a^{\ast}-a_{2}}}{\sqrt{a^{\ast}-a_{1}}+\sqrt{a^{\ast}-a_{2}}}\in\left(0,1\right),\ {\rm where}\ 0<a_{1},a_{2}<a^{\ast}.

(1.20)

which plays a fundamental role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The second main result focuses on the concentration behavior of the nonnegative minimizers for (
1.8
) as
\beta\to\beta^{\ast}
with a general trapping potential satisfying
\left(\Omega\right)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\left(\Omega\right)
holds and let
\left(u_{1\beta},u_{2\beta}\right)
be a nonnegative minimizer of
e\left(a_{1},a_{2},\beta\right)
, where
0<a_{1},a_{2}<a^{\ast}
and
0<\beta<\beta^{\ast}
. Then for any sequence
\left\{\beta_{k}\right\}
with
\beta_{k}\to\beta^{\ast}
as
k\to\infty
, there exists a subsequence of
\left\{\beta_{k}\right\}
, still denoted by
\left\{\beta_{k}\right\}
, such that for
i=1,2
, each
u_{i\beta_{k}}
has at least one global maximum point
z_{i\beta_{k}}
, which satisfies

\lim_{k\to\infty}z_{1\beta_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}z_{2\beta_{k}}=x_{0}\quad{\rm for\ some}\ x_{0}\in\mathcal{V},

(1.21)

where

\mathcal{V}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:V_{1}(x)=V_{2}(x)=0\right\}.

(1.22)

Moreover, we have

\begin{cases}\lim_{k\to\infty}\varepsilon_{\beta_{k}}^{\frac{3}{2}}u_{1\beta_{k}}\left(\varepsilon_{\beta_{k}}x+z_{1\beta_{k}}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{\left\|Q\right\|_{2}}Q\left(x\right),\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}\varepsilon_{\beta_{k}}^{\frac{3}{2}}u_{2\beta_{k}}\left(\varepsilon_{\beta_{k}}x+z_{2\beta_{k}}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{1-\gamma}}{\left\|Q\right\|_{2}}Q\left(x\right),\end{cases}

(1.23)

strongly in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)
, where
\gamma
is given by (
1.20
) and
Q(x)
is a positive radially symmetric ground state of (
1.13
),
\varepsilon_{\beta_{k}}>0
satisfies

\varepsilon_{\beta_{k}}:=\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\left|(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1\beta_{k}}\right|^{2}+\left|(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{2\beta_{k}}\right|^{2}\right)dx\right)^{-1}\to 0\quad{\rm as}\ k\to\infty,

(1.24)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\left|z_{1\beta_{k}}-z_{2\beta_{k}}\right|}{\varepsilon_{\beta_{k}}}=0,

(1.25)

and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\beta^{\ast}-\beta_{k}}{\varepsilon_{\beta_{k}}}=0.

(1.26)

Observe that, there are two non-local terms in our system (pseudo-relativistic operator
\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}
and Hartree nonlinearities), we can call
it
a double non-local problem. It is worth mentioning that the pseudo-relativistic operator
\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}
is translation invariant but not scaling invariant, which makes our estimates of the energy functional more complicated and challenging. Indeed, denote
w(x)=u(x-x_{0})
for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
w,u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then by Fourier transform, we have

\begin{split}\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}w(x)=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\left|2\pi{s}\right|^{2}+m^{2}}\mathcal{F}w({s})\right)\left(x\right)\\
=&\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i2\pi{s}x}\sqrt{\left|2\pi{s}\right|^{2}+m^{2}}\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i2\pi{s}y}w\left(y\right)dy\right)d{s}\\
=&\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i2\pi{s}x}\sqrt{\left|2\pi{s}\right|^{2}+m^{2}}\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i2\pi{s}y}u\left(y-x_{0}\right)dy\right)d{s}\\
=&\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i2\pi{s}(x-x_{0})}\sqrt{\left|2\pi{s}\right|^{2}+m^{2}}\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i2\pi{s}\left(y-x_{0}\right)}u\left(y-x_{0}\right)dy\right)d{s}\\
=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\left|2\pi{s}\right|^{2}+m^{2}}\mathcal{F}u({s})\right)\left(x-x_{0}\right)\\
=&\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}u(x-x_{0}),\end{split}

(1.27)

which implies the translation invariance holds. On the other hand, let
w(x)=u(\lambda x)
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
w,u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then we can obtain by taking
\xi=\lambda y
and
\delta=\frac{{s}}{\lambda}
that

\begin{split}\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}w(x)=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\left|2\pi{s}\right|^{2}+m^{2}}\mathcal{F}w({s})\right)\left(x\right)\\
=&\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i2\pi{s}x}\sqrt{\left|2\pi{s}\right|^{2}+m^{2}}\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i2\pi{s}y}w\left(y\right)dy\right)d{s}\\
=&\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i2\pi{s}x}\sqrt{\left|2\pi{s}\right|^{2}+m^{2}}\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i2\pi{s}y}u\left(\lambda y\right)dy\right)d{s}\\
=&\lambda\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i2\pi\delta\lambda x}\sqrt{\left|2\pi\delta\right|^{2}+\lambda^{-2}m^{2}}\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i2\pi\delta\xi}u\left(\xi\right)d\xi\right)d\delta\\
=&\lambda\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\left|2\pi\delta\right|^{2}+\lambda^{-2}m^{2}}\mathcal{F}u(\delta)\right)\left(\lambda x\right)\\
=&\lambda\sqrt{-\triangle+\lambda^{-2}m^{2}}u(\lambda x),\end{split}

(1.28)

which indicates that the scaling invariance of
\sqrt{-\triangle+m^{2}}
fails with multiplying an extra scaling coefficient
\lambda
. Also, it is easy to verify that the Hartree nonlinear terms have similar translation and scaling properties. Therefore, our aim is to develop some new techniques to overcome these difficulties arising from the double non-local terms according to the Fourier transform developed in
[
27
]
and the classical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality.

Through the results of reference
[
3
]
and its proof we found that the existence of the global maximum point of each nonnegative minimizer is based heavily on the tool of non-local De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory introduced in
[
2
]
and
[
28
]
. But unfortunately we can not obtain the uniqueness of the global maximum point for our problem (
1.8
) since the deficiency of classical elliptic regularity theory in fractional Sobolev space, which plays a crucial role in proving the smoothness of the minimizer sequences. Therefore, this can be an open question for future research.

In order to give a further analysis on the concentration behavior of nonnegative minimizers, in the following, we basically assume the trapping potential
V_{i}(x)
of the form

V_{i}(x)=h_{i}(x)\prod_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\left|x-x_{ij}\right|^{q_{ij}},\ q_{ij}>0\ {\rm and}\ m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{+},\ i=1,2,

(1.29)

where
h_{i}(x)\in C_{loc}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfies
\frac{1}{C}<h_{i}(x)<C
for some positive constant
C
and
x_{ij}\neq x_{ik}
for
j\neq k
. Also, we suppose, without losing generality, that there is a positive integer

satisfying
1\leq b\leq\min\left\{m_{1},m_{2}\right\}
such that

\begin{cases}x_{1j}=x_{2j}:=x_{j},\ \quad{\rm where}\ j=1,\cdots,b;\\
x_{1j}\neq x_{2k},\quad\quad\ \ \ \ \ \ {\rm where}\ j\in\left\{b+1,\cdots,m_{1}\right\}\ {\rm and}\ k\in\left\{b+1,\cdots,m_{2}\right\}.\end{cases}

(1.30)

Notice that, (
1.30
) indicates

\mathcal{V}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:V_{1}(x)=V_{2}(x)=0\right\}=\left\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{b}\right\}.

(1.31)

Additionally, we define
\lambda_{j}
for
j=1,\cdots,b
by

\lambda_{j}:=\begin{cases}\lim_{x\to x_{j}}\frac{\gamma V_{1}(x)}{\left|x-x_{j}\right|^{q_{1j}}}\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|{y}\right|^{q_{1j}}Q^{2}({y})d{y}\ \ \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ {\rm if}\quad q_{1j}<q_{2j},\\
\lim_{x\to x_{j}}\frac{\gamma V_{1}(x)+(1-\gamma)V_{2}(x)}{\left|x-x_{j}\right|^{q_{1j}}}\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|{y}\right|^{q_{1j}}Q^{2}({y})d{y}\quad\quad\quad\ \ {\rm if}\quad q_{1j}=q_{2j},\\
\lim_{x\to x_{j}}\frac{(1-\gamma)V_{2}(x)}{\left|x-x_{j}\right|^{q_{2j}}}\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|{y}\right|^{q_{2j}}Q^{2}({y})d{y}\ \ \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \ {\rm if}\quad q_{1j}>q_{2j},\end{cases}

(1.32)

where
\gamma
is given by (
1.20
). Define

q_{j}:=\min\left\{q_{1j},q_{2j}\right\},\ j=1,\cdots,b;\quad q_{0}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq b}\ q_{j},

(1.33)

so that

Z:=\left\{x_{j}:q_{j}=q_{0},j=1,\cdots,b\right\}\subset\mathcal{V}.

(1.34)

Set

\lambda_{0}:=\min_{j\in\Gamma}\lambda_{j},\quad{\rm where}\ \Gamma:=\left\{j:x_{j}\in Z\right\},

(1.35)

and let

Z_{0}:=\left\{x_{j}\in Z:\lambda_{j}=\lambda_{0}\right\}

(1.36)

denote the set of the flattest common minimum points of
V_{i}(x)

u_{10}=\nu Q\left(\alpha x+\eta\right)

with some
\nu\neq 0,\alpha>0
and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Therefore, one can deduce

\left(u_{10}(x),u_{20}(x)\right)=\left(\tau sin\theta Q\left(\alpha x+\eta\right),\tau cos\theta Q\left(\alpha x+\eta\right)\right)

with some
\tau>0,\alpha>0,\theta\in\left[0,2\pi\right)
and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which implies the proof is finished.

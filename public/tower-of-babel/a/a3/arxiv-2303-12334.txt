Locally finite completions of polyhedral complexes
Desmond Coles, Netanel Friedenberg

Abstract
. We develop a method for subdividing polyhedral complexes in a way that restricts the possible recession cones and allows one to work with a fixed class of polyhedron. We use these results to construct locally finite completions of rational polyhedral complexes whose recession cones lie in a fixed fan, locally finite polytopal completions of polytopal complexes, and locally finite zonotopal completions of zonotopal complexes.



1.
Introduction

The goal of this article is to develop a method for completing a polyhedral complex such that the recession cones of its polyhedra lie in a fixed collection, and the resulting completion is locally finite. Let
N
be a lattice of rank
n
and set
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}=N\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\Phi
be a polyhedral complex in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
(which is not necessarily finite). We say that
\Phi
is
complete
if
\cup_{P\in\Phi}P=N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
; a
completion
of
\Phi
is a polyhedral complex
\overline{\Phi}\supseteq\Phi
which is complete. Let
\operatorname{rec}P
denote the recession cone of a polyhedron
P
. Our first result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
denote an additive subgroup of
{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\Sigma
be a finite rational fan in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
and let
\Phi
be a finite
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral complex in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
such that the recession cone
\operatorname{rec}P
of any
P\in\Phi
is in
\Sigma
.
Then there is a complete
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral complex
\overline{\Phi}
in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
containing
\Phi
as a subcomplex such that

•

\{\operatorname{rec}P\mid P\in\overline{\Phi}\}=\Sigma
and

•

\overline{\Phi}
is locally finite in the tropical toric variety
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}(\Sigma)
.

Moreover, if
\Phi
admits a finite completion whose vertices are all in
N_{\Gamma}
then
\overline{\Phi}
can be chosen so that all of its vertices are in
N_{\Gamma}
.

Recall that a polyhedron
P
in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is called
\Gamma
-rational
if it can be written in the form

P=\{w\in N_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mid\left\langle u_{i},w\right\rangle\geq\gamma_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,m\}

with
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in M
and
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma
. The tropical toric variety
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}(\Sigma)
is a partial compactification of
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, see §
2.4
for the definition of
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}(\Sigma)
. See
[
Pay09
, §3]
for further discussion on
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}(\Sigma)
and the connection to algebraic geometry. We will have to work with tropical toric varieties for future algebro-geometric applications discussed below. Here
N_{\Gamma}
denotes
N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
. All
\Gamma
-rational polyhedron have vertices in
N_{{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma}:=N\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
, where
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
is the divisible hull of
\Gamma
. Note that it is not always the case that a
\Gamma
-rational polyhedron has vertices in
N_{\Gamma}
; see Remark
2.3
.

In the case where
\Gamma={\mathbb{Q}}
, Theorem
1.1
gives a result about locally finite completions of rational polyhedral complexes. In Theorem
4.9
we generalize this to complexes of polyhedra which can be defined using coefficients in a given subfield of
{\mathbb{R}}
.

The motivation for Theorem
1.1
comes from algebraic geometry; in
[
GS15
]
a correspondence between toric varieties over a valuation ring and certain fans is established. In a companion article we will apply Theorem
1.1
to construct of algebraizable formal models of algebraic varieties. Our method also allows us to construct completions while working with a fixed class of polyhedron (such as zonotopes). We prove two other results on completions, one about polytopal completions, Theorem
1.2
, and one about zonotopal completions, Theorem
1.4
. Let
W
denote an arbitrary finite dimensional real vector space; we will use
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
when we need to keep track of particular additive subgroups of our vector space and otherwise we will use
W
for simpler notation.

Theorem 1.2
.

Every finite polytopal complex in
W
admits a polytopal completion that is locally finite in
W
.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.2
is a polytopal generalization of Whitehead’s Completion Lemma, which says that any finite simplicial complex in a real vector space admits a locally finite completion
[
Zie95
, Page 238]
,
[
Whi35
, Section 4]
. We note also that Whitehead’s Completion Lemma follows from Theorem

by ordering the vertices and applying a pulling triangulation; this proof of the simplicial completion lemma is new and different from the traditional one.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Phi
be a finite zonotopal complex in
W
. If
|\Phi|
is a polytope or a star-shaped ball then
\Phi
admits a zonotopal completion which is locally finite in
W
.

See §
4.2
for the definition of a star-shaped ball. Recall that a zonotope is a polytope which can be written as a Minkowski sum of finitely many line segments. A zonotopal complex is a polyhedral complex where every polyhedron is a zonotope, and a zonotopoal completion is a completion which is a zonotopal complex. Zonotopal decompositions of various regions have been studied both purely within the context of polyhedral geometry and combinatorics and for relations to algebraic geometry; see
[
Zie95
, Lecture 7]
,
[
EPTY18
, §4.2]
,
[
ABKS14
]
, and
[
OS79
]
.

The paper is structured as follow. In §
2
we give our notations, conventions, and an overview of background material. Then we prove Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.4
by proceeding in two parts. Let
\Phi
be as in Theorem
1.1
. First, in §

we establish that if we are given an extension of complexes (satisfying some conditions)
\Pi\supseteq\Phi
then we can subdivide
\Pi
in a way that preserves
\Phi
, restricts the possible recession cones of the polyhedron in the subdivision, and preserves local finiteness in

. Our work on subdivisions in this section culminate in Proposition
3.12
. The second part is in §
4
where we establish when an extension
\Pi\supseteq\Phi

and Theorem

.

2.
Preliminaries

We fix our notations and establish some basic facts about polyhedra, polyhedral complexes, and tropical toric varieties. We follow the same conventions as
[
Zie95
]
and refer the reader to
[
Pay09
, §3]
and
[
Rab12
, §3]
for details about tropical toric varieties.

Throughout this paper we fix a finite dimensional real vector space
W
. A polyhedron in
W
is finite intersections of half-spaces in
W
, and a polytope is a bounded polyhedron.

2.1.
Polyhedra

Let
W^{*}
be the dual space of
W
and let
\left\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\right\rangle\colon W^{*}\times W\to{\mathbb{R}}
denote the natural pairing. Given a polyhedron
P
in
W
we let
\operatorname{rec}P
denote its recession cone, defined as:

\operatorname{rec}P=\{v\in W\mid u+tv\in P\text{ for any $t\geq 0$ and $u\in P$}\}.

Write
Q\leq P
if
Q
is a face of
P
. We will use the following two elementary lemmas about images of polyhedra under linear maps.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon V\to W
be a linear map of real vector spaces and let
P\subset V
be a polyhedron. Then
\varphi(\operatorname{rec}P)=\operatorname{rec}(\varphi(P))
.

Proof.

Write
P=Q+\operatorname{rec}P
with
Q
a polytope in
V
.
Then
\varphi(P)=\varphi(Q)+\varphi(\operatorname{rec}(P))
with
\varphi(Q)
a polytope and
\varphi(\operatorname{rec}P)
a cone, so
\operatorname{rec}(\varphi(P))=\varphi(\operatorname{rec}P)
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
P
be a polyhedron in
W
, let
\sigma:=\operatorname{rec}P
be the recession cone of
P
, and let
\tau
be a face of
\sigma
. Let
\pi\colon W\to W/\operatorname{span}(\tau)
be the canonical projection map. For any face
F
of
P
such that
\tau\leq\operatorname{rec}F
,
\pi(F)
is a face of
\pi(P)
. If
F
is a proper face of
P
then
\pi(F)
is a proper face of
\pi(P)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{*}
be such that
F
is the subset of
P
on which
u
is minimized, and let
a
be this minimal value. Because
u
takes a constant value on
F
and
\tau\leq\operatorname{rec}F
, we have
\tau\subset u^{\perp}
and so
u\in(\operatorname{span}\tau)^{\perp}
.
Thus
u
descends to a linear function
\tilde{u}
on
W/\operatorname{span}\tau
, so
u=\tilde{u}\circ\pi
.
Hence
\left\langle\tilde{u},v\right\rangle\geq a
for
v\in\pi(P)
and
\tilde{u}
is constant with value
a
on
\pi(F)
. So
\pi(F)
is contained in the face
F^{\prime}:=\pi(P)\cap\{v\in W/\operatorname{span}\tau\mid\left\langle\tilde{u},v\right\rangle=a\}
of
\pi(P)
.
We also have that

\displaystyle P\cap\pi^{-1}(F^{\prime})

\displaystyle=P\cap\pi^{-1}(\pi(P))\cap\pi^{-1}(\{v\in W/\operatorname{span}\tau\mid\left\langle\tilde{u},v\right\rangle=a\})

\displaystyle=P\cap\{w\in W\mid\left\langle u,w\right\rangle=a\}=F,

and because
F^{\prime}\subset\pi(P)
,
F^{\prime}=\pi(P\cap\pi^{-1}(F^{\prime}))
, so
F^{\prime}=\pi(F)
.

If
F
is a proper face of
P
then picking some
w\in P\smallsetminus F
we have
\left\langle u,w\right\rangle>a
. Then
\pi(w)\in\pi(P)
is such that
\left\langle\tilde{u},\pi(w)\right\rangle=\left\langle u,w\right\rangle>a
, so
\pi(w)\in\pi(P)\smallsetminus\pi(F)
and we have that
\pi(F)
is a proper face of
\pi(P)
.
∎

2.2.
Rationality and Definablity

For more many of our constructions we will need consider not only a vector space but a lattice inside of it (as well as other additive subgroups). In this case we use the following notation. Let
N
be a lattice and let
M:=\operatorname{Hom}(N,{\mathbb{Z}})
be its dual lattice. Let
\Gamma
be an additive subgroup of
{\mathbb{R}}
and let
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
be the divisible hull of
\Gamma
. Let
N_{\Gamma}:=N\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\Gamma
, in particular
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}=N\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}
. We will always consider
N_{\Gamma}
as a subgroup of
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. A polyhedron
P
in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is called
\Gamma
-rational
if it can be written in the form

P=\{w\in N_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mid\left\langle u_{i},w\right\rangle\geq\gamma_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,m\}

with
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in M
and
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma
.
Polyhedra which are
\{0\}
-rational are cones, and we call such cones
rational
. Every face of a
\Gamma
-rational polyhedron is
\Gamma
-rational. A polyhedron
P
in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is
\Gamma
-rational if and only if the affine hull of each face is the translation of a rational linear subspace of
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
by an element of
N_{{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma}
(
[
Gub13
, A.2]
). It follows that the Minkowski sum of two
\Gamma
-rational polyhedra is
\Gamma
-rational.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that any
\Gamma
-rational polyhedron has vertices in
N_{{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma}
, however it may not have vertices in
N_{\Gamma}
. For example, let
\Gamma
be the group generated by
\sqrt{3}
, and considered the polyderon given by the line segment between the origin in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
\sqrt{3}/2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a subfield of
{\mathbb{R}}
. A polyhedron in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is called
\mathbb{F}
-definable
if it can be written in the form

\{w\in N_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mid\left\langle u_{i},w\right\rangle\geq a_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,m\}

with
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in M_{\mathbb{F}}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{F}
. Alternatively, we can characterize the
\mathbb{F}
-definable polyhedra as follows. The
\mathbb{F}
-definable polyhedra in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
are those which can be written as a Minkowski sum of an
\mathbb{F}
-definable polytope and an
\mathbb{F}
-definable cone. A polytope is
\mathbb{F}
-definable if and only if it can be written as the convex hull of finitely many points in
N_{\mathbb{F}}
, and a cone
\sigma
is
\mathbb{F}
-definable if and only if there are
w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}\in N_{\mathbb{F}}
such that
\sigma={\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}w_{1}+\cdots+{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}w_{m}
.

2.3.
Polyhedral complexes

A
polyhedral complex
in
W
is a collection
\Pi
of polyhedra in
W
such that

•

if
P\in\Pi
and
F
is a face of
P
then
F\in\Pi
, and

•

if
P,Q\in\Pi
are not disjoint then
P\cap Q
is a face of both
P
and
Q
.

A
subcomplex
of
\Pi
is a polyhedral complex
\Pi^{\prime}\subset\Pi
or, equivalently, a subset
\Pi^{\prime}\subset\Pi
such that if
P\in\Pi^{\prime}
and
F
is a face of
P
then
F\in\Pi^{\prime}
.
A
fan
in
W
is a nonempty polyhedral complex consisting of pointed cones. For a cone
\sigma
let
\operatorname{Face}(\sigma)
be the fan given by the faces of
\sigma
.
A
polytopal complex
is a polyhedral complex consisting of polytopes.
Note that we do not require any of these to be finite.

The
support
of a polyhedral complex
\Pi
is the union
|\Pi|:=\cup_{P\in\Pi}P
of all the polyhedra in
\Pi
.
We say that
\Pi
is
complete
if
|\Pi|=W
.
A
completion
of
\Pi
is a complete polyhedral complex containing
\Pi
as a subcomplex.

Let
P
be a polyhedron. A
subdivision
of
P
is a polyhedral complex
\Pi
such that
|\Pi|=P
. More generally, if
\Pi
and
\Pi^{\prime}
are polyhedral complexes we say that
\Pi^{\prime}
subdivides
\Pi
if
|\Pi^{\prime}|=|\Pi|
and every polyhedron in
\Pi
can be written as a union of polyhedra in
\Pi^{\prime}
.
A polyhedral complex
\Pi
subdivides a polyhedron
P
if and only if
\Pi
is a subdivision of the polyhedral complex
\operatorname{Face}(P):=\{F\mid F\leq P\}
of all faces of
P
.

A polyhedral complex
\Pi
in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is
\Gamma
-rational
if every polyhedron
P\in\Pi
is
\Gamma
-rational.
A fan in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is
rational
if each of its cones is rational.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a subfield of
{\mathbb{R}}
. A polyhedral complex in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is called
\mathbb{F}
-definable
if all of its polyhedra are
\mathbb{F}
-definable. Note that a fan
\Delta
in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
is
\mathbb{F}
-definable if and only if
\Delta|_{N_{{\mathbb{R}}}\times\{1\}}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-definable polyhedral complex and
\Delta|_{N_{{\mathbb{R}}}\times\{0\}}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-definable fan.

2.4.
Tropical toric varieties

Our presentation of tropical toric varieties follows
[
Rab12
, §3]
(and
[
Pay09
, §3]
though this source works with rational fans).

Let
\Sigma
be a finite fan in
W
. The
tropical toric variety

W(\Sigma)
corresponding to
\Sigma
is a partial compactification of
W
constructed as follows. As a set,

W(\Sigma):=\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}W/\operatorname{span}\sigma.

Consider the extended real line
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}={\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
with the topology such that the homeomorphism
{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
given by
a\mapsto e^{-a}
extends to a homeomorphism
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
. We equip
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
with the addition map for which
a+\infty=\infty
for all
a\in\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
, so the aforementioned homeomorphism
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
is also an isomorphism of topological monoids
(\overline{{\mathbb{R}}},+)\to({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0},\cdot)
.

Given
\sigma\in\Sigma
, let

\sigma^{\vee}:=\{u\in W^{*}\mid\left\langle u,w\right\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }w\in\sigma\}

be the dual cone of
\sigma
.
Let
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})
denote the set of monoid homomorphisms
\sigma^{\vee}\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
that preserve multiplication by elements of
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
.
We can identify
W(\sigma):=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\tau\leq\sigma}W/\operatorname{span}\tau
with
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})
by sending
w\in W/\operatorname{span}\tau
to the monoid homomorphism
\varphi_{w}\colon\sigma^{\vee}\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
given by

u\mapsto\begin{cases}\left\langle u,w\right\rangle&\text{if }u\in\tau^{\perp}\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This gives a bijection
W(\sigma)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})
, which gives
W(\sigma)
the structure of a topological monoid. Note that for
\tau\leq\sigma
, the subspace topology on
W/\operatorname{span}\tau\subset W(\sigma)
is the usual topology on
W/\operatorname{span}\tau
as a finite-dimensional
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space. For
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\leq\sigma
,
w_{1}\in W/\operatorname{span}\tau_{1}
, and
w_{2}\in W/\operatorname{span}\tau_{2}
, we have that
w_{1}+w_{2}
is given by projecting each
w_{i}
to
W/\operatorname{span}(\tau_{1}\vee\tau_{2})
, where
\tau_{1}\vee\tau_{2}
is the smallest face of
\sigma
containing both
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
, and then applying the usual vector space addition in
W/\operatorname{span}(\tau_{1}\vee\tau_{2})
.

For each
\sigma\in\Sigma
and any finite generating set
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
for
\sigma^{\vee}
as a polyhedral cone, the map
W(\sigma)=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}
defined by
\varphi\mapsto(\varphi(u_{1}),\ldots,\varphi(u_{m}))
gives a homeomorphism from
W(\sigma)
to a subspace of
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}
.

If
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
, then the inclusion
W(\tau)\subset W(\sigma)
identifies
W(\tau)
with an open topological submonoid of
W(\sigma)
.
The topology on
W(\Sigma)
is given by gluing the topologies on the various
W(\sigma)
s, and the previous sentence guarantees that each
W(\sigma)\subset W(\Sigma)
is an open subspace whose topology is the same as the original topology on
W(\sigma)
. We consider
W(\Sigma)
as a partial compactification of
W
by identifying
W
with
W/\{0\}\subset W(\Sigma)
. See Figure
1
for an example.
Since
W=W/\{0\}=W(\{0\})
is a submonoid of each
W(\sigma)
and the inclusion maps
W(\tau)\hookrightarrow W(\sigma)
are monoid homomorphisms, we get that the actions of
W
on each
W(\sigma)
, given by addition, glue to give a continuous action
W\times W(\Sigma)\to W(\Sigma)
, which we will also write as addition.

We will use the fact that tropical toric varieties are Hausdorff several times, both directly and by using
[
Rab12
, Remark 3.21]
, which implicitly uses that tropical toric varieties are Hausdorff. The following is well known among tropical geometers but we include the proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite fan in
W
. Then
W(\Sigma)
is Hausdorff.

Proof.

We show that
W(\Sigma)
is Hausdorff by tropicalizing the proof, as in
[
CLS11
, Theorem 3.1.5]
, that the toric variety associated to a rational fan is separated. First, recall that, for any
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
W(\sigma)
is Hausdorff because it is homeomorphic to
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})
which is a subspace of the Hausdorff space
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\left(\sigma^{\vee}\right)}
.
So it suffices to show that, for any two cones
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma
with intersection
\tau
, the diagonal map

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\tau^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})=W(\tau)\to W(\sigma_{1})\times W(\sigma_{2})=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma_{1}^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})\times\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma_{2}^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})

has closed image.
This diagonal map is induced by the inclusions
\sigma_{1}^{\vee},\sigma_{2}^{\vee}\subset\tau^{\vee}
, and if we identify

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma_{1}^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})\times\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma_{2}^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})

with

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma_{1}^{\vee}\times\sigma_{2}^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})
,
then the diagonal map
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\tau^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}(\sigma_{1}^{\vee}\times\sigma_{2}^{\vee},\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})
is induced by the addition map
\sigma_{1}^{\vee}\times\sigma_{2}^{\vee}\to\tau^{\vee}
.
That this map has closed image follows because
\sigma_{1}^{\vee}+\sigma_{2}^{\vee}=\tau^{\vee}
and in general, if
\psi\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
is a surjective homomorphism of semimodules over a semiring
{\mathcal{R}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}
is a Hausdorff topological
{\mathcal{R}}
-semimodule, then the image of the induced map
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{R}}}({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{M}})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{R}}}({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{M}})
is closed. To see this last fact, note that the image of this map is the intersection of the sets

\{\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{R}}}({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{M}})\mid\varphi(s_{1})=\varphi(s_{2})\}

over all pairs
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}
such that
\psi(s_{1})=\psi(s_{2})
. Because
{\mathcal{M}}
is Hausdorff, each of the sets
\{\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{R}}}({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{M}})\mid\varphi(s_{1})=\varphi(s_{2})\}
is closed.
∎

Lastly, recall that a collection
{\mathcal{A}}
of subsets of a topological space
X
is called
locally finite in
X
if every point of
X
has a neighborhood which meets only finitely many of the sets
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Note that
{\mathcal{A}}
is locally finite in
X
if and only if the family
\{\overline{A}\mid A\in{\mathcal{A}}\}
of the closures of the sets in
{\mathcal{A}}
is locally finite in
X
.
If
{\mathcal{A}}
is locally finite in
X
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
is locally finite in
X^{\prime}
, then the product family
{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}:=\{A\times A^{\prime}\mid A\in{\mathcal{A}},A^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\}
is locally finite in
X\times X^{\prime}
. If
Y
is a closed subspace of
X
which contains
\cup_{A\in{\mathcal{A}}}A
, then
{\mathcal{A}}
is locally finite in
X
if and only if it is locally finite in
Y
.

3.
Locally finite subdivisions

In this section we develop the subdivision technique used to prove our main theorems. We give a simple example of this technique in Example
3.1

3.1.
Subdividing a polyhedron

The following two lemmas will allow us to construct subdivisions of a polyhedron using its boundary and a ray interior to the recession cone.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
P
be an unbounded, pointed polyhedron in
W
. Fix a ray
\rho
that meets the relative interior of
\operatorname{rec}P
. Let
B
be the union of the faces of
P
whose recession cones are proper faces of
\operatorname{rec}P
. Then any point in
P
can be written uniquely as the sum of a point in
\rho
and a point in
B
.

Proof.

Let
w\in P
. Fix a nonzero point
v\in\rho
. Consider the function
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to W
given by
t\mapsto w+tv
. Because
v
is in
\operatorname{rec}P
,
\varphi(t)\in P
for
t\geq 0
. On the other hand, because
P
is a pointed polyhedron,
\varphi^{-1}(P)
is a polyhedron but not all of
{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus
\varphi^{-1}(P)=[t_{0},\infty)
for some
t_{0}\leq 0
.We have that
\{\varphi(t_{0})\}
is a face of the polyhedron
\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(P))=\varphi({\mathbb{R}})\cap P
. Since a face of an intersection of two polyhedra must be an intersection of faces of the polyhedra, there is a face
Q
of
P
such that
\{\varphi(t_{0})\}=\varphi({\mathbb{R}})\cap Q
. The recession cone of
Q
must be a face of
\operatorname{rec}P
but does not contain
\rho
because
Q
contains
\varphi(t_{0})
but no other point of
\varphi(t_{0})+\rho=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(P))
. Since
\rho
meets the relative interior of
\operatorname{rec}P
this exactly says that the recession cone of
Q
is a proper face of
P
. So
\varphi(t_{0})\in B
. Thus
w=\varphi(t_{0})+(-t_{0}v)
is the desired representation.

To show uniqueness, suppose
z_{1}+v_{1}=z_{2}+v_{2}
with
z_{1},z_{2}\in B
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in\rho
. We may assume without loss of generality that
v_{1}-v_{2}\in\rho
. If
v_{1}=v_{2}
then
z_{1}=z_{2}
and we are done, so assume
v_{1}\neq v_{2}
. Thus
v_{1}-v_{2}\in\rho\smallsetminus\{0\}
, so
z_{2}=z_{1}+v_{1}-v_{2}
is in the relative interior of the polyhedron
z_{1}+\rho
. Now if
Q
is a face of
P
containing
z_{2}
then because
z_{1}+\rho\subset P
,
Q\cap(z_{1}+\rho)
must be a face of
z_{1}+\rho
containing
z_{2}
, and so must be all of
z_{1}+\rho
. Thus any such
Q
contains
z_{1}+\rho
which implies that the recession cone of
Q
must contain
\rho
and therefore be all of
\operatorname{rec}P
. This contradicts the assumption that
z_{2}\in B
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
F\subset W
be a polyhedron and
\rho\subset W
be a cone such that the addition map
\rho\times F\rightarrow F+P
is a bijection. Let
\varphi\colon F+\rho\to F
and
\psi\colon F+\rho\to\rho
be the maps such that
w=\varphi(w)+\psi(w)
for all
w\in F+\rho
. Then
\varphi
and
\psi
are affine maps. In particular,
\varphi
and
\psi
are continuous.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
0
is in the relative interior of
F
. In this case we see that the sum
\operatorname{span}F
and
\operatorname{span}\rho
is a direct sum. We thus have that the maps
\varphi
and
\psi
are restrictions of the projection maps
(\operatorname{span}F)\oplus(\operatorname{span}\rho)\to\operatorname{span}F
and
(\operatorname{span}F)\oplus(\operatorname{span}\rho)\to\operatorname{span}\rho
, respectively.
∎

We can now give a subdivision of a polyhedron by subdividing a ray intersecting the relative interior of the recession cone.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
P
be an unbounded, pointed polyhedron in
W
. Fix a ray
\rho
that meets the relative interior of
\operatorname{rec}P
and let
B
be the union of those faces of
P
whose recession cones are proper faces of
\operatorname{rec}P
. Let
T
be any polyhedral subdivision of
\rho
and let
\Pi
be a polyhedral complex with support
B
. Then
\Pi^{\prime}:=\{Q+R\mid Q\in\Pi,R\in T\}
is a polyhedral subdivision of
P
.

Proof.

Say
Q\in\Pi
and
R\in T
. Lemma
3.3
implies that the addition map
Q\times R\to Q+R
is an affine isomorphism of polyhedra. Since faces of
Q\times R
are products of a face of
Q
and a face of
R
, the addition map
Q\times R\to Q+R
being an affine isomorphism gives us that the faces of
Q+R
are exactly those sets of the form
E+F
with
E\leq Q
and
F\leq R
. In particular, every face of
Q+R
is also in
\Pi^{\prime}
.

Say
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\Pi
and
R_{1},R_{2}\in T
are such that
Q_{1}+R_{1}
and
Q_{2}+R_{2}
are not disjoint.
The uniqueness part of Lemma
3.2
shows that

(Q_{1}+R_{1})\cap(Q_{2}+R_{2})=(Q_{1}\cap Q_{2})+(R_{1}\cap R_{2}),

so
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}\neq\emptyset
and
R_{1}\cap R_{2}\neq\emptyset
.
Since
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
is a face of both
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, and
R_{1}\cap R_{2}
is a face of both
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
, the previous paragraph gives us that
(Q_{1}\cap Q_{2})+(R_{1}\cap R_{2})
is a face of both
Q_{1}+R_{1}
and
Q_{2}+R_{2}
. Thus
\Pi^{\prime}
is a polyhedral complex.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

With the same setup as in Proposition

, if

\Pi
are both locally finite in

\Pi^{\prime}
is locally finite in

as well.

It follows from Lemma
3.2
the addition map
+:\rho\times B\rightarrow P
is a homeomorphism. Since

is locally finite in

and

of images of sets in

under the homeomorphism

.

Lemma 3.6

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
P\subset W
be a unbounded, pointed polyhedron with recession cone

and let
\Sigma
be a finite fan in
W
such that
\Sigma\cup\operatorname{Face}(\sigma)
is also a fan. Let
\rho
be a ray that meets the relative interior of
\sigma

B
be the union of those faces of
P
whose recession cones are strictly smaller than
\sigma
.
Then the addition map
+\colon W\times W(\Sigma)\to W(\Sigma)
sends
\rho\times\overline{B}
to
\overline{P}
.
Moreover, if
\Sigma
does not contain

\rho\times\overline{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{P}
is a bijection.

Proof.

First we describe
\overline{B}
. Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the set of faces of
P
whose recession cones are strictly smaller than
\sigma
. By Lemma

(1)

\overline{B}=\bigcup_{F\in{\mathcal{B}}}\overline{F}=\bigcup_{F\in{\mathcal{B}}}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\leq\operatorname{rec}F\\

\overline{F}
is the closure of

in

.

Now we show that the addition map
+\colon W\times W(\Sigma)\to W(\Sigma)
sends
\rho\times\overline{B}
to

. Notice that if

then by (

)

for some

and
\tau\leq\operatorname{rec}F
. If

then we can compute

.

Finally, we show that

does not contain
\sigma
. For any

and

if and only if

. Thus we can proceed by showing that for every

\rho\times(\overline{B}\cap W/\operatorname{span}\tau)\rightarrow\overline{P}\cap W/\operatorname{span}\tau

\overline{B}\cap W/\operatorname{span}\tau\subset\overline{P}\cap W/\operatorname{span}\tau=\emptyset

3.2
to the map
\rho\times(\overline{B}\cap W/\operatorname{span}\tau)\rightarrow\overline{P}\cap W/\operatorname{span}\tau
.

Because
\tau<\sigma
it follows from Lemma
2.1
it follows that

is pointed and unbounded.

We also have that

\overline{B}\cap W/\operatorname{span}\tau=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in{\mathcal{B}}\\
\tau\leq\operatorname{rec}F\end{subarray}}\pi_{\tau}(F).

gives that
\pi_{\tau}(F)
is a face of

gives that, for each such face

Lemma 3.8
.

Using the same notation as above, assume that
\Sigma
does not contain
\sigma
. Then the addition map

First, observe that we can reduce to the case where

. Indeed, by Lemma

we know that

is the same as the closure

in

\operatorname{cl}_{W(\Delta_{\sigma})}(B)
of

in

is a homoemomorphism onto its image, which is equal to

by Lemma

.

Now consider the case in which

. Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the set of faces of

whose recession cones are in

, so
\overline{B}=\bigcup_{F\in{\mathcal{B}}}\overline{F}
where
\overline{F}
is the closure of

in

is the same as the closure of

in

[

3.3.

4.
Locally finite completions of polyhedral complexes

We now use Proposition

to deduce the existence of completions of polyhedral complexes with various properties.
In §

we prove general theorems on the existence of locally finite subdivisions and completions. We then apply them to our main theorems.

4.1.
General subdivision and completion theorems

To state our subdivision results in full generality we require a few technical definitions.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a class of polyhedra in
W
and let
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{P}})
be the class of recession cones of polyhedra
P\in{\mathcal{P}}
.
A
{\mathcal{P}}
-complex
is a polyhedral complex consisting of polyhedra in
{\mathcal{P}}
. The definitions of a
{\mathcal{C}}
-fan, a
{\mathcal{P}}
-completion, and a
{\mathcal{P}}
-polytopal subdivision are analogous.
If
\Sigma
is a
{\mathcal{C}}
-fan then we define
({\mathcal{P}},\Sigma)
to be the class of those
P\in{\mathcal{P}}
with
\operatorname{rec}P\in\Sigma
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
{\mathcal{P}}
satisfies condition
(\dagger)
if

(I)

{\mathcal{P}}
is closed under taking Minkowski sums,

(II)

for every nonzero
\sigma\in{\mathcal{C}}
there is a ray
\rho\in{\mathcal{C}}
which meets the relative interior of
\sigma
,

(III)

for every ray
\rho\in{\mathcal{C}}
there is a
{\mathcal{P}}
-polytopal subdivision of
\rho
which is locally finite in
\rho
.

Using this definition, we can state our general subdivision theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a class of polyhedra in
W
which satisfies condition
(\dagger)
.
Let
\Sigma
be a finite
{\mathcal{C}}
-fan.
Suppose that
\Phi\subset\Pi
is an extension of
{\mathcal{P}}
-complexes which are both locally finite in
W(\Sigma)
.
Assume
\operatorname{rec}P\in\Sigma
for
P\in\Phi
and
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\Sigma\cup\{\operatorname{rec}P\mid P\in\Pi\}
is a fan. Then there is a
{\mathcal{P}}
-subdivision
\widetilde{\Pi}
of
\Pi
which is locally finite in
W(\Sigma)
such that
\Phi\subset\widetilde{\Pi}
and
\{\operatorname{rec}P\mid P\in\widetilde{\Pi}\}=\Sigma\cap\{\operatorname{rec}P\mid P\in\Pi\}
.

Proof.

For each
\sigma\in\Sigma^{\prime}\smallsetminus\Sigma
pick a ray
\rho_{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{C}}
which meets the relative interior of
\sigma
and a
{\mathcal{P}}
-polytopal subdivision

of

which is locally finite in
\rho_{\sigma}
.
This puts us in situation
(\star)
. Let
\widetilde{\Pi}(i)
for

,
\Pi_{\sigma}
, and
{\mathcal{S}}(P)
be defined as in Construction
3.11
.
Induction on

shows that every

,

. Indeed, the base case is true by assumption, as

. For

we have by definition:

\widetilde{\Pi}(i):=\widetilde{\Pi}(i-1)\cup\bigg(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\Sigma\\

By the inductive step the elements of

and so all its elements are in

In order to state our general result on locally finite completions, we need one more definition.

In this section we use Theorem

Before starting the proof of Theorem
1.4
we define the notion of a star-shaped ball in
W

Definition 4.10
.

A subset
D
of
W
is called a
star-shaped ball
if there is a point
w\in W
such that every ray
\rho
starting at
w
intersects
\partial D
in a unique point
v\neq w
and
D\cap\rho=\operatorname{Conv}(w,v)
. We say that
w
is a
center
for
D
and that
D
is a
star-shaped ball around
w
.

Here
\partial D
denotes the topological boundary of
D
as a subset of
W
.

We follow
[
ES92
]
and
[
Ewa96
, Ch. III.5]
in using this definition, which is particularly suited to several operations on polyhedral complexes, even though it is somewhat stronger than another common notion of
star-shaped region
. We refer to
[
ES92
]
and
[
Ewa96
, Ch. III.5]
for relevant facts about star-shaped balls.

The case of Theorem
1.4
where
|\Phi|
is a polytope will be reduced to the case where
|\Phi|
is a star-shaped ball. This case will be proved by constructing a certain finite completion of
\Phi
and then applying Theorem
4.2
. The construction of the finite completion of
\Phi
works in a broader generality, and so we start with this construction.

Let
\Phi
be a finite polytopal complex in
W
such that
D:=|\Phi|
is a star-shaped ball around
0
. For any
F\in\Phi
with
F\subset\partial D
, let
U(F):={\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 1}F=\{tv\mid t\geq 1,v\in F\}
. We immediately get that
U(F)\cap D=F
. Since
F
is a polyhedron contained in
\partial D
,
\operatorname{aff}(F)
does not contain 0. So there is some
u\in W^{*}
and a positive real number
a
such that
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=a
for all
v\in F
. Note that
U(F)=({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}F)\cap\{v\in W\mid\left\langle u,v\right\rangle\geq a\}
, and is therefore a polyhedron. Also, because every face of an intersection of two polyhedra can be written as an intersection of faces of the original polyhedra, and because the faces of
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}F
are exactly the zero cone and
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}G
for
G
a face of
F
, we have that the faces of
U(F)
are exactly the faces of
F
and the polyhedra
U(G)
where
G
is a face of
F
.

Lemma 4.11
.

For any finite polytopal complex
\Phi
in
W
such that
D:=|\Phi|
is a star-shaped ball around
0
,
\Phi\cup\{U(F)\mid F\in\Phi,F\subset\partial D\}
is a complete polyhedral complex, where
U(F):={\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 1}F
.

Proof.

Given what we have established in the preceding paragraph, the proof is a straightforward analogue of the standard proof that, under the same hypotheses,
\{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}F\mid F\in\Phi,F\subset\partial D\}\cup\{0\}
is a complete fan. We leave the details to the reader.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{P}}_{zon}
be the class of polyhedra
P\subset W
such that every bounded face of
P
is a zonotope. In order to use Theorem
4.2
to obtain an appropriate subdivision of the completion constructed above, we need to show that
{\mathcal{P}}_{zon}
satisfies
(\dagger)
.

Lemma 4.12
.

The class
{\mathcal{P}}_{zon}
satisfies condition
(\dagger)
.

Proof.

For
P,Q\in{\mathcal{P}}_{zon}
, any bounded face of
P+Q
is the Minkowski sum of a bounded face
F
of
P
and a bounded face
G
of
Q
. Then
F
and
G
are zonotopes so
F+G
is also a zonotope.
The class
{\mathcal{C}}_{zon}=\{\operatorname{rec}P\mid P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{zon}\}
consists of all cones in
W
, so (
II
) and (
III
) follow from the fact that any ray is contained in
{\mathcal{C}}
and a subdivision of a ray is consists of points, line segments, and possibly a ray.
∎

We now have all of the ingredients we need to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

First suppose that
|\Phi|
is a star-shaped ball
D
. By shifting we may assume without loss of generality that
0
is a center for
D
. Lemma
4.11
tells us that
\Pi:=\Phi\cup\{U(F)\mid F\in\Phi,F\subset\partial D\}
is a complete polyhedral complex in
W
. If
F\in\Phi
is contained in
\partial D
then every bounded face of
U(F)
is also a face of
F
, and is therefore a zonotope. Thus
\Pi
is a
{\mathcal{P}}_{zon}
-complex. Since
\Phi\subset\Pi
consists of polytopes we have that the zero cone is in
\{\operatorname{rec}P\mid P\in\Pi\}
, so by
[
BGS11
, Corollary 3.10]
,
\{\operatorname{rec}P\mid P\in\Pi\}
is a fan.
By Lemma
4.12
we can apply Theorem
4.2
with
{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{P}}_{zon}
and
\Sigma
being the zero fan to get that there is a
{\mathcal{P}}_{zon}
-subdivision
\overline{\Phi}
of
\Pi
which is locally finite in
W(\Sigma)=W
such that
\Phi\subset\overline{\Phi}
and
\{\operatorname{rec}P\mid P\in\overline{\Phi}\}
is the zero fan.
Because
\Pi
is complete,
\overline{\Phi}
is a completion of
\Phi
. Finally,
\{\operatorname{rec}P\mid P\in\overline{\Phi}\}
being the zero fan gives us that
\overline{\Phi}
is a polytopal
{\mathcal{P}}_{zon}
-complex, i.e., a zonotopal complex.

Now suppose that
|\Phi|
is a polytope
P
. If
|\Phi|
is a full-dimensional polytope in
W
, then
|\Phi|
is a star-shaped ball with each point in the interior of
|\Phi|
being a center, so the desired completion exists by the previous case. If
P
is not full-dimensional then we can reduce to the full-dimensonal case as follows. By shifting we may assume without loss of generality that 0 is in the affine span of
P
, so
\operatorname{aff}(P)
is a linear subspace of
W
. Let
V\subset W
be a linear complement to
\operatorname{aff}(P)
, so
W=\operatorname{aff}(P)\oplus V
. Let
Q
be a full-dimensional cube in
V
with 0 as a vertex. Then the direct sum of
\Phi
and the complex of all faces of
Q
is a finite zonotopal subdivision of the full-dimensional polytope
P+Q
in
W
which contains
\Phi
as a subcomplex.
∎

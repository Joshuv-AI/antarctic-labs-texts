A Cancellation Law for Probabilistic Processes
Rob van Glabbeek, Jan Friso Groote, Erik de Vink

Abstract
We show a cancellation property for probabilistic choice. If μ ⊕ ϱ \mu\oplus\varrho and ν ⊕ ϱ \nu\oplus\varrho are branching probabilistic bisimilar, then μ \mu and ν \nu are also branching probabilistic bisimilar. We do this in the setting of a basic process language involving non-deterministic and probabilistic choice and define branching probabilistic bisimilarity on distributions. Despite the fact that the cancellation property is very elegant and concise, we failed to provide a short and natural combinatorial proof. Instead we provide a proof using metric topology. Our major lemma is that every distribution can be unfolded into an equivalent stable distribution, where the topological arguments are required to deal with uncountable branching.

We are interested in whether the cancellation law
also holds for weaker notions of process equivalence for probabilistic
processes, especially for branching probabilistic bisimilarity as proposed

A number of initial attempts were directed towards finding a straightforward

a topological setting, employing the notion of sequential compactness to deal
with potentially infinite sequences of transitions is reported in this paper.
We leave the existence of a shorter, combinatorial proof as an open question.

Our strategy to prove the above cancellation law

Intuitively, a stable process

\displaystyle(\text{since $\xi=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\nu_{j}$})\mkern 1.0mu.

Definition 4

(Syntax)

The classes

4
Branching probabilistic bisimilarity

In this section we recall the notion of branching probabilistic
bisimilarity
[
17
]
. The notion is based on a
decomposability property due to
[
10
]
and a transfer property.

Definition 9

(Branching probabilistic bisimilarity)
.

(a)

A relation
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})\times\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
is called
weakly decomposable iff it is symmetric and for all

\mu,\nu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
such that
\mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\nu
and

\mu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}\mathop{\cdot}\mu_{i}
there are

\bar{\nu},\nu_{i}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
, for
i\in I
, such that

\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\nu},\ \mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\bar{\nu},\ \bar{\nu}=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}\mathop{\cdot}\nu_{i},\ \text{and\,}\ \mu_{i}\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\nu_{i}\,\ \text{for
all~$i\in I$.}

(b)

A relation
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})\times\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
is called a

branching
probabilistic bisimulation relation
iff it is weakly decomposable and for all

\mu,\nu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
with
\mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\nu
and

\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\mu^{\prime}
, there are
\bar{\nu},\nu^{\prime}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
such
that

\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\nu},\ \bar{\nu}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu^{\prime},\ \mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\bar{\nu},\ \text{and}\,\ \mu^{\prime}\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\nu^{\prime}.

(c)

Branching probabilistic bisimilarity

{\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}}\subseteq\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})\times\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
is defined as the
largest branching probabilistic bisimulation relation on
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
.

Note that branching probabilistic bisimilarity is well-defined
following the usual argument that any union of branching probabilistic
bisimulation relations is again a branching probabilistic bisimulation
relation. In particular, (weak) decomposability is preserved under
arbitrary unions.
As observed in
[
16
]
, branching probabilistic bisimilarity
is an equivalence relation.

Two non-deterministic processes are considered to be branching
probabilistic bisimilar iff their Dirac distributions are, i.e., for

E,F\in\mathcal{E}
we have
E\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}F
iff

\delta(E)\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\delta(F)
.
Two probabilistic processes are considered to be branching
probabilistic bisimilar iff their associated distributions
over
\mathcal{E}
are, i.e., for
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}
we have
P\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}Q
iff

\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}P\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}Q\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}
.

For a set
M\subseteq\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
, the convex closure
cc(M)
is defined
by

cc(M)=\{\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}p_{i}\mu_{i}\mid\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}=1,~\mu_{i}\in M,~I\textrm{ a finite index set}\}.

For a relation
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})\times\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
the convex
closure of
\mathcal{R}
is defined
by

\mathit{cc}(\mathcal{R})=\{\>\langle\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}p_{i}\mu_{i},\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}p_{i}\nu_{i}\rangle\mid\mu_{i}\mathcal{R}\nu_{i},~\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}=1,~I\textrm{ a finite index set}\>\}\mkern 1.0mu.

The notion of weak decomposability has been adopted
from
[
23
,
25
]
. The underlying idea stems
from
[
10
]
. Weak decomposability provides a convenient
dexterity to deal with combined transitions as well as with
sub-distributions. For example, regarding sub-distributions, to
distinguish the probabilistic process

\frac{1}{2}\partial(a\mathop{.}\partial({\textbf{0}}))\oplus\frac{1}{2}\partial(b\mathop{.}\partial({\textbf{0}}))
from
\partial({\textbf{0}})
a branching
probabilistic bisimulation relation relating

\frac{1}{2}\delta(a\mathop{.}\partial({\textbf{0}}))\oplus\frac{1}{2}\delta(b\mathop{.}\partial({\textbf{0}}))
and
\delta({\textbf{0}})
is by weak
decomposability also required to relate

\delta(a\mathop{.}\partial({\textbf{0}}))
and
\delta(b\mathop{.}\partial({\textbf{0}}))

to subdistributions of a weak
descendant of
\delta({\textbf{0}})
, which can only be

\delta({\textbf{0}})
itself. Since
\delta(a\mathop{.}\partial({\textbf{0}}))

has an
a
-transition while
\delta({\textbf{0}})
has not, and
similar for a
b
-transition of

\delta(b\mathop{.}\partial({\textbf{0}}))
, it follows that

\frac{1}{2}\partial(a\mathop{.}\partial({\textbf{0}}))\oplus\frac{1}{2}\partial(b\mathop{.}\partial({\textbf{0}}))
and
\partial({\textbf{0}})
are not branching
probabilistic bisimilar.

By comparison, on finite processes, as used in this paper, the notion
of branching probabilistic bisimilarity of Segala &
Lynch
[
28
]
can be defined in our framework exactly as in
(b) and (c) above, but taking a decomposable instead of a weakly
decomposable relation, i.e. if
\mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\nu
and

\mu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}\mu_{i}
then there are
\nu_{i}
for
i\in I

such that
\nu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}\nu_{i}
and

\mu_{i}\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\nu_{i}
for
i\in I
. This yields a strictly finer
equivalence.

Example

(a)

The distributions

\delta(\mathbf{G_{1}})=\delta(a\mathop{.}\mkern 1.0mu(P\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}$}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}Q))

and
\delta(\mathbf{G_{2}})=\delta(a\mathop{.}\mkern 1.0mu(\partial(\tau\mathop{.}\mkern 1.0mu(P\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}$}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}Q))\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\textstyle{\frac{1}{3}}$}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}(P\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}$}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}Q)))

at the top level

a

\displaystyle\delta(a\mathop{.}\mkern 1.0mu(P\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}$}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}Q))

\displaystyle\delta(a\mathop{.}\mkern 1.0mu(\partial(\tau\mathop{.}\mkern 1.0mu(P\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}$}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}Q))\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\textstyle{\frac{1}{3}}$}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}(P\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}$}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}Q)))

In the sequel we frequently need that probabilistic composition

\mu_{i}

\mu_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{i}

Lemma 10
.

Let distributions
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
and

0\leqslant r\leqslant 1
be such that
\mu_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{1}
and

\mu_{2}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{2}
. Then it holds that

\mu_{1}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\mu_{2}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{1}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\nu_{2}
.

We apply the above property in the proof of the next result. In
the sequel any application of
Lemma
10
will be done
tacitly.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
such that
\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu
and

\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{\>}\mu^{\prime}
for some
\mu^{\prime}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
. Then there are

\nu^{\prime}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
such that
\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{\>}\nu^{\prime}
and

\mu^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We check that a partial transition
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\mu^{\prime}
can be
matched by
\nu
given
\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu
. So, suppose

\mu=\mu_{1}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\mu_{2}
,
\mu_{1}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\tau\,$}}\mu^{\prime}_{1}
, and

\mu^{\prime}=\mu^{\prime}_{1}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\mu_{2}
. By weak decomposability
of
\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}
we can find distributions
\bar{\nu},\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
such
that
\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{\>}\bar{\nu}=\nu_{1}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\nu_{2}
and

\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\nu}
,
\nu_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu_{1}
,
\nu_{2}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu_{2}
. Choose
distributions
\bar{\nu}_{1},\bar{\nu}^{\prime}_{1}
such that

\nu_{1}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{\>}\bar{\nu}_{1}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\nu^{\prime}_{1}
and

\bar{\nu}_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu_{1}
,
\nu^{\prime}_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu^{\prime}_{1}
. Put

\nu^{\prime}=\nu^{\prime}_{1}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\nu_{2}
. Then
\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{\>}\nu^{\prime}
,
using Lemma
7
c, and we
have by Lemma
10
that

\nu^{\prime}=\nu^{\prime}_{1}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\nu_{2}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu^{\prime}_{1}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\mu_{2}=\mu^{\prime}
since
\nu^{\prime}_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu^{\prime}_{1}
and
\nu_{2}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu_{2}
.
∎

5
Branching probabilistic bisimilarity is continuous

Fix a finite set of non-deterministic processes

\mathcal{F}\mathbin{\subseteq}\mathcal{E}
that is
transition closed
, in
the sense that if
E\in\mathcal{F}
and

E\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}p_{i}{\cdot}F_{i}
then also

F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
.
Consequently, if
\mu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
and
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\mu^{\prime}

then
\mu^{\prime}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
. Also, if
\mu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
and

\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\mu}
then
\bar{\mu}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
.
By Theorem
1

\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
is a sequentially compact subspace of the complete metric
space
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
, meaning that every sequence

(\mu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}
in
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
has a subsequence

(\mu_{i_{k}})_{k{=}0}^{\infty}
such that

\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\>\mu_{i_{k}}=\mu
for some
distribution
\mu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
.
In particular, if
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\>\mu_{i}=\mu
and

\mu_{i}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
, then also
\mu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
, i.e.
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
is a
closed subset of
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
.
Due to the finitary nature of our process algebra, each distribution

\mu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
occurs in
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
for some such

\mathcal{F}
, based on
\mathit{spt}(\mu)
.

In the following three lemmas we establish a number of continuity
results. Assume

\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}=\nu
. Then Lemma
12
states that, for a Dirac
distribution
\delta(E)
, if
\delta(E)\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\nu_{i}

for
i\in\mathbbm{N}
then also
\delta(E)\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\nu
. Lemma
13
extends this and shows
that, for a general distribution
\mu
, if
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\nu_{i}

for
i\in\mathbbm{N}
then
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\nu
. Finally,
Lemma
14
establishes the limit case:
if
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}=\mu
and

\mu_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\nu_{i}
for
i\in\mathbbm{N}
then

\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\nu
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{F}
be a non-deterministic process,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
an
action,
(\nu_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}
an infinite
sequence in
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
, and
\nu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
a distribution

satisfying
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}=\nu
. If, for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
,
\delta(E)\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu_{i}

then it holds that
\delta(E)\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu
.

Proof.

For
E\in\mathcal{F}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, define

E\mathord{\upharpoonright}\alpha=\mathit{cc}(\{\>\mu\mid E\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\mu\})
,
pronounced
E
‘after’
\alpha
, to be the
convex closure in
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
of all distributions that can be
reached from
E
by an
\alpha
-transition. Then

\delta(E)\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\nu
iff
\nu\in E\mathord{\upharpoonright}\alpha
.
Recall that transitions for non-deterministic processes are not
probabilistically combined. See
Definition
5
.
Since
E\mathord{\upharpoonright}\alpha\subseteq\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
is the convex closure of a
finite set of distributions, it is certainly closed in the
space
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
. Since it holds that
\delta(E)\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\nu_{i}

for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
, one has
\nu_{i}\in E\mathord{\upharpoonright}\alpha

for
i\in\mathbbm{N}
. Hence,
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}=\nu

implies that
\nu\in E\mathord{\upharpoonright}\alpha
, i.e.
\delta(E)\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\nu
.

For
E\in\mathcal{F}
, define

E\mathord{\upharpoonright}(\tau):=\mathit{cc}(\{\mu\mid E\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\tau\,$}}\mu\}\cup\{E\})
.
Then
\delta(E)\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\nu
iff

\nu\in E\mathord{\upharpoonright}(\tau)
. The set

E\mathord{\upharpoonright}(\tau)\subseteq\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
is closed, and thus

\nu_{i}\in E\mathord{\upharpoonright}(\tau)
implies
\nu\in E\mathord{\upharpoonright}(\tau)
, which
means
\delta(E)\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\nu
.
∎

The above result for Dirac distributions holds for
general distributions as well.

Lemma 13
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
,

(\nu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}
, and assume

\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}=\nu
.

If it
holds that
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu_{i}\,
for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
, then
also
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Let

\mu=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\mathop{\cdot}E_{j}
.

By
Lemma
8
, for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
and

1\leqslant j\leqslant k
there are
\nu_{ij}
such that

\delta(E_{j})\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu_{ij}
and

\nu_{i}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\nu_{ij}
. The countable
sequence
(\nu_{i1},\nu_{i2},\dots,\nu_{ik})_{i=0}^{\infty}
of

k
-dimensional vectors of probability distributions need not have a
limit. However, by the sequential compactness of

\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
this sequence has an infinite subsequence in which the
first components
\nu_{i_{1}}
converge to a limit
\eta_{1}
. That
sequence in turn has an infinite subsequence in which also the
second components
\nu_{i_{2}}
converge to a limit
\eta_{2}
. Going
on this way, one finds a subsequence

(\nu_{i_{h}1},\nu_{i_{h}2},\dots,\nu_{i_{h}k})_{h=0}^{\infty}
of

(\nu_{i1},\nu_{i2},\dots,\nu_{ik})_{i=0}^{\infty}
for

i_{0}<i_{1}<\dots
that has a limit, say

\lim_{\,h\rightarrow\infty}(\nu_{i_{h}1},\nu_{i_{h}2},\dots,\nu_{i_{h}k})=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{k})
. Using that

\lim_{\,h\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i_{h}}=\nu
, one obtains

\nu=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\eta_{j}
. For each

j=1,\dots,k
, by Lemma
12
, since

\delta(E_{j})\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu_{ij}
for all
i\in I
and

\lim_{\,h\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i_{h}j}=\eta_{j}
, we conclude that

\delta(E_{j})\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\eta_{j}
. Thus, by Lemma
7
,

\mu=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\mathop{\cdot}E_{j}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\eta_{j}=\nu
.
∎

Next, we consider a partial transition over a convergent sequence of
distributions.

Lemma 14
.

Let

(\mu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty},(\nu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}
such that

\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}=\mu
and

\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}=\nu
.

If it
holds that
\mu_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
, then
also
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu
.

Proof.

Since
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mkern 1.0mu\mu_{i}=\mu
, we can
write

\mu_{i}=(1-r_{i})\mu\mathrel{\oplus}r_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\mu^{\prime\prime}_{i}
, for
suitable
\mu^{\prime\prime}_{i}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
and
r_{i}\geqslant 0
such that

\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mkern 1.0mur_{i}=0
, as guaranteed by
Lemma
2
.
Because
\mu_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu_{i}
, by
Lemma
8
there are
distributions
\nu^{\prime}_{i},\nu^{\prime\prime}_{i}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
for
i\in\mathbbm{N}
such
that
\nu_{i}=(1-r_{i})\nu^{\prime}_{i}\mathrel{\oplus}r_{i}\nu^{\prime\prime}_{i}
,

\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu^{\prime}_{i}
, and
\mu^{\prime\prime}_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu^{\prime\prime}_{i}
.

We have
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{i}=\nu
as well, since

\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}r_{i}=0
.
Thus,
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{i}=\nu
and
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu^{\prime}_{i}
for
i\in\mathbbm{N}
.
Therefore, it follows by Lemma
13

that
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\nu
.
∎

For
\mu,\nu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
, we write
\mu\Rightarrow_{n}\nu
if there
are
\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
such that

\mu=\eta_{0}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\eta_{1}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\dots\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\eta_{n}=\nu
. Clearly, it holds that
\mu\Rightarrow_{n}\nu
for
some
n\in\mathbbm{N}
in case
\mu\Rightarrow\nu
, because
\Rightarrow

is the transitive closure of
\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}
.

We have the following pendant of Lemma
14
for
\Rightarrow_{n}
.

Lemma 15
.

Let

(\mu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty},(\nu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}
,
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}=\mu

and
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}=\nu
. If

\mu_{i}\Rightarrow_{n}\nu_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
then

\mu\Rightarrow_{n}\nu
.

Proof.

By induction on
n
. Basis,
n=0
: Trivial.
Induction step,
n{+}1
: Given

(\mu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty},(\nu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}\!
,
\mu=\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}
,
and
\nu=\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}
, suppose

\mu_{i}\Rightarrow_{n+1}\nu_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
.

Let
(\eta_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}
be such that

\mu_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\eta_{i}\Rightarrow_{n}\nu_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
.
Since
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
is

sequentially compact, the
sequence
(\eta_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}
has a

convergent
subsequence
(\eta_{i_{k}})_{k{=}0}^{\infty}
; put

\eta=\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\eta_{i_{k}}
.
Because
\mu_{i_{k}}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\eta_{i_{k}}
for all
k\in\mathbbm{N}
,
one has
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\eta
by Lemma
14
.
Since
\eta_{i_{k}}\Rightarrow_{n}\nu_{i_{k}}
for
k\in\mathbbm{N}
, the
induction hypothesis yields
\eta\Rightarrow_{n}\nu
.
It follows that
\mu\Rightarrow_{n+1}\nu
.
∎

We adapt Lemma
15
to obtain a
continuity result for weak transitions
\,{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}
.

Lemma 16
.

Let

(\mu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty},(\nu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}
,
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}=\mu

and
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}=\nu
. If

\mu_{i}\Rightarrow\nu_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
, then

\mu\Rightarrow\nu
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}
contains only finitely many non-deterministic
processes, which can do finitely many
\tau
-transitions only,
a global upperbound
N
exists such that if
\mu\Rightarrow\nu

then
\mu\Rightarrow_{k}\nu
for some
k\leqslant N
.

Moreover, as each sequence

\mu=\eta_{0}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\eta_{1}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\dots\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\eta_{k}=\nu
with
k<N
can be extended to a sequence

\mu=\eta_{0}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\eta_{1}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\dots\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}\eta_{N}=\nu
, namely by taking
\eta_{i}=\nu
for all

k<i\leqslant N
, on
\mathcal{F}
the relations
\Rightarrow

and
\Rightarrow_{N}
coincide. Consequently, Lemma
16
follows from Lemma
15
.
∎

The following theorem says that equivalence classes of branching
probabilistic bisimilarity in
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
are closed sets of
distributions.

Theorem 17
.

Let
\hat{\mu},\hat{\nu}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
and

(\nu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}
such that

\hat{\mu}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
and

\hat{\nu}=\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}
. Then it holds
that
\hat{\mu}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\hat{\nu}
.

Proof.

Define the relation
\mathcal{R}
on
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
by

\mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\nu\iff\begin{array}[t]{l}\exists(\mu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty},(\nu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}\colon\\
\qquad\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}=\mu\land\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}=\nu\land\forall i\mathbin{\in}\mathbbm{N}\colon\mu_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{i}\end{array}

As
\hat{\mu}\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\hat{\nu}
(taking
\mu_{i}:=\hat{\mu}
for all
i\in I
),
it suffices to show that
\mathcal{R}
is a branching probabilistic bisimulation.

Suppose
\mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\nu
. Let

(\mu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty},(\nu_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\mathbin{\in}\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}
be such that

\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}\mathbin{=}\mu
,

\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i}\mathbin{=}\nu
, and

\mu_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{i}
for all
i\mathbin{\in}\mathbbm{N}
.
Since
\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}\mathbin{=}\mu
, there exist

(\mu^{\prime}_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\mathbin{\in}\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})^{\infty}
and

(r_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}\in\mathbbm{R}^{\infty}
such that

\mu_{i}=(1-r_{i})\mkern 1.0mu\mu\mathbin{\oplus}r_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\mu^{\prime}_{i}

for all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
and

\lim_{\,i\rightarrow\infty}\mkern 1.0mur_{i}=0
.

(i) Towards weak decomposability of
\mathcal{R}
for
\mu
vs.
\nu
,
suppose
\mu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\bar{\mu}_{j}
. So, for all

i\mathbin{\in}\mathbbm{N}
, we have that

\mu_{i}=(1-r_{i})\mkern 1.0mu\bigl(\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\bar{\mu}_{j}\bigr)\mathrel{\oplus}r_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\mu^{\prime}_{i}
. By weak decomposability
of
\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}
, there exist
\bar{\bar{\nu}}_{i}
,
\nu^{\prime}_{i}
and
\nu_{ij}

for
i\mathbin{\in}\mathbbm{N}
and
j\mathbin{\in}J
such that

\nu_{i}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\bar{\nu}}_{i}
,
\mu_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\bar{\nu}}_{i}
,

\bar{\bar{\nu}}_{i}=(1-r_{i})\bigl(\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\nu_{ij}\bigr)\mathrel{\oplus}r_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\nu^{\prime}_{i}
,

\mu^{\prime}_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu^{\prime}_{i}
, and
\bar{\mu}_{j}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{ij}

for
j\mathbin{\in}J
.

The sequences
(\nu_{ij})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}
for
j\in J
may not
converge. However, by sequential compactness of
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
(and
successive sifting out for each
j\in J
) an index sequence

(i_{k})_{k{=}0}^{\infty}
exists such that the sequences

(\nu_{i_{k}j})_{k{=}0}^{\infty}
converge, say

\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i_{k}j}=\bar{\nu}_{j}

for
j\in J
. Put

\bar{\nu}=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\bar{\nu}_{j}
.
Then it holds that

\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\bar{\nu}}_{i_{k}}=\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}(1-r_{i_{k}})\bigl(\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\nu_{i_{k}j}\bigr)\mathrel{\oplus}r_{i_{k}}\mkern 1.0mu\nu^{\prime}_{i_{k}}=\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\nu_{i_{k}j}=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\bar{\nu}_{j}=\bar{\nu}

as
\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}r_{i_{k}}=0
and probabilistic
composition is continuous.
Since
\nu_{i_{k}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\bar{\nu}}_{i_{k}}
for all
k\in\mathbbm{N}
, one has

\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i_{k}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\bar{\nu}}_{i_{k}}
, i.e.
\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\nu}
, by
Lemma
16
. Also,

\mu_{i_{k}}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\bar{\nu}}_{i_{k}}
for all
k\in\mathbbm{N}
. Therefore, by
definition of
\mathcal{R}
, we obtain
\mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\bar{\nu}
. Since

\bar{\mu}_{j}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{i_{k}j}
for all
k\in\mathbbm{N}
and
j\in J
,
it follows that
\bar{\mu}_{j}\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\bar{\nu}_{j}
for
j\in J
.
Thus,
\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\nu}=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}\bar{\nu}_{j}
,
\mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\bar{\nu}
, and
\bar{\mu}_{j}\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\bar{\nu}_{j}
for all
j\in J
, as was
to be shown.
Hence the relation
\mathcal{R}
is weakly decomposable.

(ii) For the transfer property, suppose
\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\mu^{\prime}

for some
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
. Since, for
each
i\mathbin{\in}\mathbbm{N}
,
\mu_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{i}
and

\mu_{i}=(1-r_{i})\mkern 1.0mu\mu\mathbin{\oplus}r_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\mu^{\prime}_{i}
, it follows from weak
decomposability of
\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}
that
distributions
\bar{\nu}_{i}
,
\nu^{\prime}_{i}
and
\nu^{\prime\prime}_{i}
exist such that

\nu_{i}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\nu}_{i}
,
\mu_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\nu}_{i}
,

\bar{\nu}_{i}=(1-r_{i})\mkern 1.0mu\nu^{\prime}_{i}\mathrel{\oplus}r_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\nu^{\prime\prime}_{i}
and
\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu^{\prime}_{i}
.
By the transfer property for
\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}
, for each
i\in\mathbbm{N}
exist

\bar{\eta}_{i},\eta^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
such that

\nu^{\prime}_{i}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\eta}_{i},\ \bar{\eta}_{i}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\eta_{i}^{\prime},\ \mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\eta}_{i},\ \text{and}\ \mu^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\eta_{i}^{\prime}.

We have
\bar{\nu}^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
for
i\in\mathbbm{N}
. Also,

\bar{\eta}_{i},\eta^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
for
i\in\mathbbm{N}
, since
\mathcal{F}
is
assumed to be transition closed. Therefore, by sequential
compactness of
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
, the sequences

(\bar{\nu}^{\prime}_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}
,

(\bar{\eta}_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}
,

(\bar{\eta}^{\prime}_{i})_{i{=}0}^{\infty}
have converging subsequences

(\bar{\nu}^{\prime}_{i_{k}})_{k{=}0}^{\infty}
,

(\bar{\eta}_{i_{k}})_{k{=}0}^{\infty}
, and

(\bar{\eta}^{\prime}_{i_{k}})_{k{=}0}^{\infty}
, respectively. Put

\bar{\nu}=\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{i_{k}}
,

\bar{\eta}=\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\eta}_{i_{k}}
, and

\eta^{\prime}=\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\eta^{\prime}_{i_{k}}
. As

\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}r_{i_{k}}=0
, one has

\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\nu}_{i_{k}}=\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{\prime}_{i_{k}}=\bar{\nu}
.

Since
\nu_{i_{k}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\nu}_{i_{k}}
for
k\in\mathbbm{N}
, we obtain

\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{i_{k}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\lim_{\,k\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\nu}_{i_{k}}
by Lemma
16
, thus
\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\nu}
. Likewise, as

\nu^{\prime}_{i_{k}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\eta}_{i_{k}}
for all
k\in\mathbbm{N}
,
one has
\bar{\nu}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\eta}
, and therefore

\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\eta}
. Furthermore, because

\bar{\eta}_{i_{k}}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\eta^{\prime}_{i_{k}}
for
k\in\mathbbm{N}
, it
follows that
\bar{\eta}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\eta^{\prime}
, now by
Lemma
14
. From

\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\eta}_{i_{k}}
for all
k\in\mathbbm{N}
, we obtain

\mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\bar{\eta}
by definition of
\mathcal{R}
. Finally,

\mu^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\eta^{\prime}_{i_{k}}
for all
k\in\mathbbm{N}
yields

\mu^{\prime}\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\eta^{\prime}
. Thus
\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\eta}\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\alpha)\,$}}\eta^{\prime}
,
\mu\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\bar{\eta}
, and
\mu^{\prime}\,{\mathcal{R}}\,\bar{\eta}^{\prime}
,
which was to be shown.
∎

The following corollary of Theorem
17
will be
used in the next section.

Corollary 18
.

For each
\mu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
, the set

T_{\mu}=\{\>\nu\mathbin{\in}\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})\mid\nu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu\land\mu\mathbin{\Rightarrow}\nu\>\}
is a sequentially compact set.

Proof.

For
\mu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}\mathop{\cdot}E_{i}
, the set of processes

\mathcal{F}=\{\>E\in\mathcal{E}\mid\text{$E$ occurs in~$E_{i}$ for
some~$i\in I$}\>\}
is finite and closed under
transitions. Clearly,
\mu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
. Moreover,
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
is a
sequentially compact subset of
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
. Taking
\mu_{i}=\mu
for
all
i\in\mathbbm{N}
in Lemma
16
yields that

\{\>\nu\mid\mu\mathbin{{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}}\nu\>\}
is a closed subset
of
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
. Similarly, the set

\{\>\nu\mid\nu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu\>\}
is a closed subset
of
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
by Theorem
17
. The statement
then follows since the intersection of two closed
subsets of
\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{F})
is itself closed, and hence sequentially compact.
∎

6
Cancellativity for branching probabilistic bisimilarity

With the results of Section
5
in place, we turn to
stable processes and cancellativity.
In the introduction we argued that in general it doesn’t need to be
the case that two branching probabilistic bisimilar distributions
assign the same weight to equivalence classes.
Here we show that this property does hold when restricting to stable
distributions.
We continue to prove the announced unfolding result, that for every
distribution
\mu
there exists a stable distribution
\sigma
such that

\mu\Rightarrow\sigma
and
\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\sigma
.
That result will be pivotal in the proof of the cancellation theorem,
Theorem
22
.

Definition 19
.

A distribution
\mu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
is called
stable
if, for all

\bar{\mu}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
,
\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\mu}
and

\mu\mathrel{\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}}\bar{\mu}
imply that
\bar{\mu}=\mu
.

Thus, a distribution
\mu
is called stable if it cannot perform
internal activity

without leaving its branching
bisimulation equivalence class.
By definition of
\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,(\tau)\,$}}
it is immediate that if

\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}{\cdot}\mu_{i}
is a stable distribution with

p_{i}>0
for
i\in I
, then also each probabilistic
component
\mu_{i}
is stable.
Also, because two stable distributions
\mu
and
\nu
don’t have any
non-trivial partial
\tau
-transitions, weak decomposability between
them amounts to decomposability, i.e. if
\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu
and

\mu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}\mu_{i}
then distributions
\nu_{i}

for
i\in I
exist such that
\nu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}\nu_{i}
and

\mu_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu_{i}
for
i\in I
.

The next result states that, contrary to distributions in general, two
stable distributions are branching bisimilar precisely when they
assign the same probability on all branching bisimilarity classes
of
\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
be two stable distributions. Then it
holds that
\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu
iff
\mu[C]=\nu[C]
for each
equivalence class
C
of branching probabilistic bisimilarity
in
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}\mathop{\cdot}E_{i}
,

\nu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}F_{\mkern-1.0muj}
, and
\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu
. By weak decomposability,

\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\nu}=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}{\cdot}\nu_{i}
for
suitable
\nu_{i}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
for
i\in I
with

\nu_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\delta(E_{i})
and
\bar{\nu}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu
. Hence,

\bar{\nu}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu
. Thus, by stability of
\nu
, we
have
\bar{\nu}=\nu
. Say,

\nu_{i}=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathop{\cdot}F_{\mkern-1.0muj}
with

q_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\geqslant 0
, for
i\in I
,
j\in J
.
Since
\nu_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\delta(E_{i})
, we have by weak decomposability,

\delta(E_{i}){\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{i\mkern-1.0muj}{\cdot}\mu^{\prime}_{i\mkern-1.0muj}
such
that
\delta(E_{i})\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{i\mkern-1.0muj}{\cdot}\mu^{\prime}_{i\mkern-1.0muj}

and
\mu^{\prime}_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\delta(F_{\mkern-1.0muj})
for suitable

\mu^{\prime}_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
. Since
\mu
is stable, so
is
\delta(E_{i})
. Hence

\delta(E_{i})=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{i\mkern-1.0muj}{\cdot}\mu^{\prime}_{i\mkern-1.0muj}
,

\mu^{\prime}_{i\mkern-1.0muj}=\delta(E_{i})
, and
E_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}F_{\mkern-1.0muj}
if
q_{i\mkern-1.0muj}>0
. Put
p_{i\mkern-1.0muj}=p_{i}\mkern 1.0muq_{i\mkern-1.0muj}
,
E_{i\mkern-1.0muj}=E_{i}
if

q_{i\mkern-1.0muj}>0
, and
E_{i\mkern-1.0muj}={\textbf{0}}
otherwise,
F_{i\mkern-1.0muj}=F_{\mkern-1.0muj}
if

q_{i\mkern-1.0muj}>0
, and
F_{i\mkern-1.0muj}={\textbf{0}}
otherwise, for
i\in I
,
j\in J
. Then it holds that

\begin{array}[]{l@{\;}c@{\;}l@{\;}c@{\;}l@{\;}c@{\;}l@{\;}c@{\;}l}\mu&=&\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}\mathop{\cdot}E_{i}&=&\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}{\cdot}\bigl(\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathop{\cdot}E_{i}\bigr)&=&\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\,\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>p_{i}\mkern 1.0muq_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathop{\cdot}E_{i}&=&\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\,\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>p_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathop{\cdot}E_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\\
\nu&=&\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}{\cdot}\nu_{i}&=&\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}{\cdot}\bigl(\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathop{\cdot}F_{\mkern-1.0muj}\bigr)&=&\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\,\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>p_{i}\mkern 1.0muq_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathop{\cdot}F_{\mkern-1.0muj}&=&\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\,\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>p_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathop{\cdot}F_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mkern 1.0mu.\end{array}

Now, for any equivalence class
C
of
\mathcal{E}
modulo
\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}
, it
holds that
E_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\in C\Leftrightarrow F_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\in C
for all indices

i\in I
,
j\in J
. So,

\mu[C]=\textstyle{\sum_{i{\in}I,j{\in}J\colon E_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\in C}}\>p_{i\mkern-1.0muj}=\textstyle{\sum_{i{\in}I,j{\in}J\colon F_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\in C}}\>p_{i\mkern-1.0muj}=\nu[C]
.

For the reverse direction, suppose

\mu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\>p_{i}\mathop{\cdot}E_{i}
,

\nu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>q_{j}\mathop{\cdot}F_{\mkern-1.0muj}
, with
p_{i},q_{j}>0
, and

\mu[C]=\nu[C]
for each equivalence class

C\in\mathcal{E}/{\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[6.99997pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}}
.

For
i\in I
and
j\in J
, let
C_{i}
and
D_{\!j}
be the
equivalence class in
\mathcal{E}
of
E_{i}
and
F_{\mkern-1.0muj}

modulo
\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}
. Define

r_{i\mkern-1.0muj}=\delta_{\mkern 1.0mui\mkern-1.0muj}p_{i}q_{j}/\mu[C_{i}]
,
for
i\in I
,
j\in J
, where

\delta_{\mkern 1.0mui\mkern-1.0muj}=1

if
E_{i}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}F_{\mkern-1.0muj}
and

\delta_{\mkern 1.0mui\mkern-1.0muj}=0
otherwise. Then it holds
that

\textstyle{{\textstyle\sum_{\,j{\in}J}}}\>r_{i\mkern-1.0muj}=\textstyle{{\textstyle\sum_{\,j{\in}J}}}\>\frac{\mbox{\small$\delta_{\mkern 1.0mui\mkern-1.0muj}p_{i}\mkern 1.0muq_{j}\rule[-3.0pt]{0.0pt}{7.0pt}$}}{\mbox{\small$\mu[C_{i}]\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{7.0pt}$}}=\frac{\mbox{\small$p_{i}\rule[-3.0pt]{0.0pt}{7.0pt}$}}{\mbox{\small$\mu[C_{i}]\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{7.0pt}$}}\textstyle{{\textstyle\sum_{\,j{\in}J}}}\>\delta_{\mkern 1.0mui\mkern-1.0muj}\mkern 1.0muq_{j}=\frac{\mbox{\small$p_{i}\nu[C_{i}]\rule[-3.0pt]{0.0pt}{7.0pt}$}}{\mbox{\small$\mu[C_{i}]\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{7.0pt}$}}=p_{i}.

Since

\delta_{\mkern 1.0mui\mkern-1.0muj}p_{i}\mkern 1.0muq_{j}/\mu[C_{i}]=\delta_{\mkern 1.0mui\mkern-1.0muj}p_{i}\mkern 1.0muq_{j}/\nu[D_{\!j}]
for

i\in I
,
j\in J
, we also have

{\textstyle\sum_{\,i{\in}I}}\>r_{i\mkern-1.0muj}=q_{j}
. Therefore, we can write

\mu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\,\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>r_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathop{\cdot}E_{i\mkern-1.0muj}
and

\nu=\textstyle{\bigoplus_{i\mathord{\in}I}}\,\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j\mathord{\in}J}}\>r_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathop{\cdot}F_{i\mkern-1.0muj}
for suitable
E_{i\mkern-1.0muj}

and
F_{i\mkern-1.0muj}
such that

E_{i\mkern-1.0muj}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}F_{i\mkern-1.0muj}
. Calling
Lemma
10
it follows that

\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu
.
∎

Next, in Lemma
21
, we are about to prove a crucial
property for our proof of cancellativity, the proof of
Theorem
22
below. Generally, a distribution may
allow inert partial transitions. However, the
distribution can be unfolded to reach via inert partial transitions a
stable distribution, which doesn’t have these by definition. To obtain
the result we will rely on the topological property of sequential
compactness of the set

T_{\mu}=\{\>\mu^{\prime}\mid\mu^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu\land\mu\mathbin{{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}}\mu^{\prime}\>\}
introduced in the previous section.

Lemma 21
.

For all
\mu\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
there is a stable
distribution
\sigma\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
such that
\mu\Rightarrow\sigma
.

Proof.

Define the
weight
of a distribution by

\mathit{wgt}(\mu)\mathbin{=}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}}\>\mu(E)\cdot\mkern 1.0muc(E)
,
i.e., the weighted

average of the complexities of the
states in its support. In view of these definitions,

E\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\mu
implies
\mathit{wgt}(\mu)<\mathit{wgt}(\delta(E))
and

\mu\mkern 1.0mu\xrightarrow{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.3014pt][-0.3014pt]{\scriptsize$\,\alpha\,$}}\mu^{\prime}
implies
\mathit{wgt}(\mu^{\prime})<\mathit{wgt}(\mu)
. In addition,

\mu\mathbin{{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}}\mu^{\prime}
implies

\mathit{wgt}(\mu^{\prime})\mathbin{\leqslant}\mathit{wgt}(\mu)
.

For a distribution
\mu\mathbin{\in}\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
, the set
T_{\mu}
is
given by

T_{\mu}\mathrel{=}\{\>\mu^{\prime}\mid\mu^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu\land\mu\mathbin{{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}}\mu^{\prime}\>\}
. Consider the value

\inf\{\>\mathit{wgt}(\mu^{\prime})\mid{\mu^{\prime}\in T_{\mu}}\>\}
. By
Corollary
18
,
T_{\mu}
is a
sequentially compact set. Since the infimum over a sequentially
compact set will be reached, there exists a distribution
\sigma

such that
\mu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\sigma
,
\sigma\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu
, and

\mathit{wgt}(\sigma)=\inf\{\>\mathit{wgt}(\mu^{\prime})\mid{\mu^{\prime}\in T_{\mu}}\>\}
. By
definition of
T_{\mu}
, the distribution
\sigma
must be stable.
∎

We have arrived at the main result of the paper, slightly more general
formulated compared to the description in the introduction. The
message remains the same: if two distributions are branching
probabilistic bisimilar and have components that are branching
probabilistic bisimilar, then the components that remain after
cancelling the earlier components are also branching probabilistic
bisimilar. As we see, the previous lemma is essential in the proof as
given.

Theorem 22

(Cancellativity)
.

Let
\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathit{Distr}(\mathcal{E})
and
0<r\leqslant 1
be
such that
\mu\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\nu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu^{\prime}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\nu^{\prime}
and

\nu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu^{\prime}
. Then it holds that
\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.17776pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Choose
\mu
,
\mu^{\prime}
,
\nu
,
\nu^{\prime}
, and
r
according to the
premise of the theorem. By Lemma
21
, a stable
distribution
\sigma
exists such that

\mu\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\nu{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\sigma
and

\sigma\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\nu
. By weak decomposability, we can
find distributions
\bar{\mu}
and
\bar{\nu}
such that

\sigma{\mathrel{\to}^{\,{}\;\,}}{}\bar{\mu}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}
,
\bar{\mu}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu
, and

\bar{\nu}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu
. By stability of
\sigma
we have

\sigma=\bar{\mu}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}
. Thus
\bar{\mu}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}
is
stable. Symmetrically, there are distributions
\bar{\mu}^{\prime}

and
\bar{\nu}^{\prime}
such that
\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu^{\prime}
,

\bar{\nu}^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\nu^{\prime}
and such that
\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}^{\prime}
is
stable. Note,

\bar{\mu}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\nu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu^{\prime}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\nu^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}^{\prime}
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathcal{E}
be an equivalence class
of
\mathcal{E}/{\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}}
. The distributions
\bar{\mu}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}

and
\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}^{\prime}
are stable and

{\bar{\mu}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}{\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}^{\prime}}
. From
Lemma
20
we obtain that

(\bar{\mu}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu})[C]=(\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}^{\prime})[C]
. Since

\nu
and
\bar{\nu}
are stable and
\bar{\nu}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\nu}^{\prime}
, we have

\bar{\nu}[C]=\bar{\nu}^{\prime}[C]
for the same reason. Because

(\bar{\mu}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu})[C]=r\cdot\bar{\mu}[C]+(1{-}r)\cdot\bar{\nu}[C]
and

(\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}^{\prime})[C]=r\cdot\bar{\mu}^{\prime}[C]+(1{-}r)\cdot\bar{\nu}^{\prime}[C]
, we calculate

r\cdot\bar{\mu}[C]=(\bar{\mu}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu})[C]-(1{-}r)\cdot\bar{\nu}[C]=(\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\mathbin{\mbox{${}_{\scriptstyle r\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip-0.8pt\oplus\,$}}\bar{\nu}^{\prime})[C]-(1{-}r)\cdot\bar{\nu}^{\prime}[C]=r\cdot\bar{\mu}^{\prime}[C]\mkern 1.0mu.

Since
r\neq 0
, it follows
\bar{\mu}[C]=\bar{\mu}^{\prime}[C]
. Since

\bar{\mu}
and
\bar{\mu}^{\prime}
are stable it follows by Lemma
20

that
\bar{\mu}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\mu}^{\prime}
. Consequently,

\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\mu}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu^{\prime}
. In particular

\mu\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[8.00003pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}_{\,b}$}\mkern-1.0mu}\mu^{\prime}
, as was to be shown.
∎

7
Concluding remarks

We have shown a cancellation law for distributions with
respect to branching probabilistic bisimilarity.
The result rests on the notion of a stable distribution.
Stable distributions enjoy two properties that have been essential to
our set-up.
(i) Every distribution has a weak unfolding towards a stable
distribution that is branching probabilistic bisimilar.
(ii) Branching probabilistic
bisimilarity for stable distributions is determined by their summed
probability for equivalence classes of non-deterministic processes.
Techniques from metric topology have been used to establish the first
result.

We used the cancellativity result in
[
17
]
in
order to obtain a complete axiomatisation of branching probabilistic
bisimilarity.
The technical report
[
16
]
contains a proof
sketch in line with this paper.
Yet, as cancellativity is such a fundamental property, and the notion
of branching probabilistic bisimulation is mathematically quite
involved, we regard it necessary to provide a full, detailed proof.

The continuity results of Section
5
, as well as the
argumentation from metric topology at other places, are exploited to
deal with the uncountable number of inert transitions that arise from
combined transitions.
One may wonder if the main theorems of the paper can be achieved based
on combinatorial arguments.
Intuitively, transitions span a convex polyhedron and the
uncountability of the branching of transitions may be reduced to the
finiteness of the transitions spanning the polyhedron.
Despite a number of attempts, we have been forced to leave
the question of a simpler combinatorial proof open.

We leave it as open question for future research weather
cancellativity holds for larger classes of probabilistic processes, as
could be obtained, for instance, by adding recursion, uncountable
choice and/or parallel composition to the syntax.
A further topic for
future research is the study of cancellativity for other weak variants
of probabilistic bisimulation, in particular weak probabilistic
bisimulation.

Other future work is to be devoted
to the construction of an efficient decision algorithm for branching
probabilistic bisimilarity.
A decision procedure for strong probabilistic bisimilarity based on
so-called extended ordered binary trees has been proposed
in
[
5
]
. An improved algorithm based on partition
refinement is presented in
[
19
]
.
Partition refinement algorithms for weak and branching probabilistic
bisimilarity on states are proposed in
[
30
]
.
Reduction of weak probabilistic bisimilarity checking of the
state-based approach of
[
9
]
to linear programming is
studied in
[
13
]
.
Although it is currently not clear how to construct an algorithm
deciding branching probabilistic bisimilarity as put forward in this
paper, it is likely that the procedures of
[
18
]
and
[
30
]

can serve as a starting point.

Real Poincaré series of a plane divisorial valuation
A. Campillo, F. Delgado, S.M. Gusein-Zade

Abstract
Earlier, there was computed the Poincaré series of a valuation or of a collection of valuations on the ring of germs of holomorphic functions in two variables. For a collection of several plane curve valuations it appeared to coincide with the Alexander polynomial of the corresponding algebraic link. Recently, the authors defined Poincaré series of a valuation or of a collection of valuations in the real setting. (Actually, there were defined three versions of them, however, one of them was found to be “not computable”.) These two Poincar’e series were computed for one plane curve valuation. Here we compute them for a plane divisorial valuation.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{K}
be either the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
or the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
,
let
(V,0)
be a germ of an analytic variety over
\mathbb{K}
, and let
{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathbb{K}}_{V,0}
be the
ring of germs of functions on
(V,0)
.
(For
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
(the ring of complex numbers)
{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathbb{K}}_{V,0}

is usually denoted by
{\mathcal{O}}_{V,0}
.)
A function

\nu:{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathbb{K}}_{V,0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup{\{+\infty\}}

is a (discrete, rank one)
valuation
on
{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathbb{K}}_{V,0}
if

1)

\nu(0)=+\infty
;

2)

\nu(\lambda f)=\nu(f)
for
f\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathbb{K}}_{V,0}
,

\lambda\in\mathbb{K}^{*}:=\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}
;

3)

\nu(f_{1}+f_{2})\geq\min\{\nu(f_{1}),\nu(f_{2})\}
for
f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathbb{K}}_{V,0}
;

4)

\nu(f_{1}f_{2})=\nu(f_{1})+\nu(f_{2})
for
f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathbb{K}}_{V,0}
.

(Sometimes, for a valuation, one demands that
\nu(f)\neq+\infty
for
f\neq 0
. In this case a function described above is usually called a pre-valuation.)

For a valuation
\nu
on
{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathbb{K}}_{V,0}
and
v\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

J(v)=J_{\mathbb{K}}(v):=\{f\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathbb{K}}_{V,0}:\nu(f)\geq v\}
.
The
Poincaré series
of the valuation
\nu
is

P_{\nu}(t)=\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(J(v)/J(v+1)\right)\cdot t^{v}\,.

(C,0)
be a germ of an irreducible curve on

The main (and the most effective) way to compute the
Poincaré series of a collection of valuations on

(sometimes with additional structures, say,

Theorem 2
.

One has

if
\delta=\overline{\delta}
, then

\displaystyle\ P_{\nu}^{\chi}(t)=P_{\nu}(t)\cdot\frac{1-t^{m_{\delta}}}{1+t^{m_{\delta}}};

if
\delta\neq\overline{\delta}
, then

\displaystyle\ P_{\nu}^{\chi}(t)=P_{\nu}(t)\cdot\frac{1-t^{m_{\rho}}}{1+t^{m_{\rho}}}.

Proof.
.

The coefficients of the series

P_{\nu}^{\chi}(t)
are equal to the residues
of the corresponding coefficients
b_{v}
of the
series
P_{\nu}(t)=\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}b_{v}t^{v}
modulo
2
, i. e.,
the coefficient of
t^{v}
in
P_{\nu}^{\chi}(t)

is equal to
0
if
b_{v}
is even and is equal
to
1
if
b_{v}
is odd. The equations for

P_{\nu}(t)
are somewhat different for real

\delta
(i. e.,
\delta=\overline{\delta}
) and for
\delta
not real
(i. e.,
\delta\neq\overline{\delta}
).
Let us consider
these cases separately.

Case
\delta=\overline{\delta}
.

One can write

P_{\nu}(t)\cdot\frac{1-t^{m_{\delta}}}{1+t^{m_{\delta}}}=\frac{P^{S}(t)}{1-t^{m_{\delta}}}\cdot\frac{1-t^{m_{\delta}}}{1+t^{m_{\delta}}}=P^{S}(t)\cdot\frac{1-t^{m_{\delta}}}{1-t^{2m_{\delta}}}=Q(t)\cdot(1-t^{m_{\delta}})\,.

The coefficients of the series
Q(t)=\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}q_{v}\cdot t^{v}
can be
computed in the following way. If
v\notin S
, then
q_{v}=0
. Let
v\in S
be such
that
v=k(2m_{\delta})+v^{\prime}
, where
v^{\prime}\in S
and

v^{\prime}-2m_{\delta}\notin S
. Then
q_{v}=k+1
.
Now,

Q(t)\cdot(1-t^{m_{\delta}})=\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}(q_{v}-q_{v-m_{\delta}})t^{v}.

Let
v^{\prime}=\varepsilon m_{\delta}+v^{\prime\prime}
, where
\varepsilon

is equal to
0
or
1
,
v^{\prime\prime}\in S
, and

v^{\prime\prime}-m_{\delta}\notin S
. Then one has the following possibilities:

•

If
\varepsilon=1
, then
v-m_{\delta}=k(2m_{\delta})+v^{\prime\prime}
and therefore

q_{v-m_{\delta}}=k+1
,
q_{v}-q_{v-m_{\delta}}=0
.
Moreover,

v=k(2m_{\delta})+m_{\delta}+v^{\prime\prime}=(2k+1)m_{\delta}+v^{\prime\prime}
and thus

b_{v}=2k+2\equiv 0\mod 2
.

•

If
\varepsilon=0
, then
v=k(2m_{\delta})+v^{\prime\prime}
,

v-m_{\delta}=(k-1)(2m_{\delta})+(m_{\delta}+\epsilon^{\prime})
and therefore

q_{v-m_{\delta}}=k
and
q_{v}-q_{v-m_{\delta}}=1
.
Moreover,

v=k(2m_{\delta})+v^{\prime\prime}
and
v^{\prime\prime}-m_{\delta}\notin S
, thus

b_{v}=2k+1\equiv 1\mod 2
.

Case
\delta\neq\overline{\delta}
.

One can write

P_{\nu}(t)\cdot\frac{1-t^{m_{\rho}}}{1+t^{m_{\rho}}}=\frac{P_{\nu_{C}}(t)}{1-t^{M_{\delta}}}\cdot\frac{1-t^{m_{\rho}}}{1+t^{m_{\rho}}}=\frac{P^{S}(t)\cdot(1+t^{m_{\rho}})}{1-t^{M_{\delta}}}\cdot\frac{1-t^{m_{\rho}}}{1+t^{m_{\rho}}}=Q(t)\cdot(1-t^{m_{\rho}})\;,

where
Q(t)=P^{S}(t)/(1-t^{M_{\delta}})=\sum q_{v}t^{v}
.
The coefficients
q_{v}
can be computed as above, i. e.,
write
v=kM_{\delta}+v^{\prime}
such that
v^{\prime}-M_{\delta}\notin S
, then

q_{v}=k+1
.
Since
P_{\nu}(t)=\sum b_{v}t^{v}=Q(t)\cdot(1+t^{m_{\rho}})
, one has

b_{v}=q_{v}+q_{v-m_{\rho}}
for all
v\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Now,
v-m_{\rho}=kM_{\delta}+(v^{\prime}-m_{\rho})
and therefore

•

If
v^{\prime}-m_{\rho}\in S
, then
q_{v-m_{\rho}}=q_{v}=k+1
and thus

b_{v}=2q_{v}=2(k+1)\equiv 0\mod 2
.

•

If
v^{\prime}-m_{\rho}\notin S
, then

v-m_{\rho}=(k-1)M_{\delta}+(M_{\delta}-m_{\rho}+v^{\prime})
.
We know that
M_{\delta}=m_{\delta}+\ell m_{\rho}
with

\ell\in\mathbb{Z},\ell\geq 1
. Thus

v^{\prime\prime}=M_{\delta}-m_{\rho}+v^{\prime}\in S
and
v^{\prime\prime}-M_{\delta}\notin S
.
This implies that

q_{v-m_{\rho}}=k=q_{v}-1
and therefore

b_{v}=2q_{v}-1=2k+1\equiv 1\mod 2
.

On the other hand,
Q(t)(1-t^{m\rho})=\sum(q_{v}-q_{v-m_{\rho}})\cdot t^{v}
and
the computations above show also that
the coefficients are

q_{v}-q_{v-m_{\rho}}=0
if
v^{\prime}-m_{\rho}\in S
and

q_{v}-q_{v-m_{\rho}}=1
if
v^{\prime}-m_{\rho}\notin S
.
This proves the statement of the Theorem.
∎

Introduction and statements of the results

The moduli space of parabolic
\Lambda

[

]

\Lambda

[

Let

Let

2.
Preliminaries

We recall the notion of parabolic vector bundles and parabolic connections on a parabolic vector bundle over a compact Riemann surface.
Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 3
. Let
S=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
be a finite subset of
X
, which we call the set of parabolic points. Let

E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
X
.

A
quasi-parabolic structure
on
E
at a point
x\in X
is a
strictly decreasing flag

E_{x}=E^{1}_{x}\supseteq E^{2}_{x}\supseteq\dotsb\supseteq E^{k}_{x}\supseteq E^{k+1}_{x}=0

of linear subspaces in the fibre
E_{x}
. We set

m^{x}_{j}=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(E^{j}_{x})-\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(E^{j+1}_{x}).

The integer
k
is called the
length
of the flag and the

k
-tuple
(m^{x}_{1},\dotsc,m^{x}_{k})
is called the
type

of the flag. We say that the flag is a full flag if
m^{x}_{j}=1
for
all
1\leq j\leq k
. A
parabolic structure
in
E
at
x
is
just a quasi-parabolic structure at
x
together with a sequence of
real numbers

0\leq\alpha^{x}_{1}<\dotsb<\alpha^{x}_{k}<1.

The real
numbers
\alpha^{x}_{j}
are called the
weights
.
We denote by
\alpha=\{(\alpha_{1}^{x},\ldots,\alpha_{k}^{x})\}_{x\in S}
the system of real weights
corresponding to a fixed parabolic structure.

A
parabolic vector bundle
with parabolic structure on
S
is a
holomorphic vector bundle
E
together with a parabolic structure in

E
at each point
x\in S
. We shall write
E_{*}
to denote the
parabolic vector bundle with underlying vector bundle
E
.

For a
parabolic bundle
E_{*}
with system of weights
\alpha
the
parabolic degree
is defined to be
the real number

p\deg(E_{*})=\deg(E)+\sum_{x\in D}\sum_{j=1}^{k}m^{x}_{j}\alpha^{x}_{j},

where
\deg(E)
denotes the degree of
E
, and we put

p\mu(E_{*})=\frac{p\deg(E_{*})}{\mathrm{rk}(E)},

where
\mathrm{rk}(E)
is the rank of
E
. The real number
p\mu(E_{*})
is
called the parabolic slope of
E_{*}
.

Let
E_{*}
be a parabolic bundle and let
F
be a vector subbundle of

E
. Then the parabolic structure on
E
induces a parabolic
structure on
F
as follows: first we take the induced filtration on

F
. Next, for each
x\in S
and for every

j\in\{1,\dotsc,k_{x}\}
, we set

\alpha^{x}_{j}(F):=\alpha^{x}_{i}(E)
, where
i
is the largest
integer such that
F^{j}_{x}\subset E^{i}_{x}
and
k_{x}
is the length of
the flag in
F_{x}
. The vector bundle
F
together with this
parabolic structure is denoted by
F_{*}
and is called a parabolic
subbundle of
E
.

A parabolic bundle
E_{*}
is said to be
parabolic semistable
if
for every non-zero proper parabolic subbundle
F_{*}
we have

(2.1)

p\mu(F_{*})\leq p\mu(E_{*}).

The parabolic bundle
E_{*}
is said to be
parabolic stable
if
all the inequalities in (
2.1
) are strict.

In what follows, we consider the full flag filtration.
We fix the integers
r\geq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We can represent the system of weights
\alpha=\{(\alpha_{1}^{x},\ldots,\alpha_{k}^{x})\}_{x\in S}
in matrix form
(\alpha^{x_{i}}_{j})^{1\leq i\leq m}_{1\leq j\leq r}\in\mathbb{Q}^{mr}
.
For the definition of generic system of weights
\alpha
; see
[
2
, Definition 2.2]
, and for more details
see
[
4
]
.
Let

W^{(m)}_{r}(d):=\{\alpha=(\alpha^{x_{i}}_{j})^{1\leq i\leq m}_{1\leq j\leq r}\in\mathbb{Q}^{mr}\,|\,\alpha\,\text{is generic and}\,d+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha^{x_{i}}_{j}=0\}.

In
[
14
, Definition 2.2]
, Inaba defined the system of special weights, and the system of weights which is not special is called generic.
See
[
5
, section 2.2]

for the non-emptiness of the space
W^{(m)}_{r}(d)
of admissible parabolic weights.

Let

\alpha=(\alpha^{x}_{1},\ldots,\alpha^{x}_{r})_{x\in S}=(\alpha^{x_{i}}_{j})^{1\leq i\leq m}_{1\leq j\leq r}\in W^{(m)}_{r}(d)

be the fixed system of generic rational parabolic weights
corresponding to the full flag filtration.
Let
\mathcal{M}^{ss}(r,d,\alpha)
be the moduli space of
semi-stable parabolic vector bundles of rank
r
, degree
d
and weight system
\alpha
(see
[
16
]
). The moduli space

\mathcal{M}^{ss}(r,d,\alpha)
is a normal projective variety
of dimension

\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{M}^{ss}(r,d,\alpha))=r^{2}(g-1)+1+\frac{m(r^{2}-r)}{2}.

Moreover, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{s}(r,d,\alpha)
of stable parabolic
bundles is an smooth and open subset of
\mathcal{M}^{ss}(r,d,\alpha)
.

Let
\xi
be a fixed holomorphic line bundle over
X

of degree
d
such that

(2.2)

d=\deg{\xi}=-\sum_{x\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{x}.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{ss}(r,\alpha,\xi)
be the moduli space of semi-stable parabolic vector bundles on
X
of rank
r

and determinant
\xi
, that is,

\bigwedge^{r}E\cong\xi
, with weight system
\alpha
.
Then
\mathcal{M}^{ss}(r,\alpha,\xi)
is a projective variety of dimension (see
[
5
, p.n. 557]
)

(r^{2}-1)(g-1)+\frac{m(r^{2}-r)}{2}.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{s}(r,\alpha,\xi)\subset\mathcal{M}^{ss}(r,\alpha,\xi)
be the open subset parametrizing the stable parabolic bundles. This is an irreducible smooth variety.

Since the full flag weight system
\alpha

is generic (see
[
2
, Definition 2.2]
), we have

\mathcal{M}(r,d,\alpha):=\mathcal{M}^{ss}(r,d,\alpha)=\mathcal{M}^{s}(r,d,\alpha),

and

\mathcal{M}(r,\alpha,\xi):=\mathcal{M}^{ss}(r,\alpha,\xi)=\mathcal{M}^{s}(r,\alpha,\xi).

Let
E_{*}
be a parabolic bundle. We say that
an endomorphism

\phi:E\longrightarrow E
is
strongly parabolic

if for every
x\in S
, we have

\phi(E^{i}_{x})\subset E^{i+1}_{x}.

Similarly,
\phi:E\longrightarrow E
is said to be

weakly
parabolic
or just
parabolic
if it satisfies

\phi(E^{i}_{x})\subset E^{i}_{x}.

The sheaf of strongly parabolic endomorphism on
E_{*}
is
denoted by
\mathrm{SParEnd}(E_{*})
and the sheaf of parabolic endomorphism on
E_{*}
is denoted by
\mathrm{ParEnd}(E_{*})
.

Now, we recall the notion of logarithmic connections (see
[
6
]
and
[
11
]
)
and its residues, and using these notions we define
parabolic connections.
Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
X
.
Let

S=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}

be the reduced effective
divisor associated with
S
.
A
logarithmic connection
on
E
singular over
S
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

(2.3)

D:E\to E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)=E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(S)

which satisfies the Leibniz identity

(2.4)

D(fs)=fD(s)+df\otimes s,

where
f
is a local section of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
s
is a
local section of
E
.

We next describe the notion of residues of a
logarithmic connection
D
in
E
singular over

S
.

Let
v\in E_{x_{\beta}}
be any vector in the fiber of
E

over
x_{\beta}\in S
. Let
U
be an open set around
x_{\beta}
and

s:U\to E
be a holomorphic section of
E
over
U

such that
s(x_{\beta})=v
. Consider the following
composition

\Gamma(U,E)\to\Gamma(U,E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(S))\to(E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(S))_{x_{\beta}}=E_{x_{\beta}},

where the equality is given because
for any
x_{\beta}\in S
, the fibre
(\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(S))_{x_{\beta}}
is canonically
identified with
\mathbb{C}
by sending a meromorphic form to
its residue at
x_{\beta}
.
Then, we have an endomorphism on
E_{x_{\beta}}

sending
v
to
D(s)(x_{\beta})
. We need to check that
this endomorphism is well defined. Let
s^{\prime}:U\to E
be another holomorphic section such that
s^{\prime}(x_{\beta})=v
. Then

(s-s^{\prime})(x_{\beta})=v-v=0.

Let
t
be a local coordinate at
x_{\beta}
on
U
such that

t(x_{\beta})=0
, that is, the coordinate system
(U,t)
is centered at
x_{\beta}
.
Since
s-s^{\prime}\in\Gamma(U,E)
and
(s-s^{\prime})(x_{\beta})=0
,
s-s^{\prime}=t\sigma

for some
\sigma\in\Gamma(U,E)
. Now,

\displaystyle D(s-s^{\prime})=D(t\sigma)

\displaystyle=tD(\sigma)+dt\otimes\sigma

\displaystyle=tD(\sigma)+t(\frac{dt}{t}\otimes\sigma),

and hence
D(s-s^{\prime})(x_{\beta})=0
, that is,

D(s)(x_{\beta})=D(s^{\prime})(x_{\beta})
.

Thus, we have a well defined
endomorphism, denoted by

(2.5)

Res(D,x_{\beta})\in\mathrm{End}(E)_{x_{\beta}}=\mathrm{End}(E_{x_{\beta}})

that sends
v
to
D(s)(x_{\beta})
. This endomorphism

Res(D,x_{\beta})
is called the
residue
of the logarithmic
connection
D
at the point
x_{\beta}\in S
(see
[
11
]
for the details).

From
[
18
, Theorem 3]
, for a logarithmic connection

D
singular over
S
, we have

(2.6)

\deg{E}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{Tr}(Res(D,x_{j}))=0,

where,
\deg{E}
denotes the degree of
E
, and

\mathrm{Tr}(Res(D,x_{j}))
denote the trace of the
endomorphism
Res(D,x_{j})\in\mathrm{End}(E_{x_{j}})
, for all

j=1,\ldots,m
.

Let
E_{*}
be a parabolic vector bundle over
X
with
a fixed system of weights
\alpha
for full flag filtration. A parabolic
connection (for the group
\text{GL}(r,\mathbb{C})
) on
E_{*}
is a logarithmic
connection
D
on the underlying vector bundle
E

satisfying following conditions:

(1)

For each
x\in S
the homomorphism
induced in the filtration over the fibre

E_{x}
satisfies

(2.7)

D(E^{i}_{x})\subset E^{i}_{x}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)|_{x}

for every
i=1,\ldots,r
.

(2)

For every
x\in S
and for every

i=1,\ldots,r
the action of
Res(D,x)
on the quotient
{E^{i}_{x}}/{E^{i+1}_{x}}
is the multiplication by

\alpha_{i}^{x}
. Since
Res(D,x)
preserves the filtration
it acts on each quotient.

We denote the parabolic connections by the pair

(E_{*},D)
.

Let
\xi
be the fixed line bundle of degree
d
and weight
\alpha

such that

(2.8)

d=\deg{\xi}=-\sum_{x\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{x}.

We want to fix a parabolic connection on
\xi
. We first equip
\xi
with a parabolic structure as follows.
Let
\xi
be given a trivial filtration at each
x\in S
with parabolic weight

\beta^{x}:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{x}.

We denote
\xi
with a parabolic structure by
\xi_{*}
.
Then, from (
2.8
), the parabolic degree of
\xi_{*}
is

p\deg(\xi_{*})=\deg(\xi)+\sum_{x\in S}\beta^{x}=\deg(\xi)+\sum_{x\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{x}=0.

Since
p\deg(\xi_{*})=0
, from
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
,

\xi_{*}
admits a parabolic connection
D_{\xi_{*}}

such that
Res(D_{\xi_{*}},x)=\beta(x)
for every

x\in S
. Now, we fix a pair
(\xi_{*},D_{\xi_{*}})
as
described above.

Next, given a pair
(E_{*},D)
, we say that
D
is a parabolic connection (for the group
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
)
if the logarithmic connection

\mathrm{Tr}(D):\bigwedge^{r}E\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{r}E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)

induced from
D

coincides with
D_{\xi_{*}}
, that is,
we have an isomorphism

(2.9)

((\bigwedge^{r}E)_{*},\mathrm{Tr}(D))\cong(\xi_{*},D_{\xi_{*}}).

A parabolic connection
(E_{*},D)
(for the group

\text{GL}(r,\mathbb{C})
or
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
) is said to be semi-stable (respectively, stable) if for every non-zero
proper parabolic subbundle
F_{*}
of
E_{*}
, which is
invariant under
D
, that is,

D(F)\subset F\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)
, we have

p\mu(F_{*})\leq p\mu(E_{*})~~\text{(respectively,}<).

Note that
(E_{*},D)

is stable does not imply that
E_{*}
is stable.

Recall that a parabolic connection
(E_{*},D)
is said to be
irreducible
if there does not exists a non-zero proper parabolic subbundle of
E_{*}
which is invariant
under
D
.

We have an useful result as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that a parabolic connection
(E_{*},D)
is
irreducible. Then
(E_{*},D)
is stable.

Let
(E_{*},D)
and
(E^{\prime}_{*},D^{\prime})
be two full flag parabolic connections with same generic weight
\alpha
.
A morphism between parabolic connections
(E_{*},D)
and
(E^{\prime}_{*},D)
is a parabolic morphism (already defined)

\phi:E_{*}\longrightarrow E^{\prime}_{*}

of parabolic vector bundles such that
the following diagram involving logarithmic connections

(2.10)

\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{D}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)}}
\textstyle{E^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{D^{\prime}}
\textstyle{E^{\prime}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)}

commutes. Moreover, we say that
(E_{*},D)

and
(E^{\prime}_{*},D^{\prime})
are isomorphic if

is an isomorphism.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

(E_{*},D)
and
(E^{\prime}_{*},D^{\prime})
be semi-stable full flag parabolic connections over
X
with same generic weight
\alpha
.
Then we have the followings.

(1)

Suppose
(E_{*},D)
and
(E^{\prime}_{*},D^{\prime})
are stable and
p\mu(E_{*})=p\mu(E^{\prime}_{*}).

If

\phi:(E_{*},D)\to(E^{\prime}_{*},D^{\prime})

is a non-zero morphism of parabolic connections, then it is an isomorphism.

(2)

If
(E_{*},D)
is stable, then the only parabolic endomorphisms of the pair
(E_{*},D)
are scalars.

Proof.

(1)

First note that a subbundle and its quotient bundle of a parabolic vector bundle admits a parabolic structure from its original parabolic bundle (see the remark after Definition 1.8 of
[
16
]
). We shall use usual technique of Kernel-Image and Image-Coimage sequences to prove the first part.

Let
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{*}\subset E_{*}
is a parabolic subbundle.
Then,
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{*}
is
D
-invariant parabolic subbundle of
E
. Since
(E_{*},D)
is stable, we have
p\mu(\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{*})<p\mu(E_{*})
. Since
\phi\neq 0
, we have
\mathrm{Im}(\phi)\neq 0
. Now,
\mathrm{Im}(\phi)
inherits a parabolic structure from
E^{\prime}_{*}
.

Consider the Kernel-Image short exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{*}\to E_{*}\to\mathrm{Im}(\phi)_{*}\to 0.

Then,
p\mu(E_{*})<p\mu(\mathrm{Im}(\phi)_{*})
.
Next, consider the Image-Coimage short exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Im}(\phi)_{*}\to E^{\prime}_{*}\to(E^{\prime}/\mathrm{Im}(\phi))_{*}\to 0.

Note that
\mathrm{Im}(\phi)_{*}
is a
D^{\prime}
-invariant parabolic subbundle of
E^{\prime}
. Since
(E^{\prime}_{*},D^{\prime})
is stable, we have

p\mu(\mathrm{Im}(\phi)_{*})<p\mu(E^{\prime}_{*})
. Thus, we get that

p\mu(E_{*})<p\mu(E^{\prime}_{*})
which contradicts the assumption that

p\mu(E_{*})=p\mu(E^{\prime}_{*})
. Therefore, on underlying vector bundles, we have
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)=0
and

\mathrm{Im}(\phi)=E^{\prime}
.

(2)

Let
\psi:(E_{*},D)\to(E_{*},D)
be the given endomorphism. Let
x\in X

3.
Picard group of the moduli spaces

The Picard group of moduli spaces is a very important
invariant while studying the classification
problems in algebraic geometry. In this section, we compute the Picard group of the moduli spaces

\mathcal{M}_{pc}(r,d,\alpha)
.

We first compute the dimension of the space of
isomorphic stable parabolic connections on a parabolic
vector bundle. Let
E_{*}
be a full flag parabolic vector bundle
over
X
with a fixed
\alpha\in W^{(m)}_{r}(d)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}onn_{\alpha}(E_{*})
denote the space of all
parabolic connections
D
on
E_{*}
such that
(E_{*},D)
is stable. Notice that
\mathcal{C}onn_{\alpha}(E_{*})
is an affine space modelled over the vector space
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)\otimes\mathrm{SParEnd}(E_{*}))
.

Given a parabolic automorphism
\Phi
of
E_{*}
and a parabolic connection
D
on
E_{*}
, the
\mathbb{C}
-linear morphism

\Phi\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)}\circ D\circ\Phi^{-1}

E_{*}
.
In fact,

defines a natural action of
\text{Aut}(E_{*})
on
\mathcal{C}onn_{\alpha}(E_{*})
, called gauge transformation.
We would like to compute the dimension of the quotient
space
\mathcal{C}onn_{\alpha}(E_{*})/\text{Aut}(E_{*})
, which
parametrizes all isomorphic parabolic connections on
E_{*}
.

The Lie algebra of the holomorphic automorphism group

\text{Aut}(E_{*})
is
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathrm{ParEnd}(E_{*}))
. Therefore,

\text{dim}~\text{Aut}(E_{*})=\text{dim}~\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathrm{ParEnd}(E_{*})).

Choose any
D\in\mathcal{C}onn_{\alpha}(E_{*})
. Since the pair
(E_{*},D)
is stable, from Lemma
2.2
(
2
) the isotropy subgroup

\text{Aut}(E_{*})_{D}=\{\Phi\in\text{Aut}(E_{*})~|~\Phi\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)}\circ D\circ\Phi^{-1}=D\}

consists of the scalar

.
Then, the dimension of the space
\mathcal{C}onn_{\alpha}(E_{*})/\text{Aut}(E_{*})
is

\displaystyle\text{dim}~\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)\otimes\mathrm{SParEnd}(E_{*}))-\text{dim}~\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathrm{ParEnd}(E_{*}))\,+\,1

\displaystyle\,=

\displaystyle\,\text{dim}~\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathrm{ParEnd}(E_{*}))-\text{dim}~\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathrm{ParEnd}(E_{*}))\,+\,1

(3.1)

\,=\,-\chi(\mathrm{ParEnd}(E_{*}))+1,

denotes the Euler-Poincaré characteristic of
\mathrm{ParEnd}(E_{*})
over
X
.

Clearly
\mathrm{ParEnd}(E_{*})
is a subsheaf of
\mathrm{End}(E)
. Then,
there is a natural skyscraper sheaf

supported on parabolic points

(3.2)

\chi(\mathrm{End}(E))=\chi(\mathrm{ParEnd}(E_{*}))+\chi(\mathcal{K}_{S})

Since we are considering full flag filtrations, from

Moreover, from Riemann-Roch Theorem, we have

Therefore, from (
3.1
), (
3.2
), (
3.3

\mathcal{P}=\mbox{d}(\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathcal{H}_{P},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{H}_{P}}))\subset\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathcal{H}_{P},\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{P}})
the space of all exact algebraic
1
-form.
Define a map

(4.21)

\theta:\mathrm{H}^{0}(T^{*}\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{T^{*}\mathcal{M}})\to\mathcal{P}

by
g\mapsto d\tilde{g}
, where
\tilde{g}
is the function which is defined by descent of
g
. Then
\theta
is an isomorphism.

From (
4.20
) and (
4.21
), we have

(4.22)

\theta:\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{S}ym^{k}T\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{P}

which is an isomorphism.
Let us denote the restriction of
h_{P}
on
T^{*}\mathcal{M}
by
h^{\prime}_{P}
.

Let
T_{h^{\prime}_{P}}=T_{T^{*}\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{H}_{P}}=\mathcal{K}er(\mbox{d}h^{\prime}_{P})

be the relative tangent sheaf on
T^{*}\mathcal{M}
,
where

\mbox{d}h^{\prime}_{P}:T(T^{*}\mathcal{M})\to{h^{\prime}}_{P}^{*}T\mathcal{H}_{P}

morphism
of bundles.

Note that

\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathcal{H}_{P},\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{P}})\subset\mathrm{H}^{0}(T^{*}\mathcal{M},T_{h^{\prime}_{P}})
, and hence from (
4.22
), we have
an injective homomorphism

\nu:\mathcal{P}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\theta(\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{S}ym^{k}T\mathcal{M}))\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(T^{*}\mathcal{M},T_{h^{\prime}_{P}}).

Consider the morphism

\mathrm{H}^{0}(T^{*}\mathcal{M},T_{h^{\prime}_{P}})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(T^{*}\mathcal{M},T_{h^{\prime}_{P}}\otimes T^{*}T^{*}\mathcal{M})

defined
by taking cup product with the first Chern class
c_{1}(p^{*}L)\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(T^{*}\mathcal{M},T^{*}T^{*}\mathcal{M})
.

Using the pairing

T_{h^{\prime}_{P}}\otimes T^{*}T^{*}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{O}_{T^{*}\mathcal{M}},

we get a homomorphism

\eta:\mathrm{H}^{0}(T^{*}\mathcal{M},T_{h^{\prime}_{P}})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(T^{*}\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{T^{*}\mathcal{M}})

Since
c_{1}(p^{*}L)=p^{*}c_{1}(L)
, we have

(4.23)

k\eta\circ\nu\circ\theta(\omega_{k})=\tilde{\mu}(\omega_{k}),

for all
\omega_{k}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{S}ym^{k}T\mathcal{M}))
.
Since
\nu
and
\theta
are injective homomorphisms, it is enough to
show that
\eta|_{\nu(\mathcal{P})}
is injective homomorphism.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{0\}
be a non-zero
exact
1
-form. Choose
a\in\mathcal{H}_{P}
such that
\omega(a)\neq 0
.
In view of (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), the generic fibre has the form

{h^{\prime}_{P}}^{-1}(a)=A\setminus F,

where
A
is an abelian variety and
F
is a subvariety of
A
such that
\mbox{codim}(F,A)\geq 2
.
Now,
\eta(\nu(\omega))\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(T^{*}\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{T^{*}\mathcal{M}})
and we have restriction map

\mathrm{H}^{1}(T^{*}\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{T^{*}\mathcal{M}})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}({h^{\prime}_{P}}^{-1}(a),\mathcal{O}_{{h^{\prime}_{P}}^{-1}(a)}).

Since
\omega(a)\neq 0
,
\eta(\nu(\omega))\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({h^{\prime}_{P}}^{-1}(a),\mathcal{O}_{{h^{\prime}_{P}}^{-1}(a)})
.
Because of the following isomorphisms

\mathrm{H}^{1}({h^{\prime}_{P}}^{-1}(a),\mathcal{O}_{{h^{\prime}_{P}}^{-1}(a)})\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(A,\mathcal{O}_{A})\cong\mathrm{H}^{0}(A,TA),

it follows that
\eta(\nu(\omega))\neq 0
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we generalise

Proposition 5.2
.

Consider the smooth divisor
\bf{H}_{0}
defined in Proposition
5.1
. Then,
the divisor
\bf{H}_{0}
is numerically effective if and only if the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{M}(r,\alpha,\xi)
is numerically effective.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{V})/\bf{H}_{0}}
denote the normal
bundle of the divisor
{\bf H}_{0}\subset\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{V})
,
where

\Psi:\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\xi}:=\mathcal{M}(r,\alpha,\xi)

is the vector bundle (see (
5.1
)) in Proposition
5.1
.

Recall that the effective divisor
\bf{H}_{0}
is numerically effective if and only if the restriction of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{V})}(\bf{H}_{0})
to
\bf{H}_{0}
is numerically effective.
From Poincaré adjunction formula we have the following

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{V})}(\bf{H}_{0})|_{\bf{H}_{0}}\cong\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{V})/\bf{H}_{0}}.

Therefore,
\bf{H}_{0}
is numerically effective if and only if the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{V})/\bf{H}_{0}}

T\mathcal{M}_{\xi}
is numerically effective if and only if the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(T\mathcal{M}_{\xi})}(1)
is numerically
effective.

Thus, to prove the proposition it is enough to show that
the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{V})/\bf{H}_{0}}
is canonically isomorphic to the tautological
line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(T\mathcal{M}_{\xi})}(1)
.

First we show that the divisor
\bf{H}_{0}
is canonically
isomorphic to projective bundle
\mathbf{P}(T\mathcal{M}_{\xi})
.
Let

\tilde{\Psi}:\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{V})\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\xi}

be the projective bundle. Let
E_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\xi}
, and

(5.3)

\theta\in\tilde{\Psi}^{-1}(E_{*})\cap\bf{H}_{0}\subset\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{V}).

Then,
\theta
represents a hyperplane in the fibre

\mathcal{V}(E_{*})=\pi_{\xi}^{-1}(E_{*})^{\vee}
of the vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
, where
\pi_{\xi}
is defined in
(
2.15
). Let
H_{\theta}
denote this hyperplane represented by
\theta
.
Note that
H_{\theta}\subset\pi_{\xi}^{-1}(E_{*})^{\vee}
and
\pi_{\xi}^{-1}(E_{*})

over the vector space
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)\otimes\mathrm{SParEnd^{\prime}}(E_{*})),

for any

\text{d}f\in(T^{*}_{E_{*}}\mathcal{M}_{\xi})^{*}=T_{E_{*}}\mathcal{M}_{\xi}.

Since
\theta\in\bf{H}_{0}
, and
H_{\theta}
is a hyperplane, the subspace of
T_{E_{*}}\mathcal{M}_{\xi}

generated by
\{\text{d}f\}_{f\in H_{\theta}}

\tilde{\theta}\in\mathbf{P}(T_{E_{*}}\mathcal{M}_{\xi})

Next, note that the fibre

\mathcal{V}(E_{*})\longrightarrow T_{E_{*}}\mathcal{M}_{\xi}

Since image of the hyperplane

A Better Angle on Hadron Transverse Momentum Distributions at the EIC Preprint: MIT-CTP 5462
Anjie Gao, Johannes K. L. Michel, Iain W. Stewart, Zhiquan Sun

Abstract
We propose an observable q ∗ q_{*} sensitive to transverse momentum dependence (TMD) in e ​ N → e ​ h ​ X eN\to ehX , with q ∗ / E N q_{*}/E_{N} defined purely by lab-frame angles. In 3D measurements of confinement and hadronization this resolves the crippling issue of accurately reconstructing small transverse momentum P h ​ T P_{hT} . We prove factorization for d ​ σ h / d ​ q ∗ \mathrm{d}\sigma_{h}/\mathrm{d}q_{*} for q ∗ ≪ Q q_{*}\ll Q with standard TMD functions, enabling q ∗ q_{*} to substitute for P h ​ T P_{hT} . A double-angle reconstruction method is given which is exact to all orders in QCD for q ∗ ≪ Q q_{*}\ll Q . q ∗ q_{*} enables an order-of-magnitude improvement in the expected experimental resolution at the EIC.



I
Introduction

A deeper understanding of the emergent properties of the nucleon, such as
confinement and hadronization,
has been a frontier of nuclear and particle physics research
since the inception of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) five decades ago.
An important one-dimensional view of the nucleon is provided by the deep-inelastic scattering (DIS) process

e^{-}(\ell)+N(P)\to e^{-}(\ell^{\prime})+X
,
where the scattering is mediated by an off-shell photon
of momentum
q=\ell-\ell^{\prime}
(with
Q^{2}\equiv-q^{2}>0
).
Confinement is probed by measurements of
x=Q^{2}/(2P\cdot q)
,
the momentum fraction carried by the colliding parton inside the nucleon
N
.
A more intricate view is obtained by identifying a hadron
h
in semi-inclusive DIS (SIDIS),

e^{-}(\ell)+N(P)\to e^{-}(\ell^{\prime})+h(P_{h})+X
.
Here measurements of the longitudinal momentum fraction
z=(P\cdot P_{h})/(P\cdot q)
that the hadron retains when forming from the struck quark give insight into the complex dynamics of hadronization.
Measuring the hadron’s
transverse
momentum
\vec{P}_{hT}
relative to
\vec{q}

gives access to a three-dimensional view of the confinement and hadronization processes for
N
and
h
,
together with spin correlations that probe these processes.

The region most sensitive to these dynamics occurs for small transverse momentum,
P_{hT}\sim\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}\ll Q
, where
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
is the QCD confinement scale.
Here the cross section obeys a rigorous factorization theorem
Collins 2011
, with the confinement and hadronization dynamics encoded in universal transverse momentum-dependent (TMD) parton distribution functions (PDFs) and fragmentation functions (FFs).
SIDIS cross sections have been extensively studied experimentally at HERMES
Airapetian et al. 2013
;
Airapetian et al. 2019
;
Airapetian et al. 2020
,
COMPASS
Alekseev et al. 2009
;
Aghasyan et al. 2018
;
Parsamyan 2018
,
RHIC
Aschenauer et al. 2015
;
Adamczyk et al. 2016
, and
JLab
Avakian et al. 2004
;
Jawalkar et al. 2018
;
Moran et al. 2022

and together with the Drell-Yan process
have enabled extractions of TMD PDFs and FFs (TMDs) by various groups, e.g.
Scimemi and Vladimirov 2020
;
Bacchetta et al. 2020
;
Bury et al. 2021
.
A key scientific goal of the upcoming Electron-Ion-Collider (EIC)
Abdul Khalek et al. 2022
is to study SIDIS with enormous beam luminosities to determine TMD PDFs and FFs with unprecedented precision. Progress has also been made towards calculations of TMD PDFs from lattice QCD
Ebert et al. 2019
;
Shanahan et al. 2020
;
Shanahan et al. 2021
;
Schlemmer et al. 2021
;
Li et al. 2022
;
Chu et al. 2022
.

A key challenge in experimental studies of TMDs is
that measurements of
\vec{P}_{hT}

require reconstructing the photon momentum (or Breit frame)
to great accuracy to avoid loss of precision on
P_{hT}=\lvert\vec{P}_{hT}\rvert\ll Q
.
A misreconstruction of
\vec{\ell}^{\prime}
by
\mathcal{O}(\Delta)
leads to a misreconstruction of
\vec{q}
and therefore
\vec{P}_{hT}
by
\mathcal{O}(\Delta)
, which is a large uncertainty for
P_{hT}\ll Q
.
For example, for a nominal measurement at
P_{hT}/z=1\,{\rm GeV}
with
Q=20\,{\rm GeV}
, a typical detector resolution of
\Delta=0.5\,{\rm GeV}
leads to a 50% uncertainty.
This puts in peril the EIC physics program to unveil the dynamics of hadronization and confinement in the kinematic region with the largest sensitivity.

In this paper we construct a novel SIDIS observable,
q_{*}
,
designed to be maximally resilient against resolution effects while delivering the same sensitivity to TMD dynamics as
\vec{P}_{hT}
.
The key insight is that while the magnitude of the electron and hadron three momentum
is subject to limited detector resolution,
modern tracking detectors deliver near-perfect resolution on the
angles
of charged particle tracks.
We will therefore construct
q_{*}
to satisfy the following three criteria:
(i) it is purely defined in terms of lab-frame angles and the beam energies;
(ii) at small values
q_{*}\ll Q
, the differential cross section
\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}q_{*}
,
including spin correlations, still satisfies a rigorous factorization theorem
in terms of the standard TMD PDFs and FFs;
(iii) it does not dilute the statistical power of the available event sample.
Our construction is inspired by, but features key differences to,
the Drell-Yan
\phi^{*}_{\eta}
observable in hadron-hadron collisions
Banfi et al. 2011a
,
which has enabled tests of perturbative QCD from permil-level
Z
-pole measurements
at the Tevatron and LHC
Abazov et al. 2011
;
Aaij et al. 2013
;
Aad et al. 2016
;
Aaij et al. 2015
;
Aad et al. 2020
;
CMS Collaboration 2022
.

Below we define
q_{*}
in detail,
prove the factorization theorem for
q_{*}
with standard TMDs,
and evaluate the expected detector resolution,
statistical power, and resilience against systematic biases of
q^{*}
versus
\vec{P}_{hT}
.

IV.1
Constructing
q_{*}
with finite target mass

In the main text, we present our results in the light target mass limit
M\ll Q
.
Here, we give the corresponding results when fully retaining the target mass
M
, which can be important when
Q
is not sufficiently large or when
M
is large (such as for a nucleus). This amounts to including in our construction the dependence on

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{2xM}{Q}\,.

(S1)

We also have the following variable generalizations:

\displaystyle\epsilon=\frac{1-y-\frac{1}{4}y^{2}\gamma^{2}}{1-y+\frac{1}{4}y^{2}\gamma^{2}+\frac{1}{2}y^{2}}\,,\qquad\kappa_{\gamma}=\Bigl[1-\Bigl(\frac{m_{hT}\,\gamma}{zQ}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]^{-1/2}\,,\qquad m_{hT}^{2}=M_{h}^{2}+P_{hT}^{2}\,.

(S2)

Note that we still take
P_{hT},M_{h}\ll Q
(appropriate for example when
N
is a proton or ion and
h
is a pion),
and hence can still take
\kappa_{\gamma}=1
.
Only
\epsilon
receives mass corrections through
\gamma
.
We will show that the above corrections do not change any of the conclusions regarding the utility of
q_{*}
.

We continue to work with the leading power (LP) kinematics
\lambda\sim P_{hT}/(zQ)\ll 1
.
For the relationship between the acoplanarity angle
\phi_{\mathrm{acop}}^{\mathrm{rest}}

and the hadron transverse momentum
P_{hT}
, we now have

\displaystyle\tan\phi_{\mathrm{acop}}^{\mathrm{rest}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\phi_{h}\,P_{hT}}{zQ}\sqrt{\frac{1+4M^{2}x^{2}/Q^{2}}{1-M^{2}x^{2}y^{2}/Q^{2}-y}}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\phi_{h}\,P_{hT}}{zQ}\sqrt{\frac{1+\gamma^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}y^{2}/4-y}}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2})\,.

(S3)

We now wish to construct an optimized observable
q^{M}_{*}
for a massive target with
M\sim Q
,
such that
q^{M}_{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M\ll Q}}{{=}}q_{*}
while retaining the desired leading power relation
q^{M}_{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{LP}}}{{=}}-\sin\phi_{h}\frac{P_{hT}}{z}
.

First of all, we emphasize that the following relations presented in the main text
are independent of
M
:

\displaystyle y=1-\frac{\sin\theta_{h}}{\cos\alpha}\,,\qquad Q^{2}=(\ell^{0}_{\mathrm{rest}})^{2}\Bigl[\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{e}}{\cos^{2}\alpha}-\bigl(1-\frac{\sin\theta_{h}}{\cos\alpha}\bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\,.

(S4)

Furthermore, we notice that
\gamma^{2}
can also be written in terms of angles in a manner that is independent of
M
:

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}=\frac{4M^{2}x^{2}}{Q^{2}}=\frac{M^{2}Q^{2}}{(P\cdot q)^{2}}=\Bigl(\frac{\sin\theta_{e}}{\cos\alpha-\sin\theta_{h}}\Bigr)^{2}-1\,.

(S5)

This allows us to define
q^{M}_{*}
in terms of
target rest frame
quantities
completely free of
M
dependence:

\displaystyle q^{M}_{*}\equiv-\ell^{0}_{\mathrm{rest}}\frac{[\cos(\theta_{e}+\theta_{h})+\cos\theta_{h}]\tan(\frac{\theta_{e}}{2})\sin\theta_{h}}{\sin(\theta_{e}+\theta_{h})}\,\tan\phi_{\mathrm{acop}}^{\mathrm{rest}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{LP}}}{{=}}-\sin\phi_{h}\frac{P_{hT}}{z}\,.

(S6)

This relation is especially useful for fixed target experiments,
where all the quantities can be readily measured.
For collider experiments like the EIC, the above equation may be expressed in terms of lab frame quantities
simply by substituting

\tan\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}=\exp\bigl[\cosh^{-1}(P^{0}_{\mathrm{EIC}}/M)+\eta_{i}\bigr]
for
i=e,\,h
,
where
\eta_{i}
are the lab frame pseudorapidities,

\ell^{0}_{\mathrm{rest}}=\exp\bigl[\cosh^{-1}(P^{0}_{\mathrm{EIC}}/M)\bigr]\ell^{0}_{\mathrm{EIC}}
,
and
\phi_{\mathrm{acop}}^{\mathrm{rest}}=-\phi_{\mathrm{acop}}^{{\mathrm{EIC}}}
.
We remind the reader that the last relation is due to the different convention
for the orientation of the
z
axis at the EIC, see Fig.
1
.

We emphasize that since
q^{M}_{*}
has the desired LP limit
q^{M}_{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{LP}}}{{=}}-\sin\phi_{h}\frac{P_{hT}}{z}
,
the factorization formula for
\mathrm{d}\sigma/(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}q_{*}^{M})

is the same as written in Eq. (
III
), does not receive mass corrections,
and is valid for both fixed-target and collider experiments.

IV.2
Power corrections to double angle formulas for
Q^{2}
,
y
, and
x

In the main text, we give the expression of

and
y
in Eq. (
II
) in terms of lab frame angles
to leading order in
\lambda\sim P_{hT}/(zQ)
.
Here, we derive the leading power correction to these kinematic relations.
These results can be used to get an idea of the size of power corrections to an analysis, including both i) the size of corrections to the double angle construction for
x
,
y
, and

Note that in this section we still work in the massless target limit

We have the following relations:

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=(P+\ell)^{2}=2P\cdot\ell=\frac{Q^{2}}{xy}\,,

(S7a)

\displaystyle\frac{(2P^{0}_{\mathrm{EIC}})^{2}}{s}\,\exp(-2\eta_{e})

\displaystyle=\frac{P\cdot\ell^{\prime}}{\ell\cdot\ell^{\prime}}=\frac{P\cdot q-P\cdot\ell}{\ell\cdot q}=\frac{1}{x}\frac{1-y}{y}\,,

(S7b)

\displaystyle\frac{(2P^{0}_{\mathrm{EIC}})^{2}}{s}\exp(-2\eta_{h})

\displaystyle=\frac{P\cdot P_{h}}{\ell\cdot P_{h}}=\frac{P\cdot q}{x(\ell\cdot P)+\ell\cdot q+\frac{1}{z}\,\ell\cdot P_{hT}}

(S7c)

where
\eta_{e}
and
\eta_{h}
are lab frame pseudorapidities.
Note that we have indicated that Eq. (
S7c
) is the only equation here that receives corrections in
\lambda\sim P_{hT}/(zQ)
when expanding in this ratio.
Solving the above relations for
x
,
y
, and
Q^{2}
,
we get

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{(2P^{0}_{\mathrm{EIC}})^{2}}{s}e^{\eta_{e}+\eta_{h}}\,\biggl(1+\cos\phi_{h}\sqrt{1+e^{-\Delta\eta}}\,\frac{P_{hT}}{zQ}\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2}),

(S8a)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+e^{\Delta\eta}}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2}),

(S8b)

\displaystyle Q^{2}

\displaystyle=(2P^{0}_{\mathrm{EIC}})^{2}\frac{e^{\eta_{e}+\eta_{h}}}{1+e^{\Delta\eta}}\,\biggl(1+\cos\phi_{h}\sqrt{1+e^{-\Delta\eta}}\,\frac{P_{hT}}{zQ}\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2})\,,

(S8c)

where
\Delta\eta=\eta_{h}-\eta_{e}
is boost invariant along the
z

Notice that in Eq. (
S8b
),
y
does not receive linear corrections in
\lambda

An application of Eq. (
S8
) is to test the size of the power corrections in the expressions for
x
and
Q^{2}
in a given data set, and thus apply cuts to restrict the data to TMD regions where the leading term is dominant.

We can also invert the formulae in Eq. (

and
Q_{*}

The variables
x_{*},\,y_{*}
and

agree with the kinematic invariants

and

\displaystyle x_{*}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\bigl(2P_{\mathrm{EIC}}^{0}\bigr)^{2}}{s}e^{\eta_{e}+\eta_{h}}\,=x\biggl(1-\cos\phi_{h}\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-y}}\,\frac{P_{hT}}{zQ}\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2}),

(S9a)

\displaystyle y_{*}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{1+e^{\Delta\eta}}=y+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2}),

(S9b)

\displaystyle Q_{*}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\bigl(2P_{\mathrm{EIC}}^{0}\bigr)^{2}\frac{e^{\eta_{e}+\eta_{h}}}{1+e^{\Delta\eta}}\,=Q^{2}\biggl(1-\cos\phi_{h}\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-y}}\,\frac{P_{hT}}{zQ}\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2})\,.

(S9c)

The all-order definition of
q_{*}
in the main text, Eq. (
3

q_{*}\equiv Q_{*}\sqrt{1-y_{*}}\tan\phi_{\mathrm{acop}}^{\mathrm{EIC}}
.
This allows us to easily determine the leading power correction to the formula for

obtained by expanding in

\displaystyle q_{*}=-\sin\phi_{h}\frac{P_{hT}}{z}\biggl(1-\frac{\cos\phi_{h}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-y}}\,\frac{P_{hT}}{zQ}\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{3})\,.

(S10)

This kinematic correction to the relationship between variables is the only power correction that would give non-trivial dependence on

\cos\phi_{h}

to the factorization formula in Eq. (
III

\delta(q_{*}+\ldots)

and
\phi_{h}
(cf. the example given in Eq. (
III

P_{hT}\ll zQ

\mathrm{d}\sigma/(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\vec{P}_{hT})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}q_{*}}=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{*}[f_{1}D_{1}]+\lambda_{e}S_{L}\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}\,\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{*}[g_{1L}D_{1}]

\displaystyle\qquad+\epsilon\,\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{*}\!\left[\left[(\hat{h}\cdot\hat{x}^{\prime})^{2}-(\hat{h}\cdot\hat{y})^{2}\right]\frac{-2(\hat{h}\cdot{\vec{k}}_{T})(\hat{h}\cdot{\vec{p}}_{T})+{\vec{k}}_{T}\cdot{\vec{p}}_{T}}{MM_{h}}\,h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1}^{\perp}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+S_{T}\cos\phi_{S}\Biggl(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{*}\left[(\hat{h}\cdot\hat{y})\frac{-\hat{h}\cdot{\vec{k}}_{T}}{M}f_{1T}^{\perp}D_{1}\right]+\epsilon\,\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{*}\!\left[(\hat{h}\cdot\hat{y})\frac{-\hat{h}\cdot{\vec{p}}_{T}}{M_{h}}h_{1}H_{1}^{\perp}\right]\Biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad+S_{T}\cos\phi_{S}\,\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{*}\!\left[\left[3(\hat{h}\cdot\hat{y})-4(\hat{h}\cdot\hat{y})^{3}\right]\frac{2(\hat{h}\cdot{\vec{k}}_{T})\,(\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{p}_{T})+k_{T}^{2}\,(\hat{h}\cdot{\vec{p}}_{T})-4(\hat{h}\cdot{\vec{k}}_{T})^{2}\,(\hat{h}\cdot{\vec{p}}_{T})}{2M^{2}M_{h}}\,h_{1T}^{\perp}H_{1}^{\perp}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda_{e}S_{T}\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}\sin\phi_{S}\,\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{*}\!\left[(\hat{h}\cdot\hat{y})\frac{\hat{h}\cdot{\vec{k}}_{T}}{M}g_{1T}D_{1}\right]\,.

(S23)

Note that we have used
\sin(3\phi_{h})=3\sin\phi_{h}-4\sin^{3}\phi_{h}
.
Here we define
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{*}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{*}[\omega_{*}\,\mathcal{H}\,g\,D]\equiv 2z^{3}\sum_{f}\mathcal{H}_{f}(Q^{2})\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}{\vec{k}}_{T}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}{\vec{p}}_{T}\,\delta\bigl(q_{*}+\hat{y}\cdot{\vec{k}}_{T}-\hat{y}\cdot{\vec{p}}_{T}\bigr)\,\omega_{*}(\vec{k}_{T},\vec{p}_{T})\,g_{f}(x,k_{T})\,D_{f}(z,p_{T})\,,

(S24)

where the weight function
\omega_{*}({\vec{k}}_{T},{\vec{p}}_{T})
includes the full prefactor structures, and can be understood as product of
\kappa({\vec{k}}_{T},{\vec{p}}_{T})
and
\omega({\vec{k}}_{T},{\vec{p}}_{T})
in Eq. (
IV.4
).

IV.6
Details on Bayesian reweighting

IV.6.1
Theory templates

In this section we describe in detail how the pseudodata
d_{i}
and theory replicas
t_{i}(\omega_{i})

in the main text are constructed. They are defined as the normalized bin-integrated spectrum
for the observable
\mathcal{O}=P_{hT}/z
or
\mathcal{O}=q_{*}
being tested,

\displaystyle\{d_{n},t_{n}(\omega_{i})\}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{d}\sigma/(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}Q^{2})}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{a,n}}^{\mathcal{O}_{b,n}}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{O}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{O}}\,,

(S25)

where the right-hand side is evaluated with the appropriate
values of
\omega_{i}
(either central or chosen according to the Monte-Carlo replica) inserted into Eq. (
IV
).
Restricting Eqs. (
III
) and (
III
)
to the unpolarized contribution from
W_{UU,T}
, the differential leading-power spectrum
explicitly reads

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{O}}=\frac{2yz^{3}}{Q^{2}}\,\sigma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}b_{T}\,K_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}b_{T})\sum_{f}\mathcal{H}_{f}(Q^{2},\mu)\,\tilde{f}_{1f}(x,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)\,\tilde{D}_{1f}(z,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)\,,

(S26)

where
\mu
is the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scale that the TMD PDF and FF are being evolved to,

\zeta=Q^{2}
is the Collins-Soper scale, and

\displaystyle K_{P_{hT}/z}(x)=xJ_{0}(x)\,,\qquad K_{q_{*}}=\frac{2\cos(x)}{\pi}\,,

(S27)

is the integral kernel that depends on the respective observable.
At tree-level, the hard function simply reduces to the electric charge,
\mathcal{H}_{f}=e_{f}^{2}
.
Using tree-level matching onto collinear PDFs and FFs,
the evolved TMDs are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{1f}(x,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle=f_{1f}(x,\mu)\,U(b_{T},\mu_{0},\zeta_{0},\mu,\zeta)\,\tilde{f}_{1}^{\,\mathrm{NP}}(x,b_{T})\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{1f}(z,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle=D_{1f}(z,\mu)\,U(b_{T},\mu_{0},\zeta_{0},\mu,\zeta)\,\tilde{D}_{1}^{\mathrm{NP}}(z,b_{T})\,,

(S28)

where
\tilde{f}_{1}^{\,\mathrm{NP}}
and
\tilde{D}_{1}^{\mathrm{NP}}
are the nonperturbative model function of interest
and satisfy
\tilde{f}_{1}^{\,\mathrm{NP}}(x,b_{T}),\tilde{D}_{1}^{\mathrm{NP}}(z,b_{T})=1+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{2}b_{T}^{2})
.
Here we have used that at our leading-logarithmic working order,
we are free to evaluate the collinear PDF
f_{1f}(x,\mu)
and collinear FF
D_{1f}(z,\mu)

at the high scale
\mu\sim Q
.
The evolution factor
U
accounts for the virtuality and Collins-Soper evolution
of the TMD PDF and at leading-logarithmic order is given by

\displaystyle U(b_{T},\mu_{0},\zeta_{0},\mu,\zeta)=\exp\Bigl[-\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}}{\mu^{\prime}}\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu^{\prime})}{2\pi}\,\Gamma_{0}^{q}\ln\frac{\sqrt{\zeta}}{\mu^{\prime}}\Bigr]\,\exp\Bigl[\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\gamma}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{NP}}(b_{T})\ln\frac{\zeta}{\zeta_{0}}\Bigr]\,,

(S29)

where
\Gamma_{0}^{q}=4C_{F}
is the one-loop quark cusp anomalous dimension
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{NP}}(b_{T})=\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{2}b_{T}^{2})
is the nonperturbative contribution
to the Collins-Soper kernel.
The initial scales
\mu_{0}
and
\zeta_{0}
define
the boundary condition at which the nonperturbative TMDs are defined.
We use the prescription
Bacchetta et al. 2020
;
Bacchetta et al. 2022

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(b_{T})=\frac{b_{0}}{b_{\mathrm{max}}}\Bigl(\frac{1-e^{-b_{T}^{4}/b_{\mathrm{max}}^{4}}}{1-e^{-b_{T}^{4}/b_{\mathrm{min}}^{4}}}\Bigr)^{-1/4}\,,\quad b_{0}=2e^{\gamma_{E}}\approx 1.123\,,\quad b_{\mathrm{max}}=b_{0}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}\,,\quad b_{\mathrm{min}}=\frac{b_{0}}{\mu}\,,\quad\zeta_{0}=1\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2}\,.

(S30)

This ensures that the coupling
\alpha_{s}(\mu^{\prime})
under the integral in Eq. (
S29
)
remains perturbative due to
\mu^{\prime}\geq b_{0}/b_{\mathrm{max}}=1\,\mathrm{GeV}
.
In addition,
\mu_{0}(b_{T}\to 0)\to b_{0}/b_{\mathrm{min}}=\mu

together with
\gamma_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{NP}}\to 0

ensures that
U\to 1
for
b_{T}\to 0
.
We use N
3
LL
\alpha_{s}
evolution with
n_{f}=5
active flavors
and evolve to
\mu=\sqrt{\zeta}=Q
throughout our predictions.

Several further simplifications apply to Eq. (
S25
).
We may analytically perform the bin integral
over
\mathcal{O}_{a}\leq\mathcal{O}\leq\mathcal{O}_{b}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}_{b}b_{T})-\tilde{K}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}_{a}b_{T})\equiv b_{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{a}}^{\mathcal{O}_{b}}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{O}\,K_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}b_{T})\,,\qquad\tilde{K}_{P_{hT}/z}(x)=xJ_{1}(x)\,,\qquad\tilde{K}_{q_{*}}(x)=\frac{2\sin(x)}{\pi}\,.

(S31)

An important limit is the total integral over
\mathcal{O}
, which involves the integral kernel

\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{O}_{b}\to\infty}\frac{1}{b_{T}}\tilde{K}_{P_{hT}/z}(\mathcal{O}_{b}b_{T})=\lim_{\mathcal{O}_{b}\to\infty}\frac{1}{b_{T}}\tilde{K}_{q_{*}}(\mathcal{O}_{b}b_{T})=\delta(b_{T})\,.

(S32)

This allows us to analytically evaluate the total cross section
that appears in the denominator in Eq. (
S25
),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}Q^{2}}=\frac{2yz^{3}}{Q^{2}}\,\sigma_{0}\sum_{f}e_{f}^{2}\,f_{1f}(x,\mu)\,D_{1f}(z,\mu)\,,

(S33)

which recovers the tree-level SIDIS total cross section.
Taking the ratio in Eq. (
S25
), many factors drop out,
including in particular the sum over collinear PDFs and FFs,
leaving behind only the flavor-independent model functions and TMD evolution,

\displaystyle\{d_{n},t_{n}(\omega_{i})\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{T}}{b_{T}}\,\Bigl[\tilde{K}_{\mathcal{O}}(b_{T}\mathcal{O}_{b,n})-\tilde{K}_{\mathcal{O}}(b_{T}\mathcal{O}_{a,n})\Bigr]\,\bigl[U(b_{T},\mu_{0},\zeta_{0},\mu,\zeta)\bigr]^{2}\,\tilde{f}_{1}^{\,\mathrm{NP}}(x,b_{T})\,\tilde{D}_{1}^{\mathrm{NP}}(z,b_{T})\,,

(S34)

for
\mathcal{O}=P_{hT}/z
and
\mathcal{O}=q_{*}
.
It is easy to see from Eq. (
S34
) and Eq. (
S32
)
that the pseudodata and theory templates indeed satisfy

\sum_{n}d_{n}=\sum_{n}t_{n}(\omega_{i})=1
,
where we note that we include an overflow bin
\mathcal{O}\geq 4\,\mathrm{GeV}

in all sums over
n
, including in particular the likelihood function used in the main text.

The simple form of the normalized
P_{hT}/z
and
q_{*}
spectrum in Eq. (
S34
)
in terms of the underlying nonperturbative TMD distributions
ensures that our conclusions about the TMD sensitivity
are fully general, even though we consider fixed
x
and
z
.
This is because
x
and
z
only enter through
\tilde{f}_{1}^{\,\mathrm{NP}}(x,b_{T})
and
\tilde{D}_{1}^{\mathrm{NP}}(z,b_{T})
,
which for fixed
x
and
z
map onto specific values of
\omega_{\{1,2,3\}}
and
\alpha

in Eq. (
IV
) as discussed below,
and hence only affect the initial values of our study.
We stress again that the analytic relation
we exploited between the cross sections in TMD and collinear factorization
is due to our leading-logarithmic working order and the specific perturbative scheme choices we made,
but the simplifications above are fully justified for an exploratory study like this one.
In particular, Eq. (
S34
) still retains all the key characteristic
features of a realistic TMD study such as the correct amount of Sudakov suppression
at long distances.

IV.6.2
Nonperturbative model parameters and Bayesian priors

We work with the nonperturbative model used in the MAPTMD22 global fit
of TMD PDFs and FFs from Drell-Yan and SIDIS data
Bacchetta et al. 2022
.
The model for the CS kernel reads

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{NP}}(b_{T})=g_{K}(b_{T}^{2})=-g_{2}^{2}\frac{b_{T}^{2}}{2}

(S35)

Since our reweighting study is performed at fixed
Q
,
we do not expect to be sensitive to CS evolution between different values of
Q

and hold the parameter
g_{2}=(0.248\pm 0.008)\,\mathrm{GeV}
fixed at its central value.
(A full four-dimensional measurement differential in
x
,
z
,
Q
, and
q_{*}

would of course exhibit the usual sensitivity to the CS kernel.)

For fixed
x
and
z
, the nonperturbative models of
Bacchetta et al. 2022
for the TMD PDF and FF relate to Eq. (
IV
) as

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=\frac{g_{1}(x)}{4}\,,\qquad\omega_{2}=\frac{g_{3}(z)}{4z^{2}}\,,\qquad\omega_{3}=\frac{g_{3B}(z)}{4z^{2}}\,,\qquad\alpha=\frac{g_{3}(z)}{g_{3}(z)+\lambda_{F}\,g_{3B}^{2}(z)/z^{2}}\,,

(S36)

where
g_{\{1,3,3B\}}
are functions of
x
and
z
defined
in terms of the underlying model parameters in
Bacchetta et al. 2022
.
In the comparison, we have used that the fit result of
Bacchetta et al. 2022

down to
x\geq 0.1
is compatible with a single Gaussian for the TMD PDF
to a good approximation, and have set
\lambda=\lambda_{2}=0
.
For
x=0.1
and
z=0.15
as considered in the main text,
the first three parameters evaluate to

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=(0.0791\pm 0.0063)\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2}\,,\qquad\omega_{2}=(0.0167\pm 0.0059)\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2}\,,\qquad\omega_{3}=(0.8153\pm 0.0637)\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2}\,,

(S37)

In our reweighting study,
we take the variance of the Gaussian prior probability distributions for
\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\}

to be numerically equal to the
1\sigma
confidence intervals above.
(We ignore nondiagonal entries in the covariance matrix of
Bacchetta et al. 2022
,
which were found to be small there.)
We hold
\alpha=0.079\pm 0.029
fixed at its central value for simplicity.
We note that we have also performed the reweighting study
using a simplified version of the nonperturbative model used in
Scimemi and Vladimirov 2020
,
which features an approximately exponential term at large distances,
and have arrived at similar conclusions.

we apply an additional weight
\epsilon(X)

\Delta\epsilon_{X}
to the variance of the distribution
ensures that the impacts of
\Delta\epsilon_{X}
for different
X
are comparable
even when individual
X
have more or less narrow underlying distributions
or different mass dimension.
The explicit values of
\langle X\rangle
,
\Delta X
for the bin we consider
are collected in Table
S1

we take one of the bias parameters

B=\{\Delta\epsilon_{p_{e}},\Delta\epsilon_{\eta_{e}},\Delta\epsilon_{p_{h}},\Delta\epsilon_{\eta_{h}},\delta_{p_{e}}\}
to be nonzero at a time and evaluate the biased normalized pseudodata as

\displaystyle d_{n}^{\mathrm{bias}}=d_{n}\frac{h_{n}^{\mathrm{bias}}}{h_{n}}\biggl(\sum_{m}d_{m}\frac{h_{m}^{\mathrm{bias}}}{h_{m}}\biggr)^{-1}\,,

(S40)

where

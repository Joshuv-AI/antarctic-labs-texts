Abstract
. The ultimate goal of any numerical scheme for partial differential equations (PDEs) is to compute an approximation of user-prescribed accuracy at quasi-minimal computation time. To this end, algorithmically, the standard adaptive finite element method (AFEM) integrates an inexact solver and nested iterations with discerning stopping criteria balancing the different error components. The analysis ensuring optimal convergence order of AFEM with respect to the overall computational cost critically hinges on the concept of R-linear convergence of a suitable quasi-error quantity. This work tackles several shortcomings of previous approaches by introducing a new proof strategy. Previously, the analysis of the algorithm required several parameters to be fine-tuned. This work leaves the classical reasoning and introduces a summability criterion for R-linear convergence to remove restrictions on those parameters. Second, the usual assumption of a (quasi-)Pythagorean identity is replaced by the generalized notion of quasi-orthogonality from [Feischl, Math. Comp., 91 (2022)]. Importantly, this paves the way towards extending the analysis of AFEM with inexact solver to general inf-sup stable problems beyond the energy minimization setting. Numerical experiments investigate the choice of the adaptivity parameters.

Lemma 3.3

(tail summability criterion)
.

Let

be scalar sequences in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
With given constants
0<q<1
,
0<\delta<1
, and

, suppose that

(17)

a_{\ell+1}\lx@nobreakspace\leq qa_{\ell}+b_{\ell},\quad b_{\ell+N}\leq C_{1}\,a_{\ell},\,\,\text{and}\,\,\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{\ell+N}b_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}\leq C_{2}\,(N+1)^{1-\delta}\,a_{\ell}^{2}\,\,\,\text{for all }\ell,N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Then,

is R-linearly convergent, i.e.,
there exist

and

Proof 3.4

(
Proof of Theorem
3.1
)
.

We employ Lemma
3.3
for the sequences defined by
a_{\ell}=\eta_{\ell}(u_{\ell}^{\star})
and
b_{\ell}\coloneqq C_{\textup{stab}}\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u_{\ell+1}^{\star}-u_{\ell}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|
.
First, we note that

(19)

|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}^{\star}-u_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{axiom:reliability}}}{{\lesssim}}\eta_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(u_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}^{\star})+\eta_{\ell^{\prime}}(u_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\star})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:quasi-monotonicity}}}{{\lesssim}}\eta_{\ell}(u_{\ell}^{\star})\quad\text{for all $\ell,\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$}\text{ with $\ell\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell^{\prime\prime}$}.

In particular, this proves

for all

\ell,N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Moreover,

(A4)
) and

(A3)
) show

(20)

\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{\ell+N}\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u^{\star}_{\ell^{\prime}+1}-u^{\star}_{\ell^{\prime}}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|^{2}\leq C_{\textup{orth}}\,C_{\textup{rel}}^{2}\,(N+1)^{1-\delta}\,\eta_{\ell}(u^{\star}_{\ell})^{2}\quad\text{for all }\ell,N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

In order to verify (
17
), it thus only remains to prove the perturbed
contraction of
a_{\ell}
.
To this end, let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then, stability (
(A1)
) and

) show

\eta_{\ell+1}(u^{\star}_{\ell})^{2}\leq\eta_{\ell}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell+1}\cap\mathcal{T}_{\ell},u^{\star}_{\ell})^{2}+q_{\textup{red}}^{2}\eta_{\ell}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{\ell+1},u^{\star}_{\ell})^{2}=\eta_{\ell}(u_{\ell}^{\star})^{2}\!-\!(1\!-\!q_{\textup{red}}^{2})\,\eta_{\ell}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{\ell+1},u^{\star}_{\ell})^{2}.

Moreover, Dörfler marking (

) and refinement of (at least) all marked elements lead to

\theta\eta_{\ell}(u^{\star}_{\ell})^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:doerfler}}}{{\leq}}\eta_{\ell}(\mathcal{M}_{\ell},u^{\star}_{\ell})^{2}\leq\eta_{\ell}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{\ell+1},u^{\star}_{\ell})^{2}.

The combination of the two previously displayed formulas results in

\eta_{\ell+1}(u^{\star}_{\ell})\leq q_{\theta}\,\eta_{\ell}(u_{\ell}^{\star})\quad\text{with}\quad 0<q_{\theta}\coloneqq\big[1-(1-q_{\textup{red}}^{2})\theta\big]^{1/2}<1.

Finally, stability (
(A1)
) thus leads to the desired estimator reduction estimate

\eta_{\ell+1}(u^{\star}_{\ell+1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{axiom:stability}}}{{\leq}}q_{\theta}\,\eta_{\ell}(u_{\ell}^{\star})+C_{\textup{stab}}\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u_{\ell+1}^{\star}-u_{\ell}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Altogether, all the assumptions (
17
) are satisfied and Lemma
3.3
concludes the proof.

The following comments on the computational cost of implementations of standard finite element methods
underline the importance of full linear convergence (

).

•

Solve
&
Estimate.

The Dörfler marking strategy (

[
Ste07
]
for

Since the adaptive algorithm depends on the full history of

(rates = complexity)
.

For

In the previous sections, the particular focus was on general second-order linear elliptic PDEs (

[

Given a nonlinearity
\boldsymbol{A}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we consider the nonlinear elliptic PDE

(69)

-\operatorname{div}\big(\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla u^{\star})\big)=f-\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{f}\text{
in }\Omega\quad\text{subject to}\quad u^{\star}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

We define the associated nonlinear operator
\mathcal{A}\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{-1}(\Omega)\coloneqq H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{*}
via
\mathcal{A}u\coloneqq\langle\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla u),\nabla(\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, where we suppose
that the
L^{2}(\Omega)
scalar product on the right-hand side is well-defined.
Then, the weak formulation of (
69
) reads

(70)

\langle\mathcal{A}u^{\star},v\rangle=F(v)\coloneqq\langle f,v\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\boldsymbol{f},\nabla v\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all }v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on the left-hand side denotes the duality brackets
on
H^{-1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Let
a(\cdot,\cdot)
be an equivalent scalar product on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
induced norm
|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\,\cdot\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|
. Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is strongly monotone and
Lipschitz continuous, i.e., there exist
0<\alpha\leq L
such that, for all

u,v,w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

(71)

\alpha\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u-v|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|^{2}\leq\langle\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v,u-v\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v,w\rangle\leq L\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u-v|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|w|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|.

Under these assumptions, the Zarantonello theorem from
[
Zar60
]
(or
main theorem on strongly monotone operators
[
Zei90

yields existence and uniqueness of the solution

to (
70

For
\mathcal{T}_{H}\in\mathbb{T}
and

\langle\mathcal{A}u_{H}^{\star},v_{H}\rangle=F(v_{H})\quad\text{for all }v_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H},

12

As already discussed in Section
5
, the proof of the
Zarantonello theorem relies on the Banach fixed-point theorem:
For

From this and the telescopic sum, we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{\ell+N}\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u_{\ell^{\prime}+1}^{\star}-u_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:nonlinear:equivalence}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{\ell+N}\big[E(u_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\star})-E(u_{\ell^{\prime}+1}^{\star})\big]=E(u_{\ell}^{\star})-E(u_{\ell+N+1}^{\star})

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:nonlinear:minimization}}}{{\leq}}E(u_{\ell}^{\star})-E(u^{\star})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:nonlinear:equivalence}}}{{\leq}}\frac{L}{2}\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u^{\star}-u_{\ell}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|^{2}\quad\text{for all }\lx@nobreakspace\ell,N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Since the right-hand side is independent of
N
, we conclude the proof for
N\to\infty
.

Thus, full R-linear convergence from Theorem
5.1
and optimal complexity from Theorem
5.5
apply also to the nonlinear PDE (
69
) under the assumptions on the nonlinearity from Proposition
6.1
. Unlike
[
HPSV21
]
, we can guarantee full R-linear convergence (
48
) for arbitrary
\theta
, arbitrary
\lambda_{\textup{sym}}
, and a weaker constraint (
46
) on
\lambda_{\textup{alg}}
.
As in

[
HPSV21
, Theorem 5]
, optimal complexity follows if the adaptivity parameters are sufficiently small.

Remark 6.4
.

The cost-optimal numerical solution of nonlinear PDEs is widely open beyond the case of strongly monotone and Lipschitz continuous nonlinearities considered here. We stress that this problem class even excludes the
p
-Laplacian, for which linear convergence in
[
DK08
]
and optimal convergence rates in
[
BDK12
]
are known under the constraint of the exact solution of the arising nonlinear discrete systems. Convergent linearization strategies for the
p
-Laplacian are the topic of recent research, e.g.,
[
DFTW20
,
BDS23
,
Hei23
]
. However, optimal complexity appears to be still out of reach. Nevertheless, the present work could outline potential strategies also in this respect.

3.3

Proof A.1

(
Proof of Lemma
3.3
)
.

The proof is split into four steps.

Step 1.

We consider the perturbed contraction of
(a_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
from (
17
).
By induction on
n
, we see with the empty sum understood (as usual) as zero that

a_{\ell+n}\leq q^{n}a_{\ell}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}q^{n-j}b_{\ell+j-1}\quad\text{for all }\ell,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

From this and the geometric series, we infer that

(79)

a_{\ell+n}\leq q^{n}a_{\ell}+C_{1}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}q^{n-j}\Big)a_{\ell}\leq\Big(q^{n}+\frac{C_{1}}{1-q}\Big)\,a_{\ell}\eqqcolon C_{3}\,a_{\ell}\quad\text{for all }\lx@nobreakspace\ell,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Step 2.

Next, we note that the perturbed contraction of
(a_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
from
(
17
) and the
Young inequality with sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
ensure

0<\kappa\coloneqq(1+\varepsilon)\,q^{2}<1\quad\text{and}\quad a_{\ell+1}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:summability:criterion}}}{{\leq}}\kappa\,a_{\ell}^{2}+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\,b_{\ell}^{2}\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

This and the summability of

(b_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
from (
17
) guarantee

\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell+1}^{\ell+N}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{\ell+N-1}a_{\ell^{\prime}+1}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:summability:criterion}}}}{{\leq}}\kappa\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{\ell+N-1}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})C_{2}\,N^{1-\delta}\,a_{\ell}^{2}.

Rearranging the estimate, we arrive at

(80)

\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{\ell+N}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\Bigl[1+\frac{\kappa+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})C_{2}\,N^{1-\delta}}{1-\kappa}\Bigr]\,a_{\ell}^{2}\eqqcolon D_{N}\,a_{\ell}^{2}\quad\text{for all }\ell,N\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where we note that
1\leq D_{N}\simeq N^{1-\delta}
as
N\to\infty
. In the following, we prove that this already guarantees that (
80
) holds with an
N
-independent constant (instead of the constant
D_{N}
growing with
N
); see also Lemma
4.4
below.

Step 3.

We show by mathematical induction on
n
that (
80
) implies

(81)

a_{\ell+n}^{2}\leq\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-D_{j}^{-1})\biggr)\,\sum_{{\ell^{\prime}}=\ell}^{\ell+n}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}\quad\text{for all }\ell,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Note that (
81
) holds for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
n=0
(with the empty product interpreted as
1
). Hence, we may suppose that (
81
) holds for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and up to
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then,

\displaystyle a_{\ell+(n+1)}^{2}=a_{(\ell+1)+n}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq2:feischl}}}{{\leq}}\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-D_{j}^{-1})\biggr)\,\sum_{{\ell^{\prime}}=\ell+1}^{(\ell+1)+n}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}=\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-D_{j}^{-1})\biggr)\,\bigg(\sum_{{\ell^{\prime}}=\ell}^{\ell+(n+1)}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}-a_{\ell}^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq1:feischl}}}}{{\leq}}\ \biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-D_{j}^{-1})\biggr)\,\bigg(\sum_{{\ell^{\prime}}=\ell}^{\ell+(n+1)}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}-D_{n+1}^{-1}\sum_{{\ell^{\prime}}=\ell}^{\ell+(n+1)}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}\bigg)=\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}(1-D_{j}^{-1})\biggr)\,\sum_{{\ell^{\prime}}=\ell}^{\ell+(n+1)}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}.

This concludes the proof of (
81
).

Step 4.

From (
80
)–(
81
), we infer that

(82)

a_{\ell+n}^{2}\leq\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-D_{j}^{-1})\biggr)D_{n}\,a_{\ell}^{2}\quad\text{for all }\lx@nobreakspace\ell,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that

M_{n}\coloneqq\log\bigg[\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-D_{j}^{-1})\biggr)D_{n}\biggr]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\log(1-D_{j}^{-1})+\log D_{n}.

With

1-x\leq\exp(-x)
for all
0<x<1
, it follows for
x=D_{j}^{-1}
that

M_{n}\leq\log D_{n}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}D_{j}^{-1}\simeq(1-\delta)\,\log n-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^{1-\delta}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}-\infty,

since
\log n\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1/j)

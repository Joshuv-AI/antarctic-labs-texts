New Look at the Molecular Superbubble Candidate in the Galactic Center Journal: ApJ NOSTAR, Starlink ( Currie et al. 2014 )
Shiho Tsujimoto, Tomoharu Oka, Shunya Takekawa, Yuhei Iwata, Asaka Uruno, Hiroki Yokozuka, Ryosuke Nakagawara, Yuto Watanabe, Akira Kawakami, Sonomi Nishiyama, Miyuki Kaneko, Shoko Kanno, Takuma Ogawa

Abstract
The l = + 1 ​ . ∘ ​ 3 l\!=\!+1\fdg 3 region in the Galactic center is characterized by multiple shell-like structures and their extremely broad velocity widths. We revisit the molecular superbubble hypothesis for this region, based on high resolution maps of CO J =1–0, 13 CO J =1–0, H 13 CN J =1–0, H 13 CO + J =1–0, SiO J =2–1, and CS J =2–1 lines obtained from the Nobeyama radio observatory 45-m telescope, as well as CO J =3–2 maps obtained from the James Clerk Maxwell telescope. We identified eleven expanding shells with total kinetic energy and typical expansion time E kin ∼ 10 51.9 \hbox{$E_{\rm kin}$}\!\sim\!10^{51.9} erg and t exp ∼ 10 4.9 \hbox{$t_{\rm exp}$}\!\sim\!10^{4.9} yr, respectively. In addition, the l = + 1 ​ . ∘ ​ 3 l\!=\!+1\fdg 3 region exhibited high SiO J =2–1/H 13 CN J =1–0 and SiO J =2–1/H 13 CO + J =1–0 intensity ratios, indicating that the region has experienced dissociative shocks in the past. These new findings confirm the molecular superbubble hypothesis for the l = + 1 ​ . ∘ ​ 3 l\!=\!+1\fdg 3 region. The nature of the embedded star cluster, which may have supplied 20–70 supernova explosions within 10 5 yr, is discussed. This work also show the importance of compact broad-velocity-width features in searching for localized energy sources hidden behind severe interstellar extinction and stellar contamination.



I
Introduction

The Galactic center contains a large quantity of warm (
T_{\rm k}\!=\!30\mbox{--}60\;{\rm K}
) and dense [
n\left({\rm H_{2}}\right)\!\geq\!10^{4}\;{\rm cm^{-3}}
] molecular gas
(
Morris et al. 1983
;
Paglione et al. 1998
)
. Gases in the central molecular zone (CMZ;
Morris & Serabyn 1996
) exhibit highly turbulent and complex kinematics with large velocity dispersions
(
Oka et al. 1998b
)
. In addition to the ubiquity of shock-origin molecules
(
Hüttemeister et al. 1998
;
Requena-Torres et al. 2006
)
, the highly turbulent kinematics of molecular gas in the CMZ can be attributed to the release of kinetic energy by numerous supernova (SN) explosions. It has been suggested that the instantaneous and repetitive phases of star formation have dominated continuous star formation in the CMZ in the past. Despite the abundance of dense molecular gas, star formation is currently inactive in the CMZ. The current star formation rate (SFR)
\,\sim 0.04\mbox{--}0.1\,M\hbox{${}_{\odot}$}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}

(
Yusef-Zadeh et al. 2009
;
Immer et al. 2012
)
is at least an order of magnitude lower than that expected from the amount of dense gas in the CMZ
(
Kruijssen et al. 2014
)
.

In contrast, two well-known young super star clusters in the CMZ, the Arches and Quintuplet clusters, are considered to be formed by microbursts of star formation several Myr ago
(
Figer et al. 1999a
;
Figer et al. 2002
)
. Recent gamma-ray observations have revealed a pair of huge shell-like structures called “Fermi bubbles,” extending up to 50
∘
above and below the Galactic center. The “Fermi bubbles” are considered to have been formed by a pair of energetic jets from Sgr A* or a nuclear starburst in the last several tens of Myr
(
Su et al. 2010
)
.
In addition, dozens of high-velocity compact clouds (HVCCs) with large kinetic energy were detected in our recent studies on the CMZ
(
Oka et al. 1998b
;
Oka et al. 1999
;
Oka et al. 2001
;
Oka et al. 2007
;
Oka et al. 2012
;
Tanaka et al. 2014
;
Takekawa et al. 2017
;
Takekawa et al. 2019b
)
. Some of the HVCCs exhibit one or more shell-like structures that may have been accelerated by a series of SN explosions that occurred in massive star clusters 10–30 Myr ago
(
Tanaka et al. 2007
;
Tsujimoto et al. 2018
)
. The presence of these shells suggests that numerous micro-starbursts have occurred in the CMZ 10–30 Myr ago. The intense 6.7 keV ionized iron emission line widely detected in the CMZ via Galactic center diffuse X-ray (GCDX) was reported to be produced in hot plasma, whose possible energy source is
10^{2\mbox{--}3}
SN explosions at a rate of
10^{-2}\mbox{--}10^{-3}\,{\rm yr^{-1}}

(
Yamauchi et al. 1990
;
Koyama et al. 2007c
)
. This GCDX also supports the past active star formation scenario.

The
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
and
l\!=\!-1\fdg 2
regions have drawn attention as remarkably high CO
J
=3–2/
J
=1–0 intensity ratio (
R_{3\mbox{--}2/1\mbox{--}0}\geq 1.5
) clumps with extremely broad velocity widths (
Oka et al. 2007
;
Oka et al. 2012
; Figure
1
). The
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region is associated with a large “molecular flare”
(
Oka et al. 1998b
)
, which resembles the chimneys observed in starburst galaxies (e.g.,
Garcia-Burillo et al. 2001
).
Two expanding shells were reported in the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region
(
Oka et al. 2001
)
before the detection of the high
R_{3\mbox{--}2/1\mbox{--}0}
clump. These shells typically have huge kinetic energies of
\hbox{$E_{\rm kin}$}\!\sim\!10^{52}
erg, which corresponds to
10^{1\mbox{--}2}
SNe. The follow-up observations of dense and shocked gas tracers in these regions have exhibited shell/arc-dominated morphology and expanding kinematics
(
Tanaka et al. 2007
;
Tsujimoto et al. 2018
)
. Moreover, SiO clumps were detected at the high-velocity ends of the shells. Because SiO is a well-established probe of shocked molecular gas, we suggested that both of the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
and
l\!=\!-1\fdg 2
regions are proto-superbubbles containing massive (
\sim\!10^{5\mbox{--}6}\,M\hbox{${}_{\odot}$}
) star clusters. Note that the lack of a corresponding infrared observation challenges the superbubble scenario, hinting at another star formation scenario for the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region (e.g.,
Matsunaga et al. 2021
).

In this paper, we present newly obtained molecular line datasets of the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region as parts of a large-scale survey of the CMZ (§2). The new CO maps show the detailed spatial and velocity distribution of this region, revealing multiple expanding shells. In addition, the distribution and kinematics of shocked gas are discussed based on the SiO line data (§3). Based on these datasets, we revisit the molecular superbubble scenario for the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region (§4). Then we summarize this work at the last section (§5). The distance to the Galactic center is assumed as
D=8.3
kpc in this paper.

IV
Discussion

IV.1
Classification of Identified Shells

Highly complex molecular gas distribution and kinematics in the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region prevents probing the shells deeply. Thus, the identifications of shells by eye are inevitably subjective. To prioritize more “reliable” shells in our analyses, we first ranked the 11 identified shells by their appearance into three classes: “
\circledcirc
” (undoubtedly expanding shells), “
\bigcirc
” (possibly expanding shells), and “
\triangle
” (could be expanding shells). The shells in the “
\circledcirc
”-class exhibited clear ellipsoidal structures in the
l–b–V
space. The shells in the “
\bigcirc
”-class exhibited elliptical shapes in the
l–b
,
l–V
, or
b–V
plane. The sets of possibly fragmented ellipses are categorized into the “
\triangle
”-class. Note that the physical parameters of the lower-ranked shells generally have larger uncertainties.

IV.2
Physical Parameters

Here, we estimate the physical parameters of the identified shells. The mass
M
of each shell can be derived from the sum of the column density of molecular gas associated to the shell. We assume that the entire molecular gas within each
l–b–V
ellipsoid, which are listed in Table
1
, belongs to each shell. Assuming the local thermodynamic equilibrium and adopting
[^{12}{\rm CO}]/[^{13}{\rm CO}]\!=\!24

(
Langer & Penzias 1990
)
,
[^{12}{\rm CO}]/[{\rm H_{2}}]\!=\!10^{-4}

(
Frerking et al. 1982
)
, we calculated the column densities from the
12
CO
J
=1–0 line intensity.

The radius of each shell is defined as
R\!\equiv\!\sqrt{\Delta l\,\Delta b}/2
. Assuming that each shell expands at constant velocity
V_{\rm exp}
, we can calculate the expansion time by
\hbox{$t_{\rm exp}$}\!=\!R/\hbox{$V_{\rm exp}$}
. The kinetic energy was calculated by
\hbox{$E_{\rm kin}$}\!=\!M\hbox{$V_{\rm exp}$}^{2}/2
, and the kinetic power was calculated by
\hbox{$P_{\rm kin}$}=\hbox{$E_{\rm kin}$}/\hbox{$t_{\rm exp}$}
. The physical parameters
M
,
E_{\rm kin}
,
t_{\rm exp}
, and
P_{\rm kin}
, are also listed in Table
1
.

The kinetic energy lies in the range of
\hbox{$E_{\rm kin}$}\!=\!10^{49.5\mbox{--}51.8}
erg. Because an SN explosion releases baryonic energy of
(1\mbox{--}3)\!\times\!10^{50}
erg into interstellar space
(
Sashida et al. 2013
)
, the kinetic energy of each
\circledcirc
-class shell corresponds to more than one SN. The expansion time is in the range of
t_{\rm exp}\!=\!(5.6\mbox{--}16.6)\times 10^{4}
yr. Unlike the shells in the
l\!=\!-1\fdg 2
region
(
Tsujimoto et al. 2018
)
, no
t_{\rm exp}
gradient along the spatial coordinates was observed. Except for two
\triangle
-class shells, the calculated
t_{\rm exp}
coincide within a factor of 2, and
V_{\rm sys}
are confined in the range of
40

km s
-1
width. The total kinetic energy and total kinetic power of these shells amount to
10^{51.9}
erg and
10^{39.5}

Here, we examine the expanding-shell kinematics for the three
\circledcirc

Sashida et al. 2013

V_{\rm sys}
and
V_{\rm exp}
are determined by fitting the uniform expansion model:

\displaystyle\hbox{$V_{\rm LSR}$}=

\displaystyle\hbox{$V_{\rm sys}$}+\hbox{$V_{\rm exp}$}\sqrt{1-s\left(x,\,y\right)^{2}}\quad

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(s>1\right),

(2)

\Delta l/2

\circledcirc

IV.4
SiO Line Emission

SiO is a well-established shocked gas tracer (e.g.,
Martín-Pintado et al. 1992
). In the CMZ, where the molecular clouds exhibit highly turbulent kinematics, the SiO
J
=2–1 line is widely detected (e.g.,
Tsuboi et al. 2015
;
Takekawa et al. in preparation
). Nevertheless, we expect some enhancement in SiO abundance just after the passage of dissociative shock. Because the expansion times of the shells in the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region is
10^{4.7\mbox{--}5.2}
yr, its current chemical properties must have been influenced significantly by shocks.

In Figure
3
, green or cyan dominates the maps in velocities higher than
117

-1
, where the ellipses of S2 and N5 are evident. Cyan indicates that CO
J
=3–2 and SiO
J
=2–1 emissions are present, favoring the edges of the identified shells. This indicates a higher density and/or higher SiO abundance at the edges of the shells.

Here, we refer to the SiO
J
=2–1/H
13
CN
J
=1–0 (
R_{\rm SiO/H^{13}CN}
) and SiO
J
=2–1/H
13
CO
+

J
=1–0 (

) intensity ratios to examine the enhancement of SiO abundance. These ratios are considered as indicators of shock strength (e.g.,
Handa et al. 2006
). As the critical densities of these lines are similar (
n_{\rm cr}\!\sim\!10^{5\mbox{--}6}
cm
-3
), they trace roughly the same spatial regions.
Before the analyses, all datasets were smoothed with a
36\hbox{${}^{\prime\prime}$}\times 36\hbox{${}^{\prime\prime}$}\times 5\,\hbox{km s${}^{-1}$}

l\!=\!+1\fdg 3

than the CMZ.
The average values of

and

IV.5
Origin of the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
Region

The broad-velocity-width nature, association of the shells/arcs and molecular flares, definite evidence for expanding kinematics in the shells, and abundance enhancement of a shock-origin molecule toward the region all support the notion that the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region has been accelerated by multiple SN explosions. Such a region is called a “molecular superbubble” (e.g.,
Oka et al. 2001
;
Tanaka et al. 2007
;
Tsujimoto et al. 2018
). Such a superbubble must contain a young star cluster that yields SNe frequently. The absence of H
II
regions indicate a cluster age of
\geq\!10
Myr, the main-sequence lifetime of a
15\,\hbox{$M_{\odot}$}
star. The presence of type-II SNe indicates that the cluster is younger than 30 Myr. This cluster age implies that the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region may have experienced SNe over several Myr, which is consistent with the vertically elongated structure and widespread high
R_{\rm SiO/H^{13}CN}
over the region.

The total kinetic power of the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region is
P_{\rm kin}\!\simeq\!10^{39.5}
erg s
-1
. As an SN explosion releases
(1\mbox{--}3)\!\times\!10^{50}
erg energy into interstellar space
(
Sashida et al. 2013
)
, this kinetic power corresponds to an SN rate of
\sim\!10^{-3.1}\mbox{--}10^{-3.5}
yr
-1
. Assuming that the star cluster formed instantaneously, i.e., the stellar members have the same age, the cluster mass is estimated to be
\hbox{$M_{\rm cl}$}\!\sim\!10^{7.5}\,\hbox{$M_{\odot}$}
, employing the Scalo initial mass function (IMF;
Scalo 1986
) with the low-/high-mass cutoffs and mass of the heaviest stars as
0.08\,\hbox{$M_{\odot}$}/100\,\hbox{$M_{\odot}$}
and
8\,\hbox{$M_{\odot}$}
, respectively. This unbelievably high cluster mass is a challenge that is yet to be overcome in the molecular superbubble hypothesis for the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region.

The absence of a bright infrared counterpart presents another challenge to the hypothesis. A massive cluster with an initial mass of
\hbox{$M_{\rm cl}$}\!\sim\!10^{7.5}\,\hbox{$M_{\odot}$}
and age of
10\mbox{--}30
Myr should have a total luminosity of
\hbox{$L_{\rm cl}$}\sim\!10^{9.0}\,\hbox{$L_{\odot}$}

(
Williams & Perry 1994
)
. However, the far infrared (100
{\rm\mu m}
) luminosity of the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region is only
L_{\rm IR}\!\sim\!10^{6.4}\,\hbox{$L_{\odot}$}
, which is 2.6 orders of magnitude lower than the estimated total luminosity. This situation is similar to that encountered at the
l\!=\!-1\fdg 2
region
(
Tsujimoto et al. 2018
)
. This serious discrepancy between
L_{\rm cl}
and
L_{\rm IR}
could be partly explained by an abnormal IMF with a shallower slope and/or higher low-mass cutoff for the putative star cluster. The other types of explosions, e.g., Type Ia SNe and neutron star mergers
(
Rosswog et al. 2013
)
can also contribute to the kinetic power. Some extreme ideas such as the dark stellar remnant clusters should be considered in future studies.

V
Summary

We performed high resolution mapping observations of the H
13
CN
J
=1–0, H
13
CO
+

J
=1–0, SiO
J
=2–1, and CS
J
=2–1 lines toward the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region with the NRO 45-m telescope.
By combining them with the previously obtained
12
CO
J
=1–0, 3–2, and
13
CO
J
=1–0 data, we attained the following results.

1.

We obtained high-quality maps of multiple molecular lines, including high-density and shocked gas probes in the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region.

2.

The
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region exhibits higher
R_{\rm SiO/H^{13}CN}
and
R_{\rm SiO/H^{13}CO^{+}}
than those in the CMZ.
This indicates that a strong shock has passed through this region, enhancing the SiO emission.

3.

Investigating the CO
J
=3–2 and SiO
J
=2–1 maps, we identified 11 shells.
Of the 11 shells, 9 coincide with previously reported shells.

E_{\rm kin}
and
t_{\rm exp}
of these shells were
10^{49\mbox{--}52}
erg and
\sim 10^{5}
yr, respectively.

4.

We ranked the shells into three classes based on how apparent their elliptical shapes are in the
l–b–V
space, and confirmed clear expanding motion for three shells in the highest
\circledcirc
-class.
These results support the hypothesis that the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region is a superbubble.

5.

The total kinetic energy of the shells are
\hbox{$E_{\rm kin}$}\sim 10^{52}
erg, and their expansion times are typically
\hbox{$t_{\rm exp}$}\sim 10^{5}
yr. Then, the SN rate of this region is estimated to be
\sim 10^{-3.1}\mbox{--}10^{-3.5}\,{\rm yr^{-1}}
.

6.

For the molecular superbubble hypothesis, there needs to be young (10–30 Myr) massive (
\hbox{$M_{\rm cl}$}\sim 10^{7.5}\,\hbox{$M_{\odot}$}
) cluster. The kinematics and morphologies of the molecular gas and energetics of this region generally support this scenario.

These new results reinforced the molecular superbubble hypothesis for the
l\!=\!+1\fdg 3
region, while the sharp discrepancy between the far infrared and theoretical luminosities of the embedded cluster challenges the scenario. This work also demonstrated the importance of broad-velocity-width compact molecular features in searching for localized energy sources hidden behind severe interstellar extinction and stellar contamination.

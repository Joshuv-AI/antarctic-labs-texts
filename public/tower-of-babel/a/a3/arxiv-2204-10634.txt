Existence and asymptotic behavior of normalized ground states for Sobolev critical Schrödinger systems
Thomas Bartsch, Houwang Li & Wenming Zou

Abstract




Abstract

The paper is concerned with the existence and asymptotic properties of normalized ground states of the following nonlinear Schrödinger system with critical exponent:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u+{\nu\alpha}|u|^{\alpha-2}|v|^{\beta}u,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=|v|^{2^{*}-2}v+{\nu\beta}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta-2}v,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}=a^{2},\;\;\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{2}=b^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

where
N=3,4
,
\alpha,\beta>1
,
2<\alpha+\beta<2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
.
We prove that a normalized ground state does not exist for
\nu<0
.
When
\nu>0
and
\alpha+\beta\leq 2+\frac{4}{N}
, we show that the system has a normalized ground state solution for
0<\nu<\nu_{0}
,
the constant
\nu_{0}
will be explicitly given.
In the case
\alpha+\beta>2+\frac{4}{N}
we prove the existence of a threshold
\nu_{1}\geq 0
such that a normalized ground state solution exists for
\nu>\nu_{1}
, and does not exist for
\nu<\nu_{1}
. We also give conditions for
\nu_{1}=0
. Finally we obtain the asymptotic behavior of the minimizers as
\nu\to 0^{+}
or
\nu\to+\infty
.

Key words:
Critical Schrödinger systems; Normalized solution; Ground states; Asymptotic behaviors.

Introduction and statement of results

The Schrödinger system

\begin{cases}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{1}=\Delta\Phi_{1}+\mu_{1}|\Phi_{1}|^{p-2}\Phi_{1}+\nu\alpha|\Phi_{1}|^{\alpha-2}|\Phi_{2}|^{\beta}\Phi_{1},\\
-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{2}=\Delta\Phi_{2}+\mu_{2}|\Phi_{2}|^{q-2}\Phi_{1}+\nu\beta|\Phi_{1}|^{\alpha}|\Phi_{2}|^{\beta-2}\Phi_{2},\\
\Phi_{j}=\Phi_{j}(x,t)\in{\mathbb{C}},\ (x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\times{\mathbb{R}},\ j=1,2,\end{cases}

(1.1)

has received a lot of attention in recent years. It appears, for instance, in nonlinear optics, or in mean field models for binary mixtures of Bose-Einstein condensates, or binary gases of fermion atoms in degenerate quantum states (Bose-Fermi mixtures, Fermi-Fermi mixtures). The constants
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
and
\nu
describe the intraspecies and interspecies scattering lengths. The sign of
\nu
determines whether the interactions are repulsive or attractive, i.e., the interaction is attractive if
\nu>0
, and it is repulsive if
\nu<0
. The most prominent case is that of the coupled Gross-Pitaevskii equations where
p=q=4
and
\alpha=\beta=2
. Models for certain ultracold quantum gases however use different exponents; see
[
2
,
3
,
19
,

,

The ansatz
\Phi_{1}(x,t)=e^{i\lambda_{1}t}u(x)
and
\Phi_{2}(x,t)=e^{i\lambda_{2}t}v(x)
for solitary wave solutions of system (
1.1
) leads to the following system of elliptic equations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=\mu_{1}|u|^{p-2}u+\nu\alpha|u|^{\alpha-2}|v|^{\beta}u,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=\mu_{2}|v|^{q-2}v+\nu\beta|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta-2}v,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&u(x),v(x)\to 0~~\text{as }|x|\to\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

Fixing the parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
the existence and multiplicity of solutions to (
1.2
) has been investigated in the last two decades by many authors. We refer the reader to

An important feature of system (
1.1
) is that the

-norms

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|\Phi_{1}(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|\Phi_{2}(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

of the wave functions are conserved. These have important physical significance: in Bose-Einstein condensates, for example,
|\Phi_{1}(t,\cdot)|_{2}
and
|\Phi_{2}(t,\cdot)|_{2}
represent the number of particles of each component; in the nonlinear optics framework,
|\Phi_{1}(t,\cdot)|_{2}
and
|\Phi_{2}(t,\cdot)|_{2}
represent the power supply. Therefore it is natural to consider (

) with the constraints

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}=a^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{2}=b^{2}.

Then

cannot be prescribed but appear as Lagrange multipliers in a variational approach.
The existence and properties of these
normalized solutions
recently has attracted the attention of many reasearchers
but compared with the results about (

This is even true for scalar nonlinear Schrödinger equations.

In the present paper we are interested in the existence of ground state normalized solutions of (
1.2
) when
p=q=2^{*}
is the critical Sobolev exponent,
2<\alpha+\beta<2^{*}
, and
N=3
or

|u|^{2^{*}-2}u
and
|v|^{2^{*}-2}v
, in dimension 4 with the classical cubic one. We would like to mention that it would be possible to add lower order terms to these self-interactions. In particular, in dimension 3 we could deal with a cubic-quintic nonlinearity which is used in some applications. For simplicity we just deal with homogeneous nonlinearities, and we assume
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=1
; so we consider the problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u+{\nu\alpha}|u|^{\alpha-2}|v|^{\beta}u,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=|v|^{2^{*}-2}v+{\nu\beta}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta-2}v,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}=a^{2},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{2}=b^{2}\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

under the assumptions

N\in\{3,4\},\ a,b>0,\text{ and }\alpha>1,\ \beta>1,\ \alpha+\beta<2^{*}.

(1.4)

It is worth to point out that all the papers about normalized solutions of (
1.2
) mentioned above, except
[
31
]
, deal with the Sobolev subcritical case
2<p,q<2^{*}
. In
[
31
]
only the case

has been considered.

Solutions of (
1.3
) correspond to critical points of the energy functional
I_{\nu}:H=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

I_{\nu}(u,v):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\left(|u|^{2^{*}}+|v|^{2^{*}}\right)-{\nu}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}

(1.5)

and constrained to the
L^{2}
-torus

{\cal T}(a,b):=\left\{(u,v)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}):\|u\|_{2}=a,~\|v\|_{2}=b\right\}.

The parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
will appear as Lagrange multipliers. It is easy to see that
I_{\nu}
is of class
C^{1}
, and that it is unbounded from below on
{\cal T}(a,b)
. Due to the critical exponents
I_{\nu}
will not satisfy the Palais-Smale condition in general. Recall that solutions of (
1.3
) satisfy the Pohozaev identity

P_{\nu}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}\right)-\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\left(|u|^{2^{*}}+|v|^{2^{*}}\right)-\nu\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}=0.

where

\gamma_{p}:=\frac{N(p-2)}{2}.

(1.6)

Setting

{\cal P}_{\nu}(a,b):=\left\{(u,v)\in{\cal T}(a,b):P_{\nu}(u,v)=0\right\},

(1.7)

we are interested in the problem whether

m_{\nu}(a,b):=\inf_{(u,v)\in{\cal P}_{\nu}(a,b)}I_{\nu}(u,v).

(1.8)

is achieved. A
normalized ground state
is a solution
(u,v)\in{\cal P}_{\nu}(a,b)
of (
1.3
) that achieves
m_{\nu}(a,b)
. We first see that a ground state can only exist if the interaction is attractive.

Theorem 1.1
.

If (
1.4
) holds and
\nu\leq 0
then
m_{\nu}(a,b)
is not achieved.

Next we consider the case
\nu>0
of attractive interaction.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose (
1.4
) holds.

a)

If
\alpha+\beta<2+\frac{4}{N}
then there exists a normalized ground state, provided
0<\nu<\nu_{0}
where
\nu_{0}
is explicitly defined in (
2.8
) below. The normalized ground state is a local minimizer of
I_{\nu}
on
{\cal T}(a,b)
.

b)

If

and
\alpha+\beta=2+\frac{4}{N}=3
then there exists a normalized ground state, provided
0<\nu<\nu_{0}
where
\nu_{0}
is explicitly defined in (
2.26
) below. The normalized ground state is of mountain pass type as critical point of
I_{\nu}
on
{\cal T}(a,b)
.

c)

If
\alpha+\beta>2+\frac{4}{N}
, then there exists
\nu_{1}\geq 0
such that there exists a normalized ground state if, and only if,
\nu>\nu_{1}
. Moreover, we have

\begin{cases}\nu_{1}=0&\quad\text{if $N=4$, or $N=3$ and $|\alpha-\beta|>2$,}\\
\nu_{1}>0&\quad\text{if $N=3$, $\alpha\geq 2$, $\beta\geq 2$.}\end{cases}\

(1.9)

The normalized ground state is of mountain pass type as critical point of
I_{\nu}
on
{\cal T}(a,b)
.

d)

Both components of the normalized ground states in a), b) are positive, radially symmetric and radially decreasing. The corresponding Lagrange multipliers are positive:
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
.

Remark 1.3
.

a) In the setting of Theorem
1.2
, it is an interesting open problem whether there exists a second solution of mountain pass type. The difficulty is to control the mountain pass value at a level where the Palais-Smale condition holds (in the space of radial
H^{1}
functions).

b) Observe that in case
N=3
the assumption
\alpha+\beta<2^{*}
from (
1.4
) and
\alpha,\beta\geq 2
imply

, hence the two cases in (
1.9
) exclude each other. However, there are still cases in Theorem
1.2
c),
N=3
, where we do not know whether
\nu_{1}=0
or
\nu_{1}>0
. We conjecture that
\nu_{1}=0
if
\min\{\alpha,\beta\}<2
.

c) When
N=3
and
\alpha+\beta=2+\frac{4}{N}
, we can not obtain a similar result as Theorem
1.2
b).
Indeed, in such case, we could obtain two numbers
\nu_{0}>0,\nu_{1}\geq 0
such that there exists a normalized ground state provided
both
\nu<\nu_{0}
and
\nu>\nu_{1}
. However, we are unable to compare the values of
\nu_{0}
and
\nu_{1}
, i.e., there may hold

, while when

and
\alpha+\beta=2+\frac{4}{N}

\nu_{0}>\nu_{1}
.

Existence in the attractive case
\nu>0

In this section we fix
\nu>0

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that

(2.1)

Let

(2.2)

I_{\nu}(u_{n},v_{n})\to c,\quad P_{\nu}(u_{n},v_{n})\to 0,

(2.3)

u_{n}^{-},v_{n}^{-}\to 0,~\text{a.e. in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},

(2.4)

with

c\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad c<\frac{1}{N}{\cal S}^{N/2}+\min\left\{0,m_{\nu}(a,b)\right\}.

(2.5)

Then there exists a
(u,v)\in H_{rad}
,
u,v>0
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
such that

(u_{n},v_{n})\to(u,v)
in

Step 1.

\displaystyle c+1

\displaystyle\geq I_{\nu}(u_{n},v_{n})-\frac{1}{2}P_{\nu}(u_{n},v_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}(\|u_{n}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+\|v_{n}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}})+\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}-2}{2}\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{\alpha}|v_{n}|^{\beta}

\displaystyle\geq C(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+o_{n}(1))

for some

. This implies that

l(a,b)\leq K(\bar{u},\bar{v})=\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}-2}{2\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}(\|\nabla\bar{u}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\bar{v}\|_{2}^{2})\leq\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}-2}{2\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}(\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{2}^{2})=K(u_{0},v_{0})=l(a,b).

We finally deduce
(\bar{u},\bar{v})\in{\cal G}(a,b)
, i.e.,

dist_{H}\left((-t_{\nu})\star(u_{\nu},v_{\nu}),{\cal G}(a,b)\right)\to 0,\quad\text{as}~\nu\to 0^{+}.

∎

Now we turn to the case
\alpha+\beta\geq 2+\frac{4}{N}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
\alpha+\beta\geq 2+\frac{4}{N}
then

dist_{{\cal D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}\big((u_{\nu},v_{\nu}),{\cal U}\times\{0\}\big)\to 0\quad\text{as}~\nu\to 0^{+},

or

dist_{{\cal D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}\big((u_{\nu},v_{\nu}),\{0\}\times{\cal U}\big)\to 0\quad\text{as}~\nu\to 0^{+};

here
{\cal U}=\left\{U_{\varepsilon}:\varepsilon>0\right\}
.

Proof.

First observe that
\{(u_{\nu},v_{\nu})\}
is bounded in
H
because
(u_{\nu},v_{\nu})\in{\cal P}_{\nu}(a,b)
satisfies
I_{\nu}(u_{\nu},v_{\nu})=m_{\nu}(a,b)
.
Let
\|\nabla u_{\nu}\|_{2}\to l_{1}
and
\|\nabla v_{\nu}\|_{2}\to l_{2}
with
l_{1}\geq 0
,
l_{2}\geq 0
.
If
l_{1}=l_{2}=0
then

0=\lim_{\nu\to 0}I_{\nu}(u_{\nu},v_{\nu})=\lim_{\nu\to 0}m_{\nu}(a,b)>0,

using
which is a contradiction. The Sobolev inequality and
P_{\nu}(u_{\nu},v_{\nu})\to 0
imply

l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2}\leq{\cal S}^{-2^{*}/2}\big(l_{1}^{2^{*}}+l_{2}^{2^{*}}\big),

hence
l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2}\geq{\cal S}^{N/2}
and equality holds if and only if
l_{1}=0
or
l_{2}=0
.
Consequently we have

\frac{1}{N}{\cal S}^{N/2}\geq\lim_{\nu\to 0}I_{\nu}(u_{\nu},v_{\nu})=\frac{1}{N}(l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2})

and therefore either
l_{1}=0
,
l_{2}={\cal S}^{N/4}
, or
l_{1}={\cal S}^{N/4}
,
l_{2}=0
.
If
l_{1}=0
,
l_{2}={\cal S}^{N/4}
then

\|\nabla u_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}\to 0,\quad\|\nabla v_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}\to{\cal S}^{N/2},\quad\|v_{\nu}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}\to{\cal S}^{N/2}\quad\text{as}~\nu\to 0,

hence
v_{\nu}
is a minimizing sequence of (
1.10
). Now
[
45
, Theorem 1.41]
implies that there exists
\theta_{\nu}>0
such that for some
\varepsilon_{*}>0

\Big(u_{\nu},\theta_{\nu}^{\frac{N-2}{2}}v_{\nu}(\theta_{\nu}\cdot)\Big)\to(0,U_{\varepsilon_{*}})\quad\text{strongly in}~{\cal D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})~\text{as}~\nu\to 0^{+},

up to a subsequence. The case
l_{1}={\cal S}^{N/4}
,
l_{2}=0
can be treated analogously.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

This follows immediately from Lemma
5.1
, Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Since the proof is similar to that of Lemma
5.2
, we only sketch it.
Given
(u,v)\in{\cal T}(a,b)
we use
t_{\nu}(u,v)\star(u,v)
as a test function for
m_{\nu}(a,b)

where
t_{\nu}(u,v)
is given by Lemma
2.4
.
Using similar arguments as in the proof of Lemma
5.1
and direct calculations we get

m_{\nu}(a,b)\lesssim\nu^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}
as
\nu\to+\infty
.
It follows that

\|\nabla u_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\|u_{\nu}^{\alpha}v_{\nu}^{\beta}\|_{1}\to 0\quad\text{as}~\nu\to+\infty.

Since

\|\nabla u_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u_{\nu}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+\|v_{\nu}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}\nu\|u_{\nu}^{\alpha}v_{\nu}^{\beta}\|_{1},

(5.23)

using the Sobolev inequality and (
A.5
), we obtain

\|\nabla u_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\big(1+o_{\nu}(1)\big)\left(\nu\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}D_{0}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}

and

\|u_{\nu}^{\alpha}v_{\nu}^{\beta}\|_{1}\geq\big(1+o_{\nu}(1)\big)\left(\nu\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}D_{0}^{\frac{2}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}},

where
D_{0}
is defined by (
5.5
).
For any
(u_{0},v_{0})\in{\cal G}(a,b)
, let
t_{\nu}:=t_{\nu}(a,b)
be given by Lemma
2.4
.
Then

e^{2t_{\nu}}\big(\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\big)=e^{2^{*}t_{\nu}}\big(\|u_{0}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+\|v_{0}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}\big)+e^{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}t_{\nu}}\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}\nu\||u_{0}|^{\alpha}|v_{0}|^{\beta}\|_{1}.

It follows from (
5.5
) that

e^{2t_{\nu}}\big(\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\big)\leq\left(\nu\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}D_{0}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}},

and

e^{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}t_{\nu}}\|u_{0}^{\alpha}v_{0}^{\beta}\|_{1}\leq\left(\nu\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}D_{0}^{\frac{2}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}.

Thus we get

\displaystyle I_{\nu}\big(t_{\nu}\star(u_{0},v_{0})\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}-2}{2\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}e^{2t_{\nu}}\big(\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\big)+\frac{2^{*}-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}{2^{*}\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}e^{2^{*}t_{\nu}}\big(\|u_{0}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+\|v_{0}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}\big)

(5.24)

\displaystyle\leq\big(1+o_{\nu}(1)\big)\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}-2}{2\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}\left(\nu\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}D_{0}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}.

Note that
I_{\nu}(t_{\nu}\star(u_{0},v_{0})\geq m_{\nu}(a,b)
and

m_{\nu}(a,b)=I_{\nu}(u_{\nu},v_{\nu})=\big(1+o_{\nu}(1)\big)\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}-2}{2\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}\left(\nu\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}D_{0}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}},

as
\nu\to+\infty
, we must have

\|\nabla u_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}=\big(1+o_{\nu}(1)\big)\left(\nu\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}D_{0}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}.

(5.25)

Similarly, we also have

\|u_{\nu}^{\alpha}v_{\nu}^{\beta}\|_{1}=(1+o_{\nu}(1))\left(\nu\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}D_{0}^{\frac{2}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}.

Moreover, using
K(u_{0},v_{0})=l(a,b)
and
L(u_{0},v_{0})=0
, we have

\|u_{0}^{\alpha}v_{0}^{\beta}\|_{1}=\left(\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}D_{0}^{\frac{2}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}},

which implies

\|u_{\nu}^{\alpha}v_{\nu}^{\beta}\|_{1}=(1+o_{\nu}(1))\nu^{\frac{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}\|u_{0}^{\alpha}v_{0}^{\beta}\|_{1}.

(5.26)

Let
(\bar{u}_{\nu},\bar{v}_{\nu})=s_{\nu}\star(u_{\nu},v_{\nu})
with
e^{s_{\nu}}:=\nu^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}-2}}
.
Then

\|\nabla\bar{u}_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}=a^{2},\quad\|\nabla\bar{v}_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}=b^{2},

and from (
5.25
),

\|\nabla\bar{u}_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\bar{v}_{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}=\big(1+o_{\nu}(1)\big)\left(\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}D_{0}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}}.

Therefore
\{(\bar{u}_{\nu},\bar{v}_{\nu})\}
is bounded in
H
. Since
(\bar{u}_{\nu},\bar{v}_{\nu})
are radial, we have up to a subsequence:

(\bar{u}_{\nu},\bar{v}_{\nu})\rightharpoonup(\bar{u},\bar{v})\quad\text{in}~H_{rad},

(\bar{u}_{\nu},\bar{v}_{\nu})\rightarrow(\bar{u},\bar{v})\quad\text{in}~L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\times L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}),~\text{for}~2<q<2^{*},

(\bar{u}_{\nu},\bar{v}_{\nu})\rightarrow(\bar{u},\bar{v})\quad\text{a.e. in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.

Moreover,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta\bar{u}_{\nu}+\lambda_{1,\nu}e^{2s_{\nu}}\bar{u}_{\nu}=e^{-(2^{*}-2)s_{\nu}}\bar{u}_{\nu}^{2^{*}-1}+\alpha\bar{u}_{\nu}^{\alpha-1}\bar{v}_{\nu}^{\beta},\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&-\Delta\bar{v}_{\nu}+\lambda_{2,\nu}e^{2s_{\nu}}\bar{v}_{\nu}=e^{-(2^{*}-2)s_{\nu}}\bar{v}_{\nu}^{2^{*}-1}+\beta\bar{v}_{\nu}^{\alpha}\bar{v}_{\nu}^{\beta-2},\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{u}_{\nu}^{2}=a^{2},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{v}_{\nu}^{2}=b^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.27)

Since

\lambda_{1,\nu}a^{2}+\lambda_{2,\nu}b^{2}=(1-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta})\nu\|u_{\nu}^{\alpha}v_{\nu}^{\beta}\|_{1}\sim\nu^{\frac{2}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}-2}},

by the Pohozaev identity, we see that
\lambda_{1,\nu}e^{2s_{\nu}}
and
\lambda_{2,\nu}e^{2s_{\nu}}
are bounded.
Then as in Lemma
5.2
, we obtain that

dist_{H}\left(s_{\nu}\star(u_{\nu},v_{\nu}),{\cal G}(a,b)\right)\to 0,

as
\nu\to+\infty
.
∎

Appendix A
Vector-valued Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

Recall the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality,

\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq{\cal C}(N,p)\|u\|_{2}^{p-\gamma_{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\gamma_{p}}\quad\text{for all $u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})$,}

(A.1)

where the sharp constant
{\cal C}(N,p)
satisfies

{\cal C}(N,p)^{-1}=\inf_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|_{2}^{p-\gamma_{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\gamma_{p}}}{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}=\gamma_{p}^{\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}(1-\gamma_{p})^{1-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}\|Z\|_{2}^{p-2}.

(A.2)

Here
Z
is the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta w+w=|w|^{p-2}w&&\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&w>0\ \text{and}\ w(x)\to 0&&\quad\text{as}~|x|\to\infty,\\
&w(0)=\max_{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}w(x).\end{aligned}\right.

(A.3)

For a detailed proof we refer to
[
44
]
. Moreover, the function
Z^{\sigma,\mu}(x):=\sigma Z(\mu x)
satisfies

-\Delta Z^{\sigma,\mu}+\mu^{2}Z^{\sigma,\mu}=\sigma^{2-p}\mu^{2}|Z^{\sigma,\mu}|^{p-2}Z^{\sigma,\mu},

and (
A.2
) is achieved by
u
if and only if

u\in\left\{Z^{\sigma,\mu}(\cdot+y):\sigma>0,\mu>0,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\right\}.

When considering systems we need a vector-valued version of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.
For
\alpha>1
,
\beta>1
,
\alpha+\beta<2^{*}
we set

Q(u,v):=\frac{\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{(\alpha+\beta-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta})/2}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}/2}}{\||u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}\|_{1}}.

and

{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}:=\inf_{u,v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\setminus\{0\}}Q(u,v).

(A.4)

It follows easily from (
A.1
) that
0<{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}<+\infty
. From definition (
A.4
) we obtain the vector-valued Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\big\||u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}\big\|_{1}\leq{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{(\alpha+\beta-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta})/2}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}/2},

(A.5)

which holds for
u,v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
. The vector-valued Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality has been studied by many researchers,
see
[
33
,
36
,
18
]
and the reference therein.

Proposition A.1
.

The sharp constant
{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)
is determined by

{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)=\left[\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}+\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}}\right]^{-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}{\cal C}(N,\alpha+\beta).

Moreover,
{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}
is achieved by
(u,v)
if and only if

(u,v)\in\left\{\big(Z_{1}^{\sigma,\mu}(\cdot+y),Z_{2}^{\sigma,\mu}(\cdot+y)\big):\sigma>0,\mu>0,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\right\},

(A.6)

where

(Z_{1},Z_{2})=\left(\alpha^{-\frac{2-\beta}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}\beta^{-\frac{\beta}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}},\alpha^{-\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}\beta^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}\right)Z(x)

is the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+u=\alpha u^{\alpha-1}v^{\beta}&&\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&-\Delta v+v=\beta u^{\alpha}v^{\beta-1}&&\quad\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
&u>0,v>0,\quad u,v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}).\end{aligned}\right.

(A.7)

Proof.

Let
(u_{n},v_{n})
be a minimizing sequence of
{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}
, i.e.,
Q(u_{n},v_{n})\to{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}
as
n\to\infty
.
It is easy to check that
Q(u^{\sigma,\mu},v^{\sigma,\mu})=Q(u,v)
for any
\sigma,\mu>0
.
Then by scaling and rearrangement, we may assume that

u_{n},v_{n}\in H_{rad}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
,
u_{n},v_{n}\geq 0
and

\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=1,\quad\text{for all}~n\geq 1.

Passing to a subsequence we get
(u_{n},v_{n})\rightharpoonup(u,v)
in
H_{rad}
. Since
2<\alpha+\beta<2^{*}
, we have

\|u_{n}^{\alpha}v_{n}^{\beta}\|_{1}\to\|u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\|_{1},

which means that
\|u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\|_{1}={\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)
and
u\neq 0
,
v\neq 0
.
Noting that

\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\big)=1

and

\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\big)=1,

we obtain

{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}\leq Q(u,v)\leq\|u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\|_{1}^{-1}={\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}.

It follows that
(u_{n},v_{n})\rightarrow(u,v)
in
H
and
Q(u,v)={\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}
.
Moreover, by
Q^{\prime}(u,v)=0
we conclude that

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\frac{\alpha+\beta-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}u=\frac{\alpha{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}u^{\alpha-1}v^{\beta}\quad&\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
-\Delta v+\frac{\alpha+\beta-\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}v=\frac{\beta{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}}u^{\alpha}v^{\beta-1}\quad&\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.\end{cases}

(A.8)

Hence by maximum principle
u>0
,
v>0
.
By scaling, there exists
\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}>0
such that

\begin{cases}-\Delta u^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}}+u^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}}=\alpha(u^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}})^{\alpha-1}(v^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}})^{\beta},\quad&\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
-\Delta v^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}}+v^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}}=\beta(u^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}})^{\alpha}(v^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}})^{\beta-1},\quad&\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.\end{cases}

(A.9)

Setting

u^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}}=\alpha^{-\frac{2-\beta}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}\beta^{-\frac{\beta}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}U(x),

and

v^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}}=\alpha^{-\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}\beta^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}V(x),

we have

\begin{cases}-\Delta U+U=U^{\alpha-1}V^{\beta}\quad&\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\\
-\Delta V+V=U^{\alpha}V^{\beta-1}\quad&\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.\end{cases}

Following the proof of
[
33
, Theorem 1]
, we can prove
U=V=Z(x)
.
Thus we have

u^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}}=\alpha^{-\frac{2-\beta}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}\beta^{-\frac{\beta}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}Z(x),

v^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}}=\alpha^{-\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}\beta^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{2(\alpha+\beta-2)}}Z(x),

and

{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}=Q(u,v)=Q(u^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}},v^{\sigma_{0},\mu_{0}})=\left[\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}+\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}}\right]^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}{\cal C}(N,\alpha+\beta)^{-1}.

Finally, for any minimizer
(u,v)
of
{\cal C}(N,\alpha,\beta)^{-1}
, repeating the above process we can prove (
A.6
).
∎

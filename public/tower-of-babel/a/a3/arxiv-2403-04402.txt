Analytic torsion for fibred boundary metrics and conic degeneration
Jørgen Olsen Lye, Boris Vertman

Abstract
. We study the renormalized analytic torsion of complete manifolds with fibred boundary metrics, also referred to as ϕ \phi -metrics. We establish invariance of the torsion under suitable deformations of the metric, and establish a gluing formula. As an application, we relate the analytic torsions for complete ϕ \phi - and incomplete wedge-metrics. As a simple consequence we recover a result by Sher and Guillarmou about analytic torsion under conic degeneration.

1.
Introduction: basics on analytic torsion

1.1.
Ray-Singer analytic torsion

Analytic torsion is an important topological invariant, which is defined using spectral data of a compact Riemannian manifold.
We start by recalling its definition. Let
(M,g)
be a closed oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
m
, equipped with a flat Hermitian
vector bundle
(E,\nabla,h)
. Consider the corresponding Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{k}

acting on
E
–valued differential forms
\Omega^{k}(M,E)
of degree

k
. Its unique self-adjoint extension is a discrete operator with spectrum
\sigma(\Delta_{k})

accumulating at
\infty
according to Weyl’s law. As a consequence, we can define its zeta-function as the following convergent sum

(1.1)

\zeta(s,\Delta_{k}):=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{k})\setminus\{0\}}m(\lambda)\,\lambda^{-s},\ \mathrm{Re}(s)>\frac{m}{2},

where
m(\lambda)
is the geometric multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{k})
.
The zeta-function is linked to the
heat trace

\mathrm{Tr}\left(e^{-t\Delta_{k}}\right)
via a Mellin transform

(1.2)

\zeta(s,\Delta_{k})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left(e^{-t\Delta_{k}}\right)-\dim\ker\Delta_{k}\right)\,dt.

The short time asymptotic expansion of the
heat trace yields a meromorphic extension of
\zeta(s,\Delta_{k})
, which is a priori a holomorphic function
for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>\frac{m}{2}
,
to the whole complex plane
\mathbb{C}
with at most simple poles and
s=0
being a regular point.
This allows us to define the
scalar analytic torsion
of the flat bundle
E
by

(1.3)

T(M,E;g):=\exp\left(\,\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\dim M}(-1)^{k}\cdot k\cdot\zeta^{\prime}(0,\Delta_{k})\right).

1.2.
Analytic torsion as a norm

Below, it will become convenient to reinterpret the analytic torsion as a norm on the determinant line of the
cohomology
H^{*}(M,E)
with values in the flat vector bundle
E
. This is defined as

\det H^{*}(M,E):=\bigotimes_{k=0}^{m}\left(\bigwedge^{b_{k}}H^{k}(M,E)\right)^{(-1)^{k}},

where
b_{k}=\dim H^{k}(M,E)
. The determinant line bundle has an induced
L^{2}
structure
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

from the inclusion
H^{k}(M,E)\cong\ker\Delta_{k}\subset L^{2}\Omega^{k}(M,E)
, where the
L^{2}
-inner product
is induced by the Riemannian metric
g
and the Hermitian metric
h
.
The
analytic torsion norm
, also called the Ray-Singer metric or the Quillen metric, is then defined as

(1.4)

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(M,E,g)}^{RS}:=T(M,E;g)\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

By an argument of Ray and Singer
[
38
]
, this is actually independent of
g
.
In case of non-compact or singular manifolds,
H^{*}(M,E)
is replaced by the
reduced
L^{2}
-cohomology
H^{*}_{(2)}(M,E)
, which is still isomorphic to the
space of harmonic forms. Moreover, the Quillen metric, if well-defined,
may no longer be independent of
g
.

1.3.
Analytic torsion norm on manifolds with (regular) boundary

Similar construction applies to manifolds
(M,g)
with regular boundary
\partial M
.
Imposing relative and absolute boundary conditions as in
[
5
]
, one defines
the respective analytic torsion norms

(1.5)

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(M,E,\partial M,g)}^{RS},\quad\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(M,E,g)}^{RS}.

1.4.
Cheeger-Müller theorem

The analytic torsion
T(M,E;g)
was introduced by Ray and Singer
[
38
]

as an analytic analogue of the Reidemeister
torsion
\tau(M,E;\omega)
. The latter is defined in terms of a simplicial or cellular decomposition of
M
,
where
\omega
is a choice of a non-zero element in
\det H^{*}(M,E)
.
This invariant was introduced by Reidemeister
[
40
]

and Franz
[
12
]
and is a homeomorphism-invariant, which distinguishes spaces which are homotopy-equivalent but not homeomorphic.
The central result of this field is due to Cheeger
[
7
]
and Müller
[
34
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
7
]
,
[
34
]
)
.

Consider a smooth closed Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and a flat Hermitian vector bundle
(E,\nabla,h)
.
Then, when
\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1
, the analytic torsion
T(M,E;g)
equals the combinatorial
Reidemeister torsion
\tau(M,E;\omega)
.

For smooth, compact manifolds, several other proofs of the Cheeger-Müller theorem have been
obtained. In particular, there is a proof using microlocal surgery methods by Hassel

[
19
]
as well as one using Witten deformation by Bismut-Zhang

[
4
]
. Müller
[
35
]
generalized the
result to unimodular representations of the fundamental
group, while
[
4
]
are even able to dispense with
the unimodularity assumption. The theorem was extended to manifolds with boundary by Lück

[
27
]
, Vishik
[
47
]
and Brüning-Ma

[
6
]
. A highly readable recent survey of analytic torsion for compact manifolds is
[
24
]
.

1.5.
Cheeger-Müller type theorems in singular settings

Extending Theorem
1.1
to non-compact manifolds and to spaces with singularities
is still partially open, and constitutes a central part of Cheeger’s
[
8
]

celebrated program of “extending the theory of the Laplace operator to Riemannian spaces
with singularities”. The first question is, of course, how to define the two torsions.

For the Reidemeister torsion, Dar
[
10
]
has introduced intersection R-torsion using
the intersection homology groups by Goresky and MacPherson

[
14
]
. On the analytic side one first has to address if

\zeta(s,\Delta_{k})
is well-defined, which is non-trivial since on singular or non-compact
spaces, the heat operator may not be trace class and heat trace asymptotics need not hold.

These issues were addressed on spaces with fibered cusps by Albin, Rochon and Sher
in
[
1
,
2
]
as well as the second author
[
46
]
.
For spaces with wedge singularities, analytic torsion has been studied by Mazzeo and
the second named author
[
29
]
and a Cheeger-Müller type result is due to Albin, Rochon
and Sher
[
3
]
over an odd dimensional edge
B
, see also
[
18
]
.
In the case of asymptotically conical spaces, renormalized analytic torsion has been
studied by Guillarmou and Sher
[
17
]
.

We would also like to mention the work of Ludwig on analytic torsion on manifold with conical singularities,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
.
She uses Witten deformation rather than microlocal analysis and builds on Bismut and Zhang,
[
4
]
.

1.6.
Gluing formula and reduction to the model case

Lesch
[
23
]
, building on the work of Vishik
[
47
]
, has proven a gluing
formula for the analytic torsion in case of discrete spectrum. The intersection
R-torsion also satisfies a gluing property, see
[
23
]
. This allows one to
establish a Cheeger-Müller type theorem on a singular space by comparing
the analytic and combinatorial torsions in a singular neighborhood only.

2.
Statement of the main results

2.1.
Manifolds with fibred boundary and
\phi
-metrics

We shall always assume that
M
is connected.
To deal with non-compact manifolds
M
, we consider a compactification
\overline{M}

and view
M
as its open interior. Assume that the boundary
\partial\overline{M}=\partial M

is the total space of a fibration

\phi\colon\partial M\to B

for some closed manifold
B
and typical fibre being a closed manifold
F
.
A
\phi
-metric
g_{\phi}
on
M
is by definition of the following form in a collar neighbourhood
\mathscr{U}=(0,1)\times\partial M

of the boundary
\partial M

(2.1)

g_{\phi}=\frac{dx^{2}}{x^{4}}+\frac{\phi^{*}g_{B}}{x^{2}}+g_{F}+\mathfrak{h}=:g_{0}+\mathfrak{h},

where
x\in(0,1)
is the radial function on the collar
\mathscr{U}
,
g_{B}
is a
Riemannian metric on
B
and
g_{F}
is a symmetric bilinear form on

\partial M
, which restricts to a Riemannian metric on the fibres
F
.
The higher order term satisfies
|\mathfrak{h}|_{g_{0}}=\mathcal{O}(x)
as
x\to 0
. The triple
(\overline{M},\phi,g_{\phi})
will be called a
\phi
-manifold
. We will often suppress part of the structure and refer to
(M,g_{\phi})
as a
\phi
-manifold.
We also consider a flat Hermitian vector bundle
E
over
\overline{M}
.

Consider local coordinates
(x,y,z)\in\mathscr{U}
, where
y=(y_{i})
is the lift of local coordinates
on the base
B
, and
z=(z_{j})
restrict to local coordinates on the fibres
F
.
We introduce the
\phi
-tangent bundle over
\overline{M}
by specifying its smooth sections
(to be smooth in the interior and) locally near
\partial M
as

C^{\infty}(\overline{M},{}^{\phi}TM)=C^{\infty}(\overline{M})\text{-span}\left\langle x^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x},x\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\right\rangle.

Note that the metric
g_{\phi}
extends to a smooth positive definite quadratic form on

{}^{\phi}TM
over all of
\overline{M}
.
The sections of the dual bundle
{}^{\phi}T^{*}M
, the so-called
\phi
-cotangent bundle,
are smooth in the interior and locally near
\partial M
are given by

C^{\infty}(\overline{M},{}^{\phi}T^{*}M)=C^{\infty}(\overline{M})\text{-span}\left\langle\frac{dx}{x^{2}},\frac{dy_{i}}{x},dz_{j}\right\rangle.

We are building on the works of Grieser, Talebi and the second named author

[
16
,
44
,
43
]
.
There, some restrictions are imposed on the geometry, which we now gather together in a single assumption.

Assumption 2.1
.

a)

The higher order term satisfies
|\mathfrak{h}|_{g_{0}}=\mathcal{O}(x^{3})
as
x\to 0
.

b)

The fibration
\phi\colon(\partial M,g_{F}+\phi^{*}g_{B})\to(B,g_{B})
is a Riemannian submersion.

c)

The base manifold
B
of the fibration
\phi
has dimension
b=\dim(B)\geq 2
.

d)

The twisted Gauss-Bonnet operator
D_{B}
on the base
B
with values in the bundle of fibre-harmonic forms
\mathscr{H}^{*}(F,E)
over
B

satisfies some spectral conditions and commutes with the projection onto the fibre-harmonic forms. We refer to

[
16
, Assumption 1.4, 1.5]
for precise statements.

The simplest example of a
\phi
-manifold is
M=F\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
g_{\phi}=g_{F}+g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,
where
(F,g_{F})
is a closed Riemannian manifold and
g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote by

Y
the spherical compactification of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
at infinity, so that
\overline{M}=F\times Y
and
\partial M=F\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
The fibration
\phi:F\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is the projection onto the second factor.
If
r>0
denotes the Euclidean distance to the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
x=\frac{1}{r}
is a boundary defining function
at infinity. The metric induced from the Euclidean metric is precisely

g_{\phi}=\frac{dx^{2}}{x^{4}}+\frac{\phi^{*}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}{x^{2}}+g_{F}.

This structure might seem contrived, but arises naturally in several interesting examples.
We refer to
[
20
, Section 7]
for a more extensive treatment and mention just a few examples here.
Known
4
-dimensional examples of non-compact, complete hyperkähler manifolds
with Riemann tensor in
L^{2}
(so-called gravitational instantons), are all
\phi
-manifolds, some of them with non-trivial fibrations. The most well-known class are the ALE spaces,
like the Eguchi-Hanson space
[
11
]
with trivial fibre and
\partial M=\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{3}
. The second class of spaces are the ALF-spaces
(asymptotically locally flat) where
F=\mathbb{S}^{1}
, like the Gibbons-Hawking spaces
[
13
]
or Taub-NUT. The final family of

\phi
-manifolds we would like to mention are the ALG manifolds with

F=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
as constructed in
[
9
]
.

2.2.
First main result: invariance of analytic torsion

In case
\dim F=0
, the (renormalized) analytic torsion for
\phi
-metrics
has been studied by Guillarmou and Sher
[
17
]
.
An extension to the case
\dim F\geq 1
was outlined by Talebi
[
43
]
. In our first main result, we will present
a different construction and establish invariance properties of the analytic torsion. The result is
is a combination of Definition
6.3
and Corollary

.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(M,g_{\phi})
be a
\phi
-manifold and
(E,h)
a flat vector bundle over
\overline{M}
.
We impose Assumption
2.1
. Then the following statements hold.

a)

the renormalized analytic torsion

is well-defined;

b)

if

is odd, then the torsion norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(M,E,g_{\phi})}^{RS}
on the determinant line

-cohomology is invariant under perturbations of

of the form
g_{\phi}+h
with

|h|_{g_{\phi}}=\mathcal{O}(x^{b+1+\alpha})
as

for any
\alpha>0
and
b=\dim B
.

2.4.
Third main result: wedge degeneration

Our third and final main result is an application of the first two results, and is
concerned with a singular degeneration. We consider a
\phi
-manifold
(M,g_{\phi})

and a family
\mathscr{U}_{\varepsilon}=(0,\varepsilon)_{x}\times\partial M
of collars of the boundary.
We set
M_{\varepsilon}:=M\backslash\mathscr{U}_{\varepsilon}
. We can now consider a family of closed manifolds
(K_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})

which are obtained by gluing rescaled
\varepsilon M_{\varepsilon}
to a compact manifold
K^{\prime}
with boundary

\partial K^{\prime}=\partial M
. Taking
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain a singular manifold
(\Omega,g_{\omega})
in the
limit, with the metric given in terms of
r=1/x
in the singular neighborhood near
r=0
by

(2.3)

g_{\omega}\restriction(0,1)_{r}\times\partial M=dr^{2}+r^{2}\phi^{*}g_{B}+g_{F}+\mathfrak{h}(1/r).

Figure 2.
Conic and wedge degeneration

This is illustrated in Figure
2

\dim F=0
,
which has been studied in
[
17
]
under the name of

.
The metric

is not quite a wedge metric, since in the wedge case,
\partial M

is the total space of a fibration

with typical fibre

\displaystyle dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{B}+\psi^{*}g_{F},

i.e. the roles of base and fibres being reversed, when compared to (

Hence we can regard the limit space
(\Omega,g_{\omega})
as a wedge space only for
trivializable fibrations
\phi
. Our third main result is proved below
in Theorem

and reads as follows (we refer the reader to Theorem

for precise definitions of the spaces

and

).

Theorem 2.4
.

Consider the singular degeneration as described above.

a)

impose Assumption
2.1
on the complete
\phi
-manifold
(M,g_{\phi})
;

b)

write
(K,g):=(K_{1},g_{1})
for the compact smooth manifold in Figure

c)

assume

and consider the limiting wedge manifold

in Figure

with wedge along the base

and cone link

(note the interchange of the

Then there is a canonical isomorphism of determinant lines

\begin{split}\log T(K_{\varepsilon},E;g_{\varepsilon})&=\log T(M,E;g_{\phi})+\log T(\Omega,E;g_{\omega})\\

8.

There are gluing formulas for the analytic torsion, which
were established by Lesch
[
23
, Theorem 6.1]
for
manifolds with discrete spectrum. These were extended by the second author

[
46
, Theorem 2.9]
to non-compact manifolds with continuous
spectrum with either a spectral gap around zero or an acyclic vector bundle

. The gluing formula in

[
46
, Theorem 2.9]
applies to
\phi
-manifolds as well, and, after an extension, allows us to
reproduce and generalize a result by Guillarmou and Sher
[
17
, Theorem 10 & Lemma 42]
,
which they attain without gluing formulas. Our result requires
a restrictive assumption on the asymptotic end
\partial M
.

Assumption 8.1
.

We impose one of the following two assumptions.

a)

either
H^{k}(\partial M,E)=0
for all degrees
0\leq k\leq m-1=\dim(\partial M)
,

b)

or
H^{k}(\partial M,E)=0
for
1\leq k\leq m-2
and

E
is a trivial vector bundle over
\overline{M}
.

Note that the second case of the assumption above covers the setting of
Guillarmou and Sher
[
17
, Theorem 10 & Lemma 42]
. Note also that
the second case of the assumption is not present in
[
46
, Theorem 2.9]
.

To state the gluing formula, we need some notation. Continuing in the notation of
Section
2.1
, we write
N=(0,1]\times\partial M

and
K=M\backslash((0,1)\times\partial M)
. Let
\iota_{1}\colon\partial M\to N
and
\iota_{2}\colon\partial M\to K

denote the natural inclusions of
\partial M
as
\{1\}\times\partial M\subset N\cap K
. Define the
corresponding complexes, where the lower index
(2)
indicates
L^{2}
integrability with respect to
g_{\phi}

\displaystyle\Omega^{*}_{r}(K,E):=\{\omega\in\Omega^{*}(K,E)\,\colon\,\iota_{2}^{*}\omega=0\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{*}_{r}(N,E):=\{\omega\in\Omega_{(2)}^{*}(N,E)\,\colon\,\iota_{1}^{*}\omega=0\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{*}_{r}(M,E):=\{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in\Omega_{(2)}^{*}(N,E)\oplus\Omega^{*}(K,E)\,\colon\,\iota_{1}^{*}\omega_{1}=\iota_{2}^{*}\omega_{2}\}.

These complexes fit into short exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to\Omega^{*}_{r}(N,E)\xrightarrow{\alpha}\Omega_{r}^{*}(M,E)\xrightarrow{\beta}\Omega^{*}(K,E)\to 0,

\displaystyle 0\to\Omega_{r}^{*}(N,E)\oplus\Omega_{r}^{*}(K,E)\xrightarrow{\iota}\Omega^{*}_{r}(M,E)\xrightarrow{\rho}\Omega^{*}(\partial M,E)\to 0,

with
\iota
being the inclusion and
\rho
the restriction,

\alpha(\omega)=(\omega,0)
and
\beta(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\omega_{2}
.
Repeating the argument of Vishik
[
47

, the

harmonic forms of

and
\Omega^{*}(M,E)
coincide. So we get long-exact sequences in
(reduced

\displaystyle\dots\to H^{p}_{(2)}(N,\partial M,E)\xrightarrow{\alpha_{*}}H^{p}_{(2)}(M,E)\xrightarrow{\beta_{*}}H^{p}(K,E)\xrightarrow{\delta}H^{p+1}_{(2)}(N,\partial M,E)\to\dots,

\displaystyle\dots\to H^{p}_{(2)}(N,\partial M,E)\oplus H^{p}(K,\partial M,E)\xrightarrow{\iota_{*}}H^{p}_{(2)}(M,E)\xrightarrow{\rho_{*}}H^{p}(\partial M,E)\xrightarrow{\delta}\dots,

where

is the connecting homomorphism. These long exact sequences produce
isomorphisms of the determinant line bundles (see for instance
[
37
, Proposition 1.12 & Section 1.3]
)

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\det H^{*}_{(2)}(N,\partial M,E)\otimes\det H^{*}(K,E)\to\det H_{(2)}^{*}(M,E),

\displaystyle\Psi\colon\det H^{*}_{(2)}(N,\partial M,E)\otimes\det H^{*}(K,\partial M,E)\otimes\det H^{*}(\partial M,E)\to\det H_{(2)}^{*}(M,E).

With these maps, we can now write down the gluing formulas.

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Psi(\alpha\otimes\beta\otimes\gamma)\right\rVert_{(M,E)}^{RS}=\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{(N,\partial M,E)}^{RS}\otimes\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert^{RS}_{(K,N,E)}\otimes\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\det H^{*}(\partial M,E)},

Proof.

This is a strengthening of

Manifolds with corners

Assume

and polar coordinates around
Y
in

are smooth. The blow-up is equipped with the blow-down map

We denote by

The notion of polyhomogeneous functions uses the notion of b-vector fields and

the set

The extended union of two index sets is

(A.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\overline{\cup}\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{E}\cup\mathcal{F}\cup\{(\alpha,k+\ell+1)\,|\,(\alpha,k)\in\mathcal{E}\,\&\,(\alpha,\ell)\in\mathcal{F}\}.

We will rarely specify index sets explicitly. Instead we give lower bounds, where the notation is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}>c\iff\forall(\alpha,k)\in\mathcal{E}:\,\textup{Re}(\alpha)>c,

. A function

Appendix C
Renormalized analytic torsion of a wedge

Let
(B,g_{B})
,
(F,g_{F})
be two compact Riemannian manifolds. The
Riemannian cone over
B
is
\mathscr{C}(B)\coloneqq(0,\infty)\times B
equipped with the warped product metric
g_{C(B)}\coloneqq dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{B}
. The
Model wedge
is
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{C}(B)\times F
, with the product metric
g_{\mathscr{U}}=g_{\mathscr{C}(B)}+g_{F}
. We should point out that
on wedges,
F
is usually reserved for the cone link and
B
for the fibration base (in this appendix the fibration is trivial). Their roles are reversed here to match the
\phi
-setting as in (
2.4
) and the rest of the paper.

Proposition C.1
.

Assume that
\dim F
is even. Then the renormalized scalar analytic torsion of
the model wedge
(\mathscr{U},g_{\mathscr{U}})

equals
1
for any choice of
(B,g_{B})
and
(F,g_{F})
.

Proof.

We will as usual suppress the vector bundle
E\to\mathscr{U}
in the notation, and
just write
\Omega^{*}(\mathscr{U})
when we mean
\Omega^{*}(\mathscr{U},E)
.
Consider
\Delta
, the relative or absolute self-adjoint extension
of the Hodge Laplacian on
(\mathscr{U},g_{\mathscr{U}})
. See

[
5
]
for the discussion of relative and absolute boundary conditions,
see also
[
36
, (2.5)]
.

Case 1 -
F=\{pt\}
:

We will actually prove a bit more, namely that the associated zeta-functions

\zeta(s,\Delta_{\mathscr{C}(B)})
vanish identically in each degree.
Let
\lambda>0
and introduce the rescalings

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}\colon\mathscr{C}(B)\to\mathscr{C}(B),\quad f_{\lambda}(r,y)=(\lambda r,y),

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}\colon(0,\infty)\times\mathscr{C}(B)\to(0,\infty)\times\mathscr{C}(B),\quad F_{\lambda}(t,r,y)=(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda r,y).

f_{\lambda}
is a smooth map, so
f_{\lambda}^{*}d=df^{*}_{\lambda}
. If
\tilde{g}=\lambda^{2}g
, then
\tilde{*}_{|\Omega^{p}}=\lambda^{m-2p}*_{|\Omega^{p}}
. So
f^{*}d^{*}=\lambda^{-2}d^{*}f^{*}
. So

f^{*}_{\lambda}\Delta=\lambda^{-2}\Delta f^{*}_{\lambda}.

From here we conclude that the rescalings preserve the relative and absolute domains
of
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}(B)}
. Now, one easily checks that
\omega
solves the
heat equation with initial data
\omega_{0}
if and only if
F^{*}_{\lambda}\omega

solves the heat equation with initial data
f_{\lambda}^{*}\omega_{0}
. Hence,
with
\dim B=b
,

\displaystyle\omega(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda r,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B}e^{-t\Delta}(t,r,y,\tilde{r},\tilde{y})\ \omega_{0}(\lambda\tilde{r},\tilde{y})\,\tilde{r}^{b}d\tilde{r}\,\mathrm{dVol}_{g_{B}}(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B}\lambda^{-1-b}e^{-t\Delta}(t,r,y,\tilde{r}/\lambda,\tilde{y})\ \omega_{0}(\tilde{r},\tilde{y})\,\tilde{r}^{b}d\tilde{r}\,\mathrm{dVol}_{g_{B}}(\tilde{y}).

On the other hand,
\omega(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda r,y)
can be obtained as

\displaystyle\omega(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda r,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B}e^{-t\Delta}(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda r,y,\tilde{r},\tilde{y})\ \omega_{0}(\tilde{r},\tilde{y})\,\tilde{r}^{b}d\tilde{r}\,\mathrm{dVol}_{g_{B}}(\tilde{y}).

Comparing these two expressions, we obtain

(C.1)

\displaystyle e^{-t\Delta}(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda r,y,\lambda\tilde{r},\tilde{y})=\lambda^{-1-b}e^{-t\Delta}(t,r,y,\tilde{r},\tilde{y}).

Note that here
(1+b)
is the total dimension of
\mathscr{C}(B)
.
Similar results were discussed in
[
8
, Section 2]
, for instance.
We now consider the regularized heat trace in a single degree.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}^{R}e^{-t\Delta_{k}}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B}\mathrm{tr}(e^{-t\Delta_{k}})\,r^{b}dr\mathrm{dVol}_{g_{B}}=:\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{b}H^{\prime}_{k}(t,r)dr,

where we recall the notation for a regularized integral

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}:=\LIM_{\varepsilon\to 0}\LIM_{R\to\infty}\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}.

Using (
C.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}^{R}e^{-t\Delta_{k}}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{k}^{\prime}(t,r)dr=t^{-\frac{b+1}{2}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{k}^{\prime}\Bigl(1,\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\Bigr)dr.

There is a variable change rule for regularized integrals, see e.g.

[
22
, Lemma 2.1.4]
. Namely, if
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
admits polyhomogeneous
expansions as
x\to 0
and
x\to\infty
, so that its regularized integral exists, then
for any
\lambda>0

(C.2)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}f(\lambda x)\,dx=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{f}}c_{\ell}\log(\lambda)^{\ell}

for some coefficients
c_{k}
and
n_{f}\in\mathbb{N}
, which can be explicitly given
in terms of the coefficients in the asymptotic expansions of
f
. Consequently

(C.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}^{R}e^{-t\Delta_{k}}=t^{-\frac{b+1}{2}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{k}^{\prime}\Bigl(1,\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\Bigr)r^{b}dr=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{k}^{\prime}\Bigl(1,r\Bigr)r^{b}dr+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{k}}c_{k,\ell}\log(t)^{\ell}.

By definition (
6.2
) we find

\displaystyle\Gamma(s)\zeta(s,\Delta_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{k}^{\prime}\Bigl(1,r\Bigr)r^{b}dr\cdot\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}t^{s-1}dt+\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{s-1}dt\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{k}}c_{k,\ell}\cdot\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}t^{s-1}\log(t)^{\ell}dt+\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\log(t)^{\ell}dt\Bigr),

where we of course mean analytic continuations of the four integrals.
For any
(\alpha,k)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
f(x)=x^{\alpha}\log(x)^{k}

[
22
, Equation 1.12]
asserts that (and one can easily check this)

\int_{0}^{1}f(x)x^{s-1}\,dx=(-1)^{k}\frac{k!}{(\alpha+s)^{k+1}},

\int_{1}^{\infty}f(x)x^{s-1}\,dx=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{k!}{(\alpha+s)^{k+1}}.

So we get the somewhat strange looking formula.

(C.4)

\int_{0}^{1}f(x)x^{s-1}\,dx+\int_{1}^{\infty}f(x)x^{s-1}\,dx=0.

This proves
\zeta(s,\Delta_{k})\equiv 0
for each
k
and the claim
follows for the special case
F=\{pt\}
.

Case 2 -
F
arbitrary:

We start by deriving a partial product rule for the analytic torsion following
[
38
, Theorem 2.5]
and
[
23
, Proposition 2.3]
. Since the metric on
\mathscr{U}
is of product type,
g_{\mathscr{U}}=g_{\mathscr{C}(B)}+g_{F}
, the Laplacian (acting on forms) splits,

\Delta_{\mathscr{U}}=\Delta_{\mathscr{C}(B)}+\Delta_{F}.

Splitting
\Omega^{*}(\mathscr{U})=\Omega^{*}(\mathscr{C}(B))\otimes\Omega^{*}(F)
, we get a corresponding split of the heat operators (and their integral kernels)

e^{-t\Delta_{\mathscr{U}}}=e^{-t\Delta_{\mathscr{C}(B)}}\otimes e^{-t\Delta_{F}}.

Finally, we also split the number operator
N
as
N=N_{\mathscr{C}(B)}+N_{F}
, where
N_{\mathscr{C}(B)}\coloneqq N_{\mathscr{C}(B)}\otimes\mathds{1}
and
N_{F}\coloneqq\mathds{1}\otimes N_{F}
. One readily checks that if
V,W
are vector spaces and
A\in\mathrm{End}(V),B\in\mathrm{End}(W)
, then
\mathrm{tr}_{V\otimes W}(A\otimes B)=\mathrm{tr}_{V}(A)\cdot\mathrm{tr}_{W}(B)
. So the pointwise trace satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left((-1)^{N}Ne^{-t\Delta_{\mathscr{U}}}\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left((-1)^{N_{\mathscr{C}(B)}}(-1)^{N_{F}}(N_{\mathscr{C}(B)}+N_{F})e^{-t\Delta_{{\mathscr{C}(B)}}}\otimes e^{-t\Delta_{F}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left((-1)^{N_{\mathscr{C}(B)}}N_{{\mathscr{C}(B)}}e^{-t\Delta_{{\mathscr{C}(B)}}}\right)\cdot\mathrm{tr}\left((-1)^{N_{F}}e^{-t\Delta_{F}}\right)

(C.5)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{tr}\left((-1)^{N_{\mathscr{C}(B)}}e^{-t\Delta_{{\mathscr{C}(B)}}}\right)\cdot\mathrm{tr}\left((-1)^{N_{F}}N_{F}e^{-t\Delta_{F}}\right).

Here the traces on the first line are over
\Omega^{*}(\mathscr{U})
, whereas the traces on line 2 (and 3) are over
\Omega^{*}(\mathscr{C}(B))
and
\Omega^{*}(F)
respectively.
Since
F
is compact, we can integrate over
F
on both sides of (
C.5
).
By the McKean-Singer formula
[
30
, Section 6, eq. 1]
we have

\int_{F}\mathrm{tr}\left((-1)^{N_{F}}e^{-t\Delta_{F}}\right)\,\mathrm{dVol}_{g_{F}}=\chi(F,E)

for any
t>0
, where
E
really means
E_{|F}
. In particular, it does not depend on
t
. So after taking the regularised heat trace of
\mathscr{C}(B)
and the regularized time integral, the second line of (
C.5
) will become

\Gamma(s)\sum_{k=0}^{b+1}(-1)^{k}\cdot k\cdot\zeta(s,\Delta_{\mathscr{C}(B),k})\cdot\chi(F,E).

This vanishes by case 1 since each
\zeta(s,\Delta_{\mathscr{C}(B),k})=0
.

We therefore turn to the second term in (
C.5
). We perform the regularized spatial integral over
\mathscr{C}(B)
and the integral over
F
. Using (
C.3
) we find

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}^{R}\left((-1)^{N_{\mathscr{C}(B)}}e^{-t\Delta_{{\mathscr{C}(B)}}}\right)\cdot\mathrm{Tr}\left((-1)^{N_{F}}N_{F}e^{-t\Delta_{F}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{b+1}(-1)^{k}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}H^{\prime}_{k}(1,r)\,r^{b}dr+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{k}}c_{k\ell}\log(t)^{\ell}\right)\mathrm{Tr}\left((-1)^{N_{F}}N_{F}e^{-t\Delta_{F}}\right).

We abbreviate a bit. Set
c_{k\ell}=0
for
k>n_{k}
, let
n\coloneqq\max_{k}n_{k}
,
C_{0}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{b+1}(-1)^{k}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}H^{\prime}_{k}(1,r)\,r^{b}dr
, and
C_{\ell}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{b+1}(-1)^{k}c_{k\ell}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}^{R}\left((-1)^{N_{\mathscr{C}(B)}}e^{-t\Delta_{\mathscr{C}(B)}}\right)\cdot\mathrm{Tr}\left((-1)^{N_{F}}N_{F}e^{-t\Delta_{F}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}C_{\ell}\log(t)^{\ell}\,\mathrm{Tr}\left((-1)^{N_{F}}N_{F}e^{-t\Delta_{F}}\right),

The trace over the heat kernel
e^{-t\Delta_{F}}
is really over all eigenvalues, but we can change it to a sum over only the positive eigenvalues. The reason is that the difference is given by

\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}C_{\ell}\log(t)^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\dim(F)}(-1)^{j}\,j\,b_{j}(F,E),

where
b_{j}(F,E)\coloneqq\dim(\mathrm{Ker}(\Delta_{F,j}))
.
Multiplying this by
t^{s-1}
and performing the integrals in time yields zero by (
C.4
). As such, we are left with studying

\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}C_{\ell}\log(t)^{\ell}\,\mathrm{Tr}^{\prime}\left((-1)^{N_{F}}N_{F}e^{-t\Delta_{F}}\right),

where
Tr^{\prime}
means the trace but omitting zero eigenvalues.
We have to compute

\int_{0}^{1}t^{s-1}\log(t)^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\dim(F)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{j}\in\mathrm{spec}(\Delta_{F,j})\\
\lambda_{j}>0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}je^{-t\lambda_{j}}\,dt

and

\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\log(t)^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\dim(F)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{j}\in\mathrm{spec}(\Delta_{F,j})\\
\lambda_{j}>0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}je^{-t\lambda_{j}}\,dt,

where the sums over the eigenvalues are with multiplicity.
Both integrals converge for
\text{Re}(s)>1
, so we can combine them into one integral. One readily checks

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}e^{-\lambda t}\log(t)^{\ell}\,dt=\frac{d^{\ell}}{ds^{\ell}}(\lambda^{-s}\Gamma(s)).

Hence our integral reads

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}C_{\ell}\log(t)^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\dim(F)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{j}\in\mathrm{spec}(\Delta_{F,j})\\
\lambda_{j}>0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}je^{-t\lambda_{j}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}C_{\ell}\frac{d^{\ell}}{ds^{\ell}}\left(\Gamma(s)\sum_{j=0}^{\dim(F)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{j}\in\mathrm{spec}(\Delta_{F,j})\\
\lambda_{j}>0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}j\lambda_{j}^{-s}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}C_{\ell}\frac{d^{\ell}}{ds^{\ell}}\left(\Gamma(s)\sum_{j=0}^{\dim(F)}(-1)^{j}j\zeta(s,\Delta_{F,j})\right).

But the sum
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\dim(F)}(-1)^{j}j\zeta(s,\Delta_{F,j})
vanishes by
[
38
, Theorem 2.3]
since
\dim(F)
is even.
Hence the torsion of
\mathscr{C}(B)\times F
is trivial.

∎

Remark C.2
.

One could possibly drop the assumption that the dimension of
F
is even. The obstruction in the above argument is the term

\mathrm{Tr}^{R}\left((-1)^{N_{\mathscr{C}(B)}}e^{-t\Delta_{\mathscr{C}(B)}}\right)\eqqcolon\chi^{R}(\mathscr{C}(B),t).

If one can show that is time-independent like in the compact case, the product formula would read (suppressing the metric from the notation)

\log(T(\mathscr{C}(B)\times F,E))=\log(T(\mathscr{C}(B),E))\chi(F,E)+\chi^{R}\log(T(F,E))=\chi^{R}(\mathscr{C}(B))\log(T(F,E)).

We do not have an argument that
\chi
is time-independent in general. When
B=\mathbb{S}^{b}/\Gamma
for some finite group
\Gamma\subset O(b+1)
acting freely and
g_{B}
is the round metric, then
\chi^{R}(\mathscr{C}(B),t)=0
for all
t
. To see this, assume
B=\mathbb{S}^{b}
. Then
\mathscr{C}(B)\cong\mathbb{R}^{b+1}\setminus\{0\}
with the Euclidean metric. The heat kernel acting on
k
-forms is
H_{k}=H_{0}\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{k}}
, and
H_{0}(t,x,y)=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{b+1}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|}{4t}\right)
. So
\mathrm{tr}(H_{k})=c_{k}t^{-\frac{b+1}{2}}
for all
r
and the regularized heat trace vanishes by (
C.4
). When
B=\mathbb{S}^{b}/\Gamma
, the heat kernel is the same since it is rotationally invariant and therefore descends to the quotient.

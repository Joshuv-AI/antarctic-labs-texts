Leading-order gravitational radiation to all spin orders Preprint: IRMP-CP3-23-54 Preprint: UUITP-27/23 Preprint: CALT-TH-2023-040 Preprint: CERN-TH-2023-177
Rafael Aoude, Kays Haddad, Carlo Heissenberg, Andreas Helset

Abstract
Starting with on-shell amplitudes compatible with the scattering of Kerr black holes, we produce the gravitational waveform and memory effect including spin at their leading post-Minkowskian orders to all orders in the spins of both scattering objects. For the memory effect, we present results at next-to-leading order as well, finding a closed form for all spin orders when the spins are anti-aligned and equal in magnitude. Considering instead generically oriented spins, we produce the next-to-leading-order memory to sixth order in spin. Compton-amplitude contact terms up to sixth order in spin are included throughout our analysis.

IV
Gravitational memory effect

Rather than requiring the full five-point amplitude, or even its cut part used above, the gravitational memory effect is related to the limit of the five-point amplitude as the emitted graviton is soft
Strominger and Zhiboedov 2016
.
Combining this with existing high-spin, two-to-two amplitudes compatible with Kerr scattering up to 2PM order puts the next-to-leading-order (NLO) memory effect including high spin orders within reach
Guevara et al. 2019b
;
Aoude et al. 2023
(see also refs.
Chen et al. 2022
;
Bern et al. 2022c
;
Bautista 2023
for high-but-finite-spin scattering amplitudes at 2PM order).
In this section we present the gravitational memory effect at leading order to all spin orders and for generic spin orientations, before computing the next-to-leading-order memory effect to all spin orders for anti-aligned spins and to sixth order for generic orientations.

IV.1
Leading order

At leading order in Newton’s constant, the memory effect is expressed simply in terms of the soft limit of the tree-level two-to-two amplitude as
Strominger and Zhiboedov 2016
;
Herderschee et al. 2023

\displaystyle\left.\Delta(h_{+}^{\infty}+ih_{\times}^{\infty})\right|^{\rm LO}=-\frac{i\kappa}{32\pi|\boldsymbol{x}|}

(23)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int\hat{d}^{4}q\,\hat{\delta}(p_{1}\cdot q)\hat{\delta}(p_{2}\cdot q)e^{iq\cdot b}\varepsilon^{\mu}_{-}\varepsilon^{\nu}_{-}\mathcal{S}(\rho,q)_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{M}_{t}(q).

Here,
\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(\rho,q)=\omega\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(k,q)
is the soft factor multiplied by the frequency of the soft graviton, and
\mathcal{M}_{t}(q)
is the
t
-channel graviton-exchange amplitude.
Orienting the transfer momentum such that
q=p_{1}-p_{1}^{\prime}=p_{2}^{\prime}-p_{2}
, these take the forms
Weinberg 1965
;
Guevara et al. 2019b

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(k,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{p_{i}^{\mu}p_{i}^{\nu}}{p_{i}\cdot k}-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{p_{i}^{\prime\mu}p_{i}^{\prime\nu}}{p_{i}^{\prime}\cdot k},

(24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{t}(q)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{2}m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}}{4q^{2}}\sum_{\pm}\left(\gamma\pm\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}\right)^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left[\pm\frac{i\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}v_{1}^{\mu}v_{2}^{\nu}q^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\left(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\beta}+\mathfrak{a}_{2}^{\beta}\right)\right].

The soft factor admits an
\hbar
expansion when scaling
k,q\sim\hbar
, which we can write as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(k,q)=\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{\mu\nu;\rho}(k)q^{\rho}+\mathcal{S}^{(1)}_{\mu\nu;\rho\tau}(k)q^{\rho}q^{\tau}+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{2}),

(26)

with
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{\mu\nu;\rho_{1}\dots\rho_{i+1}}(k)\sim\hbar^{-1}
.
The classically-relevant portion of
eq.

23
only needs the leading term in
eq.

26
.

The fact that the spin dependence of the amplitude in
eq.

25
is contained entirely in an exponential means that the evaluation of the gravitational memory effect for all spins at leading order is nearly identical to the scalar case
Alessio and Di Vecchia 2022
.
Defining

\displaystyle b^{\mu}_{\pm}\equiv b^{\mu}\pm\frac{\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}v_{1}^{\nu}v_{2}^{\alpha}\left(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\beta}+\mathfrak{a}_{2}^{\beta}\right)}{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}},

(27)

the memory effect is

\displaystyle\left.\Delta(h_{+}^{\infty}+ih_{\times}^{\infty})\right|^{\rm LO}=\frac{i\kappa^{3}m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}}{128\pi|\boldsymbol{x}|}\sum_{\pm}\left(\gamma\pm\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\times\varepsilon^{\mu}_{-}\varepsilon^{\nu}_{-}\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{\mu\nu;\tau}(\rho)\int\hat{d}^{4}q\,\hat{\delta}(p_{1}\cdot q)\hat{\delta}(p_{2}\cdot q)\frac{e^{iq\cdot b_{\pm}}}{q^{2}}q^{\tau}.

(28)

From this expression, it is immediate to explain an observation made in ref.
Jakobsen et al. 2022
up to quadratic order in spin and to see that it extends to all spin orders: for the anti-aligned-spin setup,
\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\mu}=-\mathfrak{a}_{2}^{\mu}
, the leading-order memory effect for two scattering Kerr black holes is equivalent to that for Schwarzschild black holes.
This is because the shifted impact parameters are identical to the unshifted impact parameter in this configuration.

We recognize in
eq.

28
the expression for the leading-order classical impulse,
\mathcal{Q}_{\rm 1,1PM}^{\mu}=p_{1}^{\prime\mu}-p_{1}^{\mu}
, experienced by particle 1 in the scattering
Kosower et al. 2019
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{1,{\rm 1PM}}^{\tau}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{4}\int\hat{d}^{4}q\hat{\delta}(p_{1}\cdot q)\hat{\delta}(p_{2}\cdot q)q^{\tau}e^{ib\cdot q}\mathcal{M}_{t}(q)

(29)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa^{2}m_{1}m_{2}}{32\pi\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\sum_{\pm}\left(\gamma\pm\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}\right)^{2}\frac{b_{\pm}^{\tau}}{b_{\pm}^{2}}.

In terms of the impulse, the leading-order, all-spin memory effect is

\displaystyle\left.\Delta(h_{+}^{\infty}+ih_{\times}^{\infty})\right|^{\rm LO}=\frac{\kappa\varepsilon^{\mu}_{-}\varepsilon^{\nu}_{-}}{8\pi|\boldsymbol{x}|}\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{\mu\nu;\tau}(\rho)\mathcal{Q}^{\tau}_{1,{\rm 1PM}},

(30)

with generic spin orientations.
Eq. (
30
) thus agrees with the leading-PM expansion of the leading classical soft theorem
Saha et al. 2020
;
Sahoo and Sen 2022
;
Di Vecchia et al. 2022a
;
Di Vecchia et al. 2022b
, which fixes the memory effect in the time domain, equivalently the
1/\omega
terms in frequency domain, in terms of the initial and final momenta of the scattering.
We have also checked that the spinless contribution is in agreement with ref.
Herderschee et al. 2023
.
Specializing to the aligned-spin case and expanding to quadratic order in spin, we find agreement with the result in ref.
Jakobsen et al. 2022
, taking into account the factor of 2 mentioned in
footnote

1

IV.2
Next-to-leading order

To subleading PM order, following ref.
Caron-Huot et al. 2023
, we modify the integrand of

23
to contain the classical part of the 2PM instead of the tree-level amplitude and include a two-massive-particle cut contribution that arises from the KMOC formalism.
4
4

4

We thank Zvi Bern for discussions on this point.

Additional classical cuts involving intermediate on-shell massless and massive particle lines are subleading in the soft limit

;
Herderschee et al. 2023

Elkhidir et al. 2023

Georgoudis et al. 2023

The next-to-leading-order memory effect is thus given by

\displaystyle\left.\Delta(h_{+}^{\infty}+ih_{\times}^{\infty})\right|^{\rm NLO}=-\frac{i\kappa}{32\pi|\boldsymbol{x}|}\int\hat{d}^{4}q\,\hat{\delta}\left(p_{1}\cdot q-\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right)\hat{\delta}\left(p_{2}\cdot q+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right)e^{iq\cdot b}\varepsilon^{\mu}_{-}\varepsilon^{\nu}_{-}

\displaystyle\times\left\{\mathcal{S}(\rho,q)_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{M}_{\rm 2PM}+\frac{i}{8}\int\hat{d}^{4}\ell\,\hat{\delta}\left(p_{1}\cdot\ell-\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\right)\hat{\delta}\left(p_{2}\cdot\ell+\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\right)\left[\mathcal{S}(\rho,q-\ell)_{\mu\nu}-\mathcal{S}(\rho,\ell)_{\mu\nu}\right]\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\dagger}(q-\ell)\mathcal{M}_{t}(\ell)\right\}.

(31)

The first term in curly brackets is the 2PM analog of
eq.

23
, and will be related to the portion of 2PM impulse transverse to the incoming momenta,
\mathcal{Q}_{1,{\rm 2PM}}^{\mu}
, while the second term is the cut contribution.
One must account for the full delta functions

;
Di Vecchia et al. 2023b
;
Caron-Huot et al. 2023
, and not only their linearized versions, because the cut contribution is superficially superclassical, so it must be expanded to subleading order in
\hbar

Scrutinizing the cut contribution in more detail, we must be careful to correctly interpret
\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}_{t}(q)
when spin is involved.
Importantly, in contrast to the spinless case,
\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}_{t}(q)\neq\left[\mathcal{M}_{t}(q)\right]^{*}
.
Rather, for the
S
-matrix defined by
S=1+iT
,
5
5

5

The overall momentum-conserving delta function has the abbreviated argument
p=p_{1}+p_{2}-p_{1}^{\prime}-p_{2}^{\prime}
.

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}^{D}(p)\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}(q)

\displaystyle=\langle p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime}|T^{\dagger}|p_{1},p_{2}\rangle

(32)

\displaystyle=\left(\langle p_{1},p_{2}|T|p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime}\rangle\right)^{*}=\hat{\delta}^{D}(p)\left[\mathcal{M}(-q)\right]^{*},

where we can safely make the final identification in the classical limit, in which
\mathcal{O}(\hbar)
modifications of the massive momenta do not affect the scattering amplitude
Haddad 2022
;
Aoude et al. 2022a
.
Specializing to tree-level two-to-two scattering,
eq.

25

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}_{t}(q)=\mathcal{M}_{t}(q).

(33)

This conclusion is consistent with unitarity of the
S
-matrix, a consequence of which is that the
T
-matrix is Hermitian for the leading-order two-to-two scattering process.

Now, as the first quantum corrections to
\mathcal{M}_{t}
are suppressed by two powers of
\hbar
, the expansion of the cut contribution to next-to-leading order in
\hbar
is controlled by the expansion of the product of the delta functions and soft factors.
A consequence of

33

IV.2
is classical at leading order in
\hbar

Evaluating the remaining classical part of the cut contribution, the NLO memory effect expressed in terms of the 1PM and 2PM (transverse) impulses on particle 1 is

\displaystyle\left.\Delta(h_{+}^{\infty}+ih_{\times}^{\infty})\right|^{\rm NLO}=\frac{\kappa\varepsilon^{\mu}_{-}\varepsilon^{\nu}_{-}}{8\pi|\boldsymbol{x}|}\left\{\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{\mu\nu;\tau}(\rho)\mathcal{Q}^{\tau}_{1,{\rm 2PM}}\right.

(34)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{Q}_{1,{\rm 1PM}}^{\alpha}\mathcal{Q}_{1,{\rm 1PM}}^{\beta}\left[\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{\mu\nu;\tau}(\rho)\left(\frac{\check{v}_{1}^{\tau}}{2m_{1}}-\frac{\check{v}_{2}^{\tau}}{2m_{2}}\right)\eta_{\alpha\beta}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\mathcal{S}_{-\mu\nu;\alpha\beta}^{(1)}(\rho)\right]\right\},

where

\displaystyle\check{v}_{1}^{\mu}=\frac{\gamma v_{2}^{\mu}-v_{1}^{\mu}}{\gamma^{2}-1},\quad\check{v}_{2}^{\mu}=\frac{\gamma v_{1}^{\mu}-v_{2}^{\mu}}{\gamma^{2}-1}.

(35)

This is in precise agreement with the gravitational memory (see, e.g., refs.
Sahoo and Sen 2022
;
Di Vecchia et al. 2022a
;
Di Vecchia et al. 2022b
) expanded to next-to-leading PM order, when the initial and final momenta are related by the classical impulse

\displaystyle Q^{\mu}

\displaystyle=p_{1}^{\prime\mu}-p_{1}^{\mu}=-(p_{2}^{\prime\mu}-p_{2}^{\mu})

(36)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{1,{\rm 1PM}}^{\mu}+\mathcal{Q}_{1,{\rm 2PM}}^{\mu}-\left(\frac{\check{v}_{1}^{\mu}}{2m_{1}}-\frac{\check{v}_{2}^{\mu}}{2m_{2}}\right)\mathcal{Q}_{1,{\rm 1PM}}^{2}.

Without the cut contribution in
section

IV.2
,
eq.

34
would be missing the contributions quadratic in
\mathcal{Q}_{1,{\rm 1PM}}^{\mu}
. Note that, up the PM order considered here, we were able to focus on the so-called

memory, whose expression is captured by the soft factor (

and

appear.

;

As we have evaluated the 1PM impulse above, the last ingredient for writing the NLO memory effect explicitly is the evaluation of the 2PM transverse impulse.
We will do so for generic and anti-aligned spin orientations, beginning with the latter.

This statement is true independently of contact-term contributions, as contact terms enter from the hexadecapole while the memory effect is sensitive to lower spin multipoles.
Notably, however, the dependence on odd spin orders vanishes if we additionally take the two masses to be equal.

In this configuration the anti-aligned spins are collinear with the orbital angular momentum, which implies

Defining

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we have employed on-shell amplitudes and spinor-helicity variables to access all-spin-order contributions to the leading-order waveform and the gravitational memory effect up to next-to-leading order.
In particular, gluing the Kerr-compatible, all-spin gravitational Compton amplitude derived in ref.
Aoude et al. 2022a
(but in the form written in ref.
Aoude et al. 2022b
) with the all-spin Kerr three-point amplitude
Levi and Steinhoff 2015
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2021
;
Vines 2018
;
Guevara et al. 2019a
;
Chung et al. 2019
gives the portion of the single-graviton-emission, five-point amplitude containing long-distance information to all spin orders.

The KMOC formalism
Kosower et al. 2019
;
Cristofoli et al. 2022
provides a means for relating this portion of the five-point amplitude to the leading-order gravitational waveform, producing an expression for the waveform, which is valid to all spin orders.
As written,
section

\displaystyle=I_{w}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}[\{x,b_{1},b_{2}+i\mathfrak{a}_{2}\};1/q_{2}^{2}].

(77)

Computing with the method in appendix C of ref.
Cristofoli et al. 2022
, the rank-0 integral is

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{(1)}(b_{(1)})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi|\boldsymbol{b}_{(1)}|},

(78)

where
\boldsymbol{b}_{(1)}^{\mu}=b_{(1)\nu}\Pi_{{\rm 3d,}(1)}^{\mu\nu}
.
Differentiating,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{(1)}^{\mu}(b_{(1)})

\displaystyle=\frac{-i\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{b}_{(1),\mu}}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}(b_{(1)})=\frac{i}{4\pi}\frac{\boldsymbol{b}^{\mu}_{(1)}}{|\boldsymbol{b}_{(1)}|^{3}}.

(79)

We have corroborated this expression through explicit computation as for the rank-0 case, thus verifying the validity of this derivative operation even in the presence of spin.

Equation

79
is in agreement with ref.
Jakobsen et al. 2022
, only the result here encodes effects at all spin orders.
Generating higher ranks is straightforward with the integral written in this form, keeping in mind that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{b}_{(1)}^{\mu}}{\partial\boldsymbol{b}_{(1),\nu}}=\Pi_{{\rm 3d,}(1)}^{\mu\nu},

(80)

such that the result remains orthogonal to
v_{2}^{\mu}
.

B.2
Memory-effect integration

For the leading-order memory effect, the only integral we need is
Kosower et al. 2019

\displaystyle I_{m}^{\mu}[b;1/q^{2}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2\pi\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\frac{b^{\mu}}{b^{2}},

(81)

where
|x|\equiv\sqrt{-x^{2}}
.
In terms of this, the all-spin leading-order memory effect is obtained by a redefinition of the impact parameter.

At next-to-leading order, all integrals we need can be obtained by differentiating the base integral
Aoude et al. 2021

\displaystyle I_{m}[b;1/|q|]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\frac{1}{|b|}.

(82)

In terms of this, the rank-
n
integral is

\displaystyle I^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}_{m}

\displaystyle=-i\partial_{b}^{\mu_{n}}I^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n-1}}_{m}.

(83)

When employing this relation, it is crucial to keep in mind that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial b^{\mu}}{\partial b_{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{{\rm 2d}},

(84)

such that the result of the differentiation remains in the 2-plane containing
b^{\mu}
.
We needed an arbitrary number of such derivatives to present all-order-in-spin results for the anti-aligned configuration.
This task was simplified in two ways.
First, the complexity of the derivatives is reduced by considering instead
I_{m}[q^{2n}/|q|]
, which enter in the calculation for
0\leq n\leq 3
.
Then, taking advantage of the fact that
v_{i}\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{\rm aa}=b\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{\rm aa}=0
, we were able to find a closed form for the projection of the rank-
2k
Fourier transform into the hyperplane orthogonal to
\mathfrak{a}_{\rm aa}^{\mu_{i}}\mathfrak{a}_{\rm aa}^{\nu_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq k
.
Specifically,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\mathfrak{a}_{{\rm aa},\mu_{i}}\mathfrak{a}_{{\rm aa},\nu_{i}}-\eta_{\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}\mathfrak{a}_{{\rm aa}}\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{{\rm aa}}\right)I^{\mu\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\dots\mu_{k}\nu_{k}}_{m}[b;q^{2n}/|q|]

\displaystyle=-i\frac{4^{n}(1/2)_{n}}{(1)_{n}}\frac{(-4)^{k}(3/2)_{k+n}(1)_{k+n}}{2\pi\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\rm aa}^{2k}b^{\mu}}{|b|^{2k+2n+3}},

(85)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\mathfrak{a}_{{\rm aa},\mu_{i}}\mathfrak{a}_{{\rm aa},\nu_{i}}-\eta_{\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}\mathfrak{a}_{{\rm aa}}\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{{\rm aa}}\right)I^{\mu\nu\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\dots\mu_{k}\nu_{k}}_{m}[b;q^{2n}/|q|]

\displaystyle=-\frac{4^{n}(1/2)_{n}}{(1)_{n}}\frac{(-4)^{k}(3/2)_{k+n}(1)_{k+n}}{2\pi\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\rm aa}^{2k-2}}{|b|^{2k+2n+3}}

\displaystyle\times\left[\mathfrak{a}_{\rm aa}^{2}\Pi^{\mu\nu}+(2k+2n+3)\mathfrak{a}_{\rm aa}^{2}\frac{b^{\mu}b^{\nu}}{|b|^{2}}+\frac{k}{n+1}\mathfrak{a}_{\rm aa}^{\mu}\mathfrak{a}_{\rm aa}^{\nu}\right].

(86)

These integrals are sufficient for completely determining the next-to-leading-order memory effect in the anti-aligned configuration to all spin orders, including in the presence of higher-spin-order Compton-amplitude contact terms than those considered here.

For the case of more generally oriented spins, we evaluated the next-to-leading-order memory effect up to sixth order in spin.
This necessitated up to six derivatives of
eqs.

85
and
86
with
k=0
.

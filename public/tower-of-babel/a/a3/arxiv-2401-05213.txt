The role of the pseudogap in cuprate superconductors demonstrated by the Hall effect
Júlia C. Anjos, Hércules Santana and E. V. L. de Mello

Abstract
Cuprate high-temperature superconductors are known to have a normal-state pseudogap but, after many years of intense research, its relation to the superconductivity is still a mystery. Similarly, the in-plane Hall coefficient R H R_{\rm H} has a large temperature variation caused by changes in the carrier density which is also a long-standing puzzle. We approach both problem by a kinetic theory of phase separation that reproduces the charge density wave by a free energy array of potential wells. The charge modulation favors local hole-hole attraction yielding local superconducting pairs and a transition by long-range phase order at T c T_{\rm c} . As the temperature increases the modulations decrease and the charge density increases by thermal activation. This approach reproduces the R H R_{\rm H} of La 2-x Sr x CuO 4 compounds of x = 0.0 − 0.25 x=0.0-0.25 and clarifies the role of the pseudogap and the disorder in cuprates.

The underlying physics of the pseudogap has long been probed by many different
experiments and techniques

but it is still shrouded in mystery

.
It is observed in both electron and hole-doped cuprate,
but remains unresolved despite many years of intense studies

.
More concerning still, there is not a agreement on the
precise phase boundaries and phenomenology of this normal state
gap. It is agreed that it represents a partial gap in the electronic
density of states
Hüfner et al. 2008
and is seen in
the antinodal region of
k
-space
Wise et al. 2008
. Once, it was believed
to be solely associated with the under-doped side of the phase diagram but now
it is described as a line falling to zero either at a critical doping in the
overdoped region
Badoux et al. 2016
;
Hüfner et al. 2008
or at the end of the superconducting
dome
Hüfner et al. 2008
;
Hashimoto et al. 2014
.

On the other hand, the in-plane Hall coefficient
R_{\rm H}=1/ne

charge and
n
the itinerant carrier density, probes
the charge dynamics of metals and semiconductors

In metals described by the Fermi liquid model,

is constant
and negative because the conduction band density of electrons.
In cuprate high-temperature superconductors
(HTS), it is positive due to the hole carriers and has a large variation
with the temperature.
Comparison between powder and single crystals data demonstrated
that the magnitude and temperature dependence of
R_{\rm H}

is dominated by the in-plane contribution

but
after all these years there is not an explanation

We propose here that

is influenced by the planar charge instabilities
that is observed in different forms like stripes

checkerboard
Hanaguri et al. 2004

Lang et al. 2002

this was a phenomenon attributed solely to weakly doped compounds
around

more intense

;
Hücker et al. 2014
;
Blanco-Canosa et al. 2014

a variety of complementary experimental probes have detected charge instability
in all hole-doped HTS families

More recently, CDW has been detected in overdoped La
2-x

x

4

;

This overall presence of various forms of incommensurate charge order (CO)

Accordingly, we have developed a model which the pseudogap is

We study this phenomenon by the time-dependent non-linear Cahn–Hilliard (CH)
differential equation used to describe the order-disorder transition
of binary alloys formation. The method minimizes a Ginzburg-Landau (GL) free energy given by
the (phase separation) order parameter power expansion

The GL potential (

The low density regions favor antiferromagnetic (AF) fluctuations that are

When

;

by calculating the flow of holes tunnelling through

The presence of

and the similarities between CO in hole-
and electron-doped phase diagrams

suggest the existence of

p=0

even the insulator compounds with
n\approx 1
posses the

p

Then, in a typical compound with

In this scenario, the tunnelling process through the array of

Expressing these two energies as proportional to

The calculations start with the time-dependent CH equation which
reproduces the CDW charge instability. It
is written in terms of the local phase separation order parameter
u(r_{i},t)=(p(r_{i},t)-p)/p
, where

p(r_{i},t)
is the local charge or hole density at a unit cell
position
i

de Mello and Sonier 2017
;
de Mello 2021
;
de Mello 2020a
;
de Mello 2020b
;
Santana and de Mello 2022
.
The simulation evolves to total phase separation, but
it is stopped when the charges reach a configuration
close to a given CO with the
\lambda_{\rm CO}
of a sample.
The CH equation is based on minimizing the
GL free energy:
de Mello and Kasal 2012
;
deMello and Sonier 2014
;
de Mello and Sonier 2017
:

f(u)={{\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon|\nabla u|^{2}+V_{\rm GL}(u,T)}},

(1)

where
\varepsilon
is the parameter that controls the charge modulations

\lambda_{\rm CO}
and
{V_{\rm GL}}(u,T)=-A^{2}u^{2}/2+B^{2}u^{4}/4+...]
is a
double-well potential. The charge
oscillations appear below the phase separation temperature

T_{\rm PS}
,
B=1
and
A^{2}=\alpha(p)[T_{\rm PS}-T]
.
In Fig.
1
we show a typical low temperature
V_{\rm GL}(u(r_{i},T))

simulation and its valleys which tend to bound the carriers in alternating
high and low densities CDW domains. We show in the inset (a), the
V_{\rm GL}(u(r_{i},T)
amplitude
along the
x
-direction
that diminishes when the temperature raises towards
T_{\rm PS}\geq T^{*}

where the system becomes homogeneous. The size of the barriers
between the two (high and low charge density) phases is proportional to
A^{4}/B^{2}
or

\langle V_{\rm GL}(p,T)\rangle=\alpha(p)^{2}[T^{*}-T]^{2}
, for
T\leq T^{*}
.

Thus, at low temperatures,
{V_{\rm GL}}
produces alternating high and low charge domains and such
inhomogeneous carrier distribution induces ions‘ fluctuations that may lead to
hole-hole attraction
de Mello 2020a
;
de Mello 2021
. Such indirect interaction
yields local superconducting pairs proportional to the barrier size

\langle V_{\rm GL}(p,T)\rangle

\xi_{\rm sc}\leq\lambda_{\rm CO}

On the other side, when the temperature increases,
V_{\rm GL}(p,T)
diminishes
as shown in inset (a) and the carriers become uniform which
leads to the temperature effect on
R_{\rm H}(p,T)
that we will detail

In conclusion, the pseudogap‘s role in cuprate superconductors as a
mediator of phase separation
and the two-particle and two-gap scenario
Hüfner et al. 2008
are identified by the

R_{\rm H}(p,T)
calculations.
The model based on phase-ordering kinetics
de Mello 2021
reveal the nature of the two types of
carriers in a typical compound of

hole doping: The
half-filled charges (
n=1

the phase separation free energy shown in Fig.
1
and
scaled by the temperature

. The other

compound
fill in the alternating CDW domains breaking the uniform charge
distribution and inducing ion-mediated pair interaction. The energy of
these pairs are scaled by the onset temperature
of superconducting pair formation

;
Kanigel et al. 2008

T_{\rm c}^{\rm max}(p)
,
that is just a few times

(see Table

).
For low doping the physical properties like superfluid density or quantum
oscillations

;
de Mello 2020b
are dominated by the thermal activated

holes
because
T_{\rm c}^{\rm max}(p)\ll T^{*}(p)

Increasing doping, above

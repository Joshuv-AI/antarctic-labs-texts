Abstract




Abstract
In this paper, we consider Hankel operators, with locally integrable symbols, densely defined on a family of Fock-type spaces whose weights are
C^{3}
-logarithmic growth functions with mild smoothness conditions. It is shown that a Hankel operator is bounded on such a Fock space if and only if its symbol function has bounded distance to analytic functions BDA which is initiated by Luecking(J. Funct. Anal. 110:247-271, 1992). We also characterize the compactness and Schatten class membership of Hankel operators. Besides, we give characterizations of the Schatten class membership of Toeplitz operators with positive measure symbols for the small exponent
0<p<1
. Our proofs depend strongly on the technique of Hömander’s
L^{2}
estimates for the
\overline{\partial}
operator and the decomposition theory of BDA spaces as well as integral estimates involving the reproducing kernel.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2010).
Primary 47B35, 30H20; Secondary 32A36, 32A37.

Keywords.
Fock-type space, Hankel operator, Schatten class.

Introduction

Let
\Psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a
C^{3}
-weight function such that

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}(x)>0,\quad\Psi^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0\textup{ and }\Psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0\textup{ for }x\in[0,\infty).

(1.1)

We refer to such a function as a logarithmic growth function. Suppose that there exists a real number
\eta<1/2
satisfying

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)=O(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}[\Phi^{\prime}(x)]^{1+\eta})\textup{ as }x\to\infty,

(1.2)

where
\Phi(x):=x\Psi^{\prime}(x)
. Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. When
n>1
, we also assume that

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime\prime}(x)=O(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}[\Psi^{\prime}(x)]^{1+\eta})\textup{ as }x\to\infty,

(1.3)

for some
\eta<1/2.

Let
\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional complex Euclidean space and
dV
be the usual Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Set
d\mu_{\Psi}(z)=e^{-\Psi(|z|^{2})}\,dV(z)
. The weighted Lebesgue space
L^{2}_{\Psi}
consists of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with the norm

\|f\|_{\Psi}:=\left\{\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|f(z)|^{2}\,d\mu_{\Psi}(z)\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty.

The inner product in
L^{2}_{\Psi}
is given by

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}d\mu_{\Psi}(z).

The Fock-type space
F^{2}_{\Psi}
consists of all holomorphic functions in
L^{2}_{\Psi}
with inherited norm and inner product. The space
F_{\Psi}^{2}
was first studied in
[
27
]
. Let
K(z,w)
be the reproducing kernel. For simplicity, we write
K_{w}(z):=K(z,w),
and denote by
k_{w}
the normalized kernel of
K_{w}
. Let
P
be the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}_{\Psi}
onto
F^{2}_{\Psi}
, then

Pg(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}g(w)K(z,w)\,d\mu_{\Psi}(w)\textup{ for }g\in L^{2}_{\Psi},\,z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Let
\Gamma=\textup{span}\{K_{z}:z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}
, then
\Gamma
is dense in
F^{2}_{\Psi}

[
30
]
. Consider the class of symbols

\mathcal{S}=\{f\textup{ measurable on }\mathbb{C}^{n}:fg\in L^{2}_{\Psi}\textup{ for }g\in\Gamma\}.

Given
f\in\mathcal{S}
, define the operator
H_{f}
with domain
\Gamma
as follows:

H_{f}(g)(z):=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}K(z,w)g(w)[f(z)-f(w)]\,d\mu_{\Psi}(w).

This densely defined operator from
F^{2}_{\Psi}
to
L^{2}_{\Psi}
is called the Hankel operator
H_{f}
with symbol
f
. Obviously, the symbol class
\mathcal{S}
contains all essentially bounded measurable functions.

We need to introduce some notations in order to state our results precisely. For every
z\neq 0
, let
P_{z}
denote the orthogonal projection in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
onto the complex line
\{\xi z:\xi\in\mathbb{C}\}
. We write
P_{0}
for the identity map.
For any
0<r<\infty
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the set
D(z,r)
is defined by

\displaystyle D(z,r):=\left\{w:|z-P_{z}(w)|\leq r\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}},|w-P_{z}w|\leq r\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\}.

Let
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be the collection of square locally Lebesgue integrable functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For
f\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we define the function
G_{r}(f)(z)
as follows:

G_{r}(f)(z)=\inf\left\{\left(\frac{1}{|D(z,r)|}\int_{D(z,r)}|f-h|^{2}dV\right)^{1/2}:h\in H(D(z,r))\right\},

where
H(D(z,r))
is the set of all holomorphic functions on
D(z,r)
and
|D(z,r)|
is the Euclidean volume of
D(z,r)
. For any
0<r<\infty
, the space
\textup{BDA}_{\Psi,r}
of bounded distance to analytic functions consists of all
f\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that

\|f\|_{\textup{BDA}_{\Psi,r}}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}G_{r}(f)(z)<+\infty.

The space
\textup{VDA}_{\Psi,r}
consists of all
f
in
\textup{BDA}_{\Psi,r}
such that
G_{r}(f)(z)\to 0
as
z\to\infty.

We now state our main results on boundedness and compactness of Hankel operators.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
f\in\mathcal{S}
. Then
H_{f}:F^{2}_{\Psi}\to L^{2}_{\Psi}
is bounded if and only if
f\in\textup{BDA}_{\Psi,r}
for some
0<r<\infty.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|H_{f}\|\simeq\|f\|_{\textup{BDA}_{\Psi,r}}.

(1.4)

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
f\in\mathcal{S}
. Then
H_{f}:F^{2}_{\Psi}\to L^{2}_{\Psi}
is compact if and only if
f\in\textup{VDA}_{\Psi,r}
for some
0<r<\infty.

The notion of bounded distance to analytic functions BDA was introduced by Luecking
[
20
]
in the context of the Bergman space. Hu and Virtanen
[

[

f\in\text{BDA}_{\Psi,r_{0}}
for some

for all

It is interesting to ask whether the space
\text{BDA}_{\Psi,r}
is independent of the choice of
r

[
15
]
for this problem. We do not address it in our present work.

In the case when

is holomorphic, Seip and Youssfi
[

characterized the boundedness and compactness of the Hankel operator

in terms of the mean oscillation of the symbol function
f
. More specifically, for any function

and

, the mean oscillation of
f
at

is defined by

(\textup{MO }f)(z):=\sqrt{\widetilde{|f|^{2}}(z)-|\widetilde{f}(z)|^{2}},

where
\widetilde{f}(z):=\langle fk_{z},k_{z}\rangle
is the Berezin transform of
f
. The space
BMO
is the set of all functions
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\widetilde{|f|^{2}}(z)
is finite for every
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and

\textup{MO }f
is bounded on
\mathbb{C}^{n}.
The space
VMO
consists of all
f\in\textup{BMO}
with the property
(\textup{MO }f)(z)\to 0
as
z\to\infty.

It was shown in
[
27
]
that, for
f
holomorphic,
H_{\overline{f}}
is bounded if and only if
f\in\textup{BMO}
;
H_{\overline{f}}
is compact if and only if
f\in\textup{VMO}.

Wang et al.
[
29
]
and Tu
[

considered more general symbol functions that are not necessary to be anti-holomorphic and extended some results of
[

MO_{r}(f)(z)=\left\{\frac{1}{|D(z,r)|}\int_{D(z,r)}|f(w)-\widehat{f}_{r}(z)|^{2}\,dV(w)\right\}^{1/2},

where

is a positive number and

\widehat{f}_{r}(z)=\frac{1}{|D(z,r)|}\int_{D(z,r)}f(w)\,dV(w).

We say that
f
in
\textup{BMO}_{r}
if
MO_{r}(f)
is bounded;
f
in
\textup{VMO}_{r}
if
MO_{r}(f)\to 0
as

. It was proved in
[

]
that

(A)

and

are both bounded
\Leftrightarrow f\in\text{BMO}\Leftrightarrow f\in\textup{BMO}_{r}
for all

;

(B)
H_{f}
and
H_{\overline{f}}
are both compact
\Leftrightarrow f\in\text{VMO}\Leftrightarrow f\in\textup{VMO}_{r}
for all
0<r<\infty
.

We will show that
f
in
BMO
if and only if

We also study the Schatten class membership of Toeplitz operators and Hankel operators. Given two separable Hilbert spaces
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
and
0<p<\infty
, we say that a compact linear operator
A:H_{1}\to H_{2}
belongs to the Schatten class
S_{p}:=S_{p}(H_{1},H_{2})
if the sequence of eigenvalues
\{s_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of
|A|:=(A^{*}A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
satisfies
\|A\|_{S_{p}}^{p}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}^{p}<\infty.

This defines a norm when
1\leq p<\infty
and a quasi-norm otherwise.
Given a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we define the Toeplitz operator
T_{\mu}
with the symbol
\mu
as

T_{\mu}g(z):=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}K(z,w)g(w)e^{-\Psi(|w|^{2})}\,d\mu(w)\textup{ for }g\in F^{2}_{\Psi},z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

In particular, when
d\mu(z)=f(z)\,dV(z)
and
f
is a positive measurable function, the induced Toeplitz operator is denoted by
T_{f}.

For a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we define the Berezin transform of
\mu
as

\widetilde{\mu}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|k_{z}(w)|e^{-\Psi(|w|^{2})}\,d\mu(w)\textup{ for }z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

For all
0<r<\infty
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the integral mean of
\mu
over
D(z,r)
is given by
\widehat{\mu}_{r}(z):=\mu(D(z,r))/|D(z,r)|.

Set
d\nu_{\Psi}(z)=K(z,z)\,d\mu_{\Psi}(z).
The following theorem gives characterizations of the membership in the Schatten classes of Toeplitz operators with positive measure symbols.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
0<p<\infty
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(A)
The Toeplitz operator
T_{\mu}
is in
S_{p}
.

(B)
The function
\widetilde{\mu}(z)
is in
L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\nu_{\Psi})
.

(C)
The function
\widehat{\mu}_{r}(z)
is in
L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\nu_{\Psi})
for any
0<r<\infty
.

(D)
The sequence
\{\widehat{\mu}_{r}(z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is in
l^{p}
for any
\Psi
-lattice
\left\{z_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|T_{\mu}\|_{S_{p}}\simeq\|\widetilde{\mu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\nu_{\Psi})}\simeq\|\widehat{\mu}_{r}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\nu_{\Psi})}\simeq\|\{\widehat{\mu}_{r}(z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\|_{l^{p}}.

(1.5)

The equivalence between (A) and (D) was proved in
[
27
]
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Our contribution is on the small exponent
0<p<1
. The characterizations of Schatten class Hankel operators are the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. Suppose
f\in\mathcal{S}
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(A)
The Hankel operator
H_{f}
is in
S_{p}
.

(B)

G_{r}(f)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\nu_{\Psi})
for some
0<r<\infty.

(C)

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\|H_{f}k_{z}\|_{\Psi}^{p}\,d\nu_{\Psi}<\infty
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|H_{f}\|_{S_{p}}\simeq\|G_{r}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\nu_{\Psi})}\simeq\left\{\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\|H_{f}k_{z}\|_{\Psi}^{p}\,d\nu_{\Psi}(z)\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(1.6)

As a consequence of the last theorem, the following corollary extends the result in
[

]

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. Suppose
f\in\mathcal{S}
. Then the Hankel operators
H_{f}
and
H_{\overline{f}}
are both in
S_{p}
if and only if
MO_{r}(f)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\nu_{\Psi})
for some

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|H_{f}\|_{S_{p}}+\|H_{\overline{f}}\|_{S_{p}}\simeq\|MO_{r}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\nu_{\Psi})}.

(1.7)

The key ingredients in the proof of our main results are
\bar{\partial}
-techniques, Carleson measure and the decomposition theory of functions in
\textup{BDA}_{\Psi,r}
. As the canonical solution to
\bar{\partial}v=g\bar{\partial}f
,
H_{f}g
is naturally connected with the
\bar{\partial}
-theory. The decomposition theory allows us to decompose

into two parts. One part is associated with Carleson measures while we deal with the other part by the technique of
Hömander’s
L^{2}
estimates for the
\overline{\partial}
operator. We mention that
[
27
]
also applies Hömander’s
L^{2}
theory when

is holomorphic. However, the estimate in
[
27
]
cannot be straightforward extended to non-holomorphic functions. New estimates and methods must be developed(see Lemma
3.10
).

There are two main difficulties we meet while studying Hankel operators on the Fock-type space, one for which is the non-isotropic behavior of the Bergman metric induced by
F^{2}_{\Psi}
and the other is the lack of decay estimate on
K(z,w)
with the Bergman distance
\varrho(z,w)
. In these references
[

,
16
,
18
,
19
,
32
]
, the Bergman balls are equivalent to Euclidean balls which makes it easier to do analysis along the complex tangential directions. On the classical Fock space
F^{2}
, we have an identity that

|K(z,w)|e^{-|z|^{2}}e^{-|w|^{2}}=e^{\frac{1}{2}|z-w|^{2}}\ \ \textrm{for }\ z,w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}.

On
F^{2}_{\phi}
with
\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
satisfying
i\partial\overline{\partial}\phi\simeq i\partial\overline{\partial}|z|^{2}
, we have

|K(z,w)|e^{-\phi(z)}e^{-\phi(w)}\leq Ce^{-\theta|z-w|}\ \ \textrm{for }\ z,w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n},

where
C
and

are two positive constants. Similar estimate also holds on those Fock spaces studied in
[
4
,
10
,
14
]
. This estimate is crucial to the study on Fock spaces, but unfortunately there is no analogy we know on
F^{2}_{\Psi}
. This brings us to the new approach of the present work. More precisely, we use integral estimates for the reproducing kernel established in Section
4

As an application, we obtain a characterization of the simultaneous membership in

Preliminaries

Set
\Lambda_{\Psi}(z):=\log K(z,z)
and

\beta^{2}(z,\xi):=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}\Lambda_{\Psi}(z)}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}\xi_{j}\overline{\xi_{k}},

for arbitrary vectors
z=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The associated function
\varrho(z,w)
, the Bergman distance, is given by

\varrho(z,w):=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\beta(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt,

where the infimum is taken over all piecewise
C^{1}
-smooth curves
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\gamma(0)=z
and
\gamma(1)=w
. For any
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
r>0
, denote by

B(z,r):=\{w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\varrho(z,w)<r\}

the Bergman ball centered at
z
with radius
r
. Let
|B(z,r)|
be the Euclidean volume of
B(z,r)
.

Given some
r>0
, we say that a sequence of distinct points
\{z_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a
\Psi
-lattice if the balls
\{B(z_{k},r)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
cover
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\{B(z_{k},r/2)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are pair-wisely disjoint. We refer to
r
as the covering radius. From
[
27
, Lemma 7.4]
, we know that given
r>0
there exists a
\Psi
-lattice. Moreover, given a
\Psi
-lattice
\{z_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
there exists an integer
N
such that each
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
belongs to at most
N
balls of
B(z_{k},2r)
. More specifically,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\chi_{B(z_{k},2r)}(z)\leq N\textup{ for }z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

(2.1)

In our analysis, it is convenient to use the ball
D(z,r)
. From
[
27
, Lemma 7.2]
, we know that for every positive number

, there exist two positive numbers

and

such that for every

,

\displaystyle D(z,r_{1})\subseteq B(z,r)\subseteq D(z,r_{2}).

(2.2)

It follows that the Euclidean volume of

can be estimated as

\displaystyle|B(z,r)|\simeq[\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-1}[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-(n-1)}.

(2.3)

Moreover, given

there exists a sequence

and a positive constant

such that

.

(2.4)

for every

3
Boundedness and Compactness of Hankel Operators

To establish the decomposition theory of the space
\textup{BDA}_{\Psi,r}
, we need to construct a partition of unity subordinated to a certain lattice. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0<r\leq 1/8
. For every
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
w\in D(z,r)
, we have

\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})\simeq\Psi^{\prime}(|w|^{2})\textup{ and }\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})\simeq\Phi^{\prime}(|w|^{2}).

Proof.

It follows immediately from the definition of
\Phi
that
\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})\leq\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})
. Hence, for
w\in D(z,r)
, we have

|z-w|\leq|z-P_{z}w|+|w-P_{z}w|\leq 2r[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-1/2}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left||z|^{2}-|w|^{2}\right|\leq|z-w|(|z|+|w|)\leq|z-w|(|z|+|z-w|+|z|)=|z-w|^{2}+2|z|\cdot|z-w|.

It follows that for
|z|\geq[\Psi(0)]^{1/2}
and
r\leq 1/8
,

\left||z|^{2}-|w|^{2}\right|\leq\left(4r^{2}|z|^{-1}[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-1/2}+4r\right)|z|[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-1/2}\leq|z|[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-1/2}.

By applying
[
27
, Lemma 7.1]
with
\alpha=1/2
, we know that

\Psi^{\prime}(|w|^{2})=(1+o(1))\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})\textup{ as }|z|\to\infty.

Therefore, we have for every
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
w\in D(z,r)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}(|w|^{2})\simeq\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2}).

(3.1)

From (
2.2
), we know that there is a positive constant
r_{1}
such that
D(z,r)\subseteq B(z,r_{1}).

If
w\in D(z,r)
, then
B(w,r_{1})\subseteq B(z,2r_{1})
and
B(z,r_{1})\subseteq B(w,2r_{1})
.
This, together with (
2.3
), implies that

[\Phi^{\prime}(|w|^{2})]^{-1}[\Psi^{\prime}(|w|^{2})]^{1-n}\lesssim[\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-1}[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{1-n},

and

[\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-1}[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{1-n}\lesssim[\Phi^{\prime}(|w|^{2})]^{-1}[\Psi^{\prime}(|w|^{2})]^{1-n}.

Combining the last two inequalities with (
3.1
), we obtain
\Phi^{\prime}(|w|^{2})\simeq\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})

for every
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
w\in D(z,r)
. The proof is completed.
∎

Recall that for
v\in C^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, the
\overline{\partial}
operator is defined as

\overline{\partial}v=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}d\overline{z}_{j},

and the
\overline{\partial}
operator acting on a (0,1) form
u=\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{j}d\overline{z_{j}}
is defined by the formula

\overline{\partial}u=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left(\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial\overline{z}_{k}}-\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}\right)d\overline{z}_{j}\land d\overline{z}_{k}.

Set

|u|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|u_{j}|^{2}\textup{ and }|\overline{\partial}u|^{2}=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}-\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial\overline{z}_{k}}\right|^{2}.

It is easy to see that for
v\in C^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
\overline{\partial}v
is
\overline{\partial}
-closed, i.e.,
\overline{\partial}^{2}v=0.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r>0
and

. Then there exists a real-valued function
\gamma_{z}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, satisfying the following properties.

(A)

0\leq\gamma_{z}\leq 1
,
\gamma_{z}|_{D(z,r/2)}\equiv 1
,
\textup{supp}\,\gamma_{z}\subseteq D(z,r)

(B)

|\nabla\gamma_{z}(w)|\leq C_{1}[\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{\frac{1}{2}}/r
.

(C)

\left|\bar{\partial}\gamma_{z}(w)\land\overline{\partial}|w|\right|\leq C_{2}[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{\frac{1}{2}}/r
, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are absolute constants.

Proof.

Choose a function

and
-3\leq T^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
. Given

\gamma_{z}
as

\gamma_{z}(w):=T\left(\frac{|P_{z}w-z|}{r[\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\cdot T\left(\frac{|w-P_{z}w|}{r[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

It is easy to see that
0\leq\gamma_{z}(w)\leq 1
,
\gamma_{z}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
\gamma_{z}|_{D(z,r/2)}\equiv 1
and
\textup{supp}\,\gamma_{z}\subseteq D(z,r)
. Thus we have proved (A). To prove (B) and (C), we begin with considering the case
z=(z_{1},0,\cdots,0)

\gamma_{z}
is reduced to

\gamma_{z}(w)=T\left(\frac{|w_{1}-z_{1}|}{r[\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\cdot T\left(\frac{|(0,w_{2},\cdots,w_{n})|}{r[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\gamma_{z}}{\partial w_{1}}(w)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|T^{\prime}\left(\frac{|w_{1}-z_{1}|}{r[\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{2r[\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right|\cdot T\left(\frac{|(0,w_{2},\cdots,w_{n})|}{r[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\leq\frac{3\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2r},

and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\gamma_{z}}{\partial w_{j}}(w)\right|

\displaystyle=T\left(\frac{|w_{1}-z_{1}|}{r[\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\cdot\left|T^{\prime}\left(\frac{|(0,w_{2},\cdots,w_{n})|}{r[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\cdot\frac{\overline{w}_{j}}{2r|(0,w_{2},\cdots,w_{n})|[\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2r}\leq\frac{3\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2r},

for
j\neq 1
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\gamma_{z}}{\partial\overline{w}_{1}}(w)\right|\leq\frac{3\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2r},\textup{ and }\left|\frac{\partial\gamma_{z}}{\partial\overline{w}_{j}}(w)\right|\leq\frac{3\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2r}\leq\frac{3\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2r}\textup{ for }j\neq 1.

(3.2)

Thus, (B) holds with
z=(z_{1},0,\cdots,0)
. Notice that

\overline{\partial}|w|=\frac{1}{2|w|}\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}d\overline{w}_{j}.

We have

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\gamma_{z}(w)\land\overline{\partial}|w|=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\frac{1}{2|w|}\left(\frac{\partial\gamma_{z}}{\partial\overline{w}_{j}}(w)w_{k}-\frac{\partial\gamma_{z}}{\partial\overline{w}_{k}}(w)w_{j}\right)d\overline{w}_{j}\land d\overline{w}_{k}.

\displaystyle\left|\overline{\partial}\gamma_{z}(w)\land\overline{\partial}|w|\right|^{2}=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\frac{1}{4|w|^{2}}\left|\frac{\partial\gamma_{z}}{\partial\overline{w}_{j}}(w)w_{k}-\frac{\partial\gamma_{z}}{\partial\overline{w}_{k}}(w)w_{j}\right|^{2}.

(3.3)

By the fact that

Obeserve that, if

Let

be a plurisubharmonic function. For a (0,1) form

plurisubharmonic, if

\overline{\partial}
-closed (0,1) form with

]

, with

. The precise formulation can be found in
[
11
]
. For

Lemma 3.9
.

Let

be a

be a subspace of

. If there exist

, where

is the projection from
\mathbb{C}^{n}
onto

, then

is inversible and

\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

Define a linear tansformation
\mathscr{A}
on

by
\mathscr{A}\xi=\xi A\textup{ for }\xi=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

is self-adjoint. Define another linear tansformation

on

[

Therefore,
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{B}

This, together with the fact

\displaystyle\|H_{f_{1}}g\|^{2}_{\Psi}

\displaystyle=\|S(g\overline{\partial}f_{1})\|^{2}_{\Psi}-\|H_{f_{1}}g-S(g\overline{\partial}f_{1})\|^{2}_{\Psi}\leq\|S(g\overline{\partial}f_{1})\|^{2}_{\Psi}.

(3.35)

Combining this with (
3.34

\displaystyle\|H_{f_{1}}g\|^{2}_{\Psi}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left(\frac{|g(z)\overline{\partial}f_{1}(z)|^{2}}{\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})}+\frac{\left|g(z)\overline{\partial}f_{1}(z)\land\overline{\partial}|z|\right|^{2}}{\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})}\right)e^{-\Psi(|z|^{2})}dV(z)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left\{\frac{|\overline{\partial}f_{1}(z)|^{2}}{\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})}+\frac{\left|\overline{\partial}f_{1}(z)\land\overline{\partial}|z|\right|^{2}}{\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})}\right\}\|g\|_{\Psi}^{2}.

This shows that
H_{f_{1}}
is bounded. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|H_{f_{1}}\|\leq C\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left\{\frac{|\overline{\partial}f_{1}(z)|^{2}}{\Phi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})}+\frac{\left|\overline{\partial}f_{1}(z)\land\overline{\partial}|z|\right|^{2}}{\Psi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})}\right\}.

(3.36)

Estimate (
1.4
) comes from (
3.29
), (
3.31
), (
3.32
), (
3.33
) and (
3.36
). The proof is completed.

4
Some Integral Estimates

This section is devoted to give integral
type estimates about the reproducing kernels. We will deal with integrals of the following type frequently.

\displaystyle I_{p}(r,a,s,b)=\int_{a}^{\infty}y^{s}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+y)^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2}+y)}\,dy,

(4.1)

where
0\leq r,a<\infty
,
-1<s<\infty
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<p<\infty
. For simplicity, we set

C(r,a,s)=[a\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})]^{s}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})a}+\int_{\frac{p}{2}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})a}^{\infty}u^{s}e^{-u}\,du.

In this paper, we are interested in the following two choices:(1)
a=0
and (2)
a\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})\to\infty.
Set

C_{\Sigma}(r,a,s):=C(r,a,0)+C(r,a,1)+C(r,a,s).

Since
y^{s^{\prime}}\leq 1+y+y^{s}
for all
y\geq 0
and
0\leq s^{\prime}\leq s
, we have

\sup\{C(r,a,s^{\prime}):0\leq s^{\prime}\leq s\}\leq C_{\Sigma}(r,a,s).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0\leq r,a<\infty
,
b\leq 0
. For fixed
s\geq-1
and
0<p<\infty
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}y^{s}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+y)^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2}+y)}\,dy\lesssim C(r,a,s)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b-s-1}.

(4.2)

Proof.

Since
\Psi^{\prime}
is monotone increasing, we have

\displaystyle\Psi(r^{2}+y)-\Psi(r^{2})\geq\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})y\textup{ for }y\geq 0,

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+y)^{b}\leq\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b}\textup{ for }y\geq 0.

(4.4)

From (
4.3
), we see that

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}y^{s}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2}+y)}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\int_{a}^{\infty}y^{s}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})y}\,dy

\displaystyle\simeq e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{-s-1}\int_{\frac{p}{2}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})a}^{\infty}u^{s}e^{-u}\,du

\displaystyle\leq C(r,a,s)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{-s-1}.

(4.5)

This and (
4.4
) yield (
4.2
). The proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0\leq a,s<\infty
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<p<\infty.
Then,

\displaystyle a^{s}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+a)^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2}+a)}\leq[a\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})]^{s}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})a}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b-s},

(4.6)

as
r\to\infty
.

Proof.

Set

f_{r}(x)=\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+x)^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2}+x)}\textup{ for }x\geq 0.

From (
1.3
), we know that there is a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime\prime}(r^{2}+x)\leq C(r^{2}+x)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+x)^{1+\eta}\leq Cr^{-1}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+x)^{1+\eta},\textup{ as }r\to\infty.

(4.7)

Therefore,

\displaystyle f_{r}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=b\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+x)^{b-1}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(r^{2}+x)e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2}+x)}-\frac{p}{4}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+x)^{b+1}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2}+x)}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{-1}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+x)^{b+\eta}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2}+x)}-\frac{p}{4}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+x)^{b+1}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2}+x)}

\displaystyle=\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+x)^{b+\eta}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2}+x)}\left[\frac{C}{r}-\frac{p}{4}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+x)^{1-\eta}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+x)^{b+\eta}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2}+x)}\left[\frac{C}{r}-\frac{p}{4}\Psi^{\prime}(0)^{1-\eta}\right]

\displaystyle<0,

(4.8)

as
r\to\infty
. It follows that
f_{r}(x)
is monotone decreasing. Thus,
f_{r}(a)\leq f_{r}(0)
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+a)^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2}+a)}}{\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2})}}\leq 1\textup{ as }r\to\infty.

(4.9)

By this and (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle a^{s}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+a)^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2}+a)}

\displaystyle\leq\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+a)^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2}+a)}a^{s}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})a}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+a)^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2}+a)}}{\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi(r^{2})}}[a\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})]^{s}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})a}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b-s}

\displaystyle\leq[a\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})]^{s}e^{-\frac{p}{4}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})a}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b-s},

as
r\to\infty
. The proof is finished.
∎

For

and

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0\leq r<\infty.
For fixed
s\geq 0
,

and
0<p<\infty
, there exists

\displaystyle I_{p}(r,a,s,b)\leq CC_{\Sigma}(r,a,s)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b-s-1},

(4.10)

as
r\to\infty

By Lemma
4.1

4.10
) holds for
b\leq 0
. We only need to deal with

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}y^{s}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+y)^{b}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2}+y)}\,dy

\displaystyle\simeq-\int_{a}^{\infty}y^{s}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+y)^{b-1}\,de^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2}+y)}

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{s}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+a)^{b-1}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2}+a)}+s\int_{a}^{\infty}y^{s-1}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+y)^{b-1}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2}+y)}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{a}^{\infty}y^{s}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2}+y)^{b-2}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(r^{2}+y)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2}+y)}\,dy.

(4.11)

By condition (
1.3
), we see that there is a constant

as

We prove (
4.10
) for

first. From (
4
),(
4.13
) and Lemma

, we deduce that

\displaystyle I_{p}(r,a,s,b)\lesssim C(r,a,s)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b-s-1}+I_{p}(r,a,s-1,b-1)+I_{p}(r,a,s,b+\eta-1)\textup{ for }s>0,

(4.14)

as

and

\displaystyle I_{p}(r,a,0,b)

\displaystyle\lesssim C(r,a,0)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b-1}+I_{p}(r,a,0,b+\eta-1)

\displaystyle\lesssim C(r,a,0)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b-1}+I_{p}(r,a,0,b+j(\eta-1)),

as
r\to\infty
where

This and Lemma

imply that

\displaystyle I_{p}(r,a,0,b)

(4.16)

By (
4.14
) and (
4.16
), we see that

\displaystyle\lesssim C(r,a,1)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b-2}+I_{p}(r,a,0,b-1)+I_{p}(r,a,1,b+\eta-1)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\Sigma}(r,a,s)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\Psi(r^{2})}\Psi^{\prime}(r^{2})^{b-2}+I_{p}(r,a,1,b+\eta-1)

as

as

Lemma 4.5

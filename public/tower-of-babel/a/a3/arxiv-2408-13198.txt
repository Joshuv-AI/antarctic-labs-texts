Geometric Actions for Lower-Spin Gravity
Stéphane Detournay, and Quentin Vandermiers

Abstract
We perform the Hamiltonian reduction of Lower Spin Gravity, the simplest bulk dual for a Warped Conformal Field Theory (WCFT), consisting in an S ​ L ​ ( 2 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) SL(2)\times U(1) Chern-Simons model. We identify the boundary action as the geometric action on coadjoint orbits of the Warped Virasoro group. We use this reduced action to compute one-loop contributions to the torus partition function and compare them to the Warped Virasoro characters.



1
Introduction and Summary

Warped Conformal Field Theories (WCFTs) are a class of two-dimensional field theories characterized by chiral scaling symmetries, acting only on right-movers, differing from traditional 2d CFTs
Hofman:2011zj
;
Detournay:2012pc
. These theories are governed by a Virasoro algebra coupled with a U(1) current algebra, leading to modular covariance of the partition function and providing powerful constraints akin to those in conformal field theories. The studies of free model partition functions
Castro:2015uaa
, correlators
Song:2017czq
, the bootstrap program
Apolo:2018eky
, entanglement entropy
Castro:2015csg
;
Apolo:2020qjm
, the complex SYK model
Chaturvedi:2018uov
, chaos
Apolo:2018oqv
, anomalies
Jensen:2017tnb
and complexity
Bhattacharyya:2022ren
to name a few have provided deeper insights into their structure and potential physical implications.

One significant application of WCFTs lies in holography, particularly in describing the near-horizon geometries of extremal black holes
Aggarwal:2019iay
, or so-called Warped AdS
3
(WAdS
3
) black holes
qiao2008edgestatesdestroyeddisordered
whose isometries match the global
SL(2,R)\times U(1)
subgroup of WCFTs. In the latter case, the asymptotic symmetries are seen to match the full local WCFT symmetries
Masina:2008zv
;
Compere:2009zj
, the same way the asymptotic symmetries of AdS
3
spaces match those of 2d CFTs
Brown:1986nw
(a similar statement holds for 3d flat space, whose asymptotic symmetries match those of a 2d Carrollian CFT
Barnich:2006av
). WCFTs are noted for their non-local Lorentz-violating nature, expanding the scope of holography beyond asymptotically AdS spacetimes. These theories’ unique symmetries facilitate the derivation of the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of black holes, linking quantum gravity insights with field theory descriptions, in the same spirit as
Strominger:1997eq
for BTZ black holes
Banados:1992wn
;
Banados:1992gq
.

Bulk theories potentially dual to WCFTs typically involve higher curvature terms (such as Topologically Massive Gravity
DESER2000409
) or matter fields
Banados:2005da
;
Compere:2007in
;
Israel:2004vv
, and hence degrees of freedom. One notable exception consists in an
SL(2,R)\times U(1)
Chern-Simons theory, dubbed
L
ower-Spin Gravity introduced in
Hofman:2014loa
. It was argued that this model was the minimal setup for a holographic description of WCFTs, in analogy with the gauge theory formulations of pure gravity with a negative or without cosmological constant
Witten:1988hc
based on Chern-Simons models with
SO(2,2)
or
ISO(2,1)
gauge groups respectively. The exploration of the boundary properties of these models started in the AdS
3
case with Coussaert, Henneaux, and van Driel who performed a Hamiltonian reduction of the corresponding action. They found that the action can be presented as two chiral Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) models, that combine into a single non-chiral WZW. Under the Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions, this action reduces further to the Liouville action on the asymptotic boundary.
To incorporate BTZ black holes and their descendants, more complex topologies than the filled cylinder of global AdS
3
must be considered. This involves using Chern-Simons theory on a disk with a puncture, where the holonomy measures the black hole mass and angular momentum. Repeating the Hamiltonian reduction in this context results in two chiral bosons instead of a combined WZW model, with the zero modes of the bosons related to the bulk holonomy
(
Henneaux:2019sjx
, p. 56–59)
. An equivalent formulation of this action – named Alekseev-Shatashvili (AS) or geometric action – arises from the Kirillov-Konstant coadjoint orbit method for the Virasoro group
(
Henneaux:2019sjx
, p. 60)
. The latter could be obtained as a Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction of the SL(2,R) WZW model, which is the CFT counterpart of picking Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions. The connection between pure 3d gravity and coadjoint orbits of the Virasoro group can also be understood as follows. The Bañados geometries
(
Henneaux:2019sjx
, p. 69)
– the classical saddles of pure 3d gravity with Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions in Fefferman-Graham gauge – are parametrized by two functions that are the dual stress-tensor expectation values semi-classically. Since the stress-tensor transforms in the coadjoint representation of the Virasoro group
(
Henneaux:2019sjx
, p. 61)
, the Ba˜nados geometries are intrinsically related to the coadjoint orbits of the Virasoro group
(
Henneaux:2019sjx
, p. 57, 70–72)
with the orbit representative corresponding to the global charges of the bulk Banados geometry. Similar ideas can be developed in flat spacetimes, with the Virasoro group being replaced by BMS
3

Merbis:2019wgk
. Under Barnich and Compère’s boundary conditions, the Chern-Simons reduces to a two-dimensional boundary theory akin to a flat space version of Liouville theory for vanishing bulk holonomies. This boundary theory is found to coïncide with the geometric action on the coadjoint orbit of the BMS
3
group, where orbit representatives correspond to gravitational saddle parameters like mass and angular momentum. The global Minkowski vacuum corresponds to the first exceptional BMS
3
orbit, while flat space cosmologies
Cornalba:2002fi
;
Cornalba:2002nv
– the flat counterparts of the BTZ black holes – correspond to generic massive orbits, with orbit representatives corresponding to gravitational charges’ zero modes.

The goal of this note will be to study the Hamiltonian reduction of Lower-Spin Gravity under the boundary conditions proposed in
Azeyanagi:2018har
. They were shown the include the ”warped counterpart” of the Banados geometries, parametrized by two functions, whose zero modes can be identified with WAdS
3
black holes. We will revisit the theory of coadjoint orbits of the Warped Virasoro group and relate the orbit representatives to the Chern-Simons charges of the bulk solutions. We will derive the geometric action on the coadjoint orbits of the warped Virasoro group with generic (constant) orbit representatives, which generalizes the warped Schwarzian action (4.5) of
Afshar:2019tvp
to a generic orbit. Next, we will perform the reduction of Chern-Simons Lower-Spin Gravity with constant charges and observe it matches with the geometric action. As a next step, we compute the one-loop torus partition function of Warped Virasoro descendants around the global vacuum and the Chern-Simons counterparts of WAdS
3
black holes. A similar computation was done for AdS
3

Cotler:2018zff
and Minkowski
Merbis:2019wgk
and the results shown to match with the appropriate characters of the corresponding symmetry groups. In this case, the matching is not immediate, and we trace this to the analytic continuation procedure (WCFTs being intrinsically Lorentzian) and to an ambiguity in the definition of WCFT partition functions (”canonical” vs. ”quadratic” ensemble). Using the language of coadjoint orbits and geometric actions would in principle allow one to very efficiently compute a variety of interesting physical quantities, such as the Warped conformal blocks, along the lines of
Cotler:2018zff
;
Merbis:2019wgk
. We leave this as an interesting direction to pursue for future work, alongside a thorough analysis of Lower-Spin Gravity in quadratic ensemble.

2
Lower Spin Gravity

Lower Spin Gravity is one of the simplest warped gravity dual, alongside with AdS
3
spacetimes with CSS boundary conditions
Compere:2013bya
. It consists in an
SL(2,\mathbb{R})\times U(1)
Chern-Simons action

S=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{{\cal M}}\left<A\wedge{\rm d}A+\frac{2}{3}A\wedge A\wedge A\right>+\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\int_{{\cal M}}\left<C\wedge{\rm d}C\right>\,,

(1)

where
k
is a continuous parameter determining the central charge of the Virasoro algebra, and
\kappa
a discrete parameter describing the sign of the level of the
U(1)
Kac-Moody algebra. It was first constructed in
Hofman:2014loa
from the coupling of WCFTs to a background geometry.

A consistent set of boundary conditions on the connections
A
and
C
for this model was proposed in
Azeyanagi:2018har
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{\varphi}=L_{1}-\mathfrak{L}\,L_{-1}\quad&,\quad A_{t}=\mu\,A_{\varphi}-\mu^{\prime}\,L_{0}+\frac{\mu^{\prime\prime}}{2}L_{-1}\,,\\
C_{\varphi}=\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}\quad&,\quad C_{t}=\mu\,C_{\varphi}+\nu\,,\end{split}

(2)

where

\mathfrak{L}=\frac{2\pi}{k}\left({\cal L}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}^{2}\right)\,,

(3)

L_{i}
are
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
generators,
(t,\varphi)
the boundary coordinates, and the prime denotes a derivative with respect to
\varphi
.
The functions
{\cal L}
and
{\cal P}
appearing in the boundary conditions depend on
t
and
\varphi
and we can interpret them as functions characterizing the physical state. Hence,
\mu
and
\nu
, also functions of
t
and
\varphi
, can be viewed as chemical potentials and are therefore fixed, i.e.
\delta\mu=0=\delta\nu
.
Those boundary conditions were shown to be preserved by warped conformal transformations represented by the following charge algebra:

\displaystyle\begin{split}[L_{n},L_{m}]&=(n-m)\,L_{n+m}+\frac{c}{12}\,n^{3}\,\delta_{n+m}\ ,\\
[L_{n},P_{m}]&=-m\,P_{n+m}\,,\\
[P_{n},P_{m}]&=\kappa\,n\,\delta_{n+m}\,,\end{split}

(4)

with
c=6k
. We recognize the Virasoro-Kac-Moody algebra of a WCFT. One can perform a metric interpretation of the connection and show that the boundary conditions contain the different warped AdS
3
spacetimes
Hofman:2014loa
, as well as the warped black hole in the canonical ensemble
Azeyanagi:2018har
.

3
Coadjoint representation

The warped conformal group is the centrally extended semi-direct product group of diffeomorphisms of the circle
{\rm Diff}^{+}(S^{1})
with
C^{\infty}(S^{1})
. In Appendix A of
Afshar:2015wjm
, the coadjoint representations of this group were studied, which we will summarize here for convenience and to fix conventions.

We take
\varphi
to be a coordinate along the circle and denote elements of
{\rm Diff}^{+}(S^{1})
by
f(\varphi)
, satisfying
f^{\prime}(\varphi)>0
and
f(\varphi+2\pi)=f(\varphi)+2\pi
, and elements of
C^{\infty}(S^{1})
by
p(\varphi)
. The group multiplication of the semi-direct product group
G={\rm Diff}^{+}(S^{1})\ltimes C^{\infty}(S^{1})
is

(f_{1},p_{1})\cdot(f_{2},p_{2})=(f_{1}\circ f_{2},p_{1}+\sigma_{f_{1}}p_{2})\,,\qquad\text{with}\;\sigma_{f}p=p\circ f^{-1}\,

(5)

where
\circ
denotes functional composition,
f\circ\varphi=f(\varphi)
.

Since the second cohomology space of
G
is three-dimensional, the warped conformal group has three central extensions. They are defined by the three 2-cocycles in
G
. Denoting elements of the centrally extended group
\hat{G}=G\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
by
(f,p;\lambda,\mu,\nu)
, the group operation now reads:

\displaystyle(f_{1},p_{1};\lambda_{1},\mu_{1},\nu_{1})\cdot(f_{2},p_{2};\lambda_{2},\mu_{2},\nu_{2})=

\displaystyle\,\big(f_{1}\circ f_{2},p_{1}+\sigma_{f_{1}}p_{2};\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+B(f_{1},f_{2}),

\displaystyle\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+C(f_{1},p_{2}),\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+D(p_{1},\sigma_{f_{1}}p_{2})\big)\,.

(6)

Here
B,C
and
D
are real-valued 2-cocycles in the group. They are given explicitly by

\displaystyle\begin{split}B(f_{1},f_{2})&=-\frac{1}{48\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\log(\partial_{\varphi}f_{1}\circ f_{2})\partial_{\varphi}\log\partial_{\varphi}f_{2}\,,\\
C(f_{1},p_{2})&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;p_{2}\partial_{\varphi}\log(\partial_{\varphi}f_{1})\,,\\
D(f_{1},\sigma_{f_{1}}p_{2})&=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;p_{1}\partial_{\varphi}\sigma_{f_{1}}p_{2}\,.\end{split}

(7)

We recognize for the 2-cocycle
B(f_{1},f_{2})
, the Bott-Thurston cocycle of the Virasoro group
Unterberger:2012
;
Guieu:2007
.

The adjoint action of a group element
g_{1}=(f_{1},p_{1};\lambda_{1},\mu_{1},\nu_{1})
on an element of the algebra
(X_{2},p_{2};\lambda_{2},\mu_{2},\nu_{2})
is computed as

{\rm Ad}_{g_{1}}(X_{2},p_{2};\lambda_{2},\mu_{2},\nu_{2})=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\epsilon}\left.g_{1}\cdot(e^{\epsilon X_{2}},\epsilon p_{2},\epsilon\lambda_{2},\epsilon\mu_{2},\epsilon\nu_{2})\cdot g_{1}^{-1}\right|_{\epsilon=0}\,,

(8)

which explicitly evaluates to

\displaystyle{\rm Ad}_{(f,p_{1})}(X,p_{2};\lambda,\mu,\nu)=

\displaystyle\;\Big({\rm Ad}_{f}X,\sigma_{f}p_{2}+\Sigma_{{\rm Ad}_{f}X}p_{1};\lambda-\frac{1}{24\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;X(\varphi)\{f;\varphi\},

\displaystyle\mu-\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\left(p_{2}\partial_{\varphi}\log f^{\prime}-X(\varphi)f^{\prime}{}^{2}\partial_{f}^{2}(p_{1}\circ f)\right),

\displaystyle\nu-\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\left(p_{1}\partial_{\varphi}\sigma_{f}p_{2}-\frac{1}{2}X(\varphi)[\partial_{\varphi}(p_{1}\circ f)]^{2}\right)\Big)\,.

(9)

Here, the notation
{\rm Ad}_{f}X
denotes the adjoint action of the Virasoro group on the components of a vector field
X(\varphi)\partial_{\varphi}
, i.e. the transformation of a vector field on the circle under diffeomorphisms:
{\rm Ad}_{f}X=f^{\prime}(f^{-1})X(f^{-1})
.

The symbol
\Sigma_{X}
denotes the infinitesimal version of
\sigma_{f}
, and hence
\Sigma_{X}p_{1}=X(\varphi)p_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi)
. Finally,
\{f;\varphi\}
denotes the Schwarzian derivative

\{f;x\}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\,.

(10)

The adjoint representation of the algebra on itself gives the commutators

\displaystyle-{\rm ad}_{(X_{1},p_{1})}\left(X_{2},p_{2};\lambda_{2},\mu_{2},\nu_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=[(X_{1},p_{1};\lambda_{1},\mu_{1},\nu_{1}),(X_{2},p_{2};\lambda_{2},\mu_{2},\nu_{2})]

(11)

\displaystyle=\Big(X_{1}X_{2}^{\prime}-X_{1}^{\prime}X_{2},X_{1}p_{2}^{\prime}-p_{1}^{\prime}X_{2};\frac{1}{24\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;X_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}X_{2},

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\left(X_{1}^{\prime\prime}p_{2}-p_{1}^{\prime\prime}X_{2}\right),\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;p_{1}p_{2}^{\prime}\Big)\,.

From here we can read off the warped conformal algebra, by taking the generators to be
L_{n}=(e^{in\varphi},0;0,0,0),P_{n}=(0,e^{in\varphi};0,0,0)
and
Z_{1}=(0,0;1,0,0),Z_{2}=(0,0;0,1,0)
and
Z_{3}=(0,0;0,0,1)
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}i[L_{n},L_{m}]&=(n-m)L_{n+m}+\frac{Z_{1}}{12}n^{3}\delta_{n+m}\,,\\
i[L_{n},P_{m}]&=-mP_{m+n}-in^{2}Z_{2}\delta_{n+m}\,,\\
i[P_{n},P_{m}]&=-mZ_{3}\delta_{n+m}\,.\end{split}

(12)

Elements of the dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
will be denoted by
({\cal L},{\cal P};c,k,\kappa)
. We denote by
\langle,\rangle
the pairing between the dual space and the algebra, i.e.
\langle,\rangle
is a map from
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
. In our case, we will take

\langle({\cal L},{\cal P};c,k,\kappa),(X,p;\lambda,\mu,\nu)\rangle=\oint{\rm d}\varphi\left({\cal L}(\varphi)X(\varphi)+{\cal P}(\varphi)p(\varphi)\right)+c\lambda+k\mu+\kappa\nu\,.

(13)

We can now define the coadjoint action of the group on elements of the dual space as

\langle{\rm Ad}_{(f,p_{1})^{-1}}^{*}({\cal L},{\cal P};c,k,\kappa),(X,p_{2};\lambda,\mu,\nu)\rangle=\langle({\cal L},{\cal P};c,k,\kappa),{\rm Ad}_{(f,p_{1})}(X,p_{2};\lambda,\mu,\nu)\rangle\,.

(14)

By using (
3
) and (
13
) we obtain

{\rm Ad}_{(f,p_{1})^{-1}}^{*}({\cal L},{\cal P};c,k,\kappa)=(\tilde{\cal L},\tilde{\cal P};c,k,\kappa)\,,

(15)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal L}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}{}^{2}\left({\cal L}(f)+{\cal P}(f)\partial_{f}(p\circ f)-\frac{c}{24\pi}\frac{\{f;\varphi\}}{f^{\prime}{}^{2}}+\frac{k}{2\pi}\partial_{f}^{2}(p\circ f)+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}[\partial_{f}(p\circ f)]^{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal P}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}\left({\cal P}(f)-\frac{k}{2\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime 2}}+\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\partial_{f}(p\circ f)\right)\,.

(16)

The notation here differs from the appendix A of
Afshar:2015wjm
, but this is due to the fact that there it is the quantity
{\rm Ad}^{*}_{(f,p)}({\cal L},{\cal P};c,k,\kappa)(f(\varphi))
that is computed, whereas here we have given
{\rm Ad}^{*}_{(f,p)^{-1}}({\cal L},{\cal P};c,k,\kappa)(\varphi)
. For completeness, we also list the other expression here, which is equivalent to (
3
):

\displaystyle{\rm Ad}_{(f,p)}^{*}{\cal L}(f(\varphi))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}{}^{2}}\bigg({\cal L}(\varphi)-{\cal P}(\varphi)(p\circ f)^{\prime}(\varphi)+\frac{c}{24\pi}\{f;\varphi\}-\frac{k}{2\pi}(p\circ f)^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}[(p\circ f)^{\prime}(\varphi)]^{2}\bigg)\,,

\displaystyle{\rm Ad}_{(f,p)}^{*}{\cal P}(f(\varphi))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}}\left({\cal P}(\varphi)+\frac{k}{2\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}-\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}(p\circ f)^{\prime}(\varphi)\right)\,.

4
Coadjoint orbits

Here we classify the coadjoint orbits of the warped Virasoro group and their corresponding little group.
The main point is that coadjoint orbits
{\cal O}_{({\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0})}
are defined as all elements of the dual space
({\cal L},{\cal P})
which can be obtained from a fixed representative
({\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0})
by the coadjoint action (
3
). The orbit is isomorphic to the symplectic manifold
{\cal O}\sim G/{\cal H}_{({\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0})}
, where
G
is the group manifold and
{\cal H}_{({\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0})}
is the stabilizer subgroup of the orbit, consisting of all elements which leave
({\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0})
invariant under the coadjoint action (
3
). From now on we will take
({\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0})
to be constant. Through the reduction of the Chern-Simons theory with constant charges, we should be able to relate the orbit representatives
({\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0})
to the Chern-Simons charges of the bulk solution.
Generically, the infinitesimal coadjoint action (
3
) gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm ad}^{*}_{\epsilon_{L},\epsilon_{P}}{\cal L}(\varphi)=&\epsilon_{L}{\cal L}^{\prime}+2\epsilon_{L}^{\prime}{\cal L}-\frac{c}{24\pi}\epsilon_{L}^{\prime\prime\prime}+\epsilon_{P}^{\prime}{\cal P}+\frac{k}{2\pi}\epsilon_{P}^{\prime\prime}\,,\\
{\rm ad}^{*}_{\epsilon_{L},\epsilon_{P}}{\cal P}(\varphi)=&\epsilon_{L}^{\prime}{\cal P}+\epsilon_{L}{\cal P}^{\prime}-\frac{k}{2\pi}\epsilon_{L}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\epsilon_{P}^{\prime}\,.\end{split}

(17)

We will here focus on orbits with constant representatives, hence they as obtained from the coadjoint action of the algebra on constant
{\cal L}_{0}
and
{\cal P}_{0}
, in which case the above formula simplifies to

\displaystyle{\rm ad}^{*}_{\epsilon_{L},\epsilon_{P}}{\cal L}_{0}=

\displaystyle 2\epsilon_{L}^{\prime}{\cal L}_{0}-\frac{c}{24\pi}\epsilon_{L}^{\prime\prime\prime}+\epsilon_{P}^{\prime}{\cal P}_{0}+\frac{k}{2\pi}\epsilon_{P}^{\prime\prime}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle{\rm ad}^{*}_{\epsilon_{L},\epsilon_{P}}{\cal P}_{0}=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{L}^{\prime}{\cal P}_{0}-\frac{k}{2\pi}\epsilon_{L}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\epsilon_{P}^{\prime}\,.

(19)

As we will see below, whenever
\kappa\neq 0
, one can make a field redefinition such that
k=0
, so we should distinguish two cases,
\kappa=0
and
k\neq 0
or
\kappa\neq 0
and
k=0
addressed below:

4.1
\kappa=0
and
k\neq 0

Let us first classify the orbits
{\cal O}_{p}
for
p\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
under the
\sigma
-action of
G
. This amounts to solving the differential equation

\epsilon_{L}^{\prime}{\cal P}_{0}-\frac{k}{2\pi}\epsilon_{L}^{\prime\prime}=0\,,

(20)

for
\epsilon_{L}
a function on the
S^{1}
. The general solution is

\epsilon_{L}=a+b\,e^{\frac{2\pi{\cal P}_{0}}{k}\varphi}\,.

(21)

This is only well defined on the
S^{1}
whenever
{\cal P}_{0}=ikn/2\pi
and hence there are two distinct cases

•

{\cal P}_{0}\neq ikn/2\pi
:
b=0
and the little group
G_{p}
is
U(1)

•

{\cal P}_{0}=ikn/2\pi
:
b\neq 0
and the little group

is two-dimensional:
i[L_{0},L_{n}]=-nL_{n}
for
L_{n}=e^{in\varphi}
and
L_{0}=1

In the first case, the remaining equation (
18
) gives

\epsilon_{P}^{\prime}{\cal P}_{0}+\frac{k}{2\pi}\epsilon_{P}^{\prime\prime}=0

(22)

which is solved by
\epsilon_{P}=c+de^{-\frac{2\pi{\cal P}_{0}}{k}\varphi}
. This once again gives only one solution on the circle, under the assumption that
{\cal P}_{0}\neq ikn/2\pi
. Hence these orbits are characterized by a two-dimensional Abelian little group.

The second case is more interesting. In that case the remaining equation (
18
) gives

in\epsilon_{P}^{\prime}+\epsilon_{P}^{\prime\prime}=-\frac{2\pi in}{k}(2{\cal L}_{0}+\frac{c}{24\pi}n^{2})e^{in\varphi}\equiv a_{n}e^{in\varphi}\,.

(23)

In this case, the solution reads

\epsilon_{P}=c+de^{-in\varphi}-\frac{a_{n}}{2n^{2}}e^{in\varphi}\,.

(24)

Hence,
regardless
of the value of
{\cal L}_{0}
, this solution is well defined on the circle, however, only when
a_{n}=0
, or, whenever

{\cal L}_{0}=-\frac{c}{48\pi}n^{2}\,,

(25)

does the algebra
[(\epsilon_{L},\epsilon_{P}),(\epsilon_{L},\epsilon_{P})]
, understood as (
11
), close linearly. In that case, the algebra written in the form of the generators
L_{0}=(1,0)
,
L_{n}=(e^{in\varphi},0)
,
P_{0}=(0,1)
, and

reads:

\displaystyle\begin{split}i[L_{0},L_{n}]=-nL_{n}\,&,\qquad i[L_{0},P_{-n}]=nP_{-n}\,,\\
i[L_{n},P_{-n}]=nP_{0}\,&,\qquad i[P_{0},L_{n}]=0\,.\end{split}

(26)

In the case of

P_{2}^{c}

To summarize, if

little group in (
28
), this implies that coadjoint orbits of the
\kappa\neq 0
warped Virasoro group are characterized by the value of the Sugawara shifted
\mathfrak{L}_{0}
. For generic values of this quantity there is a two-dimensional Abelian little group, whereas for
\mathfrak{L}_{0}=-\frac{n^{2}}{4}
the little group on the coadjoint orbits is
SL^{(n)}(2,\mathbb{R})\times U(1)
.

5
Geometric actions

The coadjoint orbits form symplectic manifolds, with the symplectic structure given by the Konstant-Kirillov symplectic form
\omega
. For the warped Virasoro group, this has been constructed in
Afshar:2019tvp
, which we follow here by slightly adapting notations:

\omega_{12}=\langle({\cal L},{\cal P};c,k,\kappa),{\rm ad}_{(X_{1},p_{1})}\left(X_{2},p_{2};\lambda_{2},\mu_{2},\nu_{2}\right)\rangle\,.

(32)

For the generic construction of the geometric actions for centrally extended semi-direct product groups, such as the warped Virasoro group, see also
Barnich:2017jgw
.
To consider the orbit
{\cal O}_{({\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0})}
, we take the dual space elements to be

\displaystyle{\cal L}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}{}^{2}\left({\cal L}_{0}+{\cal P}_{0}\partial_{f}(p\circ f)-\frac{c}{24\pi}\frac{\{f;\varphi\}}{f^{\prime}{}^{2}}+\frac{k}{2\pi}\partial_{f}^{2}(p\circ f)+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}[\partial_{f}(p\circ f)]^{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle{\cal P}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}\left({\cal P}_{0}-\frac{k}{2\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime 2}}+\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\partial_{f}(p\circ f)\right)\,.

(33)

Using this, (
32
) reads explicitly

\displaystyle\omega_{12}=

\displaystyle\;-\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\left(f^{\prime 2}({\cal L}_{0}+\partial_{f}(p\circ f){\cal P}_{0})\left(X_{1}X_{2}^{\prime}-X_{2}X_{1}^{\prime}\right)+{\cal P}_{0}f^{\prime}\left(X_{1}p_{2}^{\prime}-X_{2}p_{1}^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{c}{24\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\left(X_{1}^{\prime\prime}X_{2}^{\prime}+\{f,\varphi\}(X_{1}X_{2}^{\prime}-X_{2}X_{1}^{\prime})\right)

(34)

\displaystyle+\frac{k}{2\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\left(X_{1}^{\prime}p_{2}^{\prime}-X_{2}^{\prime}p_{1}^{\prime}-f^{\prime 2}\partial_{f}^{2}(p\circ f)(X_{1}X_{2}^{\prime}-X_{2}X_{1}^{\prime})+(\log f^{\prime})^{\prime}(X_{1}p_{2}^{\prime}-X_{2}p_{1}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\left(\mathbf{2}p_{1}p_{2}^{\prime}+(p\circ f)^{\prime}{}^{2}(X_{1}X_{2}^{\prime}-X_{2}X_{1}^{\prime})+2(p\circ f)^{\prime}(X_{1}p_{2}^{\prime}-X_{2}p_{1}^{\prime})\right)

In terms of differential forms, the above expression can be brought in the form

\displaystyle\omega=

\displaystyle-\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\left({\cal L}_{0}f^{\prime}{}^{2}{\rm d}X\wedge{\rm d}X^{\prime}+{\cal P}_{0}f^{\prime}{\rm d}X\wedge({\rm d}p+(p\circ f)^{\prime}{\rm d}X)^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{c}{48\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\left({\rm d}X^{\prime}\wedge{\rm d}X^{\prime\prime}-2\{f;\varphi\}{\rm d}X\wedge{\rm d}X^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{k}{2\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\left({\rm d}X^{\prime}+(\log f^{\prime})^{\prime}{\rm d}X)\wedge({\rm d}p+(p\circ f)^{\prime}{\rm d}X)^{\prime}\right)

(35)

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;({\rm d}p+(p\circ f)^{\prime}{\rm d}X)\wedge({\rm d}p+(p\circ f)^{\prime}{\rm d}X)^{\prime}

We now change variables to the finite transformations, by using that

{\rm d}f=f^{\prime}{\rm d}X\,,\qquad{\rm d}g={\rm d}p+\partial_{\varphi}(p\circ f){\rm d}X\,,

(36)

where
g\equiv p\circ f
does not only transform under
{\rm Diff}(S^{1})
but also under
C^{\infty}(S^{1})
.
The result simplifies even further by writing it as

\displaystyle\omega=-\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\Big(

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{0}{\rm d}f\wedge{\rm d}f^{\prime}+{\cal P}_{0}{\rm d}f\wedge{\rm d}g^{\prime}

(37)

\displaystyle+\frac{c}{48\pi}{\rm d}\log f^{\prime}\wedge({\rm d}\log f^{\prime})^{\prime}-\frac{k}{2\pi}{\rm d}\log f^{\prime}\wedge{\rm d}g^{\prime}+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}{\rm d}g\wedge{\rm d}g^{\prime}\Big).

It is now strikingly clear that whenever
\kappa\neq 0
, the term proportional to
k
can be removed by redefining

g=\tilde{g}+\frac{k}{\kappa}\log f^{\prime}\,.

(38)

The result is (dropping a total derivative term proportional to
{\cal P}_{0}
):

\displaystyle\omega=-\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\Big(

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{0}{\rm d}f\wedge{\rm d}f^{\prime}+{\cal P}_{0}{\rm d}f\wedge{\rm d}g^{\prime}+\frac{c_{\rm eff}}{48\pi}{\rm d}\log f^{\prime}\wedge({\rm d}\log f^{\prime})^{\prime}+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}{\rm d}g\wedge{\rm d}g^{\prime}\Big).

(39)

where

c_{\rm eff}=c-12\frac{k^{2}}{\kappa}\,.

(40)

Now that we have the Konstant-Kirillov symplectic form for any orbit with constant representatives
({\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0})
, we can obtain the geometric action on this orbit. Since
{\rm d}\omega=0
, we can locally write
\omega={\rm d}\alpha
. Then the kinetic part of the geometric action on the coadjoint orbit is the integral over the orbit of
\alpha
, i.e.

\displaystyle I[f,g;{\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\alpha

(41)

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\oint{\rm d}\varphi\Big({\cal L}_{0}f^{\prime}{\rm d}f+{\cal P}_{0}f^{\prime}{\rm d}g+\frac{c}{48\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}{\rm d}f^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}{}^{2}}+\frac{k}{2\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}{\rm d}g+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}g^{\prime}{\rm d}g\Big)\,.

Here
\gamma
is a path along the orbit. By parameterizing
\gamma
by a coordinate
t
on the orbit and using
{\rm d}={\rm d}t\partial_{t}
we find the two dimensional action

I=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}\varphi\;\left({\cal L}_{0}f^{\prime}\dot{f}+\frac{c_{\rm eff}}{48\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\dot{f}^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}{}^{2}}\right)+\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}\varphi\;\left({\cal P}_{0}f^{\prime}\dot{\tilde{g}}+\frac{k}{4\pi}\tilde{g}^{\prime}\dot{\tilde{g}}\right)\,.

(42)

Here dots denote
t
-derivatives and we have used the redefinition (
38
) to remove the term proportional to
k
. The above action is expressed in terms of
\tilde{g}\circ f\circ\varphi
. By redefining
f\circ\varphi
as
\tilde{\varphi}
and switching variables as
\tilde{g}(\tilde{\varphi})=\Phi(\varphi)
, we may equivalently write the action above as:

I=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}\varphi\;\left({\cal L}_{0}f^{\prime}\dot{f}+\frac{c_{\rm eff}}{48\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\dot{f}^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}{}^{2}}+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\Phi^{\prime}\dot{\Phi}+{\cal P}_{0}\dot{\Phi}f^{\prime}\right)\,.

(43)

The Hamiltonian for this action can be found as the action of
L_{0}
and
P_{0}
, i.e. we can add
-\int{\rm d}t(L_{0}+P_{0})
to (
42
), as in section 4 of
Afshar:2019tvp
and in
Henneaux:2019sjx
for the Virasoro group. This generalizes the warped Schwarzian action (4.5) of
Afshar:2019tvp
to a generic orbit parameterized by
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{P}_{0}
. For further insights, we refer the reader to
Barnich:2017jgw
for a discussion on invariant Hamiltonians for geometric actions. The key concept is that
L_{0}
always belongs to the stabilizer subgroup of the orbit, thereby generating a gauge symmetry on the orbit. The generator of this gauge symmetry can then be included in the action as the Hamiltonian, preserving the gauge symmetry.

From (
39
), we can make the field redefinition
\tilde{g}\rightarrow\tilde{g}-\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}f
and get the following symplectic form:

\omega=-\oint{\rm d}\varphi\;\Big(({\cal L}_{0}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}^{2}){\rm d}f\wedge{\rm d}f^{\prime}+\frac{c_{\rm eff}}{48\pi}{\rm d}\log f^{\prime}\wedge({\rm d}\log f^{\prime})^{\prime}+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}{\rm d}g\wedge{\rm d}g^{\prime}\Big).

(44)

leading to the action

I=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}\varphi\;\left(({\cal L}_{0}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}^{2})f^{\prime}\dot{f}+\frac{c_{\rm eff}}{48\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\dot{f}^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}{}^{2}}+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\Phi^{\prime}\dot{\Phi}\right)\,.

(45)

Note that now, the field
\Phi
is no longer periodic around the circle. So we need to make a last field redefinition
\Phi\rightarrow\Phi+\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}\varphi
to get the final geometric action after dropping some boundary terms:

I=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}\varphi\;\left(({\cal L}_{0}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}^{2})f^{\prime}\dot{f}+\frac{c_{\rm eff}}{48\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\dot{f}^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}{}^{2}}+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\Phi^{\prime}\dot{\Phi}+{\cal P}_{0}\dot{\Phi}\right)\,.

(46)

Adding the Hamiltonian

H=\mu L_{0}+\nu P_{0}\,,

(47)

where
\mu
and
\nu
are chemical potentials and
L_{0}
and
P_{0}
are the zero modes of
{\cal L}(\varphi)
and
{\cal P}(\varphi)
respectively:

L_{0}=\int{\rm d}\varphi\left(({\cal L}_{0}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}^{2})f^{\prime}{}^{2}+\frac{c_{\rm eff}}{48\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}{}^{2}}{f^{\prime}{}^{2}}+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\Phi^{\prime}{}^{2}+\frac{\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}^{2}\right)\,,\quad P_{0}=\int{\rm d}\varphi{\cal P}_{0}\ ,

(48)

this leads to the action

\displaystyle\begin{split}S&=I-\int{\rm d}t\,H\\
&=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}\varphi\;\left(({\cal L}_{0}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}^{2})f^{\prime}\partial_{-}f+\frac{c_{\rm eff}}{48\pi}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\partial_{-}f^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}{}^{2}}+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\Phi^{\prime}\partial_{-}\Phi+{\cal P}_{0}\dot{\Phi}-\mu\frac{\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}^{2}-\nu{\cal P}_{0}\right)\,.\end{split}

(49)

This action represents the geometric action on the coadjoint orbits of the warped Virasoro group with orbit representatives
({\cal L}_{0},{\cal P}_{0})
. We expect it to match the reduction of the Chern-Simons theory with constant charges that we will compute in the next section.

6
Chern-Simons reduction

We start with a
SL(2,\mathbb{R})\times U(1)
Chern-Simons action:

S_{CS}=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left<A\wedge{\rm d}A+\frac{2}{3}A\wedge A\wedge A\right>+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left<C\wedge{\rm d}C\right>\,,

(50)

written in Hamiltonian form:

S_{CS}=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left<A_{\varphi}\dot{A}_{r}-A_{r}\dot{A}_{\varphi}+2A_{t}F_{r\varphi}\right>{\rm d}^{3}x+\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}}(C_{\varphi}\dot{C}_{r}+C_{t}\tilde{F}_{r\varphi})\ {\rm d}^{3}x\,,

(51)

where
F={\rm d}A+A\wedge A
and
\tilde{F}={\rm d}C
. We suppose that the topology of our manifold
\mathcal{M}
is an annulus times
\mathbb{R}
, such that there are two boundaries at
r=r_{i}
and
r=r_{o}
, respectively for the inner and the outer ones. On these boundaries, we impose the following boundary conditions for
\mu
and
\nu
constant:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{At}\ r=r_{o}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}A_{\varphi}=L_{1}-\mathfrak{L}L_{-1}\\
A_{t}=\mu A_{\varphi}\end{array}\right.\text{and}\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}C_{\varphi}=\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}\mathcal{P}\\
C_{t}=\mu C_{\varphi}+\nu\end{array}\right.\\
&\text{At}\ r=r_{i}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}A_{\varphi}=L_{-1}-\tilde{\mathfrak{L}}L_{1}\\
A_{t}=-\tilde{\mu}A_{\varphi}\end{array}\right.\text{and}\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}C_{\varphi}=\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}\tilde{\mathcal{P}}\\
C_{t}=-\tilde{\mu}C_{\varphi}-\tilde{\nu}\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(52)

with
\mathfrak{L}=\frac{2\pi}{k}(\mathcal{L}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\mathcal{P}^{2})
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{L}}=\frac{2\pi}{k}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\tilde{\mathcal{P}}^{2})
.
The equations of motion fix the time evolution of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{K}
for constant chemical potentials:

\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}=\mu\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\ ,\ \partial_{t}\mathcal{P}=\mu\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\,,

(53)

where the prime denotes a derivative with respect to
\varphi
.

To have a well defined variational principle, we need to add boundary terms to the action:

\displaystyle I_{\Sigma_{o}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{o}}\mu\left<A_{\varphi}^{2}\right>{\rm d}^{2}x-\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{o}}(\mu C_{\varphi}^{2}+2\nu C_{\varphi})\ {\rm d}^{2}x

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{o}}(\mu\mathcal{L}+\nu\mathcal{P})\ {\rm d}^{2}x\,,

\displaystyle I_{\Sigma_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{i}}\tilde{\mu}\left<A_{\varphi}^{2}\right>{\rm d}^{2}x-\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{i}}(\tilde{\mu}C_{\varphi}^{2}+2\tilde{\nu}C_{\varphi})\ {\rm d}^{2}x

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{i}}(\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\mathcal{L}}+\tilde{\nu}\tilde{\mathcal{P}})\ {\rm d}^{2}x\,.

Our final action is then

S[A,C]=S_{CS}+I_{\Sigma_{i}}+I_{\Sigma_{o}}\,.

(56)

Solving the constraints
F_{r\varphi}=0
and
\tilde{F}_{r\varphi}=0
and taking care of the holonomy, we get:

\begin{array}[]{l l}A_{r}=G^{-1}\partial_{r}G\,,&A_{\varphi}=G^{-1}(\partial_{\varphi}+K(t))G\,,\\
C_{r}=\partial_{r}\lambda\,,&C_{\varphi}=\partial_{\varphi}\lambda+k_{0}(t)\,,\end{array}

(57)

where
G
is a group element of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
and is periodic, as is
\lambda
.
K(t)
is a function parametrizing the holonomy and taking value in the Lie algebra.

First, we will focus on the
U(1)
-part of the action (
56
). The
U(1)
-part in (
51
) can be expressed as

S[\Phi(t,\varphi),\Psi(t,\varphi),k_{0}(t)]=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int\left(\partial_{\varphi}\Phi\partial_{t}\Phi-\partial_{\varphi}\Psi\partial_{t}\Psi+2k_{0}(\partial_{t}\Phi-\partial_{t}\Psi)\right){\rm d}t\ {\rm d}\varphi\,,

(58)

where
\lambda(r=r_{o},t,\varphi)=\Phi(t,\varphi)
and
\lambda(r=r_{i},t,\varphi)=\Psi(t,\varphi)
. From the boundary terms (
54
) and (
55
), we have

I_{B}=-\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int\left(\mu(\partial_{\varphi}\Phi)^{2}+\tilde{\mu}(\partial_{\varphi}\Psi)^{2}+(\mu+\tilde{\mu})k_{0}^{2}+2(\nu+\tilde{\nu})k_{0}\right){\rm d}t\ {\rm d}\varphi\,.

(59)

Using the boundary conditions (
52
), the functions
\Phi
and
\Psi
are constrained by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\varphi}\Phi+k_{0}&=\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}\mathcal{P}\,,\\
\partial_{\varphi}\Psi+k_{0}&=\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}\tilde{\mathcal{P}}\,.\end{split}

(60)

So to summarize, we have

S[\Phi(t,\varphi),\Psi(t,\varphi),k_{0}(t)]=S^{(2)}+S^{(1)}+S^{(0)}\,,

(61)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}S^{(2)}&=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int{\rm d}^{2}x\left(\partial_{\varphi}\Phi\partial_{t}\Phi-\mu(\partial_{\varphi}\Phi)^{2}\right)\,,\\
S^{(1)}&=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int{\rm d}^{2}x\left(-\partial_{\varphi}\Psi\partial_{t}\Psi-\tilde{\mu}(\partial_{\varphi}\Psi)^{2}\right)\,,\\
S^{(0)}&=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\int{\rm d}^{2}x\left(k_{0}(\partial_{t}\Phi-\partial_{t}\Psi)-\frac{\mu+\tilde{\mu}}{2}k_{0}^{2}-(\nu+\tilde{\nu})k_{0}\right)\,.\end{split}

(62)

This action is invariant under the
U(1)
gauge transformation

\Phi\to\Phi+\epsilon(t)\,,\qquad\Psi\to\Psi+\epsilon(t)\,,\qquad k_{0}\to k_{0}\,,

(63)

and under the
\frac{\widehat{LG}\times\widehat{LG}}{G}
global symmetry with
G=U(1)

Henneaux:2019sjx
;
ELITZUR1989108

\Phi\to\Phi+\alpha(\varphi)\,,\qquad\Psi\to\Psi+\beta(\varphi)\,,

(64)

where
\widehat{LG}
is the loop group of a topological group
G

DeKerf1997:Chap18
. The quotient by
G
arises from the sharing of the same zero mode, related to the holonomy
k_{0}
, for
\Phi
and
\Psi
.

If we focus on one boundary only, we get:

S_{\text{bdy}}^{\Sigma_{o}}=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int d^{2}x\left(\Phi^{\prime}\partial_{-}\Phi+2k_{0}\dot{\Phi}-\mu k_{0}^{2}-2\nu k_{0}\right)\,,

(65)

where we have defined
\partial_{-}=\partial_{t}-\mu\partial_{\varphi}
and dropped a total derivative at the time boundaries.
The remaining action has lost its gauge invariance and is left with only a
\widehat{LG}
global symmetry.

Now, let us turn to the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
part of the action (
56
). The computation was already done in
Henneaux:2019sjx
for similar boundary conditions. The only difference is the presence of
\mu
in the boundary conditions (
52
) (set to
1
in
Henneaux:2019sjx
) and the replacement of
{\cal L}
by
\mathfrak{L}
.
In summary, we have at the outer boundary:

S_{\text{bdy}}^{\Sigma_{o}}=\frac{k}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{o}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}\varphi\left[\frac{\partial_{-}f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime 2}}+\alpha\partial_{-}ff^{\prime}\right]\,,

(66)

and

\mathfrak{L}=\frac{\alpha}{4}f^{\prime 2}-\frac{1}{2}\{f;\varphi\}\,,

(67)

with
\alpha
defined as

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\text{Holonomy}&K(t)&\alpha\\
\hline\cr\text{Hyperbolic}&k_{h}(t)L_{0}&k_{h}^{2}\\
\text{Elliptic}&\frac{1}{2}k_{e}(t)(L_{1}+L_{-1})&-k_{e}^{2}\\
\text{Parabolic}&k_{p}(t)L_{-1}&0\end{array}

(68)

A similar action holds for the inner boundary.
The fields
\Phi
and
f
living on the outer boundary are coupled through the boundary conditions (
52
):

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Phi^{\prime}+k_{0}&=\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}\mathcal{P}\,,\\
\frac{\alpha}{2}f^{\prime 2}-\{f;\varphi\}&=\frac{4\pi}{k}(\mathcal{L}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\mathcal{P}^{2})\,,\end{aligned}

(69)

and follow the action

S_{\text{bdy}}^{\Sigma_{o}}=\frac{k}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{o}}{\rm d}^{2}x\left(\frac{\partial_{-}f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime 2}}+\alpha\partial_{-}ff^{\prime}\right)+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int{\rm d}^{2}x\left(\Phi^{\prime}\partial_{-}\Phi+2k_{0}\dot{\Phi}-\mu k_{0}^{2}-2\nu k_{0}\right)\,.

(70)

We can now compare (

) with (

{\cal P}_{0}=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}k_{0}\,,\qquad c_{\text{eff}}=6k\,,\qquad{\cal L}_{0}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}^{2}=\frac{k}{8\pi}\alpha\,.

The residual symmetries of (

, in order to maintain the conjugacy class of the holonomy

, while the field

global symmetry

7
Dimensional reduction and link with Warped Schwarzian theory

The idea for this section is to perform the dimensional reduction of our action (
70
) as can be done for Liouville theory where one ends up with a Schwarzian theory
Cotler:2018zff
.

The first step is to make a Wick rotation
t=iy
. Then we take the time circle to be very small,
\Delta y\rightarrow 0
, with
k^{\prime}=k\Delta y
and
\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa\Delta y
. We then end up with

S=\frac{\mu k^{\prime}}{8\pi}\int{\rm d}\varphi\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime 2}}{f^{\prime 2}}+\alpha f^{\prime 2}\right)+\frac{\mu\kappa^{\prime}}{4\pi}\int{\rm d}\varphi\left(\Phi^{\prime 2}+k_{0}^{2}+2\frac{\nu}{\mu}k_{0}\right)\,.

(72)

Now, up to some boundary terms, we can repackage this using the Schwarzian derivative (
10
):

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu k^{\prime}}{8\pi}\int{\rm d}\varphi\left(-2\left\{f;\varphi\right\}+\alpha f^{\prime 2}\right)+\frac{\mu\kappa^{\prime}}{4\pi}\int{\rm d}\varphi\left(\Phi^{\prime 2}+k_{0}^{2}+2\frac{\nu}{\mu}k_{0}\right)

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu k^{\prime}}{8\pi}\int{\rm d}\varphi\left\{\tan\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}f\right);\varphi\right\}+\frac{\mu\kappa^{\prime}}{4\pi}\int{\rm d}\varphi\ \Phi^{\prime 2}+\int{\rm d}\varphi\left(\mu{\cal L}_{0}^{\prime}+\nu{\cal P}_{0}^{\prime}\right)\,,

where for the last step we used the equations (
71
).
The last equation is the Warped Schwarzian action in
Afshar:2019tvp
with additional source terms.

8
One-loop partition function

We are now interested in computing the partition function for our action (
70
) in a similar manner as Section 5 of
Cotler:2018zff
.
The general idea behind this strategy was outlined in
Maloney:2007ud
;
Giombi:2008vd
;
Maloney:2009ck
. The canonical ensemble partition function
can be thought of an Euclidean functional integral weighted by the classical action with typical coupling constant
1/K
(for AdS
3
gravity, this would be related to the Brown-Henneaux central charge as
c=24K
). At large
K
the dominant contribution to the path integral is given by the saddle point approximation, i.e. as a sum over classical saddles satisfying the appropriate boundary conditions of an exponential whose argument consist in a series expansion starting with the classical action evaluated on the saddle, followed by subleading terms representing quantum corrections to the effective action at n
th
order in perturbation theory (as it turns out, there are instances where the partition function happens to be one-loop exact). On general grounds, the partition function is expected to be expressible as a sum of characters of the corresponding symmetry algebra - Virasoro for AdS
3
gravity, and BMS
3
for flat space holography. This indeed turns out to be the case
Giombi:2008vd
;
Cotler:2018zff
;
Merbis:2019wgk
. In the present situation the relevant characters are those of the warped conformal algebra taking the following form
Apolo:2018eky
(see App.A for a brief review and notations):

\displaystyle\chi_{h,p}

\displaystyle=q^{h-\frac{c}{24}}y^{p}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2n}}\left(1-\delta_{\text{vac}}\,q\right)\quad\,\,\,\,\text{for}\>p\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(74)

\displaystyle\chi_{h,p}

\displaystyle=q^{h-\frac{c}{24}}y^{p}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}\left(1-\delta_{\text{vac}}\,q\right)\quad\text{for}\>p\in i\mathbb{R}\,.

(75)

We will therefore expand the partition function for our action (
70
) in orders of
k
and
\kappa
and limit ourselves to the 1-loop contribution. We refer the reader to
Merbis:2019wgk
for a sketch of the proof of the one-loop exactness of the partition function for any geometric action defined from the Kirillov-Konstant symplectic form. We will investigate whether this allows us to recover the above characters.

From now on, we will assume that
\mu=0
and
\nu=1
, as suggested in
Azeyanagi:2018har
. This implies that the mass and angular momentum of the solutions with boundary conditions (
54
) are given by

M=\pi{\cal P}\,,\qquad J=-2\pi{\cal L}\,.

(76)

The action (
70
) then reduces to

S_{\text{bdy}}^{\Sigma_{o}}=\frac{k}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{o}}{\rm d}^{2}x\left(\frac{\dot{f}^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime 2}}+\alpha\dot{f}f^{\prime}\right)+\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int{\rm d}^{2}x\left(\Phi^{\prime}\dot{\Phi}+2k_{0}(\dot{\Phi}-1)\right)\,.

(77)

To compute the one-loop partition function, we first need the classical saddle point of the action (
77
) and then evaluate the euclidean action on this saddle point. The equations of motion derived from (
77
) for constant orbit representative are

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{f^{\prime}}\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}f^{\prime 2}-\{f;\varphi\}\right)&=0\,,\\
\partial_{t}\Phi^{\prime}&=0\,.\end{split}

(78)

Using (
69
) and supposing
f^{\prime}\neq 0
, we recover the equations of motion of the original Chern-Simons theory (
53
) for our choice of chemical potentials. The solutions of interest to the saddle point approximation are those with constant
\mathfrak{L}
and
\mathcal{P}
, where their values are given by the zero modes
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{P}_{0}
, implying from (
69
)

\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(f^{\prime 2}-1\right)=\{f;\varphi\}\,,\qquad\Phi^{\prime}=0\,.

(79)

We get as saddle point the solution:

f(\varphi,t)=\varphi+c_{1}(t)\,,\qquad\Phi(\varphi,t)=c_{2}(t)\,.

(80)

We make a Wick rotation
t=-iy
and compute the euclidean action
iS_{E}=S_{\text{bdy}}^{\Sigma_{o}}
,

S_{E}=-i\frac{k}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{o}}{\rm d}^{2}x\left(\frac{\dot{f^{\prime}}f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime 2}}+\alpha\dot{f}f^{\prime}\right)-\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int{\rm d}^{2}x\left(i\Phi^{\prime}\dot{\Phi}+2ik_{0}\dot{\Phi}-2k_{0}\right)\,.

(81)

For this part, we will separate the discussion into two cases: the first one for a real orbit representative
{\cal P}_{0}
and the second one for a purely imaginary
{\cal P}_{0}
. Let us begin with a real
U(1)
holonomy, which corresponds to warped black hole solutions, and derive the Hamiltonian as the real part of the action

H=\kappa k_{0}=2\pi{\cal P}_{0}\,,

(82)

which could already be seen from (
47
) with
\mu=0
and
\nu=1
.
It corresponds to an orbit representative
{\cal P}_{0}
that is also real.
For a given value of the holonomy, the Hamiltonian is always bounded from below. It is interesting to note that in the case of
\mu=0
and
\nu=1
, the path integral is well-defined for any value of
\alpha
, as long as
k_{0}
is real, contrary to the Virasoro case where only values of
\alpha
greater or equal to
-1
were allowed.

Then, we periodically identify the euclidean time
y
. We get as periodicity conditions on the fields:

\displaystyle f(\varphi+\Omega\beta,y+\beta)=f(\varphi,y)

\displaystyle,\qquad\Phi(\varphi+\Omega\beta,y+\beta)=\Phi(\varphi,y)\,,

(83)

\displaystyle f(\varphi+2\pi,y)=f(\varphi,y)+2\pi

\displaystyle,\qquad\Phi(\varphi+2\pi,y)=\Phi(\varphi,y)\,.

In terms of the fields
c_{1}(y)
and
c_{2}(y)
, this gives

c_{1}(y+\beta)=c_{1}(y)-\Omega\beta\qquad,\qquad c_{2}(y+\beta)=c_{2}(y)\,.

(84)

So the solution is

f_{0}=\varphi-\Omega y\,,\quad\Phi_{0}=0\,,

(85)

and the euclidean action evaluated on this saddle point (
85
) is

S_{E}^{(0)}=\beta\left(\kappa k_{0}+ik\Omega\mathfrak{L}_{0}\right)\,.

(86)

From there, we can already state that the 0-loop contribution will give the factors

e^{-S_{E}^{(0)}}=q^{-\frac{c\alpha}{24}}\,y^{i\kappa k_{0}}

(87)

where we defined

q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\,,\qquad y=e^{2\pi iz}\,,

(88)

with
\beta\Omega=2\pi\tau
and
\beta=2\pi z
.
Furthermore, we are imposing that
\beta
and
\Omega
are determined in terms of the holonomies
\alpha
and
k_{0}

Azeyanagi:2018har

\text{exp}\left[\frac{i\beta\Omega}{2\pi}\int A_{\varphi}{\rm d}\varphi\right]=-\mathbb{1}\,,\quad\text{exp}\left[\frac{i\beta}{2\pi}\left(\int C_{t}{\rm d}\varphi+\Omega\int C_{\varphi}{\rm d}\varphi\right)\right]=e^{2\pi i\gamma}\,,

(89)

leading to the following relations between
\beta
,
\Omega
and the zero-modes:

\beta=2\pi\left(\gamma-\frac{\pi{\cal P}_{0}}{\kappa\sqrt{\mathfrak{L}}_{0}}\right)\qquad,\qquad\Omega=\frac{1}{2\gamma\sqrt{\mathfrak{L}}_{0}-\frac{2\pi{\cal P}_{0}}{\kappa}}\qquad,\qquad\beta\Omega=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{L}}_{0}}\,.

(90)

The
\gamma
is an arbitrary constant and represents a kind of deformation at the level of the holonomy
Azeyanagi:2018har
. If it is an integer, one recovers a similar result to BTZ with an holonomy living in the center of the gauge group. However, it is expected that it is not necessarily the case for warped spacetimes. For instance, for the warped black hole in canonical ensemble
Azeyanagi:2018har
;
Anninos:2008fx
,

\gamma=\frac{2\nu}{\nu^{2}+3}\,.

(91)

Now we can expand the fields
f(\varphi,y)
and
\Phi(\varphi,y)
around the saddle points as

\displaystyle f(\varphi,y)

\displaystyle=f_{0}+\sum_{m,n}\frac{f_{m,n}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{\frac{2\pi imy}{\beta}}e^{inf_{0}}\,,

(92)

\displaystyle\Phi(\varphi,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n}\frac{\Phi_{m,n}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{\frac{2\pi imy}{\beta}}e^{inf_{0}}\,.

One can check that these series expansions satisfy the periodicity conditions (
83
). Plugging this into the euclidean action, we obtain

S_{E}=S_{E}^{(0)}-\frac{i}{16\pi^{3}}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{n\neq 0}n(m-\tau n)\left(\frac{k}{2}|f_{m,n}|^{2}(n^{2}+\alpha)+\kappa|\Phi_{m,n}|^{2}\right)+...\,,

(93)

where
f_{m,n}^{*}=f_{-m,-n}
,
\Phi_{m,n}^{*}=\Phi_{-m,-n}
and the dots contains terms of the third order and more on the fields. One does not need to take the vacuum into account in this case since in holographic WCFTs it possesses an imaginary value for
{\cal P}_{0}
Azeyanagi:2018har
;
Apolo:2018eky
.

We can now compute the one-loop partition function by integrating out the fields
f_{m,n}
and
\Phi_{m,n}
:

Z_{\text{1-loop}}=\int{\cal D}f{\cal D}\Phi\,e^{-S_{E}}={\cal N}e^{-S_{E}^{(0)}}\prod_{m,n}n^{-1}(m-\tau n)^{-1}(n^{2}+\alpha)^{-1/2}\,,

(94)

where
{\cal N}
is a normalization constant independent of
\beta
and
\Omega
.
To perform the product for the
m
’s, we take the derivative of the logarithm of the partition function with respect to
\tau
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\log Z_{\text{1-loop}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\pi ik\mathfrak{L}_{0}+\sum_{n\neq 0}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{n}{m-\tau n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\pi ik\mathfrak{L}_{0}-\sum_{n\neq 0}\left(\frac{1}{\theta}+2n\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau n}{\tau^{2}n^{2}-m^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\pi ik\mathfrak{L}_{0}-\pi\sum_{n\neq 0}n\cot(n\pi\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\pi ik\mathfrak{L}_{0}-2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\cot(n\pi\tau)

for
n\tau\notin\mathbb{Z}
. This sum diverges but there are several ways to deal with it. One is to use the zeta function regularization to write

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\cot(n\pi\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n(\cot(n\pi\tau)+i)-i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\,.

(95)

Now, the first sum on the right hand side converges for
\text{Im}(\tau)>0
. This requirement is not a problem for negative value of
\alpha
, such as the vacuum. However for a positive value of the holonomy, one needs to perform an analytic continuation
\tau\to\tau+i\varepsilon
as done in
Barnich:2015mui
.
After exponentiation, the partition function reads (up to a normalization constant)

Z_{\text{1-loop}}=e^{-S_{E}^{(0)}}q^{-\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}=q^{-k\mathfrak{L}_{0}-\frac{1}{24}}y^{i\kappa k_{0}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}\,.

(96)

The 1-loop partition function for a given saddle reproduces the contribution to the full WCFT partition function (
75
), originally computed in
Apolo:2018eky
for the precise value
\kappa=-1
,

\chi_{h,p}(\tau,z)=q^{h-c/24}y^{p}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}\,,

(97)

for
p\in i\mathbb{R}
which is our case here (see (
96
)). From there we read that

h=-2\pi\left({\cal L}_{0}-\frac{\pi}{\kappa}{\cal P}_{0}^{2}\right)+\frac{c-1}{24}\qquad,\qquad p=2\pi i{\cal P}_{0}\,.

(98)

It thus seems that starting from a real
{\cal P}_{0}
, solutions representing black holes, we recover the partition function of a primary field with imaginary charge
p
, like the vacuum, and its descendants.

Such a result may seem unusual, but a similar phenomenon was discussed in
Castro:2017mfj
, where the authors computed the one-loop partition function in AdS
3
with CSS boundary conditions
Compere:2013bya
using a quasinormal mode method
Denef:2009kn
. Their primary focus was to describe the BTZ black hole, a unitary representation, but they unexpectedly obtained the vacuum character, also a unitary representation in this context, which they associated with thermal AdS. They argued that, akin to CFTs, an appropriate modular transformation relates thermal AdS to BTZ
Detournay:2012pc
;
Song:2016gtd
, rendering them indistinguishable because they both derive from Euclidean AdS
Banados:1992gq
;
Carlip:1994gc
;
Maldacena:1998bw
. The only distinguishing factor is their contractible or non-contractible thermal circle, which explains why they obtained the vacuum determinant while computing the BTZ determinant.
Another possible interpretation comes from the analytic continuation procedure. Indeed, the Wick rotation
t=-iy
has the effect of sending
\mathcal{P}_{0}
to
i\mathcal{P}_{0}
, hence swapping the reality property of
\mathcal{P}_{0}
and
p
.

We are now interested in states with purely imaginary values of
{\cal P}_{0}
, such as the vacuum. Indeed, in that case, from
Azeyanagi:2018har
,
\alpha=-(1+2j)^{2}
where
j
is an integer and
k_{0}=i\gamma
. We choose the branch
j=0
as in
Azeyanagi:2018har
to make the link with the WCFT vacuum values in
Detournay:2012pc
. When the orbit representative
{\cal P}_{0}
is purely imaginary,
\mathfrak{L}_{0}
remains real and the euclidean action (
81
) is instead

S_{E}=-\frac{ik}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{o}}{\rm d}^{2}x\left(\frac{\dot{f^{\prime}}f^{\prime\prime}}{4f^{\prime 2}}+\mathfrak{L}_{0}\dot{f}f^{\prime}\right)-\int{\rm d}^{2}x\left(\frac{i\kappa}{8\pi}\Phi^{\prime}\dot{\Phi}+i{\cal P}_{0}\dot{\Phi}-{\cal P}_{0}\right)\,.

(99)

The real part of the action is then

\text{Re}[S_{E}]=-i\int{\rm d}\varphi{\cal P}_{0}\dot{\Phi}\,.

(100)

Since we are now dealing with a non-unitary representation, (
100
) can no longer be interpreted as the Hamiltonian (
47
). For the field periodicity (
83
), a saddle point of the action (
99
) is still given by (
85
). One can show that any perturbations around this saddle point keep the real part of the action (
100
) bounded, such that the path integral derivation can also be performed. Following the previous computations, the one-loop partition function is simply

Z_{\text{1-loop}}=q^{-k\mathfrak{L}_{0}-\frac{1}{24}}y^{-\kappa k_{0}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}\,.

(101)

which is (
96
) with
k_{0}\rightarrow ik_{0}
.
This reproduces the character
for an imaginary one (
75
).

For the vacuum, the computation of the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
part of the partition function needs to be improved because
\alpha=-1
in (
94
), making start the product at
n=2
. It implies a slightly modified zeta regularization (
95
)

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n\rightarrow\zeta(-1)-1=-\frac{13}{12}\,.

(102)

The one-loop partition function for the vacuum,
\alpha=-1
and
k_{0}=i\gamma
, is thus

Z_{\text{1-loop}}^{\text{vac}}=q^{-\frac{c+13}{24}}y^{-\kappa\gamma}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}(1-\delta_{\text{vac}}q)\,.

(103)

The factor
13
raised from the zeta regularization because we wanted to ”regularize first, integrate later”. In
Barnich:2015mui
;
Merbis:2019wgk
, a different approach, ”integrate first, regularize later”, was performed which prevents the apparition of the regularization factor.

We surprisingly observe that this computation again yielded the character for
p
imaginary, as was obtained for real
\mathcal{P}_{0}
. How is it possible to obtain the characters for
p
real?
In order to address this apparent puzzle, we momentarily pause on some particularities of WCFT partition functions. We have so far implicitly assumed that the bulk and boundary theories are defined by the following partition function

Z^{\text{CE}}(\beta,\theta)=\mbox{Tr}\;e^{-\beta P_{0}+i\theta L_{0}}

(104)

which is said to be in
canonical
ensemble, and where
L_{0}
and
P_{0}
are the zero modes of the algebra (
4
). It appears however more natural in some situations
Detournay:2012pc
to define the following generators and partition function:

L_{n}^{Q}=L_{n}-\frac{2}{\mathsf{k}}P_{0}P_{n}+\frac{1}{\mathsf{k}}P_{0}^{2}\delta_{n}~,\quad P_{n}^{Q}=-\frac{2}{\mathsf{k}}P_{0}P_{n}+\frac{1}{\mathsf{k}}P_{0}^{2}\delta_{n}~.

(105)

and

Z^{\text{QE}}(\beta_{R},\beta_{L})=\mbox{Tr}\;e^{-\beta_{R}P_{0}^{Q}-\beta_{L}L_{0}^{Q}}~.

(106)

The latter defines the so-called
quadratic
ensemble partition function. Importantly, we have in particular that
P_{0}^{Q}=-P_{0}^{2}/k
, hence states with opposite values of
P_{0}
are identified.
We will refrain from reconsidering our previous analysis in quadratic ensemble, which would entail reformulating the appropriate bulk boundary conditions
Aggarwal:2020igb
in Chern-Simons/Lower-Spin Gravity language, then going through the steps of the Hamiltonian reduction, and identifying the corresponding geometric action. We will leave this for further work. However, it is natural to expect that the periodic boundary conditions on the quadratic ensemble
U(1)
field, when expressed in terms of the canonical
U(1)
field above, will lead to either periodic,
or anti-periodic
boundary conditions. Let us inspect the consequences of this observation, by considering the following (anti-)periodicity condition:

\Phi(\varphi+\Omega\beta,y+\beta)=-\Phi(\varphi,y)\,.

(107)

The new saddle point satisfying this antiperiodic condition (
107
) can be written in the form

\Phi_{0}=\sin\left(\frac{\pi y}{\beta}\right)\,,

(108)

and the expansion of the field around his saddle point is

\Phi(\varphi,y)=\Phi_{0}+\sum_{m,n}\frac{\Phi_{m,n}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{\frac{2\pi imy}{\beta}+\frac{i\pi y}{\beta}}e^{inf_{0}}\,.

(109)

The real part of the action (
100
) is also bounded for the perturbations (
109
) and we can perform the path integral derivation.
Focusing on the
U(1)
part of the action, the euclidean action around the saddle point is

S_{E}^{U(1)}=\kappa\beta k_{0}+\frac{i}{2}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{n\neq 0}\kappa\frac{|\Phi_{m,n}|^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}}n\left(m-\tau n-\frac{1}{2}\right)+...\,.

(110)

The one-loop
U(1)
partition function becomes

Z_{\text{1-loop}}^{U(1)}={\cal N}y^{i\kappa k_{0}}\prod_{m,n}n^{-1/2}\left(m-\tau n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-1/2}\,,

(111)

for
n\neq 0
. Using the same trick as in Section
8
, we derive the logarithm of the partition function according to
\tau
to perform the sum over m, then we integrate and exponentiate to get the one-loop partition function. First we perform the logarithm and rewrite the sum as

\displaystyle\log Z_{\text{1-loop}}^{U(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\neq 0}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left(m-\tau n-\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\log\left(m-\tau n-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\log\left(m+\tau n-\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left(\frac{1}{4}-m+m^{2}-n^{2}\tau^{2}\right)\,.

Now, deriving according to
\tau
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\log Z_{\text{1-loop}}^{U(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq 0}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2}\tau}{\frac{1}{4}-m+m^{2}-n^{2}\tau^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq 0}\left(\sum_{m=-\infty}^{-1}\frac{n^{2}\tau}{\frac{1}{4}-m+m^{2}-n^{2}\tau^{2}}+\frac{n^{2}\tau}{\frac{1}{4}-n^{2}\tau^{2}}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2}\tau}{\frac{1}{4}-m+m^{2}-n^{2}\tau^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq 0}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2}\tau}{\frac{1}{4}+m+m^{2}-n^{2}\tau^{2}}+\frac{n^{2}\tau}{\frac{1}{4}-n^{2}\tau^{2}}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2}\tau}{\frac{1}{4}-m+m^{2}-n^{2}\tau^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq 0}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\tan(n\pi\tau)+\frac{n^{2}\tau}{n^{2}\tau^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}+\frac{n^{2}\tau}{\frac{1}{4}-n^{2}\tau^{2}}+\frac{\pi}{2}\tan(n\pi\tau)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq 0}\pi\tan(n\pi\tau)\,.

Again this sum diverges but can be regularized using the zeta function regularization. After integration and exponentiation (up to a normalization constant), the
U(1)
part of the one-loop partition function is

Z_{\text{1-loop}}^{U(1)}={\cal N}^{\prime}y^{i\kappa k_{0}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+q^{n}}\,,

(112)

which is related to the
U(1)
part of the Virasoro-Kač-Moody character in (3.11) of
Apolo:2018eky
. Combining this with the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
part that remains the same, we end up with

Z_{\text{1-loop}}=q^{-k\mathfrak{L}_{0}-\frac{1}{24}}y^{i\kappa k_{0}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{2n})}\,,

(113)

For the vacuum,
\alpha=-1
and
k_{0}=i\gamma
. Once again, the computation of the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
part of the partition function requires improvement. Paying attention to the zeta regularization, as in (
102
), the one-loop partition for the vacuum with anti-periodic boundary conditions on the
U(1)
field
\Phi
(
107
) is

Z_{\text{1-loop}}^{\text{vac}}=q^{-\frac{c+13}{24}}y^{-\kappa\gamma}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{2n})}(1-\delta_{\text{vac}}q)\,.

(114)

It is completely equivalent to the Warped Virasoro character (
74
) with weight and real charge (since
P_{0}
is purely imaginary) (
98
).

We conclude from this section that using the periodic boundary conditions (
83
) and computing the one-loop partition function of a unitary representation (
\mathcal{P}_{0}
real), like a black hole, we get the warped character for an imaginary charge
p
.
On the other hand, the one-loop determinant for fluctuations around states with imaginary charge (which includes the vacuum for holographic WCFTs) yields the warped character for a real charge
p
only when anti-periodic boundary conditions (
107
) on the canonical
U(1)
field are used. Actually, periodic/anti-periodic boundary conditions on states with arbitrary (real or purely imaginary
\mathcal{P}_{0}
) result in a character for imaginary/real
p
. Though we do not have a clear interpretation for this result, we believe it would be interesting to reconsider our analysis for WCFTs in quadratic ensemble, or to use alternative methods (such as the Heat Kernel method
Giombi:2008vd
or the quasinormal modes approach
Castro:2017mfj
) to compute the one-loop partition function and confirm our findings.

Appendix A
Warped Virasoro characters

Modular invariance, or more precisely covariance in our case, can be used to derive the Warped Cardy formula. As stated in
Apolo:2018eky
, it can also be used to derive the warped characters as we now review.

Let us start with the WCFT partition function

Z(\tau,z)={\rm tr}\left(q^{L_{0}-\frac{c}{24}}y^{P_{0}}\right)\,,

(115)

with

q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\,,\qquad y=e^{2\pi iz}\,.

(116)

Written is such a way, we can define two transformations generating an
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
group
Detournay:2012pc

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle:\qquad\tau\to-\frac{1}{\tau}\,,\quad z\to\frac{z}{\tau}\,,

(117)

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle:\qquad\tau\to\tau+1\,.

(118)

The S-transformations correspond to modular transformations while the T-transformations represent the addition of the angular circle to the thermal circle. For instance, the S-transformations act on the partition function as

Z\left(-\frac{1}{\tau},\frac{z}{\tau}\right)=e^{-\frac{i\pi}{2}\frac{z^{2}}{\tau}}Z(\tau,z)\,.

(119)

As with CFTs, the Hilbert space decomposes into a sum over highest-weight representations of primary states
|p,h\rangle
, defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{n}\left|p,h\right>=0\quad n>0\,,&\qquad L_{n}\left|p,h\right>=0\quad n>0\,,\\
P_{0}\left|p,h\right>=p\left|p,h\right>\,,&\qquad L_{0}\left|p,h\right>=h\left|p,h\right>\,.\end{split}

(120)

It implies a decomposition of the partition function into warped Virasoro characters

Z(\tau,z)=\sum_{h,p}d_{h,p}\,\chi_{h,p}(\tau,z)\,,

(121)

where the sum runs only over primary states and
d_{h,p}
takes into account their degeneracy. We will follow the path done in
Apolo:2018eky
to compute the warped character of a primary state
|p,h\rangle
(
120
).

First, we use the Sugawara basis

L_{n}^{(s)}=L_{n}-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\,:P_{m}P_{n-m}\,:\quad,

(122)

to compute the contribution from the Virasoro descendants to the partition function. The advantage of this basis is that it commutes with every
P_{n}
, allowing us to factorize the norm of mixed states admitting both Virasoro and
U(1)
descendants. As a result, the warped Virasoro character is simply the product of both contributions. The Virasoro descendants are thus written as

\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(L_{-k}^{(s)}\right)^{n_{k}}|p,h\rangle\,,

(123)

with
n_{k}
integer characterising the descendant. As the commutator between
L_{0}
and the Sugawara basis is

[L_{0},L_{n}^{(s)}]=-nL_{n}^{(s)}\,,

(124)

the descendants (
123
) are proportional to

\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(L_{-k}^{(s)}\right)^{n_{k}}|p,h\rangle\propto|p,h+N\rangle\,.

(125)

where
N=\sum_{k}n_{k},k
. The degeneracy of the states
|p,h+N\rangle
corresponds to the number of partitions of the positive integer
N
, often denoted as
p(N)
. For instance,
p(4)=5
because there are five different ways to partition 4:
1+1+1+1
,
1+1+2
,
2+2
,
3+1
, and
4
. Under the condition that the conformal weight
h
of the primary state and the central charge
c
satisfy

c>1\,,\qquad h\geq\frac{p^{2}}{k}\ ,\qquad\text{with}\>k<0\,,

(126)

the contribution of the Virasoro descendants is

\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}p(N)\,q^{N}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{nk}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}\,,

(127)

like for CFTs.
When the primary state is the vacuum
|p,0\rangle
, the charge
L_{-1}
acts on the vacuum as

L_{-1}^{(s)}|p,0\rangle=0\,,

(128)

implying a state of vanishing norm that we need to exclude from the partition function by starting the product at
n=2
. This can be summarized as

\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}\left(1-\delta_{\text{vac}}\,q\right)\,,

(129)

where
\delta_{\text{vac}}=1
for the vacuum and 0 otherwise.

For the
U(1)
descendants, we need to separate the discussion into two cases. If the charge
p
is real, the
P_{n}
are Hermitian and the
U(1)
descendants have two distinct norms, after an appropriate normalization:

\left|\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{-k}^{n_{k}}|p,h\rangle\right|^{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}+1\quad\text{if}\>\sum_{k}n_{k}\,\text{is even}\,,\\
-1\quad\text{if}\>\sum_{k}n_{k}\,\text{is odd}\,.\end{array}\right.

(130)

The action of the
P_{-n}

Homology with the theme of Matlis
Mohsen Asgharzadeh and Elham Mahdavi

Abstract
. Matlis proved a lot of homological properties of the fraction field of an integral domain. In this paper, we simplify and extend some of them from 1-dimensional (resp. rank one) cases to the higher dimensional (resp. finite rank) cases. For example, we study the weakly cotorsion property of Ext ⁡ ( − , ∼ ) \operatorname{Ext}(-,\sim) , and use it to present splitting criteria. These are equipped with several applications. For instance, we compute the projective dimension of R ^ \widehat{R} and present some non-noetherian versions of Grothendieck’s localization problem. We construct a new class of co-Hopfian modules and extend Matlis’ decomposability problem to higher ranks. In particular, this paper deals with the basic properties of Matlis’ quadric ( Q , Q / R , R ^ , R ∼ ) . (Q,Q/R,\widehat{R},\overset{\sim}{R}).

2.
Weakly co-torsion

We start by recalling:

Definition 2.1
.

(Matlis)
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an integral domain with field of fraction
Q
, and
M
be a nonzero
R
-module.

(i)

M
is
h
-reduced if
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{0}(Q,M)=0
.

(ii)

M
is called weakly cotorsion if
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,M)=0
.

M
is called weakly cotorsion if
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,M)=0
.

(iii)

M
is called cotorsion if
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{0}(Q,M)=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,M)=0
.

(iv)

M
is called strongly cotorsion if
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(Q,M)=0
for all
i>0
.

By
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(-)
(resp.
\operatorname{id}_{R}(-)
) we mean projective dimension (resp. injective dimension).

Remark 2.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an integral domain. Matlis proved that if
\dim(R)=1
, then
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(Q)=1
. Also, he proved the following results:
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be local ring. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q/R,B)
is weakly cotorsion and
h
-reduced.

(ii)

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(Q)=1
.

(iii)

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(Q/R)=1
.

Notation 2.3
.

Let
E_{R}(-)
be the injective envelope of
(-)
.

Notation 2.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be local. The notation
(-)^{\vee}
stands for the Matlis functor defined by
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,E_{R}(k))
.

Observation 2.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local integral domain and
M
and
N
are finitely generated
R
-modules. Then
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)
is weakly cotorsion.

Proof.

Let
E:=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)
and recall that
E
is finitely generated
R
-module. By Matlis duality
E=E^{\vee\vee}
, then

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,E)=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,(E^{\vee})^{\vee})\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(Q,E^{\vee})^{\vee}.

As
Q
is flat
R
-module,
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(Q,-)=0
and so
E
is weakly cotorsion.
∎

Observation 2.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a 1-dimensional local integral domain and
\operatorname{id}(N)<\infty
and
N
be of finite Bass number. Let
M
be an
R
-module. Then
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)
is weakly cotorsion.

Proof.

We set

\eta:0\longrightarrow N\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}E(N)\longrightarrow\Omega^{-1}N\longrightarrow 0.

Without loss of generality, we assume
N
is not injective. Since
N
is not injective then
\operatorname{id}(N)=1
.
We know
\Omega^{-1}N
is injective. By Matlis’ decomposition theory

\Omega^{-1}N:=\underset{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)}{\oplus}E(R/\mathfrak{p})^{\mu(\mathfrak{p},N)}.

Recall that
\operatorname{Spec}(R)=\{0,\mathfrak{m}\}
,
so

\Omega^{-1}N=E(R)^{{m}_{1}}\oplus E(R/\mathfrak{m})^{{m}_{2}}=Q^{{m}_{1}}\oplus E(R/\mathfrak{m})^{{m}_{2}}

where
m_{i}<\infty
. Now apply
\operatorname{Hom}(M,-)
to the sequence
\eta
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\Omega^{-1}N)\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,E(N)).

Let
K:=\ker(\psi)
, then

0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\Omega^{-1}N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)\longrightarrow 0.

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,-)
to the above sequence, we have

\Delta:\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\Omega^{-1}N))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(Q,K).

In view of
[
13
, Theorem 4.2]

\operatorname{pd}(Q)=1
. This shows that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(Q,K)=0
.
Now it is enough to show that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\Omega^{-1}N))=0
.
First we analyze that
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\Omega^{-1}N)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\Omega^{-1}N)&=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,Q)^{\oplus{m}_{1}}\oplus\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E(R/\mathfrak{m}))^{\oplus{m}_{2}}\\
&=(\oplus Q)\oplus(\oplus\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E(R/\mathfrak{m}))\\
&=(\oplus Q)\oplus(\oplus M^{\vee}).\end{array}

Now

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\Omega^{-1}N))=\oplus\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,Q)\oplus(\oplus\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,M^{\vee}).

Recall that
Q
is injective. This implies that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,Q)=0
.
Since
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,M^{\vee})\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(Q,M^{\vee})
, and due to the flatness of
Q
, it is zero.
∎

Let us extend the previous observation:

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an integral domain and
M
and
N
be two
R
-modules such that
\operatorname{id}_{R}(N)\leq 1
. Then
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)
is weakly cotorsion.

Proof.

Suppose that
\eta:0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow E^{0}\longrightarrow E^{1}\longrightarrow 0
is an injective resolution of
N
.
Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,-)
to
\eta
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E^{1})\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,E^{0}).

Let
K:=\ker(\psi)
, then the following sequence

\Phi:0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E^{1})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)\longrightarrow 0,

is exact.
Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,-)
to
\Phi
, induces the following exact sequence:

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E^{1}))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(Q,K).

We know
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E^{1}))=0
, so the desired claim holds if one can show that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(Q,K)=0
.
Let the following exact sequence

\Psi:0\longrightarrow M^{\vee_{E_{0}}}\longrightarrow M^{\vee_{E_{1}}}\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow 0,

where
\vee_{i}(-):=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,E_{i})
.
Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,-)
to
\Psi
yields that the following sequence

\Delta:\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(Q,M^{\vee_{E_{1}}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(Q,K)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{3}(Q,M^{\vee_{E_{0}}}),

is exact. Now, recall that for
i>0

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(Q,M^{\vee_{E_{i}}})\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(Q,M)^{\vee_{E_{i}}}.

Since
Q
is flat,
\operatorname{Tor}_{+}^{R}(Q,M)^{\vee_{E_{i}}}=0
.
Apply this along with
\Delta
, then
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(Q,K)=0
. From this,
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N))=0
as claimed.
∎

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})

and

Let us recover one of delicate result of Matlis
[
14
, 10.4]
via an elementary calculation:

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a countable integral domain. Let
M
be torsion
R
-module and
\operatorname{id}(N)<\infty
. Then
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)
is weakly cotorsion for
i=0,1
. In fact,

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{>0}(Q,\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))=0.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n:=\operatorname{id}(N)<\infty
. The case
n=0
is clear: In view of
[
15
, A.11]
we have

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\emph{injective}))=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(Q,M),\emph{injective})\quad(\ast)

Since
Q
is flat, the corresponding
\operatorname{Tor}
is zero. By
(\ast)
,
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N))=0
. Also,
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(M,N))=0,
as
N
is injective.
We may and do assume
n>0
.
Now, let

\eta:0\longrightarrow N\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}E^{0}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Coker}(\varphi)\longrightarrow 0,

where
E^{0}:=E_{R}(N)
and let
E_{1}:=\operatorname{Coker}(\varphi)
.
Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(M,-)
to
\eta
, we have

\ldots\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{1})\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,E^{0}).

Since
E^{0}
is injective, we have
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,E^{0})=0
. Let
K:=\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
. We have the following exact sequence

\Psi:0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{1})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)\longrightarrow 0.

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,-)
to
\Psi
yields that

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{1}))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(Q,K).

Since
R
is countable, the flat module
Q
is as well. This implies that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(Q)=1
and consequently,
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(Q,K)=0
.
Since
\operatorname{id}_{R}(E_{1})=\operatorname{id}_{R}(N)-1
, and by applying the inductive hypothesis, we may assume that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}(M,E_{1}))=0
.
So,

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N))=0\quad(+)

Now, we show that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{0}(M,N)
is cotorsion.
Indeed, we show that the following sequence

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E^{0})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{1})\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow 0

is exact.
Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,-)
to
\eta

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E^{0})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{1})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,E^{0}).

We break down it into two short exact sequences

a)

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E^{0})\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0
and

b)

0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{1})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)
.

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,-)
to the exact sequence
b)
yields that

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{1})).

Recall that
M
is torsion. This gives
Q\otimes M=0
. So,

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{1}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q\otimes M,E_{1})=0.

By plugging this in the previous sequence,
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,L)=0
.
Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,-)
to the exact sequence displaced in
a)
, yields that

0=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E^{0}))\quad(\ast\ast)

Since

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,E^{0}))\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(Q,M)^{\vee_{E^{0}}}

and that
Q
is flat, we observe that
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(Q,M)
is zero.
In view of
(\ast\ast)
we conclude that

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N))=0\quad(+,+)

By
(+)+(++)
we get the desired claim. The particular case is trivial. Indeed,
since
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(Q)=1
then
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{\geq 2}(Q,-)=0
. The proof is now complete.
∎

3.
An application: the splitting result

We need the following three standard lemmas:

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
R
is an integral domain and
I
is an injective and
M
any
R
-module. Then
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(I,M)
is torsion-free.

Proof.

Let
r\in R
be such that
\exists f:I\longrightarrow M
and that
r.f=0
. Suppose
f
is not zero, then there exists
x\in I
such that
f(x)\neq 0
. We know injective modules over an integral domain are divisible, so there exists
y\in I
such that
x=ry
.
Now

f(x)=f(ry)=rf(y)=(rf)(y)=0,

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is 1-dimensional and
M
is a finitely generated weakly cotorsion
R
-module. Suppose in addition that

a)

\operatorname{id}(M)<\infty
and

b)

N
is any torsion-free module.

Then
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(N,M)=0
.

Proof.

Since
N
is torsion-free then
\mathfrak{m}\notin\operatorname{Ass}(N)
. Let

N\hookrightarrow E(N)=\oplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(N)}E(R/\mathfrak{p})^{\mu(\mathfrak{p},N)}.

Since
\mathfrak{m}\notin\operatorname{Ass}(N)
and
\operatorname{Spec}(R)=\{0,\mathfrak{m}\}
then
\operatorname{Ass}(N)=\{0\}
. Now

N\hookrightarrow E(N)=\oplus Q^{t},

where
t:=\mu(0,N)
.
Let
0\longrightarrow N\overset{\rho}{\longrightarrow}E(N)\longrightarrow C:=\operatorname{Coker}(\rho)\longrightarrow 0
. Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(-,M)
to it, yields the following exact sequence

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(E(N),M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(N,M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(C,M)\quad(\ast)

Since
M
is finite and of finite injective dimension, we deduce that
\operatorname{id}(M)\leq 0pt(R)=1
. By this,

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(C,M)=0.
Recall that
M
is weakly co-torsion, so

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(E(N),M)=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(\oplus_{t}Q,M)=\prod_{t}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(Q,M)=0.

Putting these in
(\ast)
, we conclude that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(N,M)=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local integral domain with fraction field
Q
. Then
Q/R
is torsion.

Proof.

Let
\alpha+R\in Q/R
be non-zero. then
\alpha=r/s
where
r\in R
and
s\in R\setminus\{0\}
.
Since
\alpha+R\neq 0
, then
\alpha\notin R
and
s\in\mathfrak{m}
. (any element of
R\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
is invertible). Now

\begin{array}[]{ll}s.(\alpha+R)&=(s\alpha)+R\\
&=(s.r/s)+R\\
&=r+R\\
&=0.\end{array}

By definition,
Q/R
is torsion.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
0\to M\to L\to N\to 0
be an exact sequence of finitely generated modules where
N
and
M
are as Lemma
3.2
. Then
L=N\oplus M
.

Proof.

Apply
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(N,-)
to

(R,\mathfrak{m})

In

17

Discussion 9.1
.

Adopt the above notation. Then
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)\cong Q/S.

Proof.

Apply
-\otimes_{R}S
to

gives us the following diagram:

\begin{CD}0=\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(Q,S)@>{}>{}>\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(Q/R,S)@>{}>{}>R\otimes_{R}S@>{}>{}>Q\otimes_{R}S@>{}>{}>Q/R\otimes_{R}S@>{}>{}>0\\
@A{}A{}A\cong @A{}A{}A=@A{}A{}Af@A{}A{}A\\
0@>{}>{}>S@>{}>{}>Q@>{}>{}>Q/S@>{}>{}>0\\
\end{CD}

On the one hand
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(Q/R,S)\subseteq R\otimes_{R}S=S
is torsion-free. On the other hand any higher tor is torsion. So,
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(Q/R,S)=0
. By 5-lemma,

H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)=H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\otimes_{R}S\cong Q/R\otimes_{R}S\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\cong}}Q/S,

as claimed.

.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local domain of dimension one, and let

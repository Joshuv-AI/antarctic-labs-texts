RELLICH TYPE INEQUALITIES ON THE HALF LINE IN THE FRAMEWORK OF EQUALITIES


Abstract
We establish in this paper some weighted Hardy and Rellich type inequalities on the half line in the framework of equalities, extending recent results proved by Machihara-Ozawa-Wadade and Bez-Machihara-Ozawa. In particular, the one-dimensional classical Rellich inequality is proved in the framework of equality.

Introduction and main results

Let
n\geq 2
. It is well-known that the standard Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
decomposes as

\Delta=\Delta_{r}+\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}},

where
\Delta_{r}
denotes the radial Laplacian and
\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
denotes the Laplacian-Beltrami operator on the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. More precisely, the radial Laplacian is given by

\Delta_{r}f=\partial_{r}^{2}f+\frac{n-1}{|x|}\partial_{r}f,

where
\partial_{r}
denotes the radial derivative
\partial_{r}=\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nabla
.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denote the
L^{2}
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to Lebesgue measure. Let
{\mathbf{R}}_{Q}=\frac{Q(Q-4)}{4}
, where
Q\in\mathbb{R}
. The following “Rellich type equalities” were recently proved by Machihara-Ozawa-Wadade in
[
3
]
and Bez-Machihara-Ozawa in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Bez-Machihara-Ozawa)
.

For
n\geq 2
and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
we have

(1)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}_{n}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\Delta_{r}f\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|\Delta_{r}f+{\mathbf{R}}_{n}\frac{f}{|x|^{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-2{\mathbf{R}}_{n}\left\|\frac{f^{*}}{|x|}\right\|_{2}^{2}

(2)

\displaystyle=\|\Delta_{r}f\|_{2}^{2}-\left(1+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{R}}_{n}\right)\left\|\Delta_{r}f+{\mathbf{R}}_{n}\frac{f}{|x|^{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{R}}_{n}\left\|f^{\#}\right\|_{2}^{2},

where
f^{*}=\partial_{r}f+\frac{n-4}{2|x|}f
and
f^{\#}=\partial_{r}^{2}f+(n-3)\frac{\partial_{r}f}{|x|}+\frac{(n-4)^{2}}{4}\frac{f}{|x|^{2}}
.

Note that the above results can be in fact rephrased on the ray
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=(0,\infty)
since they only involve the radial derivative. The original equalities (
1
) and (
2
) then follow from the polar decomposition formula and the radially reduced equalities (with
L^{2}(r^{n-1}{\mathrm{d}}r)
-norm on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
). Moreover, in this radially reduced one-dimensional setting, we can consider weighted analogues. Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and denote by
\|\cdot\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}
the following weighted
L^{2}
-norm:

\|f\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(r)|^{2}r^{\beta}\mathrm{d}r.

Associated to
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we also consider the weighted second order derivative

{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}:=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{r}\partial_{r}.

Obviously
{\mathfrak{L}}_{n-1}=\Delta_{r}
.

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, let

{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{(\beta-3)(2\alpha-\beta+1)}{4}.

For
{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}
, we have the following analogues of results obtained in
[
3
]
and
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
f\in\mathbb{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
there hold

(3)

{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}=\left\|{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}f\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}f+{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}-2{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}\left\|\frac{f^{*}}{r}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}

and, if
\beta-\alpha-2\neq 0
,

(4)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}f\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}-\left(1+\frac{2{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}}{\left(\beta-\alpha-2\right)^{2}}\right)\left\|{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}f+{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}}{\left(\beta-\alpha-2\right)^{2}}\left\|f^{\#}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2},

where
f^{*}=f^{\prime}+\frac{\beta-3}{2r}f
and
f^{\#}=f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\beta-2}{r}f^{\prime}+\frac{(\beta-3)^{2}}{4r^{2}}f.

Notice that
{\mathbf{C}}_{n-1,n-1}={\mathbf{R}}_{n}
, therefore Theorem 1.1 is a direct consequence of Theorem 1.2 when
\alpha=\beta=n-1
, that is,

(5)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}_{n}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{n-1}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathfrak{L}}_{n-1}f\|_{L_{n-1}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|{\mathfrak{L}}_{n-1}f+{\mathbf{R}}_{n}\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{n-1}^{2}}^{2}-2{\mathbf{R}}_{n}\left\|\frac{f^{*}}{r}\right\|_{L_{n-1}^{2}}^{2}

(6)

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathfrak{L}}_{n-1}f\right\|_{L_{n-1}^{2}}^{2}-\left(1+\frac{{\mathbf{R}}_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|{\mathfrak{L}}_{n-1}f+{\mathbf{R}}_{n}\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{n-1}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{{\mathbf{R}}_{n}}{2}\left\|f^{\#}\right\|_{L_{n-1}^{2}}^{2}

To recover (
1
)-(
2
) from (
5
)-(
6
), it suffices to use polar coordinates

(r,\omega)=\left(|x|,\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

combined with integration over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and Fubini theorem.

The equalities (
3
) and (
4
) imply the following “Rellich type inequalities”.

Theorem 1.3
.

For all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, there holds

(7)

{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{{L_{\beta}^{2}}}^{2}\leq\|{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}f\|_{{L_{\beta}^{2}}}^{2}.

For
n\geq 2
, by taking
\alpha=\beta=n-1
in Theorem 1.3 and using polar coordinates and Fubini theorem as indicated above, we get the higher dimensional results in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.4

(Bez-Machihara-Ozawa)
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
, there holds

{\mathbf{R}}_{n}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\Delta_{r}f\|_{2}^{2}.

Remark 1.5
.

There is a norm comparsion between
\Delta_{r}
and
\Delta
: if
n\geq 3
, then

\|\Delta_{r}f\|_{2}\leq\|\Delta f\|_{2}

holds for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
. This combined with Theorem 1.4 gives a proof of classical Rellich inequalities (see
[
5
,
6
]
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
in the framework of equalities. The one-dimensional Rellich inequality is included in our Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.3.

2
Proof of Theorem 1.2

Proof of (
3
).

If
\beta=3
, the equality trivially holds. So we assume
\beta\neq 3
in the following. Denote the Hermitian product with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}
by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
. Using integration by parts twice with respect to
\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}
we have

(8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|f(r)|^{2}}{r^{4}}r^{\beta}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)\overline{f(r)}r^{\beta-4}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\beta-3}\Re\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)\overline{f^{\prime}(r)}r^{\beta-3}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(\beta-2)(\beta-3)}\Re\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}+f(r)\overline{f^{\prime\prime}(r)}\right)r^{\beta-2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(\beta-2)(\beta-3)}\left(\left\|\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}+\Re\left\langle\frac{f}{r^{2}},{f^{\prime\prime}}\right\rangle\right).

We used
\beta\neq 2
in the third step and we will give details for the case
\beta=2
later.

The first term in the last expression on RHS of (
8
) is rewritten as

(9)

\left\|\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}=\left\|\left(\frac{f}{r}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}=\left\|\left(\frac{f}{r}\right)^{\prime}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}+2\Re\left\langle\left(\frac{f}{r}\right)^{\prime},\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\rangle+\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}.

We apply Lemma 2.2 below with
f
replaced by
\frac{f}{r}
to obtain

\left\|\left(\frac{f}{r}\right)^{\prime}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}={\mathbf{H}}_{\beta+1}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{{\mathbf{H}}_{\beta+1}-1}{r^{2}}f\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}.

An integration by parts gives

2\Re\left\langle\left(\frac{f}{r}\right)^{\prime},\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{|f|^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)^{\prime}}{r}r^{\beta}\mathrm{d}r=-(\beta-1)\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}.

Substituting above two equalities into (
9
) leads to

\left\|\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}=({\mathbf{H}}_{\beta+1}-1)^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\left(\frac{f}{r}\right)^{\prime}+{\mathbf{H}}_{\beta+1}\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}.

The second term in the last expression on RHS of (
8
) is rewritten as

(10)

\displaystyle\Re\left\langle\frac{f}{r^{2}},{f^{\prime\prime}}\right\rangle=\Re\left\langle\frac{f}{r^{2}},{f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\alpha}{r}f^{\prime}}\right\rangle-\alpha\Re\left\langle\frac{f}{r^{3}},{f^{\prime}}\right\rangle,

where the last term is given by

\Re\left\langle\frac{f}{r^{3}},{f^{\prime}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\Re\left\langle\frac{1}{r^{3}},{(f\bar{f})^{\prime}}\right\rangle=-\frac{(\beta-3)}{2}\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}.

If
2\alpha-\beta+1=0
, then
{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}=0
and (
3
) holds trivially. If not, then
{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}\neq 0
, hence we can gather the equalities (
9
) and (
10
) and get

\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}=-\frac{1}{{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\Re\left\langle\frac{f}{r^{2}},{f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\alpha}{r}f^{\prime}}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\left\|\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}+({\mathbf{H}}_{\beta+1}-1)\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}.

By Lemma
2.1
below, we get the desired equality.

For
\beta=2
, one can verify that

\Re\left\langle\frac{f}{r^{2}},{f^{\prime\prime}}\right\rangle=\Re\left\langle\frac{f}{r},\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}\right\rangle=\left\|\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}\right\|_{L_{2}^{2}}^{2},

and thus by similar arguments we complete the proof of (
3
).∎

For the completeness of the arguments we record the mentioned lemmas below.

Lemma 2.1

(Machihara-Ozawa-Wadade)
.

Let
\mathsf{H}
be a vector space with Hermitian scalar product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
. Let
a\in\mathbb{R},c\neq 0
and
u,v\in\mathsf{H}
. Then

\|u\|^{2}=-c\Re\left\langle u,v\right\rangle+a\Leftrightarrow\frac{1}{c^{2}}\|u\|^{2}=\|v\|^{2}-\left\|v+\frac{1}{c}u\right\|^{2}+\frac{2a}{c^{2}}.

The proof of this lemma is straightforward, noting that since
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
is Hermitian,

\left\langle f-g,f-g\right\rangle=\|f\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\|g\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}-2\Re\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\|f\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\|g\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}-2\Re\left\langle g,f\right\rangle.

Let
{\mathbf{H}}_{Q}=\frac{Q-2}{2}
, where
Q\in\mathbb{R}
.
The following can be viewed as a reduction to radial functions of the Hardy inequalities proved in
[
4
]
in the framework of equalities.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
there holds

(11)

{\mathbf{H}}_{\beta+1}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{r}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}=\left\|f^{\prime}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|f^{\prime}+{\mathbf{H}}_{\beta+1}\frac{f}{r}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}.

Proof..

Note that the lemma when
\beta=1
holds trivially. For
\beta\neq 1
, let
u=-\frac{2}{\beta-1}f^{\prime}
and
v=\frac{f}{r}
. Using integration by parts as before, we obtain the expression

\left\|v\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}=\Re\int_{0}^{\infty}v\bar{u}r^{\beta}{\mathrm{d}}r.

Then

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|v\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|u\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|v\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}+2\Re\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(|v|^{2}-v\bar{u}\right)r^{\beta}{\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|v-u|^{2}r^{\beta}{\mathrm{d}}r,

which gives the desired equality.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, by taking
\beta=t+2
in (
11
), we obtain the weighted one-dimensional Hardy inequality (see, for example, Cassano and Pizzichillo
[
2
]
)

\int_{0}^{\infty}|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}r^{t+2}{\mathrm{d}}r\geq\left(\frac{t+1}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(r)|^{2}r^{t}{\mathrm{d}}r.

As a consequence, we have the weighted Rellich inequality

\int_{0}^{\infty}|f^{\prime\prime}(r)|^{2}r^{t+4}{\mathrm{d}}r\geq\left(\frac{t+3}{2}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{t+1}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(r)|^{2}r^{t}{\mathrm{d}}r.

Proof of (
4
).

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we start by observing that

(12)

\frac{(\beta-1-a)^{2}}{4}\left\|\frac{g}{r^{1+\frac{a}{2}}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}=\left\|\frac{g^{\prime}}{r^{\frac{a}{2}}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\frac{\beta-1-a}{2}\frac{g}{r^{1+\frac{a}{2}}}+\frac{g^{\prime}}{r^{\frac{a}{2}}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}

holds for all
g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Indeed, this follows by using integration by parts to see that

2\Re\left\langle\frac{g}{r^{1+\frac{a}{2}}},\frac{g^{\prime}}{r^{\frac{a}{2}}}\right\rangle=-(\beta-1-a)\left\|\frac{g}{r^{1+\frac{a}{2}}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}.

If
\beta-1-a=0
, the equality trivially holds. By using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain (
12
).

Now taking
a=2\alpha-\beta+3
and
g
given by

g(r)=\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}+({\mathbf{H}}_{\beta+1}-1)\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right)r^{\frac{2\alpha-\beta+5}{2}},

we obtain that for
\beta-\alpha-2\neq 0
,

(13)

\left\|\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{\beta-3}{2r^{2}}f\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{(\beta-\alpha-2)^{2}}\left\|{\mathfrak{L}}_{\alpha}f+{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{(\beta-\alpha-2)^{2}}\left\|f^{\#}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2},

where
f^{\#}=f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\beta-2}{r}f^{\prime}+\frac{(\beta-3)^{2}}{4r^{2}}f.
We see that (
4
) follows from (
3
) and (
13
).
∎

3
Proof of Theorem 1.3

If
{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}\geq 0
, then (
7
) follows from (
3
). For
\beta-\alpha-2=0
, we have

{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{(\beta-3)^{2}}{4}.

Therefore, if
{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}<0
, then
\beta-\alpha-2\neq 0
. We can use the expression (
4
) for
{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}}^{2}
in Theorem 1.2. A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2{\mathbf{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}}{\left(\beta-\alpha-2\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2\alpha\beta-\beta^{2}+4\beta-6\alpha-3+(\beta-(\alpha+2))^{2}}{2\left(\beta-\alpha-2\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{2\left(\beta-\alpha-2\right)^{2}}>0,

thus (
7
) follows. This completes the proof.

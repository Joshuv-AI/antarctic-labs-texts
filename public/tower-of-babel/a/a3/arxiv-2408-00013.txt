Before briefly describing the content of each section, we next describe the essence of the factorization method with the help of the simplest possible illustration in the power weighted Hardy inequality context (for more details see Remark
2.9
): Given
\alpha,\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
, one introduces the two-parameter family of homogeneous vector-valued differential expressions

(1.10)

T_{\alpha,\gamma}:=|x|^{\gamma/2}\big[\nabla+\alpha|x|^{-2}x\big],\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

with formal adjoint, denoted by
T_{\alpha,\gamma}^{+}
,

(1.11)

T_{\alpha,\gamma}^{+}=|x|^{\gamma/2}\big[-{\rm div(\,\cdot\,)}+\big(\alpha-2^{-1}\gamma\big)|x|^{-2}x\,\cdot\big],\quad\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

such that

(1.12)

T_{\alpha,\gamma}^{+}T_{\alpha,\gamma}=-|x|^{\gamma}\Delta-\gamma|x|^{\gamma-2}x\cdot\nabla+\alpha(\alpha+2-n-\gamma)|x|^{\gamma-2}.

Thus, for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,

(1.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\leq&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|(T_{\alpha,\gamma}f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\overline{f(x)}(T_{\alpha,\gamma}^{+}T_{\alpha,\gamma}f)(x)\,d^{n}x\\
=&-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}\overline{f(x)}(\Delta f)(x)\,d^{n}x-\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}\overline{f(x)}[x\cdot(\nabla f)(x)]\,d^{n}x\\
&+\alpha(\alpha+2-n-\gamma)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}|f(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x.\end{split}

Standard integration by parts, substituting into (
1.13
), rearranging terms, and maximizing with respect to
\alpha
yields the sharp weighted Hardy inequality,

(1.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq[(n-2+\gamma)/2]^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}|f(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x,&\\
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),\;n\geq 2,\;\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\{2-n\}.&\end{split}

We will employ the idea of factorizations in terms of homogeneous vector-valued differential expressions also in the more general case of Rellich-type inequalities, but with an additional twist: In order to arrive at sharp inequalities it will be necessary to involve an operator-valued coefficient involving the Laplace–Beltrami operator

-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1};d^{n-1}\omega)
, see, for instance, (
3.7
). While essentially all approaches to Rellich and Hardy–Rellich inequalities rely on decompositions into spherical harmonics, what permits us to go beyond existing approaches is our one-dimensional Lemma
A.1
on power weighted Hardy and Rellich inequalities (a simplified version of
[
51
, Lemma 2.1]
) that will be applied to the radial variable
r\in(0,\infty)
in Appendix
A
with minimal assumptions regarding
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

In Section
2
we revisit factorizations without the additional spherical term containing
-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
. Following
[
49
]
, we thus derive a first set of inequalities that contains power weighted Rellich, Hardy–Rellich, and Schmincke-type inequalities as special cases. Our novel factorizations including the additional spherical term are then the subject of Section
3
and once again we derive an improved set of inequalities that contains power weighted Rellich and Schmincke-type inequalities as special cases. A sharp power weighted Hardy–Rellich inequality is then derived in Appendix
A
.

2.
Factorizations and Hardy–Rellich-type Inequalities

Our main result for this section is the following theorem which is an extension of

f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
(infinitely differentiable continuous functions with compact support and support away from zero) throughout.

Though many of the results proved in this section hold for
n=1
, we will focus on the multidimensional case, that is, we will assume
n\geq 2
. For results in one dimension, we refer the reader to the references cited in
[
50
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}|(\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x

\displaystyle\geq[\alpha(\gamma+n-4)-2\beta]\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x

(2.1)

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha(\alpha-4+2\gamma)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}|x\cdot(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x

\displaystyle\quad+\beta[(n-4)(\alpha-2)-\beta+\gamma(n+\alpha+\gamma-6)]\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}|f(x)|^{2}d^{n}x.

In addition, if
\alpha(\alpha-4+2\gamma)\geq 0
, then,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}|(\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x

\displaystyle\geq[\alpha(n-\alpha-\gamma)-2\beta]\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x

\displaystyle\quad+\beta[(n-4)(\alpha-2)-\beta+\gamma(n+\alpha+\gamma-6)]\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}|f(x)|^{2}d^{n}x.

Proof.

Given
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
, we introduce the three-parameter
n
-dimensional differential expression

(2.3)

\displaystyle T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}=|x|^{\gamma/2}\big[-\Delta+\alpha|x|^{-2}x\cdot\nabla+\beta|x|^{-2}\big],\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

and its formal adjoint, denoted by
T^{+}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T^{+}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}=|x|^{\gamma/2}\big\{&-\Delta-(\alpha+\gamma)|x|^{-2}x\cdot\nabla\\
&+\big[\beta-\alpha\big(n-2\big)-2^{-1}\big(2^{-1}\gamma+\alpha+n-2\big)\gamma\big]|x|^{-2}\big\},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.\end{split}

Assuming
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
throughout the proof one computes for
T^{+}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
,

\displaystyle\big(T^{+}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}f\big)(x)

\displaystyle=|x|^{\gamma}\big(\Delta^{2}f\big)(x)+\alpha(4-\alpha-2\gamma)|x|^{\gamma-4}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}x_{j}x_{k}f_{x_{j},x_{k}}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+[\alpha(\gamma+n-4)-2\beta+\gamma(\gamma+n-2)]|x|^{\gamma-2}(\Delta f)(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\big[-(n-3)\alpha^{2}+2(n-2)\alpha+4\beta

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(4\alpha-\alpha\gamma-\alpha^{2}-n\alpha-2\beta)\gamma\big]|x|^{\gamma-4}[x\cdot(\nabla f)(x)]

\displaystyle\quad+\big[\beta^{2}+(n-4)(2\beta-\alpha\beta)-(n+\alpha+\gamma-6)\beta\gamma\big]|x|^{\gamma-4}f(x)

(2.5)

\displaystyle\quad+2\gamma|x|^{\gamma-2}x\cdot(\nabla(\Delta f))(x).

One notes that for
\gamma=0
the last term of (
2.5
) vanishes.

Choosing (w.l.o.g., see Remark
1.1
)
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
to be real-valued and integrating by parts (observing the support properties of
f
, resulting in vanishing surface terms) implies

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}[(T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}f)(x)]^{2}\,d^{n}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(x)\big(T^{+}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}f\big)(x)\,d^{n}x

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}f(x)\big(\Delta^{2}f\big)(x)\,d^{n}x+\alpha(4-\alpha-2\gamma)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}f(x)\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}x_{j}x_{k}f_{x_{j},x_{k}}(x)\,d^{n}x

\displaystyle\quad+[\alpha(\gamma+n-4)-2\beta+\gamma(\gamma+n-2)]\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}f(x)(\Delta f)(x)\,d^{n}x

\displaystyle\quad+\big[-(n-3)\alpha^{2}+2(n-2)\alpha+4\beta

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(4\alpha-\alpha\gamma-\alpha^{2}-n\alpha-2\beta)\gamma\big]\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}f(x)[x\cdot(\nabla f)(x)]\,d^{n}x

\displaystyle\quad+\big[\beta^{2}+(n-4)(2\beta-\alpha\beta)-(n+\alpha+\gamma-6)\beta\gamma\big]\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}|f(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x

(2.6)

\displaystyle\quad+2\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}f(x)[x\cdot(\nabla(\Delta f))(x)]\,d^{n}x.

We now make a few observations in order to simplify (
2.6
). First, standard integration by parts yields

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}f(x)\big(\Delta^{2}f\big)(x)\,d^{n}x&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}|(\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\\
&\quad-\gamma(\gamma+n-2)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}f(x)(\Delta f)(x)\,d^{n}x\\
&\quad-2\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}f(x)[x\cdot(\nabla(\Delta f))(x)]\,d^{n}x,\end{split}

and

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}f(x)(\Delta f)(x)\,d^{n}x&=(2-\gamma)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}f(x)[x\cdot(\nabla f)(x)]\,d^{n}x\\
&\quad-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x.\end{split}

Similarly, one obtains

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}f(x)x_{j}x_{k}f_{x_{j},x_{k}}(x)\,d^{n}x&=(3-n-\gamma)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}f(x)[x\cdot(\nabla f)(x)]\,d^{n}x\\
&\quad-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}|x\cdot(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x.\end{split}

Combining (
2.6
)-(
2.9
) and simplifying yields (
2.1
).

To arrive at inequality (
2.2
), note by Cauchy’s inequality,

(2.10)

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}|x\cdot(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x,

one concludes as long as
\alpha(\alpha+2\gamma-4)\geq 0
the inequality (
2.1
) can be further estimated from below.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

\gamma=0
, the inequalities (

) and (

) coincide with those found in

, and hence contain all the special cases included therein as well.

2.2
) one obtains a power weighted Rellich inequality as follows:

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

Remark 2.9

The factorization approach was employed in the context of the classical Hardy inequality in
[

\alpha,\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
, one introduces the two-parameter family of homogeneous vector-valued differential expressions

(2.29)

T_{\alpha,\gamma}:=|x|^{\gamma/2}\big[\nabla+\alpha|x|^{-2}x\big],\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

with formal adjoint, denoted by
T_{\alpha,\gamma}^{+}
,

(2.30)

T_{\alpha,\gamma}^{+}=|x|^{\gamma/2}\big[-{\rm div(\,\cdot\,)}+\big(\alpha-2^{-1}\gamma\big)|x|^{-2}x\,\cdot\big],\quad\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

such that

(2.31)

T_{\alpha,\gamma}^{+}T_{\alpha,\gamma}=-|x|^{\gamma}\Delta-\gamma|x|^{\gamma-2}x\cdot\nabla+\alpha(\alpha+2-n-\gamma)|x|^{\gamma-2}.

Thus, for

(2.32)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\leq&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|(T_{\alpha,\gamma}f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\overline{f(x)}(T_{\alpha,\gamma}^{+}T_{\alpha,\gamma}f)(x)\,d^{n}x\\

By standard integration by parts,

(2.33)

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}\overline{f(x)}(\Delta f)(x)\,d^{n}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x+\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}\overline{f(x)}[x\cdot(\nabla f)(x)]\,d^{n}x

and hence substituting into (

) and rearranging terms yields

(2.34)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq\alpha(n-2+\gamma-\alpha)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}|f(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x.

\alpha(n-2+\gamma-\alpha)
with respect to
\alpha
yields the weighted Hardy inequality,

(2.35)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq[(n-2+\gamma)/2]^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}|f(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x,&\\
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),\;n\geq 2,\;\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\{2-n\}.&\end{split}

) is optimal (cf., e.g.,
[

]
).

Remark 2.10

Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta=-r^{1-n}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}r^{n-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}-\frac{n-1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}.

Our extension of Theorem
2.1

Theorem 3.1

Before proving Theorem
3.1
, we recall some standard facts and prove a few needed general results.
For
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, let

(3.4)

\lambda_{j}=j(j+n-2),\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

be the eigenvalues of

, that is,
\sigma(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})=\{j(j+n-2)\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, of multiplicity

m(\lambda_{j})=(2j+n-2)(j+n-2)^{-1}{j+n-2\choose n-2},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

with corresponding eigenfunctions
\varphi_{j,\ell},\ j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ \ell\in\{1,\dots,m(\lambda_{j})\}
. We may (and will) assume that

is an orthonormal basis of

, and let

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{f,j,\ell}(r)=(\varphi_{j,\ell},f(r,\,\cdot\,))_{L^{2}({{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}};d^{n-1}\omega)}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\overline{\varphi_{j,\ell}(\theta)}f(r,\theta)\,d^{n-1}\omega(\theta),&\\

Given
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}},\ n\geq 2
, we now introduce the four-parameter
n
-dimensional differential expression

(3.7)

T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\tau}=T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}+\tau|x|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-2}(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

and its formal adjoint, denoted by

,

(3.8)

T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\tau}^{+}=T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{+}+\tau|x|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-2}(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

In polar coordinates, one has for
r\in(0,\infty)
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\tau}=r^{\gamma/2}\bigg\{-r^{-(n-1)}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bigg(r^{n-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bigg)+\alpha r^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\beta r^{-2}+(1+\tau)r^{-2}(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})\bigg\},

and

(3.10)

\displaystyle T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\tau}^{+}=r^{\gamma/2}\bigg\{

\displaystyle-r^{-(n-1)}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bigg(r^{n-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bigg)-(\alpha+\gamma)r^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}

\displaystyle+\big[\beta-\alpha\big(n-2\big)-\big(2^{-1}\gamma+\alpha+n-2\big)2^{-1}\gamma\big]r^{-2}+(1+\tau)r^{-2}(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})\bigg\}.

2.1

. We first prove a few technical lemmas that will be used to derive identities and estimates employed in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. The first result shows that the square root of the Laplace–Beltrami operator nearly commutes with the radial partial derivative.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
. Then for all
r_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, one has

(3.11)

\frac{d^{k}}{dr^{k}}\big((-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})^{1/2}f(r,\,\cdot\,)\big)(r_{0})=(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})^{1/2}\bigg(\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial r^{k}}(r_{0},\,\cdot\,)\bigg)\in L^{2}({{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}};d^{n-1}\omega).

In particular, for
r\in(0,\infty)
, the function

(3.12)

r\mapsto(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})^{1/2}f(r,\,\cdot\,)\in L^{2}({{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}};d^{n-1}\omega)

lies in
C_{0}^{\infty}\big((0,\infty),L^{2}({{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}};d^{n-1}\omega)\big)
.

Proof.

First, we note that
-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
and
\partial/\partial r
commute on
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
, that is, for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,

(3.13)

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f)=-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\bigg(\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}\bigg)\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).

Hence, for
r_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
real-valued (w.l.o.g., see Remark
1.1
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{-1}\{(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f)(r_{0}+\varepsilon,\,\cdot\,)-(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f)(r_{0},\,\cdot\,)\}

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{-1}\bigg\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}+\varepsilon)\varphi_{j,\ell}-\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0})\varphi_{j,\ell}\bigg\}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}F_{\frac{\partial f}{\partial r},j,\ell}(r_{0})\varphi_{j,\ell},

therefore,

(3.15)

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}^{2}\big|\varepsilon^{-1}(F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}+\varepsilon)-F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}))-F_{\frac{\partial f}{\partial r},j,\ell}(r_{0})\big|^{2}=0.

As
\lambda_{j}\geq 1,\ j\geq 1,
this shows that

(3.16)

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}\big|\varepsilon^{-1}(F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}+\varepsilon)-F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}))-F_{\frac{\partial f}{\partial r},j,\ell}(r_{0})\big|^{2}=0,

implying

(3.17)

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\bigg\|\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\big[\varepsilon^{-1}(F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}+\varepsilon)-F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}))-F_{\frac{\partial f}{\partial r},j,\ell}(r_{0})\big]\varphi_{j,\ell}\bigg\|^{2}_{L^{2}({{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}};d^{n-1}\omega)}=0.

Thus, for
r_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\big((-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})^{1/2}f(r,\,\cdot\,)\big)(r_{0})=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{-1}\{(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})^{1/2}f(r_{0}+\varepsilon,\,\cdot\,)-(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})^{1/2}f(r_{0},\,\cdot\,)\}

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\big[\varepsilon^{-1}(F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}+\varepsilon)-F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}))-F_{\frac{\partial f}{\partial r},j,\ell}(r_{0})\big]\varphi_{j,\ell}

\displaystyle\hskip 102.43008pt+\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}F_{\frac{\partial f}{\partial r},j,\ell}(r_{0})\varphi_{j,\ell}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\big[\varepsilon^{-1}(F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}+\varepsilon)-F_{f,j,\ell}(r_{0}))

\displaystyle\hskip 102.43008pt-F_{\frac{\partial f}{\partial r},j,\ell}(r_{0})\big]\varphi_{j,\ell}+(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})^{1/2}\bigg(\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}(r_{0},\,\cdot\,)\bigg).

Hence, applying (
3.17
), one concludes that

(3.19)

\frac{d}{dr}\big(\big((-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})^{1/2}f\big)(r,\,\cdot\,)\big)(r_{0})=(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})^{1/2}\bigg(\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}(r_{0},\,\cdot\,)\bigg).

The lemma now follows by induction on
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 3.4

and supposed that for all

Thus, if

and if for all
r>0,\ g(r)
is a real-valued function on

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(r^{-(n-1)}(d/dr)\big(r^{n-1}(d/dr)\big(r^{-2}g\big)\big),g\big)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},d^{n-1}\omega)}\,r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\quad=-2\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-3}(g,g^{\prime})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},d^{n-1}\omega)}\,r^{n-1}dr+\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2}(g^{\prime},g^{\prime})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},d^{n-1}\omega)}\,r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle\quad=(n-4)\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-4}(g,g)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},d^{n-1}\omega)}\,r^{n-1}dr+\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2}(g^{\prime},g^{\prime})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},d^{n-1}\omega)}\,r^{n-1}dr.

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}r^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\big\{r^{-(n-1)}(\partial/\partial r)\big[r^{n-1}(\partial/\partial r)

\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash[-8j+2,2]
and

(A.67)

n\leq-2(\gamma-3+j)-2\big[4^{-1}\gamma^{2}+(2j-1)\gamma-4j+1\big]^{1/2}.

Then in Theorem
A.4
,

\displaystyle A_{n,\gamma}

\displaystyle={\min}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\{\alpha_{n,\gamma,\lambda_{j}}\}

(A.68)

\displaystyle={\min}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\,0\leq j\leq j_{0}}\{\alpha_{n,\gamma,\lambda_{j}}\}.

The following classical result now follows as a corollary of Theorem
A.4
and an application of Lemma
A.8
:

Corollary A.10
.

Let
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
. Then

(A.69)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|(\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq A_{n,0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{-2}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x,

where

(A.70)

A_{n,0}=\begin{cases}n^{2}/4,&n\geq 5,\\
3,&n=4,\\
25/36,&n=3,\\
0,&n=2,\end{cases}

and the constant
A_{n,0}
is sharp. In particular, (
A.69
) reduces to a trivial inequality for
n=2
, that is, there is no
A_{2,0}\in(0,\infty)
such that (
A.69
) holds for
n=2
.

Proof.

The result follows from letting
\gamma=0
in Theorem
A.4
(with sharpness as an immediate consequence) and applying Lemma
A.8
. In particular, since
\gamma=0
satisfies Condition
(i)
of Lemma
A.8
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, from (
A.55
) one has

(A.71)

A_{n,0}=\min\{\alpha_{n,0,0},\alpha_{n,0,n-1}\},

where

(A.72)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{n,0,n-1}&=\big[4^{-1}n(n-4)+n-1\big]^{2}/\big[4^{-1}(n-4)^{2}+n-1\big],\\
\alpha_{n,0,\lambda_{0}}&=\alpha_{n,0,0}=n^{2}/4.\end{split}

Hence, setting
n=3,4
in (
A.72
) shows the minimums occur when
j=1
yielding

and

\alpha_{n,0,\lambda_{1}}=\frac{\big[4^{-1}n(n-4)+n-1\big]^{2}}{4^{-1}(n-4)^{2}+n-1}=\frac{n^{4}-8n^{2}+16}{4n^{2}-16n+48}=\frac{n^{2}}{4}\cdot\frac{n^{2}-8+16n^{-2}}{n^{2}-4n+12}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{4}=\alpha_{n,0,0},

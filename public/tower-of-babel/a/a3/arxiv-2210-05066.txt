A Linearly Convergent Algorithm for Rotationally Invariant ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -Norm Principal Component Analysis
Taoli Zheng, Peng Wang, Anthony Man-Cho So, Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract
To do dimensionality reduction on the datasets with outliers, the ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -norm principal component analysis (L1-PCA) as a typical robust alternative of the conventional PCA has enjoyed great popularity over the past years. In this work, we consider a rotationally invariant L1-PCA, which is hardly studied in the literature. To tackle it, we propose a proximal alternating linearized minimization method with a nonlinear extrapolation for solving its two-block reformulation. Moreover, we show that the proposed method converges at least linearly to a limiting critical point of the reformulated problem. Such a point is proved to be a critical point of the original problem under a condition imposed on the step size. Finally, we conduct numerical experiments on both synthetic and real datasets to support our theoretical developments and demonstrate the efficacy of our approach.

Introduction

With the widespread availability of high-dimensional data, principal component analysis (PCA)
[
1
]
as a typical dimensionality reduction technique plays an increasingly important role in data analysis. It has found extensive applications in diverse fields such as computer vision
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, image processing
[
5
,
6
]
, and network analysis
[
7
,
8
]
, to name a few. Due to the fact that the conventional PCA (also known as L2-PCA) is sensitive to corruptions (generically referred to as
outliers

[
9
]
) in the datasets, L1-PCA as a robust alternative of L2-PCA has recently received significant attention; see, e.g.,
[
10
,
11
]
. In this work, we consider a particular form of L1-PCA as follows:

\max\left\{\|\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{X}\|_{1}:\bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K)\right\}.

(1)

Here,
\bm{X}=\left[\bm{x}_{1},\dots,\bm{x}_{n}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
is the data matrix with the sample mean being zero, where
n
and
d
respectively denote the number of samples and dimension of the data points,
\|\bm{A}\|_{1}=\sum_{i,j}|a_{ij}|
denotes the
\ell_{1}
-norm of the matrix
\bm{A}
,
K
is the dimension of subspace with
K\leq\min\{n,d\}
, and
{\rm St}(d,K)=\left\{\bm{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times K}:\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{Q}=\bm{I}_{K}\right\}
denotes the Stiefel manifold. Generally, this problem can be used to find a low-dimensional subspace underlying a corrupted dataset by minimizing the variation of the projections of data points onto the subspace measured by the
\ell_{1}
-norm. Different from the formulation of Problem (
1
), there are actually many other formulations for L1-PCA that have been well studied in the literature; see, e.g.,
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
However, as pointed out in
[
11
]
, no work focuses on Problem (
1
). To fill this gap, this work is devoted to develop an efficient algorithm for solving Problem (
1
) with convergence analysis.

Now, we review different formulations for L1-PCA in the literature. It is known that PCA admits two common interpretations for dimension reduction. One is that PCA aims to find a low-dimensional subspace for which the projections of data points onto it preserve the most variance. Specifically, the corresponding formulation known as L2-PCA is given by

\max\left\{\|\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{X}\|_{F}=\|\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{X}\|_{F}:\bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K)\right\},

(2)

The other sheds light on another perspective of PCA that aims to find a low-dimensional subspace for which the projections of data points onto it best approximate these data points. This leads to a minimization formulation of the form

\min\left\{\|\bm{X}-\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{X}\|_{F}:\bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K)\right\}.

(3)

One can verify that this problem is equivalent to Problem (
2
). In particular, both of them admit a closed-form solution that can be solved efficiently by computing the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the data matrix
\bm{X}
. Moreover, the subspace spanned by the columns of the obtained solution possesses many nice properties
[
1
]
. Nevertheless, the above two formulations have an essential defect that they are sensitive to outliers in the data matrix due to the fact that the
\ell_{2}
-norm is not a robust scale function; see, e.g.,
[
10
]
. The outliers could significantly disturb the singular values of the data matrix and mislead the learning of the desired subspace. To tackle this problem, a recent line of research on robust subspace recovery becomes more and more popular
[
11
]
and a great deal of work adopting different norms as scale functions have been done in the literature; see, e.g.,
[
18
,
17
,
16
,
13
,
15
]
. Notably, taking the
\ell_{1}
norm as the scale function gives L1-PCA. On one hand, along the line of maximizing the variance of projected data as in Problem (
2
), we can naturally obtain Problem (
1
) and

\max\left\{\|\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{X}\|_{1}:\bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K)\right\}.

(4)

Compared to L2-PCA, the
\ell_{1}
-norm endows PCA with robustness but introduces non-smoothness into the objective function simultaneously. Consequently, Problems (
1
) and (
4
) are both non-convex and non-smooth, and have no closed-form solutions. Unlike the equivalence of L2-PCA in Problem (
2
), Problems (
1
) and (
4
) are generally not equivalent. Indeed, the objective of the latter problem is the pointwise maximum of linear functions, while the former is the pointwise maximum of quadratic functions. In particular, Problem (
4
) is widely studied in the literature. For example, Kwak
[
19
]
proposed a fixed-point method for solving Problem (
4
) in a greedy manner and later Nie et al.
[
20
]
improved it to a non-greedy manner. Recently, Markopoulos et al.
[
21
]
developed an algorithm based on bit-flipping iteration. More recently, Wang et al.
[
22
,
14
]
proposed a proximal alternating minimization method with extrapolation for solving it. We
refer the reader to
[
23
]
for more algorithms for solving Problem (
4
). Besides, it is known that L2-PCA is rotationally invariant in the sense that the objective value remains unchanged under arbitrary subspace rotations. Specifically, the objective values of Problems (
2
) and (
3
) are unchanged if we replace
\bm{Q}
by
\bm{Q}\bm{U}
for any orthogonal matrix
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}
. However, this property generally does not hold for the L1-PCA formulation (
4
). Fortunately, the formulation (
1
) keeps this property. This also motivates us to study Problem (
1
). In the following, we refer to it as
rotationally invariant
L1-PCA. On the other hand, along the line of minimizing the approximation error of data points as in Problem (
3
), we can obtain another L1-PCA formulation of the form

\min\left\{\|\bm{X}-\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{X}\|_{1}:\bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K)\right\}.

(5)

This problem has been considered in the literature. For example, Baccini et al.
[
12
]
proposed a heuristic approach based on a canonical correlation analysis. Recently, Park et al.
[
16
]
proposed an iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) methods and provided convergence analysis. More related algorithms can be found in
[
24
,
25
]
.

Then, we review some other robust alternatives of L2-PCA closely related to L1-PCA. A popular one is the low-rank matrix factorization formulation of the form

\min\left\{\|\bm{X}-\bm{U}\bm{V}\|_{1}:\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times K},\ \bm{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times n}\right\}.

(6)

Ke and Kanade
[
17
]
proposed an alternating convex minimization method for solving it. Later, Yu et al.
[
26
]
considered an augmented lagrange multiplier method and Eriksson and Hengel proposed a generalization of the Wiberg algorithm
[
27
]
for tackling it. Up to now, the above formulations all utilize the
\ell_{1}
-norm as the scale function to achieve robustness. Actually, many other different norms have been employed in the literature. A notable one is the
\ell_{2,1}
-norm, which is defined as
\|\bm{A}\|_{2,1}=\sum_{i}(\sum_{j}a_{ij}^{2})^{1/2}
. This is also known as the
R_{1}
-norm in
[
13
]
.
This norm, together with the second interpretation of PCA, motivates the R1-PCA formulation as follows:

\min\left\{\|\bm{X}-\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{X}\|_{2,1}:\bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K)\right\}.

(7)

It is noteworthy that this problem not only satisfies the mentioned subspace rotational invariance but also satisfy another
rotationally invariant
property in the sense that it is invariant to the change of coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Specifically, under data transformations
\bm{X}\rightarrow\bm{U}\bm{X}
for any orthogonal matrix
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, the objective value is unchanged when
\bm{Q}\rightarrow\bm{U}\bm{Q}
. That is to say that when the sample space is rotated, the feature subspace will be rotated in the same way. For the sake of clarity, in our paper, we refer to rotationally invariance as invariance with respect to
subspace rotations
.
To solve this problem, Ding et al.
[
13
]
proposed a subspace iteration algorithm. Later, Wang et al.
[
28
]
generalized this robust formulation via replacing
\ell_{2,1}
-norm with
\ell_{2,p}
-norm for any
p\in(0,2)
. Recently, Nie et al.
[
15
]
considered a robust PCA model using the
\ell_{2,1}
-norm based on the first interpretation of PCA, i.e.,

\max\left\{\|\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{X}\|_{2,1}:\bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K)\right\}.

(8)

They developed an efficient non-greedy method to solve this problem. We refer the reader to
[
11
]
for more formulations of robust PCA.

I-A

Contribution

In this work, our first contribution is to propose a
proximal alternating linearized minimization
(PALM) algorithm for solving a two-block reformulation (see Problem (
10
)) of Problem (
1
). Motivated by the encouraging performance of extrapolation techniques for accelerating non-convex optimization problems (see, e.g.,
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
14
]
), we incorporate a quadratic extrapolation into the update of a block variable to possibly accelerate the PALM method. It is worth noting that compared to the widely used linear extrapolation in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
14
]
), our proposed quadratic extrapolation (see (
13
)) seems to be new. We refer to the resulting method as
proximal alternating linearized minimization with extrapolation
(PALMe). PALMe is computationally efficient since the update of each block of PALMe admits a closed-form solution and per-iteration cost is
\mathcal{O}(ndK+dK^{2})
. Our second contribution is to show that the proposed method converges at least linearly to a limiting critical point (see Definition
1
) of the reformulation problem and this point is proved to be a critical point of Problem (
1
) (see Definition
2
) under a verifiable condition imposed on the step size. To this end, we show that the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KŁ) exponent (see Definition
3
) of Problems (
10
) is
1/2
. With this characterization, we employ the convergence analysis framework in
[
32
]
to show the linear convergence of PALMe. Our third contribution is to conduct numerical experiments on synthetic and real data sets to support our theoretical results. Specifically, the experimental results demonstrate that our approach is competitive, in terms of both numerical efficiency and clustering accuracy, with other PALM-type methods for solving Problem (
1
). We also compare our approach with various robust PCA approaches on image reconstruction, which illustrates the robustness to outliers of our approach.

Lastly, let us highlight the differences between this work and a closely related one
[
14
]
. First, the proximal alternating minimization method with extrapolation (PAMe) in
[
14
]
is developed for solving Problem (
4
), in which the objective is a pointwise maximum of linear functions. By contrast, the objective of our considered Problem (
10
) is a pointwise maximum of quadratic functions and thus PAMe is not applicable to ours. Second, we apply a quadratic extrapolation step to the update of the block variable
\bm{P}
, which highly relies on the quadratic form of the objective function, while the authors in
[
14
]
utilize the standard linear extrapolation step. Moreover, this extrapolation scheme also gives rise to the third difference between our convergence analysis and that in
[
14
]
. Specifically, the quadratic extrapolation step makes it more complicated to verify the decreasing property.

Algorithm Design

We begin by reformulating Problem (
1
) as a two-block form. Noting that
|x|=\max\{x,-x\}
for any

, we can reformulate Problem (

) as

\displaystyle\min\

\displaystyle H(\bm{P},\bm{Q}):=-\langle\bm{P},\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{T}\rangle

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\

\displaystyle\bm{P}\in{\mathcal{B}}(n,d),\ \bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K),

(11)

where
\langle\bm{A},\bm{B}\rangle={\rm tr}(\bm{A}^{T}\bm{B})
denotes the Euclidean inner product of two matrices
\bm{A},\bm{B}
of the same size and
{\mathcal{B}}(n,d)=\left\{\bm{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}:p_{ij}\in\{\pm 1\},\ i=1,\ldots,n,\ j=1,\ldots,d\right\}
denotes the set of all

and

, one can apply the PALM method (see, e.g.,
[
35
,
32
,
36
]
) for solving it.
Specifically, given the current iterate
\left(\bm{P}^{k},\bm{Q}^{k}\right)\in\mathcal{B}(n,d)\times{\rm St}(d,K)

\left(\bm{P}^{k+1},\bm{Q}^{k+1}\right)\in{\mathcal{B}}(n,d)\times{\rm St}(d,K)

\bm{P}^{k+1}\in\argmin\left\{\begin{split}&\ -\langle\bm{P},\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}^{k}\bm{Q}^{k^{T}}\rangle\\
&\ +\frac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\bm{P}-\bm{P}^{k}\|^{2}_{F}:\ \bm{P}\in\mathcal{B}(n,d)\end{split}\right\},

(12a)

\bm{Q}^{k+1}\in\argmin\left\{\begin{split}&\ -\langle\bm{Q},(\bm{X}\bm{P}^{k+1}+\bm{P}^{{k+1}^{T}}\bm{X}^{T})\bm{Q}^{k}\rangle\\

(12b)

where
\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}>0
are step-size parameters. Recently, many different extrapolation techniques have been successfully applied to accelerate proximal algorithms for convex and non-convex optimization problems; see, e.g.,
[
37
,
38
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
14
]
. This motivates us to incorporate an extrapolation step into the update of
\bm{P}
to achieve possible acceleration of the PALM iterations. Specifically, we replace (
12a
) with

\displaystyle\bm{E}^{k}=\bm{Q}^{k}\bm{Q}^{k^{T}}+\gamma_{k}\left(\bm{Q}^{k}\bm{Q}^{k^{T}}-\bm{Q}^{k-1}\bm{Q}^{{k-1}^{T}}\right),

(13)

\displaystyle\bm{P}^{k+1}\in\argmin\left\{\begin{split}&\ -\langle\bm{P},\bm{X}^{T}\bm{E}^{k}\rangle\\
&\ +\frac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\bm{P}-\bm{P}^{k}\|^{2}_{F}:\ \bm{P}\in{\mathcal{B}}(n,d)\end{split}\right\},

where
\bm{E}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times K}
is the point extrapolated from
\bm{Q}^{k}\bm{Q}^{k^{T}}
and
\bm{Q}^{k-1}\bm{Q}^{{k-1}^{T}}
and
\gamma_{k}\in[0,1)
is the parameter controlling extrapolation step-size. It is worth noting that the above iterates (
12b
) and (

12a
) in its most simplified form reads as

\bm{P}^{k+1}\in\sign\left(\bm{P}^{k}+\bm{X}^{T}\bm{E}^{k}/\alpha_{k}\right).

(14)

On the other hand, the update (

) is essentially an instance of the orthogonal Procrustes problem
[

]
, whose solution is given by

\bm{Q}^{k+1}=\bm{U}^{k+1}\bm{V}^{{k+1}^{T}},

\bm{U}^{k+1}\in\mathrm{St}(d,K)
and
\bm{V}^{k+1}\in\mathrm{St}(K,K)

\bm{U}^{k+1}\bm{\Sigma}^{k+1}\bm{V}^{{k+1}^{T}}=\bm{Q}^{k}+(\bm{X}\bm{P}^{k+1}\bm{Q}^{k}+{\bm{P}^{k+1}}^{T}\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}^{k})/\beta_{k}

III
Convergence Analysis

Our goal in this section is to establish the convergence result of Algorithm
1
. Generally, it is not easy to analyze the convergence behavior of algorithms for solving a non-smooth and non-convex problem. Fortunately, Attouch et al.
[
35
]
developed a unified framework that sheds light on analyzing the convergence behavior of proximal algorithms for solving non-smooth and non-convex problems. This framework has been widely used in the literature; see, e.g.,
[
14
,
44
,
31
,
45
]
. Specifically, one needs to verify that the sequence of iterates generated by the considered algorithm satisfies the
sufficient decrease property
and the
relative error property
in terms of a proper potential function. Moreover, one needs to verify that the considered potential function satisfies the
KŁ property with the associated exponent
. Note that the first two properties are algorithm-dependent, and we can directly verify them by analyzing the updates of Algorithm
1
. By contrast, the last one depends on the function itself rather than the algorithm according to the definition of the KŁ property. To simplify our development, we define

\displaystyle\ell(\bm{Q})=-\|\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{X}\|_{1}+\delta_{{\rm St}(d,K)}(\bm{Q}),

(15)

and

\displaystyle h(\bm{P},\bm{Q})=H(\bm{P},\bm{Q})+\delta_{\mathcal{B}(n,K)}(\bm{P})+\delta_{{\rm St}(d,K)}(\bm{Q}).

(16)

Moreover, we can define the critical points of
\ell
as follows.

Definition 2

(Critical point of
\ell
)
.

We say that
\bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K)
is a critical point of
\ell
if

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}\in

\displaystyle-\bm{X}\sign(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{T})\bm{Q}-\sign(\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{T}\bm{X})\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{N}_{{\rm St}(d,K)}(\bm{Q}).

(17)

Invoking the subdifferential calculus rules in
[
34
, Chapter 10B]
, we remark that this is a necessary condition for local optimality. Besides, it should be noted that every limiting critical point of
\ell
is a critical point of
\ell
, but the converse is not known to hold.

III-A

Sufficient Decrease and Relative Error Properties

To prove the sufficient decrease and relative error properties, we need to choose a proper potential function. One immediate choice of the potential function is the function
h
defined in (
16
). However, due to the extrapolation step in Algorithm
1
, it is not clear how to verify the mentioned properties in terms of
h
. Motivated by the potential functions constructed in
[
29
,
41
,
14
]
, we consider the potential function
\Phi_{\beta}(\bm{P},\bm{Q},\bm{Q}^{\prime}):\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times K}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times K}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
defined by

\Phi_{\beta}(\bm{P},\bm{Q},\bm{Q}^{\prime})=h(\bm{P},\bm{Q})+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\bm{Q}-\bm{Q}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{F}.

(18)

Then, when the step-size and the extrapolation parameters in Algorithm
1
are suitably chosen, we can verify the desired properties in terms of
\Phi_{\beta}
as follows.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\bm{C}^{k}=\left(\bm{P}^{k},\bm{Q}^{k},\bm{Q}^{k-1}\right)
for all
k\geq 0
. Suppose that the step-size and extrapolation parameters in Algorithm
1
satisfy for all
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{*}\leq\alpha_{k}\leq\alpha^{*},\ \frac{3}{2}\beta_{*}+2\|\bm{X}\bm{P}^{k}\|\leq\beta_{k}\leq\beta^{*},

(19)

\displaystyle\ 0\leq\gamma_{k}<\gamma^{*}=\min\left\{1,\frac{\alpha_{*}\beta_{*}}{8\|\bm{X}\|^{2}}\right\}

(20)

for some
\alpha_{*},\alpha^{*},\beta_{*},\beta^{*}\in(0,\infty)
. Then, the following statements hold:

(i) The sequence
\{\bm{C}^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is bounded.

(ii) There exists a constant
\kappa_{1}>0
such that for all
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\beta_{*}}(\bm{C}^{k+1})-\Phi_{\beta_{*}}(\bm{C}^{k})\leq-\kappa_{1}\|{\bm{C}}^{k+1}-{\bm{C}}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}.

(21)

(iii) There exists a constant
\kappa_{2}>0
such that for all
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\dist\left(\bm{0},\partial\Phi_{\beta_{*}}(\bm{C}^{k+1})\right)\leq\kappa_{2}\|{\bm{C}}^{k+1}-{\bm{C}}^{k}\|_{F}.

(22)

We defer the detailed proof to Section
-A
of the appendix. Thanks to the boundness of
{\mathcal{B}}(n,d)
and
{\rm St}(d,K)
, one can easily verify the boundness of the sequence
\{\bm{C}^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
as in (i). We refer to (ii) as
sufficient decrease property
, which follows from the updates in (
12b
) and (
13
), and the Lipschitz smoothness of
H(\bm{P},\bm{Q})
on a bounded set. It ensures that the potential function
\Phi_{\beta_{*}}(\bm{C}^{k})
is monotonically decreasing. This, together with (i), implies that the sequence
\{\Phi_{\beta_{*}}(\bm{C}^{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
converges and
\|{\bm{C}}^{k+1}-{\bm{C}}^{k}\|_{F}
goes to zero as
k
increases. We refer to (iii) as
relative error property
, which is also known as
safeguard
in
[
46
,
47
]
. It is proved by the optimality condition of the updates in (
12b
) and (
13
). Armed with this lemma, we can conclude that every accumulation point of
\{{\bm{C}}^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is a critical point of
\Phi_{\beta_{*}}
, which is essentially
subsequence convergence
. Remark that compared to the proof in
[
14
, Proposition 6]
, this proof is different and more involved. Indeed, the objective function of Problem (
10
) is quadratic in terms of
\bm{Q}
, while that is linear in
[
14
]
. Moreover, our extrapolation step in (
13
) is non-linear, while that is linear in
[
14
]
.

III-B

KŁ Property

In this subsection, we verify the KŁ property of the constructed potential function
\Phi_{\beta}
and derive the corresponding KŁ exponent. For the definition of the KŁ property, we refer the reader to
[
35
, Definition 2.5]
for the details. The KŁ property is widely used for studying convergence behavior of various first-order methods; see, e.g.,
[
48
,
35
,
32
]
. It is known that the KŁ property holds for many extended-valued lower-semicontinuous functions, such as semi-algebraic functions. According to
[
48
, Section 4.3]
and
[
32
, Theorem 3]
, one can easily verify that
\Phi_{\beta}
satisfies the KŁ property. Here, we are more interested in the KŁ exponent of
\Phi_{\beta}
, since it determines the convergence rate of the proposed method.

Definition 3

(KŁ exponent)
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
is proper and lower semicontinuous. We say that the function
f
has a
KŁ exponent
of
\theta\in[0,1)
at the point
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\dom(\partial f)
if there exist constants
\epsilon,\eta>0
,
\nu\in(0,+\infty]
such that

\dist(\bm{0},\partial f(\bm{x}))\geq\eta(f(\bm{x})-f(\bar{\bm{x}}))^{\theta}

whenever
\|\bm{x}-\bar{\bm{x}}\|\leq\epsilon
and
f(\bar{\bm{x}})<f(\bm{x})<f(\bar{\bm{x}})+\nu
.

Now, we are devoted to estimating the KŁ exponent of
\Phi_{\beta}
. Let
\bm{P}_{1},\ldots,\bm{P}_{2^{nd}}
be an enumeration of the elements in
\mathcal{B}(n,d)
. By definition of the
\ell_{1}
-norm, we can express the objective function
\ell
of Problem (
15
) as the pointwise minimum of finitely many proper and lower semicontinuous functions:

\ell(\bm{Q})=\min_{i\in\{1,\ldots,2^{nd}\}}\big\{\underbrace{\langle\bm{X}\bm{P}_{i},\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{\top}\rangle+\delta_{{\rm St}(d,K)}(\bm{Q})}_{\ell_{i}(\bm{Q})}\big\}.

Using the same argument in
[
14
, Section 3.1]
, we can elucidate that estimating the KŁ exponent of
\ell
boils down determining the KŁ exponent of
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{2^{nd}}
.
Based on
[
44
, Theorem 1]
and
[
49
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can characterize the KŁ exponent of the quadratic optimization problem with orthogonality constraint (QP-OC).

Fact 1
.

For the QP-OC problem

\displaystyle\min\{\langle\bm{A},\bm{Q}\bm{B}\bm{Q}^{T}\rangle:\ \bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K)\},

where
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}
are symmetric, the KŁ exponent is
1/2
.

Using
[
49
, Theorem 3.6]
, the KŁ exponent of
h
, and relations between
h
and
\Phi_{\beta_{*}}
, we can obtain the following corollary. The detailed proof is provided in Section
-B
of the appendix.

Corollary 1
.

The KŁ exponent of
\Phi_{\beta_{*}}
is
1/2
.

III-C

Linear Convergence of Algorithm
1
and Properties of Limit Points

Equipped with Lemma
1
, Corollary
1
, and
[
35
, Theorem 2.9]
, we are ready to prove that the sequence
\{\bm{C}^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
converges at least linearly to a limiting critical point of
\Phi_{\beta_{*}}
. However, our goals are to prove the convergence rate of Algorithm
1
and figure out under what condition the limit point of the sequence
\{\bm{Q}^{k}\}
is a critical point of Problem (
1
). To tackle the issues, we need to characterize the relationships among
\ell
,
h
, and
\Phi_{\beta}
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\beta>0
be given and
\ell
,
h
, and
\Phi_{\beta}
be respectively defined in (
15
), (
16
), and (
18
). Then, the following statements hold:

(i) If
(\bm{P},\bm{Q})
with
\bm{P}\in\sign(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{T})
is a limiting critical point of
h
, then
\bm{Q}\in{\rm St}(d,K)
is a critical point of
\ell
.

(ii) Suppose that
(\bm{P},\bm{Q},\bm{Q}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}(n,d)\times{\rm St}(d,K)\times{\rm St}(d,K)
is a limiting critical point of
\Phi_{\beta}
. Then, we have
\bm{Q}=\bm{Q}^{\prime}
. Moreover,
(\bm{P},\bm{Q},\bm{Q})
is a limiting critical point of
\Phi_{\beta}
if and only if
(\bm{P},\bm{Q})
is a limiting critical point of
h
.

We defer the detailed proof to Section
-C
of the appendix. Armed with this lemma,
1
, Corollary
1
, and
[
35
, Theorem 2.9]
, we can establish the main theorem of this work, which provides convergence analysis of the proposed method.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\{(\bm{P}^{k},\bm{Q}^{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence of iterates generated by Algorithm (
1
) under the conditions (i)
\alpha_{*}\leq\alpha_{k}\leq\alpha^{*}
for some
\alpha^{*},\alpha_{*}\in(0,+\infty)
, (ii)
3\beta_{*}/2+2\|\bm{X}\bm{P}^{k}\|\leq\beta_{k}\leq\beta^{*}
for some
\beta^{*},\beta_{*}\in(0,+\infty)
, and (iii)
\gamma_{k}
satisfy
0\leq\gamma_{k}<\gamma^{*}=\min\left\{1,\alpha_{*}\beta_{*}/({8\|\bm{X}\|^{2}})\right\}
. Then, the sequence
\{(\bm{P}^{k},\bm{Q}^{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
converges at least linearly to a limiting critical point
(\bm{P}^{*},\bm{Q}^{*})
of Problem (
10
). Moreover, if
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{*}
for all
k\geq 0
such that

0<\alpha_{*}<\min\left\{\begin{split}&\ |(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}^{*}\bm{Q}^{*\top})_{ij}|:(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}^{*}\bm{Q}^{*T})_{ij}\not=0,\\
&\ \quad i=1,\dots,n,\,j=1,\dots,d\end{split}\right\},

(23)

then
\bm{Q}^{*}
is a critical point of Problem (
1
).

Proof.

Let
\bm{C}^{k}=\left(\bm{P}^{k},\bm{Q}^{k},\bm{Q}^{k-1}\right)
for all
k\geq 0
. It follows from Lemma
1
, Corollary
1
, and
[
32
, Theorem 1]
that the sequence
\{\bm{C}^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
converges at least linearly to a limiting critical point
\left(\bm{P}^{*},\bm{Q}^{*},\bm{Q}^{*}\right)
of
\Phi_{\beta_{*}}
. This, together with (ii) of Lemma
2
, gives that the sequence
\{(\bm{P}^{k},\bm{Q}^{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
converges at least linearly to the limiting critical point
(\bm{P}^{*},\bm{Q}^{*})
of
h
.

Suppose that
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{*}
for all
k\geq 0
. According to (
13
) and (
14
), we have
\bm{E}^{k}=\bm{Q}^{k}\bm{Q}^{k^{T}}+\gamma_{k}(\bm{Q}^{k}\bm{Q}^{k^{T}}-\bm{Q}^{k-1}\bm{Q}^{{k-1}^{T}})
and
\bm{P}^{k+1}\in\sign(\bm{P}^{k}+\bm{X}^{T}\bm{E}^{k}/\alpha_{k})
. This, together with
(\bm{P}^{k},\bm{Q}^{k})\rightarrow(\bm{P}^{*},\bm{Q}^{*})
and
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{*}
, yields that

\displaystyle\bm{P}^{*}\in\sign(\bm{P}^{*}+\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}^{*}\bm{Q}^{*^{T}}/\alpha_{*}).

Combining this with the result that
(\bm{P}^{*},\bm{Q}^{*})
is a limiting critical point of
h
gives

\begin{split}\mathbf{0}\in&\ -\bm{X}\sign(\bm{P}^{*}+\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}^{*}\bm{Q}^{*^{T}}/\alpha_{*})\bm{Q}^{*}\\
&\ -\sign(\bm{P}^{*^{T}}+\bm{Q}^{*}\bm{Q}^{*^{T}}\bm{X}^{T}/\alpha_{*})\bm{X}\bm{Q}^{*}+\mathcal{N}_{{\rm St}(d,K)}(\bm{Q}^{*}).\end{split}

In particular, noting that
\bm{P}^{*}\in\mathcal{B}(n,d)
and
\alpha_{*}
satisfies (
23
), we have
\sign(\bm{P}^{*}+\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}^{*}\bm{Q}^{*^{T}}/\alpha_{*})\subseteq\sign(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}^{*}\bm{Q}^{*^{T}})
. These further implies

\begin{split}\mathbf{0}\in&\ -\bm{X}\sign(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}^{*}\bm{Q}^{*^{T}})\bm{Q}^{*}-\sign(\bm{Q}^{*}\bm{Q}^{*^{T}}\bm{X}^{T})\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Q}^{*}\\
&\ +\mathcal{N}_{{\rm St}(d,K)}(\bm{Q}^{*}).\end{split}

Then, we complete the proof.

\sqcup\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\sqcap$}

The first part of this theorem demonstrates that under some conditions imposed on step-size and extrapolation parameters, the iterates generated by Algorithm
1
linearly converges to a limiting critical point of the reformulated L1-PCA problem (
10
). The conditions imposed on
\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}
and
\gamma_{k}
are introduced by proving the sufficient decreasing of objective function value. Interestingly, we found that in experiments, these parameters can be chosen flexibly even if they may violate aforementioned conditions. We leave this theory-practice gap for further research. The second part of Theorem
1
shows that the condition (
23
) is sufficient to guarantee that the point returned by Algorithm
1
is not only a limiting critical point of Problem (
10
), but also a critical point of Problem (
1
). It is worth noting that we can efficiently verify whether this condition holds after obtaining a limit point
\bm{Q}^{*}
.

IV-C

Image Reconstruction

As suggested in
[
52
,
15
]
, the robustness to outliers can be visualized by image reconstruction. In this section, we conduct image reconstruction experiments on 6 gray-scale human face images (see the first row of Fig.
4
) downloaded from
AT&T
Database of Faces
2
2

2

https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/kasikrit/att-database-of-faces?resource=download
to test robustness of different models. We assume that the clean image
\bm{A}
is not available and instead we have 9 corrupted versions
\bm{A}_{1},\dots,\bm{A}_{9}
. Here, these corrupted instances are generated by adding outliers to a block as follows. We firstly partition the original image
\bm{A}
into 9 equal-sized blocks, say
\bm{B}_{1},\dots,\bm{B}_{9}
. Next, we select one block
\bm{B}_{i}
, divide it into 4 smaller blocks of equal size, add outliers drawn from the discrete uniform distribution in range
[1,200]
to its diagonal blocks, and normalize it into range
[0,255]
to generate
\bm{A}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,9
. One instance of the corrupted image is shown in the second row of Fig.
4
. Then, we can vectorize these 9 corrupted images by stacking them to form the data matrix
\bm{X}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bm{X}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{vec}(\bm{A}_{1})&\dots&\mathrm{vec}(\bm{A}_{9}).\end{bmatrix}

Now, we apply various PCA models to do image reconstruction on
\bm{X}
, including our proposed approach, L1-PCA-based approach in
[
14
]
, R1-PCA-based approach in
[
13
]
, matrix factorization based L1-PCA (MF-L1PCA) approach in
[
26
]
, and L12-PCA-based approach in
[
15
]
. We simply denote our approach by RI-L1PCA. The projection of
\bm{X}

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed a proximal alternating linearzed minimization method with a non-linear extrapolation to solve the rotationally invariant L1-PCA problem. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that presents a fast iterative method to tackle this non-smooth non-convex problem. We proved that our method converges at least linearly to a critical point of the L1-PCA problem under a mild condition. To demonstrate the efficacy of our method, extensive experimental results on both synthetic and real datasets are reported. In particular, we compare our approach with some existing robust PCA approaches to elucidate its robustness to outliers. Motivated by our proposed non-linear extrapolation, one future direction is to extend such extrapolation scheme to more general function class. Another possible direction is to design other optimization algorithms to tackle the rotationally invariant L1-PCA problem and analyze its convergence behavior.

Constructing a weakly-interacting fixed point of the Fermionic Polchinski equation
Rafael Leon Greenblatt

Abstract
I rigorously prove the existence of a nontrivial fixed point of a family of continuous renormalization group flows corresponding to certain weakly interacting Fermionic quantum field theories with a parameter in the propagator allowing the scaling dimension to be tuned in a manner analogous to dimensional regularization.

3
The Grassmann algebra

In this section I formally define the Grassmann algebra alluded to in
Equation

1.1
, which is also a topological vector space, so that derivatives with respect to a real parameter have a natural meaning. The other operations on the right hand side of
Equation

1.1

This Grassmann algebra is intended to be extremely large; in particular it corresponds to an extremely weak assumption on the locality and regularity of the interaction. Correspondingly, the notion of convergence (and so of differentiability) is extremely weak.

Note that this section along with

Remark 4

Singling out one of the position arguments is very helpful for some manipulations later; the specific choice won’t play a particular role because all of the important applications involve antisymmetric functions.

With the continuity assumption in the definition of

Comparing this to the previous section, note that

4.3
Reformulation of the flow equation

Although

is a vector subspace of
\mathsf{Grassmann}

For example

Let

, and

. With this last stipulation,

\owedge
is a partially antisymmetrized product, defined in distributional notation by

\left[V_{j}\owedge W_{k}\right](z_{1},\dots,z_{j+k})=\frac{1}{(j+k-2)!}\begin{aligned} \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\Pi_{j+k}:\\
\pi(1)=1,\pi(j+1)=2\end{subarray}}&\sigma(\pi)V_{j}(z_{1},z_{\pi(2)},\dots,z_{\pi(j+1)})\\
&\times W_{k}(z_{2},z_{\pi(j+2)},\dots,z_{\pi(j+k)}),\end{aligned}

(4.30)

where
z_{j}=(a_{j},x_{j})
.

It follows from
Equation

4.15
that

\left\|Q^{(\Lambda)}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})\right\|\leq|G_{\Lambda}|_{+}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\\
j+k=\ell+2\end{subarray}}jk\|V_{j}\|\ \|W_{k}\|

(4.31)

and in particular
\mathcal{Q}^{(\Lambda)}(\mathsf{kernel},\mathsf{kernel})\subset\mathsf{kernel}
, since the sum is finite. Examining the parity and symmetry properties, also
\mathcal{Q}^{(\Lambda)}(\mathsf{pot},\mathsf{pot})\subset\mathsf{pot}
.

With this notation, we can rewrite
Equation

1.1
as

\frac{\,\text{\rm d}}{\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda}\mathcal{V}=\mathfrak{A}^{(\Lambda)}\mathcal{V}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{Q}^{(\Lambda)}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})=:\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{V}),

(4.32)

which also makes sense as a differential equation on
\mathsf{pot}
, and this is how I will approach it.

Recall the definition of
\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}
and the related maps in
Equation

3.8
, which are equivalent to
\widetilde{D}_{\Lambda}:\mathsf{slow}_{n}\to\mathsf{slow}_{n}
,
f\mapsto(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto f_{\boldsymbol{a}}(\boldsymbol{x}/\Lambda))
and
\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V}=\left(D_{\Lambda}V_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, where

D_{\Lambda}V_{j}[f]:=\Lambda^{-j[\psi]}V_{j}\left[\widetilde{D}_{\Lambda}f\right].

(4.33)

This definition is designed to make
D_{\Lambda}V_{2n}[P_{\Lambda}^{\otimes n}]
independent of
\Lambda
.
Evidently
\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}
is a jointly-continuous one-parameter semigroup on
\mathsf{kernel}
up to a reparameterization
\Lambda=e^{\eta}
, and also
\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{pot})=\mathsf{pot}
.

Then the problem of finding a scaling solution can be restated as

\frac{\,\text{\rm d}}{\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda}[\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V}^{*}]=\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V}^{*});

(4.34)

by the semigroup property of
\mathfrak{D}
, this holds either for all
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
or not at all.

I now list some other helpful formulae about the relationship of
\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}
with the other objects defined above.

Equation

1.2
can be rephrased as

\widetilde{D}_{\Lambda}G_{\Lambda}=\Lambda^{-1+2[\psi]}G,

(4.35)

and as a consequence

\begin{split}D_{\Lambda}Q_{\ell}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})[f]&=\Lambda^{-\ell[\psi]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\\
j+k=\ell+2\end{subarray}}jk\left(V_{j}\owedge W_{k}\right)[G\otimes\widetilde{D}_{\Lambda}f]\\
&=\Lambda^{1-(\ell+2)[\psi]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\\
j+k=\ell+2\end{subarray}}jk\left(V_{j}\owedge W_{k}\right)[\widetilde{D}_{\Lambda}G_{\Lambda}\otimes\widetilde{D}_{\Lambda}f]\\
&=\Lambda\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\\
j+k=\ell+2\end{subarray}}jk\left(D_{\Lambda}V_{j}\owedge D_{\Lambda}W_{k}\right)[G_{\Lambda}\otimes f]\end{split}

(4.36)

(as with the propagator, I use the abbreviation
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}
),
implying

\mathcal{Q}^{(\Lambda)}(\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W})=\Lambda^{-1}\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}).

(4.37)

Similarly (abbreviating
\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}^{(1)}
)

(\mathfrak{A}^{(\Lambda)}\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V})_{\ell}[f]=\Lambda^{-1}D_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{A}\mathcal{V})_{\ell}[f]

(4.38)

and so

\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V})\equiv\Lambda^{-1}\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{V})

(4.39)

with
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

4.4
Wick ordering

\mathfrak{A}
has an important relationship to Wick ordered polynomials, corresponding to
Equation

1.8
: it acts on them as an infinitesimal change in the propagator which defines the Wick ordering.

Wick ordering is a linear operation which, using antisymmetry, acts on
\mathsf{kernel}_{\ell}
as

:\mathrel{V_{\ell}}:_{\Lambda}[f]=(-1)^{m}w_{\ell m}\left(V_{\ell},P_{\Lambda}^{\otimes m}\otimes f\right),\quad f\in\mathsf{slow}_{\ell-2m}

(4.40)

where

w_{\ell m}:=\binom{\ell}{2m}\frac{(2m)!}{2^{m}m!}=\frac{\ell!}{(\ell-2m)!2^{m}m!}

(4.41)

is the number of ways of choosing
m
unordered pairs from a set with
\ell
elements, cf.
[
GJ87
, Equation (1.5.14)]
; recall that
P_{\Lambda}:=\int_{0}^{\Lambda}G_{\Sigma}\,\text{\rm d}\Sigma
is the propagator with a UV cutoff.
It seems unlikely that this is well-defined on all of
\mathsf{kernel}
, however (and especially that
Equation

4.40
defines a continuous map), so I proceed cautiously as follows.
Let us first consider the definition on elements of
\mathsf{kernel}
which are sequences with only a finite number of nonzero entries, which I call
\mathsf{kernel}_{\textrm{finite}}
; this is dense in
\mathsf{kernel}
, bearing in mind the topology specified above.
Then

\left(\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V}\right)_{\ell}[f]:=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}w_{\ell+2m,m}V_{\ell+2m}\left[P_{\Lambda}^{\otimes m}\otimes f\right]

(4.42)

defines a linear operator coinciding with Wick ordering on
\mathsf{kernel}_{\textrm{finite}}
, preserving
\mathsf{pot}_{\textrm{finite}}:=\mathsf{kernel}_{\textrm{finite}}\cap\mathsf{pot}
, and it is invertible with inverse

\left(\mathfrak{W}^{-1}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V}\right)_{\ell}[f]:=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}w_{\ell+2m,m}V_{\ell+2m}\left[P_{\Lambda}^{\otimes m}\otimes f\right]

(4.43)

as can easily be verified by direct calculation.
I will consider
\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}^{\pm 1}
to be defined on those
\mathcal{V}
for which

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}w_{\ell+2m,m}\|\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}V_{\ell+2m}\|<\infty,\ \forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}+1

(4.44)

on some open set of
\Lambda
,
which implies that sums the right hand sides of
Equations

4.42
and
4.43
are absolutely convergent for all
f
. On this domain
\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}^{\pm 1}\mathcal{V}
is jointly continuous in
\Lambda
and
\mathcal{V}
.

Since
\frac{\,\text{\rm d}}{\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda}P_{\Lambda}=G_{\Lambda}
, using antisymmetry

\begin{split}\frac{\,\text{\rm d}}{\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda}\left(\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}^{\pm 1}\mathcal{V}\right)_{\ell}[f]&=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(\mp 1)^{m}mw_{\ell+2m,m}V_{\ell+2m}\left[P_{\Lambda}^{\otimes m-1}\otimes G_{\Lambda}\otimes f\right]\\
&=\mp\frac{(\ell+2)(\ell+1)}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(\mp 1)^{m}w_{\ell+2m,m}V_{\ell+2m+2}\left[P_{\Lambda}^{\otimes m}\otimes G_{\Lambda}\otimes f\right]\\
&=\mp\frac{(\ell+2)(\ell+1)}{2}(\mathfrak{W}^{\pm 1}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V})_{\ell+2}[G_{\Lambda}\otimes f]\end{split}

(4.45)

(all of the sums are absolutely convergent for
\mathcal{V}
in the domain of
\mathfrak{W}^{\pm 1}_{\Lambda}
)
and also using

mw_{\ell+2m,m}=\frac{(\ell+2m)!}{\ell!2^{m}(m-1)!}=\frac{(\ell+2)(\ell+1)}{2}w_{\ell+2m,m-1}

(4.46)

this combines with
Equation

4.26
to give

\frac{\,\text{\rm d}}{\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda}\mathfrak{W}^{\pm 1}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V}=\mp\mathfrak{A}^{(\Lambda)}\mathfrak{W}^{\pm 1}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V}.

(4.47)

Alternatively,

\begin{split}mw_{\ell+2m,m}&=\frac{(\ell+2m)!}{\ell!2^{m}(m-1)!}=\frac{(\ell+2m)(\ell+2m-1)}{2}\frac{(\ell+2m-2)!}{\ell!2^{m-1}(m-1)!}\\
&=\frac{(\ell+2m)(\ell+2m-1)}{2}w_{\ell+2m-2,m-1}\end{split}

(4.48)

and so

\begin{split}\frac{\,\text{\rm d}}{\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda}\left(\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}^{\pm 1}\mathcal{V}\right)_{\ell}[f]&=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(\mp 1)^{m}mw_{\ell+2m,m}V_{\ell+2m}\left[P_{\Lambda}^{\otimes m-1}\otimes G_{\Lambda}\otimes f\right]\\
&=\mp\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\begin{aligned} \frac{(\ell+2n+2)(\ell+2n+1)}{2}(\mp 1)^{n}w_{\ell+2n,n}&\\
\times\vphantom{\sum}V_{\ell+2n+2}\left[G_{\Lambda}\otimes P_{\Lambda}^{\otimes n}\otimes f\right]&\end{aligned}\\
&=\mp(\mathfrak{W}^{\pm 1}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V})_{\ell}[f]\end{split}

(4.49)

(renumbering
n=m-1
) and so also

\frac{\,\text{\rm d}}{\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda}\mathfrak{W}^{\pm 1}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V}=\mp\mathfrak{W}^{\pm 1}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{A}^{(\Lambda)}\mathcal{V}

(4.50)

(in fact it is also easy enough to check directly that
\mathfrak{W}^{\pm 1}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{(\Lambda)}
commute).
As a result, if
\frac{\,\text{\rm d}}{\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda}\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda}=\mathfrak{A}^{(\Lambda)}\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda}-\mathcal{Q}^{(\Lambda)}(\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda},\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda})
(i.e.
Equation

4.32
) and
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\equiv\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda}
, then

\begin{split}\frac{\,\text{\rm d}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}}{\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda}&=-\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{Q}^{(\Lambda)}(\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda},\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda})\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{Q}^{(\Lambda)}(\mathfrak{W}^{-1}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda},\mathfrak{W}^{-1}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda})=:\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{Q}^{\mathfrak{W},\Lambda}(\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda},\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}).\end{split}

(4.51)

The reverse implication also follows using
Equation

4.47
.
Note that this equivalence depends on the interactions involved being in the domain of
\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}^{-1}
, as defined above in terms of absolute convergence in norms.
On this domain, however, the fixed point problem is equivalent to

\left.\frac{\,\text{\rm d}}{\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda}\left[\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}^{*}\right]\right|_{\Lambda=1}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{Q}^{\mathfrak{W}}(\mathcal{W}^{*},\mathcal{W}^{*})

(4.52)

with the convention
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathfrak{W}}:=\mathcal{Q}^{\mathfrak{W},1}
, cf.
Equation

1.9
.
Note that when
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}\in\mathsf{pot}_{\textrm{finite}}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{Q}^{(\Lambda)}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})(\psi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1/2)^{m}}{m!}\left\langle\frac{\delta}{\delta\psi},P_{\Lambda}\frac{\delta}{\delta\psi}\right\rangle^{m}\left\langle\frac{\delta}{\delta\psi}\mathcal{V}(\psi),G_{\Lambda}\frac{\delta}{\delta\psi}\mathcal{W}(\psi)\right\rangle

(4.53)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\left\langle\frac{\delta}{\delta\psi},P_{\Lambda}\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi}\right\rangle^{n}\left\langle\frac{\delta}{\delta\psi}\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{V}(\psi),G_{\Lambda}\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi}\mathfrak{W}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}(\varphi)\right\rangle\Big|_{\varphi=\psi}

(cf.
Equation

1.9
; this can also be verified directly using
Equation

4.42
but the resulting calculation is more involved) since all of the sums involved are finite, and so recalling the definition of
\mathcal{Q}^{(\Lambda)}
in
Equation

4.29

Q^{\mathfrak{W},\Lambda}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})[f]=\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}&\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in 2\mathbb{N}+2\\
j+k=\ell+2n+2\\
j,k\geq n+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\tfrac{1}{2}j-1}\frac{j!}{(j-n-1)!}\frac{k!}{(k-n-1)!}\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{\ell}}\sigma(\pi)\\
&\times\int\int\begin{aligned} G_{\Lambda}(x_{1},y_{1})\prod_{r=2}^{n+1}P_{\Lambda}(x_{r},y_{r})[\pi f](x_{n+2},\dots,x_{j},y_{n+2},\dots,y_{k})&\\
\times V_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})W_{k}(\boldsymbol{y})\,\text{\rm d}^{jd}\boldsymbol{x}\,\text{\rm d}^{kd}\boldsymbol{y}&\end{aligned}\end{aligned}

(4.54)

where I have used antisymmetry to rearrange the arguments, giving an additional sign;
also, for legibility I have omitted the discrete indices, which are arranged in the same fashion as the position arguments.
Using the antisymmetry of
W_{k}
further, the product of
P_{\Lambda}
factors can be replaced by

\frac{1}{n!}\det\left[P_{\Lambda}(x_{i+1},y_{j+1})\right]_{i,j\in{\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}}=(-1)^{\tfrac{1}{2}n-1}\frac{1}{n!}\Pf\check{}\mathfrak{P}_{n}(x_{2},..,x_{n+1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n+1})

(4.55)

where
\check{}\mathfrak{P}_{n}
is the
2n\times 2n
matrix with elements

\check{}\mathfrak{P}_{ij}(\boldsymbol{z})=\begin{cases}P_{\Lambda}(z_{i},z_{j}),&i\leq n<j\text{ or }j\leq n<i\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(4.56)

so

Q^{\mathfrak{W},\Lambda}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})[f]=\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}&(-1)^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in 2\mathbb{N}+2\\
j+k=\ell+2n+2\\
j,k\geq n+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{(j+n)/2}jk\binom{j-1}{n}\binom{k-1}{n}\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{\ell}}\sigma(\pi)\\
&\times\int\int\begin{aligned} &G_{\Lambda}(x_{1},y_{1})\Pf\check{}\mathfrak{P}_{n}(x_{2},..,x_{n+1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n+1})\\
&\times[\pi f](x_{n+2},\dots,x_{j},y_{n+2},\dots,y_{k})V_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})W_{k}(\boldsymbol{y})\,\text{\rm d}^{jd}\boldsymbol{x}\,\text{\rm d}^{kd}\boldsymbol{y}.\end{aligned}\end{aligned}

(4.57)

When using this formula later, I will find it helpful to note that

jk\binom{j-1}{n}\binom{k-1}{n}

is the number of ways of choosing which of the arguments of
V_{j}
and
W_{k}
appear as arguments of which other factors in the integral.

At
\Lambda=1
, using a Gram-Hadamard bound (see
Lemmas

22
and
24
in
Appendix

B
) to bound
|\Pf\check{}\mathfrak{P}_{n}|\leq C_{\textup{GH}}^{n}
, and similarly for its derivatives, so
|\check{}\mathfrak{P}_{n}\otimes f|\leq C_{\textup{GH}}^{n}|f|
referring to the norm of
\mathsf{slow}_{\ell+2n}
; using this with
Equation

4.15

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{\ell}^{\mathfrak{W}}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in 2\mathbb{N}+2\\
j+k=\ell+2n+2\\
j,k\geq n+1\end{subarray}}jk\binom{j-1}{n}\binom{k-1}{n}C_{\textup{GH}}^{n}|G|_{+}\|V_{j}\|\|W_{k}\|

(4.58)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\textup{GH}}^{-(\ell/2)-1}|G|_{+}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in 2\mathbb{N}+2\end{subarray}}3^{j}3^{k}C_{\textup{GH}}^{j/2}C_{\textup{GH}}^{k/2}\|V_{j}\|\|W_{k}\|

and so
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathfrak{W}}
is well defined and continuous whenever the sum on the right hand side is finite.

5
Relevant and irrelevant parts

In this section, I introduce a linear operator
\mathfrak{L}
on
\mathsf{pot}
with the properties

•

\mathfrak{L}(\mathsf{pot})
, i.e. the range of
\mathfrak{L}
is finite dimensional, and

•

letting
\mathfrak{I}=1-\mathfrak{L}
, there are constants
C_{8}<\infty,C_{9}>0
such that

\|\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{I}\mathcal{V}\|_{\ell}\leq C_{\ref*{CCirrel-pre}}\Lambda^{-C_{\ref*{CCirrel-power}}}\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\ell}\quad\forall\Lambda\geq 1,\ \ell\in 2\mathbb{N}+2.

(5.1)

Following the standard renormalization group terminology reviewed in
Section

2
,
\mathfrak{L}\mathcal{V}
identifies the local relevant part of some
\mathcal{V}\in\mathsf{pot}
, while
Equation

5.1
is a precise way of stating that the remaining part
\mathfrak{I}\mathcal{V}
is irrelevant (which is how I will refer to it).

Actually it will be more convenient to decompose
\mathfrak{I}=\mathfrak{I}_{\textup{many}}+\mathfrak{I}_{\textup{int}}
, where
\mathfrak{I}_{\textup{many}}
is simply the projection onto sequences whose first few terms vanish (see
Equation

5.16
), both since the proof of
Equation

5.1
is quite different in the two corresponding cases (see
Equations

5.19
and
10
) and because I will treat the resulting parts differently in the construction of the fixed point.

For
\ell\geq 2
, let
\mathsf{mi}_{\ell}
be the set of multiindices
{\llbracket 1,\ell\rrbracket}\times{\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}\to\mathbb{N}
, and let
\mathsf{mi}_{\ell}^{-}
be the set of
{\underline{m}}\in\mathsf{mi}_{\ell}
with
m_{\ell,j}=0
for
j=1,\dots,d
. For each

, I denote

\mathcal{L}f=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{m}}\in\mathsf{mi}_{\ell}^{-}:\\
\ell[\psi]+|{\underline{m}}|\leq d\end{subarray}}f_{{\underline{m}}}^{\mathcal{L}}

(5.2)

with

f_{{\underline{m}}}^{\mathcal{L}}\colon\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\frac{1}{{\underline{m}}!}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathcal{L}})^{{\underline{m}}}\partial^{{\underline{m}}}f_{\boldsymbol{a}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathcal{L}}),

(5.3)

recall
\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathcal{L}}:=(x_{\ell},\dots,x_{\ell})
. Since we assumed at the beginning of
Section

4
that
2[\psi]+R-d>0
,

5.2
involves only

Since (recalling the definition of

in

)

and

\left|(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathcal{L}})^{{\underline{n}}}\right|\leq(\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{x}))^{|{\underline{n}}|}

\boldsymbol{x}
(note that this bound is saturated when

4.3
there is a

such that

uniformly for

,
we have

&\leq C_{10}\ |f|,\end{split}

(5.6)

and so

|\mathcal{L}f|\leq C_{11}|f|,

(5.7)

and in particular
\mathcal{L}f\in\mathsf{slow}_{\ell}
. Since the sum in

5.2
is empty for
\ell>d/[\psi]

can be taken to be uniform in
\ell
.

Then let
\mathfrak{L}
be the linear map on
\mathsf{kernel}
with

)

.
For

\displaystyle V_{\ell}[f_{{\underline{m}}}^{\mathcal{L}}]

\displaystyle=\int\,\text{\rm d}^{\ell d}\boldsymbol{x}V_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{x})(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathcal{L}})^{{\underline{m}}}\partial^{{\underline{m}}}f(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathcal{L}})=\int\,\text{\rm d}^{\ell d}\boldsymbol{x}V_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathcal{L}})(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathcal{L}})^{{\underline{m}}}\partial^{{\underline{m}}}f(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathcal{L}})

with

,

Recalling the expression in

(5.13)

It is also evident that
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}f=\mathcal{L}f
, and so
\mathfrak{L}^{2}=\mathfrak{L}
on

Appendix A
The Steiner diameter

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
{\overline{xy}}
denote the closed line segment from
x
to
y
.
Then for
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell d}

let
T(\boldsymbol{x})
be the set of spanning trees
\boldsymbol{x}
, that is

T(\boldsymbol{x}):=\left\{t\subset\{x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\}^{2}:\ \bigcup_{e\in t}{\overline{e_{1}e_{2}}}\textup{ is simply connected}\right\},

(A.1)

and for
t\in T(\boldsymbol{x})
let

|t|:=\sum_{e\in t}|e_{1}-e_{2}|.

(A.2)

Then the Steiner diameter of the set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\}
or equivalently the tuple
\boldsymbol{x}
is

\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\mathcal{T}(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}):=\inf_{k=0,1,\dots}\ \inf_{x_{\ell+1},\dots,x_{\ell+k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\ \inf_{t\in T(\boldsymbol{x})}|t|,

(A.3)

that is the infimum of the length of all trees which connect all of the points in
\boldsymbol{x}
, with additional vertices allowed. In fact the infimum is realized, but this is not important here.
What is important is that this obviously has the following properties.
For brevity, I will write
\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{x}_{n})
for the Steiner diameter of the sequence obtained by concatenating
\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{x}_{n}
.
Then for any tuples
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}
,

1.

For
\ell=2
,
\mathcal{T}(x_{1},x_{2})=|x_{1}-x_{2}|
,

2.

\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{x})\leq\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})
,

3.

\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z})\leq\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})+\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z})
.

The inequalities follow immediately from inclusion relationships over the sets of trees involved.

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as, for example,

\begin{split}g^{\textup{hk}}(x-y)&=\frac{2}{\Gamma\left(1+\tfrac{d}{4}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}\int\frac{\,\text{\rm d}^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}k^{2}e^{i\;k\cdot(x-y)}\\
&=\int\begin{aligned} &\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\Gamma\left(1+\tfrac{d}{4}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}}|k|e^{-k^{2}/2}e^{ik\cdot x}\right)\\
&\times\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\Gamma\left(1+\tfrac{d}{4}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}}|k|e^{-k^{2}/2}e^{-ik\cdot y}\right)\,\text{\rm d}^{d}k\end{aligned}\end{split}

(B.2)

giving
x,y\mapsto g^{\textup{hk}}(x-y)
a decomposition with constant

{C_{30}}=\frac{2}{\Gamma\left(1+\tfrac{d}{4}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}\int k^{2}e^{-k^{2}}\,\text{\rm d}^{d}k;

(B.3)

also noting that

\partial_{x}^{{\underline{m}}}\partial_{y}^{{\underline{n}}}g^{\textup{hk}}(x-y)=\frac{2}{\Gamma\left(1+\tfrac{d}{4}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}\int\frac{\,\text{\rm d}^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}k^{2}(ik)^{{\underline{m}}}e^{ik\cdot x}(-ik)^{{\underline{n}}}e^{-ik\cdot y}

(B.4)

there is a similar decomposition for
(x,{\underline{m}}),(y,{\underline{n}})\mapsto\partial_{x}^{{\underline{m}}}\partial_{y}^{{\underline{n}}}g^{\textup{hk}}(x-y)
(restricting to
|{\underline{m}}|,|{\underline{n}}|\leq 2
) with a different constant
C_{31}
.
Following the same procedure with the decomposition
\widetilde{\xi}=\sqrt{\widetilde{\xi}}\sqrt{\widetilde{\xi}}
gives a similar decomposition for
g^{\xi}

Given two functions
g_{1}:X_{1}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
,
g_{2}:X_{2}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
with Gram compositions with respective constants
K_{1},K_{2}
the function
(x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\mapsto g_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})g_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})
clearly has a Gram decomposition given by simply taking the tensor products, which has constant
K_{1}K_{2}
.
For example, since
\Omega_{ab}
defined in
Equation

2.2
trivially has a Gram decomposition with constant 1,
G=\Omega g
has a Gram decomposition with the same constant
C_{\ref*{CCgram_g_base}}
as
g
.

More generally, if we assume (as in
Section

4
) that
G
(with up to
R
derivatives on each argument) has Gram constant
C_{\ref*{CCgram_g}}
, then for
\Lambda\in[0,1]
, it is easy enough to see that
G_{\Lambda}(x,y)=\Lambda^{2[\psi]-1}G(\Lambda x,\Lambda y)
does with
C_{\ref*{CCgram_g}}\Lambda^{2[\psi]-1}
(for
\Lambda>1
the derivatives make this larger).
Then

Lemma 22
.

For a given
R,s\in\mathbb{N}+1
,
[\psi]>0
, and
G\in\mathsf{diffy}_{2}
with Gram constant
C_{\ref*{CCgram_g}}
,
all of the functions

(x,a,{\underline{m}}),(y,b,{\underline{n}})\mapsto\partial_{x}^{{\underline{m}}}\partial_{y}^{{\underline{n}}}\int_{0}^{1}f(\Lambda)G_{\Lambda}(x,y)\,\text{\rm d}\Lambda

(B.5)

with
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
,
\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|f(x)|\leq 1

have a Gram decomposition with constant (at most)
C_{\textup{GH}}=C_{\ref*{CCgram_g}}/2[\psi]
.

This includes
P
and also some other related functions introduced in
Section

6.2
.

Proof.

Denoting the Gram decomposition of
G_{\Lambda}
by

\partial_{x}^{{\underline{m}}}\partial_{y}^{{\underline{n}}}[G_{\Lambda}(x,y)]_{ab}=\left\langle\gamma^{(\Lambda)}_{x,a,{\underline{m}}},\tilde{\gamma}^{(\Lambda)}_{y,b,{\underline{n}}}\right\rangle=\Lambda^{2[\psi]+|{\underline{m}}|+|{\underline{n}}|-1}\left\langle\gamma_{\Lambda x,a,{\underline{m}}},\tilde{\gamma}_{\Lambda y,b,{\underline{n}}}\right\rangle

(B.6)

(I write this out to emphasize that these are all on the same Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
independent of
\Lambda
),
the function of interest trivially has a Gram decomposition on
\mathcal{H}\otimes L^{2}(0,1)
with the advertised constant.
∎

Lemma 23
.

If
f,g:X^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
have Gram decompositions with the same constant
K
, then
f-g
has a Gram decomposition with constant
2K
.

Proof.

Writing
f(x,y)=\left\langle\varphi_{x},\tilde{\varphi}_{y}\right\rangle
and
g(x,y)=\left\langle\gamma_{x},\tilde{\gamma}_{y}\right\rangle
, then

(f-g)(x,y)=\left\langle\varphi_{x}\oplus\gamma_{x},\tilde{\varphi}_{y}\oplus(-\tilde{\gamma}_{y})\right\rangle.

∎

The key application of these decompositions is the following bound:

Theorem 24

(Gram-Hadamard bound)
.

Let
M
be a
2n\times 2n
antisymmetric matrix such that
M_{ij}=g(x_{i},x_{j})
for some
x_{1},\dots,x_{2n}\in X
,
g:X^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
with Gram constant
K
.

Then
\Pf M\leq K^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
G:\mathbb{C}^{2n}\to\mathcal{H}
,
z\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{2n}z_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}}
and similarly
\tilde{G}
, so that
M=G^{*}\tilde{G}
.
Letting
P
be the projection onto the span of
\left\{\gamma_{x_{1}},\dots,\gamma_{x_{2n}}\right\}
,

M=G^{*}\tilde{G}=G^{*}P\tilde{G}=(G^{*}P)(P\tilde{G})=:\hat{G}^{*}\hat{\tilde{G}}

(B.7)

where
\hat{G},\hat{\tilde{G}}
are
2n\times 2n
matrices whose rows are vectors with norm at most
\sqrt{K}
. Then by Hadamard’s inequality

|\det\hat{G}|,|\det\hat{\tilde{G}}|\leq K^{n};

(B.8)

thus
|\det M|\leq K^{2n}
, and since
(\Pf M)^{2}=\det M
this gives the desired bound.
∎

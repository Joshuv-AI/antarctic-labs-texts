Apex Graphs and Cographs
Jagdeep Singh, Vaidy Sivaraman, Thomas Zaslavsky

Abstract
. A class 𝒢 \mathcal{G} of graphs is called hereditary if it is closed under taking induced subgraphs. We denote by 𝒢 apex \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{apex}} the class of graphs G G that contain a vertex v v such that G − v G-v is in 𝒢 \mathcal{G} . Borowiecki, Drgas-Burchardt, and Sidorowicz proved that if a hereditary class 𝒢 \mathcal{G} has finitely many forbidden induced subgraphs, then so does 𝒢 apex \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{apex}} . We provide an elementary proof of this result. The hereditary class of cographs consists of all graphs G G that can be generated from K 1 K_{1} using complementation and disjoint union. A graph is an apex cograph if it contains a vertex whose deletion results in a cograph. Cographs are precisely the graphs that do not have the 4 4 -vertex path as an induced subgraph. Our main result finds all such forbidden induced subgraphs for the class of apex cographs.

1.
Introduction

We consider finite, simple, and undirected graphs. An
induced subgraph
of a graph
G
is a graph
H
that can be obtained from
G
by a sequence of vertex deletions; we write
G[T]
for the subgraph if
T
is the set of remaining vertices. We say that
G

contains

H
when
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
. A class
\mathcal{G}
of graphs is called
hereditary
if it is closed under taking induced subgraphs.

A
cograph
is a graph that can be generated from the single-vertex graph
K_{1}
using the operations of complementation and disjoint union.
Due to the following characterization, cographs are also called
P_{4}
-free graphs
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

A graph
G
is a cograph if and only if
G
does not contain the path
P_{4}
on four vertices as an induced subgraph.

The
apex class,
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{apex}}
,
for a class
\mathcal{G}
is the class of graphs
G
that contain a vertex
v
such that
G-v
is in
\mathcal{G}
. Note that if a graph class
\mathcal{G}
is hereditary, then
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{apex}}
is also hereditary. A graph
G
is an
apex cograph
if it has a vertex
v
such that
G-v
is a cograph. In Theorem
1.3
we characterize apex cographs in the spirit of Theorem
1.1
.

For a hereditary class
\mathcal{G}
, we call a graph
H
a
forbidden induced subgraph
for
\mathcal{G}
if
H
is not in
\mathcal{G}
but every proper induced subgraph of
H
is in
\mathcal{G}
. Thus, a graph
G
is in
\mathcal{G}
if and only if
G
contains no forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}
. It is easy to see that the class of cographs is hereditary; by Theorem
1.1
it has only one forbidden induced subgraph. Borowiecki, Drgas-Burchardt and Sidorowicz
[
1
]
proved that in general, if a hereditary class
\mathcal{G}
has a finite number of forbidden induced subgraphs, then so does
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{apex}}
. The precise statement is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary class of graphs and let
c
denote the maximum number of vertices in a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}
. Then every forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{apex}}
has at most
\lfloor{\frac{1}{4}(c+2)^{2}}\rfloor
vertices.

We give a new, simple proof of Theorem
1.2
in Section 2. As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
, the number of vertices of a forbidden induced subgraph for the class of apex cographs is at most nine. In Section 3, we show that there are none with nine vertices and we list all of the forbidden induced subgraphs.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a forbidden induced subgraph for the class of apex cographs. Then
5\leq|V(G)|\leq 8
, and
G
or its complement
\overline{G}
is isomorphic to

or one of the graphs in Figures

,
2
, and

.

2.
Apex Exclusions

The main part of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is the following reduction lemma.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary class of graphs and let
c
denote the maximum number of vertices in a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}
.
If
G
is a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{apex}}
, then there are distinct subsets

and
T
of

such that
G[S]
and
G[T]
induce a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}
. Moreover, if
|S\cap T|=k
is minimal, then
|V(G)|\leq(c-k)(k+2)
.

Proof.

Since
G
is not in
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{apex}}
, there is no vertex
v
of
G
such that
G-v
is in
\mathcal{G}
. Thus,
G-v
contains a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}
for every vertex
v
of
G
. It follows that

V(G)
has distinct subsets
S
and
T
such that each of
G[S]
and
G[T]
induces a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}
.

First we consider the possibility that
S\cap T
is empty. If it were empty, there would be no vertex in
V(G)-(S\cup T)
, because if there were such a vertex
v
, then
G-v
would be a proper induced subgraph of
G
but not in
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{apex}}
as it would contain two vertex-disjoint forbidden induced subgraphs for
\mathcal{G}
. This is a contradiction to the minimality of
G
. It follows that
V(G)=S\cup T
, so
|V(G)|\leq 2c
, verifying the proposition. Therefore, we may assume
S\cap T
is nonempty for every choice of
S
and
T
.

We pick
S
and
T
such that
|S\cap T|
is minimal and let
S\cap T=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
. Since for each
1\leq i\leq k
, the graph
G-v_{i}
is not in
\mathcal{G}
, we have a subset
F_{i}
of
V(G)-v_{i}
such that
G[F_{i}]
induces a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}
. Observe that, if there is a vertex
x
in
V(G)-(S\cup T\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}F_{i})
, then
G-x
is not in
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{apex}}
, a contradiction. Therefore,
V(G)=S\cup T\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}
.

Next we show that for every
1\leq i\leq k
, we have
|F_{i}\cap(S\cup T)|\geq k+1
. By our choice of
S
and
T
, we have
|F_{i}\cap S|\geq k
and
|F_{i}\cap T|\geq k
. Since
F_{i}
does not contain
v_{i}
, it follows that
F_{i}
intersects both
S-T
and
T-S
. Therefore
|F_{i}\cap(S\cup T)|\geq k+1
. Since
V(G)=S\cup T\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}
, we have
|V(G)|\leq c+(c-k)+k(c-(k+1))=(c-k)(k+2)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Proposition
2.1
, we have
|V(G)|\leq(c-k)(k+2)
where
0\leq k\leq c
. The upper bound attains its maximum value when

. Therefore,
|V(G)|\leq\frac{1}{4}(c+2)^{2}
.
∎

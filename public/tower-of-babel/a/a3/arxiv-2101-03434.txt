Noetherian spaces in choiceless set theory 2020 AMS subject classification 03E25, 03E35.
Jindřich Zapletal

Abstract
I prove several independence results in the choiceless ZF+DC theory which separate algebraic and non-algebraic consequences of the axiom of choice.



1
Introduction

Geometric set theory
[
5
]
was developed in part to produce consistency results in choiceless ZF+DC set theory regarding various
\Sigma^{2}_{1}
sentences. In this paper, I produce several such consistency results which separate
\Sigma^{2}_{1}
sentences dealing with algebraic topics from those which do not. While the distinction may seem vague, the techniques of the paper show that there is in fact a clearly visible fracture line.

I isolate the notion of Noetherian balanced forcing and show that in the generic extensions of the choiceless Solovay model by such forcings, numerous
\Sigma^{2}_{1}
consequences of the axiom of choice fail. The following theorem, stated using the conventions of geometric set theory
[
5
, Convention 1.7.18]
, provides a somewhat representative list.

Theorem 1.1
.

In cofinally Noetherian balanced extensions of the choiceless Solovay model,

1.

if
A_{0},A_{1}\subset 3^{\omega}
are non-meager sets, then there are points
x_{0}\in A_{0}
and
x_{1}\in A_{1}
such that the set
\{n\in\omega\colon x_{0}(n)=x_{1}(n)\}
is finite;

2.

if
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is a turbulent Polish group action with all orbits dense and meager, and
A_{0},A_{1}\subset X
are non-meager sets then there are points
x_{0}\in A_{0}
and
x_{1}\in A_{1}
which are orbit equivalent;

3.

(if the extension is
\sigma
-closed) outer Lebesgue measure is continuous in arbitrary increasing unions.

The theorem implies in particular that there is no non-principal ultrafilter on natural numbers in the extensions. Such an ultrafilter
U
could be used to divide sequences in
3^{\omega}
into three non-meager sets according to their
U
-prevalent value, violating item (1). It also implies that if
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is a turbulent Polish group action with an orbit equivalence relation
E
, then every
E
-invariant function
f
from
X
to another Polish space
Y
stabilizes on a co-meager set in the extensions. The reason is that for each basic open set
O\subset Y
, the set
\{x\in X\colon f(x)\in O\}
is
E
-invariant, and therefore meager or co-meager by (2).

It turns out that many posets adding objects to the choiceless Solovay model which are related to algebraic applications of the Axiom of Choice are Noetherian balanced. The following theorem provides a good list.

The following Suslin posets are

a poset adding a Hamel basis, for a Polish field

a poset coloring a given closed Noetherian graph without an uncountable clique

;

a poset coloring

[

;

a poset coloring the hypergraph of isosceles triangles in

[

;

There are attendant consistency results too numerous to list separately. For example, it is consistent relative to an inaccessible cardinal that ZF+DC holds, there is a transcendence basis for
\mathbb{R}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, there is no nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\omega

Section

2
Noetherian pairs of generic extensions

The technology of this paper rests on the treatment of Noetherian topologies from a descriptive set theoretic point of view. After plenty of hesitation, I have decided in the favor of the following presentation.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X,Y
be Polish spaces and
C\subset Y\times X
be a closed set. The set
C
is a
Noetherian subbasis
if there is no sequence
\langle b_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of finite subsets of
Y
such that the sets
D_{n}=\bigcap_{y\in b_{n}}C_{y}
for
n\in\omega
strictly decrease with respect to inclusion.

It is not difficult to see that being a Noetherian subbasis is a coanalytic property of the set
C
, therefore transitive among well-founded models of set theory. The terminology is justified by the following routine proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
C\subset Y\times X
be a Noetherian subbasis. Then the smallest topology on
X
containing all complements of vertical sections of
C
is Noetherian.

Proof.

Close the collection of vertical sections of
C
first under finite intersections, then under finite unions, obtaining a collection
\mathcal{A}
.

Claim 2.3
.

\mathcal{A}
contains no infinite sequences strictly decreasing with respect to inclusion.

Proof.

Suppose towards a contradiction that
\langle A_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is such a sequence. The definitory property of a Noetherian subbasis shows that there is a finite set
b\subset Y
such that, writing
C_{b}=\bigcap_{y\in b}C_{y}
, the sequence of intersections
\langle A_{n}\cap C_{b}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
does not stabilize and the set
C_{b}
is inclusion-minimal such. There must be
n\in\omega
such that
A_{n}\cap C_{b}
is a proper subset of
C_{b}
. Find a finite collection
\{c_{i}\colon i\in m\}
of finite subsets of
Y
such that
A_{n}=\bigcup_{i}B_{i}
where
B_{i}=\bigcap_{y\in c_{i}}C_{y}
. It is clear that there must be
i\in m
such that the sequence of intersections
\langle A_{k}\cap C_{b}\cap B_{i}\colon k\in\omega\rangle
does not stabilize, contradicting the minimal choice of the set
C_{b}
.
∎

Claim 2.4
.

\mathcal{A}
is closed under finite unions and arbitrary intersections.

Proof.

\mathcal{A}
is closed under finite unions and finite intersections by its definition. Now, if
a\subset\mathcal{A}
is any set, then by the previous claim there is a finite subset
b\subset a
such that
\bigcap a=\bigcap b
. Since
\bigcap b\in\mathcal{A}
, it is also the case that
\bigcap a\in\mathcal{A}
as desired.
∎

It is now clear from Claim
2.4
that the collection of the complements of sets in
\mathcal{A}
is a topology, it is the smallest topology containing all complements of vertical sections of the set
C
, and that it is Noetherian by Claim
2.3
.
∎

The following observation will be key in numerous contexts.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
X,Y
be Polish spaces and
C\subset Y\times X
be a Noetherian subbasis. If
a\subset Y
is any set, then there is a finite set
b\subset a
such that
\bigcap_{y\in a}C_{y}=\bigcap_{y\in b}C_{y}
.

Proof.

If this failed for some set
a\subset Y
, it would be possible to build finite sets
b_{n}\subset a
for
n\in\omega
such that
b_{n}\subset b_{n+1}
and
\bigcap_{y\in b_{n}}C_{y}\neq\bigcap_{y\in b_{n+1}}C_{y}
. The sets
b_{n}
for
n\in\omega
would then violate the Noetherian property of the set
C
.
∎

In the way of an illustration, the following example of a Noetherian subbasis is ubiquitous in algebra.

Example 2.6
.

Let
n\in\omega
be a number and let
X
be a Polish field. Let
Y
be a Polish space indexing all polynomials with
n
free variables and coefficients in
X
. Then
C\subset Y\times X^{n}
, defined by
\langle y,\bar{x}\rangle\in C
if and only if
y(\bar{x})=0
is a Noetherian subbasis. The nonexistence of decreasing sequences of finite intersections of vertical sections of the set
C
is verified via the Hilbert Basis Theorem
[
2
]
.

The following definition is the key tool for connecting Noetherian topologies with geometric set theory.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X,Y
be Polish spaces and
C\subset Y\times X
be a Noetherian subbasis. Let
M
be a transitive model of set theory coding
X,Y
, and
C
. Let
A\subset X
be a set. The symbol
C(M,A)
denotes the set
\bigcap\{C_{y}\colon y\in Y\cap M
and
A\subseteq C_{y}\}
. If the set in the scope of the
\bigcap
sign is empty, then set
C(M,A)=X
.

The most common situation for applying this definition is that neither the set
A
nor any of its elements belong to the model
M
. Note that the set
C(M,A)
is coded in the model
M
as the intersection defining it can be obtained as an intersection
\bigcap_{y\in b}C_{y}
for a suitable finite set
b\subset Y\cap M
by Proposition
2.5
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
V[G_{0}]
and
V[G_{1}]
be generic extensions of
V
inside an ambient generic extension. Say that
V[G_{1}]
is a
Noetherian to

V[G_{0}]
(over
V
) if for every Noetherian subbasis
C\subset Y\times X
coded in
V
and for every set
A\subset X
in
V[G_{1}]
,
C(V[G_{0}],A)=C(V,A)
. Say that the extensions
V[G_{0}],V[G_{1}]
are
mutually Noetherian
if each is Noetherian to the other. Say that
V[G_{1}]
is
K_{\sigma}
-Noetherian to

V[G_{0}]
if the above equation holds for all
K_{\sigma}
Noetherian subbases coded in
V
, and similarly for the mutual
K_{\sigma}
-Noetherian relation.

It is useful to introduce special terminology for
K_{\sigma}
Noetherian subbases, since most natural examples are
K_{\sigma}
and
K_{\sigma}
subbases are easier to deal with in several respects which appear elsewhere
[
7
]
. Similar notions of perpendicularity always have a friendly relationship with product forcing, as recorded in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
n\geq 1
be a number. Let
V[G_{0}],V[G_{1}]
be generic extensions and
V[G_{1}]
is Noetherian to
V[G_{0}]
. Suppose that
P_{0}\in V[G_{0}]
and
P_{1}\in V[G_{1}]
be posets and
H_{0}\subset P_{0}
and
H_{1}\subset P_{1}
be filters mutually generic over
V[G_{0},G_{1}]
. Then
V[G_{1}][H_{1}]
is Noetherian to
V[G_{0}][H_{0}]
. Similarly for
K_{\sigma}
-Noetherian.

Proof.

Work in the model
V[G_{0},G_{1}]
and consider the poset
P_{0}\times P_{1}
. Let
X,Y
be Polish spaces and
C\subset Y\times X
be a Noetherian subbasis coded in
V
. Let
p_{0}\in P_{0}
and
p_{1}\in P_{1}
be conditions and let
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
be respective
P_{0},P_{1}
-names in the models
V[G_{0}],V[G_{1}]
such that
p_{0}\Vdash\tau_{0}\subset X
,
\tau_{1}\subset Y
is a finite set, and
\langle p_{0},p_{1}\rangle\Vdash\bigcap_{y\in\tau_{0}}C_{y}=C(V[G_{0}][H_{0}],\tau_{1})
; I must produce a ground model coded closed set
D\subset X
such that
p_{0}\Vdash\bigcap_{y\in\tau_{0}}C_{y}=D
.

Working in
V[G_{1}]
, form the closed set
A\subset X
as
A=X\setminus\bigcup\{O\colon O\subset X
is open and
p_{1}\Vdash O\cap\tau_{1}=0
. By the initial assumptions on the models
V[G_{0}]
and
V[G_{1}]
,
C(V[G_{0}],A)=C(V,A)
holds; write
D
for the common value. Observe that
p_{1}\Vdash\tau_{1}\subset D
. It will be enough to show that
p_{0}\Vdash\bigcap_{y\in\tau_{0}}C_{y}=D
.

Since
p_{1}\Vdash\tau_{1}\subset D
, the only way how the equality can fail is that there is a condition
p^{\prime}_{0}\leq p_{0}
forcing
\tau_{0}
to be a proper subset of
D
. Working in
V[G_{0}]
under this assumption, let
M_{0}
be a countable elementary submodel of some large structure containing
\tau_{0},D
, and
p^{\prime}_{0}
. Let
h_{0}\subset P_{0}\cap M_{0}
be a filter generic over the model
M_{0}
and let
b=\tau_{0}/h_{0}
. The set
E=\bigcap_{y\in b}C_{y}
is a proper subset of
D
, so
A\subseteq E
fails. Thus, there must be a basic open set
O\subset X
disjoint from
E
which contains some element of the set
A
. By the definitions, this means that there is a condition
p^{\prime\prime}_{0}\leq p^{\prime}_{0}
in the filter
h_{0}
which forces
\tau_{0}\cap O=0
, and a condition
p^{\prime}_{1}\leq p_{1}
which forces
\tau_{1}\cap O\neq 0
. This contradicts the initial assumptions on the conditions
p_{0},p_{1}
.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Mutually generic extensions are mutually Noetherian.

Proposition 2.11
.

Suppose that
V[G_{0},G_{1}]
is a generic extension. Suppose that
V[G_{0},G_{1}][K_{0}]
and
V[G_{0},G_{1}][K_{1}]
are mutually generic extensions of
V[G_{0},G_{1}]
and
H_{0}\in V[G_{0},G_{1}][K_{0}]
and
H_{1}\in V[G_{0},G_{1}][K_{1}]
are filters such that

1.

G_{0}\in V[H_{0}]
and
G_{1}\in V[H_{1}]
;

2.

V[G_{0},G_{1}]
is Noetherian to
V[H_{0}]
over
V[G_{0}]
;

3.

V[G_{1}]
is Noetherian to
V[H_{0}]
over
V
.

Then
V[H_{1}]
is Noetherian to
V[H_{0}]
over
V
. Similarly for
K_{\sigma}
-Noetherian.

Proof.

Let
X,Y
be Polish spaces,
C\subset Y\times X
a Noetherian basis coded in
V
, and suppose that
A\subset X
is a set in
V[H_{1}]
. To see that
C(V[H_{0}],A)=C(V,A)
, consider the set
B=C(V[G_{0},G_{1}],A)
. By the mutual genericity of
K_{0},K_{1}
and Proposition

,
C(V[G_{0},G_{1},K_{0}],A)=B

must hold: the left-to-right inclusion follows from

, and the right-to-left inclusion follows the fact that

C(V[H_{0}],A)=C(V,A)

∎

The following proposition collects several useful features of mutually Noetherian extensions.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
V[G_{0}],V[G_{1}]
be mutually Noetherian extensions. Then

1.

2^{\omega}\cap V[G_{0}]\cap V[G_{1}]=2^{\omega}\cap V
;

2.

let
X
be a Polish field,
p(\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1})
a polynomial with all parameters in
V
and all free variables listed. Let
\bar{x}_{0}\in V[G_{0}]
and
\bar{x}_{1}\in V[G_{1}]
be tuples such that
X\models p(\bar{x}_{0},\bar{x}_{1})=0
. Then there is a tuple
\bar{x}_{1}^{\prime}\in V
such that
X\models p(\bar{x}_{0},\bar{x}^{\prime}_{1})=0
;

3.

let
\phi(\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1})
be a formula of the language of real closed fields with real parameters in
V
, with all free variables listed. Let
V[G_{0}],V[G_{1}]
be mutually Noetherian extensions and
\bar{x}_{0}\in V[G_{0}]
and
\bar{x}_{1}\in V[G_{1}]
be tuples of reals such that
\mathbb{R}\models\phi(\bar{x}_{0},\bar{x}_{1})
holds. Then there is a tuple
\bar{x}^{\prime}_{1}\in V
of reals such that
\mathbb{R}\models\phi(\bar{x}_{0},\bar{x}^{\prime}_{1})
holds.

Proof.

For (1), let
X=Y=2^{\omega}
and let
C\subset Y\times X
be the diagonal; this is a Noetherian basis. Now, suppose that
x\in X\cap V[G_{0}]\cap V[G_{1}]
is a point. By the Noetherian assumption,
C(V[G_{0}],\{x\})=C(V,\{x\})=\{x\}
. Since the set
C(V,\{x\})
is coded in
V
, this is possible only if
x\in V
.

For (2), let
n_{0}=|\bar{v}_{0}|
and
n_{1}=|\bar{v}_{1}|
. Consider the closed set
C\subset X^{n_{0}}\times X^{n_{1}}
consisting exactly the pairs
\langle\bar{y}_{0},\bar{y}_{1}\rangle
if
p(\bar{y}_{0},\bar{y}_{1})=0
; this is a Noetherian subbasis by the Hilbert Basis Theorem. The Noetherian assumption shows that
C(V[G_{0}],\{\bar{x}_{1}\})=C(V,\{\bar{x}_{1}\})
. Any tuple
x^{\prime}_{1}
in this set in the ground model will work as in (2), since
C(V[G_{0}],\bar{x}_{1})\subseteq\{\bar{y}_{1}\colon p(\bar{x}_{0},\bar{y}_{1})=0\}
.

For (3), let
n_{0}=|\bar{v}_{0}|
and
n_{1}=|\bar{v}_{1}|
. Use the quantifier elimination theorem for real closed fields
[
6
, Theorem 3.3.15]
to assume that
\phi
is quantifier-free. Then
\phi
is a boolean combination of statements of the form
p(\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1})>0
and
p(\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1})=0
for some polynomials
p
with coefficients in
V
. Let
p_{i}
for
i\in j
be a list of all polynomials used in this boolean combination. Let
a\subset j
be the set of all indices
i
such that
p_{i}(\bar{x}_{0},\bar{x}_{1})=0
and let
q(\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1})=\Sigma_{i\in a}p_{i}(\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1})^{2}
. Consider the set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
consisting of all pairs
\langle\bar{y}_{0},\bar{y}_{1}\rangle
such that
q(\bar{y}_{0},\bar{y}_{1})=0
.
C
is a Noetherian subbasis by the Hilbert Basis Theorem, and by the Noetherian assumption on the models
V[G_{0}],V[G_{1}]
,
C(V[G_{0}],\bar{x}_{1})=C(V,\bar{x}_{1})
. Let
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
be a rational open box containing
\bar{x}_{0}
such that for each
i\in j\setminus a
, the values
p_{i}(\bar{x}_{0},\bar{y}_{1})
have the same sign for all
\bar{y}_{1}\in O
. By a Mostowski absoluteness argument, there must be a point
\bar{x}^{\prime}_{1}\in O\cap C(V,\bar{x}_{1})
in
V
since there is such a point
\bar{x}_{1}
in
V[G_{1}]
. The point
\bar{x}^{\prime}_{1}
works as required.
∎

It may seem difficult to verify that given two generic extensions are mutually Noetherian. In this paper, this is always done using the following
duplication criterion
.

Definition 2.13
.

Suppose that
P
is a poset,
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
are
P
-names for subsets of the ground model. Say that
\tau_{0}
is
duplicable over

\tau_{1}
in

if for every condition

there is a generic extension
V[K]
and in it, a sequence
\langle H_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in\kappa\rangle
such that

1.

is an uncountable ordinal in

;

2.

each

is a filter generic over the ground model containing the condition

3.

\tau_{1}/H_{\alpha}
is the same for all

;

4.

for disjoint finite sets

V[\tau_{0}/H_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in a]\cap V[\tau_{0}/H_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in b]=V
.

Proposition 2.14
.

Suppose that
P
is a poset,
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
are
P
-names for subsets of the ground model, and
\tau_{0}
is duplicable over
\tau_{1}
. Then
P
forces
V[\tau_{0}]
to be Noetherian to
V[\tau_{1}]
over
V
.

Proof.

Suppose towards a contradiction that the conclusion fails. Then there must be a condition
p\in P
, Polish spaces
X,Y
, a Noetherian subbasis
C\subset Y\times X
coded in
V
, and a name
\sigma
for a subset of
X
in the model
V[\tau_{1}]
such that
p
forces
C(V[\tau_{0}],\sigma)
to be strictly smaller than
C(V,\sigma)
. Basic forcing theory shows that
V[\tau_{1}]
is a generic extension of the ground model, and that we may assume that there is a poset
Q_{1}
such that
p\Vdash\tau_{1}\subset\check{Q}_{1}
is a filter generic over the ground model. We also may assume that
\sigma
is in fact a

-name.

Move to a generic extension
V[K]
in which a sequence
\langle H_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in\kappa\rangle
satisfies the items of Definition
2.13
. Write
A\subset X
for the common value of
\sigma/(\tau_{1}/H_{\alpha})
for all ordinals
\alpha\in\kappa
and write
C_{\alpha}=C(V[H_{\alpha}],A)
. For each ordinal
\beta\in\omega_{1}
let
D_{\beta}=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\omega_{1}\setminus\beta}C_{\beta}
. The sequence
\langle D_{\beta}\colon\beta\in\omega_{1}\rangle
is an inclusion-increasing uncountable sequence of closed subsets of
X
, and as such it has to stabilize at some ordinal
\beta_{0}
. Write
D\subset X
for the stable value. Use the Noetherian property of the subbasis

to find a finite set
b_{0}\subset\omega_{1}\setminus\beta_{0}
such that

. Let
\beta_{1}\in\omega_{1}
be an ordinal larger than
\max(b_{0})
and find a finite set

such that

. By the intersection assumption on the sequence of the generic extensions,

must hold. Thus, the set

is in fact coded in

then shows that

and the proposition follows.

This section contains several example of mutually duplicable names in forcing, which by Proposition
2.14
always lead to mutually Noetherian pairs of extensions. First, a warm-up example.

Example 3.1
.

If
Q_{0},Q_{1}
are posets with the respective names
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
for their generic filters and

, then

are mutually duplicable in

. To see that
\tau_{0}
is duplicable over
\tau_{1}
, let

be the successor of the maximum of
|Q_{0}|
and
|Q_{1}|
. For every condition

, consider the product forcing of
\kappa
-many copies of

and a single copy of
Q_{1}\restriction q_{1}
, yielding filters
G_{0}^{\alpha}\subset Q_{0}
and

H_{\alpha}=G_{0}^{\alpha}\times H_{1}
and observe that for finite disjoint sets

by the product forcing theorem.

The following two classes of examples deal with Cohen forcing. Recall that if

is a Polish space then

The first Cohen forcing example deals with product graphs. If

\tau_{1}
is duplicable over
\tau_{1}

is a condition in the poset

, where

and

are nonempty open sets. Let

-generic over

-generic over

Claim 3.7
.

Let

be a finite set. Then
V[x_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in a]\cap V[x]=V
.

Proof.

Without loss, assume that the set

is nonempty, and write
\beta=\min(a)
. The point
g_{\beta}^{-1}
is
P_{\Gamma}
-generic over
V[x_{\beta}]

t=\langle g_{\alpha}\cdot g_{\beta}^{-1}\colon\beta\in a\setminus\{\beta\}\rangle

Claim 3.8

If

are disjoint finite sets then

Proof.

4
Preservation theorems

Any notion of perpendicularity similar to Definition
2.7
comes with a natural notion of balance for analytic forcings.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
P
be an analytic forcing.

1.

A pair
\langle Q,\sigma\rangle
is
Noetherian balanced
if
Q\Vdash\sigma\in P
and for any pair
V[G_{0}]
,
V[G_{1}]
of mutually Noetherian extensions of the ground model, every pair
H_{0},H_{1}\subset Q
of filters generic over
V
and for every pair
p_{0}\in V[G_{0}]
,
p_{1}\in V[G_{1}]
of conditions stronger than
\sigma/H_{0},\sigma/H_{1}
respectively and belonging to the respective models
V[G_{0}],V[G_{1}]
, the conditions
p_{0},p_{1}\in P
have a common lower bound.

2.

P
is
Noetherian balanced
if for every condition
p\in P
there is a Noetherian balanced pair
\langle Q,\sigma\rangle
such that
Q\Vdash\sigma\leq\check{p}
.

Similar definitions apply for
K_{\sigma}
-Noetherian.

Note that if a poset is
K_{\sigma}
-Noetherian balanced, then it is Noetherian balanced, since the key quantification over pairs of generic extensions includes more of them in case of mutually
K_{\sigma}
-Noetherian pairs.

The supply of mutually Noetherian pairs of extensions provided in the previous section now makes it possible to prove several preservation theorems. They are stated using the parlance of
[
5
, Convention 1.7.18]
. Thus, given an inaccessible cardinal
\kappa
, a Suslin poset
P
is Noetherian balanced cofinally below
\kappa
if for every generic extension
V[K_{0}]
generated by poset of cardinality smaller than
\kappa
there is a larger generic extension
V[K_{1}]
generated by a poset of cardinality smaller than
\kappa
such that
V_{\kappa}[K_{1}]\models P
is Noetherian balanced.

is finite.

This proves Theorem
1.1
(1). I do not know if the conclusion holds also for non-null sets for the usual Borel probability measure on

Proof.

Let
\kappa

As a final note in this paper, it is interesting to see how the conclusions of Theorem
1.1
can be violated by balanced posets which are not Noetherian balanced. The conclusions of the first two items of Theorem
1.1
are easiest to violate using the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Borel meager graph on a Polish space
X
. In a balanced extension of the Solovay model, there are non-meager sets
A_{0},A_{1}\subset X
such that
(A_{0}\times A_{1})\cap\Gamma=0
.

Proof.

Without loss, assume that
\Gamma
is
F_{\sigma}
. Removing the meager set of all points
x\in X
such that the set
\{y\in X\colon x\mathrel{\Gamma}y\}
is non-meager from the space
X
if necessary, we may assume that for the set
\{y\in X\colon x\mathrel{\Gamma}y\}
is meager for every
x\in X
.

Let
P
be the partial order of all triples
p=\langle a_{p},b_{p},c_{p}\rangle
such that
a_{p},b_{p},c_{p}\subset X
are all countable sets,
a_{p}\cap b_{p}=0
, and
(a_{p}\times b_{p})\cap\Gamma=0
. The ordering is defined by
q\leq p
if
a_{p}\subseteq a_{q}
,
b_{p}\subseteq b_{q}
,
c_{p}\subseteq c_{q}
, and no element of
c_{p}
is
\Gamma
-connected to any element of
(a_{q}\setminus a_{p})\cup(b_{q}\setminus b_{p})
. It is not difficult to check that
P
is a
\sigma
-closed Suslin poset.

Claim 6.2
.

P
is a balanced forcing.

Proof.

Let
p\in P
be a condition. It will be enough to show that the pair
\langle\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,X),\langle\check{a}_{p},\check{b}_{p},(X\cap V)\rangle\rangle
is balanced in the poset
P
.

Indeed, let
V[G_{0}]
and
V[G_{1}]
be mutually generic extensions containing conditions
p_{0}=\langle a_{0},b_{0},c_{0}\rangle\in V[G_{0}]
and
p_{1}=\langle a_{1},b_{1},c_{1}\rangle\in V[G_{1}]
respectively, both stronger than
\langle a_{p},b_{p},(X\cap V)\rangle
. To show that
\langle a_{0}\cup a_{1},b_{0}\cup b_{1},c_{0}\cup c_{1}\rangle
is a common lower bound of the conditions
p_{0},p_{1}
, It is only necessary to observe that no element in
a_{0}\setminus a_{p}
is
\Gamma
-connected with any element of
b_{1}\cup c_{1}
; the other verification items are proved in a symmetric way.

Thus, towards a contradiction assume that
x_{0}\in a_{0}\setminus a_{p}
and
x_{1}\in b_{1}\cup c_{1}
are
\Gamma
-connected. Find a closed set
C\subset\Gamma
such that
\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\in C
. Let
e_{0}
be the set of basic open sets
O\subset X
such that
\{x_{0}\}\times O\cap\Gamma=0
; let
e_{1}
be the set of basic open sets
O\subset X
such that
x_{1}\in O_{1}
. The sets
e_{0}\in V[G_{0}]
and
e_{1}\in V[G_{1}]
are disjoint. By a mutual genericity argument
[
5
, Proposition 1.7.9]
, there are disjoint ground model sets
f_{0}\supset e_{0}
and
f_{1}\supset e_{1}
. By a Mostowski absoluteness argument between
V[G_{1}]
and
V
there is a point
x^{\prime}_{1}\in V
such that every basic open neighborhood of it belongs to
f_{1}
–since the point
x_{1}\in V[G]
is such. It follows that
\langle x_{0},x^{\prime}_{1}\rangle\in\Gamma
, and this contradicts the assumption that
p_{0}\leq\langle a_{p},b_{p},X\cap V\rangle
.
∎

If
G\subset P
is a filter generic over the choiceless Solovay model (or indeed any other model), let
A=\{a_{p}\colon p\in G\}
and
B=\{b_{p}\colon p\in G\}
. It is clear from the definition of the poset
P
that
(A\times B)\cap\Gamma=0
. To complete the proof of the theorem, it is enough to argue that the sets
A,B\subset X
are non-meager.

For this, return to the ground model, let
p\in P
be a condition, and let
C_{n}\subset X
be closed nowhere dense sets for
n\in\omega
. By a density argument, it is only necessary to find a condition
q\leq p
such that
a_{q}
contains a point in
X\setminus\bigcup_{n}C_{n}
. The arguument for
b_{q}
is the same. To produce the condition
q
, note that the set
D=\{y\in X\colon\exists z\in b_{p}\cup c_{p}\ z\mathrel{\Gamma}y\}
is meager by the initial assumptions on the graph
\Gamma
. Just pick a point
x\in X
which does not belong to the meager set
\bigcup_{n}C_{n}\cup D\cup b_{p}\}
, let
q=\langle a_{p}\cup\{x\},b_{p},c_{p}\rangle
, and note that
q\leq p
holds. The proof is complete.
∎

The conclusion of Theorem
1.1
(3) is more difficult to violate.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
X
be a Polish space and
I
a suitably definable ideal on
X
such that every countable subset of
X
is a subset of a
G_{\delta}
set in
I
. In a balanced extension of the Solovay model,
X
is the union of a collection of
I
-small sets, linearly ordered by inclusion.

In particular, it is possible to exhaust the unit interval
[0,1]
as a union of Lebesgue null sets linearly ordered with respect to inclusion.

Proof.

The partial order
P
consists of conditions
p=\langle\preceq_{p},b_{p}\rangle
such that for some countable set
\mathrm{supp}(p)\subset X
,
\preceq_{p}
is a linear preorder on
\mathrm{supp}(p)
and
b_{p}
is a countable set of pairs
\langle x,O\rangle
where
x\in\mathrm{supp}(p)
and
O\subset X
is an open set. In addition, if
y\preceq_{p}x
are points in
\mathrm{supp}(p)
and
\langle x,O\rangle\in b_{q}
then
y\in O
.
The ordering is defined by
q\leq p
if
\mathrm{supp}(p)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(q)
,
\preceq_{p}=\preceq_{p}\restriction\mathrm{supp}(p)
,
b_{p}\subseteq b_{q}
, and for every
y\in\mathrm{supp}(q)
, if
x
is
\preceq_{q}
-smallest element of
\mathrm{supp}(p)
such that
y\preceq_{q}x
, then
x\preceq_{q}y
.

To see the idea behind the definition, suppose that
G\subset P
is a generic filter and let
\preceq=\bigcup_{p\in G}\preceq_{p}
. An elementary density argument shows that
\preceq
is a linear preorder on all of
X
. The assumptions on the ideal
I
imply that for every element
x\in X
, the initial segment of
\preceq
determined by
x
belongs to the ideal
I
: if
p\in P
is a condition containing
x
in its support, there is a
G_{\delta}
set
\bigcap_{n}O_{n}\in I
containing all elements of
\mathrm{supp}(p)
, and then the condition
q\leq p
obtained from adding the pairs
\langle x,O_{n}\rangle
for
n\in\omega
into
b_{q}
ensures that the initial segment determined by
x
is a subset of
\bigcap_{n}O_{n}
.

It is not difficult to check that the poset
P
is analytic and
\sigma
-closed. The balance is the only hard part. Let
p\in P
be a condition. Adding some ordered pairs to
b_{p}
if necessary I may assume that if
x,y\in\mathrm{supp}(p)
then

1.

if
y\preceq_{p}x
and
\langle x,O\rangle\in b_{p}
then
\langle y,O\rangle\in b_{p}
;

2.

if
x\not\preceq_{p}y
then
\langle y,2^{\omega}\setminus\{x\}\rangle\in b_{p}
.

For each
y\in X
let
a(y)=\{x\in\mathrm{supp}(p)\colon y\in O
for every
O
with
\langle x,O\rangle\in b_{p}\}
. Note that by for each
x\in\mathrm{supp}(p)
,
a(x)=[x,\infty)
by (2) above. Define a relation
\preceq
on
2^{\omega}
by
y_{0}\preceq y_{1}
if
a_{y_{1}}\subseteq a_{y_{0}}
. Let also
b
be the set of all pairs
\langle y,O\rangle
such that for some
x\in a(y)
,
\langle x,O\rangle\in b_{p}
.

Claim 6.4
.

\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,X)
forces
\langle\preceq,b\rangle
is a condition in
P
stronger than
p
.

Proof.

I first show that
\langle\preceq,b\rangle
is forced to be a condition in the poset
P
. First of all,
\preceq
is clearly a preorder. It is linear: by (1) above each set
a(y)
is a cofinal segment of
\preceq_{p}
, and such sets are linearly ordered by inclusion. Now, suppose that
y\preceq x
and
\langle x,O\rangle\in b
. By the definitions,
\langle z,O\rangle\in b_{p}
for some
z\in a(x)
. Since
a(x)\subseteq a(y)
,
z\in a(y)
holds, so
y\in O
holds as desired.

To show that
\langle\preceq,b\rangle
is forced to be stronger than
p
, first note that
\preceq\restriction\mathrm{supp}(p)=\preceq_{p}
holds. To see that, let
x,y\in\mathrm{supp}(p)
be any points. If
y\preceq_{p}x
then
a(x)=[x,\infty)\subseteq a(y)=[y,\infty)
. If
y<x
are points in
\mathrm{supp}(p)
then
x\not\preceq y
by item (2) above. Finally, note that if
x
is the smallest element of
\mathrm{supp}(p)
such that
y\preceq x
, then
x\preceq y
. To see that, note that the assumptions imply that
a(y)=[x,\infty)
. The definition of
\preceq
implies that
x\preceq y
.
∎

For future reference, note that the set
\mathrm{supp}(p)
is dense in the preorder
\preceq
by the definitions. Also, for each
y\in X
, let
B_{y}=\bigcap\{O\colon\langle y,O\rangle\in b\}
and observe that
B_{y}=\{x\in X\colon x\preceq y\}
. This feature, that the initial segments of
\preceq
which include their supremum are the largest possible
G_{\delta}
sets, is the whole purpose of the construction of the preorder
\preceq
.

Now, I must show that
\langle\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,X),\langle\preceq,\check{b}\rangle\rangle
is a balanced pair. Suppose that
V[G_{0}],V[G_{1}]
are mutually generic extensions and
p_{0}=\langle\preceq_{0},b_{0}\rangle
and
p_{1}=\langle\preceq_{1},b_{1}\rangle
be any conditions in these respective models stronger than
\langle\preceq,b\rangle
. I must find a common lower bound of conditions
p_{0},p_{1}
. The common lower bound
p_{01}=\langle\preceq_{01},b_{01}\rangle
will have
\mathrm{supp}(p_{01})=\mathrm{supp}(p_{0})\cup\mathrm{supp}(p_{1})
and
b_{01}=b_{0}\cup b_{1}
. The definition of the preorder is the more difficult point.

I will have
\preceq_{0}\cup\preceq_{1}\subset\preceq_{01}
, and, naturally, if
x\preceq_{0}y\preceq_{1}z
for
x\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{0})
,
y\in V
and
z\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{1})
, then
x\preceq_{01}yz
. Finally suppose that
X\cap V=c\cup d
is a cut in the preorder
\preceq
and containing some elements of
\mathrm{supp}(p_{0})
(gathered in the set denoted by
e_{0}
) and some lements of
\mathrm{supp}(p_{1})
(gathered in a set
e_{1}
). Note that this means that
d
has no smallest element. I must show how to linearly preorder
e_{0}\cup e_{1}
in
\preceq_{01}
.

Let
f_{0},f_{1}
be the respective longest prewellordered initial segments of
\langle e_{0},\preceq_{0}\rangle
and
\langle e_{1},\preceq_{1}\rangle
inside the cut
c\cup d
. For definiteness assume that
f_{1}
is not shorter than
f_{0}
. Then, for
x_{0}\in e_{0}
and
x_{1}\in e_{1}
, put
\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle
into
\preceq_{01}
if
x_{0}\in f_{0}
and
x_{1}
is either in
e_{1}\setminus f_{1}
or
x_{1}
is in
f_{1}
at an ordinal rank at least that of the rank of
x_{0}
in
f_{0}
. Otherwise, put
\langle x_{1},x_{0}\rangle
into
\preceq_{01}
.

Claim 6.5
.

p_{01}\in P
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{01})
,
x_{0}\preceq_{01}x_{1}
and
\langle x_{1},O\rangle\in b_{01}
is a pair. I must show that
x_{0}\in O
holds. For definiteness assume that
x_{0}\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{0})
and
x_{1}\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{1})
and
\langle x_{1},O\rangle\in b_{1}
all hold.

Case 1.
There is
y\in V
such that
x_{0}\preceq_{0}y_{0}\preceq_{1}x_{1}
. In such a case, in the ground model consider the set
B_{y}
. Since
p_{0}\in P
, it must be the case that
x_{0}\in B_{y}
. Since
p_{1}\in P
, it must be the case that
B_{y}\cap V\subset O
, and by a mutual genericity argument
B_{y}\cap V[G_{0}]\subset O
. It follows that
x_{0}\in O
holds as desired.

Case 2.
Not case 1. Then
x_{0},x_{1}
are in the same
\preceq
-cut
X\cap V=c\cup d
. Since
c,d\in V[G_{0}]\cap V[G_{1}]
, a mutual genericity argument shows that
c,d\in V
. Since
p_{0},p_{1}\leq p
, it must be the case that the set
d
has no smallest element. Work in
V
. Note that the set
\mathrm{supp}(p)
is coinitial in
d
and that
c=\bigcap\{B_{y}\colon y\in\mathrm{supp}(p)\cap d\}
is a
G_{\delta}
-set. Since
p_{0}\in P
, it must be the case that
x_{0}\in c
. Since
p_{1}\in P
, it must be the case that
c\cap V\subset O
, and by a mutual genericity argument
c\cap V[G_{0}]\subset O
. It follows that
x_{0}\in O
holds as desired.
∎

It is now not difficult to show that
p_{01}
is a common lower bound of
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
. To show for example that
p_{01}\leq p_{0}
, suppose that
x\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{01})
and suppose that
y
is the smallest element of
\mathrm{supp}(p_{0})
such that
x\preceq_{01}y
; it must be proved that
y\preceq_{01}x
holds. If
x\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{0})
then this is clear from the fact that
\preceq_{0}
is a preorder. Suppose then that
x\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{1})
holds. If
y
is
\preceq_{0}
-equivalent to some element in
V
, then
y\preceq_{01}x
follows from
p_{1}\leq\langle\preceq,b\rangle
. Suppose then that
y
is not
\preceq_{0}
-equivalent to any point in
V
. Let
X\cap V=c\cup d
be the cut in
\preceq
into which both
x,y
fall. It cannot be the case that
y
falls into the illfounded part of
\mathrm{supp}(p_{0})
inside this cut, since that illfounded part is convex in
\preceq_{01}
and does not have a smallest element. Thus,
y
falls into the longest pre-well-ordered initial segment of this cut in
\mathrm{supp}(p_{0})
. Since
x\preceq_{01}y
holds, it must be the case that
x
falls into the pre-well-ordered segment of this cut in
\mathrm{supp}(p_{1})
and the ordinal rank of
x
and
y
in these two must be the same. Again,
y\preceq_{01}x
holds as desired.
∎

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a Liouville action for a harmonic diffeomorphism from a compact Riemann surface to a compact hyperbolic Riemann surface of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 . We derive the variational formula of this Liouville action for harmonic diffeomorphisms when the source Riemann surfaces vary with a fixed target Riemann surface.

Introduction

In mathematical physics, the Liouville action has been used as the action functional for the Liouville conformal field theory. In mathematics, this was constructed in the works of Takhtajan–Zograf
[
12
,
13
]
. They also proved several fundamental results of the Liouville action using the Teichmüller theory developed by Ahlfors–Bers. One of main results in
[
12
,
13
]
is that the Liouville action is a Kähler potential of the Weil–Petersson symplectic 2-form on Teichmüller space.

One novelty of the works
[
12
,
13
]
in the construction of the Liouville action
is the use of the projective structures on the Riemann surface. The projective structures determined by the geometric uniformizations in
[
9
,
12
,
13
]
define the bounding noncompact hyperbolic 3-manifolds determined by those uniformizations. In this geometric situation, the Liouville actions were proved to be the same as the renormalized volumes of the bounding hyperbolic 3-manifolds. We refer to

[
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
9
]
for the relation of the Liouville action with the renormalized volume.

The harmonic map theory has been one of the main tools in the study of Teichmüller space.
The basic fact of this approach is that
there exists a unique harmonic diffeomorphism for the hyperbolic metric on the target Riemann surface in the homotopy class of an arbitrary homeomorphism between two compact Riemann surfaces.
Given a harmonic map, there is an associated Hopf differential, which is a holomorphic quadratic differential
on the source Riemann surface. In
[
8
,
11
]
, it was proved that the Hopf differentials of harmonic diffeomorphisms give a natural parametrization of Teichmüller space
fixing the source Riemann surface. Another associated object to a harmonic map is its energy,
which can be considered as a functional on Teichmüller space varying one of the source or target (hyperbolic) Riemann surface and fixing the other.

A modest motivation of this paper was to relate two objects – the Liouville action and the energy of harmonic diffeomorphisms – so that we may
have a certain object sharing the interesting properties of these two objects. To explain our approach to this problem, let us recall the construction of the Liouville action by Takhtajan–Zograf. In
[
12
,
13
]
, they crucially used a map
denoted by
J
from the Poincaré half plane to the region of discontinuity for a Kleinian group determined by a geometric uniformization. Then the main ingredient in the definition of the Liouville action is given by the pullback of the Poincaré metric by
J^{-1}
.

A possible generalization of the Liouville action could be achieved by using other map instead of
J^{-1}
. In this paper, we develop this idea using a harmonic diffeomorphism which is canonically associated to the quasi-Fuchsian uniformization
for two marked compact hyperbolic Riemann surfaces.
By our construction, the Liouville action for a harmonic diffeomorphism contains the holomorphic energy of the harmonic map as a part.

As a first step to this study, we derive a variational formula for the Liouville action for harmonic diffeomorphisms. In this formula, the variation of the Liouville action for harmonic diffeomorphisms is described mainly in terms of the Schwarzian derivative and the Hopf differential of harmonic diffeomorphisms. The precise variational formula is given in Theorem
.
A main part of the proof of this theorem is based on the variational formula of the Liouville action for a smooth family of conformal metrics on Riemann surfaces, which generalizes the work of Takhtajan–Teo in
[
9
]
.

Our approach in this paper may raise several related questions. One of them is a possibility to obtain another Liouville action for harmonic diffeomorphisms modifying the construction of this paper. We take the term given by the holomorphic energy density in the pullback of the hyperbolic metric on the target Riemann surface by a harmonic diffeomorphism. But, we can also take
the Hopf differential part possibly among the parts of the pullback metric instead of our choice in this paper.
It is interesting to see how this different definition would provide us with a useful functional on Teichmüller space.
See Remark
3.4
for more detailed remark on this case.

Another natural question is the second variation of the Liouville action for diffeomorphisms. The second variation of the Liouville action defined by Takhtajan–Zograf in
[
12
,
13
]
gives
the Weil–Petersson symplectic 2-form on Teichmüller space. On the other hand, the energy functional of harmonic diffeomorphisms varying the source Riemann surfaces with a fixed target hyperbolic Riemann surface is a strictly plurisubharmonic function on Teichmüller space. This follows from that the Levi form given by the second variation of the energy functional is positive definite. For these, we refer to Tromba’s book
[
10
]
. Hence, as a common feature of the Liouville action and the energy functional of harmonic diffeomorphisms, one may wonder whether the Liouville action for diffeomorphisms would be a strictly plurisubharmonic function on Teichmïller space. The second variation of the Liouville action for harmonic diffeomorphisms and its possible applications will be studied elsewhere.

Finally let us explain the structure of this paper. We start with the basic definitions and terminologies for the Liouville action in Section
2
. This is a quick review of
[
9
, Section 2]
. In Section
3
, we present the basics of the Liouville action for harmonic diffeomorphisms and derive its variational formula. In Section
4
, we prove the variational formula of the Liouville action for a smooth family of conformal metrics following
[
9
, Section 4]
.

2
Liouville action for quasi-Fuchsian groups

Let us consider a compact Riemann surface
X
with genus
g\geq 2
. Then the Riemann surface
X
can be realized by the
Fuchsian uniformization
. It means that
X
is given by
a quotient space
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{U}
, where
\mathbb{U}
is the upper half plane and
\Gamma
is a marked, normalized Fuchsian group of the first kind. Here
\Gamma
is a finitely generated cocompact discrete subgroup of
\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
which has a standard representation with
2g
hyperbolic generators
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g},\beta_{g}
satisfying the relation

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\alpha_{1}^{-1}\beta_{1}^{-1}\cdots\alpha_{g}\beta_{g}\alpha_{g}^{-1}\beta_{g}^{-1}=I,

where
I
is the identity element in
\Gamma
.

On the other hand,
X
can be realized by the
quasi-Fuchsian uniformization
. It means that
X
is given by a quotient space by a marked, normalized quasi-Fuchsian group
\Gamma\subset\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
.
This group has its region of discontinuity
\Omega\subset\hat{\mathbb{C}}
, which has two invariant components
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
separated by a quasi-circle
\mathcal{C}
. There exists a quasi-conformal homeomorphism
J_{1}
of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
such that

QF1

J_{1}
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{U}
and
J_{1}(\mathbb{U})=\Omega_{1}
,
J_{1}(\mathbb{L})=\Omega_{2}
,
J_{1}(\mathbb{R})=\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathbb{U}
and
\mathbb{L}
are respectively the upper and lower half planes.

QF2

\Gamma_{1}=J_{1}^{-1}\circ\Gamma\circ J_{1}
is a marked, normalized Fuchsian group.

Since
J_{1}
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{U}
, the Riemann surface
X=\Gamma_{1}\backslash\mathbb{U}
is biholomorphic to the one given by the quasi-Fuchsian uniformization
\Gamma\backslash\Omega_{1}
. There is also a quasi-conformal homeomorphism
J_{2}
of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
, holomorphic on
\mathbb{L}
with a Fuchsian group
\Gamma_{2}=J_{2}^{-1}\circ\Gamma\circ J_{2}
so that
\Gamma_{2}\backslash\mathbb{L}
has the quasi-Fuchsian uniformization by
\Gamma\backslash\Omega_{2}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}^{-1,1}(\Gamma)
be the space of Beltrami differentials for a quasi-Fuchsian group
\Gamma
, which is the Banach space of
\mu\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying

\displaystyle\mu(\gamma(z))\frac{\overline{\gamma^{\prime}(z)}}{\gamma^{\prime}(z)}=\mu(z)\qquad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma

and

\displaystyle\mu\,\big|_{\mathcal{C}}=0.

Denote by
\mathcal{B}^{-1,1}(\Gamma)
the open unit ball in
\mathcal{A}^{-1,1}(\Gamma)
with respect to the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
norm. For each Beltrami coefficient

\mu\in\mathcal{B}^{-1,1}(\Gamma)
, there exists a unique quasi-conformal map
f^{\mu}\colon\hat{\mathbb{C}}\to\hat{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying the Beltrami equation

\displaystyle f^{\mu}_{\bar{z}}=\mu f^{\mu}_{z}

(2.1)

and fixing the points
0
,
1
and
\infty
. Set
\Gamma^{\mu}=f^{\mu}\circ\Gamma\circ(f^{\mu})^{-1}
and define the
deformation space of quasi-Fuchsian group
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}^{-1,1}(\Gamma)/{\sim},

where
\mu\sim\nu
if and only if
f^{\mu}=f^{\nu}
on
\mathcal{C}
. The space
\mathfrak{D}(\Gamma)
is a complex manifold of dimension
6g-6
. It is known that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(\Gamma)\simeq\mathfrak{T}(\Gamma_{1})\times{\mathfrak{T}}(\Gamma_{2}),

where
\mathfrak{T}(\Gamma_{i})
is the Teichmüller space of
\Gamma_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
The deformation space
\mathfrak{D}(\Gamma,\Omega_{1})
is defined using the Beltrami coefficients supported on
\Omega_{1}
. By definition,
the space
\mathfrak{D}(\Gamma,\Omega_{1})
parametrizes all deformations of
X=\Gamma\backslash\Omega_{1}
with the fixed Riemann surface
\Gamma\backslash\Omega_{2}
so that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(\Gamma,\Omega_{1})\simeq\mathfrak{T}(\Gamma_{1}).

Hence, it is possible to use the deformation space
\mathfrak{D}(\Gamma,\Omega_{1})
as the model of the Teichmüller space
\mathfrak{T}(\Gamma_{1})
.
An advantage of this model is that one can use the holomorphic variation on
\mathfrak{D}(\Gamma,\Omega_{1})
given by the quasi-Fuchsian deformations.

In the following two subsections, we review the construction of the Liouville action for quasi-Fuchsian groups in
[
9
]
. We refer to
[
9
, Section 2]
for more details.

2.1
Homology construction

Starting with a marked, normalized Fuchsian group
\Gamma
, the double homology complex
\mathsf{K}_{\bullet,\bullet}
is defined as
\mathsf{S}_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\mathsf{B}_{\bullet}
, a tensor product over the integral group ring
\mathbb{Z}\Gamma
, where
\mathsf{S}_{\bullet}=\mathsf{S}_{\bullet}(\mathbb{U})
is the singular chain complex of
\mathbb{U}
with the differential
\partial^{\prime}
, considered as a right
\mathbb{Z}\Gamma
-module, and
\mathsf{B}_{\bullet}=\mathsf{B}_{\bullet}(\mathbb{Z}\Gamma)
is the standard bar resolution complex for
\Gamma
with differential
\partial^{\prime\prime}
. The associated total complex
\text{Tot}\;\mathsf{K}
is equipped with the total differential
\partial=\partial^{\prime}+(-1)^{p}\partial^{\prime\prime}
on
\mathsf{K}_{p,q}
.

There is a standard choice of the fundamental domain

F\subseteq\mathbb{U}
for
\Gamma
as a non-Euclidean polygon with

4g
edges labeled by
a_{k}
,
a_{k}^{\prime}
,
b_{k}^{\prime}
,
b_{k}
;
1\leq k\leq g
satisfying

\alpha_{k}(a_{k}^{\prime})=a_{k}
,
\beta_{k}(b_{k}^{\prime})=b_{k}
,
1\leq k\leq g
. The orientation of the edges
is chosen such that

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}F=\sum_{k=1}^{g}(a_{k}+b_{k}^{\prime}-a_{k}^{\prime}-b_{k}).

(2.2)

Set
\partial^{\prime}a_{k}=a_{k}(1)-a_{k}(0)
,
\partial^{\prime}b_{k}=b_{k}(1)-b_{k}(0)
, so that
a_{k}(0)=b_{k-1}(0)
,
2\leq k\leq g
.

According to the isomorphism

\mathsf{S}_{\bullet}\simeq\mathsf{K}_{\bullet,0}
, the fundamental domain
F
is
identified with
F\otimes[\;]\allowbreak\in\mathsf{K}_{2,0}
. We have
\partial^{\prime\prime}F=0
, and it follows from (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}F=\sum_{k=1}^{g}\big(\beta_{k}^{-1}(b_{k})-b_{k}-\alpha_{k}^{-1}(a_{k})+a_{k}\big)=\partial^{\prime\prime}L,

where
L\in\mathsf{K}_{1,1}
is given by

\displaystyle L=\sum_{k=1}^{g}\big(b_{k}\otimes[\beta_{k}]-a_{k}\otimes[\alpha_{k}]\big).

There exists
V\in\mathsf{K}_{0,2}
such that

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}L=\partial^{\prime\prime}V.

One can verify that it is given by

\displaystyle V={}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{g}\big(a_{k}(0)\otimes[\alpha_{k}|\beta_{k}]-b_{k}(0)\otimes[\beta_{k}|\alpha_{k}]+b_{k}(0)\otimes\big[\gamma_{k}^{-1}|\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}\big]\big)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{g-1}b_{g}(0)\otimes\big[\gamma_{g}^{-1}\cdots\gamma_{k+1}^{-1}|\gamma_{k}^{-1}\big],

where
\gamma_{k}=[\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}]=\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}\alpha_{k}^{-1}\beta_{k}^{-1}
.
Define

\displaystyle\Sigma=F+L-V.

Then

\displaystyle\partial\Sigma=0.

Finally, we also define
W
in the following way. Let
P_{k}
be a
\Gamma
-contracting path (see
[
9
, Definition 2.3]
for the precise definition of
\Gamma
-contracting) connecting 0 to
b_{k}(0)
. Then

\displaystyle W=\sum_{k=1}^{g}\!\big(P_{k-1}\otimes[\alpha_{k}|\beta_{k}]-P_{k}\otimes[\beta_{k}|\alpha_{k}]+P_{k}\otimes[\gamma_{k}^{-1}|\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}]\big)-\sum_{k=1}^{g-1}\!P_{g}\otimes\big[\gamma_{g}^{-1}\cdots\gamma_{k+1}^{-1}|\gamma_{k}^{-1}\big].

If
\Gamma
is a quasi-Fuchsian group, let
\Gamma_{1}
be the Fuchsian group such that
\Gamma_{1}=J_{1}^{-1}\circ\Gamma\circ J_{1}
. The double complex associated with
\Omega_{1}
and the group
\Gamma
is a push-forward by the map
J_{1}
of the double complex associated with
\mathbb{U}
and the group
\Gamma_{1}
. Define

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}=F_{1}+L_{1}-V_{1},

where
F_{1}=J_{1}(F)
,
L_{1}=J_{1}(L)
,
V_{1}=J_{1}(V)
. Similarly we define

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}=F_{2}+L_{2}-V_{2}.

Here
F_{2}=J_{1}(F^{\prime})
,
L_{2}=J_{1}(L^{\prime})
,
V_{2}=J_{1}(V^{\prime})
, where the corresponding chains
F^{\prime}
,
L^{\prime}
,
V^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{L}
are given by the complex conjugation of
F
,
L
,
V
respectively.

2.2
Cohomology construction

The corresponding double complex in cohomology
\mathsf{C}^{\bullet,\bullet}
is defined as
\mathsf{C}^{p,q}=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathsf{B}_{q},\mathsf{A}^{p}\right)
, where
\mathsf{A}^{\bullet}
is the complexified de Rham complex on
\Omega_{1}
. The associated total complex
\text{Tot}\mathsf{C}
is equipped with the total differential
D={\rm d}+(-1)^{p}\delta
on
\mathsf{C}^{p,q}
, where
{\rm d}
is the de Rham differential and
\delta
is the group coboundary. The natural pairing
\langle\,,\,\rangle
between
\mathsf{C}^{p,q}
and
\mathsf{K}_{p,q}
is given by the integration over chains.

Denote by
\mathcal{CM}(\Gamma\backslash\Omega)
the space of conformal metrics on
\Gamma\backslash\Omega
. That is, every
{\rm d}s^{2}\in\mathcal{CM}(\Gamma\backslash\Omega)
is represented as
{\rm d}s^{2}={\rm e}^{\phi(z)}|{\rm d}z|^{2}
, where
\phi
is a smooth function on
\Omega
satisfying

\displaystyle\phi\circ\gamma+\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}=\phi\quad\quad\forall\;\gamma\in\Gamma.

(2.3)

The Liouville action is a function on the space of conformal metrics. Its construction is as follows.
Starting with the 2-form

\displaystyle\omega[\phi]=\big(|\phi_{z}|^{2}+{\rm e}^{\phi}\big){\rm d}z\wedge{\rm d}\bar{z}\in\mathsf{C}^{2,0},

we have

\displaystyle\delta\omega[\phi]={\rm d}\check{\theta}[\phi],

where
\check{\theta}[\phi]\in\mathsf{C}^{1,1}
is given explicitly by

\displaystyle\check{\theta}_{\gamma^{-1}}[\phi]=\bigg(\phi-\frac{1}{2}\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}-2\log 2-\log|c(\gamma)|^{2}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}{\rm d}z-\frac{\overline{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}}{\overline{\gamma^{\prime}}}{\rm d}\bar{z}\bigg).

Here
c(\gamma)
is the element
c
in the linear fractional transformation
\displaystyle\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\!
. Notice that
\check{\theta}_{\gamma^{-1}}[\phi]=0
if
c(\gamma)=0
.

Next, set

\displaystyle\check{u}=\delta\check{\theta}[\phi]\in\mathsf{C}^{1,2}.

From the definition of
\check{\theta}
and
\delta^{2}=0
, it follows that the
1-form
\check{u}
is closed. An explicit calculation gives

\displaystyle\check{u}_{\gamma_{1}^{-1},\gamma_{2}^{-1}}=

\displaystyle-\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\log|\gamma_{1}^{\prime}|^{2}+\log\frac{|c(\gamma_{2})|^{2}}{|c(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1})|^{2}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\gamma_{2}^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma_{2}^{\prime}}\circ\gamma_{1}\,\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}z-\frac{\overline{\gamma_{2}^{\prime\prime}}}{\overline{\gamma_{2}^{\prime}}}\circ\gamma_{1}\,\overline{\gamma_{1}^{\prime}}\,{\rm d}\bar{z}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\log|\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\circ\gamma_{1}|^{2}+\log\frac{|c(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1})|^{2}}{|c(\gamma_{1})|^{2}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\gamma_{1}^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma_{1}^{\prime}}{\rm d}z-\frac{\overline{\gamma_{1}^{\prime\prime}}}{\overline{\gamma_{1}^{\prime}}}{\rm d}\bar{z}\bigg).

For
{\rm e}^{\phi(z)}|{\rm d}z|^{2}\in\mathcal{CM}(\Gamma\backslash\Omega)
, the Liouville action is defined as

\displaystyle S[\phi]=\frac{\rm i}{2}\bigl(\langle\omega[\phi],F_{1}-F_{2}\rangle-\big\langle\check{\theta}[\phi],L_{1}-L_{2}\big\rangle+\big\langle\check{u},W_{1}-W_{2}\big\rangle\bigr).

Here
W_{1}=J_{1}(W)
and
W_{2}=J_{1}(W^{\prime})
for the chain
W^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{L}
given by the complex conjugation of
W
. We also define

\displaystyle\check{S}[\phi]=\frac{\rm i}{2}\bigl(\big\langle\check{\omega}[\phi],F_{1}-F_{2}\big\rangle-\big\langle\check{\theta}[\phi],L_{1}-L_{2}\big\rangle+\langle\check{u},W_{1}-W_{2}\rangle\bigr),

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle\check{\omega}[\phi]=|\phi_{z}|^{2}{\rm d}z\wedge{\rm d}\bar{z}\in\mathsf{C}^{2,0}.

Note that
\delta\omega[\phi]=\delta\check{\omega}[\phi]
since
{\rm e}^{\phi}\,{\rm d}z\wedge{\rm d}\bar{z}
is
\Gamma
-invariant.

3
Liouville action for harmonic diffeomorphisms

For compact Riemann surfaces
X
and
Y
with genus
g\geq 2
, let
h\colon X\to Y
denote a harmonic map for the hyperbolic metric
{\rm e}^{\psi(u)}|{\rm d}u|^{2}
on
Y
, where
u
denotes a conformal coordinate on
Y
.
The harmonic condition for
h
is given by

\displaystyle h_{z\bar{z}}+\big(\psi_{u}\circ h\big)h_{z}h_{\bar{z}}=0

(3.1)

for a conformal coordinate
z
on
X
.
Note that this condition depends on the conformal structure on
X
and the metric structure on
Y
.
The pullback metric
h^{*}\big({\rm e}^{\psi(u)}|{\rm d}u|^{2}\big)
on
X
has the following expression

\displaystyle h^{*}\big({\rm e}^{\psi(u)}|{\rm d}u|^{2}\big)={\rm e}^{\psi\circ h}h_{z}\bar{h}_{z}{\rm d}z^{2}+{\rm e}^{\psi\circ h}h_{z}\bar{h}_{\bar{z}}|{\rm d}z|^{2}+{\rm e}^{\psi\circ h}h_{\bar{z}}\bar{h}_{z}|{\rm d}z|^{2}+{\rm e}^{\psi\circ h}h_{\bar{z}}\bar{h}_{\bar{z}}{\rm d}\bar{z}^{2}.

(3.2)

We denote the
(2,0)
-component of
h^{*}\big({\rm e}^{\psi(u)}|{\rm d}u|^{2}\big)
by

\displaystyle\Phi(h)={\rm e}^{\psi\circ h}h_{z}\bar{h}_{z},

which is called a
Hopf differential
of
h
. By the harmonicity condition (
3.1
), one can easily check that
\Phi(h)
is a holomorphic quadratic differential on
X
. We also put

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{\phi}:={\rm e}^{\psi\circ h}h_{z}\bar{h}_{\bar{z}}.

By
[
2
, Theorem 3.10.1 and Corollary 3.10.1]
, we have

Proposition 3.1
.

For a compact Riemann surface
X
and a compact hyperbolic Riemann surface
Y
with same genus
g\geq 2
and a continuous map
g\colon X\to Y
of degree
1
,
there is a unique harmonic diffeomorphism
h\colon X\to Y
in the homotopy class of
g
. In this case,

{\rm e}^{\phi}={\rm e}^{\psi\circ h}h_{z}\bar{h}_{\bar{z}}
never vanishes on
X
, where
{\rm e}^{\psi(u)}|{\rm d}u|^{2}
is the hyperbolic metric on
Y
.

By Proposition
3.1
, for a harmonic diffeomorphism
h\colon X\to Y
for a hyperbolic metric on
Y
,
{\rm e}^{\phi}={\rm e}^{\psi\circ h}h_{z}\bar{h}_{\bar{z}}
defines a metric on
X
.
Similarly
|\Phi(h)|
defines a singular flat metric on
X
since the holomorphic quadratic differential
\Phi(h)
should have
-2\chi(X)
number of zeros.

Proposition 3.2
.

For a harmonic diffeomorphism
h\colon X\to Y
for a hyperbolic metric on
Y
,

\displaystyle K_{\phi}:=-2\phi_{z\bar{z}}{\rm e}^{-\phi}=\frac{|h_{\bar{z}}|^{2}}{|h_{z}|^{2}}-1.

(3.3)

In particular,
K_{\phi}
never vanishes.

Proof.

By the equality (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\phi_{z}=(\psi_{u}\circ h)\,\displaystyle h_{z}+\frac{h_{zz}}{h_{z}},

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle\phi_{z\bar{z}}=(\psi_{u\bar{u}}\circ h)\,\big(|h_{z}|^{2}-|h_{\bar{z}}|^{2}\big)=\frac{1}{2}{\rm e}^{\psi\circ h}\big(|h_{z}|^{2}-|h_{\bar{z}}|^{2}\big).

(3.5)

Here the second equality in (
3.5
) follows by the Liouville equation for
{\rm e}^{\psi}
,

\displaystyle\psi_{u\bar{u}}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm e}^{\psi}.

(3.6)

Hence we have

\displaystyle K_{\phi}=-2\phi_{z\bar{z}}{\rm e}^{-\phi}=-{\rm e}^{\psi\circ h}\big(|h_{z}|^{2}-|h_{\bar{z}}|^{2}\big)\cdot{\rm e}^{-\psi\circ h}|h_{z}|^{-2}=\frac{|h_{\bar{z}}|^{2}}{|h_{z}|^{2}}-1.

For the harmonic diffeomorphism
h\colon X\to Y
, its Jacobian
J_{h}=|h_{z}|^{2}-|h_{\bar{z}}|^{2}
never vanishes. Hence
K_{\phi}
never vanishes
by the above equality.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

For a harmonic diffeomorphism
h\colon X\to Y
for a hyperbolic metric on
Y
,

{\rm e}^{\phi}
satisfies the following equality:

\displaystyle\phi_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}\phi_{z}^{2}=\bigg(\bigg(\psi_{uu}-\frac{1}{2}\psi^{2}_{u}\bigg)\circ h\bigg)h_{z}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Phi(h)+\mathcal{S}(h)\qquad\text{on}\quad U,

where
z
is a conformal coordinate on an open set
U\subset X
and
\mathcal{S}(h)=\frac{h_{zzz}}{h_{z}}-\frac{3}{2}\big(\frac{h_{zz}}{h_{z}}\big)^{2}
.

Proof.

From (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\phi_{zz}=(\psi_{uu}\circ h)h_{z}^{2}+(\psi_{u\bar{u}}\circ h)h_{z}\bar{h}_{z}+(\psi_{u}\circ h)h_{zz}+\frac{h_{zzz}}{h_{z}}-\bigg(\frac{h_{zz}}{h_{z}}\bigg)^{2}.

(3.7)

Then the claimed equality follows by (
3.4
), (
3.6
), and (
3.7
).
∎

For two marked compact Riemann surfaces of genus
g\geq 2

\displaystyle=\delta\big({\rm d}\xi+{\rm d}(\phi_{z}\lambda\,{\rm d}z-\phi_{\bar{z}}\lambda\,{\rm d}\bar{z})\big)+L_{\mu}{\rm d}\check{\theta}

\displaystyle=\delta\big({-}L_{\mu}\check{\omega}+\big(\big(2\phi_{zz}-\phi^{2}_{z}\big)\mu-2\phi_{z\bar{z}}\lambda\big)\,{\rm d}z\wedge{\rm d}\bar{z}\big)+L_{\mu}{\rm d}\check{\theta}=-\delta L_{\mu}\check{\omega}+L_{\mu}\delta\check{\omega}=0.

This completes the proof.

Lemma 4.5
.

\displaystyle\delta\xi_{\gamma^{-1}}=-2\big(\dot{f}_{z\bar{z}}\circ\gamma\overline{\gamma^{\prime}}-\dot{f}_{z\bar{z}}\big){\rm d}\bar{z}-\phi\,{\rm d}\big(\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma-\dot{f}_{z}\big)+\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}{\rm d}\big(\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma\big).

Proof.

4.8
),

\displaystyle\dot{f}_{z\bar{z}}\circ\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\overline{(\gamma^{\prime})}=\gamma^{\prime\prime}\dot{f}_{\bar{z}}+\gamma^{\prime}\dot{f}_{z\bar{z}}.

(4.10)

Then, from

, using (
4.10
) we have

\displaystyle\delta\xi_{\gamma^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\big(2\phi_{z}\dot{f}_{\bar{z}}\big)\circ\gamma\overline{\gamma^{\prime}}{\rm d}\bar{z}-\big(\phi\,{\rm d}\dot{f}_{z}\big)\circ\gamma-2\phi_{z}\dot{f}_{\bar{z}}\,{\rm d}\bar{z}+\phi\,{\rm d}\dot{f}_{z}

\displaystyle=2\bigg(\phi_{z}-\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}\bigg)\dot{f}_{\bar{z}}\,{\rm d}\bar{z}-\big(\phi-\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}\big){\rm d}\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma-2\phi_{z}\dot{f}_{\bar{z}}\,{\rm d}\bar{z}+\phi\,{\rm d}\dot{f}_{z}

.

Lemma 4.6
.

\displaystyle L_{\mu}\check{\theta}_{\gamma^{-1}}=\bigg(\phi-\frac{1}{2}\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}-2\log 2-\log|c(\gamma)|^{2}\bigg){\rm d}\big(\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma-\dot{f}_{z}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{L_{\mu}\check{\theta}_{\gamma^{-1}}=}{}-2\bigg(\dot{f}_{zz}\circ\gamma\gamma^{\prime}-\dot{f}_{zz}-\lambda\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}\bigg){\rm d}z+\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\big(\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma+\dot{f}_{z}\big)+\frac{\dot{c}(\gamma)}{c(\gamma)}-\lambda\bigg){\rm d}\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}.

Proof.

Recall

\displaystyle\check{\theta}_{\gamma^{-1}}[\phi]=\bigg(\phi-\frac{1}{2}\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}-2\log 2-\log|c(\gamma)|^{2}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}{\rm d}z-\frac{\overline{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}}{\overline{\gamma^{\prime}}}{\rm d}\bar{z}\bigg).

For this, we observe the following equalities:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}\big(\log\big|(\gamma^{\varepsilon\mu})^{\prime}\circ f^{\varepsilon\mu}\big|^{2}\big)=\frac{\dot{\gamma}^{\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}+\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}\dot{f}=\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma-\dot{f}_{z},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}\bigg(\frac{(\gamma^{\varepsilon\mu})^{\prime\prime}}{(\gamma^{\varepsilon\mu})^{\prime}}\circ f^{\varepsilon\mu}\bigg)=\dot{f}_{zz}\circ\gamma\gamma^{\prime}-\dot{f}_{zz}-\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}\dot{f}_{z}.

For

\displaystyle-2c(\gamma)=\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}(z)}{(\gamma^{\prime}(z))^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(4.11)

Hence,

\displaystyle L_{\mu}\bigg(\phi-\frac{1}{2}\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}-2\log 2-\log|c(\gamma)|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\lambda-\dot{f}_{z}-\frac{1}{2}\big(\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma-\dot{f}_{z}\big)-\frac{\dot{c}(\gamma)}{c(\gamma)}=\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\big(\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma+\dot{f}_{z}\big)-\frac{\dot{c}(\gamma)}{c(\gamma)}=\lambda-\frac{\dot{f}_{zz}\circ\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\!-\dot{f}_{zz}}{\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle L_{\mu}\bigg(\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}{\rm d}z-\frac{\overline{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}}{\overline{\gamma^{\prime}}}{\rm d}\bar{z}\bigg)=L_{\mu}{\rm d}\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}={\rm d}\big(\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma-\dot{f}_{z}\big).

Hence,

\displaystyle L_{\mu}\check{\theta}_{\gamma^{-1}}={}

\displaystyle-\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\big(\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma+\dot{f}_{z}\big)+\frac{\dot{c}(\gamma)}{c(\gamma)}-\lambda\bigg)\bigg(2\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}{\rm d}z-{\rm d}\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-2\bigg(\dot{f}_{zz}\circ\gamma\gamma^{\prime}-\dot{f}_{zz}-\lambda\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}\bigg){\rm d}z+\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\big(\dot{f}_{z}\circ\gamma+\dot{f}_{z}\big)+\frac{\dot{c}(\gamma)}{c(\gamma)}-\lambda\bigg){\rm d}\log|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}.

This completes the proof.

Proposition 4.7

\displaystyle{\chi}=\delta\xi+\delta(\phi_{z}\lambda\,{\rm d}z-\phi_{\bar{z}}\lambda\,{\rm d}\bar{z})+L_{\mu}\check{\theta}.

4.5
and
4.6

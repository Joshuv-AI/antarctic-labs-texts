Long GMC Lifetimes: Using the Method and Data of Meidt et al. 2015 with A Correction
Jin Koda

Abstract
Meidt et al. 2015 derived short lifetimes of 20-30 Myr for giant molecular clouds (GMCs) in M51. Their novel approach utilizes a decline of the GMC population during their inter-arm passage from one spiral arm to the next. Using the inter-arm travel time t travel t_{\rm travel} as a fiducial clock, they converted the decline rate to GMC lifetimes. They implicitly adopted zero as the pattern speed of the spiral arms, resulting in a very short t travel t_{\rm travel} . However, it is well established that the spiral arm pattern in M51 is rotating ( Meidt et al. 2008 ; Meidt et al. 2013 ) , and that t travel t_{\rm travel} should be measured with respect to the rotating spiral pattern. Here we use the same method and data of Meidt et al. 2015 and re-evaluate GMC lifetimes by accounting for the pattern speed given by Meidt et al. 2013 . This correction gives a longer t travel t_{\rm travel} , and consequently longer GMC lifetimes of 60-500 Myr.



I
Introduction

Meidt et al. 2015
derived GMC lifetimes
t_{\rm GMC}
in M51 as a fraction of their inter-arm travel time
t_{\rm travel}
.
They estimated
t_{\rm travel}
as half the disk rotation timescale for the two-armed spiral galaxy,

t_{\rm travel}=\frac{\pi R}{V_{\rm rot}},

(1)

where
R
is the galactocentric radius and
V_{\rm rot}
is the rotation velocity of the disk.
However, it has been established that the spiral pattern in M51 is rotating

(
Meidt et al. 2008
;
Meidt et al. 2013
, e.g., )
.

t_{\rm travel}
should be calculated with respect to the spiral arms
in the rotating frame of the spiral pattern as,

t_{\rm travel}=\frac{\pi R}{V_{\rm rot}-\,\Omega_{\rm p}R},

(2)

where
\,\Omega_{\rm p}
is the pattern speed.
We re-evaluate GMC lifetimes with the method and data of
Meidt et al. 2015
, but with
this correction on
t_{\rm travel}
.

II
Parameters

We adopt all the notations and parameters of
Meidt et al. 2015

except for
\,\Omega_{\rm p}
which is from
Meidt et al. 2013
.
They adopted a distance to M51 of
D=7.6\,{\rm Mpc}
.
All the parameters are listed in Table
1
.

Meidt et al. 2015
performed their analysis in the radius range of
R\sim 40
-
80\arcsec
(1.5-3.0
\,{\rm kpc}
).

Meidt et al. 2008
suggested a radial decline of the pattern speed
\,\Omega_{\rm p}
in M51;
as updates in
Meidt et al. 2013
,

\,\Omega_{\rm p}
is high at
90\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}\,\,{\rm kpc}^{-1}
out to its corotation radius of
R_{\rm CR}=55\arcsec
(
2.0\,{\rm kpc}
)
and transitions to
\,\Omega_{\rm p}=55\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}\,\,{\rm kpc}^{-1}
outside.

Meidt et al. 2013
explained this transition as due to an overlap of the corotation of the inner spiral
pattern and the inner 4:1 resonance of the outer pattern.

A constant rotation velocity
V_{\rm rot}
is assumed based on the rotation curve in this radius range

(
Meidt et al. 2013
, their Figure 10)
.
From
\,\Omega_{\rm p}=90\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}\,\,{\rm kpc}^{-1}
and its corotation radius
R_{\rm CR}=55\arcsec
,
we derive
V_{\rm rot}=182\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}
.
The second corotation radius corresponding to
\,\Omega_{\rm p}=55\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}\,\,{\rm kpc}^{-1}

is
R_{\rm CR}=3.3\,{\rm kpc}
, which is outside the radius range of their analysis.

The inter-arm travel velocity with respect to the spiral pattern is
V_{\rm rot}-\,\Omega_{\rm p}R
.
The inter-arm travel time
t_{\rm travel}
is calculated with eq. (
2
).

Meidt et al. 2015
measured the fraction of lost GMC population
F_{\rm lost}
,
i.e., the decline rate of GMC population during inter-arm passage.
We read the values of
F_{\rm lost}
at different radii from Figure 7 (right) of
Meidt et al. 2015
.

Meidt et al. 2015
calculated the GMC population lifetime
\tau
as

\tau=\frac{t_{\rm travel}}{2}\frac{1}{F_{\rm lost}}.

(3)

This includes the decline and growth of the population,

\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm true}}-\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm grow}},

(4)

where
\tau_{\rm true}
is the time it would take to reduce the initial population,
and
\tau_{\rm grow}
represents the time to increase the population.

Meidt et al. 2015
assumed
\tau_{\rm grow}\gg\tau_{\rm true}
and adopted
\tau

as the GMC lifetimes.
We follow this approach as it is unlikely that a GMC population grows significantly
in inter-arm regions.

III
Results

Table
1
list the results.
The travel time of
Meidt et al. 2015
is estimated to be
t_{\rm travel}=34(R/{\rm 2kpc}){\rm Myr}

using eq. (
1
) and
V_{\rm rot}=182\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}

– this calculation is roughly consistent with the
t_{\rm travel}
line in Figure 7 of
Meidt et al. 2015
.
The corrected
t_{\rm travel}
in Table
1
(with eq.
2
)
are much longer, by a factor of 3-16, than their
t_{\rm travel}
.
The long travel time suggests that
if the GMC lifetimes were as short as the originally reported (20-30 Myr),
they would not be able to move much across the inter-arm regions.
The correction with
\,\Omega_{\rm p}
gives long GMC lifetimes of
\tau=60
-
500\,{\rm Mpc}
, much longer than those originally reported.

We note that the same method and data of
Meidt et al. 2015
are used here
with one correction on the pattern speed.
The same limitations also apply here.
For example, the lifetimes calculated here are for the GMCs detected
above their sensitivity limit (
>10^{5}{\rm M_{\odot}}
).
This massive GMC population accounts for only about half of the CO emission in M51,
and the other half arises from GMCs smaller than the detection limit.
This method is vulnerable to variations of GMC properties during inter-arm passages.
For example, if they become fainter (colder) during the passages,
a large population may sink below the detection limit,
resulting in an overestimation of
F_{\rm lost}
and underestimation of
\tau
.

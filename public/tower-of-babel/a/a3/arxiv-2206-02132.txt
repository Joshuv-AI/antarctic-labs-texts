Abstract
The rational Dunkl operators are commuting differential-reflection operators on the Euclidean space ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} associated with a root system. The aim of the paper is to study local boundary behaviour of generalized harmonic functions associated with the Dunkl operators. We introduce a Lusin-type area integral operator S S by means of Dunkl’s generalized translation and the Dunkl operators. The main results are on characterizations of local existence of non-tangential boundary limits of a generalized harmonic function u u in the upper half-space ℝ + d + 1 {\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+} associated with the Dunkl operators, and for a subset E E of ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} invariant under the reflection group generated by the root system, the equivalence of the following three assertions are proved: (i) u u has a finite non-tangential limit at ( x , 0 ) (x,0) for a.e. x ∈ E x\in E ; (ii) u u is non-tangentially bounded for a.e. x ∈ E x\in E ; (iii) ( S ​ u ) ​ ( x ) (Su)(x) is finite for a.e. x ∈ E x\in E . 2020 MS Classification: 42B99(Primary), 31B25, 51F15(Secondary) Key Words and Phrases: local non-tangential limit; area integral; root system; Dunkl operator; generalized harmonic function

The rational Dunkl operators are differential-reflection operators on the Euclidean space

1.2.
Let
R
be a root system in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
normalized so that
\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=2

\alpha\in R

\sigma_{\alpha}

\kappa

(invariant under

(

are defined by (cf.
[
12
]

\displaystyle D_{j}=\partial_{j}+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\kappa(\alpha)\alpha_{j}\frac{1-\sigma_{\alpha}}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle},

(1)

where
\sigma_{\alpha}f=f\circ\sigma_{\alpha}
,
\alpha_{j}
is the
j

\alpha
, and the associated measure is given by

\displaystyle d\omega_{\kappa}=W_{\kappa}(x)dx,\qquad{\rm with}\quad W_{\kappa}(x)=\prod_{\alpha\in R_{+}}|\langle\alpha,x\rangle|^{2\kappa(\alpha)}.

The weight

is a positive homogeneous function of degree

, where
|\kappa|=\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}k(\alpha)
.
It is proved in
[

A

\displaystyle\Delta_{\kappa}=\partial_{y}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{j}^{2}.

From

the

-Laplacian

can be explicitly expressed by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\kappa}u=\Delta u+2\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\kappa(\alpha)\delta_{\alpha}u,

(2)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}u=\frac{\langle\nabla^{(x)}u,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}-\frac{u-\sigma_{\alpha}u}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}},

(3)

\Delta=\partial_{y}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}^{2}
is the usual Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
,
\nabla^{(x)}
the usual gradient operator on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
(\sigma u)(x,y)=u(\sigma(x),y)

By

1.3. Main results.
We are concerned with characterizations on existence of non-tangential boundary values, in a local sense, of
\kappa
-harmonic functions in the upper half-space

Our first result is to extend those of Privalov

Definition 1.1

For a given function

2.1
The Dunkl kernel and the Dunkl transform

As in the last section, for a root system
R
let
G
be the associated reflection group and
\kappa
a given nonnegative multiplicity function. As shown in
[
13
]
, there exists a unique degree-of-homogeneity-preserving linear
isomorphism
V_{\kappa}
on polynomials, which intertwines the associated commutative algebra of Dunkl operators and the algebra of usual partial differential operators, namely,

D_{j}V_{\kappa}=V_{\kappa}\partial_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq d
.
V_{\kappa}
commutes with the group action of
G
. (For a thorough analysis on
V_{\kappa}
with general
\kappa
, see
[
15
]
.)
It was proved in
[
47
]
that
V_{\kappa}
is positive on the space of polynomials, and moreover, for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there exists a unique (Borel) probability measure
\mu^{\kappa}_{x}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

V_{\kappa}f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(\xi)\,d\mu^{\kappa}_{x}(\xi),

where
\mu^{\kappa}_{x}
is of compact support with

\displaystyle\hbox{supp}\,\mu^{\kappa}_{x}\subseteq\hbox{co}\,\{\sigma(x):\,\,\sigma\in G\},

(5)

the convex hull of the orbit of
x
under
G
.
The intertwining operator
V_{\kappa}
has an extension to
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and establishes a homeomorphism of this space (see
[
56
,
57
]
).

Associated with
G
and
\kappa
, the Dunkl kernel is defined by

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}(x,z)=V_{\kappa}\left(e^{\langle\cdot,z\rangle}\right)(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{\langle\xi,z\rangle}\,d\mu^{\kappa}_{x}(\xi),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,\,z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}.

According to
[
39
]
, for fixed
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
,
x\mapsto E_{\kappa}(x,z)
may
be characterized as the unique analytic solution of the system

\displaystyle D^{x}_{j}E_{\kappa}(x,z)=z_{j}E_{\kappa}(x,z),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,\,\,j=1,\dots,d,

(6)

with the initial value
E_{\kappa}(0,z)=1
; and
E_{\kappa}
has a unique holomorphic extension to
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
and is symmetric in its arguments (see
[
9
,
39
]
). Furthermore (cf.
[
9
,
13
,
47
]
), for
z,w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\sigma\in G
, one has

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}(\lambda z,w)=E_{\kappa}(z,\lambda w),\qquad E_{\kappa}(\sigma(z),\sigma(w))=E_{\kappa}(z,w),

(7)

and for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
, and all multi-indices
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{d})
,

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{z}^{\nu}E_{\kappa}(x,z)\right|\leq|x|^{|\nu|}\max_{\sigma\in G}e^{{\rm Re}\,\langle\sigma(x),z\rangle}.

(8)

For
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, its Dunkl transform is defined by

\displaystyle\left({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\right)(\xi)=c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)E_{\kappa}(-i\xi,x)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x),\qquad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

The Dunkl transform
{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}
commutes with the
G
-action, and shares many of the important properties with the
usual Fourier transform (see
[
14
,
9
,
47
,
50

), part of which are listed as follows.

Proposition 2.1

(i)
If
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then
{{\mathscr{F}}}_{\kappa}f\in C_{0}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and
\|{{\mathscr{F}}}_{\kappa}f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}}
.

(ii)

(Inversion)
If
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

such that
{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then

f(x)=[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)](-x)
.

(iii)
For
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
(the Schwartz space), we have
[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}(D_{j}f)](\xi)=i\xi_{j}({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)(\xi)
,
[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}(x_{j}f)](\xi)=i[D_{j}({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)](\xi)
for

\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
1\leq j\leq d
; and
{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}
is a homeomorphism of
{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

(iv)

(product formula)
For
f_{1},f_{2}\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we have
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f_{1}\cdot{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f_{2}\,d\omega_{\kappa}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f_{2}\cdot{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f_{1}\,d\omega_{\kappa}
.

(v)

(Plancherel)
There exists a unique extension of

{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}
to
L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with

\|{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}}=\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}}
.

(vi)
If
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is radial, then its Dunkl transform
{{\mathscr{F}}}_{\kappa}f
is also radial.

2.2
The generalized translation

For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the generalized translation
\tau_{x}
is defined in
L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by (see
[
55
]
)

\displaystyle\left[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}(\tau_{x}f)\right](\xi)=E_{\kappa}(i\xi,x)({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)(\xi).

(9)

By Proposition
2.1
(v),
\tau_{x}
is well defined and satisfies
\|\tau_{x}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}}
in view of (
8
).

If
f
is in an appropriate subclass, for example,
A_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=\left\{f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\,{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\right\}
(cf.
[
55
]
) or
{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
(\tau_{x}f)(t)
may be expressed pointwise by (see
[
46
,
55
]
)

\displaystyle(\tau_{x}f)(t)=c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}E_{\kappa}(i\xi,x)E_{k}(i\xi,t)({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)(\xi)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(\xi).

(10)

Proposition 2.2

If
f,g\in L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(\tau_{x}f)(t)g(t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t)(\tau_{-x}g)(t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t),

(11)

and both sides are continuous in
x
.

The equality (
11
) was proved in
[
55
]
for
f\in A_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
g\in L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. The general case, for
f,g\in L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, follows from a density argument.
On the continuity of
F(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\tau_{x}f\cdot g\,d\omega_{\kappa}
, for
x,x^{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
one has

\displaystyle\left|F(x)-F(x^{0})\right|\leq\|\tau_{x}f-\tau_{x^{0}}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}}\|g\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}}=\left\|\left(E_{\kappa}(ix,\cdot)-E_{\kappa}(ix^{0},\cdot)\right){\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\right\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}}\|g\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}}

by Proposition
2.1
(v) and (
9
); and then, in view of (
8
), Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem yields the desired continuity.

In
[
57
]
an abstract form of
\tau_{x}
is given by
(\tau_{x}f)(t)=V_{\kappa}^{t}V_{\kappa}^{x}\left[(V_{\kappa}^{-1}f)(x+t)\right]
for
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
x,t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
57
]
)

(i)
For fixed
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\tau_{x}
is a continuous linear mapping from

into itself, and satisfies
D_{j}(\tau_{x}f)=\tau_{x}(D_{j}f)
(

) on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for

;

(ii)
for fixed

, the mapping
f\mapsto(\tau_{x}f)(t)
defines a compactly supported distribution, whose support is contained in the ball
\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,|\xi|\leq|x|+|t|\}
;

(iii)
for fixed
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, if
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then
\tau_{x}f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
too, and both (

) and (

(iv)
If
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then the function
(x,t)\mapsto(\tau_{x}f)(t)
is in

, and for

(\tau_{t}f)(x)=(\tau_{x}f)(t)
.

Proposition 2.4

If
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then
(i)

\left[\tau_{x}(\sigma f)\right](t)=(\tau_{\sigma(x)}f)(\sigma(t))
for
x,t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\sigma\in G
;
(ii)

\left[\tau_{x}(f(a\cdot))\right](t)=(\tau_{ax}f)(at)
for
x,t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
;
(iii)

\left[\tau_{t}(\tau_{x}f)\right](z)=\left[\tau_{x}(\tau_{t}f)\right](z)
for
x,t,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

In consideration of radial functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, one may learn more about the translation operator
\tau_{x}
. In fact, for
x,t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, by
[
48
, Theorem 5.1]
there exists a unique compactly supported, radial (Borel) probability measure
\rho^{\kappa}_{x,t}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that for all radial
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle(\tau_{x}f)(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f\,d\rho^{\kappa}_{x,t};

(12)

and the support of
\rho^{\kappa}_{x,t}
is contained in

\displaystyle\left\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\quad\min_{\sigma\in G}|x+\sigma(t)|\leq|\xi|\leq\max_{\sigma\in G}|x+\sigma(t)|\right\}.

(13)

In particular, if
0\in{\rm supp}\,\rho^{\kappa}_{x,t}
, then the
G
-orbits of
x
and
-t
coincide. Since
{\rm supp}\,\rho^{\kappa}_{x,t}
is compact, (
12
) leads to a natural extension of the translation operator
\tau_{x}
to all radial
f\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Furthermore, if
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is radial, say
f(x)=f_{0}(|x|)
, from the proof of
[
48
, Theorem 5.1]
it follows that, for
x,t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle(\tau_{x}f)(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f_{0}(\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|t|^{2}+2\langle t,\xi\rangle})\,d\mu^{\kappa}_{x}(\xi).

(14)

Again, since
\mu^{\kappa}_{x}
is of compact support, (
14
) allows an extension of
\tau_{x}
to all radial
f\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by a density argument.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
55
, Theorems 3.7 and 3.8]
)

Assume
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
1\leq p\leq 2
.

(i)
The generalized translation operator
\tau_{x}
, initially defined on

, can be extended to all radial functions
f
in
L_{\kappa}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
1\leq p\leq 2
, and for these

\|\tau_{x}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}}
;

(ii)
if

is radial and nonnegative, then
\tau_{x}f
is nonnegative;

(iii)
if
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is radial, then

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(\tau_{x}f)(t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t).

2.3
The generalized spherical mean operator

We note that it is still open whether the translation operator
\tau_{x}
has a bounded extension to non-radial functions in
L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. However we may consider the generalized spherical mean operator
f\mapsto M_{f}
associated to
G
and
\kappa
, which is defined in
[
35
]
, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
r\in[0,\infty)
, by

\displaystyle M_{f}(x,r)=d_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}(\tau_{x}f)(rt^{\prime})W_{\kappa}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(15)

where
dt^{\prime}
denotes the area element on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
, and
d_{\kappa}^{-1}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}W_{\kappa}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}
. By Proposition
2.3
(i),
M_{f}
is well defined for
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
or
A_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Moreover, by
[
48
, Theorems 3.1, 4.1, and Corollary 5.2]
the mapping
f\mapsto M_{f}
is positivity-preserving on
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
r\in[0,\infty)
, there exists a unique compactly supported probability (Borel) measure
\sigma^{\kappa}_{x,r}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that for all
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle M_{f}(x,r)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f\,d\sigma^{\kappa}_{x,r};

(16)

the support of
\sigma^{\kappa}_{x,r}
is contained in

\displaystyle\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,\,|\xi|\geq||x|-r|\}\cap\left[\cup_{\sigma\in G}\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,\,|\xi-\sigma(x)|\leq r\}\right];

(17)

the mapping
(x,r)\mapsto\sigma_{x,r}^{\kappa}
is
continuous with respect to the weak topology on
M^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(the
space of probability measures);
and moreover,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\sigma(x),r}^{\kappa}(A)=\sigma_{x,r}^{\kappa}(\sigma^{-1}(A)),\qquad\sigma_{ax,ar}^{\kappa}(A)=\sigma_{x,r}^{\kappa}(a^{-1}A)

for all
\sigma\in G
,
a>0
, and all Borel sets
A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Since
{\rm supp}\,\rho^{\kappa}_{x,t}
is compact, (
16
) gives a natural extension of the generalized spherical mean operator
f\mapsto M_{f}
to all
f\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

2.4
The
\kappa
-Poisson integral

Associated to the reflection group
G
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the multiplicity function
\kappa
, we define
P(x)=c_{d,\kappa}(1+|x|^{2})^{-|\kappa|-(d+1)/2}
, where
c_{d,\kappa}=2^{|\kappa|+d/2}\pi^{-1/2}\Gamma(|\kappa|+(d+1)/2)
. It follows that
c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}P\,d\omega_{\kappa}=1
and
\left({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}P\right)(\xi)=e^{-|\xi|}
(cf.
[
50
,
55
]
). The associated Poisson kernel
P_{y}
for
y>0
is given by
P_{y}(x)=y^{-2|\kappa|-d}P(x/y)
, i.e.

\displaystyle P_{y}(x)=\frac{c_{d,\kappa}\,y}{(y^{2}+|x|^{2})^{|\kappa|+(d+1)/2}},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

For
f\in L_{\kappa}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the Poisson integral of
f
is defined as

\displaystyle(Pf)(x,y)=c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t)(\tau_{x}P_{y})(-t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t),\qquad(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}.

(18)

For convenience, we call
P_{y}(x)
the
\kappa
-Poisson kernel, and
(Pf)(x,y)
the
\kappa
-Poisson integral of
f
. Note that the function
(x,y)\mapsto P_{y}(x)
is
\kappa
-harmonic in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d+1}
, and so is the function
(x,y)\mapsto(\tau_{x}P_{y})(-t)
by Propositions
2.3
(i) and (iv). Appealing to
[
1
, Proposition 5.1]
, the following proposition is immediate.

Proposition 2.6

For
f\in L_{\kappa}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, its
\kappa
-Poisson integral
(Pf)(x,y)
is
\kappa
-harmonic in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d+1}
.

The next proposition is a consequence of Proposition
2.4
and the
G
-invariance of
P_{y}
.

Proposition 2.7

If
f\in L_{\kappa}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(
1\leq p\leq\infty
) is
G
-invariant, then its
\kappa
-Poisson integral
(Pf)(x,y)
is also
G
-invariant in
x
.

The conclusions in the following two propositions can be found in
[
1
,
55
]
, and for
d=1
,
G={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, see
[
31
, Proposition 3.2 and Corollary 3.9]
.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
55
, Theorems 4.1 and 5.4]
)
For
f\in L_{\kappa}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(
1\leq p\leq\infty
) and
y>0
,
\|(Pf)(\cdot,y)\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
; and for
f\in X=L_{\kappa}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\lim_{y\rightarrow 0+}\|(Pf)(\cdot,y)-f\|_{X}=0
.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
1
, Corollary 5.4]
)

Assume that
a>0
. If
f\in L^{p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then for almost every

x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, its
\kappa
-Poisson integral
(Pf)(t,y)
converges
to
f(x)
as
(t,y)\in\Gamma_{a}(x)
approaching to
(x,0)
.

The next proposition is the (global) Fatou-type theorem for
\kappa
-harmonic functions.

Proposition 2.10

(
[
1
, Corollary 7.4]
)

If
u
is
\kappa
-harmonic and bounded on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}
, then
u
has a non-tangential limit at almost every point of the boundary.

3
Some elementary propositions

In this section we present several propositions which will be useful subsequently.
Some conclusions may be found elsewhere, but here we treat them as elementarily as possible. In particular, it will be proved that, on a general
G
-invariant domain
\Omega
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, a twice-differentiable function
f
, harmonic associated to the Dunkl operators, is infinitely differentiable and satisfies the mean value formula
M_{f}(x,r)=f(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
provided
\overline{{\mathbb{B}}(x,r)}\subset\Omega
with
r>0
.

Proposition 3.1

If
(i)

f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and
g\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is radial and of compact support, or
(ii)

f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with compact support, and
g\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is radial, then (
11
) holds.

Proof.

Assume that
{\rm supp}\,g\subset\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,|\xi|\leq r_{0}\}
. For fixed
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we take a compactly supported
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\phi(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq|x|+r_{0}
. It then follows from Proposition
2.3
(ii) that
[\tau_{x}(\phi f)](t)=(\tau_{x}f)(t)
for
|t|\leq r_{0}
, and from (
12
) and (
13
),
(\tau_{-x}g)(t)=0
for
|t|>|x|+r_{0}
. Thus the assertion of part (i) is validated by applying Proposition
2.2
to
\phi f
and
g
. A similar treatment verifies the assertion of part (ii).
\square

Proposition 3.2

If
f\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is radial, then
\left[\tau_{x}(f(a\cdot))\right](t)=(\tau_{ax}f)(at)
for
x,t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
.

Indeed, by Proposition
2.4
the assertion is true for
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. For general radial
f\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we take a radial and compactly supported
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\phi(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq|a|(|x|+|t|)
, and then, choose a sequence of radial functions
\{\phi_{j}\}\subset{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
converging uniformly to
\phi f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Letting
j\rightarrow\infty
on the both sides of
\left[\tau_{x}(\phi_{j}(a\cdot))\right](t)=(\tau_{ax}\phi_{j})(at)
yields

\left[\tau_{x}((\phi f)(a\cdot))\right](t)=(\tau_{ax}(\phi f))(at)
, which is identical with
\left[\tau_{x}(f(a\cdot))\right](t)=(\tau_{ax}f)(at)
on account of (
13
).

In what follows, we set
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\kappa}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{j}^{2}
.

Proposition 3.3

(i)
If
f\in A_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then

\displaystyle M_{f}(x,r)=c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}j_{|\kappa|+(d-2)/2}(r|\xi|)E_{\kappa}(i\xi,x)({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)(\xi)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(\xi).

(19)

(ii)
If
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
r>0
,

\displaystyle M_{f}(x,r)-f(x)=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{s^{2|\kappa|+d-1}}\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{s}^{2|\kappa|+d-1}M_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{\kappa}f}(x,\tilde{s})\,d\tilde{s}.

(20)

Proof.

Part (i) follows from (
10
),
[
48
, Corollary 2.5]
and Fubini’s theorem.

As for part (ii), if
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, from (
19
) it is obvious that
\lim_{r\rightarrow 0+}M_{f}(x,r)=f(x)
, and by (
6
) and Proposition
2.1
(iii),
\widetilde{\Delta}^{x}_{\kappa}\left[M_{f}(x,r)\right]=M_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{\kappa}f}(x,r)
. From
[
48
, (4.5)]
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[r^{2|\kappa|+d-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}M_{f}(x,r)\right]=r^{2|\kappa|+d-1}\widetilde{\Delta}^{x}_{\kappa}\left[M_{f}(x,r)\right],

so that (
20
) follows immediately. For general
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
r>0
, we choose a compactly supported
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\phi(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq|x|+r
. It then follows that
\phi f\in{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and (
20
) holds with
\phi f
instead of
f
. But by (
16
) and (
17
)
M_{\phi f}(x,r)=M_{f}(x,r)
and
M_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{\kappa}(\phi f)}(x,s)=M_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{\kappa}f}(x,s)
for
s\in[0,r)
, so that (
20
) holds for
f
itself.
\square

Proposition 3.4

(i)
If
f\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0+}M_{f}(x,r)=f(x)

(21)

for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and the limit holds uniformly for

in a compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
;

(ii)
if
f\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and

,

\displaystyle d_{\kappa}^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}M_{f}(x,r)g_{0}(r)\,r^{2|\kappa|+d-1}dr=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t)(\tau_{-x}g)(t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t),

(22)

where

Assume that

and

, where

. If
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, (

) follows from (

), where the limit holds uniformly for all

; and (
22
) follows from Proposition
3.1
by using the spherical coordinates to the left hand side of (

). For general

, we take a compactly supported
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with

for

, and then, choose a sequence of functions
\{\phi_{j}\}\subset{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
converging uniformly to

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Note that, by (

) and (

)
M_{\phi f}(x,r)=M_{f}(x,r)
for

and

, and

converges to

uniformly for

as

tends to infinity; and further, by (

The proof of Theorem

In order to prove Theorem

\displaystyle(S^{\psi}_{a,h}u)(x)=\left(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times(0,h]}\left[\tau_{x}(\Delta_{\kappa}u^{2})\right](t,y)\,\psi\left(\frac{t}{ay}\right)y^{1-d-2|\kappa|}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)dy\right)^{1/2},

.

Lemma 5.2

If

-harmonic in

\displaystyle\left(\Delta_{\kappa}u^{2}\right)(x,y)=2\left|\nabla u(x,y)\right|^{2}+2\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\kappa(\alpha)\left(\frac{u(x,y)-u(\sigma_{\alpha}(x),y)}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\kappa(\alpha)\delta_{\alpha}u^{2}=2\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\kappa(\alpha)\left(2u\frac{\langle\nabla^{(x)}u,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}-\frac{u^{2}-\sigma_{\alpha}u^{2}}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}}\right).

{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}
is a portion of the smooth hypersurface
y=a_{1}^{-1}\delta_{\epsilon}(x)
with
|\partial_{j}\delta_{\epsilon}(x)|\leq 1
,
j=1,\dots,d
, and
{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{2}
is a portion of the hyperplane
y=h_{1}-\epsilon
.

We extract the term
\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}}u^{2}\frac{\partial y}{\partial\mathbf{n}}W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)
from (
71
), so that

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}}u^{2}\frac{\partial y}{\partial\mathbf{n}}W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)\leq\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{2}}u^{2}\frac{\partial y}{\partial\mathbf{n}}W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)-\int_{\partial\Omega_{\epsilon}}y\frac{\partial u^{2}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)+1.

Since
E
is bounded and
2h_{1}/3\leq y\leq h_{1}
for
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{2}
, the first term on the right hand side above is bounded by a constant independent of
\epsilon
, and so is the contribution coming from
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{2}
in the second term. Thus we get

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}}u^{2}\frac{\partial y}{\partial\mathbf{n}}W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)\leq-\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}}y\frac{\partial u^{2}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)+c.

(72)

Since the hypersurface
{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}
is determined by the equation
\delta_{\epsilon}(x)-a_{1}y=0
and so the direction
{\mathbf{n}}
is given by
(\nabla^{(x)}\delta_{\epsilon},-a_{1})/\sqrt{|\nabla^{(x)}\delta_{\epsilon}|^{2}+a_{1}^{2}}
, we have
\partial y/\partial{\mathbf{n}}=-a_{1}/\sqrt{|\nabla^{(x)}\delta_{\epsilon}|^{2}+a_{1}^{2}}\leq-a_{1}/\sqrt{d+a_{1}^{2}}
; and since
\Omega_{\epsilon}\subset\Omega^{E}(a_{1},h_{1})
is at a positive distance from the
x
-space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, (
70
) implies that
y\left|\partial u^{2}/\partial\mathbf{n}\right|=2y|u|\left|\partial u/\partial\mathbf{n}\right|\leq 2y|u||\nabla u|\leq 2|u|
on
{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}
. Applying these estimates to (
72
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{a_{1}}{\sqrt{d+a_{1}^{2}}}\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}}u^{2}\,W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}}|u|\,W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)+c

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}}u^{2}\,W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)\right)^{1/2}+c,

where the last inequality is due to the fact that
\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}}W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)
is bounded by a constant independent of
\epsilon
as in (
57
). This certainly implies that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}}u^{2}(x,y)W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)\leq c,

(73)

where
c>0
is independent of
\epsilon
.

If we define
f_{\epsilon}(x)=|u(x,a_{1}^{-1}\delta_{\epsilon}(x))|
for
x
satisfying
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}
with some
y>0
, and
f_{\epsilon}(x)=0
otherwise, then from (
73
), one has, for
\epsilon\in(0,h_{1}/3)
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)\leq\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}}u^{2}(x,y)W_{\kappa}(x)d\sigma(x,y)\leq c.

(74)

Now for
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}\subset\Omega^{E}(a_{1},h_{1})
with
0<y<h_{1}/2
, one can choose a constant
c\in(0,1)
independent of
(x,y)
, so that the ball
{\mathbb{B}}((x,y),cy)\subset\Omega^{E}(a,h)
. It follows that, for
(t,s)\in{\mathbb{B}}((x,y),cy)
,

|u(x,y)-u(t,s)|\leq cy\sup|\nabla u|
, where the supremum is taken over the line segment joining
(x,y)
and
(t,s)
, and by (
70
),

\displaystyle|u(x,y)|\leq|u(t,s)|+c,\qquad(t,s)\in{\mathbb{B}}((x,y),cy).

(75)

Since
|\partial_{j}\delta_{\epsilon}(x)|\leq 1
(
j=1,\dots,d
), for
(x,y),(t,s)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}
one has

|(t,s)-(x,y)|=\sqrt{|t-x|^{2}+a_{1}^{-2}|\delta_{\epsilon}(t)-\delta_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}}\leq\tilde{c}|t-x|

with
\tilde{c}=\sqrt{1+a_{1}^{-2}d}
, so that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}\cap{\mathbb{B}}((x,y),cy)}W_{\kappa}(t)d\sigma(t,s)\geq\int_{{\mathbb{B}}(x,c_{1}y)}W_{\kappa}(t)dt=|{\mathbb{B}}(x,c_{1}y)|_{\kappa},

(76)

where
c_{1}=c/\tilde{c}<c
. In addition, it follows from (
30
) that

\displaystyle(\tau_{x}P_{y})(-t)\geq c^{\prime}/\left|{\mathbb{B}}(x,(c+1)y)\right|_{\kappa},\qquad(t,s)\in{\mathbb{B}}((x,y),cy).

(77)

We then take the surface integration to (
75
) over
{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}\cap{\mathbb{B}}((x,y),cy)
and apply (
76
) and (
77
), to get

\displaystyle|u(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}\cap{\mathbb{B}}((x,y),cy)}|u(t,s)|(\tau_{x}P_{y})(-t)W_{\kappa}(t)d\sigma(t,s)+c

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}v_{\epsilon}(x,y)+c

(78)

for
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{1}
with
0<y<h_{1}/2
,
where
v_{\epsilon}(x,y)
is the
\kappa
-Poisson integral of
f_{\epsilon}(x)
. Further, since
u
has a bound on
\{(x,y)\in\partial\Omega_{\epsilon}:\,h_{1}/2\leq y\leq h_{1}-\epsilon\}
independent of
\epsilon
, we could choose the constant
c
suitably large, so that (
6
) is true for all
(x,y)\in\partial\Omega_{\epsilon}
.

Considering the function
U(x,y)=|u(x,y)|-c_{3}v_{\epsilon}(x,y)-c
, by Lemma
4.5
we have

\Delta_{\kappa}U(x,y)=\Delta_{\kappa}|u(x,y)|\geq 0

in the region where
U(x,y)>0
which implies
|u(x,y)|>0
. Since
U|_{\partial\Omega_{\epsilon}}\leq 0
from (
6
), by the maximum principle (Lemma
4.4
) we assert that (
6
) holds on the whole
\Omega_{\epsilon}
.

Finally, since
\{f_{\epsilon}:\,\epsilon\in(0,h_{1}/3)\}
is a bounded set in
L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by (
74
), there exists a sequence
\{f_{\epsilon_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, so that
f_{\varepsilon_{k}}
converges weakly to a
function
f\in L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
k\rightarrow\infty
; and in particular, if
v(x,y)
denotes the
\kappa
-Poisson integral of
f
, then
v_{\epsilon_{k}}(x,y)
converges pointwise to
v(x,y)
for
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}
. Thus, since by Lemma
5.5
,
\Omega_{\epsilon_{k}}
approaches increasingly to
\Omega^{E}(a_{1},h_{1})
, we conclude from (
6
) that

\displaystyle|u(x,y)|\leq c^{\prime\prime}v(x,y)+c,\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega^{E}(a_{1},h_{1}).

By Proposition
2.9
,
v(x,y)
is non-tangentially bounded at
(x,0)
for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and hence, the same is true for
u(x,y)
and for almost every
x\in E
. The proof of Theorem
6.1
is completed.
\square

In order to prove Theorem
6.2
, we need the following lemma for
G=Z_{2}^{d}
, that is an analog of Lemma
6.6
.

Lemma 6.8

Suppose
G=Z_{2}^{d}
with a given multiplicity parameters
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})
. Let
b,\eta>0
be given and
u
a
\lambda
-harmonic function on
\Omega=\cup_{\sigma\in G}\Gamma_{b}^{\eta}(\sigma(x^{0}))
. If
(S^{\psi}_{b,\eta}u)(\sigma(x^{0}))\leq 1
for all
\sigma\in G
, then for fixed
a\in(0,b/2)
and
h\in(0,\eta)
, there exists a constant
c=c(a,b,h,\eta)>0
, so that
y|\nabla u|\leq c
in
\Gamma_{a}^{h}(x^{0})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.6
, it suffices to show that, for
j=1,2,\dots,d
,

\displaystyle y\left|\frac{u(x,y)-u(\sigma_{j}(x),y)}{x_{j}}\right|\leq c,\qquad(x,y)\in\Gamma_{a}^{h}(x^{0}),

(79)

where
\sigma_{j}(x)=(x_{1},\dots,-x_{j},\dots,x_{d})
.

We continue to use the notation in the proof of Lemma
5.6
. From (
50
) we have

\displaystyle u(x,y)-u(\sigma_{j}(x),y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}}u(t,s)\left[\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)-\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(\sigma_{j}(t),s)\right]\,d\omega_{\lambda}(t)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}}(u(t,s)-u(\sigma_{j}(t),s)\left[\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)-\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(\sigma_{j}(t),s)\right]\,d\omega_{\lambda}(t)ds.

(80)

Recall that the intertwining operator
V_{\lambda}
for
G=Z_{2}^{d}
has the form (cf.
[
13
,
59
]
)

\displaystyle(V_{\lambda}f)(x)=\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}f(x_{1}\theta_{1},\dots,x_{d}\theta_{d})\,dm_{\lambda}(\theta),

(81)

where
dm_{\lambda}(\theta)=dm_{\lambda_{1}}(\theta_{1})\dots dm_{\lambda_{d}}(\theta_{d})
, and for
1\leq i\leq d
,
dm_{\lambda_{i}}(\theta_{i})=c_{\lambda_{i}}(1+\theta_{i})(1-\theta_{i}^{2})^{\lambda_{i}-1}d\theta_{i}
,
c_{\lambda_{i}}=\Gamma(\lambda_{i}+1/2)/\Gamma(\lambda_{i})\Gamma(1/2)
.
In view of (
14
) we have

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)-\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(\sigma_{j}(t),s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2|\lambda|+d+1}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d}}\left[\phi_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}B(x,y,t,s,\theta)\right)-\phi_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}B(x,y,\sigma_{j}(t),s,\theta)\right)\right]\,dm_{\lambda}(\theta),

where

B(x,y,t,s,\theta)=\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|t|^{2}-2(x_{1}t_{1}\theta_{1}+\dots+x_{d}t_{d}\theta_{d})+(s-y)^{2}}.

The change of variables
\theta_{j}\mapsto-\theta_{j}
to the second integrand above yields

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)-\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(\sigma_{j}(t),s)

\displaystyle=\frac{2c_{\lambda_{j}}}{\epsilon^{2|\lambda|+d+1}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d-1}}\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}B(x,y,t,s,\theta)\right)\theta_{j}(1-\theta_{j}^{2})^{\lambda_{j}-1}d\theta_{j}\,d\widetilde{m_{\lambda}}(\theta),

where
d\widetilde{m_{\lambda}}(\theta)=\prod_{k\neq j}dm_{\lambda_{k}}(\theta_{k})
. Now we take integration by parts for
\theta_{j}
to get

\displaystyle\left|\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)-\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(\sigma_{j}(t),s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{\lambda_{j}}\lambda_{j}^{-1}|x_{j}t_{j}|}{\epsilon^{2|\lambda|+d+2}}\int_{[-1,1]^{d-1}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\left|\phi^{\prime}_{0}\left(\epsilon^{-1}B(x,y,t,s,\theta)\right)\right|}{B(x,y,t,s,\theta)}(1-\theta_{j}^{2})^{\lambda_{j}}d\theta_{j}\,d\widetilde{m_{\lambda}}(\theta),

(82)

We consider the generalized translation
\tau^{+}_{x}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
associated the parameters

\lambda^{+}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{j-1},\lambda_{j}+1,\lambda_{j+1},\dots,\lambda_{d}).

As before, the associated translation
\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(x,y)}
for
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
is given by
(\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(x,y)}u)(t,s)=(\tau^{+}_{x}u)(t,y+s)
, where
\tau^{+}_{x}
acts on the first argument. Note that
\tilde{\phi}_{0}(r):=|\phi^{\prime}_{0}(r)/r|
is in
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and even. If we set
\tilde{\phi}(x,y)=\tilde{\phi}_{0}(\sqrt{|x|^{2}+y^{2}})
and
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon}(x,y)=\epsilon^{-2|\lambda^{+}|-d-1}\tilde{\phi}(\epsilon^{-1}x,\epsilon^{-1}y)
, then from (
14
), (
81
) and (
6
),

\displaystyle\left|\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)-\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(\sigma_{j}(t),s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|x_{j}t_{j}|}{2\lambda_{j}+1}\left[\left(\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(-x,-y)}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)+\left(\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(-\sigma_{j}(x),-y)}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)\right].

(83)

In what follows we set

\Psi_{j}(x,y)=\left|\frac{u(x,y)-u(\sigma_{j}(x),y)}{x_{j}}\right|.

Inserting (
6
) into (
6
) gives

\displaystyle(4\lambda_{j}+2)\Psi_{j}(x,y)\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}}\Psi_{j}(t,s)\left[\left(\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(-x,-y)}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)+\left(\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(-\sigma_{j}(x),-y)}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)\right]\,d\omega_{\lambda^{+}}(t)ds,

and by Hölder’s inequality and Proposition
2.5
(iii),

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}(x,y)^{2}\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}}\Psi_{j}(t,s)^{2}\left[\left(\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(-x,-y)}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)+\left(\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(-\sigma_{j}(x),-y)}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon}\right)(t,s)\right]\,d\omega_{\lambda^{+}}(t)ds,

where
c=\|\tilde{\phi}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1},d\omega_{\lambda^{+}}(t)ds)}/2(2\lambda_{j}+1)^{2}
. Furthermore, by Proposition
3.1
(i),

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}(x,y)^{2}\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}}\left[\left(\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(x,y)}+\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(\sigma_{j}(x),y)}\right)\Psi_{j}^{2}\right](t,s)\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon}(t,s)\,d\omega_{\lambda^{+}}(t)ds,

and since

t_{j}^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon}(t,s)\leq\epsilon^{-2|\lambda|-d-1}\Phi(\epsilon^{-1}t,\epsilon^{-1}s),\qquad\Phi(t,s)=\sqrt{|t|^{2}+s^{2}}|\phi^{\prime}_{0}(\sqrt{|t|^{2}+s^{2}})|,

it follows that

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2|\lambda|+d+1}\Psi_{j}(x,y)^{2}\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}}\left[\left(\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(x,y)}+\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(\sigma_{j}(x),y)}\right)\Psi_{j}^{2}\right](t,s)\Phi\left(\frac{t}{\epsilon},\frac{s}{\epsilon}\right)\,d\omega_{\lambda}(t)ds.

(84)

We note that the translation operation
\tilde{\tau}_{(x,y)}
can be iteratively written into

\tilde{\tau}_{(x,y)}=\tau_{x_{1}}\dots\tau_{x_{d}}\tau_{y}
,
where
\tau_{y}
is the usual translation, and
\tau_{x_{i}}
(
1\leq i\leq d
) denotes the one-dimensional generalized translation in the
x_{i}
variable associated to the parameter
\lambda_{i}
, which is given by
(cf.
[
32
,
45
]
)

\displaystyle(\tau_{x_{i}}f)(t_{i})=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(f_{e}(\tilde{B}_{x_{i},t_{i}}(\theta_{i}))+f_{o}(\tilde{B}_{x_{i},t_{i}}(\theta_{i}))\frac{x_{i}+t_{i}}{\tilde{B}_{x_{i},t_{i}}(\theta_{i})}\right)dm_{\lambda_{i}}(\theta_{i}),

(85)

where
f
is a function defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
,
f_{e}
and
f_{o}
are its even part and odd part respectively, and
\tilde{B}_{x_{i},t_{i}}(\theta_{i})=\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+t_{i}^{2}+2x_{i}t_{i}\theta_{i}}
. If we use
\tau^{+}_{x_{j}}
to denote the generalized translation in the
x_{j}
variable associated to the parameter
\lambda_{j}+1
, then
\tilde{\tau}^{+}_{(x,y)}=\tau_{x_{1}}\dots\tau_{x_{j-1}}\tau^{+}_{x_{j}}\tau_{x_{j+1}}\dots\tau_{x_{d}}\tau_{y}
.
But from (
85
), one has

\displaystyle\left[(\tau^{+}_{x_{j}}+\tau^{+}_{-x_{j}})f\right](t_{j})=c_{\lambda_{j}+1}\int_{-1}^{1}\left[f(\tilde{B}_{x_{j},t_{j}}(\theta_{j}))\left(1+\frac{t_{j}+x_{j}\theta_{j}}{\tilde{B}_{x_{j},t_{j}}(\theta_{j})}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+f(-\tilde{B}_{x_{j},t_{j}}(\theta_{j}))\left(1-\frac{t_{j}+x_{j}\theta_{j}}{\tilde{B}_{x_{j},t_{j}}(\theta_{j})}\right)\right](1-\theta_{j}^{2})^{\lambda_{j}}d\theta_{j},

which implies that
\tau^{+}_{x_{j}}+\tau^{+}_{-x_{j}}
is a positive operator, and if
f
is nonnegative, then

\displaystyle\left[(\tau^{+}_{x_{j}}+\tau^{+}_{-x_{j}})f\right](t_{j})\leq\frac{2\lambda_{j}+1}{2\lambda_{j}}\left[(\tau_{x_{j}}+\tau_{-x_{j}})f\right](t_{j}).

(86)

Now we rewrite (
84
) as

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2|\lambda|+d+1}\Psi_{j}(x,y)^{2}\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}}\left[\left(\tau_{x_{j}}^{+}+\tau_{-x_{j}}^{+}\right)\Psi_{j}^{2}\right](t,s)K_{\epsilon}(x,y,t,s)\,d\omega_{\lambda}(t)ds,

where
K_{\epsilon}(x,y,t,s)=\left[\tau_{-x_{1}}\dots\tau_{-x_{j-1}}\tau_{-x_{j+1}}\dots\tau_{-x_{d}}\tau_{-y}\left(\Phi\left(\epsilon^{-1}\cdot\right)\right)\right](t,s)
, and applying (
86
), we get

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2|\lambda|+d+1}\Psi_{j}(x,y)^{2}\leq\frac{c(2\lambda_{j}+1)}{2\lambda_{j}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}}\Psi_{j}(t,s)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left[\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\left(\Phi\left(\epsilon^{-1}\cdot\right)\right)\right)(t,s)+\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-\sigma_{j}(x),-y)}\left(\Phi\left(\epsilon^{-1}\cdot\right)\right)\right)(t,s)\right]\,d\omega_{\lambda}(t)ds,

(87)

As in the proof of Lemma
6.6
, for fixed
a\in(0,b/2)
and
h\in(0,\eta)
and for
(x,y)\in\Gamma_{a}^{h}(x^{0})
, take
\epsilon=c_{2}y
for suitable
0<c_{2}<(b-2a)/(b+2)
, independent of
(x,y)
, such that
\overline{{\mathbb{B}}((x,y),\epsilon)}\subset\Gamma_{b}^{\eta}(x^{0})\subset\Omega
. Similarly to (
61
) we have

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-x,-y)}\left(\Phi\left(\epsilon^{-1}\cdot\right)\right)\right)(t,s)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{cy^{2|\lambda|+d-1}}{s^{2|\lambda|+d-1}}\left(\tau_{-\frac{x^{0}}{bs}}\psi\right)\left(\frac{t}{bs}\right),

(88)

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\tau}_{(-\sigma_{j}(x),-y)}\left(\Phi\left(\epsilon^{-1}\cdot\right)\right)\right)(t,s)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{cy^{2|\lambda|+d-1}}{s^{2|\lambda|+d-1}}\left(\tau_{-\frac{\sigma_{j}(x^{0})}{bs}}\psi\right)\left(\frac{t}{bs}\right),

(89)

and for
s>\eta
, both the quantities on the left hand side above vanish.

Applying (
88
) and (
89
) to (
6
) we obtain

\displaystyle y^{2}\Psi_{j}(x,y)^{2}\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times(0,\eta]}\Psi_{j}(t,s)^{2}\left[\left(\tau_{-\frac{x^{0}}{bs}}\psi\right)\left(\frac{t}{bs}\right)+\left(\tau_{-\frac{\sigma_{j}(x^{0})}{bs}}\psi\right)\left(\frac{t}{bs}\right)\right]\frac{d\omega_{\lambda}(t)ds}{s^{2|\lambda|+d-1}}.

(90)

From (
46
),
\left(\tau_{-\frac{x^{0}}{bs}}\psi\right)\left(\frac{t}{bs}\right)=\left(\tau_{-\frac{\sigma_{j}(x^{0})}{bs}}\psi\right)\left(\frac{t}{bs}\right)=0
for
(t,s)\notin\Omega
, and since
u
is
\lambda
-harmonic in
\Omega
, from (
41
) it follows that

\Psi_{j}(t,s)^{2}\leq(2\lambda_{j})^{-1}\left(\Delta_{\kappa}u^{2}\right)(t,s)
for
(t,s)\in\Omega
. Thus from (
43
) and (
90
),

y^{2}\Psi_{j}(x,y)^{2}\leq c\left[(S^{\psi}_{b,\eta}u)^{2}(x^{0})+(S^{\psi}_{b,\eta}u)^{2}(\sigma_{j}(x^{0}))\right]\leq 2c

for
(x,y)\in\Gamma_{a}^{h}(x^{0})
. Thus (
79
) is proved and the proof of the lemma is finished.
\square

6.8
the condition (
70
) is true
without
G
-invariance of

.

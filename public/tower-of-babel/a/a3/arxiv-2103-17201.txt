Toward the existence of odderon as a three-gluon bound state
Hua-Xing Chen 1, Wei Chen 2, Shi-Lin Zhu 3,4,5

Abstract
Inspired by the evidence of the odderon exchange recently observed by the D0 and TOTEM Collaborations, a QCD sum rule investigation is performed to study the odderon as a three-gluon bound state. There may exist six lowest-lying three-gluon odderons with the quantum numbers J P ​ C = 1 / 2 / 3 ± ⁣ − J^{PC}=1/2/3^{\pm-} . We systematically construct their interpolating currents, and calculate their mass spectra. To verify their existence, we propose to search for the spin-3 odderons in their V ​ V ​ V VVV and V ​ V ​ P VVP decay channels directly at LHC, with V V and P P light vector and pseudoscalar mesons respectively.



Introduction.
—–
Very recently, the D0 and TOTEM Collaborations compared their
pp
and
p\bar{p}

Abazov:2012qb
cross sections, and found they differ with a significance of
3.4\sigma

Abazov:2020rus
. This leads to the evidence of a
t
-channel exchanged odderon
Cudell:2002xe
;
Martynov:2018sga
,
i.e.
, a colorless
C
-odd gluonic compound. They further combined their previous result of Ref.
Antchev:2017yns
, and increased the significance of this evidence to
5.2\sigma
–
5.7\sigma
. Accordingly, the D0 and TOTEM Collaborations claimed that they have accomplished the first experimental observation of the odderon!

Actually, in the previous TOTEM experiment
Antchev:2017yns
they had attempted to probe the existence of the odderon, and there had been some discussions on it
Khoze:2018bus
;
Csorgo:2018uyp
;
Goncalves:2018nsp
;
Xie:2019soz
. However, these discussions are mainly on the contribution of the odderon exchange in certain collisions, so it is crucial and important to directly study the odderon itself. Recall that the odderon was first introduced in 1973
Lukaszuk:1973nt
, and later reintroduced in QCD in the 1980’s
Bartels:1980pe
;
Kwiecinski:1980wb
;
Donnachie:1983ff
. There had been several attempts to prove its existence, and we refer to the review
Block:2006hy
for detailed discussions.

The odderon is probably/predominantly a
C
-odd three-gluon bound state, that is one category of glueballs. Glueballs are important for the understanding of non-perturbative QCD, but there is currently no definite
experimental evidence for their existence, although there have been tremendous of discussions on them using various theoretical methods and models, such as the MIT bag model
Chodos:1974je
;
Jaffe:1975fd
, the flux tube model
Isgur:1984bm
, the Coulomb Gauge model
Szczepaniak:1995cw
;
LlanesEstrada:2005jf
, Lattice QCD
Wilson:1974sk
;
Chen:2005mg
;
Mathieu:2008me
;
Meyer:2004gx
;
Gregory:2012hu
, and QCD sum rules
Novikov:1979va
;
Narison:1984hu
;
Narison:1996fm
;
Bagan:1990sy
, etc. Especially, mass spectra of the spin-0 odderons were systematically studies in Refs.
Latorre:1987wt
;
Hao:2005hu
;
Qiao:2014vva
;
Qiao:2015iea
;
Pimikov:2016pag
by using QCD sum rules. However, in Ref.
Szanyi:2019kkn
the authors studied glueball trajectories, and their results suggest that the odderon is essentially an object in the Regge pole model, and any derivation of its finite mass from massless gluons meets conceptual difficulties in QCD, preventing reliable calculations of its spectrum.

The lowest-lying two-, three-, and four-glueballs were systematically investigated in Ref.
Jaffe:1985qp
, where the authors classified altogether six lowest-lying odderons with the quantum numbers
J^{PC}=1/2/3^{\pm-}
, and constructed their corresponding non-relativistic low-dimension operators. Some of these operators have been successfully used in Lattice QCD calculations. This inspires us to construct their corresponding relativistic odderon currents, which can be further applied to perform QCD sum rule analyses and calculate masses of the
J^{PC}=1/2/3^{\pm-}
odderons. This is just the aim of the present letter. Comparison of our QCD sum rule results with the Lattice QCD results
Chen:2005mg
;
Mathieu:2008me
;
Meyer:2004gx
;
Gregory:2012hu
can be found in Table
2
at the end of this letter.

Relativistic odderon currents.
—–
As the first step, we use the gluon field strength tensor
G^{a}_{\mu\nu}
to construct relativistic odderon currents. Here
a
is a color index, and
\mu,\nu
are Lorentz indices. Besides, we use
d^{abc}
to denote the totally symmetric
SU_{c}(3)
structure constant, and
\tilde{G}^{a}_{\mu\nu}
to denote the dual gluon field strength tensor, defined as
\tilde{G}^{a}_{\mu\nu}=G^{a,\rho\sigma}\times\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}/2
.

In Ref.
Jaffe:1985qp
the chromoelectric and chromomagnetic fields (
i,j=1,2,3
),

E_{i}=G_{i0}~~~{\rm and}~~~B_{i}=-{1\over 2}\epsilon_{ijk}G^{jk}\,,

(1)

were used to write down the non-relativistic low-dimension odderon operators:

\displaystyle 1^{+-}~~~~~d^{abc}(\vec{E}_{a}\cdot\vec{E}_{b})\vec{B}_{c}\,,

\displaystyle 1^{--}~~~~~d^{abc}(\vec{E}_{a}\cdot\vec{E}_{b})\vec{E}_{c}\,,

\displaystyle 2^{+-}~~~~~d^{abc}\mathcal{S}[E_{a}^{i}(\vec{E}_{b}\times\vec{B}_{c})^{j}]\,,

\displaystyle 2^{--}~~~~~d^{abc}\mathcal{S}[B_{a}^{i}(\vec{E}_{b}\times\vec{B}_{c})^{j}]\,,

\displaystyle 3^{+-}~~~~~d^{abc}\mathcal{S}[B_{a}^{i}B_{b}^{j}B_{c}^{k}]\,,

\displaystyle 3^{--}~~~~~d^{abc}\mathcal{S}[E_{a}^{i}E_{b}^{j}E_{c}^{k}]\,,

(2)

where
\mathcal{S}
denotes symmetrization and subtracting trace terms in the set
\{ij\}
or
\{ijk\}
.

To perform QCD sum rule analyses, we further construct their corresponding relativistic currents:

\displaystyle J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d^{abc}G_{a}^{\mu\nu}G_{b,\mu\nu}G_{c}^{\alpha\beta}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle J_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d^{abc}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[G_{a}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\tilde{G}_{b}^{\alpha_{2}\mu}G_{c,\mu}^{\beta_{2}}-\{\alpha_{2}\leftrightarrow\beta_{2}\}],

\displaystyle J_{3}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\beta_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d^{abc}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[G_{a}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}G_{b}^{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}G_{c}^{\alpha_{3}\beta_{3}}]\,,

(5)

where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
denotes symmetrization and subtracting trace terms in the two sets
\{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{J}\}
and
\{\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{J}\}
simultaneously.

The first current
J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
contains both
J^{PC}=1^{+-}
and
1^{--}
components, and it couples to the
1^{+-}
and
1^{--}
odderons simultaneously:

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}|X_{1^{+-}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{1^{+-}}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\epsilon_{\mu}p_{\nu}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}|X_{1^{--}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{1^{--}}(p^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\beta}-p^{\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha})\,,

(7)

where
f_{1^{+-}}
and
f_{1^{--}}
are decay constants, and
\epsilon_{\mu}
is the polarization vector. This current has a partner

\tilde{J}_{1}^{\alpha\beta}=d^{abc}\tilde{G}_{a}^{\mu\nu}\tilde{G}_{b,\mu\nu}\tilde{G}_{c}^{\alpha\beta}\,,

(8)

which just has the opposite couplings:

\displaystyle\langle 0|\tilde{J}_{1}^{\alpha\beta}|X_{1^{+-}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{1^{+-}}(p^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\beta}-p^{\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha})\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\langle 0|\tilde{J}_{1}^{\alpha\beta}|X_{1^{--}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{1^{--}}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\epsilon_{\mu}p_{\nu}\,.

(10)

Therefore, we can use either
J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
or
\tilde{J}_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
to investigate the
1^{+-}
and
1^{--}
odderons at the same time. In the present study we use
J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
to investigate the
1^{+-}
odderon and
\tilde{J}_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
to investigate the
1^{--}
one, because the couplings given in Eqs. (
6
) and (
10
) can be more easily calculated.

The currents
J_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}
and its partner

\tilde{J}_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}=d^{abc}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[\tilde{G}_{a}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}G_{b}^{\alpha_{2}\mu}\tilde{G}_{c,\mu}^{\beta_{2}}-\{\alpha_{2}\leftrightarrow\beta_{2}\}],

(11)

couple to the
2^{+-}
and
2^{--}
odderons through:

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{2}^{\cdots}|X_{2^{+-}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{2^{+-}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[\epsilon^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}\epsilon_{\mu_{i}}p_{\nu_{i}}]^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\langle 0|\tilde{J}_{2}^{\cdots}|X_{2^{--}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{2^{--}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[\epsilon^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}\epsilon_{\mu_{i}}p_{\nu_{i}}]^{2}\,,

(12)

where

[\cdots]^{J}=\epsilon^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}\epsilon_{\mu_{1}}p_{\nu_{1}}\cdots\epsilon^{\alpha_{J}\beta_{J}\mu_{J}\nu_{J}}\epsilon_{\mu_{J}}p_{\nu_{J}}\,.

(13)

The current
J_{3}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\beta_{3}}
and its partner

\tilde{J}_{3}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\beta_{3}}=d^{abc}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[\tilde{G}_{a}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\tilde{G}_{b}^{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}\tilde{G}_{c}^{\alpha_{3}\beta_{3}}]\,,

(14)

couple to the
3^{+-}
and
3^{--}
odderons through:

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{3}^{\cdots}|X_{3^{+-}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{3^{+-}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[\epsilon^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}\epsilon_{\mu_{i}}p_{\nu_{i}}]^{3}\,,

\displaystyle\langle 0|\tilde{J}_{3}^{\cdots}|X_{3^{--}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{3^{--}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[\epsilon^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}\epsilon_{\mu_{i}}p_{\nu_{i}}]^{3}\,.

(15)

It is interesting to notice that all the above currents have
N=2J
Lorentz indices with certain symmetries, so that they can couple to the positive- and negative-parity odderons simultaneously. For example, the spin-2 current
J_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}
have four Lorentz indices, satisfying

J_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}=-J_{2}^{\beta_{1}\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}}=-J_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{2},\beta_{1}\alpha_{2}}=J_{2}^{\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1},\beta_{2}\beta_{1}}\,.

(16)

QCD sum rule analyses.
—–
In this section we use the current
J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
as an example to perform QCD sum rule analyses, which method has been widely applied in the study of hadron phenomenology
Shifman:1978bx
;
Reinders:1984sr
. We study the two-point correlation function

\displaystyle\Pi^{\alpha\beta,\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(q^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle i\int d^{4}xe^{iqx}\langle 0|{\bf T}[J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}(x)J_{1}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}\dagger}(0)]|0\rangle

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}g^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}-g^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}g^{\beta\alpha^{\prime}})~\Pi(q^{2})\,,

at both hadron and quark-gluon levels.

At the hadron level we use the dispersion relation to express Eq. (
17
) as

\Pi(q^{2})=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\rho(s)}{s-q^{2}-i\varepsilon}ds\,,

(18)

where
\rho(s)\equiv{\rm Im}\Pi(s)/\pi
is the spectral density. We parameterize it using one
pole dominance for the ground state
X
together with the continuum contribution,

\displaystyle\rho(s)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\delta(s-M^{2}_{n})\langle 0|J|n\rangle\langle n|J^{\dagger}|0\rangle

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{2}_{X}\delta(s-M^{2}_{X})+\rm{continuum}\,.

At the quark-gluon level we insert
J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
into Eq. (
17
) and calculate it using the method of operator product expansion (OPE). Then we perform the Borel transformation to Eq. (
18
) at both hadron and quark-gluon levels. After approximating the continuum using the spectral density above a threshold value
s_{0}
, we obtain

\Pi(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})\equiv f^{2}_{X}e^{-M_{X}^{2}/M_{B}^{2}}=\int^{s_{0}}_{0}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}\rho(s)ds\,,

(20)

which can be used to calculate the mass of
X
through

M^{2}_{X}(s_{0},M_{B})=\frac{\int^{s_{0}}_{0}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}s\rho(s)ds}{\int^{s_{0}}_{0}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}\rho(s)ds}\,.

(21)

In the present study we calculate OPEs up to the dimension eight (
D=8
) condensates, including the perturbative term, the two-gluon condensate
\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle\equiv\langle g_{s}^{2}G^{a}_{\mu\nu}G_{a}^{\mu\nu}\rangle
, the three-gluon condensate
\langle g_{s}^{3}G^{3}\rangle\equiv\langle g_{s}^{3}f^{abc}G_{a}^{\mu\nu}G_{b,\nu\rho}G_{c,\mu}^{\rho}\rangle
, and their combination
\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle^{2}
:

\displaystyle\rho_{1^{+-}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\alpha_{s}^{3}}{81\pi}s^{4}+\frac{10\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{9}s^{2}+\frac{35\alpha_{s}^{3}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{36\pi}s^{2}

(22)

\displaystyle-\frac{205\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{3}G^{3}\rangle}{54}s\,,

\displaystyle\rho_{1^{--}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\alpha_{s}^{3}}{81\pi}s^{4}-\frac{10\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{9}s^{2}+\frac{25\alpha_{s}^{3}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{36\pi}s^{2}

(23)

\displaystyle+\frac{5\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{3}G^{3}\rangle}{54}s\,,

\displaystyle\rho_{2^{+-}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}^{3}}{81\pi}s^{4}+\frac{5\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{27}s^{2}+\frac{15\alpha_{s}^{3}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{32\pi}s^{2}

(24)

\displaystyle-\frac{20\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{3}G^{3}\rangle}{27}s\,,

\displaystyle\rho_{2^{--}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}^{3}}{81\pi}s^{4}-\frac{5\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{27}s^{2}+\frac{15\alpha_{s}^{3}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{32\pi}s^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle-\frac{10\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{3}G^{3}\rangle}{27}s\,,

\displaystyle\rho_{3^{+-}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{5\alpha_{s}^{3}}{2016\pi}s^{4}+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{16}s^{2}-\frac{59\alpha_{s}^{3}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{512\pi}s^{2}

(26)

\displaystyle+\frac{13\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{3}G^{3}\rangle}{384}s\,,

\displaystyle\rho_{3^{--}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{5\alpha_{s}^{3}}{2016\pi}s^{4}-\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{16}s^{2}-\frac{49\alpha_{s}^{3}\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle}{1536\pi}s^{2}

(27)

\displaystyle+\frac{157\alpha_{s}^{2}\langle g_{s}^{3}G^{3}\rangle}{3456}s\,.

Especially, we find all the
D=8
terms proportional to
\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle^{2}
vanish, so the convergence of the above OPE series are quite good.

\alpha_{s}(Q^{2})={4\pi\over 11\ln(Q^{2}/\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2})}\,,

(28)

with the QCD scale at

MeV.

\mbox{PC}\equiv\left|\frac{\Pi(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}{\Pi(\infty,M_{B}^{2})}\right|\geq 40\%\,.

Summary and discussions.
—–
In this letter we apply the method of QCD sum rules to study the odderon as a three-gluon bound state. There may exist six lowest-lying odderons with the quantum numbers
J^{PC}=1/2/3^{\pm-}
. We systematically construct their interpolating currents using the gluon field strength tensors
G^{a}_{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{G}^{a}_{\mu\nu}
. All these currents have
N=2J
Lorentz indices with certain symmetries, so that they couple to both the positive- and negative-parity odderons, which need to be further separated at the hadron level. The construction of such currents is quite general and may be applied in fields other than hadron physics.

We construct altogether six relativistic low-dimension odderon currents with the quantum numbers
J^{PC}=1/2/3^{\pm-}
. We use them to perform QCD sum rule analyses, and calculate masses of the
J^{PC}=1/2/3^{\pm-}

From the above comparison, we can see how we poorly understand the odderon. Recall that there is currently no definite experimental evidence for the existence of any glueball yet, we quickly realize how important is the evidence of the odderon exchange recently observed by D0 and TOTEM
Abazov:2020rus
. Since this is still an indirect evidence, we propose to directly search for the odderon at LHC.

From the viewpoint of quark model, the odderon can decay after exciting three quark-antiquark pairs, and recombine into three mesons. Generally speaking, its width can be quite large, preventing it to be easily observed. We use
P
and
V
to denote the light vector and pseudoscalar mesons respectively, and its possible decay patterns are:

\displaystyle 1^{--}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle~~~~\,VPP,VVP,VVV~~~~~(S\mbox{-wave})\,,

\displaystyle 1^{+-}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle PPP,VPP,VVP,VVV~(P\mbox{-wave})\,,

\displaystyle 2^{--}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~\,VVP,VVV~~~~~~~~~(S\mbox{-wave})\,,

\displaystyle 2^{+-}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle~~~~VPP,VVP,VVV~~~~~(P\mbox{-wave})\,,

\displaystyle 3^{--}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~\,VVV~~~~~~~~~~~~~(S\mbox{-wave})\,,

\displaystyle 3^{+-}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~VVP,VVV~~~~~~~~~(P\mbox{-wave})\,.

Due to their limited decay patterns, the spin-3 odderons have relatively smaller widths probably, and so we propose to search for them in their
VVV
and
VVP
decay channels directly at LHC.

Combining QED and QCD transverse-momentum resummation for 𝑾 W and 𝒁 Z boson production at hadron colliders Preprint: IFIC/23-06, FTUV-22-1126.2949
Andrea Autieri, Leandro Cieri, Giancarlo Ferrera, and German F. R. Sborlini

Abstract
In this article, we consider the transverse momentum ( q T q_{T} ) distribution of W W and Z Z bosons produced in hadronic collisions. We combine the q T q_{T} resummation for QED and QCD radiation including the QED soft emissions from the W W boson in the final state. In particular, we perform the resummation of enhanced logarithmic contributions due to soft and collinear emissions at next-to-leading accuracy in QED, leading-order accuracy for mixed QED-QCD and next-to-next-to-leading accuracy in QCD. In the small- q T q_{T} region we consistently include in our results the next-to-next-to-leading order (i.e. two loops) QCD corrections and the next-to-leading order (i.e. one loop) electroweak corrections. The matching with the fixed-order calculation at large q T q_{T} has been performed at next-to-leading order in QCD (i.e. at 𝒪 ⁡ ( α S 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha_{S}^{2}) ) and at leading order in QED. We show numerical results for W W and Z Z production at the Tevatron and the LHC. Finally, we consider the effect of combined QCD and QED resummation for the ratio of W W and Z Z q T q_{T} distributions, and we study the impact of the QED corrections providing an estimate of the corresponding perturbative uncertainties.

2
QCD transverse-momentum resummation

In the context of high-energy physics, the calculation of cross-sections customary relies on the factorization theorem
Collins:1989gx
.
In this paper we consider the production of a vector boson
V
(
V=W,Z,\gamma^{*}
), with invariant mass

M^{2}
, at hadron colliders. The associated vector boson transverse-momentum (
q_{T}
) differential cross-section describing a collision with hadronic centre-of-mass energy
\sqrt{s}
is factorized as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{h_{1}h_{2}\rightarrow V}}{dq_{T}^{2}}(q_{T},M,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}}\int dx_{1}dx_{2}\,f_{a_{1}/h_{1}}(x_{1},\mu_{F}^{2})\,f_{a_{2}/h_{2}}(x_{2},\mu_{F}^{2})\,

(1)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{a_{1}a_{2}\rightarrow V}}{dq_{T}^{2}}(q_{T},M,\hat{s};\mu_{F}^{2})\,,

with
f_{a/h}(x,\mu_{F}^{2})
the parton distribution functions (PDFs) associated to the probability of extracting a parton of flavour
a
from an hadron
h
with longitudinal momentum fraction
x
, at the factorization scale
\mu_{F}^{2}
.
The partonic centre-of-mass energy is given by
\hat{s}=\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}s}
, under the assumption of massless colliding partons, and
d\hat{\sigma}
denotes the partonic cross-section.

The partonic cross section
d\hat{\sigma}
can be computed within perturbation theory, whereas the PDFs are extracted from data or modelled by non-perturbative methods. The dependence of the renormalization scale
\mu_{R}^{2}
is included in the partonic cross-section, and it can be accounted for by a suitable redefinition of the running couplings.

Regarding the partonic cross-section, it is well known that fixed-order perturbation theory fails to accurately describe the low-
q_{T}
region,
q_{T}\ll M
. This is because the presence of logarithmically enhanced terms, proportional to
\log(q_{T}^{2}/M^{2})
which grow faster than the suppression introduced by the higher-powers of the QCD coupling
\alpha_{\rm S}
. For this reason,
q_{T}
resummation
formalism have been developed in QCD in order to rearrange the perturbative series and resum it collecting to all orders the terms proportional to
\alpha_{\rm S}^{n}\,\log^{m}(q_{T}^{2}/M^{2})
.

The resummation of the perturbative expansion is achieved by explicitly splitting the partonic cross section as

\frac{d\hat{\sigma}}{dq_{T}^{2}}=\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{\rm(res.)}}{dq_{T}^{2}}+\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{\rm(fin.)}}{dq_{T}^{2}}\,,

(2)

with all the logarithmically enhanced contributions embodied within
d\hat{\sigma}^{\rm(res.)}
. More precisely, the finite component is defined in such a way that

\lim_{Q_{T}\rightarrow 0}\,\int^{Q_{T}^{2}}_{0}\,dq_{T}^{2}\,\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{\rm(fin.)}}{dq_{T}^{2}}=0\,.

(3)

By performing the resummation in the impact parameter space
b

Parisi:1979se
, conjugated to
q_{T}
, the resummed component can be written as
Bozzi:2005wk

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{a_{1}a_{2}\rightarrow V}^{\rm(res.)}}{dq_{T}^{2}}(q_{T},M,\hat{s};\mu_{F}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M^{2}}{\hat{s}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}db\,\frac{b}{2}\,J_{0}(b\,q_{T})\,{\cal W}_{a_{1}a_{2}}^{V}(b,M,\hat{s};\mu_{F}^{2})\,,

(4)

with
J_{0}
the 0th-order Bessel function.
The function
{\cal W}_{a_{1}a_{2}}^{V}
can be organized in an exponential structure and, by considering the
Mellin
N
-moments
{\cal W}^{V}_{a_{1}a_{2},N}

with respect to
z=M^{2}/\hat{s}
at fixed
M
, in the simplified flavour-diagonal case (
a_{1}a_{2}=c\bar{c}
) it reads
Bozzi:2005wk
:

\displaystyle{\cal W}^{V}_{N}(b,M;\mu_{F})=\hat{\sigma}^{(0)}_{V}(M)\,{\cal H}_{N}^{V}(\alpha_{\rm S};\mu_{R}^{2},\mu_{F}^{2},Q^{2})\times\exp\left\{{\cal G}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S},L;\mu_{R}^{2},Q^{2})\right\}\,,

(5)

where
\hat{\sigma}^{(0)}_{V}
is the lowest-order partonic cross section of the hard-scattering process
h_{1}h_{2}\rightarrow V
. The hard-collinear function
{\cal H}_{N}^{V}
includes contributions from the process-dependent hard-virtual corrections and can be expanded in powers of
\alpha_{\rm S}=\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2})
as

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{N}^{V}(\alpha_{\rm S})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n}{\cal H}_{N}^{V\,(n)}\,.

(6)

The exponent
{\cal G}_{N}
resums
in an universal (i.e. process-independent) way
all the logarithmically enhanced terms and can systematically expanded as:

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S},L)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int^{Q^{2}}_{b_{0}^{2}/b^{2}}\frac{dq^{2}}{q^{2}}\left(A(\alpha_{\rm S}(q^{2}))\log\left(\frac{M^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)+\widetilde{B}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S}(q^{2}))\right)

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L\,g^{(1)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L)\,+g_{N}^{(2)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L)+\sum_{n=3}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n-2}g_{N}^{(n)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L)\,,

where the functions
A(\alpha_{\rm S})
and
\widetilde{B}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S})
are perturbative functions with a customary fixed-order expansion in powers
of
\alpha_{\rm S}

Bozzi:2005wk
.
The logarithmic variable
L
reads, in terms of the resummation scale
Q
and
b_{0}=2e^{-\gamma_{E}}
(
\gamma_{E}=0.5772\cdots
is the Euler number),

L=\log\left(\frac{b^{2}\,Q^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}}+1\right)\,,

(8)

which corresponds, in the
b
-space at large
b
, to the logarithmically-enhanced contributions associated to the low-
q_{T}
region. The scale
Q

Bozzi:2005wk
is introduced with the purpose of parametrizing the arbitrariness in the factorized structure of Eq. (
5
).

Truncating Eq. (
7
) up to the first term defines the leading-logarithm (LL) approximation, up to the second term defines the NLL approximation, then NNLL and so on.

In the case of transverse-momentum resummation for processes with colourful final states the explicit structure of the functions
{\cal H}_{N}
and
{\cal G}_{N}
differs with respect to the case of production of colourless final states.
The
q_{T}
resummation formalism for colourful final states requires a colour space
diagonalization of the relevant soft-anomalous dimension which has been worked out,
in the case of heavy-quark pair production up to NLL, in Ref.
Catani:2014qha
.
In particular for heavy-quark pair production at NLL accuracy the function

{\cal G}_{N}
contains an additional component due to soft wide-angle radiation from the heavy quarks
in the final state and from initial/final-state colour interference
Catani:2014qha
. Analogously due to colour correlations produced by soft-parton radiation, the hard-collinear function
{\cal H}_{N}
is a colour space matrix.

originates from soft wide-angle radiation from the final state charged system.
Following the QCD case
Catani:2013tia
;
Catani:2014qha
, the subtraction operator in Eq. (
25
) allows us to define, starting from the renormalized IR divergent all-loop amplitude
\mathcal{M}_{V}
, an IR finite hard-virtual amplitude

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{V}=(1-\widetilde{I}\,^{\prime}_{V}(\epsilon,M^{2}))\mathcal{M}_{V}\,.

(26)

At one-loop Eq. (
26
) reads

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{V}^{(1)}=\mathcal{M}_{V}^{(1)}-\widetilde{I}\,^{\prime(1)}_{V}(\epsilon,M^{2}/\mu_{R}^{2})\mathcal{M}_{V}^{(0)}\,,

(27)

where
{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}_{V}
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}_{V}
are respectively the lowest-order and the one-loop parton-level scattering amplitude for the scattering process
h_{1}h_{2}\rightarrow V
.
In turn the knowledge of the hard-virtual amplitude
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
is sufficient to determine the process-dependent hard-virtual coefficient
Catani:2013tia
:

\displaystyle H^{\prime V}(\alpha)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{n}{H^{\prime}}^{V\,(n)}=\frac{|\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{V}|^{2}}{|{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}_{V}|^{2}}\,,

(28)

which encodes the process-dependent part of the hard-collinear coefficient
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime V}
Eq. (
6
) in QED.

As already mentioned,
in the case of
W
production, the one-loop corrections in QED cannot be included in a trivial way without
breaking the gauge invariance of the results
Wackeroth:1996hz
. This issue is relevant only for the fixed-order corrections
and it does not affect the all-order resummation of enhanced QED logarithmic effects. We solved this issue by including in our results the full EW corrections at one loop in the scattering amplitude
{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}_{V}
. To be consistent, we included the one-loop EW corrections also in the case of
Z
boson production, even if in this case the pure QED corrections can be defined in a straightforward way.

The explicit results for the (not vanishing) NLO hard-collinear functions

{\cal H}_{a_{1}a_{2},N}^{\prime V\,{(1)}}
we have included in our calculation are:

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{q_{f}\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}}\leftarrow q_{f}\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}},N}^{\prime V\,(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{q_{f}}^{2}+e_{\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}}}^{2}}{2}\,\left(\frac{1}{N(N+1)}+H^{\prime V\,(1)}\right)\,,

(29)

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{q_{f}\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}}\leftarrow\gamma\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}},N}^{\prime V\,(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\,e_{q_{f}}^{2}}{(N+1)(N+2)}\,,

(30)

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{q_{f}\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}}\leftarrow\bar{q}_{f}\gamma,N}^{\prime V\,(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\,e_{\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}}}^{2}}{(N+1)(N+2)}\,,

(31)

where the coefficient
H^{\prime V\,(1)}
for
V=\gamma^{*}/Z,W
has been obtained through Eqs. (
26
-
28
)
from the knowledge of the EW one-loop amplitudes for the processes

q\bar{q}\rightarrow Z
and
q_{f}{\bar{q}}_{f^{\prime}}\rightarrow W

Behring:2020cqi
;
Bonciani:2021iis
.

Finally we performed the matching at large
q_{T}
by evaluating the finite part of the partonic cross section in Eq. (
2
)
starting from the computation of the partonic cross section for
V+\gamma
at leading-order in QED
and subtracting from it the perturbative truncation of the resummed component at the same order:

\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{\rm(fin.)}}{dq_{T}^{2}}=\Bigg[\frac{d\hat{\sigma}}{dq_{T}^{2}}\Bigg]_{\rm(f.o.)}-\Bigg[\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{\rm(res.)}}{dq_{T}^{2}}\Bigg]_{\rm(f.o.)}\,.

(32)

Having obtained the
q_{T}
resummation formalism in QED for final state charged high-mass system we are able to
consider the combined QED and QCD resummation using the formalism developed in Ref.
Cieri:2018sfk

that we summarize below.

We combine the QED and QCD resummation formalism by
replacing the functions
{\cal W}_{N}^{V}
and
{\cal G}_{N}
in Eq. (
5
) by their generalised expressions
which include combined QCD and QED
effects through a double perturbative expansion in powers of
\alpha_{\rm S}
and of the electromagnetic coupling evaluated at the renormalization scale

\alpha=\alpha(\mu_{R}^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{N}^{\prime}(\alpha_{\rm S},\alpha,L)

\displaystyle={\cal G}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S},L)+L\;g^{\prime(1)}(\alpha L)+g_{N}^{\prime(2)}(\alpha L)+\sum_{n=3}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{n-2}g_{N}^{\prime(n)}(\alpha L)

\displaystyle+g^{\prime(1,1)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L,\alpha L)+\sum_{n,m=1\atop n+m\neq 2}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{m-1}g_{N}^{\prime(n,m)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L,\alpha L)\;\;,

(33)

and

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{N}^{\prime V}(\alpha_{\rm S},\alpha)

\displaystyle={\cal H}_{N}^{V}(\alpha_{\rm S})+\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,{\cal H}_{N}^{\prime V\,(1)}+\sum_{n=2}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{n}\,{\cal H}_{N}^{\prime V\,(n)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n,m=1}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{S}}{\pi}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{m}\,{\cal H}_{N}^{\prime V\,(n,m)}\,.

(34)

The functional form of the functions
L\,g^{\prime(1)}
,
g_{N}^{\prime(2)}
and
g^{\prime(1,1)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L,\alpha L)

can be found in Ref.
Cieri:2018sfk
.
We recall that the function
L\,g^{\prime(1)}
resums to all order
the LL contributions in QED, the function
g_{N}^{\prime(2)}
the NLL ones and so on, while the terms
g^{\prime(1,1)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L,\alpha L)
and
g_{N}^{\prime(n,m)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L,\alpha L)
include respectively the leading and subleading mixed QCD-QED corrections.
In the case of the production of a charged high-mass system, the function
g_{N}^{\prime(2)}
receive a contribution from
soft wide-angle QED radiation which has been included through the replacement in Eq. (
14
).
The coefficients
{\cal H}_{N}^{\prime V\,(n)}
control the pure QED corrections while the coefficients
{\cal H}_{N}^{\prime V\,(n,m)}

contains the mixed QCD-QED ones.

Finally we have considered, in the factorization formula Eq. (
1
),
the inclusion of the photon parton density
f_{\gamma/h}(x,\mu_{F}^{2})
and the QED effects in the evolution of parton densities.

5
Conclusions

In this article we have combined the QED and QCD transverse-momentum (
q_{T}
) resummation formalisms for the production of electrically neutral and charged high-mass systems. We started from the results
presented in Ref.
Cieri:2018sfk
for on-shell
Z
boson production obtained through an abelianization procedure of the QCD resummation framework and we extended them to the case of
W
boson production.
However, in the
W
boson case, a direct abelianization of QCD results is not possible, due to the presence of a charged final state and the corresponding additional (logarithmically enhanced) QED soft radiation.

Therefore we performed the abelianization (along the lines of Refs.
deFlorian:2015ujt
;
deFlorian:2016gvk
) of the resummation formalism for a coloured final state of Ref.
Catani:2014qha
, by replacing the heavy-quark pair with a
W
boson. As a crosscheck of our approach, in App.
A
, we performed the expansion at small
q_{T}
of the real inclusive cross section, which reproduced the resummation coefficients used in our analyses.

Analytical formulas for the
q_{T}
resummation of soft and collinear QED emissions were obtained at NLL for QED and LL for the mixed QCD-QED terms. The matching with fixed order predictions at leading order in QED and NLO in QCD was also performed, to properly describe the intermediate and large
q_{T}
region.
Different QED resummation effects for
Z
and
W
boson production appear at NLL accuracy due to soft wide-angle emission from
W
boson in the final state.

Through an implementation of our formalism in the public numerical code
DYqT
, we presented predictions at NNLL+NNLO in QCD and NLL+NLO in QED, also including the one-loop electroweak corrections both at Tevatron and LHC. We found that the resummed QED effects reach the percent level both in
Z
and
W
production.
The QED effects are more relevant at the Tevatron than at the LHC where the effect of the gluon parton density is larger.

Uncertainties due to missing higher-order logarithmic terms and fixed-order corrections have been obtained through combined variations of QED resummation (
Q^{\prime}
) and renormalization (
\mu_{R}^{\prime}
) scales. For on-shell
W
boson production,
we have found that the QED scale variation band is
\mathcal{O}(2\%\,-\,4\%)
at LL, and
\mathcal{O}(1\%-\,1.5\%)
at NLL+NLO; while for on-shell
Z
boson is
\mathcal{O}(2\%\,-\,4.5\%)
at LL and
\mathcal{O}(1.5\%-\,3\%)
at NLL+NLO. In both cases, a noticeable reduction of the scale variation band is observed increasing the accuracy of our calculation.

We also considered the ratio of the
W
and
Z

q_{T}
-distributions which is particularly relevant in the context of
m_{W}
extraction. A sizeable reduction of the scale variation band is present at LL in QED, due the cancellation of common uncertainties. However, this does not happen at NLL+NLO, where contributions due to QED emission from the
W
in the
final state cannot be simplified in the ratio of
W
and
Z
distributions.

A natural extension of this work is the inclusion of the decay of the weak bosons and the associated QED radiation from the charged leptons in the final state. Altogether, these corrections might allow a more precise confrontation of the theoretical predictions with the experimental data, thus leading to a more accurate determination of the EW parameters (with particular emphasis in
m_{W}
).

Appendix A
Radiation from a massive final state: logarithmically-enhanced terms and linear power corrections

The logarithmic terms obtained by a fixed-order truncation of the Sudakov form factor can be equivalently found by expanding, at small
q_{T}
, the integrated cross section due to real photon emission at NLO. In this way, we can check the results that we derived through the use of an abelianization-like procedure in Sec.
2
. In order to perform this crosscheck, we followed the process-independent method presented in Ref.
Cieri:2019tfv
, which introduces a small transverse-momentum cutoff (
q_{T}^{cut}
) to capture the divergent behaviour of the cross sections and the sub-leading power corrections as well.

On-shell
W

q_{f}
-

We start by writing the hadronic cross section as

\sigma=\sum_{ab}\tau\int_{\tau}^{1}\frac{dz}{z}\,\mathcal{L}_{ab}\biggl(\frac{\tau}{z}\biggl)\,\frac{1}{z}\,\int dq_{T}^{2}\,\,\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{ab}(q_{T},z)}{dq_{T}^{2}}\,,

(37)

where
\tau=Q^{2}/S
,
S
is the hadronic squared center-of-mass energy and
Q
is the vector boson mass. Here,
\mathcal{L}_{ab}
is the parton luminosity and is given by

\mathcal{L}_{ab}(y)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx}{x}f_{a}(x)f_{b}\biggl(\frac{y}{x}\biggl).

(38)

The structure at small
q_{T}
of the inclusive cross section can be inferred from

\hat{\sigma}_{ab}(z)=\int_{(q_{T}^{cut})^{2}}^{(q_{T}^{max})^{2}}dq_{T}^{2}\,\,\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{ab}(q_{T},z)}{dq_{T}^{2}}\,,

(39)

where

(q_{T}^{max})^{2}=Q^{2}\,\frac{(1-z)^{2}}{4z}\,,

(40)

is the maximum transverse momentum allowed by the kinematics of the event. Since we aim to recalculate the coefficients presented in Sec.
2
, we restrict our attention to the diagonal channel, i.e.
(a,b)=(q_{f},\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}})
, which is given by the three Feynman diagrams shown in Fig.
7
. Then, their contribution to the cross-section is:

\biggl(\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{(1)}}{dq_{T}^{2}}\biggl)=\biggl(\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{(1)}}{dq_{T}^{2}}\biggl)_{I.S.R.}+\biggl(\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{(1)}}{dq_{T}^{2}}\biggl)_{F.S.R.-int}\ ,

(41)

where the first term in the r.h.s
is due to the initial-state radiation, and it is given by:

\biggl(\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(q_{T},z)}{dq_{T}^{2}}\biggl)_{I.S.R.}=\hat{\sigma}^{(0)}\biggl(\frac{e_{q_{f}}^{2}+e_{\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}}}^{2}}{2}\biggl)\,\,z\,\frac{-4z\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{Q^{2}}+2(1-z)\hat{p}_{qq}(z)}{\sqrt{(1-z)^{2}-4z\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{Q^{2}}}}\frac{1}{q_{T}^{2}}\,.

(42)

The second term in the r.h.s. of Eq. (
41
)
is originated by final-state radiation and its interference with the initial state, and can be written as:

\biggl(\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(q_{T},z)}{dq_{T}^{2}}\biggl)_{F.S.R.-int}=\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{(0)}z^{2}}{(1-z)^{2}}\frac{(-2\hat{p}_{qq}(z)\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{Q^{2}}(1-z)-4\frac{q_{T}^{4}}{Q^{4}}z)}{\sqrt{(1-z)^{2}-4z\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{Q^{2}}}}\frac{1}{q_{T}^{2}}\,.

(43)

The functions
\hat{p}_{qq}
are the Altarelli-Parisi unregularised splitting kernels, i.e.

\hat{p}_{qq}(z)=\frac{1+z^{2}}{1-z}\,.

(44)

We underline that the initial-state radiation contribution in Eq. (
42
) is obtainable from a direct abelianization of the analogous QCD process (see Eq. (2.11) of Ref.
Cieri:2019tfv
) at NLO (i.e.
q\bar{q}\rightarrow Wg
). Explicitly, the abelianization procedure consists in the replacement (as explained in Sec.
3
):

C_{F}\rightarrow\frac{e_{q_{f}}^{2}+e_{\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}}}^{2}}{2}\,.

(45)

Nonetheless, the term due to final-state radiation, i.e. Eq. (
43

W
boson is electrically charge but colourless.

Having said this, we perform the integration over
q_{T}
, at a fixed value of
z
, obtaining

\hat{\sigma}^{>(1)}(z)=\frac{1}{C_{F}}\,\biggl(\frac{e_{q_{f}}^{2}+e_{\bar{q}_{f^{\prime}}}^{2}}{2}\biggl)\,\hat{\sigma}_{QCD}^{>(1)}(z)+\hat{\sigma}_{F.S.R.-int}^{>(1)}(z)\,,

(46)

with

\hat{\sigma}_{QCD}^{>(1)}(z)=\hat{\sigma}^{(0)}C_{F}z\biggl\{-2(1-z)\sqrt{1-\frac{4az}{(1-z)^{2}}}

+2\hat{p}_{qq}(z)\biggl[-\log\frac{az}{(1-z)^{2}}+2\log\frac{1}{2}\biggl(\sqrt{1-\frac{4az}{(1-z)^{2}}}+1\biggl)\biggl]\biggl\}\,,

(47)

and

(48)

(49)

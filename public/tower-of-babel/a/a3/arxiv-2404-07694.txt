A martingale approach to Gaussian fluctuations and laws of iterated logarithm for Ewens-Pitman model
Bernard Bercu, Stefano Favaro

Abstract
The Ewens-Pitman model refers to a distribution for random partitions of [ n ] = { 1 , … , n } [n]=\{1,\ldots,n\} , which is indexed by a pair of parameters α ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in[0,1) and θ > − α \theta>-\alpha , with α = 0 \alpha=0 corresponding to the Ewens model in population genetics. The large n n asymptotic properties of the Ewens-Pitman model have been the subject of numerous studies, with the focus being on the number K n K_{n} of partition sets and the number K r , n K_{r,n} of partition subsets of size r r , for r = 1 , … , n r=1,\ldots,n . While for α = 0 \alpha=0 asymptotic results have been obtained in terms of almost-sure convergence and Gaussian fluctuations, for α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) only almost-sure convergences are available, with the proof for K r , n K_{r,n} being given only as a sketch. In this paper, we make use of martingales to develop a unified and comprehensive treatment of the large n n asymptotic behaviours of K n K_{n} and K r , n K_{r,n} for α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) , providing alternative, and rigorous, proofs of the almost-sure convergences of K n K_{n} and K r , n K_{r,n} , and covering the gap of Gaussian fluctuations. We also obtain new laws of the iterated logarithm for K n K_{n} and K r , n K_{r,n} .



Keywords:
Almost-sure limit; Ewens-Pitman model; exchangeable random partition; Gaussian fluctuation; law of iterated logarithm; martingale; Mittag-Leffler distribution

1
Introduction

The Ewens-Pitman (EP) model refers to a distribution for random partitions, introduced by
Pitman 1995
as a generalization of the celebrated Ewens model in population genetics
(
Ewens 1972
)
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider a random partition of
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
into
K_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
partition subsets
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{K_{n}}\}

of corresponding sizes
\mathbf{N}_{n}=(N_{1,n},\ldots,N_{K_{n},n})
, where
N_{k,n}
is the cardinal number of set
A_{k}
, for
k=1,\ldots,K_{n}
, such that

n=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{n}}N_{k,n}.

For any real numbers
\alpha
in
[0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
, the EP model assigns to the random vector
(K_{n},\mathbf{N}_{n})
the probability

\mathds{P}(K_{n}=k,\mathbf{N}_{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}))=\frac{n!}{k!}\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(k)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(n_{i}-1)}}{n_{i}!}

(1)

where, for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
(a)^{(n)}
stands for the rising factorial of
a
of order
n
, that is
(a)^{(n)}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)
. Denote by
K_{r,n}
the number of partition subsets of size
r
, given for all
r=1,\ldots,n
, by

K_{r,n}=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{n}}\mathrm{I}_{\{N_{k,n}=r\}}

where
\mathrm{I}
is the indicator function. One can easily see that

n=\sum_{r=1}^{n}rK_{r,n}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274ptK_{n}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}K_{r,n}.

The distribution of
\mathbf{K}_{n}=(K_{1,n},\ldots,K_{n,n})
is known as EP sampling formula or frequency-of-frequencies distribution, and it follows by means of a combinatorial rearrangement of (
1
). The Ewens model is recovered from (
1
) by setting
\alpha=0
. We refer the reader to
Pitman 1995
;
Pitman 2006
for more details. The joint distribution (
1
) admits several constructions, with the most common being a sequential or generative construction through the Chinese restaurant process
(
Pitman 1995
;
Feng and Hoppe 1998
;
Zabell 2005
)
and a Poisson process construction through random sampling from the Pitman-Yor random probability measure
(
Perman et al. 1992
;
Pitman and Yor 1997
)
; see also
Dolera and Favaro 2021
for a construction through a class of negative Binomial compound Poisson sampling models
(
Charalambides 2007
)
. The EP model plays a critical role in a variety of research fields, such as population genetics, Bayesian nonparametric statistics, excursion theory, combinatorics, machine learning and statistical physics. We refer to the monograph by
Pitman 2006
for a comprehensive account on the EP model and generalizations thereof.

Under the EP model, there have been several studies on the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of
K_{n}
and the
K_{r,n}
’s; see
Pitman 2006
. In particular, for
\alpha=0
,
Korwar and Hollander 1973
exploited the fact that
K_{n}
is a sum of
n
independent Bernoulli random variables, and showed that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{n}}{\log n}=\theta\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(2)

Furthermore, it follows directly from Lindeberg-Lévy central limit theorem that

\frac{K_{n}-\theta\log n}{\sqrt{\log n}}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{\theta}\,\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(3)

with
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
being a standard Gaussian random variable. See
Feng and Hoppe 1998
and references therein for some functional versions of both (
2
) and (
3
). As regard to the
K_{r,n}
’s, from
Arratia et al. 1992
it follows that

K_{r,n}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}Z_{r}

(4)

and

(K_{1,n},K_{2,n},\ldots)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}(Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots),

(5)

where the
Z_{r}
’s are independent Poisson random variables with
\mathds{E}[Z_{r}]=\theta/r
, for all
r\geq 1
. We refer to
Arratia et al. 2003
for various generalizations and refinements of these asymptotic results, and their interplay with combinatorics.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
K_{n}
is no longer a sum of independent Bernoulli random variables. However,
Pitman 2006
made use of a martingale construction for
K_{n}
, based on a likelihood ratio defined through the distribution (
1
), and applied the strong law of large numbers for martingales to prove that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}=S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.},

(6)

where
S_{\alpha,\theta}
is a positive and almost surely finite random variable. If
f_{\alpha}
is the positive
\alpha
-Stable density function, then the distribution of
S_{\alpha,\theta}
has density function

f_{S_{\alpha,\theta}}(s)=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\alpha\Gamma(\theta/\alpha+1)}s^{\frac{\theta-1}{\alpha}-1}f_{\alpha}(s^{-1/\alpha}),

(7)

where
\Gamma
stands for the Euler Gamma function. Precisely, (
7
) is a generalization of the Mittag-Leffler density function, which is recovered by setting
\theta=0

(
Zolotarev 1986
)
. As regard to the
K_{r,n}
’s, from
Pitman 2006
it follows that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}=p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.},

(8)

where

p_{\alpha}(r)=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-1)}}{r!}.

The almost-sure convergences (
6
) and (
8
) are the natural counterparts of (
2
) and (
4
), respectively, though at different scales. Instead, it is still an open problem of obtaining a counterpart of (
3
), as well as Gaussian fluctuations for
K_{r,n}
.

1.1
Our contributions

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
, we make use of martingales to develop a unified and comprehensive treatment of the large
n
asymptotic behaviours of
K_{n}
and
K_{r,n}
, providing alternative, and rigorous, proofs of the almost-sure limits (
6
) and (
8
), and covering the gap of Gaussian fluctuations. In particular, we propose a new martingale construction for
K_{n}
, simpler than that applied in
Pitman 2006
, which leads to an alternative proof of (
6
), still relying on the strong law of large numbers for martingales. Further, by exploiting our martingale construction, as well as (
6
), we show that

\sqrt{n^{\alpha}}\left(\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}-S_{\alpha,\theta}\right)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{S_{\alpha,\theta}^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(9)

where
S^{\prime}_{\alpha,\theta}
is random variable independent of
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and sharing the same distribution as
S_{\alpha,\theta}
. We also prove the law of the iterated logarithm for
K_{n}
, i.e.,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\big(K_{n}-n^{\alpha}S_{\alpha,\theta}\big)^{2}}{2n^{\alpha}\log\log n}=S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(10)

Then, we extend our analysis to the
K_{r,n}
’s, for which only a sketch of the proof of (
8
) is given in
Pitman 2006
. In particular, we introduce a martingale construction for
K_{r,n}
, providing a rigorous proof of (
8
) by means of the strong law of large numbers for martingales. Further, by exploiting our martingale construction and (
8
), we establish a Gaussian fluctuation as well as the law of iterated logarithm for
K_{r,n}
. Critical for our study is the work of
Heyde 1977
, which provides sufficient conditions to achieve Gaussian fluctuations and laws of iterated logarithm from the martingale convergence theorem.

1.2
Organization of the paper

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we present the martingale construction for
K_{n}
and the proofs of the almost-sure convergence, as well as the

and
K_{r,n}
.

p\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[(K_{n})_{(p)}]\leq C(p,\alpha,\theta)b_{n}(p).

(35)

Moreover, we also have the elementary inequality

\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{p}]\leq p^{p}+p!\mathbb{E}[(K_{n})_{(p)}\mathrm{I}_{K_{n}\geq p}].

(36)

Consequently, we find from (
18
), (
35
) and (
36
)
that it exists a positive constant
D(p,\alpha,\theta)
such that for all
n\geq 1
and
p\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[M_{n}^{p}]\leq D(p,\alpha,\theta)b_{n}(p)b_{n}^{p}.

(37)

Furthermore, it is easy to see from (
17
) and (
30
) that

b_{n}(p)b_{n}^{p}=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{\alpha p}{k+\theta}\right)\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{k+\theta}\right)^{\!\!-p}\leq 1.

(38)

Hence, we deduce from (
37
) and (
38
) that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}[M_{n}^{p}]<+\infty,

which means that the martingale
(M_{n})
is bounded in
\mathbb{L}^{p}
for any
p\geq 1
. Therefore, it follows from Doob’s martingale convergence theorem
(
Hall and Heyde 1980
, Corollary 2.2)
, the sequence
(M_{n})
converges almost surely and in

\mathbb{L}^{p}
to a finite random variable
M_{\alpha,\theta}
. Our goal is now to compute all the moments of
M_{\alpha,\theta}
. We shall only carry out the proof for
\theta\neq 0
inasmuch as the proof for
\theta=0
follows exactly the same arguments.
First of all, as in (
26
),

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{(\alpha p+\theta)^{(n)}}{n^{\alpha p}(\theta)^{(n)}}=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\theta\Gamma(\alpha p+\theta)}.

Hence, we obtain once again from (
33
) that for any integer
p\geq 1
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha p}}\mathbb{E}[(K_{n})_{(p)}]=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\theta\Gamma(\alpha p+\theta)}\Big(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\Big)^{\!(p)\!}.

(39)

However, we have for all
p\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{p}]=\sum_{k=0}^{p}\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
k\end{matrix}\right\}\mathbb{E}[(K_{n})_{(k)}],

(40)

where the curly brackets are the Stirling numbers of the second kind given by

\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
k\end{matrix}\right\}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-i}{k\choose i}i^{p}.

Since,

\Big\{\begin{matrix}p\\
p\end{matrix}\Big\}=1
, we obtain from (
39
) and (
40
) that for all
p\geq 1
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha p}}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{p}]=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\theta\Gamma(\alpha p+\theta)}\Big(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\Big)^{\!(p)\!}.

(41)

We also recall from (
17
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{\alpha}b_{n}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{n^{\alpha}(\theta+1)_{(n)}}{(\alpha+\theta+1)_{(n)}}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}.

(42)

Consequently, it follows from the conjunction of (
18
), (
41
) and (
42
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}[M_{n}^{p}]=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}b_{n}^{\,p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(K_{n}+\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\Big)^{p}\Big]=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}b_{n}^{\,p}\sum_{k=0}^{p}{p\choose k}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{k}]\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{p-k},

which drastically reduces to

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}[M_{n}^{p}]=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}b_{n}^{\,p}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{p}]\!=\!\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\right)^{p}\!\!\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\theta\Gamma(\alpha p+\theta)}\!\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(p)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!.

(43)

It immediately leads, for all
p\geq 1
, to

\mathbb{E}[M_{\alpha,\theta}^{p}]=\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\right)^{p}\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\theta\Gamma(\alpha p+\theta)}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(p)},

as well as

\mathbb{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{p}]=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\theta\Gamma(\alpha p+\theta)}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(p)}.

The distribution of the random variable
S_{\alpha,\theta}
has
probability density function given by (
7
).
Finally, (
21
)
follows from (
20
) and (
41
) together with Riesz-Scheffé theorem,
which completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

An alternative proof of the convergence in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
given by (
21
), which follows directly from the Poisson process construction of the EP model
(
Pitman 2003
, Proposition 9)
can be found in Appendix B. See also
Pitman 2006
.

2.2
The Gaussian fluctuation

Our martingale approach for
K_{n}
suggests that, in addition to the almost-sure convergence, it is possible to establish a Gaussian fluctuations of
K_{n}
, properly normalized, around its almost-sure limit
S_{\alpha,\theta}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
K_{n}
be the number of partition sets in a random partition of
[n]
distributed according to the EP model with
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
. Then,

\frac{K_{n}-n^{\alpha}S_{\alpha,\theta}}{\sqrt{K_{n}}}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(44)

where
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
denotes a standard Gaussian random variable. Moreover, we also
have

\sqrt{n^{\alpha}}\left(\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}-S_{\alpha,\theta}\right)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{S_{\alpha,\theta}^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(45)

where
S_{\alpha,\theta}^{\prime}
is a random variable independent of
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and sharing the same distribution as
S_{\alpha,\theta}
.

Proof.

The proof relies on a beautiful result due to
Heyde 1977
concerning with Gaussian fluctuations for martingales.
For all
n\geq 1
, denote
\Delta M_{n+1}=M_{n+1}-M_{n}
and

\Lambda_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{k+1}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big].

We immediately have from (
19
) that

\Lambda_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}b_{k+1}^{2}\left(\frac{(\theta+\alpha K_{k})(k-\alpha K_{k})}{(k+\theta)^{2}}\right).

(46)

On the one hand, we know from (
20
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}=S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(47)

On the other hand, we already saw from (
42
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{\alpha}b_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}.

(48)

Consequently, we deduce from (
46
), (
47
) and (
48
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{\alpha}\Lambda_{n}=\Big(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\Big)^{2}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(49)

By the same token, if

s_{n}^{2}=\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n}]=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{k+1}^{2}\big],

we also have

s_{n}^{2}=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}b_{k+1}^{2}\left(\frac{\theta k+\alpha(k-\theta)\mathbb{E}[K_{k}]-\alpha^{2}\mathbb{E}[K_{k}^{2}]}{(k+\theta)^{2}}\right).

However, we already saw from (
41
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}]=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\alpha\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}

(50)

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{2}]=\frac{(\alpha+\theta)\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\alpha^{2}\Gamma(2\alpha+\theta)}.

(51)

It ensures that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{\alpha}s_{n}^{2}=\Big(\frac{\alpha+\theta}{\alpha}\Big)\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}.

Hereafter, we claim that for any
\eta>0
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{\alpha}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{k+1}^{2}\mathrm{I}_{\{|\Delta M_{k+1}|>\eta\sqrt{n^{-\alpha}}\}}\big]=0.

(52)

As a matter of fact, we clearly have for any
\eta>0
,

n^{\alpha}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{k+1}^{2}\mathrm{I}_{\{|\Delta M_{k+1}|>\eta\sqrt{n^{-\alpha}}\}}\big]\leq\frac{n^{2\alpha}}{\eta^{2}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{k+1}^{4}\big].

Moreover, it follows from tedious but straightforward calculations that for all
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{n+1}^{4}|\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]=b_{n+1}^{4}\sum_{p=0}^{4}e_{n}(p)K_{n}^{p}

(53)

where

\displaystyle e_{n}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{n\theta}{(n+\theta)^{4}}(n^{2}-n\theta+\theta^{2}),

\displaystyle e_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha(n-\theta)}{(n+\theta)^{4}}(n^{2}-4n\theta+\theta^{2}),

\displaystyle e_{n}(2)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\alpha^{2}(2n-\theta)(n-2\theta)}{(n+\theta)^{4}},

\displaystyle e_{n}(3)

\displaystyle=\frac{6\alpha^{3}(n-\theta)}{(n+\theta)^{4}},

\displaystyle e_{n}(4)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3\alpha^{4}}{n^{4}}.

Taking the expectation on both sides of (
53
), we obtain that for all
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{n+1}^{4}\big]=b_{n+1}^{4}\sum_{p=0}^{4}e_{n}(p)\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{p}]

(54)

Furthermore, we already saw from (
41
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{3\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{3}]=\frac{(\alpha+\theta)(2\alpha+\theta)\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\alpha^{3}\Gamma(3\alpha+\theta)}

(55)

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{4\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{4}]=\frac{(\alpha+\theta)(2\alpha+\theta)(3\alpha+\theta)\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\alpha^{4}\Gamma(4\alpha+\theta)}.

(56)

Hence, we deduce from (
48
) and (
54
) together with (
50
), (
51
), (
55
) and (
56
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{1+3\alpha}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{n+1}^{4}\big]=(\alpha+\theta)\Big(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\Big)^{3}.

(57)

Consequently, we obtain from (
57
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{3\alpha}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{k+1}^{4}\big]=(\alpha+\theta)\Big(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\Big)^{3}

which clearly leads to (
52
). Finally, let
(P_{n})
be the martingale defined by

P_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\alpha}\big(\Delta M_{k+1}^{2}-\mathbb{E}[\Delta M_{k+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}]\big).

(58)

Its predictable quadratic variation is given by

\langle P\rangle_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{2\alpha}\big(\mathbb{E}[\Delta M_{k+1}^{4}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}]-(\mathbb{E}[\Delta M_{k+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}])^{2}\big).

Therefore, we find from (
53
) that

\langle P\rangle_{n+1}\leq\sum_{p=0}^{4}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{2\alpha}b_{k+1}^{4}e_{k}(p)K_{k}^{p}.

One can observe that we always have
e_{n}(4)<0
. For
n
large enough,
e_{n}(2)<0
.
Furthermore, it exists some constant
C>0
such that for
n
large enough,
e_{n}(0)\leq C/n
,

e_{n}(1)\leq C/n
and
e_{n}(3)\leq C/n^{3}
. Consequently, we get that for
n
large enough,

\langle P\rangle_{n}\leq C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k^{2\alpha}b_{k}^{4}}{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k^{2\alpha}b_{k}^{4}K_{k}}{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k^{2\alpha}b_{k}^{4}K_{k}^{3}}{k^{3}}\right).

(59)

Hence, we obtain from (
47
), (
48
) and (
59
) that
\langle P\rangle_{n}
converges
a.s. to a finite random variable. Then, we deduce from the strong law of large numbers
for martingales given by the first part of
(
Duflo 1997
, Theorem 1.3.15)
that
(P_{n})

converges a.s. to a finite random variable.
All the conditions of the second part of Theorem 1 and Corollaries 1 and 2 in
Heyde 1977
are satisfied, which leads to

\frac{M_{n}-M_{\alpha,\theta}}{\sqrt{\Lambda_{n}}}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1)

(60)

as well as to

\sqrt{n^{\alpha}}(M_{n}-M_{\alpha,\theta})\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\sqrt{S_{\alpha,\theta}^{\prime}}\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(61)

where
M_{\alpha,\theta}
is the almost-sure limit of the martingale
(M_{n})
and

S_{\alpha,\theta}^{\prime}
is independent of the Gaussian
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
random variable
and
S_{\alpha,\theta}^{\prime}
shares the same distribution as
S_{\alpha,\theta}
.
However, the random variables
M_{\alpha,\theta}
and
S_{\alpha,\theta}
are tightly related through

M_{\alpha,\theta}=\Big(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\Big)S_{\alpha,\theta}.

In addition, we obtain from the asymptotic behavior of the ratio of two Gamma functions that

b_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)n^{\alpha}}\left(1-\frac{\alpha(2\theta+\alpha-1)}{2n}+O\Big(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\right).

(62)

Finally, the Gaussian fluctuations (
44
) and (
45
)
follow from (
60
) and (
61
) together with the almost-sure convergences
(
20
), (
49
), (
62
) and Slutsky’s lemma, which achieves the proof of Theorem
2.3
.
∎

2.3
The law of the iterated logarithm

We conclude our asymptotic analysis of
K_{n}
by establishing the law of iterated logarithm.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
K_{n}
be the number of partition sets in a random partition of
[n]
distributed according to the EP model with
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
. Then,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{K_{n}-n^{\alpha}S_{\alpha,\theta}}{\sqrt{2K_{n}\log\log n}}\right)=-\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{K_{n}-n^{\alpha}S_{\alpha,\theta}}{\sqrt{2K_{n}\log\log n}}\right)=1\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(63)

Moreover, we also have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{K_{n}-n^{\alpha}S_{\alpha,\theta}}{\sqrt{2n^{\alpha}\log\log n}}\right)=-\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{K_{n}-n^{\alpha}S_{\alpha,\theta}}{\sqrt{2n^{\alpha}\log\log n}}\right)=\sqrt{S_{\alpha,\theta}}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

In particular,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\big(K_{n}-n^{\alpha}S_{\alpha,\theta}\big)^{2}}{2n^{\alpha}\log\log n}=S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

Proof.

The proof is a direct application of
Heyde 1977
. Using the same notation as in the proof of Theorem
2.3
, we have for any
\eta>0
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\alpha/2}\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta M_{n}|\mathrm{I}_{\{|\Delta M_{n}|>\eta\sqrt{n^{-\alpha}}\}}\big]\leq\frac{1}{\eta^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{n}^{4}\big].

(64)

Hence, we deduce from (
57
) and (
64
) that it exists
some constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\alpha/2}\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta M_{n}|\mathrm{I}_{\{|\Delta M_{n}|>\eta\sqrt{n^{-\alpha}}\}}\big]\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1+\alpha}}<\infty.

Moreover, we also have for any
\delta>0
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{n}^{4}\mathrm{I}_{\{|\Delta M_{n}|\leq\delta\sqrt{n^{-\alpha}}\}}\big]\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{n}^{4}\big]<\infty.

Furthermore, we already saw that the martingale
(P_{n})
, defined by (
58
),
converges a.s. to a finite random variable. Consequently, all the conditions of the second part of Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 in
Heyde 1977
are satisfied, which ensures that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{M_{n}-M_{\alpha,\theta}}{\sqrt{2\Lambda_{n}\log\log n}}\right)=-\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{M_{n}-M_{\alpha,\theta}}{\sqrt{2\Lambda_{n}\log\log n}}\right)=1\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(65)

where
M_{\alpha,\theta}
is the almost-sure limit of the martingale
(M_{n})
.
Finally, the law of iterated logarithm (
63
)
follows from (
65
) together with the almost-sure convergences (
20
) and (
49
), which completes the proof of Theorem
2.4
.
∎

3
Asymptotic results for the partition subsets of size
r

We start by introducing the keystone martingale construction for
K_{r,n}
. In particular, our approach is different from that developed in Section
2
, since we are going to build a martingale that will not converge almost surely to a finite random variable. First, we consider the case
r=1
. From the sequential construction of the EP model
(
Pitman 1995
, Proposition 9)
, we have for all
n\geq 1
,

K_{1,n+1}=K_{1,n}+\xi_{1,n+1}

(66)

where the conditional distribution of the random variable
\xi_{1,n+1}
, given the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(K_{1},\ldots,K_{n},K_{1,1},\ldots,K_{1,n})
, is such that

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{1,n+1}=k\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}{p_{1,n}}&\text{ if }&k=1,\\[11.38092pt]
{q_{1,n}}&\text{ if }&k=-1,\\[11.38092pt]
{1-p_{1,n}-q_{1,n}}&\text{ if }&k=0,\end{array}\right.

(67)

where

p_{1,n}=\frac{\alpha K_{n}+\theta}{n+\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{and}\hskip 18.49988ptq_{1,n}=\frac{(1-\alpha)K_{1,n}}{n+\theta}.

(68)

We refer the reader to Appendix A for more details on (
67
). From definition of the sequence
(\xi_{1,n})
, we clearly have
\mathds{E}[\xi_{1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]=p_{1,n}-q_{1,n}
. Consequently, we obtain from (
66
) that

\mathds{E}[K_{1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\mathds{E}[K_{1,n}+\xi_{1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]=K_{1,n}+p_{1,n}-q_{1,n}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s},

which leads to

\mathds{E}[K_{1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\beta_{1,n}K_{1,n}+p_{1,n}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.},

(69)

where

\beta_{1,n}=1-\frac{1-\alpha}{n+\theta}=\frac{n-1+\theta+\alpha}{n+\theta}.

(70)

Let
(b_{1,n})
be the sequence defined by
b_{1,1}=1
and for all
n\geq 2
,

b_{1,n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\beta_{1,k}^{-1}=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{k+\theta}{k-1+\theta+\alpha}\right)=\frac{(\theta+1)^{(n-1)}}{(\alpha+\theta)^{(n-1)}}.

(71)

Now, we introduce the sequence of random variables
(M_{1,n})
that is defined, for all
n\geq 1
, by

M_{1,n}=b_{1,n}K_{1,n}-A_{1,n},

(72)

where

A_{1,n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{1,k+1}\left(\frac{\alpha K_{k}+\theta}{k+\theta}\right).

(73)

One can observe that
A_{1,n+1}=A_{1,n}+p_{1,n}b_{1,n+1}
and
b_{1,n}=\beta_{1,n}b_{1,n+1}
.
Hence, we have from (
69
) and (
70
) that for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[M_{1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=b_{1,n+1}\mathds{E}[K_{1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]-A_{1,n+1}

\displaystyle=b_{1,n+1}(\beta_{1,n}K_{1,n}+p_{1,n})-A_{1,n}-p_{1,n}b_{1,n+1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{1,n}b_{1,n+1}K_{1,n}-A_{1,n}

\displaystyle=b_{1,n}K_{1,n}-A_{1,n}

\displaystyle=M_{1,n}

almost surely. Moreover,
(M_{1,n})
is square integrable as
K_{1,n}\leq K_{n}\leq n
. Consequently,
(M_{1,n})
is a locally square integrable martingale. Because of (
67
), it is not hard to compute all the higher order conditional moments of
(M_{1,n})
. For example, as

\Delta M_{1,n+1}=M_{1,n+1}-M_{1,n}=b_{1,n+1}(K_{1,n+1}-\mathds{E}[K_{1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}])

we clearly have from (
66
) and (
69
) that

\mathds{E}[(\Delta M_{1,n+1})^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]=b_{1,n+1}^{2}(\mathds{E}[K_{1,n+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]-(\mathds{E}[K_{1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}])^{2})

with

\mathds{E}[K_{1,n+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]=K_{1,n}^{2}+2K_{1,n}(p_{1,n}-q_{1,n})+p_{1,n}+q_{1,n}

and

(\mathds{E}[K_{1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}])^{2}=(K_{1,n}+p_{1,n}-q_{1,n})^{2}

almost surely, which reduces to

\mathds{E}[(\Delta M_{1,n+1})^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]=b_{1,n+1}^{2}\big(p_{1,n}+q_{1,n}-(p_{1,n}-q_{1,n})^{2}\big)\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

(74)

The above calculations extend easily to the case
r\geq 2
. From the sequential construction of the EP model
(
Pitman 1995
, Proposition 9)
, we have for all
n\geq 1
,

K_{r,n+1}=K_{r,n}+\xi_{r,n+1}

(75)

where the conditional distribution of the random variable
\xi_{r,n+1}
, given the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(K_{r-1,1},\ldots,K_{r-1,n},K_{r,1},\ldots,K_{r,n})
, is such that

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{r,n+1}=k\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}{p_{r,n}}&\text{ if }&k=1,\\[11.38092pt]
{q_{r,n}}&\text{ if }&k=-1,\\[11.38092pt]
{1-p_{r,n}-q_{r,n}}&\text{ if }&k=0,\end{array}\right.

(76)

where

p_{r,n}=\frac{(r-1-\alpha)K_{r-1,n}}{n+\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{and}\hskip 18.49988ptq_{r,n}=\frac{(r-\alpha)K_{r,n}}{n+\theta}.

(77)

We refer the reader to Appendix A for more details on (
76
). As before,
\mathds{E}[\xi_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]=p_{r,n}-q_{r,n}
, which implies that

\mathds{E}[K_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\beta_{r,n}K_{r,n}+p_{r,n}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.},

(78)

where

\beta_{r,n}=1-\frac{r-\alpha}{n+\theta}=\frac{n-r+\theta+\alpha}{n+\theta}.

(79)

Let
(b_{r,n})
be the sequence defined by
b_{r,1}=1
and for all
n\geq 2
,

b_{r,n}=\prod_{k=r}^{n-1}\beta_{r,k}^{-1}=\prod_{k=r}^{n-1}\left(\frac{k+\theta}{k-r+\theta+\alpha}\right)=\frac{(\theta+1)^{(n-1)}}{(\alpha+\theta)^{(n-r)}}.

(80)

Hereafter, we introduce the sequence of random variables
(M_{r,n})
that is defined, for all
n\geq 1
, by

M_{r,n}=b_{r,n}K_{r,n}-A_{r,n},

(81)

where

A_{r,n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{r,k+1}\left(\frac{r-1-\alpha}{k+\theta}\right)K_{r-1,k}.

(82)

Since

A_{r,n+1}=A_{r,n}+p_{r,n}b_{r,n+1}

and

b_{r,n}=\beta_{r,n}b_{r,n+1}
, we have from (
78
) and (
79
) that for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[M_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=b_{r,n+1}\mathds{E}[K_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}]-A_{r,n+1}

\displaystyle=b_{r,n+1}(\beta_{r,n}K_{r,n}+p_{r,n})-A_{r,n}-p_{r,n}b_{r,n+1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{r,n}b_{r,n+1}K_{r,n}-A_{r,n}

\displaystyle=b_{r,n}K_{r,n}-A_{r,n}

\displaystyle=M_{r,n}

almost surely. In addition,
(M_{r,n})
is square integrable as
K_{r,n}\leq K_{n}\leq n
. Consequently,
(M_{r,n})
is a locally square integrable martingale. Because of (
76
), it is not hard to compute all the higher order conditional moments of
(M_{r,n})
, as it was previously done for
r=1
.

3.1
An alternative approach for the almost-sure convergence

Based on the above martingale construction, we present an alternative proof of (
8
), relying on the strong law of large numbers for martingale. Our proof is more rigorous than
Pitman 2006
, which contains only a sketch for the proof of (
8
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
K_{r,n}
be the number of partition subsets of size
r
in a random partition of
[n]
distributed according to the EP model with
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
. Then, for all
r\geq 1

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}=p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(83)

where

p_{\alpha}(r)=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-1)}}{r!}

and
S_{\alpha,\theta}
is a positive and almost surely finite random variable whose
distribution has probability density function (
7
).
This convergence holds in
\mathbb{L}^{p}
for any
integer
p\geq 1
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}-p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}\right|^{p}\right]=0.

(84)

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
r\geq 1
. In particular, we start by considering the case
r=1
. We already saw that
(M_{1,n})
is a locally square integrable martingale. We deduce from (
74
) that its predictable quadratic variation is given by

\langle M_{1}\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{1,k+1}^{2}\big(p_{1,k}+q_{1,k}-(p_{1,k}-q_{1,k})^{2}\big).

Consequently,

\langle M_{1}\rangle_{n}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{1,k+1}^{2}\big(p_{1,k}+q_{1,k}\big).

On the one hand, it follows directly from the definition of
p_{1,n}
in (
68
) together with the almost-sure convergence (
20
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{1-\alpha}p_{1,n}=\alpha S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(85)

On the other hand, we have from (
71
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{b_{1,n}}{n^{1-\alpha}}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}.

(86)

Accordingly, as
(n+\theta)q_{1,n}\leq K_{n}
, we deduce from (
85
) and
(
86
)
through a direct application of Toeplitz lemma that

\langle M_{1}\rangle_{n}=O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{1-\alpha}\right)=O(n^{2-\alpha})\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

Therefore, it follows from the strong law of large numbers for martingales given by

(
Duflo 1997
, Theorem 1.3.24)
that

(M_{1,n})^{2}=O(n^{2-\alpha}\log n)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.},

which leads to

(b_{1,n}K_{1,n}-A_{1,n})^{2}=O(n^{2-\alpha}\log n)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

It clearly implies that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{b_{1,n}K_{1,n}-A_{1,n}}{n}=0\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(87)

Furthermore, by combining (
85
) and (
86
) together with (
73
), we find that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{A_{1,n}}{n}=\frac{\alpha\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(88)

Consequently, we obtain from (
87
) and (
88
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{b_{1,n}K_{1,n}}{n}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{A_{1,n}}{n}=\frac{\alpha\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

Then, we deduce from (
86
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{1,n}}{n^{\alpha}}=\alpha S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

This completes the proof for
r=1
. For
r\geq 2
, we shall proceed by induction, assuming that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{r-1,n}}{n^{\alpha}}=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-2)}}{(r-1)!}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(89)

We already saw that
(M_{r,n})
is a locally square integrable martingale. As in the case

r=1
, one can easily see that its predictable quadratic variation is given by

\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{r,k+1}^{2}\big(p_{r,k}+q_{r,k}-(p_{r,k}-q_{r,k})^{2}\big).

(90)

On the one hand, it follows from the definition of
p_{r,n}
in (
77
) together with the induction hypothesis (
89
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{1-\alpha}p_{r,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(r-1-\alpha)\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-2)}}{(r-1)!}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(91)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-1)}}{(r-1)!}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

On the other hand, we have from (
80
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{b_{r,n}}{n^{r-\alpha}}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}.

(92)

Accordingly, as
(n+\theta)q_{r,n}\leq rK_{n}
, we deduce
from (
90
), (
91
) and (
92
) together with Toeplitz lemma that

\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}=O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{2r-1-\alpha}\right)=O(n^{2r-\alpha})\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

Therefore, it follows from the strong law of large numbers for martingales that

(M_{r,n})^{2}=O(n^{2r-\alpha}\log n)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.},

which leads to

(b_{r,n}K_{r,n}-A_{r,n})^{2}=O(n^{2r-\alpha}\log n)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

As before, it implies that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{b_{r,n}K_{r,n}-A_{r,n}}{n^{r}}=0\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(93)

Hereafter, by combining (
91
) and (
92
) together with (
82
), we obtain that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{A_{r,n}}{n^{r}}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-1)}}{r!}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(94)

Finally, we find from (
93
) and (
94
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{b_{r,n}K_{r,n}}{n^{r}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{A_{r,n}}{n^{r}}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-1)}}{r!}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

which ensures via (
86
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-1)}}{r!}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

It only remains to prove the convergence in
\mathbb{L}^{p}
given by (
84
).
We have from
Favaro et al. 2013
that for any integer
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[(K_{r,n})_{(p)}]

\displaystyle=(p_{r}(\alpha))^{p}\frac{n!}{(n-rp)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-pr}\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(k+p)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\mathscr{C}(n-pr,k;\alpha)

(95)

\displaystyle=(p_{r}(\alpha))^{p}\frac{n!}{(n-rp)!}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(p)}\sum_{k=0}^{n-pr}\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}+p\right)^{(k)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\mathscr{C}(n-pr,k;\alpha)

where
\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)
is the generalized factorial coefficient
(
Charalambides 2007
, Chapter 2)
,

\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}(-j\alpha)^{(n)}.

An application of
(
Charalambides 2007
, Equation 2.46)
allows to solve the summation over
k
in (
95
). More precisely,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[(K_{r,n})_{(p)}]=(p_{r}(\alpha))^{p}\frac{n!}{(n-rp)!}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(p)}\frac{(\theta+\alpha p)^{(n-rp)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}.

Consequently,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha p}}\mathds{E}[(K_{r,n})_{(p)}]=\big(p_{r}(\alpha)\big)^{p}\Big(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\Big)^{\!(p)\!}\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\theta\Gamma(\alpha p+\theta)}\Big(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\Big)^{\!(p)\!}

(96)

However, as in identity (
40
), we have

\mathbb{E}[K_{r,n}^{p}]=\sum_{k=0}^{p}\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
k\end{matrix}\right\}\mathbb{E}[(K_{r,n})_{(k)}].

(97)

Then, we deduce from (
96
) and (
97
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha p}}\mathbb{E}[K_{r,n}^{p}]=\big(p_{r}(\alpha)\big)^{p}\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\theta\Gamma(\alpha p+\theta)}\Big(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\Big)^{\!(p)\!}=\mathds{E}\big[\big(p_{r}(\alpha)S_{\alpha,\theta}\big)^{p}\big].

(98)

Finally, (
84
)
follows from (
83
) and (
98
) together with Riesz-Scheffé theorem,
which completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

∎

\mathbb{L}^{2}
given by (
3.1
), which follows directly from the Poisson process construction of the EP model
(
Pitman 2003

3.2
The Gaussian fluctuation

We shall now focus on a Gaussian fluctuation of
K_{r,n}
, properly normalized, around an estimator of its almost-sure limit
p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
K_{r,n}
be the number of partition subsets of size
r
in a random partition of
[n]
distributed according to the EP model with
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
. Then, for all
r\geq 1

\frac{1}{\sqrt{K_{r,n}}}\left(K_{r,n}-\frac{A_{r,n}}{b_{r,n}}\right)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(99)

where
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
denotes a standard Gaussian random variable. Moreover, we also
have for all
r\geq 1

\sqrt{n^{\alpha}}\left(\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{r,n}}{b_{r,n}n^{\alpha}}\right)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(100)

where

S_{\alpha,\theta}^{\prime}
is a random variable independent of
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and sharing the same distribution as
S_{\alpha,\theta}
.

Proof.

We are going to carry out the proof for any
r\geq 1
. It follows from (
83
),
(
90
), (
91
) and (
92
) together with Toeplitz lemma that the
predictable quadratic variation
\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}
satisfies

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}{n^{2r-\alpha}}=p_{\alpha}(r)\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\right)^{2}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

(101)

Moreover, we have from (
84
) with
p=1
and
p=2
that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{1-\alpha}\mathbb{E}[p_{r,n}]=(r-1-\alpha)p_{\alpha}(r-1)\mathbb{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}]

(102)

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{2-2\alpha}\mathbb{E}[p_{r,n}^{2}]=(r-1-\alpha)^{2}p^{2}_{\alpha}(r-1)\mathbb{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}].

(103)

In addition, we also have from (
84
) with
p=1
and
p=2
that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{1-\alpha}\mathbb{E}[q_{r,n}]=(r-\alpha)p_{\alpha}(r)\mathbb{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}]

(104)

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{2-2\alpha}\mathbb{E}[q_{r,n}^{2}]=(r-\alpha)^{2}p^{2}_{\alpha}(r)\mathbb{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}].

(105)

Therefore, denote
s_{r,n}^{2}=\mathbb{E}[\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}]
. We deduce from (
90
) and (
92
) together with (
102
), (
103
), (
104
) and (
105
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{s_{r,n}^{2}}{n^{2r-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2r-\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{r,k+1}^{2}\big(\mathbb{E}[p_{r,k}]+\mathbb{E}[q_{r,k}]\big),

(106)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p_{\alpha}(r)}{\alpha}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\right).

Hence, we obtain from (
101
) and (
106
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}{s_{r,n}^{2}}=\alpha\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\right)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

(107)

We are now going to show that for any
\eta>0
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2r-\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,k}^{2}\mathrm{I}_{\{|\Delta M_{r,k}|>\eta s_{r,n}\}}\big]=0.

(108)

We clearly have for any
\eta>0
,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,k}^{2}\mathrm{I}_{\{|\Delta M_{r,k}|>\eta s_{r,n}\}}\big]\leq\frac{1}{\eta^{2}s_{r,n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,k}^{4}\big].

According to (
106
), in order to prove (
108
),
it is only necessary to show that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{4r-2\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,k}^{4}\big]=0.

(109)

As in the proof of (
53
), it follows from tedious but straightforward calculations that for all
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,n+1}^{4}|\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]=b_{r,n+1}^{4}\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}e_{r,n}(\ell)

(110)

where

\displaystyle e_{r,n}(1)

\displaystyle=(p_{r,n}+q_{r,n}),

\displaystyle e_{r,n}(2)

\displaystyle=-4(p_{r,n}-q_{r,n})^{2},

\displaystyle e_{r,n}(3)

\displaystyle=6(p_{r,n}+q_{r,n})(p_{r,n}-q_{r,n})^{2},

\displaystyle e_{r,n}(4)

\displaystyle=-3(p_{r,n}-q_{r,n})^{4}.

Then, it follows from (
110
) that it exists some constant
C>0
such that for
n
large enough,

\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,n+1}^{4}|\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]\leq Cb_{r,n+1}^{4}\big(p_{r,n}+q_{r,n}\big).

(111)

Taking the expectation on both sides of (
111
), we obtain that for
n
large enough,

\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,n+1}^{4}\big]\leq Cb_{r,n+1}^{4}\big(\mathbb{E}[p_{r,n}]+\mathbb{E}[q_{r,n}]\big).

(112)

Consequently, we deduce from (
92
), (
102
), (
104
) and (
112
) that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,k}^{4}\big]=O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{4r-1-3\alpha}\right)=O(n^{4r-3\alpha})

which leads to (
109
). Hence, we find from Corollary 1 in
Heyde 1977
that

\frac{M_{r,n}}{\sqrt{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(113)

and

\frac{M_{r,n}}{s_{r,n}}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}}\,\sqrt{S_{\alpha,\theta}^{\prime}}\mathcal{N}(0,1)

(114)

where
S_{\alpha,\theta}^{\prime}
stands for a random variable independent of
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and sharing the same distribution as
S_{\alpha,\theta}
.
Finally, as
M_{r,n}=b_{r,n}K_{r,n}-A_{r,n}
, the Gaussian fluctuations (
99
) and
(
100
) follow from (
92
), (
113
) and (
114
)
together with the almost-sure convergences
(
83
), (
101
), (
107
) and Slutsky’s lemma,
which achieves the proof of Theorem
3.3
.
∎

3.3
The law of the iterated logarithm

We conclude our asymptotic analysis of
K_{r,n}
by establishing the law of the iterated logarithm.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
K_{r,n}
be the number of partition subsets of size
r
in a random partition of
[n]
distributed according to the EP model with
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
. Then, for all
r\geq 1

\displaystyle\hskip-15.49994pt\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\frac{n^{\alpha}}{2\log\log n}}\left(\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{r,n}}{b_{r,n}n^{\alpha}}\right)

(115)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\frac{n^{\alpha}}{2\log\log n}}\left(\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{r,n}}{b_{r,n}n^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

In particular,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{\alpha}}{2\log\log n}\left(\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{r,n}}{b_{r,n}n^{\alpha}}\right)^{2}=p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

Proof.

As in Section
2
, the proof is a direct application of
Heyde 1977
.
We have for any
\eta>0
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\alpha/2}}{n^{r}}\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta M_{r,n}|\mathrm{I}_{\{\sqrt{n^{\alpha}}|\Delta M_{r,n}|>\eta n^{r}\}}\big]\leq\frac{1}{\eta^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2\alpha}}{n^{4r}}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,n}^{4}\big].

(116)

Hence, we deduce from (
112
) and (
116
) together with (
92
), (
102
) and (
104
) that it exists
some constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\alpha/2}}{n^{r}}\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta M_{r,n}|\mathrm{I}_{\{\sqrt{n^{\alpha}}|\Delta M_{r,n}|>\eta n^{r}\}}\big]\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1+\alpha}}<\infty.

Moreover, we also have for any
\delta>0
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2\alpha}}{n^{4r}}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,n}^{4}\mathrm{I}_{\{\sqrt{n^{\alpha}}|\Delta M_{r,n}|\leq\delta n^{r}\}}\big]\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2\alpha}}{n^{4r}}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,n}^{4}\big]<\infty.

Hereafter, let
(P_{r,n})
be the martingale defined by

P_{r,n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k^{\alpha}}{k^{2r}}\big(\Delta M_{r,k+1}^{2}-\mathbb{E}[\Delta M_{r,k+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}]\big).

(117)

Its predictable quadratic variation is given by

\langle P_{r}\rangle_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k^{2\alpha}}{k^{4r}}\big(\mathbb{E}[\Delta M_{k+1}^{4}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}]-(\mathbb{E}[\Delta M_{k+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}])^{2}\big).

Hence, we obtain from (
111
) that it exists some constant
C>0
such that for
n
large enough,

\langle P_{r}\rangle_{n}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k^{2\alpha}}{k^{4r}}b_{r,k+1}^{4}\big(p_{r,k}+q_{r,k}\big).

(118)

Consequently, we deduce from (
90
), (
91
) and (
118
) that
\langle P_{r}\rangle_{n}
converges
a.s. to a finite random variable. Then, it follows from the strong law of large numbers
for martingales that
(P_{r,n})
converges a.s. to a finite random variable.
Therefore, all the conditions of the second part of Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 in
Heyde 1977
are satisfied, which ensures that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M_{r,n}}{\sqrt{2\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}\log\log n}}=-\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M_{r,n}}{\sqrt{2\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}\log\log n}}=1\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}

(119)

Finally, as
M_{r,n}=b_{r,n}K_{r,n}-A_{r,n}
, the law of iterated logarithm (
115
)
follows from (
92
) together with the almost-sure convergence (
101
) and (
119
), which completes the proof of Theorem
3.4
.
∎

4
Discussion

In this paper, we presented a unified and comprehensive treatment of the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of
K_{n}
and
K_{r,m}
in the EP model with
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
. By means of a novel martingale construction for
K_{n}
and
K_{r,m}
, we obtained alternative, and rigorous, proofs of the almost-sure convergence of
K_{n}
and
K_{r,n}
, and also covered the gap of Gaussian fluctuations. We argue that our martingale approach may be further investigated to refine Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
3.1
in terms of Berry–Esseen theorems, as well as sharp large deviations and concentration inequalities for
K_{n}
and
K_{r,n}
. We refer to
Feng and Hoppe 1998
,
Favaro et al. 2015
,
Favaro et al. 2018
,
Dolera and Favaro 2020
and
Oliveira et al. 2022
for early results along these lines of research. Investigating the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of functions that involve both
K_{n}
and the
K_{r,n}
’s is also a promising direction for future research. Our almost-sure limits imply

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{1,n}}{K_{n}}=\alpha\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.},

meaning that
K_{1,n}/K_{n}
is a consistent estimator for the parameter
\alpha
. Establishing a Gaussian fluctuation for
K_{1,n}/K_{n}
is an interesting open problem, with potential applications in the context of Bayesian nonparametric statistics
(
Balocchi et al. 2023
;
Favaro and Naulet 2023
)
. A further problem of interest is to establish an almost-sure limit and a Gaussian fluctuation for
(K_{1,n},K_{2,n},\ldots)
, thus providing a counterpart of (
5
).

Appendix A. Sequential or generative construction of the EP model

The purpose of this appendix is to recall the sequential construction of the EP model given in the seminal work of
Pitman 1995
. For any
\alpha\in[0,1)
,
\theta>-\alpha
, an exchangeable random partition of

[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
can be recursively constructed as follows:
conditionally on the total size
K_{n}=k
of the partition and on the partition subsets
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
of corresponding sizes
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
, the partition
[n+1]
is an extension of
[n]
such that the element
n+1
is attached to subset
A_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, with probability

\frac{n_{i}-\alpha}{n+\theta},

or forms a new subset with probability

\frac{\alpha k+\theta}{n+\theta}.

Since
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}
, we clearly have

\frac{1}{n+\theta}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(n_{i}-\alpha)+\frac{\alpha k+\theta}{n+\theta}=\frac{n-\alpha k+\alpha k+\theta}{n+\theta}=1.

Hereafter, for
K_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, denote by
\mathbf{N}_{n}=(N_{1,n},\ldots,N_{K_{n},n})
the sizes of the
partition subsets
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{K_{n}}\}
.

Pitman 1995
shows that the above construction
leads to the joint distribution (
1
) of the random vector

(K_{n},\mathbf{N}_{n})
. This is the reason with the above construction
is referred to as the sequential or generative construction of the EP model.
Equation (
11
) clearly follows from the
above construction since

\mathds{P}(K_{n+1}=K_{n}+1\,|\,K_{n})=\frac{\alpha K_{n}+\theta}{n+\theta}.

Equation (
67
) also follows from the
above construction as

\displaystyle\mathds{P}(\xi_{1,n+1}=1\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{\alpha K_{n}+\theta}{n+\theta},

while for
n_{1}=1
,

\mathds{P}(\xi_{1,n+1}=-1\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{(1-\alpha)K_{1,n}}{n+\theta}.

Moreover, we also deduce Equation (
76
) from the
above construction since for all
r\geq 2
and for
n_{i}=r-1
,

\mathds{P}(\xi_{r,n+1}=1\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{(r-1-\alpha)K_{r-1,n}}{n+\theta},

whereas for
n_{i}=r

\mathds{P}(\xi_{r,n+1}=-1\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{(r-\alpha)K_{r,n}}{n+\theta}.

Finally, it shows that Equations (
11
), (
67
) and (
76
) follow from the sequential or generative construction of the EP model.

Appendix B. Alternative proofs of the convergence in
\mathbb{L}^{2}

B.1
\mathbb{L}^{2}
convergence of the number of partition sets

We propose an alternative proof, without martingale, of the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
convergence of
K_{n}
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}-S_{\alpha,\theta}\right)^{2}\right]=0.

(B.1)

The proof follows from the Poisson process construction of the EP model
(
Pitman 2003
, Proposition 9)
. See also
Pitman 2006
. From
Pitman 2006
,

\mathds{P}(K_{n}=k)=\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(k)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha).

(B.2)

Denote
T_{\alpha,\theta}=S_{\alpha,\theta}^{-1/\alpha}
. By combining
Pitman 2003
with
Charalambides 2005
,

\mathds{P}(K_{n}=k\,|\,T_{\alpha,\theta}=t)=V_{n,k}(t)\frac{\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)}{\alpha^{k}},

(B.3)

where

V_{n,k}(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n-k\alpha)}\left(\frac{\alpha}{t^{\alpha}}\right)^{k}\int_{0}^{1}v^{n-1-k\alpha}\frac{f_{\alpha}((1-v)t)}{f_{\alpha}(t)}\mathrm{d}v

with
f_{\alpha}
being the positive
\alpha
-Stable density function. Then, it follows from (
7
) and (
B.3
), as well as the tower property of the conditional expectation, that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[K_{n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{E}[K_{n}S_{\alpha,\theta}|S_{\alpha,\theta}=s]f_{S_{\alpha,\theta}}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\Gamma\left(\theta/\alpha+1\right)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathds{E}[K_{n}|T_{\alpha,\theta}=t]t^{-\alpha-\theta}f_{\alpha}(t)\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\Gamma\left(\theta/\alpha+1\right)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathds{P}(K_{n}=k\,|\,T_{\alpha,\theta}=t)t^{-\alpha-\theta}f_{\alpha}(t)\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\Gamma\left(\theta/\alpha+1\right)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)}{\Gamma(n-k\alpha)}I_{n,k}(t),

(B.4)

where

\displaystyle I_{n,k}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{-k\alpha-\alpha-\theta}\int_{0}^{1}v^{n-1-k\alpha}f_{\alpha}((1-v)t)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}z^{-k\alpha-\alpha-\theta}\int_{0}^{1}v^{n-1-k\alpha}(1-v)^{k\alpha+\alpha+\theta-1}f_{\alpha}(z)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}z.

Since

\int_{0}^{+\infty}z^{-k\alpha-\alpha-\theta}f_{\alpha}(z)\mathrm{d}z=\frac{\Gamma\left(\theta/\alpha+k+2\right)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta+k\alpha+1)},

and

\int_{0}^{1}v^{n-k\alpha-1}(1-v)^{k\alpha+\alpha+\theta-1}\mathrm{d}v=\frac{\Gamma(n-k\alpha)\Gamma(k\alpha+\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+\theta)},

the expectation (
B.4
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[K_{n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\Gamma\left(\theta/\alpha+1\right)\Gamma(n+\alpha+\theta)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)}{(\alpha+\theta+k\alpha)}\Gamma\left(\theta/\alpha+k+2\right),

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+n)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+\theta)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}+k\right)\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(k)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha).

(B.5)

Hence, it follows from (
B.2
) and (
B.5
) that

\mathds{E}[K_{n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+n)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+\theta)}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\mathds{E}[K_{n}]+\mathds{E}[K^{2}_{n}]\right).

(B.6)

We are now in the position to prove (
B.1
). We get from (
41
)
with
p=1
and
p=2
that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}]=\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}]\hskip 22.76228pt\text{and}\hskip 22.76228pt\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{2}]=\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}],

(B.7)

leading to

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]=\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}].

(B.8)

Therefore, we deduce from (
B.7
) and (
B.8
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\left(\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}-S_{\alpha,\theta}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}^{2}]-\frac{2}{n^{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]+\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}],

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}]-2\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}]+\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}]=0.

Similar arguments can be applied in order to show the convergence in
\mathbb{L}^{p}
of
n^{-\alpha}K_{n}
to
S_{\alpha,\theta}
, for any integer
p\geq 1
, thus providing an alternative proof of (
21
).

B.2
\mathbb{L}^{2}
convergence of the number of partition subsets of size
r

We still rely on the Poisson process construction of the EP model
(
Pitman 2003
, Proposition 9)
to propose an alternative proof of the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
convergence of
K_{r,n}
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}-p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}\right)^{2}\right]=0.

(B.9)

Denote
T_{\alpha,\theta}=S_{\alpha,\theta}^{-1/\alpha}
. By combining
Pitman 2003
with
Favaro et al. 2013
,

\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}\,|\,T_{\alpha,\theta}=t]=p_{\alpha}(r)(n)_{(r)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}V_{n,k}(t)\frac{\mathscr{C}(n-r,k-1;\alpha)}{\alpha^{k}}

(B.10)

where

V_{n,k}(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n-k\alpha)}\left(\frac{\alpha}{t^{\alpha}}\right)^{k}\int_{0}^{1}v^{n-1-k\alpha}\frac{f_{\alpha}((1-v)t)}{f_{\alpha}(t)}\mathrm{d}v

with
f_{\alpha}
being the positive
\alpha
-Stable density function. Consequently, we can write that

\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\Gamma\left(\theta/\alpha+1\right)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}|T_{\alpha,\theta}=t]t^{-\alpha-\theta}f_{\alpha}(t)\mathrm{d}t,

thus obtaining, along lines similar to the computation of
\mathds{E}[K_{n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]
in
(
B.5
), that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\alpha\Gamma(n+\alpha+\theta)}p_{\alpha}(r)(n)_{(r)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}+1\right)^{(k)}\mathscr{C}(n-r,k-1;\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\alpha\Gamma(n+\alpha+\theta)}p_{\alpha}(r)(n)_{(r)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\theta/\alpha+2\right)}{\Gamma\left(\theta/\alpha+1\right)}\sum_{k=0}^{n-r}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}+2\right)^{(k)}\mathscr{C}(n-r,k;\alpha),

which leads to

\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]=\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+\theta)}p_{\alpha}(r)(n)_{(r)}\left(\frac{\alpha+\theta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)(\theta+2\alpha)^{(n-r)}.

(B.11)

We are now in position to prove (
B.9
). We find from (
98
)
with
p=2
that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{r,n}^{2}]=\big(p_{r}(\alpha)\big)^{2}\mathds{E}[\big(S_{\alpha,\theta}\big)^{2}].

(B.12)

In addition, we deduce from (
B.11
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]=p_{r}(\alpha)\left(\frac{\alpha+\theta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)\frac{\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\Gamma(2\alpha+\theta)}=p_{r}(\alpha)\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}].

(B.13)

Therefore, it follows from (
B.12
) and (
B.13
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\left(\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}-p_{r}(\alpha)S_{\alpha,\theta}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{r,n}^{2}]-\frac{2p_{r}(\alpha)}{n^{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}[K_{r,n}S_{\alpha,\theta}]+\big(p_{r}(\alpha)\big)^{2}\mathds{E}[\big(S_{\alpha,\theta}\big)^{2}],

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt=\big(p_{r}(\alpha)\big)^{2}\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}]-2\big(p_{r}(\alpha)\big)^{2}\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}]+\big(p_{r}(\alpha)\big)^{2}\mathds{E}[\big(S_{\alpha,\theta}\big)^{2}]=0.

Similar arguments can be applied to show the convergence in
\mathbb{L}^{p}
of
n^{-\alpha}K_{r,n}
to
p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}
, for any integer
p\geq 1
, thus leading to an alternative proof of (
84
).

The Nyström method for elastic wave scattering by unbounded rough surfaces
Jianliang Li, Xiaoli Liu, Bo Zhang, Haiwen Zhang

Abstract
We consider the numerical algorithm for the two-dimensional time-harmonic elastic wave scattering by unbounded rough surfaces with Dirichlet boundary condition. A Nyström method is proposed for the scattering problem based on the integral equation method. Convergence of the Nyström method is established with convergence rate depending on the smoothness of the rough surfaces. In doing so, a crucial role is played by analyzing the singularities of the kernels of the relevant boundary integral operators. Numerical experiments are presented to demonstrate the effectiveness of the method. Keywords: elastic wave scattering, unbounded rough surface, Nyström method.



1
Introduction

We consider the two-dimensional time-harmonic elastic scattering problem for unbounded rough surfaces
with Dirichlet boundary condition. This kind of problem has attracted a lot of attentions over the
last decade since it has a wide range of applications in diverse scientific areas such as seismology
and nondestructive testing.

The well-posedness of the direct scattering problems by rough surfaces has been extensively studied
in the past thirty years. For acoustic case, the authors in
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
23
]
first employed
integral equation methods to prove that the scattering problem by an infinite rough surface with
Dirichlet boundary condition is uniquely solvable.
These works have been extended to other boundary conditions, see
[
25
]
for the impedance case
and
[
22
]
for the penetrable case. By using variational formulation,
the unique solvability of the sound-soft rough surface scattering problem has been established
in
[
5
,
7
]
. For the case of elastic scattering by Dirichlet rough surfaces,
the uniqueness result was proved in
[
3
]
, while the existence result was given in
[
4
]

using the boundary integral equation method (see also
[
2
]
for a comprehensive discussion).
The authors in
[
12
]
studied the well-posedness of the elastic scattering problem by unbounded
rough surfaces via the variational approach.

The computational aspect of the scattering problem by bounded obstacles has been extensively studied
(see, e.g.,
[
14
,
21
]
for the finite elements method based on variational formulation
and
[
6
,
24
]
for the Nyström method based on boundary integral equations).
For the acoustic scattering by unbounded rough surfaces,
many numerical algorithms have been already developed.
The numerical approach using finite elements method, combined with the perfectly matched layer technique,
was presented in
[
8
]
.
By dealing with a class of integral equations on the real line, the Nyström method has been applied
to the acoustic scattering problem for the sound-soft rough surfaces
[
20
,
18
]
and for the
penetrable rough surfaces
[
17
]
. However, few works are available for the numerical solution
of elastic scattering by unbounded rough surfaces.

In this paper, we propose the Nyström method for two-dimensional time-harmonic elastic scattering
problem for unbounded rough surfaces with Dirichlet boundary condition.
Our method is based on the integral equation formulations given in
[
2
,
4
]
, which can be reduced
to a class of integral equations on the real line. A crucial role of our method is played by a thorough
analysis on the singularities of the kernels in the relevant integral equations, which involves the
Green tensor for Navier equation in the half-space. By spliting off the logarithmic singularity in
the related kernels and using the asymptotic behavior of the Bessel functions, we obtain the
convergence of the Nyström method with convergence rate depending on the smoothness of the
rough surfaces. Several numerical examples are presented to verify our theoretical results and
show the effectiveness of our method.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give a brief introduction on the
mathematical model of the scattering problem and present the existed well-posedness result using
the integral equation method. Section
3

is devoted to analyzing the singularities for the relevant kernels included in the integral impression of the solution.
In Section
4
, we establish the convergence of the Nyström method. Numerical experiments
are given to show the effectiveness of the proposed method in Section
5
.
Finally, we give a conclusion in Section
6
.

The well-posedness of the scattering problem

In this section, we present the existed results for the well-posedness of the two-dimensional elastic
wave scattering problem by unbounded rough surfaces.
Fisrt, we introduce some basic notations and function spaces used in this paper. For
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m},m=1,2
,

the set of all functions
whose derivatives up to order

{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with the norm

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}:=\max_{l=0,1,...,n}\max_{|\alpha|=l}\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}...\partial_{x_{m}}^{\alpha_{m}}\varphi\|_{\infty,{\mathbb{R}}^{m}},

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},....,\alpha_{m})

. We define

C_{0,\pi}^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

\displaystyle C_{0,\pi}^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):=\{a(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):a(s,t)=0\;\;{\rm for}\;|s-t|\geq\pi\},

\displaystyle BC_{p}^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):=\{a(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):\|a\|_{BC_{p}^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<\infty\}

with the norm

\displaystyle\|a\|_{BC_{p}^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}:=\sup_{j,k=0,...,n,j+k\leq n}\left\|\widetilde{w}_{p}(s,t)\frac{\partial^{j+k}a(s,t)}{\partial^{j}s\partial^{k}t}\right\|_{\infty},

where the weight
\widetilde{w}_{p}(s,t):=(1+|s-t|)^{p}
for some
p>1
, which are closed subspaces
of
BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Let
H^{1}(V)
and
H^{1/2}(\partial V)
be the standard Sobolev spaces for any
open set
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
provided the boundary of
V
is smooth enough. The notations
H^{1}_{\rm loc}(V)

and
H^{1/2}_{\rm loc}(V)
stand for the set of functions which are elements of
H^{1}(\mathcal{V})

and
H^{1/2}(\mathcal{V})
for any
\mathcal{V}\subset\subset V
, respectively. Here the notation
\mathcal{V}\subset\subset V
denotes that the closure of
\mathcal{V}
is a compact subset of
V
.

Throughout this paper, let
h
be a real number with
h<\inf_{x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}}f(x_{1})
, where
f
is the
function of the rough surface which will be introduced later. We define the half-plane
U_{h}
and
its boundary
\Gamma_{h}
as

\displaystyle U_{h}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{2}>h\}\quad{\rm and}\quad\Gamma_{h}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{2}=h\}.

For
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in U_{h}
,
y^{\prime}
is defined as

\displaystyle y^{\prime}:=(y_{1},2h-y_{2}).

The notations
J_{n}
and
Y_{n}
are Bessel functions and Neumann functions of order
n
, respectively.
The linear combination

\displaystyle H_{n}^{(1)}:=J_{n}+{\rm i}Y_{n}

is known as the Hankel function of the first kind of order
n
.

Physical configuration of the scattering problem by a rough surface

As shown in Figure
1
, the rough surface
\Gamma
is described as

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{2}=f(x_{1})\right\},

where
f\in B^{(n)}_{c,M}
with

\displaystyle B^{(n)}_{c,M}:=\{f\in BC^{n+2}({\mathbb{R}}):\|f\|_{BC^{n+2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq M\;\;{\rm and}\;\;\inf_{x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}}f(x_{1})\geq c\}

for some nonnegative integer
n
, some constants

and
M>0
, which implies that the
surface
\Gamma
lies above the
x_{1}
-axis.
The whole space is separated by
\Gamma
into two unbounded half-spaces and the upper one is denoted by

\displaystyle\Omega:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{2}>f(x_{1})\}.

Suppose an incoming field
\bm{u}^{\rm inc}=(u_{1}^{\rm inc},u_{2}^{\rm inc})

is incident on the infinite surface
\Gamma
from the upper region
\Omega
. Then the scattering of

\bm{u}^{\rm inc}
by the infinite rough surface
\Gamma
can be modeled by the two-dimensional
Navier equation

\mu\Delta{\bm{u}}^{\rm sc}+(\lambda+\mu)\nabla\nabla\cdot{\bm{u}}^{\rm sc}+\omega^{2}{\bm{u}}^{\rm sc}=0\quad{\rm in}\;\;\Omega,

(2.1)

where
\bm{u}^{\rm sc}=(u_{1}^{\rm sc},u_{2}^{\rm sc})
is the scattered field,
\omega>0
represents the angular frequency, and
\lambda
and
\mu
are the Lamé constants satisfying
\mu>0
and
\lambda+2\mu>0
, which leads to that the second order partial differential operator
\Delta^{*}:=\mu\Delta+(\lambda+\mu)\nabla\nabla\cdot
is strongly elliptic
[
19
]
. Let
\bm{u}:=\bm{u}^{\rm inc}+\bm{u}^{\rm sc}
denote the total field consisting of the incident field and the scattered field. Further, the Dirichlet boundary condition is imposed on
\Gamma
, that is,

\bm{u}^{\rm sc}=-\bm{u}^{\rm inc}\quad{\rm on}\;\;\Gamma.

(2.2)

Since the Navier equation (
2.1
) is imposed in the unbounded region
\Omega
, an appropriate radiation condition is needed for the considered scattering problem. In this paper, the scattered wave is assumed to satisfy the following upwards propagating radiation condition (UPRC)
[
4
]
:

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}^{\rm sc}(x)=\int_{\Gamma_{H}}{\bm{\Pi}}_{D,H}^{(2)}(x,y)\bm{\phi}(y)ds(y),\quad x\in U_{H},

(2.3)

for some
\bm{\phi}\in\left[L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{H})\right]^{2}
with
H>\sup\limits_{x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}}f(x_{1})
,
and the kernel
\bm{\Pi}_{D,H}^{(2)}(x,y)=\left(\Pi_{D,H,jk}^{(2)}(x,y)\right)_{j,k=1,2}
in (
2.3
)
is a matrix function with the elements given by

\Pi_{D,H,jk}^{(2)}(x,y)=\left(\bm{P}^{(y)}\left(\bm{G}_{D,H,j\cdot}(x,y)\right)^{\top}\right)_{k},

(2.4)

where
\bm{P}
is the generalized stress vector defined on a curve
\Lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with
\nu
being the unit normal on
\Lambda
, that is,

\displaystyle\bm{P}\bm{\varphi}:=(\mu+\widetilde{\mu})\frac{\partial\bm{\varphi}}{\partial\nu}+\widetilde{\lambda}\nu{\rm div}\bm{\varphi}-\widetilde{\mu}\nu^{\perp}{\rm div}^{\perp}\bm{\varphi}

(2.5)

with
\widetilde{\lambda}
,
\widetilde{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\widetilde{\lambda}+\widetilde{\mu}=\lambda+\mu
,
\nu^{\perp}=(\nu_{2},-\nu_{1})^{\top}
for
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})^{\top}
, and
{\rm div}^{\perp}\bm{\varphi}:=\frac{\partial\varphi_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}-\frac{\partial\varphi_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}
for
\bm{\varphi}=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})^{\top}
.
In (
2.4
),
\nu
is the unit normal on
\Gamma_{H}
pointing to the half-plane
U_{H}
and
\bm{G}_{D,H}

is the Green’s tensor of the Navier equation (
2.1
) in
U_{H}
with
Dirichlet boundary condition on
\Gamma_{H}
, which is given by

\bm{G}_{D,H}(x,y):=\bm{G}(x,y)-\bm{G}(x,y^{\prime})+\bm{U}(x,y),\quad x,y\in U_{H}\quad{\rm and}\quad x\neq y,

(2.6)

where
\bm{G}
is the Green’s tensor for the Navier equation (
2.1
) in free space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
which is defined by

\displaystyle\bm{G}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\mu}\Phi(x,y,\kappa_{\rm s})\bm{I}+\frac{1}{\omega^{2}}\nabla_{x}\nabla_{x}^{\top}\left(\Phi(x,y,\kappa_{\rm s})-\Phi(x,y,\kappa_{\rm p})\right),\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\quad{\rm and}\quad x\neq y,

(2.7)

with
\kappa_{\rm s}
and
\kappa_{\rm p}
being the shear and compressional wavenumbers defined by

\displaystyle\kappa_{\rm s}:=c_{\rm s}\omega,\quad\kappa_{\rm p}:=c_{\rm p}\omega\quad\text{with}\quad c_{\rm s}=\mu^{-1/2},\quad c_{\rm p}:=(\lambda+2\mu)^{-1/2},

and

\displaystyle\Phi(x,y,\kappa):=\frac{\rm i}{4}H_{0}^{(1)}(\kappa|x-y|)

being the fundamental solution for the two-dimensional Helmholtz equation, and
\bm{U}(x,y)
in (
2.6
) is a matrix function defined by

\displaystyle\bm{U}(x,y)=-\frac{\rm i}{2\pi\omega^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\bm{M}_{\rm p}(\tau,\gamma_{\rm p},\gamma_{\rm s};x_{2},y_{2})+\bm{M}_{\rm s}(\tau,\gamma_{\rm p},\gamma_{\rm s};x_{2},y_{2})\right]e^{-{\rm i}(x_{1}-y_{1})\tau}d\tau

with

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm p}:=\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}-\tau^{2}},\quad\gamma_{\rm s}:=\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm s}^{2}-\tau^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\bm{M}_{\rm p}(\tau,\gamma_{\rm p},\gamma_{\rm s};x_{2},y_{2}):=\frac{e^{{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm p}(x_{2}+y_{2}-2h)-e^{{\rm i}(\gamma_{\rm p}(x_{2}-h)+\gamma_{\rm s}(y_{2}-h))}}}{\gamma_{\rm p}\gamma_{\rm s}+\tau^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-\tau^{2}\gamma_{\rm s}&\tau^{3}\\
\tau\gamma_{\rm p}\gamma_{\rm s}&-\tau^{2}\gamma_{\rm p}\\
\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle\bm{M}_{\rm s}(\tau,\gamma_{\rm p},\gamma_{\rm s};x_{2},y_{2}):=\frac{e^{{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm s}(x_{2}+y_{2}-2h)-e^{{\rm i}(\gamma_{\rm s}(x_{2}-h)+\gamma_{\rm p}(y_{2}-h))}}}{\gamma_{\rm p}\gamma_{\rm s}+\tau^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-\tau^{2}\gamma_{\rm s}&-\tau\gamma_{\rm p}\gamma_{\rm s}\\
-\tau^{3}&-\tau^{2}\gamma_{\rm p}\\
\end{array}\right].

With the aid of
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have
\bm{U}(x,y)\in\left[C^{\infty}(U_{H})\cap C^{1}(\overline{U_{H}})\right]^{2\times 2}
, which will be used in the analysis on the convergence of the Nyström method.
We refer to
[
2
,
3
]
for more properties of the UPRC, and its relation to the Rayleigh expansion radiation condition for diffraction grating and the Kupradze’s radiation condition for the scattering by bounded obstacles.

To ensure the uniqueness of the scattering problem, we need the following vertical growth rate condition

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}|x_{2}|^{\beta}|\bm{u}^{\rm sc}(x)|<\infty\quad{\rm for\;some\;\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}}.

(2.10)

In summary, the scattering problem (

(ii)

(iii)

\bm{u}^{\rm sc}

(iv)

The following uniqueness result has been proved in

for the problem (DP).

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
3
, Theorem 4.6]
)

The existence of the solution to the problem (DP) has been investigated in
[
4
]
by integral
equation method. The main idea is to seek for a solution in the form of a combined single- and
double-layer potential

{\bm{u}}^{\rm sc}(x)=\int_{\Gamma}\left[{\bm{\Pi}}_{D,h}^{(2)}(x,y)-{\rm i}\eta\bm{G}_{D,h}(x,y)\right]\bm{\psi}(y)ds(y),\quad x\in\Omega,

is defined similar as

in (

) with

being the unit normal on
\Gamma
pointing to
\Omega
,

\bm{\psi}\in\left[BC(\Gamma)\cap H^{1/2}_{\rm loc}(\Gamma)\right]^{2}

,

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}=\frac{\mu(\lambda+\mu)}{\lambda+3\mu},\quad\widetilde{\lambda}=\frac{(\lambda+\mu)(\lambda+2\mu)}{\lambda+3\mu}.

(2.12)

By doing so, it follows from Theorems 2.6 and 2.7, and Lemma 2.8 in

\bm{u}^{\rm sc}
given by (

\bm{u}^{\rm sc}\in\left[C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\right]^{2}

By Theorems 2.4 and 3.2 in
[
4
]
, it can be deduced that

given by (

) satisfies the Navier equation (

) and

According to the jump relations for elastic single- and double-layer potentials shown in
Theorems 2.6 and 2.7 in
[

]
, it is easy to see that
\bm{u}^{\rm sc}
given by (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bm{\psi}(x)+\int_{\Gamma}\left[\bm{\Pi}_{D,h}^{(2)}(x,y)-{\rm i}\eta\bm{G}_{D,h}(x,y)\right]\bm{\psi}(y)ds(y)=\bm{g}(x),\quad x\in\Gamma,

\displaystyle(\bm{I}+\bm{D}_{\Gamma}-{\rm i}\eta\bm{S}_{\Gamma})\bm{\psi}=2\bm{g}\quad{\rm on}\;\;\Gamma,

(2.14)

where
\bm{D}_{\Gamma}
and
\bm{S}_{\Gamma}
are the elastic double-layer and single-layer operators given by

\displaystyle(\bm{D}_{\Gamma}\bm{\psi})(x):=2\int_{\Gamma}\bm{\Pi}_{D,h}^{(2)}(x,y)\bm{\psi}(y)ds(y),\quad x\in\Gamma,

For

(2.15)

\displaystyle(\bm{S}\widetilde{\bm{\psi}})(s):=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\bm{G}_{D,h}(x(s),y(t))\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}(t)\sqrt{1+f^{\prime}(t)^{2}}dt,\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Then the solvability of (

to the integral equation

\displaystyle(\bm{I}+\bm{D}-{\rm i}\eta\bm{S})\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}=2\widetilde{\bm{g}}

(

Remark 2.3

By Theorems

3
The singularity for the kernel of
\bm{D}-{\rm i}\eta\bm{S}

This section is devoted to analyzing the singularity of the boundary integral equation (
2.17
),
which will be used for the further investigation on the Nyström method in Section
4
.
The main idea is to write the kernel
\bm{A}(s,t)
of the integral operator

\bm{D}-{\rm i}\eta\bm{S}
in (
2.17
) in the following form

\displaystyle\bm{A}(s,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\bm{B}(s,t)\ln\left(4\sin^{2}\frac{s-t}{2}\right)+\bm{C}(s,t),\quad s,t\in{\mathbb{R}},s\neq t,

(3.1)

with smooth matrix functions
\bm{B}(s,t)
and
\bm{C}(s,t)
(see the formulas (
3.24
) and (
3.25
) below). For the details on the smoothness of
\bm{B}(s,t)
and
\bm{C}(s,t)
,
see Remark
4.2
.

According to (
2.4
), (
2.6
), (
2.15
), and (
2.16
), the operators
\bm{D}
and
\bm{S}
can be decomposed into two parts as follows

\displaystyle\bm{D}=\bm{D}_{1}-\bm{D}_{2}\quad{\rm and}\quad\bm{S}=\bm{S}_{1}-\bm{S}_{2},

where

\displaystyle(\bm{D}_{1}\widetilde{\bm{\psi})}(s):=2\int_{\Gamma}\bm{\Pi}_{1}^{(2)}(x(s),y(t))\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}(t)\sqrt{1+f^{\prime}(t)^{2}}dt,\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\bm{D}_{2}\widetilde{\bm{\psi})}(s):=2\int_{\Gamma}\bm{\Pi}_{2}^{(2)}(x(s),y(t))\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}(t)\sqrt{1+f^{\prime}(t)^{2}}dt,\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\bm{S}_{1}\widetilde{\bm{\psi})}(s):=2\int_{\Gamma}\bm{G}(x(s),y(t))\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}(t)\sqrt{1+f^{\prime}(t)^{2}}dt,\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

(3.4)

\displaystyle(\bm{S}_{2}\widetilde{\bm{\psi})}(s):=2\int_{\Gamma}\left[\bm{G}(x(s),y^{\prime}(t))-\bm{U}(x(s),y(t))\right]\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}(t)\sqrt{1+f^{\prime}(t)^{2}}dt,\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

(3.5)

with the components of
\bm{\Pi}_{1}^{(2)}(x,y)
and
\bm{\Pi}_{2}^{(2)}(x,y)
given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{1,jk}^{(2)}(x,y):=\left(\bm{P}^{(y)}\left(\bm{G}_{j\cdot}(x,y)\right)^{\top}\right)_{k}\quad{\rm and}\quad\Pi_{2,jk}^{(2)}(x,y):=\left(\bm{P}^{(y)}\left(\bm{G}_{j\cdot}(x,y^{\prime})-\bm{U}_{j\cdot}(x,y)\right)^{\top}\right)_{k}.

(3.6)

Hence, the integral equation (
2.17
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left[\bm{I}+\bm{D}_{1}-{\rm i}\eta\bm{S}_{1}-(\bm{D}_{2}-{\rm i}\eta\bm{S}_{2})\right]\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}=2\widetilde{\bm{g}}\quad{\rm on}\;\;{\mathbb{R}}.

The remaining part of this section consists of three subsections, which focus on the singularity
analysis of the kernels in the integral operators
\bm{S}_{1}
,
\bm{D}_{1}
, and

\bm{D}_{2}-{\rm i}\eta\bm{S}_{2}
, respectively.

3.1
Separating the logarithmic part of
\bm{S}_{1}

This subsection is devoted to separating the logarithmic part of the operator
\bm{S}_{1}

We first introduce some notations which will be used later. For
x(s),~y(t)\in\Gamma
,
we define the distance between

\displaystyle r=r(s,t):=|x(s)-y(t)|=\sqrt{(s-t)^{2}+(f(s)-f(t))^{2}},

and define the upward unit normal at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\ln\left(4\sin^{2}\frac{s-t}{2}\right)\bm{B}(s,t)\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}(t)dt

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}R_{j}^{(N)}(s)\bm{B}(s,t_{j})\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}(t_{j}),\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\bm{C}(s,t)\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}(t)dt

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\bm{C}(s,t_{j})\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}(t_{j}),\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

with the quadrature weights given by

\displaystyle R_{j}^{(N)}(s):=-\frac{1}{N}\left[\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{m}\cos m(s-t_{j})+\frac{1}{2N}\cos N(s-t_{j})\right].

Therefore, an approximated form of (
4.1
) is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}_{N}(s)+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{j}^{(N)}(s)\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}_{N}(t_{j})=2\widetilde{\bm{g}}(s),\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

(4.2)

with

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}^{(N)}(s):=R_{j}^{(N)}(s)\bm{B}(s,t_{j})+\frac{\pi}{N}\bm{C}(s,t_{j}).

The remaining part of this section is to study the convergence result for
\|\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}-\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}_{N}\|_{[L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})]^{2}}
, which is presented in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f\in B_{c,M}^{(n)}
and
\widetilde{\bm{g}}\in[BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}})]^{2}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
c,M>0
. There exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that (
4.2
) admits a uniquely determined numerical solution
\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}_{N}
and

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}-\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}_{N}\|_{[L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})]^{2}}\lesssim N^{-n}\|\widetilde{\bm{g}}\|_{[BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}})]^{2}}

for
N>N_{0}
, where
\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}
is the unique solution of (
2.17
).

Proof.

According to Theorem
2.2
, the integral equation (
2.17
) has exactly one solution
\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}\in[BC({\mathbb{R}})]^{2}
for every
\widetilde{\bm{g}}\in[BC({\mathbb{R}})]^{2}
and there exists
C_{0}>0
such that
\|(\bm{I}+\bm{D}-{\rm i}\eta\bm{S})^{-1}\|\leq C_{0}
.
Then by
[
20
, Theorem 3.13]
, the statement of this theorem holds if
\bm{B}(s,t)\in C_{0,\pi}^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
\bm{C}(s,t)\in BC_{p}^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for some
p>1
. With the help of
[
20
, Theorem 2.1]
, it is equivalent to showing the following three conditions: for all
j,l\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
j+l\leq n
, there exists constants
C>0
and
p>1
such that

\displaystyle{\bm{C}1}.\;\;\left|\frac{\partial^{j+l}\bm{B^{*}}(s,t)}{\partial s^{j}\partial t^{l}}\right|\leq C,\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad{s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}},\;\;|s-t|\leq\pi,

\displaystyle{\bm{C}2}.\;\;\left|\frac{\partial^{j+l}\bm{C^{*}}(s,t)}{\partial s^{j}\partial t^{l}}\right|\leq C,\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad{s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}},\;\;|s-t|\leq\pi,

\displaystyle{\bm{C}3}.\;\;\left|\frac{\partial^{j+l}\bm{A}(s,t)}{\partial s^{j}\partial t^{l}}\right|\leq C(1+|s-t|)^{-p},\qquad\;{s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}},\;\;|s-t|\geq\pi,

where
\bm{B^{*}}(s,t)
and
\bm{C^{*}}(s,t)
are defined by (
3.22
) and (
3.23
), respectively. Thus, it suffices to show that
{\bm{C}1}
–
{\bm{C}3}
hold.

For the condition
{\bm{C}1}
, we recall that
\bm{B^{*}}(s,t)=-{\rm i}\eta\bm{B}^{(1)}(s,t)+\bm{B}^{(2)}(s,t)
, where
\bm{B}^{(1)}(s,t)
and
\bm{B}^{(2)}(s,t)
are defined by (
3.8
) and (
3.16
), respectively. By the asymptotic formulas (
3.11
) and the fact that
J_{n}(r)
is analytic for all
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we obtain that
J_{n}(r)/r^{n}
is also analytic for all
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
f\in B_{c,M}^{(n)}
, it follows from (
3.8
) and (
3.16
) that
\bm{B}^{(1)}(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
\bm{B}^{(2)}(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, which implies that
{\bm{C}1}
holds.

For the condition
{\bm{C}2}
, we recall that
\bm{C^{*}}(s,t)=-{\rm i}\eta\bm{C}^{(1)}(s,t)+\bm{C}^{(2)}(s,t)-\bm{A}^{(3)}(s,t)
, where
\bm{C}^{(1)}(s,t)
,
\bm{C}^{(2)}(s,t)
,
and
\bm{A}^{(3)}(s,t)
are defined by (
3.9
), (
3.16
), and (
3.20
), respectively.
To prove this condition, we first introduce the following notations

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(\kappa,s,t):=H_{n}^{(1)}(\kappa r)-\frac{2{\rm i}}{\pi}J_{n}(\kappa r)\ln|s-t|,\quad\gamma_{1}(s,t):=\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}\rho_{1}(\kappa_{\rm s},s,t)-\kappa_{\rm p}\rho_{1}(\kappa_{\rm p},s,t)}{r},

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(s,t):=\kappa_{\rm s}^{2}\rho_{2}(\kappa_{\rm s},s,t)-\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}\rho_{2}(\kappa_{\rm p},s,t),\qquad\;\;\gamma_{3}(s,t):=r\left[\kappa_{\rm s}^{3}\rho_{3}(\kappa_{\rm s},s,t)-\kappa_{\rm p}^{3}\rho_{3}(\kappa_{\rm p},s,t)\right],

\displaystyle\xi(s,t):=\frac{(s-t)f^{\prime}(t)+f(t)-f(s)}{r^{2}},

and for
j,k=1,2
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{jk}(s,t):=\frac{(x_{j}(s)-y_{j}(t))(x_{k}(s)-y_{k}(t))}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle\sigma_{jk}(s,t):=-(\mu+\widetilde{\mu})\frac{\rm i}{2\omega^{2}}\frac{\gamma_{2}(s,t)}{r^{2}}\left[(x_{j}(s)-y_{j}(t))l_{k}(t)+(x_{k}(s)-y_{k}(t))l_{j}(t)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\widetilde{\lambda}\left[\frac{\rm i}{2\mu}\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}}{r}\rho_{1}(\kappa_{\rm s},s,t)-\frac{2\rm i}{\omega^{2}}\frac{\gamma_{2}(s,t)}{r^{2}}+\frac{\rm i}{2\omega^{2}}\frac{\gamma_{3}(s,t)}{r^{2}}\right](x_{j}(s)-y_{j}(t))l_{k}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-\widetilde{\mu}\frac{\rm i}{2\mu}\frac{\kappa_{s}}{r}\rho_{1}(\kappa_{\rm s},s,t)\left[(f(s)-f(t))\delta_{j1}-(s-t)\delta_{j2}\right]l^{\perp}_{k}(t).

Using these notations and (
3.23
), (
3.9
), and (
3.17
), we can rewrite the elements of
\bm{C^{*}}(s,t)
as

\displaystyle C^{*}_{jk}(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\rm i}\eta\left\{\left[\frac{\rm i}{2\mu}\rho_{0}(\kappa_{\rm s},s,t)-\frac{\rm i}{2\omega^{2}}\gamma_{1}(s,t)\right]\delta_{jk}+\frac{\rm i}{2\omega^{2}}\gamma_{2}(s,t)\zeta_{jk}(s,t)\right\}\sqrt{1+f^{\prime}(t)^{2}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle+(\mu+\widetilde{\mu})\left\{\left[-\frac{\rm i}{2\mu}\kappa_{\rm s}r\rho_{1}(\kappa_{\rm s},s,t)+\frac{\rm i}{2\omega^{2}}\gamma_{2}(s,t)\right]\delta_{jk}-\frac{\rm i}{2\omega^{2}}\gamma_{3}(s,t)\zeta_{jk}(s,t)\right\}\xi(s,t)

\displaystyle+\sigma_{jk}(s,t)-A_{jk}^{(3)}(s,t),\quad\quad j,k=1,2.

With the help of
H_{n}^{(1)}(z)=J_{n}(z)+{\rm i}Y_{n}(z)
and the ascending series expansions of the Bessel functions (see
[
6
, (3.97) and (3.98)]
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(\kappa,s,t)=\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa r}{2|s-t|}\right)J_{0}(\kappa r)-\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\sum_{p=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{(p!)^{2}}\left(\frac{\kappa r}{2}\right)^{2p}\phi(p),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\rm s}r\rho_{1}(\kappa_{\rm s},s,t)=\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}r}{2|s-t|}\right)\kappa_{\rm s}rJ_{1}(\kappa_{\rm s}r)-\frac{2{\rm i}}{\pi}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\sum_{p=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!(p+1)!}\left(\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}r}{2}\right)^{2+2p}\left(\phi(p+1)+\phi(p)\right),

(4.5)

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(s,t)=\kappa_{\rm s}\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}r}{2|s-t|}\right)\frac{J_{1}(\kappa_{\rm s}r)}{r}-\kappa_{\rm p}\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm p}r}{2|s-t|}\right)\frac{J_{1}(\kappa_{\rm p}r)}{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{\rm i}{2\pi}\sum_{p=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!(p+1)!}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{2p}(\kappa_{\rm s}^{2+2p}-\kappa_{\rm p}^{2+2p})(\phi(p)+\phi(p+1)),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(s,t)=\kappa_{\rm s}^{2}\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}r}{2|s-t|}\right)J_{2}(\kappa_{\rm s}r)-\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm p}r}{2|s-t|}\right)J_{2}(\kappa_{\rm p}r)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{\rm i}{\pi}(\kappa_{\rm s}^{2}-\kappa_{\rm p}^{2})-\frac{\rm i}{\pi}\sum_{p=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!(p+2)!}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{2+2p}(\kappa_{\rm s}^{4+2p}-\kappa_{\rm p}^{4+2p})(\phi(p)+\phi(p+2)),

(4.7)

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}(s,t)=\kappa_{\rm s}^{3}r\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}r}{2|s-t|}\right)J_{3}(\kappa_{\rm s}r)-\kappa_{\rm p}^{3}r\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm p}r}{2|s-t|}\right)J_{3}(\kappa_{\rm p}r)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}(\kappa_{\rm s}^{2}-\kappa_{\rm p}^{2})-\frac{{\rm i}r}{4\pi}(\kappa_{\rm s}^{4}-\kappa_{\rm p}^{4})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{{\rm i}r}{\pi}\sum_{p=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!(p+3)!}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{3+2p}(\kappa_{\rm s}^{6+2p}-\kappa_{\rm p}^{6+2p})(\phi(p)+\phi(p+3)),

(4.8)

and for
j,k=1,2
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{jk}(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\lambda+\mu}{\lambda+3\mu}\xi(s,t)\delta_{jk}+\frac{\rm i}{2\mu}\left\{\widetilde{\lambda}(x_{j}(s)-y_{j}(t))l_{k}(t)-\widetilde{\mu}\left[(f(s)-f(t))\delta_{j1}-(s-t)\delta_{j2}\right]l^{\perp}_{k}(t)\right\}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{\kappa_{\rm s}\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}r}{2|s-t|}\right)\frac{J_{1}(\kappa_{\rm s}r)}{r}-\frac{{\rm i}\kappa_{\rm s}^{2}}{2\pi}\sum_{p=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!(p+1)!}\left(\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}r}{2}\right)^{2p}(\phi(p)+\phi(p+1))\Bigg\}

\displaystyle-\frac{\rm i}{2\omega^{2}}\left\{(\mu+\widetilde{\mu})\left[(x_{j}(s)-y_{j}(t))l_{k}(t)+(x_{k}(s)-y_{k}(t))l_{j}(t)\right]+4\widetilde{\lambda}(x_{j}(s)-y_{j}(t))l_{k}(t)\right\}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{\kappa_{\rm s}^{2}\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}r}{2|s-t|}\right)\frac{J_{2}(\kappa_{\rm s}r)}{r^{2}}-\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm p}r}{2|s-t|}\right)\frac{J_{2}(\kappa_{\rm p}r)}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\rm i}{4\pi}\sum_{p=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!(p+2)!}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{2p}(\kappa_{\rm s}^{4+2p}-\kappa_{\rm p}^{4+2p})(\phi(p)+\phi(p+2))\Bigg\}+\frac{{\rm i}\widetilde{\lambda}}{2\omega^{2}}\frac{(x_{j}(s)-y_{j}(t))l_{k}(t)}{r}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{\kappa_{\rm s}^{3}\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm s}r}{2|s-t|}\right)J_{3}(\kappa_{\rm s}r)-\kappa_{\rm p}^{3}\left(1+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}C_{E}+\frac{2\rm i}{\pi}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\rm p}r}{2|s-t|}\right)J_{3}(\kappa_{\rm p}r)

\displaystyle-\frac{\rm i}{4\pi}(\kappa_{\rm s}^{4}-\kappa_{\rm p}^{4})-\frac{\rm i}{\pi}\sum_{p=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!(p+3)!}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{3+2p}(\kappa_{\rm s}^{6+2p}-\kappa_{\rm p}^{6+2p})(\phi(p)+\phi(p+3))\Bigg\},

where
\phi(0):=0
and
\phi(p):=\sum_{m=1}^{p}\frac{1}{m}
for
p=1,2,3,...
. By a straightforward
calculation and
[
1
, Section 7.1.3]
, we have
\frac{r}{|s-t|}\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

\sqrt{1+f^{\prime}(t)^{2}}\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}})
,
\zeta_{jk}(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
\xi(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.
Thus, using (
4.4
)–(
4.8
), we conclude that
\rho_{0}(\kappa,s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

\kappa_{\rm s}r\rho_{1}(\kappa_{\rm s},s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
\gamma_{1}(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

\gamma_{2}(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, and
\gamma_{3}(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. By a careful observation
from (
4.9
), we have
\sigma_{jk}(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.
Since
\bm{A}^{(3)}(s,t)=\bm{A}^{(4)}(s,t)-\bm{A}^{(5)}(s,t)
, it follows from the smoothness
of
\bm{G}(x,y^{\prime})
and
\bm{U}(x,y)
for
x,y\in U_{h}
that
\bm{A}^{(3)}(s,t)
is smooth. These,
together with (
4.3
), imply that
C^{*}_{jk}(s,t)\in BC^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Thus, the condition
{\bm{C}2}
holds.

For the condition
{\bm{C}3}
, it can be seen in
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
that the elements of
the Green’s tensor
\bm{G}_{D,h}
satisfies

\displaystyle\max_{j,k=1,2}\left|G_{D,h,jk}(x,y)\right|\lesssim\frac{1+(x_{2}-h)(y_{2}-h)}{|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{3/2}}

for
x,y\in U_{h}
and
|x_{1}-y_{1}|\geq\varepsilon>0
.
This, together with the regularity estimates for solutions to elliptic partial differential equations
(see
[
13
, Theorem 3.9]
), implies that such estimates actually hold for partial derivatives of
\bm{G}_{D,h}(x,y)
of any order. Note that
\bm{A}(s,t)
is related to
\bm{G}_{D,h}(x,y)
and its
derivatives, thus the condition
{\bm{C}3}
holds with
p=3/2
and
C>0
only depends on
M
.
The proof is thus complete.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

From the proof of Theorem
4.1
, it easily follows that
\bm{B}(s,t)\in C_{0,\pi}^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

\bm{C}(s,t)\in BC_{p}^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with

if

Conclusion

In this paper, we present a Nyström method for the two-dimensional time-harmonic elastic scattering by unbounded rough surfaces with Dirichlet boundary condition. With the aid of the ascending series expansions of the Bessel functions, we analyze the singularities of the relevant integral kernels. Based on this, we obtain the superalgebraic convergence rate of the Nyström method depending on the smoothness of the rough surfaces.
Several numerical examples demonstrate that the numerical solution converges when the number of quadrature points
N
on the rough surface increases.
A possible continuation is to extend the present work to the case of impedance boundary condition
or the case of penetrable interface, which will be our future work.

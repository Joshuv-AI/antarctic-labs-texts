The Ranking-Selberg integral on 𝐆𝐒𝐩 ⁡ ( 2 ) {\bf GSp}(2) for square free levels
Seiji Kuga and Masao Tsuzuki

Abstract
. We explicitly compute the Rankin-Selberg type integral introduced by Piatetski-Shapiro over adeles for vector-valued Siegel cusp forms of square-free levels Γ 0 ​ ( N ) \Gamma_{0}(N) . On the way, for particular test functions in the Bessel models of irreducible admissible representations, exact evaluations of the local zeta-integrals are given.

Introduction

Lately, the Bessel periods on the symplectic similitude group
\mathsf{G}:={\bf GSp}_{2}
of rank
2
progressively gain its importance in the arithmetic of Siegel modular forms. The notion of Bessel period and the associated local model of admissible representations of
\mathsf{G}
were originally introduced by Novodvorski and Piatetski-Shaprio in their work (
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
,
[

[
2
]
) of the spinor
L

[

[
24

in a style of the refined Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture. This version of Böcherer’s conjecture has been completely solved by Furusawa and Morimoto (
[

[

In this paper, we compute the Rankin-Selberg type integral a la Piatetski-Shapiro for holomorphic vector-valued Siegel cusp forms of square-free levels explicitly, using results by Pitale-Schmidt
[

on local new vectors on

[

[

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[{\bf Sp}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}):\Gamma_{0}(N)]}\sum_{\varphi\in{\mathscr{B}}(l,N)}\langle E(s,\Lambda,\mu)\mid\varphi\rangle\,B^{\Lambda}(\varphi),

of discriminant

(see §

2.4.
Haar measures

For locally compact unimodular topological groups
H
relevant to us, we fix Haar measures
\eta_{H}
on
H
in the following manner. Let
{\mathbb{A}}
be the adele ring of
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\psi:{\mathbb{A}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
the basic character.

2.4.1.

On the additive group
{\mathbb{C}}
, define
{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\tau):={\rm{d}}x{\rm{d}}y=2^{-1}|{\rm{d}}\tau\wedge{\rm{d}}\bar{\tau}|
for
\tau=x+iy\,(x,y\in{\mathbb{R}})
with
{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}y
being the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

The Haar measure
\eta_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}}
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
(resp.
{\mathbb{R}}^{\times})
is defined by
{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}}(\tau)=|\tau|_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{-1}{\rm{d}}\tau
for
\tau=x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}
(resp .
|x|_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}{\rm{d}}x
). For each
p<\infty
,
E_{p}^{\times}
(resp.
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}
) is endowed with the Haar measure
\eta_{E_{p}^{\times}}
such that
\eta_{E_{p}^{\times}}(\mathfrak{o}_{E,p}^{\times})=1
(resp.
\eta_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times})=1
). Then, viewing
{\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}
as the restricted product of
E_{p}^{\times}
, we define
\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}}=\prod_{p\leqslant\infty}\eta_{E_{p}^{\times}}
. Similarly, we set
\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}}=\prod_{p\leqslant\infty}\eta_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}}
.

2.4.2.

For a matrix
X=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]
, set
X^{\dagger}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}d&-b\\
-c&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]
, so that
XX^{\dagger}=(\det X)\,1_{2}
, and
X\mapsto X^{\dagger}
induces an involution of the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space
{\mathsf{V}}({\mathbb{Q}})
, which is viewed as a quadratic space with the symmetric bilinear form
{\operatorname{tr}}(XY^{\dagger})
. We endow
{\mathsf{V}}({\mathbb{A}})
with the self-dual Haar measure with respect to the self-duality defined by the bi-character
(X,Y)\mapsto\psi({\operatorname{tr}}(XY^{\dagger}))
. Let
\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}}
(resp.
\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}_{E}}
) be the self-dual Haar measure on
{\mathbb{A}}
(resp.
{\mathbb{A}}_{E}
) with respect to the bi-character
(x,y)\mapsto\psi(xy)
(resp.
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\psi({\operatorname{tr}}_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\alpha\bar{\beta}))
). A computation shows the relation
-{\operatorname{tr}}(X_{\alpha}X_{\beta}^{\dagger})={\operatorname{tr}}_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\alpha\bar{\beta})
for
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{A}}_{E}
, where
X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}\in V^{T_{\theta}}({\mathbb{A}})
are defined by (
2.7
). Thus, by the isomorphism
{\mathbb{A}}_{E}\ni\beta\mapsto X_{\beta}\in{\mathsf{V}}^{T_{\theta}}({\mathbb{A}})
, the Haar measure
\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}_{E}}
is transferred to a Haar measure on
\mathsf{N}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}})
. Since
{\operatorname{tr}}(T_{\theta}T_{\theta}^{\dagger})=-D/2\not=0
, we have the orthogonal direct sum decomposition
{\mathsf{V}}({\mathbb{Q}})={\mathbb{Q}}T_{\theta}^{\dagger}\oplus{\mathsf{V}}^{T_{\theta}}({\mathbb{Q}})
. By this and (
2.12
), write
X\in V({\mathbb{A}})
as
X=xT_{\theta}^{\dagger}+X_{\beta}=yT_{\theta}+X_{\alpha}

(x,y\in{\mathbb{A}},\,\beta,\alpha\in{\mathbb{A}}_{E})
. By definition of the Haar measures, we have
{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathsf{V}}({\mathbb{A}})}(X={\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}}(x)\otimes{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}_{E}}(\beta)
. Since the change of variables
(x,\beta)\rightarrow(y,\alpha)
is given as

y=\tfrac{-D}{2}\,x,\qquad\alpha=\beta+2^{-1}{\operatorname{tr}}_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\theta)(x+y)+({\operatorname{N}}_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\theta)x-y)\,\theta

and since
|\tfrac{-D}{2}|_{\mathbb{A}}=1
, we get
{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathsf{V}}({\mathbb{A}})}(X)={\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}}(y)\otimes{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}_{E}}(\alpha)
. Through the identification
{\mathsf{V}}({\mathbb{A}})\ni X\mapsto\mathsf{n}(X)\in\mathsf{N}({\mathbb{A}})
and
\beta\ni{\mathbb{A}}_{E}\mapsto\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\beta\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathsf{N}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}})
, the groups
\mathsf{N}({\mathbb{A}})
and
\mathsf{N}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}})
acquire Haar measures. Thus, we have the integration formula:

(2.18)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{N}({\mathbb{A}})}f(n)\,{\rm{d}}n=\int_{{\mathbb{A}}}\int_{\mathsf{N}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}})}f(\mathsf{n}(xT_{\theta})\,\iota_{\theta}(n^{\#}))\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}n^{\#}

for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathsf{N}({\mathbb{A}}))
. Note that
{\operatorname{vol}}(\mathsf{N}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\mathsf{N}({\mathbb{A}}))={\operatorname{vol}}(\mathsf{N}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\mathsf{N}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}}))=1
.

2.4.3.

Let
p\leqslant\infty
. Any element
g^{\#}\in\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
is written as

(2.19)

\displaystyle g^{\#}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\beta\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\tau&0\\
0&\bar{\tau}a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\,k^{\#}

with

,

,
\beta\in E_{p}
and

. Then, our Haar measure on

is symbolically defined as

(2.20)

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}\eta_{\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}(g_{p}^{\#})=|a|_{p}^{2}\times{\rm{d}}\eta_{E_{p}}(\beta)\,{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}}(a)\,{\rm{d}}\eta_{E_{p}^{\times}}(\tau)\,{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\bf K}_{p}^{\#}}(k^{\#}),

where
{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\bf K}_{p}^{\#}}(k^{\#})
is the Haar measure on
{\bf K}_{p}^{\#}
with volume
1
. For
p<\infty
, the Haar measure
\eta_{\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}
is the one with
{\operatorname{vol}}({\bf K}_{p}^{\#})=1
. On the adele group
\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}})
, we use the product measure of Haar measures on

for

(2.21)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}})}f(g^{\#})\,{\rm{d}}g^{\#}=\tfrac{\sqrt{|D|}}{2}\int_{\mathsf{N}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}})}\int_{{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}}\int_{{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}_{E}}\int_{{\bf K}^{\#}}f

\displaystyle\left(n^{\#}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\tau&0\\
0&a\bar{\tau}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\,k^{\#}\right)|a|_{\mathbb{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times{\rm{d}}n^{\#}\,{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}}(a)\,{\rm{d}}\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}}(\tau)\,{\rm{d}}k^{\#},

where
{\rm{d}}k^{\#}
is the normalized Haar measure on
{\bf K}^{\#}=\prod_{p\leqslant\infty}{\bf K}_{p}^{\#}
. Note that the measure on
\mathsf{N}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}})\cong{\mathbb{A}}_{E}
is
\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}_{E}}
defined above, which is
\frac{\sqrt{|D|}}{2}\times\prod_{p}\eta_{E_{p}}\,(p\leqslant\infty)
due to the formulas

(
[
50
, Chap V §4 Proposition 7]
) and
\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}_{E}}({\mathbb{A}}_{E}/E)=1
.

2.4.4.

We fix
\eta_{{\bf K}_{\infty}}

2.5.
Ray class characters

For each place
v
of
E
, let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}
be the corresponding maximal ideal of
{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}
. For any invertible ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
of
{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}
, define
U({\mathfrak{m}}):=\prod_{v<\infty}(1+{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}^{m_{v}}\mathfrak{o}_{E,v})\cap{\mathfrak{o}}_{E,v}^{\times}

, which is an open compact subgroup of
\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}_{E}^{\times}
. Let

be an idele class character on
{\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}
of finite order such that
\Lambda|{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}={\bf 1}
; then
\Lambda|E_{\infty}^{\times}={\bf 1}
and

factors through a finite quotient

(2.22)

\displaystyle{\rm Cl}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(E):={\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}/{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}E^{\times}E_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}U({\mathfrak{m}})

for some invertible ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
; the conductor of
\Lambda
is the maximal one among such ideals
{\mathfrak{m}}
. As is well known, the group (
2.22
) is isomorphic to a quotient group of the ray class group of conductor
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
We say that a place
v
is inert in
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
, splits in
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
or ramifies in
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
if

is an unramified field extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\oplus{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, or is a ramified field extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, respectively. We have the following:

(2.23)

\displaystyle\text{If $v$ is inert in $E/{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $\Lambda_{v}|\mathfrak{o}_{E,v}^{\times}=1$, then $\Lambda_{v}={\bf 1}$}.

Indeed, let
p
be a prime below
v
; then
\Lambda_{v}(p)=1
due to
\Lambda|{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}={\bf 1}
. Hence,
\Lambda_{v}
is trivial on
p^{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathfrak{o}_{E,v}^{\times}=E_{p}^{\times}
. Define the Galois conjugate
\Lambda^{\dagger}
of
\Lambda
by setting

(2.24)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\dagger}(\tau):=\Lambda(\bar{\tau}),\quad\tau\in{\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}.

We have
\Lambda(\tau)\Lambda^{\dagger}(\tau)=\Lambda(\tau\bar{\tau})=1
for
\tau\in{\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}
by
\Lambda|{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}={\bf 1}
. Hence

(2.25)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\dagger}=\Lambda^{-1}=\bar{\Lambda},

where
\bar{\Lambda}
is the complex conjugate of
\Lambda
.

For an idele class character
\xi
of
E^{\times}
, let
\widehat{L}(s,\xi)
be the completed Hecke
L
-function of
\xi
, and
L_{p}(s,\xi)
its local
p
-factor for
p\leqslant\infty
. Then,
L_{p}(s,\xi^{\dagger})=L_{p}(s,\xi)
for any
p<\infty
. Indeed, if
p
is not ramified in
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
, the equality is trivial. Suppose that
p
ramifies; if
\xi_{p}
is a ramified character of
E_{p}^{\times}
, then both
L
-factors are
1
. If
\xi_{p}
is unramified, then for any prime element
\varpi
of
E_{p}
, we have
(\xi^{\dagger})_{p}(\varpi)=\xi_{p}(\bar{\varpi})=\xi_{p}(\varpi)
, which implies the equality between local
p
-factors.

For any character
\mu:{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}/{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
, we define
\mu_{E}:=\mu\circ{\operatorname{N}}_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}:{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}_{E}/E^{\times}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
. Then,
\mu_{E}
is Galois invariant, and
\mu_{E}|E_{\infty}^{\times}={\bf 1}
. Hence,
(\Lambda\mu_{E})^{\dagger}=\Lambda^{\dagger}\mu_{E}^{\dagger}=\Lambda^{-1}\mu_{E}
, and

(2.26)

\displaystyle\widehat{L}(s,\Lambda^{-1}\mu_{E})=\widehat{L}(s,\Lambda^{\dagger}\mu_{E})=\widehat{L}(s,\Lambda\mu_{E}),\quad({\operatorname{Re}}(s)>1).

Note that
\widehat{L}(s,\Lambda\mu_{E})
is holomorphic except for possible simple poles at
s=1,0
, which occurs if and only if both
\Lambda
and
\mu
are trivial.

Eisenstein series and Rankin-Selberg integral

3.1.
Eisenstein series

For details, we refer to
[
15
, §19]
; the theory on
{\bf GL}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{E})
developed there carries over into the group
\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}})
by a minor modification. For a finite-dimensional vector space
V
over a local field, let
\mathcal{S}(V)
be the space of all Schwartz-Bruhat functions on
V
. For
p{\leqslant}\infty
,
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(E_{p}^{2})
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
{\operatorname{Re}}(s)>0
, we define a function
f_{\phi}^{(s,\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p})}:\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
by

(3.1)

\displaystyle f_{\phi}^{(s,\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p})}(g^{\#})=\mu_{p}(\det g^{\#})|\det g^{\#}|_{p}^{s+1}\int_{E_{p}^{\times}}\phi([\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\end{smallmatrix}][\begin{smallmatrix}\tau&0\\

When

, we assume that
\phi
is
{\bf K}_{\infty}^{\#}
-finite. By the local Tate’s theory, the function
s\mapsto f_{\phi}^{(s,\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p})}(g^{\#})
is continued meromorphically to
{\mathbb{C}}
in such a way that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{f}_{\phi}^{(s,\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p})}(g^{\#}):=L_{p}(s+1,\Lambda_{p}\mu_{E,p})^{-1}\times f_{\phi}^{(s,\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p})}(g^{\#})

is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
\mathsf{f}_{\phi}^{(s,\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p})}
belongs to the space
\mathscr{V}^{\#}(s,\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p})
consisting of all smooth functions
f
on
\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
satisfying

f([\begin{smallmatrix}\tau&\beta\\
0&a\bar{\tau}\end{smallmatrix}]g^{\#})=\Lambda_{p}(\tau)^{-1}\mu_{p}(a)^{-1}|a|_{p}^{-(s+1)}f(g^{\#})

for any
[\begin{smallmatrix}\tau&\beta\\
0&a\bar{\tau}\end{smallmatrix}]\in{\mathsf{B}}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
and
g^{\#}\in\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
.

The Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi}
of
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(E_{p}^{2})
is defined by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(x,y)=\int_{E_{p}^{2}}\phi(u,v)\psi(\langle[\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
y\end{smallmatrix}],[\begin{smallmatrix}u\\
v\end{smallmatrix}]\rangle)d\eta_{E_{p}}(u)d\eta_{E_{p}}(v).

Set
w_{0}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})

M(s):{\mathscr{V}}^{\#}(s,\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p})\longrightarrow{\mathscr{V}}^{\#}(-s,\Lambda_{p}^{-1},\mu_{p}^{-1})

is defined on

as the absolutely convergent integral

\displaystyle M(s)f(g^{\#})=\int_{E_{p}}f\left(w_{0}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\beta\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]g^{\#}\right)\,{\rm{d}}\eta_{E_{p}}(\beta),\quad g^{\#}\in\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}).

The effect of
M(s)
on the section
f_{\phi}^{(s,\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p})}
is described by the Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi}
as

(3.4)

\displaystyle f_{\widehat{\phi}}^{(-s,\Lambda_{p}^{-1},\mu_{p}^{-1})}(g^{\#})=c_{p}\Lambda_{p}(\sqrt{D})^{-1}|D|_{p}^{-s+1/2}\,\varepsilon_{p}(s,\Lambda\mu_{E},\psi_{E_{p}})\frac{L_{p}(1-s,\Lambda^{-1}\mu_{E}^{-1})}{L_{p}(s,\Lambda\mu_{E})}\times M(s)f_{\phi}^{(s,\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p})}(g^{\#})

with

if

and

if

, where

denote Tate’s local epsilon factor defined by the character

of

and the associated self-dual measure on
E_{p}
as usual. Let
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{2})

Note that
\mathsf{f}_{\phi}^{(s,\Lambda,\mu)}
is left
{\mathsf{B}}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}})
- invariant and right
{\bf K}^{\#}
-finite. The Eisenstein series attached to

is defined by the absolutely convergent series

(3.5)

\displaystyle E(\phi,s,\Lambda,\mu;g^{\#}):=\widehat{L}(s+1,\Lambda\mu_{E})\times\sum_{\delta\in{\mathsf{B}}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{Q}})}\mathsf{f}_{\phi}^{(s,\Lambda,\mu)}(\delta g^{\#}),\quad g^{\#}\in\mathsf{G}^{\#}({\mathbb{A}})

for
{\operatorname{Re}}(s)>1
. The Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi}
of

is defined by a formula similar to (

) with respect to the measure
\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}_{E}}\otimes\eta_{{\mathbb{A}}_{E}}
. The following property of the Eisenstein series is standard.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{2})
,
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\Lambda\in\widehat{{\rm Cl}({E})}
,
\mu\in\widehat{{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}/{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}}
and

(i)

The map

({\operatorname{Re}}(s)>1)
has a meromorphic continuation to
{\mathbb{C}}
, holomorphic in

unless

in which case it has possible simple poles only at

. The function

(ii)

We have the functional equation

.

(iii)

For a relatively compact subset

on which

is regular and for a compact set

and

For any cusp form
\varphi

\mathsf{G}({\mathbb{A}})
,

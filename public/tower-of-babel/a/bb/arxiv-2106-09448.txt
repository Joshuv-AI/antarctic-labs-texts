Minimizing under relaxed symmetry constraints: Triple and N N -junctions.
Giorgio Fusco

Abstract
We consider a nonnegative potential W : ℝ 2 → ℝ W:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} invariant under the action of the rotation group C N C_{N} of the regular polygon with N N sides, N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 . We assume that W W has N N nondegenerate zeros and prove the existence of a N N -junction solution to the vector Allen-Cahn equation. The proof is variational and is based on sharp lower and upper bounds for the energy and on a new pointwise estimate for vector minimizers.

Introduction

This note concerns entire solutions
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
of the elliptic system

(1.1)

\Delta u=W_{u}(u),

where
W:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth nonnegative function that satisfies

(1.2)

0=W(a)<W(u),\;\;a\in A,\;u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus A,

and
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a set of
N
distinct points.

In phase transition theory, a function
W
that satisfies (
1.2
) can be regarded as a model for the bulk free energy of a system that can exist in
N
equally preferred phases represented by the zeros
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
of
W
.

We focus on
minimizers
that is solutions
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
of (
1.1
) that satisfy

(1.3)

J_{\Omega}(u+v)\geq J_{\Omega}(u),\quad J_{\Omega}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}+W(u)\Big)dx.

for every bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
C^{1}(\bar{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{m})
map
v
that coincides with
u
on
\partial\Omega
.

In the scalar case
m=1
there is a relationship
[
19
]
between minimal surfaces and minimizers of (
1.1
) and many deep interesting results
[
15
]
,
[
9
]
have been obtained in the process of understanding this relationship.

In the vector case
m\geq 2
the situation is quite different. The lack of basic tools like the maximum principle makes the description of the set of all bounded solutions of (
1.1
) an almost impossible task. To our knowledge the asymmetric layered solutions constructed in
[
16
]
is probably the only known minimizer that does not assume any symmetry. On the other hand, beginning with the triple junction of
[
6
]
and the quadruple junction of
[
14
]
various symmetric solutions with complex geometric structure were discovered (see
[
5
]
and Ch.6 and Ch.7 in
[

]

The simplest case is where

is invariant under a finite reflection group
G

(1.4)

W(gu)=W(u),\;\;u\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\,g\in G.

and
G
acts both on the domain space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and on the target space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
The minimization is on the set of
G
-equivariant maps

(1.5)

u(gx)=gu(x),\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,g\in G

that map fundamental region

for the action of
G

\mathbb{R}^{n}
into fundamental region
\Phi
for the action of
G
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}

u(\bar{F})\subset\bar{\Phi}.

For the triple junction case

, the group of the symmetries of the equilateral triangle, for the qudruple junction

is the group of the symmetries of a regular tetrahedron.

Restricting to

-equivariant maps that satisfy (

is a finite reflection group implies that the constraint (

) is near to one or another of the zeros of

has a unique zero in each

, (

remains away from all the zeros of

but one of them. This is a very important fact that, in the analysis of the structure of

, allows to regard

has having a unique zero and, in this sense, to reduce a non convex problem to a convex one and (

The scope of this notes is to make a first step toward removing the assumption of symmetry. We relax the symmetry requirements and consider the problem of the existence of multi-junction solution in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

Proof.

1. Given

(3.4)

\begin{split}&J_{(0,\tau)}(\tilde{u})=\int_{0}^{\tau}\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\frac{d\tilde{u}}{dt}|^{2}+W(\tilde{u})\big)dt\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}|z_{+}-z_{-}|^{2}+C_{W}^{2}\delta^{2})dt\\
&=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{|z_{+}-z_{-}|^{2}}{\tau}+C_{W}^{2}\delta^{2}\tau)=C_{W}\delta|z_{+}-z_{-}|\leq 2C_{W}\delta^{2},\end{split}

where we have also used Lemma

2. The minimality of

implies that, for small

by setting:

&\omega z_{-}=\bar{u}(t^{\delta})\in\partial B_{\delta}(\omega a).\end{split}

For each
\delta\in(0,\bar{\delta}]
let

be the map defined by

u^{\delta}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{u}(t),\;\;t\in[0,\tau)\\
\bar{u}(t_{\delta}+t-\tau),\;\;t\in[\tau,t^{\delta}-t_{\delta}+\tau).\end{array}\right.

Note that the definition of

implies
\omega u^{\delta}(0)=\omega\tilde{u}(0)=\omega z_{-}=\bar{u}(t^{\delta})=u^{\delta}(t^{\delta}-t_{\delta}+\tau)

can be extended to a

We have, using also (

3. For
\delta\in(0,\delta_{W}]
we can write

with
\delta=|\bar{u}-a|
and

\begin{split}&\frac{d\delta}{dt}\leq|\frac{d\bar{u}}{dt}|=\sqrt{2W(\bar{u})}\leq C_{W}\delta,\\
&\Rightarrow\\
&t_{\delta}\leq t_{\delta_{W}}-\frac{1}{C_{W}}\ln{\frac{\delta_{W}}{\delta}}.\end{split}

A similar computation yields

Hence

From this and (

\delta\in(0,\delta_{W}]
such that

r=\frac{N}{2\pi}(t^{\delta}-t_{\delta}+\tau).

3.8
) and (
3.7

2\pi r
-periodic
C_{N}
-equivariant map with bounded energy is nonempty and therefore that the existence of a minimizer
u_{r}
follows by classical arguments of variational calculus. The minimizer
u_{r}
is a solution of (
1.9

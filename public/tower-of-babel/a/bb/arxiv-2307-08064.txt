Global solutions for a 2D BENNEY-LIN type EQUATION posed ON RECTANGLES and on a half-strip Thanks: MSC 2010:35Q53;35B35
Nikolai A. Larkin †

Abstract
. We formulate on rectangles and on the right horizontal half-strip initial-boundary value problems for a two-dimensional Benney-Lin type equation. Existence and uniqueness of a regular solution as well as the exponential decay rate for the norm of a regular solution have been established.



1.
Introduction

We are concerned with initial-boundary value problems (IBVP) posed
on rectangles and the right horizontal half-strip for the 2D Benney-Lin-Kawahara equation (BLK)

u_{t}+\Delta^{2}u+\gamma\Delta u+\Delta u_{x}+uu_{x}+\eta\partial^{5}_{x}u=0

[formula]

which is a two-dimensional analog of the well-known Benney-Lin
equation,
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
20
]
,

u_{t}+\eta D^{5}_{x}u+\beta D^{4}_{x}u+\alpha D^{3}_{x}u+\gamma D^{2}_{x}u+uu_{x}=0.

[formula]

In turn, (1.1) includes as special cases the KdV, Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, Kawahara equations.,
[
5
,
6
,
10
,
15
,
31
]
.
The theory of the Cauchy problem for (1.2) and other dispersive
equations has been extensively studied and is
considerably advanced today

[
7
,
11
,
17
,
30
]
. In
recent years, results on IBVPs for dispersive equations both in
bounded and unbounded domains have appeared

[
8
,
9
,
12
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
26
,
28
]
. It was
discovered in
[
21
,
25
]
that the KdV and Kawahara
equations have an implicit internal dissipation. This allowed to
prove exponential decay of small solutions in bounded domains
without adding any artificial damping term. Later, this effect was
proved for a wide class of dispersive equations of any odd order
with one space variable
[
15
]
.
Publications on dispersive multy-dimensional equations of a higher
order (such as the KZK equation) appeared quite recently and were
concerned with the existence of weak solutions,
[
14
]
, and
physical motivation
[
13
]
.

Our work here was motivated by
[
31
]
where stability of solutions due to presense of higher order terms has been studied. Coefficient
\gamma
in (1.1) may be positive or negative depending on wavenumbers of long waves considered. The condition
\gamma>0
is considered to be instability condition, hence to guarantee stability of the system, we must take into account the fourth-order terms. On the other hand, the condition
\gamma<0
guarantees stability while the fourth-order terms give an additional damping effect. Differently from
[
31
]
, we added the fifth-order Kawahara term putting
\eta=-1
.

We study (1.1) on rectangles

D=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\quad x\in(0,L),\quad y\in(0,B),\quad\partial D\right\},

where
\partial D
is a boundary of
D
.
For
\gamma>0
, we establish the existence and uniqueness of global regular solutions for
t\in(0,T),
where
T
is an arbitrary positive number, without smallness conditions for the initial data.
More precisely, we formulate in Section 2
IBVP (2.1)-(2.3). In order to demonstrate existence of global
regular solutions, we exploit the Faedo-Galerkin method. Estimates,
independent of the parameter of approximations
N
, permit us to
establish the existence of regular solutions for the original
problem (2.1)-(2.3). We prove these estimates in Section 3.

In Section 4, we pass to the limit as
N\to\infty
and obtain a
global regular solution of (2.1)-(2.3). In Section 5, we prove
uniqueness of a regular solution. Finally, in Section 6, we
establish the exponential decay rate of small
solutions both for
\gamma>0
and for
\gamma\leq 0.
Section 7 contains Conclusions.

Lemma 2.2

u(t)\leq u_{0}e^{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}A(\tau)\,d\tau}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\int_{t_{0}}^{s}A(r)\,dr}B(s)\,ds.

.

Proof.

Multiply the last inequality by the integrating factor
e^{\int_{t_{0}}^{s}A(r)\,dr}
and integrate from
t_{0}
to
t
.
∎

3.
Formulation of the problem

Let
T,L,B
be arbitrary real positive numbers;

\displaystyle D=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\quad x\in(0,L),\quad y\in(0,B)\right\};

\displaystyle Q_{t}=D\times(0,t),\quad t\in(0,T).

Consider in
Q_{T}
the following IBVP:

\displaystyle Lu\equiv u_{t}+\Delta^{2}u+\Delta u+\Delta u_{x}+uu_{x}-\partial^{5}_{x}u=0\quad\textrm{in}\quad Q_{t};

(3.1)

\displaystyle u|_{\partial D}=u_{yy}(x,0)=u_{yy}(x,B)=u_{x}(0,y)

\displaystyle=u_{x}(L,y)=u_{xx}(L,y)=0;

(3.2)

\displaystyle u(x,y,0)=u_{0}(x,y),\quad(x,y)\in D.

(3.3)

Here

{\Delta}=\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}
. We adopt the usual
notations
H^{k}
for
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces;

\|\cdot\|
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the norm and the scalar product in
L^{2}(D)
,
{|\nabla u|}^{2}=u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}
.

4.
Existence Theorem

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T,B,L
be arbitrary real positive numbers. Given
u_{0}(x,y)
such that

\displaystyle u_{0}\in H^{4}(D),\quad\partial_{x}^{5}u_{0}\;\in L^{2}(D),

\displaystyle u_{0}(0,y)=u_{0x}(0,y)=u_{0}(L,y)=u_{0x}(L,y)=u_{0xx}(L,y)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(x,0)=u_{0}(x,B)=u_{0yy}(x,0)=u_{0yy}(x,B)=0,

\displaystyle J_{w}=\int_{D}\big[|\Delta^{2}u_{0}|^{2}+|\Delta u_{0}|^{2}+u^{2}_{0}u^{2}_{0x}+|D^{5}_{0x}|^{2}\big]dxdy<\infty,

then there exists a unique regular solution of (3.1)-(3.3):

\displaystyle u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(D))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}(D)),

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{5}u\;\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(D)),u_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(D)).

Proof.

Approximate Solutions.

To prove the existence part of this theorem, we
use the Faedo-Galerkin Method as follows:

for all
N
natural, we
define an approximate solution of (3.1)-(3.3) in the
form

u^{N}(x,y,t)=\sum^{N}_{j=1}\omega_{j}(y)g_{j}(x,t),

(4.1)

where
\omega_{j}(y)
are orthonormal in
L^{2}(0,L)
eigenfunctions of
the following problem:

\displaystyle-D^{2}_{y}\omega=\lambda_{j}\omega_{j}(y),\;y\in(0,B);\;\omega_{j}(0)=\omega_{j}(B)=0

(4.2)

and
g_{j}(x,t)
are solutions to the following initial- boundary value
problem for the system of N generalized KdV equations:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{j}(x,t)+\sum_{l,k=1}^{N}a_{lkj}g_{l}(x,t)g_{kx}(x,t)+(\Delta u^{N},\omega_{j})

\displaystyle+(\Delta^{2}u^{N},\omega_{j})-\partial^{5}_{x}g_{j}(x,t)+(\Delta u^{N}_{x},\omega_{j})=0,

\displaystyle g_{j}(0,t)=g_{jx}(0,t)=0,\;g_{j}(x,0)=u_{0j}(x),

(4.3)

where

\displaystyle a_{klj}=\int_{0}^{L}\omega_{k}(y)\omega_{l}(y)\omega_{j}(y)\,dy,\;j,k,l=1,...,N;

\displaystyle u_{0j}(x)=\int_{0}^{L}u_{0}(x,y)\omega_{j}(y)dy.

Solvability of (4.3) (at least local in t) follows from

[
18
]
. Hence, our goal is to prove necessary a
priori estimates, uniform in
N
, which will permit us to pass to
the limit in (4.1) as
N\to\infty
and to establish the
existence result. We assume first that a function
u_{0}
is
sufficiently smooth to ensure calculations. Exact conditions for

u_{0}
will follow from a priori estimates for
u^{N}
independent of

N
and usual compactness arguments.

(

Taking into account (4.19)-(4.21), for any
t_{0}\in(0,T)
nonsingular, we can write

\displaystyle([u_{t}+D^{4}_{y}u+2D^{2}_{x}D^{2}_{y}u+\Delta u-D^{2}_{y}u],\phi)(t_{0})

\displaystyle+(D^{2}_{x}u,D^{3}_{x}\phi)(t_{0})+(D^{2}_{x}u,D^{2}_{x}\phi)(t_{0})-(D^{2}_{x}u,\phi_{x})(t_{0})=0.

(4.23)

In turn, for any
t\in(0,T)
nonsingular, (4.23) may be rewritten as a distribution in
D
:

\displaystyle D^{5}_{x}u-D^{4}_{x}u-D^{3}_{x}u

\displaystyle=u_{t}+D^{4}_{y}u+2D^{2}_{x}D^{2}_{y}u+D^{2}_{y}u_{x}+\Delta u+uu_{x}\equiv f(x,y).

(4.24)

Since
u
is a limit of
u^{N}
and satisfies (4.19)-(4.21), then
f\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(D)).

By Lemma 2.3,

\displaystyle\|D^{4}_{x}u\|\leq C\|D^{5}_{x}u\|^{4/5}\|u\|^{1/5}\leq\delta\|D^{5}_{x}u\|+C_{4}(\delta)\|u\|,

\displaystyle\|D^{3}_{x}u\|\leq C\|D^{5}_{x}u\|^{3/5}\|u\|^{2/5}\leq\delta\|D^{5}_{x}u\|+C_{3}(\delta)\|u\|,

(4.25)

where
\delta
is an arbitrary positive number. Substituting (4.25) into (4.24), we get

\|D^{5}_{x}u\|(1-2\delta)\leq f+C(\delta)\|u\|.

Taking
\delta=\frac{1}{4},
by (4.24),

\|D^{5}_{x}u\|,\|D^{4}_{x}u\|,\|D^{3}_{x}u\|\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(D)).

(4.26)

Returning to (4.24), we find that a weak soltuion of(3.1)-(3.3) is such that

u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}_{0}(D)),\;\Delta^{2}u,D^{5}_{x}u\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(D)).

(4.27)

Hence, (4.22) can be rewritten as

\int_{0}^{t}([u_{\tau}+\Delta^{2}u+\Delta u_{x}+\Delta u+uu_{x}],\phi)(\tau)d\tau=0,\;t\in(0,T),

(4.28)

where
\phi(x,y,t)
is an arbitrary function from
L^{2}(Q_{t}).

5.
Uniqueness

Let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be distinct solutions of (3.1)-(3.3)
and
z=u_{1}-u_{2}
. Then
z(x,y,t)
satisfies
the following initial-boundary value problem:

\displaystyle Lz=z_{t}+\dfrac{1}{2}(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2})_{x}+\Delta z_{x}-D_{x}^{5}z

\displaystyle+\Delta z+\Delta^{2}z=0\ \textrm{in}\ Q_{t};

(5.1)

\displaystyle z(0,y,t)=z_{x}(0,y,t)=z_{x}(L,y,t)=z_{xx}(L,y,t)

\displaystyle=z(x,0,t)=z(x,B,t)=0,;\;\;y\in(0,L),\;x>0,\;t>0;

(5.2)

\displaystyle z(x,y,0)=0,\quad(x,y)\in D.

(5.3)

From the scalar product

2(Lz,z)(t)=0,

(5.4)

acting in the same manner as by the proof of Estimate II, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|z\|^{2}(t)+2\|\Delta z\|^{2}(t)+\int_{0}^{B}z^{2}{xx}(0,y,\tau)dy+2(\Delta z,z)(t)

\displaystyle=-([u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2}]_{x},z)(t).

(5.5)

For an arbitrary positive
\delta
, we estimate

I_{1}=2(\Delta z,z)\leq\delta\|\Delta z\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta}\|z\|^{2},

I_{2}=-([u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2}]_{x},z)=(z[u_{1}+u_{2}],z_{x})\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta\|z_{x}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\delta}\|[u_{1}+u_{2}]z\|^{2}.

By Steklov‘s Lemma,

I_{3}=\|z_{x}\|^{2}\leq a\|z_{xx}\|^{2}\leq a\|\Delta z\|^{2}

and

I_{4}=\frac{1}{2\delta}\|[u_{1}+u_{2}]z\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}sup_{D}|u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}|\|z\|^{2}.

Making use of Lemma 4.3, we find that

I_{5}=\frac{1}{\delta}sup_{D}|u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}|\|z\|^{2}\leq C(\delta)[\|\Delta u_{1}\|^{2}+\|\Delta u_{2}\|^{2}]\|z\|^{2}.

Substituting
I_{1}-I_{5}
into (5.5) and taking
\delta>0

sufficiently small, we come to the inequality

\dfrac{d}{dt}\|z\|^{2}(t)\leq C(k)\sum_{i=1}^{2}[1+\|\Delta u_{i}\|^{2}(t)]\|z\|^{2}(t).

Taking into
account that by (4.27)
[1+\|\Delta u_{i}\|^{2}(t)]\in L^{1}(0,T)

(i=1,2),
we
get
\|z\|(t)\equiv 0
for
a.e.\>t\in(0,T).
This proves
uniqueness of a regular solution of (3.1)-(3.3) and
completes the proof of Theorem 3.1.

∎

6.
Decay of Solutions

In Theorem 3.1, we have proved the existence and uniqueness of global solutions for
t\in(0,T),
where
T
is an arbitrary positive number, without smallness restrictions on initial data and value of an interval
(0,L)
. Nevertheless, we could not prove decay of solutions because our estimates depended on

1. Decay of solutions to the initial-boundary value problem posed on rectangles.

First, we prove exponential decay with destabilizing effect of the term
\gamma\Delta u,\;\gamma>0
. Consider the following problem:

\displaystyle Lu\equiv u_{t}+\Delta^{2}u+\gamma\Delta u+\Delta u_{x}+uu_{x}-\partial^{5}_{x}u=0\quad\textrm{in}\quad Q_{t};

(6.1)

\displaystyle u|_{\partial D}=u_{yy}(x,0)=u_{yy}(x,B)=u_{x}(0,y)

\displaystyle=u_{x}(L,y)=u_{xx}(L,y)=0;

(6.2)

\displaystyle u(x,y,0)=u_{0}(x,y),\quad(x,y)\in D.

(6.3)

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
B,L
satisfy the following condition:
\gamma>0;\;b=\gamma\left\{\frac{\pi^{2}}{B^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{L^{2}}\right\}^{-1}<1,

\theta=1-b>0
. Given

u_{0}(x,y)
subjected to conditions ofTheorem 3.1. Then for regular solutions of (6.1)-(6.3) the following inequality is true:

\displaystyle{\|u\|}^{2}(t)\leq\|u_{0}\|^{2}e^{-\chi t},

where
\chi=2a^{2}\theta
and
a=\frac{\pi^{2}}{B^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Multiplying (6.1) by
2u
and integrating the result by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}(t)-2\gamma\|\nabla u\|^{2}(t)+2\|\Delta u\|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{B}|D^{2}_{x}(0,y,t)^{2}dy=0.

(6.4)

By Lemma 4.2,
a\|\nabla u\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta u\|^{2}
that transform (6.4) into the following inequality:

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}(t)+2\theta\|\Delta u\|^{2}(t)\leq 0.

(6.5)

Again by Lemma 4.2,
a^{2}\|u\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta u\|^{2},
hence (6.5) becomes

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}(t)+2a^{2}\theta\|u\|^{2}(t)\leq 0.

(6.6)

Integrating (6.6), we get

{\|u\|}^{2}(t)\leq\|u_{0}\|^{2}e^{-2a^{2}\theta t}.

This completes the proof of Theorem 6.1.

7.
Conclusions

In this work, motivated by
[
31
]
, we study an initial-boundary value problem for the 2D Benney-Lin equation
adding to their model the Kawahara term
-D_{x}^{5}u
, see
[
16
]
. We studied stability of the system considering an influense of the term
\gamma\Delta u
. As has been observed in
[
31
]
, this term caused instability while
\gamma
was positive and implied stability effect while it was negative.

Taking this into account, first we studied in Section 4 the case
\gamma>0
and proved the existence and uniqueness of global regular solutions on the time interval
(0,T),
where
T
was an arbitrary positive number, without restrictions on the size of initial data or on dimensions of an arbitrary bounded domain
D.
Unfortunately, we could not prove decay of solutions as
t\to+\infty

because our estimates of solutions depended on
T.
On the other hand, in Section 6, we have proved in Theorem 6.1 exponential decay of solutionsfor sufficiently small
\gamma>0
assuming some restrictions on sizes of a bounded domain
D.
In this section, we studied also the case
\gamma\leq 0
and proved in Theorem 6.2 exponential decay of global solutions without restrictions on sizes of a bounded
D
and initial data. In Theorem 6.3, we have proved exponential decay of solutions
defined on the right half-strip with
\gamma>0
sufficiently small and some restrictions on
u_{0}
and
B.
In Theorem 6.4, exponential decay have been established for global regular solutions defined on the same half-strip while
\gamma\leq 0

without restrictions on
u_{0},B.
It has been observed that for
\gamma<0
the term
\gamma\Delta u
itself supplied stability of the system while the term
\Delta^{2}u
served as additional damping. On the other hand, in the case
\gamma=0
only the term
\Delta^{2}u
guaranteed stability .

Bogomolov property and Galois representations
Francesco Amoroso (1) and Lea Terracini (2)

Abstract
. In 2013 P. Habegger proved the Bogomolov property for the field generated over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} by the torsion points of a rational elliptic curve. We explore the possibility of applying the same strategy of proof to the case of field extensions cut out by Galois representations arising from more general modular forms.

1.
Introduction

Let
\alpha
be an algebraic number of degree
d
. We denote by
h(\alpha)
the absolute and logarithmic Weil height. Assuming that
\alpha
is not a root of unity, D. H. Lehmer suggested that
dh(\alpha)
can be bounded below uniformly in
\alpha
. Known as “Lehmer’s problem”, this question is still open. Nevertheless, in some special cases, not only Lehmer’s conjecture is true, but it can also be sharpened. This different point of view was considered for the first time in
[
5
]
, where the authors introduced the notion of fields with Bogomolov property, (property (B) for short), where the height is
uniformly bounded
below outside torsion points. By Northcott’s Theorem, number fields are trivial examples of fields with (B). The compositum
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\rm tr}
of all totally real fields is a less trivial example (apply
[
25
, Corollary
1^{\prime}
]
to the linear polynomial
P(z)=z-\alpha
to get the optimal lower bound). In
[
5
]
the authors also show that fields with bounded local degrees at some finite place has Property (B). Note that by a result of
[
9
]
a Galois extension has uniformly bounded local degrees at
every
finite place if and only if its Galois group has finite exponent. Thus (infinite) Galois extensions with Galois group of bounded exponent have Property (B).

There are still other several interesting examples of subfields of the algebraic numbers with Property (B). Zannier conjectured that the maximal abelian extension
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\rm ab}
of the rational field also satisfies property (B), and this was proved in
[
2
]
. By a special case of
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
, the same is true for
{\mathbb{K}}^{\rm ab}
, for any number field
{\mathbb{K}}
. Moreover we can combine this result with the quoted result on Galois extensions of bounded exponent. Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a number field and let
{\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}}
be an infinite Galois extension with Galois group
G
with
G/Z(G)
of finite exponent. Then
{\mathbb{L}}
has Property (B) (
[
1
, Corollary 1.7]
).

Another class of fields with Property (B) has been exhibited in
[
15
]
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an elliptic curve defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then the field
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{E}}_{tors})
obtained by adjoining all torsion points of
{\mathcal{E}}
has the Bogomolov property. If
{\mathcal{E}}
has complex multiplication then
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{E}}_{tors})
is an abelian extension of a quadratic number field and thus it has the property (B) by the quoted result of
[
3
]
. However for a non-CM elliptic curve there are no number fields
{\mathbb{K}}
such that
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{E}}_{tors})
is contained in the maximal abelian extension of
{\mathbb{K}}
.

Note that by Kronecker-Weber theorem
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\rm ab}
is the field obtained by adjoining all torsion points of the multiplicative group. A conjecture due to S. David (see
[
13
, Conjecture, p.114]
) suggests that
{\mathbb{Q}}(A_{tors})
has Property (B), when
A
is an abelian variety defined on a number field, according as it is already known for the algebraic groups
{\mathbb{G}}_{\rm m}
and
{\mathcal{E}}=
elliptic curve. Note that for a CM abelian variety
A
the field
{\mathbb{Q}}(A_{tors})
is still an abelian extension of a (CM) number field, and thus property (B) holds, again by the quoted result of
[
3
]
.

Habegger’s method provides a lower bound
h(\alpha)\geq c(p)
with
c(p)>0
whenever we have a prime
p\geq 5
such that
{\mathcal{E}}
has supersingular reduction at
p
(assumption
(P1)
) and the Galois representation
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}})\to{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{E}}[p])
is surjective (assumption
(P2)
). See
[
13
, Theorem 1.3, p.114]
for an explicit result. By a result of Elkies there exist infinitely many primes satisfying
(P1)
; and by a result of Serre
(P2)
holds outside a finite set of primes. Thus we can find a prime
p
satisfying both the above conditions.
Let now A be an abelian variety. Even under similar assumptions for a prime
p
, an analogue of the result of Habegger is not known.

One of the main purposes of this article is to re-interpret some known results on property (B) in terms of Galois representations, with the aim of providing a unifying framework for a part of the theory.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a separated topological group,
\mathbb{K}
be a subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and
G_{\mathbb{K}}=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{K})
. We say that a continuous homomorphism

\rho:G_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow\Omega,

has property
(B)
if
\mathbb{K}(\rho):=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\ker(\rho)}
does.

From this point of view, the main result of
[
2
]
implies (by Galois theory) that every homomorphism of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
taking values in an abelian group has Property (B).

In this vein,
[
1
, Corollary 1.7]
has the following consequence. Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
\kappa
be any field,
\rho:G_{\mathbb{K}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\kappa)
be an absolutely irreducible representation, and
{\mathbb{L}}=\mathbb{K}(\rho)
. Schur’s Lemma asserts that every intertwining automorphism of
\rho
is a scalar operator. In particular the image of the center of
G=\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}})
is contained in the subgroup of scalar matrices of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\kappa)
, so that the reduction map
\tilde{\rho}:G\to\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\kappa)
factors through
G/Z(G)
. Thus, if the image of
\tilde{\rho}
has finite exponent, the same is true
for
G/Z(G)
and
[
1
, Corollary 1.7]
implies that
\rho
has property (B).

Habegger theorem
[
15
, Theorem 1]
establishes Property (B) for the map

\rho_{\mathcal{E}}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})

which is the product of all the
\ell
-adic representations

\rho_{{\mathcal{E}},\ell}:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}})\longrightarrow Aut(T_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}}))=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})

where
{\mathcal{E}}
is an elliptic curve defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
T_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
is the corresponding Tate module. Indeed, by Galois correspondence
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{E}}[\ell^{n}])
is the subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
fixed by the kernel of the action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
on the
\ell^{n}
-torsion points of
{\mathcal{E}}
; by passing to the projective limit we see that
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{E}}[\ell^{\infty}])=\mathbb{Q}(\rho_{{\mathcal{E}},\ell})
. Since
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{E}}_{tors})
is the compositum of
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{E}}[\ell^{\infty}])
, for every prime
\ell
, then it coincides with the field fixed by
\bigcap_{\ell}\ker(\rho_{{\mathcal{E}},\ell})=\ker(\rho_{{\mathcal{E}}})
.

By the Shimura-Taniyama-Weil conjecture, proved by Wiles, all elliptic curves defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
are associated to a cuspidal eigenform of weight
2
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, whose eigenvalues for the Hecke operators are rational numbers. A question that naturally arises starting from Habegger’s theorem is whether and to what extent it can be generalized to other Galois representations associated with modular forms. Let
k\geq 2
be a positive integer and
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a normalized eigenform for the Hecke algebra. Let
{\mathbb{K}}_{f}
be the number field generated by the Hecke eigenvalues of
f
, and let
\mathcal{O}_{f}
be its ring of integers. A construction due to Shimura and Deligne attaches to
f
a family of compatible Galois representations

\rho_{f,v}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f,v})

indexed by the finite places
v
of
\mathcal{O}_{f}
.

For a rational prime
p
, we let

\rho_{f,p}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\prod_{v|p}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f,v}).

Moreover, let

\rho_{f}=\prod_{v}\rho_{f,v}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{f}),

where
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{f}
is the profinite completion of
\mathcal{O}_{f}
.

Inspired by Habegger’s theorem, we venture the following:

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a normalized eigenform. Then,

a)

for every
v
,
\rho_{f,v}
has property
(B)
;

b)

\rho_{f}
has property
(B)
.

Notice that Conjecture
1.2
a) does not imply Conjecture
1.2
b), as property (B) is not preserved under field composition (for instance,
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\rm tr}
and obviously also
{\mathbb{Q}}(i)
have property (B) but not
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\rm tr}(i)
, see
[
1
, Theorem 5.3, p. 1902]
).

If
f
is a CM modular form, then
\mathbb{Q}(\rho_{f})
is an abelian extension of a quadratic field, (see for instance
[
21
, Proposition 4.4]
). Thus
\rho_{f}
has property (B) by the special case
D=1
of
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
.

When
k=2
, Shimura
[
29
]
associated to
f
an abelian variety
A
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, of dimension
[{\mathbb{K}}_{f}:{\mathbb{Q}}]
equipped with an action of
\mathcal{O}_{f}
; such abelian varieties have been called
of type
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
in
[
24
]
and a generalization of Wiles’ theorem
[
17
, Corollary 10.2]
establishes that all of them arise from modular forms.

Let
A
be the abelian variety associated to a modular form
f
of weight
k=2
. Then
{\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)={\mathbb{Q}}(A_{tors})
. Indeed by costruction, for every prime
p
the representation
\rho_{f,p}=\prod_{v|p}\rho_{f,v}
is given by the action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
on the
p
-adic Tate module
T_{p}(A)=\varprojlim_{n}A[p^{n}]
. Then it is immediate to see that
\mathbb{Q}(A[p^{\infty}])
is the fixed field by
\ker\rho_{f,p}
, so that
\mathbb{Q}(\rho_{f,p})=\mathbb{Q}(A[p^{\infty}])
. Passing to
\rho_{f}
, we have

{\mathbb{Q}}\left(\rho_{f}\right)={\mathbb{Q}}\left(\prod_{p}\rho_{f,p}\right)=\coprod_{p}{\mathbb{Q}}\left(A[p^{\infty}]\right)={\mathbb{Q}}\left(\bigcup_{p}A[p^{\infty}]\right)={\mathbb{Q}}\left(\sum_{p}A[p^{\infty}]\right),

where the last equality holds because the sum in
A
is given by rational functions over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Thus Conjecture
1.2
intersects the already mentioned conjecture of David, which predicts that
{\mathbb{Q}}(A_{tors})
has Property (B), when
A
is an abelian variety defined on a number field.

Assume that
\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}
is the

. Let

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a normalized eigenform. Assume that there exists a rational prime
p
which satisfies Assumption
1.3
.
Let
{\mathbb{F}}/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension unramified at
p
with property
(B)
. Then
\rho_{f,p}|_{G_{\mathbb{F}}}
has property
(B)
.

As a consequence (see section
6
for the proof), we obtain the following direct generalization of Habegger’s result:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a normalized eigenform. Assume that there exists a rational prime
p
satisfying Assumption
1.3
. Then
\rho_{f}
has property
(B)
.

In Section
6
we also prove a more general statement (Corollary
6.2
), claiming that property (B) holds for
\mathbb{F}(\rho_{f})
, where
\mathbb{F}
is any Galois algebraic field unramified at
p
and such that a power of Frobenius is central in
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{Q})
.

Recall that a modular form
f
is
supersingular
at a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{f}
above
p
if
a_{p}\in\mathfrak{p}
. When
a_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
(as in the case
{\mathbb{K}}_{f}={\mathbb{Q}}
) and

, this implies

, by the Deligne bound:
|a_{p}|\leq 2\sqrt{p}
. Therefore in the case of elliptic curves, Habegger’s assumption

a_{p}=0
. Note that when
{\mathbb{K}}_{f}\not={\mathbb{Q}}
the condition

, required in our proof, is in general stronger than supersingularity.

Our condition
(P2)
generalizes Habegger’s assumption on the surjectivity of the Galois representation
G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{E}}[p])
. It has been extensively investigated in the literature (see

In the case of modular forms associated to a non CM elliptic curve, a theorem of Serre
[

establishes property

for every

For a modular form such that
\mathbb{K}_{f}\not=\mathbb{Q}
the situation concerning the image of

is complicated by the possible of presence of

; i.e. the elements of the subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{K}_{f})

consisting of those automorphisms

It is proved in

that there is an abelian number field

such that for

\rho_{f,p}|_{G_{E}}
has image
\widehat{G}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Therefore, all we can say is that condition

holds for

has no inner twists. In the case of trivial nebentypus (the one we are dealing with) it is easy to see that inner twists must arise from a quadratic Dirichlet character; and Ribet proved that they do not appear when

is square free

Plan of the paper.

In Section
2
we introduce most of the notations we need and we make some preliminary remarks.

Section
3
is devoted to two general statements on lower bounds for the height. The proofs rely on the techniques developed in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
1
]
. We fix two (possibly infinite) Galois extensions
{\mathbb{F}}/{\mathbb{Q}}
,
{\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{Q}}
. We fix a rational prime
p
. Usually, lower bounds for the height come from a dicotomy depending on whether
p
is ramified or not in
{\mathbb{L}}
. In the second case a Frobenius argument is generally enough to conclude. In the first case we need a descent argument. Here we change the point of view, assuming
{\mathbb{F}}
not ramified at
p
, with the residual degrees at
p
not growing from
{\mathbb{F}}
to
{\mathbb{L}}
. Moreover, we avoid the equidistribution argument of
[
15
]
, by using instead the acceleration lemma of
[
1
]
. This simplifies the proofs, although it leads to worse lower bounds, as remarked in
[
12
, Section 4.2.2]
. As a toy example we deduce a generalization (Theorem
3.5
) of the main result of
[
2
]
.

One of the crucial steps in applying the height-machinery above is to have a precise as possible description of the sequence of the local higher ramification groups. This is done in
[
15
, Lemma 3.3]
using the fact that the formal group associated to the elliptic curve is a Lubin-Tate module over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
In Section
4
we show that, by local Langlands correspondence and the classification of the two dimensional crystalline representations of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
given by Breuil, our local representation at
p
is a twist of a power of a fundamental character, so that Lubin Tate theory is also applicable in our more general case.

Section
5
is devoted to prove Proposition
5.1
, which is
the analogous of Lemma 6.2 of
[
15
]
on the normal closure of the last ramification group in the field cut out by the reduction modulo
p^{n}
of our modular representation. This was possible thanks to condition
(P2)
in Assumption
1.3
. For the proof we needed a group theoretical study of the linear group of a finite product of local rings. This is done in Appendix
A
.

The concluding Section
6
contains the proof of the results announced in the introduction.

2.
Notations and preliminary results

In the following

-

local fields will be denoted by
F,K,E,L,\ldots

-

global fields will be denoted by
\mathbb{F},\mathbb{K},\mathbb{E},\mathbb{L},\ldots

(It should be noted in particular that
\mathbb{F}
denotes a global field rather than a finite one. On the other hand, in accordance with standard notation, the finite field with
q
elements will be denoted by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This should not cause any confusion to the reader.)

For a field
\kappa
,
\overline{\kappa}
denotes an algebraic closure of
\kappa
, and
G_{\kappa}=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\kappa}/\kappa)
the absolute Galois group of
\kappa
, endowed with the Krull topology.

Let
\Omega
be a separated topological group and
\rho:G_{\kappa}\to\Omega
be a continuous homomorphism; we shall denote by
\kappa(\rho)=\overline{\kappa}^{\ker\rho}
the
field cut out
by
\rho
. Then
\kappa(\rho)/\kappa
is a Galois extension whose Galois group is isomorphic to
{\rm Im}(\rho)
. Moreover, since
\ker{\rho}
is closed,
G_{\kappa(\rho)}=\ker\rho
.

When
\kappa
is a local or global field and
v
is a place of
\kappa
, we shall say that
\rho
is
unramified
(resp.
totally ramified
) at
v
if
\kappa(\rho)
is. Note that
\rho
induces an isomorphism
\mathrm{Gal}(\kappa(\rho)/\kappa)\rightarrow{\rm Im}(\rho)
which as usual we denote by the same letter
\rho
.

From now on we fix a normalized eigenform
f=\sum_{n}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
for the Hecke algebra of weight
k\geq 2
; then
k
is an even integer. We recall the relevant definitions. We denote by
{\mathbb{K}}
the number field generated by the
a_{n}
’s and by
\mathcal{O}
its integer ring. A construction due to Shimura and Deligne associates to
f
a family of compatible Galois representations:

\rho_{v}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{v}),\quad v\hbox{ finite place of }{\mathbb{K}}.

Each
\rho_{v}
is characterized by the following two properties (where we denote by
p_{v}
the rational prime divided by
v
):

(2.1)

\hbox{$\rho_{v}$ is continuous and unramified at primes $\ell$ with $\ell\nmid Np_{v}$;}

(2.2)

\hbox{for $\ell\nmid Np_{v}$, the characteristic polynomial of the image by $\rho_{v}$ of}\\
\quad\quad\quad\hbox{a Frobenius element at $\ell$ is $X^{2}-a_{\ell}X+\ell^{k-1}$.}\hfill

Remark 2.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and
v
be any place of
{\mathbb{K}}
dividing

. Then, by (
2.2
) and by Chebotarev lemma, the determinant of
\rho_{v}
is
{\varepsilon}_{p}^{k-1}
, where
{\varepsilon}_{p}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
is the
p
-adic cyclotomic character. (Indeed, for a Frobenius element
\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell}
with

, we have:
\det\big(\rho_{v}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell})\big)=\ell^{k-1}={\varepsilon}_{p}^{k-1}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell})
.)

We let

\rho=\prod_{v}\rho_{v}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}),

where
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is the profinite completion of
\mathcal{O}
. For a prime
p
and

, we also denote by
\rho(p^{n})
the reduction

of
\rho
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
p
be a prime which does not divide

in

Light baryonium spectrum
Bing-Dong Wan 1,2 , Sheng-Qi Zhang 1 and Cong-Feng Qiao 1,3

Abstract
We evaluate the light baryonium spectrum, viz. the baryon-antibaryon states, in the framework of QCD sum rules. The nonperturbative contributions up to dimension 12 are taken into account. Numerical results indicate that there might exist eight possible light baryonium states, i.e. p p - p ¯ \bar{p} , Λ \Lambda - Λ ¯ \bar{\Lambda} , Σ \Sigma - Σ ¯ \bar{\Sigma} , and Ξ \Xi - Ξ ¯ \bar{\Xi} with quantum numbers of 0 − ⁣ + 0^{-+} and 1 − ⁣ − 1^{--} . For the Λ \Lambda - Λ ¯ \bar{\Lambda} , Σ \Sigma - Σ ¯ \bar{\Sigma} , and Ξ \Xi - Ξ ¯ \bar{\Xi} states, their masses are found above the corresponding dibaryon thresholds, while the masses of p p - p ¯ \bar{p} states are not. The possible baryonium decay modes are analyzed, which are hopefully measurable in BESIII, BELLEII, and LHCb experiments.

The establishment of quark model (QM) in 1960s

To discover more exotic states and explore their properties is currently one of the most intriguing and important topics in particle physics, which may greatly enrich the hadron family and our knowledge of the nature of QCD.

Facing the observations of tetraquark and pentaquark states, it is nature to conjecture existence of the hexaquark states, and it is time for hunting them. Deuteron, created at the beginning of the Universe and its stability is responsible for the production of other elements, is a typical and well-established dibaryon molecular state with
J^{P}=1^{+}
and binding energy
E_{B}=2.225\text{MeV}

The interaction between baryon-antibaryon pair is analogous to that between two baryons, which will provide important hints to understanding the absence in observation of the stable deuteronlike dibaryon states.

Actually, the history of the investigation of baryon-antibaryon states dates back to the 1940s, when Fermi and Yang proposed that

Of those techniques, the model with the independent Shifman, Vainshtein, and Zakharov (SVZ) sum rule technique

has some peculiar advantages in exploring hadron properties involving nonperturbative QCD. Rather than a phenomenological model, QCDSR is a QCD based theoretical framework which incorporates nonperturbative effects universally order by order and has already achieved a lot in the study of hadron spectroscopy and decays. Its starting point is to construct the proper interpolating currents corresponding to the hadron of interest ,which possesses the foremost information about the concerned hadron, like quantum numbers and the constituent quark or gluon. With the currents, the two-point correlation function, which has two representations, the QCD representation and the phenomenological representation, can be constructed. Equating these two representations, the QCD sum rules will be formally established, from which the hadron mass and decay width may be deduced.

Formalism

To evaluate the mass spectrum of
\mathscr{B}
-
\bar{\mathscr{B}}
states in QCDSR, the appropriate currents coupling to the states have to be constructed. The lowest order interpolating currents for
\mathscr{B}
-
\bar{\mathscr{B}}
states with quantum numbers

,

,
1^{++}
, and
1^{--}
can be respectively constructed as

\displaystyle j^{0^{-+}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\,\bar{\eta}_{\mathscr{B}}(x)\gamma_{5}\eta_{\mathscr{B}}(x)\;,

(1)

\displaystyle j^{1^{--}}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{\mathscr{B}}(x)\gamma_{\mu}\eta_{\mathscr{B}}(x)\;,

(2)

\displaystyle j^{0^{++}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{\mathscr{B}}(x)\eta_{\mathscr{B}}(x)\;,

(3)

\displaystyle j^{1^{++}}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\,\bar{\eta}_{\mathscr{B}}(x)\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\eta_{\mathscr{B}}(x)\;.

(4)

Here, we use the notion
\eta_{\mathscr{B}}
to represent the Dirac baryon fields without free Lorentz indices. As shown in Ref.
Chung:1981wm
,
\eta_{\mathscr{B}}
may takes the following quark structure :

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathscr{B}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\epsilon_{abc}[q_{a}^{i\,T}(x)C\gamma_{5}q_{b}^{j}(x)]q^{k}_{c}(x)\;,

(5)

where the superscripts

,

, and

,

, and

are color indices, and

is the charge conjugation matrix. In our calculation,
(i,j,k)=(u,d,u)
,
(u,d,s)
,
(u,s,d)
, and (s, u, s) for

,
\Lambda
,
\Sigma
and

With the currents (
1
)
-
(
4

\displaystyle\Pi(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int d^{4}xe^{iq\cdot x}\langle 0|T\{j(x),\;j^{\dagger}(0)\}|0\rangle\;,

(6)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int d^{4}xe^{iq\cdot x}\langle 0|T\{j_{\mu}(x),\;j_{\nu}^{\dagger}(0)\}|0\rangle\;

(7)

where

are the relevant hadronic currents with

denotes the physical vacuum. For

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big(g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{q_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{q^{2}}\Big)\Pi_{1}(q^{2})+\frac{q_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{q^{2}}\Pi_{0}(q^{2})\;,

(8)

where the subscripts
1
and
0

On the phenomenological side, after separating the ground state contribution from the hadronic state, the correlation function
\Pi^{X}_{J^{PC}}(q^{2})
can be expressed as a dispersion integral over the physical regime, i.e.,

\displaystyle\Pi^{X,\;phen}_{J^{PC}}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda^{X}_{J^{PC}})^{2}}{(m^{X}_{J^{PC}})^{2}-q^{2}}+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}ds\frac{\rho^{X}_{J^{PC}}(s)}{s-q^{2}}\;,

(9)

where the superscript
X
denotes the lowest lying
\mathscr{B}
-
\bar{\mathscr{B}}

,
\rho^{X}_{J^{PC}}(s)
is the spectral density that contains the contributions from higher excited states and the continuum states above the threshold
s_{0}
, and

is the coupling constant.

On the OPE (operator product expansion) side, the dispersion relation can express the correlation function
\Pi^{X}_{J^{PC}}(q^{2})
as

\displaystyle\Pi^{X,\;OPE}_{J^{PC}}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s_{min}}^{\infty}ds\frac{\rho^{X,\;OPE}_{J^{PC}}(s)}{s-q^{2}}\;,

(10)

where
s_{min}
is the kinematic limit, which usually corresponds to the square of the sum of current-quark masses of the hadron
Wan:2020oxt
;
Wan:2020fsk
.
\rho^{X,\;OPE}_{J^{PC}}(s)=\text{Im}[\Pi^{X,\;OPE}_{J^{PC}}(s)]/\pi
is the spectral density of the OPE side and contains the contributions of the condensates up to dimension 12 which can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\rho^{OPE}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{pert}(s)+\rho^{\langle\bar{q}q\rangle}(s)+\rho^{\langle G^{2}\rangle}(s)+\rho^{\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle}(s)+\rho^{\langle\bar{q}q\rangle^{2}}(s)+\rho^{\langle G^{3}\rangle}(s)

(11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\rho^{\langle\bar{q}q\rangle\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle}(s)+\rho^{\langle\bar{q}q\rangle^{2}\langle G^{2}\rangle}(s)+\rho^{\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle^{2}}(s)+\rho^{\langle\bar{q}q\rangle^{2}\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle}(s)+\rho^{\langle\bar{q}q\rangle^{4}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}c_{n}s^{n}.

To calculate the spectral density of the OPE side, Eq. (

(12)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{i\delta_{jk}x^{2}x\!\!\!/\penalty}{1152}m_{q}\langle g_{s}\bar{q}\sigma\cdot Gq\rangle-\frac{t^{a}_{jk}\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}{192}\langle g_{s}\bar{q}\sigma\cdot Gq\rangle+\frac{it^{a}_{jk}}{768}(\sigma_{\alpha\beta}x\!\!\!/\penalty+x\!\!\!/\penalty\sigma_{\alpha\beta})m_{q}\langle g_{s}\bar{q}\sigma\cdot Gq\rangle\;,

where, the vacuum condensates are clearly displayed. For more explanation on above propagator, readers may refer to Refs.
Wang:2013vex

\displaystyle m^{X}_{J^{PC}}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})=\sqrt{-\frac{L^{X}_{J^{PC},\;1}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}{L^{X}_{J^{PC},\;0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}}\;.

\displaystyle L^{X}_{J^{PC},\;0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})=\int_{s_{min}}^{s_{0}}ds\;\rho^{X,\;OPE}_{J^{PC}}(s)e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}\;

\displaystyle L^{X}_{J^{PC},\;1}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})=\frac{\partial}{\partial{\frac{1}{M_{B}^{2}}}}{L^{X}_{J^{PC},\;0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}\;.

Furthermore, there exist two additional parameters, i.e., the continuum threshold

;

\displaystyle R^{X,\;OPE}_{J^{PC}}=\left|\frac{L_{J^{PC},\;0}^{X,\;c_{0}}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}{L_{J^{PC},\;0}^{X}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}\right|\,,

(17)

\displaystyle R^{X,\;PC}_{J^{PC}}=\frac{L_{J^{PC},\;0}^{X}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}{L_{J^{PC},\;0}^{X}(\infty,M_{B}^{2})}\;.

In summary, we investigate the light baryonium states in molecular configuration with quantum numbers of

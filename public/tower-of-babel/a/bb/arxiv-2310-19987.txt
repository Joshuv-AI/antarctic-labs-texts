A classification of curious Galois groups as direct products
Garen Chiloyan

Abstract
. Let N N be a positive integer. Let H \operatorname{H} be a group of level N N and let E E be an elliptic curve defined over the rationals with j E ≠ 0 , 1728 \textit{j}_{E}\neq 0,1728 . 2.
Structure and methodology

In Section
3
, we briefly go over the objects of study of the paper; modular curves, elliptic curves and Galois representations. In Section
4
, we briefly introduce the work of Sutherland–Zywina and Rouse–Zureick-Brown in the classification of modular curves of prime-power level that contain infinitely many rational points. In Section
5
, we go over some group-theoretic lemmas. Let
p
and
q
be distinct primes and
M
and
N
be powers of
p
and
q
respectively. Let
\operatorname{H}_{p}
be a group of level
M
and let
\operatorname{H}_{q}
be a group of level
N
. Then
\operatorname{H}=\operatorname{H}_{p}\times\operatorname{H}_{q}
is a group of level
MN
. By an application of the Chinese remainder theorem, we can determine a subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
that is isomorphic to
\operatorname{H}
. If
\operatorname{H}
generates a modular curve with infinitely many rational points, then work by Sutherland and Zywina shows that
p
and
q
are primes less than or equal to
13
.

Section
6
contains the proof of Theorem
1.3
broken up into two theorems. We take direct products of pairs of groups from the Sutherland–Zywina database to get a group
\operatorname{H}
of level
N
where
N
is a positive integer divisible by two primes. We focus on the groups
\operatorname{H}
that are arithmetically admissible. We then use code from
[
19
]
to compute the genus. If the genus of
\operatorname{H}
is strictly greater than
1
, then work by Faltings shows that the modular curve
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
defined by
\operatorname{H}
has finitely many points defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. If the genus of
\operatorname{H}
is equal to
0
, then either
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
has no points defined over
\mathbb{Q}
or
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and there is an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\overline{\rho}_{E,N}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is conjugate to
\operatorname{H}
. In either case,
\operatorname{H}
is not a curious Galois group. Hence, if
\operatorname{H}
is a curious Galois group, then
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
is an elliptic curve of positive rank.

There were a couple of ways to investigate groups
\operatorname{H}
that generate modular curves of genus
1
and positive rank to determine if they are or are not curious Galois groups. We first search the LMFDB for elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\overline{\rho}_{E,N}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is conjugate to
\operatorname{H}
. If there is such an example of an elliptic curve, then
\operatorname{H}
is not a curious Galois group. If no such example of an elliptic curve exists in the LMFDB, then we inspect the subgroups of
\operatorname{H}
of genus less than
2
. If
\operatorname{H}
contains no subgroup of genus less than
2
, then finitely many rational points on
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
correspond to elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\overline{\rho}_{E,N}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
. As
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
contains infinitely many rational points, there is an elliptic curve
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\overline{\rho}_{E,N}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is conjugate to
\operatorname{H}
and hence,
\operatorname{H}
is not a curious Galois group.

If all else fails, we suspect that
\operatorname{H}
is a curious Galois group. Note that either
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}(\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. A curious Galois group that is a direct product of groups contains some collection of proper subgroups
\operatorname{H}_{1},\ldots,\operatorname{H}_{m}
such that the modular curve
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{i}}
generated by
\operatorname{H}_{i}
is isomorphic to the modular curve
\operatorname{X}_{j}
generated by
\operatorname{H}_{j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq m
. Moreover,
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{i}}
is an elliptic curve such that
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{i}}(\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Note that
[\operatorname{H}:\operatorname{H}_{i}]=p
where
p=2
or
3
and there is an isogeny
\phi\colon\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{i}}\to\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
of degree
p
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. For
1\leq i\leq m
, let
\pi_{\operatorname{H}_{i}}\colon\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{i}}\to\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
be the map from
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{i}}
to the
j
-line. Then there is a rational morphism
\psi\colon\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{i}}\to\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
of degree
[\operatorname{H}:\operatorname{H}_{i}]
such that the following diagram commutes.

If for each rational point
P

\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
there is a rational point

\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{i}}

\pi_{\operatorname{H}}(P)=\pi_{\operatorname{H}_{i}}(P)

\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}

\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{1}},\ldots,\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{m}}
and
\operatorname{H}
is a curious Galois group. For a point

\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}

be the identity of

\psi=\tau_{\mathcal{O}}\circ\psi=\tau_{\psi(\mathcal{O})}\circ\tau_{-\psi(\mathcal{O})}\circ\psi=\tau_{\psi(\mathcal{O})}\circ\left(\tau_{-\psi(\mathcal{O})}\circ\psi\right).

\mathcal{O}
to
\mathcal{O}

At times,

and
\operatorname{H}
contains a single subgroup
\operatorname{H}_{1}

3.
Background

An elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
is a smooth projective, algebraic curve of genus
1
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with a point defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Equivalently, an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
is a smooth projective curve with the following homogeneous equation

E:Y^{2}Z+a_{1}XYZ+a_{3}YZ^{2}=X^{3}+a_{2}X^{2}Z+a_{4}XZ^{2}+a_{6}Z^{3}

with
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},
and
a_{6}
being rational constants. With this equation,
E
has the structure of an abelian group with identity being the single point at infinity,
\mathcal{O}=[0:1:0]
. After dehomogenizing with respect to
Z
, we get the following equation

E:y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}.

Let
N
be a positive integer. The set of all points on
E
of order dividing
N
with coordinates in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is a group, denoted
E[N]
and is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
. An element of
E[N]
is called an
N
-torsion point. The group
G_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\operatorname{Gal}\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}\right)
acts on
E[N]
for all positive integers
N
. From this action, we have the mod-
N
Galois representation attached to
E
:

\overline{\rho}_{E,N}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{Aut}(E[N]).

After identifying
E[N]\cong\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
and fixing a set of (two) generators of
E[N]
, we may consider the mod-
N
Galois representation attached to
E
as

\overline{\rho}_{E,N}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}).

3.1.
Quadratic Twists

Let
E:y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
be an elliptic curve defined over

and let

by

is the elliptic curve

is isomorphic to the elliptic curve
E^{(d)}:dy^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
. Then

is isomorphic to

by the map

defined by fixing

and mapping a non-zero point

-invariant of

is equal to the

-invariant of

is an elliptic curve such that the

is equal to the

-invariant of

, then

is a (possibly trivial) quadratic twist of

. We present an analogous definition of quadratic twists of subgroups of

.

Let

be an elliptic curve and let

for some prime

(1)

Maximal subgroups of

By the fact that the determinant map

(2)

Exceptional subgroups : maximal subgroups of

,

such that if

is a prime greater than

is not conjugate to an exceptional subgroup of

(3)

Maximal subgroups that are Borel.

(4)

Maximal subgroups that are conjugate to the normalizer of the split Cartan group modulo

, then

is not conjugate to the normalizer of the non-split Cartan group modulo

(5)

Maximal subgroups that are conjugate to the normalizer of the non-split Cartan group modulo

This is the final case that remains open. See

1208

.

5.
Product groups

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
be distinct primes and let
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\geq 2
be integers such that
M_{i}
is a power of
p_{i}
for all
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
. Then we will call subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M_{1}\cdots M_{n}\mathbb{Z})\cong\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M_{1}\mathbb{Z})\times\ldots\times\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M_{n}\mathbb{Z})
that arise as direct products of proper subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M_{i}\mathbb{Z})
for
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
as product groups.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
M,N\geq 2
be coprime integers. Then
\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}

Proof.

Let
\phi_{MN}\colon\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi_{MN}(x)=(\phi_{M}(x),\phi_{N}(x))
where
\phi_{M}(x)
is the reduction of
x
modulo
M
and
\phi_{N}(x)
is the reduction of
x
modulo
N
. Then
\phi
is an isomorphism.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
M,N\geq 2
be coprime integers. Then
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})\cong\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Let
\psi_{MN}\colon\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
be the map that maps
A\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
to
(\psi_{M}(A),\psi_{N}(A))
where
\psi_{M}(A)
is the reduction of the matrix
A\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
modulo
M
and
\psi_{N}(A)
is the reduction of
A
modulo
N
. Then
\psi_{MN}
is a homomorphism. Let
A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{Ker}(\psi_{MN})
. Then the reduction of
b
and
c
modulo
M
and
N
are both
0
. By Lemma
5.1
, both
b
and
c
are congruent to
0
modulo
MN
. Moreover, the reduction of
a
and
d
modulo
M
and
N
are both
1
. By Lemma
5.1
, both
a
and
d
are congruent to
1
modulo
MN
.

Let
\begin{bmatrix}r_{1}&r_{2}\\
r_{3}&r_{4}\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
and let
\begin{bmatrix}s_{1}&s_{2}\\
s_{3}&s_{4}\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
. We need to find a matrix
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
such that
\psi_{MN}\left(\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{bmatrix}r_{1}&r_{2}\\
r_{3}&r_{4}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}s_{1}&s_{2}\\
s_{3}&s_{4}\end{bmatrix}\right)
. In other words, we need to find elements
(a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z}
such that the reduction of
(a,b,c,d)
modulo
M
is equal to
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4})
and the reduction of
(a,b,c,d)
modulo
N
is equal to
(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})
. This follows from Lemma
5.1
. Finally, the matrix
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}
is invertible because
\psi_{M}\left(\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\right)
and
\psi_{N}\left(\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\right)
are both invertible.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
M,N\geq 2
be co-prime integers. Let
A\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
. Then there is a matrix
\widehat{A}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
which reduces modulo
M
to
A
.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.2
,
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
. There is an obvious surjective map, namely the projective map
\pi_{MN,M}\colon\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}N\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
.
Thus, there is an element
A^{\prime}=(A,\operatorname{Id})\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
such that
\pi_{MN,N}(A^{\prime})=A
. Next, we can identify
A^{\prime}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
with a matrix
\widehat{A}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
with the desired properties.

∎

Remark 5.4
.

Let
M,N\geq 2
be coprime integers. Then for a matrix
A\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
, at times, we will need to fix a matrix in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
that reduces modulo
M
to
A
. We will denote such a matrix from
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
as
\widehat{A}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
M
and
N
be positive integers and let
\pi\colon\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
be the natural projection map. Let
\operatorname{H}=\left\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\right\rangle
be a subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
. Then
\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{H})=\left\langle\widehat{g_{1}},\ldots,\widehat{g_{r}},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)\right\rangle
where for
1\leq i\leq r
,
\widehat{g_{i}}
is any fixed element of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
that reduces modulo
M
to
g_{i}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{H})
is a group and it is clear that
\left\langle\widehat{g_{1}},\ldots,\widehat{g_{r}},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)\right\rangle\subseteq\pi^{-1}(A)
. Let
x\in\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{H})
. Then
x=h_{1}\cdots h_{s}
for some
h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}
where
h_{i}\in\left\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\right\}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Let
\widehat{x}=\widehat{h_{1}}\cdots\widehat{h_{s}}
where
\widehat{h_{i}}=\widehat{g_{i}}
if and only if
h_{i}=g_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. it is clear that
\pi\left(\widehat{x}\right)=x
. Now let
\widehat{y}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
such that
\pi(\widehat{x})=\pi(\widehat{y})
. Then

\operatorname{Id}=\pi(\widehat{y})\cdot\pi(\widehat{x})^{-1}=\pi\left(\widehat{y}\cdot\widehat{x}^{-1}\right)

and hence,
\widehat{y}\cdot\widehat{x}^{-1}\in\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)
. Thus, we have
\widehat{y}\in\left\langle\widehat{x},\operatorname{Ker}\right\rangle
. Hence,
\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{H})\subseteq\left\langle\widehat{g_{1}},\ldots,\widehat{g_{r}},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)\right\rangle
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
p
and
q
be distinct primes. Let
M\geq 2
be a power of
p
and let
N\geq 2
be a power of
q
. Let
A
be a subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}\left(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\right)
and let
B
be a subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}\left(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\right)
. Let
\pi_{1}\colon\operatorname{GL}\left(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z}\right)\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
and
\pi_{2}\colon\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
be the respective natural projection maps. Then the group of matrices in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
that reduces modulo
M
to
A
and reduces modulo
N
to
B
is conjugate to
\left\langle\widehat{A},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{1})\right\rangle\bigcap\left\langle\widehat{B},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{2})\right\rangle
, where
\widehat{A}
is any subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
that reduces modulo
N
to
A
and
\widehat{B}
is any subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z})
that reduces modulo
N
to
B
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}\left(2,\mathbb{Z}/MN\mathbb{Z}\right)
that reduces modulo
M
to
A
. Using a similar proof to Lemma
5.5
, we see that
H
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\left\langle\widehat{A},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{1})\right\rangle
where
\widehat{A}
is any lift of
A
to level
MN
. Also,
H
reduces modulo
N
to
B
. Similarly, we see that
H
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\left\langle\widehat{B},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{2})\right\rangle
where
\widehat{B}
is any lift of
B
to level
MN
. Hence,
H
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\left\langle\widehat{A},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{1})\right\rangle\bigcap\left\langle\widehat{B},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{2})\right\rangle
. Conversely, a matrix in
\left\langle\widehat{A},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)\right\rangle\bigcap\left\langle\widehat{B},\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{2})\right\rangle
reduces modulo
M
to a matrix in
A
and simultaneously reduces modulo
N
to a matrix in
B
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

The statements of the preceding lemmas and corollaries of this section can be extended to arbitrarily large factors.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
N
be a positive integer. Suppose that
\operatorname{H}
is an arithmetically admissible group of level
N
. Let
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
be the modular curve generated by
\operatorname{H}
and suppose that

is infinite. Suppose moreover that no proper arithmetically admissible subgroup of

generates a modular curve with infinitely many rational points. Then
\operatorname{H}
is not a curious group of level

.

Proof.

The group
\operatorname{H}
has finitely many subgroups and we can apply the pigeonhole principle.
∎

.

Let

be a positive integer. Suppose that
\operatorname{H}
is an arithmetically admissible group of level

. Let
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}}
be the modular curve generated by

and suppose that

.
∎

6.
Main work

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve and let
N
be a positive integer. Then
\rho_{E}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is a subset of
\underset{n\geq 0}{\varprojlim}\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})\cong\underset{\ell}{\Pi}\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
. In this paper, we are interested in Galois images that are direct products. In other words, for primes
p_{1}<\ldots<p_{m}
, we discuss curious Galois groups of the form
\operatorname{H}_{p_{1}}\times\ldots\times\operatorname{H}_{p_{m}}
where
\operatorname{H}_{p_{i}}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}})
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Let us say that
\operatorname{H}_{p_{m}}=\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/p_{m}\mathbb{Z})
. Let
A=\operatorname{H}_{p_{1}}\times\ldots\times\operatorname{H}_{p_{m-1}}
and let
B=A\times\operatorname{H}_{p_{m}}
. Let
\operatorname{X}_{A}
be the modular curve generated by
A
and let
\operatorname{X}_{B}
be the modular curve generated by
B
. Then
\operatorname{X}_{A}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{X}_{B}
. For the rest of the paper, we will consider curious Galois groups of the form
\operatorname{H}_{p_{1}}\times\ldots\times\operatorname{H}_{p_{m}}
with
\operatorname{H}_{p_{i}}\subsetneq\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. We will call curious Galois groups of the form
\operatorname{H}_{p_{1}}\times\ldots\times\operatorname{H}_{p_{m}}
with
\operatorname{H}_{p_{i}}\subsetneq\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq m
to be curious Galois groups of type
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
.

Let
\operatorname{H}
be a curious Galois group of type
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
. For

, let
\operatorname{H}_{p_{i}}
be the reduction of
\operatorname{H}
to

-power level. Then

is a subgroup of the full preimage of

inside
\operatorname{GL}\left(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
. Let

be the modular curve defined by
\operatorname{H}
and let
\operatorname{X}_{\operatorname{H}_{p_{i}}}
be the modular curve defined by
\operatorname{H}_{p_{i}}
. Then there is a rational morphism

of (finite) degree

. As
\operatorname{X}
contains infinitely many rational points, as

maps rational points to rational points, and as the degree of

is finite, we have that

has infinitely many rational points. Hence,
\operatorname{H}_{p_{i}}
is a group from the SZ (
[
24
]
) database. The rest of this article is devoted to classifying all curious Galois groups of type

where

are distinct primes between

and

. We will show that

or

and fully classify curious Galois groups of type

and type

as

and

range through all primes. First we classify the curious Galois groups of type

Corollary 6.3

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
m
be an integer greater than equal to
2
and let
p_{2}<\ldots<p_{m}
be odd primes. Then there are no curious Galois groups of type
(2,p_{2},\ldots,p_{m})
.

Proof.

All that needs to be proven is the case when
m=3
. Let
\operatorname{H}_{2}
be a proper subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Assume by way of contradiction that
\operatorname{H}_{p_{i}}
is a proper subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}})
for
i=2,3
such that
\operatorname{H}=\operatorname{H}_{2}\times\operatorname{H}_{p_{2}}\times\operatorname{H}_{p_{3}}
is a curious Galois group. Then the genus of
\operatorname{H}
is equal to
0
or
1
. By Theorem
6.2
and its proof,
p_{2}=3
and
\operatorname{H}_{3}=\texttt{3.3.0.1}
. Moreover,
p_{3}=5
or
p_{3}=7
. We break up the proof into two cases.

(1)

p_{2}=3
and
p_{3}=5

In this case, a priori, we have that
\operatorname{H}_{2}
is one of
2.3.0.1
,
4.4.0.1
,
8.2.0.1
,
8.2.0.2
,
8.6.0.2
,
8.6.0.4
, or
8.6.0.6
,
\operatorname{H}_{3}
is
3.3.0.1
, and
\operatorname{H}_{5}
is one of
5.5.0.1
,
5.6.0.1
,
5.10.0.1
, or
5.15.0.1
. Moreover, in each case, the genus of
\operatorname{H}=\operatorname{H}_{2}\times\operatorname{H}_{3}\times\operatorname{H}_{5}
is greater than or equal to
2
. Hence,
\operatorname{H}
is not a curious Galois group.

(2)

p_{2}=3
and
p_{3}=7

In this case, a priori, we have that
\operatorname{H}_{7}=\texttt{7.21.0.1}
. Let
\operatorname{H}_{2}
be a group of
2
-power level from
[
24
]
. Then the genus of
\operatorname{H}_{2}\times\operatorname{H}_{7}
is greater than or equal to
2
. Hence,
\operatorname{H}
is not a curious Galois group.

∎

Twists on the Torus Equivariant under the 2-Dimensional Crystallographic Point Groups
Kiyonori GOMI

Abstract
A twist is a datum playing a role of a local system for topological K K -theory. In equivariant setting, twists are classified into four types according to how they are realized geometrically. This paper lists the possible types of twists for the torus with the actions of the point groups of all the 2 2 -dimensional space groups (crystallographic groups), or equivalently, the torus with the actions of all the possible finite subgroups in its mapping class group. This is carried out by computing Borel’s equivariant cohomology and the Leray–Serre spectral sequence. As a byproduct, the equivariant cohomology up to degree three is determined in all cases. The equivariant cohomology with certain local coefficients is also considered in relation to the twists of the Freed–Moore K K -theory.

Introduction

Topological
K
-theory has recently been recognized as a useful tool for a classification of topological insulators in condensed matter physics. In Kitaev’s 10-fold way
[
17
]
, the usual complex
K
-theory and also
KO
or Atiyah’s
KR
-theory are used. These classifications are in some sense the most simple cases, and a recent study of topological insulators focuses on more complicated cases. Such complicated cases arise when we take the symmetry of quantum systems into account. Then equivariant
K
-theory and its twisted version naturally fit into the classification scheme of such systems
[
8
]
. Actually, as will be explained in Section
2
, a certain quantum system on the
d
-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
invariant under a
space group
provides a
K
-theory class on the
d
-dimensional torus
T^{d}
equivariant under the point group of the space group. If the space group is
nonsymmorphic
, then the equivariant
K
-class is naturally twisted. In the case of
d=2
, such (twisted) equivariant
K
-theories are computed for the
17
classes of
2
-dimensional space groups, in view of the classification of topological crystalline insulators
[
27
,
28
]
. An outcome of these computations of twisted equivariant
K
-theories is the discovery of topological insulators which are essentially classified by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
but do not require the so-called time-reversal symmetry or the particle-hole symmetry
[
26
]
. This type of topological insulators is new in the sense that the known topological insulators essentially classified by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
so far require the time-reversal symmetry or the particle-hole symmetry.

The understanding of the importance of twisted equivariant
K
-theory in condensed matter physics leads to a mathematically natural issue: determining the possible ‘twists’ for equivariant
K
-theory. To explain this issue more concretely, let us recall that twisted
K
-theory
[
5
,
22
]
is in some sense a
K
-theory with ‘local coefficients’. The datum playing the role of a ‘local system’ admits various geometric realizations. In this paper, we realize them by
twists
in the sense of
[
7
]
. If a compact Lie group
G
acts on a space
X
, then graded twists on
X
are classified by the Borel equivariant cohomology
H^{1}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. Similarly, ungraded twists are classified by
H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, on which we focus for a moment. (Sometimes
H^{0}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
may be included in the twists, but we regard it as the degree of the
K
-theory.)

By definition, the Borel equivariant cohomology
H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is the usual cohomology
H^{n}(EG\!\times_{G}\!

X;\mathbb{Z})
of the Borel construction
EG\times_{G}X
, which is the quotient of
EG\times X
by the diagonal
G
-action, where
EG
is the total space of the universal
G
-bundle
EG\to BG
. Associated to the Borel construction is the fibration
X\to EG\times_{G}X\to BG
, and hence the Leray–Serre spectral sequence
E_{r}^{p,q}
that converges to the graded quotient of a filtration

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})\supset F^{1}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})\supset F^{2}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})\supset\cdots\supset F^{n+1}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})=0.

One can interpret
F^{p}H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})\subset H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
geometrically in the classification of twists, and there are four types (see Section
3
for details):

(i)

Twists which can be represented by group
2
-cocycles of
G
with coefficients in the trivial
G
-module
U(1)
. These twists are classified by
F^{3}H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.

(ii)

Twists which can be represented by group
2
-cocycles of
G
with coefficients in the group
C(X,U(1))
of
U(1)
-valued functions on
X
regarded as a (right)
G
-module by pull-back. These twists are classified by
F^{2}H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.

(iii)

Twists which can be represented by central extensions of the groupoid
X/\!/G
. These twists are classified by
F^{1}H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.

(iv)

Twists of general type, classified by
F^{0}H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})=H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.

The equivariant twists on
T^{d}
arising from quantum systems on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, to be explained in Section
2
, belong to
F^{2}H^{3}_{P}(T^{d};\mathbb{Z})
with
P
the point group of a
d
-dimensional space group
S
, and so are the twists considered in
[
27
]
. Now, the mathematical issue is whether the twists arising in this way cover all the possibilities or not. The present paper answers this question in the case of
d=2
by a theorem (Theorem
1.1
).

To state the theorem, let
S
be a
2
-dimensional space group, which is also known as a
2
-dimensional crystallographic group, a plane symmetry group, a wallpaper group, and so on. It is a subgroup of the Euclidean group
\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes{\rm O}(2)
of isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and is an extension of a finite group
P\subset{\rm O}(2)
called the point group by a rank
2

\Pi\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
of translations of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c}1&\ \longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}^{2}&\ \longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes{\rm O}(2)&\ \longrightarrow&{\rm O}(2)&\ \longrightarrow&1\\
&&\cup&&\cup&&\cup&&\\
1&\ \longrightarrow&\Pi&\ \longrightarrow&S&\ \longrightarrow&P&\ \longrightarrow&1.\end{array}

Being a normal subgroup of

is preserved by the action of

\mathbb{R}^{2}
through the inclusion

and the standard left action of

on

that we will consider. Since

is a finite subgroup of

, it is the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
of order

or the dihedral group

of degree

and order

. The classification of

-dimensional space groups has long been known, and there are

[

]
, which we label following

]
. Notice that some space groups share the same point group action on

, and there arise
13
distinct finite group actions on the torus. These actions realize essentially all the possible finite subgroups in the mapping class group of the torus
[
20
]
, which is isomorphic to

as is well known
[

.

Theorem
1.1
follows from case by case computations of the equivariant cohomology
H^{3}_{P}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
and the Leray–Serre spectral sequence. Roughly, there are three methods according to the nature of the point group actions: The first method is applied to the cases where the torus
T^{2}
is the product of circles with
P
-actions, i.e., the cases of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-actions arising from
p2
and
pm/pg
. In these cases, the equivariant cohomology is computed by means of the splitting of the Gysin exact sequence, as detailed in
[
10
]
. The second method is applied to the cases where the point group has no element of order
3
. In these cases, the torus
T^{2}
admits an equivariant stable splitting. As a result, the equivariant cohomology of
T^{2}
admits the corresponding splitting, and the Leray–Serre spectral sequence turns out to be trivial. Finally, the third method is applied to the remaining cases. In these cases, we take a
P
-CW decomposition of
T^{2}
to compute the equivariant cohomology by using the Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence and the exact sequence for a pair, and then study the Leray–Serre spectral sequence. In principle, the third method is the most basic, and hence is applied to all the cases. However, to simplify the computations, we use other methods.

These computations contain enough information to determine the equivariant cohomology
H^{n}_{P}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
, (

) of the torus with the actions of the possible finite subgroups in the mapping class group
{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

As is mentioned, Atiyah’s
KR
-theory is also applied to the classification of topological insulators. The symmetry of
KR
-theory however concerns
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-actions only, and its use is limited to rather simple cases. To take more general symmetries into account, Freed and Moore introduced a

-theory which unifies
KR
-theory and equivariant

-theory
[
8
]
. Their

-theory is defined for a space
X
with an action of a compact Lie group
G
equipped with a homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The
K
-theory of Freed–Moore reduces to the
G
-equivariant
K
-theory if
\phi
is trivial, and to the
KR
-theory if
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\phi
non-trivial. There also exists the notion of twists for the Freed–Moore
K
-theory. A computation of the twisted Freed–Moore
K
-theory is carried out in
[
27
]
, leading to the discovery of a novel
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
-phase.

The knowledge about the twists of the Freed–Moore
K
-theory has therefore potential importance to condensed matter physics as well, and the present paper provides it also in the case where
X
is the torus
T^{2}
and
G
is the point group
P
of a
2
-dimensional space group. Notice that the classification of the twists for the Freed–Moore
K
-theory parallels that of the twists for equivariant
K
-theory (actually a generalization). In general, the graded twists are classified by
H^{1}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})
and the ungraded twists by
H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})
. Here
\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}
denotes a local system for the Borel equivariant cohomology associated to the
G
-module
\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}
such that its underlying group is
\mathbb{Z}
and
G
acts via
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The cohomology group
H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})
also admits a filtration

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\supset F^{1}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\supset F^{2}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\supset\cdots\supset F^{n+1}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})=0.

The associated graded quotient is computed by the Leray–Serre spectral sequence, and the subgroups
F^{p}H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\subset H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})
have geometric interpretations as well (Proposition
5.1
).

To state our results in the ‘twisted’ case, we introduce the following definition for the point group
P
of a
2
-dimensional space group
S
that admits a non-trivial homomorphism
\phi\colon P\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

•

In the cases of
p2
,
p4
and
p6
, the point group
P
is the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{2m}=\langle C\,|\,C^{2m}\rangle
of even order. We write
\phi_{1}\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for the unique non-trivial homomorphism given by
\phi_{1}(C)=-1
.

•

In the other case, the point group

2
From quantum systems to twisted
\boldsymbol{K}
-theory

We here illustrate how twisted equivariant
K
-theory arises from a quantum system with symmetry, mainly based on a formulation in
[
8
]
. (We refer the reader to
[
29
]
for a
C^{*}
-algebraic approach.)

2.1
Setting

Let us consider the following mathematical setting:

•

A lattice
\Pi\subset\Pi\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
of rank
d
.

•

A subgroup
S
of the Euclidean group
\mathbb{R}^{d}\rtimes{\rm O}(d)
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is an extension of a finite group
P\subset{\rm O}(d)
by
\Pi
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c}1&\ \longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}^{d}&\ \longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}^{d}\rtimes{\rm O}(d)&\ \longrightarrow&{\rm O}(d)&\ \longrightarrow&1\\
&&\cup&&\cup&&\cup&&\\
1&\ \longrightarrow&\Pi&\ \longrightarrow&S&\ \overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}&P&\ \longrightarrow&1.\end{array}

•

A unitary representation
U\colon P\to U(V)
on a finite-dimensional Hermitian vector space
V
.

The group
S
is nothing but a
d
-dimensional space group
, and
P
is called the
point group
of
S
. When
S
is the semi-direct product of
P
and
\Pi
, it is called
symmorphic
, otherwise
nonsymmorphic
.

Based on the mathematical setting above, we can introduce a quantum system on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which has
S
as its symmetry and
V
as its internal freedom:

•

The ‘quantum Hilbert space’ consisting of ‘wave functions’ is the
L^{2}
-space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
, on which
g\in S
acts by

\psi(x)\mapsto(\rho(g)\psi)(x)=U(\pi(g))\psi(g^{-1}x)
.

•

The ‘Hamiltonian’ is a self-adjoint operator
H
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
invariant under the
S
-action:
H\circ\rho(g)=\rho(g)\circ H
. A typical form of
H
is
H=\Delta+\Phi
, where
\Delta=\sum\partial^{2}/\partial x_{i}^{2}
is the Laplacian and
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathrm{End}(V)
is a potential term.

2.2
Bloch transformation

Even if the Hamiltonian
H
is invariant under the translation of
\Pi
, a solution
\psi
to the ‘time-independent Schrödinger equation’
H\psi=E\psi
with
E\in\mathbb{R}
is not necessarily
S
-invariant. The so-called ‘Bloch transformation’ allows us to deal with such a situation.

Let
\hat{\Pi}=\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi,U(1))
denote the Pontryagin dual of the lattice
\Pi
, which is often called the ‘Brillouin torus’ in condensed matter physics. We define the space
L^{2}_{\Pi}(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
by

\displaystyle L^{2}_{\Pi}\big(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big)=\big\{\hat{\psi}\in L^{2}\big(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big)\,|\,\hat{\psi}(\hat{k},x+m)=\hat{k}(m)\hat{\psi}(\hat{k},x)\ (m\in\Pi)\}.

We also define transformations
\hat{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mathcal{B}
, inverse to each other:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{B}}\colon\ L^{2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big)\longrightarrow L^{2}_{\Pi}\big(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big),\qquad

\displaystyle(\hat{\mathcal{B}}\psi)\big(\hat{k},x\big)=\sum_{n\in\Pi}\hat{k}(n)^{-1}\psi(x+n),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\colon\ L^{2}_{\Pi}\big(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big)\longrightarrow L^{2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big),\qquad

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}\hat{\psi})(x)=\int_{\hat{k}\in\hat{\Pi}}\hat{\psi}\big(\hat{k},x\big)d\hat{k}.

As is described in
[
8
]
, the space
L^{2}_{\Pi}(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
can be identified with the space
L^{2}(\hat{\Pi},\mathcal{E}\otimes V)
of
L^{2}
-sections of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}\otimes V\to\hat{\Pi}
. The infinite-dimensional vector bundle
\mathcal{E}\to\hat{\Pi}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\bigcup_{\hat{k}\in\hat{\Pi}}L^{2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi,\mathcal{L}|_{\{\hat{k}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi}\big),

where
\mathcal{L}\to\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
is the Poincaré line bundle, the quotient of the product line bundle
\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}\to\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
by the following
\Pi
-action

\displaystyle\Pi\times\big(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}\big)\longrightarrow\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{C},\qquad\big(m,\hat{k},x,z\big)\mapsto\big(\hat{k},x+m,\hat{k}(m)z\big).

In summary, we get an identification of
L^{2}
-spaces

\displaystyle L^{2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big)\cong L^{2}_{\Pi}\big(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big)\cong L^{2}\big(\hat{\Pi},\mathcal{E}\otimes V\big).

The Hamiltonian
H
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
then induces an operator
\hat{H}
on
L^{2}_{\Pi}(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V)\cong L^{2}(\hat{\Pi},\mathcal{E}\otimes V)
by
\hat{H}\circ\mathcal{\hat{B}}=\mathcal{\hat{B}}\circ H
. If, for instance,
H
is of the form
H=\Delta+\Phi
, then
\hat{H}
preserves the fiber of
\mathcal{E}\otimes V
. Generally, this is a consequence of the translation invariance of the Hamiltonian. When the present quantum system is supposed to be an ‘insulator’, a finite number of discrete spectra of
\hat{H}(\hat{k})
would be confined to a compact region in
\mathbb{R}
as
\hat{k}\in\hat{\Pi}
varies. Then the corresponding eigenfunctions form a finite rank subbundle
E\subset\mathcal{E}\otimes V
, called the ‘Bloch bundle’. The
K
-class of this vector bundle
E\to\hat{\Pi}
is regarded as an invariant of the quantum system under study.

2.3
Nonsymmorphic group and twisted
\boldsymbol{K}
-theory

We now take the symmetry into account. From the extension
1\to\Pi\to S\overset{\pi}{\to}P\to 1
, we can associate a twisted
P
-equivariant vector bundle on
\hat{\Pi}
to the
S
-module
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
. This is a version of the so-called ‘Mackey machine’.

Recall that the Euclidean group
\mathbb{R}^{d}\rtimes{\rm O}(d)
is the semi-direct product of the orthogonal group
{\rm O}(d)
and the group of translations
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence a collection of representatives
\{s_{p}\}_{p\in P}
of
p\in P\cong S/\Pi
in
S
is expressed as
s_{p}=(a_{p},p)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\rtimes{\rm O}(d)
by means of a map
a\colon P\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
we put

\displaystyle\nu(p_{1},p_{2})=a_{p_{1}}+p_{1}a_{p_{2}}-a_{p_{1}p_{2}}.

Since
\Pi\subset S
is normal, the action of
P\subset{\rm O}(d)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
preserves
\Pi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then we have
\nu(p_{1},p_{2})\in\Pi
, and
\nu\colon P\times P\to\Pi
is a group
2
-cocycle of
P
with values in
\Pi
regarded as a left
P
-module through the action
m\mapsto pm
of
p\in P
on
m\in\Pi
. This group
2
-cocycle measures the failure for
S
to be symmorphic.

By means of the
S
-action
\rho
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
, we define an ‘action’ of
p\in P
by

\displaystyle\rho(p)\colon\ L^{2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big)\longrightarrow L^{2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big),\qquad\rho(p)=\rho((a_{p},p)),

whose explicit formula for
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
is given by
(\rho(p)\psi)(x)=U(p)\psi(p^{-1}x+a_{p^{-1}})
. The Bloch transformation then induces the following ‘action’ of
P
,

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(p)\colon\ L^{2}_{\Pi}\big(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big)\to L^{2}_{\Pi}\big(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V\big),\qquad\hat{\rho}(p)\circ\hat{\mathcal{B}}=\hat{\mathcal{B}}\circ\rho(p),

whose explicit formula for
\hat{\psi}\in L^{2}_{\Pi}(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
is

(\hat{\rho}(p)\hat{\psi})(\hat{k},x)=U(p)\hat{\psi}(p^{-1}\hat{k},p^{-1}x+a_{p^{-1}})
.
Here the left
P
-action on
\hat{\Pi}
is defined by
(p\hat{k})(m)=\hat{k}(p^{-1}m)
, where
p\in P
acts on
m\in\Pi
through the inclusion
P\subset{\rm O}(d)
and the left action of
{\rm O}(d)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Notice that
\rho
and
\hat{\rho}
can be honest actions of
P
in the case of symmorphic
S
, but not in the case of nonsymmorphic
S
, for the usual composition rule is violated:

\displaystyle\big(\hat{\rho}(p_{1})\big(\hat{\rho}(p_{2})\hat{\psi}\big)\big)\big(\hat{k},\xi\big)=\big(p_{2}^{-1}p_{1}^{-1}\hat{k}\big)\big(\nu\big(p_{2}^{-1},p_{1}^{-1}\big)\big)\big(\hat{\rho}(p_{1}p_{2})\hat{\psi}\big)\big(\hat{k},\xi\big).

To interpret the ‘action’
\hat{\rho}(p)
in terms of the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}\otimes V
through
L^{2}_{\Pi}(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V)\cong L^{2}(\hat{\Pi},\mathcal{E}\otimes V)
, recall that the fiber of
\mathcal{E}\otimes V
at
\hat{k}\in\hat{\Pi}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}|_{\hat{k}}\otimes V=L^{2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi,\mathcal{L}|_{\{\hat{k}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi}\otimes V\big),

and
\hat{\psi}\in L^{2}_{\Pi}(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
corresponds to the following section
\Psi\in L^{2}(\hat{\Pi},\mathcal{E}\otimes V)
:

\displaystyle\Psi\big(\hat{k}\big)\colon\ \mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}|_{\{\hat{k}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi}\otimes V,\qquad x\mapsto\big[\hat{k},x,\hat{\psi}(\hat{k},x)\big].

Define for
p\in P
and
\hat{k}\in\hat{\Pi}
a linear map

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}\big(p;\hat{k}\big)\colon\ \mathcal{E}|_{\hat{k}}\otimes V\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}|_{p\hat{k}}\otimes V

by the assignment of the sections

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}\big(p;\hat{k}\big)\big(\big[x\mapsto\big[\hat{k},x,\hat{\psi}(\hat{k},x)\big]\big]\big)=\big[x\mapsto\big[p\hat{k},x,U(p)\hat{\psi}\big(\hat{k},p^{-1}x+a_{p^{-1}}\big)\big]\big].

These maps constitute a vector bundle map
\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}(p)\colon\mathcal{E}\otimes V\to\mathcal{E}\otimes V
covering the action
\hat{k}\mapsto p\hat{k}
on
\hat{\Pi}

\displaystyle\begin{CD}\mathcal{E}\otimes V@>{\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}(p)}>{}>\mathcal{E}\otimes V\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\hat{\Pi}@>{p}>{}>\hat{\Pi}.\end{CD}

This is a
\tau
-twisted
P
-action, in the sense that the formula

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}\big(p_{1};p_{2}\hat{k}\big)\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}\big(p_{2};\hat{k}\big)\xi=\tau\big(p_{1},p_{2};\hat{k}\big)\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}\big(p_{1}p_{2};\hat{k}\big)\xi

holds for
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
,
\hat{k}\in\hat{\Pi}
and
\xi\in\mathcal{E}|_{\hat{k}}\otimes V
. Here
\tau\colon P\times P\times\hat{\Pi}\to U(1)
is defined by

\displaystyle\tau(p_{1},p_{2};\hat{k})=\hat{k}\big(\nu\big(p_{2}^{-1},p_{1}^{-1}\big)\big),

and is regarded as a group
2
-cocycle of
P
with its coefficients in the group
C(\hat{\Pi},U(1))
of
U(1)
-valued functions on
\hat{\Pi}
thought of as a right
P
-module through the pull-back under the left action
\hat{k}\mapsto p\hat{k}
of
p\in P
on
\hat{k}\in\hat{\Pi}
. The map
\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}(p)
on the vector bundle induces the transformation on the sections

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}(p)\colon\ L^{2}\big(\hat{\Pi},\mathcal{E}\otimes V\big)\longrightarrow L^{2}\big(\hat{\Pi},\mathcal{E}\otimes V\big)

by
(\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}(p)\Psi)(\hat{k})=\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}(p;p^{-1}\hat{k})\Psi(p^{-1}\hat{k})
. One can verify that: if
\Psi\in L^{2}(\hat{\Pi},\mathcal{E}\otimes V)
corresponds to
\hat{\psi}\in L^{2}_{\Pi}(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
, then
\rho_{\mathcal{E}\otimes V}(p)\Psi
corresponds to
\hat{\rho}(p)\hat{\psi}
. Hence the ‘action’
\hat{\rho}(p)
on
L^{2}_{\Pi}(\hat{\Pi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},V)\cong L^{2}(\hat{\Pi},\mathcal{E}\otimes V)
agrees with the one induced from the
\tau
-twisted
P
-action on
\mathcal{E}\otimes V
.

Now, under the assumption that
\hat{H}
describes an insulator, the Bloch bundle
E\subset\mathcal{E}\otimes V
inherits a
\tau
-twisted
P
-action from
\mathcal{E}\otimes V
. This is a consequence of the invariance of the Hamiltonian under the space group action. Therefore the Bloch bundle, being a
\tau
-twisted
P
-equivariant vector bundle of finite rank, defines a class in the
\tau
-twisted
P
-equivariant
K
-theory
K^{\tau+0}_{P}(\hat{\Pi})
, which is regarded as an invariant of the insulating system under study.

As is obvious from the construction, we can apply the construction of the group
2
-cocycle
\tau
to symmorphic space groups. However, in the symmorphic case, the cocycle
\nu
and hence
\tau
can be trivialized.

So far a linear representation of
P
on
V
is considered. We can relax this representation to be a projective representation of
P
with its group
2
-cocycle
\omega\colon P\times P\to U(1)
. In this case, the resulting Bloch bundle defines a class in the twisted equivariant
K
-theory
K^{\tau+\omega+0}_{P}(\hat{\Pi})
.

Remark 2.1
.

The phase factor in the composition rule of
\hat{\rho}
,

\displaystyle\tau_{R}\big(\hat{k};p_{1},p_{2}\big)=\big(p_{2}^{-1}p_{1}^{-1}\hat{k}\big)\big(\nu\big(p_{2}^{-1},p_{1}^{-1}\big)\big)=\hat{k}\big(p_{1}p_{2}\nu\big(p_{2}^{-1},p_{1}^{-1}\big)\big)

defines a group
2
-cocycle of
P
with coefficients in
C(\hat{\Pi},U(1))
, when regarded as a left
P
-module by the right action
\hat{k}\mapsto\hat{k}p=p^{-1}\hat{k}
of
p\in P
on
\hat{k}\in\hat{\Pi}
. The
2
-cocycles
\tau
and
\tau_{R}
are related by
\tau_{R}(\hat{k};p_{1},p_{2})=\tau(p_{1},p_{2};(p_{1}p_{2})^{-1}\hat{k})
. This extends to a cochain bijection of group cochains with coefficients in the left/right
P
-modules
C(\hat{\Pi},U(1))
. Thus,
\tau
and
\tau_{R}
have cohomologically the same information. We also remark that
\tau
and
\tau_{R}
are respectively cohomologous to the following

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}\big(p_{1},p_{2};\hat{k}\big)=\big(p_{1}p_{2}\hat{k}\big)(\nu(p_{1},p_{2}))^{-1},\qquad\tau^{\prime}_{R}\big(\hat{k};p_{1},p_{2}\big)=\hat{k}(\nu(p_{1},p_{2}))^{-1}.

Remark 2.2
.

Given a homomorphism

, we can impose that the Hamiltonian

and the symmetry
\rho(g)
with

are graded commutative,

H\circ\rho(g)=c(\pi(g))\rho(g)\circ H
. Then the quantum system with symmetry in question leads to an element of the twisted equivariant

. It should be noticed that the construction of the element uses Karoubi’s formulation of

[
13
]
and requires a reference quantum system. These points of discussion, which will not be detailed in this paper, are implicit in the absence of the graded twist.

Remark 2.3
.

A group
2
-cocycle

2.4
Actions of the point group on the torus

To close Section
2
, we compare some natural actions of the point group on the torus: Let
S
be a
d
-dimensional space group,
\Pi
its lattice, and
P
its point group.

(A)

By the inclusion
P\subset{\rm O}(d)
and the standard left action of
{\rm O}(d)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the point group
P
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, preserving
\Pi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence the left action of
P
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
descends to give a left action of
P
on the torus
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
.

(B)

By the action (A), the point group
P
acts on the Pontryagin dual
\hat{\Pi}=\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi,U(1))
of
\Pi
from the left: For
p\in P
and
\hat{k}\in\hat{\Pi}
, we define
p\hat{k}\in\hat{\Pi}
by
(p\hat{k})(m)=\hat{k}(p^{-1}m)
for all
m\in\Pi
.

(C)

By the inclusion
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\rtimes{\rm O}(d)
and the standard left action of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\rtimes{\rm O}(d)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the space group
S
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The subgroup
\Pi\subset S
preserves
\Pi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so that the point group
P\cong S/\Pi
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
.

The action (A) is what we consider in our main results, and the action (B) is relevant to quantum systems as reviewed in this section.

On the one hand, the actions (A) and (B) clearly fix
0\in\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
and
0\in\hat{\Pi}
, respectively, where we regard
\hat{\Pi}
as
\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
via
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\cong U(1)
and
0\in\hat{\Pi}
stands for the trivial homomorphism. On the other hand, if
(a_{p},p)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\rtimes{\rm O}(d)
is a lift of
p\in P\subset{\rm O}(d)
, then the action of
p\in P
on
k\in\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
in (C) admits the description
k\mapsto pk+a_{p}
. If
S
is symmorphic, then we can choose
a_{p}
to be in
\Pi
. In this case, the actions (A) and (C) are equivalent. However, if
S
is nonsymmorphic, then
a_{p}
cannot be in
\Pi
. Thus, in this case, the action of
p\in P
does not fix any point on
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
, so that the actions (A) and (C) are not equivalent. For example, in the case of
pg
, the action of
P=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
on the
2
-dimensional torus is free, and its quotient is the Klein bottle.

To compare the actions (A) and (B), we need to identify
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
with
\hat{\Pi}=\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
, which are topologically
d
-dimensional tori. In general, such an identification may not be unique. A way to implement the identification is to choose a basis
\{v_{j}\}
of the lattice
\Pi\cong\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. This choice induces the following identifications of tori inverse to each other:

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi,\qquad

\displaystyle\hat{k}\mapsto\sum_{j}\hat{k}(v_{j})v_{j},

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi\longrightarrow\hat{\Pi},\qquad

\displaystyle\sum_{j}k_{j}v_{j}\mapsto\bigg[\sum_{j}m_{j}v_{j}\mapsto\sum_{j}m_{j}k_{j}\bigg].

With this identification of tori, the left
P
-action on
\hat{\Pi}
in (B) induces a right
P
-action on
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
. Considering the action of
p^{-1}
instead of
p
, we finally get a left action of
P
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
, induced from (B) and the identification
\hat{\Pi}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
. In general, this left action of
P
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
induced from (B) is not equivalent to the action (A). In the
2
-dimensional case, their relationship is as follows:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
S
be a
2
-dimensional space group,
P_{S}
its point group,
\Pi_{S}
its lattice, and
\hat{\Pi}_{S}
the Pontryagin dual of
\Pi_{S}
.

(a)

We choose a basis
\{v_{j}\}
of
\Pi_{S}
to identify
\hat{\Pi}_{S}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Pi_{S}
, and let the action in
(B)
induce an action of
P_{S}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Pi_{S}
. Then, up to equivalence, this action is independent of the choice of the basis.

(b)

If
S
is not
p3m1
or
p31m
, then the action of
P_{S}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Pi_{S}
induced from
(B)
is equivalent to the action of
P_{S}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Pi_{S}
in
(A)
.

(c)

If
S
is
p3m1

(
respectively
p31m
)
, then the action of
P_{S}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Pi_{S}
induced from
(B)
is equivalent to the action of
P_{S^{\prime}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Pi_{S^{\prime}}
in
(A)
, where
S^{\prime}
is
p31m

(
respectively
p3m1
)
.

We remark that the space groups
p3m1
and
p31m
share the same lattice and the same point group, as can be seen in Appendix

. Hence we have
P_{S}=P_{S^{\prime}}
and
\Pi_{S}=\Pi_{S^{\prime}}
in the third item in the lemma above.

Proof.

Considering the action
(A)
, we define
\psi(p)_{\ell j}\in\mathbb{Z}
by
pv_{j}=\sum_{\ell}\psi(p)_{\ell j}v_{\ell}
, and a homomorphism
\psi\colon P_{S}\to{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
by
\psi(p)=(\psi(p)_{\ell j})
. Since
P_{S}
is the point group of a
2
-dimensional space group
S
, the homomorphism
\psi
is injective and its image
\psi(P_{S})
is a finite subgroup of
{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. Let
S^{\prime}
be another
2
-dimensional space group with its point group
P^{\prime}
. Choosing a basis of its lattice
\Pi^{\prime}
, we similarly get from the action (A) a homomorphism
\psi^{\prime}\colon P_{S^{\prime}}\to{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. If the images
\psi(P_{S})
and
\psi^{\prime}(P_{S^{\prime}})
are conjugate to each other in
{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, then the actions of
P_{S}
and
P_{S^{\prime}}
in (A) are equivalent. The action of
P_{S}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Pi_{S}
induced from (B) also yields an associated homomorphism

. This homomorphism turns out to be the transpose inverse
{}^{t}\psi^{-1}\colon P_{S}\to{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, which is again injective and defines a finite subgroup
{}^{t}\psi^{-1}(P_{S})\subset{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. If we alter the basis
\{v_{j}\}
, then

changes by a conjugation of a matrix in

. Thus, up to conjugations, the image
{}^{t}\psi^{-1}(P_{S})\subset{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
is independent of the choice of
\{v_{j}\}
, showing (a). Now we can directly verify (b) and (c), by computing the homomorphism
\psi
based on the explicit basis in Appendix, and comparing the images
\psi(P_{S})
and
{}^{t}\psi^{-1}(P_{S})
in
{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

Another way of identifying
\hat{\Pi}
with
\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi
is to choose a bilinear form
\langle\ ,\ \rangle\colon\Pi\times\Pi\to\mathbb{Z}
. We assume that this form is non-degenerate in the sense that the matrix

of

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Pi\longrightarrow\hat{\Pi}=\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}),\qquad k\mapsto[m\mapsto\langle m,k\rangle].

If the bilinear form is

agrees with the action (B) on
\hat{\Pi}
under the induced identification

. For the

3
The Leray–Serre spectral sequence and twists

This section gives a geometric interpretation of the filtration of
H^{3}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
for the Leray–Serre spectral sequence through types of twists. This is carried out by identifying the Leray–Serre spectral sequence with another natural spectral sequence which computes the Borel equivariant cohomology.

Throughout this section, we assume that
G
is a finite group acting from the left on a ‘reasonable’ space
X
, such as a locally contractible, paracompact and regular topological space as in
[
7
]
, or a
G
-CW complex
[
19
]
.

3.1
Spectral sequences

The Borel equivariant cohomology
H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is defined to be the (singular) cohomology of the quotient space
EG\times_{G}X
of
EG\times X
under the diagonal
G
-action
(\xi,x)\mapsto(\xi g,g^{-1}x)
, where
EG
is the total space of the universal
G
-bundle
EG\to BG
. Associated to the fibration
X\to EG\times_{G}X\to BG
is the Leray–Serre spectral sequence

\displaystyle E_{r}^{p,q}\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})

converging to the graded quotient of a filtration

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})=F^{0}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})\supset F^{1}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})\supset\cdots\supset F^{n+1}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})=0,

that is,
E_{\infty}^{p,q}=F^{p}H^{p+q}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})/F^{p+1}H^{p+q}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. The
E_{2}
-term is given by the group cohomology of
G

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;H^{q}(X;\mathbb{Z})),

where the coefficient
H^{q}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is regarded as a right
G
-module by the pull-back action. As a convention of this paper, the group of
p
-cochains with values in a right
G
-module
M
is denoted by
C^{p}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;M)=C(G^{p},M)=\{\tau\colon G^{p}\to M\}
, and the coboundary
\partial\colon C^{p}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;M)\to C^{p+1}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;M)
is given by

\displaystyle(\partial\tau)(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p+1})=\tau(g_{2},\ldots,g_{p+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{i}\tau(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{p+1})

\displaystyle\hphantom{(\partial\tau)(g_1, \ldots, g_{p+1})=}{}+(-1)^{p+1}\tau(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p})g_{p+1}.

As an application of the spectral sequence, we can obtain an identification
H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\mathbb{Z})
. (We also have
H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{n-1}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;U(1))
for
n\geq 2
by the so-called exponential exact sequence.)

For a better geometric understanding of the spectral sequence, let us start with the fact that the Borel equivariant cohomology
H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to the cohomology
H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\mathbb{Z})
of a
simplicial space

G^{\bullet}\times X
with its coefficients in the constant sheaf
\mathbb{Z}
. This is a consequence of a more general theorem about simplicial space (see
[
6
]
for example) together with the fact that the geometric realization
\lvert G^{\bullet}\times X\rvert
of
G^{\bullet}\times X
is identified with
EG\times_{G}X
.

The
simplicial space

G^{\bullet}\times X
is associated to the left
G
-action on
X
, and consists of a sequence of spaces
\{G^{p}\times X\}_{p\geq 0}
together with the
face map

\partial_{i}\colon G^{p}\times X\to G^{p-1}\times X
,
i=0,\ldots,p
, and the
degeneracy map

s_{i}\colon G^{p}\times X\to G^{p+1}\times X
,
i=0,\ldots,p
, given by

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p},x)=\begin{cases}(g_{2},\ldots,g_{p},x),&i=0,\\
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{p},x),&i=1,\ldots,p-1,\\
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p-1},g_{p}x),&i=p,\end{cases}

\displaystyle s_{i}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p},x)=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1},1,g_{i},\ldots,g_{p},x).

The cohomology
H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\mathbb{Z})
is then defined to be the total cohomology of the double complex
(C^{i}(G^{j}\times X;\mathbb{Z}),\delta,\partial)
, where
(C^{i}(G^{j}\times X;\mathbb{Z}),\delta)
is the complex computing the cohomology of
G^{j}\times X
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
and
\partial\colon C^{i}(G^{j}\times X;\mathbb{Z})\to C^{i}(G^{j+1}\times X;\mathbb{Z})
is
\partial=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{j+1}(-1)^{i}\partial_{i}^{*}
. The double complex admits a natural filtration
\{\oplus_{j\geq p}C^{i}(G^{j}\times X;\mathbb{Z})\}_{p\geq 0}
. The associated spectral sequence agrees with the Leray–Serre spectral sequence
E_{r}^{p,q}
, since
G
is finite.

Now, let us consider the standard exponential exact sequence of sheaves on the simplicial space

0\to\mathbb{Z}\to\underline{\mathbb{R}}\to\underline{U(1)}\to 0
, where
\underline{\mathbb{R}}
consists of the sheaf of
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions on
G^{p}\times X
and
\underline{U(1)}
consists of the sheaf of
U(1)
-valued functions on
G^{p}\times X
. As in
[
9
, Lemma 4.4]
, we can readily show that
H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\underline{\mathbb{R}})=0
for
n>0
. This vanishing together with the associated long exact sequence leads to the following isomorphism for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\underline{U(1)})\cong H^{n+1}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\mathbb{Z}).

The cohomology
H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\underline{U(1)})
can be defined exactly in the same way as in the case of
H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\mathbb{Z})
by using a double complex. Therefore we have a spectral sequence

\displaystyle{}^{\prime}E^{p,q}_{r}\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\underline{U(1)})

converging to the graded quotient of a filtration

\displaystyle{}^{\prime}F^{0}H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\underline{U(1)})=H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\underline{U(1)})\supset{}^{\prime}F^{1}H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\underline{U(1)})\supset\cdots,

whose
E_{2}
-term is

\displaystyle{}^{\prime}E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;H^{q}(X;\underline{U(1)})),

where
H^{q}(X;\underline{U(1)})
is regarded as a right
G
-module by pull-back. It is clear that
H^{0}(X;\underline{U(1)})\cong C(X,U(1))
and
H^{n}(X;\underline{U(1)})\cong H^{n+1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
for
n\geq 1
. Since
{}^{\prime}E_{2}^{p,q}
involves the group cohomology with coefficients in
C(X,U(1))
, its computation seems to be more complicated than that of
E_{2}^{p,q}
. However, the spectral sequence is useful from a geometric viewpoint, as will be seen shortly.

In view of the exponential exact sequence, the filtrations of
H^{n+1}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\underline{U(1)})
for
n\geq 1
are related as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c@{}c}H^{n}(G^{\bullet}\times X;\underline{U(1)})&\ =&{}^{\prime}F^{0}H^{n}&\ \supset&{}^{\prime}F^{1}H^{n}&\ \supset&\cdots&\supset&{}^{\prime}F^{p}H^{n}&\supset&\cdots\\
\parallel&&&&\downarrow&&&&\downarrow&&\\
H^{n+1}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z})&\ =&F^{0}H^{n+1}&\ \supset&F^{1}H^{n+1}&\ \supset&\cdots&\supset&F^{p}H^{n+1}&\ \supset&\cdots.\end{array}

The spectral sequences are related by a map
{}^{\prime}E^{p,q}_{r}\to E^{p,q+1}_{r}
. In particular, the
E_{2}
-terms
{}^{\prime}E_{2}^{p,0}
and
E_{2}^{p,1}
are related by a map
C(X,U(1))\to H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
fitting into the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to H^{0}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to C(X,\mathbb{R})\to C(X,U(1))\to H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

As is mentioned, because of the isomorphism
H^{q}(X;\underline{U(1)})\cong H^{q+1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, we have
{}^{\prime}E_{2}^{p,q}\cong E_{2}^{p,q+1}
for
q\geq 1
. A more detailed relation between these spectral sequences will be given later under some hypotheses.

3.2
Twists

We here recall the definition of twists for equivariant
K
-theory in
[
7
,
8
]
for the convenience of the reader. We mainly consider ungraded twists, and refer the reader to
[
7
]
for the details about graded twists (see also Remark
3.7
). Recall that associated to an action of a finite group
G
on a space
X
is the
groupoid

X/\!/G
such that its set of objects is
X
and the set of morphisms is
G\times X
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
central extension

(L,\tau)
of the groupoid
X/\!/G
consists of the following data:

•

a Hermitian line bundle
L\to G\times X
, which we write
L_{g}\to X
for the restriction to
\{g\}\times X
for each
g\in G
,

•

In

3.3
Comparison of two spectral sequences

The relation between the spectral sequences
E_{r}^{p,q}
and
{}^{\prime}E_{r}^{p,q}
can be made more clear under a simple assumption. To present this here, we begin with a key lemma: Recall that the exponential exact sequence of sheaves on
X
induces a natural exact sequence of right
G
-modules

\displaystyle 0\to H^{0}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to C(X,\mathbb{R})\to C(X,U(1))\to H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

Let us fold this into a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to C(X,\mathbb{R})/H^{0}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to C(X,U(1))\to H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

In general, this does not split as an exact sequence of
G
-modules. (Such an example is provided by the circle
S^{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the action of
D_{2}\subset{\rm O}(2)
.) Notice that if
X
is path connected, then
H^{0}(X;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

If a finite group
G
acts on a compact and path connected space
X
fixing a point
\mathrm{pt}\in X
, then the following exact sequence of
G
-modules splits

\displaystyle 0\to C(X,\mathbb{R})/\mathbb{Z}\to C(X,U(1))\to H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

Proof.

For notational convenience, we use the identification
U(1)\cong\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
in this proof. Let
C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R})\subset C(X,\mathbb{R})
be the subgroup consisting of functions taking
0
at
\mathrm{pt}
. The inclusion
\iota\colon\mathrm{pt}\to X
induces an isomorphism of
G
-modules

\displaystyle C(X,\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathbb{R},\qquad f\mapsto(f-\iota^{*}f,\iota^{*}f).

Similarly, we have an isomorphism
C(X,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})\cong C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})\oplus\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
of
G
-modules. Thus the exact sequence of
G
-modules in question is equivalent to

\displaystyle 0\to C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R})\to C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})\overset{\delta}{\to}H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

Since
X
is supposed to be compact,
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is a free abelian group of finite rank. Let us choose a basis
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{Z}a_{i}
, and also
\varphi_{i}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
such that
\delta\varphi_{i}=a_{i}
and
\varphi_{i}(\mathrm{pt})=0
. Modifying the splitting
a_{i}\mapsto\varphi_{i}
of the exact sequence of
abelian groups
, we construct a splitting of the exact sequence of
G
-modules
, which will complete the proof.

For the modification, we introduce a square matrix
A(g)=(A_{ij}(g))
with integer coefficients to each
g\in G
by
g^{*}a_{i}=\sum_{j}A_{ij}(g)a_{j}
. It holds that
A(gh)=A(g)A(h)
. Because of the exact sequence, there are functions
f^{i}_{g}\in C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R})
such that the following holds in
C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
:

\displaystyle g^{*}\varphi_{i}=\sum_{j}A_{ij}(g)\varphi_{j}+\big(f_{g}^{i}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Z}\big).

This can be expressed as
g^{*}\Phi=A(g)\Phi+F_{g}
by using the vectors
\Phi=(\varphi_{i})
and
F_{g}=(F^{i}_{g})
. It then holds that
F_{gh}=A(g)F_{h}+h^{*}F_{g}
in
C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R})
. Since
A(g)
is invertible, this is equivalent to

\displaystyle A(gh)^{-1}F_{gh}=A(h)^{-1}F_{h}+A(h)^{-1}h^{*}\big(A(g)^{-1}F_{g}\big).

Write
\lvert G\rvert
for the order of
G
, and put
\overline{F}=\frac{1}{\lvert G\rvert}\sum\limits_{g\in G}A(g)^{-1}F_{g}
. Taking the average over
g\in G
in the formula above, we get

\displaystyle\overline{F}=A(h)^{-1}F_{h}+A(h)^{-1}h^{*}\overline{F},

which is equivalent to
F_{g}=A(g)\overline{F}-g^{*}\overline{F}
. Now
g^{*}(\Phi+\overline{F})=A(g)(\Phi+\overline{F})
. Thus, under the expression
\overline{F}=(\overline{f}^{i})
by using
\overline{f}^{i}\in C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R})
, the assignment
a_{i}\mapsto\varphi_{i}+(\overline{f}^{i}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Z})
defines a splitting
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
compatible with the
G
-module structures.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on a compact and path connected space
X
fixing a point
\mathrm{pt}\in X
. Then, for
n\geq 1
, there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;C(X,U(1)))\cong H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;U(1))\oplus H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(G;H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})\big),

where
U(1)
is the trivial
G
-module, and
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is regarded as a
G
-module through the action of
G
on
X
.

Proof.

Lemma
3.8
implies

\displaystyle H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;C(X,U(1)))\cong H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;C(X,\mathbb{R})/\mathbb{Z})\oplus H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(G;H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})\big)

for all
n\geq 1
. By the
G
-module isomorphism
C(X,\mathbb{R})/\mathbb{Z}\cong C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
utilized in Lemma
3.8
, we have

\displaystyle H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;C(X,\mathbb{R})/\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;C(X,\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{R}))\oplus H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}).

Since

is a vector space over

, we can prove the vanishing

for

by an average argument as in
[

.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a compact and path connected space
X
fixing a point
\mathrm{pt}\in X

we have

\displaystyle{}^{\prime}E^{p,0}_{r}\cong E^{p,1}_{r}\oplus E^{p+1,0}_{r},\quad p\geq 1,\qquad{}^{\prime}E^{p,q}_{r}\cong E^{p,q+1}_{r},\quad p\geq 0,q\geq 1.

Proof.

Recall that the exponential exact sequence induces the connecting homomorphism
\delta\colon H^{q}(X;\underline{U(1)})\to H^{q+1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
and this induces a natural homomorphism
\delta\colon{}^{\prime}E^{p,q}_{r}\to E^{p,q+1}_{r}
compatible with the differentials
{}^{\prime}d_{r}
and

. In the case of
r=2
, the homomorphism
\delta\colon{}^{\prime}E_{2}^{p,q}\to E^{p,q+1}_{2}
is bijective for

and
p\geq 0
, and we have
{}^{\prime}E^{p,0}_{2}\cong E^{p,1}_{2}\oplus E^{p+1,0}_{2}
for

as a consequence of Lemma

. Notice that, under this isomorphism,
\delta\colon{}^{\prime}E^{p,0}_{2}\to E^{p,1}_{2}
for

restricts to the identity on the direct summand

. Note also that

for any

, because

\displaystyle E^{p,0}_{2}=H^{p}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\mathbb{Z})=H^{p}(BG;\mathbb{Z})=H^{p}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})

is a direct summand of

, where

is the reduced cohomology. Thus, for

is the projection onto

and the image of the differential

is in the direct summand

\displaystyle{}^{\prime}E^{p,0}_{3}\cong E^{p,0}_{3}\oplus E^{p,1}_{3},\quad p\geq 1,\qquad{}^{\prime}E^{p,q}_{3}\cong E^{p,q+1}_{3},\quad p\geq 0,q\geq 1.

The calculation above can be repeated inductively on

.
∎

Corollary 3.11

Theorems

4.1

The cohomology

For the point group

of any

, so that

\displaystyle H^{3}_{P}\big(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}\big)=F^{0}H^{3}_{P}\big(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}\big)=F^{1}H^{3}_{P}\big(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}\big).

Note that each point group

\displaystyle F^{3}H^{3}_{P}\big(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}\big)=H^{3}_{P}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})=H^{3}_{\mathrm{group}}(P;\mathbb{Z})=H^{2}_{\mathrm{group}}(P;U(1)).

Then the main task for the proof of Theorem

is to compute

and

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr P&H^{0}_{P}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})&H^{1}_{P}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})&H^{2}_{P}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})&H^{3}_{P}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\tsep{2pt}\bsep{2pt}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{Z}_{n}&\mathbb{Z}&0&\mathbb{Z}_{n}&0\\
\hline\cr D_{n}&\mathbb{Z}&0&\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}&(\mbox{$n$: odd})\\
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\oplus 2}&(\mbox{$n$: even})\end{cases}&\begin{cases}0&(\mbox{$n$: odd})\\

The degree

In the rest of the section, we may use a structure of

as a

-CW complex. In general, for a compact Lie group

We later compute a group cohomology via cohomology of a space:

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that a finite group

acts on a path connected space

fixing at least one point

. Suppose further that

is a CW complex consisting of only cells of dimension less than or equal to

\displaystyle H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(G;H^{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\big)\cong\tilde{H}^{n+1}_{G}(Y;\mathbb{Z}),

where
\tilde{H}^{n+1}_{G}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
stands for the reduced cohomology.

Notice that a

-CW complex is naturally a CW complex.

Proof.

Consider the Leray–Serre spectral sequence

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;H^{q}(Y;\mathbb{Z}))\Longrightarrow H^{*}_{G}(Y;\mathbb{Z}).

Note that
H^{q}(Y;\mathbb{Z})=0
for

. The

-term

must survive into the direct summand

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{p}_{G}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\cong E^{p-1,1}_{\infty}=E^{p-1,1}_{2}\cong H^{p-1}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(G;H^{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\big),

which completes the proof.
∎

We also prepare a simple lemma about group cohomology: Let
G
be a finite group,

a surjective homomorphism, and
\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{Z}_{c}
the

and

. A typical example is a finite subgroup
P\subset{\rm O}(2)
such that
P\not\subset{\rm SO}(2)
with
c
the composition of the inclusion
P\to{\rm O}(2)
and the determinant
{\rm O}(2)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
,
c
and
\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}
be as above. Then,
H^{0}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\tilde{\mathbb{Z}})=0
and
H^{1}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\tilde{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Proof.

For any
n\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\tilde{\mathbb{Z}})=\mathbb{Z}
, its coboundary
\partial n\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
is
(\partial n)(g)=n(1-c(g))
. Thus, the assumption that
c
is surjective implies the vanishing of the
0
th cohomology. The inclusion
\operatorname{Ker}(c)\subset G
induces an injection on
1
-cocycles

\displaystyle Z^{1}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(G;\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}\big)\to Z^{1}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(\operatorname{Ker}(c);\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}\big)=\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Ker}(c),\mathbb{Z})=0.

Thus, given a group
1
-cocycle
\phi\in Z^{1}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\tilde{\mathbb{Z}})
, it holds that
\phi(g)=0
for all
g\in\operatorname{Ker}(c)
. If
g,h\not\in\operatorname{Ker}(c)
, then the cocycle condition
(\partial\phi)(g,h)=0
implies
\phi(g)=\phi(h)
. Therefore
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
is always of the form
\phi(g)=n(1-c(g))/2
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. This provides the identification
Z^{1}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\tilde{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathbb{Z}
as well as
B^{1}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\tilde{\mathbb{Z}})\cong 2\mathbb{Z}
, which completes the proof.
∎

In some cases, the computations of the Leray–Serre spectral sequence are similar, which we summarize as follows:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on the torus
T^{2}
such that:

•

there is a fixed point
\mathrm{pt}\in T^{2}
,

•

the
G
-action does not preserve the orientation of
T^{2}
.

Then the following holds true about the Leray–Serre spectral sequence:

(a)

F^{2}H^{3}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong E_{2}^{2,1}\oplus E_{2}^{3,0}
,

(b)

H^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=n}E_{2}^{p,q}
for
n\leq 2
.

Proof.

In the Leray–Serre spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;H^{q}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}))
, the coefficient in the group cohomology
H^{0}(T^{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}
is identified with the trivial
G
-module, and
H^{2}(T^{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}
with the
G
-module in Lemma
4.2
. Then the relevant
E_{2}
-terms can be summarized as follows:

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr q=3&0&0&0&0&0\\
\hline\cr q=2&0&\mathbb{Z}_{2}&&&\\
\hline\cr q=1&E_{2}^{0,1}&E_{2}^{1,1}&E_{2}^{2,1}&&\tsep{2pt}\bsep{2pt}\\
\hline\cr q=0&E_{2}^{0,0}&E_{2}^{1,0}&E_{2}^{2,0}&E_{2}^{3,0}&E_{2}^{4,0}\tsep{2pt}\bsep{2pt}\\
\hline\cr E_{2}^{p,q}\tsep{2pt}\bsep{2pt}&p=0&p=1&p=2&p=3&p=4\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Since
G
fixes
\mathrm{pt}\in T^{2}
, we have the decomposition
H^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\oplus\tilde{H}^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
, where
\tilde{H}^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
is the reduced cohomology. Therefore the
E_{2}
-term
E_{2}^{n,0}=H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})
must survive into the direct summand
H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})
in
H^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
. This implies that
E_{2}^{n,0}=E_{\infty}^{n,0}
is always a direct summand of the subgroups
F^{p}H^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})\subset H^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
and that
d_{2}\colon E_{2}^{p-2,1}\to E_{2}^{p,0}
is trivial. As a result, we get
E_{2}^{2,1}=E_{\infty}^{2,1}
and the isomorphism (a). Also
E_{2}^{p,q}=E_{\infty}^{p,q}
for
p+q\leq 2
, and the isomorphism (b) follows.
∎

The degeneration of the spectral sequence in the above lemma can be generalized in some cases. For this aim, the key is the following equivariant stable splitting of
T^{2}
(cf.
[
8
, Theorem 11.8]
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose a finite group
G
acts on the torus
T^{2}=S^{1}\times S^{1}
and

•

there is a fixed point
\mathrm{pt}=(x_{0},y_{0})\in T^{2}
under the
G
-action,

•

G
preserves the subspace
S^{1}\vee S^{1}=S^{1}\times\{y_{0}\}\cup\{x_{0}\}\times S^{1}\subset T^{2}
.

Then there is a
G
-equivariant homotopy equivalence

\displaystyle\Sigma T^{2}\simeq\Sigma\big(S^{1}\vee S^{1}\big)\vee\Sigma\big(T^{2}/S^{1}\vee S^{1}\big),

where
\Sigma
stands for the reduced suspension.

Proof.

The argument of the proof of Proposition 4I.1
[
11
, p. 467]
can be applied to our equivariant case.
∎

We remark that the point groups of the
2
-dimensional space groups without elements of order
3
fulfill the assumptions of the lemma above.

Lemma 4.5
.

Under the assumption in Lemma
4.4
, we have the following isomorphism of groups for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}\big(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}\big)\cong H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\oplus\tilde{H}^{n}_{G}\big(S^{1}\vee S^{1};\mathbb{Z}\big)\oplus\tilde{H}^{n}_{G}\big(T^{2}/S^{1}\vee S^{1};\mathbb{Z}\big).

Further, the Leray–Serre spectral sequence for
H^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
degenerates at
E_{2}
and the relevant extension problems are trivial, so that

(a)

F^{2}H^{3}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong E_{2}^{2,1}\oplus E_{2}^{3,0}
,

(b)

H^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=n}E_{2}^{p,q}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

The stable splitting in Lemma
4.4
immediately gives the first isomorphism. For the trivial
G
-space
\mathrm{pt}
, the Leray–Serre spectral sequence clearly degenerates at
E_{2}
, and we have

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(G;H^{0}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\big).

Since
H^{0}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{0}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
as
G
-modules, we get the following identification of the
E_{2}
-term
E_{2}^{n,0}
of the Leray–Serre spectral sequence for
H^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle E_{2}^{n,0}=H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(G;H^{0}\big(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}\big)\big)\cong H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z}).

For the
G
-space
S^{1}\vee S^{1}
, we can see, as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, that the Leray–Serre spectral sequence also degenerates at
E_{2}
and the extension problems are trivial. Because
H^{1}(S^{1}\vee S^{1};\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{1}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
as
G
-modules, the
E_{2}
-term

of the Leray–Serre spectral sequence for

is

\displaystyle E_{2}^{n-1,1}=H^{n-1}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(G;H^{1}\big(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}\big)\big)\cong\tilde{H}^{n}_{G}\big(S^{1}\vee S^{1};\mathbb{Z}\big).

Exactly in the same way, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}^{n-2,2}=H^{n-2}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(G;H^{2}\big(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}\big)\big)\cong\tilde{H}^{n}_{G}\big(T^{2}/S^{1}\vee S^{1};\mathbb{Z}\big),

since

as

\cong E_{2}^{n,0}\oplus E_{2}^{n-1,1}\oplus E_{2}^{n-2,2}

∎

4.2
The outline of computations

Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
follow from case by case computations. As mentioned in Section
1
, three methods are applicable.

1.

In the cases of
p2
and
pm/pg
, the point group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=D_{1}
acts on the torus
T^{2}=S^{1}\times S^{1}
preserving the direct product structure, so that we can think of
T^{2}
as a stack of certain equivariant circle bundles (
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariant principal circle bundles and/or ‘Real’ circle bundles in the sense of
[
10
]
). For such circle bundles, we can use the Gysin exact sequence to compute the equivariant cohomology, as detailed in
[
10
]
. In particular, in the cases of
p2
and
pm/pg
, the Gysin exact sequences are split, and the computations are very simple. (The computation by using the Gysin sequence is also valid for
cm
, even though the sequence is non-split.) In the case of
p2
, we do not need to compute the Leray–Serre spectral sequence, since the third cohomology is trivial. In the case of
pm/pg
, the spectral sequence can be computed directly.

2.

In the cases of
p4
,
cm
,
pmm/pmg/pgg
,
cmm
and
p4m/p4g
, we can verify that the action of the point group on
T^{2}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.4
, by inspecting the explicit presentation in Appendix
A
. Hence we can apply Lemma
4.5
to the computation of the equivariant cohomology and the spectral sequence. In this application, the only non-trivial part is the equivariant cohomology of the invariant subspace
S^{1}\vee S^{1}
, which we compute by using the Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence.

3.

In the cases of
p3
,
p6
,
p3m1
,
p31m
and
p6m
, the computation can be divided into two parts. One part is to compute
H^{3}_{P}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
. This is carried out by taking a
P
-CW decomposition of
T^{2}
, and by using the Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence and the exact sequence for a pair. The other part is to compute the Leray–Serre spectral sequence. In this part, we need to know the group cohomology with coefficients in
H^{1}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
. For this aim, we take an invariant subspace
Y\subset T^{2}
of one dimension. The equivariant cohomology of
Y
is computed by using Mayer–Vietoris sequence, which allows us to know the group cohomology with its coefficients in
H^{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
through Lemma
4.1
. The coefficients
H^{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
and
H^{1}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
are related by a short exact sequence. The associated long exact sequence then computes the group cohomology with coefficients in
H^{1}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
. Depending on the cases, one of these parts happens to be enough to complete the computation.

In the cases of
p2
,
p4m/p4g
and
p6m
, the detail of the computation is provided in the following subsections. The details for the other cases can be found in old versions of
arXiv:1509.09194
.

4.3
p2

The lattice
\Pi\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the standard one
\Pi=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
and the point group
P=\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\pm 1\}
acts on
\Pi

\mathbb{R}^{2}

Theorem 4.6

(

.

The

-equivariant cohomology of

is given as follows

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&n=0&n=1&n=2&n=3\\
\hline\cr H^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z})&\mathbb{Z}&0&\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\oplus 3}&0\tsep{2pt}\bsep{2pt}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Proof.

We use the Gysin exact sequence for ‘Real’ circle bundles in
[
10
]
: We write
H^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X)=H^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(X;\mathbb{Z})
for the equivariant cohomology and
H^{n}_{\pm}(X)\cong H^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X;\mathbb{Z}(1))
for a variant of the equivariant cohomology, which can be formulated by the equivariant cohomology with local coefficients. The torus

is the product of two copies of

, where
\tilde{S}^{1}=U(1)
is the circle with the involution

As given in

, the cohomology

In the rest of this subsection, we will use the following notations to indicate elements in

\displaystyle C_{4},\qquad

Lemma 4.7
.

The equivariant cohomology of

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&n=0&n=1&n=2&n=3\\

We use the Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence: Cover

\displaystyle U\simeq\mathrm{pt},\qquad V\simeq D_{4}/D_{2}^{(v)},\qquad U\cap V\simeq D_{4}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(v)},

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr n=3&&\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}&0\\

This section concerns the equivariant cohomology with local coefficients. We start with some remarks about the Leray–Serre spectral sequence, focusing on the similarities and the differences with the case of the usual Borel equivariant cohomology. We then summarize tools for computation in the version adapted to the case with local coefficients. After that, we prove Theorems

5.1
The Leray–Serre spectral sequence

For a finite group
G
and a homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we define
\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}
to be the
G
-module
\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}
such that its underlying group is
\mathbb{Z}
and
G
acts via
\phi
. Suppose that
G
acts on a space
X
. Associated to the fibration
X\to EG\times_{G}X\to BG
is the long exact sequence of homotopy groups:

\displaystyle\cdots\to\pi_{n}(X)\to\pi_{n}(EG\times_{G}X)\to\pi_{n}(BG)\to\cdots.

Since
\pi_{1}(BG)\cong G
, we get a homomorphism
\pi_{1}(EG\times_{G}X){\to}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by composing
\pi_{1}(EG\times_{G}X){\to}G
with
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The homomorphism defines a local system on the Borel construction

, which we denote with the same notation
\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}
. By using this local system, we define the
G
-equivariant cohomology with local coefficients

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})=H^{n}(EG\times_{G}X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}).

Of course, if
\phi

.

For

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})=F^{0}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\supset F^{1}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\supset F^{2}H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\supset\cdots.

Its

-term is again given by the group cohomology

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;H^{q}(X;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}),

where
H^{q}(X;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}
is the tensor product of the
G
-modules
H^{q}(X;\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}
, namely, its underlying group is

\displaystyle H^{q}(X;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}=H^{q}(X;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Z}\cong H^{q}(X;\mathbb{Z}),

and

by

As in the usual equivariant cohomology,

can be identified with a sheaf cohomology of the simplicial space

. (See for instance the appendix of
[
10
]

[

]

(see
[

5.2
Tools

As long as we are concerned with the local system
\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}
associated to a homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, the reduced cohomology
\tilde{H}^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})
makes sense for a
G
-space
X
with a fixed point
\mathrm{pt}\in X
, and we have the direct sum decomposition
H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\cong H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\oplus\tilde{H}^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})
. Then we can generalize the proof of Lemma
4.1
to show:

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a path connected space
Y
fixing at least one point
\mathrm{pt}\in Y
. Suppose further that
Y
is a CW complex consisting of only cells of dimension less than or equal to
1
. Then, for any homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
n\geq 0
, the following holds true:

\displaystyle H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}\big(G;H^{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}\big)\cong\tilde{H}_{G}^{n+1}(Y;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}).

Similarly, we can generalize Lemma
4.5
as follows:

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose a finite group
G
acts on the torus
T^{2}=S^{1}\times S^{1}
and

•

there is a fixed point
\mathrm{pt}=(x_{0},y_{0})\in T^{2}
under the
G
-action,

•

G
preserves the subspace
S^{1}\vee S^{1}=S^{1}\times\{y_{0}\}\cup\{x_{0}\}\times S^{1}\subset T^{2}
.

Then there is the following isomorphism of groups for any homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and all
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}\big(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}\big)\cong H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\oplus\tilde{H}^{n}_{G}\big(S^{1}\vee S^{1};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}\big)\oplus\tilde{H}^{n}_{G}\big(T^{2}/S^{1}\vee S^{1};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}\big).

Further, the Leray–Serre spectral sequence for
H^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})
degenerates at
E_{2}
and the relevant extension problems are trivial, so that

(a)

F^{2}H^{3}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\cong E_{2}^{2,1}\oplus E_{2}^{3,0}
,

(b)

H^{n}_{G}(T^{2};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=n}E_{2}^{p,q}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Besides the generalizations above, we will use the following universal coefficient theorem in the sequel:

Lemma 5.4
.

For any
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, there is a split exact sequence of groups

\displaystyle 0\to H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to\operatorname{Tor}\big(H^{n+1}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}),\mathbb{Z}_{2}\big)\to 0.

Proof.

For any homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, let
(\mathbb{Z}_{2})_{\phi}
be the
G
-module such that its underlying group is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and its
G
-action is given by
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. This
G
-module
(\mathbb{Z}_{2})_{\phi}
agrees with the trivial
G
-module
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, even if
\phi
is non-trivial. Then, looking at the cochain complexes defining the equivariant cohomology, the usual proof of the universal coefficient theorem leads to the lemma. Another proof is to use the Thom isomorphism, which unwinds the local coefficients: Let
\underline{\mathbb{R}}_{\phi}\to X
be the
G
-equivariant real line bundle on
X
whose underlying bundle is
X\times\mathbb{R}
and the action of
g\in G
on
(x,r)\in X\times\mathbb{R}
is
(x,r)\mapsto(gx,\phi(g)r)
. The Thom isomorphism theorem then provides

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}(X;A_{\phi})\cong H^{n+1}_{G}(D,S;A),

where
A
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
or
\mathbb{Z}
, and
D\subset\underline{\mathbb{R}}_{\phi}
and
S\subset\underline{\mathbb{R}}_{\phi}
are the unit disk bundle and the unit sphere bundle, respectively. Then the usual universal coefficient theorem leads to the present lemma.
∎

To compute the equivariant cohomology
H^{n}_{G}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})
, we usually need the cohomology of the point
H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})
. This cohomology is identified with the group cohomology
H^{n}_{\mathrm{group}}(G;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})
by the degeneration of the Leray–Serre spectral sequence, but its direct computation is not realistic except for the simplest cases (cf. Lemma
4.2
). A useful way to compute it is:

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, there are natural exact sequences

\displaystyle\cdots\to H^{n-1}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\to H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\overset{i^{*}}{\to}H^{n}_{\operatorname{Ker}\phi}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\to H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\to\cdots,

\displaystyle\cdots\to H^{n-1}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\to H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\overset{i^{*}}{\to}H^{n}_{\operatorname{Ker}\phi}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\to H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\to\cdots,

where
i^{*}
is induced from the inclusion
i\colon\operatorname{Ker}\phi\to G
.

Proof.

Let
G
act on
\mathbb{R}_{\phi}=\mathbb{R}
via
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. We can regard
\mathbb{R}_{\phi}
as a
G
-equivariant real line bundle on
\mathrm{pt}
. We have the Thom isomorphisms

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\cong H^{n+1}_{G}(D,S;\mathbb{Z}),\qquad H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{n+1}_{G}(D,S;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}),

where
D
and
S
are the unit interval in
\mathbb{R}_{\phi}
and its boundary, respectively. Note that
D
is equivariantly contractible. Note also that
S\cong G/\operatorname{Ker}\phi
as a
G
-space. Thus, we have isomorphisms

\displaystyle H^{n}_{G}(D;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\cong H^{n}_{G}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z}_{\phi}),\qquad H^{n}_{G}(S;\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\cong H^{n}_{\operatorname{Ker}\phi}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{Z}).

Substituting these isomorphisms and the Thom isomorphisms into the exact sequences for the pair
(D,S)
, we complete the proof.
∎

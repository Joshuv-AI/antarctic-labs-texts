A Linearized L1-Galerkin FEM for Non-smooth Solutions of Kirchhoff type Quasilinear Time-fractional Integro-differential Equation
Lalit Kumar, Sivaji Ganesh Sista, Konijeti Sreenadh

Abstract
In this article, we study the semi discrete and fully discrete formulations for a Kirchhoff type quasilinear integro-differential equation ( D α ) involving time-fractional derivative of order α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) . For the semi discrete formulation of the equation ( D α ), we discretize the space domain using a conforming FEM and keep the time variable continuous. We modify the standard Ritz-Volterra projection operator to carry out error analysis for the semi discrete formulation of the considered equation. In general, solutions of the time-fractional partial differential equations (PDEs) have a weak singularity near time t = 0 t=0 . Taking this singularity into account, we develop a new linearized fully discrete numerical scheme for the equation ( D α ) on a graded mesh in time. We derive a priori bounds on the solution of this fully discrete numerical scheme using a new weighted H 1 ​ ( Ω ) H^{1}(\Omega) norm. We prove that the developed numerical scheme has an accuracy rate of O ⁡ ( P − 1 + N − ( 2 − α ) ) O(P^{-1}+N^{-(2-\alpha)}) in L ∞ ​ ( 0 , T , L 2 ​ ( Ω ) ) L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)) as well as in L ∞ ​ ( 0 , T , H 0 1 ​ ( Ω ) ) L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)) , where P P and N N are degrees of freedom in the space and time directions respectively. The robustness and efficiency of the proposed numerical scheme are demonstrated by some numerical examples.



Keywords:

Nonlocal, Finite element method (FEM), Fractional time derivatives, Integro-differential equation, Graded mesh.

Introduction

The purpose of this article is to study the following time-fractional integro-differential equation with Kirchhoff type principal operator. Find
u:=u(x,t)
such that

^{C}D^{\alpha}_{t}u-M\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}~dx\right)\Delta u=f(x,t)+\int_{0}^{t}b(x,t,s)u(s)~ds~\text{in}~\Omega\times(0,T],

)

\displaystyle u(x,0)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(x)\quad\text{in}~\Omega,

\displaystyle u(x,t)

is a Lipschitz continuous function, initial data

is defined as a second order partial differential operator with smooth coefficients in each variable

b(x,t,s)u(s)=-\nabla\cdot(b_{2}(x,t,s)\nabla u(s))+\nabla\cdot(b_{1}(x,t,s)u(s))+b_{0}(x,t,s)u(s),

(1.1)

with

is a symmetric and positive definite matrix with entries
[b_{2}^{ij}(x,t,s)]~(1\leq i,j\leq d)
,
b_{1}:\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a vector with entries

b_{0}:\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a scalar function. The notation

^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}u:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}(s)~ds,

(1.2)

The novelty of this work is summarized below.

1.

Modified Ritz-Volterra projection operator (

We modify the standard Ritz-Volterra projection operator in such a way that it simplifies the complexities arising from the nonlocal nonlinear diffusion coefficient

2.

Graded mesh in time (
4.1

Solutions of the linear time-fractional PDEs have a weak singularity near initial time

3.

Linearization technique (

The presence of the nonlocal nonlinear diffusion coefficient in the equation (

[

4.

Weighted

Due to the approximation of the memory term on a graded mesh in time, we cannot apply the discrete fractional Grönwall’s inequalities
[

27

to establish the global rate of convergence. We derive the global rate of convergence for the proposed numerical scheme using a new weighted

4
Linearized L1 Galerkin FEM on graded mesh

Let
N
be a positive integer and set the nodal points
\left(t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{N}\right)
on the interval
[0,T]
as

t_{n}=T\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^{\delta}~~\text{for}~~n=0,1,2,\dots,N,

(4.1)

where
\delta\geq 1
is called grading parameter. The time step
\tau_{n}
is given by
\tau_{n}=t_{n}-t_{n-1}
for
n=1,2,3,\dots,N
. The nodal points
t_{n}
satisfy the following relation

t_{n}\leq 2^{\delta}t_{n-1},~\text{for}~n\geq 2.

(4.2)

Proposition 4.1
.

The time step size
\tau_{n}
for
n=1,2,3,\dots,N
satisfy the following estimate

\tau_{n}\leq\delta TN^{-\delta}n^{\delta-1}\leq\delta T^{1/\delta}N^{-1}t_{n}^{(1-1/\delta)}.

(4.3)

Proof.

The estimate (
4.3
) follows by applying mean value theorem to the function
{\Phi}(x)=T\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)^{\delta}
on
(n-1,n)
.
∎

The time steps
\tau_{n}
satisfy the
M
-Conv property
[
27
]
, i.e., there exists a positive constant
C_{\delta}
such that for every
N
, we have

\frac{\tau_{n}}{\tau_{n-1}}\leq C_{\delta}\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq n\leq N.

(4.4)

Let
u^{n}~(1\leq n\leq N)
be the value of
u
at
t_{n}
then the Caputo fractional derivative of
u
at
t_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle{}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}u^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}(s)~ds=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}(s)~ds.

The Caputo fractional derivative is approximated by the L1 scheme
[
15
]
as

\displaystyle{}^{C}D^{\alpha}_{t}u^{n}\approx\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{u^{j}-u^{j-1}}{\tau_{j}}\right)\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)^{-\alpha}ds.

(4.5)

Thus,
{}^{C}D^{\alpha}_{t}u^{n}
can be written as

\displaystyle{}^{C}D^{\alpha}_{t}u^{n}=\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}_{t}u^{n}+\mathbb{T}^{n},

(4.6)

where
\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}_{t}u^{n}
denotes the approximation (
4.5
) of Caputo fractional derivative and
\mathbb{T}^{n}
denotes the truncation error that arises due to this approximation. We write
\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}_{t}u^{n}
in a simpler way as

\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}_{t}u^{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{n,j}(u^{j}-u^{j-1})=k_{n,n}u^{n}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(k_{n,j}-k_{n,j-1})u^{j-1},

(4.7)

where
k_{n,j}
are called discrete kernels, that are given by

k_{n,j}:=\frac{\tau_{j}^{-1}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}ds~~\text{for}~~1\leq j\leq n,~~~\text{and}~~k_{n,0}:=0.

(4.8)

Based on the approximation (
4.7
), we develop the following linearized fully discrete numerical scheme for the equation (

D
α

).

Find
u_{h}^{n}\in X_{h}
for
n=1,2,3,\dots,N
which satisfy the following equations for all

,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}_{t}u_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)+M\left(\|\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}\right)\left(\nabla u_{h}^{n},\nabla v_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(f_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\tau_{j+1}\bar{B}\left(t_{n},t_{j},u_{h}^{j},v_{h}\right),~n\geq 2,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}_{t}u_{h}^{1},v_{h}\right)+M\left(\|\nabla u_{h}^{1}\|^{2}\right)\left(\nabla u_{h}^{1},\nabla v_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(f_{h}^{1},v_{h}\right)+\tau_{1}B\left(t_{1},t_{1},u_{h}^{1},v_{h}\right)~\text{for}~n=1,

(4.10)

\displaystyle u_{h}^{0}

\displaystyle=R_{h}u_{0}~\text{in}~X_{h},

(4.11)

\displaystyle u_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=0~\text{on}~\partial\Omega,~\text{for}~n=1,2,3,\dots,N,

(4.12)

(4.13)

(4.14)

As discussed in the introduction that the numerical schemes based on the Newton method demand high computational cost as well as huge computer storage to solve the nonlinear system at each time steps in

\displaystyle\||\theta^{1}\||_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau_{1}^{\alpha}t_{1}^{-\alpha}N^{-\delta\alpha}+\tau_{1}^{\alpha}h+\tau_{1}^{\alpha/2}N^{-\delta(1+\alpha)}+\tau_{1}^{\alpha/2}h+h+N^{-\delta\alpha}+\tau_{1}h+\tau_{1}N^{-\delta\alpha}

(4.97)

\displaystyle+\tau_{1}^{1}(h+N^{-\delta\alpha}).

This implies

\||\theta^{1}\||_{1}\lesssim h+N^{-\delta\alpha}.

(4.98)

Proceed further as we prove estimates (
4.98
), (
4.77
) and (
4.34
) to complete the proof of this theorem.
∎

6
Concluding Remarks

In this work, we introduced a new type of Ritz-Volterra projection operator using which semi discrete error analysis for the equation
(\mathcal{D}^{\alpha})
has been established. In comparison to the Newton-Raphson method, we have successfully reduced the computational cost and storage by constructing a novel linearization algorithm (
4.9
)-(
4.12
) for the equation
(\mathcal{D}^{\alpha})
. We presented a new approach to derive a priori bounds on the numerical solution and global convergence rate for the proposed fully discrete scheme. Finally, numerical experiments revealed the robustness and efficiency of the developed numerical scheme.

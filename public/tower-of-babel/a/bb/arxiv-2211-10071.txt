Convergence in distribution of the product of random variables from an independent sample on a compact algebraic group
Styrt O. G

Abstract




An equivalent condition for the product of elements of an independent random sample on a compact algebraic group converging in distribution to some
random variable as the sample size increases is obtained. Namely, a limit distribution exists and is uniform on the support of the parent distribution if
a random variable with such a distribution does not belong with the unit probability to any non-trivial coset over an algebraic subgroup that lies in its
normalizer; otherwise, it does not exist.

Key words
:
compact algebraic group, Haar measure, random variable, probability measure, convergence in distribution, uniform distribution.

Introduction

The papers
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
are devoted to researching various properties of random variables with values in a finite set

is uniquely

with coordinates

If a set

of operations of arbitrary arities is given on

over the set

depending on finitely many formal variables. The strict definition of a formula is inductive; it is well known in the particular case

functions and literally retranslated onto the general one. In some way, a formula over

represents a finite expression that involves formal

If a formula involves

, i. e. a mapping

Thus, for arbitrary random variables
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
on

, the random variable
\eta:=f(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
is defined on
G
. In doing so, in the case
of (mutual) independence of the random variables
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
, the distribution vector
p(\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
is uniquely expressed through the
vectors
p(\xi_{i})
(
i=1,\ldots,n
) via an
n
-
ary operation
\widehat{f}
on the simplex
S^{(k)}
. With one more assumption that the formula is
read-once, the replacement in it of all operations from
\mathcal{F}
with the corresponding operations on
S^{(k)}
and of the variables
x_{i}
— with
the vectors
p_{i}\in S^{(k)}
gives exactly the operation
\widehat{f}
.

For read-once formulas of i. i. d. random variables, the question of convergence of distribution to the uniform one as the complexity and (or) the
depth increases is actively studied. Present one important result in this direction.

Definition.
A set with a binary operation on it is called a

(see Theorem 1 in
[

, § 3]
)

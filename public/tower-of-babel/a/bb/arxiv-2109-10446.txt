Thermodynamics of an Ising-like X ​ X ​ Z XXZ chain in a longitudinal magnetic field in the framework of the Quantum Transfer Matrix approach
P. N. Bibikov

Abstract
Taking the Ising chain as a reference model we have derived a perturbative expression for the free energy density of the Heisenberg-Ising chain with strong easy-axis anisotropy. All calculations are performed on the ground of the Quantum Transfer Matrix approach. The obtained result agrees with the direct high-temperature expansion. It also agrees with the low-temperature cluster expansion in the special subregime when quantum fluctuations are weak against thermodynamical ones.



1
Introduction

One of the basic models of one-dimensional quantum magnetism is the
XXZ
spin chain in a
longitudinal
magnetic field
h

[
1
]
. It corresponds to the Hamiltonian

\hat{H}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big[\frac{J}{2}\Big({\bf S}^{+}_{n}{\bf S}^{-}_{n+1}+{\bf S}^{-}_{n}{\bf S}^{+}_{n+1}\Big)+J_{z}\Big({\bf S}^{z}_{n}{\bf S}^{z}_{n+1}-\frac{1}{4}I\Big)-h{\bf S}_{n}^{z}\Big],\qquad h\geq 0,

(1)

where
{\bf S}_{n}^{z}
and
{\bf S}_{n}^{\pm}={\bf S}_{n}^{x}\pm i{\bf S}_{n}^{y}
are the usual spin-1/2 operators.
At
J=0
the model (1) reduces to the Ising chain solvable by a rather simple machinery
[
2
]
.

Being purely
classical
(
[{\bf S}_{m}^{z},{\bf S}_{n}^{z}]=0
) the Ising model is rather poor (for example the Ising magnons are dispersionless). Nevertheless it was suggested for a number
of magnetic compounds
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
However since the condition
J=0
does not follow from any symmetry it is natural to suppose that a more adequate model for these compounds is the Ising-like chain described by Hamiltonian (1)
supplemented by the condition

\Delta\equiv\frac{J_{z}}{J}\gg 1.

(2)

The model (1), (2) was also suggested for some real compounds
[
6
,
7
]
. Being
quantum
it has a more rich physical behavior and at the same time should allow a perturbative treatment around the Ising model.

In the last two decades a new machinery for evaluation of thermodynamics of quantum spin chains was suggested basing on the Quantum Transfer Matrix (QTM) approach
(see reviews
[
8
,
9
]
and references therein). The latter has two main stages.
Within the former one a system of integral equations on specially introduced auxiliary functions is derived. Within the latter one an integral representations for the free energy density

f(T,h)=-\frac{1}{\beta}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln{Z_{N}(T,h)},

(3)

(as usual
Z_{N}(T,h)
is the partition function and
\beta\equiv 1/T
) and correlation functions are obtained in this framework.

In the Ising
J=0
case the corresponding free energy density has the form

f^{Is}(T,h)=e_{0}(h)-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln{\left(\frac{1+{\rm e}^{-\beta h}+\sqrt{(1-{\rm e}^{-\beta h})^{2}+4{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-h)}}}{2}\right)},

(4)

where

e_{0}(h)=-\frac{h}{2}

(5)

is the ground state energy density. Formula (4) readily follows from the representation
[
2
]

f^{Is}(T,h)=-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln{\Lambda_{+}(\beta,h)},

(6)

where

\Lambda_{\pm}(\beta,h)=\cosh{\frac{\beta h}{2}}\pm\sqrt{\sinh^{2}{\frac{\beta h}{2}}+{\rm e}^{\beta J_{z}}},

(7)

are eigenvalues of the special transfer matrix

T=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm e}^{\beta h/2}&{\rm e}^{\beta J/2}\\
{\rm e}^{\beta J/2}&{\rm e}^{-\beta h/2}\end{array}\right),

(8)

related to the Hamiltonian (1) at
J=0
. As it has been mentioned in
[
10
]
formula (4) also may be obtained within the approach of
[
8
,
9
]
.

In the present paper we extend the result of
[
10
]
studying the model (1), (2) at the vicinity of the Ising point in the first two perturbation orders. The paper is organized as follows.
In Sect. 2 following
[
10
]
we introduce the basic auxiliary functions and corresponding integral equations. We use however rather different notations which seem us to be more convenient
for our treatment. We also show how to account singularities in the kernels of the integral equations. In Sect. 3 we extract
the auxiliary functions related to the Ising model
[
10
]
and reduce the integral equations to the form convenient for perturbative expansion.
In Sect. 4 we calculate the first two terms of the perturbation expansion and evaluate the corresponding correction to the free energy density.
In Sect. 5 treating the high-temperature regime

h,|J_{z}|,|J|\ll k_{B}T\Longleftrightarrow\beta\cdot\max{(h,|J_{z}|,|J|)}\ll 1,

(9)

we compare the first order terms of the high-temperature expansion which follows from the obtained formula for
f(T,h)
with the one directly related to (3).
Showing that both approaches give the same result we confirm the effectiveness of the approach
[
8
,
9
]
at high temperatures
[
11
]
.
In Sect. 6 we study the low-temperature regime in the phase related to the ferromagnetically polarized ground state

|\emptyset\rangle=\dots\otimes|\uparrow\rangle\otimes|\uparrow\rangle\otimes|\uparrow\rangle\otimes\dots,\qquad{\bf S}^{z}|\uparrow\rangle=\frac{1}{2}|\uparrow\rangle,

(10)

and non-zero magnon gap energy
[
12
]

E_{gap}=h-J_{z}-|J|>0.

(11)

Comparing the calculated peturbative result with the one obtained previously by the low-temperature cluster expansion
[
12
]
we show that in the
extreme

low-temperature subregime

k_{B}T\ll\frac{E_{width}}{2},

(12)

where

E_{width}=2|J|,

(13)

is the magnon band width, the both approaches totally disagree. However they give similar results in the
scaled
low-temperature subregime

\frac{E_{width}}{2}\ll k_{B}T\ll E_{gap}+\frac{E_{width}}{2}.

(14)

If one suggest that low-temperature quantum fluctuations arise from transitions within the magnon band then the associated fluctuation of energy should
be
E_{width}
. Hence the scaled low-temperature regime corresponds to weakness of quantum fluctuations against thermodynamical ones.

At the same time according to (56)

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{+}^{(1;2)}}{\mathfrak{B}_{+}^{Is}}-\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{-}^{(1;2)}}{\mathfrak{B}_{-}^{Is}}-\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{+}^{(1;-2)}}{\mathfrak{B}_{+}^{Is}}-\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{-}^{(1;-2)}}{\mathfrak{B}_{-}^{Is}}=2J_{z}\beta\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{+}^{Is}+\mathfrak{b}_{-}^{Is}+\mathfrak{b}_{+}^{Is}\mathfrak{b}_{-}^{Is}}{\mathfrak{B}_{+}^{Is}\mathfrak{B}_{-}^{Is}}=2J_{z}\beta\Big(1-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{V}}\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{+}^{(1;2)}(x)\mathfrak{b}_{+}^{(1;-2)}(x)}{(\mathfrak{B}_{+}^{Is})^{2}}+\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{-}^{(1;2)}(x)\mathfrak{b}_{-}^{(1;-2)}(x)}{(\mathfrak{B}_{-}^{Is})^{2}}=\frac{J_{z}^{2}\beta^{2}}{\mathfrak{B}_{+}^{Is}\mathfrak{B}_{-}^{Is}}\Big(\frac{(\mathfrak{b}_{+}^{Is})^{2}}{\mathfrak{B}_{+}^{Is}}+\frac{(\mathfrak{b}_{-}^{Is})^{2}}{\mathfrak{B}_{-}^{Is}}\Big)

\displaystyle=J_{z}^{2}\beta^{2}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{U}-4}{\mathfrak{V}}+\frac{\mathfrak{U}}{\mathfrak{V}^{2}}\Big),

(68)

and a substitution of (67) and (68) into (57) results in

f^{(2)}(T,h)=2J_{z}\Big(\frac{1}{\mathfrak{U}}-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{V}}\Big)+J_{z}^{2}\beta\Big(\frac{2}{{\mathfrak{U}}{\mathfrak{V}}}-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{V}}\Big).

(69)

According to (16), (32), (33) and (66)

\mathfrak{U}=2+\mathfrak{T},\qquad\mathfrak{V}=1+{\rm e}^{-\beta J_{z}}+\mathfrak{T},

(70)

where

\mathfrak{T}=2{\rm e}^{-\beta J_{z}}\Big(\cosh{\frac{\beta h}{2}}\sqrt{\sinh^{2}{\frac{\beta h}{2}}+{\rm e}^{\beta J_{z}}}+\sinh^{2}{\frac{\beta h}{2}}\Big).

(71)

With the use of (70) one may rewrite (69) in the form

f^{(2)}(T,h)=2J_{z}\mathfrak{f}_{1}+J_{z}^{2}\mathfrak{f}_{2},

(72)

where

\mathfrak{f}_{1}=\frac{{\rm e}^{-\beta J_{z}}-1}{(2+\mathfrak{T})(1+{\rm e}^{-\beta J_{z}}+\mathfrak{T})},\qquad\mathfrak{f}_{2}=-\frac{\beta\mathfrak{T}}{(2+\mathfrak{T})(1+{\rm e}^{-\beta J_{z}}+\mathfrak{T})}.

(73)

Formulas (71)-(73) express the main result of the paper.

5
The high-temperature regime

Rigorously speaking validity of the QTM approach was proved only for high temperatures
[
11
]
. That is why a comparison between
the direct high-temperature expansion and the one which follows from (71)-(73) may be considered only as a good check of the calculations.

At high temperatures one has

f(T,h)=-\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\beta N}\ln{{\rm tr}\Big(I-\beta\hat{H}+\frac{(\beta\hat{H})^{2}}{2}\Big)}+o(\beta).

(74)

Since

\displaystyle{\rm tr}I=2^{N},\qquad{\rm tr}\hat{H}=2^{N}\frac{N}{4}{\rm tr}H,

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\hat{H}^{2}=2^{N}\frac{N}{4}\Big({\rm tr}H^{2}+{\rm tr}H_{12}H_{23}+\frac{N-3}{4}({\rm tr}H)^{2}\Big),

(75)

where the
4\times 4
matrix (here
I_{n}
is
n\times n
identity matrix and
I=I_{2^{N}}
)

H=\frac{J}{2}\Big({\bf S}^{+}\otimes{\bf S}^{-}+{\bf S}^{-}\otimes{\bf S}^{+}\Big)+J_{z}\Big({\bf S}^{z}\otimes{\bf S}^{z}-\frac{1}{4}I_{4}\Big)-\frac{h}{2}\Big({\bf S}^{z}\otimes I_{2}+I_{2}\otimes{\bf S}^{z}\Big),

(76)

is the Hamiltonian density related to (1) and

H_{12}=H\otimes I_{2},\qquad H_{23}=I_{2}\otimes H.

(77)

Substituting (75) into (74) and expanding the logarithm one readily gets

\displaystyle f(T,h)=-\frac{\ln{2}}{\beta}-\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\beta N}\ln\Big[1-\frac{\beta N}{4}{\rm tr}H+\frac{\beta^{2}N}{8}\Big({\rm tr}(H^{2}+H_{12}H_{23})

\displaystyle+\frac{N-3}{4}({\rm tr}H)^{2}\Big)\Big]+o(\beta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ln{2}}{\beta}+\frac{{\rm tr}H}{4}-\beta\Big(\frac{{\rm tr}H^{2}+{\rm tr}H_{12}H_{23}}{8}-\frac{3}{32}({\rm tr}H)^{2}\Big)\Big]+o(\beta),

(78)

or

f(T,h)=-\frac{2}{\beta}+\frac{{\rm tr}H}{4}+\frac{\beta}{32}\Big(3({\rm tr}H)^{2}-4[{\rm tr}H^{2}+{\rm tr}(H_{12}H_{23})]\Big)+o(\beta).

(79)

At the same time from (76) follows that

{\rm tr}H=-J_{z},\qquad{\rm tr}H^{2}=\frac{J^{2}+J_{z}^{2}+h^{2}}{2},\qquad{\rm tr}(H_{12}H_{23})=\frac{J_{z}^{2}+h^{2}}{2}.

(80)

So a substitution of (80) into (78) yields

\tilde{f}(T,h)=-\frac{\beta J_{z}^{2}}{16\Delta^{2}}+o(\beta),

(81)

and according to (47)

f^{(2)}(T,h)=-\frac{\beta J_{z}^{2}}{4}.

(82)

This formula may be also readily obtained from (72), (73) and the high temperature expansion

\mathfrak{T}=2-J_{z}\beta+o(\beta),

(83)

which directly follows from exact formula (71).

6
The low-temperature polarized regime

Before evaluating the low-temperature expansion for the free energy density of the Ising-like chain in the polarized phase (10), (11) we shall study the pure Ising chain.
A low-temperature expansion of the free energy density (4) under the condition (11) (with
J=0
) results in the formula

f^{Is}(T,h)=e_{0}(h)+f^{Is}_{magn}(T,h)+f^{Is}_{bound}(T,h)+f^{Is}_{scatt}(T,h)+o({\rm e}^{2\beta(J_{z}-h)}),

(84)

where

\displaystyle f^{Is}_{magn}(T,h)=-\frac{{\rm e}^{-\beta E^{Is}_{magn}}}{\beta},\qquad f^{Is}_{bound}(T,h)=-\frac{{\rm e}^{-\beta E^{Is}_{bound}}}{\beta},

\displaystyle f^{Is}_{scatt}(T,h)=\frac{3{\rm e}^{-\beta E^{Is}_{scatt}}}{2\beta}.

(85)

The parameters

E^{Is}_{magn}=h-J_{z},\qquad E^{Is}_{bound}=2h-J_{z},\qquad E^{Is}_{scatt}=2(h-J_{z}),

(86)

are the energies of a one magnon state (a single excited spin
\dots\otimes|\uparrow\rangle\otimes|\downarrow\rangle\otimes|\uparrow\rangle\dots
),
a two-magnon bound state (two neighboring excited spins) and a two-magnon scattering states (two isolated excited spins).
The physical meaning of the expansion (84) is quite clear and expresses a subdivision of the energy spectrum on independent subsectors.
More specific is hierarchy of the terms in (84). Of course the condition (11) yields
E^{Is}_{magn}<E^{Is}_{bound},E^{Is}_{scatt}
so that

f^{Is}_{magn}(T,h)
is the
leading
term of the expansion (if
e_{0}(h)
is treated as the
constant
term)

f^{Is}_{lead}(T,h)=f^{Is}_{magn}(T,h).

(87)

The
subleading
terms are however different for ferromagnets and (magnetically polarized) antiferromagnets. Namely as it follows from (85) and (86)

\displaystyle f^{Is}_{sublead}(T,h)=f^{Is}_{bound}(T,h),\qquad J_{z}<0,

(88)

\displaystyle f^{Is}_{sublead}(T,h)=f^{Is}_{scatt}(T,h),\qquad h>J_{z}>0.

(89)

Let us now again turn back to the Ising-like chain. First of all let us note that reproducing the cluster expansion result of
[
12
]
we have to account that the Hamiltonian (17) of
[
12
]

turns into (1) only after changing signs of the couplings
J_{z}\rightarrow-J_{z}
,
J\rightarrow-J
and
renormalization of the Zeeman term
{\bf S}_{n}^{z}-1/2I\longrightarrow{\bf S}_{n}^{z}
. Under this procedure the low-temperature cluster expansion formula for the
free energy density of the XXZ spin chain in the polarized phase has the form (84) however with

\displaystyle f_{magn}(T,h)=-\frac{{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-h)}}{2\pi\beta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm e}^{-\beta J\cos{k}}dk=-\frac{{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-h)}}{2\pi\beta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm e}^{\beta|J|\cos{k}}dk,

(90)

\displaystyle f_{bound}(T,h)=-\frac{{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-2h)}}{2\pi\beta}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk\Theta(J_{z}^{2}-J^{2}\cos^{2}{k/2}){\rm e}^{-\beta J^{2}/J_{z}\cos^{2}(k/2)},

(91)

\displaystyle f_{scatt}(T,h)=\frac{{\rm e}^{2\beta(J_{z}-h)}}{4\pi\beta}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk\Big({\rm e}^{-2\beta J\cos{k}}+\frac{J_{z}}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\kappa\frac{{\rm e}^{2\beta J\cos{k/2}\cos{\kappa}}}{J_{z}-J{\rm e}^{-i\kappa}\cos{k/2}}\Big).

(92)

(Here (91) is an improved version of (77) in
[
12
]
). Accounting that in the Ising-like case
\Theta(J_{z}^{2}-J^{2}\cos^{2}{k/2})=1
one readily reduce (91) to the form

f_{bound}(T,h)=-\frac{{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-2h)}}{2\pi\beta}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk{\rm e}^{-\beta J^{2}/J_{z}\cos^{2}(k/2)}.

(93)

As in the Ising case the magnon contribution
f_{magn}(T,h)
gives the leading low-temperature asymptotics to the free energy density while
f_{bound}(T,h)
and
f_{scatt}(T,h)

give a subleading one correspondingly
in ferromagnetic and polarized antiferromagnetic cases.

The expansion (84) as well as the corresponding one based on(90)-(93) are efficient only in the low-temperature regime governed by the inequality

\beta(h-J_{z})\gg 1,

(94)

or according to (11) and (13)

k_{B}T\ll E_{gap}+\frac{E_{width}}{2}.

(95)

In the
extreme
low-temperature subregime when (94) or (95) are replaced by a more strict inequality

\beta|J|\gg 1\Longleftrightarrow k_{B}T\ll\frac{E_{width}}{2},

(96)

the saddle-point integration reduces (90) to an approximative asymptotic expression

f_{magn}(T,h)\approx f^{extr}_{magn}(T,h)=-\frac{{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}+|J|-h)}}{2\pi\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\beta|J|k^{2}/2}dk=-\frac{{\rm e}^{-\beta E_{gap}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\beta^{3}|J|}},

(97)

which obviously can not be reproduced within the perturbation theory based on the QTM approach.

At the same time if

\frac{|J|}{h-J_{z}}=\frac{E_{width}}{2E_{gap}+E_{width}}\ll 1,

(98)

then there is additionally the
scaled
low-temperature subregime governed by the supplemental condition

\beta|J|\ll 1\Longleftrightarrow k_{B}T\gg\frac{E_{width}}{2}.

(99)

In this case the first two terms of the power expansion for the exponent in (90) yield

f_{magn}(T,h)\approx f^{scal}_{magn}(T,h)=-\frac{{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-h)}}{\beta}\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}J^{2}}{4}\Big)=-\frac{{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-h)}}{\beta}\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}J_{z}^{2}}{4\Delta^{2}}\Big).

(100)

In a similar manner (93) gives

f_{bound}(T,h)\approx f_{bound}^{scal}(T,h)=-\frac{{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-2h)}}{\beta}\Big(1-\frac{J_{z}\beta}{2\Delta^{2}}\Big).

(101)

Derivation of an analogous expansion for
f_{scatt}(T,h)
is a bit more cumbersome. Namely

\displaystyle\frac{J_{z}}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\kappa\frac{{\rm e}^{-2\beta J\cos{k/2}\cos{\kappa}}}{J_{z}-J{\rm e}^{-i\kappa}\cos{k/2}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\kappa\Big(1+\frac{{\rm e}^{-i\kappa}}{\Delta}\cos{\frac{k}{2}}+\frac{{\rm e}^{-2i\kappa}}{\Delta^{2}}\cos^{2}{\frac{k}{2}}+o\Big(\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\Big(1-2\beta J\cos{\frac{k}{2}}\cos{\kappa}+2\beta^{2}J^{2}\cos^{2}{\frac{k}{2}}\cos^{2}{\kappa}+o(\beta^{2}J^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\beta^{2}J_{z}^{2}-\beta J_{z}}{\Delta^{2}}\cos^{2}{\frac{k}{2}}+o(\beta^{2}J^{2})+o\Big(\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\Big).

(102)

So according to (92) and (102)

f_{scatt}(T,h)\approx f^{scal}_{scatt}(T,h)=\frac{{\rm e}^{2\beta(J_{z}-h)}}{\beta}\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{2\beta^{2}J_{z}^{2}-\beta J_{z}}{2\Delta^{2}}\Big).

(103)

Let us now reproduce the asymptotic formulas (100), (101) and (103) by the QTM approach.
Representing (71) in the form

\mathfrak{T}={\rm e}^{-\beta J_{z}}({\rm e}^{\beta h}-1)\left[1+\frac{1+{\rm e}^{-\beta h}}{2}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{{\rm e}^{\beta J_{z}}}{\sinh^{2}{(\beta h/2)}}}-1\right)\right],

(104)

one readily gets at
\beta\rightarrow\infty

\mathfrak{T}={\rm e}^{\beta(h-J_{z})}-{\rm e}^{-\beta J_{z}}+1+o(\min(1,{\rm e}^{-\beta J_{z}})).

(105)

So according to (73) and (105)

\mathfrak{f}_{2}=\beta\Big(-{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-h)}+4{\rm e}^{2\beta(J_{z}-h)}\Big)+o({\rm e}^{2\beta(J_{z}-h)}).

(106)

The corresponding expression for
\mathfrak{f}_{1}
cardinally depends on the sign of
J_{z}
. Namely

\mathfrak{f}_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-2h)}+o({\rm e}^{\beta(J_{z}-2h)}),\qquad J_{z}<0\\
-{\rm e}^{2\beta(J_{z}-h)}+o({\rm e}^{2\beta(J_{z}-h)}),\qquad J_{z}>0.\end{array}\right.

(107)

Now a substitution of (106) and (107) into (72) reproduces (100), (101) and (103).

In order to visualize the difference between the low-temperature and scaled low-temperature regimes we may introduce according to (90), (97) and (100)
the following two universal functions

\displaystyle g_{extr}(\zeta)=\frac{\displaystyle f_{magn}^{extr}(T,h)}{\displaystyle f_{magn}(T,h)}=\frac{\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi}{\rm e}^{\zeta}}{\displaystyle\sqrt{\zeta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm e}^{\zeta\cos{k}}dk},

\displaystyle g_{scal}(\zeta)=\frac{\displaystyle f_{magn}^{scal}(T,h)}{f_{magn}(T,h)}=\frac{\displaystyle\pi(4+\zeta^{2})}{\displaystyle 2\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm e}^{\zeta\cos{k}}dk}.

(108)

where
\zeta=\beta|J|
. According to (96), (99) and (108) the extreme low-temperature regime corresponds to
\zeta\gg 1
and
g_{extr}(\zeta)\approx 1
, while the scaled low-temperature regime corresponds
to
\zeta\ll 1
and
g_{scal}(\zeta)\approx 1
. Namely, as it readily follows from (108)

\lim_{\zeta\rightarrow\infty}g_{extr}(\zeta)=\lim_{\zeta\rightarrow 0}g_{scal}(\zeta)=1.

(109)

7
Summary

In the present paper extending the result of
[
10
]
belonging to the Ising chain we studied within the QTM approach a highly anisotropic Heisenberg-Ising chain
and have suggested the perturbative formula (see (71)-(73)) for the free energy density.
At high temperatures the result agrees with the direct high-temperature expansion. At low temperatures an agreement with the cluster expansion is only in the
special scaled regime (14) which may be realized only under the condition (98) and for which quantum fluctuations are small against the thermodynamical ones.
We suggest that the obtained result confirms effectiveness of the QTM approach and may be extended on perturbative evaluation of correlation functions.

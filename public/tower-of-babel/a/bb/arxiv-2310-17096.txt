Morse theory for the Allen-Cahn functional
Jingwen Chen 1 , Pedro Gaspar 2

Abstract
. In this article, we use Morse-theoretic techniques to construct connections between low energy critical submanifolds of the Allen-Cahn energy functional in the 3 3 -sphere via the negative gradient flow.



1.
Introduction

Morse theory, which allows for the analysis of the topology of a manifold by studying differentiable functions on that manifold, was extensively investigated in infinite-dimensional settings by various mathematicians – Smale
[
60
,
61
]
, Palais
[
53
]
, Bott
[
6
,
7
]
, Conley-Zehnder
[
21
]
, Floer
[
28
]
, Chang
[
13
]
, and many others. Abbondandolo-Majer
[
1
]
introduced a comprehensive Morse homology theory for functionals on a Hilbert space. Their approach revolves around the idea of examining the negative gradient flows of functionals that connect critical points, which are then utilized to construct the Morse complex. They also address a transversality condition between unstable and stable manifolds, a concept that has substantial applications in the fields of differential geometry, topology, and dynamical systems. Some applications of infinite-dimensional Morse theory include the construction of closed geodesics – see the survey
[
52
]
and the references therein – and the solution to the triangulation conjecture in topology, by C. Manolescu
[
42
]
.

Motivated by the achievements of Morse Theory and recognizing the deep connections between the Allen-Cahn equation and Mean Curvature Flow, we study Morse theory for the
Allen-Cahn energy functional
in the
3
-sphere, namely:

E_{\epsilon}(u)=\int_{M}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}W(u)\right).

on the Hilbert space
W^{1,2}(S^{3})
, aiming to understand the negative gradient flows that connect critical submanifolds of
E_{\epsilon}
at low energy levels. Here,
W
represents a symmetric, nonnegative double-well potential with wells located at
\pm 1
, and a typical example is
W(u)=(1-u^{2})^{2}/4
. These flows correspond to solutions of the
parabolic Allen-Cahn equation
:

(PAC)

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-{\textstyle\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}}W^{\prime}(u)

that connect critical points of
E_{\epsilon}
, which are stationary solutions of this equation.

In this article, we study the dynamics of solutions to (
PAC
) in
S^{3}
and demonstrate that there exist connections between solutions at the second lowest (nontrivial) energy level and solutions at the lowest energy level via a negative gradient flow of the Allen-Cahn functional.

Mean curvature flow and Morse theory for the area functional

The
Mean Curvature Flow
(MCF) is one of the most studied extrinsic geometric flows and evolves hypersurfaces in their normal direction with a speed equal to the mean curvature at each point. It represents the steepest descent flow for the
area functional
– that is, the volume of codimension-one submanifolds – and finds numerous profound applications in fields such as geometry, topology, material science, image processing, and general relativity. The simplest examples of MCFs in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
include the shrinking spheres and cylinders. However, there are comparatively few examples of ancient (and eternal, weak) MCFs in compact manifolds – we mention here
[
10
,
19
,
35
,
41
]
.

In the last decades, there has been intense activity in the development of a Morse theory for the area functional using tools from Geometric Measure Theory connected to the existence of minimal hypersurfaces, which are the stationary solutions of the MCF, and with deep consequences in geometry and topology. This program was initiated by F. Almgren and J. Pitts
[
55
]
(see also Schoen-Simon
[
59
]
) and was further developed by F. Marques and A. Neves
[
43
,
44
]
, who proposed a Morse-theoretic program for minimal hypersurfaces. After the contribution of many mathematicians – we name here Y. Liokumovich
[
39
]
, A. Song
[
64
]
, and X. Zhou
[
69
]
, and refer to the survey
[
45
]
for a more complete list of references – this program established, for instance, the characterization of low area minimal surfaces in
S^{3}

[
43
]
and the existence of infinitely many minimal hypersurfaces in compact Riemannian manifolds of dimension
3\leq n\leq 7
, with a concrete description of their Morse indexes and their area growth
[
64
,
44
]
.

Regularization by the Allen-Cahn energy

The Allen-Cahn equation serves as a model for phase transition and separation phenomena
[
11
]
. The motion of the diffuse transition region, where
u
remains bounded away from the minima of
W
, has been extensively studied by several authors. They have established the convergence of this interface, as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, to a hypersurface that evolves according to the mean curvature flow, albeit in different formulations. In that sense, the parabolic equation (
PAC
) can be regarded as a regularization for the MCF. Prominent contributions in this regard include works by
[
9
,
16
,
17
,
24
,
22
,
57
,
63
]
and their respective references.

The classical variational convergence for
stationary
solutions of (
PAC
) was studied in the works of Modica and Mortola
[
48
,
49
]
, who proved that the Allen–Cahn energy functional
\Gamma
-converges to the
perimeter functional
, a generalization of the
(n-1)
-dimensional volume defined on the space of sets of finite perimeter. In particular, the interfaces of locally minimizing critical points of
E_{\epsilon}
(namely, the sets where these functions are bounded away from
\pm 1
) converge, as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, to local minimizers of the perimeter and are thus regular away from a singular set of dimension
\leq(n-8)
.

Later, a general convergence result for families of stationary solutions with uniformly bounded energy and index was developed in the combined work of J. Hutchinson, Y. Tonegawa, N. Wickramasekera, and M. Guaraco
[
36
,
66
,
32
]
. This result is based on the deep regularity theory for minimal surfaces developed by Wickramasekera
[
68
]
. In essence, a sequence of critical points
{u_{j}}
of
E_{\epsilon}
with
\epsilon=\epsilon_{j}\downarrow 0
and bounded energy and Morse index has a subsequence such that its nodal set accumulates on a minimal hypersurface in
M
that is smooth and embedded away from a closed set of Hausdorff dimension
\leq(n-8)
. We also mention the strong convergence theory in
3
-dimensional manifolds developed by O. Chodosh and C. Mantoulidis
[
18
]
, based on earlier work of K. Wang and J. Wei
[
67
]
, which establishes the graphical convergence of the nodal sets to the limit minimal surface under nondegeneracy conditions.

The convergence of solutions of (
PAC
) to measure-theoretic solutions of the Mean Curvature Flow (in the sense of Brakke
[
8
]
) has been explored by Ilmanen
[
37
]
, Soner
[
63
]
, and more recently by Tonegawa
[
65
]
, Mizuno-Tonegawa
[
47
]
, Pisante-Punzo
[
54
]
, and Sato
[
58
]
. We also mention here the strong convergence results of Nguyen-Wang
[
50
]
and the recent results of Fischer-Laux-Simon
[
27
]
based on relative entropy methods.

Regarding the existence of stationary solutions of (
PAC
), this was studied through Morse-theoretic and variational methods in
[
62
]
, where G. Smith employed Morse theory for the Allen-Cahn energy in Hölder spaces to produce an increasing number of solutions of (
AC
) as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
. For a min-max approach, see
[
32
,
30
]
.

We now describe our main contribution. Recall that any least energy nonconstant critical point of the Allen-Cahn energy
E_{\epsilon}
in
S^{3}
vanishes exactly along an equatorial sphere as demonstrated in
[
12
]
, where these solutions are called
ground state solutions
. For small
\epsilon
, the critical points of
E_{\epsilon}
at the second lowest nontrivial energy level is a solution that vanishes precisely along a Clifford torus. Let
u_{\epsilon}^{-\infty}
be a critical point of
E_{\epsilon}
in
S^{3}
which vanishes precisely along
\{x\in S^{3}\mid x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}=x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}=1/2\}
.

Main Theorem
.

For any ground state solution
u_{\epsilon}^{+\infty}
of the Allen-Cahn equation, there is an eternal solution
u_{\epsilon}\colon S^{3}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
to (
PAC
) such that

\|u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)-u_{\epsilon}^{\pm\infty}\|_{W^{1,2}(S^{3})}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\pm\infty.

Previously in
[
15
]
, we constructed a
2
-parameter family of eternal solutions to (
PAC
) that join
u^{-\infty}_{\epsilon}
to Allen-Cahn approximations of certain equatorial spheres. The Main Theorem of the present article can be seen as an extension of this result. We note nevertheless that the techniques used in this article are substantially different, in which we replace the symmetry argument used in
[
15
]
with a topological argument that allows us to obtain further information about the unstable manifold of
u^{-\infty}_{\epsilon}
and their forward limit, as we outline next.

Due to the symmetry properties of the round sphere, the Allen-Cahn functional is a
Morse-Bott function
along critical points with low energy, as elucidated by the rigidity results in
[
12
,
34
]
. The concept of Morse-Bott functions generalizes Morse functions and allows for non-isolated and degenerate critical points. Formally, a Morse–Bott function is a smooth function defined on a manifold, where the critical set forms a submanifold, and the Hessian remains nondegenerate in the normal direction.

In order to use infinite-dimensional Morse theory and gain deeper insights into the behavior of the Allen-Cahn energy function, we employ a general perturbation technique introduced in
[
4
,
5
]
. In essence, we can perturb a Morse-Bott function in a tubular neighborhood of each critical submanifold to produce a one-parameter family of Morse functions. For example, consider the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
f(x,y,z)=x^{2}-y^{2}
. This function qualifies as a Morse-Bott function, with the
z
-axis serving as its critical submanifold. By introducing perturbations near the
z
-axis, we obtain a family of Morse functions given by
f_{\delta}=x^{2}-y^{2}+\delta z^{2}
.

Inspired by this argument, we perturb the energy functional
E_{\epsilon}
in a tubular neighborhood surrounding the low energy critical submanifolds in order to get a one-parameter family of Morse function with controlled energy and Morse indices. The perturbed Allen-Cahn equation is a nonlinear parabolic partial differential equation that can be regarded as an abstract evolution equation in certain function spaces. Our study explores the existence, regularity, continuity, and long-time existence properties of certain solutions to this equation, as described in
[
2
,
33
]
.

Additionally, employing the Morse index and the structure of the unstable manifold of critical points of the perturbed functional as a key ingredient, we establish the existence of a negative gradient flow connecting any critical point at the second least positive energy level to any critical point at the least positive energy level. More precisely, we use tools from Morse Theory in Hilbert spaces
[
53
]
and the variational min-max characterization of Clifford tori (as first obtained in
[
43
]
), which allow us to develop a topological contradiction argument to produce an impossible
5
-sweepout
, in the sense of the min-max construction of critical points of the Allen-Cahn energy studied in
[
30
]
.

Potential geometric applications.

In the proof of our main result, we develop a method to study the structure of the unstable manifold and stable manifold of critical points of the Allen-Cahn energy, with many potential geometric applications. Motivated by the Measure-theoretic convergence of the Allen-Cahn flow to the MCF, one of these applications would be to apply a method similar to the one that the authors presented in
[
15
]
to construct Brakke flows with prescribed asymptotic limits within a compact manifold, as we briefly describe below.

In our previous work
[
15
]
, we employed the convergence theory of solutions of (
PAC
) to Brakke flows to obtain a
2
-parameter family of eternal Brakke flows in
S^{3}
which converge backward in time to the Clifford torus and forward in time to an equatorial sphere. These flows are smooth for large positive and negative times and can be thought of as a connection between the unstable manifold of the Clifford torus, as described in
[
19
]
, and the stable manifold of the equatorial spheres. We believe that the Allen-Cahn flows produced in the present work should give rise to the entire space of connections between these minimal surfaces through mean curvature flows. We will explore this application in upcoming work.

We also mention that the methods in the present article could be useful to study variational characterization results of low-energy or low-index solutions of the Allen-Cahn equation in other manifolds, similar to those obtained in
[
12
,
34
]
. This could potentially be used to describe certain min-max minimal hypersurfaces which are obtained as Allen-Cahn limits, motivated by the close connections between the variational theories for these functionals
[
30
,
23
]
.

Organization

In Section
2
, we state some results concerning the Allen-Cahn equation, min-max theory, existence theory and dynamics for abstract parabolic differential equations, and the Morse-Bott theory on Hilbert spaces which will be used in the sequel.

In Section
3
, we present the Morse-Bott perturbation argument, examine heteroclinic orbits for the perturbed Allen-Cahn energy, and ultimately utilize these perturbed orbits to construct the desired orbit for the Allen-Cahn energy.

In Section
4
, we employ a topological argument to construct appropriate eternal solutions for the negative
L^{2}
-gradient flow of perturbations to the Allen-Cahn energy. This provides us with the orbits for the perturbed Allen-Cahn energy as utilized in the previous section.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Phase transitions

This subsection collects some basic facts and results about the
Allen-Cahn
equation.

Definition
.

A function
W\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a
(symmetric) double-well potential
if:

(1)

W
is nonnegative and vanishes precisely at
\pm 1
;

(2)

W
satisfies
W^{\prime}(0)=0
,
W^{\prime\prime}(0)\neq 0
, and
sW^{\prime}(s)<0
for
|s|\in(0,1)
;

(3)

W^{\prime\prime}(\pm 1)>0
;

(4)

W(s)=W(-s)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

We will also assume that
W
verifies the following technical
quadratic growth
condition: there exist
C>0
and
R_{0}>1
such that

(Q1)

|W(s)|\geq\frac{1}{C}|s|^{2}
for all
|s|\geq R_{0}
;

(Q2)

|W^{\prime\prime}|
is bounded.

The standard example of a double-well potential is the function
W(t)=\frac{1}{4}(1-t^{2})^{2}
. Even though this potential does not satisfy the condition (Q2) above, we observe that, by the maximum principle, any nonconstant classical solution of (
PAC
) in a compact manifold
M
defined for
t\geq t_{0}
and such that
|u(\cdot,t_{0})|\leq 1
satisfies
|u(\cdot,t)|<1
for all
t>t_{0}
. This means, taking into account that our domains are compact manifolds, one can modify such potential
W(t)
outside of
[-2,2]
to obtain a new nonlinearity
\tilde{W}
that satisfies (Q1)–(Q2) and such that any solution
u
with
|u|\leq 1
of (
PAC
) for the potential
\tilde{W}
also satisfies (
PAC
) for the original double-well potential
W
. Compare with the modifications employed in
[
32
, Section 4]
in order to apply variational methods for the Allen-Cahn energy.

Definition
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold. We define the
Allen-Cahn energy
on
M
by:

E_{\epsilon}(u):=\int_{M}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}W(u)\right)d\mu_{g},\ \ u\in W^{1,2}(M),

where
d\mu_{g}
is the volume measure with respect to
g
.

We observe that the technical conditions above ensure that
E_{\epsilon}
is a
C^{3}
functional in
W^{1,2}(M)
for compact
3
-dimensional manifolds
M
, and that
E_{\epsilon}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition along sequences that have bounded energy – see
[
56
]
,
[
12
, Lemma 4.1]
.

One can check that
u
is a critical point of
E_{\epsilon}
on a closed manifold
(M^{n},g)
if and only if
u
(weakly) solves the
elliptic Allen-Cahn equation
:

(AC)

\Delta_{g}u-{\textstyle\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}}W^{\prime}(u)=0\quad\text{on}\ M.

We write
\sigma=\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{W(t)/2}dt
. This is the energy of the
heteroclinic solution

\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}(t)
of (
AC
) on
\mathbb{R}
, that is, the unique bounded solution in
\mathbb{R}
(modulo translation) such that
\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}(t)\to\pm 1
when
t\to\pm\infty
. We refer to
[
18
, Section 1.3]
for more information on this one-dimensional solution.

Recall that the
Morse index
of a solution
u
of (
AC
) (as a critical point of
E_{\epsilon}
), denoted
\ind_{\epsilon}(u)
, is the index of the quadratic form given by the second variation of the energy
E_{\epsilon}
at
u
, namely

D^{2}E_{\epsilon}[u](\phi,\psi):=\int_{M}\epsilon\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\psi\rangle+\frac{1}{\epsilon}W^{\prime\prime}(u)\phi\psi\ d\mu_{g},\qquad\text{for}\quad\psi,\phi\in C^{\infty}(M).

Note that
\ind_{\epsilon}(u)
is the number of negative eigenvalues of the self-adjoint, second-order linear elliptic operator

DAC_{\epsilon}(f):=\Delta f-\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}f,

counted with multiplicity. In particular,
\ind_{\epsilon}(u)
is finite (note that we assumed
M
to be compact). We also recall that
u
is said to be a
stable
solution if
\ind_{\epsilon}(u)=0
.

2.2.
Min-max theory for even functionals

We briefly recall the definition of the cohomological
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index (for subsets of a Hilbert space), and the definition of the
p
-widths for an even functional on such spaces; see
[
25
,
30
]
. Along this section, we consider a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and a
C^{2}
even functional
f\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
that has the origin in
\mathcal{H}
as an isolated critical point and that satisfies the Palais-Smale property along sequences
\{u_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{H}
for which
\{f(u_{j})\}
is bounded. We also assume that
D^{2}f(u)
is a Fredholm operator for every critical point
u\in\mathcal{H}
of
f
.

We say that
A\subset\mathcal{H}
is a
symmetric
set if
-u\in A
whenever
u\in A
. For every symmetric
A\subset\mathcal{H}
with
0\notin A
, denote by
q_{A}\colon A\to\tilde{A}
the quotient map under the action of the antipodal map
u\in A\mapsto-u\in A
. If
S^{\infty}
and
\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}
denote the infinite dimensional sphere and real projective space (with the final topology given by the inclusions
S^{n}\to S^{n+1}
and
\mathbb{R}P^{n}\to\mathbb{R}P^{n+1}
), then there exists a
classifying map
for
A
, namely a continuous map
f_{A}\colon A\to S^{\infty}
which is equivariant with respect to the antipodal maps, and hence induces a continuous
\tilde{f}_{A}\colon\tilde{A}\to\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}
such that
\tilde{f}_{A}\circ q_{A}=\mathrm{pr}\circ f
, where
\mathrm{pr}\colon S^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}
is the quotient map. We recall that the
cohomological
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
index of
A
is defined as

\ind_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)=\sup\{k\in\mathbb{N}\mid\tilde{f}_{A}^{*}(w^{k-1})\neq 0\in H^{k-1}(\tilde{A},\mathbb{Z}_{2})\},

where
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}P^{\infty},\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is the generator of the cohomology of
\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}
, and we use Alexander-Spanier cohomology groups with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Moreover, we adopt the convention that
\ind_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\emptyset)=0
and that
\ind_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)=\infty
whenever
A
is symmetric and
0\in A
.

For each
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the families

\mathcal{F}_{p}=\{A\subset\mathcal{H}\mid A\ \text{is symmetric},\ \text{and}\ \ind_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)\geq p+1\}.

For instance, the unit sphere in any
(p+1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{H}
is in
\mathcal{F}_{p}
. We define the
p
-
widths
of
f
as

\omega_{p}(f):=\inf_{A\in\mathcal{F}_{p}}\sup_{u\in A}f(u).

By using variational methods (see
[
31
]
), one proves that
\omega_{p}(f)
are critical values of
f
, and are achieved by critical points
u\in\mathcal{H}
of
f
such that
\ind(D^{2}f(u))\leq p\leq\ind(D^{2}f(u))+\dim\ker D^{2}f(u)
.

We also observe that if
f,g\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
are even
C^{2}
functionals, then

\sup_{u\in A}f(u)\leq\sup_{u\in A}g(u)+\sup_{\mathcal{H}}|f-g|

for any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
, so that
\omega_{p}(f)\leq\omega_{p}(g)+\sup_{\mathcal{H}}|f-g|
. The following lemma is a useful consequence of this observation:

Lemma 1
.

Let
\{f_{\delta}\}
be a family of even
C^{2}
functionals defined on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
satisfying the conditions described above, for every small
\delta>0
. Assume that there exists an even
C^{2}
functional
f\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
which also satisfies the conditions above and such that
\sup_{H}|f-f_{\delta}|\to 0
, as
\delta\downarrow 0
. Then
\omega_{p}(f_{\delta})\to\omega_{p}(f)
as
\delta\downarrow 0
, for all
p
.

We now focus on the min-max values
\omega_{p}(E_{\epsilon})
for the Allen-Cahn energy functional
E_{\epsilon}
. The convergence theory described in the introduction indicates that these critical values should converge to critical values for the
area functional
, whose critical points are stationary surfaces for the codimension-one MCF, namely, minimal hypersurfaces. We recall that the variational theory for the latter was initiated by F. Almgren, J. Pitts
[
55
]
, and motivated many recent outstanding developments; see
[
43
,
44
,
69
,
64
,
45
]
and the references therein. These results explore the
volume spectrum
of a manifold – a min-max sequence of critical values for the area – to prove a number of results about the existence and multiplicity of such surfaces.

The convergence of
\{\omega_{p}(E_{\epsilon})\}_{p}
to the volume spectrum as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
was established in
[
30
,
23
]
(where
\omega_{p}(E_{\epsilon})
are denoted by
c_{\epsilon}(p)
) and motivates the search of classification results for solutions of (
AC
) from a geometric perspective, under curvature or symmetry assumptions. In the result below we summarize the classification of solutions at energy levels corresponding to the first min-max values
\{\omega_{p}(E_{\epsilon})\}_{p=1}^{5}
in the round
3
-sphere which follows from the classification of low area stationary varifolds in
S^{3}

[
43
]
, its phase-transitions analog – the combined results of
[
12
]
and
[
34
]
–, and the index bounds for limit interfaces
[
29
,
18
]
(see Lemma 1 in
[
14
]
for a proof):

Theorem 1
.

There exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that, for any
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
, the following holds. Let
u
be a nonconstant solution of (
AC
) with energy
E_{\epsilon}(u)\leq\omega_{5}(E_{\epsilon})
. Then either

(i)

If
E_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})<\omega_{5}(E_{\epsilon})
, then
u
is a radially symmetric solution of Morse index 1, energy
E_{\epsilon}(u)=\omega_{1}(E_{\epsilon})=\ldots=\omega_{4}(E_{\epsilon})
and vanishes precisely on an equatorial sphere.

(ii)

If
E_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})=\omega_{5}(E_{\epsilon})
, then up to an isometry
u
is a
\mathrm{SO}(2)\times\mathrm{SO}(2)
-symmetric solution of Morse index 5 and vanishes precisely on the Clifford torus
\{x\in S^{3}\mid x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}=x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\}
.

The solutions of the first type are called
ground state solutions
, as those are precisely the lowest energy nonconstant solutions (and also the lowest energy unstable solutions) of (
AC
). We note that these solutions, as well as the solutions with symmetries of tori, can be constructed by direct minimization and reflection methods, see
[
12
]
for details.

2.3.
Existence theory and dynamics for a class of abstract differential equations

For the sake of completeness, we state some results about the existence and continuous dependence on initial conditions for solutions of a class of abstract parabolic partial differential equations on a compact Riemannian
3
-manifold
(M,g)
. We refer to the results of Sections 7 and 8 of
[
2
]
(see also
[
33
]
, for the case of semilinear equations, or
[
40
]
). We will consider initial value problems associated with abstract quasilinear equations in fractional Sobolev spaces
X^{\alpha}=W^{2\alpha,2}(M)
, and write
X=L^{2}(M)
. We fix once and for all
\alpha_{0}\in\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)
so that, by the Sobolev embedding for compact
3
-manifolds, the space
X^{\alpha}
continuously embeds into
L^{\infty}(M)\cap W^{1,2}(M)
for any
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},1)
.

Remark
.

Here, we utilize the Sobolev embedding theorems for compact manifolds, as detailed in references such as
[
3
]
or
[
38
]
(Theorem
2.2
). These theorems establish that, in a certain sense, the Sobolev inequality holds with a consistent constant across all compact manifolds
M
.

Consider the differential equation

(1)

\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}-(1+\delta\theta(u))\Delta u=F(u,\delta),

where
F\colon W^{1,2}(M)\times[0,\delta_{0})\to W^{1,2}(M)
is a locally Lipschitz map,
\theta\colon W^{1,2}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
is a perturbation term with bounded
\|\theta\|_{C^{2}(W^{1,2}(M))}
and
\delta_{0}>0
is sufficiently small.

2.4.
Morse-Bott and Morse functions on Hilbert spaces

The classification result for low-energy solutions of the Allen-Cahn equation in
S^{3}
can be used to describe the topology of certain sublevel sets of the energy
E_{\epsilon}
, as well as certain perturbations of this functional, using tools of Morse theory.

First, recall some basic results about
Morse-Bott
functionals following the exposition of Feehan
[
26
]
in the (simpler) case of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.

Definition
.

For an open subset
X
of
\mathcal{H}
and a
C^{2}
function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
, we say that
f
is
Morse-Bott
at a critical point
x_{0}\in X
if

(1)

There exist an open subset
U\subset X
and a connected submanifold
C\subset U
such that
U\cap\{x\in X\mid Df(x)=0\}=C
;

(2)

T_{x_{0}}C=\ker D^{2}f(x_{0})
.

We note that, as pointed in
[
26
]
, since
C
is a submanifold of
X
and
T_{x_{0}}C=\ker D^{2}f(x_{0})
, this space has a closed complement in
\mathcal{H}
.

If
f
is Morse-Bott at any critical point
x_{0}
with
f(x_{0})\leq c
, we will say that
f
is a
Morse-Bott function on

\{f\leq c\}
. The following lemma is central to the study of Morse-Bott functions.

Lemma

(Morse-Bott Lemma for, see
[
26
]
, see Theorem 2.10 and Remark 2.12)
.

Let
X
be an open subset of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and let
f\in C^{k+2}(X)
,
k\geq 1
. Suppose that
f
is Morse-Bott at
x_{0}\in X
. Then there are open neighborhoods
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{H}
of the origin, and
\mathcal{U}\subset X
of
x_{0}
, and a
C^{k}
diffeomorphism
\Phi\colon\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{U}
such that
\Phi(0)=x_{0}
,
D\Phi(0)=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{H}}
, and

f(\Phi(y))=f(x_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle y,Ay\right\rangle,\quad\text{for all}\quad y\in\mathcal{V},

where

A=D^{2}f(x_{0})=D^{2}(f\circ\Phi)(0).

We will be mostly interested in the case
D^{2}f(x_{0})
is represented by a self-adjoint operator with a discrete spectrum

\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{p}<\lambda_{p+1}=\ldots=\lambda_{p+k}=0<\lambda_{p+k+1}<\ldots,

where
p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
is the
Morse index
of
f
at
x_{0}
, and
k=\dim\ker D^{2}f(x_{0})
is the
nullity
of the critical point
x_{0}
. In this case, we can decompose
\mathcal{H}
in terms of the negative eigenspaces
E_{1},\ldots,E_{p}
, and positive eigenspaces
E_{+}
of
D^{2}f(x_{0})
, so that

f(\Phi(y))=f(x_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}\|P_{i}y\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle P_{+}y,A(P_{+}y)\right\rangle,

where
P_{i}\colon\mathcal{H}\to E_{i}
and
P_{+}\colon\mathcal{H}\to E_{+}
are the orthogonal projections.

We also recall the following useful:

Lemma 2
.

Let
C\subset X
be a compact, connected, embedded
C^{k+2}
submanifold. Denote by
N(C)
the normal bundle of
C
in
X
endowed with the metric induced by
X
. For each
r>0
, let
N_{r}(C)=\{v\in N(C)\mid\|v\|<r\}
. Given an open
U_{0}\subset X
containing
C
, there exist
r>0
such that the map
\zeta\colon N(C)\to X
given by
\zeta(v)=u+v
for
v\perp T_{u}C
in
N_{r}(C)
is a diffeomorphism onto a neighborhood
U\subset U_{0}
of
C
. Moreover, for every
u\in U
, the derivative of the associated nearest point projection
\Pi\colon U\to C
at
u
is the orthogonal projection onto
T_{u}C
.

Corollary
.

Let
f
and
X
be as in the Morse-Bott Lemma, suppose
C\subset\crit f
is an isolated compact, connected, embedded submanifold of
\crit f
. Then the projection map
\Pi\colon U\to C
given by the Lemma above satisfies
Df(u)v\neq 0
for any
u\in U\setminus C
and any
v\in\ker D\Pi(u)
.

Next, we check that the Allen-Cahn energy functional on

is Morse-Bott at the first three lowest critical values and describe the critical levels at the first min-max values for

. This characterization uses the convergence of the Allen-Cahn widths
[

]
and the description of the first

Proposition 1
.

There exists
\epsilon_{1}>0
with the following property. For every

is a Morse-Bott functional on
\{E_{\epsilon}\leq\omega_{5}(E_{\epsilon})=\omega_{6}(E_{\epsilon})=\omega_{7}(E_{\epsilon})\}
. Moreover,

has precisely four connected components:

(1)

the global minimizers
\{1\}
and
\{-1\}
(as constant functions),

(2)

the set

(3)

the set

Proof.

We first note that if

which have energy

is Morse-Bott at these constant critical points, for any

The fact that

is Morse-Bott on each set of critical points described in (2) and (3) follows from the rigidity results proved in
[
12
]
and
[
34
]
, respectively, provided we show that
\omega_{5}(E_{\epsilon})=\omega_{6}(E_{\epsilon})=\omega_{7}(E_{\epsilon})

such that

Finally, the regularity of these submanifolds is a consequence of the compactness of these critical manifolds, the symmetry of such solutions, and the following observation. Let

be a family of polynomial functions

.
∎

3.
Heteroclinic orbits for a perturbed flow

We would like to perturb the Allen-Cahn energy functional in
S^{3}
to obtain a Morse function defined on

with controlled energy and Morse indices near the critical manifolds given by the solutions at the energy levels
\omega_{1}(E_{\epsilon})=\inf\{E_{\epsilon}(u)\mid DE_{\epsilon}(u)=0,E_{\epsilon}(u)>0\}
– that is, the ground state solutions – and
\omega_{5}(E_{\epsilon})
, which are the solutions that vanish on a Clifford torus. This can be achieved by the following general perturbation argument, which is inspired by the construction of
[
4
,
5
]
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
X
be an open subset of a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and let
f\in C^{k+2}(X)
satisfy the Palais-Smale condition. Suppose that
C_{1},\ldots,C_{N}\subset\crit f
are disjoint compact
C^{k+2}
submanifolds of

, and that

is Morse-Bott with finite index at any point of

. Suppose also that

are
C^{k+2}
Morse functions, let
g:C\to\mathbb{R}
be the function such that
g|_{C_{i}}=g_{i}
, and let

be a tubular neighborhood of
C
. There exist
\delta_{1}>0
and
C^{k+2}
functions
f_{\delta}\colon X\to R
, for
\delta\in[0,\delta_{1})
such that
f_{0}=f
and, for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{1})
:

(i)

is a Morse function, and
f=f_{\delta}
outside
U
;

(ii)

there exist a smaller neighborhood

,

of
C
such that
f_{\delta}=f+\delta G
in
N_{r^{\prime}}(C)
, where

is an extension of

is the nearest point projection associated to

. In particular,

(iii)

(\crit f_{\delta})\cap U=\bigcup_{i}(\crit g_{i})
, and the Morse index of

(iv)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume

. We have that

and is supported in

It follows that

, and

At any critical point

for

\Pi^{\perp}
is the projection onto

proving the characterization of the Morse index

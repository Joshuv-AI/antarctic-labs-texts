m b ​ ( m Z 0 ) m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}}) revisited with Zedometry
S.Kluth

Abstract
Precision measurements of Z 0 \mathrm{Z^{0}} boson properties could enable a determination of the mass of the b quark at the scale of the Z 0 \mathrm{Z^{0}} boson mass m b ​ ( m Z 0 ) m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}}) . The dependence of Standard Model predictions on the b quark mass using the program Gfitter is studied. The precision of the currently available measurements by the LEP experiments and SLD, together with measurements from the LHC experiments of the mass of the top quark and the Higgs boson, is not sufficient for a relevant determination. The predicted precision of Z 0 \mathrm{Z^{0}} boson resonance measurements at future e + ​ e − \mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}} colliders will allow a competitive direct determination of m b ​ ( m Z 0 ) m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}}) .

Introduction

The theory of strong interactions, Quantum Chromo Dynamics
(QCD)
fritzsch73
;
gross73a
;
gross73b
;
politzer73
is a part of the
Standard Model (SM) of particle physics via QCD corrections to all
electroweak interactions
glashow61
;
weinberg67
;
salam79
involving
quarks. QCD has as the only free parameters the strong coupling
constant, usually given as its value at the
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
mass scale
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
,
and the masses of the six quarks of the SM. The values of the strong
coupling as well as of the quark masses depend on and decrease with
the energy scale of the interaction, which is known as asymptotic
freedom.

The mass of the b quark in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalisation
scheme
msbar
was determined from analysis of B-hadron mass
spectra and thus at energy scales corresponding to the B-hadron masses
with the current world average

m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{b}})=(4.183\pm 0.004)
GeV
pdg20
. Measurements of the mass of
the b quark at the energy scale of the
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
boson mass
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
were
performed by the LEP experiments using jet production with events
tagged by B-hadron decays and next-to-leading order QCD
calculations. A review
kluth06
quotes
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})=(2.90\pm 0.31)
GeV
where the error is dominated by experimental and hadronisation
systematic uncertainties. These results for the b quark mass in the

\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme are different by more than four standard deviations and
thus already provide strong evidence for the presence of a running b
quark mass. This finding was recently reproduced and
improved
aparisi21
.

The new analysis
aparisi21
studied the dependence of the
branching ratio of Higgs boson decays to a
\mathrm{b\overline{b}}
pair

\Gamma(H\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})
normalised to the branching ratio for
Higgs boson decays to a pair of
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
bosons. The branching ratio

\Gamma(H\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})\sim m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{H})^{2}
and therefore a measurement
is highly sensitive to
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{H})
. The analysis obtains

m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{H})=(2.60^{+0.36}_{-0.31})
GeV from combined recent ATLAS and CMS
measurements of

\Gamma(H\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})/\Gamma(H\rightarrow\mathrm{Z^{0}}\mathrm{Z^{0}})
, which
has the same precision as the combined LEP measurements of
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
.

We propose to study the dependence of
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
boson properties
connected with b quarks on the value of the b quark mass
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
used
in a SM prediction. The observables are the partial width

\Gamma(Z\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})
, the branching ratio

BR(Z\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})=\Gamma(Z\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})/\Gamma_{Z,tot}
,
where
\Gamma_{Z,tot}
is the total width of the
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
boson, and

\mathrm{R_{0,b}}=\Gamma(Z\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})/\Gamma(Z\rightarrow hadrons)
. A
comparison of these predictions with precision measurements of these
quantities can be used to extract
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
.

A determination of
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{H})
from
\Gamma(H\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})
assumes
the Yukawa coupling of the Higgs boson to b quarks to have the
expected SM value
bi20
;
aparisi21
. A complementary determination
of the b quark mass at high scales, i.e.
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
, but without direct
dependence on the b quark Yukawa coupling, will thus help to resolve
possible ambiguities.

We will briefly review the dependence of SM predictions for

\mathrm{Z^{0}}
boson properties on
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
, then explain the programs used to
obtain predictions, compare the predictions to measurements with
uncertainties as valid now and with uncertainties expected for the
proposed future facility FCC-ee
fcc18
.

2.2
zfitter predictions

The zfitter program implements heavy quark masses in the on-shell (OS)
scheme using a value of
m_{\mathrm{b}}^{\mathrm{OS}}=4.7
GeV. The on-shell mass is
converted in zfitter to the running mass in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme and
then evolved to the
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
mass scale. The zfitter program calculates
and prints the predictions of
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
properties such as

\Gamma(\mathrm{Z^{0}}\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})
via the routine
ZVWEAK
. The
value of the input OS b quark mass
m_{\mathrm{b}}^{\mathrm{OS}}
is varied over a range
from 1.0 to 7.0 GeV in steps of 0.2 GeV. The scan of
m_{\mathrm{b}}^{\mathrm{OS}}
is
repeated for three values of
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
given by
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})=0.1179\pm 0.0010
.

In Gfitter the

\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})=0.1179\pm 0.0010

applied. The calculations available in

4
Conclusions

We have studied the possibility to determine
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
from precision
measurements of
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
boson properties from LEP and SLD or the
proposed future
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
facility FCC-ee. The theory predictions for the
three observables
\Gamma(\mathrm{Z^{0}}\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})
,

BR(Z\rightarrow\mathrm{b\overline{b}})
, and
\mathrm{R_{0,b}}
are obtained from the program
Gfitter as functions of the b quark mass
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
, evolved from the
Gfitter input parameter
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{b}})
. A comparison of the predictions with
actual measurements from LEP and SLD does not provide a meaningful
extraction of
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
. Using expected central values with current
uncertainties corresponding to the input b quark mass of the
predictions we find that with the data from LEP and SLD the
uncertainties for
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
from the analysis of
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
boson properties
would be between one and two GeV. These uncertainties are much larger
than other existing determinations of
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
from jet production in
b-tagged events at LEP or
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{H})
from Higgs boson decays to b quark
pairs.

Using the expected uncertainties for measurements of
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
boson
properties at the future
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
collider FCC-ee, which are taken to be
smaller by a factor 1/10 w.r.t. the current uncertainties, a
determination of
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
with a relative error of 5% is possible. The
total uncertainty would be dominated by the experimental errors in all
cases. Depending on the observable either the uncertainty from the
current world average value of
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
or from the N3LO theory error
would be the second largest uncertainty.

At future
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
colliders determinations of
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{H})
with O(10) MeV
precision could be obtained
aparisi21
. The uncertainty for a
determination of
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
from jet production in b-tagged hadronic final
states in
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
annihilation at the proposed ILC based on a large
sample of
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
boson decays was estimated as

\Delta m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})=0.12
GeV
ildphyspub2021001
. This uncertainty is
dominated by theory uncertainties. Our proposed determination of

m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
is thus expected to have a compareable uncertainty with
complementary sources of uncertainties.

A precision determination of
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{\mathrm{Z^{0}}})
from
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
boson decays combined
with improved determinations of
m_{\mathrm{b}}(m_{H})
from Higgs boson decays would
constitute a new and stringent test of the SM. The b quark couples to
the
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
via the electroweak interaction and the observed mass is
related to the strong interaction while the Higgs boson couples to the
b quark directly via its Yukawa coupling. It will be interesting to
see such a test become reality with data from future experimental
facilities.

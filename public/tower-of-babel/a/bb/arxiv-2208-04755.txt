Abstract
. In this paper, we provide several characterisations for uniform amenability concerning a family of finitely generated groups. More precisely, we show that the Hulanicki-Reiter condition for uniform amenability can be weakened in several directions, including cardinalities of supports and certain operator norms.



Keywords: (Uniform) amenability, (Uniform) Property A, Hulanicki-Reiter condition.

1.
Introduction

The notion of amenability for groups was introduced by von Neumann
[
7
]
as the obstruction to the Banach–Tarski paradox
[
1
]
. Amenability admits a large number of characterisations, and is now fundamental in many areas of mathematics. There are a lot of excellent references on amenability and we guide interested readers to,
e.g.
,
[
9
,
11
,
12
]
.

On the other hand, Yu introduced the notion of Property A for discrete metric spaces
[
14
]
to attack the coarse Baum-Connes conjecture, and hence the Novikov conjecture in the case of groups. Property A can be regarded as a non-equivariant version of amenability, and has gained a lot of attractions due to its close relations with operator algebras and other areas of mathematics (see,
e.g.
,
[
13
]
).

In this paper, we mainly focus on the case of a family of groups and study a uniform version of amenability and Property A for the family. Roughly speaking, a family of groups is uniformly amenable if the quantifiers in the definition of amenability can be made uniform. More precisely, we consider the following:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of finitely generated groups with finite generating set
S_{i}
for
i\in I
. We say that
\{(G_{i},S_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is
uniformly amenable
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
D>0
and a family of functions
\{f_{i}:G_{i}\to[0,1]\}_{i\in I}
such that
\sum_{\gamma\in G_{i}}|f_{i}(\gamma)|^{2}=1
,
\sum_{\gamma\in G_{i}}|f_{i}(\gamma)-f_{i}(\gamma s)|^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}
for any
s\in S_{i}
and
\mathrm{supp}(f_{i})\subseteq B(1_{G_{i}},D)
.

The main contribution of this paper is to provide several characterisations for uniform amenability. To be more precise, we first consider the general case and obtain two conditions equivalent to uniform amenability (see Theorem
3.1
), which indicate that the uniform boundedness on the diameter of
\mathrm{supp}(f_{i})
in Definition
1.1
can be relaxed to the uniform boundedness on their cardinalities, or even to that on the
\ell^{1}
-norm of
f_{i}
. Furthermore, we realise that when the group
G_{i}
is amenable, the square of the
\ell^{1}
-norm of
f_{i}
coincides with the operator norm of certain linear bounded operator on
\ell^{2}(G_{i})
associated to the function
f_{i}
(see Proposition
3.6
). Therefore, in this special case we obtain two extra characterisations for uniform amenability, one of which also relates to uniform Property A (see Theorem
3.8
). Applying our main results to a sequence of finite groups, we obtain characterisations for Property A on their coarse disjoint union (see Corollary
3.9
).

The paper is organised as follows: After recalling the background notions and notation in Section
2
, we state and prove our main results in Section
3
. Finally, we give some couterexamples and open questions in Section
4
.

2.1.
Notions from coarse geometry

Let us start with some basic notions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a discrete metric space.

(1)

For
x\in X
and
R>0
, denote the closed ball
B(x,R):=\{y\in X:d(x,y)\leq R\}
.

(2)

We say that
(X,d)
has
bounded geometry
if
\sup_{x\in X}\sharp B(x,R)
is finite for any
R>0
. Here we use the notation
\sharp A
to denote the cardinality of the subset
A\subseteq X
.

(3)

For a subset
A\subseteq X
and
R>0
, denote
\mathcal{N}_{R}(A):=\{x\in X:d(x,A)\leq R\}
the
R
-neighborhood of
A
in
X
. Also denote
\partial_{R}A:=\{x\in X\setminus A:d(x,A)\leq R\}
the
R
-boundary of
A
in
X
.

(4)

For
R>0
, we say that a subset
A\subseteq X
is
R
-connected
if for any
x,y\in A
there exists a sequence
x_{0}=x,x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}=y
in
A
such that
d(x_{i},x_{i+1})\leq R
for
i=0,1,\cdots,n-1
.

(5)

For a subset
A\subseteq X
, we denote
\chi_{A}
the characteristic function of
A
. For
x\in X
, also denote
\delta_{x}:=\chi_{\{x\}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces and
f:X\to Y
be a map. We say that
f
is a
coarse embedding
if there exist functions
\rho_{\pm}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\rho_{\pm}(t)=+\infty
such that for any
x,y\in X
we have

\rho_{-}(d_{X}(x,y))\leq d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))\leq\rho_{+}(d_{X}(x,y)).

If additionally there exists
C>0
such that
Y=\mathcal{N}_{C}(f(X))
, then we say that
f
is a
coarse equivalence
and
(X,d_{X}),(Y,d_{Y})
are
coarsely equivalent
.

Discrete groups provide a large class of interesting examples of metric spaces with bounded geometry (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Chapter 1]
). For a finitely generated group
G
with a finite generating set
S
, there is the
word length function
\ell_{S}
defined by

\ell_{S}(g)=\min\{k:g=s_{i_{1}}^{\pm}s_{i_{2}}^{\pm}\cdots s_{i_{k}}^{\pm}\mbox{~where~}s_{i_{j}}\in S\mbox{~for~}1\leq j\leq k\}.

Note that
\ell_{S}
is a proper function on
G
in the sense that
\sharp\{g:\ell_{S}(g)\leq R\}
is finite for all
R\geq 0
. This leads to a discrete left-invariant metric
d_{S}
on
G
by
d_{S}(g,h):=\ell_{S}(g^{-1}h)
, which is clearly of bounded geometry. We call
d_{S}
the
word length metric
on
G
(associated to
S
). Gromov
[
4
]
observed that different finite generating sets
S
give coarsely equivalent word length metrics on
G
(see also
[
8
, Theorem 1.3.12]
).

Given a sequence of finite metric spaces, we can build a single metric space encoding the information of coarse geometry of the sequence in a uniform way:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\{(X_{n},d_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of finite metric spaces.
A coarse disjoint union
of
\{X_{n}\}_{n}
is a metric space
(X,d)
such that
X=\bigsqcup_{n}X_{n}
as a set and the metric
d
coincides with
d_{n}
on each
X_{n}
and satisfies:

d(X_{n},X_{m})\geq n+m+\mathrm{diam}(X_{n})+\mathrm{diam}(X_{m})\quad\mbox{for~each~}n\neq m\mbox{~in~}\mathbb{N}.

Note that the metric
d
in Definition
2.3
is
not
uniquely determined, however, it is easy to see that they are mutually coarsely equivalent to each other.

Also recall the following notion:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces and
f:X\to Y
be a map. Given
\varepsilon,R>0
, we say that the map
f
has
(\varepsilon,R)
-variation
if for any
x,y\in X
with
d_{X}(x,y)\leq R
, then
d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))\leq\varepsilon
.

2.2.
Amenability

The notion of amenability was originally introduced by von Neumann
[
7
]
as the obstruction to the Banach-Tarski paradox. Here we use the following equivalent definition due to Følner
[
2
,
3
]
:

Definition 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group equipped with a word length metric. We say that
G
is
amenable
if for any
R>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a finite subset
F\subseteq G
such that

\frac{\sharp\partial_{R}F}{\sharp F}\leq\varepsilon.

We also need an analytic characterisation for amenability. For a set
X
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, we denote

\ell^{p}(X):=\big\{f:X\to\mathbb{C}~\big|~\sum_{x\in X}|f(x)|^{p}<\infty\big\}.

For
f\in\ell^{p}(X)
, we endow the
\ell^{p}
-norm by
\|f\|_{p}:=(\sum_{x\in X}|f(x)|^{p})^{1/p}
. Also denote

\ell^{p}(X)_{1,+}=\big\{f\in l^{p}(X):\|f\|_{p}=1\text{ and }f(x)\geq 0\text{ for any }x\in X\big\}.

Given a group
G
, a function
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
and
\gamma\in G
, denote the
right
\gamma
-translation
of
f
by
\gamma\cdot f
. In other words,
(\gamma\cdot f)(h)=f(h\gamma)
for any
h\in G
. We also need to consider the
left
\gamma
-translation
of
f
defined by
(\gamma\star f)(h):=f(\gamma^{-1}h)
for
h\in G
.

The following result was essentially due to Hulanicki and Reiter
1
1

1

They originally consider the
\ell^{1}
-case, while this is easily seen to be equivalent to the
\ell^{2}
-case shown in Proposition
2.6
using the Mazur maps (see,
e.g.
,
[
13
, Definition (Theorem) 6.1.1]
).

[
6
,
10
]
:

Proposition 2.6

(Hulanicki-Reiter condition)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group with finite generating set
S
and the associated word length metric. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is amenable;

(2)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a function
f\in\ell^{2}(G)_{1,+}
such that
\|f-s\cdot f\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for any
s\in S
and
\mathrm{supp}(f)
is finite.

We remark that since word length metrics on groups are indeed graph metrics on their Cayley graphs, condition (2) above is also equivalent to the following: for any
\varepsilon>0
and
R>0
, there exists a function
f\in\ell^{2}(G)_{1,+}
such that
\|f-\gamma\cdot f\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for any
\gamma\in G
with
\ell_{S}(\gamma)\leq R
and
\mathrm{supp}(f)
is finite. This form resembles the original Hulanicki-Reiter condition for the
\ell^{1}
-case.

As mentioned in Section
1
, our main focus will be the notion of uniform amenability for a family of groups (see Definition
1.1
). Roughly speaking, it requires that condition (2) in Proposition
2.6
holds uniformly for the given family. In fact, this is also equivalent to a uniform version of the Følner condition (Definition
2.5
):

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of finitely generated groups with finite generating set
S_{i}
such that
\sup_{i}\sharp S_{i}<\infty
and the associated word length metric. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\{(G_{i},S_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is uniformly amenable,
i.e.
, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
D>0
and a family of functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that each
f_{i}\in\ell^{2}(G_{i})_{1,+}
satisfies
\|f_{i}-s\cdot f_{i}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for
s\in S_{i}
and
\mathrm{supp}(f_{i})\subseteq B(1_{G_{i}},D)
.

(2)

For any
R>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
D^{\prime}>0
and a family of finite subsets
\{F_{i}\subseteq G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that
\sharp\partial_{R}F_{i}/\sharp F_{i}\leq\varepsilon
and
F_{i}\subseteq B(1_{G_{i}},D^{\prime})
.

The proof follows directly from that of Proposition
2.6
(see,
e.g.
,
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.2]
) while noticing that the quantifiers can be uniformly controlled thanks to the assumption of
\sup_{i}{\sharp S_{i}}<\infty
, hence we omit the details. It is worth noticing that the condition
\sup_{i}{\sharp S_{i}}<\infty
is also equivalent to that the coarse disjoint union
\bigsqcup G_{i}
has bounded geometry.

We also remark that changing the generating sets
S_{i}
might change the coarse geometry of the family
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, since the control function
\rho_{\pm}
in Definition
2.2
might alter according to
i\in I
. Hence when talking about uniform amenability, we have to fix a generating set for each group in the family.

2.3.
Property A

The notion of Property A was introduced by Yu in his celebrated work
[
14
]
to attack the coarse Baum-Connes conjecture. Intuitively speaking, Property A is a non-equivariant version of amenability, hence these two notions enjoy analogous descriptions.

Definition 2.8
.

A discrete metric space
(X,d)
is said to have property A if for any
\varepsilon,R>0
there exists a collection of finite subsets
\{A_{x}\}_{x\in X}
in
X\times\mathbb{N}
and a constant
S>0
such that

(1)

\frac{\sharp(A_{x}\Delta A_{y})}{\sharp(A_{x}\cap A_{y})}\leq\varepsilon
for
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)\leq R
;

(2)

A_{x}\subset B(x,S)\times\mathbb{N}
for any
x\in X
.

Analogous to the case of amenability, we also need the following analytic characterisation for Property A due to Higson and Roe
2
2

2

Again note that the
\ell^{2}
-case shown in Proposition
2.9
is equivalent to the
\ell^{1}
-case using the Mazur maps (see,
e.g.
,
[

).

[

]
.

Proposition 2.9

(Higson-Roe condition)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a discrete metric space of bounded geometry. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(X,d)
has Property A;

(2)

For any
\varepsilon>0
and
R>0
, there exists a map
\xi:X\to\ell^{2}(X)_{1,+}
such that
\xi
has
(\varepsilon,R)
-variation and there exists a constant
S>0
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\xi(x))\subset B(x,S)
for any
x\in X
.

Remark 2.10
.

Note that when
X
comes from a finitely generated group equipped with a word length metric, then condition (2) above is also equivalent to the following: for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a map
\xi:X\to\ell^{2}(X)_{1,+}
such that
\xi
has
(\varepsilon,1)
-variation and there exists a constant
S>0
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\xi(x))\subset B(x,S)
for any
x\in X
.

It is well-known that for a finitely generated group
G
with a word length metric, amenability implies Property A. This can be easily seen either from the definitions or the analytic characterisations.

Similarly, we would also like to consider the following notion of Property A for the family case.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of discrete metric spaces. We say that
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
has
uniform Property A
if for any
\varepsilon>0
and
R>0
, there exists
D>0
and a family of functions
\{\xi_{i}:X_{i}\to\ell^{2}(X_{i})_{1,+}\}_{i\in I}
such that each
\xi_{i}
has
(\varepsilon,R)
-variation and
\mathrm{supp}(\xi_{i}(x))\subset B(x,D)
for any
x\in X_{i}
and
i\in I
.

3.
Main results

In this section, we present several versions of the main results under different hypotheses and provide detailed proofs. Let us start with the general case.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of finitely generated groups with finite generating set
S_{i}
for each
i
. Consider the following conditions:

(1)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
M>0
and a family of functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that each
f_{i}\in\ell^{2}(G_{i})_{1,+}\cap\ell^{1}(G_{i})
satisfies
\|f_{i}-s\cdot f_{i}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for
s\in S_{i}
and
\|f_{i}\|_{1}\leq M
.

(2)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N>0
and a family of functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that each
f_{i}\in\ell^{2}(G_{i})_{1,+}
satisfies
\|f_{i}-s\cdot f_{i}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for
s\in S_{i}
and
\sharp\mathrm{supp}(f_{i})\leq N
.

(3)

The family
\{(G_{i},S_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is uniformly amenable.

Then (1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3). Additionally, if there exists
L>0
such that
\sharp S_{i}\leq L
for each
i
, then the three conditions above are equivalent.

Note that when the index set
I
is finite, Theorem
3.1
holds trivially by an elementary approximation argument. Hence the main contribution of this result is on the uniformality of parameters.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we need the following auxiliary lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a set and
f:X\to[0,1]
be a function in
\ell^{2}(X)_{1,+}
with finite support. Then for any
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, there exists
c\geq 0
such that
\|f_{c}\|_{2}=\varepsilon
where
f_{c}(x):=\min\{f(x),c\}
for
x\in X
.

Proof.

It is clear that the function
F(c):=\|f_{c}\|_{2}
for
c\in[0,1]
is continuous since
\mathrm{supp}(f)
is finite. Note that
F(0)=0
and
F(1)=1
, hence the result follows from the Intermediate Value Theorem for continuous functions.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

“(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)”: Given
\varepsilon>0
, take
M>0
and a family of functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
satisfying condition (1) for
\varepsilon
and
M
. Approximating each
f_{i}
by finitely support functions, we can assume that each
f_{i}
has finite support. Set
\hat{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\varepsilon}{2+2\varepsilon}
. By Lemma
3.2
, there exists
c_{i}>0
for each
i\in I
such that
\|g_{i}\|_{2}=\hat{\varepsilon}
, where
g_{i}(\gamma):=\min\{f_{i}(\gamma),c_{i}\}
for
\gamma\in G_{i}
. Set
\mathrm{supp}_{\hat{\varepsilon}}(f_{i}):=\{\gamma\in G_{i}:f_{i}(\gamma)\geq c_{i}\}
.

We claim:
\sharp\mathrm{supp}_{\hat{\varepsilon}}(f_{i})
is uniformly bounded with respect to
i\in I
. In fact, we have:

\hat{\varepsilon}^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in G_{i}}g_{i}(\gamma)^{2}\leq c_{i}\cdot\sum_{\gamma\in G_{i}}g_{i}(\gamma)

since
g_{i}(\gamma)\leq c_{i}
for
\gamma\in G_{i}
. Then we have

M\geq\|f_{i}\|_{1}=\sum_{\gamma\in G_{i}}f_{i}(\gamma)\geq\sum_{\gamma\in G_{i}}g_{i}(\gamma)\geq\frac{\hat{\varepsilon}^{2}}{c_{i}},

which implies that
c_{i}\geq\hat{\varepsilon}^{2}/M
for all
i\in I
. On the other hand, we have

\hat{\varepsilon}^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in G_{i}}g_{i}(\gamma)^{2}\geq c_{i}^{2}\cdot\sharp\mathrm{supp}_{\hat{\varepsilon}}(f_{i})\geq\frac{\hat{\varepsilon}^{4}}{M^{2}}\cdot\sharp\mathrm{supp}_{\hat{\varepsilon}}(f_{i}).

Hence we obtain that
\sharp\mathrm{supp}_{\hat{\varepsilon}}(f_{i})\leq M^{2}/\hat{\varepsilon}^{2}
, which conclude the claim.

Back to the proof, let us define
h_{i}:=\frac{f_{i}-g_{i}}{\|f_{i}-g_{i}\|_{2}}
for
i\in I
. Then
\mathrm{supp}(h_{i})\subseteq\mathrm{supp}_{\hat{\varepsilon}}(f_{i})
, which has uniformly bounded cardinality by the claim above. Also for any
s\in S_{i}
, we have

\|h_{i}-s\cdot h_{i}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\|f_{i}-s\cdot f_{i}\|_{2}+\|g_{i}-s\cdot g_{i}\|_{2}}{\|f_{i}-g_{i}\|_{2}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon+2\hat{\varepsilon}}{1-\hat{\varepsilon}}=2\varepsilon.

Hence we conclude condition (2).

“(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)”: Given
\varepsilon>0
, take
N>0
and a family of functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
satisfying condition (2) for
\varepsilon
and
N
. For each
i\in I
, let
\{U_{i,k}\}_{k=1}^{N_{i}}
be the family of
2
-connected components of
\mathrm{supp}(f_{i})
, and denote
f_{i}^{(k)}:=f_{i}\cdot\chi_{U_{k}}
for each
k
. Note that the family
\{U_{i,k}\}_{k=1}^{N_{i}}
is mutually at least
3
-apart from each other, hence for any
s\in S_{i}
we have:

\varepsilon^{2}\geq\|f_{i}-s\cdot f_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{i}}\|f_{i}^{(k)}-s\cdot f_{i}^{(k)}\|_{2}^{2}.

Also note that
N_{i}\leq N
and for each
i\in I
and
k=1,\cdots,N_{i}
, there exists
\alpha_{i,k}\in G_{i}
such that
U_{i,k}\subseteq B(\alpha_{i,k},2N)
.

If
\mathrm{diam}(G_{i})\leq 4(N+1)N
, then we simply take
g_{i}:=\frac{1}{(\sharp G_{i})^{1/2}}\chi_{G_{i}}
. Otherwise, choose a point
\beta_{i}\in G_{i}
such that
d_{i}(\beta_{i},1_{G_{i}})=4(N+1)N
and a finite sequence of elements
1_{G_{i}}=\alpha^{\prime}_{i,1},\alpha^{\prime}_{i,2},\cdots,\alpha^{\prime}_{i,N_{i}}
on a geodesic connecting
1_{G_{i}}
and
\beta_{i}
such that
d_{i}(\alpha^{\prime}_{i,k},\alpha^{\prime}_{i,l})=4(N+1)|k-l|
for
k,l=1,\cdots,N_{i}
(note that we do not require
\alpha^{\prime}_{i,N_{i}}=\beta_{i}
). Set
\gamma_{i,k}:=\alpha^{\prime}_{i,k}\alpha_{i,k}^{-1}
for
k=1,\cdots,N_{i}
. Then we have the following:

(i)

d(\gamma_{i,k}\cdot U_{i,k},\gamma_{i,l}\cdot U_{i,l})\geq 3
for any
k\neq l
in
\{1,2,\cdots,N_{i}\}
;

(ii)

the union
\bigcup_{k=1}^{N_{i}}\gamma_{i,k}\cdot U_{i,k}
is contained in
B(1_{G_{i}},4(N+2)N)
.

Set
g_{i}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{i}}\gamma_{i,k}\star f_{i}^{(k)}
, where
\gamma_{i,k}\star f_{i}^{(k)}
is the left
\gamma_{i,k}
-translation of
f_{i}^{(k)}
defined in Section
2.2
. It follows from the construction (see condition (i) above) that

\|g_{i}\|^{2}_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{i}}\|\gamma_{i,k}\star f_{i}^{(k)}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{i}}\|f_{i}^{(k)}\|_{2}^{2}=\|f_{i}\|^{2}_{2}=1,

and

\displaystyle\|g_{i}-s\cdot g_{i}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{i}}\|\gamma_{i,k}\star f_{i}^{(k)}-s\cdot(\gamma_{i,k}\star f_{i}^{(k)})\|^{2}_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{i}}\|\gamma_{i,k}\star(f_{i}^{(k)}-s\cdot f_{i}^{(k)})\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{i}}\|f_{i}^{(k)}-s\cdot f_{i}^{(k)}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}

for
s\in S_{i}
, where we use the commutativity of the left translation with the right translation in the second equality. Finally, it follows from condition (ii) above that

\mathrm{supp}(g_{i})\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{N_{i}}\gamma_{i,k}\cdot U_{i,k}\subseteq B(1_{G_{i}},4(N+2)N).

Hence we conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

As mentioned above, the main contribution of Theorem
3.1
is to provide quantitative relations among these conditions. Indeed it follows from the proof that for
\varepsilon,M
in condition (1), we may choose
2\varepsilon,N=\frac{4M^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}
for condition (2). For
\varepsilon^{\prime},N^{\prime}
in condition (2), we may choose
\varepsilon^{\prime},4(N^{\prime}+2)N^{\prime}
for condition (3).

Note that in Theorem
3.1
, we do not require the assumption of amenability on each
G_{i}
at the very beginning, while this becomes an outcome of conditions therein. In the following, we will explore the situation when we already know that each
G_{i}
is amenable,
e.g.
,
G_{i}
is finite.

Let us first recall the following, which is the
\ell^{2}
-version of
[
8
, Theorem 4.4.4]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated amenable group with finite generating set
S
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
and
D>0
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a function
f\in\ell^{2}(G)_{1,+}
such that
\|f-s\cdot f\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for
s\in S
and
\mathrm{supp}(f)\subseteq B(1_{G},D)
.

(2)

There exists a map
\xi:G\to\ell^{2}(G)_{1,+}
such that
\xi
has
(\varepsilon,1)
-variation and
\mathrm{supp}(\xi_{x})\subseteq B(x,D)
.

Proof.

Applying the Mazur maps

M_{2,1}:\ell^{2}(G)_{1,+}\to\ell^{1}(G)_{1,+}\mbox{~defined~by~}(M_{2,1}f)(x):=f(x)^{2},x\in G

and

M_{1,2}:\ell^{1}(G)_{1,+}\to\ell^{2}(G)_{1,+}\mbox{~defined~by~}(M_{1,2}f)(x):=\sqrt{f(x)},x\in G,

it is easy to see (
e.g.
,
[
13
, Theorem 1.2.4, (2)
\Rightarrow
(3)]
) that condition (1) and (2) are equivalent to their
\ell^{1}
-counterparts (
i.e.
, replacing
\ell^{2}(G)_{1,+}
by
\ell^{1}(G)_{1,+}
therein) without changing the supports, respectively. Now we conclude the result thanks to
[
8
, Theorem 4.4.4]
.
∎

Note that the emphasis of the above lemma is on the parameter
D
. Hence it helps to bridge the condition of uniform amenability in Theorem
3.1
with uniform Property A, which will be packaged into Theorem
3.8
.

On the other hand, we would also like to explore an alternative description for condition (1) in Theorem
3.1
in terms of operator norms under the assumption of amenability, where the operator is defined as follows:

Definition 3.5
.

For a finitely generated group
G
and a function
f\in\ell^{1}(G)\cap\ell^{2}(G)=\ell^{1}(G)
with range in
[0,1]
, the
associated kernel of
f
is the
G
-by-
G
matrix
K_{f}
defined by
K_{f}(x,y):=\langle x\star f,y\star f\rangle_{\ell^{2}(G)}
for
x,y\in G
.

For any
G
-by-
G
matrix
K
with complex values, we can associate an operator
T_{K}
on the space
C_{c}(G)
of finitely supported complex-valued functions on
G
to itself by formal multiplication:

\left(T_{K}(h)\right)(\gamma):=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in G}K(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})h(\gamma^{\prime})

for any
h\in C_{c}(G)
and
\gamma\in G
. To simplify the notation, we denote
T_{f}
instead of
T_{K_{f}}
for
f\in\ell^{1}(G)
.

The following result relates the operator norm of
T_{f}
with the
\ell^{1}
-norm of
f
.

Proposition 3.6
.

For a finitely generated amenable group
G
and a function
f\in\ell^{1}(G)
with range in
[0,1]
, the operator
T_{f}
can be continuously extended to a bounded linear operator on
\ell^{2}(G)
with operator norm equals
\|f\|_{1}^{2}
.

Proof.

Given
\alpha=\sum_{\gamma\in G}\alpha(\gamma)\delta_{\gamma}\in C_{c}(G)
, we have:

\displaystyle\langle

\displaystyle T_{f}\alpha,\alpha\rangle_{\ell^{2}(G)}=\sum_{x,y\in G}\alpha(x)\overline{\alpha(y)}K_{f}(y,x)=\sum_{x,y\in G}\alpha(x)\overline{\alpha(y)}\cdot\langle y\star f,x\star f\rangle_{\ell^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y,z\in G}\alpha(x)\overline{\alpha(y)}f(x^{-1}z)f(y^{-1}z)=\sum_{z\in G}\left(\sum_{x\in G}\alpha(x)f(x^{-1}z)\right)\cdot\overline{\left(\sum_{y\in G}\alpha(y)f(y^{-1}z)\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in G}\left|\sum_{x\in G}\alpha(x)f(x^{-1}z)\right|^{2}=\|\alpha\ast f\|_{2}^{2},

where
\alpha\ast f
is the standard convolution of functions on groups. Note that
\alpha\in C_{c}(G)
and
f\in\ell^{1}(G)
, hence it follows from Young’s inequality that

\|\alpha\ast f\|_{2}\leq\|\alpha\|_{2}\cdot\|f\|_{1}.

Therefore using the polarization identity,
T_{f}
can be extended to a bounded linear operator on
\ell^{2}(G)
, still denoted by
T_{f}
. It is obvious that
T_{f}
is symmetric, hence we obtain:

\|T_{f}\|=\sup\big\{|\langle T_{f}\alpha,\alpha\rangle|:\alpha\in\ell^{2}(G)\mbox{~with~}\|\alpha\|_{2}=1\big\}\leq\|f\|_{1}^{2}.

Since
G
is amenable, we can choose a sequence of Følner sets
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
i.e.
, each
F_{n}
is a finite subset in
G
such that
F_{n}\subset F_{n+1}
,
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}=G
and

\frac{\sharp\big((F_{n}\cdot g)\Delta F_{n}\big)}{\sharp F_{n}}\to 0\mbox{~for~each~}g\in G.

It follows that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{x\in F_{m}}f(x)\geq\|f\|_{1}-\varepsilon
. For the finite subset
F_{m}
, the Følner condition above provides an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\frac{\sharp(F_{n}\setminus F_{n}g)}{\sharp F_{n}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\sharp F_{m}}

for each
g\in F_{m}
. Set
F_{n,m}:=\{x\in G:F_{m}\subseteq F_{n}^{-1}x\}=\bigcap_{g\in F_{m}}F_{n}g
. Then we have

\frac{\sharp(F_{n}\setminus F_{n,m})}{\sharp F_{n}}=\frac{\sharp\left(\bigcup_{g\in F_{m}}(F_{n}\setminus F_{n}g)\right)}{\sharp F_{n}}\leq\frac{\sum_{g\in F_{m}}\sharp(F_{n}\setminus F_{n}g)}{\sharp F_{n}}\leq\varepsilon,

which implies that

\frac{\sharp F_{n,m}}{\sharp F_{n}}\geq\frac{\sharp(F_{n,m}\cap F_{n})}{\sharp F_{n}}=1-\frac{\sharp(F_{n}\setminus F_{n,m})}{\sharp F_{n}}\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Now we have:

\displaystyle\|T_{f}\|

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\frac{1}{(\sharp F_{n})^{1/2}}\chi_{F_{n}}\ast f\right\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{\sharp F_{n}}\sum_{x\in G}\left|\sum_{y\in G}\chi_{F_{n}}(xy^{-1})f(y)\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{\sharp F_{n}}\sum_{x\in G}\left|\sum_{y\in F_{n}^{-1}x}f(y)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sharp F_{n}}\sum_{x\in F_{n,m}}\left|\sum_{y\in F_{n}^{-1}x}f(y)\right|^{2}\geq\frac{\sharp F_{n,m}}{\sharp F_{n}}\cdot(\|f\|_{1}-\varepsilon)^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\cdot(\|f\|_{1}-\varepsilon)^{2}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we conclude that the operator norm of
T_{f}
coincides with
\|f\|_{1}^{2}
.
∎

Thanks to Proposition
3.6
, we introduce the following notion:

Definition 3.7
.

For a finitely generated group
G
and a function
f\in\ell^{1}(G)
with range in
[0,1]
, the
associated operator of
f
is defined to be
T_{f}:=T_{K_{f}}
, which is a bounded linear operator on
\ell^{2}(G)
with operator norm
\|f\|_{1}^{2}
.

Consequently, combining Theorem
3.1
with Lemma
3.4
and Proposition
3.6
, we obtain the following:

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of finitely generated amenable groups with finite generating set
S_{i}
and the induced word length metric for each
i
. Consider the following conditions:

(1)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
M>0
and a family of functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that each
f_{i}\in\ell^{2}(G_{i})_{1,+}\cap\ell^{1}(G_{i})
satisfies
\|f_{i}-s\cdot f_{i}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for
s\in S_{i}
and
\|f_{i}\|_{1}\leq M
.

(2)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
M^{\prime}>0
and a family of functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that each
f_{i}\in\ell^{2}(G_{i})_{1,+}\cap\ell^{1}(G_{i})
satisfies
\|f_{i}-s\cdot f_{i}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for
s\in S_{i}
and the operator norm of the associated operator
T_{f_{i}}
in Definition
3.7
is bounded by
M^{\prime}
.

(3)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N>0
and a family of functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that each
f_{i}\in\ell^{2}(G_{i})_{1,+}
satisfies
\|f_{i}-s\cdot f_{i}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for
s\in S_{i}
and
\sharp\mathrm{supp}(f_{i})\leq N
.

(4)

The family
\{(G_{i},S_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is uniformly amenable.

(5)

The family
\{(G_{i},S_{i})\}_{i\in I}
has uniform Property A.

Then (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
\Rightarrow
(4)
\Leftrightarrow
(5). Additionally, if there exists
L>0
such that
\sharp S_{i}\leq L
for each
i\in I
, then all the conditions above are equivalent.

3.8
can be simplified as follows:

Let

\varepsilon>0

satisfies
\|f_{n}-s\cdot f_{n}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for

and

.

(2)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

4.
Questions

Recall that our main results (Theorem
3.1
and
3.8
) indicate that the uniform Hulanicki-Reiter condition is indeed equivalent to some formally weaker versions. One might ask whether the same phenomenon happens for the Higson-Roe condition. More precisely:

Question 4.1
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of finitely generated groups with finite generating set
S_{i}
for each
i
. Assume that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
M>0
and a family of maps
\{\xi_{i}:G_{i}\to\ell^{2}(G_{i})_{1,+}\cap\ell^{1}(G_{i})\}_{i\in I}
such that each
\xi_{i}
has
(\varepsilon,1)
-variation and
\|\xi_{i}(x)\|_{1}\leq M
for
x\in G_{i}
. Does the family
\{(G_{i},S_{i})\}_{i\in I}
have uniformly Property A?

It is easy to construct a counterexample and provide a negative answer:

Example 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}
be the free group generated by
S=\{a,b\}
, and
\{1\}=N_{0}\triangleleft N_{1}\triangleleft N_{2}\triangleleft\cdots
be a sequence of normal subgroups in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with finite index and trivial intersection. Take
G_{n}:=\mathbb{F}_{2}/N_{n}
and
S_{n}=\{aN_{n},bN_{n}\}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is non-amenable, the sequence
\{(G_{n},S_{n})\}_{n}
does not have uniform Property A. However, taking
\xi_{n}(g):=\delta_{N_{n}}\in\ell^{2}(G_{n})\cap\ell^{1}(G_{n})
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in G_{n}
, we obtain that each
\xi_{n}
has
(0,1)
-variation and
\|\xi_{n}(g)\|_{1}=1
for any
g\in G_{n}
. Hence we obtain a negative answer to Question
4.1
.

In fact, Question
4.1
has a negative answer even for finite
I
. One can just take a group without Property A and choose a function
\xi
as in Example
4.2
.

We realise that the obstruction to generalise the Higson-Roe condition is due to the lack of equivariance for the Higson-Roe functions, which allows too much flexibility for the choice. Hence in order to obtain Property A, we need some extra requirement on their supports. Currently we do not have any appropriate candidate, therefore we would like to pose the following question:

Question 4.3
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group. Is it possible to weaken the requirement on the support of the Higson-Roe functions as in Theorem
3.1
or
3.8
while still guaranteeing that
G
has Property A?

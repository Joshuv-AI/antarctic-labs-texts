Abstract
. We continue our study from [ 13 ] of finite dimensional definable groups in models of the theory T ∂ T_{\partial} , the model companion of an o-minimal ℒ \mathcal{L} -theory T T expanded by a generic derivation ∂ \partial as in [ 6 ] . We generalize Buium’s notion of an algebraic D D -group to ℒ \mathcal{L} -definable D D -groups, namely ( G , s ) (G,s) , where G G is a ℒ \mathcal{L} -definable group in a model of T T , and s : G → τ ⁡ ( G ) s:G\to\tau(G) is an ℒ \mathcal{L} -definable group section. Our main theorem says that every definable group of finite dimension in a model of T ∂ T_{\partial} is definably isomorphic to a group of the form ( G , s ) ∂ = { g ∈ G : s ⁡ ( g ) = ∇ g } , (G,s)^{\partial}=\{g\in G:s(g)=\nabla g\}, for some ℒ \mathcal{L} -definable D D -group ( G , s ) (G,s) (where ∇ ( g ) = ( g , ∂ g ) \nabla(g)=(g,\partial g) ). We obtain analogous results when T T is either the theory of p p -adically closed fields or the theory of pseudo-finite fields of characteristic 0 0 .

2.
Manifolds, tangent spaces and tangent bundles

We fix an o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
K
in a language
\mathcal{L}
. All definability in this section is in the o-minimal structure.

We first recall the basic definition of a differentiable manifold and its tangent bundle in the o-minimal setting (for differentiability in this context, see

[
4

).

Notation
Let
U\subseteq K^{r}\times K^{n}
be an open definable set, and
f:U\to K^{m}
a definable
C^{1}
-map, written as
f(x,y)
,
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
. Given
(a,b)\in U
,
we let
(D_{x}f)_{(a,b)}:K^{r}\to K^{m}
, and
(D_{y}f)_{(a,b)}:K^{n}\to K^{m}

denote the corresponding
K
-linear maps defined as follows:
(D_{x}f)_{(a,b)}
is the
m\times r
matrix of partial derivatives

\left(\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(a,b)\right)_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq r},

and
(D_{y}f)_{(a,b)}
is the
m\times n

\left(\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial y_{t}}(a,b)\right)_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq t\leq n}.

Then,
(Df)_{(a,b)}
is the
m\times(r+n)
-matrix

\left((D_{x}f)_{(a,b)},(D_{y}f)_{(a,b})\right).

For a
C^{1}
map

between open subsets of
K^{n}
and
K^{m}
, respectively, we write
Df:V\times K^{n}\to W\times K^{m}
, for the map

(a,u)\mapsto(f(a),(Df)_{a}\cdot u),

where
(Df)_{a}\cdot u=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(a)u_{i})_{j=1}^{m}.

2.1.
Definable manifolds and their tangent bundles

Definition 2.1
.

An
\mathcal{L}
-definable
C^{1}
manifold of dimension
r
, with respect to
K
, is a topological Hausdorff space
M
, together with a finite open cover
M=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}
, and homeomorphisms
\phi_{i}:V_{i}\to W_{i}
, where
V_{i}\subseteq K^{r}
is a definable open set, such that
V_{i,j}=\phi_{i}^{-1}(W_{i}\cap W_{j})
is a definable open subset of
V_{i}
,
and
each map
\phi_{i.j}=\phi_{j}^{-1}\circ\phi_{i}:V_{i,j}\to V_{j,i}
is a definable
C^{1}
-map (between definable open subsets of
K^{r}
).

The collection
\{(V_{i},W_{i},\phi_{i})_{i\in I}\}
is

for

Thus, we may identify
M
with the quotient of the disjoint union
\bigsqcup_{i}V_{i}
by the equivalence relation

a\sim_{M}b\Leftrightarrow b=\phi_{i,j}(a).

Definition 2.2
.

For
M
an
\mathcal{L}
-definable
C^{1}
-manifold of dimension

given as above, we let

T(M)
be the quotient of
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}\times K^{r}
by the equivalence relation, denoted by
\sim_{T(M)}
, given via the maps:

D\phi_{i,j}:V_{i,j}\times K^{r}\to V_{j,i}\times K^{r}\,\,;\,\,D\phi_{i,j}(c,u)=(\phi_{i,j}(c),D(\phi_{i,j})_{c}\cdot u).

We then write

T(M)=\bigsqcup_{i}V_{i}\times K^{n}\diagup\sim_{T(M)},

and
denote
(equivalence classes of) elements in
T(M)
by
[a,u]
,
a\in\bigsqcup_{i}V_{i}
,
u\in K^{r}
.

Note that if
M=U\subseteq K^{r}
is a definable open set with the identity atlas then
T(M)=U\times K^{r}
.

The following are easy to verify.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
M
and
N
are
\mathcal{L}
-definable,
C^{1}
-manifolds, given by atlases
(W_{i},V_{i},\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(U_{j},Z_{j},\psi_{j})_{j\in J}
. If
f:M\to N
is a
C^{1}
-map
(read through the charts)
then there is a well defined continuous map

satisfying, whenever the elements are in the appropriate

and

Dh([a,u])=[(f(a),D(\psi_{j}^{-1}\circ f\circ\phi_{i})_{a}\cdot u)].

Proof.

In fact, the map
(a,u)\mapsto(f(a),D(\psi_{j}^{-1}\circ f\circ\phi_{i})_{a}\cdot u)
induces a well defined map from

into
\bigsqcup_{j\in J}U_{j}\times K^{\dim N}
. The quotient by
\sim_{T(N)}
gives the desired map.∎

Lemma 2.4
.

(1)

For
M,N

\mathcal{L}
-definable,
C^{1}
-manifolds,
T(M\times N)=T(M)\times T(N)
.

(2)

(Chain rule) For

-definable

Summarizing, we have:

3.1.

-definable partial function on some open

Proof.

By Fact

-independent, and

-definable. By the

\partial f(a)=\partial g(a,b)=(Dg)_{(a,b)}(\partial a,\partial b)=

It follows that

Remark 3.7

Let

We briefly review the construction of the algebraic prolongation

, thus assume that

, and in particular,

, so
Dg_{(0,b)}=(D_{x}g_{(0,b)},0)\in M_{r\times(m+n)}(K)
.

To simplify notation below, we view

,

Notice that in order to show that

\|g(x,y)-g(0,b)-Dg_{(0,b)}\cdot(x-0,y-b)\|<\epsilon\|(x,y-b)\|,

which, since
g(0,b)=0

D_{y}g_{(0,b)}=0
, equals

(5.1)

\left\{(x,y)\in K^{m}\times K^{n}:\|g(x,y)-D_{x}g_{(0,b)}\cdot x\|<\epsilon\|(x,y-b)\|\right\}.

We fix
\epsilon\in\Gamma
.
By our assumption that

is differentiable at

\left\{x\in K^{m}:\|g(x,b)-D_{x}g_{(0,b)}\cdot x\|<\epsilon\|x\|\right\}.

By Claim

g(x,y)-D_{x}g_{(0,b)}\cdot x=g(x,y)-D_{x}g_{(0,y)}\cdot x+(D_{x}g_{(0,y)}-D_{x}g_{(0,b)})\cdot x.

Claim 5.6
.

There is
\delta_{2}\in\Gamma

\|(D_{x}g_{(0,y)}-D_{x}g_{(0,b)})\cdot x\|<\epsilon\|x\|.

\|D_{x}g_{(0,y)}-D_{x}g_{(0,b)}\|<\epsilon.
The result follows from our above observation.∎

If we now take

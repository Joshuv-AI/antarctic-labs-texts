Asymptotic distribution for the proportional covariance model
Myung Geun Kim

Abstract
Asymptotic distribution for the proportional covariance model under multivariate normal distributions is derived. To this end, the parametrization of the common covariance matrix by its Cholesky root is adopted. The derivations are made in three steps. First, the asymptotic distribution of the maximum likelihood estimators of the proportionality coefficients and the Cholesky inverse root of the common covariance matrix is derived by finding the information matrix and its inverse. Next, the asymptotic distributions for the case of the Cholesky root of the common covariance matrix and finally for the case of the common covariance matrix itself are derived using the multivariate δ \delta -method. As an application of the asymptotic distribution derived here, a hypothesis for homogeneity of covariance matrices is considered.



Keywords
: Asymptotic distribution, Cholesky decomposition, maximum likelihood estimators,
proportionality of covariance matrices.

1
Introduction

Let
{\bf X}_{k}
be a generic
p
-variate random vector having a multivariate
normal distribution

N_{p}(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{k},{\bf\Sigma}_{k})
for each
k=1,...,K,
where the
\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{k}
are
column vectors of the
p
-dimensional
Euclidean space and the
{\bf\Sigma}_{k}
are
p
by
p
positive definite covariance
matrices.
Let the
{\bf S}_{k}

be the mutually independent unbiased estimators of the
{\bf\Sigma}_{k}
,
each for a sample of size
N_{k}
and let
n_{k}=N_{k}-1
.
Then the
n_{k}{\bf S}_{k}
are
independently distributed according to Wishart distribution

W_{p}({\bf\Sigma}_{k},n_{k})
.

The hypothesis that
K
covariance matrices are proportional to each other
can be expressed as

H_{p}:{\bf\Sigma}_{k}=c_{k}{\bf\Sigma}_{1}\mbox{~~~~for all k = 2, ... ,K}

(1)

where the
c_{k}
are
unknown positive proportionality coefficients.
In the two sample case, estimation for proportional covariance models was studied by
Khatri(1967), Guttman, Kim and Olkin (1985) and Rao (1983). The proportional
covariance model was adopted in classification by 0wen (1984). An algorithm to find
the maximum likelihood estimators was given independently by 0wen (1984), Manly and
Rainer (1987) and Eriksen (1987). The convergence of the algorithm and the
uniqueness of maximum likelihood estimates were proved by Eriksen (1987). Using a
parametrization of the spectral decomposition of the common covariance matrix, Flury
(1986) considered maximum likelihood estimation of the proportionality coefficients
and the common covariance matrix, and suggested an iterative method for performing
computations. Under the same parametrization as in Flury (1986), Boente et al.
(2007, 2009) studied influence diagnostics and robust estimation for proportional
covariance models.

The Cholesky decomposition theorem states that there exists a lower
triangular matrix
{\bf A}
with positive diagonal elements such that

{\bf\Sigma}_{1}={\bf A}{\bf A}^{T},

and this expression is unique. We use
T
to denote a transpose of a matrix. Then
the model (
1
) is parametrized as

{\bf\Sigma}_{k}=c_{k}{\bf A}{\bf A}^{T}~~~(1\leq k\leq K).

(2)

For notational convenience, it
is assumed that
c_{1}
is identical with 1. The parametrization (
2
) can
be considered as the special case of the model treated by Jöreskog (1971) and
the easily accessible LISREL (linear structural relationships) program is available
for getting the maximum likelihood estimates of the model parameters (Jöreskog
and Sörbom, 1996).

Let
{\bf B}^{T}={\bf A}^{-1}
. The matrix
{\bf B}
is then an upper triangular matrix with
positive diagonal elements. Then the parametrization (
2
) can be written as

{\bf\Sigma}_{k}^{-1}=\frac{1}{c_{k}}{\bf B}{\bf B}^{T}~~~(1\leq k\leq K).

(3)

In this work, we derive the asymptotic distribution of the maximum likelihood
estimators of the proportionality coefficients and the common covariance matrix,
using the well-known properties of the maximum likelihood estimators under
regularity conditions (Rao, 1973, Chapter 6). The procedure for doing so is as
follows. Firstly, the information matrix for the parameters
c_{k}
and
{\bf B}
in
(
3
) is derived. Secondly, the asymptotic covariance matrix for the maximum
likelihood estimators of the parameters
c_{k}
and
{\bf B}
in (
2
) is found
by inverting the corresponding information matrix. Thirdly, we derive the asymptotic
covariance matrix for the maximum likelihood estimators of the parameters
c_{k}

and
{\bf A}
in (
2
), using the multivariate
\delta
-method. Finally, the
asymptotic covariance matrix for the maximum likelihood estimators of the
proportionality coefficients and the common covariance matrix is derived using the
multivariate
\delta
-method. Finally, a hypothesis for homogeneity of covariance
matrices is considered as an application of the asymptotic distribution derived
here.

2
The information matrix for the parameters
c_{k}
and
{\bf B}

In this section, we derive the information matrix for the

c_{k}
and
{\bf B}
. The likelihood function of the
{\bf\Sigma}_{k}
given the
{\bf S}_{k}

is

L({\bf\Sigma}_{1},...,{\bf\Sigma}_{K})=C\times\prod_{k=1}^{K}[\mid{\bf\Sigma}_{k}\mid^{-\frac{n_{k}}{2}}\mbox{exp}\{-\frac{n_{k}}{2}\mbox{tr}({\bf\Sigma}_{k}^{-1}{\bf S}_{k})\}],

where
C
is a constant not depending on the parameters. Since we are interested in
covariance matrices and a covariance matrix is location invariant, there is no
restriction on mean vectors.

Under the parametrization (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\mid{\bf\Sigma}_{k}^{-1}\mid

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{k}^{-p}\prod_{i=1}^{p}b_{ii}^{2}

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}({\bf\Sigma}_{k}^{-1}{\bf S}_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{p}{\bf b}_{i}^{T}{\bf S}_{k}{\bf b}_{i},

where
b_{ij}
denotes the
(i,j)
th element of
{\bf B}
and
{\bf b}_{i}
the

i
th column of
{\bf B}
. Thus the log-likelihood function, ignoring
constant term, becomes

l({\bf c},{\bf B})=n_{+}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mbox{log}(b_{ii})-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{2}\{p\mbox{log}(c_{k})+\frac{1}{c_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{p}{\bf b}_{i}^{T}{\bf S}_{k}{\bf b}_{i}\},

where
n_{+}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}n_{k}
and
{\bf c}=(c_{2},...,c_{K})^{T}
.
It is easily shown that all the expectations of
first derivatives of
l({\bf c},{\bf B})
with respect to the parameters are zero,
and the expectations of second derivatives of the log-likelihood function
are as follows:

\displaystyle-E\{\frac{\partial^{2}l({\bf c},{\bf B})}{\partial c_{k}^{2}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{pn_{k}}{2c_{k}^{2}}~~~~(2\leq k\leq K)

\displaystyle-E\{\frac{\partial^{2}l({\bf c},{\bf B})}{\partial b_{ii}^{2}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{+}(a_{ii}^{2}+{\bf a}_{(i)}^{T}{\bf a}_{(i)})~~~~(1\leq i\leq p)

\displaystyle-E\{\frac{\partial^{2}l({\bf c},{\bf B})}{\partial b_{ii}\partial c_{k}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{k}a_{ii}}{c_{k}}~~~~(2\leq k\leq K,~1\leq i\leq p)

\displaystyle-E\{\frac{\partial^{2}l({\bf c},{\bf B})}{\partial b_{ji}\partial c_{k}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0~~~~(2\leq k\leq K,~j<i)

\displaystyle-E\{\frac{\partial^{2}l({\bf c},{\bf B})}{\partial b_{ji}\partial b_{j^{\prime}i}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{+}{\bf a}_{(j)}^{T}{\bf a}_{(j^{\prime})}~~~~(1\leq j,j^{\prime}<i),

where
a_{ij}
denotes the
(i,j)
th element of
{\bf A}
and
{\bf a}_{(i)}
the
column vector formed by the elements in the
i
th row of
{\bf A}
,
and all the other cases have zero expectations.

Let
{\bf I}({\bf c},{\bf B})
be the information matrix for the parameters
{\bf c}
and

{\bf B}
, and let it be partitioned as

{\bf I}({\bf c},{\bf B})=\pmatrix{{\bf I}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})&{\bf I}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B})\cr{\bf I}_{21}({\bf c},{\bf B})&{\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})\cr},

such that
{\bf I}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
is the information matrix for
{\bf c}
and

{\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})
for the nonzero elements of
{\bf B}
.
Let
r_{k}=n_{k}/n_{+}.
Then
{\bf I}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
is a
diagonal matrix of order
K-1
:

\frac{p}{2}\mbox{diag}(\frac{r_{2}}{c_{2}^{2}},...,\frac{r_{K}}{c_{K}^{2}}).

We are interested in nonzero elements of
{\bf B}
,
b_{ij}~(i<j)
and
the number of nonzero elements of
{\bf B}
is
p(p+1)/2
.
Let
{\bf b}_{i.j}

=(b_{1i},...,b_{ji})^{T}.
Then we can notice that

{\bf b}_{i.1}=b_{1i}
and
{\bf b}_{i.p}={\bf b}_{i}.

Let
\alpha

=(r_{2}/c_{2},...,r_{K}/c_{K})^{T}.
We write as
{\bf 1}_{p.i}

the unit column vector
of dimension
p
having one in the
i
th position and
zeroes in the other positions.
Note that
{\bf 1}_{1.1}=1.

Then the
(K-1)
by
p(p+1)/2
matrix
{\bf I}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B})
is

\bordermatrix{&{\bf b}_{1.1}^{T}&{\bf b}_{2.2}^{T}&\ldots&{\bf b}_{p.p}^{T}\cr{\bf c}&-a_{11}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}&-a_{22}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}{\bf 1}_{2.2}^{T}&\ldots&-a_{pp}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}{\bf 1}_{p.p}^{T}}.

Let
{\bf A}_{i}
be the leading principal submatrix of
{\bf A}
having order
i
.
Note that
{\bf A}_{1}=a_{11}
and
{\bf A}_{p}={\bf A}.

Then the
p(p+1)/2
by
p(p+1)/2
matrix
{\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})
is
a block diagonal matrix whose
i
th block element for
{\bf b}_{i.i}

is given by

a_{ii}^{2}{\bf 1}_{i.i}{\bf 1}_{i.i}^{T}+{\bf A}_{i}{\bf A}_{i}^{T}.

This
i
th block element is of order
i
.

3
Asymptotic distribution of the maximum likelihood estimators
of the
c_{k}
and
{\bf B}

The asymptotic distribution of the maximum likelihood estimators of

{\bf c}
and
{\bf B}
is characterized by its asymptotic covariance matrix,
and the asymptotic covariance matrix is just the inverse of the
corresponding information matrix.

Let
{\bf V}({\bf c},{\bf B})
be the asymptotic covariance matrix for the maximum
likelihood estimators of the parameters
{\bf c}
and the nonzero elements
of
{\bf B}
, and let it be partitioned as

{\bf V}({\bf c},{\bf B})=\pmatrix{{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})&{\bf V}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B})\cr{\bf V}_{21}({\bf c},{\bf B})&{\bf V}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})\cr},

such that
{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
is the covariance matrix
for the maximum likelihood estimator of
{\bf c}
and

{\bf V}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})
for the maximum likelihood estimators of
the nonzero elements of
{\bf B}
.

The following lemma gives the inverse of
{\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})
and
enables us to get
{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
,

Lemma 3.1.
For each
i=1,...,p,
the inverse of

a_{ii}^{2}{\bf 1}_{i.i}{\bf 1}_{i.i}^{T}+

{\bf A}_{i}{\bf A}_{i}^{T}
is

{\bf B}_{i}{\bf B}_{i}^{T}-\frac{1}{2}{\bf b}_{i.i}{\bf b}_{i.i}^{T}

where
{\bf B}_{i}
is the leading principal submatrix of
{\bf B}
having
dimension
i
.

Lemma 3.1 is easily proved using the result (Sherman-Morrison
formula) by Bartlett (1951) and
the fact that
{\bf B}_{i}^{T}={\bf A}_{i}^{-1}.

Lemma 3.2.
The matrix
{\bf I}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})~-

{\bf I}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B}){\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})^{-1}{\bf I}_{21}({\bf c},{\bf B})
,

{\bf U}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
say, of dimension
K-1
is given by

\frac{p}{2}\{\mbox{diag}(\frac{r_{2}}{c_{2}^{2}},...,\frac{r_{K}}{c_{K}^{2}})-\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}^{T}\}.

Proof of Lemma 3.2.
The
i
th block element of

{\bf I}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B}){\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})^{-1}
is a
(K-1)
by
i
matrix
for the pair
{\bf c}
and
{\bf b}_{i.i}
and is given by

-\frac{1}{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}{\bf b}_{i.i}^{T}.

Then
{\bf I}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B}){\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})^{-1}{\bf I}_{21}({\bf c},{\bf B})

is given by

\frac{p}{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}^{T}

and therefore the proof is completed.

The inverse of
{\bf U}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
is just
{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})

and is stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3.
The
(K-1)
by
(K-1)
matrix
{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})

is given by

\frac{2}{p}\{\mbox{diag}(\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{r_{2}},...,\frac{c_{K}^{2}}{r_{K}})+\frac{1}{r_{1}}{\bf c}{\bf c}^{T}\}.

The matrix
{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
is easily derived using the result
by Bartlett (1951).

The matrix
{\bf I}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B}){\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})^{-1}
is derived in the
proof of Lemma 3.2 and

{\bf V}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B})~=

-{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B}){\bf I}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B}){\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})^{-1}
.
Hence we get easily the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4.
The
(K-1)
by
p(p+1)/2
matrix
{\bf V}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B})
is
given by the following matrix multiplied by
(1/pr_{1})
:

\bordermatrix{&{\bf b}_{1.1}^{T}&{\bf b}_{2.2}^{T}&\ldots&{\bf b}_{p.p}^{T}\cr{\bf c}&{\bf c}{\bf b}_{1.1}^{T}&{\bf c}{\bf b}_{2.2}^{T}&\ldots&{\bf c}{\bf b}_{p.p}^{T}}.

Since
{\bf V}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})~=

{\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})^{-1}-

{\bf I}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})^{-1}
{\bf I}_{21}({\bf c},{\bf B})
{\bf V}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B})
,
Lemma 3.1, the proof of Lemma 3.2 and Lemma 3.4 yield
{\bf V}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})
.

Lemma 3.5.

The
p(p+1)/2
by
p(p+1)/2
matrix
{\bf V}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf B})
is given by

\bordermatrix{&{\bf b}_{1.1}^{T}&{\bf b}_{2.2}^{T}&\ldots&{\bf b}_{p.p}^{T}\cr{\bf b}_{1.1}&{\bf B}_{1}{\bf B}_{1}^{T}+d{\bf b}_{1.1}{\bf b}_{1.1}^{T}&e{\bf b}_{1.1}{\bf b}_{2.2}^{T}&\ldots&e{\bf b}_{1.1}{\bf b}_{p.p}^{T}\cr{\bf b}_{2.2}&e{\bf b}_{2.2}{\bf b}_{1.1}^{T}&{\bf B}_{2}{\bf B}_{2}^{T}+d{\bf b}_{2.2}{\bf b}_{2.2}^{T}&\ldots&e{\bf b}_{2.2}{\bf b}_{p.p}^{T}\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr{\bf b}_{p.p}&e{\bf b}_{p.p}{\bf b}_{1.1}^{T}&e{\bf b}_{p.p}{\bf b}_{2.2}^{T}&\ldots&{\bf B}_{p}{\bf B}_{p}^{T}+d{\bf b}_{p.p}{\bf b}_{p.p}^{T}},

where
d=\{1-(1+p)r_{1}\}/2pr_{1}
and
e=(1-r_{1})/2pr_{1}
.

We use hereafter the
hat
notation to denote the maximum
likelihood estimator.

The general properties of the maximum likelihood estimators
yield the asymptotic distribution of
\hat{{\bf c}}
and
\hat{{\bf B}}

under the proportional covariance model.

Theorem 3.1.
As
n_{+}
tends to infinity with each
r_{k}

held fixed, the asymptotic distribution of

\sqrt{n_{+}}(\hat{{\bf c}}-{\bf c},\hat{{\bf B}}-{\bf B})

is a multivariate normal with mean zero and covariance matrix

{\bf V}({\bf c},{\bf B})
.

Lemma 3.3 shows that the asymptotic distribution of

\sqrt{n_{+}}(\hat{c}_{2}-c_{2},...,\hat{c}_{K}-c_{K})

is free of
{\bf\Sigma}_{1}
. Guttman et al. (1985) derived the asymptotic
distribution
of
\sqrt{n_{+}}(\hat{c}_{2}-c_{2})
when
K=2
and Lemma 3.3 reduces
to Theorem 4.1 of Guttman et al. (1985).

When
r_{1}=1
, that is, the single-population case, the coefficients

d
and
e
become
-1/2
and zero, respectively. Hence Lemma 3.5
shows that the
\hat{{\bf b}}_{i.i}

(i=1,...,p)
are asymptotically
independent.
By the invariance property of the maximum likelihood estimator,
the Cholesky inverse root of
{\bf S}_{1}
is just
\hat{{\bf B}}
,
that is,

{\bf S}_{1}^{-1}=\hat{{\bf B}}\hat{{\bf B}}^{T}.
Thus Lemma 3.5 gives the asymptotic
distribution of the Cholesky inverse root of the sample covariance matrix.

Asymptotic distribution of the maximum likelihood
estimators of the
c_{k}
and
{\bf A}

The asymptotic distribution of the maximum likelihood estimators
of the
c_{k}
and
{\bf A}
is derived from that of the
c_{k}
and

{\bf B}
, using the multivariate
\delta
-method.

Let
{\bf V}({\bf c},{\bf A})
be the asymptotic covariance matrix for the maximum
likelihood estimators of the parameters
{\bf c}
and
{\bf A}
. We write

{\bf V}({\bf c},{\bf A})
as a partitioned form:

{\bf V}({\bf c},{\bf A})=\pmatrix{{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf A})&{\bf V}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf A})\cr{\bf V}_{21}({\bf c},{\bf A})&{\bf V}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf A})\cr},

such that
{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf A})
is the covariance matrix
for the maximum likelihood estimator of
{\bf c}
and

{\bf V}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf A})
for the maximum likelihood estimators of
the nonzero elements of
{\bf A}
.

Since
{\bf B}^{T}={\bf A}^{-1}
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1.

The partial derivative of
a_{ji}
with respect to
b_{hg}
is

\frac{\partial a_{ji}}{\partial b_{hg}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}-a_{jg}a_{hi}&(i\leq h\leq g\leq j)\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Let
{\bf a}_{i.j}=(a_{1i},...,a_{ji})^{T}
and

{\bf a}_{i(-j)}

=(a_{j+1,i},...,a_{pi})^{T}
. Note that
{\bf a}_{i(-0)}
and

{\bf a}_{i.p}
are equivalent to
{\bf a}_{i}
, the
i
th column of
{\bf A}

and that
{\bf a}_{i.1}=a_{1i}
.
Then the Jacobian,
J({\bf A},{\bf B})
, of
{\bf A}
with respect to
{\bf B}
is

\bordermatrix{&{\bf b}_{1.1}^{T}&{\bf b}_{2.2}^{T}&{\bf b}_{3.3}^{T}&\ldots&{\bf b}_{p.p}^{T}\cr{\bf a}_{1}&-{\bf a}_{1}{\bf a}_{1.1}^{T}&-{\bf a}_{2}{\bf a}_{1.2}^{T}&-{\bf a}_{3}{\bf a}_{1.3}^{T}&\ldots&-{\bf a}_{p}{\bf a}_{1.p}^{T}\cr{\bf a}_{2(-1)}&{\bf 0}&-{\bf a}_{2(-1)}{\bf a}_{2.2}^{T}&-{\bf a}_{3(-1)}{\bf a}_{2.3}^{T}&\ldots&-{\bf a}_{p(-1)}{\bf a}_{2.p}^{T}\cr{\bf a}_{3(-2)}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&-{\bf a}_{3(-2)}{\bf a}_{3.3}^{T}&\ldots&-{\bf a}_{p(-2)}{\bf a}_{3.p}^{T}\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&\ldots&-{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}{\bf a}_{p.p}^{T}}.

Hence the Jacobian
J({\bf A},{\bf B})
is a block upper triangular matrix.

Under the transformation
{\bf B}^{T}={\bf A}^{-1}
, the asymptotic distribution
of
\hat{{\bf c}}
is not changed and in fact
{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf A})=

{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
.

Since
{\bf V}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf A})

={\bf V}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf B})J({\bf A},{\bf B})^{T}
,
we get the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2.
The
(K-1)
by
p(p+1)/2
matrix
{\bf V}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf A})
is
given by the following matrix multiplied by
-1/pr_{1}
:

\bordermatrix{&{\bf a}_{1}^{T}&{\bf a}_{2(-1)}^{T}&\ldots&{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}^{T}\cr{\bf c}&{\bf c}{\bf a}_{1}^{T}&{\bf c}{\bf a}_{2(-1)}^{T}&\ldots&{\bf c}{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}^{T}}.

Proof of Lemma 4.2.
By Lemma 3.4,
{\bf V}_{12}({\bf c},{\bf A})
has as
its
i
th block element for the covariance matrix of
\hat{{\bf c}}
and

\hat{{\bf a}}_{i(-i+1)}

-\frac{{\bf c}}{pr_{1}}\sum_{j=i}^{p}{\bf b}_{j.j}^{T}{\bf a}_{i.j}{\bf a}_{j(-j+1)}^{T}~=~-\frac{1}{pr_{1}}{\bf c}{\bf a}_{i(-i+1)}^{T}

Let

{\bf A}_{-0}={\bf A}
and

.

Lemma 4.3.

The
p(p+1)/2
by
p(p+1)/2
matrix
{\bf V}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf A})
is given by

\bordermatrix{&{\bf a}_{1}^{T}&{\bf a}_{2(-1)}^{T}&\ldots&{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}^{T}\cr{\bf a}_{1}&{\bf A}{\bf A}^{T}+d{\bf a}_{1}{\bf a}_{1}^{T}&e{\bf a}_{1}{\bf a}_{2(-1)}^{T}&\ldots&e{\bf a}_{1}{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}^{T}\cr{\bf a}_{2(-1)}&e{\bf a}_{2(-1)}{\bf a}_{1}^{T}&{\bf A}_{-1}{\bf A}_{-1}^{T}+d{\bf a}_{2(-1)}{\bf a}_{2(-1)}^{T}&\ldots&e{\bf a}_{2(-1)}{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}^{T}\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}&e{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}{\bf a}_{1}^{T}&e{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}{\bf a}_{2(-1)}^{T}&\ldots&(1+d){\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}{\bf a}_{p(-p+1)}^{T}},

where

and

Proof of Lemma 4.3.
The
(i,j)
th block element of
{\bf V}_{22}({\bf c},{\bf A})

is the covariance matrix of
\hat{{\bf a}}_{i(-i+1)}
and

, and is given by

th block element of

which is the covariance matrix of
\hat{{\bf b}}_{l.l}

and
\hat{{\bf b}}_{m.m}
. Let
\delta_{ij}
be the Kronecker delta.
Since
{\bf a}_{i.l}^{T}{\bf b}_{l.l}~=

{\bf 1}_{l.i}^{T}

which completes the proof.

The asymptotic distribution of

6
A hypothesis for homogeneity of covariance matrices

As an application of Theorem 5.1, consider a hypothesis for homogeneity
of covariance matrices

H_{0}:c_{2}=...=c_{K}=1.

(8)

The inverse
{\bf U}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
of
{\bf V}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf\Sigma}_{1})
is needed to
test the hypothesis (
8
), which is given in Lemma 3.2.

The asymptotic distribution of

n_{+}(\hat{c}_{2}-1,...,\hat{c}_{K}-1){\bf U}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})(\hat{c}_{2}-1,...,\hat{c}_{K}-1)^{T}

under (
8
) is a chi-squared distribution with degrees of freedom
K-1
.
However,
{\bf U}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
is still unknown and can be estimated by replacing

{\bf c}
with the corresponding maximum likelihood estimator. We denote the
estimated
{\bf U}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
by
\hat{{\bf U}}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
. Since

\hat{{\bf c}}
is a consistent estimator of
{\bf c}
,
\hat{{\bf U}}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})

converges in probability to
{\bf U}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})
and therefore a test statistic
for testing the hypothesis (
8
) is given by

n_{+}(\hat{c}_{2}-1,...,\hat{c}_{K}-1)\hat{{\bf U}}_{11}({\bf c},{\bf B})(\hat{c}_{2}-1,...,\hat{c}_{K}-1)^{T}

(9)

whose asymptotic distribution under (
8
) is a
chi-squared distribution with
K-1
degrees of freedom (Rao, 1973, Chapter 6).
The test statistic (
9
) can be expressed
in a simple form

n_{+}\{\frac{pr_{1}(1-r_{1})}{2}-pr_{1}\sum_{k=2}^{K}\frac{r_{k}}{\hat{c}_{k}}+\frac{p}{2}[\sum_{k=2}^{K}\frac{r_{k}}{\hat{c}_{k}^{2}}-(\sum_{k=2}^{K}\frac{r_{k}}{\hat{c}_{k}})^{2}]\}

=\frac{n_{+}p}{2}[\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{r_{k}}{\hat{c}_{k}^{2}}-(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{r_{k}}{\hat{c}_{k}})^{2}],

where
\hat{c}_{1}\equiv 1
.

Introduction

One never-ending source of inspiration in Combinatorial Commutative Algebra is the study of the minimal free resolutions of graded ideals. Let
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
, and let
I\subset R
be a graded ideal. The behaviour of the minimal resolution of
I
is hard to predict. Two important homological invariants of
I
, that provide a measure of the complexity of its minimal resolution, are the
projective dimension
,
\textup{proj\,dim}(I)
, and the
regularity
,
\textup{reg}(I)
. The pair
(\textup{proj\,dim}(I),\textup{reg}(I))
determines the size of the
Betti table
of
I
.

A central question is the following. For a given class
\mathcal{C}
of graded ideals of
R
, can we determine the set of the sizes of the Betti tables of the ideals in
\mathcal{C}
? That is, can we determine all pairs
(\textup{proj\,dim}(I),\textup{reg}(I))
,
I\in\mathcal{C}
? Such a problem is very difficult, and the behaviour of these pairs is quite mysterious. On the other hand, if the graded ideals in
\mathcal{C}
arise from combinatorics, then their combinatorial nature helps us to better understand and sometimes also answer such a question.

A problem of the type discussed above is considered in
[
9
]
. Hereafter, by a graph
G
we mean a finite simple graph. Recall that the
edge ideal

I(G)
of
G
is the ideal generated by the monomials
x_{i}x_{j}
where
\{i,j\}
is an edge of
G
. In
[
9
]
, Hà and Hibi studied the question of determining all admissible pairs
(\textup{proj\,dim}(I(G)),\textup{reg}(I(G)))
, as
G
ranges over all graphs on a given number of vertices. This question is related to the search of a
max min vertex cover
and a
min max independent set
. These classical problems of graph theory are known to be NP-hard, and they received a lot of attention lately
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
10
]
. On the other hand, in
[
9
]
the combinatorics of
G
yields the following surprising lower bound:
\textup{proj\,dim}(I(G))\geq 2\sqrt{n}-3
. The authors determined all pairs
(\textup{proj\,dim}(I(G)),\textup{reg}(I(G)))
when the projective dimension reaches this lower bound, and also when the regularity reaches its minimal possible value, namely
\textup{reg}(I(G))=2
. For the class of connected bipartite graphs, the Hà–Hibi problem was completely solved by Erey and Hibi in
[
7
]
. Similar questions are treated in
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
and the references therein.

Another family of graded ideals arising from graphs is that of
binomial edge ideals
. In 2010, Herzog, Hibi, Hreinsdóttir, Kahle and Rauh in
[
11
]
, and independently, Ohtani in
[
24
]
, introduced the
binomial edge ideal
. Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices
1,\dots,n
. Then
J_{G}
is defined to be the graded ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
generated by the binomials
x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
for all edges
\{i,j\}
of
G
. This class of ideals generalizes the classical determinantal ideals. Indeed,
J_{G}
may be seen as the ideal generated by an arbitrary set of maximal minors of a
(2\times n)
-matrix of indeterminates. A huge effort has been made to understand the homological properties of binomial edge ideals. Consult the surveys
[
5
,
28
]
for the current state of art.

In this article, we address the Hà–Hibi problem for the class of binomial edge ideals. One would guess, as in the case of edge ideals, that this problem is difficult, and that answering this question in an explicit fashion may not be possible. Quite surprisingly, we succeed to deliver a fairly comprehensive and explicit solution.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. Denote by
\textup{Graphs}(n)
the class of all finite simple graphs on
n
non-isolated vertices. Then, we define

\textup{pdreg}(n)\ =\ \big\{(\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G}),\textup{reg}(J_{G})):G\in\textup{Graphs}(n)\big\},

which is the set of the sizes of the Betti tables of
J_{G}
, as
G
ranges over all graphs on
n
non-isolated vertices. Our main result in this article is the following theorem.

Theorem
3.1

For all
n\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\textup{pdreg}(n)\ =\ \big\{(n-2,2),(n-2,n)\big\}\cup\bigcup_{r=3}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1}\big(\bigcup_{p=n-r}^{2n-5}\{(p,r)\}\big)\ \cup

\displaystyle\cup\bigcup_{r=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1}^{n-2}\big(\bigcup_{p=r-2}^{2n-5}\{(p,r)\}\big)\cup A_{n},

where
A_{n}=\{(p,r)\in\textup{pdreg}(n):r=n-1\}
.

The reader may see that when the regularity is
n-1
we leave the set
A_{n}
not determined. Indeed, our experiments show a quite unexpected behaviour.

Conjecture
3.3
Let
G
be a graph on
n\geq 7
non-isolated vertices. Suppose that
\textup{reg}(J_{G})=n-1
. Then
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})\leq n
.

The article is structured as follows. In Section
1
, we state some general bounds for the projective dimension and the regularity of binomial edge ideals. The proof of Theorem
3.1
is by induction on the number of vertices of the graph. On the other hand, there are some special graphs giving some of the pairs
(p,r)\in\textup{pdreg}(n)
that we did not obtain by inductive arguments. In Section
2
, we discuss these special classes of graphs. They give the pairs
(p,r)\in\textup{pdreg}(n)
with
p=2n-5,2n-6
, or
r=3,n-2
. Section
3
contains the main result in the article. Our answer is nearly complete. Indeed, only for the graphs with almost maximal regularity, namely
\textup{reg}(J_{G})=n-1
, we do not know yet the projective dimension. It would be interesting to classify the binomial edge ideals with almost maximal regularity.

1.
General bounds for the betti table of binomial edge ideals

Let
I
be a graded ideal of a standard graded polynomial ring
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, where
K
is a field. Then
I
possesses a unique minimal graded free resolution

\mathbb{F}\ \ :\ \ \cdots\rightarrow F_{i}\rightarrow F_{i-1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0,

with
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}R(-j)^{\beta_{i,j}(I)}
, where the
\beta_{i,j}(I)
are the
graded Betti numbers
of
I
. The
projective dimension
and the
regularity
of
I
, are, respectively,

\displaystyle\textup{proj\,dim}(I)\

\displaystyle=\ \max\{i:\beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0,\ \text{for some}\ j\},

\displaystyle\textup{reg}(I)\

\displaystyle=\ \max\{j-i:\beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0,\ \text{for some}\ i\ \text{and}\ j\}.

Throughout the article, we consider only finite simple graphs. Hence we will refer to them simply as graphs. Let
G
be a graph. By
V(G)
we denote the
vertex set
of
G
, and by
E(G)
the
edge set
of
G
. Two vertices
u
and
v
are
adjacent
if
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
. A vertex
u
is called
isolated
if
\{u,v\}\notin E(G)
for all
v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}
.

Let
K
be a field and
G
be a graph with vertex set
\{1,\dots,n\}
. The
binomial edge ideal

J_{G}
of
G
is the following binomial ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
:

J_{G}=(x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}\ :\ \{i,j\}\in E(G)).

Let
G
be a graph. If
W
is a subset of
V(G)
we denote by
G_{W}
the
induced subgraph of
G
on
W
, that is
V(G_{W})=W
and
E(G_{W})=\{\{u,v\}\in E(G):u,v\in W\}
. It is known by
[
21
, Corollary 2.2]
that

\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})\geq\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G_{W}})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \textup{reg}(J_{G})\geq\textup{reg}(J_{G_{W}}),

for all subsets
W
of
V(G)
. We will use freely this fact.

Suppose that
G
is connected. Then, we say that
G
is
\ell
-vertex-connected
if for all subsets
W
of
V(G)
with
|W|<\ell
, the induced subgraph
G_{V(G)\setminus W}
is connected. The
vertex-connectivity
of
G
, denoted by
\ell(G)
, is the maximum integer
\ell
such that
G
is
\ell
-vertex-connected. It is clear that
\ell(G)\geq 1
.

We denote by
K_{n}
the
complete graph
on
n
vertices, that is
V(K_{n})=\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\{i,j\}\in E(K_{n})
for all
i,j\in V(K_{n})
,
i\neq j
. Whereas, by
P_{n}
we denote the
path
of length
n-1
, that is
V(P_{n})=\{1,\dots,n\}
and
E(P_{n})=\{\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\ldots,\{n-1,n\}\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n\geq 3
non-isolated vertices. Then

\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})\leq 2n-5.

Furthermore, if
G
is connected on
n\geq 2
vertices, then
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})\geq n-2
.

Proof.

By the work of
[
26
, Theorem 5.2]
it is known that
\textup{depth}(S/J_{G})\geq 4
for all graphs
G
. Therefore, by the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula we have

\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=\textup{proj\,dim}(S/J_{G})-1=2n-\textup{depth}(S/J_{G})-1\leq 2n-5.

Suppose that
G
is connected and not complete. By
[
1
, Theorems 3.19 and 3.20]
we have
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=\textup{proj\,dim}(S/J_{G})-1\geq n+\ell(G)-3
. Since
\ell(G)\geq 1
, we obtain
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})\geq n-2
. Else, if
G=K_{n}
is the complete graph, then
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=n-2
because the Eagon-Northcott complex is the minimal free resolution of
J_{K_{n}}
.

∎

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n\geq 2
non-isolated vertices. Then

2\leq\textup{reg}(J_{G})\leq n.

Moreover,

(i)

\textup{reg}(J_{G})=2
if and only if
G=K_{n}
and, in this case,
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{K_{n}})=n-2
.

(ii)

\textup{reg}(J_{G})=n
if and only if
G=P_{n}
and, in this case,
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{P_{n}})=n-2
.

Proof.

Since
J_{G}
is generated in degree two,
\textup{reg}(J_{G})\geq 2
. By
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\textup{reg}(J_{G})\leq|V(G)|=n
. Statement (i) follows from
[
29
, Theorem 2.1]
. Statement (ii) follows from
[
19
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
.

∎

The following observation will be used several times.

Remark 1.3
.

Suppose
G=G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}\sqcup\dots\sqcup G_{c}
is a graph without isolated vertices and
c
connected components
G_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,c
. Here
\sqcup
denotes the disjoint union of the graphs
G_{i}
. Let
S_{i}=K[x_{v},y_{v}:v\in V(G_{i})]
and
n_{i}=|V(G_{i})|
, for all
i
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{c}n_{i}=n
and
J_{G_{i}}
is a binomial ideal of
S_{i}
. Since the polynomial rings
S_{i}
are in pairwise disjoint sets of variables, we have

S/J_{G}\cong\bigotimes_{i=1}^{c}S_{i}/J_{G_{i}}.

Hence,
\textup{proj\,dim}(S/J_{G})=\sum_{i=1}^{c}\textup{proj\,dim}(S_{i}/J_{G_{i}})
and
\textup{reg}(S/J_{G})=\sum_{i=1}^{c}\textup{reg}(S_{i}/J_{G_{i}})
. Taking into account that for a graded ideal
I
of a polynomial ring
R
we have
\textup{proj\,dim}(R/I)=\textup{proj\,dim}(I)+1
and
\textup{reg}(R/I)=\textup{reg}(I)-1
, we obtain the following useful identities,

\displaystyle\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=1}^{c}\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G_{i}})+(c-1),

(1)

\displaystyle\textup{reg}(J_{G})\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=1}^{c}\textup{reg}(J_{G_{i}})-(c-1).

(2)

Next we provide a different lower bound for the projective dimension of a binomial edge ideal, in terms of the regularity.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
r
be positive integers with
3\leq r\leq n-1
. Then

\max\{n-r,r-2\}\leq\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})\leq 2n-5,

for all graphs
G
on
n
non-isolated vertices such that
\textup{reg}(J_{G})=r
.

Proof.

The upper bound for
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})
is stated in Theorem
1.1
. To prove the lower bound, assume the notation of Remark
1.3
. Notice that

\max\{n-r,r-2\}\ =\ \begin{cases}n-r,&\textup{if}\,\ 3\leq r\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1,\\
r-2,&\textup{if}\,\ \lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1\leq r\leq n-1.\end{cases}

In view of this computation, we distinguish the two possible cases.

Suppose
\max\{n-r,r-2\}=n-r
. By formula (
2
) and Theorem
1.2
,

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=1}^{c}\textup{reg}(J_{G_{i}})-(c-1)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{c}2-(c-1)

(3)

\displaystyle=\ 2c-c+1=c+1.

Therefore,
c\leq r-1
. Now, by formula (
1
) and Theorem
1.1
,

\displaystyle\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=1}^{c}\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G_{i}})+(c-1)

(4)

\displaystyle\geq\ \sum_{i=1}^{c}(n_{i}-2)+(c-1)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=1}^{c}n_{i}-2c+c-1

\displaystyle=\ n-c-1\geq n-r,

since
c\leq r-1
.

Suppose now
\max\{n-r,r-2\}=r-2
. By formula (
2
) and Theorem
1.2
,

\displaystyle r\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=1}^{c}\textup{reg}(J_{G_{i}})-(c-1)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{c}n_{i}-(c-1)

\displaystyle=\ n-c+1.

Thus,
c\leq n-r+1
. Using the computation in (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})\

\displaystyle\geq\ n-c-1\geq r-2,

since
c\leq n-r+1
.

∎

2.
Special classes of graphs

In this section, we determine some classes of graphs that have a given projective dimension or a given regularity. These families will be used to get our main result.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be two graphs with disjoint vertex sets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
and edge sets
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. The
join
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is defined to be the graph
G_{1}*G_{2}
with vertex set
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
and edge set
E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup\{\{v_{1},v_{2}\}:v_{1}\in V_{1},v_{2}\in V_{2}\}
.

The following formula due to Madani and Kiani (
[
30
, Theorem 2.1]
) plays a pivotal role in our article. Suppose
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are graphs with disjoint vertex sets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, and that not both of them are complete. Then,

\textup{reg}(J_{G_{1}*G_{2}})=\max\{\textup{reg}(J_{G_{1}}),\textup{reg}(J_{G_{2}}),3\}.

(5)

It is worth mentioning that, even if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
may have isolated vertices, all vertices in
G_{1}*G_{2}
are non-isolated, and
G_{1}*G_{2}
is always a connected graph. Moreover, if
G_{1}\neq K_{1}
and
G_{2}=K_{1}
, then
G_{1}*G_{2}
is called a
cone
.

2.1.
Graphs with projective dimension
2n-5

In
[
26
, Theorem 5.3]
, Malayeri, Madani and Kiani have characterized all graphs
G
that have minimal depth possible,
i.e.
,
\textup{depth}(S/J_{G})=4
. For such graphs, by the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula we have maximal projective dimension:

\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=\textup{proj\,dim}(S/J_{G})-1=2n-\textup{depth}(S/J_{G})-1=2n-5.

Hereafter, for a positive integer
m\geq 1
, we denote by
mK_{1}
a graph consisting of
m
isolated vertices.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n\geq 5
non-isolated vertices. Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=2n-5
.

(ii)

G=\widetilde{G}*2K_{1}
for some graph
\widetilde{G}
on
n-2
vertices.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n\geq 5
non-isolated vertices with
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=2n-5
. Then,

3\leq\textup{reg}(J_{G})\leq n-2.

Furthermore, for any integers
n\geq 5
and
r\in\{3,\dots,n-2\}
, there exists a graph
G
on
n
non-isolated vertices such that
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=2n-5
and
\textup{reg}(J_{G})=r
.

Proof.

By the previous theorem,
G=\widetilde{G}*2K_{1}
for some graph
\widetilde{G}
on
n-2
vertices. Since
2K_{1}
is not complete and
J_{2K_{1}}=(0)
, by formula (
5
),

\textup{reg}(J_{G})=\max\{\textup{reg}(J_{\widetilde{G}}),\textup{reg}(J_{2K_{1}}),3\}=\max\{\textup{reg}(J_{\widetilde{G}}),3\}.

Hence
\textup{reg}(J_{G})\geq 3
. Moreover, by Theorem
1.2
,
\textup{reg}(J_{\widetilde{G}})\leq|V(\widetilde{G})|=n-2
and so
\textup{reg}(J_{G})\leq n-2
, since
n-2\geq 3
.

Now, let
r\in\{3,\dots,n-2\}
. Set
\widetilde{G}=P_{r}\sqcup(n-2-r)K_{1}
and
G=\widetilde{G}*2K_{1}
. Then
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=2n-5
, by the previous theorem. Moreover,

\textup{reg}(J_{G})=\max\{\textup{reg}(J_{\widetilde{G}}),\textup{reg}(J_{2K_{1}}),3\}=\max\{r,3\}=r,

since
J_{\widetilde{G}}=J_{P_{r}}
has regularity
r
by Theorem
1.2
(ii).

∎

2.2.
Graphs with projective dimension
2n-6

After the case of minimal depth, Malayeri, Madani and Kiani classified in
[

the graphs

with

, that is

.

To state their result, we introduce the following class of graphs. Hereafter, if

the set

,

.

Let

Now, we introduce the class of

.

The following observation will be useful later.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
n\geq 5
. By Theorems
2.1
and

, it follows that all graphs

on

non-isolated vertices such that
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=2n-5
or

For the proof of the next result, we need a lemma of Ohtani
[
24
, Lemma 4.8]
, see also
[
20
, Lemma 3.1]
and formula (1) in the same article.

We recall that a
clique
of a graph
G
is a subset
W
of
V(G)
such that
G_{W}
is an induced complete subgraph of
G
. A
maximal clique
of
G
is a clique of
G
that is not contained in any other clique of
G
. A vertex

is called
simplicial
if
N_{G}(v)
is a clique of
G
, otherwise is called
internal
. Let
v\in V(G)
. We denote
G_{V(G)\setminus\{v\}}
by
G\setminus v
. Whereas, by
G_{v}
we denote the graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)\cup\{\{w_{1},w_{2}\}:w_{1},w_{2}\in N_{G}(v)\}
.

Let
v
be an internal vertex of a graph
G
. Then Ohtani lemma, see also formula (1) in
[
20
]
, implies that the following short sequence is exact:

0\rightarrow\frac{S}{J_{G}}\longrightarrow\frac{S}{(x_{v},y_{v},J_{G\setminus v})}\oplus\frac{S}{J_{G_{v}}}\longrightarrow\frac{S}{(x_{v},y_{v},J_{G_{v}\setminus v})}\rightarrow 0.

(6)

In the next proposition we also use freely the following upper bound for the regularity proved in
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
, see also formula (3) in the same article. For a connected graph
G
on
n
vertices,

\textup{reg}(J_{G})\leq n+2-|W|,\ \ \ \text{for any clique}\ W\ \text{of}\ G.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
G

\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=2n-m-4
and
\textup{reg}(J_{G})=n-2
. Since

\{2n-m-4:m=1,\dots,n-3\}=\{n-1,n,\dots,2n-5\},

the claim will conclude the proof.

Firstly, we note that if
m=1
, then
G=G_{[n-2]}*G_{\{n-1,n\}}
. Since
G_{[n-2]}
is a path on
n-2
vertices and
G_{\{n-1,n\}}
consists of two isolated vertices, by Theorem
2.1
and formula (
5
) we obtain

\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=2n-5,\ \ \ \textup{reg}(J_{G})=n-2.

If
m=2
, then
G
is a
D_{5}
-type graph. Indeed,
G\in\mathcal{G}_{[n-2]}
. To see why this is true, using the notation of Definition
2.3
, it is enough to set
u=n-1
,
v=1
,
V_{0}=\{2\}
,
V_{1}=\{3,\dots,n-2\}
and
V_{2}=\{n\}
. Hence
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=2n-6
. Furthermore,
\textup{reg}(J_{G})\geq\textup{reg}(J_{G_{[n-2]}})=n-2
, but also
\textup{reg}(J_{G})\leq n-2
by Proposition
2.7
.

Thus our claim holds for
m=1,2
. Now, we proceed by induction on
n\geq 5
. If
n=5
, then
m\in\{1,2\}
and there is nothing to prove. Suppose
n\geq 6
and let
m\in\{3,\dots,n-3\}
. Set
e=\{1,n\}
and let
f_{e}=x_{1}y_{n}-x_{n}y_{1}
. Then, by the short exact sequence (
19
), see also
[
19
, Proposition 2.1(a)]
, we have

\textup{reg}(J_{G})\leq\max\{\textup{reg}(J_{G\setminus e}),\textup{reg}(J_{G\setminus e}:f_{e})+1\}.

(21)

Note that
G\setminus e
is decomposable as
G\setminus e=G_{\{1,2\}}\cup_{2}G_{\{2,\dots,n\}}
. Using the induction on
G_{\{2,\dots,n\}}
we have

\displaystyle\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G\setminus e})

\displaystyle=\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G_{\{1,2\}}})+\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G_{\{2,\dots,n\}}})+1

\displaystyle=2(n-1)-(m-1)-4+1

(22)

\displaystyle=2n-m-4,

\displaystyle\textup{reg}(J_{G\setminus e})

\displaystyle=\textup{reg}(J_{G_{\{1,2\}}})+\textup{reg}(J_{G_{\{2,\dots,n\}}})-1=2+n-3-1=n-2.

(23)

By equation (
20
),
J_{G\setminus e}:f_{e}=J_{(G\setminus e)_{e}}+I_{G}
. Note that any path in
G
from
1
to
n
different from the path
1,n
must contain the vertex 2. Hence
I_{G}=(x_{2},y_{2})
. Consequently,
J_{G\setminus e}:f_{e}=(J_{\widetilde{G}},x_{2},y_{2})
, where
\widetilde{G}
is the graph
(G\setminus e)_{e}\setminus\{2\}
with
V(\widetilde{G})=\{3,\dots,n\}
and

\displaystyle E(\widetilde{G})\ =

\displaystyle\ \big\{\{i,j\}:3\leq i<j\leq n-2\big\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \big\{\{i,n\}:i=3,\dots,n-2\big\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \big\{\{j,n-1\}:j=m,\dots,n-2\big\}.

Therefore, using Lemma
2.13
applied on
\widetilde{G}
we get

\displaystyle\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G\setminus e}:f_{e})

\displaystyle=\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{\widetilde{G}},x_{2},y_{2})=\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{\widetilde{G}})+2

\displaystyle\leq 2(n-2)-m-3+2

(24)

\displaystyle=2n-m-5,

\displaystyle\textup{reg}(J_{G\setminus e}:f_{e})+1

\displaystyle=\textup{reg}(J_{\widetilde{G}})+1\leq 4\leq n-2,

(25)

as
n\geq 6
. By (
22
) and (
24
) we have
\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G\setminus e}:f_{e})<\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G\setminus e})
. Thus, using the short exact sequence (
19
) we obtain

\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G})=\textup{proj\,dim}(J_{G\setminus e})=2n-m-4.

Whereas, combining (
21
) with (
23
) and (
25
) we obtain
\textup{reg}(J_{G})\leq n-2
. Since also
\textup{reg}(J_{G})\geq\textup{reg}(J_{G_{[n-2]}})=n-2
, as
G_{[n-2]}
is a path on
n-2
vertices, we obtain the equality. The inductive proof is complete.

Ab-initio study of the effect of bromide mixing into RbPbI 3 \text{RbPbI}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} on the structural, electronic and optical properties
Anupriya Nyayban, Subhasis Panda, Avijit Chowdhury

Abstract
The ultra-high efficiency and cost-effective photovoltaics based on halide preovskites have brought a revolution to ongoing photovoltaic research, surpassing the expectations of the scientific community. However, structural stability is a severe issue that hinders their wide-scale integration at the device level. Compositional engineering with the halide mixing has become an efficient way to deal with this issue without compromising device efficiency. Herein, the structural, electronic and optical properties of the bromide mixed orthorhombic RbPb ( I 1 – x ​ Br x ​ ) 3 \text{RbPb}\text{(}\text{I}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417ptx\/}}}\text{Br}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} (where, x = 0.25 {}\mathrm{x}=0.25 , 0.50 0.50 and 0.75 0.75 ) are calculated using the density functional theory. The electronic bandstructure and density of states (DOS) are calculated using both PBE (Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof) and TB-mBJ (Tran Blaha modified Becke Johnson) potential. The lowest energy bandgaps of 2.288 2.288 and 2.986 2.986 eV for bromide mixing of x = 0.50 {}\mathrm{x}=0.50 are obtained using PBE and TB-mBJ, respectively. In contrast, the mixed bromide phases possess a smaller effective mass, facilitating a better carrier transport through the mixed halide. Using PBE, the excitons appear to be the Mott-Wannier type. However, the TB-mBJ predicts the exciton to be Frenkel type for bromide mixing of x = 0.75 {}\mathrm{x}=0.75 and a Mott-Wannier type for all other mixing under study. The spectroscopic limited maximum efficiency (SLME) is observed to be at the highest values of 14.0 14.0 % and 4.1 4.1 % for the equal admixture of I and Br using PBE and TB-mBJ, respectively. The calculated properties are consistent with the reported data of the similar structures.

IV
Conclusion

In summary, the first principle calculations using PBE and TB-mBJ potential have been carried out to investigate the effect of iodide-bromide mixing in
\text{RbPbI}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
on the electronic and optical properties. The calculated badstructures show all
\text{RbPb}\text{(}\text{I}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417ptx\/}}}\text{Br}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
structures have indirect bandgaps. The lowest bandgap of
2.288
and
2.986
eV are estimated for
{}\mathrm{x}=0.50
using PBE and TB-mBJ, respectively. PDOS analysis exhibits the ascending contribution of
Br
-4p in the conduction band region with the increasing
Br
concentration. The calculated TDOS depicts a large number of available states for both
\text{RbPb}\text{(}\text{I}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{Br}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.25}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{RbPb}\text{(}\text{I}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.25}}}\text{Br}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, therefore, this enhances the probability of high charge carrier concentrations. The absorption estimated using TB-mBJ also shifts to the higher energy region than that using PBE. The absorption also decreases with increasing
Br
concentration. The effective masses using both PBE and TB-mBJ for all the structures are found to be low and, thus, is beneficial for the ease of the carrier transport. In case of TB-mBJ potential, the binding energy of exciton is high for
{}\mathrm{x}=0.75
whereas it is low for all other cases. The highest SLME of
14.0
% using PBE and
4.1
% using TB-mBJ are observed for
\text{RbPB}\text{(}\text{I}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.50}}}\text{Br}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.50}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
.
\text{RbPb}\text{(}\text{I}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.50}}}\text{Br}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.50}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
shows the lowest bandgap and also highest efficiency than all other structures under our investigation, hence, turns out to be the suitable one for the photovoltaic operation.

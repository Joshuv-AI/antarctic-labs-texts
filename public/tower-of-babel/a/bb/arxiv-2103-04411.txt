Abstract
. In this paper we deal with a particular class of rank two vector bundles ( instanton bundles) on the Fano threefold of index one F := 𝔽 1 × ℙ 1 F:=\mathbb{F}_{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1} . We show that every instanton bundle on F F can be described as the cohomology of a monad whose terms are free sheaves. Furthermore we prove the existence of instanton bundles for any admissible second Chern class and we construct a nice component of the moduli space where they sit. Finally we show that minimal instanton bundles (i.e. with the least possible degree of the second Chern class) are aCM and we describe their moduli space.

1.
Introduction

The study of vector bundles on a variety
X
is a fruitful field of research in algebraic geometry. In particular the study of vector bundles
{\mathcal{E}}
with some prescribed cohomology groups gives interesting information on the variety itself.

For example, when
X
is endowed with a (very) ample polarization
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
, then it is natural to deal with bundles defined by some particular vanishings. E.g., on the one hand it is interesting to understand when the bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
is
aCM
, i.e.
h^{i}\big(X,{\mathcal{E}}(th)\big)=0
for each
t\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0<i<\dim(X)
. On the other hand, one can also try to classify bundles with very few vanishings.

Examples of vector bundles of the latter type are provided by instanton bundles on a
Fano threefold

X
, i.e. a smooth threefold over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
whose anticanonical line bundle
\omega_{X}^{-1}
is ample: see
[
17
]
for the classification of such threefolds. In particular, the
index of
X
is the greatest
i_{X}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\omega_{X}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-i_{X}h)
for an ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
. It is well–known that
1\leq i_{X}\leq 4
and that such an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
is uniquely determined: it is called the
fundamental line bundle
of
X
.

The easiest Fano threefold is
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
: an instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
is a rank
2
bundle such that
h^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}^{3}},{\mathcal{E}}\big)=h^{1}\big({\mathbb{P}^{3}},{\mathcal{E}}(-2)\big)=0
and
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=0
. Instanton bundles on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
were first introduced in the seminal paper
[
5
]
and widely studied from different viewpoints since the discovery of their connection, through the Atiyah–Penrose–Ward transformation, with the solutions of the Yang–Mills equations (see
[
6
]
).

Because of its importance, the notion of instanton bundle has been extended in several papers to different classes of varieties. Without any claim of completeness we refer the interested reader to
[
12
,
20
]
for instanton bundles on a Fano threefold
X
. If
\varrho_{X}=1
, then the condition
h^{0}\big(X,{\mathcal{E}}\big)=0
is equivalent to the
\mu
–stability of
{\mathcal{E}}
with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
(see
[
16
]
for the definition and properties of
\mu
–(semi)stable bundles). When
\varrho_{X}\geq 2
this is no longer true. For this reason in
[
9
]
instanton bundles are defined as rank
2
bundles
{\mathcal{E}}
such that
h^{0}\big(X,{\mathcal{E}}\big)=h^{1}\big(X,{\mathcal{E}}(-q_{X}h)\big)=0
,
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=(2q_{X}-i_{X})h
, where
q_{X}:=\left[\frac{i_{X}}{2}\right]
, and which are
\mu
–semistable with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
. The study of such instanton bundles has been carried out on several Fano threefolds in
[
21
,
3
,
9
,
10
]
: see also
[
2
]
for further generalizations. We collect in Section
2
the results on instanton bundles that we need in the paper.

In this paper we deal with instanton bundles on
F:={\mathbb{P}^{1}}\times\mathbb{F}_{1}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{1}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}^{8}}
is the del Pezzo surface of degree
8
obtained by blowing up a single point in
{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
. In order to better explain the results proved in the paper we fix some notation. The threefold
F
is endowed with two natural projections
p\colon F\to\mathbb{F}_{1}
and
\pi\colon F\to{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
. If
\sigma\colon\mathbb{F}_{1}\to{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
is the blow up morphism, then
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{F}_{1})
is freely generated by the classes
\ell
of a the pull–back via
\sigma
of a general line in
{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
and
e
of the exceptional divisor. The intersection theory on
\mathbb{F}_{1}
is given by
\ell^{2}=-e^{2}=1
and
\ell e=0
.

The group
A(F)
decomposes as
\pi^{*}A({\mathbb{P}^{1}})\otimes p^{*}A(\mathbb{F}_{1})
, hence it is free. Let
\xi
be the pull–back of a point via
\pi
. By abuse of notation, we will denote by
\ell
and
e
also the pull–backs of the generators of
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{F}_{1})
with the same name via
p
. The dual
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(h)
of

\omega_{F}\cong p^{*}\omega_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}\otimes\pi^{*}\omega_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-3\ell+e-2\xi).

is very ample and not divisible in
\operatorname{Pic}(F)
, hence
F
is a Fano threefold and
i_{F}=1
. Thanks to the decomposition of
A(F)
, we deduce that
A^{2}(F)
has generators
\ell^{2}
,
\ell\xi
,
e\xi
where
\ell^{2}=-e^{2}
is the class of a fibre of
p
and
\ell e=\xi^{2}=0
. Finally, the same decomposition also yields that
A^{3}(F)
is generated by
\ell^{2}\xi
. See Section
3
for some other results on
F
used in the paper.

Our first result proved in Section
4
is the description of each instanton bundle on
F
as the cohomology of a monad. More precisely, for every choice of integers
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle\alpha\geq 3,\qquad\gamma\geq 2,\qquad\alpha+\gamma\geq 6,\qquad\alpha-\beta\geq 2

(1.1)

\displaystyle\epsilon\geq 2-\beta-\gamma,\qquad\delta\geq 1-\beta.

(1.2)

we define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}:={\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-2\ell+e-\xi)^{\oplus\alpha+\gamma-6}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-\ell-\xi)^{\oplus\epsilon},

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathcal{C}}^{0}:={\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-\ell-\xi)^{\oplus\beta+\gamma+\epsilon-2}&\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-\ell+e-\xi)^{\oplus\alpha-\beta+\gamma-4}\oplus\\
&\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-2\ell+e)^{\oplus\alpha-3}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-\ell)^{\oplus\delta},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{1}:={\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-\xi)^{\oplus\gamma-2}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-\ell)^{\oplus\beta+\delta-1}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-\ell+e)^{\oplus\alpha-\beta-2}.

In Section
4
we prove that if
{\mathcal{E}}
is an instanton bundle with
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2}
, then inequalities (
1.1
) necessarily hold (see Remark
4.4
).

The main result of the section is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Each instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
with
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2}
is the cohomology of a monad
{\mathcal{C}}^{\bullet}
of the form

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{0}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{1}\longrightarrow 0

(1.3)

where
\delta:=h^{1}\big({F},{\mathcal{E}}(-e)\big)
and
\epsilon:=h^{1}\big({F},{\mathcal{E}}(-e-\xi)\big)
.

Conversely, if the cohomology
{\mathcal{E}}
of the monad
{\mathcal{C}}^{\bullet}
is a
\mu
–semistable bundle, then
{\mathcal{E}}
is an instanton bundle with
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2}
on
F
such that

(1)

h^{1}\big({F},{\mathcal{E}}(-e)\big)\leq\delta
;

(2)

h^{1}\big({F},{\mathcal{E}}(-e-\xi)\big)\leq\epsilon
;

(3)

h^{1}\big(F,{\mathcal{E}}(-D)\big)=0
for each effective integral divisor
D\not\in|e|
.

An almost immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that an instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on
F
(if any) satisfies
h^{1}\big(F,{\mathcal{E}}(-D)\big)=0
whenever
|D|\neq\emptyset
contains smooth integral elements if and only if
h^{1}\big({F},{\mathcal{E}}(-e)\big)=0
(see Corollary
4.5
): following
[
9
]
we call such bundles earnest.

It is easy to construct examples of decomposable instanton bundles (see Example
5.1
). Thus, it is natural to ask if indecomposable instanton bundles actually exist on
F
.

We positively answer this question in Section
5
showing that for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying inequalities (
1.1
), there exists a
\mu
–stable instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on
F
with
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2}
via the classical Serre correspondence (see Construction
5.2
): in particular, monads
{\mathcal{C}}^{\bullet}
as in the previous statement actually exist.

More precisely we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

For each choice of integers
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
satisfying inequalities (
1.1
) the bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
obtained via Construction
5.2
is a
\mu
–stable instanton bundle with
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2}
, such that
{\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{L}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-1)
for the general line
L\subseteq F
. Moreover, the following assertions hold.

•

If
\beta\geq 1
, then
{\mathcal{E}}
is earnest.

•

If
\beta\leq 0
, then
{\mathcal{E}}
is non–earnest and

-\beta\leq h^{1}\big(F,{\mathcal{E}}(-e-\xi)\big)\leq h^{1}\big(F,{\mathcal{E}}(-e)\big)=1-\beta.

Thanks to
[
2
, Theorem 1.8]
each indecomposable instanton bundle on a Fano threefold is simple. Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{F}(2;-h,\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2})
be the moduli space of simple rank
2
bundles
{\mathcal{E}}
on
F
with
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=-h
and
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2}
(see
[
1
]
) and denote by

{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{I}}_{F}(\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2})\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{F}(2;-h,\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2})

the locus of points corresponding to instanton bundles. The semicontinuity of
h^{1}\big(F,{\mathcal{E}}\big)
implies that such a locus is open, hence it can be considered as a moduli space for instanton bundles on
F
with second Chern class as above.

The instanton bundles obtained via Construction
5.2
, being
\mu
–stable, are simple. In Proposition
5.4
we show that they correspond to smooth points of the same component inside
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{I}}_{F}(\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2})
.

In Remark
5.3
we recall that necessarily
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})h\geq 14
for each instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on
F
. In Section
6
we completely characterize instanton bundles for which the equality holds. In order to deal with such a characterization, notice that the projection
{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
from the blown up point induces a morphism
\pi_{0}\colon{\mathbb{F}_{1}}\to{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
, hence there is a surjective morphism
\varphi:=id\times\pi_{0}\colon F\to Q:={\mathbb{P}^{1}}\times{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
. If
q_{i}\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is the projection on the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
factor, we set
{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(\sigma_{i}):=q^{*}_{i}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(1)
.

The main result of Section
6
is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

There exist instanton bundles
{\mathcal{E}}
on
F
such that
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})h=14
.

Every such an instanton bundle is aCM,
\mu
–stable, earnest, satisfies
{\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{L}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-1)
for the general line
L\subseteq F
and
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})
is either
4\ell\xi-2e\xi+2\ell^{2}
or
3\ell\xi-e\xi+3\ell^{2}
.

•

c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=4\ell\xi-2e\xi+2\ell^{2}
if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}(\ell)=\varphi^{*}\mathcal{A}
where
\mathcal{A}
is
\mu
–stable with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})
such that
c_{1}(\mathcal{A})=-2\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{A})=2
.

•

c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=3\ell\xi-e\xi+3\ell^{2}
if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}(\xi)=p^{*}\mathcal{B}
where
\mathcal{B}
is
\mu
–stable with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(2\ell-e)
such that
c_{1}(\mathcal{B})=-3\ell+e
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{B})=3
.

Finally, both
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{I}}_{F}(3\ell\xi-e\xi+3\ell^{2})
and
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{I}}_{F}(4\ell\xi-2e\xi+2\ell^{2})
are smooth, irreducible and rational of dimension
1
.

We finally make some general comments on minimal instanton, weakly Ulrich, Ulrich and aCM bundles on Fano threefolds (see Section
6
for the definitions and more details on the partial results that we obtain).

2.
Preliminary results

Let
X
be any smooth variety with canonical line bundle
\omega_{X}
. The Riemann–Roch formulas for a vector bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
on a smooth variety
X
of dimensions
3
and
2
are respectively

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \chi({\mathcal{F}})&=\mathrm{rk}({\mathcal{F}})\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})+{\frac{1}{6}}(c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})^{3}-3c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})c_{2}({\mathcal{F}})+3c_{3}({\mathcal{F}}))\\
&-{\frac{1}{4}}(\omega_{X}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})^{2}-2\omega_{X}c_{2}({\mathcal{F}}))+{\frac{1}{12}}(\omega_{X}^{2}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})+c_{2}(\Omega_{X})c_{1}({\mathcal{F}}))\end{aligned}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\chi({\mathcal{F}})=\mathrm{rk}({\mathcal{F}})\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})+{\frac{1}{2}}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})^{2}-{\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{X}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})-c_{2}({\mathcal{F}})

(2.2)

(see
[
14
, Theorem A.4.1]
).

If
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
are coherent sheaves on
X
, then the Serre duality holds

\mathrm{Ext}_{X}^{i}\big({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\omega_{X}\big)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{X}^{\dim(X)-i}\big({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\big)^{\vee}

(2.3)

(see
[
15
, Proposition 7.4]
).

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a vector bundle of rank
2
on a smooth variety
X
of dimension
n
and let
s\in H^{0}\big(X,{\mathcal{F}}\big)
. If we consider its zero locus

(s)_{0}\subseteq X
then we observe that it is either empty or its codimension is at most

2
. In particular we can write
(s)_{0}=Z\cup S
as the union of two components
where
Z
has pure codimension
2
(or it is empty) and
S
is either empty or an effective divisor. Thus
{\mathcal{F}}(-S)
has a section vanishing
on
Z
and we can consider its Koszul complex

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(S)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{Z|X}(-S)\otimes\det({\mathcal{F}})\longrightarrow 0.

(2.4)

Sequence
2.4
tensored by
{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
yields
{\mathcal{I}}_{Z|X}/{\mathcal{I}}^{2}_{Z|X}\cong{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}(S)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
, whence

{\mathcal{N}}_{Z|X}\cong{\mathcal{F}}(-S)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}.

(2.5)

Notice that if
S=0
, then
Z
is locally complete intersection inside
X
and it has no embedded components.

The above construction can be reversed thanks to the Serre correspondence as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety of dimension

and let

be a locally complete intersection subscheme of dimension

If

such that

, then there exist vector bundles
{\mathcal{F}}
of rank
2
on
X
such that:

(1)

\det({\mathcal{F}})\cong\mathcal{L}
;

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}
has a section

such that

coincides with the zero locus

of

.

Moreover, the vector bundles
{\mathcal{F}}

Proof.

The vector bundles
{\mathcal{F}}

.
∎

A vector bundle

on
X
is

If

Proof.

See
[
9

.
∎

If

is an ample line bundle, then we define the

of a vector bundle

Let

Then

is

3.
The threefold
F

In this section we list some basic results on the threefold
F:={\mathbb{P}^{1}}\times\mathbb{F}_{1}
where
\sigma\colon\mathbb{F}_{1}\to{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
is the blow up at a fixed point.

We now recall the notation and some facts from the Introduction about the morphisms
p\colon F\to\mathbb{F}_{1}
,
\sigma\colon\mathbb{F}_{1}\to{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
,
\pi_{0}\colon{\mathbb{F}_{1}}\to{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
,
q_{i}\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
.

We will use the same symbols
\ell
and
e
for denoting both the classes of the pull–back of the general line via
\sigma
and of the exceptional divisor of
\sigma
, and their pull–backs via
p
.

There is an isomorphism
{\mathbb{F}_{1}}\cong\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-1))
and
\pi_{0}
coincides with the standard projective bundle morphism (see
[
14
, Example V.2.11.5]
). It follows that the classes of a fibre of
\pi_{0}
and of the unisecant of minimal self–intersection
-1
inside
A^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{1})\cong\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{F}_{1})
are
\ell-e
and
e
respectively. In particular
|e|
contains a single divisor
E\cong{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
.

Finally, the line bundles
{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(\sigma_{i})
are exactly the standard spinor bundles on
Q
. We have
\Omega_{Q}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-2\sigma_{1})\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-2\sigma_{2})
,
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(\xi)\cong\varphi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(\sigma_{1})
,
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(\ell-e)\cong\varphi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(\sigma_{2})
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} h^{2}\big({\mathbb{F}_{1}},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(-(a+1)\ell+be)\big)&=h^{0}\big({\mathbb{F}_{1}},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}((a-2)\ell-(b-1)e)\big)=\\
&=\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(-(a+1)\ell+be))\end{aligned}

hence equalities (
4.4
) hold true. In particular
h^{1}\big(F,{\mathcal{E}}\big)=0
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Each monad on
F
can be also viewed as a family of monads on
\mathbb{F}_{1}
parameterized by
{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
with
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2}
. If
S\cong\mathbb{F}_{1}
is any fibre of
\pi
, then
{\mathcal{E}}_{S}:={\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
is a vector bundle satisfying
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{S})=-3\ell+e
and
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{S})=\gamma
. Since
{\mathcal{E}}_{S}
is a vector bundle, then the restrictions of the sequences (
4.3
) to
S
yields the monad

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-2\ell+e)^{\oplus\alpha+\gamma-6}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-\ell)^{\oplus\epsilon}\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-\ell)^{\oplus\beta+\gamma+\delta+\epsilon-2}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-\ell+e)^{\oplus\alpha-\beta+\gamma-4}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-2\ell+e)^{\oplus\alpha-3}\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{\oplus\gamma-2}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-\ell)^{\oplus\beta+\delta-1}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-\ell+e)^{\oplus\alpha-\beta-2}\longrightarrow 0

whose cohomology is
{\mathcal{E}}_{S}
.

Monads on
\mathbb{F}_{1}
have been widely studied: e.g. see
[
19
,
7
,
8
]
. In particular, if
\sigma_{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{S}
is semistable, then
[
8
, Proposition 1.10]
implies that
{\mathcal{E}}_{S}
is the cohomology of a monad of the form

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-\ell)^{\oplus k}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow 0

where
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{L}
are direct sums of suitable line bundles on
S\cong\mathbb{F}_{1}
and
k
depends only on
\gamma
.

The two monads above are trivially different. Moreover, the cohomologies of the restrictions to
S
of the sequences (
4.3
) yield
h^{0}\big(S,{\mathcal{E}}_{S}(\ell)\big)\geq 3-\gamma
. The Leray spectral sequence
E_{2}^{p,q}:=H^{p}\big({\mathbb{P}^{2}},R^{q}\sigma_{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{S}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}(1)\big)
abuts to
H^{p+q}\big(S,{\mathcal{E}}_{S}(\ell)\big)
, hence

h^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}^{2}},\sigma_{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{S}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}(1)\big)\geq 3-\gamma.

Lemma
2.3
then implies that
\sigma_{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{S}
is not
\mu
–semistable if
\gamma=2
. It follows that
\sigma_{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{S}
is not even semistable in this case.

Let us conclude this section with a remark on earnest instanton bundles. Let us recall this notion (see
[
9
]
for more details): an instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on a Fano threefold is called

if
h^{1}\big(X,{\mathcal{E}}(-q_{X}h-D)\big)=0
whenever
|D|\neq\emptyset
contains smooth integral elements. The following corollary is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
4.5
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an instanton on
F
. Then
{\mathcal{E}}
is earnest if and only if
h^{1}\big({F},{\mathcal{E}}(-e)\big)=0
.

5.
Existence of instanton bundles

In this section we show that instanton bundles on
F
exist for each admissible value of the second Chern class. As pointed in the introduction it is easy to give examples of decomposable instanton bundles on

.

Example 5.1
.

Let

and
\mathcal{B}:={\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-\ell+e-2\xi)

In order to construct indecomposable instanton bundles we need two different constructions, one for the earnest bundles and the other for non–earnest ones.

Construction 5.2

If

We use Theorem

for constructing an instanton bundle from the union

If

, then there is a vector bundle

on

such that

,

fits into

(5.1)

If
\beta\leq 0
, then there is a vector bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
on
F
such that
c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})=\ell+e
,
c_{2}({\mathcal{F}})=Z
and fitting into sequence (
2.4
). Thus

It is easy to check that
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=-h
and
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=\alpha\ell\xi-\beta e\xi+\gamma\ell^{2}
.

Remark 5.3
.

Thanks to inequalities (

) we deduce

if

,

3.5

\displaystyle h^{1}\big(F,{\mathcal{E}}(-D)\big)

\displaystyle\leq h^{1}\big(F,{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-(a+2)\ell+be-(c+1)\xi)\big)+

\displaystyle+h^{1}\big(F,{\mathcal{I}}_{Z|F}(-(a+1)\ell+be-(c+1)\xi)\big).

) and the aforementioned restrictions imply that the first righthand summand vanishes. Moreover, a similar argument applied to the cohomology of sequence

Now we turn our attention to the case

6.
Minimal instanton bundles

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is an instanton bundle on a Fano threefold
X
, then
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})h
is bounded from below. Indeed

c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})h\geq k_{X}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{if $i_{X}=4,3$,}\\
2&\text{if $i_{X}=2$,}\\
\left\lceil\frac{\deg(X)}{4}\right\rceil&\text{if $i_{X}=1$}\end{array}\right.

(see
[
9
, Corollary 4.2]
). We say that
{\mathcal{E}}
is
minimal
if
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})h

On the one hand, if

2.6
) finally yields that
{\mathcal{F}}:={\mathcal{E}}(h)
is weakly Ulrich and satisfies equalities (
6.5
).

On the other hand
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=0
,
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=2
and
h^{1}\big(X,{\mathcal{E}}(-h)\big)=0
. Moreover, it is not difficult to check the
\mu
–semistability of
{\mathcal{E}}
using the classification of Fano threefold with
i_{X}=2
(see
[
17
]
) and Lemma
2.3
. Nevertheless
{\mathcal{E}}
is obviously not an instanton bundle because
h^{0}\big(X,{\mathcal{E}}\big)=1
.

Remark 6.4
.

When
i_{X}=1
(hence
q_{X}=0
) the picture is even vaguer, because the number
k_{X}
is generally not attained by instanton bundles on
X
(see
[
12
]
where it is proved a sharp lower bound for
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})h
when
\varrho_{X}=1
). Anyhow, in all the examples in the literature, every minimal instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
is aCM (e.g. see
[
12
,
10
]
and Theorem
1.3
above). If
{\mathcal{F}}:={\mathcal{E}}((2-q_{X})h)\cong{\mathcal{E}}(2h)
, then
h^{0}\big(X,{\mathcal{F}}(-2h)\big)=h^{3}\big(X,{\mathcal{F}}(-2h)\big)=0
. It follows that
{\mathcal{F}}
is still weakly Ulrich in all these cases.

Thus it is natural to ask the following questions.

Question 6.5
.

Is each minimal instanton bundle on
X
weakly Ulrich up to twists when
i_{X}=1
?

Question 6.6
.

Is it possible to classify weakly Ulrich bundles
{\mathcal{F}}
of rank
2
on
X
with
c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})=(4-i_{X})h
, at least for low values of
c_{2}({\mathcal{F}})h
?

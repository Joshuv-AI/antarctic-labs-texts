Recent charm results at Belle
*

Abstract

Belle experiment

The Belle experiment ran at the
KEKB energy-asymmetric collider
[
1
]
.
The only detector operating at KEKB, Belle detector, is a large-solid-angle magnetic spectrometer
consisting of a silicon vertex detector, a
50
-layer central drift chamber, an array of aerogel threshold Cherenkov counters, a barrel-like arrangement of time-of-flight scintillation counters, and an electromagnetic calorimeter comprising CsI(Tl) crystals located inside a superconducting solenoid coil providing a
1.5
T magnetic field.
A detailed description of the
detector is given in Refs.
[
2
]
.
These subdetectors contribute to Belle to be a detector with advanced performances on the momentum and vertex resolution; the particle identification; and so on.
Belle accumulated her final data set of integrated luminosity 1
\mbox{\,ab}^{-1}

[
2
]
more than ten years ago. This data set provides us a large charm sample to study charm physics. In this proceedings, we present some selected recent charm results at Belle.

2
C\!P
asymmetries and Branching fractions for the decays of charm mesons

The time-integrated
C\!P
asymmetry is defined as

A_{C\!P}=(\mathcal{B}(D\to f)-\mathcal{B}(\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}\to\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptf}{}))/(\mathcal{B}(D\to f)+\mathcal{B}(\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}\to\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptf}{}))
where
\mathcal{B}
is the branching fraction for
D\to f
decays. Taking
D^{0}
from
D^{*+}\to D^{0}\pi^{+}_{s}
for example, it is produced from
e^{+}e^{-}\to c\bar{c}\to D^{*+}+X
inclusive process, where the charge of
\pi^{+}_{s}
is used to tag the
D^{0}
flavor.
The raw asymmetry is determined by the asymmetry of signal yields.
It includes several sources for asymmetry: the
C\!P
asymmetry in
D^{0}
decay, the final state detection asymmetry, and
D^{*}
production forward-backward asymmetry
A_{\rm FB}
, which is arising from the
\gamma
-
Z^{0}
interference and higher-order QED effects.
Experimentally, we usually use two methods to remove these additional asymmetries: (1) use a reference mode of which
A_{C\!P}
has been well-measured.
(2) perform a correction for the charged track detection asymmetry, e.g. weighting
D^{0}
and
\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}^{0}
samples separately with the factors to correct the
\pi^{+}_{s}
asymmetry:
w=1\mp A_{\varepsilon}^{\pi_{s}^{+}}[\cos\theta,p_{T}]

where
A_{\varepsilon}^{\pi_{s}^{+}}
is the
\pi_{s}^{+}
efficiency map dependent on its polar angle and transverse momentum, and determined with the tagged and untagged
D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}
samples.
Then, the weighted samples have the corrected raw asymmetry, which only has two terms:
A_{\rm corr}=A_{C\!P}+A_{\rm FB}
.
Since
A_{C\!P}
is independent on
\cos\theta^{*}
and
A_{\rm FB}
is odd function of
\cos\theta^{*}
, we can obtain
A_{C\!P}
and
A_{\rm FB}
in symmetric bins of
\cos\theta^{*}
:

\displaystyle A_{C\!P}=\dfrac{A_{\rm corr}(\cos\theta^{*})+A_{\rm corr}(-\cos\theta^{*})}{2}\,,\quad\quad A_{\rm FB}=\dfrac{A_{\rm corr}(\cos\theta^{*})-A_{\rm corr}(-\cos\theta^{*})}{2}\,.

(1)

Finally, fitting these
A_{C\!P}
values to a constant gives the final measurement of
A_{C\!P}
of our signal decay that we are interested in.

2.1
A_{C\!P}
and
\mathcal{B}
for
D^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
,
D^{0}\to K^{+}K^{-}\eta
, and

Recently we present a measurement of
C\!P
asymmetries and branching fractions for three singly Cabibbo-suppressed (SCS) decays
D^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
,
K^{+}K^{-}\eta
, and
\phi\eta

[
3
]
.
All their current world averaged branching fractions have large uncertainties, especially
D^{0}\to K^{+}K^{-}\eta
, of which significance is still below
5\sigma
.
The Cabibbo-favored (CF) decay
D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}\eta
is chosen as the reference mode, which has been well-measured (with a fractional uncertainty

\delta\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{B}\sim 3\%

[
4
]
) by both Belle
[
5
]
and BESIII
[
6
]
.

Based on full Belle data set of 980
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
, we extract signal yields with fitting on
Q=M(h^{+}h^{-}\eta\,\pi_{s})-M(h^{+}h^{-}\eta)-m_{\pi_{s}}
, which is the
kinetic energy released in the
D^{*+}
decay and divided by
c^{2}
.
We find 13 thousands
D^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
, and 1.5 thousands
D^{0}\to K^{+}K^{-}\eta
, and 660 yields for
\phi(1020)
-excluded
D^{0}\to K^{+}K^{-}\eta
, along with 180 thousands
D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}\eta
.

Then we determine the efficiency-corrected yield across on Dalitz-plot to consider the bin-to-bin variation of efficiencies. This efficiency correction is based on the efficiency plane from PHSP signal MC, Dalitz-plot in
Q
signal region, and in
Q
sideband for background shape.
Thus, we have the relative branching ratio by the efficiency-corrected yield ratio.

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta)/\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[6.49\pm 0.09\,({\rm stat})\pm 0.12\,({\rm syst})]\times 10^{-2}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to K^{+}K^{-}\eta)/\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[9.57^{+0.36}_{-0.33}\,({\rm stat})\pm 0.20\,({\rm syst})]\times 10^{-3}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to K^{+}K^{-}\eta)_{{\rm ex.-}\phi}/\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[5.26\,^{+0.45}_{-0.38}\,(\rm{stat})\pm 0.11\,({\rm syst})]\times 10^{-3}\,.

(4)

Using
\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}\eta)=(1.88\pm 0.05)\%

[
4
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[1.22\pm 0.02\,({\rm stat})\pm 0.02\,({\rm syst})\pm 0.02\,(\mathcal{B}_{\rm ref})]\times 10^{-3}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to K^{+}K^{-}\eta)

\displaystyle[1.80\,^{+0.07}_{-0.06}\,({\rm stat})\pm 0.04\,({\rm syst})\pm 0.03\,(\mathcal{B}_{\rm ref})]\times 10^{-4}\,,

\displaystyle[0.99\,^{+0.08}_{-0.07}\,({\rm stat})\pm 0.02\,({\rm syst})\pm 0.02\,(\mathcal{B}_{\rm ref})]\times 10^{-4}\,.

The last one is somewhat higher, but more precise, than a similar measurement by BESIII
[
6

.

In the Dalitz plot of
D^{0}\to K^{+}K^{-}\eta
, we observe a very clear structure for
\phi(1020)

To extract the signal yield, we perform
M_{KK}
-
Q

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to\phi\eta,\,\phi\to K^{+}K^{-})/\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}\eta)=[4.82\pm 0.23\,({\rm stat})\pm 0.16\,({\rm syst})]\times 10^{-3}\,.

\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}\eta)=(1.88\pm 0.05)\%

]
and
\mathcal{B}(\phi\to K^{+}K^{-})=(49.2\pm 0.5)\%

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to\phi\eta)=[1.84\pm 0.09\,({\rm stat})\pm 0.06\,({\rm syst})\pm 0.05\,(\mathcal{B}_{\rm ref})]\times 10^{-4},

where the systematic uncertainty includes the small uncertainty on
\mathcal{B}(\phi\to K^{+}K^{-})

This result is consistent with the current world average of
(1.8\pm 0.5)\times 10^{-4}

[

]
, but notably more precise than it.

D^{0}
and
\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}^{0}

\cos\theta^{*}

We perform
Q
or

-
Q
fit for weighted

and
\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}^{0}

Branching fractions for the decays of charmed baryons

3.1
\mathcal{B}
of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
and
p\eta

We perform a measurement of branching fractions for SCS decays
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
and
p\eta

[
9
]
.
These decays proceed predominantly through internal W emission and W exchange. The theoretical calculations predict
\mathcal{B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta)
to be at least one order of magnitude greater than
\mathcal{B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0})
.
BESIII reported the first evidence of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta
; and set an upper limit for branching fraction for
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
at 90% C.L.
We use the 980
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
data set at Belle to measure the branching fractions relative to reference CF mode
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+}
.
We obtain about 8 thousand
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta
signals, and no significant
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
signal,
A large sample for reference mode and its averaged efficiency from MC which is adjusted on Dalitz plot.
Finally, we obtain the relative branching fractions.

\frac{\mathcal{B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta)}{\mathcal{B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+})}=[2.258\pm 0.077({\rm stat})\pm 0.122({\rm syst})]\times 10^{-2}
, and
\frac{\mathcal{B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0})}{\mathcal{B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+})}<1.273\times 10^{-3}
at 90% C.L.
Using world average
\mathcal{B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+})

[
4
]
, we have
\mathcal{B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\eta)=[1.42\pm 0.05({\rm stat})\pm 0.11({\rm syst})]\times 10^{-3}
, consistent with and more precise than other results.
An upper limit on BF of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0}
is set:
\mathcal{B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\pi^{0})<8.0\times 10^{-5}
.
The ratio of these two branching fractions is consistent with the theoretical prediction.

4.2
determination of
J^{P}(\Xi_{c}(2970)^{+})

The first determination of spin-parity of a charmed-strange baryon,
\Xi_{c}(2970)^{+}
, is presented in Ref.
[
13
]
.
The unclear theoretical situation motivates this experimental determination, which provides important information to test predictions and help decipher its nature.

The spin of
\Xi_{c}(2970)^{+}
is determined by angular analysis of this decay chain
\Xi_{c}(2970)^{+}\to\Xi_{c}(2645)^{0}\pi^{+}\to\Xi_{c}~+\pi^{-}\pi^{+}
:
(1) helicity angle of
\Xi_{c}(2970)
: we fit the background-subtracted and efficiency-corrected yields with expected decay-angle distribution for different spin hypotheses. The best fit is for
J=1/2
but this is inconclusive, since others hypotheses are excluded with small significance.
(2) helicity angle of
\Xi_{c}(2645)
: the expected angular correlation is used to fit. The half-spin hypothesis over the others at the level of
5.1\sigma
and
4.0\sigma
, respectively.

The parity of
\Xi_{c}(2970)^{+}
is established from the relative branching fraction:
\mathcal{B}(\Xi_{c}(2970)^{+}\to\Xi_{c}(2645)^{0}\pi^{+})/\mathcal{B}(\Xi_{c}(2970)^{+}\to\Xi_{c}^{\prime 0}\pi^{+})=1.67\pm 0.29(\text{stat})^{+0.15}_{-0.09}(\text{syst})\pm 0.25(\text{IS})
, where the last uncertainty is due to possible isospin-symmetry-breaking effects. This result favors
J^{P}=\frac{1}{2}^{+}
with the zero spin of the light-quark degrees of freedom.

Definitions

Maximal Operators

Let
\left\{a(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}
be a sequence. We define the following three types of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators as follows:

Definition 3.1
.

If
I_{r}
is the interval
\left\{-r,-r+1,\dots,0,1,2,\dots,r-1,r\right\}
, define centered Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

M^{\prime}a(m)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{(2r+1)}\sum_{n\in I_{r}}\lvert a(m-n)\rvert

For any positive integer
J
, define truncated centered Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator as

(M^{\prime}_{J}a)(m)=\sup_{J>r>0}\frac{1}{(2r+1)}\sum_{n\in I_{r}}\lvert a(m-n)\rvert

Definition 3.2
.

We define Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator as follows

Ma(m)=\sup_{m\in I}\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{n\in I}\lvert a(n)\rvert

where the supremum is taken over all intervals containing
m
.
For any positive integer
J
, define truncated Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator as

M_{J}a(m)=\sup_{J\geq m\in I}\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{n\in I}\lvert a(n)\rvert

Definition 3.3
.

We define dyadic Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator as follows:

M_{d}a(m)=\sup_{m\in I}\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in I}\lvert a(k)\rvert

where supremum is taken over all dyadic intervals containing
m
.

Definition 3.4
.

Given a sequence
\left\{a(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}
and an interval
I
, let
a_{I}
denote average of
{\left\{a(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}}
on
I
.
Let,
a_{I}=\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{m\in I}a(m)
. Define the sharp maximal operator
M^{\#}
as follows

M^{\#}a(m)=\sup_{m\in I}\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{n\in I}\lvert a(n)-a_{I}\rvert

where the supremum is taken over all intervals
I
containing
m
. We say that sequence
\left\{a(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}
has bounded mean oscillation if the
sequence
M^{\#}a
is bounded. The space of sequences with this property is denoted by BMO(
{\mathbb{Z}}
).
We define a norm in BMO(
{\mathbb{Z}}
) by
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\star}=\left\lVert M^{\#}a\right\rVert_{\infty}
. The space BMO(
{\mathbb{Z}}
) is studied in
[
8
]
.

Weights

Definition 3.5
.

For a fixed
p
,
1<p<\infty
, we say that a non-negative sequence
\left\{w(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}

\biggl(\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in I}w(k)\biggr)\biggl(\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in I}w(k)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\biggr)^{p-1}\leq C

A_{p}

We say that
\left\{w(m):m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}

A_{1}

I
in
{\mathbb{Z}}

\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in I}w(k)\leq Cw(m)

for all
m\in I
.
This constant
C

A_{1}

\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell_{w}^{p}({\mathbb{Z}})}=\biggl(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\lvert a(k)\rvert}^{p}w(k)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle w(\left\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}:Ma(m)>\lambda\right\})\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{p}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lvert a(m)\rvert^{p}w(m)

A

Theorem 5.1
.

If
\left\{w(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}\in A_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
\left\{a(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}
is a sequence in
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}})
, then for
p>1
, there exists a constant
C_{p}
such that

\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}Ma(m)^{p}w(m)\leq C_{p}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lvert a(m)\rvert^{p}Mw(m)

Furthermore, for
p=1
, there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that

\sum_{\left\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}:Ma(m)>\lambda\right\}}w(m)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\lambda}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lvert a(m)\rvert Mw(m)

Proof.

We will show that
\left\lVert Ma\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}_{w}({\mathbb{Z}})}\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}_{Mw}({\mathbb{Z}})}
and that weak(1,1) inequality holds; the strong(p,p) inequality then follows from the
Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem.

Take
C>\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}_{Mw}({\mathbb{Z}})}
. Then

\sum_{\left\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\lvert a(n)\rvert>C\right\}}Mw(n)=0

which shows that

\left\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\lvert a(n)\rvert>C\right\}=\emptyset

Hence
\lvert a(n)\rvert\leq C,\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
which implies that
\lvert Ma(n)\rvert\leq C,\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
Therefore

\left\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\lvert Ma(n)\rvert>C\right\}=\emptyset

So,

\sum_{\left\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\lvert Ma(n)\rvert>C\right\}}w(n)=0

which gives
w(\left\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\lvert Ma(n)\rvert>C\right\})=0
.

Therefore
\left\lVert Ma\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}_{w}({\mathbb{Z}})}\leq C
.
Taking
\inf\left\{C:\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}_{Mw}({\mathbb{Z}})}<C\right\}

we get

\left\lVert Ma\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}_{w}({\mathbb{Z}})}\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}_{Mw}({\mathbb{Z}})}

To prove the weak(1,1) inequality we may assume that
\left\{a(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}\in\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Form Calderon-Zygmund decomposition of sequence
\left\{a(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}
at height
\frac{\lambda}{4}>0
. Then we get a sequence
\left\{I_{j}\right\}
of dyadic intervals in
{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\frac{1}{\lvert I_{j}\rvert}\sum_{k\in I_{j}}\lvert a(k)\rvert>\frac{\lambda}{4}

Further as we showed in the proof of Lemma [
\ref{Sak_lem_2.12}](See

[
1

),

\left\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}:M^{\prime}a(m)>\lambda\right\}\subset\cup_{j}3I_{j}

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\left\{m:M^{\prime}a(m)>\lambda\right\}}w(m)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\sum_{m\in 3I_{j}}w(m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}3\lvert I_{j}\rvert\frac{1}{\lvert 3I_{j}\rvert}\sum_{m\in 3I_{j}}w(m)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{12}{\lambda}\sum_{j}\biggl(\biggl(\sum_{n\in I_{j}}\lvert a(n)\rvert\biggr)\biggl(\frac{1}{\lvert 3I_{j}\rvert}\sum_{m\in 3I_{j}}w(m)\biggr)\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{12C}{\lambda}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lvert a(n)\rvert Mw(n)

Since by Lemma[
4.1
],
\left\{m:Ma(m)>\lambda\right\}\subseteq\left\{m:M^{\prime}a(m)>\frac{\lambda}{3}\right\}

\sum_{\left\{m:Ma(m)>\lambda\right\}}w(m)\leq\sum_{\left\{m:M^{\prime}a(m)>\frac{\lambda}{3}\right\}}w(m)\leq\frac{36C}{\lambda}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lvert a(n)\rvert Mw(n)

∎

6
Maximal Ergodic Operator

Let
(X,\textbf{B},\mu)
be a probability space and
U
an invertible measure preserving transformation on
X
.
We define maximal ergodic operator as

\tilde{M}f(x)=\sup_{J\geq 1}\frac{1}{2J+1}\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\lvert f(U^{-k}x)\rvert

For any positive integer
J
, we also define truncated maximal ergodic operator as

\tilde{M}_{J}f(x)=\sup_{1\leq n\leq J}\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\lvert f(U^{-k}x)\rvert

In the following theorem using transference, we prove that the maximal ergodic operator is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(X,\mathcal{B},\mu),1<p<\infty
where
w
is ergodic
A_{p}
weight and the maximal ergodic operator satisfies weak type (1,1) inequality on
L^{1}_{w}(X,\mathbf{B},\mu)
space.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(X,\textbf{B},\mu)
be a probability space and
U
an invertible measure preserving transformation on
X
.
satisfies

1.

If
w
is an ergodic
A_{p}
weight,
1<p<\infty
and
f\in L^{p}_{w}(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
, then the maximal ergodic operator

\left\lVert\tilde{M}f(x)\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{w}(X)}\leq C_{p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{w}(X)}\quad\text{if}\quad 1<p<\infty

2.

If w is an ergodic
A_{1}
weight and
f\in L^{1}_{w}(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
, then

\int_{\left\{x\in X:\lvert\tilde{M}f(x)\rvert>\lambda\right\}}w(x)d\mu(x)\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert w(x)d\mu(x)

Proof.

Take
p,1\leq p<\infty
. Fix
J>0
and take a function
f\in L^{p}_{w}(X)
.

\tilde{M}_{J}f(x)=\sup_{1\leq n\leq J}\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\lvert f(U^{-k}x)\rvert

It is enough to prove that
\tilde{M_{J}}
satisfies (1) and (2) with constants not depending on
J
. Let
\lambda>0
and put

E_{\lambda}=\left\{x\in X:\lvert\tilde{M_{J}}f(x)\rvert>\lambda\right\}

For x lying outside a
\mu
null set and a positive integer
L
, define sequences

\displaystyle a_{x}(k)=\begin{cases}\,f(U^{-k}x)\quad if\quad\lvert k\rvert\leq L+J\\
\,0\quad{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle w_{x}(k)=\begin{cases}\,w(U^{-k}x)\quad if\quad\lvert k\rvert\leq L+J\\
\,0\quad{otherwise}\end{cases}

Using Lemma[
4.1
], observe that for an integer
m
with
\lvert m\rvert\leq L

\tilde{M}_{J}f(U^{-m}x)=M^{\prime}_{J}a_{x}(m)\leq Ma_{x}(m)

Therefore,

\displaystyle w(\left\{x\in X:\lvert\tilde{M}_{J}f(x)\rvert>\lambda\right\})=\int_{E_{\lambda}}w(x)d\mu(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\int_{E_{\lambda}}\lambda^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\int_{E_{\lambda}}\lvert\tilde{M}_{J}f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\int_{X}\lvert\tilde{M}_{J}f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}\sum_{m=-L}^{L}\int_{X}\lvert\tilde{M}_{J}f(U^{-m}x)\rvert^{p}w(U^{-m}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}\sum_{m=-L}^{L}\int_{X}\lvert{M}a_{x}(m)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(m)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}\int_{X}\sum_{m=-L}^{L}\lvert{M}a_{x}(m)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(m)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}\int_{X}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\lvert{M}a_{x}(m)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(m)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}\int_{X}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\lvert a_{x}(m)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(m)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}\int_{X}\sum_{m=-(L+J)}^{(L+J)}\lvert a_{x}(m)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(m)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}\int_{X}\sum_{m=-(L+J)}^{(L+J)}\lvert f(U^{-m}x)\rvert^{p}w(U^{-m}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}\sum_{m=-(L+J)}^{(L+J)}\int_{X}\lvert f(U^{-m}x)\rvert^{p}w(U^{-m}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}\sum_{m=-(L+J)}^{(L+J)}\int_{X}\lvert f(U^{-m}x)\rvert^{p}w(U^{-m}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}\sum_{m=-(L+J)}^{(L+J)}\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{p}}\frac{1}{2L+1}2(L+J)+1\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}_{w}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{p}}(\frac{2L}{2L+1}+\frac{2J+1}{2L+1})\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}_{w}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{p}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}_{w}(X)}

by choosing
L
appropriately. Conclusion
(1)
of the theorem now follows by using the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem.
∎

Now, we prove the converse of Theorem[
6.1
] for
p>1
with the additional assumptions (1)
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
is a probability space and (2)
U
is ergodic measure preserving transformation.
Using transference method, we prove the converse of Theorem[
6.1
]. A direct proof can be seen in
[
3
]
. For this we require the concept of ergodic rectangles which we define below
[
3
]
.

Definition 6.2

(Ergodic Rectangle)
.

Let
E
be a subset of
X
with positive measure and let
K\geq 1
be such that
U^{i}E\cap U^{j}E=\phi\quad\text{if}\quad i\neq j
and
-K\leq i,j\leq K
. Then the set
R=\cup_{i=-K}^{K}U^{i}E
is called ergodic rectangle of length
2K+1
with base
E
.

For the proof of following lemma[
6.3
], refer
[
3
]
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(X,\textbf{B},\mu)
be a probability space,
U
an ergodic invertible measure preserving transformation on
X
and
K
a positive integer.

1.

If
F\subseteq X
is a set of positive measure then there exists a subset
E\subseteq F
of positive measure such that
E
is base of an ergodic rectangle of length
2K+1
.

2.

There exists a countable family
\left\{E_{j}\right\}
of bases of ergodic rectangles of length
2K+1
such that
X=\cup_{j}E_{j}
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a probability space, U an invertible ergodic measure preserving transformation on
X
. If
\tilde{M}f
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(X)
for some
1<p<\infty
, then
w\in A_{P}(X)
.

Proof.

For the given function
w
on
X
, for a.e
x\in X
define the sequence
w_{x}(k)=w(U^{-k}x)
.
We shall prove that

esssup_{x\in X}\biggl(\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in I}\lvert w_{x}(k)\rvert\biggr)\biggl(\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in I}\lvert w_{x}(k)\rvert^{p^{\prime}-1}\biggr)^{p-1}\leq C

This will prove that
w\in A_{p}(X)
. In order to prove this, we shall prove that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
is bounded on
\ell^{p}_{w_{x}}({\mathbb{Z}})
and

\left\lVert Ma\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}_{w_{x}}({\mathbb{Z}})}\leq C_{p}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}_{w_{x}}({\mathbb{Z}})}

where
C_{p}
is independent of
x
.
In order to prove the above inequality, take a sequence
a=\left\{a(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}\in\ell^{p}_{w_{x}}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

Let
R=\cup_{k=-2J}^{2J}U^{k}E
be an ergodic rectangle of length
4J+1
with base
E
. Let
F
be any measurable subset of
E
. Then
F
is also base of an
ergodic rectangle of length
4J+1
. Let
R^{\prime}=\cup_{k=-2J}^{2J}U^{k}F
.
Define function
f
and
w
as follows.

\displaystyle f(U^{-k}x)=\begin{cases}\,a(k)\quad if\quad x\in F\quad\text{and}-J\leq k\leq J\\
\,0\quad{otherwise}\end{cases}

Then,

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}_{w}(X)}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)=\int_{R^{\prime}}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\int_{U^{k}F}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\int_{F}\lvert f(U^{-k}x)\rvert^{p}w(U^{-k}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\int_{F}\lvert a(k)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(k)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{F}\biggl(\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\lvert a(k)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(k)\biggr)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}_{w_{x}}({\mathbb{Z}})}\mu(F)

Using Lemma[
4.1
], it is easy to observe that for
-J\leq m\leq J
and
x\in F

\tilde{M_{J}}f(U^{-m}x)=M^{\prime}_{J}a(m)\geq\frac{1}{3}M_{J}a(m)

Now,

\displaystyle C\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}_{w}(X)}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{X}\lvert\tilde{M_{J}}f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{R^{\prime}}\lvert\tilde{M_{J}}f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\int_{U^{k}F}\lvert\tilde{M_{J}}f(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\int_{F}\lvert\tilde{M_{J}}f(U^{-k}x)\rvert^{p}w(U^{-k}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\int_{F}\lvert{M^{\prime}_{J}a}(k)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(k)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{F}\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\lvert{M^{\prime}_{J}a}(k)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(k)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{3}\int_{F}\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\lvert{M_{J}a}(k)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(k)d\mu(x)

So from the above estimates

\frac{1}{\mu(F)}\int_{F}\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\lvert{M_{J}a}(k)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(k)d\mu(x)\leq C\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}_{w_{x}}({\mathbb{Z}})}

Since
F
was an arbitrary subset of
E
, we get

\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\lvert{M_{J}a}(k)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(k)\leq C\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}_{w_{x}}({\mathbb{Z}})}

a.e
x\in E
. Since
U
is ergodic,
X
can be written as countable union of bases of ergodic rectangles of length
4J+1
. Therefore for a.e
x\in X
,

\sum_{k=-J}^{J}\lvert{M_{J}a}(k)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(k)\leq C\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}_{w_{x}}({\mathbb{Z}})}

Since
C
is independent of
J
, a.e
x\in X
,

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lvert{Ma}(k)\rvert^{p}w_{x}(k)\leq C\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}_{w_{x}}({\mathbb{Z}})}

It follows that the sequence
\left\{w_{x}(n):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}
as defined by
w_{x}(k)=w(U^{k}x)
belongs to
A_{p}({\mathbb{Z}})
a.e
x\in X
and
A_{p}
weight constant for
w_{x}
is independent of
x
so that
w\in A_{p}(X)
.
∎

Conclusion

The study of maximal ergodic operator on
L^{p}_{w}(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
spaces paves the way to study this operator on variable
L^{{p(\cdot)}}(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
spaces. Using Rubio de Francia extrapolation method
[
6
]
and appropriate variable Holder’s inequality, we hope to achieve this result.

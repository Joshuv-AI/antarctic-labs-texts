Maximizing the validity of the Gaussian Approximation for the biphoton State from parametric down-conversion
Baghdasar Baghdasaryan, Fabian Steinlechner, Stephan Fritzsche

Abstract
Spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) is widely used in quantum applications based on photonic entanglement. The efficiency of photon pair generation is often characterized by means of a sinc ⁡ ( L ​ Δ ​ k / 2 ) \sinc(L\Delta k/2) function, where L L is the length of the nonlinear medium and Δ ​ k \Delta k is the phase mismatch between the pump and down-converted fields. In theoretical investigations, the sinc behavior of the phase mismatch has often been approximated by a Gaussian function exp ⁡ ( − α ​ x 2 ) \exp{(-\alpha x^{2})} in order to derive analytical expressions for the SPDC process. Different values have been chosen in the literature for the optimization factor α \alpha , for instance, by comparing the widths of sinc and Gaussian functions or the momentum of down-converted photons. As a consequence, different values of α \alpha provide different theoretical predictions for the same setup. Therefore an informed and unique choice of this parameter is necessary. In this paper, we present a choice of α \alpha which maximizes the validity of the Gaussian approximation. Moreover, we also discuss the so-called super -Gaussian and cosine -Gaussian approximations as practical alternatives with improved predictive power for experiments.

I
Introduction

In spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC), a nonlinear-responding quadratic crystal is pumped by a laser field, in order to convert (high-energy) photons into entangled photon pairs. Entangled states generated by SPDC have provided an experimental platform for fundamental quantum technologies, such as quantum cryptography
Ursin et al. 2007
, quantum teleportation
Bouwmeester et al. 1997
, or optical quantum information processing
Kok et al. 2007
, and by including recent milestone experiments in photonic quantum computing
Zhong et al. 2020
.

The quantum theory of down-converted pairs is well known and plays a crucial role in the modeling of SPDC experiments. The state of such a SPDC pair is also known as a biphoton state. Within the paraxial approximation, where the longitudinal and transversal components of the wave vector are treated separately,
\bm{k}=\bm{q}+k_{z}(\omega)\bm{z}
, the biphoton state can be written as
Walborn et al. 2010
;
Karan et al. 2020
;
Baghdasaryan et al. 2022

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi}=N\iint

\displaystyle d\bm{q}_{s}\>d\bm{q}_{i}\>d\omega_{s}\>d\omega_{i}\>\overbrace{\mathrm{V}(\bm{q}_{s}+\bm{q}_{i})\,\mathrm{S}_{p}(\omega_{s}+\omega_{i})}^{pump}

\displaystyle\times\underbrace{\sinc\biggl(\frac{L\Delta k_{z}}{2}\biggr)}_{phase\medspace matching}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{s}(\bm{q}_{s},\omega_{s})\>\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i}(\bm{q}_{i},\omega_{i})\ket{vac}.

(1)

In expression (
I
),
N
is the normalization factor,
V(\bm{q}_{p})
is the spatial and
S_{p}(\omega_{p})
the spectral distribution of the pump beam,
L
is the length of the nonlinear crystal,
\ket{vac}
is the vacuum state,
\omega_{s,i}
and
\bm{q}_{s,i}
are the energies and transverse components of wave vectors of down-converted fields (signal and idler), and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{s,i}(\bm{q}_{s,i})
are the corresponding creation operators.

Expression (
I
) can be simplified if we approximate the
sinc
function in terms of the Gaussian function
\sinc(x^{2})\approx\exp(-x^{2})
. Many useful analytical expressions can be then defined for SPDC within this approximation. A good example is the expression for the Schmidt number of the biphoton state presented in Ref.
Law and Eberly 2004
.

Already in Ref.
Law and Eberly 2004
, the calculations within the Gaussian approximation delivered a small deviation from experimentally measured values for the Schmidt number. It has often been suggested in the literature to optimize this approximation by presenting an optimization factor
\alpha
in the exponential expression
\exp{(-\alpha x^{2})}
. The value of
\alpha
has been chosen, for example, by matching the widths of Gaussian and
sinc
functions
Joobeur et al. 1996
;
Valencia et al. 2007
;
Miatto et al. 2012
, by matching second-order momenta
Schneeloch and Howell 2016
, by comparing momentum
correlations in SPDC
Gómez et al. 2012
, or by comparing the coincidence and single-particle spectral widths of the biphoton state
Fedorov et al. 2009
.

Obviously, different values of
\alpha
deliver different theoretical predictions for the same setup. A unique choice of
\alpha
, which should minimize the error of the approximation, is lacking. In order to solve this problem, we look in this paper at the states themselves, instead of comparing just the
sinc
and Gaussian functions or other observable. Eventually, the goal is to make the distance between
sinc
- and Gaussian-like states as small as possible. We use an appropriate distance measure for this purpose such as fidelity
Nielsen and Chuang 2000
and find the particular
\alpha
that maximizes it. Except for the Gaussian approximation, we will also discuss the
cosine
- and
super
-Gaussian approximations and will compare them with the usual Gaussian approximation. (All these functions can be compared in Fig.
3
, where we already included the optimized values of
\alpha
.)

II
Theory and results

We first should determine the pump and phase-matching characteristics from expression (
I
), in order to calculate the fidelity of the
sinc
- and Gaussian-like states. The pump beam is usually fixed by the experimental setup, and its function in expression (
I
) is well known. The phase mismatch in the
z
direction
\Delta k_{z}=k_{p,z}-k_{s,z}-k_{i,z}
requires more careful determination, which depends on many characteristics of the experiment, such as the crystal type, the polarization of interacting beams, and the geometry of the setup. The derivation of
\Delta k_{z}
has already been reported for a very general experimental scenario in Ref.
Baghdasaryan et al. 2022
. In comparison to Ref.
Baghdasaryan et al. 2022
, here, we additionally assume degenerate momentum vectors for signal and idler photons
k_{p}\approx 2k_{s}
, which allows us to apply the Gaussian approximation. With this in mind, the phase mismatch
\Delta k_{z}
can be written as
Baghdasaryan et al. 2022

\Delta k_{z}=\frac{\Omega_{s}+\Omega_{i}}{u_{p}}-\frac{\Omega_{s}}{u_{s}}-\frac{\Omega_{i}}{u_{i}}+\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{s}-\bm{q}_{i}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{2k_{p}},

(2)

where
\Omega_{j}
is the deviation from the central frequency
\omega_{j,0}
,
\omega_{j}=\omega_{j,0}+\Omega_{j}
with the assumption
\Omega_{j}\ll\omega_{j,0}
, and
u_{j}=1/(\partial k_{j}/\partial\Omega)
is the group velocity evaluated at the central frequency.

The right-hand side of Eq. (
2
) can be divided into two parts. The first three terms represent the spectral and the last term the spatial properties of the biphoton state. The spectral and spatial degrees of freedom (DOFs) are in general coupled due to the phase-matching characteristics in SPDC
Osorio et al. 2008
;
Gatti et al. 2009
;
Baghdasaryan et al. 2022
;
Sevilla-Gutiérrez et al. 2022
. However, under certain approximations, such as the narrowband
Miatto et al. 2011
, thin-crystal
Yao 2011
;
Baghdasaryan et al. 2021
, or plane wave approximations
Valencia et al. 2007
, either only the first three terms survive (frequency-resolved biphoton state) or only the last one survives (spatially resolved biphoton state). In this paper, we first develop the Gaussian approximation for spectral and spatial DOFs separately. The description of the coupling in spectral and spatial domains is more challenging in the scope of the Gaussian approximation, which is also discussed in this paper.

II.1
Spatially resolved biphoton state

\displaystyle d\bm{q}_{s}\>d\bm{q}_{i}\mathrm{V}(\bm{q}_{s}+\bm{q}_{i})\>\sinc\biggl(\frac{L\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{s}-\bm{q}_{i}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{4k_{p}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\times\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{s}(\bm{q}_{s})\>\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i}(\bm{q}_{i})\ket{vac}.

(3)

Expression (
II.1
) has now a simple form so that we can apply the Gaussian,

-Gaussian, or
cosine
-Gaussian approximations and calculate the corresponding fidelity. We will present the detailed calculation only for the Gaussian approximation since the derivations for the

-Gaussian and

-Gaussian approximations are similar.

Gaussian approximation

\displaystyle\braket{\Psi|\Psi_{G}}=

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d\bm{r}^{{}^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{V}_{F}(\bm{r}^{{}^{\prime}})\,e^{i\bm{r}^{{}^{\prime}}\bm{q}_{+}},

where the transformed functions are given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{F}(\bm{r})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d\bm{q}_{-}\,\sinc\biggl(\frac{L\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{-}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{4k_{p}}\biggr)\>\exp\biggl(-\alpha\frac{L\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{-}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{4k_{p}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\times e^{-i\bm{r}\bm{q}_{-}}

and

The integrals over momentum space can now be implemented

\displaystyle\braket{\Psi|\Psi_{G}}=

\displaystyle N\cdot N_{G}\iint d\bm{r}\>d\bm{r}^{{}^{\prime}}\>\Phi_{F}(\bm{r})\,\mathrm{V}_{F}(\bm{r}^{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d\bm{q}_{s}\,\,e^{i\bm{q}_{s}(\bm{r}+\bm{r}^{{}^{\prime}})}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d\bm{q}_{i}\,e^{-i\bm{q}_{i}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{{}^{\prime}})},

that give rise to two

II.1.3
Cosine-Gaussian approximation

As suggested in Ref.

-Gaussian approximation

\displaystyle\sinc{\biggl(\frac{L\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{s}-\bm{q}_{i}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{4k_{p}}\biggr)}\approx

\displaystyle\exp\biggl(-\alpha\frac{L\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{s}-\bm{q}_{i}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{4k_{p}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\times\cos\biggl(\beta\frac{L\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{s}-\bm{q}_{i}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{4k_{p}}\biggr).

(12)

This is a better approximation than the usual Gaussian approximation, since the side oscillation of the
sinc

cosine

cosine

\displaystyle\sinc{\biggl(\frac{L\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{s}-\bm{q}_{i}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{4k_{p}}\biggr)}\approx Re\biggl\{\exp\biggl[-(\alpha-i\beta)\frac{L\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{s}-\bm{q}_{i}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{4k_{p}}\biggr]\biggr\},

(13)

\displaystyle\braket{\Psi|\Psi_{CG}}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\biggr(\arctan{\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha}}+\arctan{\frac{1+\beta}{\alpha}}\biggl)

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{3}+\alpha\beta^{2}}{2\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}}.

for the optimization factors
\alpha=0.39
and

. Thus the same fidelity of the

-Gaussian approximation can be achieved also for the

II.2
Frequency-resolved biphoton state

We assume large area collection modes
\bm{q_{s}}=\bm{q_{i}}\approx 0

Valencia et al. 2007
, in order to remain only with the spectral part of the right-hand side of Eq. (
2
). The biphoton state transforms into

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi}=N\iint

\displaystyle d\Omega_{s}\>d\Omega_{i}\,\mathrm{S}_{p}(\Omega_{s}+\Omega_{i})\>\sinc\biggl(\frac{L\Delta k_{z}}{2}\biggr)

\displaystyle\times\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{s}(\Omega_{s})\>\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i}(\Omega_{i})\ket{vac},

(14)

where the phase mismatch now is

\Delta k_{z}=\frac{\Omega_{s}+\Omega_{i}}{u_{p}}-\frac{\Omega_{s}}{u_{s}}-\frac{\Omega_{i}}{u_{i}}.

The temporal distribution of the pump with a Gaussian envelope of pulse duration
t_{0}
can be modeled as
\mathrm{S}_{p}(\omega_{p})=\exp{[-(\omega_{p}-\omega_{p,0})^{2}\,t_{0}^{2}/4]}
. The corresponding Gaussian-like state is given by

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{G}}=N\iint

\displaystyle d\Omega_{s}\>d\Omega_{i}\,\mathrm{S}_{p}(\Omega_{s}+\Omega_{i})\>\exp\biggl[-\alpha\biggl(\frac{L\Delta k_{z}}{2}\biggr)^{2}\biggl]

\displaystyle\times\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{s}(\Omega_{s})\>\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i}(\Omega_{i})\ket{vac}.

(15)

Unfortunately, we could not find any analytical expression for the fidelity
\braket{\Psi|\Psi_{G}}
and performed the calculations numerically. The optimum value of
\alpha
is
0.25
, similar to the
super
-Gaussian approximation of the spatially resolved biphoton state. Although the value
\alpha=0.25
is independent of
t_{0}
, the maximum value for the fidelity
\braket{\Psi|\Psi_{G}}_{max}
does depend on the pulse duration, but not on the crystal length
L
. So, if
t_{0}
is large enough so that the pump spectral envelope
\mathrm{S}_{p}(\omega_{p})
dominates over the
sinc
function, the maximum overlap value is close to unity. This means that the
sinc
term is negligible. If the
sinc
function starts to dominate over
\mathrm{S}_{p}(\omega_{p})
(small
t_{0}
), the fidelity decreases till the lowest value
0.95
, i.e.,
\braket{\Psi|\Psi_{G}}_{max}(t_{0})\geq 0.95
, for
\alpha=0.25
.

Up to this point, we handled the Gaussian-like state (
II.2
) as an approximation to the real
sinc
-like state (
II.2
). However, in recent years, domain-engineered crystals with a Gaussian phase-matching function have attracted attention. These engineered crystals have been mainly used in order to engineer pure spectral states

;
Graffitti et al. 2017
;
Graffitti et al. 2018
, where only the spectral DOF of the biphoton state has been considered. Thus the state from Eq. (

II.3
Spatiotemporal biphoton state

In this section, we investigate, how well the Gaussian approximation preserves the coupling between the spatial and spectral DOFs in SPDC. The description of the spatiotemporal biphoton state with a Gaussian phase-matching function is rather rare but has been successfully used to characterize the correlation strength between these two DOFs
Osorio et al. 2008
. Here, the phase-matching function
\sinc(L\Delta k_{z}/2)
is again approximated by
\exp{[-\alpha(L\Delta k_{z}/2})^{2}]
, but the momentum mismatch is characterized by the full expression from Eq. (
2
). Surprisingly, this approximation holds very well for
\alpha=0.25
giving a fidelity value of
0.94
. Similar to the frequency-resolved biphoton state, the fidelity depends on the pulse duration
t_{0}
, but not on the crystal length. The argument here is the same: As long as the
sinc
function dominates over the pump function, the fidelity remains the same with a value of
0.94
. This is the minimum value for the fidelity, and it becomes larger if
t_{0}
is increased.

II.4
Biphoton state in the Laguerre-Gaussian basis

At the end of this paper, we apply the Gaussian approximation to the results from our previous work. In Ref.
Baghdasaryan et al. 2022
, we derived a general expression for the spatiotemporal biphoton state and successfully tested it in Ref.
Sevilla-Gutiérrez et al. 2022
. The expression for the biphoton state from Ref.
Baghdasaryan et al. 2022
still includes one-dimensional integration over the crystal length, which should be performed numerically. The Gaussian or
cosine
-Gaussian approximations would transform that expression into a fully analytical form.

We construct the Gaussian approximation for the spatially-resolved biphoton state by ignoring the dispersive properties of interacting beams, so that the phase matching has the form
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{s}-\bm{q}_{i}}\right\rvert}^{2}/2k_{p}
. We consider the biphoton state with a Laguerre-Gaussian (LG) pump beam of well-defined radial index

and projection of orbital angular momentum (OAM)
\ell

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{p,\ell}}=N\iint

\displaystyle d\bm{q}_{s}\>d\bm{q}_{i}\>\mathrm{LG}_{p}^{\ell}(\bm{q}_{s}+\bm{q}_{i})\,\exp\biggl(-\alpha\frac{L\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\bm{q}_{s}-\bm{q}_{i}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{4k_{p}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\times\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{s}(\bm{q}_{s})\>\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i}(\bm{q}_{i})\ket{vac},

(16)

where the spatial distribution of the pump in the momentum space is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{LG}_{p}^{\ell}(\rho,\varphi)=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{-\rho^{2}\,w^{2}}{4}}\,e^{i\ell\,\varphi}\,\sum_{u=0}^{p}\,T_{u}^{p,\ell}\,\rho^{2u+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\ell\right\rvert}}

(17)

with

being

\displaystyle T_{u}^{p,\ell}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{p!\,(p+|\ell|)!}{\pi}}\,\biggr(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2}}\biggl)^{2u+|\ell|+1}\,\frac{(-1)^{p+u}(i)^{\ell}}{(p-u)!\,(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\ell\right\rvert}+u)!\,u!}.

Similar to Ref.
Baghdasaryan et al. 2022
, we perform a mode decomposition of the biphoton state in the LG basis
\ket{p,\ell}=\int d\bm{q}\,\mathrm{LG}_{p}^{\ell}(\bm{q})\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\bm{q})\ket{vac}
for the characterization of the biphoton spatial DOF

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{p,\ell}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{s},p_{i}=0}^{\infty}\sum^{\infty}_{\ell_{s},\ell_{i}=-\infty}C_{p,p_{s},p_{i}}^{\ell,\ell_{s},\ell_{i}}\ket{p_{s},\ell_{s}}\ket{p_{i},\ell_{i}},

(18)

where the coincidence amplitudes can be calculated from the overlap integral

III
Conclusion

The Gaussian-like phase-matching functions turn out to deliver very good approximations to
sinc
-like states. For most experiments, we recommend the
cosine
-Gaussian approximation with
\alpha=0.39
and
\beta=0.49
for a spatially resolved biphoton state, as this provides analytical results for many parameters of interest with a high fidelity. For a spatiotemporal and frequency-resolved biphoton state, we suggest using the Gaussian approximation with
\alpha=0.25
. However, with more challenging experiments aiming to maximize fiber coupling efficiencies or spatial entanglement, we still recommend using the general
sinc
-like state.

Loops in surfaces, chord diagrams, interlace graphs: operad factorisations and generating grammars
Christopher-Lloyd Simon

Abstract
A filoop is a generic immersion of a circle in a closed oriented surface, whose complement is a disjoint union of discs, considered up to orientation preserving diffeomorphisms. It gives rise to a chord diagram C C which has an interlace graph G G , called a chordiagraph. For a graph G G with even degrees, we compute a quantity mg ⁡ ( G ) \ming(G) which yields, for every chord diagram C C with interlace graph G G , the minimal genus of filoops with chord diagram C C . If mg ⁡ ( G ) = 0 \ming(G)=0 then C C admits exactly two framings of genus 0 0 , corresponding to spheriloops. After recalling the Cunningham factorisation of connected graphs, we describe a canonical factorisation of filoops into spheric sums followed by toric sums, for which the genus is additive. This is analogous to the factorisation of compact connected 3-manifolds along spheres and tori. We describe unambiguous context-sensitive grammars generating the set of all graphs and with mg = 0 \ming=0 and deduce stability properties with respect to spheric and toric factorisations. Similar results hold for chordiagraphs with mg = 0 \ming=0 and their corresponding spheriloops. Keywords: Curves in surfaces, chord diagrams, interlace graphs, split decomposition, unambiguous grammar, genus.

0.1
Homological invariants of filoops and their interlace graphs

The study of spheriloops and their chord diagrams was initiated by Gauss in
[

]

0.2
Unique factorisations of graphs, chord diagrams and filoops

We first recall Cunningham’s of graphs and Bouchet’s factorisation of chord diagrams.

Definition 0.4
.

A

A connected graph is called

A connected graph is called

or stars

Definition 0.5
.

A

\rho_{x}\colon V_{x}\to N(x)

to the neighbours of

The

, and distinct vertices

are connected when for every node

of the injective sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\in V_{T}
joining

to

and

are connected.

The GLT is

is prime or degenerate, and for connected nodes

A connected (Gaussian chordia)graph and its reduced GLT factorisation.

The
[

, Theorem 3]
of Cunningham revisted as
[
GP12
, Theorem 2.9]
by Gioan–Paul states that a connected graph is the accessibility graph of a unique reduced graph-labelled-tree.

Bouchet lifted this unique factorisation to chord diagrams by showing in
[
Bou87
]
that a connected chordiagraph, either prime or degenerate, is the interlace graph of a unique chord diagram up to orientation reversal.
Thus starting from the Cunningham factorisation of a connected graph, one may reconstruct all chord diagrams with that interlace graph by inserting chord diagrams in the place of chords. The only ambiguity arising during this reconstruction appears while replacing a chord by a sub-chord-diagram: the choices form a torsor under the rectangular group
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
.
In particular, two (unoriented) chord diagrams have the same interlace graphs if and only if they are related by a sequence of
mutations
, which consist in applying a symmetry of the rectangular group
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
to a sub-chord diagram consisting of (at most) two intervals of the circle.

Our main results in section

consists in lifting these unique factorisations to filoops into

and

and
2.22
can be sketched as follows.

Definition 0.6

(Sketch)

A

A
plumbing
of filoops consists in performing a connected sum along discs intersecting each filoop in a pair of transverse intervals.
Essential plumbings corresponds to splits of interlace graphs.

Corollary 0.7

(Spheric-sum factorisation)
.

A filoop admits a unique spheric-sum factorisation into filoops with connected interlace graphs: it has the structure of a plane tree, which can be read off its chord diagram, whose vertices correspond to the connected components of its interlace graph.

The genus is additive under spheric sums.

Theorem 0.8

(Essential-plumbing factorisation)
.

Fix a filoop
\gamma
with connected interlace graph
G
.

A split factorisation of
G
into a graph-labelled-tree
(T,G_{x})
yields an essential-plumbing factorisation of
\gamma
into a filoop-labelled-tree
(T,\gamma_{x})
.

The interlace graph of
\gamma_{x}
is the local-complement of
G_{x}
at all its vertices in any order, which will prescribe the order in which one must perform the plumbings associated to its incident tree-edges.

The genus of
\gamma
is the sum of genera of the
\gamma_{x}
.

0.3
Generating grammars for Gaussian graphs and spheriloops

The following algorithm, combining the results in the previous section with Corollary
0.3
, generates all spheriloops with a fixed connected Gaussian chordiagraph in linear time on its number of vertices.

Corollary 0.9
.

Consider a connected Gaussian chordiagraph
G
. The set of all spheriloops whose chord diagrams have interlace graph
G
are obtained as follows.

1.

Compute the Cunningham decomposition
(T,G_{x})
of
G
, and realise every (prime or degenerate) factor by a (unique) unoriented chord diagram.

2.

Compose the chord diagrams according to the tree
T
in all possible ways (at most
4^{k-1}
choices where
k
is the number of internal nodes of the tree).

3.

Compute the two framings of genus
0
, by integrating the Rosentiehl form as in Corollary
0.3
.

4.

Construct the loop from the framed chord diagram as in Proposition
1.3
.

In particular, if a connected Gaussian chordiagraph is prime or degenerate, then it corresponds to a unique loop in the sphere up to homeomorphism.

Finally we characterise the GLT factorisations of connected Gaussian graphs in

Theorem 0.10

(Grammatical generation of Gaussian GLT)
.

The following dynamical generation algorithm yields all and only connected Gaussian graphs:

Atoms

For every (prime or degenerate) graph
G
, compute its CL2-weightings
\bmod{2}

Bonds

Choose weighted GLT

and

connected to control vertices
u_{a}\in A_{x}
and
u_{b}\in B_{y}

is equal to the degree of
f_{b}
and the weight of
f_{a}
is equal to the degree of
u_{b}

Molecules

Among the weighted GLT so constructed, those for which all leaves are connected to control vertices of odd weight and even degree are Gaussian.

When restricting to prime and degenerate graphs in the first step, every Gaussian graph is generated only once (possibly up to automorphisms depending on the implementation).

Remark 0.11

(Generating spheriloops)

Definition 1.6

(Graphs and Rosenstiehl form)
.

A simple graph
G
on a set of vertices
V_{G}
is encoded by its adjacency matrix
\E\colon V_{G}\times V_{G}\to\{0,1\}
, which is symmetric and vanishes on the diagonal.

We define its
Rosentiehl
symmetric bilinear form
\CR\colon V_{G}\times V_{G}\to\mathbb{Z}/2
by
\CR=\E+\E^{2}
, so that:

\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\notin E_{G},\quad\CR(x,y)=0+\Card N(x)\cap N(y)

(RC0)

\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\in E_{G},\quad\CR(x,y)=1+\Card N(x)\cap N(y)

(RC1)

A symmetric function
V_{G}\times V_{G}\to\mathbb{Z}/2
restricted to
E_{G}
yields a
1
-cochain, which is a
1
-cocycle as
G
has no
2
-cells: we call it the associated cocycle in
Z^{1}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
and denote it by the same letter.

Definition 1.7

(Even and Gaussian conditions)
.

We say that a graph
G
satisfies the property:

EN1

when every vertex has an even degree:

\forall x\in V_{G},\qquad\CR(x,x)\equiv\Card N(x)\equiv 0

(EN1)

EN2

when distinct non-adjacent vertices share an even number of neighbours:

\forall x,y\in V_{G},\quad x\neq y,\qquad\E(x,y)=0\implies\CR(x,y)\equiv\Card N(x)\cap N(y)\equiv 0

(EN2)

RC

when the Rosenstiehl cocycle is null in cohomology, namely
\CR\in B^{1}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
, or when:

\forall(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}=x_{0})\in V_{G}^{l},\qquad\textstyle\prod_{1}^{l}\E(x_{i-1},x_{i})\neq 0\implies\textstyle\sum_{1}^{l}\CR(x_{i-1},x_{i})\equiv 0

(RC)

A graph will be called
Gaussian
when it satisfies
EN1
and
EN2
and
RC
.

A

To a framed chord diagram

Note that a chord

Corollary 1.13

(Bicolourable)

The Proposition

\iff

\iff

\iff

In

Theorem 1.14
.

Consider a bicolourable framed chord diagram
C_{\varphi}
.

If
x,y\in V_{G}
are not connected by an edge then
\I_{\varphi}(x,y)\equiv\E^{2}(x,y)\equiv\Card N(x)\cap N(y)
.

The cohomology class of
\I_{\varphi}\in Z^{1}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
is independant of the framing
\varphi
.

Each cocycle in this cohomology class arises from
2^{b_{0}}
framings, where
b_{0}=\dim H^{0}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
.

(In particular
\I_{\varphi}
determines
\varphi
up to frame inversions on connected components of
G
.)

Proof.

On the one hand, for distinct
x,y\in C
with
\E(x,y)=0
, one may change the framing at
x
or
y
so that
(x_{\infty},x_{0})
and
(y_{\infty},x_{0})
are disjoint, and thus
\I_{\varphi}(x,y)=\Card N(x)\cap N(y)=\CR(x,y)
.

On the other hand Lemma
1.12
implies that if two framings
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}
differ only at
z
then the cocycles
\I_{\varphi},\I_{\varphi^{\prime}}\in Z^{1}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
differ by the coboundary
d\delta_{z}
of the indicator function
\delta_{z}\colon V_{G}\to\{0,1\}
of
x
.
Hence the cohomology classes
[\I_{\varphi}]\in H^{1}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
do not depend on the framings
\varphi
.

Moreover the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-vector space
B^{1}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
of
1
-coboundaries is generated by the
d\delta_{x}
for

with one relation of the form
\sum d\delta_{y}
for each connected connected component of
G
.
Hence in the cohomology class
[\I_{\varphi}]
, each cocycle arises from exactly
2^{b_{0}}
framings (related to one another by changing orientations of all chords in unions of connected components of
G
).
∎

Proposition 1.15

(Bicolourings = framings)
.

Consider a bicolourable chord diagram
C
.

To a framing
\varphi\colon C\to\{\infty,0\}
we associate two bicolourings
\chi\colon V_{G}\to\{\bullet,\circ\}
by choosing one for the corresponding filoop
F\setminus\gamma
and colouring each intersection
x\in\gamma
according to the region in the direction of the sum of the tangent vectors
\vec{\gamma}(x_{\infty})+\vec{\gamma}(x_{0})
. They differ by a global change in colour.

To a bicolouring
\chi\colon V_{G}\to\{\bullet,\circ\}
we associate two functions
\varphi\colon C\to\{\infty,0\}
by choosing the value on some letter, and extending so that consecutive letters have the same value if and only if they have different colours.
These functions are framings and they differ by a global frame inversion.

Seen as functions along the circle with letters of
C
, the bicolouring
\chi
is the skew-derivative of the framing
\varphi
, and we just integrated the two skew-primitives.
This yields a correspondence between bicolourings
\chi\colon V_{G}\to\{\bullet,\circ\}
and framings
\varphi\colon C\to\{\infty,0\}
up to global inversion.

Proof.

We first show that the integration
\chi\mapsto\varphi
yields framings.
This is because between two occurrences of any letter
x\in C
, there is an even number of letters in total, hence an odd number of consecutive pairs; and an even number of alternations in colours, hence an odd number of repetitions in colours. Consequently the two occurrences of
x
have different framings.

Now we show that it is indeed inverse to the derivation
\varphi\mapsto\chi
.
For this observe that while traversing the intersections of the filoop associated to
C_{\varphi}
, the variation of framing is related to the variation of colours as claimed.
In particular while constructing the filoop from the framing
(C,\varphi)
as in
1.3
, one could consistently use only the bicolouring
(C,\chi)
by attaching the bicoloured crosses so as to match the colours of their regions.
∎

Definition 1.16

(Bicolouring form)

The

Theorem 1.17

(Bilinear forms)
.

Consider a bicolourable framed chord diagram
C_{\varphi}
.

Its associated intersection form
\I_{\varphi}
, colour form
\CB_{\chi}
and Rosenstiehl form
\CR
are related by:

\I_{\varphi}-\CB_{\chi}\equiv\E+\E^{2}

Hence the intersection form can be computed from the bicoloured graph.
We also recover the fact that
\I_{\varphi}
determines
\CB_{\chi}
, whence
\varphi
up to inversions on connected components of the interlace graph.

In particular when
G
satisfies
EN2
, namely
\E^{2}
vanishes on the non-edges, the intersection form can be computed from the graph and its colour coboundary
d\chi\in B^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)
.

Proof.

Note that changing the framing
\varphi
at a chord
z
corresponds to changing colouring
\chi
of this chord
z
, hence to changing the colour form
d\chi
by the coboundary
d\delta_{z}
.
Hence the quantity
\I_{\varphi}-\CB_{\chi}
is invariant by a change of framing, and we may thus choose them to alternate as reading the letters of
C
, so that all colours are equal and
\CB_{\chi}=0
.

We must now prove that such an alternating framing satisfies
\I_{\varphi}=\E+\E^{2}
.
For chords that are not interlaced this follows from Proposition
1.14
.
Consider interlaced chords
x,y
,
and recall that:

\I_{\varphi}(x,y)\equiv 1+\Card\{z_{j}\in C_{\varphi}\mid z_{j}\in(x_{\infty},x_{0})\wedge z_{1/j}\in(y_{0},y_{\infty})\}

Hence must show that
\Card\{z_{j}\in C_{\varphi}\mid z_{j}\in(x_{\infty},x_{0})\wedge z_{1/j}\in(y_{0},y_{\infty})\}\equiv\Card N(x)\cap N(y)
, and by partitioning those sets, cancelling those in common and those with even cardinal, we want:

\displaystyle\Card\{z_{j}\in C_{\varphi}\mid z_{j}\in(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})\wedge z_{1/j}\in(y_{\infty},x_{0})\}+\Card\{z_{j}\in C_{\varphi}\mid z_{j}\in(y_{\infty},x_{0})\wedge z_{1/j}\in(x_{0},y_{0})\}

\displaystyle\equiv\Card\{z_{j}\in C_{\varphi}\mid z_{j}\in(y_{\infty},x_{0})\wedge z_{1/j}\in(y_{0},x_{\infty})\}

These three sets yield a partition of the chords having one endpoint in the open interval
(y_{\infty},x_{0})
.
Since
\varphi
is alternating, this open interval
(y_{\infty},x_{0})
contains an even number of letters, so the sum of these three cardinals is
\equiv 0
, and this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 1.25

(Variety of Lagrangian graphs)

Let us work over the field

For us, a graph

x_{1},\dots,x_{l}
, which singles out a pair of vertices
u_{j},v_{j}
in each graph
G_{x_{j}}
decorating its nodes
x_{j}
.

Suppose that the two leaves are disconnected.
If there is more than one disconnecting node then they have no common neighbours.
If there is a unique disconnecting node
x_{j}
, then their common neighbours are the leaves immediately accessible from the common neighbours of the vertices
u_{j},v_{j}
in
G_{x_{j}}
, and their cardinal is
\CR_{j}(u_{j},v_{j})
.
This proves the claim for the condition
EN2
.

Suppose that the leaves are connected.
Their common neighbours are the disjoint union over the nodes
x_{j}
along the geodesic that connects them of the leaves that are immediately accessible from the common neighbours of the vertices
u_{j},v_{j}
in
G_{x_{j}}
, and their cardinal is
\sum_{j}\CR_{j}(u_{j},v_{j})
.

Finally consider a cycle of the accessibility graph
G
, and observe that in the GLT it decomposes as a connected sum of local cycles in the graphs
G_{x_{j}}
.
The condition
RC
for the global cycle in
G
is thus equivalent to the sum of the conditions
RC
for the local cycles in the weighted graphs
G_{x_{j}}
.
∎

The following Theorem, which is a Corollary of
2.3
and
3.9
, says that connected Gaussian graphs form a language generated by an arborescent grammar which is context-sensitive and unambiguous:

\circ,\circ

non-terminal symbols are leaves in CL2-weighted (prime or degenerate) graphs,

\circ-\circ

branching rules are constrained by matching weights and internal degrees,

\bullet-\bullet

terminal symbols are (prime or degenerate) CL2L1-weighted graphs.

Moreover this generation is unique when all primitive and terminal graphs are prime or degenerate.

Theorem 3.10

(Grammatical generation of Gaussian GLT)
.

The following dynamical generation algorithm yields all and only connected Gaussian graphs:

Atoms

For every (prime or degenerate) graph
G
, compute its CL2-weightings
\bmod{2}
and construct a weighted GLT whose tree has only one node decorated by
G
.

Bonds

Choose weighted GLT
A
and
B
from those already constructed with leaves
f_{a}
and
f_{b}
connected to control vertices
u_{a}\in A_{x}
and
u_{b}\in B_{y}
such that the weight of
u_{a}
is equal to the degree of
f_{b}
and the weight of
f_{a}
is equal to the degree of
u_{b}
, and form the grafting
C=K_{2}((A,f_{a}),(B,f_{b}))
.

Molecules

Among the weighted GLT so constructed, those for which all leaves are connected to control vertices of odd weight and even degree are Gaussian.

When restricting to prime and degenerate graphs in the first step, every Gaussian graph is generated only once (possibly up to automorphisms depending on the implementation).

Corollary 3.12

(Constructing Gaussian graphs)
.

The following constructions yields a GLT factorisation of a Gaussian graph from any CL2-weighted graph.

1.

Choose a graph
G_{0}
with a CL2 weighting and construct a graph-labelled-tree whose tree has only one node and is decorated by

2.

Definition 3.15

(Relative evenness poperties)
.

Consider a simple graph
G
with adjacency matrix
\E
and Rosenstiehl form
\CR=\E+\E^{2}
, so that
\forall x,y\in V_{G}
we have
\CR(x,y)=\E(x,y)+\Card N(x)\cap N(y)
.
The restriction of
\CR
to the edges
E_{G}
defines the Rosenstiehl cocycle
\CR\in Z^{1}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
.

We say that graph
G
satisfies the property:

EN1

at
v\in V_{G}
when
\CR(v,v)\equiv 0
.

EN1

on
W\subset V_{G}
when
\forall v\in W
we have
\CR(v,v)\equiv 0
.

EN2

at
v
when
\forall x\in V_{G}
, we have
\E(v,x)=0\implies\CR(v,x)\equiv 0
.

EN2

on
W\subset V_{G}^{2}
when
\forall(x,y)\in W
with
x\neq y
, we have
\E(x,y)=0\implies\CR(x,y)\equiv 0
.

RC

at
v\in V_{G}
when
\forall x,y\in N_{G}(v)
, we have
\E(x,y)\neq 0\implies\CR(x,y)\equiv\CR(v,x)+\CR(y,v)
.

RC

relative to
W\subset V_{G}
when the integral of
\CR
vanishes along along paths
(x_{0},\dots,x_{l})\in V_{G}^{l+1}
with
\prod_{1}^{l}\E(x_{i-1},x_{i})\neq 0
and either
x_{0}=x_{l}
or else
x_{0},x_{l}\in W
and
\CR(x_{0},x_{l})=0
.

(Note that if
U\subset W
then
RC
relative to
W
implies
RC
relative to
U
.)

Lemma 3.16
.

Consider a graph
G
with a vertex
v\in V_{G}
, and let
G^{\prime}=(G*v)\setminus v
be its

.

If
G
is Gaussian the then

is Gaussian and even satisfies

relative to
N_{G}(v)
.

If
G^{\prime}
is Gaussian and satisfies
RC
relative to
N_{G}(v)
then

is Gaussian provided it satisfies

and

at

as well as
EN2

\{v\}\cup N_{G}(v)
.

More precisely:

1.

If
G
satisfies
EN1
at
v
, then
G
satisfies
EN1
if and only if
G^{\prime}
satisfies
EN1
.

2.

If
G
satisfies

RC
at
v
, then
G
satisfies
EN2
implies
G^{\prime}
satisfies
EN2
.

If
G
satisfies
EN2
on
\{v\}\cup N_{G}(v)
, then
G^{\prime}
satisfies
EN2
implies
G
satisfies
EN2
.

3.

If
G
satisfies
EN2
on
\{v\}\cup N_{G}(v)
then
G
satisfies
RC
implies
G^{\prime}
satisfies
RC
.

If
G
satisfies
EN2
on
\{v\}\cup N_{G}(v)
and
RC
at
v
, then
G^{\prime}
satisfies
RC
relative to
N_{G}(v)
implies
G
satisfies
RC
.

Proof.

We may assume
G
to be connected by restricting it to the connected component of
v
.

1.
Suppose
G
satisfies
EN1
at
v
and let
x\in V_{G^{\prime}}
.

If
x\notin N_{G}(v)
then
N_{G^{\prime}}(x)=N_{G}(x)
.

If
x\in N_{G}(v)
then
N_{G^{\prime}}(x)=(N_{G}(x)\setminus\{v\})\triangle(N_{G}(v)\setminus\{x\})=N_{G}(x)\triangle\{x,v\}\triangle N_{G}(v)
so by additivity of the cardinal modulo
2
we have
\Card N_{G^{\prime}}(x)\equiv\Card N_{G}(x)
.

2.
Suppose
G
satisfies
EN2
at
v
and
RC
at
v
. Let
x,y\in V_{G^{\prime}}
be distinct.

If
x,y\notin N_{G}(v)
then
\E(x,y)=\E^{\prime}(x,y)
and
N_{G^{\prime}}(x)\cap N_{G^{\prime}}(y)=N_{G}(x)\cap N_{G}(y)
.

If
x\in N_{G}(v)
and
y\notin N_{G}(v)
then on the one hand
\E^{\prime}(x,y)=\E(x,y)
and on the other hand, using the previous computations and the distributivity of
\cap
over
\triangle
, we find that:

\displaystyle N_{G^{\prime}}(x)\cap N_{G^{\prime}}(y)

\displaystyle=(N_{G}(x)\triangle\{x,v\}\triangle N_{G}(v))\cap N_{G}(y)

\displaystyle=\big(N_{G}(x)\cap N_{G}(y)\big)\triangle\big(\{x,v\}\cap N_{G}(y)\big)\triangle\big(N_{G}(v)\cap N_{G}(y)\big)

so in this case
\CR^{\prime}(x,y)-\E^{\prime}(x,y)=\CR(x,y)+\CR(v,y)
, and as
G
satsfies
EN2
at
v
we have:

\CR^{\prime}(x,y)-\E^{\prime}(x,y)=\CR(x,y).

In particular, if
\E^{\prime}(x,y)=\E(x,y)=0
then
\CR^{\prime}(x,y)=\CR(x,y)=0
, proving the second point on
(x,y)
.
However, if
\E^{\prime}(x,y)=\E(x,y)=1
then
\CR^{\prime}(x,y)=1+\CR(x,y)
and this will serve later to prove 3.

If
x,y\in N_{G}(v)
, then on the one hand
\E^{\prime}(x,y)=1-\E(x,y)
and on the other hand we compute:

\displaystyle N_{G^{\prime}}(x)\cap N_{G^{\prime}}(y)

\displaystyle=\big((N_{G}(x)\setminus\{v\})\triangle(N_{G}(v)\setminus\{x\})\big)\cap\big((N_{G}(y)\setminus\{v\})\triangle(N_{G}(v)\setminus\{y\})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(N_{G}(x)\cap N_{G}(y)\setminus\{v\}\big)\triangle\big(N_{G}(v)\setminus\{x\}\cap N_{G}(v)\setminus\{y\}\big)

\displaystyle\triangle\big(N_{G}(x)\cap N_{G}(v)\setminus\{y\}\big)\triangle\big(N_{G}(y)\cap N_{G}(v)\setminus\{x\}\big)

so
\CR^{\prime}(x,y)-\E^{\prime}(x,y)\equiv\big(\CR(x,y)-\E(x,y)-1\big)+\big(\CR(x,v)-\E(x,v)-1\big)+\big(\CR(y,v)-\E(y,v)-1\big)

\CR^{\prime}(x,y)\equiv\CR(v,x)+\CR(x,y)+\CR(y,v).

Thus when
\E^{\prime}(x,y)=1-\E(x,y)=0
, as

satisfies

at

\CR(v,x)+\CR(x,y)+\CR(y,v)\equiv 0
, whence
\CR^{\prime}(x,y)\equiv 0
, and this concludes the proof of 2.

However when
\E^{\prime}(x,y)=1-\E(x,y)=1
, if

satsfies

then
\CR(x,y)\equiv 0
, whereby
\CR^{\prime}(x,y)\equiv\CR(x,v)+\CR(v,y)
, and this will serve to prove 3.

3.
Suppose
G
satisfies
EN2
on
\{v\}\cup N_{G}(v)
and
RC
at
v
(so all previous calculations hold).

Consider a relative cycle
\gamma^{\prime}\in Z_{1}(G^{\prime},N_{C}(v))
, represented as a sequence
x_{0},\dots,x_{l}\in V_{G^{\prime}}^{l+1}
with
\prod_{1}^{l}\E^{\prime}(x_{i-1},x_{i})=1
, and either
x_{0}=x_{l}
or else
x_{0},x_{l}\in N_{G}(v)
and
\CR(x_{0},x_{l})=0
.
We construct a cycle
\gamma\in Z_{1}(G)
obtained from
\gamma^{\prime}

by replacing every segment
(x_{i-1},x_{i})\in N_{G}(v)^{2}
with the segment
(x_{i-1},v,x_{i})
, and appending

.

Let us show

If
x,y\in\gamma^{\prime}

then
\CR(x,y)=\CR^{\prime}(x,y)
.
The edges

of
\gamma^{\prime}
with exactly one endpoint in

are in even number, and they all satisfy

If

Corollary 3.18
.

If
(G_{A},a)
and
(G_{B},b)
are Gaussian then so is
(G_{A},a)\triangle(G_{B},b)
.

If
(G_{A},a)\triangle(G_{B},b)
is Gaussian then
\Card N_{A}(a)
and
\Card N_{B}(b)
have the same parity, and if it is even then
(G_{A},a)
and
(G_{B},b)
are Gaussian.

Proof.

If both
(G_{A},a)
and
(G_{B},b)
are Gaussian, then they come from a spherical loop, and so does
(G_{A},a)\triangle(G_{B},b)
by the the previous construction, which is therefore Gaussian.

If
(G_{C},c)=(G_{A},a)\triangle(G_{B},b)
is Gaussian then its GLT factorisations satisfy the weighted Gaussian conditions in Proposition
3.9
.
Consider a (possibly non-reduced) GLT factorisation of
(G_{C},c)
in which we may identify GLT factorisations of
(G_{A},a)
and
(G_{B},b)
composed into a rooted triangle.

The vertices of the triangle have weights
1
,
\Card N_{A}(a)
,
\Card N_{B}(b)
, so the Gaussian hypothesis implies that
\Card N_{A}(a)\equiv\Card N_{B}(b)\bmod{2}
.
The weights of the vertices in the factors of
G_{C}
are obtained from those of
G_{A}
and
G_{B}
by adding
\Card N_{B}(b)
to the vertices directly accessible from
a
in the GLT of
G_{A}
and
\Card N_{A}(a)
to the vertices directly accessible from
b
in the GLT of
G_{B}
.

Consequently if these cardinals are even, the GLT factorisations of
(G_{A},a)
and
(G_{B},b)
satisfy the weighted Gaussian conditions in Proposition
3.9
.
∎

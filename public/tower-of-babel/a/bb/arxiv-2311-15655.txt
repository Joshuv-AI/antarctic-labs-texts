Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the optimal (partial) transport problems when the target is a non-convex polygonal domain in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . For the complete optimal transportation, we prove that the singular set is locally a 1-dimensional smooth curve, except for a finite number of points. For the optimal partial transportation, we prove that the free boundary is smooth except for a finite number of singular points.

35J96, 35J25, 35B65.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
and
\Omega^{*}
be two bounded domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
m
be a positive constant satisfying:

m\leq\min\Big\{|\Omega|,\,|\Omega^{*}|\Big\},

where
|\cdot|
denotes the two-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
A non-negative, finite Borel measure
\gamma
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a
transport plan
(with mass
m
) from
\Omega
to
\Omega^{*}
if
\gamma(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})=m
and for any Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the following conditions hold:

\begin{split}\gamma(E\times\mathbb{R}^{2})&\leq|E\cap\Omega|,\\
\gamma(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times E)&\leq|E\cap\Omega^{*}|.\end{split}

A transport plan
\gamma
is
optimal
if it minimises the cost functional:

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x-y|^{2}\,d\gamma(x,y)

among all transport plans, see
[
29
,
30
]
.

When
|\Omega|=|\Omega^{*}|=m
, the above is the complete optimal transport problem.
By Brenier’s theorem
[
2
]
, there exists a convex function
u:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},
globally Lipschitz,
such that the optimal transport plan
\gamma=(Id\times Du)_{\sharp}\chi_{{}_{\Omega}}
, with

(1.2)

(Du)_{\sharp}\chi_{{}_{\Omega}}=\chi_{{}_{\Omega^{*}}}

and
Du(x)\in\overline{\Omega^{*}}
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The regularity of the potential function
u
has been investigated intensively, see
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
9
,
11
,
13
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
.
If the target domain
\Omega^{*}
is non-convex, singularities may arise, see
[
3
]
for instance.
Let

(1.3)

A:=\{x\in\Omega:u\text{ is not differentiable at }x\}

be the singular set.
The structure of singular set in optimal transportation was first investigated by Yu in
[
31
]
, where he proved that if
\Omega
is convex and
\Omega^{*}
is a planar
C^{1}
domain satisfying certain mild conditions, then the singular set is a 1-dimensional
C^{1}
manifold (a disjoint union of countably many
C^{1}
curves) except for a countable set. Subsequently, Figalli
[
16
]
established the same result for general non-convex planar domains.

Our first result gives a more elaborate description of the structure of the singular set
A
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a bounded domain and
\Omega^{*}
is a non-convex polygonal domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
|\Omega|=|\Omega^{*}|
. Suppose
u
is a convex solution to (
1.2
). Then, the singular set
A
is either a finite set or, except for a finite number of points

\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
A
is locally a 1-dimensional smooth curve. Moreover, for each
i=1,\dots,n
, either
x_{i}
is an isolated singularity, or
A
is a union of a finite number of Lipschitz curves near
x_{i}
.

Optimal transport between polygonal domains has important practical applications. For example, in numerical computations, particularly in finite element methods, the generation of high-quality meshes over polygonal domains is crucial. Optimal transport can be used to design mesh generation algorithms that distribute mass over polygonal domains, improving the accuracy and efficiency of simulations. We refer the reader to
[
22
,
24
]
and references therein.

Remark 1.1
.

Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be as in the above theorem. Denote
F:=\{x_{i}:i=1,\dots,n\}
.
If
x\in A\setminus F
, then
B_{r_{x}}(x)\cap A
is a smooth curve for some small
r_{x}>0
.
Due to the finiteness of
F
, for each
x_{i}
, if it is not an isolated singular point, there exists a small
r_{i}>0
such that

B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\cap A=\cup_{j=1}^{k_{i}}\gamma_{ij}
for some positive integer
k_{i}
, where
\gamma_{ij}
is a Lipschitz curve that is smooth except at the point
x_{i}
.

When
m<\min\Big\{|\Omega|,\,|\Omega^{*}|\Big\}
, the minimisation problem (
1.1
) is called the
optimal partial transportation
.
The word “partial” indicates that not all of the mass in
\Omega
is transported to
\Omega^{*}
. The existence and uniqueness of the optimal transport plan have been proved in
[
7
]
, under the assumption that
\Omega
and
\Omega^{*}
are separated by a straight line. For general cases, the reader is referred to
[
14
,
15
,
20
]
and references therein.
Let
U\subset\Omega
be the sub-domain in which the mass
m=|U|
is transported to
V\subset\Omega^{*}
by the optimal transport plan. The sets
\mathcal{F}:=\partial U\cap\Omega
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}:=\partial V\cap\Omega^{*}
are called the “free boundaries” of the problem. Our second main result of this paper concerns the regularity of the free boundary.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a bounded domain and
\Omega^{*}
is a non-convex polygonal domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, separated by a straight line. Let
m<\min\Big\{|\Omega|,\,|\Omega^{*}|\Big\}
be the mass transported. Then the free boundary
\mathcal{F}
is smooth except for a finite number of singular points.

Remark 1.2
.

The results in Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
also hold for general positive smooth densities. If the densities are only
bounded from below and above by positive constants, then except a finite number of points, the singular set
(resp. free boundary) in Theorem
1.1
(resp. Theorem
1.2
) is locally a
C^{1,\alpha}
regular curve.

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows: In Section
2
, we introduce some tools and definitions that will be used in subsequent analyses. In Section
3
, we investigate the structure of the singular set
A
and prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section
4
, we study the regularity of the free boundary
\mathcal{F}
and prove Theorem
1.2
. In Section
5
, we sketch the proof of the
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate that completes the smoothness result.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X,Y
be two bounded open sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfying
|X|=|Y|
. By Brenier’s theorem
[
2
]
, there exists a convex function
w:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, globally Lipschitz, such that

(Dw)_{\sharp}\chi_{{}_{X}}=\chi_{{}_{Y}},\quad\text{ with }Dw(x)\in\overline{Y}\ \text{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Let
w^{*}
denote the Legendre transform of
w,
namely,

w^{*}(y):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\{y\cdot x-w(x)\},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

We need the following definition of sub-level sets. Suppose
w:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a convex function, whose graph does not contain any infinite straight line.

Definition 2.1
.

The sub-gradient of
w
at
y_{0}
is defined by

\partial^{-}w(y_{0})=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:w(y)\geq w(y_{0})+(y-y_{0})\cdot p\quad\forall\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\},

and

\partial^{-}w(E)={\cup}_{y\in E}\partial^{-}w(y)\quad\forall\,E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}.

For any domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and any non-negative function
f
defined on
D,

we say

\mbox{det}\,D^{2}w=(\geq,\leq)\,f\quad\text{ in }D

in the Alexandrov sense, if

|\partial^{-}w(E)|=(\geq,\leq)\int_{E}f\quad\forall\,E\subset D.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a point
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a small constant
h>0
, we define the centred sub-level set of
w
at
y_{0}
with height
h
as

S^{c}_{h}[w](y_{0}):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:w(y)<w(y_{0})+(y-y_{0})\cdot\bar{p}+h\right\},

where
\bar{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is chosen such that the centre of mass of
S^{c}_{h}[w](y_{0})
is
y_{0}
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
w(0)=0
and
w\geq 0
, by
[
12
, Remark 2.2]
, we have that

(2.1)

w\leq Ch\quad\text{in}\ S^{c}_{h}[w](0),

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
h
.
By
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
,

S^{c}_{h}[w](0)
is balanced with respect to
0,
namely,

y\in S^{c}_{h}[w](0)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad-Cy\in S^{c}_{h}[w](0)

for a constant
b>0
independent of
h
.

3.
Singularities in optimal transportation

Suppose that
\Omega,\Omega^{*}
satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
.
Let the vertices of
\Omega^{*}
be
\{b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\ i=1,\cdots,m\}
; and the edges are
[b_{i}b_{i+1}]
, with
b_{m+1}:=b_{1}.

We use the notation
[pq]
to denote a closed segment connecting
p
and
q,
and use
(pq)
to denote the relative interior of
[pq].
The notation
(pq]:=(pq)\cup\{q\}.
We call a vertex
b_{i}
a concave vertex, if
B_{r}(b_{i})\setminus\Omega^{*}
is convex
for
r>0
sufficiently small.

Let
u
be the globally Lipschitz convex function satisfying (
1.2
). Similarly, by
[
2
]
there exists
a globally Lipschitz convex function
v
satisfying

(Dv)_{\sharp}\chi_{{}_{\Omega^{*}}}=\chi_{{}_{\Omega}},

with
Dv(y)\in\overline{\Omega}
for almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Note that
v^{*}=u
in
\Omega.

By (
1.2
), we have that
u
satisfies
\mbox{det}\,D^{2}u\geq\chi_{{}_{\Omega}}
in the Alexandrov sense. In dimension 2, it is well known that
u
is strictly convex in
\Omega
, see
[
5
]
,
[
23
, Theorem 2.5]
or
[
25
, Lemma 2.3]
for instance.
Note that for any
x\in\Omega
,
\partial^{-}u(x)
is a bounded closed convex set.
Since
v^{*}=u
in
\Omega
, we have

\partial^{-}u(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:v(y)=x\cdot(y-p)+v(p)\}\quad\forall\,p\in\partial^{-}u(x),

namely, on the set
\partial^{-}u(x)
,
v
agrees with an affine function that has gradient
x
.
By the strictly convexity of
u
in
\Omega
, we then have

(3.1)

v\text{ is differentiable at any point }y\in\partial^{-}u(\Omega).

Let

\Omega^{\prime}:=\{x\in\Omega:\partial^{-}u(x)\cap\Omega^{*}\neq\emptyset\}.

We have the following observations:

(i)

The singular set
A\subset\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
and
|\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}|=0
.

From the discussion following
[
17
, Proposition 2]
,
\Omega^{\prime}
is an open set with
|\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}|=0
, and furthermore,
u
is strictly convex and smooth inside
\Omega^{\prime}
. Consequently, we have the singular set
A
defined in (
1.3
) satisfies

A\subset\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}.
∎

(ii)

\forall\,x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
, the set
\partial^{-}u(x)
is a closed convex polygon.

Given any
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
, we have
\partial^{-}u(x)\cap\Omega^{*}=\emptyset
. By the discussion above
[
17
, Proposition 2]
, we have that
\text{ext}(\partial^{-}u(x))\subset\partial\Omega^{*}
, where
\text{ext}(\partial^{-}u(x))
denotes the set of extreme points of
\partial^{-}u(x)
. Since a bounded convex set is the convex hull of its extreme points,
\partial^{-}u(x)
must be a closed convex polygon (which may be a one-dimensional segment). ∎

(iii)

A localisation property, (
3.3
).

Let
x_{0}\in A
. Fix any
p\in\text{ext}(\partial^{-}u(x_{0})).

Suppose
p\in[b_{i_{0}}b_{i_{0}+1}]
for some
i_{0}=1,\cdots,m.

From (
3.1
)
v
is differentiable at
p
, one can choose
r_{0}
small enough such that
\partial^{-}v(B_{r_{0}}(p)\cap\Omega^{*})\subset\Omega
and that

(3.2)

r_{0}<\min\big\{\text{dist}(p,[b_{i}b_{i+1}]):1\leq i\leq m,\ p\notin[b_{i}b_{i+1}]\big\}.

By a compactness argument similar to the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
, there exist positive constants
h_{0}
and
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

(3.3)

S_{h}^{c}[v](y)\subset B_{r_{0}}(p)\quad\forall\,y\in B_{\delta_{0}}(p)\cap\overline{\Omega^{*}},\ \ \forall\,h\leq h_{0}.

Lemma 3.1
.

The function
v
satisfies

\mbox{det}\,D^{2}v=\chi_{{}_{B_{r_{0}}(p)\cap\Omega^{*}}}\quad\text{ in }B_{r_{0}}(p),

in the Alexandrov sense, where
r_{0}
is as in (
3.2
).

Proof.

By the choice of
r_{0}
, we have that
\partial^{-}v(y)\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
for any

y\in B_{r_{0}}(p)\cap\Omega^{*}
. Hence, according to
[
17
, Theorem 3.3]
,

v
is strictly convex and smooth in
B_{r_{0}}(p)\cap\Omega^{*}
, which implies

(3.4)

\mbox{det}\,D^{2}v(y)=1\quad\forall\,y\in B_{r_{0}}(p)\cap\Omega^{*}.

On the other hand, for
y\in B_{r_{0}}(p)\setminus\Omega^{*}
, let
x\in\partial^{-}v(y)\cap\Omega
.
It follows that
y\in\partial^{-}u(x)
. If
x\in\Omega^{\prime}
, since
u
is strictly convex and smooth in
\Omega^{\prime}
,
one has
y=Du(x)\in\Omega^{*}
, contradicting
y\in B_{r_{0}}(p)\setminus\Omega^{*}
. Therefore,
x
must be in
\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
.
This implies that

(3.5)

|\partial^{-}v(B_{r_{0}}(p)\setminus\Omega^{*})|=0.

By combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), it follows that

\mbox{det}\,D^{2}v=\chi_{{}_{B_{r_{0}}(p)\cap\Omega^{*}}}
within
B_{r_{0}}(p)
, in the Alexandrov sense.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
p\in\text{ext}(\partial^{-}u(x_{0}))\cap[b_{i}b_{i+1}]
for some
i\in\{1,\cdots,m\},
then

(3.6)

\partial^{-}u(x_{0})\cap[b_{i}b_{i+1}]\setminus\{p\}=\emptyset.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that there is a point
p^{\prime}\in\partial^{-}u(x_{0})\cap[b_{i}b_{i+1}]\setminus\{p\}.

Let

q:=p+\frac{1}{M}\frac{p^{\prime}-p}{|p^{\prime}-p|}\in\partial^{-}u(x_{0})\cap(b_{i}b_{i+1}),

where
M>0
is a large constant
such that
\frac{1}{M}\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}
and
q\in B_{\delta_{0}}(p)
with
\delta_{0}
as in (
3.3
).

By subtracting an affine function, we may assume that
x_{0}=0
,
v(p)=0
, and
v\geq 0.

Then
\partial^{-}u(0)=\{v=0\}
, and
v=0
on the segment
[pp^{\prime}].

Since
\Omega^{*}
is a polygon and
q\in\partial\Omega^{*},
by (
3.3
) we have that the section
S_{h}^{c}[v](q)
(
h\leq h_{0}
) satisfies the doubling property,
namely,
|\frac{1}{2}S_{h}^{c}[v](q)\cap\Omega^{*}|\geq\frac{1}{4}|S_{h}^{c}[v](q)|,
where the dilation

\frac{1}{2}S_{h}^{c}[v](q)
is with respect to
q.

For
h\leq h_{0},
let

q^{\prime}:=\{q+t(p-q):t>0\}\cap\partial S_{h}^{c}[v](q).

By (
2.1
) we have
v\leq Ch
in
S_{h}^{c}[v](q)
. Since

\partial^{-}u(0)=\{v=0\}
is a convex polygon and
p\in\text{ext}(\partial^{-}u(0)),
it follows that
q^{\prime}\to p
, as
h\to 0.

Let
\ell
be the affine function such that
S_{h}^{c}[v](q)=\{v<\ell\}
.
Since
S_{h}^{c}[v](q)
is balanced with respect to
q,
we have that
p^{\prime}\notin S_{h}^{c}[v](q)
provided
M
is
sufficiently large and
h
is sufficiently small.
Hence,
\ell(p^{\prime})<0<\ell(q^{\prime}),
which implies that
\ell
is monotone increasing in
p^{\prime}q^{\prime}
direction, in particular,

\ell(p)>\ell(q).
It follows that

|v(p)-\ell(p)|>|v(q)-\ell(q)|=h.

Then, by convexity and (
2.1
) again, we have

(3.7)

|v(p)-\ell(p)|>h\approx\sup_{S_{h}^{c}[v](q)}|v-\ell|.

However, on the other hand, by
[
6
, Corollary 2.1]
we have that

|v(p)-\ell(p)|\leq C\frac{|q^{\prime}-p|}{|q-p|}\sup_{S_{h}^{c}[v](q)}|v-\ell|,

which contradicts to (
3.7
) for
h
sufficiently small, since
\frac{|q^{\prime}-p|}{|q-p|}\rightarrow 0
as
h\rightarrow 0.

∎

With the above observations in mind, we now return to our study of the singular set
A
.
First, we divide the singular set
A
into two parts based on whether the set
\partial^{-}u(x)
contains a vertex of
\Omega^{*}
.
Let

\Sigma_{1}:=\{x\in A:b_{i}\in\partial^{-}u(x)\text{ for some }i=1,\cdots,m\},\quad\text{ and }\ \Sigma_{2}:=A\setminus\Sigma_{1}.

Observe that for any
x\in\Sigma_{2}
,
\text{ext}(\partial^{-}u(x))
are located in the relative interior of the edges of
\Omega^{*}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

\Sigma_{1}
consists of finitely many points.

Proof.

Given any
x\in\Sigma_{1}
, from (
3.1
)
v
is differentiable at any
y\in\partial^{-}u(x)
. Hence,
x=Dv(b_{i})
for some
i=1,\cdots,m
. This implies that
\Sigma_{1}
is a finite set with a cardinality no greater than
m
.
∎

Next, we decompose
\Sigma_{2}
into
\Sigma_{2}=\Sigma^{\prime}_{2}\cup\Sigma^{\prime\prime}_{2}
such that

\Sigma^{\prime}_{2}:=\{x\in\Sigma_{2}:\partial^{-}u(x)\text{ touches exactly two open edges of }\Omega^{*}\},\text{ and }\Sigma^{\prime\prime}_{2}=\Sigma_{2}\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}_{2}.

Lemma 3.4
.

\Sigma^{\prime\prime}_{2}
consists of finitely many points.

Proof.

Let
x\in\Sigma^{\prime\prime}_{2}
. By the definition of
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}_{2}
and (
3.6
),
\partial^{-}u(x)
must touch at least three open edges of
\Omega^{*}
, say, at
p_{x}^{1}\in(b_{i_{1}}b_{i_{1}+1})
,
p_{x}^{2}\in(b_{i_{2}}b_{i_{2}+1})
, and
p_{x}^{3}\in(b_{i_{3}}b_{i_{3}+1})
, respectively. Then
v=\ell
for some affine function
\ell
in the open triangle
\triangle_{x}
with vertices
p_{x}^{k}
,
k=1,2,3
. Note that
D\ell=x
.

Let
z
be another point in
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}_{2}
, and suppose
\partial^{-}u(z)
touches the same three open edges of
\Omega^{*}
, at
\bar{p}_{z}^{1}\in(b_{i_{1}}b_{i_{1}+1})
,
\bar{p}_{z}^{2}\in(b_{i_{2}}b_{i_{2}+1})
, and
\bar{p}_{z}^{3}\in(b_{i_{3}}b_{i_{3}+1})
, respectively. Then
v=\ell^{\prime}
for some affine function
\ell^{\prime}
in the open triangle
\triangle^{\prime}_{z}
with vertices
\bar{p}_{z}^{k}
,
k=1,2,3
. Note that
D\ell^{\prime}=z\neq x
.

The planar nature of this problem forces
\triangle^{\prime}_{z}
to intersect
\triangle_{x}
, contradicting the differentiability of
v
in
\triangle^{\prime}_{z}\cap\triangle_{x}
; see (
3.1
). Hence
x=z
.
The above discussion implies that
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}_{2}
is a finite set with a cardinality no greater than
\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)}{6}
.
∎

Figure 3.1.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
x\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{2}
, there exists a small constant
r_{x}
such that
B_{r_{x}}(x)\cap A
is a smooth curve.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{2}
. By a translation of coordinates, we may assume
x_{0}=0
. By the definition of
\Sigma^{\prime}_{2}
,
\partial^{-}u(0)
is a segment with endpoints on two open edges of
\Omega^{*}
, denoted by
(b_{i_{1}}b_{i_{1}+1})
and
(b_{i_{2}}b_{i_{2}+1})
. Let
y_{0}=\partial^{-}u(0)\cap(b_{i_{1}}b_{i_{1}+1})
and
\hat{y}_{0}=\partial^{-}u(0)\cap(b_{i_{2}}b_{i_{2}+1})
; see Figure 3.1.

Denote
V_{1}:=B_{r}(y_{0})\cap\overline{\Omega^{*}}
and
V_{2}:=B_{r}(\hat{y}_{0})\cap\overline{\Omega^{*}}
.
By taking
r>0
sufficiently small, we have that
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are convex and
\text{dist}(V_{1},V_{2})>0
.
Denote
U_{i}=Dv(V_{i})
for
i=1,2
.
From Lemma
3.2
,
v
is strictly convex in
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. It follows that

B_{r_{1}}(0)\subset U:=U_{1}\cup U_{2}

for some sufficiently small
r_{1}>0
.
Denote
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
. We have that
(Du)_{\sharp}\chi_{{}_{U}}=\chi_{{}_{V}}
.
Then, by
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have that
A\cap B_{r_{1}}(0)
is a
C^{1,\beta}
curve for some
\beta\in(0,1)
. To
improve the regularity to
C^{\infty}
, we follow the methods developed in
[
10
,
12
]
. For the reader’s convenience, we sketch the proof below.

Let
u_{i}(x)=\sup_{y\in V_{i}}\{y\cdot x-v(y)\}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
i=1,2
.
Then
u_{i}=u
in
U_{i}
,
i=1,2
. The singular set of
u
in
B_{r_{1}}(0)
is characterised
by

A\cap B_{r_{1}}(0)=\{u_{1}=u_{2}\}\cap B_{r_{1}}(0)=\partial U_{i}\cap B_{r_{1}}(0),\ i=1,2.

It follows that the unit inner normal of
U_{2}
(which is also the unit normal of
A
) at
x\in A\cap B_{r_{1}}(0)
is given by

(3.8)

\nu_{{}_{A}}(x)=\frac{Du_{2}(x)-Du_{1}(x)}{|Du_{2}(x)-Du_{1}(x)|}.

4.
Free boundary in optimal partial transportation

Suppose
\Omega
is a bounded domain and
\Omega^{*}
is a non-convex polygonal domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, separated by a straight line
L
. By a change of coordinates, we may assume that

L=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}=0\},

\Omega\subset\{x_{1}<0\}
, and
\Omega^{*}\subset\{x_{1}>0\}
.
Suppose that the vertices of
\Omega^{*}
are given by
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
i=1,\cdots,m
, and the edges are given by
[b_{i}b_{i+1}]
,
i=1,\cdots,m
, with
b_{m+1}:=b_{1}
.

For a fixed constant
m
satisfying
m<\min\Big\{|\Omega|,\,|\Omega^{*}|\Big\}
, it is shown in
[
7
]
that the optimal transport plan
\gamma
, namely the minimiser of (
1.1
), is characterised by:

\gamma=(\text{Id}\times T)_{\#}\chi_{{}_{U}}=(T^{-1}\times\text{Id})_{\#}\chi_{{}_{V}},

where
T
is the optimal transport map from the active domain
U\subset\Omega
to the active target
V\subset\Omega^{*}
.
Moreover, there exists a globally Lipschitz convex function
v:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
T^{-1}=Dv
, and

(Dv)_{\#}\left(\chi_{{}_{V}}+\chi_{{}_{\Omega\setminus U}}\right)=\chi_{{}_{\Omega}}

with
Dv(y)\in\Omega
for almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and

v(x)=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}+C\quad\text{for }x\in\Omega\setminus U,

C
.

Denote
\bar{u}=v^{*}
, the Legendre transform of
v
. It is well known that
(D\bar{u})_{\#}\chi_{{}_{U}}=\chi_{{}_{V}}
. It is also straightforward to check that

(4.1)

\mbox{det}\,D^{2}\bar{u}\geq 1\quad\text{in}\ \Omega

in the sense of Alexandrov. Furthermore, it is well known that in dimension
2
, if a convex function satisfies inequality (
4.1
), it must be strictly convex in
\Omega
.

It is also proved in
[
7
]
that the free boundary
\mathcal{F}:=\partial U\cap\Omega
is a graph of some semiconvex function (hence a Lipschitz function) defined over the line

.
Moreover, there holds the interior ball property
[

, Corollary 2.4]
, namely, for any
x\in U
, if
\bar{u}
is differentiable at

(4.2)

B_{|x-y|}(x)\cap\Omega^{*}\subset V,\quad\text{ and }\quad B_{|x-y|}(y)\cap\Omega\subset U

.

For any
x\in\mathcal{F}
, note that
\partial^{-}\bar{u}(x)
is a closed convex set.
By a translation of coordinates, we may assume
x=0\in\mathcal{F}
.
By adding a constant, we may also assume that
v\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
v=0
on

\partial^{-}\bar{u}(0)
, and

(4.3)

\bar{u}=v=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}\quad\text{ in }\ \ \Omega\setminus U.

Since
\bar{u}
is strictly convex in
\Omega
, and
\bar{u}=v^{*}
, it follows that

(4.4)

Lemma 4.1
.

\partial^{-}\bar{u}(0)
is a bounded convex polygonal set satisfying
\partial^{-}\bar{u}(0)\cap\big((\Omega\setminus\overline{U})\cup V\big)=\emptyset

\partial^{-}\bar{u}(0)\cap\big((\Omega\setminus\overline{U})\cup V\big)\neq\emptyset

cannot be differentiable. Hence, we obtain

Next, we show that

is bounded. Suppose not, since

, by convexity of

\nu_{\mathcal{F}}(x)=\frac{Du(x)-x}{|Du(x)-x|}.

Then, the
C^{1,\beta}
regularity of
\bar{u}
implies that
\mathcal{F}\cap B_{r}(0)
is
C^{1,\beta}
, noting that
\bar{u}=u
on
U
.

Since
0\in\mathcal{F}_{3}
, by the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{3}
and the strict convexity of
v
in
B_{\delta_{0}}(p_{0})\cap\overline{\Omega^{*}}
, we have that
\partial^{-}\bar{u}(0)\cap\partial V=\{p_{0}\}
.
It follows that

\nu_{{}_{U}}(0)\cdot\nu_{{}_{V}}(p_{0})>0,

where
\nu_{{}_{V}}(p_{0})
is the unit inner normal of
V
at
p_{0}
, and
\nu_{{}_{U}}(0)=\nu_{\mathcal{F}}(0)
is the unit inner normal of
U
at
0
, namely, the obliqueness estimate holds at
0
.

Finally, similar to the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we can apply the arguments of
[
12
, Sections 3 and 4]
to show that

\bar{u}\in C^{\infty}(B_{r}(0)\cap\overline{U})
, which implies that
\mathcal{F}\cap B_{r}(0)
is smooth.
Therefore,
\mathcal{F}
is smooth away from a finite number of points in
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

We remark that in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
,
\Omega^{*}
does not need to be simply connected and may contain a finite number of “polygonal” holes. The proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are also valid for these more general cases. The method described in this paper is also applicable when the edges of the target domain are replaced by smooth convex curves.

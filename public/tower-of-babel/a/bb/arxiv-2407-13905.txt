Quantum statistical effects in one-particle densities: scattering and pair production
X. Gutiérrez de la Cal a,d, M. Pons b,d, A. Matzkin e, D. Sokolovski a,c,d,∗

Abstract
We study space-time resolved densities of particle-hole pairs produced by an external time-dependent field acting on non-interacting non-relativistic particles. It is shown that, at least in some cases, the densities are not affected by Fermi-Dirac or Bose-Einstein statistics, and are determined only by the initial state of the multi-particle system. The second quantisation technique is extended to Dirac electrons and, with some modifications, to Klein-Gordon bosons. The difference in pair production in these two relativistic cases is analysed in some detail.



I
Introduction

In
[
1
]
the authors used quantum field theory in order to evaluate spatially resolved densities
of electron and positron pairs produced by a strong external field. Pair production was found to be reduced
in the presence of an electronic wave packet propagating through the region where positrons are being produced.
In Refs.
[
1
,
2
]
the method was extended to non-interacting Klein-Gordon bosons.

The questions posed in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
can equally be asked in a non-relativistic context, e.g.,
regarding the particle-hole production in semiconducting solids
[
4
]
.
Non-relativistic analysis, relying on the second quantisation technique
[
5
,
6
]

is particularly simple if the interaction between the particles can be neglected. This approximation is often made
in applications such as the theory of Bose-Einstein condensates (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
), or electronic point contacts (see e.g.,
[
8
]
).
If so, to predict the behaviour of a multi-particle system, subjected to an external field, and described by a symmetrised or antisymmetrised wave function,
it is sufficient to know the one-particle transition amplitudes. One-particle densities, which offer only a crude description of the system,
are often unaffected by the type of statistics obeyed by the particles. In this case one needs to consider full counting statistics in order to find a difference
between fermions and bosons.

The purpose of this paper is twofold. Firstly, we will study, in the non-relativistic case, spatially resolved particle and hole densities,
in order to establish whether these are affected by the Pauli exclusion principle. We will demonstrate that the answer must be no,
since the same densities would be produced if the fermions were to be replaced by bosons.

Secondly, we will extend the second quantisation procedure to Dirac electrons and, with a small modification,
to Klein-Gordon bosons. Unlike in the non-relativistic case, pair production rates for relativistic fermions and bosons
are known to be very different
[
2
,
9
]
, and we will study the origin of this discrepancy.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In Sect.II we will specify the condition under which it is meaningful
to consider the probabilities of finding, at a given location, a particle with positive, or negative energy.
In Sect.III we consider several distinguishable non-relativistic particles, each occupying one of the orthogonal states,
and evaluate the
“particle”
and
“hole”
densities once the external field is switched off.
In Sect.IV we replace the particles with non-relativistic fermions or bosons, and look for the difference in
the densities introduced in Sect.III.
In Sect.IV we use the hole theory of positrons to extend the treatment to Dirac electrons in one dimension.
In Sect.V we apply the second quantisation technique to Klein-Gordon bosons, and analyse both the similarities
and differences with the Dirac case.
In Sect.V we illustrate the difference between relativistic fermions and bosons by employing an
exactly solvable toy model.
Section VII contains our conclusions.

II
A
“double slit problem”

Consider a simple quantum system (e.g., a particle in an infinite rectangular well) with a Hamiltonian

\hat{h}_{0}
,
and an energy spectrum
E_{j}
,
\hat{h}_{0}|j{\rangle}=E_{j}|j{\rangle}

j=1,2,...
. Choosing the zero energy to lie between

E_{N}
and
E_{N+1}
divides the spectrum into (n)egative and (p)ositive parts,

E_{n}=E_{j}
for
j\leq N
, and
E_{p}=E_{j}
for
j>N
, as shown in Fig.1.
Using notations
|n{\rangle}
,
n=1,...,N
and
|p{\rangle}
,
p=N+1,N+2,...

for the states corresponding to the negative and positive energies, respectively,
we will omit the limits of summations, and write
\equiv\sum_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}
,

\sum_{p}=\sum_{p=N+1}^{\infty}
, and
\sum_{j}=\sum_{n}+\sum_{p}
.

A particle prepared in a state
|j{\rangle}
(
|p{\rangle}
or
|n{\rangle}
) is subjected, after
t=0
, to an external field
\hat{V}(t)=\sum_{jj^{\prime}}|j{\rangle}V_{jj^{\prime}}(t){\langle}j^{\prime}|
, which causes transitions between
the field-free states
|j{\rangle}
. For the Hamiltonian
\hat{h}(t)=\hat{h}_{0}+\hat{V}(t)
one has

\displaystyle\hat{h}(t)=\sum_{pp^{\prime}}|p{\rangle}h_{pp^{\prime}}(t){\langle}p^{\prime}|+\sum_{pn}|p{\rangle}h_{pn}(t){\langle}n|+\sum_{np}|n{\rangle}h_{np}(t){\langle}p|+\sum_{nn^{\prime}}|n{\rangle}h_{nn^{\prime}}(t){\langle}n^{\prime}|,\quad\quad

(1)

\displaystyle h_{jj^{\prime}}(t)=h^{*}_{j^{\prime}j}(t),\quad j,j^{\prime}=n,p.\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad

Once the field
V
is switched off at a time
t
, what is the likelihood of a particle at
x
, to have, for example, positive energy?

The two properties are represented by projectors
\hat{\pi}_{x}=|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|
and
\hat{\pi}_{+}=\sum_{p}|p{\rangle}{\langle}p|
which do not commute,
and cannot be established simultaneously. It is, however possible to measure one projector immediately after the other,
e.g., by employing a von Neumann pointer
[
10
]
.
With
\pi_{+}
set to be measured first, one has a
“double slit problem”
shown in Fig.
1
.

Figure
1:
The quantum system can reach a final state
|x{\rangle}
from an initial state
|j{\rangle}
by passing, immediately before, through one of the intermediate states,
|n{\rangle}
,
E_{n}<0
, or
|p{\rangle}
,
E_{p}>0
. The corresponding amplitude is
A(x\leftarrow n(p)\leftarrow j)={\langle}x|n(p){\rangle}{\langle}n(p)|\hat{u}(t)|j{\rangle}
. The amplitudes to be in
x
and have a positive (negative) energy just before,
A_{-(+)}(x,j,t)=\sum_{n(p)}=A(x\leftarrow n(p)\leftarrow j)
,are given in Eq.(
2
).

The two amplitudes of the two pathways connecting
|j{\rangle}
at
t=0
with
|x{\rangle}
at
t
are given by

\displaystyle A_{+}(x,j,t)

\displaystyle={\langle}x|\pi_{+}\hat{u}(t)|j{\rangle}=\sum_{p}{\langle}x|p{\rangle}{\langle}p|{\hat{u}}(t)|j{\rangle}={\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{+}|j(t){\rangle},

(2)

\displaystyle A_{-}(x,j,t)

\displaystyle={\langle}x|\pi_{-}\hat{u}(t)|j{\rangle}=\sum_{n}{\langle}x|n{\rangle}{\langle}n|{\hat{u}}(t)|j{\rangle}={\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{-}|j(t){\rangle},

with
|j(t){\rangle}\equiv\hat{u}(t)|j{\rangle}
,
\hat{u}(t)=\exp[-i\int_{0}^{t}\hat{h}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}]
is the evolution operator (a time-orderd product is assumed where
\hat{h}(t^{\prime})
and
\hat{h}(t^{\prime\prime})
do not commute),
and

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{+}\equiv\sum_{p}|p{\rangle}{\langle}p|,\quad\hat{\pi}_{-}\equiv\sum_{n}|n{\rangle}{\langle}n|,\quad\hat{\pi}_{+}+\hat{\pi}_{-}=\hat{1}.

(3)

If no measurement of
\pi_{+}
made,
the probability
of finding the particle at a location
x
is given by

\displaystyle Prob(x\leftarrow j,t)=|A_{+}(x,j,t)+A_{-}(x,j,t)|^{2}\equiv\sum_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}=+,-}{\langle}j(t)|\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(x)|j(t){\rangle},

(4)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(x)=\hat{\pi}_{\mu}\hat{\pi}_{x}\hat{\pi}_{\mu^{\prime}}.

(5)

If a projective measurement of
\hat{\pi}_{+}
is made, and a pointer records a result,
+
or
-
, the interference is destroyed.
The probabilities of two possible outcomes,
(x,+)
and
(x,-)
are given by

\displaystyle Prob(x\leftarrow\mu\leftarrow j,t)=|A_{\mu}(x,j,t)|^{2}=\rho_{\mu\mu}(x,j,t),

(6)

where
\rho_{++}(x,t)
and
\rho_{--}(x,t)
are the probabilities of finding at
x
a particle with positive and negative energy, respectively.
The total probability is conserved,
\int dxProb(x,t)=\sum_{\mu=+,-}\int dxProb(x,\mu,t)
, since the interference terms

\rho_{+-}(x,t)
and
\rho_{-+}(x,t)
integrate to zero,
\int dx\rho_{+-}(x,t)=\int dx\rho_{-+}(x,t)=0
.
With several non-interacting particles present, one can use the probabilities in Eqs.(
4
) to evaluate the energy resolved particle densities, as we will do next.

III
N
distinguishable particles

Consider next
N
non-interacting distinguishable particles, each occupying one of the negative energy states (see Fig.2).

Figure
2:
a) Initial configuration of
N
non-interacting distinguishable or identical particles occupying all negative
states
|n{\rangle}
.
b) A time-dependent external field causes transitions, producing

“particles”
with positive energies, and
“holes”
among the initially filled
negative energy levels.

The Hamiltonian of the system is a sum,

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{dist}=\sum_{n=1}^{n}\hat{h}_{(n)}(t)

(7)

where
\hat{h}_{(n)}
acts on the
n
-th particle’s variable. With the first particle occupying the first of the negative states,
the second the second, and so on, the system’s initial state,
|\Phi^{dist}(0){\rangle}=\prod_{n=1}^{N}|n{\rangle}_{(n)}
, evolves into

\displaystyle|\Phi_{N}^{dist}(t){\rangle}=\prod_{n=1}^{N}|n(t){\rangle}_{(n)}.

(8)

Throughout the paper we will consider a one-particle density (occupation number), defined as the sum of probabilities
of finding each particle in a given state, regardless of where the remaining particles are. The density at
x
,
\rho(x,t)
, can, therefore,
be obtained as

\displaystyle\rho(x,t)=\sum_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}=+,-}{\langle}\Phi_{N}^{dist}(t)|\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(x)|\Phi_{N}^{dist}(t){\rangle}\equiv\sum_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}=+,-}\rho_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(x,t),

(9)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hat{\pi}_{\mu}^{(n)}\hat{\pi}_{x}^{(n)}\hat{\pi}_{\mu^{\prime}}^{(n)}.

(10)

As in Eqs.(
7
) index
(n)
indicates that an operator acts on the variables of the
n
-th particle.
In the following we will assume that the sign of the particle’s energy is made prior to measuring its position,
so that interference terms
\rho_{+-}(x,t)
and
\rho_{-+}(x,t)
can be neglected,
\rho(x,t)=\rho_{++}(x,t)+\rho_{--}(x,t)
,
and the densities of the positive- and negative-energy particles are given by

\displaystyle\rho_{++}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}|{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{+}|n(t){\rangle}|^{2},\quad\rho_{++}(x,0)=0,

(11)

\displaystyle\rho_{--}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}|{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{-}|n(t){\rangle}|^{2},\quad\rho_{--}(x,0)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}|{\langle}x|n{\rangle}|^{2}.

If an extra particle in a superposition of, say, positive energy states,

|\varphi{\rangle}=\sum_{p^{\prime}}C_{p^{\prime}}|p^{\prime}{\rangle}
,
\quad\sum_{p^{\prime}}|C_{p^{\prime}}|^{2}=1,

is added at
t=0
, the state of the system at
t
is given by

|\Phi_{N+1}^{dist}(t){\rangle}=|\varphi(t){\rangle}_{(N+1)}\prod_{n=1}^{N}|n(t){\rangle}_{(n)}
.
where
|\varphi(t){\rangle}=\hat{u}(t)|\varphi{\rangle}
.
The presence of an additional independent particle simply adds to (
11
) an extra term

\displaystyle\rho^{\varphi}_{++}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\rho_{++}(x,t)+|{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{+}|\varphi(t){\rangle}|^{2},\quad\rho^{\varphi}_{++}(x,0)=|{\langle}x|\varphi{\rangle}|^{2},

(12)

\displaystyle\rho^{\varphi}_{--}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\rho_{--}(x,t)+|{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{-}|\varphi(t){\rangle}|^{2},\quad\rho^{\varphi}_{--}(x,0)=\sum_{n}|{\langle}x|n{\rangle}|^{2}.

III.1
“Particles”
and
“holes”

For purely cosmetic purposes, one may refer to the positive energy particles as
“particles”
,
and treat the absence of a particle in a negative state as a
“hole”
.
Defined in this manner, the two densities become

\displaystyle\rho^{\varphi}_{ptcl}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}|{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{+}|n(t){\rangle}|^{2}+|{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{+}|\varphi(t){\rangle}|^{2},

(13)

\displaystyle\rho^{\varphi}_{hole}(x,t)

\displaystyle\equiv\rho^{\varphi}_{--}(x,0)-\rho^{\varphi}_{--}(x,t)=\sum_{n}|{\langle}x|n{\rangle}|^{2}-\sum_{n}|{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{-}|n(t){\rangle}|^{2}-|{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{-}|\varphi(t){\rangle}|^{2},

so that
\rho^{\varphi}_{ptcl}(x,0)=|{\langle}x|\varphi{\rangle}|^{2}
and
\rho^{\varphi}_{hole}(x,0)=0
.
Integration of Eqs.(
13
) yields gives the total number of
“particles”
and
“holes”
at time
t
,

\displaystyle N^{\varphi}_{ptcl}(t)

\displaystyle=\int\rho^{\varphi}_{ptcle}(x,t)dx=\sum_{p,n}|{\langle}p|n(t){\rangle}|^{2}+|{\langle}p|\varphi(t){\rangle}|^{2}

(14)

\displaystyle N^{\varphi}_{hole}(t)

\displaystyle=\int\rho^{\varphi}_{hole}(x,t)dx=N-\sum_{n^{\prime},n}|{\langle}n^{\prime}|n(t){\rangle}|^{2}-|{\langle}n|\varphi(t){\rangle}|^{2}

It is readily seen that at any time the number of
“particles”
exceeds the number of
“holes”
by one,
N_{ptcl}(t)=N_{hole}(t)+1
.
Equations (
14
) have a simple interpretation. Scattering of the first
N
particles into positive energy states creates both
“particles”

and
“holes”
. Scattering of the extra particle into a negative state reduces the number of
“holes”
, hence the minus before the last term
in the second of Eqs.(
14
). Absolute square of the matrix element of the evolution operator,

|{\langle}n|\varphi(t){\rangle}|^{2}=|{\langle}n|\hat{u}(t)|\varphi{\rangle}|^{2}
gives the corresponding probabilty.

III.2
Useful inequalities

We note further that, with the chosen initial configuration (one particle per each negative state plus one more, a wave packet, built from positive states), the population of a positive energy level,

\displaystyle N^{\varphi}_{ptcl}(p,t)\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{N+1}{\langle}\Phi_{N+1}^{dist}(t)|\hat{\pi}^{(n)}_{p}|\Phi_{N+1}^{dist}(t){\rangle}=\sum_{n}|{\langle}p|n(t){\rangle}|^{2}-|{\langle}p|\varphi(t){\rangle}|^{2},\quad\hat{\pi}_{p}\equiv|p{\rangle}{\langle}p|,\quad\quad

(15)

cannot exceed unity.
Indeed, one can introduce a new complete orthonormal set,
|\varphi_{p}{\rangle}
, spanning the positive sub-space and choose
|\varphi_{N+1}{\rangle}=|\varphi{\rangle}
.
Time-evolved states
|n(t){\rangle}=\hat{u}(t)|n{\rangle}
and
|\varphi_{p}(t){\rangle}=\hat{u}(t)|\varphi_{p}{\rangle}
now form a complete basis in the entire
Hilbert space,
\sum_{n}|n(t){\rangle}{\langle}n(t)|+\sum_{p}|\varphi_{p}(t){\rangle}{\langle}\varphi_{p}(t)|=\hat{\mathds{1}}
. Writing

\displaystyle N^{\varphi}_{ptcl}(p,t)=\sum_{n}|{\langle}n(t)|p{\rangle}|^{2}+|{\langle}\varphi(t)|p{\rangle}|^{2}\leq\sum_{n}|{\langle}n(t)|p{\rangle}|^{2}+\sum_{p}|{\langle}\varphi_{p}(t)|p{\rangle}|^{2}={\langle}p|p{\rangle}=1\quad\quad

(16)

yields the desired result.
A similar argument shows that a
“hole”
occupation number

\displaystyle N^{\varphi}_{hole}(n,t)\equiv 1-\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{N+1}{\langle}\Phi_{N+1}^{dist}(t)|\hat{\pi}^{(n^{\prime})}_{n}|\Phi_{N+1}^{dist}(t){\rangle}=1-\sum_{n^{\prime}}|{\langle}n^{\prime}(t)|n{\rangle}|^{2}-|{\langle}\varphi(t)|p{\rangle}|^{2},

(17)

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{n}\equiv|n{\rangle}{\langle}n|,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad

in the second of Eqs.(
14
)
always has a value between
0
and
1
.

Next we see if anything would change in the case of identical particles obeying Fermi-Dirac or Bose-Einstein statistics.

IV
Non-relativistic fermions and bosons

By Eq.(
91
) of Appendix A, second-quantised versions of the Hamiltonian (
1
) and the density operators (

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{B,F}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{pp^{\prime}}h_{pp^{\prime}}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(p){\hat{a}}(p^{\prime})+\sum_{pn}h_{pn}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(p){\hat{a}}(n)+\sum_{np}h_{np}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(n){\hat{a}}(p)+\sum_{nn^{\prime}}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(n){\hat{a}}(n^{\prime}),

(18)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{++}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{pp^{\prime}}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(p){\hat{a}}(p^{\prime}){\langle}p|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|p^{\prime}{\rangle},\quad\hat{\rho}_{+-}(x)=\sum_{pn}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(p){\hat{a}}(n){\langle}p|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|n{\rangle}

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{-+}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{np}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(n){\hat{a}}(p){\langle}n|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|p{\rangle},\quad\hat{\rho}_{--}(x)=\sum_{nn^{\prime}}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(n){\hat{a}}(n^{\prime}){\langle}n|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|n^{\prime}{\rangle},

where the operators annihilating and creating a particle in a state

\displaystyle[{\hat{a}}(j),{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(j^{\prime})]_{\xi}={\hat{a}}(j){\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(j^{\prime})-\xi{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(j^{\prime}){\hat{a}}(j)=\delta_{jj^{\prime}},\quad[{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(j),{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(j^{\prime})]_{\xi}=[{\hat{a}}(j),{\hat{a}}(j^{\prime})]_{\xi}=0,\quad

(19)

with
\xi=1
for bosons (B) , and
\xi=-1
for fermions (F) (below we will write simply
[...]_{\pm}
for

\displaystyle|\Phi^{B,F}(0){\rangle}=\prod_{n=1}^{N}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(n)|\text{vac}{\rangle},

where

{\hat{a}}(j)|\text{vac}{\rangle}=0

\displaystyle\rho_{\mu\mu}(x)={\langle}\Phi^{B,F}(t)|\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu}(x)|\Phi^{B,F}(t){\rangle}={\langle}\Phi^{B,F}(0)|\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu}(x|t)|\Phi^{B,F}(0){\rangle}.

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(x|t)=\exp\left[i\int_{0}^{t}\hat{H}^{B,F}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right]\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(x)\exp\left[-i\int_{0}^{t}\hat{H}^{B,F}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right].

IV.1
Two kinds of time-dependent operators

The time-evolved state can be obtained by first evolving the product state [cf. (
8
)], and then symmetrising the result.
This yields

\displaystyle|\Phi^{B,F}(t){\rangle}=\prod_{n=1}^{N}{\hat{a}}^{+}(n(t))|\text{vac}{\rangle},

(23)

where the newly introduced operators

{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(j(t))
,
j=p,n
, create a particle in a time-evolved state

|j(t){\rangle}=\hat{u}(t)|j{\rangle}
. They obey the same commutation rules
[
6
]
, since the evolution of one-particle states is unitary,
and
|j(t){\rangle}
also form a complete orthonormal basis,

\displaystyle[{\hat{a}}(j(t)),{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(j^{\prime}(t))]_{\xi}={\langle}j(t)|j^{\prime}(t){\rangle}={\langle}j|j^{\prime}{\rangle}=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}.

(24)

Since
|j(t){\rangle}=\sum_{j^{\prime}}|j^{\prime}{\rangle}{\langle}j^{\prime}|\hat{u}(t)|j{\rangle}
, one finds [ cf. (
89
) of Appendix A]

\displaystyle{\hat{a}}(j(t))=\sum_{j^{\prime}}{\langle}j^{\prime}|\hat{u}(t)|j{\rangle}^{*}{\hat{a}}(j^{\prime})=\sum_{j^{\prime}}{\langle}j|\hat{u}(-t)|j^{\prime}{\rangle}{\hat{a}}(j^{\prime}).

(25)

This can be inverted
to yield an expansion

\displaystyle{\hat{a}}(j)=\sum_{j^{\prime}}{{\langle}j^{\prime}|}\hat{u}(-t)|j{\rangle}^{*}{\hat{a}}(j^{\prime}(t))=\sum_{j^{\prime}}{\langle}j|\hat{u}(t)|j^{\prime}{\rangle}{\hat{a}}(j^{\prime}(t)),

(26)

more useful for our purposes.
We note that that
{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(j(t))
should not be confused with the Heisenberg operators

which satisfy

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\hat{a}}(j|t)=i[\hat{H}^{B,F}(t),{\hat{a}}(j|t)]_{+},

(27)

and obey the same commutation relations (

Time dependent operators of both types will be useful in what follows.

IV.2
“Particle-hole”
production

Using the first equality in Eq.(
21
), the time-evolved state (
23
), and expressing
{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(j)
in terms of
{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(j(t))
with the help of Eq.(

\displaystyle\rho_{ptcl}(x,t)

\displaystyle={\langle}\Phi^{B,F}(t)|\hat{\rho}^{B,F}_{++}(x)|\Phi^{B,F}(t){\rangle}=\sum_{n}|{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{+}|n(t){\rangle}|^{2}

(28)

\displaystyle\rho_{hole}(x,t)

\displaystyle={\langle}\Phi^{B,F}(0)|\hat{\rho}^{B,F}_{--}(x)|\Phi^{B,F}(0){\rangle}-{\langle}\Phi^{B,F}(t)|\hat{\rho}^{B,F}_{--}(x)|\Phi^{B,F}(t){\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\left[|{\langle}x|n{\rangle}|^{2}-|{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{-}|n(t){\rangle}|^{2}\right].

Equations (

The search for statistical effects in one-particle densities must continue elsewhere.

IV.3
“Particle-hole”
production with an extra
“particle”
present

One may expect that adding at
t=0
an extra particle in a state
|\varphi{\rangle}=\sum_{p^{\prime}}C_{p^{\prime}}|p^{\prime}{\rangle}
might affect the

“particle-hole”
production for bosons and fermions differently.
Indeed, a hand waving argument suggests that with some of the
“particle”
states
already occupied, fermions would be less likely to leave negative energy states
due to Pauli principle. For bosons, with their tendency to
“bunch together”

[
11
]
, the
transitions could be enhanced by the presence of the additional
“particle”
.
It is, however, easy to demonstrate that this is not the case.

The state of the
(N+1)-
particle system at
t
is now given by

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\varphi}^{B,F}(t){\rangle}={\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(\varphi(t))\prod_{n=1}^{N}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(n(t))|\text{vac}{\rangle}={\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(\varphi(t))|\Phi^{B,F}(t){\rangle},

(29)

where
{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(\varphi(t))
creates a particle in a state
|\varphi(t){\rangle}=\hat{u}(t)|\varphi{\rangle}=\sum_{p}C_{p}|p(t){\rangle}
.
To analyse the effect of adding an extra
“particle”
it is
convenient to rewrite operators
\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu}(x)
in Eqs.(
18
) in an equivalent form,

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu}(x)={\hat{a}}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(x){\hat{a}}_{\mu}(x),\quad{\hat{a}}_{+}(x)=\sum_{p}{\langle}x|p{\rangle}{\hat{a}}(p),\quad{\hat{a}}_{-}(x)=\sum_{n}{\langle}x|n{\rangle}{\hat{a}}(n).

(30)

For the
“particle”
density we, therefore, have

\displaystyle\rho^{\varphi}_{ptcl}(x,t)={\langle}\Phi^{B,F}(t)|{\hat{a}}(\varphi(t)){\hat{a}}^{\dagger}_{+}(x){\hat{a}}_{+}(x){\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(\varphi(t))|\Phi^{B,F}(t){\rangle},\quad\quad\quad\quad

(31)

where, [cf. Eq.(
30
)]

\displaystyle{\hat{a}}(\varphi(t)){\hat{a}}^{\dagger}_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=\xi{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}_{+}(x){\hat{a}}(\varphi(t))+{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{+}|\varphi(t){\rangle}^{*}

V.2
Electron-positron production with an extra electron present

With all prerequisites in place, one can simply repeat the calculation of the previous Section,

VI
Klein-Gordon bosons in one dimension

A similar treatment can be extended to spinless bosons, albeit only up to a point.
In its Hamiltonian form

14

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}|\psi{\rangle}=\hat{h}^{KG}|\psi{\rangle}=[\hat{h}^{KG}_{0}+\hat{V}(x,t)]|\psi{\rangle}.

As in the case of Dirac electrons, the spectrum of the field-free Hamiltonian consists of the positive and negative parts [cf. Eq.(

\displaystyle\sum_{k}|\phi^{+}_{k}{\rangle}{\langle}\phi^{+}_{k}|-\sum_{k}|\phi^{-}_{k}{\rangle}{\langle}\phi^{-}_{k}|\equiv\hat{\pi}_{+}-\hat{\pi}_{-}=\hat{\mathds{1}}.

(58)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{KG}(x)=\sum_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}\lambda_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}\hat{\rho}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(x)=\sum_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\lambda_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}\sum_{kk^{\prime}}|\phi^{\mu}_{k}{\rangle}{\langle}v_{k}^{\mu}|v_{k^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime}}{\rangle}{\langle}k|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|k^{\prime}{\rangle}{\langle}\phi^{\mu^{\prime}}_{k^{\prime}}|

Second quantisation

As in the case of Dirac electrons, the second-quantised versions of the Hamiltonian (

in Eq.(

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{KG}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}\lambda_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}h^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}_{kk^{\prime}}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(\phi^{\mu}_{k}){\hat{a}}(\phi^{\mu^{\prime}}_{k^{\prime}}),

VIII
Conclusions and discussion

We started, in a non-relativistic case, with a number of non-interacting particles.
In the chosen basis, each particle occupies one of the orthogonal states, while the rest of the states are left empty.
An external time-dependent field, switched on for a period of time, causes transitions between the states,
thus altering the initial configuration.

One can consider a projective measurement onto a subspace spanned by the initially occupied states, followed by a position measurement of the particle’s position.
The subspace can be chosen to include all negative energy states (see Fig.
2
a). The first projective measurement eliminates the interference term,
and one obtains, separately, local densities of particles with positive and negative energies.
Treating the particle appearing in a previously unoccupied state as a
“particle”
, and the deficit of particles in a formerly occupied state
as a
“hole”
, one defines spatially resolved
“particle”
and
“hole”
densities,
\rho_{ptcle}(x,t)
and
\rho_{hole}(x,t)

both zero in the initial configuration,
\rho_{ptcle}(x,0)=\rho_{hole}(x,0)=0
[cf. Eq.(
13
)].

The just described one-particle-per-state initial configuration can be created for distinguishable particles as well as for non-relativistic fermions and bosons.
It was our purpose to establish whether
\rho_{ptcle}(x,t)
and
\rho_{hole}(x,t)
should be different for each particle type.
Simple calculations of Sects. III and IV demonstrated that in all three cases the two densities have the same form, affected neither by Pauli exclusion principle, or by
Bose-Einstein statistics.

One can hope that quantum statistical effects would become important, provided an extra
“particle”
in a superposition
in otherwise unoccupied (positive energy) states is added to the initial configuration. Indeed, with some of the target states occupied, one can expect the transitions (and, therefore, production of holes)
to be quenched for fermions, and enhanced for bosons, since the latter are known for their tendency to
“bunch”
together (see e.g.
[
11
]
).
Again a simple calculation shows this expectation to be wrong, as the densities for all types of particles remain identical [cf. Eqs.(
34
)].
The number of
“holes”
does decrease in the presence of an extra
“particle”
, but only because the particle can be scattered and fill a
“hole”
,
as would be the case regardless of the type of particles involved.

As an aside, we obtained a proof that in all cases
“particle”
and
“hole”
occupation numbers cannot exceed
1
[cf. Eqs.(
16
) and (
17
)].
Although for fermions it looks like a consequence of Pauli principle, it is in fact a property of the chosen initial configuration, and has little
to do with quantum statistics.

The analysis can be extended to Dirac electrons (at least in one spatial dimension) with minor modifications,
such as doubling the dimension of the Hilbert space in order to include an infinite number of negative energy states
[
12
]
,
and filling the Fermi sea by acting with operators creating particles in the negative energy states upon the vacuum state
of the second quantised theory
[
14
]
.
With the ground state created in this manner, a
“hole”
can be taken to represent a positron, and casting Eqs.(
46
) into a symmetric form (
47
) allows one
any explicit reference to the Fermi sea. Still it is difficult to see Eqs.(
46
) and (
47
) as a consequence of the Pauli principle.
Indeed, filling the negative states (
39
) with one boson per state should yield the same result for the densities.
[We are far from suggesting that such a bosonic state is physically meaningful, yet it is allowed by the theory, and can in principle be discussed.]

Klein-Gordon bosons, studied here also in one dimension, is a case apart.
The reason is the negative sign acquired by the scalar product of a negative energy state with itself (for Dirac electrons the sign was a plus).
Second quantisation of the Klein-Gordon theory still can be carried out, and renaming of creation and annihilation operators for the negative
states restores the usual bosonic commutation relations.
However, the ground state containing neither bosons, nor antibosons, cannot be constructed by acting with creation
operators on the vacuum state of the second-quantised theory, as was possible in the case of Dirac electrons.
Rather, such a state must be introduced independently, as was done in Eq.(
64
). With the theory thus complete,
spatially resolved boson and antiboson densities can be expressed in terms of solutions of the one-particle
Klein-Gordon equation. Despite the departure from the standard second quantisation procedure, the resulting
expressions for the densities in Eqs.(
71
) are remarkably similar to those obtained for the Dirac electrons in Eqs.(
47
).

Notwithstanding this formal similarity, the physical behaviour of relativistic fermions and bosons is very different, as demonstrated by the two-level example
of Sect. VII. This can be traced back to the already mention negative scalar product of the one-particle Klein-Gordon states.
To evaluate many-body densities one needs to evaluate projections of a time-evolved one-particle state onto
the positive and negative eigenstates of the field-free Hamiltonian.
In the case of Dirac electrons these projections satisfy a system of linear equations with a Hermitian matrix, since in Dirac theory the number of particles is conserved.
For Klein-Gordon bosons, the matrix is no longer Hermitian, since the theory conserves the total charge.
As a result bosonic densities and numbers can exhibit unlimited growth, shown in Fig.
6
b.

Finally, our failure to find significant statistical effects in the behaviour of identical particles can be blamed on the chosen level of description.
The densities, studied here, offer only a crude description of a many-body system. They can be refined by considering instead full-counting statistics,
more likely to be affected by fermionic or bosonic nature of the particles.

\displaystyle\sum_{k}{\langle}\phi^{-}_{k}|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|\phi^{-}_{k}{\rangle}-\sum_{k}{\langle}\phi^{+}_{k}(t)|\hat{\pi}_{-}|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{-}|\phi^{+}_{k}(t){\rangle}.

Inserting (

Both equations can be derived by using the Heisenberg equations of motion, (

VIII.1
Electron/boson density

From Eqs.(
53
) and (

), the density for the electrons/bosons is

\displaystyle\rho_{el,bos}^{\varphi}(x,t)={\langle}\phi^{D,KG}_{\varphi}(0)|\sum_{kk^{\prime}}{\langle}\phi_{k}^{+}|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{+}{\rangle}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(\phi_{k}^{+}|t){\hat{a}}(\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{+}|t)|\Phi^{D,KG}_{\varphi}(0){\rangle},

(97)

with

\displaystyle{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(\phi_{k}^{+}|t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}}\left[u_{kk^{\prime}}^{++}(t)^{*}{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{+})-\xi\hat{u}_{kk^{\prime}}^{+-}(t)^{*}{\hat{b}}(\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{-})\right],

(98)

\displaystyle{\hat{a}}(\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{+}|t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime\prime}}\left[u_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}^{++}(t){\hat{a}}(\phi_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{+})-\xi\hat{u}_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}^{+-}(t){\hat{b}}^{\dagger}(\phi_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{-})\right],

where
u_{kk^{\prime}}^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(t)\equiv{\langle}\phi_{k}^{\mu}|\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime}}(t){\rangle}
,
\xi=\pm 1
for bosons and fermions,
and the initial state is
|\Phi_{\varphi}^{D,KG}(0){\rangle}={\hat{a}}^{\dagger}(\varphi)|\text{Vac}{\rangle}
. The only non-zero contributions, therefore, are

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\langle}{\text{Vac}}|\hat{a}(\varphi)\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{+})\hat{a}(\phi_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{+})\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\varphi)|\text{Vac}{\rangle}=

(99)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\langle}\text{Vac}|\left[{\langle}{\varphi|\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{+}}{\rangle}+\xi\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\phi_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{+})\hat{a}(\varphi)\right]\left[{\langle}{\phi_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{+}|\varphi}{\rangle}+\xi\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\varphi)\hat{a}(\phi_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{+})\right]|\text{Vac}{\rangle}=\braket{\varphi|\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{+}}\braket{\phi_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{+}|\varphi},

and

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\langle}\text{Vac}|\hat{a}(\varphi)\hat{b}(\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{-})\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\phi_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{-})\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\varphi)|\text{Vac}{\rangle}=

(100)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\langle}\text{Vac}|\hat{a}(\varphi)\left[\delta_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}+\xi\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\phi_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{-})\hat{b}(\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{-})\right]\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\varphi)|\text{Vac}{\rangle}=\delta_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}},

where we have used that
\xi^{2}=1
.

Inserting (

\displaystyle\rho_{el,bos}^{\varphi}(x,t)=\sum_{k}{\langle}\phi_{k}^{-}(t)|\hat{\pi}_{+}|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{+}|\phi_{k}^{-}(t){\rangle}+{\langle}\varphi(t)|\hat{\pi}_{+}|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|\hat{\pi}_{+}|\varphi(t){\rangle}.

(101)

From Eqs.(52) and (67), the density for the positrons/antibosons is

\displaystyle{\hat{b}}^{\dagger}(\phi_{k}^{-}|t)

Appendix D. One-particle states for free relativistic fermions and bosons

For free particles, the sums over
k
should be replaced by integrals
\sum_{k}\to\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk
.
One can introduce a coordinate basis
|x,i{\rangle}
,
i=1,2
spanning the entire Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}{{}_{2}}
,

\displaystyle{\langle}x,1|x^{\prime},1{\rangle}=\delta(x-x^{\prime}),\quad{\langle}x,1|x^{\prime},2{\rangle}={\langle}x,2|x^{\prime},1{\rangle}=0,\quad{\langle}x,2|x^{\prime},2{\rangle}=\pm\delta(x-x^{\prime}),

(104)

where the upper and lower signs are for fermions and bosons respectively.
Any state
|\psi{\rangle}=\int[|x,1{\rangle}{\langle}x,1|\psi{\rangle}\pm|x,2{\rangle}{\langle}x,2|\psi{\rangle}]dx
can be represented by a two-component wave function with
coefficients
{\langle}x,1|\psi{\rangle}
and
{\langle}x,2|\psi{\rangle}
.
For free one-particle Dirac states one has
[
14
]

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{\langle}{x,1|\phi_{k}^{\pm}}{\rangle}\\
{\langle}{x,2|\phi_{k}^{\pm}}{\rangle}\end{pmatrix}=

\displaystyle N_{k}^{F\pm}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\frac{k}{\pm E_{k}+m}\end{pmatrix}e^{ikx},

(105)

where
N_{k}^{F\pm}=\sqrt{\frac{\pm E_{k}+m}{\pm 4\pi E_{k}}}
,
and
{\langle}{\phi_{k}^{\mu}|\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime}}}{\rangle}=\int dx\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\langle}{\phi_{k}^{\mu}|x,i}{\rangle}{\langle}{x,i|\Phi_{k^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime}}}{\rangle}=\delta(k-k^{\prime})\delta_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}
.

Free one-particle Klein-Gordon states in the Feschbach-Villars representation are given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{\langle}{x,1|\phi_{k}^{\pm}}{\rangle}\\
{\langle}{x,2|\phi_{k}^{\pm}}{\rangle}\end{pmatrix}=

\displaystyle N_{k}^{B}\begin{pmatrix}m\pm E_{k}\\
m\mp E_{k}\end{pmatrix}e^{ikx},

(106)

where
N_{k}^{B}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi E_{k}m}}
, and

{\langle}{\phi_{k}^{\mu}|\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime}}}{\rangle}=\int dx\left[{\langle}{\phi_{k}^{\mu}|x,1}{\rangle}{\langle}{x,1|\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime}}}{\rangle}-{\langle}{\phi_{k}^{\mu}|x,2}{\rangle}{\langle}{x,2|\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime}}}{\rangle}\right]=\lambda_{\mu}\delta(k-k^{\prime})\delta_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}
, with
\lambda_{\pm}=\pm 1
.

With the help of Eqs.(
105
) and (
106
) one can evaluate quantities like

\displaystyle{\langle}\phi^{\mu}_{k}|x{\rangle}{\langle}x|\phi^{\mu^{\prime}}_{k^{\prime}}{\rangle}={\langle}\phi^{\mu}_{k}|x,1{\rangle}{\langle}x,1|{\langle}x|\phi^{\mu^{\prime}}_{k^{\prime}}{\rangle}\pm{\langle}\phi^{\mu}_{k}|x,2{\rangle}{\langle}x,2|{\langle}x|\phi^{\mu^{\prime}}_{k^{\prime}}{\rangle},

(107)

where, as before, upper sign is for fermions, and lower sign is for bosons.

\displaystyle\rho^{\varphi}_{pos(abos)}(x,t)

Multiplying Eq.(
109

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\underline{\psi}^{+}(t)\\

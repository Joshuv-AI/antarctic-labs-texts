Abstract
. The class of coarsely convex spaces is a coarse geometric analogue of the class of nonpositively curved Riemannian manifolds. It includes Gromov hyperbolic spaces, CAT(0) spaces, proper injective metric spaces and systolic complexes. It is well known that quasi-isometric embeddings of Gromov hyperbolic spaces induce topological embeddings of their boundaries. Dydak and Virk studied maps between Gromov hyperbolic spaces which induce continuous maps between their boundaries. In this paper, we generalize their work to maps between coarsely convex spaces.

1.
Introduction

The class of coarsely convex spaces is
introduced by the second author and Shin-ichi Oguni
[
7
]
.
This class includes Gromov hyperbolic spaces, and
Busemann nonpositively curved spaces, especially, CAT(0) spaces.
Moreover, it is shown that systolic complexes and proper injective
metric spaces are coarsely convex spaces, by Osajda and
Przytycki
[
12
]
, respectively, by Descombes and
Lang
[
3
]
.
Recently,
Chalopin, Chepoi, Genevois, Hirai and Osajda
[
2
]

showed that Helly graphs and coarsely Helly spaces are
coarsely dense in their injective hulls,
especially, they are coarsely convex.
This result may become a rich source of coarsely convex
spaces. In fact, Osajda and Huang
[
11
]

showed that Artin groups of type FC and
Garside groups act geometrically on Helly graphs, hence they do
on coarsely convex spaces.
Haettel, Hoda and Petyt
[
8
]

showed that any
hierarchically hyperbolic space admits a new metric that is coarsely
Helly, so it is coarsely convex.

Let
X
be a proper coarsely convex space. In
[
7
]
, the authors
constructed the ideal boundary
\partial X
of
X
, and they showed that
the open cone
\mathcal{O}\partial X
is coarsely homotopy equivalent to
X

via the “exponential map” and the “logarithmic map”. As a corollary,
it follows that the coarse Baum-Connes conjecture holds for coarsely
convex spaces. In this paper, we consider maps between coarsely convex spaces which induce continuous maps between their boundaries.

Dydak and Virk
[
4
]

introduced the class of visual maps between Gromov hyperbolic spaces
that induce continuous maps between their boundaries. They also introduced its subclass, called radial maps, and showed that these maps
induce Hölder maps between boundaries.

Then they showed that radial coarsely
n
-to-one maps between proper
geodesic Gromov hyperbolic spaces induces continuous
n
-to-one maps
between boundaries. As a
consequence, they obtained dimension rising theorem for radial
maps, with respect to the topological dimensions of the Gromov
boundaries, and to the asymptotic dimensions of hyperbolic groups.

A metric space
(X,d)
is coarsely convex if

X
has a family of quasi-geodesics
{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}
,
called the
system of good quasi-geodesics
,
satisfying some conditions.
Especially, along quasi-geodesics in
{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}
, the metric
d

satisfies
coarsely convex
inequality
(Definition
2.1
(
ii
)
q
).

In this paper, we introduce the class of visual maps between coarsely convex spaces, that do induce continuous maps between
their ideal boundaries (Proposition
3.5
and Corollary
3.7
).

We present a fixed point property of the maps induced by the visual isometries. Let
M
be a metric space and

f\colon M\to M
be an isometry. We say that
f
is
elliptic

if the set
\{f^{n}(x)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded for some

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be an isometry. We suppose that

either

Let

and

be coarsely convex spaces with systems of
good quasi-geodesics
{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}
and
{{}_{Y}\mathcal{L}}

We also introduce the class of

{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}

{{}_{Y}\mathcal{L}}
-equivariant maps,
which is in fact a subclass of visual maps (Theorem

The following is a generalization of the large scale dimension raising
theorem for hyperbolic spaces by Dydak and Virk
[

Let

We also study extension problems.

2.1.
Coarse map

Let
X,Y
be metric spaces. Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map.

(1)

The map
f
is
bornologous

if there exists a non-decreasing function

\psi\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, we have

\,\overline{f(x),f(x^{\prime})}\,\leq\psi(\,\overline{x,x^{\prime}}\,).

Especially, for constants
A\geq 1,\,B\geq 0
,

f
is a
(A,B)
-large scale Lipschitz
map if
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, we have
\,\overline{f(x),f(x^{\prime})}\,\leq A\,\overline{x,x^{\prime}}\,+B
.

(2)

The map
f
is
metrically proper
if for each bounded subset

B\subset Y
, the inverse image
f^{-1}(B)
is bounded.

(3)

The map
f
is
coarse
if it is bornologous and
metrically proper.

(4)

The map
f
is a
coarse embedding
if it is bornologous and there exists
a non-decreasing function
\psi^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

\psi^{\prime}(t)\to\infty\,(t\to\infty)
and for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, we have

\psi^{\prime}(\,\overline{x,x^{\prime}}\,)\leq\,\overline{f(x),f(x^{\prime})}\,.

(5)

The map
f
is
coarsely surjective
if there exists
C\geq 0

such that
C
-neighbourhood of
f(X)
is
Y
.

(6)

The map
f
is a
coarse equivalence map
if it is
a coarse embedding and coarsely surjective.

For more details on the above notions, we refer
[
13
]
.

2.2.
Coarse homotopy

Let
f,g\colon X\to Y
be coarse maps between metric
spaces. The maps
f
and
g
are

coarsely homotopic
if there exists a metric subspace

Z=\{(x,t):0\leq t\leq T_{x}\}

of
X\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and a coarse map

h\colon Z\to Y
, such that

(1)

the map
X\ni x\mapsto T_{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is bornologous,

(2)

h(x,0)=f(x)
, and

(3)

h(x,T_{x})=g(x)
.

Here we equip
X\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with the
l_{1}
-metric, that is,

d_{X\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}((x,t),(y,s)):=\,\overline{x,y}\,+\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert
for

(x,t),(y,s)\in X\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

(i)

Definition 3.1

Lemma 3.2

3.2.
Maps compatible with systems of good quasi-geodesics

Let
\tau\geq 0
be a constant.
We say that a map
\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a
\tau
-
rough contraction

if
\sigma(0)=0
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\sigma(t)=\infty

\displaystyle\rho(t)\leq t\quad(\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}),

\displaystyle\left\lvert\rho(t)-\rho(s)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert+\tau\quad(\forall t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}),

\displaystyle t\leq s\Rightarrow\rho(t)\leq\rho(s)\quad(\forall t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},).

We say that
\sigma
is a
rough contraction
if it is
\tau
-rough contraction for some

\tau\geq 0
.

Dydak and Virk

introduced a class of radial function,
in the setting of Gromov hyperbolic spaces. The following is its analogue in the setting of
coarsely convex spaces. We remark that the following is slightly weaker than the one defined by

Definition 3.8

is
{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}
-radial
if it is

\sigma
-
{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}_{a}
-radial for some
\sigma
and

.

The above condition is in fact equivalent to the following.
Let
\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a rough contraction.
We say that a map

is

\sigma
-
{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}_{a}
-radial
if
for all
x\in X
, there exists
\gamma_{x}\in{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}_{a}
and

such that

\displaystyle\sigma(T_{x})\leq\,\overline{f(x),f(a)}\,.

\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

Let
\gamma\in{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}_{a}

Since

\sigma

7.
Radial extension

In this section, we will show that continuous maps between
the boundaries of coarsely convex spaces extend to visual maps
between those coarsely convex spaces. The idea is the following. Let

X
and
Y
be coarsely convex spaces and let

f\colon\partial X\to\partial Y
be a continuous map.
We first construct a map

\mathcal{O}f\colon\mathcal{O}\partial X\to\mathcal{O}\partial Y
, where

\mathcal{O}\partial X
and
\mathcal{O}\partial Y
are open cones over
\partial X
,
and
\partial Y
, respectively.
Then the extension might be obtained by a composition of
the “logarithmic map”

\log^{\epsilon}\colon X\to\mathcal{O}\partial X
,
\mathcal{O}f
,
and the “exponential map”
\exp_{\epsilon}\colon\mathcal{O}\partial Y\to Y
.
However, in general, the map
\log^{\epsilon}
is defined only on the subset of

X
, and the composite
\exp_{\epsilon}\circ\mathcal{O}f
is not necessarily
bornologous. So we need some modifications.

7.1.
Open cone

Let
Z
be a compact metrizable space.
The
open cone
over
Z
, denoted by
\mathcal{O}Z
, is
the quotient
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times Z/(\{0\}\times Z)
.
For
(t,z)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times Z
,
we denote by
tz
the point in
\mathcal{O}Z
represented by
(t,z)
.

Let
d_{Z}
be a metric on
Z
. We assume that the diameter of
Z
is at most 2.
We define a metric
d_{\mathcal{O}Z}
on
\mathcal{O}Z
by

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{O}Z}(tx,sy):=\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert+\min\{t,s\}d_{Z}(x,y).

The function
d_{\mathcal{O}Z}
does satisfy the triangle inequality.
See
[
14
]
for details.
We call
d_{\mathcal{O}Z}
the induced metric by
d_{Z}
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
Z
and
W
be compact metric spaces and let
f\colon Z\to W

be a continuous map. We define the map

\mathcal{O}f\colon\mathcal{O}Z\to\mathcal{O}W
by

\mathcal{O}f(tz):=tf(z)
.

We remark that
\mathcal{O}f
is proper, but not necessarily bornologous.
However, by composing with the following radial contraction,
we will obtain a coarse map.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
r\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a rough contraction.
The radial contraction associated
to
r
is the map
\phi_{r}\colon\mathcal{O}Z\to\mathcal{O}Z
defined by

\phi_{r}(tz):=r(t)z
for
tz\in\mathcal{O}Z
.

The following proposition says that for
any proper continuous map from the open cone,
by composing an appropriate
radial contraction, we obtain a coarse map.
In fact, being metrically proper and pseudocontinuous is enough
to this result. We refer
[
9
, Section 4]
and

[
7
, Definition 5.2]
for the definition of
pseudocontinuous maps. We remark that continuous maps are pseudocontinuous.

Proposition 7.3

(
[
9
, Lemma 4.12]
)
.

Let
V
be a metric space, and let
F\colon\mathcal{O}Z\to V

be a metrically proper pseudocontinuous map.
Then there exists a 0-rough contraction
r\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that,
for the radial contraction
\phi_{r}\colon\mathcal{O}Z\to\mathcal{O}Z

associated to
r
,
the composite
F\circ\phi_{r}\colon\mathcal{O}Z\to V
is a coarse map.

Remark 7.4
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be a constant which will be fixed in the beginning of Section
7.2
.
We can assume without loss of generality that the function
r

in Proposition
7.3
satisfies the following.
For
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
t^{\epsilon},t^{\prime\epsilon}\in r^{-1}([1,\infty))
,
we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert r(t^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}-r(t^{\prime\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert.

Indeed, we define the map
r^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by
r^{\prime}(t):=r(t)
for

t\in r^{-1}([0,1))
, and
r^{\prime}(t):=r(t)^{\epsilon}
for
t\in r^{-1}([1,\infty))
.
Then
r^{\prime}(t)\leq r(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
for
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
t^{\epsilon},t^{\prime\epsilon}\in r^{-1}([1,\infty))
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert r^{\prime}(t^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}-r^{\prime}(t^{\prime\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}\right\rvert=\left\lvert r(t^{\epsilon})-r(t^{\prime\epsilon})\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert t^{\epsilon}-t^{\prime\epsilon}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert.

If the radial contraction associated to

satisfies the statement
of Proposition
7.3
, so does the one associated to

.
Therefore we can replace

by

.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let

be a 0-rough contraction satisfying
Remark
7.4
.
Set

For

\displaystyle\left\lvert r(u^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}-r(u^{\prime\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert u-u^{\prime}\right\rvert+2\delta.

Especially, the map

is a

-rough contraction.

Proof..

If

\displaystyle\left\lvert r(u^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}-r(u^{\prime\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}\right\rvert\leq r(u^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}+r(u^{\prime\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}\leq u+u^{\prime}\leq\left\lvert u-u^{\prime}\right\rvert+2\delta.

If
\min\{r(u^{\epsilon}),r(u^{\prime\epsilon})\}>1

then by Remark

\displaystyle\left\lvert r(u^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}-r(u^{\prime\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert u-u^{\prime}\right\rvert.

∎

\varphi(x_{n})\to[\gamma]
.

∎

7.5.
Radial extension of the induced map

In this section, we give a proof of the statement (
ii
)
of Theorem
7.7
.
Let
F\colon X\to Y
be a visual

\rho
-
T
-
{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}_{a}^{\infty}
-
{{}_{Y}\mathcal{L}}_{b}^{\infty}
-equivariant map.
We will show the that the radial extension
\mathop{\mathrm{rad}}\nolimits\partial F
of the
induced map
\partial F\colon\partial X\to\partial Y
is coarsely homotopic
to
F
.

First, we will show that

\mathop{\mathrm{rad^{\prime}}}\nolimits\partial F\colon X^{\text{Vis}}\to Y
is coarsely homotopic
to the restriction
F|_{X^{\text{Vis}}}
.
For each
x\in X^{\text{Vis}}
, we choose
\gamma_{x}\in{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}_{a}^{\infty}
and

T_{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

such that
\log^{\epsilon}(x)=T_{x}^{\epsilon}[\gamma_{x}]
.
We also choose
\eta_{x}\in{{}_{Y}\mathcal{L}}_{b}^{\infty}
such that

\partial F([\gamma_{x}])=[\eta_{x}]
and

\exp_{\epsilon}(s[\eta_{x}])=\eta_{x}(s^{1/\epsilon})
.

Set
Z^{\text{Vis}}:=\{(x,t)\in X^{\text{Vis}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\colon t\leq T_{x}\}
.
We define a coarse homotopy

H\colon Z^{\text{Vis}}\to Y
by

\displaystyle H(x,t)=\eta_{x}(\rho(T_{x}-t)+r(t^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}).

By Proposition
7.11
, we can assume without loss of
generality that
r(t^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}\leq\rho(t)
for all

t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

Lemma 7.15
.

The map
H
is a coarse map.

Proof..

It is easy to show that
H
is metrically proper, so we will show that
H

is bornologous.

Let
(x,t),(y,t^{\prime})\in Z^{\text{Vis}}
. For
x
,
y
,
we choose
\gamma_{x},\gamma_{y}\in{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}_{a}^{\infty}
,

T_{x},T_{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
\eta_{x},\eta_{y}\in{{}_{Y}\mathcal{L}}_{b}^{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle x=\gamma_{x}(T_{x}),

\displaystyle\quad\log^{\epsilon}(x)=T_{x}^{\epsilon}[\gamma_{x}],\quad\partial F([\gamma_{x}])=[\eta_{x}],\quad\exp_{\epsilon}(s[\eta_{x}])=\eta_{x}(s^{1/\epsilon})\quad(\forall s),

\displaystyle y=\gamma_{(}T_{y}),

\displaystyle\quad\log^{\epsilon}(y)=T_{y}^{\epsilon}[\gamma_{y}],\quad\partial F([\gamma_{y}])=[\eta_{y}],\quad\exp_{\epsilon}(s[\eta_{y}])=\eta_{y}(s^{1/\epsilon})\quad(\forall s).

Set
\delta:=\sup\{u\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}:r(u^{\epsilon})\leq 1\}
.
By Lemma
7.5
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert r(t^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}-r(t^{\prime\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert+2\delta.

Let
\tau
be a constant such that
\rho
is a
\tau
-rough contraction.
Then

\displaystyle\left\lvert\rho(T_{x}-t)-\rho(T_{y}-t^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert T_{x}-T_{y}\right\rvert+\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert+\tau\leq\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert+\tilde{\theta}(\,\overline{x,y}\,)+\tau.

Set
s:=\rho(T_{x}-t)+r(t^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}
and

s^{\prime}:=\rho(T_{y}-t^{\prime})+r(t^{\prime\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert s-s^{\prime}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\rho(T_{x}-t)-\rho(T_{y}-t^{\prime})\right\rvert+\left\lvert r(t^{\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}-r(t^{\prime\epsilon})^{1/\epsilon}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\theta}(\,\overline{x,y}\,)+2\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert+2\delta+\tau.

Now set
q:=\min\{s,s^{\prime}\}
.
Then by Lemma
2.4
, we have

(15)

\displaystyle\,\overline{H(x,t),H(y,t^{\prime})}\,=

\displaystyle\,\overline{\eta_{x}(s),\eta_{y}(s^{\prime})}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\overline{\eta_{x}(q),\eta_{y}(q)}\,+\lambda\left\lvert s-s^{\prime}\right\rvert+k_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E^{\prime}\left(\,\overline{\eta_{x}(\rho(T_{x})),\eta_{y}(\rho(T_{y}))}\,+\lambda^{\prime}\tilde{\theta}(\,\overline{\eta_{x}(\rho(T_{x})),\eta_{y}(\rho(T_{y}))}\,\right)

\displaystyle+D^{\prime}+\lambda(\tilde{\theta}(\,\overline{x,y}\,)+2\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert+2\delta+\tau)+k^{\prime}_{1}.

Since
F
is
\rho
-
T
-
{{}_{X}\mathcal{L}}_{a}^{\infty}
-
{{}_{Y}\mathcal{L}}_{b}^{\infty}
-equivariant,
there exists
\xi_{x},\xi_{y}\in{{}_{Y}\mathcal{L}}_{b}^{\infty}
such that

(16)

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\max\left\{\,\overline{\xi_{x}(\rho(u)),F(\gamma_{x}(u))}\,,\,\overline{\xi_{y}(\rho(u)),F(\gamma_{y}(u))}\,\right\}<T.

By Lemma
3.15
,
[\eta_{x}]=\partial F([\gamma_{x}])=[\xi_{x}]
and

[\eta_{y}]=\partial F([\gamma_{y}])=[\xi_{y}]
. So by Lemma
2.5
,

(17)

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\max\left\{\,\overline{\eta_{x}(\rho(u)),\xi_{x}(\rho(u)))}\,,\,\overline{\eta_{y}(\rho(u)),\xi_{y}(\rho(u))}\,\right\}\leq D.

Then we have

(18)

\displaystyle\,\overline{\eta_{x}(\rho(T_{x})),\eta_{y}(\rho(T_{y}))}\,\leq

\displaystyle\,\overline{\eta_{x}(\rho(T_{x})),F(\gamma_{x}(\rho(T_{x})))}\,+\,\overline{F(\gamma_{x}(\rho(T_{x}))),F(\gamma_{y}(\rho(T_{y})))}\,

\displaystyle+\,\overline{F(\gamma_{y}(\rho(T_{y}))),\eta_{y}(\rho(T_{y}))}\,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\overline{F(\gamma_{x}(\rho(T_{x}))),F(\gamma_{y}(\rho(T_{y})))}\,+2(T+D).

Set
T:=\min\{T_{x},T_{y}\}
. Then by Lemma
2.4

(19)

\displaystyle\,\overline{\gamma_{x}(\rho(T_{x})),\gamma_{y}(\rho(T_{y}))}\,\leq

\displaystyle\,\overline{\gamma_{x}(\rho(T)),\gamma_{y}(\rho(T))}\,+\lambda\left\lvert\rho(T_{x})-\rho(T_{y})\right\rvert+k_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E(\,\overline{x,y}\,+\lambda\tilde{\theta}(\,\overline{x,y}\,)+k_{1})+D+\lambda(\tilde{\theta}(\,\overline{x,y}\,)+\tau)+k_{1}.

The estimates (
15
), (
18
), (
19
)
and the assumption that
F
is a large scale Lipschitz map imply

H
is bornologous.
∎

Lemma 7.16
.

The map
\mathop{\mathrm{rad^{\prime}}}\nolimits\partial F

Proof..

By Lemma
7.15
, the map

For

) and (

\,\overline{\eta_{x}(\rho(T_{x})),F(\gamma_{x}(T_{x}))}\,\leq T+D

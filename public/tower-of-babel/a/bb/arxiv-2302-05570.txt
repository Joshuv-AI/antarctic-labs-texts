Multilinear θ \theta -type Calderón–Zygmund operators and commutators on products of weighted Morrey spaces
Xia Han, Hua Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the boundedness properties of multilinear θ \theta -type Calderón–Zygmund operators T θ T_{\theta} recently introduced in the literature. First, we prove strong type and weak type estimates for multilinear θ \theta -type Calderón–Zygmund operators on products of weighted Morrey spaces with multiple weights. Then we discuss strong type estimates for both multilinear commutators and iterated commutators of T θ T_{\theta} on products of these spaces with multiple weights. Furthermore, the weak end-point estimates for commutators of T θ T_{\theta} and pointwise multiplication with functions in bounded mean oscillation are established too. MSC(2020): 42B20; 42B25; 47B38; 47G10 Keywords: Multilinear θ \theta -type Calderón–Zygmund operators; multilinear commutators; iterated commutators; weighted Morrey spaces; multiple weights; Orlicz spaces

Introduction

In this paper, the symbols
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{N}
stand for the sets of all real numbers and natural numbers, respectively. Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space with the Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
and the Lebesgue measure
dx
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}=\overbrace{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{m}
be the
m
-fold product space. We denote by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of all Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and by
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
its dual space, the set of all tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Calderón–Zygmund singular integral operators and their generalizations on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
have been extensively studied (see
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
23
]
for instance). In particular, Yabuta
[
27
]
introduced certain
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operators to facilitate his study of certain classes of pseudo-differential operators. Following the terminology of Yabuta
[
27
]
, we introduce the so-called
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operators as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\theta
be a nonnegative, nondecreasing function on
\mathbb{R}^{+}:=(0,+\infty)
with
0<\theta(1)<+\infty
and

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\theta(t)}{t}\,dt<+\infty.

A measurable function
K(x,y)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{(x,y):x=y\}
is said to be a
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund kernel, if there exists a constant
A>0
such that

1.

\big|K(x,y)\big|\leq\frac{A}{|x-y|^{n}}
,
for any

x\neq y;

2.

\big|K(x,y)-K(z,y)\big|+\big|K(y,x)-K(y,z)\big|\leq\frac{A}{|x-y|^{n}}\cdot\theta\Big(\frac{|x-z|}{|x-y|}\Big)
,

for

|x-z|<\frac{|x-y|}{2}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}
be a linear operator from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into its dual
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We say that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}
is a
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operator with associated kernel
K
if

1.

{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}
can be extended to be a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n});

2.

for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and for all
x\notin\mathrm{supp\,}f
, there is a
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund kernel
K(x,y)
such that

{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy,

where
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space consisting of all infinitely differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that have compact support.

Note that the classical Calderón–Zygmund operator with standard kernel (see
[
4
,
5
]
) is a special case of
\theta
-type operator
{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}
when
\theta(t)=t^{\delta}
with
0<\delta\leq 1
.

In 2009, Maldonado and Naibo
[
15
]
considered the bilinear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operators which are natural generalizations of the linear case, and established weighted norm inequalities for bilinear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operators on products of weighted Lebesgue spaces with Muckenhoupt weights. Moreover, they applied these operators to the study of certain paraproducts and bilinear pseudo-differential operators with mild regularity. Later, in 2014, Lu and Zhang
[
14
]
introduced the general
m
-linear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operators and their commutators for
m\geq 2
, and established boundedness properties of these multilinear operators and multilinear commutators on products of weighted Lebesgue spaces with multiple weights. In addition, they gave some applications to the paraproducts and bilinear pseudo-differential operators with mild regularity and their commutators too. Following
[
14
]
, we now give the definition of the multilinear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operators.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\theta
be a nonnegative, nondecreasing function on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
0<\theta(1)<+\infty
and

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\theta(t)}{t}\,dt<+\infty.

(1.1)

A measurable function
K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
, defined away from the diagonal
x=y_{1}=\cdots=y_{m}
in
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m+1}
, is called an
m
-linear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund kernel, if there exists a constant
A>0
such that

1.

\big|K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\big|\leq\frac{A}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn}}

(1.2)

for all
(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m+1}
with
x\neq y_{k}
for some
k\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, and

2.

\begin{split}&\big|K(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{m})-K(x^{\prime},y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\big|\\
\leq&\frac{A}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn}}\cdot\theta\bigg(\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|}\bigg)\end{split}

(1.3)

whenever
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{\,1\,}{2}\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|x-y_{i}|
, and

3.

for each fixed
k
with
1\leq k\leq m
,

\begin{split}&\big|K(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k},\dots,y_{m})-K(x,y_{1},\dots,y^{\prime}_{k},\dots,y_{m})\big|\\
\leq&\frac{A}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn}}\cdot\theta\bigg(\frac{|y_{k}-y^{\prime}_{k}|}{|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|}\bigg)\end{split}

(1.4)

whenever
|y_{k}-y^{\prime}_{k}|\leq\frac{\,1\,}{2}\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|x-y_{i}|
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
T_{\theta}
be an
m
-linear operator initially defined on the
m
-fold product of Schwartz spaces and taking values into the space of tempered distributions, i.e.,

T_{\theta}:\overbrace{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{m}\to\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

We say that
T_{\theta}
is an
m
-linear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operator if

1.

T_{\theta}
can be extended to be a bounded multilinear operator from

for some
q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}\in[1,+\infty)
and
q\in[1/m,+\infty)
with
1/q=\sum_{k=1}^{m}1/{q_{k}};

2.

for any given
m

\vec{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})

m
-linear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund kernel

\begin{split}T_{\theta}(\vec{f})(x)&=T_{\theta}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})(x)\\
&:=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}K(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{m})f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\end{split}

[

Theorem 1.1

(
[

Let

where
\nu_{\vec{w}}=\prod_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}^{p/{p_{k}}}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
T_{\theta}
be an
m
-linear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operator with

satisfying the condition (

\nu_{\vec{w}}=\prod_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}^{p/{p_{k}}}

For any given

and

Next, we give the definition of the commutator for the multilinear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operator. Given a collection of locally integrable functions
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
, the
m
-linear commutator of
T_{\theta}
with
\vec{b}
is defined by

\begin{split}\big[\Sigma\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](\vec{f})(x)&=\big[\Sigma\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)\\
&:=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\big[b_{k},T_{\theta}\big]_{k}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x),\end{split}

(1.5)

where each term is the commutator of
b_{k}
and
T_{\theta}
in the
k
-th entry of
T_{\theta}
; that is,

\begin{split}&\big[b_{k},T_{\theta}\big]_{k}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)\\
&=b_{k}(x)\cdot T_{\theta}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k},\dots,f_{m})(x)-T_{\theta}(f_{1},\dots,b_{k}f_{k},\dots,f_{m})(x).\end{split}

Then, at a formal level

\begin{split}&\big[\Sigma\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](\vec{f})(x)=\big[\Sigma\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)\\
&=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\big[b_{k}(x)-b_{k}(y_{k})\big]K(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{m})f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.\end{split}

Obviously, when
m=1
in the above definition, this operator coincides with the linear commutator
[b,{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}]
(see
[
13
,
29
]
), which is defined by

[b,{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}](f):=b\cdot{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}(f)-{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}(bf).

Let us now recall the definition of the space of
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
4
,
9
]
). A locally integrable function
b(x)
is said to belong to
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if it satisfies

\|b\|_{*}:=\sup_{B}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|b(x)-b_{B}|\,dx<+\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
b_{B}
stands for the average of
b
over
B
, i.e.,

b_{B}:=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}b(y)\,dy.

In the multilinear setting, we say that
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m}
, if each
b_{k}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
k=1,2,\dots,m
. For convenience, we will use the following notation

\big\|\vec{b}\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{m}}:=\max_{1\leq k\leq m}\big\|b_{k}\big\|_{\ast},\quad\mbox{for}\;\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m}.

In 2014, Lu and Zhang
[
14
]
also proved some weighted estimate and
L\log L
-type estimate for multilinear commutators
\big[\Sigma\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big]
defined in (
1.5
) under a stronger condition (
1.6
) assumed on
\theta

\vec{b}\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m}
.

(

(

Remark 1.2

\theta

Motivated by

\begin{split}\big[\Pi\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](\vec{f})(x)&=\big[\Pi\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)\\

Then

\begin{split}&\big[\Pi\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](\vec{f})(x)=\big[\Pi\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)\\
&=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big[b_{k}(x)-b_{k}(y_{k})\big]K(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{m})f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.\end{split}

(1.7)

.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\big[\Pi\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big]
be the iterated commutator generated by
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operator
T_{\theta}
and
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m};
let
\theta
satisfy

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\theta(t)\cdot(1+|\log t|^{m})}{t}\,dt<+\infty.

(1.8)

If
p_{k}=1
,
k=1,2,\dots,m
and
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in A_{(1,\dots,1)}
, then for any given
\lambda>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
and
\lambda
such that

\begin{split}&\nu_{\vec{w}}\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\big|\big[\Pi\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](\vec{f})(x)\big|>\lambda^{m}\Big\}\Big)\\
&\leq C\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi^{(m)}\bigg(\frac{|f_{k}(x)|}{\lambda}\bigg)w_{k}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/m},\end{split}

where
\nu_{\vec{w}}=\prod_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}^{1/{m}}
,
\Phi(t)=t\cdot(1+\log^{+}t)
and
\Phi^{(m)}:=\overbrace{\Phi\circ\cdots\circ\Phi}^{m}
.

Remark 1.6
.

It was proved in
[
18
]
that when
\theta(t)=t^{\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
, the estimate in Theorem
1.6
is sharp in the sense that
\Phi^{(m)}
cannot be replaced by
\Phi^{(k)}
for any
k<m
.

On the other hand, the classical Morrey spaces
L^{p,\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
were originally introduced by Morrey in
[
16
]
to study the local regularity of solutions to second order elliptic partial differential equations. Nowadays these spaces have been studied intensively in the literature, and found a wide range of applications in harmonic analysis, potential theory and nonlinear dispersive equations. In 2009, Komori and Shirai
[
10
]
defined and investigated the weighted Morrey spaces
L^{p,\kappa}(w)
for
1\leq p<+\infty
, which could be viewed as an extension of weighted Lebesgue spaces, and obtained the boundedness of some classical integral operators on these weighted spaces. In order to deal with the multilinear case
m\geq 2
, we consider the weighted Morrey spaces
L^{p,\kappa}(w)
here for all
0<p<+\infty
. We will extend the results obtained in
[
14
]
for
m
-linear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operators to the product of weighted Morrey spaces with multiple weights. Moreover, the corresponding weighted estimates for both multilinear commutators and iterated commutators are also considered. Let us first recall the definition of the spaces
L^{p,\kappa}(w)
and
WL^{p,\kappa}(w)
.

Definition 1.5

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
0<p<+\infty
,
0\leq\kappa<1
and let
w
be a weight on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The weighted Morrey space
L^{p,\kappa}(w)
is defined to be the set of all locally integrable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\|f\|_{L^{p,\kappa}(w)}:=\sup_{B}\bigg(\frac{1}{w(B)^{\kappa}}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}<+\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 1.6

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
0<p<+\infty
,
0\leq\kappa<1
and let
w
be a weight on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The weighted weak Morrey space
WL^{p,\kappa}(w)
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\begin{split}\|f\|_{WL^{p,\kappa}(w)}:=&\sup_{B}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\cdot w\big(\big\{x\in B:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)^{1/p}<+\infty,\end{split}

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
\lambda>0
.

Note that when
w\in\Delta_{2}
, then
L^{p,0}(w)=L^{p}(w)
,
WL^{p,0}(w)=WL^{p}(w)
and
L^{p,1}(w)=L^{\infty}(w)
by the Lebesgue differentiation
theorem with respect to
w
.

In order to deal with the end-point case of the commutators, we have to consider the following
L\log L
-type space, which was introduced by the second author in
[
25
,
26
]
(for the unweighted case, see also
[
12
]
and
[
21
]
).

Definition 1.7
.

Let
p=1
,
0\leq\kappa<1
and let
w
be a weight on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We denote by
(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w)
the weighted Morrey space of
L\log L
type, the space of all locally integrable functions
f
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite norm

\big\|f\big\|_{(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w)}
.

(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w):=\Big\{f:\big\|f\big\|_{(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w)}<\infty\Big\},

where

\begin{split}\big\|f\big\|_{(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w)}:=&\sup_{B}w(B)^{1-\kappa}\big\|f\big\|_{L\log L(w),B}.\end{split}

Here
\|\cdot\|_{L\log L(w),B}
denotes the weighted Luxemburg norm, whose definition will be given in Section
3
below. Note that
t\leq t\cdot(1+\log^{+}t)
for any
t>0
. By definition, for any ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
w\in A_{\infty}
, then we have

\big\|f\big\|_{L(w),B}\leq\big\|f\big\|_{L\log L(w),B},

which means that the following inequality (it can be viewed as a generalized Jensen’s inequality)

\big\|f\big\|_{L(w),B}=\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)|w(x)\,dx\leq\big\|f\big\|_{L\log L(w),B}

(1.9)

holds for any ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence, for all
0<\kappa<1
and
w\in A_{\infty}
, we can further obtain the following inclusion
from (
1.9
):

(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w)\hookrightarrow L^{1,\kappa}(w).

It is known that
L^{p,\kappa}
is an extension of
L^{p}
in the sense that
L^{p,0}=L^{p}
.
Motivated by the works in
[
10
,
14
,
15
]
, the main purpose of this paper is to establish boundedness properties of multilinear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operators and their commutators on products of weighted Morrey spaces with multiple weights.

In what follows, the letter
C
always stands for a positive constant independent of the main parameters and not necessarily the same at each occurrence. The symbol
\mathbf{X}\lesssim\mathbf{Y}
means that there is a constant
C>0
such that
\mathbf{X}\leq C\mathbf{Y}
.
The symbol
\mathbf{X}\approx\mathbf{Y}
means that there is a constant
C>0
such that
C^{-1}\mathbf{Y}\leq\mathbf{X}\leq C\mathbf{Y}
.

3
Notations and preliminaries

3.1
Multiple weights

For any
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
B(x,r)=\big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-y|<r\big\}
denote the open ball centered at
x
with radius
r
,
B(x,r)^{\complement}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B(x,r)
denote its complement and
|B(x,r)|
be the Lebesgue measure of the ball
B(x,r)
. We also use the notation
\chi_{B(x,r)}
to denote the characteristic function of
B(x,r)
. For some
t>0
, the notation
tB
stands for the ball with the same center as
B
whose radius is
t
times that of
B
.

A weight
w
is said to belong to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
for
1<p<+\infty
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{-p^{\prime}/p}\,dx\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\leq C

for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
such that
1/p+1/{p^{\prime}}=1
. The class
A_{1}
is defined replacing the above inequality by

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)\,dx\leq C\cdot\underset{x\in B}{\mbox{ess\,inf}}\;w(x)

for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since the
A_{p}
classes are increasing with respect to
p
, the
A_{\infty}
class of weights is defined in a natural way by

A_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{1\leq p<+\infty}A_{p}.

Moreover, the following characterization will often be used in the sequel. There are positive constants
C
and
\delta
such that for any ball
B
and any measurable set
E
contained in
B
,

\frac{w(E)}{w(B)}\leq C\bigg(\frac{|E|}{|B|}\bigg)^{\delta}.

(3.1)

Given a Lebesgue measurable set
E
, we denote the characteristic function of
E
by
\chi_{E}
. We say that a weight
w
satisfies the doubling condition, simply denoted by
w\in\Delta_{2}
, if there is an absolute constant
C>0
such that

w(2B)\leq C\,w(B)

(3.2)

holds for any ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
w\in A_{p}
with
1\leq p<+\infty
(or
w\in A_{\infty}
), then we have that
w\in\Delta_{2}
.

Recently, the theory of multiple weights adapted to multilinear Calderón–Zygmund operators was developed by Lerner et al. in
[
11
]
. New more refined multilinear maximal function was defined and used in
[
11
]
to characterize the class of multiple
A_{\vec{P}}
weights, and to obtain some weighted estimates for multilinear Calderón–Zygmund operators. Now let us recall the definition of multiple weights. For
m
exponents
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in[1,+\infty)
, we will often write
\vec{P}
for the vector
\vec{P}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
, and
p
for the number given by
1/p=\sum_{k=1}^{m}1/{p_{k}}
with
p\in[1/m,+\infty)
. Given
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
, let us set

\nu_{\vec{w}}=\prod_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}^{p/{p_{k}}}.

We say that
\vec{w}
satisfies the multilinear
A_{\vec{P}}
condition if it satisfies

\sup_{B}\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w_{k}(x)^{-p^{\prime}_{k}/{p_{k}}}\,dx\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}_{k}}}<+\infty.

(3.3)

When
p_{k}=1
for some
k\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, the condition
\big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w_{k}(x)^{-p^{\prime}_{k}/{p_{k}}}\,dx\big)^{1/{p^{\prime}_{k}}}
is understood as
\big(\inf_{x\in B}w_{k}(x)\big)^{-1}
. In particular, when each
p_{k}=1
,
k=1,2,\dots,m
, we denote
A_{\vec{1}}=A_{(1,\dots,1)}
. One can easily check that
A_{(1,\dots,1)}
is contained in
A_{\vec{P}}
for each
\vec{P}
, however, the classes
A_{\vec{P}}
are NOT increasing with the natural partial order (see
[
11
, Remark 7.3]
). It was shown in
[
11
]
that these are the largest classes of weights for which all multilinear Calderón–Zygmund operators are bounded on weighted Lebesgue spaces. Moreover, in general, the condition
\vec{w}\in A_{\vec{P}}
does not imply
w_{k}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
1\leq k\leq m
(see
[
11
, Remark 7.2]
), but instead

Lemma 3.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in[1,+\infty)
and
1/p=\sum_{k=1}^{m}1/{p_{k}}
. Then
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in A_{\vec{P}}
if and only if

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nu_{\vec{w}}\in A_{mp},\\
&w_{k}^{1-p^{\prime}_{k}}\in A_{mp^{\prime}_{k}},\quad k=1,\ldots,m,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

where
\nu_{\vec{w}}=\prod_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}^{p/{p_{k}}}
and the condition
w_{k}^{1-p^{\prime}_{k}}\in A_{mp^{\prime}_{k}}
in the case
p_{k}=1
is understood as
w_{k}^{1/m}\in A_{1}
.

Observe that in the linear case
m=1
both conditions included in (
3.4
) represent the same
A_{p}
condition. However, in the multilinear case
m\geq 2
neither of the conditions in (
3.4
) implies the other. We refer the reader to
[
11
]
for further details.

3.2
Orlicz spaces and Luxemburg norms

Next we recall some basic definitions and facts from the theory of Orlicz spaces. For more information about these spaces the reader may consult the book
[
20
]
. Let
\mathcal{A}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a Young function. That is, a continuous, convex and strictly increasing function with
\mathcal{A}(0)=0
and such that
\mathcal{A}(t)\to+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
. Given a Young function
\mathcal{A}
and a ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we consider the
\mathcal{A}
-average of a function
f
over a ball
B
, which is given by the following Luxemburg norm:

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{A},B}:=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\mathcal{A}\bigg(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\bigg)dx\leq 1\bigg\}.

When
\mathcal{A}(t)=t^{p}
with
1\leq p<+\infty
, it is easy to see that

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{A},B}=\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\big|f(x)\big|^{p}\,dx\bigg)^{1/p};

that is, the Luxemburg norm coincides with the normalized
L^{p}
norm. Associated to each Young function
\mathcal{A}
, one can define its complementary function
\bar{\mathcal{A}}
by

\bar{\mathcal{A}}(s):=\sup_{0\leq t<+\infty}\big[st-\mathcal{A}(t)\big],\quad 0\leq s<+\infty.

It is not difficult to check that such
\bar{\mathcal{A}}
is also a Young function. A standard computation shows that for all
t>0
,

t\leq\mathcal{A}^{-1}(t)\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}(t)\leq 2t.

From this, it follows that the following generalized Hölder’s inequality in Orlicz spaces holds for any given ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\big|f(x)\cdot g(x)\big|\,dx\leq 2\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{A},B}\big\|g\big\|_{\bar{\mathcal{A}},B}.

A particular case of interest, and especially in this paper, is the Young function
\Phi(t)=t\cdot(1+\log^{+}t)
, and we know that its complementary Young function is given by
\bar{\Phi}(t)\approx\exp(t)-1
. The corresponding averages will be denoted by

\big\|f\big\|_{\Phi,B}=\big\|f\big\|_{L\log L,B}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\big\|g\big\|_{\bar{\Phi},B}=\big\|g\big\|_{\exp L,B}.

Consequently, from the above generalized Hölder’s inequality in Orlicz spaces, we also get

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\big|f(x)\cdot g(x)\big|\,dx\leq 2\big\|f\big\|_{L\log L,B}\big\|g\big\|_{\exp L,B}.

(3.5)

To obtain endpoint weak-type estimates for the multilinear and iterated commutators on the product of weighted Morrey spaces, we need to define the
\mathcal{A}
-average of a function
f
over a ball
B
by means of the weighted Luxemburg norm; that is, given a Young function
\mathcal{A}
and
w\in A_{\infty}
, we define (see
[
20
,
28
]
)

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{A}(w),B}:=\inf\bigg\{\sigma>0:\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}\mathcal{A}\bigg(\frac{|f(x)|}{\sigma}\bigg)\cdot w(x)\,dx\leq 1\bigg\}.

When
\mathcal{A}(t)=t
, this norm is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L(w),B}
, when
\Phi(t)=t\cdot(1+\log^{+}t)
, this norm is also denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L\log L(w),B}
. The complementary Young function of
\Phi(t)
is
\bar{\Phi}(t)\approx\exp(t)-1
with the corresponding Luxemburg norm denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{\exp L(w),B}
. For
w\in A_{\infty}
and for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can also show the weighted version of (
3.5
). Namely, the following generalized Hölder’s inequality in the weighted context is true for
f,g
(see
[
28
]
for instance).

\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}\big|f(x)\cdot g(x)\big|w(x)\,dx\leq C\big\|f\big\|_{L\log L(w),B}\big\|g\big\|_{\exp L(w),B}.

(3.6)

This estimate will play an important role in the proof of Theorem
2.4
.

, and

Lemma 4.3

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let

. Then for any ball

, the following reverse Jensen’s inequality holds.

\int_{B}w(x)\,dx\leq C|B|\cdot\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\log w(x)\,dx\bigg).

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
1<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}<+\infty
and
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})
be in
L^{p_{1},\kappa}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m},\kappa}(w_{m})
with
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})\in A_{\vec{P}}
and
0<\kappa<1
. For any given ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(denote by
x_{0}
the center of
B
, and
r>0
the radius of
B
), it is enough for us to show that

\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|T_{\theta}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.

(4.1)

To this end, for any
1\leq i\leq m
, we represent
f_{i}
as

f_{i}=f_{i}\cdot\chi_{2B}+f_{i}\cdot\chi_{(2B)^{\complement}}:=f^{0}_{i}+f^{\infty}_{i};

and
2B=B(x_{0},2r)
. Then we write

\begin{split}\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(y_{i})&=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Big(f^{0}_{i}(y_{i})+f^{\infty}_{i}(y_{i})\Big)\\
&=\sum_{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}\in\{0,\infty\}}f^{\beta_{1}}_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f^{\beta_{m}}_{m}(y_{m})\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{m}f^{0}_{i}(y_{i})+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}}f^{\beta_{1}}_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f^{\beta_{m}}_{m}(y_{m}),\end{split}

where

\mathfrak{L}:=\big\{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}):\beta_{k}\in\{0,\infty\},\mbox{there is at least one $\beta_{k}\neq 0$},1\leq k\leq m\big\};

that is, each term of
\sum
contains at least one
\beta_{k}\neq 0
. Since
T_{\theta}
is an
m
-linear operator, then by Lemma
4.1
(
N=2^{m}
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|T_{\theta}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|T_{\theta}(f^{0}_{1},\dots,f^{0}_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}}\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|T_{\theta}(f^{\beta_{1}}_{1},\ldots,f^{\beta_{m}}_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle:=I^{0,\dots,0}+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}}I^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}.

(4.2)

By the weighted strong-type estimate of
T_{\theta}
(see Theorem
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle I^{0,\dots,0}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg(\int_{2B}|f_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}}w_{i}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\cdot\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}(2B)^{\kappa/{p_{i}}}.

(4.3)

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in[1,+\infty)
and
p\in[1/m,+\infty)
with
1/p=\sum_{i=1}^{m}1/{p_{i}}
. We first claim that under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
(or Theorem
2.2
), the following result holds for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}w_{i}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{p/{p_{i}}}\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{B}}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx,

(4.4)

provided that
w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}\in A_{\infty}
and
\nu_{\vec{w}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}^{p/{p_{i}}}
.
In fact, since
w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}\in A_{\infty}
, by using Lemma
4.3
, then we have

\begin{split}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}w_{i}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{p/{p_{i}}}&\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg[|\mathcal{B}|\cdot\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\log w_{i}(x)\,dx\bigg)\bigg]^{p/{p_{i}}}\\
&=C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg[|\mathcal{B}|^{p/{p_{i}}}\cdot\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\log w_{i}(x)^{p/{p_{i}}}\,dx\bigg)\bigg]\\
&=C\cdot\big(|\mathcal{B}|\big)^{\sum_{i=1}^{m}p/{p_{i}}}\cdot\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\log w_{i}(x)^{p/{p_{i}}}\,dx\bigg).\end{split}

Note that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}p/{p_{i}}=1\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}(x)^{p/{p_{i}}}.

Thus, by Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}w_{i}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{p/{p_{i}}}&\leq C\cdot|\mathcal{B}|\cdot\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\log\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)\\
&\leq C\int_{\mathcal{B}}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx.\end{split}

This gives (
4.4
). Moreover, in view of Lemma
3.1
, we have that
\nu_{\vec{w}}\in A_{mp}
with
1/m<p<+\infty
.
This fact, together with (
4.4
) and (
3.2
), implies that

\begin{split}I^{0,\dots,0}&\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\cdot\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2B)^{\kappa/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\\
&\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.\end{split}

(4.5)

To estimate the remaining terms in (
4
), let us first consider the case when
\beta_{1}=\cdots=\beta_{m}=\infty
. By a simple geometric observation, we know that

\overbrace{\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash 2B\big)\times\cdots\times\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash 2B\big)}^{m}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}\backslash(2B)^{m},

and

(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}\backslash(2B)^{m}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}(2^{j+1}B)^{m}\backslash(2^{j}B)^{m},

where we have used the notation
E^{m}=\overbrace{E\times\cdots\times E}^{m}
for a measurable set
E
and a positive integer
m
.
By the size condition (
1.2
) of the
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund kernel
K
, for any
x\in B
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|T_{\theta}(f^{\infty}_{1},\ldots,f^{\infty}_{m})(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}\backslash(2B)^{m}}\frac{|f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})|}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn}}\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{(2^{j+1}B)^{m}\backslash(2^{j}B)^{m}}\frac{|f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})|}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn}}\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\int_{(2^{j+1}B)^{m}\backslash(2^{j}B)^{m}}\big|f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})\big|\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg),

(4.6)

where we have used the fact that
|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|\approx 2^{j+1}r\approx|2^{j+1}B|^{1/n}
when
x\in B
and
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\in(2^{j+1}B)^{m}\backslash(2^{j}B)^{m}
. Furthermore, by using Hölder’s inequality, the multiple
A_{\vec{P}}
condition on
\vec{w}
, we can deduce that

\begin{split}&\big|T_{\theta}(f^{\infty}_{1},\ldots,f^{\infty}_{m})(x)\big|\\
\lesssim&\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg\{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|^{p_{i}}w_{i}(y_{i})\,dy_{i}\bigg)^{1/{p_{i}}}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w_{i}(y_{i})^{-p^{\prime}_{i}/{p_{i}}}\,dy_{i}\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}_{i}}}\bigg\}\\
\leq&\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\cdot\frac{|2^{j+1}B|^{1/p+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1-1/{p_{i}})}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{1/p}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg(\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}w_{i}(2^{j+1}B)^{\kappa/{p_{i}}}\bigg)\bigg\}\\
=&\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{1/p}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}(2^{j+1}B)^{\kappa/{p_{i}}}\bigg\},\end{split}

where in the last step we have used the fact that
1/p+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1-1/{p_{i}})=m
. Hence, from the above pointwise estimate and (
4.4
), we obtain

\begin{split}I^{\infty,\dots,\infty}&\lesssim\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{1/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{\kappa/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{1/p}}\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}.\end{split}

Since
\nu_{\vec{w}}\in A_{mp}\subset A_{\infty}
by Lemma
3.1
, then it follows directly from the inequality (
3.1
) with exponent
\delta>0
that

\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)}\lesssim\bigg(\frac{|B|}{|2^{j+1}B|}\bigg)^{\delta},

(4.7)

which further implies

\begin{split}I^{\infty,\dots,\infty}&\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{|B|}{|2^{j+1}B|}\bigg)^{{\delta(1-\kappa)}/p}\\
&\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})},\end{split}

(4.8)

where in the last estimate we have used the fact that
0<\kappa<1
and
\delta>0
. We now consider the case where exactly
\ell
of the
\beta_{i}
are
\infty
for some
1\leq\ell<m
. We only give the arguments for one of these cases. The rest are similar and can be easily obtained from the arguments below by permuting the indices. In this case, by the same reason as above, we also have

\overbrace{\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash 2B\big)\times\cdots\times\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash 2B\big)}^{\ell}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\ell}\backslash(2B)^{\ell},

and

(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\ell}\backslash(2B)^{\ell}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}(2^{j+1}B)^{\ell}\backslash(2^{j}B)^{\ell},\quad 1\leq\ell<m.

Using the size condition (
1.2
) again, we deduce that for any
x\in B
,

\displaystyle\big|T_{\theta}(f^{\infty}_{1},\ldots,f^{\infty}_{\ell},f^{0}_{\ell+1},\ldots,f^{0}_{m})(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\ell}\backslash(2B)^{\ell}}\int_{(2B)^{m-\ell}}\frac{|f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})|}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=\ell+1}^{m}\int_{2B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\times\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\int_{(2^{j+1}B)^{\ell}\backslash(2^{j}B)^{\ell}}\big|f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{\ell}(y_{\ell})\big|\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=\ell+1}^{m}\int_{2B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\times\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg),

(4.9)

where in the last inequality we have used the inclusion relation
2B\subseteq 2^{j+1}B
with
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and hence we arrive at the same expression considered in the previous case. Hence, we can now argue exactly as we did in the estimation of
I^{\infty,\dots,\infty}
to obtain that for all
m
-tuples
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}
,

\displaystyle I^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{|B|}{|2^{j+1}B|}\bigg)^{{\delta(1-\kappa)}/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.

(4.10)

Combining these estimates (
4.5
), (
4.8
) and (
4
), then (
4.1
) holds and concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
1\leq p_{1},\dots,p_{m}<+\infty
,
\min\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}=1
and
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})
be in
L^{p_{1},\kappa}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m},\kappa}(w_{m})
with
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})\in A_{\vec{P}}
and
0<\kappa<1
. For an arbitrary ball
B=B(x_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

, we need to show that the following estimate holds.

\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\lambda\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}\big(\big\{x\in B:\big|T_{\theta}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big|>\lambda\big\}\big)^{1/p}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.

(4.11)

To this end, we represent
f_{i}
as

f_{i}=f_{i}\cdot\chi_{2B}+f_{i}\cdot\chi_{(2B)^{\complement}}:=f^{0}_{i}+f^{\infty}_{i},\quad\mbox{for}~~i=1,2,\dots,m.

By using Lemma
4.2
(
N=2^{m}
), one can write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\lambda\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}\big(\big\{x\in B:\big|T_{\theta}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big|>\lambda\big\}\big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\lambda\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}\big(\big\{x\in B:\big|T_{\theta}(f^{0}_{1},\dots,f^{0}_{m})\big|>\lambda/{2^{m}}\big\}\big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}}\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\lambda\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}\big(\big\{x\in B:\big|T_{\theta}(f^{\beta_{1}}_{1},\dots,f^{\beta_{m}}_{m})\big|>\lambda/{2^{m}}\big\}\big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle:=I^{0,\dots,0}_{\ast}+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}}I^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\ast},

(4.12)

where

\mathfrak{L}=\big\{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}):\beta_{k}\in\{0,\infty\},\mbox{there is at least one $\beta_{k}\neq 0$},1\leq k\leq m\big\}.

By the weighted weak-type estimate of
T_{\theta}
(see Theorem
1.2
), we can estimate the first term on the right hand side of (
4
) as follows.

\displaystyle I^{0,\dots,0}_{\ast}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg(\int_{2B}|f_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}}w_{i}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}(2B)^{\kappa/{p_{i}}}.

(4.13)

Moreover, in view of Lemma
3.1
again, we also have
\nu_{\vec{w}}\in A_{mp}
with
1/m\leq p<+\infty
. Then we apply the inequalities (
3.2
) and (
4.4
) to obtain that

\begin{split}I^{0,\dots,0}_{\ast}&\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2B)^{\kappa/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\\
&\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.\end{split}

(4.14)

In the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we have already showed the following pointwise estimate for all
m
-tuples
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}
(see (
4
) and (
4
)).

\begin{split}&\big|T_{\theta}(f^{\beta_{1}}_{1},\ldots,f^{\beta_{m}}_{m})(x)\big|\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg).\end{split}

(4.15)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

p_{1}=\cdots=p_{\ell}=\min\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}=1\quad\mathrm{and}\quad p_{\ell+1},\ldots,p_{m}>1

with
1\leq\ell<m
. The case that
p_{1}=\cdots=p_{m}=1
can be dealt with quite similarly and more easily. Using Hölder’s inequality, the multiple
A_{\vec{P}}
condition on
\vec{w}
, we obtain that for any
x\in B
,

\begin{split}&\big|T_{\theta}(f^{\beta_{1}}_{1},\ldots,f^{\beta_{m}}_{m})(x)\big|\\
&\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg)\times\bigg(\prod_{i=\ell+1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg)\\
&\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|w_{i}(y_{i})\,dy_{i}\bigg)\bigg(\inf_{y_{i}\in 2^{j+1}B}w_{i}(y_{i})\bigg)^{-1}\\
&\times\prod_{i=\ell+1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|^{p_{i}}w_{i}(y_{i})\,dy_{i}\bigg)^{1/{p_{i}}}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w_{i}(y_{i})^{-p^{\prime}_{i}/{p_{i}}}\,dy_{i}\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}_{i}}}\\

where in the last inequality we have invoked (
4.4
). Observe that
\nu_{\vec{w}}\in A_{mp}
with

. Thus, it follows directly from Chebyshev’s inequality and the above pointwise estimate that

\displaystyle I^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\ast}

\displaystyle\leq\cdot\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|T_{\theta}(f^{\beta_{1}}_{1},\ldots,f^{\beta_{m}}_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}.

Moreover, in view of (
4.7
), we obtain that for all
m
-tuples
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}
,

\begin{split}I^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\ast}&\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{|B|}{|2^{j+1}B|}\bigg)^{{\delta(1-\kappa)}/p}\\
&\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})},\end{split}

(4.16)

where in the last step we have used the fact

and

. Putting the estimates (
4.14
) and (

) together produces the required inequality (

). Thus, by taking the supremum over all

2.2
.

Let
1\leq p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq+\infty
. We say that
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}A_{p_{i}}
, if each
w_{i}
is in
A_{p_{i}}
,
i=1,2,\dots,m

\prod_{i=1}^{m}A_{p_{i}}\subset A_{\vec{P}}.

Moreover, it was shown in

(4.17)

A natural question appearing here is whether the above inclusion relation is also strict. Thus, as a direct consequence of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, we immediately obtain the following results.

.

m\geq 2
and

-linear
\theta

Corollary 4.2
.

Let

and
T_{\theta}

m
-linear
\theta
-type Calderón–Zygmund operator with
\theta
satisfying the condition (
1.1
). If
1\leq p_{1},\dots,p_{m}<+\infty
,
\min\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}=1
and
1/m\leq p<+\infty
with
1/p=\sum_{i=1}^{m}1/{p_{i}}
, and
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}A_{p_{i}}
, then for any
0<\kappa<1
, the multilinear operator
T_{\theta}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1},\kappa}(w_{1})\times L^{p_{2},\kappa}(w_{2})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m},\kappa}(w_{m})
into
WL^{p,\kappa}(\nu_{\vec{w}})
with
\nu_{\vec{w}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}^{p/{p_{i}}}
.

5
Proofs of Theorems
2.3
and
2.4

To prove our main theorems for multilinear commutators in this section, we need the following lemmas about
\mathrm{BMO}
functions.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
b
be a function in
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

1.

For every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\big|b_{2^{j+1}B}-b_{B}\big|\leq C\cdot(j+1)\|b\|_{*}.

2.

Let
1\leq p<+\infty
. For every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all
\omega\in A_{\infty}
,

\bigg(\int_{B}\big|b(x)-b_{B}\big|^{p}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\|b\|_{*}\cdot\omega(B)^{1/p}.

Proof.

For the proofs of the above results, we refer the reader to
[
24
]
.
∎

Based on Lemma
5.1
, we now assert that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega\in A_{\infty}
, the estimate

\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|b(x)-b_{B}\big|^{p}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C(j+1)\|b\|_{*}\cdot\omega(2^{j+1}B)^{1/p}

(5.1)

holds whenever
b\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
1\leq p<+\infty
. Indeed, by using Lemma
5.1
(1) and (2), we could easily obtain

\begin{split}&\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|b(x)-b_{B}\big|^{p}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|b(x)-b_{2^{j+1}B}\big|^{p}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}+\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|b_{2^{j+1}B}-b_{B}\big|^{p}\omega(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&\leq C\|b\|_{*}\cdot\omega(2^{j+1}B)^{1/p}+C(j+1)\|b\|_{*}\cdot\omega(2^{j+1}B)^{1/p}\\
&\leq C(j+1)\|b\|_{*}\cdot\omega(2^{j+1}B)^{1/p},\end{split}

as desired. Next, let us set up the following result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
b
be a function in
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then for any ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\omega\in A_{\infty}
, we have

\big\|b-b_{B}\big\|_{\exp L(\omega),B}\leq C\|b\|_{*}.

(5.2)

Proof.

By the well-known John–Nirenberg’s inequality (see
[
9
]
), we know that there exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, depending only on the dimension
n
, such that for any
\lambda>0
,

\big|\big\{x\in B:|b(x)-b_{B}|>\lambda\big\}\big|\leq C_{1}|B|\exp\bigg\{-\frac{C_{2}\lambda}{\|b\|_{*}}\bigg\}.

This result shows that in some sense logarithmic growth is the maximum possible for BMO functions (more precisely, we can take
C_{1}=\sqrt{2}
,
C_{2}=\log 2/{2^{n+2}}
, see
[
4
, p.123–125]
). Applying the comparison property (
3.1
) of
A_{\infty}
weights, there is a positive number
\delta>0
such that

\omega\big(\big\{x\in B:|b(x)-b_{B}|>\lambda\big\}\big)\leq C_{1}\omega(B)\exp\bigg\{-\frac{C_{2}\delta\lambda}{\|b\|_{*}}\bigg\}.

From this, it follows that (
c_{0}
and
C
are two constants)

\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}\exp\bigg(\frac{|b(y)-b_{B}|}{c_{0}\|b\|_{*}}\bigg)\omega(y)\,dy\leq C,

which yields (
5.2
).
∎

Furthermore, by (
5.2
) and Lemma
5.1
(1), it is easy to check that for each
\omega
in
A_{\infty}
and for any ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\big\|b-b_{B}\big\|_{\exp L(\omega),2^{j+1}B}\leq C(j+1)\|b\|_{\ast},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.3)

We are now in a position to give the proofs of Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
1<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}<+\infty
and
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})
be in
L^{p_{1},\kappa}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m},\kappa}(w_{m})
with
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})\in A_{\vec{P}}
and
0<\kappa<1
. As was pointed out in
[
11
]
, by linearity it is enough to consider the multilinear commutator
[\Sigma b,T_{\theta}]
with only one symbol. Without loss of generality, we fix
b\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and then consider the operator

\big[b,T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(\vec{f})(x)=b(x)\cdot T_{\theta}(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})(x)-T_{\theta}(bf_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})(x).

For each fixed ball
B=B(x_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is enough to prove that

\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|\big[b,T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.

(5.4)

As before, we decompose
f_{i}
as

f_{i}=f^{0}_{i}+f^{\infty}_{0}
,
where
f^{i}_{0}=f_{i}\cdot\chi_{2B}
and
f^{\infty}_{i}=f_{i}\cdot\chi_{(2B)^{\complement}}
,
i=1,2,\dots,m
. We set
tB=B(x_{0},tr)
for any
t>0
. Let
\mathfrak{L}
be the same as before. By using Lemma
4.1
(
N=2^{m}
), we can write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|\big[b,T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|\big[b,T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(f^{0}_{1},\dots,f^{0}_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}}\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|\big[b,T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(f^{\beta_{1}}_{1},\ldots,f^{\beta_{m}}_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle:=J^{0,\dots,0}+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}}J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}.

(5.5)

To estimate the first summand of (
5
), applying Theorem
1.3
along with (
3.2
) and (
4.4
), we get

\displaystyle J^{0,\dots,0}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg(\int_{2B}|f_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}}w_{i}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\cdot\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}(2B)^{\kappa/{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\cdot\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2B)^{\kappa/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.

(5.6)

To estimate the remaining terms in (
5
), let us first consider the case when
\beta_{1}=\cdots=\beta_{m}=\infty
. It is easy to see that for any
x\in B
,

\big[b,T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(\vec{f})(x)=[b(x)-b_{B}]\cdot T_{\theta}(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})(x)-T_{\theta}((b-b_{B})f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})(x).

Hence, we divide the term
J^{\infty,\dots,\infty}
into two parts below.

\begin{split}J^{\infty,\dots,\infty}&\leq C\cdot\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|[b(x)-b_{B}]\cdot T_{\theta}(f^{\infty}_{1},f^{\infty}_{2},\dots,f^{\infty}_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&+C\cdot\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|T_{\theta}((b-b_{B})f^{\infty}_{1},f^{\infty}_{2},\dots,f^{\infty}_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&:=J^{\infty,\dots,\infty}_{\star}+J^{\infty,\dots,\infty}_{\star\star}.\end{split}

Next, we estimate each term separately. In the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we have already shown that (see (
4
))

\big|T_{\theta}(f^{\infty}_{1},f^{\infty}_{2},\ldots,f^{\infty}_{m})(x)\big|\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg).

Note that
\nu_{\vec{w}}\in A_{mp}\subset A_{\infty}
. From Lemma
5.1
(2), it follows that

\begin{split}J^{\infty,\dots,\infty}_{\star}&\lesssim\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg)\\
&\times\bigg(\int_{B}\big|b(x)-b_{B}\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{1/{p}-\kappa/p}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg).\end{split}

We then follow the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
to get

\displaystyle J^{\infty,\dots,\infty}_{\star}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.

(5.7)

Using the same methods as in Theorem
2.1
, we can also deduce that

\begin{split}&\big|T_{\theta}((b-b_{B})f^{\infty}_{1},f^{\infty}_{2},\dots,f^{\infty}_{m})(x)\big|\\
&\lesssim\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}\backslash(2B)^{m}}\frac{|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\cdot|f_{2}(y_{2})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})|}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn}}\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{(2^{j+1}B)^{m}\backslash(2^{j}B)^{m}}\frac{|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\cdot|f_{2}(y_{2})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})|}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn}}\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\int_{(2^{j+1}B)^{m}\backslash(2^{j}B)^{m}}|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\cdot\big|f_{2}(y_{2})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})\big|\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\bigg)\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\int_{2^{j+1}B}|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\,dy_{1}\prod_{i=2}^{m}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg)\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\,dy_{1}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{i=2}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg).\end{split}

Then we have

\begin{split}J^{\infty,\dots,\infty}_{\star\star}&\lesssim\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}\\
&\times\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\,dy_{1}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{i=2}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg).\end{split}

(5.8)

For each
2\leq i\leq m
, by using Hölder’s inequality with exponent
p_{i}
, we obtain that

\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\leq\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|^{p_{i}}w_{i}(y_{i})\,dy_{i}\bigg)^{1/{p_{i}}}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w_{i}(y_{i})^{-p^{\prime}_{i}/{p_{i}}}\,dy_{i}\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}_{i}}}.

According to Lemma
3.1
, we have
w_{i}^{1-p^{\prime}_{i}}=w_{i}^{-p^{\prime}_{i}/{p_{i}}}\in A_{mp^{\prime}_{i}}\subset A_{\infty}
,
i=1,2,\dots,m
. By using Hölder’s inequality again with exponent
p_{1}
and (
5.1
), we deduce that

\begin{split}&\int_{2^{j+1}B}|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\,dy_{1}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{1}(y_{1})\big|^{p_{1}}w_{1}(y_{1})\,dy_{1}\bigg)^{1/{p_{1}}}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}|b(y_{1})-b_{B}|^{p^{\prime}_{1}}w_{1}(y_{1})^{-p^{\prime}_{1}/{p_{1}}}\,dy_{1}\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}_{1}}}\\
&\lesssim\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{1}(y_{1})\big|^{p_{1}}w_{1}(y_{1})\,dy_{1}\bigg)^{1/{p_{1}}}(j+1)\|b\|_{*}\cdot\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w_{1}(y_{1})^{-p^{\prime}_{1}/{p_{1}}}\,dy_{1}\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}_{1}}},\end{split}

where the last inequality is valid by the fact that
w_{1}^{-p^{\prime}_{1}/{p_{1}}}\in A_{\infty}
. Substituting the above two estimates into the formula (
5.8
), we have

\begin{split}J^{\infty,\dots,\infty}_{\star\star}&\lesssim\|b\|_{*}\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(j+1)\bigg\{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|^{p_{i}}w_{i}(y_{i})\,dy_{i}\bigg)^{1/{p_{i}}}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w_{i}(y_{i})^{-p^{\prime}_{i}/{p_{i}}}\,dy_{i}\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}_{i}}}\bigg\}\\
&\lesssim\|b\|_{*}\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(j+1)\bigg\{\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{1/p}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg(\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}w_{i}(2^{j+1}B)^{\kappa/{p_{i}}}\bigg)\bigg\}\\
&\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(j+1)\cdot\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}},\end{split}

where in the last two inequalities we have used the
A_{\vec{P}}
condition and (
4.4
). Moreover, in view of (
4.7
)(since
\nu_{\vec{w}}\in A_{mp}
with
1<mp<+\infty
), the last expression is bounded by

\displaystyle\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(j+1)\cdot\left(\frac{|B|}{|2^{j+1}B|}\right)^{\delta{(1-\kappa)}/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})},

(5.9)

where the last series is convergent since the exponent
\delta{(1-\kappa)}/p
is positive. Consequently, combining the inequality (
5
) with (
5
), we get

J^{\infty,\dots,\infty}\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.

We now consider the case where exactly
\ell
of the
\beta_{i}
are
\infty
for some
1\leq\ell<m
. We only give the arguments for one of these cases. The rest are similar and can be easily obtained from the arguments below by permuting the indices. Meanwhile, we consider only the case
\beta_{1}=\infty
here since the other case can be proved in the same way. We now estimate the term
\big|\big[b,T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(f^{\beta_{1}}_{1},\ldots,f^{\beta_{m}}_{m})(x)\big|
when

\beta_{1}=\cdots=\beta_{\ell}=\infty\quad\&\quad\beta_{\ell+1}=\cdots=\beta_{m}=0.

In our present situation, we first divide the term
J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}
into two parts as follows.

\begin{split}J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}&\leq C\cdot\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|[b(x)-b_{B}]\cdot T_{\theta}(f^{\infty}_{1},\ldots,f^{\infty}_{\ell},f^{0}_{\ell+1},\ldots,f^{0}_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&+C\cdot\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(\int_{B}\big|T_{\theta}((b-b_{B})f^{\infty}_{1},\ldots,f^{\infty}_{\ell},f^{0}_{\ell+1},\ldots,f^{0}_{m})(x)\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&:=J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\star}+J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\star\star}.\end{split}

Next, we estimate each term respectively. Recall that the following result has been proved in Theorem
2.1
(see (
4
)).

\big|T_{\theta}(f^{\infty}_{1},\ldots,f^{\infty}_{\ell},f^{0}_{\ell+1},\ldots,f^{0}_{m})(x)\big|\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg).

From Lemma
5.1
(2), it then follows that

\begin{split}J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\star}&\lesssim\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg)\\
&\times\bigg(\int_{B}\big|b(x)-b_{B}\big|^{p}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{1/{p}-\kappa/p}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg).\end{split}

We now proceed exactly as we did in the proof of Theorem
2.1
to obtain that

\displaystyle J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\star}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.

(5.10)

On the other hand, by adopting the same method given in Theorem
2.1
, we can see that

\displaystyle\big|T_{\theta}((b-b_{B})f^{\infty}_{1},\ldots,f^{\infty}_{\ell},f^{0}_{\ell+1},\ldots,f^{0}_{m})(x)\big|

(5.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\ell}\backslash(2B)^{\ell}}\int_{(2B)^{m-\ell}}\frac{|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\cdot|f_{2}(y_{2})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})|}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=\ell+1}^{m}\int_{2B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\int_{(2^{j+1}B)^{\ell}\backslash(2^{j}B)^{\ell}}|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\cdot\big|f_{2}(y_{2})\cdots f_{\ell}(y_{\ell})\big|\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=\ell+1}^{m}\int_{2B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\int_{2^{j+1}B}|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\,dy_{1}\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\int_{2^{j+1}B}|(b(y_{1})-b_{B})f_{1}(y_{1})|\,dy_{1}\prod_{i=2}^{m}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|f_{i}(y_{i})\big|\,dy_{i}\bigg),

where in the last inequality we have used the inclusion relation
2B\subseteq 2^{j+1}B
with
j\in\mathbb{N}
. For the same reason as above, we get the desired estimate.

\displaystyle J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\star\star}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(j+1)\cdot\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{{(1-\kappa)}/p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.

(5.12)

Combining (
5
) and (
5
), we conclude that

J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(w_{i})}.

Summarizing the estimates derived above, then (
5.4
) holds and hence the proof of Theorem
2.3
is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Given
\vec{f}=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})
, for any fixed ball
B=B(x_{0},r)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as before, we decompose each

,

. Again, we only consider here the multilinear commutator with only one symbol by linearity; that is, fix

and consider the operator

\big[b,T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(\vec{f})(x)=b(x)\cdot T_{\theta}(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})(x)-T_{\theta}(bf_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})(x).

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be the same as before. Then for any given

, by using Lemma

N=2^{m}
), one can write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\cdot\Big[\nu_{\vec{w}}\big(\big\{x\in B:\big|\big[{b},T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(\vec{f})(x)\big|>\lambda^{m}\big\}\big)\Big]^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\cdot\Big[\nu_{\vec{w}}\big(\big\{x\in B:\big|\big[{b},T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(f^{0}_{1},\dots,f^{0}_{m})(x)\big|>\lambda^{m}/{2^{m}}\big\}\big)\Big]^{m}

\displaystyle+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}}\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\cdot\Big[\nu_{\vec{w}}\big(\big\{x\in B:\big|\big[{b},T_{\theta}\big]_{1}(f^{\beta_{1}}_{1},\ldots,f^{\beta_{m}}_{m})(x)\big|>\lambda^{m}/{2^{m}}\big\}\big)\Big]^{m}

\displaystyle:=J^{0,\dots,0}_{\ast}+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})\in\mathfrak{L}}J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\ast}.

Observe that the Young function
\Phi(t)=t\cdot(1+\log^{+}t)
satisfies the doubling condition, that is, there is a constant
C_{\Phi}>0
such that for every

,

\Phi(2t)\leq C_{\Phi}\,\Phi(t).

This fact together with Theorem
1.4
yields

\begin{split}J^{0,\dots,0}_{\ast}&\leq\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi\bigg(\frac{2|f^{0}_{i}(x)|}{\lambda}\bigg)\cdot w_{i}(x)\,dx\bigg)\\

&=\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}(2B)\bigg(\frac{1}{w_{i}(2B)}\int_{2B}\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f_{i}(x)|}{\lambda}\bigg)\cdot w_{i}(x)\,dx\bigg)\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}(2B)\cdot\bigg\|\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f_{i}|}{\lambda}\bigg)\bigg\|_{L\log L(w_{i}),2B},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used the estimate (
1.9
). Since
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in A_{(1,\dots,1)}
, by definition, we know that

\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{m}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}}w_{i}(x)

(5.13)

holds for any ball
\mathcal{B}

, where

. We can rewrite this inequality as

\begin{split}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)&\leq C\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}}w_{i}(x)\bigg)^{1/m}=C\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}}w_{i}(x)^{1/m}\bigg)\\
&\leq C\bigg(\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}(x)^{1/m}\bigg)=C\cdot\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x),\end{split}

which means that
\nu_{\vec{w}}\in A_{1}
. Moreover, for each
w_{i}
,
i=1,2,\dots,m
, it is easy to see that

\begin{split}\bigg(\prod_{j\neq i}\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}}w_{j}(x)^{1/{m}}\bigg)^{m}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\int_{\mathcal{B}}w_{i}(x)^{1/{m}}\,dx\bigg)^{m}&\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\int_{\mathcal{B}}w_{i}(x)^{1/{m}}\cdot\prod_{j\neq i}w_{j}(x)^{1/{m}}\,dx\bigg)^{m}\\

Also observe that

\bigg(\prod_{j\neq i}\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}}w_{j}(x)^{1/{m}}\bigg)^{m}=\prod_{j\neq i}\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}}w_{j}(x).

From this, it follows that

\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\int_{\mathcal{B}}w_{i}(x)^{1/{m}}\,dx\bigg)^{m}\leq C\cdot\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}}w_{i}(x),

which implies that

(
i=1,2,\dots,m
). Thus, by the inequality (
3.2
) and (
4.4
)(taking

and

), we have

\begin{split}J^{0,\dots,0}_{\ast}&\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg\|\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f_{i}|}{\lambda}\bigg)\bigg\|_{(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w_{i})}\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}(2B)^{\kappa}\\
&\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg\|\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f_{i}|}{\lambda}\bigg)\bigg\|_{(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w_{i})}\cdot\frac{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2B)^{m\kappa}}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\\
&\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg\|\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f_{i}|}{\lambda}\bigg)\bigg\|_{(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w_{i})}.\end{split}

It remains to estimate the term

. Recall that for any

\begin{split}J^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\ast}\leq&\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\Big[\nu_{\vec{w}}\Big(\Big\{x\in B:\big|[b(x)-b_{B}]\cdot T_{\theta}(f^{\beta_{1}}_{1},f^{\beta_{2}}_{2},\ldots,f^{\beta_{m}}_{m})(x)\big|>\lambda^{m}/{2^{m+1}}\Big\}\Big)\Big]^{m}\\
&+\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\Big[\nu_{\vec{w}}\Big(\Big\{x\in B:\big|T_{\theta}((b-b_{B})f^{\beta_{1}}_{1},f^{\beta_{2}}_{2},\dots,f^{\beta_{m}}_{m})(x)\big|>\lambda^{m}/{2^{m+1}}\Big\}\Big)\Big]^{m}\\

By using the previous pointwise estimates (

&\leq\frac{C}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m\kappa}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}\frac{|f_{i}(y_{i})|}{\lambda}\,dy_{i}\bigg)\\
&\times\bigg(\int_{B}\big|b(x)-b_{B}\big|^{\frac{\,1\,}{m}}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{m}.\end{split}

We claim that for

and

,

\bigg(\int_{B}\big|b(x)-b_{B}\big|^{\frac{\,1\,}{m}}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{m}\lesssim\|b\|_{*}\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m}.

\begin{split}\widetilde{J}^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\star}&\lesssim\|b\|_{*}\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m(1-\kappa)}\\

A further application of Hölder’s inequality leads to that

\begin{split}\int_{B}\big|b(x)-b_{B}\big|^{\frac{\,1\,}{m}}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)\,dx&\leq|B|\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\big|b(x)-b_{B}\big|^{s^{\prime}/m}\,dx\bigg)^{1/s^{\prime}}\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\nu_{\vec{w}}(x)^{s}\,dx\bigg)^{1/s}\\

\begin{split}&\int_{2^{j+1}B}\big|b(y_{1})-b_{B}\big|\cdot\frac{|f_{1}(y_{1})|}{\lambda}w_{1}(y_{1})\,dy_{1}\\

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}^{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}}_{\star\star}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{*}\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m(1-\kappa)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(j+1)\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}\big(2^{j+1}B\big)\bigg\|\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f_{i}|}{\lambda}\bigg)\bigg\|_{L\log L(w_{i}),2^{j+1}B}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{*}\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}}(B)^{m(1-\kappa)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(j+1)\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{w}}(2^{j+1}B)^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg\|\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f_{i}|}{\lambda}\bigg)\bigg\|_{(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w_{i})}\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}(2^{j+1}B)^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{*}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bigg\|\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f_{i}|}{\lambda}\bigg)\bigg\|_{(L\log L)^{1,\kappa}(w_{i})}.

(5.15)

where the last two inequalities follow from (

) and (

), we can see that

\begin{split}&\big[\Pi\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)\\
&=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big[b_{k}(x)-b_{k}(y_{k})\big]K(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{m})f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\\
&=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{e^{i\varphi_{k}}[b_{k}(x)-b_{k}(y_{k})]}\cdot e^{-i\varphi_{k}}d\varphi_{k}\bigg)\\
&\times K(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{m})f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\\
&=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}\bigg[\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{m}e^{e^{i\varphi_{k}}b_{k}(x)}\cdot e^{-i\varphi_{k}}\bigg)d\varphi_{1}\cdots d\varphi_{m}\bigg]\\
&\times K(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\prod_{k=1}^{m}e^{-e^{i\varphi_{k}}b_{k}(y_{k})}\cdot f_{k}(y_{k})\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}T_{\theta}\Big(e^{-e^{i\varphi_{1}}b_{1}}\cdot f_{1},\dots,e^{-e^{i\varphi_{m}}b_{m}}\cdot f_{m}\Big)(x)\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{m}e^{e^{i\varphi_{k}}b_{k}(x)}\cdot e^{-i\varphi_{k}}\bigg)d\varphi_{1}\cdots d\varphi_{m}.\end{split}

From this, it follows that

\begin{split}&\big|\big[\Pi\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)\big|\\
\leq&\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}\Big|T_{\theta}\Big(e^{-e^{i\varphi_{1}}b_{1}}\cdot f_{1},\dots,e^{-e^{i\varphi_{m}}b_{m}}\cdot f_{m}\Big)(x)\Big|\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{m}e^{\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}(x)}\bigg)d\varphi_{1}\cdots d\varphi_{m}.\end{split}

For any
(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m})\in[0,2\pi]^{m}
, define
m
-tuples

\vec{g}_{\varphi}=\big(g^{1}_{\varphi_{1}},\dots,g^{m}_{\varphi_{m}}\big),\quad\mbox{where}\;\;g^{k}_{\varphi_{k}}=e^{-e^{i\varphi_{k}}b_{k}}\cdot f_{k},\;\;k=1,2,\dots,m,

and define

\vec{w}_{\varphi}=\big(w^{1}_{\varphi_{1}},\dots,w^{m}_{\varphi_{m}}\big),\quad\mbox{where}\;\;w^{k}_{\varphi_{k}}=w_{k}\cdot e^{p_{k}\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}},\;\;k=1,2,\dots,m.

Set

\nu^{\ast}_{\vec{w}}=\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big(w^{k}_{\varphi_{k}}\big)^{p/{p_{k}}}.

Then we have

\nu^{\ast}_{\vec{w}}=\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big(w_{k}\cdot e^{p_{k}\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}}\big)^{p/{p_{k}}}=\nu_{\vec{w}}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{m}e^{p\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}}.

Using Minkowski’s inequality, we thus obtain

\begin{split}\Big\|\big[\Pi\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](\vec{f})\Big\|_{L^{p}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}&\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}\bigg\|T_{\theta}(\vec{g}_{\varphi})\prod_{k=1}^{m}e^{\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}d\varphi_{1}\cdots d\varphi_{m}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}\big\|T_{\theta}(\vec{g}_{\varphi})\big\|_{L^{p}(\nu^{\ast}_{\vec{w}})}d\varphi_{1}\cdots d\varphi_{m}.\end{split}

Since
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in A_{\vec{P}}
, we have
\nu_{\vec{w}}\in A_{mp}
and
w_{k}^{1-p^{\prime}_{k}}\in A_{mp^{\prime}_{k}}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,m
, by using Lemma
3.1
. Hence, by the self-improvement property of
A_{p}
weights (see
[
4
,
5
]
), there exist some positive numbers
\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{m}>0
such that

\nu_{\vec{w}}^{1+\varepsilon^{\prime}}\in A_{mp}\quad\&\quad\big(w_{k}^{1-p^{\prime}_{k}}\big)^{1+\varepsilon_{k}}\in A_{mp^{\prime}_{k}},~k=1,2,\dots,m.

Now choose

\varepsilon:=\min\big\{\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{m}\big\}.

Then we have

\nu_{\vec{w}}^{1+\varepsilon}\in A_{mp}\quad\&\quad\big(w_{k}^{1-p^{\prime}_{k}}\big)^{1+\varepsilon}=\big(w_{k}^{1+\varepsilon}\big)^{1-p^{\prime}_{k}}\in A_{mp^{\prime}_{k}},~k=1,2,\dots,m,

which implies
(\vec{w})^{1+\varepsilon}:=(w_{1}^{1+\varepsilon},\ldots,w_{m}^{1+\varepsilon})\in A_{\vec{P}}
by using Lemma
3.1
again.
Note that

\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big(w_{k}^{1+\varepsilon}\big)^{p/{p_{k}}}=\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}^{p/{p_{k}}}\bigg)^{1+\varepsilon}=(\nu_{\vec{w}})^{1+\varepsilon}.

Thus by Theorem
1.1
,

T_{\theta}:L^{p_{1}}(w_{1}^{1+\varepsilon})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(w_{m}^{1+\varepsilon})\longrightarrow L^{p}((\nu_{\vec{w}})^{1+\varepsilon}).

(6.2)

On the other hand, for any fixed
\eta>0
, it is known that when
b\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\|b\|_{*}<\min\{C_{2}/{\eta},C_{2}(p-1)/{\eta}\}
, where
C_{2}
is the constant in the John–Nirenberg inequality mentioned above, we have
e^{\eta b(x)}\in A_{p}
for
1<p<+\infty

(see
[
3
, Lemma 1]
). For
b_{k}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
1\leq k\leq m
), we now choose

\eta_{k}:=\frac{p_{k}(1+\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}.

For such
\eta_{k}>0
, we may assume that
\|b_{k}\|_{*}<\min\{C_{2}/{\eta_{k}},C_{2}(p_{k}-1)/{\eta_{k}}\}
. The general case can be proved using the linearity of
T_{\theta}
as well. Then for any
\varphi_{k}\in[0,2\pi]
, we have
\cos\varphi_{k}\cdot b_{k}(x)\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

\|\cos\varphi_{k}\cdot b_{k}\|_{*}\leq\|b_{k}\|_{*}<\min\big\{C_{2}/{\eta_{k}},C_{2}(p_{k}-1)/{\eta_{k}}\big\},

which implies that each
\nu_{k}(x):=e^{\eta_{k}\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}(x)}\in A_{p_{k}}
for
1<p_{k}<+\infty
,
k=1,2,\dots,m
. Notice that

\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big(e^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}p_{k}\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}}\big)^{p/{p_{k}}}=\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big(e^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}p\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}}\big)=\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{m}e^{p\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}}\bigg)^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}.

This fact along with (
4.17
) and Theorem
1.1
gives us that

T_{\theta}:L^{p_{1}}\big(e^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}p_{1}\cos\varphi_{1}b_{1}}\big)\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}\big(e^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}p_{m}\cos\varphi_{m}b_{m}}\big)\longrightarrow L^{p}\Big(\big(\prod_{k=1}^{m}e^{p\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}}\big)^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}\Big).

(6.3)

Interpolating between (
6.2
) and (
6.3
)(see
[
2
,
22
]
) we obtain that

T_{\theta}:L^{p_{1}}\big(w_{1}e^{p_{1}\cos\varphi_{1}b_{1}}\big)\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}\big(w_{m}e^{p_{m}\cos\varphi_{m}b_{m}}\big)\longrightarrow L^{p}\Big(\nu_{\vec{w}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}e^{p\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}}\Big),

that is

T_{\theta}:L^{p_{1}}\big(w^{1}_{\varphi_{1}}\big)\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}\big(w^{m}_{\varphi_{m}}\big)\longrightarrow L^{p}\big(\nu^{\ast}_{\vec{w}}\big).

(6.4)

By (
6.4
) we have

\big\|T_{\theta}(\vec{g}_{\varphi})\big\|_{L^{p}(\nu^{\ast}_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big\|g^{k}_{\varphi_{k}}\big\|_{L^{p_{k}}(w^{k}_{\varphi_{k}})}.

(6.5)

Since
f_{k}\in L^{p_{k}}(w_{k})
, it is easy to check that for any
\varphi_{k}\in[0,2\pi]
,

\begin{split}\big\|g^{k}_{\varphi_{k}}\big\|_{L^{p_{k}}(w^{k}_{\varphi_{k}})}&=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|g^{k}_{\varphi_{k}}(x)\big|^{p_{k}}w_{k}(x)\cdot e^{p_{k}\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}(x)}dx\bigg)^{1/{p_{k}}}\\
&=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|f_{k}(x)\big|^{p_{k}}e^{-p_{k}\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}(x)}\cdot w_{k}(x)\cdot e^{p_{k}\cos\varphi_{k}b_{k}(x)}dx\bigg)^{1/{p_{k}}}\\
&=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|f_{k}(x)\big|^{p_{k}}w_{k}(x)dx\bigg)^{1/{p_{k}}}=\big\|f_{k}\big\|_{L^{p_{k}}(w_{k})}.\end{split}

Therefore

\begin{split}\Big\|\big[\Pi\vec{b},T_{\theta}\big](\vec{f})\Big\|_{L^{p}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}&\leq C\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big\|g^{k}_{\varphi_{k}}\big\|_{L^{p_{k}}(w^{k}_{\varphi_{k}})}d\varphi_{1}\cdots d\varphi_{m}\\
&=C\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big\|f_{k}\big\|_{L^{p_{k}}(w_{k})}d\varphi_{1}\cdots d\varphi_{m}\\
&\leq C\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big\|f_{k}\big\|_{L^{p_{k}}(w_{k})},\end{split}

which is our desired estimate.

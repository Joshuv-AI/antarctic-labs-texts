Ill-posedness for the Camassa-Holm equation in B p , 1 1 ∩ C 0 , 1 B_{p,1}^{1}\cap C^{0,1}
Jinlu Li, Yanghai Yu, Yingying Guo, Weipeng Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem for the Camassa-Holm equation on the real line. By presenting a new construction of initial data, we show that the solution map in the smaller space
B_{p,1}^{1}\cap C^{0,1}
with
p\in(2,\infty]
is discontinuous at origin. More precisely,
u_{0}\in B_{p,1}^{1}\cap C^{0,1}
can guarantee that the Camassa-Holm equation has a unique local solution in
W^{1,p}\cap C^{0,1}
, however, this solution is instable and can have an inflation in
B_{p,1}^{1}\cap C^{0,1}
for certain initial data.

Keywords:
Camassa-Holm equation; Ill-posedness; Besov space.

MSC (2010):
35Q53, 37K10.

1
Introduction

In this paper we are concerned with the Cauchy problem for the classical Camassa-Holm (CH) equation

\begin{cases}u_{t}-u_{xxt}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx},\\
u(x,t=0)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the scalar function
u=u(t,x)
stands for the fluid velocity at time
t\geq 0
in the
x
direction.
We can transform the CH equation equivalently into the following transport type equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u=-\partial_{x}\big(1-\partial^{2}_{x}\big)^{-1}\Big(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\Big),\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.2)

The CH equation was firstly proposed in the context of hereditary symmetries studied in
[
16
]
and then was derived explicitly as a water wave equation by Camassa-Holm
[
3
]
. Many aspects of the mathematical beauty of the CH equation have been exposed over the last two decades. Particularly, (CH) is completely integrable
[
3
,
6
]
with a bi-Hamiltonian structure
[
5
,
16
]
and infinitely many conservation laws
[
3
,
16
]
. Also, it admits exact peaked
soliton solutions (peakons) of the form

u(x,t)=ce^{-|x-ct|},\;c>0,

which are orbitally stable
[
13
]
. Another remarkable feature of the CH equation is the wave breaking phenomena: the solution remains bounded while its slope becomes unbounded in finite time
[
4
,
9
,
10
]
. It is worth mentioning that the peaked solitons present the characteristic for the travelling water waves of greatest height and largest amplitude and arise as solutions to the free-boundary problem for incompressible Euler equations over a flat bed, see Refs.
[
7
,
11
,
12
,
31
]
for the details.

Due to these interesting and remarkable features, the CH equation has attracted much attention as a class of integrable shallow water wave equations in recent twenty years. Its systematic mathematical study was initiated in a series of papers by Constantin and Escher, see
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. After the CH equation was derived physically in the context of water waves, there are
a large amount of literatures devoted to studying the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) (see
Molinet’s survey
[
29
]
). Particularly, the continuous dependence is rather important when PDEs are used to model
phenomena in the natural world since measurements are always associated with errors. Next we recall some progresses in this field.

Well-posedness.
Li and Olver
[
28
]
proved that the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is locally well-posed with the initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>3/2
(see also
[
30
]
). Danchin
[
14
]
proved
the local existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to (
1.1
) with initial data in
B^{s}_{p,r}
for
s>\max\{1+1/p,3/2\}
with
p\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in[1,\infty)
. Meanwhile, he
[
14
]
only obtained the continuity of the solution map of (
1.1
) with respect to the initial data in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s^{\prime}}_{p,r})
with any
s^{\prime}<s
. Li-Yin
[
26
]
proved the continuity of the solution map of (
1.1
) with respect to the initial data in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
with
r<\infty
. In particular, they
[
26
]
proved that the solution map of (
1.1
) is weak continuous with respect to initial data
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,\infty}
. For the endpoints, Danchin
[
15
]
obtained the local well-posedness in the space
B^{3/2}_{2,1}
. Recently, Ye-Yin-Guo
[
33
]
proved the uniqueness and continuous dependence of the Camassa-Holm type equations in critical Besov spaces
B^{1+1/p}_{p,1}
with
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Ill-posedness.
When considering further continuous dependence, we proved the non-uniform dependence on initial data for (
1.1
) under both the framework of Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}
for
s>\max\big\{1+1/p,3/2\big\}
with
p\in[1,\infty],r\in[1,\infty)
and
B^{3/2}_{2,1}
in
[
24
,
25
]
(see
[
21
,
19
,
20
]
for earlier results in
H^{s}
). Danchin
[
15
]
obtained the ill-posedness of (
1.1
) in
B^{3/2}_{2,\infty}
(the data-to-solution map is not continuous by using peakon solution). Byers
[
2
]
proved that the Camassa-Holm equation is ill-posed in
H^{s}
for
s<3/2
in the sense of norm inflation, which means that
H^{3/2}
is the critical Sobolev space for well-posedness. Moreover, Guo-Liu-Molinet-Yin
[
18
]
showed the ill-posedness of (
1.1
) in
B_{p,r}^{1+1/p}(\mathbb{R}\;\text{or}\;\mathbb{T})
with
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]\times(1,\infty]
(especially in
H^{3/2}
) by proving the norm inflation. In our recent paper
[
27
]
, we established the ill-posedness for (
1.1
) in
B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
by proving the solution map to the CH equation starting from
u_{0}
is discontinuous at
t=0
in the metric of
B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Very recently, Guo-Ye-Yin
[
17
]
obtained the ill-posedness for the CH equation in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
by proving the norm inflation.

Now we would like to point out the idea that motivate the current work, which is inspired by Wang
[
32
]
who proved ill-posedness for the 3D Navier-Stokes equation in critical Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{-1}_{\infty,q}
in the sense of norm inflation. For arbitrarily small
\delta>0
, Wang
[
32
]
showed that the solution map
\delta u_{0}\rightarrow u
of the 3D Navier-Stokes equation in critical Besov spaces
\dot{B}_{\infty,q}^{-1}
with any
q\in[1,2]
is discontinuous at origin. Koch-Tataru
[
23
]
proved that the 3D Navier-Stokes equation is globally well-posed for small data in
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
. Due to the embedding
\dot{B}_{\infty,q}^{-1}\subset\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}(q\leqslant 2)
, then for sufficiently small
\delta>0,u_{0}\in\dot{B}_{\infty,q}^{-1}(q\leqslant 2)
can guarantee that the 3D Navier-Stokes equation has a unique global solution in
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
. However, Wang
[
32
]
obtained that this solution is instable in
\dot{B}_{\infty,q}^{-1}
and the solution can have an inflation in
\dot{B}_{\infty,q}^{-1}
by constructing a special initial data. For the CH equation, we should note that, the solution map in
W^{1,p}\cap C^{0,1}
is continuous at origin since the following estimate

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}\cap C^{0,1}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,p}\cap C^{0,1}}\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|_{C^{0,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\right).

Taking notice of the inclusion
B^{1}_{p,1}\hookrightarrow W^{1,p}
, it seems natural to conjecture that the solution map of the CH equation in
B_{p,1}^{1}\cap C^{0,1}
is also continuous at origin. However, we shall show the following negative result in this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty]
. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
large enough there exists
u_{0}
with

\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{p,1}\cap C^{0,1}}\leq\frac{1}{\log\log n},

such that if we denote by
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,1];H^{3})
, the solution of the Camassa-Holm equation with initial data
u_{0}
, then

\|u(t_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{p,1}}\geq{\log\log n},

with
t_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{\log n}]
.

Remark 1.1
.

Theorem
1.1
demonstrates the ill-posedness of the Camassa-Holm equation in
B^{1}_{p,1}\cap C^{0,1}
in the sense of that Hadamard (the norm inflation implies discontinuity with respect to the initial data).

Remark 1.2
.

Compared with the recent result by Guo-Ye-Yin
[
17
]
who showed ill-posedness of the Camassa-Holm equation in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}
, Theorem
1.1
seems more general owing to
B^{1}_{\infty,1}\hookrightarrow C^{0,1}
. We should also mention that our result is not just a little bit of improvement. The key point is that, our construction of initial data is different essentially from that in
[
17
]
since their initial data is no longer valid when proving the norm inflation in
B^{1}_{p,1}
if
p\neq\infty
.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we list some notations and known results and recall some Lemmas which will be used in the sequel. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.2
Littlewood-Paley analysis

Next, we will recall some facts about the Littlewood-Paley decomposition, the nonhomogeneous Besov spaces and their some useful properties (see
[
1
]
for more details).

Let
\mathcal{B}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:|\xi|\leq 4/3\}
and
\mathcal{C}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:3/4\leq|\xi|\leq 8/3\}.

Choose a radial, non-negative, smooth function
\chi:\mathbb{R}\mapsto[0,1]
such that it is supported in
\mathcal{B}
and
\chi\equiv 1
for
|\xi|\leq 3/4
. Setting
\varphi(\xi):=\chi(\xi/2)-\chi(\xi)
, then we deduce that
\varphi
is supported in
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\chi(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\mbox{ for any }\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

We should emphasize that the fact
\varphi(\xi)\equiv 1
for
4/3\leq|\xi|\leq 3/2
will be used in the sequel.

For every
u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
, the inhomogeneous dyadic blocks
{\Delta}_{j}
are defined as follows

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle 0,

if
j\leq-2
;

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle\chi(D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi\mathcal{F}u),

if
j=-1
;

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\mathcal{F}u\big),

if
j\geq 0
.

In the inhomogeneous case, the following Littlewood-Paley decomposition makes sense

u=\sum_{j\geq-1}{\Delta}_{j}u\quad\text{for any}\;u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}).

Definition 2.1

(see
[
1
]
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. The nonhomogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\displaystyle B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}):=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}):\;\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty\Big\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{sjr}\|\Delta_{j}f\|^{r}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},

if
1\leq r<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}=

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq-1}2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})},

if
r=\infty
.

The following Bernstein’s inequalities will be used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1

(see Lemma 2.1 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Ball and
\mathcal{C}
be an annulus. There exist constants
C>0
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, any positive real number
\lambda
and any function
f\in L^{p}
with
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm{supp}}\hat{f}\subset\lambda\mathcal{B}\;\Rightarrow\;\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C^{k+1}\lambda^{k+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|f\|_{L^{p}},

\displaystyle{\rm{supp}}\hat{f}\subset\lambda\mathcal{C}\;\Rightarrow\;C^{-k-1}\lambda^{k}\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C^{k+1}\lambda^{k}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

Lemma 2.2

(see Lemma 2.100 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
1\leq r\leq\infty
,
1\leq p\leq p_{1}\leq\infty
and
\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{1}}
. There exists a constant
C
depending continuously on
p,p_{1}
, such that

\left\|\left(2^{j}\left\|[\Delta_{j},v\partial_{x}]f\right\|_{L^{p}}\right)_{j}\right\|_{\ell^{r}}\leq C\left(\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{B_{p,r}^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\|\partial_{x}v\|_{B_{p_{1},r}^{0}}\right).

Finally, let us end this section with the key estimates.

Lemma 2.3

(see Lemma 3.26 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
u_{0}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Assume that
\partial_{x}u\in L^{1}([0,T];L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))
solves (
1.2
).
Then there exists a universal constant
C
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})}\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{x}u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}\tau\right).

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we shall present details for the proof of the main theorem.

3.1
Construction of Initial Data

Define a smooth cut-off function
\chi
with values in
[0,1]
which satisfies

\displaystyle\chi(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,\quad\mathrm{if}\ |\xi|\leq\frac{1}{4},\\
0,\quad\mathrm{if}\ |\xi|\geq\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

From now on, we set
\gamma:=\frac{17}{24}
just for the sake of simplicity. Letting

n\in 16\mathbb{N}=\left\{16,32,48,\cdots\right\}

and

\mathbb{N}(n)=\left\{k\in 8\mathbb{N}:\frac{n}{4}\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}\right\},

we can define the initial data
u_{0}
by

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=2^{-n}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left(\cos(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\cdot(2^{n}\gamma+2^{\ell}\gamma)+\cos(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\cdot(2^{n}\gamma-2^{\ell}\gamma)\right)\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty]
.
There exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C2^{-n}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n,

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{p,1}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\log n.

Proof.
Notice that the simple fact
\cos(a)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\cos(a)+\cos(-a)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\big(e^{ia}+e^{-ia}\big)
, then by easy computations, we give that

\displaystyle u_{0}

\displaystyle=2^{-n-1}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left({\Phi^{++}_{\ell}}+{\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}}+{\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}}+{\Phi^{--}_{\ell}}\right),

where

\displaystyle\Phi^{++}_{\ell}=e^{i(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\cdot(2^{n}\gamma+2^{\ell}\gamma)}\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma),

\displaystyle\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}=e^{i(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\cdot(2^{n}\gamma-2^{\ell}\gamma)}\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma),

\displaystyle\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}=e^{i(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\cdot(-2^{n}\gamma+2^{\ell}\gamma)}\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma),

\displaystyle\Phi^{--}_{\ell}=e^{i(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\cdot(-2^{n}\gamma-2^{\ell}\gamma)}\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma).

It is easy to see that its Fourier transform

\displaystyle\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)=2^{-n-1}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left(\widehat{\Phi^{++}_{\ell}}+\widehat{\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}}+\widehat{\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}}+\widehat{\Phi^{--}_{\ell}}\right),

where

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi^{++}_{\ell}}=e^{i2^{\ell+1}\xi\cdot\gamma}\chi(\xi-2^{n}\gamma-2^{\ell}\gamma),

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}}=e^{i2^{\ell+1}\xi\cdot\gamma}\chi(\xi-2^{n}\gamma+2^{\ell}\gamma),

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}}=e^{i2^{\ell+1}\xi\cdot\gamma}\chi(\xi+2^{n}\gamma-2^{\ell}\gamma),

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi^{--}_{\ell}}=e^{i2^{\ell+1}\xi\cdot\gamma}\chi(\xi+2^{n}\gamma+2^{\ell}\gamma),

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{u_{0}}\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\ 2^{n}\gamma-2^{\ell}\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{n}\gamma+2^{\ell}\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

Since
\check{\chi}
is a Schwartz function, we have

\displaystyle|\check{\chi}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-M},\qquad M\gg 1,

then

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-n}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\left\|\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-n}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\left\|\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\frac{1}{(1+|x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma|)^{M}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-n}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n,

and

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-n}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-n}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{(1+|x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma|)^{M}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-n}n^{\frac{1}{2}}\log n.

Due to the fact
\Delta_{j}u_{0}=u_{0}
if
j=n-1
and
\Delta_{j}u_{0}=0
if
j\neq n-1
, using the classical interpolation inequality
L^{p}=[L^{1},L^{\infty}]_{(1/p,(p-1)/p)}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{p,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{n}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\log n.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty]
.
There exists a positive constant
c
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\left\|(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}\right\|_{B^{0}_{p,1}\left(\mathbb{N}(n)\right)}\geq c\log^{2}n,\qquad n\gg 1.

Proof.
Since
\mathrm{supp}\ \chi(\cdot-a)*\chi(\cdot-b)\subset B(a+b,1)
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{\nu\lambda}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{\nu\mu}_{m}}\subset B(\nu 2^{n+1}\gamma,2^{2+\frac{n}{2}}),\quad\lambda,\mu,\nu\in\{+,-\}.

Moreover, noticing that

\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{++}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{--}_{m}}\subset B((2^{\ell}-2^{m})\gamma,1),

we see that

\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{++}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{--}_{\ell}}\subset B(0,1).

It follows that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}(n)
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}[(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}]=2^{-2n-1}n^{-1}\log^{2}n\cdot\Delta_{j}U.

(3.3)

where

U:=\sum_{\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\Big(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{m}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{m}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{m}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{m}\Big).

We rewrite
U
as

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\Big(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{\ell}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\sum_{\ell<m\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\Big(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{m}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{m}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{m}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{m}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\sum_{\ell>m\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\Big(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{m}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{m}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{m}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{m}\Big)

\displaystyle:=U_{1}+U_{2}+U_{3}.

To deal with term
U_{1}
, using the facts that

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{++}_{j}}*\widehat{\Phi^{--}_{j}}\subset B(0,1),

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{+-}_{j}}*\widehat{\Phi^{-+}_{j}}\subset B(0,1),

thus, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}(n)
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{j}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{j})=0.

(3.4)

Note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{++}_{j}}*\widehat{\Phi^{-+}_{j}}\subset B(2^{j+1}\gamma,1)\subset 2^{j}\mathcal{C}\left(0,4/3,3/2\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{+-}_{j}}*\widehat{\Phi^{--}_{j}}\subset B(2^{j+1}\gamma,1)\subset 2^{j}\mathcal{C}\left(0,4/3,3/2\right),

and

\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)\equiv 1
for
\xi\in 2^{j}\mathcal{C}\left(0,4/3,3/2\right),

we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{j}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{j})=\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{j}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{j}.

(3.5)

(
3.4
) and (
3.5
) gives that

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}U_{1}=\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{j}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{j}.

(3.6)

To deal with term
U_{2}
, we have if
j<m\in\mathbb{N}(n)

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{++}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{-+}_{m}}\subset B((2^{\ell}+2^{m})\gamma,1)\subset 2^{j}\mathcal{C}(0,3,2^{N}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{++}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{--}_{m}}\subset B((2^{\ell}-2^{m})\gamma,1)\subset 2^{j}\mathcal{C}(0,3,2^{N}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{-+}_{m}}\subset B((-2^{\ell}+2^{m})\gamma,1)\subset 2^{j}\mathcal{C}(0,3,2^{N}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{--}_{m}}\subset B((-2^{\ell}-2^{m})\gamma,1)\subset 2^{j}\mathcal{C}(0,3,2^{N}),

and if
j>m\in\mathbb{N}(n)

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{++}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{-+}_{m}}\subset B((2^{\ell}+2^{m})\gamma,1)\subset 2^{j}B(0,1/2),

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{++}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{--}_{m}}\subset B((2^{\ell}-2^{m})\gamma,1)\subset 2^{j}B(0,1/2),

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{-+}_{m}}\subset B((-2^{\ell}+2^{m})\gamma,1)\subset 2^{j}B(0,1/2),

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}}*\widehat{\Phi^{--}_{m}}\subset B((-2^{\ell}-2^{m})\gamma,1)\subset 2^{j}B(0,1/2).

Thus,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}U_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\Big(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{j}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{j}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{j}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{j}\Big).

(3.7)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}U_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\Big(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{\ell}\Big).

(3.8)

Therefore, plugging (
3.6
)-(
3.8
) into (
3.3
), we have

\Delta_{j}[(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}]:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}

where

I_{1}=2^{-2n-1}n^{-1}\log^{2}n\cdot(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{j}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{j})

and

I_{2}=2^{-2n-1}n^{-1}\log^{2}n\cdot\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{j})

I_{3}=2^{-2n-1}n^{-1}\log^{2}n\cdot\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{j})

I_{4}=2^{-2n-1}n^{-1}\log^{2}n\cdot\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}(\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{j})

I_{5}=2^{-2n-1}n^{-1}\log^{2}n\cdot\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}(\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{+-}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{--}_{j}).

We will show that the term
I_{1}
contributes the main part and the other terms are much less than the first one.
By direct computations give that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=n^{-1}\log^{2}n\left(I_{1,1}+I_{1,2}+I_{1,3}\right).

where

\displaystyle I_{1,1}=(1-2^{2j-2n})\gamma^{2}\cdot\cos[(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)\cdot 2^{j+1}\gamma]\check{\chi}^{2}(x+2^{j+1}\gamma),

\displaystyle I_{1,2}=2^{-2n}\cdot\cos[(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)\cdot 2^{j+1}\gamma][\partial_{x}\check{\chi}(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)]^{2},

\displaystyle I_{1,3}=-2^{j-2n}\gamma\cdot\sin[(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)\cdot 2^{j+1}\gamma][(\check{\chi}\partial_{x}\check{\chi})(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)].

By Riemann’s lemma, we have for large enough
n
,

\displaystyle\|I_{1,1}\|_{L^{p}}=(1-2^{2j-2n})\gamma^{2}\left\|\cos\left((2^{j+1}\gamma)x\right)\check{\chi}^{2}(x)\right\|_{L^{p}}\geq C,

\displaystyle\|I_{1,2}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C2^{-2n},

\displaystyle\|I_{1,3}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C2^{j-2n}\leq C2^{-n}.

Thus, for large enough
n
, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\geq cn^{-1}\log^{2}n.

(3.9)

Since
\mathrm{supp}\ \Phi^{\mu,\nu}_{\ell}\subset 2^{n}\mathcal{C},\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n),\mu,\nu\in\{+,-\}
, by Bernstein’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-1}\log^{2}n2^{-2n}\left\|\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}(\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{j}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{++}_{\ell}\partial_{x}\Phi^{-+}_{j})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-1}\log^{2}n\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|(1+|x+2^{j+1}\gamma|^{2})^{-M}(1+|x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma|^{2})^{-M}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-1}\log^{2}n\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|(1+|x|^{2})^{-M}(1+|x-(2^{j+1}-2^{\ell+1})\gamma|^{2}))^{-M}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-1}\log^{2}n\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left(\gamma(2^{j}-2^{\ell})\right)^{-2M}

\displaystyle\leq C\log^{2}n2^{-\frac{nM}{2}},

where we have separated
\mathbb{R}
into two different regions
\{x:|x|\leq\gamma(2^{j}-2^{\ell})\}
and
\{x:|x|>\gamma(2^{j}-2^{\ell})\}
.

We easily see that

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\log^{2}n.

Using the classical interpolation inequality leads to

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|I_{2}\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{1}}\|I_{2}\|^{\frac{p-1}{p}}_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\log^{2}n2^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})nM}.

(3.10)

Applying similar argument as in (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\|I_{i}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\log^{2}n2^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})nM}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad i=3,4,5.

(3.11)

Combining (
3.9
)-(
3.11
), we deduce that for
n\gg 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta_{j}[(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}]\right\|_{L^{p}}\geq cn^{-1}\log^{2}n.

Therefore, by the definition of the Besov norm, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}\right\|_{B^{0}_{p,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}\geq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|\Delta_{j}[(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}]\right\|_{L^{p}}\geq c\log^{2}n.

This completes the proof of lemma
3.2
.

3.2
The Equation Along the Flow

Given a Lipschitz velocity field
u
, we may solve the following ODE to find the flow induced by
u
:

\displaystyle\quad\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\phi(t,x)=u(t,\phi(t,x)),\\
\phi(0,x)=x,\end{cases}

(3.12)

which is equivalent to the integral form

\displaystyle\phi(t,x)=x+\int^{t}_{0}u(\tau,\phi(\tau,x))\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.13)

Considering the equation

\displaystyle\quad\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+u\partial_{x}v=P,\\
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(3.14)

then, we get from (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\Delta_{j}v)+u\partial_{x}\Delta_{j}v

\displaystyle=R_{j}+\Delta_{j}P,

with
R_{j}=[u,\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}v=u\Delta_{j}\partial_{x}v-\Delta_{j}(u\partial_{x}v)
.

Due to (
3.12
), then

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left((\Delta_{j}v)\circ\phi\right)

\displaystyle=R_{j}\circ\phi+\Delta_{j}P\circ\phi,

which means that

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}v\circ\phi=\Delta_{j}v_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}R_{j}\circ\phi\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\Delta_{j}P\circ\phi\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.15)

3.3
Norm Inflation

For
n\gg 1
, using Lemma
2.1
, we have for
t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{0,1}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{C^{0,1}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log n,

and also

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\log n.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that there exists
t_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{\log n}]
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{B^{1}_{p,1}}\geq\log\log n.

(3.16)

We prove (
3.16
) by contraction. If (
3.16
) were not true, then

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,\frac{1}{\log n}]}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{B^{1}_{p,1}}<\log\log n.

(3.17)

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1: Lower bounds for
(\Delta_{j}u)\circ\phi

Now we consider the equation along the Lagrangian flow-map associated to
u
.
Utilizing (
3.15
) to (
1.2
) yields

\displaystyle(\Delta_{j}u)\circ\phi

\displaystyle=\Delta_{j}u_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}R^{1}_{j}\circ\phi\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\Delta_{j}F\circ\phi\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\big(\Delta_{j}E\circ\phi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}\big)\mathrm{d}\tau+t\Delta_{j}E_{0},

where

\displaystyle R^{1}_{j}=[u,\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}u,

\displaystyle F=-\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}u^{2},

\displaystyle E=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}.

Due to Lemma
3.2
, we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\|\Delta_{j}\partial_{x}E_{0}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq c\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\|\Delta_{j}(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq c\log^{2}n.

(3.18)

Notice that (
3.13
), then we have for
t\in(0,\frac{1}{\log n}]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq|\partial_{x}\phi|\leq 2,

thus for
p\in(2,\infty)

\displaystyle\|f(t,\phi(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{p}}\approx_{p}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f(t,\phi(t,x))\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Then, using the commutator estimate from Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|R^{1}_{j}\circ\phi\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|R^{1}_{j}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{B^{1}_{p,1}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log^{3}n.

(3.19)

Also, we have

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}F\circ\phi\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}F\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}})\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-1}n^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\log^{3}n,

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}F\circ\phi\|_{L^{p}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\log^{3}n.

(3.20)

Combining (
3.3
)-(
3.20
) and using Lemmas
3.1
-
3.2
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|(\Delta_{j}u)\circ\phi\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq t\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{p}}-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E\circ\phi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{p}}-Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}}\log^{3}n-C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{p,1}}

\displaystyle\geq ct\log^{2}n-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E\circ\phi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{p}}-Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}}\log n.

Step 2: Upper bounds for
\Delta_{j}E\circ\phi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}

By easy computations, we can see that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E+u\partial_{x}E=G,

(3.21)

where

\displaystyle G=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}u^{3}-\frac{1}{2}u(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}-(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{3}u^{3}-\frac{1}{2}u(\partial_{x}u)^{2}-\partial_{x}\Big(\partial_{x}u(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}u)^{2})\Big)\right).

Utilizing (
3.15
) to (
3.21
) yields

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}E\circ\phi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}=\int^{t}_{0}[u,\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}E\circ\phi\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\Delta_{j}G\circ\phi\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using the commutator estimate from Lemma
2.2
, one has

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|[u,\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}E\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|E\|_{B^{1}_{p,\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}E\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{B^{1}_{p,\infty}})\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{C^{0,1}}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}

and

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}G\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{C^{0,1}}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E\circ\phi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|u\|^{2}_{C^{0,1}}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq Cn^{-1}n^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\log^{3}n,

which leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E\circ\phi-\Delta_{j}E_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}}\log^{3}n.

Combining Step 1 and Step 2, then for
t=\frac{1}{\log n}
, we obtain for
n\gg 1

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{B^{1}_{p,1}}

\displaystyle\geq\|u(t)\|_{B^{1}_{p,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}

\displaystyle\geq C\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|(\Delta_{j}u)\circ\phi\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq ct\log^{2}n-Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}}\log n

\displaystyle\geq\log\log n,

which contradicts the hypothesis (
3.17
).

In conclusion, we obtain the norm inflation and hence the ill-posedness of the CH equation. Thus, Theorem
1.1
is proved.

Global theta lifting and automorphic periods associated to nilpotent orbits
Bryan Wang Peng Jun

Abstract
. A systematic way to organise the interesting periods of automorphic forms on a reductive group G G is via the theory of nilpotent orbits of G G . On the other hand, it is known that the theta correspondence can be used effectively to relate automorphic periods on each member of a dual pair. In this paper, we establish this relation in full generality, facilitated by a certain transfer of nilpotent orbits via moment maps. This is the analogous global result to the local result previously established by Gomez and Zhu.



1.
Introduction

Period integrals have long been of key interest in the study of automorphic forms, and more specifically also in the Langlands program. For instance, one of the central problems in the relative Langlands program is to characterize the non-vanishing of certain periods of automorphic forms as the image of certain Langlands functorial liftings, or by analytic properties of automorphic
L
-functions.

A systematic way to organise the possible periods that one is interested in is via the (Dynkin-Kostant) theory of nilpotent orbits for a reductive group
G
. For an introduction, one may refer to the notes of Jiang
[
Ji
]
. Much more recently, the work of Ben-Zvi, Sakellaridis and Venkatesh on the relative Langlands program
[
BZSV
]
has led to a far-reaching framework for the study of periods of automorphic forms, indexed by nilpotent orbits of
G
(among other data).

One of the main tools used to approach such problems involving automorphic periods is the theory of theta correspondence (the other being the relative trace formula). It is a known principle that theta correspondence often relates two periods on each member of a reductive dual pair. As such, the theta correspondence can often be used to great effect to establish instances of the characterization of non-vanishing of periods.

A natural question which arises is the following. Suppose
(G,G^{\prime})
is a reductive dual pair, and theta correspondence relates an automorphic period on
G
corresponding to a nilpotent orbit
\gamma
of
G
, with an automorphic period on
G^{\prime}
corresponding to a nilpotent orbit
\gamma^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
. Then can one understand the possible relations between
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
, so that one has a better understanding of the possible periods that can be related by the theta correspondence?

This was studied in the analogous local setting by Gomez and Zhu
[
GZ
]
, where they showed that
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
can be understood as being related by a certain natural transfer of nilpotent orbits via moment maps (Section
2.3
). It was further shown in
[
GW
]
that this moment map transfer has natural interpretations in the
[
BZSV
]
-framework indexing period problems by Hamiltonian spaces (which come equipped with moment maps).

The aim of this paper is to establish the analogous result of
[
GZ
]
(and its generalisation as given in
[
Zh
]
), in the global setting. Suppose
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are related by the moment map transfer of nilpotent orbits (as in Section
2.3
). Then we explicate the relation between the
\gamma
-period on a (cuspidal) automorphic representation
\pi
of
G
, and the
\gamma^{\prime}
-period of its global theta lifting
\Theta(\pi)
of
G^{\prime}
(Theorem
4.5
). This allows one to characterize the non-vanishing of the
\gamma
-period on
\pi
in terms of the non-vanishing of the
\gamma^{\prime}
-period on
\Theta(\pi)
(and vice versa).

We leave the formulation of the precise statements to the main body of the paper, with the main results summarised in Theorems
3.3
,
4.6
and
4.7
. The paper is organised as follows. We set up the necessary notation and preliminaries in Section
2
. We then compute the period integral of the relevant theta function in Section
3
, which is the heart of the computation. We complete the computation and obtain our main results in Section
4
. Finally, we illustrate some examples in Section
5
.

Let us finally remark that such global computations of periods of global theta lifts have been done multiple times in the literature, all in various special cases, and it would be impossible to give an exhaustive list of references here. We mention here only three such examples (again, there are many others).

•

[
GRS

, where the case corresponding, in the notation of this paper, to
(G,G^{\prime})=(\mathrm{Sp}_{2n},\mathrm{O}_{2n})
,

with partition
[2n-2,1,1]
, and
\gamma^{\prime}
with partition
[2n-1,1]
(the regular orbit), was considered (as well as several other similar cases).

•

\gamma

Remark 2.1
.

It is perhaps more common to work with the symplectic vector space

Remark 2.2

By the Jacobson-Morozov theorem (and other results of Kostant)

Under the adjoint

{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}=\{v\in{\mathfrak{g}}\mid{\rm ad}(h)v=jv\}

for

\mathfrak{p}=\oplus_{j\leq 0}{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{u},

We get corresponding subgroups

. Denote the centraliser of

which is reductive.

\chi_{\gamma,\psi_{v}}(\exp u):=\psi_{v}(\kappa(e,u)),\ \ \ \ \forall\ u\in\mathfrak{u}^{+}.

In the adelic setting, we thus have also a character

2.2.1.
Classification of nilpotent orbits for classical groups

Continue to suppose

.

From an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple as above, we obtain an

-representation on

V=\oplus_{j=1}^{l}W_{j}^{\oplus a_{j}},

where

is the isotypic component of

of

Recall, from standard

-invariant forms

on

The form

on the

is symplectic if

The centraliser

is in fact (canonically isomorphic to) the direct product of the isometry groups of

M_{\gamma}\cong\prod_{j=1}^{l}G(V^{j},B_{j}).

The above furnishes a parameterisation of the nilpotent orbits in

•

the partition

of

, and

•

the forms on the multiplicity spaces

such that the

In particular if

2.4.
Weil representation and Lagrangians

Write
\omega_{(l),(m)}
for the Weil representation associated to the dual pair
(G_{(l)},G^{\prime}_{(m)})
; it is realised on the space of Schwarz functions on a Lagrangian
Y_{(l),(m)}
of the symplectic vector space
\mathrm{Hom}(V_{(l)},V^{\prime}_{(m)})
.

Now let us choose Lagrangian subspaces once and for all in the following way. Choose a Lagrangian
Y_{(0),(0)}
of
\mathrm{Hom}(V_{(0)},V^{\prime}_{(0)})
. Then for each
0\leq j\leq r
, set

Y_{(j),(j+1)}=\mathrm{Hom}(V_{(j)},V^{\prime}_{j+1})\oplus Y_{(j),(j)}

and for each
1\leq j\leq r
, set

Y_{(j),(j)}=\mathrm{Hom}(V_{-j},V^{\prime}_{(j)})\oplus Y_{(j-1),(j)}.

It is clear that the Weil representation
\omega_{(r),(r+1)}
may be viewed as realised on the space of Schwarz functions on
\mathrm{Hom}(V_{(r)},V^{\prime}_{r+1})
taking values in
\omega_{(r),(r)}
. We record here some pertinent formulas for the Weil representation
\omega_{(r),(r+1)}
, to be used later in the global computation.

For the action of
G_{(r)}
, one has

(2.3)

(\omega_{(r),(r+1)}(g)\phi)(F)=\omega_{(r),(r)}(g)\phi(Fg)

for
g\in G_{(r)}
and
F\in\mathrm{Hom}(V_{(r)},V^{\prime}_{r+1})
.

For the action of
U^{\prime}_{r+1}
, recall from section
2.2.2
that one has the vector space decomposition

\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{r+1}\cong\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}_{r+1}\oplus\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}_{r+1}\cong\mathrm{Hom}(V^{\prime}_{r+1},V^{\prime}_{(r)})\oplus\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}_{r+1}

and
\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}_{r+1}\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}(V^{\prime}_{r+1},V^{\prime}_{-(r+1)})
.

Then one has

(2.4)

(\omega_{(r),(r+1)}(\exp z^{\prime})\phi)(F)=\psi(\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}z^{\prime}FF^{\ast}/2)\phi(F)

for
z^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}_{r+1}
and
F\in\mathrm{Hom}(V_{(r)},V^{\prime}_{r+1})
, and

(2.5)

(\omega_{(r),(r+1)}(\exp q^{\prime})\phi)(F)=\omega_{(r),(r)}(q^{\prime}F)\phi(F)

One also has

2.5.
Periods

Observe that

We thus obtain the Heisenberg-Weil representation

{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{-1}

Definition 2.4

P_{\gamma^{\prime},\psi}(f^{\prime},\phi_{\gamma^{\prime}},\tau^{\prime})=\int_{[L^{\prime}]}\int_{[U^{\prime}]}f^{\prime}(u^{\prime}l^{\prime})\cdot\overline{\theta_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\phi_{\gamma^{\prime}})(u^{\prime}l^{\prime})\cdot\tau^{\prime}(l^{\prime})}\mathop{du^{\prime}}\mathop{dl^{\prime}}

L,U

By

3.
Key computation

Let
\pi

G

\tau^{\prime}
a cusp form on
L^{\prime}

\phi\in\omega

\displaystyle P_{\gamma^{\prime},\psi}(\theta(\phi,f),\phi_{\gamma^{\prime}},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{[L^{\prime}]}\int_{[U^{\prime}]}\int_{[G]}\overline{f(g)}\cdot\theta(\phi)(gu^{\prime}l^{\prime})\cdot\overline{\theta_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\phi_{\gamma^{\prime}})(u^{\prime}l^{\prime})\cdot\tau^{\prime}(l^{\prime})}\mathop{dg}\mathop{du^{\prime}}\mathop{dl^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{[G]}\overline{f(g)}\int_{[L^{\prime}]}\int_{[U^{\prime}]}\theta(\phi)(gu^{\prime}l^{\prime})\cdot\overline{\theta_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\phi_{\gamma^{\prime}})(u^{\prime}l^{\prime})\cdot\tau^{\prime}(l^{\prime})}\mathop{du^{\prime}}\mathop{dl^{\prime}}\mathop{dg}

3.1.
Key computation

Hence, one sees that the key step to relate the above integral to a period integral of

(3.1)

\int_{U^{+\prime}(k)\backslash U^{+\prime}(\mathbb{A})}\overline{\chi_{\gamma^{\prime}}(u^{\prime})}\cdot\theta(\phi)(gu^{\prime})\mathop{du^{\prime}}\mathop{dg}

4.7.
Converse of Theorem
4.6

One would now like to show the converse of Theorem
4.6
.

It is instructive to focus first on the even case, that is, we want to show that if
P_{\gamma,\overline{\psi}}(\cdot)
is not identically zero on
\pi
, then
P_{\gamma^{\prime},\psi}(\theta(\cdot,\cdot))
is not identically zero on
\Theta(\pi)
.

First, one may certainly suppose
P_{\gamma,\overline{\psi}}(f)\neq 0
, that is,

g\mapsto P_{\gamma,\overline{\psi}}(g\cdot f)

is non-vanishing in a neighbourhood of the identity, and the aim is to show that one may choose
\Phi
such that the integral (
4.9
) is non-vanishing.

Now since
\phi
is an arbitrary Schwarz function, then analogously to
[
CG
, Proposition 7.1]
, the results of
[
GZ
, Proposition 6.5]
show that

\Phi:g\mapsto(\omega_{(r),(r+1)}(g)\phi)(f_{r})(f_{-r})\cdots(f_{1})(f_{-1})(f_{0})

is an arbitrary Schwarz function on
LU\backslash G
, at least in the local setting.

However, in the global setting, one does not immediately have the analogous conclusion to
[
CG
, Proposition 7.1]
(since
LU\backslash G
is not closed in
\mathrm{Hom}(V,V^{\prime})
). What is pertinent here is therefore the computation at the unramified places.

Let
S
be the finite set of places of
k
, including the Archimedean places, such that
f
and
\psi
are unramified outside of
S
. For each place
v\notin S
, we would like to choose
\phi_{v}
the characteristic function of
Y_{(r),(r+1)}(\mathfrak{o}_{k_{v}})
.

Recall (section
2.2
) that one has the parabolic subgroup
P=MU
of
G
. Furthermore one has, in the notation of section
2.2.2
,

M=M_{0}\times M_{1}\times\cdots\times M_{r}\cong G(V_{0})\times\mathrm{GL}(V_{-1})\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}(V_{-r}).

Accordingly, for each
m\in M
, we write
m=m_{0}m_{1}\cdots m_{r}
. Recall also (cf. section
2.3
) that
L=G(\mathrm{ker}f_{0})\subseteq G(V_{0})=M_{0}
.

For each place
v\notin S
, by the Iwasawa decomposition,
\Phi
is specified by its value on
L(k_{v})\backslash M(k_{v})
. Now combining (suitable analogues of) the formulas (
2.3
), (
2.6
) and (
3.4
) describing the action of the Weil representation, one readily sees that

(4.10)

\displaystyle(\omega_{(r),(r+1)}(m_{0}m_{1}\cdots m_{r})\phi_{v})(f_{r})(f_{-r})\cdots(f_{1})(f_{-1})(f_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu(m)\cdot\phi_{v}(f_{r}m_{r})(f_{-r}m_{r})\cdots(f_{1}m_{1})(f_{-1}m_{1})(f_{0}m_{0}).

Observe that, for each
1\leq j\leq r
, under the identification
M_{j}\cong\mathrm{GL}(V_{-j})
, the element
m_{j}
is effectively acting on
f_{j}\in\mathrm{Hom}(V_{j},V^{\prime}_{j+1})
by its matrix inverse (transpose), and acting on
f_{-j}\in\mathrm{Hom}(V_{-j},V^{\prime}_{-(j-1)})
by itself.

Furthermore we may WLOG assume we have chosen
f_{j}
and
f_{-j}
, and suitable bases, such that they are represented by identity matrices (or matrices with only 1s in some diagonal entries, and 0s in all other entries), cf.
[
Zh
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Finally, note also that, as in section
3.4
,
L
is the stabiliser of
f_{0}
under the
M_{0}
-action.

Since
\phi_{v}
is the characteristic function of
Y_{(r),(r+1)}(\mathfrak{o}_{k_{v}})
, one therefore sees immediately that
\Phi
, and hence the entire integrand, is supported on

L\backslash M_{0}(\mathfrak{o}_{k_{v}})\times\mathrm{GL}(V_{-1})(\mathfrak{o}_{k_{v}})\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}(V_{-r})(\mathfrak{o}_{k_{v}})

for
\mathfrak{o}_{k_{v}}
the ring of integers of
k_{v}
.

Therefore, with this choice of
\phi
and hence
\Phi
, analogously to
[
GS
, p.2719]
, the integral reduces to an integration over (groups with coefficients in)
\mathbb{A}_{S}
, and one may then use as above that
\Phi
is an arbitrary Schwarz function on
LU\backslash G
to complete the argument.

The argument in the general case is similar, since the main point is really the support of the Schwarz function, and hence the integrand, at the unramified places
v\not\in S
. For the remaining places
v\in S
, one may multiply
\Phi(g)
by an arbitrary Schwarz function on
LU\backslash G
. In summary, one has

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose
\gamma^{\prime}
is in the image of the moment map
\phi^{\prime}
. Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
G
and
\tau^{\prime}
a cusp form on
L^{\prime}
. If
P_{\gamma,\overline{\psi}}(\cdot,\cdot,\theta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}(\cdot,\tau^{\prime}))
is not identically zero on
\pi
, then
P_{\gamma^{\prime},\psi}(\theta(\cdot,\cdot),\cdot,\tau^{\prime})
is not identically zero on
\Theta(\pi)
.

5.
Examples

In this section, we give several examples to illustrate how the results above may be commonly used to relate certain well-known periods.

5.1.
Bessel / Fourier-Jacobi to Whittaker

Suppose
(G,G^{\prime})=(\mathrm{O}_{2n},\mathrm{Sp}_{2m})
with
n\geq m
, and
\gamma^{\prime}
corresponds to a regular nilpotent orbit in
G^{\prime}
, with corresponding partition
\lambda^{\prime}=[2m]
. Then
\gamma
has corresponding partition
\lambda=[2m-1,1^{2n-2m+1}]
, and we have
L=\mathrm{O}(V_{new})\cong\mathrm{O}_{2n-2m+1}
for the subspace
V_{new}\subseteq V
of dimension
2n-2m+1
. The
\gamma
-period
P_{\gamma}
on
\pi
corresponds to the Bessel period on
\pi

[
GGP
]
.

Therefore, in this case, we are relating the Bessel period on
\pi
to the Whittaker period on
\Theta(\pi)
. This is essentially the case considered in
[
GW
, Section 7]
, with several more very similar cases considered there as well. For example, one may similarly relate the Fourier-Jacobi period on
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}
to the Whittaker period on
\mathrm{O}_{2m}
.

5.2.
Bessel / Fourier-Jacobi to Bessel / Fourier-Jacobi

From the preceding example (cf. also
[
GW
, Section 7]
), we see that partitions of the general form
\lambda=[k,1^{a}]
(for
k>1
), or the so-called ‘hook-type’ partitions, correspond either to Bessel or Fourier-Jacobi periods.

It is easy to see that if
\gamma^{\prime}
is chosen to have ‘hook-type’ partition
\lambda^{\prime}=[k,1^{a}]
(and is in the image of the moment map
\phi^{\prime}
), then
\gamma
will also have ‘hook-type’ partition
\lambda=[k-1,1^{b}]
(for some
b
). If
k\geq 3
,
a\geq 2
and
b\geq 2
, then one sees that we are able to relate a Bessel / Fourier-Jacobi period to another Bessel / Fourier-Jacobi period in a specific way.

If
k=2
, then
G^{\prime}
must be symplectic and
G
must be orthogonal, and the
\gamma
-period on
G
is the reductive Bessel period on
G
(i.e. the subspace
V_{new}\subseteq V
is of codimension 1).

5.3.
Whittaker to Bessel / Fourier-Jacobi

If, in the preceding section,
b=0
(
G
symplectic) or 1 (
G
orthogonal), then one sees that we are relating a Whittaker period on
\pi
to a Bessel or Fourier-Jacobi period on
\Theta(\pi)
.

5.4.
Shalika to reductive periods

Finally, suppose
\gamma^{\prime}
has corresponding partition
\lambda^{\prime}=[2^{a}]
for some
a
, then the
\gamma^{\prime}
-period on
G^{\prime}
is the so-called Shalika period. Furthermore
\gamma
must correspond to the trivial partition
\lambda=[1^{a+b}]
for some
b
, with
L\cong G(V_{new})
and
V_{new}
of dimension
b
.

This case has been used in various settings to relate the Shalika period on
\Theta(\pi)
to the reductive
G(V_{new})
-period on
\pi
. When
a=b
for instance, we are relating the Shalika period on
\Theta(\pi)
to the so-called linear or Friedberg-Jacquet period on
\pi
.

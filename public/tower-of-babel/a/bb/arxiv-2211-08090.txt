On generalized definitions of ultradifferentiable classes Thanks: J. Jiménez-Garrido is supported by project PID2019-105621GB-I00, D.N. Nenning and G. Schindl are supported by FWF-Project P33417-N
Javier Jiménez-Garrido, David Nicolas Nenning and Gerhard Schindl

Abstract
. We show that the ultradifferentiable-like classes of smooth functions introduced and studied by S. Pilipović, N. Teofanov and F. Tomić are special cases of the general framework of spaces of ultradifferentiable functions defined in terms of weight matrices in the sense of A. Rainer and the third author. We study classes “beyond geometric growth factors” defined in terms of a weight sequence and an exponent sequence, prove that these new types admit a weight matrix representation and transfer known results from the matrix-type to such a non-standard ultradifferentiable setting.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
26A12, 26A48, 46A13, 46E10

1.
Introduction

Spaces of ultradifferentiable functions are sub-classes of smooth functions with certain restrictions on the growth of their derivatives. Two classical approaches are commonly considered, either the restrictions are expressed by means of a weight sequence
M=(M_{j})_{j}
, also called
Denjoy-Carleman classes
(e.g. see
[
11
]
), or by means of a weight function
\omega
also called
Braun-Meise-Taylor classes
; see
[
3
]
. More precisely (in the one-dimensional case) for each compact set
K
, the sets

(1.1)

\left\{\frac{f^{(j)}(x)}{h^{j}M_{j}}\,\,:\,\,j\in\mathbb{N},\,\,x\in K\right\},\quad\text{respectively}\quad\left\{\frac{f^{(j)}(x)}{\exp(\frac{1}{h}\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(hj))}\,\,:\,\,j\in\mathbb{N},\,\,x\in K\right\},

are required to be bounded, where
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
denotes the Young-conjugate of
t\mapsto\omega(e^{t})
. We shall mention that in the second situation the classes can be defined directly by using
\omega
and controlling the decay of the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
with growth factors
t\mapsto\exp(h\omega(t))
,
h>0
. In fact, this is the original description; see
[
1
]
and also the discussion in
[
3
]
where the original approach is transferred to the boundedness condition expressed in (
1.1
).

In the literature standard growth and regularity conditions are assumed for
M
and
\omega
and in both settings we can consider two different types of spaces: For the
Roumieu-type
the boundedness of the sets in (
1.1
) is required for some
h>0
, whereas for the
Beurling-type
it is required for all
h>0
.

The most well-known examples are the
Gevrey sequences
of type
\alpha>0
with
G^{\alpha}_{j}:=j^{\alpha j}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
(or equivalently use
M^{\alpha}_{j}:=j!^{\alpha}
). Alternatively, one can use the function
t\mapsto t^{1/\alpha}=:\omega_{\alpha}(t)
.

It is then a natural question how both classical settings are related. In
[
2
]
this problem is studied and it has been shown that in general both approaches are mutually distinct. However, based on this work, in
[
27
]
and
[
23
]
A. Rainer and the third author have introduced the notion of
weight matrices

\mathcal{M}=\{M^{(x)}:x>0\}
which allows to treat both classical methods in a unified way and to transfer proofs from one context to the other. This can be achieved when considering
\mathcal{M}=\{M\}
for the weight sequence and the so-called
associated weight matrix

\mathcal{W}:=\{W^{(\ell)}:\ell>0\}
with
W^{(\ell)}_{j}:=\exp(\frac{1}{\ell}\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(\ell j))
in the weight function case. But one is also able to describe more classes, e.g. take the Gevrey matrix
\mathcal{G}:=\{G^{\alpha}:\alpha>1\}
; see
[
23
, Thm. 5.22]
.

A second recent generalization was presented by S. Pilipović, N. Teofanov and F. Tomić; see
[
19
]
. For given parameters
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
they consider the sequence
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{j}:=j^{\tau j^{\sigma}}
. However, in their definition the geometric growth factor
h^{j}
appearing in (
1.1
) is replaced by
h^{j^{\sigma}}
. Observe that the growth of
j\mapsto h^{j^{\sigma}}
is closely connected with
j\mapsto M^{\tau,\sigma}_{j}
. The authors called their framework “beyond Gevrey regularity” because
M^{\tau,1}_{j}=j^{\tau j}
for
\sigma=1
, i.e., the Gevrey sequence of type
\tau>0
. Since all the classes considered in this work are, in some sense, generalizations of Gevrey classes, these spaces will be called
Pilipović-Teofanov-Tomić classes
, or PTT-classes for short.

The difference between the growth of
j\mapsto h^{j}
and
j\mapsto h^{j^{\sigma}}
suggests that the PTT-classes can be viewed as “non-standard ultradifferentiable classes” and one can ask how both generalizations are related. In the introduction of
[
21
]
it was claimed that the PTT-classes are not covered by the weight matrix approach which is due to the different growth of the factors mentioned before. However, the aim of this paper is to show that also the PTT-classes are contained in the weight matrix approach.

In fact, we treat a more abstract setting by considering an
exponent sequence

\Phi=(\Phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and by replacing in (
1.1
) the growth
j\mapsto h^{j}
by
j\mapsto h^{\Phi_{j}}
. This notion yields “ultradifferentiable classes beyond geometric growth factors” and we show that under mild regularity and growth assumptions on
\Phi
such spaces admit a representation as weight matrix classes (as locally convex vector spaces) by involving the canonical matrix

\mathcal{M}_{M,\Phi}:=\{M^{(c,\Phi)}:c>0\},\quad M^{(c,\Phi)}_{j}:=c^{\Phi_{j}}M_{j}.

Applying this main result to the PTT-classes, we are also able to see that when both
\sigma>1
and
\tau>0
are fixed then the corresponding space cannot be represented by a single weight sequence
M
or by a weight function
\omega
; i.e., one requires the general weight matrix setting to describe these classes. In other words PTT-classes constitute genuine examples of ultradifferentiable classes defined by weight matrices.

On the other hand, in the very recent paper
[
33
]
it is shown that when only
\sigma>1
is fixed and when one considers matrix-type classes with parameter
\tau>0
, i.e. PTT-limit classes, then these spaces can alternatively be defined in terms of a weight function (in particular of a so-called associated weight function). We give an independent proof of this result by applying purely weight matrix techniques; see Theorem
6.8
.

The paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
all necessary and relevant conditions on weight sequences, weight functions and weight matrices are given and the corresponding classes are defined. In Section
3
we introduce ultradifferentiable spaces “beyond geometric growth factors” and prove in Section
4
the main characterization results, i.e., Theorems
4.4
,
4.7
and
4.9
, showing that, in particular, the PTT-classes can be represented as weight matrix spaces. In Section
5
we apply this fact for fixed parameters
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
, and study properties of the relevant matrix
\mathcal{M}^{\tau,\sigma}
in order to transfer known results from the matrix setting to PTT-classes. In Section
6
this is done analogously for so-called limit classes when fixing
\sigma
but letting
\tau\rightarrow 0
resp.
\tau\rightarrow+\infty
. It is shown that such spaces can be represented as Braun-Meise-Taylor classes (see Theorem
6.8
) and satisfy additional properties since in this weight structure both
mixed moderate growth conditions
of the particular type are valid.

2.
Weights and conditions

2.1.
General notation

We write
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{>0}:=\{1,2,3,\dots\}
. With
\mathcal{E}
we denote the class of all smooth functions. We use the standard multi-index notation and
f^{(\alpha)}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, stands for the
\alpha
-th derivative of a given smooth function
f
(defined in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
).

Occasionally, we write the symbol
[\cdot]
if we mean either
\{\cdot\}
(Roumieu-type) or
(\cdot)
(Beurling-type) for spaces and growth conditions.

2.2.
Weight sequences

Given a sequence
M=(M_{j})_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
we also use
m=(m_{j})_{j}
defined by
m_{j}:=\frac{M_{j}}{j!}
and
\mu_{j}:=\frac{M_{j}}{M_{j-1}}
,
j\geq 1
, and set
\mu_{0}:=1
. Analogously these conventions are used for all other appearing sequences, i.e.,
N\leftrightarrow n\leftrightarrow\nu
etc.
M
is called
normalized
if
1=M_{0}\leq M_{1}
holds true.

M
is called
log-convex
, denoted by
(\text{lc})
, if

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;M_{j}^{2}\leq M_{j-1}M_{j+1},

equivalently if
\mu
is nondecreasing. If
M
is log-convex and normalized, then both
M
and
j\mapsto(M_{j})^{1/j}
are nondecreasing. In this case we get
M_{j}\geq 1
for all
j\geq 0
and

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;(M_{j})^{1/j}\leq\mu_{j}.

Moreover we get
M_{j}M_{k}\leq M_{j+k}
for all
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
; e.g. see
[
26
, Lemma 2.0.4, Lemma 2.0.6]
.

If
m
is log-convex, then
M
is also log-convex and in this case we call
M

strongly log-convex
and write that
M
is
(\text{slc})
.

For any
M=(M_{j})_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
it is well-known that

(2.1)

\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{j}\leq\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}\leq\limsup_{j\rightarrow\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}\leq\limsup_{j\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{j}.

For convenience we introduce the following set of sequences:

\hypertarget{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}:=\{M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}:\;M\;\text{is normalized, log-convex},\;\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}=\infty\}.

M
has
moderate growth
, denoted by
(\text{mg})
, if

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;j,k\in\mathbb{N}:\;M_{j+k}\leq C^{j+k+1}M_{j}M_{k}.

A weaker condition is
derivation closedness
, denoted by
(\text{dc})
, if

\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:\;M_{j+1}\leq A^{j+1}M_{j}\Leftrightarrow\mu_{j+1}\leq A^{j+1}.

M
is called
non-quasianalytic
, denoted by
(\text{nq})
, if

\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}<+\infty.

In the literature
(\operatorname{mg})
is also known under
stability of ultradifferential operators
or
(M.2)
,
(\operatorname{dc})
under
(M.2)^{\prime}
and
(\operatorname{nq})
under
(M.3)^{\prime}
; see
[
11
]
. It is also known that for log-convex (normalized) weight sequences
(\operatorname{nq})
is equivalent to

\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{(M_{j})^{1/j}}<+\infty,

which holds by the so-called
Carleman-inequality
; see
[
26
, Prop. 4.1.7]
and the references therein.

M
has
(\beta_{1})
(named after
[
18
]
) if

\exists\;Q\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu_{Qj}}{\mu_{j}}>Q,

and
(\gamma_{1})
if

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\frac{\mu_{j}}{j}\sum_{k\geq j}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}<\infty.

In
[
18
, Proposition 1.1]
it has been shown that for
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
both conditions are equivalent and in the literature
(\gamma_{1})
is also called “strong nonquasianalyticity condition”. In
[
11
]
it is denoted by
(M.3)
(in fact, there
\frac{\mu_{j}}{j}
is replaced by
\frac{\mu_{j}}{j-1}
for
j\geq 2
but which is equivalent to having
(\gamma_{1})
).

A weaker condition on
M
is
(\beta_{3})
(named after
[
27
]
, see also
[
2
]
) which reads as follows:

\exists\;Q\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu_{Qj}}{\mu_{j}}>1.

For two weight sequences
M=(M_{j})_{j}
and
N=(N_{j})_{j}
we write
M\hypertarget{mpreceq}{\preceq}N
if

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{j}}{N_{j}}\right)^{1/j}<\infty,

and call them equivalent, denoted by
M\hypertarget{approx}{\approx}N
, if

M\hyperlink{mpreceq}{\preceq}N\;\text{and}\;N\hyperlink{mpreceq}{\preceq}M.

In the relations above one can replace
M
and
N
simultaneously by
m
and
n
because
M\hyperlink{mpreceq}{\preceq}N\Leftrightarrow m\hyperlink{mpreceq}{\preceq}n
. Let us also write
M\leq N
if
M_{j}\leq N_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Finally, we write
M\hypertarget{triangle}{\vartriangleleft}N
, if

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{M_{j}}{N_{j}}\right)^{1/j}=0.

For any
s\geq 0
we set
G^{s}:=(j!^{s})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, so for
s>0
this denotes the classical
Gevrey sequence
of index/order
s
.

2.3.
Weight functions

According to
[
16
, Sect. 2.1]
and
[
17
, Sect. 2.2]
a function
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is called a
pre-weight function,
if it is continuous, non-decreasing,
\omega(0)=0
and such that

(*)

\log(t)=o(\omega(t))
,
t\rightarrow+\infty
,

(*)

t\mapsto\varphi_{\omega}(t):=\omega(e^{t})
is convex.

Consequently, for each pre-weight function we have
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
.
\omega
is called a
weight function
if
\omega
satisfies in addition

(*)

\omega(2t)=O(\omega(t))
,
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

If
\omega(t)=0
for all
t\in[0,1]
, then we call
\omega
a
normalized (pre-)weight function
.

Let
\sigma,\tau
be pre-weight functions, we write
\sigma\hypertarget{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
if
\tau(t)=O(\sigma(t))\;\text{as}\;t\rightarrow+\infty
. We call them equivalent, denoted by
\sigma\hypertarget{sim}{\sim}\tau
, if

\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
and
\tau\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma
.

2.4.
Associated weight function

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
(with
M_{0}=1
), then the
associated function

\omega_{M}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined by

\omega_{M}(t):=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\log\left(\frac{t^{j}}{M_{j}}\right)\;\;\;\text{for}\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\hskip 30.0pt\omega_{M}(0):=0.

For an abstract introduction of the associated function we refer to
[
13
, Chapitre I]
; see also
[
11
, Definition 3.1]
.

If
\liminf_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}>0
, then
\omega_{M}(t)=0
for sufficiently small
t
, since
\log\left(\frac{t^{j}}{M_{j}}\right)<0\Leftrightarrow t<(M_{j})^{1/j}
holds for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. (In particular, if
M_{j}\geq 1
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\omega_{M}
is vanishing on
[0,1]
.) Moreover, under this assumption
t\mapsto\omega_{M}(t)
is a continuous nondecreasing function, which is convex in the variable
\log(t)
and tends faster to infinity than any
\log(t^{j})
,
j\geq 1
, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.
\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}=+\infty
implies that
\omega_{M}(t)<+\infty
for each finite
t
which shall be considered as a basic assumption for defining
\omega_{M}
.

Summarizing, if
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
, then
\omega_{M}
is a normalized pre-weight function (e.g. see
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
), however in general
\omega_{M}(2t)=O(\omega_{M}(t))
is not clear; see the recent characterization
[
30
, Thm. 3.1]
.

Finally, if
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
(or even if
M
is log-convex with
M_{0}=1
and
\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}=+\infty
), then by
[
13
, Chapitre I, 1.4, 1.8]
and also
[
11
, Prop. 3.2]
we get

(2.2)

M_{j}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{j}}{\exp(\omega_{M}(t))},\;\;\;j\in\mathbb{N}.

2.5.
Weight matrices

For the following definitions and conditions see
[
23
, Sect. 4]
and
[
27
, Sect. 7]
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
denote the index set (equipped with the natural order). A
weight matrix

\mathcal{M}
associated with
\mathcal{I}
is a (one parameter) family of weight sequences
\mathcal{M}:=\{M^{(\alpha)}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}:\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\}
, such that

\forall\;\alpha\leq\beta:\;\;\;M^{(\alpha)}\leq M^{(\beta)}.

We call a weight matrix
\mathcal{M}

standard log-convex,
denoted by
(\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{sc}})
, if

\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}:\;M^{(\alpha)}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}.

Moreover, we put
m^{(\alpha)}_{j}:=\frac{M^{(\alpha)}_{j}}{j!}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mu^{(\alpha)}_{j}:=\frac{M^{(\alpha)}_{j}}{M^{(\alpha)}_{j-1}}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
,
\mu^{(\alpha)}_{0}:=1
.

A matrix is called
constant
if
M^{(\alpha)}\hyperlink{approx}{\approx}M^{(\beta)}
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{I}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M^{(\alpha)}:\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\}
and
\mathcal{N}=\{N^{(\alpha)}:\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\}
be given. We write
\mathcal{M}\hypertarget{Mroumpreceq}{\{\preceq\}}\mathcal{N}
if

\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}:\;\;\;M^{(\alpha)}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}N^{(\beta)},

and call
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}

to be
R
-equivalent, or
\mathcal{M}\{\approx\}\mathcal{N}
for short, if

\mathcal{M}\hyperlink{Mroumpreceq}{\{\preceq\}}\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}\hyperlink{Mroumpreceq}{\{\preceq\}}\mathcal{M}
. Analogously, we write
\mathcal{M}\hypertarget{Mbeurpreceq}{(\preceq)}\mathcal{N}
if

\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}:\;\;\;M^{(\beta)}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}N^{(\alpha)},

and call
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
to be
B
-equivalent, or
\mathcal{M}(\approx)\mathcal{N}
for short, if

\mathcal{M}\hyperlink{Mbeurpreceq}{(\preceq)}\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}\hyperlink{Mbeurpreceq}{(\preceq)}\mathcal{M}
.

If
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
are both
R
- and
B
-equivalent, then we say for simplicity that they are equivalent.

We recall several growth and regularity assumptions on a given weight matrix:

\displaystyle\hypertarget{holom}{(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{H}})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}:\;\;\;\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}(m^{(\alpha)}_{j})^{1/j}>0,

\displaystyle\hypertarget{R-Comega}{(\mathcal{M}_{\{\text{C}^{\omega}\}})}

\displaystyle\exists\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}:\;\;\;\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}(m^{(\alpha)}_{j})^{1/j}>0,

\displaystyle\hypertarget{B-Comega}{(\mathcal{M}_{(\text{C}^{\omega})})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}:\;\;\;\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}(m^{(\alpha)}_{j})^{1/j}=+\infty,

\displaystyle\hypertarget{R-rai}{(\mathcal{M}_{\{\text{rai}\}})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;C>0\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}\;\forall\;1\leq j\leq k:\;\;\;(m^{(\alpha)}_{j})^{1/j}\leq C(m^{(\beta)}_{k})^{1/k},

\displaystyle\hypertarget{B-rai}{(\mathcal{M}_{(\text{rai})})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;C>0\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}\;\forall\;1\leq j\leq k:\;\;\;(m^{(\beta)}_{j})^{1/j}\leq C(m^{(\alpha)}_{k})^{1/k},

\displaystyle\hypertarget{R-mg}{(\mathcal{M}_{\{\text{mg}\}})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;C>0\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}\;\forall\;j,k\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M^{(\alpha)}_{j+k}\leq C^{j+k+1}M^{(\beta)}_{j}M^{(\beta)}_{k},

\displaystyle\hypertarget{B-mg}{(\mathcal{M}_{(\text{mg})})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;C>0\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}\;\forall\;j,k\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M^{(\beta)}_{j+k}\leq C^{j+k+1}M^{(\alpha)}_{j}M^{(\alpha)}_{k},

\displaystyle\hypertarget{R-dc}{(\mathcal{M}_{\{\text{dc}\}})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;C>0\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M^{(\alpha)}_{j+1}\leq C^{j+1}M^{(\beta)}_{j},

\displaystyle\hypertarget{B-dc}{(\mathcal{M}_{(\text{dc})})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;C>0\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M^{(\beta)}_{j+1}\leq C^{j+1}M^{(\alpha)}_{j},

\displaystyle\hypertarget{R-BR}{(\mathcal{M}_{\{\text{BR}\}})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}:\;\;\;M^{(\alpha)}\hyperlink{triangle}{\vartriangleleft}M^{(\beta)},

\displaystyle\hypertarget{B-BR}{(\mathcal{M}_{(\text{BR})})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}:\;\;\;M^{(\beta)}\hyperlink{triangle}{\vartriangleleft}M^{(\alpha)},

\displaystyle\hypertarget{R-FdB}{(\mathcal{M}_{\{\text{FdB}\}})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}:\;\;\;(m^{(\alpha)})^{\circ}\hyperlink{mpreceq}{\preceq}m^{(\beta)},

\displaystyle\hypertarget{B-FdB}{(\mathcal{M}_{(\text{FdB})})}

\displaystyle\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}:\;\;\;(m^{(\beta)})^{\circ}\hyperlink{mpreceq}{\preceq}m^{(\alpha)},

with
(m^{(\alpha)})^{\circ}:=((m^{(\alpha)}_{j})^{\circ})_{j}
being the sequence defined by

(m^{(\alpha)}_{k})^{\circ}:=\max\left\{m^{(\alpha)}_{\ell}\cdot m^{(\alpha)}_{j_{1}}\cdots m^{(\alpha)}_{j_{\ell}}:j_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0},\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}j_{i}=k\right\},\;\;\;\;\;(m^{(\alpha)}_{0})^{\circ}:=1.

R
-equivalence between matrices preserves all Roumieu-type conditions listed above and
B
-equivalence all Beurling-type conditions.

Finally, let us recall

\displaystyle\hypertarget{R-L}{(\mathcal{M}_{\{\text{L}\}})}

\displaystyle\forall\;C>0\;\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;D>0\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:C^{j}M^{(\alpha)}_{j}\leq DM^{(\beta)}_{j},

\displaystyle\hypertarget{B-L}{(\mathcal{M}_{(\text{L})})}

\displaystyle\forall\;C>0\;\forall\;\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\;\exists\;D>0\;\exists\;\beta\in\mathcal{I}\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:C^{j}M^{(\beta)}_{j}\leq DM^{(\alpha)}_{j}.

A matrix is called
non-quasianalytic
if any sequence
M^{(\alpha)}
is non-quasianalytic; see
[
29
, Sect. 4]
. When dealing with Roumieu type classes then it suffices to assume that there exists
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{I}
such that
M^{(\alpha_{0})}
is non-quasianalytic since smaller indices can be skipped; see also the discussion in
[
25
, Sect. 5.1]
.

2.6.
Ultradifferentiable classes

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be non-empty open and for
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
compact we write
K\subset\subset U
if
\overline{K}\subseteq U
, i.e.,
K
is in
U
relatively compact. We introduce now the following spaces of ultradifferentiable function classes.
First, for weight sequences we define the (local) classes of
Roumieu-type
by

\mathcal{E}_{\{M\}}(U):=\{f\in\mathcal{E}(U):\;\forall\;K\subset\subset U\;\exists\;h>0:\;\|f\|_{M,K,h}<+\infty\},

and the classes of

by

\mathcal{E}_{(M)}(U):=\{f\in\mathcal{E}(U):\;\forall\;K\subset\subset U\;\forall\;h>0:\;\|f\|_{M,K,h}<+\infty\},

where we denote

For a sufficiently regular compact set
K
(e.g. with smooth boundary and such that

\mathcal{E}_{\{M\}}(U)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\varprojlim}\;\underset{h>0}{\varinjlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{M,h}(K)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\varprojlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{\{M\}}(K).

Similarly, we get

\mathcal{E}_{(M)}(K):=\underset{h>0}{\varprojlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{M,h}(K),

and

\mathcal{E}_{(M)}(U)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\varprojlim}\;\underset{h>0}{\varprojlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{M,h}(K)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\varprojlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{(M)}(K).

For a weight function, we define the corresponding Roumieu-type classes

\mathcal{E}_{\{\omega\}}(U):=\{f\in\mathcal{E}(U):\;\forall\;K\subset\subset U\;\exists\;h>0:\;\|f\|_{\omega,K,h}<+\infty\},

and the classes of Beurling-type by

\mathcal{E}_{(\omega)}(U):=\{f\in\mathcal{E}(U):\;\forall\;K\subset\subset U\;\forall\;h>0:\;\|f\|_{\omega,K,h}<+\infty\},

where we denote

\|f\|_{\omega,K,h}:=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d},x\in K}\frac{|f^{(\alpha)}(x)|}{\exp(\frac{1}{h}\varphi_{\omega}^{*}(h|\alpha|))}.

The spaces are topologized in complete analogy to the weight sequence case. First we define for a sufficiently regular compact set
K
the Banach space

\mathcal{E}_{\omega,h}(K):=\{f\in\mathcal{E}(K):\|f\|_{\omega,K,h}<+\infty\},

and set

finally we endow

\mathcal{E}_{\{\omega\}}(U)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\varprojlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{\{\omega\}}(K),\quad\mathcal{E}_{(\omega)}(U)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\varprojlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{(\omega)}(K).

Next, we consider classes defined by weight matrices of Roumieu-type

3.
Ultradifferentiable classes beyond geometric growth factors

The main objective is to prove that the PTT-classes can be represented as classes defined by (suitable) weight matrices. Indeed, we can obtain a more general result by letting the exponents of the defining estimates be
\Phi_{j}
instead of
{j}
or

where
\Phi=(\Phi_{j})_{j}
is arbitrary and only satisfying some mild regularity property.

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}
is called
exponent sequence
if it satisfies

(3.1)

\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Phi_{j}}{j}>0.

In particular every exponent sequence tends to infinity.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be non-empty open,
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}
. We introduce now
\Phi
-ultradifferentiable function classes (defined in terms of a single weight sequence

): The (local) class of Roumieu-type is given by

\mathcal{E}_{\{M,\Phi\}}(U):=\{f\in\mathcal{E}(U):\;\forall\;K\subset\subset U\;\exists\;h>0:\;\|f\|_{M,\Phi,K,h}<+\infty\},

and the class of Beurling-type by

\mathcal{E}_{(M,\Phi)}(U):=\{f\in\mathcal{E}(U):\;\forall\;K\subset\subset U\;\forall\;h>0:\;\|f\|_{M,\Phi,K,h}<+\infty\},

where we denote

(3.2)

\|f\|_{M,\Phi,K,h}:=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d},x\in K}\frac{|f^{(\alpha)}(x)|}{h^{\Phi_{|\alpha|}}M_{|\alpha|}}.

For a sufficiently regular compact set
K

\mathcal{E}_{M,\Phi,h}(K):=\{f\in\mathcal{E}(K):\|f\|_{M,\Phi,K,h}<+\infty\}

is a Banach space and so we have the following topological vector space representations

\mathcal{E}_{\{M,\Phi\}}(K):=\underset{h>0}{\varinjlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{M,\Phi,h}(K),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{E}_{\{M,\Phi\}}(U)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\varprojlim}\;\underset{h>0}{\varinjlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{M,\Phi,h}(K)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\varprojlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{\{M,\Phi\}}(K).

Similarly, we get

\mathcal{E}_{(M,\Phi)}(K):=\underset{h>0}{\varprojlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{M,\Phi,h}(K),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{E}_{(M,\Phi)}(U)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\varprojlim}\;\underset{h>0}{\varprojlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{M,\Phi,h}(K)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\varprojlim}\;\mathcal{E}_{(M,\Phi)}(K).

Note that condition (
3.1
) means that the factor

in the seminorms is at least geometric.

We have two important examples in mind:

4.
Classes beyond geometric factors versus weight matrices

The aim of this section is to verify that, under mild growth and regularity assumptions on
\Phi
, the classes
\mathcal{E}_{\{M,\Phi\}}
resp.
\mathcal{E}_{(M,\Phi)}
can be represented (as locally convex vector spaces) by the matrix classes
\mathcal{E}_{\{\mathcal{M}\}}
resp.
\mathcal{E}_{(\mathcal{M})}
for a suitable choice of the matrix
\mathcal{M}
.

4.1.
Preparatory results

We introduce an appropriate matrix of sequences. Let
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}
, then consider the set

(4.1)

\mathcal{M}_{M,\Phi}:=\{M^{(c,\Phi)}:c>0\},\hskip 15.0ptM^{(c,\Phi)}_{j}:=c^{\Phi_{j}}M_{j},\;\;\;j\in\mathbb{N}.

For any sequence
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}
, we have that
\mathcal{M}_{M,\Phi}
is a weight matrix in the notion of Section
2.5
. Next we show that the mild growth restriction (
3.1
) on
\Phi
is equivalent to the fact that the matrix

allows to absorb exponential growth.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\Phi
satisfies (

), i.e.,
\Phi
is an exponent sequence.

(ii)

\mathcal{M}_{M,\Phi}
satisfies
(\mathcal{M}_{\{\operatorname{L}\}})
.

(iii)

\mathcal{M}_{M,\Phi}
satisfies
(\mathcal{M}_{(\operatorname{L})})
.

Proof.

(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
First we observe that
\mathcal{M}_{M,\Phi}
satisfies
(\mathcal{M}_{\{\text{L}\}})
if and only if

\forall\;h>0\;\forall\;c>0\;\exists\;D>0\;\exists\;c_{1}>0\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:h^{j}c^{\Phi_{j}}\leq Dc_{1}^{\Phi_{j}}.

Consequently, if (ii) holds, then for
h=2
,
c=1
there exists

c_{1}>1
and
D>0
such that

\frac{\Phi_{j}}{j}\geq\frac{\log(2)-(1/j)\log(D)}{\log(c_{1})},

for all

, so

satisfies (
3.1
).

Conversely, let
h,c\geq 1
be given. Then (
3.1
) yields the existence of some

and

such that for all

such that
\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c}\right)^{\Phi_{j}/j}\geq\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c}\right)^{\epsilon}\geq h
for all

. Thus, when choosing

large enough, then we have that for all

which shows that

satisfies
(\mathcal{M}_{\{\text{L}\}})
.

(i)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
Follows similarly.
∎

If we assume more growth requirements on the sequence
\Phi
, then we can deduce further regularity conditions for

Proposition 4.2

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}

(i)

If

(ii)

Theorem 4.4

The Roumieu case.

. There it has been commented that
\theta_{N}\notin\mathcal{E}_{(N)}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
and the proof shows that in (

) we can replace

by
\nu_{j+1}
.

The proof of the Beurling case makes use of the following functional analytic result.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
E,F
be Fréchet spaces, such that
E
is a linear subspace of
F
(not assuming continuous inclusion). Assume that both are continuously included in
C(U)
(or even in any Hausdorff space), where
U
is some open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
E
is continuously included in
F
.

Proof.

We want to show that the inclusion
\iota:E\rightarrow F
is continuous. By the closed graph theorem, it suffices to show that if
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
E
(and thus in
C(U)
), and
f_{n}\rightarrow g
in
F
(and thus in
C(U)
), we have
g=0
. But this is now clear since
C(U)
is Hausdorff.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

In the light of the previous Proposition, any inclusion (as sets) of Beurling classes is automatically a continuous inclusion. And equality as sets yields equality as Fréchet spaces.

Now we are in the position to formulate a converse to Theorem

Theorem 4.7
.

Let

has to satisfy (

).

We immediately get the following consequence:

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given such that the requirements of the particular case in Theorem
4.7
are valid. If
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Phi_{j}}{j}=0
, then
\mathcal{E}_{[M,\Phi]}
cannot be identified with any weight matrix class
\mathcal{E}_{[\mathcal{L}]}
with
\mathcal{L}
being
(\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{sc}})
.

Proof.

(i):

Choose
c
such that
M^{(c,\Phi)}
is log-convex and normalized. By applying the optimal functions
\theta_{N}
from (
4.4
) to
N\equiv M^{(c,\Phi)}
we get by the equality of the classes that there exist
h,x_{0}
(w.l.o.g. greater than
1
) such that

(4.5)

\forall x\geq x_{0}~\forall j\in\mathbb{N}:\quad M_{j}\leq c^{\Phi_{j}}M_{j}\leq h^{j+1}L^{(x)}_{j},

and therefore we get

(4.6)

\forall x\geq x_{0}~\forall j\in\mathbb{N}:\quad\frac{1}{h^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{L^{(x)}_{j}}{M_{j}}\right)^{1/j}.

In addition we infer, again by working with optimal functions, but now for the sequences
j\mapsto n^{j}L^{(n)}_{j}
, that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
c_{n}
(w.l.o.g. increasing in

such that

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:\quad n^{j}L^{(n)}_{j}\leq c_{n}^{\Phi_{j}}M_{j},

and by taking roots we end up with

(4.7)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\quad n(L^{(n)}_{j})^{1/j}\leq c_{n}^{\Phi_{j}/j}M_{j}^{1/j}.

Now let us assume that (
3.1
) is violated, i.e., that

\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Phi_{j}}{j}=0,

then we can find a sequence of integers
j_{n}
such that

c_{n}^{\Phi_{j_{n}}/j_{n}}\leq 2.

Combining (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) we infer (for all

with
n\geq x_{0}

\frac{n}{h^{2}}\leq n\left(\frac{L^{(n)}_{j_{n}}}{M_{j_{n}}}\right)^{1/j_{n}}\leq 2,

which yields a contradiction as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

(ii):

By Proposition
4.5
, we infer that the spaces are isomorphic as Fréchet spaces. Thus we get that for any compact set
K\subset\subset\mathbb{R}
there exist
h,x_{0}>0
and a compact set

such that for all

we have

\|f\|_{M,\Phi,K,1}\leq\frac{1}{h}\|f\|_{L^{(x_{0})},J,h},

which yields, by plugging in the family of functions
f_{s}(t):=e^{ist}
,

\exp(\omega_{M}(s))\leq\frac{1}{h}\exp(\omega_{L^{(x_{0})}}(s/h)).

Due to log-convexity we can apply (
2.2
) and get from this estimate (since the sequences of
\mathcal{L}
are pointwise ordered), that for all
x\leq x_{0}
and

h^{j+1}L^{(x)}_{j}\leq M_{j}

and finally, since w.l.o.g.

, we get that for all
x\leq x_{0}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}

(4.8)

h^{2}\leq\left(\frac{M_{j}}{L^{(x)}_{j}}\right)^{1/j}.

Analogously we argue to get that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
c_{n}>0
such that

c_{n}^{\Phi_{j}+1}M_{j}\leq\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{j}L^{(1/n)}_{j},

and therefore

(4.9)

\left(\frac{M_{j}}{L_{j}^{(1/n)}}\right)^{1/j}\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}\right)^{(\Phi_{j}+1)/j}.

Now again assume that (
3.1
) is violated, i.e., that

\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Phi_{j}}{j}=0,

then we can find a sequence of integers
j_{n}
such that

\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}\right)^{(\Phi_{j_{n}}+1)/j_{n}}\leq 2.

Combining (
4.8
) and (

) we infer

h^{2}\leq\frac{2}{n},

which again gives the desired contradiction and thus finishes the proof.
∎

4.3.
More general (matrix) situations

For the sake of completeness let us comment on even more general situations compared with the definitions in Section

\Phi

4.1

(4.11)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{N},\Phi}:=\{N^{(c,c,\Phi)}:c>0\},\hskip 15.0ptN^{(c,c,\Phi)}_{j}:=c^{\Phi_{j}}N^{(c)}_{j},\;\;\;j\in\mathbb{N}.

4.1
can be transferred to this setting and Theorem

by

,

Proposition 4.14

Let

(a)

(a)(i)\Rightarrow(ii)

Note that, in particular, Theorem

.

(ii)

Obviously, by the convention

c=M^{(c,\tau,\sigma)}_{1}
for all

for each

Let us verify that the quotients of each sequence

are non-decreasing from some index

on. This then implies the statement, since each

can then be replaced by an equivalent sequence

which is even strongly log-convex and normalized:

By

for all

are ordered (even w.r.t. their quotient sequences). Moreover,

So let us show that the quotients of

\frac{m^{(c,\tau,\sigma)}_{j}}{m^{(c,\tau,\sigma)}_{j-1}}=\mu^{(c,\tau,\sigma)}_{j}\frac{1}{j}=c^{j^{\sigma}-(j-1)^{\sigma}}\frac{j^{\tau j^{\sigma}}}{(j-1)^{\tau(j-1)^{\sigma}}}\frac{1}{j},

and we set now
f(t):=\log(c)(t^{\sigma}-(t-1)^{\sigma})+\tau t^{\sigma}\log(t)-\tau(t-1)^{\sigma}\log(t-1)-\log(t)
,
t>1
. Then for all
t>1
:

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma\log(c)(t^{\sigma-1}-(t-1)^{\sigma-1})+\sigma\tau t^{\sigma-1}\log(t)+\tau t^{\sigma}\frac{1}{t}

\displaystyle-\tau\sigma(t-1)^{\sigma-1}\log(t-1)-\tau(t-1)^{\sigma}\frac{1}{t-1}-\frac{1}{t}

\displaystyle=(\sigma\log(c)+\tau)(t^{\sigma-1}-(t-1)^{\sigma-1})+\tau\sigma(t^{\sigma-1}\log(t)-(t-1)^{\sigma-1}\log(t-1))-\frac{1}{t}\geq 0

We now continue to show

(5.4)

(\sigma\log(c)+\tau)t(t^{\sigma-1}-(t-1)^{\sigma-1})+\tau\sigma t\log(t-1)(t^{\sigma-1}-(t-1)^{\sigma-1})\quad\rightarrow\quad\infty,~t\rightarrow\infty,

which obviously implies the above statement.

Observe that
t^{\sigma-1}-(t-1)^{\sigma-1}=(\sigma-1)\xi^{\sigma-2}
for some
\xi\in(t-1,t)
. Therefore, for

and

Plugging in the appropriate term into (

(iii)

We claim that for some choices

and some

(large) we get for all

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(c)+\tau(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(j+1)\leq\log(A)+j^{\sigma}\log(c_{1})+\tau j^{\sigma}\log(j)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\tau\underbrace{\frac{(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(j+1)-j^{\sigma}\log(j)}{(j+1)-j}}_{=:\Delta_{j}}\leq\log(A)+j^{\sigma}\log(c_{1})-(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(c).

Note that the very first estimate is clear for

by the convention

and when taking

. Set

\displaystyle j^{\sigma}\log(c_{1})-(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(c)

\displaystyle=(2j)^{\sigma}\log(2c)-(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(c)

\displaystyle\geq(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(2c)-(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(c)=(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(2).

Thus, in the Roumieu case we are able to conclude when choosing

When given

and get for all

\displaystyle j^{\sigma}\log(c_{1})-(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(c)

\displaystyle=j^{\sigma}\log(c_{1})-(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(c_{1})+(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(2)

\displaystyle\geq(j+1)^{\sigma}\log(2),

\displaystyle c^{j^{\sigma-1}}j^{\tau j^{\sigma-1}-1}=\frac{c^{j^{\sigma-1}}j^{\tau j^{\sigma-1}}}{j}=\frac{(M^{(c,\tau,\sigma)}_{j})^{1/j}}{j}

Recall that each strongly log-convex sequence satisfying

6.
PTT-limit classes as spaces defined by weight matrices

In
[
19
]
, for fixed
\sigma\geq 1
, the authors also consider limits with respect to the parameter
\tau
, i.e., the spaces
\mathcal{E}_{\infty,\sigma}
and
\mathcal{E}_{0,\sigma}
, presented in Subsection
2.7
, which are endowed with the natural inductive resp. projective limit topology.

The main reason to consider these classes is represented by the fact, that they are stable with respect to so-called
ultradifferential operators.
Observe here that a function
f
lies in
\mathcal{E}_{\infty,\sigma}(U)
if and only if there exists a uniform
\tau
such that
f\in\mathcal{E}_{\{\tau,\sigma\}}(K)
for all
K\subset\subset U
and thus the limit classes
\mathcal{E}_{\infty,\sigma}(U)
do not quite fit in the realm of Roumieu-type classes defined via weight matrices since quantifiers are exchanged. In the latter case it is allowed that
\tau
is also depending on
K
; see (
2.3
).

In order to comment on this subtle difference, first for
\sigma\geq 1
let us from now on consider the matrix (using the notation from (
5.2
))

(6.1)

\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}:=\{M^{(\tau,\tau,\sigma)}:~\tau>0\},\hskip 15.0ptM^{(\tau,\tau,\sigma)}_{j}:=\tau^{j^{\sigma}}j^{\tau j^{\sigma}}.

We get the following connection with the respective matrix class defined in terms of
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
U,V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open, and
\overline{V}\subset\subset U
. Then
as locally convex vector spaces we get

(6.2)

\mathcal{E}_{\infty,\sigma}(U)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\{\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}\}}(U)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\infty,\sigma}(V),\hskip 15.0pt\mathcal{E}_{0,\sigma}(U)=\mathcal{E}_{(\mathcal{M}^{\sigma})}(U).

Proof.

The Roumieu case.

The first inclusion is clear from the definition of the respective spaces. For the second one, observe that any
f\in\mathcal{E}_{\{\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}\}}(U)
lies in
\mathcal{E}_{\{M^{(\tau,\tau,\sigma)}\}}(\overline{V})
for some
\tau
, i.e., there exists
h\geq 1
and
A>0
such that for all
x\in\overline{V}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
we have

|f^{(\alpha)}(x)|\leq Ah^{|\alpha|}M^{(\tau,\tau,\sigma)}_{|\alpha|},

and for any
\tau^{\prime}>\tau
, we find
B>0
such that for all
j
we have

h^{j}M^{(\tau,\tau,\sigma)}_{j}\leq BM^{(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime},\sigma)}_{j},

which finishes the Roumieu case.

The Beurling case.

Since there is a universal quantifier for the compact set, the index
\tau
, and the geometric factor
h
, we do not have to worry about interchanging those.
The rest follows from Proposition
5.1
.

∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\sigma\geq 1
and
\mathcal{N}=\{N^{(x)}:x>0\}
be a
(\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{sc}})
and non-quasianalytic weight matrix. Suppose that we have

\mathcal{E}_{\infty,\sigma}(U)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\{\mathcal{N}\}}(U).

Then it follows that

\mathcal{E}_{\infty,\sigma}(U)\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{\{\mathcal{N}\}}(U).

Proof.

Similarly as in
(viii)
in Theorem
5.2
we get

\forall\;\tau_{1}>\tau>0:\;\;\;M^{(\tau,\tau,\sigma)}\lhd M^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{1},\sigma)}.

The inclusion
\mathcal{E}_{\infty,\sigma}(U)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\{\mathcal{N}\}}(U)
and the optimal function
\theta_{M^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{1},\sigma)}}
(see (
4.4
)) implies the following:

(6.3)

\forall\;\tau>0\;\exists\;x>0:\;\;\;M^{(\tau,\tau,\sigma)}\lhd N^{(x)}.

Therefore, note that
\theta_{M^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{1},\sigma)}}\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty,\sigma}(U)
for any
\tau_{1}>0
since the estimate
|\theta_{M^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{1},\sigma)}}(t)|\leq 2^{j+1}\tau_{1}^{j^{\sigma}}j^{\tau_{1}j^{\sigma}}
holds globally on whole
\mathbb{R}
; see again
[
35
, Thm. 1]
,
[
23
, Lemma 2.9]
and the detailed proof in
[
26
, Prop. 3.1.2]
.

Let
K_{j}
be a sequence of mutually disjoint compact sets with non-empty interior contained in
U
such that they accumulate at the boundary of
U
, i.e., for any compact set
K\subset\subset U
there exists
j
such that
K_{j}\cap K=\emptyset
. Let
S_{j}
be also a sequence of compact sets such that
S_{j}\subseteq K_{j}^{\circ}
. Finally, let
x_{j}\in S_{j}
. Then by
[
22
, Cor. 3.12]
there exists
\phi_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{\{N^{(j)}\}}(K_{j}^{\circ})
such that
\phi_{j}\equiv 1
on
S_{j}
. Let
\theta_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{\{N^{(j)}\}}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
|\theta_{j}^{(k)}((x_{j})_{1})|\geq N^{(j)}_{k}
(where
(x_{j})_{1}
is the first component of
x_{j}
), and set
\Theta_{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}):=\theta_{j}(x_{1})
. Finally, set

h_{j}:=\Theta_{j}\phi_{j},\quad h:=\sum_{j}h_{j}.

Then clearly

(and therefore not in

Proof.

In order to shorten the notation, we write in this proof

(i)

(ii)

\displaystyle c(2j)^{\sigma-1}\log(2j)\leq j^{\sigma-1}c_{1}\log(j)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 2^{\sigma-1}\log(2j)\leq\frac{c_{1}}{c}\log(j)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 2^{\sigma-1}\log(2)\leq\log(j)(\frac{c_{1}}{c}-2^{\sigma-1}).

6.2.
PTT-limit classes as Braun-Meise-Taylor classes

Let
\sigma>1
be given. Then, on the one hand
\mathcal{E}_{[\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}]}

cannot
be described by a single weight sequence which follows by
(vii)
in Theorem
6.4
. On the other hand, the aim of this section is to show that it actually can be understood as a Braun-Meise-Taylor class. This question has very recently been studied and solved in
[
33
]
(for the modified defined limit classes mentioned in Remark
6.3
). There the authors give precise asymptotics of
\omega
in terms of the so-called
Lambert function

W
; cf.
[
33
, Prop. 3.1]
. However, we give an independent proof of their main result by involving only weight matrix techniques.

Let us emphasize that for
\sigma=1
this statement is not true: By (
6.1
) the matrix
\mathcal{M}^{1}:=\{M^{(\tau,\tau,1)}:~\tau>0\}
consists of sequences
M^{(\tau,\tau,1)}_{j}=\tau^{j}j^{\tau j}
and hence
\mathcal{M}^{1}
is equivalent to the Gevrey matrix
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\{(j!^{\tau})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}:\tau>0\}
. From (the first paragraph in the proof of)
[
23
, Thm. 5.22]
it follows that the corresponding weight matrix class cannot be described by a space given by a log-convex
M
(in particular
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
) or by a weight function
\omega
.

We prove now an abstract result on the connection between weight sequences and their associated weight functions which is important in the ultradifferentiable setting on its own.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
M,N\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

M
and
N
are related by

(6.4)

\exists\;c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;N_{j}\leq A(M_{cj})^{1/c}.

(ii)

The associated weight functions are related by

\omega_{M}(t)=O(\omega_{N}(t)),\;\;\;t\rightarrow+\infty.

Moreover, the following are equivalent:

(i)^{\prime}

M
and
N
are related by

(6.5)

\forall\;c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;(N_{cj})^{1/c}\leq AM_{j}.

(ii)^{\prime}

The associated weight functions are related by

\omega_{M}(t)=o(\omega_{N}(t)),\;\;\;t\rightarrow+\infty.

Proof.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
We have
\omega_{M}(t)\leq c\omega_{N}(t)+c
for some
c\geq 1
(large) and all
t\geq 0
. W.l.o.g. take
c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and then (
2.2
) yields for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle M_{cj}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{cj}}{\exp(\omega_{M}(t))}\geq\frac{1}{e^{c}}\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{cj}}{\exp(c\omega_{N}(t))}=\frac{1}{e^{c}}\left(\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{j}}{\exp(\omega_{N}(t))}\right)^{c}=\frac{1}{e^{c}}N_{j}^{c}.

Thus (
6.4
) is shown with
A=e
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
For given
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
we set

\widetilde{M}^{c}_{j}:=(M_{cj})^{1/c},

hence
\widetilde{M}^{1}\equiv M
is clear. If
M
is log-convex, then each
\widetilde{M}^{c}
as well and
\widetilde{M}^{c}_{0}=1
if
M_{0}=1
. If
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
, then
\widetilde{M}^{c}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
(for some/any
c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
). Thus by definition and assumption we get

(6.6)

\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega_{\widetilde{M}^{c}}(t)\leq\omega_{N}(t)+\log(A).

We obtain for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}

\widetilde{M}^{c}_{j}:=(M_{cj})^{1/c}=\left(\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{cj}}{\exp(\omega_{M}(t))}\right)^{1/c}.

Moreover, since
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
we get
\omega_{M}(t)=0
for
t\in[0,1]
(i.e., normalization) and so:

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}^{c}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{j}}{\exp(c^{-1}\omega_{M}(t))}=\sup_{t\geq 1}\frac{t^{j}}{\exp(c^{-1}\omega_{M}(t))}=\exp(\sup_{t\geq 1}j\log(t)-c^{-1}\omega_{M}(t))

\displaystyle=\exp(\sup_{s\geq 0}js-c^{-1}\omega_{M}(e^{s}))=:\exp(\varphi^{*}_{c^{-1}\omega_{M}}(j)).

Thus we may apply
[
8
, Lemma 2.5]
to
\omega\equiv c^{-1}\omega_{M}
and get

c^{-1}\omega_{M}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\omega_{\widetilde{M}^{c}}.

More precisely, by setting the weight matrix parameter
x=1
, we see

(6.7)

\exists\;D>0\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega_{\widetilde{M}^{c}}(t)\leq c^{-1}\omega_{M}(t)\leq 2\omega_{\widetilde{M}^{c}}(t)+D.

Combining (
6.7
) with (
6.6
) immediately yields

\frac{1}{2c}\omega_{M}(t)-\frac{D}{2}\leq\omega_{\widetilde{M}^{c}}(t)\leq\omega_{N}(t)+\log(A)\Longrightarrow\omega_{M}(t)\leq 2c\omega_{N}(t)+Dc+2c\log(A).

Thus
\omega_{M}(t)=O(\omega_{N}(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
is verified.

(ii)^{\prime}\Rightarrow(i)^{\prime}
For all
c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
we can find
D\geq 1
such that
\omega_{M}(t)\leq\frac{1}{c}\omega_{N}(t)+D
for all
t\geq 0
and so, analogously as before, we obtain (
6.5
) with
A:=e^{D}
.

(i)^{\prime}\Rightarrow(ii)^{\prime}
Using the notation from above, (
6.6
) transfers into

(6.8)

\forall\;c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega_{M}(t)\leq\omega_{\widetilde{N}^{c}}(t)+\log(A).

Then we follow the arguments in
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
and combine (
6.8
) with the first half from (
6.7
) applied to
N
in order to get

\forall\;c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega_{M}(t)\leq c^{-1}\omega_{N}(t)+\log(A).

Thus
\omega_{M}(t)=o(\omega_{N}(t))
is verified.
∎

The importance of Lemma
6.5
is that it enables the possibility to express all requirements in
[
29
, Cor. 3.17
(ii)
]
purely in terms of the given matrix
\mathcal{N}
directly:

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be
(\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{sc}})
. Then as locally convex vector spaces

\mathcal{E}_{[\mathcal{M}]}=\mathcal{E}_{[\omega]},

with
\omega
being a weight function in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor (see
[
3
]
,
[
29
]
) if and only if there exists a
(\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{sc}})
matrix
\mathcal{N}=\{N^{(\alpha)}:\alpha>0\}
which is
R
- resp.
B
-equivalent to
\mathcal{M}
and such that

(*)

\mathcal{N}
has
(\mathcal{M}_{[\operatorname{L}]})
,

(*)

\mathcal{N}
has
(\mathcal{M}_{[\operatorname{mg}]})
,

(*)

\mathcal{N}
has (cf. (
6.4
))

(6.9)

\forall\;\alpha,\beta>0\;\exists\;c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;N^{(\alpha)}_{j}\leq A(N^{(\beta)}_{cj})^{1/c}.

Remark 6.7
.

Any
\omega_{N^{(\alpha)}}
(for
N^{(\alpha)}\in\mathcal{N}
) is a valid choice for
\omega
in Corollary
6.6
.

Note that (
6.9
) is clearly preserved under R- and B-equivalence of weight matrices.

In particular we can apply this statement to PTT-limit classes and get the following.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
\sigma>1
and put
\omega^{(\sigma)}:=\omega_{M^{(1,1,\sigma)}}
; i.e. the associated weight function of
M^{(1,1,\sigma)}=(j^{j^{\sigma}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then as locally convex vector spaces we get

(6.10)

\mathcal{E}_{[\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}]}=\mathcal{E}_{[\omega^{(\sigma)}]}.

Moreover, all associated weight functions of the matrix
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}
are equivalent, i.e.,

\forall\;h,h^{\prime},\tau,\tau^{\prime}>0:\;\;\;\omega_{M^{(h,\tau,\sigma)}}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\omega_{M^{(h^{\prime},\tau^{\prime},\sigma)}},

and consequently in (
6.10
) we can replace
\omega^{(\sigma)}
by any
\omega_{M^{(h,\tau,\sigma)}}
.

Proof.

We only have to verify (
6.9
) in Corollary
6.6
for the matrix
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}
(the first two conditions are contained in Theorem
6.4
). But this is clear for our concrete matrix
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}
since (
6.9
) holds for
\beta\geq\alpha
taking

and for
\beta<\alpha
we have that

is bounded from above for

large enough. ∎

The previous result fails for

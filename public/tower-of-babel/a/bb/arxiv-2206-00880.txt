Abstract
. Previously the authors proved subconvexity of Shintani’s zeta function enumerating class numbers of binary cubic forms. Here we return to prove subconvexity of the Maass form twisted version.

1.
Introduction

In a previous paper
[
14
]
, the authors proved the subconvexity of Shintani’s zeta function enumerating class numbers of binary cubic forms. The purpose of this article is to demonstrate that the same approach proves subconvexity for the Maass cusp form twisted version of the zeta function
[
12
]
. This is of interest because F. Sato
[
18
]
has demonstrated a general method of twisting zeta functions of prehomogeneous vector spaces with automorphic forms, creating a large class of interesting arithmetic objects, some of which can be proved to be subconvex in this way.

Let
V_{\mathbb{Z}}=\{f(x,y)=ax^{3}+bx^{2}y+cxy^{2}+dy^{3}:a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be the space of integral binary cubic forms and let
\phi
be a Maass cusp form for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The cusp form twisted zeta function is

(1)

{\mathscr{L}}^{\pm}(\phi,s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash V_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
\pm\operatorname{Disc}(f)>0\end{subarray}}\frac{\phi(f)}{|\operatorname{Stab}(f)|}\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|^{s}},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

Although this zeta function does not satisfy a known functional equation, its analytic growth is degree four as for the untwisted zeta function and it has holomorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
. Parallel to the subconvex estimate for the Shintani zeta function, we prove the following subconvex estimate on the critical line for the twisted version.

Theorem 1
.

The twisted Shintani zeta function satisfies the subconvex bound, for any
\epsilon>0
,

(2)

{\mathscr{L}}^{\pm}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\tau,\phi\right)\ll_{\epsilon,\phi}\tau^{\frac{26}{27}+\epsilon}

as
\tau\to\infty
.

This can be compared to an error term of
\tau^{\frac{98}{99}+\epsilon}
for the untwisted zeta function, proved in
[
14
]
.

1.1.
Discussion of method

Since the twisted version of the Shintani zeta function does not satisfy a known functional equation, a significant part of extending the subconvexity estimate in the untwisted case to the twisted case lies in developing an analogue of the approximate functional equation for this zeta function, developed directly from the theory of prehomogeneous vector space zeta functions. Once this is developed, methods in the same spirit as those used on the original zeta function suffice to prove the subconvexity. These include translation between Cartesian and homogeneous coordinates, and van der Corput’s method of exponential sums to obtain cancellation in exponential sums.

2.2.
Automorphic forms

We consider Maass form
\phi
which is a smooth function on
\Gamma\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
that is an eigenfunction of the Casimir operator (hyperbolic Laplacian) and Hecke algebra. The Maass form
\phi
has a Fourier expansion

(15)

\phi\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&u\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\
0&\frac{1}{t}\end{pmatrix}\right)=2t\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_{\phi}(n)K_{\nu}(2\pi nt^{2})\cos(2\pi nu).

This satisfies
\phi(g)=\phi((g^{-1})^{t})=\phi(g^{\iota})
. The Fourier coefficients satisfy the Hecke relations

(16)

\rho_{\phi}(m)\rho_{\phi}(n)=\sum_{d|(m,n)}\rho_{\phi}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right).

The bound
|\rho_{\phi}(n)|\ll n^{\frac{7}{64}+\epsilon}
is proved in
[
16
]
, while
\sum_{n\leq X}|\rho_{\phi}(n)|^{2}\ll X
follows from Rankin-Selberg Theory.

The
K
-Bessel function satisfies exponential decay in large argument,
K_{\nu}(z)\ll e^{-z}
as
z\to\infty
, so that
\phi
decays rapidly in large
t
. Several integral identities involving the
K
-Bessel function are used,

(17)

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{\nu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-t-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}\right)\frac{dt}{t^{\nu+1}}

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(xz)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)(2z)^{\nu}}{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\nu}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(xt)}{(t^{2}+z^{2})^{\nu+\frac{1}{2}}}dt

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(z)K_{\nu}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{2}-\frac{z^{2}+\zeta^{2}}{2t}\right)K_{\nu}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}.

The Mellin transform of a pair of
K
Bessel functions has the following evaluation.

Lemma 4
.

We have in
\operatorname{Re}(\alpha+\beta)>0
and
\operatorname{Re}(s)>|\operatorname{Re}(\mu)|+|\operatorname{Re}(\nu)|
,

(18)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{\mu}(\alpha x)K_{\nu}(\beta x)x^{s-1}dx

\displaystyle=2^{s-3}\alpha^{-s-\nu}\beta^{\nu}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(s+\mu+\nu)\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(s-\mu-\nu)\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{\frac{s-\mu+\nu}{2}-1}(1-t)^{\frac{s+\mu-\nu}{2}-1}\left(1-\left(1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)t\right)^{-\frac{s+\mu+\nu}{2}}dt.

Proof.

The claimed Mellin transform can be evaluated in terms of Gauss’s Hypergeometric function,

(19)

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b,c;z)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-b)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{b-1}(1-t)^{c-b-1}(1-tz)^{-a}dt,\qquad|z|<1.

We have in
\operatorname{Re}(\alpha+\beta)>0
and
\operatorname{Re}(s)>|\operatorname{Re}(\mu)|+|\operatorname{Re}(\nu)|
, (
[
1
]
p.334 eqn (47))

(20)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle K_{\mu}(\alpha x)K_{\nu}(\beta x)x^{s-1}dx=\frac{2^{s-3}\alpha^{-s-\nu}\beta^{\nu}}{\Gamma(s)}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(s+\mu+\nu)\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(s-\mu+\nu)\right)

\displaystyle\times\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(s+\mu-\nu)\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(s-\mu-\nu)\right){}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{s+\mu+\nu}{2},\frac{s-\mu+\nu}{2},s;1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right).

Combining the evaluations proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5
.

If
\alpha>\beta>0
and for some
\epsilon>0
,
\operatorname{Re}(s)>|\operatorname{Re}(\mu)|+|\operatorname{Re}(\nu)|+\epsilon
, then

(21)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{\mu}(\alpha x)K_{\nu}(\beta x)x^{s-1}dx

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon}2^{\operatorname{Re}(s)-3}\alpha^{\operatorname{Re}(\mu)}\beta^{-\operatorname{Re}(s+\mu)}

\displaystyle\times\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\mu+\nu}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s-\mu-\nu}{2}\right)\right|.

Proof.

Bound

(22)

\left(1-\left(1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)t\right)^{-\frac{s+\mu+\nu}{2}}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\operatorname{Re}(s+\mu+\nu)}}{\beta^{\operatorname{Re}(s+\mu+\nu)}},

which is the value at
t=1
.
Bound the rest of the integral in
t
by a constant depending on
\epsilon
. This gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 6
.

The automorphic form is an eigenfunction of the convolution equation

(23)

\int_{G^{1}}\exp(-\operatorname{tr}g^{t}g)\phi(hg^{-1})dg=C\phi(h)

with eigenvalue
C=\sqrt{\pi}K_{\nu}(2).

Proof.

The convolution is right
K
invariant by definition, since
\exp(-\operatorname{tr}g^{t}g)
is, and is an eigenfunction of the Casimir operators and Hecke algebra, with the same eigenvalues as
\phi
, by passing the operators under the integral and applying them to
\phi
. By strong multiplicity 1, the convolution is a multiple of
\phi
.

To evaluate the eigenvalue, we calculate

(24)

\frac{1}{\phi(\operatorname{id})}\int_{G^{1}}\phi(g^{-1})\exp(-\operatorname{tr}g^{t}g)dg.

Use
\phi(g^{-1})=\phi(g^{t})
, and integrate away the compact subgroup
K
under which the integrand is invariant. The integral becomes

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(\operatorname{id})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-t^{2}-\frac{1}{t^{2}}-\left(\frac{u}{t}\right)^{2}\right)2t\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_{\phi}(n)K_{\nu}(2\pi nt^{2})\cos(2\pi nu)du\frac{dt}{t^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_{\phi}(n)}{\phi(\operatorname{id})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-t^{2}-\frac{1}{t^{2}}-u^{2}\right)K_{\nu}(2\pi nt^{2})\cos(2\pi nut)du\frac{dt}{t}.

Integrating the Gaussian in
u
obtains

(26)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}\frac{2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_{\phi}(n)}{\phi(\operatorname{id})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-t^{2}(1+\pi^{2}n^{2})-\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)K_{\nu}(2\pi nt^{2})\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}\frac{2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_{\phi}(n)}{\phi(\operatorname{id})}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{2}-\frac{4+4\pi^{2}n^{2}}{2t}\right)K_{\nu}\left(\frac{4\pi n}{t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}K_{\nu}(2)\frac{2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_{\phi}(n)K_{\nu}(2\pi n)}{\phi(\operatorname{id})}=\sqrt{\pi}K_{\nu}(2).

∎

2.3.
The prehomogeneous vector space of binary cubic forms

Estimates for oscillatory integrals

This section collects together estimates for oscillatory integrals used in proving Theorem
2
. Stein
[
21
]
gives a thorough treatment of oscillatory integrals as used in this section. In one variable, the standard type integral is

(52)

I(\lambda)=\int_{a}^{b}e^{i\lambda\phi(x)}\psi(x)dx

where
\phi,\psi
are smooth,
\phi^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
and
\psi
is compactly supported in
[a,b]
. Define the differential operator
Df(x)=\frac{1}{i\lambda\phi^{\prime}(x)}\frac{df}{dx}
, with transpose operator
D^{t}f=-\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{f}{i\lambda\phi^{\prime}(x)}\right)
. Then
D^{N}e^{i\lambda\phi(x)}=e^{i\lambda\phi(x)}
, and hence, repeated integration by parts obtains, for any
N\geq 0
,

(53)

\int_{a}^{b}e^{i\lambda\phi(x)}\psi(x)dx=\int_{a}^{b}e^{i\lambda\phi(x)}(D^{t})^{N}(\psi)(x)dx.

The following lemma is useful for giving uniform estimates.

Lemma 12
.

The operator
(D^{t})^{N}\psi
is a sum of monomials with coefficients bounded only in terms of
N
,

(54)

(D^{t})^{N}(\psi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=(\alpha_{0},...,\alpha_{k})\\
|\alpha|=N\end{subarray}}C_{N,\alpha}\frac{\partial^{\alpha_{0}}\psi\partial^{\alpha_{1}+1}\phi\partial^{\alpha_{2}+1}\phi...\partial^{\alpha_{k}+1}\phi}{\lambda^{N}(\phi^{\prime})^{N+k}}.

Proof.

Applying
D^{t}

N
times differentiates a total of
N
times, multiplies by a factor of
\lambda

N
times and divides by
\phi^{\prime}

N
times. Each derivative goes on either
\psi
, the denominator, or a derivative of
\phi
in the numerator. Differentiating the denominator increases the power of the denominator by 1, and introduces a new factor of a derivative of
\phi
in the numerator.
∎

5.
The approximate functional equation in the twisted case

The goal of this Section is to prove Theorem
2
.

Choose a test function
F_{\pm}(g\cdot f_{\pm})=n_{\pm}\lambda^{2k}\exp\left(-\lambda^{2}-t^{2}-\frac{1}{t^{2}}-\left(\frac{u}{t}\right)^{2}\right)
with

sufficiently large. Notice that, for
x\in S
in the singular set, to approach
x
in homogeneous coordinates from
V_{\pm}
we must have
\lambda\downarrow 0
and hence at least one of
u
,
t
or
\frac{1}{t}
becomes unbounded, and hence
F_{\pm}
is
C^{\infty}
on
V_{\mathbb{R}}
due to the exponential decay.

The orbital zeta function is the function

(102)

\Lambda^{\pm}(s,\phi,F_{\pm})=\int_{G^{+}/\Gamma}\phi(g^{-1})(\det g)^{2s}\sum_{x\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{\pm}}F_{\pm}(g\cdot x)dg.

The advantage of the test function is that on the time side, integration against the Maass form can be written as a convolution equation that has the Maass form as an eigenfunction. We don’t currently know of a test function which satisfies this property on the Fourier side as well, so that the convolution equation on the Fourier side will be analyzed directly.

Lemma 20
.

We have the factorization in
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1

(103)

Proof.

We have

(104)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\pm}(s,\phi,F_{\pm})

\displaystyle=\int_{G^{+}}\phi(g^{-1})(\det g)^{2s}\sum_{x\in\Gamma\backslash V_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{\pm}}\frac{F_{\pm}(g\cdot x)}{|\operatorname{Stab}(x)|}dg

\displaystyle=\int_{G^{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(g^{-1})\lambda^{4s}\sum_{x\in\Gamma\backslash V_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{\pm}}\frac{F_{\pm}(\lambda g\cdot x)}{|\operatorname{Stab}(x)|}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}dg

. The integral in

\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(2s+k)

∎

By the Poisson summation formula,

\displaystyle=\int_{G^{1}/\Gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{4(1-s)}\phi(g^{-1})\sum_{\xi\in\hat{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap S}\hat{F}_{\pm}\left(\lambda g\cdot\xi\right)\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}dg.

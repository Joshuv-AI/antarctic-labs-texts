On Structural Non-commutativity in Affine Feedback of SISO Nonlinear Systems Supported by MASCOT project in UiT The Arctic Univeristy of Tromsø.
Venkatesh G. S

Abstract
The affine feedback connection of SISO nonlinear systems modeled by Chen–Fliess series is shown to be a group action on the plant which is isomorphic to the semi-direct product of shuffle and additive group of non-commutative formal power series. The additive and multiplicative feedback loops in an affine feedback connection are thus proven to be structurally non-commutative. A flip in the order of these loops results in a net additive feedback loop.



keywords

Nonlinear Systems, Affine Feedback, Structural Non-commutativity, Chen–Fliess Series, Formal Groups, Formal Lie Algebras.

1
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to
quantify
the structural non-commutativity between the additive and multiplicative feedback loops in an
affine feedback
connection of single-input single-output(SISO) nonlinear systems modeled by
Chen–Fliess
series. Chen–Fliess series is an weighted iterated integral series that models the input–output behavior of a (input-affine) nonlinear system. The prologue of using input-output maps for analysis of systems is that it is unique to the system (under analysis) and hence a coordinate free approach. Thus any properties proven via this paradigm is intrinsic while the state space realizations are co-ordinate dependent. The argument is akin to significance of Laplace-domain transfer functions/ time-domain input-output maps to state-space modeling in the domain of linear time-invariant systems. Affine feedback (as shown in Figure
1
) is a pivotal interconnection in the design of both static and dynamic feedback linearization of nonlinear systems
(
Isidori 1995
;
Gray & Ebrahimi-Fard 2017
)
.

Figure 1
:
Affine Feedback Interconnection

The affine feedback interconnection of nonlinear systems modeled by Chen–Fliess was first dealt in the work of
Gray & Ebrahimi-Fard 2017
. The affine feedback group (transformation group in the affine feedback interconnection) was explicitly detailed there along with the Hopf algebra of coordinate functions on formal power series which provides a computational framework to compute the group product and the inverse. The work of
Foissy 2018
has defined a
post-Lie
algebra purely from the Hopf algebra of coordinate functions corresponding to the affine feedback. Recently in the work of
Ebrahimi-Fard et al. 2023
, the affine feedback group is proved to be the
Grossman–Larson
or derived group of a
post-group
(post group is the image of exponential map in the universal enveloping algebra of the post-Lie algebra). The document will not delve into post-groups and post-Lie algebras. For more details on post-groups and post-Lie algebras, refer
Al-Kaabi et al. 2023
.

The document shows firstly that affine feedback is a group action on the plant, where the group is same as the group in the definition of post-group (explained in Section
4
). The commutator of two subgroups, corresponding to the multiplicative feedback loop and additive feedback loop in Figure
1
quantifies the structural non-commutativity between the loops viz., if one flips the order between the inner(additive) and outer(multiplicative) loops, the overall input–output map (
v\mapsto y
) will not remain the same. As such, proving the
existence
of structural non-commutativity is not a difficult question but the contribution of the paper stands out in
quantifying
it via the commutator of subgroups corresponding to additive and multiplicative feedback loops (in its Lie algebra at the infinitesimal level).

The organization of the document is as follows: Section
2
introduces notations and defines the shuffle algebra of formal power series. Section
3
gives a brief on Chen–Fliess series. Section
4
defines the affine feedback transformation group and recollects some of the material from the works of
Gray & Ebrahimi-Fard 2017
and
Ebrahimi-Fard et al. 2023
. Further, this section leads up to the main results of this document.

Formal Power Series over

The section recalls some preliminaries on non-commutative formal power series and bialgebras. For more details regarding the latter, we refer to

Let

(i)

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\scriptscriptstyle\;\sqcup\hskip-1.42271pt\sqcup\;}}x_{i}x_{j}

\displaystyle=x_{i}x_{j}\otimes\mathbf{1}+x_{i}\otimes x_{j}+x_{j}\otimes x_{i}+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\mathbf{1}\otimes x_{i}x_{j},

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\scriptscriptstyle\;\sqcup\hskip-1.42271pt\sqcup\;}}x_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=x_{i}^{2}\otimes\mathbf{1}+2x_{i}\otimes x_{i}+\mathbf{1}\otimes x_{i}^{2}.

\displaystyle c^{{\scriptscriptstyle\;\sqcup\hskip-1.42271pt\sqcup\;}-1}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(\hbox{$1$}-c)^{{\scriptscriptstyle\;\sqcup\hskip-1.42271pt\sqcup\;}k}.

3
Chen–Fliess Series

The section acts as a review of Chen–Fliess series and the affine feedback interconnection. Let

L_{1}

\displaystyle U(t)=\int_{0}^{t}d\tau u(\tau).

Define a monoid morphism

\displaystyle F_{x_{1}\eta}[u](t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}dU_{i}(\tau)F_{\eta}[u](\tau),

\displaystyle F_{x_{0}\eta}[u](t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}d\tau F_{\eta}[u](\tau),

where
\eta\in X^{+}\cup\{\emptyset\}
. The monoid morphism implies
F_{\emptyset}[u](t)=1

\displaystyle F_{c}[u](t)=c(\hbox{$1$})+\sum_{\eta\in X^{+}}c(\eta)F_{\eta}[u](t).~\quad\text{\cite[citep]{(\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{Fliess_81}{\@@citephrase{, }}{})}}

to algebra of functions viz.

for all
c,d\in\mbox{${\mathbb{R}}\langle\langle X\rangle\rangle$}

)

The composition of Chen–Fliess series

, as illustrated in Figure

. The formal power series

is called the composition product of

\displaystyle c\circ d=\sum_{\eta\in X^{*}}c\left(\eta\right)\eta\circ d,

(1)

where the composition product restricted to words is inductively defined as

)
:

\displaystyle\emptyset\circ d

\displaystyle=\emptyset,

\displaystyle x_{0}\eta\circ d

\displaystyle=x_{0}\left(\eta\circ d\right),

(2)

\displaystyle x_{1}\eta\circ d

\displaystyle=x_{0}\left(d{\scriptscriptstyle\;\sqcup\hskip-1.42271pt\sqcup\;}\left(\eta\circ d\right)\right),

for all

. The following theorem states that composition product distributes over the shuffle product on the left.

Theorem 3.1

(
Ferfera 1980
)
Let
c,d
and

, then

Let
B_{p}(R,T):=\{u\in L_{p}[0,T]:\|u\|_{p}<R\}

is the usual

-norm. A Chen–Fliess

is called

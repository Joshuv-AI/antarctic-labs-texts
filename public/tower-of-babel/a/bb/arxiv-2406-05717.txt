Banach algebras associated to twisted étale groupoids: simplicity and pure infiniteness
Krzysztof Bardadyn, Bartosz Kwaśniewski, Andrew McKee

Abstract
. We define reduced and essential Banach algebras associated to a twisted étale (not necessarily Hausdorff) groupoid ( 𝒢 , ℒ ) (\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}) and extend some fundamental results from C ∗ C^{*} -algebras to this context. We prove that for topologically free groupoids the associated essential Banach algebras have the ideal intersection property, and thus such an algebra is simple if and only if the groupoid is minimal. We give conditions under which reduced algebras are essential (for example Hausdorffness of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is sufficient). This in particular solves the simplicity problem posed by Gardella–Lupini for L p L^{p} -operator algebras associated to 𝒢 \mathcal{G} . In addition, using either the n n -filling or locally contracting condition we give pure infiniteness criteria for essential simple Banach algebras associated to ( 𝒢 , ℒ ) (\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}) . This extends the corresponding C ∗ C^{*} -algebraic results that were previously known to hold in the untwisted Hausdorff case. The results work nicely, and allow for characterization of the generalized intersection property, in the realm of L P L^{P} -operator algebras where P ⊆ [ 1 , ∞ ] P\subseteq[1,\infty] is a non-empty set of parameters. Such algebras cover in particular L p L^{p} -operator algebras, for p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ] p\in[1,\infty] , and their Banach ∗ * -algebra versions. We apply our results to Banach algebra crossed products by twisted partial group actions, Roe-type Banach algebras with coefficients in finite-rank operators on a Banach space, twisted tight L P L^{P} -operator algebras of inverse semigroups, graph L P L^{P} -operator algebras, and L P L^{P} -versions of Exel-Pardo algebras.

1.
Introduction

The characterization of simplicity of the crossed product of a discrete group action on a topological space,
[
KT90
]
,
[
AS93
]
, is one of the most fundamental facts from the theory of
C^{*}
-algebras associated to dynamical systems.
The dynamical conditions appearing there,
topological freeness
and
minimality
, are easy to check, applicable to a number of actions, and, most importantly, they are also sufficient for simplicity of twisted reduced crossed products.
This classical result was generalized to twisted actions of second countable Hausdorff groupoids in
[
Ren91
, Corollary 4.6]
, though it seemed to gain more attention when phrased in terms of second countable Hausdorff étale groupoids in
[
Exe11
]
,
[
BCFS14
]
.
One boost came from Renault’s theory of Cartan inclusions
[
Ren08
]
, as they are modeled by twisted étale Hausdorff groupoids satisfying a version of topological freeness for groupoids, which in the separable case is
topological principality
.
Since the example of Skandalis from the Appendice to
[
Ren91
]
, see also
[
Exe11
]
, it was unclear how to formulate simplicity criteria in the non-Hausdorff case.
On the other hand, a number of important constructions lead to non-Hausdorff étale groupoids, cf.
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
, and recently, after a significant breakthrough obtained in
[

]
,
[
KM21
]

In this article, we present a cleaned version of this modern theory.
The main achievement is that we develop a well-oiled machinery that works not only for
C^{*}
-algebras but in the much broader setting of Banach algebras.
By removing a few important stumbling blocks in this setting, such as the simplicity problem posed in
[
GL17
]
, this should open the door to many new applications and research directions connecting the current theories of
C^{*}
-algebras, Banach algebras and harmonic analysis.

For

-algebra

associated to
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
(or a real version of it).
To cover other relevant Banach algebras appearing in the literature, such as group Banach algebras
[

]
,
[
LY17
]
,
[

]
, Banach algebra crossed products
[

]
,
[

, Cuntz Banach algebras
[

[

]

*
-Banach algebras associated to groupoids
[

]
,
[

, in general we consider Banach algebras

that are completions of the underlying convolution algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
of quasi-continuous sections in some seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}}
bounded by the norm coming from some
S

We view the twist
\mathcal{L}
over

as a Fell line bundle over

In this paper, we turn to the study of simplicity and pure infiniteness of algebras of the form
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
This requires introducing
reduced groupoid Banach algebras
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and in the non-Hausdorff case also
essential groupoid Banach algebras
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
As it is simpler, we discuss first the case when the étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff.
This holds if and only if its locally compact Hausdorff unit space

is a closed subset of
\mathcal{G}
.
Then the convolution algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=C_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
consists of compactly supported continuous sections.
We say that a Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is

if it is a completion of
C_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
such that the inclusion map
C_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\subseteq C_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
extends to an injective contractive map
j_{\mathcal{R}}:F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to C_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
which isometric on
C_{0}(X)
, and so
C_{0}(X)
is naturally a subalgebra of
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
This definition covers reduced group algebras
[
Phi19
]
, reduced crossed products
[
BK24
]
, as well as the reduced
L^{p}
-algebras
F^{p}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
,

studied in
[
GL17
]
,
[
CGT24
]
,
[
AO22
]
,
[
HO23
]
,
[
BKM25
]
.
Following
[

]
, we generalize
L^{p}
-operator algebras to
L^{P}
-operator algebras where
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
is a non-empty set of parameters.
In particular, we introduce the full
F^{P}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and reduced
F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
groupoid
L^{P}
-algebras.

The main difficulty is to establish the relationship between
topological freeness
of
\mathcal{G}
, as defined in
[
KM21
]
, and the
intersection property
for
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, which means that
I\cap C_{0}(X)\neq\{0\}
for every non-zero (closed two-sided) ideal
I
in
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
; equivalentely, every representation of
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
which is injective on
C_{0}(X)
is injective on
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
We achieve this by establishing the relationships between the canonical conditional expectation onto
C_{0}(X)
and the map
j_{\mathcal{R}}
.
Here the main idea is to extrapolate the conditional expectation through bisections and the crucial technical tool is the pinching lemma (Lemma
5.8
).
For a Hausdorff groupoid, topological freeness is equivalent to
effectiveness
, that is, the interior of the isotropy bundle being only the unit space.
In particular, combining Proposition
5.11
and Theorems
5.10
,
5.16
,
5.13
we have the following result:

Theorem A
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
be a twisted groupoid where
\mathcal{G}
is a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid with unit space
X
.
Let
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
be any non-empty set of parameters and let
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
be any reduced twisted groupoid Banach algebra, which
is graded by some unital inverse semigroup
S
of bisections covering
\mathcal{G}
.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{G}
is topologically free (equivalently effective);

(2)

C_{0}(X)
is a maximal abelian subalgebra in
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
;

(3)

for every ideal
I
in the (untwisted) full algebra
F^{P}(\mathcal{G})
, if
I\cap C_{0}(X)=0
then
I\subseteq\ker\Lambda_{P}
, where
\Lambda:F^{P}(\mathcal{G})\to F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{G})
is the regular representation;

(4)

C_{0}(X)\subseteq F^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{G})
has the intersection property for some (and hence any)
P_{0}\subseteq\{1,\infty\}
;

(5)

the inclusion
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
has the intersection property for every open subgroupoid
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
containing
X
.

If the above equivalent conditions hold, then
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
has the intersection property.

The equivalence between
(1)
and
(3)
is a far reaching generalization of the Kawamura–Tomiyama theorem
[
KT90
]
,
[
AS93
]
, see also
[
KM21
, Theorem 7.29]
.
Condition
(4)
is a special case of
(3)
because for
P\subseteq\{1,\infty\}
we have

F^{P}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
– these algebras are both full and reduced, see
[
BKM25
]
.
Condition
(5)
is reminiscent of the recently discovered fact that topological freeness (aperiodicity) is equivalent to the intersection property for all intermediate
C^{*}
-algebras between
C_{0}(X)
and the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=F_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, see
[
KM22
, Theorem 7.3]
.
As an immediate corollary we get the following simplicity criteria (where by simplicity of a Banach algebra we mean lack of non-trivial closed two-sided ideals):

Corollary B
.

Retaining the notation of Theorem
A
, the reduced twisted groupoid Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is simple and contains
C_{0}(X)
as a maximal abelian subalgebra if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and minimal.

Corollary
B
applies to reduced algebras
F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
for
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
.
In particular, it solves in positive the problem posed in
[
GL17
, Problem 8.2]
, which asked whether minimality and topological principality of a (second countable Hausdorff) groupoid
\mathcal{G}
implies simplicity of
F^{p}_{\red}(\mathcal{G})
for
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Combining Theorem
A
with
[
GL17
, Theorem 6.19]
, we get the following characterization of simplicity of full
L^{P}
-operator algebras for amenable (untwisted) groupoids.

Corollary C
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is an amenable second countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid, then for any non-empty set
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
we have that

(1)

the inclusion
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F^{P}(\mathcal{G})
has the intersection property
if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free;

(2)

the algebra
F^{P}(\mathcal{G})
is simple
if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and minimal.

In the non-Hausdorff setting, we generalize the above results by replacing reduced Banach algebras with essential ones.
Reduced algebras
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
are completions of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
equipped with a canonical injective contractive map
j_{\mathcal{R}}:F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
where
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is the completion of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
(see Definition
3.1
).
By an
essential Banach algebra
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
we mean a Hausdorff completion
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
equipped with an injective contractive map
j_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ess}:F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})/\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, where
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is the subspace of
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
consisting of functions with meager open support (see Definition
4.9
).
Below we phrase these definitions in terms of existence and faithfulness of canonical generalized expectations, which makes clear that they are consistent with existing
C^{*}
-algebraic notions.
Every reduced Banach algebra can be quotiented by an ideal to get an essential Banach algebra. There are cases when this ideal is zero, for instance when
\mathcal{G}
is ample and every compact open set in
\mathcal{G}
is regular (see Lemma
4.8
below).

Finally, we introduce the notion of pure infiniteness for simple (not necessarily unital) Banach algebras, which is consistent with the previous notions of this type (see Definition
6.1
and Proposition
6.3
).
We modify the classical conditions of locally contracting
[
A-D97
]
, and

-filling
[

]
,
[
Suz17
]
, to include the inverse semigroup

of bisections in

(Definitions

,

).
Combining Theorems

,

and
6.13

Theorem D
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
be a twisted étale groupoid where
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and minimal.
Let
S
be any unital inverse semigroup of bisections covering
\mathcal{G}
.
Then every
S
-graded essential Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is simple.
If in addition
S
consists of bisections where the bundle
\mathcal{L}
is trivial and
\mathcal{G}
is
locally contracting or
n
-filling with respect to
S
, then
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is purely infinite simple.

Groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras are always graded by the full inverse semigroup of bisections
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
.
Already in this context, the above pure infiniteness criteria are new, as until now they were known to work only in the Hausdorff untwisted case.
In general, we need to assume local contractiveness or
n
-filling with respect to the inverse semigroup
S(\mathcal{L})
consisting of bisections where the bundle
\mathcal{L}
is trivial.
But when the bundle
\mathcal{L}
is trivial, equivalently
\mathcal{L}
is coming from a groupoid cocycle, we do not need to mention inverse semigroups at all, and we get for instance:

Corollary E
.

Retaining the assumptions on
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
from Theorem
D
, assume that
\mathcal{L}
comes from a cocycle and
\mathcal{G}
is either locally contracting or
n
-filling.
Let
\emptyset\neq P\subseteq[1,\infty]
.
Then
F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
has a simple quotient
F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
which is purely infinite.
This quotient is just
F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
if
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff or more generally when
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=\{0\}
.

If
\mathcal{G}
can be covered by a countable family of open bisections, then the quotient
F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in the above corollary has a natural injective representation on a direct sum of
L^{p}
-spaces,
p\in P
, and it is natural to view the corresponding Banach algebra as the
essential
L^{P}
-groupoid algebra
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
(see Corollary
4.19
).

We illustrate our results by using them to generalize and extend the main results of
[

]
from global to partial actions (Subsection

).
We present a general groupoid picture for Roe-type Banach algebras of uniformly locally finite coarse structures with coefficients in the compact operators on some Banach space.
Such algebras occur naturally, for instance in

]
,

]

We introduce reduced and essential twisted tight

This extends, and sometimes improves, parts of

]

It also covers all

Using groupoid methods we extend the main results of

Finally, we generalize Exel–Pardo algebras

2.
Preliminaries

We recall here some notation and facts from
[
BKM25
]
.
Throughout this paper
\mathcal{G}
denotes an étale groupoid with locally compact Hausdorff unit space
X
.
Thus the range and domain maps
r,d:\mathcal{G}\to X
are local homeomorphisms,
X
is an open subset of
\mathcal{G}
, and
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff if and only if
X
is also closed in
\mathcal{G}
.
We consider spaces, and algebras, over the field
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
of real or complex numbers.
By an ideal in a Banach algebra, unless stated otherwise, we mean a closed and two-sided ideal.

2.1.
Twisted groupoids

As in
[
BKM25
, Section 4]
, we define twists of
\mathcal{G}
as Fell line bundles.
A
line bundle

\mathcal{L}
over
\mathcal{G}
is a topological space
\mathcal{L}=\bigsqcup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}}L_{\gamma}
such that the canonical map
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{G}
is continuous and open, and each fiber
L_{\gamma}\cong\mathbb{F}
is a one dimensional Banach space with a structure consistent with the topology of
\mathcal{L}
in the sense that the maps

\bigsqcup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}}L_{\gamma}\times L_{\gamma}\ni(z,w)\mapsto z+w\in\mathcal{L}
,

\mathbb{F}\times\mathcal{L}\ni(\lambda,z)\mapsto\lambda z\in\mathcal{L}
and

\mathcal{L}\ni z\mapsto|z|\in\mathbb{R}
are continuous.
For any open
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, we write
C_{c}(U,\mathcal{L})
,
C_{0}(U,\mathcal{L})
,
C(U,\mathcal{L})
and
C_{u}(U,\mathcal{L})
for the spaces of continuous sections of
\mathcal{L}|_{U}:=\bigsqcup_{\gamma\in U}L_{\gamma}
that are compactly supported, vanishing at infinity, bounded and unitary, respectively.
We use similar notation
\mathcal{B}_{c}(U,\mathcal{L})
,
\mathcal{B}_{0}(U,\mathcal{L})
,
\mathcal{B}(U,\mathcal{L})
when continuous is replaced by Borel.
In particular,
\mathcal{B}_{0}(U,\mathcal{L})
consists of Borel sections
f:U\to\mathcal{L}
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
the set
\{\gamma\in U:|f(\gamma)|\geq\varepsilon\}
is precompact.
We consider these spaces equipped with the supremum norm
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\gamma\in U}|f(\gamma)|
.
Every line bundle is locally trivial, which means that every point has a neighborhood
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}
such that
C_{u}(U,\mathcal{L})\neq\emptyset
.
Note that, for any
c\in C_{u}(U,\mathcal{L})
, the map
f\mapsto f\cdot c
establishes isometric isomorphisms
C_{*}(U,\mathcal{L})\cong C_{*}(U)
and

\mathcal{B}_{*}(U,\mathcal{L})\cong\mathcal{B}_{*}(U)
where
*
stands for either
c
,
0
or an empty space.

Definition 2.1
.

A
twist over the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is a line bundle
\mathcal{L}=\bigsqcup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}}L_{\gamma}
over
\mathcal{G}
equipped with two continuous maps
\cdot:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\supseteq\bigsqcup_{d(\gamma)=r(\eta)}L_{\gamma}\times L_{\eta}\to\mathcal{L}
and
{}^{*}:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}
called
multiplication
and
involution
, such that:

(1)

\cdot
restricts to bilinear maps
L_{\gamma}\times L_{\eta}\to L_{\gamma\eta}
,
(\gamma,\eta)\in\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
, that make the norm
|\cdot|
multiplicative, and
\cdot
is associative, that is,
(\mathcal{L},\cdot)
is a semigroupoid;

(2)

∗
restricts to conjugate-linear maps
L_{\gamma}\to L_{\gamma^{-1}}
,
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
, that preserve the norm, are antimultiplicative, and we have
z\cdot z^{*}=|z|^{2}
for all
z\in L_{\gamma},\ \gamma\in\mathcal{G}
.

Then
\mathcal{L}|_{X}
is necessarily trivial and
we shall always assume that
\mathcal{L}|_{X}=X\times\mathbb{F}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The trivial bundle
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{G}\times\mathbb{F}
with pointwise operations is the
trivial twist
.
More generally, if
\sigma:\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\to\{z\in\mathbb{F}:|z|=1\}
is a
continuous normalised
2
-cocycle
on
\mathcal{G}
, that is,
\sigma\big(r(\gamma),\gamma\big)=1=\sigma\big(\gamma,d(\gamma)\big)
and
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)\sigma(\alpha\beta,\gamma)=\sigma(\beta,\gamma)\sigma(\alpha,\beta\gamma)

for every composable triple
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
,
then we may consider a twist
\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}
given by the trivial bundle
\mathcal{G}\times\mathbb{F}
with operations given by

(\alpha,w)\cdot(\beta,z):=\big(\alpha\beta,\sigma(\alpha,\beta)wz\big)
,

(\alpha,w)^{*}:=\big(\alpha^{-1},\overline{\sigma(\alpha^{-1},\alpha)w}\big).

Every twist based on a trivial line bundle is of this form.
In general, twists defined above are equivalent to Kumjian’s twist
[
Kum86
]
defined using extensions of
\mathcal{G}
.

The set of
open bisections

\Bis(\mathcal{G}):=\{U\subseteq\mathcal{G}\text{ open}:\text{$r|_{U}$ and $d|_{U}$ are injective}\}

forms a unital inverse semigroup with operations defined by

U\cdot V:=\{\gamma\eta:\text{$\gamma\in U,\ \eta\in V$ are composable}\},\qquad U^{*}=U^{-1}:=\{\gamma^{-1}:\gamma\in U\},

where
U,V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
. The unit space
X
is the unit in
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
.
For any twist
\mathcal{L}
over
\mathcal{G}
, the set

S(\mathcal{L}):=\{U\in\Bis(\mathcal{G}):\text{the restricted bundle $\mathcal{L}|_{U}$ is trivial}\}

forms a unital inverse subsemigroup
S(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
which is
wide
in the sense that it covers
\mathcal{G}
and the intersection of any two elements in
S(\mathcal{L})
is a union of elements from
S(\mathcal{L})
, see
[
BKM25
, Lemma 4.2]
.

2.2.
Banach algebras associated to twisted groupoids

Fix a twisted groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
We will work with the set of
quasi-continuous compactly supported functions
, defined as

\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}):=\spane\{f\in C_{c}(U,\mathcal{L}):U\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})\},

where a section in
C_{c}(U,\mathcal{L})
is viewed as a section defined on
\mathcal{G}
which vanishes off
U
.
This set is equipped with a
*
-algebra structure as follows:

(f*g)(\gamma):=\sum_{r(\eta)=r(\gamma)}f(\eta)\cdot g(\eta^{-1}\gamma),\quad f^{*}(\gamma):=\big(f(\gamma^{-1})\big)^{*},\quad f,g\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}),\ \gamma\in\mathcal{G},

where
\cdot
and the final
*
indicate the product and involution from
\mathcal{L}
.
When the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff this reduces to the usual
*
-algebra
C_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
We associate a norm on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
with any unital inverse subsemigroup
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
which covers
\mathcal{G}
.
By
[
BKM25
, Lemma 4.5]
, the formula

\|f\|^{S}_{\textrm{max}}:=\inf\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|f_{k}\|_{\infty}:f=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k},\ f_{k}\in C_{c}(U_{k},\mathcal{L}),\ U_{k}\in S\right\},

defines the maximal submultiplicative norm on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
which agrees with
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
on each
C_{c}(U,\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, for
U\in S
.
The involution in
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is isometric in
\|\cdot\|^{S}_{\textrm{max}}
.
Thus

F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}):=\overline{\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})}^{\|\cdot\|^{S}_{\textrm{max}}}

is a Banach
*
-algebra.
The Banach spaces
C_{0}(U,\mathcal{L})\subseteq F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
,
U\in S
, naturally embed into
F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, and they form an
S
-grading
of
F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in the sense that for any
U,V\in S
we have
\overline{C_{0}(U,\mathcal{L})\cdot C_{0}(V,\mathcal{L})}=C_{0}(UV,\mathcal{L})
in
F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and these spaces span a dense
*
-subalgebra of
F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
Unitality of
S
means that
X\in S
.
In particular,
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
sits in
F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
as a non-degenerate Banach subalgebra (any approximate unit in
C_{0}(X)
is a two-sided approximate unit in
F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
).
We also write
F(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}):=F^{\Bis(\mathcal{G})}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
\|\cdot\|_{\textrm{max}}=\|\cdot\|^{\Bis(\mathcal{G})}_{\textrm{max}}
.
In particular,
\|\cdot\|_{\textrm{max}}\leq\|\cdot\|^{S}_{\textrm{max}}
for any
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
as above.

Definition 2.3
.

By a
representation
of a Banach algebra
A
in a Banach algebra
B
we mean a contractive algebra homomorphism
\psi:A\to B
.
Given a unital inverse subsemigroup
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
which covers
\mathcal{G}
and a class
\mathcal{R}
of representations of
F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, we define
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
to be the Hausdorff completion of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in the seminorm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{R}}:=\sup\big\{\|\psi(f)\|:\psi\in\mathcal{R}\big\}.

The closures of images of the spaces
C_{0}(U,\mathcal{L})
,
U\in S
, form an
S
-grading of
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
To emphasize the role of
S
we will say that
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is
S
-graded
.

Remark 2.4
.

The class of algebras
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
defined above consists of Banach algebra Hausdorff completions of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}}
for which there exists a unital inverse subsemigroup
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
covering
\mathcal{G}
such that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
on every
C_{c}(U,\mathcal{L})
,
U\in S
(this is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\textrm{max}}^{S}
on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
).
Without loss of generality, we may always assume here that
S
is closed under taking subsets, so that in particular it is wide, and that
S\subseteq S(\mathcal{L})
, see
[
BKM25
, Remark 4.7]
.

Example 2.5

(Hahn’s completions)
.

The domain and range maps
d,r:\mathcal{G}\to X
induce the following norms on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
dominated by
\|\cdot\|_{\textrm{max}}
:

\|f\|_{*d}:=\max_{x\in X}\sum_{d(\gamma)=x}\big|f(\gamma)\big|,\qquad\|f\|_{*r}:=\max_{x\in X}\sum_{r(\gamma)=x}\big|f(\gamma)\big|,\qquad\|f\|_{I}:=\max\{\|f\|_{*d},\|f\|_{*r}\}.

The completions of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in these norms are denoted by
F_{*d}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
,
F_{*r}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
, and
F_{I}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
respectively, and they are
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
-graded.
The above norms, especially
\|\cdot\|_{I}
, are common in the literature, cf.
[
Pat99
, Section 2.2]
or
[
Ren80
, II, 1.4]
,
and
\|\cdot\|_{I}
is often called
Hahn’s
I
-norm
, as it was introduced in
[
Hah78
]
.
Thus we call
F_{I}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})

Hahn’s algebra
.
The algebras
F_{*d}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
and
F_{*r}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
coincide respectively with the groupoid
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-operator algebras that are introduced in the next example.
The identity on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
extends to representations (with dense ranges)

F({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})\to F_{I}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})\to F_{*d}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}}),F_{*r}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}}).

Example 2.6

(Full groupoid
L^{p}
-operator algebras)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
.
We recall (see e.g.
[
Phi13b
]
) that a Banach algebra
A
is an
L^{p}
-operator algebra
if there is an isometric representation
\psi:A\to B(L^{p}(\mu))
, where
L^{p}(\mu)
is the Banach
L^{p}
-space associated to some measure
\mu
.
Following
[
BKM25
, Definition 5.12]
, we define the
full
L^{p}
-operator algebra of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
as
F^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}):=F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
where
\mathcal{R}
consists of representations
\psi:F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to B(E)
, for some unital inverse subsemigroup
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
which covers
\mathcal{G}
, such that

(R1)

E=L^{p}(\mu)
for a measure
\mu
;

(R2)

operators in
\psi(C_{c}(X)^{+})
preserve the cone of positive functions in
E
.

The following comments hold by
[
BKM25
, Theorem 5.13]
.
The above definition does not depend on the choice of
S
, and so
F^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
-graded.
In fact, denoting by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
the norm in
F^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, for
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
q\in[1,\infty]
with
1/p+1/q=1
(we put
q=1
if
p=\infty
and vice versa) we get

(2.1)

\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|f\|_{*d}^{1/p}\,\|f\|_{*r}^{1/q}\leq\|f\|_{I}.

In the case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, condition
(R2)
can be dropped (we get the same norm without it).
Thus

\|f\|_{L^{p}}=\sup\{\|\psi(f)\|:\text{$\psi$ is a representation of $F_{I}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})$ into an $L^{p}$-operator algebra}\}

in the complex case.
In the real case trouble may come from representations as in
[
BKM25
, Example 2.14]
.
For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
and
p=2
,
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
is the maximal submultiplicative norm on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
satisfying the
C^{*}
-equality.
Thus
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}):=F^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is a (real or complex)
C^{*}
-algebra, which is universal in the sense that
*
-homomorphisms from
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
into a
C^{*}
-algebra
B
extend to representations of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in
B
, and every representation of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in
B
is automatically
*
-preserving.
The norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}}
does not change, if we replace the
L^{\infty}
-space in
(R1)
by
E=C_{0}(\Omega)
for some locally compact Hausdorff space.
Thus
F^{\infty}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
could also be viewed as the
full
C_{0}
-operator algebra
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
Moreover, if
q\in[1,\infty]
satisfies
1/p+1/q=1
, then the involution in
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
extends to an isometric anti-isomorphism
F^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{anti}}}{{\cong}}F^{q}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.

Example 2.7

(Reduced groupoid
L^{p}
-operator algebras)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
.
For any section
f
of
\mathcal{L}
we put
\|f\|_{p}=(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}}|f(\gamma)|^{p})^{1/p}
when
p<\infty
and
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}}|f(\gamma)|
when
p=\infty
.
We denote by
\ell^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
the Banach space of all sections of
\mathcal{L}
for which the norm
\|f\|_{p}
is finite.
The formula

(2.2)

\Lambda_{p}(f)\xi(\gamma):=(f*\xi)(\gamma)=\sum_{r(\eta)=r(\gamma)}f(\eta)\cdot\xi(\eta^{-1}\gamma),\qquad f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}),\ \xi\in\ell^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}),

defines a representation
\Lambda_{p}:F^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to B(\ell^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}))
which is injective on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, see
[
BKM25
, Proposition 5.1]
.
We call
F_{\red}^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}):=F_{\red}^{\{\Lambda_{p}\}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
the
reduced
L^{p}
-operator algebra of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and we denote the norm by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p},\red}
.
By
[
BKM25
, Theorem 5.13]
we have

(2.3)

F^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=F_{\red}^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=F_{*d}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})\quad\text{ and }\quad F^{\infty}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=F^{\infty}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=F_{*r}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}).

By
[
GL17
, Theorem 6.19]
, if
\mathcal{G}
is second countable and amenable, then
F^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=F_{\red}^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
for every
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Remark 2.8
.

When
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, the reduced algebra
F_{\red}^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
was studied in
[
HO23
]
, and in the untwisted case in
[
CGT24
]
.
The full
L^{p}
-operator algebra
F_{p}({\mathcal{G}})
was introduced in a different but equivalent way by Gardella–Lupini
[
GL17
]
in the complex, separable, finite
p
case.

3.
Reduced groupoid Banach algebras

We fix a twisted groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
In this section, we will consider completions of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
that are equipped with ‘generalized conditional expectations’ that extend the map
E_{X}:\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{B}_{c}(X)
, given by restriction
f\mapsto f|_{X}
.
When
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff, then
E_{X}:C_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to C_{c}(X)\subseteq C_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is a projection.

Definition 3.1
.

A
groupoid Banach algebra
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is a Banach algebra completion
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
such that
C_{0}(X)
canonically embeds into
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and there is a contractive map
E_{X}^{\mathcal{R}}:F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{B}_{0}(X)
that extends
E_{X}
.
We say that
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is
reduced
if
E_{X}^{\mathcal{R}}
is faithful in the sense that the only ideal contained in
\ker E_{X}^{\mathcal{R}}
is
\{0\}
.

Remark 3.2
.

When
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff a groupoid Banach algebra is a completion of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in a submultiplicative seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}}
such that
\|f|_{X}\|_{\infty}=\|f|_{X}\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{R}}
for all
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.

Remark 3.3
.

For any unital inverse subsemigroup
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
covering
\mathcal{G}
, the algebra
F^{S}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is a groupoid Banach algebra.
For any
p\in[1,\infty]
, the
L^{p}
-operator algebras
F^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
F^{p}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
are groupoid Banach algebras and
F^{p}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is reduced, cf. Proposition
3.15
below. There is a unique
C^{*}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{C^{*}_{\red}}
on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
yielding a reduced Banach algebra, and then this algebra coincides with the standard reduced
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
(or its real counterpart).
The full
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, which is a completion of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in the maximal
C^{*}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{C^{*}\max}
, is a groupoid Banach algebra of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
Thus a
C^{*}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}}
on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
yields a groupoid Banach algebra if and only if it satisfies
\|\cdot\|_{C^{*}_{\red}}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\|\cdot\|_{C^{*}\max}
.

We make some preparations to reveal more structure in groupoid Banach algebras.
For any
U\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, we view the space of bounded Borel sections
\mathcal{B}(U,\mathcal{L})
as a Banach
C(X)
-bimodule:

(a*f)(\gamma):=a(r(\gamma))\cdot f(\gamma),\qquad(f*b)(\gamma):=f(\gamma)\cdot b(d(\gamma)),\qquad a,b\in C(X),\ f\in\mathcal{B}(U,\mathcal{L}).

It contains
\mathcal{B}_{c}(U,\mathcal{L})
and
\mathcal{B}_{0}(U,\mathcal{L})
as non-degenerate
C_{0}(X)
-subbimodules.
The restriction

E_{U}(f):=f|_{U},\qquad f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}),

gives a
C_{0}(X)
-bilinear map
E_{U}:\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{B}(U,\mathcal{L})
.
The map
E_{U}
on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(r(U)\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=C_{c}(r(U))*\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, takes values in compactly supported Borel sections
\mathcal{B}_{c}(U,\mathcal{L})=C_{c}(r(U))*\mathcal{B}(U,\mathcal{L})
.
When
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff
E_{U}:\mathfrak{C}_{c}(r(U)\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to C_{c}(U,\mathcal{L})
takes values in continuous functions.
Indeed,
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(r(U)\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is spanned by elements
f\in C_{c}(V,\mathcal{L})
for some
V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
with
r(V)\subseteq r(U)
, and if
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff then
V\cap U
is closed (and open) in
V
, and hence
f|_{U}
is continuous for every
f\in C_{c}(V,\mathcal{L})
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
the closure of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
in the space
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
of bounded Borel sections from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{L}
.
Thus

\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=\overline{\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})}^{\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}

is a Banach space, which coincides with
C_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
when
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff.
The involution on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
gives a
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-isometric involution on
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, but the convolution product might not be defined on the whole of
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
Nevertheless we have:

Lemma 3.4
.

Convolution defines a
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
-bimodule structure on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
which is continuous in the sense that if a sequence
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
converges to
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, then, for any
g\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, we have
g*f_{n}\to g*f
and
f_{n}*g\to f*g
in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.
It restricts to a continuous
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
-bimodule structure on
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.

Proof.

Fix
g\in C_{c}(U^{*},\mathcal{L})
, where
U\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
.
For any
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, we have

(3.4)

g*f(\gamma)=\begin{cases}g\big(\gamma\tilde{h}_{U}(\gamma)^{-1})\cdot f(\tilde{h}_{U}(\gamma)\big),&\gamma\in r^{-1}(d(U)),\\
0,&\gamma\not\in r^{-1}(d(U)),\end{cases}

where
\tilde{h}_{U}:r^{-1}(d(U))\to r^{-1}(r(U))
is the homeomorphism given by
\tilde{h}_{U}(\gamma):=d|_{U}^{-1}(r(\gamma))\gamma
(see
[
BKM25
, Example 2.19]
).
This implies that
g*f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
\|g*f\|_{\infty}\leq\|g\|_{\infty}\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}
.
Thus
\|g*f_{n}-g*f\|_{\infty}\to 0
whenever
\|f_{n}-f\|_{\infty}\to 0
.
Since elements in
C_{c}(U,\mathcal{L})
,
U\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, span
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, this proves that
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is a left
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
-module continuous in the desired sense.
Similarly, one checks that
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is a continuous right
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
-module, and so a bimodule by associativity of convolution.
Since
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is dense in
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and closed under convolution, we conclude that
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})*\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
,
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})*\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
U\in S(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
and
f\in C_{c}(r(U))*\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, there is a norm one section
b\in C_{c}(U^{*},\mathcal{L})
such that
|(b*f)|_{X}|=|f\circ s|_{U}^{-1}|
as functions on
X
(the modulus can be dropped in the untwisted case).

Proof.

The range
r(K)
of the closed support
K
of
f\in C_{c}(r(U))*\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is a compact subset of
r(U)
.
Thus there is a positive norm one function
a\in C_{c}(r(U))
which is
1
on
r(K)
. Taking any unitary section
c\in C_{u}(U^{*},\mathcal{L})
gives the desired
b\in C_{c}(U^{*},\mathcal{L})
by putting
b(\gamma):=a(s(\gamma))c(\gamma)
for
\gamma\in U^{*}
.
∎

The essence of the following proposition is that every groupoid Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is equipped with a natural contractive map into
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and this map is injective
if and only if
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is reduced. Also we could formally weaken the definition of groupoid Banach algebras using Hausdorff completions of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, rather then completions.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
be a Banach algebra Hausdorff completion of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and let
i_{\mathcal{R}}:\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
be the canonical homomorphism.
Assume
i_{\mathcal{R}}
is isometric on
C_{c}(X)
, so that
C_{0}(X)
embeds isometrically into
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\|f|_{X}\|_{\infty}\leq\|i_{\mathcal{R}}(f)\|_{\mathcal{R}}
for all
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
;

(2)

F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is a groupoid Banach algebra of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
(in particular
i_{\mathcal{R}}
is injective);

(3)

(4)

(2)

Remark 3.8

In view of Proposition

The following lemma generalizes

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}_{i}}
,
i\in I
, be norms on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
that define groupoid Banach algebras
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
F_{\mathcal{R}_{i}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
,
i\in I
, for
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})

define norms that yield groupoid Banach algebras

,

for
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, provided

is finite.

defines a groupoid algebra

which is a Banach
*
-algebra.
Moreover,
F_{\mathcal{R}^{*}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and

, are reduced.

The involution on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
extends to an antimultiplicative antilinear isometry from

onto

.

for

.
Hence

is a groupoid Banach algebra.
Moreover,
j_{\mathcal{R}^{*}}(f)=j_{\mathcal{R}}(f^{*})^{*}
for

.
Thus
j_{\mathcal{R}^{*}}
is injective if and only if
j_{\mathcal{R}}
is, equivalently
F_{\mathcal{R}^{*}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is reduced if and only
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is.

Assuming it is finite,
\|\cdot\|_{\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i}}
is clearly a submultiplicative norm on
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
which coincides with
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
on
C_{c}(X)
and
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i}}
for
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, as this holds for every
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}_{i}}
,
i\in I
.
Thus

is a groupoid Banach algebra, and we have a canonical contraction
j_{\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i}}:F_{\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})\to\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
Note that
\pi(a):=\prod_{i\in I}a
, for

, determines an isometric embedding of
F_{\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
into the direct product
\prod_{i\in I}F_{\mathcal{R}_{i}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
.
Moreover,
\prod_{i\in I}j_{\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i}}=\prod_{i\in I}j_{\mathcal{R}_{i}}\circ\pi
, as maps from
F_{\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
to
\prod_{i\in I}\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
Hence
j_{\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i}}
is injective if all
j_{\mathcal{R}_{i}}
,
i\in I
, are injective.
That is,
F_{\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is reduced if all
F_{\mathcal{R}_{i}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
,
i\in I
, are reduced.
∎

For the sake of completeness and further reference, we give a complete proof based on the argument behind

Namely, we identify

with the corresponding subalgebra of

Clearly, for every

Since

, we get

\begin{split}\sum_{r(\eta)=r(\gamma)}\left|j_{p}^{\red}(f_{i}-f)(\eta)\cdot j_{p}^{\red}(g_{j})(\eta^{-1}\gamma)\right|\leq\|(f_{i}-f)^{*}1_{r(\gamma)}\|_{q}\|g_{j}1_{\gamma}\|_{p}&\leq\|(f_{i}-f)^{*}\|_{{}_{L^{q},\red}}\|g_{j}\|_{{}_{L^{p},\red}}\\
&=\|f_{i}-f\|_{L^{p},\red}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{p},\red}.\end{split}

Similarly, we get
\sum_{r(\eta)=r(\gamma)}|j_{p}^{\red}(f)(\eta)\cdot j_{p}^{\red}(g_{j}-g)(\eta^{-1}\gamma)|\leq\|f\|_{L^{p},\red}\|g_{j}-g\|_{L^{p},\red}
.
Altogether, since
j_{p}^{\red}(f_{i}\cdot g_{j})=f_{i}*g_{j}
, this implies that

\begin{split}\left|j_{p}^{\red}(f_{i}\cdot g_{j})(\gamma)-\sum_{r(\eta)=r(\gamma)}j_{p}(f)(\eta)\cdot j_{p}(g)(\eta^{-1}\gamma)\right|\leq\|f_{i}-f\|_{L^{p},\red}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{p},\red}+\|f\|_{L^{p},\red}\|g_{j}-g\|_{L^{p},\red}\end{split}

tends to zero, which proves (
3.6
), as
j_{p}^{\red}(f_{i}\cdot g_{j})(\gamma)
tends to
j_{p}^{\red}(f\cdot g)(\gamma)
.
∎

Remark 3.16
.

If
P=P^{*}
, then
F^{P}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})

*
-algebras and
\Lambda_{P}
is a

with
1/p+1/q=1

-algebras
F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
are sometimes called
symmetrized pseudo-function algebras
.

[

]
and in the group case in
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

.

4.
Essential and exotic groupoid Banach algebras

The reader interested only in Hausdorff groupoids may skip this section.
When the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is non-Hausdorff we need to extend the previous definition of groupoid Banach algebras to allow constructions that ‘disregard’ the points of discontinuity for quasi-continuous functions in
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
In the
C^{*}
-algebraic context, this quotient was introduced in
[
KM21
]
and examples go back (at least) to the work of Nekrashevych
[
Nek04
]
,
[
Nek09
]
.
The crucial observation is that elements of
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
are continuous modulo
meager sets
(that is, countable unions of nowhere dense sets).

Lemma 4.1

(
[
KM21
, Lemma 7.13]
)
.

For every
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, there is a meager set
M\subseteq\mathcal{G}
such that
f
is continuous at every point
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}\setminus M
.

Proof.

When
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is supported on
U\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, then
f
can be discontinuous only on the nowhere dense set
\partial U\subseteq\mathcal{G}
.
Thus
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
can be discontinuous only on a finite union of nowhere dense sets.
Since every
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is a limit of a sequence of functions in
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, the set of discontinuity points of
f
is contained in a countable union of nowhere dense sets.
∎

4.1.
Quasi-continous functions with meager support

We will first discuss the space

\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}):=\{f\in\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}):\text{$\supp(f)$ is meager}\},

where by
\supp
we mean the strict support, given by

\supp(f):=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:f(\gamma)\neq 0\}.

We show that the elements in
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
have to be supported on a subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
where Hausdorffness fails.
To this end, we adopt the nomenclature of Dixmier
[
Dix51
]
.

Definition 4.2

(Dixmier)
.

Let
Y
be a topological space. We call
x\in Y
a
Hausdorff point
in
Y
if
for any other point
y\in X
there are disjoint open neighbourhoods of
x
and
y
.
We denote by
Y_{\Hau}
the set of all Hausdorff points in
Y
.

Remark 4.3
.

We apply the above definition to the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
.
In particular,
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
if there is
\eta\in\mathcal{G}\setminus\{\gamma\}
such that
\gamma
and
\eta
cannot be separated by disjoint open sets (equivalently, there is a net
(\gamma_{i})_{i}
in
\mathcal{G}
converging to
\gamma
and
\eta
).
The authors of
[
BM25
]
call arrows in
\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}

dangerous
, which was inspired by
[
KM21
, Definition 7.14]
where the points in
X\setminus\mathcal{G}_{H}
were given this name.

Statements similar to those in the following lemma can also be found in
[
BM25
]
, which appeared on arXiv after the first preprint of this article, and were proved independently.

Lemma 4.4
.

Both
\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
and
\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
are full subgroupoids of
\mathcal{G}
and so
\mathcal{G}
decomposes into the groupoid disjoint union
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}\sqcup\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
.
In particular,
d(\mathcal{G}_{\Hau})=r(\mathcal{G}_{\Hau})=X\cap\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
and
d(\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau})=r(\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau})=X\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
.
Moreover,

(1)

for any
x\in X\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
the isotropy group
\mathcal{G}(x):=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:d(\gamma)=r(\gamma)=x\}
is non-trivial;

(2)

if
\mathcal{G}
has a countable open cover consisting of bisections, then
\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
is dense in
\mathcal{G}

(in fact
\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
is comeager and
\mathcal{G}
is Baire);

(3)

every
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is continuous at every
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
.

Proof.

Assume that a net
(\gamma_{i})_{i}
in
\mathcal{G}
converges towards two different elements
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
.
Then
(\gamma_{i}^{-1})_{i}
converges towards two different elements
\gamma^{-1},\gamma^{\prime-1}\in\mathcal{G}
, so
(\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau})^{-1}=\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
.
Also
r(\gamma)=r(\gamma^{\prime})
and
d(\gamma)=d(\gamma^{\prime})
, because
r,d
are continuous and
X
is Hausdorff.
This in particular implies
(1)
, cf.
[
KM21
, Lemma 7.15]
.
Thus, if
\eta\in\mathcal{G}
is composable with
\gamma
, it is also composable with
\gamma^{\prime}
and we may construct a net
(\eta_{i})_{i}
convergent to
\eta
such that
\eta_{i}
and
\gamma_{i}
are composable, so the net
(\eta_{i}\gamma_{i})_{i}
converges to two different elements
\eta\gamma
and
\eta\gamma^{\prime}
.
Indeed, fixing two bisections
U,V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
where
\eta\in V
,
\gamma\in U
and
d(V)=r(U)
, we may assume that
(\gamma_{i})_{i}
is a net in
U
, and then the elements
\eta_{i}:=d|_{V}^{-1}(r(\gamma_{i}))
form the desired net.
This shows that
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau})\subseteq\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
.
By taking inverses, and using
(\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau})^{-1}=(\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau})
, we also get
(\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau})\mathcal{G}\subseteq(\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau})
.
Since arrows in the groupoid are invertible, these relations for
\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
are equivalent to saying that
\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
are full subgroupoids of
\mathcal{G}
.
If
\mathcal{G}
has a countable open cover consisting of bisections, then since open bisections are Baire spaces,
\mathcal{G}
is a Baire space.
Also by
[
KM21
, Lemma 7.15]
,
\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
is meager, and therefore it has empty interior. This proves item
(2)
. Item
(3)
follows from (the proof of)
[
KM21
, Lemma 7.13]
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Recall that a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is called
principal
if there are no points
x\in X
with non-trivial isotropy, and
\mathcal{G}
is called
topologically principal
if the set of points with non-trivial isotropy has empty interior.
By Lemma
4.4
(1)
, if
\mathcal{G}
is principal then
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
is Hausdorff.
If
\mathcal{G}
is topologically principal, then
\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
is dense in
\mathcal{G}
.

The following proposition is a minor improvement of
[
KM21
, Proposition 7.18]
, cf. also
[
BM25
, Corollary 3.15]
.
Namely, in general we do not need any assumptions on
\mathcal{G}
and we consider quasi-continuous functions that vanish at infinity.

Proposition 4.6
.

For any
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\supp(f)
is meager in
\mathcal{G}
, that is,
f\in\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
;

(2)

\supp(f)
has empty interior in
\mathcal{G}
;

(3)

\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:|f(\gamma)|>\varepsilon\}
has empty interior in
\mathcal{G}
for all
\varepsilon>0
;

(4)

\supp(f^{*}*f)\cap X
is meager in
X
;

(5)

\supp(f^{*}*f)\cap X
has empty interior in
X
;

(6)

\{x\in X:|f^{*}*f(x)|>\varepsilon\}
has empty interior in
X
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Moreover, the above equivalent conditions imply

(7)

\supp(f)\cap\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}=\emptyset
,

and if
\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
is comeager or dense in
\mathcal{G}
(which holds when
\mathcal{G}
can be covered by a countable family of bisections or when
\mathcal{G}
is topologically principal), then all conditions
(1)
–
(7)
are equivalent.

Proof.

Implication
(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
holds because we can cover
\supp(f)
with a countable family of bisections (cf. the proof of Lemma
4.1
), and so its union is a Baire space.
Implication
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
is obvious.
And
(3)
implies
(1)
because if
\supp(f)
is not meager in
\mathcal{G}
, then by Lemma
4.1
there is a point
\gamma_{0}\in\supp(f)
at which
f
is continuous.
Thus, for
\varepsilon=|f(\gamma_{0})|/2
, the set
\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:|f(\gamma)|>\varepsilon\}
contains a neighbourhood of
\gamma_{0}
and so has non-empty interior.
Hence
(1)
–
(3)
are equivalent.

Implications
(4)
\Rightarrow
(5)
\Rightarrow
(6)
are clear.
Since
(f^{*}*f)(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in d^{-1}(x)}|f(\gamma)|^{2}
for
x\in X
,
(6)
implies
(3)
.
We close the cycle of implications by showing that
(1)
implies
(6)
.
To this end, note that
\supp(f)
can be covered by a countable family
\{U_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, because
f
is a uniform limit of a sequence of functions in
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, which are supported on a finite union of bisections.
So
\supp(f^{*}*f)\cap X=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}d(\supp(f)\cap U_{n})
is meager if each of the sets
\supp(f)\cap U_{n}
is meager.
This proves that
(1)
–
(6)
are equivalent.

Lemma
4.4
(3)
implies that if
\supp(f)\cap\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}\neq\emptyset
, then
\supp(f)
has non-empty interior.
Thus
(2)
implies
(7)
.
The converse implication is trivial if we assume that
\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
is dense (which is guaranteed if there is a countable cover of
\mathcal{G}
by bisections, by Lemma
4.4
(2)
, or when
\mathcal{G}
is topologically principal, see Remark
4.5
).
∎

Remark 4.7
.

In some pathological situations, see
[
KM21
, Example 7.16]
, it may happen that
\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}=\emptyset
and so in general condition
(7)
above does not imply the others.

When
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{\Hau}
is Hausdorff, then
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=C_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=\{0\}
.
Examples of non-Hausdorff groupoids with
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=\{0\}
can be constructed using the following criteria coming from
[
CEPSS19
, Lemma 3.1]
.
Recall that a topological groupoid is
ample
if it has a basis consisting of compact open bisections.
Equivalentely, it is an étale groupoid with a totally disconnected unit space.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is ample and every compact open set in
\mathcal{G}
is regular open, that is, it is equal to the interior of its closure, then
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
K(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
be the linear span of sections
g_{V}\in C(V,\mathcal{L})
where
V
is a compact open bisection and
|g_{V}|=\textit{const}
on
V
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is ample,
K(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is dense in
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
Indeed, the linear span of sections in
C_{0}(V,\mathcal{L})
,
V\in S(\mathcal{L})
, is dense in
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, and for every
V\in S(\mathcal{L})
we have
C_{0}(V,\mathcal{L})\cong C_{0}(V)
, and it is well known that the linear span of indicator functions of compact open sets is dense in
C_{0}(V)
when
V
is totally disconnected.

We claim that for any
g\in K(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
c>0
the set
|g|^{-1}(c)=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}:|g(\gamma)|=c\}
is either empty or has non-empty interior.
Indeed, we have
g=\sum_{V\in F}g_{V}
, where
F
is a finite collection of compact open bisections and
g_{V}\in C(V,\mathcal{L})
with
|g_{V}|=\textit{const}
on
V\in F
.
By the inclusion-exclusion principle, putting for any non-empty
F_{0}\subseteq F

V_{F_{0}}^{F}:=\left(\bigcap_{V\in F_{0}}V\right)\setminus\left(\bigcup_{W\in F\setminus F_{0}}W\right),

we get a family
\{V_{F_{0}}^{F}\}_{\emptyset\neq F_{0}\subseteq F}
of pairwise-disjoint sets such that
V=\bigsqcup_{V\in F_{0}\subseteq F}V_{F_{0}}^{F}
, for every
V\in F
(cf.
[
CFST14
, Remark 2.5]
or
[
BKM25
, Lemma 6.2]
).
Thus
|g|
is constant on each of the sets
V_{F_{0}}^{F}
.
Hence if
|g|^{-1}(c)\neq\emptyset
, then
|g|^{-1}(c)
contains a non-empty set
V_{F_{0}}^{F}
.
But the set
V_{F_{0}}^{F}
is the difference of two compact open sets
\bigcap_{V\in F_{0}}V
and
\bigcup_{W\in F\setminus F_{0}}W
, which are regular open by assumption.
Hence
V_{F_{0}}^{F}
has a non-empty interior by
[
CEPSS19
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Now let
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
be non-zero.
Find
g\in K(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
with
\|f-g\|_{\infty}<\|f\|_{\infty}/3
, and so
\|g\|_{\infty}>2\|f\|_{\infty}/3
.
Thus there is
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
with
c:=|g(\gamma)|>2\|f\|_{\infty}/3
.
By the above claim
|g|^{-1}(c)
has a non-empty interior.
Moreover,
|g|^{-1}(c)\subseteq\supp(f)
because, for each
\gamma\in|g|^{-1}(c)
, we have
|f(\gamma)|\geq c-\|f\|_{\infty}/3>\|f\|_{\infty}/3>0
.
Hence
\supp(f)
has nonempty interior and therefore
f\notin\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
∎

4.2.
Essential and exotic groupoid Banach algebras

For any open
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, we denote by
\mathfrak{M}(U,\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathcal{B}(U,\mathcal{L})
the space of bounded Borel sections of meager support, and we consider

\mathcal{D}(U,\mathcal{L}):=\mathcal{B}(U,\mathcal{L})/\mathfrak{M}(U,\mathcal{L})

as the quotient Banach space.
Let
q_{U}:\mathcal{B}(U,\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{D}(U,\mathcal{L})
be the quotient map.
If
U,V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, then the convolution and involution induce mappings
\mathcal{D}(U,\mathcal{L})\times\mathcal{D}(V,\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{D}(UV,\mathcal{L})
and
\mathcal{D}(U,\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{D}(U^{*},\mathcal{L})
that we will still denote by
*
and
∗
.
Also we may and we will treat
\mathcal{D}(U,\mathcal{L})
as a subspace of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
When the twist is trivial we will omit writing it.
In particular,

\mathcal{D}(X)=\mathcal{B}(X)/\mathfrak{M}(X)

is a
C^{*}
-algebra, often called the
Dixmier algebra of
X
, as it was studied first in
[
Dix51
]
.
It can be identified both with the
injective hull
of
C_{0}(X)
and with the
local multiplier algebra
of
C_{0}(X)
, see
[
KM21
, subsection 4.4]
and references therein.
The Dixmier algebra
\mathcal{D}(X)
contains
C(X)
, in the sense that the quotient map
q_{X}:\mathcal{B}(X)\to\mathcal{D}(X)
is isometric on
C(X)
, so we will write
C(X)\subseteq\mathcal{D}(X)
.
Similarly, we have
C(U,\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathcal{D}(U,\mathcal{L})
for any
U\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, and for
f\in\mathcal{B}(U,\mathcal{L})
we have

(4.8)

\|q_{U}(f)\|=\inf_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M\subseteq U}}{{\text{meager}}}}\sup_{\gamma\in U\setminus M}|f(\gamma)|=\min_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M\subseteq U}}{{\text{meager}}}}\sup_{\gamma\in U\setminus M}|f(\gamma)|,

where the minimum exists because countable intersections of comeager sets are comeager.
The Banach
C_{0}(X)
-bimodule structure on
\mathcal{B}(U,\mathcal{L})
factors to a Banach
C_{0}(X)
-bimodule structure on
\mathcal{D}(U,\mathcal{L})
, and we have a
C_{0}(X)
-bimodule map
\E_{U}:=q_{U}\circ E_{U}:\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{D}(U,\mathcal{L})
.
Now we proceed as in Section
3
, but with
E_{U}
replaced by
\E_{U}
.
In the
C^{*}
-algebraic context,
\E_{X}
was called a local expectation in
[
KM21
]
.

Definition 4.9
.

An
exotic groupoid Banach algebra of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is a Hausdorff completion
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
such that
C_{0}(X)
embeds isometrically into
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
\E_{X}:\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{D}(X)
induces a contractive map
\E_{X}^{\mathcal{R}}:F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})\to\mathcal{D}(X)
, that is, the corresponding seminorm satisfies
\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{R}}
for
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
\|f\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{R}}
for
f\in C_{c}(X)
.
If in addition
\E_{X}^{\mathcal{R}}
is faithful, we call
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
an
essential groupoid Banach algebra
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.

Remark 4.10
.

A
C^{*}
-algebra is an exotic groupoid Banach algebra of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
if and only if it is an exotic
C^{*}
-algebra of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
in the sense of
[
KM21
]
.
There is a unique
C^{*}
-algebra which is an essential groupoid Banach algebra of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, and this is the algebra introduced in
[
KM21
, Definition 7.12]
(for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
). Every groupoid Banach algebra is an exotic groupoid Banach algebra and the converse holds when
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff because then, for every
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})=C_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, we have
f|_{X}\in C_{c}(X)
and therefore
\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|=\|f|_{X}\|_{\infty}
.
In particular, if
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff then the classes of essential and reduced groupoid Banach algebras coincide.

Lemma 4.11
.

The
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
-bimodule structure on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
induced by convolution, defined in Lemma
3.4
, factors to a
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
-bimodule structure on
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, which is continuous
in the sense that for any sequence
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
,
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
g\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, if
q_{\mathcal{G}}(f_{n})\to q_{\mathcal{G}}(f)
, then
q_{\mathcal{G}}(g*f_{n})\to q_{\mathcal{G}}(g*f)
and
q_{\mathcal{G}}(f_{n}*g)\to q_{\mathcal{G}}(f*g)
.
This turns
q_{\mathcal{G}}:\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
into a
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
-bimodule map.

Proof.

If
g\in C_{c}(U^{*},\mathcal{L})
, for
U\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
and
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})

) implies

Since elements in

\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, this holds for any

.
Similarly (or by using the involution) one gets the same conclusion for
q_{\mathcal{G}}(f_{n}*g)\to q_{\mathcal{G}}(f*g)
.
∎

Proposition 4.12
.

Let

be a Banach algebra Hausdorff completion of

in a seminorm satisfying

(1)

(3)

.

If the above equivalent conditions hold, then for each

,

is a

-module map given by

and
\ker j_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ess}
is the largest ideal in

contained in

Proof.

We adapt the proof of Proposition
3.6
.
Implications
(3)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow

).

For any

and

\E^{\mathcal{R}}_{U}

5.
Intersection property and topological freeness

In this section, we study conditions implying the following intersection properties for
A=C_{0}(X)
and
B
being an exotic groupoid Banach algebra, see
[
BK24
, Definition 5.6]
and
[
KM21
, Definitions 5.5, 5.6]
.

5.1.
Intersection property and topological freeness

Recall that the ideals we consider are closed and two-sided.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
A\subseteq B
be a Banach subalgebra of a Banach algebra
B
.
We say that
A
detects ideals in
B
, or that
A\subseteq B
has the
intersection property
, if for every non-zero ideal
J
in
B
, we have
J\cap A\neq\{0\}
.
The inclusion has the
generalized intersection property
if there is a largest ideal
\Null
in
B
with
\Null\cap A=\{0\}
.
In this case we call
\Null
the
hidden ideal
and put
B_{\ess}:=B/\Null
. We say
A\subseteq B
is
minimal
if for every non-zero
a\in A
we have
\overline{BaB}=B
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
A\subseteq B
has the generalized intersection property and
\overline{A}
is the closure of the range of
A
in the quotient
B_{\ess}
, then
\overline{A}\subseteq B_{\ess}
has the intersection property.
An inclusion
A\subseteq B
has the intersection property and is minimal if and only if
B
is simple.

Proof.

Assume
A\subseteq B
has the generalized intersection property, let
q:B\to B_{\ess}
be the quotient map and put
\overline{A}:=\overline{q(A)}
.
If
J
is a non-zero ideal in
B_{\ess}
then
q^{-1}(J)
is an ideal in
B
which is strictly larger than
\Null
.
Thus
A\cap q^{-1}(J)\neq\{0\}
and because
q
is injective on
A
we therefore get
\{0\}\neq q(A\cap q^{-1}(J))\subseteq\overline{A}\cap J
.
Hence
\overline{A}\subseteq B_{\ess}
has the intersection property.

Let
A\subseteq B
be any Banach algebra inclusion.
If it is minimal and has the intersection property, then for any non-zero ideal
J
in
B
,
I:=A\cap J
is a non-zero ideal in
B
and therefore
B=\overline{BIB}\subseteq J
, which proves that
B
is simple.
The converse implication is straightforward.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

If
A\subseteq B
has the generalized intersection property and
A
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, then the embedding
A\subseteq B_{\ess}
is isometric (that is, we have
A=\overline{A}
in Lemma
5.2
).

Recall that a set
U\subseteq X
is
\mathcal{G}
-invariant
if
d(\gamma)\in U
implies
r(\gamma)\in U
for all
\gamma\in G
.
The groupoid is
minimal
if there are no non-trivial
\mathcal{G}
-invariant open sets in
X
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
B
be a Banach algebra of the form
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
and such that
C_{0}(X)
embeds isometrically into
B
.
For any ideal
I
in
C_{0}(X)
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

I
is restricted, that is,
I=C_{0}(X)\cap J
for an ideal
J
in
B
;

(2)

I=C_{0}(U)
for an open
\mathcal{G}
-invariant set
U\subseteq X
;

(3)

I
is symmetric, that is,
\overline{IB}=\overline{BI}
.

Moreover, the inclusion
C_{0}(X)\subseteq B
is minimal if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is minimal.

Proof.

We have
I=C_{0}(U)
for an open set
U\subseteq X
.
For
V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, we denote the image of
C_{0}(V,\mathcal{L})
in
B=F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
by
B_{V}
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
. Assume
I=C_{0}(X)\cap J
for an ideal
J
in
B
.
For every
V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, we have
C_{0}(VUV^{*})=\overline{B_{V}C_{0}(U)B_{V^{*}}}\subseteq J\cap C_{0}(X)=C_{0}(U)
.
Hence
VUV^{*}\subseteq U
for every
V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, which implies
U
is
\mathcal{G}
-invariant.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
.
If
(2)
holds, then
VU=UV
for all
V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, which implies that
IB_{V}=B_{V}I
for
V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
.
Hence
\overline{IB}=\overline{\sum_{V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})}IB_{V}}=\overline{\sum_{V\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})}B_{V}I}=\overline{BI}
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1)
. If
(3)
holds, then
J=\overline{IB}=\overline{BI}
is an ideal in
B
(generated by
I
) and an approximate unit
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i}
in
I
is an approximate unit in
J
.
Using this, one gets
I=J\cap C_{0}(X)
.

Now assume
\mathcal{G}
is minimal and let
a\in C_{0}(X)
be non-zero.
By the equivalence
(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2)
,
\overline{BaB}\cap C_{0}(X)=C_{0}(U)
where
U
is an open
\mathcal{G}
-invariant set.
Since
U
contains the open support of
a
it is non-empty.
Hence
U=X
by
\mathcal{G}
-minimality, and therefore
C_{0}(X)\subseteq\overline{BaB}
.
Since
C_{0}(X)
contains an approximate unit for
B
, this implies that
\overline{BaB}=B
.
Thus
C_{0}(X)\subseteq B
is minimal.
For the converse, assume
\mathcal{G}
is not minimal.
Let
U\subseteq X
be a non-trivial open
\mathcal{G}
-invariant set and put
I=C_{0}(U)
.
By the equivalence
(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)
,
\overline{BIB}=\overline{IB}
and so any approximate unit in
I
is also an approximate unit in
\overline{BIB}
.
This implies that
\overline{BIB}\cap C_{0}(X)=I\neq C_{0}(X)
.
Hence for any non-zero
a\in I
we get
\overline{BaB}\neq B
.
Thus
C_{0}(X)\subseteq B
is not minimal.
∎

Topological freeness for étale groupoids was introduced in
[
KM21
, Definition 2.20]
.

Definition 5.5
.

An étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is
topologically free
if there is no non-empty open set
V\subseteq\mathcal{G}\setminus X
with
r|_{V}=d|_{V}
.

Remark 5.6
.

The groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free if and only if for every bisection
V\subseteq\mathcal{G}\setminus X
, the set
\{x\in X:\mathcal{G}(x)\cap V\neq\emptyset\}=\{x\in d(V):h_{V}(x)=x\}
has empty interior in
X
(
h_{V}=r\circ d|_{V}^{-1}
is the partial homeomorphism induced by
V
). The relationship between this and other popular non-triviality conditions is presented in the following diagram:

Effectiveness
of
\mathcal{G}
means that for any open
V\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
r|_{V}=d|_{V}
we have
V\subseteq X
. It implies topological freeness and the converse is true if
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff (
X
is closed in
\mathcal{G}
). If
\mathcal{G}
is topologically principal, that is if
\{x\in X:\mathcal{G}(x)\neq\{x\}\}
has empty interior,
then
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and the converse is true if
\mathcal{G}
is covered by a countable family of bisections (so, for example, when
\mathcal{G}
is second countable or more generally
\sigma
-compact).
In general, all these conditions are different and topological freeness is the weakest.
See
[
KM21
, Section 2.4]
for more details.

The following lemma is the technical heart of our simplicity and pure infiniteness criteria.
For these results, we will need an
S
-grading, that we have been able to ignore until now.
In what follows, we suppress writing the convolution sign ‘
*
’ when multiplying elements of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.

Definition 5.7
.

We call
a\in C_{c}(X)
a
bump function
if it is positive norm-one and equal to
1
on some non-empty open set.

Lemma 5.8

(Pinching property)
.

Assume
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and let
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
be a unital inverse semigroup covering
\mathcal{G}
.
For any
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a contractive element
b\in C_{c}(X)
and a bump function
a\in C_{c}(X)
such that
bf|_{X}:=(bf)|_{X}=b(f|_{X})
is in
C_{0}(X)^{+}
and

\big\|a\cdot\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|-a(bf|_{X})\big\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\|abfa-a(bf|_{X})a\|^{S}_{\max}\leq\varepsilon.

In particular, the first inequality implies that
\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|\leq\|a(bf|_{X})a\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Write
f=\sum_{V\in F}f_{V}
where
F\subseteq S
is finite and
f_{V}\in C_{c}(V,\mathcal{L})
for
V\in F
.
The section
f
is continuous outside of the closed meager set
\bigcup_{V\in F}\partial V
.
Hence all the more it is continuous on the complement of the closed meager set
M=\bigcup_{V\in F}\partial V\cup r\left(\partial X\cap\overline{\supp(f_{V})}\right)
.
Hence
\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|=\sup_{x\in X\setminus M}|f(x)|
and for any
\varepsilon>0
there is a nonempty open set
W\subseteq X\setminus M
such that

(5.10)

|f(x)|\leq\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|<|f(x)|+\varepsilon,\qquad x\in W.

We may assume that
\varepsilon<\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|
, so that
f\neq 0
on
W
.
Let
V\in F
and consider the open bisection
V\setminus\overline{X}\subseteq\mathcal{G}\setminus X
. By topological freeness and
[
KM21
, Proposition 2.24]
, see also
[
ELQ02
, Lemma 2.2]
, the finite union

R:=\bigcup_{V\in F}\{x\in d(V\setminus\overline{X}):h_{V\setminus\overline{X}}(x)=x\}=\bigcup_{V\in F}\{x\in X:\mathcal{G}(x)\cap V\setminus\overline{X}\neq\emptyset\}

has empty interior.
Thus there is
x_{0}\in W\setminus R
.
For each
V\in F
, we claim there is a bump function
a_{V}\in C_{c}(W)
equal to
1
in a neighbourhood of
x_{0}
and

(5.11)

|a_{V}(r(\gamma))\cdot f_{V}(\gamma)\cdot a_{V}(d(\gamma))|\leq\varepsilon/|F|,\qquad\gamma\in V\setminus\overline{X}.

Recall that
x_{0}\notin r\left(\partial X\cap\overline{\supp(f_{V})}\right)
and so only the following three cases are possible:

(1)
x_{0}\in X\setminus r\left(\overline{\supp(f_{V})}\right)
.
Then
f_{V}(r|_{V}^{-1}(x_{0}))=0
so there is a neighbourhood
U\subseteq W
of
x_{0}
where
|f_{V}\circ(r|_{V})^{-1}|\leq\varepsilon/|F|
.
Taking any norm one
a_{V}\in C_{0}(U)
, we get
|a_{V}(r(\gamma))\cdot f_{V}(\gamma)|\leq\varepsilon/|F|
for
\gamma\in V\setminus\overline{X}
, and so (
5.11
) is satisfied.

(2)
x_{0}\in r\left(\overline{\supp(f_{V})}\cap X\right)\subseteq r(V\cap X)=V\cap X
.
Thus
x_{0}\in V\cap W
and, for any
a_{V}\in C_{c}(V\cap W)
, we get
a_{V}(r(\gamma))\cdot f_{V}(\gamma)=0
for
\gamma\in V\setminus\overline{X}
because
d(V\setminus\overline{X})\cap V=\emptyset
.

(3)
x_{0}\in r\left(\overline{\supp(f_{V})}\setminus\overline{X}\right)\subseteq r(V\setminus\overline{X})
.
Since
x_{0}\not\in R
, there is a neighborhood
U\subseteq r(V\setminus\overline{X})\cap W
of
x_{0}
such that
h_{V}^{-1}(U)\cap U=\emptyset
.
Then, for any
a_{V}\in C_{0}(U)
, we have
a_{V}(r(\gamma))a_{V}(d(\gamma))=0
for
\gamma\in V\setminus\overline{X}
, and so (
5.11
) holds.

This proves our claim.
Since
W\cap r\left(\partial X\cap\overline{\supp(f_{V})}\right)=\emptyset
, we have
(a_{V}f_{V})|_{\partial X}=0
.
Thus
(a_{V}f_{V})|_{X}=a_{V}(f_{V}|_{X})\in C_{0}(X)
,
(a_{V}f_{V})|_{V\setminus\overline{X}}\in C_{0}(V\setminus\overline{X},\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
a_{V}f_{V}=(a_{V}f_{V})|_{X}+(a_{V}f_{V})|_{V\setminus\overline{X}}
.
Therefore putting
a:=\prod_{V\in F}a_{V}\in C_{c}(W)
, we get a bump function equal to
1
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}

such that

af=af|_{X}+\sum_{V\in F}af_{V}|_{V\setminus\overline{X}}

where
af|_{X}:=(af)|_{X}=a\cdot(f|_{X})\in C_{0}(X)
and
af_{V}|_{V\setminus\overline{X}}:=(af_{V})|_{V\setminus\overline{X}}\in C_{0}(V\setminus\overline{X},\mathcal{L})
, for
V\in F
.
Take any bump function
c\in C_{c}(W)
equal to
1
on the support of
a
.
Then
b(x):=c(x)\cdot\frac{\overline{f}(x)}{|f(x)|}
defines a norm one continuous function on
X
and
b\cdot(f|_{X})=c|(f|_{X})|\in C_{c}(W)^{+}
.
Moreover,
ab\cdot(f|_{X})=a|(f|_{X})|\in C_{c}(W)^{+}
.
Thus using (
5.10
) we get the first inequality in the assertion.
By the triangle inequality, the definition of
\|\cdot\|^{S}_{\max}
and (
5.11
) we get

\|\sum_{V\in F}af_{V}|_{V\setminus\overline{X}}a\|^{S}_{\max}\leq\sum_{V\in F}\|af_{V}|_{V\setminus\overline{X}}a\|^{S}_{\max}=\sum_{V\in F}\|(af_{V}a)|_{V\setminus\overline{X}}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon.

Thus
\|abfa-a(bf|_{X})a\|^{S}_{\max}\leq\|afa-af|_{X}a\|^{S}_{\max}=\|\sum_{V\in F}af_{V}|_{V\setminus\overline{X}}a\|^{S}_{\max}\leq\varepsilon
.
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

Assume
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and let
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
be a unital inverse semigroup covering
\mathcal{G}
.
If
\psi:F^{S}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})\to B
is a representation which is injective on
C_{0}(X)
, then
\overline{\psi(F^{S}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}}))}
is naturally an exotic groupoid Banach algebra.

Proof.

Since
\psi|_{C_{0}(X)}
is injective,
\psi|_{C_{0}(X)}
is isometric by minimality of the supremum norm, cf.
[
BKM25
, Proposition 2.10]
.
Let
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and take
\epsilon>0
.
For
a,b\in C_{0}(X)
as in Lemma
5.8
, using the inequalities there, we get

\begin{split}\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|&\leq\|a(b\cdot f|_{X})a\|_{\infty}+\epsilon=\|\psi(a(b\cdot f|_{X})a)\|_{B}+\epsilon\\
&\leq\|\psi(a(b\cdot f)a)\|_{B}+2\epsilon\leq\|\psi(f)\|+2\epsilon,\end{split}

where the third step uses the reverse triangle inequality, contractiveness of
\psi
and Lemma
5.8
:

\begin{split}\|\psi\big(a(b\cdot f)a\big)\|_{B}-\|\psi\big(a(b\cdot f|_{X})a\big)\|_{B}&\leq\|\psi\big(a(b\cdot f)a\big)-\psi\big(a(b\cdot f|_{X})a\big)\|_{B}\\
&\leq\|a(b\cdot f)a-a(b\cdot f|_{X})a\|_{\max}^{S}\leq\epsilon.\end{split}

Thus we conclude that
\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|\leq\|\psi(f)\|_{B}
.
It follows that
\overline{\psi(F^{S}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}}))}
is exotic.
∎

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
be a unital inverse semigroup covering
\mathcal{G}
.
Assume
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and consider an
S
-graded Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
such that
C_{0}(X)
embeds isometrically into
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
.

(1)

F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is an exotic groupoid Banach algebra and the inclusion
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
has the generalized intersection property with the hidden ideal
\ker j_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ess}
.

(2)

F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is an essential groupoid Banach algebra if and only if
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
has the intersection property.

(3)

The Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is simple if and only if
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is an essential groupoid Banach algebra and
\mathcal{G}
is minimal.

Proof.

Applying Corollary
5.9
to the canonical map
\Lambda_{\mathcal{R}}:F^{S}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})\to F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
we get that
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is an exotic groupoid Banach algebra.
For an ideal
J
in
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
with
J\cap C_{0}(X)=\{0\}
, Corollaries
4.15
and
5.9
applied to the quotient map

give that
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})/J
is an exotic groupoid Banach algebra and

.
Hence the inclusion
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
has the generalized intersection property with the hidden ideal
\ker j_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ess}
.
Thus it has the intersection property if and only if
\ker j_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ess}=\{0\}
, which by definition means that
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is an essential groupoid Banach algebra.
Combining this with Lemmas
5.2
,
5.4
one gets that
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is simple if and only if
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is essential and
\mathcal{G}
is minimal.
∎

5.2.
Characterizations of topological freeness

We start by linking topological freeness to maximal abelianness of
C_{0}(X)
in an essential groupoid algebra.
In the setting of
C^{*}
-algebra crossed products this goes back to
[
ZM68
, Proposition 4.14]
and plays a crucial role in the theory of Cartan
C^{*}
-subalgebras, cf.
[
Ren80
, Proposition II.4.7]
,
[
Ren08
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Proposition 5.11
.

If
C_{0}(X)
is maximal abelian in some exotic groupoid Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
, then
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free.
If
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and Hausdorff, then
C_{0}(X)
is maximal abelian in every reduced groupoid Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}
is not topologically free, so that there is a non-empty open bisection
V\subseteq\mathcal{G}\setminus X
for which
r|_{V}=d|_{V}
.
Thus every
b\in C_{c}(V,\mathcal{L})\subseteq F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
commutes with
C_{0}(X)
for any algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
that contain the spaces
C_{c}(U,\mathcal{L})
,
U\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
(so for instance an exotic Banach algebra).
Hence
C_{0}(X)
is not maximal abelian in
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
.

Now assume that
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff,
F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
is a reduced groupoid Banach algebra, and let
b\in F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})\setminus C_{0}(X)
commute with all elements in
C_{0}(X)
.
Since
j_{\mathcal{R}}:F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})\to C_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is injective and
E^{\mathcal{R}}_{X}(b):=j_{\mathcal{R}}(b)|_{X}\in C_{0}(X)
, we get that
c=b-E^{\mathcal{R}}_{X}(b)\in F_{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})
commutes with elements in
C_{0}(X)
and
\supp(j_{\mathcal{R}}(c))
has non-empty interior and is contained in
\mathcal{G}\setminus X
.
Thus there is a non-empty open bisection
V\subseteq\supp(j_{\mathcal{R}}(c))\subseteq\mathcal{G}\setminus X
and we have
a*j_{\mathcal{R}}(c)=j_{\mathcal{R}}(ac)=j_{\mathcal{R}}(ca)=j_{\mathcal{R}}(c)*a
for all
a\in C_{c}(V)
, because
j_{\mathcal{R}}
is a
C_{0}(X)
-bimodule map.
This implies that
r|_{V}=d|_{V}
.
Hence
\mathcal{G}
is not topologically free.
∎

It is known that, in the untwisted case, topological freeness is equivalent to the generalized intersection property for essential groupoid

-algebras, see

This generalizes classical results for crossed products by Kawamura–Tomiyama

A corresponding result for

We now generalize all these results to essential groupoid Banach algebras associated to

.
To this end, we use the following representation, which in the

]

Lemma 5.12
.

For any

, we have a representation
\Lambda^{\tr}_{p}:F^{p}({\mathcal{G}})\to B(\ell^{p}(X))

\Lambda^{\tr}_{p}(f)\xi(x):=\sum_{d(\gamma)=x}f(\gamma)\xi(r(\gamma)),\qquad f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G}),\ \xi\in\ell^{p}(X).

Proof.

Consider the canonical action

of any wide inverse semigroup

[

One readily sees that

\pi^{\tr}(a)\xi(x):=a(x)\xi(x),\qquad v_{U}^{\tr}\xi(x):=\begin{cases}\xi(h_{U^{*}}(x)),&x\in r(U),\\
0,&x\notin r(U),\end{cases}

for

,

Theorem 5.13
.

Let

be an étale groupoid.
For any non-empty

(2)

where

,

, assume that

is not topologically free.

with

.

fails.

(1)
is.

and
5.4
, we get that either of
(3)
or
(4)
implies
(1)
by Theorem
5.13
.
∎

In the twisted case, we have the following characterization of topological freeness that generalizes
[
BK24
, Theorem 5.13]
, which is formulated for group actions, and
[
KM22
, Proposition 6.4]
in the case of a line bundle.

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
be a twisted étale groupoid and let
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
by any non-empty set.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{G}
is topologically free;

(2)

the inclusion
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
has the intersection property for every open subgroupoid
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
containing
X
;

(3)

the inclusion
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
has the intersection property for every open subgroupoid
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
generated by
X
and an open bisection
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}\setminus X
.

Proof.

(1)
implies
(2)
by Theorem
5.10
, because topological freeness of
\mathcal{G}
passes to an open subgroupoid
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
containing
X
.
It is clear that
(2)
implies
(3)
. To prove that
(3)
implies
(1)
, assume that
(1)
fails.
Then there is a non-empty bisection
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}\setminus\overline{X}
with
r|_{U}=d|_{U}
.
Then the union
\mathcal{H}:=X\cup\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}U^{k}
is an open subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
generated by
X
and
U
.
The open set
U^{0}:=d(U)=r(U)
is
\mathcal{H}
-invariant.
In particular, the groupoid
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}U^{k}
is an ideal in
\mathcal{H}
(that is,
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{0}\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{0}
), and so if
B
denotes the closure of the image of
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}})
in
F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
, we see that
B
is an ideal.
In fact,
B
is an ideal in
F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
generated by
C_{0}(U^{0})=B\cap C_{0}(X)
.

We claim that if
C_{0}(U^{0})\subseteq B
does not have the intersection property, then
C_{0}(X)\subseteq F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
does not have it either.
Indeed, assume that there is a non-zero ideal
I
in
B
with
I\cap C_{0}(U^{0})=\{0\}
.
Using that an approximate unit in
C_{0}(U_{0})
is an approximate unit in
B
, and that
B
is an ideal in
F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
, one concludes that
I
is an ideal in
F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
and
I\cap C_{0}(X)=I\cap C_{0}(U^{0})=\{0\}
.
Hence
C_{0}(X)
does not detect ideals in
F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
.
This proves our claim.

Thus to show that
(3)
fails, which finishes the proof, it is enough to show that
C_{0}(U^{0})\subseteq B
does not have the intersection property.
We have the following two cases:

1) If
U^{n}\cap U^{0}=U^{n}\cap X=\emptyset
for all
n>0
, then
\{U^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
are pairwise disjoint and
\mathcal{H}_{0}\cong U^{0}\times\mathbb{Z}
is the transformation groupoid for the trivial
\mathbb{Z}
-action (since
U
is a bisection with
r|_{U}=d|_{U}
, we have
U^{n}:=\{\gamma^{n}:\gamma\in U\}
).

2) If
U^{n}\cap U^{0}\neq\emptyset
for some
n>0
then, assuming
n
is the smallest such number and replacing
U
by
U(U^{n}\cap U^{0})
, we may assume that
\{U^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
are pairwise disjoint and
U^{n}=U^{0}
.
Then
\mathcal{H}_{0}\cong U^{0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is the transformation groupoid for the trivial
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-action.

Thus we may assume that
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is the transformation groupoid for the trivial
G
-action where
G=\mathbb{Z}
or
G=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.
Then
\mathcal{H}
is a transformation groupoid for a partial
G
-action generated by
\textrm{id}_{U^{0}}
, cf. Example
7.2
below.
In particular,
\mathcal{H}
is Hausdorff and
F^{P}_{\ess}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})=F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
is a reduced groupoid algebra.
This implies that
B\subseteq F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}})
can be naturally identified with the reduced algebra
F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}})
.
Thus
B
can be identified with a reduced
L^{P}
-operator algebra crossed product studied in
[
BK24
]
.
Since the group
G
is amenable, the trivial action is amenable and
B
is in fact also a full
L^{P}
-operator algebra crossed product by
[
BK24
, Corollary 4.14]
.
Since the
G
-action is trivial, by passing to a subgroup of
G
if necessary, we get that
C_{0}(U^{0})\subseteq B
does not have the intersection property by
[
BK24
, Corollary 5.13]
.
Formally, in
[
BK24
]
it was assumed that
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, but a quick inspection of the proofs of the invoked results show that they remain valid for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, also due to our definition of the universal algebras in the real case.
∎

6.
Pure infiniteness

Cuntz
[
Cun81
]
defined pure infiniteness for a simple
C^{*}
-algebra
B
as the property that every nonzero hereditary
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
B
has an infinite projection.
The success of this notion inspired a number of attempts to transfer it to other contexts.
In particular, Phillips
[
Phi13a
]
, see also
[
DH22
]
, introduced pure infiniteness for unital simple Banach algebras, which agrees with the pure infiniteness for unital simple rings from
[
AGP02
]
.
Recently, this definition was extended to arbitrary (not necessarily unital) Banach algebras in
[
CMR23
]
using an approximation condition mimicking the Cuntz preorder.
Here we will define pure infiniteness for simple Banach algebras using an algebraic condition from
[
AGP02
]
,
[
AAM17
]
.

Recall that idempotents
e,f
in a ring
B
are equivalent (written
e\sim f)
, provided there are
x,y\in B
such that
e=xy
and
f=yx
.
For any idempotents, we write
e\leq f
if
ef=fe=e
, and
e<f
if in addition
e\neq f
.
We call an idempotent
e\in B

infinite
if there exists an idempotent
f\in R
such that
e\sim f<e
.
Note that
\sim
is an equivalence relation that preserves infiniteness.
A
simple ring

B
is called
purely infinite
, see
[
AAM17
, Definition 3.1.8]
,
[
AGP02
, Definition 1.2]
, if

(
\dagger
)

for every
y\in B\setminus\{0\}
, there are
x,z\in B
such that
xyz
is an infinite idempotent in
B
.

Definition 6.1
.

We say that a simple Banach algebra
B
is
purely infinite
if it satisfies (
\dagger
).

Remark 6.2
.

Condition (
\dagger
) holds if and only if every non-zero left ideal (not necessarily closed) in
B
contains an infinite idempotent, see
[
AAM17
, Proposition 3.1.7]
.
Thus (
\dagger
) is equivalent to the apparently stronger and unsymmetrical condition that, for every
y\in B\setminus\{0\}
there is
x\in B
such that
xy
is an infinite idempotent in
B
. A version of this remark where left ideals are replaced by right ideals is also true.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
B
be a simple Banach algebra.

(1)

If
B
is unital then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

B
is a purely infinite Banach algebra;

(b)

B
is a purely infinite simple ring in the sense of
[
AGP02
]
,
[
AAM17
]
;

(c)

B
is purely infinite in the sense of
[
Phi13a
]
,
[
DH22
]
, that is,
B\not\cong\mathbb{F}
and for every
y\in B\setminus\{0\}
there are
x,z\in B
such that
xyz=1
.

(2)

If
B
has a bounded (two-sided) approximate unit consisting of idempotents, then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

B
is a purely infinite Banach algebra;

(b)

B
is purely infinite in the sense of
[
CMR23
]
, that is,
B\not\cong\mathbb{F}
and for every
a,b\in B\setminus\{0\}
there are sequences
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(z_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
B
such that
x_{n}bz_{n}\to a
;

(c)

for every idempotent
e\in B\setminus\{0\}
, the unital Banach algebra
eBe
is purely infinite in the sense of the equivalent conditions in
(1)
.

(3)

If
B
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, then
B
is a purely infinite Banach algebra if and only if it is purely infinite in the sense of Cuntz
[
Cun81
]
, that is, every non-zero hereditary
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
B
contains a partial isometry
u
with
uu^{*}<u^{*}u
.

Proof.

(1)
.
The conditions
(1)(a)
and
(1)(b)
differ only in that one assumes algebraic simplicity and the other analytic simplicity, but for unital Banach algebras these are equivalent by openness of the set of invertible elements.
The equivalence
(1)(b)
\Leftrightarrow
(1)(c)
holds by
[
AGP02
, Theorem 1.6]
.

(2)
.
The implications
(2)(a)
\Rightarrow
(2)(c)
and
(2)(b)
\Rightarrow
(2)(c)
hold in general.
Indeed, for
(2)(b)
\Rightarrow
(2)(c)
see
[
CMR23
, Corollary 3.4]
, and for
(2)(a)
\Rightarrow
(2)(c)
assume
(2)(a)
and let
e\in B\setminus\{0\}
be an idempotent.
Then
eBe
is a unital Banach algebra, and for any nonzero
y\in eBe
, there is
x\in B
such that
xy\in Be
is an infinite projection in
Be
.
Then
xy=xye\sim exy=exey\in eBe
, and so for
x^{\prime}:=exe\in eBe
the product
x^{\prime}y
is an infinite projection in
eBe
.
Hence
eBe
is purely infinite, see Remark
6.2
.

Now let us assume
(2)(c)
and that
B
has a bounded two-sided approximate unit consisting of idempotents.
By assumption
B\neq\mathbb{F}
.
Let
a,b\in B\setminus\{0\}
.
Then we may choose a sequence
(e_{n})_{n}
of idempotents in
B
such that
e_{n}ae_{n}\to a
and
e_{n}be_{n}\to b
, and by
(2)(c)
for each
n
we may find
x_{n},z_{n}\in e_{n}Be_{n}
such that
x_{n}(e_{n}be_{n})y_{n}=e_{n}ae_{n}
.
Then
x_{n}bz_{n}\to a
, which proves
(2)(b)
.
Now let
y\in B\setminus\{0\}
.
Then there is an idempotent
e\in B
such that
eye\in eBe
is nonzero.
By
(2)(c)
there are
x,z\in eBe
such that
xeyez
is an infinite idempotent in
eBe
.
This proves
(2)(a)
.

(3)
.
Assume
B
is purely infinite in the sense of Cuntz
[
Cun81
]
.
Let
y\in B\setminus\{0\}
.
We may assume that
\|y\|=1
.
Put
a:=y^{*}y
and let
f
be a continuous function which takes the value
0
on
(-\infty,1/2]
and the value
1
on
[1,+\infty)
, and is linear on
[1/2,1]
.
Then
af(a)\geq f(a)/2>0
in
\overline{aBa}\subseteq\overline{y^{*}By}
.
By assumption, there is a partial isometry
u\in\overline{f(a)Bf(a)}
such that
uu^{*}<u^{*}u
.
Writing
u=f(a)b
with
b\in\overline{f(a)Bf(a)}
, we get
u^{*}au=b^{*}f(a)af(a)b\geq 1/2\cdot b^{*}f(a)f(a)b=1/2\cdot u^{*}u
.
Thus
u^{*}au
is invertible in the unital algebra
\overline{u^{*}Bu}
with
u^{*}u
being a unit.
Denote the inverse of
u^{*}au
by
z
, and put
w:=zu^{*}a=zu^{*}y^{*}y\in By
.
Then
uw\in By
is an idempotent equivalent to the infinite idempotent
wu=u^{*}u
.
Hence
uw\in By
is infinite.

Conversely, assume
B
is purely infinite in the sense of Definition
6.1
and let
y\in B\setminus\{0\}
.
Then there is an infinite idempotent
e
in
By
.
Every idempotent
e
in a
C^{*}
-algebra is similar (in fact even homotopy equivalent) to a projection
p
with
p=pe
, see the proof of
[
Bla98
, Proposition 4.6.2]
.
Since
p\in Be\subseteq By
and
p
is self-adjoint, we get
p\in\overline{y^{*}By}
.
Since
e
is an infinite element, it follows that
p
is an infinite projection.
Indeed,
p
is an infinite element because it is equivalent to an infinite element
e
, and so there is an idempotent
f
with
p\sim f<p
.
Then, as mentioned above, there is a projection
q\sim f
with
q=qf
.
Thus we have
q\sim p
in
\overline{y^{*}By}
and so they are Murray–von Neumann equivalent, see
[
Bla98
, Proposition 4.6.4]
.
Using that
f<p
and
q=qf
, we get
q=qf=q(fp)=(qf)p=qp
.
Therefore
q\leq p
(because
p
and
q
are self-adjoint) and in fact
q<p
(because
q=p
would imply
f=p
).
∎

Remark 6.4
.

Without the standing assumption in
(2)
above, we do not know the relationship between pure infiniteness in our sense and that of
[
CMR23
]
.
When
B
is a simple unital
C^{*}
-algebra, the equivalence between the Cuntz definition and the condition in
(1)
(b) is well known, see
[
Bla98
, Proposition 6.11.5]
.

We now consider two known pure infiniteness criteria introduced for
C^{*}
-algebras associated to Hausdorff groupoids.
We generalize them to twisted Banach algebras associated to not necessarily Hausdorff groupoids.
The first criterion was introduced for group actions on compact spaces
[
LS96
]
,
[
JR00
]
and then generalized to groupoids with compact unit spaces by Suzuki
[
Suz17
]
.
Recall that
S(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
is the wide inverse semigroup of bisections on which the bundle
\mathcal{L}
is trivial.
Thus
S(\mathcal{L})=\Bis(\mathcal{G})
when the twist comes from a cocycle.

Definition 6.5

(cf.
[
Suz17
, Definition 3.2]
)
.

Let
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say that
\mathcal{G}
is
n
-filling with respect to
S
if
X
is
compact, infinite
and for every non-empty open set
U\subseteq X
there are
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}\in S
with
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}r(W_{i}U)=X
.

Lemma 6.6

(cf.
[
Suz17
, Lemma 3.10]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be
n
-filling with respect to a unital inverse semigroup
S\subseteq S(\mathcal{L})
covering
\mathcal{G}
.
For any
b\in C(X)^{+}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there is
c\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
such that
\|c\|_{\max}^{S}\leq n
,
c^{*}*b*c\in C(X)
and
c^{*}*b*c\geq\|b\|_{\infty}-\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\|b\|_{\infty}=1
.
Using the
n
-filling property, exactly as in the proof of
[
Suz17
, Lemma 3.10]
, one may construct mutually disjoint non-empty subsets
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
of
U:=\{x\in X:b(x)\geq 1-\varepsilon\}
, relatively compact bisections
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, where each
W_{i}
is a subset of an element of
S
, and closed subsets
K_{i}\subseteq W_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, such that

d(W_{i})\subseteq U_{i},\ i=1,\ldots,n,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}r(K_{i})=X.

Now, since the bundle over each
W_{i}
can be trivialized, we may pick sections
f_{i}\in C_{c}(W_{i},\mathcal{L})
such that
\|f_{i}\|_{\infty}=1
and
|f_{i}|_{K_{i}}|=1
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Putting
c:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{*}
, we have
\|c\|_{\max}^{S}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}^{*}\|_{\infty}=n
, and since
\{W_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are bisections with mutually disjoint domains
\{d(W_{i})\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
, we get
c^{*}*b*c=\sum_{i=1}f_{i}^{*}*b*f_{i}\in C(X)
.
Since
d(W_{i})\subseteq U
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}r(K_{i})=X
, we further obtain
c^{*}*b*c\geq 1-\varepsilon
.
∎

Theorem 6.7
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and
n
-filling with respect to a unital inverse semigroup
S\subseteq S(\mathcal{L})
covering
\mathcal{G}
.
Then every
S
-graded essential Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is purely infinite simple (and unital).

Proof.

Let
y\in F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\setminus\{0\}
.
By Proposition
6.3
(1)
, it suffices to show that there are
x,z\in F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
such that
xyz
is invertible.
By Proposition
4.12
, there is
U\in S(\mathcal{L})
and
a_{U}\in C_{c}(r(U))
such that
\E^{\mathcal{R}}_{U}(a_{U}y)\neq 0
.
Replacing
y
with
a_{U}y
and scaling, we may assume that
y\in C_{c}(r(U))F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
and
\|\E^{\mathcal{R}}_{U}(y)\|=1
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be such that
\varepsilon<1/(2n^{2}+2)
.
Let
i_{\mathcal{R}}:\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\to F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
be the canonical homomorphism, which is isometric on the subspaces
C_{c}(V,\mathcal{L})
,
V\in S
.
The argument from the beginning of the proof of
[
JR00
, Theorem 1.2]
shows that we may find
f\in C_{c}(r(U))\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
with
\|y-i_{\mathcal{R}}(f)\|_{\mathcal{R}}<\varepsilon
and
\|\E^{\mathcal{R}}_{U}(i_{\mathcal{R}}(f))\|=\|q_{U}(f\circ r|_{U}^{-1})\|=1
.
By Lemma
3.5
, there is a contractive
b\in C_{c}(U^{*},\mathcal{L})
such that
|b*f|_{X}|=|f\circ r|_{U}^{-1}|
.
Thus, replacing
y
with
by
and
f
with
bf
, we may assume that
U=X
, that is, we have
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
with

\|y-i_{\mathcal{R}}(f)\|_{\mathcal{R}}<\varepsilon

and
\|\E^{\mathcal{R}}_{X}(i_{\mathcal{R}}(f))\|=\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|=1
.
By Lemma
5.8
there are norm one functions
a,b\in C(X)
such that
a,bf|_{X}\in C(X)^{+}
,
1=\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|\leq\|a(bf|_{X})a\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon
and

\|abi_{\mathcal{R}}(f)a-a(bf|_{X})a\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\|a(bf)a-a(bf|_{X})a\|_{\max}^{S}\leq\varepsilon.

In particular,
\|a(bf|_{X})a\|\geq 1-\varepsilon
.
Applying Lemma
6.6
to
a(bf|_{X})a
, we get
c\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
such that
\|c\|_{\max}^{S}\leq n
and
g:=c^{*}(a(bf|_{X})a)c\in C(X)
satisfies
g\geq 1-2\varepsilon
.
In particular,
d:=i_{\mathcal{R}}(c)\in F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
satisfies
\|d\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\|c\|_{\max}^{S}\leq n
and
g
is invertible in
C(X)\subseteq F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
where
\|g^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq(1-2\varepsilon)^{-1}
.
Thus every
h\in F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
with
\|h-g\|_{\mathcal{R}}<1-2\varepsilon
is invertible in
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
Using the above displayed inequalities, together with
\|d\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq n
,
\|a\|,\|b\|\leq 1
, and our choice of
\varepsilon
, we get

\begin{split}\big\|d^{*}(abya)d-g\big\|_{\mathcal{R}}&\leq\big\|d^{*}\big(abya-abi_{\mathcal{R}}(f)a\big)d\big\|_{\mathcal{R}}+\big\|d^{*}\big(abi_{\mathcal{R}}(f)a-abf|_{X}a\big)d\big\|_{\mathcal{R}}\\
&\leq n^{2}\varepsilon+n^{2}\varepsilon=2n^{2}\varepsilon<1-2\varepsilon.\end{split}

Hence
d^{*}(adya)d
is invertible in
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
∎

We now turn to the second criterion which is due to Anantharaman-Delaroche and is sometimes easier to apply.
Namely, we generalize the classical result
[
A-D97
, Proposition 2.4]
from (untwisted) groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras to the twisted Banach algebra case.
Recall that
h_{W}=r\circ d|_{W}^{-1}:d(W)\to r(W)
is the homeomorphism induced by
W\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
.

Definition 6.8

(cf.
[
A-D97
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

Let
S\subseteq\Bis(\mathcal{G})
.
We call
\mathcal{G}

locally contracting with respect to
S
if for every non-empty open
U\subseteq X
there are open
V\subseteq U
and
W\in S
with
\overline{V}\subseteq d(W)
and
h_{W}(\overline{V})\subsetneq V
(equivalently
r(W\overline{V})\subsetneq V
).

Remark 6.9
.

If
S=S(\mathcal{L})=\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, so for instance when the twist comes from a
2
-cocycle (equivalently
\mathcal{L}
is trivial as a line bundle, see Example
2.2
), then the properties in Definitions
6.5
and
6.8
do not depend on the twist, and if they hold, we simply call the groupoid
\mathcal{G}

n
-filling

[
Suz17
]
or
locally contractive

[
A-D97
]
, respectively.

Remark 6.10
.

The
n
-filling property implies that
\mathcal{G}
is minimal, while locally contracting does not.
Assuming minimality, the relationship between
n
-filling and locally contracting is not completely clear.
However, when
\mathcal{G}
is ample, it is known that
n
-filling does not depend on
n
(is equivalent to
1
-filling) and it implies locally contracting, see
[
Ma22
, Theorem 5.8]
(it also implies paradoxicality of all non-empty compact open sets, see
[
RS20
, Proposition 7.7]
).

Lemma 6.11
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}
is locally contracting with respect to a unital inverse semigroup
S\subseteq S(\mathcal{L})
covering
\mathcal{G}
.
For any bump function
a\in C_{c}(X)
, there is an idempotent
p\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
with
p=ap=pa
and
\|p\|_{\max}^{S}\leq 4
, and there is
u\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, with
\|u\|_{\max}^{S}\leq 8
, such that
u^{*}u=p
and
uu^{*}<p
(so
p
is infinite).
Moreover, these relations remain valid for the images of
u
and
p
in any
S
-graded essential Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.

Proof.

We adapt the argument from the proof of
[
A-D97
, Proposition 2.2]
, see also
[
Sim20
, Theorem 4.4.2]
.
If
X
is not compact, we let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})}
be the minimal unitization of the algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
.
Then
1\mathbb{C}+C_{c}(X)=\widetilde{C_{c}(X)}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})}
is naturally a subalgebra and
\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\subseteq\widetilde{\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})}
is a two-sided ideal.
Moreover, every positive function in
\widetilde{C_{c}(X)}
has a square root in
\widetilde{C_{c}(X)}
.

Let
a\in C_{c}(X)
be a bump function.
Let
U
be a non empty open set such that
a|_{U}\equiv 1
. Take
V\subseteq U
and
W\in S
with
\overline{V}\subseteq d(W)
and
h_{W}(\overline{V})\subsetneq V
.
Find an open
V_{0}
such that
h_{W}(\overline{V})\subseteq V_{0}\subseteq\overline{V_{0}}\subsetneq V
.
Fix a positive norm one function
b\in C_{c}(V_{0})
which is equal to
1
on
h_{W}(\overline{V})
.
Then
1-b^{2}\in\widetilde{C_{c}(X)}
vanishes on
h_{W}(\overline{V})
and
b\circ h_{W^{*}}\in C_{c}(h_{W}(V_{0}))
, so in particular
(1-b^{2})^{1/2}(b\circ h_{W^{*}})=0
.
Since the bundle
\mathcal{L}
can be trivialized on
W
, we may pick a unitary section
c_{W}\in C(W,\mathcal{L})
.
Put
x:=c_{W}b=(b\circ h_{W^{*}})c_{W}
.
Then
x^{*}x=b^{2}\in C_{c}(V)
and
xx^{*}=b^{2}\circ h_{W^{*}}\in C_{c}(h_{W}(V))
.
It follows that
(1-x^{*}x)^{1/2}x=0
(
x
is the so called
scaling element
).
Therefore

v:=x+(1-x^{*}x)^{1/2}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})}

satisfies
v^{*}v=1
.
Thus
vv^{*}
is an idempotent and also

p:=1-vv^{*}=x^{*}x-xx^{*}-x(1-x^{*}x)^{1/2}-(1-x^{*}x)^{1/2}x^{*}\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})

is an idempotent with
\|p\|_{\max}^{S}\leq 4
(as the four elements in the above expression are
\|\cdot\|_{\max}^{S}
contractive).
Since
a|_{U}\equiv 1
and
d(\supp(x)),r(\supp(x))\subseteq V\subseteq U
, we have
ax=xa=x
.
By the above formula for
p
, this implies that
p=ap=pa\in a\cdot\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\cdot a
.

Since
h_{W}(\overline{V_{0}})\subsetneq h_{W}(V)
, the open set
O:=h_{W}(V)\setminus h_{W}(\overline{V_{0}})
is non-empty, and
b\circ h_{W^{*}}
vanishes on
O
.
The function
x^{*}x-xx^{*}=b^{2}-b^{2}\circ h_{W^{*}}
on
O
is constant equal to
1
, and
(1-x^{*}x)^{1/2}
vanishes on
O
.
Thus
p|_{O}\equiv 1
and therefore for any
S
-graded essential Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, using the canonical homomorphism
i_{\mathcal{R}}:F^{S}({\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}})\to F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, we have

\|\E_{X}^{\mathcal{R}}(i_{\mathcal{R}}(p))\|=\|q_{X}(p|_{X})\|\geq\|q_{O}(p|_{O})\|=1,

and so
i_{\mathcal{R}}(p)
is non-zero.

To prove that in fact
i_{\mathcal{R}}(p)
is infinite we take any bump function
c\in C_{c}(X)
with support in
O
, so that we get
cx^{*}x=c
and
cx=0
.
Applying the above reasoning to
c
(instead of
a
), we get a scaling element
y
with
cy=yc=y
.
This implies
yx^{*}x=x^{*}xy=y
and
yx=0
.
Hence
py=yp=y
.
As a consequence
p
commutes with the elements in the Banach subalgebra of
\widetilde{C_{c}(X)}
generated by the positive function
1-y^{*}y
, and in particular with
(1-y^{*}y)^{1/2}
.
Thus
p
commutes with
w:=y+(1-y^{*}y)^{1/2}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})}
.
Therefore putting
u:=pw\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
, and using that
w^{*}w=1
(which holds because
y
is scaling), we get
u^{*}u=p
and

uu^{*}=p-(y^{*}y-yy^{*}-y(1-y^{*}y)^{1/2}-(1-y^{*}y)^{1/2}y^{*}).

The reasoning showing that
i_{\mathcal{R}}(p)\neq 0
shows that
i_{\mathcal{R}}
applied to the term in the brackets is non-zero.
Hence the image of
p
in
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
is infinite.
Clearly,
\|u\|_{\max}^{S}\leq\|p\|_{\max}^{S}\|w\|_{\max}^{S}\leq 8
.
∎

Proposition 6.12
.

Assume
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and locally contracting with respect to a unital inverse semigroup
S\subseteq S(\mathcal{L})
covering
\mathcal{G}
.
Then every non-zero left ideal in every
S
-graded essential Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
contains an infinite idempotent.

Proof.

Let
y\in F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})\setminus\{0\}
.
We show that
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})y
contains an infinite idempotent.
Let
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{192}
.
Arguing as in the beginning of the proof of Theorem
6.7
, we may assume that
\|\E^{\mathcal{R}}_{X}(y)\|=1
and that there is
f\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
with
\|y-i_{\mathcal{R}}(f)\|_{\mathcal{R}}<\varepsilon
and
\|E^{\mathcal{R}}_{X}(i_{\mathcal{R}}(f))\|=\|q_{X}(f|_{X})\|=1
.
By Lemma
5.8
there is a contractive
b\in C_{c}(X)
and a bump function
a\in C_{c}(X)
such that
bf|_{X}\in C_{c}(X)^{+}
and

\|a^{2}-a(bf|_{X})a\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\|a(bf)a-a(bf|_{X})a\|_{\max}^{S}\leq\varepsilon.

By Lemma
6.11
, there is
u\in\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
such that
\|u\|_{\max}^{S}\leq 8
,
p:=u^{*}u
is an idempotent with
p=ap=pa
,
\|p\|_{\max}^{S}\leq 4
,
uu^{*}<p
and
i_{\mathcal{R}}(uu^{*})<i_{\mathcal{R}}(p)
.
In particular,
i_{\mathcal{R}}(p)
is an infinite idempotent in
aF_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})a
.
Using these relations we get

\begin{gathered}\|p-u^{*}(bf|_{X})u\|_{\max}^{S}=\|u^{*}a^{2}u-u^{*}a(bf|_{X})au\|_{\max}^{S}\leq 64\varepsilon<1/3,\\
\|u^{*}bfu-u^{*}(bf|_{X})u\|_{\max}^{S}=\|u^{*}a(bf)au-u^{*}a(bf|_{X})au\|_{\max}^{S}\leq 64\varepsilon<1/3,\\
\|i_{\mathcal{R}}(u^{*})(by)i_{\mathcal{R}}(u)-i_{\mathcal{R}}(u^{*}bfu)\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq 16\|y-i_{\mathcal{R}}(f)\|_{\mathcal{R}}<64\varepsilon<1/3.\end{gathered}

This implies that
\|i_{\mathcal{R}}(p)-i_{\mathcal{R}}(u^{*})(by)i_{\mathcal{R}}(u)\|_{\mathcal{R}}<1
and therefore
i_{\mathcal{R}}(u^{*})(by)i_{\mathcal{R}}(u)
is invertible in the Banach algebra
i_{\mathcal{R}}(p)F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})i_{\mathcal{R}}(p)
.
We denote its inverse by
c
and we put
w:=c\cdot i_{\mathcal{R}}(u^{*})(by)\in F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})y
.
Then
wi_{\mathcal{R}}(u)=i_{\mathcal{R}}(p)
is an infinite idempotent and therefore the equivalent idempotent
i_{\mathcal{R}}(u)w\in F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})y
is also infinite.
∎

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
be a twisted étale groupoid where
\mathcal{G}
is topologically free and minimal. If
\mathcal{G}
is locally contracting with respect to a unital subsemigroup
S\subseteq S(\mathcal{L})
covering
\mathcal{G}
, then every
S
-graded essential Banach algebra
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L})
of

Proof.

Combine Proposition
6.12
and Theorem
5.10
.
∎

7.
Applications and examples

7.1.
Crossed products by twisted partial group actions

We use a transformation groupoid model to apply our results to twisted partial group actions.
In particular, we will generalize and extend some of the results of
[
BK24
]
from global to partial actions.

We fix a (topological)
partial action
of a discrete group
G
on a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
.
This is a map
\theta:G\to\PHomeo(X)
such that
\theta_{1}=\id_{X}
,
\theta_{t}^{-1}=\theta_{t^{-1}}
and the partial homeomorphism
\theta_{ts}
extends
\theta_{t}\circ\theta_{s}
for all
t,s\in G
.
Thus for each
t\in G
we have a homeomorphism
\theta_{t}:X_{t^{-1}}\to X_{t}
between two open subsets of
X
and for each
s\in G
the natural domain
\theta_{t^{-1}}(X_{s^{-1}}\cap X_{t})=X_{t^{-1}}\cap X_{(st)^{-1}}
for
\theta_{s}\circ\theta_{t}
is contained in
X_{(st)^{-1}}
.
Fixing
\theta
is equivalent to fixing an algebraic
partial action

\alpha:G\to\PAut(C_{0}(X))
where
\alpha_{t}(a):=a\circ\theta_{t}^{-1}
,
a\in C_{0}(X_{t^{-1}})
,
t\in S
.
We refer to
\theta
as
dual
to
\alpha
.

We also fix a
2-cocycle of the partial action
\alpha
(or of
\theta
), which is a family of maps
u(s,t):G\times G\to C_{u}(X_{s}\cap X_{st})
,
s,t\in G
, satisfying
u(1,t)=u(t,1)=1
for
t\in G
, and

\alpha_{r}\big(au(s,t)\big)u(r,st)=\alpha_{r}(a)u(r,s)u(rs,t),\qquad a\in C_{0}(X_{r^{-1}}\cap X_{s}\cap X_{st}),\ r,s,t\in G,

see
[
Exe97
]
.

Definition 7.1

(cf.
[
BK24
, Definitions 2.1, 4.12]
)
.

The
universal Banach algebra crossed product
for the twisted partial action
(\alpha,u)
on
C_{0}(X)
is the Banach
*
-algebra

F(\alpha,u):=\{f\in\ell^{1}(G,C_{0}(X)):f(t)\in C_{0}(X_{t}),\ t\in G\}

with
\ell^{1}
-norm from
\ell^{1}(G,C_{0}(X))
and operations

(f*g)(r):=\sum_{st=r}\alpha_{s}\Big(\alpha_{s}^{-1}\big(f(s)\big)g(t)\Big)u(s,t),\qquad f^{*}(t):=\alpha_{t}\big(f(t^{-1})^{*}\big)u(t,t^{-1})^{*},

for
f,g\in F(\alpha,u)
and
s,t,r\in G
.
For any class
\mathcal{R}
of representations of
F(\alpha,u)
, we denote the Hausdorff completion of
F(\alpha,u)
in the seminorm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{R}}:=\sup\{\|\psi(f)\|:\psi\in\mathcal{R}\}
,
f\in F(\alpha,u)
, by
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)
.
For any non-empty
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
we define the
L^{P}
-crossed product
for
(\theta,u)
as the completion
F^{P}(\alpha,u):=\overline{F(\alpha,u)}^{\|\cdot\|_{L_{P}}}
, where
\|f\|_{L^{P}}
is the supremum of
\|\psi(f)\|
over representations
\psi:F(\alpha,u)\to B(L^{p}(\mu))
, where
p\in P
and
\mu
is a measure, and if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
we also assume that
\psi|_{C_{c}(X)}
is positive.

We briefly describe how to view the algebras
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)
as groupoid Banach algebras.
More details, as well as description of representations of
F(\alpha,u)
in terms of covariant representations of
(\alpha,u)
, can be found in an earlier preprint version of
[
BKM25
]
, preserved as arXiv:2303.09997v3, see subsection 7.1 therein.

Example 7.2
.

The
transformation groupoid for
\theta
is

\mathcal{G}_{\theta}:=\bigsqcup_{t\in G}\{t\}\times X_{t^{-1}}

equipped with the product topology from
G\times X
and the algebraic structure given by

r(t,x):=\theta_{t}(x),\quad d(t,x):=x,\quad\big(s,\theta_{t}(x)\big)\cdot(t,x):=(st,x),\qquad s,t\in G,\ x\in X_{t^{-1}}.

The
twist associated to
u
is given by the trivial line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{u}=\mathcal{G}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{F}
and the
2
-cocycle
\sigma_{u}
for
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
given by

\sigma_{u}((s,\theta_{t}(x)),(t,x))=u(s,t)(\theta_{st}(x)).

Let
S_{\theta}
be the inverse semigroup consisting of bisections
\{t\}\times U\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
where
U\subseteq X_{t^{-1}}
is open.
Then one sees that the map
C_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{\theta},\mathcal{L}_{u})\ni\hat{f}\mapsto f\in C_{c}(G,C_{c}(X))
where
f(t)(x):=\hat{f}(t,\theta_{t^{-1}}(x))
extends to an isometric isomorphism

(7.12)

F^{S_{\theta}}(\mathcal{G}_{\theta},\mathcal{L}_{u})\cong F(\alpha,u).

Thus for any class
\mathcal{R}
of representations of
F(\alpha,u)
there is a class
\mathcal{R}_{0}
of representations of
F^{S_{\theta}}(\mathcal{G}_{\theta},\mathcal{L}_{u})
such that (
7.12
) induces an isometric isomorphism
F^{S_{\theta}}_{\mathcal{R}_{0}}(\mathcal{G}_{\theta},\mathcal{L}_{u})\cong F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)
.

Lemma 7.3
.

For any Banach algebra of the form
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\|f(1)\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{R}}
for all
f\in F(\alpha,u)
, and so
F(\alpha,u)\ni f\mapsto f(1)\in C_{0}(X)
extends to a contractive map
E^{\mathcal{R}}:F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)\to C_{0}(X)
;

(2)

\max_{t\in G}\|f(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{R}}
for all
f\in F(\alpha,u)
, and so for any
t\in G
,
F(\alpha,u)\ni f\mapsto f(t)\in C_{0}(X_{t})
extends to a contractive map
E^{\mathcal{R}}_{t}:F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)\to C_{0}(X_{t})
;

(3)

the inclusion
F(\alpha,u)\subseteq C_{0}(\mathcal{G}_{\theta})
extends to a contractive linear map
j_{\mathcal{R}}:F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)\to C_{0}(\mathcal{G}_{\theta})
.

If the above equivalent conditions hold, then
\bigcap_{t\in G}\ker(E_{t}^{\mathcal{R}})=\ker j_{\mathcal{R}}
is an ideal in
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)
(and
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)
is necessarily a completion of
F(\alpha,u)
).

Proof.

Using the groupoid model from Example
7.2
, the assertion follows from Proposition
3.6
, see also Remark
3.8
.
∎

Definition 7.4
.

If the equivalent conditions in Lemma
7.3
hold, then we call
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)
a
Banach algebra crossed product
for
(\alpha,u)
.
If in addition
\ker j_{\mathcal{R}}=\bigcap_{t\in G}\ker E_{t}^{\mathcal{R}}=\{0\}
, we say that

is a
reduced Banach algebra crossed product
.

For each
p\in[1,+\infty]
, the representation (
2.2
) composed with the isomorphism (
7.12
) gives a representation
\Lambda_{p}:F(\alpha,u)\to B(\ell^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{\theta}))
, where

\Lambda_{p}(b)\xi(t,x):=\sum_{s\in G\text{ if }x\in X_{s}}b(s)(x)u(s,s^{-1}t)(x)\xi\big(s^{-1}t,\theta_{s^{-1}}(x)\big),\qquad\xi\in\ell^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{\theta}),\ b\in F(\alpha,u).

Definition 7.5
.

For any non-empty
P\subseteq[1,+\infty]
, we call
F^{P}_{\red}(\alpha,u):=F_{\{\Lambda_{p}:p\in P\}}(\alpha,u)
the
reduced

-crossed product
.
We define the
full
L^{P}
-crossed product

as
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha,u)
where
\mathcal{R}
is the class of all representations
\psi
of
F(\alpha,u)
on
L^{p}
-spaces

, for

, and if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
we additionally assume that

We let

be the canonical representation.

By (

) and Proposition

, the algebras

are reduced crossed products isometrically isomorphic to

.
The above definitions agree with those introduced in
[

]
for full actions and

.

Definition 7.6

(
[
ELQ02
]
)
.

A partial action
\theta
is

if
\{x\in X_{t^{-1}}:\theta_{t}(x)=x\}
has empty interior for every

.
A set

Remark 7.7
.

It is obvious that a partial action
\theta
is minimal if and only if the associated transformation groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
is minimal.
It is also easy to see from Remark

that the partial action
\theta
is topologically free if and only if
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
is topologically free.

If the twist
u
is trivial, then we omit writing it.
For a subgroup

we denote the obvious restriction of the twisted action
(\alpha,u)
of

to a twisted action of

.

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
(\alpha,u)
be a twisted partial action of a discrete group
G
on the algebra
C_{0}(X)
, with dual partial action
\theta
of
G
on
X
.
Let
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
be a non-empty set and let

be any reduced crossed product Banach algebra.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

\theta
is topologically free;

(2)

C_{0}(X)
is a maximal abelian subalgebra in

;

(3)

C_{0}(X)
detects ideals in all

(4)

detects ideals in all

(5)

(6)

,

,

.

detects ideals in

and so

Proof.

Extending isomorphism (

), we may view a reduced crossed product Banach algebra

of

as an

-graded reduced groupoid Banach algebra of

.
As noted in Remark

, topological freeness and minimality of

are equivalent to topological freeness and minimality of

. The groupoid

is Hausdorff. Hence the assertion follows from Proposition

and Theorems

,
5.13
,
5.16
, cf. also Theorem

and Corollary

.
Formally, to get from

to

∎

Moving towards pure infiniteness, we generalize the definitions of

Definition 7.9
.

For

Remark 7.10

For full actions,

In general, the

Definition 7.11
.

Remark 7.12

If

is locally contracting (in fact the latter notion was inspired by the former, see

]

We arrive at the following generalization of the classical results of

]
,
[

, Example 2.1]
, to the twisted, partial action and Banach algebra case.

Theorem 7.13
.

Let
(\alpha,u)
be a twisted partial action of a discrete group

on

.
If the dual action
\theta

-filling or a local boundary action, then every reduced twisted crossed product Banach algebra

is purely infinite simple.

Proof.

Extending isomorphism (

), we may view

as a reduced groupoid Banach algebra of

.
Since

is Hausdorff, this is also an essential groupoid Banach algebra, and
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
is topologically free and minimal.
Clearly, if
\theta
is
n
-filling then
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
is
n
-filling with respect to
S_{\theta}
, and if
\theta
is a local boundary action then
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
is locally contracting with respect to
S_{\theta}
.
The assertion therefore follows from Theorem
6.7
and Theorem
6.13
.
∎

Remark 7.14

7.2.
Banach Roe-type operator algebras

In this subsection, we associate to any uniformly locally finite coarse structure
(X,\E)
a family of Banach algebras that cover Roe-type Banach algebras with compact coefficients considered by Špakula and Willett
[
SW17
]
, as well as
\ell^{p}
Roe-type algebras of Chung and Li
[
CL18
]
.
We prove that they can be naturally viewed as a reduced Banach algebra of a principal étale groupoid, so parts of our theory apply.

Let
\E
be a (unital)
coarse structure
on a set
X
in the sense of
[
STY02
]
, that is,
\E
is a collection of subsets of the pair groupoid
X\times X
which contains all finite subsets, and is closed under inverses, products, unions and formation of subsets.
Elements of
\E
are called
entourages
.
The coarse space
(X,\E)
is called
unital
if
\E
contains the diagonal
\{(x,x):x\in X\}
.
We will assume that
(X,\E)
is
uniformly locally finite
, which means that for any
E\in\E
the range and domain of
E
is finite, that is, the following number

n(E):=\sup_{x\in X}|\{y\in X:(x,y)\in E\text{ or }(y,x)\in E\}|=\sup_{x\in X}|(r^{-1}(x)\cup d^{-1}(x))\cap E|

is finite.
As explained in
[
STY02
, page 810]
, up to coarse equivalence, coarse structures coming from metric spaces are exactly countably generated unital coarse structures.
In this case uniform local finiteness corresponds to what is called
bounded geometry
for metric spaces.
In this sense, our setting covers those in
[
SW17
]
or
[
CL18
]
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Banach space with a fixed normalized (not necessarily countable) Schauder basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
For every finite
I_{0}\subseteq I
, we denote by
M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E})\cong M_{|I_{0}|}(\mathbb{F})
the set of operators on the finite dimensional space
\spane\{e_{i}:i\in I_{0}\}
.
We view
M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
as a subaglebra of
\BB(\mathcal{E})
and we put
M_{I}(\mathcal{E}):=\bigcup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I_{0}\subseteq I}}{{\text{finite}}}}M_{I_{0}}\subseteq\BB(\mathcal{E})
.
Up to an equivalence of the norm in
\mathcal{E}
, we may and shall assume that the unit
1_{I_{0}}
in
M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
has norm one as an element of
\BB(\mathcal{E})
, cf.
[
Daw06
]
.
We will refer to such a Schauder basis as
standardized
.

Definition 7.15
.

A
controlled propagation matrix
on
(X,\E)
with coefficients in
\BB(\mathcal{E})
is an
X
-by-
X
matrix
T=\{T_{x,y}\}_{x,y\in X}\subseteq\BB(\mathcal{E})
such that:

(1)

T_{x,y}\subseteq M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
for some finite
I_{0}\subseteq I
and all
x,y\in X
;

(2)

the norms
\|T_{x,y}\|
,
x,y\in X
are uniformly bounded;

(3)

the support
\supp(T):=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:T_{x,y}\neq 0\}
is an entourage (
\supp(T)\in\E
).

The collection of all such controlled propagation matrices is denoted by
\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
.

Remark 7.16
.

The above definition differs from
[
SW17
, Definition 2.2]
in that we impose the restriction
(1)
.
Obviously,
(1)
is automatic when
\mathcal{E}
is finite dimensional.

Example 7.17

(cf.
[
SW17
, Example 2.3]
)
.

Fix a finite
I_{0}\subseteq I
and an entourage
E\in\E
.
Bounded maps from
X
to
M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
supported on
r(E):=\{x:(x,y)\in E\}
, that is elements of
\ell^{\infty}(r(E),M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E}))
, when identified with diagonal matrices, are elements of
\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
, referred to as
multiplication operators
.
If in addition the entourage
E
is a bisection in
X\times X
, the characteristic function of
E
multiplied by the unit
1_{I_{0}}
in
M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
is an element of
\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
, referred to as a
partial translation
.
Namely, for a bisection entourage
E
and any
f\in\ell^{\infty}(r(E),M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E}))
, the formulas

f_{x,y}=\begin{cases}f(x),&x=y\in r(E),\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad E^{I_{0}}_{x,y}=\begin{cases}1_{I_{0}},&(x,y)\in E,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

define basic elements of
\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
.

The uniform local finiteness of
(X,\E)
guarantees that
\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
becomes an algebra under the usual matrix operations and the algebra structure of
\BB(\mathcal{E})
.
Moreover, for every
p\in[1,\infty]
,
\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
can be naturally represented on the space
\ell^{p}(X,\mathcal{E})
of
\mathcal{E}
valued functions, with the usual norm
\|\xi\|_{p}=(\sum_{x\in X}\|\xi(x)\|^{p})^{1/p}
.
More specifically, we have:

Lemma 7.18
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
and put
N:=n(\supp(T))^{2}+1<\infty
.
Then there are pairwise disjoint bisection entourages
E_{1},\ldots,E_{N}\subseteq\supp(T)
and functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\in\ell^{\infty}(X,M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E}))
, for some finite
I_{0}\subseteq I
, with
\supp_{x\in X,k=1,\ldots,N}\|f_{k}(x)\|\leq\sup_{x,y}\|T_{x,y}\|
and such that

T=\sum_{k=1}^{N}f_{k}E_{k}^{I_{0}},

where we use the usual matrix operations.
Thus
T
is a finite combination of partial translations with coefficients in multiplication operators.

Proof.

Let
I_{0}\subseteq I
be a finite set such that
T_{x,y}\subseteq M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
all
x,y\in X
.
By
[
Roe03
, Lemma 4.10]
, see also
[
STY02
, Lemma 2.7]
, there are pairwise disjoint bisection entourages
E_{1},\ldots,E_{N}
such that
\supp(T)=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{N}E_{i}
.
For each
i=1,\ldots,n
define
f_{i}\in\ell^{\infty}(r(E_{i}),M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E}))
by
f_{i}(x):=T_{x,y}
if
(x,y)\in E
for some
y\in X
and
f_{i}(x)=0
otherwise.
Then the displayed formula holds.
∎

Proposition 7.19
.

The usual matrix operations and the algebra structure of
\BB(\mathcal{E})
turn
\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
into an algebra.
Moreover, for every
p\in[1,+\infty]
, a matrix
T\in\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
defines a bounded operator
T_{p}\in\BB(\ell^{p}(X,\mathcal{E}))
via

(T_{p}\xi)(x):=\sum_{y\in X}T_{x,y}\xi(y),\qquad\xi\in\ell^{p}(X,\mathcal{E}).

Then
\|T_{p}\|\leq\sup_{x,y}\|T_{x,y}\|\cdot n(\supp(T))^{2}+1
.
The assignment
\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]\ni T\mapsto T_{p}\in\BB(\ell^{p}(X,\mathcal{E}))
is an injective algebra homomorphism.

Proof.

Let
E\in\E
be a bisection entourage and let
f\in\ell^{\infty}(r(E),M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E}))
, for a finite
I_{0}\subseteq I
.
The norm of
(fE^{I_{0}})_{p}
, as an operator on
\ell^{p}(X,\mathcal{E})
, is not greater than
\sup_{x\in X}\|f(x)\|
due to our standing normalization assumption that
1_{I_{0}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
has norm one, which implies that
(E^{I_{0}})_{p}
has norm one.
Thus, applying Lemma
7.18
to
T\in\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
, we get that
T_{p}\in\BB(\ell^{p}(X,\mathcal{E}))
is bounded with the prescribed estimate on the norm.

It is clear that the map
\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]\ni T\mapsto T_{p}\in\BB(\ell^{p}(X,\mathcal{E}))
is linear and injective.
To see that it is multiplicative, in view of Lemma
7.18
, it suffices to consider only two elements of the form
f_{1}E_{1}^{I_{1}},f_{2}E_{2}^{I_{2}}\in\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
where
f_{1}\in\ell^{\infty}(X,M_{I_{1}}(\mathcal{E}))
,
f_{2}\in\ell^{\infty}(X,M_{I_{2}}(\mathcal{E}))
,
I_{1},I_{2}\subseteq I
are finite, and
E_{1},E_{2}\in\E
are bisections.
Then we have

(7.13)

(f_{1}E_{1}^{I_{1}})(f_{2}E_{2}^{I_{2}})=f_{1}(f_{2}\circ h_{E_{1}})(E_{1}E_{2})^{I_{1}\cap I_{2}},

where
f_{2}\circ h_{E_{1}}\in\ell^{\infty}(d(E_{1}),M_{I_{2}}(\mathcal{E}))
is given by
f_{2}\circ h_{E_{1}}(y):=f_{2}(x)
for
(x,y)\in E_{1}
.
This readily implies that
(f_{1}E_{1}^{I_{1}})_{p}(f_{2}E_{2}^{I_{2}})_{p}=(f_{1}E_{1}^{I_{1}}f_{2}E_{2}^{I_{2}})_{p}
.
∎

Definition 7.20
.

For any non-empty
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
we define the
L^{P}
-
\mathcal{E}
Roe-type Banach algebra
of
(X,\E)
as the completion
F^{P}[X;\mathcal{E}]=\overline{\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]}^{\|\cdot\|_{L^{P},\mathcal{E}}}
in the norm
\|T\|_{L^{P},\mathcal{E}}:=\sup_{p\in P}\|T_{p}\|
.
When
P=\{p\}
is a singleton, we omit writing braces in the above notation.

The
translation groupoid
associated to the coarse space
(X,\E)
is defined as the union of closures of entourages in the Stone–Čech compactifaction of the discrete space
X\times X
, see
[
STY02
, 3.2]
,
[
Roe03
, Definition 10.19]
.
That is, as a topological space, we put

\mathcal{G}(X):=\bigcup_{E\in\E}\overline{E}\subseteq\beta(X\times X).

The range and domain maps
r,d:X\times X\to X
extend uniquely to continuous maps
r,d:\beta(X\times X)\supseteq\mathcal{G}(X)\to\beta(X)
and with this structure
\mathcal{G}(X)
becomes an étale Hausdorff ample principal groupoid.
Also the bisection entourages
S^{X}:=\{E\in\E:\text{$E$ is a bisection in $X\times X$}\}
form an inverse semigroup whose canonical action by partial bijections on
X
induces an action by partial homeomorphisms of
\beta(X)
, and we have a natural groupoid isomorphism
\mathcal{G}(X)\cong\beta(X)\rtimes S^{X}
, see
[
STY02
, Proposition 3.2]
.

An
ideal in the coarse space

(X,\E)
is a coarse space
(X,\E_{0})
where
\E_{0}\subseteq\E
is such that
E_{0}E,EE_{0}\in\E_{0}
for all
E\in\E
and
E_{0}\in\E_{0}
, cf.
[
CWT04
, Definition 4.1]
.
We will say that
(X,\E)
is
simple
if there are no non-trivial ideals in
(X,\E)
.

Theorem 7.21
.

Let
(X,\E)
be a uniformly locally finite coarse structure.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Banach space with a standardized Schauder basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, and
let
\mathcal{G}:=\mathcal{G}(X)\times\mathcal{R}_{I}
where
\mathcal{R}_{I}:=I\times I
is the full equivalence relation on
I
.
Then there is a canonical algebraic isomorphism

C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\cong\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}],

under which for any non-empty
P\subseteq[1,\infty)
(or
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
when
\mathcal{E}
is reflexive), the Roe-type algebra

becomes a reduced
S
-graded groupoid Banach algebra of
\mathcal{G}
where
S
consists of
\overline{E}\times\{(i,j)\}
,
E\in S^{X}
,
i,j\in I
, and the unit space
\beta(X)
.

In particular, the diagonal
C_{0}(\beta(X)\times I)
is a maximal abelian subalgebra,
C_{0}(\beta(X)\times I)\subseteq F^{P}[X;\mathcal{E}]
has the intersection property, and
F^{P}[X;\mathcal{E}]
is simple if and only if the coarse space
(X,\E)
is simple.

Proof.

Clearly,
S
described in the assertion is a wide unital inverse subsemigroup of
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
.
In particular, every element in
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
is a finite linear combination of continuous functions with supports in elements in
S
.
Moreover, for every
E\in S^{X}
and finite
I_{0}\subseteq I
we have a linear isomorphism
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\cong\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
,

C(\bigcup_{i,j\in I_{0}}\overline{E}\times\{(i,j)\})\ni\overline{f}\longmapsto f\in\ell^{\infty}(X,M_{I_{0}}(\mathcal{E}))

where
f(x)_{i,j}:=\overline{f}(x,y,i,j)
if
(x,y)\in E
for some
y\in X
, and
f(x)=0
otherwise.
Using this and Lemma
7.18
, one sees that the maps

C\big(\bigcup_{i,j\in I_{0}}\overline{E}\times\{(i,j)\}\big)\ni\overline{f}\longmapsto fE^{I_{0}}\in\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}],\qquad E\in S^{X},

integrate to a linear isomorphism
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\cong\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
.
In view of (
7.13
), it readily follows that this isomorphism is multiplicative.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
.
Composing
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\cong\mathbb{F}[X;\mathcal{E}]
with the monomorphism from Proposition

, we get a monomorphism
\Lambda_{p}:C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\to\BB(\ell^{p}(X,\mathcal{E}))
such that

[\Lambda_{p}(f)\xi](x)=\sum_{y\in X}f(x,y,i,j)e_{i}\otimes e_{j}^{*}(\xi(y)),\qquad f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G}),\xi\in\ell^{p}(X,\mathcal{E}),

where

is the contractive one-dimensional projection given by
(e_{i}\otimes e_{j}^{*})(\sum_{k\in I}\xi_{k}e_{k})=\xi_{j}e_{i}
.
For
f
supported on
\overline{E}\times\{(i,j)\}\in S
, we clearly have
\|\Lambda_{p}(f)\|=\|f\|_{\infty}
, and so
\Lambda_{p}
extends to a representation
\Lambda_{p}:F^{S}(\mathcal{G})\to F^{p}[X;\mathcal{E}]
.
For
(y,j)\in X\times I
, we let
1_{(y,j)}\in\ell^{p}(X,\mathcal{E})
be such that
1_{(y,j)}(y):=e_{j}
and
1_{(y,j)}(x):=0
for

.
Then putting

(7.14)

j(T)(x,y,i,j):=e_{i}^{*}\big(T1_{(y,j)}\big),\qquad T\in F^{P}[X;\mathcal{E}],\ (x,y,i,j)\in\mathcal{G}_{0}:=\bigcup_{E\in\E}E\times\mathcal{R}_{I},

we get a contractive linear map

from
F^{P}[X;\mathcal{E}]
to
C_{b}(\mathcal{G}_{0})
, equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.
For

, we have
j(\Lambda_{p}(f))=f|_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}
which has a unique continuous extension to
\mathcal{G}
(which is

) and therefore it may be treated as an element of
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
.
Thus we get that on a dense subalgebra

takes values in
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
and therefore we may assume that

maps

to
C_{0}(\mathcal{G})
.
In particular,

is a groupoid Banach algebra.
If

∎

Remark 7.22

7.3.
Tight algebras of inverse semigroups

In this subsection, we define reduced and essential tight
L^{P}
-operator algebras associated to inverse semigroups, and phrase our main results in terms of the inverse semigroup.
This extends, and sometimes improves, the corresponding results for
C^{*}
-algebras.

We fix an inverse semigroup
S
with zero
0
. We recall our notation from
[
BKM25
, Section 6]
for the tight groupoid associated to
S

in
[
Exe08
]
.
The set of idempotents
E:=E(S)
is a semilattice with the ‘meet’ and ‘preorder’ defined by
e\wedge f:=ef
and
e\leq f
if and only if
ef=e
.
The set of filters
\widehat{E}
in
E
, with the product topology inherited from the Cantor space
\{0,1\}^{E}
, is a totally disconnected Hausdorff space.
A
cover of
e\in E
is a finite set
F\subseteq E
such that, for every non-zero
z\leq e
, we have
zf\neq 0
for some
f\in F
.
A filter
\phi\in\hat{E}
is
tight
if for every
e\in\phi
and any cover
F\subseteq eE
of
e
, we have
\phi\cap F\neq\emptyset
.
The set of tight filters
\widehat{E}_{\tight}
is called the
tight spectrum
of
E
.
It is the closure in
\hat{E}
of the set of all ultrafilters (if
E
is a Boolean algebra, then the tight filters are precisely the ultrafilters).
For each
e\in E
, the set
Z_{e}:=\{\phi\in\widehat{E}_{\tight}:e\in\phi\}
is compact open in
\widehat{E}_{\tight}
.
For each
t\in S
, we have a homeomorphism
h_{t}:Z_{t^{*}t}\to Z_{tt^{*}}
given by

h_{t}(\phi):=\{e\in E:t^{*}et\in\phi\}=\{e\in E:\text{$f\geq tft^{*}$ for some $f\in\phi$}\}.

This yields an inverse semigroup action
h:S\to\PHomeo(\widehat{E}_{{\tight}})
and we can form the transformation groupoid

\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S):=S\ltimes_{h}\widehat{E}_{{\tight}}.

Let
u=\{u(s,t)\}_{s,t\in S}
be a twist of the action
h:S\to\PHomeo(\widehat{E}_{{\tight}})
described above.
That is,
u(s,t)\in C_{u}(Z_{t^{*}s^{*}st})
,
t,s\in S
, are maps satisfying the conditions in
[
BE11
, Definition 4.1]
, or in
[
BKM25
, Definition 3.4]
. Also let
\mathcal{L}_{u}
be the twist associated to
u
, as in
[
BKM25
, Subsection 4.3]
.
Then
(\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S),\mathcal{L}_{u})
is an ample twisted groupoid, and every ample twisted groupoid is of this form, see Remark
7.25
below.

We will now represent the twisted inverse semigroup
(S,u)
via spatial partial isometries on the spaces
\ell^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S))
,
p\in[1,\infty]
.
We recall that for any localizable measure
\mu
, in particular for a counting measure, we have a natural inverse semigroup
\SPIso(L^{p}(\mu))\subseteq B(L^{p}(\mu))
of contractive weighted composition operators called
spatial partial isometries
on
L^{p}(\mu)
, see
[
BKM25
, Definition 2.26]
,
[
Phi12
, Definition 6.4]
,
[
Gar21
, Definition 6.2]
.

Proposition 7.23
.

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup with zero and let
u=\{u(s,t)\}_{s,t\in S}
be a twist of the associated action
h:S\to\PHomeo(\widehat{E}_{{\tight}})
.
For each
p\in[1,\infty]
, we have a map
v:S\to\SPIso\big(\ell^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S))\big)
into spatial isometries on
\ell^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S))
given by

v_{t}\xi[s,\phi]=\begin{cases}u(t,t^{*}s)\big(h_{s}(\phi)\big)\xi\big([t^{*}s,\phi]\big),&\phi\in h_{s}^{-1}(Z_{tt^{*}}),\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\xi\in\ell^{p}(\mathcal{G})
,
\phi\in Z_{s^{*}s}
,
t,s\in S
.
Denoting by
v_{t}^{*}
the unique generalized inverse of
v_{t}
in
\SPIso\big(\ell^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S))\big)
, we get

F^{p}_{\red,{\tight}}(S,u):=\clsp\{v_{t}v_{s}^{*}:t,s\in S\}

is a Banach algebra naturally isometrically isomorphic to the twisted reduced groupoid algebra
F_{\red}^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S),\mathcal{L}_{u})
via an isomorphism that restricts to

Lemma 7.30
.

An idempotent

is fixed by

if and only if

.

Proposition 7.31
.

Let

be an inverse semigroup with zero.
Then
S
is closed (resp. topologically free or minimal) if and only if
\mathcal{G}_{\tight}(S)
is Hausdorff (resp. topologically free or minimal).
If

is locally contractive, then
\mathcal{G}_{\tight}(S)
is.

The claims about

being closed, minimal, and locally contractive follow by
[

, Theorems 3.16, 5.5 and 6.5]
.
For the claim about topological freeness, recall that topological freeness of

is equivalent to topological freeness of

in the sense of

[

, Definition 2.20]
, by

This means that for every

has empty interior.
By

and density of ultrafilters in

contains a set of the form

, for some

∎

7.4.
Graph algebras

Let
Q=(Q^{0},Q^{1},r,s)
be a directed graph.
So
Q^{0}
,
Q^{1}
are countable sets whose elements are interpreted as vertices and edges respectively, and
r,s:Q^{1}\to Q^{0}
are the range and source maps.
For the standard inverse semigroup
S_{Q}
associated to
Q
, the associated tight groupoid is the standard Deaconu–Renault groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
for the one sided shift on the boundary path space of
Q
, see, for instance,
[
BKM25
, Subsection 6.1]
.
The groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
is amenable, Hausdorff and second countable.
For any
p\in[1,\infty]
, by Proposition
7.23
,
[
BKM25
, Corollary 6.25]
and
[
GL17
, Theorem 6.19]
, the algebra
F^{p}_{\red,{\tight}}(S_{Q})
is naturally isometrically isomorphic to the graph
L^{p}
-operator algebra
F^{p}(Q)
considered in
[
BKM25
, Definition 6.22]
,
[
CR19
]
,
[
CMR23
]
,
[
Phi12
]
.
Therefore, for every non-empty
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
, we call

F^{P}(Q):=F^{P}_{\red,{\tight}}(S_{Q})\cong F^{P}(\mathcal{G}_{Q})=F^{P}_{\red}(\mathcal{G}_{Q})

the
graph
L^{P}
-operator algebra
of
Q
.

A
path
in
Q
is a sequence
\mu=\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots
(finite or infinite) where
s(\mu_{i})=r(\mu_{i+1})
for all
i
.
We also treat vertices in
Q^{0}
as paths of length zero.
A finite path
\mu=\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{n}
in
Q
such that
s(\mu_{n})=r(\mu_{1})
is called a
cycle
.
A vertex
x\in Q^{0}
is called
singular
if it is a source or an infinite receiver, that is, if
|r^{-1}(v)|\in\{0,\infty\}
.
A path in
Q
is called
boundary
if either it is infinite or if it starts in a singular vertex.
The set of boundary paths
\partial Q
is naturally equipped with the topology that makes it homemomorphic to the tight spectrum of
S_{Q}
and it is the unit space of
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
. In particular, for any vertex
v\in Q^{0}
, the cylinder

Z_{v}:=\{\mu\in\partial Q:r(\mu)=b\}
is compact open in
\partial Q
and so its indicator
1_{Z_{v}}
is a projection in
C_{0}(\partial Q)\subseteq F^{P}(Q)
.
For two vertices
v,w\in Q^{0}
, we write
v\leq w
if there is a path
\mu
in
Q
that ends in
v
and starts in
w
.

Definition 7.35
.

We say that the graph
Q
is
cofinal
if every boundary path
\mu=\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots
in
Q
is cofinal in
(Q^{0},\leq)
in the sense that for every
v\in Q^{0}
there is
i
such that
v\leq s(\mu_{i})
.
We say that a cycle
\mu=\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{n}
has an
entry
if for some
i=1,\ldots,n
there is an edge
e\in Q^{1}
such that
r(e)=r(\mu_{i})
but
e\neq\mu_{i}
.

The main results of
[
CMR23
]
and
[
GT25
]
, proved using more direct methods, can be generalized using the groupoid methods as follows.

Theorem 7.36
.

Let
Q=(Q^{0},Q^{1},r,s)
be a directed graph and let
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
be non-empty.

(1)

If every cycle in
Q
has an entry, then every representation of
F^{P}(\mathcal{G})
(into any Banach algebra) is injective whenever it is non-zero on projections
1_{Z_{v}}\in C_{0}(\partial Q)
,
v\in Q^{0}
.

(2)

The graph
L^{P}
-operator algebra
F^{P}(Q)
is simple if and only if
Q
is cofinal and every cycle in
Q
has an entry.

(3)

If
F^{P}(Q)
is simple, then either it is purely infinite, which holds when
Q
contains a cycle, or it is approximately finite in the sense that it is the inductive limit of finite direct sums of full finite dimensional matrix algebras over
\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

We apply Corollary
C
from the introduction to
F^{P}(Q)\cong F^{P}(\mathcal{G}_{Q})
. It is well known, see, for instance,
[
NO19
, Propositions 8.2, 8.3]
, that
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
is topologically free if and only if every cycle in
Q
has an entry, and
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
is minimal if and only if
Q
is cofinal.
Thus
(2)
in the assertion follows from Corollary
C
(2)
.
Assume now that every cycle in
Q
has an entry.
By Corollary
C
(1)
, any representation
\pi:F^{P}(Q)\to B
is injective whenever its restriction to
C_{0}(\partial Q)
is injective.
But
\pi
is injective on
C_{0}(\partial Q)
if it is non-zero on projections
1_{Z_{v}}\in C_{0}(\partial Q)
,
v\in Q^{0}
.
This can be proved using that
\pi
is non-zero on projections spanning finite-dimensional algebras turning
C_{0}(\partial Q)
into an AF-algebra, cf. the proof of
[
KPR98
, Theorem 3.7]
, or by applying the criterion in the last part of
[
BKM25
, Theorem 6.15]
to
S_{Q}
.
This shows
(1)
.
Assume now
Q
is cofinal and that every cycle in
Q
has an entry.
It is well known and easy to see that then
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
is locally contracting if and only if
Q
contains a cycle.
If this holds, then
F^{P}(Q)
is purely infinite by Theorem
D
.
If
Q
has no cycles, then
F^{P}(Q)
is an inductive limit of finite-dimensional graph algebras,
which are
\mathbb{F}
-matricial algebras, see
[
AAM17
, Proposition 2.6.20]
.
This proves
(3)
.
∎

7.5.
Algebras associated to self-similar group actions on graphs

In this subsection, we use our theory to extend a number of non-trivial, recent results for
C^{*}
-algebras associated to self-similar group actions on graphs, as defined by Exel and Pardo
[
EP17
]
, to the context of
L^{P}
-operator algebras.

We fix a self-similar group action on a directed graph as defined in
[
EP17
]
.
Thus we let
Q=(Q^{0},Q^{1},r,s)
be a
finite directed graph without sources
.
Hence
Q^{0}
,
Q^{1}
are finite, and
r^{-1}(v)\neq\emptyset
for all
v\in Q^{0}
.
We also fix a countable group
G
.
An
automorphism of
Q
is a bijective map
\sigma:Q^{0}\sqcup Q^{1}\to Q^{0}\sqcup Q^{1}
such that
\sigma(Q^{0})=Q^{0}
,
\sigma(Q^{1})=Q^{1}
, and
r\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ r
and
s\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ s
.
A
self-similar action
of
G
on
Q
is a group homomorphism
\sigma
from
G
into the group of automorphisms of
Q
and a map
\varphi:G\times Q^{1}\to G
, called a one-cocycle or a
restriction map
, whose values are sometimes denoted by
g|_{e}:=\varphi(g,e)
, such that

gh|_{e}=g|_{\sigma_{h}(e)}h|_{e},\qquad\sigma_{g|_{e}}(s(e))=\sigma_{g}\big(s(e)\big)\qquad\text{ for all $g,h\in G,\ e\in E^{1}$.}

Both the
G
-action and the restriction maps extend uniquely to paths in
Q
, in such a way that
\sigma_{g}(e\mu)=\sigma_{g}(e)\sigma_{g|_{e}}(\mu)
and
g|_{e\mu}=(g|_{e})|_{\mu}
for all
g\in G
,
e\in Q^{1}
and
\mu\in Q^{*}
with
s(e)=r(\mu)
, see
[
EP17
, Proposition 2.4]
.
There is a natural inverse semigroup associated to these data,
[
EP17
, Sections 4, 8]
.
Namely, the set

S_{G,Q}:=\big\{(\alpha,g,\beta)\in Q^{*}\times G\times Q^{*}:s(\alpha)=\sigma_{g}\big(s(\beta)\big)\big\}\cup\{0\}

with the semigroup law defined by

(\alpha,g,\beta)(\gamma,h,\delta):=\begin{cases}\big(\alpha\sigma_{g}(\epsilon),g|_{\epsilon}h,\delta\big)&\text{if $\gamma=\beta\epsilon$},\\
\big(\alpha,g(h^{-1}|_{\epsilon})^{-1},\delta\sigma_{h^{-1}}(\epsilon)\big)&\text{if $\beta=\epsilon\gamma$},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and
(\alpha,g,\beta)^{*}:=(\beta,g^{-1},\alpha)
, is an inverse semigroup with zero
[
EP17
, Proposition 4.3]
.
We use it to define
L^{P}
-analogues of Exel–Pardo

-algebras:

Definition 7.37
.

For non-empty
P\subseteq[1,\infty]

7.27
, we define the
full
,

and
essential

-operator algebra of the self-similar action

respectively.

When the group

discussed in the previous subsection.

is equivalent to
\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S_{G,Q})
being effective, see
[
EP17
, Theorem 14.10]
and
[
EP16
, Theorem 4.7]
.
Also, under our assumptions,
\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S_{G,Q})
is locally contracting, see
[
EP17
, Theorem 15.1]
.
Thus since
F^{P}_{\ess}(G,Q)
is an essential Banach algebra for
\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S_{G,Q})
, cf. Corollary
7.26
,
F^{P}_{\ess}(G,Q)
is simple purely infinite by Theorem
6.13
.
Every
g\in G
admits at most finitely many minimal strongly fixed paths if and only if
\mathcal{G}_{{\tight}}(S_{G,Q})
is Hausdorff, see
[
EP17
, Theorem 12.2]
.
Thus then
F^{P}_{\ess}(G,Q)=F^{P}_{\red}(G,Q)
.
If
[
CEPSS19
, (5.9)]
holds, then
S_{G,Q}
satisfies condition (S) mentioned in Remark
7.34
, and so then
F^{P}_{\red}(G,Q)
is an essential Banach algebra.
∎

Example 7.43

(Katsura algebras)
.

Let
A
and
B
be two
N\times N
matrices with integer entries.
Katsura
[
Kat08
]
associated to them a
C^{*}
-algebra, and Exel–Pardo noticed in
[
EP17
, Example 3.4]
that there is a natural self-similar action
(\mathbb{Z},Q_{A})
associated to
A
and
B
that yield the same algebra.
Thus, for any non-empty
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
, one could view
F^{P}(\mathbb{Z},Q_{A})=F^{P}_{\red}(\mathbb{Z},Q_{A})
as
L^{P}
-versions of Katsura algebras.
In particular, if the underlying groupoid is Hausdorff, which is characterized in terms of
A
and
B
in
[
EP17
, Theorem 18.6]
, then
F^{P}(\mathbb{Z},Q_{A})
is simple (and purely infinite) if and only if the matrix
A
is irreducible, see
[
EP17
, Theorem 18.12]
.
An example of
A
and
B
such that the underlying groupoid is non-Hausdorff, but satisfies
[
CEPSS19
, (5.9)]
, is analyzed in
[
CEPSS19
, Subsection 5.4]
.
Thus Theorem
7.42
applied to this example gives that
F^{P}(\mathbb{Z},Q_{A})
is simple and purely infinite.

Example 7.44

(Self-similar groups)
.

Let
X
be a set with at least two elements.
Self-similar groups
(G,X)
, cf.
[
Nek04
]
,
[
Nek09
]
, are equivalent to self-similar group actions
(G,Q_{X})
on the graph
Q_{X}
that has only one vertex, the set of edges
E^{1}=X
, and such that the extended action of
G
on the set of finite paths
X^{*}
is faithful.
Then the associated groupoid is necessarily minimal and topologically free, cf.
[
EP17
, Corollary 14.14]
, and so the first part of Theorem
7.42
applies.
Thus, for any self-similar group
(G,X)
and any non-empty
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
, the associated essential algebra
F^{P}_{\ess}(G,X):=F^{P}_{\ess}(G,Q_{X})
is always purely infinite and simple.
If
(G,X)
is
\omega
-faithful in the sense of
[
CEPSS19
, Definition 5.20]
, then also
[
CEPSS19
, (5.9)]
holds, and so
F^{P}_{\red}(G,X):=F^{P}_{\red}(G,Q_{X})
is also simple purely infinite in this case.
The second part of Theorem
7.42
does not apply to the self-similar action
(G,X)
modeling the
Grigorchuk group
.
Nevertheless, the proof of
[
CEPSS19
, Theorem 5.22]
shows that
F^{P}(G,X):=F^{P}(G,Q_{X})
is simple purely infinite in this case as well.

Introduction

The concept of Freiman ideals appeared the first time in
[
15
]
. Based on a famous theorem of Freiman
[
8
]
, it was shown in
[
13
]
that if
I
is an
equigenerated monomial ideal, i.e., all its
generators are of the same degree, then
\mu(I^{2})\geq\ell(I)\mu(I)-{\ell(I)\choose 2}
. Here
\mu(I)
denotes the minimal number of generators of the ideal
I
and
\ell(I)
denotes its analytic spread which by definition is the Krull dimension of the
fiber ring
F(I)=\oplus_{k\geq 0}I^{k}/{\mathfrak{m}}I^{k}
, where
{\mathfrak{m}}
denotes the graded maximal ideal of
S
.
If the equality holds, then Herzog and Zhu in
[
15
]
called the ideal
I
a Freiman ideal (or simply Freiman).
As a generalization of Freiman’s theorem, a result was proved by Böröczky et. al.

[
2
]
which in algebraic terms say that for any equigenerated monomial ideal
I
,
one has

\mu(I^{k})\geq{\ell(I)+k-2\choose k-1}\mu(I)-(k-1){\ell(I)+k-2\choose k}

for all
k\geq 1
.
It was shown in
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
that the equality holds if and only if
I
is a Freiman ideal if and only if the fiber cone
F(I)
of
I

has minimal multiplicity if and only if
F(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay and its defining ideal has a
2
-linear
resolution. This is a very restrictive condition for ideals arising from combinatorial structures, which
often guarantees strong combinatorial properties.

Many classes of Freiman ideals such as Hibi ideals, Veronese type ideals, matroid ideals, sortable ideals, principal Borel
ideals,
t
-spread principal Borel ideals, edge ideals and cut ideals of several graphs, and cover ideals of some classes of graphs such as trees, circulant graphs, whiskered graphs and simple connected unmixed bipartite graphs have been studied (see
[
6
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
).

Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
and
I\subset S
a graded ideal.
By famous theorems of Galligo
[
9
]
and Bayer-Stillman
[
1
]
, the generic initial ideal of
I
is fixed under the action of the Borel subgroups of
GL(n,K)
.
Moreover, if
\operatorname{char}(K)=0
, then this generic initial ideal is precisely a strongly stable ideal (also known as Borel ideal), see
[
10
, Proposition 4.2.4]
.

Let
\operatorname{Mon}(S)
denote the set of monomials in
S
and for any
u\in\operatorname{Mon}(S)
, we set
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{i:\ x_{i}|u\}
.
Let
k
be a positive integer. A monomial
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}\in\operatorname{Mon}(S)
is called to be
k
-bounded
, if
a_{i}\leq k
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. A monomial ideal which is generated by
k
-bounded monomials is said to be
k
-Borel
, if for any
k
-bounded monomial
u\in I
, and for any
j\in\operatorname{supp}(u)
and
i<j
, we have
x_{i}(u/x_{j})\in I
, provided
x_{i}(u/x_{j})
is again
k
-bounded. Thus
k
-Borel ideals are strongly stable ideals respecting the
k
-boundedness and
1
-Borel ideals are squarefree strongly stable ideals, and also
1
-spread Borel ideals. Other interesting restrictions of strongly stable ideals have been considered in
[
4
]
.
For
k
-bounded monomials
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
, Herzog et al. showed in
[
12
]
that there exists a unique smallest
k
-Borel ideal, denote by
B_{k}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
, which containing
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
. These monomials
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
are called to be the
k
-Borel generators
of this ideal. A
k
-Borel ideal with one
k
-Borel generator is called
principal
k
-Borel
.

For a monomial ideal
I\subset S
, the unique minimal set of monomial generators
of
I
is denoted by
G(I)
. A monomial ideal
I\subset S
is called
Borel
, if for any
u\in G(I)
and any
j\in\operatorname{supp}(u)
, we
have
x_{i}(u/x_{j})\in I
for any
i<j
. It is clear that Borel ideals are
k
-Borel for
k\gg 0
, since large
k
imposes no conditions on the exponents of the generators. Hence for any monomials
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
, there is also unique smallest Borel ideal containing
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
. Similarly, we denote this Borel ideal by
B(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
and call these monomials to be the
Borel generators
of
B(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
. A Borel ideal with one Borel generator is called
principal Borel
.

In this paper, we are interested in when some Borel type ideals, including Borel ideals with multiple Borel generators and principal
k
-Borel ideals, are Freiman.

We give a complete classification of Freiman Borel ideal of degree
2
. For the Borel ideal of high degree, we give some sufficient conditions for it to be Freiman. The reason is that checking the Freiman condition for such ideals leads to difficult numerical problems, which at this moment, we are not able to handle. Maybe there exists another approach to these problems which we are aware of at present.
Let
B_{k}(u)
be a principal
k
-Borel ideal of degree
d
. If
k\geq d
, then
B_{k}(u)=B(u)
. In this case, Herzog and the first author in
[
16
, Theorem 4]
answered when it is Freiman. If
k=1
, then
B_{k}(u)
is
a
1
-spread Borel ideal. In this case, a complete classification has been given in
[
18
]
. We provide a complete classification of Freiman principal
k
-Borel ideals of degree
d
if
k=2
or
k=d-1
.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section 1, we classify all Freiman Borel ideals of degree
2
.
In Section 2, we study Borel ideals of degree
d\geq 3
and ask which of them are Freiman. For such ideals, we only have very partial results.
In Section 3, we first show that principal
k
-Borel ideals are sortable ideals and then, appling
[
16
, Theorem 3]
, we will
give a complete classification of Freiman principal
k
-Borel ideals of degree
d
if
k=2
or
k=d-1
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
n\geq 3
is an integer and
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
.

1.
Borel ideals of degree
2

Herzog and the first author in
[
16
]
gave a complete classification of Freiman principal Borel ideal of any degree.
In this section, we classify all Freiman Borel ideals with multiple Borel generators of degree
2

Let
u\in S
be a monomial, we set
m(u)=\max\{j\;\mid\;j\in\operatorname{supp}(u)\}
. We need the following lemmas.

Lemma 1.1
.

(
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
)
Let
I=B(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a Borel ideal, where
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in S
are monomials of same degree. Then
\ell(I)=\max\{m(v)\mid v\in G(I)\}
.

Herzog and the first author in
[
16
, Theorem 4]
gave a complete characterization of Freiman principal Borel ideals. Therefore, in the succeeding sections we will assume that

I=B(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
is a Borel ideal with Borel generators
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
and
m\geq 2
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
I=B(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be an equigenerated Borel ideal of degree
2
with Borel generators
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
.
Set
u_{k}=x_{i_{k}}x_{j_{k}}
with
i_{k}\leq j_{k}
for any
1\leq k\leq m
.
Then the minimum number of Borel generators of
I
is
m
if and only if,
after relabelling of
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
if necessary, one has

[formula]

i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq j_{m}<j_{m-1}<\cdots<j_{1}.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
It is trival if
I
is a principal Borel ideal. If the minimum number of Borel generators of
I
is two, we may
suppose that
I=B(u_{1},u_{2})
, where
u_{k}=x_{i_{k}}x_{j_{k}}
with
i_{k}\leq j_{k}
for
k=1,2
. In this case, one has
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
.
Otherwise, we may assume that
j_{1}>j_{2}
since the case
j_{1}<j_{2}
can be
shown by similar arguments.
Thus
u_{2}=x_{j_{2}}(u_{1}/x_{j_{1}})
, which implies that
u_{2}\in B(u_{1})
. It follows that
B(u_{2})\subseteq B(u_{1})
. Hence
I=B(u_{1},u_{2})=\sum_{k=1}^{2}B(u_{k})=B(u_{1})
, a contradiction.

Let
i_{1}<i_{2}
. If
j_{1}\leq j_{2}
, then
x_{i_{2}}x_{j_{1}}=x_{j_{1}}(u_{2}/x_{j_{2}})\in B(u_{2})
, which forces
u_{1}=x_{i_{1}}(x_{i_{2}}x_{j_{1}}/x_{i_{2}})\in B(u_{2})
. Hence
B(u_{1})\subseteq B(u_{2})
, which implies
I=B(u_{1},u_{2})=B(u_{2})
, a contradiction.

Assume that the minimum number of Borel generators of
I
is
m\geq 3
and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
are Borel generators of
I
, where
u_{k}=x_{i_{k}}x_{j_{k}}
with
i_{k}\leq j_{k}
. By minimality of the number of Borel generators, one has

i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}
are different from each other. Let
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{m}
, then, from the above arguments, one has
j_{m}<j_{m-1}
since
i_{m-1}<i_{m}
. Step by step, one has
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq j_{m}<j_{m-1}<\cdots<j_{1}
.

(\Leftarrow)
Let
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq j_{m}<\cdots<j_{1}
, then
u_{\ell}\notin B(u_{k})
and
u_{k}\notin B(u_{\ell})

for any two different Borel generators
u_{\ell},u_{k}
of
I
. It follows that
u_{\ell}\notin\sum_{k=1,\\
k\neq\ell}^{m}B(u_{k})
, i.e.,
u_{\ell}\notin B(u_{1},\ldots,\hat{u_{\ell}},\ldots,u_{m})
, as desired.

∎

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
I=B(x_{1}x_{j_{1}},x_{2}x_{j_{2}})
be a Borel ideal, where
2\leq j_{2}<j_{1}\leq n
. Then
I
is Freiman.

Proof.

By simple calculations, one has

I=(x_{1}^{2})+(x_{1}x_{2})+x_{1}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})+(x_{2}^{2})+x_{2}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{j_{2}})

and

\displaystyle I^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{4})+(x_{1}^{3}x_{2})+x_{1}^{3}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})+(x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2})+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})+(x_{1}x_{2}^{3})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle x_{1}^{2}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})^{2}+x_{1}x_{2}^{2}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})+x_{1}x_{2}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{j_{2}})^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle x_{1}x_{2}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{j_{2}})(x_{j_{2}+1},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})+(x_{2}^{4})+x_{2}^{3}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{j_{2}})+x_{2}^{2}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{j_{2}})^{2}.

Since the minimal sets of monomial generators in each sum above are pairwise disjoint, it follows that

\mu(I)=j_{1}+j_{2}-1
and

\mu(I^{2})={j_{1}-1\choose 2}+2{j_{2}-1\choose 2}+(j_{2}-2)(j_{1}-j_{2})+3j_{1}+j_{2}-3={j_{1}\choose 2}+j_{1}j_{2}.

Therefore, we obtain that
I
is Freiman by Lemma
1.1
.

∎

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
I=B(x_{1}x_{j_{1}},x_{3}^{2})
be a Borel ideal with
3<j_{1}\leq n
. Then
I
is Freiman.

Proof.

By direct calculations, one has

I=(x_{1}^{2})+x_{1}(x_{2},x_{3})+(x_{2},x_{3})^{2}+x_{1}(x_{4},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})

and

\displaystyle I^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{4})+x_{1}^{3}(x_{2},x_{3})+x_{1}^{2}(x_{2},x_{3})^{2}+x_{1}^{3}(x_{4},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})+x_{1}^{2}(x_{2},x_{3})(x_{4},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle x_{1}(x_{2},x_{3})^{3}+x_{1}^{2}(x_{4},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})^{2}+x_{1}(x_{2},x_{3})^{2}(x_{4},\ldots,x_{j_{1}})+(x_{2},x_{3})^{4}.

Since the minimal sets of monomial generators in each sum above are pairwise disjoint, it follows that

\mu(I)=j_{1}+3
and
\mu(I^{2})={j_{1}-2\choose 2}+6j_{1}-3=\frac{1}{2}j_{1}(j_{1}+7)
. The desired result follows from Lemma
1.1
.

∎

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
I=B(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a Borel ideal of degree
2
with Borel generators
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}

u_{k}=x_{i_{k}}x_{j_{k}}

where we stipulate
i_{0}=0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.2
, we may assume

i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq j_{m}<j_{m-1}<\cdots<j_{1}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle B(u_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{m}})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j_{m}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{m-1}})+(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{i_{m}})][(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{m-1}})+(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{j_{m}})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{m-1}})^{2}+(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{m-1}})(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{j_{m}})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{i_{m}})(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{j_{m}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{m-1}})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j_{m}})+(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{i_{m}})(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{j_{m}}).

where the third equality holds because of
i_{m}\leq j_{m}
. Thus

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}B(u_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}B(u_{i})+B(u_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{k}})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j_{k}})+(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{m-1}})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j_{m}})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{i_{m}})(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{j_{m}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{k}})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j_{k}})+(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{i_{m}})(x_{i_{m-1}+1},\ldots,x_{j_{m}}).

where the last equality holds because of
j_{m}<j_{m-1}
. By repeating the above arguments, we obtain the desired result.

∎

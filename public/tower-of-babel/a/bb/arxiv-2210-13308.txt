AUXILIARY MONGE-AMPERE EQUATIONS IN GEOMETRIC ANALYSIS 1 1 1 Contribution to Surveys in Differential Geometry in honor of S.S. Chern. Work supported in part by the National Science Foundation under grant DMS-22-03273.


Abstract
This is an introduction to a particular class of auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equations which had been instrumental in L ∞ L^{\infty} estimates for fully non-linear equations and various questions in complex geometry. The essential comparison inequalities are reviewed and shown to apply in many contexts. Adapted to symplectic geometry, with the auxiliary equation given now by a real Monge-Ampère equation, the method gives an improvement of an earlier theorem of Tosatti-Weinkove-Yau, reducing Donaldson’s conjecture on the Calabi-Yau equation with a taming symplectic form from an exponential bound to an L 1 L^{1} bound.



Bin Guo and Duong H. Phong

1
Introduction

It is well-known that comparisons with an auxiliary equation can be of powerful assistance in the study of partial differential equations. Comparisons with harmonic functions were used early on by De Giorgi
[
17
]
, and have been applied since in many contexts, including e.g. in the book of L. Simon
[
58
]
, and in X.J. Wang’s method for Schauder estimates for the Poisson equation
[
71
]
. Since then, comparisons with many other equations have proved to be effective. Complex Monge-Ampère equations are particularly suitable as auxiliary equations, since the existence and smoothness of their solutions for given right hand sides have been established by Yau
[
72
]
in the case of compact Kähler manifolds, and by Caffarelli, Kohn, Nirenberg, and Spruck
[
7
]
in the case of the Dirichlet problem. Notable successes of Monge-Ampère equations as auxiliary equation include the estimates of Dinew and Kolodziej
[
20
]
relating volume and capacity, the bounds of Song and Tian
[
60
]
for the Kähler-Ricci flow, and the entropy estimates of Chen and Cheng
[
9
]
for the constant scalar curvature equation.

Very recently, a specific class of auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations has been instrumental in many significant advances in complex geometry.
This class was first introduced
by the authors in joint work with F. Tong in
[
31
]
, and already led to a pure PDE proof of the
L^{\infty}
estimates of Kolodziej
[
45
]
, a goal which had eluded researchers in the field for close to a quarter of a century. As a PDE proof, the method extends immediately Kolodziej’s estimates to a general class of non-linear equations satisfying a structural condition. Remarkably, this class has been shown by Harvey and Lawson
[
43
]
to be quite large, and include in particular all invariant Garding-Dirichlet operators. But the method turns out to be even more flexible and powerful than naively anticipated, and it has had since many unexpected applications. These include stability estimates for Monge-Ampère and Hessian equations

[
33
]
;
L^{\infty}
estimates for Monge-Ampère equations on nef classes rather than just Kähler classes
[
34
]
; sharp modulus of continuity for non-Hölder solutions
[
35
]
; extensions to parabolic equations
[
10
]
; extensions to equations on Hermitian manifolds
[
37
]
; extensions to form-type equations
[
37
]
; lower bounds for the Green’s function
[
36
]
; uniform entropy estimates
[
38
]
; and diameter estimates and convergence theorems in Kähler geometry not requiring bounds on the Ricci curvature
[
40
,
39
]
.

The main purpose of this paper is to provide a survey of these developments. We shall describe in some detail the essential features of the particular class of auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equations of interest. The main applications are then sketched, with explanations of how the auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations are used and precise statements of the results obtained. In all but one case, the full treatment is left to references to the original papers in the literature. The one exceptional case is the application to taming symplectic forms on almost-Kähler manifolds. It is a conjecture of Donaldson
[
22
]
, motivated by symplectic geometry, that on a compact
4
-manifold equipped with an almost-complex structure
J
and a taming symplectic form
\Omega
, the Calabi-Yau equation would admit a priori bounds to all orders.
This conjecture had been reduced by Weinkove
[
71
]
to an
L^{\infty}
bound for the potential, and subsequently by Tosatti, Weinkove, and Yau
[
68
]
to a single exponential estimate.
Using an auxiliary real Monge-Ampère equation, we can reduce it further to a single
L^{1}
estimate. This result is treated in detail because it is new and does not appear anywhere else. But it may also be noteworthy as evidence that the methods here can extend to the real or symplectic context as well.

2
The auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation

We begin by describing the class of Monge-Ampère equations which will serve later as comparison equations. Some key inequalities needed for the maximum principle are broadly described, which can be adapted later for different applications.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact
n
-dimensional Hermitian manifold. For any Hermitian form
\omega
on
X
and any smooth function
\varphi
with
{\rm sup}_{X}\varphi=0
, set
\omega_{\varphi}=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
, and consider the relative endomorphism

\displaystyle h_{\varphi}=\omega_{X}^{-1}\omega_{\varphi}.

(2.1)

We denote by
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]
the (un)-ordered vector of its eigenvalues.
Let
f(\lambda)
be a function on a convex cone
\Gamma\subset{\bf R}^{n}
invariant under permutations,
and consider the family of equations parametrized by
\omega
,

\displaystyle f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=k_{\omega}(z),\quad\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma,

(2.2)

where
k_{\omega}(z)
is a given positive function. For some estimates, it is convenient to introduce the constant
c_{\omega}>0
defined by the following normalization

\displaystyle k(z)=c_{\omega}\,e^{F_{\omega}},\quad\int_{X}e^{nF_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n}=\int_{X}\omega_{X}^{n}.

(2.3)

As in
[
31
]
, we require that
f:\Gamma\to{\bf R}_{+}
satisfies

(1)
\Gamma\subset{\bf R}^{n}
is a symmetric cone with

\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}.

(2.4)

Here
\Gamma_{k}
is the cone of vectors
\lambda
with
\sigma_{j}(\lambda)>0
for
1\leq j\leq k
, where
\sigma_{j}(\lambda)
is the
j
-th symmetric polynomial in
\lambda
. In particular,
\Gamma_{1}
is the half-space defined by
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}>0
, and
\Gamma_{n}
is the first octant, defined by
\lambda_{j}>0
for
1\leq j\leq n
.

(2)
f(\lambda)
is symmetric in
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma
and it is homogeneous of degree one;

(3)
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}>0
for each
j=1,\ldots,n
and
\lambda\in\Gamma
;

(4) There is a
\gamma>0
such that

\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}\geq\gamma\,{\rm det}^{-1}(\omega_{X})\,\quad\forall\lambda\in\Gamma.

(2.5)

It is well-known that equations such as the Monge-Ampère equation, with
f(\lambda)=(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j})^{1\over n}
, or the Hessian equation with
f(\lambda)=\sigma_{k}(\lambda)^{1\over k}
where
\sigma_{k}
is the
k
-th order symmetric polynomial, or the
p
-Monge-Ampère equation of Harvey and Lawson
[
41
,
42
]
with

f(\lambda)=\Big(\prod_{I}\lambda_{I}\Big)^{\frac{n!}{(n-p)!p!}}

where
I
runs over all distinct multi-indices
1\leq{i_{1}}<\cdots<{i_{p}}\leq n
,
\lambda_{I}=\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}}
, and
\Gamma
is the cone defined by
\lambda_{I}>0
for all
p
-indices
I
, all satisfy the structural condition (4). A remarkable recent result of Harvey and Lawson
[
43
]
is that the condition (4) actually holds for very large classes of non-linear operators, including all invariant Garding-Dirichlet operators. As noted in
[
43
]
, the condition (4) also arose independently in
[
2
]
in the study of
W^{2,p}
interior regularity.

We would like to compare the solution
\varphi
of the equation (
2.2
) with the solution
\psi
of the following complex Monge-Ampère equation

\displaystyle(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi)^{n}={\tau(-\varphi+q(z)-s)\over A}k_{\omega}^{n}(z)\,\omega_{X}^{n}.\qquad{\rm sup}_{X}\psi=0

(2.6)

where
s\geq 0
is a nonnegative constant,
\tau(t)
is a smooth strictly positive function,
q(z)
is a given function, and
A
is a normalizing constant.

Let
G^{j\bar{k}}
be the linearized operator of
\,{\rm log}\,f(\lambda)
, defined by

\displaystyle G^{j\bar{k}}={\partial\over\partial h_{\bar{k}j}}\,{\rm log}\,f(\lambda[h])

(2.7)

Fix a point
z_{0}\in X
. Since
\omega_{X}
is positive definite, and
h_{\varphi}
is a self-adjoint endomorphism with respect to
\omega_{X}
,
we can choose a holomorphic coordinate system centered at
z_{0}
where
(\omega_{X})_{\bar{j}k}=\delta_{jk}
and
h_{\varphi}(z_{0})
is diagonal,
(h_{\varphi})_{\bar{j}k}(z_{0})=\lambda_{j}\delta_{jk}
.
In particular
(\omega_{\varphi})_{\bar{k}j}(z_{0})=\lambda_{j}\delta_{jk}
.

Lemma 1

The linearized endomorphism
G^{j\bar{k}}
then satisfies the following: for
\lambda\in\Gamma

(a)

G^{j\bar{k}}(z_{0})={1\over f(\lambda)}{\partial f\over\partial\lambda_{j}}\delta_{jk}
, and
G^{j\bar{k}}
is positive definite;

(b)

G^{j\bar{k}}(\omega_{\varphi})_{\bar{k}j}(z_{0})=1
and
G^{j\bar{k}}\omega_{\bar{k}j}(z_{0})\geq 0
,

(c)
and, taking into account the equations satisfied by
\varphi
and
\psi
,

\displaystyle{1\over n}G^{j\bar{k}}(\omega_{\psi})_{\bar{k}j}\geq({\gamma\tau(-\varphi+q-s)\over A})^{1\over n}.

(2.8)

Proof.
The formula in (a) is a classic formula for the linearization of a fully non-linear operator
\,{\rm log}\,f(\lambda[h])
at a diagonal matrix
h
from the theory of non-linear equations
[
61
]
.
The positive-definiteness of
G^{j\bar{k}}
is an immediate consequence of the ellipticity condition
\partial_{j}f(\lambda)>0
of the function
f
. Next, we can write at
z_{0}
,

\displaystyle G^{j\bar{j}}(\omega_{\varphi})_{\bar{k}j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{1\over f(\lambda)}{\partial f\over\partial\lambda_{j}}\lambda_{j}=1

(2.9)

by Euler’s relation for homogeneous functions
f(\lambda)
of degree
1
. This proves the first equation in (b). To establish the second equation in (b), we observe that since
(\omega_{X})_{\bar{k}j}=\delta_{jk}
at
z_{0}
,
\omega
can be identified with the relative endomorphism
h_{0}=(\omega_{X})^{-1}\omega
, which is hermitian and positive with respect to
\omega_{X}
. The expression
G^{j\bar{k}}\omega_{\bar{k}j}
can then be identified with the trace
{\rm Tr}(Gh_{0})
, which is then the inner product of two positive matrices. As such, it is positive, as can be seen for example by writing it in a basis where both endomorphisms are diagonal.

Finally, to establish (c), we apply the arithmetic-geometric inequality to write

\displaystyle{1\over n}G^{j\bar{k}}(\omega_{\psi})_{\bar{k}j}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigg({\rm det}\,(G^{j\bar{k}}(\omega_{\psi})_{\bar{k}m})\bigg)^{1\over n}=\bigg({\rm det}G^{j\bar{k}}\cdot{\rm det}(\omega_{\psi})_{\bar{k}m})\bigg)^{1\over n}

(2.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg({1\over f(\lambda)^{n}}(\prod_{j=1}^{n}{\partial f\over\partial\lambda_{j}})\cdot{\tau(-\varphi+q-s)\over A}k_{\omega}(z)^{n}{\rm det}(\omega_{X})\bigg)^{1\over n}.

Applying the equation for
\varphi
, this inequality simplifies to

\displaystyle{1\over n}G^{j\bar{k}}(\omega_{\psi})_{\bar{k}j}\geq\big(\prod_{j=1}^{n}{\partial f\over\partial\lambda_{j}}\cdot{\tau(-\varphi+q-s)\over A}{\rm det}(\omega_{X})\big)^{1\over n}

(2.11)

Applying now the structural condition on
f(\lambda)
, we obtain the desired inequality (c). Q.E.D.

Next, we need a “comparison function
\Phi
”, relating the solution
\varphi
of the equation (
2.2
) to the solution
\psi
of the auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation (
2.6
). For the applications considered in this survey, the comparison function
\Phi
is usually of the form

\displaystyle\Phi=-\varepsilon(-\psi+q(z)+\Lambda)^{b}-\varphi+\tilde{q}(z)-s

(2.12)

with
0<b<1
a fixed constant,
\varepsilon,\Lambda
non-negative constants to be chosen later, and
\tilde{q}(z)
a smooth function.
We obtain the key inequality relating
\varphi
and
\psi
if we can choose all the data in
\Phi
so as to guarantee that
\Phi\leq 0
everywhere. To do so, we typically apply the maximum principle, and make use of the following general calculations:

Lemma 2

Fix a point
z_{0}
and a holomorphic coordinate system centered at
z_{0}
as above. Then we have the following inequality at
z_{0}

\displaystyle G^{j\bar{k}}\Phi_{\bar{k}j}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\varepsilon nb(-\psi+q(z)+\Lambda)^{b-1}({\gamma\tau(-\varphi+q-s)\over A})^{1\over n}-1

(2.13)

\displaystyle+G^{j\bar{k}}\big\{\omega_{\bar{k}j}+(1-\varepsilon b(-\psi+q+\Lambda)^{b-1})(\omega_{q})_{\bar{k}j}+\tilde{q}_{\bar{k}j}\big\}.

Proof.
A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bar{k}j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon b(-\psi+q(z)+\Lambda)^{b-1}(\psi-q)_{\bar{k}j}-\varphi_{\bar{k}j}+\tilde{q}_{\bar{k}j}

(2.14)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon b(b-1)(-\psi+q(z)+\Lambda)^{b-2}(\psi-q)_{j}(\psi-q)_{\bar{k}}

Rewriting this expression using
(\psi-q)_{\bar{k}j}=(\omega_{\psi})_{\bar{k}j}-(\omega_{q})_{\bar{k}j}
and
\varphi_{\bar{k}j}=(\omega_{\varphi})_{\bar{k}j}-\omega_{\bar{k}j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle G^{j\bar{k}}\Phi_{\bar{k}j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon b(-\psi+q+\Lambda)^{b-1}G^{j\bar{k}}(\omega_{\psi})_{\bar{k}j}-\varepsilon b(-\psi+q+\Lambda)^{b-1}G^{j\bar{k}}(\omega_{q})_{\bar{k}j}

(2.15)

\displaystyle-G^{j\bar{k}}(\omega_{\varphi})_{\bar{k}j}+G^{j\bar{k}}\omega_{\bar{k}j}+G^{j\bar{k}}\tilde{q}_{\bar{k}j}

\displaystyle-\varepsilon b(b-1)(-\psi+q+\Lambda)^{b-2}G^{j\bar{k}}(\psi-q)_{j}(\psi-q)_{\bar{k}}

Since
G^{j\bar{k}}>0
and
0<b<1
, we can drop from the right hand side the expression involving
G^{j\bar{k}}(\psi-q)_{j}(\psi-q)_{\bar{k}}
. The desired inequality follows then from the inequalities in Lemma
1
. Q.E.D.

The lemma is particularly useful when the last term on the right hand side of (
2.13
) happens to be positive and can be dropped.
We obtain then an upper bound for the solution
\varphi
of the given equation (
2.2
) in terms of the solution
\psi
of the auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation.
The following is the simplest illustration, which will be shown later to apply to
L^{\infty}
estimates on Kähler manifolds:

Lemma 3

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
satisfy the equations (
2.2
) and (
2.6
), under the preceding hypotheses on the operator
f(\lambda)
. Assume that the function
\tau(t)
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\tau(t)\geq t^{a},\quad t\in[0,\infty)

(2.16)

for some fixed power
a>0
. Then for any
s\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle-\varphi-s\leq c_{n,a,\gamma}A^{1\over a+n}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{n\over n+a}

(2.17)

for all
z\in X
and all
s\geq 0
, if the constants
b
,
\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
are chosen to be

\displaystyle b={n\over n+a},\quad\varepsilon=(nb\gamma^{1\over n})^{-{n\over a+n}}A^{1\over a+n},\quad\Lambda^{1-b}=\varepsilon b.

(2.18)

Here
c_{n,a,\gamma}
is a constant depending only on
n,a,\gamma
.

Proof.
Let
\Phi
be defined as in (
2.12
), with
q(z)=\tilde{q}(z)=0
. We apply Lemma
2
. Since
q=\tilde{q}=0
, we have
\omega_{q}=\omega
and the last expression on the right hand side of (
2.13
) reduces to

\displaystyle G^{j\bar{k}}\big\{\omega_{\bar{k}j}+(1-\varepsilon b(-\psi+q+\Lambda^{b-1}))(\omega_{q})_{\bar{k}j}+\tilde{q}_{\bar{k}j}\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{j\bar{k}}\big\{1-\varepsilon b(-\psi+\Lambda)^{b-1}\big\}\omega_{\bar{k}j}

(2.19)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon b\Lambda^{b-1})G^{j\bar{k}}\omega_{\bar{k}j}\geq 0

since
-\psi\geq 0
,
0<b<1
, and
G^{j\bar{k}}\omega_{\bar{k}j}\geq 0
by (b) of Lemma
1
.

Let
z_{0}
be a point where
\Phi
attains its maximum on
X
. We shall show that
\Phi(z_{0})\leq 0
.
If
-\varphi(z_{0})-s\leq 0
, the function
\Phi
is manifestly
\leq 0
at its maximum
z_{0}
, and we are done.
Otherwise, we note that
0\geq G^{j\bar{k}}\Phi_{\bar{k}j}(z_{0})
and apply Lemma
2
. As just noted,
we can drop the last term on the right hand side of (
2.13
), and bound
\tau(-\varphi-s)
from below by
(-\varphi-s)^{a}
. We find

\displaystyle 1\geq n\varepsilon b(-\psi+\Lambda)^{b-1}({\gamma(-\varphi-s)^{a}\over A})^{1\over n}

(2.20)

at
z_{0}
, which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle-(nb\gamma^{1\over n})^{-{n\over a+n}}A^{1\over a+n}(-\psi+\Lambda)^{n\over n+a}-\varphi-s\leq 0.

(2.21)

With the choice of
b
,
\varepsilon
, and
\Lambda
indicated in the lemma, we can recognize the left hand side as
\Phi(z_{0})
. Since
z_{0}
is a maximum for
\Phi
, it follows that
\Phi(z)\leq 0
for any
z\in X
.
This last statement can be recast in the form stated in the Lemma. Q.E.D.

3
Application to the compact Kähler case

The first application that we discuss is the one where the above class of auxiliary equations was originally introduced
[
31
]
, in order to provide a PDE proof of the
L^{\infty}
estimates for the complex Monge-Ampère equation originally established by Kolodziej
[
45
]
.
We discuss this case in some detail, since it also serves as a template for other subsequent applications.

Assume in this section that
X
is a compact Kähler manifold, and both
\omega_{X}
and
\omega
are Kähler forms. We would like to derive
L^{\infty}
estimates for the solutions
\varphi
of the equation (
2.2
).
Our goal is to find in the present case of non-linear equations an adaptation of the strategy going back to De Giorgi for
L^{\infty}
bounds for linear equations in divergence form. In this strategy, a lower bound for
\varphi
is obtained by showing that the set

\displaystyle\Omega_{s}=\{\varphi<-s\}

(3.1)

is empty starting from some
S_{0}
which can be estimated. We shall deduce this from suitable growth conditions on the function

\displaystyle\phi(s)={1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}k_{\omega}^{n}(z)\omega_{X}^{n},\quad V_{\omega}=\int_{X}\omega^{n},

(3.2)

which will follow themselves from a reverse Hölder inequality for the key function
A_{s}
defined for
s>0
by

\displaystyle A_{s}={1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi-s)k_{\omega}^{n}(z)\omega_{X}^{n}.

(3.3)

For this we need an auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation. Let
\tau_{\ell}(t)
a sequence of smooth strictly positive functions on
{\bf R}
which decreases monotonically to the function
{\bf R}\ni t\to t\chi_{{\bf R}_{+}}(t)
as
\ell\to\infty
, and which are uniformly bounded from above by
1+t\chi_{{\bf R}_{+}}(t)
. Here
\chi_{{\bf R}_{+}}
is the characteristic function of
{\bf R}_{+}
.
For each
s\in{\bf R}
, let
\psi_{\ell}(z)
be the solution of the following auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation

\displaystyle(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{\ell})^{n}={\tau_{\ell}(-\varphi-s)\over A_{\ell,s}}k_{\omega}^{n}(z)\omega_{X}^{n},

(3.4)

where the constant
A_{\ell,s}
is defined by

\displaystyle A_{\ell,s}={1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\tau_{\ell}(-\varphi-s)k_{\omega}^{n}(z)\omega_{X}^{n}.

(3.5)

By Yau’s theorem
[
72
]
, the above equation admits a unique smooth and
\omega
-plurisubharmonic (PSH) solution
\psi_{\ell}
normalized by
{\rm sup}_{X}\psi_{\ell}=0
.

We can now apply Lemma
3
, with
a=1
,
b=n/(n+1)
,
q\equiv 0
. Since
\psi_{\ell}\leq 0
, the condition
-\psi_{\ell}+q\geq 0
is satisfied, and we obtain an estimate of the form

\displaystyle{-\varphi-s\over A_{\ell,s}^{1/(n+1)}}\leq c_{n,\gamma}(-\psi_{\ell}+c_{n,\gamma}A_{\ell,s})^{n\over n+1}

(3.6)

where
c_{n,\gamma}
denote generically positive constants depending only on
n
and
\gamma
. Restricting to the set
\Omega_{s}=\{-\varphi-s>0\}
, we can take the
n/(n+1)
-root of both sides, multiply the resulting inequality by a constant
\beta_{0}
, taking the exponential, and integrate over
\Omega_{s}
. We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}{\rm exp}\big\{\beta_{0}({-\varphi-s\over A_{\ell,s}^{1/(n+1)}})^{n+1\over n}\big\}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq{\rm exp}(c_{n,\gamma}\beta_{0}A_{\ell,s})\int_{\Omega_{s}}{\rm exp}(-c_{n,\gamma}\beta_{0}\psi_{\ell})\omega_{X}^{n}.

(3.7)

We can now invoke the well-known inequality for
\alpha
-invariants, which states that for all
\omega
Kähler forms with
\omega\leq\kappa\omega_{X}
for some fixed
\kappa>0
, there exists a constant
\alpha
so that,

\displaystyle\int_{X}{\rm exp}(-\alpha_{0}\psi)\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C(\alpha_{0},n,\omega_{X},\kappa)

(3.8)

for any
\alpha_{0}<\alpha
and any
\omega
-plurisubharmonic function
\psi
with
{\rm sup}_{X}\psi=0

[
44
,
64
]
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}{\rm exp}\big\{\beta_{0}({-\varphi-s\over A_{\ell,s}^{1/(n+1)}})^{n+1\over n}\big\}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq c_{\alpha_{0},n,\omega_{X},\kappa,\gamma}{\rm exp}({c_{n,\gamma,\beta_{0}}A_{\ell,s}}).

(3.9)

We can now let
\ell\to\infty
and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}{\rm exp}\big\{\beta_{0}({-\varphi-s\over A_{s}^{1/(n+1)}})^{n+1\over n}\big\}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq c_{\alpha_{0},n,\omega_{X},\kappa,\gamma}{\rm exp}({c_{n,\gamma,\beta_{0}}A_{s}}).

(3.10)

where
A_{s}={\rm lim}_{\ell\to\infty}A_{\ell,s}
with
A_{s}
defined as in (
3.3
).

Let
E
be the energy, defined by

\displaystyle E={1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{X}(-\varphi)k_{\omega}^{n}(z)\omega_{X}^{n}.

(3.11)

Then
A_{s}\leq E
for any
s\geq 0
, and the preceding inequality implies the following,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}{\rm exp}\big\{\beta_{0}({-\varphi-s\over A_{s}^{1/(n+1)}})^{n+1\over n}\big\}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq c\,{\rm exp}(c\,E)

(3.12)

which suffices for our purposes.

The inequality (
3.10
) is the key inequality in our method. It is not difficult to show that it implies a reverse Hölder inequality. For this, we apply the Young’s inequality in the following form

\displaystyle UV\leq U\eta(U)+V\eta^{-1}(V)

(3.13)

for any monotone strictly increasing function
\eta:{\bf R}_{+}\to{\bf R}_{+}
with
{\rm lim}_{u\to 0}\eta(u)=0
. Here
\eta^{-1}
is the inverse of the function
\eta
. We make the choice
\eta(u)=(\,{\rm log}\,(1+u))^{p}
,
\eta^{-1}(v)={\rm exp}(V^{1\over p})-1
, and
U=e^{nF_{\omega}}
,
V=v(z)^{p}
, where we have rewritten
k_{\omega}(z)
as
k_{\omega}(z)=c_{\omega}e^{F_{\omega}}
as in (
2.3
). This gives

\displaystyle e^{nF_{\omega}}v(z)^{p}\leq e^{nF_{\omega}}\,{\rm log}\,^{p}(1+e^{nF_{\omega}})+v(z)^{p}(e^{v(z)}-1)\leq c_{p}\big\{e^{nF_{\omega}}(1+|nF_{\omega}|^{p})+e^{2v(z)}).

Next, take

and integrate both sides over
\Omega_{s}

The following inequality holds

We observe that

is essentially the

as in (

(b)

,

where the constant

.

Proof.
We begin with the proof of (a). By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle A_{s}\leq\big\{{1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi-s)^{p{n+1\over n}}k_{\omega}^{n}(z)\omega_{X}^{n}\big\}^{n\over(n+1)p}({1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}k_{\omega}^{n}(z)\omega_{X}^{n})^{1\over q}

where

\displaystyle A_{s}\leq B_{0}({1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{nF_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n})^{1+n\over qn}=B_{0}\phi(s)^{1+n\over qn}.

\displaystyle A_{s}={1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi-s)e^{nF_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n}\geq r{1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s+r}}e^{nF_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n}=r\phi(s+r)

establishing (b). The last statement (c) is a trivial consequence of (a) and (b). Q.E.D.

We can now invoke a classic lemma of De Giorgi, which says that positive monotone decreasing functions

In the case of the Monge-Ampère equation

4
Application to energy estimates from entropy

The previous Theorem
1

This problem of bounding uniformly the energy by the entropy is addressed by the following theorem

Let
(X,\omega_{X})

n

\omega

X

\displaystyle\omega\leq\kappa\,\omega_{X}

2.2

Then for any
p>0

C^{2}
solution

(i)
Trudinger-like inequalities

\int_{X}e^{\alpha(-\varphi)^{q}}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C_{T},

(4.2)

(ii)
and energy-like estimates

\int_{X}(-\varphi)^{pq}e^{nF_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C_{e}.

(4.3)

Here the exponent

and the term

We observe that, in the case of a fixed background Kähler metric

[
31

\omega
may degenerate to the boundary of the Kähler cone. For this same reason, we consider the case

, when the background Kähler form
\omega
is fixed, it follows from
[
31
]
, Theorem 1, that the solution
\varphi
of the equation is actually bounded, and the above Trudinger-like and energy-like estimates follow at once. But here again, the existing results do not give the estimates uniform in

We only describe the version of the auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equation that we need, together with the comparison inequality, leaving fuller details to
[
38
]

Thus let

Here the constant

A_{s,\ell}=\frac{c^{n}}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\tau_{\ell}(-\varphi-s)^{a}e^{nF_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n}

(4.6)

to make the equation (
4.5

[

The inequality

It is not difficult to adapt the same method of

As in Section
3
, let

Under these assumptions, there is a constant

, and

We outline the main idea of the proof, and refer to

So far, we have considered equations whose background form

As above,

7
Application to the modulus of continuity

It has been shown by Kolodziej
[
45
]
that the solution of the complex Monge-Ampère equation on a compact Kähler manifold is Hölder continuous if the right hand side is of class
L^{q}
for some
q>1
. He has also shown that if the solution is in some Orlicz space, then it must be continuous. But even so, his arguments are not direct, and we don’t have any information on the modulus of continuity of the solution. The modulus of continuity is typically a delicate question. However, it turns out that it can also be addressed using the class of auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations discussed in the present paper.

More specifically,
let
(X,\omega_{X})
be again a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
. We consider the complex Monge-Ampère equation with
\int_{X}e^{F}\omega_{X}^{n}=\int_{X}\omega_{X}^{n}

(\omega_{X}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=e^{F}\omega_{X}^{n},\quad\omega_{\varphi}=\omega_{X}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi>0.

(7.1)

Then the following estimate is established in
[
35
]
:

Theorem 5

Fix
p>n
. Then we have

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq\frac{C}{|\,{\rm log}\,d(x,y)|^{\alpha}},\quad\forall x,y\in X

for some constant
C>0
depending on
n,p,\omega_{X}
and
\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
. Here
d(x,y)
denotes the geodesic distance of
x,y
in the Riemannian manifold
(X,\omega_{X})
, and
\alpha=\min\{\frac{p}{n+1},\frac{p-n}{n}\}>0
.

The proof of Theorem
5
relies on the following auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equation:

\displaystyle(\omega_{X}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi)^{n}={\tau_{\ell}(-\varphi+q(z)-s)\over A_{\ell,s}}e^{F}\omega_{X}^{n}

(7.2)

with
q=(1-|\,{\rm log}\,\delta|^{-p/(n+1)})\varphi_{\delta}-2\delta
and
\varphi_{\delta}
is the (rescaled) Kiselman-Legendre transform of
\varphi
at level
\delta>0
. With the comparison function

\displaystyle\Phi=-\varepsilon(-\psi+\Lambda)^{n\over n+1}-\varphi+q(z)-s,

(7.3)

we can argue as in Section
3
to conclude that
\varphi_{\delta}-\varphi\leq 2\delta+|\,{\rm log}\,\delta|^{-p/(n+1)}\varphi_{\delta}+S_{\infty}
for
S_{\infty}={C}{|\,{\rm log}\,\delta|^{-(p-n)/n}}
. The proof of Theorem
5
then follows from the fact that
\varphi_{\delta}(z)
is equal to
\max\varphi
over the ball
B(z,\delta)
up to a controlled error term.

8
Application to the Hermitian case

A striking feature of the above method of auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations is the ease with which it can be adapted to the case of Hermitian manifolds. Thus we let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Hermitian manifold with
\omega_{X}
a fixed Hermitian metric,
\omega_{\varphi}=\omega_{X}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
,

h_{\varphi}=\omega^{-1}_{X}\omega_{\varphi}
, and consider the equation

\displaystyle f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=e^{F},\qquad\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma,\qquad{\rm sup}_{X}\varphi=0,

(8.1)

where the operator
f(\lambda)
defined on a cone
\Gamma
satisfies the structural conditions (1-4) spelled out in section §2. We have then
[
37
]

Theorem 6

For any
p>n
, and
C^{2}
solution
\varphi
of the equation (
8.1
) must satisfy the
L^{\infty}
bound

\displaystyle{\rm sup}_{X}|\varphi|\leq C

(8.2)

where
C
is a constant depending only on
X,\omega_{X},n,p,\gamma
and

\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
.

In the case of the Monge-Ampère equation,
L^{\infty}
estimates were first obtained in the Hermitian setting by Cherrier
[
14
]
and Tosatti and Weinkove
[
65
]
, assuming a pointwise bound for
e^{F}
. The sharper version with entropy bounds
\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
was obtained by Dinew and Kolodziej
[
20
]
, and required a highly non-trivial extension of pluripotential theory to the Hermitian setting. An approach based on envelopes has been recently proposed by Guedj and Lu
[
30
]
. Our theorem applies to much more general classes of equations, and its proof is arguably the simplest, as it bypasses the complicated integration by parts with torsion terms which arise in Hermitian geometry. We provide a brief sketch.

A first observation is that the previous auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equation cannot be applied as it is. The reason is that, on a compact Hermitian manifold, unlike on Kähler manifolds, solutions exist only up to an undetermined constant. Because of this, we shall use instead as auxiliary equation the Dirichlet problem for a complex Monge-Ampère equation on a Euclidean ball, which has been shown by Caffarelli, Kohn, Nirenberg, and Spruck
[
7
]
to admit always a smooth solution.

Thus, fix
r_{0}
small enough, but depending only on
(X,\omega_{X})
so that, for any
z_{0}\in X
, there is a coordinate system
z
centered at
z_{0}
so that

\displaystyle{1\over 2}i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}\leq\omega_{X}\leq 2i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}\mbox{ in }B(z_{0},2r_{0})=\{|z|<2r_{0}\}.

(8.3)

Let
x_{0}\in X
be a point where
\varphi
attains its minimum. We shall show that

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{0})\geq-C

(8.4)

where
C
is a constant depending only on
X,\omega_{X},p,\gamma,\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
, and
\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(X,\omega_{X})}
. It is not difficult to show by a separate argument that
\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(X,\omega_{X})}
is bounded by a constant depending only
n,\omega_{X}
, for all functions with
{\sup}_{X}\varphi=0
with
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}=\{\lambda;\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}>0\}
. The desired theorem would follow.

We introduce now the auxiliary equation. Let
\Omega=B(x_{0},2r_{0})
, and for each
s
with
0<s<2r_{0}^{2}
, set

\displaystyle u_{s}(z)=\varphi(z)-\varphi(x_{0})+{1\over 2}|z|^{2}-s

(8.5)

and
\Omega_{s}=\{z\in\Omega;u_{s}(z)<0\}
.
Let
\psi_{s,\ell}
be the solution of the following Dirichlet problem,

\displaystyle(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,\ell})^{n}={\tau_{\ell}(u_{s})\over A_{s,\ell}}e^{nF(z)}\omega_{X}^{n}\quad{\rm on}\ \Omega,\quad\psi_{s,\ell}=0\ {\rm on}\ \partial\Omega,

(8.6)

where the coefficients
A_{s,\ell}
are defined by

\displaystyle A_{s,\ell}=\int_{\Omega}\tau_{\ell}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega_{X}^{n}\ \to\ A_{s}=\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega_{X}^{n},\mbox{ as }\ell\to\infty.

(8.7)

By
[
7
]
, the solution

We can now state the key comparison inequality between

and

\displaystyle-u_{s}\leq C(n,\gamma)\,A_{s,\ell}^{1\over n+1}(-\psi_{s,\ell})^{n\over n+1}

on

, where

depends only on

,

,
and

, (and

). In particular

. It is then easy to apply the maximum principle to the function

\displaystyle\Phi=-\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,\ell})^{n\over n+1}-u_{s}

(8.9)

with the preliminary computations as in

\varepsilon^{n+1}=A_{s,\ell}\gamma^{-1}(n+1)^{n}n^{-2n}

We can now follow the template provided by the compact Kähler case. First we establish the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}{\rm exp}\{\beta{(-u_{s})^{n+1\over n}\over A_{s}^{1/n}}\}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C

(8.10)

where

on bounded pseudoconvex domains

with

on

is a strictly positive constant
1

1

.

Next, we can apply Young’s inequality to the exponential inequality (

) and obtain for any

\displaystyle A_{s}\leq C_{0}(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{nF}\omega_{X}^{n})^{1+\delta_{0}}

(8.12)

and

(8.13)

\displaystyle t\phi(s-t)\leq C_{0}(\phi(s))^{1+\delta_{0}}.

We can now apply a version of De Giorgi’s lemma for monotone increasing and positive functions

on

and the growth rate

and

Finally, we note the elementary inequality

\displaystyle\phi(s_{0})\,{\rm log}\,(-\varphi(x_{0})-s_{0})^{1\over 2}\leq\int_{\Omega_{s_{0}}}\,{\rm log}\,{-\varphi-2^{-1}|z|^{2}\over(-\varphi(x_{0})-s_{0})^{1\over 2}}e^{nF}\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle\phi(s_{0})\,{\rm log}\,(-\varphi(x_{0})-s_{0})^{1\over 2}\leq{1\over 2}{\rm max}\big\{\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}+C^{\prime}_{p}C_{1}(\omega_{X}),C_{p}^{\prime\prime}{\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}+C_{2}(\omega)\over(-\varphi(x_{0})-s_{0})^{1\over 2}}\big\}

9
Application to
(n-1)
-form Monge-Ampère equations

A new generation of problems in complex geometry, notably from non-Kähler geometry and mathematical physics, has led to non-linear equations of the form
f(\lambda)=e^{F}
, but where
\lambda
are not the eigenvalues of a hessian matrix
h_{\varphi}=\omega_{X}^{-1}(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)
, but of a more general matrix involving the derivatives of
\varphi
to second order. One basic example of such an equation is the so-called
(n-1)
-form Monge-Ampère equation, solved by G. Székelyhidi, V. Tosatti, and B. Weinkove
[
63
]
in their solution of the Gauduchon conjecture. Central to the study of this equation is the
L^{\infty}
estimate. In this section, we show how our method of auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations can be used to establish this estimate, with in fact slightly weaker and more general hypotheses than in
[
63
]
.

Indeed, let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Hermitian manifold. If
\omega
is any other Hermitian metric, we consider the smooth functions
\varphi
for which the form

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{\varphi}=\omega+{1\over n-1}(\Delta_{\omega_{X}}\varphi\omega_{X}-i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)

(9.1)

is positive, where
\Delta_{\omega_{X}}\varphi=n{i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-1}\over\omega_{X}^{n}}
is the rough Laplacian with respect to the metric
\omega_{X}
. Then

Theorem 7

Let
\tilde{h}_{\varphi}=\omega_{X}^{-1}\tilde{\omega}_{\varphi}
, and
\lambda[\tilde{h}_{\varphi}]
be its eigenvalues. Fix any
p>n
. Then any smooth solution
\varphi
of the equation

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\tilde{\lambda}_{\varphi})_{j}=e^{nF}

(9.2)

with
\tilde{\omega}_{\varphi}>0
and
{\rm sup}_{X}\varphi=0
satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm sup}_{X}|\varphi|\leq C

(9.3)

where
C
is a constant depending only on
\omega_{X},n,p,\omega
and
\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}(X,\omega_{X})}
.

We observe that this result is more precise than in
[
67
,
66
]
in the sense that only the norm
\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}(X,\omega_{X})}
is needed, and not pointwise norms. In
[
62
]
, G. Székelyhidi obtains an estimate which depends on
\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{q}}
for
q>2
, using Blocki’s approach
[
3
]
based on the Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci (ABP) maximum principle.

We sketch the proof. A first observation is that the tensor
\Theta^{i\bar{j}}
, as defined in
[
67
]
by

\displaystyle\Theta^{k\bar{j}}={1\over n-1}(({\rm Tr}_{\tilde{\omega}_{\varphi}}\omega_{X})\omega_{X}^{k\bar{j}}-\tilde{\omega}_{\varphi}^{k\bar{j}})

(9.4)

is positive definite. In fact, in coordinates where
(\omega_{X})_{j\bar{k}}=\delta_{jk}
,
(\tilde{\omega}_{\varphi})_{j\bar{k}}=\lambda_{j}\delta_{jk}
at a given point, then
\Theta^{k\bar{j}}={1\over n-1}(\sum_{\ell\not=j}{1\over\lambda_{\ell}})\delta_{jk}
.

Next, fix
r_{0}
small enough so that, at any point
z_{0}\in X
, there is a holomorphic coordinate system
z
with

\displaystyle{1\over 2}i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}\leq\omega_{X}\leq 2i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2},\qquad z\in B(z_{0},2r_{0})=\{|z|<2r_{0}\}.

(9.5)

Let
x_{0}
be a minimum point for
\varphi
,
\Omega=B(x_{0},2r_{0})
, and fix a small constant
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
depending only on
X,\omega_{X},\omega
so that

\displaystyle\omega\geq{2\varepsilon^{\prime}\over n-1}({\rm Tr}_{\omega_{X}}\omega)\omega_{X}.

(9.6)

Define now for
s\in(0,s_{0})
,
s_{0}=4\varepsilon^{\prime}r_{0}^{2}
,

\displaystyle u_{s}(z)=\varphi(z)-\varphi(x_{0})+\varepsilon^{\prime}|z|^{2}-s,\qquad z\in\Omega.

(9.7)

Then
u_{s}(z)>0
for
z\in\partial\Omega
, and hence the sublevel set
\Omega_{s}=\{z|u_{s}(z)<0\}\cap\Omega
is relatively compact in
\Omega
and also an open set. We can now consider the solution
\psi_{s,\ell}
of the following auxiliary Dirichlet problem on
\Omega
for the complex Monge-Ampère equation,

\displaystyle(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,\ell})^{n}={\tau_{\ell}(-u_{s})\over A_{s,\ell}}e^{nF}\omega_{X}^{n}\quad{\rm on}\ \Omega,\quad\psi_{s,\ell}=0\ {\rm on}\ \partial\Omega

(9.8)

with
A_{s,\ell}
defined by

\displaystyle A_{s,\ell}=\int_{\Omega}\tau_{\ell}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega_{X}^{n}\to A_{s}=\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega_{X}^{n}.

(9.9)

By
[
7
]
, this Dirichlet problem admits a unique solution
\psi_{s,\ell}
. From the very equation, the solution
\psi_{s,\ell}
satisfies
\int_{\Omega}(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,\ell})^{n}=1
.

The key comparison estimate is now

\displaystyle-u_{s}\leq({n+1\over n})^{n\over n+1}A_{s,\ell}^{1\over n+1}(-\psi_{s,\ell})^{n\over n+1}

(9.10)

which follows from the non-positivity on
\Omega
of the test function

\displaystyle\Phi=-\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,\ell})^{n\over n+1}-u_{s},\qquad\varepsilon=A_{s,\ell}^{1\over n+1}({n+1\over n})^{n\over n+1}.

(9.11)

To show that
\Phi\leq 0
, we apply the maximum principle at a maximum point
x_{0}
for
\Psi
, but with the operator

Lv=\Theta^{j\bar{k}}v_{\bar{k}j}
, so that
L\Phi(x_{0})\leq 0
. The desired inequality follows using the arithmetic-geometric inequality, along the same lines as Lemmas.

Once we have the key comparison estimate (
9.10
), the proof follows the same template as in the compact Kähler case, or more precisely the compact Hermitian case. Thus we apply the exponential estimate of Kolodziej to arrive at a reverse Hölder inequality, which allows an application of De Giorgi’s lemma, and the proof is completed using a general
L^{1}
bound for
-\varphi
. This completes the proof of Theorem
7
.

Finally, we note that there has been considerable interest recently in more general equations involving
f(\lambda[\tilde{h}_{\varphi}])
, motivated in part by non-Kähler geometry, mirror symmetry, and equations from string theories (see e.g.

[
26
,
16
,
56
,
50
]
). In particular, subsolutions and general

estimates have been obtained in
[

,

]

[
25
,
29
,
15
,
51
,
52
,
53

and references therein.

10
Application to Green’s functions

Perhaps surprisingly, the auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations which we have been discussing turn out to very effective in many seemingly unrelated problems in geometric analysis. In this section, we show how they can be applied to derive lower bounds for the Green’s function. The Green’s function is the solution of a linear partial differential equation, so this would be a case of a linear problem solved by a non-linear method.
Such methods had been instrumental in the study of Schrödinger equations
[
13
,
57
,
49
,
59
]
, and in the celebrated work of M. Kuranishi
[
46
,
47
,
48
]
on the embeddability of strongly pseudo-convex manifolds, as pointed out by C. Fefferman. However, it does not appear that comparisons with the Monge-Ampère equation had been used before in linear problems. In our case, this non-linear method is needed because it gives bounds which are uniform in the underlying geometry.

In this section, we describe some of the results obtained in
[
36
]
. The setting is then a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega_{X})
of dimension
n
, and a closed nonnegative
(1,1)
-form
\chi
whose cohomology class
[\chi]
is nef and big, that is,
[\chi]
lies in the closure of the Kähler cone and
\int_{X}\chi^{n}>0
. Then
[\chi+t\omega_{X}]
is a Kähler class for any
t\in(0,1]
. For any
\varepsilon>0
,
N>0
,
\gamma\geq 1
, and any fixed
t\in(0,1]
, we introduce the following class of Kähler metrics,

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{t}^{\prime}(N,\varepsilon,\gamma)=\{\omega\in[\chi+t\omega_{X}];{1\over V}\int_{X}e^{(1+\varepsilon)F_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq N,\ {\rm sup}_{X}e^{-F_{\omega}}\leq\gamma^{-1}\}.

(10.1)

where
F_{\omega}
denotes the relative volume form of
\omega
,

\displaystyle F_{\omega}=\,{\rm log}\,({\omega^{n}/[\omega^{n}]\over\omega_{X}^{n}/[\omega_{X}^{n}]}).

(10.2)

We are interested in bounds which hold uniformly for
\omega\in{\cal M}^{\prime}_{t}(N,\varepsilon,\gamma)
.
The following is a basic example of an estimate which would be well-known and readily established for a fixed Kähler form
\omega
, but requires a non-linear method in order to have uniform bounds with respect to
\omega\in{\cal M}_{t}^{\prime}(N,\varepsilon,\gamma)
:

Theorem 8

Let
\omega
be any Kähler form in the class

{\cal M}_{t}^{\prime}(N,\varepsilon,\gamma)
.

(a)
Let
v\in L^{1}(X,\omega^{n})
be any function satisfying
\int_{X}v\omega^{n}=0
. Let
\Omega_{s}=\{v>s\}
be the super-level sets of
v
. Assume that

\displaystyle v\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega}_{0}),\qquad\Delta_{\omega_{t}}v\geq-a\ \ {\rm in}\ \Omega_{0}

(10.3)

for some
a>0
. Then

\displaystyle{\rm sup}_{X}v\leq C(a+\|v\|_{L^{1}(X,\omega^{n})})

(10.4)

where
C
is a constant depending only on
n,\omega_{X},\chi,\varepsilon,N
, and
\gamma
.

(b)
Assume now that
v\in C^{2}(X)
and that

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\omega}v|\leq 1,\quad{\rm and}\quad\int_{X}v\omega^{n}=0.

(10.5)

Then there is a constant
C
depending only on
n,\omega_{X},\chi,\varepsilon,N,\gamma
such that
\|v\|_{L^{1}(X,\omega^{n})}\leq C
.

The proof relies again on an auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equation. Replacing
v
by
v/a
we may assume
a=1
. Thus let as before

u\to\tau_{\ell}(u)
be again a sequence of smooth strictly positive functions which decrease to the function
u\to u\chi_{{\bf R}_{+}}(u)
as
\ell\to\infty
, and consider the solution
\psi_{s,\ell}
of the following equation

\displaystyle(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,\ell})^{n}={\tau_{\ell}(v-s)\over A_{s,\ell}}\omega^{n},\qquad{\rm sup}_{X}\psi_{s,\ell}=0,

(10.6)

where
A_{s,\ell}
is again a normalizing constant

\displaystyle A_{s,\ell}={1\over[\omega^{n}]}\int_{X}\tau_{\ell}(v-s)\omega^{n}\ \to\ {1\over[\omega^{n}]}\int_{\Omega_{s}}(v-s)\omega^{n}=A_{s}

(10.7)

as
\ell\to\infty
. Yau’s theorem insures then the existence of a unique solution
\psi_{s,\ell}
.

We need to compare
v
to
\psi_{s,\ell}
. For this, we express
\omega
by the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma as

\displaystyle\omega=\chi+t\omega_{X}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi,\qquad{\rm sup}_{X}\varphi=0

(10.8)

for a unique function
\varphi
. Then the key estimate is the following: there exists a constant
\Lambda
depending only on
n,p,\chi,\omega_{X},{\rm Ent}_{p}(\omega)
so that

\displaystyle-\psi+\varphi+\Lambda\geq 1,\quad{\rm and}\quad\Phi\leq 0

(10.9)

and the function
\Phi
is defined by

\displaystyle\Phi=-\varepsilon(-\psi+\varphi+\Lambda)^{n\over n+1}+v-s

(10.10)

with
\varepsilon^{n+1}=({n+1\over n^{2}})^{n}(a+\varepsilon n)^{n}A_{s,\ell}
. The proof of this is analogous to the ones we have used for the previous auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equations.

Once we know that
\Phi\leq 0
, we can apply an
\alpha
-invariant inequality, uniform for all Kähler classes bounded by a fixed multiple of
\omega_{X}
to obtain an inequality of the form

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}{\rm exp}(\alpha{(v-s)^{n+1\over n}\over A_{s,\ell}^{1/n}})\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C

(10.11)

and hence, using Young’s inequality, the following reverse Hölder inequality for
p>n
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}(v-s)^{(n+1)p\over n}e^{F}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C\,A_{s,\ell}^{p\over n}\to C\,A_{s}^{p\over n}.

(10.12)

This readily implies
A_{s}\leq(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{F}\omega_{X}^{n})^{1+n\over np^{\prime}}
, with
p^{\prime}
the dual exponent of
p(n+1)/n
. An easy consequence is the growth inequality

\displaystyle r\phi(s+r)\leq C\phi(s)^{1+\delta_{0}},\qquad s\geq 0,\ r>0

(10.13)

for the monotone decreasing function
\phi(s)=\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{F}\omega_{X}^{n}
. Again by a De Giorgi lemma, it follows that
\phi(s)
must vanish for
s>S_{0}
, where
S_{0}
can be estimated by the constants in the growth condition. Thus
v\leq S_{0}
, and part (a) of Theorem
8
is proved.

Next, we sketch the proof of Part (b) of Theorem
8
. We observe that the proof of Part (a) did not require a uniform lower bound
\gamma
for the volume form in the definition of the class
{\cal M}^{\prime}_{t}(N,\varepsilon,\gamma)
, but the proof of Part (b) will.

We argue by contradiction. Thus assume that there exists a sequence of metrics
\omega_{j}\in{\cal M}_{t_{j}}^{\prime}(N,\varepsilon,\gamma)
with
\{t_{j}\}_{j}\subset(0,1)
and a sequence of functions
\hat{v}_{j}\in C^{2}(X)
satisfying
\|\hat{v}_{j}\|_{L^{1}(X,\omega_{j}^{n})}=1
and

\displaystyle\Delta_{\omega_{j}}\hat{v}_{j}=\hat{h}_{j},\quad\int_{X}\hat{v}_{j}\omega_{j}^{n}=0,\quad{\rm sup}_{X}|\hat{h}_{j}|\to 0

(10.14)

as
j\to\infty
. Multiplying the above equation by
\hat{v}_{j}
and integrating by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{X}|{\nabla}\hat{v}_{j}|_{\omega_{j}}^{2}\omega_{j}^{n}=|\int_{X}\hat{h}_{j}\hat{v}_{j}\omega_{j}^{n}|\leq V_{\omega_{j}}^{1\over 2}\,{\rm sup}_{X}|\hat{h}_{j}|\to 0.

(10.15)

On the other hand, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{X}|{\nabla}\hat{v}_{j}|_{\omega_{X}}\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}(|{\nabla}\hat{v}_{j}|_{\omega_{j}}^{2}{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}\omega_{j})^{1\over 2}\omega_{X}^{n}

(10.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\int_{X}|{\nabla}\hat{v}_{j}|_{\omega_{j}}^{2}e^{F_{j}}\omega_{X}^{n})^{1\over 2}(\int_{X}({\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}\omega_{j})e^{-F_{j}}\omega_{X}^{n})^{1\over 2}.

The first factor in the right hand side tends to
0
as
j\to\infty
, since

\displaystyle\int_{X}|{\nabla}\hat{v}_{j}|_{\omega_{j}}^{2}e^{F_{j}}\omega_{X}^{n}={V_{X}\over V_{\omega_{j}}}\,\int_{X}|{\nabla}\hat{v}_{j}|_{\omega_{j}}^{2}\omega_{j}^{n}\to 0

(10.17)

since
V_{\omega_{j}}\geq[\chi^{n}]
for all
\omega_{j}
. In view of the lower bound for the volume form of metrics
\omega_{j}\in{\cal M}_{t_{j}}^{\prime}(N,\varepsilon,\gamma)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}({\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}\omega_{j})e^{-F_{j}}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq{1\over n\gamma}\int_{X}\omega_{j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-1}\leq C

(10.18)

where
C
is a constant independent of
j
. This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{X}|{\nabla}\hat{v}_{j}|_{\omega_{X}}\omega_{X}^{n}\to 0\quad{\rm as}\ j\to\infty.

(10.19)

From this, it is not difficult to deduce that
\hat{v}_{j}
is uniformly bounded in the Sobolev space
W^{1,1}(X,\omega_{X})
, and that, ater passing to subsequences, it must converge to a constant
\hat{v}_{\infty}
in
L^{1}(X,\omega_{X}^{n})
. This constant can on one hand be verified to be nonzero in view of the normalization
\|\hat{v}_{j}\|_{L^{1}(X,\omega_{j}^{n})}=1
, and on the other hand to be
0
, in view of the condition
\int_{X}\hat{v}_{j}\omega_{j}^{n}=0
satisfied by
\hat{v}_{j}
. This is a contradiction and Theorem
8
is proved.

We can now establish uniform estimates for the Green’s function.
Recall now that the Green’s function with respect to the Kähler metric
\omega
is defined by the equations

\displaystyle\Delta_{\omega}G(x,y)=-\delta_{x}(y)+{1\over V_{\omega}},\qquad\int_{X}G(x,y)\omega^{n}=0.

(10.20)

Theorem 9

Fix
\varepsilon>0,N>0
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Then for any Kähler metric
\omega\in{\cal M}_{t}^{\prime}(\varepsilon,N,\gamma)
, the corresponding Green’s function
G(x,y)
satisfies the following estimates, with constants
C
which depend only on
n,\omega_{X},\chi,\varepsilon,N
and
\gamma
:

(a)

\int_{X}|G(x,\cdot)|\omega^{n}\leq C
;

(b)

{\rm inf}_{y\in X}G(x,y)\geq-C
;

(c)
For any
\delta>0
, there is a constant
C_{\delta}
depending additionally on
\delta
so that, for all
x\in X
,

\displaystyle\int_{X}|G(x,\cdot)|^{{n\over n-1}-\delta}\omega^{n}+\int_{X}|{\nabla}G(x,\cdot)|^{{2n\over 2n-1}-\delta}\omega^{n}\leq C_{\delta}.

(10.21)

Parts (a) and (b) of Theorem
9
are direct consequences of Theorem
8
. For example,
for any fixed
x
, the function
v=-G(x,y)
is in
C^{\infty}(X\setminus\{x\})
and satisfies the conditions in Part (a) of Theorem
8
with
\Delta_{\omega}v(y)=-{1\over V_{\omega}}\geq-{1\over[\chi^{n}]}
for
y\in\{v\geq 0\}
. Thus Theorem

8
applies, giving the lower bound

\displaystyle{\rm inf}_{y\in X}G(x,y)\geq-C(1+\|G(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(X,\omega^{n})})

(10.22)

for some constant depending only on
n,p,\omega_{X},\chi,N
. With a bit more work, we can deduce from Part (b) of Theorem
8
that
\|G(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(X,\omega^{n})}\leq C
.
Combined with the preceding inequality, we obtain Parts (a) and (b) of Theorem
9
.

The proof of Part (c) is harder and requires a new idea, involving comparisons with another auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equation. The key inequality to be established is a uniform bound for the
L^{q}
norm of
G(x,\cdot)
,

\displaystyle\int_{X}|G(x,y)|^{q}\omega^{n}(y)\leq C_{q}

(10.23)

first for
q\in 1+{1\over r_{0}}
for some
r_{0}>n
, and then iteratively for any
q<{n\over n-1}
.
By Part (b), we can add a uniform constant to
G(x,\cdot)
to obtain a function
{\cal G}(x,\cdot)\geq 1
. Fix
r_{0}>n
and a large
k>>1
and set

\displaystyle H_{k}(y)={\rm min}\{{\cal G}(x,y),k\}

(10.24)

which we assume is smooth, by smoothing it out if necessary.
The above integral is closely related to the following integral

\displaystyle\int_{X}{\cal G}(x,y)H_{k}(y)^{1\over r_{0}}\omega^{n}(y)

(10.25)

which is itself closely related to the solution
u_{k}
of the following Laplace equation

\displaystyle\Delta_{\omega}u_{k}=-H_{k}^{1\over r_{0}}+{1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{X}H_{k}^{1\over r_{0}}\omega^{n},\qquad{1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u_{k}\omega^{n}=0.

(10.26)

To estimate
u_{k}
, we introduce another auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equation,

\displaystyle(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{k})^{n}={H_{k}^{n\over r_{0}}+1\over V_{\omega}^{-1}\int_{X}(H_{k}^{n\over r_{0}}+1)\omega^{n}}\omega^{n},\qquad{\rm sup}_{X}\psi_{k}=0.

(10.27)

It can then be shown that

\displaystyle{\rm sup}_{X}|\psi_{k}|\leq C

(10.28)

and that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\prime}u_{k}+(\psi_{k}-\varphi)-{1\over V_{\omega}}\int_{X}(\psi_{k}-\varphi)\omega^{n}\leq C

(10.29)

where
C
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}
are uniform constants, and
\varphi
is the potential for the Kähler metric
\omega\in[\chi+t\omega_{X}]
introduced earlier in (
10.8
). From here, the desired inequality follows.

Finally, we establish integral bounds for
{\nabla}G(x,y)
. First we note the elementary inequality

\displaystyle\int_{X}{|{\nabla}_{y}{\cal G}(x,y)|_{\omega(y)}^{2}\over{\cal G}(x,y)^{1+\beta}}\omega^{n}(y)\leq{1\over\beta}

(10.30)

which holds for all
\beta>0
, and follows from applying Green’s formula to
u(y)={\cal G}(x,y)^{-\beta}
. Next, setting

\displaystyle H_{k}(y)={\rm min}\{{|{\nabla}_{y}{\cal G}(x,y)|_{\omega(y)}^{2}\over{\cal G}(x,y)^{1+\beta}},k\}

(10.31)

and arguing as in the estimate of
\|G(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1+{1\over r_{0}}}(X,\omega^{n})}
, we can show that

\displaystyle\int_{X}{\cal G}(x,y)H_{k}(y)^{1\over r_{0}}\omega^{n}(y)\leq C.

(10.32)

The desired
L^{s}(X,\omega^{n})
bound for
{\nabla}G(x,\cdot)
ultimately follows. Q.E.D.

It is instructive to compare the preceding theorem with the classic result of Cheng and Li
[
12
]
on lower bounds for the Green’s function in Riemannian geometry. This result asserts the existence of a uniform lower bound depending only the dimension, the diameter, the volume, and a lower bound on the Ricci curvature. In our Kähler setting, Theorem
9
is easily seen to imply the following theorem,
where only a lower bound on the scalar curvature, combined with an integral estimate for the volume form, suffice to give lower bounds for the Green’s function which are uniform in
\omega
:

Theorem 10

Let
\omega
be any Kähler metric in
[\omega_{X}]
. Then if
\|e^{F_{\omega}}\|_{L^{1+\varepsilon}(X,\omega_{X})}\leq N
for some
\varepsilon>0,N>0
, and the scalar curvature
R(\omega)
satisfies
R(\omega)\geq-\kappa
for some
\kappa\geq 0
, then the Green’s function of
(X,\omega)
satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm inf}_{y\in X}G(x,y)\geq-C,\quad x\in X,

(10.33)

for a constant
C
depending only on
n,\omega_{X},\varepsilon,N
, and
\kappa
.

We note that the class
{\cal M}_{t}^{\prime}(\varepsilon,N,\gamma)
of Hermitian metrics which we have used so far is not the only class to which the methods of this section apply. Other classes are described in
[
36
]
as well, with a key difference being the replacement of a pointwise lower bound on
e^{F_{\omega}}
by an integral bound. In fact, for applications to diameter bounds to be described in the next section, it will be important to relax further the constant lower bound
\gamma
to a non-negative function which may vanish along a closed set of Hausdorff dimension strictly less than
2n-1
.

In the remaining part of this section, we describe some applications of the above bounds for the Green’s function to a priori estimates for the complex Monge-Ampère equation. A priori estimates are often obtained by applying the maximum principle to elliptic differential inequalities satisfied by the quantity under consideration. Sharp lower bounds for the Green’s function can provide a more effective tool, especially if we consider conditions involving integrals. The following is a sample of sharp estimates which can be obtained in this manner.

Recall that we have assumed that the
(1,1)
-form
\chi
is non-negative, and the Kähler class
[\chi]
is big. By Kodaira’s lemma, there is an effective divisor
D
in
X
such that

\displaystyle\chi-\varepsilon_{0}{\rm Ric}(h_{D})\geq\delta_{0}\,\omega_{X}

(10.34)

for some positive constants
\varepsilon_{0},\delta_{0}
, and a Hermitian metric
h_{D}
on the line bundle
[D]
associated with
D
. Let
s_{D}
be a holomorphic section defining
D
with
{\rm sup}_{X}|s_{D}|_{h_{D}}^{2}=1
. Let
\omega
be any metric in
[\chi+t\omega_{X}]
. Let
\varphi
be its potential, i.e.

\omega=\chi+t\omega_{X}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
and
{\rm sup}_{X}\varphi=0
. Thus, in the notation (
10.2
) for the relative volume form
F_{\omega}
,
\varphi
satisfies the complex Monge-Ampère equation

\displaystyle(\chi+t\omega_{X}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=ce^{F_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n},\qquad c={V_{\omega}\over V_{X}},\quad{\rm sup}_{X}\varphi=0.

(10.35)

Theorem 11

Fix
\varepsilon,N,\gamma\in(0,1)
and
p>n
. Then for any
t\in(0,1]
and
\omega\in{\cal M}_{t}^{\prime}
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle|{\nabla}\varphi|_{\omega_{X}}^{2}\leq{C\over|s_{D}|_{h_{D}}^{2A}}

(10.36)

where
C
depends only on
n,\varepsilon,\chi,\omega_{X},N,\gamma,p
, and

depends only on
n,\varepsilon,\chi,\omega_{X},N,\gamma
.

Note that gradient bounds had been obtained before, but under pointwise assumptions on
|{\nabla}F|

[
4
,
55
]
. An earlier result requiring an

bound for
|{\nabla}F|
with

is in
[

,
32
]
. The range

in the above theorem is sharp.

Theorem 12

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
11
, but with
p>2n
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle|i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi|_{\omega_{X}}^{2}\leq{C\over|s_{D}|_{h_{D}}^{2B}}

(10.37)

where

depends only on
n,\varepsilon,\chi,\omega_{X},N,\gamma,p
and

\int_{X}|{\nabla}F_{\omega}|_{\omega_{X}}^{p}e^{F_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n}
, and

depends only on
n,\varepsilon,\chi,\omega_{X},N,\gamma
.

We have formulated the above estimates for families of degenerating metrics. But even in the case of a fixed background Kähler form
\omega
, the above estimates improve on the known ones. For example, we have

Theorem 13

Consider the complex Monge-Ampère equation

\displaystyle(\omega_{X}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=e^{F}\omega_{X}^{n},\quad{\rm sup}_{X}\varphi=0

(10.38)

on an
n

\displaystyle\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1+\varepsilon}(X,\omega_{X})}\leq N

Then for any

(a)

, where

and

.

(b)

{\rm sup}_{X}|{\nabla}i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi|_{\omega_{X}}^{2}\leq C
, where
C>0
is a constant depending only on

,

\displaystyle\gamma,\int_{X}|{\nabla}F|_{\omega_{X}}^{p}e^{F}\omega_{X}^{n},\quad\int_{X}|i\partial\bar{\partial}F|_{\omega_{X}}^{p}e^{F}\omega_{X}^{n},

(10.40)

and upper and lower bounds for the endomorphism

For the estimate in (a) to hold in general, we do need

. We also note that previous

bounds had required a

11
Application to diameter bounds

In general, estimates for the Green’s function can imply estimates for the diameter of the underlying metric. This can be seen as follows
[
39
]
.

Let
x_{0},y_{0}
be points with
d_{\omega}(x_{0},y_{0})={\rm diam}(X,\omega)
, and define the function
d
on
X
by
d(y)=d_{\omega}(x_{0},y)
. Then
d
is a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant
1
. The Green’s formula applied to
d(y)
gives

\displaystyle d(x)={1\over[\omega^{n}]}\int_{X}d(y)\omega(y)^{n}+\int_{X}\langle{\nabla}_{y}G(x,y),{\nabla}d(y)\rangle_{\omega(y)}\omega(y)^{n}.

(11.1)

The fact that
d(x_{0})=0
gives

\displaystyle{1\over[\omega^{n}]}\int_{X}d(y)\omega(y)^{n}=-\int_{X}\langle{\nabla}_{y}G(x_{0},y),{\nabla}d(y)\rangle_{\omega(y)}\omega(y)^{n}\leq\int_{X}|{\nabla}_{y}G(x_{0},y)|_{\omega(y)}\omega(y)^{n}.

We can then write

\displaystyle{\rm diam}(X,\omega)=d(y_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over[\omega^{n}]}\int_{X}d(y)\omega(y)^{n}+\int_{X}\langle{\nabla}_{y}G(y_{0},y),{\nabla}d(y)\rangle_{\omega(y)}\omega(y)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}|{\nabla}_{y}G(x_{0},y)|_{\omega(y)}\omega(y)^{n}+\int_{X}|{\nabla}_{y}G(y_{0},y)|_{\omega(y)}\omega(y)^{n}

which shows that the diameter can be estimated by an integral bound for the gradient of the Green’s function.

Thus the bounds obtained in the previous section already imply some diameter bounds. However, for many geometric applications, such as diameters in the Kähler-Ricci flow, it is important to relax the conditions on the lower bound
\gamma
for the volume form. It turns out that this is possible, albeit quite non-trivial. Thus the following theorems were established in
[
39
]
:

Theorem 14

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be an
n
-dimensional connected compact Kähler manifold. For given parameters
A,K>0,p>n
and
\gamma
a continuous non-negative function, let
{\cal V}(X,\omega_{X},n,

A,p,K,\gamma)
be the following space of Kähler metrics

\displaystyle{\cal V}(X,\omega_{X},n,A,p,K,\gamma)=\{\omega;[\omega]\cdot[\omega_{X}]^{n-1}\leq A,\,{\cal N}_{X,\omega_{X},p}(\omega)\leq K,\,{\omega^{n}\over\omega_{X}^{n}}\geq\gamma V_{\omega}\}

(11.2)

where
{\cal N}_{X,\omega_{X},p}(\omega)
is the
p
-Nash entropy, defined by

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{X,\omega_{X},p}(\omega)=\int_{X}|F|^{p}e^{F}\omega_{X}^{n}=\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}(\omega_{X})},\quad F={1\over V_{\omega}}{\omega^{n}\over\omega_{X}^{n}}.

(11.3)

Assume that

\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{\cal H}\{\gamma=0\}<2n-1

(11.4)

where
{\rm dim}_{\cal H}
denotes the Hausdorff dimension. Then for any
A,K>0
and
p>n
, there exist constants
C,c>0
depending only on
X,\omega_{X},n,A,p,K,\gamma
and
\alpha
depending only on
n
and
p
so that

(a)

\int_{X}|G(x,\cdot)|\omega^{n}+\int_{X}|{\nabla}G(x,\cdot)|\omega^{n}+(-{\rm inf}_{y\in X}G(x,y))V_{\omega}\leq C
;

(b)

{\rm diam}\,(X,\omega)\leq C
;

(c)

{Vol_{\omega}(B_{\omega}(x,R))\over Vol_{\omega}(X)}\geq c\,R^{\alpha}
for any
x\in X
,
R\in(0,1)
.

We stress that the above theorem can give bounds on the diameter even when no lower bound on the Ricci curvature is available. This is of particular importance for the Kähler-Ricci flow. More generally, we obtain the following Kähler analogue of Gromov’s precompactness theorem for metric spaces:

Theorem 15

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a connected
n
-dimensional Kähler manifold, and let
\gamma
be a non-negative function with

\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{\cal H}\{\gamma=0\}<2n-1.

(11.5)

Then for any
A,K>0
,
p>n
, any sequence
\{\omega_{j}\}
in

{\cal V}(X,\omega_{X},n,A,p,K,\gamma)
admits a subsequence converging in Gromov-Hausdorff topology to a compact metric space
(X_{\infty},d_{\infty})
.

Several applications of these theorems to the Kähler-Ricci flow and to the asymptotic behavior of fibrations near the singular fibre can be found in
[
39
]
.

12
Application to the taming of symplectic forms

Let
(X,J)
be a compact

manifold with

the
almost complex structure
. Suppose
m=2n
is the real dimension of

\tilde{g}
on

\tilde{g}(JY,JZ)=\tilde{g}(Y,Z)

\omega_{\tilde{g}}(Y,Z)=\tilde{g}(JY,Z)

Let

be a
taming symplectic form
, that is,

\Omega(Y,JY)>0
for

,
and
g
be the associated
almost Hermitian metric
of
\Omega

g(Y,Z)=\frac{1}{2}\Omega(Y,JZ)+\frac{1}{2}\Omega(Z,JY),\quad\forall\mbox{ vector fields }Y,Z.

Write
dV_{g}
for the volume form of the Riemannian metric
g
. For a smooth function
F\in C^{\infty}(X)
normalized by
\int_{X}e^{F}dV_{g}=\int_{X}\Omega^{n}

{\rm det}\tilde{g}=e^{2F}{\rm det}g,

(12.1)

where we require that
\omega_{\tilde{g}}
is an
almost Kähler form
with
[\omega_{\tilde{g}}]=[\Omega]
. As shown by Donaldson
[
22
]
, the existence of solutions to this equation would have important consequences in symplectic geometry.

It has been proved by Tosatti, Weinkove, and Yau in
[
68
]
that the
C^{2}
-

estimates of
\tilde{g}
satisfying the equation (
12.1
) can be derived by the

estimates of

, which satisfies the linear equation

\Delta_{\tilde{g}}\varphi=2n-2n\frac{\omega_{\tilde{g}}^{n-1}\wedge\Omega}{\omega_{\tilde{g}}^{n}}=2n-{\rm tr}_{\tilde{g}}g,\quad{\rm sup}_{X}\varphi=0.

(12.2)

Here
\Delta_{\tilde{g}}
is the usual Riemannian Laplacian operator of the metric
\tilde{g}
. Note that the equation (
12.2
) admits a unique solution since the function on the right-hand-side has integral zero against the volume form
dV_{\tilde{g}}
.

We assume

\|e^{F}\|_{L^{2}(X,dV_{g})}^{2}=\int_{X}e^{2F}dV_{g}\leq K

for some constant
K>0
. Our main result is the following

estimate of
\varphi
in (

Theorem 16

Suppose
\tilde{g}
is an almost Kähler metric solving the equation (
12.1
) and
\varphi
solves (
12.2
). Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
n,g
and
K
such that

{\rm sup}_{X}|\varphi|\leq C(1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(X,e^{2F}dV_{g})}).

In the following, we denote
\{x^{1},\ldots,x^{m}\}
a local
real
coordinates on some open subset of
X
. Let
\tilde{g}
be an
almost Kähler
metric, and
\tilde{\omega}(Y,Z)=\tilde{g}(JY,Z)
be the associated symplectic
2
-form. Locally we have

\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}_{ij}dx^{i}\otimes dx^{j},\quad\tilde{\omega}=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\omega}_{ij}dx^{i}\wedge dx^{j},\quad J=J_{i}^{j}dx^{i}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}},

(12.3)

where the summations are taken over
i,j\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
. It follows from straightforward calculations that

\tilde{\omega}_{ij}=\tilde{g}_{ik}J_{j}^{k},\quad\tilde{\omega}_{ij}=-\tilde{\omega}_{ji}.

(12.4)

\tilde{\omega}
being

means

0=d\tilde{\omega}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\tilde{\omega}_{ij}}{\partial x^{l}}dx^{l}\wedge dx^{i}\wedge dx^{j}=\frac{1}{6}(\frac{\partial\tilde{\omega}_{ij}}{\partial x^{l}}+\frac{\partial\tilde{\omega}_{li}}{\partial x^{j}}+\frac{\partial\tilde{\omega}_{jl}}{\partial x^{i}})dx^{l}\wedge dx^{i}\wedge dx^{j}

in other words,

\frac{\partial\tilde{\omega}_{ij}}{\partial x^{l}}+\frac{\partial\tilde{\omega}_{li}}{\partial x^{j}}+\frac{\partial\tilde{\omega}_{jl}}{\partial x^{i}}=0,\quad\forall i,j,l.

(12.5)

Multiplying both sides of (

) by
\tilde{\omega}^{ij}:=\tilde{g}^{ik}J_{k}^{j}
which is skew-symmetric in

, and taking summation over

, we get

(12.6)

Substituting (
12.4
) to (

), we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{ik}J_{k}^{j}\frac{\partial\tilde{g}_{ip}}{\partial x^{l}}J_{j}^{p}+\tilde{g}^{ik}J_{k}^{j}\tilde{g}_{ip}\frac{\partial J_{j}^{p}}{\partial x^{l}}+2\tilde{g}^{ik}J_{k}^{j}\frac{\partial\tilde{g}_{jp}}{\partial x^{i}}J_{l}^{p}+2\tilde{g}^{ik}J_{k}^{j}\tilde{g}_{jp}\frac{\partial J_{l}^{p}}{\partial x^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tilde{g}^{ik}\frac{\partial\tilde{g}_{ik}}{\partial x^{l}}+J_{k}^{j}\frac{\partial J_{j}^{k}}{\partial x^{l}}+2\tilde{g}^{ik}J_{k}^{j}\tilde{g}_{jp}\frac{\partial J_{l}^{p}}{\partial x^{i}}+2\tilde{g}^{ik}\frac{\partial\tilde{g}_{kl}}{\partial x^{i}}

\displaystyle-2\tilde{g}^{ik}\tilde{g}_{jp}J_{l}^{p}\frac{\partial J_{k}^{j}}{\partial x^{i}}-2\tilde{g}^{ik}\tilde{g}_{jp}J_{k}^{j}\frac{\partial J_{l}^{p}}{\partial x^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tilde{g}^{ik}\frac{\partial\tilde{g}_{ik}}{\partial x^{l}}+2\tilde{g}^{ik}\frac{\partial\tilde{g}_{kl}}{\partial x^{i}}+J_{k}^{j}\frac{\partial J_{j}^{k}}{\partial x^{l}}-2J_{p}^{i}\frac{\partial J_{l}^{p}}{\partial x^{i}}+2\tilde{g}^{ik}\tilde{g}_{lp}J_{j}^{p}\frac{\partial J_{k}^{j}}{\partial x^{i}}+2J_{p}^{i}\frac{\partial J_{l}^{p}}{\partial x^{i}},

A_{s,\ell}\to A_{s}:=\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})e^{2F}{\rm det}g

and
A_{s}\leq C(n,g)K\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{1}
, where
C_{1}>0
depends only on
n,g
and
K
. Hence for all
\ell
sufficiently large which we always assume, we have

A_{s,\ell}\leq C_{1}.

(12.15)

Let
\beta_{n}
be the volume of the unit ball in
{\bf R}^{2n}
.

Lemma 6

There exist a constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(n)>0
such that

-\inf_{B(x_{0},2r_{0})}\psi_{s,\ell}\leq C_{2}r_{0},\quad{\rm sup}_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}|\nabla\psi_{s,\ell}|\leq C_{2}.

Proof.
Since by the definition of
\psi_{s,\ell}
,
\int_{B(x_{0},2r_{0})}{\rm det}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{s,\ell}}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}\Big)dx=1
, so it follows from the standard ABP maximum principle

-\inf_{B(x_{0},2r_{0})}\psi_{s,\ell}\leq-\inf_{\partial B(x_{0},2r_{0})}\psi_{s,\ell}+\frac{4r_{0}}{\beta_{n}}\Big[\int_{B(x_{0},2r_{0})}{\rm det}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{s,\ell}}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}\Big)dx\Big]^{1/m}=\frac{4}{\beta_{n}}r_{0}.

To see the second inequality, for any fixed point
x\in B(x_{0},r_{0})
, denote
V=\frac{D\psi_{s,\ell}(x)}{|D\psi_{s,\ell}(x)|}
to be the unit vector in the direction of
D\psi_{s,\ell}(x)
(if
D\psi_{s,\ell}(x)=0
, there is nothing to prove, so here we assume
D\psi_{s,\ell}(x)\neq 0
). Then clearly
|D\psi_{s,\ell}(x)|=D\psi_{s,\ell}(x)\cdot V
. Consider the half line
L:

0\leq t\mapsto x+tV
which intersects
\partial B(x_{0},r_{0})
and
\partial B(x_{0},2r_{0})
at
L(t_{1})
,
L(t_{2})
, respectively. We have
t_{2}-t_{1}\geq r_{0}
and
0>\psi_{s,\ell}(L(t_{1}))\geq-\frac{4}{\beta_{n}}r_{0}
and
\psi_{s,\ell}(L(t_{2}))=0
. Then by the convexity of the function
t\mapsto\psi_{s,\ell}(L(t))
, we have

|D\psi_{s,\ell}(x)|=\psi_{s,\ell}(L(t))^{\prime}|_{t=0}\leq\frac{\psi_{s,\ell}(L(t_{2}))-\psi_{s,\ell}(L(t_{1}))}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\leq\frac{4}{\beta_{n}}.

Taking supremum over all
x\in B(x_{0},r_{0})
finishes the proof of the lemma. Q.E.D.

Take positive constants

\Lambda=\frac{2n}{1+2n}(10C_{J}C_{2})^{2n+1}A_{s,\ell},\quad\varepsilon=\Big(\frac{2n+1}{2n}\Big)^{\frac{2n}{2n+1}}A_{s,\ell}^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}

(12.16)

where
C_{2}>0
is the constant in Lemma
6
and
C_{J}>0
as in (
12.11
). We observe that by (
12.15
), it holds that
\Lambda
is bounded above by the uniform constant
\frac{2n}{1+2n}(10C_{J}C_{2})^{2n+1}C_{1}
.

Define a function
\Phi
on
B(x_{0},2r_{0})
by

\Phi(x)=-\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,\ell}(x)+\Lambda)^{\frac{2n}{2n+1}}-u_{s}(x),\quad\forall x\in B(x_{0},2r_{0}).

(12.17)

We claim that
\Phi\leq 0
on this ball. As a continuous function,
\Phi
achieves its maximum at some point
x_{\max}\in{\overline{B(x_{0},2r_{0})}}
. If
x_{\max}\not\in\Omega_{s}
, then by the definition of
\Omega_{s}
, clearly we have
\Phi(x_{\max})<0
. So we assume
x_{\max}\in\Omega_{s}\subset B(x_{0},r_{0})
. By the maximum principle, it follows that
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x^{q}}\Big|_{x_{\max}}=0
and
\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}\Big|_{x_{\max}}\leq 0
. Hence at
x_{\max}

\Delta_{\tilde{g}}\Phi=\tilde{g}^{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}-\tilde{g}^{ij}\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{q}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x^{q}}=\tilde{g}^{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}\leq 0.

We then calculate at
x_{\max}
.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Delta_{\tilde{g}}\Phi=\frac{2n\varepsilon}{2n+1}(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}\Delta_{\tilde{g}}\psi_{s,\ell}-\Delta_{\tilde{g}}\varphi-\eta\Delta_{\tilde{g}}|x|^{2}

(12.18)

\displaystyle+\frac{2n\varepsilon}{(2n+1)^{2}}(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{2n+2}{2n+1}}|\nabla\psi_{s,\ell}|^{2}_{\tilde{g}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2n\varepsilon}{2n+1}(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}\Delta_{\tilde{g}}\psi_{s,\ell}-2n+{\rm tr}_{\tilde{g}}g-\eta\Delta_{\tilde{g}}|x|^{2}.

We first look at the term
-\eta\Delta_{\tilde{g}}|x|^{2}
in (
12.18
). It satisfies

-\eta\Delta_{\tilde{g}}|x|^{2}=-2\eta\tilde{g}^{ij}\delta_{ij}+2\eta\tilde{g}^{ij}\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{q}x^{q}\geq-4\eta{\rm tr}_{\tilde{g}}g-2\eta C_{J}r_{0}{\rm tr}_{\tilde{g}}g\geq-\frac{1}{10}{\rm tr}_{\tilde{g}}g,

(12.19)

where
C_{J}>0
is the uniform constant in (
12.11
) and we have chosen
\eta>0
such that

\eta(4+2C_{J}r_{0})=1/10

and this fixes the uniform constant
\eta
. We will denote
D^{2}_{ij}\psi_{s,\ell}=\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{s,\ell}}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}
to be (Euclidean) Hessian of the function
\psi_{s,\ell}
.
For the first term in (
12.18
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{2n\varepsilon}{2n+1}(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}\Delta_{\tilde{g}}\psi_{s,\ell}

(12.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2n\varepsilon}{2n+1}(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}\tilde{g}^{ij}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{s,\ell}}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}\Big)+\frac{2n\varepsilon}{2n+1}(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}\tilde{g}^{ij}\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{q}\frac{\partial\psi_{s,\ell}}{\partial x^{q}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{4n^{2}\varepsilon}{2n+1}(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}\Big(\frac{{\rm det}D^{2}\psi_{s,\ell}}{{\rm det}\tilde{g}}\Big)^{1/2n}-\frac{2n\varepsilon\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}}{2n+1}C_{J}|\frac{\partial\psi_{s,\ell}}{\partial x^{q}}|\cdot{\rm tr}_{\tilde{g}}g

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{4n^{2}\varepsilon}{2n+1}(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}\Big(\frac{-u_{s}}{A_{s,\ell}}\Big)^{1/2n}-\frac{2n\varepsilon\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}}{2n+1}C_{J}C_{2}\cdot{\rm tr}_{\tilde{g}}g

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{4n^{2}\varepsilon^{1+\frac{1}{2n}}}{(2n+1)A_{s,\ell}^{1/2n}}\Big(\frac{-u_{s}}{\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{2n/(2n+1)}}\Big)^{1/2n}-\frac{1}{10}{\rm tr}_{\tilde{g}}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2n\Big(\frac{-u_{s}}{\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{2n/(2n+1)}}\Big)^{1/2n}-\frac{1}{10}{\rm tr}_{\tilde{g}}g.

Combining the equations (
12.20
), (
12.19
), (
12.18
), we see that at
x_{\max}

0\geq 2n\Big(\frac{-u_{s}}{\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{2n/(2n+1)}}\Big)^{1/2n}-2n+\frac{4}{5}{\rm tr}_{\tilde{g}}g

from which we easily derive that
\frac{(-u_{s})}{\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{2n/(2n+1)}}<1
, that is,
\Phi|_{x_{\max}}<0
. Hence we finish the proof of the claim that
\Phi\leq 0
. In particular on
\Omega_{s}
it holds that

-u_{s}\leq C(n)A_{s,\ell}^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}(-\psi_{s,\ell}+\Lambda)^{\frac{2n}{2n+1}}\leq C_{3}A_{s,\ell}^{\frac{1}{2n+1}},

(12.21)

for some
C_{3}>0
that depends on
n,g,J,K
. Here we have applied Lemma
6
to see that
|\psi_{s,\ell}|\leq C_{2}r_{0}
and the fact that
\Lambda
is a uniformly bounded constant. Letting
\ell\to\infty
we conclude from (
12.21
) that

-u_{s}\leq C_{3}A_{s}^{\frac{1}{2n+1}},

(12.22)

Integrating both sides of (
12.22
) agaist the measure
e^{2F}({\rm det}g)dx
over
\Omega_{s}
, we get

A_{s}=\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})e^{2F}({\rm det}g)dx\leq C_{3}A_{s}^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{2F}({\rm det}g)dx.

(12.23)

So we have

A_{s}\leq C_{3}^{\frac{2n+1}{2n}}\Big(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{2F}({\rm det}g)dx\Big)^{1+\frac{1}{2n}}=C_{4}\phi(s)^{1+\frac{1}{2n}},

(12.24)

where we denote
\phi(s)=\Big(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{2F}({\rm det}g)dx\Big)^{1+\frac{1}{2n}}
. On the other hand, for any
0<t<s
, on the open set
\Omega_{s-t}
we have

u_{s}(x)=u_{s-t}(x)-t<-t,\mbox{ i.e. }-u_{s}(x)>t

It is elementary that

A_{s}\geq\int_{\Omega_{s-t}}(-u_{s})e^{2F}({\rm det}g)dx\geq t\phi(s-t).

Combining the above, we see that

t\phi(s-t)\leq C_{4}\phi(s)^{1+\frac{1}{2n}},\quad\forall\,0<t<s\leq s_{0}.

(12.25)

It is not hard to see that
\phi(s)
is an
increasing
and
continuous
function in
s\in(0,s_{0}]
and
\phi(s)>0
for any
s\in(0,s_{0}]
and
\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\phi(s)=0
. We can apply a version of De Giorgi’s lemma to show that
\frac{2C_{4}}{1-2^{-1/2n}}\phi(s_{0})^{1/2n}\geq s_{0}
. Hence there is a uniform constant
c_{0}>0
such that

\phi(s_{0})\geq c_{0}>0.

(12.26)

Applying (
12.24
) with
s=s_{0}
, we obtain
A_{s_{0}}\leq C_{5}
for a constant
0<C_{5}=C_{4}(2^{2n}\beta_{n}K)^{1+\frac{1}{2n}}
. From the definition of
A_{s_{0}}
, we derive that

(-\varphi(x_{0}))\cdot\phi(s_{0})\leq s_{0}\phi(s_{0})+\int_{\Omega_{s_{0}}}(-\varphi)e^{2F}({\rm det}g)dx+C_{5}.

(12.27)

The equation (
12.5
) then implies that

-{\rm inf}_{X}\varphi=-\varphi(x_{0})\leq C_{6}+C_{7}\int_{X}(-\varphi)e^{2F}dV_{g},

(12.28)

for some constant
C_{7}>0
. So we have proved the inequality for the solution
\varphi
to the equation (
12.2
)

{\rm sup}_{X}|\varphi|\leq C_{8}(1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(X,e^{2F}dV_{g})}).

(12.29)

This finishes the proof of Theorem
16
. Q.E.D.

The stable cohomology of self-equivalences of connected sums of products of spheres
Robin Stoll

Abstract
We identify the cohomology of the stable classifying space of homotopy automorphisms (relative to an embedded disk) of connected sums of S k × S l {\mathrm{S}^{k}}\times{\mathrm{S}^{l}} , where 3 ≤ k < l ≤ 2 ​ k − 2 3\leq k<l\leq 2k-2 . The result is expressed in terms of Lie graph complex homology.

Introduction

The starting point of this article is the following manifold with boundary

M^{k,l}_{g,1}\coloneqq\mathbin{\#}_{g}\big({\mathrm{S}^{k}}\times{\mathrm{S}^{l}}\big)\setminus{\accentset{\circ}{\mathrm{D}}^{k+l}}

i.e. the
g
-fold connected sum of
{\mathrm{S}^{k}}\times{\mathrm{S}^{l}}
with an open disk removed.
Given this, a natural object to consider is the classifying space

{\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}\big(M^{k,l}_{g,1}\big)

of the topological monoid of homotopy automorphisms of
M^{k,l}_{g,1}
that fix the boundary pointwise.
It classifies fibrations with fiber
M^{k,l}_{g,1}
under the trivial fibration with fiber its boundary (see e.g.
[
HL15
, Appendix B]
).
In particular the cohomology of
{\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1})
consists of the characteristic classes for such fibrations.

This cohomology appears to be hard to understand completely.
However, at least rationally, it is possible to say something about the behavior of the
stabilization maps

{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g+1,1});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)

(1.1)

obtained by gluing on, along one of its boundary components, a copy of
{\mathrm{S}^{k}}\times{\mathrm{S}^{l}}
with two disjoint open disks removed.
Namely, in the case
2\leq k=l
, Berglund–Madsen
[
BM20
]
have shown this map to be an isomorphism in a certain stable range of degrees
1
1

1

This stable range was extended significantly by Krannich
[
Kra20
]
.
, and this was extended by Grey
[
Gre19
]
to the cases
3\leq k\leq l\leq 2k-2
.

Moreover
[
BM20
]
contains, in the case
3\leq k=l
, a combinatorial description (in terms of graph complex homology) of the
stable cohomology
, i.e. the limit of the maps (
1.1
) as
g
goes to infinity.
In this paper we provide a similar description when
k\neq l
.
The following is our main result.

Theorem 1.1

(see
Corollary

5.9
)
.

Let
3\leq k<l\leq 2k-2
and
2\leq g
be integers.
Then there is, in cohomological degrees
\leq g-2
, an isomorphism of graded algebras

{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\cong{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\otimes{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({{\mathfrak{UG}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}^{\vee}}\big)

compatible with the stabilization maps on the left hand side.
Here we set
{\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}})\coloneqq\mathinner{\operatorname{colim}\displaylimits_{g\in{\mathbb{N}}}}{\operatorname{GL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Z}})
, denote by
{\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
the
m
-twisted graph complex
associated to the cyclic Lie operad (with its canonical coalgebra structure), and write
{(-)^{\vee}}
for linear dualization.

In particular, after stabilizing, we obtain an isomorphism

\mathinner{\operatorname{lim}\displaylimits_{g\in{\mathbb{N}}}}{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\cong{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\otimes{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({{\mathfrak{UG}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}^{\vee}}\big)

of graded algebras.

Remark
.

We also prove a version of
Theorem

1.1
for cohomology with certain local coefficients, see
Theorem

5.7
.
In this generality the description we give is in terms of a
directed
graph complex (see below).
It should be possible to relate this to an undirected graph complex, just as in the case of trivial coefficients above, but we do not carry this out in this paper.

Remark
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
does not rely on the stability result of Grey
[
Gre19
]
(though we do reuse some of its ingredients).
In particular we obtain a new proof of his result (for the manifolds
M^{k,l}_{g,1}
), while also roughly doubling the stable range.
This is made possible by a result of Li–Sun
[
LS19
]
, which yields a slope
1
vanishing of the rational cohomology of
{\operatorname{GL}_{n}}({\mathbb{Z}})
with certain coefficients.
That a result of this form would lead to an improvement of the stable range had been suggested by Krannich
[
Kra20
, p. 1070]
.

Surprisingly, the graph complex appearing in
Theorem

1.1
only depends on the dimension
k+l
and not on
k
and
l
themselves.
Moreover, when
k+l
is even, it is the same graph complex that appears in
[
BM20
]
, i.e. in the case
k=l
(when
k+l
is odd, it is defined similarly to the even case but appears to have quite different homology; see further below).
For both of these observations there seems to be no a priori reason to expect them to be true.
The second one is particularly interesting when considering our proof, where first a different graph complex appears, of which we then show that it actually has the same homology as
{\mathfrak{UG}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
.

We will now describe the terms appearing on the right hand sides of the isomorphisms of
Theorem

1.1
in more detail.
First note that

{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\cong\mathrm{\Lambda}{{\mathbb{Q}}{\langle x_{i}\mid i\in{{\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}}}\rangle}}\qquad\text{where}\quad|x_{i}|=4i+1

by a classical result of Borel
[
Bor74
]
.
The graph complex
{\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}
, for
\mathscr{C}
a cyclic operad, is also a well-known object.
Versions of it were first described by Kontsevich
[
Kon93
,
Kon94
]
, but they have, since then, appeared in many different places, see e.g.
[
GK98
,
CV03
,
LV08
,
BM20
]
.

We now sketch the definition of this graph complex.
It is generated by graphs (potentially with loops and parallel edges) equipped with an orientation of each edge and a labeling of each vertex with an element of
\mathscr{C}
of cyclic arity the valence of the vertex (which we assume to be at least three).
We consider a vertex to have homological degree
1-m
and an edge to have homological degree
m
.
The resulting graded vector space is quotiented by the actions of the automorphism groups of the graphs, taking into account the homological degrees of the vertices and edges, and yielding an extra sign
(-1)^{m+1}
whenever the orientation of an edge is flipped.
The differential is defined to be the sum over all possible contractions of non-loop edges.
When an edge is contracted, the label of the new vertex is obtained via the cyclic operad composition of the labels of the two contracted vertices, as in the following picture.
\xi_{1}
\xi_{2}
i
j
\longmapsto
\xi_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}_{{{i\,}}}}\circ^{{\kern-5.52933pt\kern 5.91821pt}}_{{\kern-8.8841pt{i\,}\kern 5.91821pt{j}}}}{\hphantom{{}_{{{i\,}}}}\circ^{{\kern-5.52933pt\kern 5.91821pt}}_{{\kern-8.8841pt{i\,}\kern 5.91821pt{j}}}}{\hphantom{{}_{{{i\,}}}}\circ^{{\kern-3.8821pt\kern 4.27098pt}}_{{\kern-6.75455pt{i\,}\kern 4.27098pt{j}}}}{\hphantom{{}_{{{i\,}}}}\circ^{{\kern-3.1821pt\kern 3.57098pt}}_{{\kern-6.05455pt{i\,}\kern 3.57098pt{j}}}}}\xi_{2}

The graph complex has the structure of a cocommutative differential graded coalgebra with comultiplication given by the sum over all possible ways to partition the connected components of a graph into two sets.

Note that, up to regrading, the graph complex and in particular its homology only depend on the parity of
m
.
Even though this homology could theoretically be computed directly from its definition, this has only be feasible in low degrees so far.
Its structure in higher degrees remains largely mysterious.
By a result of Kontsevich
[
Kon93
]
(for
m
even; see also Conant–Vogtmann
[
CV03
]
) and Lazarev–Voronov
[
LV08
]
(for
m
odd), Lie graph complex homology is closely related to the rational homology (with twisted coefficients if
m
is odd) of the group
\mathrm{Out}(F_{g})
of outer automorphisms of the free group on
g
generators.
See
Remark

4.30
for a summary of what is known about this.
In particular, computations of Brun–Willwacher
[
BW23
]
imply that
{\operatorname{H}_{p}}({\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})})
is trivial for
0<p\leq 3m+2
when
m\geq 3
is odd (see
Remark

4.30
).
Using this, we obtain the following consequence of our main theorem.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
3\leq k<l\leq 2k-2
and
2\leq g
be integers such that
k+l
is odd.
Then there is, in cohomological degrees
\leq{\min}\big(g-2,3(k+l)-4\big)
, an isomorphism of graded algebras

{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\cong{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)

compatible with the stabilization maps on the left hand side.

Sketch of the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

From now on we fix some
3\leq k<l\leq 2k-2
and drop them from the notation.
The starting point of our proof are methods recently developed by Berglund–Zeman
[
BZ22
]
which in particular yield an isomorphism

{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M_{g,1});P\big)\cong{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big(\Gamma_{g}^{\mathbb{Z}};{\operatorname{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{CE}}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)

(1.2)

for any
\Gamma^{\mathbb{Z}}_{g}
-representation
P
(the details of applying their methods to relative self-equivalences are worked out in the upcoming paper
[
BS
]
joint with Berglund).
Here the differential graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{g}
is the Quillen model of the 1-connected cover of
{\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M_{g,1})
, we write
{\operatorname{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{CE}}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})
for its Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology, and we set

\Gamma_{g}^{R}\coloneqq{\operatorname{Aut}}\big({\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}_{*}}(M_{g,1};R),{{\langle-,-\rangle_{\cap}}}\big)\cong{\operatorname{GL}_{g}}(R)

where
R
is a commutative ring and
{{\langle-,-\rangle_{\cap}}}
is the intersection pairing.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{g}
has an explicit description which was shown by Berglund–Madsen
[
BM20
]
to be isomorphic to

{{({\mathrm{s}^{-(k+l-2)}}\mathscr{\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie}})\big[{\mathrm{s}^{-1}}{\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}_{*}}(M_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}})\big]}}^{+}

equipped with the trivial differential.
Here
\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie}
is (the symmetric sequence underlying) the cyclic Lie operad,
{\mathrm{s}}
denotes a degree shift,
{\mathscr{A}[-]}
is the Schur functor associated to
\mathscr{A}
, and
{(-)}^{+}
denotes positive truncation.

The next step is to simplify the right hand side of (
1.2
).
To this end we prove, using the previously mentioned work of Li–Sun
[
LS19
]
, that the canonical map

{\operatorname{H}^{p}}\big(\Gamma_{g}^{\mathbb{Z}};{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\otimes{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\Big(\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)^{\Gamma_{g}^{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{H}^{p}}\big(\Gamma_{g}^{\mathbb{Z}};{\operatorname{H}^{q}_{\mathrm{CE}}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)

is an isomorphism in a stable range when
P
is an algebraic representation of
\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}
.
(See also work of Krannich
[
Kra20
]
, where an analogous argument is made to obtain a similar stable range in the case
k=l
.)

This shows that it is enough to identify the (stable) cohomology of the invariants
\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)^{\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}}
.
For sake of exposition, we will now focus on the case
P={\mathbb{Q}}
.
In the paper, we do carry out the first part of the argument for a certain class of non-trivial representations as well, though.
Similar situations, arising from
{\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}}
or
{\operatorname{O}_{g,g}}
instead of
{\operatorname{GL}_{g}}
, have been considered by Kontsevich
[
Kon93
,
Kon94
]
(see also Conant–Vogtmann
[
CV03
]
) in the symplectic case and by Berglund–Madsen
[
BM20
]
in both cases.
In our situation we use coinvariant theory methods similar to those of
[
BM20
]
to obtain a description of the stable cohomology in terms of a directed version of the Lie graph complex, which we denote by
{\mathfrak{DG}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
.
It is defined in the same way as
{\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
, except that every edge is equipped with a direction, which (unlike an orientation) must be respected by automorphisms of the graph.

Theorem 1.3

(see
Corollary

3.33
)
.

There is an isomorphism

\mathinner{\operatorname{colim}\displaylimits_{g\in{\mathbb{N}}}}{{\operatorname{C}^{\mathrm{CE}}_{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})_{\Gamma_{g}^{\mathbb{Q}}}}\cong{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}

of differential graded coalgebras, compatible with the stabilization maps on the left hand side.
Here
{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
is the subcomplex of
{\mathfrak{DG}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
spanned by
truncated
directed graphs, which are those that do not have vertices of valence zero or one and whose vertices of valence two have only incoming edges.

Remark
.

At the heart of this theorem actually lies an unstable identification of a certain subcomplex of
{{\operatorname{C}^{\mathrm{CE}}_{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})_{\Gamma_{g}}}
.
Moreover we prove a more general statement, which also applies to a certain class of non-trivial representations
P
, as well as to all cyclic operads and not just
\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie}
.

While less ubiquitous than undirected graph complexes, directed versions have appeared in the literature before, see for example
[
Wil14
,
Živ20
,
DR19
]
.
Surprisingly it turns out that the homology of the truncated directed graph complex
{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
is actually isomorphic to the one of
{\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
.
We will now explain the idea of this argument.
Restricting the differential of
{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
to the two edges incident to a vertex of valence two (which must be labeled by the identity operation) yields the following picture.
\xi_{1}
\operatorname{id}
\xi_{2}
\longmapsto
\xi_{1}
\xi_{2}
+
\xi_{1}
\xi_{2}

Thus, intuitively, taking homology should kill the difference between the two orientations of an edge.
This can be formalized using a spectral sequence argument to prove the following theorem.
Together with the preceding results, this implies
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 1.4

(see
Theorem

4.28
)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a cyclic operad such that
{\mathscr{C}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mkern-4.0mu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2}}\right)\mkern-4.0mu}}\right)}\cong{{\mathbb{Q}}\langle\operatorname{id}\rangle}
.
Then there is, for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, an isomorphism

{\operatorname{H}_{*}}\big({\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}\big)\cong{\operatorname{H}_{*}}\big({\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}\big)

of graded coalgebras.

Remark
.

We only carry out this argument in the situation arising from trivial coefficients
P={\mathbb{Q}}
.
There should exist a more general statement of this form, though.

Remark
.

For the non-truncated directed graph complex an argument similar to this has been sketched by Willwacher
[
Wil14
, Appendix K]
.

Related work and further research.

Building on their work for homotopy automorphisms, Berglund–Madsen
[
BM20
]
also computed, for
k\geq 3
, the stable cohomology of the classifying spaces
{\mathrm{B}}\widetilde{\mathrm{Diff}}_{\partial}\big(M^{k,k}_{g,1}\big)
of block diffeomorphisms.
Combining their methods with the methods of our paper (as well as an upgrade of the methods of
[
BZ22
]
to automorphisms of bundles, which is planned to be contained in the upcoming paper
[
BS
]
joint with Berglund or a follow-up) should yield a computation of the stable cohomology of
{\mathrm{B}}\widetilde{\mathrm{Diff}}_{\partial}\big(M^{k,l}_{g,1}\big)
for
3\leq k<l\leq 2k-2
.
This is currently work in progress.
It should significantly simplify and extend a result of Ebert–Reinhold
[
ER22
]
, who compute, in a hands-on way, the stable cohomology of
{\mathrm{B}}\widetilde{\mathrm{Diff}}_{\partial}\big(M^{k,k+1}_{g,1}\big)
up to degree roughly
k
.
From this they deduce, using classical methods as well as a recent result of Krannich
[
Kra22
]
, the stable cohomology of
{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}\big(M^{k,k+1}_{g,1}\big)
in the same range.
A complete calculation of the stable cohomology of block diffeomorphisms would, in the same way, allow to extend this result to degree roughly
2k
.
Moreover, one might hope to extend this calculation even further, for example by using methods recently developed by Krannich–Randal-Williams
[
KR21
]
.
This is the goal of current work in progress joint with Krannich.

One question one might hope to attack with this approach is to determine the images of the cohomology classes coming from the graph complex under the morphism

{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}\big(M^{k,l}_{g,1}\big);{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}\big(M^{k,l}_{g,1}\big);{\mathbb{Q}}\big)

induced by the forgetful map.
When
k=l
, the stable cohomology of the right hand side has been computed completely in celebrated work of Galatius–Randal-Williams
[
GR14
]
, but even in that case it is mostly unknown what the graph cohomology classes map to.
Results in this direction have the potential to yield very interesting information about both the cohomology of the diffeomorphism groups as well as the homology of the graph complex and thus the homology of
\mathrm{Out}(F_{g})
.

Preliminaries

In this section we collect a number of basic conventions, notations, definitions, and lemmas which we will use throughout the rest of this paper.

2.1
Graded vector spaces

Convention 2.1
.

The base field is
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Notation 2.2
.

We denote by
{{\bm{\mathbf{Vect}}}}
the category of vector spaces and linear maps.

Convention 2.3
.

All gradings are by
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Notation 2.4
.

We denote by
{{\bm{\mathbf{GrVect}}}}
the category of graded vector spaces and grading preserving linear maps.
Sometimes we will implicitly consider an ungraded vector space (such as
{\mathbb{Q}}
) as a graded vector space concentrated in degree
0
.

Notation 2.5
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we denote by
{\mathrm{s}^{k}}(-)
a
k
-fold degree shift.
For
V
a graded vector space, the graded vector space
{\mathrm{s}^{k}}V
is given by
({\mathrm{s}^{k}}V)_{n}\coloneqq V_{n-k}
.
When
k=1
, we just write
{\mathrm{s}}\coloneqq{\mathrm{s}^{1}}
.

Notation 2.6
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
A
graded symplectic form
of degree
-m
on a graded vector spaces
V
is a non-degenerate bilinear form of degree
-m

{\langle-,-\rangle}\colon V\otimes V\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}

such that
{\langle v,w\rangle}=(-1)^{|v||w|+1}{\langle w,v\rangle}
(i.e. it is graded anti-symmetric).

We denote by
{{\bm{\mathbf{Sp}}}^{m}}
the category of finite-dimensional graded vector spaces equipped with a graded symplectic form of degree
-m
with morphisms those linear maps of degree
0
that preserve the bilinear form.

Remark 2.7
.

Note that a morphism of
{{\bm{\mathbf{Sp}}}^{m}}
is automatically injective.

2.2
Double complexes

Convention 2.8
.

A
double complex
is a bigraded vector space
C_{*,*}
equipped with a differential
d^{1}
of bidegree
(-1,0)
and a differential
d^{2}
of bidegree
(0,-1)
such that
d^{1}\circ d^{2}=-d^{2}\circ d^{1}
.

Notation 2.9
.

Let
(C_{*,*},d^{1},d^{2})
be a double complex.
We denote by
\Tot C
its
total complex
, i.e. the chain complex with underlying graded vector space

(\Tot C)_{p}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k+l=p}C_{k,l}

and differential
d=d^{1}+d^{2}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
(C_{*,*},d^{1},d^{2})
be a double complex concentrated in non-negative degrees such that
{\operatorname{H}_{*}}(C_{p,*},d^{2})
is concentrated in degree
0
for all
p
.
Denote by

f\colon\Tot C\to\big({\operatorname{H}_{0}}(C_{\bullet,*},d^{2}),d^{1}\big)

the map of chain complexes given by the canonical projections
C_{p,0}\to{\operatorname{H}_{0}}(C_{p,*},d^{2})
and the trivial map on
C_{p,q}
for
q>0
.
Then
f
is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

The map
f
canonically lifts to a map
g\colon C_{*,*}\to\big({\operatorname{H}_{0}}(C_{\bullet,*},d^{2}),d_{1}\big)
of double complexes, where the target is equipped with the double complex structure concentrated in bidegrees
(p,0)
.
The map
g
induces an isomorphism between the
E^{1}
-pages of the spectral sequences associated to these double complexes whose zeroth differential is given by
d^{2}
(see e.g. Weibel
[
Wei95
, §5.6]
).
By the Comparison Theorem for spectral sequences, this implies that
f
is a quasi-isomorphism (see e.g.
[
Wei95
, Theorem 5.2.12]
).
∎

\mathcal{C}
can be equivalently described as a family
(X_{s})_{s\in S}
of objects of
\mathcal{C}
equipped with morphisms
g_{s}\colon X_{s}\to X_{g\mathbin{.}s}
for every
g\in G
and
s\in S
such that they are functorial in the sense that
h_{g\mathbin{.}s}\circ g_{s}=(hg)_{s}
.

Moreover, let
H\trianglelefteq G
be a normal subgroup.
If
\mathcal{C}
is cocomplete, then there is an isomorphism

{\big(\coprod_{s\in S}X_{s}\big)_{H}}\cong\mathop{\operatorname{Lan}_{\mathop{\mathchoice{\textstyle}{}{}{}\int_{G}}S\to{{G}/{H}}}}X

of
({{G}/{H}})
-modules in
\mathcal{C}
.
On the left hand side
g\in G
acts on
\coprod_{s\in S}X_{s}
by mapping
X_{s}
to
X_{g\mathbin{.}s}
via
g_{s}
; the action of
{{G}/{H}}
is the induced action on the coinvariants.
On the right hand side we have the left Kan extension of
X
along the composite
\mathop{\mathchoice{\textstyle}{}{}{}\int_{G}}S\to G\to{{G}/{H}}
of the canonical projection and the quotient map.

Proof.

The first claim follows directly from the definition of the Grothendieck construction.
For the second claim note that the
G
-module
\coprod_{s\in S}X_{s}
is isomorphic to the left Kan extension of
X
along the canonical projection
\mathop{\mathchoice{\textstyle}{}{}{}\int_{G}}S\to G
.
Taking
H
-coinvariants of this corresponds to left Kan extending further along the quotient map
G\to{{G}/{H}}
.
Since Kan extensions compose, the result is isomorphic to the left Kan extension of
X
along the composite
\mathop{\mathchoice{\textstyle}{}{}{}\int_{G}}S\to{{G}/{H}}
.
∎

We will now provide an explicit identification of the left Kan extension in the lemma above.
This takes the form of the following (more general) lemmas.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid,
H
a group, and
F\colon\mathcal{G}\to H
a functor.
Denote by
\mathcal{G}^{F}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
the wide subcategory of those morphisms that
F
maps to the neutral element
e_{H}\in H
.
Also denote by
*
the unique object of
H
considered as a groupoid, and by
F\mathbin{\downarrow}*
the corresponding comma category.
Then there is a fully faithful functor

\Phi_{F}\colon\mathcal{G}^{F}\longrightarrow F\mathbin{\downarrow}*

given by sending an object
G\in\mathcal{G}^{F}
to
(G,e_{H}\colon F(G)=*\to*)
and a morphism
g\colon G\to G^{\prime}
to itself.

Moreover assume that, for all
G\in\mathcal{G}
and
h\in H
, there exists a morphism
g_{G,h}\colon G\to K_{G,h}
in
\mathcal{G}
such that
F(g_{G,h})=h
.
Then
\Phi_{F}
is an equivalence of categories with an inverse (up to natural isomorphism) given by the functor

\Psi_{F}\colon F\mathbin{\downarrow}*\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}^{F}

given on objects by
(G,h)\mapsto K_{G,h}
and on morphisms by
(g\colon(G,h)\to(G^{\prime},h^{\prime}))\mapsto g_{G^{\prime},h^{\prime}}g{g_{G,h}^{-1}}
.
(Note that
\Psi_{F}
depends on the choice of
g_{G,h}
for all
G\in\mathcal{G}
and
h\in H
.)
The natural isomorphism
\operatorname{id}_{F\mathbin{\downarrow}*}\to\Phi_{F}\circ\Psi_{F}
is, at an object
(G,h)
, given by the map
g_{G,h}\colon G\to K_{G,h}
.

Proof.

This follows easily from the definitions.
∎

Lemma 2.18
.

In the situation of the second part of
Lemma

2.17
, let
\mathcal{C}
be a cocomplete category and
X\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{C}
a functor.
Furthermore, for an element
h\in H
, let
A_{h}
denote the functor
\mathcal{G}^{F}\to\mathcal{G}^{F}
that is given by
G\mapsto K_{G,h}
on objects and that sends a morphism

to

.
Then there is an isomorphism

\mathop{\operatorname{Lan}_{F\colon\mathcal{G}\to H}}X\cong\mathinner{\operatorname{colim}\displaylimits\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}^{F}}}(X\circ\operatorname{inc}_{\mathcal{G}^{F}})

of
H
-modules in
\mathcal{C}
.
Here an element

acts on the right hand side via the functor

and the natural transformation
\operatorname{inc}_{\mathcal{G}^{F}}\to{\operatorname{inc}_{\mathcal{G}^{F}}}\circ A_{h}
given, at an object

, by

.

Proof.

The left Kan extension in the statement is, at the unique object
*
of
H
, isomorphic to
\mathinner{\operatorname{colim}\displaylimits_{F\mathbin{\downarrow}*}}(X\circ\operatorname{pr})
, where
\operatorname{pr}\colon F\mathbin{\downarrow}*\to\mathcal{G}
denotes the projection.
The action of an element

on this object is induced by the functor
F\mathbin{\downarrow}*\to F\mathbin{\downarrow}*
given by
(G,h^{\prime})\mapsto(G,hh^{\prime})
(note that this functor commutes with the projection).
That this is isomorphic, as an
H
-module, to the description given in the statement follows from
Lemma

2.17
.
∎

2.5
Symmetric sequences

Definition 2.19
.

We denote by
{{\bm{\mathbf{LinSet}}}}
the category with objects finite sets equipped with a linear order and morphisms the maps of sets (not necessarily respecting the order), by
{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}\coloneqq{\operatorname{Core}({{\bm{\mathbf{LinSet}}}})}

Remark 2.20
.

There exist canonical equivalences of categories
{{\bm{\mathbf{LinSet}}}}\to{{\bm{\mathbf{FinSet}}}}
,
{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}\to{\operatorname{Core}({{\bm{\mathbf{FinSet}}}})}
, and
{{\bm{\mathbf{P}}}}\to{\operatorname{Core}({{\bm{\mathbf{FinSet}}}})}

The inclusion
{{\bm{\mathbf{P}}}}\to{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}

S\in{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}
with an object in
{{\bm{\mathbf{P}}}}
via the unique map that respects the linear order.

Definition 2.21
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a symmetric monoidal category and

.
Then there is a functor

{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}\to\mathcal{V}

.

Similarly, for

and

for

, where

is the unique order-preserving bijection.
We will also use the notation
\bigotimes_{s\in S}v_{s}
to denote elementary tensors of this tensor product.

Definition 2.22
.

For
\mathscr{M}
a
{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}
-module and

a morphism of
{{\bm{\mathbf{LinSet}}}}
, we write

\mathscr{M}(a)\coloneqq\bigotimes_{t\in T}\mathscr{M}\big({a^{-1}}(t)\big)

.
This construction is functorial in

, with the comma category being taken over
{{\bm{\mathbf{LinSet}}}}
.

Definition 2.23
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
and
\mathscr{Q}
be two
{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}
-modules.
Then we can form their
composition product
, i.e. the
{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}
-module
\mathscr{P}\circ\mathscr{Q}
given on objects by

S\longmapsto\mathinner{\operatorname{colim}\displaylimits_{(a\colon S\to T)\in S\mathbin{\downarrow}{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}}}\big(\mathscr{P}(T)\otimes\mathscr{Q}(a)\big)

where the slice category

is taken over
{{\bm{\mathbf{LinSet}}}}
.
A morphism

of

.

2.6

Notation 2.24
.

Let
\mathscr{M}

-module.

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
and
\mathscr{Q}
be
{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}
-modules.
Then there is a natural isomorphism

{(\mathscr{P}\circ\mathscr{Q})[-]}\xlongrightarrow{\cong}{\mathscr{P}[{\mathscr{Q}[-]}]}

We define the isomorphism by sending the element of

2.7
Differential graded Lie algebras

Definition 2.27
.

Let

Its

is the differential graded Lie subalgebra

given by

({L}^{+})_{i}\coloneqq\begin{cases}L_{i},&\text{for }i\geq 2\\
\ker(\delta\colon L_{1}\to L_{0}),&\text{for }i=1\\

where

Definition 2.28
.

Let
L
be a differential graded Lie algebra.
We denote by
{\operatorname{C}^{\mathrm{CE}}_{*}}(L)
its

, i.e. the underlying graded vector space of the free graded commutative algebra
\mathrm{\Lambda}({\mathrm{s}}L)
, equipped with the differential

, where

\displaystyle d_{0}({\mathrm{s}}x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{\mathrm{s}}x_{k})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{b_{i}}{\mathrm{s}}x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{\mathrm{s}}\delta(x_{i})\wedge\dots\wedge{\mathrm{s}}x_{k}

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 1+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}|{\mathrm{s}}x_{j}|

\displaystyle d_{1}({\mathrm{s}}x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{\mathrm{s}}x_{k})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{b_{i,j}}{\mathrm{s}}{[x_{i},x_{j}]}\wedge{\mathrm{s}}x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{\widehat{{\mathrm{s}}x_{i}}}\wedge\dots\wedge{\widehat{{\mathrm{s}}x_{j}}}\wedge\dots\wedge{\mathrm{s}}x_{k}

\displaystyle b_{i,j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq|{\mathrm{s}}x_{i}|(1+|{\mathrm{s}}x_{j}|)+|{\mathrm{s}}x_{i}|\sum_{l=1}^{i-1}|{\mathrm{s}}x_{l}|+|{\mathrm{s}}x_{j}|\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}|{\mathrm{s}}x_{l}|

where
\delta
is the differential of

The Chevalley–Eilenberg complex comes equipped with the structure of a cocommutative differential graded coalgebra.
Its counit
\varepsilon\colon{\operatorname{C}^{\mathrm{CE}}_{*}}(L)\to{\mathbb{Q}}
is given by

on the empty wedge and

on all higher wedges.
The comultiplication
\Delta\colon{\operatorname{C}^{\mathrm{CE}}_{*}}(L)\to{\operatorname{C}^{\mathrm{CE}}_{*}}(L)\otimes{\operatorname{C}^{\mathrm{CE}}_{*}}(L)

\Delta({\mathrm{s}}x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{\mathrm{s}}x_{k})\coloneqq\sum_{A\subseteq{\underline{k}}}\varepsilon(A)(\textstyle\bigwedge_{a\in A}{\mathrm{s}}x_{a})\otimes(\textstyle\bigwedge_{b\in{\underline{k}}\setminus A}{\mathrm{s}}x_{b})

where the sum runs over all subsets

{\underline{k}}=\{1,\dots,k\}

Here
\varepsilon(A)\in\{\pm 1\}
is the sign incurred by permuting

{\mathrm{s}}x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes{\mathrm{s}}x_{k}
into
{\textstyle\bigotimes}_{a\in A}{\mathrm{s}}x_{a}\otimes{\textstyle\bigotimes}_{b\in{\underline{k}}\setminus A}{\mathrm{s}}x_{b}
.

Moreover, we write
{\operatorname{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{CE}}}(L)
for the
Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology
of

, i.e. the cohomology of the linear dual of
{\operatorname{C}^{\mathrm{CE}}_{*}}(L)
, equipped with its graded commutative algebra structure.

Lemma 2.29
.

Let
\mathrm{\Lambda}{\mathrm{s}}
denote the
{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}
-module given by
\mathrm{\Lambda}{\mathrm{s}}(S)=({\mathrm{s}}{\mathbb{Q}})^{\otimes S}
.
Then there is a canonical isomorphism of graded vector spaces, natural in

\displaystyle{(\mathrm{\Lambda}{\mathrm{s}})[V]}

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{\Lambda}({\mathrm{s}}V)

\displaystyle{[({\mathrm{s}}1)^{\otimes k}\otimes(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{k})]}

3
The directed graph complex

In this section our goal is to prove a version of

That is, we want to identify (in a stable range)

{\Big({\operatorname{C}^{\mathrm{CE}}_{*}}\big({{({\mathrm{s}^{-(k+l-2)}}\mathscr{\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie}})\big[{\mathrm{s}^{-1}}{\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}_{*}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}})\big]}}^{+}\big)\otimes Q\Big)_{\Gamma_{g}^{\mathbb{Q}}}}

\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}
-module, and the graded Lie algebra structure on the Schur functor is obtained from

To this end, we begin, in the first two subsections, with studying

\mathscr{P}
is a
{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}
-module,
H
is an object of
{{\bm{\mathbf{Sp}}}^{m}}

-representation.
From the results we obtain for this situation we will, via

Identifying the result in this case with its induced

[

.

3.1
Coinvariants of monomial functors

In this subsection, we let

Moreover, we assume that

We write

Let

{\big(({{P}^{\mathrm{op}}}\otimes_{{\Sigma_{S}}}H^{\otimes S})\otimes V^{\otimes I}\otimes W^{\otimes J}\big)_{\Gamma}}

Lemma 3.1
.

The map

0

After identifying

with
{\mathrm{s}^{m}}{V^{\vee}}
via this isomorphism the inner product on

{\langle(v,{\mathrm{s}^{m}}\phi),(v^{\prime},{\mathrm{s}^{m}}\phi^{\prime})\rangle}=\phi(v^{\prime})-(-1)^{n(m-n)}\phi^{\prime}(v)

Proof.

The composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\oplus W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(V\oplus W)^{\vee}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V^{\vee}}\oplus{W^{\vee}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\langle h,-\rangle}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.{\phi}}}\right|_{V}},{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.{\phi}}}\right|_{W}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

is a degree
-m

\begin{pmatrix}0&f\\
f^{\prime}&0\end{pmatrix}

since
{\langle v,v^{\prime}\rangle}=0={\langle w,w^{\prime}\rangle}

In particular

Noting that

The second follows from

.
∎

Using the preceding lemma we will, in the following, implicitly identify

with

.
We now give a description of
\Gamma
in these terms.

Lemma 3.2
.

The map
{\operatorname{GL}}(V)\to\operatorname{Aut}(V\oplus{\mathrm{s}^{m}}{V^{\vee}},{\langle-,-\rangle})=\Gamma
given by
f\mapsto f\oplus{({f^{-1}})^{\vee}}
is an isomorphism of groups.
(Here we implicitly identify automorphisms of
{\mathrm{s}^{m}}{V^{\vee}}
with automorphisms of
{V^{\vee}}
.)

Proof.

Let
f\in{\operatorname{GL}}(V)
.
Then the computation

\displaystyle\;{\langle{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(f(v),{({f^{-1}})^{\vee}}({\mathrm{s}^{m}}\phi)}}\right)},{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(f(v^{\prime}),{({f^{-1}})^{\vee}}({\mathrm{s}^{m}}\phi^{\prime})}}\right)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(\phi\circ{f^{-1}})(f(v^{\prime}))-(-1)^{n(m-n)}(\phi^{\prime}\circ{f^{-1}})(f(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\phi(v^{\prime})-(-1)^{n(m-n)}\phi^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;{\langle(v,{\mathrm{s}^{m}}\phi),(v^{\prime},{\mathrm{s}^{m}}\phi^{\prime})\rangle}

shows that the map in the statement is actually well-defined.
It is clearly injective.
We will now show that it is also surjective.
For this purpose let

, where
f\in{\operatorname{GL}}(V)
and
g\in{\operatorname{GL}}({\mathrm{s}^{m}}{V^{\vee}})
.
By using that

as well, we obtain

and hence

.
This shows surjectivity.
∎

By basic linear algebra, there is a canonical isomorphism of graded vector spaces

(here

is concentrated in degree

We will now use this to identify
{(H^{\otimes S}\otimes V^{\otimes I}\otimes W^{\otimes J})_{\Gamma}}
in terms of

.
To make this precise, we need to introduce some notation.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
S
be a finite linearly ordered set,
A
and
B

into two subsets), and

and

Then we denote by

the tensor product
\bigotimes_{s\in S}V_{s}
where

if

and
V_{s}\coloneqq V_{2}
if

.

A morphism

of
{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}
induces a map

f_{*}\colon(V_{1},V_{2})^{\otimes(A,B)}\to(V_{1},V_{2})^{\otimes(f(A),f(B))}

by permuting the factors.
When

is concentrated in degree
n
and
V_{2}
in degree

, we denote by
\operatorname{sgn}_{A,B}(f)\in\{\pm 1\}

f_{*}({\textstyle\bigotimes}_{s\in S}v_{s})=\operatorname{sgn}_{A,B}(f)\cdot{\textstyle\bigotimes}_{s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}}v_{{f^{-1}}(s^{\prime})}

holds.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
S
be a finite linearly ordered set.
We denote by
\operatorname{OM}(S)
the set of
ordered matchings
of
S
, i.e. (ordered) pairs
(A,B)
such that
A
and
B
are disjoint subsets of
S
with
|A|=|B|
and
A\mathbin{\mathaccent 0{\cdot}\cup}B=S
.
(In particular
\operatorname{OM}(S)

is odd.)
This assembles into a functor
\operatorname{OM}\colon{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}\to{{\bm{\mathbf{Set}}}}
by letting a morphism

(A,B)\mapsto(f(A),f(B))
.

Also note that the linear order of
S
restricts to a linear order on
A
and a linear order on

.
In particular there is a unique order-preserving bijection

.
We will denote this bijection by
\mu_{A,B}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
S
,
I
, and
J
be finite linearly ordered sets.
We denote by
\operatorname{OM}(S,I,J)\subseteq\operatorname{OM}(S\amalg I\amalg J)
the subset of those ordered matchings

such that

and
J\subseteq B
.
This assembles into a functor
\operatorname{OM}\colon{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}\times{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}\times{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}\to{{\bm{\mathbf{Set}}}}

act via

.

Remark 3.6
.

Note that there is a canonical bijection
\operatorname{OM}(S,I,J)\cong\operatorname{OM}(S)
, which is natural in
{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}\times{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}\times{{\bm{\mathbf{\Sigma}}}}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
S
,
I
, and

be finite linearly ordered sets.
Then there is an isomorphism of

-modules

where

acts on

via the conjugation
g\mathbin{.}f=gf{g^{-1}}
, and diagonally on the tensor products.
The

-module structure on the left hand side is given as follows: a morphism
(f,g,h)
of
{\Sigma_{S}}\times{\Sigma_{I}}\times{\Sigma_{J}}
maps the factor corresponding to
(A,B)\in\operatorname{OM}(S,I,J)
into the factor corresponding to
(c(A),c(B))
via the map given by

{\mathrm{s}^{m|A|}}{[{\textstyle\bigotimes}_{a\in A}\operatorname{E}_{i_{a},j_{a}}]}\longmapsto{\mathrm{s}^{m|A|}}{[\operatorname{sgn}_{A,B}(c)\cdot{\textstyle\bigotimes}_{a^{\prime}\in c(A)}\operatorname{E}_{i_{{\sigma^{-1}}(a^{\prime})},j_{{\tau^{-1}}(a^{\prime})}}]}

There is a canonical isomorphism

Now let

Then it is clear that the isomorphism

Lastly note that there is a

-module isomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi\colon{\mathrm{s}^{m}}{V^{\vee}}\otimes V

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathrm{s}^{m}}{\mathop{\operatorname{End}}(V)}

\displaystyle{\mathrm{s}^{m}}\phi\otimes v

\displaystyle\longmapsto{\mathrm{s}^{m}}(v^{\prime}\mapsto\phi(v^{\prime})v)

4
The undirected graph complex

Throughout this section, we let
m\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a natural number and
\mathscr{C}
a cyclic operad concentrated in non-negative even degrees such that
{\mathscr{C}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mkern-4.0mu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2}}\right)\mkern-4.0mu}}\right)}={{\mathbb{Q}}{\langle\operatorname{id}\rangle}}
with the trivial
{\Sigma_{2}}
-action.
(As in the previous section, the condition that

is concentrated in even degrees is purely for the convenience of avoiding more signs.)

Our goal is to obtain a simpler description of the homology of the truncated directed graph complex
{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}
of
Definition

3.31
.
In fact, we will obtain a description in terms of

The rough idea is as follows.
Restricting the differential of
{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}
to the two edges incident to a vertex of valence two (which must be labeled by the identity operation by our assumption on
\mathscr{C}
) yields the following picture.
\xi_{1}
\operatorname{id}
\xi_{2}
\longmapsto
\xi_{1}
\xi_{2}
+
\xi_{1}
\xi_{2}

Thus, intuitively, taking homology should kill the difference between the two orientations of an edge.
We formalize this using a spectral sequence argument: filtering
{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
by the number of vertices of valence two yields a spectral sequence, for which we prove that its first page is concentrated in a single row.
We moreover show that this row is isomorphic as a chain complex to a (well-known) undirected version of the graph complex, which we denote by
{\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}
.
The spectral sequence then implies that there is an isomorphism
{\operatorname{H}_{*}}({\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})})\cong{\operatorname{H}_{*}}({\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\mathscr{C})})
.
(This argument is similar to a proof sketched by Willwacher
[
Wil14
, Appendix K]
.)

Remark 4.1
.

There should also be a version of everything we do in this section in the more general situation of the truncated directed
(I,J)
-graph complex
{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}_{I,J}
.
We will not elaborate on this here, though.
It might appear in future work.

We begin by noting that the degree

{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}
splits as a direct sum

{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}_{k}=\bigoplus_{l\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}_{k,l}

where
{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}_{k,l}
is the subspace generated by elements represented by truncated decorated graphs with exactly
l
vertices of valence two.
Note that a truncated decorated graph with
N
vertices and

edges represents an element of homological degree

(since
\mathscr{C}
is concentrated in non-negative degrees).
Since all of its vertices are at least bivalent we have

and hence

(since

).
Thus, in the above decomposition, a summand is trivial if

and hence the sum is finite.

The differential restricts to a map

so that regrading via

yields a double complex

with a differential

of bidegree

and a differential

follows immediately from

.
The total complex of

is canonically isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}
.
Moreover

.
Hence we obtain a convergent spectral sequence of the following form

(see e.g.
[

).

We will use this spectral sequence to identify the homology of

However we will need a few preliminaries before we can do so, beginning with an undirected version of

Definition 4.2

We denote by

•

Remark 4.24
.

The
(undirected) graph complex

{\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}
, equipped with the differential graded coalgebra structure of

Variants of it were first described by Kontsevich
[

Kon94
]
and have, since then, appeared in the works of many different authors.
Using the language of modular operads due to Getzler–Kapranov
[

]
, one alternative description is

{{\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}^{\vee}}\cong\mathsf{F}_{\mathfrak{T}^{1-m}}(\Sigma^{1-m}\mathscr{C})\cong\Sigma^{m-1}\mathsf{F}_{\mathrm{Det}^{1-m}}(\mathscr{C})

denotes the “Feynman transform”,

and

are certain “hyperoperads”, and

Other descriptions have been given for example by Lazarev–Voronov
[

(though with a different grading convention) and, in the case

[

, §2.3]
.

The homology of
{\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}
is in general very complicated and in most cases of interest only partial information is known.
See, however, the following remarks for a couple of basic observations.
Also see
Remark

4.30
for an overview of what is known about this homology in the case of the cyclic Lie operad

.

Remark 4.25
.

Note that, up to a regrading, the differential graded coalgebra
{\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}

Remark 4.26
.

It follows from the description of the coalgebra structure of
{\mathfrak{UG}^{m}(\mathscr{C})}
that the cohomology

of its linear dual is a free graded commutative algebra generated by the cohomology of the dual of the subcomplex

spanned by the connected graphs.
This subcomplex splits as a direct sum

Remark 4.27

Let

Reduction to invariants

In this subsection our goal is to prove that there is an isomorphism of bigraded algebras

{\operatorname{H}^{p}}\big(\Gamma_{g};{\operatorname{H}^{q}_{\mathrm{CE}}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)\cong{\operatorname{H}^{p}}(\Gamma_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\Big(\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)^{\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}}\Big)

in a stable range of degrees.
This will mainly rely on a result of Li–Sun
[
LS19
]
.
We need some preliminaries.

Definition 5.4
.

A
Schur bifunctor
is a functor
F\colon{{\bm{\mathbf{Vect}}}}\times{{\bm{\mathbf{Vect}}}}\to{{\bm{\mathbf{Vect}}}}
of the form

F(V_{1},V_{2})=\bigoplus_{n_{1},n_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\mathscr{M}(n_{1},n_{2})\otimes_{{\Sigma_{n_{1}}}\times{\Sigma_{n_{2}}}}(V_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes n_{2}})

where the
\mathscr{M}(n_{1},n_{2})
are some right
({\Sigma_{n_{1}}}\times{\Sigma_{n_{2}}})
-modules in
{{\bm{\mathbf{Vect}}}}
.
We say
F
has
degree
\leq d
if
\mathscr{M}(n_{1},n_{2})\cong 0
for all pairs
(n_{1},n_{2})
such that
n_{1}+n_{2}>d
.
We say
F
is of
finite type
if each
\mathscr{M}(n_{1},n_{2})
is finite dimensional.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
F
be a finite type Schur bifunctor of degree
\leq d
.
Then the canonical inclusion

F(V,{V^{\vee}})^{{\operatorname{GL}}(V)}\longrightarrow F(V,{V^{\vee}})^{{\operatorname{SL}}(V)}

is an isomorphism for all finite-dimensional vector spaces
V
such that
\dim V>d
.
Here
f\in{\operatorname{GL}}(V)
acts on
{V^{\vee}}
by
{({f^{-1}})^{\vee}}
and diagonally on
F(V,{V^{\vee}})
.

Proof.

Using, for
G
a group and
W
a
G
-module, the natural (in both
W
and
G
) isomorphism
{({W_{G}})^{\vee}}\cong({W^{\vee}})^{G}
, we can pass to the dual situation, i.e. take coinvariants instead of invariants.
(To see that
{F(V,{V^{\vee}})^{\vee}}
is again a Schur bifunctor, we use that
\Sigma\coloneqq{\Sigma_{n_{1}}}\times{\Sigma_{n_{2}}}
is finite and that we are working in characteristic zero, so that
{(M\otimes_{\Sigma}N)^{\vee}}\cong{M^{\vee}}\otimes_{\Sigma}{N^{\vee}}
.)

Both the
{\operatorname{GL}}(V)
-action and the
{\operatorname{SL}}(V)
-action are compatible with the direct sum composition of
F(V,{V^{\vee}})
, so that it is enough to prove for a single summand

W\coloneqq\mathscr{M}(n_{1},n_{2})\otimes_{{\Sigma_{n_{1}}}\times{\Sigma_{n_{2}}}}\big(V^{\otimes n_{1}}\otimes({V^{\vee}})^{\otimes n_{2}}\big)

with
n_{1}+n_{2}<\dim V
that
{W_{{\operatorname{SL}}(V)}}\to{W_{{\operatorname{GL}}(V)}}
is an isomorphism.
Denote by
e_{1},\dots,e_{g}
some basis of
V
.
Then the equivalence relation defining
{W_{{\operatorname{GL}}(V)}}
is generated by

m\otimes\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n_{1}}e_{i_{j}}\otimes\bigotimes_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n_{2}}e^{\#}_{i^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}}\sim m\otimes\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n_{1}}f(e_{i_{j}})\otimes\bigotimes_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n_{2}}{({f^{-1}})^{\vee}}(e^{\#}_{i^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}})

(5.1)

for all
f\in{\operatorname{GL}}(V)
,
m\in\mathscr{M}(n_{1},n_{2})
, and
1\leq i_{j},i^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}\leq g
.
Choose some
1\leq i\leq g
such that
i\neq i_{j},i^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}
for all
j
and
j^{\prime}
(this is possible by our assumption that
\dim V>n_{1}+n_{2}
).
Now let
f^{\prime}\coloneqq f\circ h\in{\operatorname{GL}}(V)
where
h
is defined by
h(e_{i})={(\det f)^{-1}}e_{i}
and
h(e_{j})=e_{j}
for all
j\neq i
.
Then
f^{\prime}\in{\operatorname{SL}}(V)
and it yields the same relation (
5.1
) as
f
did.
Hence
{W_{{\operatorname{SL}}(V)}}\to{W_{{\operatorname{GL}}(V)}}
is injective.
Since it is clearly surjective, it is thus an isomorphism.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this subsection.
See
[
BM20
, Theorem 8.4]
for the analogous statement in the case
k=l
; the stable range occurring in their result has been improved by Krannich
[
Kra20
]
using an argument similar to the one we will employ.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
Q
be a finite type Schur bifunctor of degree
\leq r
and set
P\coloneqq Q\big({\operatorname{H}_{k}}(M_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}}),{\operatorname{H}_{l}}(M_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}})\big)
as a
\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}
-module.
Then the canonical map of bigraded vector spaces

{\operatorname{H}^{p}}(\Gamma_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\Big(\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)^{\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}}\Big)\cong{\operatorname{H}^{p}}\Big(\Gamma_{g};{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\Big(\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)^{\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}}\Big)\Big)\longrightarrow\\
{\operatorname{H}^{p}}\Big(\Gamma_{g};{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)\Big)\cong{\operatorname{H}^{p}}\big(\Gamma_{g};{\operatorname{H}^{q}_{\mathrm{CE}}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)

is an isomorphism for
g>{\max}\big(p+1,\frac{1}{2}(q+1)+r\big)
.

Proof.

A result of Li–Sun
[
LS19
, Example 1.10]
implies that the canonical map

{\operatorname{H}^{p}}\Big(\Gamma_{g};{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)^{\Gamma_{g}}\Big)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{H}^{p}}\Big(\Gamma_{g};{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)\Big)

is an isomorphism for
g\geq p+2
.
This uses that
[
Gre19
, (Proof of) Proposition 7.1]
implies that
{\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{q}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})
, and hence also
{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)
, is a finite dimensional rational representation of the reductive group
{\operatorname{GL}_{g}}
and thus completely reducible (see e.g.
[
Mil17
, Theorem 22.42]
).

Now, by
[
Gre19
, Lemma 3.4]
, the group
\Gamma_{g}
is rationally perfect (cf.
[
BM20
, Definiton B.3]
).
Hence, taking
\Gamma_{g}
-invariants is exact when restricted to the category of finite dimensional representations.
In particular the canonical map

{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\Big(\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)^{\Gamma_{g}}\Big)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P\big)^{\Gamma_{g}}

is an isomorphism.

Lastly we use that, by a result of Borel
[
Bor66
, Theorem 1]
, the arithmetic subgroup
{\operatorname{SL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Z}})
is Zariski dense in
{\operatorname{SL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Q}})
(note that the condition of the theorem is fulfilled since
{\operatorname{SL}_{g}}
is almost simple for
g\geq 2
).
Hence we have
V^{{\operatorname{SL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Z}})}=V^{{\operatorname{SL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Q}})}
for any rational representation
V
of
{\operatorname{SL}_{g}}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
In particular, for
F
a finite type Schur bifunctor of degree
\leq d
and
V\coloneqq F({\mathbb{Q}}^{g},{({\mathbb{Q}}^{g})^{\vee}})
, we thus have, when
g>d
, that

V^{{\operatorname{GL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Z}})}=\big(V^{{\operatorname{SL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Z}})}\big)^{{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}}}=\big(V^{{\operatorname{SL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Q}})}\big)^{{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}}}=\big(V^{{\operatorname{GL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Q}})}\big)^{{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}}}=V^{{\operatorname{GL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Q}})}

where the third equality uses
Lemma

5.5
.
Now we note that, by
[
Gre19
, (Proof of) Proposition 7.1]
, there exists a finite type Schur bifunctor
F_{q}
of degree
\leq\frac{q}{2}
such that there is an isomorphism

{\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{q}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\cong F_{q}\big({\operatorname{H}_{k}}(M_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}}),{\operatorname{H}_{l}}(M_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}})\big)

of
\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}
-modules (where the action on the right hand side is diagonally).
Hence
{\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{q}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes P
is, in the same way, the value of a Schur bifunctor of degree
\leq\frac{q}{2}+r
.
This finishes the proof.
For the condition on
g
given in the statement, note that an isomorphism of chain complexes in a range induces an isomorphism of homology in a range smaller by
1
.
∎

5.2
The main theorem

We are now ready to deduce our main theorem from everything we have done so far.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
3\leq k<l\leq 2k-2
and
2\leq g
be integers, and let
I
and
J
be finite linearly ordered sets.
Furthermore, set
H_{g;i}\coloneqq{\operatorname{H}_{i}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}})
as a
{\operatorname{\pi}_{0}}\big({{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1})\big)
-module.
Then there is, in cohomological degrees

\leq\begin{cases}g-2,&\text{when $|I|+|J|\leq\frac{1}{2}g$}\\
2(g-|I|-|J|-1),&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

an isomorphism of graded
({\Sigma_{I}}\times{\Sigma_{J}})
-modules

{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1});H_{g;l}^{\otimes I}\otimes H_{g;k}^{\otimes J}\big)\cong\\
{\mathrm{s}^{(k-1)|I|+(l-1)|J|}}{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\otimes{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({({\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}_{I,J})^{\vee}}\big)\otimes\operatorname{sgn}_{I}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\operatorname{sgn}_{J}^{\otimes l-1}

compatible with the stabilization maps on the left hand side (here the shift refers to the cohomological grading).
It is an isomorphism of algebras when
I=J=\varnothing
, and an isomorphism of modules over these algebras for general
I
and
J
.
Here
{\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}})\coloneqq\mathinner{\operatorname{colim}\displaylimits_{g\in{\mathbb{N}}}}{\operatorname{GL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
\operatorname{sgn}_{S}
is the sign representation of
{\Sigma_{S}}
.
Also recall that
{\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}_{I,J}
is the truncated directed
(I,J)
-graph complex (see
Definition

3.31
) associated to the cyclic Lie operad.

In particular, after stabilizing, we obtain an isomorphism

\mathinner{\operatorname{lim}\displaylimits_{g\in{\mathbb{N}}}}{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1});H_{g;l}^{\otimes I}\otimes H_{g;k}^{\otimes J}\big)\cong\\
{\mathrm{s}^{(k-1)|I|+(l-1)|J|}}{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\otimes{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({({\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}_{I,J})^{\vee}}\big)\otimes\operatorname{sgn}_{I}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\operatorname{sgn}_{J}^{\otimes l-1}

of graded
({\Sigma_{I}}\times{\Sigma_{J}})
-modules.
It is an isomorphism of algebras when
I=J=\varnothing
, and an isomorphism of modules over these algebras for general
I
and
J
.

Proof.

Combining
Propositions

5.3
and
5.6
, we obtain, in the stable range

g>\max_{p+q=s}{\max}\big(p+1,\tfrac{1}{2}(q+1)+|I|+|J|\big)={\max}\big(s+1,\tfrac{1}{2}(s+1)+|I|+|J|\big)

an isomorphism of graded
({\Sigma_{I}}\times{\Sigma_{J}})
-modules

{\operatorname{H}^{s}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1});H_{g;l}^{\otimes I}\otimes H_{g;k}^{\otimes J}\big)\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=s}{\operatorname{H}^{p}}(\Gamma_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes{\operatorname{H}^{q}}\Big(\big({\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes H_{g;l}^{\otimes I}\otimes H_{g;k}^{\otimes J}\big)^{\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}}\Big)

which is compatible with the stabilization maps and the algebra/module structures.
It follows from work of Borel
[
Bor74
]
(see also
[
KMP22
, Theorem A]
3
3

3

They actually prove a slightly better range than Borel, which we will not need.
) that the canonical map
{\operatorname{H}^{p}}({\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}})\to{\operatorname{H}^{p}}({\operatorname{GL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}})
is an isomorphism for
g\geq p+2
; in particular we can replace
{\operatorname{H}^{p}}(\Gamma_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})\cong{\operatorname{H}^{p}}({\operatorname{GL}_{g}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}})
with
{\operatorname{H}^{p}}({\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}})
in the formula above.

Now, by
[
BM20
, Proposition 6.6]
, there is an isomorphism of Lie algebras

\mathfrak{g}_{g}={{{\mathrm{Der}_{\omega}}}\big({\mathbb{L}}({\mathrm{s}^{-1}}{\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}_{*}}(M_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}}))\big)}^{+}\cong{{({\mathrm{s}^{-(k+l-2)}}\mathscr{\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie}})\big[{\mathrm{s}^{-1}}{\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}_{*}}(M_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}})\big]}}^{+}

that is compatible with the
\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}
-actions and the stabilization maps.
Here the right hand side is equipped with the graded Lie algebra structure of
Lemma

2.34
, where
{\mathrm{s}^{-1}}{\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}_{*}}(M_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}})
is equipped with the anti-symmetric pairing given by
{\langle{\mathrm{s}^{-1}}a,{\mathrm{s}^{-1}}b\rangle}=(-1)^{|a|+1}{{\langle a,b\rangle_{\cap}}}
.
Then
Corollary

3.33
implies that, in degrees
q\leq\frac{4}{3}g-1
if
l=2k-2
or
q\leq 2g-1
if
l\neq 2k-2
, there is an isomorphism of graded
({\Sigma_{I}}\times{\Sigma_{J}})
-modules

{\operatorname{H}_{q}}\Big({{\big({\operatorname{C}^{\mathrm{CE}}_{*}}(\mathfrak{g}_{g})\otimes({\mathrm{s}^{k-1}}H_{g;k})^{\otimes I}\otimes({\mathrm{s}^{l-1}}H_{g;l})^{\otimes J}\big)_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}}}}\Big)\cong{\operatorname{H}_{q}}\big({\mathfrak{DG}_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}_{I,J}\big)

compatible with the stabilization maps on the left hand side as well as the coalgebra/comodule structures.
(Note that an isomorphism of chain complexes up to degree
p
induces an isomorphism on homology up to degree
p-1
.)
Dualizing this yields the desired statement since there is an isomorphism

({\mathrm{s}^{k-1}}H_{g;k})^{\otimes I}\otimes({\mathrm{s}^{l-1}}H_{g;l})^{\otimes J}\cong{\mathrm{s}^{(k-1)|I|+(l-1)|J|}}H_{g;k}^{\otimes I}\otimes H_{g;l}^{\otimes J}\otimes\operatorname{sgn}_{I}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\operatorname{sgn}_{J}^{\otimes l-1}

of
(\Gamma^{\mathbb{Q}}_{g}\times{\Sigma_{I}}\times{\Sigma_{J}})
-modules and
H_{g;k}
and
H_{g;l}
are dual to each other.
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
3\leq k<l\leq 2k-2
and
2\leq g
be integers.
Then there is, in cohomological degrees
\leq g-2
, an isomorphism of graded algebras

{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\cong{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\otimes{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({{\mathfrak{UG}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}^{\vee}}\big)

(Recall that
{\mathfrak{UG}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}
denotes the undirected graph complex associated to the cyclic Lie operad, see
Definition

4.22
.)

In particular, after stabilizing, we obtain an isomorphism

\mathinner{\operatorname{lim}\displaylimits_{g\in{\mathbb{N}}}}{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\mathrm{B}}{{\mathrm{aut}_{\partial}}}(M^{k,l}_{g,1});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\cong{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({\operatorname{GL}}({\mathbb{Z}});{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\otimes{\operatorname{H}^{*}}\big({{\mathfrak{UG}^{k+l-2}(\operatorname{\mathscr{L}ie})}^{\vee}}\big)

of graded algebras.

Proof.

This follows from
Theorem

5.7
with
I=J=\varnothing
and
Theorem

4.28
.

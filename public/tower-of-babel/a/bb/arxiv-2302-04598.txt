Equivariant Euler characteristics on permutohedral varieties
Vincenzo Galgano, Hanieh Keneshlou, Mateusz Michałek

Abstract
By the work of J. Huh, one can interpret binomial coefficients as a solution to an intersection problem on a permutohedral variety X E X_{E} . Applying Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch, this intersection problem is equivalent to computing Euler characteristic of a specific element of K K -theory of X E X_{E} . This element has a natural lifting to equivariant K K -theory and thus the Euler characteristic may be upgraded to a Laurent polynomial. We provide and implement three different approaches, in particular a recursive one, to computing these polynomials.



1
Introduction

Binomial coefficients have played a very important role in mathematics for over thousand years. Indeed, the first known written exposition of what we now call Pascal triangle comes from an Indian mathematician Halayudha from 10-th century. In this article, we study naturally defined multivariate Laurent polynomials which are extensions of binomial coefficients: evaluated at
(1,\dots,1)
they give binomial coefficients.

The motivation for our work comes from a modern, groundbreaking work of June Huh
[
Huh12
]
, who related basic combinatorial invariants of graphs and matroids with multidegree of complex projective algebraic varieties, and more precisely with intersection theory on a permutohedral variety. To any
\mathbb{C}
-representable matroid
M
on a set
E
of cardinality
n+1
one associates a class
Y_{M}
in the Chow ring of an
n
-dimensional permutohedral variety
X_{E}
, the latter being the blow-up of the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
at its torus invariant linear subspaces, or equivalently the resolution of the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
at the Cremona singularities. In particular,
X_{E}
comes with two projections to
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and we consider the divisors
\Gamma
and
\Delta
given by the pullback of a hyperplane under the respective projections. If we fix a representation of
M
, we may consider
Y_{M}
as an irreducible subvariety of
X_{E}
. By
[
Huh12
,
AHK18
]
the absolute value of the
a
-th coefficient of the reduced chromatic polynomial
\chi_{M}
of
M
is equal to
\int_{X_{E}}Y_{M}[\Gamma]^{a}[\Delta]^{r-a}
, where the product is taken in the Chow ring
A(X_{E})
of
X_{E}
and
r
is the rank of
M
.

In fact, in
[
AHK18
]
the authors associate to any matroid a ring, called the Chow ring, and prove (analogue of) Hard Lefschetz theorem and Hodge-Riemann relations for it. This major breakthrough allowed, among others, to prove log-concavity conjectures for coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of a matroid. This was a generalization of the case of matroids representable over
\mathbb{C}
, where the Chow ring was indeed a Chow ring of a complex, compact variety and Lefschetz theorem was known
[
Huh12
]
. In this article we focus our attention on other properties of this ring, looking at intersections with pull-backs of hyperplane divisors from the equivariant perspective and through the isomorphism of the Chow ring with the
K^{0}
-theory ring given by the Chern character.

Arguably, the simplest case of this theory is when
M
is a uniform matroid of rank
r
. In this case, the absolute value of the
i
-th coefficient (from the top) of the reduced chromatic polynomial equals
\binom{n}{i}
. Further, the class
Y_{M}
is simply given as a power of
[\Gamma]
. We thus obtain

\int_{X_{E}}[\Gamma]^{a}\cdot[\Delta]^{n-a}={n\choose a}\ .

Starting from the above equality, our aim is to take one step further and take into account the natural torus action on
X_{E}
. The key for this is the passage from the Chow-theoretic expression of this intersection to the
K
-theoretic expression of it in the Grothendieck ring
K^{0}(X_{E})
via the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch formula
\chi(\mathcal{F})=\int_{X_{E}}ch(\mathcal{F})\Td(X_{E})
. This leads in our case to

\chi\left(([\mathcal{O}_{X_{E}}]-[\mathcal{O}_{X_{E}}(-\Gamma)])^{\otimes a}\otimes([\mathcal{O}_{X_{E}}]-[\mathcal{O}_{X_{E}}(-\Delta)])^{\otimes n-a}\right)=\int_{X_{E}}[\Gamma]^{a}\cdot[\Delta]^{n-a}\ .

(1.1)

We note that all of the line bundles on the left-hand side come with a natural torus action.
Thus our computation may be upgraded to the computation of the equivariant Euler characteristic of a certain class in the equivariant Grothendieck ring
K^{0}_{T}(X_{E})
. This is now a symmetric Laurent polynomial that evaluates to the given binomial coefficient on
(1,\dots,1)
. The polynomials we obtain are highly non-trivial and we provide three different ways to compute them. The challenge to compute the given Euler characteristic lead us, among others, to proving various vanishing theorems, exact formulas for equivariant cohomology groups for various bundles on the permutohedral variety as well as exhibiting relations to other mathematical object, like Stirling numbers of the second kind. We find these results interesting on their own and plan to exploit them further in future.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we start by recalling the construction of the permutohedral variety and the description of important divisor classes on it. We also briefly recall the equivariant localization formula and discuss in detail the case of the projective line. In Section
3
, we start by proving the vanishings of the intermediate cohomology groups for various sheaves, which simplifies the expression of the Euler characteristic. Here our main tools are toric Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing and Batyrev-Borisov vanishing. Combined with equivariant Serre duality, we compute the equivariant Euler characteristics as integral combinations of global section of toric line bundles, cf. formula (
3.4
). This results in a direct, albeit long, formula where the final Laurent polynomial is a result of identifying lattice points with Laurent monomials and summing over various polytopes. It could be seen as an analogue of the formula
[
MMMSV20
, Theorem 3.11]
, where the variety of complete quadrics is considered, while we deal with the permutohedral variety. Our second approach is based on using the so-called equivariant localization formula. Here the final result is presented as a sum of rational functions, cf. formula (
3.10
). In Section
4
we provide a recursive formula that reduces the computation of the Euler characteristic to the computation of classes on lower dimensional permutohedra. It could be regarded as an equivariant analog of the formulas in the proof of
[
BEST21
, Lemma I.1]
. However, there is one central difference: in non-equivariant Chow ring intersecting more divisors than the dimension of the variety always gives zero, and thus such contributions disappear; in the equivariant Chow ring, we obtain several contributions related to intersecting more divisors than the dimension of the variety.

The three presented approaches are implemented in the computer algebra system
Macaulay2

[
GS
]
and they are available under
EquivariantEC.m2
as supplementary material to this article.
Finally, we would like to point out that our approach to upgrading important numeric invariants to polynomials is certainly not the only possibility. In particular, in the recent work
[
BEST21
]
there is an explicit formula for
Y_{M}
in the equivariant Chow ring. We leave the implementation and comparison of those for future work.

\forall S\subsetneq[n+1],S\neq\emptyset,\ \ \ u_{S}:=\sum_{i\in S}e_{i}\ \ (mod\ \mathbbm{1})\ .

(2.1)

Notice that the following relations hold

u_{S}=-u_{[n+1]\setminus S},\ \ \ \sum_{i\in S,j\notin S}u_{S}=-\sum_{i\notin S,j\in S}u_{S}\ \ \ \forall i,j\in[n+1]\ .

(2.2)

Considering the natural action of

Divisors on the permutohedral variety.

As for any toric variety, the group of
T
-invariant divisors
\Div^{T}(X_{n})
is generated by the so-called

-invariant divisors corresponding to the rays of
\Sigma_{X_{n}}

S\subsetneq[n+1]

be the prime

-invariant divisor corresponding to the ray

so that any
T
-invariant divisor
D\in\Div^{T}(X_{n})
is of the form
D=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq[n+1]}a_{S}D_{S}
for certain

. For simplicity, from now on we always write

meaning only non-empty proper subsets.

Let
H_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
and

be the two coordinate hyperplanes corresponding to the

-th and the

[

, Subsection 3.1]

\Gamma_{i}:=p^{\ast}(H_{i})=\sum_{i\in S}D_{S},\ \ \ \Delta_{j}:=q^{\ast}(\hat{H}_{j})=\sum_{j\notin S}D_{S}

(2.3)

and the canonical divisor on

is

K:=K_{X_{n}}\ =\ -\sum_{S\subset[n+1]}D_{S}\ =\ -\Gamma_{i}-\Delta_{i}\ .

(2.4)

For our purpose of proving the vanishing of the intermediate cohomology groups, we show the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
k,l\geq 0
, not both equal to zero, the divisor
k\Gamma_{i}+\ell\Delta_{j}\in\Div^{T}(X_{n})
is nef and big. In particular, the anticanonical divisor
-K
is big and nef.

Proof.

As the pullback of a very ample divisor under a generically finite map is big and nef (see
[
Laz17
, Subsection 2.2.A]
), the divisors
\Gamma_{i}
and
\Delta_{j}
are big and nef. In particular, for any
k,l\geq 0
(not both zero) the divisor
k\Gamma_{i}+\ell\Delta_{j}\in\Div^{T}(X_{n})
is big and nef. Thus, the anticanonical divisor
-K=\Gamma_{i}+\Delta_{i}
is big and nef.
∎

2.3
Example of projective line

In this section, we present our approach in the simplest case when
n=1
and
X_{1}=\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In general, we will not be working over a projective space, thus
the computations will be much more involved. However, we hope that the example below can give the flavor of our results.
In particular, our aim here is to highlight the differences among:

•

equivariant and non-equivariant case,

•

the big
T:=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
and small
T^{\prime}:=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}/(\lambda,\lambda)
tori actions,

•

different methods of computation.

Let us start with the non-equivariant case. Let
H
be a point in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and let
\mathcal{O}(1)
be the associated line bundle. As the Cremona map is a linear
isomorphism,
H
plays the role of both divisors:
\Gamma
and
\Delta
. By abuse of notation, let
H
be also the corresponding class in the Chow ring
A(\mathbb{P}^{1})
.
Clearly, we have
H=1pt
and
H^{a}=0
for
a>1
. The Hirzebruch-Rieman-Roch formula immediately implies:

\displaystyle\chi([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-1)])=1,\quad\chi(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-1)])^{\otimes a})=0,\ \ \ \forall a>0.

(2.11)

Here the situation is exceptionally nice. By the Chern character isomorphism,
[\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-1)]
is mapped exactly to
H
. For larger
n
, we also obtain higher-order
terms that are not visible in such a small example. We note that the above formulas may also be computed directly, as all cohomology groups of line bundles
on projective spaces are known
[
Har13
, Theorem III.5.1]
.

We now turn to the equivariant case. Both
T
and
T^{\prime}
act on
X_{1}=\mathbb{P}^{1}
. From the point of view of toric geometry, it is more natural to choose the action of
T^{\prime}
.
However, once we consider the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-1)
the only natural action is that of
T
. Here, we have two choices: we either have to
work with
T^{\prime}
and choose a linearization of the line bundle or we work with
T
, but we remember that our group has a stabilizer. In the article, in general,
we cover both of those approaches.

Let us start with
T^{\prime}
. We have two torus invariant divisors
H_{1}:=[1:0]
and
H_{2}:=[0:1]
. These two correspond to choosing the action of
T^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{O}(1)
or
equivalently on
\mathcal{O}(-1)
. In fact, there are more choices, as we could represent
H=2H_{2}-H_{1}
, however, we focus on those two. Hence, we fix
H_{i}
, where

i=1
or
i=2
. We may now compute all cohomology groups as a representation of
T^{\prime}
. That is, we identify each cohomology group, and hence Euler characteristics,
with the representation of
T^{\prime}
and consequently with the character, that is a Laurent polynomial.
This approach is carried out in general in Section
3.1
. First, we obtain:

\chi_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}\big([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-H_{i})]\big)=\chi_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}([\mathcal{O}])=1.

This looks similar to the formula (
2.11
), however now the value
1
on the right-hand side is a polynomial in the variables
t_{1},t_{2}
that evaluated at the point
(1,1)
gives
1
from
(
2.11
). A more interesting case is
a=2
:

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}\big(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-H_{i})])^{\otimes 2}\big)

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}([\mathcal{O}])-2\chi_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}([\mathcal{O}(-H_{i}]))+\chi_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}([\mathcal{O}(-2H_{i})])

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}([\mathcal{O}])+\chi_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}([\mathcal{O}(-2H_{i})])=1-\HH^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-2H_{i}))\ .

Here, we used the fact that many cohomology groups vanish, which in this case can be deduced from
[
Har13
, Theorem III.5.1]
. However, as we will see
in the article, more powerful tools are needed in general. In order to compute the top cohomology group, we apply toric Serre duality
[
CLS11
, Theorem 9.2.10]
, which
gives us:

\HH^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-2H_{i}))=\HH^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-H_{1}-H_{2}+2H_{i}))^{\vee}=\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{t_{i}^{2}}.

Hence, we obtain

\chi_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}\big(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-H_{i})])^{\otimes 2}\big)=1-\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{t_{i}^{2}},

which indeed evaluated at
(1,1)
gives
0
, but clearly it is a non-zero Laurent polynomial. Alternatively, if we replace
([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-H_{i})])^{\otimes 2}
by
([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-H_{1})])\otimes([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-H_{2})])
, we get the equivariant Euler characteristic as

{\chi}_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}\big(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-H_{1})])\otimes([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-H_{2})])\big)={\chi}_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}([\mathcal{O}])+{\chi}_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}([\mathcal{O}(-H_{1}-H_{2})])=1-\HH^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O})^{\vee}=0,

which actually is the zero polynomial (not only its evaluation at
(1,1)
is zero).

Next, we consider the action of
T
. While it is still possible to perform the toric computations as above, let us present a different method, based on equivariant localization.
In general, this is carried out in Section
3.2
. Note that
\mathcal{O}(1)
comes with the natural
T
-action. The two global sections
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are acted on as follows:

(t_{1},t_{2})\cdot x_{1}=t_{1}^{-1}x_{1},\quad(t_{1},t_{2})\cdot x_{2}=t_{2}^{-1}x_{2}.

The inverses come from the fact that
T
acts on
\mathbb{P}^{1}=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and the global sections of
\mathcal{O}(1)
are identified naturally with
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\vee}
. The very powerful method to deal
with equivariant
K
-theory is, for any vector bundle, to look at the vector spaces over torus invariant points. For
\mathcal{O}(-1)
we obtain the following two vector spaces:

•

(\star,0)\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
over
[1:0]
and

•

(0,\star)\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
over
[0:1]
.

Here, the reader may recall that
\mathcal{O}(-1)
is the universal bundle over the projective space. In particular, we obtain:

•

the character
t_{1}
over
[1:0]
and

•

the character
t_{2}
over
[0:1]
.

To simplify notation, we will write the pair of characters
(t_{1},t_{2})
to denote the class
[\mathcal{O}(1)]
over the two torus fixed points. In fact, previously, the linearizations of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
,
with respect to the torus

(t_{1}/t_{2},1)

(1,t_{2}/t_{1})

T^{\prime}

However, for now, we fix
(t_{1},t_{2})

[\mathcal{O}(-2)]
is represented by
(t_{1}^{2},t_{2}^{2})

We also represent
[\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-1)]

In this example, for

we have one character

and for

we have
t_{2}/t_{1}
. Hence, we have:

\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-1)])=\frac{1-t_{1}}{1-t_{1}/t_{2}}+\frac{1-t_{2}}{1-t_{2}/t_{1}}=1

as computed before so that the choice of linearization of
\mathcal{O}(-1)
is not seen here. However, as the
K
-class of
([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-1)])^{\otimes 2}
is represented by

((1-t_{1})^{2},(1-t_{2})^{2})
, we obtain

\chi_{{}_{T}}(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-1)])^{\otimes 2})=\frac{(1-t_{1})^{2}}{1-t_{1}/t_{2}}+\frac{(1-t_{2})^{2}}{1-t_{2}/t_{1}}=1-t_{1}t_{2},

which compare to the previous computation, explains the difference in the choice of linearization of
\mathcal{O}(-1)
.

3
Computation of the Euler characteristic

In this section, we compute the equivariant Euler characteristic of the desired
K
-class in two ways.
Fix
i,j\in[n+1]
, it is known that for any
0\leq a\leq n
the following holds

\int_{X_{E}}[\Gamma_{i}]^{a}\cdot[\Delta_{j}]^{n-a}=\binom{n}{a}\ .

However, the above equality is only a degree information, we are interested in determining the intersection class by taking into account the
T
-action. As already pointed out in Subsection
2.3
, fixing the indices
i,j
means choosing linearizations for
\mathcal{O}(1)
. In the following, we mainly consider the action of the small torus
T^{\prime}=T/\langle\mathbbm{1}\rangle
and representation of the characters in its lattice
M_{T^{\prime}}
.

In order to reach our goal, we pass from the Chow-theoretic expression of this intersection to the
K
-theoretic expression of it in the Grothendieck ring
K^{0}(X_{n})
via the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch formula
\chi(\mathcal{F})=\int_{X_{n}}ch(\mathcal{F})\Td(X_{n})
. By
[
MMMSV20
, Lemma 3.10]
, we obtain

\chi\left(\big([\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}]-[\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}(-\Gamma_{i})]\big)^{\otimes a}\otimes\big([\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}]-[\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}(-\Delta_{j})]\big)^{\otimes n-a}\right)=\int_{X_{E}}[\Gamma_{i}]^{a}\cdot[\Delta_{j}]^{n-a}\ .

Our main aim is the computation of the equivariant Euler characteristic on the left-hand side
for the
T
-equivariant class

\big([\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}]-[\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}(-\Gamma_{i})]\big)^{\otimes a}\otimes\big([\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}]-[\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}(-\Delta_{j})]\big)^{\otimes n-a}\in K^{0}_{T}(X_{n}).

To simplify the notation, we usually omit
X_{n}
in the presentation of the
K
-classes of bundles in
K_{T}(X_{n})
, and the tensor products in the exponents.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{n,i}:=([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-\Gamma_{i})])
and
\mathcal{B}_{n,j}:=([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-\Delta_{j})])
be the
T
-equivariant classes on the
n
-dimensional permutohedral variety
X_{n}
, and let
\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}=\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{\otimes a}
and
\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{b}=\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{\otimes b}
. In this notation, our goal can be reformulated in the computation of

\chi_{{}_{T}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a}).

By expanding the tensor products and by setting

D_{ij}^{k\ell}:=k\Gamma_{i}+\ell\Delta_{j}\in\Div^{T}(X_{n}),

for any
0\leq k\leq a
and
0\leq\ell\leq n-a
, we have

\chi_{{}_{T}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})=\sum_{k=0}^{a}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-a}(-1)^{k+l}\binom{a}{k}\binom{n-a}{\ell}\chi_{{}_{T}}\left([\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})]\right).

In the following subsections, we compute the equivariant Euler characteristics
\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})])
via two different approaches: the first approach consists in determining the cohomology groups of the line bundles
\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})
, while the second one is based on the direct computation of
\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})])
using the equivariant localization formula. Within the first approach, the equivariant Euler characteristic of a
K
-class is a formal sum of irreducible
T
-representations, which are always one-dimensional and correspond to characters on the torus, hence to points in
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
or equivalently to Laurent polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{n+1}^{\pm 1}]
, where a character
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n+1})
is identified with the Laurent monomial
t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{n+1}^{m_{n+1}}
. Throughout this paper, we represent the Euler characteristic of a
K
-class as a Laurent polynomial.

3.1
First approach: computing cohomology groups

In this subsection, we determine the equivariant cohomology groups appearing in the equivariant Euler characteristic
\chi_{{}_{T}}\left([\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})]\right)
as
k
and
\ell
vary. First, we prove the vanishing of many intermediate cohomology groups and, consequently, we reduce the computation of the equivariant Euler characteristic to the computation of only the vector spaces of the global sections, that is
\HH^{0}
-terms. Since each such a cohomology group inherits the
T
-action on
X_{n}
, and hence it is a representation of the torus
T
, we determine their irreducible components.

We recall the following vanishing results, used frequently in this section.

Theorem 3.1

(Toric Kawamata-Viehweg; Theorem 9.3.10,
[
CLS11
]
)
.

Let
X
be a toric variety. For any big and nef divisor
D\in\Div^{T}(X)
, it holds

\HH^{p}(X,\mathcal{O}(K_{X}+D))=0,\ \ \ \forall p>0\ .

Theorem 3.2

(Batyrev-Borisov vanishing; Theorem 9.2.7,
[
CLS11
]
)
.

Let
X
be a toric variety. For any nef divisor
D\in\Div^{T}(X)
, it holds

\HH^{p}(X,\mathcal{O}(-D))=0,\ \ \ \forall p\neq\dim P_{D}\ .

where
P_{D}
is the associated polytope to the divisor
D
.

The first step is computing the equivariant Euler characteristic of the structure sheaf
\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}])
. Since the anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is big and nef, by Theorem
3.1
we get the vanishings
\HH^{p}(X,\mathcal{O})=\HH^{p}(X,K_{X}+(-K_{X}))=0
for any
p>0
and we conclude that

\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}])=\HH^{0}(X,\mathcal{O})=1.

(3.1)

Next, we consider the case in which either
k=0
or
\ell=0
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
1\leq k,\ell\leq n
, it holds

\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}(-k\Gamma_{i})])=\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}(-\ell\Delta_{j})])=0\ .

Proof.

By symmetry and freedom on the choice of the pullbacks
\Gamma_{i}
and
\Delta_{j}
, the sheaves
\mathcal{O}(-k\Gamma_{i})
and
\mathcal{O}(-\ell\Delta_{j})
have common vanishings in cohomology, although their non-zero cohomology groups could split in different representations. In light of this, it is enough to prove the statement for
\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}(-k\Gamma_{i})])
as
1\leq k\leq n
varies. Since the divisor
k\Gamma_{i}
is big and nef, by Theorem
3.2
we get

\HH^{p}(X,\mathcal{O}(-k\Gamma_{i}))=0\ \ \ \ \ ,\forall p\neq\dim P_{k\Gamma_{i}}=n\ .

Moreover, by Serre duality (SD)

\HH^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}(-k\Gamma_{i}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle SD}}{{=}}\HH^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(K_{X}+k\Gamma_{i}))^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{canonical divisor}}}{{=}}\HH^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}((k-1)\Gamma_{i}-\Delta_{i}))^{\vee}\ .

The characters
m\in M_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
giving the splitting of
\HH^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}((k-1)\Gamma_{i}-\Delta_{i}))
into irreducible one-dimensional torus representations are exactly the ones satisfying the inequalities

\langle m,u_{S}\rangle\leq\begin{cases}k-1&\text{if }i\in S\\
-1&\text{if }i\notin S\end{cases}

(3.2)

for any non-empty proper subset
S\subset[n+1]
. In particular, by (
2.1
) we get

\begin{cases}\langle m,u_{\{i,j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}}\rangle\leq(k-1)-r\\
\langle m,u_{\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}}\rangle\leq-r\end{cases}

while by (
2.2
) it holds

u_{[n+1]\setminus\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}\}}=-u_{\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}\}}\implies\begin{cases}1\leq\langle m,u_{\{i,j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}}\rangle\\
-k+1\leq\langle m,u_{\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}}\rangle\end{cases}\ ,

where each
j_{l}\neq i
. Since we consider only proper subsets,
r
can be at most
n-1
. Consider
r=n-1
and recall that
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Thus, since
k<n+1
, we have
1\leq\langle m,u_{\{i,j_{1},\ldots,j_{n-1}\}}\rangle\leq k-n<1
, leading to a contradiction. Therefore,

\HH^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}(-k\Gamma_{i}))=\HH^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}((k-1)\Gamma_{i}-\Delta_{i}))^{\vee}=0,

and from the common vanishings for the sheaves
[\mathcal{O}(-k\Gamma_{i})]
and
[\mathcal{O}(-\ell\Delta_{j})]
, we conclude that

\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}(-k\Gamma_{i})])=\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}(-k\Delta_{j})])=0,\ \ \ \forall 1\leq k\leq n.

(3.3)

∎

It remains to deal with the divisors
D_{ij}^{k\ell}=k\Gamma_{i}+\ell\Delta_{j}
for
k,\ell\geq 1
. Since they are big and nef, by Theorem
3.1
, we know that
\HH^{p}(X,\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell}))=0
for any
p\neq n
, thus we only have to determine

\displaystyle\HH^{n}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell}))=\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(K_{X}+D_{ij}^{k\ell}))^{\vee}=\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}((k-1)\Gamma_{i}-\Delta_{i}+\ell\Delta_{j}))^{\vee}\ .

At this point we have obtained that
\chi_{{}_{T}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})
only depend on global sections:

\chi_{{}_{T}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})=1+\sum_{k=1}^{a}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-a}(-1)^{n+k+\ell}\binom{a}{k}\binom{n-a}{\ell}\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}((k-1)\Gamma_{i}-\Delta_{i}+\ell\Delta_{j}))^{\vee}\ .

(3.4)

We simplify the notation by denoting the divisor
S_{ij}^{k\ell}:=(k-1)\Gamma_{i}-\Delta_{i}+\ell\Delta_{j}
, which can be rewritten with respect to the prime divisors as follows:

S_{ij}^{k\ell}\ =\ (k-1)\!\!\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset S}\!\!D_{S}\ +\ (k+\ell-1)\!\!\!\sum_{i\in S,\ j\notin S}\!\!D_{S}\ -\!\!\!\sum_{i\notin S,\ j\in S}\!\!D_{S}\ +\ (\ell-1)\!\!\sum_{i,j\notin S}D_{S}\ .

The above description allows determining the system of inequalities that a character
m\in M_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
has to satisfy so that
\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(S_{ij}^{k\ell}))_{m}\neq 0
. Let
m_{S}=\sum_{h\in S}m_{h}=\langle m,u_{S}\rangle
, then we get

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq m_{\{i,s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}}\leq k+\ell-1

\displaystyle-(\ell-1)

\displaystyle\leq m_{\{i,j,s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}}\leq k-1

(3.5)

\displaystyle-(k+\ell-1)

\displaystyle\leq m_{\{j,s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}}\leq-1

\displaystyle-(k-1)

\displaystyle\leq m_{\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}}\leq\ell-1\ .

for any subset
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}\subset[n+1]\setminus\{i,j\}
(the empty subset is admitted too).

Remark 3.4
.

For
i=j
, one gets
S_{ii}^{k\ell}=(k-1)\Gamma_{i}+(\ell-1)\Delta_{i}=\ (k-1)\!\!\sum_{i\in S}\!\!D_{S}\ +\ (\ell-1)\!\!\sum_{i\notin S}D_{S}
, leading to the system of inequalities:

\displaystyle-(\ell-1)

\displaystyle\leq m_{\{i,s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}}\leq k-1

\displaystyle-(k-1)

\displaystyle\leq m_{\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}}\leq\ell-1,

which always admits
(0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
as a solution. On the other hand, for
i\neq j
, the character
(0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
is never a solution of (
3.1
) for any
k,\ell\geq 1
, thus

\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(S_{ij}^{k\ell}))_{(0,\ldots,0)}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i=j\\
0&\text{if }i\neq j\end{cases}\ .

Remark 3.5
.

For
i\neq j
and
k=\ell=1
the only solution is
m=e_{i}-e_{j}\in M_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}
, that is

\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(S_{ij}^{11}))=\HH^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(-\Delta_{i}+\Delta_{j}))=t_{i}t_{j}^{-1}.

Remark 3.6
.

Given
1\leq k\leq k^{\prime}\leq a
and
1\leq\ell\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq n-a
, from the lower and upper bounds in the system (
3.1
), we deduce that the lattice solutions for
(k,\ell)
are lattice solutions for
(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
too. Indeed, the coordinate intervals for solutions increase as
k
and
\ell
increase. It follows that

\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(S_{ij}^{k\ell}))\subset\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(S_{ij}^{k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}})),\ \forall(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\geq(k,\ell),

thus for any character
m\in M_{{}_{T^{\prime}}}
such that
H^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(S_{ij}^{k\ell}))_{m}\neq 0
one gets

\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(S_{ij}^{k\ell}))_{m}=\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(S_{ij}^{k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}))_{m}\ .

For instance, if
i\neq j
, Remark
3.5
implies that
\HH^{0}(X_{n},\mathcal{O}(S_{ij}^{k\ell}))_{e_{i}-e_{j}}^{\vee}=t_{j}t_{i}^{-1}
for any
k,\ell\geq 1
, thus we can compute its coefficients in the equivariant Euler characteristic
\chi_{{}_{T}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})
:

\chi_{{}_{T}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})_{e_{j}-e_{i}}=\sum_{k=1}^{a}(-1)^{n+k}\binom{a}{k}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-a}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{n-a}{\ell}\right]\left(t_{j}t_{i}^{-1}\right)=(-1)^{n}\left(t_{j}t_{i}^{-1}\right).

The above remark allows restricting the search of (possible) irreducible summands of the virtual representation
\chi_{{}_{T}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})
among the ones of
\HH^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(S_{ij}^{a,n-a}))
.

In the

file

.

3.2
Second approach: equivariant localization

In this section, we exploit techniques from equivariant geometry, in particular equivariant
K
-theory. As the hallmark of this theory, we use the localization formula
2.4
.

In fact, using this formula to compute the equivariant Euler characteristic for the sheaf
[\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})]
one needs to compute the class
[\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})]_{\sigma}
for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}
which corresponds to the piece-wise linear functions on the cones
C_{\sigma}
defined by the coefficients of
-D_{ij}^{k\ell}
with respect to the prime
T
-invariant divisors. In particular, it holds

\displaystyle-D_{ij}^{k\ell}=(-k-\ell)\cdot\!\!\!\sum_{S\cap\{i,j\}=\{i\}}\!\!\!D_{S}+0\cdot\!\!\!\sum_{S\cap\{i,j\}=\{j\}}\!\!\!D_{S}-\ k\cdot\!\!\sum_{S\supset\{i,j\}}\!\!\!D_{S}-\ \ell\cdot\!\!\!\sum_{S\subset[n+1]\setminus\{i,j\}}\!\!\!D_{S}\ .

(3.6)

Recall that, via the bijection (
2.5
), any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}
uniquely corresponds to the cone

C_{\sigma}=Cone\left(u_{S_{1}},\ldots,u_{S_{n}}\right)

where
S_{h}=\{\sigma(1),\sigma(2),\ldots,\sigma(h)\}
for any
1\leq h\leq n
. For simplicity, we identify the cones with their defining flags

C_{\sigma}=(S_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq S_{n})\ .

Lemma 3.8
.

Given
-D_{ij}^{k\ell}\in\Div^{T}(X_{n})
as in (
3.6
), the Grothendieck class
[\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})]\in K^{0}_{T}(X_{n})
has fiber at any
T
-invariant point
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}\simeq X_{n}^{T}
given by

[\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})]_{\sigma}=t_{\sigma(1)}^{-\ell}t_{i}^{-k}t_{j}^{\ell}t_{\sigma(n+1)}^{k}\ .

(3.7)

Proof.

The value of the piece-wise linear functions on the cones
C_{\sigma}
depends on the intersections
S_{h}\cap\{i,j\}
and we have the following cases:

(i)

\sigma(1)=i
and
\sigma(n+1)=j
;

(ii)

\sigma(p)=i
for some
1\lneq p\leq n
, and
\sigma(n+1)=j
;

(iii)

\sigma(1)=j
and
\sigma(n+1)=i
;

(iv)

\sigma(p)=j
for some
1\lneq p\leq n
, and
\sigma(n+1)=i
;

(v)

\sigma(1)=i
and
\sigma(p)=j
for some
1\lneq p\leq n
;

(vi)

\sigma(1)=j
and
\sigma(p)=i
for some
1\lneq p\leq n
;

(vii)

\sigma(p)=i
and
\sigma(q)=j
for some
1\lneq p<q\leq n
;

(viii)

\sigma(p)=j
and
\sigma(q)=i
for some
1\lneq p<q\leq n
.

Let us consider case (i). The cone
C_{\sigma}
corresponds to the flag

S_{1}=\{i\}\subsetneq S_{2}=\{i,\sigma(2)\}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq S_{n}=\{i,\sigma(2),\ldots,\sigma(n)\}\ .

Then for any above
S_{h}
it holds
S_{h}\cap\{i,j\}=\{i\}
, that is from (
3.6
) we get a linear function
\phi_{\sigma}
which has value
-k-\ell
on any ray generator from the given cone, i.e. corresponding to one
S_{h}
. In particular,
-k-\ell=\phi_{\sigma}(u_{\{i\}})
, while for any
h\geq 2
we get
-k-\ell=\phi_{\sigma}(u_{S_{h}})=\phi_{\sigma}(u_{\{i\}}+u_{\sigma(2)}+\ldots+u_{\sigma(h)})
which implies that
\phi_{\sigma}(u_{\sigma(p)})=0
for any
2\leq p\leq n
. Thus for any
\sigma
satisfying (i), one gets

[\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{kl})]_{\sigma}=t_{i}^{-k-\ell}t_{j}^{k+\ell}\ .

By repeating the above arguments for each one of the cases (i)-(viii), we obtain the desired result. ∎

By replacing (
3.7
) in the localization formula (
2.10
), we get

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{T}}([\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})])

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}}\dfrac{[\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})]_{\sigma}}{\prod_{s=1}^{n}\left(1-t_{\sigma(s)}^{-1}t_{\sigma(s+1)}\right)}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=t_{i}^{-k}t_{j}^{\ell}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}}\dfrac{t_{\sigma(1)}^{-\ell}t_{\sigma(n+1)}^{k}}{\prod_{s=1}^{n}\left(1-t_{\sigma(s)}^{-1}t_{\sigma(s+1)}\right)}\ .

(3.9)

By using the vanishings in the previous section, we get

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{T}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{a}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-a}(-1)^{k+\ell}\binom{a}{k}\binom{n-a}{\ell}\chi_{{}_{T}}\left([\mathcal{O}(-D_{ij}^{k\ell})]\right)

(3.10)

4
Recursive formula for Euler characteristic

In this section we present a recursive formula for computing the equivariant Euler characteristic
\chi_{{}_{T}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})
. The key will be a restriction to one of the divisors
\Gamma
or
\Delta
, which can be represented as the sum of torus-invariant divisors
D_{S}
, so that
D_{S}\cong X_{|S|-1}\times X_{|E\setminus S|-1}
. Hence, the restriction to each such a divisor, and moreover to their intersections, comes with two natural projections to lower dimensional permutohedral varieties. We use this to write the corresponding Euler characteristic as the product of the Euler characteristic of simpler line bundles on the lower dimensional permutohedral varieties.

More precisely, let
D\subset X_{n}
be an irreducible torus-invariant divisor embedded with
i:D\hookrightarrow X_{n}
, and let
\pi:X_{n}\longrightarrow\{pt\}
be the projection to a point. As
X_{n}
is smooth, so is
D
. We recall that the Euler characteristic map

\chi_{{}_{X_{n}}}:K_{T}(X_{n})\longrightarrow K_{T}(pt)

is defined as the pushforward map along the projection
\pi
. Therefore, for a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X_{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{X_{n}}}(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D)])\otimes[\mathcal{F}])

\displaystyle=[\pi_{*}(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D)])\otimes[\mathcal{F}])]

\displaystyle=[\pi_{*}([i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}]\otimes[\mathcal{F}])]

\displaystyle=[\pi_{*}i_{*}([\mathcal{O}_{D}]\otimes i^{*}[\mathcal{F}])]

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{D}}(i^{*}[\mathcal{F}])

where the second equality follows from the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(-D)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\rightarrow i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}\rightarrow 0,

and the third equality follows from the projection formula. We conclude that:

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{X_{n}}}(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D)])\otimes[\mathcal{F}])=\chi_{{}_{D}}([\mathcal{F}_{|D}]).

(4.1)

This is the key to allowing the restriction to a divisor
D
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}D_{i}
be a divisor such that the divisors
D_{i}
are prime, they intersect transversally and each non-empty intersection of them is smooth.
Then the following recursive formula holds:

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{X_{n}}}\bigg(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D)])\otimes[\mathcal{F}]\bigg)=\sum_{\emptyset\neq\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}\subset[\ell]}(-1)^{r+1}\chi_{{}_{D_{j_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap D_{j_{r}}}}\bigg(\bigg[\mathcal{F}_{|_{D_{j_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap D_{j_{r}}}}\bigg]\bigg)

(4.2)

where the sum is taken over all possible nonempty subsets of
[\ell]
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\ell
, the case
\ell=1
being (
4.1
). Let
D^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}D_{i}
. We have

\displaystyle\chi\left(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D^{\prime})])\otimes([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D_{\ell})])\otimes[\mathcal{F}]\right)

\displaystyle=\chi\left(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D^{\prime})]-[\mathcal{O}(-D_{\ell})]+[\mathcal{O}(-D^{\prime}-D_{\ell})])\otimes[\mathcal{F}]\right)

\displaystyle=\chi\left((([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D^{\prime})])+([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D_{\ell})])-([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D^{\prime}-D_{\ell})]))\otimes[\mathcal{F}]\right).

As
D=D^{\prime}+D_{\ell}
and the Euler characteristic is additive, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi\left(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D)])\otimes[\mathcal{F}]\right)

\displaystyle=\chi\left(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D^{\prime})])\otimes[\mathcal{F}])+\chi(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D_{\ell})])\otimes[\mathcal{F}]\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\chi\left(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D^{\prime})])\otimes([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D_{\ell})])\otimes[\mathcal{F}]\right).

Applying induction to the first term we obtain the part of the RHS of (
4.2
) where
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\}\subset[\ell-1]
. The second term contributes one summand
\{j_{1}\}=\{\ell\}
. For the last summand

\chi\left(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D^{\prime})])\otimes([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D_{\ell})])\otimes[\mathcal{F}]\right)

we again apply induction, treating
([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D_{\ell})])\otimes[\mathcal{F}]
as one element. We conclude by noting that:

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{D_{j_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap D_{j_{r}}}}\left(\left[\big(([\mathcal{O}]-[\mathcal{O}(-D_{\ell})])\otimes[\mathcal{F}]\big)_{|_{D_{j_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap D_{j_{r}}}}\right]\right)=

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{D_{j_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap D_{j_{r}}\cap D_{\ell}}}\left(\left[\mathcal{F}_{|_{D_{j_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap D_{j_{r}}\cap D_{\ell}}}\right]\right),

by the case
\ell=1
. Thus the last term corresponds to
r
-tuples
j_{1},\dots,j_{r}
where
r>1
and
j_{r}=\ell
. This concludes the inductive step and hence the proof.
∎

For a flag
S_{1}\subsetneq S_{2}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq S_{k}
of proper non-empty subsets of
E
, consider the corresponding intersection:

D_{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}}:=D_{S_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap D_{S_{k}}.

Corollary 4.2
.

By restriction to
\Gamma_{i}
, for
a>0
, it holds

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{X_{n}}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{X_{n}}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a-1}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i\in S_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq S_{k}}(-1)^{k+1}\chi_{{}_{D_{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}}}}\left({\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a-1}}_{|_{D_{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}}}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a}}_{|_{D_{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}}}}\right)

where, after fixing
k
, the second sum is taken over all possible flags of length
k
whose first subset contains
i
. A similar formula holds for restriction to
\Delta_{j}
, where the second sum runs over all possible flags not containing
j
.

Theorem 4.3
.

By restriction to
\Gamma_{i}
, for
a>0
, it holds

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{X_{n}}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in S\subsetneq E}\chi\left(\mathcal{A}_{{}_{|E\setminus S|-1,i}}^{a-1}\right)\chi\left(\mathcal{B}_{{}_{|S|-1,j}}^{n-a}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +\sum_{k=2}^{n}\sum_{i\in S_{1}\subsetneq S_{k}\subsetneq E}(-1)^{k+1}(k-1)!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|S_k\setminus S_1|}{k-1}\chi\left(\mathcal{A}_{{}_{|E\setminus S_{k}|-1,i}}^{a-1}\right)\chi\left(\mathcal{B}_{{}_{|S_{1}|-1,j}}^{n-a}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in S\subsetneq E}\chi\left(\mathcal{A}_{{}_{|E\setminus S|-1,i}}^{a-1}\right)\chi\left(\mathcal{B}_{{}_{|S|-1,j}}^{n-a}\right)+\sum_{i\in S\subsetneq S^{\prime}\subsetneq E}(-1)^{|S^{\prime}\setminus S|}\chi\left(\mathcal{A}_{{}_{|E\setminus S^{\prime}|-1,i}}^{a-1}\right)\chi\left(\mathcal{B}_{{}_{|S|-1,j}}^{n-a}\right)

where
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|S_k\setminus S_1|}{k-1}
is the Stirling function counting the number of
k-1
partitions of the set
S_{k}\setminus S_{1}
into non-empty subsets, and we set
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{0}=1
. A similar formula holds for restriction to
\Delta_{j}
, where the second sum runs over all possible
2
-flags not containing
j
.

Proof.

We note that each intersection can be described as a product of lower dimensional permutohedral varieties

D_{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}}\cong X_{|S_{1}|-1}\times X_{|S_{2}\setminus S_{1}|-1}\times\ldots\times X_{|S_{k}\setminus S_{k-1}|-1}\times X_{|E\setminus S_{k}|-1}

where the torus
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{|S_{i}\setminus S_{i-1}|}
with coordinates
(t_{\ell})_{\ell\in S_{i}\setminus S_{i-1}}
acts on the
i
-th factor. Thus, one has two natural projections onto lower-dimensional permutohedral varieties
D_{S_{1}\ldots S_{k}}
X_{|S_{1}|-1}\times X_{|E\setminus S_{k}|-1}
X_{|S_{1}|-1}
X_{|E\setminus S_{k}|-1}
\phi
\pi_{1}
\pi_{2}

so that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}_{n,i}}_{|_{D_{S_{1}\ldots S_{k}}}}\cong\phi^{*}\circ\pi_{2}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{{}_{|E\setminus S_{k}|-1,i}}),\ \ \ \ {\mathcal{B}_{n,j}}_{|_{D_{S_{1}\ldots S_{k}}}}\cong\phi^{*}\circ\pi_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{{}_{|S_{1}|-1,j}})

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{D_{S_{1}\ldots S_{k}}}}\left({\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a-1}}_{|_{D_{S_{1}\ldots S_{k}}}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a}}_{|_{D_{S_{1}\ldots S_{k}}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{X_{|S_{1}|-1}\times X_{|E\setminus S_{k}|-1}}}\left(\pi_{2}^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{a-1}_{{}_{|E\setminus S_{k}|-1,i}})\otimes\pi_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{B}^{n-a}_{{}_{|S_{1}|-1,j}})\right)

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{X_{|E\setminus S_{k}|-1}}}\left(\mathcal{A}^{a-1}_{{}_{|E\setminus S_{k}|-1,j}}\right)\chi_{{}_{X_{|S_{1}|-1}}}\left(\mathcal{B}^{n-a}_{{}_{|S_{1}|-1,j}}\right),

where the second equality follows from the Künneth formula, and the first one follows from the definition of the Euler characteristic, as pushforward map along the projection to a point. By Corollary
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{X_{n}}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i\in S_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq S_{k}}(-1)^{k+1}\chi\left(\mathcal{A}_{{}_{|E\setminus S_{k}|-1,i}}^{a-1}\right)\chi\left(\mathcal{B}_{{}_{|S_{1}|-1,j}}^{n-a}\right)

where, after fixing
k
, the second sum is taken over all possible flags of length
k
whose first subset contains
i
. Since only the tail and the head of a flag are seen by the formula, we can reduce to length-
2
flags
S_{1}\subsetneq S_{k}
and use the Stirling function
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|S_k\setminus S_1|}{k-1}
, multiplied with
(k-1)!
, to count the number of
k
-flags with such a given head and tail.

To obtain the final formula we need to prove that for any
s>0
:

\sum_{k=1}^{s}(-1)^{k}k!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{s}{k}=(-1)^{s}.

The first proof of the above formula is by induction on
s
, the case
s=1
being trivial. For
s>1
, we note that
k!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{s}{k}
counts the number of ordered partitions
(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
of the set {1,…,s}. All partitions for which
A_{1}\neq\{s\}
can be paired as follows. If
A_{l}=\{s\}
then we pair
(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
with
(A_{1},\dots,A_{l-2},A_{l-1}\cup\{s\},A_{l+1},\dots,A_{k})
. Similarly, if each
A_{l}\neq\{s\}
then we must have
s\in A_{l}
for some
l
. We pair the partition by splitting
A_{l}
into
A_{l}\setminus\{s\}
, followed by
\{s\}
. Each pair contributes exactly one
1
and one
-1
to the sum. Thus it is enough to compute the contributions when
A_{1}=\{s\}
, which we know by induction.

For the second proof we recall the well-known formula:

\sum_{k=0}^{s}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{s}{k}x(x-1)\cdots(x-(k-1))=(x)^{s}.

Our formula follows by substituting
x=-1
.
∎

We remark that in the computation of the terms

We may reconstruct the formula for the usual Euler characteristic from our formulas for the equivariant Euler characteristic. We recall that the reduced chromatic polynomial of the uniform matroid on

\sum_{i=1}^{r}(-1)^{r-i}\binom{n}{r-i}t^{i}.

Corollary 4.4
.

In the non-equivariant setting:

\chi_{{}_{X_{n}}}(\mathcal{A}_{n,i}^{a}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n,j}^{n-a})=\binom{n}{a}.

Proof.

We note that intersecting more than dimension of the variety many divisors always return zero in the non-equivariant setting. In particular, the only non-zero contributions in Theorem
4.3

\binom{n}{n-a}=\binom{n}{a}

Determinant of 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -hypergeometric solutions under ample reduction
Alexander Varchenko ⋆

Abstract
. We consider the KZ differential equations over ℂ {\mathbb{C}} in the case, when the hypergeometric solutions are one-dimensional integrals. We also consider the same differential equations over a finite field 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} . We study the polynomial solutions of these differential equations over 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} , constructed in a previous work joint with V. Schechtman and called the 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -hypergeometric solutions. The dimension of the space of 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -hypergeometric solutions depends on the prime number p p . We say that the KZ equations have ample reduction for a prime p p , if the dimension of the space of 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -hypergeometric solutions is maximal possible, that is, equal to the dimension of the space of solutions of the corresponding KZ equations over ℂ {\mathbb{C}} . Under the assumption of ample reduction, we prove a determinant formula for the matrix of coordinates of basis 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -hypergeometric solutions. The formula is analogous to the corresponding formula for the determinant of the matrix of coordinates of basis complex hypergeometric solutions, in which binomials ( z i − z j ) M i + M j (z_{i}-z_{j})^{M_{i}+M_{j}} are replaced with ( z i − z j ) M i + M j − p (z_{i}-z_{j})^{M_{i}+M_{j}-p} and the Euler gamma function Γ ⁡ ( x ) \Gamma(x) is replaced with a suitable 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -analog Γ 𝔽 p ​ ( x ) \Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(x) defined on 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} .

1.
Introduction

The KZ equations were introduced in
[
KZ
]
as the differential equations satisfied by
conformal blocks on sphere in the Wess-Zumino-Witten model of conformal field theory.
The hypergeometric solutions of the KZ equations were constructed more than 30 years ago,
see
[
SV1
,
SV2
]
.
The KZ equations and the hypergeometric solutions
are related to many subjects in algebra, representation theory,
theory of integrable systems, enumerative geometry, to name a few.

The polynomial
solutions of the KZ equations over the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

with a prime number
p
of elements were constructed recently
in
[
SV3
]
, see also
[
V4
,
V5
,
V6
,
V7
]
.
We call these solutions the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions
.
The general problem is to understand relations between the hypergeometric solutions
of the KZ equations over
{\mathbb{C}}
and the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions and observe how the
remarkable properties of hypergeometric solutions are reflected in the properties
of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions. This program is in the first stages, where
we consider essential examples and study the corresponding

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions by direct methods.

In this paper we consider the KZ differential equations
in the case, when the hypergeometric solutions over
{\mathbb{C}}
are
one-dimensional integrals.

The dimension of the
space of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions depends on the prime number
p
.
We say that the KZ equations have ample reduction for a prime
p
, if the
dimension of the space of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions is maximal possible,
that is, equal to the dimension
of the space of solutions
of the corresponding KZ equations over
{\mathbb{C}}
. Under the assumption of ample reduction,
we prove a determinant formula for the matrix of coordinates of basis
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions.
The formula is analogous to the corresponding formula for the determinant of the matrix of
coordinates of basis
complex hypergeometric solutions, see
[
V1
]
,
in which binomials
(z_{i}-z_{j})^{M_{i}+M_{j}}

are replaced with
(z_{i}-z_{j})^{M_{i}+M_{j}-p}
and the Euler gamma function
\Gamma(x)
is replaced with
a suitable
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-analog
\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(x)
defined on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

In Section
2
we describe our KZ equations and their reduction modulo
p
. We define the hypergeometric solutions over
{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions.
The ample reduction is defined in Section
2.5
.

As mentioned earlier, the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions are polynomials. In Section
3
we give a formula
for their coefficients.

In Section
4
, we consider the particular case of our KZ equations,
whose space of solutions over
{\mathbb{C}}
is one-dimensional, with the basis solution
given by the Euler beta integral. We describe the corresponding
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solution,
which we call the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-beta integral
.

In Section
5
we consider an arbitrary polynomial solution (not necessarily
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric)
of our KZ
equations over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and describe
its leading term with respect to the lexicographical ordering of monomials, see Theorem
5.3
.
It turns out that the notion of leading term and the formula for the leading term
in Theorem
5.3

are useful in studying polynomial
solutions of the KZ equations over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. The notion of leading term replaces, to some extend,
the notion of initial condition, when the differential equations are over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

The module of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions has a natural basis. In Section
6
we describe the leading
terms of the basis
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions. Section
7
contains our main result,
Theorem
7.2
, describing the determinant of coordinates of the basis
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions,
under assumption of ample reduction.

In Section
2.6
we give an example of KZ equations and a prime
p
,
such that the space of complex solutions is one-dimensional, the space of polynomial solutions over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is
one-dimensional,
and the KZ equations have no
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions.
In Section
8
we show that if the reduction of our KZ equations is ample for a prime
p
, then all polynomial solutions are
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric.

The author thanks Alexey Slinkin for useful discussions.

2.
KZ equations

2.1.
Description of equations

In this paper the numbers
p
,
q

are prime numbers,

n
is a positive integer,
p>n\geqslant 2
,
p>q
.

We fix
a vector
(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{>0}
, such that

m_{i}<q
for all
i=1,\dots,n
,

and study the system of equations
for a column vector
I(z)=(I_{1}(z)
, …,
I_{n}(z))
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{i}}\ =\ {\frac{1}{q}}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\Omega_{ij}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}I,\quad i=1,\dots,n,\qquad m_{1}I_{1}(z)+\dots+m_{n}I_{n}(z)=0,

where
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
,
the
n\times n
-matrices
\Omega_{ij}
have the form:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Omega_{ij}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}&\vdots^{i}&&\vdots^{j}&\\
{\scriptstyle i}\cdots&{-m_{j}}&\cdots&m_{j}&\cdots\\
&\vdots&&\vdots&\\
{\scriptstyle j}\cdots&m_{i}&\cdots&{-m_{i}}&\cdots\\
&\vdots&&\vdots&\end{pmatrix},

and all other entries are zero.
This joint system of
differential and
algebraic equations
is called the
system of KZ equations
in this paper.

Remark
.

System of equations (
2.1
) is the system of standard KZ differential equations with parameter
q
,
associated with the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
and the subspace of singular
vectors of weight
\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}-2
of the tensor
product

V_{m_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes V_{m_{n}}
,
where
V_{m_{i}}
is the irreducible
m_{i}+1
dimensional
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-module, up to a gauge transformation, see
this example in
[
V3
, Section 1.1]
.

We consider system (
2.1
) over the field
{\mathbb{C}}
and over the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with
p
elements.

2.2.
Solutions over
{\mathbb{C}}

Consider the
master function

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Phi(t,z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\prod_{a=1}^{n}(t-z_{a})^{-m_{a}/q}

and the column
{n}
-vector of hypergeometric integrals

(2.4)

\displaystyle I^{(\gamma)}(z)=(I_{1}(z),\dots,I_{n}(z)),

where

(2.5)

\displaystyle I_{j}=\int\Phi(t,z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\,\frac{dt}{t-z_{j}},\qquad j=1,\dots,{n}\,.

The integrals
I_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n
, are over an element
\gamma
of the first homology group
of the algebraic curve with affine equation

\displaystyle y^{q}=(t-z_{1})^{m_{1}}\dots(t-z_{n})^{m_{n}}\,.

Starting from such
\gamma
, chosen for given values

\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\}
, the vector
I^{(\gamma)}(z)
can
be analytically continued as a multivalued holomorphic function of
z
to the complement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
of the union of the
diagonal hyperplanes
z_{i}=z_{j}
,
i\neq j
.

The complex vector space of such integral solutions is the
n-1
-dimensional vector space of all solutions of
system (
2.1
).
See these statements in the example in
[
V3
, Section 1.1]
, also in
[
SliV1
]
, see also the determinant formula
(
7.1
) below.

2.3.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Integrals

Let
P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
be a polynomial
with coefficients in an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-module,

\displaystyle P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\sum_{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}}\,c_{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}}\,x_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots x_{k}^{d_{k}}.

Let
(l_{1},\dots,l_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}^{k}
. The coefficient

c_{l_{1}p-1,\dots,l_{k}p-1}
is called the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral over cycle
[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}

and denoted by

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}.

We have an analog of Stokes’ Theorem:

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}\,=\,0\,

for any
[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}
.

2.4.
Solutions over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

Polynomial solutions of system (
2.1
), considered over the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
,
were constructed in
[
SV3
]
.

For
i=1,\dots,n
, choose the least positive integers
M_{i}
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle M_{i}\equiv-\frac{m_{i}}{q}\qquad(\operatorname{mod}\,p)\,.

Let

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}(t,z,M)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(t-z_{i})^{M_{i}},

(2.8)

\displaystyle P(x,z)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\Phi_{p}(x,z)}{x-z_{1}},\dots,\frac{\Phi_{p}(x,z)}{x-z_{n}}\Big)\,=\,\sum_{i}P^{i}(z)\,x^{i}\,,

where
P(x,z)
is considered as a column
n
-vector of polynomials in
x,z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
and

P^{i}(z)
as column
n
-vectors of polynomials in
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

For a positive integer
l
, denote

(2.9)

\displaystyle I^{[l]}(z)\,:=\,\int_{[l]_{p}}\Big(\frac{\Phi_{p}(x,z)}{x-z_{1}},\dots,\frac{\Phi_{p}(x,z)}{x-z_{n}}\Big)\,dx.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
SV3
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

For any positive integer
l
, the vector of polynomials
I^{[l]}(z)

is a solution of KZ system (
2.1
).

The solutions
I^{[l]}(z)

given by this construction are called the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions
of equations (
2.1
).

Remark
.

The polynomial
\Phi_{p}(t,z)
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-analog of the master function
\Phi(t,z)
.
The polynomial
P(t,z)
is an analog of the integrand of integral (
2.4
).
The transformation
P(t,z)\to I^{[l]}(z)
is an analog of the integral
and the index
[l]_{p}
is an analog of the integration cycle.

Denote
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z^{p}]:={\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z_{1}^{p},\dots,z_{n}^{p}]
.
The set of all polynomial solutions of system (
2.1
) with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

is a module over the ring

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z^{p}]
since equations (
2.1
) are linear and

\frac{\partial z_{i}^{p}}{\partial z_{j}}=0
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z]
for all
i,j
.

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z^{p}]
-module

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\,=\,\Big\{\sum_{l}c_{l}(z)I^{[l]}(z)\ |\ c_{l}(z)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z^{p}]\Big\},

spanned by
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions,
is called the
module of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions
.

The range for the index
l
is defined by the inequalities

0<lp-1\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}-1
. Hence

l=1,\dots,r
, where

(2.11)

\displaystyle r\,:=\,\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}/p\Big],

the integer part of the number
\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}/p
.

Theorem 2.2
.

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions
I^{[l]}(z)
,
l=1,\dots,r
, are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z^{p}]
.

Proof.

The proof coincides with the proof of
[
V5
, Theorem 3.1]
, see also the proof of

[
SliV1
, Theorem 3.2]
. Other proofs see in
[
V6
, Section 4.1]
and in Section
6.3
below.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

We have
r<n
.
∎

2.5.
Ample reduction

We say that system (
2.1
) has
ample reduction
for a prime
p
if

(2.12)

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}/p\Big]=n-1\,,

that is, the rank
r=\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}/p\Big]
of the module

\mathcal{M}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions takes the possible maximum
value
n-1
.

Example 2.4
.

If
q>n
,
p=lq+q-1
for some
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and
m_{i}=1
,
i=1,\dots,n
. Then

M_{i}=((q-1)p-1)/q=p-l-1
and

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{M_{i}}{p}\Big]=\Big[n\frac{(q-1)p-1}{pq}\Big]=\Big[n-\frac{n}{q}-\frac{n}{pq}\Big]=n-1.

Hence under these assumptions system (
2.1
) has ample reduction.

Lemma 2.5
.

If system (
2.1
) has ample reduction for a prime
p
, then for any
l=1,\dots,n-1

and any subset
I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
with
|I|=l
, we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle(l-1)p\,<\,\sum_{i\in I}M_{i}\,<\,lp\,.

Proof.

The second inequality holds since
0<M_{i}<p
for any
i
. Assume that the first inequality is not true and

\sum_{i\in I}M_{i}\leqslant(l-1)p
for some
l
and
I
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\leqslant(l-1)p+\sum_{i\in\bar{I}}M_{i}<(l-1)p+(n-l)p=(n-1)p,

where
\bar{I}
is the complement of
I
. That contradicts to the ampleness of the reduction.
∎

2.6.
Example

Let
n=2
,
p=3
,
q=2
,
m_{1}=m_{2}=1
. Then

M_{1}=M_{2}=1
. The KZ equations take the form

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{1}}=\frac{\Omega}{z_{1}-z_{2}}I,\qquad\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{2}}=\frac{\Omega}{z_{2}-z_{1}}I,\qquad\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}\,\,\,1&-1\\
-1&\,\,\,\,1\end{pmatrix}.

The polynomial

(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}\!\begin{pmatrix}\,\,\,\,1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}
is a solution. At the same time
r=[(M_{1}+M_{2})/p]=0
and there are no
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions.

3.
Coefficients of polynomials

3.1.
Coefficients of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions

For
l=1,\dots,r
, the coordinates of the column vector

I^{[l]}(z)=(I^{[l]}_{1}(z),\dots,I^{[l]}_{1}(z))

are homogeneous polynomials in
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
of degree

(3.1)

\displaystyle\delta_{l}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}M_{j}-lp\,.

Let

\displaystyle I^{[l]}(z)=\sum_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}I^{[l]}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\,,\qquad I^{[l]}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}\,.

Lemma 3.1
.

The coefficient
I^{[l]}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
is nonzero if and only if

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}=\delta_{l},\quad\operatorname{and}\quad d_{i}\leqslant M_{i}\quad\operatorname{for}\quad i=1,\dots,n\,,

moreover,

(3.3)

\displaystyle I^{[l]}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}\,=\,(-1)^{\delta_{l}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\binom{M_{j}}{d_{j}}\,\Big(1-\frac{d_{1}}{M_{1}},\dots,1-\frac{d_{n}}{M_{n}}\Big).

If
(I^{[l]}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n};1},\dots,I^{[l]}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n};n})
are coordinates of

I^{[l]}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}
, then

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,m_{i}\,I^{[l]}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n};i}\,=\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,M_{i}\,I^{[l]}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n};i}\,=\,0\,.

Proof.

The first statements follow from formulas (
2.7
) and (
2.8
).
Formula (
3.4
) follows from formulas (
3.3
), (
2.6
).
∎

3.2.
Coefficients and singular vectors

Consider the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
over the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with standard generators
e,f,h

and relations

[e,f]=h
,
[h,e]=2e
,

.

For
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
,

, let
V_{m}
be the irreducible
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
-module over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with highest weight

-action.

Consider the
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
-module

For

, let

(3.5)

\displaystyle f^{(i)}v:=v_{m_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{m_{i-1}}\otimes fv_{m_{i}}\otimes v_{m_{i+1}}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{m_{n}}\ \in\ \otimes_{j=1}^{n}V_{m_{j}}\,.

\displaystyle hf^{(i)}v=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}-2\Big)f^{(i)}v\,,\qquad ef^{(i)}v=m_{i}\,v_{m_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{m_{i}}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{m_{n}}\,.

the

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Sing}}V[-2]=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}f^{(i)}v\ \Big|\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}m_{i}=0\Big\}\,\subset\,V[-2]\,.

Operators

4.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Beta integral and KZ equations for

4.1.
Solutions over
{\mathbb{C}}

Consider the system of KZ equations (
2.1
) over
{\mathbb{C}}
for
n=2
. Then the master function is

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Phi(t,z_{1},z_{2})=(t-z_{1})^{-m_{1}/q}(t-z_{2})^{-m_{2}/q}\,,

and the one-dimensional space of solutions is generated by the 2-column vector

(4.2)

\displaystyle I(z_{1},z_{2})=\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\Phi(t,z)\Big(\frac{f^{(1)}v}{t-z_{1}}+\frac{f^{(2)}v}{t-z_{2}}\Big)dt.

To determine this integral we assume that
z_{1},z_{2}
are real,
z_{1}<z_{2}
, and
fix a univalued branch on
[z_{1},z_{2}]
of each of the factors
(t-z_{1})^{-m_{1}/q}
,
(t-z_{2})^{-m_{2}/q}
.
Then

(4.3)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!I(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{2}-z_{1})^{-m_{1}/q}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{-m_{2}/q}\frac{\Gamma(-m_{1}/q+1)\Gamma(-m_{1}/q+1)}{\Gamma(-m_{1}/q-m_{2}/q+1)}\Big(\frac{f^{(1)}v}{-m_{1}/q}-\frac{f^{(2)}v}{-m_{2}/q}\Big),

where
(z_{k}-z_{l})^{-m_{l}/q}
is the value of the chosen branch of the function
(t-z_{l})^{-m_{l}/q}
at
t=z_{k}
.

Remark
.

Calculation of each
coordinate of the vector
I(z_{1},z_{2})
is reduced to the beta integral,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha-1}(1-t)^{\beta-1}dt=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}\ ,

after change of variables. Formula (
4.3
) shows how
the beta integral appears in hypergeometric solutions
of KZ equations.

If
(-m_{1}/q,-m_{2}/q)=(M_{1},M_{2})
, where
M_{1},M_{2}
are positive integers, then

(4.5)

\displaystyle I(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{2}-z_{1})^{M_{1}}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{M_{2}}\frac{\Gamma(M_{1}+1)\,\Gamma(M_{2}+1)}{\Gamma(M_{1}+M_{2}+1)}\Big(\frac{f^{(1)}v}{M_{1}}-\frac{f^{(2)}v}{M_{2}}\Big).

4.2.
Factorial and gamma functions

Recall that
p
is an odd prime number. The
p
-adic factorial function is
defined on positive integers by

\displaystyle(x!)_{p}\,=\,\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant x,\ (j,p)=1}\,j\ .

The Morita
p
-adic gamma function is the unique continuous function of a
p
-adic integer
x
(with values in

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) such that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}(x)=(-1)^{x}\prod_{1\leqslant j<x,\ (j,p)=1}\ j\,,

for positive integers
x
. Thus
\Gamma_{p}(x+1)=(-1)^{x}(x!)_{p}
for positive integers
x
.

Define the function

\displaystyle\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

by setting
\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(0)=1
,
\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(1)=-1
and
mapping an integer
x>1
to the image of the integer
\Gamma_{p}(x)
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have
\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(x+p)=\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(x)
for all
x
.

Proof.

The lemma follows from Wilson’s theorem,
(p-1)!\equiv-1
(mod
p
).
∎

We extend the function
\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
to the set
{\mathbb{Z}}
by periodicity,
\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(x+p)=\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(x)
.
Then we get

\displaystyle\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(x)\,\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(1-x)\,=\,(-1)^{x}

also by Wilson’s theorem.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A,B
be positive integers such that
A<p
,
B<p
,
p\leqslant A+B
. Then we have an identity in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle B\,\binom{B-1}{A+B-p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\,\binom{B-1}{p-A-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{A+1}\,\frac{A!\,B!}{(A+B-p)!}=(-1)^{A}\,\frac{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(A+1)\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(B+1)}{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(A+B-p+1)}\,.

∎

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle B\binom{B-1}{p-A-1}=\frac{B(B-1)\cdots(A+B-p+1)}{1\cdots(p-A-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{B\cdots(A+B-p+1)(A+B-p)!A!}{(-1)^{p-A-1}(p-1)(p-2)\cdots(A+1)\,A!(A+B-p)!}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{A+1}\,\frac{A!\,B!}{(A+B-p)!}=(-1)^{A}\,\frac{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(A+1)\,\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(B+1)}{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(A+B-p+1)}\,.

∎

4.3.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions

Consider the system of KZ equations (
2.1
) over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
for

.
Assume that system (

\displaystyle 0\,<M_{1},\,M_{2},\,M_{1}+M_{2}-p+1\,<\,p\,.

In this case the module

-hypergeometric solutions is of rank one and generated by

in the Taylor expansion
of the polynomial 2-vector

(4.8)

\displaystyle P(x,z_{1},z_{2})=(x-z_{1})^{M_{1}}(x-z_{2})^{M_{2}}\Big(\frac{f^{(1)}v}{x-z_{1}}+\frac{f^{(2)}v}{x-z_{2}}\Big),

cf. (
4.2
)

Theorem 4.3
.

We have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}I^{[1]}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{M_{2}}(z_{2}-z_{1})^{M_{1}+M_{2}-p}\frac{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{1}+1)\,\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{2}+1)}{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{1}+M_{2}-p+1)}\Big(\frac{f^{(1)}v}{M_{1}}-\frac{f^{(2)}v}{M_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{M_{1}}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{M_{1}+M_{2}-p}\frac{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{1}+1)\,\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{2}+1)}{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{1}+M_{2}-p+1)}\Big(\frac{f^{(2)}v}{M_{2}}-\frac{f^{(1)}v}{M_{1}}\Big),

cf. (

Proof.

Make the transformation

\displaystyle P(x,z_{1},z_{2})\ \mapsto\ P(x+z_{1},z_{1},z_{2})=x^{M_{1}}(x+z_{1}-z_{2})^{M_{2}}\Big(\frac{f^{(1)}v}{x}+\frac{f^{(2)}v}{x+z_{1}-z_{2}}\Big).

in the Taylor expansion

and the proof of

\displaystyle I^{[1]}(z_{1},z_{2})\,=\,(z_{1}-z_{2})^{M_{1}+M_{2}-p}\Big(\binom{M_{2}}{p-M_{1}}f^{(1)}v+\binom{M_{2}-1}{p-M_{1}-1}f^{(2)}v\Big).

Then

(4.10)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}\binom{M_{2}}{p-M_{1}}f^{(1)}v+\binom{M_{2}-1}{p-M_{1}-1}f^{(2)}v=M_{2}\binom{M_{2}-1}{p-M_{1}-1}\Big(\frac{f^{(1)}v}{p-M_{1}}+\frac{f^{(2)}v}{M_{2}}\Big).

Now the theorem follows from Lemma
4.2
.
∎

5.1.

\displaystyle z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\,,\qquad d_{1},\dots,d_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}\,,

For a nonzero polynomial

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\,

let

be the summand

corresponding to the largest

with a nonzero coefficient. We call

the
\sigma
-leading term
of

, the corresponding

the
\sigma
-leading coefficient
, the corresponding

z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
the
\sigma
-leading monomial
.

In particular, consider elements
f(z)=(f_{1}(z),\dots,f_{n}(z))\in({\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z])^{n}
as polynomials
in
z
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
. Then for any
\sigma\in S_{n}
a nonzero element
f(z)
has

\sigma
-leading term

f_{\sigma}(z)=a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
and
\sigma
-leading coefficient

.

Consider the lexicographical ordering

of monomials
corresponding to the identity permutation

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}C_{j}=0,\quad\sum_{l=j+1}^{n}\,\Omega_{jl}\,C\,=\,qd_{j}\,C\,,\quad j=1,\dots,n-1\,,\quad d_{n}\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,p)\,,

For

Let a pair

\displaystyle\neq

\displaystyle=

Lemma 7.3

The function

(7.6)

\displaystyle(n-1)\sum_{j=1}^{n}M_{j}-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\,p

(7.8)

If

6.1

(7.9)

\displaystyle I^{[l]}_{\operatorname{id}}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{n-l}M_{j}}\,\frac{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{n-l}+1)\,\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\!\left(\sum_{j={n-l}+1}^{n}M_{j}-(l-1)p+1\right)}{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\!\left(\sum_{j={n-l}}^{n}M_{j}-lp+1\right)}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sum_{j={n-l}+1}^{n}f^{(j)}v}{\sum_{j={n-l}+1}^{n}M_{j}}-\frac{f^{({n-l})}v}{M_{{n-l}}}\right)z_{1}^{M_{1}}z_{2}^{M_{2}}\dots z_{{{n-l}-1}}^{M_{{{n-l}-1}}}z_{{n-l}}^{\sum_{j={n-l}}^{n}M_{j}\,-\,lp}\,,

Expanding vectors
\Big(\frac{\sum_{j={n-l}+1}^{n}f^{(j)}v}{\sum_{j={n-l}+1}^{n}M_{j}}-\frac{f^{({n-l})}v}{M_{{n-l}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\det C({\operatorname{id}})=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)M_{j}}\,\frac{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{1}+1)\cdots\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{n}+1)}{\Gamma_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{1}+\dots+M_{n}-(n-1)p+1)}\,.

Lemma 7.4

(7.10)

Lemma 7.6
.

For any
i\neq j
, the polynomial
\det c(z)
is divisible by
(z_{i}-z_{j})^{M_{i}+M_{j}-p}
.

Proof.

We will prove that
\det c(z)
is divisible by
(z_{n-1}-z_{n})^{M_{n-1}+M_{n}-p}
. The divisibility by

(z_{i}-z_{j})^{M_{i}+M_{j}-p}
for other

Introduce new variables
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
by the equaions:
u_{n}=z_{1}+\dots+z_{n}

(7.13)

\displaystyle z_{j+1}-z_{j}=u_{1}u_{2}\dots u_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,n-1.

The variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
are polynomials in

Hence if

is a polynomial solution of system (
2.1
), then

is a polynomial
solution of the transformed differential KZ equations, which take the form:

(7.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I}{\partial u_{i}}=\Big(\frac{\sum_{j>i}\Omega_{ij}^{M}}{u_{i}}+\operatorname{Reg}_{i}(u)\Big)I,\quad i=1,\dots,n-1,\qquad\frac{\partial I}{\partial u_{n}}=0,

where

(7.15)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I}{\partial u_{n-1}}=\Big(\frac{\Omega_{n-1,n}^{M}}{u_{n-1}}+\operatorname{Reg}_{n-1}\Big)I.

Hence the polynomial
\det c(z(u))
a solution of the system of differential equations;

(7.16)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial u_{i}}=\Big(\frac{\sum_{j>i}(M_{i}+M_{j})}{u_{i}}+\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Reg}_{i}(u)\Big)y,\quad i=1,\dots,n-1,\qquad\frac{\partial y}{\partial u_{n}}=0,

Theorem 8.1
.

is a homogeneous polynomial solution of system (

(8.2)

8.2.
L
-admissible solutions and filtration on space of all polynomial solutions

Let
L=

(L_{1}
,…,
L_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geqslant 0}
. Let
I(z)
be a polynomial in
z
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
.
We say that
I(z)
is
L
-admissible
if

(8.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{L_{j}+1}I}{\partial z_{i}^{L_{j}+1}}(z)=0,\qquad j=1,\dots,n\,.

Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{L}
the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z^{p}]
-module of all
L
-admissible polynomial solutions of (
2.1
).

For example,
\mathcal{M}_{(0,\dots,0)}
consists of polynomial solutions

I(z)=(I_{1}(z)
, …,
I_{n}(z))
lying in
({\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z^{p}])^{n}
, in other words,
it consists
of all

, such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\Omega_{ij}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}I(z)=0\,,\quad i=1,\dots,n,\qquad m_{1}I_{1}(z)+\dots+m_{n}I_{n}(z)=0.

In particular, if system (
2.1
) has constant solutions then they lie in

\mathcal{M}_{(0,\dots,0)}

The modules
\mathcal{M}_{L}
form a filtration on the module
of all polynomial solutions of system (
2.1
). Namely,
if
L=(L_{1},\dots,L_{n})
,
L^{\prime}=(L_{1}^{\prime},\dots,L_{n}^{\prime})
and
L_{j}\leqslant L_{j}^{\prime}
for all
j
, then

\mathcal{M}_{L}\subset\mathcal{M}_{L^{\prime}}
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
L=(M_{1}+1,\dots,M_{n}+1)
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{L}
coincides with the module
\mathcal{M}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions
of system (
2.1
).

Proof.

Any
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solution
I^{[l]}(z)
lies in
\mathcal{M}_{L}
by construction. We show that any element
I(z)\in\mathcal{M}_{L}

is a linear combination of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric
solutions with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z^{p}]
. Since system (
2.1
) is homogeneous,
it is enough to assume that
I(z)
is a homogeneous polynomial.

Assume that
I(z)
is a homogeneous polynomial.
Let
(0,\dots,0,C_{i},\dots,C_{n})z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
be the
{\operatorname{id}}
-leading term of
I(z)
,
where
1\leqslant i<n
,

.
Divide each

by

\displaystyle d_{j}=q_{j}p+r_{j}\,,\qquad 0\leqslant r_{j}<p,\qquad j=1,\dots,n\,.

Then
r_{j}\leqslant M_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n
, since

,
and
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
has the form

(M_{1},\dots,M_{i-1},r_{i},0,\dots,0)

,
by Theorem

.
We have

\displaystyle r_{i}\,=\,\sum_{j=i}^{n}M_{j}\,-\,lp\,

for some positive integer

, by Corollary
5.4
.

Consider the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solution
I^{[l]}(z)
. By Theorem
5.3
the

{\operatorname{id}}
-leading term of

\displaystyle(0,\dots,0,C^{l}_{i},\dots,C_{n}^{l})\,z_{1}^{M_{1}}\dots z_{i-1}^{M_{i-1}}z_{i}^{r_{i}}\,,

\displaystyle(0,\dots,0,C_{i},\dots,C_{n})\,=\,c\,(0,\dots,0,C^{l}_{i},\dots,C_{n}^{l})\,.

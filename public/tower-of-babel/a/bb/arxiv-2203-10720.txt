Linear Convergence of Generalized Proximal Point Algorithms for Monotone Inclusion Problems
Hui Ouyang

Abstract
We focus on the linear convergence of generalized proximal point algorithms for solving monotone inclusion problems. Under the assumption that the associated monotone operator is metrically subregular or that the inverse of the monotone operator is Lipschitz continuous, we provide Q Q -linear and R R -linear convergence results on generalized proximal point algorithms. Comparisons between our results and related ones in the literature are presented in remarks of this work.

All algorithms considered in this work are based on nonexpansive operators.

(\forall y\in D)

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad\chi_{k}:=\prod^{k}_{i=0}\rho_{i}\text{ and }\xi_{k}:=\sum^{k}_{i=0}\left(\prod^{k}_{j=i+1}\rho_{j}\right)\varepsilon_{i}.

Suppose that
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty
and that
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\chi_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}<1
. Then the following hold.

(i)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\chi_{k}<\infty
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\chi_{k}=0
.

(ii)

Suppose that
(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})

\rho_{k+1}\leq\rho_{k}
. Then
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\xi_{k}<\infty
.

Proof.

Item

(i)
: Because
\chi:=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\chi_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}<1
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
and
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\chi+\varepsilon<1
and

\displaystyle\left(\forall k\geq K\right)\quad\left(\chi_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}<\chi+\varepsilon,\quad\text{that is,}\quad\chi_{k}<\left(\chi+\varepsilon\right)^{k}.

(2.7)

Hence,

\displaystyle(\forall k\geq K)\quad\sum^{k}_{i=0}\chi_{i}=\sum^{K}_{i=0}\chi_{i}+\sum^{k}_{i=K+1}\chi_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lemma:cauchyproduct:chi:EQ}}}{{\leq}}\sum^{K}_{i=0}\chi_{i}+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\chi+\varepsilon\right)^{i}=\sum^{K}_{i=0}\chi_{i}+\frac{1}{1-\left(\chi+\varepsilon\right)}<\infty,

which yields that
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\chi_{k}<\infty
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\chi_{k}=0
.

Item

(ii)
: Suppose that there exists
\bar{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\rho_{\bar{k}}=0
. Then, by the assumption
(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})

\rho_{k+1}\leq\rho_{k}
and
\rho_{k}\geq 0
, we know that
(\forall k\geq\bar{k})

\rho_{k}=0
. So
(\forall k\geq\bar{k})

\left(\forall i\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}\right)

\prod^{k}_{j=i+1}\rho_{j}=0
, which implies that
(\forall k\geq\bar{k})

\xi_{k}=0
. Hence, in this case, it is trivial that
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\xi_{k}<\infty
.

Suppose that
(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})

\rho_{k}>0
.
Notice that

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad\xi_{k}=\sum^{k}_{i=0}\left(\prod^{k}_{j=i+1}\rho_{j}\right)\varepsilon_{i}=\sum^{k}_{i=0}\left(\left(\prod^{k-i}_{j=0}\rho_{j}\right)\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\frac{\left(\prod^{k}_{j=i+1}\rho_{j}\right)}{\left(\prod^{k-i}_{j=0}\rho_{j}\right)}\right)

and that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}

\displaystyle\frac{\prod^{k}_{j=i+1}\rho_{j}}{\prod^{k-i}_{j=0}\rho_{j}}=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\prod^{k}_{j=i+1}\rho_{j}}{\prod^{k-i}_{j=0}\rho_{j}}\quad&\text{if }k-i\leq i+1;\\
\frac{\prod^{k}_{j=k-i+1}\rho_{j}}{\prod^{i}_{j=0}\rho_{j}}\quad&\text{if }k-i>i+1,\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\prod^{k-i}_{j=1}\frac{\rho_{j+i}}{\rho_{j}}\quad&\text{if }k-i\leq i+1;\\
\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\prod^{i}_{j=1}\frac{\rho_{j+k-i}}{\rho_{j}}\quad&\text{if }k-i>i+1,\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{0}},

where we invoke the assumption
(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})

\rho_{k+1}\leq\rho_{k}
in the last inequality.

On the other hand, due to
[
21
, Page 80]
, the Cauchy product
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum^{k}_{i=0}\chi_{k-i}\varepsilon_{i}
of the two absolutely convergent series
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\varepsilon_{k}
and
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\chi_{k}
is absolutely convergent, that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum^{k}_{i=0}\chi_{k-i}\varepsilon_{i}<\infty.

(2.8)

Taking all results obtained above into account, we derive that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\xi_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum^{k}_{i=0}\left(\prod^{k-i}_{j=0}\rho_{j}\right)\varepsilon_{i}=\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum^{k}_{i=0}\chi_{k-i}\varepsilon_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lemma:cauchyproduct:sum:cauchy}}}{{<}}\infty,

which reaches the required inequality in
Item

(ii)
.
∎

Metrical subregularity is a critical assumption for the linear convergence of algorithms studied in this work. In this section, we exhibit results related to metrical subregularity.

Definition 3.1

Theorem 3.4

6
Conclusion and Future Work

In this work, we considered the metrical subregularity of set-valued operators, which is a popular assumption for the linear convergence of optimization algorithms. We
also provided an
R
-linear convergence result on the non-stationary Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann iterations. Because the generalized proximal point algorithm is a special instance of the non-stationary Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann iterations, the result on the non-stationary Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann iterations was applied to the generalized proximal point algorithm. In addition, we showed some

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the existence of positive solutions with prescribed
L^{2}
-norm for the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+V(x)u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\;\;\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\|u\|_{2}=a>0,\\
\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
N\geq 3
,
\mu>0
,
p\in[2+\frac{4}{N},2^{*})
with

2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
, and

\|u\|^{2}_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

The parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
arises as a Lagrange multiplier with respect to the mass constraint
\|u\|_{2}=a
. Here,
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a potential function.

Problem (
1.1
) comes from the study of standing waves for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation

iw_{t}-\Delta w+V(x)w=f(w)\;\;\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),

(1.2)

where
w
has the form

w(x,t)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x),\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),

(1.3)

and
u
is a real function. For problem (
1.2
)-(
1.3
), the
L^{2}
-norm of
u
stands for the mass of a particle. If
f(w)=e^{-i\lambda t}f(u)
, (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) will be reduced to

-\Delta u+V(x)u=\lambda u+f(u)\;\;\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.4)

Such problem for given
\lambda
is called the
fixed frequency problem
, which has been widely studied for the decades. A huge literature exists, we will not intend to summarize here, the interested readers can refer to
[
2
,
5
,
22
]
and references therein. Here we are concerned with the case of prescribed mass, that is, for some fixed
a>0
, we try to find solutions of (
1.4
) on the manifold

S_{a}:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\|u\|_{2}=a\},

(1.5)

with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Commonly, such solutions are called normalized solutions. A natural approach is applying variational method to (
1.4
)-(
1.5
). One can derive solutions to (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) by looking for critical points of the associated energy functional on
S_{a}
,

\displaystyle E_{V}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)u^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)\mathrm{d}x

(1.6)

where

F(u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(t)\mathrm{d}t.

1.1
Non-potential case
V\equiv 0
.

The simplest example is the power nonlinearities
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
. Consider

-\Delta u+u=|u|^{p-2}u\penalty\ \text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\;\;u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.7)

It is well-known that for
p\in(2,2^{*})
, (
1.7
) has a unique positive solution up to a translation, which can be chosen to be radial (see
[
13
]
). If we denote the radial solution by
U_{p}
, one can check that

U_{\lambda,p}(x):=\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-2}}U_{p}({\sqrt{\lambda}}x),\;\;\lambda>0,

is the unique positive solution (up to a translation) to

-\Delta u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u\;\;\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\;\;u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.8)

A direct computation shows that

\|U_{\lambda,p}\|_{2}^{2}=\lambda^{\frac{4-(p-2)N}{2(p-2)}}\|U_{p}\|_{2}^{2}.

If
p\neq\hat{p}:=2+\frac{4}{N}
, for any
a>0
, there exists a unique
\lambda>0
such that
\|U_{\lambda,p}\|_{2}=a
, which means that (
1.8
)
has a unique positive radial normalized solution
u\in S_{a}
for any
p\in(2,\hat{p})\cup(\hat{p},2^{*})
.
While for
p=\hat{p}
, the equation (
1.8
) has a positive normalized solution
u\in S_{a}
if and only if
a=\|U_{p}\|_{2}
. Therefore,
\hat{p}=2+\frac{4}{N}
is called the mass critical exponent or
L^{2}
-critical exponent to (
1.8
).

Moreover, the mass critical exponent plays an important role in the geometry of energy functional. In order to preserve the
L^{2}
-norm, usually we use the scaling

u_{h}(x):=h^{\frac{N}{2}}u(hx),\;\;h>0,

(1.9)

which gives readily

\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{2}^{2}=h^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2},\;\;\|u_{h}\|_{p}^{p}=h^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2}}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

Remark that to have
h^{2}=h^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2}}
for
h\neq 1
, one needs
p=\hat{p}
. Thus, for fixed
u\in S_{a}
, the sign of
p-\hat{p}
decides the shape of
h\mapsto E_{0}(u_{h})
, seeing the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see
[
18
]
):
For any
N\geq 3
and
p\in[2,2^{*}]
, there exists a constant
C_{N,\,p}
depending on
N
,
p
such that

\|u\|_{p}\leq C_{N,\,p}\|u\|_{2}^{1-\gamma_{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\gamma_{p}},\;\;\forall\penalty\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(1.10)

where
\gamma_{p}=\frac{N(p-2)}{2p}
. The inequality holds true also in
N\leq 2
for any
2\leq p<\infty
.

We can observe that

p\gamma_{p}\begin{cases}<2\;\;\text{if}\penalty\ 2<p<\hat{p}\\
=2\;\;\text{if}\penalty\ p=\hat{p}\\
>2\;\;\text{if}\penalty\ \hat{p}<p<2^{*},\\
\end{cases}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\gamma_{2^{*}}=1.

The sign of
p-\hat{p}
decides whether the functional
E_{0}
is bounded from below on
S_{a}
.
For
p=2^{*}
, we have a particular case of (
1.10
), the Sobolev inequality

\mathcal{S}\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2},\;\;\forall\penalty\ u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\penalty\ N\geq 3.

(1.11)

The sharp constant
\mathcal{S}
for (
1.11
) is called the best Sobolev constant. It is well known from
[
23
]
that

\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon,y}(x)=[N(N-2)]^{\frac{N-2}{4}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}+|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}},\;\;\varepsilon>0,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

are the unique functions to achieve (
1.11
), which are also the only classical solutions (see
[
8
]
) to

-\Delta u=u^{2^{*}-1},\;\;u>0\;\;\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

Clearly,
\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon,y}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if and only if
N\geq 5
. Thus, for the critical case
p=2^{*}
, (
1.8
) admits a positive radial normalized solution
\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon,0}\in S_{a}
with
\lambda=0
and a unique choice of
\varepsilon>0
if
N\geq 5
, and no positive solution exists any more if
N\leq 4
.

For nonhomogeneous nonlinearities, the scaling method does not work. Jeanjean
[
10
]
did a seminal work and considered a class of mass supercritical and Sobolev subcritical problem where
f(u)
can be chosen as
\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}a_{j}|u|^{\sigma_{j}-2}u
with
k\geq 1,a_{j}>0
and

\hat{p}<\sigma_{j}<2^{*}\;\;\text{if}\penalty\ N\geq 3;\;\;\hat{p}<\sigma_{j}\;\;\text{if}\penalty\ N=1,2.

He showed that the energy functional
E_{0}
possesses a mountain pass geometry on
S_{a}
. A crucial step in
[
10
]
is to construct an augmented functional

\widetilde{E_{0}}(u,h):=E_{0}(u_{h}),\;\;(u,h)\in S_{a}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},

in order to obtain a Palais-Smale sequence approaching the Pohozaev manifold

\mathcal{P}_{0}:=\left\{u\in S_{a}:\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}E_{0}(u_{h})\Big|_{h=1}=0\right\}.

For more references on normalized solution problem with Sobolev subcritical growth, we refer to
[
20
,
12
]
and references therein. A lot of works have been done following Jeanjean’s method, see for instance
[
3
,
17
,
12
,
21
,
20
]
. Here we are mainly concerned with the Sobolev critical growth problem.

Recently, Soave
[
21
]
considered the combined nonlinearities involving Sobolev critical exponent

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\;\;\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\|u\|_{2}=a>0,\\
\end{cases}

(1.12)

where
N\geq 3
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. The corresponding energy functional to (
1.12
) is

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}},\;\;u\in S_{a}.

Recall that a ground state of (
1.12
) is a solution having the minimal energy among all solutions. The ground state of (
1.12
) can be obtained by finding the minimizers of
J_{\mu}
on the
Pohozaev manifold

\mathcal{P}_{\mu}:=\{u\in S_{a}:\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}=0\}.

Let

m_{a}:=\underset{u\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}}{\inf}{J_{\mu}(u)}.

(1.13)

Soave investigated the existence of ground state solutions to (
1.12
) for different exponent
p
.
He proved that for
p\in(2,2^{*})
, (
1.13
) can be attained provided
N\geq 3
,
\mu
,
a>0
and

\mu a^{{p(1-\gamma_{p})}}<\alpha_{N,\,p}

(1.14)

for some
\alpha_{N,\,p}>0
, see
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
. Also, he proposed some natural questions, including

(
Q_{1}
)

: Does
J_{\mu}|_{S_{a}}
have a critical point of mountain-pass type in the case
p\in(2,\hat{p})
?

(
Q_{2}
)

: Does
J_{\mu}|_{S_{a}}
have a ground state if
\mu>0
and
\mu a^{{p(1-\gamma_{p})}}
is large?

Jeanjean and Le
[
11
]
and Wei and Wu
[
24
]
answered question
(Q_{1})
for
N\geq 4
and
N=3
respectively. Under the condition (
1.14
), the authors in
[
11
,
24
]
showed that
J_{\mu}
has a mountain pass geometry around the ground state, and the mountain pass level
M_{a}
can be upper bounded by

M_{a}<m_{a}+\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}^{\frac{N}{2}}.

(1.15)

It was verified in
[
11
, Proposition 1.11]
that for
N\geq 3
, if the mountain pass level
M_{a}
satisfies (
1.15
), then any radial Palais-Smale sequence with level
M_{a}
approaching the Pohozaev manifold
P_{\mu}
is relatively compact in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, Wei and Wu also answered
(Q_{2})
in
[
24
]
for
p\in[\hat{p},2^{*})
. Now we summarize the existence results of
[
21
,
24
]
for (
1.12
) with
p\in[\hat{p},2^{*})
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
\hat{p}\leq p<2^{*}
and let
\mu,a>0
.

(i)

If
p=\hat{p}
, then for

\mu a^{\frac{4}{N}}<\alpha:=\frac{\hat{p}}{2(C_{N,\hat{p}})^{\hat{p}}},

m_{a}
can be attained by some
u
which is positive, radially symmetric, and a solution to (
1.12
) for some
\lambda<0
. Here
C_{N,\hat{p}}
is the best constant for Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality given by (
1.10
). Moreover,
m_{a}
can not be attained if
\mu a^{\frac{4}{N}}\geq\alpha
.

(ii)

If
\hat{p}<p<2^{*}
, then for any
\mu,a>0
,
m_{a}
is attained by some
u
which is positive, radially symmetric, and a solution to (
1.12
) with
\lambda<0
.

Remark 1.2
.

When
p=\hat{p}
, we can get the precise value of
\alpha
owing to
[
21
, (5.1)]
and
[
24
, (3.13)]
.

1.2
Potential case
V\not\equiv 0
.

When
V\not\equiv 0
, many works were done with variational method to find solutions of (
1.4
)-(
1.5
).

1.2.1
Mass-subcritical growth

When
f
has mass-subcritical growth, for example,
f(u)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}a_{j}|u|^{\sigma_{j}-2}u
with
k\geq 1,a_{j}>0
,
N\geq 1
and

2<\sigma_{j}<\hat{p},

the associated energy functional
E_{V}
(see (
1.6
)) is bounded from below on
S_{a}
. In this case, to get a minimizer, the main difficulty is to prove that the minimum level is sub-additive with respect to the mass. For example:

•

In
[
9
]
, Ikoma and Miyamoto assumed that

V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;\;\underset{|x|\to\infty}{\lim}V(x)=\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\sup}V(x)=0;

if
N\geq 5
, the following additional assumption is imposed

V\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;\;\nabla V(x)\cdot x\leq\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{2|x|^{2}}\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ a.e.\penalty\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}.

They applied the concentration-compactness arguments of Lions
[
15
,
16
]
.

•

Zhong and Zou
[
26
]
considered
V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying

\underset{|x|\to\infty}{\lim}V(x)=\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\sup}V(x)\in(0,\infty],\;\;V(0)=\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\min}V(x).

They presented a new approach based on iteration to obtain the strictly sub-additive inequality.

•

Alves and Ji
[
1
]
considered
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
with
p\in(2,\hat{p})
, and a positive,
{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
-periodic (or asymptotically periodic) potential
V
. In their proof,
{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
-periodicity prevents the vanishing of the minimizing sequence at infinity.

1.2.2
Mass-supercritical and Sobolev subcritical growth

When
f
has mass-supercritical and Sobolev subcritical growth, for example
f(u)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}a_{j}|u|^{\sigma_{j}-2}u
with
k\geq 1,a_{j}>0
and

\hat{p}<\sigma_{j}<2^{*}\;\;\text{if}\penalty\ N\geq 3;\;\;\hat{p}<\sigma_{j}\;\;\text{if}\penalty\ N=1,2,

the energy functional is unbounded from below on
S_{a}
. We often use the mountain pass geometry to handle the existence issue of solutions to (
1.4
)-(
1.5
).

•

Molle
et al.

[
17
]
investigated the case
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
with
p\in(\hat{p},2^{*})
. They constructed a splitting lemma to obtain the compactness of Palais-Smale sequence. If moreover

\max\{\|V\|_{N/2},\left\|V(x)|x|\right\|_{N}\}<L,\;\;V(x)\leq 0,

for some
L=L(N,p)>0
, they derived a mountain-pass solution at a positive level.

•

Bartsch
et al.

[
3
]
discussed the non-trapping potential case, with

V(x)\geq\underset{|x|\to+\infty}{\lim\inf}\,V(x)>-\infty,

where the mountain pass structure by Jeanjean is destroyed. They constructed a linking geometry developed by
[
4
]
, and using a minimax argument, they obtained the existence of solutions with high Morse index.

•

In
[
6
]
, Ding and Zhong treated the case for more general
f
with mass super-critical and Sobolev subcritical growth, satisfying some Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz type condition. They assumed that
V
is negative, twice differentiable a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and required some compactness conditions or Poincaré inequality on
V
, which yields that the Pohozaev manifold is a natural constraint. By considering a minimizing sequence on the Pohozaev manifold, they derived existence results.

•

The Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction approach also has been applied to problem (
1.4
)-(
1.5
), see
[
19
, Section 3]
.

1.3
Existence results with Sobolev critical growth

It seems that all the above works can not be directly extended to the nonlinearities involving Sobolev critical growth. As far as we are aware, we did not find any works on normalized solution with Sobolev critical growth and potential
V\not\equiv 0
. Motivated by
[
24
,
21
]
for the non-potential case, we consider (
1.1
) with mass-critical or mass-supercritical, and Sobolev critical nonlinearities. For potential
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we will use the following assumptions:

(V_{1})

\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}V(x)=\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\sup}V(x)=:V_{\infty}<\infty
and there exists
\sigma_{1}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|V-V_{\infty}\right|u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\sigma_{1}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2},\;\;\forall\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.16)

(V_{2})

Let
W(x):=\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla V(x),x\rangle
, there holds
\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}W(x)=0
and there is
\sigma_{2}>0
such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\frac{1}{2}(V-V_{\infty})+\frac{1}{p\gamma_{p}}W\right]u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\sigma_{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2},\;\;\forall\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.17)

(\widetilde{V}_{2})

There exists
\widetilde{\sigma}_{2}>0
such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Wu^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\widetilde{\sigma}_{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}},\;\;\forall\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.18)

(V_{3})

\underset{|x|\to\infty}{\lim}|x|(V-V_{\infty})(x)=0
. There exists
{\sigma_{3}}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(V-V_{\infty}\right)^{2}|x|^{2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq{\sigma_{3}^{2}}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2},\;\;\forall\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.19)

(V_{4})

V+W\leq V_{\infty}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Remark 1.3
.

The condition (
1.19
) implies (
1.16
). In fact, using Hardy’s inequality, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|V-V_{\infty}\right|u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\|(V-V_{\infty})|x|u\|_{2}\left\|\frac{u}{|x|}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{2}{N-2}\sigma_{3}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus
\sigma_{1}\leq\frac{2}{N-2}\sigma_{3}
.

Remark 1.4
.

In
(V_{1})
, if
V_{\infty}\neq 0
, one may replace
(V,\lambda)
by
(V-V_{\infty},\lambda+V_{\infty})
. Hence, without loss of generality, we can assume that
V_{\infty}=0
.

By virtue of Remark
1.4
, from now on,
we assume that
V_{\infty}=0
. Problem (
1.1
) can be regarded as a perturbation problem of
[
21
,
24
]
, whose energy functional is given by

\displaystyle J_{\mu,V}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)u^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\mu}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}.

Recall that for
p\in[\hat{p},2^{*})
, the solutions of
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
are mountain pass type solutions of Jeanjean. In present paper, we seek for
V
to preserve the mountain pass geometry of
J_{\mu,V}
on
S_{a}
. When different integrability assumptions are required on
V
, the Pohozaev identity for (
1.1
) could have two different type of forms, that is,

(a)

if
V\in L_{loc}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies (
1.19

2.3

P_{\mu,V}(u):=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{N}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)u^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)u\nabla u\cdot x\mathrm{d}x-\mu\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}=0;

(1.20)

(b)

if
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and (
1.16

2.3
) will satisfy

\widetilde{P}_{\mu,V}(u):=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W(x)u^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\mu\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}=0.

(1.21)

We will work with either (
1.20
) or (
1.21

In previous section, we have obtained a bounded Palais-Smale sequence

Lemma 5.1

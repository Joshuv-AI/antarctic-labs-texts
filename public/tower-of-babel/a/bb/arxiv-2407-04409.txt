The Fibonacci-Fubini and Lucas-Fubini numbers
Yahia Djemmada 1 , Abdelghani Mehdaoui 2 , László Németh 3 , and László Szalay 4

Abstract
Based on the combinatorial interpretation of the ordered Bell numbers, which count all the ordered partitions of the set [ n ] = { 1 , 2 , … , n } [n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\} , we introduce the Fibonacci partition as a Fibonacci permutation of its blocks. Then we define the Fibonacci-Fubini numbers that count the total number of Fibonacci partitions of [ n ] [n] . We study the classical properties of this sequence (generating function, explicit and Dobiński-like formula, etc.), we give combinatorial interpretation, and we extensively examine the Fibonacci-Fubini arithmetic triangle. We give some associate linear recurrence sequences, where in some sequences the Stirling numbers of the first and second kinds appear as well. Key Words: Ordered partitions, Fubini numbers, Fibonacci permutations, Lucas permutations, Stirling numbers. MSC 2020: 05A15, 05A18, 11B39, 11B37, 11B73.

The Stirling numbers of the second kind satisfy the recurrence relation

{n\brace k}={n-1\brace k-1}+k{n-1\brace k},\quad\text{for all }1\leq k\leq n,

(1)

with initial condition

Their explicit formula is given by

\displaystyle{n\brace k}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}j^{n},

(2)

and their exponential generating function is

\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{k}}{k!}.

(3)

Expanding on this, authors often explore the Fubini numbers or ordered Bell numbers (A000670 in the OEIS

w_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!{n\brace k},\quad\text{for all }0\leq n.

denotes the number of partitions into exactly

The Fibonacci-Fubini numbers

Let
S_{n}
denote the set of all
n
-permutations of the set
[n]
.
A permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
, represented as
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{n}
, is termed a
Fibonacci permutation
if it satisfies the condition
|\sigma_{i}-i|\leq 1
for each element
i
, see
[
4
,
5
]
.
In other words, each element
i
in a Fibonacci permutation should either be in its original position or in a position adjacent to
i
(either
i-1
or
i+1
). For instance, the
4
-permutation
1243
is a Fibonacci permutation, whereas
1423
is not because
(\sigma_{2}-2>1)
.

Foundas et al.
[
5
,
4
]
demonstrated that the count of Fibonacci permutations aligns with the well-known Fibonacci sequence. The authors established that the number of
n
-permutations of Fibonacci type is equal to
F_{n+1}
, where
F_{n}
defined by recurrence
F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}
for
2\leq n
, and with initial values
F_{0}=0
,
F_{1}=1
. Moreover, it is sequence A000045 in OEIS
[
6
]
.

Additionally, the number of Fibonacci permutations can be expressed using Binet’s formula given by

F_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta},

(4)

where
\alpha=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
,
\beta=(1-\sqrt{5})/2
, and
\alpha-\beta=\sqrt{5}
. The Binet’s formula of the well-known Lucas sequence
L_{n}
(defined by
L_{n}=L_{n-1}+L_{n-2}
for
2\leq n
,
L_{0}=2
,
L_{1}=1
, and A000032 in OEIS
[
6
]
) is

L_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n}.

(5)

In our current research, we focus on exploring the number of Fibonacci-ordered partitions. In other words, we examine the arrangements of blocks such that each block is either in its original position or in one of the positions of its neighbors.

Definition 1
.

A Fibonacci ordered partition
\pi_{F}
of the set
[n]
is a Fibonacci permutation of its blocks
B_{1}|B_{2}|\ldots|B_{k}
. We represent this as

\pi_{F}([n])=B_{\sigma_{1}}|B_{\sigma_{2}}|\ldots|B_{\sigma_{k}},

where
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{k}
is a Fibonacci permutation.

Let Fibonacci-Fubini numbers be the total numbers of Fibonacci-ordered partitions, denoted
\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

Proposition 1
.

The Fibonacci-Fubini numbers
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are given by

\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}F_{k+1}{n\brace k}.

(6)

Proof.

Using a combinatorial argument, the number of partitions having exactly
k
blocks is
{n\brace k}
; the number of Fibonacci permutations of the blocks is
F_{k+1}
. Therefore, the number of Fibonacci ordered partitions is
F_{k+1}{n\brace k}
. Summing it over
k
we count the total number of Fibonacci ordered partitions, which yields the result.
∎

Based on equality (
6
), we define the Lucas-Fubini numbers
(\mathcal{L}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
by

\mathcal{L}_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{k+1}{n\brace k}.

(7)

Computing the first few item of the Fubini, Fibonacci-Fubini and Lucas-Fubini sequences we obtain

\displaystyle(w_{n})_{n\geq 0}=

\displaystyle(1,1,3,13,75,541,4683,47293,545835,7087261,102247563,\ldots),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 0}=

\displaystyle(1,1,3,10,38,164,791,4194,24138,149423,988033,6939682,\ldots),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{n})_{n\geq 0}=

\displaystyle(1,3,7,22,84,366,1771,9388,53990,334093,2209039,15516716,\ldots).

In the last section, we give a detailed combinatorial interpretation of the Fibonacci-Fubini number.

3
Some properties of Fibonacci-Fubini and Lucas-Fubini numbers

In this section, we give some fundamental properties of Fibonacci-Fubini and Lucas-Fubini numbers.

3.1
Explicit formulas

Theorem 1
.

The explicit formula for the Fibonacci-Fubini numbers
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\alpha^{k+1}-\beta^{k+1}}{k!}j^{n}.

Proof.

Using the explicit formula of Stirling numbers (
2
) and the Binet formula (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}F_{k+1}\;{n\brace k}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\alpha^{k+1}-\beta^{k+1}}{\alpha-\beta}\cdot\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}j^{n}.

Since
\alpha-\beta=\sqrt{5}
, we obtain the statement.
∎

Using the explicit formula of Stirling numbers (
2
) and the Binet formula (
5
) one can prove similarly the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

The explicit formula of the Lucas-Fubini numbers
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\alpha^{k+1}+\beta^{k+1}}{k!}j^{n}.

3.2
Exponential generating functions

Let
\mathcal{F}(t)
and
\mathcal{L}(t)
be the exponential generator functions of Fibonacci-Fubini and Lucas-Fubini numbers, respectively. Then

\mathcal{F}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{L}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Theorem 3
.

The exponential generating function of the sequence
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\alpha e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-\beta e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right).

Proof.

Using the Binet formula (
4
) for the Fibonacci numbers,
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}=0
for
n<k
, and the generating function (
3
)
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}F_{k+1}\;{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\alpha^{k+1}-\beta^{k+1}}{\alpha-\beta}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\alpha^{k+1}\sum_{n\geq 0}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}-\beta^{k+1}\sum_{n\geq 0}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\alpha^{k+1}\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{k}}{k!}-\beta^{k+1}\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{k}}{k!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\left(\alpha\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left(\alpha(e^{t}-1)\right)^{k}}{k!}-\beta\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left(\beta(e^{t}-1)\right)^{k}}{k!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\alpha e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-\beta e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right).

∎

Theorem 4
.

The exponential generating function for the
\mathcal{L}_{n}
numbers is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)=\alpha e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}+\beta e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}.

(8)

Proof.

Using the Binet formula (
5
) for the Lucas numbers, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{k+1}\;{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\alpha^{k+1}+\beta^{k+1}\right){n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\alpha^{k+1}\sum_{n\geq 0}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}+\beta^{k+1}\sum_{n\geq 0}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\alpha^{k+1}\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{k}}{k!}+\beta^{k+1}\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{k}}{k!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left(\alpha(e^{t}-1)\right)^{k}}{k!}+\beta\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left(\beta(e^{t}-1)\right)^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}+\beta e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}.

∎

3.3
Dobiński-like formulas

In this subsection, we give the Dobiński-like formulas of the Fibonacci-Fubini and Lucas-Fubini numbers. Dobiński
[
3
]
gave the useful formula
(1/e)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k^{n}/k!)
for Bell numbers.

Theorem 5
.

For all
n\geq 0
the Dobiński-like formula of Fibonacci-Fubini numbers is

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{e^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}k^{n}}{k!}-\frac{\beta}{e^{\beta}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}k^{n}}{k!}\right).

Proof.

From Theorem
3
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{F}_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\alpha e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-\beta e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\alpha e^{\alpha e^{t}}e^{-\alpha}-\beta e^{\beta e^{t}}e^{-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{e^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}e^{kt}}{k!}-\frac{\beta}{e^{\beta}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}e^{kt}}{k!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{e^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{k^{n}t^{n}}{n!}-\frac{\beta}{e^{\beta}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}}{k!}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{k^{n}t^{n}}{n!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{e^{\alpha}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}k^{n}}{k!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}-\frac{\beta}{e^{\beta}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}k^{n}}{k!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{e^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}k^{n}}{k!}-\frac{\beta}{e^{\beta}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}k^{n}}{k!}\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By comparing the coefficients of
{t^{n}}/{n!}
on both sides we get the result.
∎

Theorem 6
.

The Dobinski-like formula for the Lucas-Fubini numbers is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}=\frac{\alpha}{e^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}k^{n}}{k!}+\frac{\beta}{e^{\beta}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}k^{n}}{k!}.

(9)

Proof.

Similarly, from Theorem
4
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{L}_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=

\displaystyle\alpha e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}+\beta e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha e^{\alpha e^{t}}e^{-\alpha}+\beta e^{\beta e^{t}}e^{-\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{e^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}e^{kt}}{k!}+\frac{\beta}{e^{\beta}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}e^{kt}}{k!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{e^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{k^{n}t^{n}}{n!}+\frac{\beta}{e^{\beta}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}}{k!}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{k^{n}t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{e^{\alpha}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}k^{n}}{k!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}+\frac{\beta}{e^{\beta}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}k^{n}}{k!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\frac{\alpha}{e^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}k^{n}}{k!}+\frac{\beta}{e^{\beta}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}k^{n}}{k!}\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

The comparison of the coefficients of
{t^{n}}/{n!}
on the two sides implies the result.
∎

3.4
Higher order derivatives

In this subsection, derivative of order
r
of exponential generating function of the Fibonacci-Fubini and Lucas-Fubini numbers.

Theorem 7
.

The
r
th higher derivative of the exponential generating function of the Fibonacci-Fubini numbers is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(r)}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}e^{it}{r\brace i}\left(\alpha^{i+1}e^{\alpha(e^{t}-1)}-\beta^{i+1}e^{\beta(e^{t}-1)}\right).

Proof.

We prove the theorem using induction on
r
.
For
r=1
,

\mathcal{F}^{(1)}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}e^{t}\left(\alpha^{2}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-\beta^{2}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right)=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(t).

We suppose the equation is true for
r-1
and show it holds for
r
.

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}^{(r-1)}(t)\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}e^{it}{r-1\brace i}\left(\alpha^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-\beta^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right)\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(r)}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{r-1\brace i}\left(\alpha^{i+1}e^{e^{(t-1)\alpha}+it}-\beta^{i+1}e^{e^{(t-1)\beta}+it}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{r-1\brace i}\left(\left(\alpha e^{t}+i\right)\alpha^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha+it}-\left(\beta e^{t}+i\right)\beta^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta+it}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{r-1\brace i}\left(e^{(i+1)t}\alpha^{i+2}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-e^{(i+1)t}\beta^{i+2}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}i{r-1\brace i}e^{it}\left(\alpha^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-\beta^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{r}{r-1\brace i-1}\left(e^{it}\alpha^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-e^{it}\beta^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}i{r-1\brace i}e^{it}\left(\alpha^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-\beta^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left({r-1\brace i-1}+i{r-1\brace i}\right)\left(e^{it}\left(\alpha^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-\beta^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}{r\brace i}e^{it}\left(\alpha^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\alpha}-\beta^{i+1}e^{\left(e^{t}-1\right)\beta}\right).

Recall that
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r-1}{i-1}=0
when
i=1
.
∎

Theorem 8
.

The
r
th higher derivative of the exponential generating function of the Lucas-Fubini numbers is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(r)}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}e^{it}{r\brace i}\left(\alpha^{i+1}e^{\alpha(e^{t}-1)}+\beta^{i+1}e^{\beta(e^{t}-1)}\right).

Since the proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
7
, we leave it to the reader.

k

0=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{k-j+1}\mathcal{F}(n-j,k),\quad n\geq k+1,

where the coefficients are the Stirling numbers of the first kind (see the triangle A008276 in the OEIS

Proof.

Recall the identities for the Stirling numbers of the first kind for
n\geq 1
that
{n\brack k}={n-1\brack k-1}-(n-1){n-1\brack k}
for
k\geq 1
, moreover,
{n\brack 0}=0
,
{n\brack 1}=(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!
,
{n\brack n}=1
, and
{n\brack k}=0
, if
k>n

We prove the statement by induction on
k

If
k=1

\sum_{j=0}^{1}{2\brack 2-j}\mathcal{F}(n-j,k)=\mathcal{F}(n,1)-\mathcal{F}(n-1,1)=0

Suppose that (

k-1

0=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{k\brack k-j}\mathcal{F}(n-j,k-1)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{k\brack k-j}F_{k}{n-j\brace k-1},\text{ for }n\geq k.

Indeed

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{k\brack k-j}{n-j\brace k-1}=0

(12)

also holds.
Using the induction hypothesis (
12

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k+1\brack k-j+1}\mathcal{F}(n-j,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k+1\brack k-j+1}\mathcal{F}(n-j,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k+1\brack k-j+1}F_{k+1}{n-j\brace k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left({k\brack k-j}-k{k\brack k-j+1}\right){n-j\brace k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{k+1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{k\brack k-j}{n-j\brace k}-k\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k\brack k-j+1}{n-j\brace k}\right)

\displaystyle F_{k+1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{k\brack k-j}\left({n-j\brace k}-k{n-j-1\brace k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{k\brack k-j}{n-j\brace k-1}=0.

Theorem 11
.

The characteristic polynomial of recurrence sequence
(d_{n}^{(r)})
is

q^{(r)}(x)=(x^{2}-x-1)^{2r-1}.

Proof.

The zeros
\alpha
and
\beta
of
q^{(r)}(x)
both have multiplicity
2r-1
.
Note that
\beta=(1-\sqrt{5})/2
is the algebraic conjugate of
\alpha=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
. Hence it is sufficient to prove that
d_{n}^{(r)}
can be written in the form

d_{n}^{(r)}=p_{r}(n)\alpha^{n+2-r}+\bar{p}_{r}(n)\beta^{n+2-r},

where
p_{r}(n)
is a polynomial of
n
with coefficients from
\mathbb{Q}[\alpha]
,
\deg(p_{r})=2r-2
, and
\bar{p}_{r}(n)
is conjugate of
p_{r}(n)
(i.e.,
\alpha
is replaced by
\beta
in the coefficients).

We will use the technique of induction. Obviously, the statement is true for
r=1
. Indeed,

d_{n}^{(1)}=\mathcal{F}(n,n)=F_{n+1}{n\brace n}=F_{n+1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\alpha^{n+1}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\beta^{n+1}\quad(n\geq 1),

that is
p_{1}(n)=1/\sqrt{5}
is a constant polynomial (
\deg(p_{1})=0
),
\bar{p}_{1}(n)=-1/\sqrt{5}
.

Assume now that the theorem is true for some
r\geq 1
. We will prove that there exist polynomial
p_{r+1}(n)
with the given conditions above. In order to do that we are looking for
d_{r+1}(n+1)
in the form

d_{n+1}^{(r+1)}=p_{r+1}(n+1)\alpha^{n+2-r}+\bar{p}_{r+1}(n+1)\beta^{n+2-r},

and will show the (unique) existence of it.
Multiply (
13
) by
F_{n+1-r}
, and use the induction hypothesis to obtain

F_{n+1-r}\left(p_{r+1}(n+1)\alpha^{n+2-r}+\bar{p}_{r+1}(n+1)\beta^{n+2-r}\right)=\\
F_{n+2-r}\left(p_{r+1}(n)\alpha^{n+1-r}+\bar{p}_{r+1}(n)\beta^{n+1-r}\right)\\
+(n+1-r)F_{n+1-r}\left(p_{r}(n)\alpha^{n+2-r}+\bar{p}_{r}(n)\beta^{n+2-r}\right).

Recall that
\alpha\beta=-1
. Eliminate the coefficients of the terms
\alpha^{2n+3-2r}
,
\beta^{2n+3-2r}
, and
(-1)^{n+1-r}
, respectively. We obtain

\displaystyle p_{r+1}(n+1)

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}p_{r+1}(n)+(n+1-r)p_{r}(n),

\displaystyle-\bar{p}_{r+1}(n+1)

\displaystyle=-\bar{p}_{r+1}(n)-(n+1-r)\bar{p}_{r}(n),

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{r+1}(n+1)\beta-{p}_{r+1}(n+1)\alpha

\displaystyle=-{p}_{r+1}(n)\beta+\bar{p}_{r+1}(n)\alpha+(n+1-r)\bar{p}_{r}(n)\beta

\displaystyle-(n+1-r){p}_{r}(n)\alpha,

respectively. The first two equalities are equivalent since they are the conjugates of each other. The first information leads to

(n+1-r)p_{r}(n)=p_{r+1}(n+1)-p_{r+1}(n)
. Plug it into the third equality. After straightforward calculations we have

(\alpha-\beta)(p_{r+1}(n)+\bar{p}_{r+1}(n))=0,

subsequently
\bar{p}_{r+1}(n)=-p_{r+1}(n)
follows since
\alpha-\beta=\sqrt{5}\neq 0
.

Thus we need to consider only the first equality
p_{r+1}(n+1)=p_{r+1}(n)+(n+1-r)p_{r}(n)
again. Clearly, it is equivalent to

p_{r+1}(n+1)-p_{r+1}(n)=(n+1-r)p_{r}(n),

(14)

and from the theory of difference equations we know that this condition, together with the ”final condition”

it provides uniquely the polynomial
p_{r+1}(n)
. Since
\deg(p_{r})=2r-2
and
(n+1-r)
is a linear factor, then the degree of

is

.
∎

Combinatorial interpretation of Fibonacci-Fubini numbers

We give a combinatorial problem of packing goods on a train in two different ways, the solution of which is given by the Fibonacci-Fubini numbers. Suppose that we have
n
items of goods, which we distribute into
k
boxes and load the boxes onto a train of containers as follows.

Given
n
and
k
identical and labeled objects and not labeled boxes, respectively. Put the objects into the boxes so that each box contains at least one object. Thus,
k\leq n
. Then sort the boxes in order of the smallest number of elements and label the boxes according to that.

If the empty boxes were originally labeled and ordered, then the objects are placed in the boxes by putting the first one in the first box; the second one either in the first or in the second box. We always put the next object in the sequence into one of the non-empty boxes, or into the next labeled non-empty box. This is also the way to get the sequence of previous filled boxes. Figure
1
shows the second type of boxing.

We also have a
k
-box-long train and on it some
2
-box-long containers. Load the boxes on the train in order from the front of the train, but if there is a container next in line, put the box in the back of the container (e.g., the box always slides backwards in the container).

It is easy to see that the number of possible trains with containers and boxes having objects is
\mathcal{F}(n,k)
, the sum considering all
k
from
1
to
n
is the Fibonacci-Fubini number
\mathcal{F}_{n}
. In Figure
2
, there is a container in the front of the train, in Figure

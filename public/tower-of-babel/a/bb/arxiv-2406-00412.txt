Essential norm of generalized Integral type Operators from Mixed-Norm to Zygmund-Type Spaces
Y. Estaremi, M. Hassanlou and M. S. Al Ghafri

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the generalized integration operator from mixed-norm space into Zygmund-type and Bloch-type spaces and find an estimation for the essential norm of this operator.

47B33; 47B38; 30H05.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

We denote by
\mathbb{D}
the open unit disc in the complex plan
\mathbb{C}
and
H(\mathbb{D})
the space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. Every analytic function (self-map)
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
induces a linear operator via composition called composition operator
C_{\varphi}
,
C_{\varphi}f=f\circ\varphi
. A complete history of these operators on the spaces of analytic functions can be found in
[
3
]
. Throughout the recent decades there has been a significant interest on investigating the operator properties of composition operator and generalization of it while they act on some spaces of analytic or measurable functions. The above operator can be generalized by

\left(C_{\varphi}^{g}f\right)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f^{\prime}(\varphi(\xi))g(\xi)\ d\xi,\quad f\in H(\mathbb{D}),z\in\mathbb{D},

where
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
, see
[
5
]
. Also, if we replace n-th derivative with 1-th derivative, then we have

\left(C_{\varphi,g}^{n}f\right)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f^{(n)}(\varphi(\xi))g(\xi)\ d\xi,\quad f\in H(\mathbb{D}),z\in\mathbb{D},

where
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. If
n=0
, then
C_{\varphi,g}^{0}
is the Volterra composition operator. Also for
\varphi(z)=z
and
g^{\prime}
we have Volterra type operator
T_{g}
,

T_{g}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(w)g^{\prime}(w)dw.

C_{\varphi,g}^{n}
is called generalized integration operator, see for example
[
4
,
12
]
and references therein. A natural question dealing with this kind of operators or other classes of operators is that, under which conditions they are bounded, compact, have closed range and
\cdots
. Another aspect is computing or estimating norm, essential norm, numerical range,
\cdots
of the operator. All characterizations are in terms of symbols induce the operators.

The aim of this paper is to estimate the essential norm of generalized integration operators from mixed-norm spaces, which include some well-known spaces of analytic functions, into weighted Zygmund space. As a result, compactness criteria for the operator, has been proved in
[
4
]
, is obtained.

A positive continuous function
\phi
on
[0,1)
is called normal if there are two constants
b>a>0
such that

(i)

\frac{\phi(r)}{(1-r)^{a}}
is non-increasing in
[0,1)
and

\frac{\phi(r)}{(1-r)^{a}}\downarrow 0

(ii)

\frac{\phi(r)}{(1-r)^{b}}
is non-decreasing in
[0,1)
and

\frac{\phi(r)}{(1-r)^{b}}\uparrow\infty

as
r\rightarrow 1^{-}
. For
0<p,q<\infty
and
\phi
normal, the mixed-norm space
H(p,q,\phi)
consists of all analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
for which

\|f\|_{p,q,\phi}^{p}=\int_{0}^{1}M_{q}^{p}(f,r)\frac{\phi^{p}(r)}{1-r}dr<\infty

where

M_{q}(f,r)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{q}d\theta\right)^{1/q}.

If
1\leq p<\infty
, then
H(p,q,\phi)
becomes a Banach space equipped with the norm

\|.\|_{p,q,\phi}
. In the case
0<p<1
,
\|.\|_{p,q,\phi}
is a quasi-norm on

H(p,q,\phi)
and this space is a Fr
\acute{e}
chet space but is not a Banach space.
For
\alpha>-1
,
\phi(r)=(1-r)^{(\alpha+1)/p}
is a normal function. If
p=q
, then
H(p,p,(1-r)^{(\alpha+1)/p})
is weighted Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}
which is defined by

A_{\alpha}^{p}=\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)<\infty\},

where
dA
is the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}
.

A positive continuous function
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is called weight.
For a weight
\mu
, the weighted Zygmund space
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
, Zygmund-type space, is the space of
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
such that

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|f^{\prime\prime}(z)|<\infty.

The weighted Zygmond space
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is a Banach space with respect to the norm:

\|f\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}=|f(0)|+|f^{\prime}(0)|+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|f^{\prime\prime}(z)|.

The weighted Bloch space
\mathcal{B}_{\mu}
defined by

\mathcal{B}_{\mu}=\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\|f\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}=|f(0)|+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty\}.

Note that if
\mu(z)=(1-z^{2})
, then we obtain classical Zygmund space
\mathcal{Z}
and Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
.
For a complete information about classical Bloch space, see
[
13
]
.

Hu in
[
6
]
has characterized those holomorphic symbols on the unit ball for which the induced extended ces
\grave{a}
ro operator is bounded (compact) on mixed norm space. The boundedness of ces
\grave{a}
ro operators on mixed norm space is studied in
[
7
]
. Boundedness and compactness of the generalized
integration operator from a mixed norm space into a weighted Zygmund space investigated by Gu and Liu in
[
4
]
. More results on integral type operators acting on mixed norm, Zygmond and Bloch spaces can be found in
[
1
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

Gu and Liu in
[
4
]
have characterized compact generalized integration operators
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}
between mixed norm and weighted Zygmund spaces. Indeed, they have proved that
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is compact if and only if

A=\lim_{|\varphi(z)|\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n+1}}=0

B=\lim_{|\varphi(z)|\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n}}=0

In this paper, motivated by the above results, we find an approximation of the essential norm of the generalized integration operators
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
and prove that

\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}\|_{e}\approx\max\{A,B\},

it means that there exists constants
C,D
such that
CB\leq A\leq DB
or equivalently
CA\leq B\leq DA
.

Recall that for any operator
T
between two Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, the essential norm of
T
is denoted by
\|T\|_{e,X\to Y}
and is defined as follows

\|T\|_{e,X\rightarrow Y}=\inf\{\|T-S\|:\ S\ \text{is a compact operator from}\ X\ \text{to}\ Y\}.

The operator
T
is compact if and only if
\|T\|_{e,X\to Y}=0
.

2.
Essential norm of
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
or
\mathcal{B}_{\mu}

In this section we give an estimation for the essential norm of the operator
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
or
\mathcal{B}_{\mu}
. First we recall a fundamental lemma for later use in the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
10
]

Assume that
p,q\in(0,\infty)
,
\phi
is normal and
f\in H(p,q,\phi)
. Then
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there is a positive constant
C
in dependent of
f

such that

|f^{(n)}(z)|\leq C\frac{\|f\|_{p,q,\phi}}{\phi(|z|)(1-|z|^{2})^{1/q+n}},\ \ \ z\in\mathbb{D}.

The following lemma can be obtained by a standard argument, see
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. Then

C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is compact if and only if
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is bounded and
for any bounded sequence
\{f_{k}\}
in
H(p,q,\phi)
which converges uniformly to
zero on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}f_{k}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

By the above observations, in the next Proposition, we get that every bounded generalized integration operator
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}
with
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}<1
, is compact.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty)
,
\phi
is normal,
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
\varphi
be a analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. If
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}<1
and the operator
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is bounded then
it is compact

Proof.

Since
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}
is bounded, then there exists a positive constant
C
such that for

(2.1)

\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}f\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}\leq C\|f\|_{p,q,\phi},

for all
f\in H(p,q,\phi)
. Let
f_{1}(z)=\frac{z^{n}}{n!}
. Then we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)|=\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}f_{1}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}\leq C\|f_{1}\|_{p,q,\phi}<\infty.

Similarly for
f_{2}(z)=\frac{z^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}
, by using triangle inequality and the fact that
|\varphi(z)|<1
, we get that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)|<\infty.

Now assume that
{\{f_{k}\}}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a bounded sequence in
H(p,q,\phi)
such that converges to
0
, uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, as
k\to\infty
. This implies that
\{f_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
\{f_{k}^{\prime\prime}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
converge uniformly to
0
, on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, as
k\to\infty
.
By the Lemma (
2.2
) and Proposition (
2.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}f_{k}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}=

\displaystyle|(C_{\varphi,g}^{n}f_{k})^{\prime}(0)|+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(z)|(C_{\varphi,g}^{n}f_{k})^{\prime\prime}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f_{k}^{(n)}(\varphi(0))g(0)|+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|(C_{\varphi,g}^{n}f_{k})^{\prime\prime}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f_{k}^{(n)}(\varphi(0))g(0)|+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|f_{k}^{(n+1)}(\varphi(z))\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)|

\displaystyle+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|f_{k}^{(n)}(\varphi(z))g^{\prime}(z)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|f_{k}^{(n)}(\varphi(0))g(0)|+\sup_{|\varphi(z)|\leq\rho}\mu(|z|)|f_{k}^{(n+1)}(\varphi(z))\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)|

\displaystyle+\sup_{|\varphi(z)|\leq\rho}\mu(|z|)|f_{k}^{(n)}(\varphi(z))g^{\prime}(z)|,

which tends to
0
, as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Here
\rho=\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
,
\rho\in(0,1)
. Hence by Lemma
2.2
,
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}
is compact.
∎

Now, in the next Theorem we estimate the essential norm of
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
. We use
\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}\|_{e}
for the essential norm
of
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
instead of
\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}\|_{e,H(p,q,\phi)\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty)
,
\phi
is normal and
\varphi
be a analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. If the operator
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is bounded then

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}\|_{e}\approx\max\{

\displaystyle\limsup_{|\varphi(z)|\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n+1}},

\displaystyle\limsup_{|\varphi(z)|\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n}}\}.

Proof.

If
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}<1
, then by the Proposition
2.3
, the operator
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}
is compact and therefore
\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}\|_{e}=0
. Also by Theorem 2.7 of
[
4
]
, we have

\limsup_{|\varphi(z)|\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n+1}}=0

and

\limsup_{|\varphi(z)|\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n}}=0.

Now assume that
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}=1
.
Let
\{z_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|\varphi(z_{j})|\rightarrow 1
, as
j\rightarrow\infty
.
Since
\phi
is a normal function, then we have a positive constant
b
from the definition. Put

\alpha=1/q+b+1
and define the sequence
\{f_{k}\}
as follows

\displaystyle f_{k}(z)=\frac{(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{b+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha}}-\frac{\alpha(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})}{(\alpha+n)(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha+1}}\right).

Direct calculations shows that

\displaystyle f_{k}^{(n)}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{b+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)}\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}^{n}}{(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha+n}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{b+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)}\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)(\alpha+n)(1-|\varphi(z_{n})|^{2})\overline{\varphi(z_{n})}^{n}}{(\alpha+n)(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha+n+1}}

\displaystyle f_{k}^{(n+1)}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{b+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)}\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n)\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}^{n+1}}{(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha+n}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{b+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)}\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)(\alpha+n)(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}^{n}}{(\alpha+n)(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha+n+1}}.

It is not difficult to show that
\{f_{k}\}
is a bounded sequence in
H(p,q,\phi)
, i.e., there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(2.4)

\sup_{k}{\|f_{k}\|_{p,q,\phi}}\leq C.

Also, it is convergent to zero uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, we have

f_{k}^{(n)}(\varphi(z_{k}))=0,\ \ f_{k}^{(n+1)}(\varphi(z_{k}))=-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}^{n+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{1/q+n+1}}.

Therefore for any compact operator
T:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|Tf_{k}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}=0.

By these observations we get that

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-T\|

\displaystyle\succeq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}f_{k}-Tf_{k}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\succeq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}f_{k}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}-\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Tf_{k}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\succeq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(|z_{k}|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z_{k})g(z_{k})||\varphi(z_{k})|^{n+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{1/q+n+1}}.

And so

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}\|_{e}=\inf_{T}\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-T\|\succeq\limsup_{|\varphi(z)|\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n+1}}.

Now we define the sequence
\{h_{k}\}
as follows

\displaystyle h_{k}(z)=\frac{(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{b+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)}\left(\frac{\alpha+n+1}{(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha}}-\frac{\alpha(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})}{(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha+1}}\right).

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle h_{k}^{(n)}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{b+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)}\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)(\alpha+n+1)\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}^{n}}{(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha+n}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{b+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)}\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n)(1-|\varphi(z_{n})|^{2})\overline{\varphi(z_{n})}^{n}}{(\alpha+n)(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha+n+1}}

\displaystyle h_{k}^{(n+1)}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{b+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)}\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n+1)\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}^{n+1}}{(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha+n+1}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{b+1}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)}\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n+1))(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}^{n+1}}{(1-\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}z)^{\alpha+n+2}}.

Then
\{h_{k}\}
is a bounded sequence in
H(p,q,\phi)
converging to zero uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
and

h_{k}^{(n)}(\varphi(z_{k}))=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)\overline{\varphi(z_{k})}^{n}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{1/q+n}},\ \ h_{k}^{(n+1)}(\varphi(z_{k}))=0.

Hence

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|Th_{k}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}=0,

for any compact operator
T:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
.
By definition of the operator norm we have

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-T\|

\displaystyle\succeq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}h_{k}-Th_{k}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\succeq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}h_{k}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}-\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Th_{k}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\succeq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(|z_{k}|)|g(z_{k})||\varphi(z_{k})|^{n}}{\phi(|\varphi(z_{k})|)(1-|\varphi(z_{k})|^{2})^{1/q+n+}}.

Therefore, using the definition of the essential norm we obtain

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}\|_{e}=\inf_{T}\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-T\|\succeq\limsup_{|\varphi(z)|\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|g(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n}}.

From (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), the lower estimate of the essential norm is achieved.

Suppose that
\{r_{j}\}
be a sequence in
(0,1)
and
r_{j}\rightarrow 1
as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Since
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\mu}
is bounded, then it is clear that
C_{r_{j}\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\mu}
is also bounded. Also, since
\|r_{j}\varphi\|_{\infty}<1
, then by Proposition
2.3
, we get that
C_{\varphi,g}^{n}:H(p,q,\phi)\rightarrow{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\mu}
is a compact operator for any

. Thus

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}\|_{e}\leq\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-C_{r_{j}\varphi,g}^{n}\|=\sup_{\|f\|_{p,q,\phi}\leq 1}\|(C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-C_{r_{j}\varphi,g}^{n})f\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}},\ \ \ j\in\mathbb{N}.

Now let
f\in H(p,q,\phi)
and
\|f\|_{Q_{K}(p,q)}\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\|(C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-C_{r_{j}\varphi,g}^{n})f\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}=

\displaystyle|((C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-C_{r_{j}\varphi,g}^{n})f)(0)|+|((C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-C_{r_{j}\varphi,g}^{n})f)^{\prime}(0)|

\displaystyle+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|{((C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-C_{r_{j}\varphi,g}^{n})f)^{\prime\prime}}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(f^{(n)}(\varphi(0))-f^{(n)}(r_{j}\varphi(0)))g(0)|

\displaystyle+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)(f^{(n+1)}(\varphi(z))-r_{j}f^{(n+1)}(r_{j}\varphi(z)))|

\displaystyle+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)(f^{(n)}(\varphi(z))-f^{(n)}(r_{j}\varphi(z)))|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|(f^{(n)}(\varphi(0))-f^{(n)}(r_{j}\varphi(0)))g(0)|

\displaystyle+\sup_{|\varphi(z)|\leq\delta}\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)(f^{(n+1)}(\varphi(z))-r_{j}f^{(n+1)}(r_{j}\varphi(z)))|

\displaystyle+\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)(f^{(n+1)}(\varphi(z))-r_{j}f^{(n+1)}(r_{j}\varphi(z)))|

\displaystyle+\sup_{|\varphi(z)|\leq\delta}\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)(f^{(n)}(\varphi(z))-f^{(n)}(r_{j}\varphi(z)))|

(2.7)

\displaystyle+\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)(f^{(n)}(\varphi(z))-f^{(n)}(r_{j}\varphi(z)))|,

where
\delta\in(0,1)
, is fixed.
Since
f_{r_{j}}\rightarrow f
uniformly on compact

\mathbb{D}

|(f^{(n)}(\varphi(0))-f^{(n)}(r_{j}\varphi(0)))g(0)|\rightarrow 0

\limsup_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|\varphi(z)|\leq\delta}\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)(f^{(n+1)}(\varphi(z))-r_{j}f^{(n+1)}(r_{j}\varphi(z)))|=0

\limsup_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|\varphi(z)|\leq\delta}\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)(f^{(n)}(\varphi(z))-f^{(n)}(r_{j}\varphi(z)))|=0.

Here we use (

\displaystyle\|(C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-C_{r_{j}\varphi,g}^{n})f\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)(f^{(n+1)}(\varphi(z))-r_{j}f^{(n+1)}(r_{j}\varphi(z)))|

\displaystyle+\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)(f^{(n)}(\varphi(z))-f^{(n)}(r_{j}\varphi(z)))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)f^{(n+1)}(\varphi(z))|

\displaystyle+\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)r_{j}f^{(n+1)}(r_{j}\varphi(z))|

\displaystyle+\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)f^{(n)}(\varphi(z)))|

\displaystyle+\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)f^{(n)}(r_{j}\varphi(z))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n+1}}

(2.8)

\displaystyle+2C\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n}}.

So

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}\|_{e}\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{p,q,\phi}\leq 1}\|(C_{\varphi,g}^{n}-C_{r_{j}\varphi,g}^{n})f\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n+1}}

\displaystyle+2C\sup_{\delta<|\varphi(z)|<1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n}}.

Therefore as
\delta\rightarrow 1
we obtain

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi,g}^{n}\|_{e}\preceq\max\{

\displaystyle\limsup_{|\varphi(z)|\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)g(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n+1}},

\displaystyle\limsup_{|\varphi(z)|\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu(|z|)|g^{\prime}(z)|}{\phi(|\varphi(z)|)(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{1/q+n}}\}.

This completes the proof.
∎

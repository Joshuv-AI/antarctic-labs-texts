Ciprian Demeter

Abstract
. We prove (essentially) sharp L 4 L^{4} level set estimates for the periodic Schrödinger maximal operator in a certain range of the cut-off parameter.



1.
Introduction

We use number theory and elementary graph theory to prove the following level set estimate.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\|a_{n}\|_{l^{2}}=1
and
N^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{10}}{\;\lessapprox}\;\lambda\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then

|\{x\in[0,1]:\;\sup_{t}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}t)|\geq\lambda\}|{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{N}{\lambda^{4}}.

(1)

Our result covers a range of estimates in the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Assume
\|a_{n}\|_{l^{2}}=1
and
N^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq\lambda\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then

|\{x\in[0,1]:\;\sup_{t}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}t)|\geq\lambda\}|{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{N}{\lambda^{4}}.

Apart from the
N^{\epsilon}
losses hidden in the symbol
{\;\lessapprox}\;
, the conjectured level set estimates are sharp for all
\lambda
. Indeed, recall (see e.g. Proposition 13.4 in
[
8
]
) that if
q\sim Q\ll N
and

x\in\bigcup_{b=0}^{q-1}\;[\frac{b}{q}-\frac{o(1)}{N},\frac{b}{q}+\frac{o(1)}{N}]\,

then for each
q\sim Q
we have

|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(nx+\frac{n^{2}}{q})|\sim\frac{N}{q^{1/2}}.

When
Q=\frac{N}{\lambda^{2}}\ll N^{1/2}
, the intervals in
\bigcup_{q\sim Q}\bigcup_{1\leq b\leq q-1\atop{{\operatorname{gcd}}(b,q)=1}}\;[\frac{b}{q}-\frac{o(1)}{N},\frac{b}{q}+\frac{o(1)}{N}]
are pairwise disjoint, so this set has measure
\sim\frac{Q^{2}}{N}=\frac{N}{\lambda^{4}}
. Moreover, letting
a_{n}=N^{-1/2}
, we find that for each
x
in this set we have

\sup_{t}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}t)|\sim\frac{N^{1/2}}{Q^{1/2}}=\lambda.

Conjecture
1.2
admits the following equivalent reformulation.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Assume
\|a_{n}\|_{l^{2}}=1
. Then for each
p\leq 4

\|\sup_{t}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}t)|\|_{L^{p}([0,1],dx)}{\;\lessapprox}\;N^{1/4}.

This is an estimate about the solution of the free Schrödinger equation on the torus. If proved, it would imply that, under enough Sobolev regularity, the solution converges to the initial data almost everywhere as
t\to 0
. The Euclidean version of this problem has been solved by Carleson
[
6
]
. There are higher dimensional versions of Conjecture
1.2
that will not be investigated here. The more recent papers
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
solved the Euclidean version in all dimensions.

The best upper bound in the literature for Conjecture
1.3
is
{\;\lessapprox}\;N^{\frac{1}{3}}
, in the range
1\leq p\leq 6
, see
[
13
]
. This is only sharp for
p=6
. The simplest argument to prove it uses Bourgain’s
L^{6}
inequality

\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}t)\|_{L^{6}([0,1]^{2},dxdt)}{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{n}\|_{l^{2}}

(2)

and the fact that
|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}t)|
is essentially constant in
t
at scale
1/N^{2}
.

No level set estimates (
1
) exist in the literature, neither do they follow from the moment estimate (
2
) or from the known
N^{1/3}
bound in Conjecture
1.3
. On the other hand, in the last section we show how this latter bound is easily recovered using our estimates.

In Section
2
we use a variant of the
TT^{*}

method to reduce Conjecture
1.2
to a (potentially stronger) conjecture with deep arithmetic flavor. We are left with a question that involves the quadratic kernel
{\mathcal{K}}
. Using the known sharp estimates for its size, we are led to considering Conjecture
2.4
. The rest of the paper is devoted to verifying this conjecture in a certain range. We develop and operate in a rather combinatorial framework that takes advantage of sharp number theoretical input. In Section
4
we refine the combinatorics to suggest a further improvement to the range
\lambda{\;\gtrapprox}\;N^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{12}}
, subject to some potentially difficult solution counting. Apart from this latter step of purely number theoretic nature, the rest of the argument works (and in fact is explicitly written) for
\lambda{\;\gtrapprox}\;N^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{12}}
. Further improvements of this range seem possible with a more efficient use of various structures that arise in the argument.

The paper
[
12
]
shows that the upper bound
N^{1/3}
is sharp for Conjecture
1.3
, within the general framework of convex sequences that fall under the scope of
l^{2}
decoupling,
[
5
]
. Our methods here exploit the specific nature of the kernel
{\mathcal{K}}
, and are fundamentally different from decoupling. Notably, our argument involves no Fourier analysis.

We would like to mention that Conjecture
1.3
has been verified when all
a_{n}
are equal to 1, first by Barron
[
2
]
with epsilon losses, and shortly after by Baker
[
1
]
. The latter identified the precise magnitude

\|\sup_{t}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(nx+n^{2}t)|\|_{L^{p}([0,1],dx)}\sim N^{a(p)}(\log N)^{b(p)}

for each
p\geq 1
.

Specializing to other sequences
a_{n}
, Theorem
1.1
leads to some potentially interesting consequences. We mention one of them, for reader’s amusement. It may hint about the number theoretic flavor of our argument, and about the potential difficulties involved in proving the full Conjecture
1.2
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{N}
be a subset of
\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
with density
{\;\gtrapprox}\;1
, such as the primes, or any of their subsets of logarithmic density. Then for each
\theta\in[0,1]

|\{x\in[0,1]:\;\sup_{t}|\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{S}}_{N}}e(nx+n^{2}t+n^{3}\theta)|\geq\lambda\}|{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{N}{\lambda^{4}}

for each
\lambda{\;\gtrapprox}\;N^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{10}}
.

2.
Initial reductions

Let
w
be an arbitrary Schwartz function supported on
[-2,2]
. It will suffice to prove

|\{x:\;\sup_{t}|\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}w(\frac{n}{N})e(nx+n^{2}t)|\geq\lambda\}|{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{N}{\lambda^{4}}.

We write
\lambda=MN^{1/4}
, with
1\leq M\leq N^{\frac{1}{4}}
. As a consequence of the Fourier Uncertainty Principle, the function
|\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}w(\frac{n}{N})e(nx+n^{2}t)|
is essentially constant for
(x,t)
in any box of size
1/N\times 1/N^{2}
.
We fix a choice of
1/N
-separated
x_{r}\in[0,1]
and pick
t_{r}\in[0,1]
,
1\leq r\leq R
such that

|\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}w(\frac{n}{N})e(nx_{r}+n^{2}t_{r})|\geq MN^{1/4}.

(4)

We need to prove that

R{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{N}{M^{4}}.

Remark 2.1
.

It can be easily shown using
L^{2}
orthogonality that each
t_{r}
is associated with at most
\lesssim N^{1/2}/M^{2}
points
x_{r}
.

The rest of the argument will depend critically on the way we handle inequality (
4
).
Let
|c_{r}|=1
be such that

|\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}w(\frac{n}{N})e(nx_{r}+n^{2}t_{r})|=c_{r}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}w(\frac{n}{N})e(nx_{r}+n^{2}t_{r}).

Using Cauchy–Schwarz we find

\displaystyle RMN^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=1}^{R}c_{r}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}w(\frac{n}{N})e(nx_{r}+n^{2}t_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}\sum_{r=1}^{R}c_{r}w(\frac{n}{N})e(nx_{r}+n^{2}t_{r})

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\sum_{r=1}^{R}c_{r}w(\frac{n}{N})e(nx_{r}+n^{2}t_{r})|^{2})^{1/2}.

Expanding the square and using the triangle inequality leads to

RMN^{1/4}\leq(\sum_{r,r^{\prime}}|\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}w(\frac{n}{N})^{2}e(n(x_{r}-x_{r^{\prime}})+n^{2}(t_{r}-t_{r^{\prime}}))|)^{1/2}.

Writing
z_{r}=(x_{r},t_{r})
and defining the weighted Gauss sum

{\mathcal{K}}(x,t)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}w(\frac{n}{N})^{2}e(nx+n^{2}t),

we arrive at

R^{2}M^{2}N^{1/2}\leq\sum_{r,r^{\prime}}|{\mathcal{K}}(z_{r}-z_{r^{\prime}})|.

(5)

The derivation of (
5
) is similar to the argument in
[
4
]
. Note that this argument eliminates the coefficients
a_{n}
and reduces things to a question about the kernel
{\mathcal{K}}
. It is a close variant of the
TT^{*}
method.
In
[
7
]
, we used the
TT^{*}
method to prove sharp results for a class of special cases in Conjecture
1.3
.

To exploit (
5
), we recall the following classical upper bounds for
{\mathcal{K}}
, see e.g. Lemma 9.2 in
[
9
]
. Due to periodicity, we may replace the range
[0,1]
for
t
with
[-\frac{1}{N},1-\frac{1}{N}]
. Letting
J_{1,0}=[-\frac{1}{N},\frac{1}{N}]
and
J_{q,a}=[\frac{a}{q}-\frac{1}{Nq},\frac{a}{q}+\frac{1}{Nq}]
for each
2\leq q\leq N
and
1\leq a\leq q-1
with
(a,q)=1
, we recall that these intervals cover
[-\frac{1}{N},1-\frac{1}{N}]
. The intervals may overlap, but that will be irrelevant for our argument.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume
t\in J_{q,a}
, and assume that either
|t-\frac{a}{q}|\sim\frac{1}{2^{l}NQ}
for
2^{l}<N/Q
or that
|t-\frac{a}{q}|\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{l}NQ}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}
when
2^{l}=N/Q
. Then for each
\epsilon>0
we have

|{\mathcal{K}}(x,t)|\lesssim{N^{1/2}}2^{l/2}

if

x\in{\mathcal{M}}=\bigcup_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\;[\frac{b}{q}-\frac{N^{\epsilon}}{Q2^{l}},\frac{b}{q}+\frac{N^{\epsilon}}{Q2^{l}}],

and

|{\mathcal{K}}(x,t)|\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{-100}

if

x\not\in{\mathcal{M}}.

Let us write
{\mathcal{A}}(1)=\{0\}
, and for
2\leq q\leq N

{\mathcal{A}}(q)=\{1\leq a\leq q-1:\;{\operatorname{gcd}}(a,q)=1\}.

Fix
\epsilon
. All further uses of
{\;\lessapprox}\;
will hide implicit dependence on this particular
\epsilon
. For dyadic
1\leq Q\leq N
and for
1\leq 2^{l}\lesssim\frac{N}{Q}
we write

S_{Q,l}=\bigcup_{q\sim Q}\bigcup_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}(q)}\bigcup_{0\leq b\leq q-1}\;[\frac{b}{q}-\frac{N^{\epsilon}}{2^{l}q},\frac{b}{q}+\frac{N^{\epsilon}}{2^{l}q}]\times[\frac{a}{q}-\frac{1}{2^{l}qN},\frac{a}{q}+\frac{1}{2^{l}qN}].

The previous lemma shows that for each
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
we have

|{\mathcal{K}}(x,t)|\lesssim 2^{l/2}N^{1/2}\sum_{1\leq Q\leq N}\sum_{1\leq 2^{l}\lesssim N/Q}\tilde{1}_{S_{Q,l}}(x,t)+N^{-100}.

We have denoted by
\tilde{1}_{S_{Q,l}}
the
1\times 1
-periodic extension of
1_{S_{Q,l}}
.

Remark 2.3
.

In fact, slightly more is true, and this will be used later. Writing

S_{Q,l}^{dyad}=\bigcup_{q\sim Q}\bigcup_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}(q)}\bigcup_{0\leq b\leq q-1}\;[\frac{b}{q}-\frac{N^{\epsilon}}{2^{l}q},\frac{b}{q}+\frac{N^{\epsilon}}{2^{l}q}]\times\{t:\;|t-\frac{a}{q}|\sim\frac{1}{2^{l}qN}\}

for
2^{l}<N/Q
and

S_{Q,l}^{dyad}=S_{Q,l}

for
2^{l}=N/Q
,
we still have

|{\mathcal{K}}(x,t)|{\;\lessapprox}\;2^{l/2}N^{1/2}\sum_{1\leq Q\leq N}\sum_{1\leq 2^{l}\lesssim N/Q}\tilde{1}_{S^{dyad}_{Q,l}}(x,t)+N^{-100}.

Combining this with (
5
) and pigeonholing, we find that there must be a
Q
and some
l
such that

R^{2}{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{2^{l/2}}{M^{2}}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}}\tilde{1}_{S_{Q,l}}(z_{r}-z_{r}^{\prime}).

We note that we must have

M^{4}{\;\lessapprox}\;2^{l},\;\text{and thus also }Q{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{N}{M^{4}}.

(6)

At this point, we make the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2.4
.

Assume
\|a_{n}\|_{l^{2}}=1
.
Assume
1\leq M\leq N^{1/4}
and
1\leq 2^{l}\lesssim\frac{N}{Q}
.

Let
z_{r}=(x_{r},t_{r})
,
1\leq r\leq R
, with
x_{r}

1/N
-separated, satisfy

|\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}w(\frac{n}{N})e(nx_{r}+n^{2}t_{r})|\geq MN^{1/4}

(7)

and

R^{2}{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{2^{l/2}}{M^{2}}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}}\tilde{1}_{S_{Q,l}}(z_{r}-z_{r}^{\prime}).

(8)

Then we have

R{\;\lessapprox}\;M^{-4}N.

(9)

The previous discussion can be summarized as follows.

Proposition 2.5
.

If Conjecture
2.4
holds for some
M
(and all
l,Q
), then the level set estimate

|\{x\in[0,1]:\;\sup_{t}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}t)|\geq\lambda\}|{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{N}{\lambda^{4}}

also holds for
\lambda\sim MN^{1/4}
.

Conjecture
2.4
is true when
M\ggg N^{1/8}
, without hypothesis (
7
), and with (
8
) replaced by the weaker hypothesis

R^{2}{\;\lessapprox}\;\frac{2^{l/2}}{M^{2}}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}}\tilde{1}_{X_{Q,l}}(x_{r}-x_{r}^{\prime}),

(10)

where

X_{Q,l}=\bigcup_{q\sim Q}\bigcup_{0\leq b\leq q-1}\;[\frac{b}{q}-\frac{N^{\epsilon}}{2^{l}q},\frac{b}{q}+\frac{N^{\epsilon}}{2^{l}q}].

The proof (cf.
[
3
]
) follows the Fourier argument in
[
4
]
and does not make use of the
t
component.

If only the weaker hypothesis (
10
) for the
x
component is assumed, (
9
) is false when
M{\;\lessapprox}\;N^{1/8}
. Indeed, assume
Q=2^{l}=N^{1/2}/100
. Then it is easy to see that
X_{Q,l}
has measure
\sim 1
. Indeed, there are
\sim Q^{2}\sim N
distinct fractions
b/q
with
q\sim Q
, and the corresponding intervals
[\frac{b}{q}-\frac{1}{N},\frac{b}{q}+\frac{1}{N}]
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
X_{Q,l}
.
Using this and the fact that
X_{Q,l}
is a union of intervals of length
\geq 1/N
, we guarantee that
X_{Q,l}
contains
\sim N
of the points
j/N
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. For each such
j
, there are exactly
N
pairs
(r/N,r^{\prime}/N)
with
r,r^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, such that
\frac{r}{N}-\frac{r^{\prime}}{N}\equiv\frac{j}{N}\pmod{1}
. This implies that
\tilde{1}_{X_{Q,l}}(\frac{r}{N}-\frac{r^{\prime}}{N})=1
for
\sim N^{2}
such pairs. Thus, taking
x_{r}=r/N
for
1\leq r\leq N
, (
10
) is trivially satisfied since
M^{4}{\;\lessapprox}\;2^{l}
. On the other hand,
R{\;\lessapprox}\;M^{-4}N
fails when
M\ggg 1
, since
R=N
.

Let us discuss a sharp example for Conjecture
2.4
. Fix a prime
q\sim Q
. Consider a maximal family of
1/N
-separated points
x_{i}
, each lying in the
1/Q2^{l}
neighborhood of some
b/q
,
0\leq b\leq q-1
. There are
\sim N/2^{l}
such
x_{i}
. Consider the points
z_{i,j}=(x_{i,j},t_{i,j})=(x_{i}+\frac{j}{Q2^{l}},b/q)
, with
b
the numerator corresponding to
x_{i}
and
2\leq j\leq J
, for some
J\ll 2^{l}
.
It follows that
x_{i,j}
are
1/N
-separated. There are
R\sim JN/2^{l}
points
z_{i,j}
.
Note that if
x_{i}
is near
b/q
and
x_{i^{\prime}}
is near
b^{\prime}/q
with
b\not=b^{\prime}
then
\tilde{1}_{S_{Q,l}}(z_{i,j}-z_{i^{\prime},j})=1
for each
j
. There are
\sim\frac{1}{J}R^{2}
such pairs.
Thus, (
8
) holds if
J\sim 2^{l/2}/M^{2}
. Note also that (
9
) is satisfied, in fact we have the superficially stronger estimate
R\sim\frac{N}{2^{l/2}M^{2}}
.

For the rest of the paper, we will drop the term
N^{\epsilon}
in the definition of
S_{Q,l}
. We do so only to simplify notation, this has no impact on the argument.

It remains to prove Conjecture
2.4
for
M{\;\gtrapprox}\;N^{1/10}
. We find it plausible that the conjecture should be true in the full range of
M
, in fact even without the hypothesis (
7
). The reason we include (
7
) is because our argument in Section

makes use of it at some point.

3.
A combinatorial approach for Conjecture
2.4
in the range
M{\;\gtrapprox}\;N^{1/10}

This section has a few subsections, all contributing to a complete proof of Conjecture
2.4
in the range
M{\;\gtrapprox}\;N^{1/10}
.

We will use the notation for the density parameter

K=\frac{2^{l/2}}{M^{2}}.

(11)

Recall our setup (
8
)

\frac{R^{2}}{K}{\;\lessapprox}\;|\{(r,r^{\prime}):\;z_{r}-z_{r^{\prime}}\in S_{Q,l}\}|,

which forces

K{\;\gtrapprox}\;1.

We need to prove that
R{\;\lessapprox}\;N/M^{4}
. We may assume
K\ll\sqrt{R}
, or else
R\lesssim K^{2}=2^{l}/M^{4}\leq N/M^{4}
, and we are done.

3.1.
A brief outline of the argument

Here is an outline of our approach. We construct a graph
G
with vertex set
(z_{r}:\;1\leq r\leq R)
, by drawing an edge between
z_{r}
and
z_{r^{\prime}}
if
z_{r}-z_{r^{\prime}}\in S_{Q,l}.

By deleting edges if necessary, we may assume that

E(G)\approx R^{2}/K.

(12)

We focus attention on the
popular
pairs
r,r^{\prime}
, those that share at least
R/K^{2}
neighbors
r^{\prime\prime}
.
The equality

z_{r}-z_{r^{\prime}}=(z_{r}-z_{r^{\prime\prime}})+(z_{r^{\prime\prime}}-z_{r^{\prime}})

(13)

guarantees that
z_{r}-z_{r^{\prime}}
has many representations as sums of elements in
S_{Q,l}
. Each of these is associated with a unique triple
q,a,b
, say

z_{r}-z_{r^{\prime\prime}}\in[\frac{b_{1}}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{2^{l}Q},\frac{b_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{2^{l}Q}]\times[\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{2^{l}QN},\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{2^{l}QN}]

z_{r^{\prime\prime}}-z_{r^{\prime}}\in[\frac{b_{2}}{q_{2}}-\frac{1}{2^{l}Q},\frac{b_{2}}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{2^{l}Q}]\times[\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}}-\frac{1}{2^{l}QN},\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{2^{l}QN}].

We will indicate this by writing

(z_{r}-z_{r^{\prime\prime}})\blacksquare(a_{1},b_{1},q_{1}),\;\;(z_{r^{\prime\prime}}-z_{r^{\prime}})\blacksquare(a_{2},b_{2},q_{2}).

(14)

The key task is this: given
z_{r},z_{r^{\prime}}
, find a good upper bound on the number of such
z_{r^{\prime\prime}}
. This has the following reformulation, upon observing that the value of
b_{1}/q_{1}
(or alternatively
b_{2}/q_{2}
) determines the value of
z_{r^{\prime\prime}}
up to
N/Q2^{l}
choices (due to the
1/N
-separation of the
x_{r^{\prime\prime}}
, and the fact that
x_{r^{\prime\prime}}
determines
z_{r^{\prime\prime}}
).

Definition 3.1
.

We cover
[0,1]^{2}
with pairwise disjoint boxes
B=I\times J\subset[0,1]^{2}
, with
|I|\sim 1/2^{l}Q
and
|J|\sim 1/N2^{l}Q
. For each
B
, let
n_{B}
be the number of pairs
(b_{1},q_{1})
with
|b_{1}|\leq q_{1}-1\sim Q
such that there are integers
a_{1},a_{2},b_{2},q_{2}
satisfying
|b_{2}|,|a_{2}|\leq q_{2}-1\sim Q
,
|a_{1}|\leq q_{1}-1
,
{\operatorname{gcd}}(a_{1},q_{1})={\operatorname{gcd}}(a_{2},q_{2})=1
and

(\frac{b_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{b_{2}}{q_{2}},\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}})\in B.

If in this definition we also require that
{\operatorname{gcd}}(q_{1},q_{2})=1
, the number of pairs will be denoted by
n_{B}^{*}
. Note that
n_{B}^{*}\leq n_{B}
.

We can easily close the argument if we find a popular pair
z_{r},z_{r^{\prime}}
, such that if
B
is the box for which
z_{r}-z_{r^{\prime}}\in B
, we have

n_{B}{\;\lessapprox}\;Q.

Indeed, as observed earlier, this forces (

{\;\lessapprox}\;Q\times\frac{N}{Q2^{l}}=\frac{N}{2^{l}}

As we shall soon see, the intended bound

Proposition 3.2

The sum over all (
\sim NQ^{2}2^{2l})

Thus, when

, the average value of

satisfies the intended upper bound

\frac{1}{\sharp B}\sum_{B}n_{B}{\;\lessapprox}\;Q

for all admissible
Q,l
(satisfying (
6
)).

If
M\ggg N^{1/12}
, we have the lower bound

\sum_{B}n_{B}^{*}{\;\gtrapprox}\;Q^{6}.

Thus, when
N^{1/10}\ggg M\ggg N^{1/12}

(and thus also that of

) satisfies the lower bound

\frac{1}{\sharp B}\sum_{B}n_{B}^{*}\ggg Q

for some admissible

(satisfying (

Proof.

Write

N_{B}=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2},q_{1},q_{2}):\;q_{i}\sim Q,\;{\operatorname{gcd}}(a_{i},q_{i})=1,\;(\frac{b_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{b_{2}}{q_{2}},\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}})\in B\}|

(\frac{b_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{b_{2}}{q_{2}},\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}})\in B\text{ and }{\operatorname{gcd}}(q_{1},q_{2})=1\}|.

It is immediate that

and that

Our assumption

(\frac{b_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{b_{2}}{q_{2}},\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}}),\;(\frac{b_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{b_{2}^{\prime}}{q_{2}^{\prime}},\frac{a_{1}^{\prime}}{q_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}^{\prime}}{q_{2}^{\prime}})\in B

Second, since

Third,

It is easy to see that (for example, assume

are both primes)

When

Example 3.3

3.2.
Pigeonholing and graph considerations

The rest of the analysis is driven by the size of the greatest common divisor of

Let

Pigeonholing, we may assume that a big chunk (by that we mean the total number divided by
\log N

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume
{\operatorname{gcd}}(q_{1},q_{2})=d
, so
q_{1}=dm_{1}
,
q_{2}=dm_{2}
,
{\operatorname{gcd}}(m_{1},m_{2})=1
. Assume also that
{\operatorname{gcd}}(a_{1},q_{1})={\operatorname{gcd}}(a_{2},q_{2})=1
. Let

p={\operatorname{gcd}}(\frac{a_{1}q_{2}+a_{2}q_{1}}{d},\frac{q_{1}q_{2}}{d}).

Then
p|d
, so in particular
p\leq d
. Also,
{\operatorname{gcd}}(p,m_{1})={\operatorname{gcd}}(p,m_{2})=1
.

Proof.

Any prime factor
p_{1}
of

must divide
dm_{1}m_{2}
and
m_{2}a_{1}+m_{1}a_{2}
. Assume for contradiction that it divides
m_{1}
. Then it must also divide
m_{2}a_{1}
. But since
(a_{1},m_{1})=1
, we are left with
p_{1}|m_{2}
, contradicting that
(m_{1},m_{2})=1
.

∎

The relevance of

is that it determines the denominator

in the least terms representation

\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}}=\frac{A}{q},\;\;q=\frac{d}{p}m_{1}m_{2},\;{\operatorname{gcd}}(A,q)=1.

Given
b_{1},b_{2}
with
|b_{1}|\leq q_{1}-1
,
|b_{2}|\leq q_{2}-1
, we will also consider the parameter
f
given by

f={\operatorname{gcd}}(\frac{b_{1}q_{2}+b_{2}q_{1}}{d},p).

(16)

Note that we may write

\frac{b_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{b_{2}}{q_{2}}=\frac{B}{\frac{p}{f}q},\;\;q=\frac{d}{p}m_{1}m_{2}.

While
{\operatorname{gcd}}(B,p/f)=1
,
B
and
q
are allowed to share factors.
B
might be equal to zero, in which case

. The parameter

will play a critical role in the counting arguments in Subsections
3.5
and
3.7
. See Remark
3.16
for the subtlety behind our choice of

. See also Remark
3.23
for the key difference between

and

Let us get to the details of the argument. Recall the graph
G
introduced earlier. We will call its vertices

with the letter

For two vertices

in

for the common neighbors. Define the popular pairs

{\mathcal{P}}=\{(v_{1},v_{2}):\;|{\mathcal{N}}(v_{1},v_{2})|\gtrsim R/K^{2}\}.

We rely on the following easy result.

Lemma 3.5
.

For each graph
G
with
R
vertices
V(G)
and
R^{2}/K
edges

,
K\ll\sqrt{R}
, we have

\sum_{(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathcal{P}}}|{\mathcal{N}}(v_{1},v_{2})|\gtrsim R^{3}/K^{2}.

(17)

Proof.

Writing
{\mathcal{N}}_{v}
for the neighbors of

, we note that

\|\sum_{v\in V(G)}1_{{\mathcal{N}}_{v}}\|_{1}=2E(G)\geq R^{2}/K.

Using Hölder we get

\|\sum_{v\in V(G)}1_{{\mathcal{N}}_{v}}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{R}\|\sum_{v\in V(G)}1_{{\mathcal{N}}_{v}}\|_{1}^{2}\geq\frac{R^{3}}{K^{2}}.

On the other hand

\|\sum_{v\in V(G)}1_{{\mathcal{N}}_{v}}\|_{2}^{2}=2E(G)+2\sum_{v_{1}\not=v_{2}}|{\mathcal{N}}(v_{1},v_{2})|<\frac{R^{3}}{2K^{2}}+2\sum_{v_{1}\not=v_{2}}|{\mathcal{N}}(v_{1},v_{2})|.

We conclude that

\sum_{v_{1}\not=v_{2}}|{\mathcal{N}}(v_{1},v_{2})|>\frac{R^{3}}{2K^{2}}.

(18)

Let

{\mathcal{V}}=\{(v_{1},v_{2}):\;v_{1}\not=v_{2},\;|{\mathcal{N}}(v_{1},v_{2})|<\frac{R}{4K^{2}}\}.

Since

\sum_{(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathcal{V}}}|{\mathcal{N}}(v_{1},v_{2})|<\frac{R}{4K^{2}}R^{2},

(19)

the conclusion follows by combining (
18
) and (
19

∎

For dyadic

{\mathcal{N}}_{D,P,F}(v_{1},v_{2})=\{v_{3}\in{\mathcal{N}}(v_{1},v_{2}):\;

Note the partition

Call

.

\displaystyle\sum_{D,P,F}\sum_{(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{D,P,F}}{\mathcal{N}}_{D,P,F}(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle{\;\gtrapprox}\;\sum_{D,P,F}\sum_{(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{D,P,F}}|{\mathcal{N}}(v_{1},v_{2})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathcal{P}}}|{\mathcal{N}}(v_{1},v_{2})|{\;\gtrapprox}\;R^{3}/K^{2}.

Write

Since

{\;\lessapprox}\;1

(repeat the above argument for

Lemma 3.13

Each

{\;\lessapprox}\;\min\{P,F+\frac{QP}{DF2^{l}}\}+\frac{Q^{3}}{2^{l}ND^{2}P}

Example 3.14

Next, we analyze an interesting example for the case

Example 3.15

Let

. We prove that

,

3.4.
Brief discussion of different ranges

This is a brief outline of the remaining subsections.
Recall that we may assume (
20
) for some fixed
D,P,F
, which we repeat here for easy reference

\sum_{(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{D,P,F}}{\mathcal{N}}_{D,P,F}(v_{1},v_{2}){\;\gtrapprox}\;R^{3}/K^{2}.

(31)

There are three cases. We exploit (
31

{\;\gtrapprox}\;R/K^{2}
neighbors.

On the other hand, Cases 2 and 3 use another consequence of (
31

{\;\gtrapprox}\;R/K

1. The first range we discuss corresponds to

and
P{\;\lessapprox}\;2^{l}
. Heuristically, this is the case of small
D
, in particular it covers the case
D\lesssim\sqrt{Q}
. In this range Proposition
3.22
gives the ideal estimate
{\mathcal{L}}(x,t){\;\lessapprox}\;Q
, and working with just one popular pair solves the problem.

This argument requires
M{\;\gtrapprox}\;N^{1/10}
.
It is this case only that enforces this severe restriction on
M
with exponent
1/10
. Moreover, this restriction is only needed when
D{\;\lessapprox}\;1
. See Remark
3.25
.

2. We next cover the range of large
P
, namely
P\ggg 2^{l}
. This case is the most delicate and the most interesting one. We identify a localized piece of the graph we call
fork
and find arithmetic structure in it. This is the only case that uses the hypothesis (

Remark 3.23

.

Assume

∎

3.6.
The argument for the case

We will prove the estimate
R{\;\lessapprox}\;N/M^{4}
assuming

, in the slightly more relaxed setting

. We have in particular

The arguments in this section cover the situation when

This scenario is implied by e.g. the choice of parameters

and

. If a graph with such parameters exists (we do not have an example), the earlier counting argument falls apart as

. An inspection of the proofs of Lemmas

and

reveals that this hypothetical graph exhibits some rigidity. Most notably, (
26

\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}}=\frac{a_{3}}{q_{3}}+\frac{a_{4}}{q_{4}}.

We do not see a way to exploit this rigidity in order to simplify the argument in this subsection.

We start with an easy, very instructive version of the argument that proves the bound

when

We recall our working hypothesis, with

\sum_{(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{D,P,F}}{\mathcal{N}}_{D,P,F}(v_{1},v_{2}){\;\gtrapprox}\;R^{3}/K^{2}.

\sum_{(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{D,P,F}}{\mathcal{N}}_{D,P,F}(v_{1},v_{2})=\sum_{(v_{1},v_{3})\in E(G)}\sum_{v_{2}:\;(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{D,P,F}\atop{v_{3}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{D,P,F}(v_{1},v_{2})}}1\leq\sum_{(v_{1},v_{3})\in E(G)}\sum_{v_{2}:\atop{\;v_{3}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{D,P,F}(v_{1},v_{2})}}1.

Combining it with (

|S=\{v_{2}:\;v_{3}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{D,P,F}(v_{1},v_{2})\}|{\;\gtrapprox}\;R/K.

(and its later refinements) a

(the handle of the fork), and there are edges (the tines) connecting

to each element

of

Let

be the unique numbers such that
(v_{1}-v_{3})\blacksquare(a_{1},b_{1},q_{1})
. For each

in the set

from above we must have, for some

, but with the same

(since

Lemma 3.26
.

Let

. Assume

Here

are integers.
We find that

If

. So we may assume

. Recalling that

. We may take

∎

We next recall (see (

Repeating the argument in Section

4.

and beyond

A (much) simpler version of the earlier argument recovers the sharp

Assuming again that for some

It suffices to prove this for

otherwise the inequality is trivial. There are only two cases.

Case 1. Assume

. We use the bound

and the fact that there is at least one popular pair.
We analyze the six terms. First,

\frac{Q^{3}}{2^{l}ND^{2}P}(\frac{Q}{2^{l}}+D)\frac{N}{QM^{4}}\lesssim\frac{Q^{3}}{2^{2l}M^{4}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{2^{l}M^{4}}\lesssim\frac{N^{3}}{M^{24}}+\frac{N^{2}}{M^{16}}\lesssim\frac{N^{3/2}}{M^{6}},

Then, using that

precisely when

.
Then, since

we have

\frac{QP}{DF2^{l}}\frac{Q}{2^{l}}\frac{N}{QM^{4}}\lesssim\frac{Q}{2^{2l}}\frac{N}{M^{4}}\lesssim(\frac{N}{M^{4}})^{3/2}.

Finally, using the crude bound
P\lesssim Q

\frac{QP}{DF2^{l}}D\frac{N}{QM^{4}}\lesssim\frac{Q}{2^{l}}\frac{N}{M^{4}}\lesssim\frac{N^{2}}{M^{12}}\lesssim(\frac{N}{M^{4}})^{3/2},

.

Case 2. If
FD\gtrsim Q
, recall that we got the bound

in (
42

Abstract
Due to the divergence-instability, low-order conforming finite element methods (FEMs) for nearly incompressible elasticity equations suffer from the so-called locking phenomenon as the Lamé parameter λ → ∞ \lambda\to\infty and consequently the material becomes more and more incompressible. For the piecewise linear case, the error in the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the standard Galerkin conforming FEM is bounded by C λ ​ h 2 C_{\lambda}h^{2} . However, C λ → ∞ C_{\lambda}\to\infty as λ → ∞ \lambda\to\infty , resulting in poor accuracy for practical values of h h if λ \lambda is sufficiently large. In this short paper, we show that for 2D problems the locking phenomenon can be controlled by replacing λ \lambda with λ α \lambda^{\alpha} in the stiffness matrix, for a certain choice of α = α ∗ ​ ( h , λ ) \alpha=\alpha_{*}(h,\lambda) in the range 0 < α ≤ 1 0<\alpha\leq 1 . We prove that for this optimal choice of α \alpha , the error in the L 2 L^{2} -norm is bounded by C ​ h Ch where C C does not depend on λ \lambda . Numerical experiments confirm the expected convergence behaviour and show that, for practical meshes, our locking-free method is more accurate than the standard method if the material is nearly incompressible. Our analysis also shows that the error in the H 1 H^{1} -norm is bounded by C ​ h 1 / 2 Ch^{1/2} , but our numerical experiments suggest that this bound is not sharp.

Introduction

Consider the following linear elasticity equation

-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}({\bf u}({\boldsymbol{x}}))={\bf f}({\boldsymbol{x}})\quad\text{for ${\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}$,}

(1.1)

subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
{\bf u}={\bf 0}
on the polygonal
boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
. The Cauchy stress
tensor
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2\times 2}
is defined as

\boldsymbol{\sigma}({\bf u})=\lambda\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\,{\bf I}+2\mu{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})\quad\text{on $\Omega$,}

with
{\bf u}
being the displacement vector field and the symmetric strain
tensor
{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u}):=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla{\bf u}+(\nabla{\bf u})^{T})
.
Here,
{\bf f}
is the body force per unit volume, and
{\bf I}
is the identity tensor. The gradient (
\nabla
) and the divergence (
\nabla\cdot
) are understood to be with respect to the physical variable
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega
. The constant Lamé parameters
\mu
and
\lambda
are strictly positive, and we are
interested in the case when the Poisson ratio
\nu
of the elastic material approaches
1/2
. Thus,

\lambda
is large and we are dealing with a nearly incompressible material. It is known in this case that
locking is a major issue when conforming FEMs are employed to approximate the solution of (
1.1
).
It was shown that locking is absent for polynomials of degree
\geq 4
on a triangular
mesh
[
28
]
. However, on quadrilateral meshes, it was shown that locking cannot be
avoided for any polynomial of degree
\geq 1

[
4
]
. In a related result,
Vogelius
[
31
]
proved that the
p
-version of the conforming FEM on smooth domains is
unaffected by locking.

The locking phenomenon
[
1
]
in conforming FEMs has motivated researchers to explore
and develop various locking-free numerical methods (
h
-,
p
-, and
hp
-versions, low-order, and
high-order) for solving the nearly incompressible elasticity equation (
1.1
). These methods include nonconforming FEMs
[
3
,
7
,
9
,
16
,
23
,
26
,
34
]
, conforming-nonconforming mixed FEMs
[
5
,
17
,
19
,
35
]
, the weak Galerkin FEM
[
10
,
20
,
22
,
24
,
32
]
, the discontinuous
Petrov–Galerkin method
[
8
]
, the discontinuous Galerkin method
(with hybridization)
[
11
,
14
,
18
,
29
,
33
]
, and the virtual element method
[
15
,
21
,
30
,
36
,
37
]
. It is worth mentioning that most of these methods are more complicated both
computationally and in their analysis than the conforming FEM. Furthermore, these methods become more complicated or not applicable for the case of an inhomogeneous material, where the Lamé parameters are functions of
{\boldsymbol{x}}
.

The aim of this work is to demonstrate that the locking phenomenon in the popular piecewise linear conforming FEM can be avoided with a slight modification of the scheme. More precisely, we replace the Lamé parameter
\lambda
with
\lambda^{\alpha}
in the standard conforming FEM, where
0<\alpha\leq 1
. The mechanism for choosing the optimal value
\alpha=\alpha_{*}(h,\lambda)
depends on the finite element mesh size
h
, the Lamé parameter
\lambda
and on diameter
d_{\Omega}
of
\Omega
. Although most of our analysis is independent of the dimension, we need to assume that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
so that we can apply certain
H^{2}
-regularity results
of Brenner and Sung
[
7
]
.

In the next section, we succinctly discuss the weak formulation of Problem (
1.1
) and the well-posedness of the modified conforming FEM. In Section
3
, we demonstrate that the
scheme is
O(h)
accurate, uniformly in
\lambda
. Additionally, we include a remark on the applicability of these results in the case of mixed Dirichlet-traction boundary conditions. Furthermore, we include another remark on how to extend the proposed finite element scheme and the convergence analysis for the case of an inhomogeneous body, where the Lamé parameters
\lambda
and
\mu
depend on the space variable
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega
.
To support our theoretical findings, we present some numerical results in Section
4
for two different examples, including Cook’s benchmark problem.

2
Modified conforming FEM

In this section, we briefly discuss the existence and uniqueness of the weak solution of (
1.1
), and then define our piecewise linear modified conforming Galerkin FEM. We investigate the error estimates in a separate section.

In the weak formulation of equation (
1.1
), we seek
{\bf u}\in{\bf V}
satisfying

\mathcal{B}({\bf u},{\bf v})=\ell({\bf v}),\quad\text{for all~~${\bf v}\in{\bf V}$,}

(2.1)

where the linear functional
\ell
and the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
are defined by

\ell({\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}{\bf f}\cdot{\bf v}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{B}({\bf u},{\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}[2\mu\,{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u}):{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf v})+\lambda(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})(\nabla\cdot{\bf v})]\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}.

The colon operator is the inner product between tensors. Let
\|\cdot\|
denote the norm
in
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)=[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
, and let
\|\cdot\|_{\bf V}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\bf H}
denote the
norms in
{\bf V}=[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{2}
and
{\bf H}=[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
, respectively. Here,
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and

H^{2}(\Omega)
are the usual Sobolev spaces of scalar-valued functions on
\Omega
. Finally,
{\bf V}^{*}
is the
dual space of
{\bf V}
with respect to the inner product in
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Since
\mathcal{B}({\bf v},{\bf w})\leq C_{\lambda,\mu}\|\nabla{\bf v}\|\,\|\nabla{\bf w}\|\leq C_{\lambda,\mu}\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}}\,\|{\bf w}\|_{{\bf V}}
for
{\bf v},{\bf w}\in{\bf V}
, the bilinear
form
\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
is bounded over
{\bf V}\times{\bf V}
. The coercivity of
\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\cdot)

on
{\bf V}
follows from Korn’s inequality,

\|{\bf v}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}:=\mathcal{B}({\bf v},{\bf v})\geq 2\mu\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf v})\|^{2}\geq c\mu\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}}^{2},\quad{\bf v}\in{\bf V}.

(2.2)

Owing to these two properties, and since
|\ell({\bf v})|\leq\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}}
, an application of
the Lax–Milgram theorem completes the proof of the next theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For every
f\in{\bf V}^{*}
, the problem (
2.1
) has a unique solution
{\bf u}\in{\bf V}
.

For the convergence analysis, we require the following regularity properties
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For every
f\in{\bf V}^{*}
, the weak solution
{\bf u}
of (
2.1
) satisfies

\|{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}+\lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\leq C\,\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}.

(2.3)

Furthermore, if the domain
\Omega
is convex and if
{\bf f}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
, then

{\bf u}\in{\bf V}\cap{\bf H}
and
\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}\leq C\,\|{\bf f}\|.

In both estimates, the constant
C
depends on
\Omega
and
\mu
, but is independent of
\lambda
.

Remark 2.1
.

Our need for (
2.3
) is the only reason that Theorem
3.1
is restricted to two dimensional problems.

For the finite element scheme, we introduce as usual a family of regular
quasi-uniform triangulations
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of the domain
\Omega
and let

h=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(h_{K})
, where
h_{K}
denotes the diameter of
the element
K
. The space of polynomials with degree at most
1
is denoted by
P_{1}
, and the conforming finite element space is then defined by

{\bf V}_{h}=\{\,{\bf w}_{h}\in{\bf V}:\text{${\bf w}_{h}|_{K}\in[P_{1}]^{2}$ for all $K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}$}\,\}.

In the standard conforming FEM we seek
{\bf u}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}
such that
\mathcal{B}({\bf u}_{h},{\bf v}_{h})=\ell({\bf v}_{h})

for all
{\bf v}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}
. To avoid locking when
\lambda
is large, more precisely,
when
\lambda\gg 1/h
, we modify this scheme as follows: find
{\bf u}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}
such that

\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}_{h},{\bf v}_{h})=\ell({\bf v}_{h}),\quad\text{for all ${\bf v}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}$},

(2.4)

where the bilinear operator
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
is defined in the same way as
\mathcal{B}
in (
2
),
except with
\lambda^{\alpha}
in place of
\lambda
, that is,

\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u},{\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}[2\mu\,{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u}):{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf v})+\lambda^{\alpha}(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})(\nabla\cdot{\bf v})]\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}},

for an appropriately chosen constant
\alpha
that
depends on
h
,
\lambda
and
d_{\Omega}
, and that satisfies
0<\alpha\leq 1
; see Theorem
3.1

below. The existence and uniqueness of the solution
{\bf u}_{h}
of (
2.4
) is guaranteed because the
operator
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
is bounded and positive-definite on
{\bf V}_{h}
.

3
Convergence analysis

For convenience, we introduce the following notations: let

\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha},\quad\psi(\lambda)=(\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha})^{2}\lambda^{-\alpha}\quad{\rm and}\quad\chi(\lambda)=(\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha})^{2}\lambda^{-\alpha-2}.

For the error analysis, we use a projection operator
\Pi_{h}:{\bf V}\to{\bf V}_{h}
with the following error estimate properties:

\|{\bf v}-\Pi_{h}{\bf v}\|+h\|\nabla({\bf v}-\Pi_{h}{\bf v})\|\leq Ch^{2}\|{\bf v}\|_{\bf H}\,.

(3.1)

The regularity results in Theorem
2.2
imply that

\|{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}\leq C\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u})\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{\lambda^{\alpha}}\,\nabla\cdot({\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u})\|^{2}\leq C(1+\phi(\lambda))h^{2}\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}^{2}\leq C\phi(\lambda)h^{2}\|{\bf f}\|^{2}.

(3.2)

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is convex with diameter
d_{\Omega}
, and that

{\bf f}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
{\bf u}
and

{\bf u}_{h}
be the solutions of problems (
1.1
) and (
2.4
), respectively, and choose

\alpha=\alpha_{*}(h,\lambda)\equiv\min\biggl(1,\frac{\log(d_{\Omega}/h)}{\log(\lambda)}\biggr)\quad\text{so that}\quad\lambda^{\alpha}=\begin{cases}d_{\Omega}/h&\text{for $\lambda>d_{\Omega}/h$,}\\
\lambda&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

with
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Then, there is a constant
C
depending on
\Omega
and
\mu
, but independent
of
\lambda
, such that

\|{\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}\leq Ch^{1/2}\|{\bf f}\|.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{L}:{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded linear functional (that is,
|\mathcal{L}({\bf w})|\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\,\|{\bf w}\|
), then

|\mathcal{L}({\bf u}_{h})-\mathcal{L}({\bf u})|\leq C\,h\|{\bf f}\|\|\mathcal{L}\|.

(3.3)

Proof.

From the weak formulation in (
2.1
) and the numerical scheme in (
2.4
), we have the property

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u},{\bf v}_{h})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}_{h},{\bf v}_{h})-\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u},{\bf v}_{h})-\mathcal{B}({\bf u},{\bf v}_{h})+\mathcal{B}({\bf u},{\bf v}_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}({\bf u},{\bf v}_{h})-\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u},{\bf v}_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha})\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\,\nabla\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\quad\text{for all ${\bf v}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}$.}

(3.4)

Using this formula, the fact that
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}(\cdot,\cdot)
is a bounded bilinear operator, and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain,

\displaystyle\|{\bf u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u},{\bf u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u})+\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha})\,\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\,\nabla\cdot({\bf u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|{\bf u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}+\|{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}+C\psi(\lambda)\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\lambda^{\alpha}}\,\nabla\cdot({\bf u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u})\|^{2}\,.

Using
\|\sqrt{\lambda^{\alpha}}\,\nabla\cdot({\bf u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u})\|^{2}\leq\|{\bf u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2},
then cancelling the similar terms, we get

\|{\bf u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}\leq\|{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}+C\psi(\lambda)\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|^{2}.

Hence, using (
3.2
) and the regularity results in Theorem
2.2
, we have

\|{\bf u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}\leq C\phi(\lambda)h^{2}\|{\bf f}\|^{2}+C\chi(\lambda)\|{\bf f}\|^{2}.

For
\lambda>d_{\Omega}/h
, we choose
\alpha
to balance the quantities
\phi(\lambda)h^{2}
and

\chi(\lambda)
. More precisely, we require that

\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha}=\lambda^{1+\alpha}h/d_{\Omega}
. Dividing through by
\lambda^{1+\alpha}
yields
\lambda^{-\alpha}=1/\lambda+h/d_{\Omega}
, and then
taking the natural logarithm of both sides, we obtain

\alpha=-\log(h/d_{\Omega}+1/\lambda)/\log(\lambda)\approx\log(d_{\Omega}/h)/\log(\lambda)=\alpha_{*}(h,\lambda)
.
If this condition is satisfied, then

\|{\bf u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}\leq Ch\|{\bf f}\|^{2}.

(3.5)

Using this together with (
3.2
), shows

\|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}\leq Ch\|{\bf f}\|^{2}.

(3.6)

Now, if
\lambda\leq d_{\Omega}/h
, we simply repeat the above steps with
\alpha=1
(that is, standard
conforming FEM) and arrive at the same bound.

To show the second estimate, we replace
\ell
with
\mathcal{L}
in (
2.1
), and then, by
Theorem
2.1
, there exists a unique
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\in{\bf V}
satisfying

\mathcal{B}({\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf v})=\mathcal{L}({\bf v})\quad\text{for all ${\bf v}\in{\bf V}$.}

(3.7)

Furthermore, using Theorem
2.2
(with
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}
in place of
{\bf u}
), we deduce that

\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\|_{{\bf V}}+\lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\|\leq C\|\mathcal{L}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\|_{\bf H}\leq C\|\mathcal{L}\|.

(3.8)

The modified conforming finite element approximation to
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}
is the function
{\bf\Theta}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}

such that

\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf\Theta}_{h},{\bf v}_{h})=\mathcal{L}({\bf v}_{h})\quad\text{for all ${\bf v}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}$.}

(3.9)

By repeating the arguments that led to (
3.6
) and using (
3.8
), we obtain

\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}-{\bf\Theta}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}\leq Ch\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\|_{\bf H}\|\mathcal{L}\|\leq Ch\|\mathcal{L}\|^{2}.

(3.10)

Notice that

\mathcal{B}({\bf w},{\bf v})-\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf w},{\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha})(\nabla\cdot{\bf w})(\nabla\cdot{\bf v})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\quad\text{for all ${\bf w}$, ${\bf v}\in{\bf V}$,}

so by (
3.7
) and (
3.9
) it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf v}_{h})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}({\bf v}_{h})-\mathcal{B}({\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf v}_{h})+\mathcal{B}({\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf v}_{h})-\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf v}_{h})

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha})(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})(\nabla\cdot{\bf v}_{h})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\quad\text{for all ${\bf v}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}$.}

By (
2.4
) and (
3.9
), we have

\mathcal{L}({\bf u}_{h})=\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf\Theta}_{h},{\bf u}_{h})=\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}_{h},{\bf\Theta}_{h})=\ell({\bf\Theta}_{h})=\mathcal{B}({\bf u},{\bf\Theta}_{h})
, whereas (
3.7
) and (
2.1
) imply that

\mathcal{L}({\bf u})=\mathcal{B}({\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf u})=\mathcal{B}({\bf u},{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})
, so

\mathcal{L}({\bf u}_{h})-\mathcal{L}({\bf u})=\mathcal{B}({\bf u},{\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})=\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u},{\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})+\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha})(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})(\nabla\cdot({\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}.

Since
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}_{h},{\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})=\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf\Theta}_{h},{\bf u}_{h})-\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf u}_{h})=\mathcal{L}({\bf u}_{h})-\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf u}_{h})=\mathcal{B}({\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf u}_{h})-\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf u}_{h})
,

\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u},{\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})=\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h},{\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})+\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha})(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{h})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}

and hence

\mathcal{L}({\bf u}_{h})-\mathcal{L}({\bf u})=\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h},{\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})+\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha})(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\nabla\cdot({\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\\
+\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha})\bigl[(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})\nabla\cdot({\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u})+(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}.

(3.12)

By Theorem
2.2
and (
3.8
),
\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\leq C\lambda^{-1}\|{\bf f}\|
and

\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\|\leq C\lambda^{-1}\|\mathcal{L}\|
. Therefore, from the achieved estimates
in (
3.6
) and (
3.10
), and using

\|\nabla\cdot{\bf v}\|\leq C\lambda^{-\alpha/2}\|{\bf v}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}({\bf u})-\mathcal{L}({\bf u}_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}-\Theta_{h}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}+C\lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\|\nabla\cdot({\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})\|

\displaystyle\qquad{}+C\lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\|\|\nabla\cdot({\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u})\|+C\lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\|\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigr(h+\lambda^{-\alpha/2}h^{1/2}+\lambda^{-1}\bigr)\|\mathcal{L}\|\|{\bf f}\|.

If
\lambda>d_{\Omega}/h
, then
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
\lambda^{\alpha}=d_{\Omega}/h
, so

\lambda^{-\alpha/2}\leq Ch^{1/2}
and
\lambda^{-1}\leq Ch^{1/\alpha}\leq Ch
, implying the
estimate (
3.3
). If
\lambda\leq d_{\Omega}/h
, then
\alpha=1
so

\lambda-\lambda^{\alpha}=0
and (
3.3
) again holds.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

There is a constant
C
, independent of
\lambda
, such that

\|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}\|\leq Ch\|{\bf f}\|\quad\text{and}\quad\|{\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}\leq Ch^{1/2}\|{\bf f}\|.

Proof.

By choosing

\mathcal{L}({\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}({\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u})\cdot{\bf v}\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}
in (
3.3
), we have

\|{\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u}\|^{2}=\mathcal{L}({\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u})=\mathcal{L}({\bf u}_{h})-\mathcal{L}({\bf u})\leq Ch\|{\bf f}\|\|{\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u}\|,

implying that
\|{\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u}\|\leq Ch\|{\bf f}\|
. By (
2.2
) and (
3.6
),

\|{\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}^{2}\leq C\|\varepsilon({\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u})\|^{2}\leq C\|{\bf u}_{h}-{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}^{2}\leq Ch\|{\bf f}\|^{2},

implying the second estimate.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

To discuss mixed boundary conditions, let
\boldsymbol{n}
denote the outward
unit normal vector with respect to
\Omega
. Recall that
\Omega
is a convex polygonal domain, and let the
closed line segment
\Gamma_{i}
denote the
i
th edge of the boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
, ordered
counterclockwise. Thus,
\Gamma=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma_{i}
for some finite
m
. We assume that

u={\bf 0}
on
\Gamma_{D}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m_{0}}\Gamma_{i}
and that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}={\bf g}

on
\Gamma_{N}=\bigcup_{m_{0}+1}^{m}\Gamma_{i}
. In other words, homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
apply on
\Gamma_{D}
, whereas traction boundary conditions apply on
\Gamma_{N}
. The measure of
\Gamma_{D}

must be positive, and the Neumann data
{\bf g}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Gamma_{2})
.

For
\ell({\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}({\bf f}\cdot{\bf v})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}({\bf g}\cdot{\bf v})\,\mathrm{d}s
, the
existence and uniqueness theorem (Theorem
2.1
) remains valid with

|\ell({\bf v})|\leq C(\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}+\|{\bf g}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\Gamma_{2})})\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}}
and

{\bf V}=\{\,{\bf w}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{2}:\text{${\bf w}=0$ on $\Gamma_{D}$}\,\}
. The conforming finite element scheme (
2.4
) is also well-defined if we put

{\bf V}_{h}=\{\,{\bf w}_{h}\in{\bf V}:\text{${\bf w}_{h}|_{K}\in[P_{1}]^{2}$ for all $K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}$}\,\}.

The convergence results in Theorem
3.1
hold true provided the regularity results
in Theorem
2.2
remain valid. In these two theorems, we anticipate that

\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}
and
\|{\bf f}\|
should be replaced with

\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}+\|{\bf g}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}
and

\|{\bf f}\|+\|{\bf g}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})]^{2}}
, respectively. For more details, see for example Arnold and
Falk
[

Remark 3.2
.

In this remark, we discuss the case of variable Lamé parameters
\lambda
and
\mu
. More precisely, when
\mu=\mu({\boldsymbol{x}})
and
\lambda=\Lambda\widehat{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{x}})
, where the constant factor
\Lambda
is expected to be very large (and approaches infinity for materials which are increasingly incompressible). We assume that
\mu_{\min}\leq\mu\leq\mu_{\max}
and
\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}\leq\widehat{\lambda}\leq\widehat{\lambda}_{\max}
on
\Omega
for some positive constants
\mu_{\min}
,
\mu_{\max}
,
\widehat{\lambda}_{\min},
and
\widehat{\lambda}_{\max}.
The regularity results in Theorem
2.1
remains valid in this case with
\Lambda
in place of
\lambda
, where the constant
C
depends on
\Omega
,
\mu
and
\widehat{\lambda}
, but is independent of
\Lambda
; see Dick et al.
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
.

We define the modified conforming FEM as in (
2.4
) with

\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}({\bf u},{\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}[2\mu\,{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u}):{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf v})+\Lambda^{\alpha}\widehat{\lambda}(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})(\nabla\cdot{\bf v})]\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}.

For the convergence results, we replace
\lambda
with
\Lambda
in the statements of Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
. In the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we replace
\lambda
with
\Lambda
in: (1) the functions
\phi,\psi,
and
\chi,
(2) the text before (
3.5
) and after (
3.6
), and (3) the material after (
3.12
).

4
Numerical experiments

In this section, we illustrate the theoretical convergence results. Based on
Corollary
3.2
, we expect to observe
O(h)
and
O(h^{1/2})
convergence rates
in the
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
-norm for
{\bf u}
and
\nabla{\bf u}
,
respectively, uniformly in
\lambda
,
provided that all the imposed assumptions are satisfied. The Julia code used in these experiments is available online
[
25
]
.

We chose
\Omega=(0,\pi)^{2}
and chose the body force so that the exact solution is

{\bf u}(\boldsymbol{x})=\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\\

1\end{bmatrix},\quad{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2}).

{\bf u}=\mathbf{0}
on
\partial\Omega
. We used a family of unstructured triangulations obtained by
uniform refinement of an initial coarse mesh. Table
1
compares the errors and

{\bf L^{2}}(\Omega)
-norm of the standard conforming, piecewise-linear Galerkin FEM (

) with our conforming scheme (

from Theorem
3.1
). The number of unknowns in the

. As

increases from

to
10{,}000

to

the material becomes more nearly incompressible and, as expected, the standard

convergence uniformly
in

.

Table

compares the errors in

Example 2.

(Cook’s membrane problem
[
12
]
) This is a benchmark problem for linear elasticity which combines bending and shearing. As illustrated in Figure
1
,

\Omega
is the convex domain formed by connecting the vertices
(0,0)
,
(48,44)
,
(48,60),
and
(0,44)
.
The left-hand side of
\Omega
is clamped (that is,
{\bf u}={\bf 0}
), and a constant shear load in the vertical
direction is applied on the right-hand side, indicated by the red arrow:
\boldsymbol{\sigma}{\bf n}=(0,g)
where
g
is
a positive constant. The remaining part of the boundary is traction-free, that is,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}{\bf n}={\bf 0}
,
and the body force
{\bf f}={\bf 0}
. The elastic material has Young’s modulus
E=1.12499998125
MPa and
Poisson ratio
\nu=0.499999975
, so the Lamé parameters are

\lambda=\frac{E\nu}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)}=7.5\times 10^{6}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}=0.375.

The shear load gives rise to the functional
\ell({\bf v})=\int_{44}^{60}gv_{2}\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}
, where

{\bf v}=(v_{1},v_{2})^{T}
,
{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
g=1/16
. Starting from a common initial mesh with
h=2.57881
, we
solved for
{\bf u}_{h}
using
\alpha=1
and
\alpha=\alpha_{*}(h,\lambda)=0.214
, and then displaced each node by
the respective solution
{\bf u}_{h}
to obtain the deformed meshes shown in Figure
2
. Even with this relatively coarse mesh, the
locking effect is apparent.

We also compared the computed values for the vertical displacement at the midpoint
A=(48,50)
of the right-hand edge with the benchmark value

u_{2}(A)=16.442

[
24
, p. 3491]
. Figure
3
shows that using
\alpha=1
gives a much less accurate result than using
\alpha=\alpha_{*}(h,\lambda)
.

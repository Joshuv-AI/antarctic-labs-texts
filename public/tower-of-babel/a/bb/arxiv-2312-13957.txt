Two invariant subalgebras of rational Cherednik algebras
Gwyn Bellamy, Misha Feigin, Niall Hird

Abstract
. Originally motivated by connections to integrable systems, two natural subalgebras of the rational Cherednik algebra have been considered in the literature. The first is the subalgebra generated by all degree zero elements and the second is the Dunkl angular momentum subalgebra. In this article, we study the ring-theoretic and homological properties of these algebras. Our approach is to realise them as rings of invariants under the action of certain reductive subgroups of SL 2 \mathrm{SL}_{2} . This allows us to describe their centres. Moreover, we show that they are Auslander–Gorenstein and Cohen–Macaulay and, at t = 0 t=0 , give rise to prime PI-algebras whose PI-degree we compute. Since the degree zero subalgebra can be realized as the ring of invariants for the maximal torus T ⊂ SL 2 \mathrm{T}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2} and the action of this torus on the rational Cherednik algebra is Hamiltonian, we also consider its (quantum) Hamiltonian reduction with respect to T \mathrm{T} . At t = 1 t=1 , the quantum Hamiltonian reduction of the spherical subalgebra is a filtered quantization of the quotient of the minimal nilpotent orbit closure 𝒪 ¯ min \overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\min} in 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n ) \mathfrak{gl}(n) by the reflection group W W . At t = 0 t=0 , we get a graded Poisson deformation of the symplectic singularity 𝒪 ¯ min / W \overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\min}/W .



1.
Introduction

One key motivation for the introduction of rational Cherednik algebras
H_{t,c}
by Etingof and Ginzburg
[
21
]
was their connection with the generalized Calogero–Moser integrable systems. For instance, the algebras can be used to give a uniform proof of the complete integrability of these systems
[
29
]
.
In the case of a real reflection group
W
and parameter
t=1
, the second author together with Hakobyan introduced in
[
22
]
the
Dunkl angular momentum algebra

H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
as the subalgebra of a rational Cherednik algebra generated by the (Dunkl deformed) angular momentum operators
x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
and the reflection group
W
. Part of the motivation for introducing this algebra is the fact that it contains in its centre essentially the angular generalized Calogero–Moser Hamiltonian. This makes the angular part of generalized Calogero–Moser systems amenable to study via representation theory. At the same time, the closely related
degree zero
subalgebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
was also introduced in
[
22
]
. This algebra is generated by the elements
x_{i}y_{j}
and the reflection group, and it is related, in turn, to Calogero–Moser systems in the harmonic confinement.

Another interesting context where the algebras
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
and
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
arise is deformed Howe duality. The algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
plays a role in the Dunkl deformed Howe dual pair
(\mathfrak{so}(n),\mathfrak{sl}(2))

[
16
]
while the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
relates to the Dunkl deformation of
(\mathfrak{gl}(n),\mathfrak{gl}(1))
duality
[
24
]
.

In this article, we study the ring-theoretic and homological properties of these two subalgebras. Our primary focus is on understanding their properties in the case
t=0
. Since the algebras are defined in terms of a set of generators, it can be difficult to prove directly any of their abstract (e.g. homological) properties. Our key observation is that both of these algebras can be realised as rings of invariants under certain groups of automorphisms of the rational Cherednik algebra
H_{t,c}
.

Let
\mathrm{T}
be the maximal torus of diagonal matrices in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. Then
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}=H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{T}}
. In the case of a real reflection group
W
the entire group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
acts by automorphisms on the rational Cherednik algebra
H_{t,c}

[
21
]
. In this case we show in Theorem
4.9
that
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}
.

Realizing both algebras as rings of invariants under a connected reductive group
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}
allows us to give a remarkably uniform treatment of these algebras; this we do in Sections
6
and
7
.
For
\Gamma=SL_{2}
we assume that
W
is a real reflection group.

The centre of the algebras

The rational Cherednik algebra
H_{t,c}
has a big centre at
t=0

[
21
]
. Our original motivation was to describe the centre of the algebras
H_{0,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
and
H_{0,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
. Let
P=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}]
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is the reflection representation of
W
and
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is its dual. Recall that
H_{0,0}
is the skew group ring
P\rtimes W
. As above,
\Gamma
can be either
\mathrm{T}
or
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. Let
Z(A)
be the centre of an algebra
A
, and let
\gr A
be the associated graded algebra of a filtred algebra
A
.

Theorem
.

(
7.6
)
For any
c
,
\gr Z(H_{0,c}^{\Gamma})=Z(P^{\Gamma}\rtimes W)
.

Let us assume that the group
W
is irreducible. Then the centre
Z(W)
is a cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathbf{a}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{\ell-1}
be the complete set of primitive idempotents in
\mathbb{C}Z(W)
.

Theorem
7.6
, together with the simple fact that if
A\subset B
and
\gr A=\gr B
then
A=B
, leads to the following corollary.

Corollary
.

(
7.7
)
There is equality of commutative rings

Z(H_{0,c}^{\Gamma})=Z(H_{0,c})^{\Gamma}\otimes\mathbb{C}Z(W),

where
\mathbb{C}Z(W)
is the group algebra of the centre
Z(W)
of the group
W
.
In particular, as a
Z(H_{0,c})^{\Gamma}
-module,

Z(H_{0,c}^{\Gamma})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\ell-1}Z(H_{0,c})^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}_{i}.

If we set
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}_{0}
to be the symmetrising idempotent then it follows that the centre of
\mathbf{a}H_{0,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}
equals
\mathbf{a}Z(H_{0,c}^{\Gamma})\cong Z(H_{0,c})^{\Gamma}
. In fact, each summand
Z(H_{0,c})^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}_{i}
of
Z(H_{0,c}^{\Gamma})
is isomorphic to
Z(H_{0,c})^{\Gamma}
. Being a ring of invariants, the latter is very well-behaved (despite being singular), as illustrated by the following result.

Theorem
.

(
7.5
)
The affine scheme
Y_{c}:=\Spec Z(H_{0,c})^{\Gamma}
is a normal irreducible variety. It is Gorenstein with rational singularities.

Ring-theoretic and homological properties

Since the idempotents
\mathbf{a}_{i}
are central in
H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}
, there is a decomposition of algebras
H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}_{i}
. If
\overline{W}
is the quotient
W/Z(W)
then associated to each idempotent

is a twisted group algebra
\mathbb{C}_{f_{i}}\overline{W}
for a corresponding 2-cocycle
f_{i}
, and we show that the associated graded of
\mathbf{a}_{i}H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}_{i}
equals
P^{\Gamma}\rtimes\mathbb{C}_{f_{i}}\overline{W}
. This implies that each of the rings
\mathbf{a}_{i}H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}_{i}
is prime. Our main result on the general properties of these algebras is summarized below.

Theorem
.

(
6.13
)
The algebras
H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}
and
\mathbf{a}_{i}H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}_{i}
are Auslander–Gorenstein and (GK) Cohen–Macaulay. These algebras have Gelfand–Kirillov dimension
2\dim\mathfrak{h}-\dim\Gamma
.

Our main tool is to compute the associated graded of these algebras, and their centres and lift ring-theoretic and homological properties from the associated graded. Our approach follows closely that of Brown and Changtong
[
10
]
.

When
t=0
, Corollary
7.7
implies that both
H_{0,c}^{\Gamma}
and
\mathbf{a}H_{0,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}
are finite modules over their respective centres. In particular, they are Polynomial Identity (PI) rings. We study the simple modules over the prime PI ring
\mathbf{a}H_{0,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}
. Our main result is summarized as follows.

Theorem
.

(
7.18
)
For all parameters
c
,

(i)

H_{0,c}^{\Gamma}
and
\mathbf{a}H_{0,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}
are PI algebras and the PI degree of the prime PI-algebra
\mathbf{a}H_{0,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}
equals
|\overline{W}|
.

(ii)

Let
L
be a simple
\mathbf{a}H_{0,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}
-module whose support is contained in the regular locus of
Y_{c}
. Then
L|_{\overline{W}}\cong\mathbb{C}\overline{W}
.

(iii)

The regular locus of
Y_{c}
is contained in the Azumaya locus of
\mathbf{a}H_{0,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}
.

We do not know if the two loci in Theorem
7.18
(iii) are actually equal.

We also examine the centre of the degree zero subalgebra for
t\neq 0
. Since the centre of the rational Cherednik algebra is trivial in this case, one would expect that the centre of
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
is also small when
t\neq 0
. This is indeed the case (cf.
[
22
]
, where one central generator was identified in the case of real reflection groups).

Proposition
.

(
6.21
)
For
t\neq 0
, the centre of
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
equals
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{eu}_{c}]\otimes\mathbb{C}Z(W)
,
where
\mathrm{eu}_{c}\in H_{t,c}
is the Euler element.

(Quantum) Hamiltonian reduction

The action of
\mathrm{T}
on the generalized Calogero–Moser space
\Spec Z(H_{0,c})
is Hamiltonian and we have seen that the centre of
H_{0,c}^{\mathrm{T}}
is essentially given by the ring of invariants
Z(H_{0,c})^{\mathrm{T}}
. In applications to symplectic singularities, it is natural to consider instead the Hamiltonian reduction of
\Spec Z(H_{0,c})
with respect to
\mathrm{T}
. This is done in Section
8
. For
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}=(\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{T})^{*}
, we define the algebra of quantum Hamiltonian reduction to be the quotient

A_{t,c,\zeta}=A_{t,c,\zeta}(W)=\mathbf{a}H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}\mathbf{a}/\langle\mathbf{a}\mathrm{eu}_{c}-\zeta\rangle,

and the Hamiltonian reduction of
\Spec Z(H_{0,c})
is

\mu^{-1}(\zeta)/\!\!/\!\,\mathrm{T}=\Spec\left(Z(H_{0,c})^{\mathrm{T}}/\langle\mathrm{eu}_{c}-\zeta\rangle\right),

where
\mu
is the moment map. Let
e=\frac{1}{|W|}\sum_{w\in W}w
. As a consequence of the fact that the double centralizer property holds for the
(A_{t,c,\zeta},eA_{t,c,\zeta}e)
-bimodule
A_{t,c,\zeta}e
, (see Theorem
8.11
), we show that:

Corollary
.

(
8.14
)
The centre of
A_{0,c,\zeta}
is isomorphic to
Z(H_{0,c})^{\mathrm{T}}/\langle\mathrm{eu}_{c}-\zeta\rangle
.

Rational Cherednik algebras provide a means to study Poisson deformations of the symplectic quotient singularity
(\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}^{*})/W
because the centre of
H_{0,0}
can be identified with the ring of functions on this quotient. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{min}}\subset\mathfrak{gl}(n)
be the minimal nilpotent orbit with respect to the adjoint action of
\GL(n)
. Corollary
8.14
implies that the centre of
A_{0,0,0}
can be identified with the ring of functions on the symplectic singularity
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{min}}/W
and hence
A_{t,c,\zeta}
can be used to study Poisson deformations of this singularity. Our first result in this direction is the following.

Theorem
.

(
8.9
)
For
t\neq 0
, the algebra
eA_{t,c,\zeta}e
is a filtered quantization of
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{min}}/W
.

The algebra
A_{t,c,\zeta}
can also be identified with a summand of the global sections of a sheaf of Cherednik algebras on the projective space
\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h})
, as studied in
[
4
]
. Since the latter sheaf of algebras has finite global dimension and localization holds for Weil generic parameters, we deduce that:

Theorem
.

(
8.10
)
For Weil generic
(t,c,\zeta)
,
A_{t,c,\zeta}
has finite global dimension.

At
t=0
, we can consider the commutative algebras
Z(A_{0,c,\zeta})
as forming a flat family. More precisely, let
\mathfrak{a}
be the parameter space of all possible pairs
(c,\zeta)
. Then there exists a
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}]
-algebra
A_{0}
such that

A_{0,c,\zeta}=A_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}]}\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}]
acts on
\mathbb{C}
by evaluation. More importantly, one has the flatness of the centre, that is

Z(A_{0},c,\zeta)=Z(A_{0})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}]}\mathbb{C}
.

As one would hope, this gives rise to Poisson deformations of the symplectic singularity
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{min}/W
.

Theorem
.

(
8.17
)

\Spec Z(A_{0})\to\mathfrak{c}
is a graded Poisson deformation of the symplectic singularity

This result should provide a representation-theoretic way to enumerate the projective
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalizations of the singularity

2.
Filtered and graded algebras

In this section, we recall basic facts about filtered and graded algebras that will be required later. The centre of an algebra
A
is denoted
Z(A)
.

2.1.
Graded algebras

For a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
M
,
M_{i}
will denote the degree
i
part. Let
B
be a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. A (
\mathbb{Z}
-)grading on
B
is a direct sum decomposition
B=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
such that
B_{i}\cdot B_{j}\subset B_{i+j}
. The fact that
B
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded can be interpreted as an action of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
on
B
by algebra automorphisms such that

B_{i}:=\{b\in B\,|\,\lambda\cdot b=\lambda^{i}b,\ \forall\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\}.

Then
B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}=B_{0}
.

2.2.
Filtered algebras

Let
A
be a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. A filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
A
is a nested collection of subspaces
\cdots\subset\mathcal{F}_{i}\subset\mathcal{F}_{i+1}\subset\cdots
such that
\mathcal{F}_{i}\mathcal{F}_{j}\subset\mathcal{F}_{i+j}
. The
associated graded algebra
of a filtered algebra
A
is

\gr A=\gr_{\mathcal{F}}A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\,(\gr_{\mathcal{F}}A)_{i},\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{ with }\hskip 10.00002pt(\gr_{\mathcal{F}}A)_{i}=\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}.

Given
a\in A
we denoted by
\sigma(a)
the corresponding element in
\gr A
,
\sigma(a)
is called symbol of
a
.

The following summarizes basic results on filtered algebras that we need.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra equipped with a filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{-1}=0
and
\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{F}_{i}=A
.

(i)

Let
B\subset A
be a subalgebra filtered by restriction. Then
\gr_{\mathcal{F}}B
is a subalgebra of
\gr_{\mathcal{F}}A
and
\gr_{\mathcal{F}}B=\gr_{\mathcal{F}}A
implies that
B=A
.

(ii)

\gr_{\mathcal{F}}Z(A)\subset Z(\gr_{\mathcal{F}}A)
.

(iii)

Assume that there exists a reductive group
G
acting on
A
such that each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is
G
-stable. Then
G
acts on
\gr_{\mathcal{F}}A
and
\gr_{\mathcal{F}}(A^{G})=(\gr_{\mathcal{F}}A)^{G}
.

Recall that an element
u
of an algebra
U
is
normal
if
uU=Uu
is a two-sided ideal in
U
. Also,
u
is said to be
regular
if
ur=0
or
ru=0
implies
r=0
, where
r\in U
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
U
be a filtered algebra and
u\in U
a normal element such that its symbol
\sigma(u)\in\gr U
is regular. Then
\gr(U/\langle u\rangle)=(\gr U)/\langle\sigma(u)\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
I=\langle u\rangle
and assume
u
has degree
d
. By definition of the quotient filtration, the exact sequence
0\to I\to U\to U/I\to 0
is strictly filtered. This means that the associated graded sequence
0\to\gr I\to\gr U\to\gr(U/I)\to 0
is also exact. Therefore, it suffices to show that
\gr I=\langle\sigma(u)\rangle
. Since
u
is normal, an element of
\gr I
is of the form
\sigma(ur)
for some
r\in U
. If
r\in\mathcal{F}_{i}U
then

0\neq\sigma(u)\sigma(r)=ur\,\textrm{mod}\,\mathcal{F}_{i+d-1}\in\gr_{i+d}U.

This implies that
ur\in\mathcal{F}_{i+d}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{i+d-1}
and hence
\sigma(ur)=\sigma(u)\sigma(r)
. In particular,
\sigma(ur)\in\langle\sigma(u)\rangle
. Hence
\gr I\subset\langle\sigma(u)\rangle
. The opposite inclusion is obvious.
∎

3.
The rational Cherednik algebra

In this section we recall the definition of rational Cherednik algebras associated to complex reflection groups, following
[
21
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
26
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a complex vector space. Recall that a
complex reflection
is an invertible linear map
s\colon\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
of finite order that fixes a hyperplane
\Ker\alpha_{s}
pointwise for some
\alpha_{s}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. That is, the rank of
(s-1)
is one.
Let
\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{h}
be an eigenvector of
s
with the eigenvalue different from 1. Let
\lambda_{s}\in\mathbb{C}
be the non-trivial eigenvalue of
s
on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
:
s(\alpha_{s})=\lambda_{s}\alpha_{s}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
complex reflection group

W
is a finite subgroup of
GL(\mathfrak{h})
generated by complex reflections. The set of complex reflections in
W
is denoted
\mathcal{S}
.

We will also be denoting a complex reflection group as a pair
(W,\mathfrak{h})
. A complex reflection group
(W,\mathfrak{h})
is said to be
irreducible
if
\mathfrak{h}
is an irreducible representation of
W
.

We assume throughout that
(W,\mathfrak{h})
is irreducible. Note that
Z(W)=\mathbb{Z}/{\ell}\mathbb{Z}
because
Z(W)\subset\mathbb{C}^{\times}\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{h}}
, using the fact that
\mathfrak{h}
is irreducible. The order of the centre of an irreducible complex reflection group is the greatest common divisor of the degrees of a set of homogeneous fundamental invariants of
W

[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
. In particular, for the infinite series
G(m,p,n)
of irreducible complex reflection groups,
Z(G(m,p,n))=\mathbb{Z}/(m/p)\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Fix a complex reflection group
(W,\mathfrak{h})
,
t\in\mathbb{C}
and a conjugation invariant function
c\colon\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. The associated
rational Cherednik algebra

H_{t,c}=H_{t,c}(W)
is the quotient of the skew-group ring
T(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*})\rtimes W
by the relations

x\otimes x^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\otimes x,\ y\otimes y^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\otimes y,\ y\otimes x-x\otimes y-\kappa(x,y),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,x,x^{\prime}\,\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},y,y^{\prime}\,\in\mathfrak{h},

where
T(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*})
is the tensor algebra on
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and

\kappa(x,y)=tx(y)-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})}x(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})\alpha_{s}(y)s\in\mathbb{C}W.

The space of parameters for the rational Cherednik algebra is
\mathbb{C}\oplus\Hom_{W}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{C})
. For ease of notation, we write
H_{t,c}(W)
as
H_{t,c}
whenever
W
is clear from the context.

3.1.
The Euler element

Let
n=\dim\mathfrak{h}
, and let us choose a basis
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\mathfrak{h}
, and a dual basis
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.
The Euler element
\mathrm{eu}_{c}\in H_{t,c}(W)
is defined to be

\mathrm{eu}_{c}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{1-\lambda_{s}}s.

It does not depend on a basis, and it satisfies

[\mathrm{eu}_{c},x]=tx,\hskip 10.00002pt[\mathrm{eu}_{c},y]=-ty,\hskip 10.00002pt[\mathrm{eu}_{c},w]=0,

(3.1)

for all
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},y\in\mathfrak{h}
and
w\in W
.
Indeed, in order to check the first equality we consider

[\mathrm{eu}_{c},x]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}[y_{i},x]-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{1-\lambda_{s}}[s,x],

(3.2)

where

[y_{i},x]=tx(y_{i})-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})}\alpha_{s}(y_{i})x(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})s.

(3.3)

Then substitution of (
3.3
) into (
3.2
) gives

[\mathrm{eu}_{c},x]=tx-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})}x(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})\alpha_{s}s-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{1-\lambda_{s}}(s(x)-x)s.

Let us choose
\alpha_{s}
and
\alpha_{s}^{\vee}
so that
s(x)=x-x(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})\alpha_{s}
. Then
\lambda_{s}=1-\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})
and we get that
[\mathrm{eu}_{c},x]=0
. Similarly,

[\mathrm{eu}_{c},y]=-ty+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})}\alpha_{s}(y)s(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})s-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{1-\lambda_{s}}(s(y)-y)s=-ty

since
s(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})=\lambda_{s}^{-1}\alpha_{s}^{\vee}
.
The remaining relation in (
3.1
) can also be checked.

The algebra
H_{t,c}(W)
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded with
\deg y=-1,\deg x=1
and
\deg w=0
for
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},y\in\mathfrak{h}
and
w\in W
. We see from the above equations that this grading is inner when
t=1
, given by the adjoint action of
\mathrm{eu}_{c}
. As explained in Section
2.1
, this can be thought of as equipping with an action of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Since we wish to consider
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
as a maximal torus in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
(at least when
W
is a real reflection group) we write
\mathrm{T}:=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
from now on. Then
H_{t,c}(W)_{0}=H_{t,c}(W)^{\mathrm{T}}
.

3.2.
Automorphisms

We have explained that
\mathrm{T}
acts on
H_{t,c}
by algebra automorphisms. When
W
is a real reflection group, there is an action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
on
H_{t,c}
, realising
\mathrm{T}
as the maximal torus of diagonal matrices. This action was first defined in
[

, Corollary 5.3]
and we recall it here. Since
W
is real, we can fix a
W
-equivariant isomorphism
\mathfrak{h}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathfrak{h}^{*}
given by
v\mapsto v^{*}
. Then
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
acts trivially on
W
and

g\cdot v=av+bv^{*},\hskip 10.00002ptg\cdot v^{*}=cv+dv^{*},\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{for }g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2},\textrm{ and }v\in\mathfrak{h}.

4.
Two subalgebras: the presentations

In this section we introduce two subalgebras of a rational Cherednik algebra
H_{t,c}(W)
, and we find their defining relations. We denote these subalgebras as
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
, where
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(n)
or
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(n)
. In the latter case
W
is assumed to be a real reflection group. The algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
at
t=1
is a flat deformation of the skew product of a quotient of the universal enveloping algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}})
with the group
W
, which is established in
[
22
]
for the case of real reflection groups
W
.

4.1.
The degree zero subalgebra

Let
(W,\mathfrak{h})
be an irreducible complex reflection group. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and let
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in\mathfrak{h}
be the dual basis.

Definition 4.1
.

The
degree zero subalgebra

H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}=H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}(W)
of
H_{t,c}(W)
is the subalgebra generated by all
E_{ij}=x_{i}y_{j}
and the group
W
.

Note that the
E_{ij}
are a basis of
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{*}
in
H_{t,c}
. Thus,
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
can equivalently be described as the subalgebra generated by
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
W
.

The algebra
H_{t,c}
admits a filtration
\mathcal{F}_{i}
with
\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}^{*}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
W\subset\mathcal{F}_{0}
. We give all subalgebras of
H_{t,c}
a filtration by restriction, also denoted
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. In particular,
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
is a filtered algebra. Let
P=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}]
. The terminology of Definition
4.1
is justified by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}=H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{T}}
is the subalgebra of all elements of degree zero.

Proof.

The inclusion
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}\subset H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{T}}
is immediate since
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
is generated by elements of degree zero. For the opposite inclusion, we equip both algebras with the subalgebra filtration coming from
H_{t,c}
. Then Lemma
2.1
(i) implies that it suffices to show equality of associated graded algebras. The filtration
\mathcal{F}_{i}
on
H_{t,c}
is
\mathrm{T}
-stable. Therefore Lemma
2.1
(iii) says that
\gr H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{T}}=(\gr H_{t,c})^{\mathrm{T}}
and it is a consequence of the PBW Theorem for rational Cherednik algebras
[
21
, Theorem 1.3]
that

(\gr H_{t,c})^{\mathrm{T}}=(P\rtimes W)^{\mathrm{T}}=P^{\mathrm{T}}\rtimes W.

On the other hand,
\gr H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
certainly contains the
E_{ij}
and
W
. Therefore, it suffices to note that the
E_{ij}\in P
generate
P^{\mathrm{T}}
and hence
P^{\mathrm{T}}\rtimes W\subset\gr H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

\gr H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}=P^{\mathrm{T}}\rtimes W=(\gr H_{t,c})^{\mathrm{T}}
.

Recall that

S_{jk}:=[y_{j},x_{k}]=t\delta_{jk}-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})}\alpha_{s}(y_{j})x_{k}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})s.

(4.1)

Let us rescale elements
\alpha_{s}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
,
\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{h}
so that a reflection
s\colon{\mathfrak{h}}\to\mathfrak{h}
has the form
s(v)=v-\alpha_{s}(v)\alpha_{s}^{\vee}
for any
v\in{\mathfrak{h}}
.

The next statement generalises a result from
[
22
]
to arbitrary
t
and complex reflection groups.

Theorem 4.4
.

The algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
is isomorphic to the quotient of the skew product of the tensor algebra
T(\mathfrak{h}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{h})\rtimes{W}
by the relations

\displaystyle[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=t\delta_{jk}E_{il}-t\delta_{il}E_{kj}+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})}\bigl(\alpha_{s}(y_{l})x_{i}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})E_{kj}-\alpha_{s}(y_{j})x_{k}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})E_{il}\bigr)s

\displaystyle+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})}\alpha_{s}(y_{j})\alpha_{s}(y_{l})\sum_{r=1}^{n}x_{r}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})\bigl(x_{k}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})E_{ir}-x_{i}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})E_{kr}\bigr)s,

(4.2)

\displaystyle E_{ij}E_{kl}-E_{il}E_{kj}=t\delta_{jk}E_{il}-t\delta_{lk}E_{ij}+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{2c_{s}}{\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})}x_{k}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})\bigl(\alpha_{s}(y_{l})E_{ij}-\alpha_{s}(y_{j})E_{il}\bigr)s.

(4.3)

A basis of the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
is given by the elements

E_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{m_{1}}\ldots E_{i_{k}j_{k}}^{m_{k}}w,

(4.4)

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
m_{s}\in\mathbb{N}
,
w\in W
and

1\leq i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{k}\leq n,\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq j_{1}\leq\ldots\leq j_{k}\leq n,\text{ and }(i_{s},i_{s+1})\neq(j_{s},j_{s+1})\text{ for }1\leq s\leq n-1.

(4.5)

Proof.

For the elements
E_{ij}=x_{i}y_{j}\in H_{t,c}
we have that

[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=x_{i}S_{jk}y_{l}-x_{k}S_{li}y_{j},

where
S_{jk}=[y_{j},x_{k}]
has the form (
4.1
). This implies relation (
4.2
) in
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}\subset H_{t,c}
. Similarly, relation (
4.3
) can be checked by making use of the equality

E_{ij}E_{kl}-E_{il}E_{kj}=x_{i}S_{jk}y_{l}-x_{i}S_{lk}y_{j}.

It is also clear that the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
is generated by the elements
E_{ij}
(
1\leq i,j\leq n
) and
w\in W
. Note that elements
(
4.4
)
with the specified restrictions (
4.5
) span the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
. Indeed, by moving elements of the group to the right and by the relation (
4.2
) together with an induction on the degree in
E_{ij}
, any element of the algebra can be represented as a linear combination of monomials (
4.4
), where restriction
1\leq i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{k}\leq n
holds. Then by employing relations (
4.3
) and induction on the degree in
E_{ij}
one can assume that all the restrictions (
4.5
) hold.

We are left to show that elements (
4.4
) from the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
with the specified restrictions (
4.5
) are linearly independent. Observe that any element

\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a_{i}}y_{i}^{b_{i}}w\in H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}},\hskip 10.00002pta_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\,w\in W

(4.6)

from the standard PBW basis of the algebra
H_{t,c}
can
be uniquely represented as a linear combination of elements (
4.4
), (
4.5
).
Indeed, there will be a unique term
h
in the linear combination of degree
\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}
in elements
E_{ij}
. If
a_{1}
and
b_{1}
are both positive then the first factor of
h
has to be
E_{11}^{\min(a_{1},b_{1})}
. If
a_{2}
and
b_{2}
are both positive then the second factor will be
E_{21}^{\min(a_{2},b_{1}-a_{1})}
or
E_{12}^{\min(a_{1}-b_{1},b_{2})}
or
E_{22}^{\min(a_{2},b_{2})}
in the cases
b_{1}>a_{1}
or
a_{1}>b_{1}
or
a_{1}=b_{1}
, respectively. This process can be continued to determine
h
completely. In the cases when some
a_{i}
or
b_{i}
vanish, the corresponding variable
x_{i}
or
y_{i}
is not present in the monomial (
4.6
) and the index
i
does not appear in the first or second place, respectively, in the factors of
h
, otherwise the process of building the element
h
is similar to the above.

This proves that elements (
4.4
), (
4.5
) form a basis of the algebra, which also implies that relations (
4.2
), (
4.3
) are complete.
∎

4.2.
The Dunkl angular momentum subalgebra

Now let
W
be a finite real reflection group. Let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{h}
for a fixed
W
-invariant inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
be the dual basis of
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
. Note that
\mathfrak{h}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{h}
as
W
-modules. Define elements
M_{ij}=x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}\in H_{t,c}(W)
, where
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Note that
M_{ij}=-M_{ji}
.

Definition 4.5
.

(cf.
[
22
]
)
The
Dunkl angular momentum subalgebra

H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}(W)
of
H_{t,c}(W)
is the subalgebra generated by all
M_{ij}
and the group
W
.

In the real case we have
\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})=2
and formula (
4.1
) takes the form

S_{jk}=[y_{j},x_{k}]=t\delta_{jk}-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}c_{s}\alpha_{s}(y_{j})x_{k}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})s.

(4.7)

Consider elements
m_{ij}\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{h}
given by
m_{ij}=y_{i}\wedge y_{j}
. The next statement was established in
[
22
]
for
t=1
. The case of general
t
is similar and we give details below.

Theorem 4.6
.

The algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is isomorphic to the quotient
B
of the skew product of the tensor algebra
T(\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{h})\rtimes{W}
by the relations

\displaystyle[m_{ij},m_{kl}]=m_{il}S_{jk}+m_{jk}S_{il}-m_{ik}S_{jl}-m_{jl}S_{ik},

(4.8)

\displaystyle m_{ij}m_{kl}+m_{jk}m_{il}+m_{ki}m_{jl}=m_{ij}S_{kl}+m_{jk}S_{il}+m_{ki}S_{jl}.

(4.9)

The isomorphism is given by the map
\rho\colon B\to H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
such that
\rho(m_{ij})=M_{ij}
and
\rho(w)=w
for
w\in W
.
A basis of the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is given by the elements

M_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\ldots M_{i_{k}j_{k}}^{r_{k}}w,

(4.10)

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
r_{s}\in\mathbb{N}
,
w\in W
and

i_{s}<j_{s},\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{k}\leq n,\hskip 10.00002pti_{s}=i_{s+1}\implies j_{s}<j_{s+1},\hskip 10.00002pti_{s}<i_{s^{\prime}}<j_{s}\implies j_{s^{\prime}}\leq j_{s}.

(4.11)

Proof.

Consider the isomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}^{*}
given by
\varphi(v)(u)=(v,u)
for any
v,u\in\mathfrak{h}
. Under this isomorphism
\varphi(y_{i})=x_{i}
and
\varphi(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee},\alpha_{s}^{\vee})\alpha_{s}
since
\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})=2
and by orthogonality of the reflection
s\in\mathcal{S}
. We also have
\beta(v)=\varphi(v)(\varphi^{-1}(\beta))
for any
v\in\mathfrak{h},\beta\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Therefore

\alpha_{s}(y_{j})x_{i}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee})=x_{j}\left(\frac{2\alpha_{s}^{\vee}}{(\alpha_{s}^{\vee},\alpha_{s}^{\vee})}\right)\frac{(\alpha_{s}^{\vee},\alpha_{s}^{\vee})}{2}\alpha_{s}(y_{i})=\alpha_{s}(y_{i})x_{j}(\alpha_{s}^{\vee}).

(4.12)

Note that
M_{ij}=E_{ij}-E_{ji}
, and elements
E_{ij}
satisfy relations given in Theorem
4.4
. We apply the first relation of Theorem
4.4
to

[M_{ij},M_{kl}]=[E_{ij},E_{kl}]-[E_{ji},E_{kl}]-[E_{ij},E_{lk}]+[E_{ji},E_{lk}],

and we simplify the result by making use of relation (
4.12
). After collecting terms at each
M_{ij}
and cancellations of the terms coming from the second sum in the right-hand side of equality (
4.2
) we get

[M_{ij},M_{kl}]=M_{il}S_{jk}+M_{jk}S_{il}-M_{ik}S_{jl}-M_{jl}S_{ik}.

(4.13)

Consider now

M_{ij}M_{kl}+M_{jk}M_{il}+M_{ki}M_{jl}=(E_{ij}E_{kl}-E_{kj}E_{il})-(E_{ij}E_{lk}-E_{ik}E_{lj})-(E_{ji}E_{kl}-E_{ki}E_{jl})\\
+(E_{ji}E_{lk}-E_{jk}E_{li})+(E_{jk}E_{il}-E_{ik}E_{jl})+(E_{kj}E_{li}-E_{ki}E_{lj})\\
=-(x_{i}S_{jl}y_{k}-x_{i}S_{kl}y_{j})+(x_{j}S_{il}y_{k}-x_{j}S_{kl}y_{i})+(x_{k}S_{jl}y_{i}-x_{k}S_{il}y_{j}),

(4.14)

where we used the relation
S_{ij}=S_{ji}
which follows from (
4.12
). By the Jacobi identity,

[S_{jl},y_{k}]=[S_{kl},y_{j}]
. This implies that relation (
4.14
) can be rearranged to the form

M_{ij}M_{kl}+M_{jk}M_{il}+M_{ki}M_{jl}=M_{ij}S_{kl}+M_{jk}S_{il}+M_{ki}S_{jl}.

(4.15)

Consider the map
\rho_{0}\colon\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{h}\to\langle M_{ij}|1\leq i<j\leq n\rangle
given by
\rho(m_{ij})=M_{ij}
, where in the codomain we have the linear span. The map
\rho_{0}
is
W
-equivariant. It follows from relations (
4.13
) and (
4.15
) that
\rho_{0}
extends to a well-defined homomorphism of algebras
\rho\colon B\to H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
by defining
\rho(w)=w
. It is also clear that
\rho
is an epimorphism. Furthermore, monomials (
4.10
) with restrictions (
4.11
) linearly generate the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}(W)
. This follows from a noncrossing visualisation of these monomials and since application of the relation (
4.15
) allows to reduce the number of crossings in the monomials from the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}(W)
(see
[
22
]
).
Consider now the highest symbol of the monomials (
4.10
) in the Dunkl embedding, it has the form

L_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\ldots L_{i_{k}j_{k}}^{r_{k}}w
, where
L_{ij}=x_{i}p_{j}-x_{j}p_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}]
. These highest symbols are linearly independent (see e.g.
[
23
]
), which implies that elements (
4.10
) with restrictions (
4.11
) form a basis of the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}(W)
.

Similarly, by applying relations (
4.8
), (
4.9
) we deduce that the linear span of the corresponding monomials
m_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\ldots m_{i_{k}j_{k}}^{r_{k}}w\in B
gives the entire algebra
B
. These monomials are linearly independent since
\rho
maps them to a basis of
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}(W)
, and it follows that

\rho
is an isomorphism.
∎

As a corollary we get the following statement.

Proposition 4.7
.

There is an isomorhism of algebras

H_{0,0}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}\cong\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}.

Proof.

The isomorphism
H_{0,0}\cong\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}
restricts to an injective morphism

H_{0,0}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}.

It is sufficient to show that the image of this map equals
\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
.

Theorem
4.6
says that a basis of
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is given by the monomials (
4.10
) satisfying restrictions (
4.11
). Consider a basis element
b=M_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots M_{i_{k}j_{k}}^{r_{k}}w
and let
r=2\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}
. Under the standard filtration
b\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
and
b\not\in\mathcal{F}_{r-1}
. Then the symbol of
b
in
\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}=H_{0,0}
is also
M_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots M_{i_{k}j_{k}}^{r_{k}}w
. Since Theorem
4.6
again says that all such monomials are a basis of
H_{0,0}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
the equality
H_{0,0}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=\gr H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
holds.

∎

Let
\mathfrak{c}
be the parameter space
\mathbb{C}\oplus\Hom_{W}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{C})
. Then there exists a
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{c}]
-algebra
H^{so(n)}
which specialises to
H_{t,c}^{so(n)}
.
An immediate consequence of Proposition
4.7
is that:

Corollary 4.8
.

The algebra
H^{so(n)}
is flat over
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{c}]
.

The next result is key to understanding the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
since rings of invariants are often much easier to study than algebras given by generators and relations.

Theorem 4.9
.

There is equality
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}
of subalgebras of
H_{t,c}
.

Proof.

First we show that there is an inclusion
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}\subset H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}
.

As in (
3.4
), the action of any
g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}
is
g(x_{i})=ax_{i}+by_{i}
,
g(y_{j})=cx_{j}+dy_{j}
, where
ad-bc=1
. The group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
acts trivially on
W
by definition. It suffices then to show that
g
fixes any generator
x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
. We perform the calculation explicitly

g(x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i})=adx_{i}y_{j}+bcy_{i}x_{j}-adx_{j}y_{i}-bcy_{j}x_{i}.

(4.16)

Since
W
is a real reflection group, (
4.12
) implies that
[x_{i},y_{j}]=[x_{j},y_{i}]
and thus
y_{i}x_{j}-y_{j}x_{i}=x_{j}y_{i}-x_{i}y_{j}
. Therefore, (
4.16
) can be rearranged to give

adx_{i}y_{j}+bcx_{j}y_{i}-adx_{j}y_{i}-bcx_{i}y_{j}=(ad-bc)(x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i})=x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}.

We conclude that
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}\subset H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}
.

Identifying
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}
with the space of
2\times n
matrices, it is a classical result in invariant theory
[
25
, Proposition 9.2]
that the subalgebra
A
of
P
generated by the determinants
x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
is the invariant ring
P^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}
. Since
H_{0}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=A\rtimes W
and
H_{0}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}=P^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}\rtimes W
, we deduce that
H_{0}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=H_{0}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}
.

We have shown that
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}\subset H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}
. If we can show that
\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}
the result will follow by Lemma
2.1
(i). As
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
is a reductive group we can apply Lemma
2.1
(iii) to get
\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}=(\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c})^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}=H_{0}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}
. Combining this with Proposition
4.7
, which says that
H_{0}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
, implies that
\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=H_{0}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=H_{0}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}=\mathrm{gr}\,H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}
.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

If
W
is not a real reflection group then it does not preserve the linear span of the

and the algebra generated by the

and

Remark 4.11
.

One can generalise the construction of the degree zero and the Dunkl angular momentum subalgebras as follows. Let

be a reductive Lie subalgebra. Pick
W\subset N_{GL(\mathfrak{h})}(\mathfrak{g})
a (finite) reflection group. Then one can consider the subalgebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{g}}
of
H_{t,c}
generated by
\mathfrak{g}
and

. It would be interesting to classify all pairs
(\mathfrak{g},W)
such that
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{g}}
defines a flat family of quotients of

when

.

4.3.
Subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}

One can consider
H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}
for other reductive subgroups
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}
and ask if there exists
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{*}
such that
H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}=H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{g}}
. We do not consider finite
\Gamma
here. Other than
\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
, this leaves the normalizer
N
of
\mathrm{T}
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
.

Let
F
be the automorphism of
H_{t,c}
defined by
F(x_{i})=y_{i},F(y_{i})=-x_{i}
and
F(w)=w
. Then
F^{2}=-1\in\mathrm{T}
and we can consider the action of the group
N=\langle\mathrm{T},F\rangle
on
H_{t,c}
.

Recall that
M_{ij}:=x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
and
H^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}_{t,c}
is generated by the
M_{ij}
and
W
.

Lemma 4.12
.

There is a proper inclusion
H^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}_{t,c}\subset H^{N}_{t,c}
.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}\subset H_{t,c}^{N}
. Since this is an inclusion of filtered algebras, it suffices
to show that the inclusion is proper for the associated graded algebras. By Lemma
2.1
(iii) and Theorem
4.9
it is sufficient to establish that
H_{0,0}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}\neq H_{0,0}^{N}
.
We have

H^{N}_{0,0}=(P\rtimes W)^{N}=P^{N}\rtimes W.

Then it suffices to show that the subalgebra
B
of
P
generated by the
M_{ij}
is a proper subalgebra of
P^{N}
. Consider the elements
C_{ij,kl}:=x_{i}x_{j}y_{k}y_{l}+x_{k}x_{l}y_{i}y_{j}
. They belong to
P^{N}
. We claim that they do not belong to
B
. For this, we define
\Delta\colon P^{N}\to\mathbb{C}[x,y]^{N}=\mathbb{C}[x^{2}y^{2}]
by
\Delta(x_{i})=x,\Delta(y_{i})=y
. Notice that
\Delta(M_{ij})=0
but
\Delta(C_{ij,kl})=2x^{2}y^{2}\neq 0
. The claim follows.
∎

Consider now the degree two component of
H_{0,0}^{N}=P^{N}\rtimes W
. Notice that
P_{2}^{\mathrm{T}}=\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{*}\supset P_{2}^{N}
since
\mathrm{T}\subset N
. Under the identification
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{*}=\End(\mathfrak{h})
, the automorphism
F
corresponds to the map
A\mapsto-A^{T}
. Therefore,
P_{2}^{N}=P_{2}^{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}=\mathfrak{so}(n)
and hence
(\gr H_{t,c}^{N})_{2}=\mathfrak{so}(n)\otimes\mathbb{C}W
. This, combined with Lemma
4.12
, implies that there is no subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
such that
H^{N}_{t,c}=H^{\mathfrak{g}}_{t,c}
.

4.4.
The centralizer of
\mathfrak{so}(n)
in
W

Assume now that
(W,\mathfrak{h})
is an arbitrary complex reflection group. Each element
m\in W
acts as a linear transformation of
\mathfrak{h}
, denote this corresponding map
M\in\GL(\mathfrak{h})
. Recall that
W
has a natural action on the dual space
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, given by
(m\cdot y)(x)=y(m^{-1}x)
and therefore the element
m\in W
corresponds to
(M^{-1})^{\mathrm{T}}\in\GL(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
.

We can identify the space
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{*}
with
\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{h})
via the map
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{h})

given by
\varphi(x_{i}\otimes y_{j})=x_{i}y_{j}
. Equip
\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{h})
with a
W
-action by letting
w\cdot M=wMw^{-1}
. With this action the map
\varphi
becomes an isomorphism of
W
-modules.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
m\in W
commute with all elements
x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
. If
n\geq 3
then
m
acts as a scalar multiple of the identity. If
n=2
then
m
acts as the sum of a scalar multiple of the identity and a skew-symmetric matrix.

Proof.

Using the identification
\varphi
with
\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{h})
, the pair
x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
corresponds to the matrix with
1
in the
i^{th}
row and
j^{th}
column,
-1
in the
j^{th}
row and
i^{th}
column and
0
everywhere else. Let us denote this matrix by
P_{ij}
. Then for
m
to commute with
x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
we require
m\cdot P_{ij}=MP_{ij}M^{-1}=P_{ij}
. Let
m_{ij}
denote the entry in the
i^{th}
row and
j^{th}
column of
M
. Calculating
MP_{ij}M^{-1}=P_{ij}
gives
m_{ij}=-m_{ji}
,
m_{ii}=m_{jj}
and other entries of
M
are
0
. There are two cases to consider.

If
\dim\,\mathfrak{h}\geq 3
we must have that
m_{k\ell}=0
for all
k\neq\ell
. This is because the commutation relation for
x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1}
implies that
m_{k\ell}=0
for all
(k,\ell)\neq(1,2)
or
(2,1)
. But then the commutation relation for
x_{1}y_{3}-x_{3}y_{1}
forces
m_{k\ell}=0
for all
(k,\ell)\neq(1,3)
or
(3,1)
. The only non-zero terms of
M
are the diagonal entries which are all equal.

If
\dim\,\mathfrak{h}=2
then we need only consider the element
x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1}
and we have
m_{11}=m_{22}
and
m_{12}=-m_{21}
. Hence the matrix associated to the action of
m
has the form

M=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
-b&a\end{bmatrix}

for
a,\,b\in\mathbb{C}
, as required.
∎

Corollary 4.14
.

Suppose that
W\subset\mathrm{O}(n)
. Then the centraliser
Z_{W}(\mathfrak{so}(n))
of
\mathfrak{so}(n)
in
W
equals
Z(W)
for
n\geq 3
, and
Z_{W}(\mathfrak{so}(2))=W\cap\mathrm{SO}(2)
.

If
W
is a real reflection group then we identify
\mathfrak{so}(n)\subset\End(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{*}
with
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}
using the
W
-equivariant isomorphism
\mathfrak{h}{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Assume that
W
is an irreducible real reflection group and
\dim\mathfrak{h}\geq 3
. Then the kernel of
W\to GL(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h})
equals
Z(W)
.

Proof.

Since
Z(W)
is either trivial, or equals
\{\pm 1\}
, the kernel clearly contains
Z(W)
. Let
g\in W
act trivially on
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}
. Since
g
has finite order, it is diagonalizable. Let
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
g
on
\mathfrak{h}
. Recall that each
\omega_{i}
is a root of unity. Then in a suitable basis
g
acts on
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}
as a diagonal matrix with diagonal entries
\omega_{1}\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{n-1}\omega_{n}
. If this is the identity matrix then
\omega_{i}\omega_{j}=1
for
i\neq j
. If
\omega_{1}\notin\{\pm 1\}
say then
\omega_{2}=\omega_{3}=\omega_{1}^{-1}
and
\omega_{3}=\omega_{2}^{-1}
is a contradiction. Thus,
\omega_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
for all
i
. If
\omega_{i}=-1
for some
i
then this forces
\omega_{i}=-1
for all
i
. Thus,
g=-\mathrm{Id}
in this case.
∎

5.
The centre of the angular momentum algebra in rank two

In this section we assume
\dim\mathfrak{h}=2
. Then
W=D_{\ell}
is the dihedral group of order
2\ell
, for
\ell\geq 3
, and we write
D_{\ell}=\{1,\sigma,\dots,\sigma^{\ell-1},s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}\}
, where
\sigma
is a rotation and the
s_{i}
are the reflections. Note that
s_{i}\sigma s_{i}=\sigma^{-1}
. Let
a_{0},\dots,a_{\ell-1}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\sigma\rangle
be the basis of the group algebra consisting of primitive idempotents.
Explicitly, we have
a_{i}=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\zeta^{-ki}\sigma^{k}
, where
\zeta
is a fixed primitive
\ell
th root of unity.
Then
\sigma a_{i}=\zeta^{i}a_{i}
. In
\mathbb{C}D_{\ell}
we have
s_{i}a_{j}=a_{-j}s_{i}
where we define
a_{i+\ell}:=a_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. We set

b_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{i}+a_{-i}&2i\neq 0\,\textrm{mod}\,\ell\\
a_{i}&2i=0\,\textrm{mod}\,\ell\end{array}\right.\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{for $i=0,\dots,N:=\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor$}.

We also set
d_{i}=a_{i}-a_{-i}

for
i=1,\dots,\widetilde{N}:=\lfloor\frac{\ell-1}{2}\rfloor
.
Note that
N+\widetilde{N}=\ell-1
.
Moreover,
d_{i}^{2}=b_{i}
and
d_{i}b_{i}=d_{i}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The spaces

Z_{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\langle\sigma\rangle\,|\,s_{i}z=zs_{i}\,\forall\,i\},\hskip 10.00002ptZ_{-}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\langle\sigma\rangle\,|\,s_{i}z=-zs_{i}\,\forall\,i\},

have basis
b_{0},\dots,b_{N}
and
d_{1},\dots,d_{\widetilde{N}}
respectively.

Proof.

Since
\langle\sigma\rangle
is a normal subgroup of
D_{\ell}
, the latter acts by conjugation on
\mathbb{C}\langle\sigma\rangle
and this action factors through the quotient
D_{\ell}/\langle\sigma\rangle=\langle s_{1}\rangle
. The
a_{i}
are a basis of
\mathbb{C}\langle\sigma\rangle
. Therefore, we get a basis of
Z_{+}
and
Z_{-}
respectively by applying the averaging operator
(1/2)(1+s_{1})
(and rescaling if necessary), respectively the operator
(1/2)(1-s_{1})
. The result follows.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

The centre of
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(2)}(D_{\ell})
is a direct sum of
N+1
polynomial rings. More specifically, when
\ell
is even,

Z(H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(2)}(D_{\ell}))=b_{0}\mathbb{C}[M_{1,2}^{2}]\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N-1}b_{i}\mathbb{C}[M_{1,2}d_{i}]\right)\oplus b_{N}\mathbb{C}[M_{1,2}^{2}],

and when
\ell
is odd,

Z(H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(2)}(D_{\ell}))=b_{0}\mathbb{C}[M_{1,2}^{2}]\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}\mathbb{C}[M_{1,2}d_{i}]\right).

Proof.

The subalgebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(2)}(D_{\ell})\subset H_{t,c}(D_{\ell})
is generated by the element
M_{1,2}
and elements
w\in D_{\ell}
. It has a basis
\{M_{1,2}^{j}w\}
, where
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. The subalgebra is graded with
\deg M_{1,2}=2
and
\deg w=0
. Therefore, its centre is also graded.
We consider each graded piece in turn. In even degrees, we are looking for elements
M_{1,2}^{2k}z
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, with
z\in\mathbb{C}D_{\ell}
acting trivially on
M_{1,2}
and commuting with
D_{\ell}
. By Corollary
4.14
, the first condition forces
z\in\mathbb{C}\langle\sigma\rangle
since
\dim\mathfrak{h}=2
. Then, the second condition in turn means that
z
belongs to
Z_{+}
. Hence,
z
is a linear combination of the
b_{i}
(which are all in the centre). In odd degrees, we have elements of the form
M_{1,2}^{2k+1}z
, again for some
z\in\mathbb{C}D_{\ell}
. For this to commute with
M_{1,2}
, we must have
[M_{1,2},z]=0
forcing
z\in\mathbb{C}\langle\sigma\rangle
. To commute with
D_{\ell}
it suffices to check that
[s_{1},M_{1,2}^{2k+1}z]=0
. This forces
s_{1}z=-zs_{1}
; that is,
z\in Z_{-}
. Thus, the element is a linear combination of the elements
M_{1,2}^{2k+1}d_{i}
. Since
d_{i}d_{j}=\delta_{i,j}b_{i}
the result follows.
∎

We note that for
t\neq 0
the centre of the algebra
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is described in
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
for
n\geq 3
. However, it is missed in these references that the case
n=2
requires different considerations as above.

6.
Invariant subalgebras

In order to give a uniform treatment of the rings of invariants under both
\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
, we make a number of general assumptions, which we check hold for both of these groups.

6.1.
Setup

Recall that the rational Cherednik algebra can be considered as an algebra
H
over the polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{c}]
such that specializing the parameters to some
(t,c)\in\mathfrak{c}
gives
H_{t,c}
. We will consider automorphisms of
H
that are
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{c}]
-linear and hence induce automorphisms of each
H_{t,c}
.

Recall, e.g.
[
36
, § 6.46]
, that a connected linear algebraic group
\Gamma
is said to be
reductive
if its unipotent radical
R_{u}(\Gamma)
is trivial. By Weyl’s complete reducibility theorem, this is equivalent to either (a) there exists a finite-dimensional faithful semi-simple representation of
\Gamma
; or (b) every finite-dimensional representation of
\Gamma
is semi-simple; see
[
36
, Theorem 22.42]
.

Throughout, we fix
\Gamma
to be a group of automorphisms of
H
satisfying

(I)

\Gamma
is reductive; and

(II)

\Gamma
acts trivially on
W
and
R
and linearly on
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, preserving the symplectic form.

The group
W
acts by conjugation as inner automorphisms on
H
. The action of
Z(W)
is trivial on
W
and
R
and linear on
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Therefore, we may also impose that

(III)

the image of
Z(W)\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
lies in
\Gamma
.

In view of (II) we may write
H^{\Gamma\times W}=(H^{W})^{\Gamma}=(H^{\Gamma})^{W}
without ambiguity. The following elementary observation will be used repeatedly.

Lemma 6.1
.

The group
\Gamma
preserves the filtration on
H_{t,c}
and hence acts as graded automorphisms of
H_{0,0}=P\rtimes W
.

Proof.

Notice that since
\mathcal{F}_{0}H_{t,c}=\mathbb{C}W
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}H_{t,c}=\mathbb{C}W\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and

\mathcal{F}_{k}H_{t,c}=(\mathcal{F}_{k-1}H_{t,c})(\mathcal{F}_{1}H_{t,c}(W))

the claim follows from (II).
∎

6.2.
GIT quotients

We consider a representation
V
of the group
\Gamma
. For
\Gamma=\mathrm{T}
we assume
V=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, and for
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
we assume
V=\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Let
\pi\colon V\to V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
be the quotient map.

Recall that a representation
U
of an algebraic group
\Gamma
is said to be
stable
if a generic
\Gamma
-orbit is closed in
U
.

Lemma 6.2
.

The representation
V
is a stable representation.

Proof.

For
\Gamma=\mathrm{T}
, a generic orbit in
V
is defined by the system of equations
x_{i}y_{j}=\alpha_{ij}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
\alpha_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\alpha_{ij}\neq 0
for some pair
i,j
. In particular, it is closed and hence
V
is stable.

For
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
, we identify
V
with the space of
2\times n
matrices, with
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
acting by left multiplication; here
n=\dim\mathfrak{h}
. Then
\mathbb{C}[V]^{\Gamma}
is generated by the column determinants
\mathrm{det}_{i,j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Let
us choose any
p\in V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
other than
0
. Then there exists some
i<j
such that
\mathrm{det}_{i,j}(A)\neq 0
for all
A\in\pi^{-1}(p)
. Since
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
acts freely (by left multiplication) on the space of all
2\times 2
matrices of fixed non-zero determinant, it implies that
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
acts freely on
\pi^{-1}(p)
. Each fiber
\pi^{-1}(p)
contains a unique closed
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-orbit; see e.g.
[
34
, II 3.3 Satz 3 (b)]
. It is the orbit of minimal dimension in the fiber. If
\pi^{-1}(p)
was not a single
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-orbit, then the closed
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-orbit in
\pi^{-1}(p)
would have dimension less than
\dim\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. In particular, it is not a free orbit. We deduce that
\pi^{-1}(p)
is a single orbit and hence
V
is stable.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

The spaces
V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
and
V/\!\!/\!\,(\Gamma\times W)
are Gorenstein.

The fact that the quotient
V/\!\!/\!\,\mathrm{T}
is Gorenstein follows from standard results e.g.
[
15
, Corollary 6.4.3]
. However, there is a general result of Knop, ensuring the quotient is Gorenstein
[
33
]
, that can be
applied in both cases which we do in the proof.

Proof.

We consider first
V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
.
The main result of
[
33
]
states that
V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
is Gorenstein if the following three conditions hold:

(a)

V
is a stable representation.

(b)

V_{s}:=\{v\in V\,|\,\dim\Gamma_{v}>0\}
has codimension at least two in
V
.

(c)

\lambda_{V}=\lambda_{\mathrm{ad}}
.

Here, for a representation
U
,
\lambda_{U}
is the linear character of
\Gamma
given by the composed map
\Gamma\to\GL(U)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\det}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
\mathrm{ad}
denotes the adjoint representation of
\Gamma
. We note that for both
\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
,
\lambda_{V}=\lambda_{\mathrm{ad}}
is the trivial character. Therefore (c) holds. Lemma
6.2
says that (a) holds. Thus, we only need to check (b).

For
T
, the space
V_{s}
equals
\{0\}
. Therefore, (b) is satisfied.

For
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
, we make the identification of
V
with the space of
2\times n
matrices, as in the proof of Lemma
6.2
. It follows from what is written there that
V_{s}\subset\pi^{-1}(0)
. In fact, we claim
V_{s}=\pi^{-1}(0)
. Notice that
\pi^{-1}(0)
is precisely the set of
2\times n
matrices of rank at most
1
. Let
A
have rank one. Then
gA=A
for
g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}
if and only if
g
acts trivially on
\mathrm{Im}\,A
. The subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
fixing a line pointwise is one-dimensional. Hence
A\in V_{s}
. The space of
2\times n
matrices of rank at most
1
is
n+1
-dimensional. Therefore, we see that (b) holds if
n\geq 3
. When
n=1
,
V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma=\{0\}
and when
n=2
,
V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma=\mathbb{A}^{1}
since
\mathbb{C}[V]^{\Gamma}=\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{det}_{1,2}]
. In both cases,
V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
is Gorenstein.

Now the argument for
V/\!\!/\!\,(\Gamma\times W)
is similar. Since
W
is finite, properties (a) and (b) follow immediately from the fact that they hold for
V
as a
\Gamma
-module. For (c),
\lambda_{V}|_{W}
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{ad}}|_{W}
are both the trivial character (the former because
W\subset\mathrm{Sp}(V)
) so once again (c) follows from the fact that it holds for
V
as a
\Gamma
-module.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume
\dim\mathfrak{h}>1
for
\Gamma=\mathrm{T}
and
\dim\mathfrak{h}>2
for
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. The action of
\overline{W}
on
V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
is generically free.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{T}
and
w\in W\setminus Z(W)
. Then we can choose
v\in\mathfrak{h}
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
such that neither is an eigenvector for
w
. Since
v,\lambda\neq 0
, the
\mathrm{T}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{(v,\lambda)}
of
(v,\lambda)
is closed but free. Thus, to show that
w\cdot\pi(v,\lambda)\neq\pi(v,\lambda)
, it suffices to show that
w(\mathcal{O}_{(v,\lambda)})\neq\mathcal{O}_{(v,\lambda)}
. But this follows from the fact that
(w(v),w(\lambda))
does not belong to the line spanned by
(v,\lambda)
.

Now let
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. Consider a generic element
p\in V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
. The orbit passing through the preimage
\pi^{-1}(p)=(x,y)
,
x,y\in\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, contains a point with coordinates
x_{1}=y_{2}=0
hence is uniquely determined by
p
. We can identify this orbit as well as
p
with a point in the cone over the Grassmanian
Gr(2,n)
, and let
U=\langle x,y\rangle\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the two-dimensional plane representing this point. One can assume that
U
does not contain eigenvectors of any
w\in W\setminus Z(W)
, hence
w(U)\neq U
as required.

∎

Remark 6.5
.

If
\dim\mathfrak{h}=2
then
W\cap SO(2)
acts trivially on
V/\!\!/\!\,\mathrm{SL}_{2}
and so Lemma
6.4
is false in that case.

Proposition 6.6
.

Assume
\dim\mathfrak{h}>1
when
\Gamma=\mathrm{T}
and
\dim\mathfrak{h}>2
when
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. The group
\overline{W}
acts freely on an open subset of
V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
with complement of codimension at least two.

Proof.

For
\Gamma=\mathrm{T}
,
\pi^{-1}(0)=\mathfrak{h}\times\{0\}\cup\{0\}\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}
has dimension
\dim\mathfrak{h}
and
\pi^{-1}(\overline{u})
is a single closed free
\mathrm{T}
-orbit for any
\overline{u}\neq 0
,
\overline{u}\in V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
. It suffices to show that
\overline{W}
acts freely on an open subset of
(V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}
whose complement has codimension at least two (in either
(V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}
or in
V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
). Similarly, for
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
, it is shown in the proof of Lemma
6.2
that
\pi^{-1}(\overline{u})
is a single closed free
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-orbit for any
\overline{u}\neq 0
. Therefore, it suffices to show that
\overline{W}
acts freely on an open subset of
(V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}
whose complement has codimension at least two. Let
U=V\setminus\pi^{-1}(0)
.

For
u\in U
, let
\overline{u}=\pi(u)
. If
w\in W
fixes
\overline{u}
then
w(u)\in\Gamma\cdot u
so there must exist
z\in\Gamma
such that
zw(u)=u
. The element
z
is unique because the stabilizer
\Gamma_{u}=\{1\}
. Moreover, since
w
has finite order,
z
must also have finite order. In particular, it is a diagonalizable element of
\Gamma
. We must show that
\dim T_{\overline{u}}(V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma)-\dim(T_{\overline{u}}(V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma))^{w}
is at least two. If this difference is zero then
w
acts trivially on
V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
since the variety is irreducible; in this case
w\in Z(W)
by Lemma
6.4
and can be ignored. Thus, it suffices to show that the difference is always even. Equivalently, the number of non-trivial eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity) of
w
acting on
T_{\overline{u}}(V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma)
is even.

If
\mathfrak{k}
is the Lie algebra of
\Gamma
then

T_{\overline{u}}(V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma)\cong(T_{u}V)/(\mathfrak{k}\cdot u)=V/(\mathfrak{k}\cdot u).

and the moment map for the Hamiltonian action of
\Gamma
on
V
is given by
\mu(u)(X)=\omega(X\cdot u,u)
, for
X\in\mathfrak{k}
. Then
(d_{u}\mu)(v)(X)=2\omega(X\cdot u,v)
which implies that
\Ker(d_{u}\mu)=\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}
(this is a general property of moment maps). Identifying
\mathfrak{k}
with the Lie algebra of
\Gamma\times W
, there is a
\langle wz\rangle
-equivariant short exact sequence

0\to\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}/\mathfrak{k}\to V/(\mathfrak{k}\cdot u)=T_{\overline{u}}(V/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{u}\mu}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{k}^{*}\to 0.

Now
\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}/\mathfrak{k}
is a symplectic vector space on which
zw
acts symplectically, so it is certainly the case that
\dim\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}/\mathfrak{k}-\dim(\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}/\mathfrak{k})^{wz}
is even. The action of
zw
on
\mathfrak{k}^{*}
equals the action of
z
. Since
z
is semi-simple, we can choose a maximal torus
\mathrm{T}\subset\Gamma
containing
z
. The fact that every root in the root space decomposition of
\mathfrak{k}
with respect to
\mathrm{T}
appears with its negative implies that
\dim\mathfrak{k}^{*}-\dim(\mathfrak{k}^{*})^{z}
is even too.

∎

For the remainder of this article, we assume:

\dim\mathfrak{h}>1
when
\Gamma=\mathrm{T}
and
\dim\mathfrak{h}>2
when
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
.

By assumption (III), the conjugation action of

The following is an immediate consequence of this fact.

Lemma 6.7

H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}_{i}

Let
\overline{W}=W/Z(W)

[
14
]
,
[
31

that a
2
-cocycle on

\overline{W}

f(g_{2},g_{3})f(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3})=f(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3})f(g_{1},g_{2})

For any

-cocycle

Choose coset representatives

Lemma 6.8
.

Define

Proof.

We will check directly that

then
f_{i}(\bar{w}_{a},\bar{w}_{b})=\chi_{i}(z_{ab})

∎

We will usually focus on

We recall that a noetherian algebra

is
Auslander–Gorenstein
if it has both finite left and right injective dimensions and for each finitely generated (left or right)
A
-module
M
the following holds: for every integer
i\geq 0
and every submodule
N
of
\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,A)
, we have
\Ext^{j}_{A}(N,A)=0
for all
j<i
. As noted in
[
10
]
, by a theorem of Zaks, if a noetherian algebra has finite right and left injective dimensions, then these are equal.

The
grade
of a finitely generated left
A
-module

is defined to be

j_{A}(M)=\min\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\,|\,\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,A)\neq 0\}.

Assume that
A
has finite Gelfand–Kirillov dimension. Then
A
is said to be (GK)
Cohen–Macaulay
if

j_{A}(M)+\mathrm{GK}\textrm{-}\mathrm{dim}(M)=\mathrm{GK}\textrm{-}\mathrm{dim}(A),

for all finitely generated left
A
-modules
M
.

Now let
A=P^{\Gamma}\rtimes W
.
Consider
A
as the left
P^{\Gamma}
-module. Then
the space
\Hom_{P^{\Gamma}}(A,P^{\Gamma})
is a left
A
-module via
(a\star\phi)(b)=\phi(ba)
for
a,b\in A
and
\phi\in\Hom_{P^{\Gamma}}(A,P^{\Gamma})

Lemma 6.11
.

There is an isomorphism of left
A
-modules
\sigma\colon{}_{A}A{\;\stackrel{{{}_{\sim}}}{{\to}}\;}\Hom_{P^{\Gamma}}({}_{P^{\Gamma}}A,P^{\Gamma})
given by

\sigma(f\otimes u)(b)=b_{u^{-1}}u^{-1}(f),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\ f\in P^{\Gamma},u\in W,

where

b=\sum_{w\in W}b_{w}\otimes w\in A
,
b_{w}\in P^{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

It is immediate that
\sigma(f\otimes u)\in\Hom_{P}({}_{P}A,P)
. We check that it is a morphism of left
A
-modules. Let
f_{i}\in P^{\Gamma},u_{i}\in W
. Then
(f_{1}\otimes u_{1})(f_{2}\otimes u_{2})=f_{1}u_{1}(f_{2})\otimes u_{1}u_{2}
. So

\displaystyle\sigma((f_{1}\otimes u_{1})(f_{2}\otimes u_{2}))(b)

\displaystyle=\sigma(f_{1}u_{1}(f_{2})\otimes u_{1}u_{2})(b)

\displaystyle=b_{u_{2}^{-1}u_{1}^{-1}}u_{2}^{-1}(u_{1}^{-1}(f_{1}u_{1}(f_{2})))

\displaystyle=b_{u_{2}^{-1}u_{1}^{-1}}u_{2}^{-1}(u_{1}^{-1}(f_{1})f_{2})

\displaystyle=b_{u_{2}^{-1}u_{1}^{-1}}(u_{2}^{-1}u_{1}^{-1}(f_{1}))u_{2}^{-1}(f_{2}),

and

\displaystyle(f_{1}\otimes u_{1})\star\sigma(f_{2}\otimes u_{2})(b)

\displaystyle=\sigma(f_{2}\otimes u_{2})(b(f_{1}\otimes u_{1}))

\displaystyle=\sigma(f_{2}\otimes u_{2})\left(\sum_{w\in W}b_{w}w(f_{1})\otimes wu_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=b_{u_{2}^{-1}u_{1}^{-1}}(u_{2}^{-1}u_{1}^{-1}(f_{1}))u_{2}^{-1}(f_{2}).

∎

Lemma 6.12

Let

be a finitely generated left

Since

\Ext^{i}_{P^{\Gamma}}(M,P^{\Gamma})=\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,\Hom_{P^{\Gamma}}(A,P^{\Gamma}))=\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,A).

∎

Theorem 6.13
.

The algebras
H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}
and
\mathbf{a}_{i}H_{t,c}^{\Gamma}\mathbf{a}_{i}

Proof.

By Proposition

[

[

Since the generic

[

Finally, we check that

6.4.
Double centralizer property

Let
A
and
B
be rings, and let
M={{}_{A}}M_{B}
be an
(A,B)
-bimodule. Then there are natural multiplication homomorphisms

\varphi\colon B^{\mathrm{op}}\to\End_{A}M,\hskip 10.00002pt\psi\colon A\to\End_{B}M,

given by, respectively, right and left multiplication.

Lemma 6.15
.

Let
e
be an idempotent in
A
, and let
B=eAe
,
M=Ae
. Then
\varphi
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We have
\varphi(b)(e)=eb=b
, which implies that
\varphi
has zero kernel.

Let now
f\in\End_{A}M
. Let
b=f(e)\in M
. Hence
b=ae
for some
a\in A
. Note that

b=f(e)=f(e^{2})=ef(e)=eb=eae.

Hence
b=ebe\in B
.

Let us consider the image
f(m)
for an arbitrary element
m\in M
. Let
m=xe
, where
x\in A
. We have

f(m)=f(xe)=xf(e)=xb=\varphi(b)(m)

since
xeb=xb
. It follows that
f=\varphi(b)
and therefore
\varphi
is surjective.
∎

By taking
A=\mathbf{a}H^{\Gamma}_{t,c}\mathbf{a}
and
e
the trivial idempotent in
A
we get by Lemma
6.15
that

\End_{\mathbf{a}H^{\Gamma}_{t,c}\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{a}H^{\Gamma}_{t,c}e)\cong(eH^{\Gamma}_{t,c}e)^{\rm op}.

The next theorem shows that under these settings
\psi
is also an isomorphism so that the double centralizer property holds for the bimodule
\mathbf{a}H^{\Gamma}_{t,c}e
.

Theorem 6.16
.

We have an isomorphism

\End_{eH^{\Gamma}_{t,c}e}(\mathbf{a}H^{\Gamma}_{t,c}e)\cong\mathbf{a}H^{\Gamma}_{t,c}\mathbf{a}
.

Proof.

First, we consider the case
t=c=0
. Let
S
be a normal domain with an action of
\overline{W}
such that the corresponding action on
\Spec S
is free on an open subset with complement of codimension at least 2. Then the left multiplication map
\psi\colon S\rtimes\overline{W}\to\End_{eSe}S
is an isomorphism by
[
7
, Proposition E.2.2(2)]
.

Now let
S=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}]^{\Gamma}
. The variety
(\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}^{*})/\!\!/\!\,\Gamma
is normal by
[
34
, § 3.3, Satz 1]
. The action of
\overline{W}
on
\Spec S
is free on an open subset with complement of codimension at least
2
by Proposition
6.6
. Therefore, the statement follows for zero parameters by the previous paragraph since
\mathbf{a}H^{\Gamma}_{0,0}\mathbf{a}=H^{\Gamma}_{0,0}\mathbf{a}\cong S\rtimes\overline{W}

Then, using filtration arguments analogously to the proof of

.
∎

We note that the Satake isomorphism holds in this situation. The proof is identical to that of the usual Satake isomorphism for symplectic reflection algebras; see

.

6.5.
The centre of
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
at
t\neq 0

We note that it is not true that the centre of
H_{t,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}
is trivial when
t\neq 0
. Indeed, it was shown in
[
22
, Section 7]
that
Z(H_{1,c}^{\mathfrak{gl}(n)}(\mathfrak{S}_{n}))=\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{eu}_{c}]
. We show that a similar result holds for any complex reflection group.

By Proposition
7.1
below, the centre of
P^{\mathrm{T}}\rtimes W
equals
P^{\mathrm{T}\times W}\otimes\mathbb{C}Z(W)
. It is a Poisson algebra because
H_{t,c}^{\mathrm{T}}
is a quantization of
P^{\mathrm{T}}\rtimes W
for any
(t,c)
with
t\neq 0
. The Poisson structure on
P^{\mathrm{T}\times W}\otimes\mathbb{C}Z(W)
is independent of the choice of
(t,c)
.

If
B
is a Poisson algebra then
\mathrm{Cas}(B)\subset B
denotes the Poisson centre, consisting of all elements
b
such that
\{b,-\}=0
(
\mathrm{Cas}
stands for ”Casimirs”).

Lemma 6.19
.

Let
A,B
be
\mathbb{C}
-algebras. Assume
B
is a Poisson domain and
A\subset B
a Poisson subalgebra such that
B
is a finite
A
-module. Then
\mathrm{Cas}(A)=\mathrm{Cas}(B)\cap A
.

Proof.

This statement is implicit in the final paragraph of the proof of
[
13
, Proposition 7.2(2)]
, but we include the proof for the reader’s convenience. It is clear that
\mathrm{Cas}(B)\cap A\subset\mathrm{Cas}(A)
. Let
p\in\mathrm{Cas}(A)
and
u\in B
. Since
B
is a finite
A
-module,
u
is integral over
A
. Therefore, there exists a polynomial of minimal degree
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}X^{i}
with
a_{n}=1
and
a_{i}\in A
such that
\sum a_{i}u^{i}=0
. Then

0=\left\{p,\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}u^{i}\right\}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(\{p,a_{i}\}u^{i}+a_{i}\{p,u^{i}\})=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}ia_{i}u^{i-1}\right)\{p,u\}.

By minimality of the polynomial and the fact that we are working over a field of characteristic zero,
\sum ia_{i}u^{i-1}\neq 0
and hence
\{p,u\}=0
because
B
is a domain. Therefore,
p\in\mathrm{Cas}(B)
.
∎

Lemma 6.20
.

The Poisson centre of
P^{\mathrm{T}\times W}\otimes\mathbb{C}Z(W)
equals
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{eu}_{0}]\otimes\mathbb{C}Z(W)
.

Proof.

The idempotents
\mathbf{a}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}Z(W)
are central in
H_{1,c}^{\mathrm{T}}(W)
. This means that they are Poisson central in
P^{T\times W}\otimes\mathbb{C}Z(W)
. Hence the map
(f_{i})\mapsto\sum_{i}f_{i}\otimes\mathbf{a}_{i}
is an isomorphism of Poisson algebras
(P^{\mathrm{T}\times W})^{\oplus\ell}\cong P^{\mathrm{T}\times W}\otimes\mathbb{C}Z(W)
. Therefore, it suffices to show that
\mathrm{Cas}(P^{\mathrm{T}\times W})=\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{eu}_{0}]
.

The Poisson structure on
P^{\mathrm{T}\times W}
comes by restriction from the Possion structure on
P^{\mathrm{T}}
. In other words,
P^{\mathrm{T}\times W}\subset P^{\mathrm{T}}
is a (finite) extension of Poisson algebras. Both these algebras are noetherian domains since they are subrings of invariants in
P
. Therefore, Lemma
6.19
says that

Proposition 6.21

For

In this section we consider the structure of a generic simple

Definition 7.9
.

Let

be a maximal (two-sided) ideal and
\mathfrak{m}=M\cap Z(A)

belongs to

(a)

(b)

there is a unique simple
A

whose annihilator in

equals
\mathfrak{m}
(and thus,
\mathrm{ann}_{A}L=M
);

(c)

(d)

for some

We say that

is a

of

if the equivalent conditions above hold.

For the proof and other equivalent characterizations of regular maximal ideal, see
[
12

Note that

from the property (d) equals the PI-degree of the algebra

by the cited theorem.

The following lemma says that given a flat family of PI-algebras, where the centre of the algebras also varies flatly, the PI-degree of the algebras is constant. Hence, it needs only be computed for one fibre.

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
B\subset Z(A)
be a subalgebra and
\mathfrak{p}\lhd B
a (prime) ideal such that

is prime. Assume that the map

is surjective. Then

\mathrm{P.I.deg}(A)=\mathrm{P.I.deg}(A/\mathfrak{p}A).

Proof.

Let
\overline{M}
be a regular maximal ideal of

and
M
its preimage in
A
. It suffices to show that
M
is a regular (maximal) ideal in
A
. Let
\overline{\mathfrak{m}}=Z(A/\mathfrak{p}A)\cap\overline{M}
and write
\mathfrak{m}
for the preimage of
\overline{\mathfrak{m}}
in
Z(A)
. Then
\overline{M}=\overline{\mathfrak{m}}(A/\mathfrak{p}A)
by
[
12
, Theorem III.1.6]
. We claim that
\mathfrak{m}=M\cap Z(A)
and

. If this is indeed the case then

is regular in
A
, again by
[
12
, Theorem III.1.6]

Let

such that
\overline{z}\in\overline{\mathfrak{m}}
. Then
\overline{z}\in\overline{M}
and
\overline{z}\in Z(A/\mathfrak{p}A)
. Hence

and

. But both are maximal ideals of

, thus
M\cap Z(A)=\mathfrak{m}
.

Finally, we argue that

. Clearly,
\mathfrak{m}A\subset M
. If

then there exist
\overline{z}_{i}\in\overline{\mathfrak{m}}
and
\overline{x}_{i}\in A/\mathfrak{p}A
such that
\overline{x}=\sum_{i}\overline{z}_{i}\overline{x}_{i}
in

. We choose lifts

and

. But

since

. Thus,

.
∎

Since

Lemma 7.14

Let

be an affine, commutative,

-algebra and a domain. Let

Proof.

We recall that Beauville

introduced the notion of symplectic singularities. A normal irreducible variety

The following is well-known to experts, but we sketch the proof since we do not know of a suitable reference.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
X
have symplectic singularities. Then

(i)

X
has rational singularities,

(ii)

is Gorenstein, and hence

(iii)

is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

Let
n=(1/2)\dim X
. If
\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{U}
is the symplectic form then
s=\wedge^{n}\omega
is a non-vanishing top form. It trivializes
\Omega_{U}
and hence implies that the canonical sheaf
\omega_{X}=j_{*}\Omega_{U}
is trivial, where
j\colon U\hookrightarrow X
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a resolution of singularities which is an isomorphism over
U
. Let
K_{X}
and
K_{Y}
denote the canonical divisor on
X
and
Y
respectively. Since
\omega
extends to a regular two form on
Y
,
s
also extends to a regular form on
Y
. Therefore, in the equality

K_{Y}=f^{*}(K_{X})+\sum_{i}m_{i}E_{i},

the coefficients
m_{i}
are non-negative since they are the order of vanishing of
f^{*}s
along the exceptional divisor
E_{i}
. Here the sum is over all exceptional divisors of
f
. This means that
X
has canonical singularities. By
[
20
, Theoreme 1]
this means that
X
has rational singularities. In particular, Kempf’s Theorem
[
32
]
says that
X
is Cohen-Macaulay. This implies that the dualizing complex
\omega_{X}^{{\>\raisebox{2.0pt}{\text{\circle*{1.5}}}}}
of
X
is quasi-isomorphic to
\omega_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Thus,
\mathcal{O}_{X}
has finite injective dimension i.e.
X
is Gorenstein. ∎

The variety

with symplectic singularities is said to be
a conic symplectic singularity
if it is affine,
\mathbb{C}[X]
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded with
\mathbb{C}[X]_{0}=\mathbb{C}
and the form
\omega
is homogeneous of weight

. Examples of conic symplectic singularities include the normalization of a nilpotent orbit closure in a semi-simple Lie algebra and symplectic quotient singularities.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{min}}\subset\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n)
denote the minimal nilpotent orbit (consisting of all nilpotent rank one matrices). Recall that it is a symplectic variety. Let
(-,-)
be the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
. The tangent space to the orbit at a point

can be represented as
T_{x}\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{min}}\cong\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{x}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{x}=\{y\in\mathfrak{g}|[x,y]=0\}
. Then the Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau symplectic form is given by
\omega_{x}(\bar{y},\bar{z}):=([y,z],x)
, where
\bar{y},\bar{z}\in\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{x}
are images of
y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, respectively.

The closure
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{min}}
is normal and hence is a conic symplectic singularity; see
[
1
, (2.6)]
. A subgroup

acts on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{min}}
by conjugation. Since the Killing form is invariant under such an action, the symplectic form on the orbit is preserved as well, and hence
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{min}}/W
is again a (conic) symplectic singularity by
[

, Proposition 2.4]
.

Lemma 8.6

Consider the Hamiltonian action of the torus

The moment map

Consider also the map

In particular, the left hand side is a normal domain.

Finally, we need to check that there is at least one point

Proposition 8.8

The algebra

As noted above, the variety

This implies that

implies that

is also

∎

Theorem 8.9

Theorem 8.10
.

For Weil generic
(t,c,\zeta)
,
A_{t,c,\zeta}(W)
has finite global dimension.

Proof.

We assume
t=1
. Then the claim follows from the fact that localization holds for Weil generic parameters. We use here the notion of sheaves of Cherednik algebras on
\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h})
, as studied in
[
4
]
. By
[
4
, Lemma 5.4.1]
, the sheaf
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta,c}(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),W)
of Cherednik algebras on the projective space
\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h})
has global sections

\Gamma(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathcal{H}_{\zeta,c}(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),W))=H_{1,c}^{\mathrm{T}}/\langle\mathrm{eu}_{c}-\zeta\rangle.

This implies that
A_{1,c,\zeta}
is a direct summand of
H(\mathbb{P}):=\Gamma(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathcal{H}_{\zeta,c}(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),W))
and hence it suffices to show that
H(\mathbb{P})
has finite global dimension for Weil generic
(c,\zeta)
.

The stalks of
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta,c}(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),W)
have finite global dimension (bounded by
2\dim\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h})
). This implies that the sheaf
\mathcal{E}xt^{q}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
is zero for
q>2\dim\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h})
if
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
are coherent over
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta,c}(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),W)
. The Grothendieck spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathcal{E}xt^{q}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}))\Rightarrow\Ext_{\mathcal{H}}^{p+q}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})

converges because
H^{p}(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathcal{F})=0
for
p>2\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h})
by Grothendieck’s
[
28
, Theorem 2.7]
for any sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h})
. In particular, since the second page is bounded we deduce that
\Ext_{\mathcal{H}}^{k}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=0
for
k
sufficiently large (independent of
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
). By
[
4
, Theorem 1.2.1]
, the global sections functor
\Gamma\colon\mathcal{H}_{\zeta,c}(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),W)\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}\to H(\mathbb{P})\text{-}{\mathsf{mod}}
from the category of coherent
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta,c}(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),W)
-modules to finitely generated modules over
H(\mathbb{P})
is an equivalence (i.e. affinity holds) for Weil generic
(c,\zeta)
. If we assume affinity then

\Ext_{\mathcal{H}}^{i}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=\Ext_{H(\mathbb{P})}^{i}(\Gamma(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathcal{M}),\Gamma(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathcal{N}))

for any pair of coherent
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}),W)
-modules
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
. It follows that
\Ext_{H(\mathbb{P})}^{k}(M,N)=0
(again, independent of
M,N
) for
k
sufficiently large and all finitely generated
M,N
. This implies that
H(\mathbb{P})
has finite global dimension.

Compare with
[
8
, VI, Theorem 1.10(ii)]
, which is the proof in the case of
\mathscr{D}
-modules.
∎

Note that it is not true that

always has finite global dimension. For instance, if

8.1.
The double centralizer property

Consider the space
A_{t,c,\zeta}e
as a left
A_{t,c,\zeta}
-module and as a right

-module. The next theorem establishes the double centralizer property for the bimodule

Theorem 8.11
.

We have an isomorphism

\End_{eA_{t,c,\zeta}e}(A_{t,c,\zeta}e)\cong A_{t,c,\zeta}
.

Proof.

First, we note that
A_{0,0,0}=\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{min}}]\rtimes\overline{W}
. Since
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{min}}
is a normal variety and the group

\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{min}}
with complement of codimension at least two (Lemma
8.7
), the double centralizer property holds for

by
[
7
, Proposition E.2.2(2)]
. Therefore the proof of
[
21

shows that it holds for all parameters

∎

As for the usual rational Cherednik algebra, we say that the parameters

Corollary 8.12

The algebra

Lemma 8.15
.

Let
X
be an irreducible affine variety with symplectic singularities. Then
\mathrm{Cas}(\mathcal{O}(X))=\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists
t\in\mathrm{Cas}(\mathcal{O}(X))\setminus\mathbb{C}
. Since
\mathcal{O}(X)
is a domain, the morphism
\mathbb{C}[t]\to\mathcal{O}(X)
is an embedding and hence defines a dominant morphism
p\colon X\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
. Every non-empty fiber of
p
defines a closed Poisson subvariety of
X
. Each of these fibers is therefore a union of symplectic leaves. Since
p
is dominant there are infinitely many non-zero fibers and hence
X
contains infinitely many symplectic leaves. However, by
[
30
, Theorem 2.5]
the variety
X
is a holonomic Poisson variety, and it has only finitely many symplectic leaves by
[
30
, Proposition 3.1]
. This is a contradiction. Hence
\mathrm{Cas}(\mathcal{O}(X))=\mathbb{C}
.
∎

As a consequence of the Satake isomorphism, we deduce that:

Corollary 8.16
.

If
t\neq 0
then the centre of
A_{t,c,\zeta}

\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
8.13
, it suffices to show that the centre of

, this algebra is a filtered quantization of

. We give the centre of

a filtration by restriction. Then

is contained in

says that

\Spec Z(A_{0})\to\mathfrak{\tilde{c}}

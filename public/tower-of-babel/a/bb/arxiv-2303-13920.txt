Sharp metastability transition for two-dimensional bootstrap percolation with symmetric isotropic threshold rules
Hugo Duminil-Copin, Ivailo Hartarsky

Abstract
We study two-dimensional critical bootstrap percolation models. We establish that a class of these models including all isotropic threshold rules with a convex symmetric neighbourhood, undergoes a sharp metastability transition. This extends previous instances proved for several specific rules. The paper supersedes a draft by Alexander Holroyd and the first author from 2012. While it served a role in the subsequent development of bootstrap percolation universality, we have chosen to adopt a more contemporary viewpoint in its present form.

Introduction

A threshold bootstrap percolation model is a simple cellular automaton that provides a useful model for studying several phenomena such as metastability, dynamics of glasses or crack formation. A famous example of a threshold model is the
2
-neighbour bootstrap percolation
originally introduced by Chalupa, Leath and Reich
[
8
]
(also see
[
20
]
). In this model, sites of the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are infected or healthy. At time 0, sites are infected with probability
p
independently of each other (we denote the corresponding measure by
\mathbb{P}_{p}
). At each time step, a site becomes infected if two or more of its nearest neighbours are infected.

The first rigorous result on this model
[
22
]
, dating back to 1987, established that every site of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
becomes infected almost surely whenever
p>0
. This motivates the study of the (random) first time
\tau
at which 0 becomes infected as
p
goes to 0. In
[
1
]
, Aizenman and Lebowitz proved that there exist two constants
c,C\in(0,\infty)
such that

\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(e^{c/p}\leq\tau\leq e^{C/p}\right)=1.

We refer to this article for an enlightening exposition of the metastability effects in the model. The question of whether
c
and
C
could be chosen arbitrary close to each other was left open for a long time. Finally, a sharp metastability transition was shown to occur in
[
19
]
:
p\log\tau
converges in probability to
\pi^{2}/18
as
p\rightarrow 0
. More precise estimates for
\tau
were derived later in
[
16
]
.

Several authors investigated more general growth rules and the right order of magnitude for
\log\tau
is now known for all rules
[
5
]
, hence generalising the result of Aizenman and Lebowitz. The sharp metastability transition, though, remained available only for a handful of isolated examples
[
4
,
9
,
19
,
18
]
. The goal of this paper is to prove sharp metastability for a wide class of models. In particular, we show that every isotropic symmetric convex threshold bootstrap percolation model exhibits a sharp transition.

1.1
\mathcal{U}
-bootstrap percolation

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be the set of all 2-vectors of integers and
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\dots\}
. Elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are called

. An

is any finite non-empty subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
. An

is a finite non-empty set of update rules. An update family
\mathcal{U}
is
symmetric
, if for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
we have
-U=\{-x:x\in U\}\in\mathcal{U}
. Given an update family
\mathcal{U}
and a set
A=A_{0}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
of
initial infections
, we recursively define

A_{t+1}=A_{t}\cup\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:\exists U\in\mathcal{U},\forall u\in U,x+u\in A_{t}\}

to be the set of sites
infected at time
t
in the
\mathcal{U}
-bootstrap percolation process. The set
[A]=\bigcup_{t\geq 0}A_{t}
of eventually infected sites is called the
closure

A
. A set
A\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
is called
stable
if
[A]=A
. An observable of particular interest is the infection time of the origin

\tau=\inf\{t\in{\mathbb{N}}:0\in A_{t}\}\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}.

We will systematically be interested in the asymptotics of
\tau
when each site is initially infected independently with probability
p\rightarrow 0
. We denote the corresponding distribution of

by
{\mathbb{P}}_{p}
.

Among all update families, threshold rules initially received particular attention
[
10
]
. They are defined by a finite

\mathcal{K}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, containing
0
, and a positive integer

\theta
. Then

\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{K},\theta)=\{U\subseteq\mathcal{K}\setminus\{0\}:|U|=\theta\}

is the associated update family. In other words, a site
x
becomes infected if at least
\theta
of the sites in its neighbourhood

are already infected. A set
\mathcal{K}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is called
symmetric
if
x\in\mathcal{K}
implies
-x\in\mathcal{K}
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We say that a neighbourhood
\mathcal{K}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
is
convex symmetric
, if it is the intersection of a bounded convex symmetric subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. We say that a neighbourhood
\mathcal{K}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
is

if
\mathcal{K}\not\subset u{\mathbb{R}}
for every

. An example of a two-dimensional convex symmetric neighbourhood is given in

We will require a few definitions from the bootstrap percolation universality framework
[
6
,
5
,
10

. A

is a unit vector of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, viewed as an element of the unit circle

. We denote the open half plane with outer normal

and its boundary by

. A direction

is called

, if
{\mathbb{H}}_{u}
is stable. The direction is

otherwise, which can be reinterpreted as follows: there exists an update rule
U\subset{\mathbb{H}}_{u}
. In the case of a threshold rule, unstable directions
u
are those for which
|{\mathbb{H}}_{u}\cap\mathcal{K}|\geq\theta
.

A direction
u\in S^{1}
is called
rational
if
\lambda u\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In this case, we denote
\rho_{u}=\min\{\rho>0:\exists x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},\langle u,x\rangle=\rho\}
. Then
u{\mathbb{R}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}=(u/\rho_{u}){\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus, it will be convenient to define

, so that

. We further denote by

. Note that for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

is a translate of
l_{u}
.

For an isolated stable or an unstable direction
u\in S^{1}
, we define its

\alpha(u)=\min\{|Z|:Z\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},|[{\mathbb{H}}_{u}\cup Z]\setminus{\mathbb{H}}_{u}|=\infty\}\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(1.1)

contains a stable direction of maximal difficulty.

1.2
Main results

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any symmetric voracious isotropic update family with difficulty
\alpha
, there exists
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(|p^{\alpha}\log\tau-\lambda|>\varepsilon\right)=0.

The constant
\lambda
is identified as the solution of a variational problem, see
Definition

3.10
. Roughly speaking,
\lambda
quantifies the probability of the optimal way for a small polygonal region of infections, whose directions are dictated by the stable directions, to expand to infinity. While our definition of
\lambda
is quite implicit, we expect that the method of
[
16
]
can be used to provide numerical estimates of this constant. We note that symmetry will only be used in
Section

5
, where the lower bound on
\tau
is proved, but not for the upper one.

We further show that voracity is rather common.

Proposition 1.2
.

For every threshold rule with two-dimensional convex symmetric neighbourhood, all isolated stable directions are voracious.

This proposition provides a partial answer to Gravner and Griffeath
[
10
]
, who wrote “Such examples suggest the possibility of a general theory to the effect that voracity should be automatic for ‘nice’
\mathcal{K}
. Such a theory is far from easy to develop; at present the only result in
this direction, due to Bohman
[
3
]
and proved by a complex combinatorial argument, applies to supercritical threshold growth dynamics on box neighborhoods.” We should note that, given an arbitrary update family, using the algorithm of
[
15
]
, one can also determine whether it is voracious. However, as shown in
[
15
]
, determining the difficulty
\alpha
is NP-hard, so we expect that determining whether an update family is voracious is also NP-hard.

In addition to convex symmetric threshold rules treated by
Proposition

1.2
, to the best of our knowledge, all commonly studied isotropic update families are both symmetric and voracious—the
k
-cross model, Froböse bootstrap percolation, modified and non-modified 2-neighbour bootstrap percolation (on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
), 3-neighbour bootstrap percolation on the triangular lattice. However, for these last examples
Theorem

1.1
and more is already known
[
7
,
16
]
. Therefore, the importance of our result stems from its universality.

It is known that beyond the class of isotropic models, asymptotic behaviours that differ from the one in
Theorem

1.1
are displayed
[
5
,
2
,
6
]
. Nevertheless, the result should hold in yet greater generality, as discussed in
Section

6
. On the other hand, it should be noted that our techniques in conjunction with those of
[
17
,
14
]
should lead to sharp threshold results like
Theorem

1.1
with
\lambda
replaced by
2\lambda
for symmetric voracious isotropic kinetically constrained models.

3.1
Probabilistic tools

An event
E\subseteq\Omega=\{A:A\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\}
is
increasing
if
A\in E
and
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
imply
A^{\prime}\in E
. Two important correlation inequalities related to increasing events will be used in the article.

The first one is the
Harris inequality

[
12
]
stating that for two increasing events
E,F
,

\mathbb{P}_{p}(E\cap F)\geq\mathbb{P}_{p}(E)\mathbb{P}_{p}(F).

(3.1)

The second one is the
BK inequality

[
21
]
. For
E
and
F
two increasing events, their
disjoint occurrence

E\circ F
is defined as follows. A configuration
A\in\Omega
belongs to
E\circ F
if there exists a set
B\subseteq A
such that
B\in E
and
A\setminus B\in F
. For
k
increasing events
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
, one can define the disjoint occurrence by

E_{1}\circ\cdots\circ E_{k}=E_{1}\circ(E_{2}\circ\cdots(E_{k-1}\circ E_{k})),

that is,
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
admit disjoint witness sets. Then, for any increasing events
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
depending on a finite number of sites, the BK inequality reads

\mathbb{P}_{p}(E_{1}\circ\cdots\circ E_{k})\leq\mathbb{P}_{p}(E_{1})\cdots\mathbb{P}_{p}(E_{k}).

(3.2)

We refer the reader to the book
[
11
]
for proofs of these two classical inequalities.

(3.5)

(2)

(Behaviour near 0 and

There exist

, we are ready to prove a bound on the critical growth probability.

Proposition 4.6

(Critical growth)
.

There exists a droplet
D[Bp^{-\alpha}]\subseteq D_{p}\subseteq D[Lp^{-\alpha}]
with

\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{I}\left(D_{p}\right)\right)\geq\exp\left(-(2\lambda+C\varepsilon)/p^{\alpha}\right).

Proof.

Let
D_{n}=D[\mathbf{a}^{(n)}]
for
n\in\{0,\dots,N\}
be the droplets provided by
Lemma

4.5
. Define the rescaled droplets
(D_{n}^{Z})_{p}=D_{n}^{Zp^{-\alpha}}[\mathbf{a}^{(n)}p^{-\alpha}]
. Since
D_{0}^{Z}\subseteq D[1/B]
, we have

\mathcal{I}\left(\left(D_{N}^{Z}\right)_{p}\right)\supseteq\mathcal{I}\left(D[1/(Bp^{\alpha})]\right)\cap\bigcap_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{I}\left(\left(D^{Z}_{n}\right)_{p},\left(D^{Z}_{n+1}\right)_{p}\right).

Therefore, the Harris inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{I}\left(\left(D_{N}^{Z}\right)_{p}\right)\right)\geq{}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{I}\left(D\left[1/(Bp^{\alpha})\right]\right)\right)\prod_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{I}\left(\left(D^{Z}_{n}\right)_{p},\left(D^{Z}_{n+1}\right)_{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\varepsilon/p^{\alpha}\right)\prod_{n=0}^{N-1}p^{-C}\exp\left(-(1+\varepsilon)\frac{W_{p}((D_{n}^{Z})_{p},(D_{n+1}^{Z})_{p})}{p^{\alpha}}\right)

by
Propositions

4.2
and
4.4
. We next apply
Proposition

3.2
(3)
and
Definition

3.10
and note that
N
does not depend on
p
, to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{I}\left(\left(D_{N}^{Z}\right)_{p}\right)\right)\geq{}

\displaystyle\exp(-2\varepsilon/p^{\alpha})\prod_{n=0}^{N-1}\exp\left(-(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\frac{W(D_{n}^{Z},D_{n+1}^{Z})}{p^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{2\varepsilon+(1+\varepsilon)^{2}(2\lambda+\varepsilon)}{p^{\alpha}}\right),

further using the fourth property of the sequence
(D_{n})
in
Lemma

4.5
. The desired result follows since
C
is large and
\varepsilon
small enough.
∎

Once we are past the critical scale, growth becomes easy, as shown by the following result.

Corollary 4.7

(Supercritical growth)
.

We have

\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{I}\left(D\left[p^{-3W}\right]\right)\right)\geq\exp\left(-(2\lambda+2C\varepsilon)/p^{\alpha}\right).

Proof.

Proposition

4.6
implies that there exists a droplet
D_{p}=D[\mathbf{a}]\supseteq D(Bp^{-\alpha})
and

\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{I}(D_{p}))\geq\exp(-(2\lambda+C\varepsilon)/p^{\alpha}).

We may then proceed as in the proof of
Lemma

4.4
, growing the droplet dimensions exponentially. This leads to

{\mathbb{P}}_{p}\left(\mathcal{I}\left(D\left[p^{-3W}\right]\right)\right)\\
\geq\exp\left(-\frac{2\lambda+C\varepsilon}{p^{\alpha}}\right)p^{NWV|\mathcal{S}|}\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}\exp\left(\frac{|\mathcal{S}|(\kappa^{i+1}-\kappa^{i})CB}{p^{\alpha}}\log\left(1-e^{-\kappa^{i}B/C}\right)\right),

where
\kappa>1
is a constant close enough to 1 and we assumed for simplicity that
p^{-3W}=\kappa^{N}Bp^{-\alpha}
for some integer
N
. Taking
B
large, the above product can be made larger than
\exp[-\varepsilon/p^{\alpha}]
and we have that
N
is logarithmic in
1/p
, so the conclusion follows.
∎

Finally, we can conclude the proof of the upper bound of
Theorem

1.1
in the usual way following
[
1
]
. The idea is as follows. We consider disjoint translates of
D[p^{-3W}]
up to such a distance from the origin that it becomes likely for one to be internally filled in view of
Corollary

4.7
. Furthermore, we show that it is likely that all segments of length
p^{-3W}
in this volume contain a
W
helping set. These
W
-helping sets are sufficient to allow the droplet to grow until it reaches the origin.

Proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

Fix
\Lambda=\exp((\lambda+2C\varepsilon)/p^{\alpha})
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be the event that for all
u\in\mathcal{S}
, every translate of the rectangle
R^{u}(p^{-3W},1)
included in
D[\Lambda]
contains a
W
-helping set. The probability of this event can be bounded from below by

\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{E})\geq\left(1-\left(1-p^{W}\right)^{\lfloor p^{-3W}/W\rfloor}\right)^{|\mathcal{S}|\cdot|D[\Lambda]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}|}\rightarrow 1.

Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the event that there exists a translate of
D[p^{-3W}]
included in
D[\Lambda]
which is internally filled. Applying
Corollary

4.7
and fitting
(\Lambda p^{3W})^{2}/C
disjoint translates of
D[p^{-3W}]
into
D[\Lambda]
, one obtains

\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{F})\geq 1-\left(1-\exp[-(2\lambda+2C\varepsilon)/p^{\alpha}]\right)^{(\Lambda p^{3W})^{2}/C}\rightarrow 1.

Moreover, the simultaneous occurrence of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
implies that
p^{\alpha}\log\tau\leq\lambda+3C\varepsilon
for
p
small enough. Indeed, each site in the internally filled translate of
D[p^{-3W}]
granted by
\mathcal{F}
becomes occupied in time at most
|D[p^{-3W}]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}|
, since at least one new site becomes infected at each step. After the creation of this supercritical droplet, it only takes a time of order
p^{-3W}\Lambda
to progress and reach 0, thanks to the event
\mathcal{E}
. More precisely, growing one of the radii of our droplet by 1 only requires a time of order
p^{-3W}
regardless of its size, since each
W
-helping set grows linearly along its edge (recall
Definition

3.3
). The Harris inequality yields

\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(p^{\alpha}\log T\leq\lambda+3C\varepsilon\right)\geq\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{F})\geq\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{E})\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow 1

which concludes the proof of
Theorem

4.1
, since
C\varepsilon\ll 1
.
∎

5
Proof of the lower bound

We next turn to the lower bound.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a symmetric isotropic update family of difficulty
\alpha
. Then, recalling
\lambda
from
Definition

3.10
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}{\mathbb{P}}_{p}\left(p^{\alpha}\log\tau>\lambda-\varepsilon\right)=1.

Our aim is to control all possible ways of creating large droplets. For the rest of
Section

5
, we fix
\mathcal{U}
as in
Theorem

5.1
.

5.1
Upper bound on the probability of growth

Since the process is not obliged to form droplets, but could instead use more complicated shapes, we will need some further notions to suitably reduce them to droplets.

Definition 5.2

(
\Delta
-connected)
.

Given
\Delta>0
, we say that a set
X\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
is
\Delta
-connected
if it is connected in the graph
\Gamma=({\mathbb{Z}}^{2},\{\{x,y\}:\|x-y\|\leq\Delta\}\})
.

It is known that there exists a constant
K=K(\mathcal{U})>0
such that for all stable directions
u
and all sets
S\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
such that
S\not\in\mathcal{H}^{u}
and
|S|\leq\alpha(u)
, we have

\max\left\{d(x,S):x\in[S\cup{\mathbb{H}}_{u}]\setminus{\mathbb{H}}_{u}\right\}<K/3

(5.1)

(see
[
15
]
for an explicit bound on
K
). In particular, applying this to both
u
and
-u
, we see that for any
S\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
such that
|S|<\alpha(u)
we have

\max\{d(x,S):x\in[S]\}<K/3.

(5.2)

We further assume
K
large enough so that for any stable
u
and any
S\in\mathcal{H}^{u}
we have
\diam(S)<K/3
and
\max\{\|x\|:x\in\bigcup_{U\in\mathcal{U}}U\}<K/3
.

Definition 5.3

(Spanning)
.

For two
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
-droplets
D_{1}\subseteq D_{2}
, let
\mathcal{E}(D_{1},D_{2})
be the event that there exists a
K
-connected set
X\subseteq[(A\cap D_{2})\cup D_{1}]
such that every
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
-droplet containing
X
also contains
D_{2}
.

We further write
\mathcal{E}(D)=\mathcal{E}(\varnothing,D)
for any
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
-droplet
D
and say that
D
is
spanned
when
\mathcal{E}(D)
occurs.

Spanning events
\mathcal{E}
will play a similar role to the filling events
\mathcal{I}
used for the upper bound in
Section

4
, so our first step is again to link them to the function
W
from
Definition

3.10
. The next proposition makes use of our symmetry assumption.

Proposition 5.4
.

For any
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
-droplets
D_{1}\subseteq D_{2}
satisfying
\Phi(D_{2})\leq CBp^{-\alpha}
and
\Psi(D_{1},D_{2})\leq Tp^{-\alpha}
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{E}(D_{1},D_{2})\right)\leq C\exp\left(-(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\frac{W_{p}(D_{1}^{Zp^{-\alpha}},D_{2}^{Zp^{-\alpha}})}{p^{\alpha}}\right).

(5.3)

The idea behind the proof is as follows, roughly following
[
19
]
. Our goal is to bound the probability of
\mathcal{E}(D[\mathbf{a}],D[\mathbf{b}])
for
\mathbf{b}\geq\mathbf{a}
very close. Thinking of
D[\mathbf{a}]
with not very small dimensions, we can cut
D[\mathbf{b}]\setminus D[\mathbf{a}]
into rectangles like the shaded ones in
Fig.

4
and some remaining small leftover region. We will treat the leftover region as ‘boundary condition’, since it could, in principle, help for growing the small droplet in multiple directions simultaneously. Yet, it is small, so it is unlikely to find a lot of infections there. Once the boundary condition is fixed, the rectangles become independent and each of them needs to be ‘crossed’ separately. Before turning to the proof of
Eq.

5.3
, let us first discuss a lemma, which deals with crossing one of these trapezoids.

For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the
strip

S^{u}(n)=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:0\leq\langle x,u\rangle<n\rho_{u}\}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{n-1}l^{u}(i).

Also, consider the
crossing
events

\mathcal{C}^{u}(m,n,E)\\
=\left\{l^{u}(0)\text{ and }l^{u}(n)\text{ $K$-connected in }[(A\cap R^{u}(m,n))\cup(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus S^{u}(n))\cup E]\right\},

(5.4)

where
E\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus R^{u}(m,n)
is viewed as a “boundary condition”. For such a set
E
, define
J_{E}
to be the number of
j\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
such that
l_{u}(j)
is at distance at most
3K
from a
3K
-connected set of cardinality
\alpha(u)
in
E
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
u
be a stable direction. For
m\in[Tp^{-\alpha(u)},CBp^{-\alpha(u)}]
,
n\geq 1/T
and
E\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus R^{u}(m,n)
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{C}^{u}(m,n,E)\right)\leq\exp\left(-(1-\varepsilon)h_{p}^{u}\left(p^{\alpha(u)}m\right)\left(n-LJ_{E}\right)\right).

Proof.

We prove the result by slicing the rectangle
R^{u}(m,n)
into rectangles of fixed (but large) height
k=L/3
, which we assume divisible by
1/\rho_{u}^{2}
.

We start by crudely bounding the probability of an ‘error’ event. Let
\mathcal{E}^{u}(m,k)
be the event that
A\cap R^{u}(m,k)
contains a
3K
-connected set of size
\alpha(u)+1
or there is a site
a\in A\cap R^{u}(m,k)
such that
\langle a,u^{\perp}\rangle\in[0,3K)\cup[m-3K,m)
. In words,
\mathcal{E}^{u}(m,k)
occurs if there is an unexpectedly large cluster of infections or there is an infection close to the boundary of
R^{u}(m,k)
.

Claim 5.6
.

In this setting,
{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(\mathcal{E}^{u}(m,k))\leq\exp(-h^{u}_{p}(p^{\alpha(u)}m)k)
.

Proof.

The number of
3K
-connected sets of size
\alpha(u)+1
in
R^{u}(m,k)
is bounded by
Mkm
for some constant
M=M(K)>0
. Similarly,

\left|\left\{a\in R^{u}(m,k):\langle a,u^{\perp}\rangle\in[0,3K)\cup[m-3K,m),\langle a,u\rangle\in[0,\rho_{u}k)\right\}\right|\leq 6Kk.

Therefore, since
mp^{\alpha(u)}\leq CB
, a union bound gives

\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{E}^{u}(m,k)\right)\leq Mkmp^{\alpha(u)+1}+6Kkp\leq k(MCB+6K)p.

Further recalling the uniform bound from
Proposition

3.2
(2)
and taking
p
small enough, this gives

{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(\mathcal{E}^{u}(m,k))\leq\exp\left(-h^{u}_{p}(T)k\right)\leq\exp\left(-h^{u}_{p}\left(p^{\alpha(u)}m\right)k\right),

also taking into account that
h_{p}^{u}
is non-increasing and
T\leq p^{\alpha(u)}m
.
∎

With
5.6
at hand, we next prove
Lemma

5.5
in a specific case.

Claim 5.7
.

For any
E
with
J_{E}=0
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{p}\left(\mathcal{C}^{u}(m,k,E)\right)\leq\exp\left(-(1-2\varepsilon/3)h_{p}^{u}\left(p^{\alpha(u)}m\right)k\right).

(5.5)

Proof.

Note that
k>3K(\alpha(u)+1)
. Let us assume in the following that
\mathcal{E}^{u}(m,k)
does not occur. Then,
A^{\prime}=(A\cap R^{u}(m,k))\cup E
consists of
3K
-connected components of size at most
\alpha(u)
contained entirely in
R^{u}(m,k)
and
3K
-connected components of size at most
\alpha(u)-1
contained entirely in
E
(since
J_{E}=0
and
\mathcal{E}^{u}(m,k)
does not occur).

Consider a
3K
-connected component
\mathcal{K}
of
A^{\prime}
. If
\mathcal{K}\subseteq E
, then
|\mathcal{K}|\leq\alpha(u)-1
and
d(\mathcal{K},A^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{K})>3K
. The component
\mathcal{K}
cannot be simultaneously close to
{\mathbb{H}}_{u}
and
{\mathbb{H}}^{-u}(1-k)
. We claim that for any
x\in[\mathcal{K}\cup({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus S^{u}(k)]\cap S^{u}(k)
we have
d(x,\mathcal{K})<K/3
. Indeed, depending on whether
\mathcal{K}
is close to one boundary of
S^{u}(k)
, the other boundary, or neither boundary, this follows from either
Eq.

5.1
or
Eq.

5.2
.

Let us make the further assumption that neither
l^{u}(0)
nor
l^{-u}(-k+1)
is occupied (recall
Definition

3.1
), using the notation
l^{-u}(i)
in order to specify that the line must be occupied in direction
-u
. Then, the same reasoning as above applies to any
3K
-connected component
\mathcal{K}\subseteq A^{\prime}\cap R^{u}(m,n)
as well, using
Eq.

5.1
. But then

\left[A^{\prime}\cup({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus S^{u}(k)\right]\cap S^{u}(k)=\bigsqcup_{\mathcal{K}}\left([\mathcal{K}\cup S^{u}(k)]\cap S^{u}(k)\right),

since the sets in the disjoint union are at distance at least
3K-2K/3
apart and therefore do not interact. Thus, each
K
-connected component of
[A^{\prime}\cup({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus S^{u}(k))]\setminus S^{u}(k)
is generated by a single
3K
-connected component of
A^{\prime}
, so it cannot
K
-connect
l^{u}(0)
to
l^{u}(k)
. In conclusion, if
\mathcal{E}^{u}(m,k)
does not occur,
l^{u}(0)
or
l^{-u}(-k+1)
must be occupied in order for
\mathcal{C}^{u}(m,k,E)
to occur.

Depending on whether
l^{u}(0)
or
l^{-u}(-k+1)
is occupied, one can repeat the above argument inductively for the strip
S^{u}(k)\setminus l^{u}(0)
or
S^{u}(k)\setminus l^{-u}(-k+1)
. This procedure continues as long as the remaining strip is sufficiently thick. Namely, we deduce that for
N=3K(\alpha(u)+1)
, if
\mathcal{C}^{u}(m,k,E)\setminus\mathcal{E}^{u}(m,k)
occurs, then there exists
k^{\prime}
between 0 and
k
such that
l^{u}(0),\dots,l^{u}(k^{\prime}-1)
and
l^{-u}(-k^{\prime}+N),\dots,l^{-u}(-k+1)
are occupied in
A^{\prime}
.

Set
\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{u}(m,k))=1
for
k<0
. By the above, since lines at a distance greater than
K
are independently occupied, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{C}^{u}(m,k,E)\right)

\displaystyle{}\leq\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{E}^{u}(m,k)\right)+\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k}\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{A}^{u}(m,k^{\prime})\right)\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{A}^{-u}(m,k-k^{\prime}-N)\right)

\displaystyle\leq(k+2)\exp\left(-h^{u}_{p}\left(p^{\alpha(u)}m\right)(k-N)\right),

(5.6)

by
5.6
, symmetry and
Proposition

3.2
(1)
for the second inequality. Recalling that
mp^{\alpha(u)}\leq CB
,
3k=L\gg B,1/\varepsilon,C,K
and
Proposition

3.2
, we get

k+2\leq\exp\left(h^{u}_{p}(CB)(k-N)\varepsilon/3\right)\leq\exp\left(h^{u}_{p}\left(p^{\alpha(u)}m\right)(k-N)\varepsilon/3\right).

Combining this with
Eq.

5.6
and
k-N\geq(1-\varepsilon/3)k
, we deduce

\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{C}^{u}(m,k,E)\right)\leq\exp\left(-(1-2\varepsilon/3)h^{u}_{p}\left(p^{\alpha(u)}m\right)k\right).\qed

We can now conclude the proof of
Lemma

5.5
. Divide the rectangle
R^{u}(m,n)
into
\left\lfloor n/k\right\rfloor
translates of
R^{u}(m,k)
and a remainder of height at most
k
. Then, at least
\left\lfloor n/k\right\rfloor-2J_{E}
of these translated rectangles satisfy the condition of
5.7
. We thus obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{C}^{u}(m,n,E)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{P}_{p}\left(\mathcal{C}^{u}(m,k,E)\right)\right)^{\left\lfloor n/k\right\rfloor-2J_{E}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-(1-2\varepsilon/3)h^{u}_{p}\left(p^{\alpha(u)}m\right)\left(k\lfloor n/k\rfloor-2kJ_{E}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-(1-\varepsilon)h^{u}_{p}\left(p^{\alpha(u)}m\right)\left(n-3kJ_{E}\right)\right)

for
n\geq 1/T
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to prove
Proposition

5.4
.

Proof of
Proposition

5.4
.

Consider two droplets
D_{1}=D[\textbf{a}]\subseteq D_{2}=D[\textbf{b}]
with
\Phi(D_{2})\leq CBp^{-\alpha}
and
\Psi(D_{1},D_{2})\leq Tp^{-\alpha}
. Let
\mathbf{m}
be the dimension of
D_{1}
and
\mathbf{s}
be the location of
D_{1}\subseteq D_{2}
. For each
u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
we define
R^{u}
as in the proof of
Proposition

4.2
, namely, let
R^{u}
be the translate of the largest rectangle
R^{u}(\tilde{m}_{u},s_{u})
such that
R^{u}\subseteq D[\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}_{u}s_{u}]\setminus D[\mathbf{a}]
(recall
Figs.

4
,
4.3
and
4.2
). Let
x_{u}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
be such that
R^{u}=x_{u}+R^{u}(\tilde{m}_{u},s_{u})
. We set
\bar{R}^{u}=R^{u}
if
\tilde{m}_{u}\geq Tp^{-\alpha}
and
\bar{R}^{u}=\varnothing
otherwise and let
\bar{m}_{u}=\tilde{m}_{u}
if
\tilde{m}_{u}\geq Tp^{-\alpha}
and
\bar{m}_{u}=0
otherwise. Let

X=D_{2}\setminus\Big(D_{1}\cup\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}}\bar{R}^{u}\Big)

be the
leftover
region (in
Fig.

4
this corresponds to the larger droplet without the smaller one and those among the shaded rectangles which are not too thin). The leftover may have a rather complicated shape, but is, crucially, small.

Conditioning on
A\cap X
and recalling
Definitions

5.3
and
5.4
, we get

{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(\mathcal{E}(D_{1},D_{2}))\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{p}\Big[\prod_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}}{\mathbb{P}}_{p}\left((A-x_{u})\in\mathcal{C}^{u}(\bar{m}_{u},s_{u},(A\cap X)-x_{u})|A\cap X\right)\Big].

In words, each rectangle
\bar{R}^{u}
is crossed with boundary condition given by the infections in the leftover region. Note that this probability is simply an indicator function for
u
such that
\tilde{m}_{u}<Tp^{-\alpha}
, since it is measurable with respect to the conditioning.

Following
Lemma

5.5
, for each
u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
let
J^{u}_{A\cap X}
be the number of
j\in\{0,\dots,s_{u}-1\}
such that
l_{u}(j)+x_{u}
is at a distance at most
3K
from a
3K
-connected set of cardinality
\alpha
in
A\cap X
. Then,
Lemma

5.5
gives

{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(\mathcal{E}(D_{1},D_{2}))\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{p}\Big[\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\\
\bar{m}_{u}=0\end{subarray}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{J_{A\cap X}^{u}=s_{u}}\exp\Big(-(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\\
\bar{m}_{u}\neq 0\end{subarray}}h^{u}_{p}\left(p^{\alpha}\bar{m}_{u}\right)\left(s_{u}-LJ^{u}_{A\cap X}\right)\Big)\Big],

(5.7)

which becomes an expectation just over the
(J^{u}_{A\cap X})_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}}
.

We argue that for each
u
either
J^{u}_{A\cap X}
is small enough not to perturb
s_{u}
much or it is large, which is unlikely by itself. Indeed, denoting by
\mathbf{m}^{Z}
the dimension of
D_{1}^{Zp^{-\alpha}}
, we can bound
Eq.

5.7
from above by

\sum_{S\subset\{u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}:\bar{m}_{u}\neq 0\}}{\mathbb{P}}_{p}\left(\forall u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\setminus S,J^{u}_{A\cap X}>\varepsilon s_{u}/L\right)\exp\Big(-(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\sum_{u\in S}h^{u}_{p}\left(p^{\alpha}m^{Z}_{u}\right)s_{u}\Big),

noting that
m^{Z}_{u}\geq m_{u}\geq\bar{m}_{u}
for all
u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
. Thus, it only remains to prove that for any
S\subset\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
such that
S\supset\{u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}:\bar{m}_{u}=0\}
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}_{p}\left(\forall u\in S,J^{u}_{A\cap X}>\varepsilon s_{u}/L\right)\leq\exp\Big(-\sum_{u\in S}h_{p}^{u}\left(m_{u}^{Z}p^{\alpha}\right)s_{u}\Big).

Fix
u\in S
such that
s_{u}
is maximal. Since
u\in S
, there exist at least
\varepsilon s_{u}/(CKL)
disjoint
3K
-connected sets of
\alpha
infections in
X\setminus{\mathbb{H}}_{u}(a_{u})
. But by construction
|X|\leq Cs_{u}Tp^{-\alpha}
, so the union bound gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{p}\left(J^{u}_{A\cap X}>\varepsilon s_{u}/L\right)

\displaystyle{}\leq p^{\alpha\varepsilon s_{u}/(CKL)}\binom{K^{C}s_{u}Tp^{-\alpha}}{\varepsilon s_{u}/(CKL)}

\displaystyle\leq p^{\alpha\varepsilon s_{u}/(CKL)}\left(\frac{eK^{C}s_{u}Tp^{-\alpha}}{\varepsilon s_{u}/(CKL)}\right)^{\varepsilon s_{u}/(CKL)}

\displaystyle{}\leq\left(K^{2C}LT/\varepsilon\right)^{\varepsilon s_{u}/(CKL)}\leq\exp(-Ls_{u})

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Big(-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\setminus V}h^{v}_{p}\left(m^{Z}_{v}p^{\alpha}\right)s_{v}\Big),

since

is chosen small enough depending on
\varepsilon,C,K,Z,L
and
h^{v}_{p}(m^{Z}_{v}p^{\alpha})\leq h^{v}_{p}(Z)<L/|\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}|
, since

is chosen large enough depending on

.
∎

5.4
Truncating
\lambda_{\alpha}

In order to relate the bound from

5.18

from

Lemma 5.19

(Extension at
+\infty
)
.

For any
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
-droplet
D
with
\Phi(D)\geq G
, there exists a sequence of
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
-droplets
D=D_{0}\subseteq D_{1}\subseteq\dots
such that
\bigcup_{i\geq 0}D_{i}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}W(D_{i},D_{i+1})\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof.

After translating, we may assume that for some sufficiently large
k
depending on
\varepsilon
we have that
D\subseteq D[2^{k}]
, but
D
is not contained in any translate of
D[2^{k-1}]
. As we saw in
Eq.

3.16

large we can ensure that
\sum_{i\geq k}W(D[2^{i}],D[2^{i+1}])\leq\varepsilon/2
. Therefore it suffices to find

such that

\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}W(D_{i},D_{i+1})\leq\varepsilon/2
.

In order to achieve this, we proceed similarly to the proof of
Lemma

4.5
. Set
D=D[\mathbf{a}^{(0)}]
and
D[2^{k}]=D[\mathbf{a}^{(\infty)}]
. We define
\mathbf{a}^{(i)}
by induction as follows, set
D_{i}=D[\mathbf{a}^{(i)}]
and denote by
\mathbf{m}^{(i)}
the dimension of

. Further let

be such that
m^{(i)}_{u_{i}}=\max\{m^{(i)}_{u}:u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha},a^{(i)}_{u}\neq a^{(\infty)}_{u}\}
. As long as

(at which point the construction is done), we set

\mathbf{a}^{(i+1)}=\mathbf{a}^{(i)}+\mathbf{e}_{u_{i}}\min\left(T,a^{(\infty)}_{u_{i}}-a^{(i)}_{u_{i}}\right).

This procedure clearly yields
D_{N}=D[2^{k}]
for some finite

. Further observe that
m^{(i)}_{u_{i}}\geq 2^{k}/C
for all

and

large enough. That is, the largest edge that has not yet reached its final position is always big. Indeed, every two edges of

that have reached the final value for their radius are necessarily far apart, so there has to be a large side between them. Using this property, we have that

\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}W(D_{i},D_{i+1})\leq\sum_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}}h^{u}\left(2^{k}/C\right)a^{(\infty)}_{u}\leq\varepsilon/2

.

If
\Pod(\mathcal{H})\geq 2\lambda/(Lp^{\alpha})
, we are done by
Corollary

5.15
. We therefore assume that
\Pod(\mathcal{H})\leq 2\lambda/(Lp^{\alpha})
.

Proposition

5.18
yields the existence of a sequence
D_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq D_{N}
with
\Phi(D_{1})<1/(Bp^{\alpha})
and
\Phi(D_{N})\geq Gp^{-\alpha}
satisfying

\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}))\leq C^{N}\exp\left(-(1-\varepsilon)^{2}p^{-\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}W_{p}\left(D_{n}^{Zp^{-\alpha}},D_{n+1}^{Zp^{-\alpha}}\right)\right).

However, by
Lemmas

5.20
and
3.12
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}W_{p}\left(D_{n}^{Zp^{-\alpha}},D_{n+1}^{Zp^{-\alpha}}\right)\geq 2\lambda-2\varepsilon.

(5.9)

Thus,

\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H}))\leq C^{N}\exp\left(-(1-2\varepsilon)\left(2\lambda-2\varepsilon\right)/p^{\alpha}\right).

Since
N
and
C
do not depend on
p
, this concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.22

(Aizenman–Lebowitz lemma)
.

Let
D
be a
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
-droplet and
1/\varepsilon\leq k\leq\Phi(D)
. If
\mathcal{E}(D)
occurs, then there exists a
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
-droplet

with

such that
\mathcal{E}(D^{\prime})
occurs.

Proof of
Theorem

5.1
.

Let
\Lambda=\exp(\lambda-C\varepsilon)/p^{\alpha}
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be the event that
0\in[A\cap[-\Lambda,\Lambda]^{2}]
. We claim that
{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow 0
as
p\rightarrow 0
. Indeed, if
\mathcal{E}
occurs, then the origin belongs to a spanned
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
-droplet
D
with
1\leq\Phi(D)\leq C\Lambda
. As in

5.12
, there are at most
(C\Phi(D))^{|\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}|}
possible choices for this
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
-droplet, given its perimeter.

Case 1.
Assume that
\Phi(D)\leq\log(1/p)
. Then
{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(\mathcal{E}(D))\leq p|D\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}|
, so

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\ni 0,\\
\Phi(D)\leq\log(1/p)\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(\mathcal{E}(D))\leq p(\log(1/p))^{C}.

(5.10)

Case 2.
Assume that
\log(1/p)\leq\Phi(D)\leq CB/p^{\alpha}
. Then by
Lemma

5.14
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\ni 0,\\
\log(1/p)\leq\Phi(D)\leq CB/p^{\alpha}\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(\mathcal{E}(D))\leq C\sum_{\phi\geq\log(1/p)}\phi^{C}e^{-\phi/L}\leq 1/\log(1/p).

(5.11)

Case 3.
Assume that
CB/p^{\alpha}\leq\Phi(D)\leq C\Lambda

5.22
, if

occurs, we can find a
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}

such that
\mathcal{E}(D^{\prime})
occurs. Thus, a union bound on

gives

{\mathbb{P}}_{p}\Big(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\ni 0,\\

(5.12)

5.21

6
Future directions

In conclusion, let us comment on possible generalisations of
Theorem

1.1
. For the purposes of this discussion, let us recall a few notions from
[
5
]
. A two-dimensional update family
\mathcal{U}
is
critical
, if there exists a semi-circle of
S^{1}
containing a finite number of stable directions, but every open semi-circle contains at least one stable direction. The
difficulty
of a critical update family is given by

\alpha=\min_{u\in S^{1}}\max_{v\in S^{1}:\langle u,v\rangle>0}\alpha(v),

where
\alpha(v)
is given by
Eq.

1.1
, if
v
is isolated stable or unstable, and
\alpha(v)=\infty
otherwise (this definition coincides with
Eq.

1.2
for isotropic models). A critical update family of difficulty
\alpha
is
unbalanced
, if there exist two opposite directions
v,-v\in S^{1}
such that
\min(\alpha(v),\alpha(-v))>\alpha
, and
balanced
otherwise.

Firstly, we expect that an adapted version of
Theorem

1.1
holds for all critical models.

Conjecture 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a critical two-dimensional update family
\mathcal{U}
. Then there exists a constant
\lambda=\lambda(\mathcal{U})
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}{\mathbb{P}}_{p}\left(\left|\frac{p^{\alpha}\log\tau}{(\log(1/p))^{\gamma}}-\lambda\right|>\varepsilon\right)=0,

where
\gamma=0
, if
\mathcal{U}
is balanced, and
\gamma=2
, if
\mathcal{U}
is unbalanced.

However, in general we do not expect the constant
\lambda
in
6.1
to be given by
Definition

3.10
. We do believe that
6.1
holds with
\lambda
from
Definition

3.10
for all isotropic voracious update families. That is, we expect that
Theorem

5.1
holds without the symmetry assumption. A natural subsequent step would be to extend our methods to (non-symmetric) balanced voracious models.

On the other hand, for non-voracious models,
Theorem

4.1
does not apply and
Theorem

5.1
is not sharp. To provide an example of this, consider the update rules in
Fig.

6
and the update families given by
\mathcal{U}_{1}=\{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3},U_{4}\}
,
\mathcal{U}_{2}=\mathcal{U}_{1}\cup\{U_{5}\}
,
\mathcal{U}_{3}=\mathcal{U}_{1}\cup\{U_{6}\}
. Viewed as models on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, all of three families are isotropic non-voracious and have difficulty
1
. However,
\mathcal{U}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}
may be viewed as models on the even sublattice of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, in which case they become voracious. We may then apply
Theorem

1.1
and, inspecting the proof (also see
[
19
]
), shows that
6.1
holds with
\lambda=\pi^{2}/6
for both
\mathcal{U}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}
. Moreover, it is not hard to see that
\tau
for
\mathcal{U}_{3}
is stochastically bounded from above and from below by
\tau
for
\mathcal{U}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}
respectively. Therefore,
6.1
holds for all three models with
\lambda=\pi^{2}/6
. Yet,
\mathcal{U}_{3}
cannot be viewed as acting only on the even sublattice. For each of the three models, viewed as acting on all of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, the constant
\lambda
in
Definition

3.10
is rather given by
\pi^{2}/9
. This can be seen along the lines of
[
19
]
, since helping sets are single sites on the first or second line perpendicular to the stable direction
u=(\pm 1/\sqrt{2},\pm 1/\sqrt{2})
.

The example above is only the first symptom of the problems arising in the absence of voracity. Indeed, sublattices may have rather complex interactions and, in some cases, it may be more efficient to grow only periodically filled droplets. Furthermore, whether droplets prefer to be completely or partially filled may also depend on the direction. In view of the above, we expect voracity to be the most challenging hypothesis to remove.

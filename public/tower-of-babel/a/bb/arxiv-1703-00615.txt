Characterization of Traps at Nitrided SiO 2 /SiC Interfaces near the Conduction Band Edge by using Hall Effect Measurements
Tetsuo Hatakeyama, Yuji Kiuchi, Mitsuru Sometan, Shinsuke Harada, Dai Okamoto, Hiroshi Yano, Yoshiyuki Yonezawa, Hajime Okumura

Abstract
The effects of nitridation on the density of traps at SiO 2 /SiC interfaces near the conduction band edge were qualitatively examined by a simple, newly developed characterization method that utilizes Hall effect measurements and split capacitance-voltage measurements. The results showed a significant reduction in the density of interface traps near the conduction band edge by nitridation, as well as the high density of interface traps that was not eliminated by nitridation.



In recent years, silicon carbide (SiC) metal-oxide semiconductor field-effect transistors (MOSFETs) have been commercialized as the next-generation, high-voltage, low-loss power devices

However, even in the case of state-of-the-art SiC MOSFETs, a high MOS channel resistance caused by low mobility in the SiO

Therefore, the improvement of mobility in SiO

) near

,
which will provide us with clues to further our understanding as to what the interface traps are and how to reduce them.
However, it has been difficult to quantitatively examine the effect of

on the mobility of the fabricated SiC MOSFETs.
This is partly because a well-known Hi-Lo capacitance-voltage (

) near

On the other hand, to expand the limit of the energy range of

The core idea of the new characterization method of
D_{\mathrm{it}}(E)
near
E_{\mathrm{C}}
is to focus on the quantitative characterization of the densities of free (mobile) and trapped carriers at SiO
2
/SiC interfaces

;
saks_dit
;

;
takagi2014
;
tsujimura
;
hatakeyama2015
;
shiomimob
. The physics governing the trapping of electrons at the interface is included in these two characterized quantities. However, to extract the quantitative information on
D_{\mathrm{it}}(E)
from them, a careful examination of the relationship between the applied gate voltage (
V_{\mathrm{g}}
) and the energy of the traps at the interface (
E_{\mathrm{t}}
) is required

;
takagissdm
. In the new characterization method, the errors in the relationship between the
V_{\mathrm{g}}
and the
E_{\mathrm{t}}
are minimized by the use of the analytic formulae derived from the exact solution of the Poisson equation in a MOS structure
nicollian_ns
.

Now, the new characterization method of
D_{\mathrm{it}}(E)
near
E_{\mathrm{C}}
is described step by step. In the first step, the free carrier density (
n_{\mathrm{free}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})
) is characterized as a function of
V_{\mathrm{g}}
, by the use of Hall effect measurements for a SiC MOSFET. In the second step, the capacitance between the gate and the channel (
C_{\mathrm{gc}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})
) of a SiC MOSFET is characterized as a function of a
V_{\mathrm{g}}
, by the use of a split
C
-
V
technique. In the third step, the total density of carriers at the interface, induced by the applied
V_{\mathrm{g}}
(
n_{\mathrm{total}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})
), is obtained by integrating the
C_{\mathrm{gc}}(V)
over the scanned voltages (
V
) as follows:

n_{\mathrm{total}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})=\int^{V_{\mathrm{g}}}_{V_{\mathrm{min}}}C_{\mathrm{gc}}(V)dV,

(1)

where
V_{\mathrm{min}}
is the minimum value of the scanned gate voltage, which is set to be less than the threshold voltage. Finally, the trapped carrier density (
n_{\mathrm{trap}}(V_{\mathrm{g}}))
is obtained by subtracting
n_{\mathrm{free}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})
from
n_{\mathrm{total}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})
, as follows:

n_{\mathrm{trap}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})=n_{\mathrm{total}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})-n_{\mathrm{free}}(V_{\mathrm{g}}).

(2)

From these obtained functions (
n_{\mathrm{trap}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})
and
n_{\mathrm{free}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})
),
D_{\mathrm{it}}(E)
near
E_{\mathrm{C}}
is calculated according to the following formulae.

The density of the trapped electrons (
n_{\mathrm{trap}}
) as a function of the Fermi level at the interface (
E_{\mathrm{Fs}}
) is given by the product of the density of interface traps
D_{\mathrm{it}}(E)
and the Fermi–Dirac distribution function, as follows:

n_{\mathrm{trap}}(E_{\mathrm{Fs}})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}D_{\mathrm{it}}(E)\frac{dE}{1+g\exp\left[\beta(E-E_{\mathrm{Fs}})\right]}

(3)

where
\beta
and
g
are the thermodynamic beta factor and the degeneracy factor.
In this study,
g
is assumed to be 2.
When we examine
D_{\mathrm{it}}(E)
and
E_{\mathrm{Fs}}
at the interface,
the origin of the energy is implicitly set to the intrinsic Fermi energy of the SiC at the interface.
In this notation, the
E_{\mathrm{Fs}}
changes as
V_{\mathrm{g}}
changes. In the standard energy diagram of a MOS interface,
E_{\mathrm{Fs}}
is essentially the same as the surface potential at the interface
\phi_{\mathrm{s}}
, as follows:

E_{\mathrm{Fs}}=e\phi_{\mathrm{s}}.

(4)

The derivative of Eq.
3
gives the following relationship by approximating the derivative of the Fermi–Dirac distribution function to the Dirac delta function, as follows:

\frac{dn_{\mathrm{trap}}}{dE_{\mathrm{Fs}}}\approx D_{\mathrm{it}}\left(E_{\mathrm{Fs}}-\beta^{-1}\ln g\right),

(5)

where the shift in energy in the argument of
D_{\mathrm{it}}(E)
comes from the degeneracy factor
g
. Thus,
D_{\mathrm{it}}(E)
is given by the derivative of
n_{\mathrm{trap}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})
with respect to the
E_{\mathrm{Fs}}
. The relationship between the trap energy (
E_{\mathrm{t}}
) (which is the argument of
D_{\mathrm{it}}(E)
) and
E_{\mathrm{Fs}}
, is given by the following formula:

E_{\mathrm{t}}=E_{\mathrm{Fs}}-\beta^{-1}\ln g.

(6)

It should be noted that the measured trapped carrier density is obtained as a function of
V_{\mathrm{g}}
, and not
E_{\mathrm{Fs}}
. Thereby, a parametric differentiation is introduced as follows:

\frac{dn_{\mathrm{trap}}}{dE_{\mathrm{Fs}}}=\frac{\cfrac{dn_{\mathrm{trap}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})}{dV_{\mathrm{g}}}}{\cfrac{dE_{\mathrm{Fs}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})}{dV_{\mathrm{g}}}}.

(7)

The numerator of this equation was obtained by the numerical derivative of
n_{\mathrm{trap}}(V_{\mathrm{g}})
.

-

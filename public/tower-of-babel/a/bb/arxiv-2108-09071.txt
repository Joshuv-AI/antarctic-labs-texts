CP-violating Axions
*

Abstract
While the axion was originally introduced to “wash out” CP violation from strong interactions, new sources of CP violation beyond QCD might manifest themselves via a tiny scalar axion-nucleon component. The latter can be experimentally probed in axion-mediated force experiments, as suggested long ago by J.E. Moody and F. Wilczek. In the present note, I review the physical origin of CP-violating axion couplings and point out the special role of the QCD axion as a low-energy portal to high-energy sources of CP violation.

\frac{a}{f_{a}}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{8\pi}G\tilde{G}\,,

\theta_{\rm eff}\equiv\frac{\left\langle a\right\rangle}{f_{a}}\,,

(2)

resulting

\displaystyle e^{-V_{4}{\mathscr{E}}(\theta_{\rm eff})}

3
CP-violating axion-nucleon couplings

A remarkable consequence of
\theta_{\rm eff}\neq 0
is the generation of a
scalar

axion coupling to nucleons,
g^{S}_{aN}a\overline{N}N
(with
N=p,n
),
which can be searched for in axion-mediated force experiments
as suggested by Moody and Wilczek
[
17
]
.
The phenomenological relevance of the latter will be discussed in Sect.
5
.
To understand the origin of
g^{S}_{aN}
, let us define the canonical axion field as the
excitation over its VEV

a\to\theta_{\rm eff}f_{a}+a
and consider the two-flavour QCD-axion Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{a}=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{8\pi}\left(\theta_{\rm eff}+\frac{a}{f_{a}}\right)G\tilde{G}-\overline{q}_{L}M_{q}q_{R}+\text{h.c.}\,,

(15)

where
M_{q}=\mbox{diag}\,(m_{u},m_{d})
.
Upon the axial quark rotation

q\to e^{\frac{i}{2}\gamma_{5}\left(\theta_{\rm eff}+\frac{a}{f_{a}}\right)Q_{a}}q\,,

(16)

with
Q_{a}=M_{q}^{-1}/\mbox{Tr}\,M_{q}^{-1}=\mbox{diag}\,(\frac{m_{d}}{m_{u}+m_{d}},\frac{m_{u}}{m_{u}+m_{d}})
,
the
G\tilde{G}
term gets rotated away in Eq. (
15
), while the
quark mass matrix becomes

M_{q}\to M_{a}=e^{i\gamma_{5}\left(\theta_{\rm eff}+\frac{a}{f_{a}}\right)Q_{a}}M_{q}\,,

(17)

where we used
[M_{q},Q_{a}]=0
. Hence, in the new basis we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}

\displaystyle=-\overline{q}\left[\frac{M_{a}+M^{\dagger}_{a}}{2}+\gamma_{5}\frac{M_{a}-M^{\dagger}_{a}}{2}\right]q

\displaystyle=-\overline{q}\cos\left(\left(\theta_{\rm eff}+\frac{a}{f_{a}}\right)Q_{a}\right)M_{q}q-\overline{q}i\gamma_{5}\sin\left(\left(\theta_{\rm eff}+\frac{a}{f_{a}}\right)Q_{a}\right)M_{q}q\,.

(18)

Focussing on the scalar component (i.e. the one without
\gamma_{5}
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}

\displaystyle\supset-\overline{q}\cos\left(\theta_{\rm eff}Q_{a}\right)\cos\left(\frac{a}{f_{a}}Q_{a}\right)M_{q}q+\overline{q}\sin\left(\theta_{\rm eff}Q_{a}\right)\sin\left(\frac{a}{f_{a}}Q_{a}\right)M_{q}q

\displaystyle\simeq\theta_{\rm eff}\frac{a}{f_{a}}\overline{q}\,Q_{a}^{2}M_{q}q=\frac{\theta_{\rm eff}}{f_{a}}\frac{m_{u}m_{d}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}a\left(\frac{m_{d}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}\overline{u}u+\frac{m_{u}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}\overline{d}d\right)\,,

(19)

where in the second step we kept only a term linear in
a/f_{a}
and expanded for small
\theta_{\rm eff}
,
while in the last step we employed the explicit representation of
Q_{a}
below Eq. (
16
).
Hence, the scalar axion coupling to nucleons is

\displaystyle g^{S}_{aN}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta_{\rm eff}}{f_{a}}\frac{m_{u}m_{d}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}\left(\frac{m_{d}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}\langle N|\overline{u}u|N\rangle+\frac{m_{u}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}\langle N|\overline{d}d|N\rangle\right)

(20)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta_{\rm eff}}{f_{a}}\frac{m_{u}m_{d}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}\left[\frac{\langle N|\overline{u}u+\overline{d}d|N\rangle}{2}+\left(\frac{m_{d}-m_{u}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}\right)\frac{\langle N|\overline{u}u-\overline{d}d|N\rangle}{2}\right]\,,

where in the last step we isolated the iso-spin singlet component,

\overline{u}u+\overline{d}d
.
Focussing on the leading iso-spin singlet term (as in
[
17
]
), one has

g^{S}_{aN}\simeq\frac{\theta_{\rm eff}}{f_{a}}\frac{m_{u}m_{d}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}\frac{\langle N|\overline{u}u+\overline{d}d|N\rangle}{2}\simeq 1.3\cdot 10^{-12}\,\theta_{\rm eff}\left(\frac{10^{10}\ \text{GeV}}{f_{a}}\right)\,.

(21)

For the numerical evaluation we employed the
value
of the
pion-nucleon sigma term

\sigma_{\pi N}=\langle N|\overline{u}u+\overline{d}d|N\rangle(m_{u}+m_{d})/2=59.1\pm 3.5

[
18

As pointed out in Ref.

]

a factor

Axion-mediated forces

Including both scalar and pseudo-scalar couplings to matter fields,
the axion interaction Lagrangian can be written as

6

More general
CP-violating axion-like particle interactions have been
recently analyzed in
[
26
]
.

\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{a}=g^{S}_{aN}a\overline{N}N+g^{P}_{af}a\overline{f}i\gamma_{5}f
(here
f=N,e
),
where
g^{P}_{af}=C_{f}m_{f}/f_{a}
is the usual pseudo-scalar axion coupling,
with
C_{f}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
in benchmark axion models
[
27
]
.
By taking the non-relativistic limit of
\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{a}
one obtains different kinds of static potentials,
which can manifest themselves as new axion-mediated macroscopic forces
[
17
]
.
The latter can be tested in laboratory experiments, and hence do not rely on model-dependent axion production mechanisms,
as in the case of dark matter axions (haloscopes) or to a less extent solar axions (helioscopes).
An updated review of axion-mediated force experiments and relevant limits can be found in
Ref.
[
28
]
(see also
[
29

30

31

Axion-induced potentials can be of three types,
depending on the combination of couplings involved,
i.e.
g^{S}_{aN}g^{S}_{af}
(monopole-monopole),

g^{S}_{aN}g^{P}_{af}
(monopole-dipole)
and
g^{P}_{af}g^{P}_{af}
(dipole-dipole).
The idea of searching for dipole-dipole axion interactions
in atomic physics
is as old as the axion itself
[
3
]
.
However, dipole-dipole forces turn out to be
spin suppressed in the non-relativistic limit
and suffer from large backgrounds from ordinary magnetic forces.
Searches based on monopole-monopole interactions,
like tests of gravity on macroscopic scales, are in principle much more powerful.
However, under the theoretical prejudice that we are after the QCD axion,
the
\theta_{\rm eff}^{2}
suppression (given the nEDM bound) is such that
current experiments are still some orders of magnitude far from testing the QCD axion.

6
Outlook

In order to have a testable signal in monopole-dipole axion searches
a sizeable sources of CP violation beyond the SM is required.
As a reference value in terms of
\theta_{\rm eff}

(using Eq. (
21
) for
g^{S}_{aN}
)
one would need

\theta_{\rm eff}\gtrsim 10^{-13}
, that is
three orders of magnitude below the
current nEDM bound.
Since CP is not a symmetry of nature, there is no
reason to expect
\theta_{\rm eff}\to 0
.
The SM itself predicts
\theta_{\rm eff}\sim 10^{-19}
,
that is
far from being testable.
However,
new sources of CP violation
beyond the CKM phase
are needed to explain the
matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe
and, even if
decoupled
at
scales
as heavy as
100 TeV,
they might contribute sizeably to
\theta_{\rm eff}

(see estimate in Eq. (
12
)).

If an axion relaxation mechanism is at play,
then
axion-mediated forces provide an alternative experimental handle
for probing UV sources of CP violation in the quark sector,
with projected sensitivities that are stronger than current EDM searches.
8
8

8

Future improvements
on EDM limits
might as well play a crucial role for disentangling
different sources of CP violation coupled to QCD,
also in the presence of a light axion field
[
36
]
.

This suggests to rethink the role of the QCD axion
from a “laundry detergent” of CP violation in the strong interactions
to
a low-energy portal to high-energy sources of CP violation,
thus turning the
strong CP problem into the strong CP opportunity.

On the connected components of the moduli space of equivariant minimal surfaces in ℂ ​ ℍ 2 \mathbb{CH}^{2} .
Ian McIntosh

Abstract
. An equivariant minimal surface in ℂ ​ ℍ n \mathbb{CH}^{n} is a minimal map of the Poincaré disc into ℂ ​ ℍ n \mathbb{CH}^{n} which intertwines two actions of the fundamental group of a closed surface Σ \Sigma : a Fuchsian representation on the disc and an irreducible action by isometries on ℂ ​ ℍ n \mathbb{CH}^{n} . The moduli space of these can been studied by relating it to the nilpotent cone in each moduli space of P ​ U ​ ( n , 1 ) PU(n,1) -Higgs bundles over the conformal surface corresponding to the map. By providing a necessary condition for points on this nilpotent cone to be smooth this article shows that away from the points corresponding to branched minimal immersions or ± \pm -holomorphic immersions the moduli space is smooth. The argument is easily adapted to show that for ℝ ​ ℍ n \mathbb{RH}^{n} the full space of (unbranched) immersions is smooth. For ℂ ​ ℍ 2 \mathbb{CH}^{2} we show the connected components of the moduli space of minimal immersions are indexed by the Toledo invariant and the Euler number of the normal bundle of the immersion. This is achieved by studying the limit points of the ℂ × \mathbb{C}^{\times} -action on the nilpotent cone. It is shown that the limit points as t → 0 t\to 0 lead only to branched minimal immersions or ± \pm -holomorphic immersions. In particular, the Euler number of the normal bundle can only jump by passing through branched minimal maps.

1.
Introduction.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed oriented surface of genus
g\geq 2
and let
N
be a noncompact irreducible symmetric space. Denote
by
G
the identity component of the isometry group of
N
.
By an equivariant minimal surface we mean a minimal immersion
f:\mathcal{D}\to N
of the Poincaré disc
\mathcal{D}

which intertwines the action of a Fuchsian representation
c:\pi_{1}\Sigma\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{D})
with an irreducible
representation
\rho:\pi_{1}\Sigma\to G
. By a theorem of Corlette
[
4
]
(generalising a theorem of Donaldson
for
N=\mathbb{RH}^{3}

[
5
]
), the triple
(f,c,\rho)
is uniquely
determined by the pair
(c,\rho)
, and if one considers that
f
is essentially unchanged by pre-composition or post-composition
by isometries, then one only needs the conjugacy classes of
c
and
\rho
. Hence there is a natural way to assign a
topology to the set
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,N)
of equivalence classes
[f,c,\rho]
of equivariant minimal surfaces by embedding it
in
\mathcal{T}_{g}\times\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1}\Sigma,G)
where
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is the Teichmúller space of
\Sigma
and
\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1}\Sigma,G)
is
the character variety of
G
., i.e., the moduli space of reductive representations of
\pi_{1}\Sigma
into
G
up to conjugacy.

This topology for
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,N)
was first proposed by Loftin and the author in
[
12
]
but prior to this, in
[
11
]
,
we had studied the set
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})
and provided parametrisations for certain subsets which we called
“components”, simply to mean “parts whose union is the whole”. We showed that each parametrisation equipped
the component with
the structure of a connected smooth complex manifold, most of which had the same dimension. These parametrisations exploited the nonabelian
Hodge correspondence, through which one can identify an equivariant minimal surface with a
PU(2,1)
-Higgs bundle having
certain properties. However, since in
[
11
]
we did not equip the total space
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})

with a topology a priori, the question of its connected components had no framework in which to be addressed. One of the aims of this
article is to answer this question now, given the topology described above (in a nutshell, the components identified in

[
11
]
are, with a few exceptions, the connected components). A key to understanding the connected components
lies in having a criterion for points of the moduli space to be smooth, and this is provided below for
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,N)

when
N
is either
\mathbb{CH}^{n}
or
\mathbb{RH}^{n}
. The second aim of this article is to show how these components fit together inside
the larger set
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})
obtained by including all branched minimal surfaces. We show that this is not smooth, since
singularities occur where the closures of the connected components of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})
meet. Note that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})

was the space studied in
[
11
]
, and the distinctive role of branched immersions explained here was not at all apparent
during the writing of
[
11
]
.

To summarise the results below, first recall that a
PU(n,1)
-Higgs bundle can be represented by a pair

(E,\Phi)
where
E\to\Sigma_{c}
is a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
n+1
over the Riemann surface
\Sigma_{c}\simeq\mathcal{D}/c
.
This has a decomposition
E=V\oplus 1
,
where
V
is rank
n
and
1
denotes the trivial line bundle. Then
\Phi
is a holomorphic one-form with values in

\operatorname{Hom}(1,V)\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(V,1)
. We write
\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
to denote the two components.
In general
(E,\Phi)
corresponds to an equivariant,
but possibly branched, minimal surface when
\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{2})=0
. Zeroes of
\Phi
are branch points of the minimal surface
and therefore we require
\Phi
to have no zeroes (i.e.,
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}
have no common zeroes) to obtain a point in

\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{n})
.
From
[
11
]
we know that one of
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}
is identically zero precisely when the minimal immersion
is either holomorphic or anti-holomorphic. It is convenient to write
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{n})
as a disjoint union
\mathcal{V}\cup\mathcal{W}
where

\mathcal{W}
is the subvariety of
\pm
-holomorphic maps and
\mathcal{V}
is the complement.
Our first main result concerns
\mathcal{V}
, and in fact applies also to
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{RH}^{n})\subset\mathcal{V}
using the totally geodesic
embedding of
\mathbb{RH}^{n}
into
\mathbb{CH}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Both
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{n})
and
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{RH}^{n})
are smooth manifolds. They have complex dimension

(g-1)\dim(G)
for
G=PU(n,1)
and
SO_{0}(n,1)
respectively.

In fact what we prove is that in the nilpotent cone
\mathcal{N}^{c}
(i.e, the locus of
\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{2})=0
in the Higgs bundle moduli space

\mathcal{H}(\Sigma_{c},G)
for
G=PU(n,1)
or
SO_{o}(n,1)
) the regular points of the Hitchin function
\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{2})
are precisely
the points for which neither
\Phi_{j}
has a zero.

Next we restrict our attention to
n=2
. For
G=PU(2,1)
the connected components of
\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1}\Sigma,G)
are
indexed by the Toledo invariant,
\tau
. Using Toledo’s convention, as in
[
11
]
, this satisfies
\tau\in\tfrac{2}{3}\mathbb{Z}
and

|\tau|\leq 2(g-1)
. We denote the subspace of triples
[f,c,\rho]
for which
\rho
has Toledo invariant
\tau
by
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})_{\tau}

and it is clear that these are disconnected from each other. Minimal immersions which are not

\pm
-holomorphic have complex and anti-complex points (points
p\in\Sigma
for which
f_{*}T_{p}^{1,0}\Sigma
is a
subspace of either
T^{\prime}\mathbb{CH}^{2}
or
T^{\prime\prime}\mathbb{CH}^{2}
). These give effective divisors,
D_{2}
and
D_{1}

respectively. From
[
11
]
their degrees
d_{j}=\deg(D_{j})
satisfy

\displaystyle\tau=\tfrac{2}{3}(d_{2}-d_{1}),

\displaystyle\chi(T\Sigma^{\perp})=2(g-1)-d_{1}-d_{2},

(1.1)

\displaystyle 0\leq 2d_{1}+d_{2}<6(g-1),

\displaystyle 0\leq d_{1}+2d_{2}<6(g-1),

(1.2)

where
T\Sigma^{\perp}
is the normal bundle of
f
, which means the quotient of
T\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\subset f^{-1}T\mathbb{CH}^{2}
by the natural action of
\pi_{1}\Sigma
. The inequalities are necessary and sufficient conditions for the
existence of minimal immersions which are not
\pm
-holomorphic. In particular, the degrees
d_{1},d_{2}
are equivalent information to the
pair
\tau
and
\chi(T\Sigma^{\perp})
. For holomorphic immersions the only topological invariant is the Toledo invariant, which
determines
\chi(T\Sigma^{\perp})
through

\chi(T\Sigma^{\perp})=-\tfrac{3}{2}\tau+2(g-1).

(1.3)

This follows directly by taking the quotient of the direct sum of holomorphic bundles
f^{-1}T^{\prime}\mathbb{CH}^{2}=T^{1,0}\mathcal{D}\oplus T\mathcal{D}^{\perp}

by the
\pi_{1}\Sigma
-action.

Let
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})\subset\mathcal{V}
denote the subset of those immersions whose
divisors have these degrees fixed. It was shown in
[
11
]
that each
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
can be parametrised by
a smooth connected complex manifold of dimension
8(g-1)

(actually in
[
11
]
we included the branched minimal immersions, which occur when
D_{1}\cap D_{2}\neq\emptyset
,
but as these are described by a complex analytic subvariety
removing these does not affect the connectedness of
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
).
In Lemma
5.1
below we show that the parametrisation in
[
11
]
is smoothly compatible with the structure
it inherits from Theorem
1.1
. Since
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})

all have the same dimension they are therefore the connected components of
\mathcal{V}
. To establish the connected components
of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})
it remains to see how these fit together with points of
\mathcal{W}
. Set
\mathcal{W}_{\tau}=\mathcal{W}\cap\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})_{\tau}
for each
\tau
. We need only consider
\tau\geq 0
since

\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})
carries a natural real involution
[f,c,\rho]\mapsto[\bar{f},c,\bar{\rho}]
for which

\tau(\bar{\rho})=-\tau(\rho)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})_{\tau}
denote the space of equivariant minimal immersions with Toledo invariant
\tau
. Then
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})_{0}
has
connected components
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
for
d_{2}=d_{1}
. For
0<\tau<2(g-1)
the connected components of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})_{\tau}
are

\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
with
d_{1}\neq 0
and
\mathcal{V}(0,d_{2})\cup\mathcal{W}_{\tau}
, while
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})_{\tau}=\mathcal{W}_{\tau}
for
\tau=2(g-1)
.

In particular, the connected components are indexed by
\tau
and
\chi(T\Sigma^{\perp})
: when
d_{1}=0
the two equations
(
1.1
) become the single equation (
1.3
).

This theorem is proved through a complete description of the limit points of the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action
t\cdot(E,\Phi)=(E,t\Phi)

on
PU(2,1)
-Higgs bundles, for both
t\to\infty
and
t\to 0
. As well as proving
the theorem, it gives us information about how the closures of the connected components fit together inside the space

\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})
which includes all branched minimal immersions. On the Higgs bundle side this corresponds to the space
of all
(c,E,\Phi)
for which
(E,\Phi)
lies in the nilpotent cone
\mathcal{N}^{c}

excluding the locus
\Phi=0
(since this corresponds to constant maps).
Each
\mathcal{N}^{c}
is known to be stratified by the unstable manifolds of the downwards Morse flow for the Higgs
field energy
\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
: for each connected component
C
of critical points these agree with

\mathcal{U}_{C}=\{(E,\Phi):\lim_{t\to\infty}(E,t\Phi)\in C\}.

It was conjectured in
[
11
]
, and is proven below, that except when
C
consists of local minima (there is
precisely one such
C
for each
\tau
and it equals
\mathcal{W}_{\tau}
) these unstable manifolds
correspond to the fibres of the analytic family
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
over
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. We understand how these fit
together by examining the limit in the opposite direction, as

t\to 0
. It is well-known that when
E
is semi-stable (as a vector bundle) the limit
\lim_{t\to 0}(E,t\Phi)
must have

\Phi=0
and therefore this can only occur when
\tau=0
. When
E
is strictly unstable (and assuming
\tau\geq 0
without loss of
generality) we show that either
(E,\Phi_{1})
is a Hodge bundle, in which case this is limit, or the limit is determined by
the maximal destabilizing line subbundle of
V
and always corresponds to a branched minimal immersion.

Thus we have the the following geometric picture of how these pieces fit together. Each fibre over
c
of the space

\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})_{\tau}
is a union of these unstable
manifolds, and their closures intersect either at branched minimal surfaces or at
\pm
-holomorphic surfaces. Removing
these leaves a disjoint union of components
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
, one for each critical manifold of non-minima. In particular, one cannot
pass from one component
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
to another in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})
without forcing the minimal immersion to branch somewhere.
Another way of saying this is that
\chi(T\Sigma^{\perp})
can jump but only by passing through branched maps.

2.
Definitions.

Let
\mathbb{C}^{n,1}
denote
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
equipped with the pseudo-Hermitian inner product characterised by

\langle u,u\rangle=|u_{1}|^{2}+\ldots|u_{n}|^{2}-|u_{n+1}|^{2}.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n+1}
denote the standard basis vectors for
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.
We model
\mathbb{CH}^{n}
on the space of lines in
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
generated by a negative definite vector and fix a base
point
o=[e_{n+1}]
.
Throughout this article we set
G=PU(n,1)
, the group of orientation preserving isometries of
\mathbb{CH}^{n}
, and let
H\simeq U(n)

denote the maximal compact subgroup which fixes the base point
o
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{su}(n,1)
of
G
has Cartan decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
corresponding to the decomposition of elements

\begin{pmatrix}A&u\\
u^{\dagger}&a\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\
u^{\dagger}&0\end{pmatrix}

(2.1)

where
A\in\mathfrak{u}(n)
,
a=-\operatorname{tr}(A)
,
u\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
u^{\dagger}
is the Hermitian transpose.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed oriented surface of genus
g\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{D}
denote the Poincaré disc thought of
as the universal cover of
\Sigma
(note that
\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathbb{CH}^{1}
). A Fuchsian representation
c:\pi_{1}\Sigma\to PU(1,1)
equips
\Sigma
with
a complex structure which will be denoted
\Sigma_{c}
. We will assume this complex structure is compatible with the
orientation of
\Sigma
and hence the set conjugacy classes of such Fuchsian representations can be identified with the Teichmüller space

\mathcal{T}_{g}
of
\Sigma
. An equivariant minimal surface in
\mathbb{CH}^{n}
is the equivalence class

[f,c,\rho]
of a triple consisting of a Fuchsian representation
c
, an indecomposable representation

\rho:\pi_{1}\Sigma\to G
and a minimal immersion
f:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{CH}^{n}
which intertwines the actions of
c
and
\rho
, i.e.,

f\circ c(\delta)=\rho(\delta)\circ f,\quad\forall\delta\in\pi_{1}\mathcal{D}.

Equivalence is with respect to the action of
PU(1,1)\times G
by conjugation on
(c,\rho)
and the corresponding natural
action on
f
. Indecomposability of
\rho
means exactly that its image does not lie in a proper subgroup of
G
. By the
same argument as
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
it is equivalent to the condition that
f
is linearly full (i.e., its image
does not lie in a totally geodesic copy of
\mathbb{CH}^{k}
for
k<n
).

Remark 2.1
.

We could also say
\rho
is irreducible in its standard real representation on
\mathbb{C}^{n,1}
, although there is the potential
for confusion with other meanings of irreducibility (say, for the adjoint representation of the complexification
G^{\mathbb{C}}
).

We use
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{n})
to denote the set of equivariant minimal surfaces and give it the topology
of its natural embedding

\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{n})\to\mathcal{T}_{g}\times\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1}\Sigma,G);\quad[f,c,\rho]\to[c,\rho],

(2.2)

into the product of Teichmüller space with the character variety
\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1}\Sigma,G)
of reductive
representations of
\pi_{1}\Sigma
into
G
modulo conjugacy. This map is injective by Corlette’s uniqueness theorem
for equivariant harmonic maps under these conditions
[
4
]
. We will denote by
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{c},\mathbb{CH}^{n})
the fibre
over fixed
c\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
.

Recall that, for each choice of
c
, non-abelian Hodge theory provides a homeomorphism between
\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1}\Sigma,G)
and the
moduli space
\mathcal{H}(\Sigma_{c},G)
of polystable
G
-Higgs bundles over
\Sigma_{c}
(and this homeomorphism is analytic
away from singularities). Indeed, for any simple noncompact
G
there is a homeomorphism between
\mathcal{T}_{g}\times\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1}\Sigma,G)

and the universal Higgs bundle moduli space
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{C}_{g},G)
over
\mathcal{T}_{g}

[
1
, Thm 7.5]
(here
\mathcal{C}_{g}
denotes the universal Teichmüller curve over
\mathcal{T}_{g}
).
This is a complex analytic space for which
the fibre over
c\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
is biholomorphic to
\mathcal{H}(\Sigma_{c},G)
. This homeomorphism is smooth about smooth
points and therefore we obtain an embedding

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{C}_{g},G),

(2.3)

when
G=PU(n,1)
. This equips
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{n})
with a complex analytic structure.

We can easily characterise the image of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
Recall that in the projective equivalence class of the
G
-Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
,

E
can be taken to have the form
V\oplus 1
where
V
is
a holomorphic rank
n
bundle over
\Sigma_{c}
and
1
denotes the trivial line bundle. The Higgs field
\Phi
satisfies

\Phi\in(\operatorname{Hom}(1,V)\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(V,1))\otimes K,

and we will write
\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
to indicate the two components for this decomposition. It is well-known
that the harmonic map corresponding to
(E,\Phi)
is weakly conformal (therefore branched minimal) when
\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{2})=0
.
The branch points correspond exactly to the zeroes of
\Phi
. Consequently we have the following elementary observation.

Lemma 2.1
.

The pair
(E,\Phi)
lies in the image of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
if and only if it is stable (hence indecomposable) with
\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{2})=0
and

\Phi
having no zeroes.

Each equivariant minimal surface
(f,c,\rho)
has associated to it
the Toledo invariant
\tau
of
\rho
and we denote the subset of triples with fixed
\tau
by
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{n})_{\tau}
.
Our convention will be that
\tau=-\frac{2}{n+1}\deg(V)
so that
|\tau|\leq 2(g-1)
: this matches Toledo’s original
definition and is the one used in
[
11
]
.

We will also write
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{n})
as a disjoint union
\mathcal{V}\cup\mathcal{W}
where
\mathcal{W}
consists of all
\pm
-holomorphic
immersions and
\mathcal{V}
is the complement. Each of these has its subsets
\mathcal{V}_{\tau}
,
\mathcal{W}_{\tau}
containing those elements
with fixed Toledo invariant. We note that under the non-abelian Hodge correspondence elements of
\mathcal{W}
correspond to
Higgs bundles with
\Phi_{2}=0
for
\tau>0
or
\Phi_{1}=0
for
\tau<0
. Since we always have
\Phi\neq 0
we know

\mathcal{W}_{0}=\emptyset
. Note that
\mathcal{W}_{\tau}
corresponds to the length two Hodge bundles
[
11
]
, which are the minima
of the Higgs field energy
\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

[
3
]
.

From
[
11
]
we know that when
n=2
the space
\mathcal{V}
is a disjoint union of subsets
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
, corresponding
to
PU(2,1)
-Higgs bundles for which
V
and
\Phi
are determined by an exact sequence
[
11
]

0\to K^{-1}(D_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{1}}}{{\to}}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{2}}}{{\to}}K(-D_{2})\to 0,

(2.4)

where
d_{j}=\deg(D_{j})
satisfy the inequalities (
1.2
). There is a one-to-one correspondence between Higgs bundles of this
type and data
(c,D_{1},D_{2},\xi)
where
\xi
is the extension class of this extension: this was used in
[
11
]
to
give each
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
the structure of a complex manifold of dimension
5(g-1)
.

Finally, we will want to consider the strictly larger space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})
which includes additionally the
branched minimal immersions. Under
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
it maps to the locus
\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{2})=0
excluding the Higgs bundles with
\Phi=0
.
We will write
\bar{\mathcal{V}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{W}}
when we extend
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{W}
to
include branched minimal immersions of the respective types. The total space is disconnected into Toledo invariant
pieces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})_{\tau}
and for
\tau\neq 0
each of these is connected. This follows from the fact that for

\tau\neq 0
each fibre over
c\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
agrees with the component
\mathcal{N}^{c}_{\tau}
of the nilpotent cone in Toledo invariant

\tau
, and these are connected by the downwards Morse flow. For
\tau=0
this fibre is
\mathcal{N}^{c}_{0}
without the locus
\Phi=0
. Since
this locus is the critical manifold of minima we can no longer use the Morse flow argument, and an understanding of
the connected components would require a different approach.

3.
Smooth points of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,N)
.

For
N=\mathbb{CH}^{n}
or
\mathbb{RH}^{n}
let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,N)
denote the subvariety of points where neither
\Phi_{j}
is
identically zero. For
\mathbb{RH}^{n}
this is the whole space while for
\mathbb{CH}^{n}
it consists of
the equivariant minimal surfaces which are not
\pm
-holomorphic.
Because we assume
\rho
is indecomposable the there are no singular points of
\mathcal{T}_{g}\times\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1},G)
in
\mathcal{V}
.

Recall that
\mathcal{C}_{g}
denotes the universal Teichmüller curve over
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. If we denote by
H^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{g},K^{2})
the
bundle over
\mathcal{T}_{g}
with fibre
H^{0}(\Sigma_{c},K^{2})
then the map

\mathcal{I}:\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{C}_{g},G)\to H^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{g},K^{2});\quad(c,E,\Phi)\mapsto(c,\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{2})),

is complex analytic.
We will denote its zero locus (i.e., the preimage of the zero section of
H^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{g},K^{2})
) by
\mathcal{N}
: for real rank one groups
like
PU(n,1)
and
SO_{0}(n,1)
this is the union

4.
Limit points of the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action for

It is well-known that

acts on

-Higgs bundles by

Lemma 4.1

For

Now to show this gives the limit we use the same argument as part (iii) but with

g_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}t^{2}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&t\end{pmatrix}.

In this case

g_{t}^{-1}\bar{\partial}_{E}g_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\partial}_{V/L}&0&0\\
t^{2}\beta&\bar{\partial}_{L}&0\\
0&0&\bar{\partial}\end{pmatrix},\quad g_{t}^{-1}(t\Phi)g_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\phi_{13}\\
0&0&t^{2}\phi_{23}\\
t^{2}\phi_{31}&\phi_{32}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Setting
t=0
gives (
4.6
) with
\Phi^{0}_{1}=\varphi_{13}
, the projection of
\Phi_{1}
onto
(V/L)\otimes K
,
and
\Phi^{0}_{2}=\varphi_{32}
, the restriction of
\Phi_{2}
to
L
. The latter has zeroes
D_{2}+D
by definition, and the
former has zeroes
D_{1}+D
for the same reason as in part (iii).
∎

Note that this argument works perfectly well even when
D_{1},D_{2}
already have points in common
(although
D
will not be the full branch point divisor in that case), and
it tells us something about singularities of the nilpotent cone. When
C
consists of length three Hodge bundles

\mathcal{U}_{C}
is smooth and of dimension
5(g-1)
: this follows either from the Morse index
calculation of Gothen
[
6
, Prop 3.2]
or the dimension count in
[
11
]
for
\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{c}(d_{1},d_{2})
, given Corollary

4.2
. Any point on
C
which is a limit point as
t\to 0
(i.e., from some
\mathcal{U}_{C^{\prime}}
for
C^{\prime}\neq C
) lies on both the stable
and unstable manifolds of
C
, which are transverse, and therefore the tangent space at this point has dimension greater than
\mathcal{U}_{C}
.
Thus such limit points are singular points of the nilpotent cone.
We can ask whether every length three Hodge bundle of the form (
4.6
) (i.e., with common divisor
D
) is
such a singular point. The answer is no: by the following result there are many such Hodge bundles which are not limit
points.
Recall from
[
11
]
that the necessary and sufficient conditions for (
4.6
) to give a stable Higgs bundle are
the inequalities

0\leq 2d_{1}^{\prime}+d_{2}^{\prime}<6(g-1),\quad 0\leq d_{1}^{\prime}+2d_{2}^{\prime}<6(g-1),

(4.8)

where
d_{j}^{\prime}=\deg(D_{j}+D)
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
D_{1},D_{2},D
be effective divisors,
D>0
, set
D_{j}^{\prime}=D_{j}+D
and suppose their degrees
d_{1}^{\prime},d_{2}^{\prime}
satisfy

d_{1}^{\prime}\leq d_{2}^{\prime}
and
d_{1}^{\prime}+d_{2}^{\prime}<2(g-1)
.
Then the Hodge bundle with
V
given by (
4.6
) does not lie at the limit as
t\to 0
of a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-orbit
in a different unstable manifold.

Proof.

First, the two inequalities imply that (
4.6
) corresponds to a stable Higgs bundle with
\tau\geq 0
. By Prop.
4.3
such a limit can only be obtained from case (iv), where
V
has unique maximal destabilizing
line subbundle
L\simeq K(-D_{2}^{\prime})
and
V/L\simeq K^{-1}(D_{1}^{\prime})
. The existence of this requires

1\leq\dim H^{1}(K^{2}(-D_{1}^{\prime}-D_{2}^{\prime}))=\dim H^{0}(K^{-1}(D_{1}^{\prime}+D_{2}^{\prime})),

using Serre duality.
But the right hand dimension is zero whenever
\deg(K^{-1}(D_{1}^{\prime}+D_{2}^{\prime}))<0
, i.e., when
d_{1}^{\prime}+d_{2}^{\prime}<2(g-1)
.
∎

5.
Connected components of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})
.

Our aim is to prove Theorem
1.2
. First we must recall some more facts from
[
11
]
.
From
[

]

\mathcal{V}
is a disjoint union
\cup_{(d_{1},d_{2})}\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
where the non-negative integers

satisfy the
inequalities (

are parametrised by the data
(c,D_{1},D_{2},\xi)
where
D_{1},D_{2}
are
effective divisors of degree

. The fibre over

, which we will denote by

\{(D_{1},D_{2})\in S^{d_{1}}\Sigma_{c}\times S^{d_{2}}\Sigma_{c}:D_{1}\cap D_{2}=\emptyset\}

We need to show first that this parametrisation is smooth with respect to the smooth structure of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})

given above, which uses its embedding in
\mathcal{T}_{g}\times\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1}\Sigma,G)
. Since we are always dealing
with points which are smooth in the latter we can use the nonabelian Hodge correspondence to identify the smooth locus
of
\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1},G)
with the smooth locus of Higgs bundle moduli space
\mathcal{H}(\Sigma_{c},G)
. It therefore suffices to prove the following
lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

For each fixed
c\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
we get a holomorphic embedding

\mathcal{Z}:\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{c}(d_{1},d_{2})\to\mathcal{H}(\Sigma_{c},G),

by assigning to
(D_{1},D_{2},\xi)
the Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
for which
E=V\oplus 1
where
V
and
\Phi
arise from the
extension
\xi
using (
2.4
).

We know from
[
11
]
that
\mathcal{Z}
is one-to-one, so it suffices to show that it is a holomorphic immersion.

Before we begin the proof we need to fix a convention for which way to represent
1
-cocycles with values in a vector bundle. For a
vector bundle
F
with model fibre
A
and local transition relations
\psi_{i}=a_{ij}\psi_{j}
between local trivialisations

\psi_{i}
over a Leray cover
\{U_{i}\}
, let
(\xi_{ij},U_{i},U_{j})
be a
1
-cocycle for a class in
H^{1}(F)
, i.e.,

\xi_{ij}\in\Gamma(U_{i}\cap U_{j},F)
satisfy
\xi_{ij}+\xi_{jk}=\xi_{ik}
. Then we choose to represent this by the local functions

c_{ij}=\psi_{i}(\xi_{ij}):U_{i}\cap U_{j}\to A,

and the
1
-cocycle conditions are equivalent to

c_{ij}+a_{ij}c_{jk}=c_{ik}.

Note that the opposite convention, to use
\psi_{j}(\xi_{ij})
instead, is used in
Gunning
[
7
]
. In what follows we need this for
H^{1}(\mathcal{A}^{0})
and
H^{1}(K^{-2}(D_{1}+D_{2}))
.

Proof.

To describe the map
\mathcal{Z}
concretely about a point
(D_{1},D_{2},\xi)
, let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}
be the points in the support of

D_{1}+D_{2}
and let
U_{0},\ldots,U_{d}
be the Leray cover of
\Sigma_{c}
for which
each
U_{j}
for
j\geq 1
is an open disc about
P_{j}
, with
U_{j}\cap U_{k}=\emptyset
for
j\neq k
, and
U_{0}\cap U_{j}

is an annulus excluding an open disc about
P_{j}
. Since
V
is determined by the extension class
\xi
,

V
has
1
-cocycle
g=\{(g_{0j},U_{0},U_{j}):1\leq j\leq d\}

given by

g_{0j}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0j}&\lambda_{0j}\\
0&\beta_{0j}\end{pmatrix}

(5.1)

where

\alpha_{0j}=z^{-n_{j}}\frac{dz_{0}}{dz_{j}},\quad\beta_{0j}=z_{j}^{m_{j}}\frac{dz_{j}}{dz_{0}},\quad\lambda_{0j}=\beta_{0j}\xi_{0j}dz_{0}^{2}.

Here, for
j\geq 1
,
z_{j}
is a local parameter in
U_{j}
centred at
P_{j}
and
n_{j}
is the degree of
D_{1}
at
P_{j}

while
m_{j}
is the degree of
D_{2}
at
P_{j}
. By
dz_{0}
we simply mean a non-vanishing holomorphic
1
-form on
U_{0}
.
By
\xi_{0j}
we mean the local section of
K^{-2}(D_{1}+D_{2})
over
U_{0}\cap U_{j}
which comprises a
1
-cocycle representing
the extension class
\xi
.

The
1
-cocycle
g
determines a trivialisation
\chi_{j}
of
V
, and dual
\chi^{*}_{j}
of
V^{*}
, over
U_{j}
.
When we think of the Higgs field components as
\Phi_{1}\in H^{0}(V\otimes K)
and
\Phi_{2}\in H^{0}(V^{*}\otimes K)
these
satisfy

\chi_{0}(\Phi_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}dz_{0}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\chi^{*}_{0}(\Phi_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}0&dz_{0}\end{pmatrix},

and for
j\geq 1

\chi_{j}(\Phi_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}z_{j}^{n_{j}}dz_{j}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\chi^{*}_{j}(\Phi_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}0&z_{j}^{m_{j}}dz_{j}\end{pmatrix}.

(5.2)

An open neighbourhood around
D_{1}\in S^{d_{1}}\Sigma_{c}
(respectively
D_{2}\in S^{d_{2}}\Sigma_{c}
) is determined
by collection of monic polynomials
z_{j}^{n_{j}}+u_{j}(z_{j})
(respectively
z_{j}^{m_{j}}+v_{j}(z_{j})
) whose zeroes lie in
U_{j}
. Note that
if
P_{j}
is not in the support of
D_{1}
then
n_{j}=0
and
u_{j}
is the zero polynomial (and likewise for
m_{j}
,
v_{j}
when

P_{j}
is not in the support of
D_{2}
). The coefficients of
the polynomials
u_{j}(z_{j}),v_{j}(z_{j})
provide a local chart about
(D_{1},D_{2})
. At a pair

(D_{1}^{\prime},D_{2}^{\prime})
in this neighbourhood of
(D_{1},D_{2})
any
\xi^{\prime}\in H^{1}(K^{-2}(D_{1}^{\prime}+D_{2}^{\prime}))
can be represented by a
1
-cocycle
\{(\xi^{\prime}_{0j},U_{0},U_{j})\}
. The map
\mathcal{Z}
maps
(D_{1}^{\prime},D_{2}^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})

to the Higgs bundle constructed as above but with
z_{j}^{n_{j}}
replaced by
z_{j}^{n_{j}}+u_{j}(z_{j})
,
z_{k}^{m_{k}}
replaced by

z_{k}^{m_{k}}+v_{k}(z_{k})
, and
\xi_{0j}
replaced by
\xi^{\prime}_{0j}
. It is easy to see that smooth variations of these parameters
result in smooth variations of the Higgs bundle.

Our aim now is to show that
d\mathcal{Z}
has trivial kernel at each point. First we describe the tangent space to

\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{c}(d_{1},d_{2})
at a given point. Since
\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{c}(d_{1},d_{2})
is a vector bundle over
S^{d_{1}}\Sigma_{c}\times S^{d_{2}}\Sigma_{c}

we can fix a local trivialisation
over a neighbourhood of
(D_{1},D_{2},\xi)
and identify the tangent space at that point with

T_{D_{1}}S^{d_{1}}\Sigma_{c}\oplus T_{D_{2}}S^{d_{2}}\oplus H^{1}(K^{-2}(D_{1}+D_{2})).

We will write elements of this in the form
(w,y,\eta)
. Further, the vector

(and similarly

) can be described as
follows. It is well-known that

T_{D_{1}}S^{d_{1}}\Sigma_{c}=\oplus_{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{P_{j}}/\mathfrak{m}_{P_{j}}^{n_{j}+1}),

where
\mathfrak{m}_{P_{j}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{P_{j}}
is the maximal ideal of locally holomorphic functions about

which vanish at

. We will identify

\mathfrak{m}_{P_{j}}/\mathfrak{m}_{P_{j}}^{n_{j}+1}\simeq\{z_{j}w_{j}(z_{j}):w_{j}\in\mathbb{C}[z_{j}],\ \deg(w_{j})\leq n_{j}-1\},

and therefore write

of polynomials. This is tangent to the curve

D_{1}(t)
on

for which

is the divisor of zeroes given by the locally defined polynomial

(for

Given such a tangent vector
(w,y,\xi)

(D_{1}(t),D_{2}(t),\xi(t))

,

and
\xi(t)=\xi+t\eta
(using the local trivialisation of
\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{c}(d_{1},d_{2})
). Let

\mathcal{Z}(t)=(E(t),\Phi(t))
denote the image curve, with

denote the transition functions for

,
in the form (

). These have entries

\alpha_{0j}(t)=\frac{1}{z^{n_{j}}+tw_{j}}\frac{dz_{0}}{dz_{j}},\quad\beta_{0j}(t)=(z^{m_{j}}+ty_{j})\frac{dz_{j}}{dz_{0}},\quad\lambda_{0j}(t)=\beta_{0j}\xi(t)_{0j}dz_{0}^{2}.

In particular,
\chi_{0}(\Phi_{1})
,
\chi^{*}_{0}(\Phi_{2})
are time-independent.
The derivative
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(0)
is represented by a hypercohomology class in

\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{A}^{*})\simeq\frac{\{(A,B)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathcal{A}^{0})\oplus\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathcal{A}^{1}):\delta A=0,\ \delta B=[\Phi,A]\}}{\{(\delta C,[\Phi,C]):C\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathcal{A}^{0})\}},

[

. Specifically, it is the class of a pair
(A,B)
for which

is represented by the
1
-cocyle

g^{\prime}(0)g^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^{\prime}(0)\alpha^{-1}&-\lambda\alpha^{\prime}(0)\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-1}+\lambda^{\prime}(0)\beta^{-1}\\
0&\beta^{\prime}(0)\beta^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

(5.3)

where we have dropped the Cech cocycle subscripts for notational simplicity. If we write
B=(B_{1},B_{2})
where
B_{j}=\Phi_{j}^{\prime}(0)
then
explicit calculation gives

\chi_{0}(B_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\chi^{*}_{0}(B_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\end{pmatrix},

\chi_{j}(B_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}w_{j}dz_{j}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\chi^{*}_{j}(B_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}0&y_{j}dz_{j}\end{pmatrix}.

(5.4)

If
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(0)=0
there must exist
C\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathcal{A}^{0})
for which

and

. We may assume

is
represented by an upper triangular
0
-cocycle of the form

\begin{pmatrix}a_{j}&b_{j}\\
0&c_{j}\end{pmatrix},

where the entries are holomorphic functions in

.
The Lie bracket

corresponds to the pair
(-C\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}C)
and calculation gives

\chi_{j}(C\Phi_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}a_{j}z^{n_{j}}dz_{j}\\

Comparing this with (

and

Now

is the condition that the

is trivial.
∎

It follows that each
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
is smooth and connected as a submanifold of

, and each is open since they have the

.

The smooth submanifold
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{M}(\Sigma,\mathbb{CH}^{2})
is a disjoint union of connected components
\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
.

To complete the proof of Theorem

we need two more lemmas. Both concern

. The first says that

\mathcal{V}(d_{1},d_{2})
is disconnected from
\mathcal{W}_{\tau}
when

. The second says
\mathcal{V}(0,d_{2})
is connected to

\mathcal{W}_{\tau}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For

,
\overline{\mathcal{V}}(d_{1},d_{2})\cap\mathcal{W}_{\tau}=\emptyset
when

.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that this is true for each fixed conformal structure
c
. So fix
c
and
\tau>0
, and let
C
be the critical
manifold of minima for the Higgs field energy
\mathfrak{E}=\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
for this value of
\tau
. Let
C_{0}\subset C
be the open
subset of Higgs bundles for which
\Phi
has no zeroes. Let
C^{\prime}
be the critical manifold of
\mathfrak{E}
for which

\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{c}(d_{1},d_{2})
corresponds to
\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{C^{\prime}}
, the closure of the unstable manifold of the downward gradient
flow of
\mathfrak{E}
(equally, the unstable manifold for the

-action). The assertion is that
\mathcal{U}_{C^{\prime}}\cap C_{0}=\emptyset

whenever

. Suppose
(E,\Phi)\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{C^{\prime}}\cap C_{0}
, then it is the limit of a sequence
(E_{k},\Phi_{k})
of Higgs

\mathcal{U}_{C^{\prime}}
. We may assume without loss of generality that each

lies below the penultimate

, and therefore all
(E_{k},\Phi_{k})
lie in the stable manifold

. Taking the limit as

is a continuous map (since

(E,\Phi)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(E_{k}^{0},\Phi_{k}^{0}),

(E_{k}^{0},\Phi_{k}^{0})=\lim_{t\to 0}(E_{k},t\Phi_{k})

(E,\Phi)
lies in the closed subset

.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

For every choice of
0<d_{2}<3(g-1)
there is a Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
in
\mathcal{V}(0,d_{2})
for which

\lim_{t\to 0}(E,t\Phi)
lies in
\mathcal{W}_{\tau}
for
\tau=\tfrac{2}{3}d_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
d_{1}=0
the Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
must correspond to an extension of the form

0\to K^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{1}}}{{\to}}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{2}}}{{\to}}K(-D_{2})\to 0,

where
\deg(D_{2})=d_{2}
. By Prop.
4.3
(ii) it suffices to show we can find such an extension for which

is
a stable bundle. This is equivalent to the existence of an extension

0\to 1\to\lambda\to K^{2}(-D_{2})\to 0,

for which
s(\lambda)=\deg(\lambda)-2\max\deg(L)\geq 1
, where the maximum is taken over all line subbundles of

\lambda
. Note that such extensions are parametrised by the space
H^{1}(K^{-2}(D_{2}))\simeq H^{0}(K^{3}(-D_{2}))^{*}
.
Such a situation is covered by
[
10

. Specifically, define
d=\deg(K^{2}(-D_{2}))=4(g-1)-d_{2}

and let
s=1
when

is odd and
s=2
when
d
is even. This satisfies the conditions
4-d\leq s\leq d
required to
apply
[
10
, Prop 1.1]
(in particular note that the critical case

, in which case
4-d=s=d
).
Then by
[
10
, Prop 1.1]

s(\lambda)\geq s
if and only if the secant variety

\operatorname{Sec}_{\tfrac{1}{2}(d+s-2)}(\Sigma_{c})\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(K^{3}(-D_{2}))^{*},

is a proper subvariety. Since
\deg(K^{3}(-D_{2}))>2(g-1)
the Riemann-Roch theorem gives the dimension of
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(K^{3}(-D_{2}))^{*}

as

. By
[
9
]
this secant variety has dimension
d+s-3\leq d-1
is therefore a proper subvariety for

g\geq 2
.
∎

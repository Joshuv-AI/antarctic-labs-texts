Gradient estimates for positive weak solution to Δ p ​ u + a ​ u σ = 0 \Delta_{p}u+au^{\sigma}=0 on Riemannian manifolds Thanks: The research of authors is supported by NSFC (Nos. 11971153, 12126319).
Guangyue Huang, Qi Guo, Lujun Guo

Abstract
. In this paper, we study gradient estimates for positive weak solutions to the following p p -Laplacian equation Δ p ​ u + a ​ u σ = 0 \Delta_{p}u+au^{\sigma}=0 on a Riemannian manifold, where p > 1 p>1 and a , σ a,\sigma are two nonzero real constants. By virtue of the Morser iteration technique, we derive some gradient estimates, which show that when the Ricci curvature is nonnegative, the above equation does not admit positive weak solutions under some scopes of p p .

1.
Introduction

Let
M^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with the dimension
n\geq 3
. The study on gradient estimates for the positive harmonic functions to the equation

\displaystyle\Delta u=0

(1.1)

was firstly introduced
by Yau
[
21
]
, which states that any positive or bounded solution to the
equation (
1.1
) with the Ricci curvature
R_{ij}\geq 0
must be constant. From then on, the study to related nonlinear equations with respect to the Laplacian (can be traced back to Li
[
8
]
) have been paid more attention. For the development in this direction, see
[
9
,
19
,
20
,
10
,
5
,
11
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
23
]
and the references therein.

In
[
7
]
, Kotschwar and Ni studied the following
p
-harmonic functions to the equation

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u=0,

(1.2)

and derived a gradient estimate under the assumption that the sectional curvature is bounded from below, where the
p
-Laplacian with respect to
u
is defined by

\Delta_{p}u:={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)\ \ \ {\rm for\ every}\ u\in W^{1,p},

which is understood in distribution sense. By using the Morser iteration technique, Wang and Zhang
[
14
]
derived a gradient estimate, where their estimates depend on the Ricci curvature instead of the sectional curvature. Recently, Wang and Wei
[
15
]
also use the Morser iteration technique and study the nonexistence of positive weak solution to

\displaystyle\Delta u+au^{q+1}=0

(1.3)

on a Riemannian manifold with
a>0
and proved that if the Ricci curvature is nonnegative, then the above equation does not admit positive weak solutions provided
q\in(-\infty,\frac{2}{n-1}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}})
.

In this paper, we continue to study gradient estimates for positive weak solutions to the following
p
-Laplacian equation

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u+au^{\sigma}=0,

(1.4)

where
p>1
and
a,\sigma
are two nonzero real constants. By virtue of the Morser iteration technique, we derive the following gradient estimate:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with
R_{ij}\geq-(n-1)K
, where
K
is a positive constant. Assume that the constants
a,\sigma
satisfy that either
a>0
and
\sigma<(p-1)\big[\frac{n+1}{n-1}+\frac{2}{n-1}\sqrt{1-\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{(n-1)\alpha}}\big]
, or
a<0
and
\sigma>(p-1)\big[\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{2}{n-1}\sqrt{1-\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{(n-1)\alpha}}\big]
, where

\alpha=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{n}{n-1}(p-1)^{2},\quad p\in\big(1,3-\frac{2}{n}\big],\\
2(p-1),\quad\quad\ \ p\in(3-\frac{2}{n},2n-1).\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

Then for any positive weak solution
u
to (
1.4
) with
1<p<2n-1
on the geodesic ball
B_{x_{0}}(R)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq C_{p,n,\sigma}\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{K}R}{R}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\ \ \ on\ B_{x_{0}}(\frac{R}{2}),

(1.6)

where the constant
C_{p,n,\sigma}
depends only on
p,\sigma
and the dimension
n
.

From the above theorem, the following nonexistence results follow by taking
R\rightarrow\infty
immediately:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
M^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature with
1<p<2n-1
and
\alpha
given by (
1.5
). Assume that the constants
a,\sigma
satisfy that either
a>0
and
\sigma\in\big(-\infty,(p-1)\big[\frac{n+1}{n-1}+\frac{2}{n-1}\sqrt{1-\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{(n-1)\alpha}}\big]\big)
, or
a<0
and
\sigma\in\big((p-1)\big[\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{2}{n-1}\sqrt{1-\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{(n-1)\alpha}}\big],+\infty\big)
, then there does not exist any positive weak solution to the equation (
1.4
).

On the other hand, by using other methods, we can achieve the following gradient estimate, which does not depends on the upper bound of
p
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with
R_{ij}\geq-(n-1)K
, where
K
is a positive constant. Assume that the constants
a,\sigma
satisfy that either
a>0
and
\sigma\leq\frac{n+2}{n}(p-1)
, or
a<0
and
\sigma\geq\frac{n+2}{n}(p-1)
. Then for any positive weak solution
u
to (
1.4
) with
p>1
on the geodesic ball
B_{x_{0}}(R)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq C_{p,n}\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{K}R}{R}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\ \ \ on\ B_{x_{0}}(\frac{R}{2}),

(1.7)

where the constant
C_{p,n}
depends only on
p
and the dimension
n
.

Similarly, the following nonexistence results also follow by taking
R\rightarrow\infty
immediately:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
M^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature. Assume that the constants
a,\sigma
satisfy that either
a>0
and
\sigma\leq\frac{n+2}{n}(p-1)
, or
a<0
and
\sigma\geq\frac{n+2}{n}(p-1)
.
Then there does not exist any positive weak solution to the equation (
1.4
).

From the above theorem, the following Harnack inequality follows immediately:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
M^{n}
be an
n(n>2)
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with
R_{ij}\geq-(n-1)K
, where
K
is a positive constant.
Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.3
, then for any positive weak solution
u
to (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle u(x)\leq e^{C_{p,n,K}}u(y),

(1.8)

where
x,y\in B_{x_{0}}(\frac{R}{2})
.

Remark 1.1
.

In particular, when
R_{ij}\geq 0
, the above Harnack inequality (
1.8
) becomes

\displaystyle u(x)\leq e^{C_{p,n}}u(y),

(1.9)

where the uniform constant
C_{p,n}
does not depend on
R
.

Remark 1.2
.

Clearly, the Theorem 1.2 in
[
14
]
of Wang and Zhang follows from by taking
a\rightarrow 0
in (
1.7
).

Remark 1.3
.

For smooth metric measure spaces, our Theorem
1.3
still holds under the same conditions. On the other hand, Zhao and Yang
[
22
]
derived similar results under the condition
a>0
and
\sigma\leq p-1
, while our result holds for

a>0
and
\sigma\leq\frac{n+2}{n}(p-1)
, or
a<0
and
\sigma\geq\frac{n+2}{n}(p-1)
. Obviously, we generalize the results in

[
22
]
of Zhao and Yang.

Remark 1.4
.

When
p=2
and
a>0
, Theorem 1.4 of Wang and Wei in
[
15
]
follows from Corollary
1.2
. In the case of
a<0
, our results are new. On the other hand, if
p<2n-1
, we can check that

\frac{n+2}{n}(p-1)<(p-1)\Big[\frac{n+1}{n-1}+\frac{2}{n-1}\sqrt{1-\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{(n-1)\alpha}}\Big],

which shows that, for
a>0
and
p\geq 2n-1
, our Theorem
1.3
is new.

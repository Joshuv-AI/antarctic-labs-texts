Abstract
We study transition probabilities of generalized functions as introduced by Colombeau and Gsponer. We formally introduz the study of H. Bohr almost periodic functions in the generalized context and use them to give exact values of transition probabilities in dimension 2 \ 2 . We also prove the existence of transition probabilities, in the generalized sense, for any moderate net T = ( T ε ) \ T=(T_{\varepsilon}) with T ε : ℍ ⟶ ℍ \ T_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{H} , ℍ \mathbb{H} a Hilbert space. In particular, we consider the case of selfadjoint Hilbert-Schmidt operators. This alludes to the possibility of existence of transition probabilities in the Fock space, in the sense introduced by Colombeau and Gsponer, if the spectrum of the operators involved are pure infinities or infinitesimals. This was already indicated in two papers coauthored by J. Aragona and J.F. Colombeau et. al.

A set of real numbers

Definition 3.1

Given a complex valued function

Consider the

Theorem 3.2
(Fundamental Theorem of A.P.F.)

A complex valued function
\ f\
is almost periodic if and only if
\ f\in\overline{V}
. Moreover,
\overline{V}
is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra and its elements are bounded uniformly continuous functions.

If
f

M(f):=\lim\limits_{T\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T}f(x)dx=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}f(x)dx

In particular,
M(|sin(Ax)|)=M(|cos(Ax)|)=\frac{2}{\pi}
,
\forall\ A>0
. We shall use this in the section of examples.

Theorem 3.3
(Mean Value Theorem)

For every a.p.f. the mean value

M(f):=\lim\limits_{T\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T}f(x)dx

exist.

Hence, an a.p.f. may not be periodic but it still has a mean value. Theorem
3.3

\ f

M(|f(x)|^{2})=\sum\limits_{M(f(x)e^{-i\lambda x})\neq 0}|M(f(x)e^{-i\lambda x})|^{2}

We proceed to prove alternative formulas for the mean value of an a.p.f. and by composing them with pure infinities, we introduce their generalized transition probability int the generalized environment.

Lemma 3.4

M(f)=\lim\limits_{T\longrightarrow\infty}\gamma\cdot T^{\gamma}\cdot\int\limits_{T}^{\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x^{1+\gamma}}dx

Proof.

Case
\gamma=1

We first suppose that
T=n
is an integer and hence we may calculate its limit as
\lim\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}n\cdot\int\limits_{n}^{\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x^{2}}dx

=\lim\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}n\cdot(\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}[\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+\beta}])

=\int\limits_{n-1}^{n}\frac{1}{u(u+\beta)}du+\int\limits_{n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{u(u+\beta)}du

\lim\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}n\cdot(\int\limits_{n-1}^{n}\frac{1}{u(u+\beta)}du)=0

\lim\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}n\cdot(\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k(k+\beta)})=\lim\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}n\cdot(\int\limits_{n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{u(u+\beta)}du)=\lim\limits_{x\longrightarrow\infty}x\cdot(\int\limits_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{u(u+\beta)}du)

=\lim\limits_{x\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{x}{\beta}\cdot\ln(\frac{x+\beta}{x})=1

Now if

Fock Space

In
[
30
,
31
]
Colombeau and Gsponer propose a setting for QFT aiming to deal with the renormalization theory. We collect some fact sabout this setting. We will be using their notation and thus it will be useful for the reader to survey their paper for a more precise understanding of their setting.

The Fock space, introduced by the Soviet physicist Vladimir Aleksandrovich Fock, who did foundational work on quantum mechanics and quantum electrodynamics, is the Hilbertian direct sum

\mathbb{F}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}L^{2}_{s}((\mathbb{R}^{3})^{n}),

(1)

where
L^{2}_{s}((\mathbb{R}^{3})^{0}=\mathbb{C},\ L^{2}_{s}((\mathbb{R}^{3})^{1}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and for
n\geq 2\ L^{2}_{s}((\mathbb{R}^{3})^{n})
is the closed subspace of the space
L^{2}((\mathbb{R}^{3})^{n})
made of those (complex valued) functions which are symmetric in their
n
arguments in
\mathbb{R}^{3}.
That is, an element
F
of
\mathbb{F}
is an infinite sequence
F=(f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n},\dots)
such that

\|F\|^{2}:=|f_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\|f_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3n})}<+\infty.

(2)

They introduce the generalized functions over the Fock Space and prove that
S_{\tau}(t)
, the scattering operator, is a unitary operator in this generalized environment. According to them, this proof is actually due to Perron (see also
[
5
]
). We recall the following results of
[
42
]
.

Proposition 4.1

The interacting field operator satisfies the formula.

\Phi(x,t)=(S_{\tau}(t))^{-1}\Phi_{0}(x,t)S_{\tau}(t)

Proposition 4.2

The scattering operator is solution of the ODE.

\partial_{t}S_{\tau}(t)=-i\frac{g}{N+1}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\Phi_{0}(x,t))^{N+1}dx\ S_{\tau}(t),\ \ S_{\tau}(\tau)=id

The detailed formal calculations for the proofs of propositions 4.1 and 4.2 are carried out in
[
30
]
. Using this generalized setting over the Fock Space, Colombeau and Gsponer (see
[
30
,
42
]
) propose how to obtain numerical predictions of the transition probabilities, i.e., the probability that a state
F
before interaction produces a state
G
after interaction. Considering an observable
O(x,t)
as a product of field operators, then, after interaction, the formula one gets is

O(x,t)=S_{\tau}(t)^{-1}O_{0}(x,t)S_{\tau}(t).

(3)

The formula

|<F_{1}|O(x,t)F_{2}>|=|<F_{1}|S_{\tau}(t)^{-1}O_{0}(x,t)S_{\tau}(t)F_{2}>|=|<S_{\tau}(t)F_{1},O_{0}(x,t)S_{\tau}(t)F_{2}>|

shows that
S_{\tau}(t)F_{i}
are the states that give the results from experiments in the new free field theory after the interaction has ceased (
\tau
in the past before interaction,
t
in the future after interaction). Therefore the probability that a state
F
before interaction would be transformed after interaction into a state
G
is
|<G,S_{\tau}(t)F>|
, which is therefore interpreted as the transition probability of
F
into
G
due to the interaction. A way to view this transition probability is the following: Let
\ u,v
be orthogonal vectors of norm one of a Hilbert space
\ {\mathbb{H}}
and let
\ S
be a unitary operator on
\ {\mathbb{H}}
. Then one sees
|<\vec{u},S\vec{v}>|
as probability in the following way: as a probability by considering an average of the values
|<\vec{u},S(\epsilon)\vec{v}>|
on a large number of small values of
\epsilon
. For instance according to a classical interpretation of renormalization in the case of the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron various values of
\epsilon
could represent various spontaneous creations of virtual electron-positron pairs in the vacuum
[
62
]
section 26.9. In short, the transition probability should be

\mathcal{P}_{0}(v\rightarrow u)=\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0,\ N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|<\vec{u},S(\epsilon_{i})\vec{v}>|,random\ \epsilon_{i}\in]0,\eta[

(4)

provided this limit exists. Note that this average is naturally related to our notion of support of a generalized object. A non-discrete way to express this average is

\mathcal{P}_{0}(v\rightarrow u)=\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\eta}|\langle u,S_{\epsilon}v\rangle|d\epsilon.

(5)

This leads naturally to the definition of the
generalized transition probability
as

\mathcal{P}(v\rightarrow u)=\bigg[\varepsilon\longrightarrow\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}|\langle u,S_{t}v\rangle|dt\bigg]

Hence the existence of
\ \mathcal{P}_{0}(v\rightarrow u)
is equivalent to

\mathcal{P}(v\rightarrow u)\approx\mathcal{P}_{0}(v\rightarrow u)

or, equivalently,

supp(\mathcal{P}(v\rightarrow u))=\{\mathcal{P}_{0}(v\rightarrow u)\}

Since, in general, this obviously need not be the case, this leads us to define any element of
\ supp(\mathcal{P}(v\rightarrow u))
as a transition probability. If we suppose that
\ S_{t}
depends continuously on
\ t
and that
\ supp(\mathcal{P}(v\rightarrow u))=\{\mathcal{P}_{0}(v\rightarrow u)\}
then we have that

\mathcal{P}(v\rightarrow u)\approx\bigg|\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\langle u,S_{t}v\rangle dt\bigg|=\mathcal{P}_{0}(v\rightarrow u)

Henceforth, in this paper, the existence of a transition probability will be equal to the the support of the generalized transition probability being not empty. Colombeau and Gsponer require that the support of the generalized transition probability has a unique element.

Here we deal with the Colombeau algebra defined over
L(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
31

) and thus all operators involved are, by definition, bounded operators. This can be justified since there does not exist a structure containing the unbounded operators in which one can calculate their norm which is necessary in the construction of a Colombeau algebra. The construction of

probably contains most important operators used in Physics.

We shall now prove that the perfect destructive interference condition also holds for the transition probability of normal matrices. In fact, let

\ UU^{*}=U^{*}U

MAM^{*}=D_{1}
is a diagonal matrix,
\ \lambda\in Spec(A)
and
\ W=V_{\lambda}

\ W

\ M

\ \overline{\mathbb{K}}-

\ AB=BA

\ B
leaves
\ W

\ B_{|_{W}}

\ W

Theorem 6.1

Let
\ f=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i}g_{i}\cdot(f_{i}\circ\mu_{i})^{2}\ }
, where
g=(g_{1},\cdots,g_{n})\in({\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{as}})^{n}

\ \mu_{i}
’s are pure infinities or infinitesimals and the
\ f_{i}
’s are a.p. functions. Then
\ supp(\nu(f))\neq\emptyset

Proof.
We have that
\ f=|f|=\sqrt{\bigg|\sum\limits_{i}g_{i}\cdot(f_{i}\circ\mu_{i})^{2}\bigg|\ }\leq C\bigg(\sum\limits_{i}|f_{i}\circ\mu_{i}|\bigg)
. The result follows by Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.9
.

It follows from the previous theorem that
\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\bigg[\exp\bigg(\frac{i}{\varepsilon}\bigg)-2\exp\bigg(\frac{i}{\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg)+(1+\varepsilon)\exp\bigg(\frac{i}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\bigg)\bigg]\bigg|
has generalized transition probabilities and the same holds for every normal matrix

3.9

A Hidden Convexity of Nonlinear Elasticity
Siddharth Singh Janusz Ginster Amit Acharya

Abstract
A technique for developing convex dual variational principles for the governing PDE of nonlinear elastostatics and elastodynamics is presented. This allows the definition of notions of a variational dual solution and a dual solution corresponding to the PDEs of nonlinear elasticity, even when the latter arise as formal Euler-Lagrange equations corresponding to non-quasiconvex elastic energy functionals whose energy minimizers do not exist. This is demonstrated rigorously in the case of elastostatics for the Saint-Venant Kirchhoff material (in all dimensions), where the existence of variational dual solutions is also proven. The existence of a variational dual solution for the incompressible neo-Hookean material in 2-d is also shown. Stressed and unstressed elastostatic and elastodynamic solutions in 1 space dimension corresponding to a non-convex, double-well energy are computed using the dual methodology. In particular, we show the stability of a dual elastodynamic equilibrium solution for which there are regions of non-vanishing length with negative elastic stiffness, i.e. non-hyperbolic regions, for which the corresponding primal problem is ill-posed and demonstrates an explosive ‘Hadamard instability;’ this appears to have implications for the modeling of physically observed softening behavior in macroscopic mechanical response.



1
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to demonstrate a technique for obtaining solutions of the governing PDE of nonlinear elasticity. The elasticity may be of Cauchy type, with stress not necessarily arising as a derivative of an energy density function of the deformation gradient. The methodology is of a ‘dual’ variational nature, transforming the given ‘primal’ PDE problem written in first-order form, in terms of the physical deformation, deformation gradient, and velocity fields, to a dual variational and PDE problem in terms of dual Lagrange multiplier fields. The fundamental insight is to treat the primal PDE under consideration as constraints and invoke a more-or-less arbitrarily designable strictly convex, auxiliary potential to be optimized. Then, a dual variational principle for the Lagrange multiplier (dual) fields can be designed involving a dual-to-primal (DtP) mapping (i.e. an adapted change of variables), with the special property that its Euler-Lagrange equations are exactly the primal PDE system, interpreted as equations for the dual fields using the DtP mapping, and this, even though the primal system may not have the required symmetries necessary to be the Euler-Lagrange equations of any objective functional of the primal fields alone. As we show, the dual variational principle is convex, with its formal Euler-Lagrange system being locally degenerate elliptic, regardless of the monotonicity properties of the primal PDE system. Thus, to the extent that coercivity of the dual functional can be established, existence of minimizers of the dual functional, our variational dual solutions that are formally consistent with weak solutions of the primal PDE system, can be established. Since these ideas do not even depend on the existence of an energy density function for the primal problem, the issue of existence of minimizers of a primal energy functional is not relevant to our technique; instead it works with the structure of the stress response function and the equations of equilibrium for the primal model of elasticity invoked. Even in the case of hyperelasticity, if the
primal energy functional is not lower-semicontinuous then classical variational techniques cannot be used to prove existence of minimizers. In this case, the classical approach is to consider the relaxation of the energy functional. However, minimizers of the relaxed energy functional typically do not satisfy the original PDE system.

Our concerns are different from these earlier works and not focused on a complementary energy principle - as one difference, we require our dual solution to recover as its Euler-Lagrange equations the primal system consisting of compatibility and equilibrium (in the case of statics), not by imposing the latter as a feature of the class of dual fields under consideration. 2
Dual nonlinear Elastostatics

Consider the problem of nonlinear elastostatics with boundary conditions of place and dead loads (for simplicity):

\displaystyle\partial_{j}P_{ij}(F)+b_{i}

\displaystyle=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega

(1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{j}y_{i}-F_{ij}

\displaystyle=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega

(1b)

\displaystyle P_{ij}n_{j}=p_{i}\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{p}\

\displaystyle;\qquad y_{i}=y^{(b)}_{i}\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{y},\quad\partial\Omega=\partial\Omega_{p}\cup\Omega_{y}.

In the above,
P
is a given tensor valued function of invertible tensors
F
(delivering the First Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor for a prescribed deformation gradient), and all derivatives are w.r.t. rectangular Cartesian coordinates on a fixed reference configuration
\Omega
(
P
is not assumed to necessarily be a gradient of a scalar valued function on the space of invertible tensors
). The only restrictions on
P
we require are that it be sufficiently smooth in its argument
and that it be of the form

P(F)=F\,S\!\left(F^{T}F\right)

for
S
being any arbitrary symmetric tensor valued function of a symmetric tensor; this allows frame-indifference to be satisfied. Furthermore,
b
is the specified body force density per unit volume of the reference configuration field and
it is not required that the latter arise from a potential
.

Define the pre-dual functional by forming the scalar products of (
1a
) and (
1b
) with the dual fields
\lambda
and
\mu
, respectively, integrating by parts, and imposing the prescribed b.c.s:

\displaystyle\widehat{S}_{H}[y,F,\lambda,\mu]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}-P_{ij}\big|_{F}\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{i}b_{i}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}(-y_{i}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}-F_{ij}\mu_{ij})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}H(y,F;x)\,dx

(2)

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}p_{i}\lambda_{i}\,da\ +\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}_{i}\mu_{ij}n_{j}\,da.

Define

\displaystyle U:=(y,F);\qquad D:=(\lambda,\mu);\qquad\mathcal{D}:=(\nabla\lambda,\lambda,\nabla\mu,\mu);

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{H}(U,\mathcal{D},x):=-P_{ij}\big|_{F}\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{i}b_{i}-y_{i}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}-F_{ij}\mu_{ij}+H(y,F;x),

and require the choice of the potential
H
to be such that it facilitates the existence of a function

U=U_{H}(\mathcal{D},x)

which satisfies

\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{H}}{\partial U}\left(U_{H}(\mathcal{D},x),\mathcal{D},x\right)=0\qquad\forall\ (\mathcal{D},x).

(4)

When such a ‘change of variables’ mapping,
U_{H}=\left(y^{(H)},F^{(H)}\right)
, exists, defining the
dual
functional as

\displaystyle S_{H}[D]:=\widehat{S}_{H}\left[y^{(H)},F^{(H)},\lambda,\mu\right]=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}_{H}\left(U_{H}(\mathcal{D},x),\mathcal{D},x\right)\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}p_{i}\lambda_{i}\,da\ +\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}_{i}\mu_{ij}n_{j}\,da

\displaystyle\mbox{with $\lambda$ specified on $\partial\Omega\backslash\partial\Omega_{p}$ and $\mu$ specified on $\partial\Omega\backslash\partial\Omega_{y}$},

and noting (
4
), the first variation of
S_{H}
(about a state
D
in the direction
\delta D
, the latter constrained by
\delta\lambda=0
on
\partial\Omega\backslash\partial\Omega_{p}
and
\delta\mu=0
on
\partial\Omega\backslash\partial\Omega_{y}
), is given by

\delta S_{H}=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\Omega$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{H}}{\partial\mathcal{D}}\left(U_{H}(\mathcal{D},x),\mathcal{D},x\right)\cdot\delta\mathcal{D}\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}p_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\,da\ +\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}_{i}\delta\mu_{ij}n_{j}\,da.

Noting, now, that
\mathcal{L}_{H}
is necessarily affine in
\mathcal{D}
, its second argument, it can be checked that
the Euler-Lagrange equations and natural boundary conditions of the dual functional
S_{H}
are exactly the system (
1
) with
U
substituted by
U_{H}(\mathcal{D},x)
.

It is this simple idea that we exploit to develop variational principles for (Cauchy) Elasticity.

2.1
Quadratic nonlinearity about arbitrarily fixed base states

In this section we implement the generalities above in a context that allows to demonstrate an explicitly written out dual functional. This is only for conceptual simplicity in conveying ideas; in fact, the examples we successfully compute in Sec.
6
will be more general than the assumptions made here.

For solutions of (
1
) ‘close’ to an arbitrarily chosen (and fixed thereafter) pair of fields we refer to as a
base state
given by

x\mapsto\bar{y}(x);\qquad x\mapsto\nabla\bar{y}(x)=:\bar{F}(x),

we assume that the nominal stress fields,
x\mapsto P(x)
, for all candidate pairs
(y,F)
that belong to the domain of the functional
\widehat{S}
are well-represented by a quadratic expansion around
(\bar{y},\bar{F})
: i.e.

\displaystyle P_{ij}(F(x))

\displaystyle=\bar{P}_{ij}\left(\bar{F}(x)\right)+A_{ijkl}|_{\bar{F}(x)}\left(F_{kl}(x)-\bar{F}_{kl}(x)\right)

(5)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}B_{ijklmn}|_{\bar{F}(x)}\left(F_{kl}(x)-\bar{F}_{kl}(x)\right)\left(F_{mn}(x)-\bar{F}_{mn}(x)\right),

where
\bar{P},A,B
are given functions from the space of second order tensors to the space of second, fourth, and sixth order tensors, respectively. We assume the symmetries
B_{ijklmn}=B_{ijmnkl}
, without loss of generality. The coefficients
\bar{P},A,B
may be interpreted as those of a truncated Taylor expansion of the nominal stress response function about
\bar{F}
, but not necessarily. In the following, we will assume these functions to be bounded.

We note that the assumption on the form of the stress allows for nominal stress fields that access ‘monotone increasing’ and ‘monotone decreasing’ parts of the ‘stress-strain’ response of the elastic material at different spatial locations of the domain.

We now choose a ‘shifted quadratic’ form for the potential
H
:

Q(y,F;x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{y}\left|y-\bar{y}(x)\right|^{2}+c_{F}\left|F-\bar{F}(x)\right|^{2}\right),

where

c_{y},c_{F}\gg 1

are real-valued scalars. Then, the implementation of (
4
) in this context means algebraically solving for
\left(y^{(Q)},F^{(Q)}\right)
in terms of
(x,\nabla\lambda,\nabla\mu,\mu)
from the
dual-to-primal
(DtP) mapping equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{Q}}{\partial y_{i}}:

\displaystyle-\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}+c_{y}\left(y_{i}^{(Q)}-\bar{y}_{i}(x)\right)=0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{Q}}{\partial F_{rs}}:

\displaystyle-\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}\left(A_{ijrs}|_{\bar{F}(x)}+B_{ijklrs}|_{\bar{F}(x)}\left(F_{kl}^{(Q)}-\bar{F}_{kl}(x)\right)\right)-\mu_{rs}+c_{F}\left(F_{rs}^{(Q)}-\bar{F}_{rs}(x)\right)=0.

which can be rearranged as the following relations (suppressing the explicit
x
dependence of the base state for brevity)

\displaystyle c_{y}\left(y^{(Q)}_{i}-\bar{y}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}

(6a)

\displaystyle c_{F}\,\mathbb{K}_{rskl}|_{\bar{F}}\left(F_{kl}^{(Q)}-\bar{F}_{kl}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{rs}+\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}A_{ijrs}|_{\bar{F}}

(6b)

\displaystyle\mbox{where}\qquad\mathbb{K}_{rskl}\big|_{(\bar{F},\nabla\lambda)}

\displaystyle:=\delta_{kr}\delta_{ls}-\frac{1}{c_{F}}\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}B_{ijklrs}|_{\bar{F}}.\quad\mbox{is affine in}\ \nabla\lambda.

(6c)

Then

\displaystyle\widehat{S}_{Q}\left[y^{(Q)},\lambda,F^{(Q)},\mu\right]=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}\biggl(\bar{P}_{ij}\left(\bar{F}\right)+A_{ijkl}|_{\bar{F}}\left(F_{kl}^{(Q)}-\bar{F}_{kl}\right)+\frac{1}{2}B_{ijklmn}|_{\bar{F}}\left(F_{kl}^{(Q)}-\bar{F}_{kl}\right)\left(F_{mn}^{(Q)}-\bar{F}_{mn}\right)\biggr)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(-\mu_{ij}\left(F_{ij}^{(Q)}-\bar{F}_{ij}\right)-\mu_{ij}\bar{F}_{ij}\right)\,dx\quad+\int_{\Omega}\left(-\left(y_{i}^{(Q)}-\bar{y}_{i}\right)\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}-\bar{y}_{i}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{y}\left|y^{(Q)}-\bar{y}\right|^{2}+c_{F}\left|F^{(Q)}-\bar{F}\right|^{2}\right)\,dx\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}p_{i}\lambda_{i}\,da\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}_{i}\mu_{ij}n_{j}\,da+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{i}b_{i}\,dx.

For notational simplicity, in the following computations, we will omit mention of the superscript
(Q)
.

We obtain the dual functional through the following computation utilizing (
6
):

\displaystyle S_{Q}[\lambda,\mu]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\bar{P}_{ij}\big|_{\bar{F}}\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}-\bar{F}_{ij}\mu_{ij}-\bar{y}_{i}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}+\lambda_{i}b_{i}\right)\,dx\quad-\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mu_{rs}+\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}A_{ijrs}\big|_{\bar{F}}\Bigr)\left(F_{rs}-\bar{F}_{rs}\right)\,dx

(7)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(c_{F}\delta_{kr}\delta_{ls}-\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}B_{ijklrs}\big|_{\bar{F}}\Bigr)\left(F_{kl}-\bar{F}_{kl}\right)\left(F_{rs}-\bar{F}_{rs}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{i})\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}c_{y}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{i})(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{i})\,dx\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}p_{i}\lambda_{i}\,da\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}_{i}\mu_{ij}n_{j}\,da

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\bar{P}_{ij}\big|_{\bar{F}}\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}-\bar{F}_{ij}\mu_{ij}-\bar{y}_{i}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}+\lambda_{i}b_{i}\right)\,dx\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}p_{i}\lambda_{i}\,da\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}_{i}\mu_{ij}n_{j}\,da

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mu_{rs}+\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}A_{ijrs}\big|_{\bar{F}}\Bigr)\left(F_{rs}-\bar{F}_{rs}\right)\,dx\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{i})\,dx,

and using (
6
) again,
a dual functional for nonlinear (Cauchy) elastostatics
is given by

\displaystyle S_{Q}[\lambda,\mu]=

(8)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\biggl[\Big(\mu_{rs}+\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}A_{ijrs}\big|_{\bar{F}}\Bigr)\frac{1}{c_{F}}\left(\mathbb{K}\big|_{(\bar{F},\nabla\lambda)}^{-1}\right)_{rskl}\Big(\mu_{kl}+\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}A_{ijkl}\big|_{\bar{F}}\Bigr)\quad+\frac{1}{c_{y}}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}\partial_{k}\mu_{ik}\biggr]\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(-\bar{P}_{ij}\big|_{\bar{F}}\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}-\bar{F}_{ij}\mu_{ij}-\bar{y}_{i}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}+\lambda_{i}b_{i}\right)\,dx\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}p_{i}\lambda_{i}\,da\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}_{i}\mu_{ij}n_{j}\,da.

2.2
Formal second variation and stability of dual solutions

From the DtP equations (
6
), a primal pair
\left(y^{(Q)},F^{(Q)}\right)
corresponding to the dual fields

\bar{D}:=(x\mapsto\lambda(x)=0,x\mapsto\mu(x)=0)

is given by
(x\mapsto\bar{y}(x),x\mapsto\bar{F}(x))
.

We are now interested in obtaining the second variation of the dual functional
S_{Q}
about the dual state
\bar{D}
, along sufficiently smooth perturbations
(x\mapsto\delta D(x)\,,\,x\mapsto dD(x))
for
c_{F},c_{y}\gg 1
, as required.

We make the (physically realistic) assumption that the nominal stress response of the material is such that
A,B
are bounded functions on the space of second order tensors.

Noting

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{rskl}\big|_{(\bar{F},\nabla\lambda)}=\delta_{kr}\delta_{ls}-\frac{1}{c_{F}}\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}B_{ijrskl}\big|_{\bar{F}}

\displaystyle;\qquad\mathbb{K}_{rskl}\big|_{(\bar{F},\nabla\lambda=0)}=\delta_{kr}\delta_{ls}=:\mathbb{I}_{rskl}

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}\mathbb{K}^{-1}=\mathbb{I}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\frac{\partial\mathbb{K}^{-1}}{\partial\nabla\lambda}=-\mathbb{K}^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbb{K}}{\partial\nabla\lambda}\mathbb{K}^{-1}

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial\mathbb{K}}{\partial\nabla\lambda}\right|_{(\bar{F},\nabla\lambda)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{c_{F}}B\big|_{\bar{F}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{rskl}\big|_{(\bar{F},\nabla\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{K}_{klrs}\big|_{(\bar{F},\nabla\lambda)},

the first variation of
S_{Q}
, at
D
and in the direction
\delta D
, is given by

\displaystyle\delta S_{Q}\big|_{\delta D}[D]=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\biggl(\frac{1}{c_{F}}\left(\mathbb{K}\big|_{(\bar{F},\nabla\lambda)}^{-1}\right)_{rskl}\left(\mu_{rs}+\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}A_{ijrs}\big|_{\bar{F}}\right)\left(\delta\mu_{kl}+\partial_{j}\delta\lambda_{i}A_{ijkl}\big|_{\bar{F}}\right)\ +\ \frac{1}{c_{y}}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}\partial_{k}\delta\mu_{ik}\biggr)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu_{rs}+\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}A_{ijrs}\big|_{\bar{F}}\right)\left(\mu_{kl}+\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}A_{ijkl}\big|_{\bar{F}}\right)\frac{1}{c_{F}}\biggl(\mathbb{K}^{-1}_{rsmn}\frac{\partial\mathbb{K}_{mnij}}{(\partial\nabla\lambda)_{pq}}\mathbb{K}^{-1}_{ijkl}\biggr)\bigg|_{(\bar{F},\nabla\lambda)}\partial_{q}\delta\lambda_{p}\biggr)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(-\bar{P}_{ij}\big|_{\bar{F}}\partial_{j}\delta\lambda_{i}-\bar{F}_{ij}\delta\mu_{ij}-\bar{y}_{i}\partial_{j}\delta\mu_{ij}+b_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\right)\,dx\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}p_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\,da\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}_{i}\delta\mu_{ij}n_{j}\,da

with the evaluation

\delta S_{Q}\big|_{\delta D}[\bar{D}]=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\bar{P}_{ij}\big|_{\bar{F}}\partial_{j}\delta\lambda_{i}-\bar{F}_{ij}\delta\mu_{ij}-\bar{y}_{i}\partial_{j}\delta\mu_{ij}+b_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\right)\,dx\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}p_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\,da\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}_{i}\delta\mu_{ij}n_{j}\,da.

Our interest is in examining the second variation of
S_{Q}
, at the dual state
\bar{D}
and in arbitrarily fixed directions
\delta D
and
dD
(which we will assume to be sufficiently regular so that integrals containing mutual products of them and their derivatives further multiplied by bounded functions, are finite). To do so, it suffices to consider states
D
with its derivatives to have similar regularity (surely
\bar{D}
belongs to this class), and then find that

\displaystyle d\delta S_{Q}\big|_{(\delta D,dD)}[D]=

(9)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\biggl(\frac{1}{c_{F}}\left(d\mu_{rs}+\partial_{j}d\lambda_{i}A_{ijrs}\big|_{\bar{F}}\right)\left(\mathbb{K}\big|_{(\bar{F},\nabla\lambda)}^{-1}\right)_{rskl}\left(\delta\mu_{kl}+\partial_{j}\delta\lambda_{i}A_{ijkl}\big|_{\bar{F}}\right)\ +\ \frac{1}{c_{y}}\partial_{j}d\mu_{ij}\partial_{k}\delta\mu_{ik}\biggr)\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{c_{F}^{2}}\bigl(\cdots\bigr)\quad+\quad\frac{1}{c_{F}^{3}}\bigl(\cdots\bigr),

where the coefficients of
c_{F}^{-2},c_{F}^{-3}
are finite (the negative powers on
c_{F}
arise from repeated appearances of
\frac{\partial\mathbb{K}}{\partial\nabla\lambda}
in the integrands comprising these terms), so that for
c_{F}\gg 1
the second variation is controlled by the first term on the right hand side of (
9
). Furthermore, for any dual state for which
\nabla\lambda
is bounded on the domain,
c_{F}
can be chosen large enough so that
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathbb{K}^{-1}
are positive-definite.

Then, for
c_{F},c_{y}\gg 1
, the second variation at such states is
non-positive
and it is this feature of the dual nonlinear elastostatics problem that we term as its
hidden convexity
(with a trivial change of sign).

This result is also obtained rigorously and without approximation in Sec.
5
, with a slight reinterpretation of the DtP mapping-determining condition ‘
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial U}=0
.’

The second variation at the state
\bar{D}
is given, regardless of the magnitude of
c_{F}
, by

d\delta S_{Q}\big|_{(\delta D,dD)}[\bar{D}]=-\int_{\Omega}\biggl(\frac{1}{c_{F}}\left(d\mu_{rs}+\partial_{j}d\lambda_{i}A_{ijrs}\big|_{\bar{F}}\right)\left(\delta\mu_{rs}+\partial_{j}\delta\lambda_{i}A_{ijrs}\big|_{\bar{F}}\right)\ +\ \frac{1}{c_{y}}\partial_{j}d\mu_{ij}\partial_{k}\delta\mu_{ik}\biggr)\,dx

and is manifestly semi-definite,
regardless of the definiteness properties of the physical tensor of elastic moduli

.

An important corollary of the above observation is that if the primal base state
(\bar{y},\bar{F})
is chosen as a solution to the primal problem - even in a situation where the physical moduli field

takes positive-definite and negative definite values in distinct parts of the domain so that the

- a corresponding dual solution, given explicitly by the dual state
\bar{D}
, is neutrally stable to the class of variations considered and, in the sense of the DtP correspondence adopted here, confers a certain stability to the primal solution, when viewed as obtained from the dual variational principle.

3
Dual nonlinear Elastodynamics

An appealing feature of the dual methodology is that it seamlessly extends to time-dependent primal problems, leading to well-defined boundary-value-problems in space-time domains. We consider the system of elastodynamic equations

\displaystyle\partial_{j}P_{ij}(F)+b_{i}-\rho_{0}\,\partial_{t}v_{i}

\displaystyle=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T)

(10a)

\displaystyle\partial_{j}y_{i}-F_{ij}

\displaystyle=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T)

(10b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}y_{i}-v_{i}

\displaystyle=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T)

(10c)

\displaystyle P_{ij}n_{j}=p_{i}\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{p}\times(0,T)\qquad

\displaystyle;\qquad y_{i}=y^{(b)}_{i}\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{y}\times(0,T)

(10d)

\displaystyle\left.y_{i}(x,0)=y_{i}^{(0)}\right|_{x}\quad

\displaystyle;\quad\left.v_{i}(x,0)=v_{i}^{(0)}\right|_{x}\ \mbox{on}\ \Omega,

(10e)

where
[0,T]
is an interval of time,
P
satisfies the same conditions as in Sec.
2
and
\rho_{0}
is a given time-independent, positive, scalar-valued function on
\Omega
representing the mass density in the reference configuration.

Following the steps of Sec.
2
, we define the pre-dual functional

\displaystyle\widehat{S}_{H}[v,y,F,\lambda,\gamma,\mu]=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int^{T}_{0}\left(-P_{ij}\big|_{F}\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{i}b_{i}+\rho_{0}\,v_{i}\partial_{t}\lambda_{i}\right)\,dxdt\quad+\quad\int_{\Omega}\int^{T}_{0}(-y_{i}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}-F_{ij}\mu_{ij})\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\int^{T}_{0}\left(-y_{i}\partial_{t}\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{i}v_{i}\right)\,dxdt\quad+\quad\int_{\Omega}\int^{T}_{0}H(v,y,F;x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\int^{T}_{0}p_{i}\lambda_{i}\,da\ +\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}\int^{T}_{0}y^{(b)}_{i}\mu_{ij}n_{j}\,da+\int_{\Omega}v^{(0)}_{i}(x)\lambda_{i}(x,0)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}y^{(0)}_{i}(x)\gamma_{i}(x,0)\,dx.

In the above, for any boundary term obtained on integration by parts, all primal boundary conditions and initial conditions are imposed. Any integral on a part of the boundary of the space-time region
\Omega\times(0,T)
where no primal ‘boundary’ data is available is simply ignored at this stage.

We define the boundary of the space-time domain as
{\cal B}:=(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))\cup(\Omega\times\{0,T\})

Defining now

\displaystyle U:=(v,y,F);\qquad D:=(\lambda,\gamma,\mu);\qquad\mathcal{D}:=(\nabla\lambda,\lambda,\partial_{t}\lambda,\nabla\mu,\mu,\partial_{t}\gamma,\gamma);

(11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{H}(U,\mathcal{D},x,t):=-P_{ij}\big|_{F}\,\partial_{j}\lambda_{i}+b_{i}\lambda_{i}+\rho_{0}v_{i}\partial_{t}\lambda_{i}-y_{i}\partial_{j}\mu_{ij}-\mu_{ij}F_{ij}-y_{i}\partial_{t}\gamma_{i}-v_{i}\gamma_{i}+H(v,y,F;x,t),

and requiring the choice of the potential
H
to be such that it facilitates the existence of a function

U=U_{H}(\mathcal{D},x,t)

which satisfies

\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{H}}{\partial U}\left(U_{H}(\mathcal{D},x,t),\mathcal{D},x,t\right)=0\qquad\forall\ (\mathcal{D},x,t),

\mathbb{A}_{\Gamma\alpha\Pi\mu}(\nabla D,D,\bar{U}):=\frac{\partial{\cal F}_{\Gamma\alpha}}{\partial U_{P}}\bigg|_{U(\nabla D,D,\bar{U})}\,\frac{\partial U_{P}}{\partial(\nabla D)_{\Pi\mu}}\bigg|_{U(\nabla D,D,\bar{U})}.

From (

\displaystyle a^{-1}_{PR}\left(\delta_{\Gamma\Pi}\delta_{\mu\alpha}\frac{\partial{\cal F}_{\Gamma\alpha}}{\partial U_{R}}\,+\,\partial_{\alpha}D_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal F}_{\Gamma\alpha}}{\partial U_{R}\partial U_{S}}\frac{\partial U_{S}}{\partial(\nabla D)_{\Pi\mu}}-D_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial^{2}G_{\Gamma}}{\partial U_{R}\partial U_{S}}\frac{\partial U_{S}}{\partial(\nabla D)_{\Pi\mu}}\right)

If a solution of the primal system is close to the base state

5
Variational Analysis of dual nonlinear Elastostatics

In the remaining sections of the paper we will assume that the body force

.

In this section, we will follow the approach outlined in Section
2
and study the corresponding dual functional
S_{H}
.
First, note that under sufficient growth and regularity assumptions on
H
the relation (
4

U_{H}(\mathcal{D},x)
maximizes
-\mathcal{L}_{H}(\cdot,\mathcal{D},x)
for fixed values of
(\mathcal{D},x)

If for all
D
the maximizer is unique then we call the mapping
(\mathcal{D}(x),x)\mapsto U_{H}(\mathcal{D}(x),x)
the dual-to-primal mapping.
With a little abuse of notation we also use the same notation
U_{H}
for the mapping between functions defined for a pair of functions
D=(\lambda,\mu)
as
D\to(x\to U_{H}(\mathcal{D}(x),x))
.
In this setting the dual functional can equivalently be written as (c.f. Proposition
5.1
, (
29

31

\tilde{S}_{H}[D]:=\sup_{y,F}-\widehat{S}_{H}[y,F,\lambda,\mu]=-S_{H}[D],

(22)

but the definition of
\tilde{S}_{H}
makes sense more generally even in the absence of a well-defined mapping
U_{H}
, which suffices for the notion of a variational dual solution defined below. It is this definition of
\tilde{S}_{H}
that we adopt in this section.
We note that the functional
\hat{S}_{H}
is affine in the functions
\lambda
,
\nabla\lambda
,
\operatorname{div}(\mu)
and
\mu
.
In particular, if formulated in appropriate spaces it will be continuous with respect to weak convergence of these quantities.
This implies that
\tilde{S}_{H}
as the
\sup
over these weakly continuous functions is weakly lower semicontinuous.
This renders the dual problem
\tilde{S}_{H}
to be rather well-suited for applying the direct method of the Calculus of Variations to prove the existence of a minimizer.
If the dual functional
\tilde{S}_{H}
is regular enough, such a minimizer would then satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equations of
S_{H}
in a weak form (which are the same as for
\tilde{S}_{H}
) which correspond to the equations (
1
) if there is a well-defined mapping from the dual states to the primal states as explained above.
The direct method of the Calculus of Variations essentially corresponds to performing the following three steps:

1.

Show that
\inf_{\lambda,\mu}\tilde{S}_{H}>-\infty
and take a minimizing sequence
(\lambda_{k},\mu_{k})
such that
\tilde{S}_{H}(\lambda_{k},\mu_{k})\to\inf_{\lambda,\mu}\tilde{S}
.

2.

Prove up to a non-relabeled subsequence (weak) compactness for the minimizing sequence
(\lambda_{k},\mu_{k})\rightharpoonup(\lambda,\mu)
.

3.

Show that
S_{H}
is sequentially lower-semicontinuous with respect to the convergence established in 2. to conclude that
\tilde{S}_{H}(\lambda,\mu)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\tilde{S}_{H}(\lambda_{k},\mu_{k})=\inf_{\lambda,\mu}\tilde{S}_{H}
i.e.,
(\lambda,\mu)
minimizes
\tilde{S}_{H}
.

Note that by the argumentation above the dual functional
\tilde{S}_{H}
is (at least formally) lower semi-continuous with respect to weak convergence.
Consequently, the main work in this setting is to establish step 2.

Let us also note that as the function
\hat{S}_{H}
is affine in
\lambda
and
\mu
the function
\tilde{S}_{H}
as the supremum over affine functions is automatically convex (however, most likely not strictly convex).
This means that there is a large toolbox for the computational task to find a minimizer of the dual functional
\tilde{S}_{H}
.

In light of the discussion above, we define the notion of a
variational dual solution
to the equation (
1
) and a

.

Definition 5.1
.

Given a Borel-measurable function
H
we say that a pair
(\lambda,\mu)
is a variational dual solution to the equation (
1
) if it minimizes the dual functional
\tilde{S}_{H}
.
Moreover, we say that a pair
(\lambda,\mu)
is a dual solution to (
1
) if the dual functional
\tilde{S}_{H}
is differentiable at
(\lambda,\mu)
, the pair
(\lambda,\mu)
satisfies the weak form of the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation for
\tilde{S}_{H}
and there exists a smooth dual-to-primal mapping

corresponding to
\tilde{S}_{H}
.

By the considerations above, a dual solution gives rise to a weak solution of (
1
).
Note that for the notion of a variational dual solution in principle we do not require

to satisfy any additional assumption.
However, the notion of a dual variational solution is only meaningful if the function
H
is chosen in a way such that the dual functional
\tilde{S}_{H}
allows for nontrivial minimizers.
For example, for
H=0
it is easy to check that for the examples discussed below it follows

except for

In particular, the dual functional is minimized at
(0,0)
but every pair of primal variables is a maximizer for the middle term of (

This means that not every variational dual solution corresponds to a dual solution in the sense of Definition
5.1
or gives rise to a weak solution of (
1

Consequently, ideally we would like to find a function

such that the dual functional
\tilde{S}_{H}
is regular and the dual-to-primal mapping is well-defined and regular.
In order to obtain these two properties, it appears to be reasonable (but in most cases not sufficient) to choose

such that

has a strictly positive definite Hessian at all points (in particular
H
is convex) and the growth of
H
at

is not smaller than the growth of the nonlinearity

so that a maximizer for the problem in the middle of (

In the case of a hyperelastic materials the typical variational strategy to obtain a weak solution to (
1

on
\Omega_{y}
. Much research in this direction was inspired by the seminal works on functionals with quasiconvex
[

or polyconvex energy densities
[

]

However, these notions of convexity do not hold for numerous classical models, e.g. the Saint Venant - Kirchhoff model discussed in the section below.
In addition, even if existence of a minimizer for the free energy can be established, it is not clear that the minimizer satisfies the weak formulation of the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation, see the discussion in
[
7

and the references therein (in the case of a convex integrand see, for example,

for a positive result).

Hence, we propose the concept of a variational dual solution as another variational solution concept for nonlinear elastostatics given by (

In the following we will show that the concept of variational dual solutions is applicable in a meaningful way to some standard models, namely, we will prove, for auxiliary potentials

that render the dual functional

non-trivial, existence of variational dual solutions to (

We will focus on proving coercivity for the dual functionals

which guarantees that step 2. in the direct method can be performed.
In these examples the formal argument for the lower semi-continuity of the dual functional

can also be made rigorous and hence existence of minimizers of

can be established rigorously.

Following the arguments in the beginning of Section

one can set up analogously a dual variational problem in the elastodynamic setting. The dual variational problem is again (formally) lower semi-continuous and convex. Understanding the coercivity of the dual variational problem in the dynamical setting, which would in turn give rise to the existence of the analogue of dual variational solutions, is currently work in progress.

In the following we will assume that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is open, connected and bounded with Lipschitz boundary.

5.1
The Saint Venant-Kirchhoff model

We consider the Saint Venant-Kirchhoff energy

W(F)=\mathsf{G}|E|^{2}+\frac{\mathsf{L}}{2}\operatorname{tr}(E)^{2},

where
C=F^{T}F
is the right Cauchy-Green tensor,

is the Green-Lagrange strain tensor, the shear modulus
\mathsf{G}>0

\mathsf{G}+\frac{d}{2}\mathsf{L}>0

Correspondingly, we set

P(F)=DW(F)=F\left(\mathsf{G}\,F^{T}F+\frac{\mathsf{L}}{2}|F|^{2}Id-(\mathsf{G}+\frac{\mathsf{L}d}{2})Id\right).

For
y^{(b)}\in W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

we look for weak solutions to the equations (
1

\begin{cases}&F=\nabla y\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&\operatorname{div}P(F)=0\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&P(F)\cdot n=p^{(b)}\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{p},\\
&y=y^{(b)}\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{y}.\end{cases}

(23)

Precisely, we look for
y\in W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
F\in L^{4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
such that for all

with
\lambda=0

\partial\Omega_{y}
and

and

\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\mu)\cdot y+\mu:F+P(F):\nabla\lambda\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}\cdot\mu n\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}+\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\lambda\cdot p^{(b)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.

Note that the boundary terms are well-defined but have to be understood in the sense of traces i.e., for

there are continuous trace operators

and
\{\mu\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):\operatorname{div}(\mu)\in L^{p}\}\to\left(W^{1-1/p}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)^{\prime}
,
\mu\to\mu n
, see
[
22

and
[
14

for the case

Next, let us mention that the stored energy density

is not rank-
1

[
21
]
). In particular, the corresponding stored elastic energy is not weakly lower semi-continuous and existence for (
23
) via the minimization of the corresponding energy cannot be obtained by the direct method of the Calculus of Variations, c.f. the discussion in the preamble of this section.

Following the approach explained above we define for

H(y,F)=\frac{1}{4}|y|^{4}+\frac{\mathsf{G}+\mathsf{L}/2}{2}|F^{T}F|^{2}

the function
g:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

g(A,a,B)=\sup_{F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\cdot y+A:F+B:P(F)-H(y,F)

and consider the functional
I:\{(\mu,\lambda)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):\operatorname{div}(\mu)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu n=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{p}\text{ and }\lambda=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{y}\}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

I(\mu,\lambda)=\int_{\Omega}g(\mu,\operatorname{div}(\mu),\nabla\lambda)\,dx-\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}\cdot\mu n\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}-\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\lambda\cdot p^{(b)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.

(24)

We will first show that
I
agrees with the dual functional
\tilde{S}_{H}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

It holds for all
\mu\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
\lambda\in W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
that

\tilde{S}_{H}[\lambda,\mu]=\int_{\Omega}g(\mu,\operatorname{div}(\mu),\nabla\lambda)\,dx-\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}\cdot\mu n\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}-\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\lambda\cdot p^{(b)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for
\mu\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
\lambda\in W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
that it holds

\sup_{y\in L^{4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),F\in L^{4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\mu)\cdot y+\mu:F+P(F):\nabla\lambda-H(y,F)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}g(\mu,\operatorname{div}(\mu),\nabla\lambda)\,dx.

(25)

Clearly, it holds that

\sup_{y\in L^{4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),F\in L^{4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\mu)\cdot y+\mu:F+P(F):\nabla\lambda-H(y,F)\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}g(\mu,\operatorname{div}(\mu),\nabla\lambda)\,dx.

We will now prove the reverse inequality.
Let
\mathcal{O}=\{O\subseteq\Omega:O\text{ open}\}
and consider the countable family of functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\{(y,F):

\displaystyle F=\sum_{n=1}^{N}q_{n}\chi_{E_{n}}\text{ and }y=\sum_{n=1}^{N}q_{n}^{\prime}\chi_{E_{n}},\text{ where }q_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d\times d},\,q_{n}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}\text{ and }

(26)

\displaystyle E_{n}\text{ is a cuboid with rational side lengths and corners in }\mathbb{Q}^{d}\}.

(27)

Note that
\mathcal{A}
is countable. Moreover, it holds for all
(a,A,B)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
x\in\Omega

\sup_{y,F\in\mathcal{D}}a\cdot y(x)+A:F(x)+P(F(x)):B-H(y(x),F(x))=g(A,a,B).

For
O\in\mathcal{O}
let us now define
\mathcal{F}(O)=\sup_{y\in L^{4},F\in L^{4}}\int_{O}y\cdot\operatorname{div}(\mu)+\mu:F+P(F):\nabla\lambda-H(y,F)\,dx
.
It can be shown that for
O,O^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}
such that
\operatorname{dist}(O,O^{\prime})>0
it follows that

\mathcal{F}(O\cup O^{\prime})=\mathcal{F}(O)+\mathcal{F}(O^{\prime}).

Moreover, it holds by definition for each
(y,F)\in\mathcal{A}
and
O\in\mathcal{O}
that

\mathcal{F}(O)\geq\int_{O}y\cdot\operatorname{div}(\mu)+\mu:F+P(F):\nabla\lambda-H(y,F)\,dx.

Then it follows from
[
10
, Lemma 15.2]
that

\mathcal{F}(\Omega)\geq\int_{\Omega}\sup_{(y,F)\in\mathcal{A}}\left[y\cdot\operatorname{div}(\mu)+\mu:F+P(F):\nabla\lambda-H(y,F)\right]\,dx=\int_{\Omega}g(\mu,\operatorname{div}(\mu),\nabla\lambda)\,dx.

This proves the reverse inequality.
∎

As explained above, in order to prove existence of minimizers for
\tilde{S}_{H}
it suffices by the direct method of the Calculus of Variations to prove that
\tilde{S}_{H}
is coercive. Hence, we will now estimate the function
g
from below.

Proposition 5.2
.

There exists
c>0
such that it holds for all
(a,A,B)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

g(A,a,B)\geq c\left(|a|^{4/3}+|A|^{4/3}+|B|^{4}\right)-\frac{1}{c}.

Proof.

By optimizing in
y
it follows that

\displaystyle g(A,a,B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}|a|^{4/3}+\sup_{F}\left[\left(A-(\mathsf{G}+\frac{\mathsf{L}d}{2})B\right):F+B:\left(\mathsf{G}\,FF^{T}F+\frac{\mathsf{L}}{2}|F|^{2}F\right)-\frac{\mathsf{G}+\mathsf{L}/2}{2}|F^{T}F|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}|a|^{4/3}+2(\mathsf{G}+\mathsf{L}/2)\sup_{F}\left[\tilde{A}:F+\frac{1}{2(\mathsf{G}+\mathsf{L}/2)}B:\left(\mathsf{G}\,FF^{T}F+\frac{\mathsf{L}}{2}|F|^{2}F\right)-\frac{1}{4}|F^{T}F|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}|a|^{4/3}+2(\mathsf{G}+\mathsf{L}/2)h(\tilde{A},B).

where
\tilde{A}=\frac{1}{2\mathsf{G}+\mathsf{L}}\left(A-(\mathsf{G}+\frac{\mathsf{L}d}{2})B\right)
.
Next, we estimate
h
from below.

1.

If
|B|^{3}\leq\frac{27}{8^{3}}|\tilde{A}|
we find for
F=|\tilde{A}|^{-2/3}\tilde{A}
that

\displaystyle h(\tilde{A},B)

\displaystyle\geq|\tilde{A}|^{4/3}-|\tilde{A}||B|-\frac{1}{4}|F|^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}|\tilde{A}|^{4/3}-|\tilde{A}||B|\geq\frac{3}{8}|\tilde{A}|^{4/3}\geq\frac{3}{16}(|\tilde{A}|^{4/3}+|B|^{4}),

where we used that
|F^{T}F|^{2}\leq|F|^{4}
.

2.

If
\frac{27}{8^{3}}|\tilde{A}|<|B|^{3}\leq\frac{8d}{9}|\tilde{A}|
we find for
F=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}|B|^{-1/2}|\tilde{A}|^{-1/2}\tilde{A}
that

\displaystyle h(\tilde{A},B)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}|\tilde{A}|^{3/2}|B|^{-1/2}-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{3}}|\tilde{A}|^{3/2}|B|^{-1/2}-\frac{1}{36}|\tilde{A}|^{2}|B|^{-2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}-\frac{1}{36}\sqrt{\frac{8^{3}}{27}}\right)|\tilde{A}|^{3/2}|B|^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}-\frac{1}{36}\sqrt{\frac{8^{3}}{27}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{9}{8d}\right)^{1/6}|\tilde{A}|^{4/3}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{9}{8d}\right)^{3/2}|B|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\geq c\left(|\tilde{A}|^{4/3}+|B|^{4}\right),

where
c>0
is a universal constant.

3.

If
\frac{8d}{9}|\tilde{A}|<|B|^{3}
we find for
F=3B
using
\frac{1}{d}|B|^{4}\leq|B^{T}B|^{2}\leq|B|^{4}
that

\displaystyle h(\tilde{A},B)

\displaystyle\geq-3|\tilde{A}||B|+27|B^{T}B|^{2}-\frac{81}{4}|B^{T}B|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{27}{4d}|B|^{4}-3|\tilde{A}||B|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{27}{8d}|B|^{4}\geq\frac{27}{16d}|B|^{4}+\frac{27}{16d}\left(\frac{8d}{9}\right)^{4/3}|\tilde{A}|^{4/3}\geq c\left(|\tilde{A}|^{4/3}+|B|^{4}\right),

where
c>0
is a universal constant.

Consequently, we find for a constant
c>0
(that depends on
\mathsf{G}
and
\mathsf{L}
and might change from line to line) that

\displaystyle g(A,a,B)

\displaystyle\geq c\left(|a|^{4/3}+|\tilde{A}|^{4/3}+|B|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\geq c\left(|a|^{4/3}+|A|^{4/3}-|B|^{4/3}+|B|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\geq c\left(|a|^{4/3}+|A|^{4/3}+|B|^{4}\right)-\frac{1}{c}.

(28)

∎

Remark 5.1
.

Similarly, one can show that it also holds

g(A,a,B)\leq C\left(|a|^{4/3}+|A|^{4/3}+|B|^{4}+1\right),

which shows that the dual functional
I=\tilde{S}_{H}
is well-defined and its minimization is non-trivial.

Let us now show the coercivity of the dual functional
I=\tilde{S}_{H}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

The functional
I
as defined in (
24
) is weakly coercive i.e., for every sequence
(\mu_{k},\lambda_{k})_{k}\subseteq\{(\mu,\lambda)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):\operatorname{div}(\mu)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu n=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{p}\text{ and }\lambda=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{y}\}
such that
\sup_{k}I(\mu_{k},\lambda_{k})<\infty
there exists a (not relabeled) subsequence and
(\mu,\lambda)\in\{(\mu,\lambda)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):\operatorname{div}(\mu)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu n=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{p}\text{ and }\lambda=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{y}\}
such that

\mu_{k}\rightharpoonup\mu\text{ in }L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),\;\operatorname{div}(\mu_{k})\rightharpoonup\operatorname{div}(\mu)\text{ in }L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ and }\lambda_{k}\rightharpoonup\lambda\text{ in }W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

Let us first recall Poincaré’s inequality
\|\lambda\|_{W^{1,4}}\leq C\|\nabla\lambda\|_{L^{4}}
for all
\lambda\in W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\lambda=0
on
\partial\Omega_{y}
.
Hence, we obtain from Proposition
5.2
that

\displaystyle I(\mu_{k},\lambda_{k})\geq

\displaystyle c\left(\|\operatorname{div}(\mu_{k})\|_{L^{4/3}}^{4/3}+\|\mu_{k}\|_{L^{4/3}}^{4/3}+\|\nabla\lambda_{k}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\right)-\frac{1}{c}\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega)

\displaystyle-\|\mu_{k}n\|_{(W^{1-1/4,4}(\partial\Omega))^{\prime}}\|y^{(b)}\|_{W^{1-1/4,4}(\partial\Omega)}-\|\lambda_{k}\|_{L^{4}(\partial\Omega)}\|p^{(b)}\|_{L^{4/3}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c\left(\|\operatorname{div}(\mu_{k})\|_{L^{4/3}}^{4/3}+\|\mu_{k}\|_{L^{4/3}}^{4/3}+\|\lambda_{k}\|_{W^{1,4}}^{4}\right)-\frac{1}{c}\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega)

\displaystyle-C\left(\|\operatorname{div}(\mu_{k})\|_{L^{4/3}}+\|\mu_{k}\|_{L^{4/3}}+\|\lambda_{k}\|_{W^{1,4}}\right).

Since
\sup_{k}I(\mu_{k},\lambda_{k})<\infty
, it follows immediately that
\sup_{k}\|\operatorname{div}(\mu_{k})\|_{L^{4/3}}+\|\mu_{k}\|_{L^{4/3}}+\|\lambda_{k}\|_{W^{1,4}}<\infty
. This implies the existence of
\mu\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
\nu\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\lambda\in W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that (up to a not relabeled subsequence) it holds
\mu_{k}\rightharpoonup\mu
in
L^{4/3}
,
\operatorname{div}(\mu_{k})\rightharpoonup\nu
in
L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\lambda_{k}\rightharpoonup\lambda
in
W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then it is straightforward to check that
\operatorname{div}(\mu)=\nu\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, as the trace operators are weakly continuous it follows that
\mu n=0
on
\partial\Omega_{p}
and
\lambda=0
on
\partial\Omega_{y}
in the sense of traces as discussed at the beginning of this section.
∎

5.2
Incompressible Neo-Hookean model in two dimensions

Let us now consider the stress in an incompressible Neo-Hookean model

P(F,p)=\operatorname{det}(F)\left(\mathsf{G}\,F-pF^{-T}\right),

where
\mathsf{G}>0
is the shear modulus,
\operatorname{det}(F)=1
and
p
is the pressure which is to be determined.
For simplicity, let
\mathsf{G}=1
. Moreover, set

\tilde{P}(F,p)=F-p\operatorname{cof}(F).

Note that in two dimensions
\tilde{P}
depends linearly on
F
and for
F
with
\operatorname{det}(F)=1
it holds
\tilde{P}(F,p)=P(F,p)
.
Then for
y^{(b)}\in W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
p^{(b)}\in L^{4/3}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
we look for weak solutions to

\begin{cases}F=\nabla y\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\operatorname{det}(F)=1\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\operatorname{div}\tilde{P}(F,p)=0\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\tilde{P}(F,p)\cdot n=p^{(b)}\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{p},\\
y=y^{(b)}\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{y}.\end{cases}

Namely, we seek to find
y\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
F\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
such that it holds for all
\mu\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
with
\operatorname{div}(\mu)\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
\lambda\in W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and
\vartheta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
that

\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\mu)\cdot y+\mu:F+\vartheta(\operatorname{det}(F)-1)+\nabla\lambda:\tilde{P}(F)\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\lambda\cdot p^{(b)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}+\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}\cdot\mu n\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

As explained for the Saint Venant-Kirchhoff model, the boundary integrals have to be understood in the sense of traces.

Note that compared to the general discussion in Section
2
the additional constraint
\operatorname{det}(F)=1
is treated through an extra dual variable
\vartheta
, whereas the pressure
p
is an additional primal variable.
Following the same approach, we define the function
g:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

g(A,a,B,s)=\sup_{F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},p\in\mathbb{R}}a\cdot y+s(\operatorname{det}(F)-1)+A:F+B:P(F,p)-H(y,p,F),

where
H(y,p,F)=\frac{1}{2}y^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|F|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}p^{4}
.
Correspondingly, we define the functional

\displaystyle I:\big\{(\mu,\lambda,\vartheta)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})

\displaystyle\times W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{\infty}(\Omega):

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(\mu)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu n=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{p}\text{ and }\lambda=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{y}\big\}\to\mathbb{R}

by

I(\mu,\lambda,\vartheta)=\int_{\Omega}g(\mu,\operatorname{div}(\mu),\nabla\lambda,\vartheta)\,dx-\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}\cdot\mu n\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}-\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\lambda\cdot p^{(b)}\,\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.

(29)

By the same argument as for the Saint Venant - Kirchhoff material in Proposition
5.1
it holds for all
(\mu,\lambda,\vartheta)\in\{(\mu,\lambda,\vartheta)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{\infty}(\Omega):\operatorname{div}(\mu)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu n=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{p}\text{ and }\lambda=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{y}\}
that

\displaystyle I(\mu,\lambda,\vartheta)

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),F\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}),p\in L^{4}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\mu)\cdot y+\vartheta(\operatorname{det}(F)-1)+\mu:F+\nabla\lambda:P(F,p)-H(y,p,F)\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\lambda\cdot p^{(b)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}-\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}\cdot\mu n\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

(31)

The right hand side of the above equation is the analogue of
\tilde{S}_{H}
including the extra variable
\vartheta
.

As in the section above, in order to show existence of minimizers of
I
it suffices to show that
I
is coercive.
Again, we first prove lower bounds for the function
g
.

Proposition 5.4
.

There exist constants
C>c>0
such that the following is true.

1.

If
|s|>1
then
g(A,a,B,s)=+\infty
for all
(A,a,B)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.

2.

If
|s|\leq 1
then it holds for
(A,a,B)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

g(A,a,B,s)\geq c(|a|^{2}+|A+B|^{2}+|B|^{4})-1

3.

If
|s|\leq 1
then it holds for
(A,a,B)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
that

g(A,a,B,s)\leq C\left(|a|^{2}+\frac{1}{1-|s|}|A+B|^{2}+\frac{1}{(1-|s|)^{2}}|B|^{4}+1\right).

Proof.

First note that by optimizing in
y
we find

\displaystyle g(A,a,B,s)=

\displaystyle\sup_{F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},p\in\mathbb{R}}a\cdot y+s(\operatorname{det}(F)-1)+A:F+B:P(F,p)-H(y,p,F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|a|^{2}-s+\sup_{F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},p\in\mathbb{R}}s\operatorname{det}(F)+A:F+B:P(F,p)-\frac{1}{4}p^{4}-\frac{1}{2}|F|^{2}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|a|^{2}-s+h(A,B,s).

(32)

We will now only work with the function
h
.
We observe that it holds for all
F\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
that
F:\operatorname{cof}(F)=2\operatorname{det}(F)
.
In particular,
|\operatorname{\det}(F)|\leq\frac{1}{2}|F|^{2}
.
Let us now assume that
s>1
. Then consider for
t>0
the matrix
F=tId
. In addition, set
p=0
. It follows that

h(A,B,s)\geq st^{2}-2|A+B|t-t^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}+\infty.

The case
s<-1
can be treated similarly. This shows
1
.
Next, let us consider
|s|\leq 1
. First we assume that
\frac{1}{4\sqrt{8}}|B|^{2}\leq|A+B|
. Then set
F=(A+B)/2
and
p=0
to obtain

\displaystyle h(A,B,s)\geq(A+B):F-\frac{1}{2}|F|^{2}+s\operatorname{det}(F)\geq\frac{|A+B|^{2}}{2}-|F|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|A+B|^{2}}{4}

(33)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{512}\left(|A+B|^{2}+|B|^{4}\right).

For
|A+B|\leq\frac{1}{4\sqrt{8}}|B|^{2}
consider
F\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
such that
\operatorname{cof}(F)=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}|B|B
and
p=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|B|
. Then

\displaystyle h(A,B,s)

\displaystyle\geq(A+B):F-B:(p\operatorname{cof}(F))+s\operatorname{det}(F)-\frac{1}{4}p^{4}-\frac{1}{2}|F|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(A+B):F-B:(p\operatorname{cof}(F))-\frac{1}{4}p^{4}-|F|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}|A+B|\,|B|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|B|^{4}-\frac{1}{16}|B|^{4}-\frac{1}{8}|B|^{4}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{32}|B|^{4}+\frac{1}{16}|B|^{4}=\frac{1}{32}|B|^{4}\geq\frac{1}{64}\left(|A+B|^{2}+|B|^{4}\right)

(34)

Combining (
32
), (
33
) and (
34
) yields

g(A,a,B,s)\geq\frac{1}{2}|a|^{2}-|s|+h(A,B,s)\geq c(|a|^{2}+|A+B|^{2}+|B|^{4})-1,

which is
2
.

It remains to show
3
.
We estimate

\displaystyle h(A,B,s)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{F\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},p\in\mathbb{R}}F:(A+B-p\operatorname{cof}(B))-\frac{1}{4}p^{4}-\frac{1-|s|}{2}|F|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{2(1-|s|)}|A+B-p\operatorname{cof}(B)|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}p^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-|s|)}|A+B|^{2}+\sup_{p\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{p^{2}}{(1-|s|)}|\operatorname{cof}(B)|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}p^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-|s|)}|A+B|^{2}+\frac{1}{(1-|s|)^{2}}|B|^{4}.

Combining this with (
32
) yields
3
.
∎

Eventually, we state the coercivity statement for the dual functional
I
.

Proposition 5.5
.

The functional
I
as defined in (
29
) is weakly coercive i.e., for every sequence
(\mu_{k},\lambda_{k},\vartheta_{k})_{k}\subseteq\{(\mu,\lambda,\vartheta)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{\infty}(\Omega):\operatorname{div}(\mu)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu n=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{p}\text{ and }\lambda=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{y}\}
such that
\sup_{k}I(\mu_{k},\lambda_{k},\vartheta_{k})<\infty
there exists a (not relabeled) subsequence and
(\mu,\lambda,\vartheta)\in\{(\mu,\lambda,\vartheta)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{\infty}(\Omega):\operatorname{div}(\mu)\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu n=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{p}\text{ and }\lambda=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{y}\}

\displaystyle\mu_{k}\rightharpoonup\mu\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),\;\operatorname{div}(\mu_{k})\rightharpoonup\operatorname{div}(\mu)\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\;\lambda_{k}\rightharpoonup\lambda\text{ in }W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Proposition
5.3
using Proposition
5.4

1
. and
2
.
∎

We end this section with a brief comment on changing the base states.

Remark 5.2
.

Let us fix
\tilde{y}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\tilde{F}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
and note for
\tilde{H}(F,y,p;x)=H(F-\tilde{F}(x),y-\tilde{y}(x),p)
that

\displaystyle S_{\tilde{H}}(\lambda,\mu,\vartheta)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in L^{2},F\in L^{2},p\in L^{4}}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\mu)\cdot y+\vartheta(\operatorname{det}(F)-1)+\mu:F+\nabla\lambda:P(F,p)-\tilde{H}(F,y,p;x)\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\lambda\cdot p^{(b)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}-\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}\cdot\mu n\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in L^{2},F\in L^{2},p\in L^{4}}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\mu)\cdot y+\vartheta(\operatorname{det}(F)-1)+\mu:F+\nabla\lambda:P(F,p)-\tilde{H}(F,y,p;x)\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\lambda\cdot p^{(b)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}-\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}\cdot\mu n\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in L^{2},F\in L^{2},p\in L^{4}}\int_{\Omega}(\operatorname{div}(\mu)-\tilde{y})\cdot y+\vartheta(\operatorname{det}(F)-1)+(\mu+\nabla\lambda-p\operatorname{cof}(\nabla\lambda)-\tilde{F}):F-H(F,y,p)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\tilde{F}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\tilde{y}|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\lambda\cdot p^{(b)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}-\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}\cdot\mu n\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g(\mu-\tilde{F},\operatorname{div}(\mu)-\tilde{y},\nabla\lambda,\vartheta)\,dx-\int_{\partial\Omega_{y}}y^{(b)}\cdot\mu n\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}-\int_{\partial\Omega_{p}}\lambda\cdot p^{(b)}\,\mathcal{H}^{d-1}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\tilde{F}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\tilde{y}|^{2}\,dx.

This functional has the same coercivity and lower-semicontinuity properties as
\tilde{S}_{H}
. Hence, existence of minimizers for this functional is also guaranteed. The question of whether such a minimzer gives rise to a weak solution of the primal problem hinges on the regularity of the function
g
on the values of a minimizer. However, Proposition
5.4
shows that at least at
\vartheta=\pm 1
the function
g
is not differentiable. Making good choices for
\tilde{F}
and
\tilde{y}
could enable us to guarantee that a minimizer
(\lambda,\mu,\vartheta)
of
\tilde{S}_{\tilde{H}}
only takes values such that
g
is actually differentiable in the points
(\mu-\tilde{F},\operatorname{div}(\mu)-\tilde{y},\nabla\lambda,\vartheta)
.

6
A computational case study: non-convex elasticity in 1-d

In this section, we present computational approximations of our dual scheme applied to nonconvex elastostatics and elastodynamics in 1 space dimension and time; this provides intuition for its capabilities and evidence for its practical feasibility.

The specific variational and PDE problems we study in the elastostatic case are described in Sec.
6.1
. The finite element method based algorithm used to solve the problem is developed in Sec.
6.2
. Results of the computational studies are presented and discussed in Sec.
6.3
. In particular, in Sec.
6.3.4
is presented and discussed the stability of some dual solutions to elastodynamic problems that are ‘Hadamard unstable’ in the primal, classical elasticity formulation, in the sense of lacking continuous dependence on initial data because some regions of the domain have initial conditions where hyperbolicity is lost. While it is
not
our goal to discuss the (de)merits of conventional elasticity theory to model such situations, we give a physical interpretation of the problem that is studied. This section also contains a brief description of the extension of the computational scheme to elastodynamics.

6.1
Formulation

We computationally approximate solutions to a double-well elastostatics problem in 1 space dimension without any higher-order (strain-gradient) regularization. The objective is to compute critical points of the following energy functional:

I=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\left(u_{x}-1\right)^{2}-1\right)^{2}+\left(u-\alpha x\right)^{2}\,dx;\qquad u(0)=0;\quad u(1)=\alpha^{*}.

(35)

We use the notation
\partial_{i}a=a_{i},i=x,t
, and
\alpha,\alpha^{*}
are specified constants. The function
u
corresponds to the displacement. The Euler-Lagrange equations of (
35
) are

\displaystyle u_{x}-e

\displaystyle=0

(36a)

\displaystyle 2\partial_{x}\left((e-1)^{3}-(e-1)\right)-\left(u-\alpha x\right)

\displaystyle=0

(36b)

\displaystyle u(0)=0

\displaystyle;\qquad u(1)=\alpha^{*},

(36c)

where
e
corresponds to the strain. The stress,
\sigma
, for this model is given by

\sigma=4(e-1)\left((e-1)^{2}-1)\right).

Using the dual (Lagrange multiplier) fields
\lambda
and
\mu
for the two equations, we define the pre-dual functional

\widehat{S}\,\left[u,\lambda,e,\mu\right]\,=\,\int_{0}^{1}\,\,(-u\lambda_{x}-\lambda e-2((e-1)^{3}-(e-1))\mu_{x}-\mu(u-\alpha x)+H(u,e))\,dx+\alpha^{*}\lambda(1),

where the requirement on the function
H
is that, defining

\mathcal{D}:=\left(\lambda,\lambda_{x},\mu,\mu_{x}\right),

the following equations are solvable for
u
,
e
in terms of
\mathcal{D}:

\displaystyle-\lambda_{x}-\mu+\partial_{u}H

\displaystyle=0

(37)

\displaystyle-2(3(e-1)^{2}-1)\mu_{x}-\lambda+\partial_{e}H

\displaystyle=0.

Choosing a ‘shifted quadratic’ and ‘shifted cubic’ form for the potential
H
:

H(u,e;x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{u}(u-\bar{u}(x))^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{e}(e-\bar{e}(x))^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\left(c_{e}(e-\bar{e}(x))^{3}\right),

we obtain the following
dual-to-primal
(DtP) mapping equations

\displaystyle u^{(H)}(\lambda_{x},\mu,x)=\frac{\lambda_{x}+\mu}{c_{u}}+\bar{u}(x)

(38)

\displaystyle-6\left(e^{(H)}(\mu_{x},\lambda,x)-1\right)^{2}\mu_{x}+2\mu_{x}-\lambda

\displaystyle+c_{e}\left(e^{(H)}(\mu_{x},\lambda,x)-\bar{e}(x)\right)\left|e^{(H)}(\mu_{x},\lambda,x)-\bar{e}(x)\right|+c_{e}\left(e^{(H)}(\mu_{x},\lambda,x)-\bar{e}(x)\right)=0.

Henceforth, the function
u^{(H)}
will be referred to as
\hat{u}
with the understanding that the symbol carries argument
\mathcal{D}
and
\bar{u}
. This also applies to
e^{(H)}
:

\hat{u}(x):=u^{(H)}(\lambda_{x}(x),\mu(x),x);\qquad\hat{e}(x):=e^{(H)}(\mu_{x}(x),\lambda(x),x).

It is clear from (
37
) and (
38
) that
\hat{u}=\bar{u}
and
\hat{e}=\bar{e}
is a value of the DtP mapping for
\mathcal{D}=0
.

We substitute the DtP mapping (
38
) in the functional
\widehat{S}
to define the dual functional

S\,\left[\lambda,\mu\right]\,=\,\int_{0}^{1}\,\,\left(-\hat{u}\lambda_{x}-\lambda\hat{e}-2\left((\hat{e}-1)^{3}-(\hat{e}-1)\right)\mu_{x}-\mu\left(\hat{u}-\alpha x\right)+H\left(\hat{u},\hat{e}\right)\right)\,dx+\alpha^{*}\lambda(1).

(39)

The requirement (
37
) ensures that the dual E-L system of (
39
) is simply the primal system (
36
), with the replacements
u\to\hat{u},e\to\hat{e}
. Thus, the critical points for the dual functional
S
(
39
) is also a critical point of the primal functional
I
(
35
), interpreted through the DtP mapping. The algorithm employed to obtain such critical points is discussed next.

6.2
Finite element algorithm

Denoting the dual fields as

D=(D_{1},D_{2}):=(\lambda,\mu),

the following weak form suffices to compute solutions to the dual problem (
40
), which also constitutes the first variation of the dual functional (
39
) set to 0:

\displaystyle R[D;\delta D]

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\,\hat{u}\delta\lambda_{x}\,dx\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\hat{e}\delta\lambda\,dx

(40)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\,2\left(\left(\hat{e}-1\right)^{3}-(\hat{e}-1)\right)\delta\mu_{x}\,dx\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\left(\hat{u}-\alpha x\right)\delta\mu\,dx+\alpha^{*}\delta\lambda(1)=0;

\displaystyle\mu(0)=0,\qquad\mu(1)=0.

A Dirichlet boundary condition is applied only on
\mu
. A Neumann boundary condition is applied on
\lambda
, corresponding to the Dirichlet boundary condition on
u
in the primal problem.

We use the Finite Element (FE) method to discretize the problem. A linear span of globally continuous, piecewise smooth finite element shape functions corresponding to a FE mesh for the domain is used to achieve this discretization. These shape functions are represented by
N^{\left(\cdot\right)}
, where (
\cdot
) denotes the index of the node under consideration. We use
C^{0}
FE shape functions to approximate the dual fields. Hence, the direct evaluation of primal fields through the DtP mapping exhibit discontinuities in the domain in general, even when approximating continuous primal solutions. This is because the DtP mappings involve derivatives of the dual fields. To deal with this feature, we use an
L^{2}
projection of the DtP mapping generated primal
u
field from the dual solution to define the corresponding continuous primal field.

The discretized dual fields and test functions are expressed as

D_{i}(x)=D^{A}_{i}N^{A}(x);\qquad\delta D_{i}(x)=\delta D^{A}_{i}N^{A}(x);\qquad dD_{i}(x)=dD^{A}_{i}N^{A}(x),\qquad i=1,2,

where
D^{A}_{i}
denote the finite element nodal degrees of freedom (which are the coefficients of a linear combination of a finite dimensional representation of the dual fields). The discretized version of (
40
) generates a discrete residual
R^{A}_{i}(\mathcal{D})
given by

R[D;\delta D]=\delta D^{A}_{i}R^{A}_{i}(\mathcal{D})

and its variation in the direction
dD
, the Jacobian

J[D;\delta D,dD]=\delta D^{A}_{i}\,J^{AB}_{ij}(\mathcal{D})\,dD^{B}_{j}

given by

\displaystyle J[D;\delta D,dD]\,=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\,\delta\lambda_{x}\left[\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial\lambda_{x}}\,d\lambda_{x}+\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial\mu}\,d\mu\right]\,dx\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\delta\lambda\left[\frac{\partial\hat{e}}{\partial\mu_{x}}\,d\mu_{x}+\frac{\partial\hat{e}}{\partial\lambda}\,d\lambda\right]\,dx

(41)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\,2\left(3\left(\hat{e}-1\right)^{2}-1\right)\delta\mu_{x}\left[\frac{\partial\hat{e}}{\partial\mu_{x}}\,d\mu_{x}+\frac{\partial\hat{e}}{\partial\lambda}\,d\lambda\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\,-

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\,\delta\mu\left[\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial\lambda_{x}}\,d\lambda_{x}+\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial\mu}\,d\mu\right]\,dx

that are used in a Newton Raphson scheme. Here,
R^{A}_{i},J^{AB}_{ij}
are given by the expressions

\displaystyle R^{A}_{1}=\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\hat{u}N^{A}_{x}\,dx\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\hat{e}N^{A}\,dx

\displaystyle R^{A}_{2}=-\int_{0}^{1}\,2\left(\left(\hat{e}-1\right)^{3}-(\hat{e}-1)\right)N^{A}_{x}\,dx\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\left(\hat{u}-\alpha x\right)N^{A}\,dx

and

\displaystyle J^{AB}_{11}=\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial\lambda_{x}}N^{A}_{x}N^{B}_{x}\,dx\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\frac{\partial\hat{e}}{\partial\lambda}N^{A}N^{B}\,dx

\displaystyle J^{AB}_{12}=\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\frac{\partial\hat{e}}{\partial\mu_{x}}N^{A}N^{B}_{x}\,dx\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial\mu}N^{A}_{x}N^{B}\,dx

\displaystyle J^{AB}_{21}=\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial\lambda_{x}}N^{A}N^{B}_{x}\,dx\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,2(3(\hat{e}-1)^{2}-1)\,\frac{\partial\hat{e}}{\partial\lambda}N^{A}_{x}N^{B}\,dx

\displaystyle J^{AB}_{22}=\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,2(3(\hat{e}-1)^{2}-1)\,\frac{\partial\hat{e}}{\partial\mu_{x}}N^{A}_{x}N^{B}_{x}\,dx\,-\int_{0}^{1}\,\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial\mu}N^{A}N^{B}\,dx

The Newton-Raphson scheme involves solving the following matrix equation for the
k^{th}
correction to an initial guess
D^{B(0)}_{j}

\displaystyle-R^{A}_{i}\left(\mathcal{D}^{(k-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=J^{AB}_{ij}\left(\mathcal{D}^{(k-1)}\right)\,dD^{B}_{j}

\displaystyle D^{B(k)}_{j}

\displaystyle=D^{B(k-1)}_{j}+dD^{B}_{j},

and this is continued until convergence of
|R(\mathcal{D})|
to
0
, up to the convergence threshold
tol
.

An initial guess for the dual fields
D
is required by the Newton Raphson (NR) algorithm. It is practical, and more feasible, to make an initial guess for a primal fields
(\hat{u},\hat{e})
with physical meaning, and infer initial guesses for the dual fields consistent with it as a solution of the DtP mapping. Choosing initial guesses
\hat{u}_{(i)}
and
\hat{e}_{(i)}

\hat{u}_{(i)}=\bar{u},\qquad\hat{e}_{(i)}=\bar{e},

the initial guess for the dual fields,

D_{i}^{B}=0,

satisfies the DtP mapping equations and this is considered by us as a good initial guess to initiate the Newton-Raphson iterations. The algorithm capturing these ideas has been outlined in Table.

7
Conclusion

We have demonstrated a methodology for developing a (family of) dual variational principle(s) for nonlinear elastostatics and elastodynamics with the property that its Euler-Lagrange equations are the governing PDE of nonlinear elasticity with the primal fields represented in terms of a mapping of the dual fields and their space-time gradient, pointwise. The dual variational principles are convex by design, regardless of the properties of the primal problem. Existence of minimizers of such a dual variational principle is rigorously shown, and the notions of variational dual solutions and dual solution to the PDE of nonlinear elastostatics are defined in terms of the dual functional. Computed examples of illustrative cases corresponding to noncovex elastostatics and elastodynamics, including a stable dual calculation of an ill-posed, primal elastodynamic problem is also demonstrated.

The computed examples, especially in Sec.
6.3.4
, raise a philosophical question about the optimal, minimalist, mathematical model that should be adopted for modeling physically observed softening behavior in macroscopic mechanical response. This work seems to suggest that existence of dual solutions may not be a show-stopper, with the means of attaining uniqueness in the model transferred to the choice of special types of auxiliary functions
H
employing physically meaningful base states which, nevertheless, need not be solutions to singularly-perturbed higher order PDE when physical justification for such is not available, but include experimentally observed input on physically relevant length scales. A crucial test in this regard would be to study the details of wave ‘propagation’ through the non-hyperbolic regions in our model, and check how well such behavior squares with experiment.

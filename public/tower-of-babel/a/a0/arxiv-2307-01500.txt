An Optimal Multiple-Class Encoding Scheme for a Graph of Bounded Hadwiger Number
Hsueh-I Lu

Abstract
Since Jacobson [FOCS 1989] initiated the investigation of succinct encodings for various classes of graphs 35 years ago, there has been a long list of results on balancing the generality of the class, the encoding and decoding speed, the succinctness of the encoded string, and the query support. Let 𝒞 n \mathcal{C}_{n} denote the set consisting of the graphs in a class 𝒞 \mathcal{C} that have at most n n vertices. A class 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is nontrivial if the information-theoretically minimum number ⌈ log 2 ⁡ | 𝒞 n | ⌉ \lceil\log_{2}|\mathcal{C}_{n}|\rceil of bits to distinguish the members of 𝒞 n \mathcal{C}_{n} is Ω ⁡ ( n ) \Omega(n) . An encoding scheme based upon a single class 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -optimal if it takes a graph G G of 𝒞 n \mathcal{C}_{n} and produces in deterministic linear time an encoded string of at most log 2 ⁡ | 𝒞 n | + o ⁡ ( log 2 ⁡ | 𝒞 n | ) \log_{2}|\mathcal{C}_{n}|+o(\log_{2}|\mathcal{C}_{n}|) bits from which G G can be recovered in linear time. Despite the extensive efforts in the literature, trees and general graphs were the only nontrivial classes 𝒞 \mathcal{C} admitting 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -optimal encoding schemes that support the degree query in O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) time. Basing an encoding scheme upon a single class ignores the possibility of a shorter encoded string using additional properties of the graph input. To leverage the inherent structures of individual graphs, we propose to base an encoding scheme upon of multiple classes: An encoding scheme based upon a family ℱ \mathcal{F} of classes, accepting all graphs in ⋃ ℱ \bigcup\mathcal{F} , is ℱ \mathcal{F} -optimal if it is 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -optimal for each 𝒞 ∈ ℱ \mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F} . Although an ℱ \mathcal{F} -optimal encoding scheme is by definition 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -optimal for each 𝒞 ∈ ℱ \mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F} , having a 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -optimal encoding scheme for each 𝒞 ∈ ℱ \mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F} does not guarantee an ℱ \mathcal{F} -optimal encoding scheme. Under this more stringent optimality criterion, we present an ℱ \mathcal{F} -optimal encoding scheme A ∗ A^{*} for a family ℱ \mathcal{F} of an infinite number of classes such that ⋃ ℱ \bigcup\mathcal{F} comprises all graphs of bounded Hadwiger numbers. Precisely, ℱ \mathcal{F} consists of the nontrivial quasi-monotone classes of k k -clique-minor-free graphs for each positive integer k k . Just to name a few, examples of monotone members of ℱ \mathcal{F} are graphs with genus at most 2 2 , 3 3 -colorable plane graphs, graphs of page numbers at most 4 4 and girths at least 5 5 , 6 6 -clique-minor-free graphs, forests with diameters at most 7 7 , graphs having no minor that is an 8 8 -cycle, and each nontrivial minor-closed class of graphs other than the class of all graphs. Examples of non-monotone members of ℱ \mathcal{F} are trees, floor-plans, triconnected planar graphs, and plane triangulations. Our ℱ \mathcal{F} -optimal encoding scheme A ∗ A^{*} supports queries of degree, adjacency, neighbor-listing, and bounded-distance shortest path in O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) time per output. Hence, we significantly broaden the graph classes admitting optimal encoding schemes that also efficiently support fundamental queries. Our A ∗ A^{*} does not rely on any recognition algorithm or any explicit or implicit knowledge of the exact or approximate values of ⌈ log ⁡ | 𝒞 n | ⌉ \lceil\log|\mathcal{C}_{n}|\rceil for the infinite number of classes 𝒞 ∈ ℱ \mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F} . A ∗ A^{*} needs no given embedding of the input graph. However, A ∗ A^{*} accepts additional information like a genus- O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) embedding, an O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) -coloring, or an O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) -orientation for the input graph to be decoded from the encoded string and answered by the query algorithms of A ∗ A^{*} .

Multiple-class encoding schemes

To leverage the inherent structures within individual graphs, we
propose to base an encoding scheme upon a family
\mathcal{F}
of multiple
graph classes. The input
n
-vertex graph

\mathcal{F}

\log|\mathcal{C}_{n}|
over all classes
\mathcal{C}
that satisfy
G\in\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F}

for

\mathcal{F}

\mathcal{C}
-succinct for all classes

with
G\in\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F}

that the bit count of

f(n)+o(f(n))

continuous super-additive function

for at least one class
\mathcal{C}
with
G\in\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F}

\mathcal{F}
of the classes

\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
of

\mathcal{F}
-succinct encoded string
of a 3-colorable non-bipartite graph is

-succinct for each

\mathcal{C}

-optimal for every member class

of the family

-succinct encoded string

-optimal encoding scheme exploits a broader range of graph

-optimal encoding scheme

-optimal encoding

\mathcal{C}

We defined a class

A class

Similar to all former

\mathcal{C}
-succinct. Our encoding scheme

of graphs to have our

\mathcal{C}

2.4
Three non-monotone slim classes of graphs

To ensure that our
\mathcal{F}
-optimal encoding scheme
A^{*}
indeed
overshadows all the previous work for non-monotone classes of graphs
listed in Figure
1
, we show that the three
non-monotone classes
\mathcal{C}
of triconnected planar graphs and
triangulations and floor-plans of genus-
O(1)
surfaces are
quasi-monotone. Specifically, we verify that the subgraph
G(U)
of

G
for each nonempty proper subset
U
of
V(G)
can be obtained from
a graph of
\mathcal{C}
by deleting
O(|N_{G}(U)|)
vertices and their incident
edges. Since all graphs in these three classes
\mathcal{C}
are connected,
we have
|N_{G}(U)|\geq 1
.

•

Example 1: triconnected planar graphs. Let
H=G(U)
. Repeat the
following three steps on the current graph
H
for three iterations:
(i) Let the current graph
H
be embedded such that each of its
biconnected components is incident to the exterior face. (ii) Add a
new vertex into the exterior face of
H
. (iii) Make the new vertex a
common neighbor of all vertices on the exterior bound of the current

H
. The plane graph
H
at the end of the
k
-th round is

k
-connected. Thus, the initial
H
can be obtained from the final

H\in\mathcal{C}
by deleting exactly three vertices and their incident
edges.

•

Example 2: triangulations of a genus-
O(1)
surface. The boundary of
each non-triangle face
F
of
G(U)
contains at least two vertices of

N_{G}(U)
. Let
e_{F}
be an edge between arbitrary two vertices of

N_{G}(U)
on the boundary of
F
. The graph consisting of the edges

e_{F}
for all non-triangle faces
F
of
G(U)
has genus
O(1)
,
implying that the number of non-triangle faces of
G(U)
is

O(|N_{G}(U)|)
. Thus, adding a new vertex
u_{F}
to triangulate each
non-triangle face
F
of
G(U)
results in a triangulation of a
genus-
O(1)
surface.
G(U)
can be obtained from the resulting graph
by deleting the
O(|N_{G}(U)|)
new vertices
u_{F}
and their incident
edges.

•

Example 3: floor-plans of a genus-
O(1)
surface. Since each vertex a
floor-plan has
O(1)
degree, one can make
G(U)
a floor-plan by
adding
O(|N_{G}(U)|)
vertices and edges such that
G(U)
can be
obtained from the resulting floor-plan by deleting the new vertices
and their incident edges.

Note that there is no need to modify our
A^{*}
to accommodate these
non-monotone classes
\mathcal{C}
as did by the previous encoding schemes in
the literature. Since the above classes are clearly nontrivial and
their members have bounded Hadwiger numbers, we just have to prove
that they are quasi-monotone and then our
\mathcal{F}
-optimal encoding
scheme
A^{*}
is guaranteed to be
\mathcal{C}
-optimal encoding schemes for
each of the above classes
\mathcal{C}
. One can obtain more examples this
way.

4
Concluding remarks

We propose to base an encoding scheme on multiple classes of graphs to
exploit the inherent structures of individual graphs. As the first
nontrivial such example, we present an
\mathcal{F}
-optimal encoding scheme

A^{*}
for a family
\mathcal{F}
of an infinite number of classes.
Specifically,
A^{*}
takes an
n
-vertex
k
-clique-minor-free graph

G
with
k=O(1)
and produces a
\mathcal{C}
-succinct encoded string
X
in
deterministic
O(n)
time for each nontrivial quasi-monotone class

\mathcal{C}
of graphs that contains
G
.
A^{*}
can decode
X
back to
G

in deterministic
O(n)
time. This means that
A^{*}
automatically
exploits an infinite number of possible nontrivial quasi-monotone
structures of
G
to encode
G
as compactly as possible.
A^{*}
does
not require any embedding of
G
or any recognition algorithm or other
explicit or implicit knowledge about the member classes
\mathcal{C}
of

\mathcal{F}
.
A^{*}
also supports fundamental queries in
O(1)
time per
output. Moreover,
A^{*}
accepts additional information like an

O(1)
-coloring or an genus-
O(1)
embedding of
G
that can be
decoded back together with
G
by
A^{*}
and answered by the query
algorithms of
A^{*}
. It is of interest to see if our
\mathcal{F}
-optimal
encoding scheme
A^{*}
can be extended so that (i) the ground
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
can be a proper superclass of the graphs having bounded Hadwiger
numbers, (ii) the member classes
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{F}
can be more refined to
beyond quasi-monotonicty, or (iii) efficient updates can be supported
for the input graph
G
and its equipped information.

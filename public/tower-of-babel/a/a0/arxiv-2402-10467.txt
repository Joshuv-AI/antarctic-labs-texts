1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group,
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
be the set of its irreducible characters and for any character
\chi
of
G
let us denote the set of all irreducible constituent of
it by
c(\chi)
. First proved in
[
3
, Ch. XV, Th. IV]
by Burnside and later
refined by Steinberg
[
8
]
and Brauer
[
2
]
is a theorem which
states that for any faithful representation
\chi
, there exists a positive integer
t(\chi)>0
such that

\displaystyle c(\chi)\cup c(\chi^{2})\cup\cdots\cup c(\chi^{t(\chi)})=\mathrm{Irr}(G).

Furthermore when the center of
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, i.e.
Z(\chi)=\{g\in G:|\chi(g)|=\chi(1)\}
is trivial, Miller has shown that (see
[
7
, Lemma 4]
) there exists smallest
e(\chi)
such that

\displaystyle c(\chi^{e(\chi)})=\mathrm{Irr}(G).

The numbers
\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)\\
\chi\text{ faithful}\end{subarray}}e(\chi)
will be referred to be
character covering number
.
This terminology is motivated by the covering number problem in the case of conjugacy
classes (for example see
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
and the references therein). There is a slightly stronger definition of
character covering numbers for groups due to Arad, Chillag, and Herzog, which calls
n
to be a covering number of a group

G
, if for all
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)\setminus\{1\}
, once has

c(\chi^{n})=\mathrm{Irr}(G)
(see
[
1
]
). They further prove that
a finite nontrivial group
G
has a finite character covering number if and only if
G
is a nonabelian simple group (see
[
1
, Theorem 1]
).
Our definition of character covering number is weaker, as
we are varying characters over the set of irreducible faithful characters.
Miller proved that (see
[
7
, Theorem 1]
) for
n>4
,
and
X=\{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(S_{n}):\chi(1)>1\}

(1)

c(\chi^{k})=\mathrm{Irr}(S_{n})
for all
\chi\in X
if and only if
k\geq n-1
,

(2)

c(\chi)\cup c(\chi^{2})\cup\cdots\cup c(\chi^{k})=\mathrm{Irr}(S_{n})
for all

\chi\in X
if and only if
k\geq n-1
.

We prove a similar result for
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q\geq 8
be a power of prime and
G(q)=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
be the projective special linear group (which is also simple). Then the covering number is
4
if
q=2^{2m+1}
for some
m
and
3
otherwise.

In
Section

2
we mention the conjugacy class and
character table of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
. In
Section

3
we will prove
Theorem

1.1
.

3.
Proof of
Theorem

1.1

3.1.
When
q
is even

We start with the following lemma, which evaluates sums of roots of unity.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
q
be power of
2
,
1\leq k\leq q/2-1
,
1\leq j\leq q/2
. Further
\epsilon
and
\eta_{0}
be
q-1
-th and
q+1
-th primitive roots of unity respectively. Then for any integer
t>0
,

(3.1)

\sum\limits_{a=1}^{q/2-1}(\epsilon^{tk})^{a}+(\epsilon^{tk})^{-a}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{ if }(tk,q-1)\neq(q-1)\\
q-2&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases},

and

(3.2)

\sum\limits_{b=1}^{q/2}(\eta_{0}^{tj})^{b}+(\epsilon^{tj})^{-b}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{ if }(tj,q+1)\neq(q+1)\\
q&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}.

Proof.

Note that
\{1-q/2,2-q/2,\cdots,-1,0,1,2,\cdots,q/2-1\}
is a complete set of residue modulo
q-1
. Hence if
(tk,q-1)\neq(q-1)
, we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{a=1}^{q/2-1}(\epsilon^{tk})^{a}+(\epsilon^{tk})^{-a}=-1+\sum\limits_{a=0}^{q-2}(\epsilon^{tk})^{a}.

Since
\sum\limits_{a=0}^{q-2}(\epsilon^{tk})^{a}
can be grouped into groups of
(tk,q-1)
terms each having a zero-sum, the first conclusion follows.
If
(tk,q-1)=q-1
, then the sum is
q-2
, as there are
q-2
many terms. The second equality can be proved easily.

∎

Direct calculations prove the following equalities;

\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{1}\rangle=1,&\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{q}\rangle=1\end{array}

and using
Lemma

3.1

\displaystyle(q^{3}-q)\left\langle\left(\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\right)^{2},\psi_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(q+1)^{2}+(q^{2}-1)+q(q+1)\sum\limits_{a=1}^{q/2-1}\left(\epsilon^{ai}+\epsilon^{-ai}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2q^{2}+2q+q(q+1)\left\{\sum\limits_{a=1}^{q/2-1}\left(\epsilon^{2ai}+\epsilon^{-2ai}\right)+(q-2)\right\}

\displaystyle=q^{3}-q,

which implies
\left\langle\left(\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\right)^{2},\psi_{1}\right\rangle=1
. Using similar computation we have
\left\langle\left(\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\right)^{2},\psi_{q}\right\rangle=2
.

Note that

(3.3)

(q^{3}-q)\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\rangle=q^{2}(q+1)+0+q(q+1)\sum\limits_{a=1}^{q/2-1}\left(\epsilon^{ak}+\epsilon^{-ak}\right)+0.

Since
k<q-1
, we have
(k,q-1)\neq q-1
. Hence by
Lemma

3.1
the
Eq.

3.3
reduces to

\displaystyle q^{2}(q+1)-q(q+1)=q^{3}-q,

which in turn proves that
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\rangle=1
. Next, we have

\displaystyle(q^{3}-q)\left\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right\rangle=q^{2}(q-1)-q(q+1)\sum\limits_{b=1}^{q/2}\left(\eta_{0}^{bj}+\eta_{0}^{-bj}\right).

Since
\eta_{0}
is a primitive
q+1
-th root of unity, arguing as before
we get that

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\rangle=1
. This proves that
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c(\psi_{q}^{2})=\mathrm{Irr}(G(q))
. Similar calculations further
show that
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\left\langle\left(\psi^{(k)}_{q+1}\right)^{2},\psi_{q}\right\rangle=2,

Now,

\displaystyle(q^{3}-q)\left\langle\left(\psi^{(k)}_{q+1}\right)^{2},\psi_{q+1}^{(k^{\prime})}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q+1)^{2}+(q^{2}-1)+q(q+1)\sum\limits_{a=1}^{q/2-1}(\epsilon^{ak}+\epsilon^{-ak})^{2}(\epsilon^{ak^{\prime}}+\epsilon^{-ak^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}q^{3}-q&\text{when }2k+k^{\prime}\neq q-1\\
2(q^{3}-q)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Since for a given
k
, there exists a unique
k^{\prime}
such that
(2k+k^{\prime},q-1)=q-1
, we have that

\left\langle\left(\psi^{(k)}_{q+1}\right)^{2},\psi_{q+1}^{(k^{\prime})}\right\rangle=1
for
q/2-2
choices of
k^{\prime}
and for the rest it takes the value
2
. This proves that
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c\left(\left(\psi^{(k)}_{q+1}\right)^{2}\right)=\mathrm{Irr}(G(q)).

Although
\left\langle\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{2},\psi_{1}\right\rangle=1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\psi^{(j)}_{q-1}\right)^{2},\psi_{q}\right\rangle=q(q+1)^{2}-q(q^{2}-q)+q(q-1)=0.

Hence we do not have
c\left(\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{2}\right)=\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q))
. Also, when
3|q+1
,

c\left(\left(\psi_{q-1}^{((q+1)/3)}\right)^{3}\right)\neq\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q))
,
since
\left\langle\left(\psi_{q-1}^{((q+1)/3)}\right)^{3},\psi_{1}\right\rangle=0
. However, we have the following values,

\begin{array}[]{cc}\left\langle\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{3},\psi_{1}\right\rangle=\begin{cases}1&\text{when }3j\neq q+1\\
0&\text{when }3j=q+1\end{cases},&\left\langle\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{3},\psi_{q}\right\rangle=\begin{cases}q-3&\text{when }3j\neq q+1\\
q-2&\text{when }3j=q+1\end{cases},\end{array}

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{3},\psi_{q-1}^{(j^{\prime})}\right\rangle=\begin{cases}q-4&\text{when }(3j+j^{\prime},q+1)\neq q+1\\
q-3&\text{when }(3j+j^{\prime},q+1)=q+1\end{cases},

and

\left\langle\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{3},\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\right\rangle=q-2,
which proves that
if
3\not|(q+1)
, then
c\left(\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{3}\right)=\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q))
. In case
3|(q+1)
, we have the
following multiplicities for
j=(q+1)/3
.

\begin{array}[]{c|ccccc}\hline\cr\chi&\psi_{1}&\psi_{q}&\psi_{(q+1)}^{(k)}&\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}&\psi_{q-1}^{(j^{\prime})}\\
\hline\cr\left\langle\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{4},\chi\right\rangle&q-1&q^{2}-4q+4&q^{2}-3q+3&q^{2}-5q+6&q^{2}-5q+11\\
\hline\cr\end{array}.

This proves
Theorem

1.1
in case
q
is even.

3.2.
When
q
is odd

This will be divided into two parts as the character table is dependent on the
parity of
q
, modulo
4
. We start with the case
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}

and later derive the results for
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

3.2.1.
Case I:
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}

Let us start with the following lemma, analogous to
Lemma

3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
be power of an odd prime,
k
and
j
be even integers satisfying
2\leq k\leq(q-3)/2
,
2\leq j\leq(q-3)/2
. Further
\epsilon
and
\eta_{0}
be
q-1
-th and
q+1
-th primitive roots of unity respectively. Then for any integer
t>0
,

(3.4)

\sum\limits_{a=1}^{(q-3)/4}(\epsilon^{tk})^{a}+(\epsilon^{tk})^{-a}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{ if }(tk,q-1)\neq(q-1)\\
\frac{q-3}{2}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases},

and

(3.5)

\sum\limits_{b=1}^{(q-3)/4}\left\{(\eta_{0}^{tj})^{b}+(\epsilon^{tj})^{-b}\right\}=\begin{cases}-1-\eta_{0}^{\frac{q+1}{4}\cdot tj}&\text{ if }\left({tj},{q+1}\right)\neq(q+1)\\
\frac{q+1}{2}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}.

Proof.

We note that
\left\{-\frac{q-3}{4},-\frac{q-3}{4}+1,\cdots,-1,0,1,\cdots,\frac{q-3}{4}\right\}
is a complete set of residue modulo
q-1
. Also

\left\{-\frac{q-3}{4},-\frac{q-3}{4}+1,\cdots,-1,0,1,\cdots,\frac{q-3}{4},\frac{q+1}{4}\right\}
is a complete set of residue modulo
q+1
. The rest of the proof follows similarly, as explained in
Lemma

3.1
.
∎

We first have the following equalities;

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\left\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{1}\right\rangle=1,&\left\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{q}\right\rangle=2,&\left\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{-}^{\prime}\right\rangle=1,&\left\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{-}^{\prime\prime}\right\rangle=1.\end{array}

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{q^{3}-q}{2}\left\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=q^{2}(q+1)+q(q+1)\sum\limits_{a=1}^{(q-3)/4}(\epsilon^{ak}+\epsilon^{-ak})

\displaystyle=q^{2}(q+1)+q(q+1)\cdot(-1),

the last equality follows from
Lemma

3.2
. Hence

\left\langle\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\right\rangle=2
.
Using similar argument we get that
\left\langle\psi_{q}^{2},\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right\rangle=2
. We present the all values of the inner product of an irreducible
character with the square of a non-trivial character
in
Table

2-2
. Before that, we present two computations,
firstly the case of
\left\langle\left(\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\right)^{2},\psi_{q+1}^{(k^{\prime})}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{2},\psi^{\prime}_{-}\right\rangle
. For any
k
, there exists
a unique
k^{\prime}
such that
2k+k^{\prime}=q+1
or
2k-k^{\prime}=q+1
. We first deal with this case.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{q^{3}-q}{2}\cdot\left\langle\left(\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\right)^{2},\psi_{q+1}^{(k^{\prime})}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(q+1)^{3}+\frac{q^{2}-1}{2}\cdot 1+\frac{q^{2}-1}{2}\cdot 1

\displaystyle+q(q+1)\cdot\left\{\sum\limits_{a=1}^{(q-3)/4}\left(\epsilon^{2ak}+2+\epsilon^{-2ak}\right)\left(\epsilon^{ak^{\prime}}+\epsilon^{-ak^{\prime}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=(q+1)^{3}+(q^{2}-1)+q(q+1)\left\{\sum\limits_{a=1}^{(q-3)/4}\left(\epsilon^{(2k+k^{\prime})a}+\epsilon^{-(2k+k^{\prime})a}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.2\sum\limits_{a=1}^{(q-3)/4}\left(\epsilon^{ak}+\epsilon^{-ak^{\prime}}\right)+\sum\limits_{a=1}^{(q-3)/4}\left(\epsilon^{(2k-k^{\prime})a}+\epsilon^{-(2k-k^{\prime})a}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=(q+1)^{3}+(q^{2}-1)+q(q+1)\left(\dfrac{q-3}{2}-3\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{3(q^{3}-q)}{2}.

Now assume
k^{\prime}
to be such that none of
2k+k^{\prime},2k-k^{\prime}
is equal to

q+1
. Then we get that

\displaystyle\frac{q^{3}-q}{2}\cdot\left\langle\left(\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\right)^{2},\psi_{q+1}^{(k^{\prime})}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(q+1)^{3}+(q^{2}-1)+q(q+1)\left(-4\right)

\displaystyle=q^{3}-q.

This implies that

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\psi_{q+1}^{(k)}\right)^{2},\psi_{q+1}^{(k^{\prime})}\right\rangle=\begin{cases}3&\text{if }2k+k^{\prime}=q+1\text{ or }2k-k^{\prime}=q+1\\
2&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

For the second computation, We have

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{2},\psi_{-}^{\prime}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{(q-1)^{3}}{2}-\frac{q^{2}-1}{2}\cdot(\omega+\omega^{*})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle q(q-1)\sum\limits_{b=1}^{(q-3)/4}(\eta_{0}^{bj}+\eta_{0}^{-bj})^{2}(-1)^{b+1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2q(q-1)\eta_{0}^{(q+1)j/2}(-1)^{(q+5)/4}.

Then

\displaystyle q(q-1)\sum\limits_{b=1}^{(q-3)/4}(\eta_{0}^{bj}+\eta_{0}^{-bj})^{2}(-1)^{b+1}+2q(q-1)\eta_{0}^{(q+1)j/2}(-1)^{(q+5)/4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(q-1)\left\{\sum\limits_{b=1}^{(q-3)/4}(\eta_{0}^{2bj}+\eta_{0}^{-2bj}+2)(-1)^{b+1}+2(-1)^{(q+5)/4}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(q-1)\left\{-\sum\limits_{b=1}^{(q-3)/4}((-\eta_{0}^{2j})^{b}+(-\eta_{0}^{2j})^{-b})+2\sum\limits_{b=1}^{(q+1)/4}(-1)^{b+1}\right\}.

Since
\eta_{0}
is a primitive
q+1
-th root of unity,
j
is even and
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
(and hence
(q+1)/2
is even), we get that

\eta_{0}^{2j}
is a
(q+1)/4
-th primitive root of unity. Now we divide this into two cases.

(1)

First assume that

(q+1)/4
is even. Then
-\eta_{0}^{2j}
is again a
(q+1)/4
-th primitive root of unity. Hence
\sum\limits_{b=1}^{(q-3)/4}(-\eta_{0}^{2j})^{b}=-1
and
\sum\limits_{b=1}^{(q+1)/4}(-1)^{b+1}=0
. Thus, in this case, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\psi_{q-1}^{(j)}\right)^{2},\psi_{-}^{\prime}\right\rangle=2.

(2)

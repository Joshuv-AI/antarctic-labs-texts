Relating CP divisibility of dynamical maps with compatibility of channels
Arindam Mitra 1,2,3,4, Debashis Saha 5, Samyadeb Bhattacharya 6,7, A. S. Majumdar 8

Abstract
The role of CP-indivisibility and incompatibility as valuable resources for various information-theoretic tasks is widely acknowledged. This study delves into the intricate relationship between CP-divisibility and channel compatibility. Our investigation focuses on the behaviour of incompatibility robustness of quantum channels for a pair of generic dynamical maps. We show that the incompatibility robustness of channels is monotonically non-increasing for a pair of generic CP-divisible dynamical maps. Further, our explicit study of the behaviour of incompatibility robustness with time for some specific dynamical maps reveals non-monotonic behaviour in the CP-indivisible regime. Additionally, we propose a measure of CP-indivisibility based on the incompatibility robustness of quantum channels. Our investigation provides valuable insights into the nature of quantum dynamical maps and their relevance in information-theoretic applications.



I
Introduction

Incompatibility is one of the main features of quantum mechanics that makes it different from classical mechanics
Heinosaari et al. 2016
. A set of devices is said to be compatible if those devices can be simultaneously implemented on a quantum system. Otherwise, the set is incompatible. These devices can be measurements, channels, instruments etc. Incompatibility is a resource in several information theoretic tasks and is necessary to demonstrate non-classical advantage in such tasks. For example, measurement incompatibility is necessary and sufficient to demonstrate quantum steering
Quintino et al. 2014
. Incompatibility of measurements is also necessary to demonstrate Bell inequality violation
Son et al. 2005
;
Quintino et al. 2014
and any quantum advantage in communication tasks
Saha et al. 2023
.
Measurement incompatibility also provides advantage in some state discrimination tasks
Skrzypczyk et al. 2019
. Recently, it has been shown that incompatibility of channels and measurement-channel incompatibility both provide advantages in quantum state discrimination tasks
Mori 2020
.

A general quantum evolution is described by a completely positive trace preserving (CPTP) dynamical map
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
;
Alicki and Lendi 2007
;
Lindblad 1976
;
Gorini et al. 1976
;
Rivas et al. 2014
;
Breuer et al. 2016
;
de Vega and Alonso 2017
. This representation has wide application, since it is almost impossible to keep a quantum system truly isolated. When considering a Markovian evolution
(
Rivas et al. 2014
;
Breuer et al. 2016
)
, it is necessary to take the quantum system weakly interacting with much larger stationary environment, and hence, the reduced dynamics of the system can be considered to be memoryless, leading to one-way information flow from the quantum system to the bath degrees of freedom. Therefore, the quantum features of a system subjected to such dynamics vanishes gradually with time
Rivas et al. 2014
;
Breuer et al. 2016
;
de Vega and Alonso 2017
. However, in practical situations like in an experiment, the coupling between the system and environmental degrees of freedom may not always be sufficiently weak. Moreover, the concerning environment can very well be finite or non-stationary. These situations may lead to the signature of non-Markovian information backflow
Rivas et al. 2010
;
Laine et al. 2010
;
Bellomo et al. 2007
;
Dijkstra and Tanimura 2010
;
Kumar et al. 2017
;
Pradeep Kumar et al. 2018
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2017
;
Mukhopadhyay et al. 2017
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2020
;
Maity et al. 2020
;
Bhattacharya and Bhattacharya 2021
. Though quantum non-markovianity has been associated with varied physical attributes
(
Burgarth et al. 2021
;
Milz et al. 2019
;
Rivas et al. 2010
;
Laine et al. 2010
)
, the focus of this work is based solely on indivisibility of the dynamics exhibiting information backflow from the environment to the system
(
Rivas et al. 2010
)
.

A divisible quantum operation is the one, that can be realised as arbitrary number of completely positive trace preserving (CPTP) maps. In other words, such operations can be divided into arbitrary number of CPTP maps. The precise mathematical definition of such maps is later presented in equation (
5
) for better understanding.
Divisible maps do not exhibit information backflow from environment to the system
(
Laine et al. 2010
;
Rivas et al. 2010
)
, and hence, can be understood as Markovian operations.
The Born-Markov approximation and stationary bath state approximation are imperative to realize such quantum operations, and hence, in the absence of these initial approximations, the dynamics is bound to be CP-indivisible
and prone to show information backflow
(
Rivas et al. 2014
)
. Adopting this line of reasoning, in this work, we take CP-indivisbile quantum operations as non-Markovian. Note that CP-indivisble operations are necessary to have information backflow from the environment to the system enabling recovery
of lost information to an extent, and hence, they can be considered resourceful operations in information processing scenarios. For example, it has been shown that information backflow allows perfect teleportation with mixed states
(
Laine et al. 2014
)
, improvement of capacity for long quantum channels
(
Bylicka et al. 2014
)
and efficient work extraction from an Otto cycle
(
Thomas et al. 2018
)
.

From the above discussions, it is clear that (in-) compatibility and CP-(in-) divisibility can play the role of resources in various information-theoretic and thermodynamic tasks.
There are several resources in quantum theory that provide advantage in information-theoretic or thermodynamic tasks
Chitambar and Gour 2019
. Entanglement

Horodecki et al. 2009
, coherence
Aimet and Kwon 2023
, non-locality
Acín et al. 2006
, contextuality
Howard et al. 2014
;
Budroni et al. 2022
, and incompatibility
Buscemi et al. 2020
are examples of some widely studied resources. Evidently therefore, exploring the interplay amongst different resources forms an important avenue of research. For example, it is well-known that coherence can be measured with entanglement
Streltsov et al. 2015
.
The relation between incompatibility and Bell non-locality
Son et al. 2005
;
Quintino et al. 2014
as well as the relation between incompatibility and steerability
Quintino et al. 2014
are well-known. Furthermore, it has been recently shown that superposition and entanglement are equivalent concepts in any physical theory
Aubrun et al. 2022
.

The motivation for the
present work is
to explore the connection between the two resources of (in-)compatibility and CP-(in-)divisibility, which have been hitherto investigated separately
in the literature. It is known that both incompatibility and CP-indivisibility are resources for different information-theoretic tasks.
Moreover, to the best of our knowledge, the notion of compatibility has been considered only for devices in the static backdrop.
In Ref.
Duarte et al. 2022
, the authors have done a qualitative study regarding the relation between CP-divisibility and
incompatibility. However, they didn’t consider dynamical maps that involve time, and neither consider the robustness measure
of incompatibility to draw quantitative connection between the above-said resources. On the other hand,
in this work we introduce and characterize the notion of compatibility of dynamical maps, incorporating their evolution in time.
Through our present analysis, we characterize CP-(in-)divisibility with respect to the (in-)compatibility of channels. We study the behaviour of incompatibility robustness of quantum channels for some examples of dynamical maps. We further present an example where
the non-Markovian advantange manifested in terms of CP-indivisibility and information backflow is clearly seen to act as a quantum resource
in the task of teleportation. Moreover, we define a measure of CP-indivisibility based on incompatibility robustness of channels.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we provide definitions of various quantities required for the subsequent analysis. Our main results are presented from Sec.
III
onwards.
In Sec.
III.1
, we show that the incompatibility robustness of quantum channels for a pair of CP-divisible dynamical maps is monotonically non-increasing with respect to time. In Sec.
III.2
, we show that for any pair of dynamical maps, incompatibility robustness of measurements is upper bounded by incompatibility of channels for an arbitrary time. In Sec
III.3
, we discuss the notion of compatibility of dynamical maps and its connection to channel compatibility. In Sec.
IV
, we study the behaviour of incompatibility robustness of quantum channels for certain specific dynamical maps and show its non-monotonic behaviour in a CP-indivisible case. In Sec.
V
, we discuss the usefulness of CP-indivisibility in the context of quantum teleportation and compare the behaviour of teleportation fidelity with the incompatibility robustness w.r.t. time. In Sec.
VI
, we propose a measure of CP-indivisibility based on incompatibility of channels. Finally, in Sec.
VII
, we
present our concluding remarks.

II
Preliminaries

II.1
Compatibility of measurements

A measurement
M
acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a set of positive semi-definite matrices i.e.,
M=\{M(x)\}_{x\in\Omega_{M}}
such that
\sum_{x\in\Omega_{M}}M(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}
where
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}
is the identity matrix on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
\Omega_{M}
is known as the outcome set of
M
. The set of all measurements acting on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and with outcome set
\Omega
is denoted by
\mathbbm{M}(\mathcal{H},\Omega)
. A set of measurements
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}^{n}_{i=1}
is said to be compatible if there exists a joint measurement
M=\{M(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})\}\in\mathbbm{M}(\mathcal{H},\Omega_{M})
with
\Omega_{M}=\Omega_{M_{1}}\times\ldots\times\Omega_{M_{n}}
such that
M_{i}(j_{i})=\sum_{\{j_{k}\}\setminus j_{i}}M(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})
for all
j_{i}\in\Omega_{M_{i}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots n\}
where sum over
\{j_{k}\}\setminus j_{i}
denotes the sum over all
j_{k}
s for all
k
s except for
k=i
. Otherwise, the set is incompatible
Heinosaari et al. 2016
;
Kiukas et al. 2023
.

A measure of incompatibility of quantum measurements is the incompatibility robustness of quantum measurements, defined below. The incompatibility robustness of two quantum measurements
M_{1}\in\mathbbm{M}(\mathcal{H},\Omega_{1})
and
M_{2}\in\mathbbm{M}(\mathcal{H},\Omega_{2})
can be defined as:

\begin{split}R_{M}(M_{1},M_{2})=\min\penalty\ &\left.r\right.\\
\text{s.t.}\penalty\ &\left.\frac{M_{1}(i_{1})+r\tilde{M}_{1}(i_{1})}{1+r}=\sum_{i_{2}}M(i_{1}.i_{2})\right.\\
&\left.\frac{M_{2}(i_{2})+r\tilde{M}_{2}(i_{2})}{1+r}=\sum_{i_{1}}M(i_{1}.i_{2})\right.\\
&\left.M\in\mathbbm{M}(\mathcal{H},\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2})\right.\\
&\left.\tilde{M}_{i}\in\mathbbm{M}(\mathcal{H},\Omega_{i})\quad i=1,2.\right.\end{split}

(1)

Here,
\tilde{M}_{i}
s are arbitrary noise measurements and the optmization is over all variables, other than the given pair of measurements
(M_{1},M_{2})
. We call the set of all values of
r
that satisfies the above equalities for different noise measurements,
the compatibility range
. Clearly, the incompatibility robustness is the minimum of all values of
r
that belongs to the compatibility range.

II.2
Compatibility of quantum channels

A quantum channel
\Gamma:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})
is a CPTP linear map where
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is the bounded linear operator on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})
is the bounded linear operator on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
. We denote the set of all quantum channels from
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
to
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})
as
\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
. We also denote the composition (also known as concatenation) of two quantum channels
\Phi_{1}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\bar{\mathcal{K}})
and
\Phi_{2}:\mathcal{L}(\bar{\mathcal{K}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})
as
\Phi_{2}\circ\Phi_{1}
where
\bar{K}
is another Hilbert space. For two quantum channels
\Gamma_{1}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{1})
and
\Gamma_{2}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{2})
, if there exists another quantum channel
\Theta\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{K}_{1},\mathcal{K}_{2})
such that
\Gamma_{2}=(\Theta\circ\Gamma_{1})
then we say that
\Gamma_{2}
is a post-processing of
\Gamma_{1}
and we denote it as
\Gamma_{2}\preceq\Gamma_{1}
. Let
\Phi\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
be a quantum channel. Then the
\Phi^{*}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is called as a dual map of
\Phi
if
\text{Tr}[\Phi(T)X]=\text{Tr}[T\Phi^{*}(X)]
holds for all
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
and
X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})
. Clearly,
\Phi^{*}
is the action of
\Phi
in Heisenberg picture. As,
\Phi
is CP trace preserving,
\Phi^{*}
is CP unital. Now, consider a measurement
M=\{M(x)\}_{x\in\Omega_{M}}\in\mathbbm{M}(\Omega_{M},\mathcal{K})
and a channel
\Lambda\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
. If
\Lambda^{*}
is applied on the measurement
M
, the resulting measurement is
\Lambda^{*}(M)=\{\Lambda^{*}(M(x))\}_{x\in\Omega_{M}}\in\mathbbm{M}(\Omega_{M},\mathcal{H})
. Implementation of
M
on an arbitrary quantum system after implementation of
\Lambda
, is equivalent to implementation of
\Lambda^{*}(M(x))
before implementation of
\Lambda
on that quantum system.

We now discuss a special type of channel that maps any input state to a fixed output state. These channels are called completely depolarising (CD) channels

that may completely erase the information of input states. If
\Upsilon_{\eta}(\rho)=\eta
for all input states
\rho
then
\Upsilon_{\eta}
is a completely depolarising channel. We denote the set of all completely depolarising channels from
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
to
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})
as
\mathbbm{Ch}^{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
. We will use this type of channels in the later sections. Now, suppose
\Upsilon_{\eta}\in\mathbbm{Ch}^{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
is an arbitrary completely depolarising channel and
\Lambda_{1}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime})
is an arbitrary quantum channel. Then
(\Lambda_{1}\circ\Upsilon_{\eta})
is also a completely depolarising channel

\Lambda_{1}(T)=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}\Lambda(T);\penalty\ \Lambda_{2}(T)=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}\Lambda(T).

(2)

Otherwise,
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
are incompatible

The quantum channel

\Lambda_{1}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}\Lambda;\penalty\ \Lambda_{2}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}\Lambda.

(3)

We will use the short-hand notations throughout the paper.

Suppose

A measure of incompatibility of quantum channels is the incompatibility robustness of quantum channels that is defined below

II.3
Dynamical maps and CP-divisibility

A dynamical map is a family of CPTP linear maps
\{\Lambda_{t,t_{0}}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\}_{t}
(where
t\geq t_{0}
). Here,
t
represents the time and
t_{0}
is the fixed initial time. Without loss of generality, we can take
t_{0}
to be
0
and denote
\Lambda_{t,0}
as
\Lambda_{t}
. We denote the set of all dynamical maps on
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
(i.e., from
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
to
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
) as
\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{\Lambda^{1}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}=\{\Lambda^{2}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
. Then the convex combination of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
(w.r.t.
p\geq 0
) is defined as
p\mathcal{D}_{1}+(1-p)\mathcal{D}_{2}:=\{p\Lambda^{1}_{t}+(1-p)\Lambda^{2}_{t}\}_{t}
. Now, we provide the definition of a CP-divisible dynamical map below.

Definition 3
.

A dynamical map is
\mathcal{D}=\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t}
is called CP divisible if for all
t
and all
s
, it can be written as

\Lambda_{t}=V_{t,s}\circ\Lambda_{s},\penalty\ (t\geq s)

(5)

where
\circ
denotes the composition of maps and
V_{t,s}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is a CPTP linear map.

A dynamical map that is not CP-divisible is known as CP-indivisible dynamical map. We refer the readers to Refs.
Breuer et al. 2016
;
Rivas et al. 2014
;
de Vega and Alonso 2017
for more details.

III
Relating CP-divisibility of dynamical maps with compatibility of quantum channels

III.1
CP-indivisibility of dynamical maps and incompatibility robustness of quantum channels

, if
\bar{\Gamma}_{1}\preceq\Gamma_{1}
and
\bar{\Gamma}_{2}\preceq\Gamma_{2}
hold, then
\bar{\Gamma}_{1}
and
\bar{\Gamma}_{2}
are compatible if
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are compatible
(
Heinosaari and Miyadera 2017
, Proposition 3)
. Therefore, as
\Sigma^{1}_{t}
and
\Sigma^{2}_{t}
are compatible, we get that the quantum channels
V^{1}_{t,t+\delta{t}}\circ\Sigma^{1}_{t}=\frac{\Lambda^{1}_{t+\delta{t}}+l(V^{1}_{t,t+\delta{t}}\circ\Theta_{1})}{1+l}
and
V^{2}_{t,t+\delta{t}}\circ\Sigma^{2}_{t}=\frac{\Lambda^{2}_{t+\delta{t}}+l(V^{2}_{t,t+\delta{t}}\circ\Theta_{2})}{1+l}
are also compatible. Therefore, from the definition of incompatibility robustness of quantum channels w.r.t. only the set of completely depolarising channels and the fact that he channels
(V^{1}_{t,t+\delta{t}}\circ\Theta_{1})
and
(V^{2}_{t,t+\delta{t}}\circ\Theta_{2})
are completely depolarising, it follows that
R^{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}}_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{t},\Lambda^{2}_{t})=l\geq R^{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}}_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{t+\delta{t}},\Lambda^{2}_{t+\delta{t}})
.

∎

Note that Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
do not directly imply each other.

III.2
CP-divisibility and compatibility of quantum measurements

The relation between compatibility of measurements and CP-divisibility of dynamical maps has been studied in detail in Ref.
Addis et al. 2016
;
Kiukas et al. 2023
. Here, we further study it in the context of our present analysis.
In Ref.
Addis et al. 2016
, the authors studied the behaviour of incompatibility robustness of quantum measurements with respect to fixed noise measurements. Here, we show that incompatibility robustness of measurements (with respect to generic noise) for any pair of dynamical maps is upper bounded by incompatibility robustness of channels for that pair of dynamical maps.

Theorem 3
.

Consider an arbitrary pair of measurements
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\in\mathbbm{M}(\Omega_{M_{i}},\mathcal{H})\}_{i\in{1,2}}
and an arbitrary pair of dynamical maps
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{\Lambda^{1}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}=\{\Lambda^{2}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
. Then

\displaystyle\max_{M_{1},M_{2}}R_{M}(\Lambda^{1*}_{t}(M_{1}),\Lambda^{2*}_{t}(M_{2}))\leq R_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{t},\Lambda^{2}_{t}).

(13)

Proof.

Let
R_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{t},\Lambda^{2}_{t})=l(t)
. Then there exist two noise channels
\tilde{\Lambda}^{1}_{t}
and
\tilde{\Lambda}^{2}_{t}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{1}_{t}+l(t)\tilde{\Lambda}^{1}_{t}}{1+l(t)}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\Lambda

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{2}_{t}+l(t)\tilde{\Lambda}^{2}_{t}}{1+l(t)}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\Lambda

(15)

where
\Lambda\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})
with
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{2}=\mathcal{H}
.

Using the definition of dual maps, Eq. (
14
) and Eq. (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{1*}_{t}(M_{1}(i))+l(t)\tilde{\Lambda}^{1*}_{t}(M_{1}(i))}{1+l(t)}=\Lambda^{*}(M_{1}(i)\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}})

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{2*}_{t}(M_{2}(j))+l(t)\tilde{\Lambda}^{2*}_{t}(M_{2}(j))}{1+l(t)}=\Lambda^{*}(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\otimes M_{2}(j))

(17)

for all
i\in\Omega_{M_{1}}
and
j\in\Omega_{M_{2}}
. Let the measurement
M:=\{M(i,j)=\Lambda^{*}(M_{1}(i)\otimes M_{2}(j))\}
. Then clearly,

\displaystyle\sum_{j}M(i,j)=\frac{\Lambda^{1*}_{t}(M_{1}(i))+l(t)\tilde{\Lambda}^{1*}_{t}(M_{1}(i))}{1+l(t)}

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}M(i,j)=\frac{\Lambda^{2*}_{t}(M_{2}(j))+l(t)\tilde{\Lambda}^{1*}_{t}(M_{2}(j))}{1+l(t)}.

(19)

Hence, the measurements
M^{\prime}_{1}=\{\frac{\Lambda^{1*}_{t}(M_{1}(i))+l(t)\tilde{\Lambda}^{1*}_{t}(M_{1}(i))}{1+l(t)}\}_{i\in\Omega_{M_{1}}}
and
M^{\prime}_{2}=\{\frac{\Lambda^{2*}_{t}(M_{2}(j))+l(t)\tilde{\Lambda}^{2*}_{t}(M_{2}(j))}{1+l(t)}\}_{i\in\Omega_{M_{2}}}
are compatible. Thus, from the definition of incompatibility robustness of quantum measurements, we get
R_{M}(\Lambda^{1*}_{t}(M_{1}),\Lambda^{2*}_{t}(M_{2}))\leq l(t)=R_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{t},\Lambda^{2}_{t})
.

∎

Clearly, from Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
, it follows that the upper bound of
R_{M}(\Lambda^{1*}_{t}(M_{1}),\Lambda^{2*}_{t}(M_{2}))
is monotonically non-increasing if both
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are CP-divisible.

III.3
CP-divisibility and compatibility of dynamical maps

First, we define broadcasting quantum dynamical maps, as follows.

Definition 4
.

A broadcasting quantum dynamical map is a family of CPTP linear maps
\{\Lambda_{t,t_{0}}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})\}_{t}
(where
t\geq t_{0}
) with
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{2}
.

We denote the set of all broadcasting dynamical maps on
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
(i.e., from
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
to
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})
) with
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{2}
as
\mathbbm{BD}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{2})
. Again, without the loss of generality, we can take
t_{0}
to
0
. Now, we define the compatibility of dynamical maps.

Definition 5
.

Two dynamical map
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{\Lambda^{1}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}=\{\Lambda^{2}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
are said to be compatible if a joint broadcasting quantum dynamical map
\mathcal{J}=\{\Theta:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})\}_{t}
with
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{2}=\mathcal{H}
exists such that

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\mathcal{J};\penalty\ \mathcal{D}_{2}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\mathcal{J}.

(20)

where
\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}\mathcal{J}:=\{\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}\Theta\}_{t}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.

Definition
5
is similar to Definition
1
, but now compatibility relations should hold for all
t
.

Clearly, the implementation of
\mathcal{J}
is equivalent to the simultaneous implementation of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Note that the set of all compatible dynamical maps is convex. Therefore, a measure of incompatibility of quantum dynamical maps is the incompatibility robustness of quantum dynamical maps that we define below. The incompatibility robustness of two quantum dynamical maps
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{\Lambda^{1}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}=\{\Lambda^{2}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
can be defined as:

\begin{split}R_{D}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=\min\penalty\ &\left.r\right.\\
\text{s.t.}\penalty\ &\left.\frac{\mathcal{D}_{1}+r\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{1}}{1+r}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\mathcal{J}}\right.\\
&\left.\frac{\mathcal{D}_{2}+r\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{2}}{1+r}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\mathcal{J}}\right.\\
&\left.\mathcal{J}\in\mathbbm{BD}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{2})\right.\\
&\left.\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})\quad i=1,2.\right.\end{split}

(21)

Here
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
s are arbitrary noise dynamical maps, and the optimization is over all variables, other than the given pair of dynamical maps
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
.

Let us now, present the following Lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Consider two quantum channels
\Phi_{1}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{1})
and
\Phi_{2}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{2})
with
R_{C}(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})=l
. Then for all
\epsilon\geq 0
, there exists two quantum channels
\tilde{\Phi}_{1}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{1})
and
\tilde{\Phi}_{2}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{2})
such that the quantum channels
\frac{\Phi_{1}+l^{\prime}\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}{1+l^{\prime}}
and
\frac{\Phi_{2}+l^{\prime}\tilde{\Phi}_{2}}{1+l^{\prime}}
are compatible where
l^{\prime}=l+\epsilon\geq l
.

Proof.

As
R_{C}(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})=l
, there exist
\hat{\Phi}_{1}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{1})
,
\hat{\Phi}_{2}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{2})
and
\Phi\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{2})
such that

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{1}+l\hat{\Phi}_{1}}{1+l}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}\Phi

(22)

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{2}+l\hat{\Phi}_{2}}{1+l}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}\Phi.

(23)

Now consider two completely depolarizing channels (i.e., channels with fixed output states)
\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{1}}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{1})
and
\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{2}}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{2})
(where
\eta_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{K}_{1})
and
\eta_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{K}_{2})
) such that for all
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{1}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\eta_{1}

(24)

\displaystyle\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{2}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\eta_{2}.

(25)

In Ref.
(
Heinosaari and Miyadera 2017
, Proposition 10)
, it is proved that completely depolarizing channels are compatible with any quantum channel. Therefore,
\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{1}}
and
\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{2}}
are compatible. The corresponding joint channel is
\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}\in\mathbbm{Ch}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{2})
such that for all
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}.

(26)

Let
\gamma=\frac{\epsilon}{1+l}
. Clearly,
\gamma\geq 0
.

Now, consider the quantum channels

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{\Phi}_{1}+\gamma\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{1}}}{1+\gamma}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}\left[\frac{\Phi+\gamma\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}}{1+\gamma}\right]

(27)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{\Phi}_{2}+\gamma\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{2}}}{1+\gamma}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}\left[\frac{\Phi+\gamma\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}}{1+\gamma}\right].

(28)

Clearly,
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are compatible. Recall that
l^{\prime}=l+\epsilon
. Now, it can be easily shown that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{1}+l^{\prime}\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}{1+l^{\prime}}

(29)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{2}+l^{\prime}\tilde{\Phi}_{2}}{1+l^{\prime}}

(30)

where
\tilde{\Phi}_{1}=\frac{l\hat{\Phi}_{1}+\epsilon\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{1}}}{l+\epsilon}
and
\tilde{\Phi}_{2}=\frac{l\hat{\Phi}_{2}+\epsilon\hat{\Upsilon}_{\eta_{2}}}{l+\epsilon}
are valid quantum channels. Hence, the lemma is proved.

∎

Now, defining a quantity
R^{\max}_{C}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}):=\max_{t}R_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{t},\Lambda^{2}_{t})
, we state the following result.

Theorem 4
.

For two arbitrary quantum dynamical maps
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{\Lambda^{1}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}=\{\Lambda^{2}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
, the equality
R_{D}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=R^{\max}_{C}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
holds.

Proof.

Suppose that
R_{D}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=l
. Therefore, from Definition
5
and the definition of incompatibility robustness of dynamical maps, there exist two dynamical maps
\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{1}=\{\bar{\Lambda}^{1}_{t}\}_{t}
and
\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{2}=\{\bar{\Lambda}^{2}_{t}\}_{t}
such that the channels
\frac{\Lambda^{1}_{t}+l\bar{\Lambda}^{1}_{t}}{1+l}
and
\frac{\Lambda^{1}_{t}+l\bar{\Lambda}^{1}_{t}}{1+l}
are compatible for all
t
. We know that
R^{\max}_{C}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}):=\max_{t}R_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{t},\Lambda^{2}_{t})
and suppose the maximum occurs for
t=t^{\prime}
. Therefore,
R^{\max}_{C}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=R_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{t^{\prime}},\Lambda^{2}_{t^{\prime}})=h
(say). Now, as discussed above, the channels
\frac{\Lambda^{1}_{t^{\prime}}+l\bar{\Lambda}^{1}_{t^{\prime}}}{1+l}
and
\frac{\Lambda^{1}_{t^{\prime}}+l\bar{\Lambda}^{1}_{t^{\prime}}}{1+l}
are compatible. Then, from the definition of incompatibility robustness for quantum channels, we get

R_{D}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=l\geq R_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{t^{\prime}},\Lambda^{2}_{t^{\prime}})=R^{\max}_{C}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}).

(31)

Now, suppose
R_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{t},\Lambda^{2}_{t})=k_{t}
for an arbitrary
t
. Then
k_{t}\leq h
. Next, from Lemma
1
, it follows that there exist quantum channels
\tilde{\Lambda}^{1}_{t}
and
\tilde{\Lambda}^{2}_{t}
such that such that
\frac{\Lambda^{1}_{t}+h\tilde{\Lambda}^{1}_{t}}{1+h}
and
\frac{\Lambda^{2}_{t}+h\tilde{\Lambda}^{2}_{t}}{1+h}
are compatible for all
t
. Therefore, from the definition of incompatibility robustness of the dynamical maps, one obtains

R_{D}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})\leq h=R^{\max}_{C}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}).

(32)

Therefore, from inequalities (
31
) and (
32
), it
follows that

R_{D}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=h=R^{\max}_{C}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}).

(33)

∎

From Theorem
1
and Theorem
4
, one
can obtain the following result.

Corollary 5
.

For two arbitrary CP-divisible quantum dynamical maps
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{\Lambda^{1}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}=\{\Lambda^{2}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
, the equality
R_{D}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=R_{C}(\Lambda^{1}_{0},\Lambda^{2}_{0})
holds.

Clearly, this corollary relates CP-indivisibility with incompatibility of channels.

IV
Illustration of incompatibility robustness of quantum channels for several dynamical maps

In this Section, we study the behaviour of incompatibility robustness of quantum channels for some specific dynamical maps, for both CP-divisible and CP-indivisible regime. Our goal is to study if the information backflow induced by CP-indivisibility, can be witnessed from non-monotonic behaviour of incompatibility robustness of quantum channels with respect to time.

To obtain the incompatibility robustness for two quantum channels
\Lambda_{1}(t)
and
\Lambda_{2}(t)
, we implement the following algorithm, incorporating semi-definite optimization techniques. For the examples we consider here, the input Hilbert space (
\mathcal{H}_{in}
) and the output Hilbert spaces (
\mathcal{H}_{i,out}
) of both channels are the same as
\mathbbm{C}^{d}
(i.e.,
d
-dimensional complex Hilbert space for finite
d
), where
d=2
.

I.

Fix a value of time
t=0
, and we obtain the Choi matrices,
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{1}(t=0)},\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{2}(t=0)}
, of the channels, where
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{i}(t=0)}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{in}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{i,out})
. We denote
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{i}(t=0)}
as
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{i}}
.

II.

We start from the value of
r=0
, and execute the following optimization:

\displaystyle\max q

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{1}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{in}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{1,out}),\ \mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{1}}\geqslant 0,\ \text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{1,out}}(\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{1}})=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{in}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{2}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{in}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2,out}),\ \mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{2}}\geqslant 0,\ \text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{2,out}}(\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{2}})=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{in}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{in}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{1,out}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2,out}),\ \mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}\geqslant q\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{in}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{1,out}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2,out}},

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{2,out}}(\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma})=\frac{\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{1}}+r\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{1}}}{1+r},

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{1,out}}(\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma})=\frac{\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda_{2}}+r\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{2}}}{1+r}.

(34)

•

If the value of
q
is negative, it indicates that
\Lambda_{1}(t=0)
and
\Lambda_{2}(t=0)
are incompatible for that specific value of
r
. In this case, we proceed by repeating step (
II
) with an updated value of
r
, i.e.,
r=r+\delta r
.

•

If the value of
q
is greater than or equal to zero, it signifies that
\Lambda_{1}(t=0)
and
\Lambda_{2}(t=0)
have become compatible. We store the current value of
r
as the incompatibility robustness. Subsequently, we return to step (
I
) and increment the parameter
t
by
\delta t
.

The incompatibility robustness with respect to completely depolarising (CD) noise can be determined using the same method. In this case, instead of the constraint
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{i}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{in}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{i,out}),\penalty\ \mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{i}}\geqslant 0,\penalty\ \text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{i,out}}(\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{i}})=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{in}}
, the constraint
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\Lambda}_{i}}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{in}}\otimes\eta_{i},\eta_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{i,out})\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \eta_{i}\geqslant 0,\penalty\ \text{Tr}[\eta_{i}]=1
needs to be imposed for each
i=1,2
. We refer the readers to the Ref.
git
for the codes to generate some of plots that have been discussed below. The codes for the other plots are similar.

V
CP-indivisibility and incompatibility of channels as
resource for quantum teleportation

Quantum teleportation is a very well-known quantum communicaion protocol
Bennett et al. 1993
;
Popescu 1994
. Although perfect quantum teleportation can be performed using maximally entangled states, it can be performed imperfectly with mixed non-maximal entangled states, in general. A criterion for a two-qubit state to be useful in quantum teleportation is provided in Ref.
Horodecki et al. 1996
. Consider a two-qubit state
\rho_{AB}
. Define a
3\times 3
matrix
S_{\rho_{AB}}=[s(\rho_{AB})_{ij}]
such that
s(\rho_{AB})_{ij}=\text{Tr}[\rho_{AB}(\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j})]\penalty\ \forall i,j\in\{x,y,z\}
where
\sigma_{j}
s are Pauli matrices. Let,
N(\rho_{AB})=Tr[\sqrt{S_{\rho_{AB}}^{\dagger}S_{\rho_{AB}}}]
. A quantum state is useful for teleportation iff
N(\rho_{AB})>1
and in this case, the maximum fidelity is
F_{max}\equiv\frac{1}{2}[1+\frac{1}{3}N(\rho)]>2/3
Horodecki et al. 1996
.

Now, consider the two-qubit maximally entangled state
\ket{\Psi_{-}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[\ket{01}-\ket{10}]
where
\{\ket{0},\ket{1}\}
are the eigen basis of
\sigma_{z}
. Let us take the dynamical map
\mathcal{D}_{2}=\{\Lambda^{2}_{t}\}_{t}
from Section
IV
. On application of
\Lambda^{2}_{t}
on the state
\ket{\Psi_{-}}\bra{\Psi_{-}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}_{AB}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{I}_{A}\otimes\Lambda^{2}_{t})(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle=w(t)\ket{\Psi_{-}}\bra{\Psi_{-}}+(1-w(t))\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{4\times 4}}{4}

(39)

where
w(t)=e^{-\lambda t}cos^{2}\omega t
with
\lambda=0.5
and
\omega=5\pi
. Now,

\displaystyle S_{\rho^{\prime}_{AB}}=\begin{bmatrix}-w(t)&0&0\\
0&-w(t)&0\\
0&0&-w(t)\end{bmatrix}.

(40)

It follows that
N(\rho^{\prime}_{AB})=3w(t)
and therefore,
F_{max}=\frac{1}{2}[1+\frac{1}{3}w(t)]
for

and otherwise,

VI
Measuring CP-indivisibility using incompatibility robustness of quantum channels

In this section, we propose a measure of CP-indivisibility based on incompatibility robustness of quantum channels. As we can see from the study in Sec.
IV
, the incompatibility robustness of channels shows the signature of CP-indivisibility induced information backflow through non-monotonic behaviour. Therefore, it is evident that an information backflow measure based measure of CP-indivisibility
(
Laine et al. 2010
)
can be constructed using the incompatibility robustness of channels. Here, we follow the procedure proposed by Laine et.al
(
Laine et al. 2010
)
to construct such a measure of CP-indivisibility.

Consider a dynamical map
\mathcal{D}=\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
. We have to construct a CP-indivisibility measure of
\mathcal{D}
. For this, take an arbitrary CP-divisible dynamical map
\bar{\mathcal{D}}=\{\bar{\Lambda}_{t}\}_{t}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
that acts as a reference CP-divisible dynamical map. Let
\theta(t):=\frac{dR_{C}(\Lambda_{t},\bar{\Lambda}_{t})}{dt}
. Then, we define the CP-indivisibility measure
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{D})
as

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{D})=\sup_{\bar{\mathcal{D}}\in\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{P}}}\int_{\theta(t)>0}R_{C}(\Lambda_{t},\bar{\Lambda}_{t})dt

(41)

where
\mathbbm{DM}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{P}}
is the set of all CP-divisible dynamical map acting from
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
to
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. Clearly,
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{D})\geq 0
.
We obtain from Theorem
1
that this measure is always
zero
for CP-divisible dynamical maps (as
\theta(t)\leq 0
for those dynamical maps). But non-monotonic behaviour of incompatibility robustness of channels in Fig.
3
and Fig.
4
suggests that that the proposed measure is non-zero for the CP-indivisible depolarising dynamical map
\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Similarly, non-monotonic behaviour of incompatibility robustness of channels in Fig
5
suggests that this measure is non-zero for the CP-indivisible amplitude damping dynamical map
\mathcal{D}_{ad}
. Therefore, in short, there exists CP-indivisible dynamical maps for which this measure is non-zero. It is evident that as the expression of
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{D})
consists of integration with respect to time
t
over the range from
0
to
\infty
, this measure
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{D})
can take an arbitrary value and it is not normalised. For the sake of compactness, we can also propose a normalised measure of the form

\mathfrak{N}(\mathcal{D})=\frac{\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{D})}{1+\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{D})}

(42)

Clearly, for any dynamical map
\mathcal{D}
,
0\leq\mathfrak{N}(\mathcal{D})\leq 1
and
\mathfrak{N}(\mathcal{D})=0
for CP-divisible dynamical maps (as
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{D})=0
for CP-divisible dynamical maps) and
\mathfrak{N}(\mathcal{D})
is
non-zero
whenever
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{D})
is non-zero.
Our proposed measure is on a similar footing of the information backflow based non-Markovianity measure
(
Laine et al. 2010
)
.

VII
conclusions

To summarize, in this work, we have considered two important features of quantum theory,
viz.
CP-indivisibility and incompatibility of channels, that arise naturally in several practical situations of quantum dynamics.
These two properties have been utilized as resources in several quantum information processing protocols. Our present analysis enables the characterization of CP-indivisibility of dynamical maps using incompatibility of channels. We have shown that incompatibility robustness of channels for two CP-divisible dynamical maps is monotonically non-increasing with respect to time. We have shown that for two dynamical maps and for a particular time
t
, the incompatibility robustness of quantum measurements, is upper bounded by the incompatibility robustness of quantum channel.

Furthermore, we have explicitly analyzed the case of qubit depolarising dynamical maps and qubit amplitude damping dynamical maps as examples. We have shown that in the case of the CP-divisible regime, incompatibility robustness of channels is monotonically non-increasing with respect to time. But in the case of a CP-indivisible regime, it loses its monotonic behaviour in both cases. The examples studied here clearly illustrate the simultaneous presence of information backflow from the environment to the system, as reflected by the non-monotonic behaviour of the trace distance and non-monotonic behaviour of the incompatibility robustness of quantum channels for both generic and completely depolarising noise models. We have further shown through an example
how information backflow acts as a resource for quantum teleportation. Additionally, we have proposed a measure of CP-indivisibility based on incompatibility robustness of quantum channels.

The results obtained from our present study motivate certain directions of future research. It may be worthwhile to explore whether the incompatibility robustness of quantum channels can be used to witness CP-indivisible maps,
such as the one studied in Section IV C, that do not show information backflow
Hall et al. 2014b
. Moreover, it would also be interesting to investigate whether the CP-indivisibility measure proposed in Sec.
VI
, can be useful to quantify the performance of some specific information-theoretic or thermodynamic tasks.

Gao Huang, Song Li, Hang Xu

Abstract
We investigate the phase retrieval problem perturbed by dense bounded noise and sparse outliers that can change an adversarially chosen s s -fraction of the measurement vector. The adversarial sparse outliers may exhibit dependence on both the observation and the measurement. We demonstrate that the nonlinear least absolute deviation based on amplitude measurement can tolerate adversarial outliers at a fraction of s ∗ , 1 ≈ 0.2043 s^{*,1}\approx 0.2043 , while the intensity-based model can tolerate a fraction of s ∗ , 2 ≈ 0.1185 s^{*,2}\approx 0.1185 . Furthermore, we construct adaptive counterexamples to show that the thresholds are theoretically sharp, thereby showing the presentation of phase transition in the adversarial phase retrieval problem when the corruption fraction exceeds the sharp thresholds. This implies that the amplitude-based model exhibits superior adversarial robustness in comparison with the intensity-based model. Corresponding experimental results are presented to further illustrate our theoretical findings. To the best of our knowledge, our results provide the first theoretical examination of the distinction in robustness performance between amplitude and intensity measurement. A crucial point of our analysis is that we explore the exact distribution of some combination of two non-independent Gaussian random variables and present the novel probability density functions to derive the sharp thresholds.



Keywords:
Phase Retrieval; Adversarial Sparse Outliers; Nonlinear Least Absolute Deviation; Sharp Thresholds; Amplitude and Intensity Measurement.

1.1
Our Contributions

We summarize the main contributions of this work.
We first give a general framework in phase retrieval for the analyses of nonlinear LAD model based on a general set
\mathcal{K}
under the corruption of adversarial sparse outliers. It is an underlying paradigm for dealing with different measurements to elaborate the corresponding ROB conditions.
We present the probability density function (PDF) of the combination
\bigl|\left\lvert X\right\lvert-\left\lvert Y\right\lvert\bigr|
and
\left\lvert X\cdot Y\right\lvert
of two Gaussian variables (may be not normal or independent)
X
,
Y
. The PDF of
\bigl|\left\lvert X\right\lvert-\left\lvert Y\right\lvert\bigr|
may be first presented in this paper.
Based on this, we give the nonlinear ROB condition to cope with the adversarial outliers.
We then give the sharp sparsity threshold
s^{*,1}\approx 0.2043
of adversarial outliers in the nonlinear LAD model for amplitude measurement.
For intensity-based nonlinear LAD model, we give the sharp sparsity threshold
s^{*,2}\approx 0.1185
without the Rademacher distribution assumption for outliers.
Our analyses of these two models leads to the first theoretical examination of the distinction in robustness performance between amplitude and intensity measurement such that the amplitude-based nonlinear LAD model has superior adversarial robustness in comparison to the intensity-based one.

\displaystyle w\left(\mathcal{K}\right)\geq c\delta\sqrt{\log\mathcal{N}\left(\mathcal{K},\delta\right)}.

A Framework for Adversarial Phase Retrieval

3.1
Nonlinear Robust Outlier Bound Condition

For simplification, the corrupted measurements are written as

\boldsymbol{b}=\left\lvert{\boldsymbol{A}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert^{k}+\boldsymbol{\omega}+\boldsymbol{z},\quad\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{0}\leq sm,\quad k=1,2.

In this section
we have considered a unified form for amplitude measurement and intensity measurement without distinction.
Due to the adversarial sparse outliers
\boldsymbol{z}
’s dependence on the measurement system
(\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{A})
and ground-truth
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}
,
as well as the the nonlinearity of operators
\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lvert
and
\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lvert^{2}
, we give the definition of nonlinear ROB condition.

Definition 1

(Nonlinear Robust Outlier Bound Condition)
.

For amplitude measurement (
k=1
) or intensity measurement (
k=2
), and adversarial corruption friction
s
, sampling matrix
\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is said to satisfy the nonlinear robust outlier bound condition,
if there exists a constant
\mathcal{C}\left(s\right)>0
depending on
s\in\left[0,1\right]
,
such that the following holds for all vectors
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{K}
,

Lemma 1

(Adversarial Sparse Outlier Separation Condition)
.

For adversarial corruption fraction
s
, assume the sampling matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
satisfies the nonlinear ROB condition with coefficient
\mathcal{C}\left(s\right)>0
,

.

Proof.

By definition of
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\star}

, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\star}\right\lvert^{k}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert^{k}-\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\lVert_{1}\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\lVert_{1}.

Since

and
\mathcal{S}^{c}
, we can get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\star}\right\lvert^{k}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert^{k}-\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathcal{S}}\right\lVert_{1}+\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\star}\right\lvert^{k}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert^{k}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}\right\lVert_{1}\ \leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathcal{S}}\right\lVert_{1}+\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\omega}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}\right\lVert_{1}.

The triangle inequality yields that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\star}\right\lvert^{k}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert^{k}-\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathcal{S}}\right\lVert_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathcal{S}}\right\lVert_{1}-\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\star}\right\lvert^{k}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert^{k}\right\lVert_{1},

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\star}\right\lvert^{k}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert^{k}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}\right\lVert_{1}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\star}\right\lvert^{k}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert^{k}\right\lVert_{1}-\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\omega}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}\right\lVert_{1}.

should encompass all the subsets of

∎

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{+\infty}zg_{\rho,\alpha}(z)dz}{\mathrm{dist}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\bigl|\left\lvert X\right\lvert-\left\lvert Y\right\lvert\bigr|\right)}{\mathrm{dist}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lvert X+Y\right\lvert+\left\lvert X-Y\right\lvert-\left\lvert X\right\lvert-\left\lvert Y\right\lvert\right)}{\mathrm{dist}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}+\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}-\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lVert_{2}-\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}}{\mathrm{dist}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\left(1+\alpha^{2}+2\alpha\rho\right)^{1/2}+\left(1+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\rho\right)^{1/2}-1-\alpha}{\left(1+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\rho\right)^{1/2}}.

Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi\left(\rho,\alpha\right)}{\partial\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\alpha\left(\alpha^{2}+1\right)}{\left(1+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\rho\right)^{3/2}}\left[\frac{2}{\left(1+\alpha^{2}+2\alpha\rho\right)^{1/2}}-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha^{2}+1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\alpha\left(\alpha^{2}+1\right)}{\left(1+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\rho\right)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{2}{\alpha+1}-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha^{2}+1}\right)\geq 0.

Therefore, for fixed
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right]
,
\varphi\left(\rho,\alpha\right)
is increasing with respect to
\rho
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi\left(0,\alpha\right)}{\partial\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\alpha-1}{\left(1+\alpha^{2}\right)^{3/2}}\leq 0.

Then we can get

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left(0\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\rho,\alpha}\varphi\left(\rho,\alpha\right)=\min_{\alpha}\varphi\left(0,\alpha\right)=\varphi\left(0,1\right)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\left(2-\sqrt{2}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left(1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\max_{\rho,\alpha}\varphi\left(\rho,\alpha\right)=-\max_{\alpha}\varphi\left(1,\alpha\right)=-\varphi\left(1,\alpha\right)=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}.

(c)
The continuity of
\mathcal{M}\left(s\right)
is obvious. By Proposition
1
.(a) and Proposition
1
.(b),
\mathcal{M}\left(s\right)
is bounded, thus it is well-defined on
\left[0,1\right]
. Then by monotonicity of
\mathcal{M}\left(s\right)
, the uniqueness of zero can be obtained.
∎

(20)

Since

When

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\left\lvert Z_{\rho,\alpha}\right\lvert}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\rho}}\right\lVert_{\psi_{2}}=\left\lVert\frac{\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a},{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle\right\lvert-\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a},{\boldsymbol{y}}\rangle\right\lvert}{\mathrm{dits}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)}\right\lVert_{\psi_{2}}\leq\left\lVert\langle\boldsymbol{a},\frac{{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}}\rangle\right\lVert_{\psi_{2}}=\left\lVert X\right\lVert_{\psi_{2}}<\infty,

We then consider the mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{R^{+}}\cup\{+\infty\}

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Gamma\left(t\right)\right\lVert_{\psi_{2}}.

By the definition of sub-Gaussian norm, we verified that

is a continuous mapping.
Apart from this, the mapping

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

\displaystyle e^{i\theta}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Gamma\left(t\right)\right\lVert_{\psi_{2}}.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big|\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a},{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle\right\lvert-\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a},{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\rangle\right\lvert-\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a},{\boldsymbol{y}}\rangle\right\lvert+\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a},{\boldsymbol{y}}_{0}\rangle\right\lvert\big|&=\mathbb{E}\big|\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a},{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}\rangle\right\lvert-\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a},{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}-{\boldsymbol{y}}_{0}\rangle\right\lvert\big|\\

Due to Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{m}\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\right\lvert-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{y}\right\lvert-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\right\lvert+\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{y}_{0}\right\lvert\right\lVert_{1}\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\delta\left(1+\epsilon\right)\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-c_{1}m\epsilon^{2}\right).

(25)

We then obtained that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\min_{\mathcal{S}\subset[m],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm}\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lvert-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lvert\right\lVert_{1}-\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lvert-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}}{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lvert\right\lVert_{1}\\
&\geq m\left[\mathcal{M}\left(s+\xi\right)-2l_{1}\xi\right]\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{y}}_{0}\right)-\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lvert-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lvert+\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{y}}_{0}\right\lvert\right\lVert_{1}\\
&\geq m\left[\mathcal{M}\left(s+\xi\right)-2l_{1}\xi\right]\cdot\left(\mathrm{dist}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)-\delta\right)-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\delta\left(1+\epsilon\right)m\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)\\
&\geq m\left[\mathcal{M}\left(s+\xi\right)-2l_{1}\xi\right]\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)-\left[\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\left(1+\epsilon\right)+\mathcal{M}\left(0\right)\right]m\delta\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)\\
&=m\left[\mathcal{M}\left(s+\xi\right)-2l_{0}\xi\right]\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right).\end{split}

Case 3
: We assume that
\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}
. Then
{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{K}

Then by Sudakov’s minoration inequality in (

), for any

as we have provided

.

4.3
Nonlinear ROB Condition for Intensity Measurement

It can be checked that

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\right\lvert^{2}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{y}\right\lvert^{2}\right\lVert_{1}}{\mathrm{dist}_{2}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{i},\frac{{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}}\rangle\cdot\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{i},\frac{{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{y}}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}}\rangle\rvert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle\cdot\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{i},\boldsymbol{v}\rangle\rvert:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert X\cdot Y\rvert,

where we set
\boldsymbol{u}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\boldsymbol{v}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{y}}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
X=\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,1\right),Y=\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{i},\boldsymbol{v}\rangle\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,1\right)
.
The correlation coefficient between random variables
X
and
Y
is

\rho:=\langle\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\rangle
,
then by Lemma 2 in
[
18
]
, the PDF of
\left|Z_{\rho}\right|:=\left|X\cdot Y\right|
is

f_{\rho}\left(z\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}e^{-z/2}&\rho=\pm 1\\
\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\cosh\left(\frac{\rho z}{1-\rho^{2}}\right)K_{0}\left(\frac{z}{1-\rho^{2}}\right)&-1<\rho<1,\end{cases}

(26)

where
K_{0}(\cdot)
denotes the modified Bessel function of the second kind of order zero. For completeness, we attach the proof of (
26
) to Appendix
B
.
Then we set the CDF of
|Z_{\rho}|
as

\displaystyle F_{\rho}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f_{\rho}(z)dz.

And we define the balance function for
\left\lvert Z_{\rho}\right\lvert
:

\displaystyle J\left(\rho,s\right)=J_{1}\left(\rho,s\right)-J_{2}\left(\rho,s\right):=\left[\int_{0}^{F_{\rho}^{-1}\left(1-s\right)}-\int_{F_{\rho}^{-1}\left(1-s\right)}^{+\infty}\right]zf_{\rho}(z)dz.

We can only consider
\rho\geq 0
, thus the minimum balance function here is

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\left(s\right)=\min_{\rho\in\left[0,1\right]}\left[\int_{0}^{F_{\rho}^{-1}\left(1-s\right)}-\int_{F_{\rho}^{-1}\left(1-s\right)}^{+\infty}\right]zf_{\rho}\left(z\right)dz.

(27)

Now we present the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{x}}=\{\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{1},{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle,\cdots,\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{m},{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle\}
where
\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i.i.d}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}\left(0,\boldsymbol{I}_{n}\right)
.
Assume that
0\textless s\textless s^{*,2}\approx 0.1185
and
c_{0}
is a positive numerical constant corresponding to
l_{0}
.
If

\displaystyle m\gtrsim\xi^{-4}\cdot w^{2}\left(\text{cone}\left(\mathcal{K}\right)\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right),

then for any
s<s^{*,2}
,
\boldsymbol{A}
satisfies the
s
-nonlinear ROB condition for all
{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{K}
:

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m}\min_{\left\{\mathcal{S}\subset\left[m\right],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm\right\}}\Bigl[\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lvert^{2}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lvert^{2}\right\lVert_{1}&-\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lvert^{2}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}}{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lvert^{2}\right\lVert_{1}\Bigr]\\
&\geq\left[\mathcal{J}\left(s+\xi\right)-l_{0}\xi\right]\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{2}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)\end{split}

(28)

with probability at least
1-\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-c_{0}m\xi^{2}}\right)
.

Remark 6
.

The distinction between nonlinear ROB condition and ROB condition in intensity measurement, as discussed in
[
18
]
, lies in the specific ranges of consideration. The former limits the consideration range to
\boldsymbol{u}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}},\boldsymbol{v}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{y}}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lVert_{2}}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, while the latter considers the tangent space
T=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}{\boldsymbol{y}}^{*}+{\boldsymbol{y}}{\boldsymbol{x}}^{*}:{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
. However, both conditions ultimately lead to the same threshold value of 0.1185.

Similar to the Corollary
1
, if we set
s=0
and
s=1-\xi
in the nonlinear ROB condition for intensity measurement respectively, wo have the following corollary.

Corollary 2

When
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
m\gtrsim\xi^{-4}\cdot n
with probability at least

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{\pi}-\tilde{l}\xi\right)\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{2}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{m}\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lvert^{2}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lvert^{2}\right\lVert_{1}\leq\left(1+\tilde{l}\xi\ \right)\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{2}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right).

Therefore, our nonlinear ROB condition can be seen as a version with outliers in the stability of phase retrieval in

\begin{split}&\min_{\mathcal{S}\subset[m],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm}\frac{\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lvert^{2}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lvert^{2}\right\lVert_{1}-\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\lvert^{2}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}}{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\lvert^{2}\right\lVert_{1}}{\mathrm{dist}_{2}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\right)}\\
&\geq\min_{\mathcal{S}\subset[m],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm}\frac{\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert^{2}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{y}}_{0}\right\lvert^{2}\right\lVert_{1}-\left\lVert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}}{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\right\lvert^{2}-\left\lvert\boldsymbol{A}_{{\mathcal{S}}}{\boldsymbol{y}}_{0}\right\lvert^{2}\right\lVert_{1}}{\mathrm{dist}_{2}\left({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{y}}_{0}\right)}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\boldsymbol{a}^{*}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}-\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\boldsymbol{v}_{0}^{*}\rangle\right\lvert\\

Here, in the penultimate inequality, we use Lemma
6
for
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\right\lVert\leq\delta,\left\lVert\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{0}\right\lVert\leq\delta
and in the last inequality we set

.

Finally, we can finish the proof with probability at least

\displaystyle\begin{split}&1-\left(\mathcal{N}\left(\mathcal{K}^{-}_{\delta},\delta\right)\times\mathcal{N}\left(\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\delta},\delta\right)\right)\left(\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-cm\xi^{2}}\right)+2e^{-c_{1}m\delta^{2}}\right)\\
\geq&1-C_{0}\exp\left[w^{2}\left(\text{cone}\left(\mathcal{K}\right)\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right)/\xi^{2}\right]\cdot\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-c_{2}m\xi^{2}}\right)=1-\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-c_{0}m\xi^{2}}\right),\end{split}

provided
m\gtrsim\xi^{-4}\cdot w^{2}\left(\text{cone}(\mathcal{K})\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right)
.

Appendix A
Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz Type Inequality

The following lemma is a corollary of the standard Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz inequality, similar proof can be found in
[
21
,
40
]
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\Gamma=\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m}\}
be i.i.d. sampled from any continuous distribution function
F
on
\mathbb{R}
. Then for any
\eta,\epsilon\in[0,1]
, the following holds with probability at least
1-4e^{-2m\epsilon^{2}}
,

\displaystyle F^{-1}\left(\eta-\epsilon\right)<\widehat{F}^{-1}(\eta)<F^{-1}\left(\eta+\epsilon\right).

Here,
\widehat{F}
is the associated empirical distribution function defined by
\widehat{F}\left(x\right)={1\over m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbbm{1}_{\xi_{i}\leq x}
.

Proof.

Standard Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz inequality
[
26
]
stated that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sqrt{m}\sup_{x}\left\lvert\widehat{F}\left(x\right)-F\left(x\right)\right\lvert\geq\lambda\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-2\lambda^{2}\right).

Set
x=F^{-1}\left(\eta-\epsilon\right)
and
\lambda=\epsilon\sqrt{m}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\widehat{F}\left(t\right)-\left(\eta-\epsilon\right)\right\lvert\geq\epsilon\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-2m\epsilon^{2}\right).

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{F}\left(t\right)\leq\eta\right)\geq 1-2\exp\left(-2m\epsilon^{2}\right).

Monotonicity of
\widehat{F}\left(x\right)
then implies the left-side inequality. The right-side inequality follows similarly.
∎

PDF of

We first consider the PDF of

Volumes of Subvarieties of Complex Ball Quotients and Sparsity of Rational Points
Soheil Memarian

Abstract
Let X = Γ \ 𝔹 n X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n} be an n n -dimensional complex ball quotient by a torsion-free non-uniform lattice Γ \Gamma whose parabolic subgroups are unipotent. We prove that the volumes of subvarieties of X X are controlled by the systole of X , X, which is the length of the shortest closed geodesic of X X . There are a number of arithmetic and geometric consequences: the systole of X X controls the growth rate of rational points on X , X, uniformly in the field of definition. Also, we obtain effective global generation and very ampleness results for multiples of the canonical bundle K X ¯ , K_{\overline{X}}, where X ¯ \overline{X} is the toroidal compactification of X . X. These results follow from the bound we find for the Seshadri constant of K X ¯ K_{\overline{X}} in terms of the systole.

Introduction

Let
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional complex ball quotient by a torsion-free lattice
\Gamma.
The complex ball has an intrinsic Hermitian metric (Bergman metric) which induces a Kähler form on
X.
This Kähler form also induces a Kähler form on a subvariety
V
of
X.
The volume of
V
with respect to the induced Kähler form on
X
will be called the induced Kähler volume and will be denoted by
\operatorname{vol}_{X}(V).

The main goal of this paper is to find a uniform lower bound
for the induced Kähler and canonical volumes of all subvarieties of a non-compact ball quotient
X
in terms of a geometric quantity of
X:

Theorem A
.

(Theorem
5.2
) Let
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
be a complex ball quotient by a torsion-free non-uniform lattice
\Gamma
whose parabolic stabilizers are unipotent. Let
V\subset X
be an irreducible subvariety of dimension
m>0.
Then,

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}_{X}(V)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(4\pi)^{m}}{m!}\sinh^{2m}\big(\operatorname{sys}(X)/2\big),

(1)

where
\operatorname{vol}_{X}(V)
is the volume of
V
induced by Bergman metric on
V
and
\operatorname{sys}(X)
is the length of the shortest closed geodesic in
X.

The systole is anon-zero real number and can be estimated by the trace of the hyperbolic elements in a representation of
\Gamma.
When
X
is compact ball quotient, the inequality (
1
) is known by Hwang and To
[
HT99
]
and we generalized their inequalities for non-compact
X
with a mild assumption on the parabolic stabilizer of
\Gamma.
Note that every neat lattice
\Gamma
satisfies this assumption, and every
\Gamma
has a finite index subgroup which satisfies this assumption (see
[
Hum98
]
).

With the same assumption as Theorem
A
on
X,
admits a smooth projective toroidal compactification
\overline{X}
whose boundary divisor
D=\overline{X}\setminus X
is a disjoint union of abelian varieties with ample conormal bundle (
[
Mok12
]
). Bakker and Tsimerman
[
BT18
]
proved that if the uniform depth of cusps of
X
is sufficiently large, then the canonical bundle of the toroidal compactification
K_{\overline{X}}
is ample. We prove in Theorem
3.9
that the systole of
X
bounds the uniform depth of cusps from below. Therefore, if
\operatorname{sys}(X)
is sufficiently large, then
K_{\overline{X}}
is ample.

For a subvariety
V\subset\overline{X}
of dimension
m>0,
we denote the degree of
V
with respect to the line bundle
K_{\overline{X}}
by
\operatorname{deg}_{\overline{X}}(V):

\operatorname{deg}_{\overline{X}}(V):=K_{\overline{X}}^{m}\cdot V.

Also, we study the canonical volume of a subvariety
V
which is an intrinsic quantity of
V
and a priory does not depend on the ambient space
\overline{X}.
Let
V^{\prime}
be a smooth variety birational to
V
with a canonical bundle
K_{V^{\prime}}.
The canonical volume of the variety
V
is

\widetilde{\operatorname{vol}}_{V}:=\limsup\limits_{b\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{h^{0}(V^{\prime},bK_{V^{\prime}})}{b^{m}/m!},

which does not depend on the choice of
V^{\prime}.
In particular, if
V
is an integral curve, then the canonical volume of
V
is
2g-2.
The canonical volume of
V
measures the asymptotic growth rate of the pluri-canonical linear series
|bK_{V^{\prime}}|.
The canonical volume is a non-negative real number and it is positive if and only if the linear system

|bK_{V^{\prime}}|
embedds
V^{\prime}
birationally in a projective space for a large enough
b
, i.e.,
V
is of general type.

We prove that the systole controls both the canonical volume of
V
and its degree with respect to
K_{\overline{X}}
in the following sense:

Theorem B
.

(Theorem
5.5
+
Theorem
5.7
) With the same assumption on
X
as Theorem
A
, let
\overline{X}
be the toroidal compactification of
X
and
V\subset\overline{X}
be a subvariety of dimensional
m>0
with
X\cap V\neq\varnothing.
Suppose that
\operatorname{sys}(X)\geq 4\ln\big(5n+(8\pi)^{4}\big).

Then the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\operatorname{vol}}_{V}

\displaystyle>(\frac{m}{4\pi})^{m}e^{m\operatorname{sys}(X)/16},

\displaystyle\operatorname{deg}_{\overline{X}}(V)

\displaystyle>(\frac{n}{4\pi})^{m}e^{m\operatorname{sys}(X)/16}.

Note that systole cannot decrease in a cover and for every
X
there exists a finite cover
X^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{sys}(X^{\prime})
is sufficiently large. As a byproduct of Theorem
B
, we can observe that in a tower of covering of
X
where the systole increases, the canonical volume of subvarieties increases.

Application I: Sparsity of rational points

A smooth toroidal compactification

\overline{X}
of
X
can be defined over a number field
F.
(see
[
Fal84
]
) provided that
\Gamma
is neat and arithmetic. Combining Theorem

with
[

on the growth rate of rational points, we get that

controls the growth rate of rational points:

Corollary C
.

(Corollary
8.2
) Let
L=K_{\overline{X}}
and
\epsilon
be a positive number. Suppose that
\operatorname{sys}(X)\geq 4\ln\big(5n+(4\pi)^{4}\big).
Then, there exists a constant
c
depending on

and

such that for every
B\geq\epsilon[F:\mathbb{Q}],
one has:

\displaystyle\#\Big\{x\in X(F)\mid\operatorname{H}_{L}(x)\leq B\Big\}\leq cB^{\delta},

where

\delta=\frac{4\pi[F:\mathbb{Q}](n+3)}{e^{\operatorname{sys}(X)/16}}(1+\epsilon),

and

tells us that the growth rate of

Previous Results and Comparison

Besides the results mentioned above, the central purposes of our paper are the following technical advancements on the subject:

\bullet

Corollary
C
does not follow from the main results of Ellenberg-Lawrence-Venkatesh
[
ELV23
]
or Brunebarbe-Maculan
[
BM22
]

or Chiu
[
Chi22
]
on the growth rate of integral points.
In general, bounding the growth rate of rational points on a quasi-projective variety is more difficult than bounding the growth rate of integral points. For example, on
X=\mathbb{P}_{F}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\},
there are infinitely many
F
-rational points; however, there are only finitely many integral points on
X
because of the famous theorem of Siegel (see
[
BM22
, Remark 3.3]
).

It is observed in
[
ELV23
,
BM22
]
that if one has a control on the degree of all subvarieties, the bound on the growth rate of rational points improves in the strategy of Bombieri-Pila
[
BP89
]
and Heath-Brown
[
HB02
]
. However, to get the lower bound on the degree of subvarieties, they passed to an étale cover and this restricts them to get result only on the integral points, rather than rational points. The point is that when one pulls back rational points along finite étale maps on quasi-projective variety, the field of definition can not be controlled, but for integral points, it can be.
Our intrinsic approach has the advantage that does not require passage to a cover to raise the degree of subvarieties and hence we can get the bound on the growth rate of rational points.

\bullet

Our results show that the hyperbolicity properties of a non-compact ball quotient can be controlled by its systole, and hence the injectivity radius of the interior and depth of cusps need not to be dealt with separately (see for example
[
Won18
]
for the other approach). In our paper, this is achieved by proving that the systole bounds both the uniform depth of cusps (Theorem
3.9
) and injectivity radius of the thick part (§
4
) from below.

\bullet

Our results depend intrinsically on
X,
and it is not required to pass to a cover of
X
to apply them. In particular, our results apply even in the case that
X
is not a normal cover of another variety (see for example
[
Yeu12
, Theorem 5]
and
[
DCL21
, Corollary 1.6.]
in which the passage to a cover of
X
is required. Indeed, these results are about a cover of
X
rather than
X
itself).

\bullet

In a tower of covering, it is easy to estimate the behavior of systoles (one only needs to compute the trace of hyperbolic elements) and conclude the increasing hyperbolicity behavior via our results. This kind of increasing hyperbolicity behavior has been extensively studied for locally symmetric spaces in towers with high ramifications at cusps (see for example
[
Nad89
,
Bru20a
,
Bru20b
,
Cad22
,
AVA18
,
AVA17
,
Rou16
]
). However, our approach does not require high ramifications of cusps in towers and our results can be applied even in towers where some of the cusps do not ramify.

The following are some of the previous effective results for pluricanonical bundles:

1.

Yeung proved in

Hwang proved in

Strategy of Proof

To prove Theorem
A
, we decompose
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
into two disjoint parts. The first part is the thin neighborhoods around the cusps. This part consists of all points on
X
which have a displacement less than
\operatorname{sys}(X)/2
with respect to a parabolic element in
\Gamma.

The second part is the complement of the first part, which we call the thick part. In Proposition
4.2
we prove that the thick part is not empty. Moreover, we prove in Theorem
4.4
that every subvariety of
\overline{X}
which does not intersect the boundary
D
contains a point from the thick part. Therefore, using the inequality proved by Hwang-To
we conclude that the volume of a subvariety
V
of
X
is controlled by the systole. On the other hand, we prove in Theorem
3.9
that the uniform depth of cusps is controlled by the systole. Combining this with an inequality proved by the author in
[
Mem22
]
it follows that the canonical volume of a subvariety of
\overline{X}
which intersects with
X
is also controlled by the systole. The bounds on the degree of a subvariety with respect to
K_{\overline{X}}
follow from the ampleness of
K_{\overline{X}}
and Theorem
A
.

2.1
Geometry of complex call quotients

The complex
n
-ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
is defined as

\mathbb{B}^{n}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\ |\ |z|^{2}<1\}.

\mathbb{B}^{n}
has an intrinsic Hermitian metric called Bergman metric. The holomorphic isometry group of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with respect to this metric is the projective unitary group

G:=\operatorname{PU}(n,1)=\dfrac{\operatorname{U}(n,1)}{Z(\operatorname{U}(n,1))},

where the centre
Z(\operatorname{U}(n,1))
can be identified with the circle group
\{\mu I:|\mu|=1\}.

The group
G
acts transitively on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
and acts doubly transitively on the boundary sphere
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}.
The stabilizer of the center of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
is
\operatorname{U}(n).
Every isometry
g\in G
is continuous
on the closed ball
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
and it follows from Brouwer’s fixed point theorem that
g
has a fixed point on the closed ball
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
. Moreover, if there is no fixed point on
\mathbb{B}^{n},
there can be at most two fixed points on the boundary sphere
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}.
Accordingly,
an isometry
g\in G
is classified as:

1.

Elliptic:
g
has a fixed point
z
in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. After conjugating
g
via
h\in G
which
sends
z
to
0
,

hgh^{-1}\in\operatorname{U}(n),

and therefore all eigenvalues of
g
are roots of unity.

2.

Parabolic:
g
has a unique fixed point in
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
and this fixed point is on the boundary
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}.
Equivalently,

\displaystyle\inf_{z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}}\ d(z,gz)=0,

where
d(.,.)
denotes the Bergman metric. This infimum is not realized for a parabolic
g.

3.

Hyperbolic:
g
has exactly two fixed points in
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
and both are in
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}.
In
particular,
g
preserves the unique geodesic connecting these two fixed points in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
and acts as a translation along this geodesic. This geodesic is called the axis of
g.
The length of a hyperbolic isometry
g\in G
is

\ell(g):=\displaystyle\inf_{z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}}\ d(z,gz).

This infimum is not zero and is realized by any point on the axis of
g.
The work of Chen-Greenberg on the conjugacy classification of element of
\operatorname{U}(n,1)

[
CG74
, Theorem 3.4.1]
implies that a hyperbolic isometry
g
has two eigenvalues
re^{i\theta}
and
r^{-1}e^{i\theta}
with
r>1
and
n-1
eigenvalues with norm
1.

Let
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{PU}(n,1)
be a torsion-free lattice whose parabolic elements are unipotent.
[
Hum98
]
tells us that every lattice in
\operatorname{PU}(n,1)
has a finite index subgroup with this property. With this property, an element
g\in\Gamma
is hyperbolic if and only if
g
is semi-simple. Therefore, we will denote the set of the hyperbolic elements in
\Gamma
by
\Gamma_{s}.

Let
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}.
The systole of
X
is the length of the shortest closed geodesic with respect to the Bergman metric:

\operatorname{sys}(X):=\displaystyle\inf_{g\in\Gamma_{s}}\ell(g)=\displaystyle\inf_{g\in\Gamma_{s}}\{d(z,gz)|z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}\}.

Equivalently, the systole of
X
is the length of the shortest hyperbolic element in
\Gamma.
Consider
x\in X
with stabilizer
\Gamma_{x}
in
\Gamma.
Choose a fiber
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{x}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}\in\mathbb{B}^{n}.
The injectivity radius of
x
in
X
is defined to be

\operatorname{inj}_{x}(X):=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{inf}_{\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{x}}d(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{x}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}},\gamma\cdot\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{x}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}),

which is independent of choice of
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{x}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}.
The injectivity radius of
X
is
\operatorname{inj}(X):=\operatorname{inf}_{x\in X}\operatorname{inj}_{x}(X).
In the case that
X
is compact,
\Gamma
only has semi-simple elements and hence
\operatorname{sys}(X)=inj(X)/2.
However, this relation does not hold for a non-compact
X
because of the parabolic elements in
\Gamma.

2.2
Siegel domain model

The half-plane model of
1
-dimensional complex ball quotient is generalized by the Siegel domain model in higher dimensions. In
horospherical coordinates, the Siegel domain of (complex) dimension

n
is
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
. The points of
\mathbb{S}
will be written as
(\zeta,v,u)\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}.
The boundary of
\mathbb{S}
is

H_{0}\cup\{\infty\},

where
\infty
is a distinguished point at infinity and
H_{0}=\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}.

We will follow
[
Par98
]
to describe
\operatorname{PU}(n,1)
as a matrix group by embedding the Siegel domain as a paraboloid in
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n,1}).
To do so, we should choose a Hermitian form of signature
(n,1)
on
\operatorname{PU}(n,1).
Let

J_{0}:=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&I_{n-1}&0\\
1&0&0\\
\end{bmatrix},

and
Q:=z^{*}J_{0}z,

where
z
is a column vector in
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n,1})
and
z^{*}
is the Hermitian transpose of
z.

Consider the map
\psi:\overline{\mathbb{S}}\to\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n,1})
given by

\displaystyle\psi:(\zeta,v,u)\longrightarrow\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(-||\zeta||^{2}-u+iv)\\
\zeta\\
1\end{bmatrix},\ \mbox{for}\ (\zeta,v,u)\in\overline{\mathbb{S}}\backslash\{\infty\};\ \psi:\infty\longrightarrow\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

(2)

The image of this map is the set of points in
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n,1})
, where the
Hermitian form
Q
is negative. Also
\psi
is a homeomorphism of
\partial\mathbb{S}
onto the set of
points where
Q
is zero. We will refer to
q_{\infty}:=\psi(\infty)
as the cusp at
\infty
and
q_{0}:=\psi(0)
as the cusp at
0.

The holomorphic isometry group of
\mathbb{S}
with respect to the
Bergman metric is the projective unitary group
\operatorname{PU}(Q)
and it acts on
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n,1})
by
matrix multiplication. A matrix
h\in\operatorname{GL}(n+1,\mathbb{C})
is in
\operatorname{PU}(Q)
if and only if its inverse
h^{-1}
is given by
J_{0}h^{*}J_{0}
, where
∗
denotes the
Hermitian transpose; that is, transpose the matrix and complex conjugate each
of its entries. The general form for
h\in PU(Q)
and its inverse is given in
[
Par98
, pg 438]
:

\displaystyle h=\begin{bmatrix}a&\tau^{*}&b\\
\alpha&A&\beta\\
c&\delta^{*}&e\end{bmatrix},\ \ \ h^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{e}&\beta^{*}&\bar{b}\\
\delta&A^{*}&\tau\\
\bar{c}&\alpha^{*}&\bar{a}\end{bmatrix},

(3)

where
A
is an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix,
a,b,c,e\in\mathbb{C},
and
\tau,\delta,\alpha,\beta
are column vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}.

The following lemma easily follows:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
h
be an element of
PU(Q)
written in form
3
.

1.

(
[
Par97
, page 7]
) If
h
swaps
q_{\infty}
and
q_{0},
then it must have the following form:

h=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&1/\overline{c}\\
0&A&0\\
c&0&0\end{bmatrix},

where
A\in U(n-1)
and
c\in\mathbb{C}.
Therefore,
h
must be the following transformation:

h:(\zeta,u,v)\longrightarrow\Big(\dfrac{-2A\zeta}{c(||\zeta||^{2}+u-iv)},\dfrac{-4v}{\big|c\big|^{2}\big|||\zeta||^{2}+u-iv\big|^{2}},\dfrac{4u}{\big|c\big|^{2}\big|||\zeta||^{2}+u-iv\big|^{2}}\Big).

2.

If
h
fixes both the cusps
q_{\infty}
and
q_{0}
, then it must have the following form

h=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
0&A&0\\
0&0&1/\bar{a}\end{bmatrix},

where
A\in U(n-1)
and
a\in\mathbb{C}.

For any pair of points
z_{1}=(\zeta_{1},v_{1},u_{1})
and
z_{2}=(\zeta_{2},v_{2},u_{2})
in
\mathbb{S}
the Bergman metric is given by:

\displaystyle d(z_{1},z_{2})=2\cosh^{-1}\Big(\dfrac{1}{4u_{1}u_{2}}\big|||\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}||^{2}+u_{1}+u_{2}+iv_{1}-iv_{2}+2i\operatorname{im}\langle\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\rangle\big|^{2}\Big),

(4)

where
\langle.,.\rangle
denotes the standard positive definite Hermitian form on
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
. Since
\cosh^{-1}(x)
is increasing, the following lower bound can be obtained for the metric :

\displaystyle d\big((\zeta_{1},v_{1},u_{1}),(\zeta_{2},v_{2},u_{2})\big)\geq 2\cosh^{-1}\Big(\dfrac{|u_{1}+u_{2}|^{2}}{4u_{1}u_{2}}\Big)

(5)

The holomorphic sectional curvature of this metric is
-1
and the sectional curvature of this metric varies on
[-1,-\frac{1}{4}]
(see
[
Gol99
]
). It follows that the holomorphic bisectional curvature of this metric is bounded above by a negative constant since the holomorphic bisectional curvature always can be written as the sum of two sectional curvatures.

2.3
Toroidal Compactification

The complex ball quotient
X
has a unique toroidal compactification
\overline{X},
which is a smooth projective variety (see
[

]
). The boundary divisor of this compactification

is a disjoint union of abelian varieties with ample conormal bundle. The Kähler form of the Bergman metric on

is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathbb{S}}:=-2i\partial\bar{\partial}\log(u)

(6)

(see
[
BT18
, Lemma 2.1]
). Let

be the Kähler form induced by the Kähler form

It follows from Mumford’s work on the singular Hermitian metric

Integration against

on the open part represents (as a current) a multiple of the first Chern class

\displaystyle\operatorname{c}_{1}(K_{\overline{X}}+D)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{n+1}{2}[\omega_{X}]\in H^{1,1}(\overline{X},\mathbb{R}),

where

is the canonical bundle of

(see

).

2.4
Stabilizer of cusps

We denote the parabolic stabilizer
of
q_{\infty}
in

by

With our choice of Hermitian form, the matrices corresponding to elements of

are upper triangular. There is an equivalent way to identify these matrices:

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
Par98
]
) Let
h
be an element of
\operatorname{PU}(Q)
written in the form
3
. Then,
h
fixes the cusp

if and only if the

entry of

is

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle h\cdot q_{\infty}=\begin{bmatrix}a&\tau^{*}&b\\

0\\

\alpha\\

and therefore

fixes

projectively if and only if

and

Note that if the

is

the multiplication of the matrix of

and

in the form

(and also

∎

The group

is generated by Heisenberg isometries

and a one-dimensional torus

. Heisenberg isometries consist of Heisenberg Rotations

and Heisenberg translations

. Heisenberg Rotations

acts on
\zeta
-coordinates of
\mathbb{S}
in the usual way and Heisenberg translations

\zeta
and

\mathbb{S}
via

The element
(0,t)\in\mathfrak{N}
will be called the vertical translation by
t
, and the subgroup generated by
(0,t)
in

will be denoted by

. The vertical translation

is the center of
G_{\infty}
and the quotient

is isomorphic to the unitary transformation of

\mathbb{C}^{n-1}.

A Heisenberg translation

fixing

corresponds to the matrix
g_{\infty}
and a Heisenberg translation
(\sigma,s)\in\mathfrak{N}
fixing

corresponds to the matrix

where

\displaystyle g_{\infty}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\tau^{*}&-(|\tau|+it)/2\\
0&I&\tau\\
0&0&1\\
\end{bmatrix},\ \ \ g_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
\sigma&I&0\\
-(|\sigma|+is)/2&-\sigma^{*}&1\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(8)

With our assumption on
\Gamma,
all parabolic stabilizers of

in

i.e.,

are Heisenberg translations.

2.5
Neighborhood of cusps

A horoball centered at the cusp
q_{\infty}
with height
\tilde{u}
is the open set

B_{\infty}(\tilde{u}):=\{(\zeta,v,u)\in\mathbb{S}\ |\ u>\tilde{u}\}.

The smallest
\tilde{u}
such that
\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash B_{\infty}(\tilde{u})
injects into
X
will be called the height of the cusp
q_{\infty}
and will be denoted by
u_{\infty}.

The complex ball quotient
X
has a finitely many cusps and they are in one-to-one correspondence with the equivalence classes of parabolic fixed points of
\Gamma.
For a cusp
q_{i}
, there exists a
g\in\operatorname{PU}(Q)
translating
q_{i}
to
q_{\infty}.

The horoball based at cusp
q_{i}
with height
\tilde{u}
is defined to be the image of the horoball based at cusp
q_{\infty}
with height
\tilde{u}
translated by
g:

B_{i}(\tilde{u})=g\cdot B_{\infty}(\tilde{u})

Accordingly, the height of cusp
q_{i}
is the smallest
\tilde{u}
such that
\Gamma_{i}\backslash B_{i}(\tilde{u})
injects into
X,
where
\Gamma_{i}
is the parabolic stabilizer of
q_{i}.
We will denote the height of
q_{i}
by
u_{i}.
Thanks to the Parker’s generalization of Shimuzu’s lemma
[
Par98
]
,
B_{i}(\tilde{u})
must inject into
X
for a large enough
\tilde{u}.
Note that the height coordinate
u
on
\mathbb{S}
is invariant under the action of Heisenberg rotations
\operatorname{U}(n-1)
and Heisenberg translations
\mathfrak{N}
and hence the horoball is invariant with respect to the action of these groups.

Let
t_{i}
be the shortest vertical translation in the parabolic stabilizer of
q_{i}
. The number
d_{i}=t_{i}/u_{i}
is called the depth of cusp
q_{i}
which is invariant under conjugating
\Gamma.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
BT18
, Definition 3.7.]
) The uniform depth of the cusps of
X
is the largest
d
satisfying the following properties:

1.

for every
i
,
d\leq d_{i}
(this gives that
\Gamma_{i}\backslash B_{i}(t_{i}/d)
injects into
X
).

2.

all
B_{i}(t_{i}/d)
are disjoint.

3
Systole and depth of cusps

Let
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n},
where
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{PU}(n,1)
is a torsion-free lattice whose parabolic stabilizers are unipotent.
The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem
3.9
,
where we show that the systole
\operatorname{sys}(X)
bounds the uniform depth of cusps
d
of
X
from below.

To see the relation between the systole and depth of cusps, we first prove that the length of a hyperbolic element in
\Gamma
only depends on its non-unit eigenvalues:

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
h\in\Gamma
is a hyperbolic element whose non-unit eigenvalues are
re^{i\theta}
and
r^{-1}e^{i\theta}
. Then,

\ell(h)=2\cosh^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\cdot(r+\frac{1}{r})^{2}\Big).

Proof.

Since
h
is hyperbolic, it fixes two distinct points
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
on the boundary
\partial\mathbb{S}.
As
\operatorname{PU}(Q)
acts doubly transitive on the boundary, there exists
P\in\operatorname{PU}(Q)
such that
P(x_{1})=q_{0}
and
P(x_{2})=q_{\infty}.
Now we can write

\displaystyle d(x,hx)=d(Px,PhP^{-1}Px)=d(x^{\prime},PhP^{-1}x^{\prime}),

where
x^{\prime}=Px.
Suppose
x^{\prime}=(\zeta_{1},v_{1},u_{1}),
and
PhP^{-1}x^{\prime}=(\zeta_{2},v_{2},u_{2}).
Since
PhP^{-1}
fixes both
q_{0}
and
q_{\infty},
it follows from Lemma
2.1
that for a complex number
a
and
A\in\operatorname{U}(n-1)

\displaystyle PhP^{-1}x^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
0&A&0\\
0&0&1/\bar{a}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(-||\zeta_{1}||^{2}-u_{1}+iv_{1})\\
\zeta_{1}\\
1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{a}{2}(-||\zeta_{1}||^{2}-u_{1}+iv_{1})\\
A\zeta_{1}\\
1/\bar{a}\end{bmatrix}.

This gives that
\zeta_{2}=\bar{a}A\zeta_{1}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(-||\zeta_{2}||^{2}-u_{2}+iv_{2})=\frac{|a|^{2}}{2}(-||\zeta_{1}||^{2}-u_{1}+iv_{1}).

(9)

Therefore,
u_{2}=|a|^{2}u_{1}
and
v_{2}=|a|^{2}v_{1}.
Note that as conjugation does not change the eigenvalues, we have that
|a|^{2}=r^{2}\ \mbox{or}\ \frac{1}{r^{2}}.
On the other hand,
the inequality
5
yields that

\displaystyle d\big(x^{\prime},PhP^{-1}x^{\prime}\big)

\displaystyle\geq 2\cosh^{-1}\Big(\dfrac{|u_{1}+u_{2}|^{2}}{4u_{1}u_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq 2\cosh^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\cdot(r+\frac{1}{r})^{2}\Big).

Since this lower bound is realized on
\zeta_{1}=0,v=0,
we can conclude the desired equality.
∎

Consider
\gamma\in\Gamma
and the corresponding element in the matrix group
g_{\gamma}\in\operatorname{PU}(Q).
When we talk about
|\operatorname{tr}(\gamma)|,
we mean
|\operatorname{tr}(g_{\gamma})|.
Proposition
3.1
tells us if for every hyperbolic element
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have that
|\operatorname{tr(\gamma)}|
is sufficiently large, then systole
\operatorname{sys}(X)
will be large, i.e., the systole
\operatorname{sys}(X)
can be estimated just be the trace of the hyperbolic elements. In particular, the systole goes to infinity in a cofinal tower of any ball quotient.

Consider the set associated to
\Gamma

S_{\Gamma}:=\big\{\gamma\in\Gamma\big||\operatorname{tr}(\gamma)|>n+1\big\}.

It follows from the classification of isometries that if
\gamma\in\Gamma
has
|\operatorname{tr}(\gamma)|>n+1,
then
\gamma
must be hyperbolic. Hence, all elements of
S_{\Gamma}
are hyperbolic.
We associate the number

\lambda_{\Gamma}:=\displaystyle\inf_{\gamma\in S_{\Gamma}}|\operatorname{tr}(\gamma)|,

to
\Gamma
which will play a role as an intermediate quantity to relate the systole of
X
to the depth of cusps of
X
. Specifically, we can see how
\operatorname{sys}(X)
bounds
\lambda_{\Gamma}
from below:

Proposition 3.2
.

The following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\lambda_{\Gamma}\geq 1-n+\sqrt{2}e^{\operatorname{sys}(X)/4}.

Proof.

Consider
\gamma\in S_{\Gamma}.
Let
re^{i\theta}
and
r^{-1}e^{i\theta}
be eigenvalues of
\gamma
which are not units. As
\operatorname{sys}(X)
is the lengtg of the shortest geodesic, Proposition
3.1
implies that

r+r^{-1}\geq\sqrt{4\cosh\big(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sys}(X)\big)}.

Since the other
n-1
eigenvalues of
\gamma
have norm
1,
and
\cosh(x)>\frac{1}{2}e^{x},
by the triangle inequality we can conclude the desired inequality.

∎

We prove a lemma which will help us to see the relation between the height of cusp
q_{\infty}
in terms of the trace of the hyperbolic elements in
\Gamma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{\infty}
written in from
3
. For every
z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
the following inequality holds:

u(z)u(\gamma\cdot z)\leq\big|\dfrac{2}{c}\big|^{2}.

Proof.

There are unique Heisenberg transformations
h_{1},h_{2}
such that
h_{1}(0)=\gamma(\infty)
and
h_{2}^{-1}(0)=\gamma^{-1}(\infty).
Consider
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}=h_{1}^{-1}\gamma h_{2}^{-1}
and note that as the Heisenberg translations are stabilizers of the
u-
coordinate, we have that

u(\gamma z)=u(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}z).
Since
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}
swaps
\infty
and
0,
Lemma
2.1
tells us that
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}
must have the form:

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}:(\zeta,v,u)\longrightarrow\Big(\dfrac{A\zeta r_{\gamma}^{2}}{||\zeta||^{2}+u-iv},\dfrac{-vr_{\gamma}^{4}}{\big|||\zeta||^{2}+u-iv\big|^{2}},\dfrac{ur_{\gamma}^{4}}{\big|||\zeta||^{2}+u-iv\big|^{2}}\Big)

where
A\in U(n-1)
and
r_{\gamma}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{|c|}}.
This gives that

u(z)u(\gamma\cdot z)=u(z)u(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\gamma}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}\cdot z)=\dfrac{u^{2}}{|||\zeta||^{2}+u-iv|^{2}}\cdot\big|\dfrac{2}{c}\big|^{2}\leq\big|\dfrac{2}{c}\big|^{2}.

∎

Let
c_{m}
be the minimal value of

|c|
among all
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{\infty}
written in the form
3
:

\displaystyle\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&\tau^{*}&b\\
\alpha&A&\beta\\
c&\delta^{*}&e\end{bmatrix}.

(10)

Note that as
\gamma\not\in\Gamma_{\infty},
the
c
entry cannot be zero (see Remark
2.2
). Lemma
3.3
implies that:

Proposition 3.4
.

The horoball

B_{\infty}(\frac{2}{c_{m}})

injects into
X,
i.e., the height of the cusp
q_{\infty}
is at least
2/c_{m}
and therefore the depth of
q_{\infty}
is at least
\frac{c_{m}\cdot t_{\infty}}{2}.

We recall a lemma from Parker’s version of Shimizu’s lemma which tells us how the
c
entry shows up in the trace of (some of) hyperbolic elements:

Lemma 3.5
.

(see
[
Par98
, Lemma 2.6]
)
Let
g_{\infty}=(0,t),
be the vertical translation centered at
q_{\infty}

and
h
be an element of
\operatorname{PU}(n,1)
written in the form
3
.
Then,

\operatorname{tr}[g_{\infty},h]=n+1+|\frac{tc}{2}|^{2}.

Proof.

The matrix representations of
g_{\infty},

h
and

are given by:

g_{\infty}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&i\frac{t}{2}\\
0&I&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix},\ h=\begin{bmatrix}a&\tau^{*}&b\\

c&\delta^{*}&e\end{bmatrix}\ h^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{e}&\beta^{*}&\bar{b}\\
\delta&A^{*}&\tau\\
\bar{c}&\alpha^{*}&\bar{a}\end{bmatrix},

To find
\operatorname{tr}[g_{\infty},h]
, note that

g_{\infty}h_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}a+\frac{i}{2}ct&\tau^{*}+\frac{i}{2}t\delta^{*}&b+\frac{i}{2}te\\

Therefore, using the relation given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}[g_{\infty},h]

\displaystyle=a\bar{e}+\frac{1}{4}|ct|^{2}+\frac{it}{2}(c\bar{e}-\bar{c}a)+\delta\tau^{*}+\frac{i}{2}t|\delta|^{2}+b\bar{c}+\frac{i}{2}te\bar{c}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+\alpha\beta^{*}-\frac{i}{2}t|\alpha|^{2}+AA^{*}+\beta\alpha^{*}+c\bar{b}-\frac{i}{2}t\bar{a}c+\delta^{*}\tau+e\bar{a}

\displaystyle=n+1+\frac{1}{4}|ct|^{2}

Now, we can show the relation between the quantity
\lambda_{\Gamma}
and depth of each cusps of

Proposition 3.6
.

The depth of each cusp of

is at least
\sqrt{\lambda_{\Gamma}-n-1}.

Proof.

Since
\lambda_{\Gamma}
is invariant under the conjugation by an element of
\operatorname{PU}(n,1)
, it is sufficient to prove the lemma for the cusp
q_{\infty}.

Let
g_{\infty}=(0,t_{\infty})
be the shortest vertical translation in
\Gamma_{\infty}.
Suppose that
h\in\Gamma
is an element which does not fix
q_{\infty}
and written in the form
3
. It follows from Lemma
3.5
that

\operatorname{tr}[g_{\infty},h]=n+1+\big|\dfrac{t_{\infty}c}{2}\big|^{2}.

Since
h
does not fix
\infty,
we have that
c\neq 0,
and therefore
[g_{\infty},h]\in S_{\lambda}.
This implies that

\big|\dfrac{t_{\infty}c}{2}\big|\geq\sqrt{\lambda_{\Gamma}-n-1}.

Since this inequality holds for every

we can conclude that

\big|\dfrac{t_{\infty}c_{m}}{2}\big|\geq\sqrt{\lambda_{\Gamma}-n-1}.

Hence, Proposition
3.4
implies

d_{\infty}\geq\sqrt{\lambda_{\Gamma}-n-1},

where

is the depth of cusp

∎

To pass from the individual depth of cups to the uniform depth of cusps we will use this lemma:

Lemma 3.7
.

(
[
Par98
, Lemma 2.5]
) Let
B_{0}(\tilde{u}_{0})
be the horoball of height
\tilde{u}_{0}
based at
q_{0}
, and let
B_{\infty}(\tilde{u}_{\infty})
be the horoball of height
\tilde{u}_{\infty}
based at
q_{\infty}.
These two horoballs are disjoint if and only if

\tilde{u}_{0}\cdot\tilde{u}_{\infty}\geq 4.

.

Let
d
be the uniform depth of cusps of
X
. Then,

d\geq\operatorname{min}\{(\lambda_{\Gamma}-n-1)^{\frac{1}{4}},(\lambda_{\Gamma}-n-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
, we only need to show that if
d>(\lambda_{\Gamma}-n-1)^{\frac{1}{4}},

the horoballs are disjoint. Let
g_{0}=(0,t_{0})
be the shortest vertical translation based at
q_{0}
and
g_{\infty}=(0,t_{\infty})
be the shortest vertical translation based at
q_{\infty}.
We write

and

in the form

:

\displaystyle g_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&I&0\\
-it_{0}/2&0&0\end{bmatrix},\ \ \ \ g_{\infty}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-it_{\infty}/2\\

Consider
\tilde{u}_{0}:=\frac{t_{0}}{(\lambda_{\Gamma}-n-1)^{\frac{1}{4}}}

\tilde{u}_{\infty}:=\frac{t_{\infty}}{(\lambda_{\Gamma}-n-1)^{\frac{1}{4}}}.

Let

be the uniform depth of cusps of

\displaystyle d\geq\operatorname{min}\{\big(-2n+s^{\prime}\big)^{\frac{1}{4}},\big(-2n+s^{\prime}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\},

s^{\prime}=\sqrt{2}e^{\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{sys}(X)}.

Corollary 3.10
.

If
\operatorname{sys}(X)\geq 4\ln\big(5n+(4\pi)^{4}\big),

\displaystyle d>e^{\operatorname{sys}(X)/16}>4\pi.

(11)

4
Thin-thick decomposition

In this section, we introduce a version of thin-thick decomposition relative to the systole. This is not the same as Margulis’ thin-thick decomposition as it depends on the lattice
\Gamma.
The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem
4.4
which tells us that the thin part of
X
does not have any subvariety.

Let
q_{i}
be a cusp of
X
with unipotent stabilizer
\Gamma_{i}.

Fix
\epsilon>0.

Consider the set

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{U}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}_{i,\epsilon}=\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}|\exists g\in\Gamma_{i},d(x,g\cdot x)<\epsilon\}.

We define the
\epsilon
-thin neighborhood around the cusp
q_{i}
as the set
U_{i,\epsilon}:=\Gamma\backslash\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{U}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}_{i,\epsilon}.
Also, we fix
\rho=\operatorname{sys}(X)/2
and define the thin part of
X
as union of all
\rho
-thin neighborhood around cusps of
X:

X_{thin}:=\displaystyle\cup_{i=1}^{k}{U_{i,\rho}}.

The following Proposition shows that
X_{thin}
is actually disjoint union of the
\rho
-thin neighborhood around cusps:

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\epsilon<sys(X)/2
, then
U_{i,\epsilon}\cap U_{j,\epsilon}=\varnothing
for
i\neq j.

Proof.

For the sake of the contradiction assume that
x\in U_{i,\epsilon}\cap U_{j,\epsilon}.
It means that there exist
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma_{j}
such that

d(\tilde{x},\gamma_{1}\cdot\tilde{x})<\epsilon
and
d(\tilde{x},\gamma_{2}\cdot\tilde{x})<\epsilon,
where
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
is a fiber of
x.
This in particular implies that
d(\tilde{x},\gamma_{1}^{-1}\cdot\tilde{x})<\epsilon.

Suppose
\gamma_{1}=(\tau,t)
is a Heisenberg translation based at
q_{\infty}
and
\gamma_{2}=(\sigma,s)
is a Heisenberg translation based at
q_{0}.

We represent
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
by the matrices
g_{\infty},g_{0}\in\operatorname{PU}(Q)
respectively, where

\displaystyle g_{\infty}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\tau^{*}&-(|\tau|+it)/2\\
0&I_{n-1}&\tau\\
0&0&1\\
\end{bmatrix},\ \ \ \ g_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
\sigma&I_{n-1}&0\\
-(|\tau|+is)/2&-\sigma^{*}&1\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Note that
\gamma_{1}^{-1}=(-\tau,-t)
corresponds to
g^{-1}_{\infty}.
We can write:

\displaystyle\big|\operatorname{tr}(g_{\infty}g_{0})\big|+\big|\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}_{\infty}g_{0})\big|\geq\Big|\operatorname{tr}\big((g_{\infty}+g_{\infty}^{-1})g_{0}\big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\operatorname{tr}\Big(\begin{bmatrix}2&0&-|\tau|^{2}\\
0&2I_{n-1}&0\\
0&0&2\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
\sigma&I_{n-1}&0\\
(-|\sigma|^{2}+is)/2&-\sigma^{*}&1\\
\end{bmatrix}\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\big|2(n+1)+\frac{1}{2}|\tau|^{2}(|\sigma|^{2}-is)\big|

\displaystyle\geq 2(n+1)+\frac{1}{2}|\tau|^{2}|\sigma|^{2}.

Hence, either
|\operatorname{tr}(g_{\infty}g_{0})|\geq n+1+\frac{1}{4}|\tau|^{2}|\sigma|^{2}
or
|\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}_{\infty}g_{0})|\geq n+1+\frac{1}{4}|\tau|^{2}|\sigma|^{2}
and therefore either
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}
or
\gamma_{1}^{-1}\gamma_{2}
must be hyperbolic. But this implies that either
d(\tilde{x},\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\cdot\tilde{x})\geq\operatorname{sys}(X)
or
d(\tilde{x},\gamma_{1}^{-1}\gamma_{2}\cdot\tilde{x})\geq\operatorname{sys}(X),
which is a contradiction because
d(\tilde{x},\gamma_{2}\cdot\tilde{x})<\operatorname{sys}(X)/2,

d(\tilde{x},\gamma_{1}\cdot\tilde{x})<\operatorname{sys}(X)/2,
and
d(\tilde{x},\gamma_{1}^{-1}\cdot\tilde{x})<\operatorname{sys}(X)/2.

∎

We define the thick part of
X
as the complement of the thin part:

X_{thick}:=X\setminus\displaystyle\cup_{i=1}^{k}{U_{i,\rho}}.

Since every point in a thin part of
X
has a displacement less than
\operatorname{sys}(X)/2,
the following Proposition tells us that
X_{thcik}\neq\varnothing.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists
x\in X
such that

\operatorname{inj_{x}(X)}\geq\operatorname{sys}(X)/2.

Proof.

Note that if
\gamma\in\Gamma
is not unipotent, then it is semi-simple and for every
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n},
we have
d(x,\gamma\cdot x)\geq\operatorname{sys}(X).

Now, assume for the sake of contradiction that for all
x\in X
we have
\operatorname{inj}_{x}(X)<\operatorname{sys}(X)/2.

Therefore, the thin part of
X
covers all
X.
However, this is not possible because the thin part of
X
is disjoint union of open sets by Proposition
4.1
and
X
is connected.

∎

Now we show that the monodromy of
\rho
-thin part of
X
around each cusp is in the stabilizer of that cusp:

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
\epsilon\leq\operatorname{sys}(X)/2.

Let
U^{\prime}_{i}
be a connected component of
U_{i,\epsilon}
and
\iota:U^{\prime}_{i}\to X
be the identity map. Then,
\iota(\pi_{1}(U_{i}^{\prime}))
is a subgroup of
\Gamma_{i}.

Proof.

As
\epsilon<\operatorname{sys}(X)/2,
Proposition
4.1
implies that
U_{i,\epsilon}
s are disjoint. Fix
x\in U^{\prime}_{i,\epsilon}
and let
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
be a loop at
x
which is representative of a class in
\iota(\pi_{1}(U^{\prime},x)).
Let
\tilde{x}
be a lift of
x
to the universal cover
\mathbb{B}^{n}.
As
\gamma
is fully contained in
U^{\prime}_{i,\epsilon},
it lifts to a loop
\tilde{\gamma}(t):[0,1]\to\tilde{U}_{i,\epsilon}
which starts at
\tilde{x}.
Therefore,
\tilde{y}:=\gamma\cdot\tilde{x}=\tilde{\gamma}(1)
is in
\tilde{U}_{i,\epsilon}
which means that
d(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})<\epsilon.
Let
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{i}
such that

d(\tilde{x},\gamma^{\prime}\cdot\tilde{x})<\epsilon.
By homogeneity, we have

d(\tilde{y},\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{-1}\cdot\tilde{y})=d(\gamma\cdot\tilde{x},\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{-1}\gamma\cdot\tilde{x})=d(\tilde{x},\gamma^{\prime}\cdot\tilde{x})<\epsilon.

Since
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{-1}
fixes
\gamma(q_{i})
and
\tilde{y}\in U_{i},
we get that
\gamma(q_{i})=q_{i}
and therefore
\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}.

∎

Finally, we show that every subvariety of
X
intersects with
X_{thick}
, that is, every subvariety of
X
contains a point whose injectivity radius in
X
is larger than
\operatorname{sys}(X)/2:

Theorem 4.4
.

Every subvariety of
\overline{X}
either intersects with
X_{thick}
or fully contained in
D.

Proof.

For the sake of the contradiction assume that there exists a connected subvariety
V
which is fully contained in
X_{thin}.
Without loss of generality we can assume that
V
is fully contained in a connected component of a thin part around the cusp
q_{\infty}.
Consider the function
-u
which is a plurisubharmonic function on the Siegel domain
\mathbb{S}
and invariant under the action of stabilizer
\Gamma_{\infty}
(see
[
BT18
, §2]
). Therefore, it follows from Lemma
4.3
that
-u
is a well-defined function on every component of the thin part around
q_{\infty}.
Hence,
-u
is a well-defined plurisubharmonic function on
V.
Notice that if a plurisubharmonic function achieves its maximum, it has to be constant. Since
V
is compact,
-u
must be constant on
V.

However, it is not possible because the Kähler form on
X
is induced from
-2i\partial\bar{\partial}\log(u)
(see
[
BT18
, Lemma 2.1]
) and if
-\log(u)
were constant, the volume of
V
would be zero.
∎

5
Volumes of Subvarieties

In this section, we prove Theorem
A
, and Theorem
B
.
We first state the Hwang and To’s theorem in the following way:

Theorem 5.1
.

(
[
HT02
, Theorem 1.1]
) Take
x\in X
with injectivity radius
r=\operatorname{inj}_{x}(X).
Let
B(x,r)
be Bergman ball of radius
r
centered at
x.
Suppose
V
is an
m
-dimensional subvariety of
X
passing through
x.

Then, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol_{X}(V\cap B(x,r))}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(4\pi)^{m}}{m!}\sinh^{2m}(r)\cdot\operatorname{mult}_{x}(V).

(12)

Hwang and To generalized the above-mentioned theorem for a general Hermitian symmetric domain in
[
HT00
,
HT02
]
.

In the compact case, Theorem
5.1
gives the lower bound on volume of subvarieties in terms of the injectivity radius of
X,
however, in the case that
X
is not compact, the injectivity radius of
X
escapes to zero as we get closer to the cusps. So we use the systole, the length of the shortest closed geodesic in
X,
as a geometric invariant of
X
to uniformly bound the volumes of all subvarieties of
X.
For a compact ball quotient, the systole is twice the injectivity radius. However, for a non-compact
X
the systole is still not zero and can be estimated by the trace of the hyperbolic elements in a representation of
\Gamma.

Theorem
4.4
tells us that every subvariety of
X
has a point with injectivity as large as
\operatorname{sys}(X)/2
. Hence, we will get the following theorem:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
V
be an
m
-dimensional subvariety of
\overline{X}
which is not contained in
D.
Then,

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}_{X}(V)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(4\pi)^{m}}{m!}\sinh^{2m}\big(\operatorname{sys}(X)/2\big).

(13)

Proof.

Theorem
4.4
implies that
V\cap X_{thick}\neq\varnothing.

By Proposition
4.2
there exits a point
x\in V
with
\operatorname{inj}_{x}(X)\geq\operatorname{sys}(X)/2.
Now, Theorem
5.1
gives:

\operatorname{vol}_{X}(V)\geq\frac{(4\pi)^{m}}{m!}\sinh^{2m}\big(\operatorname{sys}(X)/2\big).

∎

Corollary 5.3
.

With the same notation as Theorem
5.2
, we have that

\displaystyle(K_{\overline{X}}+D)^{m}\cdot V

\displaystyle\geq(n+1)^{m}\sinh^{2m}\big(\operatorname{sys}(X)/2\big).

Proof.

Theorem
5.2
together with
7
gives:

\displaystyle(K_{\overline{X}}+D)^{m}\cdot V

\displaystyle=\big(\frac{n+1}{4\pi}\big)^{m}m!\operatorname{vol}_{X}(V)\mbox{\ (by \ref{ChernForm})}

\displaystyle\geq(n+1)^{m}\sinh^{2m}\big(\operatorname{sys}(X)/2\big)\mbox{\ (by Theorem \ref{Bound Hyperbolic Volume})}

∎

We recall Bakker and Tsimerman’s theorem which tells us that
the uniform depth of cusps of
X
bounds the intersection numbers of
K_{\overline{X}}
with subvarieties of
\overline{X}
which is not contained in
D.

Theorem 5.4
.

(
[
BT18
, Corrolary 3.8]
) Suppose
d
is the uniform depth of cusps. Then,

K_{\overline{X}}+(1-\lambda)D

is ample for
\lambda\in(0,d(n+1)/4\pi).

Now, putting together Theorem
5.2
, Theorem
5.4
and what we proved for the uniform depth of cusps, Theorem
3.9
, yields a lower bound for the degree of
K_{\overline{X}}
on
V
in terms of
\operatorname{sys}(X):

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
V
be an
m
-dimensional subvariety of
\overline{X}
which is not contained in
D.
If
\operatorname{sys}(X)\geq 4\ln\big(5n+(4\pi)^{4}\big),
then

\displaystyle K_{\overline{X}}^{m}\cdot V

\displaystyle>(\frac{n}{4\pi})^{m}\cdot e^{m\operatorname{sys}(X)/16}.

(14)

Proof.

We deal separately with the following two cases:

1.

V\cap D=\varnothing:
In this case we have
K_{X}^{m}\cdot V=(K_{\overline{X}}+D)^{m}\cdot V

and from Corollary
5.3
we get that

\displaystyle K_{X}^{m}\cdot V

\displaystyle\geq(n+1)^{m}\sinh^{2m}\big(\operatorname{sys}(X)/2\big)

(15)

\displaystyle\geq(\frac{n}{4\pi})^{m}e^{m\operatorname{sys}(X)}\mbox{\ \ (becasue of the bound on the systole).}

(16)

2.

V\cap D\neq\varnothing:
Since
\operatorname{sys}(X)\geq 4\ln\big(5n+(4\pi)^{4}\big)),

11
gives that the uniform depth of cusp is at least
4\pi
and it follows from Bakker-Tsimerman’s theorem (Theorem
5.4
) that
K_{\overline{X}}
is ample. In particular, this implies that
K_{\overline{X}|D}
is ample. On the other hand, we know that the conormal bundle
-D_{|D}
is ample. Therefore, for every
i>1,
we have

\displaystyle K_{\overline{X}}^{m-i}\cdot(-D)^{i}\cdot V=-(K_{\overline{X}|D})^{m-i}\cdot(-D_{|D})^{i-1}\cdot V_{|D}<0.

(17)

By Bakker-Tsimerman’s theorem, Theorem
5.4
, we get that

\Big(K_{\overline{X}}-\big((n+1)d/4\pi-1\big)D\Big)^{m}\cdot V\geq 0.

Expanding this and combining with
17
gives:

\displaystyle K_{\overline{X}}^{m}\cdot V

\displaystyle\geq\big((n+1)d/4\pi-1\big)^{m}\cdot-(-D)^{m}\cdot V

\displaystyle\geq\big((n+1)d/4\pi-1\big)^{m}\mbox{(by ampleness of $-D$)}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(nd/4\pi\Big)^{m}\mbox{ (by \ref{depth Bounds 2})}

\displaystyle\geq\big(n/4\pi\big)^{m}\cdot e^{m\operatorname{sys}(X)/16}\mbox{ (by \ref{depth Bounds 2})}.

(18)

Combining
15
and
18
gives that:

\displaystyle K_{\overline{X}}^{m}\cdot V>(\frac{n}{4\pi})^{m}\cdot e^{m\operatorname{sys}(X)/16}.

∎

The volume of a line bundle
L
on an
m
-dimensional projective variety
V
is defined as the non-negative real number

\operatorname{vol}_{V}(L):=\limsup\limits_{b\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{h^{0}(V,bL)}{b^{m}/m!},

which measures the positivity of
L
from the point of view of birational geometry. If
L
is a nef line bundle on
V
, then
vol_{V}(L)=L^{n}.
Let
V^{\prime}
be a smooth variety birational to
V
with a canonical bundle
K_{V^{\prime}}.
The canonical volume of the subvariety
V
is

\widetilde{\operatorname{vol}}_{V}:=\limsup\limits_{b\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{h^{0}(V^{\prime},bK_{V^{\prime}})}{b^{m}/m!},

which does not depend on the choice of
V^{\prime}.

To prove the bound on the canonical volume for a subvariety which does not intersect the boundary
D,
we will use the following lemma proved by Brunebarbe:

Lemma 5.6
.

[
Bru20a
, Proposition 3.2]

Let
Y
be a Kähler manifold with Kähler form
\omega
. Assume that its holomorphic bisectional curvature is non-positive and that its holomorphic sectional curvature is bounded from above by
-c<0.
Then,

\frac{1}{c}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}\geq\omega.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
V
be an
m
-dimensional subvariety of
\overline{X}
which is not contained in
D.
If
\operatorname{sys(X)}\geq 4\ln\big(5n+(8\pi)^{4}\big),
then

\displaystyle\widetilde{\operatorname{vol}}_{V}>(\frac{m}{4\pi})^{m}e^{m\operatorname{sys}(X)/16}.

(19)

Proof.

Let
V^{\prime}\longrightarrow V
be a desingularization such that the set-theoretic preimage of the boundary divisor
D^{\prime}
is a normal crossing divisor. We consider two cases:

1.

V\cap D=\varnothing:
Let
\omega
be the Kähler form induced by the Bergman metric on
V^{\prime}.
Since the holomorphic sectional curvature of the Bergman metric is
-1
and the curvature decreases on a subvariety, we can apply Lemma
5.6
to get that
K_{V^{\prime}}-K_{\overline{X}|V^{\prime}}
is nef on
V^{\prime}
and therefore

\widetilde{\operatorname{vol}}_{V}=\operatorname{vol}_{V^{\prime}}(K_{V^{\prime}})\geq\operatorname{vol}_{V^{\prime}}(K_{\overline{X}|V^{\prime}}).

Since the uniform depth of cusp is sufficiently large, by Theorem
5.4
we get that
K_{\overline{X}}
is ample and therefore

\operatorname{vol}_{V^{\prime}}(K_{\overline{X}|V^{\prime}})=K_{\overline{X}|V^{\prime}}^{m}=K_{\overline{X}}^{m}\cdot V=\operatorname{deg}_{\overline{X}}(V).

We can conclude the desired inequality for this case by Theorem
5.5
.

2.

V\cap D\neq\varnothing:
Since
\operatorname{sys}(X)\geq 4\ln\big(5n+(4\pi)^{4}\big)),

11
gives that the uniform depth of cusp is at least
4\pi.
By
[
Mem22
, Theorem A]
we get that twisted log-cotangent bundle
\Omega_{V^{\prime}}^{1}(\operatorname{log}(D))\langle-rD^{\prime}\rangle
is ample for every
r\in(0,d/2\pi).
Taking determinate gives that
K_{V^{\prime}}+(1-mr)D^{\prime}
is ample for every
r\in(0,d/2\pi).
Therefore, with the bound on the depth we get that
K_{V^{\prime}}
is ample. On the other hand, we have that the bundle
-D^{\prime}_{|D^{\prime}}
is ample. Therefore, for every
i>1,
we have

\displaystyle K_{V^{\prime}}^{m-i}\cdot(-D^{\prime})^{i}\cdot V^{\prime}=-(K_{V^{\prime}|D})^{m-i}\cdot(-D^{\prime}_{|D^{\prime}})^{i-1}\cdot V_{|D^{\prime}}<0.

(20)

Set
r^{\prime}=d/2\pi-\epsilon,
for a small
\epsilon>0.

Expanding
\Big(K_{V^{\prime}}+(1-mr^{\prime})D^{\prime}\Big)^{m}\geq 0
and using
20
gives that

\displaystyle K_{V^{\prime}}^{m}

\displaystyle\geq\big(mr^{\prime}-1\big)^{m}(-D^{\prime}_{|D^{\prime}})^{m}

\displaystyle\geq\big(mr^{\prime}-1\big)^{m}\mbox{ \ (by the ampleness of $-D^{\prime}_{|D^{\prime}}$)}

\displaystyle\geq(md/4\pi)^{m}

\displaystyle\geq(\frac{m}{4\pi})^{m}e^{m\operatorname{sys}(X)/16}\mbox{ (by \ref{depth Bounds 2})}.

By the ampleness of
K_{V^{\prime}}
we get
\widetilde{\operatorname{vol}}_{V}=\operatorname{vol}_{V^{\prime}}(K_{V^{\prime}})=K_{V^{\prime}}^{m}

and hence the claim follows.

∎

In this section, we prove Corollary

Base-point freeness and very ampleness on

In this subsection, we prove that if the uniform depth of cusps is sufficiently large, then

Due to

Lemma 6.1
.

The line bundle

is base-point free and

The adjunction formula gives that

As the conormal bundle is ample and

-2D_{i|D_{i}}

]

∎

In the next two lemmas, we see how we can lift the sections from the restricted bundle to
\overline{X}.

on

will be denoted by

.

Suppose that the uniform depth of cusps is larger than

Then, the following hold:

\operatorname{Bs}\big(2K_{\overline{X}}\big)\cap D=\varnothing

2.

For any two points on different components of

there exists a global section of

which separate them.

Proof.

Let
L
be

1.

By Lemma

Consider the following exact sequence on

Writing the long exact sequence we can see that it is sufficient to show

As

∎

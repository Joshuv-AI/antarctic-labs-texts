Higher Topological Complexity For Fibrations
MELİH İS and İSMET KARACA

Abstract
. We introduce the higher topological complexity (TC n ) of a fibration in two ways: the higher homotopic distance and the Schwarz genus. Then we have some results on this notion related to TC, TC n or cat of a topological space or a fibration. We also show that TC n of a fibration is a fiber homotopy equivalence.

1.
Introduction

The works on determining the topological complexity have been developing since Farber first computed the number of the topological complexity (TC) for a topological space
[
4
]
(see
[
5
]
for a comprehensive analysis of the investigation of this concept). Note that such a space must be path-connected. Rudyak
[
9
]
has the number of higher topological complexity TC
n
for a topological space for the first time (similarly the space is path-connected). It is a reasoned generalization on existing works of TC because of the fact that TC
{}_{2}=
TC. After that Pavesic
[
8
]
gives a new point of view to the developing explorations by determining the topological complexity for a map. Such a map must be surjective and continuous or one can choose a surjective fibration. These studies have a common point: All of these investigations can be considered by using the concept of the Schwarz genus (secat) for a fibration, introduced by Schwarz
[
11
]
. One can use a fibrational substitute, as well. On the other hand, there is one more option, which is the homotopic distance
[
6
]
. It can be used for the computations of TC or TC
n
of a topological space or a map. The higher homotopic distance
[
2
]
is a general case of the homotopic distance and especially useful for the studies of TC
n
of a topological space. Since LS-category (cat) for a topological space or a map is the base point for the research of TC, we obtain cat by using both the Schwarz genus and the homotopic distance
[
3
,
6
]
.

These studies lead us to solve an open problem and introduce a new concept the higher topological complexity of a surjective fibration
f
between any path-connected topological spaces, denoted by TC
{}_{n}(f)
. For
n=2
, we show that TC
{}_{2}(f)=
TC
(f)
. In addition, we have that TC
{}_{n}(f)=
TC
{}_{n}(Y)
if
f
is
1_{Y}:Y\rightarrow Y
. Therefore, TC
{}_{n}(f)
is a general version of respective notions TC
(Y)
, TC
n
and TC
(f)
, where
f:X\rightarrow X^{{}^{\prime}}
is a surjective fibration and
Y
is path-connected. We define TC
{}_{n}(f)
by using the two options the Schwarz genus and the higher homotopic distance.

In this study, we first give explicit definitions and significant results of TC, TC
n
and their related invariants such as D
(f,g)
and cat. Then we express TC
(f)
as the homotopic distance. With this expression, we state some results on TC
(f)
. So, we introduce the definition of TC
{}_{n}(f)
for a surjective fibration
f
. Later, we state some properties on TC
{}_{n}(f)
. In addition, we prove that TC
{}_{n}(f)
is an invariant on fiber homotopy equivalence fibrations. We also research the relationship between TC
{}_{n}(f)
and the other related notions such as TC
{}_{n}(X)
and cat
(X)
. We choose some special fibrations as examples for computing their higher topological complexities. In Section
4
, we define the higher topological complexity of a surjective fibration by using secat.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin with recalling some basic notions and results on the homotopic distance, the topological complexities, and the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category.

Definition 2.1
.

[
6
]

Given two continuous functions
f
,
f^{{}^{\prime}}:X\rightarrow X^{{}^{\prime}}
. Then the
homotopic distance
for
f
and
f^{{}^{\prime}}
, denoted by
D(f,f^{{}^{\prime}})
, is the smallest integer
l\geq 0
satisfying that there is a covering

\displaystyle X=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{l}

and the restrictions of
f
and
f^{{}^{\prime}}
on
V_{j}
,
j=1,\cdots,l
, are homotopic, i.e.,
f_{|_{V_{j}}}\simeq f^{{}^{\prime}}_{|_{V_{j}}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
6
,
2
]

Let
f_{i}:X\rightarrow X^{{}^{\prime}}
be any continuous map for
i=1,\cdots,k
. Then the
higher homotopic distance
, denoted by
D(f_{1},\cdots,f_{k})
, is the smallest positive integer
l
for which

\displaystyle X=U_{1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{l}

is a covering and the condition

\displaystyle f_{1}^{j}|_{U_{j}}\simeq\cdots\simeq f_{k}^{j}|_{U_{j}}

satisfies for each
j=1,\cdots,l
. If such a covering does not exist, then
D(f_{1},\cdots,f_{k})
equals
\infty
.

Note that, in the definition of (higher) homotopic distance, the covering of
X
has
k+1
open sets
U_{0},U_{1},\cdots U_{k}
, but we consider that
U_{1}
is the first open set in this covering for consistency of Farber’s topological complexity definition (see
[
4
]
).

Proposition 2.3
.

[

Each of the following conditions on the notion homotopic distance is satisfied:

, and

Let

is a function such that there is a function
k^{{}^{\prime}}:Z\rightarrow X^{{}^{\prime}}
satisfying
k^{{}^{\prime}}\circ k\simeq 1_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}
, then

\text{D}(k\circ f,k\circ f^{{}^{\prime}})=\text{D}(f,f^{{}^{\prime}}).

iv)
Let

Each of the following conditions on the notion higher homotopic distance is satisfied:

3.
The Higher Topological Complexity Using The Homotopic Distance

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a fibration,
p_{1}:Y\times Y\rightarrow Y
be a projection defined by
p_{1}(y,y^{{}^{\prime}})=y
, and
\pi_{2}:X\times Y\rightarrow Y
be another projection defined by
\pi_{2}(x,y)=y
. In Theorem 2.8 of
[
6
]
, if we take
X\times Y
,
p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y})
, and
\pi_{2}
instead of
X
,
f
and
g
, respectively, then we find that

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{1}}
\textstyle{PY\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{X\times Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{Y\times Y}

is commutative, where
h
is the map
(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y}),\pi_{2}):X\times Y\rightarrow Y\times Y
. This proves that

(1)

D(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y}),\pi_{2})=secat(q_{1}).

On the other hand, in Theorem 4.9 of
[
8
]
, if we take
p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y})
instead of the fibration
f
, then we observe that

\textstyle{X\sqcap PY\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{2}}
\textstyle{PY\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}

is also commutative, where

. This gives

TC(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y}))=secat(q_{2}).

Theorem 3.1
.

For a fibration
f:X\rightarrow Y
, if
p_{1}:Y\times Y\rightarrow Y
and
\pi_{2}:X\times Y\rightarrow Y
are projections with
p_{1}(y,y^{{}^{\prime}})=y
and
\pi_{2}(x,y)=y
, respectively, then

\displaystyle\text{D}(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y}),\pi_{2})=\text{TC}(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y})).

Proof.

By (1) and (2), it is enough to show that
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are fibre homotopy equivalent fibrations. Define the maps
u:P\rightarrow X\sqcap PY
and
v:X\sqcap PY\rightarrow P
with
u(x,y,\alpha)=(x,\alpha)
and
v(x,\alpha)=(x,\alpha(1),\alpha)
, respectively. Since
P\subset(X\times Y)\times PY
is given by
\{((x,y),\alpha)\ \ :\ \ \alpha(0)=p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y})(x,y),\alpha(1)=\pi_{2}(x,y)\}
, we have that
\alpha(0)=f(x)
and
\alpha(1)=y
. It follows that

\displaystyle u\circ v=1_{X\sqcap PY}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ v\circ u=1_{P}.

Thus,
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are fibre homotopy equivalent to each other.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For a fibration
f:X\rightarrow Y
, if
p_{1}:Y\times Y\rightarrow Y
and
\pi_{2}:X\times Y\rightarrow Y
are projections with
p_{1}(y,y^{{}^{\prime}})=y
and
\pi_{2}(x,y)=y
, respectively, then

\displaystyle\text{D}(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y}),\pi_{2})\leq\text{TC}(Y).

Moreover, if
Y
is contractible, then we conclude that
D(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y}),\pi_{2})=1
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle D(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y}),\pi_{2})=TC(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y})).

Since
f
is a fibration, so is
f\times 1_{Y}
. Using Proposition
2.8
(iii), and Example 4.10 in
[
8
]
, we get

\displaystyle TC(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y}))\leq TC(p_{1})=TC(Y).

Finally, the contractibility of
Y
implies that
D(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y}),\pi_{2})=1
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For a fibration
f:X\rightarrow Y
, if
\pi_{1}:X\times Y\rightarrow X
and
\pi_{2}:X\times Y\rightarrow Y
are projections with
\pi_{1}(x,y)=x
and
\pi_{2}(x,y)=y
, respectively, we have

\displaystyle\text{D}(f\circ\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=\text{TC}(f).

Proof.

Given a fibration
f:X\rightarrow Y
, we rewrite the continuous function
p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y})
as
f\circ\pi_{1}
. By Proposition
2.3
(i), we have

\displaystyle D(p_{1}\circ(f\times 1_{Y}),\pi_{2})=D(f\circ\pi_{1},\pi_{2}).

From Theorem 2.8, we find
D(f\circ\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=TC(f\circ\pi_{1})
. Define a map
u:X\rightarrow X\times Y
with
u(x)=(x,f(x))
. Therefore, we get
\pi_{1}\circ u\simeq 1_{X}
. By Proposition
2.8
(i), we obtain

\displaystyle D(f\circ\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=TC(f\circ\pi_{1})\geq TC(f).

On the other hand, Proposition
2.8
(iii)
gives us that

\displaystyle D(f\circ\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=TC(f\circ\pi_{1})\leq TC(f).

As a consequence,
D(f\circ\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=TC(f)
.
∎

Theorem
3.3
confirms the following result
[
8
]
:

Corollary 3.4
.

The topological complexity of a space coincides with the topological complexity of the identity function on it.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
f:X\rightarrow X^{{}^{\prime}}
is a fibration, then we have

\displaystyle\text{D}(f,f)\leq\text{TC}(f).

Proof.

Consider a fibration
f:X\rightarrow X^{{}^{\prime}}
and a map
h:X\rightarrow X\times X^{{}^{\prime}}
defined as
h(x)=(x,f(x))
. By Proposition
2.3
(ii), we obtain

\displaystyle TC(f)=D(f\circ\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\geq D((f\circ\pi_{1})\circ h,\pi_{2}\circ h)=D(f,f).

∎

Proposition
3.5
also confirms the well-known fact that
TC
of a fibration
f:X\rightarrow X^{{}^{\prime}}
is a nonnegative integer when considering
D(f,f)=1
.

Proposition 3.6
.

For any fibrations
f
,
f^{{}^{\prime}}:X\rightarrow X^{{}^{\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle\text{D}(f,f^{{}^{\prime}})\leq\text{TC}(f)\cdot\text{TC}(f^{{}^{\prime}}).

Proof.

Let
TC(f)=k
and
TC(f^{{}^{\prime}})=l
. Then
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
and
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{l}
are corresponding coverings of
X\times X^{{}^{\prime}}
. In addition, we get

\displaystyle(f\circ\pi_{1})\big|_{U_{i}}\simeq\pi_{2}\big|_{U_{i}}\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ (f^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\pi_{1})\big|_{V_{j}}\simeq\pi_{2}\big|_{V_{j}}.

Let
W_{i,j}=U_{i}\cap V_{j}
. This covers
X\times X^{{}^{\prime}}
and we derive

\displaystyle f\circ\pi_{1}\big|_{W_{i,j}}=(f\circ\pi_{1})\big|_{W_{i,j}}\simeq\pi_{2}\big|_{W_{i,j}}\simeq(f^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\pi_{1})\big|_{W_{i,j}}=f^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\pi_{1}\big|_{W_{i,j}}.

This means that
D(f\circ\pi_{1}\big|_{W_{i,j}},f^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\pi_{1}\big|_{W_{i,j}})\leq k\cdot l
. Recalling that
h:X\rightarrow X\times X^{{}^{\prime}}
,
h(x)=(x,f(x))
, is a right homotopy inverse of
\pi_{1}
, by Proposition

The topological complexity is an essential homotopy invariant for the work of topological robotics. TC
{}_{n}(X)

(f)

An elementary proof of a universal approximation theorem
Chris Monico

Abstract
In this short note, we give an elementary proof of a universal approximation theorem for neural networks with three hidden layers and increasing, continuous, bounded activation function. The result is weaker than the best known results, but the proof is elementary in the sense that no machinery beyond undergraduate analysis is used.



1
Introduction

There are several versions of
universal approximation theorems

known, including the very well-known ones from
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
Each of them states that some collection of neural networks
is dense in some space of continuous functions with respect to the
uniform norm. In this short note, we present what we believe to be a new
and atypically elementary proof of one such theorem.
If
\sigma
is a 0-1 squashing function (a.k.a. a
sigmoidal function
), we show that the collection
of neural networks with three hidden layers and activation function
\sigma

(except at the output) is dense in the space
C(K)
of real-valued continuous
functions on a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The result given here is weaker than the best known results,
but the argument relies only on basic results about compact sets and continuous functions
which are generally covered in an undergraduate analysis course;
it is really nothing more than an exercise in “epsilon chasing,”
though the underlying intuitive motivation is fairly natural.

2
Notations

For the entirety of this note, we assume the following:

1.

\sigma:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is increasing, continuous, and

\displaystyle{\lim_{x\to-\infty}\sigma(x)=0}
and

\displaystyle{\lim_{x\to\infty}\sigma(x)=1}
.

2.

K
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For example, the sigmoid function
\sigma(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})
satisfies the first
assumption. A function satisfying that assumption will be called a 0-1
squashing function
.
There’s nothing particularly special about 0 and 1 here. The crucial
properties used in our arguments are really just that
\sigma
is
increasing, continuous, bounded, and non-constant.
But the discussion is simplified
with these additional limiting assumptions.

The compact set
K
will represent the domain of a function to be
approximated. In particular, the unit cube
K=[0,1]^{n}
is a special case.

Of course, it’s not at all necessary for a neural network to use the same
activation function in each hidden layer, but that will suffice for our
purposes. Fix a positive integer
n
and let

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\in C(K)\,\,:\,\,f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=a_{0}+a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n},

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\mbox{ for some }a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{F\in C(K)\,\,:\,\,F=\sigma\circ f,\hskip 12.0pt\mbox{ for some }f\in\mathcal{N}_{1}\}.

That is,
\mathcal{N}_{1}
is the set of all affine functions of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
And
\mathcal{N}_{1}^{\sigma}
is the set of all possible node output functions in Layer 1.
For
k\geq 1
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{g\in C(K)\,\,:\,\,g=a_{0}+a_{1}F_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}F_{m},

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\mbox{ for some }F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\in\mathcal{N}_{k}^{\sigma},\,\,a_{0},\ldots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{R}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{k+1}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{G\in C(K)\,\,:\,\,G=\sigma\circ g,\hskip 12.0pt\mbox{ for some }g\in\mathcal{N}_{k+1}\}.

Thus,
\mathcal{N}_{k+1}^{\sigma}
is the set of all possible node output functions
in Layer
k+1
.
It follows easily from the definitions above that
\mathcal{N}_{k}^{\sigma}\subset\mathcal{N}_{k+1}
,
and if
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{k}
then
(a_{0}+a_{1}g_{1}+a_{2}g_{2})\in\mathcal{N}_{k}
for all

a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. We will use these facts frequently in the sequel.

3
Three separation lemmas

Roughly speaking, the strategy of the proof is to first show that

\sigma
separates points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in a strong sense.
We then show that
\mathcal{N}_{2}

separates points from closed sets in a similarly strong sense, and that

\mathcal{N}_{3}
separates closed sets from closed sets again in a strong sense.
Finally, the theorem will be proven using this result
about
\mathcal{N}_{3}
.

Lemma 3.1

Let
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
be distinct real numbers.
For each
\epsilon>0
there exist
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\sigma(s+tx_{0})<\epsilon

and
\sigma(s+tx_{1})>1-\epsilon
. If, in addition,
x_{0}<x_{1}
and
\epsilon<1/2
,
then
\sigma(s+tx)<\epsilon
on the interval
(-\infty,x_{0}]
and

\sigma(s+tx)>1-\epsilon
on the interval
[x_{1},\infty)
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
\epsilon<1
.
By definition of a 0-1 squashing function, there exist
y_{0},y_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such
that
\sigma(y_{0})=\epsilon/2
and
\sigma(y_{1})=1-\epsilon/2
.
Since
x_{1}-x_{0}\neq 0
, the linear system

\left(\hskip-5.0pt\,\begin{array}[]{cc}1&x_{0}\\
1&x_{1}\end{array}\hskip-5.0pt\,\right)\left(\hskip-5.0pt\,\begin{array}[]{c}s\\
t\end{array}\hskip-5.0pt\,\right)=\left(\hskip-5.0pt\,\begin{array}[]{c}y_{0}\\
y_{1}\end{array}\hskip-5.0pt\,\right),

has a solution, say
(s_{0},t_{0})^{T}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sigma(s_{0}+t_{0}x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(y_{0})=\epsilon/2<\epsilon,\hskip 12.0pt\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\sigma(s_{0}+t_{0}x_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(y_{1})=1-\epsilon/2>1-\epsilon.

Suppose, in addition, that
x_{0}<x_{1}
and
\epsilon<1/2
.
Since
\sigma(s+tx)
is monotone and
\sigma(s+tx_{0})<\epsilon<1/2<1-\epsilon<\sigma(s+tx_{1})
,
it follows that
\sigma(s+tx)
is increasing, which proves the remainder of the lemma.

The next lemma asserts that we can separate points from closed sets using
functions in
\mathcal{N}_{2}
in a strong sense. That is, one hidden layer suffices to
separate points from closed sets in the described sense.

Lemma 3.2

Let
B\subset K
be a closed set, and
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\in K-B
.
For each
\epsilon>0
there exists
g\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
such that

g>1-\epsilon
on
B
and
g(\boldsymbol{x}_{0})<\epsilon
.

Without loss of generality, assume
0<\epsilon<1/3
.
Let
{\boldsymbol{b}}\in B
. Since
{\boldsymbol{b}}\neq\boldsymbol{x}_{0}
, there exists
f_{\boldsymbol{b}}\in\mathcal{N}_{1}
such that

f_{\boldsymbol{b}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{0})<\epsilon/2
and
f_{\boldsymbol{b}}({\boldsymbol{b}})>1-\epsilon/2
.
Let

U_{\boldsymbol{b}}=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in K\,\,:\,\,f_{\boldsymbol{b}}(\boldsymbol{x})>1-\epsilon\}.

Since
f_{\boldsymbol{b}}
is continuous,
U_{\boldsymbol{b}}
is open and
{\boldsymbol{b}}\in U_{\boldsymbol{b}}
,
so
\{U_{\boldsymbol{b}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{b}}\in B}
is an open cover of the compact set
B
.
Let
{\boldsymbol{b}}_{1},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{b}}_{N}\in B
such that
\{U_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{1}},\ldots,U_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{N}}\}

is a cover of
B
.

By Lemma
3.1
, there exist
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\sigma(s+tx)<\epsilon/N

on
(-\infty,\epsilon)
and
\sigma(s+tx)>1-\epsilon
on
(1-\epsilon,\infty)
.
For each
1\leq j\leq N
, let
F_{j}=\sigma(s+tf_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{j}})
. Then
F_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{1}^{\sigma}

and
F_{j}(\boldsymbol{x}_{0})<\epsilon/N
and
F_{j}\geq 1-\epsilon
on
U_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{j}}
.

Let
g=\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_{j}
, and it follows that
g\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
,

g(\boldsymbol{x}_{0})<\epsilon
and
g\geq 1-\epsilon
on
B
.

The following lemma is the major tool in the proof of the universal approximation
theorem we will present. It asserts that two hidden layers suffice to separate
disjoint closed sets, in a sense very similar to the previous lemma.
Its proof is quite similar to the previous one.

Lemma 3.3

Let
A
and
B
be disjoint closed subsets of
K
. Then for each
\epsilon>0
,

(i)

there exists
h\in\mathcal{N}_{3}
such that
h<\epsilon
on
B
and
h>1-\epsilon
on
A
,

(ii)

there exists
H\in\mathcal{N}_{3}^{\sigma}
such that
0\leq H<\epsilon
on
B
and
1-\epsilon<H\leq 1
on
A
.

Again without loss of generality we assume
\epsilon\in(0,1/3)
.
For each
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in A
, by Lemma
3.2
, there exists

\widetilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
such that
\widetilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{a}}>1-\epsilon/2
on
B
and

\widetilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{a}}({\boldsymbol{a}})<\epsilon/2
.
Let
g_{\boldsymbol{a}}=1-\widetilde{g}_{\boldsymbol{a}}
, so that

g_{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
, and

g_{\boldsymbol{a}}<\epsilon/2
on
B
,
and
g_{\boldsymbol{a}}({\boldsymbol{a}})>1-\epsilon/2
.
Let

U_{\boldsymbol{a}}=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in K\,\,:\,\,g_{\boldsymbol{a}}(\boldsymbol{x})>1-\epsilon\}.

Since
g_{\boldsymbol{a}}
is continuous, each
U_{\boldsymbol{a}}
is open. And since

{\boldsymbol{a}}\in U_{\boldsymbol{a}}
, it follows that
\{U_{\boldsymbol{a}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in A}
is an
open cover of the compact set
A
.
Let
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{a}}_{N}\in A
for which
\{U_{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{1}},\ldots,U_{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{N}}\}

is a cover of
A
.

Let
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
for which
\sigma(s+tx)<\epsilon/N
on
(-\infty,\epsilon)

and
\sigma(s+tx)>1-\epsilon
on
(1-\epsilon,\infty)
. Set

h=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma(s+tg_{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{j}}).

First note that
h\in\mathcal{N}_{3}
. If
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in A
, then
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in U_{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{k}}
for some
k
,
which implies that
g_{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{k}}({\boldsymbol{a}})>1-\epsilon
and so
h({\boldsymbol{a}})>1-\epsilon
.
And if
{\boldsymbol{b}}\in B
it follows that
g_{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{j}}({\boldsymbol{b}})<\epsilon/2
for all
j
,
which gives
\sigma(s+g_{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{j}}({\boldsymbol{b}}))<\epsilon/N
for all
j
and
hence
h({\boldsymbol{b}})<\epsilon
. This proves the first part.

Since
\sigma
is increasing, by Lemma
3.1

there exist
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\sigma(s+tx)<\epsilon

on the interval
(-\infty,\epsilon)
and
\sigma(s+tx)>1-\epsilon

on the interval
(1-\epsilon,\infty)
. Then
H=\sigma(s+th)
has the
required properties.

Recall that if
K
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

f:K\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous, then the

sup norm
of
f
, denoted
\|f\|_{u}
,
is

\|f\|_{u}=\sup\{|f(\boldsymbol{x})|\,\,:\,\,\boldsymbol{x}\in K\}.

Theorem 3.4
(A Universal Approximation Theorem)

Let
\sigma
be a 0-1 squashing function, and
\mathcal{N}_{k},\mathcal{N}_{k}^{\sigma}

as previously defined.
Let
T:K\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function.
For each
\epsilon>0
there exists
f\in\mathcal{N}_{4}
such that

\|f-T\|_{u}<\epsilon
; that is,
\mathcal{N}_{4}
is dense in
C(K)

with respect to the
\sup
norm.

By way of contradiction, suppose that
T:K\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

is a continuous function and

\inf_{f\in\mathcal{N}_{4}}\|f-T\|_{u}=\alpha>0.

Let
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{N}_{4}
with
\alpha\leq\|\widehat{f}-T\|_{u}<4\alpha/3
.
Define

\displaystyle U^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in K\,\,:\,\,\frac{\alpha}{3}\leq(\widehat{f}-T)(\boldsymbol{x})\leq\frac{4\alpha}{3}\right\},

\displaystyle U^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in K\,\,:\,\,-\frac{4\alpha}{3}\leq(\widehat{f}-T)(\boldsymbol{x})\leq-\frac{\alpha}{3}\right\}.

Since
U^{+},U^{-}
are closed and disjoint,
by Lemma
3.3
there exists
H\in\mathcal{N}_{3}^{\sigma}
such that

0\leq H<\frac{1}{6}\hskip 12.0pt\mbox{ on }U^{-},\hskip 12.0pt\mbox{ and }\frac{5}{6}<H\leq 1\hskip 12.0pt\mbox{ on }U^{+}.

Consider
f=\widehat{f}-\alpha H+\frac{\alpha}{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{4}
.
We claim that
\|f-T\|_{u}<\alpha
.

Suppose first that
\boldsymbol{x}\in U^{+}
. Then

\displaystyle(f-T)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{f}-T)(\boldsymbol{x})-\alpha H(\boldsymbol{x})+\frac{\alpha}{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{4\alpha}{3}-\frac{5\alpha}{6}+\frac{\alpha}{2}=\alpha.

We also have that

\displaystyle(f-T)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{f}-T)(\boldsymbol{x})-\alpha H(\boldsymbol{x})+\frac{\alpha}{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{3}-\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{2}=-\frac{\alpha}{6}>-\alpha,

And therefore
|f-T|<\alpha
on
U^{+}
. Suppose now that
\boldsymbol{x}\in U^{-}
.
Then

\displaystyle(f-T)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{f}-T)(\boldsymbol{x})-\alpha H(\boldsymbol{x})+\frac{\alpha}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{3}+\frac{\alpha}{2}=\frac{\alpha}{6}<\alpha,

and

\displaystyle(f-T)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{f}-T)(\boldsymbol{x})-\alpha H(\boldsymbol{x})+\frac{\alpha}{2}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle-\frac{4\alpha}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{6}+\frac{\alpha}{2}=-\alpha,

and hence
|f-T|<\alpha
on
U^{-}
.
Finally, suppose that
\boldsymbol{x}\in K-(U^{-}\cup U^{+})
.
Since
-\frac{4\alpha}{3}<(\widehat{f}-T)(\boldsymbol{y})<\frac{4\alpha}{3}

for all
\boldsymbol{y}\in K
, it follows that
-\frac{\alpha}{3}<(\widehat{f}-T)(\boldsymbol{x})<\frac{\alpha}{3}
.
From this and
0\leq H\leq 1
it follows that

\displaystyle(f-T)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{f}-T)(\boldsymbol{x})-\alpha H(\boldsymbol{x})+\frac{\alpha}{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{3}+\frac{\alpha}{2}=\frac{5\alpha}{6}<\alpha,

and

\displaystyle(f-T)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{f}-T)(\boldsymbol{x})-\alpha H(\boldsymbol{x})+\frac{\alpha}{2}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{3}-\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{2}=-\frac{5\alpha}{6}>-\alpha.

But
f\in\mathcal{N}_{4}
and
\|f-T\|_{u}<\alpha
is a contradiction, and so the theorem is proven.

4
Remarks

There are a number of easily deduced corollaries
that extend the result.
For example, if
\sigma
is strictly increasing, we can obtain
a similar result using
\mathcal{N}_{4}^{\sigma}
for continuous
functions from
K
to the image
\sigma(\mathbb{R})
.
Of course another corollary is that
functions
C(K,\mathbb{R}^{m})
can be uniformly approximated
by functions in
(\mathcal{N}_{4})^{m}
, and these two extensions could
also be combined. But these are all very standard and obvious extensions
which apply quite generally.

There are several places where this argument is ‘wasteful’; that is,
places where we are not using the full power of a three hidden layer neural
network. This leaves room for the possibility that a very similar
technique might be used to deduce the same conclusion for neural networks
with two hidden layers.

We are grateful to Anupam Pal Choudhury for carefully reading the first
draft of this note and pointing out several places where the argument
was unclear.

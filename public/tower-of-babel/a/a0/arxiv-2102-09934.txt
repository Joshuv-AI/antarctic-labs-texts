Sobolev meets Besov: Regularity for the Poisson equation with Dirichlet, Neumann and mixed boundary values
Cornelia Schneider, Flóra O. Szemenyei

Abstract
We study the regularity of solutions of the Poisson equation with Dirichlet, Neumann and mixed boundary values in polyhedral cones K ⊂ 3 K\subset\real^{3} in the specific scale B τ , τ α , 1 τ = α d + 1 p \ B^{\alpha}_{\tau,\tau},\ \frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{\alpha}{d}+\frac{1}{p}\ of Besov spaces. The regularity of the solution in these spaces determines the order of approximation that can be achieved by adaptive and nonlinear numerical schemes. We aim for a thorough discussion of homogeneous and inhomogeneous boundary data in all settings studied and show that the solutions are much smoother in this specific Besov scale compared to the fractional Sobolev scale H s H^{s} in all cases, which justifies the use of adaptive schemes.

1
Introduction

In this article we investigate the regularity of solutions of
the Poisson equation in (bounded) polyhedral cones
K\subset\real^{3}
with different boundary conditions, i.e., we consider the model problem

-\Delta u\ =\ f\ \text{ in }\ K,\qquad u=g_{j}\ \text{ on }\ \Gamma_{j},\ j\in J_{0},\qquad\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=g_{j}\ \text{ on }\ \Gamma_{j},\ j\in J_{1},

(1)

where
\Gamma_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
denote the faces of the cone with inhomogeneous Dirichlet (
j\in J_{0}
) and/or Neumann boundary conditions (
J\in J_{1}
).
We are interested in regularity properties of the unknown solution
u
in specific nonstandard smoothness spaces, i.e., the so-called adaptivity scale of Besov spaces

B^{\alpha}_{\tau,\tau}(\Omega),\qquad\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{\alpha}{d}+\frac{1}{p},\qquad\alpha>0,

(2)

where
\alpha>0
stands for the smoothness of the solution and
\tau
displays its integrability, cf.
[
10
]
.
Our investigations are motivated by fundamental questions arising in the context of the numerical treatment of PDEs. In particular, we aim at justifying the use of adaptive numerical methods for solving such boundary value problems.
Let us explain these relationships in more detail: In an adaptive strategy, the choice of the underlying degrees of freedom is not a priori fixed but depends on the shape of the unknown solution. In particular, additional degrees of freedom are only spent in regions where the numerical approximation is still ’far away’ from the exact solution.
Although the basic idea is convincing, adaptive algorithms are hard to implement, so that beforehand a rigorous mathematical analysis to justify their use is highly desirable.

For adaptive wavelet methods the best one can expect is an optimal performance in the sense that it realizes the convergence rate of best
N
-term approximation schemes, which serves as a benchmark in this context. If we denote by
\sigma_{N}\bigl(u;X\bigr)
the error of best
N
-term approximation in a Banach space
X
(i.e., the error when we consider the best approximation of a given function by linear combinations of some basis functions of
X
consisting of at most
N
terms), then

[
12
, Thm. 11, p. 586]
implies for
\tau<p
,

\sigma_{N}\bigl(u;L_{p}(\Omega)\bigr)\leq C\,N^{-\alpha/d}\|u|B^{\alpha}_{\tau,\tau}(\Omega)\|,\qquad\frac{1}{\tau}<\frac{\alpha}{d}+\frac{1}{p},

(3)

where
d
denotes the dimension of the underlying domain
\Omega
.
Quite recently, it has turned out that the same interrelations also hold for the very important and widespread adaptive finite
element schemes, cf.
[
15
, Thm. 2.2]
. Hence, it can be seen from (
3
) that
the achievable order of adaptive algorithms depends on the regularity of the target function in the specific scale of Besov spaces (
2
).

On the other hand, it is the regularity of the solution in the scale of (fractional) Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\Omega)
, where now
s
indicates the smoothness of
u
, which encodes information on the convergence order for nonadaptive (uniform) methods.
Therefore, the use of adaptivity is justified if the smoothness within the scale of Besov spaces of the exact solution of a given PDE is high enough compared to the classical Sobolev smoothness.

At this point, the shape of the domain comes into play. As the classical model problem of elliptic equations, let us discuss the Poisson equation. It is well–known
that if the domain under consideration, the right–hand side and the coefficients are sufficiently smooth, then the problem is completely regular, cf.
[
1
]
, meaning that if the right-hand side is in
H^{s}(\Omega)
,
s\geq 1
, then the solution is contained in
H^{s+2}(\Omega)
and there
is no reason why the Besov smoothness should be higher.
However, on nonsmooth (e.g. polyhedral) domains, the situation changes completely. On these domains, singularities at the boundary may occur that diminish the Sobolev regularity of the solution significantly
[
5
,
6
,
16
,
17
,
20
]
(but can be compensated by suitable weight functions).
Moreover, recent studies show that these boundary singularities do not influence the Besov regularity too much
[
9
,
10
]
, so that for certain nonsmooth domains the use of
adaptive algorithms is completely justified. Moreover, for the specific case of polygonal domains in
2
, even more can be said, since then the exact solution can be decomposed into a regular and a singular part corresponding to the reentrant corners
[
16
]
. In this case the Besov smoothness depends on the smoothness of the right-hand side only, i.e., for arbitrary smooth right-hand sides, in principle one gets arbitrary high order of convergence, cf.
[
7
]
. In the polyhedral case, a decomposition of the solution into a singular and a regular part is also possible, however, the situation is much more complicated since edge and vertex singularities occur, cf.
[
14
,
16
]
. We contribute to these studies by extending and improving the above mentioned forerunners. For this we use Sobolev spaces with mixed weights from Maz’ya, Rossmann
[
24
]
, sometimes denoted as
V
-spaces in the sequel. In particular, spaces with mixed weights are needed when studying stochastic PDEs as is demonstrated in
[
4
,
3
]
.
To be precise, the
weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
with parameters
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
\beta\in\real
, and
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})\in\real^{n}
we use are characterized by the norm

\|u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\|:=\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{K}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq l}\rho_{0}(x)^{p(\beta-l+|\alpha|)}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(\dfrac{r_{k}(x)}{\rho_{0}(x)}\bigg)^{p(\delta_{k}-l+|\alpha|)}|\partial^{\alpha}u(x)|^{p}\>dx\bigg)^{1/p},

(4)

where
\rho_{0}
denotes the distance of a point
x
to the vertex of the cone
K
and
r_{k}(x)
the distances to the respective edges
M_{k}
of the cone. It is precisely this mixture of the different weights involved and their interplay, which allows for an even finer description when investigating the singularities of the solutions of PDEs on polyhedral cones or even general domains of polyhedral type.

Let us point out here that the above
V
-spaces are adequate when studying pure Dirichlet problems (i.e.,
J_{1}=\emptyset
in (
1
)) but for the Neumann and mixed boundary value problems we need suitable adaptations for our investigations from
[
24
]
, cf. Definition
4.7
below.

The
V
-spaces can be seen as refinements of the so called Kondratiev spaces that contain only one weight
[
11
]
and which in turn are closely related with Besov spaces in
the adaptivity scale
(
2
) in the sense that powerful embedding results exist
[
18
]
.
We generalize and improve this embedding to the setting of
V
-spaces. Based on our extension operator from
[
19
, Thm. 3.1]
when
p=2
we establish an embedding of the form

V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,2}(K)\cap H^{s}(K)\hookrightarrow B^{\alpha}_{\tau,\tau}(K),\qquad\alpha<\min\Big\{l,3(l-|\delta|),3s)\Big\},\quad\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{\alpha}{3}+\frac{1}{2},

(5)

subject to further restrictions on the appearing parameters, cf. Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
below. The results from
[
14
, Thms. 2.1, 3.1]
may be seen as a first forerunner of our result. However, due to the lack of an extension operator the authors obtained the restriction
\alpha<\frac{3}{2}s
, which we now improve to
\alpha<3s
. Furthermore, it turned out that the results for negative parameters
\delta<0
in
[
14
]
were not correct and required a more subtle analysis, which we perform in detail in Theorem
3.2
.

Afterwards, applying the embedding result (
5
) and regularity assertions from
[
23
,
24
]
, we show that the regularity of the solution in weighted Sobolev spaces is sufficent to establish Besov smoothness (in the original unweighted sense) for the solutions of (
1
). Moreover,
in comparision with the
H^{s}
-regularity results from
[
16
]
we see that the Besov regularity is much higher: since
\alpha<3s
in (
5
) by factor
3
when
l
is large. Fortunately, the embedding (
5
) transfers mutatis mutandis to the weighted spaces needed for studying Neumann and mixed boundary values. Our main results are stated in Theorems
4.5
,
4.17
, and
4.28
and demonstrate that in all cases investigated, the use of adaptive wavelet schemes is justified.

In a forthcomming paper we will further exploit (
5
) for studying similar questions for parabolic problems in the spirit of
[
13
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce Sobolev spaces with mixed weights on polyhedral cones and state some of their relevant properties needed for our later investigations. Furthermore, we provide a definition of Besov spaces via wavelet decompositions. Afterwards, in Section 3, we study embeddings between these scales of spaces. Finally, in Section 4 we apply the obtained embeddings in order to establish (and compare) regularity results in fractional Sobolev and Besov spaces for the Poisson equation with different boundary conditions.

Sobolev and fractional Sobolev spaces

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then

W^{m}_{p}(\Omega)
denotes the standard

-Sobolev spaces of order
m

\Omega

\|\,u\,|W^{m}_{p}(\Omega)\|:=\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\int_{\Omega}|\partial^{\alpha}u(x)|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{1/p}.

If

we shall also write
H^{m}(\Omega)
instead of

.
Moreover, for

{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

as the collection of all

such that

\displaystyle\|u|{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\|:=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}((1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}\mathcal{F}u)|L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\|<\infty,

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform with inverse
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
. These spaces partially coincide with the classical Sobolev spaces for

with

.
Corresponding spaces on domains can be defined via restrictions of functions from
{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|u|{H}^{s}(\Omega)\|:=\inf\left\{\|g|{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\|:\>g\in{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\>g|_{\Omega}=u\right\}.

If, additionally,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, then for

with

and
\sigma\in(0,1)
, an alternative norm comes from the Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces and is given by

\displaystyle\|u|H^{s}(\Omega)\|=\|u|H^{m}(\Omega)\|+\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\partial^{\alpha}u(x)-\partial^{\alpha}u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2\sigma}}\>dx\>dy\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

This follows from the extension operator in
[
16
, Thm. 1.2.10]
and the fact that the corresponding spaces defined on

[

2.1
Weighted Sobolev spaces and their properties

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K
be a (bounded or unbounded) polyhedral cone in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
S=\{0\}\cup M_{1}\cup...\cup M_{n}
. Then the space
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K,S)
is defined as the closure of the set

C_{*}^{\infty}(K,S):=\{u|_{K}:\>u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus S)\}

with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K,S)\|:=\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{K}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq l}\rho_{0}(x)^{p(\beta-l+|\alpha|)}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(\dfrac{r_{k}(x)}{\rho_{0}(x)}\bigg)^{p(\delta_{k}-l+|\alpha|)}|\partial^{\alpha}u(x)|^{p}\>dx\bigg)^{1/p},

(6)

where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\delta=(\delta_{1},...,\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
\rho_{0}(x)=\mathrm{dist}(0,x)
denotes the distance to the vertex, and
r_{j}(x):=\mathrm{dist}(x,M_{j})
the distance to the edge
M_{j}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We collect some remarks and properties concerning the weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K,S)
.

•

In the sequel if there is no confusion we omit
S
from the notation and write shortly
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
.

•

The space
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
is a Banach space for
1\leq p<\infty
. The proof can be derived from a generalized result, see
[
22
, p. 18, Thm. 3.6]
.

•

V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\subset L_{p}(K)
for
l\geq\beta
and
l\geq\delta_{k},\>k=1,...,n
.

•

We have the following embeddings which one obtains easily from the definition of the spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
:

V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\subset V_{\beta-1,\delta-1}^{l-1,p}(K)\subset...\subset V_{\beta-l,\delta-l}^{0,p}(K).

•

A function
\varphi\in C^{l}(K)
is a pointwise multiplier in
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
, i.e., for

u\in V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\|\leq c\|u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\|.

(7)

•

The weighted Sobolev spaces

are refinements of the Kondratiev spaces
\mathcal{K}_{a,p}^{m}(K)
, which for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

, and

are defined as the collection of all measurable functions such that

\displaystyle\|u|\mathcal{K}_{a,p}^{m}(K)\|:=\left(\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\int_{K}|\rho(x)^{|\alpha|-a}\partial^{\alpha}u(x)|^{p}\>dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

with weight function
\rho(x):=\min(1,\mathrm{dist}(x,S))
for

.
In particular, the scales coincide if

m=l,\>\delta=(\delta_{1},...,\delta_{n})=(l-a,...,l-a)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta=l-a.

This follows from
[
24
, p. 90, Subsection 3.1.1, (3.1.2)]
.

In a preceding paper
[

.

2.2
Besov spaces via wavelets

We introduce Besov spaces via wavelet decompositions as in
[
18
, Section 2, p. 563]
. Consider the wavelet construction of Daubechies with the mother function
\eta
and the scaling function
\phi
, which satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\text{ compact support:}\quad\text{ supp}\>\eta,\phi\subset[-N,N]^{d},

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad\text{ moment conditions:}\quad\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x^{\alpha}\eta(x)dx=0\>\ \text{ for }\ \>\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},\ |\alpha|\leq r,

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\quad\text{ sufficiently high smoothness:}\quad\eta,\phi\in C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We set
\psi^{0}=\phi
,
\psi^{1}=\eta
, define
E
as the set of the nontrivial vertices of
[0,1]^{d}
, and put

\psi^{e}(x_{1},...,x_{d}):=\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}\psi^{e_{j}}(x_{j})\>\text{ for }\>e\in E.

Moreover, let

\Psi^{\prime}:=\{\psi^{e}:\>e\in E\},

and consider the set of dyadic cubes,

D=\{I_{j,k}:=2^{-j}k+2^{-j}[0,1]^{d}:\>k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\>j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Now we put
\Lambda^{\prime}=D^{+}\times\Psi^{\prime}
, where
D^{+}
is the set of the dyadic cubes with measure at most 1. Furthermore, we use the notation
D_{j}:=\{I\in D:\>|I|=2^{-jd}\}
.
The functions from
\Psi^{\prime}
are rescaled in the following way:

\psi_{I_{j,k}}:=\psi_{j,k}:=2^{jd/2}\psi(2^{j}\cdot-k)\quad\text{with}\quad I_{j,k}\in D,\>k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\>j\in\mathbb{Z},\>\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}.

Then the set

\{\psi_{I}:\>I\in D,\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}\}

forms an orthonormal basis in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
Q(I)
denote some dyadic cube of minimal size such that
\text{supp}\>\psi_{I}\in Q(I)
for every
\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}
.
Then we can write every function
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

f=P_{0}f+\displaystyle\sum_{(I,\psi)\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I},

where
P_{0}
is the orthogonal projector onto the closure of the span of the function
\Phi(x)=\phi(x_{1})...\phi(x_{d})
and its integer shifts
\Phi(\cdot-k),\>k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover,
f
can be written as

f=\displaystyle\sum_{(I,\psi)\in\Lambda}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I},\quad\text{where}\quad\Lambda=D^{+}\times\Psi,\>\Psi=\Psi^{\prime}\cup\{\Phi\}.

With these considerations we can now
define Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}(\real^{d})
by decay properties of the wavelet coefficients, if the parameters fulfill certain conditions, as follows.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
and
s>\max\left(0,d(\frac{1}{p}-1)\right)
. Choose
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
r>s
and construct a wavelet Riesz basis as described above.
Then the function
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
belongs to the Besov space
B_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if it admits a decomposition of the form

\displaystyle f=P_{0}f+\displaystyle\sum_{(I,\psi)\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I}

(convergence in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) with

\displaystyle\|f|B_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\|:=\|P_{0}f|L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\|

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{j\left(s+d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)q}\Bigg(\sum_{(I,\psi)\in D_{j}\times\Psi^{\prime}}|\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle|^{p}\Bigg)^{q/p}\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

Corresponding spaces on domains
\Omega\subset\real^{d}
can be defined by restriction via

\displaystyle B_{p,q}^{s}(\Omega):=\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega):\>\exists\>g\in B_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\>g|_{\Omega}=f\},

normed by

\|f|B_{p,q}^{s}(\Omega)\|:=\displaystyle\inf_{g|_{\Omega}=f}\|g|B_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\|.

Remark 2.7
.

•

We mention that according to
[
27
]
for a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
there exists a universal extension operator
E:B_{p,q}^{s}(\Omega)\longrightarrow B_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

3
Embeddings from weighted Sobolev spaces into Besov spaces

In this section we establish embedding results, which show the close relation between the Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
with mixed weights and Besov spaces. These will be our main tools when investigating the Besov regularity of solutions to the Poisson equation with different boundary conditions in Section
4
.

In particular, the proofs rely on the ideas from
[
18
, Thm. 1]
and
[
14
, Thms. 2.1, 3.1]
and are performed
by combining Sobolev estimates involving mixed weights with the characterization of Besov spaces by wavelet expansions.

However, due to the fact that our Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
have mixed weights now – measuring the distance to the vertex and the edges, respectively – the analysis becomes more delicate compared to
[
18
, Thm. 1]
. There the embeddings were studied in the setting of Kondratiev spaces, which only involve one weight function. Moreover, we now invoke the extension operator from
[
19
]
, cf. Theorem
2.3
, which improves the results for the upper bound of the smoothness in the Besov spaces considerably compared to
[
14
, Thms. 2.1, 3.1]
.

Finally, by a modification of our arguments, we are also able to deal with the case of the parameter
\delta
having negative components, which has not been considered sufficiently so far.

\displaystyle 2^{-j\tau(l-\beta)}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{2^{j}}k^{(|\delta|-\beta)\tau\frac{p}{p-\tau}}\bigg[\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{Bk}m_{1}^{-\tau|\delta|\frac{p}{p-\tau}+2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{Bk}m_{2}^{-\tau|\delta|^{-}\frac{p}{p-\tau}}\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{m_{2}}m_{1}^{-\tau|\delta|^{+}\frac{p}{p-\tau}+1}\bigg]\bigg)^{\frac{p-\tau}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j\tau(l-\beta)}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{2^{j}}k^{(|\delta|-\beta)\tau\frac{p}{p-\tau}}\bigg[k^{3-\frac{p\tau}{p-\tau}|\delta|}+\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{Bk}\underbrace{m_{2}^{-\tau|\delta|^{-}\frac{p}{p-\tau}}m_{2}^{-\tau|\delta|^{+}\frac{p}{p-\tau}+2}}_{=m_{2}^{-\tau|\delta|\frac{p}{p-\tau}+2}}\bigg]\bigg)^{\frac{p-\tau}{p}}=:\diamond.

(25)

Since we have the condition
r>|\delta|
, we have in (
3.2
) that the exponent of
m_{2}
is
>-1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\diamond\lesssim 2^{-j\tau(l-\beta)}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{2^{j}}k^{(|\delta|-\beta)\tau\frac{p}{p-\tau}}\bigg[k^{3-\frac{p\tau}{p-\tau}|\delta|}+k^{-|\delta|\frac{p\tau}{p-\tau}+3}\bigg]\bigg)^{\frac{p-\tau}{p}}

\displaystyle=2^{-j\tau(l-\beta)}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{2^{j}}k^{(|\delta|-\beta)\tau\frac{p}{p-\tau}}\cdot 2\cdot k^{3-\frac{p\tau}{p-\tau}|\delta|}\bigg)^{\frac{p-\tau}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j\tau(l-\beta)}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{2^{j}}k^{3-\beta\frac{p\tau}{p-\tau}}\bigg)^{\frac{p-\tau}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j\tau(l-\beta)}\begin{cases}\left[(2^{j})^{3-\beta\frac{p\tau}{p-\tau}+1}\right]^{\frac{p-\tau}{p}}&\mbox{if }\>3-\beta\frac{p\tau}{p-\tau}>-1\quad\iff\quad r>\frac{3}{4}\beta\quad(C)\\
1&\mbox{if }\>3-\beta\frac{p\tau}{p-\tau}<-1\quad\iff\quad r<\frac{3}{4}\beta\quad(D).\end{cases}

Summing over
j
from
0,...,\infty
, we obtain for case
(C)
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j\tau(l-\beta)}2^{j4\frac{p-\tau}{p}-j\beta\tau}=\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j(\tau l-4\frac{p-\tau}{p})},

which is finite, if

\displaystyle\tau l-4\frac{p-\tau}{p}>0\quad\iff\quad\frac{3}{4}l>r.

For the case
(D)
we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j\tau(l-\beta)}<\infty\quad\iff\quad l>\beta.

Summarizing the conditions in these cases the function
u
belongs to the Besov space
B^{r}_{\tau,\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for

\displaystyle(C)\quad\max\left\{\frac{3}{2}|\delta|^{+},\frac{3}{4}\beta\right\}<r<\frac{3}{4}l

and for

\displaystyle(D)\quad\frac{3}{2}|\delta|^{+}<r<\frac{3}{4}\beta,\quad l>\beta.

This completes the proof.
∎

4
Regularity results in Sobolev and Besov spaces for the Poisson equation

In this chapter we compare regularity results in Sobolev and Besov spaces for the Poisson equation with Dirichlet, Neumann and mixed boundary conditions.

Fundamental problem

The general boundary value problem we want to study is the following:

\begin{cases}\ -\Delta u\ =\ f&\text{in}\quad K,\\
\quad\quad u\ =\ g_{j}&\text{on}\quad\Gamma_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad j\in{J_{0}},\\
\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}u\ =\ g_{j}\quad&\text{on}\quad\Gamma_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad j\in J_{1},\end{cases}

(26)

where
J_{0}\cap J_{1}=\emptyset
and
J_{0}\cup J_{1}=J:=\{1,...,n\}
. Moreover,
\nu
denotes the outer normal on the faces
\Gamma_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
of the polyhedral cone
K
and we denote the whole boundary by
\Gamma:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{j}
. We will also need to consider the truncated cone
\tilde{K}
with boundary
\tilde{\Gamma}
.
We separately discuss (and distinguish between) the following three cases in the sequel:

•

J_{0}=J
: Dirichlet problem,

•

J_{0}=\emptyset
: Neumann problem,

•

\emptyset\neq J_{0}\subsetneq J
: Mixed boundary values.

Concerning the regularity in the scale of fractional Sobolev spaces we will use results from
[
16
,
29
,
2
]
in order to obtain a suitable upper bound. For the regularity results in Besov spaces we apply the embeddings from Section
3
. In particular, when
p=2
we have
B_{2,2}^{s}=H^{s}
for
s>0
, and the embedding may be formulated as

V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,2}({K})\cap H^{s}(\tilde{K})\hookrightarrow B_{\tau,\tau}^{r}(\tilde{K}),\qquad\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{r}{3}+\frac{1}{2},

subject to further restrictions on the parameters, cf. Theorems
3.1
,
3.2
.
We see that for the Besov regularity we need to rely on regularity results in the weighted Sobolev spaces
V^{l,p}_{\beta,\delta}(K)
as well. However, these results are stated with the help of so-called operator pencils, which we briefly introduce below. Further information on this subject may be found in
[
24
, Sect. 7.1]
or
[
23
, Sect. 4.1]
.

Operator pencils generated by the boundary value problem

There are two types of operator pencils for problem (
26
). The first one is related to the edges of the cone and the second one to its vertex.

1. Operator pencils
A_{j}(\lambda)
for edges:
Let
M_{j}
be an edge of the cone
{K}
, and let
\Gamma_{j_{\pm}}
be the faces adjacent to
M_{j}
. Then by
\mathcal{D}_{j}
we denote the dihedron which is bounded by the half-planes
\mathring{\Gamma}_{j_{\pm}}
tangent to
\Gamma_{j_{\pm}}
at
M_{j}
. Let
r,\varphi
be polar coordinates in the plane perpendicular to
M_{j}
such that

\mathring{\Gamma}_{j_{\pm}}=\left\{x\in\real^{3}:\ r>0,\ \varphi=\pm\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\right\}.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
we define the operators
\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{B}_{j_{\pm}}(\lambda)
on the Sobolev space
H^{2}(I_{j})
with
I_{j}:=\left(\frac{-\theta_{j}}{2},\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\right)
by

\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\lambda)u(\varphi)=r^{2-\lambda}\big(-\Delta\big)(r^{\lambda}u(\varphi)),\qquad\mathcal{B}_{j\pm}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}u(\varphi)&\text{if }j_{\pm}\in J_{0},\\
r^{1-\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}\Big(r^{\lambda}u(\varphi)\Big)&\text{if }j_{\pm}\in J_{1}.\end{cases}

By
A_{j}(\lambda)
we denote the operator

H^{2}(I_{j})\ni u\mapsto\Big(\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\lambda){u},\ \mathcal{B}_{j+}(\lambda)u(\varphi)\big|_{\varphi=-\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}},\ \mathcal{B}_{j-}(\lambda)u(\varphi)\big|_{\varphi=\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}}\Big)\in L_{2}(I_{j})\times\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}.

2. Operator pencil
\mathfrak{A}(\lambda)
for the vertex:
We introduce spherical coordinates
\rho=|x|
,
\omega=\frac{x}{|x|}
on
K
and consider the sperical cap
\Omega=K\cap S^{2}
. On the space
\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):\ u=0\text{ on }\gamma_{j}=\Omega\cap\Gamma_{j}\text{ for }j\in J_{0}\}
, we consider the bilinear form

a(u,v;\lambda)=\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{K\cap\{1<|x|<2\}}\nabla U\cdot\nabla\overline{V}\penalty\ dx,\qquad u,v\in\mathcal{H}_{\Omega},

where
U(x)=\rho^{\lambda}u(\omega)
,
V(x)=\rho^{-1-\overline{\lambda}}v(\omega)
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Then the operator
\mathfrak{A}(\lambda):\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^{\ast}
is defined by

\big(\mathfrak{A}(\lambda)u,v\big)_{\Omega}=a(u,v;\lambda),\qquad u,v\in\mathcal{H}_{\Omega},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega}
denotes the extension of the
L_{2}
scalar product to
\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^{\ast}\times\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}
.

It is known that the spectra of the pencils
A_{j}
and
\mathfrak{A}
consist of isolated points, the eigenvalues of these pencils, which have no accumulation points, cf.
[
24
, p. 292]
.
Moreover, we denote by
\delta_{\pm}^{(j)}
the
greatest positive real numbers such that the strip

-\delta_{-}^{(j)}<\mathrm{Re}\lambda<\delta_{+}^{(j)}

is free of eigenvalues of the pencil
A_{j}(\lambda)
with the exception of the eigenvalue
\lambda=0
in case of the Neumann problem.

For the Poisson equation with homogeneous boundary conditions, according to
[
16
, p. 72]
and
[
21
, Ch. 2]
the eigenvalues
\big(\lambda_{j,m}\big)_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
A_{j}
are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{j,m}

\displaystyle\ =\ m\frac{\pi}{\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle\text{for}\ j,\ k\in J_{0},\quad m\neq 0,

\displaystyle(\text{Dirichlet problem})

\displaystyle\lambda_{j,m}

\displaystyle\ =\ (m-1)\frac{\pi}{\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle\text{for}\ j,\ k\in J_{1},

\displaystyle(\text{Neumann problem})

\displaystyle\lambda_{j,m}

\displaystyle=\left(m-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\pi}{\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle\text{for}\ j\in J_{0}\wedge k\in J_{1}\quad\text{or}\quad j\in J_{1}\wedge k\in J_{0}

\displaystyle(\text{Mixed problem}).

(27)

Therefore, we deduce that
\delta_{\pm}^{(j)}=\frac{\pi}{\theta_{j}}
for the pure Dirichlet and Neumann problem, whereas
\delta_{\pm}^{(j)}=\frac{\pi}{2\theta_{j}}
for the mixed boundary value problem.
Moreover, in this case the eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{A}
are given by

\Lambda_{\pm l}=-\frac{1}{2}\pm\sqrt{\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}+\frac{1}{4}},

where
\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}
are the eigenvalues of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
-\Delta_{\omega}
on
\Omega

with corresponding orthonormalized eigenfunctions
\psi_{l}
(and boundary conditions), i.e.,

-\Delta_{\omega}\psi_{l}=\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}\psi_{l}\quad\text{on}\quad\Omega.

In our regularity theorems below we will usually assume that the line
l-\beta-\frac{3}{2}
is free of the eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{A}(\lambda)
. Since the set of eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{A}
consists of isolated points, this is not a severe restriction (shift
\beta\in\real
slightly).

4.1
Regularity for the Dirichlet problem

First we want to study the regularity of the solution of the Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions only, i.e.,
J_{0}=J
in (
26
), which reads as:

-\Delta u=f\quad\text{in}\quad K,\qquad u=g_{j}\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad j=1,...,n.

(28)

For the regularity in fractional Sobolev spaces we use the following result of Grisvard, cf.
[
16
, Corollary 2.6.7]
or
[
14
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Proposition 4.1

(
Fractional Sobolev regularity for Dirchlet and Neumann problem with homogeneous boundary values).

Let
D\subset\real^{3}
be any bounded Lipschitz domain. Then there exists a number
\alpha_{0}>3/2
such that for every
f\in L_{2}(D)
the solution
u
of the Poisson equation (either with homogeneous Dirichlet or with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions) belongs to
H^{s}(D)
for every
s<\alpha_{0}
.

In order to also allow inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions we use a result of Jerison and Kenig
[
20
, Thm. 5.1]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
D\subset\real^{3}
be any bounded Lipschitz domain and let
g\in H^{s-1/2}(\partial D)
for some
1/2<s<3/2
. Then, there exists a unique harmonic function
u
, i.e.
\Delta u=0
in
D
, such that

\displaystyle u\in H^{s}(D)\qquad\text{and}\qquad u=g\quad\text{on}\quad\partial D.

Proposition
4.2
allows us to reduce (
28
) to a Dirichlet problem with homogeneous boundary values:
If
u_{1}
is the solution of the homogeneous problem

\Delta u_{1}=0\quad\text{in}\quad D,\qquad u_{1}=g\quad\text{on}\quad\partial D,

and
u_{2}
solves the problem

-\Delta u_{2}=f\quad\text{in}\quad D,\qquad u_{2}=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial D,

then
u=u_{1}+u_{2}
is the solution of the inhomogeneous problem

-\Delta u=f\quad\text{in}\quad D,\qquad u=g\quad\text{on}\quad\partial D.

(29)

Therefore, a combination of Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
gives the following regularity result:

Proposition 4.3

(
Fractional Sobolev regularity for the Dirichlet problem).

Let
D\subset\real^{3}
be any bounded Lipschitz domain and assume that
f\in L_{2}(D)
and
g\in H^{s-1/2}(\partial D)
for some
1/2<s<3/2
. Moreover, let
\alpha_{0}>3/2
be the constant from Proposition
4.1
. Then there exists a solution
u\in H^{\overline{\alpha}}(D)
of (
29
) for every
\overline{\alpha}<\alpha_{0}
in case of homogeneous boundary conditions (
g=0
) and
\overline{\alpha}=s
in case of inhomogeneous boundary conditions.

Moreover, in order to establish regularity in Besov spaces we rely on the following important regularity result of Maz’ya and Rossmann in weighted Sobolev spaces, whose proof can be found in
[
24
, p. 127, Thm. 3.5.10]
. Since we want to apply it in combination with the embeddings from Section
3
we restrict ourselves to the case when
p=2
.

Theorem 4.4

(
Weighted Sobolev regularity for the Dirichlet Problem).

Let
K
be a polyhedral cone,
f\in V_{\beta,\delta}^{l-2,2}(K),\>l\geq 1
, and
g_{j}\in V_{\beta,\delta}^{l-k+1/2,2}(\Gamma_{j})
for
j=1,...,n
. We assume that the line
\text{Re}\>\lambda=l-\beta-3/2
does not contain eigenvalues of the pencil
\mathfrak{A}(\lambda)
and that the components of
\delta
satisfy

\displaystyle-\delta_{+}^{(j)}<\delta_{j}-l+1<\delta_{-}^{(j)}\quad\text{for}\quad j=1,...,n.

(30)

Then there exists a unique solution
u\in V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,2}(K)
of the problem (
28
) satisfying the estimate

\displaystyle\|u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,2}(K)\|\leq c\left(\|f|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l-2,2}(K)\|+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ \|g_{j}|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l-k+1/2,2}(\Gamma_{j})\|\right)

(31)

with a constant
c
independent of
f
and
g_{j}
.

We can now combine Proposition
4.3
and Theorem
3.1
,
which together with Theorem
4.4
yield the following:

Theorem 4.5

(
Besov regularity for the Dirichlet problem).

Let
K
be a polyhedral cone and let the right-hand side of (
28
) satisfy
f\in V_{\beta,\delta}^{l-2,2}(K)\cap L_{2}(\tilde{K})
with
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Concerning the boundary data let
g\in H^{s-1/2}(\tilde{\Gamma})
for some
1/2<s<3/2
with
g=g_{j}
on
\Gamma_{j}
and
g_{j}\in V_{\beta,\delta}^{l-1/2,2}(\Gamma_{j})
for
j=1,...,n
. Moreover, we assume that the line
\text{Re}\>\lambda=l-\beta-3/2
does not contain eigenvalues of the pencil
\mathfrak{A}(\lambda)
and that the components of
\delta
satisfy (
30
) and
\delta_{j}>0
for all
j=1,...,n
and
l>\beta
. Then for the solution
u
of (
28
) we have

u\in B_{\tau,\tau}^{r}(\tilde{K})\qquad\text{for}\quad r<\min\Big\{l,3(l-|\delta|),3\overline{\alpha}\Big\},\qquad\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{r}{3}+\frac{1}{2},

(32)

where
\overline{\alpha}(K)=\overline{\alpha}
is the number from Proposition
4.3
, i.e.,
\overline{\alpha}<\alpha_{0}
in case of homogeneous boundary conditions (
g=0
) and
\overline{\alpha}=s
in case of inhomogeneous boundary conditions.

Proof.

Our assumptions on
f
together with the regularity results from Theorem
4.4
and Proposition
4.3
yield the existence of a solution
u
such that

u\in V^{l,2}_{\beta,\delta}(K)\cap H^{s^{\prime}}(\tilde{K})\qquad\text{for every}\quad s^{\prime}\leq\overline{\alpha}.

Using the fact that
B^{s^{\prime}}_{2,2}=H^{s^{\prime}}
in the sense of equivalent norms,
Theorem
3.1
implies that
u\in B_{\tau,\tau}^{r}(\tilde{K})
with the restrictions from (
32
).

∎

Remark 4.6
.

Theorem
4.5
also holds for
\delta
with negative components. In this case we use Theorem
3.2
in the proof of Theorem
4.5
above: Then instead of the parameter restriction

r<\min\{l,3(l-|\delta|),3\overline{\alpha}\}
we obtain that
r<3\overline{\alpha}
and one of the conditions for
r
in (
21
) holds.

4.2
Regularity for the Neumann and for mixed boundary value problems

In order to obtain similar regularity results for the Neumann and mixed boundary value problems we first need to adapt our weighted Sobolev spaces appropriately. Following
[
23
, Subsection 2.2, p. 438]
we now consider the following generalization of the spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}
.

Definition 4.7

(
Weighted Sobolev spaces
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
).

Let
K
be a (bounded or unbounded) polyhedral cone in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
S=\{0\}\cup M_{1}\cup...\cup M_{n}
. Moreover,
let
\tilde{J}
be a subset of
J=\{1,2,...,n\}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\delta=(\delta_{1},...,\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\delta_{j}>-2/p
for
j\in J\setminus\tilde{J}
.
Then the space
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
is defined as the closure of the set

C_{*}^{\infty}(K,S):=\{u|_{K}:\>u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus S)\}

with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|u|\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})\|:=\displaystyle\left(\int_{K}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq l}\rho^{p(\beta-l+|\alpha|)}\prod_{j\in\tilde{J}}\left(\frac{r_{j}}{\rho}\right)^{p(\delta_{j}-l+|\alpha|)}\prod_{j\in J\setminus\tilde{J}}\left(\frac{r_{j}}{\rho}\right)^{p\delta_{j}}|\partial^{\alpha}u|^{p}\>dx\right)^{1/p}.

(33)

Furthermore, we put
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K):=\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;J)
and
W_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K):=\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\emptyset)
.

Remark 4.8
.

(i)

According to the definition of the norms one immediately obtains that

\displaystyle V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\subset\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})\subset W_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K).

(34)

(ii)

The trace spaces for
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
and
W_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
when
l\geq 1
on
\Gamma_{j}
can be defined in the same way as in Definition
2.5
and are denoted by
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l-\frac{1}{p},p}(\Gamma_{j};\tilde{J})
and
{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l-\frac{1}{p},p}(\Gamma_{j})
, respectively.

(iii)

The set
\tilde{J}
will be chosen to contain all
j\in J
such that the Dirichlet condition of problem (
26
) is given on at least one face
\Gamma_{j_{\pm}}
adjacent to the edge
M_{j}
. In particular, for the Neumann problem this yields
\tilde{J}=\emptyset
.

(iv)

The restriction
\delta_{j}>-2/p
for
j\in J\setminus\tilde{J}
(i.e., Neumann boundary conditions on both sides) comes from the fact that for Neumann boundary conditions we need to be a little careful when dealing with the traces on the faces
\Gamma_{j_{\pm}}
adjacent to an edge
M_{j}
(the functions
g_{j_{\pm}}
need to satisfy certain ’compatibility’ conditions). In particular, if
p=2
, it can be shown that the trace of a function
u\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
with support near an edge
M_{j}
with
j\in J\setminus\tilde{J}
belongs to the Sobolev-Slobodeckij space

H_{p}^{l-\delta_{j}-2/p}(M_{j})\qquad\text{if}\qquad-2/p<\delta_{j}<l-2/p

and
\delta_{j}+2/p
is not integer, cf.
[
24
, Lem. 6.2.2]
. Note that for
p=2
the Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces coincide with our fractional Sobolev spaces
H^{s}
. For
\delta_{j}\geq l-2/p
the traces of functions
u\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
in general do not exist on
M_{j}
, cf.
[
24
, p. 225]
.

In particular, the extension operator
\mathfrak{E}
from Theorem
2.3
generalizes to the setting of the spaces
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
.

Theorem 4.9

(
Extension operator for the spaces
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
).

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a polyhedral cone and let
\tilde{J}
be a subset of
J=\{1,2,...,n\}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\delta=(\delta_{1},...,\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\delta_{j}>-2/p
for
j\in J\setminus\tilde{J}
. Then there exists a bounded linear extension operator

\mathfrak{E}:\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\tilde{J}).

Remark 4.10
.

The norm of the space
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\tilde{J})
is defined similarly as in (
33
) replacing the integral domain by
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Proof.

According to
[
23
, Sect. 2, p. 439]
the norm localization in
[
19
, Lem. 2.4]
holds also for the spaces
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
. Therefore,
[
19
, Lem. 3.8]
and Theorem
2.3
can be transferred to these spaces mutatis mutandis, which proofs Theorem
4.9
.
∎

With this extension operator at hand we can further refine the embedding results of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.

Theorem 4.11

(
Embeddings for the spaces
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
).

Let
K
be a bounded polyhedral cone in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Moreover, let
\tilde{J}
be a subset of
J=\{1,2,...,n\}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
1<p<\infty
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
l>\beta
, and
\delta=(\delta_{1},...,\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then we have a continuous embedding

\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})\cap B_{p,p}^{s}(K)\hookrightarrow B_{\tau,\tau}^{r}(K),\quad\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{r}{3}+\frac{1}{p},

where either

r<\min\Big\{l,3(l-|\delta|),3s\Big\}

if
\delta_{j}>0
for all
j=1,...,n
or

r<3s\quad\text{and one of the conditions for $r$ in \eqref{sept16} holds},

if
\delta
has at least one negative component and
\delta_{j}>-\frac{2}{p}
for all
j\in J\setminus\tilde{J}
.

Proof.

The proof follows along the same lines as in Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
replacing the space
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
by
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
. In particular, we remark that in the estimates there we only considered the term with the highest derivatives
|\alpha|=l
appearing in the norm of the spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
, which coincides with the corresponding term in the norm of the spaces
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K;\tilde{J})
.
∎

4.2.1
The Neumann problem

In this subsection we study the Besov regularity of the solution of the Poisson equation with Neumann boundary conditions only, i.e.,
J_{0}=\emptyset
in (
26
), which reads as:

-\Delta u=f\quad\text{in}\quad K,\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}u=g_{j}\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad j=1,...,n.

(35)

For the regularity of the Neumann problem with homogeneous boundary conditions in fractional Sobolev spaces we can again rely on Proposition
4.1
. For inhomogeneous boundary values we use
[
8
, Thm. 2.1]
, whose original proof can be found in
[
29
]
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
D\subset\real^{3}
be any bounded Lipschitz domain whose complement is connected. Moreover, let
g\in H^{s-3/2}_{1^{\perp}}(\partial D):=\{g\in H^{s-3/2}(\partial D):\ g(1)=0\}
for some
1/2<s<3/2
(here
H^{r}(\partial D)
with
r<0
stands for the dual space of
H^{-r}(\partial D)
). Then there exists a unique harmonic function
u
, i.e.
\Delta u=0
in
D
, such that

\displaystyle u\in H^{s}(D)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=g\quad\text{on}\quad\partial D.

Remark 4.13
.

Note that since we are interested in solutions of the Neumann problem, we consider function spaces on the boundary with functions having mean-value
0
. This is reflected in choosing the spaces

H^{s-3/2}_{1^{\perp}}(\partial D)=\{g\in H^{s-3/2}(\partial D):\ g(1)=0\},

where any
g\in H^{s-3/2}(\partial D)
with
1/2<s<3/2
can be identified as a linear
functional on the function space
H^{3/2-s}(\partial D)
. Moreover, it can be shown that if
\mathcal{H}\subset L_{1}(\partial D)
is a dense subset of
H^{s-3/2}(\partial D)
then

\mathcal{H}_{1^{\perp}}:=\left\{f-\frac{1}{|\partial D|}\int_{\partial D}f\mathrm{d}\sigma:\ f\in\mathcal{H}\right\}

is dense in
H^{s-3/2}_{1^{\perp}}(\partial D)
, cf.
[
29
, p. 1781]
.

Proposition
4.12
now allows us to reduce the inhomogeneous Neumann problem

-\Delta u=f\quad\text{in}\quad D,\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}u=g\quad\text{on}\quad\partial D,

(36)

to the homogeneous one similarly as explained for the Dirichlet problem after Proposition
4.2
. Therefore, in view of Propositions
4.1
and
4.12
we obtain the following:

Proposition 4.14

(
Fractional Sobolev regularity for the Neumann problem).

Let
D\subset\real^{3}
be any bounded Lipschitz domain whose complement is connected. Moreover, let
g\in H^{s-3/2}_{1^{\perp}}(\partial D)
for some
1/2<s<3/2
and
\alpha_{0}>3/2
be the constant from Proposition
4.1
. Then there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{\overline{\alpha}}(D)
of (
36
) for every
\overline{\alpha}<\alpha_{0}
in case of homogeneous boundary conditions (
g=0
) and
\overline{\alpha}=s
in case of inhomogeneous boundary conditions.

Concerning the regularity in Sobolev spaces for the Neumann problem, we use
the following result of Maz’ya and Rossmann
[
23
, Section 6.1, p. 459]
.

Theorem 4.15

(
Weighted Sobolev regularity for the Neumann Problem).

Let
K
be a polyhedral cone in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Moreover, let
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\>l\geq 2
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\delta=(\delta_{1},...,\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the problem (
35
) is uniquely solvable in
W_{\beta,\delta}^{l,2}(K)
for arbitrary
f\in W_{\beta,\delta}^{l-2,2}(K)
and
g_{j}\in W_{\beta,\delta}^{l-3/2,2}(\Gamma_{j})
,
j=1,...,n
, if
l-\beta-3/2
is not an eigenvalue of the pencil
\mathfrak{A}(\lambda)
and the components of
\delta
satisfy the inequalities

\displaystyle\max(l-\delta_{+}^{(j)},0)<\delta_{j}+1<l,\qquad j=1,...,n.

(37)

Remark 4.16
.

As for condition (
37
) we refer to Remark
4.8
(iv).

With Propostion
4.14
and Theorem
4.15
we obtain the following counterpart of Theorem
4.5
for the Neumann problem.

Theorem 4.17

(
Besov regularity for the Neumann problem).

Let
K
be a polyhedral cone and let
l\in\mathbb{N},\>l\geq 2
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
l>\beta
, and
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Further we assume that the right-hand side of (
35
) satisfies
f\in W_{\beta,\delta}^{l-2,2}(K)\cap L_{2}(\tilde{K})
, and for the boundary data we have
g\in H^{s-3/2}_{1^{\perp}}(\tilde{\Gamma})
for some
1/2<s<3/2
with
g=g_{j}
on
\Gamma_{j}
and
g_{j}\in W_{\beta,\delta}^{l-3/2,2}(\Gamma_{j})
for
j=1,...,n
.
Assume that
l-\beta-3/2
is not an eigenvalue of the pencil
\mathfrak{A}(\lambda)
and the components of
\delta
satisfy

\displaystyle\max(l-\delta_{+}^{(j)},0)<\delta_{j}+1<l,\qquad j=1,...,n.

(38)

Then for the solution
u
of (
35
) we have

\displaystyle u\in B_{\tau,\tau}^{r}(\tilde{K}),\quad\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{r}{3}+\frac{1}{2},

where either

r<\min\Big\{{l},{3(l-|\delta|)},{3\overline{\alpha}}\Big\}

if
\delta_{j}>0
for all
j=1,...,d
or

r<3\overline{\alpha}\quad\text{and one of the conditions for $r$ in \eqref{sept16} holds},

if
\delta
has at least one negative component, where
\overline{\alpha}(K)=\overline{\alpha}
is the number from Proposition
4.14
.

Proof.

The proof is a counterpart of Theorem
4.5
replacing Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
by Theorem
4.11
, Theorem
4.4
by Theorem
4.15
, and Proposition
4.3
by Proposition
4.14
.
∎

Comparision with previous results

We compare Theorem
4.17
with the result of Dahlke and Sickel in
[
14
, Thm. 3.1, p. 11]
which reads as follows:

Theorem 4.18

(
Besov regularity result from Dahlke/Sickel).

Suppose that the right-hand side of (
35
) satisfies
f\in W_{\beta,\delta}^{l-2,2}(K)\cap L_{2}(\tilde{K})
, where
l\geq 2
is a natural number. Further assume that
g_{j}\in W_{\beta,\delta}^{l-3/2,2}(\Gamma_{j}),\>j=1,...,n
. Let
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(K)
be the number defined in Proposition
4.1
. Then there exists a countable set
E
of complex numbers such that the following holds: If the real number
\beta
and the vector
\delta
are chosen such that
\beta<l
,

\displaystyle\lambda\neq l-\beta-3/2\quad\text{for all}\>\>\lambda\in E,

(39)

and

\displaystyle\max(l-\frac{\pi}{\theta_{j}},0)<\delta_{j}+1<l,\quad j=1,...,n,

where
\theta_{j}
denotes the angle at the edge
M_{j}
, then the solution of (
35
) satisfies

\displaystyle u\in B_{\tau,\tau}^{r}(\tilde{K}),\qquad r<\min\left\{l,\frac{3}{2}\alpha_{0},3(l-|\delta|)\right\},\quad\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{r}{3}+\frac{1}{2}.

Most obvious, we see in comparison that the restriction in the upper bound for
r
in terms of
r<\frac{3}{2}\alpha_{0}
in Theorem
4.18
was improved to
r<3\alpha_{0}
in Theorem
4.17
. The improvement comes from the fact that we use the extension operator from Theorem
4.9
for the proof of the embedding in Theorem
4.11
. This way (in the proof of Theorem
4.11
) we can consider the wavelets with support intersecting the surface of the cone in the same way as the interior wavelets. Therefore, only the wavelets which are close to the singular set, i.e., the vertex and the edges of the cone, have to be treated in a different manner. In the corresponding proof for the embedding of Dahlke and Sickel there is no such extension operator and therefore, they have to treat these wavelets as the other boundary wavelets, which leads to the additional restriction on
r
.

Moreover, we remark that Theorem
4.18
does not hold in this form for
\delta
with negative components. In this case one requires the additional assumptions from (
21
), since the estimates when the components of
\delta
are negative are more delicate, cf. the proof of Theorem
3.2
compared to the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

There is one more apparent difference between the two theorems: In Theorem
4.17
we require that
l-\beta-3/2
is not an eigenvalue of the pencil
\mathfrak{A}(\lambda)
, but according to
[
24
, p. 97, 292/293]
this is equivalent to the condition (
39
) in Theorem
4.18
.

Finally, let us remark that Theorem
4.18
above as stated in
[
14
]
is not quite correct since the assumptions on the boundary data
g_{j}
are not sufficient in order guarantee a solution
u\in H^{s}(\tilde{K})
. One has to add our assumptions from Theorem
4.17
for inhomogeneous boundary data.

Remark 4.19
.

Let us mention that there is another regularity result of Dahlke in
[
8
, Thm. 3.3]
, which gives for the homogeneous Neumann problem (
36
) on a bounded Lipschitz domain
D
with
f=0
and
boundary values
g\in H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}(\partial D)
,
1/2<s<3/2
, that the solution
u
belongs to
B^{r}_{\tau,\tau}(D)
for
0<r<\frac{3}{2}s+\frac{3}{4}
. It is obtained using a bootstrapping strategy and also improves the bound
r<\frac{3}{2}s
. However, we see that our restriction
r<3s
is even better (but restricted to polyhedral cones
K
).

4.2.2
Mixed boundary value problems

We consider now mixed boundary value problems (
26
), where sets
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are both supposed to be non-empty, i.e., some of the faces (
\Gamma_{j}
with
j\in J_{0}
) of the cone satisfy Dirichlet and the other ones (
\Gamma_{j}
with
j\in J_{1}
) Neumann boundary conditions.

Moreover, we denote by
\tilde{J}
the set of all
j\in J
such that the Dirichlet condition is given on at least one face
\Gamma_{k}
adjacent to the edge
M_{j}
, i.e.,
M_{j}\subset\overline{\Gamma}_{k}
for at least one
k\in J_{0}
.

Fractional Sobolev regularity for mixed problems

Concerning the fractional Sobolev regularity of mixed boundary value problems, we need a counterpart of Proposition
4.1
,
since it holds only for pure Dirichlet or pure Neumann boundary conditions. For this we use the following result of Grisvard, whose proof can be found in
[
16
, Thm. 2.6.3, p. 75]
.

Proposition 4.20
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded polyhedral domain. The open neighbourhood near a vertex of
D
is a polyhedral cone
K
which in spherical coordinates can be described as

K=\{(\rho,\omega):\ 0<\rho<c,\ \omega\in\Omega:=K\cap S^{2}\}.

We denote by
\Delta_{\omega}
the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Omega
with eigenvalues
\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}
and corresponding orthonormalized eigenfunctions
\psi_{l}
, i.e.,

-\Delta_{\omega}\psi_{l}=\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}\psi_{l}\quad\text{on}\quad\Omega.

For
f\in L^{2}(D)
let
u
be the solution of (
26
) with homogeneous boundary conditions. Then there exists unique numbers
c_{l}
such that

\displaystyle u_{\text{reg}}=u-\displaystyle\sum_{l}c_{l}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}+\frac{1}{4}}}\psi_{l}(\omega)\in H^{s}(K),

(40)

where the sum is over all
l
such that
0\leq\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}\leq s^{2}-2s+\frac{3}{4}
,
s\leq 2
with
s<\Lambda+1
and

\displaystyle\Lambda:=\min\{\lambda_{j,m}\in(0,1):\ j=1,\ldots,n;\ m\geq 1\},

(41)

where
\lambda_{j,m}
are the eigenvalues at the edge
M_{j}
.

Remark 4.21
.

The function
u_{\text{reg}}
is the regular part of
u
and the terms
\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}+\frac{1}{4}}}\psi_{l}
constitute the singular part of
u
denoted by
u_{\text{sing}}
.

Using this result of Grisvard we obtain the following substitute of Proposition
4.1
for mixed boundary value problems:

Proposition 4.22

(
Fractional Sobolev regularity for mixed problems with homogeneous boundary values).

Let
D
be a bounded polyhedral domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then there exists a number
\alpha_{0}\geq 5/4
such that for every
f\in L_{2}(D)
the solution
u
of the mixed boundary value problem (
26
) with homogeneous boundary conditions belongs to
H^{s}(D)
for every
s<\alpha_{0}
.

Proof.

We show first that the number
\Lambda\geq\frac{1}{4}
in Proposition
4.20
. According to
[
16
, p. 72]
the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j,m}>0
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, at an edge
\ M_{j}=\overline{\Gamma}_{j}\cap\overline{\Gamma}_{k}\
are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{j,m}

\displaystyle\ =\ m\frac{\pi}{\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad j,\ k\in J_{0},

\displaystyle\lambda_{j,m}

\displaystyle\ =\ (m-1)\frac{\pi}{\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad j,\ k\in J_{1},

\displaystyle\lambda_{j,m}

\displaystyle=\left(m-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\pi}{\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad j\in J_{0}\>\text{ and }\>k\in J_{1}\quad\text{or}\quad j\in J_{1}\>\text{ and }\>k\in J_{0},

(42)

where
\theta_{j}
denotes the angle at the edge
M_{j}
. According to the definition of
\Lambda
in (
41
) we obtain the smallest eigenvalues in (
4
) if we choose
\theta_{j}=2\pi
, which in the first line for
m=1
gives
\lambda_{j,1}=\frac{1}{2}
, in the second line for
m=2
we get
\lambda_{j,2}=\frac{1}{2}
(here
m=1
leads to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{j,1}=0
which is not admissible in the set
\Lambda
), and in the last line
m=1
yields
\lambda_{j,1}=\frac{1}{4}
. Since we have mixed boundary condition, there exist at least one edge
M_{j}
of the cone with two adjacent faces such that one of them has Dirichlet and the other one Neumann boundary conditions. Therefore, the eigenvalues in the third line of (
4
) appear and we deduce
\Lambda\geq\frac{1}{4}
. Thus, according to Proposition
4.20
we have

\displaystyle u_{\text{reg}}\in H^{s}(K)\quad\text{for}\quad s<\frac{5}{4}.

(43)

We now consider the singular part of
u
. According to
[
16
, Lem. 2.6.2, p. 75]
the eigenfunctions satisfy
\psi_{l}\in H^{s}(\Omega)
for
s<\frac{5}{4}
, where
\Omega=K\cap S^{2}
is the intersection of the cone with the unit sphere
S^{2}
. Since these eigenfunctions only depend on
\omega
but not on
\rho
, we immediately get
\psi_{l}\in H^{s}(K)
for
s<\frac{5}{4}
. Moreover, we show that

\displaystyle\rho^{\lambda}:=\rho^{-1/2+\sqrt{\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}+1/4}}\in H^{3/2+\varepsilon}(K)

(44)

for small
\varepsilon>0
. We have

\displaystyle\|\rho^{\lambda}|H^{s}(K)\|^{2}\sim\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c}\rho^{(\lambda-s)2}\rho^{2}\>d\rho\sim\Big[\rho^{(\lambda-s)2+3}\Big]_{0}^{c}<\infty\quad\iff\quad s<\frac{3}{2}+\lambda.

(45)

Since
\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}\geq 0
, we obtain
\lambda=0
when
\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}=0
in which case the function
\rho^{\lambda}=1
is constant and trivially belongs to
H^{s}(K)
for any
s
. Otherwise, we have
\lambda>0
and according to (
45
) the statement (
44
) holds for arbitrary small
\varepsilon>0
. Now we apply the multiplier results from
[
26
, Thm. 1(i), Sect. 4.6.1, p. 177]
, which state that

\|uv|H^{s_{1}}(\real^{3})\|\lesssim\|v|H^{s_{1}}(\real^{3})\|\cdot\|u|H^{s_{2}}(\real^{3})\|\quad\text{for}\quad s_{2}>s_{1},\ s_{1}+s_{2}>0,\ s_{2}>\frac{3}{2}

together with the extension operator for the
H^{s}
-spaces from
[
16
, Thm. 1.2.10]
and obtain for

u\in H^{s_{2}}(K)
and
v\in H^{s_{1}}(K)
that

\displaystyle\|uv|H^{s_{1}}(K)\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\text{Ex}\>u\cdot\text{Ex}\>v|H^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\text{Ex}\>u|H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\|\cdot\|\text{Ex}\>v|H^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u|H^{s_{2}}(K)\|\cdot\|v|H^{s_{1}}(K)\|,

(46)

where
\text{Ex}\>u,\>\text{Ex}\>v
are the corresponding extensions from
K
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
of
u
and
v
.
We choose in (
4.2.2
) the functions
u=\rho^{\lambda}
,
v=\psi_{l}
, and the regularity parameters
0<s_{1}<\frac{5}{4}
,
s_{2}=\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon
and obtain that

\displaystyle u_{\text{sing}}=\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}+\frac{1}{4}}}\psi_{l}\in H^{s}(K)\quad\text{for}\quad s<\frac{5}{4}.

(47)

In view of Proposition
4.20
, by (
43
) and (
47
) we obtain the desired regularity result, which completes the proof.
∎

In order to allow inhomogeneous mixed boundary values we invoke the following result of Agranovich
[
2
, Thm. 4.2]
.

Proposition 4.23
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and assume that the boundary
\partial D=\Gamma
consists of two domains
\Gamma^{0}
and
\Gamma^{1}
and their common boundary
\partial\Gamma^{j}
, which is a
1
-dimensional closed Lipschitz curve without self intersections. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for boundary data

g_{0}\in H^{s-1/2}(\Gamma^{0})
and
g_{1}\in H^{s-3/2}(\Gamma^{1})
with
1-\varepsilon<s<1+\varepsilon
, there exists a unique harmonic function
u
, i.e.,
\Delta u=0
in
D
, such that

u\in H^{s}(D)\qquad with\qquad u=g_{0}\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma^{0},\qquad\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=g_{1}\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma^{1}.

Remark 4.24
.

Similar results were obtained in
[
25
, Thm., p. 4145]
for so-called ’creased’ domains (here the authors additionally require that the angle between the normals to
\Gamma^{0}
and
\Gamma^{1}
at the points of
\partial\Gamma_{j}
are less than
\pi
). Unfortunately, the precise range of
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(D,\Gamma^{0},\Gamma^{1})>0
is not specified any further in
[
2
,
25
]
.

Together Propositions
4.23
and
4.22
allow us to reduce the inhomogeneous mixed problem

-\Delta u=f\quad\text{on}\quad D,\qquad u=g_{0}\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma^{0},\qquad\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=g_{1}\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma^{1}

(48)

to the homogeneous one as explained before for pure Dirichlet and Neumann problems. In total we obtain the following result:

Proposition 4.25

(
Fractional Sobolev regularity for mixed problems).

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and assume that the boundary
\partial D=\Gamma
consists of two domains
\Gamma^{0}
and
\Gamma^{1}
and their common boundary
\partial\Gamma^{j}
, which is a
1
-dimensional closed Lipschitz curve without self intersections. Moreover, let
\varepsilon>0
be small and consider boundary data

g_{0}\in H^{s-1/2}(\Gamma^{0})
and
g_{1}\in H^{s-3/2}(\Gamma^{1})
with
1-\varepsilon<s<1+\varepsilon
. Let
\alpha_{0}>5/4
be the constant from Proposition
4.20
. Then there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{\overline{\alpha}}(D)
of (
48
) for every
\overline{\alpha}<\alpha_{0}
in case of homogeneous boundary conditions (
g_{0}=g_{1}=0
) and
\overline{\alpha}=s
in case of inhomogeneous boundary conditions.

Besov regularity of the mixed problem

Before we come to the regularity of the solution of (
26
) in Besov spaces,
we first deduce the following regularity assertion in weighted Sobolev spaces as a consequence of Maz’ya and Rossmann
[
23
, Lem. 4.4, Thm. 4.4]
.

Theorem 4.26

(
Weighted Sobolev regularity).

Let
K
be a polyhedral cone and let
J_{0},\>J_{1}
be non-empty subsets of
J=\{1,2,...,n\}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
l\geq 2
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\delta=(\delta_{1},...,\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, let
f\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l-2,2}(K,\tilde{J})
,
g_{j}\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l-3/2,2}(\Gamma_{j};\tilde{J})
for
j\in J_{1}
and
g_{j}\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l-1/2,2}(\Gamma_{j};\tilde{J})
for
j\in J_{0}
. Suppose that the line
\text{Re}\>\lambda=l-\beta-3/2
does not contain eigenvalues of the pencil
\mathfrak{A}
and that the components of
\delta
satisfy the inequalities

\displaystyle l-\delta_{+}^{(j)}<\delta_{j}+1<l

\displaystyle\quad\text{for}\quad j\in\tilde{J},

\displaystyle\max\left(l-\delta_{+}^{(j)},l-2\right)<\delta_{j}+1<l

\displaystyle\quad\text{for}\quad j\in J\setminus\tilde{J},

(49)

where
\theta_{j}
denotes the angle at the edge
M_{j}
.
Then the problem (
26
) has a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,2}(K,\tilde{J})
.

Proof.

We apply
the following elementary embedding from
[
23
, p. 439]
, i.e.,

\mathcal{W}_{\beta+1,\delta}^{l+1,2}(K,\tilde{J})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta^{\prime}}^{l,2}(K,\tilde{J})

(50)

with
\delta_{j}-\delta_{j}^{\prime}\leq 1
for
j=1,...,n
and
\delta_{j},\>\delta_{j}^{\prime}>-1
for
j\in J\setminus\tilde{J}
, which together with the embedding (
34
) yields

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l-2,2}(K,\tilde{J})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\beta-(l-2),\delta-(l-2)}^{0,2}(K,\tilde{J})\hookrightarrow W_{\beta-(l-2),\delta^{\prime}}^{0,2}(K),

(51)

where
\delta_{j}^{\prime}:=\delta_{j}-(l-2)
and

\displaystyle 2-\frac{\pi}{\theta_{j}}<\delta_{j}^{\prime}+1<2

\displaystyle\quad\text{for}\quad j\in\tilde{J},

\displaystyle\max\left(2-\frac{\pi}{\theta_{j}},0\right)<\delta_{j}^{\prime}+1<2

\displaystyle\quad\text{for}\quad j\in J\setminus\tilde{J}.

(52)

Another application of (
51
) to the boundary values gives

\displaystyle g_{j}\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l-1/2,2}(\Gamma_{j};\tilde{J})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\beta-(l-2),\delta^{\prime}}^{2-1/2,2}(\Gamma_{j};\tilde{J})\qquad\text{for}\quad j\in J_{0},

\displaystyle g_{j}\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l-1-1/2,2}(\Gamma_{j};\tilde{J})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\beta-(l-2),\delta^{\prime}}^{1-1/2,2}(\Gamma_{j};\tilde{J})\qquad\text{for}\quad j\in J_{1}.

(53)

Moreover, from the initial assumptions we see that the line

\text{Re}\>\lambda=2-(\beta-(l-2))-3/2=l-\beta-3/2

does not contain eigenvalues of the pencil
\mathfrak{A}
. Hence, according to (
51
), (
4.2.2
) and (
4.2.2
) we can apply
[
23
, Thm. 4.4]
and obtain that there is a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{W}_{\beta-(l-2),\delta^{\prime}}^{2,2}(K,\tilde{J})
of (
26
). Furthermore, with this also the assumptions of
[
23
, Lem. 4.4]
are satisfied for
k=2
, and we deduce that the solution
u
in fact belongs to
\mathcal{W}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,2}(K,\tilde{J})
.
∎

(
Besov regularity for mixed boundary value problems).

K
be a polyhedral cone and
\tilde{K}
its truncation. For non-empty subsets
J_{0}
and
J_{1}

\tilde{\Gamma}^{i}:=\Gamma^{i}\cap\tilde{K}

.

\overline{\Gamma^{0}}\cap\overline{\Gamma^{1}}

K
only.

Let

,

,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}

l>\beta
,
\delta=(\delta_{1},...,\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

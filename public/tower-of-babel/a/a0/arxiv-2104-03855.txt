Modular symmetry and zeros
in magnetic compactifications

Abstract
We discuss the modular symmetry and zeros of zero-mode wave functions on two-dimensional torus T 2 T^{2} and toroidal orbifolds T 2 / ℤ N T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{N} ( N = 2 , 3 , 4 , 6 N=2,3,4,6 ) with a background homogeneous magnetic field. As is well-known, magnetic flux contributes to the index in the Atiyah-Singer index theorem. The zeros in magnetic compactifications therefore play an important role, as investigated in a series of recent papers. Focusing on the zeros and their positions, we study what type of boundary conditions must be satisfied by the zero modes after the modular transformation. The consideration in this paper justifies that the boundary conditions are common before and after the modular transformation.

\displaystyle\frac{qf}{2\pi}\equiv M\in\mathbb{Z}.

\displaystyle\xi^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\xmapsto{~T~}\xi_{T}^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha^{T}_{1},\alpha^{T}_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\xi^{j}(z,\tau+1\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha^{T}_{1},\alpha^{T}_{2})

(4.10)

\displaystyle\xi_{T}^{j}(z+\tau,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha^{T}_{1},\alpha^{T}_{2})

\displaystyle=e^{-i\pi My_{1}}e^{2\pi i(\alpha^{T}_{2}-\alpha^{T}_{1}+\rho/2)}\xi_{T}^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha^{T}_{1},\alpha^{T}_{2}),

4.3
Zeros

To discuss necessary conditions on zeros in the next sections, we now analyze the zeros of the modular-transformed mode functions
\xi^{j}_{T}
and
\xi^{j}_{S}
.

Analogously to Subsec.
3.2
, by focusing on the Jacobi theta-function appearing in (
4.9
) and solving the equation

\displaystyle\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{j+\alpha^{T}_{1}}{M}\\[3.0pt]
-\alpha^{T}_{2}\end{bmatrix}(Mz,M(\tau+1))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0,

(4.17)

one finds the zeros of
\xi^{j}_{T}
as

\displaystyle(y_{1},y_{2})=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1/2-j-\alpha^{T}_{1}+\alpha^{T}_{2}}{M},\hskip 1.0pt\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j+\alpha^{T}_{1}}{M}\right),\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3/2-j-\alpha^{T}_{1}+\alpha^{T}_{2}}{M},\hskip 1.0pt\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j+\alpha^{T}_{1}}{M}\right),

\displaystyle\hskip 110.0pt...,\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(2M-1)/2-j-\alpha^{T}_{1}+\alpha^{T}_{2}}{M},\hskip 1.0pt\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j+\alpha^{T}_{1}}{M}\right).

Solving the equation in the same way

\displaystyle\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{j+\alpha^{S}_{1}}{M}\\[3.0pt]
-\alpha^{S}_{2}\end{bmatrix}(-Mz/\tau,-M/\tau)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0,

(4.19)

one obtains the zeros of

\displaystyle(y_{1},y_{2})=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j+\alpha^{S}_{1}}{M},\hskip 1.0pt\frac{1/2-\alpha^{S}_{2}}{M}\right),\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j+\alpha^{S}_{1}}{M},\hskip 1.0pt\frac{3/2-\alpha^{S}_{2}}{M}\right),

\displaystyle\hskip 140.0pt...,\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j+\alpha^{S}_{1}}{M},\hskip 1.0pt\frac{(2M-1)/2-\alpha^{S}_{2}}{M}\right).

An important implication is that the

).
Namely, for the zeros of
\xi^{j}_{T}
and
\xi^{j}_{S}
(up to mod 1)

\displaystyle Z^{j}_{T}

\displaystyle\equiv\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(2k+1)/2-j-\alpha^{T}_{1}+\alpha^{T}_{2}}{M},\hskip 1.0pt\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j+\alpha^{T}_{1}}{M}\right)\Bigg|\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}\right\},

), which are valid for

and

as long as we impose (

) and (

).

5
Modular symmetry

5.1
Equivalence of zeros

It was explained in Sec. 3 that the generators
S
and
T
satisfy
S^{4}=(ST)^{3}=\mathbbm{1}
.
This can be rephrased as a necessity that the transformed wave functions by
S^{4}
and
(ST)^{3}
must be identical to the original ones on
T^{2}
.
We therefore turn to such wave functions and their zeros.

Under an identical transformation
S^{4}
, the mode functions transform as

\displaystyle\xi^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\xmapsto{~S^{4}~}\xi_{S^{4}}^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha^{S^{4}}_{1},\alpha^{S^{4}}_{2})

\displaystyle=\xi^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha^{S^{4}}_{1},\alpha^{S^{4}}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}\,e^{i\pi Mz\,\mathrm{Im}\,z/\mathrm{Im}\,\tau}\,\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{j+\alpha^{S^{4}}_{1}}{M}\\[3.0pt]
-\alpha^{S^{4}}_{2}\end{bmatrix}(Mz,M\tau).

(5.1)

The zeros then read

\displaystyle Z^{j}_{S^{4}}\equiv\left\{\left(\frac{(2k+1)/2+\alpha^{S^{4}}_{2}}{M},\hskip 1.0pt\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j+\alpha^{S^{4}}_{1}}{M}\right)\Bigg|\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}\right\}

(5.2)

up to mod 1. Analogously, under another identical transformation
(ST)^{3}
, one has

\displaystyle\xi^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\xmapsto{~(ST)^{3}~}\xi_{(ST)^{3}}^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{1},\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{2})

\displaystyle=\xi^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{1},\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}\,e^{i\pi Mz\,\mathrm{Im}\,z/\mathrm{Im}\,\tau}\,\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{j+\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{1}}{M}\\[3.0pt]
-\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{2}\end{bmatrix}(Mz,M\tau),

(5.3)

and

\displaystyle Z^{j}_{(ST)^{3}}\equiv\left\{\left(\frac{(2k+1)/2+\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{2}}{M},\hskip 1.0pt\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j+\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{1}}{M}\right)\Bigg|\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}\right\}.

(5.4)

Recalling the discussion in Sec. 4, we know that a combination of the flux quanta
M
, the label
j
, the twists
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
determines the positions of the zeros and vice versa.
Hence, the original zeros of
\xi^{j}
have to be kept equivalent to those of
\xi^{j}_{S^{4}}
and
\xi^{j}_{(ST)^{3}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle Z^{j}=Z^{j}_{S^{4}}=Z^{j}_{(ST)^{3}}\qquad\forall\,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}.

(5.5)

This is the necessity of zero-point equivalence, as mentioned previously, and also one evidence that the modular-transformed wave functions satisfy the same boundary conditions as the original ones (
2.7
).
This necessity can be rephrased as a restriction on the twists,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\alpha^{S^{4}}_{1}=\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{1}=\alpha_{1}\\
\alpha^{S^{4}}_{2}=\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{2}=\alpha_{2}\end{cases}\hskip-10.0pt.

(5.6)

One can show that this restriction on the twists under the identical transformations
S^{4}=(ST)^{3}=\mathbbm{1}
keeps a consistency with (
4.12
) and (
4.16
),

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle\xmapsto{~S~}(-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle\xmapsto{~S~}(-\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle\xmapsto{~S~}(\alpha_{2},-\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle\xmapsto{~S~}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=(\alpha^{S^{4}}_{1},\alpha^{S^{4}}_{2}),

(5.7)

and

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle\xmapsto{~T~}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\rho/2)

\displaystyle\xmapsto{~S~}(-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}+\rho/2)

\displaystyle\xmapsto{~T~}(-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}-\rho/2,-\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle\xmapsto{~S~}(-\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-\rho/2,-\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle\xmapsto{~T~}(\alpha_{2},-\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle\xmapsto{~S~}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=(\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{1},\alpha^{(ST)^{3}}_{2}).

(5.8)

Obviously, these relations hold for arbitrary
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in[0,1)
.

Note that the replacements (
4.12
) and (
4.16
) are not necessary at this stage.
Namely, there is no necessity that the common periodicity conditions have to be valid even after the modular transformation, at least on the torus.
However, this turns out to be necessary on the orbifolds, as we shall see in the next section.

5.2
Comments on
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

Before closing this section, we mention another definition of the modular group.
See for example
[
48
]
.
Instead of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, some authors define the modular group as the projective
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, i.e.,
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma/\{\pm\mathbbm{1}\}=\{S,T\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0ptS^{2}=-\mathbbm{1},\hskip 1.0pt(ST)^{3}=\mathbbm{1}\}/\{\pm\mathbbm{1}\},

(5.9)

where
\gamma\in\Gamma
is identified with
-\gamma
.

Unfortunately, our approach in terms of zeros does not distinguish
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
from
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.
Let us consider a transformation
S^{2}
for the zero-mode wave functions,

\displaystyle\xi^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\xmapsto{~S^{2}~}\xi_{S^{2}}^{j}(z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha^{S^{2}}_{1},\alpha^{S^{2}}_{2})

\displaystyle=\xi^{j}(-z,\tau\hskip 1.0pt|\hskip 1.0pt\alpha^{S^{2}}_{1},\alpha^{S^{2}}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}\,e^{i\pi Mz\,\mathrm{Im}\,z/\mathrm{Im}\,\tau}\,\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{j+\alpha^{S^{2}}_{1}}{M}\\[3.0pt]
-\alpha^{S^{2}}_{2}\end{bmatrix}(-Mz,M\tau).

(5.10)

Then, the zeros are obtained as

\displaystyle Z^{j}_{S^{2}}\equiv\left\{\left(\frac{(2k+1)/2+\alpha_{2}}{M},\hskip 1.0pt\frac{1}{2}-\frac{-j+\alpha_{1}}{M}\right)\Bigg|\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}\right\},

(5.11)

where
\alpha^{S^{2}}_{i}=-\alpha_{i}\,\,(i=1,2)
has been applied (see (
5.7
)).
It is now obvious that this set is not identical to the original one
Z^{j}
.
However, one finds

\displaystyle\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}Z^{j}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}Z^{j}_{S^{2}}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}Z^{j}_{(ST)^{3}}.

(5.12)

Thus, the zeros are invariant under another version of the modular transformation as long as all family indices
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}
are treated equally.
For instance, nothing gives especial weight to
j
in the framework of the 6d abelian gauge theory, and then the theory is modular symmetric as a whole.
In contrast, it is well-known that the 10d super Yang-Mills theory appearing as an effective field theory of D9-branes contains non-zero Yukawa interactions among matter fields.
Those may distinguish the labels
j
, since the spinor fields with different labels couple to other matter fields differently.
It depends on concrete setups of flux configurations, and one should be careful of this issue.

6
Implications on
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{N}

6.1
Winding numbers on
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{N}

We move to the orbifolds
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.
We first summarize the results in the previous paper
[
37
]
.
There, the authors have discovered a zero-mode counting formula.
It counts the number of independent orbifold zero modes on all the orbifolds
T^{/}\mathbb{Z}_{N}
for arbitrary pattern of the flux quanta
M
, the Scherk-Schwarz twists
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
, and the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
eigenvalue
\eta
.

In this section, we treat the fixed points (
2.12
) in the complex coordinate
z
,

\displaystyle p_{1}=0,\quad p_{2}=1/2,\quad p_{3}=\tau/2,\quad p_{4}=(1+\tau)/2

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2},

(6.1)

\displaystyle p_{1}=0,\quad p_{2}=(2+\tau)/3,\quad p_{3}=(1+2\tau)/2

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{3},

(6.2)

\displaystyle p_{1}=0,\quad p_{2}=(1+i)/2,\quad p_{3}=1/2,\quad p_{4}=i/2

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{4},

(6.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-3.0pt\begin{array}[]{c}p_{1}=0,\quad p_{2}=(1+\tau)/3,\quad p_{3}=2(1+\tau)/3,\\[3.0pt]
p_{4}=1/2,\quad p_{5}=\tau/2,\quad p_{6}=(1+\tau)/2\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{6}.

Here, in addition to the typical fixed points, we have taken into account “
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
fixed points” on
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, and also “
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
fixed points” on
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{6}
, which are not invariant under the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
rotation, but invariant under its partial transformations
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.
Such partial fixed points play an important role in the counting formula on the orbifolds.
This is because there exist localized fluxes at some orbifold singularities.
It is investigated in
[
35
,
36
]
that the mode functions vanish at the orbifold singularities where flux is localized.
We will ignore zeros on the bulk in what follows.

Next, let us consider the orbifold eigen functions (
2.18
) and drop unnecessary arguments
\tau
,
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
for simplicity.
The eigen functions are classified by the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
eigenvalue as

\displaystyle\xi^{j}_{\eta}(\omega z)=\eta\,\xi^{j}_{\eta}(z)

(6.6)

with
\eta=\omega^{k}\,\,(k=0,1,...,N-1)
and
\omega=e^{2\pi i/N}
.
Given the (pseudo-)periodicity conditions (
2.7
) and the reflection (
6.6
), one can easily obtain for a given fixed point
p_{i}
the form

\displaystyle\xi^{j}_{\eta}(\omega z+p_{i})=e^{2\pi iw/N}\,\xi^{j}_{\eta}(z+p_{i}),\qquad\omega=e^{2\pi i/N}.

(6.7)

Then, the winding number
\chi_{i}
around
z=p_{i}
is precisely equal to
w
(mod
N
)
[
37
]
.
For “
\mathbb{Z}_{N^{\prime}}
fixed points”, we exceptionally adopt
N^{\prime}
instead of
N
.
We list the winding numbers on each orbifold,

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-3.0pt\begin{array}[]{c}\chi_{1}=k,\quad\chi_{2}=k-2\alpha_{1},\quad\chi_{3}=k-2\alpha_{2},\\[3.0pt]
\chi_{4}=k-2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})-M\quad(\text{mod~}2)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2},

\displaystyle\chi_{1}=k,\quad\chi_{2}=-M-6\alpha+k,\quad\chi_{3}=2M+6\alpha+k\quad(\text{mod~}3)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{3},

(6.10)

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-3.0pt\begin{array}[]{c}\chi_{1}=k,\quad\chi_{2}=M-4\alpha+k\quad(\text{mod~}4),\\[3.0pt]
\chi_{3}=\chi_{4}=-2\alpha+k\quad(\text{mod~}2)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{4},

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-3.0pt\begin{array}[]{c}\chi_{1}=k\quad(\text{mod~}6),\\[3.0pt]
\chi_{2}=\chi_{3}=M/2-3\alpha-2k\quad(\text{mod~}3),\\[3.0pt]
\chi_{4}=\chi_{5}=\chi_{6}=-2\alpha-k\quad(\text{mod~}2)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{6}.

The explicit expressions for (
6.7
) are thoroughly listed in Appx.
C
.

6.2
Invariance of zero-mode counting formula

We turn to the zero-mode counting formula on the magnetized orbifolds
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.
In terms of the winding numbers obtained previously, the number of independent orbifold zero modes,
n
, is counted by
[
37
]

\displaystyle n=\frac{M-V}{N}+1,

(6.16)

where
V\equiv\sum_{i}\chi_{i}
denotes the sum of winding numbers around the orbifold singularities.
Note that
V
can be calculated only by the translation conditions (
2.7
) and the reflection one (
6.6
), by construction.
Hence, as long as we adopt (
4.12
) and (
4.16
), the counting formula is symmetric under the modular transformations
S
and
T
in
\Gamma
(as well as
\Gamma^{\prime}
).

We finally give a comment on a physical meaning of the orbifold fixed points, geometrically and gauge-theoretically.
As is well-known, the fixed points have a geometrical meaning, since infinite curvatures exist at them (as well as the partial fixed points).
Furthermore, there are localized flux sources at them from the gauge-theoretical point of view
[
35
,
36
]
.
Hence, the fixed points listed in (
6.1
) – (
6.1
) do have a physical meaning.
Recall that the fixed points
z_{\mathrm{fp}}
are the solution of an equation

\displaystyle z_{\mathrm{fp}}=\omega z_{\mathrm{fp}}+m+n\tau

(6.17)

for
\exists\,m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Here, the both sides transform under the modular transformation, and the fixed points (
6.1
) – (
6.1
) remain as fixed points again.
Although its connection to the index theorem has not been clarified in the literature,
a priori
the zero-mode counting formula (
6.16
) is modular-invariant.
Notice that it is not invariant if we exclude (
4.12
) and (
4.16
).
For instance, one then finds an incorrect result after some algebra: the points
p_{2}
and
p_{3}
do not behave as fixed points, and
\chi_{2}=\chi_{3}=0
for
N=3
,
M=6m+2\,\,(m\in\mathbb{N})
,
\eta=\omega^{2}
and
\alpha=1/3
.
Hence, the modular symmetry of the counting formula necessitates that the twist phases
\alpha_{i}\,\,(i=1,2)
appropriately transform as (
4.12
) and (
4.16
) under the
T
and
S
transformations.

This is subtle, but would give a correct understanding for the modular symmetry and shed new light on modular flavor symmetries.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have considered the two-dimensional torus
T^{2}
and the toroidal orbifolds
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}\,\,(N=2,3,4,6)
with a background magnetic field.
Inspired by the recent papers
[
41
,
39
,
50
]
, we have revisited the modular group in magnetic compactifications.
We have focused on the zeros of the ground-state mode functions and looked at the transformation property of the zeros.
We have also examined what type of boundary conditions must be satisfied after the modular transformation.
A crucial observation is that

•

the Scherk-Schwarz twist phases
\alpha_{i}\,\,(i=1,2)
transform under the
T
and
S
transformations if and only if the (pseudo-)periodicity conditions (
2.7
) are valid even after the modular transformation.

This observation precisely agrees with two necessities

•

the zeros are also identical under two identical transformations
S^{4}=(ST)^{3}=\mathbbm{1}
,

•

the zero-mode counting formula on the orbifolds is modular symmetric.

Furthermore, we have mentioned another definition of the modular group as
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, and it turned out that our approach in terms of zeros yields the same implication as that of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

In our conclusion, it is reasonable to adopt the twists (
4.12
) and (
4.16
) after the
T
and
S
transformations.
Thus, the conclusion justifies the claim in
[
50
]
that the periodicity conditions (
2.7
) is common under the modular transformation.
A crucial consequence in this paper is that the replacements (
4.12
) and (
4.16
) are justified only after we take the positions of zeros and their modular-equivalence into account.
It would help further analyses on modular flavor symmetries and their phenomenological applications.
We will pursue them somewhere.

Appendix B
Jacobi theta-functions

We list the properties of Jacobi theta-functions (cf.

]

The Jacobi theta-function with characteristics
a
and
b
is defined as

\displaystyle\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}a\\[3.0pt]
b\end{bmatrix}(c,d)=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\pi i(a+l)^{2}d}\,e^{2\pi i(a+l)(c+b)}.

(B.1)

It satisfied the relations

\displaystyle\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}a\\[3.0pt]
b\end{bmatrix}(c,d+1)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\pi(a^{2}-a)}~\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}a\\[3.0pt]
b+a-\tfrac{1}{2}\end{bmatrix}(c,d),

(B.2)

\displaystyle\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}a\\[3.0pt]
b\end{bmatrix}(c+1,d)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi ia}~\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}a\\[3.0pt]
b\end{bmatrix}(c,d),

(B.3)

\displaystyle\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}a\\[3.0pt]
b\end{bmatrix}(c+d,d)

\displaystyle=e^{-2\pi i(b+c+d/2)}~\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}a\\[3.0pt]
b\end{bmatrix}(c,d),

(B.4)

and these are used to compute the (pseudo-periodicity) boundary conditions for
\xi^{j}_{T}
.
Using an identity

\displaystyle\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}a\\[3.0pt]

-a\end{bmatrix}(c,d),

(B.5)

one can reduce some complicated computations to easier ones in terms of (
B.3
) and (
B.4

Weighted p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) p(\cdot) -Poincaré and Sobolev inequalities for vector fields satisfiying Hörmander’s condition and applications Thanks: The authors are partially supported by CONICET and SECYT-UNC
Lucas A. Vallejos, Raúl E. Vidal

Abstract
. In this paper we will establish different weighted Poincaré inequalities with variable exponents on Carnot-Carathéodory spaces or Carnot groups. We will use different techniques to obtain these inequalities. For vector fields satisfying Hörmander’s condition in variable non-isotropic Sobolev spaces, we consider a weight in the variable Muckenhoupt class A p ​ ( ⋅ ) , p ∗ ​ ( ⋅ ) A_{p(\cdot),p^{\ast}(\cdot)} , where the exponent p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) p(\cdot) satisfies appropriate hypotheses, and in this case we obtain the first order weighted Poincaré inequalities with variable exponents. In the case of Carnot groups we also set up the higher order weighted Poincaré inequalities with variable exponents. For these results the crucial part is proving the boundedness of the fractional integral operator on Lebesgue spaces with weighted and variable exponents on spaces of homogeneous type. Moreover, using other techniques, we extend some of these results when the exponent satisfies a jump condition and the weight is in a smaller Muckenhoupt class. Finally, we will use these weighted Poincaré inequalities to establish the existence and uniqueness of a minimizer to the Dirichlet energy integral for a problem involving a degenerate p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) p(\cdot) -Laplacian with zero boundary values in Carnot groups.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Analysis on Carnot-Carathéodory spaces

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty set. We say
(\mathcal{X},d,\mu)
is a
homogeneous space if:

•

\mathcal{X}
is endowed with a quasi-metric
d
such that the balls
are open in the topology induced by
d
(in particular, they form a base).
Recall that
d
is a quasi-metric if:

(1)

d(x,y)=0
if only if
x=y
,

(2)

d(x,y)=d(y,x)
for all
x
, and
y
in
\mathcal{X}
,

(3)

(2.1)

d(x,y)\leq K(d(x,z)+d(z,y)),\qquad\text{for all }\,x,y,z\in\mathcal{X},

where
K\geq 1
is independent of
x
,
y
and
z
.

•

\mu
is a positive borel measure on
\mathcal{X}
, satisfying the
doubling condition

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C\mu(B(x,r)),\ x\in X,r>0;

where
B(x,r)=\{y\in\mathcal{X}:d(x,y)<r\}
is the ball of radius
r
and
centered in
x
.

Let
X=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n_{1}})
be a family of infinitely differentiable
vector fields defined in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We identify each
X_{j}
with the
first order differential operator acting on Lipschitz functions. For a
Lipschitz function
f
we define
Xf(x)=(X_{1}f(x),\dots,X_{n_{1}}f(x))
,

|Xf(x)|=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}|X_{j}f(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and for
f=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n_{1}})
,

\text{div}(f)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}X_{j}f_{j}(x).

Given an open, connected set
\Omega
,
X
is said to satisfy Hörmander’s condition in
\Omega
if there exists a neighborhood
\Omega_{0}

of
\Omega
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that the family of commutators of
the vector fields in
X
up to length
m
span
\mathbb{R}^{n}
at every
point of
\Omega_{0}
. Hereafter, we will assume that
X
satisfies Hörmander’s condition on every bounded, connected subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
C_{X}
be the family of absolutely continuous curves
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
a\leq b
, such that there exist measurable functions
c_{j}(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
,
j=1,\cdots,n_{1}
, satisfying
\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}c_{j}(t)^{2}\leq 1
and
\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}c_{j}(t)X(\gamma(t))
for almost every
t\in[a,b]
.
Given
x,y\in\Omega
, define

d(x,y)=\inf\{T>0:\exists\gamma\in C_{X}\text{ s. t. }\gamma(0)=x\text{
and }\gamma(T)=y\}.

The function
d
is a quasi-metric on
\Omega
called the
Carnot-Carathéodory metric associated to
X
, and the pair
(\Omega,d)

is said to be a Carnot-Carathéodory space. We refer the reader to
[
NSW
]
for more details on Carnot-Carathéodory spaces.

Nagel, Stein and Wainger proved in
[
NSW
]
that for every compact set
K\subset\Omega
there exist positive constants
R
and
C(n_{1})
such
that

|B(x,2r)|\leq C(n_{1})|B(x,r)|,\,\forall\,x\in K\text{ and }0<r<R,

where
|E|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of the measurable set
E
.
Therefore the Carnot-Carathéodory space
(\Omega,d,dx)
is a type
homogeneous space. The homogeneous dimension of the Carnot-Carathéodory
space is
Q=\log_{2}C(n_{1})
.

An important type of Carnot-Carathéodory space are the Carnot groups. A
Carnot group is a simply connected and connected Lie group
\mathbb{G}
,
whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is stratified, this means that
\mathfrak{g}
admits a vector space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{m}
with grading
[V_{1},V_{j}]=V_{j+1}
, for
j=1,\cdots,m-1,

and has a family of dilations
\{\delta_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
such
that
\delta_{\epsilon}X=\epsilon^{j}X
if
X\in V_{j}
. Denote by
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}
, where
n_{k}
is the dimension of
V_{k}
. In this
case, the homogeneous dimension is
Q=\sum_{k=1}^{m}kn_{k}
, see
[
FS
]
.

It is well-known that the Haar measure for
\mathbb{G}
is a Lebesgue
measure. Let
X=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n_{1}}\}
be a basis of
V_{1}
. It is
clear that
X
satisfies Hörmander’s condition in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
is naturally associated with
\{X_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n_{1}}
a Carnot-Carathéodory metric
d(x,y)
for
x,y\in\mathbb{G}
. The geometry of the metric
space
(\mathbb{G},d)
is described in
[
NSW
]
,
[
FP
]
and
[
S
]
.
In particular, the
d-
topology and the Euclidean topology are equivalent
and the Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
is a homogeneous space with the metric
d

and the Lebesgue measure. We get
|B(x,r)|\sim r^{Q}
and

|B(x,2r)|\lesssim 2^{Q}|B(x,r)|.

Let
\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\}
be a basis of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

and
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
a multiindex. We set
X^{I}=X_{1}^{i_{1}}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\dots X_{n}^{i_{n}}
. The operators
X^{I}

form a basis for the algebra of left invariant differential operators on
\mathbb{G}
, by the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem. The order of the
differential operators
X^{I}
is
|I|=i_{1}+i_{2}+\dots+i_{n}
and its

homogeneous degree
is
d(I)=\lambda_{1}i_{1}+\lambda_{2}i_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{n}i_{n}
, where
\lambda_{j}=i
if
X_{j}\in V_{i}
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

|X^{m}f|=\left(\sum_{I:d(I)=m}|X^{I}f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

A function
f
on
\mathbb{G}\backslash\{0\}
will be called
homogeneous of degree
m
if
f\circ\delta_{r}=r^{m}f
for
r>0
. We say
that a function
P
on
\mathbb{G}
is a
polynomial
if
P\circ\exp

is a polynomial on
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
be the basis
for the linear forms on
\mathfrak{g}
dual to the basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}

on
\mathfrak{g}
. Let us set
\eta_{j}=\xi_{j}\circ\exp^{-1}
, then
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}
are polynomials on
\mathbb{G}
which form a global
coordinate system on
\mathbb{G}
, and generate the algebra of polynomials
on
\mathbb{G}
. Thus, every polynomial on
\mathbb{G}
can be written
uniquely as
P=\sum\limits_{I}a_{I}\eta^{I}
, for
\eta^{I}=\eta_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots\eta_{n}^{i_{n}}
,
a_{I}\in\mathbb{C}
where all but finitely many of
them vanish. The function
\eta^{I}
is homogeneous of order
d(I)
. The

homogeneous order
is
\max\{d(I):a_{I}\neq 0\}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
we define the space
\mathcal{P}_{j}
of polynomials of
homogeneous degree
\leq j
. See
[
FS
]
.

Finally we define the weak Boman chain condition for a domain
\Omega
in a
metric space
\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A domain
\Omega
in a metric space
\mathcal{X}
is said to satisfy the weak Boman chain condition of type
\sigma
,
\Lambda
, or to be a member of
\mathcal{F}(\sigma,\Lambda)

constants
\sigma>1
,
\Lambda>1
, and a family
\mathcal{F}
of metric
balls

such that

•

\Omega=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{F}}B

•

2.2.
Weighted variable Lebesgue space and clasical operators

The Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and the fractional integral operator
of order

Mf(x)=\sup_{B\ni x}\dfrac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}f(y)d\mu(y),

The weighted boundedness of these operators has been extensively studied in
the literature, see

]

[

We must first start by introducing important definitions about variable
exponents.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d,\mu)
be a homogeneous space and let

be the collection of all measurable functions

\displaystyle p_{E}^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{E}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ess~\sup_{x\in E}~p(x)\text{.}

If

we write
p^{-}
and

We next define the jump condition of a variable exponent.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a subset of a Carnot-Carathéodory space

in

Remark 2.3

The hypothesis

Let

be

satisfies

for some

and

When the exponent and the measure are clear from context, we simply write

If

and

is a Banach space with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\mu}^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho(f/\lambda)\leq 1\right\};

if

When the measura is given by

In this section we are going to define Sobolev spaces with variable exponent

3.
Proof of the main results.

In order to prove the theorems we will need to obtain the bounded fractional integral operator

[

]
for the case

. But it is not difficult to adapt for general homogeneous spaces.

We will write the extrapolation theorem for pair of functions

contained in some family
\mathcal{F}

\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq C\|g\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.\qquad\qquad(f,g)\in\mathcal{F},

where
\mathcal{X}
and

are weighted classical or variable Lebesgue spaces, then mean this inequality is true for every pair

such that the left hand side of this inequality is finite.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let be
(\mathcal{X},d,\mu)
a homogeneous space. Suppose that for some

,

\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}f(x)^{q_{0}}\omega_{0}(x)^{q_{0}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}g(x)^{p_{0}}\omega_{0}(x)^{p_{0}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\text{, \ }(f,g)\in\mathcal{F}\text{.}

Given
p(\cdot),q(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
, suppose

\frac{1}{p(x)}-\frac{1}{q(x)}=\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}\text{.}

Define
\sigma\geq 1
by
\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}

\omega\in A_{p(\cdot),q(\cdot)}
and

is a

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}_{\omega}(\mathcal{X})}\leq C\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}_{\omega}(\mathcal{X})}\text{, \ \ }(f,g)\in\mathcal{F}\text{ .}

The theorem holds if

if we assume only that the maximal operator is

(b)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\left\langle A(x)Xu(x),Xu(x)\right\rangle^{{\frac{p(x)-2}{2}}}A(x)Xu(x),Xu_{n}(x)-Xu\right\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)^{p(x)}|u|^{p(x)-2}u(u_{n}-u)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}f(x)(u_{n}(x)-u(x))\,dx.

By hypothesis

, observe that

. Let

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{-p^{\prime}(x)}\left(\left\langle A(x)Xu(x),Xu(x)\right\rangle^{{\frac{p(x)-2}{2}}}\left|A(x)Xu(x)\right|\right)^{p^{\prime}(x)}\omega^{-p^{\prime}(x)}dx

2.2
A useful orientation

The following lemma is the main tool used to prove the lower bound in (
7

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
, and fix
d\coloneqq 2\log_{2}n+1
.
Then there exists an orientation
\vec{G}
of
G
such that every acyclic set of vertices in
\vec{G}
is
d
-sparse in
G
.

Proof.

Let
\vec{G}
be a uniformly random orientation of
G
. Let
X
be any subset of vertices of
G
inducing a subgraph of average degree at least
d\coloneqq 2\log_{2}n+1
. We denote
s\coloneqq|X|
; there are at least
ds/2
edges in
G[X]
.
For each acyclic orientation of
G[X]
, there is an ordering
(x_{1},\dots,x_{s})
of
X
that
x_{i}x_{j}
is an arc if and only if
x_{i}x_{j}\in E(G)
and
i<j
.
Hence the number of acylic orientations of
G[X]
is at most the number of orderings of
X
, so at most
s!
. Since there are exactly
2^{|E(G[X])|}\geq 2^{ds/2}
orientations of
G[X]
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left[\mbox{$X$ is acyclic in $\vec{G}$}\right]\leq\frac{s!}{2^{ds/2}}.

On the other hand, there are
\binom{n}{s}
possible choices for a subset
X
of
s
vertices, so the probability that there exists an acyclic induced subdigraph of
\vec{G}
of density more than
d
is at most

\sum_{s\geq 1}\binom{n}{s}\frac{s!}{2^{ds/2}}\leq\sum_{s\geq 1}\left(\frac{n}{2^{d/2}}\right)^{s}\leq\sum_{s\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{s}<1.

We conclude that there exists an orientation
\vec{G}
of
G
such that every acyclic set of
\vec{G}
is
d
-sparse, as desired.
∎

Theorem 2.5

For every graph

\chi(G)\leq 2\vec{\chi}(G)\left(1+\left\lfloor\log_{2}n\right\rfloor\right).

Let

Then every acyclic subdigraph of
\vec{G}
is
\left\lfloor d\right\rfloor

(\left\lfloor d\right\rfloor+1)
-colouring, where

be a dicolouring of
\vec{G}

\vec{\chi}(\vec{G})\leq\vec{\chi}(G)

\chi(G)\leq\vec{\chi}(G)(\left\lfloor d\right\rfloor+1)=2\vec{\chi}(G)\left(1+\left\lfloor\log_{2}n\right\rfloor\right).

∎

The goal of this section is to prove the upper bound of (

Given a digraph

To the best of our knowledge, the possible tightness of that bound has never been discussed in the literature. We prove that there always exists

such that
\vec{\chi}(D)=\chi(D[\prec])
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For every digraph
D
, one has

\vec{\chi}(D)=\min\,\Big\{\chi(D[\prec]):\mbox{$\prec$ is a total order on $V(D)$}\Big\}.

Proof.

We have already shown that
\vec{\chi}(D)\leq\chi(D[\prec])
for every total order
\prec
on
V(D)
.
Let us prove the other direction.
Let

, and let
\phi
be a
k
-dicolouring of

.
Let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}
be the colour classes of
\phi
. For every
i\in[k]
,

is acyclic, and so induces a partial order on
C_{i}
. Let
\prec_{i}
be a total order of

that extends this partial order.
For every

, we let
u\prec v\equiv(\phi(u)<\phi(v))\vee(v\prec_{\phi(v)}u)
.
It is straightforward that each

is an independent set in

. This proves that

∎

Theorem
3.2
reduces the problem of finding a minimum dicolouring of a digraph

to finding a total order

on

Lemma 3.3

Let

Given a class of graphs

Theorem 3.6

For every triangle-free graph

\vec{\chi}(G)\leq(1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{2n}{\ln n}}\text{\hskip 10.22217ptas $n\to\infty$.}

The following proof is similar in many aspects to that of

Let

, and let

Lemma 4.1

Now, for a spanning directed linear forest

By the Gallai-Milgram Theorem
[

can be covered with at most

To show the other direction, let

be a maximum independent set of

be an orientation of

be any directed linear forest of

Lemma 4.7
.

Let

,

(i)

has no directed cycle,

(ii)

(iii)

there is no edge between

and

(iv)

2
-dicolouring of
Z
such that
c^{-1}(1)
is either empty, a single vertex or two adjacent vertices,

then
D
is
2
-dicolourable.

Proof.

Consider the
2
-dicolouring

of
Z
given by
(iv)
and extend it by colouring the vertices of
Y
with colour 1 and the vertices of
X
with colour 2. There is no monochromatic cycle in
Z
nor in
X
, and none can intersect
X
as its neighbourhood is included in
Y
, and therefore has a different colour. So if there were a monochromatic cycle, it would intersect both

and

, thus have colour 1.
Y
is pendent so the cycle contains two vertices of

, but as

is triangle-free, no vertex can be adjacent to both colour 1 vertices in

, so there cannot be any monochromatic cycle.

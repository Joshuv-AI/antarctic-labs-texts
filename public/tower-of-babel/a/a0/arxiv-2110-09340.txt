Fixed points of Koch’s maps Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 32H50, 37F99. Thanks: Keywords: holomorphic dynamics, holomorphic endomorphisms, fixed points, eigenvalues.
Van Tu Le

Abstract
. We study endomorphisms constructed by Sarah Koch in [ Koc13 ] and we focus on the eigenvalues of the differential of such maps at its fixed points. In [ Koc13 ] , to each post-critically finite unicritical polynomial, Koch associated a post-critically algebraic endomorphism of ℂℙ k {\mathbb{CP}}^{k} . Koch showed that the eigenvalues of the differentials of such maps along periodic cycles outside the post-critical sets have modulus strictly greater than 1 1 . In this article, we show that the eigenvalues of the differentials at fixed points are either 0 0 or have modulus strictly greater than 1 1 . This confirms a conjecture proposed by the author in his thesis. We also provide a concrete description of such values in terms of the multiplier of a unicritical polynomial.



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be either
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
or
{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}
and
f\colon M\to M
be a holomorphic endomorphism. Denote by
f^{\circ m}=f\circ f\circ\ldots\circ f
the
m
-th composition of
f
. A point
z\in M
is called a
preperiodic point
of
preperiod

k
and of
period

m
if
f^{\circ(k+m)}(z)=f^{\circ k}(z)
and
k,m
are the smallest integers satisfying such a property. A preperiodic point of preperiod
0
is called a
periodic point
. A periodic point of period
1
is called a
fixed point
. Given a periodic point
z
of period
m
, a value
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
is called
an eigenvalue of
f
along the orbit of
z
(or
at the fixed point
z
) if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of the differential
D_{z}f^{\circ m}\colon T_{z}M\to T_{z}M
.

A point
z\in M
is called
a critical point
if the differential
D_{z}f\colon T_{z}M\to T_{f(z)}M
is not invertible. The set
C(f)
containing all critical points of
f
is called the
critical set
of
f
. The set

PC(f)\coloneqq\bigcup\limits_{j\geq 1}f^{\circ j}(C(f))

is called the
post-critical set
of
f
. The endomorphism
f
is called
post-critically algebraic
if
PC(f)
is an algebraic set of codimension one in
M
. When
\dim M=1
, post-critically algebraic rational maps are called
post-critically finite
rational maps.

The family of post-critically finite rational maps is one of the most important families of maps in the theory of one dimensional complex dynamics. In higher dimension, post-critically algebraic endomorphisms are interesting family of maps since many results, which are well-known for post-critically finite rational maps, remain unknown. We refer to
[
Ron08
]
,
[
Ast20
]
,
[
IRS19
]
,
[
GV19
]
,
[
Ji20
]
,
[
Le20b
]
for some recent studies about post-critically algebraic endomorphisms. In this article, we focus on the following conjecture proposed by the author in his thesis
[
Le20a
]
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
f
be a post-critically algebraic endomorphism of
{\mathbb{CP}}^{n},n\geq 2
of degree
d\geq 2
and
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of
f
along a periodic cycle. Then either
\lambda=0
or
|\lambda|>1
.

The conjecture has been verified by the author in the case
n=2
and in the case in any dimension with the periodic cycles outside the post-critical set. In this article, we shall verify the conjecture for the family of post-critically algebraic endomorphisms associated to unicritical polynomials constructed by Sarah Koch in
[
Koc13
]
, or
Koch maps
for short.

We shall now describe the family of Koch maps we want to study and we refer to
[
Koc13
]
for the original construction. Throughout this article, we fix

d\in\mathbb{N},d\geq 2\text{ and }\beta^{d}=1,\beta\neq 1.

The maps
\{G_{k,m}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{k+m-1}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{k+m-1}\mid(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\}
constructed by Sarah Koch are of the following forms (see
[
Koc13
, Proposition 6.1 - 6.2]
),

•

if
k=0
,

G_{0,m}\colon\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m-1}\end{array}\right)\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-x_{m-1}^{d}\\
x_{1}^{d}-x_{m-1}^{d}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m-2}^{d}-x_{m-1}^{d}\end{array}\right),

•

if
k\neq 0
,

G_{k,m}\colon\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{k+m-1}\end{array}\right)\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\left(-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}-x_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\right)^{d}\\
\left(x_{1}-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}-x_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\right)^{d}\\
\vdots\\
\left(x_{k+m-2}-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}-x_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\right)^{d}\end{array}\right).

The map
G_{k,m}
induces a holomorphic endomorphism of
{\mathbb{CP}}^{k+m-2}
which is closely related to maps on moduli spaces used in Thurston’s topological characterization of rational maps. We refer to
[
Koc13
]
,
[
Koc08
]
,
[
DH93
]
for further discussion. In
[
Koc13
]
, Koch showed that
G_{k,m}
is post-critically algebraic. It is natural to ask whether Conjecture
1
is true for
G_{k,m}
. The eigenvalues of Koch maps along a periodic cycle outside the post-critical set are well understood. It is a consequence of its construction that those values has modulus strictly bigger than
1
.

Theorem 1

(Corollary 7.2
[
Koc13
]

)
.

Let
\mu
be an eigenvalue of
G_{k,m}
along a periodic cycle outside the post-critical set. Then
|\mu|>1
.

We refer also to
[
BEK20
]
for a further discussion about the arithmetics of such values. In
[
Koc13
]
, Koch asked whether we have the same conclusion for eigenvalues along a cycle inside the post-critical set. In this article, we answer this question in the positive (and hence verify Conjecture
1
) for the case when the cycle is a fixed point.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mu
be an eigenvalue of a map
G_{k,m}
at a fixed point. Then, either
\mu=0
or
|\mu|>1
.

In fact, we can have even better understanding about the values of such eigenvalues. Thanks to Theorem
1
, we only need to study the eigenvalues at a fixed point inside the post-critical set of
G_{k,m}
. However, the original construction does not provide much information about fixed points inside post-critical set. In order to explain our result, let us take a closer look at
G_{k,m}
.

Let
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
. To a point
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k+m-1}
, we can associate a polynomial
P_{z}
of the following form

P_{z}(t)=\begin{cases}t^{d}-z_{m-1}^{d}\text{ if $k=0$}\\
\left(t-\frac{\beta z_{k+m-1}-z_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\right)^{d}\text{ if $k\neq 0$}.\end{cases}

If
z
is a fixed point of
G_{k,m}
, then for all
1\leq i\leq k+m-1
,
z_{i}=P_{z}^{\circ i}(0)
. Moreover,
P_{z}
is post-critically finite. Indeed,

•

if
k=0
then
P_{z}(z_{m-1})=0
,

•

if
k\neq 0
then

\begin{array}[]{ccl}P_{z}(z_{k+m-1})&=&\left(z_{k+m-1}-\frac{\beta z_{k+m-1}-z_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\right)^{d}=\left(\frac{-z_{k+m-1}+z_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\right)^{d}\\
&=&\left(\frac{\beta z_{k-1}-\beta z_{k+m-1}}{\beta-1}\right)^{d}=P_{z}(z_{k-1})\end{array}

We shall call
P_{z}
the
polynomial associated to
z
since
P_{z}
plays an important role in the study of the eigenvalues of
G_{k,m}
at a fixed point
z
. More precisely, our main result, which completes the description of eigenvalues of Koch maps at fixed points, is the following.

Theorem A’
.

Let
\mu
be an eigenvalue of a map
G_{k,m}
at a fixed point
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k+m-1})
. Let
P_{z}
be the polynomial associated to
z
and
z_{1}
is preperiodic of preperiod
k^{\prime}
and of period
m^{\prime}
to a cycle of multiplier
\lambda
under
P_{z}
.

Only one of the following cases happens:

(1)

\mu=0
.

(2)

\mu
is an eigenvalue of a map
G_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
at a fixed point outside the post-critical set
PC(G_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}})
.

(3)

We have

\mu^{m}=\lambda^{\frac{m}{m^{\prime}}},\mu^{m^{\prime}}\neq\lambda.

We can see that Theorem
A
is a direct consequence of Theorem
A’
. Indeed, if Case
1
or Case
2
happens, Theorem
A
follows from Theorem
1
. If Case
3
happens, since the polynomial
P_{z}
is a post-critically finite polynomial, Theorem
A
follows from the equation
\mu^{m}=\lambda^{\frac{m}{m^{\prime}}}
and the fact that a non-vanishing multiplier of a post-critically finite polynomial has modulus strictly bigger than
1
(see
[
Mil11
, Corollary 14.5]
)

Let us explain briefly our approach. Instead of using the original construction of
G_{k,m}
, we introduce

-

a partial order
\preceq
on
{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,

-

a dynamically equivalent family of maps, that we denote by

\{F_{k,m}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m},(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\},

where
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
is a subspace of the vector spaces of complex sequences
{\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
.

More precisely, with the convention
x_{0}\coloneqq 0
, the space
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
is defined as

\mathcal{M}_{k,m}=\{\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{i})_{i\geq 1}\mid\forall\,i\geq k+1,x_{i}=x_{i+m}\text{ and }\beta x_{k+m}-x_{k}=0\}

and the map
F_{k,m}\colon\mathcal{M}_{k,m}\to\mathcal{M}_{k,m}
is defined as

F_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{y}\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\beta y_{k+m}-y_{k}=0\\
y_{i}=x_{i-1}^{d}+y_{1}\quad\text{ for all $i\geq 2$}.\end{array}\right.

The construction shall be presented in details in Section
2
. For each
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, the maps
F_{k,m}
and
G_{k,m}
are conjugate. Thus, to prove Theorem
A’
, we need to study an eigenvalue
\mu
of
F_{k,m}
at a fixed point
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathcal{M}_{k,m}
. The full statement of what we can prove is Theorem
B’
. Briefly, to each fixed point
\boldsymbol{z}
in
\mathcal{M}_{k,m}
, we associate a pair of integers
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})
and a post-critically finite polynomial
P_{z}
whose critical value is preperiodic of preperiod
k^{\prime}
to a cycle of period
m^{\prime}
. The associated polynomial
P_{z}
for a
\boldsymbol{z}=(z_{i})_{i\geq 1}\in\mathcal{M}_{k,m}
is simply
P_{z}(t)=t^{d}+z_{1}
. The partial order characterizes the following property of the family
\{F_{k,m}\}
:

(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\preceq(k,m)\Leftrightarrow\begin{cases}{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{k,m},\\
F_{k,m}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}=F_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}.\end{cases}

Moreover, we shall show that
\boldsymbol{z}\notin PC(F_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}})
. Thus, if
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})=(k,m)
, our fixed point
\boldsymbol{z}
is outside the post-critical set
PC(F_{k,m})
and we are in Case
2
.

If
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\neq(k,m)
, then
{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
is a proper subset of
\mathcal{M}_{k,m}
which is invariant under
F_{k,m}
and the restriction of
F_{k,m}
to
{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
is exactly
F_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
. If
\mu
has associated eigenvectors tangent to
{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
, since
\boldsymbol{z}\notin PC(F_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}})
, we are again in the Case
2
. Otherwise,
\mu
is the eigenvalue of the transpose
D_{\boldsymbol{z}}F_{k,m}^{*}
of the derivative
D_{\boldsymbol{z}}F_{k,m}
acting on the annihilator
{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{0}=\{\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{k,m}^{*}\mid\omega|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}=0\}
. In such a case, either
\mu=0
or we will show that
{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{0}
has a set of generators on which
D_{\boldsymbol{z}}F_{k,m}^{*}
acts cyclically and we obtain Case
3
by solving a linear algebra problem.

2.
An alternative construction of Koch maps

2.1.
Construction of
F_{k,m}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}

Recall that in this article, we fix

d\in\mathbb{N},d\geq 2\text{ and }\beta^{d}=1,\beta\neq 1

Let
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
and denote by
\mathcal{E}={\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
the vector space of complex sequences
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{i})_{i\geq 1}
. Set
\boldsymbol{0}\coloneqq(0,0,\ldots)
. Let
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{E}
be the one-dimensional subspace consisting of constant sequences,

\mathcal{L}=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{E}\mid\forall i,j\geq 1,\quad x_{i}=x_{j}\},

and
\mathcal{H}_{k,m}\subset\mathcal{E}
be the hyperspace of
\mathcal{E}
defined by

\mathcal{H}_{k,m}\coloneqq\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{E}\mid\beta x_{k+m}-x_{k}=0\}.

with the convention
x_{0}\coloneqq 0
. In particular, when
k=0
,

\mathcal{H}_{0,m}=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{E}\mid x_{m}=0\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, we have
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{H}_{k,m}\oplus\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

On the one hand, given
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{E}
, define
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{E}
by

y_{i}\coloneqq x_{i}-\kappa\text{ with }\kappa=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x_{m}&\text{ if }k=0\\
\frac{\beta x_{k+m}-x_{k}}{\beta-1}&\text{ if }k\geq 1\end{array}\right..

Then
\beta y_{k+m}-y_{k}=0
hence
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
. Note that
\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{L}
hence

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{H}_{k,m}+\mathcal{L}.

On the other hand, assume
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{H}_{k,m}\cap\mathcal{L}
. Then
\beta x_{k+m}-x_{k}=0
and
x_{k+m}=x_{k}
. Since
\beta\neq 1
, we have
x_{k}=x_{k+m}=0
. Moreover,
\boldsymbol{x}
is a constant sequence. Thus,
\boldsymbol{x}
vanishes identically; that is

{\mathcal{H}_{k,m}\cap\mathcal{L}=\{\textbf{0}\}}.\qed

Denote by

\pi_{k,m}\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}

the projection to
\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
parallel to
\mathcal{L}
. In particular,
\pi_{k,m}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
.
Consider the map
\mathcal{Q}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
defined by

\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{y}\quad\text{ with}\quad y_{1}\coloneqq 0\text{ and }y_{i}=x_{i-1}^{d},i\geq 1.

Definition 2.2
.

Given
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*},
the map
F_{k,m}\coloneqq\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
is defined as

F_{k,m}\coloneqq\pi_{k,m}\circ\mathcal{Q}.

We shall now study some important properties of
F_{k,m}
.

2.1.1.
Properties of
F_{k,m}

Lemma 2.3
.

Given
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*},
for every
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{E}
, we have

F_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{y}\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\beta y_{k+m}-y_{k}=0\\
y_{i}=x_{i-1}^{d}+y_{1}\quad\text{ for all $i\geq 2$}.\end{array}\right.

In particular, with the convention
x_{0}\coloneqq 0
, we have

y_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}-x_{m-1}^{d}&\text{ if }k=0,\\
-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}^{d}-x_{k-1}^{d}}{\beta-1}&\text{ if }k\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Assume
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{E}
and
\boldsymbol{y}=\pi_{k,m}(\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{x}))=F_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{x})
. On the one hand,
\boldsymbol{y}\in\pi_{k,m}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
, i.e.

\beta y_{k+m}-y_{k}=0.

On the other hand, set
\boldsymbol{z}=\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{x})
, i.e.
z_{1}=0
and
z_{i}=x_{i-1}^{d}
for all
i\geq 1
. Then since
\boldsymbol{y}=\pi_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{z})
, for all
i\geq 1
,

y_{i}=z_{i}-\kappa\text{ with }\kappa\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z_{m}&\text{ if }k=0\\
\frac{\beta z_{k+m}-z_{k}}{\beta-1}&\text{ if }k\geq 1\end{array}\right..

In particular,

y_{1}=z_{1}-\kappa=-\kappa=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}-x_{m-1}^{d}&\text{ if }k=0,\\
-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}^{d}-x_{k-1}^{d}}{\beta-1}&\text{ if }k\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

whence for all
i\geq 2
,
y_{i}=z_{i}+y_{1}
, i.e.

y_{i}=x_{i-1}^{d}+y_{1}.

Conversely, assume
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{E}
such that
\beta y_{k+m}-y_{k}=0
and for all
i\geq 2
,
y_{i}=x_{i-1}^{d}+y_{1}
. In particular,
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
. Set
\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{y}-\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{x})
. Then for all
i\geq 2
,

z_{i}=y_{i}-x_{i-1}^{d}=y_{1}

Moreover,
z_{1}=x_{0}^{d}+y_{1}=y_{1}
. Hence
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathcal{L}
. In other words,

\boldsymbol{y}=\pi_{k,m}(\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{x}))=F_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{x}).\qed

Although
F_{k,m}
is defined on a vector space of infinite dimension, we will now see that the dynamics of
F_{k,m}
is captured entirely by some finite dimensional vector space. Given a sequence
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{E}
,
\boldsymbol{x}
is preperiodic of preperiod
k
to a cycle of period
m
if for all
i\geq k+1,x_{i}=x_{i+m}
and
k,m
are the smallest integers satisfying such a property. Given
{(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{k,m}\subset\mathcal{E}
be the subspace of preperiodic sequences of preperiod at most
k
to a cycle of period dividing
m
, i.e.

\mathcal{P}_{k,m}\coloneqq\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{E}\mid x_{i+m}=x_{i}\text{ for }i\geq k+1\}.

Since sequences in
\mathcal{P}_{k,m}
are uniquely determined by the first
k+m
entries, the vector space
\mathcal{P}_{k,m}
has finite dimension
k+m
.

Definition 2.4
.

Given
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*},
define

\mathcal{M}_{k,m}\coloneqq\mathcal{P}_{k,m}\cap\mathcal{H}_{k,m}.

Note that the constant sequence
(1,1,\ldots)
is in
\mathcal{P}_{k,m}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
and that
\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
has codimension one in
\mathcal{E}
. Hence,
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
is a vector space of dimension
k+m-1
. The following two lemmas show the importance of
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
to the dynamics of
F_{k,m}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

We have
F_{k,m}({\mathcal{M}}_{k,m})={\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
and
F_{k,m}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
is a nondegenerate homogeneous map of degree
d
.

Proof.

Let us first prove that
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M}_{k,m})\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{k,m}.
Assume
\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
. Since
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{k,m}\cap\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
,
\boldsymbol{x}
has preperiod
k
and period dividing
m
and
\beta x_{k+m}-x_{m}=0
. In particular, since
\beta^{d}=1
, we have
x_{k+m}^{d}=x_{m}^{d}.
Consequently, setting
\boldsymbol{y}\coloneqq\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{x})
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rc}y_{k+1}=x_{k}^{d}+y_{1}=x_{k+m}^{d}+y_{1}=y_{k+m+1}&\\
y_{i}=x_{i-1}^{d}+y_{1}=x_{m+i-1}^{d}+y_{1}=y_{i+m}&\text{ for all $i\geq k+2$}.\end{array}\right.

Thus
\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{x})\in\mathcal{P}_{k,m}
.

Since
\pi_{k,m}(\mathcal{P}_{k,m})\subset\mathcal{P}_{k,m}
,
F_{k,m}(\mathcal{M}_{k,m})\subset\mathcal{P}_{k,m}
. Since
F_{k,m}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
,

F_{k,m}(\mathcal{M}_{k,m})\subset\mathcal{P}_{k,m}\cap\mathcal{H}_{k,m}=\mathcal{M}_{k,m}.

Clearly, the map
\mathcal{Q}
is homogeneous of degree
d
and the map
\pi_{k,m}
is homogeneous of degree
1
, thus
F_{k,m}
is homogeneous of degree
d
.

Let us now prove that
F_{k,m}
is nondegenerate, i.e.
F_{k,m}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{0})=\{\boldsymbol{0}\}
. Assume
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{M}_{k,m}
and

{\pi_{k,m}\circ\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{x})=F_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{x})=0}.

Then
\boldsymbol{y}\coloneqq\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{x})\in\Ker(\pi_{k,m})=\mathcal{L}.
By definition of
\mathcal{Q}
,
y_{1}=0
. Since
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{L}
,
\boldsymbol{y}
is a constant sequence thus
\boldsymbol{y}=\textbf{0}
. This implies that
x_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 1
, i.e.
\boldsymbol{x}=\textbf{0}
.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{k,m}
has finite dimension and
F_{k,m}\colon\mathcal{M}_{k,m}\to\mathcal{M}_{k,m}
is homogeneous and nondegenerate,
F_{k,m}
is surjective, i.e.
F_{k,m}({\mathcal{M}}_{k,m})={\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

We have that
\bigcap\limits_{n\geq 1}F_{k,m}^{\circ n}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{M}_{k,m}.

Proof.

According to the previous lemma,
F_{k,m}(\mathcal{M}_{k,m})=\mathcal{M}_{k,m}
. Thus,

\mathcal{M}_{k,m}\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{n\geq 1}F_{k,m}^{\circ n}(\mathcal{E}).

Conversely, it is enough to prove that

(2.1)

\bigcap\limits_{n\geq 2}F_{k,m}^{\circ n}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathcal{P}_{k,m}.

Indeed, since
F_{k,m}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
and
\mathcal{M}_{k,m}=\mathcal{H}_{k,m}\cap\mathcal{P}_{k,m}
, the inclusion (
2.1
) implies that

F_{k,m}(\mathcal{E})\cap\bigcap\limits_{n\geq 2}F_{k,m}^{\circ n}(\mathcal{E})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{k,m}\cap\mathcal{P}_{k,m},

and hence

\bigcap\limits_{n\geq 1}F_{k,m}^{\circ n}(\mathcal{E})\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{k,m}\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{n\geq 1}F_{k,m}^{\circ n}(\mathcal{E}).

To prove (
2.1
), we show the following claim: for all
n\geq 2
, if
\boldsymbol{y}\in F_{k,m}^{\circ n}(\mathcal{E})
, then
y_{i+m}=y_{i}
for all
i\in\{k+1,\ldots,k+n-1\}
.

Let us prove this claim by induction in
n
. If
n=2
, assume
\boldsymbol{y}\in F_{k,m}^{\circ 2}(\mathcal{E})
, i.e.
\boldsymbol{y}=F_{k,m}^{\circ 2}(\boldsymbol{x})
for some
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{E}
. Setting
\boldsymbol{z}=F_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{x})
, according to Lemma
2.3
, we have
\beta z_{k+m}-z_{k}=0
. Thus, since
\boldsymbol{y}=F_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{z})
and since
\beta^{d}=1
, also according to Lemma
2.3
, we have

y_{k+1+m}=z_{k+m}^{d}+y_{1}=z_{k}^{d}+y_{1}=y_{k+1},

i.e. the claim is true for
n=2.

Assume that it holds for some
n>2
. Assume
\boldsymbol{y}\in F_{k,m}^{\circ(n+1)}(\mathcal{E}),
i.e.
\boldsymbol{y}=F_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{x})
with
\boldsymbol{x}\in F_{k,m}^{\circ n}(\mathcal{E})
. The induction hypothesis implies that

x_{i+m-1}=x_{i-1}\text{ for all $i\in\{k+2,\ldots,k+n\}$},

so that

y_{i+m}=y_{i}\text{ for all $i\in\{k+2,\ldots,k+n\}$}.

In addition, since
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{H}_{k,m}
,
\beta x_{k+m}=x_{k}
, so that
y_{k+m+1}=y_{k+1}
. Thus the claim is true for
n+1
.
∎

3.1.
F_{k,m}
is conjugate to

The following lemma assures that the class of maps
F_{k,m}
for

is a good alternative when one wants to study

.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
be such that
k+m-1\geq 2
, the maps
F_{k,m}
and
G_{k,m}
are holomorphically conjugate.

Proof.

Recall that when
k=0,m\geq 3
, we have

G_{0,m}:\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m-1}\end{array}\right)\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-x_{m-1}^{d}\\
x_{1}^{d}-x_{m-1}^{d}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m-2}^{d}-x_{m-1}^{d}\end{array}\right).

and when
k\geq 1,m\geq 2
, we have

G_{k,m}:\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{k+m-1}\end{array}\right)\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\left(-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}-x_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\right)^{d}\\
\left(x_{1}-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}-x_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\right)^{d}\\
\\
\vdots\\
\left(x_{k+m-2}-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}-x_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\right)^{d}\end{array}\right).

Let
i_{k,m}\colon\mathcal{M}_{k,m}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{k+m-1},i_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{x})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+m-1})
and set

\widetilde{F_{k,m}}=i_{k,m}\circ F_{k,m}\circ i_{k,m}^{-1}.

Then we have
when
k=0
,

\widetilde{F_{0,m}}:\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m-1}\end{array}\right)\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-x_{m-1}^{d}\\
x_{1}^{d}-x_{m-1}^{d}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m-2}^{d}-x_{m-1}^{d}\end{array}\right).

and when
k\geq 1
,

\widetilde{F_{k,m}}:\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{k+m-1}\end{array}\right)\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}^{d}-x_{k-1}^{d}}{\beta-1}\\
x_{1}^{d}-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}^{d}-x_{k-1}^{d}}{\beta-1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{k+m-2}^{d}-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}^{d}-x_{k-1}^{d}}{\beta-1}\end{array}\right).

It is enough to show that
G_{k,m}
and
\widetilde{F_{k,m}}
are conjugate. Indeed, let
\tau\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{k+m-1}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{k+m-1}
be a linear map of the following form

\tau\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{k+m-1}\end{array}\right)\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\tau_{1}\\
x_{1}+\tau_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{k+m-2}+\tau_{1}\end{array}\right)\text{ with }\tau_{1}=\begin{cases}-x_{m-1}\text{ when $k=0$}\\
-\frac{\beta x_{k+m-1}-x_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\text{ when $k\geq 1$}\end{cases}

and set

\mathfrak{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+m-1})=(x_{1}^{d},\ldots,x_{k+m-1}^{d})

then
G_{k,m}=\mathfrak{d}\circ\tau,\widetilde{F_{k,m}}=\tau\circ\mathfrak{d}
. Thus,

\tau\circ G_{k,m}=\widetilde{F_{k,m}}\circ\tau

Note that
\tau
is an isomorphism, whence

and
\widetilde{F_{k,m}}
are conjugate.

∎

3.2.
Comparing
F_{k,m}
by a partial order

Our initial expectation was that for arbitrary pairs

and
(k_{2},m_{2})

and

would agree on the intersection

. However this is not true as shown in the following example. Consider the case

and
\beta=-1
, the sequence

\boldsymbol{x}\coloneqq\{2,0,0,\ldots,0,\ldots\}

Then

However,

We will now see that if some order

is satisfied, with the fixed

\beta
, we have
{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{k,m}
and

to

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\preceq
be the partial order on

defined by

(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\preceq(k,m)\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}m^{\prime}\text{ divides }m,\\
\text{either }k^{\prime}=k\text{ or }(k^{\prime}=0\text{ and }m^{\prime}\text{ divides }k).\end{array}\right.

The strict order
\prec
is defined by

(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\prec(k,m)\Leftrightarrow(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\preceq(k,m)\text{ and }(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\neq(k,m).

Proposition 3.3
.

For two pairs of integers
(k,m),(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}

(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\preceq(k,m)\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{k,m}

(k,m)\preceq(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\Leftrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{k,m}.

k^{\prime}
to a cycle of period dividing
m^{\prime}

•

,

•

and

Let us now assume that

•

either

3.3.
The post-critical set of
F_{k,m}

In this section, we fix a pair of integers
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
. Recall that

Lemma 3.4
.

We have that
C(F_{k,m})=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\mid x_{i}=0\text{ for some $1\leq i\leq k+m-1$}\}

Proof.

Recall that
F_{k,m}=\pi_{k,m}\circ\mathcal{Q}
. Differentiating both sides, we see that for any
\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
and for any
\boldsymbol{v}\in T_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}
,

On the one hand, assume
\boldsymbol{x}\in C(F_{k,m})
. Then there exists
\boldsymbol{v}\in T_{\boldsymbol{x}}{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\setminus\{\textbf{0}\}
such that
x_{i}^{d-1}v_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 1
. Observe that there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,k+m-1\}
such that

whence
x_{i}=0
. Indeed otherwise,

and by preperiodicity,

for all

.

On the other hand, given
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{E}
, define
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{E}
by

Then

. Moreover, if

. Finally, if there exists

Definition 3.5
.

Denote by

\Delta_{k,m}=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\mid\text{ there exists $1\leq i<j\leq k+m$ such that $x_{i}=x_{j}$}\}

.

We have that

and

. Consequently,
{PC(F_{k,m})\subseteq\Delta_{k,m}}
.

Let

. Then by Lemma

, there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,k+m-1\}

. By Lemma

, we have

Thus
\boldsymbol{y}\in\Delta_{k,m}

.

Now we prove that
\Delta_{k,m}
is invariant under

. Assume
\boldsymbol{x}\in\Delta_{k,m}
and set
\boldsymbol{y}=F_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{x})
. Then there exist
1\leq i<j\leq k+m
such that

. By Lemma

, for every

•

If

•

If

–

either

4.1.
Relation with post-critically finite polynomials

There is a close connection between fixed points
F_{k,m}
and post-critically finite polynomials. More precisely, we will consider monic centered unicritical polynomials of degree
d\geq 2
,

P(t)=t^{d}+c\in{\mathbb{C}}[t],c\in{\mathbb{C}}

The critical orbit of such a polynomial is the sequence
\boldsymbol{c}_{P}\in\mathcal{E}
defined by

\boldsymbol{c}_{P}=(c_{i})_{i\geq 1}\in\mathcal{E}\text{ where }c_{i}=P^{\circ i}(0).

Since the preperiod and the period of a preperiodic sequence will be extensively discussed in this chapter, we introduce the following notions.

Definition 4.1
.

Given integers
k\geq 0,m\geq 1
, a sequence
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{E}
is called
preperiodic of type
(k,m)
if for every
i\geq k+1
,
x_{i+m}=x_{i}
,
preperiodic of
exact
type
(k,m)
if, additionally,
k
and
m
are the smallest integers satisfying such conditions.

For a sequence of exact type
(k,m)
, the pair
(k,m)
consists of the preperiod
k
and the period
m
. The vector space
\mathcal{P}_{k,m}
is the space of preperiodic sequences of type
(k,m)
.

Definition 4.2
.

A
degree
d
polynomial of (exact) type
(k,m)
is a monic centered unicritical polynomial
P
of degree
d\geq 2
whose critical orbit
{\boldsymbol{c}}_{P}
is of (exact) type
(k,m)
.

In other words, a polynomial is of type
(k,m)
if and only if its critical orbit belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{k,m}
. Note that a polynomial of type
(k,m)
is post-critically finite.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
P
be a polynomial of type
(k,m)
of degree
d
. If
k=0
, then the critical value
c
of
P
is a periodic point of period dividing
m
, i.e.
P^{\circ m}(c)=c
. In other words,
P^{\circ(m-1)}(c)\in P^{-1}(c)
. However, since
P
is a unicritical polynomial,
P^{-1}(c)
consists of exactly one point which is the critical point of
P
. This means that
P^{\circ(m-1)}(c)
is in fact the critical point of
P
. This is the case if and only if the critical point of
P
is also a periodic point of type
(0,m)
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Given
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
. Let
\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
be a fixed point of
F_{k,m}
. Set
P(t)=t^{d}+z_{1}
. Then
\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{c}_{P}
and
P
is of exact type
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})
. Moreover,
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\preceq(k,m)
.

Proof.

First, let us prove that
P
is of type
(k,m)
. According to Lemma
2.3
, for every
i\geq 2
, we have that
z_{i}=z_{i-1}^{d}+z_{1}
hence

z_{i}=P(z_{i-1}).

In other words,
\boldsymbol{z}
is the sequence of iterates of
z_{1}
under
P
. Recall that by Lemma
2.6
,
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
contains every fixed point of
F_{k,m}
hence
\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\subset\mathcal{P}_{k,m}
. Therefore,
\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{c}_{P}
and the polynomial
P
is a polynomial of type
(k,m)
.

Second, let
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})
be the exact type of
P
. We prove that
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\preceq(k,m)
, i.e.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}m^{\prime}\mid m\\
\text{either }k^{\prime}=k\text{ or }(k^{\prime}=0\text{ and }m^{\prime}\mid k)\end{array}\right.

Since
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})
is the exact type of the orbit of
z_{1}
,
k^{\prime}\leq k
and
m^{\prime}\mid m
. If
k^{\prime}=k
, we are done. If
k^{\prime}\neq k
, we need to prove that
k^{\prime}=0
and
m^{\prime}\mid k
. Since
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})
is the exact type of
\boldsymbol{z}
, we have
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathcal{P}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
. Thus,
k^{\prime}+1\leq k
and
m^{\prime}\mid m
; and since
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathcal{P}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
, this implies that
z_{k+m}=z_{k}
. Moreover,
\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
implies that
\beta z_{k+m}-z_{k}=0
. Therefore,
P^{\circ k}(0)=z_{k}=0.
In other words,
0
is a periodic point of
P
, i.e.
k^{\prime}=0
, and the period of
0
is
m^{\prime}
. Moreover,
P^{\circ k}(0)=0
also implies that
k
is a multiple of
m^{\prime}
. Thus, we can conclude that
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\preceq(k,m)
.
∎

Remark 4.5

The converse statement of Proposition

•

If

•

Conversely, assume

4.2.
Eigenvalues of moduli maps at fixed points

In order to study the eigenvalues of the derivative of moduli maps at one of its fixed point, we will in fact study its transpose. Note that when
k+m=1
,
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}=\{\textbf{0}\}
and
F_{k,m}
is trivial. Let us fix
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
such that
k+m\geq 2
. Assume that
\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
is a fixed point of
F_{k,m}
. We will describe the transpose of the derivative
D_{\boldsymbol{z}}F_{k,m}\colon T_{\boldsymbol{z}}{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to T_{\boldsymbol{z}}{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}

{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
is a vector space, there is a canonical identification of

with
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}

D_{\boldsymbol{z}}F_{k,m}\colon T_{\boldsymbol{z}}{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to T_{\boldsymbol{z}}{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
identifies with a linear map

L:{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m},

and the transpose identifies with the pull-back map of
L

L^{*}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}.

4.2.1.
The dual space
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}

For
i\geq 1
, let
\omega_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}
be the linear form defined by for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
,

\omega_{i}(\boldsymbol{v})\coloneqq v_{i}.

Lemma 4.7
.

The family
\{\omega_{i},1\leq i\leq k+m-1\}
is a basis of
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}
.

Proof.

Note that
\dim{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}=k+m-1
hence it is enough to prove that
\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k+m-1}\}
are linearly independent. Assume that

\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq k+m-1}\lambda_{i}\omega_{i}=0\text{ with $\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}$}.

Let
i\geq 1
. To prove that
\lambda_{i}=0
, consider the vector
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
defined by

•

if
i<k
,
v_{j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $j=i$}\\
0&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

•

if
i=k
,
v_{j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $j=i=k$}\\
\frac{1}{\beta}&\text{ if $j>k$ and $j\equiv k\mod{m}$}\\
0&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

•

if
i>k
,
v_{j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $j\geq i$ and $j\equiv i\mod{m}$}\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

In any case, we have

4.2.2.
The transpose of the derivative

Observe that
L^{*}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}
is the pull-back of forms, i.e. for all
\omega\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}
,

L^{*}\omega=\omega\circ L.

For all
i\geq 1
, set

\delta_{i}=dz_{i}^{d-1}

where
\boldsymbol{z}=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
is the considered fixed point of
F_{k,m}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

We have that

L^{*}\omega_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\delta_{m-1}\omega_{m-1}&\text{ if $k=0$}\\
-\frac{\beta\delta_{k+m-1}\omega_{k+m-1}-\delta_{k-1}\omega_{k-1}}{\beta-1}&\text{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

and for all
i\geq 2
,

L^{*}\omega_{i}=\delta_{i-1}\omega_{i-1}+L^{*}\omega_{1}.

Proof.

Recall that for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}L(\boldsymbol{v})&=&\pi_{k,m}\circ D_{\boldsymbol{z}}\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{v}).\\
\end{array}

Set
\boldsymbol{u}=D_{\boldsymbol{z}}\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{v})
then

u_{1}=0\quad\text{ and for all $i\geq 2$},\quad u_{i}=dz_{i-1}^{d-1}v_{i-1}=\delta_{i-1}v_{i-1}.

In addition, if
\boldsymbol{w}\coloneqq L(\boldsymbol{v})=\pi_{k,m}(\boldsymbol{u})
then

\text{ for all $i\geq 2$, }\quad w_{i}=u_{i}+w_{1}\text{ with }w_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}-u_{m}&\text{ if }k=0\\
-\frac{\beta u_{k+m-1}-u_{k-1}}{\beta-1}&\text{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

Combining those formulas, we obtain that
\boldsymbol{w}=L(\boldsymbol{v})
and for all
i\geq 2
,

(4.1)

w_{i}=\delta_{i-1}v_{i-1}+w_{1}\text{ with }w_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\delta_{m-1}v_{m-1}&\text{ if $k=0$}\\
-\frac{\beta\delta_{k+m-1}v_{k+m-1}-\delta_{k-1}v_{k-1}}{\beta-1}&\text{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

We deduce that for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
,

L^{*}\omega_{1}(\boldsymbol{v})=\omega_{1}\circ L(\boldsymbol{v})=w_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\delta_{m-1}v_{m-1}&\text{ if $k=0$}\\
-\frac{\beta\delta_{k+m-1}\omega_{k+m-1}-\delta_{k-1}\omega_{k-1}}{\beta-1}&\text{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

hence

L^{*}\omega_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\delta_{m-1}\omega_{m-1}&\text{ if $k=0$}\\
-\frac{\beta\delta_{k+m-1}\omega_{k+m-1}-\delta_{k-1}\omega_{k-1}}{\beta-1}&\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

In addition, for all
i\geq 2
and for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
, we have

L^{*}\omega_{i}(\boldsymbol{v})=\omega_{i}\circ L(\boldsymbol{v})=\delta_{i-1}v_{i-1}+w_{1}=\delta_{i-1}v_{i-1}+\omega_{1}\circ L(\boldsymbol{v}),

hence

L^{*}\omega_{i}=\delta_{i-1}\omega_{i-1}+L^{*}\omega_{1}.\qed

4.2.3.
Fixed points outside the post-critical set

According to Section
3.1
, the map
F_{k,m}
is conjugate to the map
G_{k,m}
constructed by Koch
[
Koc13
]
. By
[
Koc13
, Corollary 7.2]
, the derivative of

at its fixed points outside the post-critical set has only eigenvalues of modulus strictly greater than

, whence so does
F_{k,m}
. For the sake of completeness, we give here the proof of this property. For further discussion about the arithmetics of such eigenvalues, we refer to
[
BEK20
]
. The main content of this paragraph is the following result.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
and
\boldsymbol{z}\notin PC(F_{k,m})
be a fixed point of the moduli map
F_{k,m}
. Then every eigenvalue of
D_{\boldsymbol{z}}F_{k,m}
has modulus strictly greater than
1
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
has finite dimension, it is suffice to prove that every eigenvalue of the transpose
L^{*}
of
D_{\boldsymbol{z}}F_{k,m}
has modulus strictly bigger than
1
.

Recall that, by Lemma
4.7
, the family
\{\omega_{i}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to{\mathbb{C}}\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,k+m-1\}}
is a basis of
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}
. According to Lemma
4.8
, setting
\delta_{i}=dz_{i}^{d-1}
, we have

L^{*}\omega_{1}=\begin{cases}-\delta_{m-1}\omega_{m-1}&\text{if }k=0\\
\displaystyle-\frac{\beta\delta_{k+m-1}\omega_{k+m-1}-\delta_{k-1}\omega_{k-1}}{\beta-1}&\text{if }k\geq 1,\end{cases}

and for all
i\geq 2
,

L^{*}\omega_{i}=\delta_{i-1}\omega_{i-1}+L^{*}\omega_{1}.

According to Lemma
4.6
, the point
\boldsymbol{z}
is a fixed point of
F_{k,m}
of exact type
(k,m)
. Therefore,
\delta_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k+m-1\}
. Indeed, note that according to Proposition
4.4
, the sequence
\boldsymbol{z}
is the critical orbit of
P(t)=t^{d}+z_{1}
. Assume that
dz_{i}^{d-1}=\delta_{i}=0
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,k+m-1\}
. Then
P^{\circ i}(0)=z_{i}=0
. This implies that
k=0
and
m
divides
i
. However
i\leq k+m-1=m-1<m
, hence contradiction.

We may therefore define a linear map

L_{*}:{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}
by

(4.2)

\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,k+m-1\}\quad L_{*}(\omega_{i})=\frac{\omega_{i+1}-\omega_{1}}{\delta_{i}}.

Lemma 4.10
.

The linear map
L^{*}
is invertible and its inverse is
L_{*}
.

Proof.

We need to prove that
L_{*}\circ L^{*}=L^{*}\circ L_{*}={\rm id}
.
First, observe that for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k+m-1\}
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}L^{*}\circ L_{*}(\omega_{i})=L^{*}\left(\frac{\omega_{i+1}-\omega_{1}}{\delta_{i}}\right)&=&\frac{1}{\delta_{i}}\left(L^{*}(\omega_{i+1})-L^{*}(\omega_{1})\right)\\
&=&\frac{1}{\delta_{i}}\bigl(\delta_{i}\omega_{i}+L^{*}(\omega_{1})-L^{*}(\omega_{1})\bigr)=\omega_{i}.\end{array}

Second, we prove
L_{*}\circ L^{*}=\Id_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}}
. Note that, by the definition of
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
, we have that

\omega_{k+m}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }k=0\\
\frac{1}{\beta}\omega_{k}&\text{if }k\geq 1\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\forall i\geq k+m+1\quad\omega_{i+m}=\omega_{i}.

To compute
L_{*}\circ L^{*}(\omega_{1})
, observe that if
k=0
, then

L_{*}\circ L^{*}(\omega_{1})=L_{*}(-\delta_{m-1}\omega_{m-1})=-\delta_{m-1}L_{*}(\omega_{m-1})=-(\omega_{m}-\omega_{1})=\omega_{1}

and if
k\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle L_{*}\circ L^{*}(\omega_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{*}\left(-\frac{\beta\delta_{k+m-1}\omega_{k+m-1}-\delta_{k-1}\omega_{k-1}}{\beta-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}(\omega_{k+m}-\omega_{1})-\frac{1}{\beta-1}(\omega_{k}-\omega_{1})\right)=\omega_{1}.

In both cases,
L_{*}\circ L^{*}(\omega_{1})=\omega_{1}
.
For
L_{*}\circ L^{*}(\omega_{i})
with
i\in\{2,\ldots,k+m-1\}
,

L_{*}\circ L^{*}(\omega_{i})=L_{*}\bigl(\delta_{i-1}\omega_{i-1}+L^{*}(\omega_{1})\bigr)=\delta_{i-1}\frac{\omega_{i}-\omega_{1}}{\delta_{i-1}}+L_{*}L^{*}(\omega_{1})=\omega_{i}.

Thus, the linear map
L_{*}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}
is indeed the inverse of
L^{*}

∎

In order to prove Proposition
4.9
, it is therefore enough to prove that every eigenvalue of
L_{*}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}^{*}
is contained in the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. Inspired by the proof of
[
Koc13
, Corollary 7.2]
, we will show that
L_{*}
is conjugate to a linear transformation on a space of meromorphic quadratic differentials on
{\mathbb{C}}
, whose eigenvalues are all contained in
\mathbb{D}
.

Consider the quadratic polynomial
P(t)\coloneqq t^{d}+z_{1}
, so that
z_{i}=P^{\circ i}(0)
for all
i\geq 1
.
Following Milnor
[
Mil14
]
, denote by
\mathfrak{Q}({\mathbb{C}})
the space of meromorphic quadratic differentials on
{\mathbb{C}}
which have at worst simple poles and let us use the notation
Q\in\mathfrak{Q}({\mathbb{C}})
with

Q=q(t)\text{d}t^{2}.

and
q(t)
is a meromorphic function. Let
U\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be a sufficiently large disk so that
P^{-1}(U)
is compactly contained in
U
and for
Q\in\mathfrak{Q}({\mathbb{C}})
, consider the norm

\|Q\|_{U}\coloneqq\iint_{U}\bigl|q(t)\text{d}t^{2}\bigr|.

The pushforward of
Q
by
P
is the quadratic differential
P_{*}Q\in\mathfrak{Q}({\mathbb{C}})
defined by

P_{*}Q\coloneqq\sum_{P(u)=t}\frac{q(u)}{\bigl(P^{\prime}(u)\bigr)^{2}}\text{d}t^{2}.

It follows from the triangle inequality that

\|P_{*}Q\|_{U}\leq\|Q\|_{P^{-1}(U)}<\|Q\|_{U}.

For
i\geq 1
, let
Q_{i}\in\mathfrak{Q}({\mathbb{C}})
be the quadratic differential defined by

Q_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\text{d}t^{2}}{t-z_{i}}.

The following lemma generalizes a result due to Milnor,
[
Mil14
, Lemma 1]
in the case

.

Lemma 4.11
.

For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k+m-1\}
,

(4.3)

P_{*}Q_{i}=\frac{Q_{i+1}-Q_{1}}{\delta_{i}}.

Proof of Lemma
4.11
.

Set
\xi\coloneqq e^{\frac{2\pi i}{d}}
. Observe that for a given
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
and for a given

such that
P(w)=z
, we have
\{P(u)=z\}=\{w,\xi w,\ldots,\xi^{d-1}w\}
. Thus, for a given

,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}P_{*}Q_{i}(z)&=&\sum_{P(u)=z}\dfrac{1}{u-z_{i}}\dfrac{1}{\bigl(P^{\prime}(u)\bigr)^{2}}\text{d}t^{2}\\
&=&\sum\limits_{j=0}^{d-1}\dfrac{1}{\xi^{j}w-z_{i}}\dfrac{1}{\left(d\left(\xi^{j}w\right)^{d-1}\right)^{2}}\text{d}t^{2}\\
&=&\dfrac{1}{d^{2}w^{2d-2}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{d-1}\dfrac{1}{\xi^{-j}w-\xi^{-2j}z_{i}}\right)\text{d}t^{2}.\\
\end{array}

Note that
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{d-1}\dfrac{1}{\xi^{-j}w-\xi^{-2j}z_{i}}=\dfrac{dz_{i}w^{d-2}}{w^{d}-z_{i}^{d}}
2
2

2

This equality is equivalent to the equality
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{1}{\xi^{-j}\chi-\xi^{-2j}}=\frac{d\chi^{d-2}}{\chi^{d}-1}
. The later follows from an elementary computation by comparing the partial fraction decomposition.
. Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}P_{*}Q_{i}(z)&=&\dfrac{z_{i}}{dw^{d}(w^{d}-z_{i}^{d})}\text{d}t^{2}\\
&=&\dfrac{1}{\delta_{i}}\dfrac{z_{i}^{d}}{w^{d}(w^{d}-z_{i}^{d})}\text{d}t^{2}\end{array}

Since

we have

P_{*}Q_{i}(z)=\dfrac{1}{\delta_{i}}\dfrac{z_{i+1}-z_{1}}{(z-z_{1})(z-z_{i+1})}\text{d}t^{2}=\frac{1}{\delta_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{z-z_{i+1}}-\frac{1}{z-z_{1}}\right)\text{d}t^{2}.

Thus,
P_{i}Q_{i}=\dfrac{Q_{i+1}-Q_{i}}{\delta_{i}}
∎

The quadratic differentials

span a vector space

of dimension

. According to Equation (

.
According to Equations (

) and (

is an isomorphism which conjugates

Since

for all

∎

Whence, by Proposition

,
L|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}
has only eigenvalues of modulus strictly greater than

.
In order to describe
\Spec L
, we need to study
\Spec\left(L^{*}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{0}}\right)

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
(k,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
and
\boldsymbol{z}\in PC(F_{k,m})
be a fixed point of

of exact type
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\prec(k,m)

\lambda

\Spec\left((D_{\boldsymbol{z}}F_{k,m})^{*}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{0}}\right)=\begin{cases}\{0\}&\text{if }k^{\prime}=0\\
\displaystyle\{\mu\mid\mu^{m}=\lambda^{\frac{m}{m^{\prime}}},\mu^{m^{\prime}}\neq\lambda\}&\text{if }k^{\prime}\neq 0.\end{cases}

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of this proposition. To simplify the notation, we denote by
L^{*}
the restriction of
(D_{\boldsymbol{z}}F_{k,m})^{*}
to
{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{0}
. The study of the transpose
L^{*}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{0}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{0}
is divided into two cases,

and

, and each case will be treated separately.

Proof of Proposition
4.12
when
k^{\prime}=0
.

Since
(0,m^{\prime})\preceq(k,m)
,
m^{\prime}
divides
k
and
m
. It is enough to prove that
L^{*}:{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}^{0}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}^{0}
is nilpotent. Recall that for
i\geq 1,
the form
\omega_{i}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is defined by
\omega_{i}(\boldsymbol{v})=v_{i}
. For
i\geq 1
, set
\alpha_{i}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

\alpha_{i}=\omega_{i}-\omega_{i+m^{\prime}}.

Recall that
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}^{0}=\{\omega\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to{\mathbb{C}}\text{ such that }\omega|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}}\equiv 0\}
.

Lemma 4.13
.

We have
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}^{0}=\Span\left\{\alpha_{i},1\leq i\leq k+m\right\}.

Proof.

By duality, it is equivalent to show that

{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}=\bigcap\limits_{1\leq i\leq k+m}\Ker\alpha_{i}.

Assume
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}
. Then for all
j\geq 1,v_{j}=v_{j+m^{\prime}}
. Given
i\in\{1,\ldots,k+m\}
, we have

\alpha_{i}(\boldsymbol{v})=\omega_{i}(\boldsymbol{v})-\omega_{i+m^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{v})=v_{i}-v_{i+m^{\prime}}=0.

Hence
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{1\leq i\leq k+m}\Ker\alpha_{i}
.

Conversely, assume
\boldsymbol{v}\in\bigcap\limits_{1\leq i\leq k+m}\Ker\alpha_{i}
, i.e. for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k+m\}
,
v_{i}=v_{i+m^{\prime}}
. In order to prove that
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}
, we will prove that for all
j\geq k+m+1,v_{j}=v_{j+m^{\prime}}
and that
v_{m^{\prime}}=0
. Given
j\geq k+m+1
, there exists an integer
j^{\prime}\in\{k+1,\ldots,k+m\}
such that
j\equiv j^{\prime}\mod{m}
. Since
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
, we have
v_{j}=v_{j^{\prime}}
and
v_{j+m^{\prime}}=v_{j^{\prime}+m^{\prime}}
. Moreover, the fact that
\boldsymbol{v}\in\Ker\alpha_{j^{\prime}}
implies that
v_{j^{\prime}}=v_{j^{\prime}+m^{\prime}}
. Thus

v_{j}=v_{j^{\prime}}=v_{j^{\prime}+m^{\prime}}=v_{j+m^{\prime}}.

In order to conclude, we need to show that
v_{m^{\prime}}=0
. Note that the previous argument shows that
\boldsymbol{v}
is a periodic sequence of period dividing
m^{\prime}
. Since
m^{\prime}
divides
k
and
m
,

v_{m^{\prime}}=v_{k}=v_{k+m}.

Since
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m},
we have
\beta v_{k+m}-v_{k}=0
with
\beta\neq 1
, whence

v_{m^{\prime}}=v_{k}=v_{k+m}=0.

∎

Lemma 4.14
.

We have
L^{*}\alpha_{1}=0
and for
i\geq 2
,
L^{*}\alpha_{i}=\delta_{i-1}\alpha_{i-1}
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
4.8
, for all
i\geq 2
,

L^{*}\omega_{i}=\delta_{i-1}\omega_{i-1}+L^{*}\omega_{1}.

Hence, if
i\geq 2
,

L^{*}\alpha_{i}=L^{*}(\omega_{i}-\omega_{i+m^{\prime}})=\delta_{i-1}\omega_{i-1}-\delta_{i+m^{\prime}-1}\omega_{i+m^{\prime}-1}.

Since
\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}
, we have
\delta_{i-1}=dz_{i-1}^{d-1}=dz_{i+m^{\prime}-1}^{d-1}=\delta_{i+m^{\prime}-1}
. Hence

L^{*}\alpha_{i-1}=\delta_{i-1}(\omega_{i-1}-\omega_{i+m^{\prime}-1})=\delta_{i-1}\alpha_{i-1}.

If
i=1
, since
m^{\prime}\geq 1
, we have
1+m^{\prime}\geq 2
so that

L^{*}\omega_{1+m^{\prime}}=\delta_{m^{\prime}}\omega_{m^{\prime}}+L^{*}\omega_{1}.

Hence

L^{*}\alpha_{1}=L^{*}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{1+m^{\prime}})=-\delta_{m^{\prime}}\omega_{m^{\prime}}.

Since
\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}
, we have
z_{m^{\prime}}=0
. Therefore,
\delta_{m^{\prime}}=dz_{m^{\prime}}^{d-1}=0
and
L^{*}\alpha_{1}=0
. ∎

It follows from Lemma
4.13
and Lemma
4.14
that
L^{*}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m^{\prime}}
is nilpotent.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.12
when
k^{\prime}\neq 0
.

In this case, since
(k^{\prime},m^{\prime})\prec(k,m)
, we have

k^{\prime}=k\text{ and }m=pm^{\prime}\text{ with }p\geq 2.

Let
\lambda
be the multiplier of
P(t)=t^{d}+z_{1}
at
P^{\circ k}(0)
. Note that, according to Proposition
4.4
,
\boldsymbol{z}
is the critical orbit of
P
. Since
\boldsymbol{z}
is preperiodic of preperiod
k>0
, the critical point
0
of
P
is preperiodic, i.e.
\lambda\neq 0
. We will show that

\Spec(L^{*}:{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0})=\{\mu\mid\mu^{m}=\lambda^{p},\mu^{m^{\prime}}\neq\lambda\}.

Given
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
, denote by
{\underline{j}}
the representative of
j
in
\{k+1,\ldots,k+m\}
, define a linear form
\beta_{j}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

\beta_{j}\coloneqq\omega_{\underline{j}}-\omega_{\underline{j+m^{\prime}}}.

Note that for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\beta_{j}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is non trivial. Indeed, for a given

, define
\boldsymbol{u}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
by

u_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $i\geq k+1$ and $i\equiv\underline{j}\mod{m}$}\\
\frac{1}{\beta}&\text{ if $i=k$ and $\underline{j}=k+m$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since
m^{\prime}<m
,
\beta_{j}(\boldsymbol{u})=u_{\underline{j}}=1\neq 0
.

We will show that these forms span
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}\subsetneq{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
and use them to study the linear map
L^{*}:{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}
. The properties we need are provided by the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.15
.

We have
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}=\Span\{\beta_{j},j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}\}
.

Proof.

By duality, it is equivalent to show that

{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}=\bigcap\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}\Ker\beta_{j}.

Assume
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}
. Then for all
i\geq k+1,v_{i}=v_{i+m^{\prime}}
. Since
\underline{j}\in j
and
m^{\prime}
divides
m
, we have
\underline{j}\equiv\underline{j+m^{\prime}}\mod{m^{\prime}}
. Moreover,
\underline{j}\geq k+1
and
\underline{j+m^{\prime}}\geq k+1
, whence

\beta_{j}(\boldsymbol{v})=v_{\underline{j}}-v_{\underline{j+m^{\prime}}}=0.

This shows that
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}\Ker\beta_{j}
.

Conversely, assume
\boldsymbol{v}\in\bigcap\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}\Ker\beta_{j}
. We want to prove that for all integer
i\geq k+1,v_{i}=v_{i+m^{\prime}}
and that
\beta v_{k+m^{\prime}}-v_{k}=0
. First, assume
i\geq k+1
and let
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
be the congruence class of
i
. Since
\underline{j}\in j
, we have
i\equiv\underline{j}\mod{m}
. Moreover
\boldsymbol{v}\in\Ker\beta_{j}
, and so

v_{\underline{j}}-v_{\underline{j+m^{\prime}}}=\beta_{j}(\boldsymbol{v})=0.

From the fact that
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
, we therefore deduce that
v_{i}=v_{\underline{j}}
and
v_{\underline{j+m^{\prime}}}=v_{i+m^{\prime}}.
Thus,

v_{i}=v_{i+m^{\prime}}.

Second, let us show
\beta v_{k+m^{\prime}}-v_{k}=0
. The previous argument shows that
\boldsymbol{v}
is preperiodic of preperiod at most
k
and of period dividing
m^{\prime}
. Since
m^{\prime}
divides
m
, we deduce that
v_{k+m^{\prime}}=v_{k+m}
. Since
\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
, we have
\beta v_{k+m}-v_{k}=0

\beta v_{k+m^{\prime}}-v_{k}=0
.
∎

Thus, it is now important to understand the how
L^{*}
acts on
\{\beta_{j},j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}\}
. Given
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
, set

\sigma_{j}=\delta_{\underline{j}}=dz_{\underline{j}}^{d-1}.

Lemma 4.16
.

For
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have
L^{*}\beta_{j}=\sigma_{{j-1}}\beta_{j-1}.

Proof.

For
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
, recall that
\underline{j}
is the representative of
j
in
\llbracket k+1,k+m\rrbracket
. Let us first prove that for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
,

(4.5)

L^{*}\omega_{\underline{j}}=\sigma_{{j-1}}\omega_{\underline{j-1}}+L^{*}\omega_{1}.

Indeed, if
\underline{j}=k+1
then
\underline{j-1}=k+m
, whence
\sigma_{j-1}\omega_{\underline{j-1}}=\delta_{k+m}\omega_{k+m}
. According to Lemma
4.8
, we have
L^{*}\omega_{k+1}=\delta_{k}\omega_{k}+L^{*}\omega_{1}
. Since
\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m}
, we have

\delta_{k}=dz_{k}^{d-1}=d\beta^{d-1}z_{k+m}^{d-1}=\beta^{d-1}\delta_{k+m}.

Moreover,
\omega_{k}=\beta\omega_{k+m}
, whence
\delta_{k}\omega_{k}=\beta^{d}\delta_{k+m}\omega_{k+m}
. Since
\beta^{d}=1,

L^{*}\omega_{k+1}=\delta_{k}\omega_{k}+L^{*}\omega_{1}=\delta_{k+m}\omega_{k+m}+L^{*}\omega_{1}.

If
\underline{j}\neq k+1
then
\underline{j-1}=\underline{j}-1
. According to Lemma
4.8
, we have

L^{*}\omega_{\underline{j}}=\delta_{\underline{j}-1}\omega_{\underline{j}-1}+L^{*}\omega_{1}=\delta_{\underline{j-1}}\omega_{\underline{j-1}}+L^{*}\omega_{1}=\sigma_{j-1}\omega_{\underline{j-1}}+L^{*}\omega_{1}.

In any case, we have the equality (
4.5
). Hence

L^{*}\beta_{j}=L^{*}(\omega_{\underline{j}}-\omega_{\underline{j+m^{\prime}}})=\sigma_{j-1}\omega_{\underline{j-1}}-\sigma_{j+m^{\prime}-1}\omega_{\underline{j+m^{\prime}-1}}.

Note that
\underline{j-1}
and
\underline{j+m^{\prime}-1}
, which are congruence modulo
m^{\prime}
, are two integers at least
k+1
. Since
\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}
, we have
\sigma_{j-1}=\delta_{\underline{j-1}}=\delta_{\underline{j+m^{\prime}-1}}=\sigma_{j+m^{\prime}-1}
. Thus

L^{*}\beta_{j}=\sigma_{j-1}(\omega_{\underline{j-1}}-\omega_{\underline{j+m^{\prime}-1}})=\sigma_{j-1}\beta_{j-1}.\qed

Recall that
\lambda
is the multiplier of
P(t)=t^{d}+z_{1}
at the periodic point
z_{k+1}
of period
m^{\prime}=\frac{m}{p}
.

Lemma 4.17
.

For all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
,

(L^{*})^{m^{\prime}}(\beta_{j})=\lambda\beta_{j-m^{\prime}}\quad\text{ and }\quad(L^{*})^{m}(\beta_{j})=\lambda^{p}\beta_{j}.

Proof.

The second equality is the straightforward consequence of the first one. Hence, it is enough to prove the first equality. According to Proposition
4.4
,
\boldsymbol{z}
is the critical orbit of the polynomial
P(t)=t^{d}+z_{1}
, i.e.
z_{i}=P^{i}(0)
. In particular, for any
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have
P(z_{\underline{j-1}})=z_{\underline{j}}
and
P^{\prime}(z_{\underline{j-1}})=dz_{\underline{j-1}}^{d-1}=\delta_{\underline{j-1}}
. Since
\boldsymbol{z}
is of type
(k,m^{\prime})
, the multiplier
\lambda
of the cycle of
z_{\underline{j}}
is

\lambda=\prod\limits_{i\in\llbracket 1,m^{\prime}\rrbracket}P^{\prime}(z_{\underline{j-i}})=\prod\limits_{i\in\llbracket 1,m^{\prime}\rrbracket}\delta_{\underline{j-i}}=\prod\limits_{i\in\llbracket 1,m^{\prime}\rrbracket}\sigma_{j-i}.

Hence, by Lemma
4.16
, we have

(L^{*})^{m^{\prime}}(\beta_{j})=\left(\prod\limits_{i\in\llbracket 1,m^{\prime}\rrbracket}\sigma_{j-i}\right)\beta_{j-m^{\prime}}=\lambda\beta_{j-m^{\prime}},

and

(L^{*})^{m}(\beta_{j})=(L^{*})^{m^{\prime}p}(\beta_{j})=\lambda^{p}\beta_{j}.\qed

Let
\nu
be a
m^{\prime}
-th root of
\lambda
. Set

T=\frac{L^{*}}{\nu}:{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}.

We shall prove that
T
is diagonalizable with simple eigenvalues and the eigenvalues of
T
are
m
-th roots of unity except
1
. According to Lemma
4.17
, for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
,
T^{m^{\prime}}(\beta_{j})=\beta_{j-m^{\prime}}
. In addition,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\sum\limits_{n\in m^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}\beta_{n}&=&\sum\limits_{n\in m^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}(\omega_{\underline{n}}-\omega_{\underline{n+m^{\prime}}})=0\\
&=&\sum\limits_{n\in m^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}\omega_{\underline{n}}-\sum\limits_{n\in m^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}\omega_{\underline{n+m^{\prime}}}=0.\end{array}

Recall that
p=\frac{m}{m^{\prime}}
. Hence

\beta_{0}+T^{m^{\prime}}(\beta_{0})+\ldots+T^{m^{\prime}(p-1)}(\beta_{0})=0

Applying
m^{\prime}-1
times
T
and adding the results, we deduce that

\beta_{0}+T(\beta_{0})+T^{2}(\beta_{0})+\ldots+T^{m^{\prime}p-1}(\beta_{0})=0

According to Lemma
4.15
and Lemma
4.16
, the set
\{\beta_{0},L^{*}(\beta_{0}),(L^{*})^{2}(\beta_{0}),\ldots\}
generates
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}
. Hence
\{\beta_{0},T(\beta_{0}),T^{2}(\beta_{0}),\ldots\}
also generates
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}
. Therefore,

(4.6)

\Id+T+T^{2}+\ldots+T^{m-1}=0

This means that the minimal polynomial of
T
divides the polynomial
1+X+X^{2}+\ldots+X^{m-1}
. Consequently,
T
is diagonalizable and the eigenvalues of
T
are roots of unity which are not
1
. We now show that
T
has only simple eigenvalues. Assume
\zeta\in\Spec T
. Let
v\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}
be an eigenvector associated to
\zeta
. Set

H_{\zeta}=\frac{1}{m}\left(\Id+\frac{T}{\zeta}+\ldots+\frac{T^{m-1}}{\zeta^{m-1}}\right).

The equality (
4.6
) implies that
T^{m}=\Id
. Additionally,
\zeta^{m}=1
. Hence

H_{\zeta}\circ\frac{T}{\zeta}=H_{\zeta}\quad\text{ so that }\quad H_{\zeta}\circ T^{j}=\zeta^{j}H_{\zeta}\quad\forall j\geq 1.

In addition,
\{\beta_{0},T(\beta_{0}),\ldots\}
generates
{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}
, hence

\Imm H_{\zeta}\subseteq\Span H_{\zeta}(\beta_{0}).

Note that
H_{\zeta}(v)=v
. Hence

v\subseteq\Imm H_{\zeta}\subseteq\Span H_{\zeta}(\beta_{0}).

Thus the eigenspace associated to the eigenvalue
\zeta
of
T
has dimension
1
, i.e.
T
has only simple eigenvalues.

Since
T=\frac{L^{*}}{\nu}
,
L^{*}
is diagonalizable with simple eigenvalues which are
m
-th roots of

. In addition,
1
is not an eigenvalue of
T
hence
\nu
is not an eigenvalue of
L^{*}
. Since
\nu
is an arbitrary
m^{\prime}
-th root of
\lambda
, we deduce that

\Spec L^{*}\subseteq\{\mu^{m}=\lambda^{p},\mu^{m^{\prime}}\neq\lambda\}.

Since

has only simple eigenvalues,
\#\Spec L^{*}=\dim{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}=m-m^{\prime}
. Hence

\Spec(L^{*}:{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k,m^{\prime}}^{0})=\{\mu^{m}=\lambda^{p},\mu^{m^{\prime}}\neq\lambda\}\qed
